Introduction

In 2015, T.-T. Shieh and D. Spector have considered a new type of fractional differential operators in
[
SS15
]
, combining results of Harmonic Analysis, specially pseudodifferential operators, with Calculus of Variations.
The functionals in
[
SS15
]
defining those operators are given with the distributional Riesz fractional gradient, denoted by
D^{s}
.

When applied to smooth functions, the
D^{s}
can be computed as the gradient
D
of the convolution of the function with the Riesz kernel.
More precisely, the operator
D^{s}
can be expressed as

D^{s}u=D(I_{1-s}*u),

(1.1)

where
I_{1-s}(x)=\frac{\mu_{d,s}}{d+s-1}|x|^{1-s-d}
denotes the Riesz kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
s\in(0,1)
and
\mu_{d,s}
is given by (
2.2
). These fractional derivatives have some advantages in some types of fractional differential equations.
Firstly, they can be extended to more general classes of function spaces of Sobolev type and they approach, in a natural way, the classical derivatives due to the fact that the Riesz kernel
I_{1-s}
is an approximation of the identity as
s\nearrow 1
.
Secondly, being vector-valued like the classical gradient, contrasting with the scalar fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, they are suitable for inhomogeneous and anisotropic problems, since duality methods apply to the Riesz fractional s-gradient.
Moreover, it can also provide an eleventh characterization of the fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to
[
Kwa17
]
.
Indeed, denoting by
\Phi\mapsto\mathrm{div}_{s}\Phi=D^{s}\cdot\Phi
the
s
-divergence, we have in the distributional sense

(-\Delta)^{s}u=-D^{s}\cdot D^{s}u.

(1.2)

Subsequently, A. Ponce made a brief introduction to the fractional gradient and divergence operators for smooth functions with compact support,
[
Pon16
, pages 246-251]
, and
M. Šilhavý published in
[
Š20
]
a fractional vector calculus developing ideas presented in a seminar
[
Š16
]
.
In the introduction of
[
SS18
]
it is mentioned the equivalence of the definition of the distributional Riesz fractional gradient (
1.1
) with the definition (
2.6
) of
s
-fractional gradient introduced by Šilhavý in
[
Š16
]
and
[
Š20
]
, which corresponds to

D^{s}u=-N_{-s}*u,

(1.3)

with
N_{-s}(x)=-DI_{1-s}(x)=\mu_{d,s}x|x|^{-1-s-d}
being the vector-valued kernel of Horváth
[
Hor59
]
. Actually, this definition can be seen as a special case of a definition used by Sobolev and Nikol'skiĭ in
[
SNs
]
in a more general framework, with generalized homogeneous functions of degree
-d-s
(a class of functions containing the Horváth kernel as a special case), under the name of
Liouville derivatives of order
s
. The proof of that equivalence was proven in
[
Pon16
]
and in the work of G. Comi and G. Stefani
[
CS19
]
, respectively, for smooth and Lipschitz functions with compact support.

Šilhavý’s treatment was different from the one of Shieh and Spector, as he was interested in showing that these nonlocal operators satisfy the “natural qualitative requirements on the fractional operators”.
Notably, Šilhavý has shown that
D^{s}
is, up to a multiplicative constant, the only operator applying scalar functions into vector valued functions that satisfies:

(i)
rotational and translational invariance, important for the operators to be independent of the chosen basis;

(ii)
a mild continuity hypothesis,
D^{s}
is sequential continuous for test functions in a suitable topology, and

(iii)

s
-homogeneity under isotropic scaling, which characterizes the fractional nature of the operator.
In addition, using the weaker form of the definitions of the fractional gradient and divergence, he derived explicit formulas for these operators, in particular, showing that
(-\Delta)^{(s+r)/2}u=-D^{s}\cdot D^{r}u
and computing directly the fractional gradient of distributions like the Dirac delta or the Heaviside function in
\mathbb{R}
,
[
Š20
, Examples 6.2 and 6.3]
.

With this notion of fractional gradient, for
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, in
[
SS15
]
it was also introduced the following Sobolev-type space,

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

(1.4)

i.e., the completion of the space of test functions for the norm

\|u\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/p}.

(1.5)

These spaces, that we call here Lions-Calderón spaces, were not new, as they were shown, for
1<p<\infty
, also in
[
SS15
]
, to be equivalent to the so-called Bessel potential spaces in
[
Ada75
,
AH96
,
Ste70
]
or generalized (inhomogeneous) Sobolev spaces in
[
Gra14b
]
. With this new approach, they are well suited to the study of some fractional partial differential equations, including nonhomogeneous and anisotropic variants of the fractional Laplacian. Therefore they have been well studied in the literature, as they have a long and rich history. Indeed, the oldest reference we could find for these spaces is due to N. Aronzajn and K. Smith, who in
[
AS59
,
AS61
]
defined them in the Hilbertian case
p=2
. As it is well known, this case coincides with the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Later, those spaces were independently generalized to the non-Hilbertian case
1<p<\infty
, by J.-L. Lions in
[
Lio60
]
, using complex interpolation between
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and by A. P. Calderón in
[
Cal61
]
, using Bessel potentials. The initial definition in terms of Bessel potentials
G_{s}
, with their different notations
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=G_{s}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is not sufficient to justify their terminology as “Bessel potential spaces”. In fact, both the Besov and the Triebel-Liozorkin spaces can be obtained also by the image of Bessel potential, respectively
B^{s,p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=G_{s}(B^{0,p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
F^{s,p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=G_{s}(F^{0,p}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and we have
B^{s,p}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
F^{s,p}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, see
[
Tri83
]
for instance.

On the other hand, as in
[
CS19
]
under the name of
distributional fractional Sobolev spaces
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Lions-Calderón spaces were defined directly in terms of the
s
-fractional gradient, i.e.

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,D^{s}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\},

(1.6)

which equivalence with the definition (
1.4
) was proved for
1<p<\infty
in
[
BCCS22
]
and independently in
[
KS22
]
, while for
p=1
it has been earlier proven in
[
CS19
]
.
Actually this definition was also suggested for general spaces of functions with Liouville derivatives by Sobolev and Nikol'skiĭ in the survey
[
SNs
, page 150]
. The alternative names “generalized Sobolev spaces” or “distributional fractional Sobolev spaces”, although possibly more appropriate to this general class of functional spaces of the form
\{u\in L^{p}:\mathcal{D}u\in L^{p}\}
, with
\mathcal{D}
being some broadened notion of derivatives, colides with the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which are also well-known as the fractional Sobolev spaces and do not coincide with the spaces
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, except when
p=2
or when
s
is an integer.

Another important property of the fractional gradient
D^{s}
is the continuous dependence with respect to the parameter
s
. In fact, it was noted, in
[
Š16
]
and in
[
Pon16
]
without proofs, in
[
RS19a
]
using Kurokawa’s results
[
Kur81
]
on the approximating of the identity by Riesz kernels, and in
[
BCMC21
]
using Fourier Analysis and an interpolation inequality, that the following property for sufficiently regular functions
u
,

D^{s}u\to Du\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\,\mbox{ as }s\nearrow 1,\mbox{ for }1<p<\infty.

In
[
CS22
,
BCCS22
]
, using direct estimates and properties of the singular integrals, the authors extended these type of continuity results to the variation of
s
, by showing that

D^{s}u\to D^{\sigma}u\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\,\mbox{ as }s\to\sigma\in(0,1],\mbox{ for }1\leq p<\infty,

and also

D^{s}u\to-Ru\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})\,\,\mbox{ as }s\searrow 0,\mbox{ for }1<p<\infty.

Here
R
is the Riesz transform (see
[
Ste70
,
Gra14a
]
), which motivates the notation
D^{0}=-R
.

Concerning applications to partial differential equations, these spaces had also appeared in
[
LM61
]
and in
[
JK95
]
, to study the regularity theory for linear elliptic partial differential equations, and in
[
LP15
]
to study a variety of questions about solutions to dispersive partial differential equations. However the new characterization of these spaces using the distributional Riesz fractional gradient allows the study of fractional partial differential equations, as proposed in
[
SS15
]
, as well as extensions to unilateral problems, as in
[
RS19a
,
ARS23
,
ARS22
,
LR23a
]
and other problems of the Calculus of Variations, as in
[
SS18
]
,
[
BCMC20
,
BCMC21
]
and
[
KS22
]
, in particular with applications in Peridynamics
[
Sil00
,
SEW+07
]
to model fractional hyperelasticity, see also
[
Sch21
]
. On the other hand, this functional framework for the Riesz fractional gradient is also well suited for certain classes of evolution problems, such as fractional Stefan-type problems
[
LR23b
]
and hyperbolic obstacle-type problems
[
CR23
]
.

Paraphrasing Jean Mawhin in his Foreword of
[
MBRS16
]
,
it is therefore a natural question to see which results “survive” when the gradient
is replaced by the fractional gradient
, which
is also fruitful because the
extension of classical results to new situations also sheds light on a better and deeper
understanding of the classical results
. In this work we are interested in more general types of stationary problems, with or without constraints, in (fractional) divergence form, including the variants of the
(s,p)
-Laplacian type:

\begin{cases}-D^{s}\cdot\big(\alpha(u)|D^{s}u|^{p-2}D^{s}u+\boldsymbol{e}(u)\big)+b(u,D^{s}u)=f_{0}-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.7)

This will be done in the general framework of monotone and pseudomonotone operators involving fractional Riesz gradients, in bounded and in unbounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in classes of convex constrained Dirichlet-type problems. In particular, this allow us to consider the existence of solutions to problems of the type (
1.7
), their extensions with obstacle or s-gradient constraints and to study their dependence with respect to the parameter
s
, including the limit cases
s=1
and
s=0
.

In Section 2, we start with a summary of the natural functional framework of the Riesz fractional gradient
D^{s}
, with the precise definition of the Lions-Calderón spaces
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, with special emphasis on the differences between the properties of the latter spaces when
\Omega
is bounded and when it is not. In particular, we recall the equivalent definitions of the fractional gradient and their important duality property in the Lions-Calderón spaces, the characterisation of their dual spaces
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
, by showing the representation
F=f_{0}-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}
, for all
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the contiguity relation between
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the monotone inclusions of the spaces
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, with respect with the inverse monotone variation of the parameter
s\in[0,1]
, for
1<p<\infty
. We also recall the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities and the continuity of
D^{s}
with respect to
s\in[0,1]
in those spaces. We notice that these results also hold for
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, since it may be considered as a subspace of
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Some properties of these spaces are then also complemented in the case of bounded
\Omega
, like in the classical case, as the fractional Poincaré inequality, emphasizing the dependence on the fractional parameter, and the fractional Rellich-Kondrachov compactness embedding. In particular, we prove the new explicit estimate
\|D^{t}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{t^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
of the embedding
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,

, depending on the fractional parameter

but not on
s
,
0<t<s\leq 1
, and
we show the compact embedding

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{t,q}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
0\leq t<s\leq 1
, satisfying
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{d}<\frac{1}{q}-\frac{t}{d}
. We conclude this functional section with a relevant property on the density of the non negative smooth functions with compact support, in a bounded open set
\Omega
, in the non negative cone of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In the functional framework of the Lions-Calderón spaces, we study in Section
3
, existence of solutions
u=u_{s}\in K^{s}\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to fractional variational inequalities of the type

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(x,u,D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(x,u,D^{s}u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}(v-u)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{f}\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\\
\end{split}

(1.8)

for all

. Here

may be general a non-empty closed convex subset of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for

and

, including the classical unilateral examples in the fractional setting

K^{s}_{\geq\psi}=\{u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):u\geq\psi\mbox{ in }\Omega\}\,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,\,\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g}=\{u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):|D^{s}u|\leq g\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

(1.9)

In this new framework we develop the theory of monotone operators, initiated in
[

,
Min62
]
, and pseudomonotone operators, introduced by H. Brézis in
[

]
and extended by F. Browder in
[

]
. These methods, combined with the functional properties of the fractional Riesz gradients, allow us to obtain a variety of interesting existence results for different types of problems, with

, which corresponds to a new class of nonlinear problems involving an important Calderón-Zygmund operator, namely the Riesz transform. When
\Omega
is bounded and

, we apply the method of
[

for pseudomonotone operators to obtain the novel existence results, while when

is unbounded and

we extend the method
[
Bro77
]
to the fractional framework. When

is unbounded but

, when

is unbounded the type of functions

and

In particular, we study specially fractional

-Laplacian type operators, for which the typical example is the variational

-Laplacian, or the

-Laplacian

for the variational inequality (

The following section in this article, Section

, with

for pseudomonotone operators with

, with

and similarly for monotone operators with

and with

and, in the case

The proofs of these stability results are based in a refinement of the classical monotonicity and compactness methods used to study the existence problems and a generalization of the Mosco convergence
[
Mos69
]
in a fixed Banach space

:

K_{n}\xrightarrow{M}K\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\text{w--}\limsup_{n}{K_{n}}\subset K\subset\liminf_{n}{K_{n}}\mbox{ in }V.

(1.14)

Although this notion of convergence is a powerful tool to study stability problems associated to variational inequalities with respect to the variation of the convex sets, as one can see for example in

[
Mos69
,
Mos76
,
Rod87
,
ARS21
,
Boc21
]
, it cannot be used directly in our framework, since the spaces
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
change with

. The particular case

is simpler, since the convex sets
K^{s}
are non-increasing and
K^{\sigma}\subset K^{s}
, and it has been considered in
[

,

]
for obstacle problems with
\sigma=1
, by using Mosco’s arguments directly in the space
\Lambda^{s^{*},p}_{0}(\Omega)
where
0\leq s^{*}<s<\sigma\leq 1
.

The general case requires a generalization of Mosco’s notion of convergence, which in our situation consists of working with the convergences of the pair
(u,D^{s}u)
in the space
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, instead of working directly with the functions
u
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. This allows the ambient space
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
to be independent of

without losing information about the regularity of

and to introduce, for converging sequences

, the convergence of convex sets in the generalized sense of Mosco

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\supset K^{s}\xrightarrow{s-M}K^{\sigma}\subset\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(1.15)

With this approach, we are able to obtain the new results, with different combinations of weak and strong convergences of the solutions

and their fractional gradients

, given in (
1.11
), (

) and (

\begin{cases}-D\cdot\big(\alpha(u)|Du|^{p-2}Du+\boldsymbol{e}(u)\big)+b(u,Du)=f_{0}-D\cdot\boldsymbol{f}&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.16)

In order to study also the stability, as
s\to\sigma\in[0,1]
, of the solutions to obstacle problems we give examples of
K^{s}_{\geq\psi_{s}}\xrightarrow{s-M}K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi_{\sigma}}

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g_{s}}\xrightarrow{s-M}\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{g_{\sigma}}
, with constraint on the fractional gradient
D^{s}u
instead of

, which we restrict to

and

.

In Subsection

, we provide applications to quasivariational problems, i.e. problems where the convex sets

are allowed to depend on the solution

of the problem, a class of problems considered, for instance, in the classical framework by

,
Mos76
,
JM79
,
BL82
,
BC84
,

]
. Fractional problems of this type were, as far as we know, first studied in Hilbertian framework by H. Antil and C. Rautenberg in

]
using methods that are dependent on a comparison principle. Since it is not known if the fractional operators studied in our work satisfy any type of comparison principles, we use fixed-point arguments together with variational methods, generalizing some results that were obtained in

,

]
to the non-Hilbertian framework. We give two examples with strictly monotone coercive operators, one of obstacle type, where the obstacle is given by

2
The Lions-Calderón Spaces

2.1
The natural spaces for the s-fractional gradient

Introducing the Riesz potentials

I_{r}\varphi(x)=(I_{r}*\varphi)(x)=\frac{\mu_{d,1-r}}{d-r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{d-r}}}\,dy.

(2.1)

for
r=1-s\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with

\mu_{d,s}=\frac{2^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+s+1}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)},

(2.2)

we recall the following definition introduced in
[
SS15
]
:

Definition

(Distributional Riesz fractional partial derivative)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and consider
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then we define
(D^{s}\varphi)_{j}=\frac{\partial^{s}\varphi}{\partial x^{s}_{j}}
by

\left\langle\frac{\partial^{s}\varphi}{\partial x^{s}_{j}},v\right\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(I_{1-s}\varphi)\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{j}}}\,dx,\quad\forall v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.3)

Since
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Riesz potential
I_{1-s}\varphi
is actually a well-defined function belonging to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, and so the distributional Riesz fractional partial derivative (
2.3
) makes sense in the distributional sense.

By definition of distributional Riesz partial derivative these operators are non-local, in the sense that in order to be computed they require information on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and not just information along the direction of canonical vector
e_{j}
. Similarly to the classical derivatives, we can express the distributional Riesz fractional gradient, or simply the
s
-fractional gradient, in terms of its
s
-fractional partial derivatives

D^{s}\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{s}\varphi}{\partial x^{s}_{j}}e_{j}.

(2.4)

When dealing with smooth functions with compact support, we can express the
s
-fractional gradient without the need of using distributions, since it is still a smooth vector function, although not having compact support. In fact we have the following characterization:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

D^{s}\varphi=D(I_{1-s}\varphi)=I_{1-s}(D\varphi).

(2.5)

Proof.

See
[
SS15
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

It is also possible to show that the
s
-fractional gradient
D^{s}
corresponds, up to a sign, to the convolution with the Horváth’s kernel (
1.3
)

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

D^{s}\varphi(x)=\mu_{d,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\int_{\{|y|>\varepsilon\}}{\frac{y\varphi(x+y)}{|y|^{d+s+1}}}\,dy},

(2.6)

Proof.

See
[
Pon16
, Lemma 15.9]
or
[
CS19
, Proposition 2.2]
.
∎

One consequence of the characterizations (

) and (
2.6
) is that we can extend them to the integral cases
s=1
and
s=0
, respectively. Indeed, by observing that, in a certain sense
[

]
, the limit case
I_{0}
corresponds to the identity operator, we can use (

) to extend the notion of
D^{s}
to

, identifying
D^{1}
and the classical gradient
D
. On the other hand, we can use the characterization in (

) to extend
D^{s}
to
s=0
, by identifying
D^{0}
with
-R
, where
R
is the vector-valued Riesz transform.

These
s
-fractional differential operators have some properties that are similar to the ones of the classical gradient. One of the most important, which allow us to make use of variational methods, is the duality property between the
s
-fractional gradient and the
s
-fractional divergence

D^{s}\cdot\Phi(x)=\mathrm{div}_{s}\Phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\frac{\partial^{s}\Phi_{j}}{\partial x^{s}_{j}}}.

(2.7)

Proposition 2.3

(Duality between the
s
-gradient and the
s
-divergence)
.

Let
s\in[0,1)
,
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(x)D^{s}\cdot\Phi(x)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\Phi(x)\cdot D^{s}\varphi(x)}\,dx.

(2.8)

Proof.

For
s\in(0,1)
, see
[
CS19
, Lemma 2.5]
. The limit case
s=0
is shown in
[
BCCS22
, Lemma 26]
.
∎

In fact, due to this duality property, following
[
CS19
]
for
s>0
, we can then extend the notion of fractional differentiability to a wider class of functions, including the case
s=0
.

Definition

(Weak
s
-fractional gradient)
.

Let
0\leq s<1
and consider
1\leq p\leq\infty
if
s>0
or
1<p<\infty
if
s=0
. We define the weak
s
-fractional gradient of a function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the function
\Gamma\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{fD^{s}\cdot\Phi}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\Gamma\cdot\Phi}\,dx\quad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.9)

To simplify the notation, we write
D^{s}f=\Gamma
.

It is important to observe that this definition only makes sense because
D^{s}\cdot=\mathrm{div}_{s}:C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\to{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
continuously, see
[
CS19
, Corollary 2.3]
for the case
s>0
and
[
Gra14a
, Corollary 5.2.8]
for the case
s=0
.

This notion of weak fractional gradient allows a definition of function spaces, similarly to the classical Sobolev space, suitable to study problems involving
s
-fractional gradients.

Definition

(Lions-Calderón spaces)
.

Let
0\leq s<1
and consider
1\leq p\leq\infty
if
s>0
or
1<p<\infty
if
s=0
. We define
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the space of functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with (weak)
s
-fractional gradient also in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,D^{s}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

(2.10)

This space is endowed the norm

\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}+\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(2.11)

We also define the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the norm
{\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}
as the Banach space

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}}}.

(2.12)

This last definition was introduced in
[
SS15
]
, where the authors have show that
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for
1<p<\infty
, coincides with the Bessel potential spaces or generalized Sobolev spaces

H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{((1+4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2})^{-s/2}\hat{f})^{\vee}\,:f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},

(2.13)

where it is also known the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
s\geq 0
and
p>1
, see
[
LM61
]
.

On the other hand, it was proven
that
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is also dense in
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, in
[
BCCS22
]
and in
[
KS22
]
, showing, as in the classical Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(corresponding to
s=1
), the identity of all those three spaces.

The case
s=0
in the Definition
2.10
only includes the cases
1<p<\infty
and we can identify
\Lambda^{0,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. These results can be summarized (and written more precisely) in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.4

(Lions-Calderón spaces and Bessel Potential Spaces)
.

The following identities hold:

i)

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalent norms, when
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
;

ii)

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
0<s<1
and
1\leq p<\infty
; and

iii)

\Lambda^{0,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalent norms when
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

i)

It was proven in
[
SS15
, Theorem 1.7]
.

ii)

It was proven in
[
KS22
, Theorem 2.7]
when
1<p<\infty
and in
[
BCCS22
, Theorem A.1]
when
1\leq p<\infty
.

iii)

On the one hand, since
D^{0}=-R:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
continuously for
p\in(1,\infty)
, see
[
Gra14a
, Corollary 5.2.8]
, we have that
\Lambda^{0,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the other hand, using the identity
-D^{0}\cdot D^{0}=-R\cdot R=I
and the
L^{p}
continuity of the Riesz transform, we get that
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\Lambda^{0,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

∎

An interesting consequence of the previous result is that we can establish a relationship between the Lions-Calderón spaces and the classical Sobolev spaces. In fact, unlike the Besov spaces, the class of Bessel Potential spaces

and

. Consequently, it is natural to extend the definition of
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to

by setting

(2.14)

with

, where

is the classical gradient.

On the other hand, from the point of view of the theory of interpolation, the Lions-Calderón spaces

, when

and

, is the complex interpolated space

(see
[

]
and
[

), while the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces

[

.

Although in general the Lions-Calderón spaces

do not coincide with the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces

(Contiguity between the Lions-Calderón spaces and the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces)

Let

,

Proof.

See
[

, which contains the more general case

in the framework

.
∎

, such that

\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{s}\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\forall u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mbox{ with }1\leq p<\infty.

(2.15)

Proof.

The inclusions among the Lions-Calderón spaces for

Proposition 2.7

(Fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities for

.

Let

Remark 2.1
.

In

.

To conclude this subsection, let us provide some properties of the
s
-fractional gradient in the context of the Lions-Calderón spaces. We start with the continuous dependence of
D^{s}
with respect to the parameter
s
, provided that the function where we are computing these fractional gradients is sufficiently regular.

Proposition 2.8

(Continuity
s\mapsto D^{s}
)
.

Let
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0<s\leq 1
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, for any sequence
t\in(0,s]
(not converging to
0
) we have that
t\mapsto D^{t}f
is continuous in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, if we impose the strict condition
p>1
, then this continuity result can be extended to the case in which the sequence
t
can converge to any value in
[0,s]
.

Proof.

See
[
BCCS22
, Theorem 34]
.
∎

Lastly, we provide a similar characterization to the one in Proposition
2.1
but for functions that satisfy the minimal regularity assumptions, that is, belong to the smallest Lions-Calderón space where the respective fractional gradient is well-defined.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
D^{s}u=D(I_{1-s}u)
, which means that, in these spaces, the notion of weak fractional gradient and the notion of distributional Riesz fractional gradient coincide.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Using the weak definition of fractional gradient as well as the Proposition
2.1
, we get that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi D^{s}u}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD^{s}\varphi}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{u(I_{1-s}D\varphi)}\,dx.

Noticing that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{|D\varphi(y)||u(x)|}{|y-x|^{d+s-1}}}\,dy}\,dx<+\infty,

by Fubini’s theorem we can change the order of the integrals and so

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{u(I_{1-s}D\varphi)}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(I_{1-s}u)D\varphi}\,dx.

Consequently, we conclude that
D^{s}u=D(I_{1-s}u)
, first in the sense of distributions and afterwords in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

By density, this equivalence in the definition of the fractional gradient implies the general duality property this operator
D^{s}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{vD^{s}u}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD^{s}v}\,dx.

for all
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
v\in\Lambda^{s,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0<s<1<p<\infty
and
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
. Similarly, we notice that the duality between
D^{s}
and
D^{s}\cdot=div_{s}
in (
2.8
) still holds for all
\varphi\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and all
\Phi\in\Lambda^{s,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
0<s\leq\sigma\leq 1
,
1<p<\infty
and
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
D^{s}u=I_{\sigma-s}D^{\sigma}u
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, by the semigroup property of Riesz potentials,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi D^{s}u}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD^{s}\varphi}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD(I_{1-s}\varphi)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD(I_{1-\sigma}I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)}\,dx.

Since
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
I_{\sigma-s}\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for every
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\psi D^{\sigma}(I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)D^{\sigma}\psi}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(I_{\sigma-s}D^{\sigma}\psi)}\,dx\\
=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(I_{\sigma-s}(I_{1-\sigma}D\psi))}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(I_{1-s}D\psi)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi D^{s}\psi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\psi D^{s}\varphi}\,dx.

which means that
D^{\sigma}(I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)=D^{s}\varphi\in L^{p}
, and therefore
I_{\sigma-s}\varphi\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Using Proposition
2.9
, we have
D(I_{1-\sigma}I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)=D^{\sigma}(I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)
. Consequently, from the properties of the fractional gradient, we are able to conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi D^{s}u}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD(I_{1-\sigma}I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD^{\sigma}(I_{\sigma-s}\varphi)}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{I_{\sigma-s}\varphi D^{\sigma}u}\,dx.

Finally, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{|D^{\sigma}u(x)||\varphi(y)|}{|y-x|^{d+s-\sigma}}}\,dy}\,dx\leq\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|I_{\sigma-s}|\varphi|\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty,

for
q=\frac{Np}{N(p-1)+(\sigma-s)p}
, we can apply Fubini’s theorem to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi D^{s}u}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{I_{\sigma-s}\varphi D^{\sigma}u}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(I_{\sigma-s}D^{\sigma}u)}\,dx,

concluding the proof.
∎

2.2
A fractional framework for the homogeneous Dirichlet condition

As it was observed in Proposition

,

with compact support in

2.2.1
The space
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with arbitrary
\Omega

Definition
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a any open set, and consider
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. We define the space

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

(2.19)

and we endow this space with the norm of
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We also denote the dual space of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
by
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and denote their duality by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{s}
.

We observe that, by construction, we have

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\{f\in\Lambda_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,f=0\mbox{ on }\Omega^{c}\}.

(2.20)

Due to this inclusion, it makes sense to compute the weak fractional gradient
D^{s}
of any function
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it is easy to show, that this fractional gradient
D^{s}u
coincides with the
L^{p}
-limit of
D^{s}u_{n}
where
u_{n}
are the approximating functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. In addition, we also have that for any
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v\in\Lambda^{s,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{vD^{s}u}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{vD^{s}u_{n}}\,dx}=-\lim_{n\to\infty}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{u_{n}D^{s}v}\,dx}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{uD^{s}v}\,dx.

When
s=0
, for any open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we notice that

\Lambda^{0,p}_{0}(\Omega)=L^{p}_{0}(\Omega)=\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,f=0\mbox{ on }\Omega^{c}\}.

(2.21)

Among the important properties of these spaces, we recall the generalization of the classical Sobolev inequalities to this fractional framework. We define the fractional Sobolev exponent

p^{*}_{s}=\frac{dp}{d-sp}

(2.22)

whenever
sp<d
. Notice that when
s=0
,
p_{0}^{*}=p
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Proposition 2.11

(Sobolev embeddings for
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
1<p<\infty
. Then,

1)

if
sp<d
, we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for
p\leq q\leq p^{*}_{s}
. Moreover, when
q=p^{*}_{s}
we have

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})};

(2.23)

2)

if
sp=d
, then
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
q\in[p,+\infty)
; and

3)

if
sp>d
, then we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset C^{0,s-\frac{d}{p}}(\overline{\Omega})
with the estimate

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{s-\frac{N}{p}}\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.24)

4)

for
t<s
and
1<p\leq q\leq\frac{dp}{d-(s-t)p}
, we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,q}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof we are using the embedding
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda_{0}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1)

The inequality (
2.23
) was proven in
[
SS15
, Theorem 1.8]
, which implies that
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the embedding
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\leq q\leq p^{*}_{s}
follows from Proposition
2.6
, by noticing that

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\Lambda^{s-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{p^{*}_{s-\varepsilon}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with
p\leq p^{*}_{s-\varepsilon}<p^{*}_{s}
when
0<\varepsilon\leq s
. The limit case
s=0
, when
p^{*}_{s}=p
, corresponds to the continuity in
L^{p}
of the Riesz transform
[
Gra14a
, Corollary 5.2.8]
.

2)

This follows by a similar argument to the last part of 1).

3)

It was proved in
[
SS15
, Theorem 1.11]
.

4)

We start by observing that
q=\frac{dp}{d-(s-t)p}
was already proven in
[
Cal61
, Theorem 6]
. To extend the inclusions for
p\leq q\leq\frac{dp}{d-(s-t)p}
, we write
s=t+\delta
for some
\delta>0
, and we consider
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\delta
. In this case,

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t+\delta-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\Lambda^{t,\frac{dp}{d-(\delta-\varepsilon)p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Finally, since for every
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,\frac{dp}{d-(\delta-\varepsilon)p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\Lambda^{t,\frac{dp}{d-(\delta-\varepsilon)p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and consequently
u\in\Lambda^{t,\frac{dp}{d-(\delta-\varepsilon)p}}_{0}(\Omega)
.

∎

If instead of considering just the inclusion operator like in Sobolev embeddings, we also restrict the domain of the functions to one subdomain with finite measure, then the resulting operator is compact. More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.12

(Local Rellich-Kondrachov for
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\Omega
is any open set. If we also consider the open set with finite measure
\omega\subset\Omega
and the compact set
K\subset\Omega
, we have following compact embeddings (denoted by
\Subset
) :

1)

if
sp<d
, then
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset L^{q}(\omega)
for
q<p^{*}_{s}
;

2)

if
sp=d
, then
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset L^{q}(\omega)
for every
q<+\infty)
; and

3)

if
sp>d
, then
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset C^{0,\beta}(K)
for
\beta<s-\frac{d}{p}
.

Proof.

We observe that, since
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we just need to prove this result for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The idea is to use the contiguity between the Lions-Calderón spaces and the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces, and then apply the compactness results that are already known for the latter.

In fact, if
sp\leq d
, we can consider
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
and then due to Proposition
2.5
and
[
dTGCV20
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have that

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset W^{s-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Subset L^{q}(\omega),\quad\mbox{ with }q<p^{*}_{s-\varepsilon}.

(2.25)

Since
\varepsilon
ranges in
(0,s)
, then
p^{*}_{s-\varepsilon}
ranges in
(p,p^{*})
. Then the conclusion follows using the fact that
\omega
has finite measure.

We can use the same arguments for
sp>d
but with
\varepsilon\in(0,s-\frac{d}{p})
and with the inclusions

\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset W^{s-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Subset C^{0,\beta}(K),\quad\mbox{ with }\beta<s-\varepsilon-\frac{d}{p}.

(2.26)

∎

Similarly to the classical Sobolev spaces, the Lions-Calderón spaces are closed for the composition with uniformly Lipschitz functions. In fact, as an immediate consequence of the following theorem, we have, in particular,

f^{+},f^{-}\mbox{ and }|f|\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega),\mbox{ whenever }f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.27)

Theorem 2.1

(Composition with Lipschitz functions)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R})
with
\varphi(0)=0
and
\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\lambda
, then
\varphi\circ f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
f_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be such that
f_{n}\to f
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using Strichartz’s characterization of
H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
[
Str67
, Section 2.3]
or
[
AH96
, Theorem 4.8.1]
, we know that a function
g\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

S_{s}g(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}{|g(x+ry)-g(x)|}\,dy\right)^{2}}\frac{dr}{r^{1+2s}}\right)^{1/2}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Moreover, the norm of
g
in
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equivalent to
\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|S_{s}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

We notice that
\varphi\circ f_{n}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, from the known properties of
L^{p}
functions. We have also
S_{s}(\varphi\circ f_{n})\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since
\|S_{s}(\varphi(f_{n}))\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\lambda\|S_{s}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, by taking the
L^{p}
norm on both sides of the pointwise estimate

S_{s}(\varphi(f_{n}))(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}{|\varphi(f_{n}(x+ry))-\varphi(f_{n}(x))|}\,dy\right)^{2}}\frac{dr}{r^{1+2s}}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq\lambda\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\int_{B_{1}(0)}{|f_{n}(x+ry)-f_{n}(x)|}\,dy\right)^{2}}\frac{dr}{r^{1+2s}}\right)^{1/2}=\lambda S_{s}f_{n}(x).

Therefore
\varphi\circ f_{n}\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since
\mathrm{supp}\{\varphi\circ f_{n}\}\subset\mathrm{supp}\{f_{n}\}\subset\Omega
, then
\varphi\circ f_{n}\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
and using Vitalli’s convergence theorem and the above inequality, we conclude
\varphi\circ f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Although this result was first mentioned, without proof, in
[
SS18
]
for the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it was shown in
[
Cam21
]
for the special cases
\varphi(t)=|t|
and the truncation function
\varphi_{k}(t)=(t\wedge k)\vee(-k)
. Moreover, in
[
Cam21
]
it was also proved that the truncation
T_{k}f=\varphi_{k}\circ f
for a function
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that
T_{k}f\to f
in
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
k\to\infty
.

The definition (
2.19
) and the interpretation (
2.20
) of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as subspaces of
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
yields the following interesting characterization of these spaces:

Theorem 2.2

(Netrusov’s theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, and consider
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, we have
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
if and only if, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a function
\eta
such that
\eta=0
on a neighborhood of
\Omega^{c}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
\|f-\eta f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

See
[
AH96
, Theorem 10.1.1]
.
∎

A useful property for applications to the theory of fractional differential equations is the characterization of
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
using fractional partial derivatives. Consider the operator

\displaystyle\Pi_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to L_{0}^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})=:\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)

(2.28)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\mapsto(v,D^{s}v).

Theorem 2.3

(Characterization of
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set and let
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. If
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then there exist functions
f_{0}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and
f_{1},...,f_{d}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\langle F,g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}g}\,dx+\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{f_{j}\frac{\partial^{s}g}{\partial x_{j}^{s}}}\,dx}\quad\forall g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
\Pi_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as
\Pi_{s}(g)=(g,D^{s}g)
for any
g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Note that

\|\Pi_{s}g\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}=\|g\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

which means that
\Pi_{s}
is an isometry of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto a subspace
W\subset\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Then we define the linear functional
F^{*}
on
W
as
\langle F^{*},\Pi_{s}g\rangle=\langle F,g\rangle
. By the isometry between
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
W
we obtain that
\|F^{*}\|_{W^{\prime}}=\|F\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}
. Consequently, using Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists an extension
\tilde{F}
of
F^{*}
to all
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
such that
\|F^{*}\|_{W^{\prime}}=\|\tilde{F}\|_{(\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega))^{\prime}}
. Then, by the Riesz representation theorem, there exist
f_{0}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and
f_{1},...,f_{n}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

\langle\tilde{F},h\rangle=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}h_{0}}\,dx+\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{f_{j}h_{j}}\,dx},\quad\forall h=(h_{0},h_{1},...,h_{d})\in L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Hence, for any
g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we conclude

\langle F,g\rangle=\langle F^{*},\Pi_{s}g\rangle=\langle\tilde{F},\Pi_{s}g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}g}\,dx+\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{f_{j}\frac{\partial^{s}g}{\partial x_{j}^{s}}}\,dx}.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

Due to this characterization, we may write any element
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
in the form

F=f_{0}-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}

with
f_{0}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},...,f_{d})\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, similarly to the classical Sobolev case

, cf.
[
Ada75
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Remark 2.5
.

It is also possible to write
F=f_{0}-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
with
f_{0}\in L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{f}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This is a simple consequence of the fractional Sobolev inequality (
2.23
), which allows us to use the application
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\ni g\mapsto(g,D^{s}g)\in L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

2.2.2
The space
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\Omega
bounded

So far, we have been providing some properties that hold for any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but now we are going to focus on the case in which
\Omega
is bounded. We start by providing a characterization of these spaces when the boundary of
\Omega
is sufficiently smooth.

Proposition 2.13

(Extension by
0
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and open subset with Lipschitz boundary,
0<s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Then,

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\{f\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\mathrm{supp}f\subset\overline{\Omega}\}.

(2.29)

Proof.

Since this is a property about the elements of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can use the identification
\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and then just apply
[
JK95
, Remark 2.7]
.
∎

There are also some results, similar to those valid for arbitrary
\Omega
that can be improved when
\Omega
is bounded. One such example is the Proposition
2.11
concerning the Sobolev embeddings.

Proposition 2.14

(Sobolev embeddings for
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any bounded open set and consider
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Then,

1)

if
sp<d
, we have that
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
q\in[1,p^{*}_{s}]
with

\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})};

(2.30)

2)

sp=d
implies that
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
q<+\infty)
;

3)

sp>d
implies that
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\beta\leq s-d/p
. In particular,
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for all

;

4)

when

and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{d}\leq\frac{1}{q}-\frac{t}{d}
, we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,q}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The items 1), 2), 3) and 4) are a simple application of the Proposition
2.11
together with some well known properties of the
L^{p}
and
C^{0,\alpha}
spaces.
∎

The inequality (
2.30
) is important as it also implies a fractional Poincaré inequality. In fact, in
[
BCMC21
]
, it was raised the question of how does the constant
C
in this inequality depends on
s
and it was proved that one could take this constant to be inversely proportional to
s
, at least when
q=p
. To be more precise, let us now recall their result (with a small improvement).

Theorem 2.4

(Fractional Poincaré’s inequality)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open subset. Then, there exists a sufficiently large real number
R>1
for which
\Omega\subset B_{R}(0)
, and a positive constant
C_{P}>0
depending only on
p
,
d
,
\Omega
and
R
, such that for all open sets
\Omega_{1}\supset B_{2R}(0)
(
\Omega_{1}
can be unbounded) and for all
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{P}}{s}\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.31)

Proof.

Most of the proof can be found in
[
BCMC21
, Theorem 2.9]
. In fact, when
p\in(1,\infty)
, one only needs to change the last estimate of (13) of the same article, with a
L^{p}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm instead of doing it with the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm.
Similarly, when
p=1
, one follows the same lines of the proof but with the estimates

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\left(\int_{\{|y|<2R\}}{\frac{|D^{s}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-s}}}\,dy\right)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|y|<2R\}}{|D^{s}f(y)|\left(\int_{\Omega}{\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-s}}}\,dx\right)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{s}R\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\left(\int_{\{|y|>2R\}}{\frac{|D^{s}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-s}}}\,dy\right)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|y|>2R\}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}{\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N-s}}\left(\int_{\Omega}{\frac{f(z)}{|y-z|^{N+s}}}\,dz\right)}\,dx\right)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|y|>2R\}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}{\frac{C(N)}{|y|^{N+s}|y|^{N-s}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}\,dx\right)}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(N)R^{-N}|\Omega|\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},

where for the last estimate we used the fact that
|y|/2\leq|y-x|
when
x\in\Omega
and
y\in\Omega_{1}^{c}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

It is interesting to see that the Poincaré constant obtained in the previous theorem blows-up as
s\searrow 0
. On the other hand, from part iii) of Proposition
2.4
, we know that
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|D^{0}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. This raises the question whether it is possible or not to obtain a uniform bound to the best Poincaré constants independent of
s\in(0,1)
. This is known to be possible for
p=2
due to the results of
[
FJW22
]
, where it is shown, in particular, that the best Poincaré constant
c_{2,s}=1/\sqrt{\lambda_{s}}\to 1
as
s\to 0^{+}
, where
\lambda_{s}=\inf\left\{\frac{\|D^{s}w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}:\,w\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)\right\}>0
is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem for
(-\Delta)^{s}=-D^{s}\cdot D^{s}
.

This result has several implications. The first one is that the
L^{p}
-norm of the
s
-fractional gradient is equivalent to the usual norm of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. In fact, we have:

Corollary 2.1

(An equivalent norm for
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
)
.

Let
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded subset. Then there exists a constant
C=C(s,p,d,\Omega)
such that

\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\mbox{ for all }f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Remark 2.7
.

Notice that it also possible to prove that
\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is equivalent to
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. The only extra step that one needs to take care of is the one of finding an estimate for
\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1}^{c};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
in terms of
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, which can be done using some arguments that are present in the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.9]
. This means one can consider the norm of a function in

just knowing the

of that function on a certain bounded domain

, which can be of interest for numerical methods.

Moreover we may compare the norms of the embedding
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, with
1<p<\infty
fixed, with an explicit dependence with respect to the fractional parameter
t<s
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
0<t<s\leq 1
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and open set. Then,
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with the inequality

\|D^{t}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{t^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.32)

for some
C=C(\Omega,d,p)>0
.

Proof.

This is a refinement of the proof of
[
BCMC21
, Proposition 4.1]
. In fact, if we proceed as in the proof of
[
BCMC21
, Proposition 4.1]
, and making use of the precise constants, in particular not estimating in
[
BCMC21
, eq. (22)]

\frac{\mu_{d,1-s+t}}{d-s+t}=\frac{1}{\gamma(t-s)}\leq C(d)
, we get

\|D^{t}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left(\frac{3\sigma_{d-1}R}{(s-t)}\frac{\mu_{d,1-s+t}}{d-s+t}+\mu_{d,t}\frac{C_{P}}{s}\left(\frac{2^{d+s}\sigma_{d-1}|\Omega|}{d+t}+|\Omega|^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{t}\right)^{1/p}\right)\right)\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.33)

where
C_{P}=C_{P}(\Omega,d,p)>0
is the fractional Poincaré’s constant,
\sigma_{d-1}
is the measure of the
d-1
dimensional sphere
\partial B(0,1)
and
R>1
is sufficiently large number such that
\Omega\subset B(0,R)
.
Now, we estimate in such a way that we can remove the dependence of the constant with respect to
t
. Indeed, since
0<1-s+t<1
and
t\leq d-1+t
then
[
BCMC21
, Lemma 2.4]
yields

\frac{1}{(t-s)}\frac{\mu_{d,1-s+t}}{d-s+t}\leq\frac{1}{d-1+t}\frac{\mu_{d,1-s+t}}{1-(1-s+t)}\leq\frac{1}{s}\sup_{\tau\in[-1,1)}{\frac{\mu_{d,\tau}}{1-\tau}}<\infty.

(2.34)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|D^{t}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3\sigma_{d-1}R}{t}\sup_{\tau\in[-1,1)}{\frac{\mu_{d,\tau}}{1-\tau}}+\mu_{d,t}\frac{C_{P}}{t}\left(\frac{2^{d+s}\sigma_{d-1}|\Omega|}{d+t}+|\Omega|^{1/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{d-1}}{t}\right)^{1/p}\right)\right)\|D^{t}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(2.35)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C_{1}}{t}+\frac{C_{2}}{t}+\frac{C_{3}}{t^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\right)\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C_{4}}{t^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

for some constants
C_{1}>0
depending on
\Omega
and
d
, and constants
C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
, depending only on
\Omega
,
d
and
p
.
∎

Corollary 2.3

(Characterization of
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, when
\Omega
is bounded)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. If
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then there exist functions
f_{1},...,f_{d}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\langle F,g\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{f_{j}\frac{\partial^{s}g}{\partial x_{j}^{s}}}\,dx}\quad\forall g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

By using the equivalent norm
f\mapsto\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, the result follows by applying the same arguments as those used in the proof of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

As in the general case, when

is bounded, any

may also be given as in Proposition
2.3

in the form

with

and

, where, by the fractional Sobolev embeddings,

if

,

if

and

if

.

Our improved version of the fractional Poincaré inequality, Theorem
2.4
, also allows us to prove that, for fixed

, there is an increasing order of inclusions of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with respect to the decreasing of
p
. This generalizes the already known results for

and

, for bounded

, and the characterisation of

Corollary 2.4

(Order with respect to

)
.

Let

be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

. Then
\Lambda^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{1}\supset\Omega
be an open bounded subset of

as in the statement of the theorem of the Poincaré inequality, Theorem
2.4
. Then, the proof of this corollary is just a simple consequence of the fact that

is an equivalent norm to

and that

.
∎

Corollary 2.5

.

See

.
∎

The last direct application that we make of the fractional Poincaré inequality that we present is the following compactness result, which was first proven in
[
BCMC21
]
in the case
\sigma=1
. This result is going to play a very important role in the theory of stability of solutions in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Consider a convergent sequence in
(0,1]
such that
s_{n}\to\sigma\in(0,1]
, and a sequence of functions
u_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\sup_{n}{\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}<\infty
. Then, there exists
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that for every
t\in(0,\sigma)
one can extract a subsequence also denoted by
u_{n}
satisfying

u_{n}\to u\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\rightharpoonup D^{\sigma}u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

The arguments in
[
BCMC21
, Theorem 4.2]
may be very generalized to prove that exists a function
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and a subsequence
\{u_{s}\}_{n_{j}}\subset\Lambda^{t+\varepsilon,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{s}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, where
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small to have
t+2\varepsilon<\min\{s,\sigma\}
. Now, one only needs to prove that in fact
u_{s}\to u
in
\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Using Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality, Lemma
2.7
, and the order between Lions-Calderón spaces with
p
fixed, item 4) of Proposition
2.14
, together with the fact that
t+2\varepsilon<\min{s,\sigma}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{t}(u_{s}-u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{d,p}\|u_{s}-u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{t+\varepsilon}}\|D^{t+\varepsilon}(u_{s}-u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{t}{t+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{d,p,t,\varepsilon}\|u_{s}-u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{t+\varepsilon}}\left(\|D^{s}u_{s}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{\frac{t}{t+\varepsilon}}.

Using the hypothesis that
\|D^{s}u_{s}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is uniformly bounded, and that
u_{s}\to u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set, and let
0\leq t<s\leq 1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then,

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

This is a simple consequence of Theorem
2.5
when
s_{n}\equiv s
, since by taking a sequence
u_{n}
bounded in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
there is a
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and a subsequence
u_{n}
such that
D^{t}u_{n}\to D^{t}u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

Theorem 2.6

(Fractional Rellich-Kondrachov)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set. Then, for every
0\leq t<s\leq 1
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
satisfying

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{d}<\frac{1}{q}-\frac{t}{d}

we have the compact embedding

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{t,q}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

From the Corollary
2.6
we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{s-\varepsilon,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,s)
. However, at the same time, due to the item 5) of Proposition
2.14
, we have that for
t<s-\varepsilon
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s-\varepsilon}{d}\leq\frac{1}{q}-\frac{t}{d}
we have
\Lambda^{s-\varepsilon,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{t,q}_{0}(\Omega)
. By choosing
\varepsilon
arbitrarily small, and combining the two embeddings, we conclude the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with an interesting result that we can only prove in the case where
\Omega
is bounded, although it is probably true in general domains.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded set,
s\in(0,1)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\Omega)
with
f\geq 0
. Then, there exists a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with
f_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

Proof.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Theorem
2.2
, there exists a function
\eta
such that
\eta=0
on a neighborhood of
\Omega
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
\|f-\eta f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon
. Let us now consider an approximation of the identity
\{\varphi_{n}\}\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
,
\varphi_{n}\geq 0
and compactly supported in
B_{1/n}
. Now, observe that

\|f-\varphi_{n}*(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f-\eta f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\eta f-\varphi_{n}*(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon+\|\eta f-\varphi_{n}*(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

As noted in
[
CS19
, Theorem 3.22]
,
D^{s}(\varphi_{n}*(\eta f))=\varphi_{n}*D^{s}(\eta f)
, and so, we also have that

\displaystyle\|D^{s}(f-\varphi_{n}*(\eta f))\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\|D^{s}(f-\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}(\eta f)-\varphi_{n}*D^{s}(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\|D^{s}(\eta f)-\varphi_{n}*D^{s}(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Now we may choose the index
n
sufficiently big in
\varphi_{n}
, such that

\|\eta f-\varphi_{n}*(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}\eta f-\varphi_{n}*D^{s}(\eta f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon

and conclude the proof by setting
f_{n}=\varphi_{n}*(\eta f)
and observing that

\mathrm{supp}(\varphi_{n}*(\eta f))\subset\mathrm{supp}\varphi_{n}+\mathrm{supp}(\eta f)\subset B_{1/n}+\mathrm{supp}(\eta f)\subset\mathrm{supp}f.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

This argument only works when
\Omega
is bounded because the boundary of any neighborhood of
\Omega^{c}
has to be at a positive distance of the boundary of
\Omega^{c}
. However, this seems to be a technical restriction, since in the classical case
s=1
, this result is also valid for
\Omega
unbounded (see
[
AH96
, Corollary 9.1.5]
).

Remark 2.10
.

Some of the results, that we have included in this section, are well known to have a counterpart on the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. For example, the fractional Sobolev and the Rellich-Kondrachov embeddings can be found for example in
[
Ada75
,
DNPV12
,
Tri83
]
, and the fractional Poincaré inequality can be found
[
BPS16
, Proposition 2.5]
.

3
Existence results

In this section, we study the existence of solutions
u=u_{s}\in K
to the variational inequalities of the form

\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u),v-u\rangle\geq\langle F,v-u\rangle,\quad\forall v\in K,

(3.1)

where
K
is a non-empty closed convex subset of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
being an open set, bounded or unbounded, and

\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u),v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(x,u,D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(x,u,D^{s}u)v}\,dx,\quad\forall v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.2)

Notice that the operator
\mathscr{A}_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\mapsto\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, defined by

\mathscr{A}_{s}(v)=-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(x,v,D^{s}v)+b(x,v,D^{s}v),

(3.3)

is of fractional divergence form if
0<s<1
, classical if
s=1
and of novel type if
s=0
. As in the classical case, we assume that
\boldsymbol{a}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are Carathéodory functions, that is,
\boldsymbol{a}(\cdot,r,\xi)
and
b(\cdot,r,\xi)
are measurable for fixed
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\boldsymbol{a}(x,\cdot,\cdot)
and
b(x,\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous in the variables
(r,\xi)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, satisfying suitable growth and coercivity conditions.

From Remark
2.4
, setting
F=f_{0}-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}
and replacing the nonlinear functions
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
by
\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{f}
and
b-f_{0}
, respectively, one can rewrite the inequality (
3.1
) in the integral form

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(u,D^{s}u))\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u,D^{s}u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K,

(3.4)

where we have omitted the dependence of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
on
x
to simplify the notation. On the other hand, the condition
K\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
means that we have a homogeneous Dirichlet condition
u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
. If instead, we have
u=g
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
with
g\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can set
\overline{u}=u-g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and consider the variational inequality (
3.1
) for
\overline{u}\in\overline{K}
and
\overline{\mathscr{A}}_{s}
, with
\overline{K}=K-g
and
\overline{\mathscr{A}}_{s}(\overline{u})=\mathscr{A}_{s}(u-g)
.

With this general structure we can apply classical abstract methods in the framework of Lions-Calderón spaces, in particular the general theory of pseudo-monotone operators introduced by Brézis in
[
Bre68
]
(see also the classic book
[
Lio69
]
or the more modern references
[
Rou13
,
Sho13
]
), to show the existence of solutions to general nonlinear operators. Similarly, we can also study the more restrictive class of monotone operators, which in certain cases, with additional assumptions, allow us to obtain stronger results, including the uniqueness of solutions in the strictly monotone case and to handle the novel case of
D^{0}
.

3.1
Pseudomonotone operators with
\Omega
bounded and
0<s\leq 1

In this subsection we assume
\Omega
open and bounded. For simplicity we consider first the case where
b
does not depend on
\xi
and later, in Remarks
3.1
and
3.2
, we discuss the general case. We assume the growth conditions

|\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)|\leq\gamma_{1}(x)+C^{\prime}_{1}|r|^{\frac{q_{1}}{p^{\prime}}}+C_{1}|\xi|^{p-1},

(3.5)

|b(x,r)|\leq\gamma_{2}(x)+C_{2}|r|^{q_{2}-1},

(3.6)

and the coercivity condition

\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)\cdot\xi+b(x,r)r\geq\alpha|\xi|^{p}-\beta|r|^{q_{3}}-k(x)

(3.7)

for every
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with exponents

1<q_{1}<\min\{p_{s}^{*},p^{2}/(p-1)\},\quad 1<q_{2}<\min\{p_{s}^{*},p+1\},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 1<q_{3}<p,

(3.8)

constants
C_{1},C^{\prime}_{1},C_{2},\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and functions
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
k\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In this case we assume that
\boldsymbol{a}
is only monotone with respect to the last variable, i.e.,

(\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)-\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\eta))\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq 0,

(3.9)

for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Pseudomonotonicity)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and open set,
s\in(0,1]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
satisfy the growth conditions (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), with
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
as in (
3.8
), and the monotonicity condition (
3.9
) hold. Then, the operator
\mathscr{A}_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{-s,p}(\Omega)
, as defined in (
3.3
), is pseudomonotone, i.e., it is bounded and

\left.\begin{aligned} u_{k}\rightharpoonup u\\
\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle}\leq 0.\end{aligned}\right\}\Rightarrow\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u),u-v\rangle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u_{k}),u_{k}-v\rangle},\quad\forall v\in V.

(3.10)

Proof.

We can apply a similar argument to
[
Rou13
, Lemma 2.31]
and
[
Rou13
, Lemma 2.32]
, for the boundedness and for the implication (
3.10
), respectively, by replacing
D
by
D^{s}
. In fact, as observed in the previous section, the framework of the Lions-Calderón spaces has also a fractional version of the Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities as well as of the Rellich-Kondrachov’s compactness embeddings.
∎

Theorem 3.1

(Existence solutions of fractional variational inequalities)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,
K
is a non-empty closed convex subset of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
s\in(0,1]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and let the coercivity condition (
3.7
), with
q_{3}
as in (
3.8
), hold in addition to the assumptions present in Lemma
3.1
. Then, there exists a function
u\in K
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(u,D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K.

(3.11)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
we know that
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is pseudo-monotone and, by the well-known existence theory, see
[
Lio69
, Thm. 8.2, page 247]
, it is sufficient to show that the coercivity assumption (
3.7
) implies, for some
v_{0}\in K
,

\lim_{\|v\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\to+\infty}{\frac{\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v-v_{0}\rangle}{\|v\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}}}=\infty.

(3.12)

In fact, (
3.12
) is an immediate consequence of the following estimate

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v-v_{0}\right\rangle=\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v\rangle+\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v_{0}\rangle

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|D^{s}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}-C\|D^{s}v_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\|D^{s}v\|^{\frac{q_{1}}{p^{\prime}}}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}v\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}v\|^{q_{2}-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)

\displaystyle-C\|D^{s}v\|^{q_{3}}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\|k\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\|\gamma_{1}\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}v_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\|\gamma_{2}\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

which is easily obtained using (
3.7
), (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and the fractional Sobolev inequalities together with the inclusions between Lebesgue spaces due to
\Omega
being bounded.

In fact, using (
3.7
), (
3.5
), (
3.6
), the coercivity (
3.12
) follows by recalling that from (
3.8
), we have
q_{1}/p^{{}^{\prime}}<p
,
q_{2}-1<p
and
q_{3}<p
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In the case in which
0\in K
, the existence result of Theorem
3.1
can be improved for more general functions
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
. By choosing
v_{0}=0
, the estimate (
3.13
) can be simplified to

\left\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v-v_{0}\right\rangle=\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v\rangle\geq\alpha\|D^{s}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}-C\|D^{s}v\|^{q_{3}}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\|k\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

which does not depend on
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, and so, these exponents can be extended to

1<q_{1},q_{2}<p^{*}_{s}=\begin{cases}\frac{dp}{d-sp}&\mbox{ if }sp<d\\
\forall q<\infty&\mbox{ if }sp\geq d.\end{cases}

Remark 3.2
.

When
0\in K
we can also consider
b
to be dependent on
\xi
, see for instance
[
Rou13
, Section 2.4.3]
. One particular example is when
b
is an affine transformation of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.

b(x,r,\xi)=b_{0}(x,r)+\mathbf{b}(x,r)\cdot\xi,

satisfying, in conjugation with
\boldsymbol{a}
, the coercivity condition

\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)\cdot\xi+b(x,r,\xi)r\geq\alpha|\xi|^{p}-\beta|r|^{q_{3}}-k(x)

(3.14)

for
1<q_{3}<p
, with
b_{0}
subjected to (
3.6
) and
\mathbf{b}
having the growth condition

|\mathbf{b}(x,r)|\leq\gamma(x)+C|r|^{p^{*}_{s}/(q-\varepsilon)}.

with
\gamma\in L^{q+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\varepsilon>0
, and

q=\begin{cases}\frac{dp}{dp-2d+sp}&\mbox{ if }sp<d\\
\forall r<\infty&\mbox{ if }sp\geq d.\end{cases}

However, to obtain an existence result for (
3.4
) with a more general function
b(x,r,\xi)
we not only need to impose a growth condition of the type

|b(x,r,\xi)|\leq\gamma(x)+C\left(|r|^{p^{*}_{s}-\varepsilon-1}+|\xi|^{(p-\varepsilon)/{p^{*}_{s}}^{\prime}}\right)

with
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma\in L^{{p^{*}_{s}}^{\prime}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, but we also need to require further restrictions on the main part of
\boldsymbol{a}
. In fact, we need the strict monotonicity on
\boldsymbol{a}
with respect to the last variable, also called Leray-Lions condition, i.e.,

(\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)-\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\eta))\cdot(\xi-\eta)>0\qquad\mbox{ whenever }\xi\neq\eta,

with the coercivity conditions

\forall\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\lim_{|s|\to\infty}{\frac{\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)\cdot(\xi-\eta)}{|\xi|}}=+\infty\quad\mbox{uniformly for }r\mbox{ bounded}

and (
3.14
). Under these hypothesis we can use similar arguments as in
[
Rou13
, Section 2.4.3]
to get existence results, which is also a special case of the next subsection.

Remark 3.3
.

In the particular case when the operator
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is of potential type and derives from a Gâteaux differentiable functional
\mathscr{F}_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathscr{F}_{s}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(x,u,D^{s}u)dx,

for some differentiable F, their coefficients are given by
\boldsymbol{a}(x,u,D^{s}u)=F_{\xi}(x,u,D^{s}u)
and
b(x,u,D^{s}u)=F_{r}(x,u,D^{s}u)
. General integral functionals of this gradient type have been considered in
[
SS18
]
and extensions to functionals of vector valued functions
u
in
[
BCMC21
]
and in
[
KS22
]
.

3.2
Pseudomonotone operators with
\Omega
arbitrary and
0<s\leq 1

When we allow
\Omega
to be an arbitrary open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, possibly unbounded, we cannot apply the same arguments as in the previous subsection since we do not have Poincaré inequalities nor Rellich-Kondrachov compactness embeddings.

To overcome the lack of Poincaré inequalities, we impose a stronger coercivity condition on the pair of functions
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b

\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)\cdot\xi+b(x,r,\xi)r\geq\alpha|\xi|^{p}+\beta|r|^{p}-k(x),

(3.15)

for every
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
are constants,
k\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
s\in(0,1]
. Moreover, we also impose the classical growth conditions

|\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)|\leq\gamma_{1}(x)+C^{\prime}_{1}|r|^{p-1}+C_{1}|\xi|^{p-1},

(3.16)

and

|b(x,r,\xi)|\leq\gamma_{2}(x)+C^{\prime}_{2}|r|^{p-1}+C_{2}|\xi|^{p-1},

(3.17)

with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

When
\Omega
is unbounded, the faillure of the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness embeddings can be handled by using a different argument due to Browder,
[
Bro77
]
for the classical case
s=1
, by assuming also the Leray-Lions condition of strict monotonicity with respect to the last variable on
\boldsymbol{a}
, i.e., for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\xi)-\boldsymbol{a}(x,r,\eta))\cdot(\xi-\eta)>0\qquad\mbox{ whenever }\xi\neq\eta.

(3.18)

As in the previous subsection, we start by proving that the operator
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is pseudomonotone.

Lemma 3.2

(Pseudomonotonicity when
\Omega
is arbitrary)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
s\in(0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\boldsymbol{a}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy the growth conditions (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), the strong coercivity condition (
3.15
) and assume also that
\boldsymbol{a}
is strictly monotone with respect to the last variable as in (
3.18
). Then, the operator
\mathscr{A}_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{-s,p}(\Omega)
, as defined in (
3.3
), is pseudomonotone.

Proof.

First, we notice that assumptions (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) imply that
\mathscr{A}_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{-s,p}(\Omega)
is bounded.
In order to prove (
3.10
) let
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle}\leq 0
. We may adapt the proof of
[
Bro77
, Theorem 1]
:

i)

applying local Rellich-Kondrachov compactness result, Proposition
2.12
, to an increasing sequence of bounded open sets exhausting
\Omega
, we can extract a subsequence
\{u_{k}\}
that converges to
u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

ii)

for the almost everywhere convergence of a subsequence
\{D^{s}u_{k}\}
to
D^{s}u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider the function for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

p_{k}(x)=(\boldsymbol{a}(x,u_{k},D^{s}u_{k})-\boldsymbol{a}(x,u,D^{s}u))\cdot(D^{s}u_{k}-D^{s}u)+(b(x,u_{k})-b(x,u))(u_{k}-u).

In fact, it is possible to show that
p^{-}_{j}\to 0
strongly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
due to Vitali’s convergence theorem, which afterwords also yields that
p_{k}^{+}\to 0
strongly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and consequently
p_{k}\to 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using strictly monotonicity of
\boldsymbol{a}
with respect to the last variable, we are able to conclude that in fact
D^{s}u_{k}\to D^{s}u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

iii)

Once we have proven that both
\{u_{k}\}
and
\{D^{s}u_{k}\}
converge a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using Lebesgue theorem we obtain

\langle\mathscr{A}(u_{k}),v\rangle\rightharpoonup\langle\mathscr{A}(u),v\rangle\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\,\mbox{ for all }v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle}=0,

which yields

\liminf_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}(u_{k}),u_{k}-v\rangle}\geq\lim_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}(u_{k}),u_{k}-u\rangle}+\lim_{k\to\infty}{\langle\mathscr{A}(u_{k}),u-v\rangle}=\langle\mathscr{A}(u),u-v\rangle,

proving (
3.10
).

∎

Theorem 3.2

(Existence of solutions for fractional variational inequalities with
\Omega
arbitrary)
.

Under the same assumptions of Lemma
3.2
, there exists a function
u\in K
that solves (
3.4
).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
we know that
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is pseudomonotone and we may also apply
[
Lio69
, Thm. 8.2, page 247]
as in Theorem
3.1
. It is sufficient to conclude the coercivity condition (
3.12
) by noticing that, for any fixed

, the Sobolev’s embeddings and Young’s inequalities allow us to get

\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(v),v-v_{0}\rangle\geq\alpha^{\prime}\|D^{s}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\beta^{\prime}\|v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-C(k,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},v_{0}).

(3.19)

with
\alpha^{\prime}>0
and

depending on
\alpha
and

respectively.
∎

3.3
Monotone operators with
0\leq s\leq 1

If we restrict
\boldsymbol{a}
to be dependent only on
x
and
\xi
, but not on
r
, and
b
not dependent on
\xi
, such that
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is a monotone operator, then we have an existence result with
\Omega
arbitrary for each
s\in[0,1]
. In particular, we can also consider the case
s=0
with
D^{s}
corresponding to minus the vectorial Riesz transform.

Theorem 3.3

(Existence of solutions for monotone operators)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
K\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a non-empty closed convex set and assume that
\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{a}(x,\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b=b(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are monotone with respect to
\xi
and
r
, respectively, satisfy the growth conditions (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) and the strong coercivity (
3.15
). Then, there exists a function
u\in K
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K.

(3.20)

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of well-known results (see for example,
[
Sho13
, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2]
) since the assumptions imply that
\mathscr{A}_{s}
is a bounded, coercive, monotone, and hemicontinuous operator in the Banach space
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. In particular, the hemicontinuity property is also satisfied for all
0\leq s\leq 1
, i.e. the real-valued function
t\mapsto\langle\mathscr{A}_{s}(u+tv),v\rangle

is continuous for all
u,v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, since the growth conditions (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) imply the continuity of
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by the continuity of the operator
D^{s}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0\leq s\leq 1
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In the limit case of
s=0
, since
\Lambda^{0,p}_{0}(\Omega)=L^{p}_{0}(\Omega)
, it is enough to have only one of the constants
\alpha
or
\beta
positive in the coercivity assumption (
3.15
). For
0<s\leq 1
when

\Omega
is bounded, using Poincaré inequality, we can relax the coercivity assumption (
3.15
) by letting
\beta>-\alpha(s/C_{P})^{p}
, with
C_{P}>0
given in the Poincaré inequality (
2.31
), and keeping
\alpha>0
.

As in the classical case, the uniqueness of solutions can be shown under stricter hypothesis of strict monotony on
\boldsymbol{a}
or
b
.

Proposition 3.1

(Uniqueness through strict monotonicity of
\boldsymbol{a}
or
b
)
.

If we assume that
\boldsymbol{a}
or
b
is strictly monotone with respect to the last variable, then there is at most one solution of (
3.20
) in
K\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

If
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
solve the variational inequality (
3.20
), then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{(\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{1})-\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{2}))\cdot(D^{s}u_{1}-D^{s}u_{2})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{(b(u_{1})-b(u_{2}))(u_{1}-u_{2})}\,dx\leq 0.

(3.21)

Since
(\boldsymbol{a}(x,\xi)-\boldsymbol{a}(x,\eta))\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq 0
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
(b(x,r)-b(x,t))(r-t)>0
for all
r,t\in\mathbb{R}
, we conclude that
u_{1}=u_{2}
a.e. in
\Omega
. A similar argument can be applied when
\boldsymbol{a}
is the one that is strictly monotone, since then it would follow
D^{s}u_{1}=D^{s}u_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, implying also
u_{1}=u_{2}
.
∎

3.3.1
The special case of
p
-Laplacian type operators

In this subsection we shall consider the special class of operators

\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u=-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(x,D^{s}u)

(3.22)

of
p
-Laplacian type, i.e., when
\boldsymbol{a}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Carathéodory function with
\boldsymbol{a}(x,0)=0
, satisfying the growth condition (
3.16
), and there exists a constant
\alpha_{p}>0
such that

(\boldsymbol{a}(x,\xi)-\boldsymbol{a}(x,\eta))\cdot(\xi-\eta)\geq\begin{cases}\alpha_{p}|\xi-\eta|^{p},&\mbox{ if }p\geq 2\\
\alpha_{p}\frac{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}{(|\xi|+|\eta|)^{2-p}},&\mbox{ if }1<p<2,\end{cases}

(3.23)

for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

This class contains the fractional
(s,p)
-Laplacian

-\Delta_{p}^{s}u=-D^{s}\cdot(|D^{s}u|^{p-2}D^{s}u),

(3.24)

which corresponds to choose
\boldsymbol{a}(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
, where the best constant
\alpha_{p}=2^{2-p}
for
p\geq 2
and
\alpha_{p}=p-1
for
1<p<2
, see
[
EE23
, Lemma 1.11]
whose proof for the scalar case extends easily to the vectorial case
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When
s=1
, it coincides with the usual classical
p
-Laplacian, i.e.,
-\Delta^{1}_{p}=-\Delta_{p}
, while when
s=0
is a new class of a nonlinear operator involving the vectorial Riesz transform.

Remark 3.7
.

As it was observed in
[
SSS18
]
, the fractional
(s,p)
-Laplacian (
3.24
) with the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian

\left(-\Delta\right)^{s}_{p}u(x)=\kappa_{s,p,d}\,\,\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}}\,dy,

with
\kappa_{s,p,d}
being the normalized constant computed in
[
dTGCV21
]
. This is a consequence of the fact that the fractional
(s,p)
-Laplacian is the Euler-Lagrange in the Lions-Calderón spaces
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, while the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian arises from the Euler-Lagrange equation associated to the Gagliardo seminorm of the Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, which are algebraically and topologically different when
p\neq 2
,
0<s<1
. In the Hilbertian case
s=2
for the Dirichlet boundary condition, as
W^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega)=\Lambda^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega)=H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
, the corresponding variational problems for the fractional Laplacian are related, as observed in
[
LR23a
]
, and have had a great increase of interest, as can be seen, for instance, in

[
BV16
,
MBRS16
]
.

As a simple application of Theorem
3.3
and Proposition
3.1
, we have the following simple existence and uniqueness result, which is stated here for completeness.

Corollary 3.1

(Existence and uniqueness for
p
-Laplacian type of operators)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open and bounded set,
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Consider a closed, convex and non-empty set
K\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, a
p
-Laplacian type operator
\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}
and any functional
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in K
to the variational inequality

\langle\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u,v-u\rangle\geq\langle F,v-u\rangle,\quad\forall v\in K.

(3.25)

This last result shows that for any functional
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, if
u\in K
denotes the corresponding solution of (
3.25
), the solution operator
\mathscr{A}_{K}^{-1}F=u\in K
is well defined. Notice that when
K=\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, the operator
\mathscr{A}^{-1}_{K}
is the inverse of the fractional
p
-Laplacian type operator
\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}
with Dirichlet homogeneous boundary conditions. This solution operator has interesting properties.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Consider a
p
-Laplacian type operator
\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}
, a closed and convex set
K\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, with
0\in K
, and the variational inequality (
3.25
). Then, setting
u_{1}=\mathscr{A}_{K}^{-1}(F_{1})
and
u_{2}=\mathscr{A}_{K}^{-1}(F_{2})
, the solution map
\mathscr{A}_{K}^{-1}:\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\to K
is

•

\frac{1}{p-1}
-Hölder continuous when
p\geq 2
, with

\|D^{s}u_{1}-D^{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\alpha_{p}^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)};

(3.26)

•

locally Lipschitz continuous when
1<p<2
, with

\|D^{s}u_{1}-D^{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p}(F_{1},F_{2})\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)},

(3.27)

where
C_{p}(F_{1},F_{2})=2^{\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{p}}\alpha_{p}^{\frac{3-p}{1-p}}\left(\|F_{1}\|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\|F_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}
.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.1
, we have that
\mathscr{A}_{K}^{-1}:\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\to K
is well defined.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and the inequality (
3.25
) to the previous identities, testing them with
v=u_{2}
and
v=u_{1}
respectively, we get

\langle\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u_{1}-\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle=\langle F_{1}-F_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle\leq\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.28)

On the other hand, by definition of a p-Laplacian type operator, we also have

\langle\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u_{1}-\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\rangle\geq\begin{cases}\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})},&\mbox{ if }p\geq 2\\
\alpha_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})|^{2}}{(|D^{s}u_{1}|+|D^{s}u_{2}|)^{2-p}}},&\mbox{ if }1<p<2.\end{cases}

(3.29)

Combining the estimates (
3.28
) and (
3.29
), we can easily see that for
p\geq 2
, we get

\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

In the case
1<p<2
, we can use Hölder’s inequality together with (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) to get

\frac{\|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\left(\|D^{s}u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{2-p}}\leq 2^{\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{p}}\alpha_{p}^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

(3.30)

But since
\boldsymbol{a}(x,0)=0
and

\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}u_{j}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{\langle\mathscr{A}^{s}_{p}u_{j},u_{j}\rangle}{\|D^{s}u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\leq\|F_{j}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

for

, we can improve the estimate (
3.30
) to

\|D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2^{\frac{(p-1)(2-p)}{p}}\alpha_{p}^{\frac{3-p}{1-p}}\|F_{1}-F_{2}\|_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\left(\|F_{1}\|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+\|F_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{1-p}}_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\right)^{2-p}.

∎

As a consequence of the Theorem

4
Mosco convergence and continuous dependence

In this section, we consider the convergence of solutions of variational inequalities in the framework of Section
3
with respect to the convergence of the convex sets, specially with respect to the variation of the fractional parameter
s\in[0,1]
. We start by recalling the Mosco’s convergence for convex sets
K_{n}\xrightarrow{M}K
. We say that
K_{n}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges in the sense of Mosco to
K
in a Banach space
X
if: (i) for every
u\in K
, there exists a sequence
u_{n}\in K_{n}
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
X
, i.e.
K\subset\liminf_{n}{K_{n}}
; and (ii) if there exists a sequence
u_{n}\in K_{n}
and a function
u\in X
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
X
, then
u\in K
, i.e.
\text{w--}\limsup_{n}{K_{n}}\subset K
.

However, in this section we are considering convergences of
u_{n}\in K_{n}\subset\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
solutions of the variational inequalities

\langle-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(u_{n},D^{s_{n}}u_{n})+b(u_{n},D^{s_{n}}u_{n})-F_{n},v-u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in K_{n}\subset\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega).

(4.1)

Here not only the convex sets
K_{n}
depend on the parameter
s_{n}
but also the space
\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
and the operator, including the
F_{n}
, depend on
s_{n}
. These terms, may be represented in the form

\langle F_{n},v\rangle_{s_{n}}=\langle{f_{0}}_{n}-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}_{n},v\rangle_{s_{n}}=\int_{\Omega}{{f_{0}}_{n}v}\,dx+\sum_{j=1}^{d}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{{f_{j}}_{n}\frac{\partial^{s_{n}}v}{\partial x_{j}^{s_{n}}}}\,dx}\quad\forall v\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)

(4.2)

with
{f_{0}}_{n}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{f}_{n}=({f_{1}}_{n},...,{f_{d}}_{n})\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Since the space of the solutions depends on
n
and we need to consider not only the convergence of the solutions but also of their fractional gradients, we are lead to the following adaptation of the notion of Mosco’s convergence:

Definition
.

Let us consider a sequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset[0,1]
that converges to
\sigma\in[0,1]
, convex sets
K_{n}\subset\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
K\subset\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, and define the linear map

\displaystyle\Pi_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)=L_{0}^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

(4.3)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto(u,D^{s}u).

We say that
K_{n}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K
, denoted
K_{n}\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}K
, when:

M1)

for every
u\in K
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
with
u_{n}\in K_{n}
, such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
; and

M2)

if there exists a sequence
{u_{n}}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
with
u_{n}\in K_{n}
and a function
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, then
u\in K
.

Remark 4.1
.

When the sequence
\{s_{n}\}
is a constant sequence, i.e.,
s_{n}=\sigma
for all

, the generalized Mosco convergence reduces to the classical notion of Mosco’s convergence
[

]

In the following subsections we shall consider sequences
\{s_{n}\}\subset[0,1]
converging to
\sigma\in[0,1]
and a corresponding converging sequence of convex sets in the generalized sense of Mosco

\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)\supset K_{n}\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}K\subset\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega),

(4.4)

and sequence of given data, such that

{f_{0}}_{n}\to f_{0}\mbox{ in }{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{f}_{n}\to\boldsymbol{f}\mbox{ in }{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(4.5)

which implies

in the distributional sense, i.e.

for all

.

For simplicity, we shall consider the functions
\boldsymbol{a}
and

and

and

, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})\cdot D^{s_{n}}(w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(w_{n})(w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx\\
\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}u_{n})\cdot D^{s_{n}}(w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{n})(w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx=\langle F_{n},w_{n}-u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}.

Using
\Pi_{s_{n}}(w_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(w)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, the continuity of the operators
\boldsymbol{a}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\to{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
b:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the convergence of
f_{n}\to f
in
{\mathbb{L}^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the weak limits (
4.31
), we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{\sigma}w)\cdot D^{\sigma}(w-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(w)(w-u)}\,dx\geq\langle F,\varphi-u\rangle_{\sigma}.

(4.33)

Setting in (
4.33
)
w=u+\theta(v-u)
, with
0<\theta<1
and an arbitrary
v\in K
, dividing by
\theta
and letting
\theta\to 0
, we conclude that
u\in K
is a weak solution of (
4.30
):

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{\sigma}u)\cdot D^{\sigma}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq\langle F,v-u\rangle_{\sigma},\quad\forall v\in K.

∎

4.3.1
Stronger convergence for
p
-Laplacian type operators

In the framework of Corollary
3.3
, we can prove a strong convergence result for the fractional gradients in a Mosco type theorem for
p
-Laplacian operators with lower order terms, in addition to the conclusions of Remark
4.3
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let us assume the hypothesis of Theorem
4.3
and let us also assume that
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
satisfy (
3.23
) and (
3.31
), with
\beta_{p}>-\alpha_{p}(s/C_{P})^{p}
when
\Omega
is bounded and with
\beta_{p}>0
when
\Omega
is unbounded, respectively. Then, the sequence of solutions
u_{n}\in K_{n}
of (
4.28
) satisfies

u_{n}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\to D^{\sigma}u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
u\in K
is the unique solution of (
4.30
).

Proof.

From Theorem
4.1
we know that there exists a subsequence
\{u_{n}\}
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
where
u
is the solution to (
4.30
). We only need to prove that this weak convergence is a strong convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
.

Using the assumption
K_{n}\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}K
we may consider a sequence
\{w_{n}\}
, with
w_{n}\in K_{n}
, such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(w_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Since
u_{n}
are solutions to (
4.28
), we have

\langle D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}u_{n})-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})+b(w_{n})-b(u_{n}),w_{n}-u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})-\boldsymbol{f}_{n})\cdot(D^{s}w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{(b(w_{n})-{f_{0}}_{n})(w_{n}-u_{n})}\,dx.

(4.34)

On the other hand, since
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
satisfy (
3.23
) and (
3.31
), respectively, we have, for
p\geq 2
,

\langle D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}u_{n})-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})+b(w_{n})-b(u_{n}),w_{n}-u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}\\
\geq\alpha_{p}\|D^{s_{n}}(u_{n}-w_{n})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\beta_{p}\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

(4.35)

and, for
1<p<2
, also

\langle D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}u_{n})-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})+b(w_{n})-b(u_{n}),w_{n}-u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}\\
\geq\frac{1}{2^{\frac{2-p}{p}}(C_{u}+C_{w})^{\frac{2-p}{p}}}\left(\alpha_{p}\|D^{s_{n}}(u_{n}-w_{n})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\beta_{p}\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right).

(4.36)

Here
C_{u},C_{w}>0
are constants, independent of
n
, such that
\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{u}
and
\|w_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s_{n}}w_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{w}
.
Combining (
4.34
) with (
4.35
), when
p\geq 2
, and with (
4.36
), when
1<p<2
, and using
b(w_{n})\to b(u)
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s_{n}}w_{n})\to\boldsymbol{a}(D^{\sigma}u)
in
{L^{p^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively by the continuity of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
, together with the convergences (
4.5
), we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|_{\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left(\beta_{p}\|u_{n}-w_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s_{n}}(u_{n}-w_{n})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/p}}=0.

Hence, using Poincaré inequality when
\Omega
is bounded (see Remark
3.6
),
recalling
\Pi_{s_{n}}(w_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
, we conclude
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
and the convergence of the whole sequence is a consequence of the uniqueness of the limit problem (
4.30
), by Corollary
3.3
.
∎

Remark 4.5

, if

is bounded.

Remark 4.6
.

In the context of problems without unilateral constraints where

and

5
Applications to unilateral problems

5.1
Examples of generalized Mosco convergence with
s
in
[0,1]

In order to illustrate the Mosco type convergence theorems, in this section we provide some examples of families of convex sets that converge in the generalized sense of Mosco. The first example corresponds to the case in which the convex sets coincide with the Lions-Calderón spaces. The two examples of unilateral constraints, leading to variational inequalities, will be given by the translations of cones in those spaces, corresponding to obstacle problems, and by a special case of fractional gradient constraints.

Theorem 5.1

(No obstacle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and a sequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset[0,1]
with
s_{n}\to\sigma\in[0,1]
. Then
\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
converge in the generalized sense of Mosco to
\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ as }s_{n}\to\sigma.

(5.1)

Proof.

Condition
M2)
follows immediately from the fact that
K=\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

To prove
M1)
, let
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\{\varphi_{j}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
be a sequence such that
\varphi_{j}\to u
in
\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as
j\to\infty
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
q_{1}(m)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
j\geq q_{1}(m)
, for which

\|\varphi_{j}-u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{\sigma}\varphi_{j}-D^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}<1/m

For each
j\geq q_{1}(m)
, by Proposition
2.8
there exists a positive integer
q_{2}(m,j)\geq j
such that for all
k\geq q_{2}(m,j)
and

\|D^{s_{k}}\varphi_{j}-D^{\sigma}\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1/m.

Define the sequence

u_{n}=\begin{cases}\varphi_{1}&\mbox{ if }n<q(1)\\
\varphi_{q(k)}&\mbox{ if }q(k)\leq n<q(k+1),\quad\mbox{ for }k\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases}

where
q(k)=q_{2}(k,q_{1}(k))
. For
n\geq q_{2}(m)\geq q_{1}(m)
, then

\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}-D^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}-D^{\sigma}u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{\sigma}u_{n}-D^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2/m,

which implies that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the case
s_{n}\leq\sigma
, the proof of (
5.1
) is much simpler, since
M1)
holds directly from Proposition
2.8
by considering the sequence
u_{n}\equiv u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

In the case
s_{n}\geq\sigma
we can also give a different proof of (
5.1
). Let
u_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the solution of

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}|^{p-2}D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\cdot D^{s_{n}}v}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}v}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}v}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{f}\cdot D^{s_{n}}v}\,dx,\quad\forall v\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega).

(5.2)

Here the functions
f_{0}
and
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},...,f_{d})
are given such that

\langle-\Delta^{\sigma}_{p}u+|u|^{p-2}_{\sigma}u,v\rangle_{\sigma}=\langle F,v\rangle_{\sigma}=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}v}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{f}\cdot D^{\sigma}v}\,dx\quad\forall v\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega),

where the functional
F=-\Delta^{\sigma}_{p}u+|u|^{p-2}u\in\Lambda^{-\sigma,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{-s_{n},p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and the
f_{0},f_{1},...,f_{d}\in{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be obtained from Theorem
2.3
to characterize
F
.

We prove that, up to a subsequence,
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Testing (
5.2
) with
v=u_{n}
we get

\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}|^{p-2}D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\cdot D^{s_{n}}u_{n}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{|u_{n}|^{p}}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{f_{0}u_{n}}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{f}\cdot D^{s_{n}}u_{n}}\,dx.

(5.3)

This identity, together with Hölder’s inequality, allow us to obtain a uniform estimate of
\|u_{n}\|_{\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
with respect to
n
, and so, there exist two functions
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\eta\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

u_{n}\rightharpoonup\tilde{u}\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\rightharpoonup\eta\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(5.4)

We conclude that
\eta=D^{\sigma}\tilde{u}
as in (
4.32
). Using Minty’s lemma and the uniqueness of the solution of the limit problem, we deduce that
\tilde{u}=u
.

Finally, since
u
is a weak-solution of (
5.2
) when
s_{n}
is replaced by
\sigma
, and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, from (
5.3
), we obtain

\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{n}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\langle F,u_{n}\rangle_{s_{n}}\to\langle F,u\rangle_{\sigma}=\|D^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Consequently, since
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
is uniformly convex for
p\in(1,\infty)
, we conclude that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
strongly.

In the general case
s_{n}\to\sigma
we can replace the proof of
M1)
in Theorem
5.1
by choosing
u_{n}=u
if
s_{n}\leq\sigma
, and
u_{n}
as the solution of (
5.2
) if
s_{n}\geq\sigma
.

As a simple consequence of the Theorems of Section
3
and
5.1
, we get the following general stability result for fractional partial differential equations.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\{s_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
be a sequence such that
s_{n}\to\sigma\in(0,1]
, under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
,
4.2
or
4.3
, with
F_{n}={f_{0}}_{n}-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{f}_{n}\in\Lambda^{-s_{n},p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
satisfying (
4.5
). If
u_{n}
are solutions to

\begin{cases}-D^{s_{n}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(u_{n},D^{s_{n}}u_{n})+b(u_{n},D^{s_{n}}u_{n})=F_{n}&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

(5.5)

then one can extract a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
, such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, i.e.

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\rightharpoonup D^{\sigma}u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a solution of

\begin{cases}-D^{\sigma}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(u,D^{\sigma}u)+b(u,D^{\sigma}u)=F&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.6)

Remark 5.2
.

If in the previous corollary we assume the hypothesis of Theorem
4.3
for the functions
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
, we can then make also take sequences
s_{n}
tending to
0
. Similarly, also under the stronger assumptions of Theorem
4.4
for fractional operators of
p
-Laplacian type we have
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
strongly, for the whole sequence
s_{n}\to\sigma\in[0,1]
.

For obstacle problems, we consider families of convex sets of the following type

K^{s}_{\geq\psi}:=\{v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):v\geq\psi\},

with their convergence in the generalized sense of Mosco. For
\psi\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, the condition
\psi^{+}=\sup\{\psi,0\}\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is sufficient for the convex set
K^{s}_{\geq\psi}
to be non-empty. We start with the case, inspired in
[
Boc21
, Proposition 3 (1)]
, where we have an upper bound on the obstacles
\psi_{n}
by its limit
\psi
and the fractional parameters
s_{n}
are non-decreasing.

Proposition 5.1

(With bound on the obstacles and order in the fractional parameters)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and a non-decreasing sequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with
s_{n}\nearrow\sigma\in(0,1]
. For a sequence of functions
\{\psi_{n}\}
with
\psi_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(\psi_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(\psi)
weakly in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
for some
\psi\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\psi_{s_{n}}\leq\psi
, then
K^{s_{n}}_{\geq\psi_{s_{n}}}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}
.

Proof.

To verify
M1)
, we just need to observe that for every
v\in K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}
we can simply consider the sequence
v_{n}\equiv v
for each
n
since
K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}\subset K^{s_{n}}_{\geq\psi_{n}}
.

On the other hand, to verify that
M2)
holds, we start by considering a sequence
\{v_{n}\}
with
v_{n}\in K^{s_{n}}_{\geq\psi_{n}}
for each
n
, and a function
v\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(v_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(v)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
as
s_{n}\to\sigma
. Now consider two compact sets
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
such that
\omega\Subset\omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
and consider also a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\omega^{\prime})
such that
\phi\equiv 1
in
\omega
. Moreover, from Proposition
2.5
, we know that
\|D^{s_{n}}(\phi u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{C}{s}\|u\|_{\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
, which together with Theorem
2.5
, implies the existence of two subsequences
\{\phi v_{n}\}
and
\{\phi\psi_{n}\}
such that

\begin{cases}\phi v_{n}\to\phi v\\
\phi\psi_{n}\to\phi\psi\end{cases}\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\omega^{\prime})\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\begin{cases}v_{n}\to v\\
\psi_{n}\to\psi\end{cases}\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\omega).

Since this is valid for every compact
\omega\Subset\Omega
, and since
v_{n}\geq\psi_{n}
for each
n
, we can easily deduce that
v\in K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}
.
∎

There are also some examples of sets that converge in the generalized sense of Mosco that do not require the order hypothesis on the obstacles, nor the order of the fractional parameters. As a first example, we consider
\psi_{n}=0
, where the convex sets are the cones of non negative functions.

Theorem 5.2

(Positive cones)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open and bounded set,
p\in(1,\infty)
and a sequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with
s_{n}\to\sigma\in[0,1]
. Then
K^{s_{n}}_{\geq 0}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K^{\sigma}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

For
M1)
we use the same arguments as in the proof of the Theorem
5.1
, but instead of using the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we use the density of the cone of non negative functions
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in the cone of non negative functions of
\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
of Theorem
2.7
.

For
M2)
, let us consider a sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
v_{n}\in K^{s_{n}}_{\geq 0}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and a function
v\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(v_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(v)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, we have

0\leq\int_{\Omega}{v_{n}\varphi}\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}{v\varphi}\,dx\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\mbox{ with }\varphi\geq 0,

which implies
v\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and, consequently,
v\in K^{\sigma}_{\geq 0}
.
∎

This result is the basis for other examples of sequences of convex sets of obstacle type that converge in the generalized sense of Mosco. These examples can be obtained when we combine Theorem (
5.2
) with the following result, adapted from
[
Mos69
, Lemma 1.6 on p. 529]
, concerning the convergence of translations of the convex sets, for the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
framework.

Proposition 5.2

(Translations of convex sets)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and a sequence
s_{n}
in
(0,1)
with
s_{n}\to\sigma\in[0,1]
. Consider a family of non-empty, closed convex sets
\{K_{n}\}
such that
K_{n}\subset\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
converging in the sense of Mosco to
K\subset\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, and also a sequence of functions
\{\psi_{n}\}
such that
\psi_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
n
and
\Pi_{s_{n}}(\psi_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(\psi)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
for some function
\psi\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, the convex set
K_{n}+\psi_{n}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K+\psi
.

Proof.

In order to check that
M1)
holds, let us consider a function
v\in K+\psi
, i.e.,
v=\bar{v}+\psi
for some
\bar{v}\in K
. Since
K_{m}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K
, then there exists
\bar{v}_{n}\in K_{n}
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(\bar{v}_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(\bar{v})
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. This allow us to construct the functions
v_{n}=\bar{v}_{n}+\psi_{n}\in K_{n}+\psi_{n}
with the property that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(v_{n})=\Pi_{s_{n}}(\bar{v}_{n})+\Pi_{s_{n}}(\psi_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(\bar{v})+\Pi_{\sigma}(\psi)=\Pi_{\sigma}(v)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
as
s\to\sigma
.

For the condition
M2)
, we start by considering a sequence
\{v_{n}\}
with
v_{n}\in K_{n}+\psi_{n}
and a function
v\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(v_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(v)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
. Since
v_{n}-\psi_{n}\in K_{n}
for each
n
,
\Pi_{s_{n}}(v_{n}-\psi_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(v-\psi)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, and
K_{n}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K
, then
v-\psi\in K
.
∎

As consequence of the last proposition, we can adapt
[
Mos69
, Lemma 1.7 on p. 531]
to prove a result about the generalized Mosco convergence of convex sets when the obstacles, and their fractional gradients, converge strongly.

Corollary 5.2

(Without order between obstacles)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
\{s_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
a sequence with
s\to\sigma\in[0,1]
. Consider
\{\psi_{n}\}
to be a family of measurable functions with
\psi_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
for each
n
.
If
\Pi_{s_{n}}(\psi_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(\psi)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
for some
\psi\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, then
K^{s_{n}}_{\geq\psi_{n}}
converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}
.

Proof.

As
K^{s_{n}}_{\geq 0}\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}K^{\sigma}_{\geq 0}
by Theorem
5.2
, and
\Pi_{s_{n}}(\psi_{n})\to\Pi_{\sigma}(\psi)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, Proposition
5.2
immediately yields
K^{s_{n}}_{\geq\psi_{n}}=K^{s_{n}}_{\geq 0}+\psi_{n}\xrightarrow{s_{n}-M}K^{\sigma}_{\geq 0}+\psi=K^{\sigma}_{\geq\psi}
.
∎

.

Remark 5.5

Theorem

and Corollary

are also valid for convex sets of the obstacle type of the form

with the appropriate adaptations of the proofs.

Another type of admissible sets that one can consider are the ones that correspond to constraints on the fractional gradient, namely convex sets of the form

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g}=\{v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):|D^{s}v|\leq g\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\}\neq\emptyset.

(5.7)

Concerning the generalized Mosco convergence, the case of the fractional gradient constraints are, in general, more difficult to deal with than those with obstacle constraints on the function. However, we present an interesting example of a family of convex sets of that type that converge in the generalized sense of Mosco, using the approximation of the Riesz operator
I_{\alpha}
to the identity as
\alpha\to 0
(
[
Kur81
]
).

Theorem 5.3

(Constraint in the fractional gradient)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
any open set,
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
\sigma\in(0,1]
and an increasing sequence
\{s_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
such that
s_{n}\nearrow\sigma\in(0,1]
. Consider a function
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<q<p
and
g\geq 0
a.e., and a sequence of functions
g_{n}=I_{\sigma-s_{n}}g
. Then
\mathcal{K}^{s_{n}}_{g_{n}}
, as defined in (
5.7
), converges in the generalized sense of Mosco to
\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{g}
.

Proof.

We start by noting that
g_{n}\to g
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
[
Kur81
, Theorem C]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{g}\subset\mathcal{K}^{s_{n}}_{g_{n}}
for every
n
. In fact, if
u\in\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{g}
, then Proposition
2.10
allow us to obtain the estimate

|D^{s_{n}}u|=|I_{\sigma-s_{n}}D^{\sigma}u|\leq I_{\sigma-s_{n}}|D^{\sigma}u|\leq I_{\sigma-s_{n}}g=g_{n}.

Consequently, we can set
u_{n}\equiv u\in\mathcal{K}^{\sigma}_{g}\subset\mathcal{K}^{s_{n}}_{g_{n}}
for all
n
, and
M1)
holds.

For the condition
M2)
, consider a sequence of functions
u_{n}\in\mathcal{K}{s_{n}}_{g_{n}}
such that
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Then,
D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\rightharpoonup D^{\sigma}u
in
L^{p}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all measurable sets
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, because
\Pi_{s_{n}}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\Pi_{\sigma}(u)
in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, and we have

\int_{\omega}{|D^{\sigma}u|^{p}}\,dx\leq\liminf_{s_{n}\nearrow\sigma}{\int_{\omega}{|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}|^{p}}}\,dx\leq\liminf_{s_{n}\nearrow\sigma}{\int_{\omega}{|g_{n}|^{p}}}\,dx=\int_{\omega}{|g|^{p}}\,dx.

Since this inequality is valid for all measurable sets
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we conclude that
|D^{\sigma}u|\leq g
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

5.2
Quasi-variational Inequalities

As a natural application of the Mosco convergence in variational inequalities, quasi-variational inequalities correspond to unilateral problems where the convex sets depend on the solutions themselves. This type of problems with
s=1
arise in different models of control theory, solid mechanics or superconductivity, for instance. In this section, we provide examples of quasi-variational inequalities involving the
s
-fractional gradient. In particular, we study the existence of solutions, and their continuous dependence with respect to the fractional parameter
s
, to a quasi-variational inequality of obstacle type, where the obstacle is itself a solution to a coupled problem. We also study the existence of solutions to a quasi-variational inequality of
s
-gradient constraint type.

5.2.1
Quasi-variational inequality of obstacle type

Consider the problem of finding a solution
u\in K^{s}_{r}(u)
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)D^{s}(w-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(w-u)}\,dx\geq\langle F,w-u\rangle_{s},\quad\forall w\in K^{s}_{t}(u),

(5.8)

where the convex set is of obstacle type

K^{s}_{t}(u)=\{w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):\,w\geq\Psi(u)\},

(5.9)

with the obstacle
\psi=\Psi(u)
being itself a solution to the problem, depending on
u
, given by

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{t}_{p}\psi+|\psi|^{p-2}\psi=T(u,D^{s}u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\psi=0&\mbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

(5.10)

where
T
is a suitable continuous operator.
The implicit obstacle problem (
5.8
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) may be solved with the general abstract existence theory for quasi-variational inequalities of
[
JM79
]
or
[
KKM08
, Theorem 2.2]
. However, for this type of application it is simpler to use directly a combination of the Mosco continuous dependence results with a compactness argument. To illustrate also the continuous dependence on the fractional parameter in the case of monotone operators, including of
p
-Laplacian type, we present the following theorem, which proof we develop for completeness.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be any open set,
s\in[0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let us consider two Carathéodory functions
\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{a}(x,\xi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
b=b(x,r):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, both monotone with respect to their last variable, and at least on of them being strictly monotone, satisfying the growth conditions (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) and the strong coercivity (
3.15
). Consider also a family of closed convex sets
\{K(v):\,v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\}
such that

i)

there exists a bounded set
E\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with the property that
K(v)\cap E\neq\emptyset
for all
v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
;

ii)

whenever
v_{n}\rightharpoonup v
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have
K(v_{n})\to K(v)
in the (classical) sense of Mosco.

Then, there exists a function
u\in K(u)
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(w-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(w-u)}\,dx\geq 0,\quad\forall w\in K(u).

(5.11)

Proof.

For each
v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can apply Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.1
, to get the existence and uniqueness of the solution
u_{v}=S(v)\in K(v)
to the problem

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{v}))\cdot D^{s}(w-u_{v})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{v})(w-u_{v})}\,dx\geq 0,\quad\forall w\in K(v).

(5.12)

We show that
S
has a fixed point. We start by obtaining an a priori estimate for the functions
u_{v}
. Choose, for each
v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, a function
g_{v}\in E\cap K(v)
. With these functions, we can apply the coercivity condition to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{v})\cdot D^{s}g_{v}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{v})g_{v}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{v})\cdot D^{s}u_{v}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{v})u_{v}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|D^{s}u_{v}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\beta\|u_{v}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}-\|k\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

On the other hand, from the growth conditions, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{v})\cdot D^{s}g_{v}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{v})g_{v}}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|\gamma_{1}\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}g_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C_{1}\|D^{s}u_{v}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}g_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\|\gamma_{2}\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)}\|g_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C_{2}\|u_{v}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Combining this two estimates, and using the fact that
E
is bounded in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we deduce that there exists
R>0
, such that
\|u_{v}\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq R
, and so

S\left(\overline{B_{R}}\right)\subset S(\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))\subset\overline{B_{R}},

(5.13)

where
\overline{B_{R}}=\{v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):\,\|v\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq R\}
. Since
\overline{B_{R}}
is weakly compact, then there exists
v,z\in\overline{B_{R}}
such that

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad S(v_{n})\rightharpoonup z\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

The weak convergence of
v_{n}
, implies
K(v_{n})\to K(v)
in the sense of Mosco by assumption
ii)
. As a consequence of Theorem
4.3
, which in this case is equivalent to the classical Mosco’s theorem because
s_{n}\equiv\sigma
, see Remark
4.1
, we also get
S(v_{n})\rightharpoonup S(v)
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

With these properties, we can apply Tychonov’s fixed point theorem,
[
BC84
, Theorem 9.16]
to deduce that
S
has a fixed point in
\overline{B_{R}}
, completing the proof.
∎

As an application of this type of fixed point argument, we have the following existence result for (
5.8
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), where we can relax the continuity assumption on the dependence
v\mapsto K^{s}_{r}(v)
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set,
0<s<t\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Let
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
satisfy the same assumptions as in Theorem
5.4
. Let
T:\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)\mapsto L^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
be a continuous operator such that
\|T(g)\|_{L^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M<\infty
for all
g\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Then, the quasi-variational inequality (
5.8
), (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) has a solution.

Proof.

We apply Schauder’s fixed theorem to
v\mapsto S(v)=Q\circ\Psi(v)
, where
\Psi=\overline{\Psi}\circ T\circ\Pi_{s}(v)
, being
T\circ\Pi_{s}:\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{-t,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
a compact operator since
L^{(p^{s}_{*})^{\prime}}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{-t,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
by Corollary
2.6
, and with
\overline{\Psi}:\Lambda^{-t,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\ni T_{v}\mapsto\psi\in\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
being the solution of (
5.10
) given by Corollary
3.3
, and
Q:\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\to\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is the solution to the variational inequality (
5.8
) with the convex
K^{s}_{t}(v)
given by (
5.9
).

In order to obtain the inclusion (
5.13
) for some
R>0
, we need the following a priori estimate. For every
v\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, let
\Psi(v)
denote the solution to (
5.10
) with
T_{v}=T(v,D^{s}v)
, so, recalling
\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

C^{\prime}\|\Psi(v)\|^{p}_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\|\Psi(v)\|^{p}_{\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\langle-\Delta^{t}_{p}\Psi(v)+|\Psi(v)|^{p-2}\Psi(v),\Psi(v)\rangle_{\sigma}=\int_{\Omega}{T_{v}\Psi(v)}\,dx\\
\leq\|T_{v}\|_{L^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\Psi(v)\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime}_{s}\|T_{v}\|_{L^{(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\Psi(v)\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}.

(5.14)

Since
\|T_{v}\|_{{L^{p_{s}^{*}}}^{\prime}(\Omega)}\leq M
, then we have

\|\Psi(v)\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq(M/C_{s})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=R,

(5.15)

and we have a fixed point
u=Su\in\overline{B_{R}}\cap K^{s}_{t}(u)
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

An example may be given by the Uryson operator

.

Theorem 5.6

in
\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we also have
D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u_{n})\to D^{\sigma}\Psi(u)
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
because

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\|D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u_{n})-D^{\sigma}\Psi(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left(\|D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u_{n})-D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u)-D^{\sigma}\Psi(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left(C_{s_{*}}\|\Psi(u_{n})-\Psi(u)\|_{\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)}+\|D^{s_{n}}\Psi(u)-D^{\sigma}\Psi(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)}=0.

To conclude, we just need to apply Corollary
5.2
.
∎

5.2.2
Quasi-variational inequality of
s
-gradient constraint type

Consider the problem of finding
u\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{G(u)}
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)\cdot D^{s}(v-u)}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(v-u)}\,dx\geq\langle F_{s},v-u\rangle_{s},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{G(u)}

(5.20)

where
G:L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a continuous and bounded operator, with

L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,g\geq\nu\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

for some
\nu>0
, and

\mathcal{K}^{s}_{G(u)}=\{w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):\,|D^{s}w|\leq G(u)\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

(5.21)

This extends a result of
[
RS19a
]
to the nonlinear fractional setting, inspired in examples of the classic case
s=1
surveyed in
[
RS19b
]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,
s\in(0,1]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\nu>0
. Let
\mathscr{A}_{s}(u)=-D^{s}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u)
be a
p
-Laplacian type operator, let
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy the growth condition (
3.17
) and the monotonicity condition (
3.31
), with the constant
\beta_{p}\geq 0
, and let
F\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. If
G:L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a continuous and bounded operator, then there exists a solution
u\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{G(u)}
to (
5.20
).

Proof.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We know from Corollary
3.3
that there exists a unique solution
u_{g}=S(F,g)\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{g})\cdot D^{s}(v-u_{g})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u)(v-u_{g})}\,dx\geq\langle F,v-u_{g}\rangle_{s},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g}.

Moreover, since
0\in\mathcal{K}^{s}_{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}u_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}+\beta_{p}\|u_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{g})\cdot D^{s}u_{g}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{g})u_{g}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\langle F,u_{g}\rangle_{s}\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}u_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which implies the existence of a positive constant
C_{F}
, independent of
g
, such that

\|u_{g}\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{s}\|D^{s}u_{g}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{F}.

Let us consider the operator
T=S(F,\cdot)\circ G
. Notice that for every
w\in\overline{B_{C_{F}}}=\{v\in L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega):\,\|v\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{F}\}
, we have
T(w)=u_{G(w)}\in\overline{B_{C_{F}}}\cap\mathcal{K}^{s}_{G(w)}
, so by proving that
T
is continuous and compact in
L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can apply Schauder’s fixed point theorem on
\overline{B_{C_{F}}}
and deduce that there exists a function
u\in\overline{B_{C_{F}}}
such that
T(u)=u
.

To prove that
T:L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega)\to L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuous, we only need to prove that
S(F_{s},\cdot):L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to L^{p^{*}_{s}}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuous since
G:L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is by hypothesis a continuous operator. Consider two functions
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)
and define
u_{i}=S(F,g_{i})
as well as
\tilde{u}_{i}=\frac{\nu}{\nu+\eta}u_{i}
, where
\eta:=\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, for
i=1,2
. Notice that
\tilde{u}_{1}\in K^{s}(g_{2})
and
\tilde{u}_{2}\in K^{s}(g_{1})
because

|D^{s}\tilde{u}_{1}|\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu+\eta}|g_{1}|\leq\frac{\nu}{\nu+\eta}(|g_{1}-g_{2}|+|g_{2}|)\leq\frac{\eta}{\nu+\eta}\nu+\frac{\nu}{\nu+\eta}|g_{2}|\leq|g_{2}|,

and similarly
|D^{s}\tilde{u}_{2}|\leq|g_{1}|
. Moreover, these functions also satisfy

|u_{i}-\tilde{u}_{i}|=\frac{\eta}{\nu+\eta}|u_{i}|\leq\frac{\eta}{\nu}|u_{i}|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|D^{s}(u_{i}-\tilde{u}_{i})|=\frac{\eta}{\nu+\eta}|D^{s}u_{i}|\leq\frac{\eta}{\nu}|D^{s}u_{i}|.

These estimates, in conjugation with Hölder’s inequality, the fractional Poincaré inequality and the fact that
u_{g_{i}}=S(F_{s},g_{i})
, allows us to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{1})-\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{2}))\cdot D^{s}(u_{1}-u_{2})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{(b(u_{1})-b(u_{2}))(u_{1}-u_{2})}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\langle F,(u_{1}-\tilde{u}_{1})+(u_{2}-\tilde{u}_{2}))\rangle_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{1})\cdot D^{s}(\tilde{u}_{2}-u_{2})}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}(D^{s}u_{2})\cdot D^{s}(\tilde{u}_{1}-u_{1})}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{1})(\tilde{u}_{2}-u_{2})}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{b(u_{2})\cdot(\tilde{u}_{1}-u_{1})}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta}{\nu}\|f\|_{{L^{p}}^{\prime}(\Omega)}(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)})+2C\frac{\eta}{\nu}\left(\|D^{s}u_{1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{1}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Moreover, if we combine this estimate with the coercivity conditions on
\boldsymbol{a}
and
b
, (
3.23
) and (
3.31
) respectively, we have for
p>2

C_{\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{p}_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and for
1<p<2

C_{\alpha_{p},\beta_{p}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|^{2}_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\|D^{s}u_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{s}u_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{2-p}\\
\leq(2C_{F})^{2-p}C\|g_{1}-g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

concluding the continuity of
S(F_{s},\cdot):L^{\infty}(\Omega)\to L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)
.

To prove the compactness of
T
, we use the boundedness of
G:L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)\to L^{\infty}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the estimate

\|D^{s}u_{G(w)}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}u_{G(w)}|^{q}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}u_{G(w)}|^{q-p}|D^{s}u_{G(w)}|^{p}}\,dx\\
\leq\|D^{s}u_{G(w)}\|^{q-p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}u_{G(w)}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\leq\|G(w)\|^{q-p}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{s}u_{G(w)}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

with
q>\frac{N}{s}

q>p
and
\gamma=s-\frac{N}{q}
, to derive
T(\overline{B_{C_{F}}})\subset C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})\Subset C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\subset L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)
, by using Ascoli-Arzelà’s theorem.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Similarly to

and continuous in

. Moreover, for

with

The case of the convergence of the solutions of the quasi-variational problems with gradient constraints is much more delicate, since the criteria of the generalized Mosco convergence of the convex sets

]

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set,

4.5
) as

u_{s}\to u\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{s_{*},p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\lambda}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s}u_{s}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}Du\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),

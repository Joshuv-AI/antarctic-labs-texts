The TAP free energy for high-dimensional linear regression
Jiaze Qiu, Subhabrata Sen

Abstract
We derive a variational representation for the log-normalizing constant of the posterior distribution in Bayesian linear regression with a uniform spherical prior and an i.i.d. Gaussian design. We work under the “proportional" asymptotic regime, where the number of observations and the number of features grow at a proportional rate. This rigorously establishes the Thouless-Anderson-Palmer (TAP) approximation arising from spin glass theory, and proves a conjecture of [ 22 ] in the special case of the spherical prior.

Introduction

The analysis of high-dimensional probability distributions is a central challenge in modern Statistics and Machine Learning. This is particularly true in the context of Bayesian Statistics, where scientists carry out inference based on the posterior distribution. In modern applications, the posterior distribution is typically high-dimensional, and analytically intractable. Variational Inference (VI) has emerged as an attractive option to approximate these intractable distributions, facilitating fast, parallel computations in state-of-the-art applications
[
32
,
10
]
. In this approach, the distribution of interest is approximated (in KL divergence) by distributions from a pre-specified, more tractable collection. The simplest version of VI is the Naive Mean-field approximation (NMF), where the distribution of interest is approximated by a product distribution. This strategy has roots in classical approximations for spins-systems in statistical physics
[
23
]
.

Despite the rapidly growing popularity of variational approximations in Statistics and Machine Learning, the corresponding theoretical guarantees for these approximations are quite limited. In this paper, we study Bayesian linear regression, a standard workhorse of modern Statistics, via the lens of (advanced) Mean-field approximations. Given data
\{(y_{i},x_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\}
,
y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the scientist posits a linear model
y_{i}=x_{i}^{\top}\beta+\varepsilon_{i}
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\varepsilon_{i}\sim N(0,\Delta)
are iid. Given a prior distribution
\pi_{0}
, setting
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
X^{\top}=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
, the scientist constructs the posterior

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}}{\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}}(\beta|y,X)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{p}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}}\text{,}

(1)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
is the normalizing constant of the posterior distribution. Borrowing terminology from statistical physics, we will refer to
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
as the partition function of the model.

Using the classical Gibbs variational principle
[
32
]
, one has

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}=\sup_{Q\ll\pi_{0}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{Q}\Big[-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\|y-X\beta\|_{2}^{2}\Big]-\mathrm{D_{KL}}(Q\|\pi_{0})\Big),

with equality if
Q
is the posterior distribution. Restricting the supremum above to product measures, one obtains the NMF approximation to the log-partition function. Note that the NMF approximation is always a valid lower bound:

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq\sup_{Q=\prod_{i}Q_{i}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{Q}\Big[-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\|y-X\beta\|_{2}^{2}\Big]-\mathrm{D_{KL}}(Q\|\pi_{0})\Big).

We say that the NMF approximation is
correct to leading order
if

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}-\sup_{Q=\prod_{i}Q_{i}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{Q}\Big[-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\|y-X\beta\|_{2}^{2}\Big]-\mathrm{D_{KL}}(Q\|\pi_{0})\Big)=o(p).

(2)

In recent joint work with Sumit Mukherjee
[
25
]
, the second author leveraged recent advances in the theory of non-linear large deviations to derive sufficient conditions on
X
for the accuracy of NMF approximation when
\pi_{0}=\prod^{\otimes p}\pi
is a product distribution. In this setting, it is easy to see that the supremum in (
2
) is obtained by
Q^{*}=\prod_{i}Q_{i}^{*}
, with

\frac{\mathrm{d}Q_{i}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}\pi}(\beta_{i})\propto\exp\Big(-\frac{(\beta_{i}-R_{i})^{2}}{2\Sigma_{i}^{2}}\Big)
.
The approximation (
2
) can thus be equivalently parametrized in terms of
a_{i}=\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}^{*}}[\beta_{i}]
and
c_{i}=\mathrm{Var}_{Q_{i}^{*}}[\beta_{i}]
, so that

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}=\sup_{a,c}\Big(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\|y-Xa\|_{2}^{2}-\mathrm{D_{KL}}(Q^{*}\|\pi_{0})\Big)+o(p)

(3)

if the NMF approximation is accurate to leading order.

In the special case where
x_{i}
’s are iid standard gaussian,
[
25
]
establishes the accuracy of the NMF approximation as long as
p=o(n)
. This result is expected to be tight—using insights from spin glasses, physicists predict that Bayes linear regression is
not
approximately NMF if the number of samples
n
and the number of features
p
grow proportionally
[
22
]
. In fact, using the Thouless-Anderson-Palmer (TAP) formulation, one obtains the following prediction for the log-partition function.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

Assume
\pi_{0}=\prod^{\otimes p}\pi
, where
\pi
is a probability distribution on
[-1,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}=\max_{\|a\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\Big[-\frac{\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}}{2\Delta}-\frac{n}{2}\log\Big(1+\frac{\bar{c}}{\Delta}\Big)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\log\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\Big[\exp(-\frac{(\beta_{i}-R_{i})^{2}}{2\Sigma_{*}^{2}})\Big]\Big]+o(p),

(4)

where the parameters
R_{i}
,
c_{i}
and
\Sigma_{*}
are specified as follows: define

(\mathrm{d}Q_{i}/\mathrm{d}\pi)(\beta)\propto\exp(-(\beta-R_{i})^{2}/(2\Sigma_{*}^{2}))
.
R_{i}
is chosen such that
\mathbb{E}_{Q_{i}}[\beta]=a_{i}
. Set
c_{i}=\mathrm{Var}_{Q_{i}}(\beta)
. Finally, the parameter
\Sigma_{*}
depends on
\Delta
and
\alpha
, and is specified implicitly as the smallest solution to a fixed point equation.

The interval
[-1,1]
is arbitrary, the conjecture generalizes directly to any prior
\pi
supported on a bounded set.
The main difference between (
3
) and (
4
) is the second term in the RHS of (
4
). This term is referred to as the
Onsager Correction
term in statistical physics. The representation (
4
) is crucial for a number of reasons:

(i)

It suggests that the widely popular NMF approximation is actually incorrect in certain regimes, and thus practitioners should exercise caution before using the NMF approximation.

(ii)

In settings where the TAP formalism is conjectured to be correct, it is further conjectured that the variational problem (
4
) has a unique global optimizer. Further, this optimizer is expected to be an asymptotically optimal estimator (in a Bayesian sense).

(iii)

This suggests the following approach—optimize the RHS of (
4
) directly using an out of the box algorithm such as gradient descent, and then use the resulting estimator for inference.

Exploring the validity of the TAP framework in this problem thus has vital practical implications. From a theoretical perspective, the posterior distribution (
1
) can be viewed as a
planted
spin glass model. Due to special symmetries present in these models (e.g. the Nishimori identities), posterior distributions naturally provide examples of
replica symmetric
models in spin glass theory. They provide ideal test beds to study the broader applicability of ideas developed to study mean-field spin glass models such as the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) and mixed p-spin models. Techniques developed originally in the context of mean-field spin glasses have already been utilized to characterize the limiting mutual information between the data and the underlying signal in the Bayesian linear regression model
[
6
]
. In this paper, we adopt a complementary approach, and initiate a study of Bayes linear regression model through the lens of the TAP approximation.

1.1
Main Result

Our main result in this paper is the analogue of Conjecture
1.1
under a uniform spherical prior on
\beta
. Specifically, we consider Bayesian linear regression with a uniform prior on
S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})\coloneqq\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\lVert\beta\rVert=\sqrt{p}\}
. Crucially, we assume throughout that we are in the
well-specified
setting, i.e. the data
\{(y_{i},x_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\}
is also generated from the model
y_{i}=x_{i}^{\top}\beta_{0}+\varepsilon_{i}
, where
\beta_{0}\sim\pi_{0}
, and
\pi_{0}
henceforth denotes the uniform distribution on
S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})
.

We assume that
x_{i}\sim\textit{N}_{p}(0,I/n)
are iid. We will work under the
proportional asymptotics
setting, i.e., the number of samples
n
and the number of features
p
grow proportionally, with
n/p\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. For convenience, we will track the asymptotics in terms of
p
, and will think of
n
as a function of
p
.
Note that in this setting, the
partition function
is

\mathcal{Z}_{p}=\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{.}

(5)

The partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
is a function of both
y
and
X
, so we use the notation
\mathcal{Z}_{p}(y,X)
when we want to highlight its dependence on
y
and
X
.
We refer to

F_{p}(y,X)=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}(y,X)

as the
free energy
and

\mathcal{H}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}

(6)

as the
Hamiltonian
, borrowing terminology from statistical physics. Armed with these notions, we can state our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\Delta>\Delta_{0}
, as
p\to\infty
,

\left|F_{p}-\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)\right|\overset{\text{P}}{\longrightarrow}0\text{,}

where

f_{\text{TAP}}(a)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2\Delta p}\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\lVert a\rVert^{2}/p}{\Delta\alpha}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)\text{.}

(7)

Remark
1
.

Note from (
5
) that
1/(2\Delta)
is similar to an inverse temperature parameter in this model. Thus our TAP representation is valid
at high temperature
. The high-temperature requirement comes up when we lower bound the free energy in terms of the TAP formula. We emphasize that the matching upper bound is valid in general, and does not require any additional conditions. Establishing a valid TAP representation at any
\Delta>0
remains an open problem in this setting.

Remark
2
.

A close look at our proof indicates that the TAP representation is valid for any
\Delta>0
if
\alpha
is sufficiently large. In this case, the Signal-to-Noise Ratio is high, and the model is again effectively at high-temperature. We state all our results at fixed
\alpha
, with
\Delta>0
sufficiently large, but note that all results transfer immediately to the
fixed
\Delta
large
\alpha
regime.

1.2
Background and Challenges

We describe here the broader context of our result. We also take this opportunity to highlight the main differences from the existing literature, and describe the key challenges in our setting.

The TAP formalism was originally introduced for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model by Thouless-Anderson-Palmer in
[
29
]
, predating Parisi’s groundbreaking
replica-symmetry breaking
solution. The TAP approach yields a variational representation for the free energy, as well as a fixed point equation for the global magnetizations, referred to as the TAP equations. The TAP equations were established rigorously using a number of different approaches—Stein’s method
[
12
]
, cavity method
[
28
]
, dynamical methods
[
1
]
etc. At high-temperature, the Gibbs measure is in one
pure-state
, and the magnetizations satisfy the TAP equations. The behavior at low temperatures is significantly more complicated—the Gibbs measure decomposes into exponentially many
pure states
, and a version of the TAP equations are expected to hold within each pure state. A rigorous version of this conjecture was verified in
[
2
]
using the approximate ultrametric decomposition from
[
21
]
.

The progress on the TAP formula for the free energy is more recent.
[
13
]
verified the TAP representation by evoking the Parisi formula. More recently, direct proofs of the TAP representation have been derived in the literature. In this regard,
[
9
]
utilizes Random Matrix Theory to establish the variational formula for the spherical SK model, while subsequent developments establish these results for mixed p-spin models
[
14
,
15
]
. In this regard, the notion of
multi-samplability
plays a crucial role. We will also crucially exploit a related idea in our analysis. We emphasize this connection in our discussion of the proof strategy below. We note that in sharp contrast with the model studied in this paper, the models studied in the prior literature are
null
models, i.e. do not have a latent signal. The planted setting is arguably more natural in the context of Statistics and Machine Learning. To the best of our knowledge, this is the very first proof of a TAP formula for the free energy for a planted spin glass model. We note that the random hamiltonians in the study of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick and mixed p-spin models are centered Gaussian processes—this directly facilitates the use of tools tailored for Gaussian variables e.g. interpolation and comparison techniques
[
14
,
15
]
. This is no longer true in our setting, and thus necessitates new ideas. In this setting, the hamiltonian roughly looks like a two-spin model, with the sample covariance matrix as the couplings among the spins. We directly utilize this structure in our proofs, exploiting spectral properties of this matrix. We expect our proof ideas to be applicable for more general random matrix ensembles, e.g., the orthogonally invariant matrix ensembles
[
26
,
7
]
.

The behavior of the TAP free energy has been examined in the setting of finite rank spiked matrix models in
[
18
]
.
[
18
]
establishes that the Naive Mean Field approximation exhibits an undesirable instability property below the Information Theoretic threshold, thus rendering variational methods based on this approximation untrustworthy in practice. In turn, it suggests using the TAP framework to carry out variational inference in this setting. In follow up work
[
16
,
11
]
, the accuracy of TAP-approximation based variational inference has been established in the context of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
synchronization problem, a special case of the general setting examined in
[
18
]
.

1.3
Proof strategy

Our proof has two separate arguments—first, we lower bound the log-partition function
\log\mathcal{Z}_{p}
in terms of the TAP free energy
f_{\text{TAP}}(a)
, as defined in (
7
). To this end, fix
a\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\|a\|\leq\sqrt{p}
. We re-center the hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(\beta)
around the vector
a
; this yields the term
-\|y-Xa\|^{2}/(2\Delta p)
in
f_{\text{TAP}}(a)
. To obtain a valid lower bound, we restrict the integral in
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
to the intersection of
S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})
and a subspace that is orthogonal to
a
and the resulting external magnetization
X^{\top}(y-Xa)
from the previous re-centering step. The volume of this
band
is
[\ln(1-\|a\|^{2}/p)]/2
—this contributes the third term in
f_{\text{TAP}}(a)
. Finally, the model restricted to the
band
is a spherical two-spin model with hamiltonian
H(\beta)=-\|X(\beta-a)\|^{2}/(2\Delta)
. The free energy of this model is calculated using the second moment method, and contributes
-(\alpha/2)\ln[1+(1-\|a\|^{2}/p)/(\Delta\alpha)]
in
f_{\text{TAP}}(a)
. This yields a natural interpretation of the Onsager correction term in this problem. We note that this step explicitly uses the “high-temperature" condition stated in the Theorem statement. This shows that
(\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p})/p\geq f_{\text{TAP}}(a)

uniformly
over all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\|a\|/\sqrt{p}
is bounded away from one. We argue separately that the end-point can be neglected. Our argument is similar to the one of
[
9
]
in the context of the spherical two-spin Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model.

Our main contribution is the corresponding upper bound on the free energy in terms of the TAP free energy functional. To this end, we crucially leverage
overlap concentration
properties under the well-specified posterior distributions
[
5
]
. Roughly, this implies that given a vanishing amount of additional side information, with high-probability (over the law of the disorder
(y,X)
), we can sample many iid
replicas

\beta^{1},\cdots,\beta^{m_{p}}
from the posterior distribution such that all the pairwise overlaps are concentrated around a fixed value. Such overlap values are also termed as
multi-samplable
in
[
27
]
in the study of the TAP formalism for mixed p-spin spherical spin glasses. This overlap concentration property allows us to derive an upper bound to the log-partition function
\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}
in terms of a restricted partition function for a
replicated
system, where all the pairwise overlaps are restricted to approximately the same value. We now set
a
as the sample average of these replicas, and re-center the hamiltonian around this vector. The concentration of all pairwise overlaps around a fixed value implies that the
\beta^{i}-a
’s are approximately orthogonal, and this allows us to cancel the external magnetic field
X^{\top}(y-Xa)
created from the re-centering step. Finally, due to the approximate orthogonality of the
\beta^{i}-a
vectors, the integral in the replicated system approximately de-couples, and provides
m_{p}
copies of the Onsager correction and volume contributions. In particular, this establishes that

(\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p})/p\leq f_{\text{TAP}}(a)+o(1)
for some (random)
a\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and thus yields the desired upper bound.

We note that the overlap concentration property plays a very crucial role in our proof. This concentration property has been established recently for well-specified Bayesian posteriors (see e.g.
[
8
]
). It should be possible to combine the ideas in this paper with overlap concentration to establish similar TAP formulae in other Bayesian inference problems. We leave this for future work.

1.4
Directions for future research

We collect here some open questions for future research.

(i)

An immediate open question is to extend our TAP representation to any
\Delta>0
. We note that the high-temperature condition is explicitly used to calculate the free energy on the band—this is the only step in our proof which uses this condition.

(ii)

The main question for future research concerns a proof of Conjecture
1.1
. We believe that the ideas introduced in this paper will be crucial in this effort. The spherical prior crucially allows us to use ideas from random matrix theory; specifically, after the re-centering step described above, one has to compute the free energy of a two spin model on the sphere. Further, one requires this estimate to be
uniform
over the choice of the center. Exploiting the spherical symmetry, we use asymptotics of spherical integrals
[
19
]
to derive these uniform estimates. This symmetry is crucially absent for other natural priors, e.g., product priors. Thus our arguments do not generalize directly to these settings.

(iii)

In Statistics and Machine Learning, one is often interested in Variational Inference based on the TAP free energy. Specifically, given Theorem
1
, a natural recipe is to start at some appropriate initialization, and subsequently optimize
f_{\text{TAP}}
directly by some out-of-the-box optimization algorithm such as gradient descent. Of course,
f_{\text{TAP}}
is non-convex in general, and thus the algorithm will usually reach a local optimum, rather than the global maximum. This yields a natural estimator, and one naturally wishes to study the statistical performance of this estimator. It would be of interest to characterize this performance. Such results have been achieved recently in the context of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
synchronization problem
[
17
,
11
]
. We expect these insights for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
synchronization to also be useful in the context of Bayesian linear regression.

. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle\geq e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}+O(p\epsilon)}\int_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert X(\beta-a)\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{.}

Equivalently, there is a positive constant
C^{\prime}
, such that

\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}-C^{\prime}\epsilon+\ln\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert X(\beta-a)\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right)\text{.}

Note that for any 2-dimensional linear subspace
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, length of the projection of
\beta
on to
\mathcal{L}
divided by
\sqrt{p}
has density

f_{\text{Proj}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\right)}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-4}{2}}

(see for instance of
[
9
, (2.8)]
). Without loss of generality, we only consider the non-degenerate case, namely when
a
and
X^{\top}(y-Xa)
are not linearly dependent.
Thus when
\epsilon
is small enough,

\frac{1}{p}\ln\operatorname{Vol}(a,\epsilon)\geq\frac{1}{p}\ln\left[\sqrt{2}\epsilon\cdot f_{\beta^{\perp}}\Big(\frac{\lVert a\rVert}{\sqrt{p}}-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)\cdot\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}\pi\lVert a\rVert},1\right\}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)+o(1)\text{.}

Note that the
o(1)
term is uniform over different choices of
a
such that
\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}
. By Theorem
3
, denoting the event on the LHS of (
3
) as
\Omega_{p}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-2(C+C^{\prime})\epsilon+o(1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-2(C+C^{\prime})\epsilon+o(1)\right\}\cap\Omega\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigg(\Bigg\{\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}-C^{\prime}\epsilon+\ln\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert X(\beta-a)\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right),\

\displaystyle\forall a\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}\Bigg\}\cap\Omega\cap\Omega_{p}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega\cap\Omega_{p})\to 1\text{,}

where
C
is the one in statement of Theorem
3
. In other words, since
\epsilon
can be chosen to be arbitrarily small, we have for any
\eta>0
,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\leq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-\eta\right)=0.

∎

2.2
Proof of Theorem
3

Building towards the proof of Theorem
3
, we first introduce the following lemma, which gives an explicit formula for the high-dimensional limit of the free energy in a system without external field. We establish this using a second moment method argument
(see Section
2.3
for
its proof). Note that we use the notation
\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta
instead of
\lVert X\beta\rVert^{2}
in this subsection since we would like to emphasize the fact that our proofs depend strongly on the properties of the sample covariance matrix
X^{\top}X
.

Lemma 1
(High-dimensional limit of free energy: a model without external field).

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\Delta>\Delta_{0}
, as
p\to\infty

\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right)\overset{\text{P}}{\longrightarrow}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta\alpha}\right)\text{.}

Note the left hand side is Lipschitz in
T=\frac{1}{\Delta}
with high probability. Thus for any
\Delta_{0}>0
, we can divide the interval
[0,\frac{1}{\Delta_{0}}]
into a sufficiently fine grid. Lemma
1
holds simultaneously for each grid-point; combining this with the Lipschitz continuity of the free energy in
\frac{1}{\Delta}
, we immediately obtain the following uniform convergence statement.

Lemma 2
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that as
p\to\infty

\sup_{\Delta\geq\Delta_{0}}\left|\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right)-\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta\alpha}\right)\right)\right|\overset{\text{P}}{\longrightarrow}0\text{.}

As the definition of
\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)
in (
9
) suggests, we want to consider integral over the intersection of the sphere and a
p-2
dimensional linear space, which can be formalized into the following uniform convergence statement.

Lemma 3
(High-dimensional limit of free energy without external field: on a linear subspace).

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\Delta>\Delta_{0}
, as
p\to\infty

\sup_{\Delta\geq\Delta_{0},\text{ }u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left|\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})\cap\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}(\beta)\right)-\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{\Delta\alpha}\right)\right)\right|\overset{\text{P}}{\longrightarrow}0\text{,}

where
\operatorname{Span}(u,v)
denotes the linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
generated by
u
and
v
.
Note that
\pi^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}
refers to the uniform measure on
S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})\cap\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}
.

Now we proceed to prove Theorem
3
, assuming the convergence results mentioned above. The proofs of these results are included in Section
2.3
.

Recall the definition of
\Omega
in (
8
). From now on, we only consider
(X,y)\in\Omega
for the rest of this proof. Fix any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
with
\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}
. Let
\hat{\beta}=\beta-a
. Define

H(\hat{\beta})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\Delta}\hat{\beta}^{\top}X^{\top}X\hat{\beta}=\frac{1}{2\Delta}(\beta-a)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\beta-a)\text{.}

(11)

Note that
H
is different from the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
6
). By (
10
),
\lVert X^{\top}(y-Xa)\rVert=O(\sqrt{p})
. By the definition of
\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)
in (
9
), for any
\beta\in\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)
, one has

|a^{\top}\hat{\beta}|=|a^{\top}(\beta-a)|<c^{\prime}p\epsilon

and

|(X^{\top}(y-Xa))^{\top}\hat{\beta}\rangle|=|(y-Xa)^{\top}X(\beta-a)|<c^{\prime}p\epsilon\text{,}

for some positive constant
c^{\prime}
. Therefore

\lVert\hat{\beta}\rVert^{2}=\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}-\lVert a\rVert^{2}-2(\beta-a)^{\top}a=p-\lVert a\rVert^{2}+O(p\epsilon)

Let
\hat{\beta}^{\perp}
be the projection of
\hat{\beta}
onto the hyper-plane
\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}
. For the sake of notation clarity, let
\beta^{\perp}=\sqrt{p}\hat{\beta}^{\perp}/\lVert\hat{\beta}^{\perp}\rVert
and
\gamma=\lVert\hat{\beta}^{\perp}\rVert/\sqrt{p}
.
Note that, again by the definition of
\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)

\lVert\hat{\beta}-\gamma\beta^{\perp}\rVert=\lVert\hat{\beta}-\hat{\beta}^{\perp}\rVert<c^{\prime}\sqrt{p}\epsilon\text{,}

and

\gamma=\gamma_{0}+O(\epsilon)\text{,}

where
\gamma_{0}\coloneqq\sqrt{p-\lVert a\rVert^{2}}/\sqrt{p}
. Therefore,

H(\hat{\beta})=H(\gamma\beta^{\perp})+O(p\epsilon)=H(\gamma_{0}\beta^{\perp})+O(p\epsilon)\text{.}

which is due to the fact that
\lVert\nabla H(\beta)\rVert=\lVert-X^{\top}X\beta/\Delta\rVert\leq(\sqrt{p}/\Delta)\lVert X^{\top}X\rVert_{2}=O(\sqrt{p})
for
(X,y)\in\Omega
. Now we come back to the targeted integral

\int_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(\beta-a)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\beta-a)}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0}}[\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-H(\gamma_{0}\beta^{\perp})+O(p\epsilon)}]\text{.}

Since
\beta^{\perp}
is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere intersected with
\operatorname{Span}(a,X^{\top}(y-Xa))^{\perp}
under
\pi_{0}
and
\gamma
is independent of
\mathbf{1}_{\beta\in\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0}}[\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-H(\gamma_{0}\beta^{\perp})+O(p\epsilon)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{O(p\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0}}[\mathbf{1}_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}]\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p-\lVert a\rVert^{2}})\cap\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi^{\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{O(p\epsilon)}\operatorname{Vol}(a,\epsilon)\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})\cap\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\tilde{\Delta}}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi^{\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{O(p\epsilon)}\operatorname{Vol}(a,\epsilon)\mathbb{E}^{\operatorname{Span}(v_{1},v_{2})^{\perp}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\tilde{\Delta}}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta\right)\right]\text{,}

where

\tilde{\Delta}=\frac{p}{p-\lVert a\rVert^{2}}\Delta\text{.}

Note that for any
a
such that

\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}
, we have
\tilde{\Delta}>\Delta/2
. By Lemma
3
and the fact that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega)\to 1
as
p\to\infty
, we finally get with high probability

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{\operatorname{B}(a,\epsilon)}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(\beta-a)^{\top}X^{\top}X(\beta-a)}d\pi_{0}(\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(\epsilon)+\frac{1}{p}\ln\operatorname{Vol}(a,\epsilon)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln(1+\frac{(1-\lVert a\rVert^{2}/p)}{\Delta\alpha})+o(1)\text{,}

in which the
o(1)
terms are uniform with respect to the choice of
\tilde{\Delta}
, equivalently, with respect to the choice of
a
.
∎

In this subsection, we will prove Lemma

and Lemma

.

2.3.1
Proof of Lemma

Recall that
H(\beta)=H(X,\beta)=(1/{2\Delta})\lVert X\beta\rVert^{2}
, which was first defined in (
11
).
We define the partition function
Z_{p}(\Delta,X)
as

Z_{p}(\Delta,X)\coloneqq\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{.}

which is different from
\mathcal{Z}_{p}
defined in (
5
). Let

\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)\coloneqq\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)=\frac{1}{p}\log\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{0}}[\exp(H(\beta))]=\frac{1}{p}\ln Z_{p}(\Delta,X)

be the
quenched
free energy of the model without external field and

\phi_{p}(\Delta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}[Z_{p}(\Delta,X)]

be the
annealed
free energy.

To prove Lemma
1
, we need the following concentration result about
\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)
.

Lemma 4
.

For any
\Delta>0
,
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
\delta>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
and positive integer
p_{0}
(depending only on
\Delta,\alpha,\delta
) such that for any
p>p_{0}

\mathbb{P}(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)\right|>\delta)\leq e^{-Cp}.

Let
\sigma_{\text{max}}(X)
be the largest singular value of
X
. Define

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\left\{X:\sigma_{\text{max}}(X)<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{n}+t)\right\}.

First, we prove that
\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)
is Lipschitz as a function of
X
on
\mathcal{G}
, with Lipschitz constant
K_{p}=2(1+\alpha^{-1/2}+t/\sqrt{n})/\sqrt{p}
. By triangle inequality, for any
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
,

\displaystyle|H(X_{1},\beta)-H(X_{2},\beta)|

\displaystyle\leq|\beta^{\top}(X_{1}-X_{2})^{\top}X_{2}\beta|+|\beta^{\top}X_{1}^{\top}(X_{1}-X_{2})\beta|

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}\left[\lVert(X_{1}-X_{2})^{\top}X_{2}\rVert_{2}+\lVert X_{1}^{\top}(X_{1}-X_{2})\rVert_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{p}\left[(\sigma_{\text{max}}(X_{1})+\sigma_{\text{max}}(X_{2}))\lVert\sqrt{p}X_{1}-\sqrt{p}X_{2}\rVert_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha^{-1/2}(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{n}+t)\lVert\sqrt{p}X_{1}-\sqrt{p}X_{2}\rVert_{F}\text{,}

which implies

\displaystyle|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X_{1})-\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X_{2})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\left|\ln\frac{\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-H(X_{1},\beta)}d\pi_{0}(\beta)}{\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-H(X_{2},\beta)}d\pi_{0}(\beta)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\left[2\alpha^{-1/2}(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{n}+t)\lVert\sqrt{p}X_{1}-\sqrt{p}X_{2}\rVert_{F}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha^{-1/2}(1+\sqrt{\alpha}+t/\sqrt{p})}{\sqrt{p}}\lVert\sqrt{p}X_{1}-\sqrt{p}X_{2}\rVert_{F}\text{.}

On the other hand, by Lemma
15
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})\leq 2e^{-t^{2}}
.
Note that
\Phi(\Delta,0)=0
and

\mathbb{E}\left[\Phi_{p}^{2}(\Delta,X)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sigma^{2}_{\text{max}}(X)\right]<\infty\text{.}

Therefore, by Lemma
14
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that for any
r>6(C+\sqrt{npK_{p}})\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})}
we have

\mathbb{P}(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)\right|>r)\leq 2e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{16K_{p}^{2}}}+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})\text{.}

Now let
\delta>0
be any fixed constant. Let
t=\sqrt{p}
. Then there exists
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
p>p_{0}
,
\delta>6(C+\sqrt{npK_{p}})\sqrt{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})}
and

\mathbb{P}(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)\right|>\delta)\leq 2e^{-C_{1}\delta^{2}p}+2e^{-p}\leq e^{-Cp}\text{,}

which concludes the proof of lemma
4
.
∎

Concerning large-p behaviour of
\phi_{p}(\Delta)
, we have the following characterization.

Lemma 5
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
\Delta\in(0,\infty)
. One has

\lim_{p\to\infty}\phi_{p}(\Delta,X)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln(1+\frac{1}{\Delta\alpha}).

We compute the LHS explicitly, namely

\displaystyle\phi_{p}(\Delta,X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{X}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\operatorname{tr}(X^{\top}X\beta\beta^{\top})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}_{\beta}\mathbb{E}_{X}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}(X^{\top}X)_{11}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}_{X}e^{-\frac{p}{2\Delta}(X^{\top}X)_{11}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathbb{E}_{V\sim\chi^{2}(n)}e^{-\frac{p}{2\Delta n}V}\text{.}

Note that moment generating function of
\chi^{2}
exists for any
t<\frac{1}{2}
, for any
\Delta>0
we have

\phi_{p}(\Delta,X)=\frac{1}{p}\ln((1+\frac{p}{\Delta n})^{-n/2})\to-\frac{\alpha}{2}\ln(1+\frac{1}{\Delta\alpha})\text{.}

∎A point worth noting is that there is no non-trivial restriction on the value of
\Delta
. Now we proceed to use second moment method to show
\Phi(\Delta,X)
also converges to the same quantity. Let
\sigma
and
\beta
be independent samples from the prior distribution
\pi_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}H(\sigma)}\mathbb{E}_{\beta}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}H(\beta)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(H(\sigma)+H(\beta))}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\operatorname{tr}\left\{X^{\top}X\left(\sigma\sigma^{\top}+\beta\beta^{\top}\right)\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\left\{(p+\beta^{\top}\sigma)(X^{\top}X)_{11}+(p-\beta^{\top}\sigma)(X^{\top}X)_{22}\right\}\right)\text{,}

where the last line follows from
\operatorname{rank}(\sigma\sigma^{\top}+\beta\beta^{\top})=2
and in fact its non-zero eigenvalues are
\lambda_{1}=p+\beta^{\top}\sigma
and
\lambda_{2}=p-\beta^{\top}\sigma
. For
i=1,2
, let
V_{i}=(X^{\top}X)_{ii}
. Note that
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
are iid with
V_{i}\sim\frac{1}{n}\chi^{2}(n)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{V}\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\left\{(p+\beta^{\top}\sigma)V_{1}+(p-\beta^{\top}\sigma)V_{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{V}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(\lambda_{1}V_{1}+\lambda_{2}V_{2})\right)\text{.}

Again, using MGF of
\chi^{2}
distribution, we further have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\mathbb{E}_{V}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}(\lambda_{1}V_{1}+\lambda_{2}V_{2})\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\left\{(1+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\Delta n})^{-n/2}(1+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\Delta n})^{-n/2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\left\{\left[(1+\frac{p}{\Delta n})^{2}-(\frac{\beta^{\top}\sigma}{\Delta n})^{2}\right]^{-n/2}\right\}\text{.}

Let

\gamma_{0}=\frac{4\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})}{(\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)))^{2}}\text{.}

In order to apply the second moment method, we first establish the following characterization of
\gamma_{0}
.

Lemma 6
.

Fix
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\Delta>\Delta_{0}
,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}\ln\gamma_{0}=0\text{.}

Due to symmetry,
\beta^{\top}\sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\Sigma_{i=1}^{p}\beta_{i}
, equivalently, we can assume
\sigma=(1,1,\dots,1)
without loss of generality. By Lemma
16
, letting
Q=\beta^{\top}\sigma/p\in(-1,1)

\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\sigma}(|Q|>t)<e^{\pi-pt^{2}/4}\text{.}

Armed with this deviation bound, we derive an upper bound on
\mathbb{E}_{X}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})
.
Setting
r=p/(\Delta n)
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta,\sigma}\left\{\left[(1+\frac{p}{\Delta n})^{2}-(\frac{\beta^{\top}\sigma}{\Delta n})^{2}\right]^{-n/2}\right\}

(13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left\{\left[(1+r)^{2}-(Qr)^{2}\right]^{-n/2}\right\}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[e^{pf(Q)}\right]\text{,}

where
f(t)\coloneqq-(\alpha/2)\ln\left[(1+r)^{2}-(tr)^{2}\right]
. Let
g(t)=f(t)-t^{2}/4

Note that
{g}^{\prime}(0)=0

\displaystyle{g}^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{\alpha r^{2}\left[(1+r)^{2}+t^{2}r^{2}\right]}{\left[(1+r)^{2}-t^{2}r^{2}\right]^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}<{g}^{\prime\prime}(1)=:-C_{Q}\text{,}

(14)

for any

. Note that

, and thus
C_{Q}>0

\Delta>0

We have
g(t)\leq-C_{Q}t^{2}/2+g(0)=-C_{Q}t^{2}/2-\alpha\ln(1+r)
.
By (
12
), integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{p}(\Delta,X)^{2})

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}e^{pf(t)}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\sigma}(|Q|>t)

\displaystyle\leq-\left[e^{pf(t)}\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\sigma}(|Q|>t)\Big|_{t=0}^{t=1}-\int_{0}^{1}\left[e^{pf(t)}\right]^{\prime}e^{\pi-t^{2}/4}\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq-e^{pf(0)}+e^{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{p\alpha r^{2}t}{(1+r)^{2}-t^{2}r^{2}}e^{pg(t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq-e^{pf(0)}+e^{\pi}\frac{p\alpha r^{2}}{(1+r)^{2}-r^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{pg(t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq e^{\pi-p\alpha\ln(1+r)}\left[1+Cp\int_{0}^{1}e^{-pC_{Q}t^{2}/2}\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle\leq e^{\pi-p\alpha\ln(1+r)}(C\sqrt{n}+1)\text{.}

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\ln\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\ln\left\{\frac{4e^{\pi}(1+r)^{-n}(C\sqrt{n}+1)}{(1+r)^{-n}}\right\}\to 0\text{,}

(15)

as
p\to\infty
.
∎For the readers’ convenience, we re-state

, Lemma 4.1.1]
here.

Lemma 7
.

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\phi_{p}(\Delta)\right|<\frac{1}{p}\ln\gamma_{0}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\text{.}

Now we are finally ready to prove Lemma

.

Putting Lemma
4
, Lemma
7
, and Lemma
6

\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists a large

such that for any

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)\right|\leq\delta\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\phi_{p}(\Delta)\right|<q_{p}\right)\geq 1-e^{Cp}+e^{q_{p}p}>1\text{,}

where

as

\left|\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\phi_{p}(\Delta)\right|\leq\delta\text{,}

which further implies

\limsup_{p\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}\Phi_{p}(\Delta,X)-\phi_{p}(\Delta)\right|\leq\delta\text{.}

Since it holds for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, proof of Lemma

is therefore complete.
∎

Now we establish Lemma

based on Lemma

.

For any

, let

be an orthonormal basis such that
\operatorname{Span}(u,v)=\operatorname{Span}(w_{p-1},w_{p})
. Let
\{\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{p-2}\}
be the eigenvalues of
Q
, where Q is the top left
(p-2)
by
(p-2)
minor of
X^{\top}X
under this basis.
Namely, let

,

and
\beta=W\tilde{\beta}
, where
\tilde{\beta}=(\tilde{\beta}_{1},\tilde{\beta}_{2},\dots,\tilde{\beta}_{p})
, with
\tilde{\beta}_{p}=\tilde{\beta}_{p-1}=0
for

. Equivalently,
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{\beta}
are nothing but

and

under this new basis and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\beta}^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}[\exp(-H(\beta))]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta}^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\beta^{\top}X^{\top}X\beta\right)]

(16)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta}^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\tilde{\beta}^{\top}\tilde{X}^{\top}\tilde{X}\tilde{\beta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\beta}^{\operatorname{Span}(u,v)^{\perp}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\tilde{\beta}_{1:(p-2)}^{\top}Q\tilde{\beta}_{1:(p-2)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{p-2}_{\tilde{\beta}_{1:(p-2)}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}\lambda_{i}\tilde{\beta}_{i}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{p-2}_{\beta}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{p-2}\lambda_{i}\beta_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\text{,}

where

denotes expectation under
\operatorname{Unif}(S^{(p-3)}(\sqrt{p}))
. Note that the second last line is due to the distributional invariance of
\tilde{\beta}
under rotations, which itself is inherited from that of
\beta\sim\operatorname{Unif}(S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p}))
and the last line above is nothing but a change of notation.
Similarly, let

(p-2)
by
(p-2)

when written in the standard basis

(18)

Therefore almost surely

3
A matching upper bound on the free energy

We derive an upper bound to the TAP free energy in terms of the log-partition function in this section. We collect this bound in Theorem
4
below.
To establish this result, we first introduce a model perturbation and discuss overlap concentration under the perturbed model in Section
3.1
. These results are subsequently used to establish Theorem
4
in Section

.

Theorem 4
(Upper bound).

Fix
\Delta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.
For any
\eta>0
as
p\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)+\eta\right)\to 0\text{.}

3.1
Perturbed model and overlap concentration

Assume that apart from
X
and
y
, we observe the following additional side information

y^{\text{Pert}}=\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}\beta_{0}+Z\text{,}

with
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{p})
,
\lambda_{0}\in[1/2,1]
,
\epsilon_{p}\to 0
, and
p\epsilon_{p}\to+\infty
. We refer to
(y^{\text{Pert}},y,X)
as the perturbed model.
Having observed
(y^{\text{Pert}},y,X)
, one constructs the posterior distribution

\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\pi_{0}}(\beta|y^{\text{Pert}},y,X)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{\text{Pert}}}e^{-\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta)},

(21)

where
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Pert}}:=\mathcal{H}_{p}(\beta)+\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}(\beta)
is the
Hamiltonian
of the perturbed model,
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}(\beta)\coloneqq-\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta-\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}Z^{\top}\beta+\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}/2
is the Hamiltonian of the Gaussian side channel.
The normalizing constant of this posterior distribution is

\mathcal{Z}^{\text{Pert}}_{p}\coloneqq\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta)}d\pi_{0}(\beta)\text{.}

(22)

Finally, the free energy of the perturbed model is

F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda_{0})\coloneqq\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(y^{\text{Pert}},y,X)\text{.}

(23)

In the subsequent discussion, we abuse notation slightly, and use
\langle\cdot\rangle
to also denote the expectation under the perturbed posterior (
21
).
The notion of overlap will play a crucial role in our subsequent discussion.

Definition
1
.

Let
\beta^{1}
,
\beta^{2}
be two iid samples from the posterior distribution (
21
). We refer to
R_{12}:=(\beta^{1})^{\top}\beta^{2}/p
as the

between two replicas.

Theorem 5
.

For any
\Delta>0
and
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, as
p\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{p}\left|\mathbb{E}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{\textrm{Pert}}-\mathbb{E}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\right|\leq O(\epsilon_{p})\text{.}

Furthermore, assuming
\lambda_{0}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\frac{1}{2},1)
, there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon_{p}}\Big(\frac{v_{p}}{p\epsilon_{p}}+\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{1/3}\text{,}

(24)

where
v_{p}\coloneqq p\sup_{\lambda_{0}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\{\mathbb{E}(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda_{0})-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda_{0}))^{2}\}
and
v_{p}<C_{2}
for any

.

In our application of Theorem
5
we will choose
\epsilon_{p}\to 0
slow enough such that the RHS in (

) converges to zero as
p\to\infty
. Our next result characterizes the limiting behavior of
\mathbb{E}[\langle R_{1,2}\rangle]
.

Lemma 8
.

For any
\Delta,\alpha>0
, as
p\to\infty
,
there exists
C>0
depending on
\alpha
and
\Delta
such that

1-C\geq\limsup_{p\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\langle R_{1,2}\rangle]\geq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\langle R_{1,2}\rangle]\geq 0\text{.}

Note that
\mathbb{E}[\langle R_{1,2}\rangle]\in[-1,1]
—thus any subsequence has a further subsequence such that
\mathbb{E}[\langle R_{1,2}\rangle]
converges to a limit along the subsequence. Further, Lemma

implies that this subsequential limit lies in
[0,1-C]
.
Armed with these results, we relate the log-partition function to that of a
replicated
system, where the overlaps have been restricted to have a specific value.

Lemma 9
.

For
m\geq 1
and
c>0
, define

\mathcal{S}_{p}(m,c)=\left\{(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m}):\left|\frac{1}{p}(\beta^{i})^{\top}\beta^{j}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle\right|\leq c,\forall i\neq j\right\}\text{.}

(25)

There exists
c_{p}\to 0
and
m_{p}\to\infty
, such that

\frac{1}{pm_{p}}\ln\left(\int e^{-\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}(\beta^{i})}\mathbf{1}_{\{(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m_{p}})\in\mathcal{S}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta^{i})\right)=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}+o(1).

In the equation above and henceforth, we suppress the dependence of
\mathcal{S}
on
p
,
c_{p}
and
m_{p}
for the sake of notational simplicity.

Using Theorem
5
, we can choose a sequence
\{\lambda_{0}^{p}:p\geq 1\}
such that for any
p\geq 1
we have

\mathbb{E}\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle\leq a_{p}\text{,}

where
a_{p}\to 0
as
p\to\infty
. For any positive sequence
\{b_{p}:p\geq 1\}
, Markov inequality yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle>b_{p}\right)\leq\frac{a_{p}}{b_{p}}\text{.}

We choose
\{b_{p}:p\geq 1\}
such that
b_{p}\to 0
,
a_{p}/b_{p}\to 0
. Let
m_{p}\geq 1
(to be chosen later) and let
\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m_{p}}
be iid samples from the posterior distribution (
21
). Define
R_{i,j}=(\beta^{i})^{\top}\beta^{j}/p
for

to be the overlaps among the samples. By symmetry, we have
\langle R_{1,2}\rangle=\langle R_{i,j}\rangle
and
\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle R_{i,j}\rangle
for any

. Define the event
\mathcal{C}_{p}\coloneqq\{(y^{\text{Pert}},y,X):\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle>b_{p}\}

. On
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{c}

\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle\leq b_{p}\text{.}

Using Chebychev inequality, we have, on

By union bound, on
\mathcal{C}_{p}^{c}

\langle\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}\rangle\leq\sum_{i\neq j}\langle\mathbf{1}_{\{\left|R_{i,j}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{i,j}\rangle\right|>c_{p}\}}\rangle=\sum_{i\neq j}\langle\mathbf{1}_{\{\left|R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle\right|>c_{p}\}}\rangle\leq\frac{m_{p}^{2}b_{p}}{c_{p}^{2}}\text{.}

We choose

and

as

. This implies, on

1\geq\langle\mathbf{1}_{S}\rangle=\frac{1}{(\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{\text{Pert}})^{m_{p}}}\int e^{-\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta^{i})}\mathbf{1}_{\{(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m_{p}})\in\mathcal{S}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}}d\pi_{0}(\beta^{i})\geq 1-\frac{m_{p}^{2}b_{p}}{c_{p}^{2}}=1-o(1)\text{.}

(26)

, we have with high probability with respect to

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{H}(\beta)-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathcal{H}^{\text{Gauss}}(\beta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta\right|+\left|\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}Z^{\top}\beta\right|+\left|\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}\right|

This implies that with high probability with respect to

\left|\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}^{\text{Pert}}-\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\right|\leq O(\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}})\lambda_{0}p\text{,}

(27)

and

(28)

Putting (

,
27

\frac{1}{pm_{p}}\ln\left(\int e^{-\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}(\beta^{i})}\mathbf{1}_{\{(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m_{p}})\in\mathcal{S}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta^{i})\right)=\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}+O(\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}})\text{.}

Recalling
\epsilon_{p}\to 0
as

, the claim follows.
∎

3.2
Proof of Theorem

We prove Theorem

in this section. To this end, recall that
\mathcal{H}(\beta)=-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}
. Let
\beta^{1},\cdots,\beta^{m_{p}}
be iid samples from the posterior distribution, and set

to be the sample average. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\lVert y-X\beta^{i}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{m_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\lVert X(\beta^{i}-a)\rVert^{2}-\frac{2}{m_{p}}(X^{\top}(y-Xa))^{\top}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}(\beta^{i}-a)

\displaystyle=\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{m_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\lVert X(\beta^{i}-a)\rVert^{2}\text{,}

since
\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}(\beta^{i}-a)=0
. Thus with high probability,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{pm_{p}}\ln\left(\int e^{-\frac{m_{p}}{2\Delta}\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\lVert X(\beta^{i}-a)\rVert^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{(\beta^{1},\beta^{2},\dots,\beta^{m_{p}})\in\mathcal{S}\}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{p}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta^{i})\right)+o(1)

(29)

Our next result, Lemma

Lemma 10
.

,

assuming Lemma

p_{k}

Lemma 12

\delta
) such that with high probability,
\sup_{\delta^{\prime}\in[-1,\delta]}f_{p}(\delta^{\prime})>-C^{\prime}
. We now proceed as follows: fix any
\delta_{0}<1
, and assume
\sup_{\delta^{\prime}\in[-1,\delta_{0}]}f_{p}(\delta^{\prime})>-C_{\delta_{0}}
. On the other hand, let
\delta_{1}<1
be such that with high probability,
\sup_{\delta^{\prime}\in[\delta_{1},1]}f_{p}(\delta^{\prime})<-2C_{\delta_{0}}
. Thus with high-probability,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}(\beta_{1}>\delta_{1}\sqrt{p})\rangle=\frac{\int_{\delta_{1}}^{1}\exp(pf_{p}(\delta^{\prime}))d\delta^{\prime}}{\int_{-1}^{1}\exp(pf_{p}(\delta^{\prime}))d\delta^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\int_{\delta_{1}}^{1}\exp(pf_{p}(\delta^{\prime}))d\delta^{\prime}}{\int_{-1}^{\delta_{0}}\exp(pf_{p}(\delta^{\prime}))d\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\delta_{1})\exp(-2C_{\delta_{0}}p)}{(\delta_{0}+1)\exp(-pC_{\delta_{0}})}\leq\frac{1-\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{0}}\exp(-pC_{\delta_{0}}).

Thus with probability
1-o(1)
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}(\beta_{1}<\delta_{1}\sqrt{p})\rangle>1-\frac{1-\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{0}}\exp(-pC_{\delta_{0}}).

(44)

This implies, with probability at least
1-o(1)
,

\displaystyle\langle\beta_{1}\rangle\leq\delta_{1}\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{p}\cdot\frac{1-\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{0}}\exp(-pC_{\delta_{0}}).

Finally, this implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\beta_{1}\rangle]\leq\delta_{1}\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{p}\cdot\frac{1-\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{0}}\exp(-pC_{\delta_{0}})+\sqrt{p}o(1)=(\delta_{1}+o(1))\sqrt{p}.

The required upper bound follows upon setting
C:=(1-\delta_{1})/2
.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem

from Theorem

within Theorem

First, the upper bound, namely

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{p}\ln\mathcal{Z}_{p}\geq\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)+\eta\right)\to 0\text{,}

follows immediately from Theorem

.
For the matching lower bound, for any

,
\|a\|\leq\sqrt{p}
, define

a^{\star}\coloneqq\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert x\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}}\lVert a-x\rVert,

then

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\lVert a\rVert\leq(1-c)\sqrt{p}}f_{\text{TAP}}(a)

(45)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{0,\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\left(f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-f_{\text{TAP}}(a^{\star})\right)\right\}\text{,}

the second term of which can be further controlled by

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\left(f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-f_{\text{TAP}}(a^{\star})\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2\Delta p}\left|\lVert y-Xa\rVert^{2}-\lVert y-Xa^{\star}\rVert^{2}\right|

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\lVert a\rVert^{2}/p}{\Delta\alpha}\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\lVert a^{\star}\rVert^{2}/p}{\Delta\alpha}\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)-\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a^{\star}\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2\Delta p}\left[\left|2y^{\top}X(a-a^{\star})\right|+\left|a^{\top}X^{\top}Xa-(a^{\star})^{\top}X^{\top}Xa^{\star}\right|\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\Big\{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\lVert a^{\star}\rVert^{2}/p}{\Delta\alpha}\right)-\ln\left(1+\frac{1-\lVert a\rVert^{2}/p}{\Delta\alpha}\right)\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)-\ln\left(1-\frac{\lVert a^{\star}\rVert^{2}}{p}\right)\right]\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\Delta p}\left[2c\sqrt{p}\lVert y^{\top}X\rVert+2p(2c-c^{2})\lVert X^{\top}X\rVert_{2}\right]+\tau(c)\right\}\text{,}

where

as a deterministic function, goes to

as

.

\lVert X^{\top}X\rVert_{2}=O(1)

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\left(f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-f_{\text{TAP}}(a^{\star})\right)\to 0

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{p},(1-c)\sqrt{p}\leq\lVert a\rVert\leq\sqrt{p}}\left(f_{\text{TAP}}(a)-f_{\text{TAP}}(a^{\star})\right)\leq\frac{\eta}{3}\text{.}

(46)

Let

In this section, we list some well-known facts about the random matrix

C.1
Proof of Theorem

First of all, please note that in this subsection we will use
\mathbb{E}
as a shorthand for
\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z,\beta_{0}}
. Recall that
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}
is the Hamiltonian of the side information channel, namely

\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}=-\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta-\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}Z^{\top}\beta+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}\text{,}

which can be seen as only keeping the
\beta
-related terms in
\lVert Y^{\text{Gauss}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}\beta\rVert^{2}/2
. Let
\lambda_{0,p}=\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}
. Furthermore, let

H^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}=-\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta-\frac{Z^{\top}\beta}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}+\frac{\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}}{2}\text{.}

(47)

We begin the proof of overlap concentration, i.e. Theorem
5
, by introducing the following lemma, which upper bounds the fluctuations of
R_{1,2}
by the fluctuations of
\mathcal{L}\coloneqq H^{\prime}/p
.

Lemma 18
.

Let

\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\frac{H^{\prime}}{p}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\text{Gauss}}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\text{.}

Then

\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\text{.}

Let
R_{1,*}=\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta/p
. Using the definition of
H^{\prime}
gives

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left\langle R_{1,*}\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-2\left\langle R_{1,*}^{2}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{p\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\left\langle R_{1,*}Z^{\top}\beta\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{p}\left\langle\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-2\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{p\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}Z^{\top}\langle\beta\rangle\right]\text{.}

Gaussian integration by parts implies

\frac{1}{p\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}Z^{\top}\beta\right\rangle=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\beta^{\top}\langle\beta\rangle\right\rangle=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle^{2}\right]\text{,}

in which
\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\beta^{\top}\langle\beta\rangle\right\rangle=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta\beta^{\top}\rangle\langle\beta\rangle\right]=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta^{1}(\beta^{1})^{\top}\beta^{2}\rangle\right]=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle(\beta^{1})^{\top}\beta_{0}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta^{2}\rangle\right]=p\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle^{2}\right]
is due to the Nishimori identity. Again, using Gaussian integration by parts

\frac{1}{p\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\mathbb{E}\langle Z^{\top}\beta\rangle=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle\text{.}

Putting them together, we have

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}^{2}\rangle+(\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}^{2}\rangle-(\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle)^{2})-(\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}^{2}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle^{2})

\displaystyle\leq-\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0\text{.}

Finally, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)\right\rangle|

\displaystyle=2|\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,*}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle)(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)\right\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\{\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,*}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\right\}^{1/2}\text{,}

which implies

\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle(R_{1,*}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,*}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\text{.}

∎Since the above lemma basically says the fluctuations of the overlaps are upper bounded by those of
\mathcal{L}
, it is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{L}
will concentrate. Before moving on, let us recall a few notations. As in (
21
),
\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}
is the Hamiltonian of the whole perturbed model, i.e. including both the original model and the side information channel, and
F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}
is the corresponding free energy, as defined in (
23
).
Moreover, let

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\text{.}

Please note
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
defined in (
47

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Gauss}}
does not depend on
\lambda_{0,p}
.
Armed with these notations, we observe,

\frac{\mathrm{d}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}=-\frac{1}{p}\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle,\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}^{2}}=\frac{1}{p}\left\{\left\langle\left(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}-\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\frac{\langle\beta\rangle^{\top}Z}{4\lambda_{0,p}^{3/2}}\right\}\text{.}

Exchanging expectation and derivatives, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}=-\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,2}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}^{2}}=\frac{1}{p}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left\langle\left(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}-\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{0,p}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\|\beta-\langle\beta\rangle\|^{2}\right\rangle\right\},

in which we have utilized both Nishimori identity and the following identity obtained using Gaussian integration by parts

\frac{1}{p\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\mathbb{E}\langle Z^{\top}\beta\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle\|\beta\|^{2}\right\rangle-\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,*}\right\rangle\text{.}

Lemma 19
(Fluctuations of
\mathcal{L}
).

Let
\lambda_{0}\sim\operatorname{Unif}[1/2,1]
. If
\frac{v_{p}}{p\epsilon_{p}}\to 0
then there exists a constant

such that

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\leq\frac{C}{\epsilon_{p}}(\frac{v_{p}}{p\epsilon_{p}}+\frac{1}{p})^{1/3}\text{.}

To prove this lemma, we consider the following decomposition and split our proof into controlling these two terms respectively,

\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle+\mathbb{E}(\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\text{.}

We start with the first term. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}-\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}\left(p\frac{\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}^{2}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}^{2}}+\frac{p}{\lambda_{0,p}}\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}=p\left(\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\right|_{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}/2}^{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}}\right)+p\ln 2\text{.}

Since

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\right|=\frac{1}{p}|\mathbb{E}\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle|=\left|\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,2}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\text{,}

For any
\lambda_{0,p}
, we naturally have a trivial bound of
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\right|_{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}/2}^{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}}

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\right|_{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}/2}^{\lambda_{0,p}=\varepsilon_{p}}\leq 1\text{.}

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{H^{\prime}}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{H^{\prime}}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle\leq\frac{1+\ln 2}{p}\text{.}

\quad\widetilde{F}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)=\left(F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)\right)-\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}A_{p}\text{,}

Thus Lemma
17

|\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle|\leq\delta^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left(\left|F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(u)-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(u)\right|+\left|A_{p}\right|\sqrt{u}\right)+C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)+\frac{\left|A_{p}\right|}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\text{,}

where

and

Note that by using
[

, there exists a positive constant

such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\lVert Z\rVert-\mathbb{E}\lVert Z\rVert\right)^{2}\right]\leq C_{Z}
independently of

in the statement of Theorem

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\leq 9

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\{\delta^{-2}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left[\left|F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(u)-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{pert }}(u)\right|^{2}+(\left|A_{p}\right|\sqrt{u})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\left|A_{p}\right|}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\right)^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 9\left\{\frac{3}{p\delta^{2}}[v_{p}+(\lambda_{0,p}+\delta)C_{Z}]+C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+\frac{C_{Z}}{4p\lambda_{0,p}}\right\}\text{.}

Note that

|\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)|=\left|\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}R_{1,2}+\frac{\mathbb{E}\lVert Z\rVert}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}p}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{0,p}}}\right)\text{,}

and thus

\left|C_{\delta}^{\pm}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)\right|\leq 1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\text{,}

for

, which further gives us

\begin{array}[]{r}\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}d\lambda_{0,p}\left\{C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}\right\}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\right)\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}d\lambda_{0,p}\left\{C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)\right\}\\
=\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\right)\left[\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}/2+\delta\right)-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta\right)\right)+\left(\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\varepsilon_{p}-\delta\right)-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\varepsilon_{p}+\delta\right)\right)\right]\text{.}\end{array}

By the mean value theorem,

|\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}/2+\delta\right)-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta\right)|\leq 2\delta\max_{x\in[\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta,\epsilon_{p}/2+\delta]}{\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}^{\prime}(x)}\leq\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\right).

Similarly,

|\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}+\delta\right)-\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}\left(\epsilon_{p}-\delta\right)|\leq 2\delta\max_{x\in[\epsilon_{p}-\delta,\epsilon_{p}+\delta]}{\mathbb{E}\widetilde{F}^{\prime}(x)}\leq\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}-\delta}}\right)\leq\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\right).

Therefore

\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}\left\{C_{\delta}^{+}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}+C_{\delta}^{-}\left(\lambda_{0,p}\right)^{2}\right\}\leq 2\delta\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}}\right)^{2}\leq 4\delta\frac{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta+1}{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}\leq\frac{8\delta}{\epsilon_{p}/2-\delta}

where we set
\delta=\delta_{p}
so that
{\delta_{p}}/{\epsilon_{p}}\to 0
and
{\delta_{p}}/{\epsilon_{p}}<1/4
for any
p
.
Putting them all together, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}\mathbb{E}(\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{27\epsilon_{p}}{2p\delta_{p}^{2}}(v_{p}+2\epsilon_{p}C_{Z})+9\int_{\epsilon_{p}/2}^{\epsilon_{p}}\frac{C_{Z}}{4p\lambda_{0,p}}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}+\frac{288\delta_{p}}{\epsilon_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{27\epsilon_{p}}{2p\delta_{p}^{2}}(v_{p}+2\epsilon_{p}C_{Z})+\frac{288\delta_{p}}{\epsilon_{p}}+\frac{9C_{Z}\ln 2}{4p}\text{.}

Finally, we choose
\delta_{p}=\left\{\left[3\epsilon_{p}^{2}(v_{p}+2\epsilon_{p}C_{Z})\right]/(64p)\right\}^{1/3}
to optimize the bound, which together with the previous bound on
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle
would result in

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left\langle(\mathcal{L}-\mathbb{E}\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{576}{\epsilon_{p}}\left(\frac{3\epsilon_{p}^{2}(v_{p}+2\epsilon_{p}C_{Z})}{64p}\right)^{1/3}+\frac{9C_{Z}\ln 2}{4p}+\frac{1+\ln 2}{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{v_{p}}{p\epsilon_{p}}+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{1/3}\text{,}

for some positive constant
C
.
∎Combining Lemma
18
and Lemma
19
gives us the following lemma, which is essentially the first half of Theorem
5
.

Lemma 20
(Overlap concentration: Fluctuations of
R_{1,2}
).

Assuming
\lambda_{0}\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\frac{1}{2},1)
, there exist a positive constant
C
such that

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda_{0}}\mathbb{E}\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle)^{2}\rangle\leq C\Big(\frac{v_{p}}{p\epsilon_{p}}+\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{1/3}\text{,}

where
v_{p}\coloneqq p\sup_{\lambda_{0}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}\{\mathbb{E}(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda_{0})-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda_{0}))^{2}\}
.

C.2
Concentration of the free energy with respect to the Gaussian quenched variables

Lemma 21
.

There exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
(depending only on
\Delta,\alpha
) such that for any
r\geq O(e^{-C_{1}p^{1/2}})
, we have for any fixed
\beta_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}\in[1/2,1]
,

\mathbb{P}(\frac{1}{p}|F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}-\mathbb{E}[F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}|\beta_{0}]|\geq r|\beta_{0})\leq e^{-C_{2}r^{2}p^{1/2}}\text{.}

Consider

\mathcal{G}=\{X,z,Z:\max_{\mu}\left|z_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{D_{1}}\ \cap\ \max_{i}\left|Z_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{D_{1}}\ \cap\ \text{ for all }\beta,\beta_{0}:\|X(\beta-\beta_{0})\|^{2}\leq D_{2}p\}\text{.}

In order to use the known results regarding concentration of almost Lipschitz functions, we first try to control
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})
. Since if
U\sim N(0,1)
then
\mathbb{P}(|U|\geq\sqrt{A})\leq 4e^{-A/4}
, we have by union bound

\mathbb{P}(\max_{\mu}\left|z_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{D_{1}}\ \cap\ \max_{i}\left|\widetilde{z}_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{D_{1}})\leq 4p(1+\alpha)e^{-D_{1}/4}\text{,}

and

\mathbb{P}(\text{for all }\beta,\beta_{0}:\|X(\beta-\beta_{0})\|^{2}\leq D_{2}p)=\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{1}(X^{\top}X)\leq D_{2}/4)\text{.}

Thus

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})\leq 4p(1+\alpha)e^{-D_{1}/4}+\left(1-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma_{\text{max}}(X)^{2}\leq\frac{D_{2}}{4}\right)\right)\text{,}

which will be made small with a suitable choices of
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
.
Now we proceed to show, for any fixed
\beta_{0}
, free energy of the perturbed model
F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(X,z,Z,\beta_{0})
is Lipschitz on
\mathcal{G}
with respect to
(X,z,Z)
, as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{np+n+p}
. From now on, we treat
\beta_{0}
as fixed and omit it in the arguments of
\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}
and
F^{\text{Pert}}
. Recall that, as defined in (
23
),

\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(X,z,Z)=\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}-\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta-\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}Z^{\top}\beta+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}\text{.}

Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(X,z,Z)-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{2\Delta}\left|\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z\rVert^{2}-\lVert X^{\prime}(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z^{\prime}\rVert^{2}\right|

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\lambda\epsilon_{p}}\left|Z^{\top}\beta-(Z^{\prime})^{\top}\beta\right|\text{,}

in which

\displaystyle\left|\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z\rVert^{2}-\lVert X^{\prime}(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z^{\prime}\rVert^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)\rVert^{2}+2z^{\top}X(\beta_{0}-\beta)+\lVert z\rVert^{2}\right)-\left(\lVert X^{\prime}(\beta_{0}-\beta)\rVert^{2}+2z^{\prime\top}X^{\prime}(\beta_{0}-\beta)+\lVert z^{\prime}\rVert^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(\beta-\beta_{0})^{\top}(X^{\top}X-X^{\prime\top}X^{\prime})(\beta-\beta_{0})|+2|(z^{\top}X-z^{\prime\top}X^{\prime})(\beta-\beta_{0})|+|\lVert z\rVert^{2}-\lVert z^{\prime}\rVert^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lVert\beta-\beta_{0}\rVert_{2}^{2}\left(\lVert X^{\top}(X-X^{\prime})\rVert+\lVert(X-X^{\prime})^{\top}X^{\prime}\rVert\right)+(O(\sqrt{pD_{1}})+O(\sqrt{pD_{2}}))(\sqrt{p}\lVert X-X^{\prime}\rVert_{F}+\lVert z-z^{\prime}\rVert)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(O(\sqrt{pD_{1}})+O(\sqrt{pD_{2}}))(\sqrt{p}\lVert X-X^{\prime}\rVert_{F}+\lVert z-z^{\prime}\rVert)\text{,}

and

\displaystyle\left|Z^{\top}\beta-(Z^{\prime})^{\top}\beta\right|\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}p}\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert\text{.}

Since
\lambda_{0}\in[1/2,1]
and
\lim_{p\to\infty}\epsilon_{p}=0
, we get, as long as both
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are always lower bounded by a positive constant,

|\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(X,z,Z)-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})|\leq(O(\sqrt{pD_{1}})+O(\sqrt{pD_{2}}))(\sqrt{p}\lVert X-X^{\prime}\rVert_{F}+\lVert z-z^{\prime}\rVert+\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert)\text{.}

For the difference of free energies, we thus have

\displaystyle F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(X,z,Z)-F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\frac{\int\mathrm{d}\beta\pi_{0}(\beta)e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y)}}{\int\mathrm{d}\beta\pi_{0}(\beta)e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime})}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\ln\left(\frac{\int\mathrm{d}\beta\pi_{0}(\beta)e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime})+|\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y)-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime})|}}{\int\mathrm{d}\beta\pi_{0}(\beta)e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}_{\lambda}(y^{\prime})}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}(O(\sqrt{pD_{1}})+O(\sqrt{pD_{2}}))(\sqrt{p}\lVert X-X^{\prime}\rVert_{F}+\lVert z-z^{\prime}\rVert+\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert)\text{.}

By symmetry, we can further conclude that

\displaystyle\left|F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(X,z,Z)-F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq(O(p^{-1/2}\sqrt{D_{1}})+O(p^{-1/2}\sqrt{D_{2}}))(\sqrt{p}\lVert X-X^{\prime}\rVert_{F}+\lVert z-z^{\prime}\rVert+\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert)

\displaystyle\leq(O(p^{-1/2}\sqrt{D_{1}})+O(p^{-1/2}\sqrt{D_{2}}))\lVert(\sqrt{p}X,z,Z)-(\sqrt{p}X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})\rVert\text{,}

where with a slight abuse of notation both
(\sqrt{p}X,z,Z)
and
(\sqrt{p}X^{\prime},z^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
are treated as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{np+n+p}
. In order to apply Lemma
14
, the last thing we need to show is that
\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}\left[\left(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\right)^{2}\big|\beta_{0}\right]
is bounded. In fact, the upper bound constant does not depend on
\beta_{0}
.

\displaystyle\left|F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert y-X\beta\rVert^{2}-\lambda\epsilon_{p}\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta-\sqrt{\lambda\epsilon_{p}}Z^{\top}\beta+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z\rVert^{2}-\lambda\epsilon_{p}\left|\beta_{0}^{\top}\beta\right|-\sqrt{\lambda\epsilon_{p}}\left|Z^{\top}\beta\right|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda\epsilon_{p}\lVert\beta\rVert^{2}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{p}\ln\int_{S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})}e^{-\frac{1}{\Delta}(\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)\rVert^{2}+\lVert z\rVert^{2})-p\lambda\epsilon_{p}-\sqrt{p\lambda\epsilon_{p}}\lVert Z\rVert-\frac{1}{2}p\lambda\epsilon_{p}}\mathrm{d}\pi_{0}(\beta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\left[\frac{4p}{\Delta}\lVert X^{\top}X\rVert_{2}+\frac{1}{\Delta}\lVert z\rVert^{2}+\frac{3}{2}p\lambda\epsilon_{p}+\sqrt{p\lambda\epsilon_{p}}\lVert Z\rVert\right]\text{,}

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{4}{p^{2}}\left[\frac{16p^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\sigma_{\text{max}}(X)^{4}+\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\lVert z\rVert^{4}+\frac{9\lambda\epsilon_{p}}{4}p^{2}+p\lambda\epsilon_{p}\lVert Z\rVert^{2}\right]\right\}=O(1)\text{.}

By applying lemma
14

and choosing
D_{1}=p^{\gamma},D_{2}=O(1),K_{p}=O(p^{-(1-\gamma)/2})
(accordingly
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}^{c})=O(pe^{-p^{\gamma}/4})
), for any
r\geq O(e^{-C_{1}p^{\gamma}})\geq O(p^{(5+\gamma)/4}e^{-p^{\gamma}/8})
,

\mathbb{P}(|F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda)|\beta_{0}]|\geq r|\beta_{0})\leq O(e^{-Cr^{2}p^{1-\gamma}})+O(pe^{-p^{\gamma}/4})\text{.}

Finally, the choice
\gamma=1/2
renders the optimal bound

\mathbb{P}(|F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}[F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda)|\beta_{0}]|\geq r|\beta_{0})\leq e^{-C_{2}r^{2}p^{1/2}}\text{.}

∎We will see that, with an integral argument, lemma
21
further implies the following lemma, which gives a upper bound on the conditional variance of
F^{\text{Pert}}_{p}(\lambda)
giving any fixed
\beta_{0}
.

Lemma 22
.

For any fixed
\beta_{0}\in S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}\left[F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\big|\beta_{0}\right]\right)^{2}\bigg|\beta_{0}\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\text{.}

By Lemma
21
, there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2},
and
C_{3}
, depending only on
\Delta
and
\alpha
, such that for any
r\geq t_{0}\coloneqq C_{1}e^{-C_{2}p^{1/2}}
, we have for any
\beta_{0}\in S^{p-1}(\sqrt{p})
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\right|>r\bigg|\beta_{0}\right)\leq e^{-C_{3}r^{2}p^{1/2}}\text{.}

Let
V=\left|F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\right|/p
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\big|\beta_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\mathbb{1}_{V\leq t_{0}}\big|\beta_{0}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[V^{2}\mathbb{1}_{V>t_{0}}\big|\beta_{0}\right]

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}^{2}\mathbb{P}(V\leq t_{0}\big|\beta_{0})+\int_{t_{0}^{2}}^{\infty}s\mathbb{P}(V^{2}>s\big|\beta_{0})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}^{2}+\int_{t_{0}^{2}}^{\infty}se^{-C_{3}sp^{1/2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=t_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{C_{3}p^{1/2}}\left(t_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{C_{3}p^{1/2}}\right)e^{-C_{3}p^{1/2}t_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\text{,}

where the last equality is because of
t_{0}^{2}=C_{1}^{2}e^{-2C_{2}p^{1/2}}=o(p^{-1/2})
.
∎

C.3
Concentration of the free energy with respect to the signal

In this subsection, we state and prove Lemma
23
, which is an upper bound on
\mathbb{E}\left[F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\big|\beta_{0}\right]
, or equivalently, we prove that free energy of the perturbed model concentrates with respect to the randomness of
\beta_{0}
.

Lemma 23
.

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\big|\beta_{0}\right]-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}(\lambda)\right)^{2}\right]=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\text{.}

By Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\langle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\tilde{\beta}_{0})-\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta_{0})\rangle_{\tilde{{\beta}}_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{p}(\ln\mathcal{Z}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta_{0})-\ln\mathcal{Z}^{\text{Pert}}(\tilde{\beta}_{0}))\leq\frac{1}{p}\langle\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\tilde{\beta}_{0})-\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta_{0})\rangle_{{\beta}_{0}}\text{,}

where
\langle f(\beta)\rangle_{\beta_{0}}
refers to the expectation of
f(\beta)
under the distribution proportional to
\mathrm{d}\pi(\beta)e^{-\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta,\beta_{0})}
and similarly for
\langle f(\beta)\rangle_{\tilde{\beta}_{0}}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta,\beta_{0})
is the same as
\mathcal{H}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta)
, only to emphasize the different values of
\beta_{0}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}(\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta,\tilde{\beta}_{0})-\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta,\beta_{0}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2\Delta}\left|\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\beta)+z\rVert^{2}-\lVert X(\tilde{\beta}_{0}-\beta)+z\rVert^{2}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}(\beta_{0}-\tilde{\beta}_{0})^{\top}\beta\right|\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2\Delta}\left(\lVert X(\beta_{0}-\tilde{\beta}_{0})\rVert^{2}+2\left|\langle X(\beta_{0}-\tilde{\beta}_{0}),X(\tilde{\beta}_{0}-\beta)+z\rangle\right|\right)+O(\sqrt{p})\lVert\beta_{0}-\tilde{\beta}_{0}\rVert\bigg\}

\displaystyle=O(\sqrt{p})\lVert\beta_{0}-\tilde{\beta}_{0}\rVert\text{.}

Upon defining

f(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\beta_{0})\coloneqq\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}\ln\mathcal{Z}^{\text{Pert}}(\beta_{0})=\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\text{,}

we see that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\left|f(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\beta_{0})-f(\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\tilde{\beta}_{0})\right|\leq C\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\beta_{0}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\tilde{\beta}_{0}\rVert\text{.}

Finally, Lévy’s lemma, i.e. Lemma
16
, implies

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}-\mathbb{E}_{X,z,Z,\beta_{0}}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\right|>Ct\right)\leq e^{\pi-pt^{2}/4}\text{,}

for any
t>0
, which further leads to

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\mathbb{E}\left[F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\big|\beta_{0}\right]-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert}}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4C^{2}e^{\pi}}{p}=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\text{.}

∎

C.4
Completing the proof of Theorem
5

By Lemma
21
and
23
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(\lambda))=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\operatorname{Var}(F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(\lambda)\big|\beta_{0})\right]+\operatorname{Var}\left(\mathbb{E}[F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(\lambda)\big|\beta_{0}]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\text{.}

Recall that

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}=\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,2}\right\rangle\text{,}

which gives

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0}}\right|=\left|\epsilon_{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0,p}}\right|=\left|\frac{\epsilon_{p}}{p}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|=\left|\frac{\epsilon_{p}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left\langle R_{1,2}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon_{p}}{2}\text{.}

Finally, noting that when
\lambda_{0}=0
, the perturbed model would be the same as our original linear regression model. Thus,

\left|\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(\lambda_{0})-\mathbb{E}F_{p}\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(\lambda_{0})-\mathbb{E}F_{p}^{\text{Pert }}(0)\right|\leq\frac{\lambda_{0}\epsilon_{p}}{2}=O(\epsilon_{p}),

since
\lambda_{0}\in[1/2,1]
. Together with Lemma
20
, the proof is therefore complete.
∎

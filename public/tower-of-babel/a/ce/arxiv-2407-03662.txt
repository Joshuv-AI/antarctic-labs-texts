Compact Binary Formation in Open Star Clusters III: Probability of Binary Black Holes Hidden in Gaia Black Hole Binary
Ataru Tanikawa 1, Long Wang 2,3, Michiko S. Fujii 4, Alessandro A. Trani 5, Toshinori Hayashi 6, Yasushi Suto 7

Abstract
The Gaia mission and its follow-up observations have discovered a few candidates of non-interacting single black holes (BHs) and visible stars, Gaia BH1, BH2, and BH3, collectively called “astrometric BH binaries”. This paper investigates whether any of these candidates harbor binary BHs (BBHs), namely, whether any such candidates are previously undiscovered “astrometric BBH triples”. Focusing on open star clusters, which are promising formation sites of astrometric BH binaries, we estimate the formation rate of astrometric BBH triples through gravitational N N -body simulations. We find a competitively high formation efficiency of astrometric BBH triples ( ∼ 10 − 6 ​ M ⊙ − 1 \sim 10^{-6}{\rm M}_{\odot}^{-1} or ∼ 10 \sim 10 % of astrometric BH binaries) in low-metallicity environments but no astrometric BBH triples in solar-metallicity environments. Most of the astrometric BBH triples in our simulations were dynamically stable for 10 10 Gyrs, indicating that ∼ 10 \sim 10 % of astrometric BH binary candidates may indeed harbor inner BBHs if they originate from open star clusters in low-metallicity environments. Astrometric BBH triples can be distinguished from astrometric BH binaries through radial velocity follow-up of the tertiary star. According to the statistics of our simulated samples, a small percent of astrometric BH binary candidates should exhibit detectable radial-velocity modulations generated by inner BBHs. Such candidates preferentially exhibit “outer” orbital periods of ≳ 10 3 \gtrsim 10^{3} days and moderately high “outer” orbital eccentricities ( ≳ 0.7 \gtrsim 0.7 ). Our current result will strongly motivate the search for astrometric BBH triples in the upcoming Gaia Data Release 4 and Gaia Final Data Release.

2.1
Terminology

Our terminology is summarized in Figure
1
. A
binary system consisting of one BH and one visible star is called a
“BH binary”. A visible star orbiting a BBH forms a triple system
called a “BBH triple” characterized by two independent
attributes. First, a BBH can reside inside the triple (an “inner
BBH”) or outside the triple (a “non-inner BBH” or simply a
“BBH”). Second, a BBH can merge within 10 gigayears (Gyrs) (a
“merging BBH”) or not merge within 10 Gyrs (a non-merging BBH). The
timescale of a BBH merger is calculated as the GW radiation timescale
(
t_{\rm gw}

(

Mathews, 1963
;
Peters, 1964
)

as merging if

Gyrs, not if
t_{\rm gw}<13.8
Gyrs (the Hubble
time). At the end of our simulation, at least 1 Gyr has passed since
the birth of the universe and
10
Gyrs is a well-rounded number.

) cannot form astrometric BBH triples, their
exclusion from the present paper does not affect our
conclusions. Therefore, we consider that the

,
\rho=20{\rm M}_{\odot}~{\rm pc}^{-3}
, and
f_{\rm b,<5{\rm M}_{\odot}}=20

The formation efficiencies of astrometric BBH triples in low-metallicity
environments likely depend on the cluster parameters, as observed for
astrometric BH binaries in solar-metallicity environments

(
Tanikawa
et al., 2024a
)
. This proposition is reasonable because
astrometric BBH triples are formed similarly to astrometric BH binaries; that is,
a BH (or BBH) closely interacts with a main-sequence binary and
captures one of its members. However, a thorough survey of cluster
models with various metallicities, cluster masses, cluster densities,
initial binary fractions, and other parameters is prohibitively
time-intensive and beyond the scope of this paper.

Considering different galactocentric distances and eccentric orbits of
the open clusters, the formation efficiencies of astrometric BBH triples can
also depend on the galactic tidal field. However, we consider that the
galactic tidal field in our cluster models is reasonable because the
currently discovered astrometric BH binaries are
\lesssim 1
kpc from the
Sun, implying that their galactocentric distances (
7
–
9
kpc) are
similar to those in our cluster models. Therefore, our results can be
directly compared with observations of astrometric BH binaries. Moreover, if
their time-averaged galactocentric distances are fixed, clusters on
eccentric orbits evolve similarly to those on circular orbits

(
Baumgardt &
Makino, 2003
)
. We thus suppose that clusters forming
astrometric BH binaries and astrometric BBH triples at a galactocentric distance
of
\sim 8
kpc evolve similarly, regardless of whether their orbits
are circular or eccentric.

From our clusters aged
1
Gyr, we extracted the astrometric BH binaries and
astrometric BBH triples. The conditions of the astrometric BH binaries and astrometric BBH triples are detailed in subsection
2.1
.

2.3
Three-body simulations of astrometric BBH triples

To assess the stability of the astrometric BBH triples over
10
Gyrs, we
followed the secular evolutions of the astrometric BBH triples in the

okinami
code
(
Trani &
Spera, 2023
)
, which numerically
solves a stable hierarchical triple system by integrating the
equations of motion in Delunay elements derived from a three-body
Hamiltonian expanded at the octupole-level interaction and averaged
over the mean anomalies of the inner and outer binaries

(
Naoz, 2016b
;
Tremaine, 2023
)
. The equations
are integrated using an 8th-order Dormand–Prince scheme with an
adaptive step size. Post-Newtonian corrections of order 1, 2, and 2.5
were applied using the midpoint step method described in

Mikkola &
Merritt (2008)
.

The observed mass ratios are interpreted as follows. Similarly to the
inner BBHs of astrometric BBH triples, all BBHs with orbital periods of

\lesssim 10^{2}

\sim 1

words, most BBHs that can potentially become inner BBHs of astrometric BBH

possess mass ratios of
\sim 0.5
–
1

should be limited to
\lesssim 10^{3}
days by configurational
stability. In all BBHs with orbital periods of
\lesssim 10^{3}
days,
the mass ratios are
\sim 0.5
–
1
.

In BBHs with short orbital periods, the mass ratios tend to approach
unity because short-period BH progenitors undergo binary interactions
such as stable mass transfer and common envelope evolution, which
reduce the mass difference between the two BH progenitors. Many binary
population synthesis models have demonstrated near-unity mass ratios
in BBHs with short orbital periods

et al., 2018

et al., 2022

\lesssim 0.3

et al., 2022

ratios would explain the octupole-level interactions in triple systems

(

Antognini, 2015

. However, such BBHs

5
Discussion and Conclusion

We investigated the intrinsic probability of astrometric BBH triples, or
BBHs hidden within astrometric BH binary candidates. The existence
probability is high in low-metallicity environments (
\sim 10
% and

\sim 1
% at
Z\leq 0.005
and
0.01
, respectively) and zero in
solar-metallicity environments (
Z=0.02
). This result is reflected by
the formation efficiency of BBHs with orbital periods of
\lesssim 10^{2}
days, which also depends on the adopted binary evolution model.
Such BBHs can capture visible stars as tertiary stars, forming triple
systems (astrometric BBH triples) with outer-orbital periods of

10^{2}
–
10^{4}
days. Half of the inner BBHs of astrometric BBH triples are
non-merging BBHs and the remainder are usually characterized by long
GW radiation on timescales of
t_{\rm gw}>1
Gyrs. We also confirmed that
3-body effects do not accelerate the merger processes of the inner
BBHs in astrometric BBH triples.

Astrometric BBH triples exhibited three distinct features. First, the
dark-object masses in astrometric BBH triples are twice those of astrometric BH
binaries, simply because astrometric BBH triples contain two BHs. Second,
the visible star mass is lower in astrometric BBH triples than in astrometric BH
binaries. This feature arises not from 3-body effects but from the
intrinsic mass distribution of the clusters forming astrometric BBH
triples. During cluster evolution, the inner BBHs of astrometric BBH triples
eject massive visible stars through binary-single and binary-binary
interactions. Third, astrometric BBH triples move in more circular orbits
than astrometric BH binaries because eccentric astrometric BBH triples are
unstable.

The mass ratios of the inner BBHs of astrometric BBH triples is close to
unity, reflecting the near-unity mass ratios in BBHs with orbital
periods of
\lesssim 10^{2}
days. Such BBH progenitors experience
binary interactions that bring BH progenitor masses closer. The orbits
are more circular in the inner BBHs than in all BBHs because they are
influenced by tertiary stars; moreover, the eccentric inner BBHs have
already merged through GW radiation.

If the radial-velocity precision in the spectroscopic observations is

\sim 1
m/s, then radial-velocity modulations should distinguish

30
% of our astrometric BBH triples from astrometric BH binary candidates. Astrometric BBH triples are more easily distinguished from astrometric BH binaries when
their orbital eccentricities are large and their ratios of
inner-to-outer BBH period are high, thereby enlarging the
radial-velocity modulations. The detection probability of GWs from the
inner BBH of an astrometric BBH is
<0.1
%.

Based on our simulation results (see Figures

6
and
7
), we
inferred that Gaia BH3 is probably not an astrometric BBH triple. In
particular, its dark-object mass is too small and its visible star is
too massive. Its eccentricity also appears excessively high in the
bottom panel of Figure
7
), but is within the
acceptable limits given the long orbital period (
\sim 4190
days) of
Gaia BH3. If Gaia BH3 is a genuine astrometric BBH triple, its high orbital
eccentricity (
0.726
) possibly gives rise to large radial-velocity
modulations around its pericenter passage.

As a guideline for discovering astrometric BBH triples among a large number
of astrometric BH binaries, we suggest that the following astrometric BH binaries
are astrometric BBH triple candidates that are easily distinguishable from
astrometric BH binary candidates:

•

Astrometric BH binary candidates with low metallicity (
Z\leq 0.005
) visible stars. Astrometric BBH triples with solar metallicities
have not been found.

•

Astrometric BH binary candidates with twice the expected
theoretical BH mass. Astrometric BBH triples possess two BHs.

•

Astrometric BH binary candidates with half the expected mass of
visible stars. The inner BBHs of astrometric BBH triples tend to eject
high-mass stars from their open-cluster host, providing little
opportunity for high-mass star capture.

•

Astrometric BH binary candidates with high eccentricities (
\gtrsim 0.7
) and long periods (
\gtrsim 1000
days). If they are astrometric BBH
triples, they have large radial velocity modulations at their
pericenter passages. The periods should be
\gtrsim 1000
days,
otherwise such eccentric astrometric BBH triples are unstable. Such
modulations will be strong indicators of astrometric BBH triples.

As no astrometric BBH triples were found in the
Z=0.02
model (see Figure

2
), we claim that astrometric BH binaries formed in
low-metallicity environments are likelier candidates of astrometric BBH
triples than astrometric BH binaries in solar-metallicity
environments. However, this assessment may strongly depend on the
adopted binary evolution model. The binary evolution model assumes
that when a star in the Hertzsprung-gap phase fills its Roche lobe and
its mass transfer destabilizes, that the Hertzsprung-gap star merges
with its companion star because it may not establish a steep density
gradient between the helium core and the hydrogen envelope

(
Ivanova &
Taam, 2004
)
. This assumption largely reduces the
formation efficiency of BBHs with orbital periods of
\lesssim 10^{2}

days in solar-metallicity environments
(
Dominik
et al., 2012
;
Giacobbo
et al., 2018
;
Tanikawa
et al., 2022
)
. If this assumption is
invalid, many astrometric BBH triples should form in solar-metallicity
environments. The discovery of an astrometric BBH triple in a
solar-metallicity environment would impose strong constraints on BBH
formation scenarios.

According to the above guidelines, an astrometric BH binary candidate can be
an astrometric BBH triple if its BH mass is unexpectedly large. However,
this suggestion depends on the selected single-star evolution and
supernova models. Past discoveries of unexpectedly massive BHs, such
as LB-1
(
Liu
et al., 2019
)

Note that the formation efficiency of astrometric BH binaries is almost
constant from low metallicities to the solar metallicity (see Figure

\sim 1

found. Astrometric BH binaries that are astrometric BBH triples can be more easily
found than actual astrometric BH binaries because they contain more massive
dark objects and produce wider swings of their tertiary visible stars
than astrometric BH binaries with the same orbital period. Thus, Gaia
astrometry can detect astrometric BBH triple candidates more efficiently
than astrometric BH binaries. In other words,
40
discovered astrometric BH
binaries may contain more than one astrometric BBH
triple.
Nagarajan
et al. (2025)
predicts that approximately
30

and
45
astrometric BH binaries will be detected by Gaia DR4 and Final DR,
respectively. In the era of these data releases, we can expect to
discover one or more astrometric BBH triples.

Even the discovery of one astrometric BBH triple will exert important and
complementary impacts. A triple containing a BBH merging within the
Hubble time will constrain the formation mechanism of BBHs observed by
GW observatories
(
Abbott
et al., 2023b
)
, whose origins have been
debated
(
Abbott
et al., 2023a
)
. GW observations can reveal the BH
masses and spins of a BBH. On the other hand, an astrometric BBH triple will
provide its position, velocity, and metallicity via its visible
companion star. Its position and velocity will be helpful to make
clear what galactic components (the Galactic disk, halo, bulge, and
center) the astrometric BBH triple belong to. Note that the sky localization
and distance obtained from GW observations are unhelpful for
elucidating the origin of a BBH, because a huge number of galaxies can
be located at the possible position of the BBH. The metallicity of the
astrometric BBH triple’s companion will indicate the metallicity of the
observed BBHs. Several studies have claimed that lower-metallicity
stars are responsible for more massive BBHs found by GW observatories

(
Belczynski et al., 2016
;
Giacobbo
et al., 2018
;
Kinugawa et al., 2021b
;
Tanikawa
et al., 2022
;
Santoliquido
et al., 2023
, e.g.)
. The
discovery of an astrometric BBH triple would consolidate these claims. A BBH
in an astrometric BBH triple that remains stable during the Hubble time
would be the first case of a non-merging BBH.

Appendix B
Alternative binary evolution model

As shown in Figure
2
, the formation efficiency of
astrometric BBH triples is zero at
Z=0.02
., the metallicity of typical
open clusters in the Milky Way galaxy

(
Portegies Zwart et al., 2010
)
. Thus, our simulation results indicate
that no astrometric BBH triples have formed in Milky Way open clusters at
the present time. Note that we need not consider open clusters outside
the Milky Way galaxy, because astrometric BH binaries will be found within
only 10 kpc (within the Milky Way galaxy) even by Gaia DR4 and Final
DR
(
Nagarajan
et al., 2025
)
.

However, the formation efficiency of astrometric BBH triples at
Z=0.02

strongly depends on the adopted binary evolution model, as described
in subsection
3.3
. In an alternative binary evolution
model that admits common envelope evolution in the Hertzsprung gap
phase of a donor star, short-period BBHs can be formed at
Z=0.02

(
Belczynski
et al., 2020a
, e.g.)
and the formation efficiency of
astrometric BBH triples at
Z=0.02
may be comparable to those at
Z\leq 0.005
; that is,
10^{-6}\;{\rm M}_{\odot}^{-1}
. Assuming that the formation
efficiencies of astrometric BBH triples are
10^{-6}\;{\rm M}_{\odot}^{-1}
at all
metallicities, Milky Way open clusters can yield astrometric BBH triples at
the present time. This alternative binary evolution model is
described below.

We first estimate the probability of discovering astrometric BBH triples
inside open clusters. We suppose that the formation rate of open
clusters (CFR) in the Milky Way galaxy has remained constant during
the past
10
Gyrs. The
t_{\rm gw}
distribution of astrometric BBH triples is
modeled as

\displaystyle f(t_{\rm gw})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&(t_{\rm gw}/{\rm yr}\leq 10^{7})\\
1&(10^{7}\leq t_{\rm gw}/{\rm yr}\leq 10^{8})\\
(t_{\rm gw}/10^{8}\;{\rm yr})^{-1}&(10^{8}\leq t_{\rm gw}/{\rm yr}\leq 10^{13})\\
0&(t_{\rm gw}/{\rm yr}\geq 10^{13})\end{array}\right..

The
t_{\rm gw}
distribution over
t_{\rm gw}\leq 10^{10}
yrs is based on the
results of
Dominik
et al. (2012)
and

Di Carlo
et al. (2020b)
. As shown in Figure

3
, the logarithmic flat distribution ends at

t_{\rm gw}=10^{13}
yrs. The number of astrometric BBH triples within
t_{0}\leq t_{\rm gw}\leq t_{1}
is determined as

\displaystyle N(t_{0},t_{1})={\rm CFR}\;\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}f(t_{\rm gw})\max(t_{\rm gw},10^{10}\;{\rm yr})\;dt_{\rm gw}.

(11)

Here, we assume that astrometric BBH triples are formed at the same time as
their host cluster and remain in the host until the host clusters are
disrupted. In other words, astrometric BBH triples are located in open
clusters while those clusters remain alive. Under this assumption, the
probability is overestimated. As the lifetime of open clusters is
1

Gyrs, the fraction of astrometric BBH triples inside open clusters can be
written as

\displaystyle N(0,10^{9})/N(0,10^{13})\sim 0.012.

(12)

This fraction is smaller than the ratio of the cluster lifetime (
1

Gyrs) to the Hubble time (
10
Gyr), because most astrometric BBH triples
contain inner BBHs with
t_{\rm gw}>10^{10}
Gyrs. Ultimately, the
probability of finding astrometric BBH triples inside open clusters is only

\sim 1
% even in the alternative binary evolution model, where the
probability is overestimated.

We now assess the detectability of astrometric BBH triples by LISA and
TianQin. The number of astrometric BBH triples detectable by these
instruments is

\displaystyle N_{\rm det}(t_{0},t_{1})={\rm CFR}\;\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}f(t_{\rm gw})10^{7}\;{\rm yr}\;dt_{\rm gw},

(13)

where astrometric BBH triples are limited to those with
t_{0}\leq t_{\rm gw}\leq t_{1}
. For an optimistic estimate, we place these astrometric BBH triples at

1
kpc where they are detectable in
10^{7}
yrs. Note that when the
distance increases to
10
kpc, the detectable duration reduces to

10^{6}
yrs and the detectability becomes smaller. The detection
probability can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\rm det}(0,10^{10})}{N(0,10^{13})}\sim 0.0011,

(14)

similar to that estimated in subsection

4.2
. Assuming that astrometric BBH triples with

t_{\rm gw}\leq 10^{9}
are inside open clusters as described above, the
detection probability of astrometric BBH triples inside open clusters is
calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\rm det}(0,10^{9})}{N(0,10^{13})}\sim 0.0011,

(15)

which nearly equals the detection probability of all astrometric BBH
triples. Thus, if an astrometric BBH triple is detected by LISA and TianQin,
it will likely occupy an open cluster. However, we remark that the
detection probability is small, only
\sim 0.1
%.

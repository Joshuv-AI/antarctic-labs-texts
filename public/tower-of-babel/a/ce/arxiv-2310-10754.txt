1.
Introduction

Throughout this article, by
operator

we mean a bounded linear operator on a complex Banach space
X
.
The Banach algebra of all bounded operators on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(X)
.
An operator
T
is a
contraction
if
\|T\|\leq 1
.
We denote the spectrum of
T
by
\sigma(T)
.

Our story begins with the following result of Zarrabi
[
12
]
,
which was based on earlier work of Atzmon
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
, Théorème 3.1]
)
.

Let
T
be a Banach-space contraction such that

\sigma(T)
is a countable subset of the unit circle
\mathbb{T}

and

(1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|T^{-n}\|}{\sqrt{n}}=0.

Then
T
is an isometry, i.e.,
\|T\|=\|T^{-1}\|=1
.

Zarrabi further showed that the condition that
\sigma(T)
be countable
is sharp in the following sense:
given an uncountable closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
,
there exists a contraction
T
on a suitably chosen Banach space
such that
\sigma(T)\subset E

and such that (
1
) holds, but
\sup_{n}\|T^{-n}\|=\infty
.

This suggests that, to treat the case where
\sigma(T)
is uncountable,
we need to adapt the condition (
1
).
The next result, due to Esterle
[
6
]
does just that.

Let
A(\mathbb{T})
and
A^{+}(\mathbb{T})
denote the algebras
of absolutely convergent Fourier and Taylor series respectively.
Given a closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
,
we denote by
J(E)
the closure of the set of functions in
A(\mathbb{T})
vanishing
on a neighbourhood of
E
.
We say that
E
is a
strong
AA^{+}
-set
if,
for every
f\in A(\mathbb{T})
, there exists
g\in A^{+}(\mathbb{T})
such that
f-g\in J(E)
.
Examples of strong
AA^{+}
-sets include closed countable subsets of
\mathbb{T}

and the Cantor middle-thirds set in
\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
E
be a strong
AA^{+}
-subset of
\mathbb{T}
.
Then there exists a positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
and a constant
K

with the following property:
if
T
is a Banach-space contraction such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and

\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})
as
n\to\infty
, then
\sup_{n}\|T^{-n}\|\leq K
.

The condition that
E
be a strong
AA^{+}
-set may seem restrictive,
but in fact it is optimal.
Esterle had previously shown in
[
4
]
that,
given a closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
that is not a strong
AA^{+}
-set,
and given any positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
,
there exists a contraction
T
on a suitably chosen Banach space such that

\sigma(T)\subset E
and

\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})
, but
\sup_{n}\|T^{-n}\|=\infty
.

The situation is quite different for contractions on
Hilbert spaces
.
In a private communication,
Esterle expressed the belief that the following result is true.
We formulate it as a conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero.
Then there exists a positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
with the following property:
if
T
is a Hilbert-space contraction such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and
\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})

as
n\to\infty
, then
T
is a unitary operator.

The conjecture is known to be true if
E
is a so-called
Beurling–Carleson set
,
namely a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\dist(e^{i\theta},E)\,d\theta>-\infty.

In fact Esterle established the following result.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 6.4]
)
.

Let
E
be a Beurling–Carleson set.
If
T
is a Hilbert-space contraction such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and

\|T^{-n}\|=O(n^{k})
for some
k\geq 0
,
then
T
is a unitary operator.

Esterle also showed that Theorem
1.4
does not extend to
general closed sets
E
of measure zero. In fact, by a result of Kellay
[
7
, Théorème 3.4]
,
given any positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
, there exists a Hilbert-space contraction
T

such that
\sigma(T)
is a subset of
\mathbb{T}
of measure zero and
\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})
,
but
\sup_{n}\|T^{-n}\|=\infty
.
Thus, in Conjecture
1.3
,
no single sequence
(u_{n})
can work simultaneously for all sets
E
of measure zero.

Esterle wrote in
[
5
]
‘The subject clearly deserves further investigation’.
Our main goal in this paper is to establish the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero.
Then there exists a positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
with the following property:
if
T
is a Hilbert-space contraction such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and
\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})

and
\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
, then
T
is a unitary operator.

This result stops short of proving Conjecture
1.3
because of the extra
condition
\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
.
However, Theorem
1.5
does apply to every closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
of measure zero,
the first such result as far as we know.

The condition of measure zero is sharp. This is our second result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
of positive Lebesgue measure.
Then, for any positive sequence
u_{n}\to\infty
,
there exists a Hilbert-space contraction
T
such that
\sigma(T)\subset E
and
\|T^{-n}\|=O(u_{n})

and
\rank(I-T^{*}T)=1
, but
\sup_{n}\|T^{-n}\|=\infty
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
both proceed via a study
of compressed shift operators.
In §
2
we recall the basic properties of these operators,
and derive estimates for the norms of their negative powers. These estimates depend on the
decay properties of singular inner functions, which we study in detail in §
3
.
These results are then applied in §
4
, both to prove Theorem
1.6

and to obtain Theorem
1.5
for the case of compressed shifts.
The general case of Theorem
1.5
is proved using similar ideas,
based on the Sz-Nagy–Foias theory of functional models. This theory is presented
in §
5
,
and then applied in §
6
to complete the proof Theorem
1.5
.
We conclude in §
7
with a brief discussion of what
would be needed to obtain a complete proof of Conjecture
1.3
.

2.
Compressed shifts

Let
H^{2}
be the classical Hardy space on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, namely the Hilbert space of holomorphic functions

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}z^{n}
such that
\|f\|_{2}^{2}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}|a_{n}|^{2}<\infty
.
We denote by
S:H^{2}\to H^{2}
the shift operator, defined by
Sf(z):=zf(z)
.

Throughout this section,
\theta
denotes a (non-constant) inner function on
\mathbb{D}
,
namely a bounded holomorphic function whose radial boundary values satisfy

|\theta|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
We write
K_{\theta}:=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
,
the orthogonal complement of
\theta H^{2}
in
H^{2}
,
and denote by
P_{K_{\theta}}
the orthogonal projection of
H^{2}
onto
K_{\theta}
.

The operator
S_{\theta}:=P_{K_{\theta}}S|_{K_{\theta}}

is called the
compressed shift
on
K_{\theta}
.
In this section, we study some of its properties.
To the best of our knowledge, this operator was first
studied by Sarason in
[
10
]
.
Our treatment mostly follows that in Nikol’skiĭ’s book
[
8
]
.

2.1.
Basic results

We begin with the following result,
which is an
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus for
S_{\theta}
.
Here
H^{\infty}
denotes the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
,
equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
\phi\in H^{\infty}
, define the operator
\phi(S_{\theta}):K_{\theta}\to K_{\theta}
by

\phi(S_{\theta})f:=P_{K_{\theta}}(\phi f)\quad(f\in K_{\theta}).

Then the map
\phi\mapsto\phi(S_{\theta})
is a contractive homomorphism
from
H^{\infty}
into
\mathcal{L}(K_{\theta})
such that
z\mapsto S_{\theta}
.
Moreover,
\phi(S_{\theta})=0
if and only if
\phi\in\theta H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Chapter III, §2, p.64]
.
∎

The following result,
originally due to Sarason
[
10
]
,
gives a precise value for the operator norm of
\phi(S_{\theta})
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
\phi\in H^{\infty}
, we have

(2)

\|\phi(S_{\theta})\|=\inf_{h\in H^{\infty}}\|\phi+\theta h\|_{\infty}.

Moreover, the infimum is attained.

Proof.

See
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
or
[
8
, Lecture VIII, p.182]
.
∎

The next result is known as the Livšic–Moeller theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

The spectrum and point spectrum of
S_{\theta}
are given respectively by

\sigma(S_{\theta})=Y_{\theta}\cup Z_{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{p}(S_{\theta})=Z_{\theta},

where
Y_{\theta}
is the set of singular points of
\theta
in
\mathbb{T}
,
and
Z_{\theta}
is the set of zeros of
\theta
in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Lecture III, §1, p.62]
.
∎

We conclude this subsection with two results on the adjoint
S_{\theta}^{*}
of
S_{\theta}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

For all
f\in K_{\theta}
, we have
\|S_{\theta}^{*n}f\|_{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

A standard calculation(see e.g.
[
8
, p.62]
) shows that
S_{\theta}^{*}=S^{*}|_{K_{\theta}}
,
where
S^{*}
is the
H^{2}
-adjoint of
S
. Hence, for each
f\in K_{\theta}
,
we have
\|S_{\theta}^{*n}f\|_{2}=\|S^{*n}f\|_{2}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

The operator
I-S_{\theta}^{*}S_{\theta}
has rank one.

Proof.

See e.g.
[
2
, Corollary 1.5]
.
∎

2.2.
Norms of negative powers

If
\theta(0)\neq 0
, then, by Theorem
2.3
, the operator
S_{\theta}
is invertible.
We are interested in computing or estimating the norms of negative powers of
S_{\theta}
.
We begin with a very simple result.

Theorem 2.6
.

We have
\|S_{\theta}^{-n}\|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4
,
S_{\theta}^{*n}\to 0
strongly.
It follows that
\|(S_{\theta}^{*})^{-n}\|\to\infty
, and hence that
\|S_{\theta}^{-n}\|\to\infty
.
∎

The exact value of
\|S_{\theta}^{-n}\|
,
and more generally that of
\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|

for
\phi\in H^{\infty}
, can be expressed
in terms of the solution to a corona-type problem.
The connection between the spectral properties of
the compressed shift and the corona theorem is not new:
it appears for example in
[
8
, Lecture III]
.
We establish the following result.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\phi\in H^{\infty}
, and suppose that
\phi(S_{\theta})
is invertible. Then

(3)

\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|=\inf\Bigl\{\|g\|_{\infty}:g,h\in H^{\infty},\phi g+\theta h=1\Bigr\}.

Moreover, the infimum is attained.

Proof.

Suppose first that
g,h\in H^{\infty}
with
\phi g+\theta h=1
.
By the functional calculus, Theorem
2.1
,
we have
\phi(S_{\theta})g(S_{\theta})=g(S_{\theta})\phi(S_{\theta})=I
. Hence

\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|=\|g(S_{\theta})\|\leq\|g\|_{\infty}.

Now, as
\phi(S_{\theta})
is invertible,
it follows by the spectral mapping theorem in
[
8
, Lecture III, p.66]
that
\inf_{\mathbb{D}}(|\phi|+|\theta|)>0
.
By the corona theorem, there exist
g_{0},h_{0}\in H^{\infty}

such that
\phi g_{0}+\theta h_{0}=1
.
By a normal-family argument, there exists
f_{0}\in H^{\infty}

such that
\|g_{0}\|_{H^{\infty}/\theta H^{\infty}}=\|g_{0}-\theta f_{0}\|_{\infty}
.
Set

g:=g_{0}-\theta f_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad h:=h_{0}+\phi f_{0}.

Then
g,h\in H^{\infty}
and

\phi g+\theta h=1
, so as before
\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}=g(S_{\theta})
.
Also, since
\|g\|_{H^{\infty}/\theta H^{\infty}}=\|g\|_{\infty}
, Theorem
2.2
gives

\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|=\|g(S_{\theta})\|=\|g\|_{H^{\infty}/\theta H^{\infty}}=\|g\|_{\infty}.

Thus the infimum in (
3
) is attained.
∎

Using this result, we derive some quantitative estimates for
\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\phi\in H^{\infty}
. If
\phi(S_{\theta})
is invertible and
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, then

\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta}-1\Bigr)\leq\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|\leq A\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{\delta^{2}},

where
\delta:=\inf_{\mathbb{D}}\max\{|\phi(z)|,|\theta(z)|\}
and
A
is an absolute constant.

Proof.

We begin with the lower bound.
By Theorem
2.7
,
there exist
g,h\in H^{\infty}
with

\phi g+\theta h=1
such that
\|\phi(S_{\theta})^{-1}\|=\|g\|_{\infty}
.
Now, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=|\phi(z)g(z)+\theta(z)h(z)|

\displaystyle\leq|\phi(z)|\|g\|_{\infty}+|\theta(z)|\|h\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{|\phi(z)|,|\theta(z)|\}(\|g\|_{\infty}+\|h\|_{\infty}).

Note also that
\|h\|_{\infty}\leq\|g\|_{\infty}+1
. Indeed

\|h\|_{\infty}=\|\theta h\|_{\infty}=\|1-\phi g\|_{\infty}\leq\|1\|_{\infty}+\|\phi g\|_{\infty}\leq 1+\|g\|_{\infty},

the last inequality because
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
It follows that

1\leq\max\{|\phi(z)|,|\theta(z)|\}(2\|g\|_{\infty}+1)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Taking the infimum of the right-hand side over all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we obtain

1\leq\delta(2\|g\|_{\infty}+1),

where
\delta:=\inf_{\mathbb{D}}\max\{|\phi(z)|,|\theta(z)|\}
.
This gives the lower bound in the theorem.

For the upper bound, we invoke a quantitative form of the corona theorem.
According to that theorem, if
a,b\in H^{\infty}
and
\epsilon\leq\max\{|a(z)|,|b(z)|\}\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
then there exist
g,h\in H^{\infty}
with
ag+bh=1

and
\|g\|_{\infty},\|h\|_{\infty}\leq A\epsilon^{-2}\log(2/\epsilon)
,
where
A
is an absolute constant (see e.g.
[
8
, Appendix 3, p.288]
).
Applying this result, we deduce
that there exist
g,h\in H^{\infty}
with
\phi g+\theta h=1

and
\|g\|_{\infty},\|h\|_{\infty}\leq A\delta^{-2}\log(2/\delta)
.
Now apply Theorem
2.7
.
∎

We record the special case of
Theorem
2.8
in which
\phi(z)=z^{n}
.
Note once again that, by Theorem
2.3
, the condition that

\theta(0)\neq 0
implies that
S_{\theta}

is invertible.

Corollary 2.9
.

If
\theta(0)\neq 0
, then, for all
n\geq 1
,

(4)

\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta_{n}(\theta)}-1\Bigr)\leq\|S_{\theta}^{-n}\|\leq A\frac{\log(2/\delta_{n}(\theta))}{\delta_{n}(\theta)^{2}},

where

\delta_{n}(\theta):=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\max\{|z|^{n},|\theta(z)|\},

and
A
is an absolute constant.

3.
Decay of singular inner functions

Corollary
2.9
shows that,
if
\theta
is an inner function with
\theta(0)\neq 0
, then the growth of
\|S_{\theta}^{-n}\|

is essentially governed by the decay of the sequence

\delta_{n}(\theta)
.
In this section we examine the sequence
\delta_{n}(\theta)
in detail.

If there exists
w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\theta(w)=0
,
then clearly
\delta_{n}(\theta)\leq|w|^{n}
for all
n
.
Thus, in this case,
\delta_{n}(\theta)
decays exponentially fast.

Henceforth we shall be concerned with the more delicate case where
\theta
has no zeros,
i.e., it is a singular inner function.
Thus it has the form

(5)

\theta(z)=\exp\Bigl(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}\,d\nu(e^{it})\Bigr)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
\nu
is a finite positive singular measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
Define

m_{\theta}(r):=\inf_{|z|=r}|\theta(z)|\quad(0\leq r<1).

Then
m_{\theta}(r)
is a strictly decreasing function such that
m_{\theta}(0)=|\theta(0)|

and
m_{\theta}(r)\to 0
as
r\to 1^{-}
.
Clearly

(6)

\delta_{n}(\theta)\geq r^{n}\iff m_{\theta}(r)\geq r^{n}.

The following result shows that
\delta_{n}(\theta)
can
decay arbitrarily slowly.
We denote by
|\cdot|
the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|>0
,
and let
(\eta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
(0,1)
such that
\eta_{n}\to 0
.
Then there exists a finite positive singular measure
\nu
on
E
such that,
if
\theta
is the singular inner function defined by (
5
),
then
\delta_{n}(\theta)\geq\eta_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Choose a strictly increasing sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset(0,1)

such that
\rho_{n}>\eta_{n}^{1/n}
for each
n
.
By the main result of
[
9
]
, there exists a
non-constant singular inner function
\theta
such that

m_{\theta}(\rho_{n})\geq\eta_{n}
for all
n
.
Hence
m_{\theta}(\eta_{n}^{1/n})\geq\eta_{n}
for all
n
.
From (
6
), it follows that
\delta_{n}(\theta)\geq\eta_{n}

for all
n
.

Although it is not explicitly mentioned in
[
9
]
,
it follows easily from the proof therein that
\theta
may be chosen
so that its measure
\nu
is supported on a given
closed subset
E
of
\mathbb{T}
, provided that
|E|>0
.
∎

Theorem
3.1
is no longer true if
|E|=0
.
Indeed, in this case,

\delta_{n}(\theta)
must respect a certain rate of decay.
This is made precise by the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|=0
.
Then there exists a positive sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0

such that, if
\nu
is any (non-zero) finite positive measure
on
E
, and
\theta
is the singular inner function defined by
(
5
), then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n}(\theta)}{\epsilon_{n}}=0.

To prove this result, we need to develop some ideas from
the theory of Hausdorff measures.

A
measure function
is a continuous increasing function

h:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)
such that
h(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
Given a measure function
h
, a subset
A\subset\mathbb{T}
and
\eta>0
,
we define

\Lambda_{h}^{\eta}(A):=\inf\Bigl\{\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}h(|I|):\mathcal{I}\text{~is a countable cover of $A$ by arcs $I$ with $|I|<\eta$}\Bigr\},

and then

\Lambda_{h}(A):=\lim_{\eta\to 0^{+}}\Lambda_{h}^{\eta}(A).

The set function
\Lambda_{h}
is an outer measure on
\mathbb{T}
,
called the
Hausdorff measure
corresponding to the
measure function
h
.
If
h(t)=t
for all
t
, then
\Lambda_{h}
is simply the Lebesgue (outer) measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

We require two general results about Hausdorff measures.
The first of these is a simple consequence of the definitions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
h
be a measure function and let
\mu
be a positive
Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}
. Suppose that there exists
\eta>0

such that
\mu(I)\leq h(|I|)
for all arcs
I
with
|I|<\eta
.
Then
\mu(B)\leq\Lambda_{h}(B)
for all Borel subsets
B\subset\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Borel subset of
\mathbb{T}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}
be a countable covering of
B
by arcs
I
such that
|I|<\eta
.
Then we have

\mu(B)\leq\mu(\cup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}I)\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\mu(I)\leq\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}h(|I|).

Taking the infimum over all such coverings
\mathcal{I}
, we obtain

\mu(B)\leq\Lambda_{h}^{\eta}(B)\leq\Lambda(B)
.
∎

The second result is a special case of an observation of Besicovitch
[
3
, Remark 2]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|=0
.
Then there exists a measure function
h
such that

\Lambda_{h}(E)=0
and
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}h(t)/t=\infty
.

Proof.

We construct a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\geq 0}
recursively as follows.
Set
t_{0}:=1
. Suppose that
n\geq 1
and that
t_{n-1}
has already been chosen.
Since
E
is a compact set of Lebesgue measure zero,
we can find a
finite
cover
\mathcal{I}_{n}
of
E
by arcs
I
with
|I|<t_{n-1}
such that

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|I|<4^{-n}.

Set

t_{n}:=\min\Bigl\{\min_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|I|,\,t_{n-1}/4\Bigr\}.

Clearly, the sequence
(t_{n})
obtained in this way is strictly decreasing and tends to
0
.

Define
h:(0,1]\to(0,\infty)
by

h(t):=\min\{2^{n}t,\,2^{n-1}t_{n-1}\}\quad(t\in(t_{n},t_{n-1}],\,n\geq 1).

Clearly
h
is continuous and increasing on interval
(t_{n},t_{n-1}]
.
Also, since
t_{n-1}\geq 4t_{n}
, we have

\lim_{t\to t_{n}^{+}}h(t)=\min\{2^{n}t_{n},2^{n-1}t_{n-1}\}=2^{n}t_{n}=h(t_{n}),

so in fact
h
is continuous and increasing on the whole of
(0,1]
.
Further, we have

h(t_{n})=2^{n}t_{n}\leq 2^{n}4^{-n}t_{0}=2^{-n}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty),

whence
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}h(t)=0
. Thus
h
is a measure function.

For each
n\geq 1
, every arc
I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
satisfies
|I|\in[t_{n},t_{n-1}]
,
so
h(|I|)\leq 2^{n}|I|
, and hence

\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}h(|I|)\leq 2^{n}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{n}}|I|\leq 2^{n}4^{-n}=2^{-n}.

It follows that
\Lambda_{h}^{t_{n-1}}(E)\leq 2^{-n}
for all
n
,
whence
\Lambda_{h}(E)=0
.

Finally, if
t\in[t_{n},t_{n-1}]
, then

\frac{h(t)}{t}=\min\Bigl\{\frac{2^{n}t}{t},\,\frac{2^{n-1}t_{n-1}}{t}\Bigr\}\geq\min\{2^{n},2^{n-1}\}=2^{n-1}.

Hence
h(t)/t\to\infty
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
∎

We also need an estimate for singular inner functions.
As before, we consider
\theta
of the form

\theta(z)=\exp\Bigl(-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z}\,d\nu(e^{it})\Bigr)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
\nu
is a finite positive singular measure on
\mathbb{T}
,
and we set

m_{\theta}(r):=\inf_{|z|=r}|\theta(z)|\quad(0\leq r<1).

Lemma 3.5
.

With the above notation, we have

-\log m_{\theta}(1-\eta)\geq\frac{1}{(\pi+1)^{2}}\sup_{|I|=\eta}\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}\quad(0<\eta\leq 1),

where the supremum is taken over all arcs
I
with
|I|=\eta
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in(0,1],r\in[0,1)
and
e^{is}\in\mathbb{T}
. A simple calculation gives

\displaystyle-\log|\theta(re^{is})|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1-r^{2}}{|e^{it}-re^{is}|^{2}}\,d\nu(e^{it})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|t-s|\leq\pi\eta}\frac{1-r^{2}}{|e^{it}-re^{is}|^{2}}\,d\nu(e^{it})

\displaystyle=\int_{|t-s|\leq\pi\eta}\frac{(1+r)(1-r)}{|e^{i(t-s)}-1+1-r|^{2}}\,d\nu(e^{it})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-r}{|\pi\eta+1-r|^{2}}\nu([s-\pi\eta,s+\pi\eta]).

Taking the supremum of both sides over all
e^{is}\in\mathbb{T}
, we
deduce that

-\log m_{\theta}(r)\geq\frac{1-r}{|\pi\eta+1-r|^{2}}\sup_{|I|=\eta}\nu(I).

Finally, setting
r:=1-\eta
, we obtain

-\log m_{\theta}(1-\eta)\geq\frac{\eta}{|\pi\eta+\eta|^{2}}\sup_{|I|=\eta}\nu(I)=\frac{1}{(\pi+1)^{2}}\sup_{|I|=\eta}\frac{\nu(I)}{|I|}.\qed

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By Lemma
3.4
, there exists a measure
function
h
such that
\Lambda_{h}(E)=0
and

h(t)/t\to\infty
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
For
n\geq 1
set

t_{n}:=\inf\Bigl\{t\in(0,1):\frac{h(t)}{-t\log(1-t)}\leq n(\pi+1)^{2}\Bigr\}.

Note that
h(t)/(-t\log(1-t))\to\infty
as
t\to 0^{+}
,
so the sequence
(t_{n})
is strictly decreasing and
tends to zero. We shall prove that the sequence

\epsilon_{n}:=(1-t_{n+1})^{n/2}
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem.

First of all, we show that
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
. To see this, note that,
by continuity, we have

\frac{h(t_{n})}{-t_{n}\log(1-t_{n})}=n(\pi+1)^{2}\quad(n\geq 1).

Hence

\log\epsilon_{n-1}=\frac{(n-1)}{2}\log(1-t_{n})=-\frac{1}{(\pi+1)^{2}}\frac{n-1}{2n}\frac{h(t_{n})}{t_{n}}\to-\infty\quad(n\to\infty),

which implies that
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
.

Now let
\nu
be a positive finite measure on
E

and let
\theta
be the inner function defined by (
5
).
Since
\nu(E)>0
and
\Lambda_{h}(E)=0
,
Lemma
3.3
implies that there exist arcs
I_{k}

with
|I_{k}|\to 0
such that
\nu(I_{k})>h(|I_{k}|)
for all
k
.
Set
\eta_{k}:=|I_{k}|
.
Suppose that
n
and
k
are such that

t_{n+1}\leq\eta_{k}<t_{n}
. Then,
using Lemma
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle-\log m_{\theta}(1-\eta_{k})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(\pi+1)^{2}}\frac{\nu(I_{k})}{|I_{k}|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(\pi+1)^{2}}\frac{h(\eta_{k})}{\eta_{k}}

\displaystyle>-n\log(1-\eta_{k}),

the last inequality because
\eta_{k}<t_{n}
.
Thus
m_{\theta}(1-\eta_{k})<(1-\eta_{k})^{n}
,
which, together with (
6
), implies that

\delta_{n}<(1-\eta_{k})^{n}\leq(1-t_{n+1})^{n}=\epsilon_{n}^{2}.

To summarize, we have shown that
\delta_{n}<\epsilon_{n}^{2}

for each
n
for which there exists an
\eta_{k}\in[t_{n+1},t_{n})
.
Since
\eta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, there are infinitely many such
n
.
It follows that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}/\epsilon_{n}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

5.1.
Compressed shifts for operator-valued inner functions

In this section,
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
denote fixed separable Hilbert spaces.
We write
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
for the Banach space of bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{F}
into
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Also we set
H^{2}(\mathcal{F}):=H^{2}\otimes\mathcal{F}

(with the Hilbert-space tensor product). The shift
S_{\mathcal{F}}:H^{2}(\mathcal{F})\to H^{2}(\mathcal{F})
is defined by

S_{\mathcal{F}}:=S\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}}
, where
S
denotes the usual shift on
H^{2}
. Explicitly, we have

(S_{\mathcal{F}}f)(\lambda)=\lambda f(\lambda)\quad(f\in H^{2}(\mathcal{F}),\,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}).

A function
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
is said to be
inner

if
\Theta(\zeta)
is an isometry for a.e.
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function.
The
compressed shift
associated to
\Theta
is the operator

S_{\Theta}
acting on
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}:=H^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\ominus\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{F})
defined by

S_{\Theta}:=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}S_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}.

Clearly
S_{\Theta}
is a contraction operator on
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function.
Then
S_{\Theta}^{*n}\to 0
strongly as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

We remark that, just as in the scalar case,
S_{\Theta}^{*}=S_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}^{*}|_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}
.
Hence, for all
f\in\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
,

S_{\Theta}^{*n}f=S_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}^{*n}f\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).\qed

The next result is the analogue, in this context, of the Livšic–Moeller theorem,
Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function.
Then the spectrum and point spectrum of
S_{\Theta}
are given respectively by

\sigma(S_{\Theta})=Y_{\Theta}\cup Z_{\Theta}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{p}(S_{\Theta})=Z_{\Theta}^{0},

where:

•

Y_{\Theta}
is the complement in
\mathbb{T}
of
the union of open arcs where
\Theta
is regular and unitary-valued,

•

Z_{\Theta}
is the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\Theta(\lambda)
is not invertible,

•

Z_{\Theta}^{0}
is the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\Theta(\lambda)
is not injective.

Proof.

This follows by combining
[
11
, p.265, Theorem 4.1]

and
[
2
, p.116, Proposition 1.18]
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

If
\sigma(S_{\Theta})
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{T}
,
then
\Theta
has a holomorphic extension to
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\sigma(S_{\Theta})
.
This extension satisfies

\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}=\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}\qquad(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\sigma(S_{\Theta})).

Proof.

The function
\lambda\mapsto\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}

is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.
Moreover, by Theorem
5.2
, it continues analytically across
each point of
\mathbb{T}\setminus\sigma(S_{\Theta})

and coincides with
\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}
at these points.
It therefore provides an analytic continuation of
\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}

to the whole of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\sigma(S_{\Theta})
.
∎

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function.
Given
\Phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
,
we write
A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}
for the truncated Toeplitz operator on

\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
defined by

A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}f:=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}(\Phi f)\quad(f\in\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}).

The next result is a version of the commutant lifting theorem.
In particular, it yields a formula for
\|A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}\|

which is the vector-valued analogue of Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function.
If
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K}_{\Theta})
and

XS_{\Theta}=S_{\Theta}X
,
then
X=A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))

with
\Phi\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{F})\subset\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{F})

and
\|\Phi\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))}=\|X\|
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, p.118, Proposition 1.24]
.
∎

We use this result to obtain a lower bound for
\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|
when
S_{\Theta}
is invertible,
which corresponds to the fact that
\Theta(0)
is invertible.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function. Assume that
S_{\Theta}
is invertible.
Then

\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{{\delta_{n}(\Theta)}}-1\Bigr)\quad(n\geq 1),

where

(7)

\delta_{n}(\Theta):=\inf\Bigl\{\max\{|\lambda|^{n},\,\|\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\|\}:~\lambda\in\mathbb{D},\ x\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\ \|x\|=1\Bigr\}.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
. Clearly
S_{\Theta}^{-n}
commutes with
S_{\Theta}
,
so by Theorem
5.4
,
we can write
S_{\Theta}^{-n}=A_{\Phi}^{\Theta}
, where
\Phi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
with

\Phi\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{F})\subset\Theta H^{2}(\mathcal{F})
and
\|\Phi\|_{\infty}=\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|
.
Moreover, since we have
S_{\Theta}^{n}S_{\Theta}^{-n}=I_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}
,
it follows that

P_{\mathcal{K}_{\theta}}(S_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}^{n}\Phi-I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})})=0,

which implies that

S_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}^{n}\Phi-I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})}=\Theta\Xi,

where
\Xi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}))
(see
[
2
, p.119]
).

For all
x\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(8)

\lambda^{n}\Phi(\lambda)x-x=\Theta(\lambda)\Xi(\lambda)x.

Taking the
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
-inner product with
x
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\langle\Phi(\lambda)x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}-\langle\Theta(\lambda)\Xi(\lambda)x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n}\langle\Phi(\lambda)x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}-\langle\Xi(\lambda)x,\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\rangle_{\mathcal{F}},

whence, if
\|x\|=1
, then

(9)

1\leq|\lambda|^{n}\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+\|\Xi\|_{\infty}\|\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\|.

Now, taking radial limits in (
8
) as
\lambda\to\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
,
and using the fact that
\Phi(\zeta)
is an isometry
for a.e.
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
, we obtain

\|\zeta^{n}\Phi(\zeta)x-x\|=\|\Xi(\zeta)x\|
for a.e.
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
and every
x\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
In particular,
\|\Xi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+1
.
Feeding this information back into (
9
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|^{n}\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+(\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+1)\|\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\max\Bigl\{|\lambda|^{n},\ \|\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\|\Bigr\}\Bigl(2\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+1\Bigr).

As this holds for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
and all
x\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
\|x\|=1
,
we deduce that
1\leq\delta_{n}(\Theta)(2\|\Phi\|_{\infty}+1)
,
where
\delta_{n}(\Theta)
is defined by (
7
).
Thus
\|\Phi\|_{\infty}\geq(1/2)(1/\delta_{n}(\Theta)-1)
. Finally, as
\|S_{\Theta}^{-n}\|=\|\Phi\|_{\infty}
,
the result follows.
∎

Here are some alternative formulas for
\delta_{n}(\Theta)
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\Theta\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
be an inner function, and let
\delta_{n}(\Theta)
be given by
(
7
).

(1)

If
\sigma(S_{\Theta})\subset\mathbb{T}
, then

1/\delta_{n}(\Theta)=\sup_{|\lambda|<1}\min\bigl\{|\lambda|^{-n},\ \|\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}\|\bigr\}.

(2)

If
\sigma(S_{\Theta})\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
, then

1/\delta_{n}(\Theta)=\sup_{|\lambda|>1}\min\bigl\{|\lambda|^{n},\ \|\Theta(\lambda)\|\bigr\}.

Proof.

(i) By Theorem
5.2
, if
\sigma(S_{\Theta})\subset\mathbb{T}
, then
\Theta(\lambda)
is invertible
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
. In this case, we have

\inf\{\|\Theta(\lambda)^{*}x\|:\|x\|=1\}=1/\|(\Theta(\lambda)^{*})^{-1}\|=1/\|\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}\|.

The result follows upon feeding this information into (
7
).

(ii) Combine part (i) with Corollary
5.3
.
∎

The next result is an operator-valued analogue
of Theorem
3.2
.
An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is said to be

purely contractive
if

\|Tf\|<\|f\|\quad(f\in\mathcal{F},~f\neq 0).

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|=0
.
Then there exists a positive sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0

with the following property.
If
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces,
if
\Theta:(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a holomorphic function such that

\Theta|_{\mathbb{D}}
is an inner function,
and if
\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n}(\Theta)}{\epsilon_{n}}=0.

Proof.

The case
\dim(\mathcal{F})=\dim(\mathcal{F})=1
is just Theorem
3.2
.
Let
(\epsilon_{n})
be the sequence furnished by that theorem.
We shall show that it also works whenever
\dim(\mathcal{F}),\dim(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})<\infty
.

Fix
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
. Then
\Theta(\zeta_{0})

is a unitary operator from
\mathcal{F}
onto
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.
Thus, replacing
\Theta
by
\Theta(\zeta_{0})^{-1}\Theta
,
we can suppose from the outset that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a Hilbert space of dimension
N
,
and let
\Theta:(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F})

be a non-constant holomorphic function such that

\Theta|_{\mathbb{D}}
is an inner function.
Note that, since
\Theta(\lambda)\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}

is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
and

\Theta(\lambda)\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}=\Theta(1/\overline{\lambda})^{*}\Theta(\lambda)=I

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
,
the same equality persists for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E
.
In particular,
\Theta(\lambda)
is invertible for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E
,
and thus
\sigma(S_{\Theta})\subset E
.

Consider the function
\Delta:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\Delta(\lambda):=\det(\Theta(\lambda))^{1/N}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{D}).

Since
\Theta(\lambda)
is invertible, we have
\det(\Theta(\lambda))\neq 0
,
so there exists a holomorphic choice of
N
-th root on
\mathbb{D}
(any such choice will do).
Thus
\Delta
is holomorphic and nowhere zero on
\mathbb{D}
,
and has boundary values of modulus
1
on
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\setminus E
.
Also
|\Delta(\lambda)|<1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
since
\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.
In particular,
\Delta
is not a constant function.
Thus
\Delta
is a singular inner function with singular measure supported on
E
.

By Theorem
3.2
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n}(\Delta)}{\epsilon_{n}}=0.

Now, if
A
is any
N\times N
matrix, then
|\det(A)|\leq\|A\|^{N}
.
Applying this inequality with
A=\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}
,
we deduce, using Proposition
5.6
(i), that

\displaystyle 1/\delta_{n}(\Theta)

\displaystyle=\sup_{|\lambda|<1}\min\Bigl\{|\lambda|^{-n},\ \|\Theta(\lambda)^{-1}\|\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{|\lambda|<1}\min\Bigl\{|\lambda|^{-n},\ |\det(\Theta(\lambda)^{-1})|^{1/N}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|\lambda|<1}\min\Bigl\{|\lambda|^{-n},\ |\Delta(\lambda)|^{-1}\Bigr\}=1/\delta_{n}(\Delta).

Hence
\delta_{n}(\Theta)\leq\delta_{n}(\Delta)
for all
n
, and the result follows.
∎

5.2.
The model theorem

Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space, and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(H)
be a contraction.

The
defect operators
of
T
and
T^{*}
are respectively

D_{T}:=(I-T^{*}T)^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{T^{*}}:=(I-TT^{*})^{1/2}.

The corresponding
defect spaces
are

\mathcal{D}_{T}:=\overline{D_{T}(H)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}:=\overline{D_{T^{*}}(H)}.

Since
D_{T}
is a limit of polynomials in
(I-T^{*}T)
(and likewise for
D_{T^{*}}
),
the relation
T(I-T^{*}T)=(I-TT^{*})T
implies that

TD_{T}=D_{T^{*}}T.

In particular, we have
T(\mathcal{D}_{T})\subset\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
.

The
characteristic function
of
T
is the function

\Theta_{T}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
defined by

\Theta_{T}(\lambda)x:=\Bigl(-T+\lambda D_{T^{*}}(I-\lambda T^{*})^{-1}D_{T}\Bigr)x\quad(x\in\mathcal{D}_{T},\ \lambda\in\mathbb{D}).

Clearly
\Theta_{T}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}
. It extends, via the same formula, to
be holomorphic on
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}:1/\overline{\lambda}\notin\sigma(T)\}
.
In particular, if
\sigma(T)\subset\mathbb{T}
,
then
\Theta_{T}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\sigma(T)
.

The following result is a version of the model theorem for contractions.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
T
be a contraction on a separable Hilbert space
such that
T^{*n}\to 0
strongly as
n\to\infty
.
Then
\Theta_{T}
is a non-constant inner function,
and
T
is unitarily equivalent to
S_{\Theta_{T}}
.

Proof.

See
[
2
, p.115, Corollary 1.14]
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

If
T
is a non-unitary contraction, then, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
the operator
\Theta_{T}(\lambda)
is purely contractive, i.e.,

\|\Theta_{T}(\lambda)f\|<\|f\|\quad(f\in\mathcal{D}_{T},\ f\neq 0).

Proof.

See
[
11
, p.245]
.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

We begin with some preliminary reductions.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T
be a non-unitary contraction on a Hilbert space
H
.
Then there exists a separable, closed,
T
-reducing subspace
H_{1}
of
H

such that
T_{1}:=T|_{H_{1}}

is completely non-unitary.
If
(I-T^{*}T)
has finite rank, then so does
(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})
.
Furthermore, if
\sigma(T)\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
, then
\sigma(T_{1})\subset\sigma(T)

and
\|T_{1}^{-n}\|\leq\|T^{-n}\|
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By Langer’s Lemma
[
8
, p.7]
,
we can decompose
H
as the orthogonal direct sum
H^{\prime}\oplus H^{\prime\prime}
,
where
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
are closed,
T
-invariant subspaces such that

T|_{H^{\prime}}
is unitary and
T|_{H^{\prime\prime}}
is completely non-unitary.
Since
T
is non-unitary, we have
H^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
.
Pick a non-zero
x\in H^{\prime\prime}
,
and let
H_{1}
be the closure of the set
\{p(T,T^{*})x\}

as
p(T,T^{*})
runs through all non-commutative polynomials in
T,T^{*}
.
Then
H_{1}
is closed in
H
, separable,
T
-reducing,
and
T_{1}:=T|_{H_{1}}
is completely non-unitary.

Since
H_{1}
is
T
-reducing, we have
T_{1}^{*}=T^{*}|_{H_{1}}
.
It follows easily that
\rank(I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1})\leq\rank(I-T^{*}T)
.

Suppose now that
\sigma(T)\subsetneq\mathbb{T}
.
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(T)
.
By Runge’s theorem, there exists a
sequence of polynomials
p_{n}(z)
converging uniformly
to
1/(z-\lambda)
for
z
in a neighbourhood
of
\sigma(T)
, which implies that
p_{n}(T)\to(T-\lambda I)^{-1}
in norm.
In particular
(T-\lambda I)^{-1}H_{1}\subset H_{1}
.
This shows that
(T_{1}-\lambda I)
is invertible in
\mathcal{L}(H_{1})
,
with inverse
(T-\lambda I)^{-1}|_{H_{1}}
.
Thus
\sigma(T_{1})\subset\sigma(T)
.
It also follows that
\|T_{1}^{-n}\|=\|T^{-n}|_{H_{1}}\|\leq\|T^{-n}\|
for all
n\geq 1
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
T
be a completely non-unitary contraction on a separable Hilbert space,
and suppose that
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{T}
has Lebesgue measure zero. Then both
T^{n}
and
T^{*n}

converge strongly to zero as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

See
[
11
, Chapter II, Proposition 6.7]
.
∎

Finally, we assemble all the pieces.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|=0
.
Let
(\epsilon_{n})
be the sequence furnished by Theorem
5.7
,
and set
u_{n}:=1/\epsilon_{n}
.
Then
(u_{n})
is a positive sequence such that
u_{n}\to\infty
.
Let
T
be a non-unitary Hilbert-space contraction such that

\sigma(T)\subset E
and
\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
.
We shall prove that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|T^{-n}\|}{u_{n}}=\infty.

By Lemma
6.1
, it suffices to consider the case where,
in addition,
T
is a completely non-unitary contraction acting on a
separable Hilbert space. By Lemma
6.2

T^{*n}\to 0
strongly. Define
\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
and

\Theta_{T}
as in §
5.2
.
Since
(I-T^{*}T)
has finite rank, it follows that
\dim\mathcal{D}_{T}=\dim\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}<\infty
.
By Theorem
5.8
,
\Theta_{T}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})

is an inner function, and
T
is unitarily equivalent to the corresponding
compressed shift operator
S_{\Theta_{T}}
.
Furthermore, by Theorem
5.9
,
\Theta_{T}(\lambda)
is
purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.5
, we have

\|S_{\Theta_{T}}^{-n}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta_{n}(\Theta_{T})}-1\Bigr)\quad(n\geq 1),

where

\delta_{n}(\cdot)
is defined as in that theorem.
From Theorem
5.7
we also have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n}(\Theta_{T})}{\epsilon_{n}}=0.

Combining all these various facts, we deduce that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|T^{-n}\|}{u_{n}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\epsilon_{n}\|S_{\Theta_{T}}^{-n}\|\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\delta_{n}(\Theta_{T})}-1\Bigr)=\infty,

as was to be shown.
∎

7.
Conclusion

As mentioned in the introduction, Theorem
1.5

stops short of establishing Conjecture
1.3

because of the additional hypothesis that

\rank(I-T^{*}T)<\infty
. This hypothesis is needed in the proof
in order to use the determinant trick in Theorem
5.7
.
The full conjecture would be proved
if we could establish the following general version of Theorem
5.7
,
which we formulate as a conjecture.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}
such that
|E|=0
.
Then there exists a positive sequence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0

with the following property.
If
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are separable Hilbert spaces,
if
\Theta:(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus E)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a holomorphic function such that

\Theta|_{\mathbb{D}}
is an inner function,
and if
\Theta(\lambda)
is purely contractive for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta_{n}(\Theta)}{\epsilon_{n}}=0.

This remains unproved.
The ingredient that we lack is an explicit formula for
\Theta
of the type (
5
).

The sequence of higher order Mersenne numbers and associated binomial transforms
Kalika Prasad, Munesh Kumari, Rabiranjan Mohanta, Hrishikesh Mahato

Abstract
In this article, we introduce and study a new integer sequence referred to as the higher order Mersenne sequence. The proposed sequence is analogous to the higher order Fibonacci numbers and closely associated with the Mersenne numbers. Here, we discuss various algebraic properties such as Binet’s formula, Catalan’s identity, d’Ocagne’s identity, generating functions, finite and binomial sums, etc. of this new sequence, and some inter-relations with Mersenne and Jacobsthal numbers. Moreover, we study the sequence generated from the binomial transforms of the higher order Mersenne numbers and present the recurrence relation and algebraic properties of them. Lastly, we give matrix generators and tridiagonal matrix presentation for higher order Mersenne numbers.



Keywords:
Higher order Mersenne numbers, Partial and binomial sums, Binomial transform, Generating functions, Recurrence relations, Matrix generators.

[

Motivated by these works on the extension of a classical number sequence to a higher order, we propose and study the higher order Mersenne numbers. Here, we investigate various algebraic properties of higher order Mersenne numbers and show connections with Mersenne, Mersenne-Lucas, and Jacobsthal numbers.

Mersenne numbers
\{M_{n}\}
are given by the formula
2^{n}-1

p

may or may not be a prime and this investigation can be extended for our proposed sequence of higher order Mersenne numbers.

The Mersenne sequence
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is defined recursively as
[
2
]

M_{n+2}=3M_{n+1}-2M_{n}\quad\text{with }~M_{0}=0,~M_{1}=1.

(1.1)

Note that, it can be also given as
M_{n+1}=2M_{n}+1
with
M_{0}=0
. Thus, the first few Mersenne numbers are
0,1,3,7,15,31,63,127,255,...
.

The Mersenne-Lucas sequence
\{m_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is given by the same relation (
1.1
) but with
m_{0}=2,m_{1}=3
. Thus, the first few Mersenne-Lucas numbers are
2,3,5,9,17,33,65,129,...
.

The characteristic equation corresponding to (
1.1
) is
r^{2}-3r+2=0
whose roots are
r_{1}=1
and
r_{2}=2
. Thus, the Binet’s formulas for Mersenne and Mersenne-Lucas numbers are given as

M_{n}=2^{n}-1\quad\text{and}\quad m_{n}=2^{n}+1.

(1.2)

The aim of this study is to extend the Mersenne numbers to higher order in the same way that researchers did for the higher order Fibonacci, Jacobsthal and Jacobsthal-Lucas numbers. Besides algebraic properties of this new sequence, we also present some relations between Mersenne numbers, higher order Mersenne numbers, higher order Jacobsthal and Jacobsthal-Lucas numbers, etc. Finally, we give partial sum, binomial sum, binomial transform, sequence generated from binomial transform, and matrix representations for this new sequence.

In next section, we start with establishing some results for Mersenne numbers which will be required in our work. Then we introduce higher order Mersenne numbers and present their algebraic properties starting with Binet’s formula.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every positive integer
k
and
n\geq 0
, we have

M_{k(n+1)+\alpha}=2^{k}M_{kn+\alpha}+M_{k};\quad\text{with }\alpha=0,1,2,...,k-1.

Proof.

From Binet’s formula (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle M_{k(n+1)+\alpha}

\displaystyle=2^{k(n+1)+\alpha}-1

\displaystyle=2^{kn+\alpha}2^{k}-2^{k}+2^{k}-1

\displaystyle=2^{k}(2^{kn+\alpha}-1)+(2^{k}-1)

\displaystyle=2^{k}M_{kn+\alpha}+M_{k}.

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 0
. Then

M_{k(n+1)}=2^{k}M_{kn}+M_{k}.

(2.1)

Thus, for given
k

M_{kn},M_{kn+1},...,M_{kn+(k-1)}

follow the same recurrence relation provided in Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For non negative integers n and m, we have

M_{n+m}=2^{n}M_{m}+M_{n}.

Proof.

It can be easily proved using the Binet’s formula of Mersenne numbers.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The following identities hold

\displaystyle M_{2k}

\displaystyle=M_{k}(2^{k}+1)=M_{k}m_{k},

\displaystyle M_{3k}

\displaystyle=2^{k}M_{k}m_{k}+M_{k}=M_{k}(2^{k}m_{k}+1).

\displaystyle M_{4k}

\displaystyle=M_{k}m_{k}m_{2k}.

Proof.

For the third identity,

\displaystyle M_{4k}=2^{k}M_{k}(2^{k}m_{k}+1)+M_{k}=M_{k}[2^{k}(2^{k}m_{k}+1)+1]=M_{k}m_{k}m_{2k}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

and k be a positive integer then
M_{k}
divides
M_{kn}

Proof.

If we can show that

\displaystyle M_{kn}

\displaystyle=2^{kn}-1

\displaystyle=(2^{k})^{n}-1^{n}

\displaystyle=(2^{k}-1)[(2^{k})^{n-1}+(2^{k})^{n-2}+...+(2^{k})^{n-n+1}+1]

\displaystyle=M_{k}X(k,n),\quad\text{where }X(k,n)=(2^{k})^{n-1}+(2^{k})^{n-2}+...+(2^{k})+1.

Thus, this completes the proof.
∎

.

2.2
Binet’s formula and some identities

For the fixed positive integer
k
, the characteristic equation for (
2.3
) is
x^{2}-(2^{k}+1)x+2^{k}=0
, whose roots are
2^{k}
and 1. Thus, the Binet’s formula for higher order Mersenne numbers
M_{n}^{(k)}
is given by

M_{n}^{(k)}=\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}.

(2.6)

Theorem 2.7
.

The limiting ratio of higher order Mersenne numbers is
2^{k}
i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{M_{n+1}^{(k)}}{M_{n}^{(k)}}=2^{k}.

Proof.

From Binet’s formula (
2.6
), we can write

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{M_{n+1}^{(k)}}{M_{n}^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\dfrac{\frac{(2^{k})^{n+1}-1}{2^{k}-1}}{\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\dfrac{(2^{k})^{n+1}-1}{(2^{k})^{n}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\dfrac{1-\frac{1}{(2^{k})^{n+1}}}{\frac{1}{2^{k}}-\frac{1}{(2^{k})^{n+1}}}\right).

Now taking into account that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{(2^{k})^{n+1}}\right)=0
as
|\frac{1}{2^{k}}|<1
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{M_{n+1}^{(k)}}{M_{n}^{(k)}}=2^{k}.

∎

Theorem 2.8

(Catalan’s identity)
.

For
n\geq r
, we have

M_{n-r}^{(k)}M_{n+r}^{(k)}-{\Big(M_{n}^{(k)}\Big)}^{2}=-(2^{k})^{n-r}{\Big(M_{r}^{(k)}\Big)}^{2}.

(2.7)

Proof.

From Binet’s formula (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle M_{n-r}^{(k)}M_{n+r}^{(k)}-{\Big(M_{n}^{(k)}\Big)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n-r}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n+r}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)-\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big((2^{k})^{2n}-(2^{k})^{n-r}-(2^{k})^{n+r}+1-(2^{k})^{2n}+2(2^{k})^{n}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big(2(2^{k})^{n}-(2^{k})^{n-r}-(2^{k})^{n+r}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{k})^{n}}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big(2-(2^{k})^{-r}-(2^{k})^{r}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{k})^{n}}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big(\frac{2(2^{k})^{r}-1-(2^{k})^{2r}}{(2^{k})^{r}}\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2^{k})^{n-r}}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}{\Big((2^{k})^{r}-1\Big)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(2^{k})^{n-r}{\Big(M_{r}^{(k)}\Big)}^{2}.

∎

Corollary 2.9

(Cassini’s identity)
.

For positive integer n, we have

M_{n-1}^{(k)}M_{n+1}^{(k)}-{\Big(M_{n}^{(k)}\Big)}^{2}=-(2^{k})^{n-1}.

Theorem 2.10

(d’Ocagane identity)
.

For
m>n
, we have

M_{n+1}^{(k)}M_{m}^{(k)}-M_{n}^{(k)}M_{m+1}^{(k)}=(2^{k})^{n}M_{m-n}^{(k)}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Theorem 2.11

(Vajda’s identity)
.

For every

M_{n+m}^{(k)}M_{n+r}^{(k)}-M_{n}^{(k)}M_{n+m+r}^{(k)}=-(2^{k})^{n}\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{r}+1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)M_{m}^{(k)}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to Theorem

Theorem 2.12

(Honsberger’s identity)
.

For any integer
p>0
, we have

\displaystyle M_{p-1}^{(k)}M_{n}^{(k)}+M_{p}^{(k)}M_{n+1}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{k})^{p+n-1}m_{2k}-\big[(2^{k})^{p-1}+(2^{k})^{n}\big]m_{k}+2}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}.

Proof.

From the Binet’s formula (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle M_{p-1}^{(k)}M_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle+M_{p}^{(k)}M_{n+1}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{p-1}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)+\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{p}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n+1}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big((2^{k})^{p+n-1}-(2^{k})^{p-1}-(2^{k})^{n}+1+(2^{k})^{p+n+1}-(2^{k})^{p}-(2^{k})^{n+1}+1\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big((2^{k})^{p+n-1}-(2^{k})^{p-1}-(2^{k})^{n}+(2^{k})^{p+n+1}-(2^{k})^{p}+(2^{k})^{n+1}+2\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big((2^{k})^{p+n-1}(1+2^{2k})-(2^{k})^{p-1}(1+2^{k})-(2^{k})^{n}(1+2^{k})+2\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{({2^{k}-1})}^{2}}\Big[(2^{k})^{p+n-1}m_{2k}-\Big((2^{k})^{p-1}+(2^{k})^{n}\Big)m_{k}+2\Big]

∎

Theorem 2.13

(Generating function)
.

For higher order Mersenne sequence, we have

G(x,k)=\frac{x}{(x-1)(2^{k}x-1)}.

Proof.

Let
G(x,k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n}^{(k)}x^{n}
be the generating function for higher order Mersenne sequence.
Now multiplying both sides of (
2.3
) by
x^{n}
and then taking summation over
0
to
\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{M_{n+2}^{(k)}x^{n}-(2^{k}+1)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n+1}^{(k)}x^{n}+2^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n}^{(k)}}x^{n}=0.

(2.8)

Since,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n+2}^{(k)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big[G(x,k)-M_{1}^{(k)}x-M_{0}^{(k)}\Big]

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n+1}^{(k)}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\Big[G(x,k)-M_{0}^{(k)}\Big].

Thus, from (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big[G(x,k)-M_{1}^{(k)}x-M_{0}^{(k)}\Big]-(2^{k}+1)\frac{1}{x}\Big[G(x,k)-M_{0}^{(k)}\Big]+2^{k}G(x,k)=0

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{x^{2}}\Big[G(x,k)-x-0\Big]-(2^{k}+1)\frac{1}{x}\Big[G(x,k)-0\Big]+2^{k}G(x,k)=0

\displaystyle\implies\Big[\frac{1}{x^{2}}-\frac{2^{k}+1}{x}+2^{k}\Big]G(x,k)=\frac{1}{x}

\displaystyle\implies G(x,k)=\frac{x}{1-(2^{k}+1)x+2^{k}x^{2}}

\displaystyle\implies G(x,k)=\frac{x}{(x-1)(2^{k}x-1)}.

∎

For instance, setting
k=1
in the above theorem gives the generating function for Mersenne sequence i.e.

G(x,1)=\frac{x}{1-3x+2x^{2}}.

Theorem 2.14
.

The exponential generating function
E(x,k)
for higher order Mersenne numbers is

E(x,k)=\frac{{e}^{2^{k}x}-{e}^{x}}{2^{k}-1}.

Proof.

Using Binet’s formula (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle E(x,k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{n}^{(k)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(2^{k}x)}^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{{e}^{2^{k}x}-{e}^{x}}{2^{k}-1}.

∎

2.3
Partial sum and Binomial sum

Theorem 2.17

(Partial sum)
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}^{(k)}=\frac{2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}-n}{M_{k}}.

Proof.

Using the Binet’s formula of higher order Mersenne number, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{k}-1}{M_{k}}+\frac{{(2^{k})}^{2}-1}{M_{k}}+...+\frac{{(2^{k})}^{n}-1}{M_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big(2^{k}+{(2^{k})}^{2}+...+{(2^{k})}^{n}-n\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big(2^{k}\frac{{(2^{k})}^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}-n\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big(2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}-n\Big).

∎

Theorem 2.18

(Partial sum with even indexes)
.

For
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{2i}^{(k)}=\frac{2^{2k}M_{n}^{(2k)}-n}{M_{k}}.

Proof.

Proceeding with the Binet’s formula as above, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{2i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=M_{2}^{(k)}+M_{4}^{(k)}+...+M_{2n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{k})^{2}-1}{M_{k}}+\frac{{(2^{k})}^{4}-1}{M_{k}}+...+\frac{{(2^{k})}^{2n}-1}{M_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big((2^{k})^{2}+{(2^{k})}^{4}+...+{(2^{k})}^{2n}-n\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big(2^{2k}\frac{{(2^{k})}^{2n}-1}{(2^{k})^{2}-1}-n\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M_{k}}\Big(2^{2k}M_{n}^{(2k)}-n\Big).

∎

k,n\in\mathbb{N}

\sum_{i=0}^{n}M_{2i+1}^{(k)}=\frac{2^{k}M_{n+1}^{(2k)}-n-1}{M_{k}}.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to the above theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.20

(Binomial sum)
.

For the higher order Mersenne numbers
\{M_{n}^{(k)}\}
, we have

\displaystyle 1.~

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}M_{n}^{(k)}=\frac{(1+2^{k})^{s-1}-2^{s-1}}{2^{k}-1}.

\displaystyle 2.~

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{n}\binom{s-1}{n}M_{n}^{(k)}=(-1)^{s-1}M_{k}^{s-2}.

Proof.

1. From Binet’s formula (
2.6
), we write

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}M_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}(2^{k})^{n}-\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big((1+2^{k})^{s-1}-2^{s-1}\Big)\quad(\text{using~Binomial~theorem}).

2. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{n}\binom{s-1}{n}M_{n}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{n}\binom{s-1}{n}\frac{(2^{k})^{n}-1}{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}(-2^{k})^{n}-\sum_{n=0}^{s-1}\binom{s-1}{n}(-1)^{n}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big((1-2^{k})^{s-1}-0\Big)\quad(\text{using~Binomial~theorem})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-2^{k})^{s-1}}{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s-1}M_{k}^{s-2}.

∎

2.4
Binomial transforms

Now, we give the binomial transform
B_{k}=\{b_{k,n}\}
for the higher order Mersenne numbers, where

b_{k,n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i}^{(k)}.

(2.11)

Note that the binomial transform of the classical Mersenne numbers is
B_{1}={0,1,5,19,65,211,...}
which is indexed in OEIS as A001047. Thus on following (
2.11
), the binomial transforms of the higher order Mersenne numbers for different values of
k
are

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,5,19,65,211,...\}:A001047

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,7,39,203,1031,...\}:A016127\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,11,103,935,8431,..\}:A016133\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,19,327,5567,94655,...\}

\displaystyle B_{5}

\displaystyle=\{0,1,35,1159,38255,1262431,...\}.

Lemma 2.21
.

For the binomial transform, the following relation is provided

b_{k,n+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big(M_{i+1}^{(k)}+M_{i}^{(k)}\Big).

Proof.

Note that, from binomial theorem we can write

\binom{n+1}{i}=\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n}{i-1}

Hence from (
2.11
)

\displaystyle b_{k,n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}M_{i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\Big[\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n}{i-1}\Big]M_{i}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i+1}^{(k)}

∎

Thus, from Lemma
2.21
we deduce that

b_{k,n+1}-b_{k,n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i+1}^{(k)}.

Theorem 2.22

(Recurrence relation)
.

The binomial transform
\{b_{k,n}\}
holds the following relation

b_{k,n+1}=(2+m_{k})b_{k,n}-2m_{k}b_{k,n-1},\quad n\geq 1

(2.12)

with initial conditions
b_{k,0}=0
and
b_{k,1}=1
where
m_{k}
is the kth Mersenne-Lucas number.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.21
, we have

\displaystyle b_{k,n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big(M_{i+1}^{(k)}+M_{i}^{(k)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big(M_{i+1}^{(k)}+M_{i}^{(k)}\Big)+(M_{1}^{(k)}+M_{0}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big((2^{k}+1)M_{i}^{(k)}-2^{k}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+M_{i}^{(k)}\Big)+(M_{1}^{(k)}+M_{0}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\Big((2^{k}+2)M_{i}^{(k)}-2^{k}M_{i-1}^{(k)}\Big)+1

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+2)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i}^{(k)}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+2)b_{k,n}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1.

(2.13)

Now, replacing
n+1
by
n
, we get

\displaystyle b_{k,n}

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+2)b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+2)b_{k,n-1}+2^{k}b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1

\displaystyle=2(2^{k}+1)b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}M_{i}^{(k)}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1

\displaystyle=2m_{k}b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[\binom{n-1}{i-1}+\binom{n-1}{i}\Big]M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1

\displaystyle=2m_{k}b_{k,n-1}-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}+1,\quad\text{( Since, $\binom{n-1}{n}=0$)}.

Now substituting expression
1-2^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{n}{i}M_{i-1}^{(k)}
in (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle b_{k,n+1}

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+2)b_{k,n}+b_{k,n}-2m_{k}b_{k,n-1}

\displaystyle=(2+m_{k})b_{k,n}-2m_{k}b_{k,n-1}

where
b_{k,0}=0
and
b_{k,1}=1
.
∎

Theorem 2.23

(Binet-type formula for
b_{k,n}
)
.

An explicit formula for binomial transform of the higher order Mersenne sequence is given by

b_{k,n}=\frac{{m_{k}}^{n}-2^{n}}{2^{k}-1}.

Proof.

On substituting
b_{k,n}=r^{n}
in (
2.12
) and simplifying it, we get the characteristic equation as

r^{2}-(2+m_{k})r-2m_{k}=0

whose characteristic roots are obtained as
r_{1}=m_{k}
and
r_{2}=2
.
Hence, solution of (
2.12
) is given as

\displaystyle b_{k,n}=C_{1}{m_{k}}^{n}+C_{2}2^{n}

(2.14)

and by using the initial values of recurrence relation (
2.12
), we get the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
as
1/M_{k}
and
-1/{M_{k}}
, respectively. After, replacing the values of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
in (
2.14
), we get the required result.
∎

Theorem 2.24

(Generating function )
.

For binomial transform of the higher order Mersenne sequence, we have

b_{k}(x)=\frac{x}{1-(2+m_{k})x+2m_{k}x^{2}}

(2.15)

Proof.

By virtue of Barry
[
1
]
and Prodinger
[
13
]
, if
f(x)
is the ordinary generating function of sequence
\{a_{n}\}
then the generating function of the binomial transform of
\{a_{n}\}
is given by
\frac{1}{1-x}f\Big(\frac{x}{1-x}\Big)
.

Thus, considering
f(x)=G(x,k)
from Theorem
2.13
, we get the required generating function.
∎

Theorem 2.25

(Exponential generating function)
.

For binomial transform of higher order Mersenne numbers, we have

E_{k}(x)=\frac{e^{m_{k}x}-e^{2x}}{2^{k}-1}.

Proof.

Let
E_{k}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{k,n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
be the exponential generating function for
\{b_{k,n}\}
. Then from Theorem
2.23
, we write

\displaystyle E_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{{m_{k}}^{n}-2^{n}}{2^{k}-1}\Big)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({m_{k}}^{n}-2^{n})x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}-1}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({m_{k}}x)^{n}}{n!}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2x)^{n}}{n!}\Big).

Thus, on simplification we get the required result.
∎

3
Matrix representation

Here, we give matrix representation and tridiagonal matrix structure for higher order Mersenne numbers and study closed form formula for higher order Mersenne numbers via determinant of tridiagonal matrices.

Consider the matrix
U=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-2^{k}&2^{k}+1\\
\end{bmatrix}
,
then clearly
\det U=2^{k}
.

Now, in next theorem we give a generating matrix which consists higher order Mersenne numbers as their entries and having
U
as base matrix.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle U^{n}=\begin{bmatrix}-2^{k}M_{n-1}^{(k)}&M_{n}^{(k)}\\
-2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}&M_{n+1}^{(k)}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(3.1)

Proof.

We prove it by inductive hypothesis on
n
. For
n=1
,

\displaystyle U=\begin{bmatrix}-2^{k}M_{0}^{(k)}&M_{1}^{(k)}\\
-2^{k}M_{1}^{(k)}&M_{2}^{(k)}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-2^{k}&2^{k}+1\\
\end{bmatrix}

which is verified using (
2.3
) by considering the initial values.
Suppose the result i.e. (
3.1
) is true for
n
. Then

\displaystyle U^{n+1}=U^{n}U

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-2^{k}M_{n-1}^{(k)}&M_{n}^{(k)}\\
-2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}&M_{n+1}^{(k)}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-2^{k}&2^{k}+1\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}&-2^{k}M_{n-1}^{(k)}+(2^{k}+1)M_{n}^{(k)}\\
-2^{k}M_{n+1}^{(k)}&-2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}+(2^{k}+1)M_{n+1}^{(k)}\\
\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-2^{k}M_{n}^{(k)}&M_{n+1}^{(k)}\\
-2^{k}M_{n+1}^{(k)}&M_{n+2}^{(k)}\\
\end{bmatrix}\qquad\text{(using relation \eqref{recurrence_homn})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For
n\geq 1
, the determinant of matrix
U^{n}
is
2^{kn}
and the trace of matrix
U^{n}
is
1+2^{kn}
.

Proof.

Since,
\det U^{n}=-2^{k}[M_{n-1}^{(k)}M_{n+1}^{(k)}-(M_{n}^{(k)})^{2}]
.
Hence, the determinant follows from Corollary
2.9
.
Similarly, for the
trace
of
U^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle trace(U^{n})

\displaystyle=-2^{k}M_{n-1}^{(k)}+M_{n+1}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-2^{k}\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n-1}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)+\Big(\frac{(2^{k})^{n+1}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)\quad\text{(from \eqref{binethomn})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{2^{k}-2^{kn}+2^{kn+k}-1}{2^{k}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{k}-1)(1+2^{kn})}{2^{k}-1}=1+2^{kn}

as required.
∎

Thus, we can say that the matrix
U^{n}
is a non-singular matrix independent of
k
and
n
and its eigenvalues are
2^{kn}
and
1
.

Now, we use tridiagonal matrices and give that the
n
th term of the higher order Mersenne numbers can be obtained as the determinant of tridiagonal matrix
V_{n-1}
, where matrix
V_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle V_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{2}^{(k)}&M_{1}^{(k)}&&&&\\
2^{k}&(2^{k}+1)&1&&&\\
&2^{k}&(2^{k}+1)&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&2^{k}&(2^{k}+1)&1\\
&&&&2^{k}&(2^{k}+1)\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n}.

Note that, a tridiagonal matrix is a square matrix whose all the elements except on the main diagonal, first sub-diagonal and first super-diagonal, are zero.

Taking into account the properties of determinant and continuant of a tridiagonal matrix, we have

\displaystyle\det(V_{1})

\displaystyle=M_{2}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\det(V_{2})

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+1)\det(V_{1})-2^{k}M_{1}^{(k)}=M_{3}^{(k)}\quad\text{(from \eqref{recurrence_homn})}

\displaystyle\det(V_{3})

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+1)\det(V_{2})-2^{k}M_{2}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+1)\det(V_{2})-2^{k}\det(V_{1})=M_{4}^{(k)}\quad\text{(from \eqref{recurrence_homn})}

\displaystyle~~\vdots

\displaystyle\det(V_{n})

\displaystyle=(2^{k}+1)\det(V_{n-1})-2^{k}\det(V_{n-2})=M_{n+1}^{(k)}.

Thus, we have the following result involving higher order Mersenne numbers and the determinant of a tridiagonal matrix.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
V_{n}
be a tridiagonal matrix as defined above, then

M_{n+1}^{(k)}=\det(V_{n}).

We should note that for all
n\geq 1
,
M_{n+1}^{(k)}>0
, so we can conclude that the above tridiagonal matrix
V_{n}
is a non-singular matrix. Let us assume that
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{n}
are
n
eigenvalues of
V_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}...\lambda_{n}=M_{n+1}^{(k)}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+...+\lambda_{n}=n(2^{k}+1).

(3.2)

Since (
3.2
) is valid for all
n\geq 1
, hence all the eigenvalues of
V_{n}
are positive, therefore matrix
V_{n}
is a positive definite matrix.

4
Conclusion

In summary, we introduced a sequence of higher order Mersenne numbers, which is closely associated with the Mersenne numbers. We presented Binet’s formula, famous identities, generating functions, finite and binomial sums, etc. of this sequence, and established some inter-relations with Mersenne and higher order Jacobsthal numbers. In addition, a new sequence associated with the binomial transforms of higher order Mersenne numbers is identified and their recurrence relation and some properties are also examined. Lastly, we presented generator matrix and tridiagonal matrix associated with higher order Mersenne numbers.

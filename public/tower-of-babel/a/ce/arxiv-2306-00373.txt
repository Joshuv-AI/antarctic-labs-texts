Intersection cohomology groups of instanton moduli spaces and cotangent bundles of affine flag varieties
Hiraku Nakajima

Abstract




This is an abstract for my talk at the 68th Geometry Sympoisum on
August 31, 2021. It is based on my joint work in progress with
Dinakar Muthiah
.

1.
Affine Grassmannian and the cotangent bundle of the flag variety

Let us first recall a result of Arkhipov-Bezrukavnikov-Ginzburg

[
ABG04
]
.
Let
G
be a simply-connected complex semisimple group with the Lie
algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We fix a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
and the maximal
torus
T\subset B
. Let
G/B
be the flag variety and
T^{*}(G/B)
its
cotangent bundle. Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the Lie algebra of
B
. Then

T^{*}(G/B)=G\times_{B}({\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{b})^{*}
.
Let
\mu
be a dominant weight of
G
and consider a line bundle

\mathcal{O}(\mu)
over
T^{*}(G/B)
, pull-backed from
G/B
. Let us
consider the space

H^{0}(T^{*}(G/B),\mathcal{O}(\mu))

of sections of
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
.

On the other hand, let us consider the affine Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
associated with the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
,
which is an infinite dimensional partial flag variety
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})/G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
.
Here
\mathcal{O}={\mathbb C}[[z]]
(formal power series ring) and

\mathcal{K}={\mathbb C}((z))
(formal Laurent power series ring).
Let us denote by
T^{\vee}
a maximal torus of
G^{\vee}
, which is the
dual torus of
T
.
We consider a coweight
\lambda
as an element of
T^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})
, and
further a point
z^{\lambda}\in G^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})/G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
by the
composite
T^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})\to G^{\vee}(\mathcal{K})\to\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
.
Note that
\lambda
can be also regarded as a weight of
G
.
We have an action of
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
on
\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
. It is well-known
that
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
-orbits are parametrized by dominant coweights

\lambda
of
G^{\vee}
as
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})z^{\lambda}
. Let

\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}
denote the closure of

G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})z^{\lambda}
. It is known that

\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}
is a projective variety. It is an
analog of a Schubert variety in the (finite dimensional) flag
variety. We consider the intersection cohomology complex

\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda})
associated with the constant
local system.

We consider the above
\mu
as a dominant coweight of
G^{\vee}
, and
denote the inclusion of the point
z^{\mu}
in
\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
by

i_{\mu}
. Then one of main results in
[
ABG04
]
is a

G
-equivariant graded vector space isomorphism

(1.1)

H^{0}(T^{*}(G/B),\mathcal{O}(\mu))\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)^{*}\otimes H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}))[\mu(2\rho^{\vee})],

where the summation runs over dominant weights
\lambda
.
We denote the irreducible representation of
G
with the highest
weight
\lambda
by
V(\lambda)
,
a cohomological degree shift by
[\ \ ]
,
and the sum of fundamental coweights of
G
by
\rho^{\vee}
. The grading on
the left hand side is given by the
{\mathbb C}^{\times}
-action, the dilatation
on fibers of
T^{*}(G/B)
. Moreover the isomorphism (
1.1
) is
compatible with products: the product in the left hand side is given
by
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1})\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mu_{2})\cong\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})
. The
product on the right hand side is given by the convolution on

\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
.
See
[
ABG04
, Th. 8.5.2]
.

We view (
1.1
) as a construction of
T^{*}(G/B)
by

topology
of the affine Grassmannian, as
T^{*}(G/B)
can be
recovered from
\bigoplus_{\mu}H^{0}(T^{*}(G/B),\mathcal{O}(\mu))
as a
homogeneous spectrum.
We will see similar constructions below.

For a later purpose let us extend (
1.1
). The maximal torus

T^{\vee}
acts on
\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
so that
z^{\mu}
is a fixed point.
Therefore we can consider the
T^{\vee}
-equivariant cohomology

H^{*}_{T^{\vee}}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee}}))
. On the
other hand we replace
T^{*}(G/B)\cong G\times_{B}({\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{b})^{*}
by
a larger space
G\times_{B}({\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{u})^{*}
where
\mathfrak{u}
is
the nilpotent radical of
\mathfrak{b}
. It gives Grothendieck’s
simultaneous resolution
G\times_{B}({\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{u})^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
of the
deformation
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
of the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}
, parametrized
by
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Here
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is the dual of the Lie
algebra of
T
, which is the Lie algebra of
T^{\vee}
, and

\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{*}_{T^{\vee}}(\mathrm{pt})
. We have

(1.2)

H^{0}(G\times_{B}({\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{u})^{*},\mathcal{O}(\mu))\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)^{*}\otimes H^{*}_{T^{\vee}}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}))[\mu(2\rho^{\vee})].

This is a
G
-equivariant isomorphism of graded

{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]\cong H^{*}_{T^{\vee}}(\mathrm{pt})
-modules.
It is specialized to (
1.1
) at the fiber of
0
in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

2.
Singular monopoles

Let us rewrite
H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}))
so
that it looks a slightly familiar to geometers and physicists.

Let us first define a slice to the
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
-orbit through

z^{\mu}
. Let
G^{\vee}[z^{-1}]_{1}
be the kernel of the evaluation at

\infty
homomorphism
G^{\vee}[z^{-1}]\to G^{\vee}
. Let

\mathcal{W}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
denote the orbit
G^{\vee}[z^{-1}]_{1}z^{\mu}
and
define

\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{W}_{G^{\vee},\mu}.

It is known that
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
is a transversal slice to

\mathrm{Gr}^{\mu}_{G^{\vee}}
in
\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}
. Then we can replace

H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}))[\mu(2\rho^{\vee})]
by

H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu})),

where
i_{\mu}^{!}
is now regarded as an inclusion to

\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
. Thus (
1.1
) means that
T^{*}(G/B)

is constructed by topology of
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
.

The space, called
affine Grassmannian slice
is also a
differential geometric object.
Let
G^{\vee}_{c}
be the maximal compact subgroup of
G^{\vee}
. We
consider a
G^{\vee}_{c}
-monopole on
{\mathbb R}^{3}
, i.e., a solution of the
Bogomolny equation for a pair
(A,\Phi)
, a
G^{\vee}_{c}
-connection and
a section of the adjoint bundle:

*F_{A}=d_{A}\Phi.

They satisfy the asymptototic condition at
|x|\to\infty
, given by

\mu
. To make sense of this condition,
\mu
must be a coweight of

G^{\vee}
, but not necessarily dominant.
(We are considering the so-called maximal symmetry breaking
case.)

We further assume that
(A,\Phi)
has a Dirac-type singularity at
0
,
which means that it comes from an
S^{1}
-equivariant instantons on

{\mathbb R}^{4}
locally around
0
. Then the fiber over the origin in
{\mathbb R}^{4}

is equipped with a homomorphism
\tilde{\lambda}\colon S^{1}\to G^{\vee}_{c}
,
whose conjugacy class is the dominant coweight
\lambda
of
G^{\vee}

above.

Then we consider the framed moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
of
such singular
G^{\vee}_{c}
-monopoles. As for moduli spaces of instantons
on
4
-manifolds, we need to consider Uhlenbeck (partial)
compactification to get a reasonable space. Since we are considering

S^{1}
-equivariant instantons, the bubbling could occur the origin in

{\mathbb R}^{4}
. Therefore the symmmetric product of the base
4
-manifold
does not appear, and we consider

\bigcup_{\mu\leq\nu\leq\lambda}\mathcal{M}^{\nu}_{G^{\vee},\mu},

where
\nu
runs over the set of dominant coweights between
\mu
and

\lambda
.
Then this space is homeomorphic to
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
. This
was proved by Braverman-Finkelberg
[
BF10
]
.
A very similar result, namely for local behaviour around
0
, was
explained earlier by Kapustin-Witten
[
KW07
]
.

A little bit more precisely, Braverman-Finkelberg
[
BF10
]

imposed a different boundary condition at
|x|\to\infty
from the
usual one imposed for monopoles on
{\mathbb R}^{3}
such as in

[
Hit83
,
HM89
,
AH88
]
.
Considering a singular monopole on
{\mathbb R}^{3}
as an
S^{1}
-equivariant
instanton on
{\mathbb R}^{4}
as above, they imposed that the instanton extends
to
S^{4}={\mathbb R}^{4}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Then
\mu
is regarded as the homomorphism corresponding to the fiber
at
\infty
. Such
\mu
is dominant.
Moreover it is expected that two monopole moduli spaces with different
asymptotic behavior are isomorphic as algebraic varieties (but
different hyper-Kähler structures) when
\mu
is dominant and the
latter space can be defined.
We will return back this issue when we discuss Coulomb branches of
quiver gauge theories in
section

5
.

Let me also mention that similar results in closely related situations
were studied in
[
CH11
,
Smi16
]
.

3.
Geometric Satake correspondence

The isomorphism (
1.1
) was built on the geometric Satake
correspondence

[
Lus83
,
Gin95
,
MV07
,
BD00
]
, which gives
an equivalence of tensor categories

\operatorname{Rep}G\cong\operatorname{Perv}_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}.

Here
\operatorname{Rep}G
is the category of finite dimensional
representations of
G^{\vee}
, which is a semisimple abelian category
equipped with the tensor product
\otimes
. The right hand side is the
category of
G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant perverse sheaves on

\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
, which is an abelian category equipped with the tensor
product givey by the convolution. Moreover this equivalence sends

V(\lambda)
to
\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda})
.

The weight space
V(\lambda)_{\mu}
of
V(\lambda)
is realized as
follows (see
[
MV07
]
and also
[
BF10
]
for an explicit
use of
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
): Let
\rho
be the sum of
fundamental weights of
G
. We consider it as a regular dominant
cocharacter
\rho\colon{\mathbb C}^{\times}\to T^{\vee}
. We then consider the
induced
{\mathbb C}^{\times}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
.
It is well-known that the fixed point set, denoted by

(\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu})^{\rho=0}
, consists of a single point

z^{\mu}
.
More generally we consider an affine algebraic variety
X
with
a
{\mathbb C}^{\times}
-action.
We define the
repelling set

X^{\rho\leq 0}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\left\{x\in X\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\rho(t)\cdot x\text{ exists}\right\}.

We have a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\rho=0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\rho\leq 0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where
\iota
,
j
are the inclusion, and
p
denotes the morphism
given by taking the limit
\eta(t)\cdot x
for
t\to\infty
.
Then the hyperbolic restriction functor
\Phi
is defined as

\iota^{*}j^{!}
on objects on the
{\mathbb C}^{\times}
-equivariant derived
category. See
[
Bra03
,
DG14
]
.
Then we have

V(\lambda)_{\mu}\cong H^{0}(\Phi(\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}))).

In this case it is also known that the hyperbolic restriction functor
vanishes in other degrees than
0
.

Note that there is a difference between this cohomology and one
appears in (
1.1
), i.e.,

H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}))[\mu(2\rho^{\vee})]\cong H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}_{G^{\vee},\mu}^{\lambda}))
. It is the costalk
at
z^{\mu}
. On the other hand, the above is defined by

\Phi=\iota^{*}j^{!}
. Compare it with
i_{\mu}^{!}=\iota^{!}j^{!}
.
They are same dimensional vector spaces, it is hard to distinguish
them.
We will clarify the difference in
section

6
. In the situation of
the geometric Satake for the finite dimensional group
G
, it was
clarified by Ginzburg-Riche
[
GR15
]
. We will follow it
in the Kac-Moody setting.

4.
Coulomb branches and ring objects

Let us return to (
1.1
). We rewrite the right hand side as

H^{*}(i_{\mu}^{!}\mathcal{A}_{R})[\mu(2\rho^{\vee})]\quad\text{where }\mathcal{A}_{R}=\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)^{*}\otimes\operatorname{IC}(\overline{\mathrm{Gr}}_{G^{\vee}}^{\lambda}).

Then observe that
\mathcal{A}_{R}
corresponds to

\bigoplus_{\lambda}V(\lambda)^{*}\otimes V(\lambda)
under the geometric
Satake correspondence. It is further isomorphic to the regular
representation
{\mathbb C}[G]
by the Peter-Weyl theorem. Note
{\mathbb C}[G]
is a
commutative ring object in
\operatorname{Rep}(G)
(more precisely its
completion as we take an infinite sum). The concept of a ring object
is an abstract notion: it is equipped with a homomorphism

{\mathbb C}[G]\otimes{\mathbb C}[G]\to{\mathbb C}[G]
satisfying associativity,
commutativity, etc. Therefore
\mathcal{A}_{R}
is a ring object in

\operatorname{Perv}_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
.

This observation gave motivation to study more general
ring
objects
in
\operatorname{Perv}_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
, or the
corresponding derived category
D_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
in

[
BFN19a
]
. Given a ring object
\mathcal{A}
in

D_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
, we apply the same construction to get a
graded comutative ring. We further take its spectrum or homogeneous
spectrum to define a scheme. The result could be a new scheme. Even if
we get a known scheme, e.g.
T^{*}(G/B)
as above, we could get a new
perspective to the known scheme.

The mathematical definition of the Coulomb branch of a
3D
SUSY gauge
theory
[
Nak16
,
BFN18
]
can be regarded
as a construction of a ring object. Let us briefly recall the
construction. Suppose a representation
\mathbf{N}
of
G^{\vee}
is given. We
consider the
variety
\mathcal{R}
of BFN triples

(\mathcal{P},\varphi,s)
:

B
undle
:

a principal
G^{\vee}
-bundle
\mathcal{P}
over the formal disk

D=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits\mathcal{O}
,

F
raming
:

a framing

\varphi\colon\mathcal{P}|_{D^{*}}\to G\times D^{*}
of
\mathcal{P}

over the formal punctured disk
D^{*}=\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits\mathcal{K}
,

N
-valued section
:

a section
s
of an associated vector
bundle
\mathcal{P}\times_{G^{\vee}}\mathbf{N}
over
D
.

We impose the condition that
\varphi(s)
extends across
0\in D
. It
is a nontrivial constraint on
s
as
\varphi
is defined only over
D^{*}
.

If we only consider a pair
(\mathcal{P},\varphi)
, it defines the
affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
.

A convolution product is defined on the equivariant Borel-Moore
homology
H^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R})
, which is shown to be commutative. Then
the Coulomb branch for a SUSY gauge theory associated with

(G^{\vee},\mathbf{N})
is defined as
\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R})
.

Let
\omega_{\mathcal{R}}
be the dualizing complex on
\mathcal{R}
. Then

H^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R})\cong H^{*}_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}(\omega_{\mathcal{R}})
. The cohomology

H^{*}_{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}
can be regarded as pushforward for the projection

\mathcal{R}\to\mathrm{pt}
. We forget
\mathbf{N}
-sections from the variety of
triples, we have the morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{R}\to\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
.
The convolution product on
H^{G^{\vee}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R})
also gives a structure
of a ring object on
\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{R}}
. See
[
BFN19a
]
.

We explain two nontrivial sources of ring objects in

D_{G^{\vee}}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}})
so far. We further have several
manupulations constructing new ring objects from given ones. Therefore
we get many ring objects, which give interesting spaces, such as Higgs
branches of class
S
theories asked by Moore-Tachikawa

[
MT12
]
.

5.
Geometric Satake correspondence for a Kac-Moody Lie
algebra

Braverman-Finkelberg
[
BF10
]
proposed analog of
geometric Satake correspondence for an affine Kac-Moody group
G

(assumed to be dual of untwisted and simply-connected type).
They used Uhlenbeck partial
compactification of
(G^{\vee})_{{\operatorname{fin}},c}
-instantons on

{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}{\mathbb R}^{4}/({\mathbb Z}/\ell{\mathbb Z})
, where
G^{\vee}
is the dual of

G
,
(G^{\vee})_{\operatorname{fin}}
is the associated finite dimensional group,

(G^{\vee})_{{\operatorname{fin}},c}
is a maxmimal compact subgroup of
(G^{\vee})_{\operatorname{fin}}
,
and
\ell
is the level of representations of
G
.
Uhlenbeck space serves as an analog of
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
. It
is a natural analog as monopoles are
S^{1}
-equivariant instantons as
we explained above.

When
G
is of type
A^{(1)}_{n-1}
, proposed statements were mostly
checked by the second-named author
[
Nak09
]
. Uhlenbeck
spaces are quiver varieties of type
A_{\ell-1}^{(1)}
, hence related
to representations of
\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(\ell)_{\mathrm{aff}}

[
Nak94
,
Nak98
]
. Then level rank duality

[
Fre82
,
Has89
]
between
\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(n)_{\mathrm{aff}}
at level
\ell
and

\operatorname{\mathfrak{sl}}(\ell)_{\mathrm{aff}}
at level
n
implies the statements.
One of missed statements in
[
Nak09
]
is about the costalk
of
\operatorname{IC}
of Uhlenbeck space. It will be deduced from analog of
(
1.1
) in the affine setting.

In
[
BFN19b
]
Braverman-Finkelberg and the
second-named author generalized and refined the proposal in

[
BF10
]
by replacing Uhlenbeck spaces by Coulomb
branches
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
of quiver gauge theories.

Let us fix the notation for Coulomb branches
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
. We
take a quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices,
and
Q_{1}
is the set of oriented edges. Each oriented edge
h
defines
the outgoing and incoming vertices, denoted by

\operatorname{o}(h)\xrightarrow{h}\operatorname{i}(h)
.
We take two
Q_{0}
-graded vector spaces

V=\bigoplus_{i}V_{i},\qquad W=\bigoplus_{i}W_{i}

and define
\lambda
,
\mu
as
\lambda=\sum\dim W_{i}\Lambda_{i}
,

\mu=\lambda-\sum\dim V_{i}\alpha_{i}
, where
\Lambda_{i}
(resp.
\alpha_{i}
) is the
i
-th fundamental weight (resp. of simple root)

{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}G
. We then define

\mathbf{G}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\prod_{i}\operatorname{GL}(V_{i}),\qquad\mathbf{N}\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\bigoplus_{h}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{\operatorname{o}(h)},V_{\operatorname{i}(h)})\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\operatorname{Hom}(W_{i},V_{i}).

We consider the variety
\mathcal{R}
of BFN triples for
\mathbf{G}
(instead
of
G^{\vee}
),
\mathbf{N}
in the construction explained in
section

4
.
Then we define the Coulomb branch as

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)\overset{\operatorname{\scriptstyle def.}}{=}\operatorname{Spec}\nolimits H^{\mathbf{G}(\mathcal{O})}_{*}(\mathcal{R}).

We have

Theorem 5.1

(
[
BFN19b
]

cf.
[
NW19
]
for nonsymmetric case)
.

Suppose
Q
is of finite type and let
G^{\vee}
be the corresponding
group of adjoint type. Assume further that
\mu
is dominant. Then

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)\cong\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
.

When
\mu
is not a dominant, we need to introduce a variant of the
affine Grassmannian slice
\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
, called a
generalized slice. The above result holds for general
\mu
if we replace

\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{G^{\vee},\mu}
by a generalized slice.

The generalized slice is expected to be isomorphic to the Uhlenbeck
partial compactification of the moduli space of singular

G_{c}
-monopoles on
{\mathbb R}^{3}
. Here we impose the usual asymptotic
behavior at
|x|\to\infty
, contrary to the result by
Braverman-Finkelberg
[
BF10
]
mentioned in

section

2
.

Let us consider the case
G
as above, dual of an untwisted affine
Kac-Moody group, and let
(G^{\vee})_{{\operatorname{fin}},c}
as above. Then it is
expected that
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
is the Uhlenbeck partial
compactification of
(G^{\vee})_{{\operatorname{fin}},c}
-instantons on the Taub-NUT
space divided by
{\mathbb Z}/\ell{\mathbb Z}
.
The Taub-NUT space is isomorphic to
{\mathbb C}^{2}
as a complex manifold, but
has a hyper-Kähler metric different from the Euclidean one.
The difference between instantons on
{\mathbb R}^{4}
and ones on the Taub-NUT
space corresponds to the difference of the asymptotic behavior of
monopoles at
|x|\to\infty
mentioned above.

When
G
is of affine type
A
, instantons on the Taub-NUT space are
described by variants of the ADHM description, which also involves a
certain nonlinear ordinary equation, called the Nahm’s equation. This
is a result of Cherkis
[
Che09
]
. He named instanton moduli
spaces
bow varieties
in this description.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
NT17
]
)
.

Suppose
Q
is of affine type
A
. Then
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
is
isomorphic to a bow variety.

Considering
S^{1}
-equivariant instantons on the Taub-NUT space, we can
get singular monopoles with the ordinary asymptotic behavior at

|x|\to\infty
. Therefore the expectation above is true for arbitrary

\mu
when
G
is of type
A
. Moreover when
\mu
is dominant, a bow
variety is isomorphic to a quiver variety, which is the moduli space
of instantons on
{\mathbb R}^{4}/({\mathbb Z}/\ell{\mathbb Z})
, as expected.

Let us return back to geometric Satake correspondence for a Kac-Moody
Lie group.

It is known that
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
has an action of
T^{\vee}
.
It is conjectured that the fixed point set
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)^{\rho=0}

is either empty or a single point, and the latter happens if and only
if
V(\lambda)_{\mu}\neq 0
.
We consider the hyperbolic restriction functor
\Phi
as above.
Let us denote by
\operatorname{IC}^{\lambda}_{\mu}
the intersection cohomology complex

\operatorname{IC}(\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu))
for brevity.
The proposal in
[
BFN19b
]
is an existence of a
natural isomorphism

(5.3)

H^{0}(\Phi(\operatorname{IC}^{\lambda}_{\mu}))\xrightarrow[\cong]{\text{?}}V(\lambda)_{\mu}.

This generalization makes sense for a symmetric Kac-Moody Lie group

G
of simply-connected type, and can be even generalized to
symmetrizable non-symmetric cases thanks to

[
NW19
]
. It is a refinement as Coulomb branches
make sense for any weights
\mu
, while Uhlenbeck spaces, as for

\overline{\mathcal{W}}{}^{\lambda}_{\mu}

dominant
weights.
For finite type, this refinement is not so crucial, as we could use

z^{\mu}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{G^{\vee}}
for nondominant
\mu
. But it does not make
sense for general Kac-Moody Lie algebras.

The proposal in
[
BFN19b
]
was further refined by the
second-named author
[
Nak18
]
: A concrete
construction of representations of
G
was given under assumptions on
geometric properties of Coulomb branches. These assumptions were
proved for type
A^{(1)}_{n-1}

6(ii).
Strategy of a proof

Let us explain a strategy of a proof of the above conjecture. This
strategy was used in the finite dimensional case in
[
GR15
]
,
and will be also used in affine type
A
in the work in progress with
D. Muthiah.

Let
{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{rs}}^{*}]
be the localization of
{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]

inverting all coroots. Thanks to the localization theorem for
equivariant cohomology groups, (
6.1
) becomes an isomorphism
after
\otimes{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{rs}}^{*}]
.
On the other hand, the left vertical arrow in (
6.3
) also
becomes an an isomorphism after
\otimes{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{rs}}^{*}]
.
Therefore
\heartsuit
exists over
\otimes{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{rs}}^{*}]
.
Hence what we need to show is that
\heartsuit
is an isomorphism over

{\mathbb C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
. Possible poles of
\heartsuit
appear at coroot
hyperplanes
\alpha^{\vee}=0
.

Suppose
\alpha^{\vee}
is a real coroot, i.e., a Weyl group translate
of a simple coroot
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. The Weyl group symmetry can be
realized in the diagram (
6.3
) by the dynamical Weyl group
introduced by Etingof-Varchenko
[
EV02
]
. This was shown in

[
GR15
]
for the finite dimensional setting, and expected to
be true in general.
Thanks to the dynamical Weyl group,
\heartsuit
extends across

\alpha^{\vee}=0
if and only if it extends across
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}=0
.
By the framework in
[
Nak18
]
to define the geometric
Satake isomorphism (
5.3
), the behavior at
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}=0

corresponds to the restriction to the Levi subgroup
L_{i}

corresponding to
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
. In particular, the extension of

\heartsuit
across
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}=0
follows from the same assertion
for
L_{i}
. Then
L_{i}
is basically
\operatorname{\rm SL}_{2}
, one checks the extension
by an explicit calculation of all entries of (
6.3
).

If
G
is a finite dimensional group, all coroots are real, hence
there is nothing more to be done.

If
G
is of affine type, there is a primitive imaginary coroot
c
, and all imaginary roots are its multiple. Thus we need to
understand what happens at
c=0
.
Instead of
L_{i}
above, we need to treat the (homogeneous) Heisenberg
subgroup
\operatorname{Heis}
.

On the other hand, in the right column of (
6.3
), we replace

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
by the fixed point set with respect to a
cocharacter
\rho_{\delta}\colon{\mathbb C}^{\times}\to T^{\vee}
, whose
differential is contained in
c=0
, but not in other coroot
hyperplanes thanks to the localization theorem of equivariant
cohomology groups.
It is expected that the fixed point set

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)^{\rho_{\delta}=0}

is an appropriate symmetric power of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}={\mathbb C}^{2}/({\mathbb Z}/\ell{\mathbb Z})
.

In order to relate the symmetric power of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}
with the
Heisenberg subgroup, we consider its resolution given by a Hilbert
scheme of points on the minimial resolution of
{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}
. Then one
uses the construction
[
Nak97
]
. Two entries of the right
column of (
6.3
) are easily understood by this construction, and
can be compared with the left column. This finihses the proof in
affine type
A
.

This strategy should work more general affine types. Most of
difficulties appear in the proof of geometric Satake (
5.3
). As
we remarked at the end of
section

5
, we need to check some
geometric properties of
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,\mu)
.

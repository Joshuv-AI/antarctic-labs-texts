Maurer-Cartan characterizations and cohomologies of compatible Lie algebras
Jiefeng Liu, Yunhe Sheng, Chengming Bai

Abstract
. In this paper, we give Maurer-Cartan characterizations as well as a cohomology theory for compatible Lie algebras. Explicitly, we first introduce the notion of a bidifferential graded Lie algebra and thus give Maurer-Cartan characterizations of compatible Lie algebras. Then we introduce a cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras and use it to classify infinitesimal deformations and abelian extensions of compatible Lie algebras. In particular, we introduce the reduced cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and establish the relation between the reduced cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and the cohomology of the corresponding compatible linear Poisson structures introduced by Dubrovin and Zhang in their study of bi-Hamiltonian structures. Finally, we use the Maurer-Cartan approach to classify nonabelian extensions of compatible Lie algebras.

17B56, 13D10

1.
Introduction

A compatible Lie algebra is a pair of Lie algebras such
that any linear combination of these two Lie brackets is still a
Lie bracket. Such structures closely relate to the
(infinitesimal) deformations of Lie algebras. Compatible Lie
algebras appear in a lot of fields in mathematics and mathematical
physics. For example, compatible Lie algebras naturally
one-to-one correspond to compatible linear Poisson structures,
whereas the latter are important Poisson structures related to
bi-Hamiltonian structures. Recall that a bi-Hamiltonian structure
is a pair of Poisson structures on a manifold which are
compatible, i.e. their sum is again a Poisson structure. Note that
the pioneering work in the integrable bi-Hamiltonian systems was
done by Magri in
[
24
]
and many completely integrable
Hamiltonian systems arising in mechanics, mathematical physics and
geometry have the bi-Hamiltonian structures
(
[
12
,
20
,
25
]
). Bi-Hamiltonian structures are very
powerful to construct separation of variables and description of
properties of solutions in the theory of integrable Hamiltonian
systems. See the book
[
22
]
for more details on linear
Poisson structures and
[
2
,
3
,
29
,
31
]
for more details
of compatible Lie algebras and compatible linear Poisson
structures and their applications. Furthermore, compatible Lie
algebras also appear in the study of the classical Yang-Baxter
equation and principal chiral field (
[
14
]
), loop algebras
over Lie algebras (
[
15
]
) and elliptic theta functions
(
[
16
]
). Also see
[
6
,
28
,
30
,
32
]
for more details on
operads, classifications and bialgebra theory of compatible Lie
algebras.

The cohomology theory is a classical approach associating
invariants to a mathematical structure. Cohomology controls
deformations and extension problems of the corresponding algebraic
structures. Cohomology theories of various kinds of algebras have
been developed and studied in
[
4
,
13
,
18
,
19
]
. See the
review paper
[
17
]
for more details.

In this paper, we study the cohomology theory of compatible Lie
algebras. By using the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
constructed by a compatible Lie algebra, we give a
cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras. We would like to
point out that the cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras
given here is not merely a combination of cohomologies of the two
Lie algebras as many other properties or structures of compatible
Lie algebras do so. In particular, we construct a new cochain
complex for a compatible Lie algebra which is independent of the
usual cochain complex for the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of
a Lie algebra.
Then we study infinitesimal deformations of a compatible Lie
algebra. We show that infinitesimal deformations of a compatible
Lie algebra are classified by the second cohomology group. In particular, it leads to the
introduction of the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible
Lie algebra which generates a trivial deformation of the
compatible Lie algebra, which is similar to the study of Nijenhuis
operators on Lie algebras that play an important role in
the study of integrability of nonlinear evolution equations
(
[
5
]
).

Furthermore, we introduce the cohomology of a compatible Lie
algebra associated to arbitrary representation. We would also like to
point out that there might be more than one cohomology theory for compatible Lie algebras. Such a phenomenon
has already appeared in the study of cohomologies of some
algebraic systems with more than one product such as Poisson
algebras (
[
1
,
10
]
). In particular, the coproduct
for a compatible Lie bialgebra given in
[
32
]
is not a

1
-cocycle in the sense of the cohomology theory in this paper,
that is, there might be another cohomology theory for compatible
Lie algebras such that it is a
1
-cocycle. Then we study abelian
extensions of a compatible Lie algebra and show that they are
classified by the second cohomology group. We also
introduce the reduced cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and
establish the relation between the reduced cohomology of a
compatible Lie algebra and the cohomology of the corresponding
compatible linear Poisson structures introduced by Dubrovin and
Zhang in
[
8
]
in the study of bi-Hamiltonian structures.
Finally, we study nonabelian extensions of compatible Lie algebras
by a similar method introduced in
[
11
]
. We construct a bidifferential graded Lie algebra associated
to compatible Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
and describe nonabelian
extensions of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
by its Maurer-Cartan elements.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we
briefly recall the cohomology theory and the Nijenhuis-Richardson
bracket for Lie algebras. In Section
3
, we
first recall the notion of compatible Lie algebras and their
representations. Then we introduce the notions of a bidifferential
graded Lie algebra and its Maurer-Cartan elements. In particular,
we give the Maurer-Cartan characterizations of compatible Lie
algebras. Next we introduce
a cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras and study
infinitesimal deformations of compatible Lie algebras. In Section

4
, we introduce a cohomology theory of a compatible
Lie algebra with coefficients in a representation and use this
cohomology to classify abelian extensions of a compatible Lie
algebra. Then we introduce the reduced cohomology of a
compatible Lie algebra and establish the relation between the
reduced cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and the cohomology
of the corresponding compatible linear Poisson structures. In
Section
5
, we use the Maurer-Cartan
approach to classify nonabelian extensions of compatible Lie
algebras.

In this paper, all the vector spaces are over algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
, and finite dimensional.

2.
The Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket

In this section, we recall the cohomology theory for Lie algebras
and the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket.

A permutation
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-unshuffle if
\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(i)
and
\sigma(i+1)<\cdots<\sigma(n)
. If
i=0
or
i=n
, we assume
\sigma=\rm{Id}
. The set of all
(i,n-i)
-unshuffles will be denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{(i,n-i)}
.

A
Maurer-Cartan element
of a differential graded Lie algebra
(L=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{i},[-,-],\partial)
is an element
P\in L_{1}
such that

\partial P+\frac{1}{2}[P,P]=0.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. We consider the graded vector space
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, where
C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and the degree of elements in
C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
are defined to be
n-1
. Then
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
equipped with the
Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket

(1)

[P,Q]_{\mathrm{NR}}=P\circ Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\circ P,\quad\forall~P\in C^{p+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

is a graded Lie algebra, where
P\circ Q\in C^{p+q+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

(2)

P\circ Q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(q+1,p)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P(Q(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(q+1)}),x_{\sigma(q+2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(p+q+1)}).

Note that any graded Lie algebra is a differential
graded Lie algebra with the zero differential.
The following result is well known.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
26
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector
space. Then
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
defines a Lie algebra if and only if
\pi
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}})
, i.e.
[\pi,\pi]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a Lie algebra. Because of the graded Jacobi identity, we get a coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}:C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow C^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
defined by

\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}\omega=(-1)^{n-1}[\pi,\omega]_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\forall~\omega\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

More precisely, for
\omega\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
and
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi}\omega(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\pi(x_{i},\omega(x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n+1}))

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}\omega(\pi(x_{i},x_{j}),x_{1},\cdots,\hat{x_{i}},\cdots,\hat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n+1}),

which is just the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator for the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
associated to the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad})
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be two vector spaces. The elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
are denoted by
x,y,x_{i}
and the elements in
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are denoted by
u,v,v_{i}
. For a given linear map
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, we define a linear map
\hat{f}\in C^{k+l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
by

\hat{f}((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},v_{k+l}))=(\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(k,l)}}\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)f(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},v_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,v_{\tau(k+l)}),0).

Similarly, for
f:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}
, we define a linear map
\hat{f}\in C^{k+l}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
by

\hat{f}((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},v_{k+l}))=(0,\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{S}_{(k,l)}}\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)f(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},v_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,v_{\tau(k+l)})).

The linear map
\hat{f}
is called a
lift
of
f
. For
example, the lifts of linear maps
\alpha:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}

and
\beta:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{2}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are respectively
given by

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}\big((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha(x_{1},x_{2}),0),

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}\big((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,\beta(x_{1},v_{2})-\beta(x_{2},v_{1})).

We define
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l}=\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The vector space
\wedge^{n}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l}
with
k+l=n
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
be two vector
spaces. A linear map

f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+l+1}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}),\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
has a

bidegree

k|l
, if the following conditions hold:

(i)

If
X
is an element in
\mathfrak{g}^{k+1,l}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{1};

(ii)

If
X
is an element in
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l+1}
, then
f(X)\in\mathfrak{g}_{2};

(iii)

All the other case,
f(X)=0.

We call a linear map
f

homogeneous
if
f
has a bidegree and denote its bidegree by
||f||
. The linear maps
\hat{\alpha},~\hat{\beta}\in C^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
given by (
3
) and (
4
) have the bidegree
||\hat{\alpha}||=||\hat{\beta}||=1|0
.

The following lemma shows that the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket on
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2})
is compatible with the
bigrading.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
||f||=l_{f}|k_{f}
and
||g||=l_{g}|k_{g}
, then
[f,g]_{\mathrm{NR}}
has the bidegree
l_{f}+l_{g}|k_{f}+k_{g}.

The following result is well known. See the survey
[
17
]
for more details.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have

(5)

[\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
a representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}\in C^{1\mid 0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
. Denote by
C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V):=C^{n\mid-1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
and
C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.
By the lift
map defined above, we have

C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V):=C^{n\mid-1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V).

Define the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}:C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

(6)

\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}f:=(-1)^{n-1}[\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho},\hat{f}]_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\forall~f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V).

In fact, since
\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}\in C^{1|0}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
and
f\in C^{n|-1}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V)
, by Lemma
2.3
, we have
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}f\in C^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. By Proposition
2.4
, we have
[\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0
. Because of the graded Jacobi identity, we have
\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi+\rho}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}=0
. Thus we obtain a well-defined cochain complex
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V),\mathrm{d}^{*}_{\pi+\rho})
.

By a direct calculation, we have

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
be a Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
a representation of

\mathfrak{g}
. Then for all
f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and

x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho({x_{i}})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}f(\pi(x_{i},x_{j}),\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,\hat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1}).

Thus the coboundary operator
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi+\rho}
defined by (
6
) is exactly the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator for the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\pi)
with coefficients in
(V;\rho)
.

3.
Bidifferential graded Lie algebras, Maurer-Cartan characterizations and cohomologies of compatible Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notions of a bidifferential graded Lie algebra and its
Maurer-Cartan elements. We construct the bidifferential graded Lie
algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are compatible Lie algebra
structures. We also give a cohomology
theory of a compatible Lie algebra, and use the second cohomology
group to classify infinitesimal deformations.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
14
,
15
,
27
]
)
A
compatible Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space,

[-,-]
and

\{-,-\}
are Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}

such that

(7)

[\{x,y\},z]+[\{y,z\},x]+[\{z,x\},y]+\{[x,y],z\}+\{[y,z],x\}+\{[z,x],y\}=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.2
.

(
[
21
,
30
]
)

A triple
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
is a compatible Lie
algebra if and only if

[-,-]
and

\{-,-\}
are Lie algebra structures on
\mathfrak{g}
such that for any

k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
, the following bilinear operation

(8)

\left\llbracket x,y\right\rrbracket=k_{1}[x,y]+k_{2}\{x,y\},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

defines a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
homomorphism
between two compatible Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a linear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
\varphi
is both a Lie algebra homomorphism between
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and a Lie algebra homomorphism between
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Definition 3.4
.

(
[
32
]
)

A
representation
of a compatible Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
on a
vector space
V
consists of a pair of linear maps

\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
such that
\rho
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
on
V
,
\mu
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
on
V
, and

(9)

\displaystyle\rho(\{x,y\})+\mu([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x),\mu(y)]-[\rho(y),\mu(x)].

Example 3.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra. Define
\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
\mathrm{ad}_{x}y=[x,y]
and
\mathfrak{ad}_{x}y=\{x,y\}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{ad})
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
, which is called the
adjoint representation
.

3.1.
Bidifferential graded Lie algebras and Maurer-Cartan characterizations of compatible Lie algebras

In this subsection, we introduce the notions of a bidifferential
graded Lie algebra and its Maurer-Cartan elements. In particular,
we give the Maurer-Cartan characterizations of compatible Lie
algebras and the bidifferential graded Lie algebra that controls
deformations of a compatible Lie
algebra.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1})
and
(L,[-,-],\partial_{2})
be two differential graded Lie algebras. We call the quadruple
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
a
bidifferential graded Lie algebra
if
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}

satisfy

(10)

\partial_{1}\circ\partial_{2}+\partial_{2}\circ\partial_{1}=0.

The following proposition gives an equivalent description of
a bidifferential graded Lie algebra.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1})
and
(L,[-,-],\partial_{2})
be two differential graded Lie algebras. Then
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
is a bidifferential graded Lie algebra if and only if for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
,
(L,[-,-],\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}})
is a differential graded Lie algebra, where
\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}=k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2}
.

Proof.

Assume that
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
is a bidifferential graded Lie algebra. Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}\circ\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2})\circ(k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}^{2}\partial_{1}\circ\partial_{1}+k_{1}k_{2}(\partial_{1}\circ\partial_{2}+\partial_{2}\circ\partial_{1})+k^{2}_{2}\partial_{2}\circ\partial_{2}=0.

Furthermore, for any homogenous elements
x,y\in L
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2})[x,y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}([\partial_{1}x,y]+(-1)^{|x|}[x,\partial_{1}y])+k_{2}([\partial_{2}x,y]+(-1)^{|x|}[x,\partial_{2}y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2})x,y]+(-1)^{|x|}[x,(k_{1}\partial_{1}+k_{2}\partial_{2})y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}x,y]+(-1)^{|x|}[x,\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}y].

Thus for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
,
(L,[-,-],\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}})
is a differential graded Lie algebra.

The converse can be proved similarly. We omit the details.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
be a bidifferential graded Lie algebra. A pair
(P_{1},P_{2})\in L_{1}\oplus L_{1}
is called a
Maurer-Cartan element
of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are Maurer-Cartan elements of the differential graded Lie algebras
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1})
and
(L,[-,-],\partial_{2})
respectively, and

(11)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}P_{2}+\partial_{2}P_{1}+[P_{1},P_{2}]=0.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
be a bidifferential graded Lie algebra. A pair
(P_{1},P_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
if and only if for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
,
k_{1}P_{1}+k_{2}P_{2}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}})
.

Proof.

Assume that the pair
(P_{1},P_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
. Then for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}}(k_{1}P_{1}+k_{2}P_{2})+\frac{1}{2}[k_{1}P_{1}+k_{2}P_{2},k_{1}P_{1}+k_{2}P_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}^{2}(\partial_{1}P_{1}+\frac{1}{2}[P_{1},P_{1}])+k_{1}k_{2}(\partial_{1}P_{2}+\partial_{2}P_{1}+[P_{1},P_{2}])+k_{2}^{2}(\partial_{2}P_{2}+\frac{1}{2}[P_{2},P_{2}])=0.

Thus
k_{1}P_{1}+k_{2}P_{2}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie
algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{k_{1},k_{2}})
.

The converse can be proved similarly. We omit the details.
∎

Let
(L,[-,-])
be a graded Lie algebra. It is obvious that
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)
is a bidifferential graded Lie algebra. Consider the graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}})
, we obtain the following main result.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space,
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is a compatible Lie algebra if and only if
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
,

\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
define Lie algebra structures
on
\mathfrak{g}
respectively if and only if

[\pi_{1},\pi_{1}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,\quad[\pi_{2},\pi_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Moreover,
[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0
exactly corresponds to the compatibility condition (
7
).
Thus,
(\mathfrak{g},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is a compatible Lie algebra if and only if

(12)

[\pi_{1},\pi_{1}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,\quad[\pi_{1},\pi_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,\quad[\pi_{2},\pi_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,

which means that
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)
.
∎

By twisting with a Maurer-Cartan element, we can obtain a new bidifferential graded Lie algebra, which controls deformations the original Maurer-Cartan element.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(P_{1},P_{2})
be a Maurer-Cartan element of a bidifferential
graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
. Then

(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})
is a
bidifferential graded Lie algebra, where
\tilde{\partial}_{1}
and

\tilde{\partial}_{2}
are defined by

\tilde{\partial}_{1}Q=\partial_{1}Q+[P_{1},Q],\quad\tilde{\partial}_{2}Q=\partial_{2}Q+[P_{2},Q],\quad\forall~Q\in L.

For any
\tilde{P}_{1},\tilde{P}_{2}\in L_{1}
,

(P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1},P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of
the bidifferential graded Lie algebra

(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
if and only if

(\tilde{P}_{1},\tilde{P}_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the
bidifferential graded Lie algebra

(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})
.

Proof.

Since
P_{1}
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the differential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1})
,
(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{1})
is a differential graded Lie algebra. Similarly,
(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{2})
is a differential graded Lie algebra. Since
\partial_{1}P_{2}+\partial_{2}P_{1}+[P_{1},P_{2}]=0
, by properties of
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
and the graded Jacobi identity, for any
Q\in L
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{1}\tilde{\partial}_{2}Q+\tilde{\partial}_{2}\tilde{\partial}_{1}Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\partial_{2}Q+[P_{1},\partial_{2}Q]+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{1}[P_{2},Q]+\frac{1}{2}[P_{1},[P_{2},Q]]

\displaystyle+\partial_{2}\partial_{1}Q+[P_{2},\partial_{1}Q]+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{2}[P_{1},Q]+\frac{1}{2}[P_{2},[P_{1},Q]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{2}P_{1},Q]+\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{1}P_{2},Q]+\frac{1}{2}[[P_{1},P_{2}],Q]=0,

which implies that
\tilde{\partial}_{1}\tilde{\partial}_{2}+\tilde{\partial}_{2}\tilde{\partial}_{1}=0
.
Thus
(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})
is a bidifferential graded Lie algebra.

If
(P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1},P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
, then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{1}(P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}[P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1},P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{2}(P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2})+\frac{1}{2}[P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2},P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\partial_{1}(P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2})+\partial_{2}(P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1})+[P_{1}+\tilde{P}_{1},P_{2}+\tilde{P}_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Furthermore, since
(P_{1},P_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{1}\tilde{P}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{P}_{1},\tilde{P}_{1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{2}\tilde{P}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{P}_{2},\tilde{P}_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{1}\tilde{P}_{2}+\tilde{\partial}_{2}\tilde{P}_{1}+[\tilde{P}_{1},\tilde{P}_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus
(\tilde{P}_{1},\tilde{P}_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(L,[-,-],\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})
. The converse can be proved similarly. We omit the details.
∎

Now we are ready to give the bidifferential graded Lie algebra that controls deformations of a compatible Lie algebra.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
be a compatible Lie algebra. Then
we have the following conclusions.

(1)

(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})

\tilde{\partial}_{1}
and
\tilde{\partial}_{2}

\tilde{\partial}_{1}={[\pi_{1},Q]}_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\tilde{\partial}_{2}={[\pi_{2},Q]}_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\forall~Q\in C^{q}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}).

(2)

For any

\tilde{\pi}_{1},\tilde{\pi}_{2}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\tilde{\partial}_{1},\tilde{\partial}_{2})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.10
,
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)

3.11
, we obtain the conclusions.
∎

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})

n

.

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])

(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
.

3.3.
Infinitesimal deformations of compatible Lie algebras

In this subsection, we study infinitesimal deformations of compatible Lie algebras using the cohomology theory of compatible Lie algebras. In particular, we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible Lie algebra which gives rise to a trivial infinitesimal deformation.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra,

(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

Define

(13)

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})

We set

\pi_{1}(x,y)=[x,y],\quad\pi_{2}(x,y)=\{x,y\}.

By Theorem

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{t},\{-,-\}_{t})

\begin{array}[]{rclrclrcl}[{\pi}_{1},{\omega}_{1}]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,&[{\pi}_{1},{\omega}_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}+[{\pi}_{2},{\omega}_{1}]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,&[{\pi}_{2},{\omega}_{2}]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,\\

The first line of (

) means that

(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is a
2

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
, i.e.

(

(\mathfrak{g},\pi_{1},\omega_{1})

In the sequel, we recall Nijenhuis operators on Lie algebras and introduce the notion of Nijenhuis operators on compatible Lie algebras.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})

is a compatible Lie algebra
homomorphism from

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{N},\{-,-\}_{N})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
.

Proof.

Let
N:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a Nijenhuis operator on the compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
. By Proposition
3.21
,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket)
, where the bracket
\left\llbracket-,-\right\rrbracket
is given by (
8
). By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y\right\rrbracket_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket N(x),y\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket x,N(y)\right\rrbracket-N\left\llbracket x,y\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}[x,y]_{N}+k_{2}\{x,y\}_{N}.

By Proposition
3.19
,

(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{N},\{-,-\}_{N})
is a compatible Lie algebra and
N
is a
compatible
Lie algebra
homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{N},\{-,-\}_{N})
to
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
.
∎

Definition 3.23
.

An infinitesimal deformation
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{t},\{-,-\}_{t})
of a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
generated by
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is
trivial
if there exists
N\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
{\rm{Id}}+tN:(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{t},\{-,-\}_{t})\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
is a compatible Lie algebra homomorphism.

One can deduce that
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{t},\{-,-\}_{t})
is a trivial infinitesimal deformation if and only if

(25)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,N(y)]+[N(x),y]-N([x,y]),

(26)

\displaystyle N(\omega_{1}(x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[N(x),N(y)],

(27)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,N(y)\}+\{N(x),y\}-N(\{x,y\}),

(28)

\displaystyle N(\omega_{2}(x,y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{N(x),N(y)\}.

By
(\ref{Nij1})
and
(\ref{Nij2})
,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
. By
(\ref{Nij3})
and
(\ref{Nij4})
,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
. Thus
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
.

We have seen that a trivial infinitesimal deformation of a compatible Lie algebra gives rise to a Nijenhuis operator. In fact, the converse is also true.

Theorem 3.24
.

Let
N
be a Nijenhuis operator on a compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
. Then an infinitesimal
deformation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
can be
obtained by putting

(29)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,N(y)]+[N(x),y]-N[x,y],

(30)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,N(y)\}+\{N(x),y\}-N\{x,y\},

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Furthermore, this infinitesimal
deformation is trivial.

Proof.

Since
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\mathbbm{d}^{1}N
,
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is a
2
-cocycle. Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
,
\omega_{1}(x,y)=[x,y]_{N}
and
\omega_{2}(x,y)=\{x,y\}_{N}
, by Proposition
3.22
,
(\mathfrak{g},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is also a compatible Lie algebra. Thus (
14
) holds and then
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{t},\{-,-\}_{t})
is an infinitesimal
deformation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
.

It is straightforward to verify that
(
25
)-(
28
) are satisfied. Thus this infinitesimal
deformation is trivial.
∎

4.
Cohomologies of compatible Lie algebras: general theory

In this section, we give cohomologies of
compatible Lie algebras with coefficients in arbitrary
representations, and use the second cohomology group
to classify abelian extensions of a compatible Lie algebra. We also
introduce the reduced cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and
give its connection to Dubrovin and Zhang’s cohomology of
compatible Poisson structures.

4.1.
Cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra with coefficients in arbitrary representation

In the following, we give the cohomology of a compatible Lie
algebra associated to arbitrary representation.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra with
\pi_{1}(x,y)=[x,y]
and
\pi_{2}(x,y)=\{x,y\}
and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

With the above notations, the pair
(\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)
, i.e.,

(31)

\displaystyle[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,\quad[\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0,\quad[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Proof.

Since
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
, by Proposition
2.4
, we have

[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Similarly, since
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
is a Lie algebra and
(V;\mu)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
, we have

[\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

For
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V
, by (
7
) and (
9
) we have

\displaystyle[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu}]_{\mathrm{NR}}((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2}),(x_{3},v_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})\big((\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2})),(x_{3},v_{3})\big)+(\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})\big((\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})((x_{2},v_{2}),(x_{3},v_{3})),(x_{1},v_{1})\big)

\displaystyle+(\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})\big((\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})((x_{3},v_{3}),(x_{1},v_{1})),(x_{2},v_{2})\big)+(\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})\big((\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})((x_{1},v_{1}),(x_{2},v_{2})),(x_{3},v_{3})\big)

\displaystyle+(\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})\big((\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})((x_{2},v_{2}),(x_{3},v_{3})),(x_{1},v_{1})\big)+(\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})\big((\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho})((x_{3},v_{3}),(x_{1},v_{1})),(x_{2},v_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})+\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}(x_{2},x_{3}),x_{1})+\pi_{1}(\pi_{2}(x_{3},x_{1}),x_{2})

\displaystyle+\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),x_{3})+\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}(x_{2},x_{3}),x_{1})+\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}(x_{3},x_{1}),x_{2}),

\displaystyle\rho(\pi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}))v_{3}+\mu(\pi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}))v_{3}-[\rho(x_{1}),\mu(x_{2})](v_{3})

\displaystyle-[\rho(x_{2}),\mu(x_{1})](v_{3})+\rho(\pi_{2}(x_{2},x_{3}))v_{1}+\mu(\pi_{1}(x_{2},x_{3}))v_{1}

\displaystyle-[\rho(x_{2}),\mu(x_{3})](v_{1})-[\rho(x_{3}),\mu(x_{2})](v_{1})+\rho(\pi_{2}(x_{3},x_{1}))v_{2}

\displaystyle+\mu(\pi_{1}(x_{3},x_{1}))v_{2}-[\rho(x_{3}),\mu(x_{1})](v_{2})-[\rho(x_{1}),\mu(x_{3})](v_{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0,0),

which implies that
[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu}]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.
Thus
(\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu})
is a Maurer-Cartan element of the bidifferential graded Lie algebra
(C^{*}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,\mathfrak{g}\oplus V),[-,-]_{\mathrm{NR}},\partial_{1}=0,\partial_{2}=0)
.
∎

By Proposition
2.5
, the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operators for the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
with coefficients in
(\mathfrak{g};\rho)
and the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{-,-\})
with coefficients in
(\mathfrak{g};\mu)
are given by

\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}f=(-1)^{n-1}[\hat{\pi}_{1}+\hat{\rho},f]_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}f=(-1)^{n-1}[\hat{\pi}_{2}+\hat{\mu},f]_{\mathrm{NR}},\quad\forall~f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V).

By the graded Jacobi identity, (
31
) is also equivalent to

(32)

\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}=0,\quad\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}=0,\quad\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}+\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}=0.

Define the space of
0
-cochains
{\mathfrak{C}}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

{\mathfrak{C}}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{v\in V\mid\rho(x)v=\mu(x)v,~\forall~x\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

For
n\geq 1
, define the space of
n
-cochains
{\mathfrak{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

{\mathfrak{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\underbrace{C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\oplus C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\cdots\oplus C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)}_{\mbox{$n$~{copies}}}.

Note that
\mathrm{d}^{0}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}(v)(x)=\rho(x)v
and
\mathrm{d}^{0}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}(v)(x)=\mu(x)v
. Define
\delta^{0}:{\mathfrak{C}}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{C}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\delta^{0}v=\mathrm{d}^{0}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}v=\mathrm{d}^{0}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}v,\quad v\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},V),

define
\delta^{1}:{\mathfrak{C}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{C}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\delta^{1}f=(\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}f,\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}f),\quad f\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},V)

and
define
\delta^{n}:{\mathfrak{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{C}}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\displaystyle\delta^{n}(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n})=(\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\omega_{1},\cdots,\underbrace{\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\omega_{i-1}+\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\omega_{i}}_{i},\cdots,\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\omega_{n}),

where
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\cdots,\omega_{n})\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
and
2\leq i\leq n
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.13
or using the relation (
32
), we also have
\delta^{n+1}\circ\delta^{n}=0
. Thus
({\mathfrak{C}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathfrak{C}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\delta^{*})
is a cochain complex.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a
representation.
The cohomology of the cochain complex
({\mathfrak{C}}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V),\delta^{*})
is called
the cohomology of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
with coefficients in
V
. We denote the
n
-th cohomology group by
\mathcal{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

4.2.
Abelian extensions of compatible Lie algebras

In this subsection, we study abelian extensions of a compatible Lie
algebra and show that they are classified by the second
cohomology group.

Definition 4.3
.

(1)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
,
(\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{h}})
,
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},[-,-]_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}},\{-,-\}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}})
be compatible Lie algebras. An extension of compatible Lie algebras is a short exact sequence of compatible Lie algebras:

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{p}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0.

We say that
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is an
extension
of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
. An
extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
is called
abelian
if the
compatible Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
is trivial.

(2)

A
linear section
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
is a linear map

\sigma:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\mathbbm{p}\circ\sigma=\rm{Id}
.

(3)

Two extensions
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}
and
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
of
\mathfrak{g}

by
\mathfrak{h}
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists a
compatible Lie algebra isomorphism

\theta:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\longrightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
such that we have
the following commutative diagram:

(33)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{h}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{i}_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{p}_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathfrak{g}&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\Big\|&&\theta\Big\downarrow&&\Big\|&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{h}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{i}_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbbm{p}_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathfrak{g}&\longrightarrow&0.\end{array}

Let
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
be an extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
, and

\sigma:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
a linear section. Define

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and

\rho,\mu:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
respectively by

(34)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sigma(x),\sigma(y)]_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}-\sigma[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(35)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\sigma(x),\sigma(y)\}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}-\sigma\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}},

(36)

\displaystyle\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sigma(x),v]_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}},

(37)

\displaystyle\mu(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\sigma(x),v\}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Given a linear section, we have
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
as
vector spaces, and the compatible Lie algebra structure on

\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
can be transferred to
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
:

(38)

\displaystyle[(x,u),(y,v)]_{(\omega_{1},\rho)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+\omega_{1}(x,y)+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),

(39)

\displaystyle\{(x,u),(y,v)\}_{(\omega_{2},\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu(x)v-\mu(y)u+\omega_{2}(x,y)+\{u,v\}_{\mathfrak{h}}).

Then
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[-,-]_{(\omega_{1},\rho)},\{-,-\}_{(\omega_{2},\mu)})

is a compatible Lie algebra.

Now we assume that the compatible Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
is trivial, it is routine to check that
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
is a representation of the compatible Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a
2
-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
.

If we choose another linear section
\sigma^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and let
\omega_{1}^{\prime},\omega_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be the corresponding
2
-cocycle given by

\omega_{1}^{\prime}(x,y)=[\sigma^{\prime}(x),\sigma^{\prime}(y)]_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}-\sigma^{\prime}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\omega_{2}^{\prime}(x,y)=\{\sigma^{\prime}(x),\sigma^{\prime}(y)\}_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}-\sigma^{\prime}(\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Set
\varphi=\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbbm{p}\circ\sigma=\mathbbm{p}\circ\sigma^{\prime}=\rm{Id}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{p}\circ\varphi=\mathbbm{p}\circ\sigma-\mathbbm{p}\circ\sigma^{\prime}=0,

which implies that
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

Since
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian, thus

\displaystyle\rho(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\sigma(x),v]_{\hat{g}}=[\sigma^{\prime}(x),v]_{\hat{g}},

\displaystyle\mu(x)v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\sigma(x),v\}_{\hat{g}}=\{\sigma^{\prime}(x),v\}_{\hat{g}}.

This means that the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
of the compatible Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
does not depend on the choice of linear sections.
Then we obtain that

\displaystyle(\omega_{1}-\omega_{1}^{\prime})(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)\varphi(y)-\rho(y)\varphi(x)-\varphi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\varphi(x,y),

\displaystyle(\omega_{2}-\omega_{2}^{\prime})(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(x)\varphi(y)-\mu(y)\varphi(x)-\varphi(\{x,y\}_{\mathfrak{g}})=\mathrm{d}^{1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\varphi(x,y).

The classical argument in Eilenberg-MacLane cohomology
(
[
9
]
) may easily be extended to a proof of the
following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}},\{-,-\}_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a compatible Lie algebra. Assume
that the compatible Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
is trivial and

(\rho,\mu)
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then the abelian
extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
determined by a
2
-cocycle

(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in{\mathfrak{C}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
builds a one-to-one
correspondence between the isomorphism
classes of abelian extensions of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
and the cohomology
classes in
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

4.3.
Reduced cohomologies of compatible Lie algebras
and cohomologies of bi-Hamiltonian structures

In this
subsection, we introduce the reduced cohomology of a compatible
Lie algebra and establish the relation between the reduced
cohomology of a compatible Lie algebra and the cohomology of the
corresponding compatible linear Poisson structures introduced by
Dubrovin and Zhang in
[
8
]
in the study of
bi-Hamiltonian structures.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
its representation. Denote by
\tilde{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\ker~\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\mid_{C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)}
. By the fact
\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}+\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}=0
, for
f\in\tilde{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, we have

\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}(f)=-\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\circ\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{1}+\rho}(f)=0,

which implies that
\mathrm{d}^{n}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}f\in\tilde{C}^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.
Since
\mathrm{d}^{n+1}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}\circ\mathrm{d}_{\pi_{2}+\mu}^{n}=0
,
(\tilde{C}^{\ast}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{C}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\mathrm{d}^{\ast}_{\pi_{2}+\mu})
is a cochain complex.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
be a compatible Lie algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a
representation. The cohomology of the cochain complex

(\tilde{C}^{*}(\mathfrak{g},V),\mathrm{d}^{*}_{\pi_{2}+\mu})
is called
the
reduced cohomology
of
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\{-,-\})
with
coefficients in
V
. We denote the
n
-th cohomology group by

\tilde{H}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. The space of multi-vector fields on
M
:

\mathfrak{X}^{\ast}(M)=\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M),\mbox{ with }\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M):=\Gamma(\wedge^{n}TM)

carries a Schouten bracket
[-,-]_{S}:\mathfrak{X}^{p}(M)\otimes\mathfrak{X}^{q}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{p+q+1}(M)
, which makes
\mathfrak{X}^{\ast}(M)
into a graded Lie algebra. A Poisson structure on
M
is a bivector field
\Pi\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
satisfying

[\Pi,\Pi]_{S}=0.

The Poisson coboundary operator
\delta^{p}_{\Pi}:\mathfrak{X}^{p}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{p+1}(M)
is given by

\delta^{p}_{\Pi}(P)=(-1)^{p-1}[\Pi,P]_{S},\quad\forall~P\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(M).

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(\mathfrak{X}^{\ast}(M),\delta^{*}_{\Pi})
is called the Poisson cohomology complex of
M
. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
H^{n}(M,\Pi)
.

Assume that the Poisson manifold
(M,\Pi_{1})
is endowed with a second Poisson structure
\Pi_{2}
which is compatible with
\Pi_{1}
in the sense that any linear combination of
\Pi_{1}
and
\Pi_{2}
is still a Poisson structure on
M
. In this case we say that the manifold has a bi-Hamiltonian structure
(\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2})
. The two Poisson structures define on
\mathfrak{X}^{\ast}(M)
two coboundary operators:

\delta^{p}_{\Pi_{1}}(P)=(-1)^{p-1}[\Pi_{1},P]_{S},\quad\delta^{p}_{\Pi_{2}}(P)=(-1)^{p-1}[\Pi_{2},P]_{S},\quad\forall~P\in\mathfrak{X}^{p}(M),

which satisfies

\delta_{\Pi_{1}}^{p+1}\circ\delta^{p}_{\Pi_{2}}+\delta^{p+1}_{\Pi_{2}}\circ\delta^{p}_{\Pi_{1}}=0.

Denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{p}(M)=\ker~\delta^{p}_{\Pi_{1}}\mid_{\mathfrak{X}^{p}(M)}
. Then
(\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{\ast}(M)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathfrak{X}}^{n}(M),\delta^{*}_{\Pi_{2}})
is a cochain complex, which is called the
bi-Hamiltonian cohomology complex
of
M
given by Dubrovin and Zhang in
[
8
]
. See
[
7
,
8
,
23
]
for more details on this cohomology complex and its applications. We denote the corresponding
n
-th cohomology group by
\tilde{H}^{n}(M,\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2})
.

Recall that a Poisson structure on a vector space is called
linear
if the set of linear
functions is closed under the Poisson bracket. Usually the linear Poisson structures are also called Lie-Poisson structures. The Lie-Poisson structures are in one-to-one correspondence with Lie algebra structures. Namely, let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
be a finite dimensional Lie algebra and the associated Lie-Poisson structure
\Pi
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is determined by

\Pi(dl_{x},dl_{y})=\{l_{x},l_{y}\}=l_{[x,y]},\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

where for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
l_{x}
is the linear function on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by

l_{x}(\xi)=\langle x,\xi\rangle,\quad\forall~\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

Furthermore, the linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))
defined by

\rho(x)(f)=\{x,f\},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},f\in C^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

gives a representation of the Lie algebra

Hamiltonian structure of a gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell model
Alain J. Brizard 1,a)

Abstract
The Hamiltonian structure of a set of gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations is presented in terms of a Hamiltonian functional and a gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket. The bracket is used to show that the gyrokinetic angular-momentum conservation law can be expressed in Hamiltonian form. The Jacobi property of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket is also demonstrated explicitly.

Morrison_Greene_1980
and the Vlasov-Maxwell equations
M
;
PJM_1982
;
MW
;
B

Squire_2012
;
Evstatiev_Shadwick_2013
;
He_2015
;
He_2016
;
GEMPIC_2017
;
Xiao_Qin_Liu_2018
;
Glasser_Qin_2020
. The generic guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket was presented by Morrison
Morrison_2013
, while the generic gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket was presented by Burby
et al.

Burby_Brizard_2015
;
Burby_2015
;
Burby_2017
;
Burby_Tronci_2017
. The general Hamiltonian formulation for the reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations was also derived by Lie-transform methods by Brizard
et al.

Brizard_2016
.

The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations suitable for Hamiltonian formulation were initially presented by Pfirsch and Morrison
Pfirsch_Morrison_1985
and recently presented in simplified form (without guiding-center polarization) by Brizard and Tronci
Brizard_Tronci_2016
, while two gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell models suitable for Hamiltonian formulation were presented by Burby and Brizard
Burby_Brizard_2019
and Brizard
Brizard_2020
;
Brizard_2021
.

After having asymptotically eliminated the gyroangle
\zeta
and constructed the gyroaction
J\equiv(mc/q)\,\mu
as an adiabatic invariant (
\mu
denotes the magnetic moment of a particle of mass
m
and charge
q
), a reduced Lagrangian
L_{\rm g}
is expressed in general form as

\displaystyle L_{\rm g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{c}\,{\bf A}+\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm g}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}+J\;\frac{d\zeta}{dt}-\left(q\,\Phi\;+\frac{}{}K_{\rm g}\right),

(1)

where the reduced phase-space coordinates
Z^{\alpha}=({\bf X},p_{\|},J,\zeta)
include the reduced particle position
{\bf X}
and the parallel kinetic momentum
p_{\|}
, the reduced symplectic momentum and kinetic energy are denoted

\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm g}
and
K_{\rm g}
, respectively. We note that, because of the “minimal-coupling” potential terms
(q/c){\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,d{\bf X}/dt-q\,\Phi
, the reduced Lagrangian (
1
) is invariant under an electromagnetic gauge transformation
(\Phi,{\bf A})\rightarrow(\Phi-c^{-1}\partial\chi/\partial t,{\bf A}+\nabla\chi)
since the reduced dynamics is invariant under the Lagrangian gauge transformation
Brizard_2015

L_{\rm g}\rightarrow L_{\rm g}+(q/c)\,d\chi/dt
, i.e., this transformation does not change the reduced equations of motion obtained from Eq. (
1
). Hence, a gauge-free reduced Lagrangian formulation is obtained when the reduced symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm g}
and the reduced kinetic energy
K_{\rm g}
in Eq. (
1
) depend on the electric and magnetic fields
({\bf E}\equiv-\nabla\Phi-c^{-1}\partial{\bf A}/\partial t,{\bf B}\equiv\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A})
only. For example, in guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell theory, Pfirsch and Morrison
Pfirsch_Morrison_1985
used
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}=p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,q\widehat{{\sf b}}/\Omega
and
K_{\rm gc}=\mu\,B+|\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}|^{2}/2m
, while Brizard and Tronci
Brizard_Tronci_2016
considered the simpler guiding-center Lagrangian with
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}=p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}
and
K_{\rm gc}=\mu B+p_{\|}^{2}/2m
. The Hamiltonian structure for the Brizard-Tronci version of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations was recently presented elsewhere
Brizard_gcVM
, with an extensive proof of the Jacobi property for the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket
Brizard_proof_2021
.

In gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell theory, on the other hand, the electromagnetic potentials in Eq. (
1
) are decomposed in terms of background and perturbed components:
(\Phi,{\bf A})=(\epsilon\Phi_{1},{\bf A}_{0}+\epsilon{\bf A}_{1})
, where

{\bf B}_{0}=\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}_{0}
is the time-independent background (unperturbed) magnetic field, and the dimensionless parameter
\epsilon\ll 1
orders the perturbation amplitudes in a manner consistent with standard gyrokinetic theory
Brizard_Hahm_2007
. We note that, unless a gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell model is formulated exclusively in terms of the perturbed electric and magnetic fields (with or without the minimal-coupling potential terms), the appearance of potentials in the gyrocenter kinetic energy
K_{\rm gy}
prevents a Hamiltonian formulation. Hence, standard gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell models
Brizard_Hahm_2007
are not suitable for a Hamiltonian formulation.

The recent work of Burby and Brizard
Burby_Brizard_2019
is suitable for a Hamiltonian formulation, however, since the gyrocenter symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gy}=p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}
is expressed only in terms of the unperturbed magnetic field, while the gyrocenter kinetic energy
K_{\rm gy}
is a function of
({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
up to second order in
\epsilon
[see Eq. (
3
) below]. The purpose of the present paper is, therefore, to derive the explicit Hamiltonian structure for the gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations presented by Burby and Brizard
Burby_Brizard_2019
. This direct approach is in contrast to the formal derivation presented by Burby
Burby_2015
. The case of the gauge-free gyrokinetic equations derived by Brizard
Brizard_2020
;
Brizard_2021
, in which the gyrocenter symplectic momentum
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gy}=p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}+\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1{\rm gy}}({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})
includes first-order electromagnetic corrections, will be considered in future work.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we present the gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations derived by Burby and Brizard
Burby_Brizard_2019
, which are presented here in the drift-kinetic limit in order to simplify our presentation. In Sec.
III
, the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket is explicitly constructed from the Hamiltonian formulation of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations. This gyrokinetic bracket structure is immediately applied to the proofs that the gyrokinetic entropy functional is a Casimir of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket and the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell toroidal angular momentum conservation law, first derived in variational (Lagrangian) form in Refs.
Hirvijoki_2020
;
Brizard_2021
, can be expressed in Hamiltonian form. In Sec.
IV
, the explicit proof of the Jacobi property of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket derived in Sec.
III
is given, and a summary of our work is presented in Sec.
V
.

II
Gauge-free Gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell Equations

We begin with the gauge-free gyrocenter single-particle Lagrangian

\displaystyle L_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{q}{c}\left({\bf A}_{0}+\epsilon\frac{}{}{\bf A}_{1}\right)+p_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\;-\;J\,{\bf R}_{0}^{*}\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}+J\,\frac{d\zeta}{dt}

(2)

\displaystyle-\;\left(q\,\epsilon\,\Phi_{1}+\frac{}{}K_{\rm gy}\right)\equiv P_{\alpha}\,\frac{dZ^{\alpha}}{dt}-H_{\rm gy},

where the higher-order guiding-center corrections
{\bf R}_{0}^{*}\equiv{\bf R}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}
include the background gyrogauge vector field
{\bf R}_{0}
(which ensures that the guiding-center equations of motion are independent of the gyroangle as well as how the gyroangle is measured
RGL_1983
), and the guiding-center polarization correction
\frac{1}{2}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}

Tronko_Brizard_2015
. Next, the gyrocenter kinetic energy is expanded up to second order in
\epsilon\ll 1

Burby_Brizard_2019
:

\displaystyle K_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\;+\;\mu\left(B_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,B_{1\|}\;+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2B_{0}}\;|{\bf B}_{1}|^{2}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle-\,\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf E}_{1}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{mc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle-\;\epsilon^{2}\;\frac{mc^{2}}{2B_{0}^{2}}\left|{\bf E}_{1}\;+\;(p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}/mc)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}_{1}\right|^{2}

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}
denotes the guiding-center electric-dipole moment
Tronko_Brizard_2015
. For the sake of clarity, the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell model considered here is presented in its drift-kinetic limit, where finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) corrections are retained only through the guiding-center electric-dipole moment

\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle 0

Here, the gyrocenter Poisson bracket

III
Gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket

In Eq. (
31
), the antisymmetric gyrokinetic Poisson operator
{\sf J}_{\rm gy}^{ab}(\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$})\,\circ
guarantees the antisymmetry property:
[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}=-\,[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}]_{\rm gy}
; and the bilinearity of Eq. (
31
) guarantees the Leibniz (product-rule) property:
[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\,{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gy}=[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}\,{\mathcal{K}}+{\mathcal{G}}\,[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gy}
. The Jacobi property of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket is expressed as the requirement that the

Jacobiator
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gy}+\left[[{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{F}}\right]_{\rm gy}

(32)

\displaystyle+\left[[{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{F}}]_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gy}\;=\;0

must vanish for arbitrary functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
, which imposes constraints on the Poisson operator
{\sf J}_{\rm gy}^{ab}(\mbox{\boldmath$\Psi$})
.

From the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
28
)-(
30
), we can now extract the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket from Eq. (
31
), which is expressed in terms of two arbitrary gyrocenter functionals

({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})
as

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{G}}\right]_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left\{\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}+4\pi q\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}\;-\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\;\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{2}\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\right)+4\pi c\int_{X}\left[\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta{\bf B}_{1}}\right)-\frac{\delta\mathcal{G}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\frac{\delta\mathcal{F}}{\delta{\bf B}_{1}}\right)\right].

Here, the first term is the Vlasov sub-bracket, the next three terms (multiplied by first and second powers of
4\pi q
) represent the Interaction sub-bracket, and the last two terms (multiplied by
4\pi c
) represent the Maxwell sub-bracket. We note that the Interaction sub-bracket term proportional to
(4\pi q)^{2}
does not appear in the standard Vlasov-Maxwell bracket
M
;
PJM_1982
;
MW
;
B
, since the Poisson bracket
\{{\bf x},{\bf x}\}\equiv 0
vanishes in particle phase space. The generic form of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket was first presented by Burby
et al.

Burby_Brizard_2015
. In the electrostatic limit, where
{\bf E}_{1}=-\,\nabla\Phi_{1}
and
{\bf B}_{1}=0
, the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Poisson bracket retains only the contributions from the Vlasov and Interaction sub-brackets.

We postpone the proof of the Jacobi property (
32
) until the next Section and, instead, we now look at two applications of the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
33
): first, we present the proof that the gyrokinetic entropy functional is a Casimir of the gyrokinetic bracket (
33
); and, second, we present the proof that the gyrokinetic toroidal angular momentum conservation law can be expressed in Hamiltonian form.

III.1
Gyrokinetic entropy functional

A Casimir functional
{\mathcal{C}}
associated with the gyrokinetic bracket (
33
) satisfies the equation
[{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{K}}]_{\rm gy}=0
, which holds for an arbitrary functional
{\mathcal{K}}
. It is well known that the gyrokinetic entropy functional

{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy}[F_{\rm gy},{\bf B}_{1}]\;\equiv\;-\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\ln(F_{\rm gy}/B_{\|}^{*})

(34)

is a Casimir for the gyrokinetic bracket (
33
), which is one example of the generic form
{\mathcal{C}}[F_{\rm gy},{\bf B}_{1}]=\int_{Z}B_{\|}^{*}\,C(F_{\rm gy}/B_{\|}^{*})

Burby_Brizard_2015
for an arbitrary function
C(F)
, where
F=F_{\rm gy}/B_{\|}^{*}
.

From Eq. (
34
), using
\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy}/\delta F_{\rm gy}=-\,1-\ln F
and
\epsilon^{-1}\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy}/\delta{\bf B}_{1}=\int_{P}F\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}
, we find

\displaystyle\left[{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\int_{Z}B_{\|}^{*}\,\left\{F,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}

(35)

\displaystyle-\;4\pi q\int_{Z}\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left({\bf B}^{*}\;\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_{\|}}+\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{q}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla F\right)

\displaystyle-\;4\pi c\int_{Z}\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\times\left(F\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi c\int_{Z}F\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{q}{c}\frac{\partial{\bf B}^{*}}{\partial p_{\|}}-\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

where the first term vanishes since, according to Eq. (
14
), it is an exact phase-space divergence, while the remaining terms cancel out.

The concept of gyrokinetic entropy can play an important role in the investigation of magnetized plasma turbulence (see Ref.
Schekochihin_2008
, for example). The gyrokinetic entropy functional (
34
) can also be used to formulate the gyrokinetic metriplectic evolution of an arbitrary gyrokinetic functional
{\mathcal{F}}

Kaufman_1984
;
Morrison_1984
;
Morrison_1986
:

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t}\;=\;\left[{\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gy}\right]_{\rm gy}\;+\;\left({\mathcal{F}},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy}\right)_{\rm gy},

(36)

in terms of a self-adjoint collisional bracket
(\;,\;)_{\rm gy}
that conserves energy and momentum, i.e.,
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm gy})_{\rm gy}=0
, and satisfies the second law of thermodynamics:
\partial{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy}/\partial t=({\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy},{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm gy})_{\rm gy}\geq 0
. This metriplectic formulation
Kraus_Hirvijoki_2017
;
Hirvijoki_Burby_2020
can assist in the investigation of dissipative turbulent transport in magnetized plasmas based on structure-preserving algorithms.

III.2
Gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell angular momentum conservation law

The conservation laws of energy-momentum and angular momentum for the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell (

)-(

Brizard_2021
and Hirvijoki
et al.

Hirvijoki_2020

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gy}\varphi}[F_{\rm gy},{\bf D}_{\rm gy},{\bf B}_{1}]

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;P_{\varphi}

We now evaluate the Hamiltonian evolution of the gyrokinetic functional (

\displaystyle-\;\int_{X}\frac{\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gy}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gy}\;\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial{\bf B}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}_{1}}{\partial\varphi}\;+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\epsilon\,{\bf B}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{M}_{\rm gy}\right),

where we used the definitions (

) and (

) for the gyrokinetic H-field and the gyrocenter magnetization, respectively, and we used the axisymmetric vector identity

and the vector identity

{\bf V}{\bf W}:\nabla(\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi)\;=\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,({\bf W}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf V}),

(43)

which holds for arbitrary vectors fields
({\bf V},{\bf W})
. Next, the third term in Eq. (

) yields the non-vanishing terms

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gy}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\;(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf D}_{\rm gy})-{\bf D}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\epsilon}{4\pi}\frac{\partial{\bf E}_{1}}{\partial\varphi}

\displaystyle-\;{\bf D}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gy}\;\left(\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial{\bf E}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf E}_{1}}{\partial\varphi}-\epsilon\,q{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\epsilon\,{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{P}_{\rm gy}\right),

where we used the vector identity (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gy}\varphi}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left[\frac{dP_{\varphi}}{dt}-\epsilon\,q\left({\bf E}_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf B}_{1}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right]

(45)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left(\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial{\bf E}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf E}_{1}}{\partial\varphi}+\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial{\bf B}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf B}_{1}}{\partial\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{X}\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\epsilon\,{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{P}_{\rm gy}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon\,{\bf B}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{M}_{\rm gy}\right).

Next, using the explicit expressions for the gyrocenter polarization and magnetization (

)-(

) become the polarization and magnetization torques

\displaystyle\int_{X}\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\epsilon\,{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{P}_{\rm gy}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon\,{\bf B}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mathbb{M}_{\rm gy}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\epsilon{\bf B}_{1}\;+\;\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gy}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\epsilon{\bf B}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{mc}\right)\right].

(46)

\partial K_{\rm gy}/\partial\varphi
:

({\bf E}_{1},{\bf B}_{1})

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\prime}K_{\rm gy}}{\partial\varphi}

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\epsilon{\bf B}_{1}\;+\;\epsilon{\bf E}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gy}\right)

(48)

\displaystyle-\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\frac{p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{mc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\epsilon{\bf B}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gy}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\frac{p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{mc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\epsilon{\bf B}_{1}\right)\right],

where we used Eq. (
42
). If we now combine these expressions in Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathcal{P}}_{{\rm gy}\varphi}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left[\frac{dP_{\varphi}}{dt}-\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial\varphi}\right.

(49)

\displaystyle\left.-\;\epsilon\,q\left({\bf E}_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf B}_{1}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right],

IV
Jacobi Property of the Gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell Bracket

We now verify that the gyrokinetic bracket (
33
) satisfies the Jacobi property (
32
). The proof will rely on several Poisson-bracket identities derived from the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
).

According to the Bracket theorem
PJM_1982
;
Morrison_2013
, the proof of the Jacobi property involves only the explicit dependence of the gyrocenter Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
33
), where the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell Poisson operator
{\sf J}_{\rm gy}^{ab}(F_{\rm gy},{\bf B}_{1})\circ
is independent of the gyrokinetic displacement field
{\bf D}_{\rm gy}
, where we note that the dependence on the magnetic field
{\bf B}_{1}
enters through Eq. (
11
) appearing in the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
). Hence, we can write the double-bracket involving three arbitrary gyrocenter functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
:

\displaystyle\left[[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\mathcal{K}}\right]_{\rm gy}^{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\left\{\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}

(51)

\displaystyle+4\pi q\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle-4\pi c\int_{X}\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\epsilon\delta{\bf B}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}},

where the terms involving
\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}
vanish on the basis of the Bracket theorem.

Here, the Poisson variation
\delta^{P}
of the bracket (
33
) only involves variations with respect to
(F_{\rm gy},{\bf B}_{1})
:

\displaystyle\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}\left(\delta F_{\rm gy}-\frac{F_{\rm gy}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\epsilon\,\delta{\bf B}_{1}\right)\left[\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}\;+\frac{}{}4\pi q\,\left({\bf G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right)+(4\pi q)^{2}{\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf G}\right]

(52)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\,\frac{\epsilon\,\delta{\bf B}_{1}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\left(\nabla f\;-\frac{}{}4\pi q\,{\bf F}\right)\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\left(\nabla g\;-\frac{}{}4\pi q\,{\bf G}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{\|}}\right],

where we use the notation
(f,g,k)\equiv(\delta{\mathcal{F}}/\delta F_{\rm gy},\delta{\mathcal{G}}/\delta F_{\rm gy},\delta{\mathcal{K}}/\delta F_{\rm gy})
and
({\bf F},{\bf G},{\bf K})\equiv(\delta{\mathcal{F}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy},\delta{\mathcal{G}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy},\delta{\mathcal{K}}/\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy})
. In addition,
\delta B_{\|}^{*}=\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\epsilon\delta{\bf B}_{1}
represents the variation of the Jacobian, which appears in the denominator of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
), while
\delta{\bf B}^{*}=\epsilon\,\delta{\bf B}_{1}
appears only in the first term of Eq. (
10
). In the first two terms of Eq. (
51
), we therefore have the Vlasov sub-bracket

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}},\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gy}\;+\;4\pi q\left\{\left({\bf G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right),\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gy}

(53)

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{2}\left\{{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gy},

and the Interaction sub-bracket

\displaystyle 4\pi q\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\delta F_{\rm gy}}\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi q\,{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}\right\}_{\rm gy}+(4\pi q)^{2}\;{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}\left({\bf G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right)\right\}_{\rm gy}

(54)

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{3}\;{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G}\right\}_{\rm gy},

while the third term in Eq. (
51
) is the Maxwell sub-bracket

\displaystyle-4\pi c\int_{X}\frac{\delta^{P}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}]_{\rm gy}}{\epsilon\,\delta{\bf B}_{1}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\delta\mathcal{K}}{\delta{\bf D}_{\rm gy}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,4\pi q\int_{Z}\frac{cF_{\rm gy}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\left(\nabla f\;-\frac{}{}4\pi q\,{\bf F}\right)\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\;-\;\left(\nabla g\;-\frac{}{}4\pi q\,{\bf G}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{\|}}\right]

(55)

\displaystyle+\;4\pi q\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\left[\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}\;+\frac{}{}4\pi q\,\left({\bf G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\;(4\pi q)^{3}\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\left({\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G}\right).

From these terms, it is clear that the proof of the Jacobi property (
32
) must hold separately for each power of
4\pi q
:

{\mathcal{J}ac}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]\;\equiv\;\sum_{n=0}^{3}(4\pi q)^{n}\int_{Z}F_{\rm gy}\;{\sf Jac}_{n}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}],

(56)

where each Jacobiator term
{\sf Jac}_{n}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]
involves the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
). At zeroth order, for example, the Vlasov sub-bracket (
53
) yields

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{0}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\{f,g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}k\right\}_{\rm gy}\;+\;\left\{\{g,k\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}f\right\}_{\rm gy}

(57)

\displaystyle+\;\left\{\{k,f\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}\;=\;0,

which vanishes because of the Jacobi property (
12
) of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
). In what follows, we will use cyclic permutations (denoted by
\leftturn
) of the functionals
({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}})
in order to combine similar terms from the sub-brackets (
53
)-(
55
) at the next three orders in
4\pi q
.

IV.1
First-order Jacobi property

By using the Leibniz property of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
), the cyclic permutations of the Vlasov sub-bracket (
53
) include the first-order terms

\displaystyle\{{\bf K},f\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\;-\;\{{\bf K},g\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle+\;{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\left\{\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}f\right\}_{\rm gy}+\left\{\{f,{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}\right),

(58)

while the cyclic permutations of the Interaction sub-bracket (
54
) include

{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}\right\}_{\rm gy}\;=\;{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{\{g,\;f\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}{\bf X}\right\}_{\rm gy},

(59)

where we used the antisymmetry of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket. Lastly, the first-order terms in cyclic permutations of the Maxwell sub-bracket (
55
) are

\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\;\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}-\frac{c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left(\nabla f\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}-\nabla g\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{\|}}\right).

(60)

Using the gyrocenter Poisson bracket identity

\displaystyle\frac{c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\left(\nabla f\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}-\nabla g\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_{\|}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\{f,\;g\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle+\;\{{\bf X},\;f\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\{{\bf X},\;g\}_{\rm gy}

Eq. (
60
) becomes

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},\;g\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\{{\bf X},\;f\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=\;\{{\bf K},g\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},f\}_{\rm gy}\;-\;\{{\bf K},f\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy},

(61)

where we used the identity

\{{\bf U},v\}_{\rm gy}\equiv\{{\bf X},v\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla{\bf U}

(62)

for a vector field
{\bf U}
assumed to be independent of
p_{\|}
, while
v({\bf X},p_{\|})
is an arbitrary gyrocenter function.

By combining Eqs. (
58
), (
59
), and (
61
), we obtain the first-order term in the Jacobiator (
56
):

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{1}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{i}\left[\left\{\{X^{i},g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}f\right\}_{\rm gy}\frac{}{}\right.

(63)

\displaystyle+\;\left\{\{f,X^{i}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{}{}\left\{\{g,\;f\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gy}\right]+\leftturn=0,

which vanishes because of the Jacobi property (
12
) of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
).

IV.2
Second-order Jacobi property

Cyclic permutations of the Vlasov sub-bracket (
53
) include the second-order terms

\displaystyle\left\{{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf F},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i}\,K_{j}\left\{\{X^{j},X^{i}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}

(64)

\displaystyle+\;\{{\bf K},g\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf F}

\displaystyle+\;{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf F},g\}_{\rm gy},

where summation over repeated indices is implied in the first term. Next, using the identity (
62
), the second and third terms become

\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\nabla{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf F}-\nabla{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\right),

(65)

where we used the Poisson-bracket identity

{\bf U}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf V}\;=\;-\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf U}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf V}

(66)

for any two vector fields
({\bf U},{\bf V})
.

Cyclic permutations of the Interaction sub-bracket (
54
) include the second-order terms

\displaystyle{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right\}_{\rm gy}\;-\;{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle=\;F_{i}\,K_{j}\left(\left\{\{g,X^{j}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gy}+\left\{\{X^{i},g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{j}\right\}_{\rm gy}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\left({\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf K}\}_{\rm gy}\;-\frac{}{}{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},{\bf F}\}_{\rm gy}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy},

(67)

where the last two terms can be expressed as

\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla{\bf F}\;-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla{\bf K}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}.

(68)

Hence, by combining Eqs. (
65
) and (
68
), we obtain

\displaystyle\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left[\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}-\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left[\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\,({\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy})-({\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K})\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\right]

(69)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left[\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\,({\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy})-({\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F})\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\right],

where we used the Poisson-bracket identity
(c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}/qB_{\|}^{*})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy}=(c/qB_{\|}^{*})\,\partial g/\partial p_{\|}
.

Cyclic permutations of the Maxwell sub-bracket (
55
) include the second-order terms

-\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left[\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\,({\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy})-({\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K})\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\right]\,+\,\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left[\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\,({\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},g\}_{\rm gy})-({\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F})\,\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_{\|}}\right].

(70)

which exactly cancel the terms in Eq. (
69
), so that the second-order Jacobi term in Eq. (
56
) is obtained by adding Eqs. (
64
), (
67
), and (
70
), which yields the second-order term in the Jacobiator (
56
):

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{2}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i}\,K_{j}\left[\left\{\{X^{i},g\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{j}\right\}_{\rm gy}\frac{}{}\right.

(71)

\displaystyle+\;\left\{\{X^{j},X^{i}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}g\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{}{}\left\{\{g,\;X^{j}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gy}\right]+\leftturn=0,

which vanishes because of the Jacobi property (
12
) of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
).

IV.3
Third-order Jacobi property

At the third order in
4\pi q
, we note that only the Interaction and Maxwell sub-brackets (
54
) and (
55
) contribute terms in
{\sf Jac}_{3}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]
. First, the third-order terms in cyclic permutations of Eqs. (
54
) and (
55
) are

{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf G}\right\}_{\rm gy}+\leftturn\;=\;{\sf Jac}_{3}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]\;+\;\left[{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf G}\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\right)\;+\;\leftturn\right],

(72)

and

{\bf G}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf K}\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf F}\right)\;+\;{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf F}\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf G}\right)\;+\;{\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf G}\left(\frac{c\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}{\bf K}\right),

(73)

which when combined, using the antisymmetry property of Eq. (

{\bf K}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left\{{\bf X},\frac{}{}{\bf F}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\{{\bf X},{\bf X}\}_{\rm gy}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}{\bf G}\right\}_{\rm gy}+\leftturn\;=\;{\sf Jac}_{3}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}],

where the third-order term in the Jacobiator (
56
):

\displaystyle{\sf Jac}_{3}[{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{i}\,G_{j}\,K_{\ell}\left[\left\{\{X^{i},X^{j}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{\ell}\right\}_{\rm gy}\frac{}{}\right.

(74)

\displaystyle+\;\left\{\{X^{j},X^{\ell}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{i}\right\}_{\rm gy}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{}{}\left\{\{X^{\ell},\;X^{i}\}_{\rm gy},\frac{}{}X^{j}\right\}_{\rm gy}\right]=0,

vanishes because of the Jacobi property (
12
) of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
).

V
Summary

The explicit Hamiltonian structure of the gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell equations was constructed directly from Eqs. (
28
)-(
30
) in terms of a gyrokinetic Hamiltonian functional (
23
) and the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
), which resulted in the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
33
). The gauge-free gyrokinetic equations were presented here in their drift-kinetic limit, which simplified the expressions for the gyrocenter polarization and magnetization (
20
)-(
21
). Future work will consider extensions of our gyrokinetic Hamiltonian formulation to include higher-order FLR effects.

As simple applications of our gauge-free gyrokinetic Hamiltonian formulation, we demonstrated in Sec.
III
that the gyrokinetic entropy function (
34
) is a Casimir functional for the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
33
), and that the gyrokinetic toroidal angular momentum conservation law can be expressed in Hamiltonian form (
50
).

In Sec.
IV
, we presented an explicit proof that the gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell bracket (
33
) satisfies the Jacobi property (
32
). While this may seem to be an academic exercise, our proof follows similar proofs for
several Vlasov-Maxwell models presented in the Appendix of Ref.
Morrison_2013
, for example. In analogy to the recent more extensive proof
Brizard_proof_2021
of the Jacobi property of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell bracket, the proof of the Jacobi property (
32
) relies on identities derived from the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
) and the vanishing gyrokinetic Jacobiator (
32
) is inherited from the Jacobi property (
12
) of the gyrocenter Poisson bracket.

Future work will consider an alternate gauge-free gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell model
Brizard_2021
in which the gyrocenter polarization drift
\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,d{\bf X}/dt
is added to the symplectic part of the gyrocenter Lagrangian (
2
), where
\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{1}\equiv[{\bf E}_{1}+(p_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}/mc)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}_{1}]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,q\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}/\Omega_{0}
. This extension will explicitly introduce electric-field terms in the gyrocenter Poisson bracket (
10
) and introduce new terms in the Poisson variation (
52
).

Appendix A
Gyrocenter angular momentum equation of motion

In this Appendix, we derive the equation of motion for the gyrocenter angular momentum (
38
) in an axisymmetric background magnetic field.

We begin with the covariant azimuthal (toroidal) component of the Euler-Lagrange equation (
4
):

\displaystyle\epsilon q\left({\bf E}_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}_{1}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}-\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial\varphi}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)+\frac{q}{c}{\bf B}_{0}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi},

(75)

where the toroidal derivative of the gyrocenter kinetic energy
\partial K_{\rm gy}/\partial\varphi=(\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla K_{\rm gy}
is given in Eq. (
47
).

Next, we explicitly evaluate the gyrocenter time derivative of
P_{\varphi}
:

\displaystyle\frac{dP_{\varphi}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{q}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\nabla{\bf A}_{0}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}+\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf A}_{0}^{*}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)+\frac{q}{c}{\bf B}_{0}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}_{0}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{q}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\;+\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{q}{c}\,{\bf A}_{0}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{\|}}{dt}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}\right)+\frac{q}{c}{\bf B}_{0}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi},

where we used the gyrocenter invariance of the magnetic moment
\mu
and we used
\partial{\bf A}_{0}^{*}/\partial\varphi\equiv\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}_{0}^{*}
under the assumption of axisymmetry of the background magnetic field, while we used the identity
{\bf W}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla(\partial{\bf X}/\partial\varphi)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf V}\equiv\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,({\bf W}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf V})
, which holds for arbitrary vectors
({\bf V},{\bf W})
. Hence, Eq. (
75
) yields the gyrocenter angular momentum equation of motion

\frac{dP_{\varphi}}{dt}\;=\;\epsilon q\left({\bf E}_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}_{1}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\varphi}-\frac{\partial K_{\rm gy}}{\partial\varphi},

(77)

We note that in the electrostatic limit, where
{\bf E}_{1}=-\,\nabla\Phi_{1}
and
{\bf B}_{1}=0
, Eq. (
77
) yields the standard equation
dP_{\varphi}/dt=-\,\partial H_{\rm gy}/\partial\varphi
, where
H_{\rm gy}=\epsilon q\,\Phi_{1}+K_{\rm gy}
.

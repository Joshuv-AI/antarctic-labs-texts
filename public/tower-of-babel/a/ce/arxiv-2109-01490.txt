A Scalable Track-Before-Detect Method With Poisson/Multi-Bernoulli Model This research was supported by the Office of Naval Research under Grant N00014-21-1-2267.
Thomas Kropfreiter 1 , Jason L. Williams 2 , and Florian Meyer 1

Abstract
We propose a scalable track-before-detect (TBD) tracking method based on a Poisson/multi-Bernoulli model. To limit computational complexity, we approximate the exact multi-Bernoulli mixture posterior probability density function (pdf) by a multi-Bernoulli pdf. Data association based on the sum-product algorithm and recycling of Bernoulli components enable the detection and tracking of low-observable objects with limited computational resources. Our simulation results demonstrate a significantly improved tracking performance compared to a state-of-the-art TBD method.

Many existing

However, due to their high computational complexity, they are often unsuitable for real-time operation.
A well-established real-time TBD method is the Histogram Probabilistic Multi-Hypothesis Tracker (H-PMHT)

]
, which is based on the expectation-maximization algorithm.
However, tuning the parameters of the H-PMHT is known to be difficult

17

A state-of-the-art conventional tracking method is the track-oriented marginal multi-Bernoulli/Poisson (TOMB/P) filter
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
, which models an unknown number of objects and their states by the union of a Poisson RFS and a MB RFS.
The TOMB/P filter relies on a high-dimensional marginalization operation performed by means of message passing

18

“recycles” Bernoulli components with low existence probability by transferring them to the Poisson RFS instead of pruning them. This recycling step can significantly improve tracking performance in scenarios with reduced sensor performance
[
19

The TOMB/P filter has also been derived and extended to multiple sensors using the framework of factor graphs and the sum-product algorithm (SPA)
[
6

7

[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

In this paper, we propose a scalable TBD method that is based on a Poisson/MB model similar to the one of the TOMB/P filter. We first review the general TBD measurement model and discuss a simplification that is suitable for the case where at any discrete time step, the influence of an object is limited to a single “bin” or “pixel” of the raw sensor data. Based on this simplified TBD measurement model, we derive an exact update step.
Since the resulting exact posterior probability density function (pdf) is a MB mixture pdf, we approximate it by an MB pdf to limit computational complexity. To guarantee reliable multiobject tracking performance, we recycle Bernoulli components with a low existence probability instead of pruning them.
Finally, we verify the performance of the proposed method numerically and compare it to a state-of-the-art TBD tracking method
[
16
]
.

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as

follows
:

•

We establish a Bayesian Poisson/MB model for the development of scalable TBD methods.

•

We derive a scalable Poisson/MB filter for TBD that relies on recycling of Bernoulli components.

•

We demonstrate performance improvements of our TBD method compared to a state-of-the-art technique.

III
System Model

In this section, we describe the system model underlying the proposed algorithm.
The multiobject state at time
k
is represented by an RFS
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}=\{\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{(1)},\ldots,\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{(n)}\}
.
The single-object state
\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{(i)}

consists of the object’s intensity
\sgamma_{k}^{(i)}

\bm{\mathsfbr{p}}_{k}^{(i)}
, and possibly motion related parameters.
The measurement
\bm{\mathsfbr{z}}_{k}\triangleq[\mathsfbr{z}_{k}^{(1)}\ldots\mathsfbr{z}_{k}^{(M)}]^{\text{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt
at time
k
, consists of
M

m\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\ldots,M\}

III-A

State-Transition Model

We use the well-established conventional RFS state-transition model
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
. At time
k\hskip-0.85358pt-\!1
, an object with state
\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k-1}\!\in\hskip-0.85358pt\mathsfbr{X}_{k-1}
either survives or dies with probabilities
p_{\text{S}}
and
1\!-\hskip-0.85358ptp_{\text{S}}
, respectively.
If it survives, its new state
\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}
is distributed according to the single-object state transition pdf
f(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k-1})
.

We assume that the states of different objects survive/die and evolve in time independently, i.e., given
\bm{x}_{k-1}
, the single-object state
\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}
is conditionally independent of all the other single-object states
\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{\prime}
.
Thus, conditioned on the multiobject state
{\cal{X}}_{k-1}
, the multiobject state of the survived objects
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{S}}
, can be modeled by an MB RFS, i.e.,
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{S}}\hskip-0.85358pt=\bigcup_{\bm{x}_{k-1}\in{\cal{X}}_{k-1}}\!\mathsfbr{S}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k-1})
. The components
\mathsfbr{S}_{k}(\bm{x}_{k-1})
of this MB RFS are Bernoulli RFSs (cf. (
3
)) with existence probabilities
p_{\text{S}}
and spatial pdfs

f(\bm{x}_{k}|\bm{x}_{k-1})
.

Newborn objects are modeled by a Poisson RFS
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{B}}\hskip-0.85358pt
with mean
\mu_{\text{B}}
, spatial pdf
f_{\text{B}}(\bm{x}_{k})
and, hence, PHD
\lambda_{\text{B}}(\bm{x}_{k})\hskip-0.85358pt=\mu_{\text{B}}\hskip 0.85358ptf_{\text{B}}(\bm{x}_{k})
. Conditioned on
{\cal{X}}_{k-1}
, surviving objects
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{S}}
are assumed independent of the newborn objects
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{B}}
. Thus, for
{\cal{X}}_{k-1}
fixed, the overall multiobject state at time
k
, is given

by

\mathsfbr{X}_{k}=\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{S}}\cup\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{B}}=\Bigg(\bigcup_{\bm{x}_{k-1}\in\hskip 0.85358pt{\cal{X}}_{k-1}}\!\!\!\mathsfbr{S}_{k}\big(\bm{x}_{k-1}\big)\hskip-0.85358pt\Bigg)\cup\mathsfbr{X}_{k}^{\text{B}}\hskip 0.85358pt.

This model defines the state-transition pdf

, which can be calculated explicitly via set convolution
[
4
]

III-C

Considered Simplified Measurement Model

As an approximation for simplified inference, we consider a model where (i) every object contributes to exactly one distinct measurement
\mathsfbr{z}_{k}^{(m)}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt
,
m\hskip-0.85358pt\in\{1,\ldots,M\}

\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{(i)}
contributes to measurement
\mathsfbr{z}_{k}^{(m)}
is proportional to the value of the PSF

To describe the associations of objects and measurements at time

Here,

\bm{\mathsfbr{x}}_{k}^{(i)}

i\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\ldots,n\}
only contributes to measurement
\mathsfbr{z}_{k}^{(m)}\hskip-0.85358pt

An object-measurement association vector

For a fixed association vector
\bm{a}_{k}\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\mathcal{A}_{n,M}

\mathsfbr{X}_{k}

\tilde{D}^{(m)}({\cal{X}}_{k})\hskip 0.85358pt=\hskip 0.85358pt\begin{cases}d^{(m)}(\bm{x}_{k}^{(i)})\hskip 0.85358pt,&\exists i\hskip 2.84526pt\text{such that}\hskip 2.84526pta_{k}^{(i)}=m\hskip 0.85358pt,\\[3.41432pt]

IV
Exact Estimation

In the Bayesian sequential estimation framework, the statistics of the state
\mathsfbr{X}_{k}
at time
k
, conditioned on all received measurements
\bm{z}_{1:k}\triangleq[\bm{z}_{1}\ldots\bm{z}_{k}]^{\text{T}}
up to time
k
, can be described by the posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k})
.
This pdf is calculated from the previous posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k-1}|\bm{z}_{1:k-1})
via a prediction and an update step. The prediction step calculates the predicted posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k-1})
from the previous posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k-1}|\bm{z}_{1:k-1})
based on the state-transition pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|{\cal{X}}_{k-1})
discussed in Section
III-A
. It is identical to the one performed by the TOMB/P filter
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
and will be skipped. Note that
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k-1})
is a Poisson/MB pdf with
J_{k-1}
Bernoulli components and intensity function
\lambda_{k|k-1}(\bm{x}_{k})
.

The update step calculates the current posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k})
from the predicted posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k-1})
based on the likelihood function
f(\bm{z}_{k}|{\cal{X}}_{k})
in (
8
). As we show in Appendix
B
, the updated posterior pdf
f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k})
is no longer a Poisson/MB pdf but a MB mixture pdf (cf. (
6
)).
Contrary to the conventional TOMB/P filter, the updated posterior pdf contains no Poisson part anymore. This is a direct consequence of the fact that the TBD measurement model (cf. Section
III-C
) does not consider missed detections.
However, this is not restrictive for practical scenarios, since objects with very low intensities are also admitted by our model (and thus difficult to distinguish from pure noise).
More precisely, as derived in Appendix
B
, the exact updated posterior pdf can be expressed as

\displaystyle f({\cal{X}}_{k}|\bm{z}_{1:k})

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt=\sum_{\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k}\hskip 0.85358pt\in\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{J_{k},M}}\hskip 0.85358ptp(\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k})\hskip 0.85358pt\hskip 0.85358ptf^{\text{MB}}_{\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k}}({\cal{X}}_{k})\hskip 0.85358pt,

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt=\hskip-4.83694pt\sum_{{\cal{X}}_{k}^{(1)}\uplus\ldots\uplus{\cal{X}}_{k}^{(J_{k})}={\cal{X}}_{k}}\hskip 0.85358pt\sum_{\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k}\hskip 0.85358pt\in\hskip 0.85358pt\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{J_{k},M}}\hskip 0.85358ptp(\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k})\hskip 0.85358pt\prod_{j\hskip 0.85358pt=\hskip 0.85358pt1}^{J_{k}}\hskip 0.85358ptf^{(j,a_{k}^{\prime(j)})}({\cal{X}}_{k}^{(j)})

V
Approximate Update Step

In the following, we will describe the approximate update step used by our TBD method. This update step approximates the exact MB mixture posterior pdf in (
11
) by a Poisson/MB pdf. This limits computational complexity and, thus, enables real-time tracking of multiple low-observable objects

.

V-A

MB Approximation

First, we approximate the exact MB mixture posterior pdf in (
11
) by an MB pdf.
This approximation is based on approximating the association pmf
p(\bm{a}^{\prime}_{k})
in (
12

We first extend the association alphabet
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{J_{k},M}
in (

V-B

Recycling of MB Components

The approximate posterior pdf in (
23
) consists of
J_{k}=J_{k-1}+M
Bernoulli components, i.e., the number of Bernoulli components increases by
M
at each time step
k

\lambda_{\text{B}}(\bm{x}_{k})
, may appear in the scene and potentially contribute to all of the
M
measurements. However, many Bernoulli components typically have a very low existence probability and are thus unlikely to represent an existing object.

In order for real-time tracking to remain feasible, the number of Bernoulli components has to be limited. Contrary to most multiobject tracking methods (see e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
), rather than pruning components with an existence probability below a fixed threshold and potentially discarding valuable tracking information, we employ the concept of recycling
[
19
]
. Here, Bernoulli components
j\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\mathcal{J}_{k}^{\text{R}}\hskip-0.85358pt\subseteq\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\ldots,J_{k}\}
with an existence probability
r_{k}^{(j)}
below a predefined threshold
\eta_{\text{R}}
are “transferred” to the Poisson part of the posterior pdf by means of moment matching
[
19
]
. This yields the approximated posterior

PHD

\lambda(\bm{x}_{k})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{k}^{\text{R}}}r_{k}^{(j)}\hskip 0.85358ptf^{(j)}(\bm{x}_{k})\vskip-3.41432pt

where
r_{k}^{(j)}
is given by (
24
) or (
26
) and
f^{(j)}(\bm{x}_{k})
by (
25
) or (
27
), respectively.
After applying this recycling step, the approximate posterior pdf is again a Poisson/MB pdf. In particular, the Poisson part is represented by the approximate posterior PHD
\lambda(\bm{x}_{k})
in (
V-B
) and the MB part by the existence probabilities
r_{k}^{(j)}
and spatial pdfs
f^{(j)}(\bm{x}_{k})
,
j\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\ldots,J_{k}\}\setminus\mathcal{J}_{k}^{\text{R}}
in (
24

to

.

The example shown in Fig.
1
suggests excellent detection and estimation performance of the proposed method.
For a quantitative assessment and comparison of the performance of both filters,
we computed the mean Euclidean distance based optimal subpattern assignment (MOSPA) metric
[
22
]

with cutoff parameter
c\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt20
, order
p\hskip-0.85358pt=\!2
, and averaged over 1000 simulation Monte Carlo runs.
Fig.
2
shows the obtained results of T-TOMB/P and T-MB for
\gamma_{\text{I}}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt10
and
\gamma_{\text{I}}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt4
.
It is seen that T-TOMB/P consistently outperforms T-MB.
This can be attributed to the excellent behavior of the Bernoulli component generation/recycling scheme of T-TOMB/P and the fact that T-TOMB/P considers data association, i.e., a measurement is allowed to be associated to at most one object and an object to exactly one measurement, respectively.
The performance of T-MB depends greatly on the choice of
\eta_{\text{new}}
.
A smaller value of
\eta_{\text{new}}
results in a faster detection of newly appearing objects,
but in a higher number of false tracks, i.e., Bernoulli components modeling not existing objects, on the other hand.
Another cause for the performance difference can be understood by considering the case where two objects come close to each other. Since T-MB allows a measurement to be associated to more than one object, one of the two objects can be tracked by both tracks and the other object by none after object separation. This is a direct consequence of the fact that T-MB does not consider data

association.

VII
Conclusion

We proposed a scalable track-before-detect (TBD) method for the tracking of low-observable objects that relies on a Poisson/multi-Bernoulli model. To limit the computational complexity, we approximated the exact posterior pdf by a multi-Bernoulli pdf. For a reliable tracking in real time, a recycling of Bernoulli components is performed. We demonstrated that with the proposed method, a significant improvement in tracking performance can be achieved compared to a state-of-the-art TBD tracking method. A possible direction for future research is an extension of the proposed method to a more general measurement model by using sum-product algorithms for data association with extended

objects
[
23
,
24
]
.

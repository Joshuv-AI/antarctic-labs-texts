Constructing Galois representations with prescribed Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariant
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 be a prime number. We consider the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariants associated to modular Bloch-Kato Selmer groups, considered over the cyclotomic ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Let g g be a p p -ordinary cuspidal newform of weight 2 2 and trivial nebentype. We assume that the μ \mu -invariant of g g vanishes, and that the image of the residual representation associated to g g is suitably large. We show that for any number n n greater than or equal to the λ \lambda -invariant of g g , there are infinitely many newforms f f that are p p -congruent to g g , with λ \lambda -invariant equal to n n . We also prove quantitative results regarding the levels of such modular forms with prescribed λ \lambda -invariant.

1.
Introduction

The Iwasawa theory of Selmer groups associated to Galois representations captures significant arithmetic information about motives. Let
p
be a prime number. Given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, and a number field extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
, the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
F
is of fundamental importance and captures information about the Mordell-Weil group
E(F)
and the
p
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/F)
. The fundamental object of study in the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves is the
p
-primary Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, denoted
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
. Mazur
[
Maz72
]
initiated the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves
E
with good ordinary reduction at
p
, and associated structural invariants associated with these Selmer groups.

1.1.
Main results

In this paper we consider
\lambda
-invariants associated to Bloch-Kato Selmer groups attached to modular Galois representations. We prove certain qualitative and quantitative results about the levels of modular forms that arise in natural families, for which the
\lambda
-invariant is prescribed to be a fixed value. We fix a cuspidal Hecke newform
g
of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{g})
. Associated with a fixed choice of embedding
\iota_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, let
\rho_{g}
be the associated Galois representation. It is assumed that the image of the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is up to conjugation, equal to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. When we assume that
p
is odd and
g
is
p
-ordinary, the Iwasawa
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants are well defined, and denoted by
\mu_{p}(g)
and
\lambda_{p}(g)
respectively (cf. section
3.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime and let

be a normalized newform of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{g})
. We assume the following conditions

(1)

We shall assume that the image of the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{g}
lies in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Moreover, the Galois representation

\bar{\rho}_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is surjective.

(2)

The modular form

is
p
-ordinary and
p\nmid N_{g}
,

(3)

g
has optimal level, i.e.,

is the prime to
p
part of the Artin conductor of the residual representation,

(4)

\mu_{p}(g)=0
.

Associated with

be the set of prime numbers

(2)

1.2.
Relationship with previous work

The existence of Galois representations for which the associated Selmer groups have large
\lambda
-invariant has been studied by various authors, cf.
[
Gre99
,
Ray23
]
. Our results are significantly stronger since we are able to explicitly realize any large enough integer as a
\lambda
-invariant. If at a given prime
p\geq 5
, there exists a newform
g
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, and such that
\lambda_{p}(g)=0
, then, every integer
n\geq 0
is seen to arise from a modular form which is
p
-congruent to
g
. This is indeed the case for a density
1
set of primes
p
, as shown by the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Furthermore, not only is one able to construct infinitely many modular Galois representations giving rise to a prescribed
\lambda
-invariant, one also obtains an explicit and satisfactory quantitative description of their levels. We contrast Theorem
1.1
to the results of recent work by Hatley and Kundu
[
HK22
]
, where it is shown that there are infinitely many modular forms
f
that are
p
-congruent to a fixed modular form
g
, for which
\lambda_{p}(f)=\lambda_{p}(g)
. This
\lambda
-stability result requires an additional assumption on
g
: that the
\lambda
-invariant of
g
is minimal in the family of all
\lambda
-invariants for modular forms that are
p
-congruent to
g
. For further details, we refer to p.15 of
loc. cit.
This assumption is clearly satisfied when
\lambda_{p}(g)
is
0
. This
\lambda
-stability result follows from Theorem
1.1
in the special case when
n=0
, without the additional hypothesis. The method used in proving our results does draw some inspiration from
[
Ray23
,
HK22
]
.

The level raising results of Diamond-Taylor

Let

is

[

]

arises from a cuspidal newform
g
of weight

on
\Gamma_{1}(N_{\bar{\rho}})
, where

is the prime to
p
part of the Artin-conductor of

(cf.
[
RS99
, p.11]
). The weight

is prescribed according to
[
Ser87

. Khare and Wintenberger
[

,

]
proved Serre’s conjecture, building upon prior work of Ribet
[
Rib90
]
. Suppose that

is a newform of weight

coprime to

lifts

. Then, the optimal level

divides

]

Theorem 2.1

(Carayol)
.

Suppose there exists a modular form

of weight
k
of level

such that
\bar{\rho}=\bar{\rho}_{f}
is absolutely irreducible. The level

admits a factorization
N_{f}=N_{\bar{\rho}}\prod_{\ell}\ell^{\alpha(\ell)}
,
and for each
\ell
with

, one of the following holds:

(1)

\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
,
\ell\left(\operatorname{trace}\bar{\rho}\left(\sigma_{\ell}\right)\right)^{2}=\left(1+\ell\right)^{2}\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}\left(\sigma_{\ell}\right)
in
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and
\alpha(\ell)=1
;

(2)

\ell\equiv-1\mod{p}
and one of the following holds:

(a)

\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
,
\operatorname{trace}\bar{\rho}\left(\sigma_{\ell}\right)\equiv 0
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and

;

(b)

\ell\mid\!\mid N_{\bar{\rho}}
,
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}
is unramified at
\ell
, and
\alpha(\ell)=1
;

(3)

\ell\equiv 1\mod{p}

\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
and
\alpha(\ell)=2

(b)

\ell\mid\!\mid N_{\bar{\rho}}
and
\alpha(\ell)=1
or
\ell\nmid N_{\bar{\rho}}
and
\alpha(\ell)=1
.

Definition 2.2
.

The set of levels satisfying the conditions outlined in Theorem

\Lambda
denote the Iwasawa algebra
\mathcal{O}\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}\left[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}\right]
. Given a module
M
over
\Lambda
, we shall set
M^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cnts}}\left(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
. A module
M
over
\Lambda
is said to be cofinitely generated (resp. cotorsion) if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated (resp. torsion) as a module over
\Lambda
. The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})
is a cofinitely generated module over
\Lambda
. There are many cases of interest for which it is known to be cotorsion over
\Lambda
, which we shall further discuss in the next subsection. Given
\Lambda
-modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
, a
pseudo-isomorphism
is a map of
\Lambda
-modules
M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
whose kernel and cokernel both have finite cardinality. Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module, then, as is well known, there is a pseudo-isomorphism

M^{\vee}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(\varpi^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})\right).

In the above sum,
s,t,m_{i},n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
f_{j}(T)
is an irreducible distinguished polynomial in
\Lambda
, i.e., an irreducible monic polynomial whose non-leading coefficients are divisible by
\varpi
. The
\mu
-invariant is given by

\mu_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i}m_{i}&\text{ if }s>0;\\
0&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

On the other hand, the
\lambda
-invariant is given by

\lambda_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{j}n_{j}\operatorname{deg}(f_{j})&\text{ if }t>0;\\
0&\text{ if }t=0.\end{cases}

It follows from results of Kato
[
K+04
]
that
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}/A_{f})
is a cotorsion module over
\Lambda
. Then, the
\mu
(resp.
\lambda
) invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})
is denoted by
\mu_{p}(f)
(resp.
\lambda_{p}(g)
). We note that
\mu_{p}(f)=0
if and only if
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})
is cofinitely generated as an
\mathcal{O}
-module, and in this case,

\lambda_{p}(f)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathcal{O}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})\right).

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that the
\lambda
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})
is equal to the
\lambda
-invariant of
\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{BK}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}},A_{f})
. We shall set
\operatorname{rank}^{\operatorname{BK}}(f)
to denote the
\mathcal{O}
-corank of the Bloch-Kato Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}^{\operatorname{BK}}(\mathbb{Q},A_{f})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume the
\mu_{p}(f)=0
. The following assertions hold:

(1)

\operatorname{rank}^{\operatorname{BK}}(f)\leq\lambda_{p}(f)
,

(2)

\operatorname{rank}^{\operatorname{BK}}(f)\equiv\lambda_{p}(f)\mod{2}
,

(3)

suppose that
\lambda_{p}(f)\leq 1
, then,
\operatorname{rank}^{\operatorname{BK}}(f)=\lambda_{p}(f)
.

Proof.

Part (
1
) follows as a direct consequence of
[
Och00
, Theorem A]
. Part (
2
) follows from a standard argument due to Greenberg
[
Gre99
, Proposition 3.10]
, see
[
HL19
, p.1288, proof of Theorem 5.7, ll. 9-15]
. Part (
3
) is a direct consequence of (
1
) and (
2
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We note that the conventions used here are the same as those in
[
GV00
, section 2]
. Note that the ring
\mathcal{O}
is chosen to be the valuation ring of
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
. It is easy to see that the definition of the
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{p}(f)
is independent of the choice of field
F
, and thus the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
. Also, we note that the
\mu
-invariant vanishes for the Selmer group over
\mathcal{O}
, if and only if it vanishes after base-change by any valuation ring
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}/\mathcal{O}
.

4.
Congruence relations between Selmer groups

We recall some results of Greenberg and Vatsal
[
GV00
]
, and apply these results to study the structure of Selmer groups of ordinary Galois representations. Let
g
be a Hecke newform of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{g})
. Here,
N_{g}
is the level of
g
. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

p\nmid N_{g}
,

(2)

g
is ordinary at
p
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\rho}_{g}
is absolutely irreducible,

(4)

g
has optimal level, i.e.,
N_{g}
is the prime to
p
part of the Artin conductor of
\bar{\rho}
.

Let
f
be a Hecke new cuspform of weight
2
on such that
\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{g}
. The understanding here is that the field
F
is chosen so that all the Fourier coefficients of both
f
and
g
are contained in
F
. Remark
3.3
establishes that one may choose any large field
F
when studying the
\lambda
-invariant or the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant. Let
N_{f}
be the level of
f
. We assume that
p\nmid N_{f}
. Note that by Theorem
2.1
,
N_{f}\in\mathcal{S}(\bar{\rho})
. We note that
N_{g}
divides
N_{f}
. We note that
f
has ordinary reduction at
p
(cf.
[
Ray23
, Lemma 3.3]
). Let
\epsilon_{f}
be the nebentype of
f
, and
\bar{\epsilon}_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\kappa^{\times}
its mod-
\varpi
reduction. We find that
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{g}=\bar{\chi}
, and
\operatorname{det}\bar{\rho}_{g}=\bar{\chi}\bar{\epsilon}_{f}
where
\bar{\chi}
is the mod-
p
cyclotomic character. Since
\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{g}
, we find that
\bar{\epsilon}_{f}=1
. The principal units in
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
are the units that reduce to
1
modulo
(\varpi)
. It is clear that if a principal unit is a root of unity, then it is equal to
1
. Since
\epsilon_{f}
is a finite order character,
\bar{\epsilon}_{f}=1
implies that
\epsilon_{f}=1
. Therefore,
f
is a weight
2
cuspform on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{f})
.

Theorem 4.1

(Greenberg-Vatsal
[
GV00
]
)
.

With respect to above notation, assume that
\mu_{p}(g)=0
. Then,
\mu_{p}(f)=0
, and

\lambda_{p}(f)-\lambda_{p}(g)=\sum_{\ell\mid N_{f}}\left(\sigma_{\ell}(g)-\sigma_{\ell}(f)\right),

where the sum ranges over the primes
\ell
dividing
N_{f}
.

We study the numbers
\left(\sigma_{\ell}(g)-\sigma_{\ell}(f)\right)
for primes
\ell|N_{f}
. Let
A
be a local
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, and residue field
A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}
isomorphic to
\kappa
. Fix an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra isomorphism
\varphi_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A/\mathfrak{m}_{A}\rightarrow\kappa
. Let
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow\kappa
be the map obtained upon composing the reduction modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
map, with
\varphi_{0}
. We set

\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\mid\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi(a)&\varphi(b)\\
\varphi(c)&\varphi(d)\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\right\}.

For
n\geq 1
, the map
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}\rightarrow\kappa
is the reduction modulo
\varpi
map.

Definition 4.2
.

Associated with
g
, let
\Pi_{g}
be the set of prime numbers
\ell
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\ell\nmid N_{g}p
,

(2)

\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
and
\ell^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod{p^{2}}
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{g}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
for a suitable choice of basis for the residual representation.

Let
\Omega_{g}
be the set of prime numbers such that

(1)

\ell\nmid N_{g}p
,

(2)

\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{g}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}-\ell&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}}\right)
for a suitable choice of basis for the residual representation.

In section
5
, we shall introduce further assumptions on the image of
\bar{\rho}_{g}
, so that the sets
\Pi_{g}
and
\Omega_{g}
have positive density. For
\ell\in\Pi_{g}
(resp.
\Omega_{g}
), we note that
\rho_{g}
is unramified at
\ell
. Since
\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{g}
, we find that
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is unramified at
\ell
as well. Thus,
\rho_{f}(\operatorname{I}_{\ell})
is contained in
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
, which is a pro-
p
group. Hence,
\rho_{f|\ell}
factors through
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{p-tr}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, where
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{p-tr}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
is the maximal tamely ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
with pro-
p
inertia. The Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{p-tr}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is generated by a frobenius
\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}
, and the pro-
p
tame inertia generator
\tau_{\ell}
, subject to the relation
\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}\tau_{\ell}\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}^{-1}=\tau_{\ell}^{\ell}
(cf. for instance
[
Ram02
, p.123, ll.15-16]
).

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and consider the reduction mod-
\varpi^{n}
map

\pi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}).

Given a ring
A
, denote by
\operatorname{M}_{2}(A)
the group of
2\times 2
matrices with entries in
A
. A matrix
M
in the kernel of
\pi_{n}
can be written in the following form

M=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right).

Here,
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)
belongs to
\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1})
. However, since the coefficients of the matrix
M
belong to
\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1}
, it follows that
a,b,c,d
are determined modulo
\varpi
. Thus the matrix
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)
can be assumed to be in
\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi)
.

To illustrate this, consider an example in the case when
n=1
, and
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\\
\end{array}}\right)
. We find that

M=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right).

On the other hand,

\operatorname{Id}+\varpi\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi&0\\
0&0\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi+\varpi^{2}&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)=M,

since
\varpi^{2}=0
when the coefficients are in
\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{2}
.

For
i=1,2
and
M_{i}\in\operatorname{ker}\pi_{n}
, and let
B_{i}\in M_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi)
be such that
M_{i}=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B_{i}
. Then, since
2n\geq n+1
, we have
\varpi^{2n}=0
. Therefore, we find that

M_{1}M_{2}=\left(\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B_{1}\right)\left(\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B_{2}\right)=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}(B_{1}+B_{2}).

The inverse of
M=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B
is given by
M^{-1}=\operatorname{Id}-\varpi^{n}B
. It is easy to see that the map

\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi)\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}\pi_{n},

mapping
B
to
\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B
is an isomorphism of abelian groups. Let
B\in\operatorname{ker}\pi_{n}
and
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1})
. We write
B=\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B^{\prime}
, where
B^{\prime}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi)
. We write

[B^{\prime},M]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=B^{\prime}\bar{M}-\bar{M}B^{\prime}\in\operatorname{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi),

where
\bar{M}
is the reduction of
M
modulo
\varpi
.

Lemma 4.3
.

With respect to notation above,

BMB^{-1}=M+\varpi^{n}[B^{\prime},M].

Proof.

We find that since
\varpi^{n+1}=0
, in particular,
\varpi^{2n}=0
. Therefore, we find that

\begin{split}BMB^{-1}&=(\operatorname{Id}+\varpi^{n}B^{\prime})M(\operatorname{Id}-\varpi^{n}B^{\prime})\\
&=M+\varpi^{n}[B^{\prime},M].\end{split}

Note that
\varpi^{n}[B^{\prime},\bar{M}]
is determined by the reduction of
M
modulo
\varpi
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that

(1)

\ell\nmid N_{g}p
,

(2)

\ell\nmid\pm 1\mod{p}
,

(3)

\bar{\rho}_{g}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta\ell&\\
&\eta\\
\end{array}}\right)
, where
\eta=\pm 1
,

(4)

\rho_{f}
is ramified at
\ell
.

Then, up to conjugation by a matrix in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
,

\rho_{f}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell x&\\
&x\\
\end{array}}\right)\text{ and }\rho_{f}(\tau_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&y\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right),

where
x\equiv\eta\mod{\varpi}
and
y\equiv 0\mod{\varpi}
.

Proof.

We choose an integral basis for
T_{f}
so that
\bar{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta\ell&0\\
0&\eta\\
\end{array}}\right)
. Since
\ell\nmid N_{g}p
,
\bar{\rho}_{g}
is unramified at
\ell
. Since
f
and
g
satisfy the congruence
\bar{\rho}_{f}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{g}
, it follows that
\bar{\rho}_{f}
is unramified at
\ell
. Since
\tau_{\ell}
is in the inertia group at
\ell
, we find that
\bar{\rho}_{f}(\tau_{\ell})=\operatorname{Id}
. Thus, for
n=1
, the upper triangular entry is
0
.

We show that there is a matrix
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
so that
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A\rho_{f}A^{-1}
satisfies the above conditions. In fact, the matrix
A
can be chosen to be in
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
. For ease of notation, we set
\rho^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{f}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
denote the
\mod{\varpi^{n}}
reduction of
\rho^{\prime}
. We shall inductively specify matrices
A_{n}\in\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
such that
A_{m}\equiv A_{n}\mod{\varpi^{n}}
for all integers
m>n
. We shall then let
A
denote the matrix
(A_{n})
in the inverse limit

\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O})=\varprojlim_{n}\widehat{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}).

Let us set
\rho_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A_{n}\rho_{n}^{\prime}A_{n}^{-1}
. The matrices
A_{n}
shall have the property that there exist

such that
\rho_{n}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell x_{n}&\\
&z_{n}\\
\end{array}}\right)
and
\rho_{n}(\tau_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&y_{n}\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, where

and

. Since

, it shall then follow that

,

. Setting

to denote the inverse limits

,
(y_{n})
and

&z\\

&1\\
\end{array}}\right).

The relation

Suppose that for some

\end{array}}\right).

We find that

\begin{split}r_{n+1}^{\prime}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}\tau_{\ell}\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}^{-1})=&\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell x_{n+1}&\\
&z_{n+1}\\
\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi^{n}e&\widetilde{y}_{n}+\varpi^{n}f\\
\varpi^{n}g&1+\varpi^{n}h\\
\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell x_{n+1}&\\
&z_{n+1}\\
\end{array}}\right)^{-1},\\
=&\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi^{n}e&\ell x_{n+1}z_{n+1}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{y}_{n}+\varpi^{n}f\right)\\
\varpi^{n}\ell^{-1}z_{n+1}x_{n+1}^{-1}g&1+\varpi^{n}h\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
=&\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi^{n}e&\ell x_{n+1}z_{n+1}^{-1}\widetilde{y}_{n}+\varpi^{n}\ell f\\
\varpi^{n}\ell^{-1}g&1+\varpi^{n}h\\
\end{array}}\right),\\
r_{n+1}^{\prime}(\tau_{\ell})^{\ell}=&\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\varpi^{n}\ell e&\ell\widetilde{y}_{n}+\varpi^{n}\ell f\\
\varpi^{n}\ell g&1+\varpi^{n}\ell h\\
\end{array}}\right).\end{split}

We note that
x_{n+1}\equiv z_{n+1}\equiv\eta\mod{\varpi}
, and thus
x_{n+1}z_{n+1}^{-1}\equiv 1\mod{\varpi}
. The coefficients of the above matrices are in
\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n+1}
, and hence, the relations

\varpi^{n}x_{n+1}z_{n+1}^{-1}=\varpi^{n}\text{ and }\varpi^{n}z_{n+1}x_{n+1}^{-1}=\varpi^{n}

are used in the above equations. Since
\ell\not\equiv\pm 1\mod{p}
, we find that
e=g=h=0
. Hence, we find that

\begin{split}&r_{n+1}^{\prime}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell x_{n+1}&\\
&z_{n+1}\\
\end{array}}\right);\\
&r_{n+1}^{\prime}(\tau_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&y_{n+1}\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right).\end{split}

We set
A_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=BB_{n+1}
, we note that
A_{n}\equiv B_{n+1}\mod{\varpi^{n}}
, and
B\equiv\operatorname{Id}\mod{\varpi^{n}}
. With respect to this choice,
\rho_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=r_{n+1}^{\prime}
, and
A_{n}=A_{n+1}\mod{\varpi^{n}}
. This completes the inductive lifting argument. By previous remarks in the proof, this is enough to establish the result.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\ell\nmid N_{g}p
be a prime that divides
N_{f}
. Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
\ell\in\Pi_{g}
, then,
\sigma_{\ell}(g)=1
and
\sigma_{\ell}(f)=0
.

(2)

If
\ell\in\Omega_{g}
, then,
\sigma_{\ell}(g)=0
and
\sigma_{\ell}(f)=0
.

Proof.

We begin by proving part (
1
). Since
\rho_{g}
is unramified at
\ell
, we find that
V^{\prime}_{g}=V_{g}
. Since
\bar{\rho}_{g}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\ell&\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, it follows that
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)=(1-X)(1-\ell X)
. Since
\ell\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
, it follows that
\ell^{-1}
is a root of
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)
with multiplicity
1
. Therefore,
d_{\ell}(g)=1
. On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that
V_{f}^{\prime}
is the trivial
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
-module, we find that
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(f;X)=1-X
, and
\ell^{-1}
is not a root of
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(f;X)
. Therefore,
d_{\ell}(f)=0
. Since
\ell^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\mod{p^{2}}
, it follows that that
s_{\ell}=1
. Since
\sigma_{\ell}(\cdot)=s_{\ell}d_{\ell}(\cdot)
, we find that
\sigma_{\ell}(g)=1
and
\sigma_{\ell}(f)=0
.

Next, we prove assertion (
2
). Since
\bar{\rho}_{g}(\operatorname{fr}_{\ell})=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}-\ell&\\
&-1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, it follows that
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)=(1+X)(1+\ell X)
. Thus, the roots of
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)
are
\{-1,-\ell^{-1}\}
. Since
\ell\not\equiv-1\mod{p}
, we find that
\ell^{-1}
is not a root of
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)
, and therefore,
d_{\ell}(g)=0
. Hence, it follows that
\sigma_{\ell}(g)=0
. As in the proof of part (
1
), it follows that
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(f;X)=(1+X)
. Therefore,
\ell^{-1}
is not a root of this polynomial, and it thus follows that
\sigma_{\ell}(f)=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)
, and let
\ell\neq p
be a prime which divides
N_{g}
. Then,
\sigma_{\ell}(f)=\sigma_{\ell}(g)
.

Proof.

It suffices for us to show that
d_{\ell}(f)=d_{\ell}(g)
. Let
h\in\{g,f\}
, recall that
V_{h}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(V_{h})_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}
. Note that as modules over the inertia group
\operatorname{I}_{\ell}
,
V_{h}
and
A_{h}[\varpi]
are self dual. Since
\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(N_{f})=\operatorname{ord}_{\ell}(N_{g})
, and
N_{g}
is the prime to
p
part of the Artin-conductor of
\bar{\rho}
, it follows that

(4.3)

\operatorname{dim}A_{h}[\varpi]_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}=\operatorname{dim}(V_{h})_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}},

(cf.
[
EPW06
, proof of Lemma 4.1.2]
). Recall that
P_{\ell}(h;X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}X\mid V_{h}^{\prime}\right)
and that
\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(h;X)
is the mod-
\varpi
reduction of
P_{\ell}(h;X)
. Set
T_{h}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(T_{h})_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}
. We identify
A_{h}[\varpi]
with
T_{h}/\varpi T_{h}
, and thus
A_{h}[\varpi]_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}
with
T_{h}^{\prime}/\varpi T_{h}^{\prime}
. The equality (
4.3
) implies that
T_{h}^{\prime}
is torsion free. We identify
T_{h}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
with
V_{h}^{\prime}
, and since
T_{h}^{\prime}
is torsion free, we find that
T_{h}^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice in
V_{h}^{\prime}
, and that
P_{\ell}(h;X)=\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}X\mid T_{h}^{\prime}\right)
. Therefore, the mod-
\varpi
reduction of

is given by

\begin{split}\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(h;X)=&\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}X\mid\left(T_{h}^{\prime}/\varpi T_{h}^{\prime}\right)\right),\\
=&\operatorname{det}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{fr}_{\ell}X\mid A_{h}[\varpi]_{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}\right),\\
\end{split}

Since
A_{g}[\varpi]\simeq A_{f}[\varpi]
as
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
-modules, we find that

\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}

\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(f;X)=\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(g;X)

.
∎

\lambda
-invariant

Throughout this section,
p\geq 5

g
be a normalized newform of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{g})
. Throughout this section, we assume the following conditions.

(1)

The residue field
\kappa=\mathcal{O}/\varpi
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, i.e.,

where
\mathfrak{p}

(2)

The Galois representation

has good ordinary reduction at

(2)

\bar{\rho}_{g}\simeq\bar{\rho}_{f}
,

\operatorname{rank}^{\operatorname{BK}}(f)=\lambda_{p}(g)

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It suffices to show that there is a non-CM Hecke newform
g
of weight
2
on
\Gamma_{0}(N_{g})
and a density
1
set of primes
\Sigma
such that for all
p\in\Sigma
,

(1)

p\geq 5
,

(2)

The residue field
\kappa=\mathcal{O}/\varpi
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, i.e.,
f(\mathfrak{p}/p)=1
.

(3)

The Galois representation

\bar{\rho}_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is surjective.

(4)

The modular form
g
is
p
-ordinary and
p\nmid N_{g}
,

(5)

g
has optimal level, i.e.,
N_{g}
is the prime to
p
part of the Artin conductor of the residual representation,

(6)

\mu_{p}(g)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(g)=0
.

It then follows from Theorem
1.1
that for
p\in\Sigma
, there are infinitely many Hecke newforms of weight
2
such that

(1)

f
has good ordinary reduction at
p
,

(2)

\bar{\rho}_{f}
is surjective,

(3)

\mu_{p}(f)=0
,

(4)

\lambda_{p}(f)=n
.

Let
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be any non-CM elliptic curve with Mordell-Weil rank
0
, and let
g
be the associated Hecke newform. Consider the following observations.

•

The field of Fourier coefficients of
g
is
\mathbb{Q}
, since it is associated to an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. In particular, it follows that the residue field
\kappa
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for all primes
p
.

•

The set of primes at which
E
has good ordinary reduction has density
1
, by a result of Serre
[
Ser81
]
.

•

Serre’s open image theorem
[
Ser72
]
shows that for all but finitely many primes
p
, the residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{g}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

•

If at a given prime

is not optimal, then there must be a
p
-congruence between

and a newform

of weight
2
and strictly smaller level. This follows from Ribet’s level lowering theorem. There are only finitely many newforms of weight
2
with level strictly less than

. Also, there are only finitely many primes
p
for which two newforms are
p
-congruent. Therefore, for all but finitely many primes

, the level

is optimal.

•

Finally, it follows from a theorem of Greenberg
[
Gre99
, Theorems 4.1, 5.1]
that for a density
1
set of primes

,
\mu_{p}(g)=\lambda_{p}(g)=0
.

Therefore, there is a set of primes
\Sigma
of density
1
such that the above conditions are satisfied. This completes the proof.
∎

The complex Ginzburg–Landau equation perturbed by a force localised both in physical and Fourier spaces
Vahagn Nersesyan

Abstract
In the paper [ KNS20a ] , a criterion for exponential mixing is established for a class of random dynamical systems. In that paper, the criterion is applied to PDEs perturbed by a noise localised in the Fourier space. In the present paper, we show that, in the case of the complex Ginzburg–Landau (CGL) equation, that criterion can be used to consider even more degenerate noise that is localised both in physical and Fourier spaces. This is achieved by checking that the linearised equation is almost surely approximately controllable. We also study the problem of controllability of the nonlinear CGL equation. Using Agrachev–Sarychev type arguments, we prove an approximate controllability property in the case of a control force which is again localised in physical and Fourier spaces. . \{\eta_{n}\}\subset\Theta(u_{0},0,T_{n})\cap L^{2}(J_{T_{n}},{\cal H})
such that
N_{n}\to+\infty
and

\|{\cal R}_{T_{n}}(u_{0},0,h+\chi\eta_{n})-u_{0}-\chi^{q^{N_{n}}}\eta\|_{s}<\varepsilon/2,\quad n\geq 1.

(3.13)

Combining this with (
3.12
), the fact that
\chi(x)\in[0,1)
for
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\setminus{\cal O}
, and the Lebesgue theorem on dominated
convergence, we derive approximate controllability in small time from the initial position
u_{0}\in H^{s+1}
to the target
u_{0}+{\mathbb{I}}_{{\cal O}}u_{1}
in the
L^{2}
-norm. Taking
\eta
equal to zero on a small interval of time and using the regularising property of the CGL equation, we obtain approximate controllability in small time from arbitrary
u_{0}\in H^{s}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

The starting point of the proof is (
3.13
), where we take
\chi\equiv 1
. The saturation assumption (Definition
3.7
) implies that we have
approximate controllability in small time in the sense that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
T_{0}>0
,
and
u_{0},u_{1}\in H^{s}
, there are
T\in(0,T_{0})
and
\eta\in\Theta(u_{0},0,T)\cap L^{2}(J_{T};{\cal H})
such that

\|{\cal R}_{T}(u_{0},0,h+\eta)-u_{1}\|_{s}<\varepsilon.

Thus, the theorem will be proved if we show that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
T>0
and
u_{1}\in H^{s}
, there is
\eta\in\Theta(u_{1},0,T)\cap L^{2}(J_{T};{\cal H})
verifying

\|{\cal R}_{T}(u_{1},0,h+\eta)-u_{1}\|_{s}<\varepsilon

(with initial condition coinciding with the target
u_{1}
). By Proposition
3.1
, there are constants
r\in(0,\varepsilon)
and
\tau>0
such that
(0,0)\in\hat{\Theta}(v,\tau)
and

\|{\cal R}_{t}(v,0,h)-u_{1}\|_{s}<\varepsilon\quad\text{for any $v\in B_{H^{s}}(u_{1},r),\,\,t\in J_{\tau}$}.

If
\tau\geq T
, then the proof of the theorem is complete. If
\tau<T
, we use the approximate controllability property with initial condition
u_{0}={\cal R}_{\tau}(v,h)
, small time
T^{\prime}<T-\tau
, and target
u_{1}
. Thus, we find
\hat{\eta}\in\Theta(u_{0},0,T^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(J_{T^{\prime}},{\cal H})
such that

{\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}(u_{0},0,h+\hat{\eta})\in B_{H^{s}}(u_{1},r).

Again, if
2\tau+T^{\prime}>T
, then the proof is complete. If
2\tau+T^{\prime}<T
, we apply the controllability property to return to
B_{H^{s}}(u_{1},r)
. Iterating finitely many times this argument, we complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

If
{\cal I}
is a generator, then
{\cal H}({\cal I})
is saturating by Proposition
4.2
. Applying Theorem
3.8
, we derive the required approximate controllability property.

If
{\cal I}
is not a generator, let
l
be any vector in the non-empty set
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus\tilde{\cal I}
. The assumption that
h\in L^{2}(J_{T};H^{s-1}({\cal I}))
, the fact that
H^{s}({\cal I})
is invariant for the linear CGL equation (i.e., Eq. (
3.5
) with
B=0
), and that the term
B
maps
H^{s}({\cal I})
to itself imply that
the set

{\cal A}=\{{\cal R}_{T}(0,0,h+\eta):\eta\in\Theta(u_{0},0,T)\cap L^{2}(J_{T};{\cal H})\}

is contained in
H^{s}({\cal I})
. Thus,
\cos\langle l,x\rangle
and
\sin\langle l,x\rangle
are orthogonal to
{\cal A}
, so the set attainable from the origin with
{\cal H}
-valued controls is not dense in
H^{s}
.
∎

4
Examples of saturating subspaces

4.1
Linearised equation

Let
s\geq 0
be an arbitrary number,
{\cal I}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a finite set containing the zero vector, and
{\cal H}({\cal I})
be defined by (
1.10
). We denote by
{\cal H}_{k}({\cal I})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
the subspaces
{\cal H}_{k}
given by relations (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) for
{\cal H}={\cal H}({\cal I})
.
In this section, we prove the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

The subspace
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})
is danse in
H^{s}

if and only if
{\cal I}
is a generator.

Proof.

Assume that
{\cal I}
is a generator. From the identities

\displaystyle 2\cos\langle l,x\rangle\cos\langle m,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos\langle l-m,x\rangle+\cos\langle l+m,x\rangle,

\displaystyle 2\sin\langle l,x\rangle\sin\langle m,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\cos\langle l-m,x\rangle-\cos\langle l+m,x\rangle,

\displaystyle 2\sin\langle l,x\rangle\cos\langle m,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sin\langle l-m,x\rangle+\sin\langle l+m,x\rangle,

\displaystyle 2\cos\langle l,x\rangle\sin\langle m,x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sin\langle m-l,x\rangle+\sin\langle l+m,x\rangle

it follows that if
l,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{*}^{d}
are such that

\cos\langle m,x\rangle,\,\sin\langle m,x\rangle\in{\cal H}({\cal I})\,\,\text{ and}\,\,\cos\langle l,x\rangle,\,\sin\langle l,x\rangle\in{\cal H}_{k}({\cal I})

for some
k\geq 0
,
then

\cos\langle m\pm l,x\rangle,\,\sin\langle m\pm l,x\rangle\in{\cal H}_{k+1}({\cal I}).

This implies that

\cos\langle l,x\rangle,\,\sin\langle l,x\rangle\in{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})\quad\text{for any $l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}$},

so the space
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})
is danse in
H^{s}
for any
s\geq 0
.

If
{\cal I}
is not a generator, we argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.9
. Let
l
be any vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}_{*}\setminus\tilde{\cal I}
. From the above trigonometric identities it follows that
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})\subset{\cal H}(\tilde{\cal I})
. Thus, the functions
\cos\langle l,x\rangle
and
\sin\langle l,x\rangle
are orthogonal to the subspace
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})
in
H^{s}
. Hence,
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})
is
not dense in
H^{s}
. ∎

4.2
Nonlinear equation

We start this section with a proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let us denote by
{\cal G}
the subspace on the right-hand side of (
3.9
). Clearly, we have
{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}\subset{\cal G}
. To prove the inverse inclusion,
we take any vectors
\zeta_{l}\in{\cal H}_{j-1}^{\prime}
,
l=1,\ldots,2p+1
and
consider the function
F:{\mathbb{R}}^{2p+1}\to{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
defined by

F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2p+1})=B\left(\sum_{l=1}^{2p+1}x_{l}\zeta_{l}\right).

As the subspace
{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
is invariant under complex conjugation, we have
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\zeta\in{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
for any
\zeta\in{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
. Let us choose
\zeta_{l}\in{\cal H}_{j-1}^{\prime}
,
l=1,\ldots,2p+1
to be real vectors. Then

F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2p+1})=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{2p+1}x_{l}\zeta_{l}\right)^{2p+1}.

As the subspace
{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
is closed, we have

\frac{\partial^{2p+1}}{\partial_{x_{1}}\ldots\partial_{x_{2p+1}}}F(0,\ldots,0)=(2p+1)!\,\zeta_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\zeta_{2p+1}\in{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}.

By linearity, this implies that

\zeta_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\zeta_{2p+1}\in{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}
for any vectors
\zeta_{l}\in{\cal H}_{j-1}^{\prime}
,
l=1,\ldots,2p+1
.
∎

Let
s\geq 0
be arbitrary,
{\cal I}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a finite set containing zero, and
{\cal H}_{k}^{\prime}({\cal I})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
be the subspaces defined by (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) for
{\cal H}={\cal H}({\cal I})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The subspace
{\cal H}_{\infty}^{\prime}({\cal I})
is dense in
H^{s}
if and only if
{\cal I}
is a generator.

Proof.

Let
{\cal I}
be a generator. From Lemma
3.3
and the assumption that
1\in{\cal H}({\cal I})
it follows that
{\cal H}_{j}({\cal I})\subset{\cal H}_{j}^{\prime}({\cal I})
for any
j\geq 1
. Thus, we have
{\cal H}_{\infty}({\cal I})\subset{\cal H}_{\infty}^{\prime}({\cal I})
, so that
{\cal H}_{\infty}^{\prime}({\cal I})
is danse in
H^{s}
, by
Proposition
4.1
. The other assertion is proved as in Proposition
4.1
, by noticing that
{\cal H}_{\infty}^{\prime}({\cal I})\subset{\cal H}(\tilde{\cal I})
.
∎

Liouville type theorems for stable solutions of elliptic system involving the Grushin operator.
Foued Mtiri

Abstract
We examine the degenerate elliptic system − Δ s u = v p , − Δ s v = u θ , u , v > 0 in ℝ N = ℝ N 1 × ℝ N 2 , where s ≥ 0 and p , θ > 0 . -\Delta_{s}u=v^{p},\quad-\Delta_{s}v=u^{\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},\quad\mbox{where }\;\;\;\;s\geq 0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;p,\theta>0. We prove that the system has no smooth stable solution provided p , θ > 0 p,\theta>0 and N s < 2 + α + β , N_{s}<2+\alpha+\beta, where α = 2 ​ ( p + 1 ) p ​ θ − 1 and β = 2 ​ ( θ + 1 ) p ​ θ − 1 . \alpha=\frac{2(p+1)}{p\theta-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta=\frac{2(\theta+1)}{p\theta-1}. This result is an extension of some result in [ 15 ] . In particular, we establish a new the integral estimate for u u and v v (see Proposition 1.1), which is crucial to deal with the case 0 < p < 1 . 0<p<1.

Introduction

We start by noting that throughout this article,
N_{s}:=N_{1}+(1+s)N_{2}
is called the
homogeneous dimension associated to the Grushin operator:

\Delta_{s}=\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2s}\Delta_{y},

where
s\geq 0,
and

\Delta_{x}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\;\;\Delta_{y}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y_{j}^{2}},

are Laplace operators with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}},

y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
and
|x|^{2s}=\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{1}}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{s}
.

In this paper, we are interested in the classification of stable solutions to the following degenerate elliptic system

\displaystyle-\Delta_{s}u=v^{p},\quad-\Delta_{s}v=u^{\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},\quad\mbox{where }\;p,\theta>0.

(1.1)

In the case
s=0,
the system (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle-\Delta u=v^{p},\quad-\Delta v=u^{\theta},\quad u,v>0\quad\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\mbox{where }\;p,\theta>0.

(1.2)

It is well studied and there
are many interesting results on classifying the solutions of this system for various
p
and
\theta.

In the last decade, increased attention has focused on the system (
1.1
).
We note that the Sobolev critical hyperbola

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{\theta+1}=\frac{N-2}{N},

which is introduced independently by Mitidieri
[
13
]
and
Van der Vorst
[
27
]
, plays a crucial role in the analysis of (
1.1
). Among the main interests, the Lane–Emden conjecture says that there has no positive classical solution if and only if the pair
(p,\theta)
lies below the Sobolev critical hyperbola, i.e.,

Conjecture.
Let
p,\theta>0
. If the pair
(p,\theta)
is subcritical i.e., if
(p,\theta)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{\theta+1}>\frac{N-2}{N},

(1.3)

Then system (
1.2
) has no positive classical solutions.

The full Lane-Emden conjecture is still open. Only partial results for nonexistence are known, and many researchers have made contribution in pushing the progress forward. We shall briefly present some
important recent developments of this conjecture

The case of radial solutions was solved by Mitidieri
[
12
]
and Serrin-Zou
[
20
]
constructed positive radial solutions on and
above the critical hyperbola, i.e

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{\theta+1}\leq\frac{N-2}{N},

which means that the nonexistence theorem is optimal for radial solutions. For non-radial solutions of the Lane-Emden
system, certain Liouville theorems are known. Denote the scaling exponents of system (
1.2
) by

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{2(p+1)}{p\theta-1},\quad\beta=\frac{2(\theta+1)}{p\theta-1},\quad p\theta>1.

(1.4)

Then subcritical condition (
1.3
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle N<2+\alpha+\beta,\quad\mbox{for }\quad p\theta>1.

(1.5)

There are no positive classical super-solutions to (
1.2
) if

\displaystyle p\theta\leq 1\quad\mbox{or }\quad p\theta>1\quad\mbox{and }\quad\max\,\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}>N-2,

see
[
12
,
23
,
21
]
. This implies the conjecture for
N=1,2.
Also, in
[
1
]
the conjecture is true for

\displaystyle\min\,\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\}>\frac{N-2}{2}\quad\mbox{with }\quad(\alpha,\beta)\neq(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N-2}{2}).

In dimension

By the definition of stability, there exist smooth positive functions

Using

\displaystyle-\Delta_{s}\varphi=pv^{p-1}\chi,\quad-\Delta_{s}\chi=\theta u^{\theta-1}\varphi\quad\mbox{in }\,\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\theta u^{\theta-1}\varphi=\Delta_{s}\left(\frac{1}{p}v^{1-p}\Delta_{s}\varphi\right)\quad\mbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}.

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta u^{\theta-1}\gamma^{2}dxdy\\

(2.1)

Using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and the fact that

we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|-4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v^{1-p}}{p}\Delta_{s}\varphi\frac{\nabla_{s}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{s}\gamma}{\varphi^{2}}\gamma dxdy\right|\\
&\leq-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v^{1-p}}{p}\Delta_{s}\varphi\frac{|\nabla_{s}\gamma|^{2}}{\varphi}dxdy-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v^{1-p}}{p}\Delta_{s}\varphi\frac{\gamma^{2}|\nabla_{s}\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi^{3}}dxy.\end{split}

(2.2)

Combining (
2.1
) and (

), one obtains, using again the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\theta u^{\theta-1}\gamma^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}\Delta_{s}\varphi\frac{\gamma\Delta_{s}\gamma}{\varphi}dxdy-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}\frac{(\Delta_{s}\varphi)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}\gamma^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}\frac{(\Delta_{s}\varphi)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}\gamma^{2}dxdy+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}(\Delta_{s}\gamma)^{2}dxdy-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}\frac{(\Delta_{s}\varphi)^{2}}{\varphi^{2}}\gamma^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}(\Delta_{s}\gamma)^{2}dxdy.

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\;C_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u|\nabla_{s}^{2}u||\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}-2}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}-2+2}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy\\
\leq&\;C_{\epsilon,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(u|\nabla_{s}^{2}u|\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy++C_{p}\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy\\
\leq&\;C_{\epsilon,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy+C_{p}\epsilon^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
&+C_{p}\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.8)

Now we shall estimate the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k}dxdy.

For the last inequality, we have used the following estimates from [
[
18
]
, Proof of Theorem 2.6]

\|\nabla_{x_{i}x_{j}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}})}\leq C(N_{1})\|\Delta_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}})},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq N_{1},

and

\|\nabla_{y_{h}y_{k}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}})}\leq C(N_{2})\|\Delta_{y}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}})},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq h,k\leq N_{2}.

Let
\psi^{r}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
with
r>2.
By direct calculations, we get

\displaystyle|\nabla_{s}^{2}(u)|\psi^{r}\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{r}\Big[u\left(|\nabla_{s}\psi|^{2}\psi^{r-2}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\psi|\psi^{r-1}\right)+|\nabla_{s}u||\nabla_{s}\psi|\psi^{r-1}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}(u\psi^{r})|\Big].

Consider
\psi=\zeta,
and
r=\frac{4kp}{p+1}\geq 2,
so that
k\geq\frac{p+1}{2p}.
We can claim, for any
0\leq\zeta\leq 1,
there exists
C_{p,k}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
&\;\leq C_{p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}(u\zeta^{\frac{4kp}{p+1}})|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy+C_{p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy\\
&\;+C_{p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.9)

Clearly
u\zeta\in H_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
so from a standard approximation and scaling argument imply then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}(u\zeta^{\frac{4kp}{p+1}})|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy&\leq\;C_{N,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta_{s}(u\zeta^{\frac{4kp}{p+1}})|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy\\
&\leq\;C_{N,p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy+C_{N,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
&+\;C_{N,p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.10)

Combining (
2.7
)–(
2.10
), we obtain the estimate for the first left term in (
2.6
):

\displaystyle Q_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{N,p,k}\epsilon^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy+C_{N,p,k}\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\;C_{N,p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.

Furthermore, by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle Q_{2}=

\displaystyle-\frac{p+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\Big(|\nabla_{s}u||\nabla_{s}\zeta|\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{p}-2}\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta(\nabla_{s}\zeta,\nabla_{s}u)\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy

\displaystyle-(4k-\frac{p+1}{p})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}-2}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}(\nabla_{s}u\cdot\nabla_{s}\zeta)\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}-1}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\Big(|\nabla_{s}u||\nabla_{s}\zeta|\Big)^{\frac{p+1}{p}-1}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{2}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}-1}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{\epsilon,p,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy+\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy.

Combining the last tow inequality with (
2.6
), we get readily

\displaystyle\;(1-C_{N,p,k}\epsilon)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{s}u|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\zeta^{4k-\frac{p+1}{p}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,p,N,k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy

\displaystyle\;+C_{N,p,k}\epsilon^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy.

Take
\epsilon
small enough, the lemma follows.∎

Now, using Lemma
2.2
and (
2.5
) , we obtain also

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{1-p}[\Delta_{s}(u\zeta^{2k})]^{2}dxdy\leq&\;C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy\\
&\;+\left(1+C\epsilon\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.11)

Thanks to the approximation argument, the stability property (
1.7
), with
\gamma=u\zeta^{2k}
where
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\;\theta p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy-\left(1+C\epsilon\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
\leq&\;C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.12)

Multiplying the equation
-\Delta_{s}v=u^{\theta}
by
u\zeta^{4k}
and integrating by parts, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv|\Delta(\zeta^{4k})|dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v|\nabla_{s}u||\nabla_{s}(\zeta^{4k})|dxdy.

Using Young’s inequality and applying again Lemma
2.2
, we can conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C=C_{N,\epsilon,p,k}>0

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\;\left(1-C\epsilon\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
\leq&\;C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.13)

Now, multiplying (
2.13
) by
\frac{1+2C\epsilon}{1-C\epsilon}
, adding it with (
2.12
), we get

\displaystyle C\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy+\left(p\theta-\frac{1+2C\epsilon}{1-C\epsilon}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.

As
\theta>p^{-1}>1.
Fix
0<\epsilon<\frac{p\theta-1}{C(p\theta+2)}
, there holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy\\
\leq&\;C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\left(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\right)\zeta^{4k-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}dxdy.\end{split}

(2.14)

Apply Young’s inequality, we deduce then

\displaystyle\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{p+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{p\beta}{2}}dxdy+\epsilon^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\theta+1}\zeta^{\frac{4kp(\theta+1)}{p+1}-2(\theta+1)}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;C_{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(|\Delta_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}\zeta|^{\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}+|\nabla_{s}^{2}\zeta|^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\Big)^{\frac{p\beta}{2}}dxdy+\epsilon^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{\theta+1}\zeta^{4k}dxdy.

Choose
k\geq\frac{\beta(p+1)}{4}=\frac{\alpha(\theta+1)}{4}
so that
4k\leq\frac{4kp(\theta+1)}{p+1}-2(\theta+1),
for the last line. Take
\epsilon^{\prime}
small enough, the estimate (
2.3
) is proved. ∎

We are now in position to conclude. Choose
\chi
a cut-off function in
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}},[o,1]\right),
such that

\chi=1\quad\mbox{on}\quad B_{1}\times B_{2},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi=0\quad\mbox{outside }\quad B_{2}\times B_{2^{1+s}}.

For
R>0,
put
\eta_{R}(x,y)=\chi(\frac{x}{R},\frac{y}{R^{1+s}}),
it is easy to verify that there exists
C>0
independent of
R
such that

|\nabla_{x}\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{C}{R}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{C}{R^{1+s}},

|\nabla^{2}_{x}\eta_{R}|+|\Delta_{x}\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\nabla^{2}_{y}\eta_{R}|+|\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2(1+s)}}.

Applying (
2.3
) with
\zeta=\eta_{R}(x,y),
we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\times B_{R^{1+s}}}u^{\theta+1}dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{\theta+1}\eta_{R}^{4k}dxdy\leq CR^{\frac{2N_{s}}{p\beta}-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}}.

Since under our assumptions
N_{s}<\frac{2(p+1)(\theta+1)}{p\theta-1}:=2+\alpha+\beta,
the desired claim follows by letting
R\to\infty.
∎

2.2
The case
\theta>p\geq 1
.

Let us recall a consequence of Theorem 1.1 (with
\alpha=0
there) in
[
16
]
.

Theorem B

Let
x_{0}
be the largest root of the polynomial

\displaystyle H(x)=x^{4}-p\theta\alpha\beta\left[4x^{2}-2(\alpha+\beta)x+1\right].

(2.15)

1.

If
\frac{4}{3}<p\leq\theta
then (
1.1
) has no stable solution if
N_{s}<2+2x_{0}.

2.

If
1<p\leq\min(\frac{4}{3},\theta)
, then (
1.1
) has no bounded stable solution, if

N_{s}<2+2x_{0}\left[\frac{p}{2}+\frac{(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)}\right].

For
\theta>p\geq 1,
we can proceed
similarly as the proof of Proposition in
[
15
]
, we sketch a proof here for the reader’s convenience.
Performing the change of variables
x=\frac{\beta}{2}z
in (
2.15
), a direct computation shows that
H(x)=\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^{4}L(z)
where

L(z):=z^{4}-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)}{\theta+1}z^{2}+\frac{16p\theta(p+1)(p+\theta+2)}{(\theta+1)^{2}}z-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{2}}{(\theta+1)^{2}}.

Denote by
z_{0}
the largest root of
L,
hence
x_{0}=\frac{\beta}{2}z_{0}
and
H(x)<0
if and only if
L(z)<0
.
For
\theta>p\geq 1
, there holds

\displaystyle L(p+1)

\displaystyle=(p+1)^{4}-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{3}}{(\theta+1)}+\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{2}(p+\theta+2)}{(\theta+1)^{2}}-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{2}}{(\theta+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(p+1)^{4}-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{3}}{(\theta+1)}+\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{2}}{(\theta+1)}+\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{3}}{(\theta+1)^{2}}-\frac{16p\theta(p+1)^{2}}{(\theta+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=(p+1)^{2}\left[(p+1)^{2}-\frac{16p^{2}\theta}{(\theta+1)}+\frac{16p^{2}\theta}{(\theta+1)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p+1}{\theta+1}\right)^{2}\left[(p+1)^{2}(\theta+1)^{2}-16p^{2}\theta^{2}\right]<0.

The last inequality holds true since

4p\theta-(p+1)(\theta+1)>4p^{2}-(p+1)^{2}\geq 0,\quad\forall\;\theta>p\geq 1.

As
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}L(z)=\infty,
it follows that
z_{0}>p+1.
We get then

\displaystyle 2x_{0}>(p+1)\beta=2+\alpha+\beta,\quad\forall\;\theta>p\geq 1.

If
p>\frac{4}{3}
, by
(i)
of Theorem
B
, the system (
1.1
) has no classical stable solution if
N_{s}<2+\alpha+\beta
. Suppose now
1\leq p\leq\min(\frac{4}{3},\theta)
.
Observe that for all
\theta\geq p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left[p+\frac{2(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)}\right]\beta\geq\alpha+\beta

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left[p+\frac{2(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)}\right](\theta+1)\geq p+\theta+2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p\theta-1+\frac{2(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{\theta+p-2}\geq\theta+1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(p\theta-1)\left[1+\frac{2(2-p)}{\theta+p-2}\right]\geq\theta+1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(p\theta-1)(\theta+2-p)\geq(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p\theta^{2}-\theta+(2-p)p\theta\geq\theta^{2}+(p-1)\theta

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(p-1)(\theta-p)\geq 0.

As
z_{0}>p+1\geq 2,
we have
x_{0}=\frac{\beta z_{0}}{2}\geq\beta
and

2+\alpha+\beta\leq 2+\beta\left[p+\frac{2(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)}\right]\leq 2+x_{0}\left[p+\frac{2(2-p)(p\theta-1)}{(\theta+p-2)(\theta+1)}\right].

If
N_{s}<2+\alpha+\beta,
using
(ii)
of Theorem
B
, we are done.

To conclude

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all}\quad\theta>p\geq 1,\mbox{ and}\quad N_{s}<2+\alpha+\beta,\quad\eqref{1.1}\,\mbox{has no smooth stable solution}.

The proof is finished. ∎

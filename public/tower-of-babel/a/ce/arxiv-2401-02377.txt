The “exponential” torsion of superelliptic Jacobians
Jędrzej Garnek

Abstract
. Let J J be the Jacobian of a superelliptic curve defined by the equation y ℓ = f ⁡ ( x ) y^{\ell}=f(x) , where f f is a separable polynomial of degree non-divisible by ℓ \ell . In this article we study the “exponential” (i.e. ℓ \ell -power) torsion of J J . In particular, under some mild conditions on the polynomial f f , we determine the image of the associated ℓ \ell -adic representation up to the determinant. We show also that the image of the determinant is contained in an explicit ℤ ℓ \mathbb{Z}_{\ell} -lattice with a finite index. As an application, we prove the Mumford–Tate conjecture for a generic superelliptic Jacobian of the above type.

1.
Introduction

A subject of a special study among abelian varieties are Jacobians of projective curves.
Often, when studying the Jacobians, one restricts further to some class of
curves with particularly nice properties, for example to superelliptic ones.
Recall that a
superelliptic curve
over a field
K
is a smooth projective curve
C
with the affine part given by the equation of the form

(1.1)

C:y^{m}=f(x),

where
f\in K[x]
is separable of degree
r
and
m
is a natural number (the
exponent
of
C
).
Similarly one says that
J
, the Jacobian of
C
, is superelliptic of exponent
m
.
If
m=2
, we call
C
and
J
hyperelliptic.
There is a vast literature concerning various properties of hyper- and superelliptic Jacobians, for instance their endomorphism rings (cf.
[
58
]
,

[
56
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
),
their Hodge groups (cf.
[
49
]
,
[

]
,
[
48
]
,

[

]
), their rational points (cf.
[
38
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
), their Galois representations (see e.g.
[

]
,

[
15
]
,
[
1
]
for hyperelliptic curves and
[

]
,
[

]
,
[
8
]
for superelliptic curves) and the isogenies between them (cf.
[
54
]
).
Usually when considering the torsion or Galois representations of superelliptic Jacobians one excludes the primes dividing the exponent of
C
.
In this article we would like to study this “exponential” torsion.

To formulate the problem precisely, let
g:=\dim J
. For any prime
\ell
write
\rho_{J,\ell}:\Gal_{K}\to\Gl_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
for the Galois representation on
T_{\ell}J:=\varprojlim J[\ell^{n}]
, the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
J
. We are interested in the following question.

Question 1.1
.

What can be said about the image of
\rho_{J,\ell}
for primes
\ell|m
?

Recall that a piece of
J[m]
can be described using the roots of
f
.
Namely, there exists a subgroup
\Delta\subset J[m]
such that
K(\Delta)
is the splitting field of
f
,
see
[
43
, Proposition 1]
. In the simplest case,
when
\GCD(m,r)=1
, one has:

(1.2)

\Delta=\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}((\alpha_{i},0)-\infty:i=1,\ldots,r)\subset\Pic^{0}(C)[m],

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
are the roots of
f
and
\infty
is unique point at infinity of
C
, see
[
43
, Proposition 1]
.
In case when
\ell=2
, one has
\Delta=J[2]
. In particular,
\Gal(K(J[2])/K)=\Gal(f)\leq S_{r}
. This observation allows one to answer Question
1.1
for a generic hyperelliptic curve.
For any ring epimorphism
R\to R/I

and any subgroup
G\leq\Gl_{n}(R)
, write

G^{\mathcal{S}}
for the preimage of a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\Gl_{n}(R/I)
in
G
. Note that
S_{r}
may be treated as a subgroup of
\GSp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
.
The above reasoning together with the existence of the Weil pairing easily implies that

(1.3)

\im\rho_{J,2}\subset\GSp_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{\Gal(f)}.

In case when
2|r
,
r\geq 6
and
\Gal(f)=S_{r}
, this was shown to be an equality, cf.
[
50
, Corollary 1.2]
.

The goal of this article is to generalize (
1.3
) to the case when
m=\ell
is an odd prime not dividing
r
. In the sequel, we consider only superelliptic Jacobians defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})
for simplicity, but similar methods should apply for any number field containing
\zeta_{\ell}
. In other words, we use the following setup.

Setup 1.2
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime,
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})
,
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{\ell}]
and
\lambda:=1-\zeta_{\ell}
. Denote by
J
the Jacobian of the superelliptic
curve
C
defined by (
1.1
), where
m=\ell
and
f\in K[x]
is monic and separable of degree
r\geq 4
,
\ell\nmid r
. Also, denote the roots of
f
by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
.

In this case, the set
\Delta
is a proper subset of
J[\ell]
. It seems that determining the whole
\ell
-torsion explicitly is an impossible task.
There are however several known results for particular curves, see e.g.
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
43
]
.
The “exponential” torsion was also studied in
[
32
]
, but in a different context.
A result of Arul from
[
3
]
allows in principle to explicitly determine the
\ell
-torsion, but in practice the provided formulas are not well-suited for computations.
However, we can utilize the fact that
J
has an additional structure of multiplication by
\mathcal{O}
. By comparing dimensions one checks that
\Delta=J[\lambda]
, see Section
2
.
Moreover,
T_{\ell}J=\varprojlim J[\lambda^{n}]
becomes a module over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:=\varprojlim\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\zeta_{\ell}]
. These observations will be crucial in the sequel.

In order to describe the image of
\rho_{J,\ell}
, we need some notation concerning matrix groups. For any ring
R
, subgroup
G\leq\Gl_{n}(R)
, and
X\subset R^{\times}
we use the following notation:

G_{\det\in X}:=\{A\in G:\det A\in X\}.

Also, we use the notation
G^{\mathcal{S}}_{\det\in X}
to denote the intersection
G^{\mathcal{S}}\cap G_{\det\in X}
.
Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
has a unique involution
z\mapsto\overline{z}
. Let
V
be a free
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-module
of finite rank and suppose that
\textbf{e}:V\times V\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is a non-degenerate Hermitian
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear form. Denote by
\GU(V,\textbf{e})
the associated general unitary group, i.e.

\GU(V,\textbf{e})=\{A\in\Gl(V):\textbf{e}(AP,AQ)=\mu\cdot\textbf{e}(P,Q)\textrm{ for some }\mu\in\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}\,\textrm{ and all }P,Q\in V\}.

Recall that up to an isomorphism, there exist two non-degenerate Hermitian forms on
V:=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d}
, namely

\textbf{e}^{+}
(given by the
d\times d
identity matrix
I_{d}
) and
\textbf{e}^{-}
(given by the matrix
\diag(1,\ldots,1,\alpha)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
is an arbitrary
non-square). We abbreviate
\GU_{d}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}):=\GU(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d},\textbf{e}^{+})
and
\GU_{d}^{-}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}):=\GU(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d},\textbf{e}^{-})
.
In fact, if
2|d
,
\GU_{d}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})\cong\GU_{d}^{-}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
.
See Section
2
for the details. Finally, denote by
\mathcal{D}_{J}

the image of the map

\Omega_{J,\lambda}:=\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\circ\rho_{J,\ell}:\Gal_{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times},

where
\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}:\Gl_{r-1}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}
denotes the
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear determinant.

Theorem 1.3
.

Keep Setup
1.2
. Then:

(1.4)

\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\subset\GU_{r-1}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\det\in\mathcal{D}_{J}}^{\Gal(f)},

where
\Gal(f)\subset S_{r}
is considered as a subgroup of
\GU_{r-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and

\varepsilon
is the sign of the Legendre symbol
\left({\frac{r}{\ell}}\right)
. If moreover the following conditions hold:

(1)

\Gal(f)
is a
2
-transitive subgroup of
S_{r}
,

(2)

there exists a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\ell
, such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\disc(f))=1
and that the coefficients of
f
are
\mathfrak{p}
-integral,

then the inclusion (
1.4
) is an equality.

Note that both conditions (1) and (2) hold for a generic polynomial
f\in K[x]
. Indeed,
the Galois group of a generic polynomial of degree
r
is just the symmetric group
S_{r}
.
Moreover,
[
41
, Corollary 28]
proves that (2) holds for most polynomials
in the case when
K=\mathbb{Q}
(the proof for number fields is similar). The idea behind the proof of Theorem
1.3
is to use that the reduction of
\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})

modulo
\lambda
equals
\Gal(f)
and to observe that under some additional conditions this is the only subgroup of
\GU_{r-1}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\det\in\mathcal{D}_{J}}^{\Gal(f)}
with this property. In the course of the proof we use also some additional information on
K(J[\lambda^{2}])
coming from the descent theory.

Theorem
1.3
brings the study of the image of
\rho_{J,\ell}
to studying
\mathcal{D}_{J}
.
One easily checks that
\mathcal{D}_{J}
is contained in

\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}:=\{d\in\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}:d\cdot\overline{d}\in(1+\ell\cdot(r-1)\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\}.

Up to torsion, one may treat
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-lattice of rank
\frac{\ell+1}{2}
, see Section
5
.
It is natural to ask, whether the index of
\mathcal{D}_{J}
in
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}
is finite. The following result
yields a positive answer.

Theorem 1.4
.

Keep the Setup
1.2
. Then
[\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}:\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}]=\ell^{\kappa}
for some integer

\kappa\leq\ord_{\ell}(h_{\ell}^{-}\cdot c_{\ell,r})-1,

where
h_{\ell}^{-}
is the relative class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})
,
r_{\ell}
is the multiplicative order of
r
modulo
\ell
and

(1.5)

c_{\ell,r}:=\begin{cases}(r^{r_{\ell}}-1)^{(\ell-1)/2r_{\ell}},&\textrm{ if }2\nmid r_{\ell},\\
(r^{r_{\ell}/2}+1)^{(\ell-1)/r_{\ell}},&\textrm{ if }2|r_{\ell}.\end{cases}

The strategy behind the proof of Theorem
1.4
is to relate
\Omega_{J,\lambda}
to
a certain Hecke character with a specific infinity type. This allows one to construct an explicit
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-lattice
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}
inside of
\mathcal{D}_{J}
and the question comes down to computing the determinant measuring the index of
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}
in
\mathcal{D}_{J}
. The matter of finding the torsion part of
\mathcal{D}_{J}
comes down to studying
K(J[\lambda^{2}])
. In particular, if
J
satisfies the assumptions (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.3
, then
\mu_{2\ell}\subset\mathcal{D}_{J}
(see Proposition
5.1
).

The behaviour of the image of the Galois representation associated with an abelian variety is in general predicted by the Mumford–Tate conjecture. To every abelian variety
A
over a number field
K
one can associate a linear group
\MT(A)

defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, using the Hodge structure of the abelian variety. The Mumford–Tate conjecture states that for any prime
\ell
,
the connected component of the Zariski closure of
\rho_{A,\ell}(\Gal_{K})
should be
\MT(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. This conjecture has been proven for many abelian varieties of types I, II and III in Albert classification (cf. e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
18
]
; see also
[
9
]
for a survey).
The superelliptic Jacobians are of type IV in Albert classification. For this type fewer results are known (see e.g.
[
12
]
or
[
6
]
for some partial results). Also, it is known that superelliptic Jacobians of exponent
3
,
whose defining polynomial has a large Galois group, satisfy the Mumford–Tate conjecture (cf.
[
52
, p. 103]
– note that for abelian varieties the Tate conjecture implies the Mumford–Tate conjecture).

Corollary 1.5
.

Keep the Setup
1.2
. If
J
satisfies the assumptions (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.3
, then the Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
J
.

We end the introduction with a numerical example.

Example 1.6
.

Consider the superelliptic curve

C:y^{11}=f=x^{8}+x+1

over the field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{11})
. Then
J
satisfies the assumptions (1) and (2) of Theorem
1.4
with
\mathfrak{p}:=14731\mathcal{O}
. In particular, the Mumford–Tate conjecture holds for
J
. Moreover,
\left({\frac{8}{11}}\right)=-1
and
\ord_{11}(h_{11}^{-}\cdot c_{11,8})=1
. Using Proposition
5.1
one checks also that
\mu_{22}\subset\mathcal{D}_{J}
.
Thus
\mathcal{D}_{J}=\mathcal{U}_{11,8}
and

\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})=\GU_{7}^{-}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})_{\det\in\mathcal{U}_{11,8}}^{S_{8}}.

In particular (see Example
5.6
):

\displaystyle[K(J[11]):K]=8!\cdot 11^{260}.

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to introduce the needed notation and to discuss some preliminaries on the superelliptic Jacobians.
In particular, we prove the inclusion (
1.4
). For any number field
L
we write
\mathcal{O}_{L}
for its ring
of integers and
\disc(L/\mathbb{Q})
for its discriminant over
\mathbb{Q}
. For any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
we denote:

•

the associated valuation by
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}
,

•

the completion of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
by
L_{\mathfrak{p}}
,

•

the valuation ring of
L_{\mathfrak{p}}
by
\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{p}}
,

•

an arbitrary uniformizer of
\mathfrak{p}
by
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Similarly, if
v
is a place of
L
, we write
L_{v}
for the corresponding completion.
If
v
is finite, we use the notation
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
for the corresponding
prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and put
\mathcal{O}_{L,v}:=\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{p}_{v}}
.
Also, let
\mathcal{O}_{L,v}:=L_{v}
for any infinite place
v
.
For any polynomial
f
we write
\disc(f)
for its discriminant.

From now on, we use the Setup
1.2
. In this case we abbreviate
\mathcal{O}_{K,v}
to
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, etc. Note that
\lambda
is a prime element of
\mathcal{O}
. We identify it with the prime ideal
\lambda\mathcal{O}
. Moreover,
\ell\mathcal{O}=\lambda^{\ell-1}\mathcal{O}
.
In the sequel, we use several times the congruence:

(2.1)

\overline{\lambda}\equiv-\lambda\pmod{\lambda^{2}},

which can be proven as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}

\displaystyle=1-\zeta_{\ell}^{\ell-1}=\frac{1-\zeta_{\ell}^{\ell-1}}{1-\zeta_{\ell}}\cdot\lambda

\displaystyle=(1+\zeta_{\ell}+\ldots+\zeta_{\ell}^{\ell-2})\cdot\lambda\equiv(\ell-1)\cdot\lambda\equiv-\lambda\pmod{\lambda^{2}}.

For a matrix
\textbf{A}\in\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
we often write

\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A}
to denote the determinant of
A
considered as an
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear map
and
\det_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\textbf{A}
when we consider
A
to be an element of
\Gl_{d\cdot(\ell-1)}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
.
Note that
\det_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\textbf{A}=N_{K_{\lambda}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A})
.

Recall that the map

[\zeta_{\ell}]:C\to C,\quad(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta_{\ell}\cdot y),

is an automorphism of order
\ell
. This leads to a map
\mathcal{O}\to\End(J)
, which turns out to be an embedding (see e.g.
[
56
, Section 1]
). In what follows, we use often the fact that the Rosati involution on
\mathcal{O}
is given by the complex conjugation. The dimension of
J
, denoted
g
, equals
\frac{1}{2}(r-1)\cdot(\ell-1)
(this follows e.g. from
[
37
, Proposition 3.7.3 (c)]
). Therefore, by
[
33
, Proposition 2.2.1]
,
for every
n\geq 1
:

J[\lambda^{n}]\cong(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})^{2\dim J/(\ell-1)}\cong(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})^{r-1}.

In particular,
T_{\ell}J\cong\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{r-1}
. Observe that since
[\zeta_{\ell}](\alpha_{i},0)=(\alpha_{i},0)
, the group
\Delta
defined by (
1.2
) is an
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-linear subspace of
J[\ell]
.
Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\Delta=r-1
,
since
\Delta
is spanned by the divisors
(\alpha_{i},0)-\infty
for
i=1,\ldots,r
with the only relation:

\sum_{i=1}^{r}((\alpha_{i},0)-\infty)=\divv(y)\sim 0\quad\textrm{ in }\Pic^{0}(C),

cf.
[
43
, Proposition 1]
. Hence
J[\lambda]=\Delta
. In particular,

\Gal(f)=\Gal(K(J[\lambda])/K)\leq\Gl_{r-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Note that this inclusion
is compatible with
\Gal(f)\leq S_{r}
and
S_{r}\leq\Gl_{r-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
where the second inclusion comes from the permutation action on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{r}/\diag(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

We study now the Weil pairing on
T_{\ell}J
. To this end we quote the general result on Weil pairings on abelian varieties
with multiplications by an order in some number field. We give also the proof based on
[
51
]
for a lack of better reference. For any Galois module
M
, write

M(1)
for its twist by the cyclotomic character.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a principally polarized abelian variety over a number field
L
.
Suppose that a number field
M
embedds into
\End(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathbb{O}:=M\cap\End(A)
.
Let also
\mathbb{O}^{*}
denote the different ideal of
\mathbb{O}
over
\mathbb{Z}
. For any natural number
n
there exists a unique skew-Hermitian (with respect to the Rosati involution),
\mathbb{O}
-linear on the second coordinate

\Gal_{L}
-equivariant and non-degenerate pairing

\mathbbm{e}_{n}:A[n]\times A[n]\to(\mathbb{O}^{*}/n)(1)

(where
\mathbb{O}^{*}/n
is considered with the trivial Galois action) such that:

•

\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbbm{e}_{n}
equals the standard Weil pairing:

e_{n}:A[n]\times A[n]\to(\mathbb{Z}/n)(1),

•

for any
n_{1},n_{2}
,
P\in A[n_{1}\cdot n_{2}]
and

Q\in A[n_{1}]
one has:

\mathbbm{e}_{n_{1}n_{2}}(P,Q)=\mathbbm{e}_{n_{1}}([n_{2}]P,Q).

Similarly, for any prime
\ell
there exists a unique skew-Hermitian (with respect to the Rosati involution),
(\mathbb{O}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
-linear on the second variable
and
\Gal_{L}
-equivariant non-degenerate pairing

\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{\infty}}:T_{\ell}A\times T_{\ell}A\to(\mathbb{O}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})(1)

(where
\mathbb{O}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
is considered with the trivial Galois action) such that
\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{\infty}}
equals the standard Weil pairing

e_{\ell^{\infty}}:T_{\ell}A\times T_{\ell}A\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
.

Proof.

In the proof we will often use the fact that the map

(2.2)

\mathbb{O}^{*}/n\to\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{O}/n,\mathbb{Z}/n),\qquad a\mapsto f_{a},

where
f_{a}(x):=\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(a\cdot x)
, is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.
Indeed, by definition
\mathbb{O}^{*}\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{Z})

(see e.g.
[
30
, Definition III.2.1]
).
Moreover, since
\mathbb{O}
and
\mathbb{Z}
are finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}
-modules,

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}/n\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{O}/n,\mathbb{Z}/n)
.

Denote the Rosati involution on
\mathbb{O}
by
z\mapsto\overline{z}
.
Fix
P,Q\in A[n]
and consider the map:

\mathbb{O}/n\to\mathbb{Z}/n,\qquad a\mapsto e_{n}(P,a\cdot Q).

Since (
2.2
) is an isomorphism, there exists a unique element
\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P,Q)\in\mathbb{O}^{*}/n
such that:

e_{n}(P,a\cdot Q)=\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P,Q)\cdot a)\qquad\textrm{ for any }a\in\mathbb{O}/n.

The map
(P,Q)\mapsto\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P,Q)
is
\mathbb{Z}
-bilinear and
\mathbb{O}
-linear in the second coordinate. Indeed, for example we have:

\displaystyle\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}((\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{1},Q)+\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{2},Q))\cdot a)

\displaystyle=\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{1},Q)\cdot a)+\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{2},Q)\cdot a)

\displaystyle=e_{n}(P_{1},a\cdot Q)+e_{n}(P_{2},a\cdot Q)=e_{n}(P_{1}+P_{2},a\cdot Q)

\displaystyle=\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{1}+P_{2},Q)\cdot a),

which implies that
\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{1}+P_{2},Q)=\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{1},Q)+\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P_{2},Q)
.
We prove now that
\mathbbm{e}_{n}
is skew-Hermitian. Indeed, this follows from the fact that
a
and
\overline{a}

are dual maps with respect to
e_{n}
:

\displaystyle\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P,Q)\cdot a)

\displaystyle=e_{n}(P,a\cdot Q)=-e_{n}(a\cdot Q,P)=-e_{n}(Q,\overline{a}\cdot P)

\displaystyle=-\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbbm{e}_{n}(Q,P)\cdot\overline{a})=-\tr_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{\mathbbm{e}_{n}(Q,P)}\cdot a).

But since (
2.2
) is an isomorphism, we obtain that
\mathbbm{e}_{n}(P,Q)=-\overline{\mathbbm{e}_{n}(Q,P)}
. Similarly one shows the remaining properties of
\mathbbm{e}_{n}
.
The pairing
\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{\infty}}
is constructed as the inverse limit of
pairings
\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{n}}
.
∎

We switch now back to the notation of the article.

Corollary 2.2
.

Keep Setup
1.2
. There exists an
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear Hermitian non-degenerate pairing of
\Gal_{K}
-modules:

\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}:T_{\ell}J\times T_{\ell}J\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}(1).

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
for
L=M=K
, we obtain the
\mathcal{O}
-linear Weil pairing

\mathbbm{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}:T_{\ell}J\times T_{\ell}J\to(\mathcal{O}^{*}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})(1).

Recall that
\mathcal{O}^{*}=\lambda^{-(\ell-2)}\cdot\mathcal{O}
(see e.g.
[
30
, exercise III.§2.5]
). Define:

\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}:=(\lambda^{\ell-2}-\overline{\lambda}^{\ell-2})\cdot\mathbbm{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}.

Then
\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}(P,Q)\in\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
for any
P,Q\in T_{\ell}J
, since
\ord_{\lambda}(\lambda^{\ell-2}-\overline{\lambda}^{\ell-2})=\ell-2
by (
2.1
).
Moreover,
since
\mathbbm{e}_{\ell}
is skew-Hermitian and
\lambda^{\ell-2}-\overline{\lambda}^{\ell-2}

is an imaginary number,
\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}
is a Hermitian pairing.
∎

We discuss now briefly the theory of Hermitian forms over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
. Suppose that
V
is a
free
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-module of rank
d
and that

\textbf{e}:V\times V\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}

is a non-degenerate
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear Hermitian form. In this case we define
\GU(V,\textbf{e})
as in Section
1
. Note that the multiplier
\mu
yields
a well-defined homomorphism:

\mu:\GU(V,\textbf{e})\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}.

The kernel of
\mu
is denoted by
\U(V,\textbf{e})
and the kernel of
\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}:\U(V,\textbf{e})\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}
by
\SU(V,\textbf{e})
.
Note that if
\Gamma
is the matrix of
\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}
, then
for any
\textbf{A}\in\GU(V,\textbf{e})
by taking the
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-linear determinant of the equation

\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\cdot\Gamma\cdot\textbf{A}=\mu(A)\cdot\Gamma,

(where
\textbf{A}^{\dagger}
denotes the conjugate transpose of a matrix
A
) we obtain:

(2.3)

\overline{\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A}}\cdot\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A}=\mu(\textbf{A})^{r-1}.

Also, we write
\GU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})
for the group of automorphisms
of the free
\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n}
-module
V/\lambda^{n}
preserving up to a scalar the form
\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{n}}
, the reduction of

modulo
\lambda^{n}
.
In an analogous way one defines

\U(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})
and
\SU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})
.

It turns out that if
\textbf{e}_{\lambda}
is non-degenerate, it uniquely determines
e
, cf.
[
14
, Theorem 2]
.
The form
\textbf{e}_{\lambda}
is then a symmetric form (since the involution
z\mapsto\overline{z}
is trivial on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
).
Recall that the determinant of a pairing is defined as the determinant of its matrix in any basis.
It is a well-defined invertible element up to squares in the given ring.
In case of finite fields, the determinant is the complete invariant for non-degenerate symmetric forms of the same dimension (cf.
[
34
, §1.7, Proposition 5]
).
Thus there exist exist two non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{d}
, namely

\textbf{e}^{+}_{\lambda}
(given by the identity matrix
I_{d}
) and
\textbf{e}^{-}_{\lambda}
(given by the matrix
\diag(1,\ldots,1,\alpha)
, where
\alpha\in(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})^{\times}
is an arbitrary
non-square residue). By the above discussion, there exist also two non-degenerate bilinear Hermitian forms on
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d}
,
which we denote by
\textbf{e}^{+}
and
\textbf{e}^{-}
. We abbreviate
\GU^{+}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})=\GU(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d},\textbf{e}^{+})
,
\GU^{-}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})=\GU(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{d},\textbf{e}^{-})
, etc.
In case when
2\nmid d
, the symmetric form given by the matrix
\alpha\cdot I_{d}
has non-square determinant, thus it is equivalent
to
\textbf{e}^{-}
and
\GU^{+}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})\cong\GU^{-}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\subset\GU_{r-1}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
, where
\varepsilon
is the sign of the Legendre symbol
\left({\frac{r}{\ell}}\right)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\subset\GU(T_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}})
. Thus
by the previous discussion, the proof is immediate if
2|r
. Therefore, from now on we suppose that
r
is odd.
We start by proving that if
\Gal(f)
is a
2
-transitive subgroup of
S_{r}
, then
\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}
is equivalent to
\textbf{e}^{\varepsilon}
. Let
P_{i}:=(\alpha_{i},0)-\infty\in J[\lambda]
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.
Denote
c:=\textbf{e}_{\lambda}(P_{1},P_{2})
. By the assumption for any
i\neq j
there exists
\sigma\in\Gal(f)
such that
\sigma(P_{1})=P_{i}
,
\sigma(P_{2})=P_{j}
.
Thus, since
\textbf{e}_{\lambda}
is Galois equivariant and
\zeta_{\ell}\in K
:

\textbf{e}_{\lambda}(P_{i},P_{j})=\textbf{e}_{\lambda}(\sigma(P_{1}),\sigma(P_{2}))=\textbf{e}_{\lambda}(P_{1},P_{2})=c.

Moreover, for any
1\leq i\leq r
, since
P_{1}+\ldots+P_{r}=0
:

\displaystyle\textbf{e}_{\lambda}(P_{i},P_{i})=\textbf{e}_{\lambda}\left(P_{i},-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{r}P_{j}\right)=-(r-1)\cdot c.

Therefore, in the basis
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r-1}
the matrix of
\textbf{e}_{\lambda}
is given by
c\cdot(E-r\cdot I_{r-1})
,
where the matrix
E\in M_{r-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is entirely composed of ones. The matrix
E
is of rank
1
and its eigenvalues are
r-1,0,0,\ldots,0
.
Thus the eigenvalues of
c\cdot(E-r\cdot I_{r-1})
are
-c,-c\cdot r,\ldots,-c\cdot r
and
\det(c\cdot(\mathcal{J}-r\cdot I_{r-1}))=c^{(r-1)^{2}}\cdot r^{r-2}
. In particular, the determinant of
\textbf{e}_{\lambda}
is the class of
r
in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
. This ends the proof in this case.

In general, let
\mathcal{K}:=K(a_{0},\ldots,a_{r})
be the function field in
r+1
variables.
Write
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}:=\mathbb{Q}[a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}]
. Let
\textbf{f}_{\textbf{a}}(x):=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\ldots+a_{r}x^{r}\in\mathcal{K}[x]
.
Consider the family of curves
\textbf{C}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}})
given by
y^{\ell}=\textbf{f}(x)
.
Let
\mathcal{J}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}})
be the relative Jacobian. Write
J_{\eta}
for the generic fiber of this family.
Note that
\Gal(\textbf{f})\cong S_{r}
(see e.g.
[
27
, Example VI.§2.4, p. 272]
).
Therefore, by the above discussion,
\rho_{J_{\eta},\lambda}(G_{\mathcal{K}})\subset\GU_{r-1}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
. On the other hand,
since
\mathcal{J}
specializes to

, we have:

\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\subset\rho_{J_{\eta},\lambda}(\Gal_{\mathcal{K}})

(see e.g.
[
11
, Lemma 6.1.6 (b)]
). This ends the proof.
∎

3.
A lifting result

When computing images of abelian varieties, one often encounters
lifting problems of the following form.

Question 3.1
.

Let
R
be a topological ring with a quotient
R/\mathcal{I}
. Assume that

\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{H}
are closed subgroups of
\Gl_{d}(R)
such that

\mathcal{H}\leq\mathcal{G}
and that the images of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
in
\Gl_{d}(R/\mathcal{I})

are equal. Does it follow that
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{H}
?

The answer is positive under some additional assumptions if for instance
R
is the ring of Witt vectors over a finite field,
\mathcal{I}=\ell R
and
\mathcal{G}
is an algebraic group (see
[
42
]
and
[
44
]
)
or if
R=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
,
\mathcal{I}=\ell R
and
\mathcal{G}
is an intersection of an algebraic group and preimage of a subgroup of
\Gl_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
under the reduction map (cf.
[
24
]
and
[
50
]
).
In our case, we would like to take
R:=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{G}:=\SU_{d}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})^{\Gal(f)}
. Thus there arise the following two difficulties:

•

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is a ramified extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
,

•

\mathcal{G}
is an intersection of a preimage of a subgroup of
\Gl_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

with a group
\SU_{d}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
, which is not algebraic over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
.

However, it turns out that in this context the answer to Question
3.1

is still positive if we take
\mathcal{I}:=\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
. The following is
the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
\textbf{e}:V\times V\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is a Hermitian
form on a free
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
-module
V
of rank
d\geq 3
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subgroup of
\SU(V/\lambda,\textbf{e}_{\lambda})
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}\leq\SU(V,\textbf{e})^{\mathcal{S}}
is a closed subgroup such that its reduction modulo
\lambda^{2}
is
\SU(V/\lambda^{2},\textbf{e})^{\mathcal{S}}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}=\SU(V,\textbf{e})^{\mathcal{S}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Observe that Proposition
3.2
is not true with
\lambda^{2}
replaced by
\lambda
. Indeed, take for example
\mathcal{H}:=\SO_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
(the special orthogonal group) and
\mathcal{G}:=\SU_{d}^{+}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
. Then both
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
surject onto

\SU_{d}^{+}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, but clearly
\mathcal{H}\neq\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that even though
\SU(V,\textbf{e})
is not an algebraic group over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
,
it can be treated as an algebraic group over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{+}:=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1}]
via a “Weil restriction”.
In other words, it is easy to check that the group

\{(X,Y)\in M_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{+})\times M_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{+}):X+\zeta_{\ell}Y\in\SU_{d}(V,\textbf{e})\}

is algebraic.

The rest of this section may be skipped at first reading.

\displaystyle=I_{d}+\lambda^{n-2}\cdot(2A_{N}B_{N}+A_{N}^{2}+B_{N}^{2}-(A_{N}+B_{N})^{2})

\displaystyle+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot((A_{N}B_{N+1}+A_{N+1}B_{N})+(A_{N}A_{N+1}+A_{N+1}A_{N}+B_{N}B_{N+1}+B_{N+1}B_{N}

\displaystyle+A_{N}B_{N+1}+A_{N+1}B_{N})-(A_{N}+B_{N})(A_{N+1}+B_{N+1})-(A_{N+1}+B_{N+1})(A_{N}+B_{N}))

\displaystyle=I_{d}+\lambda^{n-2}\cdot[A_{N},B_{N}]+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot([A_{N},B_{N+1}]+[A_{N+1},B_{N}]).

This ends the proof.

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
,

, the subgroup
\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{j}

\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{i}

If
\textbf{A}\in\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{i}
,
\textbf{B}\in\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{j}
,

Recall that if
G\leq\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
is an algebraic group with a Lie algebra
\Lie(G)\leq M_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
, then there is a natural isomorphism

\Lie(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\to G(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{n-1},\qquad A\mapsto I_{d}+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot A.

We want now to prove a similar isomorphism for
\SU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})_{n-1}
. It turns
out however that since
\SU
is not an algebraic group over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, the result will actually depend on the parity of

Denote for any
n\geq 1
and any ring

\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(R):=\{A\in M_{d}(R):\Gamma A=(-1)^{n}\cdot A^{T}\Gamma,\,\,\tr(A)=0\}.

The following lemma shows that the spaces
\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
play the role of the Lie algebra
\Lie(G)
for the unitary group.

Lemma 3.7
.

The map:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\to\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{n-1}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto I_{d}+\lambda^{n-1}A

is an isomorphism onto
\SU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\textbf{A}:=I_{d}+\lambda^{n-1}A_{n-1}\in\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})_{n-1}
.
The congruence (
2.1
) implies that in
\Gl_{d}(\mathcal{O}/\lambda^{n})
:

\displaystyle\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\Gamma\textbf{A}

\displaystyle=(I_{d}+\overline{\lambda}^{n-1}A_{n-1}^{T})\Gamma(I_{d}+\lambda^{n-1}A_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\Gamma+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot((-1)^{n-1}A_{n-1}^{T}\Gamma+\Gamma A_{n-1}).

This shows that
\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\Gamma\textbf{A}=\Gamma
if and only if
\Gamma A_{n-1}=(-1)^{n}A_{n-1}^{T}\Gamma
. Moreover,
the equality (
3.2
) implies that
\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A}=1
if and only if
\tr(A_{n-1})=0
.
∎

For any
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
n\geq 1
define the following matrices:

E_{ij}^{(n)}:=E_{ij}+(-1)^{n}\cdot E_{ji},

where
E_{ij}
is the matrix with one on the position
(i,j)
and zeros on other positions.
In the sequel, we use the following equalities for any
1\leq i,j,l\leq d
,
j\neq i,l
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle[\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}^{(m)},\Gamma^{-1}E_{jl}^{(n)}]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\alpha_{j}^{-1}\cdot\Gamma^{-1}E^{(m+n+1)}_{il},&i\neq l,\\
(1+(-1)^{n+m+1})\cdot\alpha_{i}^{-1}\cdot\alpha_{j}^{-1}\cdot(E_{ii}-E_{jj}),&i=l.\end{cases}

In fact, they follow from the equalities
[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{li}E_{kj}

and
\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}=\alpha_{i}^{-1}E_{ij}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

(1)

If
2\nmid n
, then the matrices
\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}^{(n)}
,
1\leq i<j\leq d
are the basis of the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector space
\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. In particular

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})={d\choose 2}
.

(2)

If
2|n
, then the matrices
\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}^{(n)}
,
1\leq i<j\leq d
and the matrices
E_{ii}-E_{dd}
,
1\leq i<d
, are the basis of the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector space
\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. In particular

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})={d+1\choose 2}-1
.

Proof.

(1)

This follows easily from the fact that the map
A\mapsto\Gamma^{-1}A
is an isomorphism between the space of antisymmetric matrices in
M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

and
\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

(2)

As in (1), note that we have an isomorphism
A\mapsto\Gamma^{-1}A
between the space of symmetric matrices in
M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

and
\{A:\Gamma A=A^{T}\Gamma\}
. Thus the basis of the latter space is given by the matrices
\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}^{(n)}
for

.
By adding the condition on the vanishing of the trace we easily see that the basis of this space is

\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij}-\frac{\tr(\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij})}{\tr(\Gamma^{-1}E_{dd})}\cdot\Gamma^{-1}E_{dd}=\begin{cases}\Gamma^{-1}E_{ij},&i\neq j\\
\alpha_{i}^{-1}\cdot(E_{ii}-E_{dd}),&i=j,\end{cases}

for
1\leq i\leq j<d
,
(i,j)\neq(d,d)
.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For every
X\in M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that
X^{T}=(-1)^{n}\cdot X
there exists
Y\in M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

such that
X=\Gamma Y+(-1)^{n-1}\cdot Y^{T}\Gamma
. Moreover:

•

if
2|n
, then the matrix
Y
can be chosen to have an arbitrary trace
c\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
,

•

if
2\nmid n
, then
\tr(Y)=\frac{1}{2}\tr(\Gamma^{-1}X)
.

Proof.

Fix
n
and consider the map

\displaystyle\varphi:M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\to M_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\times\mathbb{F}_{\ell},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\mapsto(\Gamma Y+(-1)^{n-1}Y^{T}\Gamma,\tr(Y)).

Note that its kernel is
\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and that
\im\varphi\subset\mathcal{Z}
, where

(3.4)

\mathcal{Z}:=\begin{cases}\{A:A^{T}=-A\}\times\mathbb{F}_{\ell},&\textrm{ if }2|n,\\
\{(A,\frac{1}{2}\tr(\Gamma^{-1}A)):A^{T}=A\},&\textrm{ if }2\nmid n.\end{cases}

One easily computes that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathcal{Z}=\begin{cases}{d\choose 2}+1,&2|n,\\
{d+1\choose 2},&2\nmid n.\end{cases}

On the other hand, one checks that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\textrm{im}\varphi=d^{2}-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathfrak{su}^{(n)}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\mathcal{Z}

by using Lemma
3.8
. Thus
\im\varphi=\mathcal{Z}
, which ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

. Then the natural map
\SU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})_{1}\to\SU(V/\lambda^{n-1},\textbf{e})_{1}

Proof.

Let
\textbf{A}\in\SU(V/\lambda^{n-1},\textbf{e})_{1}

and

for some

. By (
3.2
) it is easy to check that

\tr\Gamma^{-1}X=c+(-1)^{n-1}\cdot c
.
Note that
(\textbf{A}^{\prime})^{\dagger}\Gamma\textbf{A}^{\prime}
is Hermitian, so that by (
2.1
) we obtain:

(\Gamma+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot X)^{\dagger}=\Gamma+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot X\Rightarrow X^{T}=(-1)^{n-1}\cdot X.

By Corollary
3.9

\Gamma Y+(-1)^{n-1}\cdot Y^{T}\Gamma

Moreover, if

\displaystyle(\textbf{A}^{\prime\prime})^{\dagger}\cdot\textbf{A}^{\prime\prime}=\Gamma+\lambda^{n-1}\cdot X-\lambda^{n-1}\cdot(Y\Gamma+(-1)^{n-1}\cdot Y^{T}\Gamma)=\Gamma.

\displaystyle\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\textbf{A}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\textbf{A}^{\prime}-\lambda^{n-1}\cdot Y)=\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\textbf{A}^{\prime})\cdot\det{}_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(I_{d}-\lambda^{n-1}\cdot(\textbf{A}^{\prime})^{-1}Y)

\SU(V/\lambda^{n},\textbf{e})

∎

Proof of Theorem

In order to apply Proposition
3.2
, we need additional information on the
\lambda^{2}
-torsion field
of
J
. By descent theory, see e.g.
[
2
, Corollary 3.0.8.]
:

K(J[\lambda^{2}])=K(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r},\sqrt[\ell]{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}}:1\leq i<j\leq r).

Thus
[K(J[\lambda^{2}]):K(J[\lambda])]\leq\ell^{{r\choose 2}}
. The following lemma shows that under some mild assumptions
the equality is achieved.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in L[x]
be a monic separable polynomial of degree
r
over a number field
L
. Let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
be the roots of
f
and let
M
be its splitting field. Suppose that:

(1)

\Gal(f)
acts
2
-transitively on
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
,

(2)

that there exists
a prime
\mathfrak{p}\nmid 2\disc(L/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\disc(f))=1
and that the coefficients of
f
are
\mathfrak{p}
-integral.

Then the elements
\{\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq r\}
are independent in the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector space
L^{\times}/(L^{\times})^{\ell}
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}
any prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
.
Since
\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\disc(f))=1
and
\mathfrak{p}\nmid 2\disc(L/\mathbb{Q})
,
the polynomial
f
has a single double root modulo
\mathfrak{p}

(see e.g.
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
for the case
L=\mathbb{Q}
; the general case can be proven in a similar manner). Thus
\mathcal{P}|\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}
for precisely
one pair
1\leq i<j\leq r
, without loss of generality
\mathcal{P}|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
.
We claim that
e(\mathcal{P}/\mathfrak{p})
(the ramification index of
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathfrak{p}
) equals
2
.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{P}_{1}:=\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{O}_{L(\alpha_{1})}
and let
f=(x-\alpha_{1})\cdot f_{1}
for some
f_{1}\in L(\alpha_{1})[x]
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{1}\nmid\disc(f_{1})=\prod_{1<i<j\leq r}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{2}
. Therefore
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is unramified in
M
, the splitting field of
f_{1}
, and
e(\mathcal{P}/\mathfrak{p})=e(\mathcal{P}_{1}/\mathfrak{p})
. Moreover, by
[

, Theorem 3.41 and Remark 3.43]
we have

, which proves the claim. Thus:

\ord_{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}\ord_{\mathcal{P}}(\disc(f))=\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\disc(f))=1,

i.e.
\ord_{\mathcal{P}}(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})=1
.
Since
\Gal(f)
is a
2
-transitive subgroup of
S_{r}
,
for any

we may choose
\sigma_{ij}\in\Gal(f)
such that

\sigma_{ij}(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{i}
,
\sigma_{ij}(\alpha_{2})=\alpha_{j}
.
Define
\mathcal{P}_{ij}:=\sigma_{ij}(\mathcal{P})
. Then:

\ord_{\mathcal{P}_{ij}}(\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{l})=\begin{cases}1,&(k,l)=(i,j),\\
0,&(k,l)\neq(i,j).\end{cases}

Suppose now that for some

we have:

\prod_{i<j}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{a_{ij}}=\gamma^{\ell}\qquad\textrm{ for some }\gamma\in M^{\times}.

Then, by computing the valuation of both sides with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{ij}
, we obtain

,
i.e.
\ell|a_{ij}
. This ends the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the composition
S_{r}\to\Gl_{r-1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\det}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
is the signature homomorphism. Thus the reduction of
\mathcal{D}_{J}

modulo
\lambda
contains
\mu_{2}
if and only if
\Gal(f)\leq S_{r}
is not a subgroup of
A_{r}

(the alternating group of degree
r
).

To say more about
\mathcal{D}_{J}
, we need the theory of Hecke characters. Let
L
be an arbitrary number field. Consider the the group of ideles of
L
, i.e.

\mathbb{I}_{L}:=\{(a_{v})_{v}\in\prod_{v}L_{v}^{\times}:a_{v}\in\mathcal{O}_{L,v}^{\times}\textrm{ for almost all }v\}

where the product is taken over all (finite and infinite) places of
L
. Recall that the class field
theory yields an epimorphism
\mathbb{I}_{L}/L^{\times}\to\Gal_{L}^{ab}
. A continuous homomorphism

\chi:\mathbb{I}_{L}/L^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is called a
Hecke character
.
For any place
v
, denote by
\iota_{v}:L_{v}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{I}_{L}
the natural embedding.
For every finite
v
there exists a number
m_{v}\geq 0
such that
\chi(\iota_{v}(1+\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{m_{v}}))=\{1\}

(where we write
1+\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{0}:=\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
). If
m_{v}
can be taken to be
0
, we say that
\chi
is
unramified
at
v
. In particular, for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{L,v}^{\times}
,
\chi(\iota_{v}(a))
is a root of unity, since
a^{n}\in 1+\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{m_{v}}

for
n
large enough. Let
L_{\infty}^{\times}:=\prod_{v|\infty}L_{v}^{\times}
and write
L_{\infty}^{\times,\circ}
for the connected component
of
L_{\infty}^{\times}
(note that if
L_{\infty}^{\times}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r_{1}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{\times})^{r_{2}}
, then
L_{\infty}^{\times,\circ}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r_{1}}\times(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{r_{2}}
).
We say that a character
L_{\infty}^{\times,\circ}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is
algebraic
, if it comes from a homomorphism
T:L^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,

T=\prod_{\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}}\sigma^{n_{\sigma}}
for some integers
n_{\sigma}
. We say that a Hecke character

\chi
is
algebraic
, if
\chi|_{L_{\infty}^{\times,\circ}}
is algebraic. In this case, we say that the corresponding homomorphism
T
is the
infinity type
of
\chi
. If
\chi
is
algebraic, its image must lie in a number field
M
. Fix a rational prime
p
and
let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime of
M
over
p
. The homomorphism
T
extends uniquely
to a homomorphism
T_{\mathfrak{q}}:\prod_{v|p}L_{v}^{\times}\to M_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
.
This allows one to define a character
\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}:\mathbb{I}_{L}\to M_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}

(the
\mathfrak{q}
-adic avatar of
\chi
) by the formula:

\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}(a):=\chi(a)\cdot T(a_{\infty})^{-1}\cdot T_{\mathfrak{q}}(a_{p}),

where
a_{p}
denotes the components of
a
lying in
\prod_{v|p}L_{v}^{\times}
. One shows that
\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}
descends to a Galois character
\Gal_{L}\to M_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\times}
, which we also denote by
\chi_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

We return now to Setup
1.2
. Consider the

endomorphism character of
J
, i.e. the Galois character
\Omega_{J,\lambda}:\Gal_{K}\to K_{\lambda}^{\times}
,
\Omega_{J,\lambda}:=\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}\circ\rho_{J,\ell}
.
The endomorphism character has been studied mostly in case when the considered abelian variety has complex multiplication
– for example the article
[
13
]
relates the endomorphism character of the Jacobian of the Fermat curve to
Jacobi sums. The article
[
20
]
studies the reduction of the endomorphism character of a superelliptic Jacobian
modulo a prime different then
\lambda
.
As explained in
[
20
]
, the endomorphism character comes from an algebraic Hecke character
\Omega_{J}:\mathbb{I}_{K}/K^{\times}\to K^{\times}
.
Moreover, the infinity type of
\Omega_{J}
can be computed knowing the action of
\mathbb{Z}/\ell
on
H^{0}(C,\Omega_{C/\mathbb{C}})
, see
[
16
, Proposition 14]
. An explicit formula for this action in case of superelliptic
curves in given e.g. in
[
53
, Remark 3.7]
. Thus the
infinity type of
\Omega_{J}
is given by the formula:

T_{\ell,r}:=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma_{j}^{n(j)},

where
\sigma_{j}\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
is defined by
\sigma_{j}(\zeta_{\ell})=\zeta_{\ell}^{j}
and
n(j):=\lfloor\frac{r\cdot(\ell-j)}{\ell}\rfloor
(cf.
[
20
, §5.3]
). Define
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}:=T_{\ell,r}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times})\subset\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}
.
We show below (Proposition
5.4
) that
[\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}:\mathcal{D}_{J}]
is finite if and only if
[\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}:\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}]
is finite.

Example 5.3
.

Let
C
be the curve
y^{3}=x^{2}-D
, where
D\in\mathbb{Z}
,
D\neq 0
.
Then
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
. By
[
35
, Example II.10.6]
,

\Omega_{J}
is unramified for primes
\mathfrak{p}\nmid 6D
and for any such prime
\mathfrak{p}

with uniformizer
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, we have:

\Omega_{J}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})=-\overline{{\left(\frac{4D}{\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)_{6}}}\cdot\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Note that these conditions determine
\Omega_{J}
uniquely. Moreover,
the above formula shows that the infinity type of
\Omega_{J}
is
T=\id
, which is consistent with the above formula.

Proposition 5.4
.

We have
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}\subset\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}
. Moreover:

[\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}:\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}]\leq\ell^{\ord_{\ell}(h_{\ell})},

where
h_{\ell}
is the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell})
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}\subset\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}
.
Indeed, let
a\in\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}
. Recall that
\Omega_{J}(\iota_{\lambda}(a))
is a root of unity
in
K
. Therefore
\Omega_{J}(\iota_{\lambda}(a))\in\mu_{2\ell}
and:

\displaystyle T_{\ell,r}(a)=\Omega_{J}(\iota_{\lambda}(a))^{-1}\cdot\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{\lambda}(a))\in\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}.

This shows the desired inclusion. We prove now the bound on the index of
\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}
in
\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}
. Observe that the group
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\mu_{\ell-1}
is a pro-
\ell
group and that
\mu_{\ell-1}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}=\mu_{2}
. Therefore
\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}/\mu_{2}

is also a pro-
\ell
group and it is enough to show that
[\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{D}_{J}:\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}]
divides
h_{\ell}
. Clearly,
\Omega_{J,\lambda}(K_{v}^{\times})=\{1\}
for any infinite place
v
. For any finite place
v
and
a\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
we have either

\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(a))=\Omega_{J}(\iota_{v}(a))\in\mu_{2\ell}
if
v\neq\lambda
or
\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(a))=\Omega_{J}(\iota_{v}(a))\cdot T_{\ell,r}(a)\in\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}
if
v=\lambda
.
Moreover, since
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
for
v\neq\lambda
,
we have

\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{\lambda}(\lambda))=\prod_{v\neq\lambda}\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(\lambda))^{-1}\in\mu_{2\ell}.

Finally, if
v\neq\lambda
is a finite place, then
\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{h_{\ell}}=\alpha\mathcal{O}
is a principal ideal. Thus
\pi_{v}^{h_{\ell}}=u_{1}\cdot\alpha
for some
u_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Observe also that
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{v^{\prime}}^{\times}
for
v^{\prime}\neq v
.
Hence:

\displaystyle\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\pi_{v})^{h_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(u_{1}))\cdot\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(\alpha))

\displaystyle=\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(u_{1}))\cdot\prod_{v^{\prime}\neq v}\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v^{\prime}}(\alpha))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v}(u_{1}))\cdot\prod_{v^{\prime}\neq v,\lambda}\Omega_{J,\lambda}(\iota_{v^{\prime}}(\alpha))^{-1}\cdot\Omega_{J}(\iota_{\lambda}(\alpha))^{-1}\right)\cdot T_{\ell,r}(\alpha)^{-1}\in\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}.

This ends the proof.
∎

Note that

\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}=\mu_{2\ell}\times(1+\ell\cdot(r-1)\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\times\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}:=\{d\in 1+\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:d\cdot\overline{d}=1\}
.
Moreover,
T_{\ell,r}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma_{j}^{(r-1)/2}\cdot T_{\ell,r}^{\prime}
, where

T_{\ell,r}^{\prime}:=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell-1}\sigma_{j}^{n^{\prime}(j)}
and

(5.1)

n^{\prime}:(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad n^{\prime}(j):=\left\lfloor\frac{(\ell-j)r}{\ell}\right\rfloor-\frac{r-1}{2}

(here we identify
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}
with
\{1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
).
For a future use observe that

(5.2)

n^{\prime}(j):=\frac{r-1}{2}-\left\lfloor\frac{jr}{\ell}\right\rfloor=-n^{\prime}(\ell-j).

Defining
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\ell,r}:=T_{\ell,r}^{\prime}(1+\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
,
we obtain the following decomposition:

\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}=T_{\ell,r}(\mu_{2\ell})\times(1+\ell\cdot(r-1)\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\times\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\ell,r}.

Thus we are left with investigating
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\ell,r}
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}\cong\mu_{\ell(\ell-1)}\times(1+\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})
.
Moreover, the
\ell
-adic logarithm
\log:1+\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\to\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is an isomorphism of topological
groups, with the inverse given by the exponential function (see e.g.
[
36
, Example IV.3.1.2, Theorem IV.6.4 (b)]
).
Hence we may identify
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}
with the set
\{d\in\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:d+\overline{d}=0\}

and
T_{\ell,r}^{\prime}
with the map
\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\to\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
given by the formula
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell-1}n^{\prime}(j)\cdot\sigma_{j}
. Note that
\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module of rank
\ell-1
. One easily proves that
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}\subset\lambda^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}

is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module of rank
\frac{\ell-1}{2}
with the basis given by

\lambda^{i}-\overline{\lambda}^{i}\quad\textrm{ for }i=2,\ldots,\frac{l+1}{2}.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By the above discussion,
[\mathcal{U}_{\ell,r}:\mu_{2\ell}\mathcal{T}_{\ell,r}]=[\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\ell,r}]
.
Consider the map

T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime}:=T_{\ell,r}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}}:\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}.

By the Smith normal theorem,
|\coker T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime}|=\ell^{t}
, where
t:=\ord_{\ell}(\det T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime})
.
Let
C\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}
be an arbitrary set of representatives for
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}/\{1,\ell-1\}
.
We will compute the matrix of
T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
in the basis
\{\zeta^{i}_{\ell}-\zeta^{-i}_{\ell}:i\in C\}
. For any
i\in C
we have:

\displaystyle T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta^{i}_{\ell}-\zeta^{-i}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in C}n^{\prime}(j)\cdot(\zeta_{\ell}^{ij}-\zeta^{-ij}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in C}2n^{\prime}(j)\cdot(\zeta_{\ell}^{ij}-\zeta_{\ell}^{-ij})=\sum_{k\in C}2n^{\prime}(i^{-1}k)\cdot(\zeta_{\ell}^{k}-\zeta_{\ell}^{-k}).

(we used (
5.2
) and that
\ell-i\not\in C
for
i\in C
).
Hence, the matrix of
T_{\ell,r}^{\prime\prime}
is given by
2\cdot[n^{\prime}(i^{-1}j)]_{i,j\in C}
.
The proof follows from Lemma
5.5
below.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime and
r
a natural number non-divisible by
\ell
. Let
C\subset(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}
be an arbitrary
set of representatives of
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}/\{1,\ell-1\}
. Define
n^{\prime}:(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}
by the formula (
5.1
)
and
c_{\ell,r}
by (
1.5
).
Then:

\det[n^{\prime}(i\cdot j^{-1})]_{i,j\in C}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\cdot h_{\ell}^{-}}{2\ell}\cdot c_{\ell,r}.

Proof.

This is Hirabayashi’s formula for the determinant of the Demjanenko matrix from
[
21
]
.
We will use this formula in the form
[
25
, formula (13)]
. In the notation of ibid. we take
(K,m,b):=(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}),\ell,r)
.
Note that then
K^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})
,
w_{K}=2\ell
,
Q_{K}=2
. Hence:

\det[n^{\prime}(i\cdot j^{-1})]_{i,j\in C}=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\cdot h_{\ell}^{-}}{2\ell}\cdot c_{K}(r)

and we are left with proving that
c_{K}(r)=c_{\ell,r}
. Denote
R:=r_{\ell}
and
L:=\frac{\ell-1}{R}
.
Then
r\equiv g^{L}\pmod{\ell}
for a generator
g
of
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{\times}
. Moreover, the set
of odd Dirichlet characters for
K
is given by
X_{-}=\{\chi_{a}:a=1,\ldots,\ell-1,\,2\nmid a\}
, where

\chi_{a}(g^{i}):=\zeta_{\ell-1}^{a\cdot i}
. Therefore for any
\chi\in X_{-}
we have
f_{\chi}=\ell
and:

\displaystyle c_{K}(r)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\chi\in X_{-}}c_{K}^{\chi}(r)=\prod_{\chi\in X_{-}}(r-\chi(r))

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
2\nmid a\end{subarray}}^{\ell-1}(r-\zeta_{\ell-1}^{L\cdot a})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
2\nmid a\end{subarray}}^{\ell-1}(r-\zeta_{R}^{a}).

Thus, if
2|R
, then:

\displaystyle c_{K}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\
2\nmid a\end{subarray}}^{R}(r-\zeta_{R}^{a})\right)^{L}=\left(\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\end{subarray}}^{R}(r-\zeta_{R}^{a})}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\end{subarray}}^{R/2}(r-\zeta_{R}^{2b})}\right)^{L}=\left(\frac{r^{R}-1}{r^{R/2}-1}\right)^{L}=(r^{R/2}+1)^{L}.

If
2\nmid R
, then
2|L
and:

\displaystyle c_{K}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\end{subarray}}^{R}(r-\zeta_{R}^{a})\right)^{L/2}=(r^{R}-1)^{L/2}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Note that the Demjanenko matrix has been used previously in a related context
in order to study the Sato–Tate group of the Jacobians of Fermat curves,
see
[
17
]
.

6.
Mumford–Tate conjecture for superelliptic Jacobians

In the last section we prove Corollary
1.5
, i.e. the Mumford–Tate conjecture
for the Jacobians satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
.
Before the proof we recall some key notions. See e.g.
[
9
]
for a reference. The
Mumford–Tate group
of
J
, denoted
\MT(J)
, is the largest algebraic subgroup of
\GSp(H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q}),\phi_{J})
fixing all the Hodge vectors. The
Hodge group
of
J
is
\Hg(J):=(\MT(J)\cap\Sl(H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})))^{\circ}
. Let
\phi_{J}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear symplectic pairing
H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})\times H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}
defined
by the principal polarisation. Then
\Hg(J)\subset\Sp(H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q}),\phi_{J})
and

\MT(J)=\mathbb{G}_{m}\cdot\Hg(J)
. Write
V_{\ell}J:=T_{\ell}J\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
and define the
\ell
-adic Galois monodromy group
of
J
as

H_{\ell}:=\left(\overline{\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})}^{Zar}\cap\Sl(V_{\ell}J)\right)^{\circ}

(from now on,
\Sl(V_{\ell}J)
denotes the group of matrices
\textbf{A}\in\Gl(V_{\ell}J)
with
\det_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\textbf{A}=1
).
The Betti-étale comparison isomorphism
H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\cong V_{\ell}J
leads to an inclusion
H_{\ell}\subset\Hg(J)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. The Mumford–Tate conjecture is equivalent
to the equality of these groups. Moreover, the pairing
\phi_{J}
can be upgraded to an
\mathcal{O}
-linear skew-Hermitian pairing
\Phi_{J}:H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})\times H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q})\to\mathcal{O}

similarly as
e_{\ell^{\infty}}
in Lemma
2.1
(cf.
[
49
]
). Under the comparison isomorphism
the pairing
\phi_{J}
corresponds to the pairing
\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{\infty}}
defined in Lemma
2.1
.
Thus:

\Hg(J)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\subset\U(H_{1}(J,\mathbb{Q}),\Phi_{J})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\cong\U(V_{\ell}J,\mathbbm{e}_{\ell^{\infty}})=\U(V_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\ell^{\infty}})

(in fact, the article
[
46
]
shows that these inclusion is an equality if
\Gal(f)\cong S_{r}
or
A_{r}
).
Note that the unitary groups in question can be considered as algebraic groups over
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
by
taking the Weil restriction from
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{\ell}+\zeta_{\ell}^{-1})
(cf. Remark
3.4
) to
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
.

On the other hand, note that

\det_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}(\U^{\varepsilon}_{r-1}(\mathcal{O})\cap\Sl(T_{\ell}J))=\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}
. Hence by Theorem
1.3
and Remark
5.2
:

\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\cap\Sl(T_{\ell}J)=U(T_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}})^{\Gal(f)\cap A_{r}}_{\det\in\mathcal{D}_{J}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}}.

By Theorem
1.4
, the index of
\mathcal{D}_{J}\cap\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{U}_{\ell}^{\prime}
is finite.
Thus
\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\cap\Sl(T_{\ell}J)
has a finite index in
\U(T_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}})
.
This easily implies that in
\Gl(V_{\ell}J)
:

\overline{\rho_{J,\ell}(\Gal_{K})\cap\Sl(T_{\ell}J)}^{Zar,\circ}=\overline{\U(T_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}})}^{Zar,\circ}=\U(V_{\ell}J,\textbf{e}_{\lambda^{\infty}}),

which ends the proof.

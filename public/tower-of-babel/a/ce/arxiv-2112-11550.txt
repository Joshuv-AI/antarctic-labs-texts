Homogenization of a non-linear strongly coupled model of magnetorheological fluids Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Thuyen Dang, Yuliya Gorb, Silvia Jiménez Bolaños

Abstract
This paper concerns the rigorous periodic homogenization for a non-linear strongly coupled system, which models a suspension of magnetizable rigid particles in a non-conducting carrier viscous Newtonian fluid. The fluid drags the particles, thus alters the magnetic field. Vice versa, the magnetic field acts on the particles, which in turn affect the fluid via the no-slip boundary condition. As the size of the particles approaches zero, it is shown that the suspension’s behavior is governed by a generalized magnetohydrodynamic system, where the fluid is modeled by a stationary Navier-Stokes system, while the magnetic field is modeled by Maxwell equations. A corrector result from the theory of two-scale convergence allows us to obtain the limit of the product of several weakly convergent sequences, where the div-curl lemma, which is a typical tool in these types of problems, is not applicable.



keywords

Homogenization, two-scale convergence, Navier-Stokes equation, strong coupling, magnetic particles.

2.3

Denote by

We

\displaystyle\rho_{f}\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\right]-\Div\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\rho_{f}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{f},

(1a)

\displaystyle\Div\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{f}

(1b)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s},

(1c)

\displaystyle\Curl\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{f},

(1d)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{\nu_{es}}\Curl\Curl\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s},

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle=\mu\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega.

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{\mathrm{mag}}=\Curl\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{f},\\
\Curl\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}\times\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon},&\text{ in }\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},p^{\varepsilon})\coloneqq 2\eta{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})-p^{\varepsilon}I

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\coloneqq\frac{\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}+\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\right)^{\top}}{2}

Since

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{f}},&\text{ if }x\in\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{f},\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{s}},&\text{ if }x\in\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}.\end{cases}

2.5.1
Abstract framework for our non-linear problem

The results for linear saddle point problems date back to the seminal
works by I. Babuška and F. Brezzi,

[

]
. They
are then adapted to the non-linear cases such as the Navier-Stokes
equations and magnetorhydrodynamic equations,
c.f.
[
30
,
54
,
36
,
29
,
35
]
.
We summarize here the results used in our paper and refer the readers
to the works cited above for their proofs.

Let
X
and
P
be two real Hilbert spaces, and
f\in X
.
Let
a(\,\cdot\,;\cdot,\cdot)\colon X\times X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a
non-linear form such that for any
w\in X
,
a(w;\cdot,\cdot)
is a
bilinear continuous form on
X\times X
. Let
b\colon X\times P\to{\mathbb{R}}
be
a continuous bilinear form.
Consider the following
non-linear problem:

Find
(u,p)\in X\times P
, such that for
all
(v,q)\in X\times P
,

\displaystyle a(u;u,v)+b(v,p)

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,v\right\rangle,

(12a)

\displaystyle b(u,q)

\displaystyle=0,

(12b)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the dual pairing.
The unknown
p
can be regarded as the Lagrange multiplier associated
with the constraint (
12b
). The idea is to embed the constraint
(
12b
) into

by introducing the space

\displaystyle M=\left\{u\in X\colon b(u,q)=0\text{ for all }q\in P\right\},

and consider a simpler problem that reads:

such that for

\displaystyle a(u;u,v)=\left\langle f,v\right\rangle.

(13)

The continuity of

implies that

is a closed linear subspace of

is also a Hilbert space.

Theorem 1
(Existence and uniqueness of solution of

If the following conditions hold:

(i)

such that for all

\displaystyle a(v;v,v)\geq\alpha\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{X};

(14)

(ii)

that

(

. If in addition, we assume that:

(iii)

the elliptic property (i) holds uniformly with respect to
the first variable, i.e. there exists
\alpha>0

\displaystyle a(w;v,v)\geq\alpha\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{2}_{X},

(15)

(iv)

there exists a constant

\displaystyle\left\lvert a(u_{2};v,w)-a(u_{1};v,w)\right\rvert\leq\gamma\left\lVert u_{2}-u_{1}\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{X},

then problem (

, provided

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{M}}{\alpha^{2}}<1,

where

, with

.

theorem

1

Definition 2

The following is called the inf-sup condition or the

\displaystyle\exists\,\beta>0\quad\text{ such that }\quad\inf_{q\in P\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\sup_{v\in X\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{b(v,q)}{\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{X}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{P}}\geq\beta.

The inf-sup condition can be verified by

Proposition 3
.

Let

the continuous bilinear form

(ii)

(v,B^{\top}q)_{X}=(Bv,q)_{P}

(iii)

is surjective.

2.5.2
The two-scale convergence method

Two-scale convergence was invented by G. Nguetseng and
further developed by G. Allaire. We collect here the important notions
and results relevant to this paper, whose proofs can be found in
[
11
,
57
,
38
,
39
]
. The following spaces are used in the paper below.

•

C_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)
– the subspace of
C({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of
Y
-periodic functions;

•

C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)
– the subspace of
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of
Y
-periodic functions;

•

H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)
– the closure of
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)
in the
H^{1}
-norm;

•

\mathcal{D}(\Omega,X)
– where
X
is a Banach space – the space infinitely differentiable functions from
\Omega
to
X
, whose support is a compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
contained in
\Omega
.

•

L^{p}(\Omega,X)
– where
X
is a Banach space and
1\leq p\leq\infty
– the space of measurable functions
w\colon x\in\Omega\mapsto w(x)\in X
such that

\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega,X)}\coloneqq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left\lVert w(x)\right\rVert^{p}_{X}\di x\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

•

L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}\left(Y,C(\bar{\Omega})\right)
– the space of measurable functions
w\colon y\in Y\mapsto w(\cdot,y)\in C(\bar{\Omega})
, such that
w
is periodic with respect to
y
and

\int_{Y}\left(\sup_{x\in\bar{\Omega}}\left\lvert w(x,y)\right\rvert\right)^{p}\di y<\infty.

Definition 4
(
L^{p}-
admissible test function).

Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. A function
\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega\times Y)
,
Y
-periodic in the second component, is called an
L^{p}-
admissible test function if for
all
\varepsilon>0
,

\psi\left(\cdot,\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)
is measurable and

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\psi\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x=\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}\left\lvert\psi(x,y)\right\rvert^{p}\di y\di x.

(19)

It is known that functions belonging to the spaces
\mathcal{D}\left(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(Y)\right)
,
C\left(\bar{\Omega},C_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)\right)
,
L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}\left(Y,C(\bar{\Omega})\right)
or
L^{p}\left(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)\right)
are admissible
[
3
]
, but the precise
characterization of those admissible test functions is still an
open question.

Definition 5
.

A sequence
\{v^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is said to
two-scale converge
to
v=v(x,y)
, with
v\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)
, and we write
v^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup{~2~}v
, if and only if:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(x)\psi\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\di x=\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}v(x,y)\psi(x,y)\di y\di x,

(20)

for any test function
\psi=\psi(x,y)
with
\psi\in\mathcal{D}\left(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(Y)\right)
.

In (
20
), we can choose
\psi
be any (
L^{2}-
)admissible test
function. Any bounded sequence
v^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
has
a subsequence that two-scale converges to a limit
v^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)
. Moreover, from
[
3
, Theorem 1.8, Remark 1.10 and
Corollary 5.4]
, we have

Theorem 6
(Corrector result).

Let
u^{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
that
two-scale converges to a limit
u^{0}(x,y)\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)
. Assume that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left\lVert u^{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)}.

(21)

Then for any sequence
v^{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
that
two-scale converges to
v^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y),
one has

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup[\hphantom{~2~}]{}\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{Y}u^{0}(x,y)v^{0}(x,y)\di x\di y\text{ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega).

(22)

Furthermore, if
u^{0}(x,y)
belongs to
L^{2}\left(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}(Y)\right)
or
L^{2}_{\mathrm{per}}\left(Y,C(\Omega)\right)
,
then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\lVert u^{\varepsilon}(x)-u^{0}\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

(23)

In fact, the smoothness assumption on
u^{0}
in (

) is needed
only for

is to be measurable and to belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Finally, we recall that if

is a Carathéodory
function then
\psi\left(\cdot,\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)

is measurable. This fact is used later on to prove that

is an admissible test function.

\displaystyle{}+{}\frac{{A}_{{l}}}{{R}_{{m}}}\left[\int_{\Omega}\Div\mathbf{B}\cdot\Div\mathbf{C}\di x+\int_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}_{s}}\Curl\mathbf{B}\cdot\Curl\mathbf{C}\di x\right],

Lemma 17

The space

Let the space

Since

4
Conclusions

The results obtained in Section
7
demonstrate the
effective
response of a viscous fluid with a locally periodic array of magnetic particles suspended in it. The original fine-scale problem is described by the system of equations (
8
)-(
11
), and the effective equations are given by (
83
), in Section
3.6
, with the effective coefficients defined by (
84
). As evident from the effective system obtained, these effective quantities depend on the instantaneous position of the particles, their geometry, and the magnetic and flow properties of the original suspension decoded in the cell problems (
67
) and (
70
)-(
71
). The effective medium is an
incompressible electromagnetic fluid
described by the coupled set of Navier-Stokes and Maxwell’s equations. The effective Cauchy stress of the fluid is
\displaystyle 2\mathcal{N}_{ijmn}\left[{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{0})\right]_{ij}\mathrm{e}^{m}\otimes\mathrm{e}^{n}-\Pi\,{\mathbf{I}}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is the effective viscosity, and the coupling between the homogenized fluid velocity
\mathbf{u}
and the homogenized magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
is given through the Lorentz force. The Maxwell’s equations are represented by the combination of Ampère’s law, Ohm’s law, and Faraday’s law, where the first two laws eliminate the electric field from the equation.

It is worth mentioning that this paper is not concerned with
modeling issues
for colloids with magnetizable particles, but rather focuses on the homogenization results.
This study is the promised follow-up of the work in
[
16
]
by the authors, where they considered a one-way coupling mechanism between the viscous fluid and the magnetic particles that are suspended in a viscous fluid and described by the linear relation between the magnetic flux density
\mathbf{B}
and the magnetic field strength
\mathbf{H}
. In contrast to
[
16
]
, this paper focuses on a
non-linear
model of the given magnetorheological fluid, where the two phases are interacting via the
full (two-way) coupling
mechanism.
And, as in
[
16
]
, the
rigorous justification
of the obtained effective system is derived. This is also differing from previous contributions on the topic
[
43
,
49
]
, that dealt only with formal asymptotics and did not consider the complicated non-linear model discussed in this paper.

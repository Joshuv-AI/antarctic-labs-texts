A closed-form expression for the kth power of semicirculant and r r -circulant matrices
M. Mouçouf

Abstract
. We derive a closed-form expression for the kth power of semicirculant matrices by using the determinant of certain matrices. As an application, a closed-form expression for the kth power of r r -circulant matrices is also povided.

1.
Introduction

The
n\times n

r
-circulant matrix
C_{n,r}
over a unitary commutative ring
R
is one having the
following form

(1.1)

C_{n,r}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{0}&c_{1}&c_{2}&\cdots&c_{n-2}&c_{n-1}\\
rc_{n-1}&c_{0}&c_{1}&\cdots&c_{n-3}&c_{n-2}\\
rc_{n-2}&rc_{n-1}&c_{0}&\cdots&c_{n-4}&c_{n-3}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
rc_{1}&rc_{2}&rc_{3}&\cdots&rc_{n-1}&c_{0}\end{array}\right),

where
r\in R
is a parameter. The
r
-circulant matrix
C_{n,r}
is determined by
r
and its first row elements
c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1}
, thus we denote
C_{n,r}=\text{circ}_{n,r}(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})
.

However, an infinite semicirculant matrix over
R
is one having the following form (see e.g., Henrici
[
3
]
or Davis
[
2
]
)

A=[a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]=\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}&a_{3}&\cdots\\
0&a_{0}&a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots\\
0&0&a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots\\
0&0&0&a_{0}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots\\
\end{pmatrix}.

In the work
[
1
]
, the general expression of the
k
th power
A^{k}=[a_{0}(k),a_{1}(k),a_{2}(k),\ldots]
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) of a semicirculant matrix

A=[a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
is presented. More precisely the sequence
\{a_{m}(k)\}_{m\geq 0}
is obtained using a recursive method. To do this, we have proved that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.2)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{i}(k)&=&L(A)(i,0)a_{0}^{k}\binom{k}{0}+\cdots+L(A)(i,j)a_{0}^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}+\cdots+L(A)(i,i)a_{0}^{k-i}\binom{k}{i},\end{array}

where we adopt the convention that for any element
a\in R
and any nonnegative integers
k\leq p
,
a^{k-p}\binom{k}{p}=\delta_{k,p}
.

The double sequence
\{L(A)(n,m)\}_{n,m}
of elements of
R
is defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle L(A)(n,m)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta(n,m)\end{subarray}}\binom{m}{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}a_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{k_{n}},

where
\Delta(n,m)
,
m\leqslant n
are integers, is the solution set of the following system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=m\\
k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n.\end{cases}

We note that Formula (
1.2
) expresses
a_{i}(k)
in closed-form that is not easy to use. Here we provide an easy closed-form formula in terms of determinants. As a consequence, in the last section we give a closed-form formula for the kth power of
r
-circulant matrices and we describe a method for finding the solution set of the well known Diophantine equation

\displaystyle k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n.

Throughout this paper
R
will denote an arbitrary commutative ring with identity.

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\in R
and let
\delta(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
be the following determinant

(2.5)

\delta(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccccc}a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&a_{n}\\
a_{0}&\ddots&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&a_{2}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&a_{0}&a_{1}\end{array}\right|.

Then we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\delta(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}a_{0}^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}a_{0}^{n-1})

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}(-a_{0})^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}(-a_{0})^{n-1})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N^{\ast}}
.

Proof.

The claim is trivially true for
a_{0}=0
. Suppose
a_{0}\neq 0
. For the proof of the first equality it suffices to multiply every column
C_{i}
of the determinant
\delta(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
by
a_{0}^{i-1}
, and then multiply every row
R_{i}
of the resulting determinant by

.

To obtain the last equality, it suffices to multiply each of the even rows of the determinant
\delta(1,\ldots,a_{i}a_{0}^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}a_{0}^{n-1})
by
-1
, and then multiply each of the even columns of the resulting determinant by
-1
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A=[a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots]
be a semicirculant matrix over
R
, and let
\{u_{n}(A)\}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of elements of
R
associated to

given in (

). Then we have

(2.8)

u_{n}(A)=\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

for all

.

Proof.

By (

) we know that
\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\delta(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}(-1)^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}(-1)^{n-1})
. Assume that

, and let us expand the determinant
\delta(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}(-1)^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}(-1)^{n-1})

along the first row. Then we get

\displaystyle\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\delta_{i},

where
\delta_{i}
is the cofactor associated with the entry

of the matrix
\delta(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}(-1)^{i-1},\ldots,a_{n}(-1)^{n-1})
.

It can be easily seen that the cofactor
\delta_{i}
has the form

\displaystyle\delta_{i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc;{6pt/6pt}ccclc}1&&\star&\lx@intercol\hfil\hbox{\multirowsetup\huge$\star$}\hfil\lx@intercol&\\
&\hskip 0.86108pt\raisebox{4.30554pt}{\rotatebox{-12.0}{$\ddots$}}&&&&&&\\

Hence

\displaystyle\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\delta(-1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-i-1}).

It follows that the sequence

for all

.
∎

Proposition 2.4

Let

and consider the semicirculant matrix

. Then

\displaystyle=

3.2.
The calculation of
\Delta(n)

Consider the solution set
\Delta(n,p)
of the following system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\\
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=p\\
k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n.\end{cases}

Here,
p
and
n
are positive integers such that
p\leqslant n
.

Let
\Delta(n)
be the solution set of the diophantine equation

\displaystyle k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n.

We have clearly

\displaystyle\Delta(n)=\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq n}\Delta(n,i).

Let
X,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
be independent indeterminates over
R
. From (
2.11
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{n}(X)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}L(A)(n,i)X^{n-i},

and from (
1.3
) we have

\displaystyle L(A)(n,i)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta(n,i)\end{subarray}}\binom{i}{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}},

where
A=[0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

We observe that we can determine the set
\Delta(n)
by computing the polynomial
\mathcal{X}_{n}(X)
, and then Comparing the polynomial
L(A)(n,i),1\leq i\leq n,
and the term of degree
n-i
of
\mathcal{X}_{n}(X)
.

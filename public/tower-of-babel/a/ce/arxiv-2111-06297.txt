Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu–Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova limit formulae for fractional Sobolev spaces via interpolation and extrapolation
Oscar Domínguez, Mario Milman

Abstract
. The real interpolation spaces between L p ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) and H ˙ t , p ​ ( ℝ n ) \dot{H}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) (resp. H t , p ​ ( ℝ n ) H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) ), t > 0 , t>0, are characterized in terms of fractional moduli of smoothness, and the underlying seminorms are shown to be “the correct” fractional generalization of the classical Gagliardo seminorms. This is confirmed by the fact that, using the new spaces combined with interpolation and extrapolation methods, we are able to extend the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu–Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova limit formulae, as well as the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu convergence theorem, to fractional Sobolev spaces. On the other hand, we disprove a conjecture of [ 5 ] suggesting fractional convergence results given in terms of classical Gagliardo seminorms. We also solve a problem proposed in [ 5 ] concerning sharp forms of the fractional Sobolev embedding.

\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big),\qquad r\in[0,\bar{r}].

With this result at their disposal the proof of Theorem
1.2

given in
[
5
]
now proceeds by combining Theorem
1.3

(taking limits as
r\rightarrow 1^{-})
, with (
1.12
) and the Littewood–Paley estimate

\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\qquad 1<p<\infty,

and then applying the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem.

In view of this, the following conjecture was formulated in
[
5
]
.

Open Problem 1.4

(
[
5
, Question 1.11]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
assume that

f_{k}\rightharpoonup f\quad\text{weakly in}\quad L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\text{as}\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

Let
t\in(0,1)
and
(s_{k})_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\subset(0,t)
such that

s_{k}\uparrow t
and assume that

\Lambda:=\sup_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\Big(\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(t-s_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{k}\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big)<\infty.

Then
f\in H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and there exists
C=C(p,n,t)>0
such
that

\lim_{\bar{t}\uparrow t}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\bar{t}}{2}}f_{k}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Lambda.

Note that in the case
t=1,
the conjectured result is exactly Theorem

1.2
. However, as we have indicated above, the standard Gagliardo
seminorms are “too far" from the “exact” interpolation seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\,
(cf. (
1.16
)) for
0<t<1
. We use this insight to give a counterexample to Open Problem
1.4
(cf.
Section
3
, Proposition
3.1
for the precise
statement, and Section
5
for a proof).

The problem thus remains: What spaces should we use to formulate and prove a
fractional extension of Theorem
1.2
? Once again
interpolation, via the Butzer seminorms, comes to our rescue and we are
essentially able to prove that the conjectured result in Open Problem
1.4
is true if in its
statement we replace “Gagliardo seminorms” by “Butzer seminorms” (cf. Section

3
, Theorem
3.2
for the precise statement, and
Section
5
for a complete proof).

1.4.
Sharp Sobolev inequalities

Another problem proposed in
[
5
]
deals with Sobolev type inequalities.
Let
0<s<t<1
, an elementary version of Sobolev’s inequality asserts that

(1.22)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\gamma_{s,t}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{t,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big).

Here
\gamma_{s,t}
is a positive constant which depends, in particular, on
the smoothness parameters
s
and
t
. Furthermore, at least formally,
(
1.22
) is still valid in the limiting values
s=0
and
t=1
.
According to (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), the validity of
the previous assertion should be reflected in the behavior of the equivalence
constant
\gamma_{s,t}
that appears in (
1.22
), in terms of
the blows up
\min\{t,1-t\}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\min\{s,1-s\}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Indeed, the following result was obtained in
[
5
, Corollary 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
0<s<t<1
and
f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then
there exists
C=C(n)>0
, such that

\min\{s,1-s\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\min\{t,1-t\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{t,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big).

It is well known that the
L^{p}
-counterpart of (
1.22
) is
also true, i.e.,

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C_{s,t}\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big)

provided that
0<s<t<1
and
1<p<\infty
. Accordingly, a similar result, in
the spirit of Theorem
1.5
, was expected. However, the set of
harmonic analysis techniques developed in
[
5
]
do not work outside the
Hilbert setting given by
p=2
. This leads to the following question, explicitly raised in

[
5
]
:

Open Problem 1.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty),0<\theta<s<t<1
and

f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Does there exist
C=C(n,p,\theta)>0
,
such that

(1.23)

\min\{s,1-s\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\min\{t,1-t\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big)?

We provide a positive answer to this problem. Indeed, in Section

3
, Theorem
3.9
, we state a somewhat stronger
result which is then proved in Section
5
.

We now turn to explain the organization of the paper. The brief Section

2
contains the basic background and notation concerning
the function spaces we shall consider; Section
3
contains the
statements of the main results, which we then prove in Section

5
. The interpolating Section
4
contains
detailed information with complete proofs concerning the interpolation and

Finally, in the Appendix, we collect further results and applications. In particular, we prove an extension of the classical fractional Sobolev inequalities of Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu
[

]
and Maz’ya–Shaposhnikova
[

]
, that were one of the original motivations for the limiting theorems (

) and (

).

2.
Background on function spaces

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
(fractional) Sobolev space

\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is equipped with the
Gagliardo seminorm

(2.1)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{sp+n}}\,dx\,dy\bigg)^{1/p}.

The
Riesz potential space

\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is endowed with

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

The spaces
\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
make sense for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular,
\dot{H}^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\,k\in{{\mathbb{N}}},
coincides with the
classical Sobolev space

\dot{W}_{p}^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and

(2.2)

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{W}_{p}^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\qquad p\in(1,\infty).

We let
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\cap\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(the
space of Bessel potentials
) and
\|f\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
.

The
(homogeneous) Triebel–Lizorkin space

\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\,p\in(0,\infty),\,q\in(0,\infty]
, is formed by all

f\in\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsq}|\Delta_{j}f|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty,

with the obvious modification if
q=\infty
. Here,
\{\Delta_{j}f:j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}\}
is the standard dyadic Littlewood–Paley decomposition
of
f
. The inhomogeneous counterparts,
F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
can
be introduced similarly.

Interchanging the roles of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\ell_{q}({{\mathbb{Z}}})
in the definition of
\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

we arrive at the Besov spaces. Specifically, the
(homogeneous) Besov space

\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\,s\in{\mathbb{R}},p,q\in(0,\infty]
, is
formed by all
f\in\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsq}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty,

with the obvious modifications if
q=\infty
.

We shall use the following standard notations. Given a quasi-Banach space
X

and
\lambda>0
, we will denote by
\lambda X
the space endowed with

\|f\|_{\lambda X}=\lambda\|f\|_{X},\qquad f\in X.

Let
Y
be a quasi-Banach space and let
\beta>0
. We will use the notation

\lambda X\hookrightarrow\beta Y
to indicate that there exists a positive
constant
C
, independent of
\lambda
and
\beta
, such that

\beta\|f\|_{Y}\leq C\lambda\|f\|_{X},\qquad f\in X.

In particular, if
\lambda=\beta=1
then we simply write
X\hookrightarrow Y
.
By
X=Y
we mean that
X\hookrightarrow Y
and
Y\hookrightarrow X
.

We collect some well-known relations between the function spaces described
above (cf.
[
2
]
,
[
27
]
and
[
32
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}},\,p\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
. Then

\dot{B}_{p,\min\{p,q\}}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{F}_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{p,\max\{p,q\}}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

In particular,

\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{B}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(ii)

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{B}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

(iii)

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

3.
Main results

In this section we provide a list of statements of the main results we have
obtained. The proofs will be given in Section
5
.

We start outlining some details of a counterexample that provides a negative
answer to Open Problem
1.4
. To avoid technicalities, we switch
temporarily from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
13
13

13

Spaces of periodic functions are defined similarly as their analogs on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, simply replacing
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
by
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
.
.
However, our construction can be easily modified to deal with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
p>\frac{2n}{n+1}
; in this connection see Remark

5.1
below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
t\in(0,1)
. Let
f
be
formally associated to a Fourier series as follows,

(3.1)

f(x)\sim\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{-t-1+\frac{1}{p}}\cos(\nu x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{T}}.

Then,
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
but
f\not\in H^{t,p}({\mathbb{T}})
.
Furthermore, given any sequence
(s_{k})_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\subset(0,t)

with
s_{k}\uparrow t
,

\sup_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\,(t-s_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{T}})}<\infty.

In fact, we are able to go far beyond, and we show that a correct
formulation of Open Problem
1.4
is obtained by means of replacing
the classical seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

by the Butzer seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}
(cf. (
1.16
) and (
1.19
)).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, assume that

f_{k}\rightharpoonup f\quad\text{weakly in}\quad L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\quad\text{as}\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

Let
t\in(0,1)
and
(s_{k})_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\subset(0,t)
be such that

s_{k}\uparrow t.
Assume that

\Lambda:=\sup_{k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}}\Big(\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(t-s_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f_{k}\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\Big)<\infty.

Then
f\in H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and there exists
C=C(n,p,t)>0
such
that

\lim_{\bar{t}\uparrow t}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\bar{t}}{2}}f_{k}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Lambda.

To prove Theorem
3.2
we need an extension of Theorem

1.3
formulated in terms of the Butzer seminorms

\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}
. It turns out that our
method is flexible enough to incorporate both, the case
s\rightarrow 0+
, as
well as to deal with inhomogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
0<s<t\leq 1,\,p\in(1,\infty)
and

\Lambda>1
.

(1)

Assume
t-s\leq\frac{t}{2\Lambda}
and let
t-\bar{r}=\Lambda(t-s)
. Then, there exists
C
, which depends only on
n,p,t
and
\Lambda
,
such that

(3.2)

\|f\|_{F_{p,2}^{\bar{r}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\Big).

There is a corresponding result for homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces,

(3.3)

\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\Big),\qquad r\in[0,\bar{r}].

(2)

Assume
s<\frac{1}{2\Lambda}
and let
\bar{r}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}s
.
Then there exists
C
, which depends only on
n,p,t
and
\Lambda
, such that

(3.4)

\|f\|_{F_{p,2}^{\bar{r}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+s^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\Big).

Likewise, for homogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin spaces we have

(3.5)

\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+s^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\Big),\qquad r\in[0,\bar{r}].

We apply our method to provide an extension of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) in terms
of the fractional Laplacian and Butzer seminorms.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
t\in(0,1]
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume
f\in H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then

(3.6)

\lim_{s\rightarrow t^{-}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\approx\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

and

(3.7)

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}s^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

The corresponding results for inhomogeneous spaces also hold true:

(3.8)

\lim_{s\rightarrow t^{-}}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\approx\|f\|_{H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

and

(3.9)

\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}s^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Remark 3.5
.

Observe that (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) with
t=1
give back, up to equivalence constants, the classical formulae (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

As indicated by Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
, the
classical seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
are not the
optimal choices for dealing with Sobolev-type inequalities involving

H^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. This phenomenon is illustrated in the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<r<t<1
. Then there exists

C=C(n,p,r,t)>0,
such that

(3.10)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C((s-r)(t-s))^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(s-r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\bigg),

for every
s\in(r,t)
. In the limiting cases,
r=0
and
t=1
, we have

(3.11)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\bigg)

for
0<t<1
and

(3.12)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C(s-r)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\bigg(\frac{1}{(s-r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\frac{1}{(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\bigg)

for
0<r<1
, respectively.

As an immediate consequence of (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we obtain

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
0<r<t<1
. Then

(3.13)

\sup_{s\in[r,t)}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big)

and

(3.14)

\sup_{s\in(r,t]}(s-r)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big).

Remark 3.8
.

Theorem
3.6
is a considerable improvement of Theorem 1.6 in
[
5
]
: Let
1<p<\infty
and
0\leq r<t\leq 1
. Then there exists
C=C(n,p)>0
such that

(3.15)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\,\bigg(\frac{1}{(s-r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\bigg),\qquad s\in(r,t).

The estimates provided in Theorem
3.6
sharpens (

) due to the appearance of one of the additional prefactors
((s-r)(t-s))^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}},(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}
and
(s-r)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}
. Note that the constant
C
in (

) does not depend on
r
and

, but this is not the case in (
3.10
). The reason behind this additional flexibility in (

) is explained by its non-optimality.

On the other hand, (

) sharpens the following inequality obtained in Corollary 1.7 in
[

]
: Let

and
0<r<t\leq 1
. Then there exists

such that

(3.16)

\sup_{s\in[r,t)}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big).

In particular, this inequality with
t=1
gives one of the estimates in the classical Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu formula (

). However, (

) is not optimal if

. In fact, as was already observed in

(3.17)

\sup_{s\in[r,t)}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\Big).

then the prefactor

on the

(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}
in (

). On the other hand, if

then
(
3.13
) coincides with the optimal estimate (
3.17
). On the other hand, inequality (
3.14
), with
p<2,
seems to be new.

We give a positive answer to Open Problem
1.6
. In fact, we
obtain a stronger version by means of removing the

-dependance from

and working with inhomogeneous norms on both sides of
(

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

and

.

4.1.
Revisiting some classical interpolation formulae

All the results contained in this subsection are well known. However, for the
purposes of this paper, it is important to establish these results with sharp
control on the dependance with respect to the parameters involved. For the
convenience of the reader, who may not be familiar with interpolation theory,
we give a self-contained exposition, since it is hard to find the material organized in a manner that fits our needs in this paper.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a quasi-semi-normed pair. The
real interpolation space

(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q},\,\theta\in(0,1),\,q\in(0,\infty]
, is formed by all
the elements
f\in A_{0}+A_{1},
such that

(4.1)

\|f\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{-\theta}K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty,

where
K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})
denotes the
K
-functional
for the pair

(A_{0},A_{1}),
defined for
t>0,
by

K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1}):=\inf_{f=f_{0}+f_{1}}\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{A_{1}}\Big).

We shall simply write
K(t,f)
when the underlying pair of spaces
(A_{0},A_{1})
is
understood from the context. We refer to the standard references on
interpolation theory
[
1
,
2
,
31
]
. Sometimes
it is more convenient to work with the modified
K
-functional given by

K_{p}(t,f;A_{0},A_{1}):=K_{p}(t,f)=\inf_{f=f_{0}+f_{1}}\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{A_{0}}^{p}+t^{p}\|f_{1}\|_{A_{1}}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
p\in(0,\infty)
. Clearly
K(t,f)\approx K_{p}(t,f)
with equivalence
constants depending only on
p
. Furthermore, it is plain that

(4.2)

(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}=(A_{1},A_{0})_{1-\theta,q}

with equality of norms.

Let
A
be a Banach space and
p\in(0,\infty)
. The vector-valued Lebesgue
spaces
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A)
are formed by all strongly measurable
functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow A
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A)}:=\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|f(x)\|_{A}^{p}\,dx\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty.

The next result is well known.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a pair of Banach
spaces and let
s\in(0,1),p\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{0}),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{1}))_{s,p}=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};(A_{0},A_{1})_{s,p})

and

\|f\|_{(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{0}),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{1}))_{s,p}}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};(A_{0},A_{1})_{s,p})}

where the hidden equivalence constants depend only on
p
.

Proof.

It is plain that

K(t,f;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{0}),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{1}))^{p}\approx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}K_{p}(t,f(x);A_{0},A_{1})^{p}\,dx.

Therefore using Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{0}),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};A_{1}))_{s,p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sp}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}K(t,f(x);A_{0},A_{1})^{p}\,dx\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\|f(x)\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{s,p}}^{p}\,dx.

∎

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
p\in(0,\infty)
, the space
\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})
is formed by all the scalar-valued sequences
\xi=(\xi_{j})_{j\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}},
such that

\|\xi\|_{\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}:=\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}<\infty.

Similarly, we can define the spaces
\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{N}}}_{0})
where

{{\mathbb{N}}}_{0}={{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{0\}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
-\infty<s_{0}<s_{1}<\infty,\,\theta\in(0,1),s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},
and let
p,q\in(0,\infty)
. Then

(\ell_{q}^{s_{0}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{q}^{s_{1}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\theta,p}=\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),

with

\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,q\}}}}+\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,q\}}}}\bigg)\|\xi\|_{\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}\lesssim\|\xi\|_{(\ell_{q}^{s_{0}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{q}^{s_{1}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\theta,p}}\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,q\}}}}+\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,q\}}}}\bigg)\|\xi\|_{\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}

uniformly w.r.t.
\theta
. The corresponding result also holds true for

{{\mathbb{N}}}_{0}
-indexed sequences.

Proof.

It is well known and easy to see that

K_{q}(t,\xi;\ell_{q}^{s_{0}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{q}^{s_{1}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))=\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}[\min\{2^{js_{0}},2^{js_{1}}t\}|\xi_{j}|]^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}.

By the monotonicity properties of the
K
-functional, the following estimates
hold uniformly w.r.t.
\theta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{(\ell_{q}^{s_{0}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{q}^{s_{1}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\theta,p}}

\displaystyle\approx\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l\theta(s_{1}-s_{0})p}K(2^{-l(s_{1}-s_{0})},\xi;\ell_{q}^{s_{0}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{q}^{s_{1}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\approx\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l\theta(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}[2^{js_{0}}\min\{1,2^{(j-l)(s_{1}-s_{0})}\}|\xi_{j}|]^{q}\bigg)^{p/q}\bigg)^{1/p}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\approx A+B,

where

A:=\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l(\theta-1)(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{l}2^{js_{1}q}|\xi_{j}|^{q}\bigg)^{p/q}\bigg)^{1/p}

and

B:=\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l\theta(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\bigg(\sum_{j=l}^{\infty}2^{js_{0}q}|\xi_{j}|^{q}\bigg)^{p/q}\bigg)^{1/p}.

We consider now two possible cases. Assume first
p\geq q.

Therefore

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l(\theta-1)(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{l}2^{js_{1}p}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{js_{1}p}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\sum_{l=j}^{\infty}2^{l(\theta-1)(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/p}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

By the sharp version of Hardy’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
28

),

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg(2^{l(\theta-1)(s_{1}-s_{0})q}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{l}2^{js_{1}q}|\xi_{j}|^{q}\bigg)^{p/q}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/q}}\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{lsp}|\xi_{l}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Hence the term
A
can be estimated by

(4.4)

\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/p}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim A\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/q}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Furthermore, similar estimates also hold for
B
, namely,

(4.5)

\frac{1}{\theta^{1/p}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim B\lesssim\frac{1}{\theta^{1/q}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Combining (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (

), we arrive at the desired

.

Suppose now
p<q
. Then

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{l(\theta-1)(s_{1}-s_{0})p}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{l}2^{js_{1}p}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/p}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

On the other hand, by a sharp version of Copson’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
) we find that

A\gtrsim\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/q}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Hence

\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/q}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim A\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/p}}\bigg(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{jsp}|\xi_{j}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

with hidden constants of equivalence uniform w.r.t.
\theta\in(0,1)
. The term

B
can be estimated similarly. Combining estimates yields the desired result for
p<q
.
∎

4.2.
Extrapolation theory

Generally speaking, the extrapolation theory of Jawerth and Milman

[
16
]
develops methods to recover the endpoint spaces of a
given parametrized scale of quasi-Banach spaces
\{A_{\theta}:\theta\in(0,1)\}.
In particular, it contains a careful analysis of the asymptotic
behavior of the related quasi-norms
\|\cdot\|_{A_{\theta}}
as

\theta\rightarrow 0^{+}
or
\theta\rightarrow 1^{-}
. In
[
16
]
it
is shown that
(\theta(1-\theta)p)^{\frac{1}{p}}
is the appropriate
normalization factor for the real interpolation method
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p}
(cf. (
4.1
)). This assertion is well-illustrated by the
following results.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then there exists
C>0
, which depends only on
p
, such that, for every
\theta\in(0,1)
and
f\in A_{0}\cap A_{1}
,

\|f\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p}}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\theta^{1/p}}\|f\|_{A_{0}}+\frac{1}{(1-\theta)^{1/p}}\|f\|_{A_{1}}\Big).

Proof.

Since
K(t,f)\leq\min\{\|f\|_{A_{0}},t\|f\|_{A_{1}}\}
, it
follows that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\theta p}K(t,f)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\theta p}K(t,f)^{p}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{A_{1}}^{p}\int_{0}^{1}t^{(1-\theta)p}\frac{dt}{t}+\|f\|_{A_{0}}^{p}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\theta p}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{A_{1}}^{p}\frac{1}{(1-\theta)p}+\|f\|_{A_{0}}^{p}\frac{1}{\theta p}.

∎

∎

The following result shows that the seminorm

is equivalent to the classical Gagliardo seminorm

, but the constants of

.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

and

. Then

where the hidden equivalence constants are independent of

Proof.

Recall the relationships between

and

(cf. Lemma

therefore, by interpolation, the following embeddings hold uniformly w.r.t.

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{B}_{p,q}^{t}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))_{\theta,p}

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\hookrightarrow(1-\theta)^{-1/\max\{p,q\}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))_{\theta t,p}.

In light of Lemma

(see also (

Combining (

) and (

), we find

5.
Proofs of the main results

5.1.
Proof of Proposition
3.1

We now recall some results that we need from the theory of Fourier series with
monotonic coefficients.

Suppose that the Fourier series of
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
is given by

(5.1)

f(x)\sim\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}c_{\nu}\cos(\nu x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{T}},

with

(5.2)

c_{\nu}\geq c_{\nu+1}\geq\cdots\geq 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{\nu}\rightarrow 0.

A well-known theorem of Hardy–Littlewood (see, e.g.,
[
35
, p. 129, Vol.
II]
) asserts that
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
if and only if

\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{p-2}c_{\nu}^{p}<\infty.

Furthermore

(5.3)

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}\approx\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{p-2}c_{\nu}^{p}.

This result has been further extended in
[
11
]
in order to
deal with other spaces of smooth functions (namely, Besov spaces and Sobolev
spaces), as well as more general classes of Fourier series with monotone-type
coefficients (the so called, general monotone class). In particular, it was
shown in
[
11
, Theorem 4.25]
that if
f
is given by
(
5.1
) with coefficients satisfying (
5.2
) then it belongs to

H^{t,p}({\mathbb{T}}),\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\,p\in(1,\infty),
if and only if

\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{tp+p-2}c_{\nu}^{p}<\infty.

Moreover,

(5.4)

\|f\|_{H^{t,p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}\approx\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{tp+p-2}c_{\nu}^{p}.

Let
t>0
and assume
c_{\nu}=\nu^{-t-1+\frac{1}{p}},\,\nu\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. By virtue of
(
5.3
) and (
5.4
)

(5.5)

\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}\approx\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{p-2}\nu^{-tp-p+1}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{-tp-1}<\infty

and

\|f\|_{H^{t,p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}\approx\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{tp+p-2}\nu^{-tp-p+1}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{-1}=\infty,

respectively. Consequently, the Fourier series
f
defined by
(
5.1
) belongs to
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
but not to

H^{t,p}({\mathbb{T}})
.

To conclude, it remains to show that

(5.6)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq C(t-s_{k})^{-\frac{1}{p}}

uniformly w.r.t.
k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. To proceed, we argue as follows. In
view of (
4.14
),

(5.7)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{T}})}\approx\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-s_{k}p}K(u,f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))^{p}\frac{du}{u}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with hidden constants of equivalence independent of
k
. Next we
estimate
K(u,f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))
.

For
u>0
, the classical moduli of smoothness of
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
is
defined by

(5.8)

\omega(f,u)_{p}:=\sup_{|h|\leq u}\|\Delta_{h}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Assume further that
f
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Then it
is known that
\omega(f,u)_{p}
can be estimated in terms of the Fourier
coefficients of
f
. Namely,

(5.9)

\omega(f,u)_{p}\approx\bigg(\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\min\{1,\nu u\}^{p}\nu^{p-2}c_{\nu}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad u\in(0,1);

(cf.
[
29
, Theorem 6.2]
and
[
14
, Theorem 6.1]

where this is actually done even in the more general context of general
monotone Fourier coefficients.)

Specializing (
5.9
) to
f
given by (
5.1
), yields

\omega(f,l^{-1})_{p}\approx l^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{\nu=1}^{l}\nu^{(1-t)p-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\bigg(\sum_{\nu=l}^{\infty}\nu^{-tp-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\approx\frac{1}{(t(1-t))^{\frac{1}{p}}}l^{-t}

for every
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
and
t\in(0,1)
. According to
(
4.15
) (with
t=1
) and (
5.8
), the previous estimate is equivalent to

(5.10)

K(l^{-1},f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))\approx\frac{1}{(t(1-t))^{\frac{1}{p}}}l^{-t}.

Furthermore, we have the trivial estimate

(5.11)

K(u,f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

Putting together (
5.7
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
),
applying monotonicity properties of
K
-functionals, and taking into account
that
s_{k}\uparrow t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s_{k},p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}u^{-s_{k}p}K(u,f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))^{p}\frac{du}{u}+\frac{1}{s_{k}p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{s_{k}p-1}K(l^{-1},f;L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}),\dot{W}_{p}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}))^{p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{-(t-s_{k})p-1}+\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{t-s_{k}}+\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t-s_{k}}.

This concludes the proof of (
5.6
). ∎

Appendix A: Sharp versions of fractional Sobolev inequalities

For
p\in(0,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty]
, the
Lorentz space

L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is formed by all measurable functions
f
defined on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(u^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(u)\big)^{q}\frac{du}{u}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty

(with the usual modification if
q=\infty
).
As usual,
f^{*}
denotes the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
. By
f^{**}
we denote the maximal function given by
f^{**}(u):=\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{u}f^{*}(v)\,dv
. We refer to
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for detailed accounts on Lorentz spaces.

Let
0<s<1,\,1\leq p<\frac{n}{s}
and
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
. The Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu–Maz’ya–Shasposhnikova formula (cf.
[
4
]
and
[
24
]
) claims that there exists
C=C(n,p)>0
such that

(5.23)

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p^{*},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\frac{s(1-s)}{(n-sp)^{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{p};

see also
[
20
]
.
This inequality can be considered as a sharp version of the classical Sobolev embedding

(5.24)

\dot{W}^{1}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{np}{n-p},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad 1\leq p<n.

Indeed, (
5.24
) follows from (
5.23
) by taking limits as
s\to 1^{-}
(cf. (
1.3
)).

Let
t\in(0,1)
be fixed. Note that the formula (
5.23
) does not provide any insight if
s\to t^{-}
. So the natural question here is: can we obtain an analogue of (
5.23
) for fractional smoothness
t
? Or equivalently, what is the fractional counterpart of (
5.23
) related to the classical embedding

(5.25)

\dot{H}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{np}{n-tp},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad 1<p<\frac{n}{t}?

The answer is given again in terms of the Butzer seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}
(cf. (
1.16
)).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
0<s<t\leq 1,1<p<\frac{n}{s}
and
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
. Assume
f\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then there exists
C=C(n,p,t)>0
such that

(5.26)

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p^{*},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\frac{s(t-s)}{(n-sp)^{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}^{p}.

In particular, if
t=1
then one recovers (
5.23
).

Remark 5.4
.

Let
t>0
and
1<p<\frac{n}{t}
. Taking limits as
s\to t^{-}
in (
5.26
) and applying Theorem
3.4
, we recover (
5.25
).

\displaystyle\lesssim(n-sp)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{t_{0}}{2}}f\|_{\dot{W}^{\theta(t-t_{0}),p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t-t_{0}}

\displaystyle\approx(n-sp)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{t_{0},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{H}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))_{\theta,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(n-sp)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}.

The proof is finished.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.3
and the well-known inequality between Lorentz spaces (cf.
[
28
, p. 192]
)

\Big(\frac{q}{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\Big(\frac{r}{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\qquad r<q,

we obtain the following

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
0<s<t\leq 1,1<p<\frac{n}{s}
and
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
. Assume
f\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Then there exists
C=C(n,p,t)>0
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq C\,s(t-s)(n-sp)^{1-p}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}^{p}.

Appendix B: Sharp relationships between Sobolev–Triebel–Lizorkin
spaces

The methodology proposed in
[
5
]
relies on sharp comparison estimates
between
\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

and
\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
using tools from harmonic analysis.
We now provide an approach to these results relying on the interpolation
techniques presented in Section
4
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Recall that (cf. Lemma

2.1
)

(5.41)

\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad p\geq 2,

and

\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\qquad p\leq 2.

The sharp behavior of the related equivalence constants in these embeddings
plays a key role in the Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu method. In particular, note
that the family of semi-norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:s\in(0,1)\}
converges to
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{F}_{p,p}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
as
s\rightarrow 1^{-}
, however this is not the
case for
\{\|\cdot\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}:s\in(0,1)\}

(cf. (
1.2
)). In particular, the hidden equivalence constants in
(
5.41
) must exhibit a certain blow up as
s
approaches the limiting
values
0
and
1
. The precise answer is contained in the following

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
0<s<t<\infty
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then
there exists a positive constant
C
, which is independent of
s
, such that

(a)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}

(5.42)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},

(b)

C\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\bigg)\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t},\qquad p\leq 2,

(c)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\bigg)\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})},\qquad p\geq 2.

Remark 5.7
.

The previous result can be understood as a generalization to higher order
smoothness of
[
5
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.5]
(which corresponds to the
classical case
t=1
, cf. (
1.18
)).

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

(a): By Lemma
4.8
, for
every
s\in(0,t)
,

(5.43)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}\approx\|f\|_{(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{H}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))_{\frac{s}{t},p}}.

The interpolation space given in the right-hand side of the previous estimate
can be explicitly computed via the retraction method. Specifically,

(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),\dot{H}^{t,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
can be identified
with the vector-valued pair
(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}})),\ell^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}})))
and thus, by
Lemma
4.1
,

(5.44)

\|(f_{j})\|_{(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}})),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}})))_{\frac{s}{t},p}}\approx\|(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};(\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\frac{s}{t},p})}.

Furthermore, according to Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|\xi\|_{\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\xi\|_{(\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\frac{s}{t},p}}

(5.45)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|\xi\|_{\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}.

As a combination of (
5.44
) and (
5.45
),

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\max\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(f_{j})\|_{(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}})),L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}})))_{\frac{s}{t},p}}

(5.46)

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}+\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\min\{p,2\}}}}\bigg)\|(f_{j})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))}.

By the well-known fact that
\dot{F}_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a retract
of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{p}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))
, we can put together
(
5.46
) and (
5.43
) to arrive at (
5.42
).

(b): Let
p\leq 2
. Applying Lemmas
4.2
and
4.5
(i),

(5.47)

\|\xi\|_{\ell_{2}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}})}\approx(s(t-s))^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{(\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\frac{s}{t},2}}\lesssim(s(t-s))^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\xi\|_{(\ell_{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}}),\ell_{2}^{t}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))_{\frac{s}{t},p}}.

Taking into account that
\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a retract of

L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n};\ell_{2}^{s}({{\mathbb{Z}}}))
, it follows now from
(
5.43
), (
5.44
) and (
5.47
) that

(s(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{\dot{F}_{p,2}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),t}.

The proof of (c) follows similar ideas as those given in (b).
∎

The following problem was formulated by Kato in
[
19
]
:
is the equality

(1.2)

{\rm dom\,}B^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}B^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}

true for an arbitrary unbounded maximal accretive operator

The negative answer was given in
[
24
]
by Lions. By means of the theory of interpolation spaces he showed that for the maximal accretive operator in

(1.3)

{\mathcal{B}}_{0}=\cfrac{d}{dx},\;{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}=\left\{f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}):f(0)=0\right\}

holds the inequality
{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\neq{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
Note that the operator
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
is not sectorial and, moreover, the operator
-i{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
is maximal symmetric and non-selfadjoint.

The next results were established in
[

]
and

Theorem 1.1

Let

{\rm dom\,}B^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\subseteq{\rm dom\,}B^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}

{\rm dom\,}B^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\subseteq{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq{\rm dom\,}B^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}

the same is true when

that if both

In this paper yet another approach to the abstract Kato square root problem is proposed. We essentially relay on the properties of contractions and especially of contractions which are linear fractional transformations of
m
-sectorial operators (see Section

The usage of our method allows

(

(

)

(

);

(

(

2.
Maximal sectorial operators

The numerical range
W(A)
of a linear operator
T
with domain
{\rm dom\,}A
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
is given by

W(A)=\left\{(Au,u):u\in{\rm dom\,}A,\,\|u\|=1\right\}.

By the Toeplitz-Hausdorff theorem (a short proof can be found in
[
17
]
) the numerical range is a convex set.

A linear operator
A
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is called
accretive

[
19
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
32
]

if
{\rm Re\,}(Au,u)\geq 0
for all
u\in{\rm dom\,}A
, i.e.,

W(A)\subseteq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\rm Re\,}z\geq 0\}.

An accretive operator
A
is called
maximal accretive
, or

m
-accretive
for short, if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(i)

A
has no proper accretive extensions in
{\mathfrak{H}}
;

(ii)

A
is densely defined and
{\rm ran\,}(A-\lambda I)={\mathfrak{H}}
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
{\rm Re\,}\lambda<0
;

(iii)

A
is densely defined and closed, and
A^{*}
is accretive;

(iv)

-A
generates contractive one-parameter semigroup
T(t)=\exp(-tA)
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

If
A
is
m
-accretive, then
\ker A=\ker A^{*}
and hence

\ker A\subseteq{\rm dom\,}A\cap{\rm dom\,}A^{*}.

An accretive operator
A
is called
coercive
if there is a positive constant
a
such that

{\rm Re\,}(Af,f)\geq a||f||^{2}\;\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}A.

If
A
is a coercive, then
{\rm ran\,}A
is a subspace (closed linear manifold) and
A^{-1}{\upharpoonright\,}{\rm ran\,}A
is a bounded accretive operator. If
A
is
m
-accretive and coercive in
{\mathfrak{H}}
, then
{\rm ran\,}A={\mathfrak{H}}
.
Clearly, if
A
is accretive operator, then
A-\lambda I
is coercive accretive for each
\lambda
,
{\rm Re\,}\lambda<0
.

A linear operator
A
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
is called
sectorial
with vertex
z=0
and
semi-angle
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2)
, or
\alpha
-sectorial
for short, if its numerical range is contained in a
closed sector with semi-angle
\alpha
, i.e.,

W(A)\subseteq\left\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\arg z|\leq\alpha\right\}

or, equivalently,
|{\rm Im\,}(Au,u)|\!\leq\!\tan\alpha\,{\rm Re\,}(Au,u)
for all
u\!\in\!{\rm dom\,}A
.
Clearly, an
\alpha
-sectorial operator is accretive; it is called
maximal sectorial
, or
m
-
\alpha
-sectorial
for short, if
it is
m
-accretive.

Moreover,

(2.1)

{\mathfrak{a}}[u,v]=((I+iG)A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}v),\;u,v\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{a}},

where
G
is a bounded selfadjoint operator in the subspace

\overline{{\rm ran\,}{A_{R}}}
and

iff

is

\alpha

The operator
A_{R}

[

By the first representation theorem
[

]
, the associated

-sectorial operators

are given by

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}A=A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}(I+iG)A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R},\;{\rm dom\,}A=\left\{u\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}:(I+iG)A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}u\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}\right\},\\

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm dom\,}A\cap{\rm dom\,}A^{*}=\left\{u\in{\rm dom\,}A_{R}:\;GA^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}u\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}_{R}\right\},\\[5.69054pt]

3.
Contractions of the class
\widetilde{C}_{\mathfrak{H}}

As is well known (see e.g.,
[
32
]
), if
A
is
m
-accretive operator, then the linear fractional transformation

Z:=(I-A)(I+A)^{-1}

is a contraction. Conversely, if
Z
is a contraction, then the linear relation
[
1
]

(3.1)

A:=-I+2(I+Z)^{-1}=\left\{\left\{(I+Z)h,(I-Z)h\right\}:h\in{\mathfrak{H}}\right\}

is
m
-accretive. Moreover,
A
in (
3.1
) is operator if and only if
\ker(I+Z)=\{0\}
and if this is the case, then

(Af,f)=\left((I+Z^{*})(I-Z)h,h\right)=\left((I-Z^{*}Z)h,h\right)-((Z-Z^{*})h,h),\;f=(I+Z)h.

The operator
A
is bounded if and only if
{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}
.

Note that for any contraction
Z
holds the equality
\ker(I-Z)=\ker(I-Z^{*})
(see
[
32
, Chapter I, Proposition 3.1]
).

The lemma below will be used in the next sections.

Lemma 3.1
.

(1) Let
Z
be a bounded operator in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Suppose
\ker(I-Z)=\ker(I+Z)=\{0\}
.
Then

{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\cap{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2}),

and, therefore
the equality
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2})={\mathfrak{H}}
is equivalent to the equalities
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}.

Moreover,

(a)

if
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\subseteq{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
, then
{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}
,

(b)

if
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
, then
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}.

(2) If
X
is a selfadjoint contraction and
\ker(I-X)=\ker(I+X)=\{0\},
then

{\rm ran\,}(I-X)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap{\rm ran\,}(I+X)^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-X^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

(1) Since

I-Z^{2}=(I-Z)(I+Z)=(I+Z)(I-Z),

the inclusion
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2})
implies inclusions
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)
and
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
, i.e.,
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\cap{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
.

Assume that
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\cap{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
, i.e.,
h=(I-Z)f=(I+Z)g
.
Set

\varphi:=f-g,\;\psi:=f+g

Then

\varphi=Z\psi,\;f={\frac{1}{2}}(I+Z)\psi,\;g={\frac{1}{2}}(I-Z)\psi.

It follows that
h={\frac{1}{2}}(I-Z^{2})\psi,
i.e.,
h\in{\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2})
.

Suppose that
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\subseteq{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
, then

{\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2})={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)\cap{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I-Z).

Hence, from the equality
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z^{2})=(I-Z){\rm ran\,}(I+Z)

we get
{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}
.
Consequently, the equality
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)
yields that
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\mathfrak{H}}.

(2) Let

E_{X}(t),

t\in[-1,1]
be the orthogonal spectral function of
X
. Then due to the equality
(1-t^{2})^{-1}={\frac{1}{2}}\left((1-t)^{-1}+(1+t)^{-1}\right)
we get the equivalence for a vector
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}\setminus\{0\}

\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\cfrac{d(E_{X}(t)f,f)}{1-t^{2}}<\infty\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\cfrac{d(E_{X}(t)f,f)}{1-t}<\infty\\
\int\limits_{-1}^{1}\cfrac{d(E_{X}(t)f,f)}{1+t}<\infty\end{array}\right..

Hence

f\in{\rm dom\,}(I-X^{2})^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad f\in{\rm dom\,}(I-X)^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}\cap{\rm dom\,}(I+X)^{-{\frac{1}{2}}},

i.e.,
{\rm ran\,}(I-X)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cap{\rm ran\,}(I+X)^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-X^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

The following statements are equivalent for
Z\in\widetilde{C}_{\mathfrak{H}}
:

(i)

||Z||<1
;

(ii)

{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)\subseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}
and
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\subseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}
;

(iii)

{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)\supseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}
and
{\rm ran\,}(I-Z)\supseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}
;

(iv)

{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\mathfrak{H}}
.

Proof.

Clearly

||Z||<1\Longrightarrow{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}={\mathfrak{H}}.

Suppose (ii) holds. Then by Douglas’ lemma
[
12
]
we get

||(I+Z^{*})f||\leq c_{1}||D_{Z^{*}}f||,\;||(I-Z^{*})f||\leq c_{2}||D_{Z^{*}}f||\;\;\forall f\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

Since

(3.5)

||(I\pm Z^{*})f||^{2}+||D_{Z^{*}}f||^{2}=2{\rm Re\,}\left((I\pm Z)f,f\right)=2||(I\pm{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}f||^{2}\;\;\forall f\in{\mathfrak{H}},

we get

||(I\pm{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}f||\leq b||D_{Z^{*}}f||\;\;\forall f\in{\mathfrak{H}}.

Hence

{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}},\;{\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}.

On the other side equalities (
3.5
) and the Douglas’ lemma implies the inclusions

{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}\subseteq{\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}},\;{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}\subseteq{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus

{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Because
Z\in\widetilde{C}_{\mathfrak{H}}
, from (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.1
we get the equalities

(3.6)

{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}={\rm ran\,}D_{{\rm Re\,}(Z)}={\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

{\rm ran\,}D_{{\rm Re\,}(Z)}=(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}=(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}},

equalities in (
3.6
) imply

{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}D_{{\rm Re\,}(Z)}={\mathfrak{H}}

Hence
{\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}={\mathfrak{H}}
and therefore
||Z||<1
. Thus, (ii) implies (i).

Suppose that (iii) holds. Using (
3.5
), Douglas’ lemma and arguing as above, we get the equalities

(3.7)

{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I+Z),\;{\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-Z).

Because the operator
Z
belongs to the class
\widetilde{C}_{\mathfrak{H}}
, the operators
I\pm Z
are sectorial and, therefore, admit the representations

\begin{array}[]{l}I+Z=(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}(I+iF_{+})(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}},\\[5.69054pt]
I-Z=(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}(I+iF_{-})(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{array}

where
F_{\pm}
are bounded selfadjoint operators. Hence and from (
3.7
)

{\rm ran\,}(I\pm{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{{\frac{1}{2}}}=(I\pm{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(I+iF_{\pm}){\rm ran\,}(I\pm{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Consequently, because
{\rm ran\,}(I+iF_{\pm})={\mathfrak{H}}
, we obtain the equalities

{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\mathfrak{H}}.

It follows that
\pm 1\in\rho({\rm Re\,}(Z))
. Therefore
{\rm ran\,}D_{{\rm Re\,}(Z)}={\mathfrak{H}}
and (
3.4
) implies that
{\rm ran\,}D_{Z}={\mathfrak{H}}
. Therefore
||Z||<1.
Thus, (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i).

Suppose (iv) holds. Then there are
c_{\pm}>0
such that
||(I\pm Z^{*})f||\geq c_{\pm}\,||f||
for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Consequently, from (
3.5
) we get the equalities

{\rm ran\,}(I+{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\rm Re\,}(Z))^{\frac{1}{2}}={\mathfrak{H}}.

Now Lemma
3.1
and (
3.4
) lead to the equalities
{\rm ran\,}D_{{\rm Re\,}(Z)}={\rm ran\,}D_{Z^{*}}={\mathfrak{H}}.
Hence

∎

Proof.

The equality in (

{\rm Re\,}(T)=Q

{\rm ran\,}(I+Z)={\rm ran\,}(I-Z)={\mathfrak{H}}

{\rm ran\,}T={\rm ran\,}T^{*}={\rm ran\,}{\rm Re\,}(T)

4.1
) it follows that the operator
QZQ^{-1}{\upharpoonright\,}{\rm ran\,}Q

{\mathfrak{H}}

21

imply that

\begin{array}[]{l}T=Q+QZ=Q-Z^{*}Q=Q+iQ^{\frac{1}{2}}XQ^{\frac{1}{2}},\\

Remark 4.2
.

(1) The following statement follows from Proposition
4.1
: if
Q
is a bounded nonnegative selfadjoint operator,
\ker Q=\{0\}
, then there is no a nonzero bounded selfadjoint operator
Z
which anti-commutes with
Q
(
QZ+ZQ=0
).

Actually, if
Z
is a such selfadjoint operator, then
\widehat{Z}=||Z||^{-1}Z
is a selfadjoint contraction and
Q\widehat{Z}=-\widehat{Z}Q
. The operator
\widehat{T}=Q+Q\widehat{Z}
is accretive and
\widehat{T}^{2}=Q(I-\widehat{Z}^{2})Q\geq 0,
i.e. the operator
\widehat{T}^{2}
is nonnegative selfadjoint. The uniqueness of maximal accretive square root of a maximal accretive operator (see
[
22
, Chapter V, Theorem 3.35]
) yields that
{\rm Im\,}(\widehat{T})=0
, i.e.,
QZ=0
and hence
Z=0.

On the other side the above statement can be derived from the well known Heinz inequality (see e.g.
[
15
]

Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are bounded nonnegative selfadjoint operators, then for each bounded operator
Z
and for an arbitrary

In fact, if
Q_{1}=Q_{2}=Q
and if
r={\frac{1}{2}},
then

\left\|QZ+ZQ\right\|\geq 2\left\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}ZQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|.

Hence,
\ker Q=\{0\}
and the equality
QZ+ZQ=0
yield
Z=0
.

(2) Suppose that the operator
Z
is skew-selfadjoint, i.e.,
Z=iY
, where

is a sefadjoint contraction. Then (

) means that

{\mathcal{B}}=i{\mathcal{A}}
holds the inequality
{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\neq{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}
.

Proof.

Because
{\rm Re\,}({\mathcal{B}}f,f)=0
for all

, the operator
{\mathcal{B}}
is accretive. Since
i\in\rho({\mathcal{A}})
, we get
-1\in\rho({\mathcal{B}})
. Therefore
{\mathcal{B}}
is
m
-accretive, the square root

{\mathcal{B}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is
m-\cfrac{\pi}{4}
-sectorial. Set

{\mathcal{U}}:=({\mathcal{B}}-I)(I+{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}=(I-i{\mathcal{A}})(I+i{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}.

Then
{\mathcal{U}}
is a non-unitary isometry because
{\rm ran\,}{\mathcal{U}}
is a proper subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}
. This means that
0\in\sigma({\mathcal{U}})
. The operator
I-{\mathcal{U}}
is accretive and since

I-{\mathcal{U}}=2(I+{\mathcal{B}})^{-1},

we obtain the equalities

{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}(I+{\mathcal{B}})^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\mathcal{U}})^{{\frac{1}{2}}},\;{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm dom\,}(I+{\mathcal{B}}^{*})^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm ran\,}(I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Since
\ker(I-{\mathcal{U}})=\ker(I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*})=\{0\}
and the operator

is
m-\cfrac{\pi}{4}
-sectorial, we get (see (

Set

{\mathcal{Q}}={\rm Re\,}((I-{\mathcal{U}})^{\frac{1}{2}}),\;{\mathcal{Z}}=i{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}{\rm Im\,}((I-{\mathcal{U}})^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Then
{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{Z}}=-{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
and
according to Proposition
4.4
the operators
(I-{\mathcal{U}})^{\frac{1}{2}},

(I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{{\frac{1}{2}}}
,
I-{\mathcal{U}}
, and
I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*}
admit the representations

\begin{array}[]{l}(I-{\mathcal{U}})^{\frac{1}{2}}={\mathcal{Q}}(I+{\mathcal{Z}})=(I-{\mathcal{Z}}^{*})Q,\\[5.69054pt]
(I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*})^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\mathcal{Q}}(I-{\mathcal{Z}})=(I+{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}){\mathcal{Q}},\\[5.69054pt]
I-{\mathcal{U}}=({\mathcal{Q}}(I+{\mathcal{Z}}))^{2}=((I-{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}){\mathcal{Q}})^{2}={\mathcal{Q}}(I+{\mathcal{Z}})(I-{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}){\mathcal{Q}},\\[5.69054pt]

Set

{\mathcal{M}}:=(I+{\mathcal{Z}})(I-{\mathcal{Z}}^{*}),

then
I-{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{M}}{\mathcal{Q}},

I-{\mathcal{U}}^{*}={\mathcal{Q}}{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}
,

{\mathcal{U}}^{*}{\mathcal{U}}=I\Longleftrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}^{2}{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}+{\mathcal{M}}^{*}\Longleftrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}^{2}{\mathcal{M}}-I)={\mathcal{M}}.

Now assume that

) that

. It follows that

Since

. Contradiction, because

Therefore

{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\neq{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{B}}^{*{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
∎

Exploring regular black holes within the framework of the TFD formalism
Jhonny A. A. Ruiz, A. F. Santos

Abstract
In this paper, a massive scalar field non-minimally coupled with gravity is considered. Assuming a regular and spherically symmetric background, specifically a regular black hole solution, the energy-momentum tensor is calculated. Using the Thermo Field Dynamics (TFD) formalism and its topological structure, the corresponding Stefan-Boltzmann law and Casimir effect are investigated. The results for the regular black hole are compared with those obtained for the Schwarzschild black hole.

Massive scalar field

In this section, the massive scalar field is introduced, and the energy-momentum tensor for this theory is calculated. Additionally, the RBH (Regular Black Hole) is presented as the gravitational background. Consider the Lagrangian density for a massive and non-minimal scalar field

III
Thermo Field Dynamics

In this section, a thermal quantum field theory known as the TFD (Thermo Field Dynamics) formalism is briefly introduced. For a review of this formalism, see references
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. This formalism involves considering the vacuum expectation value of an arbitrary operator as equivalent to the statistical average. To achieve this, a new ground state, called the thermal vacuum, must be proposed. Thus, the average value of an arbitrary operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}
in a thermal vacuum is defined as

\braket{\mathcal{O}}=\braket{0(\beta)|\mathcal{O}|0(\beta)}

(21)

where
\displaystyle\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
, with
\displaystyle k_{B}
being the Boltzmann constant and
\displaystyle T
the temperature. This construction requires two ingredients: (i) the doubled Fock space
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{T}
given by the tensor product

\mathcal{S}_{T}=\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{\tilde{S}},

(22)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
is the standard Fock space and
\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{S}}
is the dual (tilde) space. (ii) The Bogoliubov transformation, which involves a rotation between
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{S}}
, in the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{O}(k,\alpha)\\
\eta\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{{\dagger}}(k,\alpha)\end{array}\right)=\mathcal{B}(\alpha)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{O}(k)\\
\eta\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{{\dagger}}(k)\end{array}\right),

(23)

where
\displaystyle\eta=1,-1
, for fermions and bosons, respectively,
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)
is the Bogoliubov factor defined as

\mathcal{B}(\alpha)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u(\alpha)&-v(\alpha)\\
\eta\,v(\alpha)&u(\alpha)\end{array}\right)

(24)

and

v(\alpha)=\left(e^{\alpha\omega_{k}}-1\right)^{-1},\qquad\qquad u^{2}(\alpha)=1+v^{2}(\alpha)

(25)

which satisfy the identity

u^{2}(\alpha)+\eta\,v^{2}(\alpha)=1,

(26)

with
\displaystyle\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{D-1})
as the compactification parameter over the
\displaystyle D
-dimensions and
\displaystyle\omega_{k}=k_{0}
as the associated energy

[
21
]
.

Another characteristic of the TFD formalism is the introduction of a compactification parameter in the propagator of the theory. Here, we introduce this parameter in the scalar field propagator
[
26
]
, starting with the expression

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{0}^{AB}(x-x^{\prime};\alpha)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle i\braket{0(\alpha)|\tau\left[\phi^{A}(x)\phi^{B}(x^{\prime})\right]|0(\alpha)}

(27)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle i\int{\frac{d^{4}x}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ik(x-x^{\prime})}G_{0}^{AB}(k;\alpha)}

where

G_{0}^{AB}(k;\alpha)=\mathcal{B}^{-1}(\alpha)G_{0}^{AB}(k)\mathcal{B}(\alpha),

(28)

with

G_{0}^{AB}(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G_{0}(k)&0\\
0&\eta G_{0}^{*}(k)\end{array}\right)

(29)

and
\displaystyle G_{0}(k)
is the scalar field propagator defined in (
13
)
2
2

2

Here A, B=1,2 represent the doubled space and
\displaystyle G_{0}^{*}(k)
is the complex conjugate of
\displaystyle G_{0}(k)
.
. Its physical component is given by the component
\displaystyle A=B=1

[
23
]
, such that

G_{0}^{AB}(k;\alpha)=G_{0}(k)+\eta\,v^{2}(k;\alpha)\left[G_{0}^{*}(k)-G_{0}(k)\right],

(30)

where
\displaystyle v^{2}(k;\alpha)
is the generalized Bogoliubov transformation
[
24
]
.

In this context, let’s rewrite the vacuum expectation value of the energy-momentum tensor, given in Eq. (
10
), using the doubled notation. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle T_{\mu\nu}^{(AB)}(x;\alpha)\rangle=\lim_{x^{\prime}\to x}\left[i\,\Gamma_{\mu\nu}\,G_{0}^{(ab)}(x-x^{\prime};\alpha)-I_{\mu\nu}\,\delta(x-x^{\prime})\delta^{AB}\right].

(31)

To obtain a physical energy-momentum tensor that leads to finite quantities, a renormalization procedure is necessary. Here, the Casimir prescription is applied. The resulting finite energy-momentum tensor is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal T}_{\mu\nu(ab)}(x;\alpha)=\langle T_{\mu\nu(ab)}(x;\alpha)\rangle-\langle T_{\mu\nu(ab)}(x)\rangle.

(32)

With this procedure a measurable physical quantity is obtained. Explicitly

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal T}_{\mu\nu(ab)}(x;\alpha)=i\lim_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\left\{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}(x,x^{\prime})\overline{G}_{0}^{(ab)}(x-x^{\prime};\alpha)\right\},

(33)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\overline{G}_{0}^{(ab)}(x-x^{\prime};\alpha)=G_{0}^{(ab)}(x-x^{\prime};\alpha)-G_{0}^{(ab)}(x-x^{\prime}).

(34)

In the next sections, Eq. (
33
) is used to explore the topological structure of the TFD formalism, and then some applications are investigated.

IV
Thermal effects for a regular black hole

The TFD formalism represents a field theory defined on the topology
\displaystyle\Gamma_{D}^{d}=(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{D-d}
where
\displaystyle 1\leq d\leq D
. In this context,

\displaystyle D
denotes the total number of space-time dimensions, while
\displaystyle d
signifies the number of dimensions that have been compactified. This formalism allows for the compactification of any subset of dimensions within the manifold
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{D}
. In this construction, the circumference of each
\displaystyle nth

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{1}
in the topology
\displaystyle\Gamma_{D}^{d}
is specified by
\displaystyle\alpha_{n}
. First, let’s consider the specific case
\displaystyle\Gamma_{4}^{1}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\displaystyle\alpha=(\beta,0,0,0)
. In this case the time-axis is compactified in
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{1}
, with circumference
\displaystyle\beta
. For this case, thermal effects are introduced, and then the Stefan-Boltzmann law can be calculated.

In order to utilize the energy-momentum tensor expression, let’s outline the generalized Bogoliubov transformation for this scenario

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{2}(\beta)=\sum_{l_{0}=1}^{\infty}e^{-\beta k^{0}l_{0}}.

(35)

Then the Green function is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\overline{G}_{0}(x-x^{\prime};\beta)=2\sum_{l_{0}=1}^{\infty}G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta l_{0}n_{0}),

(36)

where
\displaystyle n_{0}=(1,0,0,0)
and
\displaystyle\overline{G}_{0}(x-x^{\prime};\beta)\equiv\overline{G}_{0}^{(11)}(x-x^{\prime};\beta)
, that is the physical component. Thus Eq. (
33
) becomes

\mathcal{T}_{\mu\nu(11)}(x;\beta)=2i\lim_{x^{\prime}\to\,x}\left\{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}\sum_{l_{0}=1}^{\infty}G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})\right\}.

(37)

The propagator for the massive scalar field takes the form

G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})=-\frac{i\,m}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{K_{1}\left(m\sqrt{-(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{-(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})^{2}}}

(38)

with

(x-x^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})^{2}=-f(r)(t-t^{\prime}-i\beta\,l_{0}\,n_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{f(r)}(r-r^{\prime})^{2}+r^{2}(\theta-\theta^{\prime})^{2}+r^{2}\sin{\theta}^{2}(\phi-\phi^{\prime})^{2},

(39)

in correspondence to the RBH line element defined in Eq. (
15
).

Using these ingredients and Eq. (
18
), the energy for the massive scalar field in the regular black hole geometry is given by the
\displaystyle 00
component of Eq. (
37
). After some calculations, the energy can be written as

\displaystyle\displaystyle E=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{l_{0}=1}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{6m^{2}(1+\xi)}{l_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}}K_{0}[f(r)]+\frac{12m(1+\xi)}{l_{0}^{3}\beta^{3}\sqrt{-f(r)}}K_{1}[f(r)]\right\},

(40)

where we have defined

K_{\nu}[f(r)]=K_{\nu}\left[ml_{0}\beta\sqrt{-f(r)}\right],\qquad\nu=0,1,

(41)

with
\displaystyle K_{\nu}[f(r)]
being the Bessel functions and
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{00(11)}(x;\beta)\equiv E
. This represents the Stefan-Boltzmann law for the massive scalar field in a gravitational background described by a regular black hole. It is not possible to perform the sum in Eq. (
40
) and obtain a complete expression for the energy. To derive a closed-form expression for the energy in this context, let us consider the massless case. In this limit, the energy takes the simple form

E=\frac{\pi^{2}(1+\xi)}{30\beta^{4}\,f(r)}.

(42)

Thus, when
\displaystyle f(r)
takes the usual Schwarzschild form
\displaystyle f(r)=1-2M/r
, we recover the energy for a massless scalar field at finite temperature, as reported in previous work
[
33
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{Sch}=\frac{\pi^{2}(1+\xi)}{30\beta^{4}}\left(\frac{r}{r-2M}\right).

(43)

On the other hand, the corresponding Bardeen and Hayward energies for the massless case are given by

E_{B}=\frac{\pi^{2}(1+\xi)}{30\beta^{4}}\left\{1-\frac{2M}{r\left[1+\left(r_{0}/r\right)^{2}\right]^{3/2}}\right\}^{-1}

(44)

E_{H}=\frac{\pi^{2}(1+\xi)}{30\beta^{4}}\left\{1-\frac{2M}{r\left[1+\left(r_{0}/r\right)^{3}\right]}\right\}^{-1}.

(45)

It is important to emphasize that these results show that the Stefan-Boltzmann law associated with the Schwarzschild black hole behaves differently when compared to the Bardeen and Hayward regular black holes. In Figure
1
, the energy is shown for these three cases. The results demonstrate that the singularity due to the Schwarzschild solution persists in the energy, while for the Bardeen and Hayward solutions, the energy is always finite. In addition, it is interesting to note that, for large radii, all three cases take the same shape.

V
Casimir effect at zero temperature

To investigate size effects in TFD formalism, let’s consider the topology
\displaystyle\Gamma^{1}_{4}=\mathbf{S^{1}}\times\mathbf{R^{3}}
and the compactification parameter
\displaystyle\alpha
defined as
\displaystyle\alpha=(0,i2b,0,0)
, where
\displaystyle 2b
is the circumference of
\displaystyle\mathbf{S^{1}}
. For this case, the Bogoliubov transformation takes the form

v^{2}(b)=\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}e^{-i2bk^{1}l_{1}}

(46)

and the Green function is

\bar{G}_{0}(x-x^{\prime};b)=2\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-2bl_{1}n_{1})

(47)

with
\displaystyle n_{1}=(0,1,0,0)
. Then the Casimir energy is calculated from the
\displaystyle 00
component of the energy-momentum tensor, which is given as

\mathcal{T}_{00}^{11}(x;b)=2i\lim_{x^{\prime}\to\,x}\left\{\Gamma_{00}\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-i2b\,l_{1}\,n_{1})\right\}.

(48)

Using Eq. (
18
) and defining
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{00}^{11}(x;b)=E_{c}
, the Casimir energy is obtained as

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{c}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{8\pi^{2}b^{2}l_{1}^{2}}\Bigl\{\left[(1+\xi){f(r)}^{2}+bl_{1}(1+2\xi)f^{\prime}(r)\right]mK_{0}(r)

(49)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[-2b^{2}l_{1}^{2}f(r)m^{2}\xi+(1+\xi){f(r)}^{2}-bl_{1}f^{\prime}(r)(1+2\xi)(b^{2}l_{1}^{2}m^{2}-f(r))\right]\frac{\sqrt{-f(r)}}{bl_{1}}K_{1}(r)\Bigr\}.

To find the Casimir pressure, we use now

\mathcal{T}_{11}^{11}(x;b)=2i\lim_{x^{\prime}\to\,x}\left\{\Gamma_{11}\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}G_{0}(x-x^{\prime}-i2b\,l_{1}\,n_{1})\right\},

(50)

which yields the expression at zero temperature

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{c}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{m}{8\pi^{2}b^{3}l_{1}^{3}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{-f(r)}}{f(r)}\right)^{3}\Bigl\{bl_{1}\frac{f(r)}{\sqrt{-f(r)}}\left[3(1+\xi)f(r)+bl_{1}f^{\prime}(r)\right]mK_{0}(r)

(51)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left[3(1+\xi){f(r)}^{2}-b^{3}l_{1}^{3}m^{2}f^{\prime}(r)+bl_{1}f(r)\left(f^{\prime}(r)-bl_{1}m^{2}\right)\right]K_{1}(r)\Bigr\},

where Eq. (
19
) has been used. Here, we have defined
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{11}^{11}(x;b)=P_{c}
and

K_{\nu}(r)=K_{\nu}\left(\frac{2mbl_{1}}{\sqrt{-f(r)}}\right),\qquad\qquad\nu=0,1.

(52)

In the case of zero mass and assuming that the gravitational background is described by a Schwarzschild black hole, these expressions for the Casimir-Schwarzschild energy and pressure, reduce to

E_{CS}=-\frac{(r-2M)^{2}}{1440\pi^{2}b^{4}r^{4}}\left[\pi^{4}r(r-2M)(1+\xi)-180bM(1+2\xi)\zeta(3)\right]

(53)

and

P_{CS}=-\frac{\pi^{4}(1+\xi)r(r-2M)+60bM\zeta(3)}{480b^{4}\pi^{2}r^{2}}.

(54)

These results are in agreement with the previous paper
[
33
]
.

From the general expressions (
49
) and (
51
), and in the massless limit for the scalar field, it is also possible to write down the Casimir-Bardeen and Casimir-Hayward energies and pressures, which are given by

VI
Conclusions

Black hole physics have been fascinating since the beginning of general relativity. The singularities inherent in their physics present a challenge that has drawn significant attention. To study the physics of black holes without singularities, regular black hole solutions such as the Bardeen and Hayward solutions have been proposed. In this work, a massive scalar field non-minimally coupled with gravity is considered. Using the topological structure of the TFD formalism, thermal and size effects are investigated, considering a regular black hole as the gravitational background. The Stefan-Boltzmann law and the Casimir effect for the massive scalar field in curved spacetime are calculated. Our results show that these quantities exhibit different behavior when different regular black holes are considered. We compare the results for regular black holes with those obtained for the Schwarzschild black hole. While the results for the Schwarzschild black hole exhibit singularities, the results for the Bardeen and Hayward black holes are always finite. One of the most important results of this paper is related to the Casimir pressure. Here, three different solutions are analyzed. When the Schwarzschild spacetime is considered, there is a transition from attractive to repulsive force. Additionally, for
\displaystyle r
smaller than the event horizon, the force is repulsive and goes to an infinite value. For the Bardeen and Hayward black holes, the force is always attractive and finite.

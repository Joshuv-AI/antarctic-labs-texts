Positive and negative extensions in extriangulated categories
Mikhail Gorsky, Hiroyuki Nakaoka, Yann Palu

Abstract
. We initiate the study of derived functors in the setting of extriangulated categories. By using coends, we adapt Yoneda’s theory of higher extensions to this framework. We show that, when there are enough projectives or enough injectives, thus defined extensions agree with the ones defined earlier via projective or injective resolutions. For categories with enough projective or enough injective morphisms, we prove that these are right derived functors of the Hom -bifunctor in either argument. Since Hom is only half-exact in each argument, it is natural to expect “negative extensions”, i.e. its left derived functors, to exist and not necessarily vanish. We define negative extensions with respect to the first and to the second argument and show that they give rise to universal δ \delta -functors, when there are enough projective or injective morphisms, respectively. In general, they are not balanced. However, for topological extriangulated categories, the existence of a balanced version of negative extensions follows from combining the work of Klemenc on exact ∞ \infty -categories with results of the second and third authors. We discuss various criteria under which one has the balance of the above bifunctors on the functorial or on the numerical levels. This happens, in particular, in the cases of exact or triangulated categories, and also in the case of the category of n n -term complexes with projective components over a finite-dimensional algebra. Given a connected sequence of functors on an extriangulated category ( 𝒞 , 𝔼 , 𝔰 ) (\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s}) , we determine the maximal relative extriangulated structure, with respect to which the sequence is a δ \delta -functor. We also find several equivalent criteria for the existence of enough projective or injective morphisms in a given extriangulated category.



1.
Introduction

The notion of
extriangulated categories
was recently introduced by the second and the third authors
[
NP1
]
as a unification of exact categories (in the sense of Quillen
[
Buh
,
Kel
,
Q
]
) and of triangulated categories
[
V
]
.
One can also consider it as an axiomatization of full extension-closed subcategories of triangulated categories, although it is not known whether every extriangulated category admits an embedding (as a full extension-closed subcategory) into a triangulated category. The datum defining an extriangulated category consists of an additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
, a bifunctor
\mathbb{E}\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\mbox{Ab}
and a so-called
realization
correspondence
\mathfrak{s}
that essentially defines the class of
conflations
. This datum must then satisfy certain axioms that simultaneously generalize axioms of exact and of triangulated categories.

The class of extriangulated categories is stable under several natural operations. An extension-closed subcategory of an extriangulated category becomes extriangulated with a naturally induced structure
[
NP1
]
. Any closed sub-bifunctor of
\mathbb{E}
induces a
relative
extriangulated structure, or extriangulated
substructure

[
HLN
,
ZH
]
. An ideal quotient of an extriangulated category by an ideal generated by morphisms that factor through projective-injective objects
[
NP1
]
or, more generally, by an ideal generated by morphisms that factor through morphisms with injective domain and projective codomain
[
FGPPP
]
, has an induced extriangulated structure.

Barwick
[
Bar
]
defined (additive)
exact
\infty
-categories
as a higher categorical framework where higher algebraic
K
-theory is well-defined and well-behaved. Recently, the second and the third authors
[
NP2
]
proved that the homotopy category of an exact
\infty
-category admits a natural extriangulated structure. They called extriangulated categories admitting such
\infty
-categorical enhancements
topological
. Klemenc
[
Kl
]
proved that each exact
\infty
-category has a
stable hull
, that is, it embeds into a stable
\infty
-category, and this embedding is universal. Since the homotopy category of a stable
\infty
-category is triangulated, this indicates that each topological extriangulated category admits an embedding as an extension closed subcategory into a triangulated category. Therefore, topological extriangulated categories inherit a notion of higher positive and negative extensions from this triangulated embedding. We study existence of such extensions in extriangulated categories, without reference to any specific embedding.

In Subsection
2.1
we recall the definitions and basic properties of extriangulated categories. In particular in Subsection
2.3

By using coends, in Section

[
Buch
,
Buh
,
BH
,
M

Y

[
HLN

via projective or injective resolutions.
As the main theorem of Section
3

We use this to introduce the notion of
positive global dimension of extriangulated categories
. For exact categories, this recovers the usual definition. Each non-zero triangulated category has infinite global dimension. A non-trivial example of an extriangulated category of global dimension
n
is given by the category of
n
-term complexes in the bounded homotopy category
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
of finitely generated projective modules over a finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
. More general extriangulated categories of global dimension
n
appeared in the recent work by Liu and Zhou
[
LZ
]
.

The case of positive global dimension
1
is quite special. This condition is equivalent to asking the bifunctor
\mathbb{E}
to be right exact in each argument. We will initiate the detailed study of such categories, which we call
hereditary
, in the upcoming sequel
[
GNP
]
. We
expect this notion to be of great importance in representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras and beyond, in particular, in
\tau
-tilting theory. The reason is that
K^{[-1,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
(where
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional algebra) carries a natural hereditary extriangulated structure, such that
\operatorname{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits\Lambda
is its costable category.
It is important to note that the (abelian) extriangulated structure on
\operatorname{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits\Lambda
is not induced by this hereditary extriangulated structure on
K^{[-1,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
whenever
\Lambda
is not hereditary.
More generally,
extended cohearts of co-t-structures
introduced by Pauksztello and Zvonareva
[
PZ
]
are hereditary. Further examples of hereditary extriangulated categories appeared in
[
PPPP
]
: these are given by
2
-Calabi–Yau triangulated categories with a cluster tilting object
T
, endowed with the relative extriangulated structure making
T
projective. More examples will be discussed in
[
GNP
]
.

Generalizing standard notions from the setting of exact categories to that of extriangulated ones, in Section
4
we define
connected sequences of functors
and
\delta
-functors
in this framework. We also consider
partial
\delta
-functors
, for which the corresponding long sequences are bounded at least on one side and exact everywhere except at the end terms. For example, each half-exact functor is a partial
\delta
-functor. More precisely, we define all these notions for sequences of bifunctors which are contravariant in one argument and covariant in the second and considered as functors in one of the arguments. This will be sufficient for our studies of positive and negative extensions.

These notions clearly depend on the choice of an extriangulated structure on an additive category. Given a connected sequence of functors for an extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, we find in Section
4.3
the maximal relative structure with respect to which the sequence becomes a
\delta
-functor. Each partial
\delta
-functor can be extended in one or two directions. For each new term, one can pose a natural universality condition. As usual, the
n
-th term of the universal
\delta
-functor having a half-exact functor
F
as the degree zero term is called its
(-n)
-th left, resp.
n
-th right derived functor, depending of the sign of
n
. If
F
is right or left exact, its derived functors on one side vanish.

In the abelian or even the exact setting, there are several ways to find derived functors of a given functor
F
. One of the construction was first considered by Cartan and Eilenberg
[
CE
]
under the name of
satellite
functors of
F
. This notion actually predates the notion of derived functors, but satisfies the same universality properties. Satellite functors are defined by an iterative procedure. The classical definition uses projective covers or injective envelopes of a functor
[
BH
,
CE
,
M
]
, see also
[
MR
]
and references therein. They can be also defined in more general situations
[
J
]
. A slightly more conceptual way to understand derived functors is to define them via projective resolutions of one argument or injective resolutions of the other. An even more conceptual approach to derived functors is to consider them as cohomologies of objects in derived categories with respect to natural
t
-structures.

In each extriangulated category, the
Hom
- and the
\mathbb{E}
-functors are at least half-exact in each argument (with respect to the extriangulated structure). In this paper, we look for derived functors of the bifunctor
Hom
. For categories having enough projective, resp. enough injective morphisms, we prove that
\mathbb{E}^{n}
is the
n
-th right derived functor of
Hom
considered as a functor in the first or in the second variable, for
n>0
. The definition of
\mathbb{E}^{n}
in
[
HLN
]
follows the second strategy above and uses projective or injective resolutions. Note that
\mathbb{E}
is the first derived functor of
Hom
precisely with respect to the extriangulated structure defined by
\mathbb{E}
, this bifunctor is not the first derived functor of
Hom
with respect to an arbitrary extriangulated structure on the underlying additive category
\mathscr{C}
.

Remark that if
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
does not come from an exact category, the
Hom
-functor is only half-exact in each argument. That means that the inflations
A\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}B
are not necessarily monomorphic, so
\mathscr{C}(-,f)\colon\mathscr{C}(-,A)\to\mathscr{C}(-,B)
may have a non-zero kernel, and dually for deflations. On the other hand, if
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
corresponds to a triangulated category, there naturally exist exact sequences in negative direction given by
\mathscr{C}(-,-[n])

(n<0)
. In Section
5
, we investigate the question of the existence of ‘natural’ negative extensions in general extriangulated categories.

There might be many possibilities to complete the partial covariant and contravariant
\delta
-functors
(\mathbb{E}^{n},n>0;\delta_{\sharp})
and
(\mathbb{E}^{n},n>0;\delta^{\sharp})
to actual
\delta
-functors. We discuss some natural options in Section
4
. One of them, first considered in
[
INP
]
has projectively, resp. injectively stable morphisms as the degree zero terms. As explained in
[
MR
]
, in the case of module categories, these can be seen as the
0
-th derived functors of the functor
Hom
; more precisely, as the
1
-st left derived functors of the functor
\mathbb{E}
. We expect
that the same happens in the general extriangulated case and these functors satisfy the relevant universal property. Let us emphasize that while
\mathbb{E}
is the
1
-st right derived functor of
Hom
(with respect to the structure
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
), the latter is not the
1
-st left derived functor of
\mathbb{E}
.

In the present paper, we are more interested in the problem of defining left derived functors of the bifunctor
Hom
, which we call the
negative extensions
. In Section
5
, we define a covariant and a contravariant connected sequences of functors in negative degrees by taking the duals of the functors
\mathbb{E}
with respect to the first, resp. to the second argument. For categories having enough projective, resp. injective morphisms, we prove that they are actually
\delta
-functors and prove their universality. Thus, in a certain reasonable generality, these give the covariant and the contravariant version of negative extensions.

The nontrivial problem in our setting is the question of
balance
. The functors
Hom
- and
\mathbb{E}
-are in general only half-exact. There is therefore no reason to expect their derived functors in the first argument to agree with their derived functors in the second argument. Also, derived categories of extriangulated categories have not yet been defined (and the naïve definition via categories of complexes fails).

Our definition of positive extensions ensures that they give derived functors in either variable, so right derived functors of
Hom
and of
\mathbb{E}
are
balanced
, i.e. one has a bivariant partial
\delta
-functor in non-negative degrees, which is universal both as a covariant and as a contravariant
\delta
-functor among those with
Hom
as the degree zero term.

For the negative extensions defined above, however, we do not have such a balance in general. We study in detail the question of the existence of the balance. We formulate and prove precise conditions under which our construction gives balanced derived functors. This happens, in particular, in all exact and all triangulated categories, but also in the case of
n
-term complexes of finitely generated projectives over a finite-dimensional algebra (Example
3.33
). One can find some natural non-examples via non-positively graded differential graded algebras.

The work of Klemenc suggests that at least for topological extriangulated categories
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, there must exist an embedding as an extension-closed subcategory into a certain triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
, namely into the homotopy category of the stable hull of the exact
\infty-
categorical enhancement of
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
. While it is not a priori unique or universal on the level of additive categories, it still makes sense to expect such an embedding to behave particularly nicely, compared to arbitrary embeddings of
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
as full extension-closed subcategories into triangulated categories.
The restriction of shifted
Hom
-functors
(\mathcal{T}(-,-[n])|_{\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathscr{C}},n\in\mathbb{Z})
, together with natural connecting morphisms, defines a bivariant
\delta
-functor on
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, which has
\mathscr{C}(-,-)
as the degree zero term. We expect that this
\delta
-functor is universal
among the bivariant
\delta
-functors having
\mathscr{C}(-,-)
as the degree zero term
. It is thus natural to expect that its terms in negative degrees give
balanced negative extensions
in the category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
. Moreover, in Section
5.5
, we conjecture that balanced negative extensions exist even beyond the topological case and give conjectural bounds on the degrees of the non-vanishing negative extensions in categories of finite positive global dimension. In general, we cannot expect the balanced negative extensions to agree with their non-balanced covariant and contravariant counterparts. We describe a conjectural relation between them. Explicit calculations of balanced negative extensions in special cases will appear in the upcoming sequel
[
GNP
]
. The partial
\delta
-functors
(\mathbb{E}^{n},n>0;\delta_{\sharp})
and
(\mathbb{E}^{n},n>0;\delta^{\sharp})
and their completions to bivariant
\delta
-functors will play an important role in the definition of Hall algebras of extriangulated categories in the forthcoming work of the first author
[
Gor
]
. Namely, they will be used to define so-called
defect-counting
bilinear forms on the split Grothendieck group of the category
\mathscr{C}
used as a part of the definition of these algebras, and also to define various bilinear forms on the Grothendieck group of the category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
yielding twists of the multiplication.

Balanced partial
\delta-
functors play a crucial role in the recent work by Adachi, Enomoto, and Tsukamoto
[
AET
]
. For an extriangulated category with a certain extra datum provided by a bifunctor and a pair of connecting morphisms, they define
s-
torsion pairs
, which form a poset in a natural way, and study intervals in this poset. They construct certain bijections generalizing those induced by HRS-tilts of
t-
structures and certain bijections related to
\tau-
tilting reduction. They call this extra datum of a bifunctor and a pair of morphisms
a negative first extension
. Let us comment on a minor difference in the notation.

(F^{-1},\delta_{\sharp},\delta^{\sharp})
is a ‘negative first extension’ in their terminology if and only if
(F^{-1},\mbox{Hom})
(or, equivalently,
(F^{-1},\mbox{Hom},\mathbb{E})
) together with connecting morphisms form a ‘bivariant partial
\delta-
functor’ in our terminology. In other words, they require the partial
\delta-
functor
(F^{-1},\mbox{Hom},\delta_{\sharp},\delta^{\sharp})
to be balanced, but do not require it to be universal. In particular, an extriangulated category may admit many different ‘negative first extensions’, in their terminology. This might explain why their datum is called
a
negative first extension, while our derived functors are called
the
negative extensions. Their work can be seen as a non-trivial indication of the importance of balanced, but not necessarily universal, ‘negative first extensions’, or, in general, of not necessarily universal bivariant (partial)
\delta-
functors.

As mentioned above, we prove the universality for positive extensions and both the acyclicity and the universality for negative extensions for categories
having enough projective (resp. injective) morphisms
. These notions appeared earlier in ideal approximation theory (
[
BM
,
FGHT
,
ZH
]
). They are slightly weaker requirements than the existence of enough projective (resp. injective) objects. We further explore these conditions and prove that, quite surprisingly, they are equivalent to many interesting properties of our category. Indeed, by Corollaries
3.26
and
6.5
, the following are equivalent:

•

(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough projective morphisms.

•

\mathbb{E}(C,-)
is finitely generated for any
C\in\mathscr{C}
.

•

\mathbb{E}^{n}(C,-)
are finitely presented for any
C\in\mathscr{C}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
.

•

The inclusion
\iota\colon\operatorname{\mathrm{def}}\nolimits\mathbb{E}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathrm{}{fp}}\nolimits\mathscr{C}
, of the category of defects into the category of finitely presented functors, has a left adjoint.

In fact, the authors first noticed the importance of some of these conditions from the perspective of the present work, and on the very late stages of work on the paper realized that they are equivalent to a previously known notion.

Projective deflations

Definition 2.8
.

A morphism

is zero. Projective morphisms form an ideal of

Remark 2.9
.

The following holds. Dually for injectives.

(1)

If either of

is a projective object, then any

is a projective morphism.

(2)

If

has enough projective objects, then a morphism is projective if and only if it factors through a projective object. In particular
\underline{\mathscr{C}}
agrees with the usual stable category of

in this case.

Definition 2.10
.

A projective morphism in
\mathscr{C}
which is also a deflation is called a

. We say that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})

has enough projective morphisms
if any

admits a projective deflation

.

Remark 2.11
.

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough projective objects, then it has enough projective morphisms.

Conversely, the following holds.

Proposition 2.12
.

If a projective deflation
G\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}C
is right minimal, then
G
is a projective object.

Proof.

Suppose that
G\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}C
is a right minimal projective deflation.
If
Y\overset{y}{\longrightarrow}G
is any deflation, then
g\circ y
is also a deflation.
By projectivity of

, there exists

such that
g\circ y\circ s=g
.
Since
g
is right minimal, it follows that
y\circ s
is an isomorphism, and that
y
is a retraction.
Hence
G
is projective.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

If every (indecomposable)

admits a right minimal projective deflation
G\to C
, then
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough projective objects.

Proof.

This is immediate from Proposition

.
∎

On the other hand, remark that if

from a projective object

This observation leads us to the following example in non-Krull–Schmidt case.

Let

Define

3.5.
Positive global dimension

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category which has
\mathbb{E}^{n}
for
n\geq 0
. More precisely, assume that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is small, or essentially small with enough projective morphisms or enough injective morphisms.

Definition 3.28
.

We say that an extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has
positive global dimension

m
, for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, if
\mathbb{E}^{m}\neq 0
and
\mathbb{E}^{m+1}=0
. Equivalently, if
\mathbb{E}^{m}\neq 0
and
\mathbb{E}^{n}=0
for any
n>m
. If such
m
does not exist, we say that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has
infinite positive global dimension
.

Remark 3.29
.

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is extriangulated and if
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is an extension-closed category, then
\mathcal{D}
can be equipped with an induced structure of an extriangulated category
(\mathcal{D},\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathcal{D}})
.
Though it satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=\mathbb{E}(X,Y)
for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}
by definition, it does not necessarily satisfy
(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}})^{n}(X,Y)\cong\mathbb{E}^{n}(X,Y)
for
n\geq 2
.

Remark that while
(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}})^{m}=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
should imply
(\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}})^{n}=0
for all
n\geq m
by definition, restrictions of
\{\mathbb{E}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
to
\mathcal{D}
do not have this property in general.
In fact, there are plenty of examples of extension-closed subcategories
\mathcal{D}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
which satisfies

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}[m])=0
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}[n])\neq 0
for
n>m>0
, as the following example shows.

Example 3.30
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an abelian category. It is well known that
{\mathcal{A}}
is the heart of the canonical
t
-structure on its bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
, and
\mbox{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{n}(A,B)={\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})(A,B[n])
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N},A,B\in{\mathcal{A}}
. If we now consider an
m
-periodic derived category
{\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}})
of
{\mathcal{A}}
(defined as the Verdier quotient of the category of
m
-periodic complexes up to homotopy by the category of
m
-periodic acyclic complexes), it does not have any
t
-structure. Since this category is periodic, we have
{\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}})(A,B[km])={\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}})(A,B)={\mathcal{A}}(A,B)
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. In general, this does not coincide with
\mbox{Ext}^{km}(A,B)
.

Example 3.31
.

(i)

For an exact category, Definition
3.28
agrees with the usual definition of the global dimension.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
A,B
be a pair of objects with
\mathcal{T}(A,B)\neq 0
. We have

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{T}}^{n}(A[n],B)=\mathcal{T}(A[n],B[n])\cong\mathcal{T}(A,B)\neq 0

for any
n>0
. Thus, any non-zero triangulated category has infinite global dimension.

Example 3.32
.

[
LZ
]

Assume that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is an extriangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives. Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be a hereditary cotorsion pair on
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
and
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{V}
. Denote
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{0}
and define by induction
\mathcal{W}_{i+1}:=\mbox{Cone}(\mathcal{W}_{i},\mathcal{W})
, for

. By
[

, Lemma 5.4]
, the category
\mathcal{W}_{i}
has positive global dimension at most

. For
i=1,
this notion generalizes
extended cohearts of co-
t-
structures on triangulated categories
, considered by Pauksztello and Zvonareva in
[

]
.

Example 3.33
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional algebra and let
\operatorname{proj}\Lambda
be the category of finitely generated projectives over

5.1.
Negative extensions given by
\mathscr{C}
-duals

Let

be a small extriangulated category.

Definition 5.1
.

For each

,

For any morphism

(2)

Using the
\mathscr{C}
-dual

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}
by the following.

•

For any

, define

by
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(C,-)=\big(\mathbb{E}^{n}(-,C)\big)^{\vee}
.

For any morphism

.

We also put
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{n}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{n}=\mathbb{E}^{n}
for positive integer

.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
be any non-negative integer.
By definition,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(C,A)=(\mathscr{C}\mbox{Mod\,})\big(\mathbb{E}^{n}(A,-),\mathscr{C}(C,-)\big)

for any

. For any

and any
\varphi\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(C,A)
, the element
a_{\ast}c^{\ast}\varphi=c^{\ast}a_{\ast}\varphi\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(C^{\prime},A^{\prime})
is given the composition of

\mathbb{E}^{n}(A^{\prime},-)\overset{a^{\ast}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}^{n}(A,-)\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{C}(C,-)\overset{-\circ c}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},-).

It can be easily checked that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathscr{C}\to\mbox{Mod\,}R
indeed forms an
R
-bilinear functor. Also remark that we naturally have
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{0}=\mathbb{E}^{0}
.
Similarly for
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{\bullet}
.

The following completes
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\bullet},\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{\bullet}
to connected sequences of functors. In non-negative degrees, these sequences coincide with the covariant, resp. contravariant partial
\delta
-functors given by
(\mathbb{E}^{n}),n\geq 0
together with the canonical connecting morphisms.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be as above.

(1)

For any
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
and any

, define

(2)

In the rest we mainly deal with

The above definitions match well with the naive ones in the following specific cases.

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and any
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
, the following holds.

(1)

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
corresponds to a triangulated category
(\mathscr{C},[1],\triangle)
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)\cong\mathscr{C}(X,Y[-n])
.

(2)

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is exact, or more generally if any inflation is monomorphic, then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)=0
.

Proof.

(1) Indeed we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathscr{C}\mbox{Mod\,})(\mathbb{E}^{n}(Y,-),\mathscr{C}(X,-))

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle(\mathscr{C}\mbox{Mod\,})(\mathscr{C}(Y[-n],-),\mathscr{C}(X,-))\cong\mathscr{C}(X,Y[-n])

by Yoneda lemma.

(2) Let
\varphi\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)=(\mathscr{C}\mbox{Mod\,})(\mathbb{E}^{n}(Y,-),\mathscr{C}(X,-))
be any element.
It suffices to show that
\varphi_{Z}(\alpha)=0
holds for any
Z\in\mathscr{C}
and any
\alpha\in\mathbb{E}^{n}(Y,Z)
. As before, by Proposition
3.9
, there exists
M\in\mathscr{C}
,
\rho\in\mathbb{E}(M,Z)
and
\theta\in\mathbb{E}^{n-1}(Y,M)
such that
\alpha=\rho\cup\theta
. Realize
\rho
to obtain an
\mathfrak{s}
-triangle
Z\overset{z}{\longrightarrow}Q\to M\overset{\rho}{\dashrightarrow}
, in which
z
is a monomorphism. By the naturality of
\varphi
,

\textstyle{\mathbb{E}^{n}(Y,Z)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}(X,Z)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{E}^{n}(Y,Q)}
\textstyle{\mathscr{C}(X,Q)}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{Z}}
\scriptstyle{z_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{z\circ-}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{Q}}
\scriptstyle{\circlearrowright}

is commutative. In particular

holds (because

), hence

.
∎

Corollary 5.29
.

Assume that
R=K
is a field and
\mathscr{C}
is
Hom
-finite over
K
, and that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough projective morphisms and enough injective morphisms. Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(i,-)=0
for any injective inflation.

(ii)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(-,p)=0
for any projective deflation.

Then
\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Y)
and
\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-2}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-2}(X,Y)
hold for any
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Take any pair of
\mathfrak{s}
-triangles
(\ref{sTri_Comparison})
as in Lemma
5.26
.
By assumption, the resulting complexes
C_{\mathrm{I}}^{\bullet},C_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{\bullet}
split in the middle. Hence the following
2
-term complexes

(5.9)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Y)\overset{\iota^{\sharp}\circ\omega_{\sharp}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}(J,Q)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Y)\overset{\omega_{\sharp}\circ\iota^{\sharp}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}(J,Q)

have isomorphic cohomologies. Similarly, so do

(5.10)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(J,Y)\overset{j^{\ast}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(I,Y)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Q)\overset{q_{\ast}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,P).

From
(\ref{2termIsoCoh1})
, we obtain
\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Y)
.

Since
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
were arbitrary, we may also apply this equality to
I,J
and
P,Q
, to obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Q)-\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,P)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Q)-\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,P)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07182pt\underset{(\ref{2termIsoCoh2})}{=}\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(J,Y)-\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(I,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(J,Y)-\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(I,Y)

from
(\ref{2termIsoCoh2})
.
Since

0\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-2}(X,Y)\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Q)\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,P)\to 0

and

0\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-2}(X,Y)\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(J,Y)\to\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(I,Y)\to 0

are exact by Theorems
5.8
and
5.10
, this means
\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-2}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-2}(X,Y)
.
∎

If we assume the existence of enough projective objects and injective objects, then we obtain equalities of dimensions in any degree. Moreover, such equalities are shown to be equivalent to (NI),(NII) in Condition
5.12
as follows.

Proposition 5.30
.

Assume that
R=K
is a field and
\mathscr{C}
is
Hom
-finite over
K
, and that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough projective objects and enough injective objects. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
satisfies conditions
(NI)
and
(NII)
.

(2)

\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(X,Y)
holds for any
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
.

(3)

\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(X,Y)
holds for any
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
and any
n>0
.

Proof.

Remark that Corollaries
5.9
and
5.11
can be applied, by assumption.
(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is shown in Corollary
5.29
. (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) is obvious, since we always have
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-1}(-,P)=0
for any projective object
P\in\mathscr{C}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-1}(I,-)=0
for any injective object
I\in\mathscr{C}
by definition. (3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is trivial.

It remains to show that (2) (
\Leftrightarrow
(1)) implies (3). Assume that (2) holds. We show

\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)=\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(X,Y)\quad(\forall X,Y\in\mathscr{C})

by an induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. By Corollary
5.29
, this holds for
n=1,2
. Assume
n\geq 3
, and suppose that we have shown for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
X,Y\in\mathscr{C}
be arbitrary, and take
\mathfrak{s}
-triangles

X\to I\to X^{\prime}\overset{\iota}{\dashrightarrow}\quad\text{and}\quad Y^{\prime}\to P\to Y\overset{\omega}{\dashrightarrow}

in which
P\in\mathscr{C}
is projective and
I\in\mathscr{C}
is injective. Then we have isomorphisms

\omega_{\sharp}\colon\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-k}(-,Y)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-(k-1)}(-,Y^{\prime})\quad\text{and}\quad\iota^{\sharp}\colon\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-k}(X,-)\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-(k-1)}(X^{\prime},-)

for any
k\geq 2
by Theorems
5.8
and
5.10
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{I}^{-n}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-(n-1)}(X,Y^{\prime})\ =\ \dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-(n-1)}(X,Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-(n-2)}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})\ =\ \dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-(n-2)}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-(n-1)}(X^{\prime},Y)\ =\ \dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-(n-1)}(X^{\prime},Y)\ =\ \dim_{K}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(X,Y)

by the hypothesis of the induction.
∎

Remark that the above (2),(3) in Proposition
5.30
are only numerical equations between
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}
. However, if we assume a slightly stronger condition than (NI),(NII) on

\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(X,Y)\cong\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(X,Y)
as in Theorem

Regarding Lemma
5.26
, we define Conditions (NI
+
) and (NII
+

+
)

Similarly to (NI),(NII), Conditions (NI

Lemma 5.33
.

(i)

Assume that the category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has enough injective objects. If we have

+

(ii)

(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})

+
)

(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
.

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})

Example 5.34
.

Conditions (NI
+
),(NII
+
) are satisfied for the category
K^{[-n,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
of complexes of length
(n+1)
of projectives
\Lambda
-modules up to homotopy, where
n\geq 0
and
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional algebra. Indeed, the case
n=0
is covered by Remark
5.32
, since the category is simply
\operatorname{proj}\Lambda
. If
n>0
, since
0
is a projective-injective object, it follows that the projective objects of
K^{[-n,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
are given precisely by complexes concentrated in degree
0
, and the injectives are given by complexes concentrated in degree
-n
.

An object
Q
appears as the first term of a dominant extension if and only if it is isomorphic to a complex in
K^{b}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
whose
-n
-th term is zero (and thus it has an inflation to
0
). But in
K^{[-n,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
, there are no non-zero maps from complexes concentrated in degree
-n
to complexes whose
-n
-th terms are zero. By
5.33
(i), the condition (NI+) is satisfied. By dual arguments, the condition (NII+) is also satisfied.

5.5.
Conjectures

An extriangulated category is called
topological
if it is equivalent to the homotopy category of an exact
\infty
-category, see
[
NP2
]
. Klemenc
[
Kl
]
constructed a
stable hull
for each exact
\infty
-category. That is, he proved that each exact
\infty
-category embeds into a stable
\infty
-category, and there is an embedding satisfying a reasonable universal property. Since the homotopy category of a stable
\infty
-category is triangulated, this shows (using
[
NP2
, Prop 4.28]
) that for each topological extriangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, there is a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
and an embedding
f\colon(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}
as an extension-closed subcategory.
This in turn indicates that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has a balanced negative extensions given by
\mathbb{E}^{-n}(A,B):=\mathcal{T}(f(A)[n],f(B))
which may have some kind of universality.
The construction of Klemenc uses quasi-categorical enhancements, and thus this morphism spaces are not easy to calculate. We make the following conjectures on the existence of balanced
\mathbb{E}^{-n}
and their relation to
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\bullet}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{\bullet}
.

Conjecture 5.40
.

Assume that
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is a topological extriangulated category. Then it admits a bivariant
\delta
-functor
(\mathbb{E}^{\bullet},\delta_{\sharp},\delta^{\sharp})
having
F^{n}=\mathbb{E}^{n}
with the canonical connecting morphisms for
n\geq 0
and universal among such bivariant
\delta
-functors. Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}^{-n}(I,-)\cong\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{-n}(I,-);\quad\mathbb{E}^{-n}(-,P)\cong\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{-n}(-,P),

for all
I
injective,
P
projective,
n>0
.

Conjecture 5.41
.

Assume that Conjecture
5.40
holds for
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
and
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has positive global dimension
n
. Then
\mathbb{E}^{-m}=0
for all
m>2n
.

For extriangulated categories for which the statement of Conjecture
5.41
holds, one might be able to define the
negative global dimension
.

Example 5.42
.

(i)

The negative global dimension of an exact category is
0
.

(ii)

Each non-zero triangulated category has infinite positive and negative global dimensions.

(iii)

If
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
has positive global dimension
n
and is such that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{m}\cong\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{m}
, for all
m
, then by definition,
\mathbb{E}^{-m}=0
for all
m>n
. Thus, the negative global dimension of
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
is
-n
.

(iv)

As a special case of the previous point, in the category
K^{[-n,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
of
n
-term complexes with finitely generated projective components over a finite-dimensional algebra, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{m}\cong\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{I\!I}}^{m}
, for all
m
. Thus, the negative global dimension is precisely
-n
.

The reader may be surprised by the bound
2n
instead of
n
. The next example explains its appearance in the conjecture.

Example 5.43
.

Let
A
be a non-positively graded dg-algebra. Consider the full extension-closed subcategory
\mathscr{C}:=A\ast A[1]\ast A[2]\ast\cdots\ast A[n]
of
\operatorname{\mathrm{per}}\nolimits A
with the induced extriangulated structure. This is an example of an extriangulated category of positive global dimension
n
. Its projective objects are given by
\mbox{add}A
and its injective objects are given by
\mbox{add}A[n]
.
Straightforward calculations show that any bivariant
\delta
-functor
F^{\bullet}
of having
F^{n}=\mathbb{E}^{n}
with the canonical connecting morphisms for
n\geq 0
has to satisfy

F^{-2n}(A,A[n])=\mbox{Hom}(A[n],A).

For an arbitrary
A
, this might be non-zero. This means the that the negative global dimension of
\mathscr{C}
can be
-2n
.

Conjectures
5.40
and
5.41
do not seem to follow easily from Klemenc’s work
[
Kl
]
. Note though that Conjecture
5.40
clearly holds for all triangulated categories, including those that do not admit enhancements, i.e. non-topological. This suggests that there is a chance that one would be able to define universal bivariant (=balanced) negative extensions for all extriangulated categories.

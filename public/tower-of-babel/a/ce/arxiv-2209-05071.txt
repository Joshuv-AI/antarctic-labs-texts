Introduction

Let

]
,
[

]
,

]

The classical approach relied heavily on vector fields integration. Initially numerous results could not be extended to the case “
\Bbbk

the zero characteristic case. The study of
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!

[
Gre.Krö.90

]

]

]

]

Let
\Bbbk
be any field, let
R_{X}
denote the quotient ring of formal power series,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, resp. analytic,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

Consider the (formal/analytic/algebraic) map-germs,
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
. The groups

\mathscr{G}f

The current paper is the next step. I construct the theory of unfoldings for
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
, the beginning of the theory of stable maps,
and prove several local Torelli-type theorems (the Mather-Yau/Gaffney-Hauser theorems in zero and positive characteristic).

§2

The classical tangent space is

§3

sets the basic notions of unfoldings (over an arbitrary field): the pullback and
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence,
the (infinitesimal)
\mathscr{G}
-triviality, the (infinitesimal)
\mathscr{G}
-versality and the (infinitesimal) stability.
These are copied verbatim from the classical case.

A remark (to avoid any confusion):

•

In the unfoldings one deforms only the map, not the source (i.e.
X
remains constant), unlike e.g.
[
Mond-Montaldi.91
]
.

•

Except for §
4.5
we consider the germs of unfoldings, the unfolding base being
(\Bbbk^{r},o)
.
Therefore (unlike e.g.
[
Gr.Ng.16
]

[

§4

treats the triviality of unfoldings.

§4.1

gives examples showing that the classical Thom-Levine criterion cannot hold in positive characteristic. Such pathological unfoldings are
“
\mathscr{G}
-inseparable”. The relation to the (in)separability of the group orbit map is explained in §
A
.

§4.2

contains the Thom-Levine theorem, “A separable unfolding is locally trivial iff it is infinitesimally-trivial, i.e.
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}
”.
(To repeat, in zero characteristic all unfoldings are separable.)

§4.3

gives an application of this triviality criterion to the factorization, in
char(\Bbbk)=0
:

i.

If the germ
X
admits
r
vector fields that are linearly independent at the origin then
X\cong{\tilde{X}}\times(\Bbbk^{r},o)
.

ii.

Moreover, suppose a map
f:X\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
“does not see” these vector fields, i.e.
T_{\mathscr{R}}f=T_{\mathscr{R}^{(o)}}f
, resp.
T_{\mathscr{K}}\!f=T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(o)}}f
, resp.

T_{\mathscr{A}}f=T_{\mathscr{R}^{(o)}}f+T_{\mathscr{L}}f
. (See §
2.2
.ii.)
Then
f
factorizes as
X\to{\tilde{X}}\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
, i.e. is
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent to the pullback of a map
{\tilde{X}}\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
.

Here part a. is well known for
\mathbb{C}
-analytic germs, but the algebraic version (over
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
) seems new.
Part b. seems new in all cases.

§4.4

is about the rank of a map
f:X\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
and the corresponding “preliminary form”, splitting
f
into its linear part and the part of order
\geq 2
.

§4.5

The unfolding trivializations of §
4.2
are local, they hold over the germ of parameter space
(\Bbbk^{r}_{t},o)
.
In the
\Bbbk
-analytic case one gets the trivialization over small balls,
o\in Ball_{\epsilon}\subset\Bbbk^{r}
. In the algebraic case (
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
)
one gets the trivialization over étale neighborhoods of
o\in\Bbbk^{r}
. But in some cases one works over a global base, e.g.
R_{X,t}
is one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[t][[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[t]\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[t]\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
. Then one wants the global trivialization, over the whole
\Bbbk^{r}
. This is possible if one
restricts to the filtration-unipotent subgroup
\mathscr{G}^{(1)}<\mathscr{G}
.
In §
4.5
we prove: If
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{t}}f_{t}
then
the (separable) unfolding
(f_{t},t)
is globally trivial.

The weaker condition
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{t}}f_{t}
implies the weaker statement: all the fibres are
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent.

§5

treats the (local) versality of unfoldings.

§5.1

gives the pre-normal form: any unfolding
(f_{t},t)

is formally
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent to the unfolding
(f_{o}+\sum a_{j}(t)v_{j},t)
, where
\{v_{\bullet}\}
go to the generators of the
\Bbbk
-vector space
T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
.

§5.2

As a simple corollary one gets: an unfolding is versal iff it is infinitesimally versal.

§5.3

Recall that the orbit of contact group,
{\mathscr{K}}\!f
, is essentially larger than that of the left-right group,
\mathscr{A}f
. Hence several
old questions: “How does a
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-orbit split into
\mathscr{A}
-orbits?”, “When do these orbits coincide?”,
“For which maps is
\mathscr{A}f\subset{\mathscr{K}}\!f
an open dense subset?”.
In §
5.3
we address the local version:
How does a
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-trivial unfolding split into a family of
\mathscr{A}
-unfoldings?

§5.4

treats the global version:
“How does a
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-orbit split into a family of
\mathscr{A}
-orbits?”.

§6

extends several results of Mather on stable maps to the maps
X\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
, in arbitrary characteristic.

§6.1

A map is locally stable iff it is infinitesimally stable, i.e.
T_{\mathscr{A}}f=R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

§6.2

Stable maps are unfoldings of their genotypes, i.e. each stable map is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to
(f_{o}+\sum u_{i}v_{i},u)
, where
the elements
\{v_{\bullet}\}
generate the vector space
(x)\cdot T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f_{o}
.

§6.3

Stable maps are determined by their local algebras. Namely, two stable maps are
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent iff their genotypes are
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-equivalent.

extends the classical results of
[
Mather-Yau
]
,
[
Gaffney-Hauser.85
]
,
[
Scherk.83
]

\Bbbk

2.1.
Rings and germs

i.

In this paper
R_{X}
is one of the rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
.
Here:

•

\Bbbk
is any field, for
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
we assume
\Bbbk
to be normed and complete with respect to its norm.

•

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
J\subseteq(x)^{2}
.

We denote the images of
\{x_{i}\}
in
R_{X}
by the same letters, this causes no confusion.
E.g. the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset R_{X}
is generated by
\{x_{i}\}
, sometimes we write
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x)
.

When
J\neq 0
we always assume the following
jet_{0}
-condition.
Take a derivation
\xi\in Der_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})
, present it as
\sum c_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}
. Suppose
\xi
vanishes at the origin to the second order,
i.e.
c_{\bullet}(x)\in(x)^{2}\subset R_{X}
. If
R_{X}
is regular, i.e.
J=0
, then the map
\Phi:x\to x+\xi(x)

defines a coordinate change,
\Phi\in Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})
.
In the non-regular case the map
x\to x+\xi(x)
is not an automorphism of
R_{X}
.

\boldsymbol{jet_{0}}
assumption:
any derivation
\xi\in Der^{(1)}_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})
induces an automorphism
x\to x+\xi(x)+h(x)
, where
h(x)
is a higher order term in the following sense:

(h(x))\subseteq(\{c_{\bullet}(x)\})^{2}\subset R_{X}
.

This
jet_{0}
-assumption holds if
char(\Bbbk)=0
,
[
B.G.K.22
]
. But in positive characteristic this is a non-trivial condition.

ii.

The source of a map is the scheme-germ
X:=Spec(R_{X})
. The target is the germ
(\Bbbk^{p},o):=Spec(R_{Y})
, where
R_{Y}
is
\Bbbk[[y]]
, resp.
\Bbbk\{y\}
,
resp.
\Bbbk\langle y\rangle
.
Fix some local coordinates in the target
(\Bbbk^{p},o)
, then the space of maps
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
is identified with the
R_{X}
-module
{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Given a map
f:X\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
take its dual
f^{\sharp}:R_{Y}\to R_{X}
and
f^{\sharp}:R^{\oplus p}_{Y}\to R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.
For a submodule
\Lambda_{Y}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{Y}
we distinguish between the image
f^{\sharp}(\Lambda_{Y})\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
and the pullback

f^{*}(\Lambda_{Y}):=R_{X}\cdot f^{\sharp}(\Lambda_{Y})\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

iii.

To work with deformations/unfoldings we take
\mathbbm{K}
as one of the rings
\Bbbk[[t]]
,

\Bbbk\{t\}
,
\Bbbk\langle t\rangle
, here
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
.
Accordingly we extend the
\Bbbk
-algebras
R_{X},R_{Y}
to
the
\mathbbm{K}
-algebras,
R_{X,t}
is one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbbm{K}[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbbm{K}\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbbm{K}\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, and
R_{Y,t}
is one of

\mathbbm{K}[[y]]
,
\mathbbm{K}\{y\}
,
\mathbbm{K}\langle y\rangle
.

iv.

We often use the Nakayama lemma over a local ring: if
M=C+{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot M
(for a finitely generated
M
-module and its subset
C
) then
M=C
.

v.

The nested version of Artin approximation,
[
Rond.18
, §5.2]
.

2.2.
The groups
\mathscr{G}\circlearrowright Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
and tangent spaces
T_{\mathscr{G}}

i.

The group of right equivalences consists of the coordinate changes in the source,
\mathscr{R}:=Aut_{X}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{X})
.
The action
\mathscr{R}\circlearrowright Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
is given by
f\to\Phi_{X}(f):=f\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1}
.

Similarly the group of left equivalences,
\mathscr{L}:=Aut_{(\Bbbk^{p},o)}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(R_{Y})\circlearrowright Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)
, acts by
f\to\Phi_{Y}(f)
.
Then group of left-right transformations is
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{L}\times\mathscr{R}
.

Recall that the orbits of the contact group,
{\mathscr{K}}\!:=\mathscr{C}\rtimes\mathscr{R}
,
coincide with the orbits of a much smaller group,
{\mathscr{K}}\!^{lin}:=GL(p,R_{X})\rtimes Aut_{X}
,
see
[
Kerner.21
, §3.2]
.

When working with families, i.e.
R_{X,t}
,
R_{Y,t}
, are
\mathbbm{K}
-algebras, we take the
\mathbbm{K}
-linear automorphisms,
Aut_{X,t}:=Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{X,t})
,

Aut_{Y,t}:=Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{Y,t})
.

The tangent spaces to the groups
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!,\mathscr{A}
are defined (and studied) in
[
Kerner.21
, §3]
.
As we mentioned in
\lx@sectionsign\ref{Sec.Intro.Contents}
,

T_{\mathscr{G}}
denotes the
extended
tangent space:

(1)

T_{\mathscr{R}}f=Der_{X}f,\quad\quad\quad T_{\mathscr{L}}f=f^{\sharp}(R^{\oplus p}_{Y}),\quad\quad\quad T_{\mathscr{K}}\!f=T_{\mathscr{R}}f+(f)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X},\quad\quad\quad T_{\mathscr{A}}f=T_{\mathscr{R}}f+T_{\mathscr{L}}f.

ii.

Take the filtration
I^{\bullet}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
on
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)\cong{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
. We get the associated filtrations,
the subgroups
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{G}^{(-1)}\geq\mathscr{G}^{(0)}\geq\mathscr{G}^{(1)}\geq\cdots
and the submodules

T_{\mathscr{G}}=T_{\mathscr{G}^{(-1)}}\supseteq T_{\mathscr{G}^{(0)}}\supseteq T_{\mathscr{G}^{(1)}}\supseteq\cdots
.
Here:

(2)

T_{\mathscr{R}^{(j)}}:=\{\xi\in Der_{X}|\ \xi(I^{\bullet})\subseteq I^{\bullet+j},\ \forall\ \bullet\geq 0\},\quad\quad T_{\mathscr{L}^{(j)}}f=f^{\#}((y)^{j+1}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{Y}),

T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(j)}}f=T_{\mathscr{R}^{(j)}}f+I^{j-1}(f)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X},\quad\quad\quad T_{\mathscr{A}^{(j)}}f=T_{\mathscr{R}^{(j)}}f+T_{\mathscr{L}^{(j)}}f.

E.g. if
R_{X}
regular (i.e.
J=0
), and
I={\mathfrak{m}}
, then the space
T_{\mathscr{G}^{(0)}}
is the “classical” tangent space.

iii.

Take a
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-finite map
f\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
. Fix some elements
\{v_{\bullet}\}
in
{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
that go to a basis of the
\Bbbk
-vector
space
{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
, here
T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f:={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{K}}\!f$}\right.}
.
Thus
Span_{\Bbbk}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{R}}f+(f)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}+\Bbbk^{p}=R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.
The following presentation is used often.

Lemma 2.1
.

a.

Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f=R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

b.

If

(f)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\subset R_{X}

then

Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{R}}f+T_{\mathscr{L}^{(0)}}f={\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Proof.
a.
We have
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$(Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f)$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}+(f)\cdot{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}
.
Consider
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}
as an
R_{Y}
-module. It is finitely generated, because
f
is
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-finite,
[
Kerner.21
, §3]
.
Therefore (by Nakayama over
R_{Y}
) we get

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}f$}\right.}
. Hence
Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f=R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Now b. follows from a.

\blacksquare

2.3.

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.},{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

Take the ring extension (in the formal case)

Given

we get the group
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbbm{K}}
,
e.g.
\mathscr{R}_{\mathbbm{K}}=Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{X,\mathbbm{K}})

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{A}

1.

Proof.
It is enough to consider only the case of
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
. For the rings
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
and the groups

§

.

1.

Suppose

for some
g_{\mathbbm{K}}\in\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{\mathbbm{K}}

g_{\mathbbm{K}}=e^{\xi^{\mathbbm{K}}_{d}}
, here

(3)

[\xi^{\mathbbm{K}}_{d}f]\in{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{4.13332pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}+\mathbbm{K}\hat{\otimes}M_{ord(f)+d+1}$}\!\!\diagup\!\!\raisebox{-4.13332pt}{$\mathbbm{K}\hat{\otimes}M_{ord(f)+d+1}$}\right.}\subset\mathbbm{K}\hat{\otimes}{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{4.13332pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!\!\diagup\!\!\raisebox{-4.13332pt}{$M_{ord(f)+d+1}$}\right.}.

. Therefore

\xi^{\mathbbm{K}}_{d+1}\in\mathbbm{K}\hat{\otimes}T_{\mathscr{G}^{(1)}}

{\tilde{f}}

Eventually we get the infinite product

.
We claim: this limit exists. Indeed,

. Then by the Artin-Rees type property,
[
Kerner.21
, §6]
,

.

Altogether we have

. This proves the statement for
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
.

2.

(We show only the case of
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence, the other cases are similar.) We should resolve the condition
\Phi_{Y}\circ f_{0}\circ\Phi_{X}=f_{1}
.
Take the Taylor expansions,
\Phi_{X}(x)=\sum_{|m|\geq 1}C^{(x)}_{m}x^{m}
and
\Phi_{Y}(y)=\sum_{|m|\geq 1}C^{(y)}_{m}y^{m}
. Here

is the multi-index, and

\{C^{(x)}_{m}\}
,
\{C^{(y)}_{m}\}
are unknowns. By comparing the coefficients of the monomials, the condition

(and

) is transformed into a countable system of polynomials equations,
\{P_{j}(\{C^{(x)}_{*}\},\{C^{(y)}_{*}\})=0\}_{j}
.
Each polynomial here is in a finite number of variables.

This system is solvable over
\mathbbm{K}
. We claim: each finite subsystem of

is solvable over
\bar{\Bbbk}
.
Indeed, this subsystem defines a subscheme in an affine space over
{\bar{\Bbbk}}
.
If this finite subsystem is not solvable then this subscheme has no points over
{\bar{\Bbbk}}
.
By Hilbert Nullstellensatz we get: the defining ideal of this subscheme is the whole polynomial ring of the affine space.
And then this finite subsystem can have
no solutions over
\mathbbm{K}
.

Finally we apply the assumptions on
\Bbbk
.

•

Suppose
\bar{\Bbbk}
is uncountable. In this case a countable system of polynomial equations over
{\bar{\Bbbk}}
is solvable iff each finite subsystem if solvable,

[
Popescu-Rond.19

. Thus the solvability over

implies that over
\bar{\Bbbk}
.

•

If
char(\Bbbk)=0
and
\sqrt{I}={\mathfrak{m}}
, then it is enough to take only finite expansions in

,

\Phi_{Y}(y)=\sum_{|m|\geq 1}C^{(y)}_{m}y^{m}

f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}_{\bar{\Bbbk}}}}{{\sim}}{\tilde{f}}

with
{\tilde{f}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{\mathbbm{K}}}}{{\sim}}f

\blacksquare

Remark 2.3

The first statement fails in positive characteristic. For example, let

Then

3.
The basic notions of unfolding

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

\mathbbm{K}
and

3.1.

Definition 3.1
.

An unfolding of a map

is the map
F:X\times(\Bbbk^{r},o){\to}(\Bbbk^{p},o)\times(\Bbbk^{r},o)
of the form

,
i.e. an element
(f_{t},t)\in({\mathfrak{m}}+(t))\cdot R^{\oplus(p+r)}_{X,t}

f_{o}=f
.

Take the actions
\mathscr{R},\mathscr{L}\circlearrowright{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.
Accordingly we have the actions
\mathscr{R}_{t},\mathscr{L}_{t}\circlearrowright({\mathfrak{m}}+(t))\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
, where

(4)

\mathscr{R}_{t}:=\{\Phi_{X,t}|\ \Phi_{X,o}=Id_{X}\}<Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{X,t}),\quad\quad\quad\quad\mathscr{L}_{t}:=\{\Phi_{Y,t}|\ \Phi_{Y,o}=Id_{Y}\}<Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{Y,t}).

Geometrically we have the coordinate changes
\mathscr{R}_{t}\circlearrowright X\times(\Bbbk^{r},o)
and
\mathscr{L}_{t}\circlearrowright(\Bbbk^{p+r},o)

that restrict to identities on the central fibres

,
\Bbbk^{p}\times\{o\}

t=const
slices,

,
(\Bbbk^{p},o)\times\{t\}
.
Equivalently: the elements of
\mathscr{R}_{t}
,
\mathscr{L}_{t}
are unfoldings of identity maps,
(x,t)\to(x,t)
and

Similarly one extends the groups

(5)

These groups act on families of maps,
\mathscr{G}_{t}\circlearrowright Maps(X\times(\Bbbk^{r},o),(\Bbbk^{p+r},o))

(for each

(resp.

The actions of

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}=(t)\cdot{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}+{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Lambda_{t}+T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}
.
Note:
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbbm{K}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(t)$}\right.}\otimes{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}\cong{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}$}\right.}
, the later quotient being finitely generated over
\Bbbk
(resp.
R_{Y}
).
Therefore
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}
is finitely generated by Weierstraß finiteness,
[
Kerner.21
, §2]
.
Finally, Nakayama over
\mathbbm{K}
(resp.
R_{Y,t}
) gives
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Lambda_{t}+T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}
.

\blacksquare

4.
Triviality of unfolding

4.1.

As in the classical case,
R_{X}=\mathbb{C}\{x\}
, one wants to establish Thom-Levine’s criterion, “triviality
vs infinitesimal triviality”. This does not hold in full generality because of the positive characteristic obstructions.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic
p
and take
R_{X}=\Bbbk[[x]]
,
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{R}
.

i.

The unfolding
f_{t}(x)=x^{n}+t^{p}x
is non-trivial, even though
\partial_{t}f_{t}=0\in T_{\mathscr{R}_{t}}f_{t}
. Note that
f_{t}
is obtained by the
t\to t^{p}
base-change
from
x^{n}+tx
.

The unfolding
f_{t}(x)=x^{n}+t^{p}x^{p}+tx^{d}\in\Bbbk[[t,x]]
, for
p<n<d
, is non-trivial, with
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{R}_{t}}f_{t}
.
And this is not a
t\to t^{p}
base change of another unfolding. Here one can take
d\gg n
, i.e. to ensure
\partial_{t}F\in T_{\mathscr{R}^{(j)}}F
for
j\gg 1
.

ii.

Consider the unfolding
f_{t}(x)=x^{p}+x^{p+d}(1+t)\in\Bbbk[[t,x]]
, with
gcd(d,p)=1
.
Then
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in x\cdot T_{\mathscr{R}_{t}}f_{t}
, but
f_{t}
is not an
\mathscr{R}
-trivial unfolding. Indeed, for a coordinate change
x\to x+x\cdot h(t,x)
one has

f_{t}(x+x\cdot h(t,x))=x^{p}+x^{p}\cdot h(t,x)^{p}+x^{p+d}(1+h(t,x))^{p+d})(1+t)
. If this coordinate change trivializes
f_{t}
then we must have

ord_{x}(x^{p}\cdot h(t,x)^{p})>p
. But then necessarily
ord_{x}(x^{p}\cdot h(t,x)^{p})=ord(x^{p+d}\cdot t)=p+d
, contradicting the divisibility

p|ord_{x}(x^{p}\cdot h(t,x)^{p})
.

4.2.
Infinitesimal vs local triviality of unfoldings

In view of example
4.1
we introduce:

Definition 4.2
.

An unfolding of the map
f_{o}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
is called
\mathscr{G}
-inseparable if
f_{t}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}_{t}}}{{\sim}}f_{o}(x)+t^{d}\cdot f_{d}(x)+(t^{d+1})\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
,
where
char(\Bbbk)\mid d
and
f_{d}\not\in T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
.

Thus in zero characteristic all unfoldings are separable.
The name “separable” is due to the relation to separability of group orbit map, see §
A
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
see §
2.1
.i.
Let
\mathscr{G}\in\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!,\mathscr{A}
. Take an unfolding
F(x,t)=(f_{t}(x),t)
, for
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})\in(\Bbbk^{r},o)
.

1.

If
F
is trivial then it is infinitesimally trivial, i.e.
\partial_{t_{1}}f_{t},\dots,\partial_{t_{r}}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}
.

2.

Suppose
F
is infinitesimally-
\mathscr{G}
-trivial. For
char(\Bbbk)>0
assume that
F
is
\mathscr{G}
-separable. Then:

•

(for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!
)
F
is trivial.

•

(for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{A}
)
F
is formally-
\mathscr{A}
-trivial. Moreover:

–

if
char(\Bbbk)=0
and
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, then the unfolding
F
is
\mathscr{A}
-trivial.

–

if the map
f_{o}\in R^{\oplus p}_{X}
is
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-finitely determined (as an element of
R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
), then the unfolding
F
is
\mathscr{A}
-trivial.

Proof.
First we reduce the case of
r
-parameters to the one-parameter unfolding.

•

(Part 1.) Consider
(f_{t},t_{r})
as a one-parameter unfolding of the map
f_{t}|_{t_{r}=0}\in R^{\oplus p}_{X,t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}}
.
Here
R^{\oplus p}_{X,t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}}
is the algebra over
\mathbbm{K}=\Bbbk[[t_{1}\dots t_{r-1}]]
, resp.
\Bbbk\{t_{1}\dots t_{r-1}\}
,
resp
\Bbbk\langle t_{1}\dots t_{r-1}\rangle
.
This unfolding is still trivial,
therefore (assuming the case
r=1
) it is infinitesimally trivial,
\partial_{t_{r}}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}
. Repeating this for all
t_{i}
we get the statement.

•

(Part 2.) Consider
(f_{t},t_{r})
as a one-parameter unfolding of the map
f_{t}|_{t_{r}=0}\in R^{\oplus p}_{X,t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}}
.
This unfolding is still infinitesimally trivial,

\partial_{t_{r}}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{t}
.
Therefore (assuming the case
r=1
) it can be trivialized. Namely,
g^{(r)}_{t}f_{t}(x)

depends on the variables
(t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1},x)
only, for an element
g^{(r)}_{t}\in\mathscr{G}_{t}
that acts as identity on
t_{1},\dots,t_{r-1}
.
Iterate this argument to get the full trivialization:
(g^{(1)}_{t}\cdot\cdots\cdot g^{(r)}_{t})f_{t}(x)=f_{o}(x)
.

Therefore, below we consider only one-parameter unfoldings.

The proof of part 1 is characteristic-free.
For part 2 we give an additional proof in
char(\Bbbk)=0
case.

1.

•

The
\mathscr{R}
-case.
Take the trivialization:
f_{t}=\Phi_{X,t}(f_{o})
for
\Phi_{X,t}\in Aut_{\mathbbm{K}}(R_{X,t})

with
\Phi_{X,o}=Id
, see §
2.1
.iii.
We should prove:
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{R}_{t}}f_{t}

Define the operator
\xi_{X}:=\partial_{t}-\Phi_{X,t}\circ\partial_{t}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{X,t}

We claim: this is a

Moreover, this action is

Now we verify the Leibniz rule. For any

Finally we observe:
T_{\mathscr{R}_{t}}f_{t}\ni\xi_{X}(f_{t})=\partial_{t}f_{t}-\Phi_{t}\partial_{t}f_{o}=\partial_{t}f_{t}
.

•

The
{\mathscr{K}}\!

(As always, we replace
{\mathscr{K}}\!
by
{\mathscr{K}}\!^{lin}

Suppose

Now we verify:

(10)

\xi_{X}(f_{t})=\partial_{t}f_{t}-\Phi_{X,t}\circ\partial_{t}(U\cdot f_{o})=\partial_{t}f_{t}-\Phi_{X,t}(\partial_{t}U\cdot U^{-1})\cdot f_{t}.

\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}
.

The
\mathscr{A}

Present the trivial unfolding in the form

Define the operator

Here

We observe:

(12)

\Psi(h(t))=(\Phi^{\prime}_{Y,t})^{-1}|_{h(t)}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{Y,t}(h(t))=(\Phi^{\prime}_{Y,t})^{-1}|_{h(t)}\cdot\Big[(\partial_{t}\Phi_{Y,t})|_{h(t)}+\Phi_{Y,t}^{\prime}|_{h(t)}\cdot\frac{\partial h(t)}{\partial t}\Big]=

(\Psi-\xi_{Y}-\frac{d}{dt})(\mathbbm{K}^{\oplus p})=0

(13)

(\Psi-\xi_{Y}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t})h|_{(x,t)}=(\Phi^{\prime}_{Y,t})^{-1}|_{h}\cdot\Phi^{-1}_{X,t}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Phi_{Y,t}|_{\Phi_{X,t}(h)})-\xi_{Y}(h)-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h=

The case
\mathbf{char(\Bbbk)=0}
,
\mathscr{G}\!\in\!\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!

•

The
\mathscr{R}

In this ring we have:

[
Kerner.21

The
{\mathscr{K}}\!

The
\mathscr{A}

4.3.

The map of spaces

ball of convergence in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Therefore one should restricts to the subgroup
\mathscr{G}^{(o)}_{t}\leq\mathscr{G}_{t}
of elements that
preserve the origins of

.

•

The filtered-nilpotent vector fields can be “integrated”, i.e. there exists a map
T_{\mathscr{G}^{(1)}}\to\mathscr{G}^{(1)}
that approximates the classical exponential,

[
B.G.K.22

. But not every element

induces an element

satisfying:

.
For example, for

Therefore below we restrict to the subgroup
\mathscr{G}^{(1)}_{t}<\mathscr{G}_{t}
.
For the subgroup

.

4.5.1.
Trivialization for the subgroup
\mathscr{G}^{(1)}<\mathscr{G}

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
R_{X,t}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[t][[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

\Bbbk

Let

. Any infinitesimally
\mathscr{G}^{(1)}
-trivial unfolding is
t
-globally
\mathscr{G}^{(1)}
-trivial.

Proof.
We use the trivializations constructed in the proof of theorem
4.3
, equations
(
14
), (
15
), (
16
).
We should only verify: the derivation
\zeta_{X}
constructed via

belongs to

.
In particular, in its
(t,x)
-expansion all the coefficients of

Indeed, by the BCH formula,
[

,

•

[T_{\mathscr{R}^{(i)}_{t}},T_{\mathscr{R}^{(j)}_{t}}]\subseteq T_{\mathscr{R}^{(i+j)}_{t}}

•

For each

-commutator

Therefore every summand that appears in

\blacksquare

4.5.2.
Comparison of fibres for the subgroup
\mathscr{G}^{(0)}\leq\mathscr{G}

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

Step 1.

We prove the statement for
\Bbbk\in\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
, and the ring
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}

(in
\Bbbk
).
Along this path (at every point on it)

F
is

\mathscr{G}
-trivializable on a small ball

is
\mathscr{G}^{(0)}
-trivializable, see remark
4.5
.ii.

to

Step 2.

(The general case,
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}

Let

\mathscr{R}^{(o)}
and

For

5.
The pre-normal form of unfolding and versality

5.1.

Let
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, see §
2.1
, and fix a map
f_{o}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
, not necessarily
\mathscr{G}
-finite.
We get the
\Bbbk
-vector space
T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}:={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}$}\right.}
, possibly of infinite dimension.
Fix (any) elements
\{v_{\bullet}\}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
whose images formally generate the
\Bbbk
-vector space
T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
.
Namely, any element of
T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
is
presentable as
\sum c_{\bullet}v_{\bullet}
, with
c_{\bullet}\in\Bbbk
, and this sum converges in
R_{X}
.
For
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{A}
we always assume
\{v_{\bullet}\}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Definition 5.1
.

The
\mathscr{G}
-pre-normal form of an unfolding of
f_{o}
is the unfolding
(f_{o}(x)+\sum a_{j}(t)\cdot v_{j}(x),t)
, where

a_{j}(t)\in(t)\cdot\Bbbk[[t]]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Any unfolding
F(t,x)=(f_{t}(x),t)\in R^{\oplus(p+r)}_{X,t}
is (formally)
\mathscr{G}
-equivalent to its pre-normal form.
Moreover, this
\mathscr{G}
-equivalence preserves the image of
Span_{\Bbbk}[\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t=o}]
inside
T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
, i.e.

(21)

T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}+Span_{\Bbbk}[\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t=o}]=T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}+Span_{\Bbbk}[\sum\partial_{t}a_{j}(t)|_{t=o}\cdot v_{j}(x)]\subseteq R^{\oplus p}_{X}.

Proof.
The transition to the pre-normal form is inductive.
Using the generators
\{v_{\bullet}\}
we can present
F(t,x)=(f_{o}+\xi^{(d)}f_{o}+\sum a^{(d)}_{j}(t)v_{j},t)
.
Here
1\leq d<\infty
and
0\neq\xi^{(d)}f_{o}\in(t)^{d}\cdot T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
, and
a^{(d)}_{\bullet}(t)\in(t)\subset\Bbbk[[t]]
.
Take a group element
g_{d}\in\mathscr{G}_{t}
of the form
g=Id-\xi^{(d)}+(t)^{d+1}
.
(This is ensured by the
jet_{0}
-assumption on
R_{X}
, see §
2.1
.)
Then
g_{d}(F)=(f_{o}+\xi^{(d+1)}f+\sum a^{(d+1)}_{j}(t)v_{j},t)
, for some new power series
\{a^{(d+1)}_{\bullet}(t)\}
.
Iterate this process. Note:
a^{(d+1)}_{j}(t)-a^{(d)}_{j}(t)\in(t)^{d}
.

The infinite product
\lim_{d\to\infty}(g_{d}\cdots g_{1})
converges (formally) to an element
g\in\mathscr{G}
.
Indeed, at
d
’th step we
do not change the
t^{i}
-elements with
i<d
.

Altogether, the unfolding
gF
is in the pre-normal form.
Finally we verify equation (
21
).

•

g_{1}
changes
Span_{\Bbbk}[\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t=o}]
only by an element of
T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
. Hence
g_{1}

preserves
T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}+Span_{\Bbbk}[\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t=o}]
.

•

For
d\geq 2
the element
g_{d}
does not affect
\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t=o}
.

\blacksquare

.

i.

The pre-normal form is usually far from being unique. E.g. suppose

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
. If
char(\Bbbk)>0
then assume

.
Any
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-trivial unfolding
(f_{t}(x),t)
of
f_{o}\in(x)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
is formally-
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-equivalent to its
\mathscr{A}
-pre-normal form.

Moreover, if the map
f_{t}\in(x)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
is
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-finitely determined then the unfolding
(f_{t}(x),t)
is
\mathscr{A}_{t}
-equivalent to its pre-normal form.

In this sense the unfolding
(f_{o}+\sum t_{j}v_{j},t)
is versal among all the
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-trivial unfoldings.

Proof.
As the unfolding
(f_{t}(x),t)
is
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-trivial, we can assume (by an
\mathscr{R}_{t}
-transformation):
(f_{t}(x))=(f_{o}(x))\subset R_{X,t}
.
Then
R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}\ni f_{t}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle GL(p,R_{X,t})}}{{\sim}}f_{o}(x)
. Thus after a
GL(p,\Bbbk[[t]])
-transformation, we can assume:

f_{t}-f_{o}\in(t)\cdot(x)\cdot(f_{o})\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}=(t)\cdot(Span_{R_{Y,t}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f_{o}\cap(x)\cdot(f_{o})\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}).

Therefore
f_{t}-f_{o}-t(\xi_{X}(f_{o})+\xi_{Y}(y)|_{f_{o}})\in(t)\cdot Span_{R_{Y,t}}\{v_{\bullet}\}
, for some derivations
\xi_{X}\in T_{\mathscr{R}},\xi_{Y}\in T_{\mathscr{L}}
.

If
R_{X}
is regular (i.e.
J=0
) then define the coordinate change
\Phi_{X,t}\in\mathscr{R}_{t}
by
x\to x+t\xi_{X}(x)
.
In the general case define
\Phi_{X,t}
as the extension
x\to x+t\xi_{X}(x)+(t)^{2}
of the map
x\to x+t\xi_{X}(x)
.
(Our assumptions ensure the
jet_{0}
-condition.) We remark that
\Phi_{X,t}
does not necessarily preserve the origin of
the source, i.e.
\Phi_{X,t}(x)\neq(x)\subset R_{X,t}
.

Define
\Phi_{Y,t}\in\mathscr{L}_{t}
by

. It does not necessarily preserve the origin of the target,
\Phi_{Y,t}(y)\neq(y)\subset R_{Y,t}
.
Altogether we get:

Iterate this process to get the formal
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence,
(f_{t},t)\sim(f_{o}+(t)\cdot Span_{R_{Y,t}}\{v_{\bullet}\},t)
.

Suppose
f_{t}
is
\mathscr{A}
-finitely determined.
We have proved:
f_{o}+Span_{R_{Y}}(\{v_{\bullet}\})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{A}_{t}}}{{\sim}}f_{t}+(t)^{d}
for
d\gg 1
. Now invoke the finite determinacy.

\blacksquare

{\mathscr{K}}\!

2.1
:

-finitely determined maps.

Proposition 5.10
.

Suppose
\Bbbk
is an infinite field. Then:

{\mathscr{K}}\!F\cap(\mathscr{A}\text{-finitely determined})\subseteq\mathscr{A}\Big(\{f\}+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}],u\Big).

Proof.
Suppose a map
\tilde{F}\in{\mathscr{K}}\!F
is
\mathscr{A}
-finitely determined. Then to bring it to the claimed form it is enough to work
over
R_{X,u}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x,u]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
and
R_{Y,u}=\Bbbk[[y,u]]
.

Step 1.

By an
\mathscr{R}
-transformation we can assume
(\tilde{F})=(F)\subset R_{X,u}
.
Then, by lemma
4.11
, we can assume

\tilde{F}=({\tilde{f}}(x,u),u)\in R^{\oplus(p+r)}_{X,u}
, with
f,{\tilde{f}}\in(x)\cdot(x,u)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}
. Here
({\tilde{f}},u)=(f,u)\subset R_{X,u}
.
Apply
GL(p,\Bbbk[[u]])
to get:
{\tilde{f}}-f\in(x)\cdot(f,u)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}
.

By lemma
2.1
we have
(x)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}=T_{\mathscr{R}}f_{o}+T_{\mathscr{L}^{(0)}}f_{o}+Span_{R_{Y}}(\{v_{\bullet}\})
.
Therefore

(23)

(x,u)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}=T_{\mathscr{R}}f+(y,u)\cdot T_{\mathscr{L}}f+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[\{v_{\bullet}\}].

Hence

{\tilde{f}}-f\in Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\{v_{\bullet}\}]+(f,u)T_{\mathscr{R}}f+(y,u)^{2}\cdot T_{\mathscr{L}}f
.

As
\tilde{F}
is
\mathscr{A}
-finitely determined, it is enough to
prove:
\tilde{F}+h_{d}\in\mathscr{A}\big(f+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}],u\big)
for some

, with

.
Therefore it is enough to prove:

(24)

\tilde{F}\in\mathscr{A}\big(f+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}],u\big)+(x,u)^{N}\cdot R^{\oplus(p+r)}_{X,u}\quad\text{ for }N\gg 1.

Step 2.

Start from
{\tilde{f}}=f+tg
, where
g\in(x)\cdot(f,u)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}
, and
t
is an indeterminate.
The transition to the form of (
24
) is done inductively.
By equation (
23
)
we can present
g\in Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}]+(f,u)^{d}\cdot T_{\mathscr{R}}f+(y,u)^{d+1}\cdot T_{\mathscr{L}}f
for some

.

•

Inductive step for the case of a regular ring,
\boldsymbol{R_{X,u}=\Bbbk[[x,u]]}
.
Define the coordinate change
\Phi_{X}\in\mathscr{R}
by
x\to x+t(f,u)^{d}\cdot c\cdot\xi(x)
and
u\to u
.
(Here

is an unknown.)
Note: this coordinate change is filtration-unipotent, i.e.
\Phi_{X}-Id
is filtration-nilpotent for

Accordingly

\Phi_{X}(f+tg)-f\in t(f,u)^{d}(c+c^{(g)})T_{\mathscr{R}}f+t^{2}\cdot(f,u)^{d+1}\cdot(c)^{2}R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}+t^{2}(f,u)^{d}\cdot(c)\cdot(x)R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}.

Expand
c=\sum c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}x^{\underline{m}}\cdot u^{\underline{n}}
, with multi-indices
{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}
. Here
\{c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}\}\in\Bbbk[[t]]
are unknowns. Then we get:

(26)

\Phi_{X}(f+tg)-f\in t(f,u)^{d}\cdot\sum\nolimits_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}\big(c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}+c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}^{(g)}+t\cdot H_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}(\{c_{*}\})\big)\cdot x^{\underline{m}}\cdot u^{\underline{n}}\cdot T_{\mathscr{R}}f+

\hskip 199.16928ptt^{2}(\{c_{*}\})^{2}\cdot Span_{R_{Y}}[(y)\cdot\{q_{\bullet}\}]+t(\{c_{*}\})\cdot Span_{R_{Y}}[(y)^{d+1}\{q_{\bullet}\}].

In the infinite summation

we need only the finite part (by the finite determinacy). Thus we get a finite system of
polynomial

(27)

\{c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}+c_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}^{(g)}+t\cdot H_{{\underline{m}},{\underline{n}}}(\{c_{*}\})=0\}_{{\underline{m}}{\underline{n}}}.

Apply
IFT_{1\hskip-2.84544pt\rm I}
to get the solution

. For these coefficients the transformation
\Phi_{X}
satisfies:

(28)

\Phi_{X}(f+tg)-f\in(x,u)^{N}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}]+t\cdot(y,u)^{d+1}T_{\mathscr{L}}f.

Apply a (filtration-unipotent)

-transformation to eliminate the term

. We get:

(29)

\Phi_{Y}\Phi_{X}(f+tg)-f\in(x,u)^{N}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}]+t\cdot(y,u)^{d+1}\cdot(x)R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}.

Now expand

•

Inductive step for the the general case,

.
The previously found transformation,

and

,

Applying this

\Phi_{Y}\Phi_{X}(f+tg)-f\in(x,u)^{N}R^{\oplus p}_{X,u}+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}],u\big)\quad\text{ for }N\gg 1.

Step 3.

We have proved:

an indeterminate.

The projection of the algebraic germ

In particular, as
\Bbbk
is infinite, the image of

Therefore

.

Step 4.

Inside the space
Maps\big(X,(\Bbbk^{p},o)\big)

with the union of
\mathscr{A}
-orbits,

\mathscr{A}\big(\{f\}+Span_{R_{Y,u}}[(y,u)\cdot\{v_{\bullet}\}],u\big)
. By the

The transformations

of Step 2 were unipotent. Thus we have the algebraic, unipotent action of
\mathscr{A}^{(1)}
on the affine space

({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X,u}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(u,x)^{N}$}\right.})^{\oplus(p+r)}
.
By Kostant-Rosenlicht theorem (see e.g.

By Step 3 the intersection of the line

.

\blacksquare

\mathscr{A}

6.2.

Take a map
F:{\tilde{X}}\to(\Bbbk^{p+r},o)
of rank
r
.
If
char(\Bbbk)>0
then we assume:
\Bbbk
is infinite and

(i.e. the germ
{\tilde{X}}
is smooth).
Then (lemma

{\tilde{X}}\cong X\times(\Bbbk^{r}_{t},o)

and

, for some

and
c(x,t)\in(x)\cdot(t)\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

F
is stable iff
F
is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to the unfolding
(f+\sum^{r^{\prime}}_{j=1}t_{j}v_{j},t)
, where

t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
,

,

is
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-finite, and

go to a set of generators of the
\Bbbk
-vector space
(x)\cdot T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
.

In this case the map
f
is called “the genotype” of

, see e.g.
[
A.G.L.V.
, III.1.7]
.

We emphasize: the generating tuple
\{v_{\bullet}\}
can be chosen arbitrarily, and is not necessarily minimal.

Proof.
\boldsymbol{\Lleftarrow}
By proposition
6.1
it is enough to prove:
T_{\mathscr{A}}F=R^{p+r}_{X,t}
.
We have
Span_{R_{Y}}\{v_{\bullet}\}+T_{\mathscr{A}}f=R^{\oplus p}_{X}
, cf. (
2.1
).

Fix some generators
\{\xi_{j}\}
of

, then the generators of

are
\{\xi_{j}\}
and
\{\partial_{t_{i}}\}
.
The submodule
T_{\mathscr{R}}F\subseteq R^{p+r}_{X,t}
is generated by the matrix with columns
\{\xi_{j}F\},\{\partial_{t_{i}}F\}
. We write these columns (as rows):

(31)

\{(\xi_{j}f+\sum t_{i}\xi_{j}q_{i},0,\dots,0)^{t}\}_{j=1,\dots,n},\quad\{q_{j-p}\cdot\hat{e}_{1}+\hat{e}_{j}\}_{j=p+1,\dots,p+r^{\prime}},\quad\hat{e}_{p+r^{\prime}+1},\dots,\hat{e}_{p+r}.

Here
\{\hat{e}_{\bullet}\}
is the standard basis of
\Bbbk^{p+r}
.

By the direct check:
R^{\oplus p}_{X}\oplus\mathbb{O}\subseteq T_{\mathscr{A}}F|_{t=0}
. And then
\mathbb{O}\oplus R^{\oplus r}_{X}\subseteq T_{\mathscr{A}}F|_{t=0}
. Altogether:

T_{\mathscr{A}}F|_{t=0}=R^{p+r}_{X}
.

By lemma
3.5
we get
R^{p+r}_{X,t}=T_{\mathscr{A}}F
.

\boldsymbol{\Rrightarrow}

By proposition
6.1
,
F
is infinitesimally-stable, i.e.
T_{\mathscr{A}}F=R^{\oplus(p+r)}_{X,t}
.
Then
F
is finitely
\mathscr{A}
-determined,
[
Kerner.21
, §7]
. Therefore below we work over
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x,t]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, with formal
\mathscr{A}
-transformations.

The map
F
is an unfolding, thus we take
F
in the pre-normal form
(f+\sum a_{j}(t,f)\cdot v_{j},t)
, see Remark
5.3
.
This unfolding is versal, therefore

Span_{\Bbbk}(\partial_{t}\sum a_{j}(t,f)v_{j}|_{t=o})
generates
(x)\cdot T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
. Thus

Span_{\Bbbk}(\partial_{t}\sum a_{j}(t,o)v_{j}|_{t=o})
generates
(x)\cdot T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
. And therefore the map
(\Bbbk^{r},o)\ni t\to\{a_{\bullet}(t,o)\}\in(\Bbbk^{r^{\prime}},o)

admits the (right) section. Thus, after an
\mathscr{R}
-coordinate change on
t
-variables, we can assume
a_{j}(t,o)=t_{j}
.
Combined with the
\mathscr{L}
-transformation on the
(\Bbbk^{r},o)
-part of the target, we get:

F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{A}}}{{\sim}}(f+\sum(t_{j}+(t)^{d}\cdot(f))v_{j},t)
, for some
1\leq d<\infty
.

The rest of the proof is induction.
Apply the
\mathscr{R}
-transformation
t_{j}+(t)^{d}\cdot(f)\rightsquigarrow t_{j}
to get to
(f+\sum t_{j}v_{j},t+(t)^{d}\cdot(f))
.
Now apply the
\mathscr{L}
-transformation of type

(y,t)\rightsquigarrow(y,t-(t)^{d}\cdot(y)))
to get to
(f+\sum t_{j}v_{j},t+(t)^{d+1}\cdot(x))
.
Now apply the
\mathscr{R}
-transformation
t_{j}+(t)^{d+1}\cdot(x)\rightsquigarrow t_{j}

to get to
(f+\sum t_{j}v_{j}+(t)^{d+1}\cdot(x),t)
. Iterate.

\blacksquare

7.
Results of Mather-Yau/Scherk/Gaffney-Hauser type

It is well known that the mapping
f:(\Bbbk^{n},o)\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
is determined (up to
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!,\mathscr{A}
-equivalence)
by its “behaviour” at the critical/singular/instability locus. One way to obtain a precise statement was given in
[
Mather-Yau
]
for
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-equivalence
of functions (
p=1
), in
[
Scherk.83
]
for
\mathscr{R}
-equivalence of functions,
and extended in
[
Gaffney-Hauser.85
]
to
{\mathscr{K}}\!
and
\mathscr{A}
-equivalences of maps (
p\geq 1
).
The initial proofs were
\mathbb{C}
-analytic. The first extension to zero/positive characteristic was
done for
p=1
,
\mathscr{R},{\mathscr{K}}\!
in
[
Gr.Ph.19
]
(in the case of isolate critical point).
We establish the general statements/strengthen the known results.

7.1.
The
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-version in zero characteristic

Consider modules over rings,
M_{j}\in mod\text{-}R_{j}
, with the following notion of isomorphism:

M_{1}\sim M_{2}
if
M_{1}\cong\phi^{*}M_{2}
for an isomorphism of rings
\phi:R_{1}\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}R_{2}
.

Below we consider the module
T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
up such isomorphisms.
For
p=1
the data “
T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f
up to isomorphism” is equivalent to the data of
\Bbbk
-algebra
T^{1}_{\mathscr{K}}\!f={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(f)+Jac(f)$}\right.}
.

Let
R_{X}
be one of
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, with
char(\Bbbk)=0
,
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}
.
Take a map
f:X\to(\Bbbk^{p},o)
.

Theorem 7.1
.

1.

The
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-type of
f
is determined by the isomorphism type of
T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}f
.

2.

If
f
is
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-finite then the
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-type of
f
is determined by the isomorphism type of
T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f
.

More precisely, if

T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}f_{0}\sim T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}f_{1}
(in the sense as above), then
f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}}{{\sim}}f_{1}
.
(And similarly for part 2.)

Proof.
The proofs of parts 1,2 are the same except for Step 3.i. To simplify notations we work mostly with
T_{\mathscr{K}}\!
and
T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f
.

Step 1.

i.

It is enough to establish the formal case,
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
.
For the statements over
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\{x\}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk\langle x\rangle$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
one applies Artin approximation.

ii.

Given an isomorphism
T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{0}\cong\phi^{*}T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{1}
coming from an automorphism
\phi\circlearrowright R_{X}
,
replace
f_{1}
by
f_{1}\circ\phi^{-1}
to
get an isomorphism of
R_{X}
-modules
T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{0}\cong T^{1}_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{1}
. After an
R_{X}
-linear automorphism of the module
R^{\oplus p}_{X}

we can assume:
T_{\mathscr{K}}\!f_{0}=T_{\mathscr{K}}\!f_{1}\subseteq R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Similarly, in the first part we can assume:
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}f_{0}=T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}f_{1}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Step 2.

Extend the ring,
R_{X,t}:=R_{X}[t]
, note that
R_{X,t}
is non-local. Take the unfolding
f_{t}:=f_{0}+t(f_{1}-f_{0})
.
We have the tangent spaces
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}
,
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}
and
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}_{t}}f_{t}
, see §
4.5
.

We claim:
\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}
for
t_{o}\in\Bbbk^{1}\setminus\{finite\ set\}
.
(For the first part:
\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}_{t}}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}
for
t_{o}\in\Bbbk^{1}\setminus\{finite\ set\}
.)
More precisely, we claim:
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}
over the factor ring
\Bbbk[t][g^{-1}]
, for some polynomial
0\neq g\in\Bbbk[t]
.

Obviously
\partial_{t}f_{t}=f_{1}-f_{0}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{1}+T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}=T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!}f_{0}
. Thus it is enough to prove:

T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}=T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t_{o}}
for all
t_{o}\in\Bbbk^{1}
except for a finite set.

i.

We have:
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}+t(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}+T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{1})=T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}
.

ii.

Similarly:
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}+t\cdot T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}(f_{1}-f_{0})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}+t\cdot T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}
.
These
R_{X,t}
-modules
are finitely generated. Localize at the ideal
(x,t)
and apply Nakayama over the local ring
(R_{X,t})_{(x,t)}
.
We get:
(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0})_{(x,t)}\subseteq(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t})_{(x,t)}
.

iii.

Similarly one has:
T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{1}\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}+(1-t)\cdot T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}(f_{1}-f_{0})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}+(1-t)\cdot T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{1}
.
Therefore
(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0})_{(x,1-t)}=(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{1})_{(x,1-t)}\subseteq(T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t})_{(x,1-t)}
.

iv.

Take the quotient module

M:={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{0}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}$}\right.}
. Thus
M
is finitely generated over
R_{X}[t]
.
Its localizations at two points vanish,
M_{(t,x)}=0
and
M_{(1-t,x)}=0
.
Therefore its support,
Supp(M)\subseteq Spec(R_{X}[t])
, does not contain the points
V(t,x),V(1-t,x)\in Spec(R_{X}[t])
. But
Supp(M)
is
a Zariski-closed subset. Therefore
Supp(M)\cap V(x)\subset\Bbbk^{1}_{t}
is a finite set of points.

Altogether, we have proved:
\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}\!\in\!T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}
(resp.
\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}\!\in\!T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}_{t}}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}
)
for
t_{o}\!\in\!\Bbbk^{1}\!\setminus\!\{finite\ set\}
.

Step 3.

i.

(
The case
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
.
) We have the unfolding
f_{t}
over
\mathbb{C}
, and

\partial_{t}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}|_{t_{o}}
for
t_{o}\in\Bbbk^{1}\setminus\{finite\ set\}
.
Take a path
\gamma:0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\rightsquigarrow}}1
in
\mathbb{C}
that avoids this finite set.
By theorem
4.3
the unfolding
f_{t}
is locally
{\mathscr{K}}\!
(resp.
{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}
)-trivial at each point of the path.

The
{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}
-trivialization (in the first case) preserves the origin of
X
by definition.
The
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-trivialization (in the second case) preserves the origin of
X
as
V(f_{0}),V(f_{1})
have isolated singularities.

As the path is compact we take a finite cover
\gamma=\cup_{i}U_{i}
, such that
f_{t}
is trivial on each
U_{i}
.
And by the connectedness of the
path we get:
f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!}}{{\sim}}f_{1}
, resp.
f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}}}{{\sim}}f_{1}
.

ii.

(
The general case,
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}
,
char(\Bbbk)=0
.
) Pass to the finite jets,
jet_{d}R_{X}:={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)^{d+1}$}\right.}
.
Then for
t\in\Bbbk^{1}\setminus\{finite\ set\}
we have:

\partial_{t}jet_{d}f_{t}=jet_{d}\partial_{t}f_{t}\in jet_{d}T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}}f_{t}\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}(jet_{d}R_{X})}jet_{d}f_{t}
. Explicitly, we have

\partial_{t}jet_{d}f_{t}=jet_{d}\xi_{X}(jet_{d}f_{t})+jet_{d}(U)\cdot jet_{d}f_{t}
.
This holds for almost all
t\in\Bbbk^{1}
, i.e. over
\Bbbk[t][g^{-1}]
.

Taylor-expand the elements
jet_{d}\xi_{X},jet_{d}U,jet_{d}(J)
up to order
d
. Let
\{C_{\bullet}\}
be the (finite) set of the coefficients.
Then
\mathbb{Q}\{C_{\bullet}\}
is a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Therefore this extension can be (re-)embedded,

\epsilon:\mathbb{Q}\{C_{\bullet}\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}
.

Over
\mathbb{C}
we still have:
\partial_{t}jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{t})\in T_{{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}(\epsilon(jet_{d}R_{X}))}jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{t})
for almost all
t\in\Bbbk^{1}
.
By part i. we get equivalence over
\mathbb{C}
:
jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}(\epsilon(jet_{d}R_{X}))}}{{\sim}}jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{1})
.
(resp.
jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!^{(0)}_{t}(\epsilon(jet_{d}R_{X}))}}{{\sim}}jet_{d}\epsilon(f_{1})
).
Then Lemma
2.2
gives equivalence over
\Bbbk
:

jet_{d}f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}(jet_{d}R_{X})}}{{\sim}}jet_{d}f_{1}
.

This holds for each
d\gg 1
. Therefore the condition
f_{1}\in{\mathscr{K}}\!_{t}(f_{0})
, which is an implicit function equation, has an order-by-order solution.
By Pfister-Popescu theorem,
[
Pfister-Popescu.75
]
, we get the equivalence
f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\sim}}f_{1}
over

{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
.
\blacksquare

Remark 7.2
.

i.

The statement does not hold if

If the singularity is not “of isolated type” then the

.

Instead of the module

7.2.
The
\mathscr{A}
-version in zero characteristic

Below we consider
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f
as a mixed
(R_{Y},R_{X})
-module. We write

if there exists an isomorphism
of algebras
(\Phi_{X},\Phi_{Y})
(see the diagram)
that induces the isomorphism of

-modules

\Phi_{Y}^{\prime}:T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}{\tilde{f}}

\begin{matrix}R_{X}\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{\Phi_{X}}R_{X}\\
\uparrow f^{\#}\ {\tilde{f}}^{\#}\uparrow\\
R_{Y}\underset{}{\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{\Phi_{Y}}}R_{Y}\end{matrix}

Example 7.3
.

i.

If
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{A}}}{{\sim}}{\tilde{f}}
, i.e.
{\tilde{f}}=\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi_{X}
, then
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f\sim T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}{\tilde{f}}
.
Indeed,
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}({\tilde{f}}\circ\Phi_{X}^{-1})={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{R}}(\Phi_{Y}f)+T_{\mathscr{L}}(\Phi_{Y}f)$}\right.}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\Phi^{\prime}_{Y}T_{\mathscr{R}}f+T_{\mathscr{L}}f$}\right.}\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{\Phi_{Y}^{\prime}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f.

ii.

Suppose
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f\sim T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}{\tilde{f}}
. Using the corresponding morphism of algebras we get
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi_{X})=T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}{\tilde{f}}
, i.e.

T_{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi_{Y}\circ f\circ\Phi_{X})=T_{\mathscr{A}}{\tilde{f}}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
R_{X}={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[x]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$J$}\right.}
, with
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}
and
char(\Bbbk)=0
. Suppose
\sqrt{I}={\mathfrak{m}}
.

1.

The
\mathscr{A}
-type of
f
is determined by the mixed module type of
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f
.

2.

If
f
is
\mathscr{A}
-finite​ and​ not​ stable​ then​ the
\mathscr{A}
-type of
f
is determined by the mixed module type of
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}}f
.

More precisely, if
T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{0}\sim T^{1}_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{1}
, then
f_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}}{{\sim}}f_{1}
. (And similarly in case 2.)

Proof.
By the example above we can assume
T_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{0}=T_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{1}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
(resp.
T_{\mathscr{A}}f_{0}=T_{\mathscr{A}}f_{1}\subset R^{\oplus p}_{X}
).
The proof is similar to the
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-case and is inductive.
We apply a sequence of transformations
g_{d}\in\mathscr{A}^{(0)}(jet_{d}(R_{X}))
and verify

jet_{d}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{d}\cdots g_{1}}}{{\sim}}jet_{d}{\tilde{f}}
. Moreover, the transformation can be chosen to satisfy
jet_{d-1}(g_{d})=Id
.

Fix any
d\geq 1
and replace
R_{X}
by the Artinian ring
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$(x)^{d+1}$}\right.}
. Assume
jet_{d-1}(f_{0})=jet_{d-1}(f_{1})
.

Step 1.

As in the
{\mathscr{K}}\!
-case we define
f_{t}:=f_{0}+t(f_{1}-f_{0})
and
R_{X,t}:=R_{X}\otimes\Bbbk[t]
,
T_{\mathscr{A}_{t}}:=T_{\mathscr{A}}\otimes\Bbbk[t]
.
We claim:
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{A}^{(j_{d})}_{t}}f_{t}
for
t\in\Bbbk^{1}\setminus
(finite set), i.e. this holds over the fraction ring
\Bbbk[t][S^{-1}]
.
Here
j_{d}\geq-1
and
\lim_{d\to\infty}j_{d}=\infty
.

We have:
\partial_{t}f_{t}=f_{1}-f_{0}\in(T_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{0}+T_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{1})\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}=T_{\mathscr{A}^{(0)}}f_{0}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.
(For part two one gets:
\partial_{t}f_{t}\in T_{\mathscr{A}}f_{0}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}
.)

By the “mixed Artin-Rees” lemma,
[
Kerner.21
, §7]
, one has
T_{\mathscr{A}}f_{0}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X}\subset T_{\mathscr{A}^{(j_{d})}}f_{0}
, where
\lim_{d}j_{d}=\infty
.

Therefore, as in the

Appendix A
Separability of unfoldings

Below we assume
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}
.
To an unfolding
F=(f_{t}(x,u),t)
we associate the element
[f_{t}]\in T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
. Define the group action

(35)

G:=Aut_{(\Bbbk^{r},o)}:=Aut_{\Bbbk}(\mathbbm{K})\circlearrowright R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}\quad\text{ by }\quad q(x,t)\to q(x,\phi(t)).

This action preserves the embedding
T_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}\subseteq R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
and hence descends to the action
G\circlearrowright T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
.
We get the group orbit
[Gf_{t}]\subseteq T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
and the orbit map
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{t}}}{{\to}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
by
g\to[g\cdot f_{t}]
.

Take an ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subset R_{X,t}
satisfying
\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}}=(x,t)
and pass to the finite jets,
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(R_{X,t}):={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mathfrak{b}$}\right.}
.
This is a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector space. Similarly one has
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(R^{\oplus p}_{X,t})
,
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}):={\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}+\mathfrak{b}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}$}\right.}

and
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(G):=Aut_{\Bbbk}({\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[t]]$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\Bbbk[[t]]\cap\mathfrak{b}$}\right.})
. We get the (regular) action of the affine algebraic group on the affine space,

jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(G)\circlearrowright jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o})
.

Definition A.1
.

(Suppose
\Bbbk=\bar{\Bbbk}
.)
The orbit map
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{t}}}{{\to}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}_{t}}f_{o}
is called separable if all its finite jets,

\{jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(f_{t})}}{{\to}}jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}\}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, are separable as morphisms onto their images.

(The morphism
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(f_{t})
is called separable if the corresponding field extensions are separable, see e.g.
[
F.-S. R.
]
.)

Take the tangent space,
T_{G}:=(t)\cdot Der_{(\Bbbk^{r},o)}
, and its finite jets,
\{jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T_{G}\}_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Take the map from the jet(image tangent space) to
the tangent space of the jet-orbit,
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(T_{G}f_{t})\to Tjet_{\mathfrak{b}}(Gf_{t})
. We write
T_{G}f_{t}\twoheadrightarrow[T(Gf_{t})]\subset T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}

if the surjectivity holds for all the finite jets.
Recall the general fact: the map
f_{t}:G\to T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
is separable (as a morphism onto its image) iff
T_{G}f_{t}\twoheadrightarrow[TGf_{t}]
.
(Namely, the surjectivity holds for all the finite jets.)

Definition A.2
.

The unfolding

F
is called separable if the map
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{t}}}{{\to}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
is separable.

If a field
\Bbbk
is not algebraically closed then we call
F
separable if
\bar{\Bbbk}\otimes F
is separable.

Example A.3
.

i.

For
char(\Bbbk)=0
the group orbit map is always separable. Hence any unfolding is separable.

ii.

The (in)separability is preserved under the
\mathscr{G}_{t}
-equivalence.
For example, for
\mathscr{G}=\mathscr{R}
and any
\mathfrak{b}
we have the commutative diagram of algebraic varieties

\begin{matrix}jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{t}}}{{\to}}jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(T^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}f_{o})\\
\downarrow f_{t}\circ\phi\ \ \|\\
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(f_{o}\circ\phi)=jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{R}}(f_{o})\end{matrix}
.

iii.

A trivial unfolding is separable. Indeed, we can take
F=(f_{o},t)
, then
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{o}}}{{\to}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
is the zero map.
Hence
[T_{G}f_{t}]=0
. And thus trivially
T_{G}f_{t}\twoheadrightarrow[T(Gf_{t})]
.

Lemma A.4
.

An unfolding
F
is inseparable iff
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{G}_{t}}}{{\sim}}f_{o}+t^{d}f_{d}+\cdots
, where
char(\Bbbk)\mid d
and
f_{d}\not\in T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
.

Proof.
\Lleftarrow
We can assume
f=f_{o}+t^{d}f_{d}+\cdots
with
char(\Bbbk)\mid d
and
f_{d}\not\in T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
.
Then
T_{G}f_{t}\subseteq(t)^{d+1}\cdot R^{\oplus p}_{X,t}
.
Thus for
\mathfrak{b}=(t^{d+1},(x)^{N})
we get
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(T_{G}f_{t})=0
.
But
Gf_{t}\ni f_{o}+c^{d}t^{d}f_{d}+\cdots
for any
c\in\Bbbk
. Therefore the subvariety
[jet_{\mathfrak{b}}(Gf_{t})]\subset jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
is of positive dimension. Thus

T(jet_{\mathfrak{b}}Gf_{t})\neq 0
. Therefore the map
T_{G}f_{t}\to[TG(f_{t})]\subset T^{1}_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
cannot be surjective.

\Rrightarrow
Suppose
f\sim f_{o}+t^{d}f_{d}+\cdots
with
char(\Bbbk)\nmid d
and
f_{d}\not\in T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
.
Then
jet_{\mathfrak{b}}T_{G}f_{t}=Span_{\Bbbk[[t]]}\{t^{d-1}f_{d}+\cdots\}

and
Tjet_{\mathfrak{b}}(Gf_{t})=Span_{\Bbbk[[t]]}\{t^{d-1}f_{d}+\cdots\}
. Hence
F
is separable.

Otherwise for each
d
we get:
f\sim f_{o}+t^{d}f_{d}+\cdots
with
f_{d}\in T_{\mathscr{G}}f_{o}
. Then
F
is formally trivial. Trivialize it to get:
(f_{o},t)
is separable.

\blacksquare

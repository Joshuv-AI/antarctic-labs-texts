Sequentially Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals
Ernesto Lax, Giancarlo Rinaldo, Francesco Romeo

Abstract
. We prove that wheels and block graphs have sequentially Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals. Moreover, we provide a construction of new families of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay graphs by cones.

Introduction

Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
[n]
and the edge set
E(G)
. The notion of binomial edge ideals was introduced by Herzog et al. in
[
14
]
, and independently by Ohtani in
[
20
]
. Many algebraic properties and invariants of such ideals were described
via
the combinatorics of the underlying graph, see
[
15
]
for a nice survey on this topic. The most important one is the so-called cutset property. Let
G\setminus T
denote the graph obtained by removing the vertices of
T\subset[n]
.
T
is called a
cutset
if the number of connected components of
G\setminus T
, denoted by
c(T)
, satisfies
c(T)>c(T\setminus\{v\})
for any
v\in T
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}(G)
the set of all cutsets for
G
. For example, the Krull dimension of the ring induced by the binomial edge ideal is

(1)

d=\max\{n+c(T)-|T|\ :\ T\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}.

One of the main topics of the study of binomial edge ideals is to classify the Cohen-Macaulay ones. As an example, after their introduction, the first paper is
[
7
]
where the main result is to classify the block graphs that are Cohen-Macaulay. After that, many authors focused on the problem (see
[
1
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
). Recently, Gorenstein binomial edge ideals (
[
10
]
) and licci binomial edge ideals (
[
8
]
) have been characterized.
A full classification of graphs whose binomial edge ideals are Cohen-Macaulay is not yet known. However, significant progress in this direction has been recently made in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[

]
. A nice and deeply studied generalization of the Cohen-Macaulay ring is the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay one (see
[
27
]
).

As in the case of the Cohen-Macaulay property, for studying sequentially Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals, one can reduce to connected indecomposable graphs as proved in
[
9
]
. In particular, in
[
9
]
, the authors classify the binomial edge ideals with quadratic Gröbner bases that are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, that is those ideals which are associated with closed graphs (see
[
7
]
). Two are the main ingredients to obtain such result: 1) a characterization of Goodarzi
[
11
]
on sequentially Cohen-Macaulay homogeneous ideals; 2) the initial ideal of closed graphs. In
[
26
]
, the authors classified the complete bipartite graphs that are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay studying the deficiency modules (see
[
25
]
).

In Section 1, we focus on the graphs with one cutset. In particular we use the Goodarzi’s filtration and the deficiency modules. An interesting observation of this section is obtained by the following invariant

\mathrm{m}(G)=\min\{n+c(T)-|T|\ :\ T\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}.

If
\mathrm{m}(G)=n+1
, namely
\mathrm{m}(G)=\dim S/P_{\emptyset}
, and
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then
G
has at least a cutpoint, namely a cutset of cardinality 1 (see Proposition
1.13
).

In Section
2
, we focus on the family of graphs that are blocks, namely graphs without cutpoints. Hence, in this case
\mathrm{m}(G)\leq n
and we prove that cycles (
\mathrm{m}(G)=n
) and wheels (
\mathrm{m}(G)=n-1
) are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay by using the Goodarzi’s filtration (see also
[
29
]
).

In Section
3
, we prove that block graphs are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. We underline that in general the filtration of Goodarzi gives us new ideals, induced by the primary decomposition of
J_{G}
, that are not anymore binomial edge ideals. Nevertheless, using elementary containment problem (see Lemma 3.1) by good properties of the block graphs, it is possible to control the filtration using the Mayer–Vietoris sequence (see Theorem 3.2).

In
[
21
]
, the authors provide a useful tool to generate new Cohen-Macaulay graphs using cones. As an application, this has been used to completely classify all bipartite Cohen-Macaulay graphs
[
4
]
. In Section
4
, we extend this result to sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property, obtaining that a cone is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if its components are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay (see Theorem
4.1
).

1.
Graphs with only one non–empty cutset

Let
G
be a graph with the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. A subset

is called a

. A

of

is a vertex that belongs to exactly one maximal clique of
G
, with respect to inclusion. A
block
of
G
is a connected subgraph of
G
that has no cutpoints, which is maximal with respect to this property. A
block graph
is a graph in which every block is a complete graph. A connected graph
G
is
decomposable
if there exist two subgraphs
G_{1},G_{2}
of
G
such that
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
and
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=\{v\}
, where
v
is a free vertex of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.

Let
K
be a field and
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial
K
-algebra in
2n
indeterminates. The
binomial edge ideal
of
G
is the ideal
J_{G}
of
S
generated by all binomials
f_{ij}=x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}
, such that
i<j
and
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
S
-module
M
is called
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
if there exist a finite filtration of submodules of
M

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{d-1}\subset M_{r}=M

such that each quotient
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\dim M_{i}/M_{i-1}<\dim M_{i+1}/M_{i}
for all
i
.

A filtration satisfying the above conditions is called a
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration
of
M
. It is easy to see that if the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration exists, then it is unique (see
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
). One can easily see that any Cohen-Macaulay module
M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, with sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}=M
. Hence, one has

Cohen-Macaulay
\Longrightarrow
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay,

but the converse is not true in general.

Constructing the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration for a module
M
may not be easy. Nevertheless many authors in the years described different ways to characterize the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property for homogeneous ideals/modules.

We first focus on a homological characterization of the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property due to Schenzel (see
[
25
]
), involving the notion of modules of deficiency.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module and put
d=\dim{M}
. For an integer
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the
i
th module of deficiency
of
M
is defined as it follows

\omega^{i}(M)=\Ext^{2n-i}_{S}(M,S(-2n)).

If
i=d
, the module
\omega^{d}(M)=\omega(M)
is the
canonical module
of
M
.

The modules of deficiency were introduced and studied in
[
25
]
. Note that, by the graded version of Local Duality, the
i
th module of deficiency is the Matlis dual of the
i
th local cohomology module, hence the following graded isomorphism holds

\omega^{i}(M)\cong\Hom_{K}(H^{i}(M),K),\quad\text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$},

where
H^{i}(M)
is the
i
th local cohomology module of
M
. From the given relation, since
\depth M=\min\{i:H^{i}(M)\neq 0\}
and
\dim M=\max\{i:H^{i}(M)=0\}
,
it follows that

\omega^{i}(M)=0,\quad\text{for all $i<\depth M$ or $i>\dim M$},

which means that, as the local cohomology modules, the modules of deficiency vanish outside the range between
\depth M
and
\dim M
.

The following results state important properties of the modules of the deficiency (see
[

]
) that will be useful throughout the section.

More recently, Goodarzi
[
11
]
provided an interesting method to construct the dimension filtration of a homogeneous ideal.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal, with
d=\dim S/I
, and let
I=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}Q_{j}
be the minimal primary decomposition of
I
. For all
1\leq j\leq r
, let
P_{j}=\sqrt{Q_{j}}
be the radical of
Q_{j}
. For all
-1\leq i\leq d
, denote by
I^{<i>}
the ideal

I^{<i>}=\bigcap_{\dim S/{P_{j}}>i}Q_{j},

where
I^{<-1>}=I
and
I^{<d>}=S
. Thus, we have a filtration

I=I^{<-1>}\subseteq I^{<0>}\subseteq I^{<1>}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq I^{<d-1>}\subseteq I^{<d>}=S.

Moreover, if we rewrite the above sequence of inclusions modulo
I
, we have

(8)

0\subseteq I^{<0>}/I\subseteq I^{<1>}/I\subseteq\ldots\subseteq I^{<d-1>}/I\subseteq S/I,

which is the dimension filtration of the
S
-module
S/I
.

By the previous definition it is possible to characterize the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property (see
[
11
, Proposition 16]
).

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal and suppose
d=\dim S/I
. Then
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

\depth S/{I^{<i>}}\geq i+1,\qquad\text{for all $0\leq i<d$}.

The above characterization can be reformulated, using the above notation, in term of

\mathcal{D}(I)=\left\{\dim S/P_{j}\ :\ P_{j}=\sqrt{Q}_{j}\right\}=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}\}.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal and
\mathcal{D}(I)=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}\}
, with
d_{1}<\ldots<d_{\ell}=d
. Then
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}=d_{j},\qquad\text{for all $j=1,\ldots,\ell$}.

Proof.

Suppose
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then by Proposition
1.8
, we have that

\depth S/{I^{<i>}}\geq i+1,\qquad\text{for all $0\leq i<d$},

in particular

\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}\geq d_{j},\qquad\text{for all $j=1,\ldots,\ell$}.

Moreover, for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, we have
\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}\leq\dim S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}
and, by the definition of
I^{<d_{j}-1>}
, by
[
6
, Proposition 1.2.13]
that
\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}\leq d_{j}
. Therefore, for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, we have

\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}=d_{j}.

Conversely, suppose

\depth S/{I^{<d_{j}-1>}}=d_{j},\qquad\text{for all $j=1,\ldots,\ell$}

and show that
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Let
0\leq i<d
. We must distinguish two cases:

I Case:
suppose
i=d_{j}-1
for some
j
, then
\depth S/I^{<i>}=d_{j}=i+1
;

II Case:
suppose
d_{j}\leq i<d_{j+1}
for some
j
, then
\depth S/I^{<i>}=d_{j+1}\geq i+1
.

Therefore, for all
0\leq i<d
,

\depth S/I^{<i>}\geq i+1,

and
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

∎

We end this section by providing some sufficient conditions and some necessary conditions for a graph
S/J_{G}
to be sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. For this aim the next notion will be crucial.

A graph
G
on
n
vertices is
\ell
-vertex-connected
(or simply
\ell
-connected) if
\ell<n
and for every subset
T\subset V(G)
of vertices such that
|T|<\ell
, the graph
G\setminus T
is connected. The
vertex-connectivity
(or simply
connectivity
) of
G
, denoted by
\kappa(G)
, is the maximum integer
\ell
such that
G
is
\ell
-vertex-connected.

In
[
1
]
, the authors proved the following result.

Theorem 1.10
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.19]
)
Let
G
be a non–complete, connected graph on
n
vertices and
J_{G}
the corresponding binomial edge ideal. Then

\depth S/{J_{G}}\leq n-\kappa(G)+2,

where
\kappa(G)
is the connectivity of the graph
G
.

The next Proposition characterizes all sequentially Cohen-Macaulay graphs with only one non–empty cutset.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
G
be a graph such that
\mathcal{C}(G)=\{\emptyset,T\}
, let
t=|T|
and let
c=c(T)
denote the number of connected components of
G\setminus T
. Then
\depth S/J_{G}=n-t+2
, and
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay in the following cases:

(i)

t=1
;

(ii)

t\geq 2
and
c=2
;

Proof.

From
\mathcal{C}(G)=\{\emptyset,T\}
we have

J_{G}=P_{\emptyset}\cap P_{T}.

We recall that
P_{\emptyset}=J_{K_{n}}
and we observe that since
(\{x_{v},y_{v}\}_{v\in T})\subset P_{T}
, then
P_{\emptyset}+P_{T}=J_{K_{n-t}}+(\{x_{v},y_{v}\}_{v\in T})
.
Let
X_{T}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1}\ldots y_{n}\}\setminus\{\{x_{v},y_{v}\}:v\in T\}
.
Hence, we have the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

0\rightarrow S/J_{G}\rightarrow S/{J_{K_{n}}}\oplus S/{P_{T}}\rightarrow{K}[X_{T}]/J_{K_{n-t}}\rightarrow 0.

In particular, from Depth Lemma

\displaystyle\depth S/J_{G}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{n+1,n-t+c,(n-t+1)+1\}

\displaystyle=n-t+2,

because
t\geq 1
and
c\geq 2
. Since
G
has connectivity
t
, from Theorem
1.10
we have

\depth S/J_{G}\leq n-t+2,

thus
\depth S/J_{G}=n-t+2
. Therefore,
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if for
i\geq n-t+2
we have
J_{G}^{<i>}\neq J_{G}
, namely

J_{G}^{<i>}\in\{J_{P_{T}},J_{K_{n}}\}.

To achieve the above condition, it is sufficient to require that
J_{G}^{<n-t+2>}\in\{J_{P_{T}},J_{K_{n}}\}
. To obtain

J_{G}^{<n-t+2>}=J_{P_{T}},

we should have
\dim S/J_{K_{n}}=n+1\leq n-t+2
.
Similarly, to obtain

J_{G}^{<n-t+2>}=J_{K_{n}},

we should have
n-t+c\leq n-t+2
.
To sum up, we have the conditions

(i)

n-t+2\geq n+1\Rightarrow t\leq 1
,

(ii)

n-t+2\geq n-t+c\Rightarrow c\leq 2
,

and the assertion follows.

∎

The next Lemma illustrates a necessary condition for the binomial edge ideal
J_{G}
to be sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
G
be a non–complete, connected graph on
n
vertices. Let
\overline{T}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
such that
\dim S/P_{\overline{T}}=\mathrm{m}(G)
. If
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then

\kappa(G)-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\leq 2,

where
\kappa(G)
is the connectivity of the graph
G
.

Proof.

We verify the statement by contradiction. Suppose

\kappa(G)-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\geq 3,

we must prove that
J_{G}
is not sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. We proceed in the following way:

\displaystyle\kappa(G)-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\geq 3\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ n-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\geq n-\kappa(G)+3

\displaystyle{}\Longrightarrow n-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\geq(n-\kappa(G)+2)+1

Since
\dim S/P_{\overline{T}}=n-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})
, it is clear that

J_{G}^{<n-\kappa(G)+2>}=J_{G}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\depth S/J_{G}^{<n-\kappa(G)+2>}

\displaystyle=\depth J_{G}

\displaystyle\leq n-\kappa(G)+2,

which, by Proposition
1.8
, contradicts the fact that
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma
1.12
, we are able to prove the following:

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
G
be a non–complete, connected graph on
n
vertices. Suppose
\mathrm{m}(G)=\dim S/P_{\emptyset}=n+1
. If
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, then
G
has at least one cutpoint.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.12
, we know that

\kappa(G)-|\overline{T}|+c(\overline{T})\leq 2,

where
\overline{T}=\emptyset
and
c(\overline{T})=1
. Hence,

\kappa(G)+1\leq 2\ \Longrightarrow\ \kappa(G)\leq 1.

Since
G
is a connected graph, necessarily
\kappa(G)=1
and the assertion follows.

∎

Inspired by Proposition
1.13
, in Section
3
, we will study block graphs. In fact, they satisfy the hypothesis of the Proposition
1.13
recursively, by removing cutpoints.

Observe that being sequentially Cohen-Macaulay and with at least a cutpoint does not imply that
m(G)=n+1
(e.g. Example
4.4
).

2.
Cycles and wheels

In this section we focus on some families of graphs with no cutpoints: we prove that cycles and wheels have sequentially Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideal. We recall that a module
M
is called
almost Cohen-Macaulay
if
\depth M\geq\dim M-1
, and
M
is said
approximately Cohen-Macaulay
(introduced in
[
12
]
) if and only if
M
is almost Cohen-Macaulay and sequentially Cohen-Macaulay (see
[
24
, Proposition 4.5]
). Let
C_{n}
be a cycle with
n
vertices. We first remark that cycle graphs are not Cohen-Macaulay in general if
n\geq 4
. It has been proved by Zafar in
[
29
]
that
\depth S/J_{C_{n}}=n
(see
[
29
, Theorem 4.5]
). Since,
\dim S/J_{C_{n}}=n+1
, then
S/J_{C_{n}}
is almost Cohen-Macaulay. In the paper
[
29
]
, Zafar also proves that cycles are approximately Cohen-Macaulay, and hence sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. Nevertheless, we provide a shorter proof for the sequential Cohen-Macaulayness, by using an elementary property of cycles. Without loss of generality, from now on, we will assume
n\geq 4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C_{n}
be a cycle with
n
vertices. Then the nonempty cutsets of
C_{n}
have cardinalities at least
2
. Moreover, if
T\neq\emptyset
is a cutset then
|T|
is equal to the number of connected components of
G\setminus T
.

Proof.

Let
V(C_{n})=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
E(C_{n})=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{n-1,n\}\}\cup\{\{1,n\}\}
. Since
C_{n}
is biconnected we need at least
2
vertices to break the cycle. Moreover, without loss of generality we may assume that a nonempty cutset contains the vertex 1. We claim that
T=\{i_{1}=1,\ldots,i_{r}\}
with
i_{k}>i_{k-1}+1
for all
2\leq k\leq r
, and
i_{r}<n
. That is
T
is a cutset if and only if it is a subset of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
of cardinality at least
2
and such that there are no adjacent vertices in
T
. Hence,
G\setminus T
is a collection of
|T|
paths and isolated vertices as stated.

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
C_{n}
be a cycle with
n
vertices. Then
J_{C_{n}}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

We first observe that, by Lemma
2.1
, it easily follows that

\dim S/J_{C_{n}}=n+1.

In fact, given a cutset
T
of
C_{n}
, if
T=\emptyset
, then
\dim S/P_{\emptyset}=n+1
. Otherwise, if
T\neq\emptyset
, by Lemma
2.1
we have that
c(T)=\lvert T\rvert
, thus
\dim S/P_{T}=n-\lvert T\rvert+c(T)=n
. Therefore,

\dim S/J_{C_{n}}=\max\{n,n+1\}=n+1,

as stated.

Our aim is to apply Lemma
1.9
to prove that
S/J_{C_{n}}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. We observe that
\mathcal{D}(J_{G})=\{n,n+1\}
, and
S/J_{C_{n}}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if

\depth S/{J_{C_{n}}}^{<n-1>}=n\mbox{ and }\depth S/{J_{C_{n}}}^{<n>}=n+1.

From
{J_{C_{n}}}^{<n-1>}=J_{C_{n}}
and
S/{J_{C_{n}}}^{<n>}=P_{\emptyset}
, the assertion follows.

∎

In order to prove a consequence of the previous result, we recall that if
G
is a graph on the vertex set
V(G)=[n]
and
v\notin V(G)
, the
cone
of
v
on
G
, denoted by
\Cone(v,G)
, is the graph on the vertex set
V(G)\cup\{v\}
and edge set
E(G)\cup\{\{v,w\}\ :\ w\in V(G)\}
. The
wheel graph
is
W_{n}=\Cone(n+1,C_{n})
. Moreover, any nonempty cutset
T
of
W_{n}
is
T^{\prime}\cup\{n+1\}
where
T^{\prime}
is a nonempty cutset of
C_{n}
. Hence,
c(T)=|T|-1
.
It is known that (see
[
18
, Corollary 3.11]
)

\depth S/J_{W_{n}}=n.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
W_{n}
be a wheel graph. Then
J_{W_{n}}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

With a similar arguing to that of Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\dim S/J_{W_{n}}=\max\{n,n+2\}=n+2.

As done in Proposition
2.2
, by applying Lemma
1.9
, the assertion follows.

∎

From the proofs of Proposition
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
, it arises that
\mathrm{m}(C_{n})=n
and
\mathrm{m}(W_{n})=(n+1)-1
.

The class of blocks that are (non-)sequentially Cohen-Macaulay is a big one, as the following and easy example shows.

Example 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph with a unique non-empty cutset of cardinality greater than
1
, then
G
is a block. By Proposition
1.11
and its notation, if
c=2
we construct an infinite family of graphs that are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. If
c>2
we have an infinite family of graphs that are non-sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

3.
Block graphs

In this Section we prove that all block graphs have sequentially Cohen-Macaulay binomial edge ideals. As observed in
[
9
, Remark 1.7]
, we reduce to the study of indecomposable connected block graphs. Moreover, for the sake of simplicity we set
J_{v}:=J_{G_{v}}
and
J_{\overline{v}}:=J_{G\setminus\{v\}}
in the Mayer–Vietoris sequence (
2
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v
a cutpoint of an indecomposable connected block graph
G
such that

G\setminus\{v\}=G_{1}\sqcup B_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup B_{r}

with
r\geq 2
, where
B_{i}
are complete graphs. Then

J_{v}^{<i>}+(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}=(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>}.

Proof.

We claim that if
G
is an indecomposable block graph, then
\mathcal{C}(G_{v})=\mathcal{C}(G\setminus\{v\})
. We first observe that the inclusion
\mathcal{C}(G\setminus\{v\})\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G_{v})
easily holds. Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{C}(G_{v})\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G\setminus\{v\})
. Moreover, if
T
is a cutset of
G
that does not intersect
N_{G_{1}}(v)
, then
T
is obviously a cutset of both graphs. Let
T
be a cutset of
G_{v}
and assume
T
is not cutset of
G\setminus\{v\}
. From the above observation,
T\cap N_{G_{1}}(v)\neq\emptyset
and hence there exists
u\in T\cap N_{G_{1}}(v)
such that
c_{G_{1}}(T\setminus\{u\})=c_{G_{1}}(T)
. That is,
u
is not a cutpoint, namely
u
is free in the graph
G_{1}
. This implies
N_{G}(u)=N_{G_{1}}(u)\cup\{v\}
and
G
is decomposable in
u
, a contradiction.

Observe now that the block containing
v
in
G_{v}
, namely
B_{v}
, is a block of
G_{v}
itself, such that
|V(B_{v})|\geq 4
and having at least
3
free vertices. Thanks to this we have that

J_{v}^{<i>}+(x_{v},y_{v})=(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>}.

In fact,

J_{v}^{<i>}=\bigcap_{\dim S/P_{T}>i}P_{T}=(\bigcap P^{\prime}_{T})+J_{F_{v}},

where
F_{v}
is the complete graph containing the free vertices of
B_{v}
, and

J_{v}^{<i>}+(x_{v},y_{v})=(\bigcap P^{\prime}_{T})+J_{F_{v}\setminus\{v\}}=J_{v}^{<i-1>}.

Hence, it is sufficient to prove that for all
i

J_{v}^{<i>}+(x_{v},y_{v})\supseteq(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}.

For any
T\in\mathcal{C}(G_{v})=\mathcal{C}(G\setminus\{v\})
let
P_{T}^{v}
and
P_{T}^{\overline{v}}
be primes of
J_{v}
and
J_{\overline{v}}
, respectively. Then

\dim P_{T}^{v}=n-|T|+c_{G_{v}}(T)\mbox{ and }\dim P_{T}^{\overline{v}}=(n-1)-|T|+c_{G\setminus\{v\}}(T).

Observe that
c_{G\setminus\{v\}}(T)=c_{G_{v}}(T)+r
. Since
r\geq 2
, then

\dim P_{T}^{\overline{v}}=(n-1)-|T|+c_{G_{v}}(T)+r=\dim P_{T}^{v}+r-1.

Let
\mathcal{D}(J_{G_{v}})=\{d_{0},\ldots,d_{l}\}
and
\mathcal{D}(J_{G\setminus\{v\}})=\{\overline{d}_{0},\ldots,\overline{d}_{l}\}
where
d_{0}=\dim P_{\emptyset}=n+1
. We have proved that
d_{k}=\overline{d}_{k}-r+1
.
we show that for any
k

J_{v}^{<d_{k}-1>}+(x_{v},y_{v})\supseteq(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<d_{k}-1>}.

If
k=0
, and
d_{0}-1<\overline{d}_{0}-1
, then

J_{v}^{<d_{0}-1>}+(x_{v},y_{v})=J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v})\supseteq(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<d_{0}-1>}=J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})

because there is no dimension shift,
i.e.
the filtered ideals
(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}
, for
i=0,1,\ldots,d_{0}-1
, coincide with
J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})
itself.

Let
0<k\leq l
, we observe that since
G\setminus v
is not connected then

(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<\overline{d}_{k}-1>}=\left(\bigcap_{\dim S/P^{\prime}_{T}\geq\overline{d}_{k}-r}P^{\prime}_{T}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}J_{B_{i}}+(x_{v},y_{v}).

Since
J_{F_{v}}
is the binomial edge ideal induced by the free vertices of the block containing
v
in
G_{v}
, then

J_{v}^{<d_{k}-1>}=\bigcap_{\dim S/P_{T}>d_{k}}P_{T}=\left(\bigcap_{\dim S/P^{\prime}_{T}\geq d_{k}-1}P^{\prime}_{T}\right)+J_{F_{v}}.

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{r}J_{B_{i}}\subset J_{F_{v}}
and
\overline{d}_{k}-r=d_{k}-1
, we have

J_{v}^{<d_{k}-1>}+(x_{v},y_{v})\supseteq(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<\overline{d}_{k}-1>}\supseteq(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<d_{k}-1>}

and the assertion follows.

∎

Whilst Lemma
3.1
holds for block graphs, it is not true in general, as showed in the next example.

Example 3.2
.

Let
G
be the graph on
7
vertices obtained by attaching a vertex to the complete bipartite graph
K_{2,4}
as shown in the figure below.

Such a graph has the cutpoint

J_{G_{6}}^{<7>}+(J_{G\setminus\{6\}}+(x_{6},y_{6}))^{<7>}\neq(J_{G_{6}\setminus\{6\}}+(x_{6},y_{6}))^{<6>}.

We are now in position to prove the main result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be an indecomposable, connected block graph. Then
J_{G}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Our aim is to apply Goodarzi’s characterization (see
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
) to
S/J_{G}
, proving that for all
0\leq i<d=\dim S/J_{G}
,

\depth(S/J_{G}^{<i>})\geq i+1.

We proceed by induction on the number
r
of the cutpoints of
G
. If
r=1
, then

G
is a block star graph. Let
v
be the unique cutpoint of
G
and let
c_{v}=c\left(G\setminus\{v\}\right)
. Hence,

J_{G}=J_{v}\cap(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})),

and
d=\dim S/J_{G}=\dim S/J_{\overline{v}}=n+c_{v}-1
. Moreover, it is clear that

J_{G}^{<i>}=\begin{cases}J_{G}&\quad\text{if $0\leq i\leq n$},\\
(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))&\quad\text{if $n<i<d$},\\
S&\quad\text{if $i=d$}.\end{cases}

Therefore, if
0\leq i\leq n
, we have

\depth(S/J_{G}^{<i>})=n+1\geq i+1.

Otherwise, if
n<i<d

\displaystyle\depth(S/J_{G}^{<i>})

\displaystyle=\depth(S/(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})))

\displaystyle=\dim(S/(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})))

\displaystyle=d

\displaystyle\geq i+1,

and this proves the base case.

Suppose now
r>1
and assume the statement true for
r-1
. Consider the Mayer–Vietoris sequence

0\rightarrow S/J_{G}\rightarrow S/{J_{v}}\oplus S/{J_{\overline{v}}}\rightarrow S/(J_{v}+(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v})))\rightarrow 0.

Fix now
i
such that
0\leq i<d
, the above sequence induces the following sequence on the filtered ideals:

(9)

0\rightarrow S/{J_{G}^{<i>}}\rightarrow S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\oplus S/({J_{\overline{v}}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}\rightarrow S/(J_{v}^{<i>}+(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}).

In order to apply the Depth Lemma to sequence (
9
), we express the ideal
J_{v}^{<i>}+(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}
in a more suitable form. By Lemma
3.1
, we know that

J_{v}^{<i>}+(J_{\overline{v}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}=(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>},

hence sequence (
9
) becomes

(10)

0\rightarrow S/{J_{G}^{<i>}}\rightarrow S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\oplus S/({J_{\overline{v}}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}\rightarrow(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>},

and, by applying the Depth Lemma to the above sequence, we obtain

\displaystyle\depth(S/J_{G}^{<i>})

\displaystyle\geq\min\big\{\depth(S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\oplus S/({J_{\overline{v}}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}),

\displaystyle\phantom{\geq\min\big\{.}\depth(S/(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>})+1\big\}.

Observe that
\depth S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\leq\depth S/({J_{\overline{v}}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}
, since
G_{v}
is a block graph and
G\setminus\{v\}
is tensor product of block graphs. Therefore,

\depth(S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\oplus S/({J_{\overline{v}}}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>})=\depth S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}.

Then

\depth(S/J_{G}^{<i>})\geq\min\big\{\depth S/{J_{v}^{<i>}},\depth(S/(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>})+1\big\}.

By inductive hypothesis, we have that

\depth S/{J_{v}^{<i>}}\geq i+1,\qquad\depth(S/(J_{v}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i-1>})\geq i,

and the assertion follows.

∎

4.
Cone graphs

Cone graphs have been studied by many authors (e.g.
[
4
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
21
]
), to control the behavior of the depth and the Cohen-Macaulay property.

In this section we study the binomial edge ideals of cone graphs and we specify under which conditions they are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup G_{r}
be a graph, where
G_{i}
is a connected graph for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, with
r\geq 2
. Let
v
be a vertex such that
v\notin V(G)
and
H=\Cone(v,G)
. Then
J_{H}
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
J_{G_{1}},\ldots,J_{G_{r}}
are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

We first observe that, since
H
is a cone graph, then

H_{v}=K_{n+1},\qquad H\setminus\{v\}=G.

Hence, we write

J_{H}=J_{K_{n+1}}\cap(J_{G}+(x_{v},y_{v})).

Moreover,

S/(J_{G}+(x_{v},y_{v}))\cong S_{1}/J_{G_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes S_{r}/J_{G_{r}},

where
S_{i}=K[x_{u},y_{u}\ :\ u\in V(G_{i})]
. Thus,
S_{i}/J_{G_{i}}
are sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

S/(J_{G}+(x_{v},y_{v}))
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

Since
J_{H}^{<i>}=J_{K_{n+1}}^{<i>}\cap(J_{G}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}
and
\dim S/J_{K_{n+1}}=n+2
, for
i\geq n+2

J_{H}^{<i>}=(J_{G}+(x_{v},y_{v}))^{<i>}.

Therefore, if
i\geq n+2
, we have

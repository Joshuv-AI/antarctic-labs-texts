Multi-dimensional continuous time quantum walks related to the birth and death chains
Yusuke Ide, Norio Konno, Akihiro Narimatsu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider multi-dimensional birth and death chains and continuous time quantum walks (CTQW) related to them. For CTQW related to our forms of multi-dimensional birth and death chains, we obtain the time scaled independence between multiple dimensions about the transition probability of CTQW. By using this feature, we analyze CTQW on the path graph, which is related to 1-dimensional Ehrenfest model. We also have a random variable which is related to our models and converges to the standard Gaussian distribution.

i

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbb{P}(X_{t+1}^{(i)}=k\ |\ X_{t}^{(i)}=k)=\frac{N-N^{(i)}}{N}=1-\frac{N^{(i)}}{N}\quad&(k=0,\dots,N^{(i)}),\\

These equations mean that a ball is selected at random and moved to the other box. Let
P_{N}
be the transition probability matrix of the Ehrenfest model written by

\displaystyle P_{N}=\frac{N^{(1)}}{N}(P_{N^{(1)}}\otimes I_{N^{(2)}})+\frac{N^{(2)}}{N}(I_{N^{(1)}}\otimes P_{N^{(2)}}),

\displaystyle p_{t}(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}^{(1)}=x^{(1)},X_{t}^{(2)}=x^{(2)}).

whose operation is written by

\displaystyle M_{t}=\sum_{0\leq x^{(1)}\leq N^{(1)}}\sum_{0\leq x^{(2)}\leq N^{(2)}}\bigl\{p_{t}(x^{(1)},x^{(2)})\langle x^{(1)}|\otimes\langle x^{(2)}|\bigr\}.

We should note that
M_{t+1}=M_{t}P_{N}
and

\displaystyle M_{t}=M_{0}P_{N}^{t}.

\bigl\{\pi^{(i)}(j)\bigr\}\ (i=1,2,j=0,\dots,N^{(i)})

We define the matrix

\displaystyle J_{N^{(i)}}=D_{N^{(i)}}^{1/2}P_{N^{(i)}}D_{N^{(i)}}^{-1/2},

diag
\bigl(\pi^{(i)}(0),\dots,\pi^{(i)}(N^{(i)})\bigr)

\displaystyle J_{N^{(i)}}=\sum_{l^{(i)}=0}^{N^{(i)}}\lambda_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}|{\bf v}_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}|,

(1)

where

is the
l^{(i)}

\displaystyle|{\bf v}_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}\rangle=\begin{bmatrix}p_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}(0)\\
p_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}(1)\\
\vdots\\
p_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}(N^{(i)})\end{bmatrix}v_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
p_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}(1)\\
\vdots\\
p_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}(N^{(i)})\end{bmatrix}v_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}

\lambda_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\mu^{(i)}(\lambda_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)})=\Bigl|v_{l^{(i)}}^{(i)}\Bigr|^{2}.

(2)

We put the matrix

as follows:

\displaystyle J_{N}=\frac{N^{(1)}}{N}(J_{N^{(1)}}\otimes I_{N^{(2)}})+\frac{N^{(2)}}{N}(I_{N^{(1)}}\otimes J_{N^{(2)}}).

By Eq.(

\displaystyle J_{N}\Bigl(|{\bf v}_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|{\bf v}_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}\rangle\Bigr)=\Biggl(\frac{N^{(1)}}{N}\lambda_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}+\frac{N^{(2)}}{N}\lambda_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}\Biggr)\Bigl(|{\bf v}_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|{\bf v}_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}\rangle\Bigr).

Let
V
be the orthogonal matrix written by

\displaystyle V=\Bigl[|v_{0}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{0}^{(2)}\rangle,\ \ |v_{0}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{1}^{(2)}\rangle,\

\displaystyle\dots\ |v_{0}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{N^{(2)}}^{(2)}\rangle,

\displaystyle|v_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{0}^{(2)}\rangle,\ \ |v_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{1}^{(2)}\rangle,\

\displaystyle\dots\ |v_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{N^{(2)}}^{(2)}\rangle,

\displaystyle\dots\ |v_{N^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes|v_{N^{(2)}}^{(2)}\rangle\Bigr].

Since

are orthogonal matrices, we have the following another description of

\displaystyle\delta_{{\bf j},{\bf k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{(1)}=0}^{N^{(1)}}\sum_{l^{(2)}=0}^{N^{(2)}}\Biggl(\overline{v_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}p_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}(j^{(1)})v_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}p_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}(j^{(2)})}v_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}p_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}(k^{(m)})v_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}p_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}(k^{(2)})\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l^{(1)}=0}^{N^{(1)}}\sum_{l^{(2)}=0}^{N^{(2)}}\Biggl(\overline{p_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}(j^{(1)})p_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}(j^{(2)})}p_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}(k^{(1)})p_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}(k^{(2)})|v_{l^{(1)}}^{(1)}|^{2}|v_{l^{(2)}}^{(2)}|^{2}\Biggr)

whose operation is written by

\displaystyle M_{t}=\sum_{0\leq x^{(1)}\leq N^{(1)}}\cdots\sum_{0\leq x^{(d)}\leq N^{(d)}}\bigl\{p_{t}(x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(d)})\langle x^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes\langle x^{(d)}|\bigr\}.

We should note that
M_{t+1}=M_{t}P_{N}
and

\displaystyle M_{t}=M_{0}P_{N}^{t}.

and
(j=0,\dots,N^{(i)})

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle(\Delta t)

\displaystyle=e^{i\Delta tJ_{N}}

\displaystyle=\exp\Biggl[i\Delta t\sum_{{\bf j}={\bf 0}}^{[N^{(1)},N^{(2)},\dots,N^{(d)}]}\Biggl\{\Biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{d}q^{(k)}\lambda_{j^{(k)}}^{(k)}\Biggr)\Biggl(\bigotimes_{l=1}^{d}|{\bf v}_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)}\rangle\Biggr)\Biggl(\bigotimes_{l=1}^{d}\langle{\bf v}_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)}|\Biggr)\Biggr\}\Biggr]

Theorem 3.1
.

The transition probability
\mathbb{P}({\bf Y}(t+\Delta t)={\bf k}\ |\ {\bf Y}(t)={\bf j})
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\bf Y}(t+\Delta t)={\bf k}\ |\ {\bf Y}(t)={\bf j})=\prod_{l=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}^{(l)}\Bigl(Y^{(l)}\Bigl(t+q^{(l)}\Delta t\Bigr)=k^{(l)}\ \Bigl|\ Y^{(l)}(t)=j^{(l)}\Bigr).

(8)

proof of Theorem
3.1
.
By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(l)}

\displaystyle\bigl(Y^{(l)}(t+\Delta t)=k^{(l)}\ |\ Y^{(l)}(t)=j^{(l)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\langle k^{(l)}|\sum_{m=0}^{N^{(l)}}\Biggl\{\exp\Bigl(i\Delta t\lambda_{m}^{(l)}\Bigr)|{\bf v}_{m}^{(l)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m}^{(l)}|\Biggr\}|j^{(l)}\rangle\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{m=0}^{N^{(l)}}\Biggl\{\exp\Bigl(i\Delta t\lambda_{m}^{(l)}\Bigr)\Bigl(\langle k^{(l)}|{\bf v}_{m}^{(l)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m}^{(l)}|j^{(l)}\rangle\Bigr)\Biggr\}\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{m=0}^{N^{(l)}}\Biggl\{\exp\Bigl(i\Delta t\lambda_{m}^{(l)}\Bigr)v_{m}^{(l)}\Bigl(p_{m}^{(l)}(k^{(l)})\Bigr)\overline{v_{m}^{(l)}\Bigl(p_{m}^{(l)}(j^{(l)})\Bigr)}\Biggr\}\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{m=0}^{N^{(l)}}\Biggl\{\exp\Bigl(i\Delta t\lambda_{m}^{(l)}\Bigr)\Bigl(p_{m}^{(l)}(k^{(l)})\overline{p_{m}^{(l)}(j^{(l)})}\Bigr)\mu^{(l)}(\lambda_{m}^{(l)})\Biggr\}\Biggr|^{2}.

(9)

Substituting Eqs.(
6
),(
9
) into Eq.(
7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle({\bf Y}(t+\Delta t)={\bf k}\ |\ {\bf Y}(t)={\bf j})

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{{\bf m}={\bf 0}}^{[N^{(1)},N^{(2)},\dots,N^{(d)}]}\Biggl[\exp\Biggl\{i\Delta t\Biggl(\sum_{h=1}^{d}q^{(h)}\lambda_{m^{(h)}}^{(h)}\Biggr)\Biggr\}\Biggl(\prod_{l=1}^{d}\langle k^{(l)}|{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}|j^{(l)}\rangle\Biggr)\Biggr]\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{m^{(1)}=0}^{N^{(1)}}\sum_{{\bf m}=[m^{(1)},0,\dots,0]}^{[m^{(1)},N^{(2)},\dots,N^{(d)}]}\Biggl[\exp\Biggl\{i\Delta t\Biggl(\sum_{h=1}^{d}q^{(h)}\lambda_{m^{(h)}}^{(h)}\Biggr)\Biggr\}\Biggl(\prod_{l=1}^{d}\langle k^{(l)}|{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}|j^{(l)}\rangle\Biggr)\Biggr]\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Biggl|\sum_{m^{(1)}=0}^{N^{(1)}}\Biggl\{\exp\Biggl(iq^{(1)}\Delta t\lambda_{m^{(1)}}^{(1)}\Biggr)\langle k^{(1)}|{\bf v}_{m^{(1)}}^{(1)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m^{(1)}}^{(1)}|j^{(1)}\rangle\Biggr\}\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\times\Biggl|\sum_{{\bf m}=[\ast,0,\dots,0]}^{[\ast,N^{(2)},\dots,N^{(d)}]}\Biggl[\exp\Biggl\{i\Delta t\Biggl(\sum_{h=2}^{d}q^{(h)}\lambda_{m^{(h)}}^{(h)}\Biggr)\Biggr\}\Biggl(\prod_{l=2}^{d}\langle k^{(l)}|{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}\rangle\langle{\bf v}_{m^{(l)}}^{(l)}|j^{(l)}\rangle\Biggr)\Biggr]\Biggr|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{(1)}\Bigl(Y^{(1)}\Bigl(t+q^{(1)}\Delta t\Bigr)=k^{(1)}\ \Bigl|\ Y^{(1)}(t)=j^{(1)}\Bigr)

This equation means that the transition probability of multi-dimensional CTQW can be calculated by the product of the transition probability of

{\bf j}=[j^{(1)},\dots,j^{(d)}]
be the initial position of the quantum walker and

\displaystyle{\bf Y}_{\bf j}(t)=[Y^{(1)}_{j^{(1)}}(t),\dots,Y^{(d)}_{j^{(d)}}(t)]

be the position at time
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Each
Y_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)}\in\{0,\dots,N/d\}
can be regarded as the random variable. By Eq.(
8
), the transition probability is written as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}({\bf Y}_{\bf j}(t)={\bf k})

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}^{(l)}\Bigl(Y_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)}\Bigl(q^{(l)}t\Bigr)=k^{(l)}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}^{(l)}\Bigl(Y_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{d}\Bigr)=k^{(l)}\Bigr).

(10)

When
t
of left-hand side of Eq.(
10
) is scaled to
t/d
, this equation means that
Y_{j^{(l)}}^{(l)},(l=1,\dots,d)
are independent. When we assume
N^{(l)}=1\ (l=1,\dots,d=N)
, we can regard CTQW on the path as the tensor product by
N
-th power of CTQW on the edge. Thus we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

The random variable about the position of CTQW related to the
1
-dimensional Ehrenfest model
Y_{0}=Y^{(1)}_{0}+Y^{(2)}_{0}+\dots,+Y^{(d)}_{0}
obeys the binomial distribution
B(d,\sin^{2}\frac{t}{d})
.

proof of Proposition
3.2
.
For each
l=0,\dots,d
, we have

\displaystyle J_{N^{(l)}}=J_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle e^{itJ_{N^{(l)}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{k}}{k!}J_{N^{(l)}}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{2k}}{(2k)!}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(it)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\cos t&i\sin t\\
i\sin t&\cos t\end{bmatrix}.

Then we get
\mathbb{P}^{(l)}\Bigl(Y_{0}^{(l)}(t)=1\Bigr)=\sin^{2}t
. By Eq.(
10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{0}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{d}j^{(l)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}({\bf Y}_{\bf 0}(t)={\bf j})

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{d}\mathbb{P}^{(l)}\Bigl(Y_{0}^{(l)}\Bigl(\frac{t}{d}\Bigr)=j^{(l)}\Bigr),

where
{\bf j}=[j^{(1)},\dots,j^{(d)}]
,
j^{(l)}\in\{0,1\}\ (l=1,\dots,d)
. Thus we obtain the conclusion.

\square

This is another easier calculation method for the analysis of the
1
-dimensional CTQW related to Ehrenfest model, obtained in
[
10
]
.

Next we consider the random variable
Z_{\bf j}^{(d)}
of position at time
T\in\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle Z_{\bf j}^{(d)}(T)=\frac{1}{d}\bigl\{Y_{j^{(1)}}^{(1)}(T)+\dots+Y_{j^{(d)}}^{(d)}(T)\bigr\}.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any constant
T\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{\bf j}^{(d)}(T)-\mathbb{E}\bigl(Y^{(1)}_{j^{(1)}}(T)\bigr)}{\sqrt{\frac{V\bigl(Y^{(1)}_{j^{(1)}}(T)\bigr)}{d}}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\quad(d\to\infty),

(11)

where
\Rightarrow
stands for the weak convergence and
N(0,1)
is the standard Gaussian distribution.

proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Noting that
Y_{j^{(1)}}^{(1)}(T),\dots,Y_{j^{(d)}}^{(d)}(T)
are independent and identically distributed. Since
T
is a constant, variance of
Y_{j^{(1)}}^{(1)}(T),\dots,Y_{j^{(d)}}^{(d)}(T)
are finite. Then we have Eq. (
11
) by central limit theorem.

\square

4
Summary

In this paper, we considered multi-dimensional birth and death chains and CTQW related to them. We obtained the time scaled independence between multiple dimensions about the transition probability of CTQW. After that we limited our discussion to the symmetric multi-dimensional Ehrenfest model case. We got the different proof from the previous study to show that the random variable about the position of CTQW related to the 1-dimensional Ehrenfest model obeys the binomial distribution. We also had a random variable which is related to our models and converges to the standard Gaussian distribution. To consider CTQW on more general graph and analyze by using the dividing method of proposition
3.2
is one of the interesting future problem.

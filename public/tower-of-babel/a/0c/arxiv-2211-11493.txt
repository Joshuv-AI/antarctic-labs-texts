On Extensions of Quasi-Overlap and Quasi-Grouping Functions Defined on Bounded Lattices
Ana Shirley Monteiro, Regivan Santiago, Benjamín Bedregal, Juscelino Araújo, Eduardo Palmeira

Abstract




1
Introduction

In this paper, we propose a method of extending quasi-overlap and grouping functions defined on a sublattice
M
of a bounded lattice
L
to this lattice considering a more general version of sublattice definition, introduced by Palmeira and Bedregral in
[
1
]
.

2
Extensions of Quasi-Overlap Functions

In what follows we recall the notion of
quasi-overlap function
.

Definition 2.1

(Quasi-overlap function,
[
2
]
)
.

A binary operator
O:L^{2}\to L
is said to be a
quasi-overlap function
on
L
if, for any
x,y,z\in L
, the following conditions hold:

(
\mathbf{QO1}
)

commutativity:
O(x,y)=O(y,x)
;

(
\mathbf{QO2}
)

O(x,y)=0_{L}
iff
x=0_{L}
or
y=0_{L}
;

(
\mathbf{QO3}
)

O(x,y)=1_{L}
iff
x=1_{L}
and
y=1_{L}
;

(
\mathbf{QO4}
)

increasingness:
O(x,y)\leq_{L}O(x,z)
whenever
y\leq_{L}z
.

Suppose there exists a retraction
r:L\to M
with pseudo-inverse
s:M\to L
. If
O
is a quasi-overlap function on
M
, we want to provide a quasi-overlap function
O^{E}
on
L
such that

O^{E}\circ(s\times s)=s\circ O

In this context we will say that
O^{E}
extends
O
from
M
to
L
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
O
is a quasi-overlap on
M
and
r:L\to M
is a retraction with pseudo-inverse
s:M\to L
such that

\displaystyle r(x)=0_{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=0_{L};

(1)

\displaystyle r(x)=1_{M}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=1_{L};

(2)

then
O^{E}:L^{2}\to L
, defined by

O^{E}(x,y)=s(O(r(x),r(y))),

(3)

is a quasi-overlap function that extends
O
from
M
to
L
.

Proof.

For all
(x,y)\in M\times M
, we have:

\displaystyle\left[O^{E}\circ(s\times s)\right](x,y)

\displaystyle=O^{E}(s(x),s(y))=s\left(O\left(r(s(x)),r(s(y))\right)\right)=s\left(O\left(x,y\right)\right)=\left(s\circ O\right)(x,y).

Thus,
O^{E}
extends
O
from
M
to
L
. Furthermore, the following properties hold:

(QO1)

O^{E}
is commutative once
O
is commutative;

(QO2)

For all
(x,y)\in L^{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle O^{E}(x,y)=0_{L}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow s\left(O\left(r(x),r(y)\right)\right)=0_{L}

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.}\eqref{eq:extensofquas3}.]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow O\left(r(x),r(y)\right)=0_{M}

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.\eqref{eq:extensofquas1} and $r\circ s=Id_{M}$.}]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r(x)=0_{M}\ \text{or}\ r(y)=0_{M}

\displaystyle[\text{By \ref{item_QO2}}.]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=0_{L}\ \text{or}\ y=0_{L}.

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.}\eqref{eq:extensofquas1}.]

(QO3)

Analogous to (QO2).

(QO4)

O^{E}
is an increasing function since
r
,
s
and
O
are increasing.

∎

3
Extensions of Quasi-Grouping Functions

In what follows we remind the notion of
quasi-grouping function
.

Definition 3.1

(Quasi-grouping function,
[
3
]
)
.

A binary operator
G:L^{2}\to L
is said to be a
quasi-grouping function
on
L
if, for any
x,y,z\in L
, the following conditions hold:

(
\mathbf{QG1}
)

commutativity:
G(x,y)=G(y,x)
;

(
\mathbf{QG2}
)

G(x,y)=0_{L}
iff
x=0_{L}
and
y=0_{L}
;

(
\mathbf{QG3}
)

G(x,y)=1_{L}
iff
x=1_{L}
or
y=1_{L}
;

(
\mathbf{QG4}
)

increasingness:
G(x,y)\leq_{L}G(x,z)
whenever
y\leq_{L}z
.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
G
is a quasi-grouping on
M
and
r:L\to M
is a retraction with pseudo-inverse
s:M\to L
satisfying Eq. (
1
) and Eq. (
2
), then
G^{E}:L^{2}\to L
, defined by

G^{E}(x,y)=s(G(r(x),r(y))),

(4)

is a quasi-grouping function that extends
G
from
M
to
L
.

Proof.

For all
(x,y)\in M\times M
, we have:

\displaystyle\left[G^{E}\circ(s\times s)\right](x,y)

\displaystyle=G^{E}(s(x),s(y))=s\left(G\left(r(s(x)),r(s(y))\right)\right)=s\left(G\left(x,y\right)\right)=\left(s\circ G\right)(x,y).

Thus,
G^{E}
extends
G
from
M
to
L
. In addition, the following properties hold:

(QG1)

G^{E}
is commutative, since
G
is commutative;

(QG2)

For all
(x,y)\in L^{2}
, we have:

\displaystyle G^{E}(x,y)=0_{L}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow s\left(G\left(r(x),r(y)\right)\right)=0_{L}

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.}\eqref{eq:extensofqgroup}.]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow G\left(r(x),r(y)\right)=0_{M}

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.\eqref{eq:extensofquas1} and $r\circ s=Id_{M}$.}]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow r(x)=0_{M}\ \text{and}\ r(y)=0_{M}

\displaystyle[\text{By \ref{item:QG2}.}]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow x=0_{L}\ \text{and}\ y=0_{L}

\displaystyle[\text{By Eq.}\eqref{eq:extensofquas1}.]

(QG3)

Analogous to (QG2).

(QG4)

G^{E}
is an increasing function since
r
,
s
and
G
are increasing.

∎

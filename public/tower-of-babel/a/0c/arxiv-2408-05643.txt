Enumerative geometry via elliptic stable envelope
Andrey Smirnov ⋆

Abstract
. Assume X X is a variety for which the elliptic stable envelope exists. In this note we construct natural q q -difference equations from the elliptic stable envelope of X X . In examples, these equations coincide with the quantum difference equations, which give a natural q q -deformation of the Dubrovin connection of X X . Solutions of the quantum difference equations provide generating functions counting curves in X X . In this way, our construction connects curve counting and equivariant elliptic cohomology. This is an overview paper based on the author’s talk at the workshop The 16th MSJ-SI: Elliptic Integrable Systems, Representation Theory and Hypergeometric Functions , Tokyo 2023.

1.
Introduction

Ideas surrounding the so called “
3D
-mirror symmetry” have been recently gaining in popularity among mathematicians.
This symmetry has its origin in theoretical physics where it generalises the “electromagnetic duality” to a more general class of quantum field theories. It is known that the Higgs branch
X
and the Coulomb branch
X^{!}
of such a theory are related in many non-trivial ways. For instance, as it was first observed in
[
Oko18
]
, certain elliptic cohomology classes of
X
and
X^{!}
, known as the elliptic stable envelopes, are connected by a set of non-trivial relations.

In
[
RSVZ21
]
we proposed to use these relations as a
definition
of the
3D
-mirror symmetry. Speaking informally, our definition says that two varieties
X
and
X^{!}
are
3D
-mirrors of each other if their elliptic stable envelope classes are related by a certain set of axioms.

The elliptic stable envelopes were defined in
[
AO21
]
, see also
[
DN23
]
for a construction in the context of quantum field theory. The existence of these characteristic classes imposes certain conditions on varieties. Nevertheless, there are large classes of varieties for which the elliptic stable envelope exists, including the Nakajima varieties, the bow varieties, etc. Many examples of
3D
-mirror pairs
X,X^{!}
satisfying the axioms of
[
RSVZ21
]
has been recently constructed
[
RSVZ21
,
RW
,
SZ22
,
BR23
]
.

The set up of this paper is the following: let us assume that we are given a pair of varieties
X
and
X^{!}
which are related by the
3D
-mirror symmetry so that their elliptic stable envelope classes satisfy the axioms of
[
RSVZ21
]
. We show that in this situation there exists a certain natural system of
q
-difference equations. In this paper we propose an explicit construction of these equations from the elliptic stable envelope classes. It is expected that the difference equations we construct in this way are the quantum difference equations for
X
and
X^{!}
which govern the quantum K-theories of these varieties.
The quantum difference equations (QDE) are the generalizations of the quantum differential equations in quantum cohomology to which they specialize in the limit
q\to 1
, see for instance
[
TZ24
]
. In this way, our construction allows one to extract the enumerative invariants of a variety, such as its quantum K-theory or cohomology, from the elliptic stable envelope classes.

The main idea of our construction is to use the elliptic stable envelope to describe the monodromy of QDE as it was suggested in
[
AO21
]
. The monodromy of QDE is rigid in the sense that the QDE itself can be reconstructed from its monodromy.

We start the exposition from a basic example of a scalar
q
-difference equation in the next section. In this example it is elementary to see that the monodromy of QDE is described by the elliptic stable envelope. It is also easy to see that the QDE can be reconstructed from the monodromy via an elementary limiting procedure. This note can be considered as a generalization of this example to the higher rank situation.

Finally, let us note that the QDE for Nakajima quiver varieties were constructed in
[
OS22
]
using a different method - in
[
OS22
]
for a Nakajima variety we fist construct a quantum group which acts on its equivariant K-theory. The building blocks of the QDE are then described in the representation theoretic terms, for instance, the wall-crossing operators for QDE are identified with the dynamical reflection operators in the corresponding quantum Weyl groups, see also
[
TZ23
]
, for details of this construction in type
A
. In this note we skip this intermediate step and describe the QDE without any reference to the representation theory and quantum groups.

This is an overview paper based on the author’s talk at the workshop
The 16th MSJ-SI: Elliptic Integrable Systems, Representation Theory
and Hypergeometric Functions
, Tokyo 2023. The narration is expository, we deal with all the relations informally and omit proofs. A more detailed discussion of ideas outlined here can be found in
[
KS22
,
KS23
,
Ko
,
Sm21
]
.

2.
basic example

2.1.

Let us consider the following
q
-difference equation (we assume
|q|<1
):

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Psi(zq)=\mathbf{M}(z)\Psi(z),\ \ \ \textrm{where}\ \ \ \mathbf{M}(z)=\dfrac{1-z}{1-z\hbar}.

This equation is the QDE for a “point” Nakajima quiver variety
X=T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{0}\cong pt
.
The solution of this equation analytic near
z=0
has the form:

\Psi_{0}(z)=\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\infty}\,\dfrac{(\hbar)_{d}}{(q)_{d}}z^{d}=\dfrac{(z\hbar,q)_{\infty}}{(z,q)_{\infty}},

where we denote

(x)_{d}=(1-x)(1-xq)\dots(1-xq^{d-1}),\ \ \ (x,q)_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{i}).

The other solution is

\Psi^{\prime}_{\infty}(z)=e^{-\frac{\ln(z)\ln(h)}{\ln(q)}}\,\Psi_{\infty}(z),\ \ \ \textrm{where}\ \ \ \Psi_{\infty}(z)=\hbar^{1/2}\sum\limits_{d=0}^{\infty}\,\dfrac{(\hbar)_{d}}{(q)_{d}}\dfrac{q^{d}}{\hbar^{d}}z^{-d}=\hbar^{1/2}\dfrac{(q/z,q)_{\infty}}{(q/(z\hbar),q)_{\infty}}.

Here
\Psi_{\infty}(z)
is part of the solution analytical near
z=\infty
.
The transition “matrix” from the solution near
z=0
to the solution near
z=\infty
is called the monodromy of the
q
-difference equation:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mon}(z)=\Psi_{\infty}(z)\Psi_{0}(z)^{-1}=\,\dfrac{\vartheta(z)}{\vartheta(z\hbar)},

where

(2.3)

\displaystyle\vartheta(z)=(z^{1/2}-z^{-1/2})(qz)_{\infty}(q/z)_{\infty}

denotes the elliptic theta function.

2.2.

The expression for monodromy (
2.2
) has the following interpretation in terms of the
3D
-mirror variety. The
3D
-mirror of
X
is the affine plane
X^{!}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
It can be viewed as a symplectic variety equipped with an action of a torus
\mathsf{A}^{!}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{z}\times\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{\hbar}
. The first factor acts on
X^{!}
by
(x,y)\to(zx,z^{-1}y)
. Its action preserves the canonical symplectic form
\omega=dx\wedge dy
on
X^{!}
. The second acts by
(x,y)\to(x,\hbar^{-1}y)
, it scales the symplectic form with the weight
\hbar^{-1}
.

The
\mathsf{A}^{!}
-fixed set consists of a single point
(X^{!})^{\mathsf{A}^{!}}=p
corresponding to the origin of the affine plane. Let us define the attracting sets of the fixed point by:

\textsf{Attr}_{+}(p)=\{a\in X^{!}:\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\,z\cdot(a)=p\}=\{y=0\},

and

\textsf{Attr}_{-}(p)=\{a\in X^{!}:\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\,z\cdot(a)=p\}=\{x=0\}.

In this case, the classes of the attracting sets
\mathsf{Stab}_{\pm}(p)=[\textsf{Attr}_{+}(p)]
in the equivariant elliptic cohomology
\textrm{Ell}_{\mathsf{A}^{!}}(X^{!})
are known as the
elliptic stable envelopes
of
p
corresponding to the chambers
z\to 0
and
z\to\infty
. Restricting these classes to
p
one finds:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left.\mathsf{Stab}_{+}(p)\right|_{p}=\vartheta(z),\ \ \ \left.\mathsf{Stab}_{-}(p)\right|_{p}=\vartheta(z\hbar).

The
elliptic
R
-matrix
of a variety
X^{!}
is defined as the transition matrix between the stable envelopes of torus fixed points corresponding to opposite chambers
[
AO21
]
. Therefore, from (
2.4
) we find that in our example the
R
-matrix equals:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{R}_{X^{!}}(z)=\mathsf{Stab}_{-}^{-1}\circ\mathsf{Stab}_{+}=\dfrac{\vartheta(z)}{\vartheta(z\hbar)}.

This is our first important observation:
the monodromy of the quantum difference equation of a variety
X
coincides with the elliptic
R
-matrix of the
3D
-mirror variety
X^{!}
.

2.3.

One can ask is it possible to reconstruct the quantum difference equation (
2.1
) from its monodromy (
2.2
)?

For
s\in\mathbb{Q}
(which we call “slope”) we have the following limit for the monodromy (
2.2
):

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})=\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\dfrac{\vartheta(zq^{s})}{\vartheta(z\hbar q^{s})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hbar^{\lfloor s\rfloor+1/2},\\
\dfrac{1-z}{1-z\hbar}\,\hbar^{s+1/2}.\end{array}\right.

where
\lfloor s\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}
denotes the floor function.
This identity follows directly from our definition of the theta function (
2.3
) as an infinite product - an elementary calculation shows that only finitely many factors of this product contribute to the limit
q\to 0
which gives (
2.3
).

Let
0<\epsilon\ll 1
be an infinitely small positive real number, then from the previous limit we find:

\displaystyle\Big(\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s+\epsilon})\Big)^{-1}\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&s\not\in\mathbb{Z},\\
\mathbf{M}(z),&s\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{array}\right.

A few things to note here: first, observe that (
2.3
) is a piecewise constant function of
s\in\mathbb{Q}
which “jumps” at the “hyperplane arrangement” in
\mathbb{Q}
corresponding to the integral points
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}
. If
s
is at a hyperplane, i.e.
z\in\mathbb{Z}
then we obtain
\mathbf{M}(z)
defining the QDE (
2.1
). The last observation suggests that
one can reconstruct the QDE by analysing the
q\to 0
limit of the monodromy
\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})
as a function of the slope
s
.

2.4.

Let us summarize the calculations we made for our basic example. First we showed that using elliptic cohomology of
3D
-mirror variety
X^{!}
one can determine the monodromy of quantum difference equation for
X
. Second, using certain limiting procedure, one can reconstruct the quantum difference equation from its monodromy. Speaking informally, this shows that the elliptic cohomology of the mirror variety
X^{!}
governs enumerative geometry of
X
.

We will show that this example has a fairly straightforward generalization to the case of
X
for which the elliptic stable envelopes and
3D
-mirrors
X^{!}
are defined. New features which appear for more general examples of
X
are the following. First, in general, QDE (
2.1
) is a first order
q
-difference equations of rank
r
, i.e.,
\mathbf{M}(z)
is a
r\times r
matrix where
r=\textrm{rk}(K(X))
. The solution
\Psi(z)
, correspondingly, will be given by
r\times r
matrix - the fundamental solution matrix of the QDE.
Similarly, the elliptic R-matrices (
2.5
) turn into rank
r
matrices with elliptic matrix elements.

In general, there are several Kähler variables
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, the analog of the limit (
2.3
) is formulated for a slope parameter
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
. Similarly to (
2.3
) the
q\to 0
limit of the monodromy
\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})
for
zq^{s}=(z_{1}q^{s_{1}},\dots,z_{m}q^{s_{m}})
turns out to be a piecewise constant function of
s
which changes value only when
s
crosses hyperplanes of a certain hyperplane arrangement
\mathfrak{H}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{m}
.

We shall identify the monodromy of QDE for a variety
X
with the elliptic
R
-matrix of
X^{!}
.
We then reconstruct the QDE for
X
by examining the
q\to 0
limit of the monodromy for non-generic values of the slope
s
, i.e., when
s\in\mathfrak{H}
. In the last section, we sketch this construction for the case of the Hilbert scheme of points
X=\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

3.
QDE for Nakajima varieties

3.1.

A nice set of varieties for which the elliptic stable envelope exists is provided by the Nakajima varieties
[
AO21
]
. These varieties are defined as GIT quotients

X_{\theta}=T^{*}\textrm{Rep}(Q)/\!\!/_{\!\theta}\mathsf{G}

where
\textrm{Rep}(Q)
is a complex vector space given by a representation of a quiver
Q
. The parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{|Q|}
, where
|Q|
is the number of vertices in the quiver, denotes a choice of the stability parameter for the GIT quotient. The space of stability parameters
\mathbb{R}^{|Q|}
is equipped with a set of hyperplanes containing
0\in\mathbb{R}^{|Q|}
which separate it into a set of chambers. The stability conditions
\theta
,
\theta^{\prime}
in the same chamber produce isomorphic GIT quotients
X_{\theta}\cong X_{\theta^{\prime}}
.

Let
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
be a complex toric variety, whose infinities correspond to chambers in
\mathbb{R}^{|Q|}
. The non-equivalent Nakajima varieties, thus, correspond to the infinities of
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
, see Fig.
1
.

\bar{\mathsf{Z}}

Example:
Let us consider a quiver
Q
consisting of one vertex and a framing Fig.
2
.

Let us consider a representation of
Q
with dimension
k
and framing dimension
n
.
In this case, the space of the stability conditions is
\mathbb{R}
and so there are only two chambers:

\theta>0,\ \ \ \theta<0.

Computation shows that for a positive stability condition
X_{\theta}=T^{*}Gr(k,n)
- the cotangent bundle over Grassmannian of
k
-subspaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a negative
\theta
we obtain
X_{\theta}=T^{*}Gr(n-k,n)
, see Section 11 in
[
MO19
]
for details. In this case the toric variety is
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The point
0\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
corresponds to
X_{\theta}=T^{*}Gr(k,n)
and
\infty\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
corresponds to
X_{\theta}=T^{*}Gr(n-k,n)
.

3.2.
Counting curves in Nakajima varieties

The Nakajima varieties have rich enumerative geometry, see
[
Oko17
]
for an introduction. One of the most important elements of this theory is the “capping operator” which counts rational
quasimaps
to
X
with non-singular and relative boundary conditions. We refer to Section 7.4 in
[
Oko17
]
for the definition of the capping. As a partition function of quasimaps, the capping is an element:

\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)\in K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta})_{loc}^{\otimes 2}[[z]]

If some basis of
K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta})
is fixed, then the capping is represented by a matrix:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)_{i,j}=\sum\limits_{H_{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,c_{d}(a)_{i,j}\,z^{d},\,\ \ \ i,j=1,\dots,\textrm{rk}(K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta}))

where
z^{d}=z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{m}^{d_{m}}
denote “Kähler parameters” counting the degrees
d=(d_{1},\dots,d_{m})\in H_{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Z})
of quasimaps.
Speaking very informally, the coefficients of this generating function are computing degree
d
curves in
X_{\theta}
:

c_{d}(a)_{i,j}=\textrm{``number of degree $d$ rational curves in $X_{\theta}$ meeting classes $i$ and $j$'' }

More precisely, the coefficients
c_{d}(a)_{i,j}
represent certain
\mathsf{A}
-equivariant integrals over the moduli space of quasimaps of degree
d
with certain incidence conditions corresponding to
i,j\in K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta})
. The equivariant integrals are computed by localization and thus are rational functions of the equivariant parameters
a
, i.e.,
c_{d}(a)_{i,j}\in K_{\mathsf{A}}(pt)_{loc}\cong\mathbb{Q}(a)
.

The sum in (
3.1
) is over the cone of effective curve classes
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{\textrm{eff}}\subset H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|Q|}
which is determined by the stability
\theta
. It is natural to think of the Kähler parameters
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
as local coordinates on
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
near the infinity corresponding to
X_{\theta}
. Capping (
3.1
) is thus a certain
analytic function in a neighborhood of
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
.

3.3.

It is known that
\Psi_{\theta}(z,a)
is a fundamental solution matrix of a certain natural
q
-difference equation
[
OS22
]
. This QDE has the following form:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(zq^{\mathcal{L}},a)\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a),\ \ \mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|Q|}

An important conjecture states that the QDE
is independent of the stability condition
\theta
. This means that the capping operators
\Psi_{\theta}(z,a)
for Nakajima varieties
X_{\theta}
with various stability conditions
\theta
provide a set of the fundamental solution matrices of
the same QDE.
The fundamental solution
\Psi_{\theta}(z,a)
corresponding a particular
\theta
is distinguished by its analiticity near
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
.

The natural question is to describe the transition matrix between two such fundamental solutions:

\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)=\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta^{\prime}}(z)\,\Psi_{X_{\theta^{\prime}}}(z,a)

The transition matrix
\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta\leftarrow\theta^{\prime}}(z)
is called
monodromy
of QDE. In practice, it is a certain rank
\textrm{rk}(K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta}))
matrix with elements given by elliptic functions of all parameters involved.

3.4.

The partition function satisfies also a certain dual system of
q
-difference equations in the equivariant parameters
a
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,aq^{\sigma})=\mathsf{S}_{\sigma}(z,a)\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a),\ \ \sigma\in\textrm{cochar}(\mathsf{A})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\dim\mathsf{A}},

which are called shift equations, see Section 8.2 in
[
Oko17
]
. The dependence of
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
on the Kähler parameters
z
and the equivariant parameters
a
is different, however.
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
is analytic in
z
(i.e., is a power series in
z
converging in some neighborhood of
0_{\theta}
). The coefficients
c_{d}(a)
are rational functions of
a
and thus
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
is not an analytic in
a
. Nevertheless, with the notion of
3D
-mirror symmetry the Kähler parameters and the equivariant parameters enter the story on equal terms as we will see in the next section.

4.
Monodromy of QDE from
3D
-mirror variety

4.1.

The symmetry
a\longleftrightarrow z
is explained best in the language of the so called
3D
-mirror symmetry
. The
3D
-mirror symmetry suggests the following picture: for varieties
X_{\theta}
there exists a set of companion varieties
X^{!}_{\sigma}
labeled by
\sigma\in\mathsf{cochar}(\mathsf{A})
. Each such variety is equipped with a natural equivariant curve counting which provides the capping operators:

\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(a,z)=\sum\limits_{d\in H_{2}(X^{!}_{\sigma},\mathbb{Z})_{\textrm{eff}}}\,c_{d}(a^{!})\,(z^{!})^{d}

where we denote by
a^{!}
the equivariant parameters and by
z^{!}
the Kähler parameters of
X^{!}_{\sigma}
.
One axiom of the
3D
-mirror symmetry is the identification of variables

(4.1)

\displaystyle z=a^{!},\ \ \ a=z^{!}

i.e., the equivariant parameters of
X_{\theta}
are identified with the Kähler parameters on the mirror side
X^{!}_{\sigma}
and vice versa. The second axiom is the existence of an isomorphism of the equivariant K-theories of
X_{\theta}
and
X^{!}_{\sigma}
. The main point is: under all these identifications the capping operators
\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(a^{!},z^{!})
provide fundamental solution matrices for
the same system of
q
-difference equations
(
3.2
) and (
3.3
). The fundamental solutions
\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(a^{!},z^{!})
are distinguished by their analyticity in
z^{!}
, i.e., via identification (
4.1
), they are analytic solutions in the equivariant parameters
a
(in a certain region).

In summary, the capping operators of
X_{\theta}
provide fundamental solution matrices analytic in
z
and the capping operators of the mirror varieties
X^{!}_{\sigma}
provide solutions analytic in
a
. This cures the asymmetry between the Kähler and the equivariant parameters discussed at the end of the previous section.

4.2.

Similarly to the case of
X_{\theta}
, the non-equivalent choices of mirror varieties
X^{!}_{\sigma}
are labeled by infinities of a certain toric variety
\bar{\mathsf{A}}
obtained by a compactification of the equivariant torus
\mathsf{A}
. The structure of
\bar{\mathsf{A}}
can be seen from the equivariant geometry of
X_{\theta}
as follows. For a choice of
\sigma\in\mathsf{cochar}(\mathsf{A})
and a torus fixed point component
i\in X^{\mathsf{A}}_{\sigma}
we define the attracting set:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Attr}_{\sigma}(i)=\{x\in X_{\sigma}:\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,\sigma(a)\cdot x\in i\}.

The space
\mathsf{cochar}(\mathsf{A})\otimes\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\dim A}
splits into a union of cones: two
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
are in the same cone if they produce the same attracting sets:
\mathsf{Attr}_{\sigma}(i)=\mathsf{Attr}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(i)
for all
i\in X^{\mathsf{A}}_{\theta}
.

This structure can also be seen as follows. Let us consider the union of hyperplanes:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\coprod\limits_{\alpha}\,\{\langle\alpha,\sigma\rangle=0\}\,\subset\mathsf{cochar}(\mathsf{A})\otimes\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\dim\mathsf{A}},

where
\alpha
runs over the set of
\mathsf{A}
-characters appearing in the normal bundles
N_{i}(X_{\theta})
to all torus fixed components
i\in X_{\theta}^{\mathsf{A}}
. These hyperplanes partition
\mathbb{R}^{\dim\mathsf{A}}
to the union of cones we need. The toric variety
\bar{\mathsf{A}}
is a compactification of the equivariant torus
\mathsf{A}
obtained by adding infinity points
0_{\sigma}
corresponding to these cones.

The whole picture is now symmetric: there are two toric varieties
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
and
\bar{\mathsf{A}}
, with local coordinates given by the Kähler and the equivariant parameters respectively. The QDE (
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is invariant under the toric coordinate changes on
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
and on
\bar{\mathsf{A}}
. In other words the QDE for all
X_{\theta}
and all
X^{!}_{\sigma}
are equivalent. The corresponding capping operators are distinguished by the following properties:
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
is the unique fundamental solution matrix which is analytic in the neighborhood of
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
, similarly
\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(z,a)
is the unique fundamental solution regular near
0_{\sigma}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}
, see Fig.
3
.

4.3.

Let us fix two infinity points
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
and
0_{\sigma}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}
. These points define two fundamental solution matrices
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
and
\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(z,a)
. Let
\Sigma_{\theta\leftarrow\sigma}(z,a)
denote the transition matrix between these two bases of solutions

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(z,a)=\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(z,a)\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a).

Note that
\Psi_{X^{!}_{\sigma}}(z,a)
is analytic in the neighborhood of
0_{\sigma}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}
. It is non-analytic in
z
and typically has infinitely many poles
z
in any neighborhood of
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
. For
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
the situation is opposite - it is analytic near
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
and has poles accumulating in any neighborhood of
0_{\sigma}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}
. The matrix
\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)
, thus, has an interesting property: it cancels the poles in
a
and adds new poles in
z
. For this reason the matrix
\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)
is sometimes refereed to as the “pole subtraction matrix”.

Such pole subtraction matrices can be constructed geometrically
[
AO21
]
using the stable envelope classes in the equivariant elliptic cohomology. Speaking very informally, these are the elliptic cohomology classes of the attracting sets:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta}}_{\sigma}(i)=[\textsf{Attr}_{\sigma}(i)]\in\textrm{Ell}_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta}).

Precise definition of these classes is more sophisticated and we refer to
[
AO21
]
for details.
The corresponding transition matrices have the form:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)_{i,j}=\left.\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta}}_{\sigma}({i})\right|_{j},\ \ \ i,j\in(X_{\theta})^{\mathsf{A}}.

The class (
4.5
) is called
the elliptic stable envelope
of the fixed point component
i\in X^{\mathsf{A}}_{\theta}
. In terms of the elliptic stable envelope maps we can write:

\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)=\Big(\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta}}_{\sigma}\Big)^{t}

where
t
denotes the transposition.

4.4.

Since the
3D
-mirror varieties come to our picture on equivalent grounds, the relation (
4.4
) remains true if we exchange
X_{\theta}
and
X^{!}_{\sigma}
, i.e., we have a transition matrix:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)=\Sigma_{\theta\leftarrow\sigma}(a,z)\Psi_{X_{\sigma}^{!}}(z,a)

which is given by the elliptic stable envelope classes of the
3D
-mirror variety
X^{!}_{\sigma}

\Sigma_{\theta\leftarrow\sigma}(a,z)=\Big(\mathsf{Stab}^{X^{!}_{\sigma}}_{\theta}\Big)^{t}.

Note that by (
4.1
) we may identify the stability condition
\theta
of
X_{\theta}
with a cocharacter of a torus
\mathsf{Z}
acting on
X^{!}_{\sigma}
. Using this cocharacter we can define the attracting sets and the elliptic envelope classes for the mirror
X^{!}_{\sigma}
in the same way as in (
4.2
) and (
4.5
).

We note that by construction:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)=\Sigma_{\theta\leftarrow\sigma}(a,z)^{-1}

It is known that the inverse of the stable envelope matrix is obtained by taking the opposite cocharacter
\sigma\to-\sigma
and a transposition, Proposition 3.4 in
[
AO21
]
. Thus, in components the equality (
4.8
) takes the form of a matrix identity:

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left.\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta}}_{\sigma}(i)\right|_{j}=\left.\mathsf{Stab}^{X^{!}_{\sigma}}_{-\theta}(j)\right|_{i}.

In (
4.9
) we assume that the parameters on both sides are identified via (
4.1
), and
X^{\mathsf{A}}_{\theta}=(X_{\sigma}^{!})^{\mathsf{Z}}
via 3D-mirror symmetry.

Equation (
4.9
) is remarkable: it relates the elliptic tautological classes of different varieties. One existing approach postulates (
4.9
) as the
definition of
3D
-mirror symmetry.
We may define that
X_{\theta}
and
X^{!}_{\sigma}
form a
3D
-mirror pair, if there exists an identification of parameters such that (
4.9
) holds. This approach was first suggested in
[
RSVZ21
]
and tested in the example of
X=T^{*}Gr(k,n)
- cotangent bundles to Grammarians (see example of Section
3.1
above). Since then, more examples of
3D
-mirror pairs satisfying this condition were constructed, see
[
RSVZ21
,
SZ22
,
RW
]
. A very rich set of
3D
-mirror pairs has been recently constructed using type
A
- bow varieties
[
BR23
]
. Type
A
bow varieties are naturally closed with respect to
3D
-mirror symmetry, i.e.,
3D
-mirror of a bow variety is also a bow variety. For this reason, describing the QDE for the bow varieties using the methods we outline in the present note might be an interesting problem.

4.5.

Let
0_{\theta},0_{-\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
be two opposite infinities. Let
\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)
and
\Psi_{X_{-\theta}}(z,a)
be the analytic solutions of QDE near these points. We denote by
\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)
the transition matrix between these bases of solutions

\Psi_{X_{\theta}}(z,a)=\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)\Psi_{X_{-\theta}}(z,a).

The matrix
\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)
is called the monodromy of QDE associated with
X_{\theta}
. From the discussion of the previous sections we have:

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)=\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow-\theta}(z,a)^{-1}\circ\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(z,a)

for any choice of
\sigma
see Fig.
3
. By (
4.6
) we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)=\Big((\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{-\theta}}_{\sigma})^{t}\Big)^{-1}\circ\Big(\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta}}_{\sigma}\Big)^{t},

and by (
4.9
) we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Mon}_{\theta}(z,a)=\Big(\mathsf{Stab}^{X^{!}_{\sigma}}_{\theta}\Big)^{-1}\circ\mathsf{Stab}^{X^{!}_{\sigma}}_{-\theta}.

The last formula expresses the monodromy matrix as the transition matrix from the basis of the elliptic cohomology of
X^{!}_{\sigma}
given by the elliptic stable envelope classes with cocharacter
\theta
to the basis of the elliptic stable envelope classes with the cocharacter
-\theta
. In
[
AO21
]
this transition matrix was called
the elliptic
R
-matrix
of
X^{!}_{\sigma}
.

Figure 3.
Transition matrices between various fundamental solutions

In summary, the monodromy of QDE can be determined via computation in the equivariant elliptic cohomology. In the next section we discuss a limiting procedure which allows one to reconstruct the QDE from its monodromy.

5.
K-theoretic limit of the monodromy matrix

5.1.
Factorization of K-theoretic limits

As in Section
2.3
we would like to investigate the limit of the monodromy matrix

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s},a),\ \ \ \textrm{where}\ \ \ zq^{s}=(z_{1}q^{s_{1}},\dots,z_{m}q^{s_{m}}),

as a function of the slope
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})\in H^{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}^{m}
. By (
4.11
) it is sufficient to understand this limit for the elliptic stable envelope. We now review the results of
[
KS22
,
KS23
,
Ko
]
where this analysis was performed under assumption that
X^{\mathsf{A}}_{\theta}
is finite, see also
[
Oko21
]
for a less restrictive approach.
Let us consider the stable envelope matrix
\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,z)

defied by (
4.6
). Recall that the space of the slopes
\mathbb{Q}^{m}
is equipped with a
\textrm{Pic}(X_{\theta})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{m}
- periodic hyperplane arrangement
\mathfrak{H}

[
OS22
]
. If
s
is generic, i.e.,
s\not\in\mathfrak{H}
, then Proposition 4.3 in
[
AO21
]
gives:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\theta}(a,zq^{s})=\mathcal{A}^{X_{\theta},s}_{\sigma}(a)

where
\mathcal{A}^{X_{\theta},s}_{\sigma}(a)
is the matrix of the fixed point components of the K-theoretic stable envelope classes (see Section 2 of
[
OS22
]
for definition of K-theoretic stable envelope) with slope
s
:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{X_{\theta},s}_{\sigma}(a)_{i,j}=\left.\mathsf{Stab}^{Kth,\,X_{\theta},\,s}_{\sigma}(i)\right|_{j}\in K_{\mathsf{A}}(pt),\ \ \ i,j\in X_{\theta}^{\mathsf{A}}.

Components of this matrix take values in the equivariant K-theory of a point, i.e., they are Laurent polynomials in the equivariant parameters
a
. Note that the matrix
\mathcal{A}^{X_{\theta},s}_{\sigma}(a)
does not depend on the Kähler variables
z
. It is known that
\mathcal{A}^{X_{\theta},s}_{\sigma}(a)
is a piecewise constant function of the slope
s
which changes value only when
s
crosses a hyperplane of
\mathfrak{H}
(compare this behavior with (
2.3
) in our basic example).

The most interesting to us is the case of non-generic slope
s\in\mathfrak{H}
. For such slopes the limit will depend on both the equivariant
a
and the Kähler parameters
z
(again, compare this behavior with (
2.3
)). The most interesting feature of this limit, is that the dependence on
a
and
z
can be separated: the limit factors to a product of two matrices one of which depends only on
a
and the second, essentially only on
z

[
KS23
]
.

Let
s
be an arbitrary slope, possibly at a wall or at an intersection of several hyperplanes of
\mathfrak{H}
. Let
s\pm\epsilon
be generic slopes, obtained from
s
by a small shift in the direction
\pm\theta
. Then, the limit with slope
s
can be factored (Theorem 3 in
[
KS23
]
):

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\,\theta}(a,zq^{s})=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s\pm\epsilon}(a).

The second factor in the right side is the matrix of the K-theoretic stable envelope (
5.3
) corresponding to the generic slopes parameters
s+\epsilon
or
s-\epsilon
.

The matrix
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)
has the following form

(5.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)=\mathcal{L}^{s}\,\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)\,(\mathcal{L}^{s})^{-1}

where in the right side we denote by
\mathcal{L}^{s}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
the fractional line bundle corresponding to
s
, namely
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}^{s})=s\in H^{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Q})
. In the basis of torus fixed points
\mathcal{L}^{s}
is represented by a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues given by certain monomials in equivariant parameters
a
with fractional powers
s
, see (
7.3
) for an example.

Finally, the matrix
\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)

depends only on the Kähler variables
z
and has the following geometric meaning. For a slope
s
we may associate certain finite group
G_{s}\subset\mathsf{Z}
acting on the mirror variety
X^{!}_{\sigma}

[
KS23
]
. Let
X^{!}_{s}=(X^{!}_{\sigma})^{G_{s}}\subset X^{!}_{\sigma}
be the subvariety in the
3D
-mirror variety fixed by
G_{s}
.
The matrix
\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)
then has the following description:

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)_{i,j}=\left.\mathsf{Stab}^{X^{!}_{s},Kth,\,\pm\epsilon}_{\theta}(i)\right|_{j},\ \ \ i,j\in(X^{!}_{s})^{\mathsf{Z}},

i.e., this is the matrix of the K-theoretic stable envelope for
X^{!}_{s}
with small ample or anti-ample slopes
\pm\epsilon
. Note that since
\mathsf{Z}
is commutative, these varieties have the same torus fixed points:
(X^{!}_{s})^{\mathsf{Z}}=(X^{!}_{\sigma})^{\mathsf{Z}}
.

We note that if
s
is generic, then
X^{!}_{s}=(X^{!}_{\sigma})^{\mathsf{Z}}
is a finite set of points. In this case
\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)=1
and (
5.4
) is same as (
5.2
).

Note that (
5.4
) produces two different factorization corresponding two choices of the slopes
s+\epsilon
and
s-\epsilon
.
Once again, in above formulas
\epsilon
denotes a small ample slope for
X_{\theta}
. One has to be careful when using this: for the variety with the opposite stability condition
X_{-\theta}
the small ample slope is
-\epsilon
and the analog of the formula (
5.4
) in this case is:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\Sigma_{\sigma\leftarrow\,-\theta}(a,zq^{s})=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\mp}_{-\theta}(z)\,\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s\pm\epsilon}(a).

5.2.
Limiting factorizations

Note that if before taking the limit in (
5.4
) we in addition deform the argument
z\to zq^{\epsilon}
by a small ample
\epsilon
then the right side turns to
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s\pm\epsilon}(a)
by (
5.2
). We note also that

zq^{\epsilon}\to 0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
as
q\to 0
. Thus from (
5.4
) we obtain:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,+}_{\theta}(0_{\theta})\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}(a)=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,-}_{\theta}(0_{\theta})\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s-\epsilon}(a)=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}(a),

in particular,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,+}_{\theta}(0_{\theta})=1.

Same logic for shifts
z\to q^{-\epsilon}
gives:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,+}_{\theta}(0_{-\theta})\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}(a)=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,-}_{\theta}(0_{-\theta})\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s-\epsilon}(a)=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s-\epsilon}(a),

in particular,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,-}_{\theta}(0_{-\theta})=1.

Applying same argument to (
5.7
) we also obtain:

(5.12)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\mp}_{-\theta}(0_{-\theta})\,\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s\pm\epsilon}(a)=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s-\epsilon}(a),

(5.13)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,\mp}_{-\theta}(0_{\theta})\,\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s\pm\epsilon}(a)=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s+\epsilon}(a).

In particular

(5.14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,+}_{-\theta}(0_{-\theta})=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{s,-}_{-\theta}(0_{\theta})=1.

5.3.
Limits of the monodromy

From (
4.10
) and using (
5.4
) with slope
-
and (
5.7
) with slope
+
we find:

(5.15)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s-\epsilon}(a)^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{s}\,\mathcal{Z}^{s,+}_{-\theta}(z)^{-1}\mathcal{Z}^{s,+}_{\theta}(z)\,(\mathcal{L}^{s})^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}(a)

The middle factor here
\mathcal{R}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)=\mathcal{Z}^{s,+}_{-\theta}(z)^{-1}\mathcal{Z}^{s,+}_{\theta}(z)
is the transition matrix between two K-theoretic stable bases in K-theory of
X^{!}_{s}
, corresponding to cocharacters
\theta
and
-\theta
. In terminology of
[
OS22
]
the matrix
\mathcal{R}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)
is the K-theoretic R-matrix of
X^{!}_{s}
.
For generic slopes
s\not\in\mathfrak{H}
,
X^{!}_{s}
is a finite set,
\mathcal{Z}^{s,\pm}_{\theta}(z)=\mathcal{Z}^{s,+}_{-\theta}(z)=1

and therefore:

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s}(a)^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s}(a),\ \ \ \textrm{if}\ \ \ s\not\in\mathfrak{H}.

5.4.
The wall crossing operators

As in Section
2
, formula (
2.3
) we define two types of
wall-crossing
operators:

(5.17)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \mathbf{B}_{s}(z)=\Big(\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s-\epsilon})\Big)^{-1}\,\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s}),\ \ \mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)=\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s})\,\Big(\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\,\mathsf{Mon}(zq^{s+\epsilon})\Big)^{-1}

We call
\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)
the wall-crossing operator for
X_{\theta}
and
\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)

the wall-crossing operator for
X_{-\theta}
corresponding to
s
.
We note that (
5.17
) defines the wall crossing operators for an arbitrary slope
s\in H^{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Q})
, including the case when
s
belongs to an intersection of several hyperplanes of
s\in\mathfrak{H}
. We note also that for generic slopes

(5.18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)=\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)=1,\ \ \ \textrm{if}\ \ \ s\not\in\mathfrak{H}.

Form (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) we obtain:

\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&s\not\in\mathfrak{H},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s-\epsilon}(a)^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}(a),&s\in\mathfrak{H}.\end{array}\right.

and

\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&s\not\in\mathfrak{H},\\
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s-\epsilon}(a)^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s+\epsilon}(a),&s\in\mathfrak{H}.\end{array}\right.

where we denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)=\mathcal{L}^{s}\,\mathcal{R}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)\,(\mathcal{L}^{s})^{-1}
the K-theoretic
R
-matrix of
X^{!}_{s}
twisted by the fractional line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{s}
.

The last formulas say that the wall-crossing operator
\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)
acts in the stable basis of
K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta})
as the K-theoretic
R
-matrix of
X^{!}_{s}
. To be more precise, it says that applying the operator
\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)
to the stable basis
\mathsf{Stab}^{s-\epsilon}_{\sigma}(i)
and expanding the results in the stable basis
\mathsf{Stab}^{s+\epsilon}_{\sigma}(j)
,
i,j\in(X_{\theta})^{\mathsf{A}}
we obtain the matrix which coincides with the twisted R-matrix of
X^{!}_{s}
:

(5.19)

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta},s+\epsilon}_{\sigma}(i)|\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)|\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{\theta},s-\epsilon}_{\sigma}(j)\rangle=\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)_{i,j}.

Similarly,
\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)
acts naturally in
K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{-\theta})
by:

(5.20)

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{-\theta},s+\epsilon}_{\sigma}(i)|\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)|\mathsf{Stab}^{X_{-\theta},s-\epsilon}_{\sigma}(j)\rangle=\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{X^{!}_{s}}(z)_{i,j}.

This formula gives a tool for computing the action of the wall-crossing operators on
K_{\mathsf{A}}(X_{\theta})
if the
3D
-mirror variety is known.

5.5.
The wall-crossing operators at infinities of
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}

Combining (
5.15
)-(
5.17
), from formulas (
5.8
)-(
5.14
) we get:

(5.21)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{s}(0_{\theta})=\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(0_{-\theta})=1

i.e., the wall crossing operators for
X_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}
trivialize in at the infinities of
\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
corresponding to these varieties.

5.6.
Transport operators

We denote limit (
5.16
) by:

{\bf T}^{s}:=\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{-\theta},s}(a)^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}^{X_{\theta},s}(a),\ \ \ s\not\in\mathfrak{H}

then from (
5.17
) we find

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)\,{\bf T}^{s+\epsilon}={\bf T}^{s-\epsilon}\,\mathbf{B}_{s}(z).

Thus from (
5.22
) and (
5.21
) we also obtain:

(5.23)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(0_{\theta})\,{\bf T}^{s+\epsilon}={\bf T}^{s-\epsilon},\ \ \ {\bf T}^{s+\epsilon}={\bf T}^{s-\epsilon}\,\mathbf{B}_{s}(0_{-\theta}).

Let
s
and
s^{\prime}
be two generic slopes. Let us fix a path from
s
to
s^{\prime}
which intersects
\mathfrak{H}
at the points
w_{1},w_{2},\dots w_{m}
in the positive direction. Iterating (
5.23
) several times we obtain:

(5.24)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{m}}(0_{\theta})\dots\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{1}}(0_{\theta})\,{\bf T}^{s}={\bf T}^{s^{\prime}},\ \ \ \mathbf{B}_{w_{m}}(0_{-\theta})\dots\mathbf{B}_{w_{1}}(0_{-\theta})\,{\bf T}^{s^{\prime}}={\bf T}^{s}.

We call the
{\bf T}^{s}

the transport operator
with slope
s
. It can be shown that this operator computes the transport (or monodromy) of the Dubrovin connection for
X_{\theta}
from
0_{\theta}
to
0_{-\theta}
over the line with slope
s
, see Section 8 in
[
Sm21
]
.

From the “window” condition for the
K
-theoretic stable envelope
[
OS22
]
it follows that the wall-crossing operators satisfy:

(5.25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{B}_{s-\mathcal{L}}(z),\ \ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z)\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s-\mathcal{L}}(z),\ \ \ \mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta}).

6.
Reconstructing QDE from monodromy matrix

6.1.
q
-difference equations

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta})
be a line bundle. Let
-\epsilon\in H^{2}(X_{\theta},\mathbb{Q})
be a small generic anti-ample slope and
-\epsilon-\mathcal{L}
be the slope shifted by
\mathcal{L}
.
Choose a path from
-\epsilon
to
-\epsilon-\mathcal{L}
which intersects the hyperplane arrangement
\mathfrak{H}
at
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
. We define operators

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)=\mathcal{L}\,\mathbf{B}_{w_{m}}(zq^{-w_{m}})\dots\mathbf{B}_{w_{1}}(zq^{-w_{1}}).

and

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)=\mathcal{L}\,\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{m}}(zq^{-w_{m}})\dots\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{1}}(zq^{-w_{1}}).

where
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a complex parameter. We will assume that
|q|<1
.

We call
\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)
and
\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)
the
q
-difference operators for
X_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}
respectively. It follows from our construction of the wall-crossing operators that
\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)
and
\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)
are well-defined, i.e., the are independent of the choice of a path from
-\epsilon
to
-\epsilon-\mathcal{L}
.

In particular, we have

\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}\mathcal{L}_{2}}(z)=\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(zq^{\mathcal{L}_{2}})\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(z)=\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(zq^{\mathcal{L}_{1}})\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(z).

and the same for
\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(z)
.
The last equation means that the following systems of
q
-difference equations

(6.3)

\displaystyle\Psi(zq^{\mathcal{L}})\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)\Psi(z),\ \ \ \mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta}),

and

(6.4)

\displaystyle\Psi(zq^{\mathcal{L}})\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)\Psi(z),

are well defined. From (
5.22
) we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle({\bf T}^{-\epsilon})^{-1}\,\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(z)\,{\bf T}^{-\epsilon}=\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(z).

and thus (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) are equivalent systems of
q
-difference equations. In the next section we show that (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) are exactly the QDEs for
X_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}
.

Since by (
5.21
)
\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(0_{\theta})
is diagonal in the basis of torus fixed points, the columns of the matrix
\mathbf{T}^{-\epsilon}
are the eigenvectors of
\mathbf{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{L}}(0_{\theta})
. Similarly,
(\mathbf{T}^{-\epsilon})^{-1}
gives the eigenvectors of
\mathbf{M}_{\mathcal{L}}(0_{-\theta})
.

6.2.
Monodromy of
q
-difference equation

The
q
-difference equation (
6.3
) is constructed so that its monodromy coincides with monodromy of QDE (
4.11
). Since
q
-difference equations with the same monodromy are equivalent, it follows that (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) are the QDEs for
X_{\theta}
and
X_{-\theta}
respectively.

To see it, we note that by the properly (
5.25
) and by
\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\theta})
-invariance of the hyperplane arrangement
\mathfrak{H}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{m}
the infinite product

(6.6)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\theta}(z)=\mathbf{B}_{w_{-1}}(zq^{-w_{-1}})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{w_{-2}}(zq^{-w_{-2}})^{-1}\cdots=\prod\limits_{i<0}^{\leftarrow}\,\mathbf{B}_{w_{i}}(zq^{{-w_{i}}})^{-1}

is a formal solution (
6.3
). The product in (
6.6
) runs over a set of intersections with hyperplanes
w_{i}
of a path from a small anti-ample slope
-\epsilon
to infinity in the direction of
-\theta
, see Fig.
4
b). By the arrow in the product we indicate the the operators are multiplied in the order in which
\langle s,w_{i}\rangle
increases:
\langle s,w_{-1}\rangle\geqslant\langle s,w_{-2}\rangle\geqslant\dots
. By (
5.21
) this infinite product converges for
z
in a small analytic neighborhood of
0_{\theta}\in\bar{\mathsf{Z}}
. Thus (
5.21
) is a fundamental solution matrix of (
6.3
) analytic near
0_{\theta}
.

Similarly, by (
5.25
) the infinite product

(6.7)

\displaystyle\Psi_{-\theta}(z)=\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{0}}(zq^{-w_{0}})\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{1}}(zq^{-w_{1}})\cdots=\prod\limits_{i\geqslant 0}^{\rightarrow}\,\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{i}}(zq^{{-w_{i}}})\,

is a formal solution of (
6.4
).
The product in (
6.7

By (

Limits of fundamental solutions

Assume that
s

\lim\limits_{q\to 0}q^{s}\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0_{\theta},&\langle s,\theta\rangle>0\\

Thus, from (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\Psi_{\theta}(zq^{s})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\langle s,\theta\rangle\geqslant 0,\\

&\\
\Big(\mathbf{B}_{w_{-1}}(0_{-\theta})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{w_{-2}}(0_{-\theta})^{-1}\dots\Big)\mathbf{B}_{s}(z)^{-1}&\langle s,\theta\rangle<0.\end{array}\right.

Similarly from (

) we obtain:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{q\to 0}\Psi_{-\theta}(zq^{s})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{0}}(0_{\theta})\mathbf{B}^{*}_{w_{1}}(0_{\theta})\dots\Big)\mathbf{B}^{*}_{s}(z),&\langle s,\theta\rangle\geqslant 0,\\

In (

by (

Combining all this together, from (

and therefore

In this section we outline the construction of the QDE in an example of the quiver variety given by the Hilbert scheme

We identify

s\in\mathbb{Q}
and the hyperplanes corresponds to the values of
s
where (
5.4

A combinatorial formula for the elliptic stable envelope of the Hilbert scheme

was obtained in
[

5.4
) and determine the hyperplane arrangement

, see Theorem 9 in

]
:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}=\{s=\dfrac{a}{b}\in\mathbb{Q}:0<|b|\leqslant n\}\subset\mathbb{Q}.

7.2.
The wall crossing operators

The Hilbert scheme
X_{\theta}
is self-dual with respect to the
3D
-mirror symmetry
X^{!}_{\sigma}\cong\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

[
KZ23
]
. Given a wall
s=\frac{a}{b}
from the set (
7.1
), we consider the group of
b
-th roots of unity

G_{s}=\langle e^{2\pi is}\rangle\subset\mathsf{Z}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Where
\mathsf{Z}
is the torus acting on the mirror Hilbert scheme
X^{!}_{\sigma}
. The fixed points
(X^{!}_{\sigma})^{G_{s}}
are determined from the quiver description of

. As a quiver variety,

is described by the Jordan quiver Fig.
5

\mathbb{C}^{n}
assigned to the vertex and
\mathbb{C}
assigned to the framing vertex.

The group

acts on
X^{!}_{\sigma}
via loop rotation, i.e., by
(I,J,A,B)\to(I,J,e^{2\pi is}A,e^{-2\pi is}B)
. By the standard argument, the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a representation of
G_{s}
splits to the weight subspaces

\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{C}^{n}_{b-1}

so that an element
e^{-2\pi is}\in G_{s}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}_{m}
via multiplication by
e^{2\pi mis}
. Thus, the
G_{s}
-invariant matrix elements of the operators
A=\oplus_{i}A_{i},B=\oplus_{i}B_{i}
are:

A_{i}:\mathbb{C}^{n}_{i}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}_{i+1},\ \ \ B_{i}:\mathbb{C}^{n}_{i+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}_{i}.

and the components of the
G_{s}
-fixed points in
X^{!}_{\sigma}
are described by the cyclic quiver varieties with
b
-vertices Fig.
5

(7.2)

\displaystyle X^{!}_{s}:=(X^{!}_{\sigma})^{G_{s}}=\coprod\limits_{n_{0}+\dots+n_{b-1}=n}X(n_{0},\dots,n_{b-1}).

Locality Galois groups of meromorphic germs in several variables
Li Guo, Sylvie Paycha, Bin Zhang

Abstract
. Meromorphic germs in several variables with linear poles naturally arise in mathematics in various disguises. We investigate their rich structures under the prism of locality, including locality subalgebras, locality transformation groups and locality characters. The key technical tool is the dependence subspace for a meromorphic germ with which we define a locality orthogonal relation between two meromorphic germs. We describe the structure of locality subalgebras generated by classes of meromorphic germs with certain types of poles. We also define and determine their group of locality transformations which fix the holomorphic germs and preserve multivariable residues, a group we call the locality Galois group. We then specialise to two classes of meromorphic germs with prescribed types of nested poles, arising from multiple zeta functions in number theory and Feynman integrals in perturbative quantum field theory respectively. We show that they are locality polynomial subalgebras with locality polynomial bases given by the locality counterpart of Lyndon words. This enables us to explicitly describe their locality Galois group. As an application, we propose a mathematical interpretation of Speer’s analytic renormalisation for Feynman amplitudes. We study a class of locality characters, called generalised evaluators after Speer. We show that the locality Galois group acts transitively on generalised evaluators by composition, thus providing a candidate for a renormalisation group in this multivariable approach.

1.
Introduction

This work seeks to reveal the rich structure of meromorphic germs in several variables with linear poles, to describe subalgebras, to explore the structure of transformation group, to evaluate them at poles in a consistent manner, and to compare different evaluations. The locality framework developed by the authors
[
CGPZ1
]
, appears to be well suited to achieve these goals.

1.1.
From one to multivariable renormalisation

Renormalisation is a procedure used to evaluate divergent expressions in various areas of physics and mathematics, ranging from the classical instance of Feynman amplitudes in perturbative quantum field theory
[
t’H
,
t’HV
]
to multiple zeta functions at poles (see
[
GZ
,
MP
]
for example) and Todd functions for toric varieties see, e.g.
[
BV2
,
P
]
.
In either case, a preliminary step is a regularisation procedure, after which one can extract divergences and evaluate at the poles.
There is a great freedom in the choice of regularisation and the method of extracting divergences, specifically, a regularisation can involve one or multiple parameters.

The algebraic structure underlying single parameter renormalisation
has attracted great interest in mathematics since the groundbreaking work of Connes and Kreimer
[
CK1
,
CK2
]
to tackle Feynman integrals.

In their approach, the combinatorics of the divergent expressions are organized into a connected Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
, while the regularisations of the divergent expressions have their Laurent series expansions in the Rota-Baxter algebra
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}[z^{-1},z]]
,
characterised by its linear decomposition

(1)

{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})={\mathcal{M}}_{-}(\mathbb{C})\oplus{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}),

into subalgebras
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(\mathbb{C}):=z^{-1}\mathbb{C}[z^{-1}],{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}):=\mathbb{C}[[z]]
and with the induced projection

(2)

\pi_{+}:{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}),\quad f(z)=\sum_{k=-K}^{\infty}a_{k}\,z^{k}\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\,z^{k}.

The regularisation map is enriched to an algebra homomorphism

(3)

\phi:\mathcal{H}\to{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}),

which then factorises according to the algebraic Birkhoff factorisation, as the convolution product
\phi=\phi_{-}^{-1}\star\phi_{+}
of a holomorphic part
\phi_{+}
with values in the subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C})
and a polar part
\phi_{-}
with values in
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}(\mathbb{C})
. The renormalised map
\phi_{+}
which is then evaluated at the poles,
is built inductively with the recursion encoded in the coproduct, reflecting the celebrated BPHZ procedure in perturbative quantum field theory
[
BP
,
Hep
,
Zi
]
.

Extending the Connes-Kreimer approach to multiple parameter regularisations leads to an algebra homomorphism

\phi:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})

with values in
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
, the algebra of multivariable meromorphic germs at zero with linear poles for the Hopf algebra of convex polyhedral cones
[
GPZ
]
and then later in a more general locality framework
[
CGPZ1
,
CGPZ2
,
CGPZ3
]
. This approach uses in an essential way a locality version of the Rota-Baxter algebraic structure on
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
.

1.2.
Locality for multivariable meromorphic germs

In physics, the principle of locality is a key feature of field theory which states that an object is influenced directly only by its immediate surroundings. We interpret locality more generally as certain binary relations
[
CGPZ1
]
, enhance algebras to locality algebras and call locality morphisms, the morphisms that preserve such locality relations. Its relation to causality in quantum field theory was discussed in
[
Re
]
.
In this paper the locality relation on meromorphic germs is induced by an inner product
Q
on the underlying vector space in the following way. A pair of meromorphic germs lies in the graph of the locality relation if the linear spaces spanned by the sets of variables they respectively depend on, called their
dependence subspaces
, are mutually orthogonal.This locality provides a natural splitting

{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})={\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})\oplus{\mathcal{M}}_{-}^{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})

into the subspace
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
of holomorphic germs and a space
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}^{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
of what we call “polar germs”.
While
{\mathcal{M}}_{-}^{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
is not a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}^{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
, it bares the remarkable property of being a
locality ideal
.

In applications
[
CGPZ2
,
CGPZ3
]
, we equip the Hopf algebra with a locality structure, turning it into a locality Hopf algebra, and
\phi
becomes a locality morphism of algebras.
Thanks to the fact that
{\mathcal{M}}^{Q}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
is a locality ideal, not only the algebraic Birkhoff factorisation of
\phi
can be recovered in the locality setting, moreso, the renormalisation procedure
\phi_{+}
simplifies to the post composition
\pi_{+}^{Q}\circ\phi
with the projection

\pi_{+}^{Q}:{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})

on the holomorphic part. Its composition
{\rm ev}_{0}\circ\pi_{+}^{Q}
with the evaluation at zero
{\rm ev}_{0}:{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})\to\mathbb{C}
on the resulting holomorphic germs, can be viewed as a
multivariable minimal substraction scheme
.

A prototype of this approach was proposed in the pioneering work of Speer
[
Sp2
,
Sp4
]
on analytic renormalisation in quantum field theory. This multivariable renormalisation method was later implemented for the baby model of Riemann integrals indexed by rooted trees in
[
CGPZ3
]
and further discussed in
[
DZ
]
.

Thus while passing from the classical approach of one parameter renormalisation to the locality approach of multiple parameter renormalisation, the focus of analysis is shifted from the source Hopf algebra
\mathcal{H}
of
\phi
to the target algebra
\phi(\mathcal{H})
in
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
discussed in this paper.

1.3.
Locality Galois groups and locality Lyndon words

In practice, we consider locality subalgebras
\phi(\mathcal{H})\subset{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
. A linear transformation
T
on
{\mathcal{A}}
induces another morphism

T\circ\phi:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty}),

from which we can again build a map

\pi_{+}^{Q}\circ T\circ\phi:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty}).

Locality isomorphisms of locality subalgebras
{\mathcal{A}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
, which restrict to the identity map on
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
form a group
{\rm Gal}^{Q}({\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{M}}_{+})
we call
locality Galois groups
. It plays the role of a renormalisation group
[
CK2
,
CM
]
in relating different renormalisations.

Evaluating at zero by the map
{\rm ev}_{0}
, gives rise to a locality character

{\mathcal{E}}_{T}:={\rm ev}_{0}\circ\pi_{+}^{Q}\circ T\circ\phi:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

which after Speer, we call an evaluator, depending on the choice of
T
.

To describe the action of the locality Galois group, we give a careful study of the structure of locality subalgebras of
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
, defined by prescribed types of linear poles. We focus on Chen type poles which typically arise from multiple zeta functions and on the more general class of Speer fractions which arise in Feynman integrals.
We show that they both form
locality polynomial algebras
, with a locality polynomial basis given a locality version of Lyndon words, we call
locality Lyndon words
. For this purpose, we enhance to the locality setup, the realisation of shuffle product algebras as polynomial algebras on Lyndon words in the classical work of Chen-Fox-Lyndon and Radford
[
CFL
,
Ra
]
.
As an application, transitivity is established for the action of the locality Galois groups on the generalised evaluators on these two classes of locality subalgebras.

1.4.
Generalised evaluators and locality characters

In his seminal work
[
Sp1
,
Sp2
,
Sp3
,
Sp4
]
on analytic renormalisation, Speer gives an axiomatic formulation for the evaluation of regularised quantities, called generalised evaluators
which he applies to spaces of meromorphic germs in several variables singled out by the regularisation step in his study of Feynman integrals. In the final renormalisation step, he proposes a generalised evaluator defined by averaging over iterated one dimensional evaluators successively applied in each variable.

Speer’s pioneering multivariable approach nevertheless lacks a covariance property since it is coordinate dependent. To circumvent this coordinate dependence, we require the evaluator to be multiplicative on products of germs depending on variables which span mutually perpendicular spaces (the dependence subspaces mentioned above) instead of them having disjoint sets of variables as in Speer’s work. This paper provides a covariant counterpart of Speer’s approach in a sound mathematical framework, with the aim of setting up a general framework to tackle divergences in various contexts and with the following three goals in mind.

Problem 1.1
.

Equip the space of germs arising in Speer’s and other multiparameter regularisations with appropriate
locality polynomial algebra
structures;

Problem 1.2
.

Interpret Speer’s generalised evaluators as
locality characters
on the corresponding (locality) algebras, leading to a general concept of
locality generalised evaluator
;

Problem 1.3
.

Build a
transformation group
which relates different locality evaluators.

1.5.
Outline of the paper

In the coalgebraic approach to renormalisation in one variable à la Connes and Kreimer, one calls upon an inductive procedure to deal with mutual compensations of divergences among different levels. Instead, here we want to avoid the occurence of such compensations by means of a multiparameter regularisation, which enables us to regularise each subdivergence in an autonomous way by introducing a different parameter at each level as we go deeper in the subdivergences. This is possible using locality structures, which take care of keeping the different levels separate in requiring the regularisation map to be a locality algebra homomorphism with range in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We first provide some background in Section
2
on the space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of meromorphic germs with linear poles and rational coefficients on the filtered lattice space
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}^{\infty})
. For a given inner product
Q
on the underlying space, a complement of the holomorphic germs
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
is given by the subspace
{\mathcal{M}}^{Q}_{\mathbb{Q}-}
of polar germs defined in Eq. (
5
).
This gives rise to Laurent expansions and various decompositions and invariants (residues) in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
(Theorem
2.5
), which serve as the building blocks of our further study.
We further give a detailed study of dependence spaces of meromorphic germs and their decompositions in §
2.3
(Theorem
2.13
), in order to define the orthogonality of meromorphic germs.

With the above orthogonality of meromorphic germs at hand, we carry out a careful study of locality algebras in Section
3
, focusing on subalgebras of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. §
3.1
gives a description of the structures of a locality subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, which contains
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
and is generated by a set of fractions
{\mathcal{S}}
. The automorphisms of such a locality subalgebra, which fix the holomorphic germs and preserve residue type invariants of polar germs, are shown to form a group in §

and §

. Inspired by Cartier’s cosmic Galois group
[
B
,
C
,
CM
]
, we call it the

of the locality subalgebra (Definition
3.13
).
A reduction theorem (Theorem
3.15
) of locality Galois groups is obtained, which shows that there is a subgroup of the locality Galois group that can be described by special automorphisms of the locality subalgebra generated
over
\mathbb{Q}
by the same set of fractions
{\mathcal{S}}
.

To obtain the structure of locality Galois groups, in Section
4
we first give a locality variant of polynomial algebras (Definition
4.1
) and then extend the polynomial generation of shuffle product algebras by Lyndon words to the locality setting (Theorem
4.4
). We finally show (Theorem
4.9
) that the locality subalgebras generated by certain classes of fractions are locality polynomial algebras. These include Chen fractions arising in multiple zeta functions, described in Example
2.7
and Speer fractions described in Example

, named after Speer in acknowledgment of his work on analytic renormalisation
[
Sp2
,
Sp3
,
Sp4

(see also
[

,
DZ
]
).

Finally in Section
5

5.1
) that the space spanned by the fractions arising from Speer’s s-families is precisely the space of the aforementioned Speer fractions, and hence it is a locality polynomial algebra. This addresses Problem
1.1
.

As an analog of Speer’s generalised evaluators, in §
5.2
we introduce the notion of locality generalised evaluators (Definition
5.2
) on a locality subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. They are linear forms
{\mathcal{E}}
extending the usual evaluation
{\rm ev}_{0}
at
0
defined on holomorphic germs and, in accordance with the locality principle, they are required to obey the following locality multiplicativity:

f_{1}\perp^{Q}f_{2}\Rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(f_{1}\,f_{2})={\mathcal{E}}(f_{1})\,{\mathcal{E}}(f_{2}).

A proptotype is the minimal subtraction evaluator

, see
Eq. (

).

We further show in Proposition

[
DPS
]
) and when adapting the locality relation appropriately. This addresses Problem

. Along the way, we discuss the difference between Speer’s coordinate dependent generalised evaluator

The locality Galois group naturally acts on locality generalised evaluators by composition (Eq. (

Applying these results to Chen fractions and Speer fractions, we obtain Corollary

Finally we show that multiple zeta values naturally give rise to a locality generalised evaluator

In particular, there is a decomposition
(
see also
[

]
)
:

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{K}}={\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{K}+}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}^{Q}_{\mathbb{K}-}.

We give further notations.

(
i
)

Corresponding to the decomposition in Eq. (

), let
q
be the highest p-order of the polar germs in a (thus every) Laurent expansion of

. Define the

of
f

[

by

{\rm pRes}(f)\,{:}=\sum_{\text{\rm p-ord}(S_{i})=q}\frac{h_{i}(0)}{\vec{L}_{i}^{\vec{s}_{i}}}.

(

Corresponding to the decomposition in Eq. (

), let

Remark 2.6
.

As proved in

2.3.
Dependence subspaces

For any subset
U
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, let

\mathbb{Q}U
denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-subspace of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{K}}
spanned by
U
.

A
simplex fraction
is a fraction of the form
\frac{1}{L_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots L_{k}^{s_{k}}}
, where
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are linearly independent and
s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of all simplex fractions over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then for any inner product
Q
in
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}^{\infty})
, we trivially have
\mathbb{Q}{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}-}^{Q}.

Example 2.7
.

In the Euclidean filtered lattice space
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}^{\infty},Q)
, let
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be an orthonormal basis.
Let
z_{i}
be the coordinate function corresponding to
e_{i}
. A fraction of the form

(12)

{\mathfrak{f}}\Big(\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}}\\
\scriptstyle{{u_{1}},\ldots,{u_{k}}}\end{array}\Big)\coloneqq\frac{1}{z_{u_{1}}^{s_{1}}(z_{u_{1}}+z_{u_{2}})^{s_{2}}\cdots(z_{u_{1}}+z_{u_{2}}+\cdots+z_{u_{k}})^{s_{k}}}\,,\ u_{i},s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},k\in\mathbb{N},u_{i}\neq u_{j}\,{\rm if}\ i\neq j,

is called a
Chen fraction
.
The set of Chen fractions is denoted by

{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm Ch}\coloneqq{\mathcal{F}}^{{\rm Ch},Q,\mathcal{B}}.

We borrow the following definitions from
[
GPZ3
]
.
A meromorphic function
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
of the form
f=g(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
, where
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
are linear forms on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
and

a meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, is said to
depend on
the linear subspace of
(\mathbb{C}^{k})^{*}
spanned by
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
.
One can check that if
f
depends on
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, then it depends on
V_{1}\cap V_{2}
. Thus it makes sense to set the following definition.

Definition 2.8
.

The
dependence subspace

{\rm Dep}(f)
of

is the smallest linear subspace of
(\mathbb{C}^{k})^{*}
on which
f
depends.

Example 2.9
.

Let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
be an orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}^{\infty},Q)
.

{\rm Dep}\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}(z_{1}+z_{2})}+\frac{1}{z_{2}(z_{1}+z_{2})}-\frac{2}{z_{1}(z_{1}+2z_{2})}-\frac{1}{z_{2}(z_{1}+2z_{2})}+\frac{1}{z_{3}}\right)=\{e_{3}\},

since the sum of the first four terms is zero.

Definition 2.10
.

Two meromorphic germs
f
and
g
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
are called
Q
-orthogonal
, which we write
f\perp^{Q}g
, if their dependence subspaces are orthogonal.

Example 2.11
.

(
i
)

Let
(e_{1},e_{2},\ldots)
be an orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}^{\infty},Q)
. We have

\frac{1}{z_{1}+z_{2}}\,\perp^{Q}\,(z_{1}-z_{2}).\vskip-2.84544pt

(
ii
)

Polar germs are precisely germs of the form

h/M

for
h
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}(\mathbb{C}^{\infty})
and
M
given by products of powers of linearly independent linear forms, such that
h\perp^{Q}M
.

An element
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is of the form
f=\frac{h}{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{r}}
for a holomorphic germ
h
and linear forms
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
. The next lemma shows that the factors in the fraction can be chosen to have their dependence subspaces contained in the dependence subspace of
f
.

Lemma 2.12
.

For any
f
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, there are linear forms
\ell_{i}=\ell_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}),i=1,\ldots,p,
and a holomorphic germ
h=h(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
for a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
of
{\rm Dep}(f)
, such that

\displaystyle{f=\frac{h}{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{p}}}.

Proof.

Let
f
be in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
for some
k\geq 1
.
We extend a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
of
{\rm Dep}(f)
to a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ldots,L_{k}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{k})^{*}
. Since
f
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
, there are linear combinations
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
of
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}
such that the product

\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{m}\,f
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}(\mathbb{C}^{k}),

that is,

(13)

\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{m}\,f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}(\mathbb{C}^{k}).

By rearrangement, we can assume that
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{p}
are linear combinations of
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
only; while
\ell_{p+1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
have nontrivial linear contributions from the extra linear forms
L_{n+1},\ldots,L_{k}
.

Then we can choose a tuple
(a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\mathbb{C}^{k-n}
such that the maps

(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\mapsto\lambda_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\coloneqq\ell_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{k}),\quad i=p+1,\ldots,m,

are affine with
\lambda_{i}(0,\ldots,0)\neq 0
.
Consequently, the maps
(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\longmapsto\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}
are holomorphic germs.

Thus setting
L_{n+1}=a_{n+1},\ldots,L_{k}=a_{k}
in Eq. (
13
) yields a holomorphic germ

h:(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\longmapsto\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{p}\,\lambda_{p+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}\,f,

from which we define another holomorphic germ

\tilde{h}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\coloneqq\frac{h(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}{\lambda_{p+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Hence,

f=f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})=\frac{h(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{p}\lambda_{p+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}}=\frac{\tilde{h}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}{\ell_{1}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\cdots\ell_{p}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}\vskip-2.84544pt

is of the desired form.
∎

Theorem 2.13
.

Write a germ
f
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
according to the decomposition in Eq. (
9
)
:

(14)

f=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}f_{U}+f_{0},

where
\mathcal{U}
is a finite set of nonzero finite-dimensional subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
,
0\neq f_{U}
is a sum of polar germs with supporting space
U
and
f_{0}
lies in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
.
We have

{\rm Dep}(f)=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}{\rm Dep}(f_{U})+{\rm Dep}(f_{0}).

Proof.

Since
f=\sum f_{U}+f_{0}
, clearly we have

{\rm Dep}(f)\subset\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}{\rm Dep}(f_{U})+{\rm Dep}(f_{0}).

It remains to show that
{\rm Dep}(f_{U})\subset{\rm Dep}(f)
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
{\rm Dep}(f_{0})\subset{\rm Dep}(f)
.

By Lemma
2.12
, there are linear forms
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{p}
and a homomorphic germ
h
, all with dependent spaces in
{\rm Dep}(f)
such that

f=\frac{h}{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{p}}=\frac{h(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})}{\ell_{1}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\cdots\ell_{p}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})},

for a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
of
{\rm Dep}(f)
.
Then we can take the Laurent expansion of
f
in
{\rm Dep}(f)
by
[
GPZ3
, Theorem 2.11]
. Thus for all the polar germs in this Laurent expansion of
f
, their linear poles and holomorphic numerators have dependence space in
{\rm Dep}(f)
.
This gives another decomposition

f=g_{0}+\sum_{V\subset{\rm Dep}(f)}g_{V}

according to Eq. (
9
).
Comparing with the decomposition of
f
in Eq. (
14
) as a sum of a holomorphic germ and polar germs, we have

f=f_{0}+\sum_{U\subset\mathcal{U}}f_{U}=g_{0}+\sum_{V\subset{\rm Dep}(f)}g_{V}.

Using the uniqueness of the decomposition in Eq. (
9
), we infer that for any subspace
U\subset\mathcal{U}
(resp.
U=0
), there is a space
V\subset{\rm Dep}(f)
(resp.
V=0
) such that
f_{U}=g_{V}
.
This implies that
{\rm Dep}(f_{U})
is contained in
{\rm Dep}(f)
.
Thus the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

For a nonzero rational linear combination
f=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}S_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
of simplex fractions
S_{i},i\in I,
with the same supporting space
U
, we have

{\rm Dep}(f)=U.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\rm Dep}(f)\subseteq U.

Suppose
{\rm Dep}(f)\subsetneq U
.
Theorem
2.13
gives

f=g_{0}+\sum_{V\subset{\rm Dep}(f)}g_{V}

where the terms in
g_{V}
have supporting space
V
. Hence

0=f-\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,S_{i}=g_{0}+\sum_{V\subset{\rm Dep}(f)}g_{V}-\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,S_{i}.

From
{\rm Dep}(f)\subsetneq U
, we have
V\subsetneq U
in the above sum. Thus the above sum is the decomposition of
0
according to the supporting spaces in Eq. (
9
), in which
\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,S_{i}
is the component with supporting space
U
. Thus

\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\,S_{i}=0
, meaning
f=0
. This is a contradiction.
∎

This leads to the following statement on sums of polar germs.

Proposition 2.15
.

If
f=\sum f_{i}
is a nonzero sum of polar germs
f_{i}
with the same supporting space
U
, then

U
is a subset of
{\rm Dep}(f).

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.14
after evaluation of the numerators of the polar germs
f_{i}
at appropriate arguments in the spirit of the proof of
[
GPZ3
, Theorem 3.7]
. Indeed, let us write the polar germs
f_{i}=h_{i}\,S_{i}
where

S_{i}=\frac{1}{L_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots L_{n}^{s_{n_{i}}}},\quad s_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\vskip-5.69046pt

are simplex fractions with the same supporting space and
h_{i}
are holomorphic germs in some common set of variables
\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
which complete the independent linear forms
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
arising in the
S_{i}
’s to an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Since
f\neq 0
we can assume without loss of generality that none of the holomorphic germs
h_{i}
is identically zero. Hence, there is some tuple
(\ell_{n+1}^{0},\ldots,\ell_{k}^{0})
such that
\alpha_{i}\coloneqq h_{i}(\ell_{n+1}^{0},\ldots,\ell_{k}^{0})\neq 0
for all
i
. We write
f=f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{k})
and take the specialisation
g(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})\coloneqq f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1}^{0},\ldots,\ell_{k}^{0})
. Then
{\rm Dep}(f)\supset{\rm Dep}(g)
. Applying Lemma
2.14
to
g=\sum_{i}h_{i}(\ell_{n+1}^{0},\ldots,\ell_{k}^{0})\,S_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}=h_{i}(\ell_{n+1}^{0},\ldots,\ell_{k}^{0})
, we obtain
{\rm Dep}(g)={\rm Supp}(g)
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result shows that without loss of generality, we can assume that a sum
f
of polar germs with the same supporting space can be written as a sum of polar germs whose numerators are holomorphic germs with dependence space in
{\rm Dep}(f)
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
f=\sum h_{i}\,S_{i}
be a nonzero sum of polar germs with the same supporting space, where
S_{i}
is a simplex fraction and
h_{i}
is a holomorphic germ. Then
f
can be written as a sum
f=\sum\tilde{h}_{i}\,S_{i}
of polar germs where the
\tilde{h}_{i}
’s are now holomorphic germs with dependence spaces in
{\rm Dep}(f)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.15
, the common supporting space
U
lies in
{\rm Dep}(f)
. Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
be a basis of
U
which we extend to a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
of
{\rm Dep}(f)
and then further to a basis
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
of
(\mathbb{C}^{k})^{*}
with
Q(L_{i},\ell_{j})=0,1\leq i\leq n,n+1\leq j\leq k
. Thus,
S_{i}
is a simplex fraction in the variables
L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}
and

f=f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{k})=\sum h_{i}(\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{k})\,S_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}).

By the definition of dependence space,
f
does not depends on
\ell_{m+1},\ldots,\ell_{k}
, so

f=f(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n},\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{m},0,\ldots,0)=\sum h_{i}(\ell_{n+1},\ldots,\ell_{m},0,\ldots,0)\,S_{i}(L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})

as announced.
∎

3.
Locality transformation groups on meromorphic germs

In this section, we study meromorphic germs with linear poles in the context of locality algebras. We then introduce the locality Galois group defined as a group of automorphisms of meromorphic germs in this locality framework.

3.1.
Locality algebras of meromorphic germs

We give general background on locality algebras and then focus on locality subalgebras of meromorphic germs.

3.1.1.
Locality algebras

We recall notations on locality structures from
[
CGPZ1
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
locality set
is a couple
(X,\top)
where
X
is a set and

\top\coloneqq X\times_{\top}X\subseteq X\times X

is a binary
symmetric
relation, called a
locality relation
, on
X
. For
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,
denote
x_{1}\top x_{2}
if
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\top
.

For a subset
U\subset X
, the
polar subset
of
U
is

U^{\top}\coloneqq\{x\in X\,|\,(x,U)\subseteq\top\}.

For locality sets
(X,\top_{X})
and
(Y,\top_{Y})
, a map
f:X\to Y
is called a
locality map
if

(15)

x_{1}\top_{X}x_{2}\Longrightarrow f(x_{1})\top_{Y}f(x_{2}),\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

We give some examples that will be further explored in the sequel.

Example 3.2
.

(
i
)

For any nonempty set
X
, being distinct:
x_{1}\top x_{2}
if
x_{1}\neq x_{2},
defines a locality relation on
X
;

(
ii
)

The
Q
-orthogonality relation
\perp^{Q}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of Definition
2.10
, turns
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
into a locality set.

(
iii
)

Let
(X,\top)=({\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\top)
be the locality set in (
i
) and
({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\perp^{Q})
the locality set in (
ii
). With the notation in Example
2.7
, the map

f:X\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\quad n\mapsto{\mathfrak{f}}\Big(\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{1}\\
\scriptstyle{n}\end{array}\Big)\coloneqq\frac{1}{z_{n}},\quad n>1,

is a locality map.

Other algebraic structures can be generalised to the locality setting.

Definition 3.3
.

(
i
)

A
locality vector space
is a vector space
V
equipped with a locality relation
\top
which is compatible with the linear structure on
V
in the sense that, for any subset
X
of
V
,
X^{\top}
is a linear subspace of
V
.

(
ii
)

A (nonunitary)
locality algebra
over
K
is a locality vector space
(A,\top)
over
K
together with a map

m_{A}:A\times_{\top}A\to A,(u,v)\mapsto u\cdot v=m_{A}(x,y)\quad\text{for all }(u,v)\in A\times_{\top}A

satisfying the following variations of the associativity and distributivity.

(a)

For
u,v,w\in A
with
u\top v,u\top w,v\top w
, we have

(16)

(u\cdot v)\top w,\quad u\top(v\cdot w),\quad(u\cdot v)\cdot w=u\cdot(v\cdot w).

(b)

For
u,v,w\in A
with
u\top w,v\top w
(and hence
(u+v)\top w,w\top(u+v)
), we have

(u+v)\cdot w=u\cdot w+v\cdot w,\quad w\cdot(u+v)=w\cdot u+w\cdot v,

(ku)\cdot w=k(u\cdot w),\quad u\cdot(kw)=k(u\cdot w),\ k\in K.

(
iii
)

A
unitary locality algebra
is a locality algebra
(A,\top,m_{A})
with a unit
1_{A}
such that, for each
u\in A
, we have
1_{A}\top u
and

1_{A}\cdot u=u\cdot 1_{A}=u.

We shall omit explicitly mentioning the unit
1_{A}
unless doing so generates ambiguity.

(
iv
)

Let
(A,\top_{A})
be a locality algebra. A subspace
B
of
A
is called a
(resp. unitary) locality subalgebra of
A
if, with the restricted relation

\top_{B}\coloneqq\top_{A}\cap(B\times B)\vskip-5.69046pt

of
\top_{A}
to
B
, the pair
(B,\top_{B})
is a (resp. unitary) locality algebra.

(
v
)

Let
(A,\top_{A})
be a commutative locality algebra and
C
a unitary locality subalgebra of
A
. A subspace
B
of
A
is called a
(resp. unitary) locality
C
-subalgebra of
A
if
B
is a (resp. unitary) locality subalgebra of
A
that contains
C
.

Given two locality algebras
(A_{i},\top_{i}),i=1,2
,
a (resp.
unitary
)
locality algebra homomorphism
is a linear map
\varphi:A_{1}\longrightarrow A_{2}
such that

a\top_{1}b
implies
\varphi(a)\top_{2}\varphi(b)
and

\varphi(a\cdot b)=\varphi(a)\cdot\varphi(b)

(resp. and
\varphi(1_{A_{1}})=1_{A_{2}}
).

Example 3.4
.

[
CGPZ1
, Corollary 3.23]
With the relation
\perp^{Q}
of Definition
2.10
, the pair
({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\perp^{Q})
is a unitary locality algebra and
the projection

(17)

\pi_{+}^{Q}:{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}={\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}\oplus{\mathcal{M}}^{Q}_{\mathbb{Q}-}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}\vskip-2.84544pt

along

is a unitary locality algebra homomorphism.

Definition 3.5
.

For a unitary locality algebra
(A,\top)
, a unitary locality endomorphism of
A
is a

if it is invertible, preserves the unit, and the inverse map is a locality algebra homomorphism.
Let
{\rm Aut}^{\top}(A)
denote the set of locality automorphisms of

.

We note that

forms a group for the composition, called the

of

.
There are counter examples that a bijective locality homomorphism needs not be a locality automorphism.

3.1.2.
Locality subalgebras of meromorphic germs

In the sequel, we consider locality subalgebras
({\mathcal{A}},\perp^{Q})
of the locality algebra
({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\perp^{Q})
in Example
3.4
. For a subset
U
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}

\widehat{U}\coloneqq U\cup\{1\},\vskip-5.69046pt

We first give the structure of locality subalgebras of

Given a subset
\mathcal{U}
of

\Pi^{Q}(\mathcal{U})\coloneqq\bigg\{\prod_{i}s_{i}\,\bigg|\,s_{i}\in\mathcal{U},\forall i,s_{i}\perp^{Q}s_{j},\,\forall i\not=j\ \bigg\}\vskip-5.69046pt

\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{\mathcal{U}})=\Pi^{Q}(\mathcal{U})\cup\{1\}.\vskip-5.69046pt

Proposition 3.6
.

Given a set
{\mathcal{S}}
of simplex fractions, the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}

{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\coloneqq\bigg\{\sum_{i}c_{i}\,S_{i}\,\bigg|\,c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},S_{i}\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\bigg\}\vskip-5.69046pt

spanned by
\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
is a unitary locality subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}{\mathcal{F}}
.

Thus
{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
is the unitary locality subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}
generated by
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

Since
c_{0}=1
serves as the unit, we just need to prove that for
f,g
in
\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
with
f\perp^{Q}g
,

lies in
\mathbb{Q}\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
. According to the grading in Eq. (

f=\sum_{U}f_{U},\quad g=\sum_{V}g_{V},\vskip-2.84544pt

where
f_{U}
is the sum of simplex fractions with the same supporting space
U
and
g_{V}
is the sum of simplex fractions with the same supporting space
V

2.13
, we have

{\rm Dep}(f)=\sum_{U}{\rm Dep}(f_{U}),\quad{\rm Dep}(g)=\sum_{V}{\rm Dep}(g_{V})\vskip-2.84544pt

from which we infer that, for any
U
and
V
appearing in the decompositions of

and
g

f\perp^{Q}g\Longrightarrow f_{U}\perp^{Q}g_{V}.

By Lemma
2.14
, each
f_{U}\not=0
(resp.
g_{V}\not=0
), being a sum of simple fractions with the same supporting space
U
(resp.
V
), gives
U={\rm Dep}(f_{U})
(resp.
V={\rm Dep}(g_{V})
). Thus we have

U\perp^{Q}V.

Hence the products

arising in the decomposition

lie in
\mathbb{Q}\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
. Consequently the product
f\,g
also lies in
\mathbb{Q}\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Given a set
{\mathcal{S}}
of simplex fractions, the set

{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}\big(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\big)\coloneqq\bigg\{\sum_{i}h_{i}S_{i},\bigg|\,h_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+},S_{i}\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}),h_{i}\perp^{Q}S_{i}\bigg\}\vskip-2.84544pt

is a unitary locality subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Thus
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}\big(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\big)
is the unitary locality
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
generated by
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
is a vector space. As in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we only need to verify that, for

with
a\perp^{Q}b
, the locality product
ab
is well defined and lies in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
. To complete this, in accordance with the grading in Eq. (
9
), write

a=\sum_{U}\sum_{i}a_{U_{i}}S_{U_{i}}=\sum_{U}a_{U},\quad b=\sum_{V}\sum_{j}b_{V_{j}}T_{V_{j}}=\sum_{V}b_{V},\vskip-2.84544pt

where
a_{U_{i}},b_{V_{j}}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
,
S_{U_{i}},T_{V_{j}}\in\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
and
a_{U_{i}}\perp^{Q}S_{U_{i}}
,
b_{V_{j}}\perp^{Q}T_{V_{j}}

{\rm Supp}(S_{U_{i}})=U
,
{\rm Supp}(T_{V_{j}})=V
(with the convention that
{\rm Supp}(h)=0
for

).

Theorem
2.13
gives
{\rm Dep}(a_{U})\subset{\rm Dep}(a)
and
{\rm Dep}(b_{V})\subset{\rm Dep}(b)
. Since

means
{\rm Dep}(a)\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}(b)
, we have
{\rm Dep}(a_{U})\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}(b_{V})
, that is
a_{U}\perp^{Q}b_{V}
.

Now let
a_{U}\not=0
and
b_{V}\not=0
.

(
i
)

By Proposition
2.15
, we have
U\subset{\rm Dep}(a)
and
V\subset{\rm Dep}(b)
. So
U\perp^{Q}V
and
S_{U_{i}}\perp^{Q}T_{V_{j}}
;

(
ii
)

From
{\rm Dep}(a_{U})\subset{\rm Dep}(a)
and
V\subset{\rm Dep}(b)
, we obtain
a_{U}\perp^{Q}T_{V_{j}}
. Similarly,
b_{V}\perp^{Q}S_{U_{i}}
;

(
iii
)

By Proposition
2.16
, there exist holomorphic germs
\tilde{a}_{U_{i}}
and
\tilde{b}_{V_{j}}
with

{\rm Dep}(\tilde{a}_{U_{i}})\subset{\rm Dep}(a_{U}),\ \tilde{a}_{U_{i}}\perp^{Q}S_{U_{i}},\ {\rm Dep}(\tilde{b}_{V_{j}})\subset{\rm Dep}(b_{U}),\ \tilde{b}_{V_{j}}\perp^{Q}T_{V_{j}},\vskip-5.69046pt

such that

From

and

In summary,

are mutually

-orthogonal. Thus

is defined and gives an element in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})))
. Therefore,

is defined and lies in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}^{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
.
∎

3.2.
Automorphism groups of simplex locality algebras

For a locality subalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of
{({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\perp^{Q})}
, let
{\rm Aut}^{Q}({\mathcal{A}})
denote the group of locality automorphisms of
{\mathcal{A}}
, following Definition
3.5
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a set of simplex fractions and let
{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})

be the unitary locality subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\widehat{{\mathcal{F}}}^{Q}
generated by
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Let
{\rm Aut}_{\rm Res}^{Q}({\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
be the set of unitary locality algebra homomorphisms
\varphi:{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\to{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
with the property that,
for any fraction
S
in
\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})
,

(20)

\varphi\left(S\right)=S+\sum_{i}{a_{i}}\,S_{i},\vskip-5.69046pt

where

a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},\,S_{i}\in\Pi^{Q}(\mathcal{S}),\,\text{\rm p-ord}(S_{i})<\text{\rm p-ord}(S),\ {\rm Supp}(S_{i})\subsetneq{\rm Supp}\left(S\right).\vskip-2.84544pt

Then
{\rm Aut}_{\rm Res}^{Q}({\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
is a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}^{Q}({\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
.

Remark 3.11
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.15
yet to come, we have

(21)

{\rm Aut}_{\rm Res}^{Q}({\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))=\Big\{\left.\varphi\in{\rm Aut}^{Q}({\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))\,\right|\,\varphi\,\text{preserves the p-residue and d-residue}\Big\}\vskip-2.84544pt

which justifies the notation with subscript “Res”.

Proof.

Denote
R\coloneqq R_{S}\coloneqq{\mathbb{Q}}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
.
We first prove that
\varphi
is one-to-one.
Let

f=\sum_{i}a_{i}S_{i}\in R,a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},S_{i}\in\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}})\cup\{1\},\vskip-2.84544pt

be nonzero.
If
f
is a constant in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, then
\varphi(f)=f\neq 0
. If
f
is not a constant, we group the terms of
f
according to the gradation in Eq. (
9
):

f=c_{0}+\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}f_{U},\vskip-5.69046pt

where
\mathcal{U}
is a finite nonempty set of nonzero subspaces of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}
and
0\neq f_{U}\in\mathbb{Q}\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
, for any
U
in
\mathcal{U}
, is a sum of fractions with supporting space
U
. Applying
\varphi
yields

\varphi(f)=c_{0}+\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}\Big(f_{U}+\sum_{V\subsetneq U}g_{V}\Big),\vskip-5.69046pt

where
g_{V}
is a sum (possibly zero) of fractions with supporting space
V
.
For a maximal element
U_{0}
in
\mathcal{U}
,
f_{U_{0}}
is the only contribution in the above sum arising in
\varphi(f)
to the component with supporting space
U_{0}
in the decomposition in Eq. (
9
). So
\varphi(f)=0
implies
f_{U_{0}}=0
. This is a contradiction. It follows that
\varphi(f)\neq 0
, which ends the proof of the injectivity.

To prove the surjectivity of
\varphi
, by the linearity of
\varphi
, we only need to show that every element
f
of
\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
lies in the range Im
\varphi
of
\varphi
. Suppose this is not the case and let
U_{0}\neq 0
be minimal among the supporting spaces of elements in
\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\backslash\text{Im}\varphi
. Let
f_{0}
be one of the simplex fractions in
\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})\backslash\text{Im}\varphi
with supporting space
U_{0}
.
Then by Eq. (
20
) we have

\varphi(f_{0})=f_{0}+\sum_{V\subsetneq U_{0}}f_{V},\vskip-2.84544pt

where
f_{V}\in\mathbb{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
is the sum of simplex fractions with supporting space
V
. The space
U_{0}
being minimal, each
f_{V}
lies in the image of
\varphi
. Therefore,

f_{0}=\varphi(f_{0})-\sum\limits_{V\subsetneq U_{0}}f_{V}

also lies in the image of
\varphi
. This is a contradiction, showing that
\varphi
is surjective.

We finally prove that
\varphi^{-1}
is a locality algebra homomorphism. We first show that
\varphi^{-1}
has the property in Eq. (
20
), that is, for any
S\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
, we have

\varphi^{-1}(S)=S+\sum_{i}h_{i}S_{i},\vskip-5.69046pt

where each
S_{i}\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
has smaller supporting space and p-order than those of
S
. Assume that this were not the case, and let
S
have a minimal supporting space
U_{0}
among the counterexamples. Then

\varphi(S)=S+\sum_{j}b_{j}T_{j},\vskip-5.69046pt

where each simplex fraction
T_{j}\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
has it supporting space and p-order smaller than those of
S
. Applying
\varphi^{-1}
gives

(22)

S=\varphi^{-1}(S)+\sum_{j}b_{j}\varphi^{-1}(T_{j}).\vskip-5.69046pt

The minimality of the supporting space of
S
yields

\varphi^{-1}(T_{j})=T_{j}+\sum_{jk}d_{jk}T_{jk},\vskip-5.69046pt

where each
T_{jk}\in\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}})
has its supporting space and p-order smaller than those of
T_{j}
and hence of
S
. Therefore, Eq. (
22
) gives

\varphi^{-1}(S)=S-\sum_{j}\varphi^{-1}(T_{j})=S-\sum_{j}b_{j}\left(T_{j}+\sum_{jk}d_{jk}T_{jk}\right),\vskip-5.69046pt

which shows that
\varphi^{-1}(S)
has the form in Eq. (
20
). This gives the desired contradiction.

To check that
\varphi^{-1}
is a locality map, we consider two linear combinations
f,g
in
R=\mathbb{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
and group the terms

f=c+\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}}f_{U}\quad{\rm and}\quad g=d+\sum_{V\in\mathcal{V}}g_{V},\vskip-2.84544pt

with
c,d
in
\mathbb{Q}
and nonzero sums
f_{U},g_{V}\in\mathbb{Q}(\Pi^{Q}(\widehat{{\mathcal{S}}}))
of fractions with supporting spaces
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\in{\mathcal{V}}
respectively. We proceed to show that
f\perp^{Q}g
implies
\varphi^{-1}(f)\perp^{Q}\varphi^{-1}(g)
.

Let
U
be an element in
\mathcal{U}
and
V
an element in
\mathcal{V}
. By Theorem
2.13
,
{\rm Dep}\,f_{U}\subset{\rm Dep}\,f
and
{\rm Dep}\,g_{V}\subset{\rm Dep}\,g
so that
{\rm Dep}\,f\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}\,g
implies
{\rm Dep}\,f_{U}\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}\,g_{V}
and hence

f_{U}\perp^{Q}g_{V}.

By Lemma
2.14
,
for the linear combination
f_{U}
(resp.
g_{V}
) of simplex fractions with the same supporting space
U
(resp.
V
), we have
U={\rm Dep}(f_{U})
(resp.
V={\rm Dep}(g_{V})
). Thus
f_{U}\perp^{Q}g_{V}
implies
U\perp^{Q}V
.

Since
\varphi^{-1}
has the property in Eq. (
20
), we have
{\rm Dep}(\varphi^{-1}(f_{U}))\subseteq{\rm Dep}(f_{U})
and

{\rm Dep}(\varphi^{-1}(g_{V})\subseteq{\rm Dep}(g_{V})
. Then

\varphi^{-1}(f_{U})\perp^{Q}\varphi^{-1}(g_{V})
.
Therefore, by the linearity of
\varphi^{-1}
, we obtain
\varphi^{-1}(f)\perp^{Q}\varphi^{-1}(g)
, as needed.

Finally for
f,g\in R
with
f\perp^{Q}g
, we have
\varphi^{-1}(f)\perp^{Q}\varphi^{-1}(g)
. Hence

\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(f)\,\varphi^{-1}(g))=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(f))\,\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(g))=fg.

Therefore, applying
\varphi^{-1}
, we obtain

\varphi^{-1}(f)\,\varphi^{-1}(g)=\varphi^{-1}(fg),

showing that
\varphi^{-1}
is a locality homomorphism.
∎

In this part we consider a locality subalgebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of
({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}},\perp^{Q})
containing

Proposition 3.12
.

For any locality morphism
\varphi:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
with
\varphi|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}}=\mathrm{Id}

{\rm Dep}(\varphi(f))\subset{\rm Dep}(f),\quad\forall f\in{\mathcal{A}}.

Proof.

For any
\ell
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
viewed as an element of
(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{*}
for some
k\geq 1
, if
\ell\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}(f)
, then

, which implies that

\varphi(\ell)\perp^{Q}\varphi(f)

since
\varphi
is a locality map.
Since
\ell
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
, we have
\varphi(\ell)=\ell
.
Thus

\ell\perp^{Q}{\rm Dep}(\varphi(x))
. So
{\rm Dep}(f)^{\top}\subseteq{\rm Dep}(\varphi(x))^{\top}
which
yields the statement.
∎

In the sequel, we fix a set
{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}

(24)

4.
Locality polynomial algebras generated by Chen and Speer fractions

In this section, we show that the locality subalgebras of
{\mathcal{F}}
generated by Chen fractions of Example
2.7
and by the more general class of Speer fractions over
\mathbb{Q}
are both locality polynomial algebras, defined to be the following locality version of polynomial algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\top)
be a locality
K
-algebra. Let
X
be a subset of
A
.

(
i
)

A
locality monomial
built from
X
is a product
x_{1}\cdots x_{r}
where
x_{i}\top x_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

(
ii
)

The set
X
is called
locality algebraically independent
if distinct locality monomials built from
X
are linearly independent.

(
iii
)

The set
X
is called a
locality generating set
of
(A,\top)
if the only locality subalgebra of
(A,\top)
containing
X
is
A
itself.

(
iv
)

The locality algebra
(A,\top)
is called a
locality polynomial algebra
generated by
X
if
X
is locality algebraically independent, and is a locality generating set of
(A,\top)
. Then we denote

A\cong{K}_{\top}[X].

4.1.
Locality shuffle algebras as locality polynomial algebras

Let us first recall how shuffle algebras can be viewed as polynomially generated algebras. For a commutative ring
K
and a set
X
, let
A={K}X
be the linear space with a basis
X
,
W(X)
be the set of words with letters in
X
including the empty word.
The shuffle algebra on
X
is the space

\mathrm{Sh}({K}X):={K}W(X),

equipped with the shuffle product
X
. More precisely,

(\alpha_{1}\vec{\alpha}^{\prime})\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,(\beta_{1}\vec{\beta}^{\prime})\coloneqq\alpha_{1}(\vec{\alpha}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,(\beta_{1}\vec{\beta}^{\prime}))+\beta_{1}((\alpha_{1}\vec{\alpha}^{\prime})\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,\vec{\beta}^{\prime}),\quad\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\in X,\vec{\alpha}^{\prime},\vec{\beta}^{\prime}\in W(X),

with the initial condition
1\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,\vec{\alpha}=\vec{\alpha}=\vec{\alpha}\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,1.

For a well-ordered set
(X,\leq)
, equip
W(X)
with the lexicographic order
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
. A word
w
in
W(X)
is called a
Lyndon word
if
w
is the smallest among all its rotations. Equivalently,
w
is Lyndon if
w
is lexicographically smaller than all of its suffixes:
w=uv
with
u,v\neq 1
implies
w\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}v
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
CFL
,
Ra
]

Let
(X,\leq)
be a well-ordered set.

(
i
)

(
Chen-Fox-Lyndon
)
Any word
w
in
W(X)
has a unique factorisation

(31)

w=w_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots w_{k}^{i_{k}}

where
w_{1}>_{\rm lex}\cdots>_{\rm lex}w_{k}
are Lyndon words and
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\geq 1
.

(
ii
)

(
Radford
)
Let
{K}
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra. The set
Lyn(X)
of Lyndon words on
X
is an algebraically independent generating set of
\mathrm{Sh}({K}X)
. Thus
\mathrm{Sh}({K}X)\cong{K}[\mathrm{Lyn}(X)]
is a polynomial algebra. In fact, with the factorisation
w=w_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots w_{k}^{i_{k}}
in Eq. (
31
), we have

(32)

w=\frac{1}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{k}!}w_{1}^{\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,i_{1}}\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,\cdots\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,w_{k}^{\,\mbox{\bf\scyr X}\,i_{k}}+\text{smaller terms}.

From a set
U
we build a set

\overline{{U}}\coloneqq\{x_{0}\}\sqcup\{x_{u}\ |\ u\in{U}\}.

For a well-ordered set
(U,\leq)
, we then define a well-order
\leq
on

by imposing

(33)

x_{0}<x_{u},\ \text{ and }x_{u}\leq x_{v}\Leftrightarrow u\leq v,\ \forall u,v\in U.

Denote

Note that
\mathrm{Sh}_{1}({K}\overline{U})
is closed under the shuffle product, and the factorisation of

in

given in Eq. (
31

. Thus we obtain (see also
[

)

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{Lyn}_{1}(\overline{U})\coloneqq\mathrm{Lyn}(\overline{U})\backslash\{x_{0}\}
, that is, the set of Lyndon words in
\overline{U}
that do not end with
x_{0}
. Then
\mathrm{Sh}_{1}({K}\overline{U})
is a subalgebra of
\mathrm{Sh}({K}\overline{U})

\mathrm{Lyn}_{1}(\overline{U})
.

We now extend these constructions to the locality setting.

For a locality set
(X,\top)
,
let
W_{\top}(X)
denote the subset of
W(X)
consisting of locality words, namely the words
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{k}
in which

, plus the empty word.
Let

\mathrm{Sh}_{\top}({K}X)\coloneqq{K}W_{\top}(X).

For

and

in

Thus for

in

with

. Since a shuffle of

\mathrm{Sh}_{\top}({K}X)
is a locality algebra.

For a well-ordered set

equipped with a locality relation

Proof.

(
i
)
In the factorisation

5.
Locality characters and analytic renormalisation

In this last section, as an application of our previous results, we address Problems

We first show that the pole structures of the generalised Feynman amplitudes in Speer’s analytic renormalisation are of the form introduced earlier. We then compare our constructions of locality generalised evaluators with those of Speer, and obtain a transitivity group action on locality generalised evaluators.

We first summarise Speer’s work, mostly following

A Feynman graph is called

For an s-family

5.3.

We consider the action of the locality Galois group on generalised evaluators.
Clearly, the group

Theorem 5.7

(51)

{{\mathcal{E}}^{Q}_{\rm MS}}\circ\tilde{\varphi}={\mathcal{E}}.

Proof.

Let

By assumption,

{\mathcal{B}}

For any

W={\rm Supp}(t)
and
N=\text{\rm p-ord}(t)
. Then by Lemma
2.2
, we can assume that all these monomials have supporting space
W
and p-order
N
.
For each of these monomials, by the locality multilplicativity, we have

\varphi(\Pi_{\alpha}s_{\alpha})=\Pi_{\alpha}(s_{\alpha}+{\mathcal{E}}(s_{\alpha}))=\Pi_{\alpha}s_{\alpha}+{\rm lower\ order\ terms}.

Here the lower order terms come from
\Pi_{\alpha}s_{\alpha}

by replacing one or more factors
s_{\alpha}
by
{\mathcal{E}}(s_{\alpha})
, so they indeed have lower p-orders and smaller supporting spaces.
Since all the monomials
\Pi_{\alpha}s_{\alpha}
have the same supporting space and p-order, we have

\varphi(t)=\varphi(P(S))=P(S)+{\rm lower\ order\ terms}.

The map
\varphi
therefore defines an element in
{\rm Aut}_{{\rm Res}}^{Q}({\mathcal{B}})
. The extension
\tilde{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Gal}^{Q}({\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+})
of
\varphi
obtained from Theorem
3.15
is a locality algebra homomorphism. Further we have

{{\mathcal{E}}^{Q}_{\rm MS}}\circ\tilde{\varphi}(s_{\alpha})={\rm ev}_{0}\circ\pi_{+}^{Q}\circ\tilde{\varphi}(s_{\alpha})={\rm ev}_{0}\circ\pi_{+}^{Q}(s_{\alpha}+{\mathcal{E}}(s_{\alpha}))={\rm ev}_{0}({\mathcal{E}}(s_{\alpha}))={\mathcal{E}}(s_{\alpha}).

Hence, the locality algebra homomorphisms
{{\mathcal{E}}^{Q}_{\rm MS}}\circ\tilde{\varphi}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
agree on the locality polynomial generating set
S
of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore
{{\mathcal{E}}^{Q}_{\rm MS}}\circ\tilde{\varphi}={\mathcal{E}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
. Since they also agree on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}
, by Proposition
3.14
, they agree on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{Q}+}(\Pi^{Q}({\mathcal{S}}))
.

To finish the paper, we use multiple zeta values to build an example of locality generalized evaluators and elements of the locality Galois group.

Recall that for
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}

with
s_{1}\geq 2

\zeta\left(s_{1},\cdots,s_{k}\right):=\sum_{n_{k}>\cdots>n_{1}\geq 1}n_{1}^{-s_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{-s_{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{m_{i}=1}^{\infty}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i})^{-s_{i}}.

For a locality Lyndon word

x_{0}^{s_{1}-1}x_{u_{1}}\ldots x_{0}^{s_{k}-1}x_{u_{k}}\in\mathrm{Sh}_{1,\top}(\overline{U})

{\mathfrak{f}}\Big(\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}}\\
\scriptstyle{{u_{1}},\ldots,{u_{k}}}\end{array}\Big)\coloneqq\frac{1}{z_{u_{1}}^{s_{1}}(z_{u_{1}}+z_{u_{2}})^{s_{2}}\cdots(z_{u_{1}}+z_{u_{2}}+\cdots+z_{u_{k}})^{s_{k}}}\,,\ u_{i},s_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},k\in\mathbb{N},u_{i}\neq u_{j}\,{\rm if}\ i\neq j,

in
{\mathcal{F}}^{\rm Ch}_{\top}

4.10

i

(53)

0,&s_{1}=1.\end{array}\right.

, this assignment

Abstract
We study the mixing properties of an important optimization algorithm of machine learning: the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) with a fixed step size. The data stream is not assumed to be independent hence the SGLD is not a Markov chain, merely a Markov chain in a random environment , which complicates the mathematical treatment considerably. We derive a strong law of large numbers and a functional central limit theorem for SGLD.

This is because customers’ reviews can be influenced by previous reviews, current trends, or the changing sentiment of other customers, leading to dependencies between reviews. Consequently, the occurrence of certain key words and the overall sentiment may not be independent across reviews. For such sentiment analysis problems, variants of stochastic gradient descent are commonly used. However, due to the lack of convexity, it is worth considering the use of SGLD.

Furthermore, sentiment analysis faces the challenge of concept drift, which refers to the situation where the underlying sentiment distribution of the data changes over time. This could be due to various factors, such as changes in product features, external events, or trends. The SGLD algorithm is capable of adapting to concept drift scenarios by continuously updating the model parameters as new data arrives.

2
The main result

First we formulate our working assumption on the stochastic iterative scheme given by (
1
).

Assumption 2.1
.

There are
\Delta,b>0
such that, for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\langle H(\theta,y),\theta\rangle\geq\Delta\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}-b,

(4)

and for some
K>\Delta/\sqrt{2}
,

\lVert H(\theta,y)\rVert\leq K(\lVert\theta\rVert+\lVert y\rVert+1).

(5)

Furthermore, we assume that the process
(Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is strictly stationary, and there is
M>0
such that

\lVert Y_{0}\rVert\leq M\ \text{a.s.}

(6)

Condition (
4
) is a standard dissipativity requirement, (
5
) is also mild and holds for Lipschitz-continuous
H
. By stationarity, (
6
) implies uniform boundedness of the data stream. This may look stringent from the mathematical
point of view, but it is evidently applicable in practice due to two main reasons. First, many real-world applications involve data that can be naturally bounded within certain ranges. For example, pixel values in images are confined to specific ranges (e.g., 0 to 255 for grayscale images). Second, scaling the data to a compact domain is a common preprocessing step in machine learning. In conclusion, the assumptions we have made are met by a wide range of learning problems of considerable practical importance.

Next, we briefly recall the main concepts of
\alpha
-mixing. Throughout this paper the probability space is
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and
for any two sub-

, we define the measure of dependence

\alpha(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H})=\sup\limits_{G\in\mathcal{G},H\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\mathbb{P}(G\cap H)-\mathbb{P}(G)\mathbb{P}(H)\right|.

(7)

Furthermore, for an arbitrary sequence of random variables
(W_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we define
the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{F}_{t,s}^{W}:=\sigma\left(W_{k},\,t\leq k\leq s\right)
,
-\infty\leq t\leq s\leq\infty
,
and introduce the dependence coefficients

\alpha_{j}^{W}(n)=\alpha\left(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,j}^{W},\mathcal{F}_{j+n,\infty}^{W}\right),\,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}.

The mixing coefficient of

is
\alpha^{W}(n)=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{j}^{W}(n)
,
n\geq 1

which is obviously non-increasing in

. Note that, for strictly stationary

does not depend on

\alpha^{W}(n)=\alpha_{0}^{W}(n)
. We say that
W

\alpha

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha^{W}(n)=0
.

Assumption 2.2
.

For some
\epsilon>0
,
the
\alpha
-mixing coefficients

,
n\in\mathbb{N}

satisfy

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{Y}(n)^{1-\epsilon}<\infty.

In

In this paper, using results of

. In the sequel,

Let Assumption

Furthermore, we have

Theorem 2.4

Under Assumptions

3.1
Drift and minorization conditions for
\theta_{t}

In this point, we establish suitable versions of the standard drift and minorization conditions, known from the theory of Markov chains (See e.g.
[

(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}

According to the next lemma, there is an

and for the parametric kernel

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
0<\lambda<\frac{\Delta}{K^{2}}
, there exist

such that for

[Q(y)V](\theta):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}V(z)\,Q(y,\theta,\mathrm{d}z)\leq\gamma V(\theta)+C,\,\,\lVert y\rVert\leq M

holds with constants
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 1

Proof.

We can write

\displaystyle[Q(y)V](\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(a\lVert\theta-\lambda H(\theta,y)+\sqrt{2\lambda}\xi_{0}\rVert^{2}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{(1-4\lambda a)^{d/2}}\exp\left({a\frac{\lVert\theta-\lambda H(\theta,y)\rVert^{2}}{1-4\lambda a}}\right),

where by Assumption
2.1
, for
\lVert y\rVert\leq M
, we have

\lVert\theta-\lambda H(\theta,y)\rVert^{2}\leq(2K^{2}\lambda^{2}-2\Delta\lambda+1)\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}+2(\lambda b+\lambda^{2}K^{2}(1+M)^{2}).

For
0<\lambda<\frac{\Delta}{K^{2}}
,
0<2K^{2}\lambda^{2}-2\Delta\lambda+1<1
hence we can choose
a>0
so small that

\frac{2K^{2}\lambda^{2}-2\Delta\lambda+1}{1-4\lambda a}<1.

To sum up, we obtained that there are
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{2}<a
and
[Q(y)V](\theta)\leq c_{1}\exp(c_{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2})
hence for
r>0

\gamma:=c_{1}e^{-(a-c_{2})r^{2}}<1
, and thus

[Q(y)V](\theta)\leq\gamma V(\theta)+C

holds with
C=c_{1}e^{c_{2}r^{2}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2

By induction, easily follows that for any collection

[Q(y_{b})\ldots Q(y_{a})V](\theta):=[Q(y_{b})[\ldots[Q(y_{a})V]\ldots]](\theta)\leq\gamma^{b-a+1}V(\theta)+\frac{C}{1-\gamma}

For every

3.2

We need to show that, starting from a suitable random initial state

Let

By Corollary

For each

However, we will need a bit more. We aim to show that there is a coupling between the iterations

initialized with the deterministic value
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and an appropriate version of
(\theta_{t}^{\ast})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
. That’s why we preferred the technology presented in
[
5
]
.

It is also shown in
[
15
]
that the distribution of
(\theta_{t}^{\ast})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is unique, moreover the process
(\theta_{t}^{\ast},Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is ergodic provided that
(Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is ergodic. The latter will be very important for us, since the proof of Theorem
2.4
relies on this result.

In addition, Truquet proved that under a milder form of the drift and minorization conditions (See Assumptions A2 and A3 in
[
15
]
),
\text{Law}(\theta_{t})\to\text{Law}(\theta_{0}^{\ast})
in total variation as
t\to\infty
. However, as Truquet remarked, assumptions of
[
15
]
did not to get a rate of convergence for
\text{Law}(\theta_{t})
.

In the rest of this subsection, we show an alternative approach to a bit stronger result on the convergence of
(\text{Law}(\theta_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
using the results of the recent paper
[
4
]
. The reader can skip this part without affecting the understanding. The
(1+V)
-weighted total variation distance for any pair of Borel probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
is defined by

d_{\text{TV}}^{1+V}(\mu,\nu):=\int_{\mathcal{X}}(1+V(\theta))|\mu-\nu|(\mathrm{d}\theta).

Lemma 3.7
.

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for
V(\theta)=e^{\frac{a}{2}\lVert\theta\rVert^{2}}
,

d_{\text{TV}}^{1+V}(\text{Law}(\theta_{n}),\text{Law}(\theta_{0}^{\ast}))\leq c_{1}e^{-c_{2}n},\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

With the above choice of
V
, we have
\mathbb{E}(V(\theta_{0})^{2}+V(\theta_{1})^{2})<\infty
hence the
moment condition on initial values
i.e. Assumption 2.6. in
[
4
]
is in force, and also the other assumptions of
[
4
]
are clearly met (with the quantities
\lambda,\alpha,K
constant
and with
\ell\equiv 0
since
Y
is bounded) too. Hence Theorem 2.11 of
[
4
]
implies the convergence of
\text{Law}(\theta_{t})
towards the limiting distribution
\text{Law}(\theta_{0}^{\ast})
at a geometric rate in
d_{\text{TV}}^{1+V}
. ∎

Corollary 3.8
.

It is clear from the definition of
d^{1+V}
and from Lemma
3.7
that for any
\phi
satisfying (
8
),

\mathbb{E}(\phi(\theta_{n}))\to\mathbb{E}(\phi(\theta_{0}^{\ast})),\,\,n\to\infty.

In particular,
\mathbb{E}(\lVert\theta_{n}\rVert^{p})\to\mathbb{E}(\lVert\theta_{0}^{\ast}\rVert^{p})
, as
n\to\infty
, for every
1\leq p<\infty
.

3.3
Coupling construction

Let
R>0
which we fix later, and
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of i.i.d. uniform variables on
[0,1]
independent of
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and also independent of
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. The next Lemma is a standard representation results for parametric kernels satisfying the minorization condition
(
10
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Under the minorization condition (c.f. (
10
) in Lemma
3.3
), there exists a measurable function
T:\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}\times[0,1]\to\mathcal{X}
such that

\mathbb{P}(T(y,\theta,\varepsilon_{0})\in\mathcal{A})=Q(y,\theta,A),

for all
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
,
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and
y\in\mathcal{Y}
such that
\lVert y\rVert\leq M
. Furthermore, for
u\in[0,\tilde{\alpha}_{R}]
,

T(y,\theta_{1},u)=T(y,\theta_{2},u),\,\,\lVert y\rVert\leq M,\,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\{\theta\mid\lVert\theta\rVert\leq R\}.

Proof.

For the proof, we refer the reader to Lemma 7.1 in
[
11
]
.
∎

We drop the dependence of the mappings T on
\varepsilon_{t}
in the notation and will simply
write
T_{t}(y)\theta:=T(\theta,y,\varepsilon_{t})
. For
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
, define the family of auxiliary
processes

Z_{s,t}^{\theta,\mathbf{y}}=\theta,\,t\leq s,\quad Z_{s,t}^{\theta,\mathbf{y}}=T_{t}(y_{t-1})Z_{s,{t-1}}^{\theta,\mathbf{y}},\,t>s,

(12)

where
\mathbf{y}=(\ldots,y_{-1},y_{0},y_{1},\ldots)\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is a fixed trajectory. Clearly, for any random variable
(\theta^{\prime}_{0},(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
Z_{s,t}^{\theta^{\prime}_{s},\mathbf{Y}}
,
t\geq s
is a version of the process
(\theta^{\prime}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
defined through the iterative scheme (
1
), starting from
\theta^{\prime}_{0}
and driven by
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Furthermore, the process
Z_{s,t}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}}
,
t\geq s
is a time-inhomogeneous Markov chain that follows the dynamics of
\theta_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
with the environment being ”frozen”. Since the process
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is almost surely bounded by
M>0
, we can restrict ourselves to trajectories
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert y_{k}\rVert\leq M
, and thus
Z_{s,t}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}}
,
t\geq s
is a
Harris recurrent
chain. The next lemma controls the coupling time between processes starting from different initial values.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
be arbitrary but fixed and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lVert y_{k}\rVert\leq M
. Then there exists constants
\kappa>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

depending only on
\lambda
,
\Delta
,
b
,
K
and
M
such that for
n\geq N
,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}})\leq\frac{V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2})+3}{2}e^{-\kappa n}.

Proof.

First, we fix
\gamma<\gamma^{\prime}<1
and choose
R>0
so large such that
2C<(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)e^{\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}
. Furthermore, we introduce the notations
\overline{Z}_{n}:=\left(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}},Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}}\right)
,
\lVert\overline{Z}_{n}\rVert:=\max\left(\left\lVert Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert,\left\lVert Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}}\right\rVert\right)
and the sequence of successive visiting times

\sigma_{0}:=0,\,\sigma_{k+1}=\min\left\{t>\sigma_{k}\middle|\lVert\overline{Z}_{t}\rVert\leq R\right\},\,k\in\mathbb{N}

that are obviously
\sigma(\varepsilon_{t},t\in\mathbb{Z})
-stopping times. Note that on
\{\lVert\overline{Z}_{t}\rVert>R\}
we have

\gamma(V(Z_{0,t}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}})+V(Z_{0,t}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}}))+2C\leq\gamma^{\prime}(V(Z_{0,t}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}})+V(Z_{0,t}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}}))

and thus for
k\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_{k}>s\mid\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{P}(\lVert\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}+s}\rVert>R\mid\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}+s-1})\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}\mathds{1}_{\lVert\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}+j}\rVert>R}\middle|\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{\prime}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{V(Z_{0,\sigma_{k}+s-1}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}})+V(Z_{0,\sigma_{k}+s-1}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}})}{e^{\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}}\prod_{j=1}^{s-2}\mathds{1}_{\lVert\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}+j}\rVert>R}\middle|\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}}\right)

Iteration of this argument leads to the following estimation.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_{k}>s\mid\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma^{\prime})^{s-1}\frac{\gamma V(Z_{0,\sigma_{k}}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}})+\gamma V(Z_{0,\sigma_{k}}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}})+2C}{e^{\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma^{\prime})^{s-1}\frac{2\gamma e^{\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}+2C}{e^{\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}}\leq(\gamma^{\prime})^{s-1}(\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma)\leq 2(\gamma^{\prime})^{s}.

Along similar lines, we can show that

\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{1}>s)\leq(\gamma^{\prime})^{s}\left[e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma^{\prime}}\right]\leq(e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1)(\gamma^{\prime})^{s}.

Let us fix
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma^{\prime\prime}<1
. For the generating function of the time elapsed between the
k
th and
(k+1)
th visits, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_{k}}}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,\sigma_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{j}}\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{k+1}-\sigma_{k}=j\mid\overline{Z}_{\sigma_{k}})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(\gamma^{\prime})^{j-1}}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{j}}=\frac{2}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}},\,k\geq 1,

and similarly, for
k=0
,

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{1}}}\right)\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}}

hence by the Markov inequality and the tower rule,for
0<m<n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{m}\geq n)

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{m}}}\right)=(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{m}-\sigma_{m-1}}}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,\sigma_{m-1}}^{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{m-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{n}\frac{2}{\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{m-1}}}\right)\leq\ldots

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1}{2}\left(\frac{2^{m}}{(\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma^{\prime})^{m}}\right)(\gamma^{\prime\prime})^{n}.

Again we fix a constant
\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}<\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}<1
, and define

m_{n}:=\left\lfloor n\frac{\log\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}-\log\gamma^{\prime\prime}}{\log 2-\log(\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma^{\prime})}\right\rfloor.

Obviously, for
n
is so large such that
m_{n}\geq 1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{m_{n}}\geq n)\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1}{2}(\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime})^{n}.

Next, we estimate the probability of no-coupling on events when the small set is visited at least
m_{n}
-times. According to Lemma
3.9
, for
j=1,\ldots,m_{n}
,
\theta\mapsto T(y,\theta,\varepsilon_{\sigma_{j}+1})
is constant of the ball
\{\theta\mid\lVert\theta\rVert\leq R\}
with probability at least
\tilde{\alpha}_{R}
hence we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}},\sigma_{m_{n}}<n)\leq\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{\sigma_{j}+1}<\tilde{\alpha}_{R};\,j=1,\ldots,m_{n})=\tilde{\alpha}_{R}^{m_{n}},

where we used that for every
j
,
\varepsilon_{\sigma_{j}+1}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,\sigma_{j}}^{\varepsilon}
.

At least, we combine this estimate with that one what we got for the tail probability of the visiting times, and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{y}},\sigma_{m_{n}}<n)+\mathbb{P}(\sigma_{m_{n}}\geq n)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\alpha}_{R}^{m_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{a}{2}R^{2}}(V(\theta_{1})+V(\theta_{2}))+1}{2}(\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime})^{n}

which completes the proof.

∎

The following annealed version of Lemma
3.10
will be important later.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
be random variables independent of
\mathcal{F}^{\varepsilon}_{m+1,\infty}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathbb{P}(Z_{m,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{m,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{Y}})\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}(V(\theta_{1}))+\mathbb{E}(V(\theta_{2}))+3}{2}e^{-\kappa(n-m)},\,\,n\geq N+m.

Proof.

Estimate the conditional probability using Lemma
3.10
above yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Z_{m,n}^{\theta_{1},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{m,n}^{\theta_{2},\mathbf{Y}}\mid\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{y},\,\theta_{1}=x_{1},\,\theta_{2}=x_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(Z_{m,n}^{x_{1},\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{m,n}^{x_{2},\mathbf{y}})=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,n-m}^{x_{1},S^{m}\mathbf{y}}\neq Z_{0,n-m}^{x_{2},S^{m}\mathbf{y}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{V(x_{1})+V(x_{2})+3}{2}e^{-\kappa(n-m)},\,\,n\geq N+m,

where
S^{m}\mathbf{Y}
refers to the
m
-times left-shifted trajectory of
Y
i.e.
\left(S^{m}\mathbf{Y}\right)_{k}=Y_{k+m}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Finally, we take expectations and obtain the claimed inequality.
∎

3.4
Mixing properties

In what follows, we show that mixing properties of the exogenous environment transfer to the process
\theta_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
. For any system of sub-
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset\mathcal{F}
,
i\in I
, we use the notation
\bigvee_{i\in I}A_{i}
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the system
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in I}
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
are sub-
\sigma
-algebras of
\mathcal{F}
such that the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{n}\vee\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
are pairwise independent. Then

\alpha\left(\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{n},\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{n}\right)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n}).

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
2
, Lemma 8 on page 13]
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

We need the special case when
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2}\subset\mathcal{F}
are
\sigma
-algebras, where
\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
are independent too. For this, Lemma
3.12
gives

\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1}\vee\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})
. By the definition of the measure of dependence between sigma algebras (
7
), the reverse inequality trivially holds hence

\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1}\vee\mathcal{A}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{1}\vee\mathcal{B}_{2})=\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1}).

The next lemma provides an upper bound for the strong mixing coefficient of the chain
(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
given
\alpha^{Y}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

For the dependence coefficient
\alpha_{j}^{\theta}(n)
, we have the following upper estimate

\alpha_{j}^{\theta}(n)\leq\alpha^{Y}(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)+\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}n},\,\,j\geq 0,\,n\geq 2N,

where
\kappa
and
N
are as in Lemma
3.10
.

Proof.

We introduce the notations
\theta_{\rightarrow t}:=(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{t})
and
\theta_{t\rightarrow}:=(\theta_{t},\theta_{t+1},\ldots)
, and also
Z_{s,\rightarrow t}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}}:=(Z_{s,s}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}},\ldots,Z_{s,t}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}})
,

Z_{s,t\rightarrow}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}}:=(Z_{s,t}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}},Z_{s,t+1}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{y}},\ldots)
. Let
A\in\mathcal{F}_{0,j}^{\theta}
and
B\in\mathcal{F}_{j+n,\infty}^{\theta}
be arbitrary events. Then by the definition of the generated
\sigma
-algebra, exist
A^{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{j+1})
and
B^{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{N}})
such that

A=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\middle|\theta_{\rightarrow j}(\omega)\in A^{\mathcal{X}}\right\}\,\,\text{and}\,\,B=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\middle|\theta_{j+n\rightarrow}(\omega)\in B^{\mathcal{X}}\right\}.

So, for any
r_{n}
satisfying
0\leq r_{n}\leq n-N
we can write

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)-\mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(B)|&=|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\rightarrow j}\in A^{\mathcal{X}}},\mathds{1}_{\theta_{j+n\rightarrow}\in B^{\mathcal{X}}})|=\left|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathds{1}_{Z_{0,\rightarrow j}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in A^{\mathcal{X}}},\mathds{1}_{Z_{0,j+n\rightarrow}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in B^{\mathcal{X}}})\right|\\
&\leq\left|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathds{1}_{Z_{0,\rightarrow j}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in A^{\mathcal{X}}},\mathds{1}_{Z_{j+r_{n},j+n\rightarrow}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in B^{\mathcal{X}}})\right|+\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,j+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{j+r_{n},j+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right).\end{split}

(13)

Observe that
\mathds{1}_{Z_{0,\rightarrow j}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in A^{\mathcal{X}}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,j-1}^{Y}\vee\mathcal{F}_{1,j}^{\varepsilon}
-measurable and
\mathds{1}_{Z_{j+r_{n},j+n\rightarrow}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in B^{\mathcal{X}}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n},j+n-1}^{Y}\vee\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n}+1,j+n}^{\varepsilon}
-measurable, moreover

\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,j-1}^{Y}\vee\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n},j+n-1}^{Y}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{1,j}^{\varepsilon}\vee\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n}+1,j+n}^{\varepsilon}
, and also the
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{F}_{1,j}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n}+1,j+n}^{\varepsilon}
are independent of each other hence by Remark
3.13
and the stationarity of
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathds{1}_{Z_{0,\rightarrow j}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in A^{\mathcal{X}}},\mathds{1}_{Z_{j+r_{n},j+n\rightarrow}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\in B^{\mathcal{X}}})\right|&\leq\alpha\left(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,j-1}^{Y}\vee\mathcal{F}_{1,j}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n},j+n-1}^{Y}\vee\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n}+1,j+n}^{\varepsilon}\right)\\
&\leq\alpha\left(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty,j-1}^{Y},\mathcal{F}_{j+r_{n},j+n-1}^{Y}\right)\leq\alpha_{j-1}^{Y}(r_{n}+1)\\
&=\alpha^{Y}(r_{n}+1).\end{split}

(14)

By Lemma
3.11
and Corollary
3.2
, we can estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
13
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,j+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{j+r_{n},j+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{j+r_{n},j+n}^{\theta_{j+r_{n}},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{j+r_{n},j+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}(V(\theta_{j+r_{n}}))+V(\theta_{0})+3}{2}e^{-\kappa(n-r_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\kappa(n-r_{n})}.

Combining this with (
14
), and taking the supremum on the left-hand side of (
13
) yields

\alpha_{j}^{\theta}(n)\leq\alpha^{Y}(r_{n}+1)+\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\kappa(n-r_{n})}.

for any
0\leq r_{n}\leq n-N
. By choosing
r_{n}=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, we obtain the desired inequality

\alpha_{j}^{\theta}(n)\leq\alpha^{Y}(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)+\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}n},\,\,j\geq 0,\,n\geq 2N.

∎

3.5
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Lemma 3.15
.

The sequence
(\phi(\theta_{t}))_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly
L^{p}
-bounded for every
1\leq p<\infty
that is

c_{p}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}^{1/p}(|\phi(\theta_{t})|^{p})<\infty.

(15)

Proof.

Using
x^{s}\leq\Gamma(s+1)e^{x}
,
x,s\geq 0
and (
8
), by Corollary
3.2
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{1/p}(|\phi(\theta_{t})|^{p})

\displaystyle\leq c_{\phi}\left(1+\Gamma\left(\frac{rp}{2}+1\right)^{1/p}\mathbb{E}^{1/p}(V(\theta_{t}))\right)\leq c_{\phi}\left(1+\Gamma\left(\frac{rp}{2}+1\right)^{1/p}\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{C}{1-\gamma}\right)^{1/p}\right),

where the upper bound does not depend on
t
hence (
15
) clearly holds.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
(\theta_{0}^{\ast},(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})
be a random variable such that
\text{Law}(\left((\theta_{0}^{\ast},(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}})\right))=\mu^{\ast}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{k})
arbitrary, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\theta_{i_{1}+n},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}+n}\right)\in A\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}},\ldots,Z_{0,i_{k}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right)\in A\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}},\ldots,Z_{0,i_{k}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right)\in A\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\theta_{i_{1}+n}^{\ast},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}+n}^{\ast}\right)\in A\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right).

By interchanging the role of
(\theta_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\theta_{t}^{\ast})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\theta_{i_{1}+n},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}+n}\right)\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\left(\theta_{i_{1}+n}^{\ast},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}+n}^{\ast}\right)\in A\right)\right|\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right),\,\,A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{k}).

Next, we take supremum on the left hand-side in
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X}^{k})
and then by Lemma
3.11
and Remark

, we arrive at

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}\left(\text{Law}((\theta_{i_{1}+n},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}+n})),\text{Law}((\theta_{i_{1}}^{\ast},\ldots,\theta_{i_{k}}^{\ast}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{0,i_{1}+n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{V(\theta_{0})+\mathbb{E}(V(\theta_{0}^{\ast}))+3}{2}e^{-\kappa(i_{1}+n)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\kappa n},\,\,n\geq N,

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
is as in Lemma
3.10
and
3.11
.

In what follows, we proceed with the proof of the law of large numbers both in strong and
L^{p}
sense. Again by Lemma
3.11
and Remark
3.5
, exist an almost surely finite random variable
\tau
such that

Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}=Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}},\,n\geq\tau.

Furthermore, for the tail distribution of
\tau
,

\mathbb{P}(\tau\geq n)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\neq Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}\right)\leq\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\kappa n},\,\,n\geq N.

holds with constants
\kappa>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
as in Lemma
3.10
and
3.11
.

When the data stream
(Y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is ergodic, by Remark
3.6
, the process
Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
is also ergodic, moreover by Remark
3.5
,
\mathbb{E}(\phi(\theta_{0}^{\ast}))<\infty
for any
\phi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying (
8
) hence by Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem,

\frac{\phi(Z_{0,0}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}})+\ldots+\phi(Z_{0,n-1}^{\theta_{0}^{\ast},\mathbf{Y}})}{n}\to\mathbb{E}(\phi(\theta_{0}^{\ast})),\,n\to\infty,\,\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

Combining this with the above result on the almost surely finite coupling time yields the strong law of large numbers for
\phi\left(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As we mentioned earlier, the discrete-time processes
(\theta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\left(Z_{0,n}^{\theta_{0},\mathbf{Y}}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are versions of each other hence the strong law of large numbers holds for
(\phi(\theta_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, as well.

Finally, by Lemma
3.15
, the sequence
\frac{1}{n}(\phi(\theta_{0})+\ldots+\phi(\theta_{n-1}))
,
n\geq 1
is uniformly integrable on every power
1\leq p<\infty
, and thus the law of large numbers holds in
L^{p}
-sense too for
1\leq p<\infty
which completes the proof.
∎

2.4
.

We are going to verify the conditions of Corollary 1 in Herrndorf’s paper
[

]
. By Lemma
3.15
, for
X_{n}:=\phi(\theta_{n})-\mathbb{E}(\phi(\theta_{n}))

n\in\mathbb{N}

,

Next, we prove for
S_{n}:=X_{1}+\ldots+X_{n}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}S_{n}^{2}/n=\sigma^{2}

holds with some

\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}S_{n}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}^{2})+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{l}),

(16)

3.16

\mathbb{D}^{2}(\phi(\theta_{0}^{\ast}))

A_{n,l}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-l}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{k+l})
,

b_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq n}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{l})=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-l}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{k+l})=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}A_{n,l}.

3.16

|A_{n,l}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-l}|\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{k+l})|\leq\Lambda\left(\alpha^{Y}(\lfloor l/2\rfloor)^{1-\epsilon}+e^{-\frac{\kappa}{4}\lfloor l/2\rfloor}\right)

(17)

hence due to Assumption
2.2

\delta>0

\tilde{N}_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}

,

|b_{n}-b_{m}|\leq\sum_{l=1}^{\tilde{N}_{\delta}-1}|A_{n,l}-A_{m,l}|+\sum_{l=\tilde{N}_{\delta}}^{n-1}|A_{n,l}|+\sum_{l=\tilde{N}_{\delta}}^{m-1}|A_{m,l}|<\sum_{l=1}^{\tilde{N}_{\delta}-1}|A_{n,l}-A_{m,l}|+2\delta.

By point i) in Lemma
3.16

A_{n,l}\to\mathrm{Cov}(\phi(\theta_{0}^{\ast}),\phi(\theta_{l}^{\ast}))
, as

, and since

At last, by Lemma
3.14

\alpha_{j}^{X}(n)=\alpha_{j}^{\phi\circ\theta}(n)\leq\alpha_{j}^{\theta}(n)\leq\alpha^{Y}(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)+\left(V(\theta_{0})+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{C}{2(1-\gamma)}\right)e^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}n},\,\,j\geq 0,\,n\geq 2N,

and thus for

,

holds.

To sum up, we have shown that all the conditions of Corollary 1 in
[
7

are satisfied hence we can conclude that,
if
\sigma>0
then

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
3.16

The following auxiliary result is a variation of Theorem 17.2.2 from the renowned book by Ibragimov and Linnik
[
9
]
. In the interest of self-contained explanation and for future reference, we have chosen to present this result here in the required form.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be random variables such that for
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
,
\Psi_{i}
is
\mathcal{A}_{i}
-measurable,
i=1,2
. Furthermore, for some
0<\epsilon<1
,

\mathbb{E}(|\Psi_{i}|^{2/\epsilon+1})<c,\,\,i=1,2
holds with some
c>0
. Then

|\mathrm{Cov}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})|\leq(4+5c)\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})^{1-\epsilon}.

Proof.

Let
L\geq 1
be a positive number which we will fix later, and define the truncated random variables

\hat{\Psi}_{i}=\Psi_{i}\mathds{1}_{|\Psi_{i}|\leq L}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\tilde{\Psi}_{i}=\Psi_{i}\mathds{1}_{|\Psi_{i}|>L},\,\,i=1,2.

We can estimate

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\Psi}_{1}\left(\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2}\mid\mathcal{A}_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2})\right)\right]\right|\leq L\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2}\mid\mathcal{A}_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2})\right|\right)

\displaystyle=L\mathbb{E}\left[\zeta_{1}\left(\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2}\mid\mathcal{A}_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2})\right)\right]=L\mathrm{Cov}(\zeta_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2}),

where
\zeta_{1}=\mathrm{sgn}\left(\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2}\mid\mathcal{A}_{1})-\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Psi}_{2})\right)
is
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-measurable hence by interchanging the role of
\zeta_{1}
and
\hat{\Psi}_{2}
, we can apply the same argument, and thus obtain
|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2})|\leq L^{2}|\mathrm{Cov}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})|
. Note that
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
take values in
\{-1,+1\}
. So, let
A_{1}=\{\zeta_{1}=+1\}
,
A_{2}=\{\zeta_{1}=-1\}
,
B_{1}=\{\zeta_{2}=+1\}
, and
B_{2}=\{\zeta_{2}=-1\}
, where
A_{i}
-s are
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-measurable, and
B_{i}
-s are
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-measurable. We can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap B_{1})+\mathbb{P}(A_{2}\cap B_{2})-\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap B_{2})-\mathbb{P}(A_{2}\cap B_{1})-(\mathbb{P}(A_{1})-\mathbb{P}(A_{2}))(\mathbb{P}(B_{1})-\mathbb{P}(B_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}|\mathbb{P}(A_{i}\cap B_{j})-\mathbb{P}(A_{i})\mathbb{P}(B_{j})|\leq 4\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}),

and thus we arrive at
|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2})|\leq 4L^{2}\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})
.
Trivially,
\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{\Psi}_{i}|)\leq cL^{-2/\epsilon}
and
\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\Psi}_{i}^{2})\leq cL^{1-2/\epsilon}
,
i=1,2
hence for
i,j=1,2
,
|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{i},\tilde{\Psi}_{j})|\leq 2L\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{\Psi}_{j}|)\leq 2cL^{1-2/\epsilon}
, and also by the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2})|+|\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\Psi}_{1},\tilde{\Psi}_{2})|+|\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde{\Psi}_{1},\hat{\Psi}_{2})|+|\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde{\Psi}_{1},\tilde{\Psi}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 4L^{2}\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})+4cL^{1-2/\epsilon}+\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\Psi}_{1}^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{\Psi}_{2}^{2})^{1/2}\leq 4L^{2}\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})+5cL^{1-2/\epsilon}.

At last, we set
L=\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})^{-\epsilon/2}
, and since
\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 4\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})^{1-\epsilon}+5c\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})^{1-\epsilon/2}\leq(4+5c)\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2})^{1-\epsilon},

which completes the proof.

∎

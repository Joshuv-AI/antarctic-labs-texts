On Hahn-Banach smoothness and related properties in Banach spaces
Soumitra Daptari

Abstract
. In this paper, we study several variants of Hahn-Banach smoothness, viz., property- ( S ​ U ) (SU) / ( H ​ B ) (HB) / ( w ​ U ) (wU) , where property- ( S ​ U ) (SU) and property- ( H ​ B ) (HB) are stronger notions and property- ( w ​ U ) (wU) is a weaker notion of Hahn-Banach smoothness. We characterize property- ( w ​ U ) (wU) and property- ( H ​ B ) (HB) . It is observed that L 1 ​ ( μ ) L_{1}(\mu) has property- ( w ​ U ) (wU) in L 1 ( μ , ( ℝ 2 , ∥ . ∥ 2 ) ) L_{1}(\mu,(\mathbb{R}^{2},\|.\|_{2})) but it does not have property- ( U ) (U) in L 1 ( μ , ( ℝ 2 , ∥ . ∥ 2 ) ) L_{1}(\mu,(\mathbb{R}^{2},\|.\|_{2})) for a non-atomic measure μ \mu . 1.
Introduction

One of the major cornerstones of functional analysis is the Hahn-Banach theorem. It ensures that a bounded linear functional on a subspace of a normed linear space can be extended to the entire space while preserving its norm. In the study, we investigate scenarios in which the extension is unique.

1.1.
Notations and definitions

By
X
, we mean a Banach space over real scalars. We consider Y to be a closed subspace of X, unless we are discussing dense linear subspaces. Closed unit ball and unit sphere of
X
are denoted by
B_{X}
and
S_{X}
. The sets
\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:x^{*}(y)=0,\,\forall\,y\in Y\}
and
\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\|x^{*}\|=\|x^{*}|_{Y}\|\}
are denoted by
Y^{\perp}
and
Y^{\#}
, respectively.

We begin this study with the following definition by R. R. Phelps.

Definition 1.1
.

[
15
, Pg. 238]

A subspace
Y
of
X
is said to have
property-
(U)
if every bounded linear functional on
Y
has a unique norm-preserving extension over
X
.

It’s also known as a Hahn-Banach smooth subspace if
Y
has the property-
(U)
. A weakened variant of the preceding concept is known as weak Hahn-Banach smoothness. We designate the same as property-
(wU)
.

Definition 1.2
.

[
12
]

A subspace
Y
of
X
is said to have
property-
(wU)
whenever
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
satisfying
\|y^{*}\|=|y^{*}(y_{0})|

for some
y_{0}\in S_{Y}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to
X
.

A few strengthenings of property-(U) are following.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
such that
Y^{\perp}
is complemented in
X^{*}
by a subspace
G
.

(a)

[
14
]

Y
is said to have
property-
(SU)
in
X
if for each
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\|x^{*}\|>\|y^{\#}\|,

\text{ whenever }x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}\text{ with }y^{\#}\in G,y^{\perp}(\neq 0)\in Y^{\perp}.

(b)

[
10
]

Y
is said to have
property-
(HB)
in
X
if for each
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\|x^{*}\|>\|y^{\#}\|~\&~\|x^{*}\|\geq\|y^{\perp}\|,

\text{ whenever }x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}\text{ with }y^{\#}\in G,y^{\perp}(\neq 0)\in Y^{\perp}.

(c)

[
9
]

Y
is called an
M
-ideal
if for each
x^{*}\in X^{*}
,

\|x^{*}\|=\|y^{\#}\|+\|y^{\perp}\|,

\text{ whenever }x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}\text{ with }y^{\#}\in G,y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}.

J. Hennefeld has introduced the
HB
-subspace in
[
10
]
. We refer to the same as property-
(HB)
.

For a subspace
Y
of
X
, we define
P_{Y}(x)=\{y\in Y:\|x-y\|=d(x,Y)\}
, the set of all the best approximations from
x
to
Y
.
P_{Y}
is known as a metric projection on the subspace

. Note that for
x\in X
,
P_{Y}(x)
may be empty. If
P_{Y}(x)
is non-empty for all

, then
Y
is said to be a proximinal subspace of
X
. The subspace
Y
is said to be a Chebyshev subspace if
P_{Y}(x)
is singleton for all
x\in X
.

1.2.
A short background and motivation of this work

The investigation into the uniqueness of the Hahn-Banach extension started in the decade from 1930 to 1939. A. E. Taylor in
[
18
]
, demonstrated that if the dual space of a normed linear space is strictly convex, then every subspace has property-
(U)
in the normed linear space. After a long period, S. R. Foguel in
[
8
]
discovered that the converse is true. R. R. Phelps in
[
15
]
first concentrated on an individual subspace with property-
(U)
and established that a subspace
Y
of
X
has property-
(U)
if and only if the metric projection on
Y^{\perp}
is single-valued. It should be noted that the metric projection may not be linear in this scenario. The author identified specific finite-dimensional and finite co-dimensional subspaces with property-
(U)
in
\ell_{1}
,
c_{0}
,
\ell_{\infty}
,
C(K)
,
L_{1}(\mu)
, and
L_{\infty}(\mu)
, where
K
is a compact Hausdorff space and
\mu
is a positive measure. The author in
[
10
]
has introduced property-
(HB)
, which is a stronger notion of property-
(U)
but very close to
M
-ideal. The author in
[
14
]
introduced property-
(SU)
as a concept stronger than property-
(U)
but weaker than property-
(HB)
after a few years. It is known that
Y
is an
M
-ideal in
X
if and only if
P_{Y^{\perp}}
is a
L
-projection, i.e.,
\|x^{*}\|=\|P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})\|+\|x^{*}-P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})\|
for all
x^{*}\in X^{*}
(see
[
9
]
). The authors in
[
6
]
have addressed a longstanding gap in the characterization of properties
(wU)/(SU)/(HB)
. They have characterized these three properties in terms of metric projection (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 3.3, Theorem 3.4]
).

From an alternative viewpoint,
Y
is an
M
-ideal in
X
if and only if
Y^{\#}
is a closed subspace of
X^{*}
and
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\oplus_{\ell_{1}}Y^{\perp}
(see
[
9
]
). The author in
[
14
]
demonstrated that
Y
has property-
(SU)
in
X
if and only if
Y^{\#}
is a closed subspace of
X^{*}
and
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\oplus Y^{\perp}
. It is crucial to note that
Y^{\#}
may not show linearity in
X^{*}
even when
Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
. According to
[
15
]
,
Y
has property-
(U)
in
X
if and only if every element in the dual space can be uniquely expressed as a sum of two elements from
Y^{\#}
and
Y^{\perp}
. The corresponding characteristics for property-
(wU)
and property-
(HB)
are missing. The gaps are filled in this study, these two properties are described in terms of
Y^{\#}
and
Y^{\perp}
. We explore these properties from different angles. For finite-dimensional subspaces, property-
(wU)
and property-
(U)
are coinside. But to date, there are no known infinite-dimensional subspaces in classical Banach spaces that have property-
(wU)
but do not have property-
(U)
. In this study, we cover a few such examples of subspaces in classical Banach spaces. We classify the finite-dimensional and finite co-dimensional subspaces as having property-
(SU)
and property-
(HB)
in classical Banach spaces, as motivated by
[
15
]
.

2.
Property-
(U)
and its alterations

In this section, we mainly study property-
(wU)
and property-
(HB)
. We demonstrate the characterizations of these properties. We discuss the transitivity of these properties. Finally, we address the fact that these two properties, along with property-
(U)
and property-
(SU)

2.1.
On property-
(wU)

We begin this subsection with the following complete characterization of property-
(wU)
, motivated by
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(
1
)

Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
.

(
2
)

P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})=0
for
x^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
\|x^{*}\|=|x^{*}(y)|
for some
y\in S_{Y}
.

(
3
)

For any
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}
in
X^{*}
with
\|x_{1}^{*}\|=\|x_{2}^{*}\|=|x_{1}^{*}(y)|=|x_{2}^{*}(y)|
for some
y\in S_{Y}
and
x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}\in Y^{\perp}
, we have
x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}=0
.

Proof.

(1)\Longleftrightarrow(2)
follows from
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
.

(1)\Longrightarrow(3):
Let
x_{1}^{*},x_{1}^{*}
in
X^{*}
with
\|x_{1}^{*}\|=\|x_{2}^{*}\|=|x_{1}^{*}(y)|=|x_{2}^{*}(y)|
for some
y\in S_{Y}
and
x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}\in Y^{\perp}
. Put
y^{*}=x_{1}^{*}|_{Y}=-x_{2}^{*}|_{Y}
. Clearly,
y^{*}
attains norm on
S_{Y}
and
x_{1}^{*},-x_{2}^{*}
are two norm-preserving extensions of
y^{*}
. Thus,
x_{1}^{*}=-x_{2}^{*}
i.e.,
x_{1}^{*}+x_{2}^{*}=0
.

(3)\Longrightarrow(1):
Let
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
such that
\|y^{*}\|=|y^{*}(y)|
for some
y\in S_{Y}
and
x_{1}^{*}
,
x_{2}^{*}
are two norm-preserving extensions of
y^{*}
. Clearly,
\|x_{1}^{*}\|=\|-x_{2}^{*}\|=|x_{1}^{*}(y)|=|-x_{2}^{*}(y)|
and
x_{1}^{*}+(-x_{2}^{*})\in Y^{\perp}
. Thus,
x_{1}^{*}+(-x_{2}^{*})=0
i.e.,
x_{1}^{*}=x_{2}^{*}
. Hence,
Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
.
∎

We observe that property-
(U)
is strictly stronger than property-
(wU)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a probability space. Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
and suppose that
X^{*}
has the RNP. Then,
Y
has property-
(wU)
in
X
if and only if
L_{1}(\mu,Y)
has property-
(wU)
in
L_{1}(\mu,X)
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in L_{1}(\mu,Y)^{*}\cong L_{\infty}(\mu,Y^{*})
such that
\|\Lambda\|=1
and
\Lambda(f)=1
for some
f\in S_{L_{1}(\mu,Y)}
. Clearly,
\Lambda(t)(f(t))\leq\|\Lambda(t)\|\|f(t)\|
fot
t\in\Omega
. Since
\Lambda\in S_{L_{\infty}(\mu,Y^{*})}
, we have that
\Lambda(t)(f(t))\leq\|f(t)\|
. Now,
\|f(t)\|-\Lambda(t)(f(t))\geq 0
and
\int_{\Omega}\|f(t)\|d\mu(t)=1=\int_{\Omega}\Lambda(t)(f(t))d\mu(t)
. Hence,
\Lambda(t)(f(t))=\|f(t)\|
a.e. Therefore,
\Lambda
has a unique norm-preserving extension
\Gamma
over
L_{1}(\mu,X)
, where
\Gamma(t)
is the unique norm-preserving extension of
\Lambda(t)
over
X
a.e.
∎

We start this subsection with the following characterization of property-

.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
. Then the following are equivalent:

(
1
)

Y
has property-
(HB)
in
X
.

(
2
)

Y^{\#}
is a closed subspace of
X^{*}
and
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\oplus Y^{\perp}
with
\|x^{*}\|\geq\|y^{\perp}\|
, whenever
x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}
,
y^{\#}\in Y^{\#}
and
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Let
Y
has property-
(HB)
in
X
. It follows from
[
14
]
that
Y^{\#}
is a closed subspace of
X^{*}
and
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\oplus Y^{\perp}
. Let
x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}
, where
y^{\#}\in Y^{\#}
and
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
. It remains to show
\|x^{*}\|\geq\|y^{\perp}\|
. Now,
d(x^{*},Y^{\perp})=\|x^{*}|_{Y}\|=\|y^{\#}\|=\|x^{*}-y^{\perp}\|
. Hence,
P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})=y^{\perp}
. By
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have
\|y^{\perp}\|=\|P_{Y^{\perp}}(x^{*})\|\leq\|x^{*}\|.

Conversely, let
Y^{\#}
is a closed subspace of
X^{*}
and
X^{*}=Y^{\#}\oplus Y^{\perp}
with
\|x^{*}\|\geq\|y^{\perp}\|
, whenever
x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}
,
y^{\#}\in Y^{\#}
and
y^{\perp}\in Y^{\perp}
. It is enough to show that
\|x^{*}\|>\|y^{\#}\|
, whenever
y^{\perp}\neq 0
. Clearly,
\|x^{*}\|\geq\|x^{*}|_{Y}\|=\|y^{\#}\|
. If
\|x^{*}\|=\|y^{\#}\|
, then
x^{*}
and
y^{\#}
are two distinct norm preserving extensions of
y^{\#}|_{Y}
as
y^{\perp}\neq 0
. It raises a contradiction as it follows from
[
14
]
that
Y
has property-
(SU)
in
X
. Hence,
\|x^{*}\|>\|y^{\perp}\|
.
∎

Let us recall
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
. We may derive the corresponding analog for property-

Theorem 2.9
.

Let

(HB)

has property-

in

. We assume that

in

. Let

be such that
x^{*}=y^{\#}+y^{\perp}
, where

. Choose

∎

Proposition
2.7
states that property-

(U)

[

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
M
be an
M
-summmand in
X
. Suppose that
Z
has property-
(HB)
in

. Then

has property-
(HB)
in

.

Proof.

Since
M
be an
M
-summmand in
X
, there exists a subspace

of
X
such that
X=M\bigoplus_{\ell_{\infty}}N
. Hence, we have that
X^{*}=M^{*}\bigoplus_{\ell_{1}}N^{*}
. Let
P:X^{*}\rightarrow M^{*}
be the linear projection such that
\|x^{*}\|=\|P(x^{*})\|+\|x^{*}-P(x^{*})\|
. Let
P_{Z^{\perp}}:M^{*}\rightarrow Z^{\perp}
be the metric projection. By
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
,
P_{Z^{\perp}}
is a linear projection of norm-
1
. Clearly,
P_{Z^{\perp}}\circ P:X^{*}\rightarrow Z^{\perp}
is a norm-

. Let

, then

\displaystyle\|(I-P_{Z^{\perp}}\circ P)(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle=\|x^{*}-P(x^{*})+P(x^{*})-P_{Z^{\perp}}\circ P(x^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{*}-P(x^{*})\|+\|I-P_{Z^{\perp}}\|\|P(x^{*})\|\leq\|x^{*}\|.

Hence,
\|I-P_{Z^{\perp}}\circ P\|=1
. Now,
[
6
, Theorem 3.4]
ensures that
P_{Z^{\perp}}\circ P:X^{*}\rightarrow Z^{\perp}
is the metric projection of norm-

. This conclude that

has property-
(HB)
in

.
∎

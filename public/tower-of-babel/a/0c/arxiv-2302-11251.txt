All countable subsets of pseudocompact quasitopological Korovin groups are closed, discrete and C ∗ C^{\ast} -embedded
Evgenii Reznichenko, Mikhail Tkachenko

Abstract
We show that all countable subsets of any pseudocompact quasitopological group in the form of a Korovin orbit are closed, discrete, and C ∗ C^{\ast} -embedded. Consequently, any infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not homeomorphic to a topological group. Moreover, infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbits are not homeomorphic to any Mal’tsev space.



1
Introduction

A group with a topology is called
semitopological
if multiplication in the group is separately continuous.
Ellis
[
8
]
proved that a locally compact semitopological group is a topological group, that is, multiplication and inverse are continuous. In
[
15
]
, it is proved that a completely regular countably compact semitopological group is a topological group.

A semitopological group is called
quasitopological
if inversion in the group
is continuous. In
[
12
]
, A. Korovin presented a method for the construction
of pseudocompact semitopological groups that are neither topological groups nor
homeomorphic to any topological group (the latter is shown in our Corollary
1
).
An analysis of Korovin’s method led to the notions of
Korovin map
and

Korovin orbit

[
3
,
11
]

(see also Theorem 2.4.13 in
[
4
]
). Korovin orbits are also used to construct pseudocompact quasitopological groups with various additional properties
[
3
,
11
,
5
,
19
]
.

Our main result states that all countable subsets of a pseudocompact
Korovin orbit are closed, discrete, and
C^{\ast}
-embedded (Theorem
2
).
Consequently, pseudocompact Korovin’s orbits are not homeomorphic to topological
groups or even paratopological groups. Moreover, pseudocompact Korovin’s orbits are
not homeomorphic to any Mal’tsev space (Corollary
2
).

Around Grothendieck’s theorem

We say that a Tychonoff space
X
is a
Korovin space
if for any continuous
mapping
f\colon X\to C_{p}(X)
, the image
f(X)
has compact closure. Here

C_{p}(X)
denotes the space of continuous real-valued functions on
X
with the
topology of pointwise convergence. Since
C_{p}(X)
contains a closed copy of
the real line, any Korovin space is pseudocompact. In
[
15
]
,
it was proved that if a semitopological group
G
is a Korovin space, then it is
a topological group.

A Grothendieck’s theorem in
[
10
]
states that the closure of a countably compact subspace of
C_{p}(X)
, for a countably compact
X
, is compact. It follows from this result that countably compact spaces are Korovin spaces, which implies the aforementioned result about countably compact semitopological groups. Numerous generalizations of Grothendieck’s theorem show that the class of Korovin spaces is quite wide, for example, it includes both separable and Fréchet–Urysohn pseudocompact spaces
[
12
,
15
,
2
]
.

As shown in Corollary
1
,
any infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not homeomorphic to a topological group.
According to
[
15
]
and the above-mentioned result, every infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not a Korovin space.
We conclude, therefore, that Grothendieck’s theorem cannot be extended to pseudocompact spaces. Using D. Shakhmatov’s example of an infinite pseudocompact space
X
in which every countable subset is closed, discrete, and
C^{\ast}
-embedded may provide another example of this type, as pointed out by V. Tkachuk. The subspace
Y=C_{p}(X,[0,1])
of
C_{p}(X)
is closed in
C_{p}(X)
, pseudocompact, and non-compact. Theorem
2
shows that in the above argument, pseudocompact Korovin orbits can be used in place of Shakhmatov’s space from
[
18
]
.

Mal’tsev spaces

In a pseudocompact topological group, any infinite subgroup is not discrete
[
11
]
. Hence, an infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not homeomorphic to a topological group. A ternary operation
M\colon X^{3}\to X
is called a
Mal’tsev operation
if the equality

M(y,y,x)=M(x,y,y)=x

holds for all
x,y\in X
. A space with a continuous Mal’tsev operation is called
Mal’tsev space
. There exists a natural Mal’tsev operation on every group
X
defined by
M(x,y,z)=xy^{-1}z
. If
X
is a topological group, then
M
is continuous. If
X
is a quasitopological group, then
M
is separately continuous. Therefore, every pseudocompact quasitopological Korovin orbit admits a separately continuous Mal’tsev operation, but it does
not admit a continuous Mal’tsev operation.

It is worth mentioning that if
X
is a pseudocompact Mal’tsev space or a Korovin space with a separately continuous Mal’tsev operation, then the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta X
of
X
is a Dugundji compactum
[
17
,
16
]
.

2
Korovin orbits

Let
(G,+)
be an Abelian group and
X
a space with at least two elements.
The group
G
acts on
X^{G}
as follows:

s:G\times X^{G}\to X^{G},\ s(g,f)(h)=f(g+h),

where
f\in X^{G}
and
g,h\in G
.
An element
f\in X^{G}
is called a
Korovin mapping
if for any
M\subset G

with
|M|\leq\omega
and
h\in X^{M}
there exists an element
g\in G
such that
h=\left.s(g,f)\right|_{M}
.

Theorem 1

(
[
12
]
and for
|G|=2^{\omega}
,
Theorem 2.4.13 in
[
4
]
)
.

If
G
is an Abelian group,
X
is a space, and
2\leq|X|\leq|G|=|G^{\omega}|
,
then there exists a Korovin mapping
f\colon G\to X
.

The subspace
G_{f}=\{s(g,f)\,:\,g\in G\}
of
X^{G}
,
for a Korovin mapping
f
, is called a
Korovin orbit
.
Let
\pi_{M}\colon X^{G}\to X^{M}
be the projection, where
M\subset G
.

Proposition 1

(
[
12
]
and Proposition 2.4.14 in
[
4
]
)
.

If
G
is an Abelian group,
X
is a space, and
f\in X^{G}
is a Korovin mapping,
then
\pi_{M}(G_{f})=X^{M}
, for any countable set
M\subset G
.

Proposition
1
implies the following result.

Proposition 2

(
[
12
,
3
]
)
.

A Korovin orbit
G_{f}\subset X^{G}
is pseudocompact if and only if
X^{\omega}
is pseudocompact. In this case,
\beta G_{f}=(\beta X)^{G}
.

Let
G_{f}\subset X^{G}
be a Korovin orbit. The mapping
{\mathfrak{i}}\colon G\to G_{f}

defined by
{\mathfrak{i}}(g)=s(g,f)
for all
g\in G
is bijective
[
3
]
.
We consider the algebraic structure on
G_{f}
induced by
G
. This means that
we define the sum in
G_{f}
be letting
{\mathfrak{i}}(g)+{\mathfrak{i}}(h)={\mathfrak{i}}(g+h)
for all
g,h\in G
.
If
e
is the identity element of
G
, then
{\mathfrak{i}}(e)=s(e,f)
is the identity of the group

G_{f}
. Also,
G_{f}
is a semitopological group provided it carries the topology inherited
from
X^{G}
and if
G
is a Boolean group, then
G_{f}
is a quasitopological group.

Proposition 3
.

Let
G
be an Abelian group,
X
a space, and
f\in X^{G}
a Korovin mapping.
The function
h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}\colon G_{f}\to X
is continuous, for each
h\in G_{f}
.

Proof.

Given an element
h\in G_{f}
, there is
\alpha\in G
such that
h=s(\alpha,f)
.

Claim.

(h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1})(x)=x(\alpha)
for each
x\in G_{f}
.

Indeed, take
\beta\in G
such that
x=s(\beta,f)
. Then

\displaystyle(h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1})(x)

\displaystyle=h({\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(x))=h({\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(s(\beta,f)))=h(\beta)=s(\alpha,f)(\beta)

\displaystyle=f(\alpha+\beta)=f(\beta+\alpha)=s(\beta,f)(\alpha)=x(\alpha).

It follows from our Claim that
h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}=\left.\pi_{\alpha}\right|_{G_{f}}
, where

\pi_{\alpha}:G^{X}\to X,\ h\mapsto h(\alpha)

is the projection. Since
\pi_{\alpha}
is continuous, the mapping
h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}
is continuous as well.
∎

Recall that a subset
Y
of a space
X
is called
C
-embedded

(
C^{\ast}
-embedded
) in
X
if any (bounded) continuous real-valued function on
Y
extends to a continuous real-valued function on
X
. Two nonempty subsets
A,B\subset X
are called
completely separated
in
X
if there exists a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(A)=\{0\}
and
f(B)=\{1\}
. According to
[
9
, 1.17]
, a subset
Y
of a space

X
is
C^{\ast}
-embedded in
X
if and only if every two completely separated subsets of

Y
are completely separated in
X
. It is essential to note that the space
X
is not assumed to satisfy any separation restriction in this fact (known as
Urysohn’s extension theorem
).

The following proposition is immediate from Urysohn’s extension theorem.

Proposition 4
.

Let
X
be a space and
D\subset X
. Then
D
is discrete and
C^{\ast}
-embedded
in
X
if and only if any two nonempty disjoint subsets of
D
are completely separated
in
X
.

The following theorem is the article’s main result. In it, we do not impose any separation
restriction on
X
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be an Abelian group,
X
be a space with
|X|\geq 2
, and

f\colon G\to X
a Korovin mapping.

(1)

If
X
is not anti-discrete, then then every countable subset of the Korovin
orbit
G_{f}
is closed and discrete in
G_{f}
.

(2)

If there exists a non-constant continuous real-valued function on
X
, then
every countable subset in the Korovin orbit
G_{f}
is
C^{\ast}
-embedded.

(3)

If there is an unbounded continuous real-valued function on
X
, then
any countable subset of the Korovin orbit
G_{f}
is
C
-embedded.

Proof.

(1)
Let
A
be a countable subset of
G_{f}
and
g\in G_{f}\setminus A
.
It suffices to check that
g\notin\overline{A}
. Let
U
be a proper nonempty
open subset of
X
. Take elements
x\in U
and
y\in X\setminus U
.
Since
f
is a Korovin mapping, there exists
h\in G_{f}
such that

h({\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(g))=x
and
h({\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(A))=\{y\}
. Proposition
3

implies that the mapping
h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}
is continuous, so

V=(h\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1})^{-1}(U)\ni g

is open in
G_{f}
and
A\subset G_{f}\setminus V
. Hence
g\notin\overline{A}
.

(2)
According to Proposition
4
it suffices to prove that any
countable disjoint subsets
A,B
of
G_{f}
are completely separated
in
G_{f}
. Let
A^{\prime}={\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(A)
and
B^{\prime}={\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(B)
.
Let
q\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-constant continuous function,

x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and
q(x_{1})\neq q(x_{2})
.
Let
M=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
and
h\colon M\to X
be a function such that

h(A^{\prime})=\{x_{1}\}
and
h(B^{\prime})=\{x_{2}\}
. Since

f
is a Korovin mapping, there exists
g\in G
such that
h=\left.s(g,f)\right|_{M}
.
Proposition
3
implies that the mapping
\varphi=s(g,f)\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}:G_{f}\to X
is continuous. Then
\varphi(A)=\{x_{1}\}
and
\varphi(B)=\{x_{2}\}
. The function
\psi=q\circ\varphi
is continuous,
\psi(A)=\{q(x_{1})\}
,

\psi(B)=\{q(x_{2})\}
, and
q(x_{1})\neq q(x_{2})
.

(3)
Let
A=\{a_{n}\,:\,n\in\omega\}\subset G_{f}
be a countable set and

v\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. Let also
q\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be an unbounded
continuous function. There is a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\omega}\subset X
such that

Q=\{q(x_{n}):n\in\omega\}
is a closed discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}
and

q(x_{n})\neq q(x_{m})
if
n\neq m
. Since
Q
is
C
-embedded in
\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a continuous function
r\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
r(q(x_{n}))=v(a_{n})

for each
n\in\omega
. Let
M={\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(A)
and
h\colon M\to X
be a function such
that
h({\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}(a_{n}))=x_{n}
for each
n\in\omega
. Since
f
is a Korovin mapping,
there exists
g\in G
such that
h=\left.s(g,f)\right|_{M}
. Proposition
3

implies that the mapping
\varphi=s(g,f)\circ{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}\colon G_{f}\to X
is continuous.
Then the mapping
\psi=r\circ q\circ\varphi
is continuous and
v=\left.\psi\right|_{A}
.
∎

For the reader convenience, we present a short proof of the following well-known
result (see e.g.
[
4
, Corollary 1.4.24]
).

Proposition 5
.

An infinite pseudocompact topological group has a countable non-closed subset.

Proof.

A pseudocompact topological group is precompact
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, a subgroup
of a precompact topological group is precompact, and a discrete precompact group is finite.
Hence, in an infinite pseudocompact topological group
G
, each infinite subgroup is not discrete, and
G
has a countable non-discrete subspace.
∎

In fact, the above argument shows that Proposition
5
remains valid for
infinite precompact topological groups.

Proposition
5
and Theorem
2
imply the following assertion
announced in the abstract.

Corollary 1
.

Any infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not homeomorphic to a topological group.

3
Discrete subsets of Mal’tsev spaces

Our aim in this short section is to extend Corollary
1
to pseudocompact
Mal’tsev spaces.

A subset
Y
of a Mal’tsev space
X
with a Mal’tsev operation
M
is called a
Mal’tsev subalgebra
if
Y
is closed under the operation
M
, that is
M(Y^{3})=Y
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a pseudocompact Mal’tsev space and
Y
be an infinite discrete Mal’tsev subalgebra of
X
. Then the closure of
Y
in the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta X

of
X
is a metrizable compact set and
Y
is not
C^{\ast}
-embedded in
X
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a continuous Mal’tsev operation on
X
. Then
M

extends to a continuous Mal’tsev operation
\widehat{M}\colon(\beta X)^{3}\to\beta X

on the Stone–Čech compactification
\beta X
of
X

[
17
]
. Let
K
be the closure of
Y
in
\beta X
. Then
K
is a Mal’tsev subalgebra of
\beta X
and
K
is a Mal’tsev space with the dense discrete subspace
Y
. Compact Mal’tsev spaces are Dugundji compact spaces
[
20
,
17
]
. Dugundji compacta are dyadic compacta,
and every dyadic compact space with a dense set of isolated points is metrizable (see
[
7
]
or
[
1
, Corollary 12]
). Hence,
K
is a metrizable
compact space. If
Y
is
C^{\ast}
-embedded in
X
, then
K
is homeomorphic to
\beta Y
and
\beta Y
is not metrizable, which is a contradiction.
∎

Every countable subset of a Mal’tsev space
X
is contained in a countable
Mal’tsev subalgebra of
X
. Therefore, Theorem
3

implies the following fact.

Proposition 6
.

An infinite pseudocompact Mal’tsev space contains either a countable non-closed
subset or a countable subset that fails to be
C^{\ast}
-embedded.

Combining Proposition
6
and Theorem
2

we deduce the following assertion that improves upon Corollary
1
.

Corollary 2
.

An infinite pseudocompact Korovin orbit is not homeomorphic to any
pseudocompact Mal’tsev space.

We do not know whether every infinite pseudocompact Mal’tsev space
contains a countable non-closed subset (see Problem
1

below). In the following proposition we establish that pseudocompact
Mal’tsev spaces have a weaker property.

Proposition 7
.

An infinite pseudocompact Mal’tsev space contains a non-closed subset
of cardinality less than or equal to
\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be an infinite pseudocompact Mal’tsev space. If
X
is countable,
then it is compact and metrizable, so the conclusion of the proposition is
evident. We assume therefore that
|X|\geq\omega_{1}
. Let
Y
be a Mal’tsev
subalgebra of
X
of cardinality
\omega_{1}
. Then
Y
is not discrete — otherwise
Theorem
3
implies that the closure of
Y
in the Stone–Čech
compactification
\beta X
of
X
is a metrizable compact space with an
uncountable discrete subspace. Consequently,
Y
is not discrete
and the set
Y\setminus\{y\}
is not closed for some non-isolated point

y
in
Y
.
∎

4
Questions

In connection with Propositions
5
–
7

it is natural to pose the following problem.

Problem 1
.

Does there exist an infinite pseudocompact Mal’tsev space in which every countable
subset is closed and discrete?

We mention the following properties of the pseudocompact space
X
constructed in
[
14
]
in relation with Problem
1
and Theorem
3
:
(1)
X
is a pseudocompact space of cardinality continuum , and each subset of
X
whose cardinality is less than the continuum is closed and discrete; (2)
\beta X
is homeomorphic to a Tychonoff cube of uncountable weight and is a Dugundji compactum; (3) the closure of every countable subset of
X
in
\beta X
is a metrizable compact space; (4)
X
is a hereditarily metalindelöf space

[
13
]
.
Proposition
7
implies that, assuming the negation of the Continuum Hypothesis, the space
X
cannot be a Mal’tsev space. If we assume the Continuum
Hypothesis, then it is not clear whether the space
X
is Mal’tsev or not.

Considering Corollaries
1
and
2
, the following problem arises.

Problem 2
.

Can an infinite Korovin orbit
G_{f}\subset X^{G}
be homeomorphic to a topological
group or a Mal’tsev space? What if
X
is a (countable) discrete space?

A space
X
is called
weakly pc-Grothendieck
if the closure of every pseudocompact subspace
C_{p}(X)
is compact
[
2
]
. A pseudocompact weakly pc-Grothendieck space is a Korovin space.

The only instances of pseudocompact semitopological groups that are not topological
groups are Korovin orbits. Also, Shakhmatov’s example in
[
18
]
is a pseudocompact space that is not weakly pc-Grothendieck because the closed
pseudocompact subspace
Y=\{f\in C_{p}(X):f(X)\subset[-1,1]\}
of
C_{p}(X)
is not
compact.

Let us call a pseudocompact space
Z

Shakhmatov space
if any countable subset of
Z
is discrete, closed, and
C^{\ast}
-embedded. The space in
[
18
]
and pseudocompact Korovin orbits are Shakhmatov spaces.

Problem 3
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be one of the classes listed below.

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
)

Pseudocompact spaces with countable extend.

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}
)

Pseudocompact spaces that have a dense subspace with countable extend.

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
)

Pseudocompact spaces that have a dense Lindelöf subspace.

Find out which of the following statements are true:

(1)

There is a Korovin orbit from
{\mathcal{P}}
.

(2)

There is a Shakhmatov space from
{\mathcal{P}}
.

(3)

There is a not weakly pc-Grothendieck space from
{\mathcal{P}}
.

(4)

There is a semitopological group from
{\mathcal{P}}
which is not a topological group.

It is easy to see that
(1)\rightarrow(2)\rightarrow(3)
and
(1)\rightarrow(4)\rightarrow(3)
.

Problem 4
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be one of the classes listed below.

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{4}
)

Pseudocompact spaces that have a dense subspace with countable tightness.

(
{\mathcal{P}}_{5}
)

Pseudocompact spaces with a dense subspace which is
k
-space.

Find out which of the following statements are true:

(1)

There is a space from
{\mathcal{P}}
that fails to be weakly pc-Grothendieck.

(2)

There is a semitopological group from
{\mathcal{P}}
which is not a topological group.

Note that in Problem

, (2) implies (1). We also mention that Shakhmatov’s space in
[
18
]
does not help to answer (1) of Problem

, while Korovin orbits do not help answering (2) of the problem. These conclusions follow from

Rotational K α K^{\alpha} -Translators in Minkowski space
Muhittin Evren Aydin, Rafael López

Abstract
. A spacelike surface in Minkowski space ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}_{1}^{3} is called a K α K^{\alpha} -translator of the flow by the powers of Gauss curvature if satisfies K α = ⟨ N , v → ⟩ K^{\alpha}=\langle N,\vec{v}\rangle , α ≠ 0 \alpha\neq 0 , where K K is the Gauss curvature, N N is the unit normal vector field and v → \vec{v} is a direction of ℝ 1 3 \mathbb{R}_{1}^{3} . In this paper, we classify all rotational K α K^{\alpha} -translators. This classification will depend on the causal character of the rotation axis. Although the theory of the K α K^{\alpha} -flow holds for spacelike surfaces, the equation describing K α K^{\alpha} -translators is still valid for timelike surfaces. So we also investigate the timelike rotational surfaces that satisfy the same prescribing Gauss curvature equation.

1.
Introduction and formulation of the problems

In this paper we consider the translating solitons of the flow by powers of the Gauss curvature
K
in the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
. Let
X:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
be a smooth immersion of a spacelike surface
\Sigma
with positive Gauss curvature
K
. The
K^{\alpha}
-flow is a one-parameter family of smooth spacelike immersions
X_{t}=X(\cdot,t)\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
,
t\in[0,T)
such that
X_{0}=X
and satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(p,t)=-K(p,t)^{\alpha}N(p,t),\quad(p,t)\in\Sigma\times[0,T),

where
\alpha\not=0
is a constant,
N(p,t)
is the unit normal of
X(p,t)
and
K(p,t)
is the Gauss curvature at
X(p,t)
. Our interest are those surfaces
\Sigma
that move under the flow along a direction
\vec{v}
of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
. A spacelike surface
\Sigma
is called a translator by the
K^{\alpha}
-flow and speed
\vec{v}
if

(1)

K^{\alpha}=\langle N,\vec{v}\rangle.

Here we understand that
K>0
discarding the trivial case
K=0
. We also observe that the left-hand side of (
1
) is positive. This implies that if we reverse the orientation
N
, which does not affect to the term
K^{\alpha}
, then the speed
\vec{v}
must change of sign. The flow by powers of the Gauss curvature in Euclidean space was initiated by Andrews, Chow and Urbas
[
1
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. In contrast, its study in Minkowski space has received less attention
[
2
,
7
]
. We point out
[
9
]
where has been investigated the Dirichlet problem associated to (
1
).

In this paper we will classify all
K^{\alpha}
-translators of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
of rotational type. A first purpose is to enrich the theory of the flow by powers of the Gauss curvature in a natural class of surfaces as the surfaces of revolution. Also rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators can be employed as barriers in order to obtain
C^{1}
and
C^{2}
apriori estimates in the Dirichlet problem associated to the equation (
1
), such as it occurs for the prescribing Gauss curvature equation
K=\mbox{ct}

[
4
,
5
,
8
]
. On the other hand, the family of surfaces of revolution of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
is greater than of Euclidean case because in the Lorentzian ambient space we have three types of surfaces depending on the causal character of the rotation axis. Together this, the speed
\vec{v}
has again three possible causal choices.

Finally, one more, and no less interesting, observation. If we now leave in mind the flow theory, it is natural to ask about the solutions of (
1
) in the case that the surfaces are timelike. Let us point out that all terms that appear in (
1
) are still valid and (
1
) can be viewed as a prescribing curvature equation. Overusing of the language, and for simplicity in the statements, we will say that a timelike surface satisfying (
1
) is a
timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator
, leaving
K^{\alpha}
-translator in case that the surface is spacelike.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some basics of local differential geometry of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
. In separated cases according to the type of surface of revolution, in Section
3
we classify the rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators (Theorems
3.2
,
3.7
,
3.11
). As a first step, we prove that the speed
\vec{v}
is not arbitrary: although initially there is not a priori relation between
\vec{v}
and the direction of the rotation axis of the surface, we will prove that the rotation axis must be parallel to the speed
\vec{v}
(Propositions
3.1
,
3.6
,
3.10
). In Section
4
, we do an analogous study for the rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translators.

As a consequence of our results, we prove the non-existence of rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators orthogonally intersecting the rotation axis when the rotation axis is timelike (Corollary
3.4
) or spacelike (Corollary
3.8
). This contrasts with the result in Euclidean setting where, for any
\alpha
, such translators exist (
[
3
]
). In spite of this, as long as
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, there are rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators that intersect the rotation axis at a conical point (Corollaries
3.4
,
4.6
). A last observation is that in some situations, we will have a
K^{\alpha}
-translator that can be extended by means of a timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with the same rotation axis. This is because the maximal domain of the function
f(r)
that determines the generating curve is a bounded interval where at the endpoint the surface leaves to be spacelike. We will prove that under this circumstance, it is possible to extend the surface beyond of the domain of
f
with a rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with the same axis.

2.
Preliminaries

The Lorentz-Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
is the real
3
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the metric
\langle,\rangle=dx^{2}+dy^{2}-dz^{2}
, where

(x,y,z)
are the canonical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\Sigma
be a surface isometrically immersed in
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
whose induced metric is non-degenerate. If the metric, denoted by
\langle,\rangle
again, is Riemannian (resp. Lorentzian), the surface is said to be
spacelike
(resp.
timelike
).
Denote by
\nabla^{0}
and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
and the induced connection on
\Sigma
. The second fundamental form
\sigma
is defined by the Gauss formula

(2)

\nabla_{U}^{0}V=\nabla_{U}V+\sigma(U,V),

for any tangent vector fields
U
and
V
on
\Sigma
. Let
N
be a (local) unit normal vector field on
\Sigma
. We know that if the immersion is spacelike, the surface is always orientable and that
\langle N,N\rangle=-1
. If
\Sigma
is timelike, then
N
is spacelike and it is only a local unit normal vector field. To simplify the next notation, let
\langle N,N\rangle=\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}
. Because
\langle N,N\rangle
is constant, we have
\langle\nabla_{U}^{0}N,N\rangle=0
. Then
\nabla^{0}_{U}N
is tangent to
\Sigma
and the Weingarten endomorphism
A
is defined by

-\nabla^{0}_{U}N=AU
. We have

\sigma(U,V)=\epsilon\,\langle\sigma(U,V),N\rangle N=\epsilon\,\langle AU,V\rangle N.

Now (
2
) writes as

\nabla^{0}_{U}V=\nabla_{U}V+\epsilon\,\langle AU,V\rangle N.

The Weingarten map
A
is self-adjoint with respect to the induced metric
\langle,\rangle
. In particular, if
\Sigma
is spacelike then
A
is real diagonalizable because the metric is Riemannian. The principal curvatures
\lambda_{i}
are the eigenvalues of
A
. If
\Sigma
is timelike, then
A
may be or not (real) diagonalizable. We define the (intrinsic) Gauss curvature
K
of the surface as

K=\epsilon\,\mbox{ det}(A).

If
A
is diagonalizable, then
K=\epsilon\,\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
. We need the expression of
K
in local coordinates.
For a parametrization
X=X(u,v)
of a (spacelike or timelike) surface, we have

K=-\frac{\mbox{det}(X_{u},X_{v},X_{uu})\mbox{det}(X_{u},X_{v},X_{vv})-\mbox{det}(X_{u},X_{v},X_{uv})^{2}}{(EG-F^{2})^{2}},

where, as usually,

E=\langle X_{u},X_{u}\rangle,\,F=\langle X_{u},X_{v}\rangle,\,G=\langle X_{v},X_{v}\rangle

are the coefficients of the first fundamental form. Recall that
EG-F^{2}>0
if
\Sigma
is spacelike and
EG-F^{2}<0
if
\Sigma
is timelike.

We now describe the parametrizations of the surfaces of revolution of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
. In
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
there are three types of surfaces of revolution according to the causal character of the rotation axis
L
. Let
B=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}=\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}
be the usual basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. After a rigid motion, we can assume that
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{3}\}
(timelike),
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{1}\}
(spacelike) or
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{2}+e_{3}\}
(lightlike).

(1)

The axis is timelike,
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{3}\}
. The generating curve is
r\mapsto(r,0,f(r))
,
r>0
, where
f
is a smooth function and
the parametrization of the surface is

(3)

X(r,\theta)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,f(r)).

Here
EG-F^{2}=r^{2}(1-f^{\prime 2})
so the surface is spacelike (resp. timelike) if
f^{\prime 2}<1
(resp.
f^{\prime 2}>1
).

(2)

The axis is spacelike,
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{1}\}
. The generating curve can be included in the
xz
-plane or in the
xy
-plane. In the first case, if the curve is
r\mapsto(f(r),0,r)

r>0
, the parametrization of the surface is

(4)

X(r,\theta)=(f(r),r\sinh\theta,r\cosh\theta).

Now
EG-F^{2}=r^{2}(f^{\prime 2}-1)
and the surface is spacelike (resp. timelike) if
f^{\prime 2}>1
(resp.
f^{\prime 2}<1
).
In the second case, if
r\mapsto(f(r),r,0)
,
r>0
, then

(5)

X(r,\theta)=(f(r),r\cosh\theta,r\sinh\theta).

This surface is always timelike because
EG-F^{2}=-r^{2}(1+f^{\prime 2})<0
.

(3)

The axis is lightlike,
L=\mbox{sp}\{e_{2}+e_{3}\}
. The generating curve is
r\mapsto(0,f(r)+r,f(r)-r)
,
r>0
, and the surface is

(6)

X(r,t)=(2rt,f(r)+r-rt^{2},f(r)-r-rt^{2}).

We have
EG-F^{2}=16r^{2}f^{\prime}
, so the surface is spacelike (resp. timelike) if
f^{\prime}>0
(resp.
f^{\prime}<0
).

3.
K^{\alpha}
-translators of rotational type

In this section, we investigate the
K^{\alpha}
-translators which are surfaces of revolution. Since there are three types of such surfaces, our investigation will be into three separated subsections where the rotation axis is timelike, spacelike and lightlike, respectively.

3.1.
The axis is timelike

First we prove that the speed
\vec{v}
must be parallel to the rotation axis.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\Sigma
is a rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with timelike axis, then
\vec{v}
is parallel to its
rotation axis.

Proof.

By applying a rigid motion of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
, we take the rotation
axis as the
z
-axis. The parametrization of the surface is given in (
3
) with
f^{\prime 2}<1
and the unit normal vector field is

(7)

N=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}(\cos\theta f^{\prime},\sin\theta f^{\prime},1).

The Gauss curvature is

K=-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(1-f^{\prime 2})^{2}}.

If
\vec{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, equation (
1
) becomes

P_{0}(r)+P_{1}(r)\cos\theta+P_{2}(r)\sin\theta=0,

where

\displaystyle P_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(1-f^{\prime 2})^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}-v_{3}(1-f^{\prime 2})^{-1/2},

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}f^{\prime}(1-f^{\prime 2})^{-1/2},

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{2}f^{\prime}(1-f^{\prime 2})^{-1/2}.

Since the functions
\left\{1,\cos\theta,\sin\theta\right\}
are linearly independent, all coefficients
P_{k}
must vanish. Assume first that
f^{\prime}\neq 0
at some point. Then
P_{1}=0
and
P_{2}=0
imply
v_{1}=v_{2}=0,
proving the result. If
f^{\prime}=0
for all
r
, then
f
is a constant function and the surface is a plane and
K=0
, which it is impossible. ∎

After this proposition, the speed is
\vec{v}=(0,0,v_{3})
. According to
N
in (
7
), and because
\langle N,\vec{v}\rangle
is positive in (
1
), without loss of generality we can assume that
\vec{v}=(0,0,1)
. Then (
1
) is equivalent to
P_{0}=0
, that is,

(8)

\left(-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(1-f^{\prime 2})^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}.

Let us introduce the auxiliary function

h=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}.

In terms of
h
, equation (
8
) writes as

\left(-\frac{hh^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\alpha}=h,

or equivalently,

h^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}h^{\prime}=-r.

A first integration gives

h(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}me^{\frac{-r^{2}}{2}},m>0,&&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\left(m-\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}r^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}},m\in\mathbb{R},&&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

From
h
, we deduce the value of
f^{\prime}
,

f^{\prime}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\left(1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}e^{r^{2}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\pm\left(1-\left(m-\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}r^{2}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Here some constraints arise naturally which determine the domain of
f
. Consequently, we have the classification of rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators with timelike axis.

Theorem 3.2
.

Any rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with timelike axis parametrizes as (
3
), where

(9)

f(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\pm\int^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}e^{t^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\,dt,m>1,&&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\pm\int^{r}\left(1-\left(m-\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}t^{2}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}\,dt,m\in\mathbb{R},&&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

The maximal domain of the above function
f(r)
is:

(1)

Case
\alpha=1/2
:
(0,\sqrt{\log m^{2}})
, where
m>1
.

(2)

Case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
:
(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}m},\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}(1-m)})
if
m<0
or
(0,\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}(1-m)})
if
0\leq m<1
.

(3)

Case
\alpha\not\in[0,1/2]
:
(0,\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}(m-1)})
and
m>1
.

Let us observe that in all cases, the function
f=f(r)
is defined in a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}
. It is important to know the behaviour of
f^{\prime}
at the endpoints of the domain because it explains what is happening. So, for any
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

\lim_{r\to r_{0}}f^{\prime}(r)=0,

where
r_{0}
is the right endpoint of the domain. Then, as independently from the value of
\alpha
, the parenthesis in (
8
) has a finite limit at
r_{0}
because the right hand-side has
1
as limit. Thus the second derivative of
f
blows up at
r_{0}
. This implies that we cannot extend the solution beyond this endpoint. In contrast, in the particular case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and
m<0
, and if
r_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}m}
, we have

\lim_{r\to r_{0}}f^{\prime}(r)^{2}=1,

which means that at
r_{0}
the surface leaves to be spacelike. As we will see in Section
4
, we can extend the surface beyond of the value
r_{0}
by means of a rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with the same rotation axis.

The case
\alpha=1/4
will be a distinguished case in this paper, independently of the causal character of the surface as well as the rotation axis. This is due to that equation (
8
) simplifies enormously because the terms involving
1-f^{\prime 2}
disappear and consequently, the integration can be done explicitly.

Corollary 3.3
.

Rotational
K^{1/4}
-translators with timelike axis parametrize as (
3
), where

f(r)=\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(r\sqrt{1-m-r^{2}}-(m-1)\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{1-m-r^{2}}}\right)\right),

where
r\in(0,\sqrt{1-m})
and
m<1
.

We study the behaviour of the rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators that intersect the rotation axis. This implies that the generating curve
r\mapsto(r,0,f(r))
is defined at the limit at
r=0
. Here we are interested in two situations whose intersection occurs orthogonally or at a conical point, that is, a point where the metric is degenerated. This is equivalent
to
\lim_{r\to 0}f^{\prime}(r)=0
(or
\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=1
) in the first case or
\lim_{r\to 0}f^{\prime}(r)^{2}=1
(
\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=\infty
) in the second one.

Corollary 3.4
.

There are not rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators with timelike axis intersecting orthogonally the rotation axis. On the other hand, if
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, there are rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators with timelike axis intersecting the rotation axis at a conical point.

Proof.

If
\alpha=1/2
,
\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=m>1
. If
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, then the generating curve arrives to the rotation axis when
m\in[0,1)
. In such a case
\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=m^{\alpha/(2\alpha-1)}\not=1
. Analogously, if
\alpha\not\in[0,1/2]
,
\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=m^{\alpha/(2\alpha-1)}\not=1
. This proves that the intersection with the rotation axis is not orthogonal.

The above limits also prove the non existence of conical points when
\alpha=1/2
and
\alpha\not\in[0,1/2]
. If
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and
0\leq m<1
, the function
f
is defined at the limit at
r=0
with

\lim_{r\to 0}h(r)=\lim_{r\to 0}m^{\alpha/(2\alpha-1)}.

This limit is
\infty
if
m=0
.
∎

This corollary contrasts with the Euclidean setting. In
[
3
]
, the authors have proved the existence of rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators intersecting orthogonally the rotation axis for all values of
\alpha
.

Remark 3.5
.

For some particular values of
\alpha
and the constant
m
, we can explicitly write the function
f(r)
in (
9
). Let us denote by
f_{\alpha}
the solution of (
9
) to indicate the value of
\alpha
.

(1)

Case
\alpha=1
. If
m=1
, then

f_{1}(r)=\pm\sqrt{r^{2}-4}\mp\sqrt{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-4}}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(2)

Case that
\alpha=1/6
. If
m=0
, then

f_{\frac{1}{6}}(r)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}(1-\sqrt{2}r)^{3/2}.

(3)

Case that
\alpha=1/10
. If we choose
m=0
then

f_{\frac{1}{10}}(r)=\pm\frac{2}{15}\sqrt{1-\sqrt{2r}}\left(-6r+\sqrt{2r}+2\right).

3.2.
The axis is spacelike

As in the case of timelike axis, we first prove that the rotation axis is parallel to the speed
\vec{v}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
\Sigma
is a rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with spacelike axis, then
\vec{v}
is parallel to its
rotation axis.

Proof.

After a rigid motion of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
, we suppose that the rotation axis is the
x
-axis. Then, the generating curve of the surface of revolution
\Sigma
is a planar curve included in the
xz
-plane and the parametrization of the surface is (
4
) where
f^{\prime 2}>1
. Recall that the parametrization (
5
) is not possible, see Section
2
. The Gauss curvature is

K=-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{2}}

and the unit normal vector field

(10)

N=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{f^{\prime 2}-1}}(1,f^{\prime}\sinh\theta,f^{\prime}\cosh\theta).

The equation (
1
) is now

P_{0}(r)+P_{1}(r)\cosh\theta+P_{2}(r)\sinh\theta=0,

where

\displaystyle P_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}+v_{1}(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{-1/2}=0,

\displaystyle P_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{2}f^{\prime}\sinh\theta(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{-1/2},

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v_{3}f^{\prime}\cosh\theta(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{-1/2}.

Again we deduce
v_{2}=v_{3}=0
, obtaining the result.
∎

By this proposition, the speed
\vec{v}
is
(v_{1},0,0)
. Since we require that
\langle N,\vec{v}\rangle
must be positive in (
1
), and taking into account the expression of
N
in
(
10
), we can assume
\vec{v}=(-1,0,0)
. Now (
1
) is equivalent to
P_{0}=0
, yielding

(11)

\left(-\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{r(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f^{\prime 2}-1}}.

Let
h=(f^{\prime 2}-1)^{-1/2}
. Therefore (
11
) is

h^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}h^{\prime}=r,

and integrating, we have

h(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}me^{r^{2}/2},m>0,&&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\left(m+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}r^{2}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}},m\in\mathbb{R},&&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

In terms of
f^{\prime}
, we write

(12)

f^{\prime}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\left(1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}e^{-r^{2}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\pm\left(1+\left(m+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}r^{2}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

From the expression of
f^{\prime}
, we have the next classification of the rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators with spacelike axis and the domain of the function
f
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Any rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with spacelike axis parametrizes as (
4
), where

(13)

f(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\pm\int^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}e^{-t^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\,dt,m>0,&&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\pm\int^{r}\left(1+\left(m+\frac{2\alpha-1}{2\alpha}t^{2}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}\right)^{1/2}\,dt,m\in\mathbb{R},&&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

The maximal domain of the above function
f(r)
is:

(1)

Case
\alpha=1/2
. The domain is
(0,\infty)
.

(2)

Case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. The domain is
(0,\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}m})
, where
m>0
.

(3)

Case
\alpha\not\in[0,1/2]
. The domain is
(0,\infty)
if
m\geq 0
or
(\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}m},\infty)
if
m<0
.

We point out that if
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\lim_{r\to\sqrt{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}m}}f^{\prime}(r)^{2}=1,

and in Section
4
, this solution will be extended beyond of this value by means of a rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with the same rotation axis. We now study if the rotational surface can orthogonally intersect the rotation axis.

Corollary 3.8
.

There are not rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translators with spacelike axis and intersecting orthogonally the rotation axis.

Proof.

The orthogonality condition with the rotation axis is equivalent to
\lim_{r\to 0}f^{\prime}(r)=0
. From (
12
), we have

\lim_{r\to 0}f^{\prime}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\pm\left(1+\frac{1}{m^{2}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\pm\left(1+m^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}\right)^{1/2},&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2},\end{array}\right.

and in both cases, this limit cannot be
0
.
∎

The particular value
\alpha=1/4
can be solved explicitly.

Corollary 3.9
.

Rotational
K^{1/4}
-translators with spacelike axis parametrize as (
4
), where

f(r)=\pm\frac{1}{2}\left(r\sqrt{1+m-r^{2}}+(1+m)\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{1+m-r^{2}}}\right)\right),

where
r\in(0,\sqrt{1+m})
and
m>-1
.

3.3.
The axis is lightlike

We assume that the causal character of the rotation axis is lightlike and we prove that the rotation axis must be parallel to the speed.

Proposition 3.10
.

If
\Sigma
is a rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with lightlike axis, then
\vec{v}
is parallel to the rotation axis.

Proof.

Up to a rigid motion of
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{3}
, we can choose the axis to be
L=\mbox{sp}\{(0,1,1)\}
and the parametrization of the surface is given by (
6
) with
f^{\prime}>0
. The Gauss curvature is

K=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{8rf^{\prime 2}}

and the unit normal vector field is

N=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f^{\prime}}}(-2t,-1+t^{2}+f^{\prime},1+t^{2}+f^{\prime}).

Thus, if
\vec{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, the equation (
1
) is

\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{8rf^{\prime 2}}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f^{\prime}}}(-2tv_{1}+(-1+t^{2}+f^{\prime})v_{2}-(1+t^{2}+f^{\prime})v_{3}).

This is a polynomial equation of degree
2
on the variable
t
and the coefficients are functions on
r
, in particular, these coefficients must vanish. The coefficient of
t^{2}
yields
v_{2}=v_{3}
and of
t
,
v_{1}=0
, proving the result.
∎

Once we have proved this proposition, the right-hand side of (
1
) is
\langle N,\vec{v}\rangle=-v_{2}/\sqrt{f^{\prime}}
, in particular,
v_{2}<0
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\vec{v}=(0,-1,-1)
. Then (
1
) is

\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{8rf^{\prime 2}}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f^{\prime}}},

or equivalently,

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime\frac{4\alpha-1}{2\alpha}}}=8r.

Integrating,

(14)

f^{\prime}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}me^{4r^{2}},m>0,&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\left(\frac{2(1-2\alpha)}{\alpha}r^{2}+m\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}},m\in\mathbb{R},&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

From (
14
), we find the domain of
f
. On the other hand, the spacelike condition
f^{\prime}(r)>0
leaves to be satisfied when
f^{\prime}(r)=0
. This occurs in the case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
and
m<0
. Indeed, we have

\lim_{r\to\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2(2\alpha-1)}m}}f^{\prime}(r)=0.

This solution can be extended to the left of the domain by means of a rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with the same rotation axis.

Theorem 3.11
.

Any rotational
K^{\alpha}
-translator with lightlike axis parametrizes as (
6
), where

(15)

f(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}m\int^{r}e^{4t^{2}}dt,m>0,&&\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\\
\int^{r}\left(\frac{2(1-2\alpha)}{\alpha}t^{2}+m\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}dt,m\in\mathbb{R},&&\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

The maximal domain of the above function
f(r)
is:

(1)

Case
\alpha=1/2
. The domain is
(0,\infty)
.

(2)

Case
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
. The domain is
(0,\infty)
if
m\geq 0
or
(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2(2\alpha-1)}m},\infty)
if
m<0
.

(3)

Case
\alpha\not\in[0,1/2]
. The domain is
(0,\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2(2\alpha-1)}m})
where
m>0
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Rotational
K^{1/4}
-translators with lightlike axis parametrize as (
6
), where

f(r)=\frac{4}{3}r^{3}+mr,

where
r\in(0,\infty)
if
m>0
and
r\in(\sqrt{-m}/2,\infty)
otherwise.

Again, for some particular values of
\alpha
and the constant
m
, we can integrate (
15
). For example,

f_{1}(r)=\pm\frac{\sqrt{2}\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}r}{\sqrt{m}}\right)}{4m^{3/2}}+\frac{r}{2m^{2}-4mr^{2}},

and for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
with
m=0
,

f_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{1-2\alpha}{1+2\alpha}\left(\frac{2(1-2\alpha)}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}r^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{1-2\alpha}}.

Proposition 4.1

4.1.

Theorem 4.2
.

Any rotational timelike
K^{\alpha}
-translator with timelike axis parametrizes as (

Corollary 4.6

K^{\alpha}
-translators with spacelike axis intersecting orthogonally the rotation axis. If

An Asymptotic Formula for Eigenvalues of the Neumann Laplacian in Domains with a Small Star-shaped Hole
Ly Hong Hai

Abstract
This article investigates a spectral problem of the Laplace operator in a two-dimensional bounded domain perforated by a small arbitrary star-shaped hole and on the smooth boundary of which the Neumann boundary condition is imposed. It is proved that the eigenvalues of this problem converge to the eigenvalues of the Laplacian defined on the unperturbed domain as the size of the hole approaches zero. Furthermore, our main theorem provides the rate of convergence by showing an asymptotic expansion for all simple eigenvalues with respect to the size and shape of the hole.

Introduction

In recent years, the application of spectral theory to the study of the effects of a small hole on the sound of a drum or bell, as well as the behaviour of the heat equation in the study of heat distribution in objects, has arisen great interest among scientific
community. A large number of studies have been conducted in this field, exploring topics such as the spectral properties of Laplacian in bounded domains under Dirichlet or Neumann conditions, the asymptotic expansion of eigenvalues for domains with small holes, the changes in eigenvalues as the domain is varied and so on. Several studies are briefly discussed as follows. Rauch and Taylor

demonstrated the convergence of the Laplacian spectrum on a hole-like compact set with zero Lebesgue measure to the original domain’s Laplacian spectrum.

As far as we are concerned, much of the previous research has focused on studying the spectral problem of the Laplace operator in domains with specifically shaped holes, such as spherical or elliptical holes. However, the exploration of domains with holes of general shape and smooth boundaries is still limited, although they offer practical advantages. General-shaped holes with smooth boundaries are commonly encountered in engineering scenarios, including the design of heat exchangers, acoustic chambers, fluid flow control devices, and electromagnetic waveguides. By investigating the spectral problem in these domains, we can analyze the influence of the hole’s shape on various physical phenomena. The smoothness of the hole’s boundary ensures the continuity of heat flow, acoustic waves, fluid motion, or electromagnetic fields, allowing for accurate modelling and efficient analysis.

Motivated by the observation above, we consider the spectral problem of the Laplace operator in a bounded planar domain
\Omega
. This domain is perforated by a small star-shaped hole
E_{\varepsilon}
with a smooth boundary, where
\varepsilon
is a fixed parameter. The notion of a star-shaped set is known as a natural generalization of a convex set. We impose the Neumann condition on the boundary of the hole and the Dirichlet condition on the outer boundary of the domain. Our main contribution in this paper is constructing an asymptotic expansion with respect to the hole size for all simple eigenvalues. To analyze the problem, we employ asymptotic techniques and perturbation methods. More precisely, we first parameterize the small hole by using curvilinear coordinates, which helps to develop approximations for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions by employing the concept of Green’s kernel. Green’s kernel has been well-known as a fundamental solution of the Laplace equation that characterizes the behaviour of the Laplace operator in the domain of interest. We construct Green operators by utilizing Green’s kernel, an integral operator associated with Laplacian. Using Green operators, we derive approximations for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplacian with the Neumann boundary condition on the hole. These approximations provide a means to estimate the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. From that, we obtain the desired asymptotic formula of eigenvalues. The case

2
Statement of the problem and main result

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with smooth
C^{1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
and the origin
\tilde{w}\in\Omega
. Let
E\subset\Omega
be an open domain of star-shaped concerning a neighbourhood of
\tilde{w}
and
\partial E
is also of class
C^{1}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the small hole
E_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon E
. We introduce the curvilinear coordinates, where
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
denotes the Cartesian coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{1}=\alpha(\rho)\beta(\theta)\cos\theta\\
x_{2}=\alpha(\rho)\omega(\theta)\sin\theta\end{cases}

with
\rho\in[0,\infty)
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, where the functions
\alpha,\beta,\omega
are
C^{1}
-functions satisfying

(i)

\beta,\omega
are periodic of period
2\pi
with
\beta(0),\beta(\pi),\omega(\pi/2),\omega(3\pi/2)\neq 0
,

(ii)

\alpha,\beta
are strictly increasing and
\alpha(0)=0
,
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\alpha(\rho)=\infty
and

(iii)

for any
\rho>0,\theta\in[0,2\pi)

\displaystyle\beta(\theta)\cos(\theta)(\beta(\theta)\cos(\theta))^{\prime}+\omega(\theta)\sin(\theta)(\omega(\theta)\sin(\theta))^{\prime}=0.

The assumptions (i) and (ii) ensure that map
(\rho,\theta)\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2})
is a diffeomorphism from
(0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi)
to
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{\tilde{w}\}
. The condition (iii) is for the orthogonality of the curve
(x_{1}(\cdot,\theta_{0}),x_{2}(\cdot,\theta_{0}))
and
(x_{1}(\rho_{0},\cdot),x_{2}(\rho_{0},\cdot))
for any
\rho_{0}>0
and
\theta_{0}\in[0,\infty)
, which is important for the sequel analysis. In the special case, it is easy to see that choice
\alpha(\rho)=e^{\rho}-1
,
\beta(\theta)=2+\cos(4\theta)
, and
\omega(\theta)=2+\frac{1}{6}\frac{\sin(3\theta)}{\sin{\theta}}+\frac{1}{10}\frac{\sin(5\theta)}{\sin\theta}
satisfies these three assumptions. Moreover, thanks to the assumption of
E
, we can find
\rho_{0}>0
and functions
\alpha,\beta,\omega
satisfying (i)–(iii) and the boundary
\partial E
is characterized by

\partial E=\{(x_{1}(\rho_{0},\theta),x_{2}(\rho_{0},\theta)):\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}.

Therefore, the small hole
E_{\varepsilon}
and its boundary is characterized by

E_{\varepsilon}=\{(\varepsilon x_{1}(\rho,\theta),\varepsilon x_{2}(\rho,\theta)):\;0\leq\rho<\rho_{0},\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}

and

\partial E_{\varepsilon}=\{(\varepsilon x_{1}(\rho_{0},\theta),\varepsilon x_{2}(\rho_{0},\theta)):\theta\in[0,2\pi)\}.

Define for
x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}
the function
f(x)=|x|^{2}
. Then,
f
is continuous thanks to the smoothness of
\partial E_{\varepsilon}
. Note that there always exist two points
x_{\min},x_{\max}\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}
such that
f(x_{\min})=\min_{x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}}f(x)
and
f(x_{\max})=\max_{x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}}f(x)
. It is immediate that

\pi|x_{\min}|^{2}\leq|E_{\varepsilon}|\leq\pi|x_{\max}|^{2}.

Since the continuous function
g(x)=f(x)-|E_{\varepsilon}|/\pi
satisfies
g(x_{\min})g(x_{\max})\leq 0
, there exists at least one point
x^{*}\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}
such that
g(x^{*})=0
, or equivalently,

(2.1)

\displaystyle|E_{\varepsilon}|=\pi|x^{*}|^{2}=\pi(M\varepsilon)^{2}\quad\text{ where }\quad M=\frac{|x^{*}|}{\varepsilon}=\left(\frac{|E|}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}.

We denote
\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}}:=\Omega\setminus\bar{E}_{\varepsilon}
and consider the following eigenvalue problem

(2.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u(x)=\lambda(\varepsilon)u(x)&x\in\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}},\\
u(x)=0&x\in\partial\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x)=0&x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

Here the symbol
\partial u/\partial\nu
is the directional derivative of
u
along the unit outward normal vector
\nu
to the domain
\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}}
, hence pointing the spectral problem mentioned above has a countable sequence of strictly positive eigenvalues, with each eigenvalue having a finite multiplicity
[
22
]
. These eigenvalues are conventionally arranged in ascending order, and if an eigenvalue has a multiplicity greater than one, it is repeated accordingly:

0<\mu_{1}(\varepsilon)<\mu_{2}(\varepsilon)\leq\dots\leq\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)\leq\dots\to\infty.

A crucial property is that as
\varepsilon
approaches zero, see e.g. Rauch & Taylor
[
21
]
, each spectral problem’s eigenvalue converges to the Dirichlet Laplacian’s corresponding eigenvalue in the original domain without a hole. More precisely,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)\to\mu_{i},\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,

where
\{\mu_{i}\}
are the eigenvalues of the following problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u(x)=\lambda u(x),&x\in\Omega\\
u(x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Let
K(x,y)
represent the Green function of the Laplacian in the domain
\Omega
, associated with the boundary condition mentioned in equation (
2.4
). Similarly,
K_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
denotes the Green function in the domain
\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}}
, associated with the boundary condition specified in equation (
2.2
).

Let
\varphi_{i}(x)
denote the eigenfunction of (
2.2
) corresponding to
\mu_{i}
, which is normalized in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. To indicate the asymptotic behaviour, we employ the notation
\mathcal{O}(\cdot)
, commonly known as the “big-
\mathcal{O}
” Landau notation. Specifically, for any sufficiently small positive
x
, the notation
f(x)=\mathcal{O}(x)
implies that
\lvert f(x)\rvert\leq Cx
, where
C
is a constant.

We will present our main result, which provides the convergence rate (
2.3
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\mu_{i}
is a simple eigenvalue. Then, as
\varepsilon
tends to zero, we have

\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)=\mu_{i}-(2\lvert\nabla\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})\rvert^{2}-\mu_{i}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})^{2})|E|\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|^{2})

where
\mu_{i}
is the corresponding eigenvalue of the unperturbed problem to
\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)
,
\tilde{w}
is the origin and
|E|
is the area of
E
.

Our result extends the analysis of eigenvalue perturbations to two dimensional domains, providing a distinct asymptotic expansion. While
[
9
]
Maz’ya’s three-dimensional result, which shows a leading order term scaling with
\varepsilon^{3}
, our findings indicate that in two dimensions, the leading order term scales with
\varepsilon^{2}
. Additionally, we prove a higher-order term
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|^{2})
for greater precision. This highlights the different behaviours of eigenvalue perturbations in various dimensional settings and underscores the importance of dimensionality in asymptotic analysis.

Remark 2.2
.

•

The asymptotic in Theorem
2.1
does not depend on the parameterization of
E
, but only on the area of
E
. This also implies that the asymptotic behaviour is invariant with respect to rotations and translations.

•

Our results and proofs are not applicable to eigenvalues with multiplicity higher than one. This case is for future investigation (see e.g.
[
1
]
).

Preliminary lemmas

For any periodic function
L(\theta)
of
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, its Fourier series is given by

\displaystyle L(\theta)=S_{0}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k}\cos k\theta+P_{k}\sin k\theta.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider the equation system described by:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta v_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\bar{E}_{\varepsilon},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=L(\theta)\quad\text{for }x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon},

where
L(\theta)
is a given smooth function on
\partial E_{\varepsilon}
. Then there exists at least one solution
v_{\varepsilon}
that satisfies the following condition:

(3.3)

\lvert v_{\varepsilon}(x)\rvert\leq C\varepsilon\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\lvert L(\theta)\rvert(1+\rho+|\log\varepsilon|),

where
C
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Now, consider the function:

v_{\varepsilon}(x)=A_{0}(\rho+\log\varepsilon)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-k)^{-1}e^{-k\rho}(A_{k}\cos k\theta+B_{k}\sin k\theta).

Thanks to the assumption (iii) of the curvilinear coordinates, we have

\Delta v_{\varepsilon}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}=\left[(\partial_{\rho}x_{1})^{2}+(\partial_{\rho}x_{2})^{2}\right]^{-1}(\partial_{\rho}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}v_{\varepsilon}).

Therefore, direct computations lead to
\Delta v_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\bar{E}_{\varepsilon}
.
Next, we consider the normal derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}
of
\partial E_{\varepsilon}
. We have

\displaystyle d\nu=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\rho}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\rho}\right)^{2}}d\rho

\displaystyle=\varepsilon|\alpha^{\prime}(\rho)|\sqrt{\beta(\theta)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\omega(\theta)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\,d\rho

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\tau(\rho,\theta)\,d\rho

where
\tau(\rho,\theta)=|\alpha^{\prime}(\rho)|\sqrt{\beta(\theta)^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+\omega(\theta)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\neq 0
due to the assumptions on
\alpha,\beta,\omega
.

Differentiating the function
v_{\varepsilon}(x)
along the exterior normal vector at
x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu}(x)|_{x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\nu}\right\rvert_{x\in\partial E_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial v_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\frac{1}{\varepsilon\tau(\rho,\theta)}\right\rvert_{\rho=\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)}\left(A_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-k\rho_{0}}(A_{k}\cos k\theta+B_{k}\sin k\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(S_{k}\sin k\theta+P_{k}\cos k\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(\theta),

which implies

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)S_{0},

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon e^{-k\rho_{0}}\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)S_{k},

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon e^{-k\rho_{0}}\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)P_{k},

for
k\geq 1
.
Thus, we rewrite the inequality as

(3.4)

\lvert v_{\varepsilon}(x)\rvert\leq\lvert S_{0}\rvert\varepsilon\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)(\rho+|\log\varepsilon|)+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(S_{k}^{2}+P_{k}^{2}\right)\right)^{1/2}\\
\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-k)^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)^{2}e^{-2k(\rho_{0}+\rho)}\right)^{1/2}.

By applying Schwarz’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-k)^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)^{2}e^{-2k(\rho_{0}+\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)^{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-4k(\rho_{0}+\rho)}\right)^{1/2}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\tau(\rho_{0},\theta)^{2}\varepsilon^{2}.

Combining equations (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), along with the inequality

S_{0}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(S_{k}^{2}+P_{k}^{2}\right)\leq C\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lvert L(\theta)\rvert^{2}d\theta\leq C^{\prime}\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\lvert L(\theta)\rvert^{2},

we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\lvert v_{\varepsilon}(x)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\lvert L(\theta)\rvert(1+\rho+|\log\varepsilon|).

Hence, the proof is complete.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}(x)d\sigma_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}g(x)^{2}d\sigma_{x}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}(x)\right\rvert^{2}d\sigma_{x}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle(2\pi\,\varepsilon M_{\max})\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|\max_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}\lvert N(x)\rvert\,\max_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}\lvert N(x)\rvert

From (

\int_{\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}}}\lvert\nabla g(x)\rvert^{2}dx\leq 2\pi\,\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|M_{\text{max}}\left(\max_{\partial E_{\varepsilon}}\lvert N(x)\rvert\right)^{2}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{E_{\varepsilon}}}\lvert\nabla g_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}\rvert^{2}dx

a.e.

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}v_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}\qquad x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|M_{\varepsilon}.

Consequently, we can conclude that the function

4.3
Convergence of eigenvalues

In the context of analyzing the convergence of eigenvalues, we introduce a new kernel
\tilde{h}(x,y)

(4.29)

\begin{split}\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=K(x,y)&+h(\varepsilon)\left\langle\nabla_{w}K(x,\tilde{w}),\nabla_{w}K(\tilde{w},y)\right\rangle\chi_{\varepsilon}(x)\chi_{\varepsilon}(y)\\

Here,

is a given kernel, and
\nabla_{w}K(x,\tilde{w})
and

represent the gradient and Hessian of

with respect to the variable

, respectively. The functions

and

are introduced as weight functions that depend on a small parameter
\varepsilon
, and

is a characteristic function defined as

if

and
\chi_{\varepsilon}(x)=0

Using the kernel
\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
, we define the operator

as:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}f)(x)=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{h}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)f(y)dy.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

(4.30)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon

Proof.

We begin by considering the operator
\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}
. Using the expression

\displaystyle\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}=(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}+\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon}),

we can estimate its norm as follows:

(4.31)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Next, we observe that in the
h(\varepsilon)
and
i(\varepsilon)
terms of (
4.29
), the product
(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\chi_{\varepsilon}
equals zero. Therefore, we have the estimate:

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lvert E_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{1/2}\|\mathbf{K}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which holds for any

. Consequently, we obtain the following inequalities:

(4.32)

\begin{split}&\|(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon\\
&\|(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon.\end{split}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\tilde{H}}_{\varepsilon}(1-\chi_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon.

We have completed the proof of the lemma by combining the estimates (

4.32

∎

Next, we aim to estimate

\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)^{-1}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)^{-1}-(\mu_{i}^{-1}-(\pi\mu_{i}^{-1}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})^{2}-2\pi\mu_{i}^{-2}\lvert\nabla\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})\rvert^{2})(\varepsilon M)^{2})\rvert\leq C\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}.

From this, we deduce the asymptotic expansion for
\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)
:

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)=\mu_{i}-(2\pi\lvert\nabla\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})\rvert^{2}-\pi\mu_{i}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})^{2})(\varepsilon M)^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}).

Equivalently,

\mu_{i}(\varepsilon)=\mu_{i}-(2\lvert\nabla\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})\rvert^{2}-\mu_{i}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{w})^{2})|E|\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}|\log\varepsilon|^{2})

Hence, we have completed the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

6
Conclusions

This paper’s main contribution is developing a framework to obtain asymptotic formulas for eigenvalues in domains with star-shaped holes, which generalise the spherical hole studied previously. Estimates and proofs are presented in Sections 3-5. The proposed framework can be applied in various scientific and engineering fields. For the future, the author proposes to construct asymptotic formulas for eigenvalues in domains with holes in compact sets with zero Lebesgue measure.

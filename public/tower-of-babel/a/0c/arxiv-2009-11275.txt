Random points are optimal for the approximation of Sobolev functions
David Krieg and Mathias Sonnleitner

Abstract
We show that independent and uniformly distributed sampling points are asymptotically as good as optimal sampling points for the approximation of functions from Sobolev spaces W p s ​ ( Ω ) W_{p}^{s}(\Omega) on bounded convex domains Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} in the L q L_{q} -norm if q < p q<p . More generally, we characterize the quality of arbitrary sampling point sets P ⊂ Ω P\subset\Omega via the L γ ​ ( Ω ) L_{\gamma}(\Omega) -norm of the distance function dist ⁡ ( ⋅ , P ) \dist(\cdot,P) , where γ = s ​ ( 1 / q − 1 / p ) − 1 \gamma=s(1/q-1/p)^{-1} if q < p q<p and γ = ∞ \gamma=\infty if q ≥ p q\geq p . This improves upon previous characterizations based on the covering radius of P P .

41A25, 41A63, 62D05, 65D15, 65D30

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain. We study the problem of approximating
a function
f
from the Sobolev space
W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)

in the
L_{q}(\Omega)
-norm based on function values
f(x_{j})

on a finite set of sampling points
P=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
This makes sense if
s>d/p
,
in which case
W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded
into the space of continuous functions
C(\Omega)
.
The minimal worst-case error that can be achieved with
the given sampling points
is the number

e\big(P,W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big):=\,\inf_{S_{P}}\,\sup_{\|f\|_{W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\|f-S_{P}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},

(1)

where the infimum is taken over all sampling operators of the form

S_{P}\colon W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\to L_{q}(\Omega),\qquad S_{P}(f)=\varphi\big(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n})\big).

(2)

In general, we admit arbitrary mappings
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to L_{q}(\Omega)
,
but sometimes it may be preferable to allow only linear mappings
\varphi
,
in which case we write
e^{\rm lin}
instead of
e
.
We also study the related problem
of numerical integration on
\Omega
. To be precise, we consider

e\big(P,{\rm INT},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\big):=\inf_{S_{P}}\sup_{\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\Omega}f(x){\rm d}x-S_{P}(f)\right|,

(3)

where the infimum is now taken over all sampling operators of the form

S_{P}\colon W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad S_{P}(f)=\varphi\big(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n})\big).

(4)

In this case, it does not matter whether we allow arbitrary
or only linear mappings
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

since the infimum
e\big(P,{\rm INT},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\big)
will be the same.
This is a classical result due to Smolyak and Bakhvalov,
see e.g. Theorem 4.7 in
[
28
]
.

There is a vast literature on the error of optimal sampling points.
For example, it is known that the rate of convergence of this error is

e\big(n,W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big):=\inf_{|P|\leq n}e\big(P,W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,n^{-s/d+(1/p-1/q)_{+}}

for the approximation problem and

e\big(n,{\rm INT},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\big):=\inf_{|P|\leq n}e\big(P,{\rm INT},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,n^{-s/d}

for the integration problem, where
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
and the infimum runs over all point sets
P\subset\Omega
with at most
n
points.
The same holds for the error
e^{\rm lin}

of linear algorithms.
These are classical results for special domains like the cube,
see e.g.
[
6
, Chapter 3]
and
[
16
, Chapter 6]
. For general domains, we refer to Narcowich, Wendland and Ward
[
25
]
as well as Novak and Triebel
[
26
]
.

In this paper, however, we are not so much interested
in optimal sampling points. Although the question is quite interesting,
we feel that there are many applications where it is unrealistic
to assume that we can choose the sampling points at our convenience.
It might rather be realistic to assume that the
points are independent random vectors
which are uniformly distributed on the domain.
That is, we get our data
f(x_{j})
for random parameters
x_{j}\in\Omega

which are not under our control. This is also a typical assumption in learning theory and uncertainty quantification. But is this a major drawback? Are random points significantly worse than optimal points?

It can be understood from
[
25
,
26
]

that the optimal order of convergence may be
achieved with any set of sampling points that cover the domain well enough, meaning that the volume of the largest
empty ball amidst the point set is of order
1/n
.
In other words, the covering radius,
which is the supremum of the distance function

\dist(\cdot,P)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty),\qquad\dist(x,P):=\min_{y\in P}\|x-y\|_{2}

over
x\in\Omega
, i.e., the radius of the largest empty ball,
has to be of order
n^{-1/d}
.
In this case,
an optimal algorithm is given by moving least squares, see e.g. Wendland
[
39
]
.
In fact, it is quite common to use the covering radius, which is also called mesh norm, to bound errors of sampling-based algorithms,
see also
[
2
,
4
,
11
,
14
,
24
,
40
]
for example.
Unfortunately, random point sets do not have optimal covering properties since the volume of the largest empty ball is of order
\log(n)/n
.
On the other hand,
most
empty balls are of order
1/n

and one might think that a few larger gaps do not matter
if the error is measured in the
L_{q}
-norm for sufficiently small
q
.
We use a local version of Wendland’s result to
show that this is indeed true for any
q<p
.
We obtain the following characterization
of the error of
L_{q}
-approximation and numerical integration.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain,

1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
.
Then we have for any nonempty and finite point set
P\subset\Omega
the equivalences

\displaystyle e^{\ast}\big(P,W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,\big\|\dist(\cdot,P)\big\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}

(
a
)

\displaystyle e\big(P,{\rm INT},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,e^{\ast}\big(P,W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{1}(\Omega)\big),

(
b
)

where
e^{\ast}\in\{e,e^{\rm lin}\}
, the implied constants are independent of
P
, and

\gamma=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s(1/q-1/p)^{-1},&q<p,\\
\infty,&q\geq p,\end{array}\right.\qquad\alpha=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s,&q<p,\\
s-d(1/p-1/q),&q\geq p.\end{array}\right.

This shows that the covering radius is not the right quantity
to characterize the error in the case
q<p
,
and in particular, for numerical integration.
In general, the assumption
of a small covering radius is unnecessarily strong.
Instead, we find that a sequence of point sets is asymptotically optimal
if and only if the
\gamma
-norm of the distance function
decays with the optimal rate
n^{-1/d}
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain,

1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
.
Assume that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
an
n
-point set
P_{n}\subset\Omega
is given.
These point sets are asymptotically optimal, i.e.,

e^{\ast}\big(n,W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,e^{\ast}\big(P_{n},W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big),

if and only if

\|\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}\,\preccurlyeq\,n^{-1/d},

with
\gamma
as in Theorem
1
,

e^{\ast}\in\{e,e^{\rm lin}\}

and implied constants independent of
n
.

We emphasize that the main contribution of this paper is the characterization in the case
q<p
. The case
q\geq p
is included for completeness. The results for
q<p
seem to be novel already for
d=1
.
To the authors, similar results only have been known
for the spaces
W_{\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
with
s\leq 2
,
see Sukharev
[
34
]
and Pagès
[
30
]
.

By Theorem
1
(b), we clearly get the same characterization for numerical integration
as for the problem of
L_{1}
-approximation.
In a certain sense, this characterization may serve as an asymptotic (and weighted) analogue
in (isotropic) Sobolev spaces to the connection between errors of quasi-Monte Carlo rules in several other function spaces
and various types of the geometric notion of discrepancy
as surveyed for example in
[
29
, Section 9]
.

Remark 1

(More general domains)
.

In fact, the proof of Theorem
1
for
q\geq p
works
for all domains that satisfy
an interior cone condition (as considered in
[
25
]
) and which admit a bounded linear extension operator

{\rm ext}\colon W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

{\rm ext}(f)|_{\Omega}=f
for all
f\in W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
.
This includes all bounded Lipschitz domains (as considered in
[
26
]
),
see Lemmas
5
and
8
.
Our proof for
q<p
additionally requires
a strong local interior cone condition as described in Lemma
6
,
which is implied by the assumed convexity of the domain.
However, we conjecture that also the case
q<p

may be extended (at least) to all bounded Lipschitz domains.

Remark 2

(More general spaces)
.

Theorem
1

may also be extended to more general function spaces,
including Sobolev-Slobodeckij spaces of fractional smoothness,
Triebel-Lizorkin spaces,
Hölder-Zygmund spaces and Bessel potential spaces,
see Section
4
.

Remark 3

(Sobolev bounds on functions with scattered zeros)
.

Theorem
1
also gives the following bound:
If
f\in W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
satisfies
f|_{P}=0
, then

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,c\,\big\|\dist(\cdot,P)\big\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}\,\|f\|_{W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)},

where
\gamma=\gamma(s,p,q)
and
\alpha=\alpha(s,p,q,d)
are as in Theorem 1
and
c
is a constant independent of
f
and
P
.
This improves upon the corresponding result in
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
for
q<p

in the sense that the covering radius is replaced by the
\gamma
-norm of the distance function.
The paper
[
25
]
also gives upper bounds for
the Sobolev norms
\|f\|_{W_{q}^{r}(\Omega)}
with
r<s-d/p
.
It would be interesting to see whether one can perform the same replacement in these bounds.

Remark 4

(Quantization)
.

The quantity
\|\dist(\cdot,P)\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}
is studied extensively in the theory of quantization of measures, where it is known as quantization error or distortion. Asymptotics for the infimum over all
n
-point sets are known and the characterization in Corollary
1
is similar to the concept of asymptotically optimal quantizers. For more information we refer to Graf and Luschgy
[
15
]
.

Our result enables us to study the quality of random sampling points which have almost optimal covering properties. In fact, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be independent and uniformly distributed random vectors on a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
0<\alpha<\infty
. Consider the random
n
-point set
P_{n}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\,\|\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}\,\asymp\,\begin{cases}n^{-\alpha/d}&\text{if }0<\gamma<\infty,\\
n^{-\alpha/d}(\log n)^{\alpha/d}&\text{if }\gamma=\infty.\end{cases}

This immediately yields the following result on the quality of random sampling points.

Corollary 2
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be independent and uniformly distributed on a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
.
Consider the random
n
-point set
P_{n}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\,e^{\ast}\big(P_{n},W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,\begin{cases}e^{\ast}\big(n/\log n,W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)&\text{if }q\geq p,\\
e^{\ast}\big(n,W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)&\text{if }q<p.\end{cases}

This means that, in expectation, random points are asymptotically optimal
for
L_{q}
-approximation on
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)

whenever
q<p
.
In particular, random points are optimal for
integration on
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)

for all
p>1
.
For an almost sure result, see Corollary
3
.

All the above upper bounds
are achieved by a linear algorithm
S_{P}\colon W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\to L_{q}(\Omega)

which only depends on the domain
\Omega
, the point set
P\subset\Omega
,
and a smoothness threshold
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
It satisfies the bounds from Theorem
1
for all
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
as well as all
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\leq m
and can be outlined as follows.

Algorithm.
Let
\Omega
as in Theorem
1
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

a)

Given a finite and nonempty point set
P\subset\Omega
construct a covering of
\Omega
by good cubes
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{N}
according to Proposition
2
.

b)

Given the data
\big(x,f(x)\big)_{x\in P}
for some unknown
f\in W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
and some
y\in\Omega\setminus P
, select an index
1\leq i\leq N
with
y\in Q_{i}
and compute
S_{P}f(y)
using the moving least squares method from
[
39
, Theorem 4.7]
for
\overline{\Omega\cap Q_{i}}
,
P\cap Q_{i}
and
m
.

In this paper, we do not discuss the implementation and the computational cost of these two steps.
Details on the second step may be found in
[
39
]
.
In the paper
[
20
]
, we propose a variant of this algorithm
where the computation of
S_{P}f(x)
at a certain point of interest
x\in\Omega

does not require to compute the hole covering from the first step.
We refer to Section
2.4
for a more detailed description of optimal algorithms in the Hilbert space case via a reproducing kernel.

For
q\geq p
, random points are not optimal.
We note, however, that we only lose a logarithmic factor
and that random information is still almost optimal. For the cube,
this was already observed in
[
17
]
.
There, in Section 2.3, the case
q<p
was stated as an open problem, which is resolved by Corollary
2
.
Further recent results on the general question of the quality of random
information may be found in
[
18
,
19
,
22
,
37
]
.
We point to the fact that there are also situations
where random information is much worse than
optimal information, see
[
19
, Section 6]
for an example.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
1
we introduce notation
and present some basic facts on domains, polynomial reproduction

Section

is dedicated to our geometric characterization

.
We also discuss algorithms for numerical integration

In Section

Finally, in Section

In particular, we encourage readers to
(a) examine the

-dependence of the constants in Theorem

,
(b) examine and improve upon the computational cost of our algorithm,
(c) derive similar bounds for existing approximation algorithms,

Note that, while this paper was under revision,
we already extended our results to compact Riemannian manifolds in
[

]
.

Preliminaries

Let us first fix some basic notation.
For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<p\leq\infty
, we write

\|x\|_{p}:=\begin{cases}\big(|x_{1}|^{p}+\cdots+|x_{d}|^{p}\big)^{1/p},&\text{if }0<p<\infty,\\
\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|,&\text{if }p=\infty\end{cases}

for the
\ell^{p}
-(quasi-)norm of

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
We write
B_{d}^{p}(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x-y\|_{p}<r\}
for
the
\ell^{p}
-ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x
.
If
c>0
, we write
cB_{d}^{p}(x,r)
for the concentric
\ell^{p}
-ball
B_{d}^{p}(x,cr)
.
If
p=2
, we often omit the
p
in these notations.
We write
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
for the unit sphere, which is the
boundary of
B_{d}^{2}(0,1)
.
We denote the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue-measure by
\vol_{d}
and frequently omit the dimension
d
.

We assume that all random vectors are defined on a common probability space
(S,\Sigma,\mathbb{P})
.
For a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite and positive volume,
an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variable
X
will be called a uniformly distributed point in
\Omega

if
\mathbb{P}\,[X\in A]=\vol(A\cap\Omega)/\vol(\Omega)
for all
Lebesgue-measurable
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The space of all continuous functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
on an open set

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
C(\Omega)
.
We write
f|_{B}
for the restriction of
f\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
to
B\subset A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The space of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

of degree at most
m\in\mathbb{N}
is written
\mathcal{P}_{m}^{d}
.
If
0<p<\infty
, the function space
L_{p}(\Omega)
is the collection of all
(equivalence classes of) Lebesgue-measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

with finite (quasi-)norm
\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}:=\big(\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p}{\rm d}x\big)^{1/p}
.
The space
L_{\infty}(\Omega)
is the space of all essentially bounded functions
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}:={\rm{esssup}}_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|
.
We use the convention that
a/0=\infty
and
a/\infty=0

as well as
\infty/a=\infty
for all
a\in(0,\infty)
.

For two nonnegative functions
a
and
b

defined on the same set,
we will write
a\preccurlyeq b
whenever
a\leq cb
holds for a third function
c

that only depends on a specific list of parameters.
Unless specified otherwise,
this list consists of the domain
\Omega
,
the dimension

, the smoothness parameter

,
and the integrability parameters
p,\tau,q,\gamma,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\,{>0}
.

1.1
Domains and discrete sets

In the following, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain (i.e., open and nonempty)
and let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be finite and nonempty. We are interested in the
L_{\gamma}(\Omega)
-norm of the distance function

\dist(\cdot,P)\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty),\qquad\dist(x,P):=\min_{y\in P}\|x-y\|_{2}

for
0<\gamma\leq\infty
. In particular, for
\gamma=\infty
, we obtain the covering radius

h_{P,\Omega}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}\dist(x,P)=\|\dist(\cdot,P)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

of
P
with respect to
\Omega
.
This is the minimal radius such that the (closures of the) balls
B(x,h_{P,\Omega}),
where
x\in P,
cover
\Omega
.
Clearly, the finite volume of
\Omega
gives
\|\dist(\cdot,P)\|_{L_{\gamma_{1}}(\Omega)}\preccurlyeq\|\dist(\cdot,P)\|_{L_{\gamma_{2}}(\Omega)}
,
whenever
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\infty
. Moreover, it is well known that we have

\inf_{|P|\leq n}\|\dist(\cdot,P)\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}\,\asymp\,n^{-1/d}\qquad\text{for every}\quad 0<\gamma\leq\infty.

(5)

The upper bound is quite obvious since the bounded set
\Omega
may be covered
by
n
balls of radius
\asymp n^{-1/d}
.
The lower bound is due to a standard volume argument:
We choose the constant
c
such that the union of the
n
balls
with center in
P
and radius
cn^{-1/d}
has volume at most
\vol(\Omega)/2
,
which means that
\dist(\cdot,P)\geq cn^{-1/d}
on a subset of
\Omega

with volume at least
\vol(\Omega)/2
.

A special Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
,
is the collection of all points
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
such that

h(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<\infty,

where
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is some Lipschitz function, i.e.,
there exists a constant
C>0
with
|h(x^{\prime})-h(y^{\prime})|\leq C\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|_{2}
for all
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
The set
\Omega
is called a bounded Lipschitz domain (cf.
[
26
]
) if there are points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\partial\Omega
on the boundary and
radii
r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}>0
such that
\partial\Omega
is covered by the balls

B(x_{1},r_{1}),\ldots,B(x_{N},r_{N})
and

B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap\Omega=B(x_{i},r_{i})\cap\Omega_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where
\Omega_{i}
is a suitable rotation of a special Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A (closed) cone with apex
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, direction
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, height (or radius)
r>0
and opening angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
is given by

C(x,\xi,r,\theta):=\left\{x+\lambda y\colon y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\langle y,\xi\rangle\geq\cos\theta,\lambda\in[0,r]\right\}.

We will need Lemma 3.7 from
[
39
]
in the following special case.

Lemma 1
.

Every cone
C(x,\xi,r,\theta)
contains a ball of radius
c_{\theta}r
with
c_{\theta}:=\frac{\sin\theta}{1+\sin\theta}
.

A general set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to satisfy
an interior cone condition (cf.
[
39
]
) if there are
r>0
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
such that for all
x\in\Omega
there is a unit vector
\xi(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that the cone

C(x,\xi(x),r,\theta)
is contained in
\Omega
. The following simple observations will be useful.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy an interior cone condition with parameters
r
and
\theta
. If
B(x,\varrho)
is a ball with center
x\in\Omega
and radius
0<\varrho\leq r
, there is a ball
B(y,c_{\theta}\varrho)
contained in
B(x,\varrho)\cap\Omega
with
c_{\theta}
as in Lemma
1
.

Proof.

By the cone condition, there is a cone with apex
x
, height
\varrho
and angle
\theta

such that its interior is contained in
B(x,\varrho)\cap\Omega
. Now Lemma
1
completes the proof.
∎

Convex domains satisfy an interior cone condition, see e.g.
[
39
, Proposition 11.26]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain containing a ball of radius
r
. Then the closure of
\Omega
, denoted
\overline{\Omega}
, satisfies an interior cone condition with radius
r
and angle
\theta=2\arcsin\bigl(r/2\diam(\Omega)\bigr)
.

Convex domains are also Lipschitz domains. A similar result may be found in Dekel and Leviatan
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 4
.

Every bounded convex domain is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Proof.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded convex domain,
we find a ball
B(x_{0},r)\subset\Omega
. As
\partial\Omega
is compact,
there are points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
such that the balls

B(x_{i},r/2)
,
i\leq N
, cover
\partial\Omega
.
For all
i\leq N
we show that

B(x_{i},r/2)\cap\Omega=B(x_{i},r/2)\cap\Omega_{i},

(6)

where
\Omega_{i}
is a rotation of a special Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Applying a suitable rotation (and translation),
we may assume that
x_{0}=0

and that
x_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,a)
for some
a\leq-r
. Consider the open ball
B^{\prime}=B_{d-1}(0,r)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
For
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, we define the set
A(x^{\prime})=\{x_{d}\in\mathbb{R}\colon(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega\}
.
Since
\Omega
is convex and open,
A(x^{\prime})
is an open interval.
Moreover,
A(x^{\prime})
is nonempty since
0\in A(x^{\prime})
.
We define
h(x^{\prime})
to be the infimum of
A(x^{\prime})
.
The convexity of
\Omega
implies that the function
h\colon B^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex.
Since every convex function on a convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
is Lipschitz
on every compact subset of the domain, see
[
38
]
,
the function
h
is Lipschitz on the closure of the ball
B^{*}=B_{d-1}(0,r/2)
.
This Lipschitz continuity carries over to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}

if we set
h(\lambda x^{\prime})=h(x^{\prime})
for all
x^{\prime}\in\partial B^{*}
and
\lambda\geq 1

(thereby redefining
h
on
B^{\prime}\setminus B^{*}
).
It remains to note that for every
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in B(x_{i},r/2)
it holds that

x\in\Omega\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x_{d}\in A(x^{\prime})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x_{d}>h(x^{\prime}),

proving (
6
) for the special Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{i}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\colon x_{d}>h(x^{\prime})\}
.
∎

It seems to be well known that bounded Lipschitz domains
satisfy an interior cone condition.
This is used in
[
26
]
and also stated by Adams and Fournier
[
1
, 4.11]
for domains with the strong local Lipschitz condition, which include bounded Lipschitz domains.
Since we did not find it elsewhere, we include the proof.

Lemma 5
.

Every bounded Lipschitz domain satisfies an interior cone condition.

Proof.

We first show that special Lipschitz domains satisfy an interior cone condition.
Let therefore

\Omega_{0}=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon x_{d}>h(x^{\prime})\right\}

where
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function and
d\geq 2
. We set

L=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{\|x-y\|},\qquad\theta=\arctan L^{-1},\qquad\xi=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
\theta=\pi/2
if
L=0
.
Let
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega_{0}
and
z=(z^{\prime},z_{d})\in C(x,\xi,\infty,\theta)
.
Then we have
z=x+\lambda y
for some
\lambda\geq 0
and
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

with
y_{d}\geq\cos\theta
, and therefore,

z_{d}=x_{d}+\lambda y_{d}>h(x^{\prime})+\lambda\cos\theta\geq h(z^{\prime})-L\|z^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|+\lambda\cos\theta\\
=h(z^{\prime})-L\lambda\sqrt{1-y_{d}^{2}}+\lambda\cos\theta\geq h(z^{\prime})+\lambda(\cos\theta-L\sin\theta)=h(z^{\prime}).

We thus obtain that
C(x,\xi,\infty,\theta)\subset\Omega_{0}
for all
x\in\Omega_{0}
.

Let now
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Choose points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in\partial\Omega
and radii
r_{1},\ldots,r_{N}>0
such that

\partial\Omega
is covered by the balls

B_{i}=B(x_{i},r_{i})
and
B_{i}\cap\Omega=B_{i}\cap\Omega_{i}
, where
i\leq N
and
\Omega_{i}
is a rotation of a special Lipschitz domain.
Clearly, the interior cone condition is preserved under rotations,
such that the
\Omega_{i}
satisfy an interior cone condition
with angle
\theta_{i}
(and infinite height).
First, we note that there is some
a\in(0,1)
such that
the balls
aB_{i}
still cover
\partial\Omega
:
Else, using the compactness of
\partial\Omega
, we would
obtain a convergent sequence
(y_{k})
in
\partial\Omega
with

\|y_{k}-x_{i}\|\geq(1-1/k)r_{i}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\leq N
.
Its limit
y\in\partial\Omega
would satisfy
\|y-x_{i}\|\geq r_{i}

and therefore
y\not\in B_{i}
, a contradiction.

Let thus
b=(a+1)/2
,

r=\min_{i}r_{i}
and
\theta=\min_{i}\theta_{i}
, and let
x\in\Omega
.
If
x\in bB_{i}
for some
i\leq N
, then there is a cone with apex
x
, height
(1-b)r=(1-a)r/2

and angle
\theta
which is contained in both
B_{i}
and
\Omega_{i}

and therefore in
\Omega
.
On the other hand, if
x\not\in bB_{i}
for all
i\leq N
,
then the whole ball with center
x
and radius
(b-a)r=(1-a)r/2

is contained in
\Omega
: Else, there would be some
y\in\partial\Omega

with
\|y-x\|\leq(b-a)r
and since
y\in aB_{i}
for some
i\leq N

also
\|x_{i}-x\|<ar_{i}+(b-a)r\leq br_{i}
, which is a contradiction.
Thus
\Omega
satisfies an interior cone condition with angle
\theta

and height
(1-a)r/2
.
∎

A convex domain also satisfies the following local version of the cone condition.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain containing a ball of radius
r>0
. Then the closure of the intersection of
\Omega
with any cube
B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)
centered at
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and having
\ell^{\infty}
-radius
0<\varrho\leq r
satisfies an interior cone condition with height
c_{\theta}\varrho
and angle
\theta^{\prime}=2\arcsin(c_{\theta}/4\sqrt{d})
, where
c_{\theta}
is as in Lemma
1
and
\theta
is as in Lemma
3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, the set
\overline{\Omega}
contains a cone with height
\varrho\leq r
,
apex
x
and angle
\theta
.
Clearly, this cone is contained in
\overline{\Omega\cap B(x,\varrho)}
,
which is a subset of
A(x,\varrho):=\overline{\Omega\cap B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)}
.
By Lemma
1
, there is a ball
B(y,c_{\theta}\varrho)
in this cone and thus in
A(x,\varrho)
.
The proof is finished if we apply Lemma
3

to the convex set
A(x,\varrho)
since its diameter is at most
2\varrho\sqrt{d}
.
∎

One of our main tools will be the following result due to Wendland,
which we state (almost) in the formulation of
Theorem 4.7 in his book
[
39
]
.
It will provide us with a well-behaved linear algorithm.

Lemma 7

(
[
39
]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set satisfying an interior cone condition with parameters
r
and
\theta
, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There
are constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending solely on
\theta,m
and
d

such that for any finite nonempty
P\subset K
with covering radius

h_{P,K}\leq c_{1}r
there exist continuous
functions
u_{x}\colon K\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\in P,
with

(i)

\displaystyle\pi(y)=\sum_{x\in P}\pi(x)u_{x}(y)
for all
y\in K

and
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{d}
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in P}|u_{x}(y)|\leq c_{0}
for all
y\in K
and

(iii)

u_{x}(y)=0
for all
y\in K
and
x\in P
with
\|x-y\|\geq c_{2}h_{P,K}
.

Proof.

To derive this statement from
[
39
, Theorem 4.7]
,
we will show that there is a subset
X
of
P
with
h_{X,K}\leq 2h_{P,K}

and separation distance

q_{X}:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{x,y\in X,x\neq y}\|x-y\|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}h_{P,K}

and apply Theorem 4.7 to the subset
X
(setting
u_{x}=0
for all
x\in P\setminus X
).
To this end, we choose an arbitrary
x_{1}\in P
and recursively choose
x_{i}\in P\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}B(x_{j},h_{P,K})
until the latter set is empty. We obtain a subset
X
of
P
which clearly satisfies
q_{X}\geq\frac{1}{2}h_{P,K}
. Moreover, for any
y\in K
, there is some
x\in P
with
\|y-x\|_{2}\leq h_{P,K}
. Since
P\setminus\bigcup_{\tilde{x}\in X}B(\tilde{x},h_{P,K})=\emptyset
,
there is some
\tilde{x}\in X
with
\|x-\tilde{x}\|_{2}\leq h_{P,K}
. The triangle inequality gives
\|y-\tilde{x}\|_{2}\leq 2h_{P,K}
and therefore
h_{X,K}\leq 2h_{P,K}
.
∎

1.2
Sobolev spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a domain.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
we consider the Sobolev space

W^{s}_{p}(\Omega):=\big\{f\in L_{p}(\Omega)\colon\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}:=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}<\infty\big\},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is a multiindex,
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{d}

and
D^{\alpha}f=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}f
denotes a weak partial derivative of order
|\alpha|
.
This is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}
.
In addition to the norm,
we will also work with the semi-norm

|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}:=\Big(\sum_{|\alpha|=s}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

which has better scaling properties. If
\Omega
is sufficiently regular, functions from the Sobolev space
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
may be
extended to
W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see Stein
[
33
, Section VI.3]
.
Note that Stein uses the notion of minimally smooth domains which entails bounded Lipschitz domains.

Lemma 8

(
[
33
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,

1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
.
Then there is a bounded linear operator

{\rm ext}\colon W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

{\rm ext}(f)|_{\Omega}=f
for all
f\in W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
.

We will also use the following result about optimal polynomial approximation on cubes.
It easily follows from the Bramble-Hilbert lemma or generalized Poincaré inequality by scaling, see
[
5
, Lemma 4.3.8]
or
[
23
, Lemma 1.1.11]
.

Lemma 9
.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
,
there is a constant
c_{\ast}>0

such that the following holds.
For any
0<\varrho\leq 1
, any

\ell^{\infty}
-ball
Q
of radius
\varrho

and any
f\in W^{s}_{p}(Q)
, there is a polynomial
\pi
of degree at most
s

such that

\sup_{x\in Q}|(f-\pi)(x)|\leq c_{\ast}\,\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q)}.

Proof.

We use an affine map
T
from
Q
to
(-1,1)^{d}
,
on which we apply the continuous embedding of
W^{s}_{p}\bigl((-1,1)^{d}\bigr)
into
C\bigl((-1,1)^{d}\bigr)

and the Bramble-Hilbert lemma
to find constants
a,b>0

and a polynomial
\pi
of degree at most
s
with

\sup_{y\in(-1,1)^{d}}\left|f\circ T^{-1}(y)-\pi(y)\right|\,\leq\,a\,\|f\circ T^{-1}-\pi\|_{W^{s}_{p}((-1,1)^{d})}\,\leq\,a\,b\,|f\circ T^{-1}|_{W^{s}_{p}((-1,1)^{d})}.

A change of variables gives

\sup_{x\in Q}\left|f(x)-\pi(Tx)\right|\,=\,\sup_{y\in(-1,1)^{d}}\left|f\circ T^{-1}(y)-\pi(y)\right|

and

|f\circ T^{-1}|_{W^{s}_{p}((-1,1)^{d})}\,=\,\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q)}.

∎

2
The geometric characterization

In this section, we give a proof of our main result, Theorem
1
. For the proof, let us fix
\Omega
and
p,q,s
as in the statement of Theorem
1
.
All constants in this section are allowed to depend on
these parameters.
Let
r\leq 1
and
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
be such that
\Omega
contains a ball of radius
r
and
\overline{\Omega}

satisfies an interior cone condition with radius
r
and angle
\theta
, see Lemma
3
.
In Section
2.1
, we first prove the characterization in the simpler case
q\geq p
,
where only the largest hole amidst the point set matters. The case
q<p
is more involved and will be treated in Section
2.2
. We then turn to numerical integration in Section
2.3
, discuss optimal algorithms for
p=2
in Section
2.4
and conclude with an example
in Section
2.5
.

2.1
The case
q\geq p

Let us start with the upper bound.
We basically extend the proof of
[
26
, Proposition 21]

to point sets with non-optimal covering radius.
To this end, let
m>s
be an integer and let
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
be as in Lemma
7
.
We can assume that
h_{P,\Omega}\leq c_{1}r
as the upper bound is trivial if this does not hold.
We consider the linear algorithm

S_{P}\colon W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\to L_{q}(\Omega),\quad S_{P}(f)=\sum_{x\in P}f(x)u_{x},

with
u_{x}
provided by Lemma
7
for the compact set
\overline{\Omega}
.
The balls
B(y,h_{P,\Omega})
,
y\in\Omega
,
cover the set
\overline{\Omega}
.
By compactness and Vitali’s Lemma,
there is a finite selection of pairwise disjoint balls

B_{i}=B(y_{i},h_{P,\Omega})
with
y_{i}\in\Omega

and
i\leq N

such that the balls
3B_{i}=B(y_{i},3h_{P,\Omega})
still cover
\overline{\Omega}
.

Let
f\in W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
with
\|f\|_{W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.
By Lemma
8
,
we may assume that
f\in W_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|f\|_{W_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq c_{3}
.
Let
Q_{i}
be the
\ell^{\infty}
-ball with center
y_{i}
and radius
(3+c_{2})h_{P,\Omega}
.
By Lemma
9
, there are polynomials
\pi_{i}

of degree at most
s
such that

\sup_{y\in Q_{i}}\big|(f-\pi_{i})(y)\big|\leq c_{4}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{s-d/p}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})}.

For each
y\in\Omega_{i}:=3B_{i}\cap\Omega
, we note that
u_{x}(y)=0
for
x\not\in Q_{i}
, and obtain

\displaystyle\big|(f-S_{P}f)(y)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|(f-\pi_{i})(y)-S_{P}(f-\pi_{i})(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|(f-\pi_{i})(y)\big|+\Big|\sum_{x\in P}(f-\pi_{i})(x)u_{x}(y)\Big|\leq c_{5}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{s-d/p}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})},

(7)

where
c_{5}=(1+c_{0})c_{4}
.
In particular, this yields

\|f-S_{P}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c_{5}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{s-d/p}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which proves the case
q=\infty
.
For
p\leq q<\infty
, we use
\Omega_{i}\subset Q_{i}
and (
7
) to get

\|f-S_{P}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\big|\big(f-S_{P}(f)\big)(y)\big|^{q}{\rm d}y\leq c_{5}^{q}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{sq-dq/p}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})}^{q}\vol(\Omega_{i})\\
\leq c_{6}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{sq-dq/p+d}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})}^{q}\leq c_{6}\,h_{P,\Omega}^{sq-dq/p+d}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})}^{p}\Big)^{q/p}.

Note that, independently of
P
, every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in at most
c_{7}\in\mathbb{N}
of the
N
cubes
Q_{i}
since
the balls
B_{i}
are disjoint and all have the same radius.
Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(Q_{i})}^{p}=\sum_{|\alpha|=s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|D^{\alpha}f(x)|^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}}(x)\Big){\rm d}x\leq c_{7}\,|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\leq c_{7}c_{3}^{p}

(8)

and we arrive at the desired inequality.

For the lower bound we use the well-known technique of
fooling functions
:
We construct a function
f_{\ast}
from the unit ball of
W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)

which vanishes on
P
and has a large
L_{q}
-norm.
Since any algorithm of the form (
2
)
cannot distinguish
f_{\ast}
from
-f_{\ast}
, i.e., it satisfies
S_{P}(f_{*})=S_{P}(-f_{*})
,
we have

\begin{split}e\big(P,W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)&\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,=\,\inf_{S_{P}}\sup_{\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\|S_{P}(f)-f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,\inf_{u\in L_{q}(\Omega)}\,\max\left\{\|u-f_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)},\|u+f_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\right\}\geq\,\|f_{\ast}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(9)

In the case
q\geq p
it is enough to consider a function
f_{\ast}

which is supported in the largest hole, compare
[
17
, Theorem 11]

for
\Omega=[0,1]^{d}
. Let therefore
x_{0}\in\Omega
be such that

\dist(x_{0},P)\geq h:=\min\{r,\frac{1}{2}h_{P,\Omega}\}
.
Then the ball
B(x_{0},h)
does not contain any point of
P
. By Lemma
2
we find a ball
B(y,\varrho)
with
\varrho:=c_{\theta}h,
which is contained in
\Omega\cap B(x_{0},h)
.
We take a smooth non-negative function
\varphi
supported in
B(0,1)
with
\varphi(0)=1
and consider
f(x)=\varphi\big(\varrho^{-1}(x-y)\big)
.
One can easily compute the
scaling properties

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\asymp\varrho^{d/q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\preccurlyeq\varrho^{-s+d/p},

(10)

taking into account that
\varrho\leq 1
and
s>d/p
. This yields that
f_{*}:=\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{-1}f
satisfies

\|f_{*}\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\quad f_{*}|_{P}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\|f_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}\succcurlyeq h_{P,\Omega}^{s-d(1/p-1/q)},

which completes the proof.

\hfill\square

2.2
The case
q<p

We start with the upper bound,
using the following approximation property.
As this useful result is somewhat technical, let us paraphrase it with “Locally good point sets yield locally good approximations”.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain containing a ball of radius
r\in(0,1]
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
There is a constant
c\in(0,1)
, depending solely on
m
and
\Omega
such that for any cube
Q=B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
0<\varrho\leq r
centered at
x\in\overline{\Omega}

and any finite point set
P\subset\Omega
with

\sup_{y\in\Omega\cap Q}\dist(y,P)\leq c\varrho,

(11)

there are continuous functions
u_{x}\colon\Omega\cap Q\to\mathbb{R}
,
x\in P
, with the following property.
For any
s\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
\Omega,s
and
p
, such that, for all
f\in W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\sup_{y\in\Omega\cap Q}\Big|f(y)-\sum_{x\in P}f(x)u_{x}(y)\Big|\leq C\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6
, the set
\overline{\Omega\cap Q}
satisfies an interior cone condition
with radius
\varrho^{\prime}=c_{\theta}\varrho
and angle
\theta^{\prime}
for some
c_{\theta}>0
. Let
c_{0},c_{1}>0
be as in Lemma
7
for the parameters
\theta^{\prime}
and
m
.
Note that
c_{1}
can be chosen to be smaller than one.
We set
c=c_{\theta}c_{\theta^{\prime}}c_{1}/2
with
c_{\theta^{\prime}}
as in Lemma
1
.
Then assumption (
11
) implies that
every ball
B(y,2c\varrho)
,
y\in Q\cap\Omega
, contains a point of
P
.
By Lemma
2
every ball
B(x,2c_{\theta^{\prime}}^{-1}c\varrho),x\in Q\cap\Omega,
contains a ball
B(y,2c\varrho)\subset Q\cap\Omega

and therefore a point of
P\cap Q
.
We thus have

h_{P\cap Q,\Omega\cap Q}\leq 2c_{\theta^{\prime}}^{-1}c\varrho=c_{1}\varrho^{\prime}.

Thus, we may apply Lemma
7
to the point set
P\cap Q

within the compact set
\overline{\Omega\cap Q}
and obtain
continuous functions
u_{x}\colon\Omega\cap Q\to\mathbb{R}
for
x\in P
with

\sum_{x\in P}|u_{x}(y)|\leq c_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi(y)=\sum_{x\in P}\pi(x)u_{x}(y)

for all
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{d}
and
y\in\Omega\cap Q
, where we set
u_{x}=0
for
x\in P\setminus Q
.
For any
f\in W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
by Lemma
9
there is a polynomial
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}^{d}
with

\sup_{y\in Q}\big|(f-\pi)(y)\big|\,\leq\,c_{\ast}\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q)}.

Similar to Section
2.1
, we get for all
y\in\Omega\cap Q
that

\Big|f(y)-\sum_{x\in P}f(x)u_{x}(y)\Big|=\Big|(f-\pi)(y)-\sum_{x\in P}(f-\pi)(x)u_{x}(y)\Big|\\
\,\leq\,\big|(f-\pi)(y)\big|+c_{0}\,\max_{x\in P\cap Q}\big|(f-\pi)(x)\big|\,\leq\,(1+c_{0})c_{\ast}\,\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q)},

as it was to be proven.
∎

In the following, we say that a cube
Q

is a
good cube
if it satisfies the conditions of Lemma
10
. We now define good cubes
Q_{P}(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}

there is some
\varrho\in(0,r)

such that

\sup_{y\in\Omega\cap B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)}\dist(y,P)<c\varrho,

(12)

where
c
is the constant from Lemma
10
.
Otherwise,
\dist(\cdot,P)
is larger than a constant
on a set of constant volume and the upper bound
of Theorem
1
(a) becomes trivial.
We define
r_{P}(x)
to be the infimum
over all
\varrho\in(0,r)
satisfying (
12
)
and let

Q_{P}(x):=B_{d}^{\infty}\big(x,r_{P}(x)\big).

(13)

Our definition ensures that
Q_{P}(x)
is indeed a good cube. We will now show
that there is a covering of the domain by such cubes that is efficient in the sense that every point is
covered only by a constant number of cubes.
For this, we need the following observation.

Lemma 11
.

The function
r_{P}\colon\overline{\Omega}\to(0,\infty)
is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. We first show
r_{P}(x)>0
. If
\varrho>0
is small enough,
then
B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)
does not contain any point of
P
except possibly
x
.
Since
c<1
, relation (
12
) cannot be satisfied for these values of
\varrho
and thus

r_{P}(x)
is positive.
To show the semi-continuity, let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is some
\varrho\in[r_{P}(x),r_{P}(x)+\varepsilon]
such that (
12
) is satisfied. Clearly, we can choose
\delta>0
such that (
12
) is still satisfied if we replace the right hand side
by
ca
with
a=\varrho-\delta
.
For any
\tilde{x}\in\overline{\Omega}
with
\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{\infty}<\delta
,
the cube
B_{d}^{\infty}(\tilde{x},a)
is contained in
B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)
.
We thus have

\sup_{y\in B_{d}^{\infty}(\tilde{x},a)\cap\Omega}\dist(y,P)\leq\sup_{y\in B_{d}^{\infty}(x,\varrho)\cap\Omega}\dist(y,P)<ca,

which implies
r_{P}(\tilde{x})\leq a<r_{P}(x)+\varepsilon
.
∎

The semi-continuity is already enough to obtain the desired covering. Let us note that one can extract a suitable covering also from a Besicovitch-type covering result by de Guzman
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact and let
r\colon K\to(0,\infty)

be upper semi-continuous.
If
K
is nonempty, then there are points
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}\in K
such that

(i)

The cubes
Q_{i}=B_{d}^{\infty}\big(y_{i},r(y_{i})\big)
,
1\leq i\leq N
, cover
K
.

(ii)

The cubes
Q_{i}/2
are pairwise disjoint.

(iii)

Every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is contained in at most
2^{d}
of the cubes
Q_{i}
.

Proof.

By compactness of
K
,
we can choose
y_{1}
as a maximizer of
r_{P}
on
K
.
We set
r_{1}=r(y_{1})
and
Q_{1}=B_{d}^{\infty}\bigl(y_{1},r(y_{1})\bigr)
.
Recursively, we can choose
y_{i}
as a maximizer of
r_{P}

on
K\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}Q_{j}
(which is also compact)
and set
r_{i}=r(y_{i})
and
Q_{i}=B_{d}^{\infty}(y_{i},r_{i})
,
as long as the set difference is nonempty.
We first show that the sets
Q_{i}^{\prime}=B_{d}^{\infty}(y_{i},r_{i}/2)

are pairwise disjoint. Assume that
y\in Q_{i}^{\prime}\cap Q_{j}^{\prime}

for some
j<i
. Then we have

\|y_{j}-y_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\|y_{j}-y\|_{\infty}+\|y-y_{i}\|_{\infty}<r_{j}/2+r_{i}/2\leq r_{j},

since the numbers
r_{i}
are nonincreasing,
and thus
y_{i}\in Q_{j}
. A contradiction.

We now show that our procedure terminates after finitely many steps.
Assume for a contradiction that we obtain a whole sequence
(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Then we must have
r_{i}\to 0
, since otherwise we would have infinitely many
disjoint cubes
Q_{i}^{\prime}

with volume larger than a constant. There is a subsequence
(y_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

that converges to some
y_{0}\in K
. By assumption,
r_{0}=r(y_{0})>0
. Since
r_{i}\to 0
, there is some
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
r_{k}<r_{0}
.
As
r_{k}
is a maximum of
r
on
K\setminus\bigcup_{\ell<k}Q_{\ell}
,
the point
y_{0}
must be contained in
Q_{\ell}
for some
\ell<k
. Since
Q_{\ell}
is open and
y_{i_{k}}\to y_{0}
,
we get that
y_{i_{k}}\in Q_{\ell}
for all sufficiently large
k
. But by our construction, we have

y_{i}\not\in Q_{\ell}
for all
i>\ell
. A contradiction.
Thus, the procedure terminates and we obtain a finite point set

y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}
, satisfying
(i)
and
(ii)
.

To prove the efficiency of the covering,
let now
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
y\in Q_{i}\cap Q_{j}
for some
j<i
.
Assume that the signs of
y_{i}-y
and
y_{j}-y
are the same
(i.e.,
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
are in the same orthant with respect to
y
).
Then we have

\|y_{j}-y_{i}\|_{\infty}=\|(y_{j}-y)-(y_{i}-y)\|_{\infty}\leq\max\left\{\|y_{j}-y\|_{\infty},\|y_{i}-y\|_{\infty}\right\}<r_{j}

and thus
y_{i}\in Q_{j}
, a contradiction.
This means that the signs of all
y_{j}-y
for which
y\in Q_{j}

must be different – and there are at most
2^{d}
different signs.
∎

Lemma
11
and Lemma
12
immediately yield an efficient covering of
\overline{\Omega}
by good cubes.
It has the following properties.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain containing a ball of radius
r\in(0,1]
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any finite and nonempty point set

,
there are

such that the good cubes

and every

of these cubes.

contains a ball

of radius

and empty of

.

With Lemma
11
and Lemma
12

we immediately get an efficient covering by good cubes
Q_{i}

such that the cubes
Q_{i}/2
are disjoint.
By the minimality of

, the set

contains
a point

with

, where

is as in Lemma

.
By Lemma

.
In particular, the balls

∎

We are ready to complete the proof of the upper bound of Theorem

(a)
for

Proof.

Let
m\geq s
and
y_{i}
,
r_{i}
,

,

and

for

For

according to Lemma

applied to the cube

This yields bounded functions

and a linear algorithm

S_{P}\colon W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)\to L_{q}(\Omega),\quad S_{P}(f)=\sum_{x\in P}f(x)u_{x}.

(14)

Let

with

.
Using Lemma
8

\|f-S_{P}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega_{i})}^{q}\leq c_{4}r_{i}^{(s-d/p)q+d}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q_{i})}^{q},

\|f-S_{P}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\preccurlyeq\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{(\gamma+d)(1-q/p)}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q_{i})}^{q}\,\leq\,\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{\gamma+d}\bigg)^{1-q/p}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f|_{W^{s}_{p}(Q_{i})}^{p}\bigg)^{q/p}.

)
to obtain

,

.
∎

This shows that the algorithm from (

, and

for all

and

and

.

We briefly explain how the upper bound for

Using the notation from the proof of the case

Replacing (

We will now prove the lower bound of Theorem

(a) for

.
Again we construct a fooling function
with large

-norm that vanishes on the point set.
However, unlike in the case

has to be supported in multiple holes.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth non-negative function supported within

with
\varphi(0)=1
. We choose
y_{i}
,
r_{i}
,
Q_{i}
and

B_{i}=B(z_{i},d_{i})
for

as in Proposition

. For every

and

define

\varphi_{i}(x):=d_{i}^{s+\gamma/p}\varphi\big(d_{i}^{-1}(x-z_{i})\big).

With this,
\varphi_{i}
is supported in

. With a substitution one can see that

\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}=d_{i}^{\gamma+d}\|\varphi\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}

.
With another substitution we obtain for

\|\varphi_{i}\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\preccurlyeq d_{i}^{\gamma+d}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.

(15)

Let

Note that

Since the

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}^{\gamma+d}\|\varphi\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}.

(16)

For the same reason we obtain from (

\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\asymp\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\preccurlyeq\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}^{\gamma+d}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.

(17)

If

\|f\|_{W^{s}_{\infty}(\Omega)}\asymp\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{W^{s}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\preccurlyeq\|\varphi\|_{W^{s}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and carry on analogously. The estimates (

Proof.

2.4
Special algorithms in the Hilbert case

In particular, the bounds from Theorem
1
are valid for the optimal algorithm using the given data, i.e., the algorithm attaining the infimum in (
1
) or (
3
). For
p=2
,
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) and this algorithm can be described explicitly via the kernel. We refer to Aronszajn
[
3
]
for a definition and properties of RKHSs.

To any RKHS
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H})
on
\Omega
there corresponds a symmetric and positive definite mapping, a kernel,
K\colon\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that if
x\in\Omega
, we have
K(x,\cdot)\in H
and the reproducing property
f(x)=\langle f,K(x,\cdot)\rangle_{H}
holds for all
f\in H
. A theorem due to Riesz yields that every continuous linear functional
\ell\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
can be represented by a function
h_{\ell}\in H
. In fact, one has
h_{\ell}(x)=\langle h_{\ell},K(x,\cdot)\rangle_{H}=\ell(K(x,\cdot))
for every
x\in\Omega
.

To be more concrete, set
H=W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
, and denote the corresponding kernel by
K_{s}
. Because of the embedding
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\Omega)
the integration functional
\int\colon W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, and, by the above, it is represented by the function
h(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}K_{s}(x,\cdot)\,{\rm d}x\in W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
. For a point set
P=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subset\Omega
and a linear algorithm
S_{P}\colon f\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}f(x_{i})
with weights
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
a well-known expression for the worst-case error of
S_{P}
on
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
is available (see e.g. (9.31) in
[
29
]
).
It reads

\sup_{\|f\|_{W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\Bigl|\int_{\Omega}f(x){\rm d}x-S_{P}(f)\Bigr|^{2}=\langle w,\mathbf{K}w\rangle-2\langle w,b\rangle+c,

(20)

where the matrix
\mathbf{K}:=\bigl(K_{s}(x_{i},x_{j})\bigr)_{i,j=1}^{n}
is positive definite,
b:=\bigl(\int_{\Omega}K_{s}(x,x_{i}){\rm d}x\bigr)_{i=1}^{n}
, and
c:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}K_{s}(x,y){\rm d}x{\rm d}y
. Finding the optimal weights
w^{\ast}=(w_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,w_{n}^{\ast})
and thus the optimal (linear) algorithm given
P
is easy in this case. As a function of the weights, the expression (
20
) is convex and therefore optimal weights are given by the unique solution of
\mathbf{K}w^{\ast}=b
.
Therefore, the knowledge of the kernel
K_{s}
permits a computation of (an approximation of) the optimal (linear) algorithm for numerical integration in Theorem
1
.

For
L_{q}
-approximation,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, consider the algorithm

f\,\mapsto\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}w^{\ast}_{j}K_{s}(\cdot,x_{j}),\quad f\in W^{s}_{2}(\Omega),

where
w^{\ast}
is the unique solution of
\mathbf{K}w=\big(f(x_{i})\big)_{i=1}^{n}
. This is the interpolant with minimal
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
-norm and satifies the error bounds from Theorem
1
as it attains the infimum in (
1
). This is known and follows for example from Theorems 13.1 and 13.5 in
[
39
]
. Note that this kind of algorithm is used in radial basis function interpolation, see, e.g.
[
39
]
and the references therein.

For obtaining the kernel
K_{s}
, we equip the Sobolev space
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
with the equivalent norm
\|f\|^{\ast}_{W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)}:=\inf\big\{\|g\|_{W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\colon g|_{\Omega}=f\big\}
,
where the equivalence is due Lemma
8
.
Then, Theorem 5 in
[
3
]
yields that the reproducing kernel
K_{s}
of
W^{s}_{2}(\Omega)
is the restriction of the reproducing kernel
K_{d,s}
of
W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\Omega\times\Omega
, that is
K_{s}(x,y)=K_{d,s}(x,y)
for
x,y\in\Omega
. An explicit form of
K_{d,s}
can be found in Theorem 1 of Novak, Ullrich, Woźniakowski and Zhang
[
27
]
, which states that

K_{d,s}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\exp\big(2\pi i\langle x-y,u\rangle\big)}{1+\sum_{0<|\alpha|\leq s}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(2\pi u_{j})^{2\alpha_{j}}}\,{\rm d}u,\quad\text{for }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

2.5
A first example

We illustrate the advantage of the characterization of optimal points in Corollary
1

compared to conditions involving the covering radius. To this end, consider
n
-point sets
P_{n}
on a bounded convex domain
\Omega
. How large can the largest hole admist
P_{n}
be for this to be still optimal as a sampling set for
L_{q}
-approximation of
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
-functions?

Assume that for each
n
the ball
B_{n}:=B(y_{n},r_{n})
with
y_{n}\in\Omega
and
r_{n}>0
does not contain any points of
P_{n}
and that the points of
P_{n}
cover
\Omega\setminus B_{n}
nicely, i.e.,
the covering radius of
P_{n}
in
\Omega\setminus B_{n}
is of order
n^{-1/d}
. Then we have

\int_{\Omega}\dist(x,P_{n})^{\gamma}~{\rm d}x\,\preccurlyeq\,n^{-\gamma/d}+r_{n}^{\gamma+d}\quad\text{for all }0<\gamma<\infty.

Rearranging, this means that

r_{n}\preccurlyeq n^{-1/d+1/(\gamma+d)}\quad\text{implies}\quad\|\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}\preccurlyeq n^{-1/d}.

(21)

This condition is also necessary since
\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\succcurlyeq r_{n}
on
\Omega\cap B(x_{n},\frac{r_{n}}{2})
,
which is of volume
\asymp r_{n}^{d}
due to the interior cone condition, and thus

\int_{\Omega}\dist(x,P_{n})^{\gamma}~{\rm d}x\,\succcurlyeq\,r_{n}^{\gamma+d}.

Letting
1\leq q<p\leq\infty
and
\gamma
as above,
we obtain from Corollary
1

that
(P_{n})
is asymptotically optimal for
L_{q}
-approximation
on
W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
if and only if

r_{n}\preccurlyeq n^{-1/d+1/(\gamma+d)}.

(22)

This means that the radius of the largest hole is allowed to exceed the optimal covering radius
n^{-1/d}

by the polynomial factor
n^{1/(\gamma+d)}
.
We also refer to
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the necessity of condition (
22
) has been observed for numerical integration on the sphere.

3
The optimality of random points

In the following we give the proof of Proposition
1
. First, we derive a strong asymptotic result from a theorem due to Cohort
[
7
]
, which he obtained in the context of random quantizers. Related work may be found in
[
15
, Section 9]
and
[
41
]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
0<\gamma<\infty
and
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots

be independent and uniformly distributed on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies an interior cone condition. Consider the random
n
-point set
P_{n}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Then we havefor all
0<p<\infty
that

n^{\gamma/d}\frac{1}{\vol(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}\dist(x,P_{n})^{\gamma}{\rm d}x\,\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.\,and in }L_{p}}\,\left(\frac{\vol(\Omega)}{\vol\bigl(B(0,1)\bigr)}\right)^{\gamma/d}\Gamma\Bigl(1+\frac{\gamma}{d}\Bigr).

Proof.

We apply Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 in
[
7
]
and
let
\mu=\nu
be the uniform distribution on
\Omega
, i.e.,
these measures have densities
f=g=\frac{1}{\vol(\Omega)}\boldsymbol{1}_{\Omega}
, with
\boldsymbol{1}_{\Omega}
being the indicator function of
\Omega
. We check the assumptions T1.1-2 and T2.1-3 of the aforementioned theorems from
[
7
]
.
As Cohort remarks in Section 2, assumption T2.1 is satisfied for bounded probability density functions.
Clearly, T1.2 and T2.3 are satisfied as well.
For checking assumptions T1.1 and T2.2, we note that the interior cone condition yields a constant
c_{\Omega}>0
such that for every
x\in\Omega
and every
0<\varrho\leq 1+\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|x\|_{2}
we have
\vol\bigl(B(x,\varrho)\cap\Omega\bigr)\geq c_{\Omega}\vol\bigl(B(x,\varrho)\bigr)
.
Since
\Omega
is bounded, assumptions T1.1 and T2.2 are validated
and Theorems 1 and 2 can be applied.
Clearly, since convergence in
L_{p}
implies convergence in
L_{q}
for all
q<p
, the condition
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
in
[
7
, Theorem 1]
is obsolete.
∎

Regarding the asymptotic constant, let us note that if
\Omega
is a centrally symmetric convex body and
B(0,1)
is the maximal volume ellipsoid inside
\Omega
, the quantity
\bigl(\vol(\Omega)/\vol\bigl(B(0,1)\bigr)\bigr)^{1/d}
is known as the volume ratio of
\Omega
, which plays an important role in Banach space geometry, see e.g. Szarek and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
35
]
. Further, as
\gamma\to\infty
the quantity
\bigl(\Gamma(1+\frac{\gamma}{d})\bigr)^{1/\gamma}
tends to infinity, which is compatible with the fact that the
L_{\infty}
-norm of
\dist(\cdot,P_{n})
is typically of larger order than
n^{-1/d}
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Let
0<\gamma<\infty
. By Lemma
3
a bounded convex domain satisfies an interior cone condition. We set
p=\alpha/\gamma
and obtain from Proposition
3
that
n^{\gamma/d}\cdot\|\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}
converges in
L_{p}
to a positive constant, which implies that also
n^{\alpha/d}\cdot\mathbb{E}\,\|\dist(\cdot,P_{n})\|_{L_{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}
converges to a positive constant. This completes the proof.

The case
\gamma=\infty
is well known.
It is strongly connected to the so-called coupon collector’s problem,
which asks for the number of coupons (points) that a collector has to draw
in order to obtain a complete collection (hit every set in a diameter-bounded equal volume partition of
\Omega
).
Using that
\Omega
satisfies an interior cone condition,
the stated result e.g. follows from Theorems 2.1 and 2.2
in Reznikov and Saff
[
31
]
with
\Phi(r)=r^{d}
.
∎

Remark 5
.

Proposition
3
extends to probability distributions different from the uniform distribution. Under certain conditions, it also allows for the sampling distribution to differ from the distribution with respect to which the
L_{\gamma}
-norm of
\dist(\cdot,P_{n})
is computed.

Proposition
1
and Theorem
1

immediately yield Corollary
2

on the quality of random sampling points in expectation.
By Markov’s inequality, it is clear that the upper bounds in Corollary
2

also hold with high probability.
In addition, we also obtain an almost sure result on the quality of random sampling points.

Corollary 3
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
be independent and uniformly distributed on a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
1\leq q<p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s>d/p
.
Consider the random
n
-point sets
P_{n}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, the following holds almost surely:

e^{\rm lin}\big(P_{n},W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(\Omega)\big)\,\asymp\,n^{-s/d}.

Proof.

The upper bound is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
and Proposition
3
.
The lower bound holds for arbitrary
n
-point sets.
∎

4
Extensions to Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

In this section we discuss how to extend
Theorem
1
to a wider range of function spaces.
To that end, we consider the Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)
for
0<p<\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
and
s>d/p

as defined e.g. in
[
26
]
. We refrain from reproducing the definition and simply note that this family covers a
variety of interesting spaces:

•

For
\tau=2
and
1<p<\infty
, we obtain the fractional Sobolev space (or Bessel potential space)
H^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
, see e.g.
[
26
]
. If additionally
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we arrive at
the classical Sobolev spaces
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
as defined in Section
1
.

•

For
s\not\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
\tau=p
, we obtain the Sobolev-Slobodeckij space
W^{s}_{p}(\Omega)
of fractional smoothness, see e.g.
[
10
]
and note that
F^{s}_{pp}(\Omega)
is the Besov space
B^{s}_{pp}(\Omega)
.

We also want to discuss the
Hölder spaces
C^{s}(\Omega)
,
which are not included in this scale.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
, the Hölder space
C^{s}(\Omega)
is the space
of all
s
times continuously differentiable functions with

\|f\|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}:=\max_{|\alpha|\leq s}\,\sup_{x\in\Omega}\,|D^{\alpha}f(x)|<\infty.

For
s\not\in\mathbb{N}
, it is defined as the space of functions
f\in C^{\lfloor s\rfloor}(\Omega)
with

|f|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}:=\max_{|\alpha|=\lfloor s\rfloor}\,\sup_{x\neq y}\,\frac{|D^{\alpha}f(x)-D^{\alpha}f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\{s\}}}<\infty,

(23)

where
s=\lfloor s\rfloor+\{s\}
with
0<\{s\}<1
.
It is equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\lfloor s\rfloor}(\Omega)}+|\cdot|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}
.

Note that in this article equality between function spaces is meant up to equivalence of norms and the equivalence constants vanish in the asymptotic notation. Further, all mentioned spaces are continuously embedded in
C(\Omega)
.
We obtain the following extension of our results on
L_{q}
-approximation.

Theorem 2
.

Theorem
1
, Corollary
1
and Corollary
2

remain valid if we admit arbitrary real parameters
0<p,q,\tau\leq\infty
and
s>d/p

and replace
W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)
either by
C^{s}(\Omega)
for
p=\infty
or
by
F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)
for
p<\infty
.

For the proof, let us first note that
the analogues of Corollary
1
and Corollary
2

immediately follow from the analogue of Theorem
1
(a)
if we employ (
5
) and Proposition
1
.
Moreover, the extension of Theorem
1
(a) to the case
C^{s}(\Omega)
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
is already included in Section
2
.
Namely, the upper bound is immediate from the continuous embedding

C^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
.
The lower bound holds since our fooling functions
f_{\ast}

for
W_{\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
are smooth and non-negative.
For the remaining cases,
we follow the lines of the proof of Theorem
1
.
We only discuss the necessary changes.

Proof of the upper bounds.

It suffices to consider the case
\tau=\infty
,
since
F^{s}_{p\tau_{1}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow F^{s}_{p\tau_{2}}(\Omega)
for
\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}
.
We replace
|f|_{W_{p}^{s}(\Omega)}
by the following semi-(quasi-)norms:

•

For
p=\infty
and
s\not\in\mathbb{N}
, we use the semi-norm
|f|_{C^{s}(\Omega)}
as defined in (
23
).

•

For
p<\infty
, we use a semi-(quasi-)norm which is defined via the means

(d_{t}^{M,\Omega}f)(x):=t^{-d}\int_{V^{M}_{\Omega}(x,t)}\bigl|(\Delta_{h,\Omega}^{M}f)(x)\bigr|\,\text{d}h,

where
M:=\lfloor s+1\rfloor
,
\Delta_{h,\Omega}^{M}

is an
M^{\rm{th}}
-order difference operator
restricted to
\Omega
and
V^{M}_{\Omega}(x,t)
is
the set of directions
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of length less than
t>0
with
x+ah\in\Omega
for all
0\leq a\leq M
.
That is,
(d_{t}^{M,\Omega}f)(x)
is an averaged mean of the
M^{\rm{th}}
-order differences of
f
around
x
.
Putting

|f|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)}:=\bigg\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\frac{(d_{t}^{M,\Omega}f)(\cdot)}{t^{s}}\bigg\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},

it is known that we then have the equality

F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)=\{f\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)\colon|f|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty\}
and

\|\cdot\|_{L_{\max\{p,1\}}(\Omega)}+|\cdot|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)}

is an equivalent quasi-norm, see Proposition 6 in
[
26
]
and set
u=1
as well as
r=\infty
.
The same is true for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

It is readily verified that these semi-(quasi-)norms have
the following scaling property.
If
T\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of the form
T(x)=\varrho^{-1}x+x_{0}

with
\varrho\leq 1
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and
f\in F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)
or
f\in C^{s}(\Omega)
, then
g=f\circ T^{-1}
satisfies

|g|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(T\Omega)}\leq\varrho^{s-d/p}|f|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)},\qquad|g|_{C^{s}(T\Omega)}=\varrho^{s}|f|_{C^{s}(\Omega)},\quad\text{respectively.}

(24)

Lemma
8
holds without changes
for the spaces
F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)
and
C^{s}(\Omega)
, see Rychkov
[
32
]
,
where we note that
C^{s}(\Omega)=B^{s}_{\infty\infty}(\Omega)
for
s\not\in\mathbb{N}
.
If we use
[
26
, Corollary 11]
(for
p<\infty
)
and
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]
(for
p=\infty
) instead of
[
23
, Lemma 1.1.11]

and the scaling properties (
24
),
we see that Lemma
9
concerning polynomial approximation on cubes
remains valid under the modifications of Theorem
2
.
Thus, also Lemma
10
defies our modifications.
It only remains to note that the semi-norm
|\cdot|_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\Omega)}
behaves equally well with respect to an efficient covering. Analogous to (
8
), we have

\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f|^{p}_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(Q_{j})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\frac{(d_{t}^{M,Q_{j}}f)(x)}{t^{s}}\right)^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{j}}(x)\,{\rm d}x\leq c_{7}|f|^{p}_{F^{s}_{p\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq c_{8}

(25)

since
d_{t}^{M,Q_{j}}f(x)\leq d_{t}^{M,\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
With these preparations at hand, we may copy the proofs of Section
2
.
∎

Proof of the lower bounds.

Also the lower bound for the cases
q\geq p
and
q<p=\infty
can be copied.
The scaling properties (
10
) for
F^{s}_{p\tau}
may be obtained
from Proposition 2.3.1/1 in
[
13
]
and the translation-invariance of the quasi-norm.
The lower bound in the case
q<p<\infty
requires different ideas
as we cannot establish
\left\|\sum_{i}\varphi_{i}\right\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\preccurlyeq\sum_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|^{p}_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for general disjointly supported
\varphi_{i}
,
as used in (
17
) for the Sobolev spaces.
For optimal point sets, this problem does not exist as we may consider bumps
\varphi_{i}
which are supported in balls of the same radius which enables the use of localization methods, see
[
26
]
.
For general point sets, a remedy can be provided with the help of atomic decompositions of the spaces
F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)
as treated by Triebel
[
36
]
.

Let
y_{i}
,
d_{i}
and
B_{i}
be as in Proposition
2
. Let
K>s
be an integer and let
Q
be the cube of sidelength
1
which is centered at the origin.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-negative with
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi\subset Q

such that
\psi(0)>0
and
\|D^{\alpha}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\leq K
. The exact choice of the function
\psi
is not important for our argument. According to Definition 13.3 in
[
36
]
the function

\psi_{\nu m}(\cdot)=2^{-\nu(s-d/p)}\psi(2^{\nu}\cdot-m)\quad\text{where }\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

is an
(s,p)_{K,-1}
-atom supported in
Q_{\nu m}
, the cube with center
2^{-\nu}m
and sidelength
2^{-\nu}
. We choose
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\nu_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
2^{-\nu_{i}}\asymp d_{i}

such that the dyadic cube
Q_{i}^{*}:=Q_{\nu_{i}m_{i}}
is contained in
B_{i}
. Set

f:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\psi_{i},\quad\text{where }\lambda_{i}:=2^{-\nu_{i}\beta}\text{ with }\beta=(\gamma+d)/p.

This function is supported in
\Omega
and satisfies
f|_{P}=0
. Also,
\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and by
[
36
, Theorem 13.8]
,

\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\lesssim\|\lambda\|_{f_{p\tau}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\lambda_{i}2^{\nu_{i}d/p}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}^{*}}(x)|^{\tau}\Big)^{p/\tau}{\rm d}x,

where the implicit constant does not depend on the balls
B_{i}
and
f_{p\tau}
is a quasi-normed space of sequences.
For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
only one summand is not equal to zero and since
\mathrm{vol}(Q_{i}^{*})=2^{-\nu_{i}d}
,

\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q_{i}^{*}}(2^{-\nu_{i}})^{\beta p-d}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}^{*}}(x){\rm d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2^{-\nu_{i}})^{\beta p}.

As
\beta p=\gamma+d
and
2^{-\nu_{i}}\asymp d_{i}
, we arrive at

\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}^{\gamma+d}.

Using a substitution we have

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{Q_{i}^{*}}(2^{-\nu_{i}})^{\beta q}\psi_{i}(x)^{q}{\rm d}x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(2^{-\nu_{i}})^{\beta q+sq-dq/p+d}\int_{2Q}\psi(x)^{q}{\rm d}x.

Since
\beta q+sq=\gamma+dq/p
we have

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\asymp\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}^{\gamma+d}.

It follows that the normalized function
f_{*}=f/\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)}
satisfies

\|f_{*}\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}=\frac{\|f\|_{L_{q}(\Omega)}}{\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega)}}\gtrsim\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}^{\gamma+d}\Big)^{1/q-1/p}\gtrsim\Big(\int_{\Omega}\dist(x,P)^{\gamma}{\rm d}x\Big)^{s/\gamma},

where the last inequality derives from (
18
). Now the fooling function
f_{\ast}
yields the lower bound analogous to (
9
).
We get the lower bound for the integration problem as the function
f_{*}
is non-negative and thus
\|f\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}f(x){\rm d}x
.
∎

Let us conclude with a few remarks on further extensions of our result.

Remark 6

(More general domains, part 2)
.

We only used that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in order to extend functions on
\Omega
to functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
without significant change of the norm. It is not hard to check that our results are valid for all bounded measurable sets
\Omega
satisfying a strong local cone condition as in Lemma
6
if the function spaces on
\Omega
are defined via restriction of the corresponding function spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

A(\Omega):=\{f|_{\Omega}\colon f\in A(\mathbb{R}^{d})\},\qquad\|g\|_{A(\Omega)}:=\inf_{f\in A(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon f|_{\Omega}=g}\|f\|_{A(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
A\in\{W_{p}^{s},F^{s}_{p\tau},C^{s}\}
.
By Lemma
8
(and its analogues), these spaces coincide
with the spaces from above
(in the sense of equivalent norms) if
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

Remark 7

(Besov spaces)
.

It remains open whether Theorem
2
carries over also to the scale of Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p\tau}(\Omega),
where
0<p,\tau\leq\infty
and
s>d/p
. The upper bounds are complicated by finding an analogue of (
25
).
Novak and Triebel
[
26
]
avoid this employing interpolation, which may also be used here
in order to treat the case
q\geq p
, but for
q<p
different methods may be required.
For the lower bound the situation is similar to the
F
-spaces. We leave the extension of Theorem
2
to Besov spaces for future research.

Remark 8

(Functions with zero boundary condition)
.

Since all our fooling functions are supported in the open set
\Omega
,
all our results hold without changes for the smaller spaces of functions
satisfying zero boundary conditions.

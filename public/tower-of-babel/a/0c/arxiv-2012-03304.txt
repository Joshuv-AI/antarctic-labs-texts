Abstract
. The symmetrized bidisc G = def { ( z + w , z w ) : | z | < 1 , | w | < 1 } , G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{def}}}{{=}}\{(z+w,zw):|z|<1,\ |w|<1\}, under the Carathéodory metric, is a complex Finsler space of cohomogeneity 1 1 in which the geodesics, both real and complex, enjoy a rich geometry. As a Finsler manifold, G G does not admit a natural notion of angle, but we nevertheless show that there is a notion of orthogonality. The complex tangent bundle T ​ G TG splits naturally into the direct sum of two line bundles, which we call the sharp and flat bundles, and which are geometrically defined and therefore covariant under automorphisms of G G . Through every point of G G there is a unique complex geodesic of G G in the flat direction, having the form F β = def { ( β + β ¯ ​ z , z ) : z ∈ 𝔻 } F^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{def}}}{{=}}\{(\beta+\bar{\beta}z,z)\ :z\in\mathbb{D}\} for some β ∈ 𝔻 \beta\in\mathbb{D} , and called a flat geodesic . We say that a complex geodesic D D is orthogonal to a flat geodesic F F if D D meets F F at a point λ \lambda and the complex tangent space T λ ​ D T_{\lambda}D at λ \lambda is in the sharp direction at λ \lambda . We prove that a geodesic D D has the closest point property with respect to a flat geodesic F F if and only if D D is orthogonal to F F in the above sense. Moreover, G G is foliated by the geodesics in G G that are orthogonal to a fixed flat geodesic F F .

Lemma 4.3

If

Proof.

Clearly

(b_{\beta}\circ b_{\alpha})(z)=\frac{1+\bar{\alpha}\beta}{1+\bar{\beta}\alpha}\ \ \frac{z-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{1+\bar{\alpha}\beta}}{1-\frac{\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}}{1+\bar{\beta}\alpha}z}.

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1+\bar{\alpha}\beta}{1+\bar{\beta}\alpha}-1\Big|<2\big|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{1+\bar{\alpha}\beta}\big|

But, as

\displaystyle|\bar{\alpha}\beta-\bar{\beta}\alpha|

\displaystyle=|\bar{\alpha}(\alpha+\beta)-\beta(\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta})|

Lemma 4.16

Let

Proof.

If conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) hold, then

Furthermore, Proposition

implies that

is purely balanced (or exceptional respectively).

Conversely, assume that

Lemma 4.17
.

If
\sigma>0
and

\left|\frac{\tau-1}{\tau+1}\right|<\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma^{2}},

\left|\frac{\tau-1}{\tau+1}\right|=\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma^{2}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle m(\sigma)=-\sigma

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m(b_{\sigma}^{-1}(0))=b_{\sigma}(0)

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\big(b_{\sigma}^{-1}\circ m\circ b_{\sigma}^{-1}\big)(0)=0

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad b_{\sigma}^{-1}\circ m\circ b_{\sigma}^{-1}=r_{\tau}\quad\mbox{ for some }\tau\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad m=b_{\sigma}\circ r_{\tau}\circ b_{\sigma}\quad\mbox{ for some }\tau\in\mathbb{T},

which proves the first assertion of the lemma.

To prove the second assertion, by direct calculation,

m(z)=\frac{\tau+\sigma^{2}}{1+\tau\sigma^{2}}\ \ \frac{z-\frac{\sigma(\tau+1)}{\tau+\sigma^{2}}}{1-\frac{\sigma(\tau+1)}{1+\tau\sigma^{2}}z}.

Consequently, by Lemma

(iii),

is hyperbolic if and only if

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tau+\sigma^{2}}{1+\tau\sigma^{2}}-1\right|<2\ \left|\frac{\sigma(\tau+1)}{1+\tau\sigma^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\ \

\displaystyle\left|\tau+\sigma^{2}-(1+\tau\sigma^{2})\right|<2\ \left|\sigma(\tau+1)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\ \

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tau-1}{\tau+1}\right|<\frac{2\sigma}{1-\sigma^{2}},

that is, inequality (

) holds.

A similar calculation using Lemma

Recall that the flat geodesics of

It can be shown that flat geodesics are truly ‘flat’: they have

11

In earlier papers we

the flat geodesics to be the

(5.1)

F^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\{(\beta+\bar{\beta}z,z):z\in\mathbb{D}\}

for some
\beta\in\mathbb{D}

Let us check that, for every

, this set is indeed a flat geodesic of

according to the definition. Firstly, if
(s,p)=(\beta+\bar{\beta}z,z)
for some

. Secondly,

is the range of the analytic disc

is a geodesic of

It remains to show that

at exactly one point

If

meets

at the unique point

has a unique royal point, and that point lies in

is a flat geodesic.

Lemma 5.14
.

Fix a flat geodesic

and a point

is orthogonal to

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

. Choose

such that

Represent

If we set

∎

By combining the previous two lemmas we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.15

is a flat geodesic, then

.

In the previous section we studied the purely balanced geodesics that are orthogonal to a fixed flat geodesic. In this section, in contrast, we

Definition 6.1
.

For any geodesic

is a purely balanced geodesic

.

Proposition

below, which gives a description of the sharp points in a purely balanced geodesic

and the fixed points of
m
.

If

, there exists hyperbolic

where, for

Lemma 6.2
.

If

is a purely balanced geodesic and

does not depend on the choice of

Proof.

Suppose that

By Lemma

, either

or

In the former case it is immediate that

and

In the latter case, since the fixed points

of

coincide with those

of

,

. Moreover, since

is a hyperbolic isometry of
\mathbb{D}

, and so

. For any

, and therefore

∎

Proposition 6.3
.

If

is a purely balanced geodesic, then

Proof.

We first show that

, we may assume that

. Since the fixed points of

are

\lambda=(0,-\sigma^{2})=h_{m_{\sigma,1}}(\sigma)\in h_{m_{\sigma,1}}(C_{\eta})=\Xi_{B}.

We now turn to the proof that

. Fix a purely balanced geodesic
B
with royal points
(2\xi,\xi^{2})
and

is parametrized as in equation (

for some hyperbolic

and

\mathbb{T}

Choose

Now fix

\displaystyle\gamma_{b_{t}}\circ g(z)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{b_{t}}(z+b_{r}(z),zb_{r}(z))

\displaystyle=(b_{t}(z)+b_{t}\circ b_{r}(z),b_{t}(z)b_{t}\circ b_{r}(z))

\displaystyle=(b_{t}(z)+b_{r}\circ b_{t}(z),b_{t}(z)b_{r}\circ b_{t}(z))=g\circ b_{t}(z),

7.3.
The closest point property and orthogonality

Theorem 7.24
.

Let
F
be a flat geodesic in
G
. Assume that
D
is a geodesic in
G
that meets
F
at the point
\lambda_{0}
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

For every
\mu\in D

\lambda_{0}
is a closest point in
F
to
\mu
;

(ii)

there exists
\mu\in D
such that
\lambda_{0}
is a closest point in
F
to
\mu
;

(iii)

D
is orthogonal to
F
.

Proof.

Clearly, (i) implies (ii). If (ii) holds, then (
7.2
) implies that
D
is purely balanced and
Lemma
7.7
implies that
(\lambda_{0},\mu)
is a critical pair. Therefore, Lemma
7.18
implies that (iii) holds.

Now suppose that (iii) holds. Fix
\mu\in\mathbb{D}
. Choose
\lambda_{1}\in F
such that
\lambda_{1}
is a closest point in
F
to
\mu
.
By (ii) implies (iii), if
D_{1}
is the geodesic passing through
\lambda_{1}
and
\mu
, then
D_{1}
is orthogonal to
F
. As
D
and
D_{1}
are both orthogonal to
F
and
\mu\in D\cap D_{1}
, Lemma
5.11
implies that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{0}
. Therefore,
\lambda_{0}
is a closest point in
F
to
\mu
.
∎

Corollary 7.25
.

Assume that
F
is a flat geodesic in
G
and
\mu\in G
. If
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are both a closest point in
F
to
\mu
, then
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose

\inf_{\lambda\in F}d_{G}(\lambda,\mu)

is attained at two points
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
in
F
. If we let
D_{1}
be the geodesic passing through
\lambda_{1}
and
\mu
and let
D_{2}
be the geodesic passing through
\lambda_{2}
and
\mu
, then the equivalence of Conditions (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
7.24
implies that
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are both orthogonal to
F
. Therefore, as
\mu\in D_{1}\cap D_{2}
, Lemma
5.11
implies that
D_{1}=D_{2}
. Hence,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.
∎

The following corollary of Theorem
7.24
is equivalent to Theorem

Corollary 7.26
.

Let
F
be a flat geodesic in
G
and let

be a geodesic in

. Then
D
has the closest point property with respect to
F

Abstract
The concept of tensor eigenpairs has received more researches in past decades. Recent works have paid attentions to a special class of symmetric tensors termed regular simplex tensors, which is constructed by equiangular tight frame of n + 1 n+1 vectors in n n -dimensional space, and the robustness of eigenpairs was investigated. In the end of the literature, a conjecture was claimed that the robust eigenvectors of a regular simplex tensor are precisely the vectors in the frame. One later work theoretically proved that the case of n = 2 n=2 was true. In this paper, we proceed further and complete the proof for the above conjecture. Some promising directions are discussed in the end for future works.

In

2.1
Optimization theories of Tensor eigenpairs

In this part, we briefly introduce the optimization theories related to the tensor eigenpairs problem.
The concept of tensor eigenpairs can be understood and derived by considering the following constrained optimization model:

\begin{cases}\max\limits_{\mathbf{v}}\quad\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m}\\
\rm s.t.\quad\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{v}=1\end{cases}.

(1)

The Lagrangian function
of (
1
) is defined as:

L(\mathbf{v},\lambda)=\frac{1}{\it m}\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{\it m}+\frac{\lambda}{2}(1-\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{v}).

(2)

When the gradient of

L(\mathbf{v},\lambda)
to

\mathbf{v}
(also known as ) is
\mathbf{0}
, the eigenpair of a symmetric tensor can be deduced, which was independently defined by Lim and Qi in 2005:

Definition 3
.

[
21
,
22
]
[
eigenpairs of symmetric tensor
]
Given a tensor
\mathcal{S}\in T^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
a pair

(\lambda,\mathbf{v})

is an eigenpair of

\mathcal{S}

if

\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}=\lambda\mathbf{v},

(3)

where

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

is the eigenvalue and

\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times 1}

is the corresponding eigenvector satisifying

\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{v}=1
.

The second-order derivation information plays an important role in identifying whether a stationary point is locally optimal given an optimization model. The second-order derivation
of

L(\mathbf{v},\lambda)

to
\mathbf{v}
, which is also termed the Hessian matrix of
(
2
), is denoted
by

\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{v})=(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n},

(4)

where

\mathbf{I}_{n}

is an
n\times n
identity matrix.

Then,
the
locally optimal solutions of (
1
)
can be identified by
checking the negative
definiteness
and determining the sign of each eigenvalue
of the following matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\bot}\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\bot}

(5)

and the detailed explanation for the
derivation of
(
5
)
can refer to our previous work
[
23
]
.

2.2
Robust eigenpairs of symmetric tensors

One of the widely used algorithms to obtain tensor eigenpairs is called the tensor power method, which
is based on the following mapping function

\phi(\mathbf{v})=\frac{\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}}{\left\|\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}\right\|}

(6)

and performs the following iterative schedule:

\mathbf{v}_{k+1}\mapsto\frac{\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{m-1}}{\left\|\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}_{k}^{m-1}\right\|}.

(7)

It can be observed
by comparing
(
7
)
with the KKT condition (
3
)
that the tensor power method
can be understood as iterative method by
using the first-order KKT equation.

Given a random initialization vector

\mathbf{v}_{0}

and iterate the above formula
(
7
) until some termination condition is satisfied, an eigenpair can be obtained.
Then, a robust eigenvector of
\mathcal{S}
is an eigenvector
\mathbf{v}
that is an attracting
fixed point of the tensor power method,
which
can be identified by calculating the spectral radius of the Jacobian matirx of the map function, which is denoted and given by

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{m-1}{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\right),

(8)

where
\lambda\neq 0
, indicating that
the robustness checking should exclude these eigenpairs with eigenvalues 0.
If the spectral radius of
\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{v})
is less than 1, it is said that the corresponding eigenpair is a robust one.
The detailed derivations can refer to
the Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 in
[
16
]
.

Remark 1
.

It should be noted that
the proof for the
the Lemma 3.3 in
[
16
]

is less rigorous or
is correct but miss
some necessary illustration for the final expression
of (
8
).

Strictly speaking, according to the derivation presented in the proof of Theorem 3.3 in
[
16
]
,
one can only obtain
the Jacobian matrix as follows:

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{v})=\frac{d-1}{|\lambda|}\left(\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{d-2}-\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\right),

(9)

which is used in the sequential work that discussed the same problem
[
17
]
.
The minor difference lies in that
one more absolute value operation
was imposed on the numerator
\lambda
.
Note that even though
such a difference
will have no impact on determining
the spectral radius,
since we still need to calculate the absolute value one more time when determining the spectral radius, as can be seen in the definition
1
.
In this sense,
the difference between
(
8
)
and
(
9
)
is trivial.

Here, we still would like to
justify
that
(
8
)
is also correct to directly use the original
(
8
).
The reasons are as follows.
When
d
is odd, both

(\lambda,\mathbf{v})
and
(-\lambda,-\mathbf{v})
are eigenpairs, without losing generality, we only take eigenpairs with non-negative eigenvalues; when
d
is even, eigenpairs appear in
\pm
pairs with the same eigenvalue, we only select
\mathbf{v}
satisfying
\sum_{i}v_{i}>0
.
In addition,
we can derive that

\lambda=\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}_{i})^{m}>0

for even
m
.

2.3
Regular simplex frame and tensor

In this part, we will introduce a special class of symmetric tensors, which is termed
regular simplex one.
First, the definition of the generalized equiangular set is introduced as follows:

Definition 4

(Definition 4.1 in Ref.
[
16
]
)
.

An equiangular set (ES) is a collection of vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
r\geq n
if there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\alpha=\left|\left\langle\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rangle\right|,\forall i\neq j\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|=1,\forall i.

Furthermore, an
\mathrm{ES}
is called an equiangular tight frame (ETF) if

\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}=a\mathbf{I},\quad\mathbf{W}:=\left(\mathbf{w}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{w}_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times r}

(10)

For example,
when
r=n
and
a=1
, the orthonormal bases

\left\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

forms an ETF, where
\alpha=0
.
When
r=n+1
and
a=\frac{n+1}{n}
,
\left\{\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n+1}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{(n)\times(n+1)}
, it is termed
regular simplex frames,
\alpha=-\frac{1}{n}
.

\mathbf{w}_{i},i=1,2\dots,n+1
are called the vectors in the frame.
In 2D space,
the regular simplex frame is a regular triangle.

Then, the regular simplex tensor is one deduced by the the regular simplex frame with the following form:

\mathcal{S}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\circ m}

(11)

where
\circ
is the outer product defined in Definition
2
,
and
\mathcal{S}
is a symmetric tensor of order

m

and dimension

n
.
In this paper, we mainly analyze the robust eigenpairs of regular simplex tensor and try to deal with the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1

(Conjecture 4.7 in Ref
[
16
]
)
.

The robust eigenvectors of a regular simplex tensor are precisely the
vectors in the frame.

This conjecture
was originally proposed in
[
16
]
.
Later, several researchers also considered this problem in
[
17
]
.
It should be noted that the above conjecture contains
the following meanings:
1) the robust eigenpairs of regular simplex tensors may not exist, which has been observed by experiments in
[
16
]
,
and will also be theoretically justified in the later analysis;
2) if exists, they are only these vectors in the frame.
In
[
16
]
, the authors only shows that
these vectors in the frame are indeed the robust one
for the tensor with dimension
n
and order
m
and
n\geq 2
,
m\geq 3
and
n+m\geq 7
. (See Theorem 4.5 of
[
16
]
for details).
However,
the uniqueness that these vectors in the frame are only robust ones is not justified, which was then left as the above Conjecture

1
.
In other words, the term "precisely" in the conjecture remains to be demonstrated.
For this purpose,
one must check the robustness of all eigenvectors given a regular simplex tensor,
and identify which are robust ones among all candidates.
And such a strategy has been adopted in the later work
[
17
]
.
In detail, they first analyzed the real eigenstructure of all eigenpairs concerning regular simplex tensor, and
determine
whether each eigenpair is robust or not by checking the spectral radius of the Jacobian matrix at each one.
However, it can be seen that such a task is computationally heavy as
n
and
m
become larger, since the number of all eigenpairs
will exponentially increase.
So, they only provided the proof for the simplest case
n=2
,
while the higher
n
cases will be difficult to be calculated and checked.
In addition,
it is also difficult
for most of eigenpairs
to
implicitly calculate the Jacobian
matrix, let alone
determine the spectral radius further.

To our best knowledge, so far,
these references are the only two works
focusing on identifying the robust eigenpairs of regular simplex tensor.
In this paper, in the basis of the two works,
we proceed further concerning the above conjecture and
finally complete the proof for the above conjecture.
The details are as follows.

3
Relation on robust and locally maximized eigenpairs

Note that in the related works
[
16
,
17
]
,
the robustness of an eigenpair is generally checked by the spectral radius of the Jacobian matirx as defined in
Subsection
2.2
.
However, as mentioned above,
it is a little complicated
to determine the spectral radius of all eigenpairs, especially for the higher
n
and
m
case.

In this part, we would like to provide an auxiliary criterion for robustness checking.
It can be observed by comparing (
8
) with (
4
)
that both of them contain a term
\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}
, and
by further investigating their relationship, the following lemma can be built:

Lemma 1
.

Given an eigenpair

(\lambda,\mathbf{v})
of a tensor
\mathcal{S}\in T^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
if the eigenpair is a robust one,
it must be locally maximized solution of model (
1
).
Conversely, it does not always hold.

Proof.

First,

\mathbf{K}
in (
5
) is rewritten as the following form:

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\bot}\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\bot}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})[(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}](\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})[(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}-(m-2)\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}]

\displaystyle=(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}-(m-2)\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}-(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}+(m-2)\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=(m-1)\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{I}_{n}-(m-1)\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}+\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}},

where in the last equation, we utilize

\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}\mathbf{v}=\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}=\lambda\mathbf{v}
. By comparing with (
8
), it holds that

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{J}={\mathbf{K}}+\lambda(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}).

(12)

Furthermore, it can be checked that

\mathbf{J}\mathbf{v}=\frac{m-1}{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}\mathbf{v}-\lambda\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{v})=\frac{m-1}{\lambda}(\lambda\mathbf{v}-\lambda\mathbf{v})=0\cdot\mathbf{v},

(13)

where we reuse

\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}\mathbf{v}=\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-1}=\lambda\mathbf{v}
, and the above result means
that

\mathbf{v}

is an eigenvector of

\mathbf{J}
with eigenvalue 0,
and in this way, the other eigenvectors must lie in the
null space of
\mathbf{v}
, denoted by

null(\mathbf{v})
.

In a similar way,
the vector
\mathbf{v}

will simultaneously
be the eigenvector for

\mathbf{K},\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}
,
which holds
that

\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v}=0\cdot\mathbf{v},\quad(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})\mathbf{v}=0\cdot\mathbf{v},

(14)

and the other eigenvectors
of
\mathbf{K},\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}
also must lie in
null(\mathbf{v})
.

This indicates that their eigendecomposition
shares the same eigenvector matrix, denoted by

\mathbf{V}=[\mathbf{v},null(\mathbf{v})]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
In other word, concerning (
12
),
they can be diagonalized
by the same orthogonal matrix
\mathbf{V}
, which follows:

\displaystyle\lambda\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{T}}[{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda(\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})]\mathbf{V}.

(15)

Note that

\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

is a projection matrix with rank
n-1
, and its eigenvalues are given by

1,1,\dots,1,0
, where the number of the eigenvalue of
1
is
n-1
.

Based on the above relationship (
15
), once
we obtain the eigen-distribution of

\mathbf{K}
,
the corresponding eigenvalues of

\mathbf{J}

and its spectral radius can be determined.
Therefore, in the following, we will discuss that under what condition,
the eigenpair may be a robust one.

Assume that
the

n
eigenvalues of
\mathbf{J}

and
\mathbf{K}

is sorted as

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{\mathbf{J}}\leq\sigma_{2}^{\mathbf{J}}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{n}^{\mathbf{J}}.

(16)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{\mathbf{K}}\leq\sigma_{2}^{\mathbf{K}}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{n}^{\mathbf{K}}.

(17)

Then, we can have that

\displaystyle\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}=\begin{cases}\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda,\quad\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}}=1\\
\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}=0,\quad\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}}}=0\end{cases}.

(18)

According to the sign of all eigenvalues of
\mathbf{K}
,
three different cases are discussed as follows:

Case 1
:
If
(\lambda,\mathbf{v})
is a locally minimized solution of model (
1
),
its corresponding matrix

\mathbf{K}

will be a non-negative definite one.
By combining
(
14
),
it can be concluded that

\sigma_{min}^{\mathbf{K}}=\sigma_{1}^{\mathbf{K}}=0
,
and

0\leq\sigma_{2}^{\mathbf{K}}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{n-1}^{\mathbf{K}}
.
By combining (
15
), it can be concluded that

\displaystyle\sigma_{min}^{\mathbf{J}}=\sigma_{1}^{\mathbf{J}}=0,\quad\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}=\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda\geq\lambda,\quad i=2,\dots,n.

(19)

which indicates
all eigenvalues of

\mathbf{J}

are non-negative, and
it can be derived that

\rho(\mathbf{J})=\sigma_{n}^{\mathbf{J}}\geq 1
,
which indicate that the corresponding eigenpair cannot be a robust one.

Case 2
:
If
(\lambda,\mathbf{v})
is a saddle solution of model (
1
),
its corresponding matrix

\mathbf{K}

will be uncertain, indicating
its eigenvalues include negative, positive, and 0.
Assuming that

k
-th eigenvalue are equal to 0, which follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{\mathbf{K}}\leq\dots\leq\sigma_{k-1}^{\mathbf{K}}\leq\sigma_{k}^{\mathbf{K}}=0\leq\sigma_{k+1}^{\mathbf{K}}\dots\leq\sigma_{n-1}^{\mathbf{K}}.

(20)

Since

.

-th eigenvalues,
the other ones of

\lambda(\mathbf{I}_{n-1}-\mathbf{v}\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{T}})
are all

\lambda
,
Then, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda\leq\lambda,\quad i=1,\dots,k-1.

(21)

\displaystyle\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda\geq\lambda,\quad i=k+1,\dots,n-1.

It can be seen that for

.
In this sense,

,

If

By combining (

\displaystyle\sigma_{max}^{\mathbf{J}}=\sigma_{n-1}^{\mathbf{J}}=0,\quad\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}=\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda\leq\lambda,\quad i=1,\dots,n-2.

(23)

In this case,

\lambda
.

\displaystyle\lambda\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{J}}=\sigma_{i}^{\mathbf{K}}+\lambda>-\lambda,i=1,\dots,n-2.\

(24)

,

To conclude,
if an eigenpair is a robust one,
it must be a locally maximized eigenpair of the corresponding constrained model (
1
).
Conversely, it does not always hold.
Therefore, the set of all robust eigenpairs
is a subset of these locally maximized eigenpairs,
which will be always no large than that of all eigenpairs.
In other words,
only these locally maximized eigenpairs could be the potential
candidates
to be the robust eigenpairs.

In this way,

we do not need to analyze and determine the spectral radius
of
\mathbf{J}

In contrast,

eigenpairs of tensor.

In this sense,

1

1) the number of eigenpairs to be checked
is greatly reduced, due to the fact that

all eigenpairs, especially when

2):
naturally, following the first merit, the difficulties existed in the previous work
that
calculating the Jacobian matrix and determining the spectral radius for most of eigenpairs

eigenpairs (these vectors in the frame), which is easy to be calculated, as can be seen from
the following part.

Therefore,

only

In this sense, we have only

4
Proof of the Conjecture
1

Conjecture 1

(Conjecture 4.7 in Ref
[
16
]
)
.

The robust eigenvectors of a regular simplex tensor are precisely the
vectors in the frame.

Proof.

Concerning the vectors in the regular simplex frame

\mathbf{W}=[\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-1)\times n}
,
it holds that

\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}_{i}=1,\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}_{j}=\alpha=-\frac{1}{n}

for

i\neq j
.

\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{T}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{n+1}{n}\mathbf{I}
.
It can be calculated that when

\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}_{j}
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{v}^{m-2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{m-2}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\circ m})\mathbf{w}_{j}^{m-2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}_{i})^{m-2}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{1}{(-n)^{m-2}}\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n+1}\mathbf{w}_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}=\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{1}{(-n)^{m-2}}(\frac{n+1}{n}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{(-n)^{m-2}})\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}+\frac{n+1}{(-n)^{m-2}n}\mathbf{I}.

(25)

In addition,
the corresponding eigenvalue can be calculated by

\lambda=\mathcal{S}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{w}_{i})^{m}=1+\frac{n}{(-n)^{m}}.

(26)

Then, according to (
8
),
the Jacobian matrix
at the eigenvector
\mathbf{w}_{j}

will satisfy

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j})\mathbf{w}_{j}=\frac{m-1}{\lambda}(\mathcal{S}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{m-2}-\lambda\mathbf{w}_{j}\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}})\mathbf{w}_{j}=0\cdot\mathbf{w}_{j}

(27)

which means
that

{\mathbf{w}_{j}}
itself
is an eigenvector of

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j})
with eigenvalue 0,
and naturally, the other eigenvectors must lie in the
null space of
\mathbf{w}_{j}
, denoted by

null(\mathbf{w}_{j})
.
By further
utilizing (
4
) and (
26
),
it can be easily checked that
there are only two different eigenvalues concerning

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j})
,
which are

0,\underbrace{\frac{(n+1)(m-1)}{1+(-n)^{m-2}n},\dots,\frac{(n+1)(m-1)}{1+(-n)^{m-2}n}}_{n-1}.

(28)

By considering
the odd and even cases for
m
separately,
it can be concluded that
the spectral radius of

\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j})

is given by

\displaystyle\rho(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j}))=\begin{cases}\frac{(n+1)(m-1)}{n^{m-1}-1},\quad m=2k+1,k\in\mathbb{N}\\
\frac{(n+1)(m-1)}{1+n^{m-1}},,\quad m=2k,k\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases},

(29)

where
for the odd
m
cases,
since
1+(-n)^{m-2}n=1-n^{m-1}<0
,
the numerator
should be changed to be positive.

Note that

n\geq 2
,
m\geq 3
,
it can be checked that

n+m\geq 7
,
it holds that

\rho(\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{w}_{j}))<1
,
and the
vectors in the frame of the corresponding
regular simplex tensor are robust.

∎

Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper,
we mainly focus on a conjecture concerning the robust eigenpair of regular simplex tensor,
and
provide a proof for it.
Different from the previous works,
our proof way first investigated the relationship between
robust eigenpairs and locally maximized eigenpairs,
which is another important concept in optimization field.
Then, such a established relation
will help us to greatly simplify
the proof progress of the focused conjecture by only considering
a smaller subset of all eigenpairs.
Benefiting from such a processing,
the conclusion in the conjecture is finally
demonstrated.

The other conjectures claimed in the original reference
[
16
]

are also worth investigating in the next stage.

Quantum Statistical Mechanics of Dissolving Vortices
N.S. Manton, Shiyi Wang

Abstract
The quantum partition function for dissolving Abelian Higgs vortices is calculated explicitly, using spectral data for the Beltrami Laplacian on the N N -vortex moduli space ℂ ​ ℙ N \mathbb{CP}^{N} with a scaled Fubini–Study metric. From the partition function, the pressure of the vortex gas is derived. There are three asymptotic regimes – High , Intermediate and Low Temperature . The phase crossover from Intermediate to Low Temperature is modelled by a Bessel function. In the Low Temperature regime the free energy is not extensive but is proportional to N 2 N^{2} .



1
Introduction

In this paper we will consider the quantum statistical mechanics of
vortices, where by vortices we mean critically coupled Abelian Higgs
vortices on a compact, oriented surface
\Sigma
having a fixed
Riemannian metric with area
A
. On such a surface,
there are static
N
-vortex solutions satisfying first-order
Bogomolny equations, provided the Bradlow inequality
4\pi N<A
is
satisfied
[
1
]
. The vortex number
N
is a topological
invariant, and a vortex can be informally seen as a ‘hard disk’ of
area
4\pi
. Here we assume
N
is large, and correspondingly
A

sufficiently large, so that we can discuss the statistical mechanics
of vortices.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{N}
of
N
-vortex solutions has real
dimension
2N
because the vortex centres, where the Higgs field
vanishes, can be at arbitrary locations on
\Sigma
.
Vortices are indistinguishable, so
\mathcal{M}_{N}
is
diffeomorphic to
\Sigma^{N}/S_{N}
, the
N
th symmetrised power of

\Sigma

[
2
]
. We will assume here that

\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

\mathcal{M}_{N}
is then diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{N}
acquires from the kinetic part of the
Lagrangian of the Abelian Higgs model a Riemannian (in fact Kähler)
metric that depends on the metric of
\Sigma
. In general, the moduli space
metric is not known explicitly, but some of its global properties,
in particular its volume
[
3
]
and total scalar curvature

[
4
]
, are known. Classical
N
-vortex dynamics can be approximated
by geodesic motion on
\mathcal{M}_{N}
with this metric. Analogously,
we shall assume here that quantized vortex dynamics is modelled by
a Hamiltonian proportional to the Beltrami Laplacian on
\mathcal{M}_{N}
.

The classical statistical mechanics of
N
vortices on

S^{2}
with a round metric of area
A
is well-understood using only the
volume of
\mathcal{M}_{N}

[
5
]
. The vortices behave as
an interacting gas of finite-sized particles, where the pressure is
of the Clausius form

P=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}\,.

(1)

It was noted in
[
2
]
that this classical formula for the
pressure remains valid for vortices on surfaces
\Sigma
with arbitrary
underlying metric and having any genus. The quantum statistical mechanics of
vortices is less fully understood, and almost certainly has some
dependence on the metric on
\Sigma
. In
[
6
]
, the first
quantum correction to the classical partition function at high
temperatures
T\gg\hbar^{2}
, and hence to the free energy and pressure,
was found. The correction depends on the total scalar curvature of
the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{N}
. Because the temperature is high,
quantum effects are not dominant.

It is known that the metric on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{N}

simplifies as one approaches the Bradlow limit,
A=4\pi N
. The
regime where
A\gtrsim 4\pi N
is called the regime of dissolving
vortices. Here the magnetic field approaches a uniform, constant value
and the Higgs field is small. (At the Bradlow limit itself, the magnetic
field is uniform and the Higgs field vanishes everywhere, so the
vortices have dissolved.) For
N
dissolving vortices on a round
sphere, it was shown in
[
7
]
by approximate calculation that the moduli
space metric approaches the standard Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
,
scaled by
A-4\pi N
. Recently, García Lara and Speight
have made this argument more rigorous
[
8
]
, and they have also shown
that the Fubini–Study metric arises for any metric on
\Sigma
,
provided
\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
. The moduli space
forgets the underlying metric in this limit.

In this paper, we investigate the quantum statistical mechanics of the
vortex gas in this dissolving limit,
exploiting the spectrum of the Beltrami Laplacian for the
Fubini–Study metric. We assume that
A
is a fixed multiple of
N
,
slightly larger than
4\pi
, but there is no constraint on the
temperature
T
, so we can extend the earlier high temperature results
to lower temperatures. We calculate the quantum partition function

Z
, and derive the free energy
F

and pressure
P
of the vortex gas. For general
T
, this requires
solution of a single transcendental equation, which is straightforward
numerically, and we also derive explicit formulae for
F
and
P
in
three asymptotic regimes:
High
,
Intermediate

and
Low Temperature
. The results are collected in eqs.(
35
)
and (
36
) below. Remarkably, a phase crossover
occurs between
Intermediate
and
Low Temperature
, and in the

Low Temperature
regime the free energy
F
is no longer extensive,
but is proportional to
N^{2}
.

2
Quantum Partition Function

The classical Lagrangian for dynamics on the

N
-vortex moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{N}
, using real coordinates
q^{i}
, is

L=\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}(\mathbf{q})\dot{q^{i}}\dot{q^{j}}

(2)

where, for dissolving vortices,
g_{ij}=(A-4\pi N)G_{ij}
with
G_{ij}

the standard Fubini–Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{N}

[
7
,
8
]
.
Passing to the Hamiltonian, we have

H=\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}(\mathbf{q})p_{i}p_{j}

(3)

with
g^{ij}
the inverse metric and
p_{i}
the conjugate momentum to
q^{i}
.

The quantum Hamiltonian is taken to be

H=\frac{1}{2}\hbar^{2}\Delta

(4)

where
\Delta=-\nabla^{2}
is the Beltrami Laplacian
for the scaled Fubini–Study metric. Including the scale factor

A-4\pi N
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of
\Delta
and their
degeneracies
g_{k}
are
[
9
]

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{4k(N+k)}{A-4\pi N}\,,

\displaystyle g_{k}=\binom{N+k}{k}^{2}-\binom{N+k-1}{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{k^{2}}{(N+k)^{2}}\right)\binom{N+k}{k}^{2}=\frac{N(N+2k)}{(N+k)^{2}}\binom{N+k}{k}^{2}

(5)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\binom{N+k}{k}
is the
combinatorial symbol. The quantum partition function
Z
at
temperature
T
is therefore

\displaystyle Z(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}g_{k}\exp\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2T}\lambda_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\frac{N(N+2k)}{(N+k)^{2}}\binom{N+k}{k}^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2T}\frac{4k(N+k)}{A-4\pi N}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k}\frac{N(N+2k)}{(N+k)^{2}}\left(\frac{(N+k)!}{N!\,k!}\right)^{2}\exp\left(-zk\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)=Z(z)\,.

(6)

The key parameter here is the
reciprocal temperature

z
, defined as

z=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\pi T}\frac{4\pi N}{A-4\pi N}\,.

(7)

We will use various techniques to calculate

Z(z)
; the results are simpler when expressed in terms of
z
, but
are easily re-expressed in terms of
T
.

3
High and Intermediate Temperatures

It is well-known that sums like (
6
) may be approximated
for most
z
by a Gaussian integral. Before doing this,
we simplify (
6
) using
Stirling’s approximation
N!\simeq\sqrt{2\pi N}(\frac{N}{e})^{N}
to obtain

Z(z)=\sum_{k}\frac{N+2k}{N+k}\frac{1}{2\pi k}\frac{(N+k)^{2(N+k)}}{N^{2N}k^{2k}}\exp\left\{-zk\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}\,,

(8)

and reorganise this in exponential form as

\displaystyle Z(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\exp\biggl(N\left\{2\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)-2\frac{k}{N}\log\frac{k}{N}-z\frac{k}{N}\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\log(N + 2k)-\log(N + k)-\log(2\pi k)\biggr)\,.

(9)

The dominant terms are with
k=O(N)
for
z\ll 2\log N
, as we will see,
so
\log Z(z)=O(N)
and the
O(\log N)
terms in the exponent
can be dropped. Hence,

Z(z)\simeq\sum_{k}\exp\left(N\left\{2\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)-2\frac{k}{N}\log\frac{k}{N}-z\frac{k}{N}\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}\right)\,.

(10)

We now replace
\frac{k}{N}
by
x
and note that the spacing of the

x
-values is
\frac{1}{N}
, so we can rewrite the sum as the integral

Z(z)\simeq N\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(NG(x))\,dx\,,

(11)

where

G(x)=2(1+x)\log(1+x)-2x\log x-zx(1+x)

(12)

and the additional dependence of
G(x)
on the reciprocal temperature
z
is
implied.

G(x)
has a single maximum, with positive value; let us denote
its location by
x=x_{0}>0
. As
N
is large, it is valid to
approximate (
11
) as the Gaussian integral

\displaystyle Z(z)

\displaystyle\simeq N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(N\left\{G(x_{0})-\frac{G^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})}{2}(x-x_{0})^{2}\right\}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi N}{G^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})}}\ \exp(NG(x_0))\simeq\exp(NG(x_0))\,.

(13)

In the final expression we have again dropped a term
giving an
O(\log N)
error in
\log Z(z)
. The free energy

F
of the vortex gas is therefore

F=-T\log Z(z)=-NTG(x_{0})\,,

(14)

where the location
x_{0}
of the Gaussian peak is related to the
reciprocal temperature
z
via
G^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
. Explicitly, by differentiating
(
12
), we derive the relation

\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x_{0}}\right)=z\left(x_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(15)

It is straightforward to numerically solve (
15
) to find

x_{0}
and use (
12
) to determine
G(x_{0})
and
F
in terms of
z
, but
there are no exact formulae. Nevertheless, asymptotic formulae
for small and large
z
can be obtained from the asymptotic solutions
of (
15
),

\displaystyle x_{0}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{z}}\left(1+\frac{z}{24}+\frac{11z^{2}}{5760}+O(z^{3})\right)-\frac{1}{2}

for small
z

(16)

\displaystyle x_{0}=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}z}+(1-z)e^{-z}+O(e^{-\frac{3}{2}z})

for large

z
,

(17)

and from these we obtain

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle-NT\left[1-\log z+\frac{z}{6}-\frac{z^{2}}{360}+O(z^{3})\right]

for
0<z<5

(18)

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle-NT[2e^{-\frac{1}{2}z}+(1-z)e^{-z}+O(e^{-\frac{3}{2}z})]

for

5<z\ll 2\log N
.

(19)

The ranges of validity will be discussed below.
As
z
is the reciprocal temperature, eqs.(
18
) and
(
19
) correspond to the
High Temperature
and

Intermediate Temperature

regimes respectively. They extend the high temperature
result
[
6
]

F=-NT\left[1-\log z+\frac{z}{6}+O(z^{2})\right]\,,

(20)

which holds for arbitrary underlying
surfaces
1
1

1

In
[
6
]
the term
\frac{z}{6}

appeared incorrectly as
\frac{z}{12}
; this resulted from
misunderstanding factors of 2 in formulae for the total

. The formulae here are more precise and cover a
larger range of temperatures, but hold only for underlying

\Sigma
diffeomorphic to
S^{2}
and vortex densities

\frac{N}{A}
close to the Bradlow density
\frac{1}{4\pi}
.

The
Intermediate Temperature
asymptotic formulae have a limited
upper range of validity in
z
because (
17
) tells us
that the peak of the integrand in (
11
) is at

x_{0}\simeq e^{-\frac{z}{2}}
for large
z
. So the dominant terms
in (
10
) are at
k\simeq Ne^{-\frac{z}{2}}
,
implying that
k=O(N)
precisely when
z\ll 2\log N
. Therefore,
(
19
) appears to be valid only for

z\ll 2\log N
. In Section 4, we will see that its validity

, but the

regime

needs to be considered separately.

We are mainly interested in the pressure of the gas,
obtained using the general expressions

\displaystyle P=-\frac{\partial F}{\partial A}=-\frac{dF}{dz}\frac{\partial z}{\partial A}=NT\frac{dG}{dz}\frac{\partial z}{\partial A}=NT\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dz}\right)\Bigg|_{x=x_{0}}\frac{\partial z}{\partial A}\,.

(21)

x_{0}
is the maximum of
G(x)
for given
z
, so

\frac{\partial G}{\partial x}\big|_{x=x_{0}}=0
, and from formula
(
12
),
\frac{\partial G}{\partial z}\big|_{x=x_{0}}=-x_{0}(1+x_{0})
.
Also, from (
7
),
\frac{\partial z}{\partial A}=-\frac{z}{A-4\pi N}
. Therefore

P=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}[zx_{0}(1+x_{0})]\,.

(22)

Again, there is no exact formula for
P
in terms of
z
. Nevertheless, the
following asymptotic formulae for small and large
z
can be obtained
by using (
16
) and (
17
),

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}\left[1-\frac{z}{6}+\frac{z^{2}}{180}+O(z^{3})\right]

for
0<z<5

(23)

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}[ze^{-\frac{1}{2}z}+z(2-z)e^{-z}+O(e^{-\frac{3}{2}z})]

for
5<z\ll 2\log N
.

(24)

These correspond to the
High
and

Intermediate Temperature
regimes, and they extend the
high temperature result
[
6
]

P=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}\left[1-\frac{z}{6}+O(z^{2})\right]\,.

(25)

We defer their plots to Section 4 where the formula in the

Low Temperature
regime
z>2\log N
is also available.

4
Phase Crossover and Low Temperature Regime

Here, we derive the formulae that hold for
z>\log N
. They model a
phase crossover, from the
Intermediate
to the

Low Temperature
regime.

Recall the sum (
6
) defining the partition function

Z(z)
. For large reciprocal temperature
z
, the sum is dominated
by terms with
k<\sqrt{N}
, so

Z(z)\simeq\sum_{k}\frac{N^{2k}}{(k!)^{2}}\exp(-zk)\,.

(26)

This is the series for the modified Bessel function

I_{0}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})
. One can show that precisely when

z>\log N
, the terms in the series for
Z(z)
and for

I_{0}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})
are both negligible for
k>\sqrt{N}
.
So we can cut the tails and write

Z(z)\simeq I_{0}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})\,.

(27)

The free energy of the vortex gas is then

F=-T\log Z(z)=-NT\left(\frac{1}{N}\log I_{0}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})\right)\qquad\text{for $z>\log N$}\,.

(28)

Fig.
4.1
shows the reduced free energy
\widetilde{F}=F/(-NT)

on a logarithmic scale plotted against
z
for
N=10^{5}
,
10^{10}

and
10^{15}
. There is a rapid but smooth change in the slope for each
N
.

\widetilde{F}
against reciprocal

z
, showing the phase crossovers for
N=10^{5}
,
10^{10}

and
10^{15}

These phase crossovers take place approximately over the range

2\log N-3<z<2\log N+3
. They require a delicate balance
between
z
and
N
, so the finiteness of
N
is crucial.
Note that for
N=10^{5}
,
2\log N=23.0\gg 5
so the

Intermediate
to
Low Temperature
crossover is at much
lower temperature than the transition from the
High
to

Intermediate Temperature
regime, and the crossover is
at an even lower temperature for larger
N
.

There is a corresponding crossover in the pressure of the
gas. For
z>\log N
,

P=-\frac{\partial F}{\partial A}=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}\left[\frac{I_{1}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})}{I_{0}(2Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z})}\,ze^{-\frac{1}{2}z}\right]\,,

(29)

where the Bessel function
I_{1}
is the derivative of
I_{0}
. Since the
classical vortex gas has pressure

\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}

[
5
]
, it is natural to consider
the
reduced pressure

\widetilde{P}=P\,/\,(\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N})
,
the quantity in square brackets. This is shown in Fig.
4.2
.

We now clarify these results using
Low Temperature
asymptotic
formulae. For
z>2\log N
, we can simplify the partition function
(
27
) further. Introduce
\mu=Ne^{-\frac{1}{2}z}
, so

Z(z)=I_{0}(2\mu)
with
\mu<1
. The
series for
I_{0}(2\mu)
has terms
1,\mu^{2}
,
\frac{1}{4}\mu^{4}
,

\frac{1}{36}\mu^{6}
,
\dots
, which decrease super-geometrically.
Keeping just the leading terms gives

Z(z)=1+\mu^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\mu^{4}+\cdots\,.

(30)

Using
\log(1+\lambda)=\lambda-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+\cdots
,
the free energy and pressure in the
Low Temperature
regime become

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=-T\log(1+\mu^2+\frac{1}{4}\mu^4+\cdots)

\displaystyle=-T\left(\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\mu^{4}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=-NT\left[Ne^{-z}-\frac{1}{4}N^{3}e^{-2z}+\cdots\right]\,,

(31)

and

P=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}[Nze^{-z}-N^{3}ze^{-2z}+\cdots]\qquad\text{for $z>2\log N$}\,.

(32)

These
Low Temperature
formulae are interesting.
For fixed
z
, the free energy
F
is no longer extensive but is
proportional to
N^{2}
. This phenomenon violates classical
thermodynamics, which says
F
should be proportional to
N
, but it
only occurs below some temperature
T
inversely proportional to
\log N
.

In the region
z<2\log N
we can use the large-argument asymptotic
formulae

I_{0}(y)\sim\frac{e^{y}}{\sqrt{2\pi y}}\left(1+\frac{1}{8y}+\cdots\right)\,,\qquad I_{1}(y)\sim\frac{e^{y}}{\sqrt{2\pi y}}\left(1-\frac{3}{8y}+\cdots\right)

(33)

to show that

P=\frac{NT}{A-4\pi N}\left[ze^{-\frac{1}{2}z}-\frac{z}{4N}+\cdots\right]\,.

(34)

This matches the leading term in the pressure (
24
) in the

Intermediate Temperature
regime.

The reduced free energy
\widetilde{F}
for
N=10^{5}
and
z>\log N
is
shown in Fig.

), which is shown solid but is hardly

5
Conclusions

We have considered a quantized gas of
N
critically coupled Abelian
Higgs vortices on a surface diffeomorphic to
S^{2}

close to the Bradlow limit, where the vortex density is just below

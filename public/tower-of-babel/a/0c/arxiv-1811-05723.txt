The twisted forms of a semisimple group over an 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -curve
Rony A. Bitan, Ralf Köhl, Claudia Schoemann

Abstract
. Let C C be a smooth, projective and geometrically connected curve defined over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . Given a semisimple C − S C-S -group scheme G ¯ \underline{G} where S S is a finite set of closed points of C C , we describe the set of ( 𝒪 S \mathcal{O}_{S} -classes of) twisted forms of G ¯ \underline{G} in terms of geometric invariants of its fundamental group F ⁡ ( G ¯ ) F(\underline{G}) .

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a projective, smooth and geometrically connected curve defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
\Omega
be the set of all closed points on
C
.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega
let
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the induced discrete valuation
on the (global) function field
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(C)
,

\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the ring of integers in the completion
\hat{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
K
with respect to
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
and
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
the residue field.
Any finite subset
S\subset\Omega
gives rise to a
Dedekind scheme
,
namely, a Noetherian integral scheme of dimension
1
whose local rings are regular;
If
S
is
nonempty
it will be the spectrum of the Dedekind domain

\mathcal{O}_{S}:=\{x\in K:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\geq 0\ \forall\mathfrak{p}\notin S\}.

Otherwise, if
S=\emptyset
, the corresponding Dedekind scheme is the curve
C
itself,
and we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{S}
the structural sheaf of
C
.

Throughout this paper
\underline{G}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group scheme whose generic fiber
G:=\underline{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}K
is almost-simple,
and whose fiber
\underline{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\underline{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
at any
\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega-S
is
semisimple
,
namely, (connected) reductive over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
and the rank of its root system equals that of its lattice of weights (
[
12
, Exp. XIX Def. 2.7, Exp. XXI Def. 1.1.1]
).
Let
\underline{G}^{\text{sc}}
be the universal (central) cover (being simply-connected) of
\underline{G}
,
and suppose that its
fundamental group

F(\underline{G}):=\ker[\underline{G}^{\text{sc}}\twoheadrightarrow\underline{G}]
(cf.
[
10
, p.40]
) is of order prime to
\text{char}(K)
.

A
twisted form
of
\underline{G}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group that is isomorphic to
\underline{G}

over some finite étale cover of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.
We aim to describe explicitly –
in terms of some invariants of
F(\underline{G})
and the group of outer automorphisms of
\underline{G}
–
the finite set of all twisted forms of
\underline{G}
, modulo
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-isomorphisms.
This is done first in Section
2
for forms arising from the torsors of the adjoint group
\underline{G}^{\text{ad}}
,
and then in Section
3
,
through the action of the outer automorphisms of
\underline{G}
on its Dynkin diagram,
for all twisted forms.
More concrete computations
are provided in Sections
4
,
5
and
6
.
The case of type
A
deserves a special consideration,
this is done in Section
7
.
The Zariski topology is treated in Section
8
.

Before we start may we quote B. Conrad in the abstract of
[
11
]
:
“The study of such
\mathbb{Z}
-groups provides concrete applications of many facets
of the theory of reductive groups over rings (scheme of Borel subgroups, auto-morphism scheme, relative non-abelian cohomology, etc.),
and it highlights the role of number theory (class field theory, mass formulas, strong approximation,
point-counting over finite fields, etc.) in analyzing the possibilities”.

2.
Torsors

A
\underline{G}
-torsor
P
in the étale topology
is a sheaf of sets on
\mathcal{O}_{S}

equipped with a (right)
\underline{G}
-action, which is locally trivial in the étale topology,
namely, locally for the étale topology on
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, this action is isomorphic to the action of
G
on itself by translation.
The associated
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group scheme
{{}^{P}}\underline{G}
, being an inner form of
\underline{G}
,
is called the
twist
of
\underline{G}
by
P

(e.g.,
[
24
, §2.2, Lemma 2.2.3, Examples 1,2]
).
We define
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})
to be the set of isomorphism classes
of
\underline{G}
-torsors relative to the étale topology (or the flat one;
these two cohomology sets coincide when
\underline{G}
is smooth; cf.
[
2
, VIII Cor. 2.3]
).
This set is finite (
[
5
, Prop. 3.9]
).
The sets
H^{1}(K,G)
(denoting the Galois cohomology) and
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{G}_{\mathfrak{p}})
are defined similarly.

There exists a canonical map of pointed-sets:

\lambda:H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})\to H^{1}(K,G)\times\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

defined by
[X]\mapsto[(X\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\text{Spec}\,K)\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}X\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\text{Spec}\,\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}]
.
Let
[\xi_{0}]:=\lambda([\underline{G}])
.
The
principal genus
of
\underline{G}
is then
\ker(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}([\xi_{0}])
,
namely, the classes of
\underline{G}
-torsors that are generically and locally trivial at all points of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.
More generally, a
genus
of
\underline{G}
is any fiber
\lambda^{-1}([\xi])
where
[\xi]\in\operatorname{Im}(\lambda)
.
The
set of genera
of
\underline{G}
is then:

\text{gen}(\underline{G}):=\{\lambda^{-1}([\xi])\ :\ [\xi]\in\operatorname{Im}(\lambda)\},

hence
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})
is a disjoint union of all genera.

The ring of
S
-integral adèles
\mathbb{A}_{S}:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}\hat{K}_{\mathfrak{p}}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a subring of the adèles
\mathbb{A}
.
A
\underline{G}
-torsor
P=\text{Iso}(\underline{G},\underline{G}^{\prime})
belongs to the principal genus of
\underline{G}
if it is both
\mathbb{A}_{S}
- and
K
-trivial, hence the principal genus
bijects as a pointed-set to the
S
-
class set
of
\underline{G}
(see
[
21
, Thm. I.3.5]
):

\text{Cl}_{S}(\underline{G}):=\underline{G}(\mathbb{A}_{S})\backslash\underline{G}(\mathbb{A})/G(K).

Being finite (
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
), its cardinality, called the
S
-
class number
of
\underline{G}
, is denoted
h_{S}(\underline{G})
.
As
\underline{G}
is assumed to have connected fibers, by Lang’s Theorem (recall that all residue fields are finite) all
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p}},\underline{G}_{\mathfrak{p}})
vanish,
which indicates that any two
\underline{G}
-torsors share the same genus if and only if they are
K
-isomorphic.

The universal cover of
\underline{G}
forms a short exact sequence of étale
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-groups (cf.
[
10
, p.40]
):

(2.1)

1\to F(\underline{G})\to\underline{G}^{\text{sc}}\to\underline{G}\to 1.

This gives rise by étale cohomology to the co-boundary map of pointed sets:

(2.2)

\delta_{\underline{G}}:H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})\twoheadrightarrow H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}))

which is surjective by (
[
13
]
, Cor. 1) as
\mathcal{O}_{S}
is of
Douai-type

(see Def. 5.2 and Exam. 5.4(iii),(v) in
[
17
]
).
It follows from the fact that
H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{sc}})

(resp.,
H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{sc}})
) has only trivial classes
and in finite number (
[
13
]
, Thm. 1.1).

A representation
\rho:\underline{G}^{\text{sc}}\to\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A)
where
A
is an Azumaya
\mathcal{O}_{S}
algebra,
is said to be
center-preserving
if
\rho(Z(\underline{G})^{\text{sc}})\subseteq Z(\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A))
.
The restriction of
\rho
to
F(\underline{G})\subseteq Z(\underline{G}^{\text{sc}})
,
composed with the natural isomorphism
Z(\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A))\cong\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
,
is a map
\Lambda_{\rho}:F(\underline{G})\to\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
,
thus inducing a map:

Together with the preceding map

we get the map of pointed-sets:

(2.3)

(\Lambda_{\rho})_{*}\circ\delta_{\underline{G}}:H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})\to{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),

which associates any class of
\underline{G}
-torsors with a class of Azuamaya
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebras in
{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.

When
F(\underline{G})=\underline{\mu}_{m}
, the following composition is surjective:

(2.4)

w_{\underline{G}}:H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})\xrightarrow{\delta_{\underline{G}}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}))\xrightarrow{i_{*}^{(2)}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow{{}_{m}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S}),

and coincides with
(\Lambda_{\rho})_{*}\circ\delta_{\underline{G}}
.

3.
Twisted-forms

Before continuing with the classification of
\underline{G}
-forms,
we would like to recall the following general construction due to Giraud and prove one related Lemma.
Let
R
be a unital commutative ring.
A central exact sequence of étale
R
-group schemes:

(3.1)

1\to A\xrightarrow{i}B\xrightarrow{\pi}C\to 1

induces by étale cohomology a long exact sequence of pointed-sets (
[
16
, III, Lemma 3.3.1]
):

(3.2)

1\to A(R)\to B(R)\to C(R)\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,A)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,B)\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,C)

in which
C(R)
acts ”diagonally“ on the elements of
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,A)
in the following way:
For
c\in C(R)
, a preimage
X
of
c
under
B\to C
is a
A
-bitorsor, i.e.,
X=bA=Ab
for some
b\in B(R^{\prime})
,
where
R^{\prime}
is a finite étale extension of
R
(
[
16
, III, 3.3.3.2]
).
Then given
[P]\in H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,A)
:

(3.3)

c*P=P\overset{A}{\wedge}X=(P\times X)/(pa,a^{-1}x).

The exactness of (
3.2
) implies that
B(R)\xrightarrow{\pi}C(R)
is surjective if and only if
\ker(i_{*})=1
.
This holds true starting with any twisted form
{{}^{P}}B
of
B
,
[P]\in H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,A)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following are equivalent:

Remark 6.1

, Cor. 3.2]

When

we have

.
In particular, if

and

such that the number of places in

E. Artin in

calls a Galois extension

.

\mathcal{O}_{S}

Lemma 6.2
.

If
R
is imaginary over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
and

is prime to
[R:\mathcal{O}_{S}]
, then
{{}_{m}}{\mathrm{Br}}(R)={{}_{m}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.

Proof.

If
S=\emptyset
and

is imaginary then
{\mathrm{Br}}(R)={\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})=1
.
Otherwise, the composition of the induced norm

with the diagonal morphism coming from the Weil restriction

\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{S}}\to\text{Res}_{R/\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R})\xrightarrow{N_{R/\mathcal{O}_{S}}}\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{S}}

is the multiplication by

.
It induces together with the Shapiro Lemma the maps:

H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{S}})\to H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(R,\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R})\xrightarrow{N^{(2)}}H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\mathcal{O}_{S}})

whose composition is the multiplication by

Identifying

thus an automorphism when

.
This means that

is a subgroup of
{{}_{m}}{\mathrm{Br}}(R)
.
As

is imaginary over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, it is obtained by removing

points from the projective curve defining its fraction field,

∎

has a trivial discriminant on a vector space
V
,
the Tits algebras of the group are
\text{End}(V)
and the two components
\textbf{C}_{+}(Q),\textbf{C}_{-}(Q)
of the even Clifford algebra of
Q
,
and the triality automorphism cyclically permutes those three.
More generally, if the group is represented as
\textbf{PGO}^{+}(A,\sigma)

for some orthogonal involution of trivial discriminant on a central simple algebra A of degree
8
,
triality automorphisms permute
A
and the two components of the Clifford algebra
\textbf{C}(A,\sigma)
;
Altogether we finally get:

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
\underline{G}
be of (absolute) type
\text{D}_{4}
being simply-connected or adjoint.

For any
[P]\in H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Theta)
let
R_{P}
be the corresponding étale extension of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.
Then:

\displaystyle\textbf{Twist}(\underline{G})

\displaystyle\cong(\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})/2\times{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))^{2}

\displaystyle\coprod_{1\neq[P]\in{{}_{2}}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Theta)}\text{Pic\penalty\ }(R_{P})/2\times{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(R_{P})

\displaystyle\coprod_{1\neq[P]\in{{}_{3}}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\Theta)}\ker(\text{Pic\penalty\ }(R_{P})/2\to\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})/2)\times(\ker({{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(R_{P})\to{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})))/\Theta(\mathcal{O}_{S}).

If
R_{P}
is imaginary over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
, then
\ker({{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(R_{P})\to{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S}))=1
.

7.
The anisotropic case

Now suppose that
\underline{G}
does admit a twisted form such that the generic fiber of its universal covering is anisotropic at
S
.
As previously mentioned, such group must be of absolute type
A
and
S\neq\emptyset
.
Over a local field
k
, an outer form of a group of type
{{}^{1}}\text{A}
which is anisotropic, must be the special unitary group arising by some hermitian form
h

in
r
variables over a quadratic extension of
k
or over a quaternion
k
-algebra (
[
27
, §4.4]
).

A
unitary

\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group is
\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma):=\textbf{Iso}(B,\sigma)

where
B
is a non-split quaternion Azumaya defined over an étale quadratic extension
R
of
\mathcal{O}_{S}

and
\sigma
is a unitary involution on
B
, i.e., whose restriction to the center
R
is not the identity.
The
special unitary group
is the kernel of the reduced norm:

\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma):=\ker({\mathrm{Nrd}}:\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\tau)\twoheadrightarrow\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(R)).

These are of relative type
{{}^{2}}\text{C}_{2m}
(
m\geq 2
) (
[
27
]
, loc. sit.)
and isomorphic over
R
to type
{{}^{1}}\text{A}_{2m-1}
.

So in order to determine exactly when
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{sc}})

does not
vanish,
we may restrict ourselves to
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-groups whose universal covering is either
\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)

or
\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma)
.
In the first case, the reduced norm applied to the units of
A
forms the short exact sequence of smooth
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-groups:

(7.1)

1\to\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)\to\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A)\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{Nrd}}}\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to 1.

Then étale cohomology gives rise to the long exact sequence:

(7.2)

1\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}\big/{\mathrm{Nrd}}(A^{\times})\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))\xrightarrow{i_{*}}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A))\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{Nrd}}_{*}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})

in which
{\mathrm{Nrd}}_{*}
is surjective since
\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)

is simply-connected and
\mathcal{O}_{S}
is of Douai-type (see above).

Definition 4
.

We say that the
local-global Hasse principle
holds for
\underline{G}
if
h_{S}(\underline{G})=|\text{Cl}_{S}(\underline{G})|=1
.

Thus the Hasse principle says that an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group is
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-isomorphic to
\underline{G}

if and only if it is
K
-isomorphic to it. This is automatic for simply-connected groups which are not of type
A
or when
S=\emptyset
for which by Lemma
2.2

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G})\cong H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}))

is trivial.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
\underline{G}=\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)
where
A
is a quaternion
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra.

(1)

If
{\mathrm{Nrd}}:A^{\times}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
is not surjective, then the Hasse principle does not hold for
\underline{G}
.

(2)

If the generic fiber
G
is isotropic at
S
, then
\textbf{Twist}(\underline{G})
is in bijection as a pointed-set with the abelian group
\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})/2\times{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.

Proof.

(1)
The generic fiber
\textbf{SL}_{1}(A)
is simply-connected thus due to Harder
H^{1}(K,\textbf{SL}_{1}(A))=1
,
which indicates that
\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)
admits a single genus (cf. Section
2
),
i.e.,
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))
is equal to
\text{Cl}_{S}(\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))
.
By the exactness of sequence (
7.2
),
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))
cannot vanish if
{\mathrm{Nrd}}(A^{\times})\neq\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}
.

(2) Being of type
\text{A}_{1}
,
\underline{G}=\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)
does not admit a non-trivial outer form,
which implies that
\textbf{Twist}(\underline{G})=H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{ad}})
.
The short exact sequence of the universal covering of
\underline{G}^{\text{ad}}=\underline{\textbf{PGL}}_{1}(A)
with fundamental group
\underline{\mu}_{2}
,
induces the long exact sequence (cf. (
2.2
)):

H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{PGL}}_{1}(A))\xrightarrow{\delta_{\underline{G}^{\text{ad}}}}\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\mu}_{2})

in which since
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A))
is trivial (due to strong approximation when
G
is isotropic at
S
),
the rightmost term is isomorphic by Lemma
2.3
to
\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})/2\times{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
.
∎

Example 7.2
.

Let
C
be the projective line defined over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
and
S=\{t,t^{-1}\}
.
Then
K=\mathbb{F}_{3}(t)
and
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathbb{F}_{3}[t,t^{-1}]
.
For the quaternion
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-algebra
A=(i^{2}=-1,j^{2}=-t)_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}
we get:

\forall x,y,z,w\in\mathcal{O}_{S}:\ {\mathrm{Nrd}}(x+yi+zj+wk)=x^{2}+y^{2}+t(z^{2}+w^{2})

which shows that
{\mathrm{Nrd}}(A^{\times})=\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\times}=\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times}\cdot t^{n},n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As
\mathcal{O}_{S}
is a UFD, the Hasse principle holds for
\underline{G}=\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)
,
though its generic fiber
G\cong\mathrm{Spin}_{q}
for
q(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}+tz^{2}
is anisotropic at
S
(cf.
[
19
, Lemma 6]
).
Also as
|{{}_{2}}{\mathrm{Br}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})|=2^{|S|-1}=2
we have two distinct classes in
\textbf{Twist}(\underline{G})
, namely,
[\underline{G}]
and
[\underline{G}^{\text{op}}]
.

Similarly, applying étale cohomology to the exact sequence of smooth
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-groups:

1\to\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma)\to\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma)\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{Nrd}}}\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(R)\to 1

induces the exactness of:

1\to R^{\times}/{\mathrm{Nrd}}(\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma)(\mathcal{O}_{S}))\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma))\to H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma))\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{Nrd}}_{*}}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\textbf{Aut}(R)).

Let
A=D(B,\sigma)
be the discriminant algebra.
If
R
splits, namely,
R\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}\times\mathcal{O}_{S}
, then
B\cong A\times A^{\text{op}}
and
\sigma
is the exchange involution.
Then
\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma)\cong\underline{\textbf{GL}}_{1}(A)
and
\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma)\cong\underline{\textbf{SL}}_{1}(A)
,
so we are back in the previous situation.

Corollary 7.3
.

If
\underline{\textbf{U}}(B,\sigma)(\mathcal{O}_{S})\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{Nrd}}}R^{\times}
is not surjective then the Hasse-principle does not hold for
\underline{\textbf{SU}}(B,\sigma)
.

8.
In the Zariski topology

A
\underline{G}
-torsor
P
is
Zariski
,
if the twisted form
{{}^{P}}\underline{G}
is generically and locally everywhere away of
S
isomorphic to
\underline{G}
,
i.e., if it belongs to the principal genus of
\underline{G}
(see Section
2
).
Let
\underline{G}_{0}
be a quasi-split semisimple
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group with an almost-simple generic fiber.
The continuous morphism between the categories of open subsets of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
:

(\mathcal{O}_{S})_{\text{\'{e}t}}\to(\mathcal{O}_{S})_{\text{Zar}}
results – given a variety
X
defined over
\mathcal{O}_{S}
–
in the opposite inclusion of cohomology sets
H^{r}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},X)\subseteq H^{r}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},X)
for all
r>0
.
The restriction of the decomposition (
3.6
)

(8.1)

\textbf{Twist}(\underline{G_{0}})\cong H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\mathrm{Aut}(\underline{G}_{0}))\cong\coprod_{[P]}H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},{{}^{P}}(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}))/\Theta(\mathcal{O}_{S})

to Zariski torsors gives (compare with
[
18
, p.181]
):

(8.2)

\displaystyle\textbf{Twist}_{\text{Zar}}(\underline{G}_{0})\cong H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\mathrm{Aut}(\underline{G}_{0}))\cong H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{ad}}_{0})/\Theta(\mathcal{O}_{S}).

But as aforementioned,
H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}^{\text{ad}}_{0})
is equal to the principal genus of
\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}

on which the action of
\Theta(\mathcal{O}_{S})
is trivial, hence (
8.2
) refines to:

(8.3)

\textbf{Twist}_{\text{Zar}}(\underline{G}_{0})\cong H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}).

Moreover, restricting the bijection
H^{1}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}})\cong H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}))
(Lemma
2.2
)
to the Zariski topology,
H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}})
can be replaced with
H^{2}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}))
.
All twisted forms of
\underline{G}_{0}
in the Zariski topology being
K
-isomorphic are isotropic, so this time this includes groups of type
A
.
Suppose
F(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}})
is admissible, splitting over an étale extension
R
of
\mathcal{O}_{S}
.
Then
H^{1}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\cong\text{Pic\penalty\ }(\mathcal{O}_{S})
while as
R
is locally factorial
H^{2}_{\text{Zar}}(R,\underline{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is trivial (
[
8
, Remark 3.5.1]
)
thus
i(F(\underline{G}_{0}))
as well (see Definition
2
).
Hence similarly as was done for
H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}))
in Lemma
2.3
,
we get that
H^{2}_{\text{Zar}}(\mathcal{O}_{S},F(\underline{G}_{0}^{\text{ad}}))\cong j(F(\underline{G}_{0}))
.

Corollary 8.1
.

Let
\underline{G}_{0}
be a semisimple
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-group with an almost-simple generic fiber and an admissible fundamental group.
Then:
\textbf{Twist}_{\text{Zar}}(\underline{G}_{0})\cong j(F(\underline{G}_{0}))
.

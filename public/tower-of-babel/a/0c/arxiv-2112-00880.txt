Random Polynomials in Several Complex Variables
T. Bayraktar, T. Bloom, N. Levenberg

Abstract
We generalize some previous results on random polynomials in several complex variables. A standard setting is to consider random polynomials H n ​ ( z ) := ∑ j = 1 m n a j ​ p j ​ ( z ) H_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{j}p_{j}(z) that are linear combinations of basis polynomials { p j } \{p_{j}\} with i.i.d. complex random variable coefficients { a j } \{a_{j}\} where { p j } \{p_{j}\} form an orthonormal basis for a Bernstein-Markov measure on a compact set K ⊂ ℂ d K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d} . Here m n m_{n} is the dimension of 𝒫 n \mathcal{P}_{n} , the holomorphic polynomials of degree at most n n in ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} . We consider more general bases { p j } \{p_{j}\} , which include, e.g., higher-dimensional generalizations of Fekete polynomials. Moreover we allow H n ​ ( z ) := ∑ j = 1 m n a n ​ j ​ p n ​ j ​ ( z ) H_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{nj}p_{nj}(z) ; i.e., we have an array of basis polynomials { p n ​ j } \{p_{nj}\} and random coefficients { a n ​ j } \{a_{nj}\} . This always occurs in a weighted situation. We prove results on convergence in probability and on almost sure convergence of 1 n ​ log ⁡ | H n | \frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}| in L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ℂ d ) L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{C}^{d}) to the (weighted) extremal plurisubharmonic function for K K . We aim for weakest possible sufficient conditions on the random coefficients to guarantee convergence.



Keywords: random polynomials, asymptotically Chebyshev polynomials, asymptotically (weighted) Bernstein-Markov measures

Introduction

We will consider random polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
of degree at most
n
of the form

H_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{nj}p_{nj}(z)

(1.1)

where
\{a_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n};\ n=1,2,...}
are an array of i.i.d. random variables;
\{p_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
form a basis for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, the holomorphic polynomials of degree at most
n
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
; and
m_{n}=
dim
(\mathcal{P}_{n}
). The random variables
a_{nj}
are defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{P})
. This occurs naturally in a weighted setting; c.f., subsection 2.2. A special case is when

H_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{j}p_{j}(z)

(1.2)

where the
\{a_{j}\}
are a sequence of i.i.d. complex random variables with a basis
\{p_{1},...,p_{m_{n}}\}
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. We always assume the random variables are nondegenerate; i.e., their distribution is not supported at a single point. In the situation where one starts with
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact and
\mu
a probability measure on
K
such that
||p||_{L^{2}(\mu)}=0
for a polynomial
p
implies
p\equiv 0
, a well-studied situation is to let
\{p_{j}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
in (
1.2
) be an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mu)
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
constructed via Gram-Schmidt applied to a monomial basis of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. In this setting, convergence of the sequence of (deterministic) plurisubharmonic (psh) functions
\{\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)\}
, where
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{j}(z)|^{2}
, to the extremal psh function
V_{K}
associated to
K
plays a major role.

We aim to generalize some of the results occurring in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
on asymptotics of normalized zero measures and currents associated to
H_{n}
in two ways. First, we allow our polynomial bases to be more general; they need not come from orthonormal polynomials associated to a single measure
\mu
on
K
. For example, we allow a basis
\{p_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
for

to come from a sequence of
asymptotically (weighted) Bernstein-Markov measures

\{\mu_{n}\}
for
K
(with weight

). In the sequence case, we allow

which are
asymptotically Chebyshev
for
K
. Secondly, we aim for the weakest conditions on the random variables
a_{j}
or
a_{nj}
in order to have the appropriate convergence as, e.g., in
[
11
]
and
[
7
]

For the classical Kac ensemble
H_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}z^{j}=a_{n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-z_{nk})
in one variable, Ibragimov and Zaporozhets
[
13

showed that the condition
\mathbb{E}(\log(1+|a_{j}))<\infty
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the sequence

to converge weakly almost surely to normalized arclength measure
\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta
on the unit circle

. This is the potential-theoretic equilibrium measure
\mu_{T}
of

; and the monomials
\{z^{j}\}
are an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}
of the same measure. Kabluchko and Zaporozhets
[
14
]
proved some results for convergence in probability of
\mu_{H_{n}}\to\mu_{T}
weakly. Pritsker and Ramachandran
[
17
]
gave some almost sure convergence results for special polynomial bases on certain compacta in
\mathbb{C}
. Bloom and Dauvergne
[
7
]
showed that for a regular compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
with equilibrium measure
\mu_{K}
, given a Bernstein-Markov measure
\mu
on

and an orthonormal basis of polynomials
\{p_{j}\}
for

weakly in probability. Dauvergne generalized these univariate results in
[
11

for
K

and essentially all “classical” sets of polynomials (Chebyshev, Fekete, Fejer, etc.), the condition

\mathbb{P}(|a_{j}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|)
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the sequence
\mu_{H_{n}}\to\mu_{K}

We work in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
with

, building upon earlier work of Shiffman and Zelditch

[

from
[

[
11
]

Deterministic results

2.1
Asymptotically Chebyshev polynomials

As in the introduction we let
m_{n}=\
dim
(\mathcal{P}_{n}
). Let

L(\mathbb{C}^{d}):=\{u\in PSH(\mathbb{C}^{d}):u(z)\leq\log|z|+0(1),\ |z|\to\infty\},

the Lelong class of psh functions of minimal growth. Given a nonpluripolar compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
, let

V_{K}(z):=\sup\{u(z):u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ u\leq 0\ \hbox{on}\ K\}

(2.1)

and
V_{K}^{*}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}V_{K}(\zeta)
. Then
V_{K}^{*}\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and it is known that

V_{K}(z)=\sup\{\frac{1}{deg(p)}\log|p(z)|:p\in\cup_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n},\ ||p||_{K}\leq 1\}.

In particular,
V_{K}=V_{\widehat{K}}
where
\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:|p(z)|\leq||p||_{K},\ p\in\cup_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}\}
. Moreover, given a
Bernstein-Markov
(BM) measure
\mu
on
K
, i.e.,
||p_{n}||_{K}\leq M_{n}||p_{n}||_{L^{2}(\mu)}
for
p_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
with
M_{n}^{1/n}\to 1
, it is straightforward to see that for the sequence of Bergman functions
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{j}(z)|^{2}
, where
\{p_{j}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, we have
\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)\to V_{K}
pointwise on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Moreover the convergence is locally uniform if
K
is regular; i.e., if
V_{K}
is continuous (cf. Proposition 3.1,
[
8
]
). We generalize this in Propositions
2.8
and
2.9
in subsection 2.2. For more on (BM) measures, see
[
6
]
.

For simplicity, throughout this subsection we assume our compact sets are regular. We fix an ordering
\prec
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
so that
\alpha\prec\beta
if
|\alpha|\leq|\beta|
. Writing

e_{i}(z)=z^{\alpha}=z^{\alpha(i)}:=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}

P(\alpha)=P(\alpha(i)):=\{e_{i}(z)+\sum_{j<i}c_{j}e_{j}(z):c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\}=\{z^{\alpha(i)}+\sum_{\beta\prec\alpha(i)}c_{\beta}z^{\beta}\}.

Given a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
we define the Chebyshev constants

\tau_{\alpha(i)}(K):=\inf\{||p||_{K}:p\in P(\alpha(i))\}.

Note this generalizes the univariate notion; if
d=1
and
K\subset\mathbb{C}
, the Chebyshev polynomial of degree
j
for
K
is the monic polynomial
t_{j}(z)=z^{j}+...
of degree

of minimal supremum norm on
K
among all such polynomials.
Following
[
19
]
, we use these to define
directional Chebyshev constants
. We have
\frac{\alpha(i)}{|\alpha(i)|}\in\Sigma
, the standard

(d-1)-
simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\Sigma=\{\theta=(\theta_{1},...,\theta_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{j=1}^{d}\theta_{j}=1,\ \theta_{j}\geq 0,\ j=1,...,d\}.

Let
\Sigma^{o}:=\{\theta\in\Sigma:\ \theta_{j}>0,\ j=1,...,d\}
.
Zaharjuta
[
19
]
showed that for
\theta\in\Sigma^{o}
, the limit

\tau(K,\theta):=\lim_{|\alpha(i)|\to\infty,\ \frac{\alpha(i)}{|\alpha(i)|}\to\theta}\tau_{\alpha(i)}(K)^{1/|\alpha(i)|}\ \hbox{exists};

(2.2)

and we call
\tau(K,\theta)
the directional Chebyshev constant of
K
in the direction
\theta
.

Following
[
4
]
, given
\theta\in\Sigma^{o}
, we call a sequence of polynomials

,

\theta-
asymptotically Chebyshev for

\lim_{k\to\infty,\ k\in Y_{\theta}}deg(q_{k})=\infty,\ \lim_{k\to\infty,\ k\in Y_{\theta}}\beta(k)/|\beta(k)|=\theta,

and

\lim_{k\to\infty,\ k\in Y_{\theta}}||q_{k}||_{K}^{1/|\beta(k)|}=\tau(K,\theta).

If we need to specify the multiindex and degree we will write
q_{j}
as

where
|\alpha|=n=deg(q_{n,\alpha})
. Here the ordering
\prec

.

Definition 2.1
.

We call the sequence
\{q_{j}\}_{j}

asymptotically Chebyshev for

\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus\widehat{K}

D_{r}
is nonpluripolar. Starting with
D_{1}
, choose
w_{1}\in D_{1}
so that (
2.7
) holds at
w_{1}
. Then for some subsequence of pairs
(n_{1},\alpha_{1}),(n_{2},\alpha_{2}),...
with
n_{j}\in Y
we have

\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{s}}\log|p_{n_{s},\alpha_{s}}(w_{1})|=V_{K}(w_{1})

where we can assume
n_{j}\leq n_{j+1}
. Let
Y_{1}=\{n_{1},n_{2},...\}\subset Y
. We claim that

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{1}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{1})=V_{K}(w_{1}).

(2.8)

Here (
2.8
) follows from (
2.5
), the previous equality, and the elementary fact that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,

\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)\geq\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n,\alpha}(z)|.

We now repeat this argument with a point
w_{2}\in D_{2}
to get a subsequence
Y_{2}\subset Y_{1}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{2}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{2})=V_{K}(w_{2}).

Continuing, we get a countable family of nested subsequences
Y\supset Y_{1}\supset Y_{2}...
and points
w_{r}\in D_{r}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{r}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{r})=V_{K}(w_{r}).

Then using a diagonalization argument, we get a sequence
Y_{0}
which is a subsequence of each
Y_{r}
(except for the first
r
terms) so that
Y_{0}\subset Y
and for each
r=1,2,...
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{0}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{r})=V_{K}(w_{r}).

∎

Remark 2.4

Suppose

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a sequence of subharmonic functions on

[\limsup_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w)]^{*}\leq u(w),\ w\in D

in

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w_{r})=u(w_{r}),\ r=1,2,...

Then

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\{q_{j}\}_{j}
be
Z-
asymptotically Chebyshev for

\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}\to V_{K}
in

Proof.

We show that for any subsequence

(2.9)

For this it suffices to observe that for any subsequence

[\limsup_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)]^{*}\leq V_{K}(z),\ z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}

(2.10)

2.2
Local uniform convergence of
\{\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}
}

In the case where
q_{j}\in P(\alpha(j))
is the
L^{2}(\mu)-
minimal (orthogonal) polynomial in
P(\alpha(j))
for a regular compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
where
\mu
is (BM), we actually have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)=V_{K}(z)
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
where
p_{j}=q_{j}/||q_{j}||_{K}
. Indeed, in this subsection, we analyze more general conditions under which we have such a conclusion. We call a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}

locally regular
if for any
z_{0}\in K
,
V_{K\cap\overline{B(z_{0},r)}}
is continuous at
z_{0}
for
r>0
where
B(z_{0},r):=\{z:|z-z_{0}|<r\}
. We also let
Q
be a continuous, real-valued function on
K
(a continuous
weight
). The weighted extremal function for
K,Q
is defined as

V_{K,Q}(z):=\sup\{u(z):u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ u\leq Q\ \hbox{on}\ K\}.

It is known that

1.

for
K
locally regular and
Q
continuous,
V_{K,Q}
is continuous on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
(cf.,
[
15
]
); and

2.

for
K
compact,

V_{K,Q}(z)=\sup\{\frac{1}{deg(p)}\log|p(z)|:p\in\cup_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n},\ ||pe^{-deg(p)\cdot Q}||_{K}\leq 1\}.

Given
K
and
Q
, for each
n=1,2,...
define

\phi_{n}(z):=\sup\{|p(z)|:p\in\mathcal{P}_{n},\ \|pe^{-nQ}\|_{K}\leq 1\}.

If
K
is locally regular and
Q
is continuous, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\phi_{n}(z)=V_{K,Q}(z)

(2.11)

locally uniformly on
\mathbb{C}^{d}

[
5
]
. The conclusion remains true if
Q=0
(the unweighted case) for
K
(globally) regular, i.e., when
V_{K}
is continuous. For
n
fixed we consider a basis
\{p_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
normalized so that
||p_{nj}e^{-nQ}||_{K}=1
. Then
|p_{nj}(z)|\leq\phi_{n}(z)
so that

B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}\leq m_{n}\cdot[\phi_{n}(z)]^{2}.

(2.12)

Now assume that
\{p_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
is an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
where
\mu_{n}
is a probability measure on
K
. Let
M_{n}
be the smallest constant such that

||qe^{-nQ}||_{K}\leq M_{n}||q||_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}=M_{n}||qe^{-nQ}||_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}\ \hbox{for all}\ q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}.

Note then
||p_{nj}||^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}\geq 1/M_{n},\ j=1,...,m_{n}
. Let
p
be a polynomial of degree at most
n
with
||pe^{-nQ}||_{K}\leq 1
. Writing
p(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{j}\frac{p_{nj}(z)}{||p_{nj}||_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}}
,

|p(z)|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|a_{j}|^{2}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\frac{|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}}{||p_{nj}||^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}}=||p||_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}^{2}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\frac{|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}}{||p_{nj}||^{2}_{L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})}}

\leq||pe^{-nQ}||_{K}^{2}\cdot M_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}\leq M_{n}B_{n}(z).

Since
\phi_{n}(z)=\sup\{|p(z)|:p\in\mathcal{P}_{n},\ \|pe^{-nQ}\|_{K}\leq 1\}
, taking the supremum over all such
p
gives

[\phi_{n}(z)]^{2}\leq M_{n}B_{n}(z).

(2.13)

From the above discussion, using (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we have the following result.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a locally regular compact set, let
Q
be a continuous, real-valued function on
K
, and let
\{\mu_{n}\}
be a sequence of probability measures on
K
such that, for
n=1,2,...
, we have

||qe^{-nQ}||_{K}\leq M_{n}||qe^{-nQ}||_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}\ \hbox{for all}\ q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\ \hbox{with}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}^{1/n}=1.

(2.14)

Let
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}
where
\{p_{n1},...,p_{nm_{n}}\}
is an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(e^{-2nQ}\mu_{n})
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
with
||p_{nj}e^{-nQ}||_{K}=1
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)=V_{K,Q}\ \hbox{locally uniformly on}\ \mathbb{C}^{d}.

We will call a sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
satisfying (
2.14
)
asymptotically weighted Bernstein-Markov
(BM) for
K
and
Q
. For the unweighted setting (
Q=0
) we have the corresponding result.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a regular compact set and let
\{\mu_{n}\}
be a sequence of probability measures on
K
such that, for
n=1,2,...
, we have

||q||_{K}\leq M_{n}||q||_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}\ \hbox{for all}\ q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\ \hbox{with}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}M_{n}^{1/n}=1.

(2.15)

Let
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}
where
\{p_{n1},...,p_{nm_{n}}\}
is an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(\mu_{n})
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
with
||p_{nj}||_{K}=1
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z)=V_{K}\ \hbox{locally uniformly on}\ \mathbb{C}^{d}.

We call
\{\mu_{n}\}
satisfying (
2.15
)
asymptotically Bernstein-Markov
(BM) for
K
. Here are some examples of such measures and corresponding basis polynomials
\{p_{n1},...,p_{nm_{n}}\}
.

1.

Let
K
be regular and for each
n
, let
\zeta_{n1},...,\zeta_{nm_{n}}\in K
be a set of
Fekete points of order
n
for
K
, i.e.,

|VDM(\zeta_{n1},...,\zeta_{nm_{n}})|=\max_{\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{m_{n}}\in K}|VDM(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{m_{n}})|.

(2.16)

Define
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{m_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\delta_{\zeta_{nj}}
. Then the collection of fundamental Lagrange interpolating polynomials
l_{m_{n}1},...,l_{m_{n}m_{n}}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
for
\zeta_{n1},...,\zeta_{nm_{n}}
, i.e.,
l_{m_{n}j}(\zeta_{nk})=\delta_{jk}
, satisfy
||l_{m_{n}j}||_{K}=1
from (
2.16
), and, by construction, these polynomials are an orthogonal basis in
L^{2}(\mu_{n})
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. Note that
||l_{m_{n}j}||_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}_{n}}
. For any
q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
,

q(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}q(\zeta_{nj})l_{m_{n}j}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}_{n}}q(\zeta_{nj})\sqrt{m_{n}}l_{m_{n}j}(z)

so that

||q||_{K}\leq||q||_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}\cdot m_{n}\sqrt{m_{n}}.

Thus, from Proposition
2.9
, setting
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|l_{m_{n}j}(z)|^{2}
, we have
\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}\to V_{K}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

2.

Generalizing the previous example, given
K
regular, for each
n
, let
\zeta_{n1},...,\zeta_{nm_{n}}\in K
be a set of points in
K
such that the corresponding sequence of Lebesgue constants

\Lambda_{\alpha(m_{n})}=\sup_{z\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|l_{m_{n}j}(z)|\ \hbox{satisfy}\ \lim_{n\to\infty}\Lambda_{\alpha(m_{n})}^{1/n}=1.

For Fekete points,
\Lambda_{\alpha(m_{n})}\leq m_{n}
. A modification of the above argument for Fekete points shows that in this setting,
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{m_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}\delta_{\zeta_{nj}}
are asymptotically (BM) for

and hence that
\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}\to V_{K}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
where
B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|l_{m_{n}j}(z)|^{2}
.

3.

Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{n}\}_{n=1,...}
be a
weakly admissible mesh
for
K
where
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{a_{n1},...,a_{ns_{n}}\}\in K
. This means that
s_{n}^{1/n}\to 1
and
||p_{n}||_{K}\leq c_{n}||p_{n}||_{\mathcal{A}_{n}}
where
c_{n}^{1/n}\to 1
. Then the canonical measures
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{s_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{s_{n}}\delta_{ns_{j}}

. Here, since generally
s_{n}>>m_{n}
, one constructs the orthogonal polynomials
\{p_{n1},...,p_{nm_{n}}\}
in
L^{2}(\mu_{n})
for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
with
||p_{nj}||_{K}=1
in a standard fashion, e.g., Gram-Schmidt.

This last example is due to F. Piazzon who introduced the notion of asymptotically Bernstein-Markov measures for
K

[
16
]
. For more on admissible meshes, c.f.,
[
10
]
.

We remark that Zaharjuta

]
used (

) to prove that the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}|VDM(\zeta_{n1},...,\zeta_{nm_{n}})|^{1/l_{n}}=:d(K)\ \hbox{(transfinite diameter of $K$)}

3
Convergence in probability

We first consider random polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
of degree at most
n
of the form (
1.1
); i.e.,

H_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{nj}p_{nj}(z)

where
\{a_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n};\ n=1,2,...}
are an
array
of i.i.d. random variables and
\{p_{nj}\}_{j=1,...,m_{n}}
are a basis for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. We work in the corresponding probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},{\mathbb{P}})
. Sometimes to emphasize the random nature of our objects, we will include the variable
\omega\in\Omega
; e.g., we may write
a_{j}(\omega),\ H_{n}(z,\omega)
, etc. We assume
p_{n1}\equiv 1
and we define, for
n=1,2,...
, the function

B_{n}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}|p_{nj}(z)|^{2}.

(3.1)

We recall the definition of the
concentration function

\mathcal{Q}
of a complex-valued random variable
X
:
\mathcal{Q}(X,r):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}(X\in B(z,r))
. This satisfies the following elementary properties:

1.

for any such
X
and
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathcal{Q}(aX,r)=\mathcal{Q}(X,r/|a|)
;

2.

if
X
are
Y
are independent,
\mathcal{Q}(X+Y,r)\leq\mathcal{Q}(X,r)
.

If, e.g.,
X
has a bounded density (with respect to Lebesgue measure), then
\mathcal{Q}(X)\leq Cr^{2}
for some
C>0
.
We state our convergence in probability results in this array setting in
\mathbb{C}^{d},\ d\geq 1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{a_{nj}\}
be an array of i.i.d. random variables. We assume that

(i)

\mathbb{P}(|a_{nj}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
and

(ii)

\mathcal{Q}(a_{nj};r)\to 0\ \hbox{as}\ r\to 0
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be compact and regular. Suppose that that
||p_{nj}||_{K}\leq 1,\ j=1,...,m_{n};n=1,2,...
and with
\{B_{n}\}
in (
3.1
), given any subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
, there is a further subsequence
Y_{0}\subset Y
and a countable dense set of points
\{w_{r}\}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{0}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{r})=V_{K}(w_{r}),\ r=1,2,...

Then, in probability,

\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|\to V_{K}\ \hbox{in}\ L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{C}^{d}).

Here bases
\{p_{nj}\}
constructed from asymptotically (BM) measures as in Proposition
2.9
provide an example where we have the much stronger property that
\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}\to V_{K}
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{a_{nj}\}
be an array of i.i.d. random variables. We assume that

(i)

\mathbb{P}(|a_{nj}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
and

(ii)

\mathcal{Q}(a_{nj};r)\to 0\ \hbox{as}\ r\to 0
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be compact and locally regular and let

be a continuous, real-valued function on
K
. Suppose that that
||p_{nj}e^{-nQ}||_{K}\leq 1,\ j=1,...,m_{n};n=1,2,...
and with
\{B_{n}\}
in (
3.1
), given any subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
, there is a further subsequence
Y_{0}\subset Y
and a countable dense set of points
\{w_{r}\}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{0}}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(w_{r})=V_{K,Q}(w_{r}),\ r=1,2,...

\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|\to V_{K,Q}\ \hbox{in}\ L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{C}^{d}).

Here bases
\{p_{nj}\}
constructed from asymptotically weighted (BM) measures as in Proposition

provide an example. For

[

Remark 3.3
.

As a corollary, for the normalized zero currents

dd^{c}(\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|):=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}(\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|)

(here,
dd^{c}\log|H_{n}|
is the current of integration on the algebraic hypersurface
\{H_{n}=0\}
), we conclude that every subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
has a further subsequence
Y_{0}\subset Y
such that almost surely

\lim_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y_{0}}dd^{c}(\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|)\to dd^{c}V_{K}\ (\hbox{or}\ dd^{c}V_{K,Q})\ \hbox{as currents}.

The proofs of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
are identical; we use the notation and give the proof of the former. We utilize a multivariate version of Lemma 3.1 of
[
7
]
, this time stated for random polynomials as in (

). We observe that
m_{n}=0(n^{d})
; hence we have the condition
\mathbb{P}(|a_{j}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
(instead of

when

in
[
7
]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{a_{nj}\}
be i.i.d. complex random variables satisfying
\mathbb{P}(|a_{nj}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
, and let
H_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{n}}a_{nj}p_{nj}(z)
be random polynomials where
||p_{nj}||_{K}\leq 1
. For any sequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
there is a further subsequence
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
such that almost surely, the family
\{\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|\}_{n\in Y^{\prime}}
is locally uniformly bounded above and for all

\limsup_{n\to\infty,\ n\in Y^{\prime}}\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}(z)|\leq V_{K}(z).

(3.2)

Proof.

There are only two modifications to the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
7
]
. First, the condition
\mathbb{P}(|a_{nj}|>e^{|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
yields that for

\mathbb{P}(|a_{nj}|>e^{\epsilon|z|})=o(1/|z|^{d})
and hence letting

\Omega_{n,\epsilon}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:|a_{nj}(\omega)|\leq e^{\epsilon n},\ j=1,...,m_{n}\}

. By Borel-Cantelli, almost surely there exists

Next, for

and hence using this (in place of local uniform convergence of

for

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{a_{nj}\}
be an array of i.i.d. random variables. We assume that

(i)

\mathbb{E}[\big(\log(1+|a_{nj}|\big)^{d}]<\infty

(ii)

For some

for

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables on a probability space

which satisfy
\mathbb{E}[\big(\log(1+|a_{j}|\big)^{d}]<\infty

|a_{j}|\leq C\exp(\sqrt[d]{j\epsilon})\ \hbox{for all}\ j.

Proof.

For a nonnegative random variable

we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[X\geq j]\leq\mathbb{E}[X]\leq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[X\geq j].

Letting

and using the assumption that

are identically distributed, we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}[|a_{j}|\geq e^{\sqrt[d]{j\epsilon}}]<\infty.

Hence, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma there exists a

so that

|a_{j}|<e^{\sqrt[d]{j\epsilon}}\ \hbox{for all}\ j\geq j_{0}.

Then we can find a a random constant

such that almost surely

|a_{j}|<Ce^{\sqrt[d]{j\epsilon}}\ \hbox{for all}\ j.

We proceed with the proof of Theorem

Proof.

is locally uniformly bounded above. Together with the previous inequalities, this give the

\{\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|\}
almost surely. Using

(\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}(z)|)^{*}\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})

\mathbb{C}^{d}
we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}(w_{j})|\geq V_{K}(w_{j}),\ j=1,2,...

almost surely. Together with (
4.1

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}(w_{j})|=V_{K}(w_{j}),\ j=1,2,...

Proposition

\frac{1}{n}\log|H_{n}|\to V_{K}
in

.

∎

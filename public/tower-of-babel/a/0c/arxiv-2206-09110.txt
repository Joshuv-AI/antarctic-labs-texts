Hochschild cohomology of some finite category algebras as simplicial cohomology
I.-I. Simion, C.-C. Todea

Abstract
. By a result of Gerstenhaber and Schack the simplicial cohomology ring H ∙ ⁡ ( 𝒞 , k ) {\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k) of a poset 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology ring HH ∙ ⁡ ( k ​ 𝒞 ) {\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}}) of the category algebra k ​ 𝒞 k{\mathcal{C}} , where the poset is viewed as a category and k k is a field. Extending results of Mishchenko [ 6 ] , under certain assumptions on a category 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} , we construct a category 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} and a graded k k -linear isomorphism HH ∙ ⁡ ( k ​ 𝒞 ) ≅ H ∙ ⁡ ( 𝒟 , k ) {\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{D}},k) . Interpreting the degree one cohomology, we also show how the k k -space of derivations on k ​ 𝒞 k{\mathcal{C}} , graded by some semigroup, corresponds to the k k -space of characters on 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} .



1.
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a finite category, i.e. a category in which the objects form a finite set and every set of morphisms is finite. By a result of Gerstenhaber and Schack
[
2
]
, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a poset, then the simplicial cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k)
is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})
of the category algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}
. The proof of this result is simplified in
[
3
]
with the notion of
R
-relative Hochschild cohomology for subrings
R
of
k{\mathcal{C}}
. By a recent result of Mishchenko
[
6
, Theorem 1]
, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a group, then
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})
is isomorphic to the cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}(BGr,k)
of the classifying space of some groupoid
Gr
. The proof of this result describes an explicit
k
-linear isomorphism. In
[
13
]
Fei Xu constructs a counterexample to a conjecture of Snashall and Solberg
[
8
]
by studying a split surjective ring homomorphism
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}(B{\mathcal{C}},k)
. The natural question which arises is: for which categories can the Hochschild cohomology of
k{\mathcal{C}}
be identified explicitly with the singular cohomology of the classifying space of some category? Due to the well-known isomorphism
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k)\cong{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}(B{\mathcal{C}},k)
(see
[
11
, §5]
) this is equivalent to asking for the existence of a category
{\mathcal{D}}
such that
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})
is isomorphic to the simplicial cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{D}},k)
.

In this paper we replace the category
F({\mathcal{C}})
used in
[
13
]
with the
adjoint category

F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
, generalizing the explicit construction of the groupoid
Gr
from
[
6
]
. We denote by
\operatorname{Ob}({\mathcal{C}})
the set of objects in
{\mathcal{C}}
and by
\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
the set of morphisms in
{\mathcal{C}}.
Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}({\mathcal{C}}).
The category
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
has as objects the subset
\operatorname{End}{\mathcal{C}}
of endomorphisms in
\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
and morphisms are defined in the following way. A morphism in
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
from
a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})
to
b\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{2})
is a morphism
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})
, making the following diagram commutative:

(1)

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a group then
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
is isomorphic to the groupoid
Gr
constructed in
[
6
]
and if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a poset then
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{C}}
.

In order to extend the construction in
[
6
]
we require certain conditions on
{\mathcal{C}}
. The category
{\mathcal{C}}
is called
left deterministic
if for any
b\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{2})
and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})
there exists
a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})
such that the diagram (
1
) commutes. Similarly, the category
{\mathcal{C}}
is called
right deterministic
if for any
a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})
and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})
there exists
b\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{2})
such that the diagram (
1
) commutes. We call
{\mathcal{C}}

deterministic
if it is both left and right deterministic. A category
{\mathcal{C}}
is left (respectively right) cancellative if the composition of morphisms has the left (respectively right) cancellation property. We call
{\mathcal{C}}

cancellative
if it is both left and right cancellative. The notion of cancellative categories needed for our results is not new. Lawson and Wallis use it to generalize some of their results
[
4
]
from monoids to categories.
There is a natural right action of the monoid
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})
on the set
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})
, given by composition:

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})\times\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(g,a)\mapsto g\circ a.

We call
{\mathcal{C}}

rr-transitive
(right-regular) if for any objects
x_{1},x_{2}\in\operatorname{Ob}{\mathcal{C}}
the right action of the monoid
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})
on the set
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1},x_{2})
is transitive, see
[
10
, Section 2.1]
for more details about actions of monoids on sets.

We are now in a position to state our first result.

Theorem A
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a finite deterministic cancellative category then there is a surjective morphism of graded
k
-vector spaces
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet}:{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}(F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}}),k)
between the Hochschild cohomology of the category algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}
and the simplicial cohomology of the category
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
. Moreover, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is rr-transitive, then
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet}
is an isomorphism.

The map
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet}
is induced from an explicitly constructed cochain map and unifies the constructions in
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
in our context. Moreover, it is easy to describe a right inverse of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet}
, again, induced from an explicit cochain map (Propositions
8
and
9
). Examples of finite deterministic cancellative rr-transitive categories are groupoids and posets (Subsection
2.3
), hence we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary B
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a finite groupoid or a finite poset then the Hochschild cohomology of the category algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to the simplicial cohomology of the category
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
.

The isomorphism
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k)
for
{\mathcal{C}}
a poset does not hold for general
{\mathcal{C}}
(see
[
11
, §5]
for more details). For certain EI categories (every endomorphism is an isomorphism) which are called
amalgams of groups and posets
, see
[
5
, Section 4]
, Lodder describes
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k)
as a direct summand of
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})
.

In Example
6
we describe an EI category
{\mathcal{C}}
, for which the map
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet}
is an isomorphism, but
{\mathcal{C}}
is not a groupoid neither a poset. The category
{\mathcal{C}}
is not even an amalgam of groups and posets.

The results in
[
6
]
build on ideas developed by Arutyunov and Mishchenko in
[
1
]
where they show that
{\operatorname{HH}}^{1}(k{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{H}}^{1}(BF^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}}),k)
in the case where
{\mathcal{C}}
is a group. By Theorem
A
, there is an isomorphism of the first cohomology groups
{\operatorname{HH}}^{1}(k{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{H}}^{1}(F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}}),k)
. However, the method in
[
1
]
relies on a description of the derivations of
k{\mathcal{C}}
in terms of characters on
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
(see Section
5
) and the fact that
\operatorname{Der}(k{\mathcal{C}})/\operatorname{Inn}(k{\mathcal{C}})\cong{\operatorname{HH}}^{1}(k{\mathcal{C}}),
where
\operatorname{Der}(k{\mathcal{C}})
is the algebra of derivations of
k{\mathcal{C}}
and
\operatorname{Inn}(k{\mathcal{C}})
is the algebra of inner derivations. In order to extend the description in
[
1
]
, we introduce in Subsection
5.1
a semigroup with zero, denoted by
S
and explain how
k{\mathcal{C}}
is graded by
S
. We denote by
\operatorname{Der}^{{\operatorname{gr}}}(k{\mathcal{C}})
the
k
-vector space of all
S
-graded derivations. Our second result extends the characterization from
[
1
]
of
\operatorname{Der}(k{\mathcal{C}})
for group algebras as follows.

Theorem C
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a finite rr-transitive deterministic cancellative category. There is a
k
-linear isomorphism
{\mathcal{T}}:\operatorname{Der}^{{\operatorname{gr}}}(k{\mathcal{C}})\rightarrow\operatorname{Char}(F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}}))
between graded derivations on
k{\mathcal{C}}
and characters on the category
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
.

We note that the above results can be given for any small category
{\mathcal{C}}
if we use a condition of finiteness of supports for the cochains appearing in the above cohomologies. In this paper we assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is finite.

In
[
2
]
, Gerstenhaber and Shack show how the simplicial cohomology of a locally finite simplicial complex can be interpreted as the Hochschild cohomology of some associative unital
k
-algebra. It is known that the simplicial cohomology agrees with the singular cohomology (which is better adapted to theory than to computation) of topological spaces which can be triangulated. It is possible to compute simplicial cohomology of a simplicial complex efficiently, hence it is better suited for calculations. In Theorem
A
we describe certain finite categories which have Hochschild cohomology isomorphic (as graded
k
-vector space) to the simplicial cohomology of some other categories, with trivial coefficients. The Hochschild cohomology is endowed with a very rich structure: it is a graded commutative algebra via the cup product; it has a graded Lie bracket of degree
-1
; with this bracket the Hochschild cohomology becomes a Gerstenhaber algebra. Recently, the second author introduced a Beilinson-Drinfeld structure (BD-structure) in group cohomology
[
9
]
and showed that the Hochschild cohomology
{\operatorname{HH}}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}_{3}C_{3})
, of the cyclic
3
group algebra, is a BD-algebra. BD-algebras are very similar to BV-algebras (Batalin-Vilkovisky), which have been intensively studied for Hochschild cohomology of various algebras. It would be interesting to investigate the extent to which the maps
{\mathcal{T}}^{\bullet},{\mathcal{X}}^{\bullet}
can transport (preserve) all these structures between Hochschild cohomology and simplicial cohomology.

The paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
we recall some facts on Hochschild cohomology and cohomology of the nerve of a small category. Subsection
2.3
discusses the concepts of deterministic, cancellative and rr-transitive categories which are needed in Section
3
to generalize the construction in
[
6
]
. The proof of Theorem
A
is given in Section
4
. In Section
5
we describe characters on the category
F^{{\operatorname{ad}}}({\mathcal{C}})
and give the proof of Theorem
C
.

2.
Preliminaries

For an object
x
in
{\mathcal{C}}
we denote by
{\operatorname{id}}_{x}
the identity endomorphism on
x
. For a morphism
a:x\rightarrow y
in
{\mathcal{C}}
we denote by
s(a)
the source
x
of
a
and by
t(a)
the target
y
of
a
. For any category
{\mathcal{C}}
and a field
k
the category algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}
is the
k
-vector space with basis the morphisms in
{\mathcal{C}}
and with product defined by

ab=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a\circ b,&\text{ if $s(b)=t(a)$}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{array}.\right.

We shall use the notation
\otimes
for
\otimes_{k}
and, if
A,B
are
k
-vector vector spaces, by
\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(A,B)
we denote the
k
-vector space of all
k
-linear maps from
A
to
B
.

2.1.
Hochschild cohomology complex

The Hochschild cochain complex
[
12
, Definition 1.1.13]
for the algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}
is

0\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{}}C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\partial^{0}}}C^{1}(k{\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\partial^{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\partial^{m-1}}}C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\partial^{m}}}C^{m+1}(k{\mathcal{C}})\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\partial^{m+1}}}\dots

where
C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}})=k{\mathcal{C}}
and for
m\geq 1

C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}})=\left\{f:\underbrace{k{\mathcal{C}}\otimes k{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\cdots\otimes k{\mathcal{C}}}_{m\text{ times}}\rightarrow k{\mathcal{C}}\,\mid\text{ $f$ is $k$-linear}\right\}.

For any
f\in C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}})
and any
a_{1},\dots,a_{m+1}\in k{\mathcal{C}}
we have

\partial^{m}(f)(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{m+1})=

\displaystyle a_{1}f(a_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{m+1})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}f(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes a_{j+2}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{m+1})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{m+1}f(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{m})a_{m+1},

if
m\geq 1
and
\partial^{0}(f)(a)=af-fa
for any
f\in C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}}
). It is well known that
{\operatorname{HH}}^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}})=\operatorname{Ker}(\partial^{0})=Z(k{\mathcal{C}})
and
{\operatorname{HH}}^{m}({\mathcal{C}}):=\operatorname{Ker}(\partial^{m})/\operatorname{Im}(\partial^{m-1})
for
m\geq 1
.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is finite, any
k
-linear map
f\in C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}})
is represented by a matrix

(f_{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}}^{h})_{g_{1},\dots,g_{m},h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}

with respect to the basis
\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
of
k{\mathcal{C}}
:

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}f_{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}}^{h}h

for all
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
. In particular, the differential operator
\partial^{m}
applied to
f
is determined by

\partial^{m}(f)(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m+1})=

2.2.
Simplicial cohomology

The simplicial cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}({\mathcal{C}},k)
is the cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}^{\bullet}(B{\mathcal{C}},k)
of the nerve of
{\mathcal{C}}
(see
[
11
, §5]
). The nerve of
{\mathcal{C}}
is the simplicial set

N{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{\sigma:a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-2}}}a_{m-1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\,\mid\text{ $\sigma$ is an $m$-chain of morphisms in ${\mathcal{C}}$}\right\}.

The subset of
m
-chains is denoted by
N{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
and the
0
-chains are
NC_{0}=\operatorname{Ob}({\mathcal{C}})
. The simplicial homology with coefficients in
k
is obtained with the simplicial chain complex

\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta_{m+1}}}kN{\mathcal{C}}_{m+1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta_{m}}}kN{\mathcal{C}}_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta_{m-1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta_{1}}}kN{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta_{0}}}kN{\mathcal{C}}_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{}}0

where
kN{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
is the
k
-vector space with basis
N{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
and where

\delta_{m}=\sum_{i=0}^{m+1}(-1)^{i}d_{i}^{m}

with
d_{i}^{m}:N{\mathcal{C}}_{m+1}\rightarrow N{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
is the
i
-th face map (extended by linearity), i.e. for
1\leq i\leq m
:

\displaystyle d_{i}^{m}(a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots a_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{i-1}}}a_{i}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{i}}}a_{i+1}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m}}}a_{m+1})

\displaystyle=a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots a_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[5.3em]{g_{i-1}\circ g_{i}}}a_{i+1}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m}}}a_{m+1};

\displaystyle d_{0}^{m}(a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m}}}a_{m+1})

\displaystyle=a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m}}}a_{m+1},

\displaystyle d_{m+1}^{m}(a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m}}}a_{m+1})

\displaystyle=a_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{0}}}a_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{g_{m-1}}}a_{m}.

One obtains the cochain complex for the calculation of the simplicial cohomology

0\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{}}C^{0}(kN{\mathcal{C}},k)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta^{0}}}C^{1}(kN{\mathcal{C}},k)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta^{1}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta^{m-1}}}C^{m}(kN{\mathcal{C}},k)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta^{m}}}C^{m+1}(kN{\mathcal{C}},k)\xrightarrow{\mathmakebox[1.5em]{\delta^{m+1}}}\dots

where

and where

Lemma 1

Let

(b)

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a right deterministic and right cancellative category, then there is unique
b\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{2})
such that diagram (
1
) is commutative.

(c)

with inverse

satisfying

. For two elements

hence, since

\varphi_{g}^{-1}:\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{2})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(x_{1})

\varphi_{g}

∎

\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}^{g_{m}h=h^{\prime}}X_{g_{m-1},\dots,g_{0}}^{h}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{m-j}X_{g_{m},\dots,g_{j+2},g_{j+1}g_{j},g_{j-1},\dots,g_{0}}^{h^{\prime}}+(-1)^{m+1}\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}^{hg_{0}=h^{\prime}}X^{h}_{g_{m},\dots,g_{1}}.

\sigma
, the equation
hg_{0}=h^{\prime}
admits the solution
h_{1}=g_{m}\dots g_{1}a_{1}
. If
h_{2}
is another solution to

{\mathcal{C}}
is left cancellative, we have the unique solution

\delta^{m}
in Subsection
2.2
this also equals

. This concludes the proof.

For clarity we treat the case
m=0
separately. If
X\in C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}})
then
{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\partial^{0}X)(\sigma)
is the coefficient of
g_{0}a_{0}
in
(\partial^{0}X)(g_{0})=g_{0}X+Xg_{0}=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}X^{h}g_{0}h-X^{h}hg_{0}
. If
g_{0}h=g_{0}a_{0}
then
h=a_{0}
and if
hg_{0}=g_{0}a_{0}
then
h=a_{1}
. Hence
{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\partial^{0}X)(\sigma)=X^{a_{0}}-X^{a_{1}}
. On the other hand
X^{a_{0}}={\mathcal{T}}^{0}(X)\circ d^{0}_{1}
and
X^{a_{1}}={\mathcal{T}}^{0}(X)\circ d^{0}_{0}
. Thus
{\mathcal{T}}^{1}(\partial^{0}X)(\sigma)=-\delta^{0}({\mathcal{T}}^{m}X)(\sigma)
.
∎

Proposition 8
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a finite deterministic cancellative category then
{\mathcal{X}}^{m+1}\circ\delta^{m}=(-1)^{m+1}\partial^{m}\circ{\mathcal{X}}^{m}
.

Proof.

For

, we show that

(6)

for any

does not exist, then

. A similar argument shows that if

) is also zero. Assume that

the right-hand side of (

+(-1)^{m+1}\sum_{a_{1}\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g_{1}))}T_{g_{m},\dots,g_{1}}^{g_{m}\cdots g_{1}a_{1}}g_{m}\cdots g_{1}a_{1}g_{0}.

4.
Relative Hochschild cohomology

The notion of relative Hochschild cohomology introduced in
[
3
]
simplifies the comparison of Hochschild cohomology and simplicial cohomology done in
[
2
]
. By
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
, if
R
is a separable subalgebra
[
12
, Definition 4.1.7]
of
k{\mathcal{C}}
, then the
R
-relative Hochschild cohomology of
k{\mathcal{C}}
is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology of
k{\mathcal{C}}
. For our purpose, we choose
R
to be the subalgebra
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
of
k{\mathcal{C}}
generated by the set of all identity morphisms on each object.

Lemma 10
.

The algebra
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
is separable.

Proof.

The algebra
R=k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
is separable if and only if there is an idempotent
e=\sum_{i}u_{i}\otimes v_{i}
in the enveloping algebra
R\otimes R^{{\operatorname{op}}}
of
R
such that
\sum_{i}u_{i}v_{i}=1
and
(r\otimes 1)e=(1\otimes r)e
. One checks that
e=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Ob}({\mathcal{C}})}1_{x}\otimes 1_{x}
satisfies this property.
∎

As in
[
3
, §1]
the
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative Hochschild cohomology of
k{\mathcal{C}}
is the cohomology of the subcomplex of
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative cochains
C^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}},k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}})\subseteq C^{\bullet}(k{\mathcal{C}})
. The
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative
m
-cochain group
C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}},k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}})
consists of
k
-linear functions
f
satisfying

•

f(ag_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=af(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})
,

•

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m}a)=f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})a
,

•

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}a\otimes g_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}\otimes ag_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})\quad 1\leq i\leq m-1,

for all
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
and any
a\in k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
. Notice that
C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}},k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}})=C^{0}(k{\mathcal{C}})=k{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proposition 11
.

If
m\geq 1
, an element
f\in C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}})
lies in
C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}},k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}})
if and only if for any
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
the following holds

•

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=0
if
t(g_{i+1})\neq s(g_{i}),
for some
1\leq i\leq m-1

and

•

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})\in k\left[\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g_{m}),t(g_{1}))\right]
, otherwise.

Proof.

Let
f
be a
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative
m
-cochain. For any
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
we have

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=f({\operatorname{id}}_{t(g_{1})}g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})={\operatorname{id}}_{t(g_{1})}f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m}).

Since
f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})}f_{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}}^{h}h
, it follows that the non-zero terms in this sum are
f_{g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m}}^{h}h
with
t(h)=t(g_{1})
. A similar argument shows that
s(h)=s(g_{m})
, hence

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g_{m}),t(g_{1}))}f_{g_{1},\dots,g_{m}}^{h}h.

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}
. Moreover

f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}\otimes g_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})=f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{t(g_{i+1})}g_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m})

=f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}{\operatorname{id}}_{t(g_{i+1})}\otimes g_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{m}).

Thus, if
s(g_{i})\neq t(g_{i+1})
then
g_{i}{\operatorname{id}}_{t(g_{i+1})}=0
, in which case
f(g_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{i}\otimes g_{i+1}\dots\otimes g_{m})=0
. Hence a
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative
m
-cochain has the indicated properties. Conversely, if
f
has the indicated properties, one checks that it is
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
-relative using the fact that
k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}}
is generated by
\{{\operatorname{id}}_{x}:x\in\operatorname{Ob}({\mathcal{C}})\}
.
∎

Corollary 12
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a left cancellative rr-transitive category. Then

if and only if

f(g_{m-1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{0})=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g_{0}))}f_{g_{m-1},\dots,g_{0}}^{g_{m-1}g_{m-2}\dots g_{1}g_{0}a}g_{m-1}g_{m-2}\dots g_{1}g_{0}a.

for any
g_{0},\dots,g_{m-1}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
11
, for any
f\in C^{m}(k{\mathcal{C}},k{\mathcal{C}}^{{\operatorname{id}}})
and any
g_{0},\dots,g_{m-1}\in\operatorname{Mor}({\mathcal{C}})
we have

f(g_{m-1}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{0})=\sum_{h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g_{0}),t(g_{m-1}))}f_{g_{m-1},\dots,g_{0}}^{h}h

where the sum is zero if
t(g_{i})\neq s(g_{i+1}),
for some
0\leq i\leq m-2
. Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is rr-transitive and left cancellative, the claim follows by Proposition
2
with
g:=g_{m-1}\circ g_{m-2}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}\circ g_{0}
and
f:=h
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

For a small category
{\mathcal{C}}

Since

{\mathcal{X}}({\mathcal{T}}(X))(g)=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g))}({\mathcal{T}}(X))^{g\circ a}_{g}g\circ a=\sum_{a\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(s(g))}X_{g}^{g\circ a}g\circ a=X(g)

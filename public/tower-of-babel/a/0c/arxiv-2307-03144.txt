\sum_{\ell|d}\Lambda(\ell)\ell^{-s}
and
\prod_{p|d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{-k})(p^{r_{2}}+\ldots+p^{-v_{p}(d)s})}}\right)

∎

We additionally need the following lemma that evaluates derivatives of meromorphic continuation of (

Corollary 2.3
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{t_{1}}\gcd(c,d)^{t_{2}}}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{1}\\
t_{2}=0\end{subarray}}=-\zeta^{\prime}(-r_{1})

and

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{t_{1}}\gcd(c,d)^{t_{2}}}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{1}\\
t_{2}=0\end{subarray}}=\zeta(-r_{1})\sum_{\ell\mid d}\Lambda(\ell)\ell^{r_{1}}.

Proof.

We use (
2.8
) with
t_{1}=-s-k
and
t_{2}=k
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{t_{1}}\gcd(c,d)^{t_{2}}=\zeta(-t_{1})\prod_{p|d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})})}}\right).

To establish (
2.12
), we note

\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})})}}\right)\big|_{t_{2}=0}=0.

Thus, taking the derivative of both sides of (
2.14
), considering the meromorphic continuation, and substituting
t_{2}=0,t_{1}=r_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{t_{1}}\gcd(c,d)^{t_{2}}}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{1}\\
t_{2}=0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\zeta^{\prime}(-r_{1})\prod_{p|d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})})}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{1}\\
t_{2}=0\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=-\zeta^{\prime}(-r_{1}),

which implies (
2.12
). In order to show (

), we note that

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}(1-p^{-t_{2}})}}\right|_{t_{2}=0}=p^{-t_{2}}\log p\big|_{t_{2}=0}=\log p

and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})})}}\right|_{t_{2}=0}=1.

We thus get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}(1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})

\displaystyle(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})}))\big|_{t_{2}=0}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}(1-p^{-t_{2}})}}\right|_{t_{2}=0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\cdot(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})})}}\right|_{t_{2}=0}

\displaystyle=\log(p)\cdot(p^{t_{1}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)t_{1}}).

Summing over all possible prime divisors of

, we obtain

\displaystyle\zeta(-r_{1})\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\prod_{p|d}(1+(1-p^{-t_{2}})(p^{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)(t_{1}+t_{2})}))}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{1}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta(-r_{1})\sum_{p\mid d}\log(p)\cdot(p^{r_{1}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)r_{1}}).

We can instead write this as a sum over all divisors of

with the help of the von Mangoldt function

∎

The following lemma allows us to rewrite the sums over
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
ad-bc=n
for a fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
as a sum over integers for
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Additionally, we denote

(2.15)

\displaystyle v_{c,d}

\displaystyle:=\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}

and

\delta_{c|n}:=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\text{$c$ divides $n$,}\\
0,\quad&\text{$c$ does not divide $n$},\end{cases}

for
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We use the notation that
2
divides
0
, thus we write

\delta_{2|0}=1.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
,
c,d\in\mathbb{N}
,
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{n}(c,d)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}f(bc),

where the sum above is over all possible
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
ad-bc=n
; if there exists no such
c,d
, then the sum is equal to zero.
Then
\mathcal{T}_{n}(c,d)
can be rewritten as

(2.16)

\mathcal{T}_{n}(c,d)=\begin{cases}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f((m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d})-\delta_{c\mid n}f(-n)-\delta_{d\mid n}f(0),\quad&\gcd(c,d)\mid n,\\
0,\quad&\gcd(c,d)\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n\end{cases}

for
u_{c,d},v_{c,d}\in\mathbb{C}
defined in (
1.5
) and (
2.15
), respectively.

Proof.

If
\gcd(c,d)\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
, then there do not exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
{ad-bc=n}
holds, and
\mathcal{T}_{n}(c,d)=0
. We let
B,b^{*}
and
a^{*}
be as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

We note that the set
B
is parameterized by
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, and each of its elements takes the form

(2.17)

\displaystyle b(m)=b^{*}+\frac{m}{\gcd(c,d)}d.

The corresponding
a(m)\in\mathbb{Z}
, defined by the property
a(m)d-b(m)c=n
, is equal to

(2.18)

a(m)=a^{*}+\frac{m}{\gcd(c,d)}c.

We rewrite

\displaystyle b(m)c=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{b^{*}\gcd(c,d)}{d}+m}}\right)\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}=(m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d}

and obtain

\mathcal{T}_{n}(c,d)=\begin{cases}\sum^{*}f((m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d}),\quad&\gcd(c,d)\mid n,\\
0,\quad&\gcd(c,d)\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n,\end{cases}

where the sum
\sum^{*}
is taken over all possible
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
a(m)b(m)\neq 0
. We consider the following possibilities:

(i)

Let
d\mid n
, then the definition of
B
implies
b^{*}=0
. Thus the definition of
u_{c,d}
, (
1.5
), implies
u_{c,d}=0
and

a(m)b(m)=\frac{md}{\gcd(c,d)}\frac{n+\frac{m}{\gcd(c,d)}cd}{d}=\frac{md}{\gcd(c,d)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{d}+\frac{mc}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right).

(1)

If
c\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
, then
\frac{n}{d}+\frac{mc}{\gcd(c,d)}=0
has no integer solutions in
m
. Thus, the only integer
m
such that
a(m)b(m)=0
is
m=0
. In this case

f((m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d})|_{m=0}=f(0).

(2)

If
c\mid n
, then either
m=0
or
m=-\frac{n\gcd(c,d)}{cd}
in which case

f((m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d})|_{m=-\frac{n\gcd(c,d)}{cd}}=f(-n).

(ii)

Let
d\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
.

(1)

If
c\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
, then
a(m)b(m)
cannot be equal to zero.

(2)

If
c\mid n
, then
b^{*}=-\frac{n}{c}
, and
a^{*}=0
. Moreover, when

a(m)b(m)=0
then
a(m)=0
and so
m=0.

Thus, if
d\mid n
, the term
f(0)
has to be omitted, and if
c\mid n
, the term
f(-n)
has to be omitted. That implies (
2.16
) and finishes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first
need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

,
r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathbb{Z}
with

, then the sum

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(bc)^{P}

equals

(-1)^{P+1}n^{P}\sigma_{r_{2}}(n)+2d^{P}\delta_{2\mid P}\zeta(-P)\zeta(-r_{2}-P)\prod_{p|d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{P})(p^{r_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)r_{2}})}}\right),

for

\displaystyle(-1)^{P+1}n^{P}\sigma_{r_{2}}(n)+d^{r_{2}+2P}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)|n}}}\frac{\zeta(-r_{2}-P,\frac{c^{\prime}}{d})}{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c^{\prime},d})^{P},

for

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

In what follows, we consider two different cases:
d\mid n
and
d\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
.

First assume
d\mid n
.
In this case, we have
\gcd(c,d)\mid n
, and the definition of
b^{*}
in (
1.4
) implies
b^{*}=0
. Hence, by (
2.15
),
u_{c,d}=0
.
With the help of Lemma
2.4
, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(bc)^{P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}(mv_{c,d})^{P}-(-n)^{P}\delta_{c\mid n}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:yn}}}{{=}}2\delta_{2\mid P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right)^{P}\zeta(-P)+(-1)^{P+1}\delta_{c\mid n}n^{P}.

Multiplying by
c^{r_{2}}
and summing over
c\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2\delta_{2\mid P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right)^{P}\zeta(-P)+(-1)^{P+1}\delta_{c\mid n}n^{P}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2d^{P}\delta_{2\mid P}\zeta(-P)\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{c^{P+r_{2}}}{\gcd(c,d)^{P}}+(-1)^{P+1}n^{P}\sigma_{r_{2}}(n).

Finally, Lemma
2.2
gives

\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{c^{P+r_{2}}}{\gcd(c,d)^{P}}=\zeta(-r_{2}-P)\prod_{p|d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+(1-p^{P})(p^{r_{2}}+\ldots+p^{v_{p}(d)r_{2}})}}\right),

and, together with (
3.3
), this implies the first statement of the lemma.

When
d\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
and
\gcd(c,d)\mid n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(bc)^{P}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{P+1}\delta_{c\mid n}n^{P}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}((m+u_{c,d})v_{c,d})^{P}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:yn}}}{{=}}(-1)^{P+1}\delta_{c\mid n}n^{P}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right)^{P}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c,d})^{P},

and when
d\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
and
\gcd(c,d)\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
,

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(bc)^{P}=0.

When
d\not\hskip 2.5pt\mid n
, multiplying (
3.4
) by
c^{r_{2}}
and summing over
c
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(bc)^{P}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{P+1}n^{P}\sigma_{r_{2}}(n)+\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c\in\mathbb{N}}}{{\gcd(c,d)\mid n}}}\frac{c^{r_{2}+P}d^{P}}{\gcd(c,d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c,d})^{P}.

We note that for any function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
,

(3.6)

\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle c\in\mathbb{N}}}{{\gcd(c,d)\mid n}}}f(c)=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)\mid n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}f(jd+c^{\prime}),

and for any

and
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3.7)

\gcd(jd+c^{\prime},d)=\gcd(c^{\prime},d)\quad\text{and}\quad u_{c^{\prime},d}=u_{jd+c^{\prime},d}.

Rewriting the second term in (
3.5

\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime}+jd,d)|n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{(jd+c^{\prime})^{r_{2}+P}d^{P}}{\gcd(c^{\prime}+jd,d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c^{\prime}+jd,d})^{P}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:gcd_doesnt_change_much}}}{{=}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)|n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(jd+c^{\prime})^{r_{2}+P}d^{P}}{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c^{\prime},d})^{P}

\displaystyle=d^{r_{2}+2P}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)|n}}}\frac{\zeta(-r_{2}-P,\frac{c^{\prime}}{d})}{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c^{\prime},d})^{P},

where

is defined as in (
1.5
).
This implies the second statement of the Lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Assuming the factorization

n_{1}=ad,\quad n_{2}=-bc,\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{Z},

we rewrite each summand in the left hand side of (

\displaystyle\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}}d^{r_{1}}\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}}{{ad-bc=n}}}(-bc)^{P}.

Then we multiply the formula in Lemma
3.1
by

and obtain (
1.6
).

Now, we need to deal with the meromorphic continuation. We note that
2\delta_{2\mid P}
can be meromorphically continued by taking

.
We consider the second term on the right side of (
1.6
). There are three possible cases:

(1)

If
r_{2}+P\in 2\mathbb{N}+1
, then
\delta_{2\mid n}
vanishes.

(2)

If

(3)

It remains to show, for

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0<c^{\prime}\leq d}}{{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)|n}}}\frac{\zeta(-r_{2}-P,\frac{c^{\prime}}{d})}{\gcd(c^{\prime},d)^{P}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c^{\prime},d})^{P}=0.

∎

Proof of Theorem

,

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +2\delta_{2\mid Q}d^{Q}\zeta(-Q)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{t_{1}}\gcd(c,d)^{t_{2}}}}\right)}}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1}=r_{2}+Q\\

since

The fourth line becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}2\delta_{2\mid Q}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right)^{Q}\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c,d})^{Q}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{cd}{\gcd(c,d)}}}\right)^{Q}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(m+u_{c,d})^{Q}\log|m+u_{c,d}|.

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}}c^{r_{2}}

∎

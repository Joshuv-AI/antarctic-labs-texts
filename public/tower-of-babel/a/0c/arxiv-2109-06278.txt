Semi-biproducts of monoids
Nelson Martins-Ferreira

Abstract
. It is shown that the category of semi-biproducts of monoids is equivalent to the category of pseudo-actions . A semi-biproduct of monoids is a new notion, obtained through generalizing a biproduct of commutative monoids. By dropping commutativity and requiring some of the homomorphisms in the biproduct diagram to be merely identity-preserving maps, we obtain a semi-biproduct. A pseudo-action is a new notion as well. It consists of three ingredients: a pre-action, a factor system and a correction system. In the category of groups all correction systems are trivial. This is perhaps the reason why this notion, to the author’s best knowledge, has never been considered before.

2.3.
The equivalence

In this subsection we will work towards an equivalence between pseudo-actions and semi-biproducts of monoids.
First we show that there is a functor from the category of semi-biproducts into the category of pseudo-actions.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
be a semi-biproduct of monoids. The system with three components

\displaystyle x^{b}=q(k(x)+s(b))

(48)

\displaystyle b\cdot x=q(s(b)+k(x))

(49)

\displaystyle b\times b^{\prime}=q(s(b)+s(b^{\prime}))

(50)

is a pseudo-action from
B
into
X
.

Moreover, if
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
is a morphism of semi-biproducts such as the one displayed in (
25
) then the pair
(f_{1},f_{3})
is a morphism of pseudo-actions.

Proof.

Clearly, the formulas given for
x^{b}
,
b\cdot x
and
b\times b^{\prime}
are well defined maps
\rho
,
\varphi
and
\gamma
consistent with the three components of a pseudo-action, respectively a correction system, a pre-action and a factor system. The identities
x^{1}=x
,
0^{b}=0
,
1\cdot x=x
,
b\cdot 0=0
,
b\times 1=0=1\times b
are immediate consequences of

,
qk=1_{X}
, and the fact that the maps
s
and
q
preserve the neutral element.

In order to prove condition (
29
) we observe that for every
x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in X
and
b,b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}\in B
, the expression

kx+sb+kx^{\prime}+sb^{\prime}+kx^{\prime\prime}+sb^{\prime\prime}\in A

can be decomposed as

\left(\left(kx+\left(\left(sb+kx^{\prime}\right)+sb^{\prime}\right)\right)+kx^{\prime\prime}\right)+sb^{\prime\prime}

(51)

and as

kx+\left(sb+\left(kx^{\prime}+\left(\left(sb^{\prime}+kx^{\prime\prime}\right)+sb^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)\right).

(52)

Using the formulas

\displaystyle kx+sb=k(x^{b})+s(b)

(53)

\displaystyle sb+kx=k(b\cdot x)+s(b)

(54)

\displaystyle sb+sb^{\prime}=k(b\times b^{\prime})+s(bb^{\prime})

(55)

when appropriate and following the order of parenthesis indicated in (

) we obtain the right hand side of (
29
). Following the order of parenthesis indicated in (
52
) we obtain the left hand side of (
29
).

The condition

(which is also a consequence of (
29
)) as well as the two conditions (

) and (

) are obtained by applying the map

on both sides of equations (

)–(

).

Let us now suppose
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
is a morphism of semi-biproducts such as the one displayed in (

is a morphism of pseudo-actions. To simplify notation let us consider

,

and

. We observe,

\displaystyle f(x)^{g(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(k^{\prime}f(x)+s^{\prime}g(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(hk(x)+hs(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}h(k(x)+s(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle fq(k(x)+s(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x^{b}).

Similarly, we prove

f(b\cdot x)=fq(sb+kx)=q^{\prime}h(sb+kx)=q^{\prime}(sg(b)+k^{\prime}f(x))=g(b)\cdot f(x)

∎

The previous result gives a functor from the category of semi-biproducts into the category of pseudo-actions. The synthetic construction (

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,B,\rho,\varphi,\gamma)
be a pseudo-action of monoids, the synthetic diagram (
42
) is a semi-biproduct of monoids.
Moreover, if

(f,g)\colon{(X,B,\rho,\varphi,\gamma)\to(X^{\prime},B^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})}

is a morphism of pseudo-actions then the triple
(f,h,g)

h\colon{R_{\rho,\varphi,\gamma}\to R_{\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}},

defined as
h(x,b)=(f(x),g(b))
, is a morphism of semi-biproducts.

Proof.

Given a pseudo-action
(X,B,\rho,\varphi,\gamma)
we consider the synthetic diagram (
42
). The operation (
43
) is well defined on the set

R_{\rho,\varphi,\gamma}=\left\{\left(x^{b},b\right)\mid x\in X,b\in B\right\}

and it is associative by (

(1,0)
we see that the set
R=R_{\rho,\varphi,\gamma}
has a monoid structure. Clearly, the maps
\langle 1,0\rangle\colon{X\to R}
and
\pi_{B}\colon{R\to B}
are monoid homomorphisms while
\langle 0,1\rangle\colon{B\to R}
and
\pi_{X}\colon{R\to X}
are zero-preserving maps. Furthermore, we have
\pi_{B}\langle 0,1\rangle=1_{B}
,
\pi_{X}\langle 1,0\rangle=1_{X}
,
\pi_{B}\langle 1,0\rangle=0
and
\pi_{X}\langle 0,1\rangle=0
. Finally, we observe that for every

which means that if

From equation (
47

is a monoid homomorphism. Since it is compatible with the respective semi-biproduct synthetic structure maps, we conclude that it is a morphism of semi-biproducts.
∎

In order to prove an equivalence of categories between the category of semi-biproducts and the category of pseudo-actions, we observe that if starting with a structure
(x^{b},b\cdot x,b\times b^{\prime})
for a pseudo-action of

The role of equations (

4.
The underlying extension

In this section we observe some slight adaptations of well-known results suggesting that semi-biproducts can be studied in the light of
S
-protomodular categories
[
5
]
.
A semi-biproduct
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
can be seen as an extension of monoids
X\to A\to B
equipped with a section
s
and a retraction
q
. Moreover, such extensions are pullback stable and the pair
(k,s)
is jointly epimorphic in the sense that every monoid homomorphism
g\colon{A\to Y}
, from
A
into a monoid
Y
, is completely determined by the homomorphism
gk\colon{X\to Y}
and the map
gs\colon{B\to Y}
, as
g(a)=gkq(a)+gsp(a)
, for every
a\in A
. Similarly, the pair
(q,p)
is jointly monomorphic in the sense that a homomorphism
f\colon{Z\to A}
, from a monoid
Z
into the monoid
A
, is completely determined by the homomorphism
pf\colon{Z\to B}
and the map
qf\colon{Z\to X}
, as
f(z)=kqf(z)+spf(z)
, for every
z\in Z
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
be a semi-biproduct of monoids. The morphism
k
is the kernel of the morphism
p
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon{Z\to A}
be a morphism such that
pf=0
. Then the map
\bar{f}=qf
is a homomorphism

\displaystyle qf(z+z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(fz+fz^{\prime})=q(kqf(z)+spf(z)+kqf(z^{\prime})+spf(z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(kqf(z)+0+kqf(z^{\prime})+0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle qk(qf(z)+qf(z^{\prime}))=qf(z)+qf(z^{\prime})

and it is unique with the property
k\bar{f}=f
. Indeed, if
k\bar{f}=f
then
qk\bar{f}=qf
and hence
\bar{f}=qf
.
∎

Proposition 2
.

Let
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
be a semi-biproduct of monoids. The morphism
p
is the cokernel of the morphism
k
.

Proof.

Let
g\colon{A\to Y}
be a morphism and suppose that
gk=0
. It follows that
g=gsp
,

g=g1_{A}=g(kq+sp)=gkq+gsp=0+gsp=gsp,

and consequently the map
\bar{g}=gs
is a homomorphism,

\displaystyle gs(bb^{\prime})=gs(ps(b)ps(b^{\prime}))=gsp(sb+sb^{\prime})=g(sb+sb^{\prime})=gs(b)+gs(b^{\prime}).

The fact that
\bar{g}=gs
is the unique morphism with the property
\bar{g}p=g
follows from
\bar{g}ps=gs
which is the same as
\bar{g}=gs
.
∎

Proposition 3
.

Semi-biproducts of monoids are stable under pullback.

Proof.

Let
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
be a semi-biproduct of monoids displayed as the bottom row in the following diagram which is obtained by taking the pullback of
p
along an arbitrary morphism
h\colon{C\to B}
, with induced morphism
\langle k,0\rangle
and map
\langle sh,1\rangle
,

\textstyle{A\times_{B}C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{q\pi_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}}

5.
Conclusion

We have introduced the notion of semi-biproduct of monoids. Considerations on categorical aspects related to this notion can be found in
[
16
]
. Several directions using different techniques are pointed out there. The main issue involves dealing with morphisms and maps on the same ground. One possibility is to consider maps as an extra structure in higher dimensions
[
6
,
15
]
. Another one is to consider maps as imaginary morphisms
[
2
,
19
]
. It seems to be relevant the investigation on both directions and to determine whether they lead to different settings or if rather there is a more general setting which unifies both directions.
Developing a general categorical framework in which to study semi-biproducts is desirable due to several interesting cases that occur in different contexts. For example, semi-biproducts can be studied in the context of preordered monoids (see
[
17
]
, see also
[
7
]
for preordered groups) where the maps
q
and
s
can be required to be monotone maps rather than merely zero-preserving maps. The context of topological monoids
[
10
]
is also worthwhile studying since we can take the maps
q
and
s
to be continuous or not and possible variations on that.

A note on the paper ”Tang et al. [Bull Iran Math Soc (2019) doi:10.1007/s41980-019-00262-y]”
Mohammad Mahdi Motamedinezhad and Rahim Kargar ∗

Abstract
. Very recently Tang et al. [Bull Iran Math Soc (2019) doi:10.1007/s41980-019-00262-y] have studied some majorization results for two certain subclasses of the starlike functions associated with the sine and cosine functions defined by 𝒮 s ∗ \mathcal{S}^{*}_{s} and 𝒮 c ∗ \mathcal{S}^{*}_{c} , respectively. In this note we pointed out that the definition of the class 𝒮 c ∗ \mathcal{S}^{*}_{c} and it’s result are incorrect and give correct definition and result.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
30C45, 30C80

1.
Introduction

We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the family of analytic functions
f
on the open unit disc
\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and by
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{H}
the class of normalized functions
f
of the form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad(z\in\Delta).

The subclass of
\mathcal{A}
consisting of all univalent functions
f(z)
in
\Delta
will be denoted by
\mathcal{U}
.
Also we denote by
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{H}
the family of functions
f
on
\Delta
satisfying the conditions
\phi(0)=0
and
|\phi(z)|<1
. A function
\phi
belongs to the class
\mathcal{B}
is said to be a Schwarz function on
\Delta
.

Let two functions
f
and
g
belong to the class
\mathcal{H}
and there exists a Schwarz function
\phi
such that
f(z)=g(\phi(z))
. Then we say that
f
is subordinate to
g
, written as
f(z)\prec g(z)
or
f\prec g
. It is clear that if
f\prec g
, then we have

(1.2)

f(0)=g(0)\quad{\rm and}\quad f(\Delta)\subset g(\Delta).

In particular, if
g
belong to the class
\mathcal{U}
, then
f\prec g
if and only if the conditions (
1.2
) hold true. In the sequel, we recall the following definition from
[
7
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f
and
g
belong to the class
\mathcal{H}
. A function
f
is said to be majorized by
g
written as
f(z)\ll g(z)
or
f\ll g
, if there exists an analytic function
\psi
in
\Delta
, satisfying

(1.3)

|\psi(z)|\leq 1\quad{\rm and}\quad f(z)=\psi(z)g(z)

for all
z\in\Delta
.

For an analytic univalent function
\varphi
with
{\rm Re}\{\varphi(z)\}>0

(z\in\Delta)
and normalized by
\varphi(0)=1
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
, Ma and Minda
[
6
]
introduced the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
. A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\varphi)
if and only if
zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
is subordinate to
\varphi
.
By choosing some special function
\varphi
, several authors have defined many new subclasses of the starlike functions in recent years, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
.

Very recently, Tang et al.
[
11
]
introduced the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}
as follows:

\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec 1+\cos z\right\}.

Also, they proved the following theorem:

Theorem A.

Let the function
f\in\mathcal{A}
and suppose that
g\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}
. If
f(z)\ll g(z)
, then for
|z|\leq r_{0}

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq|g^{\prime}(z)|,

where
r_{0}
is the smallest positive root of the following equation:

(1-r^{2})(1-\cosh r)-2r=0.

First of all we notice that the definition of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}
is incorrect, because if
f\in\mathcal{A}
, then

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\Big|_{z=0}=1

and
(1+\cos z)|_{z=0}=2
while
(zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z))\prec(1+\cos z)
.
Therefore Theorem A is incorrect, too. In this note, we first will define the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}
correctly and obtain majorization property (the correct version of Theorem A) for it.

In order to prove the main result, we need the following lemma, see
[
8
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\psi(z)
be analytic in
\Delta
and satisfying
|\psi(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\Delta
. Then

(1.4)

|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1-|\psi(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

2.
Main Result

First, we recall that if
f(z)
is majorized by
g(z)
in
\Delta
and
g(0)=0
, then

\max_{|z|=r}|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\max_{|z|=r}|g^{\prime}(z)|

for each number
r
in the interval
[0,\sqrt{2}-1]
, see
[
7
]
. By choosing
g(z)=z
, the above assertion generalizes the following well-known theorem
[
1
]
:

Theorem B.

If the function
f\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies the conditions

|f(z)|\leq 1\quad{and}\quad f(0)=0,

then
|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1
for
|z|\leq\sqrt{2}-1
.

Following we define the correct version of the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{c}
and denote by
\mathbf{S}^{*}_{c}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
belongs to the class
\mathbf{S}^{*}_{c}
if and only if

(2.1)

\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\cos z.

Since
\cos z
is univalent in
\Delta
and
\cos z|_{z=0}=1
, thus the above Definition
2.1
is well-defined. Figure
1
shows the image of
\Delta
under the function
\cos z
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
and
g\in\mathbf{S}^{*}_{c}
. If
f(z)
is majorized by
g(z)
in
\Delta
, then

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad(|z|\leq r_{1}),

where
r_{1}\approx 0.391389
is the smallest positive root of the following equation:

(2.2)

(1-r^{2})\cos r-2r=0\quad(0<r<1).

: The boundary curve of
\cos(\Delta)
،

: The graph of
(1-r^{2})\cos r-2r=0
when
0<r<1

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}
be given by (
1.1
) and
g\in\mathbf{S}^{*}_{c}
. Thus by Definition
2.1
and by the subordination principle there exists a Schwarz function
\phi
such that

\frac{zg^{\prime}(z)}{g(z)}=\cos(\phi(z))\quad(z\in\Delta),

or equivalently

(2.3)

\frac{g(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{z}{\cos(\phi(z))}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Let
\phi(z)=Re^{it}
where
R\leq r=|z|<1
and
-\pi\leq t\leq\pi
. It is a simple exercise that

(2.4)

\cos r\leq\cos R\leq|\cos(\phi(z))|\leq\cosh R\leq\cosh r\quad(R\leq r<1).

From (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we get

(2.5)

\left|\frac{g(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}\right|\leq\frac{r}{\cos r}\quad(0<r<1).

Also, since
f
is majorized by
g
in
\Delta
, thus we can find an analytic function
\psi
in
\Delta
with
|\psi(z)|\leq 1
such that

(2.6)

f(z)=\psi(z)g(z)\quad(z\in\Delta).

Taking a simple derivation of relation (
2.6
) we get

(2.7)

f^{\prime}(z)=\psi^{\prime}(z)g(z)+\psi(z)g^{\prime}(z)=g^{\prime}(z)\left(\psi^{\prime}(z)\frac{g(z)}{g^{\prime}(z)}+\psi(z)\right).

If we apply (
2.5
) and Lemma
1.1
in (
2.7
), then we obtain

(2.8)

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\left(|\psi(z)|+\frac{1-|\psi(z)|^{2}}{1-r^{2}}.\frac{r}{\cos r}\right)|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad(|z|=r<1).

Letting
|\psi(z)|=\beta

(0\leq\beta\leq 1)
, the last inequality (
2.8
) becomes

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq\left(\beta+\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{1-r^{2}}.\frac{r}{\cos r}\right)|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad(|z|=r<1).

Now we define

h(r,\beta):=\beta+\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{1-r^{2}}.\frac{r}{\cos r}\quad(0\leq\beta\leq 1,0<r<1).

To obtain
r_{1}
it is enough to consider it as follows

r_{1}=\max\{r\in[0,1):h(r,\beta)\leq 1,\forall\beta\in[0,1]\}.

Thus

\displaystyle h(r,\beta)\leq 1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\beta(1-r^{2})\cos r+(1-\beta^{2})r}{(1-r^{2})\cos r}\leq 1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\beta(1-r^{2})\cos r+(1-\beta^{2})r\leq(1-r^{2})\cos r

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 0\leq(1-\beta)(1-r^{2})\cos r-(1-\beta^{2})r

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 0\leq(1-r^{2})\cos r-(1+\beta)r=:k(r,\beta).

Since
\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}k(r,\beta)=-r<0
, therefore we see that the above function
k(r,\beta)
gets its minimum value in
\beta=1
, namely

\min\{k(r,\beta):\beta\in[0,1]\}=k(r,1)=:k(r),

where

k(r)=(1-r^{2})\cos r-2r\quad(0<r<1).

Because
k(0)=1>0
and
k(1)=-2<0
, therefore we conclude that there exists a
r_{1}
such that
k(r)\geq 0
for all
r\in[0,r_{1}]
where
r_{1}
is the smallest positive
root of the Eq. (
2.2
) and concluding the proof.
∎

Since the identity function
g(z)=z
belongs to the class
\mathbf{S}^{*}_{c}
, thus letting
g(z)=z
in the above Theorem
2.1
we get the following result. Indeed, we improve the bound
\sqrt{2}-1
in the Theorem B.

Corollary 2.1
.

If the function
f\in\mathcal{H}
satisfies the conditions

f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1\quad{and}\quad|f(z)|<1,

then

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1\quad(|z|\leq 0.391389).

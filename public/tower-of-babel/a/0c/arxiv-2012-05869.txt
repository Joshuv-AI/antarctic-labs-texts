A Linear-Time Algorithm for Minimum k k -Hop Dominating Set of a Cactus Graph
A. Karim Abu-Affash, Paz Carmi, Adi Krasin

Abstract
Given a graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and an integer k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , a k k - hop dominating set D D of G G is a subset of V V , such that, for every vertex v ∈ V v\in V , there exists a node u ∈ D u\in D whose hop-distance from v v is at most k k . A k k -hop dominating set of minimum cardinality is called a minimum k k -hop dominating set . In this paper, we present linear-time algorithms that find a minimum k k -hop dominating set in unicyclic and cactus graphs. To achieve this, we show that the k k -dominating set problem on unicycle graph reduces to the piercing circular arcs problem , and show a linear-time algorithm for piercing sorted circular arcs, which improves the best known O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n\log n) -time algorithm.

Introduction

A
dominating set
of an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
is a subset
D\subseteq V
, such that every vertex in
V\setminus D
is adjacent to at least one vertex in
D
. For arbitrary graphs, the problem of computing a minimum dominating set is NP-complete
[
9
]
. Since the problem is a special case of minimum set cover, it can be approximated within
1+\log{|V|}

[
14
]
but it cannot be approximated within
(1-\varepsilon)\log{|V|}
, for any
\varepsilon>0

[
20
]
. The problem admits a PTAS on planar graphs
[
2
]
and on unit disk graphs
[
13
,
19
]
. For trees, cactus, and permutation graphs, the problem can be solved in linear time
[
5
,
6
,
11
]
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
on
n
vertices and an integer
k\geq 1
, a
k
-
hop dominating set
of
G
is a subset
D\subseteq V
, such that the hop distance from every vertex in
V\setminus D
is at most
k
from at least one vertex of
D
.
Recently, several works devoted to the problem of finding a minimum
k
-hop dominating set in graphs.
The problem has been shown to be NP-complete
[
1
,
4
]
in general graphs.
Demaine et al.
[
8
]
gave an
O(n^{4})
-time (fixed-parameter) algorithm for the problem on planar and map graphs.
Kundu and Majumder
[
16
]
and Barman et al.
[
3
]
presented an optimal linear-time algorithm for the problem on trees and interval graphs, respectively.
The
k
-
dominating number
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\gamma_{k}(G)
, is the cardinality of a minimum
k
-
hop dominating set
of
G
. The
k
-dominating number has been widely studied in the literature; see for example
[
7
,
10
,
12
,
18
,
21
]
.

Applications on dominating and
k
-hop dominating sets in graphs are known in several areas such as wireless networks
[
1
]
and social networks
[
4
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph.
G
is called a
unicyclic
graph if it contains exactly one cycle.
A vertex
u\in V
is called a
cut vertex
if removing
u
from
G
increases the number of components of
G
. A
block
of
G
is a maximal connected subgraph of
G
without any cut vertex.
G
is called a
cactus
graph if its blocks are either edges or cycles.

In
[
17
]
, Lan and Chang considered a different variant of
k
-dominating set on unicyclic and cactus graphs, where the goal is to find a minimum dominating set
D
, such that each vertex is dominated by at least
k
vertices from
D
.

In this paper, a linear-time algorithm is presented, which computes a minimum piercing set for sorted circular arcs, this improves the current
O(n\log n)
-time algorithm by Katz at al.
[
15
]
.
We then show how to utilize this result to obtain linear-time algorithms to the minimum
k
-hop dominating set in unicyclic and cactus graphs.

Preliminaries

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph.
For two vertices
u,v\in V
, let
d_{G}(u,v)
denote the hop-distance between
u
and
v
in
G
.
For a vertex
u\in V
, let
{{\cal{N}}}_{i}(u)\subset V
denote the
i
-neighborhood of

{{\cal{N}}}_{i}(u)=\{v\in V:d_{G}(u,v)\leq i\}
.
For a subset

, let
{{\cal{N}}}_{i}(D)=\bigcup_{u\in D}{{\cal{N}}}_{i}(u)
.
For a subset
D\subseteq V
and a vertex

, let
\delta_{D}(u)
denote the distance of
u
from
D
(i.e., the distance of
u
to the closest vertex in
D
).
A
k
-hop dominating set of
G
is a subset
D\subseteq V
, such that
\delta_{D}(u)\leq k
, for each vertex
u\in V
.
Throughout the rest of the paper, when we say distance we refer to hop-distance.

Problem 0

(kHDS)
.

Given an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
and an integer

, the goal in the k-Hop Dominating Set problem is to compute a minimum k-hop dominating set of

.

2.1
kHDS
on trees

Let

be a tree on

[
16
]
, Kundu and Majumder presented a linear-time algorithm that finds a minimum

-hop dominating set of

For completeness, based on their algorithm, we give a simpler (linear-time) implementation of the algorithm that will be used as a black-box throughout the rest of the paper.

Let

be the subtree of

denote the height of

. Let

denote the parent of

in

Observation 1
.

If

, then

is a minimum
k
-hop dominating set of
T_{r}
.

Observation 2
.

There exists a minimum
k
-hop dominating set of
T_{r}
that does not contain any leaf of
T_{r}
.

Let
B
be a subset of
V
.
A
partial

-hop dominating set of

, denoted by

-
Dom_{V}(B)
, is a subset
D
of

, such that

B\subseteq{{\cal{N}}}_{k}(D)
, i.e., for each vertex
v\in B
, there exists a vertex
u\in D
, such that

d_{T_{r}}(u,v)\leq k
.
The partial
k
-hop dominating number, denoted by
\Gamma_{V}(B)
, is the cardinality of a minimum
k
-
Dom_{V}(B)
.
The following lemma follows from Observation
2
.

Lemma 3
.

If
T_{r}
contains a leaf
v\notin B
, then
\Gamma_{V}(B)=\Gamma_{V\setminus\{v\}}(B)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T_{u}
be a subtree of
T_{r}
of height
k
rooted at
u
. If there exists a leaf
v\in B
of depth
k
in
T_{u}
,
then
\Gamma_{V}(B)=\Gamma_{V^{\prime}}(B^{\prime})+1
,
where
B^{\prime}=B\setminus{{\cal{N}}}_{k}(u)
and
V^{\prime}
is the set obtained from

by removing all vertices of
T_{u}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a minimum
k
-
Dom_{V^{\prime}}(B^{\prime})
. Then, by Observation
1
,
D\cup\{u\}
is a
k
-
Dom_{V}(B)
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{V}(B)\leq|D\cup\{u\}|=\Gamma_{V^{\prime}}(B^{\prime})+1
.
On the other hand, since there exists a leaf
v\in B
of depth
k
in
T_{u}
, any minimum
k
-
Dom_{V}(B)

D
must contain at least one vertex
w
in
T_{u}
, and
D^{\prime}=D\setminus\{w\}\cup\{u\}
is also a minimum
k
-
Dom_{V}(B)
, since each vertex in

, that is dominated by

, is also dominated by

.
Thus,
D^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}
is a
k
-
Dom_{V^{\prime}}(B^{\prime})
, since
{{\cal{N}}}_{k}(u)\nsubseteq B^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{V^{\prime}}(B^{\prime})\leq|D^{\prime}\setminus\{u\}|=\Gamma_{V}(B)-1
.
∎

The construction and correctness of the algorithm is based on
Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
.
In each iteration, the algorithm finds a subtree
T_{u}
of height exactly

, adds
u
to
D
, and removes all the leaves of
T_{r}
that are in

To implement the algorithm in

In each recursive call

(u)

. If so, we add

to

, remove all vertices of

from

(since they are already dominated by

Otherwise, we check whether at least one descendant

of

is added to

and

. If so, we remove all vertices of
T_{u}
from
T_{r}
and return

Otherwise, we check again whether

(in case the descendants reduced the height of

to

u
to
D
, remove all vertices of
T_{u}
from
T_{r}
, and return
\delta_{D}(u)=0
. Otherwise (

Quadratic-time algorithm for unicyclic graphs

In

Theorem 7

Let

2.3
Piercing segments on a line

Let

next(lastNext(p))
is not pierced by
{{\cal{P}}}_{\mu}(p)

to

.
Finally, we return the set
{{\cal{P}}}_{\mu}(p)
of minimum cardinality.
Let
{{\cal{P}}}
be the set returned by the algorithm. Thus, by Observation
9
and Observation
10

is a minimum piercing set of
{{\cal{A}}}
. In the following lemma, we bound the running time of our algorithm.

Lemma 11
.

Computing

takes
O(n)
time.

Proof.

It is not hard to see that computing

, for each
p\in X
, using Algorithm

O(n)
time.
Since each vertex in
G
has exactly one out-going edge, the number of edges of

is

∎

The following theorem concludes the result of this section.

•

If

•

If

Performing

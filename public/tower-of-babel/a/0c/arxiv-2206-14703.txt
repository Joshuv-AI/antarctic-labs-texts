A friendly iteration forcing that the four cardinal characteristics of ℰ \mathcal{E} can be pairwise different
Miguel A. Cardona

Abstract
. Let ℰ \mathcal{E} be the σ \sigma -ideal generated by the closed measure zero sets of reals. We use an ultrafilter-extendable matrix iteration of ccc posets to force that, for ℰ \mathcal{E} , their associated cardinal characteristics (i.e. additivity, covering, uniformity and cofinality) are pairwise different.

3.
Covering and uniformity of
\mathcal{E}

This section is dedicated to expand Bartoszyński’s and Shelah’s
[
BS92
, Section 4]
characterizations of the covering and the uniformity of
\mathcal{E}
. What follows will prepare the way for the proof of these characterizations

The main combinatorial tool we are going to use is described below:

For
b\in\omega^{\omega}
increasing let

\mathcal{S}_{b}:=\bigg\{\langle\varphi(n)\rangle_{n<\omega}:\forall n<\omega\bigg(\varphi(n)\subseteq 2^{[b(n),b(n+1))}\,\,\&\,\,\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\bigg)\bigg\},

and

\mathcal{S}_{b}^{*}:=\bigg\{\langle\varphi(n)\rangle_{n<\omega}:\forall n<\omega\bigg(\varphi(n)\subseteq 2^{[b(n),b(n+1))}\bigg)\,\,\&\,\,\exists^{\infty}n\bigg(\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\bigg)\bigg\}.

The motivation for this definition is the following result which will be useful. But we first begin remarking the following:

Remark 3.1
.

Let
b
be a function with domain
\omega
such that
b(i)\neq\emptyset
for all
i<\omega
, and let
h\in\omega^{\omega}
. Notice that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}<1\textrm{\ iff\ }\forall A\in[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}\left(\prod_{n\in A}\frac{h(n)}{|b(n)|}=0\right)

Lemma 3.2
.

For
b\in\omega^{\omega}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{b}^{*}
such that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{|b(n)|}<1
the set

H_{b,\varphi}:=\{x\in 2^{\omega}:\forall^{\infty}n\,(x{\upharpoonright}[b(n),b(n+1))\in\varphi(n))\}

belongs to
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Notice that
H_{b,\varphi}
is a countable union of the closed null sets

B_{b,\varphi}^{m}:=\{x\in 2^{\omega}:\forall n\geq m(x{\upharpoonright}[b(n),b(n+1))\in\varphi(n))\}\textrm{\ for\ }m\in\omega.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Lb}(B_{b,\varphi}^{m})=

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq m}\mathbf{Lb}(\{x\in 2^{\omega}:x{\upharpoonright}[b(n),b(n+1))\in\varphi(n)\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{n\geq m}\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{n\in A}\frac{1}{2^{n}}=0\textrm{\ where\ }A=\bigg\{n\geq m:\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\bigg\},

the latter inequality hold by
3.1
. Hence
\mathbf{Lb}(B_{b,\varphi}^{m})=0
for all
m<\omega
, so

H_{b,\varphi}=\bigcup_{m<\omega}B_{b,\varphi}^{m}

is an
F_{\sigma}
null set and thus belongs to
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

By the next lemma, we obtain a basis of
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
BS92
, Thm. 4.3]
)
.

Suppose that
C\in\mathcal{E}
. Then there are
b\in\omega^{\omega}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{b}
such that
C\subseteq H_{b,\varphi}
.

Proof.

Let us assume wlog that
C\subseteq 2^{\omega}
is a null set of type
F_{\sigma}
. Then
C
can be written as
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
where
\langle C_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
is an increasing family of closed sets of measure zero.

Note that each
C_{n}
is a compact set. It is easy to see that, if
K\subseteq 2^{\omega}
is a compact null set, then

\forall\varepsilon>0\,\forall^{\infty}n\,\exists T\subseteq 2^{n}\,(K\subseteq[T])\,\wedge\,\frac{|T|}{2^{n}}<\varepsilon).

Hence, we can define
b\in\omega^{\omega}
increasing as follows:
b(0):=0
and

b(n+1):=\min\bigg\{m>b(n):\exists T_{n}\subseteq 2^{m}\bigg(C_{n}\subseteq[T_{n}]\textrm{\ and\ }\frac{|T_{n}|}{2^{m}}\leq\frac{1}{4^{b(n)}}\bigg)\bigg\}.

Now define, for
n\in\omega
,

\varphi(n):=\{s{\upharpoonright}[b(n),b(n+1)):s\in T_{n}\}.

It is clear that
|\varphi(n)|\leq|T_{n}|
. Hence, for every
n<\omega
,

\frac{|\varphi(n)|}{2^{b(n+1)-b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{b(n)}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Thus,
\langle\varphi(n)\rangle_{n<\omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{b}
, and by
3.2

H_{b,\varphi}\in\mathcal{E}
. We also have
C\subseteq H_{b,\varphi}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4

Let

.

On the other hand,
b_{0}(m+1)>b_{1}^{*}(n+1)
, so
b_{1}^{*}(n+1)<b_{0}(m+1)<b_{1}(m+1)\leq b_{1}^{*}(n+1)
. Hence,
b_{1}^{*}(n+1)<b_{1}^{*}(n+1)
, which is a contradiction.

We have proved that that there are infinitely many

. Hence

. Indeed, for such

and

, then

\frac{|\varphi^{*}(n)|}{2^{|I_{n}^{*}|}}=\frac{|\varphi(m)|}{2^{|I_{m}|}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{m}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Also,
H_{b,\varphi}\subseteq H_{b_{1}^{*},\varphi^{*}}
.

(1): Once again, according to

, note that if

then

\forall^{\infty}n\,\exists m\,(I_{m}\subseteq I_{n}^{*}).

Now, for each

set
\varphi_{*}(n)
by

\varphi_{*}(n):=\begin{cases}\big\{s\in 2^{I_{n}^{*}}:I_{m}\subseteq I_{n}^{*}\wedge s{\upharpoonright}I_{m}\in\varphi(m)\big\}&\textrm{if $m$ is minimal such that }\\

In a way similar to

Definition 3.5

Let

For combinatorial purposes, we use the notion of closed null set in

Denote
\mathcal{E}(\prod b):=\{X\subseteq\prod b:X\text{\ is a closed measure zero}\}

and

When

is a continuous onto function, and preserve measure. Hence, this map preserves sets between

\mathcal{E}([0,1])

Since

\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{E}(\prod b)}\preceq_{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{Lc}_{*}(b,h)

and

For

We only have to check that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Lb}(B_{\varphi,m})

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\geq m}\mathbf{Lb}\left(\left\{x\in\prod b:x(n)\in\varphi(n)\right\}\right)

It is clear that if

\min\left(\{\mathfrak{d}\}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{*}}}_{b,h}:b,h\in\omega^{\omega}\mathrm{\ and\ }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{b(n)}<1\right\}\right)\leq\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})\leq\\
\leq\min\bigg\{\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{*}}}_{b,h}:b,h\in\omega^{\omega}\mathrm{\ and\ }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{b(n)}<1\bigg\}.

\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})<\mathfrak{d}

\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})=\min\bigg\{\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{*}}}_{b,h}:b,h\in\omega^{\omega}\textrm{\ and\ }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{b(n)}<1\bigg\}.

By
3.6

It remains to prove that

\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})\geq\min\left(\{\mathfrak{d}\}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{*}}}_{b,h}:b,h\in\omega^{\omega}\textrm{\ and\ }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{b(n)}<1\right\}\right).

To see this, assume
\kappa<\min\big(\{\mathfrak{d}\}\cup\big\{\mathfrak{d}^{\mathrm{Lc_{*}}}_{b,h}:b,h\in\omega^{\omega}\textrm{\ and\ }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(n)}{b(n)}<1\big\}\big)

For each

6.
Open problems

We conclude our work with a selection of open problems concerning
E
and
Theorem 5.6
, this selection being guided mainly by the author’s interest. We choose
problems that seem to demand further development of new iteration techniques, so that their solution might give new light on the interplay between forcing and cardinal characteristics.

Though in
Theorem 5.6
we separated the four cardinal characteristics of
\mathcal{E}
, the uniformity of
\mathcal{E}
appears equal to the covering of
\mathcal{N}
as well as the covering of
\mathcal{E}
appears equal to the uniformity of
\mathcal{N}
. In view of this, it would be interesting to improve our construction to allow these cardinals to be pairwise different, so we ask if we could separate the left side of Cichoń’s diagram along with the uniformity and the covering of
\mathcal{E}
.

Question 6.1
.

Are each one the following statements consistent with ZFC?

(6.1)

\aleph_{1}<\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N})<\mathfrak{b}<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})\\
<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}.

(6.2)

\aleph_{1}<\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N})<\mathfrak{b}<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})\\
<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}.

(6.3)

\aleph_{1}<\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})<\mathfrak{b}<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})\\
<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N})=\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}.

In
[
KST19
]
, it was constructed FAMS (finitely additive measures) along a FS (finite support) iteration to force

\aleph_{1}<\mbox{\rm add}(\mathcal{N})<\mathfrak{b}<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})<\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{c}.

Using the analog of this technique for ultrafilters, in
[
GMS16

[

]

\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E})
and
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})
without increasing
\mathfrak{b}
too much. One natural approach to solve this problem would be using FAMS along a matrix iteration. The main problem with this approach is that we do not know how to preserve
\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})
in this context.

In

it was proved the consistency of

\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{E})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{N})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{M})=\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{M})=\mbox{\rm non}(\mathcal{N})<\mbox{\rm cov}(\mathcal{E}).

small (

The key to what the lemma did to prove that random forcing preserves

was the characterization of closed measure zero sets
3.3
and
3.4
(a). Since eventually different real forcing does not add dominating reals, we may ask whether

and
3.4
would help to solve:

Question 6.2

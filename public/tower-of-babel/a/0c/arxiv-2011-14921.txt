A nice involution for multivariable polynomial rings
Wiland Schmale

Abstract
Zusammenfassung The principal minors of the Toeplitz matrix ( x i − j + 1 ) 1 ≤ i , j , ≤ n \left(x_{i-j+1}\right)_{1\leq i,j,\leq n} , where x 0 = 1 , x k = 0 x_{0}=1,x_{k}=0 if k ≤ − 1 k\leq-1 , directly determine an involution of the polynomial ring R ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R[x_{1},...,x_{n}] over any commutative ring R R .



keywords:
involution, multivariable polynomial ring, principal Toeplitz minors

In
[
1
]
principal minors have been used to transform a conjecture on certain Toeplitz pencils into an equivalent algebraic conjecture. A closer look on this transition to minors instead of the original variables shows that it is actually an involution.

Let
R
be any commutative ring,
R[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
the polynomial ring in the independent variables
x_{1},...,x_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, over
R
and

T(k)={\small\left[\begin{matrix}x_{1}&1&0&\dots&0\\
x_{2}&x_{1}&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{k-1}&x_{k-2}&\dots&&1\\
x_{k}&x_{k-1}&\dots&\dots&x_{1}\\
\end{matrix}\right],m_{k}=m_{k}(x_{1},...,x_{k})=\det(T(k))\text{ for }1\leq k\leq n,}

Note that
T(k)
is a submatrix and
m_{k}
a principal minor of
T(n)
. One has
m_{1}=x_{1}
,
m_{2}=x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}
and so on.

Let now the substitution ring homomorphism
\varphi
of
R[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
over
R
be defined as

\varphi:R[x_{1},...,x_{n}]\rightarrow R[x_{1},...,x_{n}],\ \ x_{k}\mapsto m_{k},\ \text{ for }1\leq k\leq n.

The following can then be observed:

Involution property:

\varphi\circ\varphi=\text{ id}_{R[x_{1},...,x_{n}]}=\text{identity map on }R[x_{1},...,x_{n}]

The proof will be based on the following recursive properties of principal minors of
T(n)
.

Recursions for
\boldsymbol{m_{k}}
and
\boldsymbol{x_{k}}
:
For
1\leq k\leq n

m_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}x_{i}m_{k-i},\ \ m_{0}:=1

(1)

and

x_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}m_{i}x_{k-i},\ \ x_{0}:=1

(2)

Proof:

In order to obtain (1) we will prove the following slightly more general relation for any first column entries
a_{1},...,a_{k}
:

\det{\small\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&1&0&\dots&0\\
a_{2}&x_{1}&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{k-1}&x_{k-2}&\dots&&1\\
a_{k}&x_{k-1}&\dots&\dots&x_{1}\\
\end{matrix}\right]}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}m_{k-i},\text{ where }\ m_{0}:=1.

This implies relation (1) as a special case. The instance “
k=1
” beeing trivial we proceed inductively and obtain for
1\leq k\leq n-1

\displaystyle\det{\small\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1}&1&0&\dots&0\\
a_{2}&x_{1}&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{k}&x_{k-1}&\dots&&1\\
a_{k+1}&x_{k}&\dots&\dots&x_{1}\\
\end{matrix}\right]}

\displaystyle=a_{1}\cdot\det T(k)-\det{\small\left[\begin{matrix}a_{2}&1&0&\dots&0\\
a_{3}&x_{1}&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{k}&x_{k-2}&\dots&&1\\
a_{k+1}&x_{k-1}&\dots&\dots&x_{1}\\
\end{matrix}\right]}

\displaystyle=a_{1}m_{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i+1}m_{k-i}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{(by induction)}

\displaystyle=a_{1}m_{(k+1)-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}m_{k+1-i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}m_{(k+1)-i}

In order to obtain (2) one only has to solve (1) for
x_{k}
which actually is the last term in the sum up to the factor
(-1)^{k-1}
:

We obtain

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}x_{k}

\displaystyle=m_{k}x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{i-1}x_{i}m_{k-i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}x_{i}m_{k-i}+m_{k}x_{0}

\displaystyle x_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i+k-1}x_{i}m_{k-i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{k-j+k-1}m_{j}x_{k-j},

where
k-j+k-1\equiv j-1\text{ mod }2
.
\Box

The recursions (1) and (2) lead us directly to the
involution property
.
It will be sufficient to show that
\varphi(\varphi(x_{k}))=x_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Since

\varphi(\varphi(x_{1}))=\varphi(m_{1}))=\varphi(x_{1})=m_{1}=x_{1}

by induction we obtain for
1\leq k\leq n-1

\displaystyle\varphi(\varphi(x_{k+1}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(m_{k+1})=\varphi(\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}x_{i}m_{(k+1)-i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}\varphi(x_{i})\varphi(m_{(k+1)-i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}m_{i}\varphi(\varphi(x_{(k+1)-i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i-1}m_{i}x_{(k+1)-i}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{(by induction)}

\displaystyle=x_{k+1}\hskip 56.9055pt\Box

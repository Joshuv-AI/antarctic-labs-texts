On large submodules in Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -modules Thanks: The author acknowledges support by the RNF grant 24-11-00124.
V. Manuilov

Abstract
. We consider several natural ways of expressing the idea that a one-sided ideal in a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra (or a submodule in a Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -module) is large, and show that they differ, unlike the case of two-sided ideals in C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. We then show how these different notions, for ideals and for submodules, are related. We also study some permanence properties for these notions. Finally, we use essential right ideals to extend the inner product on a Hilbert C ∗ C^{*} -module to a part of the dual module.



1.
Introduction

For a closed right ideal
J
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
there are at least two different ways to express the idea that
J\subset A
is large.

J
is
essential
if

(i1)

J\cap I\neq 0
for any non-zero right ideal
I\subset A
.

This may seem too algebraic and not suited for ideals in
C^{*}
-algebras as it uses general (non-closed) ideals, but the following equivalent property (see e.g.
[
8
]
) has no such algebraic flavor:

(i1^{\prime})

for each non-zero
a\in A
there exists
b\in A
such that
ab\in J
and
ab\neq 0
.

Using only closed submodules, one may define a similar notion:

a closed right ideal
J
is
topologically essential
if

(i2)

J\cap I\neq 0
for any non-zero closed right ideal
I\subset A
.

Clearly,
(i1)\Rightarrow(i2)
. Here we do not address the question, whether
(i1)
and
(i2)
are equivalent.

Another way to express largeness of a closed right ideal

is

(i3)

aJ=0
implies

for

As far as we know, there is no standard name for ideals satisfying

. A similar property was considered for Hilbert

-modules over

-algebras in
[
11

Pay attention to the order of multiplication in

(i4)

Ja=0
implies
a=0
for
a\in A
.

The property
(i4)
differs from
(i3)
and is studied in
[
7
]
, see Prop. 4.2. Our choice of the order of multiplication comes from the Hilbert

-module theory: if we consider a right ideal

-module over

, then

is equivalent to the condition that the orthogonal complement of

is zero, while

has no interpretation in terms of the inner product.

The properties
(i1)
and
(i3)
can be considered also for

ideals, and for them
(i1)
and
(i3)
are known to be equivalent (see, e.g., the comment preceding Theorem 3.1.8 in
[
12
]
). This equivalence is used in the theory of essential extensions of

-algebras. It is a common belief that, for one-sided ideals in
C^{*}
-algebras,
(i1)
and
(i3)
are not equivalent, but to our best knowledge there was no example showing the difference between these two properties.

Our first aim is to show that
(i1)
(and
(i2)
) implies
(i3)
, but not vice versae. The implication

[

]
, and by
[

.

Our next aim is to consider, for submodules of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules, the conditions similar to the above conditions of largeness for ideals. We define properties
(m1)
–
(m5)
similar to
(i1)
–
(i3)
for submodules and show how some of them are related. In particular, we introduce the condition
(m5)
as the uniqueness for extending bounded functionals from a submodule to the larger module, and show that

sits strictly between essentiality

and thickness

]

is thick in

.

We also compare some of the properties for a submodule

-module

in the

-algebra

(resp.,

in

Finally, we use essential right ideals to the problem of extending the inner product on a Hilbert

-module over any

(i2)
is strictly stronger than
(i3)

Proposition 2.1
.

If a closed right ideal
J
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is topologically essential then it is thick, i.e.
(i2)\Rightarrow(i3)
.

Proof.

Let
J\subset A
.
Suppose the contrary:
J\subset A
is essential, but
aJ=0
does not imply
a=0
. Then there exists a non-zero
a\in A
such that
a^{*}x=0
for any
x\in J
. Let
I=\overline{aA}
be the closure of the right ideal of all elements of the form
ab
,
b\in A
. As
J
is topologically essential,
J\cap I\neq 0
, i.e. there exists
x\in J
and a family
b_{n}\in A
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
x\neq 0
and
x=\lim_{n\to\infty}ab_{n}
. Then, as
(a^{*}x)^{*}=x^{*}a=0
, we have

x^{*}x=\lim_{n\to\infty}x^{*}ab_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(x^{*}a)b_{n}=0,

hence
x=0
. The contradiction proves the claim.
∎

Now we pass to the example showing that
(i3)
does not imply
(i1)
, and even
(i2)
.

Let

, and let

denote the

-algebra of compact operators on a separable Hilbert space

, equipped with a fixed orthonormal basis

be the

-algebra of continuous

-valued functions on

Let

be the function defined by

f(t)=\sum\nolimits_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\chi_{[0,t_{i}]}(t)p_{i},\quad t\in[0,1],

where
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of the subset

and
p_{i}
denotes the rank one projection onto the vector

. Note that

is continuous at irrational points and is not continuous at rational points. Set

i.e.
J
consists of
\mathbb{K}
-valued functions

that make the product

continuous. Clearly,

is a right ideal in

and is closed.

This ideal can be described in more detail. After we fix the basis
\{e_{i}\}
, a compact operator

can be viewed as an infinite matrix,

, where
x_{ij}=(e_{i},xe_{j})
. So, a
\mathbb{K}
-valued function can be viewed as an infinite matrix with continuous functions as entries. Let
x\in J
,

, where each

.

Lemma 2.2
.

The ideal
J
consists of matrices such that the functions

vanish at
r_{i}
for any
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

As
\chi_{[0,t_{i}]}p_{i}
is discontinuous only at

, the

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

J
is thick.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a non-zero

such that
ax=0
for any

. Suppose that

for some

and for some

. As

is continuous, we may assume without loss of generality that

differs from all

,

. Let

denote the matrix unit in

at the intersection of

-th row and

-th column, considered as a constant function on
X
. Let

be a function such that

. Set

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

J
is not topologically essential.

Proof.

We will construct a non-zero (closed) ideal

such that

. To this end, let

be a unit vector such that its coordinates

differ from zero for each

. If

Let

∎

3.
Intersection of thick ideals

Note that the intersection of two (topologically) essential closed right ideals,
J
and
I
, is (topologically) essential by a very simple reason:
(J\cap I)\cap K=J\cap(I\cap K)\neq 0
for any non-zero closed right ideal

Let

(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{\infty}

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\eta_{i}|^{2}<1/2

(1)

and

, hence

is invertible in

. Set

. Clearly,

, and

is a closed right ideal isomorphic to

. As

is thick as well. Indeed, if

.

The ideal

Proof.

Let

vanish at

.

As

x_{1j}=\frac{1}{\eta_{i}}(y_{ij}-x_{ij}).

(2)

As the right hand side in (

Let

4.
Case of Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules and their submodules

The definitions of essential and thick right ideals are standardly generalized to right submodules by replacing (closed) right ideals by (closed) right submodules in modules over a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Let
N
be a right Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, and let
M\subset N
be its closed sibmodule.

Functionals on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules give one more way to express the idea that
M
is large in
N
. Here are three properties that characterize largeness of a submodule in a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module.

(m1)

M
is
essential
in
N
if
M\cap K\neq 0
for any (not neccessarily closed) non-zero submodule
K\subset N
.

(m3)

M
is
thick
in
N
if
M^{\perp}:=\{n\in N:\langle n,m\rangle=0\ \ \forall m\in M\}=0
.

(m5)

M
is
functionally thick
in

if
f|_{M}=0
implies

for any functional

on
N
.

In the case when
N=A
and

is a closed right ideal,
(m1)
and
(m3)
coincide with
(i1)
and
(i3)
, respectively. It is also possible to generalize
(i2)
and
(i4)
to submodules in an obvious way, but we do not pursue this here.

Theorem 4.1
.

One has
(m1)\Rightarrow(m5)\Rightarrow(m3)
, and all three properties are different.

Proof.

For
n\in N
set
J_{n}=\{a\in A:na\in M\}
. Then
J_{n}\subset A
is a closed right ideal.

We begin with the following well-known statement, for which we could not find a reference.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
M\subset N
is an essential submodule, then
J_{n}\subset A
is an essential right ideal for any
n\in N
.

Proof.

Let us prove
(i1^{\prime})
for
J_{n}
. Let
a\in A
,
a\neq 0
. Consider
n^{\prime}=na\in N
. If
n^{\prime}=0
then
n^{\prime}b=0
for any

, and we may choose

to satisfy

. If

then, as

is essential, there exists

such that

,

(this condition, similar to

). In both cases, there exists

. This means that

For any Hilbert

6.

•

x^{*}u_{\lambda}au_{\lambda}x\leq x^{*}x
. Passing here to the limit, we obtain that
x^{*}ax\leq x^{*}x
.

To prove that
\|a\|\leq 1
, consider first the case when
A
is unital. Then
1-a
is selfadjoint and
x^{*}(1-a)x=x^{*}x-x^{*}ax\geq 0
for any
x\in J
. Then, by Lemma
7.5
,
1-a
is positive, hence
\|a\|\leq 1
.

For the non-unital case we have to pass to the unitalization
A^{+}
of
A
and to notice that
J
is essential in
A^{+}
as well. Indeed, let
I
be a non-zero right ideal in
A^{+}
. Suppose that
I\cap A=0
in
A^{+}
. Then
I
is the set of all scalars, which is not a right ideal, hence
I^{\prime}=I\cap A\subset A
is a non-zero ideal in
A^{+}
, hence in
A
. Then
I^{\prime}\cap J
is non-zero by essentiality of
J
in
A
. It remains to apply Lemma
7.5
in
A^{+}
.
∎

For
f\in M_{0}
, let
\|f\|_{M^{\prime}}=\sup_{m\in M,\|m\|\leq 1}\|f(m)\|
be the norm of
f
in
M^{\prime}
, and let
\|f\|^{2}=\|\langle f,f\rangle\|
.

Theorem 7.7
.

\|f\|_{M^{\prime}}=\|f\|
for any
f\in M_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
c=\langle f,f\rangle
. Then

\|fx\|^{2}=\|\langle fx,fx\rangle\|=\|x^{*}cx\|\leq\|x\|^{2}\|c\|.

By Lemma
7.6
,

\displaystyle\|c\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|x^{*}c^{2}x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|x^{*}c\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|x^{*}\langle f,f\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|\langle fx,f\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|f(fx)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{\prime}}^{2}\sup_{x\in J,\|x\|=1}\|fx\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{\prime}}^{2}\|c\|,

whence
\|f\|^{2}=\|c\|\leq\|f\|_{M^{\prime}}^{2}
.

On the other hand, as
M\subset M_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in M_{0},\|g\|\leq 1}\|\langle g,f\rangle\|\geq\sup_{m\in M,\|m\|\leq 1}\|\langle m,f\rangle\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in M,\|m\|\leq 1}\|f(m)\|\ =\ \|f\|_{M^{\prime}}.

∎

Theorem 7.8
.

\widetilde{M}\subset M^{\prime}
is a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over
A
with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
that extends the inner product on
M
. This extension of the inner product is unique.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.7
, the completion of the pre-Hilbert
C^{*}
-module
M_{0}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
coinsides with its closure in
M^{\prime}
.

To prove uniqueness, consider two inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{i}
,
i=1,2
, on
\widetilde{M}
that coincide on
M
. Let
g,f\in M_{0}
,
\langle g,f\rangle_{i}=a_{i}
. Let
J\subset A
be an essential closed right ideal such that
fJ,gJ\subset M
. Then
x^{*}a_{1}y=\langle gx,fy\rangle=x^{*}a_{2}y
for any
x,y\in J
, i.e.
x^{*}(a_{2}-a_{1})y=0
. As
J
is thick,
x^{*}a_{2}-x^{*}a_{1}=0
for any
x\in J
. Passing to adjoints, we get
(a_{2}-a_{1})x=0
for any
x\in J
, whence
a_{2}=a_{1}
.
∎

8.
Examples

Example 1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}(H)\subset A\subset\mathbb{B}(H)

A

. Then
f=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in M^{\prime}
if the sums
\sum_{i=1}^{j}f_{i}^{*}f_{i}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, are uniformly bounded. Then the series
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i}^{*}f_{i}x
is norm convergent for any
x\in J
, hence
fJ\subset M
for any

, hence
M_{1}=M^{\prime}
. For general
A

M_{0}

A=\mathbb{B}(H)
then the series
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i}^{*}f_{i}
strongly converges to an element of
A
, hence determines an element of
A
as a multiplier, and the same holds for the series
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i}^{*}g_{i}
for
g\in M^{\prime}
. Thus,
M_{0}=\widetilde{M}=M^{\prime}
.

.

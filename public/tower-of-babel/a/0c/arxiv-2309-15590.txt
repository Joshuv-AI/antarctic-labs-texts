Self-energy correction to energy levels of highly charged ions in a path integral formalism
S. Banerjee, Z. Harman

Abstract
Self-energy corrections to the energy levels of bound electrons are calculated in the framework of path integrals. We arrive at the full fermion propagator, using methods of functional integrals, in the form of Schwinger-Dyson equation (SDE). From the full fermion SDE, the self-energy corrected propagator is identified and the energy shift is obtained from the poles of the spectral function. The numerical calculations are performed using complex contour integrals and the B-spline representation of basis functions. We identify ions with Lamb shifts observable via modern mass spectrometric methods.

II
Schwinger-Dyson equation for the bound-fermion propagator

We begin by deriving the complete expression for the dressed bound-fermion propagator.
This is accomplished by defining the SDE for the fermionic propagator using path integrals, in analogy to Ref.
Bufalo et al. 2011
. The QED Lagrangian
is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}(x)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial^{\mu}A_{\mu})^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle+\bar{\psi}(x)(i\not{D}-m)\psi(x)-\bar{\psi}(x)e\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}(x)\psi(x)\,,

where
D_{\mu}(x)=\partial_{\mu}(x)+ie\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(x)
,

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
is the field of the nucleus;
A_{\mu}
is the gauge-field operator for the photon field,
\psi(x)
is the field of the electron,
m
is its bare mass,

e
is the elementary charge, and
F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu}
is the electromagnetic field operator or the curvature of the field. The
\gamma
are the
usual Dirac matrices and
\mu,\nu\in\{0,1,2,3\}
represent the Lorentz indices. We apply the background field method
DeWitt 1967
;
Abbott 1981
;
Abbott 1982
, where we consider the
external field to be the classical background field of the nuclear charge that is gauge invariant, and the photon gauge field is treated as a fluctuation whose gauge has been
fixed as seen in the second term of Eq. (
1
), where
\xi
is the gauge-fixing coefficient. We do not need to concern
ourselves with fixing the gauge for the external field since the effective action in the presence or absence of a classical background field is equivalent, and hence the choice
of gauge plays no role. We can also safely ignore this gauge-fixing term since we are interested in deriving the electron propagator in the presence of an external field,
and it does not contribute to the effective action of the theory.

The generating functional is constructed using the above Lagrangian, and Grassmann-valued sources
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
of the fermion fields
\bar{\psi}
and
\psi
,
respectively, and the source
J_{\mu}
for the gauge field:

\displaystyle Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}A\exp\biggl\{i\int d^{4}x[\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}

(2)

\displaystyle+J_{\mu}(x)A^{\mu}(x)+\bar{\psi}(x)\eta(x)+\bar{\eta}(x)\psi(x)]\biggr\}\,,

where
\mathcal{D}
represents the integral measure over all field configurations.
To arrive at the SDE, we consider that the functional integral of a total derivative is zero, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}[\phi]\functionalderivative{\phi}=0\,,

where
\functionalderivative{\phi}
is the functional derivative w.r.t.
\phi
, which represents any arbitrary field variable.
For the electron propagator, the derivative is taken with respect to the
fermion field
\bar{\psi}(x)
,

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{\psi}\psi A]\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar{\psi}(x)}\exp\{i[\mathcal{S}(\bar{\psi},\psi,A)

(3)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\differential[4]{x}(J_{\mu}(x)A^{\mu}(x)+\bar{\psi}(x)\eta(x)+\bar{\eta}(x)\psi(x))]\}=0\,,

with
\mathcal{S}=\int\differential[4]{x}\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}
being the action.
Eq. (
3
) can be written in terms of a differential equation in the generating functional
Z

\displaystyle\Biggl[\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}}{\delta\bar{\psi}(x)}

\displaystyle\left(-i\frac{\delta}{\delta J_{\mu}},i\frac{\delta}{\delta\eta},-i\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\eta(x)\Biggr]Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]=0\,.

We take the functional derivative of the action
\mathcal{S}
by implementing the Gâteux derivative method
Gâteaux 1919
;
Behmardi and Nayeri 2008
, and arrive at the differential equation

\displaystyle\Biggl[

\displaystyle\Biggl(i\not{\partial}-m-e\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}-e\gamma^{\mu}(-i)\functionalderivative{J^{\mu}(x)}\Biggr)

(5)

\displaystyle\times(-i)\functionalderivative{\bar{\eta}(x)}+\eta(x)\Biggr]Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]=0\,.

To obtain the two-point Green’s function of the electron, we perform another derivation with respect to the source field
\eta(y)

and represent it in terms of the functional for the connected Green’s functions
W
, (
Z=e^{W}
)

\displaystyle e^{W[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]}\biggl[\delta(x-y)-\biggl(i\not{\partial}-m-e\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}

(6)

\displaystyle-ie\gamma^{\mu}\frac{(-i)\delta W}{\delta J^{\mu}(x)}-ie\gamma^{\mu}(-i)\functionalderivative{J^{\mu}(x)}\biggr)S(x,y)\biggr]=0\,.

Here,
S(x,y)
is the bound-fermion propagator. We rewrite Eq. (
6
) in terms of classical fields, in analogy to
Bufalo et al. 2011

\displaystyle\delta(x-y)-\biggl(i\not{\partial}-m-e\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}

(7)

\displaystyle-ie\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}-ie\gamma^{\mu}(-i)\functionalderivative{J^{\mu}(x)}\biggr)S(x,y)=0\,.

We obtain the equation

\displaystyle(-i)\frac{\delta S(x,y)}{\delta J^{\mu}(x)}

(8)

\displaystyle\qquad=-i\int\differential[4]{z}\frac{\delta A_{\nu}(z)}{\delta J^{\mu}(x)}\functionalderivative{A_\nu(z)}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma}{\delta\psi(x)\delta\bar{\psi}(y)}\right)^{-1}\,.

Using the expressions for the complete bound-electron propagator, photon propagator and the electron-photon vertex
function
\Gamma^{\mu}
, one obtains

\displaystyle(-i)\frac{\delta S(x,y)}{\delta J^{\mu}(x)}

(9)

\displaystyle=-e\int\differential[4]{z}\differential[4]{u}\differential[4]{w}D_{\mu\nu}(x-z)S(x,w)\Gamma^{\nu}(w,u;z)S(u,y)\,.

Using Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
), setting the external source fields equal to zero, and considering that the nuclear field is a static scalar field,
Eq. (
7
) reduces to

\displaystyle\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(i\not{\partial}-m-e\gamma^{0}\mathcal{A}_{0})S(x,y)

(10)

\displaystyle+\int\differential[4]{u}\Sigma(x,u)S(u,y)\,,

where

\Sigma(x-y)=-ie^{2}\gamma^{\mu}\int\differential[4]{z}\differential[4]{w}D_{\mu\nu}(z-x)S(x,w)\Gamma^{\nu}(w,y;z)\,.

(11)

To obtain the SDE for the bound propagator in coordinate space, we multiply Eq. (
10
) throughout with the inverse propagator, yielding

\displaystyle S^{-1}

\displaystyle(x,y)=(i\not{\partial}-m-e\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{0})\delta(x-y)

(12)

\displaystyle-ie^{2}\gamma^{\mu}\int\differential[4]{z}\differential[4]{w}D_{\mu\nu}(z-x)S(x,w)\Gamma^{\nu}(w,y;z)\,.

This equation is pictorially represented in Fig. (
1
).

Diagrammatic representation of the Schwinger-Dyson equation for the electron propagator. The double line represents the propagator in the nuclear

III
Derivation of the self-energy shift

The second term on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
12
), given by Eq. (
11
) gives the SE corrected
bound propagator and is the well-known SE operator. Fourier transforming this with respect to the time variable and considering the electron-photon
vertex operator
\Gamma^{\nu}
to
lowest order
, i.e. replacing it with the matrix
\gamma^{\nu}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma(E)=-ie^{2}\int\frac{\differential{\omega}}{2\pi}\gamma^{\mu}S(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w};E-\omega)D_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x};\omega)\gamma^{\nu}\,.

(13)

Using this SE operator and Feynman rules
Peskin and Schroeder 1995
, we can construct the SE corrected Green’s function between points
i
and
f
:

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{x}_{f},E_{f};\mathbf{x}_{i},E_{i})\sim ie^{2}\bigg(\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigg)^{2}\int d^{3}\mathbf{z}_{1}\int d^{3}\mathbf{z}_{2}\int d\omega

\displaystyle\qquad\times S(\mathbf{x}_{f},\mathbf{z}_{2};E_{f})e\gamma^{\mu}S(\mathbf{z}_{2},\mathbf{z}_{1};\eta)e\gamma^{\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad\times S(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{i};E_{i})D_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{z}_{1}-\mathbf{z}_{2};\omega)\delta(E_{f}-E_{i})\,.

(14)

A Green’s function, e.g. that in Eq. (
14
) can also be given in the spectral representation

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{x}_{f},\mathbf{x}_{i},E)=\sum_{n}\frac{\phi_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{f})\bar{\phi}_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}{E-E_{n}(1-i\varepsilon)}\,

(15)

in terms of the perturbed states
{\phi}_{n}
, where we have made the replacement
E=E_{i}=E_{f}
, and
\varepsilon
is infinitesimally small. The spectral function
G_{a}(E)\equiv\bra{a}G(E)\ket{a}

for a given atomic reference state
\ket{a}
, which contains the perturbed eigenenergies
E_{n}
of the basis states
\phi_{n}
, has a pole around the perturbed
eigenenergy of state
\ket{a}
:

\displaystyle G_{a}(E)\approx\frac{C_{a}}{E-E_{a}}\,,

(16)

where the constant
C_{a}
is the residue term. While in case of the path integral treatment of the quantum mechanical H atom the poles can be simply
seen in the analytical expression of the Green’s function
Duru and Kleinert 1979
;
Kayed and Inomata 1984
, here the energies can be determined from the poles using
complex contour integration
Messiah 1961
;
Lepage 1977
;
Shabaev 2002
;
Shabaev 1990
. Considering a small contour
\Gamma
which surrounds an
isolated pole at the bound-state energy
E_{a}
, one can easily obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,E\,G_{a}(E)=E_{a}C_{a}\,,\quad\text{and}

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,G_{a}(E)=C_{a}\,.

(18)

The ratio of the above equations gives us the level energy

\displaystyle E_{a}=\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,E\,G_{a}(E)}{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,G_{a}(E)}\,.

(19)

The energy shift with respect to the unperturbed (Dirac) energy is

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}=\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}(E)}{1+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta G_{a}(E)}\,,

(20)

where

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}\equiv\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}+\Delta E_{a}^{(2)}+\dots\,,

\displaystyle\Delta G_{a}\equiv\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}+\Delta G_{a}^{(2)}+\dots\,,

have been expanded in terms of the fine-structure constant,
\alpha
, in a perturbation series, and
\Delta G_{a}=G_{a}(E)-\Delta G_{a}^{(0)}
;
\Delta G_{a}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{E-E_{a}^{(0)}}
being the zeroth-order contribution.
We expand the Eq. (
20
) in a geometric series and obtain

\displaystyle\Delta E^{(1)}_{a}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\,.

(21)

We can now construct the function in Eq. (
16
) from the Green’s function in Eq. (
14
) and consider a single state with eigenenergy
E_{a}
,

\displaystyle G_{a}(E)=\bigg(\frac{i}{2\pi}\bigg)^{2}\frac{1}{(E-E_{a})^{2}}

(22)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int d\omega\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{na}}{E-\omega-E_{n}(1-i\varepsilon)}\,,

where we have introduced the photon exchange operator
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999

I(\mathbf{z}_{1}-\mathbf{z}_{2};\omega)=e^{2}\alpha^{\mu}\alpha^{\nu}D_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{z}_{1}-\mathbf{z}_{2};\omega)

with
\alpha^{\mu}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}
.
Thus, we have the SE correction to the bound-state energy level, using Eqs. (
17
)-(
21
), as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}=\bra{a}\Sigma(E_{a})-\delta m\gamma^{0}\ket{a}\,,

(23)

where
\bra{a}\Sigma(E_{a})\ket{b}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int d\omega\sum_{n}\frac{\bra{an}I(\omega)\ket{nb}}{E-\omega-E_{n}(1-i\varepsilon)}
;
\ket{an}
denotes
a two-electron tensor product state
and the
\delta m
is the mass counter-term.

Eq. (
23
) is however fraught with divergences and we follow
Blundell 1992
;
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999
and separate the SE shift into zero-, one-,
and many-potential terms

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma(E_{a})\ket{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(0)}(E_{a})\ket{a}

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(1)}(E_{a})\ket{a}+\bra{a}\Sigma^{(2+)}(E_{a})\ket{a}\,.

The individual terms in Eq. (
24
) can be written in terms of the Coulomb-Dirac Green’s functions
Kayed and Inomata 1984
,
and the photon propagator using perturbative path integrals
Bhagwat and Lawande 1989
;
Camblong and Ordóñez 2004
;
Schulman 1981
. The zero-potential term is given as

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(0)}(E_{a})\ket{a}

(25)

\displaystyle=2i\alpha\int\differential{\omega}\int\differential[3]{\vb{r}_1}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_2}\psi^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\alpha^{\mu}G^{(0)}(E_{a}-\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\alpha^{\nu}D_{\mu\nu}(\omega)\psi_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\,,

where
G^{(0)}(E_{a}-\omega)
is the free electron Green’s function.

Similarly, the one-potential term can be expressed as

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(1)}(E_{a})\ket{a}

(26)

\displaystyle=2i\alpha\int\differential{\omega}\int\differential[3]{\vb{r}_1}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_2}\psi^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\alpha^{\mu}G^{(1)}(E_{a}-\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\alpha^{\nu}D_{\mu\nu}(\omega)\psi_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\,,

where the Green’s function for a single interaction of the electron with the nuclear potential
V
in terms of the free Green’s function
is
Bhagwat and Lawande 1989
;
Camblong and Ordóñez 2004
;
Schulman 1981

\displaystyle G^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{1};E_{a}-\omega)

(27)

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential[3]{\vb{x_1}}V(\mathbf{x_{1}})\right]\left[\prod_{i=0}^{1}G^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{i+1},\mathbf{x}_{i};E_{a}-\omega)\right]

with
\mathbf{r}_{2}=\mathbf{x}_{2}
and
\mathbf{r}_{1}=\mathbf{x}_{0}
. Following the same methodology, the many-potential term is given by

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(2+)}(E_{a})\ket{a}

(28)

\displaystyle=2i\alpha\int\differential{\omega}\int\differential[3]{\vb{r}_1}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_2}\psi^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\alpha^{\mu}G^{(2+)}(E_{a}-\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\alpha^{\nu}D_{\mu\nu}(\omega)\psi_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\,.

The Green’s function for the many-potential term between the coordinates
\mathbf{r}_{3}
and
\mathbf{r}_{4}
, as seen in Fig. (
2
),
with
n
insertions of the nuclear interaction where
n\rightarrow\infty
, can be given in terms of the free Green’s function as

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r}_{4},\mathbf{r}_{3};E_{a}-\omega)

(29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg\{\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential[3]{\vb{x}_k}V(\mathbf{x}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left[\prod_{i=0}^{n}G^{(0)}(\mathbf{x}_{i+1},\mathbf{x}_{i};E_{a}-\omega)\right]\Bigg\}\,.

This effectively gives us the exact Dirac-Coulomb Green’s function
Kayed and Inomata 1984
.

Diagrammatic representation of the many-potential Green’s function. Single lines represent a free propagator, while wave lines terminated by a cross

The energy shift due to the many-potential contribution can thus be written as

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(2+)}(E_{a})\ket{a}

(30)

\displaystyle=2i\alpha\int\differential{\omega}\int\differential[3]{\vb{r}_1}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_2}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_3}\differential[3]{\vb{r}_4}\psi^{\dagger}_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{2})\alpha^{\mu}

\displaystyle\quad\times G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{4};E_{a}-\omega)V(\mathbf{r}_{4})G(\mathbf{r}_{4},\mathbf{r}_{3};E_{a}-\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times V(\mathbf{r}_{3})G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{3},\mathbf{r}_{1};E_{a}-\omega)\alpha^{\nu}D_{\mu\nu}(\omega)\psi_{a}(\mathbf{r}_{1})\,.

Using the spectral representation for the free and bound-electron propagators
Blundell 1992
, the energy shift is given as

\displaystyle\bra{a}\Sigma^{(2+)}(E_{a})\ket{a}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\differential{\omega}

(31)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\alpha,\beta,i}\frac{\bra{i}V\ket{\beta}\bra{a\beta}I(\omega)\ket{\alpha a}\bra{\alpha}V\ket{i}}{(E_{a}-\omega-\epsilon_{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{+}})(E_{a}-\omega-\epsilon_{i}{\varepsilon^{+}})(E_{a}-\omega-\epsilon_{\beta}{\varepsilon^{+}})}\,,

where
\ket{\alpha}
and
\ket{\beta}
are free-electron states, the
\ket{i}
represents bound-electron states, and we use the notation
\varepsilon^{+}=(1-i\varepsilon)
.

Following
Blundell 1992
;
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999
, we introduce frequency-dependent effective basis functions

\displaystyle\ket{\phi^{(\pm)}_i(\omega)}=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{\bra{\alpha}V\ket{i}}{\omega-\epsilon_{\alpha}(1\mp i\varepsilon)}\ket{\alpha}\,,

(32)

which simplify Eq. (
31
) to the form

\bra{a}\Sigma^{(2+)}(E_{a})\ket{a}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int\differential{\omega}\sum_{i}\frac{\bra{a\phi_i^{(-)}}I(\omega)\ket{\phi_i^{(+)} a}}{E_{a}-\omega-\epsilon_{i}({1-i\varepsilon})}\,.

(33)

One can further reduce Eq. (
33
) by expanding the numerator inside the integral on the r.h.s. in partial waves
Blundell 1992
such that the angular
integrations can be performed analytically, yielding the reduced many-potential term expressed with generalized Slater integrals
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999
.
The zero- and one-potential terms with the well-known loop functions are regularized in momentum space following Ref.
Yerokhin and Shabaev 1999
, and the numerical calculations
for the zero-, one-, and many-potential terms are performed using a B-spline representation of basis states
Johnson et al. 1988
;
Sapirstein and Johnson 1996
;
Grant 2009
,
implementing the dual kinetic balance approach
Shabaev et al. 2004
.

V
Summary

We develop an alternate formalism for the evaluation of the SE shift of atomic energy levels.
The Green’s function for the SE corrected bound electron is extracted from the Schwinger-Dyson equation derived using the functional integral technique.
We avoid the operator formalism and present an elegant and intuitive framework that preserves all the symmetries of the system, and also
treats the background nuclear field non-perturbatively. This formalism can be extended to study higher-order radiative corrections,
and energy shifts in many-electron systems. The functional methods developed in this work can be naturally generalized
to various hypothetical gauge bosons, enabling the detailed study of new physics-effects in atomic spectra.

Embeddable partial Hadamard matrices related to the projective planes
Hadi Kharaghani, Sho Suda, Yash Shamsundar Khobragade

Abstract
The existence of a projective plane of order p ≡ 3 ( mod 4 ) p\equiv 3\pmod{4} , where p p is a prime power, is shown to be equivalent to the existence of a balancedly multi-splittable embeddable p 2 × p ⁡ ( p + 1 ) p^{2}\times p(p+1) partial Hadamard matrix.

Introduction

Balancedly splittable Hadamard (BSH) matrices were introduced by Kharaghani-Suda in
[
4
]
.
The definition is broad in scope in
[
4
]
. Still, in this paper, we will consider the following definition: a Hadamard matrix of order
n^{2}
is balancedly splittable if it is Hadamard equivalent to the block form
\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}&H_{2}\end{bmatrix}
, where
H_{1}
is a
n^{2}\times\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}-n)
submatrix, and all the off-diagonal entries of
H_{1}H_{1}^{\top}
are of the absolute values
\frac{n}{2}
.
The plentiful of such matrices was shown in an extension to balancedly splittable orthogonal designs (BSOD) by Kharaghani-Pender-Suda in
[
3
]
every BSH matrix of order
n^{2}
leads to two optimal sets of equiangular lines and unbiased bases. In a recent paper
[
5
]
, Kharaghani-Suda introduced a class of balancedly multi-splittable Hadamard matrices (BMSH) and demonstrated that their existence is equivalent to projective planes of doubly even order. Such a property limits the existence of BMSH, possibly only to those of order
4^{m}
.

In this work, the focus is on the prime powers
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Plenty of balancedly multi-splittable partial Hadamard (BMSPH) matrices exist for the prime powers. Such matrices are row and column regular and embeddable in a Hadamard matrix. These features will be used to introduce a class of balanced multi-splittable BIBD (BMSBIBD). The paper’s main result is a complete characterization of BMSPH matrices and BMSBIBD in terms of orthogonal arrays and projective planes.

5
Equiangular lines

One of the most critical aspects of balanced multi-splittable partial Hadamard matrices is the existence of a relatively large lower bound for the number of specific equiangular lines in the sense that the inner product of the distinct rows in any of the half of the blocks of the constructed partial Hadamard matrix remains the same in absolute values.

Lemma 5.1
.

For an odd prime power
p
there are
p^{2}
equiangular lines in
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}
corresponding to
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}
. Furthermore, there are
p^{2}+p\choose{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}
choices of block columns of order
p^{2}\times p
leading to the same number of equiangular lines.

Proof.

A balancedly multi-splittable partial Hadamard matrix of order
p^{2}\times(p^{2}+p)
is constructed in Theorem
2.1
. The inner product of any two rows of any of the
p^{2}+p\choose{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}
choices of block columns is in
\{-\frac{p+1}{2},\frac{p+1}{2}\}
. The corresponding
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}
, and the result follows.
∎

An asymptotic result concerning the existence of equiangular lines follows.

Corollary 5.2

The ratio of the number of equiangular lines constructed in Theorem
2.1
in
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}
to the largest possible number of equiangular lines for
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}

p

Proof.

The largest possible number of equiangular lines in
\mathbb{R}^{\frac{p^{2}+p}{2}}
corresponding to
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}
is

(p+1)^{2}
. The required ratio is
(\frac{p+1}{p})^{2}
, which is close to one for large values of
p

∎

Remark 5.4
.

It follows from Corollary
5.3
that adding more than one line may be possible only for

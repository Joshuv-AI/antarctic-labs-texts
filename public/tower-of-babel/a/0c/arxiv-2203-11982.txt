Polarized products of elliptic curves with complex multiplication and field of moduli ℚ \mathds{Q}
Fabien Narbonne with an appendix of Francesc Fité and Xavier Guitart

Abstract
Let R R be the maximal order in a quadratic imaginary field K K . We give an equivalence of categories between the category of polarized abelian varieties isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves over ℂ \mathds{C} with complex multiplication (CM) by R R and the category of integral hermitian R R -lattices. Then we apply this equivalence to enumerate all the genus 2 2 and 3 3 curves with field of moduli ℚ \mathds{Q} and with Jacobian isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves with CM by R R .



Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathds{C}
with complex multiplication by a maximal order
R
in a quadratic imaginary field
K
. Then
E
admits a model over
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
. It even admits one over
\mathds{Q}(j(E))
, which has degree
\#\Cl(R)
over
\mathds{Q}
, and not over any sub-extension. It may then be surprising that powers of CM elliptic curves may be defined over smaller fields than expected, sometimes even over
\mathds{Q}
. In
[
FG20
, Theorem 1.1 and 1.2]
the authors show for instance that there are abelian surfaces defined over
\mathds{Q}
,
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
-isogenous to the square of a CM elliptic curve, for exactly
45
discriminants even though there are only
13
CM elliptic curves over
\mathds{Q}
. From this they deduce,
[
FG20
, Corollary 1.3]
that there are exactly
92

\overline{\mathds{Q}}
-endomorphism algebras of geometrically split abelian surfaces over
\mathds{Q}
. This study is motivated by the conjecture on the possible finiteness of the set of endomorphism rings of abelian varieties of a given dimension over a fixed degree extension field of
\mathds{Q}
.

A weaker requirement is to ask for the field of moduli of a (polarized) abelian variety to be
\mathds{Q}
. In
[
GHR19
]
the authors give the finite list of indecomposable principally polarized abelian surfaces (also known as Jacobian of genus
2
curves) with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
which are isomorphic to
E^{2}
.

In the present article we address the case of abelian varieties with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
isomorphic over
\mathds{C}
to products
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{g}
,
g>1
, of elliptic curves with CM by a maximal order
R
and we give an exhaustive list of these principally polarized abelian varieties for the case
g=2
in Table
1
and for
g=3
in Table
2
. Before explaining the structure of the paper and our strategy, we mention the following natural generalization to abelian varieties isomorphic to products
E_{1}×···×E_{g},g>1
, of elliptic curves with CM by possibly distinct orders in the same imaginary quadratic field
K
. By
[
Kan11
, Theorem 2]
, this is equivalent to considering abelian varieties isogenous to
E^{g}
over
\mathds{C}
for a given elliptic curve E with CM by K. In my PhD thesis, I presented heuristic results for

in this direction, see
[
Nar22
, Table A.1, A.2 and A.3]
. Note that in
[
Nar22
, Théorème 2.2.33]
, the equivalence of categories of Theorem
2
is already proved for arbitrary orders. To finish the proof, one would need to check the validity of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
in this generality. We also hope to address the finer question the existence of a model over
\mathds{Q}
for principally polarized abelian variety with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
. This theoretical result would also lead to the more refined project of having certified models over
\mathds{Q}
when the descent is possible. Very recent results contained in
[
LRR24
]
would help to achieve this. Some certified models of genus

In Section
1
we recall some properties of the classical equivalence of categories between the complex abelian varieties and polarizable tori and how it behaves with respect to polarizations. Polarizations on abelian varieties give rise to positive definite hermitian forms with a compatibility condition on the lattice.

In Section
2
, we restrict the equivalence of categories to the abelian varieties isomorphic to the product of elliptic curves with complex multiplication by
R
. For these abelian varieties, we associate the complex tori
V/\Gamma
, where
V
is a
\mathds{C}
-vector space and
\Gamma
is a lattice in
V
. The lattice
\Gamma
can be endowed with the structure of an
R
-module, providing additional structure than the
\mathds{Z}
-module one it already has. The corresponding hermitian form endows the
R
-module

with the structure of an
integral hermitian lattice
(up to rescaling the hermitian form by a constant that only depends on

). This restriction leads to an equivalence of categories between polarized abelian varieties isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves with CM by
R
and integral hermitian
R
-lattices (Theorem

). Moreover, hermitian forms corresponding to principal polarization give

through the equivalence. We may note that a similar functor is developed in
[

]
in a much wider framework since it is defined for any field, not only
\mathds{C}
. Under some conditions, it is also an equivalence of categories. However, we chose to focus our attention on a much simpler functor since we will need to refine it in the next section.

The goal of Section
3
is to translate the action of
\Gal(\overline{\mathds{Q}}/\mathds{Q})
on abelian varieties
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
-isomorphic to
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{g}
into the category of integral hermitian lattices through the previous functor. The action of

can be decomposed into two steps: the action of

and the action of

such that the action of

on abelian varieties corresponds to the tensor product of the associated lattice by a representative of

(and a rescaling of the hermitian form).

Still, for both actions, we need to rigidify the choice of the target objects under the previous equivalences of categories, which are defined only up to
\mathds{C}
-isomorphisms. Indeed, unlike
[
GHR19
]
where the compatibilities between the various abelian varieties and their conjugate were obvious, we could not find a simple way to impose them from abstract nonsense. We therefore use the explicit algebraization by the Weierstrass function for elliptic curves to be able to work out the explicit Galois action on the associated hermitian
R

4
). Notice that the main difficulty is to be able to keep the abelian varieties, their isogenies

their analytic representation defined over

to be able to translate the action of

1
Complex abelian varieties and complex tori

1.1
Abelian varieties and polarizable tori

Let us first recall that there is an equivalence of categories between abelian varieties over
\mathds{C}
and the category of polarizable tori given by
\mathbf{T}\colon A\mapsto A(\mathds{C})
, see
[
Mil08
, Theorem 2.9]
. A
complex torus
is a quotient of groups
X=V/\Gamma
with
V
a complex vector space of finite dimension and
\Gamma
a
\mathds{Z}
-
lattice
of
V
, i.e., a subgroup of
V
generating
V
over the reals. A torus
X=V/\Gamma
, or a lattice
\Gamma\subset V
, is said to be
polarizable
if there exists a positive definite hermitian form
h\colon V\times V\rightarrow\mathds{C}
such that

\im h(\Gamma,\Gamma)\subset\mathds{Z}

where
\im
is the imaginary part. Morphisms of complex tori are, in particular, morphisms of groups
\varphi\colon X=V/\Gamma\rightarrow X^{\prime}=V^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime}
. Such a map can be lifted to a
\mathds{C}
-linear map
\varphi_{\text{an}}\colon V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
that sends
\Gamma
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
called the
analytic representation
of
\varphi
. The group morphism
\varphi_{\text{rat}}={\varphi_{\text{an}}}_{|\Gamma}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
is called the
rational representation
of
\varphi
. We denote the set of the analytic representations of morphisms between
X
and
X^{\prime}
by
\Hom_{\mathds{C}}(\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime})
. We will often consider analytic representations directly as morphisms of polarizable tori.

A surjective morphism
f\colon A\rightarrow B
between abelian varieties
A
and
B
over
\mathds{C}
of equal dimension is called an
isogeny
; its kernel is finite and its cardinality
\#\ker f
is called the
degree
of
f
, denoted
\deg f
. Via the functor
\mathbf{T}
, we will also call
\varphi\colon V/\Gamma\longrightarrow V/\Gamma^{\prime}
an isogeny. The degree of
\varphi
is defined in the same way as
\deg\varphi=\#\ker\varphi
. Moreover, it satisfies

\deg\varphi=\left[\Gamma^{\prime}\colon\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma)\right]=\deg f

with
\left[\Gamma^{\prime}\colon\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma)\right]=\#(\Gamma^{\prime}/\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma))
, the index of
\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma)
in
\Gamma^{\prime}.

1.2
Duality

Let
V
be a
g
-dimensional complex vector space and
\Gamma\subset V
a
\mathds{Z}
-lattice. The dual lattice associated with
\Gamma
is defined by

\widehat{\Gamma}=\left\{\ell\in V^{*}\mid\im\ell(\Gamma)\subset\mathds{Z}\right\}

where
V^{*}
denotes the set of antilinear forms
\ell\colon V\rightarrow\mathds{C}
. This defines the dual complex torus of
V/\Gamma
, denoted
\widehat{V/\Gamma}=V^{*}/\widehat{\Gamma}
.

If
A
is a complex abelian variety such that
A(\mathds{C})\simeq V/\Gamma
then there exists an isomorphism
\widehat{A}(\mathds{C})\simeq\widehat{V/\Gamma}
, where
\widehat{A}
is the dual abelian variety of
A
(see
[
Mum70
]
). Moreover, for a morphism
f\colon A\rightarrow B
and
\varphi=\mathbf{T}(f)\colon V/\Gamma\rightarrow V^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime}
we have
\mathbf{T}(\widehat{f})_{\text{an}}=\varphi_{\text{an}}^{*}
with

\begin{array}[]{rrl}\varphi_{\text{an}}^{*}\colon&V^{\prime*}&\longrightarrow V^{*}\\
&\ell&\longmapsto\ell\circ\varphi_{\text{an}}\end{array}

where
\widehat{f}\colon\widehat{B}\rightarrow\widehat{A}
is the dual morphism of
f
(see
[
BL04
, Section 2.4]
).

1.3
Polarizations and hermitian forms

Let
A
be an abelian variety and let
\mathscr{L}
be a line bundle. We consider the map

\begin{array}[]{rrl}a_{\mathscr{L}}\colon&A&\longrightarrow\widehat{A}\\
&x&\longmapsto t_{x}^{*}\mathscr{L}\otimes\mathscr{L}^{-1}.\end{array}

with
t_{x}
the translation by
x
map. Consider isomorphisms
A(\mathds{C})\simeq V/\Gamma
and
\widehat{A}(\mathds{C})\simeq V^{*}/\widehat{\Gamma}
. Let
h=c_{1}(\mathscr{L})
be the first Chern class of
\mathscr{L}
, which we identify with a hermitian form on
V
(see
[
BL04
, Lemma 2.4.5]
). Then the map
\rho_{h}\in\Hom_{\mathds{C}}(\Gamma,\widehat{\Gamma})
defined by

\begin{array}[]{rrl}\rho_{h}\colon&V&\longrightarrow V^{*}\\
&v&\longmapsto h(v,\_).\end{array}

is the analytic representation of
a_{\mathscr{L}}
. A polarization on an abelian variety
A
is an isogeny
a_{\mathscr{L}}
with
\mathscr{L}
an ample line bundle. In this case, its first Chern class
h
is a positive definite hermitian form (see
[
BL04
, Proposition 4.5.2]
).

A pair
(A,a)
with
A
an abelian variety and
a
a polarization is called a
polarized abelian variety
. Morphisms of polarized abelian varieties are defined by maps
f\colon(A,a)\rightarrow(B,b)
such that
\widehat{f}bf=a
and we call them
polarized isogenies
. The distinction between polarized and non-polarized isogenies is essential; when we do not specify that an isogeny between polarized varieties is itself polarized, it refers to a morphism between the underlying abelian varieties without their polarizations. A
polarized torus
is a pair
(V/\Gamma,\rho_{h})
, also denoted by
(\Gamma,h)
, with
V/\Gamma
a complex torus and
\rho_{h}\in\Hom_{\mathds{C}}(\Gamma,\widehat{\Gamma})
induced by a positive definite hermitian form
h
, i.e.,
\rho_{h}(v)=h(v,\_)
. We analogously define morphisms
\varphi\colon(V/\Gamma,\rho_{h})\rightarrow(V^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime},\rho_{h^{\prime}})
between polarized tori. Their analytic representation must satisfy
\varphi_{\text{an}}^{*}\rho_{h^{\prime}}\varphi_{\text{an}}=\rho_{h}
which means that for
v,w\in V
,

\rho_{h}(v)(w)=h(v,w)=h^{\prime}(\varphi_{\text{an}}(v),\varphi_{\text{an}}(w)).

In particular, analytic representations of polarized isogenies define isometries on the associated hermitian vector spaces. In the following we will call a pair
(V,h)
made of a
\mathds{C}
-vector space and
h
a positive definite hermitian form a
hermitian space
. Since the functor
\mathbf{T}
is an equivalence of categories, it also defines an equivalence of categories
\mathbf{T}^{p}
between polarized abelian varieties and polarized tori
(A,a)\mapsto(X=\mathbf{T}(A),\rho_{h}=\mathbf{T}(a))
.

For any group schemes
A
and
B
over a field
k
there is an isomorphism of groups

A(k)\times B(k)\simeq(A\times_{k}B)(k).

We can apply it to abelian varieties over
\mathds{C}
. For
A(\mathds{C})\simeq V_{A}/\Gamma_{A}
,
B(\mathds{C})\simeq V_{B}/\Gamma_{B}
and
(A\times B)(\mathds{C})\simeq V/\Gamma
we have

V/\Gamma\simeq(V_{A}/\Gamma_{A})\times(V_{B}/\Gamma_{B}).

Hence
\Gamma\simeq\Gamma_{A}\times\Gamma_{B}
and in terms of categories, the functor
\mathbf{T}
commutes with products. In the same way, the functor
\mathbf{T}^{p}
also commutes with products.

R

Let
R
be the maximal order of a quadratic imaginary field
K=\mathds{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
with
d
a positive square-free integer. An
R
-lattice is a finitely presented, torsion-free
R
-module. We denote by
\mathbfcal{L}_{R}
the category of
R
-lattices.
In this section we want to show that there exist equivalences of categories

•

between
\mathbfcal{A}_{R}
, the category of complex abelian varieties isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves with CM by
R
, and
\mathbfcal{L}_{R}
;

•

between
\mathbfcal{A}_{R}^{p}
, the category of complex polarized abelian varieties isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves with CM by
R
, and
\mathbfcal{L}_{R}^{h,int}
, the category of integral hermitian
R
-lattices, i.e.
R
-lattices

equipped with a positive definite hermitian form
H
on
KL=L\otimes_{R}K
, such that
H(L,L)\subset R
.

For the rest of the article we fix field extensions

\mathds{Q}\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\overline{\mathds{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathds{C}.

Elliptic curve with complex multiplication over

\Delta<0
the discriminant of
K/\mathds{Q}
and

. Notice that
\alpha_{R}

with positive imaginary part.

Let

there is a unique isomorphism

[.]_{E}\colon R\rightarrow\text{End}(E)

characterized, for any

According to

all CM elliptic curves over

2.2
Totally split complex tori and
R
-lattice structure

Let
L
be an
R
-lattice, i.e., a finitely presented, torsion-free
R
-module. Since
R
is maximal,
L
is a module over a Dedekind domain and by
[
O’M00
, Theorem 81.3]
, we can always write
L
as a sum

L=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{g}\mathfrak{a}_{i}x_{i},

with
(x_{1},\dots,x_{g})
a basis of
KL
and
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{a}_{g}
fractional ideals of
R
. The family
(\mathfrak{a}_{i},x_{i})
is called a
pseudo-basis
of
L
and the
Steinitz class

\st(L)
of
L
is defined by the class of the product
\mathfrak{a}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{g}
in
\Cl(R)
. The Steinitz class of a lattice together with its rank determines its
R
-isomorphism class by
[
Con16
, Theorem 13]
.

For an
R
-ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, the norm of
\mathfrak{a}
is defined by
N(\mathfrak{a})=\#\left(R/\mathfrak{a}\right)
. We can extend the definition of the norm to any fractional ideal by the equality
N(\lambda\mathfrak{a})=|\lambda|^{2}N(\mathfrak{a})
for any
\lambda\in K
.

Throughout this article, different notations will be used to denote lattices depending on the context:

•

The notation
\Gamma
will denote a lattice in a
\mathds{C}
-vector space.

•

The notation
\Lambda
will denote a lattice in
\mathds{C}
only.

•

The notation
L
will denote a lattice over an imaginary quadratic order
R
.

Thus, the notations
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
will be reserved for the analytical aspect, for complex tori, while
L
will be used for the algebraic aspect. The functor
\mathbf{F}
, defined in Theorem
1
, connects complex tori to hermitian lattices, and these notations will help clarify the category in which we are working. For the same reasons we write
h
for the hermitian forms coming from polarizable tori and
H
for
R
-lattices.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be an order in a quadratic imaginary field
K
. There is an equivalence of categories between the category
\mathbfcal{A}_{R}
of abelian varieties isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves with CM by
R
, and the category
\mathbfcal{L}_{R}
of
R
-lattices given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbf{F}\colon&\mathbfcal{A}_{R}&\rightarrow\mathbfcal{L}_{R}\\
&A\text{ s.t. }A(\mathds{C})\simeq V/\Gamma&\mapsto\Gamma\\
&(\mathds{C}L)/L&\mapsfrom L.\end{array}

on objects and, for arrows,
f\colon A(\mathds{C})\simeq V/\Gamma\rightarrow A^{\prime}(\mathds{C})\simeq V^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime},
given by
\mathbf{F}(f)=f_{\text{rat}}.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathbfcal{A}_{R}
such that
\mathbf{T}(A)=A(\mathds{C})=V/\Gamma.
There is an isomorphism
\varphi\colon V/\Gamma\rightarrow\mathds{C}^{g}/\bigoplus\Lambda_{i}.
The
\mathds{Z}
-lattices
\Lambda_{i}
have a natural structure of and
R=\text{End}(\Lambda_{i})
-module. The lattice
\Gamma
is stable by multiplication by
R
. Indeed,

\varphi_{\text{an}}(R\Gamma)=R\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma)=R\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda_{i}=\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda_{i}=\varphi_{\text{an}}(\Gamma)

so
R\Gamma=\Gamma.

Moreover, the isomorphism
\varphi
endows
\Gamma
with a structure of an
R
-module in a natural way

r.\gamma=\varphi_{\text{an}}^{-1}(r\varphi_{\text{an}}(\gamma))=r\gamma,\text{ for }r\in R,\gamma\in\Gamma.

(1)

Hence
\Gamma
has a structure of
R
-module and we can see in (
1
) that this structure does not depend on the isomorphism
\varphi
we chose.

Conversely, let
L
be an
R
-lattice and
(\mathfrak{a}_{i},x_{i})
a pseudo-basis of it. Since the
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
are fractional ideals, there exists an integer
n
such that
n\mathfrak{a}_{i}\subset R
for all
i,
so the multiplication by
n
map defines an isogeny
\Hom_{\mathds{C}}(L,R^{g}).
If we endow
R^{g}
with the canonical hermitian form
h_{0}(x,y)={}^{t}\!x\overline{y}
, we have
h_{0}(R^{g},R^{g})=R
, hence

\im h_{0}(R^{g},R^{g})=(\im\omega)\mathds{Z}=\alpha_{R}\mathds{Z}.

Thus,
\left(R^{g},\frac{1}{\alpha_{R}}h_{0}\right)
defines a polarized torus and so does
\left(L,\frac{n^{2}}{\alpha_{R}}h_{0}\right)
. This proves that the underlying
\mathds{Z}
-lattice
\Gamma
of an
R
-lattice
L
is polarizable.

Finally, given a morphism
f\colon V/\Gamma\rightarrow V^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime},\mathbf{F}(f)=f_{\text{rat}}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
f_{\text{rat}}={f_{\text{an}}}_{|\Gamma}
and
f_{\text{an}}
is
\mathds{C}
-linear it is then
R
-linear. Hence
\mathbf{F}
maps arrows in a full and faithful way.

Hence there is an equivalence of categories between
R
-lattices and polarizable tori
X
such that there exists an isomorphism
X\rightarrow\mathds{C}^{g}/\bigoplus\Lambda_{i}.
The latter being in equivalence with the category of abelian varieties isomorphic to a power of elliptic curves with CM by
R.
This proves the equivalence of categories between
\mathbfcal{A}_{R}

It may be worth elaborating why the CM case is so special. If we consider the functor

from abelian varieties over
\mathds{C}

-lattices

Hence the structure of a

an equivalence.

2.3
Polarizable tori and integral lattices

A
hermitian
R
-lattice
is defined as a pair
(L,H)
with
L
an
R
-lattice and
H
a positive definite hermitian form on the ambient space
KL
. The
scale
of a hermitian lattice

is defined as the set
\mathfrak{s}(L)=H(L,L)\subset K
. It is a fractional ideal of
K
(see
[

, Remark 2.3.4]
). The
dual lattice

of a hermitian lattice is the lattice defined by

. For
\mathfrak{a}

we say that

-modular

\mathfrak{a}L^{\#}=L
. If

is

-modular then its scale satisfies
\mathfrak{s}(L)=\mathfrak{a}
. A hermitian

-lattice

i.e., it is integral if its scale is an integral ideal (or equivalently

). An

-modular hermitian lattice

is called unimodular (this is equivalent to
(L,H)
being integral and its scale being

). One also defines the

of a hermitian lattice
(L,H)
as the fractional ideal

\mathfrak{v}(L)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}N(\mathfrak{a}_{i})\right)\det(G(x_{1},\dots,x_{g}))R

where

the Gram matrix of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{g}).
A hermitian lattice
(L,H)
is

-modular if, and only if,

\mathfrak{v}(L)=\mathfrak{a}^{g}\text{ and }\mathfrak{s}(L)=\mathfrak{a}.

(see
[

, Section 2]
).

In this section we explain the link between polarized tori and integral lattices.

Let

Lemma 1

Let

The left-to-right direction is straightforward.

4.1
General results on the field of moduli of principally polarized abelian varieties in
\mathbfcal{A}_{R}^{p}

We recall that the
field of moduli
of a polarized abelian variety
(A,a)
over
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
is the field fixed by the subgroup

\left\{\sigma\in\Gal\left(\overline{\mathds{Q}}/\mathds{Q}\right)\mid(A^{\sigma},a^{\sigma})\simeq(A,a)\right\}.

In the same way we define the field of moduli of a curve
C
over
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
by the fixed field of
\left\{\sigma\in\Gal\left(\overline{\mathds{Q}}/\mathds{Q}\right)\mid C^{\sigma}\simeq C\right\}.

The Abel-Jacobi map
C\mapsto(\Jac(C),j)
which associates a curves to its polarized Jacobian variety induces a morphism

\begin{array}[]{rrl}[\Jac]\colon&M_{g}\longrightarrow&A_{g}\\
&[C]\longmapsto&[\Jac(C),j]\end{array}

between the moduli space of smooth absolutely irreducible projective genus
g
curves and the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
. By
[
CS86
, Chapter VII,Corollary 12.2]
,
[\Jac]
is injective so
C
and
(\Jac(C),j)
have the same field of moduli.

For
g=2
and
g=3
the moduli spaces
M_{g}
and
A_{g}
have the same dimension and are irreducible. Thus, the Jacobian map is dominant if the field is algebraically closed. More specifically, every indecomposable principally polarized abelian variety is the Jacobian of a curve (see
[
Hoy63
]
).

We give a necessary condition on the class group of
R
and on
\mathbf{F}_{h}(A,a)
for an abelian variety
(A,a)\in\mathbfcal{A}_{R}^{p}
to have field of moduli
\mathds{Q}.

Proposition 9
.

Let
(A,a)\in\mathbfcal{A}_{R}^{p}
considered over
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
and
\mathbf{F}_{h}(A,a)=(L,H)
be a hermitian lattice of rank
g
. If
(A,a)
has field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
then
\Cl(R)
has exponent dividing
g
and
\st(L)
has order at most
2.

Proof.

If
(L,H)
corresponds to a polarized abelian variety with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
then
(L,H)\simeq\left(\mathfrak{a}L,\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})}H\right)
for all fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}.
In particular,
L\simeq\mathfrak{a}L
for all
\mathfrak{a}
and then, their Steinitz classes are the same

\st(L)=\st(\mathfrak{a}L)=\mathfrak{a}^{g}\st(L)\in\Cl(R).

Hence
\dot{\mathfrak{a}}^{g}=1\in\Cl(R)
so
\Cl(R)
has exponent dividing
g.

The hermitian lattice isometry class must also be invariant by the action of the complex conjugation so

\st(L)=\st(\overline{L})=\overline{\st(L)}.

By the formula
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}\mathfrak{a}=N(\mathfrak{a})R
it means that
\st(L)

.

\mathfrak{a}
be the Steinitz class of
L
. Then

\mathbf{F}_{h}\left(\overline{A},\overline{a}\right)=\left(\overline{L},\overline{H}\right)\simeq\left(\overline{\mathfrak{a}}L,\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})}H\right).

Hence in this particular case, the action of the complex conjugation corresponds to the action of an automorphism of
\Gal(\overline{\mathds{Q}}/K)
.

Proof.

Let us write
L=Rx\oplus\mathfrak{a}y.
Let
G=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\overline{\beta}&\delta\end{pmatrix}
be the Gram matrix of
H
in the basis
(x,y)
of
KL
. Since
(A,a)
is principally polarized
(L,H)
must be unimodular and then its volume
\mathfrak{v}(L)=N(\mathfrak{a})\det(G)R=R
so
N(\mathfrak{a})\det(G)
is invertible and real in
R
so
N(\mathfrak{a})\det(G)=1.

Now consider
P=N(\mathfrak{a})\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\beta}&\delta\\
-\alpha&-\beta\end{pmatrix}
, matrix of a linear map
K\overline{x}+K\overline{y}\rightarrow Kx+Ky.
It satisfies the relation

{}^{t}\!P\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})}G\overline{P}=N(\mathfrak{a})\det(G)\overline{G}=\overline{G}.

So
P
defines an isometry between hermitian spaces.

Moreover,
\alpha=H(x,x)=N(x)\in R\cap\mathds{R}=\mathds{Z},\beta=H(x,y)
, so
\overline{\mathfrak{a}}\beta=H(x,\mathfrak{a}y)\subset R
so
\beta\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}^{-1}=\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{N(\mathfrak{a})}
and, in the same way,
\delta\in\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})}\mathds{Z}.

Hence

\displaystyle P\overline{x}

\displaystyle=N(\mathfrak{a})(\overline{\beta}x-ay)\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}x+N(\mathfrak{a})y=\overline{\mathfrak{a}}L

\displaystyle P\overline{\mathfrak{a}y}

\displaystyle=N(\mathfrak{a})\overline{\mathfrak{a}}(\delta x-by)\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}}x+N(\mathfrak{a})y=\overline{\mathfrak{a}}L.

Thus,
P
defines an isometry
(\overline{L},\overline{H})\rightarrow\left(\overline{\mathfrak{a}}L,\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{a})}H\right)

∎

Appendix A
Class groups of CM fields from polarized products of CM elliptic curves with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}

by Francesc Fité and Xavier Guitart

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and let
{H_{K}}
be its Hilbert class field. We denote by
\mathrm{C}_{g}
the cyclic group of
g
elements.

Proposition 11
.

Let
E_{1}/\overline{\mathds{Q}}
and
E_{2}/\overline{\mathds{Q}}
be elliptic curves with CM by
K
, and let
\varphi
be an indecomposable principal polarization on
E_{1}\times E_{2}
. If the field of moduli of
(E_{1}\times E_{2},\varphi)
is
\mathds{Q}
, then
\Cl(K)
is isomorphic to one of the groups
\mathrm{C}_{1},\mathrm{C}_{2}
, or
\mathrm{C}_{2}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is indecomposable there exists a curve
C/\overline{\mathds{Q}}
with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
such that
\Jac(C)\simeq E_{1}\times E_{2}
. The curve
C
is not necessarily defined over
\mathds{Q}
. By
[
Mes91
, §2.4]
there exists a number field
k^{\prime}
with
[k^{\prime}\colon\mathds{Q}]\leq 2
such that
C
admits a model over
k^{\prime}
. Since
k^{\prime}
can be chosen to be any field over which Mestre’s conic has a rational point, we can choose it so that
k^{\prime}\cap{H_{K}}=\mathds{Q}
.

Put
k=Kk^{\prime}
, and suppose from now on that
C
is defined over
k
. In particular,
A=\Jac(C)
is an abelian surface defined over
k
such that
A_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}}\sim E^{2}
, where
E/\overline{\mathds{Q}}
is an elliptic curve with CM by
K
. Denote by
L
the smallest field of definition of the endomorphisms of
A
. Since
k
contains
K
, by
[
FG18
, Theorem 2.14]
the field
F=Hk
is a subfield of
L
and
\Gal(F/k)
has exponent
\leq 2
. By
[
FG18
, Remark 3.1]

\Gal(L/k)
is either
\mathrm{C}_{n}
for
n\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
,
\mathrm{D}_{n}
for
n\in\{2,3,4,6\}
,
\mathrm{A}_{4}
or
\mathrm{S}_{4}
. Since
\Gal(F/k)
is a quotient of exponent
\leq 2
of
\Gal(L/k)
, we see that
\Gal(F/k)\simeq\mathrm{C}_{1},\mathrm{C}_{2}
, or
\mathrm{C}_{2}\times\mathrm{C}_{2}
(cf.
[
FG18
, Table1]
).
By our choice of
k^{\prime}
, we have that
{H_{K}}\cap k=K
, and hence
\Gal(F/k)\simeq\Gal({H_{K}}/K)\simeq\mathrm{Cl}(K)
.

∎

Remark 1
.

The above proposition dispenses with the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis in
[
GHR19
, Table 4]
. Moreover the complete list of quadratic imaginary field with a given class number
h
is known up to
h=100
by
[
Wat04
]
. Hence the table
1
is unconditionally complete.

A key property used in the proof of Proposition
11
is that a curve of genus
2
can be defined over a quadratic extension of its field of moduli. The same is true for curves of genus
3
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
C
be a curve of genus
3
with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
. There exist infinitely many quadratic extensions
k/\mathds{Q}
such that
C
admits a model over
k
.

Proof.

The curve
C
is either a hyperelliptic curve or a smooth plane quartic. If
C
is hyperelliptic, it is well known that it can be defined over infinitely many fields
k
with
[k\colon\mathds{Q}]\leq 2
(the curve
C/\Aut(C)
is a conic that can be defined over
\mathds{Q}
and
k
can be taken to be any field where it has rational points). Suppose that
C
is a plane quartic. If
|\Aut(C)|=1
, the curve admits a model over its field of moduli by Weil descent. If
|\Aut(C)|>2
, then the curve also admits a model over its field of moduli (cf.
[
LRRS14
, Prop. 2.3 and §3.2]
). If
|\Aut(C)|=2
the result is Lemma
6
below, which is probably well known but we include a proof for completeness.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
C/\overline{\mathds{Q}}
be a curve with field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
and such that
|\Aut(C)|=2
. There exist infinitely many quadratic extensions
k/\mathds{Q}
such that
C
admits a model over
k
.

Proof.

For every
\sigma\in G_{\mathds{Q}}:=\Gal(\overline{\mathds{Q}}/\mathds{Q})
, let
\mu_{\sigma}\colon{}^{\sigma}C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}C
be an isomorphism, chosen in such a way that the system
\{\mu_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in G_{\mathds{Q}}}
is locally constant. For any
\psi\in\Aut(C)
we have that

\displaystyle{}^{\sigma}\psi=\mu_{\sigma}^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\mu_{\sigma}.

(11)

The equality is obvious when
\psi
is the identity. When
\psi
is the non-trivial automorphism of
C
, the equality follows from the fact that then
{}^{\sigma}\psi
is the non-trivial automorphism of
{}^{\sigma}C
.

For
\sigma,\tau\in G_{\mathds{Q}}
define

\displaystyle c(\sigma,\tau)=\mu_{\sigma}\circ{}^{\sigma}\mu_{\tau}\circ\mu_{\sigma\tau}^{-1}\in\Aut(C).

A short computation using (
11
) shows that
c
is a two-cocycle in
Z^{2}(G_{\mathds{Q}},\Aut(C))
, where the action of
G_{\mathds{Q}}
on
\Aut(C)
is the trivial action. Therefore, the class of
c
can be identified with an element in
H^{2}(G_{\mathds{Q}},\{\pm 1\})
. Now
H^{2}(G_{\mathds{Q}},\{\pm 1\})
is isomorphic to the
2
-torsion of the Brauer group of
\mathds{Q}
, which is generated by quaternion algebras. Any product of quaternion algebras is equivalent in the Brauer group to a quaternion algebra, so the class of
c
can be identified with a quaternion algebra
B
. Let
k
be any quadratic splitting field of
B
(there exist infinitely many such fields
k
). The restriction
{\mathrm{Res}}_{\mathds{Q}}^{k}(c)
is a coboundary, that is to say, there exists a function
\delta\colon G_{k}{\rightarrow}\{\pm 1\}
such that

\displaystyle c(\sigma,\tau)=\delta(\sigma)\delta(\tau)\delta(\sigma\tau)^{-1}\text{ for all }\sigma,\tau\in G_{k}.

If we define
\tilde{\mu}_{\sigma}:=\delta(\sigma)^{-1}\circ\mu_{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in G_{k}
, the system
\{\tilde{\mu}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in G_{k}}
is a descent data for
C
over
k
and we see that
C
can be defined over
k
.
∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
be elliptic curves over
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
with CM by
K
. Let
\varphi
be a principal indecomposable polaritation on

such that
(A,\varphi)
has field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
. Then the class number of

is 1 or 3.

Proof.

By the results of
[
OU73
]
we have that
(A,\varphi)
is isomorphic to the canonically polarized Jacobian of some genus three curve

. Since
(A,\varphi)
has field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
, the curve
C
has field of moduli
\mathds{Q}
as well. By Proposition
12
there exists a quadratic extension
k^{\prime}/\mathds{Q}
with
k^{\prime}\cap{H_{K}}=\mathds{Q}
such that
C
admits a model over
k^{\prime}
. Put
k=k^{\prime}K
and suppose from now on that
C
is defined over
k
.

If

we have that
A
is defined over
k
and
A_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}}\sim E^{3}
, where
E
is an elliptic curve with CM by
K
. Denote by
L
the minimal extension of
k
such that
\text{End}(A_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}})=\text{End}(A_{L})
. By
[
FG18
, Theorem 2.14]
the field

is a subfield of

The argument in the proof of

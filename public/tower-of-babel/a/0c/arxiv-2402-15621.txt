A Generalization of the Graham-Pollak Tree Theorem to Even-Order Steiner Distance
Joshua Cooper, Gabrielle Tauscheck

Abstract
Graham and Pollak showed in 1971 that the determinant of a tree’s distance matrix depends only on its number of vertices, and, in particular, it is always nonzero. The Steiner distance of a collection of k k vertices in a graph is the fewest number of edges in any connected subgraph containing those vertices; for k = 2 k=2 , this reduces to the ordinary definition of graphical distance. Here, we show that the hyperdeterminant of the k k -th order Steiner distance hypermatrix is always nonzero if k k is even, extending their result beyond k = 2 k=2 . Previously, the authors showed that the k k -Steiner distance hyperdeterminant is always zero for k k odd, so together this provides a generalization to all k k . We conjecture that not just the vanishing, but the value itself, of the k k -Steiner distance hyperdeterminant of an n n -vertex tree depends only on k k and n n .

Given a graph

Equivalently, we could define the Steiner polynomial to be the

Definition 1.6

A Steiner nullvector is a point where all the polynomials within the Steiner ideal vanish. The set of all Steiner nullvectors – a projective variety – is the Steiner nullvariety.

for the row of

Proposition 2.1
.

If
e=\{u,u+1\}
is an edge of the tree
T
on
n+1
vertices
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, then
S_{u}-S_{u+1}
is a vector of the form
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
where
x_{0}=x_{1}=\ldots=x_{u}=-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{u}x_{i}\right)^{k-2}
and
x_{u+1}=\ldots=x_{n}=\left(\sum_{i=u+1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{k-2}
with
0,\ldots,u
lying in one component of
T^{\prime}:=T-e
and
u+1,\ldots,n
lying in the other component.

Proof.

Note that

S_{uw}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in V(T)^{k-2}}d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{i}}.

(2)

It is sufficient to show that for any
w
and
w^{\prime}
that lie within the same component of
T^{\prime}
,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}({u+1},w,\mathbf{i})=d_{T}(u,{w^{\prime}},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}({u+1},{w^{\prime}},\mathbf{i})

holds for all
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-2}\in V(T)
. For every case, let
d=d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})
and
d^{\prime}=d_{T}(u,{w^{\prime}},\mathbf{i})
.

Case 1: Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-2}
lie in the same component of
T^{\prime}
as
u
.

1.

Assume
w
and
w^{\prime}
also lie within the same component as
u
. Then,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}({u+1},w,\mathbf{i})=d-(d+1)=-1

d_{T}(u,{w^{\prime}},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}({u+1},{w^{\prime}},\mathbf{i})=d^{\prime}-(d^{\prime}+1)=-1.

2.

Assume instead that
w
and
w^{\prime}
lie in the same component as
u+1
. Then,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w,\mathbf{i})=d-d=0

d_{T}(u,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})=d^{\prime}-d^{\prime}=0.

Case 2: Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-2}
lie in the same component as
u+1
.

1.

Assume
w
and
w^{\prime}
also lie within the same component as
u+1
. Then,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w,\mathbf{i})=d-(d-1)=1

d_{T}(u,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})=d^{\prime}-(d^{\prime}-1)=1.

2.

Assume instead that
w
and
w^{\prime}
lie in the same component as
u
. Then,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w,\mathbf{i})=d-d=0

d_{T}(u,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})=d^{\prime}-d^{\prime}=0.

Case 3: Suppose some of the vertices among
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-2}
lie in the same component as
u
and some lie in the same component as
u+1
. Then,

d_{T}(u,w,\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w,\mathbf{i})=d-d=0

d_{T}(u,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})-d_{T}(u+1,w^{\prime},\mathbf{i})=d^{\prime}-d^{\prime}=0.

Therefore, the vector
S_{u}-S_{u+1}
has equal entries among the coordinates indexed by vertices lying in the same component of
T^{\prime}
. To compute these entries, we combine the different cases. Notice that Case 3 contributes zero to the sum in (
2
); therefore, we can omit those entries in the calculations. First, consider
x_{u}=S_{u,u}-S_{u+1,u}
. Since
u
lies in the same component as itself, we are working with Cases 1(a) and 2(b). Case 2(b) contributes a zero to the summation; therefore, we need only consider the vertices in Case 1(a), giving us
x_{u}=-\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in\{0,\ldots,u\}^{k-2}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{i}}=-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{u}x_{i}\right)^{k-2}
.

Now, consider
x_{u+1}=S_{u,u+1}-S_{u+1,u+1}
. Since
u+1
lies in the same component as itself, we are working with Cases 1(b) and 2(a). Case 1(b) contributes a zero to the summation; therefore, we need only consider the vertices in Case 2(a), giving us

∎

Let
\textbf{h}_{u}(a,b)=[h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}]\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a vector with at most two distinct entries:
h_{0}=\ldots=h_{u}=a

h_{u+1}=\ldots=h_{n}=b

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a Steiner nullvector of a tree

. All entries that correspond to leaf vertices

are the same value, equal to the summation of every entry of

except

.

Proof.

Since

is a tree on at least
2

gives

\displaystyle(D_{0}p_{T}-D_{1}p_{T})(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\textbf{h}_{0}\left(-x_{0}^{k-2},\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{k-2}\right)^{T}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=-x_{0}^{k-1}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{k-1}=0

\displaystyle(D_{u}p_{T}-D_{u+1}p_{T})(\mathbf{x})

(3)

.

For

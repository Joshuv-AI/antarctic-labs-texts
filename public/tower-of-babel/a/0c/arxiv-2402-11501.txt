BLOWN-UP CORONA OF RELATIVELY HYPERBOLIC GROUPS Thanks: T.Fukaya was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP19K03471
Tomohiro Fukaya

Abstract
. We show that under appropriate assumptions, a blown-up corona of a relatively hyperbolic group is equivariant and the compactification of the universal space for proper action by the blown-up corona is contractible. As a corollary, we establish the formula to determine the covering dimension of the blown-up corona by the cohomological dimension of the group. We also show that the blown-up corona of a hyperbolic group with respect to an almost malnormal family of quasiconvex subgroups is homeomorphic to the Gromov boundary of the group.

1.1.
Cohomological dimension

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, Bestvina and Mess
established a formula that determines the covering dimension of the
boundary of a torsion free hyperbolic group by the cohomological
dimension of the group. Theorem
A
generalizes this formula
for relatively hyperbolic groups.

Corollary B
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated
group that is hyperbolic relative to a finite family of infinite
subgroups
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
. We suppose that each subgroup

P_{i}
admits a finite
P_{i}
-simplicial complex
\underline{E}P_{i}
which is a
universal space for proper actions. For
i=1,\dots,k
, let

\overline{\underline{E}P_{i}}
be a coarse compactification of
\underline{E}P_{i}
. Set

W_{i}\coloneqq\overline{\underline{E}P_{i}}\setminus\underline{E}P_{i}
. Let
\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G
be the
blown-up corona of
(G,\mathbb{P},\{W_{1},\dots,W_{k}\})
.
Then we have

(1)

\displaystyle H^{p}(G;\mathbb{Z}G)\cong\widetilde{H}^{p-1}(\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G;\mathbb{Z})\quad\text{for all}\quad p\in\mathbb{N}.

Here
\widetilde{H}^{\bullet}(-)
denotes the reduced Alexander-Spanier
cohomology.

We also suppose that

(\overline{\underline{E}P_{i}},W_{i})
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-structure of
P_{i}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Then we have

(2)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G)=\max\{p:H^{p}(G;\mathbb{Z}G)\neq 0\}-1.

If
G
is torsion free, then

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{cd}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G)+1

where
\mathop{\mathrm{cd}}(G)
notes the cohomological dimension of
G
.

In the case when
\mathbb{P}
is empty, the formula (
2
)
is the results of Bestvina-Mess
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Blown-up corona of a hyperbolic group with an almost malnormal family of quasiconvex subgroups

Let
G
be a group. A finite family
\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
of
subgroups of

is said to be
almost malnormal
if every
infinite intersection of the form
H_{i}\cap g^{-1}H_{j}g
satisfies both

i=j
and
g\in H_{i}
. Bowditch proved the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
3
, Theorem 7.11]
Let
G
be a non-elementary hyperbolic
group, and let
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
be an almost malnormal family of
proper, quasiconvex subgroups of
G
. Then
G
is hyperbolic relative to

\mathbb{P}
.

Manning
[
16
]
gave an alternative proof of
Theorem
1.6
in terms of the dynamical
characterization of relative hyperbolicity. The main ingredient of his
proof is the explicit description of its Bowditch boundary.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group, and let
\mathbb{P}
be an almost malnormal
family of infinite quasiconvex proper subgroups of
G
. Let

\mathcal{L}
be the set of
G
-translates of limit set of elements of

\mathbb{P}
. The Bowditch boundary
\partial(G,\mathbb{P})
is obtained from the Gromov
boundary
\partial G
as a decomposition space
\partial G/\mathcal{L}
.

Using Manning’s description, we obtain the following.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, and

\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
be an almost malnormal family of proper,
infinite quasiconvex subgroups of
G
. Let
\partial G
be the Gromov
boundary of G and let
\partial P_{i}
be the limit set of
P_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Let
\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G
be the blown-up corona of
(G,\mathbb{P},\{\partial P_{1},\dots,\partial P_{k}\})
. Then
\partial^{\mathrm{bl}}G
is homeomorphic to
\partial G
.

Theorem
1.7
says that if
G
is a hyperbolic group and

\mathbb{P}
is an almost malnormal family of quasiconvex subgroups, then the
Bowditch boundary
\partial(G,\mathbb{P})
is obtained from the Gromov boundary
\partial G
. Conversely, Theorem

says that the Gromov boundary is
restored from the Bowditch boundary by blowing up all parabolic points.

2.1.
Coarse compactification

A proper coarse space is a locally compact Hausdorff space
equipped with a proper coarse structure. See
[
18
, Definition 2.22]

for proper coarse structure.
Here we recall the definitions of the Higson compactification and coarse
compactification. For details, see
[
18
]
and

[
17
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a proper coarse space. Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
be a
bounded continuous function. We denote by
\mathbf{d}f
the function

\mathbf{d}f(x,y)=f(y)-f(x)\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{C}.

We say that
f
is a
Higson function
, or, of
vanishing variation
, if for each controlled set
E
, the restriction of

\mathbf{d}f
to
E
vanishes at infinity, that is, for any
\epsilon>0
,
there exists a bounded subset
B
such that for any
(x,y)\in E\setminus B\times B
, we have
\lvert\mathbf{d}f(x,y)\rvert<\epsilon
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the case that
X
is a proper metric space with a bounded coarse structure,
the bounded continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
is a Higson function,
if and only if, for any
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
, there exists a compact
set
K\subset X
such that for all
(x,y)\in X\times X\setminus K\times K
with
d(x,y)<R
, we have
\lvert\mathbf{d}f(x,y)\rvert<\epsilon
.

The bounded continuous complex valued Higson functions on a proper coarse
space
X
form an unital

C^{*}
-subalgebra of bounded continuous functions on
X
, which we
denote
C_{h}(X)
. By the Gelfand-Naimark theory,
C_{h}(X)
is isomorphic
to a
C^{*}
-algebra of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space.

Definition 2.3
.

The compactification
hX
of
X
characterized by the property

C(hX)=C_{h}(X)
is called the
Higson compactification
. Its boundary

hX\setminus X
is denoted
\nu X
, and is called the
Higson corona

of
X
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a proper coarse space, and let
\overline{X}
be a
compactification of
X
. We say that
\overline{X}
is a

coarse compactification
if the identity map
X\to X
extends to
a continuous surjection
hX\to\overline{X}
.
We call
\overline{X}\setminus X
a
corona
of
X
.

The one point compactification of a proper coarse space is a coarse
compactification. If
X
is a proper geodesic Gromov hyperbolic space,
then the Gromov compactification
\overline{X}
is a coarse
compactification.

Coarse metric simplicial complex

Definition 2.5
.

A

is a simplicial complex

We denote by

the set of vertices
of
X
. We also denote by

•

We remark that

We equip

.
Then we equip

See

3.
Relatively hyperbolic groups

3.1.
Combinatorial horoball

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. Relative hyperbolicity of
G

is characterized by a properly discontinuous action on a hyperbolic
space in the sense of Gromov. There exist several constructions of such
a space. Here we review the
augmented space
defined by
Groves and Manning
[
13
, Section 3.2.]
. An advantage of
their construction is that the augmented space is a proper metric space,
so its coarse cohomology is well-defined.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(P,d)
be a proper metric space.

The combinatorial horoball
based on
P
, denoted by

\mathcal{H}(P)
, is the graph defined as follows:

(
a
)

\mathcal{H}(P)^{(0)}=P\times(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})
.

(
b
)

\mathcal{H}(P)^{(1)}
contains the following two type of edges:

(i)

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
p,q\in P
,
if
0<d(p,q)\leq 2^{l}

then there exists a

horizontal edge
connecting
(p,l)
and
(q,l)
.

(ii)

For each
l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
p\in P
, there exists a

vertical edge
connecting
(p,l)
and
(p,l+1)
.

For a vertex
(p,l)\in P\times(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})
, we define that
the
depth
of
(p,l)
is
l
.

We endow
\mathcal{H}(P)
with the graph metric.

When
P
is a discrete proper metric space,

\mathcal{H}(P)
is a proper geodesic space
that is hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov.
See
[
13

.

.

We also denote by

.

Conversely, we suppose that a corona

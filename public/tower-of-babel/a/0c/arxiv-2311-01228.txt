Power law in Sandwiched Volterra Volatility model
G. Di Nunno giulian@math.uio.no, A. Yurchenko-Tytarenko antony@math.uio.no

Abstract
In this paper, we present analytical proof demonstrating that the Sandwiched Volterra Volatility (SVV) model is able to reproduce the power-law behavior of the at-the-money implied volatility skew, provided the correct choice of the Volterra kernel. To obtain this result, we assess the second-order Malliavin differentiability of the volatility process and investigate the conditions that lead to explosive behavior in the Malliavin derivative. As a supplementary result, we also prove a general Malliavin product rule.



Keywords:
SVV model, stochastic volatility, sandwiched process, Gaussian Volterra noise, Malliavin calculus

91G30; 60H10; 60H35; 60G22

1
Introduction

One of the well-established benchmarks for evaluating option pricing models is comparing the model-generated Black-Scholes implied volatility surface
(\tau,\kappa)\mapsto\widehat{\sigma}(\tau,\kappa)
with the empirically observed one
(\tau,\kappa)\mapsto\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{emp}}(\tau,\kappa)
. In this context,
\tau
represents the
time to maturity
and
\kappa:=\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}S_{0}}
is the
log-moneyness
with
K
denoting the strike,
S_{0}
the current price of an underlying asset and
r
being the instantaneous interest rate. In particular, for any fixed
\tau
, the values of
\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{emp}}(\tau,\kappa)
plotted against
\kappa
are known to produce convex “
smiley
” patterns with negative slopes at-the-money (i.e. when
\kappa\approx 0
). Furthermore, as reported in e.g.
[
9
,
17
,
21
]
or
[
10
, Subsection 2.2]
, the smile at-the-money becomes progressively steeper as
\tau\to 0
with a rule-of-thumb behavior

\left|\frac{\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{emp}}(\tau,\kappa)-\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{emp}}(\tau,\kappa^{\prime})}{\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}}\right|\propto\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+H},\quad\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}\approx 0,\quad H\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right).

(1.1)

The phenomenon (
1.1
) is known as the
power law
of the at-the-money implied volatility skew, and if one wants to replicate it, one may look for a model with

\left|\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}}{\partial\kappa}(\tau,\kappa)\right|_{\kappa=0}=O(\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}),\quad\tau\to 0.

(1.2)

However, it turns out that the property (
1.2
) is not easy to obtain: for example, as discussed in
[
2
, Section 7.1]
or
[
24
, Remark 11.3.21]
, classical Brownian diffusion stochastic volatility models fail to produce implied volatilities with power law (
1.2
). In the literature, (
1.2
) is usually replicated by introducing a volatility process with very low Hölder regularity within the
rough
volatility framework popularized by Gatheral, Jaisson and Rosenbaum in their landmark paper
[
21
]
. The efficiency of this approach can be explained as follows.

•

On the one hand, a theoretical result of Fukasawa
[
18
]
suggests that the volatility process cannot be Hölder continuous of a high order in continuous non-arbitrage models exhibiting the property (
1.2
). In other words, the roughness of volatility is, in some sense, a necessary condition to reproduce (
1.2
) (at least in the fully continuous setting).

•

On the other hand, as proved in the seminal 2007 paper
[
2
]
of Alòs, León and Vives, the short-term explosion (
1.2
) of the implied volatility skew can be deduced from the explosion of the Malliavin derivative of volatility. In particular, the latter characteristic is exhibited by
fractional Brownian motion with
H<1/2
, a common driver in rough volatility literature.

However, despite the ability to reproduce the power law (
1.2
), rough volatility models are not perfect. In particular,

–

in the specific context of fractional Brownian motion, roughness contradicts the observations
[
7
,
15
,
16
,
25
,
29
]
of long memory on the market;

–

in addition, volatility processes with long memory seem to be better in replicating the shape of implied volatility for longer maturities
[
8
,
19
,
20
]
;

–

furthermore, there is no guaranteed procedure of transition between physical and pricing measures: it is not always clear whether the volatility process
\sigma=\{\sigma(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
hits zero and therefore the integral
\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}(s)}ds
that is typically present in martingale densities (see e.g.
[
6
]
) may be poorly defined;

–

just like many classical Brownian stochastic volatility models (see e.g.
[
3
]
), they may suffer from moment explosions in price, which results in complications with the pricing of some assets, quadratic hedging, and numerical methods.

For more details on rough volatility, we refer the reader to a recent review
[
10
, Subsection 3.3.2]
or a regularly updated literature list on the subject
[
1
]
.

Recently, a series of papers
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
introduced the
Sandwiched Volterra Volatility (SVV) model
which accounts for all the problems mentioned above. More precisely, the volatility process
Y=\{Y(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
is assumed to follow the stochastic differential equation

Y(t)=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,Y(s))ds+Z(t)

driven by a general Hölder continuous Gaussian Volterra process
Z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dB(s)
. The special part of the equation above is the drift
b
. It is assumed that there are two continuous functions
0<\varphi<\psi
such that for some
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle b(t,y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle y\in(\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+\varepsilon),

\displaystyle b(t,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle y\in(\psi(t)-\varepsilon,\psi(t)).

Such an explosive nature of the drift resembling the one in SDEs for Bessel processes (see e.g.
[
28
, Chapter XI]
) or singular SDEs of
[
22
]
ensures that, with probability 1,

0<\varphi(t)<Y(t)<\psi(t),

which immediately solves the moment explosion problem (see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 2.6]
) and allows for a transparent transition between physical and pricing measures
[
11
, Subsection 2.2]
. In addition, the flexibility in the choice of the kernel
\mathcal{K}

should
allow to replicate both the long memory and the power law behavior (
1.2
).

The main goal of this paper is to give the theoretical justification to the latter claim: we prove that, with the correct choice of the Volterra kernel
\mathcal{K}
, the SVV model indeed reproduces (
1.2
). In order to do that, we employ the fundamental result
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
of Alòs, León and Vives mentioned above and check that the Malliavin derivative
DY(t)
indeed exhibits explosive behavior. The difficulty of this approach is as follows. While the first-order Malliavin differentiability of
Y(t)
is established in
[
11
, Section 3]
with

D_{s}Y(t)=\mathcal{K}(t,s)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}du,

[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
actually demands the existence of the second-order Malliavin derivative. In principle, it is intuitively clear how this derivative should look like:

\displaystyle D_{r}D_{s}Y(t)

\displaystyle=D_{r}\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}du

(1.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)D_{r}\left[b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\right]du

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}D_{r}\left[b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\right]du

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))D_{r}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\right]du

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}D_{r}\left[Y(u)\right]du

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(v,Y(v))D_{r}[Y(v)]dvdu.

However, the computations in (
1.3
) are far from straightforward to be justified. For example, the functions
y\mapsto b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)
and
y\mapsto b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,y)
demonstrate explosive behavior as
y\to\varphi(t)+
and
y\to\psi(t)-
for any
t\in[0,T]
. This makes it impossible to use the classical Malliavin chain rules such as
[
26
, Proposition 1.2.3]
requiring boundedness of the derivative or
[
26
, Proposition 1.2.4]
demanding the Lipschitz condition. In order to overcome this issue, we have to use some special properties of the volatility process established in
[
13
]
and tailor a version of the Malliavin chain rule specifically for our needs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some necessary details about the sandwiched volatility process
Y
. In Section
3
, we prove second-order Malliavin differentiability of
Y(t)
. Finally, in Section
4
, we use
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
to determine conditions on the kernel under which the SVV model reproduces (
1.2
). In Appendix
A
, we gather some necessary facts from Malliavin calculus, list some of the notation and, in addition, we prove a general Malliavin product rule to fit our purposes and that we were not able to find in the literature.

2
Preliminaries on sandwiched processes

In this section, we gather all the necessary details about the main object of our study: the class of
sandwiched processes driven by Hölder-continuous Gaussian Volterra noises
.

Fix some
T\in(0,\infty)
and consider a kernel
\mathcal{K}:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

The kernel
\mathcal{K}
is of Volterra type, i.e.
\mathcal{K}(t,s)=0
whenever
t\leq s
, and

(K1)

\mathcal{K}
is square-integrable, i.e.

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}^{2}(t,s)dsdt<\infty,

(K2)

there exists
H\in(0,1)
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,H)
and
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T

\int_{0}^{T}(\mathcal{K}(t_{2},s)-\mathcal{K}(t_{1},s))^{2}ds\leq C_{\lambda}|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\lambda},

where
C_{\lambda}>0
is some constant depending on
\lambda
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that items (K1) and (K2) of Assumption
1
jointly imply that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}^{2}(t,s)ds<\infty.

(2.1)

Let
B=\{B(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
be a standard Brownian motion. Assumption
1
allows to define a
Gaussian Volterra process

Z(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dB(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(2.2)

and, moreover, Assumption
1
(K2) together with
[
4
, Theorem 1 and Corollary 4]
imply that
Z
has a modification with Hölder continuous trajectories of any order
\lambda\in(0,H)
. In what follows, we always use this modification of
Z
: in other words, with probability 1, for any
\lambda\in(0,H)
there exists a random variable
\Lambda=\Lambda(\lambda)>0
such that for all
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T

|Z(t_{2})-Z(t_{1})|\leq\Lambda|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{\lambda}.

(2.3)

Furthermore, as stated in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
, the random variable
\Lambda
from (
2.3
) can be chosen such that

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{r}]<\infty\quad\text{for all }r\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.4)

In what follows, we assume that (
2.4
) always holds.

Next, denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle:=\{(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}~|~\varphi(t)<y<\psi(t)\},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}

\displaystyle:=\{(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}~|~\varphi(t)\leq y\leq\psi(t)\}.

Take
H\in(0,1)
from Assumption
1
(K2), consider two
H
-Hölder continuous functions
\varphi
,
\psi
:
[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

0<\varphi(t)<\psi(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],

and define a function
b
:
\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
as

b(t,y):=\frac{\theta_{1}(t)}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}}}-\frac{\theta_{2}(t)}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma_{2}}}+a(t,y),

(2.6)

where the coefficients in (
2.6
) satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption 2
.

The constants
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}>0
and functions
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
,
a
are such that

(B1)

\gamma_{1}>\frac{1}{H}-1
,
\gamma_{2}>\frac{1}{H}-1
with
H\in(0,1)
being from Assumption
1
(K2);

(B2)

the functions
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
:
[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
are strictly positive and continuous;

(B3)

the function
a
:
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz in
y
uniformly in
t
, i.e. for any
N>0
there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
that does not depend on
t
such that

|a(t,y_{2})-a(t,y_{1})|\leq C_{N}|y_{2}-y_{1}|,\quad t\in[0,T],\quad y_{1},y_{2}\in[-N,N];

(B4)

a
:
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is two times differentiable w.r.t. the spatial variable
y
with
a
,
a^{\prime}_{y}
,
a^{\prime\prime}_{yy}
all being continuous on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
b^{\prime}_{y}
is bounded from above on
\mathcal{D}
: indeed,

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{1}\theta_{1}(t)}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(t)}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle<\max_{(t,y)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)<\infty.

Finally, fix
\varphi(0)<y_{0}<\psi(0)
and consider a stochastic differential equation of the form

Y(t)=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,Y(s))ds+Z(t),\quad t\in[0,T].

(2.7)

By
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
, under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the SDE (
2.7
) has a unique strong solution
Y=\{Y(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
. Moreover, with probability 1,

\varphi(t)<Y(t)<\psi(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

(2.8)

Remark 2.3
.

Motivated by the property (
2.8
), we will call the solution
Y
of (
2.7
) a sandwiched process.

In what follows, we will need to analyze the behavior of stochastic processes
|b(t,Y(t))|
,
|b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))|
and
|b^{\prime\prime}(t,Y(t))|
,
t\in[0,T]
. In this regard, the property (
2.8
) alone is not sufficient: the process
Y
can, in principle, approach the bounds
\varphi
and
\psi
which results in an explosive growth of the processes mentioned above. Luckily,
[
13
, Theorem 4.2]
provides a refinement of (
2.8
) allowing for a more precise control of
Y
near
\varphi
and
\psi
. We give a slightly reformulated version of this result below.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and
\lambda\in(0,H)
,
\Lambda=\Lambda(\lambda)>0
be from (
2.3
). Then there exist deterministic constants
C_{Y}=C_{Y}(\lambda)>0
and
\beta=\beta(\lambda)>0
such that

\varphi(t)+\frac{C_{Y}}{(1+\Lambda)^{\beta}}\leq Y(t)\leq\psi(t)-\frac{C_{Y}}{(1+\Lambda)^{\beta}}\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

In particular, since
\Lambda
can be chosen to have moments of all orders, for all
r\geq 0

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{(Y(t)-\varphi(t))^{r}}\right]<\infty,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{1}{(\psi(t)-Y(t))^{r}}\right]<\infty.

We finalize this section by citing the first-order Malliavin differentiability result for the sandwiched process (
2.7
) proved in
[
11
, Section 3]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and
Y
be the sandwiched process given by (
2.7
). Then, for any
t\in[0,T]
,
Y(t)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and, with probability 1, for a.a.
s\in[0,T]

D_{s}Y(t)=\mathcal{K}(t,s)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}du.

(2.9)

Remark 2.6
.

The result above actually holds for more general drifts than the one given in (
2.6
). The same is also, in principle, true for the results of the subsequent sections. Namely, it would be sufficient to assume that there exist deterministic constants
c>0
,
r>0
,
\gamma>\frac{1}{H}-1
and
0<y_{*}<\max_{t\in[0,T]}|\psi(t)-\varphi(t)|
such that

•

b
:
\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous on
\mathbb{D}
and has continuous partial derivatives
b^{\prime}_{y}
,
b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}
;

•

for any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}\max_{t\in[0,T]}|\psi(t)-\varphi(t)|
,

|b(t,y_{2})-b(t,y_{1})|\leq\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{r}}|y_{2}-y_{1}|,\quad t\in[0,T],\quad\varphi(t)+\varepsilon\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}\leq\psi(t)-\varepsilon;

•

b
has an explosive growth to
\infty
near
\varphi
and explosive decay to
-\infty
near
\psi
of order
\gamma>\frac{1}{H}-1
, i.e.

\displaystyle b(t,y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle y\in(\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+y_{*}),

\displaystyle b(t,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma}},

\displaystyle y\in(\psi(t)-y_{*},\psi(t));

•

for all
(t,y)\in\mathcal{D}
, the partial derivatives
b^{\prime}_{y}
and
b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}
satisfy

-C\left(1+\frac{c}{(y-\varphi(t))^{r}}+\frac{c}{(\psi(t)-y)^{r}}\right)<b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)<C

and

|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}|\leq C\left(1+\frac{c}{(y-\varphi(t))^{r}}+\frac{c}{(\psi(t)-y)^{r}}\right).

However, since (
2.6
) is the most natural choice satisfying these assumptions, we stick to this shape for notational convenience.

3
Second-order Malliavin differentiability

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and
Y=\{Y(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
be the sandwiched process defined by (
2.7
) with the drift (
2.6
).

Notation
.

Here and in the sequel,
C
will denote any positive deterministic constant the exact value of which is not relevant. Note that
C
may change from line to line (or even within one line).

The main goal of this section is to establish second-order Malliavin differentiability of the sandwiched process (
2.7
) and compute the corresponding derivative explicitly. As mentioned above, the main difficulty lies in controlling the behavior of
b(t,Y(t))
,
b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))
and
b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))
whenever
Y(t)
approaces the bounds. Luckily, Theorem
2.4
gives all the necessary tools to do that as summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a random variable
\xi>0
such that

•

for any
p\geq 1
,
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{p}]<\infty
;

•

for any
t\in[0,T]
,

|b(t,Y(t))|+|b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))|+|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))|<\xi.

In particular, for any
p\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(|b(t,Y(t))|^{p}+|b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))|^{p}+|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))|^{p}\right)\right]<\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\in(0,H)
and take the corresponding
\Lambda>0
from (
2.3
) and
C_{Y},\beta>0
be from Theorem
2.4
. Then

\displaystyle|b(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\theta_{1}(t)|}{(Y(t)-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}}}+\frac{|\theta_{2}(t)|}{(\psi(t)-Y(t))^{\gamma_{2}}}+|a(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{1}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta{\gamma_{1}}}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{1}}}+\frac{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{2}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta\gamma_{2}}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{2}}}+\sup_{(t,y)\in\mathcal{D}}|a(t,y)|

\displaystyle:=\xi_{0},

\displaystyle|b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{1}|\theta_{1}(t)|}{(Y(t)-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}+1}}+\frac{\gamma_{2}|\theta_{2}(t)|}{(\psi(t)-Y(t))^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+|a^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{1}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta(\gamma_{1}+1)}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{1}+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{2}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta(\gamma_{2}+1)}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{2}+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{(t,y)\in\mathcal{D}}|a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)|

\displaystyle:=\xi_{1},

\displaystyle|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{1}(\gamma_{1}+1)|\theta_{1}(t)|}{(Y(t)-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}+2}}+\frac{\gamma_{2}(\gamma_{2}+1)|\theta_{2}(t)|}{(\psi(t)-Y(t))^{\gamma_{2}+2}}+|a^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{1}(\gamma_{1}+1)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{1}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta(\gamma_{1}+2)}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{1}+2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{2}(\gamma_{2}+1)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\theta_{2}(t)|(1+\Lambda)^{\beta(\gamma_{2}+2)}}{C_{Y}^{\gamma_{2}+2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{(t,y)\in\mathcal{D}}|a^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,y)|

\displaystyle:=\xi_{2}.

Note that
\xi_{0}
,
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
have moments of all orders by the properties of
\Lambda
, see (
2.4
), and hence, putting

\xi:=\xi_{0}+\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},

we obtain the required result.
∎

As noted in Theorem
2.5
,
Y(t)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
for each
t\geq 0
. In fact, Proposition
3.1
together with the shape (
2.9
) of the derivative allow to establish a more general result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
t\in[0,T]
and
p>1
,
Y(t)\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
.

Proof.

Note that, by (
2.8
),
\mathbb{E}[|Y(t)|^{p}]<\infty
for any
p>1
, so, by Lemma
A.3
from the Appendix, it is sufficient to prove that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]<\infty

for any
p>1
. Note that, by Remark
2.2
,

\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}<\exp\left\{cT\right\},

where

c:=\max_{(t,y)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

and, by Proposition
3.1
, there exists a random variable
\xi
having all moments such that

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|b^{\prime}_{y}(s,Y(s))|\leq\xi.

Hence

\displaystyle|D_{s}Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{K}(t,s)|+\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))|\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}du

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{K}(t,s)|+\xi\exp\left\{cT\right\}\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)|du.

By Assumption
1
and Remark
2.1
,

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}^{2}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}<\infty,

therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}^{2}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}^{2}(u,s)(b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u)))^{2}\exp\left\{2\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}duds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}^{2}(t,s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{p}\right]\exp\left\{pcT\right\}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}^{2}(u,s)duds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle<\infty,

which ends the proof.
∎

Our next goal is to establish the Malliavin chain rule for the random variables

and
\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}

Proposition 3.3
.

For any
0\leq u\leq t\leq T
and
p>1
,

1)

b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
with

(3.3)

2)

\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}

with

D_{s}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\right]=\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(v,Y(v))D_{s}Y(v)dv.

(3.4)

Proof.

1) We shall start from proving that

. Note that

is not a bounded function itself and it does not have bounded derivatives – hence the classical chain rule from
[

cannot be applied here in a straightforward manner. In order to overcome this issue, we will use the approach in the spirit of
[
27
, Lemma A.1]
or
[
11
, Proposition 3.4]
. For the reader’s convenience, we divide the proof into steps.

Step 0.
First of all, observe that

as a direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
. Also, for any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]<\infty.

Indeed, again by Proposition
3.1
together with the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{p}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{2p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right)^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Therefore, by Lemma

, it is sufficient to prove that

with (
3.3
) being the corresponding Malliavin derivative.

Step 1.
Let

|\phi(x)|\leq|x|
for all

Observe that

is bounded. Indeed, let

be such that

-\frac{\gamma_{1}\theta_{1}(t)}{\varepsilon_{m}^{\gamma_{1}+1}}+\max_{\varphi(t)\leq x\leq\psi(t)}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,x)<m\inf\supp\phi

-\frac{\gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(t)}{\varepsilon_{m}^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+\max_{\varphi(t)\leq x\leq\psi(t)}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,x)<m\inf\supp\phi.

Then,

•

if
y\in(\varphi(t),\varphi(t)+\varepsilon_{m})
, then

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{1}\theta_{1}(t)}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(t)}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\gamma_{1}\theta_{1}(t)}{\varepsilon_{m}^{\gamma_{1}+1}}+\max_{\varphi(t)\leq x\leq\psi(t)}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,x)

\displaystyle<m\inf\supp\phi,

so
\frac{b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)}{m}\notin\supp\phi

f_{m}(y)=0
and
f^{\prime}_{m}(y)=0
;

•

if
y\in(\psi(t)-\varepsilon_{m},\psi(t))
, then, similarly,

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{1}\theta_{1}(t)}{(y-\varphi(t))^{\gamma_{1}+1}}-\frac{\gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(t)}{(\psi(t)-y)^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\gamma_{2}\theta_{2}(t)}{\varepsilon_{m}^{\gamma_{2}+1}}+\max_{\varphi(t)\leq x\leq\psi(t)}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,x)

\displaystyle<m\inf\supp\phi,

so
\frac{b^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)}{m}\notin\supp\phi

f_{m}(y)=0
and
f^{\prime}_{m}(y)=0
;

•

on the compact set

Therefore, the function

, so

and, with probability 1 for a.a.

Now it remains to prove that

in

as

.

Step 2:

f_{m}(Y(t))\to b^{\prime}(t,Y(t))
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as

. By the definitions of
f_{m}
and
\phi

Step 3:

Df_{m}(Y(t))\to b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))DY(t)

as

. By the definitions of

and
\phi

\displaystyle\left(b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))}{m}\right)\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}Y(t))^{2}ds\to(b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t)))^{2}\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}Y(t))^{2}ds

. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}f_{m}(Y(t)))^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\left(b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{b^{\prime}_{y}(t,Y(t))}{m}\right)\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}Y(t))^{2}ds

Therefore, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(D_{s}f_{m}(Y(t))-b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(t,Y(t))D_{s}Y(t)\right)^{2}ds\right]\to 0,\quad m\to\infty,

which proves the first claim of the Proposition.

2) Let us proceed with the second claim and verify that

with (
3.4
) being the corresponding Malliavin derivative. Note that, since

. Moreover, by Proposition

), we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(v,Y(v))D_{s}Y(v)dv\right)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{p}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{u}^{t}\left(D_{s}Y(v)\right)^{2}dvds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{p}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{u}^{t}\mathcal{K}^{2}(v,s)dvds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]+C\exp\left\{pcT\right\}\mathbb{E}\left[\xi^{2p}\right]\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{u}^{t}\int_{s}^{v}\mathcal{K}^{2}(u,s)dudvds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

and hence it is sufficient to prove that

.

Since the Malliavin derivative operator

is closed and the expression

Finally, the function

satisfies the conditions of the chain rule from

and hence

) holds.
∎

4
Power law in VSV model

Having the second-order Malliavin differentiability in place, we now possess all the necessary tools to analyze the behavior of implied volatility skew of a model with the sandwiched process (
2.7
) as stochastic volatility. Namely, we consider a (risk-free) market model with the price process
S=\{S(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
of the form

\displaystyle S(t)

\displaystyle=e^{X(t)},

(4.1)

\displaystyle X(t)

\displaystyle=x_{0}+rt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}Y^{2}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)\left(\rho dB_{1}(s)+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{2}(s)\right),

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,Y(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dB_{1}(s),

where
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
are two independent Brownian motions,
X=\{X(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
denotes the (risk-free) log-price of some asset starting from some level
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
r
is a constant instantaneous interest rate, and
\rho\in(-1,1)
is a correlation coefficient that accounts for the leverage effect. As previously, the drift
b
and the Volterra kernel
\mathcal{K}
satisfy Assumptions
1
and
2
.

Remark 4.1
.

The model (
4.1
) was initially introduced in
[
11
]
and, given the nature of the volatility process, is called the Sandwiched Volterra Volatility (SVV) model.

To establish the conditions under which (
4.1
) gives power law of the short-term at-the-money implied volatility, we will apply the fundamental result
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
which connects the shape of the skew with the Malliavin derivative of the volatility.

Remark 4.2
.

In the recent literature (see e.g.
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
21
]
), it is typical to characterize the implied volatility skew in terms of
\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}}{\partial\kappa}
with
\kappa=\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau+x_{0}}}
being the log-moneyness. In
[
2
]
, a slightly different parametrization
\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}(\tau,x_{0})
is considered with

\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}(\tau,x)=\widehat{\sigma}\left(\tau,\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}}-x\right).

With this parametrization,

\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}(\tau,x)}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}\left(\tau,\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}}-x\right)}{\partial\kappa},

i.e. the typically negative at-the-money skews for
\widehat{\sigma}
are equivalent to positive
\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}(\tau,x)}{\partial x}
at
x=\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}}
and the power law (
1.2
) is equivalent to

\left|\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}}{\partial x}(\tau,x)\right|_{x=\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}}}=O(\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}),\quad\tau\to 0.

With Remark
4.2
in mind, let us provide a slightly adjusted version of
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Consider a risk-free log-price

X(t)=x_{0}+rt-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s)\left(\rho dB_{1}(s)+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}dB_{2}(s)\right),

(4.2)

where
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
are two independent Brownian motions,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is a deterministic initial value,
r
is an instantaneous interest rate,
\rho\in(-1,1)
is a correlation coefficient and
\sigma=\{\sigma(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
is a square-integrable stochastic process with right-continuous trajectories adapted to the filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{t},~t\in[0,T]\}
generated by
B_{1}
.

Assume that

(H1)

\sigma\in\mathbb{L}^{2,4}
with respect to
B_{1}
;

(H2)

there exists a constant
\varphi_{*}>0
such that, with probability 1,
\sigma(t)>\varphi_{*}
for all
t\in[0,T]
;

(H3)

there exists a constant
H\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
such that, with probability 1, for any
0<s<t<T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(D_{s}\sigma(t))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(t-s)^{1-2H}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(D_{r}D_{s}\sigma(t))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{t-r}{t-s}\right)^{1-2H},

(4.4)

where
C>0
is some constant;

(H4)

\sigma
has a.s. right-continuous trajectories;

(H5)

\sup_{r,s,t\in[0,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\left[(\sigma(s)\sigma(t)-\sigma^{2}(r))^{2}\right]\to 0
when
\tau\to 0+
.

Finally, assume that there exists a constant
K_{\sigma}>0
such that, with probability 1,

\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[D_{s}\sigma(t)\right]dtds-K_{\sigma}\to 0,\quad\tau\to 0+.

(4.5)

Then, with probability 1,

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\frac{\partial\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}}{\partial x}(\tau,x)\bigg|_{x=\log\frac{K}{e^{r\tau}}}=-\frac{\rho}{\sigma(0)}K_{\sigma}.

In particular, if
\rho K_{\sigma}<0
, the at-the-money implied volatility skew exhibits the power law behavior with the correct sign of the skew.

Remark 4.4
.

The original formulation of
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
is slightly more general than Theorem
4.3
above in the sense that

1)

in
[
2
, Theorem 6.3]
, the log-price
X
is allowed to have jumps;

2)

the result in
[
2
]
is formulated for the future implied volatility surfaces
\widehat{\sigma}_{\text{log-price}}(t_{0},\tau,X(t_{0}))
,
t_{0}\geq 0
.

Since we are interested in the continuous model (
4.1
), we removed the jump component in (
4.2
) and, for the simplicity of notation, we put
t_{0}=0
.

Observe that the SVV model (
4.1
) automatically satisfies a number of assumptions of Theorem
4.3
:

•

assumption (H2) with
\varphi^{*}:=\min_{t\in[0,T]}\varphi(t)>0
;

•

assumption (H4) since
Y
is continuous a.s.;

•

assumption (H1) by the results of Section
3
above.

Therefore, it remains to check (H3), (H5), and (
4.5
). Naturally, given the shape of the Malliavin derivative (
2.9
), both (H3) and (
4.5
) require additional assumptions on the kernel, so let us start with (H5).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then with probability 1,

\displaystyle\sup_{r,s,t\in[0,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\left[(Y(s)Y(t)-Y^{2}(r))^{2}\right]\to 0,\quad\tau\to 0.

Proof.

By
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
, there exists a positive random variable
\Upsilon=\Upsilon_{T}
such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]

|Y(t_{1})-Y(t_{2})|\leq\Upsilon|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\lambda}

and, for any
r>0
,

\mathbb{E}[\Upsilon^{r}]<\infty.

Therefore, given that
\max_{t\in[0,T]}Y(t)<\max_{t\in[0,T]}\psi(t)
by (
2.8
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[(Y(s)Y(t)-Y^{2}(r))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[(Y(s)(Y(t)-Y(r))+Y(r)(Y(s)-Y(r)))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[(Y^{2}(s)(Y(t)-Y(r))^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{2}(r)(Y(s)-Y(r))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2|t-r|^{2\lambda}\max_{s\in[0,T]}\psi^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}\left[\Upsilon^{2}\right]+2|s-r|^{2\lambda}\max_{s\in[0,T]}\psi^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}\left[\Upsilon^{2}\right]

and hence, with probability 1,

\displaystyle\sup_{r,s,t\in[0,\tau]}\mathbb{E}\left[(Y(s)Y(t)-Y^{2}(r))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\tau^{2\lambda}\max_{s\in[0,T]}\psi^{2}(s)\mathbb{E}\left[\Upsilon^{2}\right]\to 0

as
\tau\to 0+
.
∎

Our next step is to handle (
4.5
).

Proposition 4.6
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold and the Volterra kernel
\mathcal{K}
be such that

\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dtds\to K_{Y},\quad\tau\to 0+,

(4.6)

where
K_{Y}
is some finite constant. Then, with probability 1

\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[D_{s}Y(t)\right]dtds-K_{Y}\to 0,\quad\tau\to 0+.

Proof.

Recall that

F_{1}(t,u):=b^{\prime}_{y}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}

and that, by Proposition (
3.1
),

|F_{1}(t,u)|\leq e^{cT}\xi,

(4.7)

where
c:=\max_{(t,y)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)
. Then we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[D_{s}Y(t)\right]dtds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dtds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{K}(u,s)\mathbb{E}\left[F_{1}(t,u)\right]dudtds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dtds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(u,s)\left(\int_{u}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{1}(t,u)\right]dt\right)duds.

The term
\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dtds
converges to
K_{Y}
by (
4.6
). As for the second term, note that, with probability 1, for any
u\in[0,\tau]
,

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{\tau}|\mathbb{E}\left[F_{1}(t,u)\right]|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\xi\right]\tau

and hence, given (
4.6
), with probability 1,

\frac{1}{\tau^{\frac{3}{2}+H}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{s}^{\tau}\mathcal{K}(u,s)\left(\int_{u}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{1}(t,u)\right]dt\right)duds\to 0,\quad\tau\to 0+,

which ends the proof.
∎

Finally, let us deal with (H3).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold with
H\in\left(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2}\right)
and the Volterra kernel
\mathcal{K}
be such that for any
0\leq s<t\leq T

|\mathcal{K}(t,s)|\leq C|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}

(4.8)

for some constant
C>0
. Then the hypothesis (H3) from Theorem
4.3
holds for the volatility process
\sigma=Y
.

Proof.

Fix
0<r,s<t
. Then, taking into account (
4.7
), with probability 1,

\displaystyle|D_{s}Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{K}(t,s)|+\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||F_{1}(t,u)|du

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}+\xi\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+T\xi)|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}

\displaystyle=:\zeta|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H},

which immediately implies (
4.3
). Next, by Proposition
3.1
,

|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(v,Y(v))|\leq\xi

for any
v\in[0,T]
and, for any
0\leq u\leq t\leq T
,

\displaystyle|F_{2}(t,u)|

\displaystyle=\left|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(u,Y(u))\exp\left\{\int_{u}^{t}b^{\prime}_{y}(v,Y(v))dv\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{cT}\xi

with
c:=\max_{(t,y)\in\overline{\mathcal{D}}}a^{\prime}_{y}(t,y)
, so we can write

\displaystyle|D_{r}D_{s}Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||F_{1}(t,u)|\left(\int_{u}^{t}|b^{\prime\prime}_{yy}(v,Y(v))||D_{r}Y(v)|dv\right)du

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||F_{2}(t,u)||D_{r}Y(u)|du

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\xi^{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)|\left(\int_{u}^{t}|D_{r}Y(v)|dv\right)du+\xi\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||D_{r}Y(u)|du\right)

\displaystyle=C\left(\xi^{2}\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)|\left(\int_{u\vee r}^{t}|D_{r}Y(v)|dv\right)du+\xi\int_{r\vee s}^{t}|\mathcal{K}(u,s)||D_{r}Y(u)|du\right).

Taking into account (
4.8
) and (
4.9
),

\displaystyle|D_{r}D_{s}Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\xi^{2}\zeta\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}\left(\int_{u\vee r}^{t}|v-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}dv\right)du

\displaystyle\qquad+\xi\zeta\int_{r\vee s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|u-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\xi^{2}\zeta\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|t-r|^{\frac{1}{2}+H}du

\displaystyle\qquad+\xi\zeta\int_{r\vee s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|u-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du\bigg).

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|t-r|^{\frac{1}{2}+H}du

\displaystyle\leq C|t-r|^{\frac{1}{2}+H}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}+H}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{t-r}{t-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-H}.

As for the integral
\int_{r\vee s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|u-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du
, we have two cases:

•

if
0<r\leq s<t
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|u-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}|u-s|^{-1+2H}du

\displaystyle\leq C(t-s)^{2H}\leq C\left(\frac{t-r}{t-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-H};

•

similarly, if
0<s<r<t
and given that
H>\frac{1}{6}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{t}|u-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}|u-r|^{-\frac{1}{2}+H}du

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{t}|u-r|^{-1+2H}du

\displaystyle\leq C(t-r)^{2H}\leq C\left(\frac{t-r}{t-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-H}.

In any case,

\displaystyle|D_{r}D_{s}Y(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\xi\zeta(\xi+1)\left(\frac{t-r}{t-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-H},

where
\xi
and
\zeta
are random variables having all moments, and hence (
4.4
) holds.
∎

Appendix A
Selected results from Malliavin calculus

A.1
Malliavin derivative and the space
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}

Hereafter, we summarize the essentials of the Malliavin derivative with respect to the classical Brownian motion. For more details, we refer the reader to the classical books
[
26
]
or
[
14
]
.

Denote
C^{(\infty)}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of all infinitely differentiable functions with the derivatives of at most polynomial growth. Let
B=\{B(t),~t\in[0,T]\}
be a standard Brownian motion. For any
h\in L^{2}([0,T])
, denote

B(h):=\int_{0}^{T}h(t)dB(t).

Definition A.1
.

The random variables
X
of the form

X=f(B(h_{1}),...,B(h_{n})),

where
n\geq 1
,
f\in C^{(\infty)}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h_{1},...,h_{n}\in L^{2}([0,T])
are called smooth. The set of all smooth random variables is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition A.2
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{S}
. The Malliavin derivative of
X
(with respect to
B
) is the
L^{2}([0,T])
-valued random variable of the form

DX:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(B(h_{1}),...,B(h_{n}))h_{k}.

By
[
26
, Proposition 1.2.1]
, the operator
D
is closable from
L^{p}(\Omega)
to
L^{p}(\Omega\times[0,T])
for any
p\geq 1
, and we use the same notation
D
for the closure. The domain of this closure
D
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, i.e. the closure of the class
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm

\|X\|_{1,p}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}X)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

is traditionally denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
. This definition can be iterated as described in
[
26
, p. 27]
to introduce the iterated derivative
D^{k}X
as a random variable with values in
(L^{2}([0,T]))^{\otimes k}\sim L^{2}([0,T]^{k})
. One can also define
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
as the completion of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the seminorm

\|X\|_{k,p}:=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{p}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{E}\left[\|D^{j}X\|_{L^{2}([0,T]^{k})}^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(A.1)

Throughout the paper, we often use the following lemma which is essentially a simplified version of
[
26
, Proposition 1.5.5]
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
p>1
and
X\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
be such that

\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{p}\right]<\infty

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}(D_{s}X)^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]<\infty.

Then
X\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
.

A.2
Generalized Malliavin product rule

Finally, let us prove a generalized version of the product rule from
[
26
, Exercise 1.2.12]
or
[
14
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
X_{1}
,
X_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
be such that

(i)

X_{1}X_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
;

(ii)

X_{2}DX_{1}
,
X_{1}DX_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
.

Then
X_{1}X_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and

D[X_{1}X_{2}]=X_{2}DX_{1}+X_{1}DX_{2}.

If, in addition,

\mathbb{E}[|X_{1}X_{2}|^{p}]<\infty,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}(X_{2}D_{u}X_{1}+X_{1}D_{u}X_{2})^{2}du\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]<\infty

for some
p\geq 2
, then
X_{1}X_{2}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a compactly supported function such that
\phi(x)=x
whenever
|x|\leq 1
and
|\phi(x)|\leq|x|
for all
|x|>1
. For
m\geq 1
, put

f_{m}(x_{1},x_{2}):=m^{2}\phi\left(\frac{x_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{x_{2}}{m}\right)

and observe that both partial derivatives

\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial x_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})=m\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{x_{2}}{m}\right),\quad\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})=m\phi\left(\frac{x_{1}}{m}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{x_{2}}{m}\right)

are bounded. Therefore, by the classical chain rule
[
26
, Proposition 1.2.3]
,

Df_{m}(X_{1},X_{2})=m\left(\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{1}+\phi\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{2}\right).

Now it is sufficient to prove that

f_{m}(X_{1},X_{2})\to X_{1}X_{2}

(A.2)

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

m\left(\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{1}+\phi\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{2}\right)\to X_{2}DX_{1}+X_{1}DX_{2}

(A.3)

in
L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T])
as
m\to\infty
.

Observe that
|f_{m}(X_{1},X_{2})|\to X_{1}X_{2}
a.s. as
m\to\infty
and

|f_{m}(X_{1},X_{2})|\leq X_{1}X_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega),

so (
A.2
) holds by the dominated convergence theorem. Next, since
\phi^{\prime}
is bounded, we have that, with probability 1,

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\left|\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{1}+\phi\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)DX_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\left(|X_{2}DX_{1}|+|X_{1}DX_{2}|\right)\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T]).

Therefore, since
m\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)\to X_{2}
a.s. and
m\phi\left(\frac{X_{1}}{m}\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{X_{2}}{m}\right)\to X_{1}
a.s. as
m\to\infty
, (
A.3
) holds by the dominated convergence, which ends the proof of the first claim.

The second claim immediately follows from Lemma
A.3
.
∎

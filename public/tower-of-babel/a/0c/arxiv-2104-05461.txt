Interpolating sequences for the Banach algebras generated by a class of test functions
Anindya Biswas, Vikramjeet Singh Chandel

Abstract
Given a domain Ω \Omega in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and a collection of test functions Ψ \Psi on Ω \Omega , we consider the complex-valued Ψ \Psi -Schur-Agler class associated to the pair ( Ω , Ψ ) (\Omega,\,\Psi) . In this article, we characterize interpolating sequences for the associated Banach algebra of which the Ψ \Psi -Schur-Agler class is the closed unit ball. When Ω \Omega is the unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} in the complex plane ℂ \mathbb{C} and the class of test function includes only the identity function on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , the aforementioned algebra is the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on 𝔻 \mathbb{D} and in this case, our characterization reduces to the well known result by Carleson. Furthermore, we present several other cases of the pair ( Ω , Ψ ) (\Omega,\,\Psi) , where our main result could be applied to characterize interpolating sequences which also show the efficacy of our main result.



Keywords—
Interpolating sequence, Test functions, Schur-Agler class, Grammian

1
Introduction.

1.1
Interpolating sequences: An overview

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\mathscr{A}
be a Banach algebra of bounded functions on
\Omega
with the norm

||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\mathscr{A}}
and having the property
that
||f||_{\mathscr{A}}\geq||f||_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{z\in\Omega}|f(z)|
for every
f\in\mathscr{A}
. In what follows,
l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
will denote the Banach algebra of
all bounded sequences with sup-norm. Given a sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
, we consider the following linear map:

L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\big(\mathscr{A},\,||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{A}\big)\longrightarrow l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N}),\ \ \ \text{defined by $L(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\phi(z_{i})\}$ for all $\phi\in\mathscr{A}$}.

(1)

Observe that
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|\phi(z_{i})|\leq||\phi||_{\infty}\leq||\phi||_{\mathscr{A}}
, hence
L
above is a bounded linear operator on
\mathscr{A}
.
Consider the following abstract interpolation problem:

(IS)

Given a Banach algebra
(\mathscr{A},\,||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\mathscr{A}})
of bounded functions on
\Omega
with the property that
||\phi||_{\mathscr{A}}\geq||\phi||_{\infty}
for every
\phi\in\mathscr{A}
. Characterize all those sequences
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
\Omega
for which the bounded linear map
L
, as defined above, is a surjective map.

A sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
for which the map
L
is surjective will be called an
interpolating sequence for the algebra
\mathscr{A}
.

Note that if
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an interpolating sequence for
\mathscr{A}
then — from the open mapping theorem — there exists a
\delta>0
such that
l^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{N})
:=the closed unit ball of
l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
, is contained in
L(\delta\,\mathscr{A}_{1})
, where
\mathscr{A}_{1}
denotes the closed unit ball of
\mathscr{A}
in its norm. The smallest of such a
\delta
is called

the constant of interpolation
for the interpolating sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
.
This, in particular, implies that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\phi_{i}\in\mathscr{A}
with
||\phi_{i}||_{\mathscr{A}}\leq M
, such that
\phi_{i}(z_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
, where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker symbol and
M
is the constant of
interpolation associated to
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. A sequences
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
for which there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{i}\in\mathscr{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
that is uniformly bounded and has
the property that
\phi_{i}(z_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
are called
strongly separated sequences
. So every interpolating sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
for
\mathscr{A}
is strongly separated. Given a sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
we shall call it is
weakly separated by
\mathscr{A}
if there exists
R>0
such that for each pair

i\neq j
there exists
\phi_{i,j}\in\mathscr{A}
with
||\phi_{i,j}||_{\mathscr{A}}\leq R
such that
\phi_{i,j}(z_{i})=1
and
\phi_{i,j}(z_{j})=0
.

The origins of the problem (IS) lies in the case when
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
, where
\mathbb{D}
denotes the open unit disc in the
complex plane centered at
0
, and with
\mathscr{A}=H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
:= the bounded holomorphic functions in the unit disc with the sup-norm.
Carleson in
1958
proved the following theorem.

Result 1. (Carleson,
[
9
]
)
Let
\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{D}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{D}
. Then the following are equivalent.

1.

\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
.

2.

\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is weakly separated and the atomic measure
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\,\big(1-|\lambda_{i}|^{2}\big)\,\delta_{i}
is a Carleson measure for the Hardy
space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

3.

\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is strongly separated.

The reader is referred to
[
1
, Chapter 9]
for the definition of Carleson measure. What is essential here is that the Carleson measure condition can be equivalently stated in terms of the boundedness of the Grammian operator on
l^{2}
corresponding to the Szego kernel on
\mathbb{D}
. For this purpose
let us introduce the Grammian associated
with a positive kernel
k
on
\Omega
. See Section
2
for the definition of a positive kernel
k
and the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
that is associated to it.
Given a positive kernel
k
on
\Omega
and a sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
, let us denote by

k_{i}
the kernel function at
z_{i}
, i.e.,
k(\boldsymbol{\cdot},z_{i})
and write

k_{i,j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle k_{j},\,k_{i}\rangle
. Let
g_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=k_{i}/||k_{i}||
be the normalized kernel functions. The
Grammian associated to the sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}

is the infinite matrix
G
given by

G_{i,j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle g_{j},\,g_{i}\rangle=\genfrac{}{}{0.5pt}{0}{k_{i,j}}{||k_i||\,||k_j||}.

It is a fact that the Grammian associated to a sequence
\{z_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded on
l^{2}
if and only if the measure

\sum_{i=1}^{n}||k_{i}||^{-2}\,\delta_{i}
is a Carleson measure for
\mathcal{H}_{k}
; see

[
1
, Proposition 9.5]
. The Hardy space on the unit disk,
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, is a reproducing kernel
Hilbert space with the kernel
k
being the Szegő kernel. So the Carleson measure condition in the above result is equivalent to boundedness
of the Grammian matrix associated to the Szegő kernel and the sequence
\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}
above.

Shapiro–Shield in
[
14
]
gave an alternative proof of Carleson’s result by replacing the notions of Carleson measure condition and strong sepration
by conditions on the the Grammian matrix associated to a sequence. They also
considered interpolating sequences for many other holomorphic function spaces on the unit disc.

An important case of the problem (IS) is when
\mathscr{A}
is the multiplier algebra, denoted by
Mult(\mathcal{H}_{k})
,
of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{k}
associated to a kernel
k
, together with the multiplier norm. This was initiated by Marshall–Sundberg
[
13
]
and by C. Bishop
[
8
]
. They also introduced a notion of interpolating sequences for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and observed that the set of interpolating sequences for multiplier algebras
is contained in the set of interpolating sequences for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Moreover, if the kernel
satisfies the Pick property, then the two notions of interpolating sequences coincide;
see
[
1
, Theorem 9.19]
.
This is important since interpolating sequences for separable Hilbert spaces are exactly
those for which the Grammian is bounded and bounded from below.

A well studied class of positive
kernels is the family of complete Nevanlinna–Pick kernels (see
[
1
]
for the definition) that satisfy a more
stronger form of Pick property.
A characterization of the interpolating sequences
for the multiplier algebra for this class of kernels is now completely known.

Result 2.
Let
\{\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
be a sequence and let
k
be an irreducible complete Nevanlinna–Pick kernel.
Then the following are equivalent:

(IM)

the sequence is interpolating for
Mult(\mathcal{H}_{k})
,

(IH)

the sequence is interpolating for
\mathcal{H}_{k}
,

(S+C)

the sequence is weakly separated and the Grammian associated to the sequences

is bounded.

As mentioned before the equivalence of (IM) and (IH) above was established by
Marshall-Sundberg and by Bishop. The implication (IH)
\implies
(S+C) holds, in general, for any reproducing kernel Hilbert space; see e.g.
[
1
]
or
[
15
]
. The implication (S+C)
\implies
(IM) for irreducible complete Nevanlinna-Pick kernels is established in a recent article by
Aleman-Hartz–McCarthy–Richter
[
3
]
, where they applied Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava theorem, a path-breaking result that established the Kadison-Singer conjecture.

The condition (S+C) implies strong separation with respect to
Mult(\mathcal{H}_{k})
for kernels
having Pick property; see
[
1
, Theorem 9.43]
. On the other hand, both Marshall–Sundberg
[
13
]
and Bishop
[
8
]
have shown that strong separation does not imply (IM) in the case of the Dirichlet space of the unit disc.

1.2
Test functions

In this article, we shall address the problem (IS) with
\mathscr{A}
being those Banach algebras of bounded functions that are obtained by taking intersections of multiplier algebras of certain reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces associated with a class of test functions.
These are algebras which are not necessarily multiplier algebras
of a reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and were first introduced by Jim Agler.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and let
\Psi
be a family of functions on
\Omega
.
We say
\Psi
is a collection of
test functions
on
\Omega
if the following conditions hold:

1.

sup
\{|\psi(x)|\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\psi\in\Psi\}<1
for each
x\in\Omega
.

2.

For each finite subset
F
of
\Omega
, the collection
\{\psi|_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\psi\in\Psi\}
together with unity generates the algebra of all

\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
F
.

The second condition is not essential part of the definition, but it makes some situations simpler (see
[
4
]
and
[
11
]
).
The collection
\Psi
is a natural topological subspace of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{\Omega}
equipped with the product topology. For every

x\in\Omega
, there is an element
E(x)
in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Psi)
,
the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded functions on

\Psi
, such that

E(x)(\psi)=\psi(x)
. Clearly,

\|E(x)\|=\text{sup}_{\psi\in\Psi}|\psi(x)|<1

for each
x\in\Omega
. The functions
E(x)
will be used at several places in this paper.

Given
\Omega
and a collection of test functions
\Psi
, let us denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
the set of all

\mathbb{C}
-valued positive kernels
k
on
\Omega
for which
the operator
M_{\psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{H}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{k}
defined by
M_{\psi}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f\,\psi
, for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, is a
contraction for each
\psi\in\Psi
. Recall a contraction on a Hilbert space is a bounded linear operator whose operator norm is atmost
1
.

Definition 1
.

Let us denote by
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
the collection of such
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
for which
there exists a
C>0
having the following property:

(*)

for each
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
, the bounded linear operator
M_{\phi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{H}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{k}
defined by

M_{\phi}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f\,\phi
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
, is a bounded linear operator with
||M_{\phi}||_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\leq C
.

Given
\phi\in H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
, define:

||\phi||_{\Psi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\big\{C\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\text{$C$ satisfying the property $(*)$ above}\big\}.

(2)

It turns out that
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
is a Banach algebra with norm
||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\Psi}
. The

scalar-valued
\Psi
-Schur-Agler class
, denoted by
\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
, is defined by

\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\big\{\phi\in H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,||\phi||_{\Psi}\leq 1\big\}
. It is a general fact that if
\phi\in H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
then
||\phi||_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup\{|\phi(z)|\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,z\in\Omega\}\leq||M_{\phi}||_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
for all
k\in K_{\Psi}
. It follows from this that
||\phi||_{\infty}\leq||\phi||_{\Psi}
.
We are now ready to state the main result of this article concerning the
problem (IS) in the case when
\mathscr{A}=H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain and let
\Psi
be a family of test functions. Consider the Banach algebra

H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
consisting of bounded functions with the norm
||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\Psi}
as above.
Let
\{w_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\Omega
be a sequence in
\Omega
. Then the following are equivalent.

1.

\{w_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an interpolating sequence for
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
.

2.

For all admissible kernels
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
, the normalized Grammians
G_{k}
are uniformly bounded from below, i.e., there exists
N>0
such that

G_{k}\geq(1/N)\,\mathbb{I}
for all
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
.

3.

\{w_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is strongly separated and for all kernels
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
, the normalized Grammians
G_{k}
are uniformly bounded, i.e., there
exists
M>0
such that
G_{k}\leq M\,\mathbb{I}
for all
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
.

4.

(2)
and
(3)
above holds together.

Here, and elsewhere in the article
\mathbb{I}
shall denote the identity operator.

Remark.
Let
k,\,l\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
be such that
l\,=\,g\,k
for some positive kernel
g
. Then if
c\,\mathbb{I}-G_{k}\geq 0
for some
c>0
then
c\,\mathbb{I}-G_{l}\geq 0
. To see that, note that
G_{g}\geq 0
and by the Schur-product theorem we get
(c\,\mathbb{I}-G_{k})\,G_{g}\geq 0
. Notice now the Schur product of
\mathbb{I}
and
G_{g}
is
\mathbb{I}
and
G_{l}\,=\,G_{k}\,G_{g}
whence the conclusion. Proceeding similarly if
G_{k}-d\,\mathbb{I}\geq 0
for some
d>0
, then
G_{l}-d\,\mathbb{I}\geq 0
, where
l,\,k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
are as before.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
and
\Psi=\{z\}
then every
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
is of the form

k\,=\,s\,g
, where
s
denotes the Szegő kernel and
g
is some positive kernel. It follows from the remark
above that conditions
(2)
and
(3)
in Theorem
2
has to be satisfied only for the Szegő kernel.
It is a fact that
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
is
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
in this case. This leads
to a characterization of interpolating sequences for
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
which is equivalent to Result 1
(
[
1
, Section 9.5]
) by Carleson.

We shall present the proof of Theorem
2
in Section
4
. Later, in Section
5
, we shall provide several other examples where the above theorem
could be applied in characterizing interpolating sequences for those algebras that could be realized as

H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
for some appropriately chosen
\Psi
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we prove a few important lemmas and gather certain basic tools that will be needed in upcoming sections. We first begin with the definition of
\Psi
-Schur-Agler class functions in operator-valued setting. To do that, we need to recall various notions of positive kernel on a domain
\Omega
.

A positive kernel
k
on a set
\Omega
is a function
k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that for any
n\geq 1
, any
n

points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
\Omega
and any
n
complex numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{c_{i}}c_{j}k(x_{i},x_{j})\geq 0.

Let
\mathcal{E}
is a Hilbert space and
k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\times\Omega\rightarrow B(\mathcal{E})
is a function, then
k
is called a positive
kernel if for any
n\geq 1
, any
n
points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
in
\Omega
and any
n
vectors
\mathbf{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}}
in
\mathcal{E}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle k(x_{i},x_{j})\mathbf{e_{j}},\mathbf{e_{i}}\rangle\geq 0.

(3)

We also recall the notion of completely positive kernels here. Let
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
be two
C^{*}
-algebras and let
\Gamma
be a function
on
\Omega\times\Omega
taking values in
B(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
(space of all bounded linear operators from
\mathscr{A}
to
\mathscr{B}
).
\Gamma
is called a

completely positive kernel
if

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}b_{i}^{*}\ \Gamma(x_{i},x_{j})(a_{i}^{*}a_{j})b_{j}\geq 0

(4)

for all
n\geq 1
,
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
,
b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathscr{B}
and
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\in\Omega
.

2.1
The
\Psi
-Schur-Agler Class: General Case

Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{E}
and a
B(\mathcal{E})
-valued kernel
K

(satisfying (
3
))

on
\Omega
, there is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}(K)
of
\mathcal{E}
-valued functions on
\Omega
such that span of the set

\displaystyle\{K(\cdot,\omega)\mathbf{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{e}\in\mathcal{E},\,\omega\in\Omega\}

is dense in
\mathcal{H}(K)
and for any
\mathbf{e}\in\mathcal{E}
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
h\in\mathcal{H}(K)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle h,K(\cdot,\omega)\mathbf{e}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(K)}=\langle h(\omega),\mathbf{e}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}.

Given a set of test functions
\Psi
on
\Omega
, a kernel
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\times\Omega\rightarrow B(\mathcal{E})
is said to be
\Psi
-admissible
if
the map
M_{\psi}
, sending each element
h\in\mathcal{H}(K)
to
\psi\cdot h
, is a contraction on
\mathcal{H}(K)
.
We denote the set of all
B(\mathcal{E})
-valued
\Psi
-admissible kernels by
\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}(\mathcal{E})
. For two Hilbert spaces
\mathscr{U}
and
\mathscr{Y}
,
we say that
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\rightarrow B(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})
is in
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})

if there is a constant
C
such that the
B(\mathscr{Y}\otimes\mathscr{Y})
-valued function

(C^{2}I_{\mathscr{Y}}-S(x)S(y)^{*})\otimes k(x,y)

(5)

is a positive
B(\mathscr{Y}\otimes\mathscr{Y})
-valued kernel for every
k
in
\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{Y})
. If
S
is in
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})
,
then we denote by
C_{S}
the smallest
C
which satisfies (
5
).
The
\Psi
-Schur-Agler class, denoted by
\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})
, is the set of those

S\in H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})
for which
C_{S}=1
. Also
observe when
\mathscr{U}=\mathbb{C}=\mathscr{Y}
, then
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{U},\mathscr{Y})=H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
.
We begin with the following important lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{H}
be Hilbert spaces such that either
\mathscr{H}=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{Y}
. Let
g\in\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
and
f\in\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{H})
.
Then if we set
fg(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=f(z)g(z)
for all
z\in\Omega
then
fg\in\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{H})
.

Proof

Notice that for each
z\in\Omega
,
fg(z)\in B(\mathscr{X},\,\mathscr{H})
. We need to show that for each
k\in\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{H})
,

\big(I_{\mathscr{H}}-f(z)g(z)g(w)^{*}f(w)^{*}\big)\otimes k(z,\,w)
is a positive
B(\mathscr{H}\otimes\mathscr{H})
-valued kernel.
Note that

\displaystyle\big(I_{\mathscr{H}}-(fg)(z)(fg)(w)^{*}\big)\otimes k(z,\,w)=

\displaystyle\big(I_{\mathscr{H}}-f(z)g(z)g(w)^{*}f(w)^{*}\big)\otimes k(z,\,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(I_{\mathscr{H}}-f(z)f(w)^{*}\big)\otimes k(z,w)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f(z)\big(I_{\mathscr{Y}}-g(z)g(w)^{*}\big)f(w)^{*}\otimes k(z,w).

(6)

The expression
\big(I_{\mathscr{H}}-f(z)f(w)^{*}\big)\otimes k(z,w)
is positive as
f\in\mathscr{S\mspace{-5mu}A}_{\Psi}(\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{H})
.
We now consider
f(z)\big(I_{\mathscr{Y}}-g(z)g(w)^{*}\big)f(w)^{*}\otimes k(z,w)
in two cases:

Case 1.

\mathscr{H}=\mathbb{C}
.

In this case, the above expression becomes
\big(f(z)(I_{\mathscr{Y}}-g(z)g(w)^{*})f(w)^{*}\big)k(z,w)
. Now we make
the following claim.

Claim.
For a
\mathbb{C}
-valued
\Psi
-admissible kernel
k
, if we let
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=k\,I_{\mathscr{Y}}
then
K

5
Examples

In this section, we present several cases where Theorem
2
could be applied to give a
characterization of interpolating sequences. A few of the cases that
appear below have already been addressed in the literature.

1.

The case of Polydisc.
This is the case when
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n},\ n\geq 1
. The class of test functions that we consider in this case is
\Psi\,=\,\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
. The case
n=1
has already been discussed
after the statement of Theorem
2
.

In the case
n=2
, due to Ando’s inequality, we know that

H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C)}\,=\,H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=
the set of all bounded holomorphic functions on the bidisc,
with the norm
||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\Psi}
being
sup-norm. Therefore, Theorem
2
provides a characterization of interpolating sequences in the bidisc
for the Banach algebra of bounded holomorphic functions with the sup-norm. This case was already considered by Agler–McCarthy in
[
2
]
.

In general, when
n\geq 3
, the Banach algebra
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
does not coincide with the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
; see e.g.
[
17
]
. One can still apply Theorem
2
to give a characterization of interpolating sequences in the polydisc for the algebra

H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
with
\Psi\,=\,\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
.

2.

The case of multiply connected planar domains.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in the complex
plane with boundary consisting of
m+1
disjoint smooth Jordan curves
\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{m}

where
\partial_{0}
denotes the boundary of the unbounded component of the complement of
\Omega
. Then
there exists a collection of test functions
\Psi\,=\,\{\psi_{\bf x}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,{\bf x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\Omega}\}
, indexed by the so-called

\Omega
-torus
\mathbb{T}_{\Omega}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\,\partial_{0}\times\partial_{1}\times\dots\times\partial_{m}

(see
[
4
, Section 4.1]
and
[
10
]
), such that
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})
is equal to the set of all bounded
holomorphic functions on
\Omega
and the norm
||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\Psi}
being equal to the sup-norm.
Using this class of test functions, Theorem
2
can be applied to characterize interpolating sequences for bounded
holomorphic functions in
\Omega
.

3.

The case of constrained algebras.
Let us denote by
A(\mathbb{D})
the Banach algebra of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}

that are continuous upto
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. The algebra
A(\mathbb{D})
is called
the disk algebra
.
Let
B
be a finite Blaschke product of degree
N\geq 2
and consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{B}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\,\mathbb{C}+B(z)\,A(\mathbb{D})
.
Let us denote by
H^{\infty}_{B}
the weak-closure
of
\mathcal{A}_{B}
. In
[
12
]
, a minimal class of test functions has been constructed for the algebras

H^{\infty}_{B}
. Therefore, one could use Theorem
2
to characterize interpolating sequences in

\mathbb{D}
for the constrained algebras
H^{\infty}_{B}
using this class of test functions.

4.

The case of symmetrized bidisc.
In this case
\Omega\,=\,\mathbb{G}_{2}
where
\mathbb{G}_{2}
is the symmetrized bidisc
defined by
\mathbb{G}_{2}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\,\pi_{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
where
\pi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{C}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the symmetrization map defined by
\pi_{2}(z_{1},\,z_{2})\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\,(z_{1}+z_{2},\,z_{1}z_{2})
. A point in

\mathbb{G}_{2}
is also denoted by a pair
(s,\,p)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. It is a fact due to Agler–Young (see e.g.
[
5
]
) that
a point
(s,\,p)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
belongs to
\mathbb{G}^{2}
if and only if for every

\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
we have

\genfrac{}{}{0.5pt}{0}{2\alpha p-s}{2-\alpha s}\,\in\,\mathbb{D}.

Because of this one could consider the family
\Psi\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\psi_{\alpha}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\psi_{\alpha}(s,\,p)=(2\alpha p-s)/(2-\alpha s)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}\}
, as a family of test functions for
\mathbb{G}_{2}
. Then it is a fact
(see
[
5
]
) that
H^{\infty}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{C})\,=\,H^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}_{2})
, the set of bounded holomorphic functions
on
\mathbb{G}_{2}
and the norm
||\boldsymbol{\cdot}||_{\Psi}
being equal to sup-norm. Hence one could apply Theorem
2
to
characterize interpolating sequences for
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}_{2})
. This case too has been dealt and is due to Bhattacharyya–Sau

[
6
]
.

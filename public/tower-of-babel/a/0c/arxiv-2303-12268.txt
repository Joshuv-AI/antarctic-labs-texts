Rationality of holomorphic vertex operator algebras
Masahiko Miyamoto Institute of Mathematics, University of Tsukuba

Abstract
We prove that if V V is a unitary simple holomorphic vertex operator algebra of CFT-type, then V V is rational, that is, all ℕ {\mathbb{N}} -gradable V V -modules are direct sums of copies of V V .



1
Introduction

A concept of vertex operator algebras (shortly VOA) was introduced by Borcherds
[
1
]
to solve the moonshine conjecture in finite group theory.
It is now considered to be a rigorous algebraic definition of a chiral algebra
in conformal field theory.
In the last two decades, there has been quite good progress on the classification of holomorphic VOAs
V
of CFT-type and with central charge
24
(for example, see
[
2
]
and

[
6
]
).
In these works, to use modular invariance properties and to get the fact that weight one space is a reductive Lie algebra, most papers were assuming strongly regular conditions on
V
, that is,
positivity (or unitarity) of an invariant bilinear form, rationality (i.e., semisimplicity for
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable modules), and
C_{2}
-cofiniteness on
V
(or finiteness of simple (weak) modules).
For the definition of unitarity, see §2.
Compared with the classification of unimodular positive definite even lattices
[
7
]
, it is natural to
assume positivity over
{\mathbb{R}}
(or unitarity over
{\mathbb{C}}
) for an invariant bilinear form on
V
, but is it necessary to
assume the rationality and
C_{2}
-cofiniteness?
For some holomorphic VOAs of CFT-type with some stronger assumptions,
their rationalities are already known, see
[
4
]
.
In this paper, we will show that rationality comes from only the positivity (or unitary) of an invariant bilinear form of
V
for a holomorphic VOA
V
of CFT-type without any additional
assumptions. We note that every holomorphic VOA has a
symmetric invariant bilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
by
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1

If
V=\coprod_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{n}
is a unitary simple holomorphic VOA of CFT-type, then
V
is rational, that is,
all
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable
V
-modules are direct sums of copies of
V
,
where holomorphic means that
V
itself is the only one
simple
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable
V
-module.

The author expresses great thanks to T. Abe, C. H. Lam, and
H. Yamauchi for suggesting many improvements to this note and also checking the proof. He thanks the staff of Academia Sinica in Taiwan for their hospitality. The author also expresses thanks to the referee for his/her advice.

Two propositions and preliminary results

Throughout this paper,
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
denotes a
simple holomorphic VOA over
{\mathbb{C}}
.
In this paper, a
V
-module
M
is called
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable if there is an
{\mathbb{N}}
-grading structure
M=\oplus_{m=0}^{\infty}M_{(m)}
such that

v_{m}(M_{(n)})\subseteq M_{(n+{\rm wt}(v)-m-1)}
for all

. We call

the top level of

.
We also use
{\rm wt}(u)
to denote an eigenvalue of
L(0)
on an eigenvector

and

denotes the eigenspace of
L(0)
with an eigenvalue

for a
V
-module

.
For a simple
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable module

-t\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

for all
n
.
Let

be the Zhu algebra of a VOA
V
introduced by Zhu
[
9
]
. Then for an
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable
V
-module

M=\oplus_{m=0}^{\infty}M_{(m)}
,
M_{(0)}
is an
A(V)
-module.
One of the crucial results of Zhu’s theory we will use is that every simple
A(V)
-module is a top level of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable simple
V
-module.
Furthermore, for any
A(V)
-module

and a
V
-module
M
generated from
Z
, we can define a canonical
{\mathbb{N}}
-grading

M=\coprod_{m=0}^{\infty}M_{(m)}
on

such that the top level
M_{(0)}
of

is
Z
. In particular, since
A(V)
is a an
A(V)
-module, if
V
is rational, then

A(V)
is a direct sum of simple
A(V)
-modules. Namely,
A(V)
is semisimple.

If
V
is simple and holomorphic, then
V=\coprod_{n=-t}^{\infty}V_{n}
(with
\dim V_{-t}\not=0
) is the only simple
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable module
and
A(V)/J(A(V))={\rm End}(V_{-t})\cong{\rm Mat}_{s,s}({\mathbb{C}})
, where

and

J(A(V))
denotes the Jacobson radical of
A(V)
.
We note that if
A(V)
is Artinian, then most results in this section are well-known, but we have to treat the other cases, for example,
the case where
\dim J(A(V))=\infty
.
We will find that

implies the rationality of
V
(see Lemma 2).
Therefore, we study
J(A(V))
for a while.
Since
A(V)/J(A(V))\cong{\rm End}(V_{-t})
,
J(A(V))
is the unique maximal both-side ideal and is the intersection
of all maximal left ideals and coincides with the intersection of all maximal right ideals.
In particular, for
\alpha\in J(A(V))
,
1-\alpha
is a unit element of
A(V)
.
As an application, we have the following elementary lemma:

Lemma 1

For an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded
V
-module
M=\coprod_{m=0}^{\infty}M_{(m)}
and any
0\not=a\in M_{(0)}
, we have
J(A(V))a\not=A(V)a
.

[Proof]
Suppose false, then there is
\alpha\in J(A(V))
such that
\alpha a=a
,
that is,
(1-\alpha)a=0
, which contradicts the fact that
1-\alpha
is a unit element.

\square

We recall the notion of unitary VOAs from
[
3
]
.

Definition 1
(
[
3
]
)

Let
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
be a VOA over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
let
\phi:V\to V
be an anti-linear involution of
V

(
i.e,
\phi(\lambda v)=\bar{\lambda}\phi(u)
,
\phi({\bf 1})={\bf 1}
,
\phi(\omega)=\omega
,

\phi(u_{n}v)=\phi(u)_{n}\phi(v)
for any
u,v\in V
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and

\phi
has order
2

)
.
Then
(V,\phi)
is said to
be unitary if there exists a positive-definite Hermitian form
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}:V\times V\to{\mathbb{C}}
, which
is
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the first vector and anti-
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the second vector, such that the following
invariant property holds of any
a,u,v\in V
:

(Y(e^{zL(1)}(-z^{-2})^{L(0)}a,z^{-1})u,v)_{V}=(u,Y(\phi(a),z)v)_{V},

[formula]

where
L(n)
is defined by
Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
.

In our case, since
V
is a holomorphic VOA,
V
has a non-singular invariant bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfying
(2.1) by
[
5
]
. Hence if we put
(u,v)_{V}=\langle u,\phi(v)\rangle
for
v,u\in V
, then
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
becomes a desired Hermitian form.
Therefore,
V
is unitary if and only
if there is an anti-linear involution
\phi
of
V
such that
\langle v,\phi(v)\rangle>0
for all
0\not=v\in V
.
We fix an anti-linear involution
\phi
of
V
.

Definition 2
(
[
4
]
)

Let
M
be a (weak)
V
-module.
We call an element
\rho\in M
a vacuum-like element
if
L(-1)\rho=0
.

If
\rho
is a vacuum-like element, we can prove
v_{n}\rho=0
for

by induction. Because, by the definition of a (weak)
V
-module, there is
m
such that
v_{s}\rho=0
for
s\geq m
. Then
0=L(-1)v_{m}\rho=-mv_{m-1}\rho
and so
we have
v_{n}\rho=0
for
n\geq 0
.
In this case,
V\!\cdot\!\rho=\{v_{-1}\rho\mid v\in V\}

becomes a
V
-module which is isomorphic to
V
, see Lemma 9.6 in
[
4
]
.
Here and there, we use a notation
V\!\cdot\!S

to denote a
V
-submodule generated from a subset
S

of a
V
-module.

.

Proposition 1

Assume that a VOA
V
of CFT-type has a non-split extension

0\to V\cong W\to\tilde{V}\xrightarrow{\psi}V\to 0

of
V
-modules with a
V
-homomorphism
\psi
and
W\cong V
.
Set
\tilde{V}_{[n]}=\{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}\mid\exists m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ such that }(L(0)-n)^{m}\tilde{v}=0\}
the genelarized eigenspace of
L(0)
with eigenvalue
n
.
Let
\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in\tilde{V}_{[0]}
be an inverse image of the vacuum element
{\bf 1}\in V
by
\psi
and
set
u=L(-1)\tilde{{\bf 1}}
.
Then
u\not=0
and by viewing
u\in V_{1}
,

u(0)
acts on
V_{k}
as a nilpotent operator for each
k
.

[Proof]
We note
u\in W_{1}
.
If
u=0
, then
\tilde{{\bf 1}}
is a vacuum-like element and

\tilde{V}
decomposes into a direct sum of
two
V
-modules
V\cong W
and
U=\{v_{-1}\tilde{{\bf 1}}\mid v\in V\}
, which contradicts
the assumption. So, we have
0\not=u\in W\cong V

and we view
u
as an element of
V
.
Set
P=\{v\in V\mid\exists m\in{\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ s.t. }u(0)^{m}v=0\}
.
Since
u(0)^{m}(v_{k}w)=\sum\binom{m}{j}(u(0)^{m-j}v)_{k}(u(0)^{j}w)
,

P
is a subVA of
V
.
Furthermore, since
\omega(2)u\in V_{0}={\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}
and
{\bf 1}(-3)=0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}u(0)\omega=&u(0)\omega(-1){\bf 1}=-[\omega(-1),u(0)]{\bf 1}\cr=&-\{(\omega(0)u)(-1)-(\omega(1)u)(-2)+(\omega(2)u)(-3)+\!\cdots\}{\bf 1}\cr=&\{u(-2)-u(-2)+0\}{\bf 1}=0.\end{array}

Hence,
P
is a full subVOA of
V
.
Assume
P\not=V
and let
\rho
be a lowest weight homogeneous
element outside of
P
. Then

P\not\ni u(0)\rho=-\rho(0)u-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-L(-1))^{k}}{k!}\rho(k)u
by the assumption of
\rho
.
For
k\geq 1
, since
{\rm wt}(\rho(k)u)<{\rm wt}(\rho)
and
\omega\in P
,
we have
\rho(k)u\in P
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-L(-1))^{k}}{k!}\rho(k)u\in P
. Therefore, we have
\rho(0)u\not\in P
.
Viewing it in
V=W\subseteq\tilde{V}
,

\rho(0)u=\rho(0)\omega(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}=\omega(0)\rho(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}
.
However, since
\psi(\rho(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}})=0
and
\rho(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in V_{{\rm wt}(\rho)-1}
,
we have
\rho(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in P
. Since
P
is a full subVOA, we have

\rho(0)u=L(-1)\rho(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in P
,
which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
P=V
and
u(0)
acts on
V_{k}
nilpotently for
every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

\square

Since
V
is of CFT-type,
v(0)\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in W_{0}=V_{0}
for all
v\in V_{1}
.
Hence we have

v(0)u=v(0)L(-1)\tilde{{\bf 1}}\in L(-1){\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}=0

for all
v\in V_{1}
. In particular,
u\in Z(V_{1})
, where
Z(V_{1})
denotes the center of

V_{1}
. Therefore,
u,\phi(u)\in Z(V_{1})
.

Since
\langle u,\phi(u)\rangle>0
, we have
\langle u+\phi(u),u+\phi(u)\rangle>0
or

\langle u-\phi(u),u-\phi(u)\rangle>0
. We can choose

such that
\langle w,w\rangle=1
.
Since
[u(0),\phi(u)(0)]=0
,
w(0)
also acts on
V_{k}
nilpotently.

Let
{\rm VA}(w)
be a vertex subalgebra generated by

.
Then
\omega^{1}=\frac{w(-1)w}{2}
becomes a conformal element of
{\rm VA}(w)
.
By the relation

, we have

as VOAs,
where
M(1)
denotes a VOA of free-boson type with central charge
1
.

Proposition 2

Under the assumptions in Proposition 1,
if
V
is a unitary VOA,
then
V={\rm VA}(w)\otimes{\rm Com}({\rm VA}(w),V)
, where

{\rm Com}({\rm VA}(w),V)=\{v\in V\mid\alpha(n)v=0\mbox{ for all }\alpha\in{\rm VA}(w)\mbox{ and }n\geq 0\}
.
In particular,
V
is not holomorphic.

[Proof]
Set
Q=\{v\in V\mid w(0)v=0\}
.
As we explained in the proof of Proposition 1,
Q
is a subVOA and

\phi
-invariant, since
\phi(w)\in\pm w
.
Since
V
has a positive definite form
(,)_{V}
,
Q^{\perp}
is also
w(0)
-invariant .
Since
w(0)
acts on
(Q^{\perp})_{k}
nilpotently for

, if

, then
Q^{\perp}
contains a nonzero element

.
However, since
(,)_{V}
is positive definite and

,
we have a contradiction. Hence, we have

, that is,
w(0)
acts on

as zero.

Since
w(0)
acts on
V
as zero and

has an invariant bilinear form

\langle,\rangle
,
V
is a direct sum of simple
M(1)
-modules

isomorphic
to
M(1)
, that is, there is

with
\omega^{1}(1)b=0

such that

.
Since

,
we have

.
We next show that
\omega-\omega^{1}\in{\rm Com}({\rm VA}(w),V)
, where

\omega^{1}=\frac{w(-1)w}{2}
.
Then
w(0)\omega^{1}=w(2)\omega^{1}=0
and

.
On the other hand,

.
Since
\omega(2)w\in{\mathbb{C}}{\bf 1}
, there is

.
Then

.
Therefore,

\square

Proof of Theorem

We first prove the following result.

Proposition 3

If

is a simple holomorphic irrational VOA of CFT-type, then

V
-module
\tilde{V}
and its submodule
W
such that

\tilde{V}/W\cong V\cong W

[Proof]
We start the proof of Proposition

by the following case:

Lemma 2

Let
V=\coprod_{m=-t}^{\infty}V_{m}
be a simple holomorphic VOA of CFT-type and assume that

is semisimple,
then all
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable

[Proof]
We note that since
V
is a holomorphic VOA of CFT-type
and

is semisimple,

and
\bar{\omega}=0
, where
\bar{\omega}
is the image of
\omega
in
A(V)
.
Let
M=\coprod_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{(n)}
be an
{\mathbb{N}}
-gradable
V
-module.
Using the notation in
[
4
]
,

\Omega(M)
denotes the set of singular elements of a
V
-module

, that is,

\Omega(M)=\{w\in M\mid v_{m}w=0\mbox{ for all }m\geq{\rm wt}(v)\mbox{ and }v\in V\}
.
Since
A(V)
acts on
\Omega(M)
,

acts on
\Omega(M)
as zero.
Furthermore, since

is the lowest eigenvalue of

on a
V
-module,

\Omega(M)
coincides with the generalized eigen subspace of

in

with eigenvalue

.

Since

,

is a direct sum of copies of a simple

-module

and furthermore a submodule

of

is a direct sum of copies of

. We will show

.
If
M_{(1)}\not\subseteq V\!\cdot\!M_{(0)}
, then

.
Therefore

.
Since the eigenvalues of

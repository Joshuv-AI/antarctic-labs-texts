Homology self closeness number and cofibration sequence
Gopal Chandra Dutta

Abstract
. We study the homology self-closeness numbers of simply connected CW complex and those of the homotopy cofiber. Self-maps of spaces in cofibrations which appear in Homology decomposition are studied. We also consider Postnikov tower and studied the relation of homology self-closeness numbers between homology decomposition and homotopy decomposition.

1.
Introduction

For a pointed space
X
, the set of homotopy classes of base point preserving maps
X\to X
is denoted by
[X,X]
. This is a monoid under composition of maps. The subset of all invertible elements of this monoid is denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. It is the group of homotopy classes of self-equivalences on
X
. For notational convenience, we do not distinguish between a map
f\colon X\to X
and that of its homotopy class in
[X,X]
. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
has been studied by several authors (cf.
[
2
,
19
]
). Choi and Lee introduced the following sub-monoids in
[
7
]
:

\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k}(X):=\big\{f\in[X,X]:~f_{\#}\colon\pi_{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{i}(X),\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq k\big\}.

For connected CW complex
X
, we have the chain:

[X,X]=\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{0}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k}(X)\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k+1}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{\infty}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X).

They introduced a numeric homotopy invariant associated to the chain, called homotopy self-closeness number
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X)
defined as

N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X):=\min\big\{k\geq 0:~\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big\}.

Similar kind of monoids
\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X)
and
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{k}(X)
for homology and cohomology were introduced in
[
16
]
.

\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X):=\big\{f\in[X,X]:~f_{\ast}\colon H_{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{i}(X),\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq k\big\};

\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{k}(X):=\big\{f\in[X,X]:~f^{\ast}\colon H^{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{i}(X),\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq k\big\}.

We know that for a simply connected CW-complex, a self-map is a homotopy equivalence if and only if it is a homology equivalence (or a cohomology equivalence). Therefore, we have the two types of chain for simply connected CW complex

[X,X]=\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{0}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X)\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k+1}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{\infty}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X),

and

[X,X]=\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{0}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{k}(X)\supseteq\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{k+1}(X)\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{\infty}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X).

This motivates to define
homology and cohomology self-closeness numbers
(cf.
[
16
]
)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X):=\min\big\{k\geq 0:~\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big\},

N\mathcal{A}^{\ast}(X):=\min\big\{k\geq 0:~\mathcal{A}^{\ast}_{k}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big\}.

In
[
15
]
, Oda and Yamaguchi studied the homotopy self-closeness number of the following type of a
fibration sequence
:

(1)

\cdots\to K(G,n+1)\to X\to Y\xrightarrow{\gamma}K(G,n+2),

where
G
is an abelian group and
K(G,n)
is the Eilenberg-MacLane space of type
(G,n)
. Note that
X
is the homotopy fiber of the map
\gamma
.

In 2021, Li studied the cohomology self-closeness number of the following type of
cofibration sequence
:

(2)

A\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y\to\Sigma{A}.

He obtained the relations between cohomology self-closeness numbers of
X
and
Y
(cf.
[
12
]
).

Recall that for an abelian group
G
and integer
n\geq 2
, the Moore space
M(G,n)
of type
(G,n)
is the simply connected CW-complex
M(G,n)
unique upto homotopy, such that

\tilde{H}_{i}(M(G,n))=\begin{cases}G&\text{if}~i=n,\\
0&\text{if}~i\neq n.\end{cases}

If
G
is free-abelian, then
M(G,n)
is just the wedge of copies of
S^{n}
and
M({\mathbb{Z}}_{m},n)=S^{n}\cup_{m}e^{n+1},
where
(n+1)
-cell is attached to
S^{n}
by a degree
m
map. Thus, for a finitely generated
G
, Moore space
M(G,n)
is a finite CW-complex of dimension
n
if
G
is free-abelian and of dimension
n+1
if
G
has torsion.
Note that
\pi_{n}(X;G):=[M(G,n),X]
is a group for
n\geq 2
and abelian group for
n\geq 3
. It is called the
n
-th homotopy group of
X
with coefficients in
G
.

In this paper, we consider the dual
cofibration sequence
of Equation
1
:

(3)

M(G,n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y\to M(G,n+1)\to\cdots.

Here
n\geq 2
,
Y
is the homotopy cofiber of the map
\gamma
and
\iota
is a cofibration. This sequence is a particular case of the cofibration sequence
2
. The group
\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
was studied by Oka, Sawashita and Sugawara in
[
13
]
. The homotopy self-closeness number corresponding to the cofibration sequence
3
was studied in
[
14
]
by considering
G=\mathbb{Z}
.

In Section
2
, we establish relations between
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
and
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
. Indeed, we find
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
by utilizing the given map
\gamma
and making certain assumptions about
X
.
First, we prove the inequality
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
, when the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is an epimorphism (see Theorem
2.2
).

Moreover, the reverse inequality
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
holds if the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is a monomorphism and
\gamma^{\#}\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big)\subseteq\gamma_{\#}\big(\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\big)
(see Theorem
2.4
).

Further, we get the equality
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is an isomorphism (see Theorem
2.5
).

As a special case, assume that
\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}}\langle\operatorname{Id}_{M}\rangle
and
\pi_{n}(X,G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/{m\mathbb{Z}}\langle\gamma\rangle
, where
q,m\geq 0
. Then

(a)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y),~~\text{if}~q=0\neq m~\text{or}~q>m.

(b)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if
q=m
and
\operatorname{conn}(X)\geq 2~(\text{or}~G~\text{is free})
.

(see Proposition
2.6
).

By taking
G=\mathbb{Z}/{p\mathbb{Z}}
for a prime
p\neq 2
, we obtain the relation
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
under some conditions (see Proposition
2.11
).

In Section
3
, we consider the
homology decomposition

\{X_{n}\}
of a simply connected CW-complex
X
, where
X_{n}
is the
n
-th
homology section
(cf.
[
5
, Section 7.3]
). In Theorem
3.3
, we obtain the relations between homology self-closeness numbers of consecutive homology sections. Moreover, in Lemma
3.6
, we prove the relationship between the group of self-homotopy equivalences of
X
and
X_{n}
. Using this Lemma, we obtain the relation between the two numbers
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
and
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
under some conditions, see Theorem
3.9
.

In Section
4
, we consider the
homotopy decomposition (Postnikov tower)

\{X^{(n)}\}
of a simply connected CW-complex
X
, where
X^{(n)}
is the
n
-th
homotopy section
. The main aim of this section is to obtain relations between homology and homotopy self-closeness numbers and their corresponding decomposition. In Lemma
4.3
, we obtain a relation between the homology self-closeness numbers of homotopy section and homology section. Theorem
4.4
gives the analogous results of Theorem
3.3
for homotopy sections. Choi and Lee have studied the relation of homotopy self-closeness numbers of
X
and
X^{(n)}

[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
. We find the homological version of the result in Theorem
4.6
. We prove the equality
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
under some conditions in Corollary
4.7
. Finally, we compute homology self-closeness numbers of homotopy sections (Postnikov tower) for some CW-complexes by the help of homology decomposition.

2.
Homology self-closeness number

In this section, we study the relation between the homology self-closeness numbers of
X,Y
for the cofibration sequence of Equation
3
.
Recall that the
connectivity
of a space is defined as

\operatorname{conn}(X):=\min\big\{k\geq 0~:~\pi_{k+1}(X)\neq 0\big\}.

Thus,
\pi_{k}(X)=0
for all
k\leq\operatorname{conn}(X)
. Moreover, we denote the
homological dimension
by

H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X):=\max\big\{k\geq 0~:~H_{k}(X)\neq 0\big\}.

Therefore,
H_{k}(X)=0
for all
k>H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)
. For any simply connected CW-complex
X
, we have

2=\operatorname{conn}(X)+1\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X).

Recall that, a map
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is called
homotopical
n
-equivalence
if
f_{\#}\colon\pi_{k}(X)\to\pi_{k}(X^{\prime})
is isomorphism for all
k<n
and an epimorphism for
k=n
. A map
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is called
homological
n
-equivalence
if
f_{\ast}\colon H_{k}(X)\to H_{k}(X^{\prime})
is isomorphism for all
k<n
and an epimorphism for
k=n
.

If
X^{\prime}
is simply connected then the mapping cone
C_{f}
of a map
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is simply connected (cf
[
5
, Proposition B.4 ]
).

2.1 Lemma
.

Let
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
be a map between simply connected spaces. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

f
is homotopical
n
-equivalence.

(b)

f
is homological
n
-equivalence.

(c)

C_{f}
is
n
-connected.

Proof.

Follows from
[
22
, Theorem 4]
and
[
5
, Lemma 6.4.12]
.

∎

From now onwards,
X
will denote a simply connected CW-complex and
G
a finitely generated abelian group.

2.2 Theorem
.

M(G,n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y
be a cofibration sequence such that the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is an epimorphism. Then

(a)

2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq n+1
if
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
.

(b)

n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if
H_{n}(X)=H_{n+1}(X)=0
and
2\leq n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
.

Proof.

(a)

Note that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n.
Moreover,
H_{k}(Y)=0
for all
k\geq n+2
using the long exact sequence of homology groups of the cofibration sequence. Therefore,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq n+1
.
Assume that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)=m
and
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(X)
. Let
m<n
, otherwise we get the desired result.

Since
\gamma_{\#}
is epimorphism, so there exists
h\colon M(G,n)\to M(G,n)
such that
g\circ\gamma=\gamma\circ h
. Therefore, there is a map
f\in[Y,Y]
such that
f\circ\iota=\iota\circ g
. Hence, we get a homotopy commutative diagram

\textstyle{M(G,n)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{M(G,n)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\textstyle{Y}

Note that
\iota_{\ast}\colon H_{k}(X)\to H_{k}(Y)
is isomorphism for all
k\leq n-1
. Therefore,
f_{\ast}\colon H_{k}(Y)\to H_{k}(Y)
is isomorphism for all
k\leq m
. So
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(Y)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
. Hence, using the commutativity of the right side diagram, we have
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n-1}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Consequently,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq m=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
and we get the desired result.

(b)

Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)=m
and
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(X)
. As in the proof of part (a), we have the diagram
a
. If possible, assume that
m<n
. Therefore,
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(Y)=\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
. Observe the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{H_{n+1}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{\ast}}

Thus
h\in\operatorname{Aut}(M(G,n))
. Therefore, using the five Lemma on the long exact sequence of homology groups of the diagram

, we have
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. This contradicts the fact that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\geq n
. Hence,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)=m\geq n
.

∎

If
\gamma=0
, in that case
Y=X\vee M(G,n+1)
for
n\geq 2

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
and
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)

2.4 Theorem
.

M(G,n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y
be a cofibration sequence such that the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is a monomorphism and
\gamma^{\#}\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big)

\subseteq\gamma_{\#}\big(\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\big)
. Then

(a)

3\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq n-1
if
X
is
2
-connected,
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
and
G
has torsion.

(b)

2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq n-1
if
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
and
G
is free.

Proof.

(a)

Observe that
\iota\colon X\to Y
is homological
n
-equivalence. So homotopical
n
-equivalence by Lemma
2.1
. Since
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
, therefore using
[
10
, Proposition 4C.1]
there exits a CW-complex say
Z
which is homotopy equivalence to
X
and
\dim(Z)\leq n
. Thus the induced map
\iota_{\#}\colon[Z,X]\to[Z,Y]
is onto. Moreover, let
p\colon Z\to X
is a homotpy equivalence map with homotopy inverse
q\colon X\to Z
. Therefore,
p^{\#}\colon[X,X]\to[Z,X]
is a bijection map defined as
f\mapsto f\circ p
. Similarly,
q^{\#}\colon[Z,Y]\to[X,Y]
is bijection. Consequently, the induced map
\iota_{\#}\colon[X,X]\to[X,Y]
is surjective.

Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=l<n
and
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(Y)
. By surjectivity of
\iota_{\#}\colon[X,X]\to[X,Y]
implies that there exist some
g\in[X,X]
such that
\iota\circ g=f\circ\iota
. Note that
\operatorname{conn}(X)=2
implies that
\operatorname{conn}(Y)=2
. Therefore, the homotopical connectivity of the map
\gamma\colon M(G,n)\to X
is 2 by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore, using
[
18
, Proposition 4.4]
there exists a map
h\colon M(G,n)\to M(G,n)
such that we get the homotopy commutative diagram
a
.
Since
\iota_{\ast}\colon H_{k}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(Y)
for all
k\leq l
, therefore
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Let
\bar{g}\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
such that
g\circ\bar{g}=\bar{g}\circ g=\operatorname{Id}_{X}
. It is sufficient to show that
h\in\operatorname{Aut}(M(G,n))
.

From the assumption (ii),
\gamma^{\#}(\bar{g})\in\gamma_{\#}\big([M(G,n),M(G,n)]\big)
. Then there exits
\bar{h}\in[M(G,n),M(G,n)]
such that
\gamma_{\#}(\bar{h})=\gamma^{\#}(\bar{g})
. So
\gamma\circ\bar{h}=\bar{g}\circ\gamma
. Therefore, we have

\gamma\circ\bar{h}\circ h=\bar{g}\circ\gamma\circ h=\bar{g}\circ g\circ\gamma=\gamma,

and similarly,
\gamma\circ h\circ\bar{h}=\gamma
. Injectivity of
\gamma_{\#}
implies that
h\circ\bar{h}=\bar{h}\circ h=\operatorname{Id}_{M(G,n)}
. So
h\in\operatorname{Aut}\big(M(G,n)\big)
. Using five Lemma on the long exact sequence of homology groups of the Diagram
a
, we have
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
. Consequently,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq l=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X).

(b)

Note that
\operatorname{conn}(X)=1
implies that
\operatorname{conn}(Y)=1
. Moreover, if
G
is free abelian group, then
\dim(M(G,n))=n
. Therefore, we can use
[
18
, Proposition 4.4]
to obtain the diagram
a
. Rest of the proof follows similarly.

∎

2.5 Theorem
.

M(G,n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y
be a cofibration sequence such that the induced homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
is an isomorphism. Then

(a)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
if
X
is
2
-connected,
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
and
G
has torsion.

(b)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
if
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
and
G
is free.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.2
, we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
.

For the converse part, surjectivity of the map
\gamma_{\#}\colon\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\to\pi_{n}(X;G)
implies that

\gamma^{\#}\big(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\big)\subseteq\pi_{n}(X;G)=\gamma_{\#}\big(\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\big).

Therefore, using Theorem
2.4
, we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
. Hence, we get the desired result.

∎

2.6 Proposition
.

M(G,n)\xrightarrow{\gamma}X\xrightarrow{\iota}Y
be a cofibration sequence such that
H_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n
. Assume that there exist non-negative integers
q,m
such that

\pi_{n}(M(G,n);G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/{q\mathbb{Z}}\langle\operatorname{Id}_{M}\rangle,~~\pi_{n}(X,G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/{m\mathbb{Z}}\langle\gamma\rangle.

Then

(a)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if
q=0\neq m\text{ or }~q>m
.

(b)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if
q=m
,
X
is
2
-connected and
G
has torsion.

(c)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(Y)
if
q=m
and
G
is a free abelian group.

Proof.

(a)

Note that
\gamma_{\#}(f_{1}+f_{2})=\gamma\circ(f_{1}+f_{2})\cong\gamma\circ f_{1}+\gamma\circ f_{2}
. So
\gamma_{\#}
is a homomorphism. It is sufficient to show that
\gamma_{\#}
is surjective. Let
g\in\pi_{n}(X;G)
. Then there exists an integer
s
such that
g=s\gamma
. Observe that

g=s\gamma=\gamma\circ s\operatorname{Id}_{M}=\gamma_{\#}(s\operatorname{Id}_{M}).

Thus
\gamma_{\#}
is surjective. Therefore using Theorem
2.2
(a), we get the desired result.

(b)

Assume that
q=m
. As in the proof of part(a), we have surjectivity of the homomorphism
\gamma_{\#}
. Moreover,
\gamma_{\#}
is injective as
q=m
. Therefore, we get the equality using Theorem
2.5
(a).

(c)

Similar as part (b).

∎

2.7 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
\dim(X)\leq n-1
and
\pi_{n}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, where
n\geq 2
. If
\gamma\colon S^{n}\to X
is a generator of
\pi_{n}(X)
, then

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X\cup_{\gamma}e^{n+1}).

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\gamma_{\#}\colon[S^{n},S^{n}]\to[S^{n},X]
is a bijection. For surjectivity, let
g\in[S^{n},X].
Therefore there exists an integer
s
such that
g=s\gamma
. We know that
s\gamma=\gamma\circ s\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}}
, where
\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}}
is a generator of
\pi_{n}(S^{n})
. Thus
g=\gamma_{\#}(s\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}})
. Hence
\gamma_{\#}
is surjective.

For injectivity, let
f_{1},f_{2}\in[S^{n},S^{n}]
such that
\gamma_{\#}(f_{1})=\gamma_{\#}(f_{2})
. This implies
\gamma\circ f_{1}=\gamma\circ f_{2}
. Moreover observe that
f_{1}=k_{1}\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}},~~f_{2}=k_{2}\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}}
for some
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore
\gamma\circ k_{1}\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}}=\gamma\circ k_{2}\operatorname{Id}_{S^{n}}
. This implies
k_{1}\gamma=k_{2}\gamma
. So that
k_{1}=k_{2}
. Then using Theorem
2.5
we get the desired result.

∎

2.8 Remark
.

Corollary
2.7
can also be deduced directly from Proposition
2.6
.

Recall the generalised Fruedenthal suspension theorem: Let
Y
be an
n
-connected CW-complex and
X
is finite dimensional CW-complex. Then the suspension map
\Sigma\colon[X,Y]\to[\Sigma X,\Sigma Y]
is isomorphism if

3.
Homology decomposition

In this section, we consider homology decomposition of a space and its relation with
homology self-closeness numbers. First, we recall the definition.

3.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex. A
homology decomposition
of
X
consists of a sequence of simply connected CW-complexes
\{X_{n}\}
and structure maps

j_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X,\iota_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n+1},~k_{n+1}:M\big(H_{n+1}(X),n\big)\to X_{n},

that satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

j_{n\ast}\colon H_{k}(X_{n})\to H_{k}(X)
is an isomorphism for all
k\leq n
and
H_{k}(X_{n})=0
for all
k>n
.

(ii)

M\big(H_{n+1}(X),n\big)\xrightarrow{k_{n+1}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{\iota_{n}}X_{n+1}
is a cofibration sequence of the cellular map
k_{n+1}
such that the induced map
k_{n+1\ast}\colon H_{n}(M(H_{n+1}(X),n))\to H_{n}(X_{n})
is trivial.

(iii)

The following diagram commutes.

\textstyle{X_{n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{n+1}}
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{X_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\iota_{n}}

The collection
\big\{X_{n},j_{n},\iota_{n},k_{n}\big\}
is called a homology decomposition of
X
. The spaces
X_{n}
are called the

-th homology sections of

and the maps

are called homological

-invariants. It is well known that every simply connected CW-complex admits a homology decomposition (see

-invariants.

3.2 Remark
.

It follows from the definition that

g^{\prime}_{n}
. Consider a map
G\colon X\times\partial{I}\cup X_{n}\times I\to X
defined as

G(x,t)=\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(x)&\forall(x,t)\in X\times\{0\},\\
g^{\prime}(x)&\forall(x,t)\in X\times\{1\},\\
F(x,t)&\forall(x,t)\in X_{n}\times I.\end{cases}

Note that
H^{k+1}\big(X\times I,X\times\partial{I}\cup X_{n}\times I;\pi_{k}(X)\big)=0
for all
k\geq n+1
. Therefore there exists an extension map
\widetilde{G}\colon X\times I\to X
of
G
by obstruction theory. Hence
\widetilde{G}
be the homotopy between
f^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}
. Thus
f\simeq g
, so
\phi
is injective.
Consequently for
n\geq m+1
we get the bijective map

\phi\colon[X,X]\to[X_{n},X_{n}].

Moreover Observe that
\phi(f\circ g)=\phi(f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime})=(f^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime})_{n}=f^{\prime}_{n}\circ g^{\prime}_{n}=\phi(f)\circ\phi(g).

Hence

\phi\colon\operatorname{Aut}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n}).

∎

3.7 Remark
.

For a CW-decomposition of X, a self-homotopy equivalence f: X → X does not necessarily induce a self-homotopy equivalence on the skeleton
[
20
, Remark]
.
Further, if the CW-decomposition of
X
comes from the homology decomposition (when all the homology groups of X are free abelian), then the induced map of f on each skeleton gives a self-homotopy equivalence.

Recall the theorem of Serre (
[
21
]
): For a simply connected CW-complex
X
, the homology group
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for all
k
if and only if
\pi_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for all
k
.

3.8 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a simply connected
CW
complex such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X_{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq n
.

Proof.

Let
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for each
k
. Therefore from the definition of homology decomposition we have
H_{k}(X_{n})
is finitely generated abelian group for each
k
. Hence we get the desired result using
[
16
, Corollary 42]
.

∎

The following Theorem deduce the relation between homology self-closeness number of
X
with its homology section.

3.9 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)=m
. Then following holds:

(a)

2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq m+1
.

(b)

2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq m
, if the group
\pi_{m}(X)
is finitely generated.

(c)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
, if
n\geq m+1
and
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated free abelian group for each
k
.

(d)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
, if
n\geq m+1
and
H_{k}(X)
is finite generated for each
k
.

(e)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq m
for
n\leq m
.

(f)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
, if
\dim(X)\leq n<\infty
.

Proof.

(a)

Since
X
is a simply connected space, thus
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\geq 2
. Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m+1}(X)
then we have
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{m}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Hence
2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq m+1
.

(b)

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(X)
. Then
f_{\ast}\colon H_{k}(X)\to H_{k}(X)
is isomorphism for all
k\leq m
. Thus,
f_{\#}\colon\pi_{k}(X)\to\pi_{k}(X)
is isomorphism for all
k\leq m-1
and
f_{\#}\colon\pi_{m}(X)\to\pi_{m}(X)
is surjective. Since
\pi_{m}(X)
is finitely generated therefore
f_{\#}\colon\pi_{m}(X)\to\pi_{m}(X)
is also an isomorphism. So,
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{m}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
and we get the desired result.

(c)

Note that
\pi_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for all
k
as
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for each
k
by Serre. Assume that
n\geq m+1
. Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=l\leq m
and
g\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(X_{n})
. Then
\phi^{-1}(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
from Lemma
3.6
. Thus
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n})
. So
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq l=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
. If possible, assume that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})<l
. Let
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l-1}(X)
then
\phi(h)\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l-1}(X_{n})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n})
. Therefore,
h\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, which contradict the fact that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=l
. Therefore, for
n\geq m+1
, we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X).

(d)

Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=r
and
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{r}(X_{n})
. Since
n\geq m+1
, as in the proof of Lemma
3.6
, we see that there exists a map
\bar{h}\colon X\to X
such that the following diagram is homotopy commutative.

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\bar{h}}
\textstyle{X}
\textstyle{X_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j_{n}}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces X_{n}}
\scriptstyle{j_{n}}

Note that the map
j_{n\ast}\colon H_{i}(X_{n})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{i}(X)
for all
i\leq n
and
r\leq m<n
. Thus,
\bar{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{r}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X).
Therefore using the commutativity of the diagram we have
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{n}(X_{n})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n})
. Hence
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq r=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X).

(e)

Observe that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})\leq n
. Therefore we get the desired result.

(f)

Let
\dim(X)<\infty
and
n\geq\dim(X)
. Thus
H^{n+1}\big(X_{n},H_{n+1}(X)\big)=0
. Therefore, for any map
f\colon X\to X
there exists a map
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n}
such that we have a diagram as of Equation
d
, which is homotopy commutative
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=k
and
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k}(X)
. Using the commutativity of the diagram, we have
h_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{k}(X_{n})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n})
. Further, using the diagram we get
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{n}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
since
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq\dim(X).
Hence,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq k=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n}).

∎

3.10 Example
.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}.
Then
\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)=2
. Note that

H_{k}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if}~k\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{if}~k\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

From Theorem
3.9
, we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
for all
n\geq 3
, where
X_{n}
is the
n
-th homology decomposition of
X
. Observe that
X_{2n+1}=X_{2n}=\mathbb{C}P^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Therefore,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{3})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{2})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C}P^{1})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(S^{2})=2.

Moreover,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C}P^{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=2
for all
n\geq 2
by Theorem
3.9
.
Consequently,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(\mathbb{C}P^{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{2n})=2,~~\text{for}~n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

3.11 Remark
.

Consider
X=\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
. Then
X_{2n+1}=X_{2n}=\mathbb{C}P^{n}
for

. Therefore,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~~n<2,\\
2&\text{if}~~n\geq 2.\end{cases}

3.12 Example
.

Consider
X=K(G,n)
for
n\geq 2
. Note that the Hurewicz map
h_{\ast}\colon\pi_{n+1}(X)\to H_{n+1}(X)
is epimorphism, thus
H_{n+1}(X)=0
. For homology sections
\{X_{m}\}
, we have

X_{m}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{if}~~m<n,\\
M(G,n)&\text{if}~~m=n,n+1.\end{cases}

By definition, the map
j_{n+2}\colon X_{n+2}\to X
is a homologial
(n+2)
-equivalence, and hence a homotopical
(n+2)
-equivalence (cf. Lemma
2.1
). So
j_{n+2\#}\colon\pi_{k}(X_{n+2})\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{k}(X)
is an isomorphism for all
k\leq n+1
. Hence,
X_{n+2}
is
(n-1)
-connected CW-complex. Therefore,

\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{n-1}(X_{n+2})=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{\#}^{1}(X_{n+2})=[X_{n+2},X_{n+2}].

Thus,
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X_{n+2})\geq n
. Using Lemma
3.8
, we get
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n+2})=N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X_{n+2})\geq n
.

Moreover, from Theorem
3.9
(d), we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n+2})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X)=n
. Hence,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n+2})=n
. Similarly, we can prove that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{m})=n
for

. Consequently,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{m})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~~m<n,\\

4.
Homotopy decomposition

In this section, we consider homotopy decomposition (Postnikov tower) of a space
X
which was studied in previous (cf.
[
6
]
). This can be thought as a dual construction to homology decomposition. Here, we prove some results related to homotopy and homology decomposition and their intermediate relations. These results help us to compute homology self-closeness numbers of Postnikov tower, some of them can be thought as generalization of those results in
[
8
]
. First, recall the definition

4.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex. A homotopy decomposition (Postnikov decomposition) of
X
consists of a sequence of simply connected CW-complexes
\{X^{(n)}\}
and maps
g_{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
such that

(i)

Each inclusion map
g_{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
induces isomorphisms
g_{n\#}\colon\pi_{k}(X)\to\pi_{k}(X^{(n)})
for all
k\leq n
and
\pi_{k}(X^{(n)})=0
for
k>n
.

(ii)

There exist maps
p_{n+1}\colon X^{(n+1)}\to X^{(n)}
such that

K\big(\pi_{n+1}(X),n+1\big)\to X^{(n+1)}\xrightarrow{p_{n+1}}X^{(n)}

is a fiber sequence.

(iii)

The following diagram is commutative

\textstyle{X^{(n+1)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{n+1}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n+1}}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}}
\textstyle{X^{(n)}}

(iv)

The fibration sequence in (ii) is equivalent to the principal fibration

K\big(\pi_{n+1}(X),n+1\big)\to X^{(n+1)}\to X^{(n)}\xrightarrow{k^{n+1}}K\big(\pi_{n+1}(X),n+2\big),

determined by a map
k^{n+1}
, where
[k^{n+1}]\in H^{n+2}\big(X^{(n)};\pi_{n+1}(X)\big)
.

We write this as
\{X^{(n)},g_{n},p_{n},k^{n}\}
and call it a homotopy decomposition (Postnikov decomposition) of
X
. The maps
k^{n+1}\colon X^{(n)}\to K\big(\pi_{n+1}(X),n+2\big)
are called
k
-invariants. The space
X^{(n)}
is called the
n
-th homotopy section and is obtained as the homotopy fiber of
k^{n}.

4.2 Remark
.

From the definition, we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{(n)})\leq n.

The following Lemma establish a relation between the homology self-closeness numbers of homotopy and homology sections.

4.3 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated abelian group for each
k
. Then the following results hold.

(i)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{(n)})\leq n.

(ii)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n}),
if
H_{n}(X)
is free-abelian group.

Proof.

(i)

From the definition of homotopy decomposition, we have
\pi_{k}(X^{(n)})
is finitely generated for all
k
. Therefore using
[
16
, Corollary 42]
, we get the desired result.

(ii)

Let
j_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X
and
g_{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
be two maps over the homology decomposition and homotopy decomposition of
X
respectively. Observe that
g_{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
is homotopical
(n+1)
-equivalence. From Lemma
2.1
, we say that
g_{n}
is homological
(n+1)
-equivalence. Therefore,
(g_{n}\circ j_{n})_{\ast}\colon H_{i}(X_{n})\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{i}(X^{(n)})
for all
i\leq n
. Moreover, for any
h\colon X^{(n)}\to X^{(n)}
there exists a
\bar{h}\colon X_{n}\to X_{n}
such that the following diagram is homotopy commutative by Compression Lemma.

\textstyle{X^{(n)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{X^{(n)}}
\textstyle{X_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}\circ j_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\bar{h}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces X_{n}}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}\circ j_{n}}

Let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=k
and
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X^{(n)})
. Thus,
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X_{n})=\operatorname{Aut}(X_{n})
. Therefore,
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n}(X^{(n)})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(n)}).
Hence,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n}).

∎

Next we deduce relations between the homology self-closeness numbers of two consecutive homotopy sections in Postnikov tower.

4.4 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated for all
k
. For homotopy decomposition
\{X^{(n)},g_{n},p_{n},k^{n}\}
of
X
, we have the following:

(a)

If the induced map

k^{m+1\#}\colon\big[K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big),K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big)\big]\to\big[X^{(m)},K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big)\big]

is surjective for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m+1)})
.

(b)

If the induced map

k^{m+1\#}\colon\big[K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big),K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big)\big]\to\big[X^{(m)},K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big)\big]

is injective such that

k^{m+1}_{\#}(\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(m)}))\subseteq k^{m+1\#}\big(\big[K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big),K\big(\pi_{m+1}(X),m+2\big)\big]\big),

then
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m+1)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m)})
.

(c)

If the induced map
k^{m+1\#}
is bijective, then
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(m+1)})
.

Proof.

Note that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{n})
for homotopy sections
\{X^{n}\}
by Lemma
4.3
. Rest of the proof directly follows from
[
15
, Theorem 3.3 & Theorem 3.9]
.

∎

4.5 Proposition
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated for all
k
. Then either
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n+1)})=n+1
or
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n+1)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})
.

Proof.

Observe that the map
p^{\#}_{n+1}\colon[X^{(n)},X^{(n)}]\to[X^{(n+1)},X^{(n)}]
is surjective, see
[
5
, Proposition 8.2.2]
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we have either
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{(n+1)})=n+1
or
N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{(n+1)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(X^{(n)})
. Moreover, using Lemma
4.3
, we get the desired result.

∎

The following Theorem is a homological version of
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
.

4.6 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex of dimension
m
such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated for each
k
. Then

(a)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
, if the conditions hold
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq n
and
m-1\leq n
.

(b)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})
, if the condition holds
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq n<m-1
.

(c)

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})<N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
, if the condition holds
n<N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
.

Proof.

(a)

We want to prove the inequality first
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
. Assume that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=k
. Consider the case
k<n
, otherwise we get the inequality trivially. Given any map
\phi\colon X^{(n)}\to X^{(n)}
there exists a map
\bar{\phi}\colon X\to X
such that the following diagram is homotopy commutative by Compression Lemma
[
10
, Lemma 4.6]
.

\textstyle{X^{(n)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{X^{(n)}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\bar{\phi}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{g_{n}}

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X^{(n)})
. Then there exists a
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X)
by Compression Lemma and the fact that
g_{n\ast}\colon H_{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{i}(X^{(n)})
for all
i\leq n
.
Since
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{k}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, therefore using the commutativity of the diagram, we have
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n}(X^{(n)})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(n)}).
Hence
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X).

Conversely, let
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=l
and
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(X)
. Then, there exists
h^{n}\colon X^{(n)}\to X^{(n)}
which makes the above Diagram
a
homotopy commutative (cf.
[
11
]
). Thus,
h^{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{l}(X^{(n)})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(n)}).
Therefore,
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n}(X)=\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Hence
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})
and we get the desired result.

(b)

Follows from the converse part of (a).

(c)

Observe that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})\leq n
. Thus, we get the desired result.

∎

4.7 Corollary
.

Let
X
be a simply connected CW-complex such that
H_{k}(X)
is finitely generated for all
k
. Assume that any one of the following conditions hold:

(a)

\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)<n<\infty
and
H_{k}(X)
is free for each
k
.

(b)

\dim(X)\leq n<\infty
.

Then,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
.

Proof.

(a)

Observe that the structure map of the Postnikov tower
g_{n}\colon X\to X^{(n)}
is a homotopy equivalence if
n\geq\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)
. Thus for
n\geq\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)
, we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})
. Therefore from Theorem
3.9
we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})
for
n\geq\pi_{\ast}\text{-}\dim(X)+1
.

(b)

Note that for
n\geq\dim(X)
, we have
X_{n}=X
. Hence using Theorem
4.6
, we get the desired result.

∎

4.8 Example
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, consider
X=\mathbb{C}P^{n}
. From Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})~\text{ for all }k.

For
k\geq 2
, we know that
X^{(k)}
are simply connected, thus
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\geq 2
. Therefore,

2\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=2~\text{ for }k\geq 2.

Hence,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<2,\\
2&\text{if}~k\geq 2.\end{cases}

4.9 Example
.

Let
G
and
H
be finitely generated abelian groups. Consider
X=M(G,n)\bigvee M(H,m)
, where
m>n>1
. Observe that

X^{(k)}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{if}~k<n,\\
K(G,n)&\text{if}~k=n.\end{cases}

Moreover,
H_{k}(X)=0
for all
n<k<m
. Therefore,

X_{k}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{if}~k<n,\\
M(G,n)&\text{if}~n\leq k<m,\\
X&\text{if}~k=m.\end{cases}

Note that, for
k\geq n
, we have
n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})
since
X^{(k)}
is
n-1
connected. From Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})
for
n<k<m
. Thus,

n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(M(G,n))=n,~~\text{for}~n<k<m.

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n}(X)=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m-1}(X)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(X)=\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m}(X)\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{m-1}(X)
, see Example
3.5
. Therefore,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=m

From Theorem
4.6
(a), we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
for all
k\geq m
. Hence,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=m
for
k\geq m
.
Consequently,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<n,\\
n&\text{if}~n\leq k<m,\\
m&\text{if}~k\geq m.\par\end{cases}

In general we have the following result.

4.10 Proposition
.

Let
G_{1},\cdots,G_{m}
be finitely generated abelian groups. Consider
X=\displaystyle\bigvee^{m}_{i=1}M(G_{i},n_{i})
, where
n_{i+1}>n_{i}>1
. Then

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<n_{1},\\
n_{i}&\text{if}~n_{i}\leq k<n_{i+1},\\
n_{m}&\text{if}~k\geq n_{m}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{i+1}}(X)\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{i}}(X)
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
, see Example
3.5
. We have already proved for the case
m=2
. It is sufficient to prove the result for the case
m=3
and then, we can say that the result holds inductively. Let
X=\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{3}M(G_{i},n_{i}).
Observe that

X^{(k)}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{ if }k<n_{1},\\
K(G_{1},n_{1})&\text{ if }k=n_{1}.\end{cases}

Further,

X_{k}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{ if }k<n_{1},\\
\bigvee\limits_{j=1}^{i}M(G_{j},n_{j})&\text{ if }n_{i}\leq k<n_{i+1},\\
X&\text{ if }k=n_{m}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
X^{(k)}
is
(n_{1}-1)
connected for
k\geq n_{1}
. Thus,
n_{1}\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})
for
k\geq n_{1}
. From Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

n_{1}\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n_{1}})=n_{1},~\text{for}~n_{1}<k<n_{2}.

Using Proposition
4.5
, we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n_{2})})=n_{2}~\text{or}~N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n_{2})})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n_{2}-1)})=n_{1}.

If possible, assume that
\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{1}}(X^{(n_{2})})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(n_{2})})
. Let
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{1}}(X)
but
f\notin\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{2}}(X)
. By a functorial construction of Postnikov tower, there exist
f^{(n_{2})}
which makes the following diagram homotopy commutative ( cf.
[
5
, Proposition 7.2.11]
).

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{n_{2}}}
\textstyle{X^{(n_{2})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists~f^{(n_{2})}}
\textstyle{X^{(n_{2})}}

Thus,
f^{(n_{2})}\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{1}}(X^{(n_{2})})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(n_{2})})
. This contradicts the fact that
f\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{2}}(X)
. Hence
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(n_{2})})=n_{2}
. Further, using Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n_{2}})=n_{2},~\text{for}~n_{2}<k<n_{3}.

If possible, assume that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})<n_{2}
for some
k\in(n_{2},n_{3})
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{1}}(X^{(k)})=\cdots=\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{2}-1}(X^{(k)})=\operatorname{Aut}(X^{(k)})~(\text{since}~H_{j}(X^{(k)})\cong H_{j}(X)~\forall j\leq k).

Let
h\in\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{1}}(X)
but
h\notin\mathcal{A}_{\ast}^{n_{2}}(X)
. Then by the similar argument as above, we arrive at contradiction. Hence
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=n_{2}
for
n_{2}<k<n_{3}
.

From Theorem
4.6
(a), we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=n_{3}
for all
k\geq n_{3}
. Consequently

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<n_{1},\\
n_{1}&\text{if}~n_{1}\leq k<n_{2},\\
n_{2}&\text{if}~n_{2}\leq k<n_{3},\\
n_{3}&\text{if}~k\geq n_{3}.\end{cases}

∎

4.11 Example
.

Consider
X=S^{m}\times S^{n}
, where
m>n>1
. Observe that

X^{(k)}=\begin{cases}\ast&\text{if}~k<n,\\
K(\mathbb{Z},n)&\text{if}~k=n.\end{cases}

Therefore
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=0
for all
k<n
. Note that
X^{(k)}
is
n-1
connected for
k\geq n
. So
n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})
for all
k\geq n
. From Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

n\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(S^{n})=n~\text{for all}~n\leq k<m.

Observe that
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(S^{m}\times S^{n})=N\mathcal{A}_{\#}(S^{m}\times S^{n})=m
(cf.
[
14
, Proposition 5]
).

Moreover, using Theorem
4.6
(a), we have
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X)
for all
k\geq m+n-1
. Therefore,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=m~\text{for all}~k\geq m+n-1.

Further, for
m\leq k<m+n-1
, we have
m\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})
from Theorem
4.6
(b) and using Lemma
4.3
(ii), we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})\leq N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{k})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X_{m})=N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(S^{m}\vee S^{n})=m.

Hence,
N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=m
for all
m\leq k<m+n-1
. Consequently,

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<n,\\
n&\text{if}~n\leq k<m,\\
m&\text{if}~k\geq m.\end{cases}

4.12 Example
.

For
l>0
, consider
X=\Sigma^{l}(S^{m}\times S^{n})
, where
m>n>1
. Note that

\Sigma(S^{m}\times S^{n})\simeq S^{n+1}\bigvee S^{m+1}\bigvee S^{m+n+1}~~\text{(cf. \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{AHAT}{}{}, Proposition 4I.1]})}.

Therefore,
X=S^{n+l}\bigvee S^{m+l}\bigvee S^{m+n+l}
. From Proposition
4.10
, we have

N\mathcal{A}_{\ast}(X^{(k)})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}~k<n+l,\\
n+l&\text{if}~n+l\leq k<m+l,\\
m+l&\text{if}~m+l\leq k<m+n+l,\\
m+n+l&\text{if}~k\geq m+n+l.\end{cases}

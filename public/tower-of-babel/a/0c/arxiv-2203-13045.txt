On recursion operators for symmetries of the Pavlov-Mikhalev equation Thanks: The work was partially supported by the Russian Science Foundation Grant 21-71-20034.
I.S. Krasil’shchik

Abstract
. In geometry of nonlinear partial differential equations, recursion operators that act on symmetries of an equation ℰ \mathscr{E} are understood as Bäcklund auto-transformations of the equation 𝒯 ​ ℰ \mathscr{TE} tangent to ℰ \mathscr{E} . We apply this approach to a natural two-component extension of the 3D Pavlov-Mikhalev equation u y ​ y = u t ​ x + u y ​ u x ​ x − u x ​ u x ​ y . u_{yy}=u_{tx}+u_{y}u_{xx}-u_{x}u_{xy}. We describe the Lie algebra of symmetries for this extension, construct two recursion operators (one of them was known earlier) and find their action. We also establish the hereditary property of these operators as well as their compatibility (in the sense of the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket). We find also twelve additional operators which are degenerate in a sense (we call them queer ) and discuss their properties. In the concluding part, a geometrical background of two-component conservation laws for multi-dimensional equations is exposed together with its relations to differential coverings.

In a recent paper

approach to nonlinear PDEs (see

Pavlov-Mikhalev equation

u_{yy}=u_{tx}+u_{y}u_{xx}-u_{x}u_{xy},

(1)

see
[
15
,
16
]
, as well as
[
5
]
. Equation (

)
belongs to the same class as the rdDym one: it is linearly

]
and admits a Lax pair with non-removable
parameter
[

,
15
,
16
]
. A recursion operator for symmetries
of (

To be more precise, we deal with the system
\mathscr{E}

\begin{array}[]{l}v_{yy}=v_{xt}-u_{x}v_{xy}-2v_{y}u_{xx}+2v_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}v_{xx},\\[3.0pt]
u_{yy}=u_{xt}-u_{x}u_{xy}+u_{y}u_{xx},\end{array}

(2)

where the first equation is obtained by applying the adjoint linearization
of (
1
) to a new variable
v
. The system may be considered as a

Lagrangian deformation
of the initial equation,
see
[
1
]
, and coincides with cotangent equation of
the latter (see details in
[
8
,
11
]
).

The algorithm of construction and analysis of recursion operators that we use
below is based on the interpretation of these operators as Bäcklund
auto-transformations of the
tangent equation
,
see
[
13
]
, and essentially uses the theory of differential

[
12
]
. It should be noted that the tangent equation
as an efficient instrument the theory of recursion operators was intensively

[
9
]
.

The algorithm itself can be shortly described as follows
1
1

1

The exact
meaning and details will be explained in
Section
1
:

•

Consider the infinite prolongation of the equation under study as a
submanifold
\mathscr{E}\subset J^{\infty}(\pi)
in an appropriate jet space.

•

Choose convenient internal coordinates in
\mathscr{E}
for particular
computations, cf.
[
14
]
.

•

Compute symmetries of
\mathscr{E}
solving the
equation
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}(\varphi)=0
, where
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}
is the
linearization operator of
\mathscr{E}
.

•

Construct the tangent equation
\mathscr{TE}
by adding the
equations
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}(q)=0
to the initial ones. Here
q
is a new

odd
(and this is essential) dependent variable.

•

Find differential coverings over
\mathscr{TE}
linear in
q
and its
derivatives. Usually (but not necessary) these coverings are associated with
two-component conservation laws of
\mathscr{TE}
. Let
w
denote the
corresponding nonlocal variables.

•

Find nonlocal shadows of symmetries linear both in
q
and
w
. A pair
(covering, shadow) provides a Bäcklund auto-transformation

•

Study the action of the operator on symmetries.

•

For hereditary and compatibility properties, find nonlocal symmetries
that correspond to the shadows, if the former exist. Their super-commutators
are exactly the needed Frölicher-Nijenhuis brackets.

All these steps are accomplished in

–
5

Some of our computational results are rather voluminous and to make the reading

Basic constructions and notation

In our exposition here we follow the books

,

]

1.1.
Jets

Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}=E\to\mathbb{R}^{n}=M
be the
trivial bundle with the coordinates
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(independent variables) and
u=(u^{1},\dots,u^{m})
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(“unknown
functions”). The space of
k
-jets
J^{k}(\pi)
is, in addition to
x
and
u
,
endowed with the coordinates
u_{\sigma}^{j}
, where
\sigma
is a symmetric
multi-index of length
\leq k
. Variables
u_{\sigma}^{j}
correspond to the
partial derivatives
\partial u^{j}/\partial x^{\sigma}
. Natural projections

\pi_{k}\colon J^{k}(\pi)\to M,\qquad\pi_{k,l}\colon J^{k}(\pi)\to J^{l}(\pi),\quad k\geq l,

are defined, and their inverse limit
J^{\infty}(\pi)
is called the space of
infinite jets. These projections define also the bundles

\pi_{\infty}\colon J^{\infty}(\pi)\to M,\qquad\pi_{\infty,k}\colon J^{\infty}(\pi)\to J^{k}(\pi)

and the embeddings

\mathscr{F}_{l}(\pi)\subset\mathscr{F}_{k}(\pi),\qquad\Lambda_{l}^{i}(\pi)\subset\Lambda_{k}^{i}(\varphi),

where
\mathscr{F}_{l}(\pi)=C^{\infty}(J^{l}(\pi))
,

\Lambda_{l}^{i}(\pi)=\Lambda^{i}(J^{l}(\pi))
are the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra of
smooth functions and the
\mathscr{F}_{l}(\pi)
-module of differential
i
-forms
on
J^{l}(\pi)
, respectively. The corresponding objects on
J^{\infty}(\pi)
are
defined by

\mathscr{F}(\pi)=\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\mathscr{F}_{l}(\pi),\qquad\Lambda^{i}(\pi)=\bigcup_{l\geq 0}\Lambda_{l}^{i}(\pi).

A vector field on
J^{\infty}(\pi)
is by definition an
\mathbb{R}
-linear
derivation
X\colon\mathscr{F}(\pi)\to\mathscr{F}(\pi)
. The module of
vector fields is denoted by
D(\pi)
.

Let
f\in\Gamma(\pi)
be a (local) section of
\pi
. Then the
section
j_{\infty}(f)\in\Gamma(\pi_{\infty})
defined by

u_{\sigma}^{j}=\frac{\partial^{\sigma}f^{j}}{\partial x^{\sigma}},\qquad j=1,\dots,m,\quad\left|\sigma\right|\geq 0,

is called the infinite jet of
f
. Graphs of infinite jets passing through a
given point
\theta\in J^{\infty}(\pi)
are tangent to each other and their
common
n
-dimensional
\pi_{\infty}
-horizontal tangent plane is denoted
by
\mathscr{C}_{\theta}
. The distribution
\mathscr{C}\colon\theta\mapsto\mathscr{C}_{\theta}
is called the Cartan distribution. It is spanned by the
vector fields

D_{x^{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}+\sum_{j,\sigma}u_{\sigma i}^{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}\in D(\pi),

or dually, annihilates
1
-forms

\omega_{\sigma}^{j}=du_{\sigma}^{j}-\sum_{i}u_{\sigma i}^{j}\,dx^{i}\in\Lambda^{1}(\pi).

The field
D_{x^{i}}
is called the total derivative with respect to
x^{i}
,
while
\omega_{\sigma}^{j}
are Cartan (or vertical) forms on
J^{\infty}(\pi)
. The
Cartan distribution is Frobenius integrable, i.e.,
[X,Y]\in\mathscr{C}

whenever
X
,
Y\in\mathscr{C}
.

Finally, one has two natural splittings

D(\pi)=\mathscr{C}\oplus D^{v}(\pi),\qquad\Lambda^{1}(\pi)=\Lambda_{v}^{1}(\pi)\oplus\Lambda_{h}^{1}(\pi),

where
D^{v}(\pi)
is the submodule of
\pi_{\infty}
-vertical fields and

\Lambda_{h}^{1}(\pi)=\{\,\sum_{i}a_{i}\,dx^{i}\mid a_{i}\in\mathscr{F}(\pi)\,\},\quad\Lambda_{v}^{1}(\pi)=\{\,\sum_{j,\sigma}b_{\sigma}^{j}\omega_{\sigma}^{j}\mid b_{\sigma}^{j}\in\mathscr{F}(\pi)\,\}

consist of horizontal and Cartan forms, respectively. Consequently,

\Lambda^{i}(\pi)=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\Lambda_{h}^{q}(\pi)\wedge\Lambda_{v}^{p}(\pi),

where

\Lambda_{h}^{q}(\pi)=\underbrace{\Lambda_{h}^{1}(\pi)\wedge\dots\wedge\Lambda_{h}^{1}(\pi)}_{q\text{ times}},\qquad\Lambda_{v}^{p}(\pi)=\underbrace{\Lambda_{v}^{1}(\pi)\wedge\dots\wedge\Lambda_{v}^{1}(\pi)}_{q\text{ times}}

(3)

with the corresponding splitting of the de Rham differential in the horizontal
and vertical (Cartan) parts:

d_{h}=\sum_{i}dx^{i}\wedge D_{x^{i}},\qquad d_{v}=\sum_{j,\sigma}\omega_{\sigma}^{j}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}.

(4)

1.2.
Equations

Consider an
\mathscr{F}(\pi)
-module
P
of rank
r
and its element

F=(F^{1},\dots,F^{r})
. An (infinitely prolonged) partial differential equation
associated with
F
is

\mathscr{E}=\{\,\theta\in J^{\infty}(\pi)\mid\left.D_{\sigma}(F^{j})\right|_{\theta}=0,\quad j=1,\dots,r,\ \left|\sigma\right|\geq 0\,\},

(5)

where
D_{\sigma}
denotes the composition of total derivatives corresponding to
the multi-index
\sigma
. The restriction
\left.\pi_{\infty}\right|_{\mathscr{E}}

will be also denoted by
\pi_{\infty}
. Solutions of
\mathscr{E}
are sections
of
\pi
such that the graphs of their infinite jets lie
in
\mathscr{E}
. Functions and forms on
\mathscr{E}
are by definition

\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})=\left.\mathscr{F}(\pi)\right|_{\mathscr{E}},\qquad\Lambda^{i}(\mathscr{E})=\left.\Lambda^{i}(\pi)\right|_{\mathscr{E}},

respectively. Vector fields on
\mathscr{E}
are derivations

X\colon\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})
; the

\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})
-module of these fields is denoted
by
D(\mathscr{E})
.

The Cartan plane at
\theta\in\mathscr{E}
is defined as

\mathscr{C}_{\theta}\cap T_{\theta}\mathscr{E}\subset T_{\theta}J^{\infty}(\pi)
. In
this way, we obtain an integrable distribution almost everywhere
on
\mathscr{E}
. We also have splittings similar to (
3
), as well as
the vertical and Cartan differentials defined exactly like in (
4
).

It follows from (
5
) that the total derivatives can be restricted
to
\mathscr{E}
. These restrictions will be also denoted by
D_{x^{i}}
and we
shall always assume that the only solutions of the system
D_{x^{i}}(f)=0
,
i=1,\dots,n
, are constants. Such equations are called differentially
connected.

1.3.
Symmetries

Everywhere below the word “symmetry” means an infinitesimal symmetry.

A symmetry of the Cartan distribution on
J^{\infty}(\pi)
is a a vector field

X\in D^{v}(\pi)
such that
[X,\mathscr{C}]\subset\mathscr{C}
. The set of
symmetries is a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{R}
denoted by
\sym(\pi)
.

Theorem 1
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\sym(\pi)
and the module

\varkappa=\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\pi))
,
where
\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\pi)

denotes the pull-back. This correspondence is given by the formula

\varkappa\ni\varphi\mapsto\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}=\sum_{j,\sigma}D_{\sigma}(\varphi^{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{j}}\in D^{v}(\pi).

The field
\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}
is said to be evolutionary;
\varphi
being generating
section. In what follows, we do not distinguish between evolutionary fields
and their generating sections when possible.

Since
\sym(\pi)
is closed with respect to the commutator,
Theorem
1
implies that

[\mathbf{E}_{\varphi},\mathbf{E}_{\varphi^{\prime}}]=\mathbf{E}_{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}}

for some element
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in\varkappa(\pi)
which is called the Jacobi
bracket of
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
. To describe it explicitly, recall the following
fact:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\xi
be a vector bundle over
M
. Then the component-wise action

\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}^{\xi}\colon\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))\to\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))

is well defined and

\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}^{\xi}(aF)=\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}(a)F+a\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}^{\xi}(F)

for all
a\in\mathscr{F}(\pi)
and
F\in\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))
. Then

\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}=\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{\prime})-\mathbf{E}_{\varphi^{\prime}}^{\pi}(\varphi).

(6)

Let now
\mathscr{E}\subset J^{\infty}(\pi)
be an equation. Its symmetry is a symmetry of
the Cartan distribution on
\mathscr{E}
. To describe the algebra

\sym(\mathscr{E})
, let us, using Proposition
1
, define
the operator

\ell_{F}\colon\xi\to\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi)),\quad\ell_{F}(\varphi)=\mathbf{E}_{\varphi}^{\xi}(F).

In coordinates, one has

\ell_{F}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{\sigma}\dfrac{\partial F^{\alpha}}{\partial u_{\sigma}^{\beta}}D_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}

(7)

Remark 1
.

An element
F\in\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))
is a nonlinear differential
operator acting from sections of
\pi
to those of
\xi
. Hence,
\ell_{F}
is
its linearization. Note that
\ell_{F}
is an operator in total
derivatives. Such operators (we call them
\mathscr{C}
-differential) admit
restrictions to graphs of infinite jets and infinite prolongations.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathscr{E}\subset J^{\infty}(\pi)
be an equation associated with an
element
F\in P=\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\xi))
and such that

\pi_{\infty,0}(\mathscr{E})=J^{0}(\pi)
. Then

\sym(\mathscr{E})=\ker\ell_{\mathscr{E}},

where
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}
is the restriction of
\ell_{F}
to
\mathscr{E}
.

1.4.
Conservation laws and cosymmetries

A conservation law of
\mathscr{E}
is a
d_{h}
-closed differential form

\omega\in\Lambda_{h}^{n-1}(\mathscr{E})
. It is trivial if
\omega=d_{h}(\rho)
for some
\rho\in\Lambda_{h}^{n-2}(\mathscr{E})
. Two conservation
laws are equivalent if they differ by a trivial one. In coordinates, if

\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\,x^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\,dx^{i-1}\wedge\,dx^{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge\,dx^{n},

then
\omega
is a conservation law if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}D_{x^{i}}(a_{i})=0.

(8)

Direct computation of conservation laws using formula (
8
) is not
simple and is complicated by the existence of trivial laws, which are of no
interest. For the “majority” of equations, the procedure can be facilitated.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be given by some
F\in P
and consider

G\in Q=\Gamma(\pi_{\infty}^{*}(\eta))
, where
\eta
is another vector bundle
over
M
. Assume
\mathscr{E}
to enjoy the following regularity property:

\left.G\right|_{\mathscr{E}}=0\text{ implies }G=\Delta(F)

for some
\mathscr{C}
-differential operator
\Delta=\left(\sum_{\sigma}a_{\beta,\sigma}^{\alpha}D_{\sigma}\right)\colon P\to Q
. For any such an
operator define its adjoint
\Delta^{*}\colon\hat{Q}\to\hat{P}
, where

\hat{\bullet}=\hom_{\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})}(\bullet,\Lambda_{h}^{n}(\mathscr{E}))
, by

\Delta^{*}=\left((-1)^{\left|\sigma\right|}D_{\sigma}\circ a_{\alpha,\sigma}^{\beta}\right)

and consider a form
\bar{\omega}\in\Lambda_{h}^{n}(\pi)
such that

\left.\bar{\omega}\right|_{\mathscr{E}}=\omega
for a conservation law
\omega

of the equation
\mathscr{E}
. Then

d_{h}\bar{\omega}=\Delta(F),\qquad\Delta\colon P\to\Lambda_{h}^{n}(\pi).

(9)

Set
\psi_{\omega}=\left.\Delta^{*}(1)\right|_{\mathscr{E}}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a
2
-line equation in the sense
of
[
17
]
. Then
:

(1)

The element
\psi_{\omega}\in\hat{P}
is well defined
,

i.e.
,
does not depend on the choice of
\bar{\omega}
.

(2)

Conservation laws
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are equivalent if and only if

\psi_{\omega}=\psi_{\omega^{\prime}}
. In particular
,

\omega
is
trivial if and only if
\psi_{\omega}=0
.

(3)

The element
\psi_{\omega}
enjoys the equation

\ell_{\mathscr{E}}^{*}(\psi_{\omega})=0,

(10)

where
\ell_{\mathscr{E}}^{*}\colon\hat{P}\to\hat{\varkappa}
.

We say that
\psi_{\omega}
is the generating section of
\omega
, while
elements
\psi\in\hat{P}
satisfying Equation (
10
) are called
cosymmetries of
\mathscr{E}
.

1.5.
Differential coverings

Let a smooth fiber bundle
\tau\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}\to\mathscr{E}
be
such that the restriction

\left.d\tau\right|_{\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{\tilde{\theta}}}
,

\tilde{\theta}\in\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
, is an isomorphism
to
\mathscr{C}_{\tau(\tilde{\theta})}
, where

\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{\tilde{\theta}}

and
\mathscr{C}_{\tau(\tilde{\theta})}
are the Cartan planes at the
corresponding points. Such maps are called (differential) coverings.

Let
w^{1},\dots,w^{\alpha},\dots
be coordinates in fibers of
\tau
in the
vicinity of some point
\theta\in\mathscr{E}
(nonlocal variables). Then the
condition that
\tau
is a covering means that the total derivatives
on
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
are of the form
\tilde{D}_{x^{i}}=D_{x^{i}}+X_{i}
,
where

X_{i}=X_{i}^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{1}}+\dots+X_{i}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{\alpha}}+\dots

are
\tau
-vertical fields such that

D_{x^{i}}(X_{j})-D_{x^{j}}(X_{i})+[X_{i},X_{j}]=0,\qquad i<j.

(11)

This also means that
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
may be understood as the
overdetermined system

\frac{\partial w^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{i}}=X_{i}^{\alpha},

(12)

whose compatibility conditions coincide with
\mathscr{E}
.

Two coverings
\tau_{i}\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to\mathscr{E}
,
i=1,2
,
are equivalent if there exists an isomorphism of bundles

f\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{1}\to\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{2}
such that

df(\mathscr{C}_{\theta})=\mathscr{C}_{f(\theta)}
,

\theta\in\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{1}
. A covering is irreducible if the covering
equation is differentially connected and is trivial if it is equivalent to

\tau_{0}\colon\mathscr{E}_{0}\times\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
with

\tilde{D}_{x^{i}}=D_{x^{i}}
. For any two coverings over
\mathscr{E}
, the
Whitney product

\tau_{1}\times\tau_{2}\colon\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{1}\times_{\mathscr{E}}\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{2}\to\mathscr{E}
is naturally endowed with the structure
of a covering and it can be shown that any covering is locally equivalent to
the Whitney product of a trivial and irreducible ones.

A covering
\tau
given, we treat all the objects on
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
to
be nonlocal with respect to
\mathscr{E}
. In particular, a
symmetry
\tilde{X}
of
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
is a nonlocal symmetry
of
\mathscr{E}
. The defining equation for nonlocal symmetries is

\ell_{\tilde{\mathscr{E}}}(\tilde{\varphi})=0
. On the other hand, the
restriction

X=\left.\tilde{X}\right|_{\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})}\colon\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{F}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})

(13)

is an
\mathscr{F}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})
-valued derivation that preserves the
Cartan distributions. Derivations of such a type are called shadows. We say
that a shadow lifts to
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
if there exists an
\tilde{X}

such that (
13
) fulfils. Any shadow is an evolutionary derivation
on
\mathscr{E}
taking values in
\mathscr{F}(\tilde{\mathscr{E}})
with the
generating section
\tilde{\varphi}
living on
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
and
satisfying the defining equation

\tilde{\ell}_{\mathscr{E}}({\tilde{\varphi}})=0.

(14)

Remark 2
.

The notation
\tilde{\ell}
in (
14
) means the lift
to
\tilde{\mathscr{E}}
. Such a lift is possible for any

\mathscr{C}
-differential operator just by changing
D_{x^{i}}

to
\tilde{D}_{x^{i}}
.

\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{E}_{1},\mathscr{E}_{2})

\mathscr{E}_{1}
and
\mathscr{E}_{2}

, is imposed on unknowns

and
\mathscr{E}_{3}
, which is called the composition of

.

.

It may happen that the top equation is not differentially

1.7.
The tangent covering and recursion operators

Consider an equation
\mathscr{E}
and its
tangent bundle
T\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
. Take the quotient bundle

\mathbf{t}\colon\mathscr{TE}=T\mathscr{E}/\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{E}

(15)

and assume that its fibers are odd. Then (
15
) is
called the tangent covering to
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{TE}
is called the
tangent equation. Locally, sections of
\mathbf{t}
may be understood as

\pi_{\infty}
-vertical vector fields on
\mathscr{E}
.

Theorem 4
.

The tangent covering posseses
the following properties
:

(1)

Sections of
\mathbf{t}
that preseve the Cartan
distributions are identified with symmetries of
\mathscr{E}
.

(2)

The superalgebra of functions on
\mathscr{TE}
is
canonically isomorphic to the Grassmann algebra
\Lambda_{v}^{*}(\mathscr{E})
.

(3)

If
\mathscr{E}
is given by
F(u)=0
,
then
\mathscr{TE}

is given by the system

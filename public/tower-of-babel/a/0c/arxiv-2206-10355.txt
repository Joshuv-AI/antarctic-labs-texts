On a conjecture of Deaconescu
Elchin Hasanalizade

Abstract
. In 2000 Deaconescu raised a question whether there exists a composite n n for which S 2 ​ ( n ) | ϕ ⁡ ( n ) − 1 S_{2}(n)|\phi(n)-1 , where ϕ ⁡ ( n ) \phi(n) is Euler’s function and S 2 ​ ( n ) S_{2}(n) is Schemmel’s totient function. We also prove that any such n n with exactly K K distinct prime divisors is necessarily less than 2 2 K + 1 2^{2^{K+1}} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\phi
denote Euler’s totient function. In 1932 Lehmer
[
7
]
conjectured that if
\phi(n)|n-1
, then
n
has to be a prime number. A composite positive integer satisfying that divisibility is called Lehmer number or number with Lehmer property. Although this problem has not been settled so far, several partial results are known. Lehmer himself proved that if
n
has Lehmer property then
n
is odd, squarefree and has at least seven distinct prime factors. Cohen and Hagis
[
3
]
using computational methods established that
\omega(n)\geq 14
, where
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime divisors of
n
. Burcsi et al.
[
1
]
showed if additionally
3|n
, then
\omega(n)\geq 40\cdot 10^{6}
and
n>10^{36\cdot 10^{7}}
. On the other hand, Pomerance
[
9
]
proved that every Lehmer number
n
is
<K^{2^{K}}
, where
K=\omega(n)
. Recently, Burek and Żmija
[
2
]
have improved this upper bound to
2^{2^{K}}-2^{2^{K-1}}
.

In 2000 Deaconescu
[
4
]
conjectured that for
n\geq 2

\displaystyle S_{2}(n)|\phi(n)-1

if and only if
n
is prime, where
S_{2}(n)
is Schemmel’s totient function defined by

\displaystyle S_{2}(n)=n\prod_{p|n}\big(1-\frac{2}{p}\big).

This problem seems to be as challenging as Lehmer’s problem. Clearly, the conjecture states that for every
M\geq 1
, the set
D_{M}
of integers satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle MS_{2}(n)=\phi(n)-1

contains only prime numbers. We say that a composite integer
n
is a Deaconescu number (or has the Deaconescu property) if it satisfies (
1.1
).

In this short note we prove the following results.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
n
is a Deaconescu number, then
n
is odd, squarefree and
\omega(n)\geq 7
.

Inspired by the work of Burek and Żmija we will also get an upper bound for Deaconescu numbers.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
n
has the Deaconescu property, then

\displaystyle n<2^{2^{K}+K}-2^{2^{K-1}+K},

where
K=\omega(n)
.

Hernandez and Luca
[
6
]
proved that there are at most finitely many Lehmer numbers
n
such that
P(\phi(n))\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ n)
, where
P(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is any monic non-constant polynomial. We will prove the analogous result for Deaconescu numbers.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
be a monic non-constant polynomial. Then there are at most finitely many composite integers
n
such that
S_{2}(n)|\phi(n)-1
and
P(S_{2}(n))\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ \phi(n))
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we shall collect some preliminary results. First, we give a group-theoretic interpretation of Schemmel’s totient function.

Definition 1
.

Ler
R
be a commutative ring with identity and
R^{*}
be the multiplicative group of its units. A unit
u\in R
is called exceptional if
1-u\in R^{*}
.

Let
R^{**}
denote the set of all exceptional units in
R
. In particular if
R=\mathbb{Z}_{n}
the ring of residue classes mod
n
, then by definition we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{**}_{n}=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}:\ \text{gcd}(a,n)=1\ \text{and}\ \text{gcd}(a-1,n)=1\}.

In 2010, Harrington and Jones
[
5
]
proved that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{Z}^{**}_{n}|=S_{2}(n).

Note that
\phi(n)
is always even if
n>2
. Thus
M
in (
1.1
) must be odd.

Lemma 2.1
.

n\in D_{1}
if and only if
n=p
for some prime
p
.

Proof.

If
n=p
, it is in
D_{1}
. If
n=p^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\ldots p^{\alpha_{r}}_{r}
(
r>1
) with
p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots p_{r}
then
S_{2}(n)<\phi(n)-1
since
1\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{n}
and
p_{1}+1\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{n}
but
1\notin\mathbb{Z}^{**}_{n}
and
p_{1}+1\notin\mathbb{Z}^{**}_{n}
.
∎

From now on we assume that
M>1
,
n
always denotes an integer greater than 1 in
D_{M}
for some
M>1
. Then we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(n)}{S_{2}(n)}>M\geq 3.

The next lemma due to Nielsen
[
8
]
plays important role in the proof of the upper bound for numbers with Deaconescu property.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r,a,b\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be integers such that
1<x_{1}<x_{2}<\ldots<x_{r}
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{r}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\bigg)\leq\frac{a}{b}<\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{x_{j}}\bigg).

Then

(2.3)

\displaystyle a\prod_{j=1}^{r}x_{j}\leq(a+1)^{2^{r}}-(a+1)^{2^{r-1}}.

3.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By definition of
S_{2}(n)
it is clear that
n
must be odd. If
n
is not squarefree then
n
has a prime factor
p_{i}
for which
p_{i}|\phi(n)
and
p_{i}|S_{2}(n)
. In this case if
n
is Deaconescu number then
p_{i}|1
which is impossible. Next, we show that
\omega(n)\neq 2
. When
n=p_{1}p_{2}
equation (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle M(p_{1}-2)(p_{2}-2)=(p_{1}-1)(p_{2}-1)-1\ \text{or}\ M-1=\frac{1}{p_{1}-2}+\frac{1}{p_{2}-2}.

Hence
0<M-1\leq 1+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{4}{3}
and
M=2
. which is impossible since
M
is odd. If
2<\omega(n)\leq 6
, then
M=3
and
3|n
. Indeed, if
n=p_{1}p_{2}\ldots p_{r}
with
p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{r}
then (
2.1
) gives
M<\frac{\phi(n)}{S_{2}(n)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{q_{i}-1}{q_{i}-2}=Q_{r}
, where
\{q_{i}\}=\{3,5,7,\ldots\}
denotes the sequence of all odd primes. Since
Q_{r}<5
for
2<r\leq 6
, we get
M=3
. If
2<r\leq 6
and
3\nmid n
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(n)}{S_{2}(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}-2}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{6}\frac{q_{i+1}-1}{q_{i+1}-2}<3

contradicting (
2.1
). Hence
3|n
. But if
n=3p_{2}\ldots p_{r}
and
M=3
equation (
1.1
) becomes

\displaystyle 3(p_{2}-2)\cdots(p_{r}-2)=2(p_{2}-1)\cdots(p_{r}-1)-1.

Taking this equation modulo 3 we see that it has no solutions in primes. Hence
\omega(n)\geq 7
.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.2.

Let us write
n=p_{1}\cdots p_{K}
where
p_{1}<p_{2}<\ldots<p_{K}
. Then

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{K}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg)=\frac{S_{2}(n)}{\phi(n)}<\frac{S_{2}(n)}{\phi(n)-1}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{S_{2}(n)}{\phi(n)-1}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg)}

\displaystyle=\frac{n\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K}\bigg(1-\frac{2}{p_{j}}\bigg)}{(\phi(n)-1)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg)}

\displaystyle=\frac{n\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{2}{p_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{2}{p_{K}}\bigg)}{(\phi(n)-1)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg)}=\frac{n\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}}\bigg)\bigg(1-\frac{2}{p_{K}}\bigg)}{\phi(n)-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi(n)}{\phi(n)-1}\cdot\frac{\bigg(1-\frac{2}{p_{K}}\bigg)}{\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{K}}\bigg)}<1

since
\phi(n)>p_{K}-1
. Thus

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg)<\frac{S_{2}(n)}{\phi(n)-1}<\prod\limits_{j=1}^{K-1}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{p_{j}-1}\bigg).

Hence, the inequality (
2.2
) is satisfied for
x_{j}=p_{j}-1
,
r=K
,
a=1
,
b=\frac{\phi(n)-1}{S_{2}(n)}
. From (
2.3
) we get

\displaystyle(p_{1}-1)\ldots(p_{K}-1)\leq 2^{2^{K}}-2^{2^{K-1}}.

Since
p-1>\frac{p}{2}
for all primes
p\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle n=p_{1}\cdots p_{K}<2^{2^{K}+K}-2^{2^{K-1}+K}

∎

Proof of Theorem 1.3.

We follow closely an argument in
[
6
]
. Let

\displaystyle P(X)=X^{d}+a_{1}X^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[X]

with
d\geq 1
. Suppose
n
is a Deaconescu number. It is known that there exists a positive constant
c
such that
S_{2}(n)\geq\frac{cn}{(\log\log{3n})^{2}}
for all odd
n
(see
[
10
]
). Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle M\ll(\log\log{n})^{2}.

Since
P(S_{2}(n))\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ \phi(n))
we have that
M^{d}P(S_{2}(n))\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ \phi(n))
. Thus by (
1.1
), we get

\displaystyle(-1)^{d}+a_{1}M(-1)^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{d}M^{d}\equiv 0(\text{mod}\ \phi(n)).

Let
S
denote the left hand side of the above congruence. Now we consider two case:

Case I
.
S\neq 0
. Note that
\phi(n)\geq\sqrt{n}
for all odd
n
. Then from the above congruence and (
3.1
), we have that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\leq\phi(n)\leq|S|<\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|a_{j}|\bigg)M^{d}\ll(\log\log{n})^{2d}

which implies
n\ll 1
, as we want.

Case II
.
S=0
. Then
(-1)^{d}+a_{1}M(-1)^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{d}M^{d}=0
or

\displaystyle\bigg(-\frac{1}{M}\bigg)^{d}+a_{1}\bigg(-\frac{1}{M}\bigg)^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{d}=0

or
P\bigg(-\frac{1}{M}\bigg)=0
. Thus we get that
-\frac{1}{M}
is both an algebraic integer and a rational number which is impossible since
M\geq 3
.
∎

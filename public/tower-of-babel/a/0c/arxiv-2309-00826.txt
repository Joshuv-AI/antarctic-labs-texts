Metrical properties of finite product of partial quotients in arithmetic progressions
Mumtaz Hussain, Nikita Shulga

Abstract
. We investigate the dynamics of continued fractions and explore the ergodic behaviour of the products of mixed partial quotients in continued fractions of real numbers. For any function Φ : ℕ → [ 2 , + ∞ ) \Phi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,+\infty) and any integer d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 , we determine the Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension of the set of real numbers for which the product of partial quotients in arithmetic progressions satisfy a n ( x ) a 2 ​ n ( x ) ⋯ a d ​ n ( x ) ≥ Φ ( n ) a_{n}(x)a_{2n}(x)\cdots a_{dn}(x)\geq\Phi(n) for infinitely many positive integers n n . Our findings shed light on the size of the set of exceptions to Bourgain’s (1988) and Host and Kra’s (2005) theorems concerning the convergence of multiple ergodic averages for Gauss dynamical systems. By exploring the Hausdorff dimension of these sets, we gain valuable insights into the behaviour of such exceptions. Overall, our research contributes to a deeper understanding of the dynamics of continued fractions and their connection to the convergence properties of ergodic averages in Gauss dynamical systems.



1.
Introduction

Let
\left(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T\right)
be a measure preserving dynamical system, where
\mathcal{B}
is a Borel
\sigma
-algebra over
X
,
\mu
is a probability measure and
T:X\to X
is an invertible transformation which preserves the measure.
A fundamental problem in ergodic theory is to understand whether the ergodic average

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f\left(T^{n}\left(x\right)\right)

converges everywhere or almost everywhere for any function
f
from some class of functions. This question is answered by the famous Birkhoff ergodic theorem, which states that the above average converges pointwise for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
for any
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
.

A new impulse for studying ergodic averages was given by Furstenberg when he provided a new proof of Szemerédi Theorem by using ergodic theoretic methods affirming the existence of arithmetic sequences of arbitrary length amongst sets of integers with positive density, see
[
12
]
. The multiple recurrence theorem is stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Furstenberg, 1977)
.

Let
\left(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T\right)
be a measure preserving dynamical system such that
\mu(A)>0
for any
A\subset X
. Then for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1.1)

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap T^{-2n}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-dn}A\right)>0.

Afterwards, many authors have studied extensions of classical ergodic theorems on ‘non-conventional’ ergodic averages. Bourgain in
[
7
]
provided the following extension to Birkhoff’s theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Bourgain, 1988)
.

Let
\left(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T\right)
be a measure preserving dynamical system, and let
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
for any
1<p<\infty
. Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[n]
be a polynomial with integer coefficients. Then the averages

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f\left(T^{P(n)}\left(x\right)\right)

converge pointwise for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
.

Furstenberg’s proof was also a starting point for studying the convergence of multiple ergodic averages. To be more precise, for any bounded measurable functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
, we want to know whether the multiple ergodic averages

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f_{1}\left(T^{n}\left(x\right)\right)\cdots f_{d}\left(T^{dn}\left(x\right)\right)

converges everywhere (or almost everywhere) in the
L^{2}
norm. Host and Kra
[
14
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.3

(Host-Kra, 2005)
.

Let
\left(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T\right)
be a measure preserving dynamical system,
d
an integer, and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
be bounded measurable functions on
X
. Then the averages

(1.2)

A_{N}(\mathbf{f})=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-1}f_{1}(T^{n}(x))f_{2}(T^{2n}(x))\cdots f_{d}(T^{dn}(x))

converges almost surely in
L^{2}(\mu)
as
n\to\infty
.

Note that, by taking
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{d}=\mathbf{I}_{A}
and integrating (
1.2
) over
A
we get that the
\liminf
in Furstenberg’s theorem is a limit. For a selection of results in this direction, readers may refer to publications such as
[
9
,
11
,
14
,
26
]
. Bergelson and Leibman
[
3
]
proved that the exponents,
n,2n,\ldots
appearing in the Furstenberg theorem can be replaced by integer polynomials.

In this paper, we focus on the Gauss dynamical system of continued fractions and as a consequence of our main theorem, we find the Hausdorff dimension of the set associated with Bourgain and Host-Kra’s theorems for this system.

It is well-known that every irrational number
x\in[0,1)
can be uniquely expanded into an infinite continued fraction

\displaystyle x=\cfrac{1}{a_{1}\left(x\right)+\cfrac{1}{a_{2}\left(x\right)+\cdots}}.

More specifically, we write
x=\left[a_{1}\left(x\right),a_{2}\left(x\right),\ldots\right]
for the continued fraction expansion of
x
, where
a_{1}\left(x\right)=\lfloor 1/x\rfloor
and
a_{n}\left(x\right)=a_{1}\left(T^{n-1}\left(x\right)\right)
for
n\geq 2
are called the partial quotients of
x
. From here and on, by
T(x)
we denote the Gauss map
T:[0,1]\to[0,1]
defined by
T(0)=0
and

T(x):=\frac{1}{x}-\left\lfloor\frac{1}{x}\right\rfloor\quad\text{ for }x\neq 0.

The finite truncation
p_{n}\left(x\right)/q_{n}\left(x\right)=\left[a_{1}\left(x\right),\ldots,a_{n}\left(x\right)\right]
is called
n
th convergent of
x
.

Let
e^{f(x)}=f_{i}(x)=a_{1}(x)
for any
i\geq 1
. Note that the function
a_{1}(x)\notin L^{1}\left([0,1],\mathcal{L}\right)
, so, for instance, we cannot directly apply Birkhoff’s theorem. However, we can deal with this by considering ‘truncated’ functions

(1.3)

h_{M}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{i}(x)&\textrm{if }f_{i}(x)\leq M;\\
0&\textrm{if }f_{i}(x)>M,\end{array}\right.\text{ for }M=1,2,\ldots,\\

applying Birkhoff’s theorem to them and then taking a limit at
M\to\infty
.
We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for more details. For this choice of
f(x)
and the Gauss dynamical system, we study metrical properties of sets, in which products of partial quotients with indices in arithmetic progression, grow at a certain rate.

To formally define our setup, let
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,+\infty)
be a positive function, for which we define

(1.4)

\log B=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\Phi\left(n\right)}{n},\textrm{ }\log b=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\Phi\left(n\right)}{n}.

Fix
d\geq 1
and define the set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right):=

\displaystyle\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}(x)\cdots a_{nd}(x)\geq\Phi(n)\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Note that when
d=1
, the classical Borel-Bernstein theorem (1912) states that the Lebesgue measure of the set

\Lambda_{1}(\psi):=\left\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}(x)\geq\Phi(n)\ {\rm for\ infinitely\ many}\ n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is either zero or one depending upon the convergence or divergence of the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Phi(n)^{-1}
respectively. Taking
\Phi(n)=n\log n
, one can conclude that for almost all
x\in[0,1)
, with respect to Lebesgue measure,
a_{n}(x)\geq n\log n
holds for infinitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that the law of large numbers does not hold, that is

(1.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N}a_{n}(x)=\infty

for almost all
x
. Khintchine
[
22
]
showed that a weak law of large number holds for a suitable normalising sequence, that is,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)/n\log n
converges to
1/\log 2
with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Philipp
[
29
]
proved that there is no reasonably regular function
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)/\phi(n)
almost everywhere converges to a finite nonzero constant. However, Diamond and Vaaler
[
8
]
showed that the strong law of large numbers with the normalising sequence
n\log n
holds if the largest partial quotient
a_{k}(x)
is trimmed from the sum.
The method of deleting some number of maximal terms from Birkhoff sum to obtain strong laws of large numbers is called
trimming
. The first result using trimming in the dynamical systems context is the aforementioned result by Diamond and Vaaler, which was later generalised to other contexts. For example, Aaronson and Nakada
[
1
]
gave strong laws of large numbers under light trimming (that is, deleting a finite number of terms) for sufficiently fast
\psi
-mixing random variables.
For other applications, see
[
20
,
21
]
for intermediate trimming (that is, deleting infinitely many terms, while the number of terms deleted should be
\overline{o}(n)
compared with the length
n
of Birkhoff sum) applied to subshifts of finite type or
[
5
]
for generalisation on other types of continued fractions.

Note that the divergence of (
1.5
) implies the divergence of product of partial quotients, that is,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N}a_{n}(x)a_{2n}(x)\cdots a_{dn}(x)=\infty.

Hence the natural question is to find a normalising sequence
\varphi(n)
such that
\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(x)a_{2i}(x)\cdots a_{di}(x)}{\varphi(n)}
converges to a certain finite limit (in the spirit of the weak or strong law of large numbers stated above). However, these can be proved by following the methods presented in
[
15
]
. Nonetheless, as a consequence of the Lebesgue measure dichotomy statement below, one can ascertain that

a_{n}(x)a_{2n}(x)\cdots a_{dn}(x)\geq n\log^{2}(n)

holds for infinitely many
n
. Thus, any
x\in\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
is an exception to Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. Hence the natural question is to quantify this set. Our first result provides the Lebesgue measure of this set, depending on the function
\Phi(n)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to[2,+\infty)
be a positive function. Then, the Lebesgue measure of the set
\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
is given by

\mathcal{L}\left(\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&{\rm if}\ \ \sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{d-1}\Phi(n)}{\Phi(n)}<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
1&{\rm if}\ \ \sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{d-1}\Phi(n)}{\Phi(n)}=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Note that if
\Phi(n)\leq 1
for infinitely many
n
, then the set
\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
has full Lebesgue measure, but the corresponding series may converge. To see this, one can take a function
\Phi(n)=1
. As the partial quotients are integer numbers, the set
\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
is unchanged if we change
\Phi
by
\lceil\Phi\rceil
. This is why we can assume that
\Phi(n)\geq 2
.

Note that for rapidly increasing
\Phi
the Lebesgue measure of the set
\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
is zero. Hence, it is pertinent to discern between sets of Lebesgue measure zero where the Hausdorff dimension serves as a suitable measure.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Phi
be as in Theorem
1.4
, for which we define quantities (
1.4
). Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&{\rm if}\ \ B=1;\\
\lambda_{d}(B)&{\rm if}\ \ 1<B<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
\frac{1}{1+b}&{\rm if}\ \ B=\infty,\end{array}\right.

where
\lambda_{d}(B)
is a continuous decreasing function, such that
\lim_{B\rightarrow 1}\lambda_{d}(B)=1,\textrm{ }\lim_{B\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{d}(B)=\frac{1}{2}.
This means that the Hausdorff dimension continuously changes with respect to the values of
B
and
b
.

The function
\lambda_{d}(B)
is equal to the following expression in terms of the pressure function.

\lambda_{d}(B):=\inf\left\{s:\mathsf{P}\left(-s\log\left|T^{\prime}(x)\right|-g_{d}(s)\log B\right)\leq 0\right\},

where
g_{n}(s)
is given by the iterative relation

(1.6)

\displaystyle g_{1}(s)=s,\,\,\,\,\,g_{n}(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{sg_{n-1}(s)}{1-s+ng_{n-1}(s)}.

Here and throughout
T^{\prime}(x)
denotes the derivative of the Gauss map
T(x)
, and
\mathsf{P}
represents the pressure function defined in the Subsection
2.1
. We prove the continuity and limit properties of
\lambda_{d}(B)
in the same subsection. The dimensional number
\lambda_{d}(B)
can also be expressed as a limit of solutions of some equations, as shown in Proposition
2.15
below. For more details on the function
g_{n}(s),
including an explicit (non-iterative) formula, see Section
2
.

We also compare the Hausdorff dimension of the set
\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
with the dimension of the well-studied set of large products of consecutive partial quotients

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{d}(\Phi)=\left\{x\in[0,1):\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{n+i}(x)\geq\Phi(n)\ \ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many\ }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

We prove that in the most interesting case of
1<B<\infty
, the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
is larger than the Hausdorff dimension of
\mathcal{D}_{d}(\Phi)
when
d\geq 2
. For details, see Section
2.2
.

It is worth stating that the consideration of products of partial quotients arose from Kleinbock and Wadleigh paper
[
24
]
in which it was shown that considering the growth of the product of consecutive partial quotients gives information about the set of Dirichlet non-improvable numbers. Since then there have been several works in considering the sets of real numbers for which the product of (finite) consecutive partial quotients growing at a rate given by any positive function. We refer the reader to
[
2
,
6
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
24
,
27
]
for a selection of results in this direction.

The most challenging part of the proof is in establishing the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension for Theorem
1.5
in the case
1<B<\infty
. For all other cases, the proofs are relatively easier. In proving the lower bound in this case, we will employ the
Mass distribution principle
. To do this, we

•

Define a good subset of
\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
in terms of union of fundamental intervals;

•

Calculate the lengths of fundamental cylinders and gaps between them;

•

Define (and calculate) the probability measure supported on a suitable subset (Cantor subset) of
\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
in terms of the diameter of the fundamental intervals and calculate the Hölder exponent;

•

Calculate the Hölder exponent for an arbitrary ball;

•

Apply the mass distribution principle to calculate the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension.

In contrast to the consecutive partial quotient consideration, where authors have considered the growth of
d
consecutive partial quotients, in our setting we deal with the product of
d
partial quotients each arising from the arithmetic progression. This leads to significant complications in constructing a suitable Cantor subset, as we will need to introduce and optimise a number of parameters. This raises some difficulties in distributing the mass over fundamental cylinders, which we overcome in the course of the proof.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we shall give some basic properties about continued fractions which we will use later.

Recall that the
n
th convergents of
x
is defined as

\displaystyle\frac{p_{n}\left(x\right)}{q_{n}\left(x\right)}=\left[a_{1}\left(x\right),\ldots,a_{n}\left(x\right)\right].

For simplicity, we will denote
p_{n}=p_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
,
q_{n}=q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
when there is no ambiguity. The rule for the formation of the convergents is as follows, for any
k\geq 2
we have

(2.1)

\begin{split}p_{k}&=a_{k}p_{k-1}+p_{k-2},\\
q_{k}&=a_{k}q_{k-1}+q_{k-2},\end{split}

where
p_{0}=0,p_{1}=1
and
q_{0}=1,q_{1}=a_{1}
.

For
n\geq 1
and any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq n
, we define
\textit{a cylinder of order }n
, denoted by
I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
as

\displaystyle I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right):=\left\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{1}\left(x\right)=a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\left(x\right)=a_{n}\right\}.

We have the following standard facts about cylinders.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
23
]
)
.

For any
n\geq 1
and
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
p_{k},q_{k}
are defined recursively by
\left(\ref{rule}\right)
where
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

(2.2)

I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\dfrac{p_{n}}{q_{n}},\dfrac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}}\right)&{\rm if}\,n\textrm{ is even},\\
\left(\dfrac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}},\dfrac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right]&{\rm if}\,n\textrm{ is odd}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the length of a cylinder is given by

(2.3)

\left|I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\right|=\dfrac{1}{q_{n}\left(q_{n}+q_{n-1}\right)}.

For a better estimation, we need the following results. For any
n\geq 1
and
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we have

Lemma 2.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

For any
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

(2.4)

\dfrac{a_{k}+1}{2}\leq\dfrac{q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)}{q_{n-1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)}\leq a_{k}+1.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
23
]
)
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we have

(2.5)

\begin{split}q_{n+k}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right)&\geq q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\cdot q_{k}\left(a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right),\\
q_{n+k}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right)&\leq 2q_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\cdot q_{k}\left(a_{n+1},\ldots,a_{n+k}\right).\end{split}

For any
n\geq 1
, since
p_{n-1}q_{n}-p_{n}q_{n-1}=\left(-1\right)^{n}
, combined with a simple calculation, we get the bound

(2.6)

q_{n}\geq 2^{\left(n-1\right)/2}.

We will also use a trivial bound

(2.7)

a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\leq q_{n}\leq(a_{1}+1)\cdots(a_{n}+1).

Next, we introduce the mass distribution principle, which is useful in calculating the lower bound of the Hausdorff dimension.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
E\subseteq\left[0,1\right)
be a Borel set and
\mu
be a measure with
\mu\left(E\right)>0
, suppose that for some
s>0
, there is a constant
c>0
such that for any
x\in\left[0,1\right)

(2.8)

\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)\leq cr^{s},

where
B\left(x,r\right)
denotes an open ball centred at
x
and radius
r
, then
\dim_{H}E\geq s
.

Let
\mu
be the Gauss measure on [0,1] defined by

\mu(A)=\frac{1}{\log 2}\int_{A}\frac{1}{1+x}dx

for any Lebesgue measurable set
A
. The Gauss measure
\mu
is
T
-invariant and equivalent to Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}
.
The following exponentially mixing property of Gauss measure is well known (see
[
4
]
or
[
29
]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a constant
0<\rho<1
such that

\mu\left(I_{m}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\cap T^{-m-n}B)\right)=\mu\left(I_{m}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\right)\mu(B)(1+O({\rho}^{n}))

for any
m\geq 1,n\geq 0
, any
m
-th cylinder
I_{m}(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})
and any Borel set
B
, where the implied constant in
O({\rho}^{n})
is absolute.

Using Lemma
2.5
and Schmidt’s orthogonal method
[
30
]
, we have the following statement.

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
k\geq 1
let
\mathcal{C}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
P_{k}=\cup_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathcal{C}_{k}}I_{d}
. Then

\mu\left(\left\{x\in[0,1):T^{k}(x)\in P_{k}\ \text{for i.m. }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&{\rm if}\ \ \sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(P_{k})<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]
1&{\rm if}\ \ \sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(P_{k})=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Using the fact that Gauss measure
\mu
is equivalent to Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}
, we get

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose that
P_{n},n\geq 1
are as in Lemma
2.6
. Then

\mathcal{L}(\{x\in[0,1)\,:\,T^{n}(x)\in P_{n}\text{ for i.m. }\,n\in\mathbb{N}\})

is null or full according to whether the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}(P_{n})
converges or not.

We also need the following technical lemma, which helps us to choose a suitable set of parameters to construct the Cantor subset. Consider a non-decreasing sequence of real numbers
1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{d-1}\leq\alpha_{d}=B
.

Proposition 2.8
.

With the notation as above, we have

(2.9)

\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{d-1}\leq B}\min\left\{\alpha_{1}^{-s},\ldots,\left(\alpha_{k-1}^{1-s}\alpha_{k}^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}},\ldots,\left(\alpha_{d-1}^{1-s}\cdot B^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\right\}=\frac{1}{B^{g_{d}(s)}},

where
2\leq k\leq d-1
and the supremum is attained when all the terms are equal.

Proof.

The proof is quite similar with the Proposition 2.13 in
[
16
]
.
When
d=2
, this problem is simplified to

\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq B}\min\left\{\alpha_{1}^{-s},\alpha_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-s)}B^{-\frac{s}{2}}\right\}.

As a function of
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{1}^{-s}
is decreasing and
\alpha_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-s)}
is increasing, so the supremum is attained when both the terms are equal. Furthermore, it is equal to
B^{-g_{2}(s)}
. Now assume that the claim holds for
d=\ell
. Then we prove it for
d=\ell+1
as,

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{\ell}\leq B}\min\left\{\alpha_{1}^{-s},\ldots,\left(\alpha_{\ell-1}^{1-s}\alpha_{\ell}^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell}},\left(\alpha_{\ell}^{1-s}\cdot B^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell+1}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{\ell}\leq B}\sup_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell-1}):1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{\ell}\leq B}\min\left\{\min\left\{\alpha_{1}^{-s},\ldots,\left(\alpha_{\ell-1}^{1-s}\alpha_{\ell}^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\right\},\left(\alpha_{\ell}^{1-s}\cdot B^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell+1}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq\alpha_{\ell}\leq B}\min\left\{\sup_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell-1}):1\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\alpha_{\ell}\leq B}\min\left\{\alpha_{1}^{-s},\ldots,\left(\alpha_{\ell-1}^{1-s}\alpha_{\ell}^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell}}\right\},\left(\alpha_{\ell}^{1-s}\cdot B^{-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell+1}}\right\}.

By induction, the following supremum

2.1.

2.2.
Comparison with other dimensional numbers

There is a well-known result about the Hausdorff dimension of a set with large consecutive partial quotients. Consider the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{d}(\Phi)=\left\{x\in[0,1):\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{n+i}(x)\geq\Phi(n)\ \ \mathrm{for\ infinitely\ many\ }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

The following statement was proven is
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.17

(Huang-Wu-Xu, 2020)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{[}2,\infty)

(2.19)

\log B=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\Phi(n)}{n}\quad{and}\quad\log b=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\log\Phi(n)}{n}.

Then,

\theta_{d}(B):=\inf\{s:\mathsf{P}(T,-f_{d}(s)\log B-s\log|T^{\prime}|)\leq 0\}&{\rm if}\ \ 1<B<\infty;\\[12.91663pt]

where

is given by the following iterative formula:

f_{1}(s)=s,\,\,\,\,\,f_{k+1}(s)=\frac{sf_{k}(s)}{1-s+f_{k}(s)}\text{ for }k\geq 1.

and

To do this, first note that we can write down an explicit formula for

2.11

Proposition 2.18
.

For any

. This means that the infimum in (
3.1
) is attained at the value
\beta
for which we have the equality

\displaystyle-s\log|T^{\prime}|+\frac{1-s}{d}\log\beta-\frac{s}{d}\log B

\displaystyle=-s\log|T^{\prime}|-g_{d-1}(s)\log\beta

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\log\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{1-s+dg_{d-1}(s)}\log B.

Now substituting the expression for
\log\beta
into the definition (
3.3
) of
t_{d-1}(\beta)
, we get the following upper bound of the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda_{d}(B)
.

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\Lambda_{d}(B)

\displaystyle\leq\inf\left\{s:P(-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|-\frac{sg_{d-1}(s)}{1-s+dg_{d-1}(s)}\log B)\leq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{s:P(-s\log|T^{\prime}(x)|-g_{d}(s)\log B)\leq 0\right\}=\lambda_{d}(B),

where we used the recursive definition of
g_{d}(s)
.

3.2.
The lower bound

We choose a rapidly increasing sequence of integers
\{\ell_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, fixed integers
M
and
N
, define
n_{0}=0,n_{k}-1=\ell_{k}N
,
m_{k}=dn_{k}+1+r_{k}N
for any
k\geq 1
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}r_{k}=\infty\text{ and }n_{k+1}-m_{k}-1\leq 2N.

Consider a following subset of
E
:

\displaystyle E_{M}=\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):

\displaystyle A_{1}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\leq 2A_{1}^{n_{k}},A_{2}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{2n_{k}}\left(x\right)\leq 2A_{2}^{n_{k}},\ldots,A_{d}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{dn_{k}}\left(x\right)\leq 2A_{d}^{n_{k}},

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}a_{m_{k}+1}(x)=\cdots=a_{n_{k+1}-1}(x)=2\textrm{ for all }k\geq 1,1\leq a_{n}\left(x\right)\leq M\textrm{ for other cases }\Big\},

where
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{d})_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence for which the minimum in (
2.8
) is attained.

3.2.1.
Structure of
E_{M}

In this subsection, we will study set
E_{M}
in symbolic space which is defined as follows. Let

\displaystyle D_{n}=\Big\{\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:

\displaystyle A_{1}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{n_{k}}\leq 2A_{1}^{n_{k}},A_{2}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{2n_{k}}\leq 2A_{2}^{n_{k}},\ldots,A_{dn_{k}}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{dn_{k}}\leq 2A_{dn_{k}}^{n_{k}},

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;\;}a_{m_{k}+1}=\cdots=a_{\min\{n_{k+1}-1,n\}}=2\textrm{ for all }k\geq 1,1\leq a_{n}\leq M\textrm{ for other cases }\Big\}

and

D=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}\quad\left(D_{0}:={\diameter}\right).

Write
I_{0}=\left[0,1\right]
. For any
n\geq 1
and
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, we call
I_{n}=I_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
a
basic interval of order n
and

(3.5)

J_{n}=J_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)=\bigcup_{a_{n+1}}\textrm{cl}I_{n+1}

a
fundamental interval of order n
, where the union in
\left(\ref{J}\right)
is taken over all
a_{n+1}
such that
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n+1}\right)\in~D_{n+1}
.
It is clear that

(3.6)

E_{M}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\bigcup_{\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}}J_{n}.

3.2.2.
Lengths of fundamental intervals

In this subsection, we calculate the lengths of fundamental intervals while splitting them into several distinct cases, which we will use later.

(1)

When
n=jn_{k}-1
for any
k\geq 1
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle J_{jn_{k}-1}=\bigcup_{A_{j}^{n_{k}}\leq a_{jn_{k}}\leq 2A_{j}^{n_{k}}}I_{jn_{k}}.

Therefore we get

\displaystyle\left|J_{jn_{k}-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{j}^{n_{k}}+1}{\left(\left(2A_{j}^{n_{k}}+1\right)q_{jn_{k}-1}+q_{jn_{k}-2}\right)\left(A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}+q_{jn_{k}-2}\right)}

and hence

(3.7)

\frac{1}{2^{3}A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}}\leq\left|J_{jn_{k}-1}\right|\leq\frac{1}{A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}}.

(2)

When
m_{k}\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-2
for any
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle J_{n}=I_{n+1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{k}},2,\ldots,2\right).

where the number of partial quotients that are equal to 2 is
n-m_{k}+1
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|J_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2q_{n}+q_{n-1})(3q_{n}+q_{n-1})}

and

(3.8)

\frac{1}{2^{4(n-m_{k})+5}q_{m_{k}-1}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{5}q_{n}^{2}}\leq\left|J_{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{2}q_{n}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{2(n-m_{k})+2}q_{m_{k}-1}^{2}}.

(3)

For other cases, one has

\displaystyle J_{n}=\bigcup_{1\leq a_{n+1}\leq M}I_{n+1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|J_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{\left(\left(M+1\right)q_{n}+q_{n-1}\right)\left(q_{n}+q_{n-1}\right)}

and

(3.9)

\frac{1}{6q_{n}^{2}}\leq\left|J_{n}\right|\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}}.

3.2.3.
Gaps in
E_{M}

For a given a fundamental interval
J_{n}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
, where
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, we estimate the gap between
J_{n}
and its adjacent fundamental intervals of the same order
n
. Without loss of generality, we assume
n
is even. Let
g_{n}^{l}=g_{n}^{l}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
and
g_{n}^{r}=g_{n}^{r}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)
are defined respectively as the left and the right minimum distance between
J_{n}
and its adjacent fundamental intervals at each side. Write

\displaystyle g_{n}=\min\left\{g_{n}^{l},g_{n}^{r}\right\}.

Since
n
is even, we note that
g_{n}=g_{n}^{r}
. In addition, we only consider the case of
J_{n}
and its right adjacent fundamental intervals of order
n
are all in
I_{n-1}
, otherwise consider their parents fundamental interval.

(1)

When
n=jn_{k}-1
for any
k\geq 1
and
1\leq j\leq d
, one will see that
g_{n}
is larger than the distance between the right endpoint of
I_{jn_{k}}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}-1},A_{j}^{n_{k}}\right)
and the right endpoint of
I_{jn_{k}-1}
. Thus

(3.10)

\begin{split}g_{n}&\geq\frac{p_{jn_{k}-1}+p_{jn_{k}-2}}{q_{jn_{k}-1}+q_{jn_{k}-2}}-\frac{A_{j}^{n_{k}}p_{jn_{k}-1}+p_{jn_{k}-2}}{A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}+q_{jn_{k}-2}}\geq\frac{1}{4A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\left|J_{n}\right|\quad\textrm{by $\left(\ref{J1}\right)$}.\end{split}

(2)

When
m_{k}\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-1
for any
k\geq 1
, one has
g_{n}
is larger than the distance between the right endpoint of
I_{n+1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},2\right)
and the right endpoint of
I_{n}
. Thus

(3.11)

\begin{split}g_{n}&\geq\frac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}}-\frac{2p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{2q_{n}+q_{n-1}}\geq\frac{1}{6q_{n}^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6}\left|J_{n}\right|\quad\textrm{by $\left(\ref{J2}\right)$}.\end{split}

(3)

For other cases, by assumption,
g_{n}^{r}
is just the distance between the right endpoint of
I_{n}
and the left endpoint of
I_{n+1}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+1,M\right)
.

(3.12)

\begin{split}g_{n}&=\frac{\left(M+1\right)p_{n}+\left(M+2\right)p_{n-1}}{\left(M+1\right)q_{n}+\left(M+2\right)q_{n-1}}-\frac{p_{n}+p_{n-1}}{q_{n}+q_{n-1}}\geq\frac{1}{10Mq_{n}^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{10M}\left|J_{n}\right|\quad\textrm{by $\left(\ref{J3}\right)$}.\end{split}

3.2.4.
The mass distribution of
E_{M}

We define a measure
\mu
supported on
E_{M}
. The mass on fundamental intervals of order
n_{k},2n_{k},\ldots,dn_{k}
depends on the total number of possible values of
a_{n_{k}},a_{2n_{k}},\ldots,a_{dn_{k}}
for any
k\geq 1
. For intervals of order
n
with
m_{k}+1\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-1
for any
k\geq 1
, the mass is same as their parents fundamental interval. As for other intervals, we define uniform distribution and use consistency of measure. More specifically, given any integers
N,M
large enough, denote

\displaystyle u:=\sum_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}^{N}}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

For any
s<\lambda_{d}(B,M,n)
, one has
u\geq 1
. Let
n_{k}-1=\ell_{k}N
for any
k\geq 1
, where
n_{0}=0
. We define mass by induction.

(1)

For any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{iN})\in D_{iN}
and
1\leq i<\ell_{1}
, let

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{iN}\right)=\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

(2)

For any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{1}}\right)\in D_{jn_{1}}
and
1\leq j\leq d
, let

\displaystyle\quad\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{A_{i}^{n_{1}}}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{1}-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

(3)

For any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{1}-1}\right)\in D_{jn_{1}-1}
and
1\leq j\leq d
, let

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}-1}\right)=A_{j}^{n_{1}}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right).

(4)

For any
1\leq j<d
, when
n=jn_{1}+iN
where
1\leq i<\ell_{1}
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}+iN}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{jn_{1}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

When
n=dn_{1}+iN
for some
1\leq i\leq r_{1}
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{dn_{1}+iN})

\displaystyle=\mu(J_{dn_{1}})\cdot\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{dn_{1}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

(5)

For any
m_{1}+1\leq n\leq n_{2}-1
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\quad\mu(J_{n})=\mu(J_{m_{1}}).

(6)

For other cases, when
n\neq jn_{1}+iN
for any
0\leq j<d
,
0\leq i<\ell_{1}
and
n\notin[m_{1}+1,n_{2}-1]
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
jn_{1}+(i-1)N\leq n<jn_{1}+iN
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{n})\leq\mu(J_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N}).

When
n\neq dn_{1}+iN
for any
0\leq i\leq r_{1}
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
dn_{1}+(i-1)N\leq n<dn_{1}+iN
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{n})\leq\mu(J_{dn_{1}+(i-1)N}).

As for the measure of other fundamental intervals, we adopt induction. Assume the mass on fundamental intervals of the order
n\leq n_{k}-1
has been defined, then

(7)

For some
k\geq 2
, for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}})\in D_{jn_{k}}
and any
1\leq j\leq d
, let

\displaystyle\quad\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}}\right)=\mu(J_{n_{k}-1})\cdot\frac{1}{A_{1}^{n_{k}}}\prod_{i=2}^{j}\frac{1}{A_{i}^{n_{k}}}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{k}-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

(8)

For some
k\geq 2
, for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}-1})\in D_{jn_{k}-1}
and any
1<j\leq d
, let

\displaystyle\quad\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}-1}\right)=A_{j}^{n_{k}}\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}}\right).

(9)

For some
k\geq 2
and some
1\leq j<d
, when
n=jn_{k}+iN
for some
1\leq i<\ell_{k}
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}+iN}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu(J_{jn_{k}})\cdot\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{jn_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

When
n=dn_{k}+iN
for some
1\leq i\leq r_{k}
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{dn_{k}+iN})

\displaystyle=\mu(J_{dn_{k}})\cdot\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{u}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{dn_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}.

(10)

For some
k\geq 2
, for any
m_{k}+1\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-1
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\quad\mu(J_{n})=\mu(J_{m_{k}}).

(11)

For other cases, when
n\neq jn_{k}+iN
for any
k\geq 1
,
0\leq j<d
,
0\leq i<\ell_{k}
and
n\notin[m_{k}+1,n_{k+1}-1]
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
jn_{k}+(i-1)N\leq n<jn_{k}+iN
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{n})\leq\mu(J_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N}).

When
n\neq dn_{k}+iN
for any
0\leq i\leq r_{k}
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
dn_{k}+(i-1)N\leq n<dn_{k}+iN
, for any
\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\in D_{n}
, let

\displaystyle\mu(J_{n})\leq\mu(J_{dn_{k}+(i-1)N}).

3.2.5.
Estimation of
\mu(J_{n})

We compare the mass with the length of fundamental interval and we will frequently use the properties
\left(\ref{c1}\right)-\left(\ref{qE}\right)
of convergents in the process.

(1)

When
n=iN
, for some
1\leq i<\ell_{1}
, we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{iN}\right)&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\\
&\leq 2^{2\left(i-1\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2s}}\quad\left(\textrm{by $\left(\ref{qE}\right)$}\right)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll\left|J_{iN}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\quad\left(\textrm{by $\left(\ref{J3}\right)$}\right).\end{split}

(2)

When
n=\ell_{1}N=n_{1}-1
, As in the
\left(\ref{E1}\right)
, we have

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{n_{1}-1}\right)&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{1}-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\\
&\leq 2^{2\left(\ell_{1}-1\right)}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s}\frac{1}{B^{\ell_{1}g_{d}(s)N}}\quad\left(\textrm{by $\left(\ref{c2}\right)$}\right)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\frac{1}{B^{\ell_{1}g_{d}(s)N}}\quad\left(\textrm{similar with $\left(\ref{E1}\right)$}\right).\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}B^{n_{1}\frac{g_{d}(s)}{s}}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

Thus, using (
3.7
) we conclude that

(3.14)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{n_{1}-1}\right)\ll\left|J_{n_{1}-1}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

(3)

When
n=jn_{1}
for any fixed
1\leq j\leq d
, we get the following estimation.

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)&\leq\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{A_{i}^{n_{1}}}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{1}-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\left(A_{1}\cdots A_{j}\right)^{n_{1}}B^{j(n_{1}-1)g_{d}(s)}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\quad\left(\textrm{similar to case (2)}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{A_{1}^{-sn_{1}j}A_{j}^{\left(1-s\right)n_{1}}\left(A_{1}\cdots A_{j}\right)^{2sn_{1}}B^{j(n_{1}-1)g_{d}(s)}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}},\end{split}

where the last equality is hold by (
2.11
). Notice that

\displaystyle q_{jn_{1}}\leq 2^{(2j-1)+j}(A_{1}\cdots A_{j})^{n_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}q_{n_{1}-1}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right).

Thus,

(3.15)

\displaystyle|J_{jn_{1}}|\geq\frac{1}{6}\frac{1}{2^{6j-2}(A_{1}\cdots A_{j})^{2n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}.

From (
2.15
) we see that
A_{1}^{-sj}A_{j}^{1-s}B^{jg_{d}(s)}=A_{j}^{1-s}>1
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)&\leq\frac{B^{jg_{d}(s)}}{\left(A_{1}\cdots A_{j}\right)^{2sn_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\leq B^{jg_{d}(s)}[62^{6j-2}]^{s-\frac{8}{N}}\left(\frac{1}{[62^{6j-2}]\left(A_{1}\cdots A_{j}\right)^{2n_{1}}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

So we come to

(3.16)

\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)\ll\left|J_{jn_{1}}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.

(4)

When
n=jn_{1}-1
for
1<j\leq d
, we note that

\frac{\left|J_{jn_{1}}\right|}{\left|J_{jn_{1}-1}\right|}\leq\frac{2^{3}A_{j}^{n_{1}}q_{jn_{1}-1}^{2}}{q_{jn_{1}}^{2}}\leq\frac{2^{3}}{A_{j}^{n_{1}}}.

We know that

(3.17)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}-1}\right)&=A_{j}^{n_{1}}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{A_{j}^{n_{1}}}{A_{1}^{-sn_{1}j}A_{j}^{\left(1-s\right)n_{1}}\left(A_{1}\cdots A_{j}\right)^{2sn_{1}}B^{j(n_{1}-1)g_{d}(s)}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{1}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

Multiply the right hand side by
\frac{A_{j}^{sn_{1}}}{A_{j}^{sn_{1}}}
and note that
A_{j}^{-1+s}A_{1}^{-sj}A_{j}^{1-s}B^{jg_{d}(s)}=1
. Therefore, proceeding as in the previous case, we come to

(3.18)

\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}-1}\right)\ll\left|J_{jn_{1}-1}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.

(5)

When
n=jn_{1}+iN
for some
1\leq j<d
and
1\leq i<\ell_{1}
, we have

(3.19)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}+iN}\right)&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{jn_{1}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\cdot\mu\left(J_{jn_{1}}\right)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2}(a_{jn_{1}+1})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{jn_{1}}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll\left(\frac{1}{q_{jn_{1}+iN}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll|J_{jn_{1}+iN}|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

(6)

When
n=dn_{1}+iN
for some
1\leq i\leq r_{1}
, we have

(3.20)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{dn_{1}+iN}\right)&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}(a_{dn_{1}+tN+1})B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\cdot\mu(J_{dn_{1}})\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2}(a_{dn_{1}+1})}\right)^{s-\frac{8}{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{dn_{1}}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll\left(\frac{1}{q_{dn_{1}+iN}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll|J_{dn_{1}+iN}|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}.\end{split}

(7)

When
m_{1}+1\leq n\leq n_{2}-1
, we have

\mu(J_{n})=\mu(J_{m_{1}}).

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{|J_{m_{1}}|}{|J_{n}|}\leq 2^{4N+5}\textrm{ and }q_{n}^{2}\geq 2^{4N^{2}+5N},

when
n_{1}
is large enough. Thus, one concludes that

(3.21)

\mu(J_{n})\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{60}{N}}.

(8)

When
n\neq jn_{1}+iN
for any
0\leq j<d
,
0\leq i<\ell_{1}
and
n\notin[m_{1}+1,n_{2}-1]
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
jn_{1}+(i-1)N\leq n<jn_{1}+iN
, we observe that

\begin{split}\frac{\left|J_{n}\right|}{\left|J_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N}\right|}&\geq\frac{1}{6}\frac{q_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N}^{2}}{q_{n}^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n-jn_{1}-(i-1)N}a_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N+1}\cdots a_{n}}\right)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{1}{4M^{2}}\right)^{N}.\end{split}

Thus, one has

(3.22)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{n})&\leq\mu(J_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N})\\
&\leq\left|J_{jn_{1}+(i-1)N}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\leq 6\cdot\left(4M^{2}\right)^{N}\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{60}{N}}.\end{split}

With the same method, when
n\neq dn_{1}+iN
for any
0\leq i\leq r_{1}
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
dn_{1}+(i-1)N\leq n<dn_{1}+iN
, one has

(3.23)

\mu(J_{n})\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{60}{N}}.

(9)

When
n=n_{k}-1
, we have

\begin{split}\mu(J_{n_{k}-1})&=\mu(J_{m_{k-1}})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{A_{1}^{n_{k-1}}}\prod_{t=0}^{r_{k-1}-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}(a_{dn_{k-1}+tN+1})B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\right)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{A_{j}^{n_{k-1}}}\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{k-1}-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}(a_{jn_{k-1}+tN+1})B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\right)\cdot\mu(J_{n_{k-1}-1})\\
&\leq\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{A_{j}^{n_{b}}}\right)\cdot\prod_{t=0}^{r_{b}-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}(a_{dn_{b}+tN+1})B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\right)\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{b}-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}(a_{jn_{b}+tN+1})B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\right)\cdot\mu(J_{n_{1}-1})\\
&\leq\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{A_{j}^{n_{b}}}\right)\cdot 2^{2(r_{b}-1)}\left(\frac{1}{q_{m_{d}-dn_{b}-1}^{2s}(a_{dn_{b}+1})B^{g_{d}(s)r_{b}N}}\right)\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}2^{2(\ell_{b}-1)}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{b}-1}^{2s}(a_{jn_{b}+1})B^{g_{d}(s)\ell_{d}N}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\\
&\leq 2^{2\sum_{b=1}^{k-1}(r_{b}-1)+2\sum_{b=1}^{k-1}(\ell_{b}-1)(n_{b}-1)}\frac{1}{B^{g_{d}(s)\sum_{b=1}^{k-1}m_{b}-2n_{b}+1}}\\
&\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{m_{d}-dn_{b}-1}^{2s}(a_{dn_{b}+1})}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{q_{n_{b}-1}^{2s}(a_{(j+1)n_{b}+1})}\right)\\
&\quad\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{{A_{1}^{-sdn_{b}}A_{d}}^{(1-s)n_{d}}(A_{1}\cdots A_{d})^{2sn_{b}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{1}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}},\end{split}

where the last inequality holds by (
2.11
). Notice that

\begin{split}q_{n_{k}-1}&\leq 2^{2d(k-1)+d(k-1)}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}A_{1}^{n_{b}}\cdots A_{d}^{n_{b}}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}q_{n_{b+1}-dn_{b}-1}(a_{dn_{b}+1})\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}q_{n_{b}-1}(a_{jn_{b}+1})\\
&\leq 2^{2d(k-1)+d(k-1)+2N}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}A_{1}^{n_{b}}\cdots A_{d}^{n_{b}}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}q_{m_{b}-dn_{b}-1}(a_{dn_{b}+1})\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}q_{n_{b}-1}(a_{jn_{b}+1})\end{split}

and

\displaystyle|J_{n_{k}-1}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8A_{1}^{n_{k}}q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8\cdot 2^{6d(k-1)}A_{1}^{n_{k}}}\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{(A_{1}\cdots A_{d})^{2n_{b}}}\cdot

\displaystyle\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{q_{m_{b}-dn_{b}-1}^{2}(a_{dn_{b}+1})}\cdot\prod_{b=1}^{k-1}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{q_{n_{b}-1}^{2}(a_{jn_{b}+1})}.

Combining everything together and using the fact that
A_{1}=B^{\frac{g_{d}(s)}{s}}
,
we have

(3.24)

\mu(J_{n_{k}-1})\leq|J_{n_{k}-1}|^{s-\frac{120}{N}}.

(10)

When
n=jn_{k}
for any
k\geq 2
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A_{1}^{n_{k}}}\prod_{i=2}^{j}\frac{1}{A_{i}^{n_{k}}}\prod_{t=0}^{\ell_{k}-1}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{\left(i-1\right)n_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\cdot\mu(J_{n_{k}-1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A_{1}^{-sjn_{k}}A_{j}^{(1-s)n_{k}}(A_{1}\cdots A_{j})^{2sn_{k}}}\cdot\frac{1}{B^{(n_{k}-1)g_{d}(s)(j-1)}}

\displaystyle\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}(a_{in_{k}+1})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\quad\left(\frac{1}{A_{1}^{n_{k}}q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{120}{N}}.

Notice that

q_{jn_{k}}\leq 2^{2(j-1)+1+j}A_{1}^{n_{k}}\cdots A_{j}^{n_{k}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}q_{n_{k}-1}(a_{in_{k}+1})\cdot q_{n_{k}-1}

and

\displaystyle|J_{jn_{k}}|\geq\frac{1}{6q_{jn_{k}}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{6\cdot 2^{6j-2}}\frac{1}{(A_{1}\cdots A_{j})^{2n_{k}}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{1}{q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}(a_{in_{k}+1})}\cdot\frac{1}{q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}}.

Using (
2.15
) and
A_{j}^{1-s}A_{1}^{s}>1
we come to

(3.25)

\mu(J_{jn_{k}})\leq|J_{jn_{k}}|^{s-\frac{120}{N}}.

(11)

When
n=jn_{k}-1
for any
k\geq 2
and
1<j\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(J_{jn_{k}-1})

\displaystyle=A_{j}^{n_{k}}\mu(J_{jn_{k}}).

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{|J_{jn_{k}}|}{|J_{jn_{k}-1}|}\leq\frac{2^{3}}{A_{j}^{n_{k}}}.

and

\displaystyle\mu(J_{jn_{k}-1})

\displaystyle=A_{j}^{n_{k}}\cdot\mu(J_{jn_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A_{j}^{n_{k}}}{A_{1}^{-sjn_{k}}A_{j}^{(1-s)n_{k}}(A_{1}\cdots A_{j})^{2sn_{k}}}\cdot\frac{1}{B^{(n_{k}-1)g_{d}(s)(j-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}(a_{in_{k}+1})}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{1}^{n_{k}}q_{n_{k}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{120}{N}}.

Combined with (
2.15
) and
A_{1}^{s}>1
, one has

(3.26)

\mu(J_{jn_{k}-1})\leq|J_{jn_{k}-1}|^{s-\frac{120}{N}}.

(12)

When
n=jn_{k}+iN
for some
1\leq j<d
and
1\leq i<\ell_{k}
, we have

(3.27)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{jn_{k}+iN})&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{jn_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\cdot\mu(J_{jn_{k}})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{B^{g_{d}(s)iN}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2}\left(a_{jn_{k}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{jn_{k}}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{120}{N}}\\
&\ll|J_{jn_{k}+iN}|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.\end{split}

(13)

When
n=dn_{k}+iN
for any
1\leq i\leq r_{k}
, we have

(3.28)

\begin{split}\mu(J_{dn_{k}+iN})&\leq\prod_{t=0}^{i-1}\frac{1}{q_{N}^{2s}\left(a_{dn_{k}+tN+1}\right)B^{g_{d}(s)N}}\cdot\mu(J_{dn_{k}})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{B^{g_{d}(s)iN}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{iN}^{2}\left(a_{dn_{k}+1}\right)}\right)^{s-\frac{2}{N}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{q_{dn_{k}}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{120}{N}}\\
&\ll|J_{dn_{k}+iN}|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.\end{split}

(14)

When
m_{k}+1\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-1
, we have

\mu(J_{n})=\mu(J_{m_{k}}).

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{|J_{m_{k}}|}{|J_{n}|}\leq 2^{4N+5}\textrm{ and }q_{n}^{2}\geq 2^{4N^{2}+5N},

when
n_{k}
is large enough. Thus, one concludes that

(3.29)

\mu(J_{n})\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.

(15)

When
n\neq jn_{k}+iN
for any
k\geq 1
,
0\leq j<d
,
0\leq i<\ell_{k}
and
n\notin[m_{k}+1,n_{k+1}-1]
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
jn_{k}+(i-1)N\leq n<jn_{k}+iN
, we observe that

\begin{split}\frac{\left|J_{n}\right|}{\left|J_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N}\right|}&\geq\frac{1}{6}\frac{q_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N}^{2}}{q_{n}^{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n-(i-1)N-jn_{k}}a_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N+1}\cdots a_{n}}\right)^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{1}{4M^{2}}\right)^{N}.\end{split}

Then, we have

(3.30)

\begin{split}\mu\left(J_{n}\right)&\leq\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N}\right)\leq\left|J_{jn_{k}+(i-1)N}\right|^{s-\frac{120}{N}}\\
&\leq 6\cdot\left(4M^{2}\right)^{N}\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{120}{N}}\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\quad\left(\textrm{by (\ref{E13}) and $\left(\ref{J3}\right)$}\right).\end{split}

With the same method, when
n\neq dn_{k}+iN
for any
0\leq i\leq r_{k}
, there is a unique integer
i
such that
dn_{k}+(i-1)N\leq n<dn_{k}+iN
, one has

(3.31)

\mu(J_{n})\ll|J_{n}|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.

In a word, for any
n\geq 1
, we conclude

(3.32)

\mu(J_{n})\ll|J_{n}|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.

3.2.6.
Estimation of
\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)

We consider the measure of a general ball
B(x,r)
with
x\in E_{M}
and
r
small. Let
n
be the integer such that

\displaystyle g_{n+1}\leq r<g_{n}.

By the definition of
g_{n}
, it is clear that
B\left(x,r\right)
can only intersect one fundamental interval of order
n
, which is
J_{n}
.

(1)

When
n=jn_{k}-1
for any
k\geq 1
and
1\leq j\leq d
, considering whether the radius of the ball sometimes is larger than the length of basic interval of order
jn_{k}
or not, we divide this proof into two parts.

(i)

When
r\leq\left|I_{jn_{k}}\right|
, the ball
B\left(x,r\right)
can intersect at most four basic intervals of order
jn_{k}
, which is
I_{jn_{k}}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}}-1\right)
,
I_{jn_{k}}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}}\right)
,
I_{jn_{k}}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}}+1\right)
and
I_{jn_{k}}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{jn_{k}}+2\right)
. Then by
\left(\ref{E16}\right)
and
\left(\ref{G1}\right)

(3.33)

\begin{split}\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)&\leq 4\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}}\right)\ll 4\left|J_{jn_{k}}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\leq 40\left|g_{jn_{k}}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\leq 40r^{s-\frac{230}{N}}.\end{split}

(ii)

When
r>\left|I_{jn_{k}}\right|
, by calculating the number of intersections between the ball and fundamental intervals of order
jn_{k}
, we obtain the mass of the ball. Note that

\displaystyle\left|I_{jn_{k}}\right|=\frac{1}{q_{jn_{k}}\left(q_{jn_{k}}+q_{jn_{k}-1}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{2q_{jn_{k}}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{32A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}},

so we estimate that the number of fundamental interval of order
n_{k}
contained in
J_{jn_{k}-1}
that the ball
B\left(x,r\right)
intersects is at most

\displaystyle 2r\cdot 32A_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}+2\leq 64rA_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}.

Thus, by
\left(\ref{E16}\right)

(3.34)

\begin{split}\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)&\leq\min\left\{\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}-1}\right),64rA_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}}\right)\right\}\\
&\leq\mu\left(J_{jn_{k}-1}\right)\min\left\{1,64rA_{j}^{n_{k}}q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}\frac{1}{A_{j}^{n_{k}}}\right\}\\
&\ll\left|J_{jn_{k}-1}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\min\left\{1,64rq_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}\right\}\\
&\ll\left(\frac{1}{q_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\left(64rq_{jn_{k}-1}^{2}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\\
&\ll r^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.\end{split}

(2)

When
m_{k}\leq n\leq n_{k+1}-2
for any
k\geq 1
, consider
r>\left|I_{n+1}\right|
, by calculating the number of intersections between the ball and fundamental intervals of order
n+1
, we obtain the mass of the ball. Note that

\displaystyle\left|I_{n+1}\right|=\frac{1}{q_{n+1}\left(q_{n+1}+q_{n}\right)}\geq\frac{1}{12q_{n}^{2}},

so we estimate the number of fundamental interval of order
n+1
contained in
J_{n}
that the ball
B\left(x,r\right)
intersects is at most

\displaystyle 2r\cdot 12q_{n}^{2}+2\leq 24rq_{n}^{2}.

Thus, by
\left(\ref{E16}\right)

(3.35)

\begin{split}\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)&\leq\min\left\{\mu\left(J_{n}\right),24rq_{n}^{2}\mu\left(J_{n+1}\right)\right\}\\
&\leq\mu\left(J_{n}\right)\min\left\{1,24rq_{n}^{2}\right\}\\
&\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\min\left\{1,24rq_{n}^{2}\right\}\\
&\ll\left(\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\left(24rq_{n}^{2}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\\
&\ll r^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.\end{split}

(3)

When
n\neq jn_{k}-1
for any
k\geq 1
, we know that
1\leq a_{n}\leq M
for any
n\geq 1
and
\left|J_{n}\right|\asymp\dfrac{1}{q_{n}^{2}}
. Then by
\left(\ref{E16}\right)
and
\left(\ref{G3}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\mu\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\left(J_{n}\right)\ll\left|J_{n}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{q_{n}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}\leq 4M^{2}\left(\frac{1}{q_{n+1}^{2}}\right)^{s-\frac{130}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq 24M^{2}\left|J_{n+1}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq 240M^{3}\left|g_{n+1}\right|^{s-\frac{130}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq 240M^{3}r^{s-\frac{130}{N}}.

By Proposition
2.4
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}E_{M}\geq s-\frac{130}{N}.

Letting
N\rightarrow\infty
and then
M\rightarrow\infty
implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}E\geq\lambda_{d}(B).

4.
Completing the proof for general function

In this section we focus on the dimension of
\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
for a general function
\Phi(n)
. First, we introduce a result we will use later. More details about this result can be found in Lüczak [12].

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
b,c>1
, sets

\Big\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq c^{b^{n}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\Big\}

and

\Big\{x\in[0,1):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq c^{b^{n}}\textrm{ for sufficiently large }n\Big\}

share the same Hausdorff dimension
1/\left(b+1\right)
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.5
that we split into several parts.

(1)

For the case
B=1
, we observe that

\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq\Phi\left(n\right)\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

By the result of Wang and Wu [3], we obtain that
\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)=1
.

(2)

For the case
1<B<\infty
, for any
0<\varepsilon<B-1
we have
\Phi\left(n\right)>\left(B-\varepsilon\right)^{n}
when
n
is large enough. Thus, we have

\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq\left(B-\varepsilon\right)^{n}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

On the other hand, one can choose a sparse integer sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, such that for all
j
we have

\Phi\left(n_{j}\right)<\left(B+\varepsilon\right)^{n_{j}}.

Then similar to Section
3.2
we will prove the lower bound by constructing and analysing set, analogous to the set
E_{M}
from the lower bound section. The only difference is that here we are given the sequence
n_{j}
as oppose to the lower bound proof, where we have chosen the sequence on our own. So in general we cannot guarantee that
n_{k}-1
is a multiple of
N
. However, we can express
n_{k}=\ell_{k}N+r_{k}
, where
0\leq r_{k}<N
. Afterwards, we restrict partial quotients in blocks of the length
N
to be from the set
\{1,\ldots,M\}
and we set partial quotients in positions corresponding to the remainder
r_{k}
to be equal to
2
.

The rigorous proof of the lower bound for this set can be carried out from Section
3.2
with no more changes. Now one can conclude

\lambda_{d}(B+\varepsilon)\leq\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\leq\lambda_{d}(B-\varepsilon).

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain
\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)=\lambda_{d}(B)
.

(3)

For the case
B=\infty
, we consider the following three cases.

(i)

For
1<b<\infty
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have
\Phi\left(n\right)>e^{\left(b-\varepsilon\right)^{n}}
for sufficiently large
n
. We observe that

\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq e^{(b-\varepsilon)^{n}}

implies that there exists
1\leq i\leq d
such that
a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq e^{(b-\varepsilon)^{n}/d}
for any
x\in\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
. We note that

e^{(b-\varepsilon)^{n}/d}\geq e^{(b-2\varepsilon)^{n}}.

Thus, we obtain an upper bound of
\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)
.

\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b-2\varepsilon\right)^{n}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
we have

\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\leq\frac{1}{1+b-2\varepsilon}.

By the arbitrary of
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\leq\frac{1}{1+b}.

As for the lower bound, we observe that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\Phi\left(n\right)<e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{n}}
for infinitely many
n
. Let

\mathcal{G}=\Big\{n:\Phi\left(n\right)<e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{n}}\Big\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{n}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathcal{G}\Big\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{in}},1\leq i\leq d,\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{n}\left(x\right)\geq e^{\left(b+\varepsilon\right)^{n}},\textrm{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Thus, by the arbitrary of
\varepsilon>0
we have

\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)=\frac{1}{1+b}

by Lemma
4.1
.

(ii)

For
b=1
, the proof of the lower bound is same as the case
1<b<\infty
, but in the end we take
b=1
. This implies that
\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\geq 1/2
. As for the upper bound, we note that
B=\infty
, so we have

\frac{\log\Phi\left(n\right)}{n}>\log B_{1}

for a sufficiently large
B_{1}>0
. This implies
\Phi\left(n\right)>B_{1}^{n}
holds for sufficiently large
n
. Then, we have

\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):\prod_{i=1}^{d}a_{in}\left(x\right)\geq B_{1}^{n}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}.

Thus

\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\leq\lambda_{d}(B_{1}).

Letting
B_{1}\rightarrow\infty
and recalling Proposition
2.16
, we obtain the desired result.

(iii)

For
b=\infty
, for a sufficiently large
B_{2}>0
, we have

\frac{\log\log\Phi\left(n\right)}{n}\geq\log B_{2}.

Thus
\Phi\left(n\right)>e^{B_{2}^{n}}
holds for sufficiently large
n
. Then similarly to the case (i) we have

\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\subseteq\Big\{x\in\left[0,1\right):a_{ni}\left(x\right)\geq e^{(B_{2}^{1/i}/d^{1/i})^{ni}}\textrm{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}

for some
1\leq i\leq d
.
This implies that

\dim_{H}\Lambda_{d}\left(\Phi\right)\leq\frac{1}{(B_{2}^{1/i}/d^{1/i})+1}\rightarrow 0

as
B_{2}\rightarrow\infty
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

For each
n\geq 1
consider the set

\Lambda_{d,n-1}(\Phi)=\{x\in[0,1)\,:\,a_{1}(x)a_{n+1}(x)\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}\geq\Phi(n)\}.

It is clear that
x\in\Lambda_{d}(\Phi)
if and only if
T^{n-1}(x)\in\Lambda_{d,n-1}(\Phi)
for infinitely many
n\geq 1
.
Thus by Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
, it suffices to see whether the Lebesgue measure the series
\sum_{n}\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{d,n}(\Phi))
is convergent or not.
We get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{d,n-1}(\Phi))

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{(d-1)n+1}:a_{1}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}\geq\Phi(n)}\frac{1}{q_{(d-1)n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{(d-1)n+1}:a_{1}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}\geq\Phi(n)}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}q_{n-1}^{2}(a_{2})a_{n+1}^{2}\cdots q_{n-1}^{2}(a_{(d-2)n+2})a_{(d-1)n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}}\frac{1}{q_{n-1}^{2}}\right)^{d}\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}\geq\Phi(n)}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}a_{n+1}^{2}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}\geq\Phi(n)}\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}a_{n+1}^{2}\cdots a_{(d-1)n+1}^{2}}.

In the last line, we used the fact that the term
\left(\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}}\frac{1}{q_{n-1}^{2}}\right)^{d}
is at least
1/2^{d}
and at most
2^{d}
. Note that as
d
is finite, omitting this factor will not change the convergence.

Finally, from the proof of Theorem 1.5 in
[
16
]
we know that

\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2}\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:a_{1}\cdots a_{d}\geq\Phi(n)}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{1}{a_{k}^{2}}\asymp\frac{\log^{d-1}\Phi(n)}{\Phi(n)},

where the implied constant depends only on
d
.

This means that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{d,n}(\Phi))=\infty\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{d-1}\Phi(n)}{\Phi(n)}=\infty.

Application of Corollary
2.7
finishes the proof.

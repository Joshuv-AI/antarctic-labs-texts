. Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{\infty}
-smooth domain,
n\geq 2
. In this article we prove (optimal)
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity for solutions of the higher order measure-valued equation

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{2}u=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma\quad\textrm{in $\Omega$},

where
\Gamma\subset\subset\Omega
is some closed
C^{1,\alpha}
-submanifold and
Q\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
for some
\alpha>0
. Later we will also study what happens if one replaces
(-\Delta)^{2}
by
(-\Delta)^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N},m\geq 2
.

For
(-\Delta)^{2}
we impose
Navier boundary conditions
with smooth boundary data
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, i.e. one would classically require that
u=u_{0}
on
\partial\Omega
and
\Delta u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. The weak formulation of this problem is given by the following

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of

(1.2)

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{2}u=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\textrm{in}\;\Omega\\
u=u_{0},\;\Delta u=0&\textrm{on}\;\partial\Omega\end{cases}

if
u-u_{0}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
and

(1.3)

\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

One might notice that the demanded a priori regularity is not enough to speak of classical Navier boundary conditions. The boundary values are however
encoded
in the usage of the large test function space
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Indeed, (
1.3
) is equivalent to

being a very weak solution of the
measure-valued Dirichlet problem

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\\
\quad v=0&\mathrm{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

in the sense of
[
Ponce
, Definition 3.1]
. If one considers (
1.4
) for some suitably regular data
Q
,
\Gamma
, one can show that
v=\Delta u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
and the Navier boundary conditions ‘
\Delta u=0
on
\partial\Omega
’ also hold classically. We refer to Section
2.2
for details.

Optimal regularity of weak solutions of (
1.4
) was also the main object of study in
[
PoissonMarius
]
, even for more general elliptic operators than (-
\Delta
). The treatment in Section
2.2
only resembles the same regularity results as in
[
PoissonMarius
]
, but with a more elementary and self-contained argument in the given special case.

Once this regularity for
\Delta u
is obtained, elliptic regularity implies

for all
q\in[1,\infty)
.

Question 1.
Optimal regularity.

While
W^{3,q}
-regularity is now understood for each
q<\infty
it is not clear whether
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
. This shall be the main question of this article.

We remark that one can not expect regularity beyond
W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
— e.g. we shall see in Section
2.2
that
u\not\in C^{3}(\Omega)
unless
Q\equiv 0
(cf. Remark
2.6
).
Also
W^{4,1}
-regularity is excluded by (
1.2
), since
Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma\not\in L^{1}(\Omega)
(and hence

This article gives a positive answer to the

-regularity under some further regularity assumptions on

Theorem 1.2

(Optimal regularity)
.

Let
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)

(p>n)
,
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}
for some
C^{2}
-smooth domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega

and
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
. Then each weak solution of (
1.2
) lies in

W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
.

From now on we will abbreviate the above condition on
\Gamma
by ‘
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
’. We also remark that
Q
was a priori only defined on
\Gamma
— so the condition ‘
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
’ requires that
Q
can be extended to a
W^{2,p}
-function on
\Omega
.

One might expect that similar methods as in
[
PoissonMarius
]
carry over to the fourth order setting. However, the approach in
[

]
relies heavily on the

, which is not available for fourth order equations.

Instead our approach relies on a suitable auxiliary equation for

, which we will derive.

Question 2.
Inherited regularity for
D^{4}u
.

Once
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity is shown, one can also ask the question of
regularity inheritance
. This is a different question from the above, as we shall explain in the following. Instead of searching the highest Sobolev class that solutions live in, we ask whether all highest order derivatives inherit their quality from the operator. For instance, for
p\in(1,\infty)
it is known that (-
\Delta

L^{p}
-regularity to
D^{2}u
in the following sense: Looking at all derivatives in the sense of distributions, one has

(1.5)

-\Delta u\in L^{p}_{loc}\quad\Rightarrow\quad D^{2}u\in L^{p}_{loc}

for each
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Let now
\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
denote the space of all signed Radon measures on
\Omega
. It seems interesting to study under which conditions
-\Delta u\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
implies that
D^{2}u\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

Definition 1.3

(Inheritance of
\mathcal{M}_{loc}
-regularity for (-
\Delta
))
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
be a subspace. We say that the differential inclusion ‘
-\Delta u\in S
’ inherits
\mathcal{M}_{loc}
-regularity to
D^{2}u
if for each function
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
one has (in the sense of distributions)

(1.6)

-\Delta u\in S\quad\Rightarrow\quad D^{2}u\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega).

Remark 1.4
.

The conclusion
D^{2}u\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
can be phrased equivalently via
\nabla u\in BV_{loc}(\Omega)
. This will be useful for our proofs.

Remark 1.5
.

Notice that we have to restrict to a subspace
S\subsetneq\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
in Definition
1.3
since conclusion (
1.6
) is not true for
S=\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
. This becomes visible if one looks at
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n},S=\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and considers
\mu=\delta_{0}\in S
. The fundamental solution
F=F_{\Delta}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves then (distributionally)
-\Delta F=\delta_{0}\in S
but one has
D^{2}F\not\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The latter can be easily seen as follows. If
D^{2}F\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then
\nabla F\in BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which would imply
\nabla F\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Noticing that
\nabla F(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{n}}
a.e. for any
n\geq 2
and one readily checks that
\nabla F\not\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In this article the studied subspace
S
of Definition
1.3
is

(1.7)

S:=S(\Gamma,p):=\{Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma:Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\}

for some

and some

. We will see in Section

that

\Delta

2

(Regularity inheritance)

2.1.
Preliminaries

Before we can study the fourth order problem we need to refine some results for the second order problem (
1.4
).

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is a (very) weak solution of (
1.4
) if

(2.1)

-\int_{\Omega}v\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that the map
T:W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
T(\phi):=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
defines an element of the dual space
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)^{*}
(due to the continuity of the Sobolev trace operator). Hence, existence of solutions of (
2.1
) is readily checked with the Lax-Milgram theorem. Solutions constructed this way actually lie in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Even more regularity can be obtained with this method: Properties of the Sobolev trace yield also that
T\in W_{0}^{1,s}(\Omega)^{*}
for all
s\in(1,\infty)
. As
\Delta:W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)\rightarrow W_{0}^{1,\frac{q}{q-1}}(\Omega)^{*}
is an isomorphism for all
q\in(1,\infty)
we obtain that solutions lie in
W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)
for all
q<\infty
.

Most of the results in this section can also be obtained from
[
PoissonMarius
]
, where the equation
-\mathrm{div}(A(x)\nabla u)=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma
for an elliptic operator
A\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
and a density
Q\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
on a surface
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{1,\alpha}
was studied.
Our approach is self-contained in the sense that results from
[
PoissonMarius
]
are not used but rather reproved in a more elementary way for the special case of
A=I_{n},Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega),(p>n)
and
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
.
The key ingredient is a comparison with the
signed distance function

(2.2)

d_{\Gamma}:B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\quad d_{\Gamma}(x):=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{dist}(x,\Gamma)&x\in\Omega^{\prime}\\
0&x\in\Gamma\\
\mathrm{dist}(x,\Gamma)&x\in\Omega^{\prime\prime},\end{cases}

where
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\Gamma)<\epsilon\}
for some
\epsilon>0
, which we will always fix and choose small enough as in
[
GilTru
, Appendix 14.6]
(and in such a way that
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)\subset\subset\Omega
). We briefly recall some properties of
d_{\Gamma}
on
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
from
[
GilTru
, Appendix 14.6]
. First note that
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
is a
C^{2}
-domain and
d_{\Gamma}\in C^{2}(\overline{B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)})
. Further, one has
\nabla d_{\Gamma}=\nu^{\Omega^{\prime}}\circ\pi_{\Gamma}
where
\pi_{\Gamma}:B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\Gamma
is the (
C^{1}
-regular)
nearest point projection
on
\Gamma
.
We will always denote
\nu:=\nu^{\Omega^{\prime}}
from now on.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
,
p>n
and let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
with outer unit normal
\nu
. Further let
d_{\Gamma}:B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the signed distance function of
\Gamma
. Then (distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) there holds

(2.3)

\partial^{2}_{ij}\left(\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)=Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma+g_{ij}

for some
g=(g_{ij})\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma);\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. In particular,

(2.4)

-\Delta\left(\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)=-Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma-\mathrm{tr}(g).

Proof.

Let

\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
be arbitrary but fixed. Denote by
\nu=\nu^{\Omega^{\prime}}
and
\nu^{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}
the outer unit normals of
\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime\prime}
(respectively).
Then using integration by parts and
d_{\Gamma}|_{\Gamma}=0
we find

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.6)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\nu_{i}^{\Omega^{\prime}}\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\nu_{i}^{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\partial_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\nu^{\Omega^{\prime}}_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\partial_{i}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\nu^{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\partial^{2}_{ij}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\phi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\partial^{2}_{ij}\left(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\right)\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

Noticing that
\partial\Omega^{\prime}\cap\mathrm{supp}(\phi)=\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathrm{supp}(\phi)=\Gamma
and
\nu_{j}^{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}=-\nu_{j}^{\Omega^{\prime}}
on
\Gamma
we infer

(2.11)

\int\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{i}(Qd_{\Gamma})\nu_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int g_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x,

where
g_{ij}:=\partial^{2}_{ij}(\frac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma})(\chi_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}-\chi_{\Omega^{\prime}})\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Now notice that on
\Gamma
one has

(2.12)

\partial_{i}(Qd_{\Gamma})=(\partial_{i}Q)d_{\Gamma}+Q(\partial_{i}d_{\Gamma})=0+Q\nu_{i}=Q\nu_{i}.

Thus we infer

(2.13)

\int\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int g_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)),

which was asserted. Formula (
2.4
) follows immediately from
-\Delta f=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{ii}f
and
and using
\sum_{i}\nu_{i}^{2}=1
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
and let
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)

(p>n)
, be such that
Q|_{\Gamma}\not\equiv 0
. Then

(2.14)

\textrm{(a)}\;\;\tfrac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\in W^{1,\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)),\quad\textrm{(b)}\;\;\nabla(\tfrac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|)\in BV(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)),\quad\textrm{(c)}\;\;\tfrac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\not\in C^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)).

Proof.

Claim (a) is immediate by Lipschitz continuity of
|d_{\Gamma}|
and the fact that
W^{2,p}\hookrightarrow C^{1}
for
p>n
. Claim (c) follows from the fact that
\nabla|d_{\Gamma}|=\mathrm{sgn}(d_{\Gamma})\nabla d_{\Gamma}=\mathrm{sgn}(d_{\Gamma})\nu\circ\pi_{\Gamma}
has a jump at each point on
\Gamma
.
To show claim (b) we compute for
i=1,...,n
and
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma);\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
|\psi|\leq 1
using Lemma
2.3

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int\partial_{i}(\tfrac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma})\mathrm{div}(\psi)\;\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int\partial_{i}(\tfrac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma})\partial_{j}\psi_{j}\;\mathrm{d}x=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int\tfrac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma}\partial^{2}_{ij}\psi_{j}\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.16)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\Gamma}Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\psi_{j}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int g_{ij}\psi_{j}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}(||Q||_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}+||g_{ij}||_{L^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))}).

This implies
\partial_{i}(\tfrac{Q}{2}d_{\Gamma})\in BV(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
and thus (b) follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfy (
2.1
) for some
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)

(p>n)
with
Q|_{\Gamma}\not\equiv 0
. Then

(2.17)

\mathrm{(i)}\;\;v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega),\qquad\mathrm{(ii)}\;\;\nabla v\in BV(\Omega),\qquad\mathrm{(iii)}\;\;v\not\in C^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Then by (
2.4
) we have

(2.18)

-\int v\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{tr}(g)\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

In particular, we have (distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) that
(-\Delta)(v+\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|)=\mathrm{tr}(g)\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. We infer by elliptic regularity that
v+\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Let now
h\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
be such that
v=-\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|+h
. The fact that
h\in W^{2,p}_{loc}\subset C^{1}
implies together with Corollary
2.4
(a) that
v\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Moreover,
v\not\in C^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
is easily deduced by contradiction — If
v
were an element of
C^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
, then also
v-h\in C^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. But
v-h=-\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\in C^{1}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
contradicts Corollary

(c). This already implies (iii). Finally,

and Corollary

(b) can be used to show

. Now observe by (

) that

is weakly harmonic on

, implying that

. In particular

. Then

solves on

(weakly)

(2.19)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=0&\textrm{in $\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)$},\\
\quad\;\;v=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$},\\
\quad\;\;v=w&\textrm{on $\partial B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)$.}\end{cases}

Elliptic regularity thus implies that

∎

.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

be any

-domain,

for some

,

. Then

Proof.

For
\delta>0

, Theorem 5.1]

2.3.
SBV-Regularity

We first briefly report on the definition of the space
SBV
. Recall that for each
BV
-function
w\in BV(\Omega)
there exists a finite signed vector-valued Radon measure
\mu_{w}
on
\Omega
such that

(2.23)

\int_{\Omega}w\;\mathrm{div}(\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now
\mu_{u}
can be decomposed (cf.
[
EvGar
, Section 5.1]
) into two finite signed vector-valued measures
\mu_{w}=\mu_{w}^{a}+\mu_{w}^{s}
, where
\mu_{w}^{a}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and
\mu_{w}^{s}
is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\Omega
.

Further we define for each Lebesgue-measurable
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the
approximate limits

(2.24)

f_{+}(x):=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{|\{f>t\}\cap B_{r}(x)|}{r^{n}}=0\right\}

and

(2.25)

f_{-}(x):=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{|\{f<t\}\cap B_{r}(x)|}{r^{n}}=0\right\}.

It can be shown that
f_{+},f_{-}
are Borel measurable and
f_{+}\geq f_{-}
, cf.
[
EvGar
, Chapter 5, Lemma 5.6]
.
We define the
jump set
of such
f
to be the Borel set

(2.26)

J_{f}:=\{x\in\Omega:f_{+}(x)>f_{-}(x)\}.

If
w\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
then
[
EvGar
, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.17]
implies that
J_{w}
is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable. Now we are ready to define the space
SBV(\Omega)
.

Definition 2.9

(The space
SBV(\Omega)
)
.

We say
w\in BV(\Omega)
lies in
SBV(\Omega)
if
\mu_{w}^{s}(\Omega\setminus J_{w})=0
.

We will make use the following characterization lemma of
SBV
which is frequently used in the study of free discontinuity problems

Lemma 2.10

(cf.
[
DeGiorgi
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
K\subset\Omega
be (relatively) closed such that
w\in C^{1}(\Omega\setminus K)\cap BV(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(K)<\infty
. Then
w\in SBV(\Omega)
and
J_{w}\subset K
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
and
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
,
p>n
. Further, let
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

(2.27)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
\quad\;\;v=0&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then
v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\nabla v\in SBV(\Omega)
. Moreover,
J_{\partial_{i}v}\subset\Gamma
for all
i=1,...,n
.

Proof.

We intend to apply Lemma
2.10
.
By Lemma
2.5
one has that
v\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
(i.e.
\nabla v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
) and also from Lamma
2.5
we conclude that
\nabla v\in BV(\Omega)
. Clearly
v
lies also in
C^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
as
v
is (weakly) harmonic on
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma\subset\subset\Omega
is a closed
C^{1}
-submanifold, we infer also that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Gamma)<\infty
. Hence, Lemma
2.10
is applicable and the claim follows.
∎

3.
Optimal regularity (Proof of Theorem
1.2
)

We will prove the theorem by showing that for all
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
as in Theorem
1.2
and
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}
the function
\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
lies actually in
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. The reason for that will be that it solves a suitable measure-valued
auxiliary problem
of second order. Together with our results from the previous section this will yield the desired regularity.

We have already discussed in (
1.4
) and thereafter that
\Delta u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\cap C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
(meaning also
\Delta u=0
on
\partial\Omega
) and
u\in W^{3,q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
. Next we turn to some local smoothness properties on
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
1.2
). Then
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
and
(-\Delta)^{2}u=0
on
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. Moreover,
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\cap N)
for an open neighborhood
N
of
\partial\Omega
.

Proof.

Notice that for each
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
one has by (
1.3
) that

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

In particular
\Delta u
is weakly harmonic on
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. This implies that
\Delta u
lies in
C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
and is harmonic on
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. Elliptic regularity now implies that
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
and harmonicity of
\Delta u
yields
\Delta^{2}u=\Delta(\Delta u)=0
. Now let
D\subset\subset\Omega
be a smooth subdomain such that
\Gamma\subset\subset D\subset\subset\Omega
. [Such subdomain clearly exists since one can observe that for
\epsilon>0
small enough the subdomain
\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega^{C})>\epsilon\}
has
C^{2}
-boundary and can hence be appoximated by smooth domains with the same construction as in the Lemma
2.8
]. Define
\varphi:=(\Delta u)|_{\partial D}\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
.
Notice that
\Omega\setminus D
is a smooth domain and a subset of
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. Therefore,
v:=\Delta u
is harmonic on
\Omega\setminus D
. Moreover
v
is continuous on
\overline{\Omega\setminus D}
with boundary values
v=0
in
\partial\Omega
and on
\partial D
one has
v=\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\partial D)
. In particular
v
is smooth on
\partial(\Omega\setminus D)
. Elliptic regularity and smoothness of
\Omega\setminus D
imply then that
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega\setminus D}).
The claim follows choosing
N:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{D}
.
∎

Finally we are able to find the desired auxiliary equation for
\partial^{2}_{ij}u
and prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
u
be as in the statement and
1\leq i,j\leq n
. We first derive a distributional equation for
\partial^{2}_{ij}u
on
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
.
To this end let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
be arbitrary. Then several integrations by parts yield

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int\partial^{2}_{ij}u\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int u\Delta(\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int\Delta u\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\Delta u-\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int\left(\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

Recall that distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
one has

(3.4)

(-\Delta)(-\Delta u)=(-\Delta)^{2}u=Q\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma

and

(3.5)

(-\Delta)\left(\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)=-Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma-\mathrm{tr}(g),

where
g=(g_{ij})
is as in Lemma
2.3
. We conclude that (again in the sense of distributions in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) one has

(3.6)

(-\Delta)\left(\Delta u-\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)=\mathrm{tr}(g)\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)).

Therefore, by elliptic regularity
h:=\Delta u-\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|
lies in
W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Using this and Lemma
2.3
once more we infer from (
3.3
)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int\partial^{2}_{ij}u\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int h\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int\left(\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\right)\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\int(\partial^{2}_{ij}h)\phi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int g_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

We infer that (distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) one has

(AUX1)

\Delta(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)=\nu_{i}\nu_{j}Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma+(\partial^{2}_{ij}h+g_{ij}).

Notice that
\partial^{2}_{ij}h+g_{ij}\in L^{p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Therefore we can decompose the second derivative on
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
as a sum of two functions, namely
\partial^{2}_{ij}u=w_{1}+w_{2}
where
w_{1}\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
solves (distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
)

(3.9)

\Delta w_{1}=\nu_{i}\nu_{j}Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma.

and
w_{2}\in L^{1}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
solves (distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
)

(3.10)

\Delta w_{2}=(\partial^{2}_{ij}h+g_{ij})\in L^{p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)).

We remark that this decomposition is not unique, only up to harmonic functions on
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
. However, since these are smooth, they do not play a role for the (local) regularity.
From Lemma
2.8
we conclude that
w_{1}\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
and elliptic regularity yields
w_{2}\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Therefore we obtain
\partial^{2}_{ij}u=w_{1}+w_{2}\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Since by Lemma
3.1

u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma))})
we also have
\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)})
. Since
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}
were arbitrary we find that
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
.
∎

4.
Regularity inheritance (Proof of Theorem
1.6
)

Next we address the question of regularity inheritance for our fourth order problem.
To this end we first need some notation. We say that a continuous function
f:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
lies in
C^{1,1}(\Gamma)
if
f\in C^{1}(\Gamma)
and there exists
L>0
such that

(4.1)

|\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)-\nabla_{\Gamma}f(y)|\leq L|x-y|\quad\forall x,y\in\Gamma.

Here all norms are taken in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nabla_{\Gamma}f(x)
is defined as in
[
Simon
, p. 42]
(and always looked at as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by means of the usual identification
T_{x}\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We now turn to an extension result which will be of use for the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2,1}
. Then each function in
f\in C^{1,1}(\Gamma)

can be extended to a function in
W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{1,1}(\Gamma)
and
L>0
be as in (
4.1
). By
[
GilTru
, Section 14.6]
and the fact that
\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2,1}
we know that for some suitably small
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\mathrm{dist}(\Gamma,\Omega^{C}))
the function
\Phi:\Gamma\times(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})\rightarrow\Omega
given by
\Phi(x):=x+t\nu(x)
is a
C^{1,1}
-regular diffeomorphism onto its image. In particular, for any fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the set
T:=\Phi(\Gamma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))
is an open neighborhood of
\Gamma
with
C^{1,1}
-boundary. Due to this fact we have
C^{1,1}(\bar{T})=W^{2,\infty}(T)
, cf.
[
Evans
, Chapter 5.8.2.(b)]
. One readily checks that
\Phi^{-1}:T\rightarrow\Gamma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
actually lies in
C^{1,1}(\overline{T};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, say
\Phi^{-1}
has Lipschitz constant
M_{0}>0
and
D\Phi^{-1}
has Lipschitz constant
M_{1}>0
on
\overline{T}
. Define now the functions
\Psi:T\rightarrow\Gamma
and
\eta:T\rightarrow(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
via the relation
\Phi^{-1}(z)=(\Psi(z),\eta(z))
for all
z\in T
and observe that both
\Psi
and
\eta
enjoy
C^{1,1}
-regularity on
\bar{T}
. Further, we define an extension
\bar{f}:T\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
f
via
\bar{f}(z):=f(\Psi(z)).

We show now that
\bar{f}\in C^{1,1}(\overline{T})
. To this end first notice that for
i=1,...,n
and all
z\in T
one has

(4.2)

\partial_{i}\bar{f}(z):=\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z)),\partial_{i}\Psi(z)\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now for
z_{1},z_{2}\in T
and
i=1,...,n
we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\bar{f}(z_{2})-\partial_{i}\bar{f}(z_{1})|=|\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{2})),\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{1})),\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{1})\rangle|

(4.4)

\displaystyle=|\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{2}))-\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{1})),\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{1})),\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{1})-\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})\rangle|

(4.5)

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{2}))-\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{1}))|\;|\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})|+|\nabla_{\Gamma}f(\Psi(z_{1}))|\;|\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{1})-\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})|

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq L|\Psi(z_{1})-\Psi(z_{2})|\;|\partial_{i}\Psi(z_{2})|+||f||_{C^{1}(\Gamma)}M_{1}|z_{2}-z_{1}|\leq(LM_{0}^{2}+||f||_{C^{1}(\Gamma)}M_{1})|z_{2}-z_{1}|.

We conclude that
\bar{f}\in C^{1,1}(\bar{T})=W^{2,\infty}(T)
. As
T\subset\Omega
is an open neighborhood of
\Gamma
one readily checks by a suitable cutoff argument that also an extension in
W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
can be found.
∎

Making use of this and the already derived auxiliary equation (
AUX1
) for the second derviatives of solutions, we are able to prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Assume

.
Following the lines of the proof of Theorem

for some

\nabla w_{1}\in BV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Moreover, one has
\nabla w_{2}\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))\subset BV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. All in all we infer
\nabla(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)=\nabla w_{1}+\nabla w_{2}\in BV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Hence,
\partial^{3}_{ijk}u\in BV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
for all
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
. The fact that
u
is smooth on
\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)
and also in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
(cf. Lemma
3.1
) finally implies that
D^{3}u\in BV(\Omega)
.
∎

4.1.
SBV-Regularity for
D^{3}u

The following
SBV
-regularity statement for the higher order equation follows immediately from the results in Section
2.2
and the auxiliary problem that has been formed in (
AUX1
).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2,1}
and
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
,
p>n
. Further, let
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
1.2
) with
\Gamma,Q
as above.
Then
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
and
D^{3}u\in SBV(\Omega)
. Moreover,
J_{\partial^{3}_{ijk}u}\subset\Gamma
for all
i,j,k=1,...,n
.

Proof.

We conclude again following the lines of the proof of Theorem
1.2
(up to (
AUX1
)) that
(distributionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) one has for all
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}

(4.9)

\Delta(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)=\nu_{i}\nu_{j}Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma+f_{ij}

for some
f_{ij}\in L^{p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Notice once again that there exists
w\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
auch that
\Delta w=f
. Therefore (distibutionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
) one has

(4.10)

\Delta(\partial^{2}_{ij}u-w)=\nu_{i}\nu_{j}Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma.

With (a slight adaptation of) Corollary
2.11
one concludes that
\nabla(\partial^{2}_{ij}u-w)\in SBV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
and hence (since
\nabla w\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))\subset SBV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
) one has
\nabla\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in SBV_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Since also
\nabla\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)})
(cf. Lemma
3.1
), we find that
\nabla\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in SBV(\Omega)
. The claim follows.
∎

5.
Optimal regularity for the biharmonic Alt-Caffarelli problem

The biharmonic Alt-Caffarelli problem deals with the minimization of

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+|\{u>0\}|

among all
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
such that
u-u_{0}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
for some
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u_{0}>0
. Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth domain. It has raised some interest in recent literature, cf.
[
Serena1
]
,
[
Serena2
]
,
[
AltCaffarelliMarius
]
.
Minimizers have to balance out two competing interests: On the one hand bending has to be minimized, but on the other hand minimizers must stay below the zero level on a large set. The set
\Gamma:=\{u=0\}
is a
free boundary
of the problem, where minimizers lose regularity due to the fact that
\mathcal{E}
is not Frechet differentiable with respect to perturbations whose support intersects
\Gamma
.

[

.

The behavior of minimizers in dimension
n=2
is studied in
[
AltCaffarelliMarius
]
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
AltCaffarelliMarius
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Suppose that
n=2
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth domain and
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),u_{0}>0
. Then each minimizer of the problem (
5.1
) lies in
C^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfies
\nabla u\neq 0
on the free boundary
\Gamma=\{u=0\}
. Moreover,
\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\partial G_{i}
for finitely many disjoint domains
G_{i}
with
C^{2}
-smooth boundary (whose boundaries are also disjoint). Furthermore, the minimizer satisfies the equation

(5.2)

\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\phi\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

In the language of this article, each minimizer
u
is a weak solution of the equation

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\\
u=u_{0},\;\Delta u=0&\mathrm{on}\;\partial\Omega\end{cases}

where
Q=Q(u)=-\frac{1}{2|\nabla u|}\big|_{\Gamma}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
.

By the discussion before (
1.4
) and Remark
2.2
one already obtains that
v=-\Delta u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
q<\infty
[whereupon elliptic regularity yields
u\in W^{3,q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
].
With Theorem
1.2
we are finally able to establish the optimal
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity.

Theorem 5.2

(Optimal regularity for the biharmonic Alt-Caffarelli problem)
.

Let
u\in W^{2,2}(\Omega)
be a minimizer for the problem (
5.1
) in dimension
n=2
. Then
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
and
u\not\in C^{3}(\Omega)
. Furthermore,
D^{3}u\in SBV(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We already know that
u\in W^{3,q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
and that
\nabla u\neq 0
on
\Gamma=\{u=0\}
. Notice in particular that
\nabla u\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
, and hence also
\nabla u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
\alpha\in[0,1)
.
Thus there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\nabla u\neq 0
on
B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
. Choose
\psi\in C^{2}_{0}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
such that
\psi\equiv 1
on
\Gamma
.
We infer that
\tilde{Q}:=\tilde{Q}(u):=-\frac{1}{2|\nabla u|}\psi
lies in
W^{2,q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in[1,\infty)
(in particular for some
q>n
) and is an extension of
Q(u)
to
\Omega
. In particular, (
5.3
) is equivalent to

(5.4)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=\tilde{Q}\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\mathrm{in}\;\Omega\\
u=u_{0},\;\Delta u=0&\mathrm{on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If now
\Gamma=\partial G
for a single
C^{2}
-domain
G
, Theorem
1.2
implies that (
5.4
) has a solution
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega).
In the general case of multiple boundary components
\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}(\partial G_{i})
(disjoint union) one has to write
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(\partial G_{i})
and use the linearity of the equation to infer the asserted
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity. Since by Theorem
5.1
there are only finitely many connected components of
\Gamma
, the
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity is shown.
That
u\not\in C^{3}(\Omega)
follows from Lemma
2.5
, applied to
v=-\Delta u
.

To derive the
BV
-regularity of
D^{3}u
we intend to apply Theorem
1.6
. In order to do so, we need to show that
\Gamma=\{u=0\}
is a
C^{2,1}
-regular manifold. This is due to the fact that
\Gamma
is the zero level set of the function
u\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)=C^{2,1}(\Omega)
and
\nabla u\neq 0
on
\Gamma
. Therefore the situation (
5.4
) satisfies all requirements of Theorem
1.6
and we finally obtain that
D^{3}u\in BV(\Omega)
. Furthermore
SBV
-regularity readily follows from (
5.4
) and Corollary
4.2
.
∎

6.
Generalization to the polyharmonic case

The findings in the above article can be generalized to polyharmonic operators
(-\Delta)^{m}
,
m\geq 2
on smooth domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For this we need some more notation. A
boundary operator

B(x,D):C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\rightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
of order
\leq N
is a formal expression of the form

(6.1)

B(x,D)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},|\alpha|\leq N}b_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}\quad\textrm{where $b_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})$ for each multiindex $\alpha$.}

Notice that Sobolev trace theory implies that each boundary operator of order
\leq N
extends to a continuous map
B(x,D):W^{N+1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
Q\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
,
p>n
and
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2}
. Let
B_{j}(x,D):W^{2m-3,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
,
j=1,...,m-1
be boundary operators of order
N_{j}\leq 2m-4
such that with
B_{m}(x,D):=(-\Delta)^{m-1}
the set
(B_{j}(x,D))_{j=1}^{m}
are
complementing boundary conditions
in the sense of
[
GGS
, Definition 2.9]
. A function
u\in W^{2m-2,2}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution to

(6.2)

\begin{cases}\;\;\;(-\Delta)^{m}u=Q\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma&\textrm{in }\Omega,\\
\;B_{j}(x,D)u=B_{j}(x,D)u_{0},\;\;j=1,...,m-1&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega,\\
B_{m}(x,D)u=0&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

if
B_{j}(x,D)(u-u_{0})=0
for all
j=1,...,m-1
and

(6.3)

\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{m-1}u]\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Gamma}Q\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\quad\forall\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})\cap W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

The regularity theorem we can show here is

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
u\in W^{2m-2,2}(\Omega)
be as in Definition
6.1
. Then
u\in W^{2m-1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction over
m
. For
m=2
the statement is Theorem
1.2
. Next let
m>2
and
i,j\in\{1,...,m\}
. Let
\epsilon>0
be chosen as in Section
2.1
.
For
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
we can compute

(6.4)

\int[(-\Delta)^{m-2}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)]\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int[(-\Delta)^{m-2}u]\Delta(\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int[(-\Delta)^{m-1}u]\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

Defining
h_{m}:=[(-\Delta)^{m-1}u]+\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|
where
d_{\Gamma}
is as in Section
2.1
we obtain

(6.5)

\int[(-\Delta)^{m-2}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)]\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int h_{m}\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

Using Lemma
2.3
we find that

(6.6)

\int[(-\Delta)^{m-2}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)]\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int h_{m}\partial^{2}_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int g_{ij}\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

for some
g_{ij}\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Next we show that
h_{m}\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. To this end observe that for each
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
one has (by (
6.3
) and Lemma
2.3
)

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int h_{m}\Delta\psi\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[(-\Delta)^{m-1}u]\Delta\psi\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{Q}{2}|d_{\Gamma}|\Delta\psi\;\mathrm{d}x

(6.8)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}Q\psi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Gamma}Q\psi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int(\mathrm{tr}(g))\psi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int(\mathrm{tr}(g))\psi\;\mathrm{d}x,

where the matrix
g=(g_{ij})\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
is as above. Elliptic regularity yields then that
h_{m}\in W^{2,p}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
and (
6.6
) can be reformulated to

(6.9)

\int(-\Delta)^{m-2}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)\Delta\phi\;\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Gamma}Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\phi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int(\partial^{2}_{ij}h_{m}-g_{ij})\phi\;\mathrm{d}x.

Distibutionally in
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))^{\prime}
there holds therefore

(AUX2)

(-\Delta)^{m-1}(\partial^{2}_{ij}u)=Q\nu_{i}\nu_{j}\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Gamma-(\partial^{2}_{ij}h_{m}-g_{ij}).

Using the induction assumption and the fact that
\partial^{2}_{ij}h_{m}-g_{ij}\in L^{p}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
one can proceed as in the discussion after (
AUX1
) to prove that
\partial^{2}_{ij}u\in W^{2(m-1)-1,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
, i.e.
D^{2}u\in W^{2m-3,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
, implying
u\in W^{2m-1,\infty}_{loc}(B_{\epsilon}(\Gamma))
. Similar to Lemma
3.1
one can also deduce that
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega\setminus B_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}(\Gamma)})
(this time using
[
GGS
, Theorem 2.20]
), yielding that
u\in W^{2m-1,\infty}(\Omega)
.
∎

Keeping using the auxiliary equation (
AUX2
), one can also deduce generalizations of the
BV
-regularity result (Theorem
1.6
) and also of the
SBV
-regularity result in Section
4.1
. All in all one obtains that for interfaces
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{\prime}\in C^{2,1}
one has
D^{2m-1}u\in SBV(\Omega)
. The details can be safely omitted as they follow the lines of the previous arguments (with the usage of the auxiliary equation (
AUX2
) instead of (
AUX1
) and the usage of elliptic regularity for
(-\Delta)^{m-1}
instead of
(-\Delta)^{1}
).

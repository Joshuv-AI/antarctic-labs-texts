A Hodge filtration of logarithmic vector fields for well-generated complex reflection groups
Takuro Abe, Gerhard Röhrle, Christian Stump, Masahiko Yoshinaga

Abstract
. Given an irreducible well-generated complex reflection group, we construct an explicit basis for the module of vector fields with logarithmic poles along its reflection arrangement. This construction yields in particular a Hodge filtration of that module. Our approach is based on a detailed analysis of a flat connection applied to the primitive vector field. This generalizes and unifies analogous results for real reflection groups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20F55, Secondary: 52C35, 32S25

1.
Introduction

The study of vector fields with logarithmic poles along the reflection arrangement of a finite Coxeter group inside a real vector space has been a particularly active and fruitful area of recent research.
Most importantly, Abe-Terao use Saito’s primitive derivation to construct an explicit basis of this vector field in
[
AT10
]
, thereby extending Saito’s Hodge filtration of invariant polynomial derivations to a Hodge filtration of the module of invariant vector fields with logarithmic poles.
Also, Wakamiko identified the concept of a universal vector field as a crucial ingredient in his construction of an explicit basis in
[
Wak11
, Sec. 2]
.

Based on a recent extension of Saito’s primitive derivation and Hodge filtration to well-generated unitary reflection groups in
[
HMRS19
]
, we establish analogues of the above constructions in this more general setting.
Specifically, we provide a framework to extend
[
AT10
, Thms. 1.1 & 1.2]
to well-generated unitary reflection groups (
Theorem

4.2
and
Corollary

4.3
), and derive universality results generalizing
[
Wak11
, Sec. 2]
(
Theorems

4.17
,
4.19
and
4.22
).
Because of the new explicit form of the flat connection in
[
HMRS19
]
, the approach we provide here has not appeared in the literature even in the real case, simplifying several arguments.

We give a brief comment on the terminology of a “Hodge filtration” in our context. This was introduced
by K. Saito in
[
Sai83
]
. The primitive form is a special element in the
relative de Rham cohomology group for the deformation of an isolated hypersurface
singularity, which satisfies lots of nice properties.
For simple singularities of type
ADE
, the parameter space
of the deformation is identified
with the quotient of the Cartan subalgebra by the Weyl group.
For a given logarithmic vector field, we can differentiate (Gauss-Manin connection)
the primitive form by the vector field, which gives an identification between the module
of logarithmic vector fields and the relative de Rham cohomology group.
The primitive derivation defines a natural filtration on which the primitive
derivation acts by degree shift by one. This structue is very similar to
the behavior of Hodge filtrations on the cohomology of the fiber with respect to
the Gauss-Manin connection of a proper smooth holomorphic map
(the so-called Griffith transversality). This is the reason why the filtration
on the module of logarithmic vector fields is called the Hodge filtration.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section

2
contains preliminaries about complex hyperplane arrangements with
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued multiplicities, and recalls the needed background on unitary reflection groups and the necessary properties of flat connections for well-generated unitary reflection groups.
In
Section

3
we provide an analogue of Saito’s criterion for
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued multiplicities, introduce universal vector fields and derive several of their properties.

Section

4
contains the main results of this paper as presented above.

For the benefit of the reader, in our proofs we have separated the properties that follow directly from the existence of a universal vector field from those that only hold in the case of well-generated complex reflection groups (where we do have such a universal vector field) see
Sections

3.2
and
4.2
.

Recall that a complex reflection group
W
is called well-generated provided it is generated by
\ell
reflections, where
\ell
is the dimension of its reflection
representation.
See Section
2.2
for an equivalent definition used throughout the paper; here the exponents and coexponents of
W
add up to the Coxeter number of
W
in a particular fashion, see (
2.6
).

We finish this introduction with a brief discussion of the crucial differences and similarities of the situations for real and complex reflection arrangements; for definitions we refer to the sections below.
Denote by
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
a general
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued multiplicity function on the reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
of a well-generated irreducible unitary reflection group
W
, and by
\omega:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
the multiplicity function assigning to a reflecting hyperplane
H
the order of its pointwise stabilizer,
\omega(H)=e_{H}=|W_{H}|
.

(1)

If
W
is real, then
\omega\equiv 2
.
In particular, for the module of derivations
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-1)
of
{\mathscr{A}}
with multiplicity
\nu-1
, we have

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-1)=\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-\omega+1).

It turns out that the counterpart of the module of derivations
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-1)
that is used in the literature in the real case is the module
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-\omega+1)
in the general complex case.
One crucial instance, where a
(-1)
-multiplicity in the real case is replaced by a
(-\omega+1)
-multiplicity in the complex case, is demonstrated in
Theorem

4.19
, which generalizes the analogous result from the real case in
[
Wak11
, Thm. 2.7]
.

(2)

It is well known that in the real case there is an isomorphism of graded modules

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-1)=\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\omega+1)\cong\Omega({\mathscr{A}},1),

where
\Omega({\mathscr{A}},1)
is the module of differential
1
-forms. As explained in
Remark

4.5
, by degree comparison, this isomorphism does not extend to complex reflection arrangements that are not the complexification of a real arrangement.

(3)

The explicit form of the flat connection that was exhibited in
[
HMRS19
]
, see
Proposition

2.17
, is the crucial ingredient in the construction of the bases in
Theorem

4.2
and of the Hodge filtration in
Corollary

4.3
.
Using this explicit form and a
system of flat invariants
and of
flat derivations
has the additional benefit of simplifying the arguments for real reflection arrangements.
We remark that, while the existence of flat derivations has been known for some time, see
[
Bes15
]
, the existence of flat invariants for well-generated complex reflection groups was only discovered quite recently in
[
KMS20
]
.

(4)

This paper does not deal with equivariant multiplicities (multiplicities that are constant along hyperplane orbits) as provided in the real case in
[
ATW12
]
.
That construction is based on a case-by-case analysis of primitive vector fields along reflection subgroups generated by orbits of reflections.
We hope for a general conceptual approach to such equivariant multiplicities.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Multi-arrangements of hyperplanes and their derivations

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension
\ell
and fix a Hermitian form
I:V\times V\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
on
V
.
Let
S=\operatorname{Sym}(V^{*})
denote the ring of
polynomial functions
on
V
and let
F
denote its quotient field of
rational functions
.
If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, we identify
S
with the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
and
F
with the field of rational functions
{\mathbb{C}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
.
Letting
S_{p}
denote the
{\mathbb{C}}
-subspace of
S
consisting of the homogeneous polynomials of degree
p
(along with
0
),
S
is naturally
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded by
S=\oplus_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S_{p}
, where we set
S_{p}=0
for
p<0
.
Denote by
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
the
S
-module of
{\mathbb{C}}
-derivations
of
S
, and by
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
the
F
-module of
{\mathbb{C}}
-derivations
of
F
.
Then
\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{\ell}}
is an
S
-basis of
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
and an
F
-basis of
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
.
We say that
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
is
homogeneous of polynomial degree
p-q
provided
\theta=\sum\frac{f_{i}}{g_{i}}\partial_{x_{i}}
, where
f_{i}\in S_{p}
and
g_{i}\in S_{q}\setminus\{0\}
for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
In this case we write
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta=p-q
.
Recall that, for
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
, we have
\theta=\theta(x_{1})\partial_{x_{1}}+\dots+\theta(x_{\ell})\partial_{x_{\ell}}
.
The
Saito matrix
of
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
is given by

M(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}):=\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{1}(x_{1})&\cdots&\theta_{1}(x_{\ell})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\theta_{\ell}(x_{1})&\cdots&\theta_{\ell}(x_{\ell})\end{bmatrix},

see
[
OT92
, Def. 4.11]
.
That is, the Saito matrix collects in the
i
-th row the coefficients of
\theta_{i}
in the
F
-basis
\{\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{\ell}}\}
of
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
.

Extending Ziegler’s concept of an
{\mathbb{N}}
-valued multiplicity function from
[
Zie89
]
, a
multi-arrangement

({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
together with a
multiplicity function

\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
assigning to each hyperplane
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
a multiplicity
\nu(H)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We use the term multi-arrangement even though we allow hyperplanes to have negative multiplicities, while disregarding hyperplanes of multiplicity zero.
We only consider
central
multi-arrangements
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
, i.e.,
0\in H
for every
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
In this case, we fix
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
with
H=\ker(\alpha_{H})
for
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
which we scale so that
I^{*}(\alpha_{H},\alpha_{H})=1
.
The
defining rational function

Q({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is

Q({\mathscr{A}},\nu):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}

which we sometimes abbreviate as
Q_{\nu}:=Q({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
We set
|\nu|:=\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\nu(H)
to be the degree of
Q_{\nu}
and separate
Q_{\nu}
via
Q_{\nu}=Q_{+}/Q_{-}
with

(2.1)

Q_{+}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in{\mathscr{A}}\\
\nu(H)>0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)},\qquad Q_{-}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in{\mathscr{A}}\\
\nu(H)<0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{H}^{-\nu(H)}.

To deal with general multiplicity functions on the arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
, we also set

Q_{\mathscr{A}}:=Q({\mathscr{A}},1)=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}

to be the defining polynomial of the
simple arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}
which we later abbreviate as
Q=Q_{\mathscr{A}}
when
{\mathscr{A}}
is clear from the context.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
be the localization of the ring
S
at the prime ideal
\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle
.
Setting
S^{\prime}:=S[Q_{\mathscr{A}}^{-1}]
, the
S
-module of
logarithmic vector fields
on
{\mathscr{A}}
is defined by

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty):=\big\{\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S^{\prime}}\mid\theta(\beta)\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}\text{ for }H\in{\mathscr{A}}\text{ and }\beta\in V^{*}\text{ with }I^{*}(\alpha_{H},\beta)=0\big\}.

Given a multiplicity function
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
, define the
S
-module of
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
-derivations
by

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu):=\big\{\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}\text{ for }H\in{\mathscr{A}}\big\}.

Observe that in general this definition depends on the chosen Hermitian form, while
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},0)={{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
.
We remark also that the given definition is an equivalent reformulation of the definitions given in
[
AT10
,
Wak11
]
.

We record the following basic containment property for multiplicities
\mu\geq\nu
, i.e.,
\mu(H)\geq\nu(H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\mu>\nu
if and only if
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subsetneqq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
In particular, we have
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subseteq{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
if and only if
\mu\geq 0
.

Proof.

The reverse implication of the asserted equivalence, as well as the fact that
\mu\geq\nu
implies
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
are immediate from the definition.
For
\mu>\nu
, let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
with
\mu(H)>\nu(H)
.
If
\nu(H)\geq 0
, then

Q_{+}\partial_{\alpha_{H}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)\setminus\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
,
and if
\nu(H)<0
, then
\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}Q_{+}\partial_{\alpha_{H}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)\setminus\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, where
Q_{+}
is as in (
2.1
).
∎

The multi-arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is
free
if
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is a free
S
-module.
In this case,
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
admits a basis
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
of homogeneous derivations, see
[
OT92
, Thm A.20]
.
While the
\theta_{i}
’s are not unique, their polynomial degrees
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{i}
are, see
[
OT92
, Prop. A.24]
.
The multiset of these polynomial degrees is the multiset of
exponents
of the free multi-arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
It is denoted by

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\nu):=\big\{\operatorname{pdeg}(\theta_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{pdeg}(\theta_{\ell})\big\}.

2.2.
Unitary reflection groups and their reflection arrangements

Let
W
be an irreducible unitary reflection group with reflection representation
V\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
, and let
I
be the associated
W
-invariant Hermitian inner form.
We refer to
[
HMRS19
]
and the references therein for all necessary background material on reflection groups.
Denote the set of reflections of
W
by
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(W)
, and the associated reflection arrangement in
V
by
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
.
For
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, let
e_{H}
denote the order of the pointwise stabilizer of
H
in
W
.
The
Coxeter number
of
W
is given by

(2.4)

h=h_{W}:=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}e_{H}=\frac{1}{\ell}\Big(|\mathcal{R}|+|{\mathscr{A}}|\Big),

generalizing the usual Coxeter number of a real reflection group to irreducible unitary reflection groups.

Before proceeding, we record, without proof, the following well-known property of the
W
-action on polynomial functions.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
g\in S
and let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
with corresponding reflection
s\in\mathcal{R}
with
{\operatorname{Fix}}(s)=H
of order
e_{H}
.
Let
\epsilon
be a primitive
e_{H}
-th root of unity such that
s(\alpha_{H})=\epsilon\alpha_{H}
.
For
1\leq k<e_{H}
, we have

s(g)=\epsilon^{k}g\ \Longrightarrow\ g\in\alpha_{H}^{k}\cdot S.

Results of Shephard and Todd
[
ST54
]
and of Chevalley
[
Che55
]
distinguish unitary reflection groups as those finite subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(V)
for which the invariant subalgebra of the action on the symmetric algebra
S=\operatorname{Sym}(V^{*})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
yields again a polynomial algebra,

S^{W}\cong{\mathbb{C}}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}]

for homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}
of degrees
d_{i}=\deg f_{i}
with
d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{\ell}
.
Let
\exp(W)=\{e_{1}\leq\dots\leq e_{\ell}\}
be the
exponents
of
W
, where
e_{i}=d_{i}-1

and let
\operatorname{coexp}(W)=\{e_{1}^{*}\leq\dots\leq e_{\ell}^{*}\}
be the
coexponents
of
W
,
cf.

[
OT92
, Def. 6.50]
.
The group
W
is called
well-generated
if it is generated by
\ell
reflections, or, equivalently, if

(2.6)

e_{i}+e_{\ell+1-i}^{*}=h\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq\ell,

see
[
OS80
]
.
Terao showed in
[
Ter80
]
that the reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
of
W
is free,
and that the exponents coincide with the coexponents of
W
,
cf.

[
OT92
, Thm. 6.60]
,

(2.7)

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\operatorname{coexp}(W).

This shows that
e_{1}^{*}=1
(coming from the
Euler derivation
, see below), implying that
e_{\ell}=h-1
in the case of well-generated groups.

For later usage, we recall from
[
HMRS19
, Sec. 3]
the diagonal matrix

(2.8)

B_{\infty}:=\tfrac{1}{h}\diag(e_{i})-{1\!\!1}_{\ell}.

Next we recall from
[
HMRS19
]
the
order multiplicity

\omega
of the reflection arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
defined by
\omega(H)=e_{H}
for
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
In other words, the multiplicities are chosen so that the defining polynomial
Q({\mathscr{A}},\omega)
of the multi-arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\omega)
is the
discriminant
of
W
,
cf.

[
OT92
, Def. 6.44]
,

Q({\mathscr{A}},\omega)=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}(W)}\alpha_{H}^{e_{H}}=QJ,

where
Q=Q_{\mathscr{A}}=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}
as before, and
J=J_{\mathscr{A}}:=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{e(H)-1}
.

2.3.
Flat connections for well-generated unitary reflection groups

Throughout this subsection let
W
be a well-generated unitary reflection group.
Let
\nabla:{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}\times{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
be the connection defined by

(2.9)

\nabla_{\theta}(\phi)=\sum_{i}\theta(p_{i})\partial_{x_{i}}

for
\theta,\phi\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
with
\phi=\sum p_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
, or equivalently, an affine connection which has
\partial_{x_{i}}
as a flat section, i.e.,
\nabla_{\theta}(\partial_{x_{i}})=0
.
Recall that
\nabla
is
F
-linear in the first parameter and
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear in the second, satisfying the Leibniz rule

\nabla_{\theta}(p\phi)=\theta(p)\phi+p\nabla_{\theta}(\phi)

for
\theta,\phi\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
and
p\in F
.
Alternatively, this can be characterized by

(2.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}(\phi)(\alpha)=\theta(\phi(\alpha))

for all
\alpha\in V^{*}
.
Observe that for

\theta,\phi
homogeneous, (
2.9
)
implies that the derivation
\nabla_{\theta}(\phi)
is again homogeneous with
polynomial degree

(2.11)

\operatorname{pdeg}\big(\nabla_{\theta}(\phi)\big)=\operatorname{pdeg}(\theta)+\operatorname{pdeg}(\phi)-1.

Let
F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{1},\ldots,F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{\ell}
be the special homogeneous fundamental invariants in
{\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{x}}]
with
{\textbf{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
, as given in
[
KMS20
, Thm. 5.5]
.
Recall that
\deg\big(F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{i}\big)=d_{i}
and

{\mathbb{C}}[F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{1},\ldots,F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{\ell}]\cong S^{W}.

Consider indeterminates
{\textbf{t}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})
together with the map
t_{i}\mapsto F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{i}
giving an isomorphism

R:={\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{t}}]\cong{\mathbb{C}}[F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{1},\ldots,F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{\ell}].

By slight abuse of notation, here
and in the rest of the paper, the variable
t_{i}
and its image
F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{i}
under this isomorphism are identified.
Set moreover
T:={\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{t}}^{\prime}]={\mathbb{C}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell-1}]
, the subring of
{\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{t}}]
generated by
{\textbf{t}}^{\prime}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell-1})
.

As usual, set

J_{\partial{\textbf{t}}/\partial{\textbf{x}}}:=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\partial_{x_{\ell}}\end{pmatrix}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})=\begin{bmatrix}\partial t_{1}/\partial x_{1}&\cdots&\partial t_{\ell}/\partial x_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\partial t_{1}/\partial x_{\ell}&\cdots&\partial t_{\ell}/\partial x_{\ell}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{x}}]^{\ell\times\ell}

with inverse matrix
J_{\partial{\textbf{x}}/\partial{\textbf{t}}}:=J_{\partial{\textbf{t}}/\partial{\textbf{x}}}^{-1}=(\partial_{t_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{t_{\ell}})^{\mathrm{tr}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
.
It is well-known that
\det J_{\partial{\textbf{t}}/\partial{\textbf{x}}}\doteq J=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{e_{H}-1}
, see
[
OT92
, Thm. 6.42]
.
Here and elsewhere the symbol
\doteq
denotes, as usual, equality up to a non-zero complex constant factor.

The
primitive vector field

D:=\partial_{t_{\ell}}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}
is given by

(2.12)

D=\det J_{\partial{\textbf{x}}/\partial{\textbf{t}}}\begin{vmatrix}\frac{\partial t_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial t_{\ell-1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial t_{1}}{\partial x_{\ell}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial t_{\ell-1}}{\partial x_{\ell}}&\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}\\
\end{vmatrix}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}.

In particular,
D
is homogeneous of degree
\operatorname{pdeg}(D)=-e_{\ell}=-(h-1)
, where we observe that
h=d_{\ell}>d_{\ell-1}
.
The primitive vector field
D
is thus, up to a non-zero complex constant, independent of the given choice of fundamental invariants.

Consider
X:=V\big/W=\spec({\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{t}}])
and let
\Delta({\textbf{t}})\in R
be the discriminant of
W
given by

\Delta\big(F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{1}({\textbf{x}}),\ldots,F^{\mathrm{fl}}_{\ell}({\textbf{x}})\big)=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{e_{H}}

with vanishing locus
\mathcal{H}:=\{\overline{p}\in X\mid\Delta(\overline{p})=0\}
,
cf.

[
OT92
, Def. 6.44]
.
Let
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}
be the
R
-module of logarithmic vector fields, and let

{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta):=\big\{\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}\mid\theta\Delta\in R\Delta\big\}

be the module of logarithmic vector fields along
\mathcal{H}
.
We have an
R
-isomorphism between such logarithmic vector fields and
W
-invariant
S
-derivations,

(2.13)

{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta)\cong{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}^{W}},

and
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta)
is a free
R
-module,
cf.

[
OT92
, Cor. 6.58]
.

Bessis showed in
[
Bes15
, Thm. 2.4]
that there exists a
system of flat homogeneous derivations

\{\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell}\}
of
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta)
with
\operatorname{pdeg}\eta_{i}=e_{i}^{*}
being the
i
-th coexponent of
W
.
This means, its Saito matrix

(2.14)

M_{\eta}:=M(\eta_{\ell},\ldots,\eta_{1})=\begin{bmatrix}\eta_{\ell}(t_{1})&\cdots&\eta_{\ell}(t_{\ell})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{1}(t_{1})&\cdots&\eta_{1}(t_{\ell})\end{bmatrix}

decomposes as

(2.15)

\displaystyle M_{\eta}=t_{\ell}{1\!\!1}_{\ell}+M^{(0)}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime})

with
M^{(0)}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\textbf{t}}^{\prime}]^{\ell\times\ell}
.
As before, we have
(\eta_{\ell},\ldots,\eta_{1})^{\mathrm{tr}}=M_{\eta}(\partial_{t_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{t_{\ell}})^{\mathrm{tr}}
.
Moreover, we obtain that
\Delta({\textbf{t}})
is a monic polynomial in
t_{\ell}
with coefficients in
T
, i.e.,

\Delta({\textbf{t}})=t_{\ell}^{\ell}+a_{\ell-1}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime})t_{\ell}^{\ell-1}+\ldots+a_{1}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime})t_{\ell}+a_{0}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime}),

where
a_{i}({\textbf{t}}^{\prime})\in T
for

.

\eta_{1}=\tfrac{1}{h}\sum d_{i}t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}

mapped to the (scaled)

(2.16)

E:=\tfrac{1}{h}\sum x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}^{W}}

under the isomorphism in (

), see
[

.

We recall from
[

, Prop. 3.15]

It involves the diagonal matrix

given in (

Proposition 2.17
.

We have
T
-isomorphisms

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!D}

\displaystyle:{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta)\longrightarrow{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}

\displaystyle:{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}\longrightarrow{{\operatorname{Der}}_{R}}(-\log\Delta)

given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\!D}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t_{1}}\\

3.
A general version of Saito’s criterion and universal vector fields

Throughout this section, we consider a general multi-arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
in
V\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}
with multiplicity function
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.

3.1.
A general version of Saito’s criterion

For later usage we generalize Ziegler’s version of Saito’s criterion
[
Zie89
, Thm. 8]
for multi-arrangements from
{\mathbb{N}}
- to
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued multiplicities.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a multiplicity function
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
is an
S
-basis of
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.

(ii)

\det\big(M(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell})\big)\doteq Q({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.

Moreover, if each
\theta_{i}
is homogeneous, then (i) and (ii) are equivalent
to

(iii)

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
are linearly independent over
S
and
\sum\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{i}=|\nu|
.

We require two lemmas for the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.
Recall from (
2.1
) that we write
Q_{\nu}=Q_{+}/Q_{-}
with
Q_{+},Q_{-}\in S

for the defining rational function
Q_{\nu}=Q({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
Then
Q_{-}\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
.

Proof.

Fix
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}
be an orthonormal basis of
V^{*}
.
Set
Q:=Q_{\mathscr{A}}
and
Q_{H}:=Q/\alpha_{H}
.
Write
\theta=\sum_{i}(f_{i}/Q^{n})\partial_{x_{i}}
with
f_{i}\in S
and
n\geq 0
.
Then
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
means that

f_{1}\in\alpha_{H}^{n+\nu(H)}\cdot S\qquad f_{i}\in\alpha_{H}^{n}\cdot S\text{ for }2\leq i\leq\ell,

or, equivalently, that
\theta=\sum_{i}(f^{\prime}_{i}/Q_{H}^{n})\partial_{x_{i}}
with

(3.3)

f^{\prime}_{1}\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\cdot S\qquad f^{\prime}_{i}\in S\text{ for }2\leq i\leq\ell.

With
Q_{H}^{-n}\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
, we obtain that
Q_{-}f_{i}\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
and thus
\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}}
.
Since this holds for any
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, we conclude that
Q_{-}\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
.
∎

In the subsequent arguments, we use the following abbreviation.
Given
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}
, we set

\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}:=\det\big(M(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell})\big)=\det\big(\theta_{i}(x_{j})\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}\in F.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
and let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
Then

\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}\in Q_{\nu}\cdot S.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider the orthonormal basis
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}
of
V^{*}
.
Since
\theta_{i}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
, we have

\theta_{i}(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\cdot{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{i}(x_{j})\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq\ell.

This implies that
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\cdot{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
.
As this holds for any
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

First we show that (
ii
) implies (
i
).
As
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}\neq 0
, we readily see that
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
are
S
-independent. So we aim to show that they span
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
To this end, let
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
Since
\theta_{i}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
, we have
Q_{-}\theta_{i}\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
by
Lemma

3.2
.
Let
M:=M(Q_{-}\theta_{1},\ldots,Q_{-}\theta_{\ell})
and observe that

\det(M)=Q_{-}\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge Q_{-}\theta_{\ell}\doteq Q_{-}^{\ell}Q_{\nu}.

Set
N=M^{-1}
and
\tilde{N}=\det(M)N\in S^{\ell\times\ell}
.
Since

\det(M)\partial_{x_{i}}=\sum_{j}\tilde{N}_{ij}Q_{-}\theta_{j},

we see that
Q_{-}\theta_{1},\ldots,Q_{-}\theta_{\ell}
generate
Q_{-}^{\ell}Q_{\nu}\cdot{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
over
S
.
With
Q_{-}\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
, we obtain

\det(M)Q_{-}\theta=Q_{+}Q_{-}^{\ell}\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}Q_{-}\theta_{i}

for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in S
, or, equivalently,
Q_{+}Q_{-}^{\ell-1}\theta=\sum f_{i}\theta_{i}
.
It thus remains to show that each
f_{i}
is divisible by
Q_{+}Q_{-}^{\ell-1}=Q_{\nu}Q_{-}^{\ell}
.
We have

\displaystyle f_{i}Q_{+}

\displaystyle\doteq f_{i}Q_{-}(\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{i-1}\wedge f_{i}Q_{-}\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}

\displaystyle=Q_{+}Q_{-}^{\ell}(\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{i-1}\wedge\theta\wedge\theta_{i+1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell})

\displaystyle=Q_{+}Q_{-}^{\ell}g_{i}Q_{\nu},

for some
g_{i}\in S
given by
Lemma

3.4
.
This implies
f_{i}=Q_{\nu}Q_{-}^{\ell}g_{i}
, as desired.

We next show (
i
) implies (
ii
).
By
Lemma

3.4
, we may write
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}=fQ_{\nu}=fQ_{+}/Q_{-}
for some
f\in S
.
Since
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
are
S
-independent, we have
f\neq 0
.
We thus need to show that
f
is constant.
Now, let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and again assume that
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
are orthonormal coordinates.
Set

Q_{+}=\alpha_{H}^{m_{+}}Q^{\prime}_{+}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{-}=\alpha_{H}^{m_{-}}Q^{\prime}_{-}

such that
Q^{\prime}_{+}
and
Q^{\prime}_{-}
are both not divisible by
\alpha_{H}
.

First, observe that

Q_{+}\partial_{\alpha_{H}},Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{2}},\ldots,Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu),

by definition.
This implies

\displaystyle Q_{+}(Q^{\prime}_{+})^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle=Q_{+}\partial_{\alpha_{H}}\wedge Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\det(g_{ij})\big(\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{\ell}\big)

\displaystyle=gfQ_{+}/Q_{-}

where
(g_{ij})
is the matrix with entries in
S
expressing
Q_{+}\partial_{\alpha_{H}},Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{2}},\ldots,Q^{\prime}_{+}\partial_{x_{\ell}}
in terms of the basis
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{\ell}
, and where we wrote
g=\det(g_{ij})\in S
.
This means that
f
is a factor of
Q_{-}(Q^{\prime}_{+})^{\ell-1}
for the given
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
Repeating this argument for every hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
, we obtain that
f
is a factor of
Q_{-}
.

Second, we also observe that

\frac{Q_{+}}{\alpha_{H}^{m_{-}}}\partial_{\alpha_{H}},Q_{+}\partial_{x_{2}},\ldots,Q_{+}\partial_{x_{\ell}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)

and analogously deduce that

\frac{Q_{+}^{\ell}}{\alpha_{H}^{m_{-}}}=g^{\prime}fQ_{+}/Q_{-}

for some
g^{\prime}\in S
.
This now means that
f
is a factor of
Q^{\prime}_{-}(Q_{+})^{\ell-1}
for the given
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
Once again repeating this for every hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
, we obtain that
f
is a factor of
(Q_{+})^{\ell-1}
.
As
Q_{-}
and
Q_{+}
do not have any common non-scalar factors, we thus deduce that
f
is constant.

The argument for (
iii
) is standard.
If
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
forms a homogeneous
S
-basis of
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
, then (
iii
) is obviously satisfied.
Vice versa, (
iii
) implies that the determinant of
M(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell})
equals
fQ_{\nu}
for some
f\in S\setminus\{0\}
by independence, and that
\deg(f)=0
, because the degrees of
\det\big(M(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell})\big)
and of
Q_{\nu}
coincide.
∎

3.2.
Properties of universal vector fields on multi-arrangements

In this section, we define universal vector fields for general arrangements and arbitrary multiplicity functions and explore properties of logarithmic vector fields that follow from the existence of universal vector fields.
Throughout, we have separated the arguments as detailed as possible to clarify their exact interplay.
In
Section

4.2
, we exhibit natural occurrences and properties of universal vector fields in the case of reflection arrangements.
We start with the definition of universality depending on a multiplicity
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
which is a slight generalization of the
\mathbf{k}
-universality in the real case in
[
Wak11
, Def. 2.2]
.

Recall the definition of the affine connection
\nabla
in (
2.9
) having
\partial_{x_{i}}
as a flat section.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
be a multi-arrangement for a multiplicity function
\nu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
and let
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
be homogeneous.
Then
\zeta
is called
\nu
-universal
provided the map

\Phi_{\zeta}:{{\operatorname{Der}}_{F}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu);\quad\theta\longmapsto\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)

is an isomorphism of
S
-modules.

Observe that
\Phi_{\zeta}
is
S
-linear by definition.
The
\nu
-universality of
\zeta
thus means that
\Phi_{\zeta}:{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is well-defined and bijective.
In particular, this implies for an
\nu
-universal
\zeta
that
({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
is free and

\big\{\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}(\zeta)\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\big\}

is an
S
-module basis of
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.

We begin with the following observation from
[
Wak11
, Ex. 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The Euler derivation
E
defined in (
2.16
) is
\mathbf{0}
-universal.

Proof.

Since
\Phi_{E}(\delta)=\nabla_{\delta}(E)=\delta
for any
\delta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
, the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
and
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
.
Then
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
\beta\in V^{*}
such that
I^{*}(\alpha_{H},\beta)=0
.
Since
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
, we have
\zeta(\beta)=f/Q_{H}
for
f\in S
and
\widetilde{Q}_{H}=(\frac{Q}{\alpha_{H}})^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.
Using (
2.10
), we obtain

\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\beta)=\theta(f/\widetilde{Q}_{H})=\frac{1}{\widetilde{Q}_{H}^{2}}(\theta(f)\widetilde{Q}_{H}-f\theta(\widetilde{Q}_{H})).

Since
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
by assumption, we obtain
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\beta)\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
.
This holds for any
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and we deduce the statement.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and let
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
.
Then
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)
if and only if
\Phi_{\zeta}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
is well-defined.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)
.
We aim to show that
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
for all
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
By
Lemma

3.7
we have that
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
in this case.
The following argument is analogous to the proof of the previous lemma.
Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.
Since
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)
, we have that

\zeta(\alpha_{H})=\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}f/Q_{H}

for
f\in S
and
Q_{H}=(\frac{Q}{\alpha_{H}})^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.
Using (
2.10
), for
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})

\displaystyle=\theta\big(\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}f/Q_{H}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q_{H}^{2}}\big(\theta(\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1})fQ_{H}+\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}\theta(f)Q_{H}-\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}f\theta(Q_{H})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\big((\nu(H)+1)\theta(\alpha_{H})fQ_{H}+\alpha_{H}\theta(f)Q_{H}-\alpha_{H}f\theta(Q_{H})\big).

This yields
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
.
If
\mu(H)=1
, we moreover get
\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}\cdot S
by the defining property of
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
In this case, we thus get an additional factor
\alpha_{H}
in (
3.9
)
and obtain
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
.
In any event
\Phi_{\zeta}(\theta)
belongs to
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
.

Assume now that
\Phi_{\zeta}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
is well-defined.
Let
\tau
be the maximal multiplicity function such that
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\tau+1)
.
This is,

\zeta(\alpha_{H})=\alpha_{H}^{\tau(H)+1}f/Q_{H}

for
f\in S
and
Q_{H}=(\frac{Q}{\alpha_{H}})^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
, such that
f\notin\alpha_{H}\cdot S
.
As in (
3.9
), for
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
we obtain

(3.10)

\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})=\frac{\alpha_{H}^{\tau(H)}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\big((\tau(H)+1)\theta(\alpha_{H})fQ_{H}+\alpha_{H}\theta(f)Q_{H}-\alpha_{H}f\theta(Q_{H})\big).

Observe that
\theta=Q_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha_{H}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
If
\mu(H)=0
, then for
\theta=Q_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha_{H}}
, we have
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\tau(H)}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
but
\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)(\alpha_{H})\notin\alpha_{H}^{\tau(H)+1}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
, since the first summand in (
3.10
) is not divisible by
\alpha_{H}
while the other two are.
By the well-definedness of
\Phi_{\zeta}
, we thus have
\tau(H)\geq\nu(H)
.

Similarly, if
\mu(H)=1
, then the first summand (
3.10
) is divisible by
\alpha_{H}
but not by
\alpha_{H}^{2}
, while the other two are divisible by
\alpha_{H}^{2}
.
Again by the well-definedness of
\Phi_{\zeta}
, we have that
\tau(H)\geq\nu(H)
.
We conclude that
\tau\geq\nu
and so by definition, we get

\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\tau+1)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1).\qed

The following lemma is the analogue of
[
Wak11
, Prop. 2.6(3)]
in our setting.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\zeta
be
\nu
-universal.
Then
\zeta\notin\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu+1)
for any
\mu>\nu
.

Proof.

Let
\mu>\nu
and suppose that
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu+1)
.

Lemmas

3.8
and
2.3
then imply that
\Phi_{\zeta}({{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}})\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subsetneqq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
.
This contradicts the fact that
\Phi_{\zeta}({{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}})=\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
owed to the
\nu
-universality of
\zeta
.
∎

Lemmas

3.8
and
3.11
give the following theorem, generalizing
[
AY09
, Thm. 2]
to the complex case and to the more general notion of universality above, see also
[
Wak11
, Prop. 2.7]
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
and let
\zeta
be
\nu
-universal.
Then

\Phi_{\zeta}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)

is an isomorphism of
S
-modules.

Observe that in the theorem we do not require any freeness assumption on
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
Together with
Theorem

4.17
below, it thus generalizes
[
HMRS19
, Thm. 3.22]
.

Proof.

We already know that
\Phi_{\zeta}
is
S
-linear and well-defined by
Lemma

3.8
.
Moreover, the universality of
\zeta
implies that
\Phi_{\zeta}
is injective on
{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
and thus on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subseteq{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
.
It thus remains to show that
\Phi_{\zeta}
is surjective.
To this end, let
\phi\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
.
Since
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu)
, we may write
\phi=\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)
for some
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}}
.
We aim to show that
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
As in the proof of
Lemma

3.8
, we have

\zeta(\alpha_{H})=\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}f/Q_{H}

for some
f\in S
and
Q_{H}=(\frac{Q}{\alpha_{H}})^{n}
with
n\geq 0
. Since
\phi=\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\theta}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\Big[(\nu(H)+1)\theta(\alpha_{H})fQ_{H}+\alpha_{H}\big(\theta(f)Q_{H}-f\theta(Q_{H})\big)\Big]\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+\mu(H)}\cdot{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}.

Observe that
Lemma

3.11
implies that
f\notin\alpha_{H}\cdot S
by the
\nu
-universality of
\zeta
.

If
\mu(H)=0
, there is no condition from (
3.13
) on
\theta
, and so trivially

\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{0}\cdot S=S
, and if
\mu(H)=1
, then
\theta(\alpha_{H})\cdot f\in\alpha_{H}\cdot S
implying that
\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}\cdot S
, because
f
is not divisible by
\alpha_{H}
, by (
3.13
).
Consequently,
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, as desired.
∎

4.
A Hodge filtration and universality for reflection arrangements

For the remainder of the paper, we fix
W
to be a well-generated irreducible complex reflection group with reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
, and the order multiplicity
\omega:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
given by
\omega(H)=e_{H}
.
Recall from (
2.14
) the flat system of derivations
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell}
.
After having collected all necessary background material in
Sections

2.2
and
2.3
, we are now able to state and prove our main results.
For
1\leq j\leq\ell
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define

\xi_{j}^{(k)}:=\nabla_{\!D}^{k}(\eta_{j})

and set

\Xi^{(k)}:=\big\{\xi_{1}^{(k)},\dots,\xi_{\ell}^{(k)}\big\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Xi:=\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Xi^{(k)}.

Thanks to
[
HMRS19
, (3.16)]
, we make the crucial observation that

(4.1)

\xi_{j}^{(1)}\doteq\partial_{t_{\ell+1-j}}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq j\leq\ell.

Theorem 4.2
.

Theorem 4.7

Let

.
Then

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega)^{W}=\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega+1)^{W}.

In particular,
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega)
does not have a
W
-invariant basis.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega)^{W}
.
Fix
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}
be an orthonormal basis of
V^{*}
.
Write
\theta=\sum_{i}(f_{i}/Q_{H}^{n})\partial_{x_{i}}
with

and
f_{i}\in S
for
2\leq i\leq\ell
, as given in (
3.3
).
Then
\theta(\alpha_{H})=f_{1}/Q_{H}^{n}=\alpha_{H}^{-ke_{H}}g/Q_{H}^{n}
with

.

We aim to show that

.
To this end let

be the reflection along
H
and let

be the

-th root of unity such that

Observe first that

.
This is because

This yields

On the other hand,

since

is

-invariant and we obtain

2.5
, we conclude

and

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega)

-invariant basis follows because

\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega+1)\subsetneqq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega)\,,

by

2.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.9

Let

In symbols, the well-definedness of

This is already known for

by
[

, Thm. 3.22]
. So we aim to show this for

.
Recall that
J=\prod\alpha_{H}^{ke_{H}-1}
.
Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider an orthonormal basis
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}
of
V^{*}
.
In this basis, for
\theta
to belong to
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-k\omega+1)^{W}
entails that there exist
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in S
such that

\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}J^{-1}\partial_{x_{i}}\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{i}\in x_{1}^{ke_{H}-1}\cdot S\quad\text{for }2\leq i\leq\ell.

Moreover,

\nabla_{\!D}(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}D\left(f_{i}J^{-1}\right)\partial_{x_{i}}.

Since

\prod\alpha_{H}^{(k+1)e_{H}-1}D\left(f_{1}J^{-1}\right)\in S,

it remains to show that

g_{i}:=\prod\alpha_{H}^{(k+1)e_{H}-1}D\left(f_{i}J^{-1}\right)\in x_{1}^{(k+1)e_{H}-1}\cdot S

for
2\leq i\leq\ell
.
First, we see that

, because
f_{i}\in x_{1}^{ke_{H}-1}\cdot S\text{ for }2\leq i\leq\ell
.
On the other hand, observe that

is a relative invariant for

,
cf.

[

, Sec. 6.2]
.
This means in particular that

s(g_{i})=\det(s)(g_{i}),

where

is a reflection along
H
generating

.
This is because
f_{i}J^{-1}
is
s
-invariant by assumption on
\theta
,
D
is
W
-invariant, and
J
is a relative invariant for
\det
, see
[
OT92
, Sec. 6.2]
.
Since

,

implies that

(\nabla_{\partial_{t_{j}}}\zeta)(x_{i})=Q_{H}^{-N}g_{i}J^{-1}\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}},

for
2\leq i\leq\ell
.
We thus have
\nabla_{\partial_{t_{j}}}\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-\omega+1)
.
Computing

\det\big((\nabla_{\partial_{t_{j}}}\zeta)(x_{i})\big)\doteq J^{-1}\det\big((\nabla_{\partial_{x_{j}}}\zeta)(x_{i})\big)\doteq J^{-1}Q_{\nu}=Q_{\nu-\omega+1}

yields the statement, thanks to
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)^{W}
be
\nu
-universal for a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued multiplicity function
\nu
on
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
be a multiplicity with
-\omega+1\leq\mu\leq 1
.
Then

\Phi_{\zeta}:\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\rightarrow\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)

is an isomorphism of
S
-modules.
In particular,
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free if and only if
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)
is free.

Before proving
Theorem

4.19
, we use the fact that
\zeta=\nabla_{\!D}^{-m}(E)
is
(m\omega)
-universal for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, by
Theorem

4.17
to obtain the special case of the
generalized Ziegler multiplicity
on a reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
given by
m\omega-1
for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For
m=1
, this multiplicity was originally considered by Ziegler
[
Zie89
]
(assigning the number of reflections along
H
to any reflecting hyperplane), and shown to be free in general in
[
Zie89
]
and
[
HR14
]
.

Corollary 4.20
.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
be the set of exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},\omega-1)
.
For any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the multi-arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m\omega-1)
is free and its exponents are given by

\big\{e_{1}+(m-1)h,\dots,e_{\ell}+(m-1)h\big\}.

Proof of
Theorem

4.19
.

As
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\omega+1)
and
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu)\subseteq\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu-\omega+1)
,
Proposition

4.18
implies that
\Phi_{\zeta}
is injective on
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
It thus remains to show that

(4.21)

\Phi_{\zeta}\big(\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\big)=\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\mu).

Fix
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
\alpha_{H}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{\ell}
be an orthonormal basis of
V^{*}
.
The element
g=\alpha_{H}^{-\nu(H)-1}\zeta(\alpha_{H})\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
is a unit in
{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
, by
Lemma

3.11
.
For
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
, compute

\displaystyle\big(\Phi_{\zeta}(\theta)\big)(\alpha_{H})

\displaystyle=\theta(\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}g)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}\theta(g)+(\nu(H)+1)\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\theta(\alpha_{H})g

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\theta(\alpha_{H})\big(\alpha_{H}\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha_{H}}+(\nu(H)+1)g\big)+\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\theta(x_{i})\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)}\theta(\alpha_{H})U+\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+1}C,

where
U=\alpha_{H}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha_{H}}+(\nu(H)+1)g
is again a unit in
{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
and
C=\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\theta(x_{i})\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial x_{i}}
.
Note that
C\in{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}
, since
\theta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
, and so is
\alpha_{H}^{1-\mu(H)}C
.
We thus obtain

\alpha_{H}^{-\nu(H)-\mu(H)}\big(\Phi_{\zeta}(\theta)\big)(\alpha_{H})=\alpha_{H}^{-\mu(H)}\theta(\alpha_{H})U+\alpha_{H}^{1-\mu(H)}C,

implying that

\big(\Phi_{\zeta}(\theta)\big)(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\nu(H)+\mu(H)}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}\Leftrightarrow\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)}{S_{\langle{\alpha_{H}}\rangle}}.

Since this holds for any
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, the reverse implication shows the containment “
\subseteq
” in (
4.21
) and the forward implication the containment “
\supseteq
”.
∎

We finally conclude the extension of
[
Wak11
, Thm. 2.8]
to the unitary setting.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\infty)
and let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then

\zeta\text{ is }(k\omega)\text{-universal }\Longrightarrow\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta\text{ is }\big((k+1)\omega\big)\text{-universal}.

Proof.

We set
\nu=k\omega
in the proof for better readability.
Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},1)^{W}
be an
S
-basis.
Due to the universality of
\zeta
, we also have that
\nabla_{\eta_{1}}\zeta,\ldots,\nabla_{\eta_{\ell}}\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)^{W}
form an
S
-basis.
Since
\nu
is a multiple of
\omega
, we also have that

\nabla_{\!D}\nabla_{\eta_{j}}\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1-\omega)^{W}.

Because the
\partial_{t_{j}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},-\omega+1)^{W}
form an
S
-basis as well, we obtain

\nabla_{\!D}\nabla_{\eta_{j}}\zeta=\sum_{i}f_{ij}\nabla_{\partial_{t_{i}}}\zeta

for
W
-invariant polynomials
f_{ij}
.
Comparing polynomial degrees yields
\deg f_{ij}=e_{i}+e_{j}^{*}-h<h
(or, respectively,
f_{ij}=0
for
e_{i}+e_{j}^{*}<h
) which shows that
f_{ij}\in T
.
Moreover,
\deg f_{i,\ell+1-i}=e_{i}+e_{\ell+1-i}^{*}-h=0
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
, and it follows that
\det f_{ij}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Applying
\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}
gives

\nabla_{\eta_{j}}\zeta=\sum_{i}f_{ij}\nabla_{\partial_{t_{i}}}\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta.

Because the vector fields on the left form an
S
-basis of
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)
, we readily see that
\det f_{ij}\neq 0
and the
\nabla_{\partial_{t_{i}}}\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1)^{W}
form an
S
-basis.
Again, because
\nu
is a multiple of
\omega
, we have
\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+1-\omega)^{W}
and we see that the derivations

\nabla_{\partial_{x_{j}}}\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta=\sum_{i}J_{\partial{\textbf{t}}/\partial{\textbf{x}}}\nabla_{\partial_{t_{j}}}\nabla_{\!D}^{-1}\zeta

form an
S
-basis of
\mathfrak{D}({\mathscr{A}},\nu+\omega)
by Saito’s criterion.
∎

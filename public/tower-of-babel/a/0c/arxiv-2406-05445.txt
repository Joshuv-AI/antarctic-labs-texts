A generalization of Inoue surfaces S + S^{+}
David Petcu

Abstract
. Using Lie groups with left-invariant complex structure, we construct new examples of compact complex manifolds with flat affine structure in arbitrarly high dimensions. In the 2-dimensional case, we retrieve the Inoue surfaces S + S^{+} .

53C55, 22E25, 32J18

1.
Introduction

The surfaces introduced by Inoue in 1974 are complex, compact, non-Kähler manifolds. Alongside Hopf surfaces, Kodaira surfaces, and tori, they serve as examples of surfaces that possess a natural flat affine structure. This means that there exists a covering of the manifold with coordinate charts such that all the transition maps between these charts are affine functions.

To construct manifolds with this property, one can begin with a real Lie group that is endowed with a left-invariant complex structure
[
Wa
]
. By taking the quotient of this Lie group by the left action of a discrete cocompact subgroup, one obtains the desired manifold. In this paper, we utilize this approach to introduce a higher-dimensional generalization of Inoue surfaces of type
S^{+}
.

There have been several constructions proposed to date that generalize different types of Inoue surfaces. For instance, Oeljeklaus-Toma (
[
OeTo
]
) and Endo-Pajitnov (
[
EnPa
]
) manifolds generalize
S^{0}
surfaces, while Oeljeklaus-Miebach manifolds (
[
OeMi
]
) generalize
S^{+}
surfaces. Despite the differences in their specific constructions, all these manifolds share the fundamental property of admitting an affine structure, as defined above.

By expanding on Inoue’s original work, our paper aims to further explore the geometric and topological properties of these higher-dimensional analogues. The main tool we use to achieve this is group cohomology.
Specifically, we use it to prove that the proposed manifolds do not contain hypersurfaces.
We also investigate the natural generalization of the left-invariant metric structure that exists on the
S^{+}
surfaces.

Remark 2.1
.

To ensure the existence of the matrix

as described above, we need to choose the value of
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>}
. The minimal polynomial of

is
P(X)=(X-\alpha)(X-\frac{1}{\alpha})
, so
\alpha
is a real quadratic integer. Thus,
\alpha
should be of the form
\frac{\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}-4}}{2}

\beta\geq 3
. For any such choice of

with the required eigenvalues, take for example the block-diagonal matrix formed by repeating the

\beta-1&\beta\\
\end{pmatrix}
. We will assume

3.
Betti numbers

The manifold
X_{d,\Lambda}
is diffeomorphic to a fiber bundle over a circle (
X_{d,\Lambda}\to\mathbb{R}_{>}/\langle\alpha\rangle
) and the fiber is
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\mathcal{N}/\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}}
. In turn,
\mathcal{M}
is a fiber bundle over a
2d
-torus

\mathbb{R}^{2d}/\langle(a_{1}^{k}\dots a_{d}^{k},b_{1}^{k}\dots b_{d}^{k})|k\in\{1\dots 2d\}\rangle

with fiber a
d^{2}
-torus (
\mathcal{Z}/D
). We will compute the first two Betti numbers of
X_{d,\Lambda}
using group cohomology and the spectral sequences associated to these two fiber bundles. The key tool is the following classical theorem (see
[
Mu
]
):

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
G
a discrete group, acting freely and discontinously on
X
. Let
Y=X/G
and
\pi:X\to Y
be the projection, then for any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
, there is a natural homomorphism

\phi:H^{p}(G,\Gamma(X,\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}))\to H^{p}(Y,\mathcal{F})

If
H^{i}(X,\pi^{*}\mathcal{F})=0
,
i\geq 1
, then the map
\phi
is an isomorphism. Furthermore, the maps
\phi
are compatible with cup products.

We start by studying the cohomology of the fiber
\mathcal{M}\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times T^{d^{2}})/F
, where
F(\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2d})\subset\mathcal{V}
is the group generated by
\{\widehat{g_{1}}\dots\widehat{g_{2d}}\}
. Consider the spectral sequence starting from the page zero given by
E_{0}^{p,q}\simeq C^{p}(F,\Gamma(\mathcal{A}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times T^{d^{2}})))
(where
{\mathcal{A}}^{q}
is the sheaf of differential
q-
forms),
d
denoting the vertical differential and
\delta
the horizontal one. Here,
E_{0}^{p,q}
is the set of all functions
F^{p}\to\Gamma(\mathcal{A}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times T^{d^{2}}))
,
\delta
is the differential of the bar resolution and
d
is the usual exterior differential of forms. By the previous theorem, taking cohomology along the lines of the bicomplex will evaluate the cohomology of the sheaves
\mathcal{A}^{q}
on
\mathcal{M}
. Since these sheaves are fine, we know that the spectral sequence will converge to the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}
. The second page of the spectral sequence is
E_{2}^{p,q}\simeq H^{p}(F,H^{q}(T^{d^{2}},\mathbb{C}))
and
F
acts trivially on the cohomology of
T^{d^{2}}
, therefore
E_{2}^{p,q}\simeq H^{p}(F,\mathbb{C})\otimes H^{q}(T^{d^{2}},\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 3.1
.

If a cohomological spectral sequence with multiplicative structure has the second page multiplicatively generated by elements of total degree one, then
E_{3}^{p,q}\simeq E_{\infty}^{p,q}
. Moreover, in order to compute page
3
, one only needs to know the map
d_{2}:E_{2}^{0,1}\to E_{2}^{2,0}
. This applies to our spectral sequence and it is true in general for spectral sequences coming from principal fiber bundles with a torus fiber over a torus base.

The generators of
H^{0}(F,H^{1}(T^{d^{2}},\mathbb{C}))
are represented by
\{\phi_{ij}:id_{F}\to dc_{ij}\}
. Let
(k_{1}\dots k_{2d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}\simeq F
, then:

\delta(\phi_{ij})(k_{1}\dots k_{2d})=(k_{1}\dots k_{2d})\cdot\phi_{ij}-\phi_{ij}=((g_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots g_{2d}^{k_{2d}})^{-1})^{*}dc_{ij}-dc_{ij}=\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}k_{s}b_{j}^{s}\right)da_{i}

Let
\psi_{ij}
be such that
d(\psi_{ij})=\delta(\phi_{ij})
, for example:

\psi_{ij}(k_{1}\dots k_{2d})=\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}k_{s}b_{j}^{s}\right)a_{i}

Let
(l_{1}\dots l_{2d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2d}\simeq F
, we compute
\delta(\psi_{ij})
:

\delta(\psi_{ij})((k_{1}\dots k_{2d}),(l_{1}\dots l_{2d}))=

=((g_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots g_{2d}^{k_{2d}})^{-1})^{*}\psi_{ij}(l_{1}\dots l_{2d})-\psi_{ij}(k_{1}+l_{1}\dots k_{2d}+l_{2d})+\psi_{ij}(k_{1}\dots k_{2d})=

=\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}l_{s}b_{j}^{s}\right)\left(a_{i}-\sum_{t=1}^{2d}k_{t}a_{i}^{t}\right)-\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}(k_{s}+l_{s})b_{j}^{s}\right)a_{i}+\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}k_{s}b_{j}^{s}\right)a_{i}=

=\left(-\sum_{s=1}^{2d}l_{s}b_{j}^{s}\right)\left(-\sum_{t=1}^{2d}k_{t}a_{i}^{t}\right)

For
\delta(\psi_{ij})
determined above, we have
d_{2}([\phi_{ij}]_{2})=[\delta\psi_{ij}]_{2}
. By identifying
E_{2}^{2,0}
with
H^{2}_{DR}(T^{2d},\mathbb{C})
,
d_{2}([\phi_{ij}]_{2})
becomes

\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq 2d}(\delta(\psi_{ij})(\hat{g_{s}},\hat{g_{t}})-\delta(\psi_{ij})(\hat{g_{t}},\hat{g_{s}}))du_{s}\wedge du_{t}=\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq 2d}(b_{j}^{s}a_{i}^{t}-b_{j}^{t}a_{i}^{s})du_{s}\wedge du_{t}

where
u_{i}
are the coordinate functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
with respect to the basis
\{\widehat{g_{i}}\}
. Rewriting this in terms of
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
, we get

\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq 2d}(b_{j}^{s}a_{i}^{t}-b_{j}^{t}a_{i}^{s})du_{s}\wedge du_{t}=db_{j}\wedge da_{i}

This proves that
E_{3}^{0,1}=0
and
E_{3}^{2,0}=(\bigwedge^{2}\langle da_{1}\dots da_{d}\rangle)\oplus(\bigwedge^{2}\langle db_{1}\dots db_{d}\rangle)
, so
H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C})=\langle da_{i},db_{j}\rangle
and
H^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C})=\bigwedge^{2}\langle da_{i}\rangle\oplus\bigwedge^{2}\langle db_{j}\rangle\oplus\langle da_{i}\otimes dc_{ij}\rangle\oplus\langle db_{j}\otimes dc_{ij}\rangle
.

We may now compute the first two Betti numbers of
X_{d,\Lambda}
. Consider the following change in coordinates on
G
:
b_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{b_{i}}{\alpha}
, for
i\in\{1\dots d\}
and all the other coordinates remain unchanged. This presents the manifold
G/\Lambda_{\mathcal{N}}
as the product
\mathbb{R}_{>}\times\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is as above. This time, we are interested in the spectral sequence whose second page is
E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(\mathbb{Z},H^{q}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))
, where the generator of
\mathbb{Z}
acts as
g_{0}
. Since
H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C})
is finite-dimensional, the dimensions of
H^{0}(\mathbb{Z},H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))
and
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))
are equal - since they are the kernel and cokernel of
(g_{0}^{-1})^{*}-id
as an endomorphism of
H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C})
. No elements of
H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C})
are invariant under the action of
g_{0}
(since
(g_{0}^{-1})^{*}(da_{i})=\frac{1}{\alpha}da_{i}
and
(g_{0}^{-1})^{*}(db_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})=\alpha db_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
), thus
H^{0}(\mathbb{Z},H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))=H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))=0
. Similarly,
H^{0}(\mathbb{Z},H^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))=0
and
H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},H^{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{C}))=H^{1}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}
. We have proved the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

The first two Betti numbers of
X_{d,\Lambda}
are
b_{1}=1
and
b_{2}=0
.

Corollary 3.1
.

The manifolds
X_{d,\Lambda}
are non-Kähler.

Definition 4.1

For

Examples:

i)

Let

Lemma 4.2

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{n})}}

A discrete approach to the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem
Tianqi Wu

Abstract
. We propose a discrete approach to approximate the solution for the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem in 2-dimensional manifolds, based on the notion of discrete conformality. Our approach gives a convenient way to numerically compute the solution by minimizing a convex functional.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
A theorem on the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem

Suppose
(M,g)
is a connected oriented closed smooth Riemannian surface with genus greater than 1. The following Theorem on the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem is well-known (see
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
for example).

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a smooth function
\kappa(x)<0
on
(M,g)
, there exists a unique function
u
on
M
such that
e^{2u}g
has the Gaussian curvature
\kappa
. Furthermore, such
u
is smooth on
M
.

In this paper we will describe a discrete approach to compute the conformal factor
u
in Theorem 1.1.
For other related results on the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem, one may refer
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Discrete conformality for polyhedral surfaces

Assume
T
is a simplicial
triangulation
of
M
. Let
V(T),E(T),F(T)
be the set of vertices, edges, and triangles of
T
respectively. Furthermore,
T
is called a
geodesic triangulation
if any edge in
T
is a geodesic arc.
Given a triangulation
T
, if an edge length
l\in\mathbb{R}^{E(T)}_{>0}
satisfies the triangle inequalities,
we can construct a Euclidean polyhedral surface
(T,l)
by isometrically gluing the Euclidean triangles with the edge lengths defined by
l
along the pairs of edges.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a triangulation
T
, a
discrete conformal factor

u
is a real-valued function on
V(T)
. Two Euclidean polyhedral surfaces
(T,l)
and
(T,l^{\prime})
are
discretely conformally equivalent
if for some discrete conformal factor
u
,

(1)

l^{\prime}_{ij}=e^{\frac{1}{2}(u_{i}+u_{j})}l_{ij}

for any edge
ij\in E(T)
.
We denote
l^{\prime}=u*l
if equation (
1
) holds.

1.3.
Triangulations with mixed background constant curvatures

Suppose
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{F(T)}_{<0}
and
l\in\mathbb{R}^{E(T)}_{>0}
. Whenever well-defined, for any
\sigma\in F(T)
we denote

(a)
g(\sigma,\kappa,l)
as a Riemmanian metric on
\sigma
such that
(\sigma,g(\sigma,\kappa,l))
is a geodesic triangle of the constant Gaussian curvature
\kappa(\sigma)
such that the lengths of an edge
e
is

\frac{2}{-\kappa(\sigma)}\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{-\kappa(\sigma)}{2}l(e)\right),

(b)

\theta^{i}_{jk}=\theta^{i}_{jk}(\kappa,l)
as the inner angle at the vertex
i
in the geodesic triangle

(\triangle ijk,g(\triangle ijk,\kappa,l)),

and

(c)

K=K(\kappa,l)\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
as a generalized discrete curvature such that

K_{i}=2\pi-\sum_{jk\in E:\triangle ijk\in F}\theta^{i}_{jk}.

As observed in Bobenko-Pinkall-Springborn
[
3
]
, if
\kappa(\sigma)=-1
, then

(\sigma,g(\sigma,\kappa,u*l))
is discretely conformal to
(\sigma,g(\sigma,\kappa,l))
as hyperbolic geodesic triangles with the discrete conformal factor
u
, in the notion of hyperbolic discrete conformality introduced in
[
3
]
.
We can naturally extend their notion of hyperbolic discrete conformality to geodesic triangles of constant negative curvature
\kappa(\sigma)
.
For fixed
\kappa,l
, we denote
K=K(\kappa,u*l)
by
K(u)
for convenience.
Then by the variational principle developed by Luo
[
4
]
and Bobenko-Pinkall-Springborn
[
3
]

\mathcal{F}(u)=\int\sum_{i\in V}K_{i}(u)du_{i}

is well-defined and locally strictly convex on the domain where
K(u)
is well-defined. Furthermore,
Bobenko-Pinkall-Sprinborn
[
3
]
gave a simple and explicit formula extending
\mathcal{F}
to a globally convex functional
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{V}
. So
K(u)=0
has at most one solution, and
can be computed efficiently by minimizing a globally convex functional
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{V}
.

If
K(u)=0
, one may view
\cup_{\sigma}(\sigma,g(\sigma,\kappa,u*l))
roughly as an approximation of a Riemannian surface with the prescribed Gaussian curvature
\kappa
. So if
\kappa
is a discrete approximation of a smooth function
\bar{\kappa}(x)<0
on
M
,
u
may be viewed as a discrete approximation of the smooth conformal factor
\bar{u}
satisfying that
e^{2\bar{u}}g
has the Gaussian curvature
\bar{\kappa}
.

Other related work on discrete conformality and geodesic triangulations could be found in

1.4.
Discrete approximation of the prescribed curvature problem

Suppose
T
is a geodesic triangulation of
(M,g)
with the edge length
l\in\mathbb{R}^{E(T)}_{>0}
measured in
(M,g)
, we view
(T,l)
as a polyhedral surface approximation of
(M,g)
. Suppose
\bar{\kappa}(x)<0
is a smooth function on
M
. By Theorem 1.1 there exists a smooth function
\bar{u}

such that
e^{2\bar{u}}g
has the Gaussian curvature
\bar{\kappa}
. We will find a discrete approximation of
\bar{u}
using the information of
(T,l)
and
\bar{\kappa}
.
For all
\sigma\in F(T)
, let
\kappa(\sigma)=\bar{\kappa}(x)
for some
x\in\sigma
. Then by minimizing a convex functional on
\mathbb{R}^{V}
, we expect to find a unique discrete conformal factor
u\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
such that
K(\kappa,u*l)=0
. Such a discerete conformal factor
u
should be an approximation of
\bar{u}

We need some regularity of the triangulation to prove the convergence.

Definition 1.3
.

A polyhedral surface
(T,l)
is called
\epsilon
-acute
if

\theta^{i}_{jk}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon

for any inner angle.

In this paper, if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{A}
is a vector for some finite set
A
, we use

|x|
to denote the infinity norm of
x
, i.e.,
|x|=|x|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in A}|x_{i}|
.
Here is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a connected closed orientable smooth Riemannian surface with genus
>1
,
\bar{\kappa}(x)<0
is a smooth function on
M
, and
\bar{u}
is the smooth function on
M
such that
e^{2\bar{u}}g
has Gaussian curvature
\bar{\kappa}(x)
.
Assume
T
is a geodesic triangulation of
(M,g)
, and
l\in\mathbb{R}^{E(T)}_{>0}
denotes the edge length in
(M,g)
. For any triangle
\sigma
in
F(T)
, let
\kappa(\sigma)=\bar{\kappa}(x)
for some

x\in\sigma
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist constants
\delta=\delta(M,g,\bar{\kappa},\epsilon)>0
and
C=C(M,g,\bar{\kappa},\epsilon)
such that if
(T,l)
is
\epsilon
-acute and
|l|<\delta
, then

(a)

there exists a unique discrete conformal factor
u\in\mathbb{R}^{V(T)}
, such that
K(u)=0
, and

(b)

\big|u-\bar{u}|_{V(T)}\big|\leq C|l|.

The main idea of the proof of Theorem 1.4 follows
[
14
]
. We describe a key discrete elliptic estimate in Section 2 and the formula of
\partial K/\partial u
in Section 3. The proof of Theorem 1.4 is given in Section 4.

2.
Discrete calculus on graphs

Assume
G=(V,E)
is an undirected connected simple graph. Let
ij
denote an edge in
E
with endpoints
i
and
j
, and
i\sim j
represent that the vertices
i
and
j
are connected by an edge
ij\in E
. Let
\mathbb{R}^{E}
and
\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
be vector spaces of dimension
|E|
such that

(a)

a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}
is represented symmetrically, i.e.,
x_{ij}=x_{ji}
, and

(b)

a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
is represented anti-symmetrically, i.e.,
x_{ij}=-x_{ji}
.

A vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
is called a
flow
on
G
.
An
edge weight

\eta
on
G
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{E}
. Given an edge weight
\eta
, the
gradient

\nabla f=\nabla_{\eta}f
of a vertex function
f\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
is a flow in
\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
defined as

(\nabla f)_{ij}=\eta_{ij}(f_{j}-f_{i})

where
f_{i}=f(i).

Given a flow
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
, its
divergence

div(x)
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{V}
such that

div(x)_{i}=\sum_{j\sim i}x_{ij}.

Given an edge weight
\eta
, the associated
Laplacian

\Delta=\Delta_{\eta}:\mathbb{R}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{V}
is defined as

\Delta f=div(\nabla_{\eta}f)
, i.e.,

(\Delta f)_{i}=\sum_{j\sim i}\eta_{ij}(f_{j}-f_{i}).

A Laplacian
\Delta_{\eta}
is a linear transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{V}
, and can be identified as a
|V|\times|V|
symmetric negative semi-definite matrix.

Now we introduce the notion of
C
-isoperimetry for a graph
G=(V,E)
associated with a positive vector
l\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{>0}
.
Given any
V_{0}\subset V
, denote

\partial V_{0}=\{ij\in E:i\in V_{0},j\notin V_{0}\},

and then define the
l
-
perimeter
of
V_{0}
and the
l
-
area
of
V_{0}
as

|\partial V_{0}|_{l}=\sum_{ij\in\partial V_{0}}l_{ij}\quad\text{ and }\quad|V_{0}|_{l}=\sum_{i,j\in V_{0},ij\in E}l_{ij}^{2}

respectively.

For a constant
C>0
,
such a pair
(G,l)
is called
C
-
isoperimetric
if for any
V_{0}\subset V

\min\{|V_{0}|_{l},|V|_{l}-|V_{0}|_{l}\}\leq C\cdot|\partial V_{0}|_{l}^{2}.

We will see, from part (b) of Lemma
4.2
, that a uniform
C
-isoperimetric condition is satisfied by regular geodesic triangulations approximating a closed smooth surface.
The following discrete elliptic estimate is a key ingredient of the proofs of our main theorems.

Lemma 2.1

(Wu-Zhu
[
14
]
)
.

Assume
(G,l)
is
C_{1}
-isoperimetric, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{>0},C_{2}>0,C_{3}>0
are such that

(i)

|x_{ij}|\leq C_{2}l_{ij}^{2}
for any
ij\in E
, and

(ii)

\eta_{ij}\geq C_{3}
for any
ij\in E
.

Then

|\Delta^{-1}_{\eta}\circ div(x))|\leq\frac{4C_{2}\sqrt{C_{1}+1}}{C_{3}}|l|\cdot|V|_{l}^{1/2}.

Furthermore, if
y\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
and
C_{4}>0
and
D\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times V}
is a non-zero diagonal matrix such that

|y_{i}|\leq C_{4}D_{ii}|l|\cdot|V|_{l}^{1/2}

for any
i\in V
, then

|(D-\Delta_{\eta})^{-1}(div(x)+y)|\leq\left(C_{4}+\frac{8C_{2}\sqrt{C_{1}+1}}{C_{3}}\right)|l|\cdot|V|_{l}^{1/2}.

3.
Differential of the discrete curvatures and angles

The following proposition can be easily derived from Proposition 6.1.7 in
[
3
]
or
Lemma 3.4 in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
(T,l)
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{F(T)}_{<0}
, and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{V(T)}
such that
K(u)
is well-defined,
denote

\tilde{\theta}^{i}_{jk}(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\pi+\theta^{i}_{jk}(u)-\theta^{j}_{ik}(u)-\theta^{k}_{ij}(u))

and

\lambda_{ij,k}=\frac{\kappa(\triangle ijk)^{2}\cdot(u*l)_{ij}^{2}}{\kappa(\triangle ijk)^{2}\cdot(u*l)_{ij}^{2}+4}

for all triangle
\triangle ijk\in F(T)
.
Then

\frac{\partial K}{\partial u}(u)=D(u)-\Delta_{\eta(u)}

where

\eta_{ij}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\cot\tilde{\theta}^{k}_{ij}(u)(1-\lambda_{ij,k})+\frac{1}{2}\cot\tilde{\theta}^{k^{\prime}}_{ij}(u)(1-\lambda_{ij,k^{\prime}}),

and

D=D(u)
is a diagonal matrix such that

D_{ii}(u)=\sum_{\triangle ijk\in F}(\cot\tilde{\theta}^{k}_{ij}(u)\lambda_{ij,k}+\cot\tilde{\theta}^{k^{\prime}}_{ij}(u)\lambda_{ij,k^{\prime}}).

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

For a constant
\epsilon>0
, we assume that
(T,l)
is
\epsilon
-acute and
|l|\leq\delta
where
\delta=\delta(M,g,\bar{\kappa},\epsilon)<1
is a sufficiently small constant to be determined. By Lemma
4.2
,
if
\delta
is sufficiently small there exists a geodesic triangulation
T^{\prime}
of
(M,e^{2\bar{u}}g)
homotopic to
T
relative to
V(T)=V(T^{\prime})
.
Let
\bar{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{E(T)}\cong R^{E(T^{\prime})}
denote the geodesic lengths of edges of
T^{\prime}
in
(M,e^{2\bar{u}}g)
.

For simplicity, we will frequently use the notion

to denote that if

is sufficiently small, then

for some constant

. For example, we have that

(a)

for any

,

(b)

(\bar{u}*l)_{ij}=O(l_{ij})
,

(c)

\bar{l}_{ij}=O(l_{ij})

l_{ij}=O(\frac{2}{-\kappa(\triangle ijk)}\sinh^{-1}(\frac{-\kappa(\triangle ijk)}{2}l_{ij}))

The remaining of the proof is divided into two steps.

(1)

Firstly we show that

(\bar{u}*l)_{ij}-\bar{l}_{ij}=O(l_{ij}^{3})

and

K(\bar{u})=div(x)+y

for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{E}_{A}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
such that
x_{ij}=O(l_{ij}^{2})
and
y_{i}=O(l_{ij}^{3})
.

(2)

Secondly, we construct a smooth path

with
u(0)=\bar{u}
such that the following identity holds

K(u(t))=(1-t)K(\bar{u}).

Furthermore, we show that
|u^{\prime}(t)|=O(|l|)
, and then
K(u(1))=0
and
u(1)-\bar{u}=O(|l|)
.

Therefore the vertex function
u(1)
is the wanted discrete conformal factor for
(T,l)
in Theorem
1.4
.

Part 1

By Lemma

,
(T,\bar{l})
is
\frac{1}{2}\epsilon

\bar{l}_{ij}-(\bar{u}*l)_{ij}=O(l_{ij}^{3}).

Notice that

\bar{\kappa}(x)-\kappa(\triangle ijk)=O(l_{ij})

Denote

Part 2

Let

\tilde{\Omega}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:u*l\text{ satisfies the triangle inequalities and $(T,u*l)$ is $\frac{\epsilon}{4}$-acute}\}

Now consider the following ODE on

BSDEs with logarithmic growth driven by Brownian motion and Poisson random measure and connection to stochastic control problem
Khalid OUFDIL e-mail: khalid.ofdil@gmail.com Université Ibn Zohr, Equipe. Aide á la decision, ENSA, B.P. 1136, Agadir, Maroc

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we study one-dimensional backward stochastic differential equation under logarithmic growth in the
z
-variable
(|z|\sqrt{|\ln|z|}|)
. We show the existence and the uniqueness of the solution when the noise is driven by a Brownian motion and an independent Poisson random measure. In addition, we highlight the connection of such BSDEs with stochastic optimal control problem, where we show the existence of an optimal strategy for the control problem.

Keywords:
Backward stochastic differential equations, optimal control, logarithmic growth.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2010):
93E20, 60H10, 60H99.

1
Introduction

The notion of non-linear Backward Stochastic Differential Equations (BSDEs in short) was first introduced by Pardoux and Peng
[
16
]
. The solution of this equation, associated with a terminal value
\xi
and a generator
f
, is a couple of stochastic processes
(Y_{t},Z_{t})_{t\leq T}
such that

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}

(1.1)

a.s. for all
t\leq T
, where
B
is a Brownian motion and the processes
(Y_{t},Z_{t})_{t\leq T}
are adapted to a natural filtration of
B
. Since then, these equations have gradually became an important mathematical tool which is encountered in many fields such as financial mathematics, stochastic optimal control, partial differential equations, and other fields.

In this paper we are concerned with the problem of the existence and the uniqueness of a solution for one-dimensional BSDEs when the noise is driven by a Brownian motion and an independent Poisson random measure. Roughly speaking we look for a triple of adapted processes
(Y,Z,V)
such that:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

(1.2)

where
\tilde{\mu}
is a compensated Poisson random measure.

Recently in
[
13
]
, Kruse and Popier
established the existence and the uniqueness
of a solution to multidimensional BSDEs in a general filtration which includes a Brownian motion and an independent Poisson random measure. The generator is under monotonic assumption w.r.t. the y-variable and the terminal condition and the driver are not necessarily square integrable. We however in this work focus on the existence and the uniqueness of the solution for BSDEs when the generator is under logarithmic growth assumption in the
z
-variable. It is worth noting that our technique is based on a localization method introduced in
[
1
,
2
]
and more developed and extended in
[
4
,
5
]
.

It is well known that BSDEs are connected with stochastic optimal control. Therefore as an application of the obtained result, we show the existence of an optimal strategy for stochastic control of diffusion. Assume that we have a system whose dynamic is given by:

\displaystyle x_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,x_{s})dB^{u}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\gamma(s,x_{s^{-}},w)\tilde{\mu}^{u}(dw,ds)

(1.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\gamma(s,x_{s^{-}},w)g(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\lambda(dw)ds.

The agent intervenes in the system in the form of controlling with the help of the process
u=(\bar{u},\check{u})
. The idea is we characterize the value function as the unique solution of a specific BSDE under more relaxed assumptions.

This paper is divided into five sections. In section 2, we begin by presenting our assumptions and the notations used trough out the paper. In section 3, we show some priori estimates for solutions of BSDEs. In section 4, we move on to study the existence and the uniqueness of the solution for the BSDEs using the localization method. Section 5 is devoted to the link between our BSDEs and the stochastic optimal control problem. We show the value function as a solution of an appropriate BSDE.

Notations and Assumptions

2.1
Notations

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\leq T},P)
be a stochastic basis such that:

•

\mathcal{F}_{0}
contains
\mathcal{N}
the set of all
P
-null sets of
\mathcal{F}
,

•

\mathcal{F}_{t^{+}}=\cap_{\epsilon>0}\mathcal{F}_{t+\epsilon}=\mathcal{F}_{t},\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t\leq T
.

We assume that
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\leq T}
is supported by the two following independent processes:

(i)

a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
,

(ii)

a Poisson random measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{U}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with intensity
\lambda(dw)dt
where
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}

(m\geq 1)
. The corresponding compensated Poisson random measure
\tilde{\mu}(dw,dt)=\mu(dw,dt)-\lambda(dw)dt
is a martingale w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}
. The measure
\lambda
is
\sigma
-finite on
\mathcal{U}
satisfying

\int_{\mathcal{U}}(1\wedge|w|^{2})\lambda(dw)<+\infty.

In this paper, let
{\mathcal{P}}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-predictable sets on
\Omega\times[0,T]
.

For a given adapted RCLL process
(X_{t})_{t\leq T}
and for any
t\leq T
we set
X_{t^{-}}=\lim_{s\nearrow t}X_{s}
with the convention that
X_{0^{-}}=X_{0}
and
\Delta X_{t}=X_{t}-X_{t^{-}}
.

Let us introduce the following spaces of processes and notations considered in this work, for
all
p\geq 1

•

\mathcal{S}^{p}
is the space of
\mathbb{R}
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted and RCLL processes
\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

||Y||_{\mathcal{S}^{p}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

•

\mathcal{M}^{p}
denote the set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-progressively measurable processes
\left(Z_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

||Z||_{\mathcal{M}^{p}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

•

\mathcal{L}_{loc}
is the space of all
{\mathcal{P}}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{U})
-measurable mapping
V:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}(|V_{s}(w)|^{2}\wedge|V_{s}(w)|)\lambda(dw)ds<+\infty
. Let
\mathcal{L}^{p}
be the set of all
V\in\mathcal{L}_{loc}
such that

||V||_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty.

•

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{p}=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U},\lambda;\mathbb{R})
is the set of measurable functions
V:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

||V||^{p}_{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{p}}=\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V(w)|^{p}\lambda(dw)<+\infty.

2.2
Assumptions

Now let
\xi
be and
\mathbb{R}
-valued and
\mathcal{F}_{T}
-measurable random variable and let
f:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a random function which associates
(t,\omega,y,z,\nu)
with
f(t,\omega,y,z,\nu)
. On the data
\xi
and
f
we make the following assumptions:

(
H.1
)

There exists a positive constant
A
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[|\xi|^{\ln(AT+2)+2}\right]<+\infty.

(
H.2
)

(i)

f
is continuous in
(y,z,\nu)
for almost all
(t,\omega)
;

(ii)

there exist two positives constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
and a process
(\eta_{t})_{t\leq T}
such that for every
t,\omega,y,z,\nu
:

\mid f(t,\omega,y,z,\nu)\mid\leq|\eta_{t}|+c_{0}|z|\sqrt{|\ln(|z|)|}+c_{1}||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}},

where the process
(\eta_{t})_{t\leq T}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}|^{\ln(Cs+2)+2}ds\right]<+\infty.

(
H.3
)

There exist
v^{1}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}

(
resp.
v^{2}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
)
satisfying
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}({v^{1}}_{s})^{q_{1}}ds\right]<+\infty

(
resp.
\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}||{v^{2}}_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}^{q_{2}}ds\right]<+\infty
)
(for some
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
)
and a constant
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that:
\forall N>2,\;\hbox{and for every}\mid y\mid,\;\mid y^{\prime}\mid,\;\mid z\mid,\;\mid z^{\prime}\mid,\;||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}},\;||\nu^{\prime}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq\ln(N)

\displaystyle\bar{y}\big(f(t,\omega,y,z,\nu)-f(t,\omega,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\big)\mathbf{1}_{\{|{v^{1}}_{t}|+||{v^{2}}_{t}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq\ln(N)\}}\leq M|\bar{y}|^{2}\ln A_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+M\mid\bar{y}\mid\mid\bar{z}\mid\sqrt{\ln A_{N}}+M|\bar{y}|||\bar{\nu}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\sqrt{\ln A_{N}},

where
(\bar{y},\bar{z},\bar{\nu})=(y-y^{\prime},z-z^{\prime},\nu-\nu^{\prime})
and
(A_{N})_{N>2}
is a real valued sequence that satisfies
1<A_{N}\leq\ln(N)^{r}
for any
N>2
and some
r>0
, moreover
\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow+\infty}A_{N}=+\infty.

3
Estimations of solutions

In this section we are going to provide estimates to the solution of the BSDE w.r.t.
\xi
and
\eta
. However we first define the notion of solution of the BSDE associated with the couple
(\xi,f)
.

Definition 3.1
.

A triplet of processes
(Y,Z,V)=(Y_{t},Z_{t},V_{t})_{t\leq T}
is said to be a solution of the BSDE with jump associated with
(\xi,f)
if the following holds:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Y\in\mathcal{S}^{\ln(AT+2)+2},\penalty\ Z\in\mathcal{M}^{2}\mbox{ and }V\in\mathcal{L}^{2};\\
Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},V_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds),\qquad t\in[0,T].\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

The following lemma has already been mentioned and proved in
[
6
]
, but for the sake of the reader we give it again.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(Y_{s},Z_{s})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in[0,T]
be such that
Y_{s}
is large enough. Then for every
C_{2}>0
there exists a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

C_{2}\mid Y_{s}\mid|Z_{s}|\sqrt{|\ln(|Z_{s}|)|}\leq\frac{|Z_{s}|^{2}}{2}+C_{3}\ln(\mid Y_{s}\mid)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{2}.

(3.2)

Now we are ready to give estimates for
Y
,
Z
and
V
. Actually we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(Y_{t},Z_{t},V_{t})_{t\leq T}
be a solution of the BSDE (
3.1
), and assume that the couple of data
(\xi,f)
satisfies
(\textbf{H.1})
and
(\textbf{H.2})
. Then there exists a constant
K>0
such that:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\mid Y_{t}\mid^{\theta(t)}+\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right]\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[\mid\xi\mid^{\theta(T)}+\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds\right].

(3.3)

where
\theta(t)=\ln(At+2)+2
for some constant
A
large enough.

Proof.
: First let us show that for some positive constant
C
we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}\mid Y_{t}\mid^{\theta(t)}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\mid\xi\mid^{\theta(T)}+\int_{0}^{T}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds\right].

(3.4)

Let
\theta
be the function from

[0,T]
into
\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

\theta(t)=\ln(At+2)+2,

and let
u
be the function from
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

u(t,x)=\mid x\mid^{\theta(t)}.

Then

•

u_{t}=\frac{A}{At+2}\ln(\mid x\mid)\mid x\mid^{\theta(t)}
;

•

u_{x}=\theta(t)\mid x\mid^{\theta(t)-2}x
;

•

u_{xx}=\theta(t)(\theta(t)-1)\mid x\mid^{\theta(t)-2}
.

Since there is a lack of integrability of the processes
Y
,
Z
and
V
we proceed by localization. Actually for
k\geq 0
, let

\tau_{k}
be the stopping time defined as follows:

\tau_{k}=\inf\{t\geq 0,\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)^{2}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{2\theta(s)-2}|V_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\theta(s)^{2}\mid Y_{s}\mid^{2\theta(s)-2}\mid Z_{s}\mid^{2}ds\geq k\}\wedge T.

Using Itô’s formula on the process
Y_{t}
and the function
y\rightarrowtail\mid y\mid^{\theta(t)}
yields:

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}=\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\frac{A}{As+2}\ln(\mid Y_{s}\mid)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)}ds

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},V_{s})ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)|Z_{s}|^{2}\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\penalty\ \penalty\ -\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\penalty\ \penalty\ -\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds).

We use now the fact that
f
satisfies (
H.2
) and we obtain

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\frac{A}{As+2}\ln(\mid Y_{s}\mid)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}(|\eta_{s}|+c_{0}|Z_{s}|\sqrt{|\ln(|Z_{s}|)|}+c_{1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}})ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)|Z_{s}|^{2}\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds).

Next by Young’s inequality it holds that

\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|\leq\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)}+\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}.

Now without loss of generality we assume that the
y
-variable is sufficiently large, then there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}\left[\frac{C_{1}\ln(\mid Y_{s}\mid)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{2}}{\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)}+\frac{|Z_{s}|^{2}}{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\frac{\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid c_{0}|Z_{s}|\sqrt{|\ln(|Z_{s}|)|}}{\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)}\right]ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}c_{1}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}ds.

Next there exist two positives constants
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that:

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}\left[C_{3}\ln(\mid Y_{s}\mid)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{2}+\frac{|Z_{s}|^{2}}{2}-C_{2}\mid Y_{s}\mid|Z_{s}|\sqrt{|\ln(|Z_{s}|)|}\right]ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}c_{1}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}ds.

By using Lemma
3.1
it follows that:

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}c_{1}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds).

Next let us deal with the Poisson quantity

-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds)\\
-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds).

Actually by following the same argument as in (
[
13
]
, Proposition 2) it follows that

\displaystyle-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}-\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\right)\mu(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\theta(s)Y_{s^{-}}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}V_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)3^{1-\theta(s)}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}\right)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds).

We plug (
3
) in (
3
) and we obtain that:

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)(\theta(s)-1)3^{1-\theta(s)}|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}^{2}ds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}\right)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}c_{1}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s^{-}}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}ds.

Let
\alpha
be a positive constant, once again by Young’s inequality we have

\theta(s)\mid Y_{s^{-}}\mid^{\theta(s)-1}||V_{s}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq\frac{(c_{1}\theta(s))^{2}}{\alpha}|Y_{s}|^{\theta(s)}+\alpha|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)-2}||V_{s}||^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}.

Therefore for
\alpha
small enough

\displaystyle\mid Y_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(t\wedge\tau_{k})}\leq\mid Y_{\tau_{k}}\mid^{\theta(\tau_{k})}+\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)^{\theta(s)-1}|\eta_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\theta(s)\mid Y_{s}\mid^{\theta(s)-2}Y_{s}Z_{s}dB_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\frac{(c_{1}\theta(s))^{2}}{\alpha}|Y_{s}|^{\theta(s)}ds-\int_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}^{\tau_{k}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|Y_{s^{-}}+V_{s}(w)|^{\theta(s)}-|Y_{s^{-}}|^{\theta(s)}\right)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds).

Then we take the expectation in (
3
) and since
\tau_{k}
is of a stationary type we take the limit when

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(T^{\prime}-\delta)^{+}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}e^{C_{N}t}|\check{Y}_{t}|^{\beta}\right]\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[e^{C_{N}T^{\prime}}|\check{Y}_{T^{\prime}}|^{\beta}\right]+\dfrac{K\beta e^{C_{N}T^{\prime}}}{\ln(N)^{\kappa}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}|\check{Y}_{s}|^{\frac{2(\beta-1)}{2-\bar{\alpha}-\kappa}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\dfrac{K\beta e^{C_{N}T^{\prime}}}{\ln(N)^{\kappa}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\left(\Phi(s)^{2}+|f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n},V_{s}^{n})-f_{m}(s,Y_{s}^{m},Z_{s}^{m},V_{s}^{m})|^{\frac{2}{\bar{\alpha}}}\right)ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2K\beta e^{C_{N}T^{\prime}}(2\ln(N))^{\beta-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\sup_{|y|,|z|,||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq N}|f_{n}(s,y,z,\nu)-f(s,y,z,\nu)|ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\sup_{|y|,|z|,||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq N}|f_{m}(s,y,z,\nu)-f(s,y,z,\nu)|ds\bigg].

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(T^{\prime}-\delta)^{+}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}|\check{Y}_{t}|^{\beta}\right]\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[e^{C_{N}\delta}|\check{Y}_{T^{\prime}}|^{\beta}\right]+\beta K\frac{A_{N}^{B\delta}}{A_{N}^{\frac{\kappa}{r}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}|\check{Y}_{s}|^{\frac{2(\beta-1)}{2-\bar{\alpha}-\kappa}}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\beta K\frac{A_{N}^{B\delta}}{A_{N}^{\frac{\kappa}{r}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\left(\Phi(s)^{2}+|f_{n}(s,Y_{s}^{n},Z_{s}^{n},V_{s}^{n})-f_{m}(s,Y_{s}^{m},Z_{s}^{m},V_{s}^{m})|^{\frac{2}{\bar{\alpha}}}\right)ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2K\beta e^{C_{N}\delta}(2\ln(N))^{\beta-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\sup_{|y|,|z|,||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq N}|f_{n}(s,y,z,\nu)-f(s,y,z,\nu)|ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{T^{\prime}}\sup_{|y|,|z|,||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq N}|f_{m}(s,y,z,\nu)-f(s,y,z,\nu)|ds\bigg]

where
B=\beta M+2\beta M^{2}+\frac{3^{\beta-1}\beta M^{2}}{\beta-1}
.
Then from
Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
we obtain for
\delta<\frac{\kappa}{Br}
that

\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{(T^{\prime}-\delta)^{+}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}|\check{Y}_{t}|^{\beta}\right]\leq\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow+\infty}K\mathbb{E}\left[e^{C_{N}\delta}|\check{Y}_{T^{\prime}}|^{\beta}\right]+\varepsilon.

(4.7)

Now we are ready to give the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that

(H.1)
,
(H.2)
and
(H.3)
are satisfied. Then the BSDE (
3.1
) has a unique solution.

Proof.
. The proof will be divided into two parts, the first one is for the existence and the second one is for the uniqueness.

-Existence.

Taking successively
T^{\prime}=T
,

T^{\prime}=(T-\delta^{\prime})^{+}
,
T^{\prime}=(T-2\delta^{\prime})^{+}...
in (
4.4
)
yields

\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y^{m}_{t}|^{\beta}\right]=0.

(4.8)

Finally, Proposition
3.1
allows us to show that

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|^{\beta}\right]=0.

(4.9)

Next with
(\check{Y},\check{Z},\check{V})=(Y^{n}-Y^{m},Z^{n}-Z^{m},V^{n}-V^{m})
, we apply once more Itô’s formula and we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\check{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|\check{V}_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds=2\int_{0}^{T}\check{Y}_{s}(f_{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s},V^{n}_{s})-f_{m}(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z^{m}_{s},V^{m}_{s}))ds

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-2\int_{0}^{T}\check{Y}_{s}\check{Z}_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\left(|\check{Y}_{s^{-}}+\check{V}_{s}(w)|^{2}-|\check{Y}_{s^{-}}|^{2}\right)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)-|\check{Y}_{0}|^{2}.

We take the expectation, and by Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\check{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|\check{V}_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\check{Y}_{s}||f_{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s},V^{n}_{s})-f_{m}(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z^{m}_{s},V^{m}_{s})|ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\check{Y}_{s}|^{2\over{2-\bar{\alpha}}}\right]^{\frac{2-\bar{\alpha}}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s},V^{n}_{s})-f_{m}(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z^{m}_{s},V^{m}_{s})|^{2\over\bar{\alpha}}ds\right]^{\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{2}}.

Then from (
4.8
) and Lemma
4.3
we have:

\lim\limits_{n,m\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{n}_{s}-Z^{m}_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V^{n}_{s}(w)-V^{m}_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right]=0.

(4.10)

Finally by using Proposition
3.1
we get

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{n}_{s}-Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V^{n}_{s}(w)-V_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right]=0.

(4.11)

Therefore, there exists a subsequence which we still denote
(Y^{n},Z^{n},V^{n})
such that:

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|+\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{n}_{s}-Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}|V^{n}_{s}(w)-V_{s}(w)|^{2}\lambda(dw)ds\right)=0,\penalty\ \text{a.e.}\penalty\ (t,\omega).

(4.12)

On the other hand, as in the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
3
]
we can show that:

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s},V^{n}_{s})-f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s},V_{s})|ds\right]=0.

The existence is proved.

-Uniqueness.

Let
(Y,Z,V)
and
(Y^{\prime},Z^{\prime},V^{\prime})
be two solutions of the BSDE (
3.1
) associated with data
(\xi,f)
. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
4.4
and by taking successively
T^{\prime}=T
,

T^{\prime}=(T-\delta^{\prime})^{+}
,
T^{\prime}=(T-2\delta^{\prime})^{+}...
, one can prove that

Y_{t}=Y^{\prime}_{t}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t\in[0,T].

Next arguing once again as in the proof of the existence above, one can also prove that:

Z_{t}=Z_{t}^{\prime}\text{ and }V_{t}=V^{\prime}_{t}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall t\in[0,T].

The proof Theorem
4.1
is now complete.

5
Application to stochastic optimal control problem

Now we are going to highlight the link between stochastic optimal control, when the noise is of gaussian and Poisson types and BSDEs with Poisson jump.

Let
D
be the space of controls, the space of the form
D_{1}\times D_{2}
, where
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are two separable metric spaces whose Borel
\sigma
-algebra are
B(D_{1})
and
B(D_{1})
respectively.
Now let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{D}_{2}
) be the set of
\mathcal{P}
-measurable processes with values in
D_{1}
(resp.
D_{2}
).
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}\times\mathcal{D}_{2}
is called the set of admissible controls.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x_{t}
be the solution of the following standard functional differential equation.

x_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,x_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\gamma(s,x_{s^{-}},w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds),

(5.1)

where the mappings
\gamma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
satisfy the following:

(i)

\sigma
is
\mathcal{P}
-measurable;

(ii)

there exists a positive constant C such that
|\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime})|\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|
and
|\sigma(t,x)|\leq C(1+|x|),
for any
(x,x^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\leq T
;

(iii)

for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the matrix
\sigma(t,x)
is invertible and
|\sigma^{-1}(t,x)|\leq C
for some constant
C
;

(iv)

for any
(t,x,x^{\prime},w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{U}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

||\gamma(t,x,w)-\gamma(t,x^{\prime},w)||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq C|x-x^{\prime}|\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ ||\gamma(t,x,w)||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq C(1+|x|)

According to Theorem 1.19 in
[
14
]
the process
(x_{t})_{t\leq T}
exists. Furthermore for
m\geq 2

(x_{t})_{t\leq T}
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\left[||x||_{t}^{m}\right]<+\infty
, for all
t
where
||x||_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}|x_{s}|
, (see
[
7
]
, Proposition 1.1).

Now we are given four functions
\varphi
,
c
,
g
and
h
:

(A.1)

\varphi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that:

(i)

for every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
the function
\varphi(t,x,.):\bar{u}\rightarrow\varphi(t,x,\bar{u})
is continuous,

(ii)

there exists a real constant
K_{1}>0
such that:

|\varphi(t,x,\bar{u})|\leq K_{1}(1+|x|)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall(t,x,\bar{u})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1};

(5.2)

(iii)

there exists a positive constant
C
such that

|\sigma^{-1}(t,x)\varphi(t,x,\bar{u})|\leq C\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall(t,x,\bar{u})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1};

(5.3)

(A.2)

c:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

(i)

for every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
the function
c(t,x,.):\bar{u}\rightarrow c(t,x,\bar{u})
is continuous,

(ii)

there exists a positive constant
K_{2}
such that:

|c(t,x,\bar{u})|\leq K_{2}(1+|x|)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall(t,x,\bar{u})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1};

(5.4)

(A.3)

g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{2}\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

(i)

for every
(t,x,w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{U}
the function
g(t,x,.,w):\check{u}\rightarrow g(t,x,\check{u},w)
is continuous,

(ii)

there exist two positives constants
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
such that for every
(t,x,\check{u},w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{2}\times\mathcal{U}

|g(t,x,\check{u},w)|\leq\alpha_{1}|w|\mathbf{1}_{\{|w|\leq 1\}}+\alpha_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|w|>1\}};

(5.5)

(A.4)

h:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{2}\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

(i)

for every
(t,x,w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{U}
the function
h(t,x,.,w):\check{u}\rightarrow h(t,x,\check{u},w)
is continuous,

(ii)

there exists a constant
C
such that:

||h(t,x,\check{u},w)||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}\leq C(1+|x|)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall(t,x,\check{u},w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{2}\times\mathcal{U};

(5.6)

For any
u=(\bar{u},\check{u})\in\mathcal{D}
, let
L^{u}:=(L^{u}_{t})_{t\leq T}
be the positive local martingale solution of:

dL^{u}_{s}=L^{u}_{s^{-}}\bigg\{\sigma^{-1}(s,x_{s})\varphi(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{\mathcal{U}}g(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)\bigg\}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ L^{u}_{0}=1.

Under the previous assumptions, for a given admissible control
u\in\mathcal{D}
the process
L^{u}
is an
(\mathcal{F}_{t},P)
-martingale and
P^{u}
defined by
dP^{u}=L^{u}dP
is a probability (
[
9
]
, Corollary 5.1, pp. 244). Moreover under
P^{u}
,
\tilde{\mu}^{u}(dw,dt)=\tilde{\mu}(dw,dt)-g(t,x_{t^{-}},\check{u}_{t},w)\lambda(dw)dt
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-martingale measure and
(B^{u}_{t}=B_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{-1}(s,x_{s})\varphi(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds)_{t\leq T}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-Brownian motion.
Then the Equation (
5.1
) becomes

\displaystyle x_{t}=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,x_{s})dB^{u}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\gamma(s,x_{s^{-}},w)\tilde{\mu}^{u}(dw,ds)

(5.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\gamma(s,x_{s^{-}},w)g(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\lambda(dw)ds.

Now we shall study a stochastic control with one player. The controller, chooses an admissible control strategy
u^{*}
to maximize the amount

\int_{0}^{T}c(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\lambda(dw)ds+\xi,

(5.8)

it is then in the interest of the controller to make the amount as big as possible at least on average, which lead us to a stochastic control with

J(u)=\mathbb{E}^{u}\left[\int_{0}^{T}c(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\lambda(dw)ds+\xi\right].

(5.9)

But first let us introduce the two Hamiltonian functions
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
associated with this control problem defined on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D_{1}
and
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{U}\times D_{2}
respectively as follows

H_{1}(t,x,z,\bar{u})=z\sigma^{-1}(t,x)\varphi(t,x,\bar{u})+c(t,x,\bar{u}).

(5.10)

and

H_{2}(t,x,\nu,\check{u})=\int_{\mathcal{U}}\nu g(t,x,\check{u},w)\lambda(dw)+\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(t,x,\check{u},w)\lambda(dw).

(5.11)

According to Benes selection theorem
[
8
]
, there exist two measurable functions
\bar{u}^{*}(s,x,z)
and
\check{u}^{*}(s,x,\nu)
with value in
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
such that

H_{1}^{*}(t,x,z)=H_{1}(t,x,z,\bar{u}^{*}(s,x,z))=\sup_{\bar{u}\in D_{1}}H_{1}(t,x,z,\bar{u}),

and

H_{2}^{*}(t,x,\nu)=H_{2}(t,x,z,\check{u}^{*}(s,x,\nu))=\sup_{\check{u}\in D_{2}}H_{2}(t,x,\nu,\check{u}).

Moreover the function
H^{*}(t,x,z,\nu)=H_{1}^{*}(t,x,z)+H_{2}^{*}(t,x,\nu)
satisfies assumptions
(H.2)
and
(H.3)
.
Indeed, there exists constants
C
,
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
such that:

|H^{*}_{1}(t,x,z)|\leq C(1+||x||_{t})^{2}+c_{0}|z|\sqrt{|\ln|z||},

and

|H^{*}_{2}(t,x,\nu)|\leq C(1+||x||_{t})^{2}+c_{1}||\nu||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}.

Next to prove that the function
H^{*}
satisfies
(H.3)
it is enough to take
{v^{1}}_{t}:=|\varphi(t,x,\bar{u})|^{2}
and
{v^{2}}
such that
||{v^{2}}_{t}||_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}:=||g(t,x,\check{u},w)||^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\lambda}}
.

Now we are ready to give the main result of this section. Actually we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that (A.1), (A.2), (A.3) and (A.4) are satisfied, and let
(Y^{*},Z^{*},V^{*})
be the solution of the BSDE associated with
(\xi,H^{*})
. Then
the admissible control
u^{*}=(\bar{u}^{*},\check{u}^{*})
is optimal strategy for the stochastic control; i.e. it satisfies

J(u^{*})=Y^{*}_{0}\geq J(u)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall u\in\mathcal{D}.

Proof.
Let
(Y^{*},Z^{*},V^{*})
be the solution of the BSDE associated with
(\xi,H^{*})
. Then we have:

\displaystyle Y^{*}_{0}=\xi+\int_{0}^{T}H^{*}(s,x_{s},Z^{*}_{s},V^{*}_{s})ds-\int_{0}^{T}Z^{*}_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad=\xi+\int_{0}^{T}c(s,x_{s},\bar{u}^{*}(s,x_{s},Z^{*}_{s}))ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}^{*}(s,x_{s},V^{*}_{s}),w)\lambda(dw)ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}Z^{*}_{s}dB^{u^{*}}_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}^{u^{*}}(dw,ds).

Since
\left(\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{s}dB^{u^{*}}_{s}\right)_{t\leq T}
and
\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}^{u^{*}}(dw,ds)\right)_{t\leq T}
are martingales, and since
Y^{*}_{0}
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable hence deterministic, then by taking the expectation we obtain

Y^{*}_{0}=\mathbb{E}^{u^{*}}\left[\xi+\int_{0}^{T}c(s,x_{s},\bar{u}^{*}(s,x_{s},Z^{*}_{s}))ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}^{*}(s,x_{s},V^{*}_{s}),w)\lambda(dw)ds\right].

It follows that

Y^{*}_{0}=J(u^{*}).

Let us now show that
Y^{*}_{0}\geq J(u)\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall u\in\mathcal{D}.
Actually

\displaystyle Y^{*}_{0}=\xi+\int_{0}^{T}H^{*}(s,x_{s},Z^{*}_{s},V^{*}_{s})ds-\int_{0}^{T}Z^{*}_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\xi+\int_{0}^{T}H(s,x_{s},Z^{*}_{s},V^{*}_{s},\bar{u}_{s},\check{u}_{s})ds-\int_{0}^{T}Z^{*}_{s}dB_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}(dw,ds)

\displaystyle\qquad=\xi+\int_{0}^{T}c(s,x_{s},\bar{u}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}h(s,x_{s^{-}},\check{u}_{s},w)\lambda(dw)ds-\int_{0}^{T}Z^{*}_{s}dB^{u}_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}^{u}(dw,ds),

where
H=H_{1}+H_{2}
. Once more since
\left(\int_{0}^{t}Z^{*}_{s}dB^{u^{*}}_{s}\right)_{t\leq T}
and
\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{U}}V^{*}_{s}(w)\tilde{\mu}^{u^{*}}(dw,ds)\right)_{t\leq T}
are martingales we take the expectation and we get

J(u^{*})=Y^{*}_{0}\geq J(u).

This finishes the proof.

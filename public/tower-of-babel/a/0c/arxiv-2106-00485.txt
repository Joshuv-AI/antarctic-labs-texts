Robust a-posteriori error estimates for weak Galerkin method for the convection-diffusion problem
Natasha Sharma

Abstract
We present a robust a posteriori error estimator for the weak Galerkin finite element method applied to stationary convection-diffusion equations in the convection-dominated regime. The estimator provides global upper and lower bounds of the error and is robust in the sense that upper and lower bounds are uniformly bounded with respect to the diffusion coefficient. Results of the numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the performance of the error estimator.

Introduction

We consider the following convection-diffusion equations as our model problem

\displaystyle-{\operatorname{div}}\big(\varepsilon\nabla u\big)+{\operatorname{div}}\big({\boldsymbol{b}}u\big)+au

\displaystyle=f\,\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u

Weak Galerkin Formulation

Let
\{\mathscr{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a uniformly shape-regular partition of

\Omega
into rectangular or triangular cells. For any
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, let

denote the diameter of
T
,

, the mesh size of
\mathscr{T}_{h}
and

denote the boundary of T.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{h}
and
\bar{\mathscr{E}_{h}}
denote the collection of all the interior edges and all the edges associated with the triangulation
\mathscr{T}_{h}
respectively.
Also, for any
E\in\bar{\mathscr{E}_{h}}
, let
h_{E}
denote the length of
E
.
We assume that there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for each
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{T}\leq\kappa h_{E}

(2.1)

We also introduce the shorthand notation for broken inner products over

\displaystyle\left(f,g\right)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\left<f,g\right>_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\left<f,g\right>_{\partial T}=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\ \int\limits_{\partial T}f\ gdx.

In the above, we have used the shorthand
\partial T
to denote the boundary of each cell

.
The notation

denotes the

norm over any

or

Here and in the sequel, we employ the standard notation for well-known Lebesque and Sobolev spaces and norms defined on them (cf., e.g,

2.1
Weak Differential Operators

Following
[
18
]
, we introduce the definitions of the weak gradient and divergence operators. For any
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, we
let
V(T)
, denote the space of weak functions on T as

\displaystyle V(T)

\displaystyle:=\Big\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}\in L^{2}(T),v_{b}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial T),\Big\}

and define the weak gradient as follows.

Definition 2.1

(Weak Gradient)
.

Given
v\in V(T),

(\nabla_{w}v,{\boldsymbol{q}})_{T}:=-\int\limits_{T}v_{0}{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{q}}\ dx+\int\limits_{\partial T}v_{b}{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}\ ds\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{q}}\in H(div;T),

where
{\boldsymbol{n}}
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial T
and

H(div;T):=\{{\boldsymbol{q}}:{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{q}}\in L^{2}(T)\}.

On
T
, let
P_{k}(T)
denote the space of all polynomials with degree no greater than
k
. For a given integer
k\geq 1
, let
V_{h,k}
be the weak Galerkin finite element space corresponding to
\mathscr{T}_{h}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle V_{h,k}:\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\ :\ v_{0}|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),\ v_{b}|_{\partial T}\in P_{k}(\partial T),\ T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}\},

and let
V_{h}^{0}
be its subspace defined as:

\displaystyle V_{h,k}^{0}:\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h,k}\ :\ \ v_{b}=0,\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

We introduce the definition of the discrete weak gradient as described in
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.2

(Discrete Weak Gradient)
.

For any
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h,k}
and for any
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
,
the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,k}v\in P_{k-1}(T)^{2}
is defined on
T

as the unique polynomial satisfying

(\nabla_{w,k}v,{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{T}=-(v_{0},{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{T}+\langle v_{b},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in P_{k-1}(T)^{2},

(2.2)

where
{\boldsymbol{n}}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial T
.

We note that since the right-hand side of (
2.2
) defines a bounded linear functional on
H(div;T)
, the definition of discrete weak gradient (
2.2
) is well defined for
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in H(div;T)
as well.
Thus,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w,k}v,{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{T}

\displaystyle=-(v_{0},{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{T}+\langle v_{b},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{w}v,{\boldsymbol{\psi}})_{T},\quad{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in H(div;T).

(2.3)

Next, we introduce the weak divergence operator by first defining the space of weak vector-valued functions on
T
as

{\boldsymbol{V}}(T):=\Big\{\{{\boldsymbol{v_{0}}},{\boldsymbol{v_{b}}}\}:{\boldsymbol{v_{0}}}\in L^{2}(T)^{2},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v_{b}}}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial T)\Big\},

so that the weak divergence operator can be defined as follows.

Definition 2.3

(Weak Divergence)
.

For
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in{\boldsymbol{V}}(T)
,

(\nabla_{w}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},\psi)_{T}:=-({\boldsymbol{v_{0}}},\nabla\psi)_{T}+\langle{\boldsymbol{v_{b}}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}},\psi\rangle_{\partial T}\quad\psi\in H^{1}(T)

where

H^{1}(T):=\{v:\nabla v\in L^{2}(T)\}.

Following
[
17
]
, the discrete weak divergence is defined as follows.

Definition 2.4

(Discrete Weak Divergence)
.

Given
v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}\in V_{h,k}
and for any
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, a discrete weak divergence
\nabla_{w,k}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}v)\in P_{k}(T)
associated with
{\boldsymbol{b}}
can be defined as the unique polynomial satisfying

(\nabla_{w,k}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}v),\psi)_{T}=-({\boldsymbol{b}}v_{0},\nabla\psi)_{T}+\langle{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}v_{b},\psi\rangle_{\partial T}\quad\forall\psi\in P_{k}(T),

(2.4)

where
{\boldsymbol{n}}
is the outward unit normal to
\partial T
.

Notice that the above definition
2.4
holds true for
{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\in H^{1}(T)
. This is because the right-hand side of (
2.4
) defines a bounded linear functional on
H^{1}(T)
.
Thus for
\psi\in H^{1}(T)
, we have

(\nabla_{w,k}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}v),\psi)_{T}=-({\boldsymbol{b}}v_{0},\nabla\psi)_{T}+\langle{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}v_{b},\psi\rangle_{\partial T}=(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}v),\psi)_{T}.

(2.5)

In view of (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we drop the subscript “
k
" from the discrete weak differential operator
\nabla_{w,k}
and instead adopt the notation
\nabla_{w}
and
\nabla_{w}\cdot
whenever the context is clear.

2.2
Weak Galerkin Method

For
w_{h}=\{w_{h0},w_{hb}\}
and
v_{h}=\{v_{h0},v_{hb}\}
in
V_{h,k}
, we define a bilinear form as

\displaystyle a_{h}(w_{h},v_{h}):=

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\nabla_{w}w_{h},\nabla_{w}v_{h})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+(aw_{h0}+\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}w_{h}),v_{h0})_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle+s(w_{h},v_{h}),

(2.6)

where
s(w_{h},v_{h})=\left<\tau^{+}(w_{h0}-w_{hb}),v_{h0}-v_{hb}\right>_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}
with

\tau^{+}
defining the stabilization parameter defined as:

\displaystyle\tau^{+}|_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}})\ {{{\boldsymbol{I}}}}_{{\partial T}^{+}}+\varepsilon(\kappa h_{T})^{-1}+(\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\varepsilon^{-1}+1)h_{T}+\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1},\quad\forall T\in\mathscr{T}_{h},

with the constant
\kappa>0
introduced in (
2.1
) and

\displaystyle I_{{\partial T}^{+}}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }{\boldsymbol{b}}(x)\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}(x)\geq 0,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A weak Galerkin approximation for (
1.1
) amounts to
seeking
u_{h}=\{u_{h0},u_{hb}\}\in V_{h,k}^{0}
satisfying the following equation:

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})=(f,v_{h0})\quad

\displaystyle\forall v_{h}=\{v_{h0},v_{hb}\}\in V_{h}^{0}.

(2.7)

We equip the space
V_{h}^{0}
with the following weak Galerkin energy norm

\displaystyle|||{v_{h}}|||^{2}

\displaystyle:=\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla_{w,k}v_{h}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\lVert{v_{0}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\left<\tau(v_{h0}-v_{hb}),v_{h0}-v_{hb}\right>_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}.

(2.8)

where

\displaystyle\tau|_{\partial T}=|{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}|+\big(\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}+\varepsilon(\kappa h_{T})^{-1}+\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\varepsilon^{-1}+1)h_{T}\big)\quad\forall T\in\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}.

The present work modifies the WG method of Lin and co-authors
[
17
, eq. (2.8)]
for solving the
convection-diffusion equation with an additional diffusion-dependent stability term
\left<(\varepsilon\kappa h_{T}^{-1}+h_{T})(w_{h0}-w_{hb}),v_{h0}-v_{hb}\right>_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}
. The presence of this additional stability term enables us to bound several terms of the a posteriori error estimator.

Associated with the solution
u
satisfying (
1.1a
)–(
1.1b
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
satisfying (
2.7
), we introduce the following weak function measuring the error

\displaystyle e_{h}:=\{e_{h0},e_{hb}\}=\{u_{0}-u_{h0},u_{b}-u_{hb}\},

(2.9)

where
u_{0}
and
u_{b}
are understood to be the restrictions of
u
to the interior and the boundary of each
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
.
Furthermore, following the discussion in
[
18
, Section 3]
, for any
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, an application of Green formula reveals

\displaystyle(\nabla u,{\boldsymbol{q}})_{T}

\displaystyle=-(u,{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{q}})_{T}+\langle u,{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=-(u_{0},{\operatorname{div}}{\boldsymbol{q}})_{T}+\langle u_{b},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{q}}\rangle_{\partial T}=(\nabla_{w}u,{\boldsymbol{q}})_{T},\quad{\boldsymbol{q}}\in P_{m-1}(T)^{2}.

This allows us to measure the error in the energy norm:

\displaystyle|||{u-u_{h}}|||^{2}=\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla_{w}u-\nabla_{w}u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\lVert{\tau^{1/2}(u_{h0}-u_{hb})}\rVert_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\lVert{u-u_{h0}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}

(2.10)

where
\nabla_{w}u_{h}
is understood to be the discrete weak gradient
\nabla_{w,k}u_{h}
of
u_{h}\in V_{h,k}
while
\nabla_{w}u
is the classical derivative of
u
.

The well-posedness of the WG formulation is guaranteed thanks to the following Lemma which appeared in of
[
17
, Section 3.1]
and readily applies to our WG form.

Lemma 2.1

(Coercivity and Continuity)
.

There exists constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that there holds

\displaystyle a_{h}(w,v)

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}|||{w}|||\ |||{v}|||\quad\forall w,\ v\in V_{h},

(2.11)

\displaystyle c_{2}|||{v}|||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}(v,v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h}.

(2.12)

Proof.

The proof has been provided in subsection 3.1 of
[
17
]
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

While the above Lemma
2.1
guarantees the well-posedness of the WG method (
2.7
), the constant
c_{1}
appearing in (
2.11
) have an undesirable dependence on
\varepsilon^{-1}
. This dependence arises when trying to control the term
(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u),w)
. Hence, our a posteriori error analysis cannot rely on the continuity property to control this term.
Instead, inspired by the strategy in
[
13
]
, we control this term by
including the following semi-norm
|\cdot|_{*}
to our energy norm (
2.8
).

Definition 2.5

(Operator)
.

For any
{\boldsymbol{q}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
, we define the following semi-norm
|\cdot|_{*}
as

\displaystyle|{\boldsymbol{q}}|_{*}=\sup_{w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\nabla w\ dx}{|||{w}|||}.

(2.13)

The definition above is well defined because
{\boldsymbol{q}}
admits the following Helmholtz decomposition

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{q}}=\nabla\phi+{\boldsymbol{q}}_{0},

(2.14)

where
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
uniquely solves

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla w\ dx=\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\nabla w\ dx\quad\forall w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

and
{\boldsymbol{q}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{q}}-\nabla\phi
satisfies the divergence-free property

\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{q}}_{0}\cdot\nabla w\ dx=0\quad\forall w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

The decomposition (
2.14
) is unique and orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
.

Consequently, we have the following upper bound:

For
u_{h}=\{u_{h0},u_{hb}\}\in V_{h,k},\ v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h}),v)

\displaystyle\leq|{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h0}|_{*}|||{v}|||,

(2.15)

where

|||{v}|||^{2}:=\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla v}\rVert^{2}+\lVert{v}\rVert^{2}
. Inequality (
2.15
) holds because

(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h}),v)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}=-({\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h0},\nabla v)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\langle{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{hb},v\rangle_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}

and using the fact that
\langle{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{hb},v\rangle_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}=0
.

Remark 2.2

(Practical estimation of
|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}
)
.

Following the arguments presented in
[

, we use the following inequality

|\b{(}u-u_{h0})|_{*}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\lVert{u-u_{h0}}\rVert

to serve as a computable upper bound for

.

We close this section by proving the inf-sup condition for

on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. The proof follows the arguments presented in
[
13
]
. However, since our definition of the energy norm

differs from the one used in
[

]
, we include the proof below for completeness.

Theorem 2.1

(inf-sup condition)
.

There exists a constant
C>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{h}(u,v)}{(|||{u}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*})|||{v}|||}\geq C>0.

(2.16)

Proof.

Let
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
0<\theta<1
. There exists
w_{\theta}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle|||{w_{\theta}}|||=1,\quad(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u),w_{\theta})\geq\ \theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}.

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\nabla u,-\nabla w_{\theta})+(au,-w_{\theta})

\displaystyle\leq\lVert{\varepsilon^{1/2}\nabla u}\rVert\ \lVert{\varepsilon^{1/2}\nabla w_{\theta}}\rVert+\|a\|_{\infty}\lVert{u}\rVert\ \lVert{w_{\theta}}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,\|a\|_{\infty}\}\ |||{u}|||.

Thus,

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,w_{\theta})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon\nabla u,\nabla w_{\theta})+(au,w_{\theta})+(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u),w_{\theta})

\displaystyle\geq-c_{1}\ |||{u}|||\ +\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*},

(2.17)

where
c_{1}=\max\{1,\|a\|_{\infty}\}
.
Noting that

Next, we define
v_{\theta}=u+\alpha w_{\theta}
where
\alpha>0
is chosen suitably.
Using

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,u)

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla_{w}u}\rVert^{2}+(c_{0}u,u)

\displaystyle\geq c_{*}|||{u}|||^{2}\quad\text{where }c_{*}=\min\{1,c_{0}\}

(2.18)

and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{\theta})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u,u)+\alpha a_{h}(u,w_{\theta})

\displaystyle\geq c_{*}|||{u}|||^{2}+\alpha\big(-c_{1}\ |||{u}|||\ +\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}\big).

(2.19)

We pick
\alpha=c_{*}|||{u}|||/(c_{1}+1)
so that,

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,v_{\theta})

\displaystyle\geq(c_{1}+1)^{-1}c_{*}\big(|||{u}|||+\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}\big)|||{u}|||\quad\text{and,}

\displaystyle|||{v_{\theta}}|||

\displaystyle=(c_{1}+1)^{-1}\{c_{1}+1+c_{*}\}|||{u}|||\quad

\displaystyle\text{ thus,}\quad\frac{a_{h}(u,v_{\theta})}{|||{v_{\theta}}|||}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{*}\big(|||{u}|||+\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}\big)}{\{c_{1}+1+c_{*}\}}=C\big(|||{u}|||+\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}\big)

where
C=c_{*}\{c_{1}+1+c_{*}\}^{-1}>0
.

Consequently, for any
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ \theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle C\big(|||{u}|||+\theta|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{h}(u,v_{\theta})}{|||{v_{\theta}}|||}\leq\sup_{v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{a_{h}(u,v)}{(|||{u}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}u|_{*})|||{v}|||}

from which, the result follows.
∎

3
A Posteriori Error Analysis

In this section, we present a residual-based estimator and prove its reliability and efficiency.

Definition 3.1

(Estimator)
.

We introduce the estimator in terms of
the cell and edge indicators defined as shown below:

\displaystyle\eta_{h}^{2}:=\sum\limits_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\Big(\eta_{T,1}^{2}+\eta_{T,2}^{2}\Big)+\sum\limits_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\eta_{E}^{2},

(3.1)

where the cell and edge residuals are

\displaystyle\eta_{T,1}^{2}:=\alpha_{T}^{2}\lVert{R_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{T},\quad\text{with}\ R_{h}:=f_{h}+{\operatorname{div}}(\varepsilon\nabla_{w}u_{h})-\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}\ u_{h0})-a_{h}u_{h0},

\displaystyle\eta_{T,2}^{2}:=\langle\tau(u_{h0}-u_{hb}),u_{h0}-u_{hb}\rangle_{\partial T},

\displaystyle\eta_{E}^{2}:=\alpha_{E}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\lVert{J_{h}}\rVert_{E}^{2},\text{ with }J_{h}:=\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\varepsilon{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right],

(3.2)

and the weights are according to
[
20
, equation (3.4)]

\displaystyle\alpha_{T}=\min\{h_{T}\varepsilon^{-1/2},c_{0}^{-1/2}\},\ \text{ and }\ \alpha_{E}=\min\{h_{E}\varepsilon^{-1/2},c_{0}^{-1/2}\}.

(3.3)

We note that if
c_{0}=0
, we define the weights as follows

\displaystyle\alpha_{T}=\min\{h_{T}\varepsilon^{-1/2},1\},\ \text{ and }\ \alpha_{E}=\min\{h_{E}\varepsilon^{-1/2},1\}.

(3.4)

We also define the oscillation of data as

\displaystyle osc(f,a)=\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\alpha_{T}^{2}\big(\lVert{f-f_{h}}\rVert_{T}^{2}+\lVert{(a-a_{h})u_{h0}}\rVert_{T}^{2}\big)\Big)^{1/2}.

(3.5)

3.0.1
Reliability of Estimator

This subsection is devoted to proving the reliability for the estimator defined in (
3.1
). The proof of the reliability relies on decomposing the discretization error into its conforming and non-conforming component and deriving upper bounds for each component. To this end, we first introduce a suitable conforming discrete space

V_{h}^{c}=V_{h}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

For any
v_{h}=\{v_{h0},v_{hb}\}\in V_{h}
we associate a conforming approximation
v_{h0}^{c}\in V_{h}^{c}
. Construction of such an approximation is a standard DG tool in the error analysis (see
[
40
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3]
for instance).
This allows us to decompose
v_{h}
as

\displaystyle v_{h}=v_{h}^{c}+v_{h}^{r}

(3.6)

where the nonconforming component
v_{h}^{r}=\{v_{h0}-v_{h}^{c},v_{hb}-v_{h}^{c}\}\in V_{h}
.

The above conforming approximation satisfies the following properties:

\displaystyle\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{h}^{c}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{E}h_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[v_{h0}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]^{2}\ ds,

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\lVert{\nabla v_{h0}-\nabla v_{h}^{c}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{E}h_{E}^{-1}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[v_{h0}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]^{2}\ ds.

(3.7b)

Thanks to the single-valuedness of
v_{hb}
over each edge
E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}
, the jump term in the rhs of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[v_{h0}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]^{2}\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[v_{h0}-v_{hb}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]^{2}\ ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}2(v_{h0}-v_{hb})^{2}\ ds.

(3.8)

Using (
3.8
) in (
3.7
) and by using
\kappa h_{T}\leq h_{E},
and
h_{E}\leq h_{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{h}^{c}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}h_{T}\int_{\partial T}(v_{h0}-v_{hb})^{2}\ ds,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lVert{\nabla v_{h0}-\nabla v_{h}^{c}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}(\kappa h_{T})^{-1}\int_{\partial T}(v_{h0}-v_{hb})^{2}\ ds.

(3.10)

The lemma below provides bounds for the non-conforming component.

Lemma 3.1

(Nonconforming term bound)
.

For
v_{h}\in V_{h}
admitting the decomposition (
3.6
) the following hold true:

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h}^{r},w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\{1,\lVert{a}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\}\ |||{v_{h}^{r}}|||\ |||{w}|||,\quad w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

(3.11)

\displaystyle|||{v_{h}^{r}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}v_{h0}^{r}|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}(\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}+\varepsilon(\kappa h_{T})^{-1}+h_{T})\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{hb}}\rVert^{2}_{\partial T}.

(3.12)

Proof.

To see (
3.11
), we apply the definitions of the WG form and weak divergence

\displaystyle a_{h}(v_{h}^{r},w)

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r},\nabla w)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}-({\boldsymbol{b}}v_{h0}^{r},\nabla w)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+(av_{h0}^{r},w)_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}+\langle{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}v_{hb}^{r},w\rangle_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(|||{v_{h}^{r}}|||+\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\lVert{v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert+\lVert{a}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert{v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert\Big)|||{w}|||,

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,\lVert{a}\rVert_{\infty},\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\}|||{v_{h}^{r}}|||\ |||{w}|||,\quad w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Here we have used (
3.9
) and
\langle{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{hb}^{r},w\rangle_{\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}}=0
.

Next, for deriving (
3.12
), we use the definition (
2.2
) of the weak gradient in conjunction with the trace, inverse and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities and (
3.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\lVert{\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}}\rVert_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla v_{h0}^{r},\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r})_{T}-\langle v_{h0}^{r}-v_{b}^{r},{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\lVert{\nabla v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert_{T}+Ch_{T}^{-1/2}\lVert{v_{h0}^{r}-v_{hb}^{r}}\rVert_{\partial T}\Big)\ \lVert{\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}}\rVert_{T}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/2}\lVert{\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}}\rVert_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\min\{1,C\}\Big(\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert^{2}_{T}+\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{hb}}\rVert^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{1/2}

Thus, summing

\displaystyle\text{over all }T\in\mathscr{T}_{h},

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{E}\varepsilon h_{E}^{-1}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[v_{h0}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]^{2}\ ds+\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{hb}}\rVert^{2}_{\partial T}.

(3.13)

Using (
3.8
) in (
3.13
) and (
3.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert{\nabla_{w}v_{h}^{r}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}^{2}+\lVert{v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}(\varepsilon h_{T}^{-1}+\varepsilon(\kappa h_{T})^{-1}+h_{T})\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{hb}}\rVert^{2}_{\partial T}.

(3.14)

Finally, for bounding
|{\boldsymbol{b}}v_{h0}^{r}|_{*}
, we use (
3.9
), Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of
|{\boldsymbol{b}}v_{h0}^{r}|_{*}

\displaystyle|{\boldsymbol{b}}v_{h0}^{r}|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1/2}\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert{v_{h0}^{r}}\rVert_{\mathscr{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\rVert_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\varepsilon^{-1}h_{T}\lVert{v_{h0}-v_{hb}}\rVert^{2}_{\partial T}\Big)^{1/2}.

(3.15)

∎

Next, we proceed to control the conforming term by first deriving the error equation. To this end, we introduce the following continuous subspace

V_{h,1}^{c}:=\{w\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):w|_{K}\in P_{1}(T),\ T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}\}

and observe that by setting
v_{b}
as trace of
v
on all the edges
E\in\partial\mathscr{T}_{h}
,

V_{h,1}^{c}
can be naturally embedded in
V_{h}
. This approach was used within the adaptive WG framework by Chen et al.
[
34
]
for obtaining partial orthogonality for second order elliptic problems.

We also introduce the following Clément interpolation operator specially designed for convection diffusion problems by Verfürth
[
20
, Lemma 3.3]
and references therein.
We denote this operator by
\Pi_{h}:H_{0}^{1}\rightarrow V_{h,1}^{c}
satisfying
|||{\Pi_{h}v}|||\lesssim|||{v}|||

and

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\alpha_{T}^{-2}\lVert{v-\Pi_{h}v}\rVert^{2}_{T}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|||{v}|||,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\varepsilon^{1/2}\alpha_{E}^{-1}\lVert{v-\Pi_{h}v}\rVert_{E}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|||{v}|||,\quad v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(3.17)

It is easy to see that for
u
satisfying (
1.1
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
satisfying the WG form (
2.7
), the following holds true:

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{h}^{c})=0\quad\ \forall v_{h}^{c}\in V_{h,1}^{c}.

(3.18)

As a consequence, we have the following error equation.

Lemma 3.2

(Error Equation)
.

Let
u
solve (
1.1a
)–(
1.1b
),
u_{h}\in V_{h}
solve (
2.7
) decomposed as
u_{h}=u_{h}^{c}+u_{h}^{r}
according to the decomposition (
3.6
).
Then, for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=(osc(f,a),\hat{v})+(R_{h},\hat{v})-\langle J_{h},\hat{v}\rangle

(3.19)

where
\hat{v}=v-\Pi^{c}_{h}v
and
osc(f,a)
,
R_{h}
and
J_{h}
are defined in (
3.5
) (
3.1
) respectively.

Proof.

Since the exact solution
u
solves (
1.1a
)–(
1.1b
), we obtain, by applying (
3.18
) for
\Pi^{c}_{h}v\in V_{h,1}^{c}
,

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=(f,v)-a_{h}(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=(f,v-\Pi^{c}_{h}v)-a_{h}(u_{h},v-\Pi^{c}_{h}v)+\{(f,\Pi^{c}_{h}v)-a_{h}(u_{h},\Pi^{c}_{h}v)\}

\displaystyle=(f-f_{h}+a_{h}u_{h0}-au_{h0},\hat{v})+(R_{h},\hat{v})-(J_{h},\hat{v}).

(3.20)

To obtain (
3.2
), we have added and subtracted the data
(f_{h}-a_{h}u_{h0},\hat{v})
and for the last two terms of (
3.2
) we apply integration by parts to
\varepsilon(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}\hat{v})
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Conforming term bound)
.

Let
u
solve (
1.1a
)–(
1.1b
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
solve (
2.7
) and express
u_{h}=u_{h}^{c}+u_{h}^{r}
according to (
3.6
).
For any
v\neq 0\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle|||{u-u_{h}^{c}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h}^{c})|_{*}\lesssim\frac{a_{h}(u_{h}^{r},v)}{|||{v}|||}+\eta_{h}+osc(f,a).

(3.21)

Proof.

Thanks to the inf-sup condition (
2.16
), for any
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle|||{u-u_{h}^{c}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h}^{c})|_{*}\lesssim\frac{a_{h}(u-u_{h}^{c},v)}{|||{v}|||}.

(3.22)

Now, for the rhs of (
3.22
), using
u_{h}^{c}=u_{h}-u_{h}^{r}
and (
3.19
) we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h}^{c},v)

\displaystyle=(f,v)-a_{h}(u_{h}-u_{h}^{r},v)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u_{h}^{r},v)+(f,v)-a_{h}(u_{h},v)

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u_{h}^{r},v)+(osc(f,a),\hat{v})+(R_{h},\hat{v})-\langle J_{h},\hat{v}\rangle.

(3.23)

In view of (
3.0.1
) and (
3.22
) and using the approximation properties (
3.16
) and (
3.16
) we obtain the desired upper bound.
∎

We are now in the position to prove the reliability of the estimator.

Theorem 3.1

(Reliability)
.

Let
u
solve (
1.1
) and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the WG approximation to (
2.7
). Then,

\displaystyle|||{u-u_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}\lesssim\eta_{h}+osc(f,a).

(3.24)

Proof.

Decompose
u_{h}=u_{h}^{c}+u_{h}^{r}
where
u_{h}^{c}\in V_{h}^{c}
. By the triangle inequality and Lemma
3.3
, for any nonzero
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle|||{u-u_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq|||{u-u_{h}^{c}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h}^{c})|_{*}+|||{u_{h}^{r}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h0}^{r}|_{*}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{a_{h}(u_{h}^{r},v)}{|||{v}|||}+\eta_{h}+osc(f,a)+|||{u_{h}^{r}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h0}^{r}|_{*}

The result now follows thanks to Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Efficiency)
.

\displaystyle\eta_{h}\lesssim|||{u-u_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}+osc(f,a).

(3.25)

Proof.

Efficiency will be shown through the following lemmas which employ the bubble function techniques introduced by Verfürth
[
20
]
.
We remark that the arguments presented here are simpler than its DG counterpart presented in
[
13
]
thanks to the simpler weak form.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Cell efficiency)
.

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\big(\eta_{T,1}^{2}+\eta_{T,2}^{2}\big)\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim|||{u-u_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}+osc(f,{\boldsymbol{b}},a).

(3.26)

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\eta_{T,2}^{2}\leq|||{u-u_{h}}|||^{2}
.
Now for bounding
\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\eta_{T,1}^{2}
, we let
b_{T}
denote the element-bubble function described in
[
20
, p. 1771]
for any cell
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
.
We first take

v_{T}=\alpha_{T}^{2}R_{h}b_{T}.

Since
R_{h}
belongs to a finite dimensional space on
T
, a local equivalence of norms yields

\displaystyle\int_{T}\alpha_{T}^{2}|R_{h}|^{2}\ dx\lesssim\int_{T}\alpha_{T}^{2}R_{h}^{2}\ b_{T}\ dx=\int_{T}R_{h}\ v_{T}\ dx.

(3.27)

Since the exact solution
u
satisfies the strong form
-\varepsilon{\operatorname{div}}\nabla u+{\operatorname{div}}({\boldsymbol{b}}u)+au=f
on each
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
, by adding and subtracting the exact data and applying integration by parts to
\int_{T}{\operatorname{div}}(\varepsilon\nabla_{w}u-\varepsilon\nabla_{w}u_{h})v_{T}\ dx
in conjunction with
v_{T}|_{\partial T}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T}R_{h}\ v_{T}\ dx=\int_{T}\varepsilon(\nabla_{w}u-\nabla_{w}u_{h})\cdot\nabla_{w}v_{T}\ dx\ +

\displaystyle\int_{T}\big(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u)-\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h})+a(u-u_{h0})\big)\nabla v_{T}\ dx\ +

\displaystyle\int_{T}(f_{h}-f+(a_{h}-a)u_{h0})v_{T}\ dx.

(3.28)

Now combining (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), summing over all
T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}
and applying Cauchy Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\eta_{T,1}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(|||{e_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}e_{h}|_{*}+osc(f,a)\big)\Big(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}|||{v_{T}}|||^{2}\ +\alpha_{T}^{-2}\lVert{v_{T}}\rVert_{T}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(3.29)

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\eta_{T,1}^{2}\lesssim\Big(|||{e_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}e_{h}|_{*}+osc(f,a)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\ \Big(|||{v_{T}}|||^{2}\ +\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\alpha_{T}^{-2}\lVert{v_{T}}\rVert_{T}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(3.30)

Using the following property of the element bubble function

|||{\sigma b_{T}}|||\lesssim\alpha_{T}^{-1}\lVert{\sigma}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)},\ \lVert{b_{T}}\rVert_{\infty}=1

|||{v_{T}}|||^{2}\lesssim\alpha_{T}^{2}\lVert{R_{h}}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}
and
\alpha_{T}^{-2}\lVert{v_{T}}\rVert_{T}^{2}\lesssim\alpha_{T}^{2}\lVert{R_{h}}\rVert_{L^{2}(T)}

the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(edge efficiency)
.

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\eta_{E}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim|||{u-u_{h}}|||+|{\boldsymbol{b}}(u-u_{h0})|_{*}+osc(f).

(3.31)

Proof.

For any edge
E\in\mathscr{E}_{h}
, let
b_{E}
denote the edge-bubble function described in
[
20
, p. 1771]
.
We first take

v_{E}=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\alpha_{E}J_{h}b_{E}.

Since
R_{h}
belongs to a finite dimensional space on
T
, once again we rely on a local equivalence of norms to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{E}J_{h}\ v_{E}\ ds\lesssim\int_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\varepsilon{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla_{w}u_{h}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\ v_{E}\ ds=-\int_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\varepsilon{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla_{w}e_{h}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\ v_{E}\ ds.

(3.32)

Let
{\omega_{E}}\subset\mathscr{T}_{h}
denote the compact support of
E
and
v_{\omega_{E}}
denote the extension of edge bubble function to
{\omega_{E}}
.

\displaystyle-\int_{E}\left[\hskip-3.5pt\left[\varepsilon{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot\nabla_{w}e_{h}\right]\hskip-3.5pt\right]\ v_{E}\ ds=-\int_{\omega_{E}}{\operatorname{div}}\varepsilon(\nabla_{w}u-\nabla_{w}u_{h})v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx\

\displaystyle-\ \int_{\omega_{E}}\varepsilon(\nabla_{w}u-\nabla_{w}u_{h})\cdot\nabla v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx\

\displaystyle\pm\ \int_{\omega_{E}}\big(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u)-\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}u_{h})+au-a_{h}u_{h0}\big)\nabla v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx.\

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega_{E}}osc(f,a)v_{\omega_{E}}dx-\ \int_{\omega_{E}}\big(\nabla_{w}\cdot({\boldsymbol{b}}e_{h})+ae_{h0}\big)v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\omega_{E}}R_{h}v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx-\int_{\omega_{E}}\varepsilon\nabla_{w}e_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{\omega_{E}}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\big(osc(f,a)+|{\boldsymbol{b}}e_{h0}|_{*}+\lVert{a}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert{e_{h0}}\rVert\big)\lVert{v_{\omega_{E}}}\rVert_{\omega_{E}}+\sum_{T\in\omega_{E}}\alpha_{T}\eta_{T,1}\alpha_{T}^{-1}\lVert{v_{\omega_{E}}}\rVert_{T}

\displaystyle+\lVert{\varepsilon^{1/2}\nabla_{w}e_{h}}\rVert_{\omega_{E}}\lVert{\varepsilon^{1/2}\nabla v_{\omega_{E}}}\rVert_{\omega_{E}}

(3.33)

Using the properties of the edge bubble function

\lVert{\sigma b_{E}}\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{E}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/4}\alpha_{E}^{1/2}\lVert{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{E},

and

|||{\sigma b_{E}}|||^{2}_{\omega_{E}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{1/4}\alpha_{E}^{-1/2}\lVert{\sigma}\rVert^{2}_{E},

the assertion follows.
∎

Numerical Results

Our sequence of adaptively refined rectangular meshes is constructed by selecting those elements for refinement which possess the top
25\%
of the largest local indicators
\eta_{T}

All the experiments were performed on the unit square

Since the norm used in proving the reliability and efficiency of the estimator involves the energy norm (

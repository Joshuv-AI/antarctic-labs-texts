Non-density of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings on non-compact manifolds
Shunsuke Ichiki

Abstract
. The problem of density of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings is a classical and venerable subject in singularity theory. In 1973, Mather showed that the set of proper C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings is dense in the set of all proper mappings, which implies that the set of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings is dense in the set of all mappings if the source manifold is compact. The aim of this paper is to complement Mather’s result and to provide new information to the subject. Namely, we show that the set of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings is never dense in the set of all mappings if the source manifold is non-compact. As a corollary of this result and Mather’s result, we can obtain a characterization of density of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings, i.e., the set of C 0 C^{0} -stable mappings is dense in the set of all mappings if and only if the source manifold is compact. To prove the non-density result, we provide a more essential result by using the notion of topologically critical points.

Remark 2.1

We give the following remarks on topologically critical points.

(1)

If

Suppose that

(2)

.
Then,

(3)

Suppose that

Namely, topologically critical points can be preserved by homeomorphisms although usual critical points are not necessarily preserved by them.

and it is an upgrade of
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
to a claim about a topological critical point instead of a usual critical point.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any mapping
g\in\mathscr{U}
, we have
g(N)\subset V
and there exists a sequence
\set{q_{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
N
with the following properties.

(1)

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
q_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is a topologically critical point of
g
in
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.

(2)

The set
\set{g(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})}{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
V
.

Proof of
lemma

3.1
.

From the definition of
\mathscr{U}
, we have
g(N)\subset V
.

Let
\alpha
be any positive integer.
Then, we have

\displaystyle(\psi_{p}\circ f\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+\gamma_{p}(\alpha)

for any
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\varphi_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha}^{\prime})

(=B^{n}(0,r_{\alpha}))
.
For any
q\in U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, we obtain
j^{2}g(q)\in O_{\alpha}
since we have
eq.

3.6
and
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is contained in
U_{\alpha}
which does not intersect with
U_{\beta}

(\beta\not=\alpha)
.
Hence,
(\psi_{p}\circ g\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})|_{B^{n}(0,r_{\alpha})}
satisfies
item

b
and
item

c
, which implies that there exists a topologically critical point of
(\psi_{p}\circ g\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})|_{B^{n}(0,r_{\alpha})}
in
B^{n}(0,r_{\alpha})
by
lemma

2.3
.
Namely, there exists a topologically critical point of
g
in
U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.
We denote this point by
q_{\alpha}^{\prime}
.

Since
\set{q_{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
item

1
, it is sufficient to prove that the sequence of points also satisfies
item

2
.
Let
V^{\prime}
be any open subset of
V
.
We show that
\set{g(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})}{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}\cap V^{\prime}\not=\varnothing
.
Then, by choosing
V^{\prime}
smaller, we can assume that
\psi(V^{\prime})=B^{p}(y_{0},\varepsilon)
, where
y_{0}
is a point of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\varepsilon
is a positive real number.
Note that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(3.7)

\begin{split}\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-y_{0}\right\|\leq&\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|+\\
&\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})\right\|+\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})-y_{0}\right\|.\end{split}

Since

\displaystyle\delta(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\left\|F(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-z_{0}\right\|}<\frac{1}{R_{\alpha}}

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}R_{\alpha}=\infty
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}
.
Here, note that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|<\delta(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})

by
item

a
since
j^{2}g(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\in O_{\alpha}
.
Thus, it follows that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}\Longrightarrow\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, since
q_{\alpha},q_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})\right\|=\left\|\eta_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}^{\prime}))-\gamma(\alpha)\right\|=\frac{\left\|\varphi_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|^{2}}{2}<\frac{r_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}r_{\alpha}=0
, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\alpha_{2}\Longrightarrow\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Since
(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})=\gamma(\alpha)
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\set{(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})}{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{Q}^{p}.

Hence, there exists
\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{3}>\max\set{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha_{3}})-y_{0}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Thus, we get
\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{3}}^{\prime})-y_{0}\right\|<\varepsilon
by (
3.7
) to
eq.

3.10
, which implies that
g(q_{\alpha_{3}}^{\prime})\in V^{\prime}
.
∎

STEP 3
.
The purpose of this step is to show that
\mathscr{U}_{d}
is dense in
\mathscr{U}
, where
d\geq 2
is a given integer.
Let
g\in\mathscr{U}
be an arbitrary mapping, and let
\mathscr{U}_{g}
be any open neighborhood of
g
.
Then, there exist a non-negative integer
k
and an open set
O^{\prime}
of
J^{k}(N,P)
such that

\displaystyle g\in\set{h\in C^{\infty}(N,P)}{j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}}\subset\mathscr{U}_{g}.

For the proof, it is sufficient to show that there exists a mapping
h\in\mathscr{U}_{d}
such that
j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, let
G_{\alpha,c}:N\to P
be the mapping defined by

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,c}=\psi^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g+\rho_{\alpha}c).

Lemma 3.2

(
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
\alpha
be any positive integer.
Then, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha}^{\prime}
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha,c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha}^{\prime})
.

Since
g\in\mathscr{U}
, note that there exists a sequence
\set{q_{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
N
satisfying
item

1
and
item

2
of
lemma

3.1
.
The following lemma can be also shown by the same method as
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m
be any positive integer.
Then, there exist
(m+1)
distinct positive integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m+1}
and
m
positive real numbers
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{\alpha_{m}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{m}}^{\prime})
such that for any
j\in\set{1,\ldots,m}
,

(1)

j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
,

(2)

\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|<\displaystyle\frac{r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}}{d-1}
, where
d\geq 2
is given in
theorem

2.2
.

Proof of
lemma

3.3
.

We will prove this lemma by induction on
m
.

Let
\alpha_{1}
be a positive integer.
By
lemma

3.2
, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{1},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})
.
From
lemma

3.1

item

2
, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{\alpha_{1}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{2}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}}{d-1}.

Thus, the case
m=1
holds.

We assume that the lemma holds for
m=i
, where
i
is a positive integer.
By
lemma

3.2
, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime}>r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{i+1},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})
.
From
lemma

3.1

item

2
, there exists
\alpha_{i+2}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i+1}}
satisfying
\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+2}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime}}{d-1}
.
Therefore, the case
m=i+1
holds.
∎

For simplicity, set
I=\set{1,\ldots,d-1}
.
By
lemma

3.3
in the case
m=d-1
, there exist
d
distinct positive integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
and
d-1
positive real numbers
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{\alpha_{d-1}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{d-1}}^{\prime})
such that for any
j\in I
,

(d)

j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
,

(e)

\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|<\displaystyle\frac{r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}}{d-1}
.

Let
h:N\to P
be the mapping defined by

\displaystyle h=\psi^{-1}\circ\left(\psi\circ g+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\rho_{\alpha_{i}}c_{i}\right),

where
c_{i}=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

First, we show that
j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}
.
Let
q\in N
be an arbitrary point.
In the case where
q
is an element of
(\bigcup_{j=1}^{d-1}\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}})^{c}
, since
h=g
on the open neighborhood
(\bigcup_{j=1}^{d-1}\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}})^{c}
of
q
, we have
j^{k}h(q)=j^{k}g(q)\in O^{\prime}
.
We consider the case where there exists
j\in I
satisfying
q\in\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}}
.
Since
\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}}\subset\bigcap_{i\in I\setminus\set{j}}(\supp\rho_{\alpha_{i}})^{c}
and
h=G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}
on the open neighborhood
\bigcap_{i\in I\setminus\set{j}}(\supp\rho_{\alpha_{i}})^{c}
of
q
, we obtain
j^{k}h(q)=j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}(q)
.
Moreover, since

(3.11)

\begin{split}\left\|c_{j}\right\|&=\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=j}^{d-1}\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime})\right\|\\
&<\sum_{i=j}^{d-1}\frac{r_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime}}{d-1}\\
&\leq r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime},\end{split}

we have
c_{j}\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
.
The last two inequalities in
eq.

3.11
follow from
item

e
and
r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{d-1}}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Thus, we obtain
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}(q)\in O^{\prime}
by
item

d
, which implies that
j^{k}h(q)\in O^{\prime}
.

Now, we show that
h\in\mathscr{U}_{d}
.
For any
i,j\in I
, since
\rho_{\alpha_{i}}(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})=\delta_{ij}
and
\rho_{\alpha_{i}}(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\psi\circ g+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\rho_{\alpha_{i}}c_{i}\right)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})+c_{j}=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime})=(\psi\circ h)(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime}),

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
Thus, we have
h(q_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})=\cdots=h(q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime})
.
Moreover, for any
j\in I
, the point
q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}
(resp.
q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime}
) is a topologically critical point of
h
since
h=\psi^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g+c_{j})
on an open neighborhood of
q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}
(resp.
h=g
on an open neighborhood of
q_{\alpha_{d}}^{\prime}
).
Therefore, we obtain
h\in\mathscr{U}_{d}
.

Finally, we will show that any mapping in
\mathscr{U}
is not
C^{0}
-stable.
Suppose that there exists a
C^{0}
-stable mapping
\tilde{g}
in
\mathscr{U}
.
Then, there exists an open neighborhood
\mathscr{U}_{\tilde{g}}
of
\tilde{g}
such that any mapping in
\mathscr{U}_{\tilde{g}}
is
C^{0}
-equivalent to
\tilde{g}
.
Since we have shown that
\mathscr{U}_{p+1}
is dense in
\mathscr{U}
, and topologically critical points are preserved by homeomorphisms as mentioned in
remark

2.1
item

3
, any mapping in
\mathscr{U}_{\tilde{g}}
has
(p+1)
topologically critical points with the same image.
This contradicts the fact that the set of all mappings with normal crossings is dense in
C^{\infty}(N,P)
.

\Box

Optimal Semiclassical Regularity of Projection Operators and Strong Weyl Law
Laurent Lafleche

Abstract
. Projection operators arise naturally as one-particle density operators associated to Slater determinants in fields such as quantum mechanics and the study of determinantal processes. In the context of the semiclassical approximation of quantum mechanics, projection operators can be seen as the analogue of characteristic functions of subsets of the phase space, which are discontinuous functions. We prove that projection operators indeed converge to characteristic functions of the phase space and that in terms of quantum Sobolev spaces, they exhibit the same maximal regularity as characteristic functions. This can be interpreted as a semiclassical asymptotic on the size of commutators in Schatten norms. Our study answers a question raised in [J. Chong, L. Lafleche, C. Saffirio, arXiv:2103.10946 [math.AP]] about the possibility of having projection operators as initial data. It also gives a strong convergence result in Sobolev spaces for the Weyl law in phase space.

1.
Introduction

Projection operators arise naturally in quantum mechanics, the simplest example being the projection operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
associated to a wave function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
verifying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|{\psi}\right|^{2}=1
, which is defined by

\boldsymbol{\rho}\varphi(x)=\psi(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\psi(y)}\,\varphi(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

for any
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. More generally, a projection operator can be defined as an operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
verifying
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
. In this paper, we will consider compact operators acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we denote by
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
the set of such operators. It is not difficult to see that such an operator is automatically trace class, and so if
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is self-adjoint, it can be thought of as a density operator representing the state of a quantum system.

In the classical limit, that is in units where the Planck constant
h=2\pi\,\hbar
becomes negligible, the classical analogue of a density operator is a phase space density
f(x,v)
representing the probability to find a particle at position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. With this point of view, projection operators can be thought of as the quantum analogue of functions verifying
f^{2}=f
, that is characteristic functions. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
24
]
) that characteristic functions cannot be infinitely regular, and the maximal regularity allowed for test functions can be formulated by saying that such functions are in the Besov space
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
at most for
s\leq 1/p
. It is the goal of this paper to prove that an analogue of this property holds true for projections operators.

1.1.
Phase space quantum mechanics

To make the analogy between density operators and phase space functions more precise, it is typical to introduce the Weyl quantization, which by analogy with the Fourier inversion formula associates to the function
f
the operator

(1)

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\widehat{f}(y,\xi)\,e^{2i\pi\left(y\cdot x+\xi\cdot\boldsymbol{p}\right)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

with
x
identified with the operator of multiplication by
x
and
\boldsymbol{p}=-i\hbar\nabla
the quantum analogue of the momentum, where
\nabla
denotes the gradient with respect to the
x
variable and
\hbar=h/(2\pi)
is the Planck constant. The integral kernel of this operator is then given by

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2i\pi\left(y-x\right)\cdot\xi}\,f(\tfrac{x+y}{2},h\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

One can also look at the inverse operation called the Wigner transform, that associates to an operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
a function on the phase space

f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\,y\cdot\xi/\hbar}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}(x+\tfrac{y}{2},x-\tfrac{y}{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Noticing for example that at least formally
h^{d}\tr(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
where
\tr
denotes the trace, and that
h^{d}\tr(|\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}|^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|{f(x,\xi)}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
, where
\left|{A}\right|=\sqrt{A^{*}A}
denotes the absolute value of an operator, it is natural to consider the following scaled Schatten norms

(2)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=h^{\frac{d}{p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{p}=h^{\frac{d}{p}}\tr\!\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

that are the quantum version of the phase space Lebesgue norms. On another side, the correspondence principle leads to define the quantum analogue of the gradients in the phase space by the following formulas

(3)

\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho}:=\left[{\nabla,\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}:=\left[{\frac{x}{i\hbar},\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right].

These formulas can also be understood as the Weyl quantization of the classical phase space gradients since

(4)

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\nabla_{x}f}=\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\nabla_{\xi}f}=\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}.

The uniform-in-
\hbar
boundedness of these quantities in the scaled Schatten norms can thus be seen as the quantum analogue of the boundedness of a phase space function to a Sobolev space. We refer to
[
14
]
for a more detailed presentation of these ideas and their applications.

1.2.
Motivation: Slater determinants and semiclassical mean-field limit

We are interested by one-particle density matrices that are projections operators. These states appear for instance when considering the one-particle reduced density of a Slater determinant. Recall that a Slater determinant
\omega_{N}
can be defined as the
N
-body wave function

(5)

\Psi_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}\det(\psi_{j}(x_{k}))_{(j,k)\in\set{1,\dots,N}^{2}}

or the associated density operator

\omega_{N}=\ket{\Psi_{N}}\bra{\Psi_{N}}

where
(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{N})
is an orthonormal family of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Its one-particle reduced density, or first marginal, is then of the form

\omega=N\tr_{2,\dots,N}(\omega_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\ket{\psi_{j}}\bra{\psi_{j}}

if we choose the normalization
\tr\!\left(\omega\right)=N
. In particular, it remains a projection, i.e. it verifies
\omega^{2}=\omega
. Reciprocally, to any self-adjoint one-particle density operator
\omega
verifying

(6)

\tr\!\left(\omega\right)=N\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega^{2}=\omega

one can associate a Slater determinant (
5
) by the spectral theorem. To be compatible with the Weyl quantization and the Wigner transform and see what is happening when units are chosen so that
\hbar
becomes negligible, or equivalently in the classical limit
\hbar\to 0
, we define
\boldsymbol{\rho}=(Nh^{d})^{-1}\omega
so that
h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=1
, and we assume that

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\mathcal{C}_{2}<\infty

converges to a constant when
\hbar\to 0
. By the properties of the Wigner transform it holds

\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{N^{2}h^{d}}\tr\!\left(\omega^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{N^{2}h^{d}}\tr\!\left(\omega\right)=\frac{1}{Nh^{d}}.

Therefore,
N
and
h
are linked through the relation
Nh^{d}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{-2}
. This is in contrast with the case of fermionic mixed states where more generally, it is possible to have
N\,h^{d}\leq\mathcal{C}_{2}^{-2}
(see
[
6
]
). Observe additionally that it follows from Equation (
6
) that the operator norm of
\omega
is given by
\left\|{\omega}\right\|_{\infty}=1
. Hence, by definition, the operator norm of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is given by
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{N\,h^{d}}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}
, and is also independent of
\hbar
. To summarize
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}=(Nh^{d})^{-1}
is independent of
\hbar
and such that
\boldsymbol{\rho}=\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\,\omega
. To simplify, we will just consider in the rest of the paper that
\mathcal{C}_{2}=1
, so that the theorems would correspond to the case
h=N^{-1/d}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}=\omega
.

One of the motivation of this investigation arises from the problem of the mean-field and semiclassical limit from the
N
-body Schrödinger equation to the Hartree–Fock and Vlasov equations when the interaction potential is singular, as studied in
[
23
,
6
]
. In the second of these works, assumptions of
semiclassical regularity
are made on the initial data in the sense of the quantum Sobolev spaces defined in Section
2.1
, and we prove here that these assumptions are not compatible with projection operators, and so Slater determinants, as was conjectured in
[
6
, Remark 4.3]
.

Precise estimates on the commutator of operators with the operators
x
and
\boldsymbol{p}
are also useful to understand the size of the self-distance of some pseudometrics in quantum optimal transport, which is related to the Wigner–Yanase Skew information (See
[
9
,
15
]
).

2.
Main results

Our results consider three main cases. The first result, Theorem
1
, is a result about the lack of high regularity for projection operators and is valid for all projection operators. Then, Theorem
2
indicates that there are states verifying the maximal regularity allowed by the first theorem. Finally, Theorem
3
claims that there are however states for which the regularity is strictly lower than the maximal regularity. It also shows as an application that the regularity obtained for projection operators can lead to improvements in the statement of the Weyl law.

2.1.
Sobolev spaces

Denote by
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
the phase space variable. Then, in the classical setting, the homogeneous Sobolev space of order
1
of functions on the phase space can be defined as the set of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}
vanishing at infinity and for which the following norm is finite

\left\|{f}\right\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\left\|{\nabla_{z}f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

As usual, one can also define the corresponding non-homogeneous space by defining the norm
\left\|{f}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}+\left\|{\nabla_{z}f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
. Analogously, the quantum Sobolev norms of order
1
are defined by the formula

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{1,p}}:=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

where
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\rho}
is the vector valued operator
(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho},\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho})
whose absolute value verifies
\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right|^{2}=\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right|^{2}
. When
s\in(0,1)
, the quantum analogue of the fractional Gagliardo–Sobolev norms are defined by
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,p}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{s,p}}
in
[
14
]
where

(7)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{s,p}}^{p}:=\gamma_{s,p}\,h^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{\tr\!\left(\left|{\mathsf{T}_{z}\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right|^{p}\right)}{\left|{z}\right|^{2d+sp}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z,

for some constant
\gamma_{s,p}>0
. Here,
\mathsf{T}_{z}
is the quantum phase space translation operator defined by

(8)

\mathsf{T}_{z_{0}}\boldsymbol{\rho}=e^{i\left(\xi_{0}\cdot x-x_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}\right)/\hbar}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}\,e^{i\left(x_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{p}-\xi_{0}\cdot x\right)/\hbar}.

More generally when
s\in[0,2)
, the quantum Besov norms
[

are defined by
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}

(9)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}:=\Big\lVert\frac{\left\|{\mathsf{T}_{2z}\boldsymbol{\rho}-2\,\mathsf{T}_{z}\boldsymbol{\rho}+\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}}{\left|{z}\right|^{s+2d/q}}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

2.2.
Maximal regularity of projections

Our main result can be summarized by telling that if
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is a compact projection operator in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, then
\boldsymbol{\rho}\notin\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}
whenever
s>1/p

Theorem 1
.

Let

—

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}\

\displaystyle\to\infty

\displaystyle\text{ if }s>1/p\text{, or }s=1/p\text{ and }q<\infty

(13)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{s,p}}

\displaystyle\to\infty

\displaystyle\text{ if }s\geq 1/p\text{ and }p>1.

—

If the operators are not self-adjoint, then the result still holds if

For any

for some

Remark 2.1
.

It might seem surprising that one can find projection operators in the space
\mathcal{W}^{1,1}
while a classical characteristic function
\chi(z)=\mathds{1}_{A}(z)
of a nonempty set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is never in
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. But such a function
\chi
can be in
BV(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the set of distributions with bounded variation, that is the distributions whose gradient is a measure. In semiclassical analysis, the set
\mathcal{L}^{1}
should indeed rather be interpreted as the quantum analogue of the space of measures. As an example, the Weyl quantization of the function
g_{h}(z)=\left(2/h\right)^{d}e^{-\left|{z}\right|^{2}/\hbar}
verifies for any
h>0
,
\big\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}_{g_{h}}\big\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=1
, but its Wigner transform converges to
\delta_{0}
. Similarly, the set
\mathcal{W}^{1,1}
should be rather interpreted as the quantum analogue of
BV(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}=\mathds{1}_{\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}

U_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the Husimi transform of
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}
converges weakly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to
\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}
for all
p\in(0,1)
when
\hbar\to 0
. Moreover, when
U
satisfies Assumption (
16
) with
\Omega
bounded, then for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in[0,1/p)
,

(21)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}}

\displaystyle\underset{\hbar\to 0}{\to}\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}\quad\text{ in }W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

(22)

\displaystyle f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}}

\displaystyle\underset{\hbar\to 0}{\to}\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}\quad\text{ in }W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\text{ with }s\in\left(\tfrac{1}{p^{\prime}},\tfrac{1}{2}+2d\left(\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right)

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. There is also convergence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.

However, there exists functions
U
with
\sqrt{U}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
s>\alpha/p
,
\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}\notin B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and so such that

(23)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}}\underset{\hbar\to 0}{\to}\infty.

3.
The explicit example of the Harmonic oscillator.

An example where one can do explicit computations is the case of non-interacting fermions in a harmonic trap, see also for example
[
4
]
. Consider the Hamiltonian given by the harmonic oscillator

\mathsf{H}_{N}=\dG(H)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}H_{n}\quad\text{ where }\quad H=\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}+\left|{x}\right|^{2}

and where each
H_{n}
is the one particle Hamiltonian
H
acting on the
n
-th particle. It is well-known that the eigenvalues of
H
are given of the form
\left(2\,n+1\right)\hbar
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with eigenvectors
\psi_{n}
given by Hermite functions. The ground state of
\mathsf{H}_{N}
is given by a Slater determinant formed by the
N
first eigenvectors of the one-body Hamiltonian. Assuming for simplicity that the number of particles is such that
N=\binom{d+n}{d}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then its one-particle density operator can be written

\boldsymbol{\rho}=\sum_{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}\leq n}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is a multi-index, with
\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d}
and
\psi_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes\psi_{\alpha_{d}}
. It verifies
\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)=N=h^{-d}
and
0\leq\boldsymbol{\rho}\leq 1
. Following similar computations as the one done for example in
[
4
]
, it holds

(24)

\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{p}=\frac{1}{(2\hbar)^{p/2}}\sum_{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}=n}\left(\alpha_{1}+1\right)^{p/2}\big(\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}+\ket{\psi_{\alpha+e_{1}}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha+e_{1}}}\,\big).

From this we deduce that for any
p\in[1,\infty]
, there exists
C_{p}
independent of
\hbar
such that

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=\frac{C_{p}}{h^{1/p^{\prime}}}

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. The constant
C_{p}
can be explicitly computed and verifies
C_{p}\leq(2d)^{1/p}\sqrt{\pi}(d!)^{\frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}
when
p\leq 2
and
C_{p}\leq 2^{1/p}\sqrt{\pi}(d!)^{\frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}
when
p\geq 2
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2d\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{d!}},

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar}},

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{(d!)^{1/d}\,\pi}}{h}.

The same estimates hold by replacing
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}
by
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}
. In particular,
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{W}^{1,1}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
and so in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
2
,
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{B}^{1/p}_{p,\infty}
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Proof.

Following similar computations as in
[
4
]
, one can compute
\left[{x_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]
using the fact that
x_{1}=\frac{a+a^{*}}{2}
where
a^{*}=x-i\boldsymbol{p}
and
a=x+i\boldsymbol{p}
are the creation and annihilation operators. This yields

\left[{x_{1},\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]=\frac{\sqrt{\hbar}}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\left|{\tilde{\alpha}}\right|_{1}\leq n}\sqrt{a+1}\,\big(\ket{\psi_{a+1}}\bra{\psi_{a}}-\ket{\psi_{a}}\bra{\psi_{a+1}}\big)\otimes\ket{\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}}\bra{\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}}

where
\tilde{\alpha}=(0,\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{d})
and
a=n-\left|{\tilde{\alpha}}\right|_{1}
. By taking
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{d})
with
\alpha_{1}=a
, this can be equivalently written

\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}\sum_{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}=n}\sqrt{\alpha_{1}+1}\,\big(\ket{\psi_{\alpha_{1}}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha_{1}+1}}-\ket{\psi_{\alpha_{1}+1}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha_{1}}}\big)\otimes\ket{\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}}\bra{\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}}.

Notice that
\psi_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\psi_{\alpha}
and
\psi_{\alpha_{1}+1}\otimes\psi_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\psi_{\alpha+e_{1}}
with
e_{1}=(1,0,\dots,0)
. Hence, taking the square of the above operator and using the fact that the family of
\psi_{n}
is orthonormal leads to

\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{2\hbar}\sum_{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}=n}\left(\alpha_{1}+1\right)\big(\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}+\ket{\psi_{\alpha+e_{1}}}\bra{\psi_{\alpha+e_{1}}}\,\big).

As this is a diagonal operator, we deduce that the more general Formula (
24
) holds. Taking the trace then yields

h^{d}\tr\!\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\xi_{1}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{p}\right)=\frac{2\,h^{d}}{(2\hbar)^{p/2}}\sum_{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}=n}\left(\alpha_{1}+1\right)^{p/2}=\frac{2\,h^{d}}{(2\hbar)^{p/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\binom{d+n-k-1}{d-2}k^{p/2}

and we deduce the result using the fact that
\binom{d+n}{d}=N=h^{-d}
and
(n+1)^{d}\leq N\,d!\leq(n+d)^{d}
.
∎

4.
The case of Schrödinger operators

Knowing Equation (
17
), the core of the proof of Theorem
2
is nothing more than the use of an interpolation inequality for quantum Besov spaces.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

It follows from the Cwikel–Lieb–Rozenblum inequality
[
8
]
that

(25)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq C\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where
U_{+}=\max(U,0)
, and so in particular the above assumptions (
16
) imply that
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
. Combined with Equation (
17
), we deduce that
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathcal{W}^{1,1}
. Since
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
, it follows that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Now notice that if
\theta\in[0,1]
,
(s_{0},s_{1})\in[0,1]^{2}
and
(p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1})\in[1,\infty]^{4}
, then it follows from the definition (
9
) of Besov norms and Hölder’s inequality for the Lebesgue and Schatten norms that for any
s_{\theta}
,
p_{\theta}
and
q_{\theta}
such that

s_{\theta}=\left(1-\theta\right)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},\qquad\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\qquad\frac{1}{q_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}

the following interpolation inequality holds

(26)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s_{\theta}}_{p_{\theta},q_{\theta}}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}^{\theta}.

We know from
[
14
, Equation (31)]
that for any
p\in[1,\infty]
,

(27)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{p,\infty}}\leq 2\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{1,p}}.

On the other hand, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
, the triangle inequality and the fact that the translation operators
\mathsf{T}_{z}
are unitary, and so preserve the Schatten norms, yields

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}=\Big\lVert\left\|{\mathsf{T}_{2z}\boldsymbol{\rho}-2\,\mathsf{T}_{z}\boldsymbol{\rho}+\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\Big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq 4\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}.

Therefore, we deduce that

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{1/p}_{p,\infty}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{0}_{\infty,\infty}}^{1-\theta}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{1,\infty}}^{\theta}\leq 2^{2-\theta}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}^{1-\theta}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{W}}^{1,1}}^{\theta}.

Together with the fact that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
, it implies that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1/p}_{p,\infty}}
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
, which finishes the proof of Equation (
18
). The interpolation Inequality (
26
) implies now that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1/p}_{p,\infty}}
. But by the fact that the Wigner transform is an isometry from
\mathcal{L}^{2}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and the integral characterization of
H^{s}
, it is not difficult to see (see also
[
14
]
) that
\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{H^{s}}=C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,2}}
, from which Equation (
19
) follows.
∎

5.
Projection operators converge to characteristic functions

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
. We first introduce some tools that will be useful to our analysis. As in
[
30
,
14
]
, we define the semiclassical convolution as the weak integral

(28)

f\star\boldsymbol{\rho}=\boldsymbol{\rho}\star f:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(z)\,\mathsf{T}_{z}\boldsymbol{\rho}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

We will be interested by the convolution by the Gaussian function defined for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
by

g_{h}(z)=\left(2/h\right)^{d}e^{-\left|{z}\right|^{2}/\hbar},

and we will use the notations

\tilde{f}:=g_{h}*f,\quad\text{ and }\quad\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}:=g_{h}\star\boldsymbol{\rho}.

In particular, the Husimi transform of an operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is nothing but

\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=g_{h}*f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=f_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}

while the Wick quantization of a measure
f
(also sometimes called superposition of coherent sates, Töplitz operator or anti-Wick quantization) can be written

\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}=g_{h}\star\boldsymbol{\rho}_{f}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\tilde{f}}=f\star\boldsymbol{\rho}_{g_{h}}.

Young’s inequality also holds for the semiclassical convolution (see e.g.
[
30
,
14
]
) and as a particular case we obtain the well known bounds for the Wick quantization and the Husimi transform

(29)

\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

More generally, it is not difficult to deduce that the same inequality holds for quantum Sobolev and Besov spaces (see e.g.
[
14
, inequalities (41) and (47)]
), that is

(30)

\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}\leq\left\|{f}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}.

The main step in the proof of our main theorem is the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1]
,
p\in[2,\infty]
,
q\in[1,\infty]
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}
be (a sequence of) operators verifying
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
,
h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)=1
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}
uniformly in
\hbar
. Then there exists a sequence
(\hbar_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
0
and a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{2d})^{\mathbb{N}}
such that the sequence of translated Wigner transforms
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\cdot+z_{n})
for
\hbar=\hbar_{n}
converges to
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}
different from
0
and verifying

\mu^{2}=\mu\in B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}).

Remark 5.1
.

The result is still true if there is no shifting sequence, i.e. if
z_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, except the fact that in this case it may happen that
\mu=0
.

Proof.

We take a discrete sequence of values for
\hbar
,
(\hbar_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that
\hbar_{n}\to 0
when
n\to\infty
. We will then also take subsequences if necessary, but to simplify we will not write the
n
dependency and just write
\hbar\to 0
. By assumption, there exists a constant
\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}
independent of
\hbar
such that

(31)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}\leq\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}.

By the quantum Sobolev inequalities
[
14
]
, this implies that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p_{s}}}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}
for some constant
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}
independent of
\hbar
and with
\frac{1}{p_{s}}:=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{2d}
if
p<\frac{2d}{s}
and
p_{s}=\infty
if
p>\frac{2d}{s}
(and any
p_{s}\geq p
, that will be taken sufficiently large, if
p=\frac{2d}{s}
). By the fact that
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
and by Hölder’s inequality for Schatten norms, for any
r\geq 2
,

1=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}^{r^{\prime}/2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}^{1-r^{\prime}/2},

where
r^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
r
. Since
p_{s}\geq p\geq 2
, it implies in particular that

(32)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p_{s}}}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}.

Combining the two above equations implies that for any
r\geq 2
,

(33)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\geq(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s})^{1-2/r^{\prime}}.

For the Husimi transform, by Formula (
29
), we deduce from Inequality (
31
) and Inequality (
32
) the following bounds

(34)

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}\qquad\text{ and }\quad\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{p_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}.

Moreover, since
p\in[1,p_{s}]
, by equations (
29
) and (
30
) and Hölder’s inequality, it holds

(35)

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}\leq\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}+1\right)\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s}=:\mathcal{D}_{s}.

These bound will allow us to extract weakly convergent subsequences. However, nothing prevents these sequences to converge weakly to
0
. Hence we will first use the ideas of the concentration-compactness principle
[
20
,
19
]
to prevent this. We mainly need to prevent the mass to escape at infinity, hence we follow the ideas of
[
19
, Lemma I.1]
(see also
[
17
]
) and look at all the sequences
T_{z}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}:=\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\cdot-z)
and select the one with approximately the more mass in a given set. More precisely, by a diagonal argument, we can choose a sequence
z_{\hbar}
such that

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\int_{Q}\left|{T_{z_{\hbar}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|=\limsup_{\hbar\to 0}\left(\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{Q}\left|{T_{z}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|\right)=:M_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\rho})

where
Q
is the unit cube
Q=\set{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},\left|{z}\right|_{\infty}\leq 1/2}
. This new sequence of functions
T_{z_{\hbar}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
verifies the same bounds (
34
) and (
35
) as
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
.
Hence
T_{z_{\hbar}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in B^{s}_{p,q}\cap L^{1}\cap L^{p_{s}}
uniformly in
\hbar
and so, up to a subsequence, converges weakly in
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and strongly in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to a function
\mu\in B^{s}_{p,q}\cap L^{1}\cap L^{p_{s}}
verifying

\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\mathcal{D}_{s}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q)}=M_{Q}(\boldsymbol{\rho}).

Let
\sigma\in(0,s)
and
\frac{1}{p_{\sigma}}:=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\sigma}{2d}
. By the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities, for any
r\in(1,p_{\sigma})
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{p_{\sigma}}+1-\theta
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\hbar
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{r}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q}\left|{T_{k}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{r}\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{Q}\left|{T_{k}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|\right)^{\left(1-\theta\right)r}\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(Q)}^{\theta\,r}.

In particular, one can choose
r
verifying
\theta\,r=p
by taking
r=p\left(1+\frac{\sigma}{2d}\right)
. Hence, it yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{r}\leq C\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{Q}\left|{T_{k}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|\right)^{\left(1-\theta\right)r}\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{p}

and so since
B^{s}_{p,q}\subset W^{\sigma,p}
, it follows that

\left\|{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\leq\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq C\left(\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q}\left|{T_{z}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}\mathcal{D}_{s}^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}

where we used Inequality (
29
) with
\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}=\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}
to get the first inequality. On the other hand, we know from
[
14
, Inequality (42)]
that
\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\,\hbar^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}}
. Since
r\geq p
, taking into account the
h
appearing in the definition of the
\mathcal{L}^{p}
norms, the inclusions between Schatten norms yields
\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\leq C\,\hbar^{s-2d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}}
, and so

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}-C\,\hbar^{s-\frac{2d\sigma}{\left(2d+s\right)p}}\,\mathcal{D}_{s}\leq C\left(\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q}\left|{T_{z}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|\right)^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}\mathcal{D}_{s}^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}.

Noticing that the exponent of
\hbar
appearing in the above equation is positive and that since
r\geq p\geq 2
we can use Inequality (
33
), we can take
\hbar\to 0
to get

(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{S}}_{s,p}\dot{\mathcal{D}}_{s})^{1-2/r^{\prime}}\leq C\,\mathcal{D}_{s}^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q)}^{\frac{\sigma}{2d+\sigma}}.

In particular,
\mu\neq 0
.

Now it remains to prove that
\mu^{2}
is also limit of the sequence
T_{z_{\hbar}}\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
. For that, we will use the following inequality proved in
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
. For any
p\geq 2
, it holds

\left\|{\frac{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{g}+\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{g}}{2}-\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{fg}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p/2}}\leq 2^{d+1}\,d\,\hbar\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},

The above inequality, together with the fact that by Hölder’s inequality

\left\|{\frac{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{g}+\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{g}}{2}-\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{fg}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p/2}}\leq\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{f}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{g}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\|{fg}\right\|_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq 2\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

leads by bilinear real interpolation (see e.g.
[
27
, Lemma 28.3]
) and taking
f=g=\mu
to

\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}-\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu^{2}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p/2}}\leq C\,\hbar^{\sigma}\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,\tilde{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}\leq C\,\hbar^{\sigma}\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2},

where
\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}=(\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu})^{2}
,
\tilde{q}\geq 2
and we used the fact that
(L^{p},W^{1,p})_{s,\tilde{q}}=B^{s}_{p,\tilde{q}}
(see e.g.
[
28
, Section 2.4.2]
) and the continuous embedding
B^{s}_{p,q}\subset B^{\sigma}_{p,\tilde{q}}
for any
\sigma<s
. That is, squaring an operator is an operation close to squaring a function. By the properties of the Husimi transform, we deduce that

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu^{2}}}}\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq C\,\hbar^{\sigma}\,\mathcal{D}_{s}^{2}.

Noticing that
\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu^{2}}}=\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\mu^{2}}}=g_{2h}*f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\mu^{2}}}=g_{2h}*\mu^{2}
, we deduce that
g_{h}*f_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}-g_{h}*\mu^{2}
converges to
0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. On another side, since
g_{h}
is an approximation of the identity,
g_{h}*\mu^{2}-\mu^{2}
also converges to
0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Therefore, strongly in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the following convergence holds true

(36)

\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}\underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\mu^{2}.

Now we want to prove that
\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}
is close to
\mu
. To this end we use the fact that
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}
where
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}=\mathsf{T}_{z_{h}}\boldsymbol{\rho}
, and write

(37)

\mu-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}=\left(\mu-\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right)+\left(\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}\right)+\left(\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}\right).

We already know that the first term on the right-hand-side converges to
0
in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. To bound the second term, we use the Husimi transform bound (
29
) to write

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}}\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}.

To control this term, it is useful to remove the squares using the fact that

(38)

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}+\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)+\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}+\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\right).

By the semiclassical Young convolution inequality (see
[
30
,
14
]
) and the fact that
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}
, it holds
\left\|{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq 1
. Therefore, it follows from (
38
) and Hölder’s inequality that

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\leq\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\|{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq 2\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

Since by
[
14
]
, we know that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\,\hbar^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}}
, it yields

(39)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{2}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}\leq C\,\hbar^{s}\,\mathcal{D}_{s}.

It remains to treat the last term on the right-hand-side of Identity (
37
). The idea is similar to the second term, but since there is only convergence of
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
to
\mu
weakly or locally, we pass to the weak topology. Take
\varphi\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
a test function. Then using again Identity (
38
) with
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}
replaced by
\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}
yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}\right)\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(f_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}-f_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}\right)\tilde{\varphi}=\tfrac{h^{d}}{2}\tr\!\left(\left(\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\boldsymbol{\nu}+\boldsymbol{\nu}^{*}\left(\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}-\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\right)\\
=\re\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\tilde{\mu}-\tilde{\tilde{f}}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right)f_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)=\re\!\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\mu-\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right)\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}\right)

where
\boldsymbol{\nu}=\left(\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}+\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\varphi}
, and
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
uniformly in
\hbar
since by Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\left(\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}+\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}\right)\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\varphi}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p^{\prime}}}\leq\left(\left\|{\mu}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\right)\left\|{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\varphi}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r^{\prime}}}\leq 2\left\|{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

Taking an approximation of
f_{\boldsymbol{\nu}}
by compactly supported functions and using the fact that
\mu-\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}}
converges strongly to
0
in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
leads to the fact that
\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}}_{h}^{2}}-\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}
converges weakly to
0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Recalling Inequality (
39
) and coming back to Equation (
37
), we proved that

\tilde{f}_{\,\tilde{\!\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\mu}^{2}}\underset{h\to 0}{\rightharpoonup}\mu^{2}

weakly in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Together with Equation (
36
), this proves that
\mu=\mu^{2}
.
∎

Assume

Case 2.

Now assume that

\frac{1}{2}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}},\qquad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{q}.

Hence it follows from Inequality (

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{1/2}_{2,r}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}^{\theta}\left(4\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p_{0}}}\right)^{1-\theta}.

If

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}^{1/p}

\hbar

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{s}_{p,q}}

In the case when

In the particular case

, then the fact that

Proof of the Weyl law in Sobolev spaces

Proof of Theorem
3
.

The beginning of the proof is classical (see e.g.
[
11
]
). Notice first that

(40)

h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\right)=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left(\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}-U(x)\right)\right)+h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\,U(x)\right)\leq M_{0}\,\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

where
M_{h}=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)
, from which it follows that
h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\right)
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
. Since
0\leq\boldsymbol{\rho}\leq 1
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is bounded in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
uniformly in
\hbar
by Inequality (
25
), up to a subsequence, the Husimi transform of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
converges weakly (for example in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for
p\in(1,\infty)
) to some function
f
verifying
0\leq f\leq 1
. The Wigner transform of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
then converges weakly to the same limit (see
[
21
]
). On the other hand, the classical asymptotic formula for the eigenvalue counting function of the Schrödinger operator
[
22
,
26
,
25
,
8
]
yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)\underset{\hbar\to 0}{\to}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=M_{0}

while from the Weyl law for the energy of the Schrödinger operator (see e.g.
[
18
,
13
]
), it holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left(U(x)-\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\right)=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left(U(x)-\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\right)\right)=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\left(U(x)-\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\right)_{+}\right)\\
\underset{\hbar\to 0}{\to}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(U(x)-\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\right)_{+}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=\max_{g\in G}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(U(x)-\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\right)g(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
G=\set{g:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{R}}{0\leq g\leq 1\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g=M_{0}}
. Hence, by the bathtub principle, we deduce that
f=\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}
.

If
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is bounded in
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}
uniformly in
\hbar
, then by Equation (
30
), its Husimi transform is bounded in
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
uniformly in
\hbar
and so
f
will be in
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
as well. If
\sqrt{U}\in C^{\alpha}
with with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}\in B^{\alpha/p}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. However, there exist examples of functions
U\in C^{\alpha}
such that
\mathds{1}_{\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\leq U(x)}\notin B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for all
s>\alpha/p
(see e.g.
[
24
, Section 4.4]
or
[
29
]
). For these functions, we therefore deduce that
\boldsymbol{\rho}
can never be bounded in
\mathcal{B}^{s}_{p,q}
uniformly in
\hbar
, proving Equation (
23
).

Step 2: convergence of the Husimi transform.

In the particular case when
U
verifies Assumption (
16
) with
\Omega
bounded, then by Theorem
2
and Equation (
30
), we deduce that the Husimi transform of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
in
B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and so converges weakly in
B^{1/p}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to
f
.

To get strong convergence in
L^{p}
, it remains to use that the moments of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
are bounded. Indeed, the Husimi transform of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
verifies
0\leq\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\leq 1
and

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{\xi}\right|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}g_{h}*f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi=h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\right)+d\,\hbar,

which is bounded uniformly in
\hbar\in(0,1]
by Inequality (
40
). On the other hand, it follows from Agmon estimates
[
1
,
2
]
that there exist positive constants
c
and
C
independent of
\hbar
and
N=h^{-d}
such that for all eigenvalues
(\psi_{j})_{j\in\set{1,\dots,N}}
of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
,

\left\|{e^{c\left|{x}\right|}\,\psi_{j}(x)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{\varepsilon},

where
C_{\varepsilon}
depends on the
\varepsilon
appearing in Assumption (
16
). Since

\boldsymbol{\rho}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\ket{\psi_{j}}\bra{\psi_{j}},

where by Inequality (
25
),
Nh^{d}\leq C\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, this implies that

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{x}\right|}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}-d\hbar\leq h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{x}\right|^{2}\right)=h^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|{\left|{x}\right|\psi_{j}(x)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

From these
\left|{\xi}\right|
and
\left|{x}\right|
moments bounds and the uniform boundedness of
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for
s<1/2
, it follows that
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
converges strongly to
f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
by the classical Riesz–Fréchet–Kolmogorov criterion of compactness in
L^{p}
(see e.g.
[
5
, Theorem IV.25]
). By interpolation and the uniform bound of
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, this implies the strong convergence in

for any

. More precisely, for

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-f}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2/p}\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-f}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{1-2/p}\leq\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2/p}

since

, and similarly for

by replacing

by

following the same scheme of proof as for

\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{W^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
,
\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{x}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
and
\left\|{\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{\xi}\right|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
uniformly in

Convergence in

and

).

Step 3: convergence of the Wigner transform.

For the Wigner transform
f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
, convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
follows from the fact that for any

,

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}-\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq C\,h^{s}\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})},

which converges to
0
when

by Equation (

p\geq 2
, one can use the classical Sobolev embeddings
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for

to get that the Wigner transforms are bounded uniformly in
\hbar
in
W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for

, and the strong convergence in
W^{t,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for

follows again by an interpolation argument, proving Equation (
22
).

Finally notice that the boundedness of moments in
x
and
\boldsymbol{p}
of the operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
allows to consider lower values of
p
. Indeed, with the notation
X=x+y/2
and
Y=x-y/2
, so that
x=X+Y
, the definition of the Wigner transform together with the multinomial formula yield for any
n\in 2\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{x}\right|^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\,y\cdot\xi/\hbar}\left|{X+Y}\right|^{n}\boldsymbol{\rho}(X,Y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\,y\cdot\xi/\hbar}\left(\left|{X}\right|^{2}+\left|{Y}\right|^{2}+2\,X\cdot Y\right)^{n/2}\boldsymbol{\rho}(X,Y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}=n}C_{\alpha,\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\,y\cdot\xi/\hbar}X^{\alpha}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}(X,Y)\,Y^{\beta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}=n}C_{\alpha,\beta}\,f_{x^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\rho}\,x^{\beta}}

where the sum is taken over all the multiindices
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that

\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}=n

and where
x^{\alpha}
means
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. The constants
C_{\alpha,\beta}
verify that their sum is
\left(2\sqrt{d}\right)^{n}
, as can be seen by taking
X=Y=(1,\dots,1)
. Hence, taking the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
norm and using the triangle inequality and the fact that the Wigner trnasform is an isometry from
\mathcal{L}^{2}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
yields

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{x}\right|^{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\sum_{\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}=n}C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\left\|{x^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\rho}\,x^{\beta}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\left(2\sqrt{d}\right)^{n}\sup_{\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}=n}\left\|{x^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\rho}\,x^{\beta}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

By the boundedness of the operator of multiplication by
\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\left|{x}\right|^{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}}}
,

\left\|{x^{\alpha}\boldsymbol{\rho}\,x^{\beta}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\left\|{\left|{x}\right|^{\left|{\alpha}\right|_{1}}\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{x}\right|^{\left|{\beta}\right|_{1}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{x}\right|^{\left|{\alpha+\beta}\right|_{1}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

where the last inequality follows from
[
16
, Inequality (56)]
(which follows mainly from Hölder’s inequality for Schatten norms and the Araki–Lieb–Thirring inequality
[
3
]
). Hence

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{x}\right|^{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}\leq\left(4d\right)^{n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{x}\right|^{n}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}=\left(4d\right)^{n}h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{x}\right|^{2n}\right)

where we used the that fact that
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}
. This last expression is bounded uniformly with respect to
\hbar
by Agmon estimates. A similar proof replacing
x
by
\xi
yields

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{\xi}\right|^{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}\leq\left(4d\right)^{n}h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2n}\right)=\left(4d\right)^{n}h^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Braket{\psi_{j}|\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2n}\psi_{j}}

where the
\psi_{j}
verify
\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2}\psi_{j}=\left(U(x)+\lambda_{j}\right)\psi_{j}
for some
\lambda_{j}\leq 0
. In particular, if
n
is odd, then
\left(U(x)-\mu_{j}\right)^{n}\leq U_{+}^{n}(x)
, so that

\Braket{\psi_{j}|\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2n}\psi_{j}}\leq\Braket{\psi_{j}|U_{+}^{n}\psi_{j}}\leq\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{n}

which for the Wigner transform yields

\displaystyle\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left|{\xi}\right|^{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(4d\right)^{n}h^{d}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\left|{\boldsymbol{p}}\right|^{2n}\right)\leq\left(4d\right)^{n}h^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}\left(4d\right)^{n}\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\|{U_{+}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{n}.

From these weighted
L^{2}
estimates,
L^{p}
estimates for
p<2
follow by Hölder’s inequality since whenever
n>2d

(41)

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq C_{d,n}\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left(1+\left|{x}\right|^{n}+\left|{\xi}\right|^{n}\right)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

with
C_{d,n}
independent of
\hbar
. The boundedness in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for
s<1/2
together with the convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
imply by interpolation the convergence in
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for any
s<1/p^{\prime}
with
p\in(1,2)
.

), one has more generally whenever

\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left(1+\left|{x}\right|^{k}+\left|{\xi}\right|^{k}\right)}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq C_{d,n}\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\left(1+\left|{x}\right|^{n}+\left|{\xi}\right|^{n}\right)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

Together with the convergence in

of the Wigner transform, which implies the convergence in

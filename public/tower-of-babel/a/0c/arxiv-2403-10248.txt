Mutual Information Bounded by Fisher Information
Wojciech Górecki, Xi Lu, Chiara Macchiavello, Lorenzo Maccone

Abstract
We derive a general upper bound to mutual information in terms of the Fisher information. The bound may be further used to derive a lower bound for the Bayesian quadratic cost. These two provide alternatives to other inequalities in the literature (e.g. the van Trees inequality) that are useful also for cases where the latter ones give trivial bounds. We then generalize them to the quantum case, where they bound the Holevo information in terms of the quantum Fisher information. We illustrate the usefulness of our bounds with a case study in quantum phase estimation. Here, they allow us to adapt to mutual information (useful for global strategies where the prior plays an important role) the known and highly nontrivial bounds for the Fisher information in the presence of noise. The results are also useful in the context of quantum communication, both for continuous and discrete alphabets.

Bounding mutual information by Fisher information.—

The estimation of a parameter
\phi
from measurements is described
by two probabilities: the prior distribution of the parameter

p(\phi)
and the conditional probability
p(x|\phi)
of the measurement
results
x
. Together they constitute the joint probability

p(x,\phi)=p(x|\phi)p(\phi)
. The MI between random variables
X
and
\Phi
is

I(X,\Phi):=H(\Phi)-H(\Phi|X)
, where

H(\Phi)=-\int d\phi\>p(\phi){\ln}p(\phi)
is the entropy of

p(\phi)
and
H(\Phi|X)
is the conditional entropy, given

x

1
1

1

Note that, as we are dealing with continuous variables, the entropies may be negative.
. MI tells us the amount of information (in nats) about
\phi

obtained from
x
. The FI in given point
\phi
is

F(\phi)=\sum_{x}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}\left(\frac{dp(x|\phi)}{d\phi}\right)^{2}
and tells
us how much information on
\phi
is contained in
p(x|\phi)
. The
following two theorems relate MI to FI.

Theorem 1
.

If the parameter is guaranteed to lie inside of a finite-size set, namely
\textrm{supp}\,p(\phi)\subseteq[a,b]
, then

I(X,\Phi)\leq{\ln}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}d\phi\sqrt{F(\phi)}\right).

(1)

Note especially, that there are no further constraints on
p(\phi)
– it is an arbitrary probability density, continuous or discrete (or a combination of these two), as long as it takes value
0
outside of
[a,b]
. This inequality holds for any
p(x|\phi)
, so in the context of
quantum metrology, by replacing the FI with the quantum Fisher
information, one obtains a bound valid for any possible
measurement. Moreover the integral is invariant for reparametrization
of the probabilities
\phi\to\phi^{\prime}
(with appropriately modified limits of integration), as
F(\phi^{\prime})=F(\phi)(\frac{d\phi}{d\phi^{\prime}})^{2}
, so
\sqrt{F(\phi^{\prime})}d\phi^{\prime}=\sqrt{F(\phi)}d\phi
. In a broader context, this feature is crucial in defining non-informative prior distributions, the Jeffreys’ prior
Clarke and Barron 1994
. Yet another way to understand reparametrization-invariance of
Eq. (
1
)
is to notice, that the FI is known to define a
metric corresponding to an Euclidean distance between

\sqrt{p(x|\phi)}
and

\sqrt{p(x|\phi+d\phi)}

Chen et al. 2022
, so the above
integral is just the size of the region
[a,b]
in this metric.

If the support of
p(\phi)
is not bounded, the following holds:

Theorem 2
.

For arbitrary differentiable probability distribution
p(\phi)
, we have

I(X,\Phi)\leq{\ln}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\sqrt{F(\phi)p(\phi)^{2}+\dot{p}(\phi)^{2}}\right)+H(\Phi),

(2)

where
\dot{p}=dp/d\phi
and the
\ln
part is obviously an
upper bound to
-H(\Phi|x)
. In contrast to the previous bound, this is not reparametrization invariant (indeed, it is well known
that the differential entropy
H(\Phi)
is defined modulo an arbitrary
additive constant). So, the upper bound, and also its tightness, will
depend on the parametrization.

Proof of the two bounds.
The mutual information may be written as:

I(X,\Phi)=\sum_{x}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\>p(x,\phi)\ln\frac{p(x,\phi)}{p(x)p(\phi)}=\\
\sum_{x}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\>p(x,\phi)\ln\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{p(x)}-\sum_{x}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\>p(x,\phi)\ln f(\phi),

(3)

where
f(\phi)
is an arbitrary non-negative function satisfying
\textrm{supp}\,f(\phi)\supseteq\textrm{supp}\,p(\phi)
. The first element of the above may be bounded by:

\leq\sum_{x}p(x)\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{p(x)}\\
\leq\max_{q_{x}:\sum_{x}q_{x}=1}\left(\sum_{x}q_{x}\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{q_{x}}\right)\\
\leq\ln\sum_{x}\max_{\phi}p(x|\phi)f(\phi),

(4)

where the last inequality comes from direct maximization over
q_{x}
, which is obtained for
q_{x}=\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{\sum_{x}\max_{\phi}p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}
(see App.
A
). If
f(\phi)
goes to
0
for
\phi\to\pm\infty
, we have:

\sum_{x}\max_{\phi}p(x|\phi)f(\phi)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\sum_{x}\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|.

(5)

From Cauchy’s inequality for vectors
\sqrt{p(x|\phi)}
and
\sqrt{\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}}
(treated as a functions of single parameter
x
, with fixed
\phi
) we have (see App.
A
):

\sum_{x}\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|\leq\\
\sqrt{F(\phi)f(\phi)^{2}+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}}.

(6)

Combining all above one may get:

I(X,\Phi)\leq\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\sqrt{F(\phi)f(\phi)^{2}+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}}\right)+\\
-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\>p(\phi)\ln f(\phi),

(7)

which may be further optimized over choice
f(\phi)
(going to zero for
\phi\to\pm\infty)
. Note especially, that for
f(\phi)=p(\phi)
, we get
Eq. (
2
)
. Alternatively, for
f(\phi)=1
on
[a,b]
and
0
outside (so
\dot{f}(\phi)=\delta(\phi-a)-\delta(\phi-b)
), we obtain
Eq. (
1
)
, see App.
B
for more details.
\square

From local to global estimation.—

The Fisher information, via the Cramér-Rao inequality, constitutes a lower bound for the variance for any locally unbiased estimator and it is a meaningful tool in the situation when the number of repetitions of the estimation protocol is large. However, if the number of measurement repetitions is finite, properly including prior knowledge about the parameter may lead, when averaged, to a smaller variance (as the optimal estimator may not satisfy the local unbiasedness condition). It may be quantified by the general relation between averaged Bayesian cost
\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}:=\int d\phi\>p(\phi)\int d\tilde{\phi}\>p(\tilde{\phi}|\phi)(\tilde{\phi}-\phi)^{2}
, the entropy
H(\Phi)
of the prior distribution, and MI
Cover and Thomas 2006
;
Hall and Wiseman 2012
:

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{e^{2H(\Phi|X)}}{2\pi e}=\frac{e^{2H(\Phi)}e^{-2I(X,\Phi)}}{2\pi e},

(8)

Note that the above is tight iff all a posteriori distributions
p(\phi|x)
are Gaussian with the same variance. The above inequality in a clear manner separates the impact of the initial knowledge
-H(\Phi)
from the knowledge obtained from the experiment
I(X,\Phi)
. Note, that while
I(X,\Phi)
is reparametrization invariant, both
\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}
and
e^{2H(\Phi)}
behave under reparametrization in a similar way, making all inequality consistent. By applying the bound
Eq. (
1
)
to the above we obtain

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{e^{2H(\Phi)}}{2\pi e}\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}d\phi\>\sqrt{F(\phi)})^{2}}.

(9)

Note that the prior information affects not only
H(\Phi)
, but also the range of the integral in the denominator. The better the parameter is known from the beginning, the more difficult it is to acquire significant new information from measurements. In
particular, for the relevant case of a rectangle prior of width
d

with FI constant over the prior, one still obtains a significant
bound, contrary to the van Trees case (see next section for discussion):

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{2}{\pi e}\frac{1}{(2/d+\sqrt{F})^{2}}.

(10)

As expected, in the limit of many repetitions
N
of the estimation
procedure, the prior knowledge becomes irrelevant, since
F
typically
grows linearly with
N
.
A disadvantage of this bound is that it is not
asymptotically tight, because of the multiplicative factor
2/\pi e
.

Of course, a further bound for the Bayesian cost can be obtained also
for unbounded-support
p(\phi)
by inserting

Eq. (
2
)
into
Eq. (
8
)
:

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{2}{\pi e}\frac{1}{(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\sqrt{F(\phi)p(\phi)^{2}+\dot{p}(\phi)^{2}})^{2}},

(11)

which, for example, for Gaussian priors leads to a bound qualitatively similar to the van Trees one (see App.
C
). Note that for specific case of rectangle prior both bounds
Eq. (
9
)
and
Eq. (
11
)
leads to the same results (see App.
B
).

Our results (
9
) and (
11
) provide bounds to the
quadratic Bayesian cost. But a relation similar to
Eq. (
8
)

may be derived for any moment (see Lemma B.1. in

Chen and Özgür 2024
). Therefore, our bounds to MI imply a bound for the
Bayesian cost with an arbitrary cost function, assuming that it may be
expanded in Taylor series. These show that the bound for the MI is
more informative than any bound to Bayesian cost.

Relation to the Efroimovich
and van Trees inequalities.—

We will now compare our results with the bounds that exist in the literature. MI may be related to FI via Efroimovich relation

(see also

Aras et al. 2019
some generalizations and
(

\begin{split}I(X,\Phi)\leq&\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[\frac{1}{2\pi e}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi F(\phi)p(\phi)+P\right)\right]+H(\Phi),\\
&{\rm with}\quad P=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\frac{1}{p(\phi)}\left(\frac{dp(\phi)}{d\phi}\right)^{2},\end{split}

(12)

While for Gaussian prior the quantity

information (the inverse of the variance), it is completely

diverges. In general,
P
depends more on the sharpness of the prior distribution on its
edges than on its actual width, which is more an artifact of the
derivation of the bound, rather than a well-motivated

Aras et al. 2019
;
Lee 2022
.

Introducing Efroimovich’s

)

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{1}{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\>F(\phi)p(\phi)+P},

(13)

which again suffers sharp

This issue does not appear in our bounds (

At last, let us discuss the asymptotical behavior of the bounds for MI. We start with showing asymptotic saturability of Efroimovich’s inequality (
12
) in the case when newly obtained data dominate initial knowledge.
Assuming that
P
does not diverge, and that the probability

is not degenerate

The bound for Holevo information.—
Now we consider the quantum case, where the conditional probability distribution of the measurement output is given via
the Born rule
p(x|\phi)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\phi}M_{x})
, where
\rho_{\phi}
is density matrix of the quantum state,
M_{x}
is a POVM element (
M_{x}\geq 0
,

\sum_{x}M_{x}=\openone
). Further, we assume that the state
\rho_{\phi}
is drawn with probability distribution
p(\phi)
. The mutual information
I(X,\Phi)
, maximized over the choice of the measurement
\{M_{x}\}_{x}
, is bounded by Holevo information
Wilde 2011
. The following quantum version of
Theorem 1
and
Theorem 2
holds.

Theorem 3
.

Given a family of quantum states
\rho_{\phi}
(differentiable with respect to
\phi
), appearing with probability distribution
p(\phi)
with
\textrm{supp}\,p(\phi)\subseteq[a,b]
, the Holevo information
\chi
is bounded by:

\chi\leq\ln\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}d\phi\sqrt{F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]}\right),

(15)

with

the quantum Fisher information of
\rho_{\phi}
and
\chi:=S\left(\int p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}d\phi\right)-\int p(\phi)S(\rho_{\phi})d\phi
, with
S(\rho):=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)
the von Neuman entropy.

For any differentiable probability distribution
p(\phi)
, the Holevo information is bounded by:

\chi\leq{\ln}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\phi\sqrt{F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]p(\phi)^{2}+\dot{p}(\phi)^{2}}\right)+H(\Phi).

(16)

Applications to quantum metrology.—

To show the usefulness of the newly derived bounds, we now show how they can be used to give bounds to noisy quantum metrology for mutual information.

Consider the
\phi
-dependent CPTP map
\Lambda_{\phi}[\cdot]
that
noisily encodes the parameter
\phi
onto a quantum probe. The phase estimation problem is a typical
example. Consider the simple case of qubit probes, where the parameter

\phi
is encoded through a unitary

U_{\phi}=\ket{0}\bra{0}+e^{i\phi}\ket{1}\bra{1}
, followed by
some kind of noise (e.g. dephasing, erasing) which together constitute
the map
\Lambda_{\phi}[\cdot]
, which we will further treat as a quantum gate. The question is: what maximal MI may be obtained with the usage of
N
gates in an optimal way?

In
Hassani et al. 2017
the Heisenberg Scaling (HS) for MI has
been defined as
I\propto{\ln}N
and the standard quantum limit as

I\propto\frac{1}{2}{\ln}N
(in analogy to the scaling of RMSE

\Delta\phi\propto 1/N
and
\Delta\phi\propto 1/\sqrt{N}

respectively). In the case of noiseless estimation, it is known that using gates separately allows at most standard quantum limits, while more complicated schemes, including entanglement or multipassing, allow for obtaining HS (for example via QPEA algorithm
Hassani et al. 2017
). However, the
noisy case has not been discussed up to now while using MI as a figure
of merit.

Note that to obtain HS it is necessary to use all available resources jointly in a single experiment realization, therefore the argument based on the asymptotic efficiency of the maximum likelihood estimator cannot be applied here. Therefore, even if intuitively we expect that the inability to obtain HS in FI should imply an inability to obtain HS also for MI, it is not trivial to prove it formally in a general case.
Note also, that in this model

\phi\in[0,2\pi[
, and the Efroimovich’s inequality or van Trees inequality cannot be applied
to a uniform prior.

We can now apply the bounds derived above, e.g.
Eq. (
1
)
, to
transfer the highly nontrivial theory of noisy-channel estimation
theory from the FI to the MI formalism. For FI, the general necessary
and sufficient conditions for the obtainability of HS in the
estimation of a given channel are known, expressed in terms of Kraus
operators
Demkowicz-Dobrzański and Maccone 2014
;
Kurdziałek et al. 2023
;
Zhou and Jiang 2021

or Lindblad operators
Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al. 2017
;
Layden et al. 2019
.
Moreover, the resulting bounds are known to be saturable in the
asymptotic limit
N\to\infty
of many
gates
Zhou and Jiang 2021
. For example, for a mentioned model of
phase estimation, it was shown that in the presence of dephasing or
erasure noise, the FI obtainable by any protocol involving
N
gates
is bounded from above by
F\leq N\eta/(1-\eta)

Demkowicz-Dobrzański and Maccone 2014
, where

\eta
is the noise parameter (
\eta\to 1
in the noiseless case,

\eta\to 0
for maximum noise). The bound remains valid even for the most general adaptive scheme, including entanglement with arbitrary large ancilla and applying unitary control after each action of gate. Applying this bound to
Eq. (
1
)

we immediately find

I(X,\Phi)\leq{\ln}(1+\pi\sqrt{N}\sqrt{\eta/(1-\eta)})
and using
Eq. (
10
)
we have

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq 2/(\pi e)(1/\pi+\sqrt{N}\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)})^{-2}.

Even if the bound is not tight
(there is an additive constant gap), it implies a standard quantum
limit asymptotic scaling for all
\eta<1
, and also gives the scaling
with the intensity of the noise (see App.
D
for more
details). Similar results can be obtained easily using different
bounds to
FI
Escher et al. 2011
;
Demkowicz-Dobrzański et al. 2012
;
Knysh et al. 2014
. Naturally,
this does not rule out quantum enhancements: for sufficiently large

\eta
one might still find an arbitrarily large
constant
gain
over the SQL.

These will provide only the upper bounds to MI and so
they can only be used to exclude the possibility of attaining the
HS, but never to prove its achievability. While there
exist lower bounds on the MI expressed in terms of the FI in the
literature, they all require additional assumptions and a general
discussion is impossible.
In the noiseless case, it may be exemplified by the N00N state which
allows the HS for the FI, but not for the MI, due to its periodicity with period
1/N
(indeed, irrespectively of the value of
\phi
, the N00N state
belongs to a two-dimensional subspace of the full Hilbert space, so it can carry at most one bit of information

I\leqslant{\ln}(2)
). This demonstrates that, while the N00N state is highly effective for local estimation, it presents significant challenges for global estimation and requires careful consideration in its application
Higgins et al. 2009
.

Applications to communication.—

Beyond the problems of estimation and metrology, our bound is also useful for transferring the theorems derived in the metrology context to communication problems. Consider a situation where Alice employs the parameter
\phi\in[a,b]
as an alphabet for communicating with a state
\rho_{\phi}
. Alice has the freedom to choose if she will use all values of
\phi
or restrict herself to a discrete subset. From
Eq. (
1
)
the MI between Alice and Bob is then bounded by

I(A,B)\leq\ln\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{a}^{b}d\phi\sqrt{F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]}\right)
. The bound works independently of what messages (and with what probabilities) Alice is sending. Moreover, one can use
Eq. (
1
)
to give bounds to the classical capacity of a quantum or classical channel when the decoding strategy is fixed and its FI is known.

Conclusions.—

In conclusion, we have derived two bounds, Eqs. (
1
) and
(
2
) for the MI in terms of the FI. We used these
bounds to give two extensions of the van Trees inequality,
Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
). We discussed the relation of
all these bounds to the Efroimovich inequality (
12
). We also derived the quantum version of these bounds, Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
), where the Holevo information is bounded in terms of QFI. To prove the usefulness of
these bounds, we have shown that they can be used to extend to the
MI-based quantum metrology many highly nontrivial results known for
the FI case. They also constitute the bounds on communication in terms of FI. See also
Lu et al. 2024
for alternative bounds linking MI with FI.

Appendix A
Derivation of inequalities (
4
) and (
6
)

First we derive inequality (
4
), i.e. we perform direct maximization of

\max_{q_{x}:\sum_{x}q_{x}=1}\left(\sum_{x}q_{x}\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{q_{x}}\right)

(17)

by using the Lagrange multiplier method. The condition for local extremum with constrain
\sum q_{x}=1
is:

\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{x}}\left[\sum_{x}q_{x}\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{q_{x}}-\lambda\left(\Big(\sum_{x}q_{x}\big)-1\right)\right]=\\
\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{q_{x}}-1-\lambda=0,

(18)

which imposes that
\ln\max_{\phi}\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{q_{x}}
are the same for all
x
. Together with
\sum q_{x}=1
is gives
q_{x}=\frac{p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}{\sum_{x}\max_{\phi}p(x|\phi)f(\phi)}
. Since it is the only stationary point and the function takes smaller values outside of this point, this is a global maximum, which was to be proven.

Next, we derive (
6
), using Cauchy’s inequality for vectors
\sqrt{\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}}
and
\sqrt{p(x|\phi)}
.

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|=

(19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\left[\sqrt{\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}}\sqrt{p(x|\phi)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{x}\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}}\sqrt{\sum_{x}p(x|\phi)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{x}\left|\frac{d(p(x|\phi)f(\phi))}{d\phi}\right|^{2}\frac{1}{p(x|\phi)}}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{x}\left[\frac{\dot{p}(x|\phi)^{2}}{p(x|\phi)}f(\phi)^{2}+2\dot{p}(x|\phi)f(\phi)\dot{f}(\phi)+p(x|\phi)\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{F(\phi)f(\phi)^{2}+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}}.

where we used
\sum_{x}p(x|\phi)=1
and
\sum_{x}\dot{p}(x|\phi)=0
.

Appendix C
Applying the bound for Gaussian distribution

Consider an a priori distribution
p(\phi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp(-\frac{\phi^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})
. Substituting
Eq. (
2
)
into
Eq. (
8
)
we obtain:

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{2}{\pi e}\frac{1}{(\int d\phi\sqrt{F(\phi)+\phi^{2}/\sigma^{4}}p(\phi))^{2}}.

(24)

Further, assuming that
F(\phi)
does not change with
\phi
, the integral may be calculated analiticaly as
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma}U[-\frac{1}{2},0,\frac{F\sigma^{2}}{2}]
, where
U(\cdot)
is Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function.

For a slightly less tight, but simpler bound
for the case where FI does not depend on
\phi
, note that after introducing the variable
\eta=\phi^{2}
, the quantity
\sqrt{F+\eta/\sigma^{4}}
is concave as a function of
\eta
, so
\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{F+\eta/\sigma^{4}}]\leq\sqrt{F+\mathbb{E}[\eta]/\sigma^{4}}
(with
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
denoting averaging), so the integral may be bounded from above by
\sqrt{F+1/\sigma^{2}}
(numerical results also show that this is a good approximation for the exact result for
F\sigma^{2}\gg 1
). For any
F,\sigma
we have:

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{2}{\pi e}\frac{1}{F+1/\sigma^{2}},

(25)

while from the van Trees inequality we have
Eq. (
13
)
(taken for constant
F
):

\overline{\Delta^{2}\tilde{\phi}}\geq\frac{1}{F+1/\sigma^{2}}.

(26)

Then, for this specific case, our bound is less tight, because of the
2/\pi e
multiplicative factor.

Appendix D
Mutual information in occurrence of noise

Figure 2:
The mutual information bound given by
Eq. (
1
)
with maximum Fisher information, for the dephasing channel and amplitude damping channel. The two dashed lines help to compare the bound with HS and SQL.

We now give a global bound to the mutual information in terms of
N
, the number of calls to the phase gate
U_{\phi}=\ket{0}\bra{0}+e^{i\phi}\ket{1}\bra{1}
, in the presence of dephasing and amplitude damping noise.

In our noise model, each unitary gate
U_{\phi}
is replaced by the noisy gate acting on the density operator as

\Lambda_{\phi}:\rho\mapsto\sum_{k}K_{k}U_{\phi}\rho U_{\phi}^{\dagger}K_{k}^{\dagger},

(27)

with
K_{k}
being the Kraus operators.

For dephasing noise the Kraus operators are

K_{0}=\openone\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{\eta}}{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad K_{1}=\sigma_{z}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\eta}}{2}\right)^{1/2},

(28)

where
\openone=\ket{0}\bra{0}+\ket{1}\bra{1}
,
\sigma_{z}=\ket{0}\bra{0}-\ket{1}\bra{1}
, and
\eta
is the noise parameter.
Also, the Kraus operators in the presence of amplitude damping noise are

K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{\eta}\end{pmatrix},\quad K_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{1-\eta}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(29)

Finally, for erasure noise the Kraus operators are

\begin{gathered}K_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\eta}&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{\eta}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad K_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\\
K_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
\sqrt{1-\eta}&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad K_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\eta}&0\end{pmatrix},\end{gathered}

(30)

where a third dimension is added to indicate loss of phase information.

In this notation, an asymptotic upper bound to the Fisher information is
Demkowicz-Dobrzański and Maccone 2014
;
Kurdziałek et al. 2023
,

F\leq NF_{\mathrm{as}},

(31)

where
F_{\mathrm{as}}=\eta/(1-\eta)
for both dephasing and erasure noise with the most general AD strategy, as well as amplitude damping noise with EN strategy
Knysh et al. 2014
.
Combining the Fisher information bound with
Eq. (
1
)
, we get a global upper bound to the mutual information,

I(X,\Phi)\leq\ln\left(1+\pi\sqrt{\frac{N\eta}{1-\eta}}\right).

(32)

For amplitude damping noise, the Fisher information bound is slightly different, and the mutual information bound can be derived similarly.

Especially, when limited to the EN strategy, a tighter bound to Fisher information for finite-
N
is given by
Kołodyński and Demkowicz-Dobrzański 2013
,

F\leq\frac{NF_{\mathrm{as}}}{1+\frac{F_{\mathrm{as}}}{N}}.

(33)

Combining the Fisher information bound with
Eq. (
1
)
, we get a global upper bound to the mutual information

I(X,\Phi)\leq\ln\left(1+\pi\sqrt{\frac{N\eta/(1-\eta)}{1+\eta/N(1-\eta)}}\right),

(34)

for all these three noise models.
We plot the right hand side of
Eq. (
34
)
in
Fig. 2
, in which we can see a transition from the HS to the SQL as
N
increases. The stronger the noise is, the earlier the transition happens.

Appendix E
Holevo information bounded by Quantum Fisher Information – proof of
Theorem 3

In this section, we prove
Theorem 3
, using a similar way of reasoning as in the proof of
Theorem 1
,
Theorem 2
, adapted to the quantum version. Namely, we introduce function
f(\phi)
and prove stronger statement:

Theorem 4
.

Given a family of quantum states
\rho_{\phi}
(differentiable with respect to
\phi
), appearing with probability distribution
p(\phi)
. For any non-negative function
f(\phi)
satisfying
\textrm{supp}\,f(\phi)\supseteq\textrm{supp}\,p(\phi)
and vanishing at
\pm\infty
,
the Holevo information
\chi
is bounded by:

\displaystyle\chi\leq

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\sqrt{F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]f(\phi)^{2}+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}}\right)

(35)

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\ln f(\phi),

with
F_{Q}
the quantum Fisher information of
\rho_{\phi}
and
\chi:=S\left(\int p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}d\phi\right)-\int p(\phi)S(\rho_{\phi})d\phi
, with
S(\rho):=-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho)
the von Neuman entropy.

Then
Theorem 3
comes as a collolary of
Eq. (
35
)
with
f(\phi)=1/(b-a)
on
[a,b]
and
0
outside or with
f(\phi)=p(\phi)
.

Before the proof we give the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

For any two vectors
\ket{a},\ket{b}
,

\mathrm{Tr}\big|\ket{a}\!\bra{b}+\ket{b}\!\bra{a}\big|\leq 2\sqrt{\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle},

(36)

where
|A|:=\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
for any operator
A
.

Proof of
Lemma 1
.

The column space of the operator
\ket{a}\!\bra{b}+\ket{b}\!\bra{a}
is
\mathcal{A}:=\mathrm{span}(\ket{a},\ket{b})
of dimension at most 2.
When
\dim\mathcal{A}\leq 1
the LHS becomes
2|\mathrm{Re}\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle|
, making it a trivial result.

When
\dim\mathcal{A}=2
, suppose
x\ket{a}+y\ket{b}\in\mathcal{A}
is an eigenvector of
\ket{a}\!\bra{b}+\ket{b}\!\bra{a}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, then

\lambda=\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle+\frac{y}{x}\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle=\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle+\frac{x}{y}\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle.

(37)

By eliminating
y/x
we get

\lambda^{2}-\big(\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle+\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle\big)\lambda-\big(\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle-\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle\big)=0.

(38)

Note that
\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle+\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
and
-\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle+\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle\leq 0
, thus the equation has two real roots
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
with opposite signs.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\big|\ket{a}\!\bra{b}+\ket{b}\!\bra{a}\big|

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}-4\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle+\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle)^{2}+4(\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle-\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle-\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle)^{2}+4\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\left\langle a\middle|a\right\rangle\left\langle b\middle|b\right\rangle},

where in the last inequality we use
\left\langle a\middle|b\right\rangle-\left\langle b\middle|a\right\rangle\in i\mathbb{R}
.

\square

Lemma 2
.

Given positive operators
A,B
satisfying
0<A\leq B
, i.e.,
A
is positive definite and
(B-A)
is positive semi-definite, then

\ln(A)\leq\ln(B).

(40)

Proof of
Lemma 2
.

The condition
0<A\leq B
implies that
0<A^{1/2}\leq B^{1/2}

Lax 2007
.
By iterating it we know
0<A^{1/2^{m}}\leq B^{1/2^{m}}
holds for any positive integer
m
.
Finally, by the identity
\ln(a)=\lim_{x\to 0}(a^{x}-1)/x
,

\ln(A)=\lim_{m\to+\infty}2^{m}(A^{1/2^{m}}-I).

(41)

then
Eq. (
40
)
is obtained.

\square

Proof of
Theorem 4
.

We first consider the pure state case where
\rho_{\phi}=\ket{\psi_{\phi}}\bra{\psi_{\phi}}
.
Write,

\rho=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}\mathrm{d}\phi.

(42)

Then
\chi=S(\rho)
, and the quantum Fisher information is also simplified to,

F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]=4\left[\left\langle\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\middle|\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\right\rangle-\left\langle\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\middle|\psi_{\phi}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{\phi}\middle|\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\right\rangle\right].

(43)

Moreover, by adjusting the global phase of
\ket{\psi_{\phi}}
we can force
\left\langle\psi_{\phi}\middle|\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\right\rangle=0
and simplify
Eq. (
43
)
to,

F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]=4\left\langle\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\middle|\dot{\psi}_{\phi}\right\rangle.

(44)

To prove
Eq. (
35
)
, we first shift the last term to the left side. For pure state (using
\chi=S(\rho)
), we therefore have:

\displaystyle\chi+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\ln f(\phi)

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\rho)+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\ln f(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\rho)+\mathrm{Tr}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}\ln[f(\phi)\rho_{\phi}].

Now, to bound it from above we will find the matrix bounding
f(\phi)\rho_{\phi}
from above (in the sense of matrix inequality).

To do that, we define an unnormalized vector

\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}=\sqrt{f(\phi)}\ket{\psi_{\phi}}
and the operator:

\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\right|\mathrm{d}\phi.

(46)

For any
\phi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\phi_{0}}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\right|\mathrm{d}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\phi_{0}}^{+\infty}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\right|\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\phi_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\mathrm{d}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\phi_{0}}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}=f(\phi_{0})\rho_{\phi_{0}},

where we use
f(\phi)|_{\pm\infty}=0
.

By
Lemma 2
,
\ln[f(\phi)\rho_{\phi}]\leq\ln\tilde{\rho}
in the support space of
\rho_{\phi}
, thus we can further bound
Eq. (
45
)
by:

\displaystyle S(\rho)+\mathrm{Tr}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}\ln[f(\phi)\rho_{\phi}]

(48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(\rho)+\mathrm{Tr}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\rho_{\phi}\ln\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\rho)+\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\ln\tilde{\rho})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S(\rho||\tilde{\rho}^{0})+\ln\mathrm{Tr}\tilde{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ln\mathrm{Tr}\tilde{\rho}.

where
\tilde{\rho}^{0}:=\tilde{\rho}/\mathrm{Tr}\tilde{\rho}
is the normalized
\tilde{\rho}
and
S(\rho||\tilde{\rho}^{0}):=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\tilde{\rho}^{0})]
is the quantum relative entropy, which is always non-negative. While calculating
\mathrm{Tr}\tilde{\rho}
we can go with the trace under integral:

\mathrm{Tr}\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Tr}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\right|\mathrm{d}\phi,

(49)

so RHS of
Eq. (
48
)
may be further bounded using
Lemma 1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\phi}\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\right|

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\big|\ket{\Psi_{\phi}}\!\bra{\dot{\Psi}_{\phi}}+\ket{\dot{\Psi}_{\phi}}\!\bra{\Psi_{\phi}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\left\langle\Psi_{\phi}\middle|\Psi_{\phi}\right\rangle\left\langle\dot{\Psi}_{\phi}\middle|\dot{\Psi}_{\phi}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{f(\phi)^{2}F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}},

which, after integration and substitution to
Eq. (
48
)
gives
Eq. (
35
)
.

Finally, in the general case where
\rho_{\phi}
can be mixed states, we can find a purification of
\rho_{\phi}
so that the proof above applies.
The quantum Fisher information of
\rho_{\phi}
is equal to the minimum quantum Fisher information of the purification
Escher et al. 2011
.
Let
\ket{\psi_{\phi}}
be a purification that reaches the minimum, i.e.,
F_{Q}[\rho_{\phi}]=F_{Q}[\ket{\psi_{\phi}}\bra{\psi_{\phi}}]
.
Since
\ket{\psi_{\phi}}
is pure, its Holevo quantity
\chi^{\prime}
satisfies,

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}\leq

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\sqrt{F_{Q}[\ket{\psi_{\phi}}\bra{\psi_{\phi}}]f(\phi)^{2}+\dot{f}(\phi)^{2}}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\phi\>p(\phi)\ln f(\phi),

Meanwhile, the Holevo quantity
\chi^{\prime}
is equal to the quantum mutual information between the two subsystems of
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}p(\phi)\ket{\phi}\bra{\phi}\otimes\ket{\psi_{\phi}}\bra{\psi_{\phi}}
(where
\ket{\phi}
is an orthonormal set), which does not increase under the partial trace operation to
\ket{\psi_{\phi}}\bra{\psi_{\phi}}
, thus
\chi\leq\chi^{\prime}
(where
\chi
is the Holevo information of
\rho
).
Combining all the above,
Eq. (
35
)
for general mixed states
\rho_{\phi}
is obtained.

\square

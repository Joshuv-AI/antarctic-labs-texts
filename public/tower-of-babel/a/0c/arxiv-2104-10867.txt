Andrea Lucchini, Daniele Nemmi

Abstract

Introduction

If a finite group
G
can be generated by
d
elements, the question of which sets of
d
elements of
G
generate
G
is nontrivial. The simplest interesting case is when
G
is 2-generated. One tool developed to study generators of 2-generated finite groups is the generating graph
\Gamma(G)
of
G.
This is the graph which has the elements of
G
as vertices and an edge between two elements
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
if
G
is generated by
g_{1}
and
g_{2}.
Note that the generating graph may be defined for any group, but it only has edges if
G
is
2
-generated.

Several strong structural results about
\Gamma(G)
are known in the case where
G
is simple, and this reflects the rich group theoretic structure of these groups. For example, if
G
is a nonabelian simple group, then the only isolated vertex of
\Gamma(G)
is the identity
[
12
]
and the graph
\Delta(G)
obtained by removing the isolated vertex is connected with diameter two
[
2
]
and, if
|G|
is sufficiently large, admits a Hamiltonian cycle
[
3
]
(it is conjectured that the condition on
|G|
can be removed). Moreover, in recent years there has been considerable interest in attempting to classify the groups
G
for which
\Gamma(G)
shares the strong properties of the generating graphs of simple groups. Recently it has been proved the remarkable result that
the identity is the unique isolated vertex of
\Gamma(G)
if and only if all proper quotients of
G
are cyclic
[
4
]
.
An open question is whether the subgraph
\Delta(G)
of
\Gamma(G)
induced by the non-isolated vertices is connected, for every finite group
G.

The answer is positive if
G
is soluble
[
7
]
and in this case
the diameter of
\Delta(G)
is at most three
[
15
]
. In
[
13
]
it is proved that when
G
is nilpotent, then
\Delta(G)
is maximally connected.

A number of important graph classes, can be defined either structurally or
in terms of forbidden induced subgraphs. The aim of this paper is to investigate some properties of the forbidden subgraphs of the generating graph.

A perfect graph is a graph in which the chromatic number of every induced
subgraph equals the order of the largest clique of that subgraph (clique number). A hole in a graph
\Gamma
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma
isomorphic to a chordless cycle of length at least 4. An antihole is an induced subgraph
\Delta
of
\Gamma
, such that
\overline{\Delta}
is a hole of the complement graph
\overline{\Gamma}
. A hole
(resp. an antihole) is odd or even according to the number of its vertices. The strong perfect graph theorem is a forbidden graph characterization of the perfect graphs as being exactly the graphs that have neither odd
holes nor odd antiholes. It was conjectured by Claude Berge in 1961. A proof by Maria Chudnovsky, Neil Robertson, Paul Seymour and Robin Thomas was announced in 2002 and published by them in 2006
[
6
]
. Motivated by the strong perfect graph theorem we analyze the existence of
m
-holes or
m
-antiholes in the generating graph of a finite group
G.
The first result that can be proved with this approach is a complete characterization of the 2-generated finite nilpotent groups with a perfect generating graph.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finite 2-generated nilpotent group. Then
\Gamma(G)
is perfect if and only if the index of the Frattini subgroup is the product of at most 4 primes.

In general the condition on the number of prime divisors of the index of the Frattini subgroup is neither necessary nor sufficient to ensure that the generating graph is perfect, as it follows for example from the study of the generating graph of dihedral groups.

Theorem 2
.

Let
D_{n}
be the dihedral group of order
2n
and degree
n.
Then

\Gamma(D_{n})
is perfect if and only if one of the following occurs:

(1)

n
is even;

(2)

n
is odd, divisible by at most two distinct primes.

An interesting and surprising consequence of Theorem

is that if
G
is a 2-generated finite group and
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, then the fact that
\Gamma(G)
is perfect, does not imply that

\Gamma(G/N)
is also perfect. For example let
m=p_{1}\cdot p_{2}\cdot p_{3}
be the product of three distinct primes and let

be the dihedral group of order

By Theorem
2
,
\Gamma(G)
is perfect, however

has a normal subgroup

of order 2 such that

and, again by Theorem

is not perfect.

We will prove (see Theorem

A path graph is a graph whose vertices can be listed in the order
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}
such that the edges are
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
where
i=1,2,\dots,n-1.
A path graph with
n
-vertices is usually denoted by
P_{n}.
A graph
\Gamma
is called a cograph if
\Gamma
has no induced subgraph isomorphic to the four-vertex path
P_{4}.
Several alternative characterizations of cographs can be given: a cograph is a graph all of whose induced subgraphs have the property that any maximal clique intersects any maximal independent set in a single vertex;
a cograph is a graph in which every nontrivial induced subgraph has at least two vertices with the same neighbourhoods;
a cograph is a graph in which every connected induced subgraph has a disconnected complement; a cograph is a graph all of whose connected induced subgraphs have diameter at most 2. We will prove
that if
N
is a normal subgroup of a 2-generated finite group
G
and
\Gamma(G/N)
contains an induced
subgraph isomorphic to
P_{n}
, then so does
\Gamma(G)
(see Lemma
10
). Thus, unlike the property that the generating graph is perfect, the property that
\Gamma(G)
is a cograph is inherited by the epimorphic images of
G.
This is a considerable advantage in the study of groups whose generating graph is a cograph and allows to obtain quite general results. For example we can completely characterize the 2-generating finite soluble group whose generating graph is a cograph.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a 2-generated finite soluble group. Then
\Gamma(G)
is a cograph if and only if one of the following occurs.

(1)

G
is cyclic, and the order of
G
is divisible by at most two different prime numbers.

(2)

G
is a
p
-group.

(3)

G/\frat(G)\cong V\rtimes\langle x\rangle
where
x
has prime
order
and
V
is a faithful irreducible
\langle x\rangle
-module.

Moreover we will prove the following theorems.

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and assume that the identity element is the unique isolated vertex of

If
\Gamma(G)

Theorem 6

Let
G
be a 2-generated finite group. If
\Gamma(G)

Let

is not a cograph.

The previous result suggests the following stronger conjecture.

Conjecture 3

Let

be a 2-generated finite group. If
\Gamma(G)
is a cograph, then

is soluble.

2.
Cographs

Our first result is that if
\Gamma(G)
is a cograph, then
\Gamma(G/N)
is also a cograph, for every normal subgroup
N
of
G.

In order to prove a more general statement which implies the previous sentence, we need to recall an auxiliary result, which generalizes an argument due to Gaschütz
[
10
]
. Given a subset
X
of a finite group
G,
we will denote by
d_{X}(G)
the smallest cardinality
of a set of elements of
G
generating
G
together with the elements of
X.
In the particular case when
X=\varnothing
,
d_{\varnothing}(G)=d(G)

is the smallest cardinality of a generating set of
G.

Lemma 9
.

[
7
, Lemma 6]
Let
X
be a subset of
G

and
N
a normal subgroup of
G
and suppose that

\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r},X,N\rangle=G.

If
r\geq d_{X}(G),
we can find
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\in N
so that
\langle g_{1}n_{1},\dots,g_{r}n_{r},X\rangle=G.

Lemma 10
.

Let
G
be a 2-generated finite group and
N
a normal subgroup of
G
and let
t\in\mathbb{N}
with
t\geq 2.
If
\Gamma(G/N)
contains an induced
subgraph isomorphic to
P_{t}
, then so does
\Gamma(G).

Proof.

Assume that
(a_{1}N,a_{2}N,\dots,a_{t}N)
is a
t
-vertex path in

\Gamma(G/N).
By Lemma
9
there exist
n_{1},n_{2}\in N
such that

\langle a_{1}n_{1},a_{2}n_{2}\rangle=G.
In particular
d_{\{a_{2}n_{2}\}}(G)\leq 1,
so, again by Lemma
9
, if
t\geq 3
then there exists
n_{3}\in N
such that
\langle a_{2}n_{2},a_{3}n_{3}\rangle=G.

By repeating this argument, we can find
n_{1},\dots,n_{t}\in N
such that

\langle a_{i}n_{i},a_{i+1}n_{i+1}\rangle=G
for
1\leq i\leq t-1.
If
(r,s)\neq(i,i+1)
for some

, then
\langle a_{r},a_{s}\rangle N\neq G,
and consequently
\langle a_{r}n_{r},a_{s}n_{s}\rangle\neq G.

So
(a_{1}n_{1},\dots,a_{t}n_{t})
is a

-vertex path in
\Gamma(G).

.

Let

It can be easily checked that

would imply that

Lemma 12
.

Let
H
be a 2-generated finite soluble group and

a non-trivial irreducible

-module. Assume that there exist

such that

(1)

H=\langle a,b\rangle

(2)

H\neq\langle a\rangle,H\neq\langle b\rangle;

(3)

Consider the semidirect product

If no complemented chief factor of

is

-isomorphic to

\Gamma(G)
contains a subgraph isomorphic to the four-vertex path

Proof.

Let
|V|=p^{t},
with

\Omega_{a}=\{v\in V\mid\langle a,bv\rangle=G\},\quad\Omega_{b}=\{v\in V\mid\langle av,b\rangle=G\}.

Assume
v\notin\Omega_{a}.
Then
\langle a,bv\rangle
is a complement of

in

. The fact that no complemented chief factor of

is

-isomorphic to

ensures that all the complements of

in

are conjugated (see

), so there exists

(a,bv)=(a^{w},b^{w}).
In particular

and

This implies
|V\setminus\Omega_{a}|\leq|[b,C_{V}(a)]|\leq|C_{V}(a)|.
Since we are assuming

, we deduce

(2.1)

|\Omega_{a}|\geq|V|-|C_{V}(a)|\geq p^{t}-p^{t-1}.

For the same reason

(2.2)

|\Omega_{b}|\geq|V|-|C_{V}(b)|\geq p^{t}-p^{t-1}.

Let
\Omega=\{(v_{1},v_{2})\in V^{2}\mid\langle av_{1},bv_{2}\rangle=G\}.

The number of pairs
(v_{1},v_{2})
in

coincides with the number of complements of

in

, so

(2.3)

|\Omega|=|V^{2}|-|V|.

If
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\Omega\cap(\Omega_{b}\times\Omega_{a})
then

(a,bv_{2},av_{1},b)
is a four-vertex path in
\Gamma(G).

In particular, if
|\Omega_{a}\times\Omega_{b}|+|\Omega|>|V|^{2},
then
(\Omega_{a}\times\Omega_{b})\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing,
and

contains
P_{4}
. So we may assume

(2.4)

|\Omega_{a}||\Omega_{b}|\leq|V^{2}|-|\Omega|=|V|.

In particular it follows from (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), that
(p^{t}-p^{t-1})^{2}\leq p^{t},
i.e.

(2.5)

p^{t}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{2}.

This implies

p=2
and
t=2
, i.e.
V\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
. We have two possibilities:

a)
H/C_{H}(V)\cong\gl(2,2)\cong\perm(3)
.
In this case
G/C_{H}(V)\cong\perm(4).
Since

((1,2),(2,3,4),(1,4),(1,2,3))
is a four-vertex path in

the conclusion follows from

.

b)
H/C_{H}(V)\cong C_{3}.
In this case
C_{V}(a)=C_{V}(b)=\{0\}
, but then, by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
),

Assume that

\dim_{\End_{H}(N)}N=1

be a complement of

in

Since

that no complemented chief factor of

is

Moreover, since

there exist

such that

With a similar argument we can prove that

is a

There exist

(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4})

There exists

a contradiction.

There is no

such that

since this would imply

3.3.

and

We have seen in the previous subsections that if

is divisible by at most three distinct primes, then

is perfect. However there exist 2-generated finite groups whose generating graph is not perfect, although their order is divisible only by two distinct primes.
Let

and let

,
h_{2}
,
h_{3}
be the nontrivial elements
of

. Let

be an odd prime number and consider

We may define an action of

on

by setting

\displaystyle x_{1}^{h_{1}}=x_{1},\quad x_{1}^{h_{2}}=x_{1}^{-1},\quad x_{1}^{h_{3}}=x_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle x_{2}^{h_{1}}=x_{2}^{-1},\quad x_{2}^{h_{2}}={x_{2}},\quad x_{2}^{h_{3}}=x_{2}^{-1},

\displaystyle x_{3}^{h_{1}}=x_{3}^{-1},\quad x_{3}^{h_{2}}=x_{3}^{-1},\quad x_{3}^{h_{3}}=x_{3}.

Let

It can be easily checked
that

is a 5-hole in

Lemma 27
.

Let
G
be a 2-generated finite group and let

be an odd integer, with
m\geq 5.
Let

If there exist two maximal subgroups
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
of

such that

then

does
not induce neither an
m
-hole nor an
m
-antihole.

Proof.

Suppose that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is an
m
-hole induced by

. We may assume
a_{1}\in M_{1}.
Since
G=\langle a_{i},a_{i+1}\rangle
, it follows
a_{i}\in M_{2}\setminus M_{1}
if

is even,
a_{i}\in M_{1}\setminus M_{2}
if
i
is odd. In particular, since
m
is odd,
G=\langle a_{1},a_{m}\rangle\leq M_{1},
a contradiction.
Now suppose that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is an
m
-antihole induced by

. Again we may assume

If

then

implies

and therefore

otherwise

We may exclude this possibility since a

-antihole is isomorphic to a

-hole.
∎

Lemma 28
.

Suppose that
G=\left(\langle x\rangle\times\langle y\rangle\right)\rtimes\langle z\rangle
, with
|x|=p_{1},

|y|=p_{2}
,
|z|=p_{3},

where

are primes.
If

is 2-generated, then
\Gamma(G)
is perfect.

Proof.

If
G
is abelian, then the conclusion follows from Theorem
1
. So we may assume
x\notin Z(G).
Let
m\geq 5
be an odd integer and suppose that
X\subseteq G
induces an
m
-hole or an
m
-antihole in
\Gamma(G).

First we claim that if
y\in Z(G)
, then
p_{2}=p_{3}.
Indeed assume
y\in Z(G)
and
p_{2}\neq p_{3}
and let

If

then

, so

is the unique maximal subgroup of

containing

and

by Lemma

. But then

with
M_{1}=\langle x,z\rangle
and
M_{2}=\langle x,y\rangle,
in contradiction with Lemma
27
.

Our second claim is that
\langle x\rangle
and
\langle y\rangle
are not
\langle z\rangle
-isomorphic. This is obvious if
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
, otherwise it is a necessary condition for

being 2-generated.

The two previous claims imply that for every

,
\langle x^{r}y^{s},x^{u}y^{v}z\rangle=G
if and only if

In particular consider

If either

or

. Moreover, the fact that
\langle x\rangle
and
\langle y\rangle
are not
\langle z\rangle

\langle x,y\rangle
is the unique maximal subgroup of

containing

In any case,

cannot be an element of

.

Let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
be an
m
-hole or an
m
-antihole in
\Gamma(G)
induced by
X
. By what we have said above, it is not restrictive to assume

with

and we may assume in particular

Notice that
\langle x^{r_{i}}y^{s_{i}}z,x^{r_{j}}y^{s_{j}}z\rangle=G
if and only if

and

If
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is an

for any

. This implies that either

or
a_{j}\in\langle y,z\rangle.
On the other hand

, so it is not restrictive to assume

On the other hand,

and

and

By Lemma

and

Since

and

and

Since

and

it follows

, a contradiction.

Now suppose that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})
is an

-antihole. We may assume

since a 5-antihole is isomorphic to a

-hole. From the conditions

and

it follows that is this not restrictive to assume

and

. Since

3.4.
The symmetric and alternating group

In this subsection we determine for which values of

, the symmetric and alternating group of degree

have perfect generating graph.
In the proofs we will need the following elementary lemmas:

Lemma 32
.

Let
H\leq S_{n}
be a transitive permutation group. If
\sigma\in H
is a
(n-1)
-cycle, then
H
is primitive.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that the fixed point of
\sigma
is
1
. Suppose
H
is imprimitive. Let
B
be the imprimitivity block which contains
1
, then
B^{\sigma}=B
since
1^{\sigma}=1
. By the imprimitivity assumption, there exists
1\neq i\in B
. But then
i,i^{\sigma},i^{\sigma^{2}},\dots i^{\sigma^{n-2}}
are all distinct elements, so
B=\{1,\dots,n\}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 33
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an odd natural number and
H\leq S_{n}
be a transitive permutation group. If
\sigma\in H
is a
(n-2)
-cycle, then
H
is primitive.

Proof.

Suppose, without loss of generality, that the fixed points of
\sigma
are
1
and
2
. Suppose
H
is imprimitive. As in the proof of the previous Lemma, take
B
to be the block containing
1
. Since
n
is odd,
|B|\geq 3
, so there is at least an element
i
in
B\setminus\{1,2\}
. Arguing as in the proof of the previous lemma, we obtain that
|B|\geq n-1
and, since
|B|
divides
n

, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 34
.

\Gamma(S_{n})

We distinguish the different possible values of

Two consecutive vertices

d)

3.6.

The alternating group
A_{5}
of degree 5 is the smallest 2-generated finite group whose generating graph is not perfect. More precisely
the following holds.

Theorem 40
.

Let
G

Then

Proof.

By Theorem
35
, we have only to prove that if

and

different primes. Hence

The other cases can be discussed with similar, but in general shorter, arguments. Let

. Let

(and consequently
a_{4}\in M_{4})

M_{1}
and

are the unique maximal subgroups of

containing

On the other hand, from

Notes on derived algebraic geometry
Can Yaylali

Abstract
. These are notes on derived algebraic geometry in the context of animated rings. More precisely, we recall the proof of Toën-Vaquié that the derived stack of perfect complexes is locally geometric in the language of ∞ \infty -categories. Along the way, we recall the necessary notions in derived commutative algebra and derived algebraic geometry. We also analyze the deformation theory and quasi-coherent modules over derived stacks.

A general theory using certain model categories (for example simplicial commutative rings, DG-rings and connective

Before working with model categories, one could ask how to extend algebraic geometry to sheaves of rings that take values in

In this article, we focus on derived algebraic geometry in the context of animated rings. The most notable difference is, that for animated rings in positive characteristic, we have a Frobenius endomorphism. This does not hold for

Another benefit of animated rings and so, derived algebraic geometry is the naturally occurring deformation theory. There is a natural notion of square-zero extensions and thus derivations. Classically, derivations are represented by the module of Kähler-differentials. In the context of derived algebraic geometry, derivations are represented by the cotangent complex.

Derived commutative algebra

The
\infty
-category of animated rings
\textup{AR}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by polynomial algebras, where we freely adjoin sifted colimits of those. Looking at over categories for any animated ring
A
, we can define the
\infty
-category of animated
A
-algebra
\textup{AR}_{A}\coloneqq(\textup{AR}_{\mathbb{Z}})_{A/}
. The benefit of this definition is that a lot of questions about functors from
\textup{AR}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
\infty
-categories with sifted colimits can be reduced to polynomial algebras. Animated rings should be thought as spectral commutative rings (i.e.
E_{\infty}
-rings) with extra structure. Especially, after forgetting this extra structure, we can also define modules over animated rings (as modules over the underlying
E_{\infty}
-ring). One important example of such a module is the cotangent complex. This module arises naturally if we want to define an analogue of the module of differentials as the module that represents the space of derivations.

By left Kan-extension of the classical square-zero extension, we can associate to any connective
A
-module, the square-zero extension

of
A
by

is discrete, then this is the classical cotangent complex of Illusie. We can also extend this definition to a relative version

for animated ring homomorphisms

The underlying

-ring of an animated ring is a commutative algebra object in spectra. Thus, we can define homotopy groups of animated rings and automatically see (with the theory developed in
[
Lur17
]
) that we can associate to every animated ring

-graded ring

A benefit of naturally occurring deformation theory is, that we can work with the cotangent complex to relate its properties to geometric properties of the underlying animated rings.

Proposition 0.1

(

)
.

Let

This cannot be achieved using the module of Kähler-differentials, as

One can also show that étale maps of animated rings

\pi_{0}A\rightarrow\pi_{0}B
of (classical) rings. So, one can think of animated rings as nilpotent thickenings into the homotopy direction of (classical) rings.

Geometricity of perfect complexes

Let us state the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 0.4

(
4.14
)
.

The derived stack

\displaystyle\textup{Perf}\colon\textup{AR}_{R}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto(\MMod_{A}^{\textup{perf}})^{\simeq}

is locally geometric and locally of finite presentation.

The proof is separated into different steps. First, write this stack as a filtered colimit of substacks
\textup{Perf}^{[a,b]}
, where we fix the Tor-amplitude of the perfect complexes by
a\leq b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Per definition, it is enough to show that these are
(b-a+1)
-geometric stacks locally of finite presentation and open in
Perf
.

The proof is by induction over
n\coloneqq b-a
. For
n=0
, this is an interesting and well known stack
\textup{Perf}^{[0,0]}\simeq\Proj\simeq\coprod_{n}\BGL_{n}
.

The idea for higher
n
is straightforward, knowing that any perfect module
M
of Tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
can be written as the fiber of
Q\rightarrow M
, where
Q
is a perfect module of Tor-amplitude
[a+1,b]
and
M
is perfect of Tor-amplitude in
[a+1,a+1]
. Inductively, this reduces to the question, if the stack classifying morphisms between perfect modules is geometric and smooth. But this this follows from explicit calculations of the cotangent complex.

For the openness, we have to consider the vanishing locus of perfect complexes. But since in classical algebraic geometry this is open, we can extend this to the derived world.

Structure of this article

We start by summarizing
[
Lur17
]
(see Section
1
). We try to show that spectral rings and modules behave in some sense like expected.

The next step is the introduction of derived commutative algebra, i.e. the theory of algebras over animated rings (see Section
2
). We first define animated rings
\textup{AR}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, show that there is a relation to
E_{\infty}
-rings and use this relation to define modules over animated rings. As a consequence, we can define the cotangent complex and define what properties of morphisms in
\textup{AR}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are. We end this section by showing that the cotangent complex is highly related to smoothness of morphisms.

After talking about derived commutative algebra, we introduce the theory derived algebraic geometry (see Section
3
). Mainly, we introduce the notion of derived stacks, geometricity of morphisms and derived schemes. We also talk about truncation of those and how it relates to classical algebraic geometry. Further, we again cover smoothness of such morphisms and how it relates to the cotangent complex.

We finish the summary on derived algebraic geometry by showing that the stack of perfect modules is locally geometric and locally of finite presentation (see Section
4
).

Definition 2.53

is free of finite rank, i.e.
P\otimes_{A}A_{i}\simeq A_{i}^{r}
for some

.

Proof.

Let
\Proj(A)
denote the full subcategory of
\MMod_{A}
of projective modules. We have an equivalence of categories
\textup{h}\Proj(A)\rightarrow\textup{h}\Proj(\pi_{0}A)\simeq\Proj_{\pi_{0}A}
given by the tensor product (see
[
Lur17
, Cor 7.2.2.19]
). By definition, this restricts to an equivalence of finite projective modules. Since classical finite projective modules on
\pi_{0}A
are finite locally free, we know that there exists an open cover
(\widetilde{A}_{i})
of
\pi_{0}A
such that
\widetilde{A}_{i}\otimes_{\pi_{0}A}\pi_{0}A\otimes_{A}P
is equivalent to some finite free
\widetilde{A}_{i}
-module. By Proposition
2.59
, we can lift this open cover to an étale cover

of
A
(certainly

is étale and faithfully flatness can be checked on

). Since

, the equivalence of the categories involved shows the claim.
∎

Proposition 3.52
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a presentable
\infty
-category and let

be an étale sheaf. Let

denote a right Kan extension of

\textup{AR}_{R}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{P}}(\textup{AR}_{R})^{\op}
. Then for any derived scheme
X
over
R

RF(X)\rightarrow\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\hookrightarrow X\\
\textup{affine open}\end{subarray}}F(U)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

This lemma is a generalization of
[
Kha18
, Lec. 1 Prop. 3.5]
but can be proven the same. For the convenience of the reader, we give a proof.

Let
X
be derived scheme and
Y\coloneqq\coprod_{i\in I}\Spec(A_{i})\rightarrow X
be a Zariski atlas. By Remark
3.50
we have

RF(X)\simeq\lim_{\Delta}RF(\check{C}(Y/X)_{\bullet}).

For any affine open
U\hookrightarrow X
let
Y_{U}\coloneqq\coprod_{i\in I}U\times_{X}\Spec(A_{i})\rightarrow U
be the induced cover on
U
. Thus the question reduces to showing the equivalence

RF(\check{C}(Y/X)_{n})\rightarrow\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\hookrightarrow X\\
\textup{ affine open}\end{subarray}}RF(\check{C}(Y_{U}/U)_{n}),

for all
[n]\in\Delta
. By cofinality, we may replace
X
in the limit argument by
\check{C}(Y/X)_{n}
for any

.

To see this, note that for every affine open

, we get a morphism
U\rightarrow U\times_{X}U\simeq\check{C}(Y/X)_{n}\times_{X}U\times_{\check{C}(Y/X)_{n}}U\simeq\check{C}(Y_{U}/U)_{n}\times_{\check{C}(Y/X)_{n}}U\rightarrow\check{C}(Y_{U}/U)
. Thus

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\hookrightarrow\check{C}(Y/X)_{n}\\
\textup{affine open}\end{subarray}}RF(U)\simeq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\hookrightarrow X\\
\textup{affine open}\end{subarray}}RF(\check{C}(Y_{U}/U)_{n}).

Assume the pairwise intersection of the
\Spec(A_{i})
is affines, then
X
is affine and the question is trivial. Now assume the pairwise intersection is not affine then it is open in an affine scheme and thus separated. Thus they admit Zariski covers, where each of the pairwise intersection is affine. Repeating the whole process concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.57

Let

]

A scheme

3.4.

This section is derived from

In this section, we will globalize the results of Section

We let

Let

Let

Remark 3.60

By Lemma

Remark 3.61

Note that any morphism of affine derived schemes

Lemma 3.62

Let

Lemma 3.63
.

Let
X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z
be a morphism of derived stacks. Assume

admits a cotangent complex, then
X/Y
admits a cotangent complex if and only if
X/Z
admits a cotangent complex. Further, we obtain a cofiber sequence

f^{*}L_{Y/Z}\rightarrow L_{X/Z}\rightarrow L_{X/Y}

of quasi-coherent modules over
X
if the cotangent complexes exist.

Proof.

This is stated in
[
Kha18
, Lec. 5 Prop. 5.7]
. But anyway we will give a proof.

Let us take a point
x\colon\Spec(A)\rightarrow X
. Then what we impose is that we have a cofiber sequence

L_{Y/Z,f\circ x}\rightarrow L_{X/Z,x}\rightarrow L_{X/Y,x}.

By Lemma
2.37
it is enough to show that for any connective
A
-module
M
the fiber of
\Der_{x}(X/Z,M)\rightarrow\Der_{f\circ x}(Y/Z,M)
at the trivial derivation (given by

) is given by
\Der_{x}(X/Y,M)
. But this is clear.
∎

Lemma 3.64
.

Let
j\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
be a monomorphism of derived stacks over
A
, then
j
admits a cotangent complex and
L_{j}\simeq 0
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
Kha18
, Lec. 5 Prop. 5.9]
, but for the convenience of the reader, we recall the proof.

It suffices to show that at any point
x\colon\Spec(B)\rightarrow X
the cotangent complex is zero, i.e. the space of derivations at
x
is contractible. Since
j
is a monomorphism, i.e. its fibers are
(-1)
-truncated (so either contractible or empty (see
[
Lur09
, Def. 5.5.6.8]
)), we know that the canonical map

X(B\oplus M)\rightarrow X(B)\times_{Y(B)}Y(B\oplus M)

is also a monomorphism (note that any point in the fiber of the above morphism defines a point in the fiber of
X(B\oplus M)\rightarrow Y(B\oplus M)
, which per definition has either contractible or empty fibers). But the canonical map
u\colon\Spec(B\oplus M)\rightarrow\Spec(B)\rightarrow X
defines a derivation, so the space of derivations is nonempty and thus contractible.
∎

Now we can easily see that the homotopy groups of the localization with respect to one element is given by the localization of the homotopy groups.

Lemma 3.65
.

Let
A
be an animated ring and
f\in\pi_{0}A=\pi_{0}\Hom_{\MMod_{A}}(A,A)
. Then we have
\pi_{i}(A[f^{-1}])\cong(\pi_{i}A)_{f}

From Proposition

is locally of finite presentation and a monomorphism. Since monomorphisms have a vanishing relative cotangent complex (see Lemma

), we conclude with Proposition

that

is étale. Hence, we conclude using the definition of étale morphisms.
∎

Lemma 3.66
.

Let
(A\rightarrow A_{i})_{i\in I}
be an étale covering in
\textup{AR}_{R}
and let

be an
A
-module. Then the family induced by base change
(A\oplus M\rightarrow A_{i}\oplus(M\otimes_{A}A_{i}))_{i\in I}
is an étale cover.

Proof.

We only need to show that
A_{i}\otimes_{A}(A\oplus M)\simeq A_{i}\oplus(M\otimes_{A}A_{i})
, since étale covers are stable under base change. But by construction the functors
A_{i}\otimes_{A}(-\oplus M)
and
(-\otimes_{A}A_{i})\oplus(M\otimes_{A}A_{i})
from
\textup{AR-Mod}^{\textup{cn}}_{R}
to

commute with sifted colimits and thus we are reduced to classical commutative algebra, where it is clear.
∎

Lemma 3.67
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a morphism of derived schemes. Then
f
admits a cotangent complex.

Proof.

A proof sketch is given in
[
Kha18
, Lec. 5 Thm. 5.12]
, but since these are lecture notes, we recall the proof.

We may assume that
Y=\Spec(R)
using Lemma
3.63
.

By Proposition
3.52
, we know that

\QQCoh(X)\simeq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Spec(B)\hookrightarrow X\\
\textup{ open immersion }\end{subarray}}\MMod_{B}.

For each of the open affines
\Spec(B)
in
X
, we take
L_{\Spec(B)}\coloneqq L_{B/\mathbb{Z}}
, viewed as a quasi-coherent sheaf on
\Spec(B)
. Since the cotangent complex is compatible with taking pullbacks, i.e.
L_{B}\otimes_{B}A\simeq L_{A}
for a triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}

where
\Spec(A),\Spec(B)
are open in
X
, we see that this indeed defines an object in the limit, which we denote with
L_{X}
(for the compatibility, note that the relative cotangent complexes of monomorphisms vanish by Lemma
3.64
). This defines a cotangent complex on
X
.

We have to show that
L_{X}
represents the space of derivations. Since we have glued the cotangent complex for affine opens, we will use a descent argument for arbitrary points. For this, we use that modules satisfy fpqc descent and that for any point
x\colon\Spec(B)\rightarrow X
, the base change with an affine open cover of
X
gives an affine open cover
(B_{i})
of
B
. Hence, for a connective
B
-module
M
, it suffices to show that

\lim\Hom_{B_{i}}(L_{X,B_{i}},M_{i})\simeq\lim\Der_{B_{i}}(X/\mathbb{Z},M_{i}),

where
M_{i}\coloneqq M\otimes_{B}B_{i}
, which is clear termwise, since each
B_{i}
factors through some affine open
A_{i}
of
X
by construction (to write the derivations as a limit use the sheaf property of
X
,
\Spec(\mathbb{Z})
and Lemma
3.66
). Note that for each affine open the cotangent complex exists and we claim that
L_{X,B_{i}}\simeq L_{X,A_{i}}\otimes_{A_{i}}B_{i}\simeq L_{\Spec(A_{i}),A_{i}}\otimes_{A_{i}}B_{i}\simeq L_{\Spec(A_{i}),B_{i}}
, which concludes the lemma.

To see this, note that
\Der_{B_{i}}(A_{i}/X,M_{i})\simeq 0
, since
\Spec(A_{i})\hookrightarrow X
is a monomorphism (see Lemma
3.64
). Therefore
\Der_{B_{i}}(A_{i}/\mathbb{Z},M_{i})\simeq\Der_{B_{i}}(X/\mathbb{Z},M_{i})
(since its fiber is
\Der_{B_{i}}(A_{i}/X,M_{i})
), the same holds if we replace the point by
A_{i}
. Since the cotangent complexes at
A_{i}
exist, we get
L_{\Spec(A_{i}),A_{i}}\simeq L_{X,A_{i}}
and after tensoring with
B_{i}
, we see that
L_{X,B_{i}}
is a cotangent complex for
X/\mathbb{Z}
at
B_{i}
if and only if
\Der_{B_{i}}(A_{i}/\mathbb{Z},M_{i})\simeq\Der_{B_{i}}(X/\mathbb{Z},M_{i})
but this we have seen above, i.e. we have equivalences

\displaystyle\Hom_{B_{i}}(L_{X,B_{i}},M_{i})\simeq\Hom_{B_{i}}(L_{\Spec(A_{i}),B_{i}},M_{i})\simeq\Der_{B_{i}}(A_{i}/\mathbb{Z},M_{i})\simeq\Der_{B_{i}}(X/\mathbb{Z},M_{i}).

We remark that by this construction and commutativity of
\tau_{\geq 0}
with limits, we have that
L_{X}
is connective. In particular, we have shown that
L_{X}
is a cotangent complex for
X/\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Remark 3.68
.

Let us give another construction of a cotangent complex. Consider the functor

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\Proj_{R}
denote the derived stack classifying finite projective modules. Then
\Proj_{R}\simeq\coprod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\BGL_{n,R}
, in particular
\Proj_{R}
is
1
-geometric and smooth.

Further,
\GL_{n,R}
is an affine derived scheme and
\Proj_{R}
has an affine diagonal.

Proof.

The proof is the same as
[
TV08
, Cor. 1.3.7.12]
but for the convenience of the reader, we give a sketch.

That
\Proj_{R}\simeq\coprod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\BGL_{n,R}
, where
\BGL_{n,R}
denotes the stack of finite projective modules of rank
n
, is clear. So it suffices to show that
\BGL_{R,n}
is a
1
-geometric smooth stack. It is enough to show that
\GL_{n,R}\rightarrow\Spec(R)
is
0
-geometric smooth. Then
\GL_{n,R}\rightarrow\Spec(R)
defines a
0
-Segal groupoid (see
[
TV08
, Def. 1.3.4.1]
) and
\BGL_{R,n}
is
1
-geometric (see
[
TV08
, Prop. 1.3.4.2]
). That
\BGL_{n,R}
is smooth follows from the fact that the natural morphism
\Spec(R)\rightarrow\BGL_{n,R}
gives a
1
-atlas.

The claim about
\GL_{n,R}
follows in the following way. The stack
\GL_{n,R}
is equivalent to the stack classifying automorphisms of
R^{n}
, i.e.
\GL_{n,R}(A)\simeq\Equiv_{A}(A^{n})
, for
A\in\textup{AR}_{R}
. But by Lemma
4.4
this is a
0
-geometric open substack of
F_{R^{n^{2}}}\simeq\Spec(\Sym_{R}(R^{n^{2}}))
, which is a
(-1)
-geometric smooth stack.

Alternatively, one could follow
[
TV08
, Prop. 1.3.7.10]
and show directly that the inclusion
\iota\colon\GL_{n,R}\hookrightarrow F_{R^{n^{2}}}
is representable and étale by showing that for any point
x\in F_{R^{n^{2}}}(A)
, we have
\iota^{-1}(x)\simeq\Spec(A[\det(x)^{-1}])
. In particular, we see in this way that
\GL_{n,R}
is representable by an affine derived scheme. This also shows that
\BGL_{n,R}
has an affine diagonal, since
\Spec(R)\times_{\Spec(R)}\Spec(R)\rightarrow\BGL_{n,R}\times_{\Spec(R)}\BGL_{n,R}
is a
1
-atlas by the above and so, we have a pullback diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL_{n,R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec(R)\simeq\Spec(R)\times_{\Spec(R)}\Spec(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\BGL_{n,R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\BGL_{n,R}\times_{\Spec(R)}\BGL_{n,R},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}

which shows that the diagonal is affine, since this can be tested after passing to a cover by affines (see
[
TV08
, Lem. 1.3.2.8]
).
∎

Remark 4.13
.

For the proof of the next theorem, we want to remark some generalities about pullbacks of Kan complexes.

Let
X,Y
be Kan complexes, i.e. elements in
\mathbb{S}
, and assume we have a morphism
X\rightarrow\Fun(\partial\Delta^{1},Y)
in
\mathbb{S}
. We want to compute the following pullback in
\mathbb{S}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\Delta^{1},Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\partial\Delta^{1},Y),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

where
i
is given by the restriction.
In general this is a pullback in the
\infty
-categorical sense, which can also be computed on the level of model categories via the homotopy pullback (recall that
\mathbb{S}
is the
\infty
-category associated to the model category of simplicial sets with the usual model structure (weak equivalences are given by weak equivalences of the underlying Kan complexes and fibrations are Kan fibrations)). We claim that the homotopy pullback of the underlying Kan complexes is equivalent in
\mathbb{S}
to the ordinary pullback of simplicial sets if
i
is a Kan fibration (i.e.
i
is a fibration in the model category of simplicial sets and since each simplicial set involved is already a Kan complex they are per definition fibrant).

Indeed, this is a classical result and remarked in
[
Lur09
, Rem. A.2.4.5]
but we will shortly sketch the idea behind it. Let
{\bf A}
be a combinatorial model category (e.g. the category of simplicial sets with the model structure explained above) and let
I
be the diagram category given by three objects
\{0,1,2\}
together with morphisms
0\rightarrow 2
,
1\rightarrow 2
and identities. One can attach the injective model structure onto the functor category
\Fun(I,{\bf A})
by defining weak equivalences to be pointwise weak equivalences and defining cofibrations also pointwise (the fibration are then given by certain lifting properties, which we will not discuss on detail). The homotopy limit is defined as the right Quillen adjoint of the constant functor
{\bf A}\rightarrow\Fun(I,{\bf A})
, which is weakly equivalent to the the limit of a
fibrant
diagram in
\Fun(I,{\bf A})
, i.e. for a diagram
a\rightarrow c\leftarrow b
, the homotopy limit is defined as an object that is weakly equivalent to ordinary limit of
a^{\prime}\rightarrow c^{\prime}\leftarrow b^{\prime}
, where
a^{\prime}\rightarrow c^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}\rightarrow c^{\prime}
are fibrations,
c^{\prime}
is fibrant and we have a commutative diagram of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b^{\prime},}

where the vertical arrows are weak equivalences (this is analogous to the theory of homotopy pushouts, which can be found in
[
Lur09
, §A.2.4]
). Now let
I_{R}
be the full subcategory of
I
generated by the elements
0
and
2
and define
I_{L}
as the full subcategory of
I
generated by the elements
1
and
2
. The tuple
(I_{L},I_{R})
makes
I
into a Reedy category on
I
(see
[
Lur09
, §A.2.9]
for more on Reedy categories). As explained in
[
Lur09
, Proposition A.2.9.19]
there is a model structure on
\Fun(I,{\bf A})
corresponding to the Reedy structure on
I
called the Reedy model structure. Important for us is that a diagram
a\rightarrow c\leftarrow b
is fibrant for the Reedy model structure if
a,b
are fibrant and either
a\rightarrow c
or
b\rightarrow c
is a fibration. Further, as remarked in
[
Lur09
, Rem. A.2.9.23]
the Reedy model structure and the injective model structure are Quillen equivalent via the identity functor. Therefore, if we have a fibrant diagram with respect to the Reedy model structure, then the homotopy limit is per definition weak equivalent to the ordinary limit in
{\bf A}
. Since the homotopy limit in this case is the homotopy pullback, we are done.

But that
i
is a Kan fibration follows from
[
Lur21
, Cor. 3.1.3.3]
and hence we can compute the above pullback in the
\infty
-category
\mathbb{S}
via the limit of the underlying simplicial sets.

Now assume that
Y
is an arbitrary
\infty
-category. We want to compute the following pullback in
\mathbb{S}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\Delta^{1},Y)^{\simeq}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\partial\Delta^{1},Y)^{\simeq},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

where
i
is naturally given by applying the functor
(-)^{\simeq}
to the restriction.
If
i
is a Kan fibration, then we can apply the argument above. In general it may not be clear if
i
is a Kan fibration. But in this case, we have that the natural morphism
F\colon\Fun(\Delta^{1},Y)\rightarrow\Fun(\partial\Delta^{1},Y)
is an isofibration of
\infty
-categories (see
[
Lur21
, 01F3]
), meaning that it is an inner fibration on the level of homotopy categories, we have the following property: if
x\in h\Fun(\Delta^{1},Y)
and we have an isomorphism
u^{\prime}\colon y\xrightarrow{\sim}F(x)
then, there exists a
x^{\prime}\in h\Fun(\Delta^{1},Y)
with an isomorphism
u\colon x^{\prime}\rightarrow x
such that
F(u)=u^{\prime}
. In particular,
[
Lur21
, Prop. 4.4.3.7]
implies that
i
is a Kan fibration.

Theorem 4.14
.

\displaystyle\textup{Perf}_{R}\colon\textup{AR}_{R}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{S}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto(\MMod_{A}^{\textup{perf}})^{\simeq}

is locally geometric and locally of finite presentation.

To be more specific, we can write
\textup{Perf}_{R}=\mathop{\textup{colim}}_{a\leq b}\textup{Perf}_{R}^{[a,b]}
, where
\textup{Perf}_{R}^{[a,b]}

The proof in the model categorical setting can be found in
[

and in the spectral setting in
[

, Thm. 5.6]

We show that

is

-geometric, where

.

For

(see Lemma

), which is

-geometric and locally of finite presentation by Lemma

Now let

and assume

n
-geometric and locally of finite presentation. Let

be defined via the pullback diagram of derived stacks

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\Delta^{1},\MMod^{\perf})^{\simeq}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Perf}^{[a+1,b]}\times_{R}\textup{Perf}^{[a+1,a+1]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(\partial\Delta^{1},\MMod^{\perf})^{\simeq}.}

Let

By sending a morphism to its fiber, we get a morphism of derived stacks

.
Using Proposition
3.39

4.7
, we see that

-geometric, so it suffices to show that

is also smooth and an effective epimorphism.

That it is an effective epimorphism follows from Lemma

. To check smoothness let
\Spec(A)\rightarrow\textup{Perf}^{[a,b]}
be a morphism classified by a perfect
A
-module
P
with Tor-amplitude in

A
-algebra

, consists of morphisms of perfect

-modules

, where

Indeed, let

A simple derivation of the integrals of products of Legendre polynomials with logarithmic weight
Sebastian Schmutzhard-Höfler

Abstract
. We explore integrals of products of Legendre polynomials with a logarithmic weight function. More precisely, for Legendre polynomials P m P_{m} and P n P_{n} of orders m m and n n , respectively, we provide simple derivations of the integrals ∫ 0 1 P n ​ ( 2 ​ x − 1 ) ​ P m ​ ( 2 ​ x − 1 ) ​ log ⁡ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x . \displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)P_{m}(2x-1)\log(x)dx.



1.
Introduction

In this short note we, compute the integrals of
products of Legendre polynomials with logarithmic
weight. Specifically, we are interested in

\displaystyle N_{n,m}:=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)P_{m}(2x-1)\log(x)dx

for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The Legendre polynomials
P_{0},P_{1},P_{2}\ldots,
are defined recursively
(
[
1
]
,
22.7.10
). The initial polynomials are given by

\displaystyle P_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle P_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=x.

For
n\geqslant 1
the polynomials
P_{n}
are defined by the
three-term-recurrence

(1)

\displaystyle(n+1)P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=(2n+1)xP_{n}(x)-nP_{n-1}(x).

In this work, we derive for
n\neq m
,

(2)

\displaystyle N_{n,m}=\frac{(-1)^{n+m+1}}{|n-m|(n+m+1)},

and for
m=n

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2n+1)N_{n,n}&=(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}-\frac{2}{(2n-1)2n(2n+1)}\\
&=-1-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2j-1)2j(2j+1)}.\end{split}

The solution for the special case
m=0
and
n>0
is obtained in

[
2
]
, and given by

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)\log(x)dx=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n(n+1)}.

This has been generalized to integrals of single Jacobi
polynomials with various weight functions, see for example

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
. In
[
6
]
[(4.6)],integrals of products of
Legendre polynomials with logarithmic weights are represented by finite sums involving
the Gamma function and the Digamma function. The result (
2
)
can be found in
[
1
]
[8.14.8,8.14.10] as integrals of
products of Legendre functions of the first and the second kind. On
the other hand
N_{n,n}
is given in
[
1
]
[8.14.9] via the
derivative of the Digamma function. Albeit being a special case and
easily derivable thereof, the simple representation of
(
3
) seems to be new. Moreover, the elementary derivations of (
2
) and
(
3
) seem, to our surprise and the best of our knowledge,
to be missing in the literature.

2.
Main results

We give rigorous derivations of the values of the integrals

\displaystyle N_{n,m}:=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)P_{m}(2x-1)\log(x)dx

for arbitrary
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1
.

For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n>m
,

(4)

\displaystyle N_{n,m}=\frac{(-1)^{n+m+1}}{(n-m)(n+m+1)}.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We prove the result by induction.

Induction base case

The base case for
m=0
and
n>m
is, as discussed above, proven
in
[
2
]
:

(5)

\displaystyle N_{n,0}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)\log(x)dx=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n(n+1)}.

For
m=1
and
n>m
, we observe that
P_{1}(2x-1)=2x-1
. It
follows from Equation
(
1
) that for
n\geqslant 1

(6)

\displaystyle P_{n}(2x-1)(2x-1)=\frac{n+1}{2n+1}P_{n+1}(2x-1)+\frac{n}{2n+1}P_{n-1}(2x-1).

Substituting (
6
) into the definition of
N_{n,1}
gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{n,1}&:=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)(2x-1)\log(x)dx\\
&=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{n+1}{2n+1}P_{n+1}(2x-1)+\frac{n}{2n+1}P_{n-1}(2x-1)\right]\log(x)dx\\
&=\frac{n+1}{2n+1}\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n+1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx+\frac{n}{2n+1}\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n-1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx\\
&=\frac{n+1}{2n+1}N_{n+1,0}+\frac{n}{2n+1}N_{n-1,0}.\end{split}

Substituting the results for

N_{n+1,0}
and
N_{n-1,0}
, see Equation (
5
), leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{n,1}&=\frac{n+1}{2n+1}\frac{(-1)^{n+2}}{(n+1)(n+2)}+\frac{n}{2n+1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n-1)n}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n+2}}{2n+1}\left[\frac{1}{n+2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\right]=\frac{(-1)^{n+2}}{2n+1}\frac{2n+1}{(n+2)(n-1)}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n+2}}{(n+2)(n-1)},\end{split}

which is the base case for
m=1
.

Induction hypothesis

We assume the result to be valid for any
\tilde{m}\in\mathbb{N}

with
\tilde{m}<m
. Specifically, we assume

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{n,m-1}&=\frac{(-1)^{n+m}}{(n-m+1)(n+m)}\textnormal{ for }n>m-1\textnormal{ and }\\
N_{n,m-2}&=\frac{(-1)^{n+m-1}}{(n-m+2)(n+m-1)}\textnormal{ for
}n>m-2.\end{split}

Induction step

It follows from Equation (
1
) that for
m>1

(8)

\displaystyle mP_{m}(2x-1)=(2m-1)(2x-1)P_{m-1}(2x-1)-(m-1)P_{m-2}(2x-1).

Multiplying
N_{n,m}
by
m
and substituting
(
8
) yields

\displaystyle mN_{n,m}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)mP_{m}(2x-1)\log(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)(2m-1)(2x-1)P_{m-1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)(m-1)P_{m-2}(2x-1)\log(x)dx,

which results in

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&mN_{n,m}=\\
&(2m-1)\int\limits_{0}^{1}(2x-1)P_{n}(2x-1)P_{m-1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx-(m-1)N_{n,m-2},\end{split}

where we made use of the definition of
N_{n,m-2}
.
It follows from Equation (
1
) that for
n\geqslant 1

(10)

\displaystyle(2n+1)(2x-1)P_{n}(2x-1)=(n+1)P_{n+1}(2x-1)+nP_{n-1}(2x-1)

Multiplying Equation (
9
) by
(2n+1)
and subsituting
(
10
) leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(2n+1)mN_{n,m}=\\
&(2m-1)\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left[(n+1)P_{n+1}(2x-1)+nP_{n-1}(2x-1)\right]P_{m-1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx\\
&-(2n+1)(m-1)N_{n,m-2}.\end{split}

Making use of the definition of
N_{n+1,m-1}
and
N_{n-1,m-1}
gives us

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(2n+1)mN_{n,m}=\\
&(2m-1)(n+1)N_{n+1,m-1}+(2m-1)nN_{n-1,m-1}-(2n+1)(m+1)N_{n,m-2}.\end{split}

The assumption that
n>m
implies that

\displaystyle n+1

\displaystyle>m-1,

\displaystyle n-1

\displaystyle>m-1\textnormal{ and }

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle>m-2,

hence the induction hypotheses (
7
) are valid for
N_{n+1,m-1}
,
N_{n-1,m-1}

and
N_{n,m-2}
. Substituting the respective results into Equation (
11
), we obtain

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2n+1)mN_{n,m}=&(2m-1)(n+1)\frac{(-1)^{n+m+1}}{(n-m+2)(n+m+1)}\\
&+(2m-1)n\frac{(-1)^{n+m-1}}{(n-m)(n+m-1)}\\
&-(2n+1)(m-1)\frac{(-1)^{n+m-1}}{(n-m+2)(n+m-1)}.\end{split}

Bringing the right hand side of (
12
) on a common denominator shows that

\displaystyle(2n+1)mN_{n,m}=(-1)^{n+m+1}\frac{(2n+1)m(n-m+2)(n+m-1)}{(n-m+2)(n+m+1)(n-m)(n+m-1)}.

Dividing by
(2n+1)m
and simplifying the fraction proves the result (
4
).
∎

Corollary 1
.

For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\neq m
,

\displaystyle N_{n,m}=\frac{(-1)^{n+m+1}}{|n-m|(n+m+1)}.

Proof.

The case of
n>m
is covered by Theorem
1
.
In the case of
m>n
, we obtain the result of the Corollary by
observing that
N_{n,m}=N_{m,n}
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

(13)

\displaystyle N_{0,0}

\displaystyle=-1,

(14)

\displaystyle 3N_{1,1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{3},

and for
n>1
,

\displaystyle(2n+1)N_{n,n}

\displaystyle=(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}-\frac{2}{(2n-1)2n(2n+1)}

\displaystyle=-1-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2j-1)2j(2j+1)}.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Equations (
13
) and (
14
) can be verified by using

P_{0}(2x-1)=1
and
P_{1}(2x-1)=2x-1
and by explicitly computing the integrals.
For arbitrary
n>1
, it follows from Equation (
1
)
that for
n>1

(15)

\displaystyle P_{n}(2x-1)=\frac{2n-1}{n}(2x-1)P_{n-1}(2x-1)-\frac{n-1}{n}P_{n-2}(2x-1).

Replacing one of the factors
P_{n}(2x-1)
in the defintion of
N_{n,n}
by
(
15
) and using
the definition of
N_{n,n-2}
yields

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{n,n}&=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)P_{n}(2x-1)\log(x)dx\\
&=\int\limits_{0}^{1}P_{n}(2x-1)\frac{2n-1}{n}(2x-1)P_{n-1}(2x-1)\log(x)dx-\frac{n-1}{n}N_{n,n-2}.\end{split}

It follows from Equation (
1
) that for
n\geqslant 1

(17)

\displaystyle(2n+1)(2x-1)P_{n}(2x-1)=(n+1)P_{n+1}(2x-1)+nP_{n-1}(2x-1).

By multiplication of Equation (
16
) with (2n+1) and substituting (
17
)
we obtain

\displaystyle(2n+1)N_{n,n}=

\displaystyle(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}+\frac{2n-1}{n}(n+1)N_{n+1,n-1}-(2n+1)\frac{n-1}{n}N_{n,n-2}

where we made use of the definition of
N_{n+1,n-1}
and

N_{n,n-2}
. Using Theorem
1
results in

\displaystyle(2n+1)N_{n,n}

\displaystyle=(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}-\frac{(2n-1)(n+1)}{2n(2n+1)}+\frac{(2n+1)(n-1)}{2n(2n-1)}=

\displaystyle(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}+\frac{-(2n-1)^{2}(n+1)+(2n+1)^{2}(n-1)}{(2n-1)2n(2n+1)}

which can be easily simplified to

\displaystyle(2n+1)N_{n,n}

\displaystyle=(2n-1)N_{n-1,n-1}-\frac{2}{(2n-1)2n(2n+1)}.

Finally, recursively substituting the last equation yields, together
with the result for
N_{0,0}
, that

\displaystyle(2n+1)N_{n,n}=-1-2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2j-1)2j(2j+1)},

which concludes the proof.

∎

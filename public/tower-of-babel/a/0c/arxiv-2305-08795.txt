dg-Hecke duality and tensor products
Peter Schneider, Claus Sorensen

Abstract
We continue our study of the monoidal category D ⁡ ( G ) D(G) begun in [ SS ] . At the level of cohomology we transfer the duality functor R ​ Hom ¯ ​ ( − , k ) R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(-,k) to the derived category of dg-modules D ⁡ ( H U ∙ ) D(H_{U}^{\bullet}) . In the process we develop a more general and streamlined approach to the anti-involution 𝒥 \mathcal{J} from [ OS ] . We also verify that the tensor product on D ⁡ ( G ) D(G) corresponds to an operadic tensor product on the dg-side (cf [ KM ] ). This uses a result of Schnürer on dg-categories with a model structure.

means

E^{*}

is the representation

The

The

As we have just seen in Corollary

We consider the Yoneda algebra

as a bimodule over itself. Observe that

Proposition 2.19
.

For any
\tau\in E^{s}
we have the identities:

i.

\langle f\cdot(1\otimes\tau),e\rangle=\langle f,\tau\cdot e\rangle
;

ii.

\langle f\cdot(\tau\otimes 1),e\rangle=(-1)^{s(d-i-s)}\cdot\langle f,e\cdot(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})(\tau)\rangle
;

for all
f\in\operatorname{Ext}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{i}(\mathbf{X}_{U}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{X}_{U},k)
and
e\in E^{d-i-s}
.

Proof.

The first identity in
2.19
is almost immediate from the definitions:

\displaystyle\langle f\cdot(1\otimes\tau),e\rangle

\displaystyle=f\circ(1\otimes\tau)\circ(1\otimes e)

\displaystyle=f\circ(1\otimes\tau\circ e)

\displaystyle=\langle f,\tau\cdot e\rangle.

For legibility we abuse notation here and suppress various shifts. For instance
\tau
is a morphism

\mathbf{X}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathbf{X}_{U}[s]
in
D(G)
, and similarly for
e
, so
\tau\circ e
really means
the composition
\tau[d-i-s]\circ e
in
D(G)
. We will adopt this convention of suppressing shifts when they are obvious from the context.

To verify the second identity we relate the two
E^{*}
-module structures via the swap
\varsigma
. Note that

\displaystyle\varsigma^{*}\big(f\cdot(1\otimes\tau)\big)

\displaystyle=f\circ(1\otimes\tau)\circ\varsigma

\displaystyle=f\circ\varsigma\circ(\tau\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\varsigma^{*}(f)\cdot(\tau\otimes 1).

As we observed in Remark
2.16
the twist
\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G}
is an anti-involution of
E^{*}
. Therefore,
after introducing the auxiliary
\tilde{f}=\varsigma^{*}(f)
the previous computation gives

\displaystyle\langle f\cdot(\tau\otimes 1),e\rangle

\displaystyle=f\circ(\tau\otimes 1)\otimes(1\otimes e)

\displaystyle=\varsigma^{*}\big(\tilde{f}\cdot(1\otimes\tau)\big)\circ(1\otimes e)

\displaystyle=\langle\varsigma^{*}\big(\tilde{f}\cdot(1\otimes\tau)\big),e\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{f}\cdot(1\otimes\tau),(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{f},\tau\cdot(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})\big(\tau\cdot(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})(e)\big)\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\deg(e)\deg(\tau)}\cdot\langle f,e\cdot(\mathcal{J}\otimes\chi_{G})(\tau)\rangle

In the fourth and sixth equalities we used Corollary
2.18

∎

\displaystyle=q_{H}\bigg(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}(\mathbf{p}H(V^{\bullet})\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}H(W^{\bullet}))\bigg)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow q_{H}\bigg(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}V^{\bullet})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}W^{\bullet})\bigg)

which we compose with the following map: Given a pair of morphisms
\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}V^{\bullet})
and

\beta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}W^{\bullet})
along with a
c\in H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)
we form
\alpha\otimes\beta
and precompose

\underline{i}(\alpha\otimes\beta)
with
c
in the dg-category
\underline{C}(G)
. This gives a map

q_{H}\bigg(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}V^{\bullet})\otimes_{k}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}W^{\bullet})\bigg)\longrightarrow H(V^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}W^{\bullet})

by sending
c\otimes\alpha\otimes\beta\mapsto\underline{i}(\alpha\otimes\beta)\circ c
. We need to show the resulting map

\psi_{V^{\bullet},W^{\bullet}}:H(V^{\bullet})\boxtimes H(W^{\bullet})\longrightarrow H(V^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}W^{\bullet})

is an isomorphism in
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
. We verify this in increasing generality starting from the case
V^{\bullet}=W^{\bullet}=\mathbf{X}_{U}
. Let us take

\mathcal{I}^{\bullet}=\mathbf{i}\mathbf{X}_{U}
for simplicity (in general they are homotopy equivalent). Then

\mathbf{p}H(\mathbf{X}_{U})=(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H})\big(\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(G)}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{\bullet},\mathbf{i}\mathbf{X}_{U})\big)=(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H})(H_{U}^{\bullet})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H_{U}^{\bullet}

since
H_{U}^{\bullet}
is obviously homotopically projective. Therefore
\psi_{\mathbf{X}_{U},\mathbf{X}_{U}}
is a composition of isomorphisms

H(\mathbf{X}_{U})\boxtimes H(\mathbf{X}_{U})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}q_{H}(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2))\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H(\mathbf{X}_{U}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{X}_{U}).

As an intermediate step we introduce the strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
D(G)
whose objects are the
V^{\bullet}
for which
\psi_{V^{\bullet},\mathbf{X}_{U}}
is an isomorphism. We have just verified that
\mathbf{X}_{U}
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
. Since
H
and
(-)\boxtimes H(\mathbf{X}_{U})
are triangulated functors the 2-out-of-3 property

[
Nee
, Prop. 1.1.20]
shows
\mathcal{C}
is a triangulated subcategory. By Lemma
3.8
below
\mathcal{C}
is furthermore closed under all coproducts. Therefore

\mathcal{C}=D(G)
by
[
DGA
, Prop. 6]
.

Finally fix an arbitrary
V^{\bullet}
and consider the strictly full subcategory
\mathcal{D}_{V^{\bullet}}
of
D(G)
with objects
W^{\bullet}
for which

\psi_{V^{\bullet},W^{\bullet}}
is an isomorphism. The above intermediate step shows
\mathbf{X}_{U}
is an object of
\mathcal{D}_{V^{\bullet}}
, and the same reasoning allows us to conclude
\mathcal{D}_{V^{\bullet}}=D(G)
as desired.
∎

The following observation was used in the previous proof.

Lemma 3.8
.

The category
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
has arbitrary coproducts. Let
(M_{i}^{\bullet})_{i\in I}
be a collection of objects from
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
. Then

(i)

\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{p}(M_{i}^{\bullet})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{p}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\bullet})
;

(ii)

There are functorial isomorphisms

\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_{i}^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\bullet})\boxtimes N^{\bullet};

(iii)

The functor
H
and its quasi-inverse
T
preserve arbitrary coproducts.

Proof.

By
[
DGA
, Rem. 2]
the category
D(G)
has arbitrary coproducts, and therefore so does the equivalent category
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
. As a left adjoint the functor
\mathbf{p}
respects arbitrary coproducts so that we have
\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{p}(M_{i}^{\bullet})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{p}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\bullet})
where the source is a direct sum of complexes. Applying
q_{H}
we deduce that
\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\bullet}
is isomorphic to
q_{H}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{p}(M_{i}^{\bullet}))
. As a result

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_{i}^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq q_{H}\bigg(\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbf{p}(M_{i}^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet})\bigg)

\displaystyle=q_{H}\bigg(\bigoplus_{i\in I}(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H})(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}(\mathbf{p}M_{i}^{\bullet}\boxtimes\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}))\bigg)

\displaystyle\simeq q_{H}\bigg(\bigoplus_{i\in I}H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}(\mathbf{p}M_{i}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet})\bigg)

\displaystyle\simeq(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}^{\bullet})\boxtimes N^{\bullet}

where the third isomorphism comes from the quasi-isomorphisms
(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H})(X^{\bullet})\longrightarrow X^{\bullet}
with

X^{\bullet}=H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}(\mathbf{p}M_{i}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet})
. Part (iii) is obvious.
∎

The internal
\operatorname{Hom}

In this section we transfer
R\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
to
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M^{\bullet}
and
N^{\bullet}
be objects of
D(H_{U}^{\bullet})
. Then

\operatorname{Hom}^{\boxtimes}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})=q_{H}\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\bigg(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\bigg],\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg).

This definition takes a bit of unwinding:

•

The tensor product
H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M
is with respect to the right
H_{U}^{\bullet}
-module structure on
H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)
arising from the
second
factor in
H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}
.

•

When forming
\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}
(introduced in
[
BL
, 10.8]
) we view
\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\big[\cdots\big]
as a left
H_{U}^{\bullet}
-module through the left
action of
H_{U}^{\bullet}
on
H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)
.

•

The space
\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}(\cdots)
on the right-hand side in
4.1
becomes a left
H_{U}^{\bullet}
-module via the right action of
H_{U}^{\bullet}
on

H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)
via the
first
factor in
H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}
.

Remark 4.2
.

At least morally
\boxtimes
is the derived tensor product
H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}^{L}M^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}N^{\bullet}
. Similarly we think of

\operatorname{Hom}^{\boxtimes}
as
R\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}^{L}M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})
. We have adopted the notation
\boxtimes
and

\operatorname{Hom}^{\boxtimes}
from
[
KM
, Part V, Df. 1.1, Df. 1.2]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

There are functorial isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet},R^{\bullet})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet},\operatorname{Hom}^{\boxtimes}(N^{\bullet},R^{\bullet})).

Proof.

First observe that the left-hand side can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet},R^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet},(q_{H}\circ\mathbf{p})(R^{\bullet}))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{K(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(\mathbf{p}(M^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet}),\mathbf{p}R^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{K(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\big[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\big]),\mathbf{p}R^{\bullet}).

Next note that there is a canonical isomorphism of left
H_{U}^{\bullet}
-modules

H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\big(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\big)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}.

(The initial tensor product in the target is via the second factor of
H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}
. The last tensor product is via the first factor.) By
[
BL
, Cor. 10.12.4.4]

\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}
is homotopically flat, so

\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg]\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\longrightarrow\big(H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\big)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}

is a homotopically projective resolution of the right-hand side. We thus have a unique isomorphism in
K(H_{U}^{\bullet})
between the two resolutions

\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg]\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\big[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\big].

This allows us to continue our calculation:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet}\boxtimes N^{\bullet},R^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{K(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg]\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet},\mathbf{p}R^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{K(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(\mathbf{p}M^{\bullet},\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\big(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg],\mathbf{p}R^{\bullet}\big))

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet},q_{H}\operatorname{Hom}_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\big(\mathbf{p}\circ q_{H}\bigg[H_{U}^{\bullet}(2)\otimes_{H_{U}^{\bullet}}\mathbf{p}N^{\bullet}\bigg],\mathbf{p}R^{\bullet}\big))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H_{U}^{\bullet})}(M^{\bullet},\operatorname{Hom}^{\boxtimes}(N^{\bullet},R^{\bullet}))

by the usual tensor-hom adjunction for dg-modules.
∎

Abstract
. Let D D be a reduced effective strict normal crossing divisor on a smooth complex variety X X , and let 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} be the associated root stack over ℂ \mathbb{C} . Suppose that X X admits an anti-holomorphic involution (real structure) that keeps D D invariant. We show that the root stack 𝔛 \mathfrak{X} naturally admits a real structure compatible with X X . We also establish an equivalence of categories between the category of real logarithmic connections on this root stack and the category of real parabolic connections on X X .

1.
Introduction

Parabolic vector bundles were introduced by Mehta and Seshadri
[
MS80
]

on a compact Riemann surface
X
together with a set
D
of marked points.
In this initial formulation, a parabolic bundle is a vector bundle together
with the data of a flag for each of its fibers over the points of
D
,
together with a set of real numbers in
[0,1)
, called weights.
The celebrated Mehta-Seshadri theorem shows a one-to-one correspondence
between polystable parabolic vector bundles on
X
of parabolic degree zero
and the unitary representations of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X\setminus D)

of the punctured Riemann surface. Later, Simpson in
[
Sim90
]
reformulated
and extended the definition of parabolic bundles to the case of schemes
X

with a normal crossings divisor
D
. When the weights are all rational, and
therefore can be assumed to be in
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
r
,
this amounts to a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of finite rank, together with
a filtration

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{1}{r}}\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{2}{r}}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{E}_{\frac{r-1}{r}}\supset\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}(-D)

Since their introduction, parabolic bundles, parabolic connections and their
moduli spaces have been an active area of research, and have been used in a
number of important applications. Even though most of the progress has been
in the case of curves, more recently, parabolic connections have made appearance
in the work of Donagi and Pantev on Geometric Langlands conjecture using Simpson’s
non-abelian Hodge theory
[
DP19
]
.

When the weights are rational, it is known that parabolic bundles can be
interpreted equivalently as equivariant bundles on a suitable ramified
Galois cover
[
Bis97
]
, and later, a more intrinsic interpretation was
found using vector bundles on certain algebraic stacks associated to
(X,D)
,
namely the stack of roots
[
Bor07
]
. More precisely, there is a Fourier-like
correspondence between parabolic vector bundles on a scheme and ordinary vector
bundles on certain stack of roots. This naturally raised the question of
understanding the parabolic connections on smooth varieties through such
Fourier-like correspondence over root stacks. This has been shown to be
true over curves
[
BMW12
,
LSS13
]
, and very recently over higher
dimensions
[
BL23
]
.

Here our aim is to study parabolic connections and similar correspondences
in the case when the variety
X
is over complex numbers and possesses a
real structure, meaning that there is an anti-holomorphic involution on
X
,
together with a given normal crossings divisor
D
invariant under the involution.
Some progress in this topic has been made for curves
[
Amr14a
,
Amr14b
]
using
suitable ramified Galois covers, but unfortunately it is unclear how to extend
it for higher dimensions. Let
r
be an integer. To the data
(r,D)
, we can
associate a stack of
r
-th roots of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{X}
.
It comes with a natural morphism
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow X
.
We show that, if
X
possesses a real structure
\sigma
, the stack
\mathfrak{X}

also possess a real structure
\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}}
compatible with that of
X
.
This is done in Proposition
.
Next, we define the notions of real parabolic bundle and real parabolic connections,
and prove the real parabolic connections on
X
(meaning those parabolic connections
which are compatible with the real structure) correspond to real logarithmic connections
on these root stacks with their induced real structure. Moreover, under this correspondence,
the strongly parabolic connections (c.f. Definition
5.1.7
)
on
X
correspond to the real holomorphic connections on the root stack.

We state our main results below.
Fix an integer
r
, and a reduced effective divisor
D
satisfying
\sigma^{*}(D)=D
.

Theorem 1.0.1

(Theorem
4.2.2
)
.

There is an equivalence between the category of real parabolic vector bundles on

(X,\sigma)
having weights in
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
and the category of real vector
bundles on
(\mathfrak{X},\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}})
.

If we moreover assume that
D
is a simple normal crossings divisor, then the pull-back
of
D
under the morphism
\pi
defines a divisor
\mathfrak{D}
on
\mathfrak{X}
satisfying

\pi^{*}(D)=r\cdot\mathfrak{D}
. In such cases, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.0.2

(Theorem
5.2.1
)
.

There is an equivalence between the category of real logarithmic connections
on the corresponding
r
-th root stack
(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{D})
, and the category of
real parabolic connections on
(X,D)
having weights in
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
.
Moreover, under this correspondence, the category of real holomorphic connections
on
\mathfrak{X}
is equivalent to the category of real strongly parabolic connections on

(X,D)
having weights in
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}
.

In the final section, we revisit the case when
X
is a curve, and give a different
proof for Theorem
5.2.1

from a different approach using suitable ramified Galois covers on
X
.

Real Structures on Root Stacks

3.1.
Root stack

By an abuse of notation, we denote a scheme and its associated stack
by the same symbol, if no confusion arises.
A
generalized Cartier divisor
on a scheme
Y
is a tuple
(M,s)
,
where
M
is an invertible sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules on
Y
and
s\in\Gamma(Y,M)

Given a generalized Cartier divisor
(L,s)
on
X
and a positive integer
r
,
let
\mathfrak{X}_{(L,\,s,\,r)}
be the stack
whose objects are given by tuples
\left(f:U\to X,M,\phi,t\right)
, where

•

f:U\to X
is a morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-schemes,

•

M
is an invertible sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules on
U
,

•

t\in\Gamma(U,M)
, and

•

\phi:M^{\otimes r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}f^{*}L

is an
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module isomorphism such that
\phi(t^{\otimes r})=f^{*}s
.

A morphism from

\left(f:U\to X,M,\phi,t\right)
to
\left(f^{\prime}:U^{\prime}\to X,M^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},t^{\prime}\right)

in
\mathfrak{X}_{(L,\,s,\,r)}
is given by a pair
(g,\psi)
, where

•

g:U\to U^{\prime}
is a morphism of
X

•

\psi:M\to g^{*}M^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-module isomorphism such that
\psi(t)=g^{*}(t^{\prime})
.

There is a natural morphism of stacks
\pi:{\mathfrak{X}}_{(L,\,s,\,r)}\to X
that sends
the object
\left(f:U\to X,M,\phi,t\right)
of
\mathfrak{X}_{(L,\,s,\,r)}

to the
X
-scheme
f:U\to X
, and a morphism
(g,\psi)
, as above,
to the morphism of
X
-schemes
g:U\to U^{\prime}
.
Note that there is a natural morphism

(arising from the structure morphism of

),
whose composition with
\pi
makes
\mathfrak{X}_{(L,\,s,\,r)}
into a
\mathbb{C}
-stack.
The stack
\mathfrak{X}_{(L,s,r)}
is known as the
stack of
r
-th roots of
(L,s)
over
X
,
and it is a Deligne-Mumford stack

We now construct a real structure on a root stack.
Let
D
be a strict normal crossing divisor on
X
,
meaning that
D
is a reduced effective divisor on
X
such that all of its
irreducible components are smooth and they intersects each other transversally.
Recall that the invertible sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is a subsheaf of
\mathcal{K}_{X}
,
where
\mathcal{K}_{X}
is the constant sheaf given by
K(X)
, the function field of
X
.
The constant function
1\in K(X)
gives rise to a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
,
which we denote by
s_{D}
. Thus we get a generalized Cartier divisor
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),\,s_{D})

on
X
. Note that
D
is the divisor of zeroes of
s_{D}
.

We say that
D
is a
real
divisor if it is invariant under
\sigma
in the sense that

\sigma(D)=D
. Since
\sigma\circ\sigma=\Id_{X}
, it follows that
D
is real if and only if

\sigma^{*}(D):=\sigma^{-1}(D)=D
. Henceforth we assume that
D
is real.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

With the above notations, the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
admits a real structure
for which
s_{D}
is a real section if and only if
D
is a real divisor.

Proof.

Let
L:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.
Since
\sigma
is an involution of
X
, we have a canonical

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear isomorphism
\sigma^{*}\sigma_{*}L\simeq L
.
Applying
\sigma^{*}
on both sides, we have an isomorphism

(3.1.2)

p:\sigma_{*}L\longrightarrow\sigma^{*}L.

The condition
\sigma(D)=D
gives rise to an isomorphism

(3.1.3)

h:L\longrightarrow\sigma^{*}L.

Thus

\sigma_{L}:=p^{-1}\circ\,h:L\longrightarrow\sigma_{*}L

is a real structure on
L
.
Note that the canonical section
s_{D}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))

remains fixed under
\sigma_{L}
, and hence is a real section of
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.
∎

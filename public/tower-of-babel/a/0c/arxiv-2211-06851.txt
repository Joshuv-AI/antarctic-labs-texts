Abstract
.

A “Composition map” is constructed, leaning heavily on earlier work [Y. Fittouhi and A. Joseph, Parabolic adjoint action, Weierstrass sections and components of the nilfibre in type
A
, Indag Math. and Y. Fittouhi and A. Joseph, The canonical component of the nilfibre for parabolic adjoint action, Weierstrass sections in type
A
, preprint, Weizmann, 2021]. It defines a composition tableau which recovers the “canonical” Weierstrass section
e+V
described in the first paper above. Moreover
without reference to this earlier work
, it is then shown that
e+V
is indeed a Weierstrass section. This results in a huge simplification. Moreover one may read off from the composition tableau the “VS quadruplets” of the second of the above papers, thereby describing the “canonical component” of the nil-fibre in which
e
lies but does not of itself determine.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Notion of a Weierstrass Section

Let
P
be a connected algebraic group acting by morphisms on a vector space
\mathfrak{m}
(necessarily finite-dimensional). Let
I:=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{m}]^{P}
be the algebra of the
P
invariant functions on
\mathfrak{m}
. A Weierstrass section
e+V
for this action is a linear translate of a subspace
V
of
\mathfrak{m}
by an element
e\in\mathfrak{m}
such that the restriction of
I
to
e+V
induces an isomorphism of
I
onto
\mathbb{C}[V]
. It results in a “canonical form” for most
P
orbits in
\mathfrak{m}
. Of course for this
I
needs to be polynomial which is rather unusual and even this is no guarantee
[
8
, 11.4, Example 2]
.

Popov and Vinberg
[
11
]
summarized early work on this subject with
P
reductive. Notably they explain how it leads to Weierstrass canonical form for elliptic curves. Let
\mathscr{N}\subset\mathfrak{m}
denote the zero variety of the augmentation
I_{+}
. A tricky point here is to show that
\mathscr{N}
is irreducible
[
9
, 2.5]
, for which there are few techniques and can often fail
[
6
, 11.2.2]
,
[
3
, 5.4.1]
.

1.2.
Adapted Pairs

The “time-honoured” way to obtain a Weierstrass section is by constructing an adapted pair
(e,h)
with
e\in\mathscr{N}
and
h
a semisimple element of
\mathfrak{p}:=\text{Lie}\ P
such that
e
is an
h
eigenvector and
P.e
is dense in an irreducible component of
\mathscr{N}
, that is to say
e
is regular. Then
V
is taken to be an
h
stable complement of
\mathfrak{p}.e
in
\mathfrak{m}
. Finally a favourable comparison of degrees of generators and eigenvalues of
h
on V gives the required result. This was the point of view taken in the classical work of Kostant
[
10
]
for
P=G
, a simple Lie group. In this he appealed to the entire principal s-triple
(e,h,f)
. By a further coincidence
e
also generated an orbit of maximal dimension in
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}\ G
.

If
P
is not reductive, it may be necessary to cut down
P
slightly, for example to its derived group
P^{\prime}
, so that it admits no non-trivial semi-invariants and this will be assumed without further mention.

Having an adapted pair works rather well for biparabolic co-adjoint action
[
6
]
in type
A
and even outside type
A

[
2
]
. It can even lead (in a backhanded fashion - by improving bounds on
I

[
7
, 6.11]
) to showing that
I
is polynomial, calculations which have been taken further notably by Fauquant-Millet
[
2
]
.

A backhanded way of obtaining a Weierstrass section when no adapted pair exists is described in
[
8
, Sects. 8,9]
for
P
being the Borel
B
of a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Yet this may lead to “undesirable” properties of
B(e+V)

[
8
, 11.4, Example 3]
, which can also be expected if
e
is not regular.

1.3.
Adjoint Action

If
P
is not reductive, the invariants for co-adjoint and adjoint action can behave rather differently - see
[
3
, 3.2]
for example. Correspondingly one might expect differences in constructing Weierstrass sections.

For the adjoint action of a parabolic
P
on the Lie algebra of its nilradical
\mathfrak{m}
which is the case considered here, the method of adapted pairs fails miserably. For one thing an irreducible component of
\mathscr{N}
can fail to admit a dense
P
orbit
[
4
, 6.10.7]
. (We expect that if each column is lengthened by one, then
\mathscr{N}
becomes irreducible.) Thus
e
need not be regular and one may not be able to find a required semisimple element
h
-
[
4
, 3.2]
.

In the case of the adjoint action of a parabolic
P
acting on its nilradical
\mathfrak{m}
, in type
A
we gave a construction of a Weierstrass section
e+V
by a combinatorial construction
[
4
, 5.4]
. It was extremely complicated involving all sorts of exotic concepts: viz. Gating
[
4
, 4.2]
, Up-going linkage, Adjacency
[
5
, 5.4.3]
, Gathering of loose ends, Minimal distance criterion
[
5
, 5.4.7]
and so on. In this
e
was often far from regular.

In the first three fifths of this paper we show how our previous work, specifically
[
5
, 3.6]
, gives rise to a “composition tableau”. Then we show (Proposition
4.9
) how this tableau can be
directly constructed
by a method which is so easy (see
4.7
) that it could have been carried out by the elderly ladies who in the distant past inspected and collected commuters’ tickets in the London Underground (or indeed in the Paris metro). Then we show in the last two fifths of this paper that
independent of previous work
the resulting
e+V
is indeed a Weierstrass section. Yet we see no way that this could have been guessed (or even suspected) a priori. Moreover to our delight and astonishment many of the previous exotic concepts needed for our construction
[
4
, 5.4]
of a Weierstrass section, drop out automatically as if by magic. In brief our previous method had a certain internal logic, the present work has none (that we can see) except that it works.

We should emphasize that the present work by no means obviates the whole of
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
but mainly just
[
4
, 4.4]
,
[
4
, 5.4]
and
[
5
, 3.4,3.5]
. That is one good reason for needing to prove that the present work gives the same Weierstrass section even if the proof is rather wearisome.

A fundamental question is what our present construction means and how it can be wider applied (for example for the other components of the nilfibre and outside type
A
). At present we have no answer and it remains a very significant challenge.

1.4.
Summary of Contents

Let
n
be a positive integer and
M
the set of
n\times n
matrices. Those of determinant
1
form a multiplicative group isomorphic to
SL(n)
.

A parabolic subgroup (of
SL(n)
) will always be one containing the Borel subgroup
B
of upper triangular matrices of
M
.

Recall
[
5
, 1.3]
that for the action of a parabolic
P
on the nilradical of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
, there is a “canonical” Weierstrass section
e+V
.
Amazingly it is determined by a family of “composition maps”
[
5
, Lemma 3.6.8]
all of which have the same structure.

We show (Theorem
3.6
) that this structure is given rather simply in terms of the notion of a column staircase. This leads to a composition tableau (Sect.
4
) which determines
e+V
, but makes no particular reference to the column staircases and can be computed in a simple and independent manner (
4.7
-
4.9
) and that indeed
e+V
is a Weierstrass section (Theorem
5.8
and Section
5.9
).

Let
\mathscr{N}^{e}
be the irreducible component of
\mathscr{N}
described in
[
4
, Cor. 6.9.8]
as a
B
saturation set of an appropriate subspace of
\mathfrak{m}
. We remark that the vector space
E_{VS}\subset\mathscr{N}^{e}
, described in
[
4
, 6.10.4]
, is determined by the pair
(e,V)
and can be simply read off from the Composition Tableau - see
5.10
. Crucially
[
5
, Prop. 4.5.3]
it has the property that
\overline{PE_{VS}}=\mathscr{N}^{e}
, whilst the inclusion
\overline{Pe_{VS}}\subset\mathscr{N}^{e}
is generally strict. Worse still
\mathscr{N}^{e}
need not have a dense
P
orbit
[
4
, 6.10.7]
. We call
\mathscr{N}^{e}
the “canonical component” of
\mathscr{N}
. We expect that it will nearly always be the only component, for example if there are no degree
1
invariants.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Tableaux

Given
u\leq v
integers, set
[u,v]:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}|u\leq i\leq v\}
.

Let
\textbf{c}:=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{k})
be a composition of
n
, that is to say a set
c
of positive integers which sum to
n
.

Let
\mathscr{D}
be the diagram given by a set of columns
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
numbered from left to right with
C_{i}
of
0ptC_{i}
being
c_{i}
. In this each column is viewed as a vertical array of descending square boxes all starting from the same row
R_{1}
.

Set
[\mathscr{D}]=\cup_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}
and
|\mathscr{D}|=\max_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}
.

Let

\mathscr{T}
, be the tableau obtained from
\mathscr{D}
in which the integers
1,2,\ldots,n
are inserted sequentially in the boxes, first down the columns and then on going from left to right.

As in
[
4
, 2.3]
, let
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be the rows of
\mathscr{D}
labelled with positive integers increasing from top to bottom. For all
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, set
R^{i}:=\{R_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i}
.

A box is labelled as
b_{i,j}
if it occurs in the
i^{th}
row and
j^{th}
column of
\mathscr{D}
. Let
x_{i,j}
denote the corresponding co-ordinate vector in
M
.

We shall also label a box
b
of
\mathscr{D}
as
b(t)
if it carries the label
t
in
\mathscr{T}
.

Two boxes
b,b^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{D}
are said to be
adjacent
in
\mathscr{D}
if they can be joined by a horizontal line passing through no other boxes in
\mathscr{D}
.

Definition
. A right going line from a box
b\in R_{u}
to a box
b^{\prime}\in R_{v}
is said to be up-going (resp. down-going) if
u\geq v
(resp.
u\leq v
). Viewed as a left going line it is said to be down-going (resp. up-going).

This convention is natural as it is the sense in which the diagrams are drawn and matrices are labelled. On the other hand when one constructs
\ell(\mathscr{D})
by an induction of rows
R_{i}:i=1,2,\ldots,
as in
[
4
, 5.4]
, it is more natural to regard
R_{i}
as being higher up than
R_{j}
if
i<j
, just as in the designation of the floors of a building. Thus the opposite convention was implicit in
[
4
, 5.4.8]
and
[
5
, 3.5]
. We always refer to the presence of this change when one must pay attention. This conventional dichotomy was already present in Column shifting
[
4
, 2.4,
N.B.
]
where we noted that it would have been better to have adopted the French convention of drawing tableaux, but who wants to do that!

Besides in reading the latter part of this paper (Sect.
5
) one should wash the brain clean of thoughts of previous work.

2.2.
Weierstrass Section Construction

The construction of
[
4
, Sect. 5]
gives a family
\ell(\mathscr{D})
of lines joining boxes in distinct columns labelled either by a
1
or by a
\ast
. If in
\mathscr{T}
the left (resp. right) hand box carries the label
i
(resp.
j
), then the corresponding line, denoted as
\ell_{i,j}
, is assigned

, which we remark always belongs to
\mathfrak{m}
. Then by definition,
e
is just the sum of the co-ordinate vectors defined by the lines labelled by
1
and
V
is just the direct sum of the co-ordinate subspaces defined by the lines labelled by
\ast
.

We aim to construct
\ell(\mathscr{D})
much more simply. The proof that this simpler formulation gives the same result is quite complicated relying heavily on the results of
[
4
, Sect. 5]
. However if one just wants to see why it gives
e+V
to be a Weierstrass section then one can turn directly to the rather easy Section
5
.

Recall that the Levi factor
L
of
P
is given by a set of square blocks
\textbf{B}_{i}
along the diagonal of
M
of size
c_{i}
. Let
\textbf{C}_{i}
denote the rectangular block in
M
, called the
i^{th}
column block, lying above
\textbf{B}_{i}
. One has
\mathfrak{m}=\oplus_{i=2}^{k}\textbf{C}_{i}
.

2.3.
Induction on Number of Columns

Let
\mathscr{D}^{k}
be the diagram obtained from
\mathscr{D}
by deleting its rightmost column
C_{k}
.

The construction of
\ell(\mathscr{D}^{k})
is of course also given by
[
4
, Sect. 5]
. Moreover by
[
4
, Lemma 5.4.9]
,
\ell(\mathscr{D})
is just the union of
\ell(\mathscr{D}^{k})
and the set
\ell(\mathscr{D}^{k},C_{k})
of lines (with their labels) joining
\mathscr{D}^{k}
to
C_{k}
. By construction our new method will also have this property (Lemma
5.2
). Thus proceeding inductively, we need only compute
\ell(\mathscr{D}^{k},C_{k})
.

2.4.
The Composition Map

When
c_{k}>|\mathscr{D}^{k}|
, the situation simplifies further. First by
[
5
, 3.6.2]
the lines joining
C_{k}
to
\mathscr{D}^{k}
are
all
labelled by a
1
.
Secondly they are independent of
c_{k}
,
[
5
, 3.6.4]
. Thirdly there is a unique right going line
\ell_{t}
labelled by
1
to the
t^{th}
row of
C_{k}
, or no line at all. In the former case let
r_{t}
be the entry of the box in
S
from which
\ell_{t}
starts, in the latter case set
r_{t}=0
.

Note that
r_{t}
is
not
the row of
\mathscr{D}
from which
\ell
starts, but the row of
\textbf{C}_{k}
in which
1
occurs in the
t^{th}
column of
M
. Thus to avoid confusion with the labelling of rows of
C_{k}
it was written in boldface in
[
5
, 3.3.2]
, that is as
\textbf{r}_{t}
, in
[
5
, 3.3.2]
.

By
[
4
, 5.4.8(vi)]
a box may carry at most one right going line carrying a

and so in addition the

are pairwise distinct.

The resulting map
\mathscr{D}^{k}\rightarrow\{r_{t}\}_{t=1}^{|\mathscr{D}^{k}|+1}
of

to the set pairwise distinct non-negative integers, is by definition

. On its own it does
not
allow one to reconstruct the set of all left going lines from
C_{k}
to
S
with their labels
[
5
, 3.6.7]
. However quite remarkably, when combined with a knowledge of

, this reconstruction can be made and by simple rules
[
5
, Lemmas 3.4.2(i), 3.6.8]
.

Thus to determine the canonical Weierstrass section

, it suffices to determine the composition maps relative to successive removal of columns of
\mathscr{D}
from the right.

3.
Towards the Composition Map

3.1.
Neighbouring Columns

Recall
[
3
, 4.1.2]
that a pair of columns
C,C^{\prime}
of height
s
are said to be neighbouring (of height
s
) if there are no columns of height
s
strictly between them. To each such pair one may assign a Benlolo-Sanderson invariant
[
1
]
and
[
3
, 3.6.3]
. The latter form a generating set for

, which is a polynomial algebra
[

, Cor. 5.2.6]
.

3.2.
Column Staircases

Fix
c
be a positive integer and
u\in[1,c]
.

Definition
. A column staircase
\mathscr{S}_{u}^{c}
in
\mathscr{D}
is a maximal subset
C^{\prime}_{i_{j}}:j\in[u,c]
of columns in
\mathscr{D}
, labelled as in
2.1
, with the property that
0ptC^{\prime}_{i_{j}}=j
, such that every
C^{\prime}_{i_{j}}:j\in[u,c]
, has a left neighbour
C_{i_{j}}
in
\mathscr{D}
and
such that there are no columns of height
\geq j
strictly between
C^{\prime}_{i_{j}},C^{\prime}_{i_{j+1}}:u\leq j<c
(which implies that
C_{i_{j}}
lies to the left of the columns in
\mathscr{S}_{u}^{c}
).

Attention.
Apart from being a left neighbour to
C_{i_{j}}
and lying to the left of
\mathscr{S}_{u}^{c}
, there are
no
other conditions on the positioning of the
C_{i_{j}}
. The origin of this flexibility is the fact that only the position of the
\ast
on horizontal lines in the Second Step
[
3
, 4.2.1]
is important and that we are using a “rightmost labelling”
[
3
, 4.2.2]
. This flexibility is also apparent when we impose
5.4
as part of our new construction. Moreover when we say that a column staircase
C^{\prime}_{i_{j}}:j\in[u,c]
lies to the right of a column

we do not imply any positioning of the set of the left neighbours
C_{i_{j}}
relative to
C
. For example if
0ptC=3
, all the compositions
(1,2,3,1,2),(2,1,3,1,2)
as well as the further four in which the first two entries are pushed across
C
, give a staircase
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{1}
of height
1
and depth
2
to the right of
C
. In this case
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{1}
is deemed to be the leftmost staircase of depth
2
to the right of
C
- see after
Notation
below. Adjoining a column of height
2
to the right would give a further staircase
\mathscr{S}^{2}_{2}
of depth
2
to the right of
C
but it would not be the leftmost one of depth
2
. Concern for these complications are obviated in our new construction.

Notation.

Let
b(\mathscr{S}^{c}_{u})
be the box left adjacent to
C^{\prime}_{i_{u}}\cap R_{u}
(the latter is the lowest box in
C^{\prime}_{i_{u}}
). It lies in
R_{u}
and in some column, noted
C^{\prime}_{i_{u-1}}
, and to the right of
C_{i_{u}}
, possibly strictly.

When the height
u
of the first column in
\mathscr{S}^{c}_{u}
is unspecified we shall simply write
\mathscr{S}^{c}
and if
c
is also unspecified we shall simple write
\mathscr{S}
. We call
u
(resp.
c
) the height (resp. depth) of
\mathscr{S}_{u}^{c}
and

its length, in accordance with the convention of

.

A column staircase
\mathscr{S}_{u}^{c}
is said to be (right) extremal if there are no columns of height
\geq c
to the right of
C^{\prime}_{i_{c}}
, which we recall has height
c
.

We say that a box

has no right adjacent box, if a horizontal line from

does not meet a box strictly to the right of
b
.

Call a box

in
\mathscr{D}
right extremal in
\mathscr{D}
, relative to

if there is no right going line from

, labelled by a
1
.

Obviously a box

in

is right extremal, but there may be others.
On the other hand a box may have no right adjacent box, yet not be right extremal and conversely a box may be right extremal and yet have a right adjacent box.

Example.
Consider the composition
(2,2,1,1)
. The construction of
[
4
, Sect. 5]
gives lines
\ell_{1,3},\ell_{3,5},\ell_{4,6}
labelled by a

and lines

labelled by a
\ast
. Thus

is right extremal with right adjacent box

which is in turn not right extremal.

Lemma
.

Assume

Adjoin a column

to obtain an “augmented” diagram

. By

nor their labels, except that if there is a right going line

Through

3.4.
A Simple Case

Suppose all columns in
\mathscr{D}
have different heights. Then the construction of
[
4
, 5.4]
says that

consists of all the horizontal lines joining adjacent boxes and are labelled by a

Now for all

Lemma

Assume that all the columns in

with no right adjacent box in row

3.5.
Replacement

The general case is a little more complicated. It involves the replacement of those boxes with no right adjacent box.

Assume in Section
3.5
that

Retain the above hypotheses and notation. Set

4.7.
A Second rather Simple Algorithm for the Composition Tableau

We now describe a different and very simple algorithm to describe

which will be used exclusively in Section

Recall the natural order
i\leq j
on the set
[1,n]
of entries of
\mathscr{T}
.

Define a new total order
i^{\prime}\preceq j^{\prime}
on these entries by
defining
them to increase strictly down the columns and then from
right to left
. Let
\{1^{\prime},2^{\prime},\ldots,n^{\prime},\}
be the set

written in increasing order for
\preceq
. This is not quite

in reverse order. In particular

is the first entry in the last column, generally different from

.

Let
\mathscr{T}(s)
be the tableau starting from
\mathscr{T}
and obtained after

\{1^{\prime},2^{\prime},(l-1)^{\prime}\}
and some of the

in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
sequentially by the inductive procedure given below.

A column

is regarded as column of
\mathscr{T}
and of
\mathscr{T}(s)
. However

will
always
refer to its height in
\mathscr{T}
, equivalently in
\mathscr{D}
. Thus if

then the box

is empty in
\mathscr{T}
, may yet have an entry in
\mathscr{T}(s)
. Also, columns are regarded as neighbours only if they are neighbours in
\mathscr{D}
(see example below).

Let

be the rightmost box of
\mathscr{T}(s)
with entry

.

Suppose

.

(i)
. If
b
is empty in
\mathscr{T}(s)
, put
l^{\prime}
in the box

of
\mathscr{T}(s)
to obtain
\mathscr{T}(s+1)
.

(ii)
. If not add no new entry

to
\mathscr{T}(s)
.

Suppose that
0ptC_{j+1}=t
.

(iii)
. If
C_{j+1}
has a left neighbour in
\mathscr{D}
and has no entry in
R_{t+1}
with respect to
\mathscr{T}(s)
, put
l^{\prime}
in

to obtain

.

(iv)
. Otherwise add no new entry
l^{\prime}
to
\mathscr{T}(s)
.

Definition
. If (ii) or (iv) apply, we say that
l^{\prime}
is stopped just before it reaches
C_{j+1}
. Notice that
l^{\prime}
goes right and downward (though not necessarily strictly). If at some point

enters

, we say it reaches

.

Let
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
be the tableau obtained starting from
\mathscr{T}
when the labels
(1^{\prime},2^{\prime},\ldots,n^{\prime})
have been inserted, possibly many times, into
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
by the above rules.

Examples.
Consider the composition
(1,2,1)
. One has

. By (iii) with

, a second

is put in

. Then by (ii), the entry

is not placed in
b
. This exactly corresponds to

not being extremal and indeed it is linked to

by line with label
1
, whilst
b(2)
is extremal. Again although

and
C_{3}
both have height

in

, they are

considered as neighbours and so

is not applied to put a

in

. This would have made

extremal which is false.

On the other hand if we omit the first column and keep the labelling of the remaining boxes then by
(i)
,
3
not

is placed in

and
b(3)
, but not
b(2)
is extremal.

These observations lie behind the proof of the proposition below.

4.8.
Semistandard Tableaux

We call a tableau (with entries in
[1,n]
) semi-standard if entries strictly increase down the columns and decrease along the rows on going from left to right, with no gaps down columns. (This might not be an accepted definition.)

Lemma
.

The tableau
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
is semi-standard for the order relation
\preceq
.

Proof.

Obviously
\mathscr{T}
is semi-standard by the definition of
\preceq
. We prove the assertion for
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
by induction adding one new integer
l^{\prime}
at a time. Observe that by
4.7
(i) at no step in the construction of
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
is there a column with a gap, that is an empty box with entries above and below. Consider a box
b
with entry
l^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{T}(s)
.

If there is no entry in a box
b^{\prime}
just to the right of
b
, we put
l^{\prime}
in
b^{\prime}
, by
4.7
(i).
By the induction hypothesis, the box above
b^{\prime}
and to the right of
b^{\prime}
has only entries than
\preceq l^{\prime}
, whilst the box below
b^{\prime}
is empty, since there are no gaps.

If
b^{\prime}
is already full but the box
b^{\prime\prime}
just below
b^{\prime}
is empty, then we
may
(resp.
may not
) be putting

l^{\prime}
in
b^{\prime\prime}
, by
4.7
(iii)(resp.
4.7
(iv)).

As before the box to the right of
b^{\prime\prime}
has an entry
\succeq l^{\prime}
, whilst the box
b^{\prime}
above
b^{\prime\prime}
also has an entry
\succeq l^{\prime}
because it is to the right of
b
. Hence the induction carries forward.
∎

Remark 1
. In particular the entries in
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
are strictly increasing down the rows for
\preceq
whilst this is generally false for
\leq
.

Remark 2
. We may obtain a “genuine” semi-standard tableau from
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
with entries in [1,n] also increasing for
\leq
from left to right and strictly down columns as well as having no gaps in rows. This is by taking the left-right mirror image of
\mathscr{T}(\infty)
and replacing
i^{\prime}
by
i
. It has as a sub-tableau, the tableau obtained by performing the same operations on
\mathscr{T}
.

4.9.
Equality of completed Tableaux.

Proposition
.

\mathscr{T}(\infty)=\mathscr{T}^{\prime}

5.7.
Composite Lines

Retain the above hypothesis and notation.

Recall
[
3
, 4.2.3]
that a line is said to be composite if it is a concatenation of lines joining boxes going from left to right. Two composite lines are said to be disjoint if they do not pass through a common box.

Proposition
.

There is a unique disjoint union of composite lines passing though every box in
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
formed from the unique left-going line
l_{\ast}
from
C\cap R_{t}
with label
\ast
together with all the lines with label
1
joining boxes in
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
, we can assume
C^{\prime}=C_{k}
, without loss of generality. Set
0ptC_{k}=t
. By Lemma
5.6.3
the left going line from
C^{\prime}\cap R_{t}
with label
1
if it exists, has end-point outside
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
and so can be ignored. By Corollary
5.5.1
there is a unique concatenation of left going lines with label
1
from
C\cap R_{t^{\prime}}:t^{\prime}\in[1,t]
, starting with
l_{\ast}
if
t^{\prime}=t
and ending in
C
or hopping over
C
. (Eventually a counting argument shows that no hopping over
C
takes place.)

By uniqueness of left going lines with label
1
from a given box and the uniqueness of right going lines in Corollary
5.5.2
, the resulting composite lines are disjoint and of those which meet
C
there are at most
t
and exactly
t
if no hopping over
C
takes place.

On the other hand by Corollary
5.5.2
also asserts the existence of a unique right going line from any box in
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}[
to a box in
R^{t}]C,C^{\prime}]
labelled by a
1
unless it be the line

(which meets

).

We conclude that there are exactly
t
disjoint composite lines passing through every box in
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
of which exactly one line has label
\ast
. In addition no hopping over

by the lines, which comprise these disjoint composite lines, can take place. Finally uniqueness of these lines with label

must be used!
∎

5.8.
The General Case

We strengthen the above result retaining the same notation.

Theorem
.

There is a unique disjoint union
\mathscr{U}^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
of composite lines passing through all the boxes in
R^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Proposition
5.7
if we can show in
any
disjoint union

of composite lines passing through all the boxes in
R_{C,C^{\prime}}^{t}
, that

is the only line in the family

of lines forming these composite lines, which is labelled by a

. (This was the point of view we adopted in

, 4.4.11]
and
[
4
, 5.4]
.)

To exclude all such lines labelled by an
\ast
excepting
l_{\ast}
from
\mathscr{U}^{t}[C,C^{\prime}]
we prove that any left going line

with right end point in

is labelled by a
1
, excepting
l_{\ast}
, by downward induction on

Then any other left going line to
b
(necessarily labelled by a
\ast
through Corollary
5.5.1
) is excluded becuase the union
\mathscr{U}^{t}(C,C^{\prime})
is required to be
disjoint
. Here we can assume

.

Now by Corollary
5.6.2
every line labelled by a
\ast
has a left end point which is some

. Thus all these lines are excluded as long as the line with label

in the conclusion of this corollary are strictly down-going.

However it can happen that such a line is horizontal. This situation is handled by

∎

As Sherlock Holmes might have said. Elementary my dear Watson!

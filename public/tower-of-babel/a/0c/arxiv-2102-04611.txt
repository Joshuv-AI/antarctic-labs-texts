Eigenvalues for the Clamped Plate Problem of 𝔏 ν 2 \mathfrak{L}^{2}_{\nu} Operator on Complete Riemannian manifolds
Lingzhong Zeng

Abstract
. 𝔏 ν \mathfrak{L}_{\nu} operator is an important extrinsic differential operator of divergence type and has profound geometric settings. In this paper, we consider the clamped plate problem of 𝔏 ν 2 \mathfrak{L}^{2}_{\nu} operator on a bounded domain of the complete Riemannian manifolds. A general formula of eigenvalues of 𝔏 ν 2 \mathfrak{L}^{2}_{\nu} operator is established. Applying this general formula, we obtain some estimates for the eigenvalues with higer order on the complete Riemannian manifolds. As several fascinating applications, we discuss this eigenvalue problem on the complete translating solitons, minimal submanifolds on the Euclidean space, submanifolds on the unit sphere and projective spaces. In particular, we get a universal inequality with respect to the ℒ I ​ I \mathcal{L}_{II} operator on the translating solitons. Usually, it is very difficult to get universal inequalities for weighted Laplacian and even Laplacian on the complete Riemannian manifolds. Therefore, this work can be viewed as a new contribution to universal inequality.

1.
Introduction

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an
n
-dimensional, complete Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider the fixed membrane problem of Laplacian on
\Omega\subset\mathcal{M}^{n}
:

(1.1)

{\begin{cases}\ \Delta u=-\lambda u,\ \ &{\rm in}\ \ \ \ \Omega,\\
\ u=0,\ \ &{\rm on}\ \ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}}

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian on the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
.
Let
\lambda_{k}
denote the
k^{th}
eigenvalue, and then the spectrum of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) is
discrete and satisfies

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty,

where each eigenvalue is repeated under counting its multiplicity.
When
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Payne,
Pólya and Weinberger
[
29
]
studied Dirichlet eigenvalue problem (
1.1
), and obtained a universal inequality as follows:

(1.2)

\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{k}\leq\frac{4}{nk}\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}.

Here, the words “universal” means that eigenvalue inequalities are independent of the domain.
Furthermore, in various settings, many mathematicians extended the universal inequality given by Payne, Pólya and Weinberger. In particular, Hile and Protter
[
22
]
proved the following universal inequality of eigenvalues:

(1.3)

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{nk}{4},

which is sharper than eigenvalues inequality (
1.2
). Furthermore, an amazing contribution to eigenvalue inequality is due to Yang
[
37
]
(cf.
[
17
]
). He obtained a very sharp universal inequality:

(1.4)

\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda_{i})\lambda_{i}.

From (
1.4
), one can infer that

(1.5)

\lambda_{k+1}\leq\frac{1}{k}(1+\frac{4}{n})\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}.

The inequalities (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are called by Ashbaugh Yang’s first inequality and second inequality,
respectively (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
). Indeed, according to Chebyshev’s inequality, we have the following connections

\eqref{y1-ineq}\Rightarrow\eqref{y2-ineq}\Rightarrow\eqref{hp-ineq}\Rightarrow\eqref{ppw-ineq}.

To describe vibrations of a clamped plate in elastic mechanics, one usually
consider the following Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for biharmonic operator:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta^{2}u=\Lambda u,\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\textbf{n}}=0,\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Delta^{2}
is the biharmonic operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and this eigenvalue problem is called a clamped
plate problem.
For this clamped plate problem, in
1956,
Payne, Pólya and Weinberger
[
29
]
established an eigenvalue inequality. They obtained a universal inequality as follows:

(1.7)

\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{k}\leq\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}.

As a generalization of their result, in
1984,
Hile and Yeh
[
23
]
proved

(1.8)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}\geq\frac{n^{2}k^{3/2}}{8(n+2)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

by making use of an improved method of Hile and Protter
[
22
]
. Furthermore, in

1990,
Hook
[
24
]
Chen and Qian
[
14
]
proved, independently, the following inequality:

(1.9)

\frac{n^{2}k^{2}}{8(n+2)}\leq\left[\sum_{i=1}\frac{\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Recently, in
[
4
]
, a survey paper on recent developments of eigenvalue problems, Ashbaugh pointed out whether one can establish inequalities for eigenvalues of the vibrating clamped plate problem which are analogous inequalities of Yang in the case of the eigenvalue problem of the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary condition. In
[
18
]
, Cheng and Yang gave an affirmative answer to the problem introduced by Ashbaugh. This is to say that they obtain the following universal inequality:

(1.10)

\Lambda_{k+1}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}\leq\left[\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left[\Lambda_{i}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is sharper than

(1.11)

\Lambda_{k+1}\leq\left[1+\frac{8(n+2)}{n^{2}}\right]\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{i}.

It is easy to see that inequality (
1.11
) is better than inequality (
1.7
) of Payne, Pólya and Weinberger. We shall also discuss the relation between inequality (
1.11
) and inequality (
1.9
) introduced by Hook
[
24
]
, and Chen and Qian
[
14
]
in the Remark 2 of Section 2.

2.
Key lemma and its proof

In this section, we would like to establish a general formula, which will play an important role in the proof of theorem
1.1
. Our general formula says the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain on an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M}^{n},g)
, and
\Lambda_{i}
be the
i^{\text{th}}
eigenvalue of the eigenvalue problem (
1.16
) and
u_{i}
be the orthonormal eigenfunction corresponding to
\Lambda_{i},
that is,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}u_{i}=\Lambda_{i}u_{i},&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{i}=\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial v}=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}u_{i}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv=\delta_{ij},&\forall i,j=1,2,\ldots,\end{array}\right.

where
\nu
is an outward normal vector field of
\partial\Omega
. Then, for any function
f\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{3}(\partial\Omega)
and any positive integer
k,
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{i}(f)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{i}(f)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv;

where

(2.2)

\Psi_{i}(f)=-2|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+4\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)^{2},

(2.3)

\Theta_{i}(f)=\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)^{2},

and

\delta
is any positive constant.

Proof.

Let

\varphi_{i}=fu_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}u_{j},

for any integer
k\geq 1,
where

a_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv=a_{ji},

then we have

\left.\varphi_{i}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{M}^{n}}=\left.\frac{\partial\varphi_{i}}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{M}^{n}}=0,

and

(2.4)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv=0,\quad\forall i,j=1,\ldots,k.

From the Rayleigh-Ritz inequality, we get

(2.5)

\Lambda_{k+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}\varphi_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By direct computation, we have

\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(fu_{i}\right)=f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f,

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left(fu_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)

\displaystyle=f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}u_{i}+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}fu_{i}+\omega_{i},

where

\omega_{i}=2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right).

From (
2.6
) and (
2.4
), we can get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}\varphi_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}\left(fu_{i}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}fu_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}b_{ij},

where

(2.8)

b_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{i}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\leq\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}b_{ij}.

Using integration by parts, we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{j}\right\rangle_{g}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}{\rm div}\left(e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\nabla f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\Delta f+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\langle\nu,\nabla f\rangle_{g_{0}}\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

From (
2.10
), we infer that,

(2.11)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{j}-\Lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(fu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}u_{j}-fu_{j}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}^{2}u_{i}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(fu_{i}\right)\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{j}-\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(fu_{j}\right)\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{j}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\left(u_{j}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{j}\right\rangle_{g}\right)\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[u_{j}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)+2\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)

\displaystyle-u_{j}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2u_{j}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\Big]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left[\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)+2\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\omega_{i}u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=b_{ij}.

It follows from (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}-\Lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}^{2}.

On the other hand, from (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\left(\omega_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}u_{j}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|^{2}\left(\left\|\omega_{i}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\left(\left\|\omega_{i}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}\right),

where

\left\|\varphi_{i}\right\|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By Bessel inequality and (
2.8
), we know that

(2.13)

\left\|\omega_{i}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}\geq 0.

It follows from (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) that

(2.14)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\geq 0.

From (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), we can get

(2.15)

\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\leq\left\|\omega_{i}\right\|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{ij}^{2}.

Multiplying (
2.15
) by (
\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}
) and summing on
i
from 1 to
k
, and using (
2.11
), we get

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left\|\omega_{i}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}\right)^{2}a_{ij}^{2}.

Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\operatorname{div}\left(u_{j}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\nabla f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{j}\right\rangle_{g}+u_{j}\langle\nu,\nabla f\rangle+u_{j}\Delta f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{j}\right\rangle_{g}+u_{j}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\operatorname{div}\left(fu_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\nabla f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\left(u_{i}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}+f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+fu_{i}\langle\nu,\nabla f\rangle+fu_{i}\Delta f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}+fu_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv,

which implies that

2\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv=-\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}+fu_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

Setting

t_{ij}=\int_{\Omega}u_{j}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv,

we have

t_{ij}=-t_{ji},

and

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-2\varphi_{i}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-2fu_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}-fu_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}t_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}t_{ij}.

By (
2.16
), (
2.17
) and the Schwarz inequality, we get

(2.18)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}t_{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}-2\varphi_{i}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{ij}u_{j}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{3}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{ij}u_{j}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{j}-\Lambda_{i}\right)a_{ij}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}{\delta}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{ij}^{2}\right),

where
\delta
is any positive constant. Noticing
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
and
t_{ij}=-t_{ji}
, we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left[\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{ij}t_{ij}\right]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}a_{ij}t_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}a_{ij}t_{ij}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{j}\right)^{2}a_{ij}t_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}+\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{j}\right)\left(\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}t_{ij}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}t_{ij}.

Summing over
i
from 1 to
k
in (
2.18
) and utilizing (
2.19
), we yield

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}\right)a_{ij}t_{ij}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)\left(\Lambda_{i}-\Lambda_{j}\right)^{2}a_{ij}^{2}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}{\delta}t_{ij}^{2}.

Hence, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta\left(\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\Lambda_{k+1}-\Lambda_{i}}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathcal{L}_{\nu}f}{2}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}div\left(fu_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\nabla f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}\left(u_{i}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}+f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+fu_{i}\langle\nu,\nabla f\rangle+fu_{i}\Delta f\right)dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\int_{\Omega}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

From the above equality and the definition of
\omega_{i},
we have

(2.21)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\omega_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\Bigg\{2\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)\right\rangle_{g}+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+2\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right)+\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)\Bigg\}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv-2\int_{\Omega}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+2\int u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+4\int\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Omega}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+\int u_{i}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}fu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv+2\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(-2|\nabla f|_{g}^{2}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+4\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}f\right)^{2}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

Introducing (
2.21
) into (
2.20
), we get (
2.1
).
This completes the proof of lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Main Results

In this section, we shall give the proofs of the main results. Throughout this paper, we will agree the following convention on ranges of indices:

1\leq i,j,\cdots,\leq n;\quad 1\leq\alpha,\beta,\cdots,\leq n+p.

Suppose that
\left(\overline{x}^{1},\cdots,\overline{x}^{n}\right)
is an arbitrary coordinate system in a neighborhood
U
of
P
in
\mathcal{M}^{n}
.
Assume that
x
with components
x^{\alpha}
defined by

x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}\left(\overline{x}^{1},\cdots,\overline{x}^{n}\right),1\leq\alpha\leq n+p,

is the position vector of
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For an
n
-dimensional submanifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
in Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
, let
x=(x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{n+p})
is the
position vector of a point
p\in\mathcal{M}^{n}
with

x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\overline{x}_{1},\cdots,\overline{x}_{n})
,
1\leq\alpha\leq n+p
, where
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
denotes a local coordinate
system of
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. Then, we have

(3.1)

\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\langle\nabla x^{\alpha},\nabla x^{\alpha}\rangle_{g}=n,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\langle\nabla x^{\alpha},\nabla u\rangle_{g}\langle\nabla x^{\alpha},\nabla w\rangle_{g}=\langle\nabla u,\nabla w\rangle_{g},

for any functions
u,w\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M}^{n})
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}(\Delta x^{\alpha})^{2}=n^{2}H^{2},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\Delta x^{\alpha}\nabla x^{\alpha}=\textbf{0},

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\mathcal{M}^{n}
.

From (
3.1
), we have

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left|\nabla x_{\alpha}\right|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv=n.

According to (
3.2
), one has

(3.6)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}=\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}.

By a direct calculation, we can conclude that,

(3.7)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}^{2}=|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
3.7
), we derive

(3.8)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\leq\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}.

It follows from (
3.4
) that,

(3.9)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\Delta x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left\langle\Delta x_{\alpha}\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}=0,

and

(3.10)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\Delta x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left\langle\Delta x_{\alpha}\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}=0.

From (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

(3.11)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left(\Delta x_{\alpha}+\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\right)\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\leq\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+p}
be the standard coordinate functions of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
. For any
i=1,2,\cdots k
and
\alpha=1,2,\cdots,n+p
, let

\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}:=\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{i}(x_{\alpha})e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv,

where function
\Psi_{i}
is given by (
2.2
). Then, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[-2nu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+4\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}|\nu^{\top}|^{2}_{g_{0}}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Taking
f=x_{\alpha}
in (
2.2
) and summing over
\alpha
from 1 to
n+p,
we get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\int_{\Omega}\left(-2\left|\nabla x_{\alpha}\right|_{g}^{2}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+4\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

From (
3.7
), (
3.10
) and (
3.3
), we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left(\Delta x_{\alpha}+\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left(\left(\Delta x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}+2\Delta x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}+\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nu\right\rangle_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}.

From (
3.1
), (
3.11
), (
3.6
), (
3.14
) and (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(-2nu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4u_{i}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}\left|\nu^{\top}\right|_{g_{0}}+4\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right)e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[-2nu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+4\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}})^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By divergence theorem and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we conclude that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\int_{\Omega}u_{i}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[-2nu_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}u_{i}+4\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+4\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}})^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which gives (
3.12
). Thus, it finishes the proof of lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+p}
be the standard coordinate functions of
\mathbb{R}^{n+p}
. For any
i=1,2,\cdots k
and
\alpha=1,2,\cdots,n+p
, let

). Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left[\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}})^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Taking
f=x_{\alpha}
in (
2.3
) and summing over
\alpha
from 1 to
n+p
, we get

(3.18)

\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}=\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}}{2}\right)^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

Utilizing (
3.11
), (
3.6
), (
3.14
) and (
3.13
), we infer that

(3.19)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left(\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}}{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+p}\left[\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}^{2}+u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\left\langle\nabla x_{\alpha},\nabla u_{i}\right\rangle_{g}+\frac{1}{4}\left(u_{i}\mathfrak{L}_{\nu}x_{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right).

By the definition of
\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}
and (
3.19

(3.20)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+u_{i}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we infer that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}})^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|_{g}^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From (
3.16
) and (

\displaystyle\sum^{n+p}_{\alpha=1}\widehat{\Theta}_{i,\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|_{g}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}u_{i}^{2}\left(n^{2}H^{2}+|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}}^{2}\right)\right]e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv

\displaystyle+\Lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u_{i}|\nu^{\top}|_{g_{0}})^{2}e^{\langle\nu,X\rangle_{g_{0}}}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, we finish the proof of lemma
3.3

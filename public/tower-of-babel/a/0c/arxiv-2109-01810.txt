Eduard Looijenga and Yunpeng Zi

Abstract
. The sextic plane curves that are invariant under the standard action of the icosahedral group on the projective plane make up a pencil of genus ten curves (spanned by a sum of six lines and a three times a conic). This pencil was first considered in a note by R. M. Winger in 1925 and is nowadays named after him. The second author recently gave this a modern treatment and proved among other things that it contains essentially every smooth genus ten curve with icosahedral symmetry. We here show that the Jacobian of such a curve contains the tensor product of an elliptic curve with a certain integral representation of the icosahedral group. We find that the elliptic curve comes with a distinguished point of order 3 3 , prove that the monodromy on this part of the homology is the full congruence subgroup Γ 1 ​ ( 3 ) ⊂ SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \Gamma_{1}(3)\subset\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathds{Z}) and subsequently identify the base of the pencil with the associated modular curve. We also observe that the Winger pencil ‘accounts’ for the deformation of the Jacobian of Bring’s curve as a principal abelian fourfold with an action of the icosahedral group. Let us elaborate on the appearance of Bring’s curve. First, recall that this is a curve of genus four that comes endowed with a faithful
action of the full symmetric group

that appears in the Winger pencil. Since the part of the Jacobian that we consider here survives in the normalization of this curve
(so in Brings’s curve), our period map ignores its six double points.

This can also be expressed as follows: Bring’s curve has no deformations as a
\mathcal{S}_{5}
-curve,
not even as a
\mathscr{I}
-curve. However the associated stable genus ten curve admits a smoothing which makes the Jacobian of Bring’s curve deform as a factor of the Jacobians of a stable family of genus ten curves with

4

A central role in the proof is played by a combinatorial model of a genus ten curve with
\mathscr{I}
-action. It is obtained by taking a regular dodecahedron that is truncated in a
\mathscr{I}
-invariant manner by removing at each vertex a small triangular neighborhood (so that we get ten antipodal pairs of triangles as boundary components) and subsequently identifying opposite boundary triangles by means the antipodal involution. The resulting surface is oriented and of genus ten and comes with a piecewise euclidean structure that is
\mathscr{I}
-invariant. This gives rise to a
\mathscr{I}
-invariant conformal and hence complex structure. We thus obtain a family of Riemann surfaces with

2.1.
Isotypical decomposition of a symplectic module over a group algebra

We begin with a brief review of the basic theory, referring to
[
5
]
for more details. Let

\mathds{R}

\mathds{C}

For

an irreducible

By the Schur Lemma the ring
\operatorname{End}_{kG}(V_{\lambda})

as a left
kG
-module and as a right
D_{\lambda}
-module.

Assume now that

Then each

G
-invariant inner product

and then the division algebra
D_{\lambda}
comes with a natural anti-involution
\sigma\to\sigma^{\ast}
characterized by
s_{\lambda}(v\sigma,v^{\prime})=s_{\lambda}(v,v^{\prime}\sigma^{\ast})
.

For a finitely generated
kG
-module

and

D_{\lambda}
-module on
V_{\lambda}

structure determines a left
D_{\lambda}
-module structure on

given as

(du)(v):=u(vd)
where

and

. This makes the natural map

\displaystyle\oplus_{\lambda\in\chi(kG)}V_{\lambda}\otimes_{D_{\lambda}}\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle v\otimes_{D_{\lambda}}u\in V_{\lambda}\otimes_{D_{\lambda}}\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle u(v)

an isomorphism of
kG
-modules. This is called the

of

and the image of

in

is called the

associated to

-linear automorphism of

will
preserve this

-isotypical decomposition and acts on each isotypical summand

V_{\lambda}\otimes_{D_{\lambda}}\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H)
through a
D_{\lambda}
-linear transformation on the second tensor factor.
This identifies
\operatorname{End}_{kG}(H)
with
\Pi_{\lambda\in\chi(G)}\operatorname{End}_{D_{\lambda}}(\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H))
.

Let us suppose further that our

-module

-invariant symplectic form

(for example when

For a fixed character

and fixed

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(du,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle dh_{\lambda}(u,u^{\prime}),\ d\in D_{\lambda}

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(u,u^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h_{\lambda}(u^{\prime},u)^{\ast}

Hence the pairing

\operatorname{Sp}(V_{\lambda}\otimes_{D_{\lambda}}\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H))^{G}\cong{U_{D_{\lambda}}(\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(V_{\lambda},H))}

3.

5.
Global Monodromy and the Period Map: proof of Theorem
1.1

We first show that
\rho_{\textrm{edge}}
and
\rho_{\textrm{trc}}
generate all of
\Gamma_{1}(3)

Let us first observe that the natural map

Now recall that

is generated by

0&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }S:=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

and that a set of defining relations is
S^{4}=(ST)^{6}=1
and
S^{2}T=TS^{2}

S^{2}
represents
-1
and generates the kernel of the projection

\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathds{Z})/\{\pm 1\}\to\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathds{Z})

\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathds{Z})

\overline{S}

\overline{T}
denotes their images in

Note that

and

(which defines defines a cusp of width
3

Proposition 5.1

The index of

in

by
\{1,T,T^{-1},S\}\cup\{1,T,T^{-1},S\}S^{2}

Since

is central, it suffices to show
that the corresponding statement holds for the subgroup

in

generated by

\overline{T}^{\prime}=\overline{S}\,\overline{T}\,\overline{S}^{-1}

represent its left cosets. In other words, we must show that this set is invariant under left multiplication by

or

. This follows from the identities

\overline{T}\,\overline{S}=\overline{S}\,\overline{T}^{\prime}\in\overline{S}\,\overline{\Gamma}

\overline{S}\,\overline{T}=(\overline{S}\,\overline{T})^{-2}=\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{S}^{-1}\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{S}^{-1}=\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{S}\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{S}=\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{T}^{\prime-1}\in\overline{T}^{-1}\overline{\Gamma}

The main result of this section is the following, which can be understood as a strong Torelli theorem for our family. It is a reformulation

Theorem 5.2
.

The map
\mathscr{P}_{V}
is an isomorphism which takes the point
t=0
represented by the triple conic to the unique orbifold point of
X_{1}(3)
of degree
3
, whereas the points
t=\infty
and

are mapped to the cusps of width
3
and

respectively.

Proof.

The map
\mathscr{P}_{V}:\mathscr{B}\to Y_{1}(3)
is a nonconstant map between connected complete complex curves and hence it must be surjective and of finite degree. We must show that the degree (that we shall denote by
d
) is
1
.
The
\mathscr{P}_{V}
-preimages of the two cusps of

must be
\{\frac{27}{5},\infty\}
so that we will have a total ramification over each cusp. Both
\mathscr{B}
and
Y_{1}(3)
are copies of
\mathds{P}^{1}
and so have euler characteristic
2
. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula then implies that there cannot be any further ramification, for these two ramification points already bring down the euler characteristic of the total space to
the desired number:
2d-(d-1)-(d-1)=2
. This implies that
\mathscr{P}^{+}_{V}
is a local isomorphism.

We claim that
\mathscr{P}_{V}^{-1}(o)=0
. This will imply the theorem, for it then follows that
d=1
.

It is clear that
\mathscr{P}_{V}(0)=o
. On the other hand, the composite map
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}^{\circ}\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\mathscr{P}}^{+}_{V}}\mathscr{H}\to X_{1}(3)
is equal to the composite map
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}^{\circ}\to\mathscr{B}^{\circ}\to X_{1}(3)

and since both
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}^{\circ}\to\mathscr{B}
and
\mathscr{B}^{\circ}\to X_{1}(3)
are local isomorphisms, so is
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}^{\circ}\to\mathscr{H}\to X_{1}(3)
. As
\mathscr{H}\to X_{1}(3)
ramifies over
o
, it follows that this composite must land in

X_{1}(3)-\{o\}
. Hence
\mathscr{P}^{+}_{V}(\mathscr{B}^{\circ})\subset X_{1}(3)-\{o\}
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The preceding theorem does not follow from the usual Torelli theorem since it only concerns the
V_{o}
-part of the cohomology.
There is a similar period map for the
E_{o}
-part of the monodromy. The corresponding monodromy group will land in an arithmetic subgroup of a group isomorphic to
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathds{Q}(\sqrt{5}))
and the period map will land in a Hilbert modular surface associated to this group. If we combine this with the above theorem, we then find a map from
X_{1}(3)
to this Hilbert modular surface. Note that the point representing Bring’s curve is mapped to a cusp point of the Hilbert modular surface and so this cannot be a morphism of Shimura varieties.

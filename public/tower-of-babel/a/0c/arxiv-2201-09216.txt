Strong closing property of contact forms and action selecting functors
Kei Irie

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of strong closing property of contact forms, inspired by the C ∞ \displaystyle C^{\infty} closing lemma for Reeb flows in dimension three. We then prove a sufficient criterion for strong closing property, which is formulated by considering a monoidal functor from a category of manifolds with contact forms to a category of filtered vector spaces. As a potential application of this criterion, we propose a conjecture which says that a standard contact form on the boundary of any symplectic ellipsoid satisfies strong closing property.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setup and notations

Throughout this paper,
unless otherwise specified,
all manifolds, bundles and maps between them are of
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
.

Let
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
\displaystyle Y
be a
\displaystyle 2n-1
-dimensional manifold.
A
contact form
on
\displaystyle Y
is
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(Y)

such that
\displaystyle\lambda\wedge d\lambda^{n-1}(y)\neq 0
for any
\displaystyle y\in Y
.
The
Reeb vector field

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{X}}(Y)

is defined by equations

\displaystyle i_{R_{\lambda}}d\lambda\equiv 0

and
\displaystyle\lambda(R_{\lambda})\equiv 1
.

Let
\displaystyle S^{1}:={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
.
A
periodic Reeb orbit
of
\displaystyle\lambda
is a map
\displaystyle\gamma:S^{1}\to Y
such that there exists
\displaystyle T_{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}\equiv T_{\gamma}\cdot R_{\lambda}(\gamma)
.
The set of all periodic Reeb orbits of
\displaystyle\lambda
is denoted by
\displaystyle P(Y,\lambda)
.
For any contact form
\displaystyle\lambda
on
\displaystyle Y
,

\displaystyle\xi_{\lambda}:=\ker\lambda
is a hyperplane field on
\displaystyle Y
.
The hyperplane field
\displaystyle\xi_{\lambda}
is co-oriented, i.e.

\displaystyle TY/\xi_{\lambda}
is oriented in the following way:
for any
\displaystyle v\in TY
,
\displaystyle[v]
is positive if
\displaystyle\lambda(v)>0
.

A
contact manifold
is a pair
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
such that
\displaystyle\xi
is a co-oriented hyperplane field on
\displaystyle Y

and there exists a contact from
\displaystyle\lambda
on
\displaystyle Y
satisfying
\displaystyle\xi_{\lambda}=\xi
.
For any contact manifold
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
, let

\displaystyle\Lambda(Y,\xi):=\{\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(Y)\mid\text{ $\displaystyle\xi_{\lambda}=\xi$ as a co-oriented hyperplane field}\}
.
The map
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(Y)\to\Lambda(Y,\xi);\,h\mapsto e^{h}\lambda
is a bijection for any
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda(Y,\xi)
.

Remark 1.1
.

Let us define a partial order
\displaystyle\leq
on
\displaystyle\Lambda(Y,\xi)
as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
if there exists
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})

which satisfies
\displaystyle e^{h}\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.
Then,
\displaystyle\Lambda(Y,\xi)
with this partial order
\displaystyle\leq
(as well as its inverse)
is a directed set.

1.2.
Strong closing property

Let us introduce the key definition in this paper.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
be a closed contact manifold.
Let us say that
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda(Y,\xi)
satisfies

positive (resp. negative) strong closing property
if the following holds:

For any
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})
(resp.
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0})
)
with
\displaystyle h\not\equiv 0
,
there exist
\displaystyle t\in[0,1]
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in P(Y,e^{th}\lambda)

such that
\displaystyle\text{\rm Im}\,\gamma\cap\text{\rm supp}\,h\neq\emptyset
.

Let us say that
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies
strong closing property
if it satisfies
positive or negative strong closing property.

Remark 1.3
.

If
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies strong closing property,
then
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies the following property (which we call local
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
closing property):

If
\displaystyle U
is any nonempty open set of
\displaystyle Y
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{U}}
is any neighborhood of
\displaystyle 0
in
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(Y)
with the
\displaystyle C^{\infty}
topology,
then there exist
\displaystyle h\in{\mathcal{U}}
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in P(Y,e^{h}\lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\text{\rm supp}\,h\subset U
and
\displaystyle\text{\rm Im}\,\gamma\cap U\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 1.4
.

If
\displaystyle\bigcup_{\gamma\in P(Y,\lambda)}\text{\rm Im}\,\gamma
is dense in
\displaystyle Y
, then
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies strong closing property.

2.
Contact action selectors

Let
\displaystyle Y
be a closed manifold, and
\displaystyle\lambda
be a contact form on
\displaystyle Y
.
Let
\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,(Y,\lambda)
denote the set of periods of periodic Reeb orbits of
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda)
:

\text{\rm Spec}\,(Y,\lambda):=\{T_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in P(Y,\lambda)\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}.

We also consider the set of finite sums of elements of
\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,(Y,\lambda)
:

\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda):=\{0\}\cup\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}T_{\gamma_{i}}\bigg|k\geq 1,\,\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}\in P(Y,\lambda)\bigg\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}.

Note that
\displaystyle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k}
on the RHS are not assumed to be distinct.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\displaystyle Y
and
\displaystyle\lambda
as above,

\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)
is a closed set of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
.
It is also a null set with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
.

The proof of Lemma
2.1
is omitted.
The proof of
[
14
]
Lemma 2.2, which is equivalent to Lemma
2.1
in the case
\displaystyle\text{\rm dim}\,Y=3
,
works in arbitrary dimension.

Let us introduce a notion of contact action selector.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
be a closed contact manifold.
A
contact action selector
of
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
is a map

\displaystyle c:\Lambda(Y,\xi)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

which satisfies the following properties for any
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda(Y,\xi)
:

(i):

\displaystyle c(\lambda)\in\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)
.

(ii):

\displaystyle c(s\lambda)=sc(\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
.

(iii):

\displaystyle c(\lambda^{\prime})\geq c(\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda(Y,\xi)
such that
\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}\geq\lambda
(see Remark
1.1
).

Let
\displaystyle\text{\rm AS}(Y,\xi)
denote the set of contact action selectors of
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
.

The properties (i), (ii), and (iii) are called spectrality, conformality and monotonicity, respectively.
Any contact action selector
\displaystyle c
satisfies the following property:

(iv):

If a sequence
\displaystyle(h_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(Y)
satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\|h_{j}\|_{C^{0}}=0
,
then
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}c(e^{h_{j}}\lambda)=c(\lambda)
.

Indeed, by conformality and monotonicity,

\displaystyle e^{\min h_{j}}c(\lambda)\leq c(e^{h_{j}}\lambda)\leq e^{\max h_{j}}c(\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle j
.
The property (iv) is called
\displaystyle C^{0}
continuity.

The following lemma is essentially equivalent to the perturbation argument in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle(Y,\xi)
be a closed contact manifold and
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda(Y,\xi)
.

(i):

Suppose that, for any
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists
\displaystyle c\in\text{\rm AS}(Y,\xi)
which satisfies

\displaystyle c(e^{h}\lambda)>c(\lambda)
.
Then
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies positive strong closing property.

(ii):

Suppose that, for any
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0})\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists
\displaystyle c\in\text{\rm AS}(Y,\xi)
which satisfies

\displaystyle c(e^{h}\lambda)<c(\lambda)
.
Then
\displaystyle\lambda
satisfies negative strong closing property.

Proof
.

We only prove (i), since the proof of (ii) is similar to that.
First note that (i) is equivalent to the following:
if
\displaystyle\lambda
does not satisfy positive strong closing property,
then there exists
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})\setminus\{0\}

such that
\displaystyle c(e^{h}\lambda)=c(\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle c\in\text{\rm AS}(Y,\xi)
.

If
\displaystyle\lambda
does not satisfy positive strong closing property, there exists
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})\setminus\{0\}

such that the following holds:

\displaystyle t\in[0,1],\,\gamma\in P(Y,e^{th}\lambda)\implies\text{\rm Im}\,\gamma\cap\text{\rm supp}\,h=\emptyset
.

Let
\displaystyle\lambda_{t}:=e^{th}\lambda
.
Since
\displaystyle\lambda_{t}\equiv\lambda
on
\displaystyle Y\setminus\text{\rm supp}\,h
,
we obtain

\displaystyle P(Y,\lambda_{t})=P(Y,\lambda)
and

\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda_{t})=\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle t\in[0,1]
.
Let
\displaystyle c\in\text{\rm AS}(Y,\xi)
.
By spectrality,

\displaystyle c(\lambda_{t})\in\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda_{t})=\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)
for any
\displaystyle t
.
By
\displaystyle C^{0}
continuity,
\displaystyle c(\lambda_{t})
is continuous on
\displaystyle t
.
Since
\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)
is a null set,

\displaystyle c(\lambda_{t})
is constant on
\displaystyle t
, in particular
\displaystyle c(e^{h}\lambda)=c(\lambda)
.
∎

3.
Action selecting functors

In this section, we introduce a notion of
action selecting functor
(of dimension
\displaystyle 2n-1
and coefficients in
\displaystyle K
)
as a symmetric (strong) monoidal functor
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}\to\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
,
requiring certain conditions.

In Section 3.1, we explain the definition of
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
,
which is a category whose objects are closed
\displaystyle 2n-1
-dimensional manifolds with contact forms.
In Section 3.2, we explain the definition of
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
,
which is a category whose objects are
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded and filtered
\displaystyle K
-vector spaces.
In Section 3.3, we explain the definition of an action selecting functor.
In Section 3.4, we explain the definition of a contact action selector associated to any action selecting functor.
In Section 3.6, we introduce a subcategory
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}^{0}_{2n-1}\subset\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
to prepare for
Section 3.7, in which we explain two examples of action selecting functors
\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{\rm ECH}}
and
\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{\rm CH}}
,
which are defined respectively by ECH and (full) contact homology.

3.1.
Category
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}

Let
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. The goal of this subsection is to define a symmetric nonunital monoidal category
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
.

An object of
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
is a pair
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda)
such that
\displaystyle Y
is a closed
\displaystyle 2n-1
-dimensional manifold
and
\displaystyle\lambda
is a contact form on
\displaystyle Y
.

For any
\displaystyle(Y_{+},\lambda_{+}),(Y_{-},\lambda_{-})\in\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
,
a generalized exact cobordism from
\displaystyle(Y_{+},\lambda_{+})
to
\displaystyle(Y_{-},\lambda_{-})

is a tuple

\displaystyle(X,\lambda,i_{+},i_{-})
which satisfies the following conditions:

•

\displaystyle X
is a
\displaystyle 2n
-dimensional manifold without boundary and
\displaystyle\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(X)
.
Moreover,
\displaystyle d\lambda
is nondegenerate (thus symplectic).

•

\displaystyle i_{+}:Y_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to X
is an open embedding such that
\displaystyle i_{+}^{*}\lambda=e^{r}\lambda_{+}
, where
\displaystyle r
denotes the coordinate on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}
.

•

\displaystyle i_{-}:Y_{-}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to X
is an open embedding such that
\displaystyle i_{-}^{*}\lambda=e^{r}\lambda_{-}
.

•

\displaystyle U_{+}:=i_{+}(Y_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
and
\displaystyle U_{-}:=i_{-}(Y_{-}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{<0})
are disjoint, and
\displaystyle X\setminus(U_{+}\cup U_{-})
is compact.

Generalized exact cobordisms

\displaystyle(X,\lambda,i_{+},i_{-})
and
\displaystyle(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}_{+},i^{\prime}_{-})

are equivalent
if there exists a diffeomorphism
\displaystyle\varphi:X\to X^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle d(\varphi^{*}\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)=0
,

\displaystyle\varphi\circ i_{+}=i^{\prime}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\varphi\circ i_{-}=i^{\prime}_{-}
,
and

\text{\rm supp}\,(\varphi^{*}\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\cap(i_{+}(Y_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})\cup i_{-}(Y_{-}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\leq 0}))=\emptyset.

We define
\displaystyle\text{\rm Hom}((Y_{+},\lambda_{+}),(Y_{-},\lambda_{-}))

to be the set of equivalence classes of generalized exact cobordisms.

For any
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda),(Y^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}),(Y^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime})\in\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
, the composition

\text{\rm Hom}((Y^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}),(Y,\lambda))\times\text{\rm Hom}((Y^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}),(Y^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}))\to\text{\rm Hom}((Y^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}),(Y,\lambda));\,(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\mapsto\mu\circ\mu^{\prime}

is defined as follows. Take
\displaystyle(X,\lambda,i_{+},i_{-})
and
\displaystyle(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}_{+},i^{\prime}_{-})
so that

\mu=[(X,\lambda,i_{+},i_{-})],\qquad\mu^{\prime}=[(X^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},i^{\prime}_{+},i^{\prime}_{-})].

Let us consider an equivalence relation
\displaystyle\sim
on
\displaystyle X\sqcup X^{\prime}
generated by

\displaystyle i_{+}(y,r)\sim i^{\prime}_{-}(y,r)
, where
\displaystyle(y,r)
runs over all elements of
\displaystyle Y^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\displaystyle X^{\prime\prime}:=(X\sqcup X^{\prime})/\sim
and
\displaystyle\pi:X\sqcup X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime\prime}
be the canonical map.
Let

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime\prime}:=\pi_{!}(\lambda\sqcup\lambda^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}_{+}:=\pi\circ i^{\prime}_{+}
, and
\displaystyle i^{\prime\prime}_{-}:=\pi\circ i_{-}
.
Then we define
\displaystyle\mu\circ\mu^{\prime}:=[(X^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},i^{\prime\prime}_{+},i^{\prime\prime}_{-})]
.

For any
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda),(Y,\lambda^{\prime})\in\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}
, i.e.
there exists
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})

which satisfies
\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=e^{h}\lambda
,
we define
\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm Hom}((Y,\lambda^{\prime}),(Y,\lambda))
by

(1)

\mu_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}:=[(Y\times{\mathbb{R}},e^{r}\lambda,i_{+},i_{-})]

where
\displaystyle i_{\pm}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle i_{+}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:Y\times{\mathbb{R}}\to Y\times{\mathbb{R}};\,(y,r)\mapsto(y,h(y)+r),

\displaystyle\displaystyle i_{-}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:Y\times{\mathbb{R}}\to Y\times{\mathbb{R}};\,(y,r)\mapsto(y,r).

For any
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda)\in\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
, we define

\displaystyle\text{\rm id}_{(Y,\lambda)}:=\mu_{\lambda\lambda}
.

Now we have defined the category
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
.
It has a natural symmetric nonunital monoidal structure,
where the monoidal product is defined by taking disjoint unions.

3.2.
Category
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}

Let
\displaystyle K
be a field.
In this subsection, we define a symmetric monoidal category
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
.
We also explain a few facts about filtered vector spaces.

Let
\displaystyle V
be a vector space over
\displaystyle K
.
In this paper, a filtration on
\displaystyle V
means a family
\displaystyle(F^{a}V)_{a\in(-\infty,\infty]}
which satisfies the following conditions:

•

\displaystyle F^{0}V=\{0\}
,
\displaystyle F^{\infty}V=V
.

•

\displaystyle a\leq b\implies F^{a}V\subset F^{b}V
.

•

For any
\displaystyle a\in(-\infty,\infty]
, there holds
\displaystyle\bigcup_{b<a}F^{b}V=F^{a}V
.

When
\displaystyle V
is
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded, i.e. equipped with a direct sum decomposition
\displaystyle V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
,
we also require that

\displaystyle F^{a}V=(F^{a}V\cap V_{0})\oplus(F^{a}V\cap V_{1})
holds for any
\displaystyle a
.

For any filtered vector spaces
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle W
,
we say that a linear map
\displaystyle f:V\to W
preserves filtrations if

\displaystyle f(F^{a}V)\subset F^{a}W
for any
\displaystyle a
.

For any filtered vector space
\displaystyle V
, let

\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,(V):=\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid\forall\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},\,F^{a-\varepsilon}V\subsetneq F^{a+\varepsilon}V\}
.
For any
\displaystyle v\in V
, let
\displaystyle|v|:=\inf\{a\mid v\in F^{a}V\}
.
The following properties are easy to confirm:

•

\displaystyle\text{\rm Spec}\,(V)
is a closed subset of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.

•

If
\displaystyle a\leq b
and
\displaystyle[a,b]\cap\text{\rm Spec}\,(V)=\emptyset
, then
\displaystyle F^{a}V=F^{b}V
.

•

\displaystyle|v|\in\text{\rm Spec}\,(V)
for any
\displaystyle v\in V\setminus\{0\}
.

•

\displaystyle|0|=-\infty
.

Let us define the category
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
.
Objects of
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
are filtered and
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded
\displaystyle K
-vector spaces,
and morphisms are degree
\displaystyle 0
linear maps which preserve filtrations.
The monoidal product is defined by taking tensor products.
Namely, for any filtered vector spaces
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle W
,
the filtration on
\displaystyle V\otimes W
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle F^{a}(V\otimes W):

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\text{\rm span}\,\{F^{b}V\otimes F^{c}W\mid b+c\leq a\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\text{\rm span}\,\{v\otimes w\mid v\in V,\,w\in W,\,|v|+|w|<a\}.

Then
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
has a natural symmetric monoidal structure
with isomorphisms

V\otimes W\cong W\otimes V;\,v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{\text{\rm deg}\,(v)\text{\rm deg}\,(w)}w\otimes v,

where
deg
denotes
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-grading.

Remark 3.1
.

We also consider a subcategory
\displaystyle\text{\rm FVect}_{K}\subset\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}

which consists of
\displaystyle V\in\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
such that

\displaystyle V_{0}=V
and
\displaystyle V_{1}=0
.

Let us now explain a few facts about filtered vector spaces.

For any filtered
\displaystyle K
-vector space
\displaystyle V
, let
\displaystyle V^{\vee}:=\text{\rm Hom}_{K}(V,K)
.
For any
\displaystyle\varphi\in V^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}
, let

\displaystyle|\varphi|:=\sup\{a\mid\varphi|_{F^{a}V}=0\}
.
It is easy to see that
\displaystyle|\varphi|\in\text{\rm Spec}\,(V)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle W
be filtered
\displaystyle K
-vector spaces,
and let
\displaystyle\varphi\in V^{\vee}
.
Let us define
\displaystyle i_{\varphi}:V\otimes W\to W
by

\displaystyle i_{\varphi}(v\otimes w):=\varphi(v)\cdot w
.
If
\displaystyle\varphi\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle|i_{\varphi}(x)|\leq|x|-|\varphi|
for any
\displaystyle x\in V\otimes W
.

Proof
.

It is sufficient to show the following claim for any
\displaystyle a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

|x|<a,\,|\varphi|>b\implies|i_{\varphi}(x)|\leq a-b.

By
\displaystyle|x|<a
,
one has
\displaystyle x=\sum_{i}c_{i}\cdot v_{i}\otimes w_{i}

such that
\displaystyle\max_{i}(|v_{i}|+|w_{i}|)<a
.
Then
\displaystyle i_{\varphi}(x)=\sum_{i}c_{i}\cdot\varphi(v_{i})\cdot w_{i}
.
By
\displaystyle|\varphi|>b
,
if
\displaystyle\varphi(v_{i})\neq 0

then
\displaystyle|v_{i}|\geq b
,
which implies
\displaystyle|w_{i}|<a-b
.
Thus we obtain
\displaystyle|i_{\varphi}(x)|<a-b
.
∎

A filtered,
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded chain complex is a pair
\displaystyle(V,\partial_{V})
such that

\displaystyle V\in\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}

and
\displaystyle\partial_{V}:V_{*}\to V_{*-1}
is a linear map
which preserves the filtration.
For any such
\displaystyle(V,\partial_{V})

\displaystyle H_{*}(V)
has a filtration defined by

\displaystyle F^{a}H_{*}(V):=\{[v]\in H_{*}(V)\mid v\in F^{a}V\}
for any
\displaystyle a
,
thus
\displaystyle H_{*}(V)\in\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle(V,\partial_{V})
and
\displaystyle(W,\partial_{W})
be filtered,
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded chain complexes.
Consider the product complex
\displaystyle(V\otimes W,\partial_{V\otimes W})

which is defined by

\partial_{V\otimes W}(v\otimes w):=\partial_{V}v\otimes w+(-1)^{\text{\rm deg}\,(v)}v\otimes\partial_{W}w.

If
\displaystyle\text{\rm dim}\,F^{a}H_{*}(V),\text{\rm dim}\,F^{a}H_{*}(W)<\infty
for any
\displaystyle a\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle a\in(-\infty,\infty]

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle 1

For any

(b):

\displaystyle\lambda=e^{r}\lambda_{-}

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\lambda_{-}=\lambda_{+}

Remark 3.11
.

The identity morphisms

\displaystyle(\alpha\sqcup\beta)\sqcup\gamma\cong\alpha\sqcup(\beta\sqcup\gamma)

Here
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are objects of
\displaystyle\text{\rm Cont}^{0}_{2n-1}
,
and
\displaystyle\sqcup
denotes the monoidal product.

The next lemma is useful to define action selecting functors in practice.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\displaystyle\Phi:\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}\to\text{\rm FVect}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}_{K}
be an action selecting functor.
For any
\displaystyle(Y,\lambda)\in\text{\rm Cont}_{2n-1}
and
\displaystyle a\in(-\infty,\infty]
,

\varinjlim_{h}F^{a}\Phi(Y,e^{h}\lambda)\to F^{a}\Phi(Y,\lambda)

is an isomorphism, where
\displaystyle h
runs over elements of
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
such that
\displaystyle e^{h}\lambda
is nondegenerate.

Proof
.

If
\displaystyle a\leq 0
then the assertion is obvious.
In the case
\displaystyle a>0
, we may assume that
\displaystyle a\notin\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)\cup\{\infty\}

because this assumption can be dropped by taking limits.
It is sufficient to show that
\displaystyle F^{a}\Phi(Y,e^{h}\lambda)\to F^{a}\Phi(Y,\lambda)
is an isomorphism
for any
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
such that
\displaystyle\|h\|_{C^{2}}
is sufficiently small.

Since
\displaystyle a\notin\text{\rm Spec}\,_{+}(Y,\lambda)\cup\{\infty\}
, there exists
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
(depending on
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle\lambda
) such that,
if
\displaystyle h\in C^{\infty}(Y,{\mathbb{R}}_{>0})
satisfies
\displaystyle\|h\|_{C^{2}}<\varepsilon

is an action selecting functor,

are isomorphisms. Then
\displaystyle F^{a}\Phi(Y,e^{h}\lambda)\to F^{a}\Phi(Y,\lambda)
is also an isomorphism.

5.
Boundaries of symplectic ellipsoids

For any
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, let

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\mid 0<a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}\}

For any
\displaystyle a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in A_{n}
, let

E_{a}:=\bigg\{(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\biggm|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(q_{i}^{2}+p_{i}^{2})}{a_{i}}\leq 1\bigg\}.

Recall that
\displaystyle\lambda_{2n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}dq_{i}-q_{i}dp_{i}}{2}
.
Then
\displaystyle\lambda_{2n}|_{\partial E_{a}}
is a contact form on
\displaystyle\partial E_{a}
with the Reeb flow

\displaystyle R_{\lambda_{2n}|_{\partial E_{a}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2\pi}{a_{i}}\cdot(p_{i}\partial_{q_{i}}-q_{i}\partial_{p_{i}})
.

As a potential application of Theorem
4.5
, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1
.

For any
\displaystyle n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
\displaystyle a\in A_{n}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2n}|_{\partial E_{a}}
satisfies strong closing property.

A recent paper
[
3
]
proposed a proof of Conjecture
5.1

(see also
[
4
]
which proved a strong closing property for “
\displaystyle\gamma
-rigid” Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms and deduced Conjecture
5.1
from this result).
The strategy of
[
3
]
is to verify a condition called “vanishing of the contact homology spectral gap”,
which is a version of (
10

\displaystyle\Phi=\Phi^{\text{\rm CH}}
.
To prove this condition for all ellipsoids,
the first step is to prove a stronger (effective) version of the condition for the periodic case (i.e. the case where all Reeb orbits are periodic),
and the second step is to prove the condition for the general case by an approximation argument using Dirichlet’s approximation theorem.

In an old version of the present paper

(i):

\displaystyle i\geq 1

\displaystyle d_{1}=0

\displaystyle(d_{i})_{i\geq 1}

Now we claim that a basis
\displaystyle(v_{k})_{k}

\displaystyle V

Suppose that (
\displaystyle\star

,

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle d_{i}\leq k

\displaystyle|v_{k}|=a_{i}

\displaystyle\text{\rm dim}\,F^{a}V>k\iff a>a_{i}

On the other hand, suppose that a basis

(ii):

(b) follows from (

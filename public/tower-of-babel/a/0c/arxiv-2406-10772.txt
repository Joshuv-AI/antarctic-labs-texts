On the maximal L1 influence of real-valued boolean functions
Andrew J. Young, Henry D. Pfister

Abstract
We show that any sequence of well-behaved (e.g. bounded and non-constant) real-valued functions of n n boolean variables { f n } \{f_{n}\} admits a sequence of coordinates whose L 1 L^{1} influence under the p p -biased distribution, for any p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) p\in(0,1) , is Ω ⁡ ( var ⁡ ( f n ) ​ ln ⁡ n n ) \Omega(\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}) .



1
Introduction

The celebrated KKL result of Kahn, Kalai and Linial
[
1
]
shows that any boolean-valued function of
n
boolean variables has a variable whose influence is
\Omega\big(\mathrm{var}(f)\frac{\ln(n)}{n}\big)
which is a factor
\ln(n)
larger than predicted.
The definition of influence in this result is the classic one and has many equivalent formulations.
The definition we use is introduced in the next paragraph and discussed in Section
3
.
Some related work
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
has sought to generalize the class of applicable functions, in particular the domain, using a variety of definitions for influence, e.g. geometric.

We provide a similar result for the
L^{1}
influence of real-valued boolean functions (Theorem
1
), where the
L^{p}
influence
of variable
i
equals the
L^{p}
norm to the
p
-th power of the difference between the function and its average over the
i
-th coordinate.
This has implications for sharp thresholds (e.g. see
[
8
,
9
]
) that follow from Rossignol’s generalization of a lemma
[
10
]
due to Margulis and Russo
[
11
,
12
]
.
The proof is based on a variation of the hypercontractivity theorem for
p
-biased measures studied by Talagrand
[
8
]
(Corollary
3
).

More recently, Kelman et al.
[
13
]
analyze
L^{1}
influences to provide variations of several well known theorems.
Their results hold for bounded functions and
p=1/2
.
In contrast, our results apply to a wider class of functions and any
0<p<1
.
Moreover, our proof technique is quite different.

The final result is of an auxiliary nature and, as such, some details are omitted.
It concerns a converse, up to a small multiplicative factor, to Theorem
1
achieved by a well known sequence of boolean functions, the tribes functions of Ben-Or and Linial
[
14
]
.
While this result is well known, we include if for completeness and to demonstrate the tightness of the constant in Theorem
1
.

2
Main result

We state our main Theorem and some direct consequences.
Some standard definitions are deferred to the subsequent sections.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu
be the
p
-biased measure,
f_{n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
f_{n}^{(i)}=f_{n}-E_{i}\left[f_{n}\right]
.
If
\mathrm{var}(f_{n})
is strictly positive and
o(n^{\varepsilon})
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\max_{i}\lVert f_{n}^{(i)}\rVert_{1}}{\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{M_{0}},

where

M_{0}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max_{i:f_{n}^{(i)}\neq 0}\frac{\lVert f_{n}^{(i)}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\lVert f_{n}^{(i)}\rVert_{1}}\qquad C_{0}=\sup_{\alpha>0}\frac{\tanh\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}{\alpha-\ln\rho_{2}(\alpha)^{2}},

\rho_{2}(\alpha)=\rho\left(e^{\alpha}+1\right)
and
\rho
is any of the smoothing parameters in Theorem
2
.

If
M_{0}
happens to be
0
, then the constant
C_{0}/M_{0}
is interpreted as infinity and the RHS of Theorem
1
can be taken to be any desired nonnegative constant.

For
p=1/2
, it is known that the constant in the original KKL theorem can be improved to
1/2
(see e.g.
[
15
]
Exercise 9.30).
This is a direct corollary because the
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
influences coincide for boolean functions giving
M_{0}=1
and
C_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
when
p=\frac{1}{2}
.
Moreover, by letting
\alpha=1
and applying Hölder’s inequality, we see that one implication of Theorem
1
is

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\max_{i}\lVert f_{n}^{(i)}\rVert_{1}}{\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}}\geq\frac{9}{20}\frac{1}{\sup_{n}\lVert f_{n}\rVert_{\infty}}\frac{1}{1+\left\lvert\ln\frac{p}{1-p}\right\rvert},

(1)

where
\rho
has the form of item
(iii)
in Theorem
2
.

3
Fourier analysis on the
p
-biased hypercube

The domain of most functions is the Cartesian product of
\{-1,1\}
, and, typically, we assign
-1
weight
1-p
and
1
weight
p
.
Such functions whose range is the real numbers will be referred to as
real-valued boolean functions
.
For any
n
, the functions
\tau_{i}^{\pm}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{-1,1\}^{n}
fix the
i
-th coordinate to be
1
for
+
and
-1
for
-
and operate as the identity on all remaining coordinates.
Given a measure
\mu
, we use
\int\cdot\,d\mu
and
E\left[\cdot\right]
interchangeably.
Moreover,
E_{i}\left[\cdot\right]
is integration over only the
i
-th coordinate, the
L^{p}
norms are defined in the usual way (i.e.
\lVert f\rVert_{q}^{q}=\int\lvert f\rvert^{q}\,d\mu
) and the
L^{q}
influence of the
i
-th coordinate is
\lVert f-E_{i}\left[f\right]\rVert_{q}^{q}
.

Let
\mu_{i}
be the measure on
\{-1,1\}
with
E_{i}\left[x_{i}\right]=2p_{i}-1
for some
0<p_{i}<1
and
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mu_{n}
.
The canonical orthonormal basis is

\chi_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_{i},

where

\chi_{i}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\left(x_{i}-E_{i}\left[x_{i}\right]\right)

and

\sigma_{i}^{2}=E_{i}\left[\left(x_{i}-E_{i}\left[x_{i}\right]\right)^{2}\right]

is the variance.
More explicitly,

\chi_{i}(x)=\begin{cases}-\sqrt{\frac{p_{i}}{1-p_{i}}}&\text{if }x_{i}=-1,\\
\sqrt{\frac{1-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}&\text{if }x_{i}=1.\end{cases}

Every real-valued boolean function has a Fourier expansion

f=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\hat{f}(S)\chi_{S},

where
[n]
is
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and

\hat{f}(S)=\left<f,\chi_{S}\right>=\sum_{x\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}f(x)\chi_{S}(x)\mu(x)

are the Fourier coefficients.
By Parseval’s theorem the variance of such a function is the sum of all its squared Fourier coefficients not indexed by the empty set.
The smoothing operator
T_{\delta}
acts on real-valued boolean functions as

T_{\delta}f:=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\delta^{\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)\chi_{S}.

If
p_{i}=p
for all
i
, then
\mu
is called the
p
-biased measure
.

The following Theorem and its immediate Corollary provide the necessary
p
-biased variation of the hypercontractivity theorem for our purposes.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mu
be the
p
-biased measure and
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then for all
q\geq 2

\lVert T_{\gamma}f\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{2},

where
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-1}}\rho(q)
and
\rho(q)
is any one of the following:

i)

\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}};

ii)

\sqrt{q-1}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh\left(-\frac{1}{q}\ln\left(\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)\right)}{\sinh\left(-\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\ln\left(\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right)\right)}};

iii)

\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}},

where
\lambda=\min\{p,1-p\}
.

Proof.

(i)
See
[
15
, Chapter 10]
.

(ii)
See
[
16
]
.

(iii)

Suppose
q=\infty
.
Then

\lVert T_{0}f\rVert_{\infty}=\lvert E\left[f\right]\rvert\leq\lVert f\rVert_{1}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

Suppose
q
is finite.
Let

\nu_{S}(x)=\rho^{\lvert S\rvert}\chi_{S}(x)

and
m
be the uniform measure on
\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Then, Fubini’s theorem implies that

\displaystyle\lVert T_{\delta}f\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int\lVert T_{\delta}f\rVert_{q}^{q}\,dm(y)

\displaystyle=\int\int\left|\sum_{S}\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{q-1}}\right)^{\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)\chi_{S}(x)\right|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\,dm(y)

\displaystyle=\int\int\left|\sum_{S}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-1}}\right)^{\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)\nu_{S}(x)y^{S}\right|^{q}\,d\mu(x)\,dm(y)

\displaystyle=\int\int\left|\sum_{S}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-1}}\right)^{\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)\nu_{S}(x)y^{S}\right|^{q}\,dm(y)\,d\mu(x).

By standard hypercontractivity, for the uniform measure applied to the function with uniform Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}(S)\nu_{S}(x)
,
[
17
]
and Parseval’s theorem

\displaystyle\int\left|\sum_{S}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-1}}\right)^{\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)\nu_{S}(x)y^{S}\right|^{q}\,dm(y)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{S}\hat{f}(S)^{2}\nu_{S}(x)^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{S}\hat{f}(S)^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle=\lVert f\rVert_{2}^{q},

where, by definition of
\rho
,

\lvert\nu_{S}(x)\rvert=\rho^{\lvert S\rvert}\prod_{i\in S}\lvert\chi_{i}(x)\rvert\leq\rho^{\lvert S\rvert}\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{p}{1-p}},\sqrt{\frac{1-p}{p}}\right\}^{\lvert S\rvert}=1.

∎

By
[
16
]
, item
(ii)
is optimal.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mu
be the
p
-biased measure and
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then for all
0\leq\delta\leq 1

\lVert T_{\rho_{1}(\delta)\delta}f\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{1+\delta^{2}},

where
\rho_{1}(\delta)=\rho\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+1\right)
.

Proof.

Since
\delta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q-1}}
for some
q\geq 2
, we choose
q
to satisfy this equality.
Let
\gamma=\rho(q)\delta=\rho\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+1\right)\delta
.
Then by Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\lVert T_{\gamma}f\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left<T_{\gamma^{2}}f,f\right>

\displaystyle\leq\lVert T_{\gamma^{2}}f\rVert_{q}\lVert f\rVert_{1+\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=\lVert T_{\gamma}\left(T_{\gamma}f\right)\rVert_{q}\lVert f\rVert_{1+\frac{1}{q-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert T_{\gamma}f\rVert_{2}\lVert f\rVert_{1+\frac{1}{q-1}}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu
be the
p
-biased measure,
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
f_{i}=f-E_{i}\left[f\right]
.
Then, for all
i
, we have

i)

\hat{f_{i}}(S)=\begin{cases}\hat{f}(S)&\text{if }S\ni i,\\
0&\text{otherwise};\end{cases}

ii)

f_{i}=\frac{\sigma}{2}\left(f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right)\chi_{i}.

Proof.

(i)

E_{i}\left[\chi_{S}\right]=\begin{cases}\chi_{S}&S\not\ni i\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}.

(ii)

\chi_{S}\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-\chi_{S}\circ\tau_{i}^{-}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{\sigma}\chi_{S\backslash\{i\}}&S\ni i\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}.

∎

The function
\rho(q)

\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i\ni S}\hat{f}(S)^{2}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad I=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\lvert S\rvert\hat{f}(S)^{2}.

By assumption

Therefore, by letting

\varepsilon\mathrm{var}(f)=\mathrm{var}(f)-\frac{1}{b}\leq\nu(\mathcal{B}).

For all

\sum_{i\ni S}\left(\rho_{1}(\delta)\delta\right)^{2\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)^{2}=\lVert T_{\rho_{1}(\delta)\delta}f_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{1+\delta^{2}}^{2}.

\displaystyle\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{1+\gamma}^{1+\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\lvert f_{i}\rvert^{1+\gamma}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{1}\left(\frac{\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\lVert f_{i}\rVert_{1}}\right)^{\gamma}

where

Taking this to the

Thus, letting

\displaystyle\sum_{S}\lvert S\rvert e^{-(\alpha-\ln\rho_{2}(\alpha)^{2})\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{i\ni S}e^{-(\alpha-\ln\rho_{2}(\alpha)^{2})\lvert S\rvert}\hat{f}(S)^{2}

Lemma 5
.

For any sequence

.

(ii)

There exists

5
Large derivatives

We use a result of Rossignol to relate the derivative of the expectation to the sum of
L^{1}
influences.

Lemma 6
.

[
10
]

Let
\mu
be the
p
-biased measure and
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then

\frac{d}{dp}E\left[f\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}E\left[f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right].

Proof.

Let

\mu(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}(x),

where

\mu_{i}(x)=\frac{2p-1}{2}(x_{i}+1)+1-p.

Then

\frac{d}{dp}\mu(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{d}{dp}\mu_{i}(x)\right)\prod_{j\neq i}\mu_{j}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}\mu_{j}(x).

∎

For any
n
, an element
\pi
of the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
x\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
by
[\pi(x)]_{i}=x_{\pi(i)}
.
Given a real-valued boolean function
f
its
symmetry group

\mathcal{G}
is the following subset of
S_{n}

\mathcal{G}:=\{\pi\in S_{n}:f\circ\pi=f\}.

The function
f
is said to be
symmetric
if
\mathcal{G}
is transitive, i.e. for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
there exists
\pi\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\pi(i)=j
.
The boolean domain is endowed with the standard partial order,
x\preceq y
if
x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all
i
.
We call a function
monotone
(or more precisely monotone increasing) if
x\preceq y
implies
f(x)\leq f(y)
.

Combining with Lemma
4
, for any monotone function

\frac{d}{dp}E\left[f\right]\geq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert f-E_{i}\left[f\right]\rVert_{1}.

If
f
is symmetric then the sum on the RHS is constant in
i
, i.e. given
i
and
j
choose
\pi\in\mathcal{G}
with
\pi(i)=j
then

f\circ\tau_{i}^{\pm}=(f\circ\pi)\circ\tau_{i}^{\pm}=(f\circ\tau_{j}^{\pm})\circ\pi=f\circ\tau_{j}^{\pm}.

Therefore, for a sequence of non-constant monotone symmetric functions all bounded by
b

\frac{d}{dp}E\left[f_{n}\right]\geq\frac{9}{10b}\frac{1}{1+\left\lvert\ln\frac{p}{1-p}\right\rvert}\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\ln n.

5.1
Conditions implying monotonicity

Let
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{n}
where each
\mu_{i}
is the measure with
E_{i}\left[x_{i}\right]=2p_{i}-1
and

g(\underline{p})=E\left[f\right]

for some
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

For
n=1

g(p)=(1-p)f(-1)+pf(1)\qquad g^{\prime}(p)=f(1)-f(-1).

Thus
g\geq 0
if and only if
f\geq 0
and
g^{\prime}\geq 0
if and only if
f
is monotone.

For the general case
g\geq 0
if and only if
f\geq 0
, evaluate
g
at e.g.
\underline{1}
for
f(\underline{1})
.
Moreover,

E_{i}\left[f\right]=(1-p_{i})f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}+p_{i}f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}=f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}+p_{i}(f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}),

where
f\circ\tau_{i}^{\pm}
are independent of
x_{i}
.
Thus

E\left[f\right]=E\left[f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right]+p_{i}E\left[f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right]

and

\frac{\partial}{\partial_{i}p_{i}}g(\underline{p})=E\left[f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right].

Hence
f
is monotone if and only if, for all
i
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial_{i}}g(\underline{p})\geq 0
.

5.2
Weak conditions

The combination of full monotonicity and symmetry is a rather strong condition.
It suffices to show that there exists
\gamma>0
such that eventually

\sum_{i=1}^{n}E\left[f_{n}\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f_{n}\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right]\geq\gamma n\max_{i}\lVert f_{n}\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f_{n}\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\rVert_{1}.

This can be decomposed into a weak monotonicity and weak symmetry condition as follows.
A real-valued boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
weakly monotone
if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}E\left[f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\right]\geq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\rVert_{1}.

Similarly, a real-valued boolean function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
weakly symmetric
if there exists
\beta>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lVert f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\rVert_{1}\geq\beta n\max_{i}\lVert f\circ\tau_{i}^{+}-f\circ\tau_{i}^{-}\rVert_{1}.

A sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}
is
weakly monotone (symmetric)
if
f_{n}
is eventually weakly monotone (symmetric) for some fixed
\alpha
(
\beta
).
It should be noted that
E\left[\cdot\right]
,
\lVert\cdot\lVert_{1}
are implicit functions of
p
.

6
Tribes

Recall the tribes
[
14
]
boolean function defined as the logical OR of a collection of disjoint logical ANDs.
This function is known to demonstrate the tightness of the original KKL.
As Theorem
1
includes boolean functions, this applies similarly, and we provided the explicit calculations for completeness.

Consider the tribes boolean function with
\frac{n}{\ell}
equal-sized tribes of size
\ell
.
Then, the influences satisfy

I_{i}=2^{-(\ell-1)}\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)^{\frac{n}{\ell}-1}

for all
i
with variance

4\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)^{\frac{n}{\ell}}\left(1-\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)^{\frac{n}{\ell}}\right).

Thus, the ratio of influence to variance is

2^{-(\ell-1)}\frac{1}{4\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)\left(1-\left(1-2^{-\ell}\right)^{\frac{n}{\ell}}\right)}.

Let
n=m2^{m}
and
f_{n}
be the boolean function defined by uniform tribes of size
\ell=m
.
Then

I_{i}(f_{n})=2^{-(m-1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)^{2^{m}-1},

where

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)^{2^{m}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{e}

and

\frac{2^{-m}}{\frac{\log n}{n}}=\frac{2^{-m}}{\frac{m+\log m}{m2^{m}}}=\frac{m}{m+\log m}=1+o(1).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{I_{i}(f_{n})}{\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\log e}{4\left(1-\frac{1}{e}\right)+o(1)}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log e}{1-\frac{1}{e}}.

Let
n=m2^{m+k}
, for a fixed
k
, and
f_{n}
be the boolean function defined by uniform tribes of size
\ell=m
.
Then

I_{i}(f_{n})=2^{-(m-1)}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)^{2^{m+k}-1},

where

\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\right)^{2^{m+k}}\rightarrow e^{-2^{k}}

and

\frac{2^{-m}}{\frac{\log n}{n}}=\frac{2^{-m}}{\frac{m+k+\log m}{m2^{m+k}}}=2^{k}\frac{m}{m+k+\log m}=2^{k}(1+o(1)).

Thus

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{I_{i}(f_{n})}{\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}2^{k}\frac{\log e}{1-e^{-2^{k}}}.

Letting
k
tend to
-\infty
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
f_{n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
such that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{I_{i}(f_{n})}{\mathrm{var}(f_{n})\frac{\ln n}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\log e+\varepsilon.

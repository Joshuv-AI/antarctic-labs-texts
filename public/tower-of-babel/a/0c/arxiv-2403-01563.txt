Abstract
. The k k -representation number of a graph G G is the minimum cardinality of the system of vertex subsets with the property that every edge of G G is covered at least k k times while every non-edge is covered at most ( k − 1 ) (k-1) times. In particular, for k = 1 k=1 this notion is equivalent to the clique number of a graph G G . Extending results of [ 9 ] and [ 6 ] we study the k k -representation number of G ⁡ ( n , 1 / 2 ) G(n,1/2) . As a tool, we will prove a sharp concentration result counting the number of induced subgraphs of G ⁡ ( n , 1 / 2 ) G(n,1/2) with density ( 1 2 + α ) (\frac{1}{2}+\alpha) . In Lemma 3.7 , we will show that the number of such subgraphs is close to its expected value with probability 1 − exp ⁡ ( − n C ) 1-\exp(-n^{C}) .



1.
Introduction

A
set representation
of a graph
G
consists of a set
S
along with a family
(S_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
, of subsets of
S
indexed by the vertices of
G
, such that

\displaystyle|S_{v}\cap S_{u}|\geq 1\text{ if and only if }uv\text{ is an edge of }G.

We say that
S

represents

G
if there exists such a family of subsets of
S
to form a set representation of
G
. The
representation number

\theta(G)
is the smallest cardinality of a set
S
that represents
G
.

The representation number of
G
depends on the structure of
G
and has been of interest for a long time. A classical result due to Erdős, Goodman and Pósa
[
8
]
from 1966 states that
\theta(G)\leq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
for any graph
G
. These authors also established a connection between the set representation of a graph and the clique covering of a graph. A collection
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
of cliques that are subgraphs of
G
is called a clique cover of
G
if every edge is contained in one of the cliques in
\mathcal{C}
. The clique covering number of
G
, denoted by
cc(G)
is defined as minimum cardinality of a clique cover of
G
. It was shown in
[
8
]
that
cc(G)=\theta(G)
.

Since then the parameter
\theta(G)
or equivalently
cc(G)
has been studied for various graphs. Alon
[
1
]
gave an upper bound for
\theta(G)
for graphs whose complements have bounded degree, and a lower bound for the same was given by Eaton and the second author
[
7
]
. Subsequently, Bollobás, Erdős, Spencer and West
[
3
]
studied
\theta(G)
for the random graph. The Erdős–Renyi random graph on
n
vertices is denoted by
G(n,p)
. It is a standard fact that in
G(n,1/2)
, the size of the largest clique is of the order of
\Theta(\log n)
, and hence each clique can cover at most
\Theta(\log^{2}n)
edges. Consequently,
\theta(G(n,1/2))=\Omega(n^{2}/\log^{2}n)
with high probability. It was shown in
[
3
]
that
\theta(G(n,1/2))=O(n^{2}\log\log n/\log^{2}n)
with high probability. This was subsequently improved by Frieze and Reed
[
9
]
, Guo, Patton and Warnke
[
11
]
, see also
[
13
]
, to show that:

\displaystyle\theta(G(n,1/2))=\Theta\left(\frac{n^{2}}{\log^{2}n}\right),

holds with high probability. The more general concept of
k
-representation where
k
is a positive integer, has been studied by a number of authors
[
6
,
2
,
4
,
10
]
.

A
k
-representation
of a graph
G
consists of a set
S
with a family of subsets of
S
indexed by the vertices of
G
,
(S_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
such that

\displaystyle|S_{v}\cap S_{u}|\geq k\text{ if and only if }uv\text{ is an edge of }G

We say that
S
represents

G
if there exists such a family of subsets of
S
to form a set representation of
G
. The
k
- representation number

\theta_{k}(G)
is the smallest cardinality of a set
S
that represents
G
. In particular,
\theta_{1}(G)
is the representation number
\theta(G)
.

Chung and West
[
4
]
, and Eaton, Gould and the second author
[
2
]
showed that
\theta_{k}(K_{n,n})=\Omega(n^{2}/k)
, and then Füredi
[
10
]
, showed that
\theta_{k}(K_{n,n})=O(n^{2}/k)
. Eaton and Grable
[
6
]
studied the order of magnitude of
\theta_{k}
for the random graph
G(n,1/2)
. They showed that there exist absolute constants
A_{1},A_{2}>0
such that,

\displaystyle\frac{A_{1}n^{2}}{k^{4}\log^{2}n}\leq\theta_{k}(G(n,1/2))\leq\frac{A_{2}n^{2}}{\log^{2}n},

(1)

with high probability. While the upper bound follows from
[
9
]
by using the fact that
\theta_{k}(G)\leq\theta_{1}(G)+(k-1)
, they give a counting argument for the lower bound.

We can see that as
k
gets large, there is a considerable gap in the lower and upper bounds for
\theta_{k}(G(n,1/2))
in
1
. The main result of this paper is to shorten this gap for larger values of
k
. In fact, we provide an alternative proof for the lower bound for
\theta_{k}(G(n,1/2))
that improves it by a factor of
k
and improve the upper bound given for large values of
k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist absolute positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{1}=n_{1}(\varepsilon)
, such that if
n\geq n_{1}
and
1<k<n^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{k}(G(n,1/2))\geq\frac{c_{1}n^{2}}{k^{3}\log^{2}n}

with high probability and if
(\log\log n)^{1/\varepsilon}\leq k\leq\log n
,

\displaystyle\theta_{k}(G(n,1/2))\leq\frac{c_{2}n^{2}}{k^{1-4\varepsilon}\log^{2}n}

with high probability.

The proof for the upper bound for Theorem
1.1
uses a lemma (Lemma
3.7
) that counts the number of induced “quasicliques” in
G(n,1/2)
- subgraphs of size
c\alpha^{-2}\log n
with density
(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)
, where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Roughly speaking, it shows that with exponentially high probability, the number of induced “quasicliques” is within a factor of
(1\pm n^{-A\alpha^{2}})
of the expected value, where
A>0
is an absolute constant and
\alpha\geq(\log n)^{-1/2}
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we restate the problem of
k
-representing a graph as a covering problem, define the notation used, and state the concentration inequalities used in the paper. In Section
3
, we state the main Lemmas used to prove Theorem
1.1
. Sections
4
–
8
are devoted to proving these Lemmas.

2.
Equivalent Statement, Notation and Concentration Inequalities

2.1.
Equivalence with the condition of
k
-covering

Recall that the clique covering number
cc(G)
is equal to the representation number of
\theta(G)=\theta_{1}(G)
. We will now define an analogous parameter for
\theta_{k}(G)
.

Definition 2.1

(
k
-cover)
.

Given a graph
G
and a multiset
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
of subsets of
V(G)
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
k
-cover of
G
if every edge of
G
is contained in at least
k
-elements of
\mathcal{C}
and every non-edge is contained in at most
k-1
elements of
\mathcal{C}
. The
k
-covering number
\Phi_{k}(G)
is the smallest cardinality of a
k
-cover.

Notice that
\Phi_{1}(G)=cc(G)
. We now establish the following fact.

Fact 2.2
.

For any graph
G
,
\theta_{k}(G)=\Phi_{k}(G)
.

Proof.

We prove this by establishing a correspondence between
k
-covers of
G
and
k
-representations of a graph
G
. For any
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
which is a multiset of vertex subsets of
G
, and a vertex
u
of
G
, let:

S_{u}:=\{i\in[m]:u\in C_{i}\}.

We say
(S_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
is a family of subsets of
[m]
corresponding to
\mathcal{C}
. Conversely, given a family
(S_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
of
[m]
, and
i\in[m]
, let

C_{i}:=\{v\in V(G):i\in S_{v}\}.

We say
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
is a multiset of subsets of
V(G)
corresponding to
(S_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
.

Note that if
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
corresponds to
(S_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
, then
(S_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
corresponds to
\mathcal{C}
, and further, for any subset of vertices
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(G)
and any
i\in[m]
we have that:

\{u,v\}\subseteq C_{i}\iff i\in S_{u}\cap S_{v}.

Consequently, there is a bijection between
k
-covers of
G
and
k
-representations and this implies
\theta_{k}(G)=\Phi_{k}(G)
.
∎

For the rest of the paper, we will consider the quantity
\Phi_{k}(G)
, and prove our statements about
k
-
covers
. As an example, note that we have
\Phi_{k}(G)\leq\Phi_{1}(G)+(k-1)
, because given any
clique-cover
, one can add
k-1
copies of
V(G)
to it, to form a
k
-cover
. This gives the upper bound in (
1
).

2.2.
Notation:

Let
G
be a graph and
W\subseteq V(G)
be a fixed subset of vertices. Given a multiset
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
of subsets of

, let
\mathcal{C}(W)
denote the multiset of

that contain

. The cardinality

of a multiset will always be the number of elements in it counted with multiplicity. As an example, if
\mathcal{C}
is a
k
-cover of
G
, for every edge

,
|\mathcal{C}(e)|\geq k
and for every non-edge
f
,
|\mathcal{C}(f)|<k
. We will use
E(G)
and
E(\overline{G})
to denote edges and non-edges of
G
respectively, and

to denote the number of edges induced by

. We use the notation

to denote
a-b\leq x\leq a+b
where
0>b>a
. Finally, for the rest of the paper, we will use
c
and
A
to denote fixed absolute constants where,

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{100\log 2};\qquad A=10^{-4}c.

2.3.
Concentration Inequalities

Throughout the paper, at various times, we use the following standard concentration inequalities, which we state for easier reference. These are available in many textbooks (see for eg. Chapter 2 of Random Graphs by Janson, Luczak and Ruciński
[
12
]
).

Lemma 2.3

(Chernoff Bounds)
.

Let
X
be a random variable with either binomial or hypergeometric distribution. Then for every
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\mathbb{P}(|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[X])<2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}[X]}{3}\right).

(2)

Further, if
\lambda>7\mathbb{E}[X]
, we have the stronger form:

\mathbb{P}(X>\lambda)<\exp(-\lambda).

(3)

Lemma 2.4

(Azuma Hoeffding Inequality)
.

Let
X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be a martingale satisfying:

|X_{k}-X_{k-1}|\leq c_{k},

for all
1\leq k\leq n
. Then, we have that:

\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}-X_{0}|>\lambda)<2\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}^{2}}\right).

(4)

Proof of Theorem

We use the following folklore property of

Definition 3.1

(Property

)
.

We say that

if for every subset of vertices

, the number of edges induced by

,

\displaystyle e(X)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{|X|}}\right){|X|\choose 2}.

(5)

Fact 3.2
.

G(n,1/2)
satisfies the Property

with high probability.

The proof of the lower bound in Theorem

will be a consequence of Fact

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

such that for integers

and

and

, if

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(G)\geq\frac{c_{1}n^{2}}{k^{3}\log^{2}n},

3.2.
Upper Bound:

Now we will formulate the statements and definitions needed for the proof. Recall that,
c,A
are absolute constants with values,

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{100\log 2};\qquad A=10^{-4}c.

As noticed before, the
1
-cover of

is the same as a clique cover. Thus for the case of

, the approach for the upper bound in
[
13
,
9
]
, considered a covering of
G(n,1/2)
by a collection of cliques of size

chosen by a nibble type method.

To construct a
k
-cover, (in view of Definition

), we need to find a collection
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of vertices of

, such that each edge is contained in more members of

than each non-edge. “Typical” members of our choice for

will be subsets of

(where

are chosen depending on

Definition 3.4
.

A graph on
t

\alpha
-quasiclique if it has
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha\right){t\choose 2}
edges and every vertex has degree
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha\pm\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)t
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a graph
G
,
\alpha>0
and positive integer
t
,
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{\alpha,t}
is the collection of all subsets of
V(G)
that induce
\alpha
-quasicliques of size
t
.

Whenever we use
\mathcal{Q}_{G}^{\alpha,t}
, the choice of
\alpha
and
t
will be clear and so we will omit these parameters and denote the collection by
\mathcal{Q}_{G}
.

As stated above, as a first step for the proof of the upper bound, we define a suitable pseudorandom property for
G(n,1/2)
.

Definition 3.6

(
(\alpha,t)
-good graphs)
.

Given
\alpha>0
and integer
t
and
T=(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha){t\choose 2}
, we say that
G
on
n
vertices is
(\alpha,t)
-good if for every
e\in E(G)
and
f\in E(\overline{G})
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}_{G}(e)|

\displaystyle=(1\pm n^{-A\alpha^{2}}){n-2\choose t-2}{{{t\choose 2}-1}\choose T-1}{2^{1-{t\choose 2}}},

(6)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}_{G}(f)|

\displaystyle=(1\pm n^{-A\alpha^{2}}){n-2\choose t-2}{{{t\choose 2}-1}\choose T}{2^{1-{t\choose 2}}}.

(7)

Further,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}_{G}|

\displaystyle=(1\pm n^{-A\alpha^{2}}){n\choose t}{{{t\choose 2}}\choose T}{2^{-{t\choose 2}}}.

(8)

Observe that, given
\alpha>0
and
t>0
, if
G
is
(\alpha,t)
-good, then, in view of (
6
) and (
7
), for every edge
e
and every non-edge
f
,
|\mathcal{Q}_{G}(e)|/|\mathcal{Q}(f)|
is roughly equal to
(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha)/(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha)
which is slightly larger than 1. Hence, every edge is covered by more
\alpha
-quasicliques of

than every non-edge. The following lemma shows that
G(n,1/2)
is
(\alpha,t)
-good with high probability.

Lemma 3.7

(Counting Lemma)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{1}:=n_{1}(\varepsilon)
, such that whenever
\alpha>0
and integers

, satisfy

\displaystyle n\geq n_{1};\qquad\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right)^{1/2-\varepsilon}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2};\qquad t=c\alpha^{-2}\log n,

(9)

G(n,1/2)
is
(\alpha,t)
-good with probability at least

.

To prove the upper bound of Theorem

, for any

-good

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}(f)]<k<\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}(e)],

Lemma 3.8
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists integer
n_{1}:=n_{1}(\varepsilon)
such that whenever

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(G)\leq\frac{c_{2}n^{2}}{k^{1-4\varepsilon}\log^{2}n},

.

We prove Lemma

in Section

and Lemma

in Sections

.

Fix

. For the lower bound, observe that Fact

4.
Proof of Lower Bound

We give a proof for of Fact
3.2
for completeness. Recall that, we say that
G
on
n
vertices satisfies property
\mathscr{P}_{1}
if for every subset of vertices
X
, the number of edges induced by

\displaystyle e(X)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{|X|}}\right){|X|\choose 2}.

(10)

Proof of Fact
3.2
.

Let
X\subseteq V=V(G(n,1/2))
. Then

is a binomial random variable with,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e(X)]=\frac{1}{2}{|X|\choose 2}.

Let
\varepsilon=4\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{|X|}}
. Observe that (

) holds trivially as long as
\varepsilon\geq 1
, i.e
|X|\leq 16\log n
. Thus we assume,
|X|>16\log n
and thus

. By (

), we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|e(X)-\mathbb{E}[e(X)]|>\varepsilon\mathbb{E}[e(X)])

\displaystyle<2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\mathbb{E}[e(X)]}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{16\log n}{3|X|}\cdot\frac{|X|(|X|-1)}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{4}{3}\log n\cdot(|X|-1)\right)<2n^{-\frac{4}{3}(|X|-1)}.

Thus for large enough

, the probability that there is a set of vertices

with size greater than

and,

\displaystyle\left|e(X)-\frac{1}{2}{|X|\choose 2}\right|>2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{|X|}}{|X|\choose 2},

is at most:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subseteq V\\
|X|\geq 16\log n\end{subarray}}2n^{-\frac{4}{3}(|X|-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=\lceil 16\log n\rceil}^{n}{n\choose x}\cdot 2n^{-\frac{4}{3}(x-1)}=o(1).

Thus

satisfies property

with high probability.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{x}}\right){x\choose 2}\subseteq\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm 3\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{x}}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{2}.

4.1.
Definition of Fractional Pseudocover.

In this section we will study a few properties of be a

\displaystyle f_{i}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{x_{i}}\right)\frac{|C_{i}|^{2}}{2}.

Claim 4.2
.

For

with
s\leq r=i

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}\in N_{G}(z_{s}))

Together, with (

n^{-1/7}<\gamma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]=j_{s-1}+\sum_{i=s}^{t-1}(1\pm\gamma)\frac{j_{i}}{i},

(83)

Recall that since
\mathbf{j}\in\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}(e)

(1)

0\leq j_{i}\leq i
for all
0\leq i\leq t-1.

(2)

j_{i}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha\pm\gamma\right)i

We consider the case where
s-1\leq c\log n
and
s-1>c\log n
separately and show the lower bound on
\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]
to complete the proof of Claim
8.5
. The upper bound follows similarly. If

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-\gamma\right)(s-1)+\sum_{i=s}^{t-1}(1-\gamma)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-\gamma\right)

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-2\gamma\right)(t-1)=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-2\gamma\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{t}\right)t.

In view of (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-(2\gamma+\alpha^{2})\right)t.

On the other hand, if

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=\lceil c\log n\rceil}^{t-1}(1-\gamma)\frac{j_{i}}{i}\geq(1-\gamma)\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-\gamma\right)(t-c\log n)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha-2\gamma\right)t\left(1-\frac{c\log n}{t}\right)

Proof of Claim
8.6
.

Fix
1\leq s\leq t
. We will show that
d_{s}
concentrates around its expected value by considering a sequence of random variables that form a Martingale. Let
d_{s,s}=\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]
. For
l
such that
s+1\leq l\leq t
, let,

\displaystyle d_{s,l}:=\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|z_{1},\dots,z_{l}]

i.e.,
d_{s,l}
is the conditional expectation of
d_{s}
after exposing vertices
z_{1},\dots,z_{l}
. In particular,
d_{s,t}=d_{s}
. Such a sequence of random variables form a Doob Martingale. We claim that, for every
s\leq l\leq t
,

\displaystyle|d_{s,l+1}-d_{s,l}|\leq 2.

Formally, the case
l=s
is slightly different from the case
l\geq s+1
. First, we consider the case where
l\geq s+1
. Fix a tuple
(\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1})
. In view of (
81
),

\displaystyle d_{s,l+1}-d_{s,l}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1}]-\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=s+1}^{t}(\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l}]).

Note that for
s+1\leq i\leq l\leq t-1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1}]=\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l}]=Y_{i},

as revealing
z_{i+1}
determines
Y_{i}
. Further,
Y_{l+1}
is determined by
\overline{z}_{l+1}
, and consequently,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[Y_{l+1}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{l+1}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l}]|\leq 1.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|d_{s,l+1}-d_{s,l}|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{i=l+2}^{t}|\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1}]-\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l}]|

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=l+1}^{t-1}|\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}\in N_{G}(\overline{z}_{s})|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1})-\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}\in N_{G}(\overline{z}_{s})|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l})|.

(84)

If
i
is such that
l+1\leq i\leq t-1
, then applying Claim
8.4
with
r=l
and
r=l+1
, respectively, we have that,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}\in N_{G}(\overline{z}_{s})|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l+1})-\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}\in N_{G}(\overline{z}_{s})|\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{l})|<2n^{-1/7}.

(85)

Consequently, in view of (
70
) and (
84
),

\displaystyle|d_{s,l+1}-d_{s,l}|<1+t\cdot 2n^{-1/7}<2.

For the case where
l=s
, a similar argument as above implies,

\displaystyle|d_{s,s}-d_{s,s+1}|

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]-\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|z_{1},\dots,z_{s+1}]|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]-\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|z_{1},\dots,z_{s}]|+|\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|z_{1},\dots,z_{s}]-\mathbb{E}[d_{s}|z_{1},\dots,z_{s+1}]|

\displaystyle\leq t\cdot n^{-1/7}+1<2.

Applying Azuma’s Inequality (Lemma
2.4
) with
\lambda=\gamma t/2
to the sequence of
(t-s)
variables,
\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]=d_{s,s},\dots,d_{s,t}=d_{s}
, we get,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|d_{s}-\mathbb{E}[d_{s}]|>\frac{\gamma t}{2}\right)

\displaystyle<2\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}}{2\cdot 4\cdot 4(t-s)}\right)

\displaystyle<2\exp\left(-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{32}t\right).

∎

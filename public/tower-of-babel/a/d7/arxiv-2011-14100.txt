1.
Introduction

It is known from
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
that the dimension of tensor product of a Lie algebra
L
of dimension
n
when
\dim L^{2}=m
is bounded by
(n-m)(n-1)+2
and the structure of the tensor product of a Heisenberg Lie algebra is given. Let
H
be a generalized Heisenberg Lie algebra of rank at most

In this paper, we complete the explicit structure of the tensor square and the exterior square of

Finally, we show that dimension of the triple tensor of a Lie algebra

2.
Preliminary and known results

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a fixed field and
[,]
is used to denote the Lie bracket. For any two Lie algebras
L
and
K,
we say there exists an action
L
on
K
if an
\mathbb{F}
-bilinear map
L\times K\rightarrow K,

(l,k)\mapsto~^{l}k
satisfying

{}^{[l,l^{\prime}]}k=^{l}(^{l^{\prime}}k)-^{l^{\prime}}(^{l}k)\qquad\text{and}\qquad^{l}[k,k^{\prime}]=[^{l}k,k^{\prime}]+[k,^{l}k^{\prime}]

for all
l,l^{\prime}\in L
and
k,k^{\prime}\in K.

The actions are compatible if

(2.1)

^{{}^{l}k}l^{\prime}=[l^{\prime},^{k}l]\qquad\text{and}\qquad^{{}^{k}l}k^{\prime}=[k^{\prime},^{l}k]

for all
k,k^{\prime}\in K,

l,l^{\prime}\in L.

Clearly, a Lie algebra
L
acts on itself by
{}^{l^{\prime}}l=[l^{\prime},l]
for all
l,l^{\prime}\in L.
Morover, for all Lie algebras
Q,

a bilinear function
\varphi:L\times K\rightarrow Q
is called a Lie pairing if for all
l,l^{\prime}\in L
and
k,k^{\prime}\in K,
we have

\displaystyle\varphi([l,l^{\prime}],k)=\varphi(l,^{l^{\prime}}k)-\varphi(l^{\prime},^{l}k),

\displaystyle\varphi(l,[k,k^{\prime}])=\varphi(^{k^{\prime}}l,k)-\varphi(^{k}l,k^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\varphi(^{k}l,^{l^{\prime}}k^{\prime})=-[\varphi(l,k),\varphi(l^{\prime},k^{\prime})].

The non-abelian tensor product
L\otimes K
is the Lie algebra generated by symbols
l\otimes k
for all
l\in L
and
k\in K
with the following defining relations

\displaystyle c(l\otimes k)=cl\otimes k=l\otimes ck,

\displaystyle(l+l^{\prime})\otimes k=l\otimes k+l^{\prime}\otimes k,

\displaystyle l\otimes(k+k^{\prime})=l\otimes k+l\otimes k^{\prime},

\displaystyle[l,l^{\prime}]\otimes k=l\otimes^{l^{\prime}}k-l^{\prime}\otimes~^{l}k,

\displaystyle l\otimes[k,k^{\prime}]=^{k^{\prime}}l\otimes k-^{k}l\otimes k^{\prime},

\displaystyle[(l\otimes k),(l\otimes k^{\prime})]=-^{k}l\otimes~^{l^{\prime}}k^{\prime}

for all
c\in\mathbb{F},

l,l^{\prime}\in L
and
k,k^{\prime}\in K.
If
L=K
and all actions are Lie multiplication, then
L\otimes L
is called the non-abelian tensor square of
L.

We know from Proposition

,
L

On the other hand, the tensor product

\otimes^{3}L

The following lemmas are useful instruments in the next.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra of nilpotency class two. Then

(i).

L\otimes L
acts trivially on
L.

(ii).

(L\otimes L)\otimes L
is an abelian Lie algebra.

Proof.

(i). We know that
L
is a Lie algebra of nilpotency class two, so
\gamma_{3}(L)=0.
By considering action of
L\otimes L
on
L,
we have
{}^{\lambda(a\otimes b)}c=[[a,b],c]=0
for all
a\otimes b\in L\otimes L
and
c\in L.
Therefore
L\otimes L
acts trivially on
L.

(ii). By using the relations of the non-abelian tensor on
(L\otimes L)\otimes L
and the part (i), we have

\displaystyle[(a\otimes b)\otimes c,(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})\otimes c^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=-(^{c}(a\otimes b))\otimes^{a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}}c

\displaystyle=-^{c}(a\otimes b)\otimes 0=0

Therefore
[(a\otimes b)\otimes c,(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})\otimes c^{\prime}]=0
for all
(a\otimes b)\otimes c,(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})\otimes c^{\prime}\in(L\otimes L)\otimes L,
and so
(L\otimes L)\otimes L
is an abelian Lie algebra.
∎

Lemma 3.2

Let

\mathrm{Im}\gamma\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\overline{\varphi}.

Proof.

By using the relation

we can see that

(a\otimes b)\otimes[x_{1},\dots,x_{k-1},x_{k}]\in(\gamma_{k}(L)\otimes L)\otimes L

∎

Proposition 3.4

We claim that
(\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L)\otimes L\cong(\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L^{ab})\otimes L^{ab}.
It is clear that

(\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L)\otimes L\cong(\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L^{ab})\otimes L.
Also,
\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L^{ab}
and

act trivially on each other. Thus

By using the following exact sequence

L^{2}\otimes L\overset{\varphi\otimes i_{L}}{\longrightarrow}L\otimes L\longrightarrow L/L^{2}\otimes L/L^{2}\longrightarrow 0,

we have

(3.1)

\dim L\otimes L=\dim L^{ab}\otimes L^{ab}+\dim\mathrm{Im}\varphi\otimes i_{L}=\dim L^{ab}\otimes L^{ab}+\dim\varphi(L^{2})\otimes L.

Let

Since

and

we have

by using (

). Since

Now, by using (

Let

\dim\varphi(L^{2})\wedge L=\dim\wedge^{2}L-\dim\wedge^{2}L^{ab}=2

\displaystyle\dim\wedge^{3}L

Theorem 3.8

Let

Since

Faithful guiding-center orbits in an axisymmetric magnetic field
Alain J. Brizard 1,a and Brook C. Hodgeman 1

Abstract
The problem of the charged-particle motion in an axisymmetric magnetic geometry is used to assess the validity of higher-order Hamiltonian guiding-center theory, which includes higher-order corrections associated with gyrogauge invariance as well as guiding-center polarization induced by magnetic-field non-uniformity. Two axisymmetric magnetic geometries are considered: a magnetic mirror geometry and a simple tokamak geometry. When a magnetically-confined charged-particle orbit is regular (i.e., its guiding-center magnetic moment is adiabatically invariant), the guiding-center approximation, which conserves both energy and azimuthal canonical angular momentum, is shown to be faithful to the particle orbit when higher-order corrections are taken into account.



I
Introduction

The guiding-center representation
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
of charged-particle orbits is at the foundation of most particle simulations of magnetized plasmas
Qin and Guan 2008
;
White 2014
;
Burby and Ellison 2017
;
Albert et al. 2020
;
Bierwage et al. 2022
. The faithfulness of this representation relies on the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment
\mu
, which is expressed as an asymptotic expansion based on the nonuniformity of the magnetic field. In addition, when the magnetic field is axisymmetric, the connection between the exact particle canonical azimuthal angular momentum and its guiding-center representation can be used as a test for the faithfulness of the guiding-center approximation
Belova et al. 2003
.

For each guiding-center orbit parametrized by the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu
(with initial guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{0}
and initial parallel guiding-center momentum
P_{\|0}
), there corresponds an infinite set of particle orbits (with local initial conditions
{\bf x}_{0}
and
\dot{\bf x}_{0}
) that are labeled by an initial gyroangle, measured on an initial Kruskal ring
Burby and Qin 2012
;
Burby 2020
(also parametrized by the magnetic moment
\mu
) defined on the two-dimensional plane perpendicular to the local magnetic field. (In a uniform magnetic field
{\bf B}=B\,\widehat{\sf z}
, the Kruskal ring is a circle in the
(x,y)
-plane of radius
\sqrt{2\mu\,B/m\Omega^{2}}
centered at the guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{0}
, where
\Omega=eB/mc
denotes the gyrofrequency of a charged particle of mass
m
and charge
e
and
c
denotes the speed of light.) By adopting a guiding-center formulation that is gyrogauge invariant
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
(i.e., a formulation that is not only gyroangle invariant but also independent how the gyroangle is measured), the guiding-center orbit can be compared to an arbitrary particle orbit belonging to the same initial Kruskal ring.

The purpose of our present work is to explore how higher-order Hamiltonian guiding-center theory is faithful to charged-particle dynamics in an axisymmetric magnetic field. In particular, compared to the exact particle canonical angular momentum invariant, we will investigate the faithfulness of the guiding-center canonical angular momentum derived either in the truncated (lowest-order) guiding-center model
White and Chance 1984
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
, in the standard work of Littlejohn
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
, which includes gyrogauge corrections, or the extended work of Brizard
Brizard 2013
and Tronko and Brizard
Tronko and Brizard 2015
, which includes effects due to guiding-center polarization
Kaufman 1986
. For this purpose, we will consider particle and guiding-center orbits in axisymmetric mirror geometry (Sec.
III
) and in axisymmetric (simple) tokamak geometry (Sec.
IV
).

II
Particle and Guiding-center Orbits in General Axisymmetric Magnetic Geometry

In the present Section, we consider the problem of charged particle motion in a generic nonuniform magnetic field. Since the primary focus of our work involves the effects of magnetic nonuniformity, we assume that the magnetic field is stationary and an electric field is absent in our formulation.

II.1
Lagrangian particle dynamics

The orbits of a charged particle moving in a nonuniform magnetic field
{\bf B}({\bf x})
are represented as solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equations obtained from the particle Lagrangian

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}({\bf x})\;+\;m\,{\bf v}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf x}\;-\;\frac{m}{2}\,|{\bf v}|^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}({\bf x})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf x}\;+\;\frac{m}{2}\;\left|\dot{\bf x}\right|^{2},

where the particle velocity is
{\bf v}=\dot{\bf x}
and the magnetic field
{\bf B}\equiv\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}\equiv B\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
(which is decomposed in terms of its magnitude
B=|{\bf B}|
and its direction unit vector
\widehat{{\sf b}}={\bf B}/|{\bf B}|
) is represented in terms of a vector potential
{\bf A}
. From this Lagrangian, we obtain the Euler-Lagrange equations

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\bf x}}\right)\;=\;\frac{\partial L}{\partial\bf x},

(2)

which yield the usual Lorentz force equation

m\;\ddot{\bf x}\;=\;\frac{e}{c}\left(\nabla{\bf A}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf x}\;-\frac{}{}\dot{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla{\bf A}\right)\;=\;\frac{e}{c}\,\dot{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B},

(3)

which is solved subject to the initial conditions
({\bf x}_{0},\dot{\bf x}_{0}={\bf v}_{0})
. Since we are interested in orbital solutions of the Lorentz force equation (
3
) over long time scales compared to the short gyration period, which is inversely proportional to the gyrofrequency
\Omega_{0}=eB_{0}/(mc)
, where
B_{0}
denotes the characteristic strength of the magnetic field, we introduce a dimensionless time
t^{\prime}=\epsilon\,\Omega_{0}\,t
, where
\epsilon\ll 1
denotes a small ordering parameter, so that
\dot{\bf x}=\epsilon\,\Omega_{0}\;{\bf x}^{\prime}
(a prime denotes a derivative with respect to
t^{\prime}
). Hence, the Lorentz force equation (
3
) becomes

\epsilon\;\overline{\bf x}^{\prime\prime}\;=\;\overline{\bf x}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\overline{\bf B}(\overline{\bf x}),

(4)

where we have introduced a characteristic length scale
R_{0}
associated with the magnetic field, so that
\overline{\bf x}\equiv{\bf x}/R_{0}
is dimensionless, and the magnetic field
{\bf B}=B_{0}\,\overline{\bf B}(\overline{\bf x})
is expressed in terms of a dimensionless field
\overline{\bf B}(\overline{\bf x})
. The solution for this equation of motion, which can be carried out as an asymptotic expansion in powers of
\epsilon

Kruskal 1958
, will be carried out numerically in this paper.

In the event the magnetic field is axisymmetric, the particle Lagrangian (
1
) is independent of the particle azimuthal angle
\varphi
, and the azimuthal canonical angular momentum

P_{\varphi}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\varphi}}\;=\;\left(\frac{e}{c}\,{\bf A}\;+\;m\,\dot{\bf x}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf x}{\partial\varphi}

(5)

is a constant of the motion for particle orbits. With the normalization discussed above, we note that the azimuthal canonical angular momentum (
5
) becomes

\frac{P_{\varphi}}{mR_{0}^{2}\Omega_{0}}\;\equiv\;\overline{P}_{\varphi}\;=\;\left(\overline{\bf A}\;+\frac{}{}\epsilon\,\overline{\bf x}^{\prime}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\bf x}}{\partial\varphi},

(6)

with the dimensionless magnetic vector potential
\overline{\bf A}\equiv{\bf A}/(B_{0}R_{0})
.

II.2
Lagrangian guiding-center dynamics

For most particle orbits that are solutions of the Lorentz force equation (
3
), the lowest-order magnetic moment

\mu_{0}\equiv\frac{m}{2B}\;|\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\dot{\bf x}|^{2}\;=\;\frac{m}{2B}\;|\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}|^{2}\,\Omega^{2}

(7)

is an adiabatic invariant (where
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\dot{\bf x}/\Omega
denotes the lowest-order gyroradius), i.e., while the time derivative

\dot{\mu}_{0}\;=\;-\,\mu_{0}\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B-\frac{mv_{\|}}{B}\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\;\neq\;0

(8)

does not vanish for a general magnetic field, its average over the fast gyromotion time scale yields

\langle\dot{\mu}_{0}\rangle=-\,\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\;+\frac{}{}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)=-\,\frac{\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}}{B}\;(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}),

where
v_{\|}\equiv{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
is the local parallel velocity,
\langle{\bf v}_{\bot}\rangle=0
and
\langle m{\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\rangle=\mu_{0}B\,(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})
. Since magnetic fields are divergenceless, we immediately find that
\langle\dot{\mu}_{0}\rangle=0
, i.e.,
\mu_{0}
is an invariant over time scales that are slow compared to the fast gyromotion time scale.

The purpose of the guiding-center transformation is to construct an expression for the guiding-center moment

\mu\;=\;\mu_{0}\;+\;\epsilon\,\mu_{1}\;+\;\cdots

(9)

represented as an asymptotic series in powers of the dimensionless parameter
\epsilon
, where the first-order correction

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{0}\nabla\ln B+\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{2\,mB}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}-\frac{3}{2}\,\mu_{0}\left(\frac{p_{\|}\,\tau}{m\Omega}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}

involves first-order magnetic-field nonuniformity associated with magnetic curvature
\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}
and magnetic twist
\tau=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
, with
d\widehat{{\sf b}}/dt\equiv\dot{\bf x}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}
in a time-independent nonuniform magnetic field. The new expression (
10
), which is derived from the standard expression found in Refs.
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
in App.
A
, is easily computed from the particle dynamics. Hence, from the magnetic-moment analysis of the particle orbit yields a relatively accurate value for the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu=\mu_{0}+\epsilon\,\mu_{1}
, which can then be used as a label for the guiding-center orbit.

The reduced guiding-center representation of charged-particle dynamics in a nonuniform magnetic field
Cary and Brizard 2009
is obtained by an asymptotic decoupling of the fast gyromotion from the slow magnetic-drift motion in a reduced dynamical phase space with guiding-center coordinates
Z^{\alpha}=({\bf X},P_{\|})
, while the fast gyromotion is represented by the canonically-conjugate guiding-center action-angle coordinates
(J,\zeta)
, where the gyroaction
J\equiv\mu B_{0}/\Omega_{0}
(defined in terms of the magnetic moment
\mu
) is canonically conjugate to the guiding-center gyroangle
\zeta
.

The guiding-center Lagrangian is expressed up to first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity as

\displaystyle L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e}{\epsilon\,c}\,{\bf A}({\bf X})\;+\;P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}({\bf X})\;-\;\epsilon\,J\;\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}({\bf X})\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}

(11)

\displaystyle+\;\epsilon\,J\;\dot{\zeta}\;-\;\left(\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{2m}\;+\;\mu B({\bf X})\right),

where the
\epsilon
-ordering introduced in Eq. (
11
) is based on the standard
macroscopic
ordering associated with the renormalization of the electric charge
e\rightarrow e/\epsilon
. The selection of the vector field

\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}({\bf X})\;=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0&(\mbox{A})\\
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}}&(\mbox{B})\\
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}&(\mbox{C})\end{array}\right.

(12)

is based on whether the gyrogauge vector field
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}\equiv\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}
(defined
Littlejohn 1983
in terms of the local orthogonal unit vectors
\widehat{\sf e}_{1}
and
\widehat{\sf e}_{2}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{1}
) is kept (B & C) or not (A), and whether the guiding-center polarization correction
Tronko and Brizard 2015
is kept (C) or not (B). The
truncated
guiding-center model (A) is the simplest guiding-center model that is used in several guiding-center orbit codes (e.g.,
White and Chance 1984
;
White 2014
) and is reviewed in Ref.
Cary and Brizard 2009
. The
standard
guiding-center model (B) was derived
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
to ensure that the guiding-center equations of motion are not only independent of the gyroangle but also independent of how this gyroangle is locally measured in the perpendicular plane spanned by the unit vectors
(\widehat{\sf e}_{1},\widehat{\sf e}_{2})
. The
extended
guiding-center model (C) was derived
Tronko and Brizard 2015
to ensure that the guiding-center transformation accurately represents the guiding-center polarization
Kaufman 1986
;
Brizard 2013
.

The guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equations

d(\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial\dot{Z}^{\alpha})/dt\;=\;\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial Z^{\alpha}

lead to the reduced guiding-center equations of motion

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{m}\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;+\;\frac{\epsilon\,c\widehat{{\sf b}}}{eB_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mu\,\nabla B,

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{P}_{\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mu\,\nabla B,

(14)

where

{\bf B}^{*}\;=\;{\bf B}\;+\;\frac{\epsilon\,c}{e}\left(P_{\|}\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\frac{}{}\epsilon\,J\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}\right),

(15)

and
B_{\|}^{*}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}
can be used as the guiding-center Jacobian. These guiding-center equations are solved subject to the initial conditions
({\bf X}_{0},P_{\|0})
, once again labeled by the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu
obtained from the particle orbit. Because the particle and guiding-center orbits share the same values of energy
E
and magnetic moment
\mu
, the initial guiding-center parallel momentum can be chosen from the initial condition
P^{2}_{\|0}/2m=E-\mu\,B({\bf X}_{0})
, where the initial guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{0}
is calculated from the initial particle position
{\bf x}_{0}
according to the guiding-center transformation
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989

{\bf X}\;=\;{\bf x}+\epsilon\,G_{1}^{{\bf x}}+\epsilon^{2}\,G_{2}^{{\bf x}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{{\bf x}}+\cdots,

(16)

which implies that the initial guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{0}
is shifted from the initial particle position
{\bf x}_{0}
. Hence, the initial guiding-center position
{\bf X}_{0}
will depend on the guiding-center model used in Eq. (
12
), which differs at second order through
G_{2}^{{\bf x}}

Tronko and Brizard 2015
.

Lastly, when the magnetic field is axisymmetric, the guiding-center Lagrangian (
11
) is independent of the guiding-center azimuthal angle
\Phi
, and the guiding-center azimuthal canonical angular momentum

P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}=\left(\frac{e{\bf A}}{\epsilon\,c}+P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-\epsilon\,J\;\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\Phi}\equiv\frac{e{\bf A}^{*}}{\epsilon c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\Phi}

(17)

is an exact guiding-center invariant. We note that the terms of third order in
\epsilon^{3}
in
{\bf A}^{*}
(i.e., second order in magnetic-field nonuniformity) and higher are ignored.

II.3
Validity of the guiding-center representation in general axisymmetric magnetic geometry

While the azimuthal canonical angular momenta (
5
) and (
17
) are constants of motion of their respective equations of motion,
they can only be compared when the guiding-center azimuthal canonical angular momentum (
17
) is pulled back into particle phase space:

\displaystyle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}+\epsilon\left(G_{1}^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\|}}-\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\right)P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}

(18)

\displaystyle+\;\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}:\nabla\nabla-\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\nabla\right)P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}+\cdots

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle P_{\varphi},

where
(G_{1}^{\mu},G_{1}^{p_{\|}})
denote the first-order corrections to the guiding-center magnetic moment and guiding-center parallel momentum, respectively, and the first-order gyroradius is defined in particle phase space as

\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\;=\;-\;\frac{\mu_{0}B\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{2m\Omega^{2}}+\left(\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B-\frac{p_{\|}\tau}{m\Omega}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}+\rho_{1\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}},

(19)

where, while an explicit expression for the parallel component
\rho_{1\|}\equiv\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
will not be needed in the present work, we note that its gyroangle average is
\langle\rho_{1\|}\rangle=(\mu B/2m\Omega^{2})\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
. We note that the first term in Eq. (
19
) appears as a result of the extension
Tronko and Brizard 2015
of the standard guiding-center transformation
Littlejohn 1983
that correctly calculates the guiding-center polarization (see App.
B
for details).

The guiding-center representation is faithful to particle dynamics if the identity (
18
) is satisfied up to an arbitrary order in
\epsilon
. This identity guarantees that the guiding-center push-forward of the particle conservation law
dP_{\varphi}/dt=0
yields the guiding-center conservation law

0={\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\left(\frac{dP_{\varphi}}{dt}\right)=\left[{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\left(\frac{d}{dt}{\sf T}_{\rm gc}\right)\right]{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}P_{\varphi}\equiv\frac{d_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}}{dt},

where
d_{\rm gc}/dt
is the time derivative generated by the guiding-center Lagrangian dynamics and the guiding-center invariant
P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}P_{\varphi}
may be truncated at an arbitrary order in
\epsilon
.

In previous work, Belova
et al.

Belova et al. 2003
considered energetic-particle orbits in the National Spherical Torus Experiment (NSTX) that satisfied the adiabatic invariance of the higher-order guiding-center magnetic moment (
9
), and numerically investigated the validity of the guiding-center representation by verifying that the explicit expression for the guiding-center pull-back of the guiding-center toroidal canonical angular momentum

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}=P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\left({\bf x}-\epsilon\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$},p_{0\|}+\epsilon\,G_{1}^{p_{\|}},\mu_{0}+\epsilon G_{1}^{\mu}\right)

(20)

is nearly invariant, where
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}={\bf x}-{\sf T}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}
includes the first-order corrections (
19
) due to magnetic nonuniformity. In Fig. 3 of Ref.
Belova et al. 2003
, Belova
et al.

Belova et al. 2003
show that the standard guiding-center expression
\psi^{*}=\psi-\epsilon\,(cP_{\|}/e)b_{\Phi}+\epsilon^{2}(cJ/e){\cal R}_{\Phi}^{*}
, where
{\cal R}_{\Phi}^{*}=b_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\tau b_{\Phi}
, yields an improved particle canonical angular momentum invariant (
20
) compared with the truncated guiding-center expression
\psi^{*}=\psi-\epsilon\,(cP_{\|}/e)b_{\Phi}
.

In the present paper, the validity of the guiding-center representation is assessed on the basis of verifying that the expansion (
18
) is exactly valid at each order in
\epsilon
for two axisymmetric magnetic geometries: mirror geometry
(Sec.
III
) and simple tokamak geometry (Sec.
IV
). Here, the guiding-center representation is shown to be faithful up to (and including) first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity, which requires retaining all terms appearing in Eq. (
17
).

III
Magnetic Mirror Geometry

\displaystyle{\bf B}

\displaystyle B_{r}(r,z)\;\widehat{\sf r}\;+\;B_{z}(z)\;\widehat{\sf z},

III.1
Particle dynamics

The normalized equations of motion (
4
) are expressed in cylindrical coordinates as

\epsilon\,{\bf x}^{\prime\prime}=r\,\varphi^{\prime}\left(B_{z}\,\widehat{r}-B_{r}\,\widehat{\sf z}\right)-\left(B_{z}\,r^{\prime}-B_{r}\,z^{\prime}\right)\widehat{\varphi},

E_{0}=1/4
, with the initial conditions

III.2
Guiding-center dynamics

The guiding-center Lagrangian in magnetic mirror geometry is

L_{\rm gc}\;=\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}\psi^{*}\,\Phi^{\prime}+P_{\|}\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf X}^{\prime}\;-\;\left(\frac{1}{2}\,P_{\|}^{2}+J\,B\right),

(32)

where we use the extended guiding-center model (C) in Eq. (
12
),
{\bf X}=(R,\Phi,Z)
denote the guiding-center position in cylindrical geometry,
P_{\|}
denotes the normalized guiding-center parallel momentum, and
J\equiv\mu/2
denotes the normalized guiding-center gyroaction. In addition, in magnetic mirror geometry, we may choose the perpendicular unit vectors
\widehat{\sf e}_{1}=\widehat{\Phi}
and
\widehat{\sf e}_{2}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\Phi}
, so that the gyrogauge vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}=\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}=b_{z}\,\nabla\Phi
and the effective magnetic flux

\psi^{*}\;\equiv{}\;\psi(R,Z)\;-\;\epsilon^{2}J\,\left(b_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\,K_{2}\right)

(33)

is expressed in terms of the extended guiding-center model (C) in Eq. (
12
):

\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}\;=\;\left(b_{z}-\frac{1}{2}K_{2}\right)\nabla\Phi.

(34)

From this Lagrangian, we obtain Euler-Lagrange equations that can be expressed as Eqs. (
13
)-(
14
), where

\displaystyle{\bf B}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\;-\;\epsilon\,P_{\|}\;K_{2}\,\nabla\Phi,

\displaystyle B_{\|}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi,

The guiding-center equations are thus expressed as

\displaystyle(\dot{R},\;\dot{Z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{RB_{\|}^{*}}\left(-\;\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial Z},\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial R}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\epsilon}{R^{2}B_{\|}^{*}}\left(P_{\|}^{2}\;K_{2}\;+\;J\,B\;K_{1}\right),

(37)

\displaystyle\dot{P}_{\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{J}{RB_{\|}^{*}}\left(\frac{\partial B}{\partial Z}\,\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial R}-\frac{\partial B}{\partial R}\,\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial Z}\right),

(38)

which exactly conserve the (dimensionless) guiding-center energy
{\cal E}=P_{\|}^{2}/2+JB
(where
J=\mu/2
is obtained from Fig.
2
) and the guiding-center canonical azimuthal angular momentum
P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}=\epsilon^{-1}\psi^{*}
. These dimensionless guiding-center equations are solved for
\epsilon=1/20
with the initial conditions
(R_{0},\Phi_{0},Z_{0})=(201/200,0,0)
, which takes into account the radial shift (
16
) from the initial particle position
(r_{0},\varphi_{0},z_{0})=(1,0,0)
, and
P_{\|0}=\sqrt{E-J\,B(R_{0},Z_{0})}
.

III.3
Validity of the guiding-center representation in mirror geometry

We now show that the guiding-center representation of particle motion in magnetic mirror geometry is faithful to the exact particle motion, by showing that the guiding-center pull-back
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}=P_{\varphi}
of the guiding-center canonical azimuthal angular momentum is equal to the particle canonical azimuthal angular momentum.

Up to second order in
\epsilon
, the guiding-center pull-back
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\psi-\epsilon\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi-\epsilon^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\right)

(39)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon}{2}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}:\nabla\nabla\psi-\epsilon\,J\left(b_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\,K_{2}\right),

where the first-order gyroradius correction is given by Eq. (
19
) and the contribution associated with the magnetic twist
\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}=0
vanishes for magnetic mirror geometry. Here, we find
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi=-\,r^{2}\varphi^{\prime}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi=-\,K_{2}B
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B=K_{1}\,\varphi^{\prime}/B
, so that

-\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{2}\,J\,K_{2}\;+\;\frac{r^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}{2\;B}\,K_{1},

while

\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}:\nabla\nabla\psi\;=\;J\,b_{z}\;-\;\frac{r^{2}\varphi^{\prime 2}}{2\;B}\,K_{1}.

Hence, up to second order in
\epsilon
, the guiding-center pull-back (
39
) yields

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\;=\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\psi\;+\;r^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\;\equiv\;P_{\varphi},

(40)

which confirms the validity of the guiding-center representation in magnetic mirror geometry.

IV
Simple Tokamak Magnetic Geometry

We now turn our attention to the problem of charged-particle motion in a simple magnetic tokamak geometry, with circular concentric magnetic surfaces without Shafranov shift. The magnetic field is represented, using the quasi-cylindrical coordinates
(r,\vartheta,\varphi)
, by the dimensionless expression

\displaystyle{\bf B}\;\equiv\;\frac{r^{2}}{qh}\,\nabla\vartheta\;+\;\nabla\varphi,

(41)

where
q(r)=q_{0}+\sigma r^{2}/2
is the safety factor (we will use
q_{0}=1
and
\sigma=2
in our numerical calculations) and
h=1+r\cos{\vartheta}
is the normalized distance from the magnetic axis
(r=0)
to the particle position in the poloidal plane (which is normalized by the major radius
R_{0}
of the magnetic axis). Since the magnetic field (
41
) is divergenceless

{\bf B}\equiv\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}\;=\;\nabla\Psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\vartheta+\nabla\varphi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\psi,

(42)

it can be written in terms of the vector potential

{\bf A}\;=\;\Psi\,\nabla\vartheta\;-\;\psi\,\nabla\varphi.

(43)

By comparing with Eq. (
41
), we find
\partial\Psi(r,\vartheta)/\partial r=r/h(r,\vartheta)
and
d\psi/dr=r/q(r)
, so that the toroidal and poloidal magnetic fluxes
\Psi
and
\psi
are

\displaystyle\Psi(r,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{u\,du}{h(u,\vartheta)}=\frac{1}{\cos{\vartheta}}\left(r-\frac{\ln h(r,\vartheta)}{\cos\vartheta}\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\psi(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{u\,du}{q(u)}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\,\ln\left(\frac{q(r)}{q_{0}}\right),

(45)

where we chose
\Psi(0,\vartheta)=0=\psi(0)
. In what follows, we use the quasi-cylindrical unit vectors
\widehat{\sf r}=\cos\vartheta\,\widehat{\rho}+\sin\vartheta\,\widehat{\sf z}
,
\widehat{\vartheta}=\partial\widehat{\sf r}/\partial\vartheta=-\,\sin\vartheta\,\widehat{\rho}+\cos\vartheta\,\widehat{\sf z}
, and
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv\widehat{\sf r}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\vartheta}=\widehat{\rho}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf z}=\partial\widehat{\rho}/\partial\varphi
, with the quasi-cylindrical Jacobian
{\cal J}=(\nabla r\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\vartheta\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\varphi)^{-1}=r\,h(r,\vartheta)
.

The magnitude of the magnetic field (
41
) is

B(r,\vartheta)\;=\;\frac{1}{h(r,\vartheta)}\sqrt{1+r^{2}/q(r)^{2}}\;\equiv\;\frac{\beta(r)}{h(r,\vartheta)},

(46)

and the unit vector along the magnetic field is

\widehat{{\sf b}}(r,\vartheta)=\frac{r}{q\beta}\;\widehat{\vartheta}\;+\;\frac{1}{\beta}\;\widehat{\varphi}\;\equiv\;b_{\vartheta}(r)\,\nabla\vartheta\;+\;b_{\varphi}(r,\vartheta)\,\nabla\varphi,

(47)

with
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}=\nabla b_{\vartheta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\vartheta+\nabla b_{\varphi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\varphi
. Next, we can calculate

\displaystyle\nabla\ln B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r\,g}{q\beta^{2}}-\frac{\cos\vartheta}{h}\right)\;\widehat{\sf r}+\frac{\sin\vartheta}{h}\;\widehat{\vartheta},

(48)

\kappa

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\vartheta}{h\beta^{2}}\left(\widehat{\vartheta}-\frac{r}{q}\widehat{\varphi}\right)-\frac{\widehat{\sf r}}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{\cos\vartheta}{h}+\frac{r}{q^{2}}\right),

(49)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(g\;+\;\frac{1}{hq}\right),

(50)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\beta}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\left(\varphi^{\prime}\cos\vartheta+\frac{r}{q}\,\vartheta^{\prime}\right)\frac{\widehat{\sf r}}{\beta}\;+\;\frac{gr^{\prime}}{\beta}\;\widehat{\vartheta}

(51)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\varphi^{\prime}\sin\vartheta}{\beta}\left(\widehat{\vartheta}\;-\;\frac{r}{q}\,\widehat{\varphi}\right),

where
g(r)\equiv d(r/q)/dr
and
\beta^{\prime}=(rg/q\beta)\,r^{\prime}
.

IV.1
Particle dynamics

The dimensionless particle Lagrangian is

L=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\Psi\,\vartheta^{\prime}\>-\>\psi\,\varphi^{\prime}\right)\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left(r^{\prime 2}+r^{2}\,\vartheta^{\prime 2}+h^{2}\,\varphi^{\prime 2}\right),

(52)

from which we obtain the following equations of motion

\displaystyle r^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{\vartheta^{\prime}}{h}-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{q}\right)+r\,\vartheta^{\prime 2}+h\,\cos\vartheta\,\varphi^{\prime 2},

(53)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{r}\left(\frac{r^{\prime}}{\epsilon\,h}+h\,\sin\vartheta\,\varphi^{\prime 2}+2\,r^{\prime}\,\vartheta^{\prime}\right),

(54)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r\,r^{\prime}}{\epsilon\,h^{2}\,q}-\frac{2\,\varphi^{\prime}}{h}\,\left(r^{\prime}\,\cos\vartheta-r\,\vartheta^{\prime}\,\sin\vartheta\right).

(55)

These dimensionless equations of motion are solved numerically for
\epsilon=1/100
, with the initial conditions
(r_{0},\vartheta_{0},\varphi_{0})=(1/2,0,0)
and
(r_{0}^{\prime},\vartheta_{0}^{\prime},\varphi_{0}^{\prime})=(0,8,\sqrt{22}/3)
associated with a dimensionless energy
E=43/2
.

We note that, because of the azimuthal symmetry of the magnetic field (
41
), i.e., the components
(B_{\vartheta},B_{\varphi})
are independent of the azimuthal angle
\varphi
, the azimuthal canonical angular momentum (
5
), expressed in dimensionless form as

P_{\varphi}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\psi(r)\>+\>h^{2}(r,\vartheta)\,\varphi^{\prime},

(56)

is a constant of the motion.

Before moving on to guiding center theory, we calculate the normalized magnetic moment
\mu=\mu_{0}+\epsilon\,\mu_{1}+\cdots
, where

\mu_{0}=\left(r^{\prime 2}+r^{2}\omega^{2}\right)/B,

with

\omega\;\equiv\;\left(\vartheta^{\prime}\;-\;h\,\varphi^{\prime}/q\right)/\beta,

(57)

and the dimensionless lowest-order gyroradius is

\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}=-\,\frac{r\omega}{B}\,\widehat{\sf r}\;+\;\frac{r^{\prime}}{\beta B}\,\left(\widehat{\vartheta}-\frac{r}{q}\widehat{\varphi}\right).

(58)

Figure
5
shows that the lowest-order normalized magnetic moment is poorly conserved, especially as the particle crosses the equatorial plane
(\vartheta=0)
. We can greatly improve the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment by calculating
\mu_{1}
from Eq. (
10
):

\mu_{1}=\mu_{0}\,G_{1}+\frac{p_{\parallel}^{2}}{B}\,G_{2}+\frac{p_{\parallel}}{B}\,G_{3}-\frac{3\,\mu_{0}\,p_{\parallel}}{2\,B}\,\tau,

(59)

where

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B=\frac{r^{\prime}}{\beta^{2}}\,\sin\vartheta-\frac{r\omega}{B}\left(\frac{r\,g}{q\beta^{2}}-\frac{\cos\vartheta}{h}\right),

(60)

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}=\frac{r^{\prime}}{\beta^{2}}\,\sin\vartheta+\frac{r\omega}{\beta^{2}B}\left(\frac{\cos\vartheta}{h}+\frac{r}{q^{2}}\right),

(61)

and

\displaystyle G_{3}\;=\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{\prime}}{B}\left(\frac{g\,r^{\prime}}{\beta^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime}\sin\vartheta\right)

(62)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{r\omega}{\beta B}\left(\frac{r\vartheta^{\prime}}{q}+\varphi^{\prime}\cos\vartheta\right).

Figure
5
shows lowest order magnetic moment
\mu_{0}
and the improved
\mu=\mu_{0}+\epsilon\,\mu_{1}
. This first-order correction causes
\mu\simeq 12.23
to be a good adiabatic invariant, numerically validating the guiding-center representation for the particle orbits in the simple tokamak magnetic field (
41
). This adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment justifies our use of the guiding-center approximation in describing particle orbits in simple magnetic tokamak geometry.

IV.2
Guiding-center dynamics

The guiding-center Lagrangian in simple tokamak geometry is

L_{\rm gc}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\Psi^{*}\,\Theta^{\prime}-\psi^{*}\,\Phi^{\prime}\right)-\epsilon\,J\,R^{\prime}\,{\cal R}_{R}^{*}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\,P_{\|}^{2}+J\,B\right),

(63)

where
{\bf X}=(R,\Theta,\Phi)
denote the guiding-center position in quasi-cylindrical geometry,
J=\mu/2
denotes the dimensionless guiding-center magnetic moment, and
P_{\|}
denotes the dimensionless guiding-center momentum parallel to the magnetic field. In addition, the effective poloidal and toroidal magnetic fluxes

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\;+\;\epsilon\,P_{\parallel}\,b_{\Theta}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\,J\,{\cal R}^{*}_{\Theta},

(64)

\displaystyle\psi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\;-\;\epsilon\,P_{\parallel}\,b_{\Phi}\;+\;\epsilon^{2}\,J\,{\cal R}^{*}_{\Phi}

(65)

are expressed in terms of the extended guiding-center model (C) in Eq. (
12
):
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}=\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
. Here, we calculate the gyrogauge vector
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}=\nabla\widehat{\sf e}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf e}_{2}
by choosing
\widehat{\sf e}_{1}=\widehat{\sf r}
and
\widehat{\sf e}_{2}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf r}
, so that we obtain
\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}=\beta^{-1}\nabla\Theta-b_{z}\nabla\Phi
, where
b_{z}\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf z}=(R/q\beta)\,\cos\Theta
, and, according to the extended guiding-center model (C) in Eq. (
12
), we find

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla b_{\Phi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\;+\frac{}{}\nabla b_{\Theta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Theta\right)

(66)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{R}^{*}\,\nabla R\>+\>{\cal{R}}_{\Theta}^{*}\,\nabla\Theta\>+\>{\cal{R}}_{\Phi}^{*}\,\nabla\Phi.

From this Lagrangian, we obtain Euler-Lagrange equations that can be expressed as Eqs. (
13
)-(
14
), where

{\bf B}^{*}=\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Theta-\nabla\psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi+\nabla\chi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla R,

(67)

with
\chi\equiv-\,\epsilon^{2}\,J{\cal R}_{R}^{*}
and
B_{\|}^{*}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}^{*}
. The guiding-center equations are thus expressed as

\displaystyle R^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{P_{\|}}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\;\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial\Theta}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon\,J}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\;b_{\Phi}\frac{\partial B}{\partial\Theta},

(68)

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\;\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial R}\;+\;\frac{\epsilon\,J}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\;b_{\Phi}\frac{\partial B}{\partial R},

(69)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\left(\frac{\partial\Psi^{*}}{\partial R}+\epsilon^{2}J\;\frac{\partial{\cal R}_{R}^{*}}{\partial\Theta}\right)\;-\;\frac{\epsilon\,J}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\;b_{\Theta}\frac{\partial B}{\partial R},

(70)

\displaystyle P_{\|}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{J}{{\cal J}_{\rm gc}}\left(\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial R}\;\frac{\partial\overline{B}}{\partial\Theta}\;-\;\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial\Theta}\;\frac{\partial B}{\partial R}\right),

(71)

where
{\cal J}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\cal J}\,B_{\|}^{*}
combines the quasi-cylindrical Jacobian
{\cal J}
and the guiding-center Jacobian
B_{\|}^{*}
. We note that these equations exactly conserve the guiding-center energy
{\cal E}=P_{\|}^{2}/2+JB
and the guiding-center canonical azimuthal angular momentum

P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\;=\;-\;\frac{1}{\epsilon}\;\psi^{*}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{\epsilon}\;\psi\;+\;P_{\|}\,b_{\Phi}\;-\;\epsilon\,J\,{\cal R}_{\Phi}^{*}.

(72)

The dimensionless guiding-center equations are solved numerically for
\epsilon=1/100
with the initial conditions
(R_{0},\Theta_{0},\Phi_{0})=(0.5380,0,0)
, which takes into account the radial shift (
16
) from the initial particle position
(r_{0},\varphi_{0},z_{0})=(0.5,0,0)
, and
P_{\|0}=\sqrt{E-J\,B(R_{0},\Theta_{0})}
.

IV.4
Validity of the guiding-center representation in simple tokamak magnetic geometry

We now show that the guiding-center approximation is faithful to the particle motion in simple tokamak magnetic geometry by showing that the guiding-center pull-back
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}=P_{\varphi}
of the guiding-center canonical azimuthal angular momentum is equal to the particle canonical azimuthal angular momentum. Up to second order in
\epsilon
, the guiding-center pull-back
{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}
is expressed as

\displaystyle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\psi\;+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;+\;p_{\|}\;b_{\varphi}

(73)

\displaystyle+\;\epsilon\;\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;-\;\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}:\nabla\nabla\psi

\displaystyle+\;\epsilon\left(G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\,b_{\varphi}-p_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla b_{\varphi}\right)\;-\;\epsilon\,J\,{\cal R}_{\varphi}^{*},

where the first-order gyroradius correction
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}
is given by Eq. (
19
) and the first-order correction to the guiding-center parallel momentum is given by Eq. (
83
):

G_{1}^{p_{\|}}\;=\;-\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2}\,G_{2}\;+\;\frac{3}{2}\,J\;\tau\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,G_{3},

(74)

where
(\tau,G_{2},G_{3})
are defined in Eqs. (
50
) and (
61
)-(
62
).

First, using
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf x}^{\prime}/B
and the simple-tokamak identity
{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial{\bf x}/\partial\varphi=\nabla\psi
, we find

\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;=\;\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf x}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\bf x}{\partial\varphi}\;=\;h^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\;-\;p_{\|}\,b_{\varphi},

so that, at first order (i.e., zeroth order in magnetic-field nonuniformity), we find

\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;+\;p_{\|}\;b_{\varphi}\;=\;h^{2}\,\varphi^{\prime}.

(75)

Hence, we now need to show that, at second order (i.e., first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity), we find the identity

\displaystyle J{\cal R}_{\varphi}^{*}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}:\nabla\nabla\psi

(76)

\displaystyle+\;G_{1}^{p_{\|}}b_{\varphi}\;-\;p_{\|}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla b_{\varphi},

so that Eq. (
73
) becomes

{\sf T}_{\rm gc}P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\;=\;-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\,\psi\;+\;h^{2}\,\varphi^{\prime}\;\equiv\;P_{\varphi},

(77)

which guarantees the validity of the guiding-center representation in simple tokamak magnetic geometry. A complete proof of the identity (
76
) is given in App.
C
.

V
Summary

In previous work
Brizard 2017
, we showed that the guiding-center approximation was valid in a straight magnetic field with constant perpendicular magnetic gradient, even in the presence of strong gradients. In addition, based on the existence of an exact analytical solution for the particle orbits, this work also confirmed that the guiding-center polarization corresponded exactly with an orbit-averaged particle displacement.

In the present work, we extended our investigation of the validity of the guiding-center approximation in describing charged single-particle motion in a nonuniform magnetic field. Here, we considered regular particle orbits in azimuthally symmetric magnetic mirror geometry and simple tokamak magnetic geometry, in which the azimuthal angular canonical momentum is conserved and the guiding-center magnetic moment is an adiabatic invariant. We successfully validated the guiding-center approximation in describing particle motion in an azimuthally symmetric magnetic field provided higher-order guiding-center corrections are taken into account, which had already been noted for the case of an axisymmetric tokamak magnetic field
Belova et al. 2003
. In particular, the guiding-center polarization correction in the guiding-center azimuthal angular canonical momentum, not taken into account in the standard guiding-center approximation
Littlejohn 1983
;
Brizard 1989
, proved crucial in establishing a faithful guiding-center representation for regular particle orbits in axisymmetric magnetic geometry.

Lastly, we note that the truncated guiding-center model
White 2014
is used extensively in guiding-center particle simulations, despite the fact that it is not as faithful to particle orbits as higher-order guiding-center models. In most applications, however, the truncated guiding-center model is either used to analyze particle orbits in the presence of perturbed electric and/or magnetic fields, or as the unperturbed component for the gyrocenter orbit used in nonlinear gyrokinetic theory
Brizard 1989
. Future work may look into the issue of faithfulness for these applications. In addition, the faithfulness of the guiding-center representation for particle orbits in non-axisymmetric magnetic geometries may be explored.

Appendix A
Guiding-center Transformation

The standard expression for the first-order correction to the guiding-center magnetic moment is
Littlejohn 1983
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015

G_{1}^{\mu}=\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu_{0}\,\nabla\ln B+\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{m\,B}\right)-\mu_{0}\;\varrho_{\|}\,(\tau+\alpha_{1}),

(78)

where
\varrho_{\|}\equiv p_{\|}/(m\Omega)
and
\alpha_{1}\equiv-\,\frac{1}{2}\,(\widehat{\bot}\widehat{\rho}+\widehat{\rho}\widehat{\bot}):\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}
is constructed from the gyrorangle-dependent unit vectors
\widehat{\bot}\equiv\partial\widehat{\rho}/\partial\zeta=\widehat{\rho}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
. Using the identity

\alpha_{1}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,\tau\;-\;\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho},

(79)

we obtain

\tau+\alpha_{1}\;=\;\frac{3}{2}\,\tau\;-\;\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}.

(80)

Next, we write

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\;\varrho_{\|}\,\left(\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\left({\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\right)

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\left(\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\;-\;v_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\;-\;\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{2\,mB}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

which yields Eq. (
10
):

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{0}\nabla\ln B+\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{2\,mB}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}-\frac{3}{2}\,\mu_{0}\left(\frac{p_{\|}\,\tau}{m\Omega}\right)

(82)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}.

Using the same identity (
80
), the standard expression for the first-order correction to the guiding-center parallel momentum
Littlejohn 1983
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
is replaced with the new expression

\displaystyle G_{1}^{p_{\|}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;p_{\|}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\;+\;\frac{\mu_{0}B}{\Omega}\;(\tau+\alpha_{1})

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\;+\;\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{\mu_{0}B}{\Omega}\;\tau\;-\;\frac{m}{2}\,\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}.

Appendix B
Guiding-center Polarization

The guiding-center polarization was calculated directly from the guiding-center transformation in our previous works
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
. It is formally defined by the multipole expansion

\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\;\equiv\;e\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\rangle\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{e}{2}\;\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\rangle\right)\;+\;\cdots,

(84)

where the dipole and quadrupole moments are shown here, while
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf x}-{\bf X}
is the guiding-center gyroradius. We note that the guiding-center gyroradius
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}
is related to the particle gyroradius
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\equiv{\bf x}-{\sf T}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}
by the identity
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}
. Using the guiding-center transformation presented by Tronko and Brizard
Tronko and Brizard 2015
, we find the dipole contribution

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\left(\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle-\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{m^{2}\Omega^{2}}\right)

(85)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}\mu B}{m\Omega^{2}}\left[2\,\nabla_{\bot}\ln B\;+\frac{}{}\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\right],

and the quadrupole contribution

\displaystyle-\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\;\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\epsilon^{2}\mu B}{2m\Omega^{2}}\;(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}})\right)

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2}\mu B}{2m\Omega^{2}}\;\nabla_{\bot}\ln B

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}\mu B}{2m\Omega^{2}}\left[\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\;+\frac{}{}\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}}\right],

which both appear at
\epsilon^{2}
at their lowest orders (i.e., first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity).

Hence, the guiding-center polarization (
84
) is expressed as

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}e\left(\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle_{\rm pol}\;+\;\frac{\mu B}{2\,m\Omega^{2}}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right)

(87)

\displaystyle+\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left[\frac{\widehat{{\sf b}}}{m\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\mu\,\nabla B+\frac{p_{\|}^{2}}{m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right)\right],

where
\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle_{\rm pol}
is the polarization correction not included in the standard guiding-center transformation
Brizard 1989
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
. We, therefore, recover the standard guiding-center polarization
\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\equiv\epsilon^{2}(e\widehat{{\sf b}}/\Omega)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,d{\bf X}/dt

Kaufman 1986
only if we choose

\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle_{\rm pol}\;=\;-\,\frac{\mu B}{2\,m\Omega^{2}}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$},

(88)

which appears as the first term on the right side of Eq. (
19
). With this choice, the magnetic vector potential
{\bf A}^{*}
defined in Eq. (
17
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{e{\bf A}^{*}}{\epsilon c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e{\bf A}}{\epsilon c}+P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\epsilon J\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}+\frac{1}{2}\tau\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;+\;\frac{m\Omega}{\epsilon}\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\rangle_{\rm pol}

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e{\bf A}}{\epsilon c}+P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\;\epsilon J\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\cal R$}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right),

where the standard correction
\frac{1}{2}\tau\widehat{{\sf b}}

Brizard 1989
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
is replaced with the correction
\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}

Brizard 2013
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
.

Lastly, it is important to keep in mind that the guiding-center polarization discussed here occurs in the absence of an external electric field and is simply due to magnetic-field non-uniformity. Since the guiding-center polarization effect is inversely proportional the the gyrofrequency, it is an important effect for ions. The electric field generated by the charge separation associated with the guiding-center polarization, therefore, requires a self-consistent treatment that must include an electric field as an integral part of the guiding-center formulation
Brizard 1995
. This self-consistent analysis, however, is outside the scope of this paper.

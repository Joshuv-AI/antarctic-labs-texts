1.
Introduction

Vortices play significant parts in many fields of theoretical physics including superconductivity theory, cosmology, condensed-matter physics, electroweak theory, and quantum Hall effect. A tide of research related to vortex equations has been accomplished; see, for example,
[
4
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
and the references therein. Recently, Han, Lin and Yang
[
12
]
investigated a system of relativistic non-Abelian Chern-Simons-Higgs vortex equations whose Cartan matrix
K
is that of arbitrary simple Lie algebra, they established a general existence result for the doubly periodic solutions of the Chern-Simons-Higgs vortex equations.

In recent years, equations on graphs have attracted extensive attention; see, for example,
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
and the references therein.

Recently, Huang, Lin and Yau
[
11
]
proved the existence of solutions to mean field equations

\Delta u+e^{u}=\rho\delta_{0}

and

\Delta u=\lambda e^{u}\left(e^{u}-1\right)+4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{p_{j}}

on graphs.

Let
G=(V,E)
is a connected finite graph, and
V
denotes the vetex set and
E
denotes the edge set.

Inspired by the work of Huang-Lin-Yau
[
11
]
, we study the relativistic Chern-Simons-Higgs equations

(1.1)

\Delta u_{i}=\lambda\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}K_{kj}K_{ji}\mathrm{e}^{u_{j}}\mathrm{e}^{u_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}K_{ji}\mathrm{e}^{u_{j}}\right)+4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\delta_{p_{ij}}(x),\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

on
G
, where
K=(K_{ij})
is the Cartan matrix of a finite dimensional semisimple Lie algebra
L
,
n\geq 1
is the rank of
L
which is the dimension of the Cartan subalgebra of
L
,
p_{ij}
,
j=1,...,N_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
, are arbitrarily chosen distinct vertices on the graph, and
\delta_{p_{ij}}
is the Dirac mass at
p_{ij}
. With a view to handling the system in a unified framework, we need some suitable assumption on the matrix K. We suppose that

(1.2)

K^{T}=PS,

P
is a diagonal matrix satisfying

(1.3)

P:=diag\{P_{1},...,P_{n}\},~P_{i}>0,~i=1,...,n,

S
is a positive definite matrix of the form

(1.4)

S\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\alpha_{11}&-\alpha_{12}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&-\alpha_{1n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-\alpha_{i1}&-\alpha_{i2}&\cdots&\alpha_{ii}&\cdots&-\alpha_{in}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-\alpha_{n1}&-\alpha_{n2}&\cdots&\cdots&-\alpha_{nn-1}&\alpha_{nn}\end{array}\right),

(1.5)

\alpha_{ii}>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,\quad\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{ji}\geq 0,i\neq j=1,\ldots,n,

and

(1.6)

\text{~all~the~entries~of~}S^{-1}\text{are positive}.

By (
1.6
), we conclude that

(1.7)

R_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(K^{\tau}\right)^{-1}\right)_{ij}>0

for
i=1,...,n
.

We now state our main results as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the matrix
K
satisfies (
1.2
)-(
1.6
), then we have the following conclusions:

(\mathrm{i})
Suppose that (
1.1
) has a solution. Then we have

(1.8)

\lambda>\lambda_{0}\equiv\frac{16\pi}{|V|}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}P_{i}^{-1}\left(K^{-1}\right)_{ji}N_{j}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}P_{i}^{-1}\left(K^{-1}\right)_{ji}}.

(\mathrm{ii})
There exists a constant
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{0}
so that if
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, then (
1.1
) admits a solution
(u^{\lambda}_{1},...,u^{\lambda}_{n})
.

2.
Preliminary results

For each edge
xy\in E
, We suppose that its weight
w_{xy}>0
and that
w_{xy}=w_{yx}
. Set
\mu:V\to(0,+\infty)
be a finite measure. For any function
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
, the Laplacian of
u
is defined by

(2.1)

\Delta u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{yx}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
means
xy\in E
. The gradient
\nabla
of function
f
is defined by a vector

\nabla f(x):=\left(\left[f(y)-f(x)\right]\sqrt{\frac{w_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}}\right)_{y\sim x}.

The gradient form of
u
reads

(2.2)

\Gamma(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

We denote the length of the gradient of
u
by

|\nabla u|(x)=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Denote, for any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, an integral of
u
on
V
by
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x)
. Denote
|V|
=
\text{Vol}(V)=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)
the volume of
V
. For
p>0
, denote
||u||_{p}:=||u||_{L^{p}(V)}=(\int\limits_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. Define a sobolev space and a norm on it by

W^{1,2}(V)=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu<+\infty\right\},

and

\|u\|_{H^{1}(V)}=\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}=\left(\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

To apply the variational method, we need the following Sobolev embedding, Truding-Moser inequlity and interpolation inequality on graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 5]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The sobolev space
W^{1,2}(V)
is precompact. Namely, if
{u_{j}}
is bounded in

, then there exists some
u\in W^{1,2}(V)
such that up to a subsequence,
u_{j}\to u
in
W^{1,2}(V)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 6]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For all functions
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=0
, there
exists some constant
C
depending only on

such that
\int\limits_{V}u^{2}d\mu\leq C\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 7]
)

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\beta
and

such that for all functions
v
with
\int\limits_{V}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu\leq 1
and
\int\limits_{V}vd\mu=0
, there holds

(2.3)

\int\limits_{V}e^{\beta v^{2}}d\mu\leq C.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Interpolation inequality for
L^{r}
-norms on graphs)
. Suppose that
\theta\in(0,1)
,
0<\theta r\leq s
,
0<(1-\theta)r\leq t
and
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\theta}{s}+\frac{1-\theta}{t}
. Then we have

||u||_{L^{r}(V)}\leq||u||^{\theta}_{L^{s}(U)}||u||^{1-\theta}_{L^{t}(U)}.

Proof.

By H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality, we see that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{V}|u|^{r}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{V}|u|^{\theta r}|u|^{(1-\theta)r}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\limits_{V}|u|^{\theta r\frac{s}{\theta r}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{\theta r}{s}}\left(\int\limits_{V}|u|^{(1-\theta)r\frac{t}{(1-\theta)r}}d\mu\right)^{\frac{(1-\theta)r}{t}}.

∎

A relaxed small-gain theorem for infinite networks Journal: Automatica
Navid Noroozi, Andrii Mironchenko, Fabian R. Wirth

Abstract
Motivated by the scalability problem in large networks, we study stability of a network of infinitely many finite-dimensional subsystems. We develop a so-called relaxed small-gain theorem for input-to-state stability (ISS) with respect to a closed set and show that every exponentially input-to-state stable system necessarily satisfies the proposed small-gain condition. Following our bottom-up approach, we study the well-posedness of the interconnection based on the behavior of the individual subsystems. Finally, we over-approximate large-but-finite networks by infinite networks and show that all the stability properties and the performance indices obtained for the infinite system can be transferred to the original finite one if each subsystem of the infinite network is individually ISS. Interestingly, the size of the truncated network does not need to be known. The effectiveness of our small-gain theorem is verified by application to an urban traffic network.



1
Introduction

Recent advances in large-scale computing, cheap distributed sensing and large-scale data management have created the potential for smart applications, in which large numbers of dispersed agents need to be regulated for a common objective.
In the domain of Smart Cities, for instance, city-wide traffic control based on cheap personal communication, car-to-car communication and the deployment of numerous sensors can provide a major step toward safe, energy-efficient and environmentally friendly traffic concepts.
The vision of safe and efficient control of such large, dispersed systems requires tools that are capable of handling
uncertain
and
time-varying
number of participating
agents, limited communication, the need for
scale-free
methods, as well as stringent
safety
specifications.

Standard tools in the literature of stability analysis and control do not scale well to such increasingly common smart networked systems.
In fact, networks designed using classic tools may lead to
fragile
systems, where stability and performance indices of the system do depend on the size of the systems in a way that the network tends to instability as the size of the network grows; cf. e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
An efficient approach to address this fragility is to over-approximate a finite-but-large network with an
infinite network
consisting of countably many subsystems
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
By treating this over-approximating network, we envisage to develop
scale-free
tools for analysis and control of the original (i.e. finite-but-large) networks.
In particular, it is expected that an infinite network captures the essence of its corresponding finite network, i.e. the performance/stability indices achieved for the infinite network will be transferable to any finite truncation of the network; cf., e.g., a vehicle platooning application in
[
4
]
.
However, this expectation has to be rigorously checked as
counter-intuitive
results for optimal control of vehicle platoons have been already observed in
[
8
]
.

Motivated by all the above discussions, this paper investigates input-to-state stability (ISS) with respect to closed sets of discrete-time infinite networks within a small-gain framework.
As an infinite network is an infinite dimensional system, the
set
stability problem raises well-posedness issues of the system, which are addressed first.
Then we develop so-called
relaxed
small-gain conditions for which, in contrast with classic small-gain conditions
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, every subsystem does
not
have to be individually ISS.
In fact, relaxed small-gain conditions allow to treat the case that
subsystems have a stabilizing effect on each other and subsystems can be even individually
unstable
; see
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
for several examples of finite networks.
In particular, in case of exponential ISS we show both necessity and sufficiency of the proposed small-gain conditions.
We then truncate the infinite network to introduce a corresponding finite network which can be arbitrarily large and possibly
unknown
in size.
We show that if each subsystem in the infinite network is individually ISS, all the stability and/or performance indices obtained for the infinite network are
preserved
for its finite counterpart.
In that way, the small-gain conditions for the finite network are

independent
on the size of the network and hence, they can be used for
arbitrarily large networks, with possibly unknown size.
We illustrate the effectiveness of our results by application to an urban traffic network.

Related literature:

ISS theory of infinite-dimensional systems has been extensively studied in the recent years.
For an overview of this subject see, e.g.,
[
15
]
.
However, most of the development is related to ISS of continuous-time systems,
with some exceptions, see, e.g.,
[
15
, Section 9.6]
, for references on ISS of infinite-dimensional impulsive systems combining continuous-time and discrete-time dynamics.

Recently, ISS small-gain theory for infinite networks of continuous-time systems has been intensively studied in
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
For
small-gain theorems in trajectory formulation
decisive results have been obtained in
[
16
]
, where it was shown that a well-posed infinite network of input-to-state stable infinite-dimensional systems is again ISS, provided the so-called
monotone limit property
holds. This property is slightly stronger than the
uniform small-gain condition
which is also introduced in
[
16
]
, but is equivalent to the uniform small-gain condition in case of networks with a linear gain operator as well as for finite networks.
Furthermore, the results in
[
16
]
fully generalize available results for finite networks, provide spectral-type criteria for ISS of networks governed by linear and homogeneous gain operators, are applicable for a very broad classes of infinite networks and assume very mild regularity properties for subsystems and the whole network.

Lyapunov-based small-gain theorems
for continuous-time systems are now restricted to the case of countable interconnections of finite-dimensional components.
In
[
17
]
it was shown that a network of an infinite number of ISS systems is also ISS, if all the nonlinear gains characterizing the influence of subsystems on each other, are uniformly less than identity.
By means of examples, it is established in
[
1
]
that classic max-form small-gain conditions (SGCs) developed for finite-dimensional systems
[
9
]
do not guarantee the stability of infinite networks of ISS systems, even if all the systems are linear.
To address this issue, more restrictive robust strong SGCs are developed in
[
1
]
, where local ISS Lyapunov functions are defined in implication form and the gain operator is used in a max formulation, which makes the gain operator nonlinear, even if all the gains are linear.
Nonlinearity of the gain operator makes the analysis of the infinite interconnection highly challenging, and the Lyapunov-based small-gain criteria obtained in
[
1
,
17
]
are not tight and more investigations are needed.

In contrast to this, for the case of linear gain operators tight results have been achieved in
[
18
]
, where sum-type SGCs for exponential ISS of infinite networks are developed.
In
[
18
]
each subsystem is assumed to be individually exponentially ISS and a small-gain condition in the form of a spectral radius criterion is presented.
This work is further extended to exponential ISS with respect to closed sets in
[
19
]
.
Such a generalized formulation of stability with respect to a closed set covers a wide range of stability/stabilization problems including incremental stability, robust consensus/synchronization, ISS of time-varying systems as well as variants of input-to-output stability in a
unified
setting
[
19
]
.
In all of the above works, ISS of an interconnection is analyzed using a Lyapunov small-gain approach.

In classic ISS small-gain theorems, including all of the above
contributions, it is required that each subsystem is

individually
ISS to conclude ISS for the whole network.
This requirement is obviously conservative and rules out a large class of systems.
To address this conservatism, one may develop SGCs letting subsystems have stabilizing effect on each other.
To formulate such a setting for
finite-dimensional
networks, the notion of finite-step Lyapunov functions
[
20
,
21
]
can be merged with Lyapunov-based small-gain methodology, which leads to so-called
relaxed SGCs

[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
A finite-step Lyapunov function is an energy function which does not have to decay every single time step, but only after a finite number of steps.
When this property of finite-step Lyapunov functions comes to a network of systems, it lets us look into future time steps of solutions and allow for considering potential stabilizing effect of subsystems on each other.
Interestingly enough, relaxed SGCs are shown to be both
necessary
and
sufficient
and therefore they can be applicable to networks with unstable subsystems.
In
[
22
]
relaxed SGCs for ISS of finite-dimensional networks with respect to closed sets have been developed and applications to incremental stability, ISS of time-varying networks and distributed observers design are discussed.

In this work, we extend the results in
[
22
]
to infinite-dimensional systems.
Such a generalization leads to several
nontrivialities
as this calls for a careful choice of an infinite-dimensional state space of the overall system,
and developing direct and converse Lyapunov theorems in an infinite-dimensional setting.
In terms of existing works in the infinite networks context, our work is close to
[
17
]
as we also assume the internal ISS gains to be less than identity. However, our setting is formulated in discrete-time domain and that of
[
17
]
is given in a continuous-time domain.
More importantly, the SGCs in
[
17
]
are only sufficient, while here we show the
necessity
of our formulation in case of exponential ISS.

A preliminary version of this work has been presented at
[
23
]
.
In comparison to our previous work, we provide all proofs.
Additionally, in this work
we rigorously investigate under which conditions the system is well-defined
and well-posed.
In particular, we define the system on an extended state space with the structure of a Fréchet space, where the system is automatically
well-defined, cf. Section
2.2
below for more details.
While in
[
23
]
we mainly assume the network to be well-posed, here we relate local stability conditions imposed on subsystems with well-posedness of the system and provide an illustrative example (cf. Lemma
20
, Theorem
21
and Example
22
below).
Finally, we discuss the preservation of ISS indices under truncation of an
infinite network in more generality than in
[
23
]
,
cf. Section
5
below.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We write
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
for the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
For vector norms on (in)finite-dimensional vector spaces, we write
|\cdot|
.
We use comparison functions formalism (see
[
24
]
):

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{K}}&:=\left\{\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\left|\ \gamma\mbox{ is continuous, strictly}\right.\right.\\
&\phantom{aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa}\left.\mbox{ increasing and }\gamma(0)=0\right\},\\
{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}&:=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{K}\left|\ \gamma\mbox{ is unbounded}\right.\right\},\\
{\mathcal{L}}&:=\left\{\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\left|\ \gamma\mbox{ is continuous and strictly}\right.\right.\\
&\phantom{aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa}\text{decreasing with }\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\gamma(t)=0\},\\
{\mathcal{KL}}&:=\left\{\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\left|\ \beta\mbox{ is continuous,}\right.\right.\\
&\phantom{aaaaaa}\left.\beta(\cdot,t)\in{\mathcal{K}},\ \beta(r,\cdot)\in{\mathcal{L}},\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \forall r>0\right\}.\\
\end{array}

For
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathcal{K}
we write
\alpha<\gamma
if
\alpha(s)<\gamma(s)
for all
s>0
.
Composition of functions is denoted by the symbol
\circ
and repeated
composition of, e.g., a function
\gamma
is defined inductively by

\gamma^{1}:=\gamma
,
\gamma^{i+1}:=\gamma\circ\gamma^{i}
.

2.2
System description

We study the interconnection of countably many systems, each
given by a finite-dimensional difference equation. The set
\mathbb{N}
is used
as the index set (by default). For each system
i
we denote by
I_{i}\subset N\setminus\{i\}
the index sets of the neighbors of system
i
, i.e. the set
of subsystems that directly influence the dynamics of system
i
. The

i
th subsystem is written as

\Sigma_{i}:\quad{x}_{i}^{+}=f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i}).

(1)

Here
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
is the state of the
i
th subsystem,
x_{i}^{+}
denotes the state of the system
\Sigma_{i}
at the next time step,

\overline{x}_{i}\in X(I_{i})
is the state of all the neighbors of system
x_{i}
, where

\displaystyle X({I_{i}}):=\prod_{j\in I_{i}}\mathbb{R}^{n_{j}}.

(2)

We impose the following assumptions on the subsystems
\Sigma_{i}
:

Assumption 1
.

The family
(\Sigma_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
comes together with sequences
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers and index sets
I_{i}\subset\mathbb{N}\backslash\{i\}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, so that the following holds

(i)

The state vector
x_{i}
of
\Sigma_{i}
is an element of
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
.

(ii)

The vector
\overline{x}_{i}=(x_{j})_{j\in I_{i}}
is composed of the state vectors

x_{j}
,
j\in I_{i}
.
The space
X({I_{i}})
is equipped with the norm
|\overline{x}_{i}|=|\overline{x}_{i}|_{X({I_{i}})}:=\sup_{j\in I_{i}}|x_{j}|
.
For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the sets
I_{i}
and
\{j\in\mathbb{N}:i\in I_{j}\}
are
finite.

(iii)

The external input vector
u_{i}
is an element of
\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}}
.

(iv)

The right-hand side is a continuous function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times X({I_{i}})\times\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
.

In other words,
Assumption
1
(
2
)
requires that each subsystem only has finitely many internal
inputs and provides an input only to finitely many other subsystems.
In system (
1
), we consider
\overline{x}_{i}
as an
internal input
and
u_{i}
as an
external input
.

To define the overall network composed of subsystems
\Sigma_{i}
, we consider the state vector
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, the input vector
u=(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and the right-hand side

\displaystyle f(x,u):=(f_{1}(x_{1},\overline{x}_{1},u_{1}),f_{2}(x_{2},\overline{x}_{2},u_{2}),\ldots).

(3)

The overall system is then formally written as

\Sigma:\quad x^{+}=f(x,u).

(4)

Fix a norm on each
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
and define the state space
X
for the system
\Sigma
as

X:=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i})):=\Bigl\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}:x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},\ \sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}|<\infty\Bigr\},

and equip this space with the norm
|x|_{\infty}:=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}|
.
The space
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i}))
is called the
l_{\infty}
-sum of spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and it is a Banach space, see
[
25
, p. 127]
.

Note that by (ii) for all
x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\overline{x}_{i}=(x_{j})_{j\in I_{i}}\in X({I_{i}})
it holds that

|\overline{x}_{i}|\leq|x|_{\infty}.

(5)

Similarly, we consider the
external input value space

U:=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(p_{i}))
,
where we fix norms on
\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}}
that we simply denote by
|\cdot|
again. The norm on
U
we denote by
|\cdot|_{\infty}
.
By the space
\mathcal{U}
of admissible
external input functions
, we mean all sequences
u:\mathbb{N}_{0}\rightarrow U

such that

\|u\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{k\geq 0}|u(k)|_{\infty}<\infty
.
The space
\mathcal{U}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
becomes a Banach space.

We also define the
extended state space

\displaystyle X_{E}:=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}.

(6)

As
I_{i}
is finite for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the state of any subsystem
\Sigma_{i}
at any finite time
k\in\mathbb{N}
is affected by only a finite number of other agents, and thus
f
is always well-defined as a map from
X_{E}\times U
to
X_{E}
and hence
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, any
u\in\mathcal{U}
and any
\xi\in X_{E}
the corresponding solution to (
4
)

x(k,\xi,u)
is well-defined.

Thus, the equations (
4
) and space of input values
U
define a forward complete control system in the extended space
X_{E}
, which we denote by
\Sigma=\Sigma(f,X_{E},U)
if we want to make the
data defining the system explicit.

The space
X_{E}
has the structure of a Fréchet space, if endowed with
the countable family of seminorms
p_{i}:X_{E}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

p_{i}\left((x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\right):=|x_{i}|
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. This yields a complete, locally
convex vector space with the translation-invariant metric

d_{E}\big((x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}},(y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\frac{|x_{i}-y_{i}|}{1+|x_{i}-y_{i}|}.

The induced topology is the topology of pointwise convergence, i.e.
for sequences
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=((x_{i}^{k})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have
x^{k}\to x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
if and only if for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{i}^{k}=x_{i}.

Thus, if we
define the concept of ISS on
X_{E}
in the usual manner, then we would
simply replace the norms by the distance to
0
in
X_{E}
.

At the same time, for stability analysis we would like to ensure that the solutions of
\Sigma
are well-defined in the space
X
.
This motivates

Definition 2
.

We say that
\Sigma
is well-posed
, if
f
is well-defined as a map from
X\times U
to
X
.

Well-posedness ensures that for each initial condition
\xi\in X
and for each input
u\in\cal{U}
the solution of the system (
4
) exists on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and lives in
X
, that is (
4
) is forward complete.
The following gives a complete characterization of well-posed systems on

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i}))
.

Lemma 3
.

Consider the systems
\Sigma_{i}
as in (
1
) and
assume Assumption
1
holds.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The induced system
\Sigma
is well-posed.

(ii)

\Sigma
is well-posed and there exist
C>0
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that for all
x\in X
and
u\in U

\displaystyle|f(x,u)|_{\infty}\leq C+\kappa(|x|_{\infty})+\kappa(|u|_{\infty}).

(7)

(iii)

There exist
C>0
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},\overline{x}_{i}\in X({I_{i}}),u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p_{i}}

|f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i})|\leq C+\kappa(|x_{i}|)+\kappa(|\overline{x}_{i}|)+\kappa(|u_{i}|).

(8)

Proof.

(
3
)
\Rightarrow
(
2
): Let
(x,u)\in X\times U
. Using (
5
), we have for each

i
that

|f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i})|\leq C+\kappa(|x_{i}|)+\kappa(|\overline{x}_{i}|)+\kappa(|u_{i}|)\\
\leq C+\kappa(|x|_{\infty})+\kappa(|x|_{\infty})+\kappa(|u|_{\infty}).

As the right hand side is independent of
i
this shows that

(f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i}))_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in X
and so
f(x,u)\in X
.

(
2
)
\Rightarrow
(
1
): Clear.

(
1
)
\Rightarrow
(
3
): By assumption, each
f_{i}
is continuous, so that
with
C_{i}:=|f_{i}(0,0,0)|

and
\kappa_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
defined by

\kappa_{i}(r):=\max\left\{|f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i})-f_{i}(0,0,0)|\ ;\ |x_{i}|,|\overline{x}_{i}|,|u_{i}|\leq r\right\},

we have for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
that always

|f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i})|\leq C_{i}+\kappa_{i}(|x_{i}|)+\kappa_{i}(|\overline{x}_{i}|)+\kappa_{i}(|u_{i}|).

(9)

If
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}C_{i}
is not finite, then
f
is not well posed, as
then
f(0,0)\notin X
.
Otherwise, we set
C:=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}C_{i}
and
define
\kappa:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
by

\kappa(r):=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\kappa_{i}(r),\quad r\geq 0.

As the supremum of
continuous increasing functions,
\kappa
is lower semicontinuous and
nondecreasing on its domain of definition.

If
\kappa(r)
is finite for every
r\geq 0
, define

\tilde{\kappa}(r):=\begin{cases}0,&r=0,\\
\kappa(r)-a,&r>0,\end{cases}

where
a:=\lim_{r\to+0}\kappa(r)\geq 0
(the limit exists as
\kappa
is nondecreasing).
By construction,
\tilde{\kappa}
is nondecreasing, continuous at
0
and
\tilde{\kappa}(0)=0
.
It is easy to see that
\tilde{\kappa}
can be upper bounded by a certain
\hat{\kappa}\in{\cal K}_{\infty}
(this follows from a more general result in
[
26
, Proposition 9]
).

Then
\kappa(r)\leq\hat{\kappa}(r)+a
for all
r\geq 0
and we obtain from (
9
) that

|f_{i}(x_{i},\overline{x}_{i},u_{i})|\leq C+3a+\hat{\kappa}(|x_{i}|)+\hat{\kappa}(|\overline{x}_{i}|)+\hat{\kappa}(|u_{i}|),

(10)

which shows the claim.

It
thus remains to show that if
\kappa(R)=\infty
for some
R>0
, then

\Sigma
is not well-posed. If
\kappa(R)=\infty
, there exist sequences

(i_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(x_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(\overline{x}_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}},(u_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

such that for all
k
:
i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
|x_{i_{k}}|,|\overline{x}_{i_{k}}|,|u_{i_{k}}|\leq R
and such that

|f_{i_{k}}(x_{i_{k}},\overline{x}_{i_{k}},u_{i_{k}})|\geq k.

(11)

By assumption, the neighborhood graph of the network is locally finite. In

, the set

\{j\in\mathbb{N}\ ;\ I_{j}\cap I_{i}\neq\emptyset\}

is finite. We may thus assume, by taking a subsequence if necessary, that

we have

\left(\{i_{k}\}\cup I_{i_{k}}\right)\cap\left(\{i_{\ell}\}\cup I_{i_{\ell}}\right)=\emptyset.

(12)

Otherwise,
by local finiteness, a certain index
i^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
would appear infinitely often in the
sequence
\{i_{k}\}
, but this contradicts the bound in (
9
)
applied to

and the bound (
11
). We may
now define points
(x,u)=((x_{i}),(u_{i}))\in X\times U
by setting

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{matrix}x_{i_{k}}&\quad&\text{if }i=i_{k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
x_{j}&&\text{if }i=j\in I_{i_{k}}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&&\text{otherwise;}\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{matrix}u_{i_{k}}&\quad&\text{if }i=i_{k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right..

This is well-defined by (
12
) and
(x,u)\in X\times U

by the choice of the sequences. Finally,
f(x,u)\notin X
by (
11
). This
completes the proof.

2.3
Distances in sequence spaces

We continue to assume that
X=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i}))
. Consider nonempty closed sets
\mathcal{A}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
we define the distance of
x_{i}
to the set
\mathcal{A}_{i}
by

|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}:=\inf_{y_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}|x_{i}-y_{i}|.

Let
\mathcal{A}(I_{i}):=\prod_{j\in I_{i}}\mathcal{A}_{j}
, and note that for
\overline{x}_{i}=(x_{j})_{j\in I_{i}}\in X({I_{i}})
it holds that

\displaystyle|\overline{x}_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}(I_{i})}:=

\displaystyle\inf_{z\in\mathcal{A}(I_{i})}|\overline{x}_{i}-z|=\inf_{(z_{j})_{j\in I_{i}}\in\mathcal{A}(I_{i})}\max_{j\in I_{i}}|x_{j}-z_{j}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{j\in I_{i}}|x_{j}|_{\mathcal{A}_{j}},

(13)

where, in the last equality, we have used that the choice of
z_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
,
j\in I_{i}
is independent of the other indices.

Further define the set

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\mathcal{A}:=\{x\in X:x_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i},\ i\in\mathbb{N}\}{=}X\cap\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{i}.

(14)

Definition 5
.

We say that
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is
uniformly bounded
, if there is a

C>0
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and all
x\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
it holds that
|x|\leq C
.

Note that if
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded, then
\mathcal{A}=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{i}
.
If
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
, we define the distance from
x\in X
to
\mathcal{A}
as

\displaystyle|x|_{\mathcal{A}}:=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{A}}|x-y|_{\infty}=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}-y_{i}|.

(15)

As
X
is infinite dimensional, it is in general not true for any
closed set
K
that for
x\in X
there is a minimizing point
y\in K

with
|x-y|_{\infty}=|x|_{K}
. The following is thus a remarkable property
of the set
{\cal A}
. The proof uses the well-known property that in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
the distance to a closed set has a minimizer. We include a proof
for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 6
.

Let
X=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i}))
.
Assume that
\mathcal{A}
defined by (
14
) is nonempty. Then for any
x\in X
there is some
y^{*}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\displaystyle|x|_{\mathcal{A}}=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=|x-y^{*}|_{\infty}.

(16)

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
and
y\in\mathcal{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|x-y\right|=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left|x_{i}-y_{i}\right|\geq\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left|x_{i}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}},

(17)

as each
y_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
.
Taking the infimum over
y\in\mathcal{A}
, we get

\displaystyle|x|_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{A}}|x-y|_{\infty}\geq\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}.

(18)

Conversely, pick any
x\in X
and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
consider the set

\displaystyle B_{i}:=\mathcal{A}_{i}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}:|z-x_{i}|\leq\left|x_{i}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}+1\},

(19)

which is a non-empty, closed, bounded, and hence compact subset of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, on which
y_{i}\mapsto|x_{i}-y_{i}|
is a continuous function.

Hence, there is at least one minimizer
y^{*}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
of
\inf_{y_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}|x_{i}-y_{i}|
, which also belongs to
B_{i}
.

As
\mathcal{A}\neq\emptyset
, there is
R>0
, such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
there is

with
\left|z_{i}\right|\leq R
.
Hence, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
and any

we have that

\left|x_{i}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=\inf_{z\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}|x_{i}-z|\leq|x_{i}-z_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|+|z_{i}|\leq|x|_{\infty}+R,

and by (

),
|y^{*}_{i}|\leq|x|_{\infty}+R+1
.

Thus, for a given
x\in X
, there exists some
y^{*}=(y^{*}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{A}
such that, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

|x_{i}-y^{*}_{i}|=|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}.

But then,

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{i}-y^{*}_{i}|=|x-y^{*}|_{\infty}\geq|x|_{\mathcal{A}},

as required.
∎

We stress that the statement is
only applicable for
\mathcal{A}\subset X=\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},(n_{i}))
, while in
general
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{i}
may contain only unbounded sequences.
Also note that if
\mathcal{A}=\{0\}
, then
|x|_{\{0\}}=|x|_{\infty}
.

3
Input-to-State Stability

We aim to establish the stability of the interconnected system with respect to a closed set
\mathcal{A}\subset X
via so-called finite-step Lyapunov functions.
For this purpose, we introduce the notions of input-to-state stability (ISS) and of
\mathcal{K}
-boundedness with respect to
\mathcal{A}
.
Throughout this section we assume:

Assumption 7
.

\Sigma
is well-posed.

Definition 8
.

Let a nonempty closed set
\mathcal{A}\subset X
be given.
A function

\mathcal{K}

Remark 9
.

In view of item (

Given a nonempty closed set

\Sigma

is invariant for the dynamics of

if

The underlying idea is to formulate stability properties from a subsequence of state trajectories.
In particular, we aim to understand, whether one can conclude ISS

We define the iterates of

If

Remark 14

Proposition

As such converse ISS Lyapunov results are not available for infinite-dimensional systems, we gave a direct non-Lyapunov proof for this fact.

Now we pursue our strategy by introducing

However, a finite-step Lyapunov function does

Such a relaxation is useful in analysis and design of control systems; see e.g.

The proof boils down to the equivalence between ISS of

ISS for Infinite Networks

The main objective of this work is to develop conditions for input-to-state stability of the interconnection of countably many subsystems (

As argued in Section

,
the solution of the overall system is well-defined in the extended state space, i.e.

The stabilizing effect is observed by looking at solutions of the system in few steps ahead, which is captured by (
29
).
This in contrast with
classic
small-gain theorems where potential stabilizing effects are
not
taken into account, and hence every single subsystem has to be individually ISS.

To state the small-gain theorem, we also make the following
uniformity
condition for the functions introduced in
Assumption
18
.

Assumption 19
.

Assume that for subsystem
\Sigma_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the following hold.

(i)

There exist
\underline{\omega},\overline{\omega}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
so that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

\underline{\omega}\leq\underline{\omega}_{i}\leq\overline{\omega}_{i}\leq\overline{\omega}.

(30)

(ii)

There exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
with
\alpha<\id
so that for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}

\gamma_{ij}\leq\alpha.

(31)

(iii)

There is
\overline{\gamma}_{u}\in\mathcal{K}
so that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

\gamma_{iu}\leq\overline{\gamma}_{u}.

(32)

Conditions (
30
) and (
31
) are necessary to construct an overall finite-step
Lyapunov function which is well-defined and coercive.
Condition (
31
) rules out nonuniform decay rates for the solutions of the subsystems.
Without this uniform asymptotic stability of the interconnection need not hold, even if the system is linear and all internal and external gains are zero.
To see this, consider the following example

\displaystyle{x}_{i}^{+}=\frac{i}{i+1}x_{i}+u,\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

where
x_{i},u\in\mathbb{R}
.
One can readily verify that the network is not exponentially stable in the absence of inputs. Moreover, for arbitrarily small inputs the network may exhibit unbounded state trajectories.
Finally, condition (
32
) is also crucial for ISS of the overall system. Consider a network composed of subsystems of the form

\displaystyle{x}_{i}^{+}=iu,\quad x_{i},u\in\mathbb{R},\,i\in\mathbb{N}.

It is easy to verify that this network is not ISS.

Condition (
31
) has been used in
[
17
]
for the analysis of nonlinear infinite networks via the small-gain approach.
It introduces some conservatism into small-gain analysis, compared with conditions for the gains, used in small-gain analysis of finite networks. Nevertheless, in contrast to
[
17
]
we use finite-step Lyapunov-like functions, which allows to significantly reduce the conservativeness of our analysis.

As we frequently work with distances, we state the following useful
sufficient condition for well-posedness of
\Sigma
, based on

.

Lemma 20
.

Assume that all
f_{i}
are uniformly
\mathcal{K}
-bounded with respect to
\mathcal{A}_{i}
,

Proof.

First let (

By the

\displaystyle-\sup_{y_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}|y_{i}|\leq|f_{i}(\xi_{i},\overline{\xi}_{i},\mu_{i})|_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}

As

\displaystyle|f_{i}(\xi_{i},\overline{\xi}_{i},\mu_{i})|\leq\overline{C}+\kappa(|\xi_{i}|)+\kappa(|\overline{\xi}_{i}|)+\kappa(|\mu_{i}|),

Now assume (

\displaystyle|f_{i}(\xi_{i},\overline{\xi}_{i},\mu_{i})|\leq\overline{C}+\kappa(|\xi_{i}|)+\kappa(|\overline{\xi}_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}(I_{i})})+\kappa(|\mu_{i}|).

\displaystyle\kappa(|\overline{\xi}_{i}|_{\mathcal{A}(I_{i})})

\displaystyle\kappa(\sup_{j\in I_{i}}|\xi_{j}|_{\mathcal{A}(I_{j})})\leq\kappa(\sup_{j\in I_{i}}(|\xi_{j}|+C))

\displaystyle\kappa(\sup_{j\in I_{i}}|\xi_{j}|+C)=\kappa(|\overline{\xi}_{i}|+C)

\displaystyle\kappa(2|\overline{\xi}_{i}|)+\kappa(2C).

Using this estimate in (

5
From Infinite to Finite Networks

The underlying idea to deal with infinite networks is to develop tools for analysis and design of arbitrarily large-but-finite networks, which are independent of the possibly
unknown
size of the network.
The question arises whether the quantitative stability indices, e.g. decay rate of solutions, obtained for an over-approximating infinite network are preserved for the original large-but-finite network.
This section addresses this question. We show that the quantitative ISS
indices given by Theorem
23
will be preserved for any truncation of
an infinite network if
M=1
in Assumption
18
.
For
M>1
, which is a more complex case, we provide a scale-free ISS stabilization
result.

For the purpose of the truncation process, we only consider the
first
n\in\mathbb{N}
subsystems of
\Sigma
and denote the truncated system
by
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
. As the states
x_{j}
for
j>n
are no
longer present in
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
, but
in general

may still appear in some of the equations, we interpret these
x_{j}
as
additional
external
inputs. We denote by

I^{\langle n\rangle}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}\setminus\{1,\ldots,n\}

the set of neighbors of the first
n
systems. By
Assumption
1
(
2
)
the set
I^{\langle n\rangle}
is finite.
The truncation of the infinite network
is represented by

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}:\quad(x^{\langle n\rangle})^{+}=f^{\langle n\rangle}(x^{\langle n\rangle},\tilde{x},u^{\langle n\rangle}),

(43)

where the state vector
x^{\langle n\rangle}=(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_{i}
, the input vector

u^{\langle n\rangle}=(u_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}^{P}
,
P:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
, the additional input vector

\tilde{x}:=(x_{j})_{j\in I^{\langle n\rangle}}\in\mathbb{R}^{L}
,
L:=\sum_{j\in I^{\langle n\rangle}}n_{j}
and the dynamics

f^{\langle n\rangle}=(f_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
,
f^{\langle n\rangle}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The network
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
is obtained by keeping the first
n
subsystems of the infinite network
\Sigma
together with the associated interconnection between these subsystems.
Note that in our formulation we do not neglect the other subsystems
\Sigma_{i}
with
i>n
(i.e.
\tilde{x}
), but instead we view them as additional inputs to the network
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
.
Clearly removal of all subsystems
\Sigma_{i},i>n
is covered by our formulation as a special case if one sets
\tilde{x}\equiv 0
.

In the following we derive conditions under which
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
is
ISS in the set
\mathcal{A}^{\langle n\rangle}:=\Pi_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}
and compute the corresponding ISS gain functions under assumption that
\Sigma
is ISS.

Theorem 26

Consider the infinite network
\Sigma
.
Suppose that Assumption

the function

\tilde{x}
are not present to
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
, the input gain
\overline{\gamma}_{u}
is preserved.
Therefore, stability/performance indices of the overall system will be
independent
of the size of the network and we obtain the scale-free ISS for all truncations of
\Sigma
. We further illustrate this aspect via numerical simulation below.

As we assumed that
M=1
, the dynamics of the modes of the truncated systems are the same as the dynamics of the corresponding modes of the infinite network, up to the fact that some states of the infinite-dimensional system become the inputs for the truncated system.
This easily implies that the functions
W_{i}
, which are ISS Lyapunov functions for subsystems of
\Sigma
are also ISS Lyapunov functions for subsystems of the truncated system
\Sigma^{\langle n\rangle}
.

For
M>1
the situation is more complex.
Firstly, if
M>1
, then some subsystems may be unstable, and thus clearly there exist truncations of the infinite network, which are not ISS. Secondly, the dynamics of the modes of
(f^{\langle n\rangle})^{M}
are different from the dynamics of
(f^{M})^{\langle n\rangle}
.
However, for
M>1
we have the following scale-free ISS stabilization
result.

Theorem 27
.

Let
M>1
and suppose that, with this
M
,
Assumption
18
and
Assumption
19
are satisfied. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be

\beta\in\mathcal{KL}
and

for all

\hskip-2.84526pt|x^{\langle n\rangle}(k;\xi^{\langle n\rangle}_{0},\tilde{x}(\cdot),u^{\langle n\rangle}(\cdot))|_{\mathcal{A}^{\langle n\rangle}}\\
\leq\max\big\{\beta(|\xi^{\langle n\rangle}_{0}|_{\mathcal{A}^{\langle n\rangle}},k),\gamma(\|u^{\langle n\rangle}\|_{\infty})\big\}.

(47)

\beta
and

\Sigma

Proof.

Suppose that Assumptions

By Theorem

\hskip-2.84526pt|x(k,\xi,u)|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\max\big\{\beta(|\xi^{\langle n\rangle}_{0}|_{\mathcal{A}^{\langle n\rangle}},k),\gamma(\|u^{\langle n\rangle}\|_{\infty})\big\}\,,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For

7
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper we have discussed ISS Lyapunov small-gain theorems for discrete time systems given as infinite networks with a locally finite neighborhood structure, where individual subsystems do not have to be ISS.
The necessity of the small-gain condition in case of exponential decay rate of solutions have been established.
It was shown how to use this approach for the over-approximation of large-but-finite networks.
In particular, it has been shown that the ISS property of the infinite network is transferable to any truncation (in size) of the network.

The following challenging problems remain for a future work: in this
work we assumed that all the coupling gains are less than the
identity. However, in view of
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
this
assumption is not needed for finite networks, where a max-type small-gain formulation is given in the form of the so-called cyclic small-gain condition. Another direction is to investigate the relationship between ISS of an infinite network and that of its truncation with time-varying size.
In view of
[
29
]
, we are also investigating application of the results of this paper to distributed control design for infinite networks.

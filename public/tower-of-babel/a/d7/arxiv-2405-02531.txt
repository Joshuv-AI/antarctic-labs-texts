Bochner-Riesz means for critical magnetic Schrödinger operators in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}^{2}}
Changxing Miao, Lixin Yan, Junyong Zhang

Abstract
. We study L p L^{p} -boundedness of the Bochner-Riesz means for critical magnetic Schrödinger operators ℒ 𝐀 \mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}} in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}^{2}} , which involve the physical Aharonov-Bohm potential. We show that for 1 ≤ p ≤ + ∞ 1\leq p\leq+\infty and p ≠ 2 p\not=2 , the Bochner-Riesz operator S λ δ ​ ( ℒ 𝐀 ) S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}) of order δ \delta is bounded on L p ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) if and only if δ > max ⁡ { 0 , 2 ​ | 1 / 2 − 1 / p | − 1 / 2 } \delta>\max\big\{0,2\big|1/2-1/p\big|-1/2\big\} . The new ingredient in the proof is to obtain the localized L 4 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) estimate of S λ δ ​ ( ℒ 𝐀 ) S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}) , whose kernel is heavily affected by the physical magnetic diffraction, and more singular than the classical Bochner-Riesz means S λ δ ​ ( Δ ) S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta) for the Laplacian Δ \Delta in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} .

1.
Introduction

Consider a scaling invariant magnetic Schrödinger operator of the form

\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}=-\left(\nabla+i\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}\right)^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

(1.1)

where
\hat{x}=\tfrac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
{{\bf A}}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1};\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies the transversality condition

{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\cdot\hat{x}=0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.2)

A primary example is
the
Aharonov-Bohm
potential

\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}=\alpha\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\right),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

(1.3)

which was introduced in
[
1
]
, in the context of Schrödinger dynamics, to show that scattering effects can even occur in regions in which the electromagnetic field is absent.
It is well known that the Aharonov-Bohm (AB) effect plays an important role in quantum theory refining the status of electromagnetic potentials (see
[
1
]
).
This effect describes the interaction between a non-relativistic charged particle and an infinitely long and infinitesimally thin magnetic solenoid field (hereafter the AB field). We refer to Avron-Herbst-Simon
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
and Reed-Simon
[
42
]
for electromagnetic phenomena in quantum mechanics,
in which many important physical potentials (e.g., the constant magnetic field and the Coulomb electric potential) are discussed. The magnetic potential is scaling critical, making the perturbation completely nontrivial. From a mathematical perspective, the singularity of the Aharonov-Bohm potential is not locally square integrable (i.e., not in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
), which makes the analysis of this operator more difficult compared to the usual theory for magnetic Schrödinger Hamiltonians (see
[
43
]
). On the other hand, the scaling invariance is advantageous from the perspective of Fourier analysis and allows for explicit calculations of generalized eigenfunctions, as we shall see in the sequel.

Recently, there has been growing interest in the harmonic analysis associated with the above conical singular operators. In
[
26
,
27
]
,
Fanelli, Zheng and the third named author proved Strichartz estimates for the wave equation and
the
L^{p}-L^{q}
resolvent estimates; and later in
[
31
]
, together with Gao, Yin and Zheng, the third named author proved Strichartz estimates for the Klein-Gordon equation by constructing the spectral measure of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
. We
studied
L^{2}
-weighted resolvent estimates in
[
30
]
. Interesting physical phenomena such as diffraction and resonance are known to play an important role in the harmonic analysis theory associated with the above conical singular operators.

In the present paper, we focus on purely magnetic potentials that are critical with respect to the scaling of the free operator. Many problems in harmonic analysis (e.g., resolvent estimates, Strichartz estimates) for the Schrödinger operators with critical potentials become harder, since the usual perturbation arguments are not available. We refer to

[
6
,
18
]
for the resolvent estimates, in which the potential is required to decay faster at infinity than the critical one. We refer to
[
12
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
22
,
44
]
and the references therein for time-decay or Strichartz estimates with subcritical magnetic potentials. However, the problems with critical magnetic potentials are more delicate and even remain open in high dimension. The space dimension
n=2
is very peculiar for this kind of problem, since the associated spherical problem is one-dimensional and several explicit expansions are available, leading to quite complete results. For example, the authors in
[
24
,
25
]
proved the time-decay estimate
for the Schrödinger equation; while
[
26
,
31
]
established the Strichartz estimates for the wave and Klein-Gordon equations.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate
the Bochner-Riesz operator
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}):=(1-{\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}/{\lambda^{2}})^{\delta}_{+}
of order
\delta
associated with a scaling critical magnetic Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
in
{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
, which is defined, for
\delta\geq 0
and
\lambda>0
, by

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})=\int_{0}^{\lambda}\Big(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}}(\rho),

(1.4)

where
E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}}(\rho)
is a spectral resolution of
\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}
. By the spectral theorem, the operator
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})
is bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})
.
The problem known as the Bochner-Riesz problem is to determine the optimal index
\delta
for
1\leq p\leq+\infty
such that

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})f
converges to
f
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})
for every
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{2}})
.
This kind of problem was initially considered for the Laplacian
\Delta=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{x_{i}}

on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, and the problem has been extensively studied by numerous authors.
It has been conjectured that the classical Bochner–Riesz mean
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta)f
converges
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if

\delta>\delta_{c}(p,n):=\max\left\{0,n\big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big|-\frac{1}{2}\right\}

(1.5)

for
p\in[1,+\infty]\setminus\{2\}
.
When
p=2
, the convergence holds true if and only if
\delta\geq 0
by
Plancherel’s theorem. In two dimensions, the conjecture has been proved by L. Carleson and P. Sjölin
[
12
]
by means of a more general theorem on oscillatory integral operators. For a variant of their proof, we refer to L. Hörmander
[
34
]
, C. Fefferman
[
28
]
and A. Cordoba
[
17
]
. However, in higher dimensions, it remains open and partial results are known. For readers who are interested in recent progress on the conjecture, we refer to
[
9
,
10
,
33
,
40
,
49
,
50
]
and references therein.

The Bochner-Riesz means for elliptic operators (e.g., Schrödinger operators with potentials, the Laplacian on manifolds) have attracted a lot of attention and have been extensively studied by many authors, see

[
2
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
32
,
38
,
39
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
41
,
46
,
52
]
. In particular, we refer to Sogge
[
46
]
, Christ-Sogge
[
16
]
for the Bochner-Riesz means on compact manifolds;
Guillarmou-Hassell-Sikora
[
32
]
for the Bochner-Riesz means on non-trapping asymptotically conic manifolds; Askey-Wainger
[
2
]
, Karadzhov
[
38
]
, Thangavelu
[

]
and
Lee-Ryu
[
41
]
for the Bochner-Riesz means for the Hermite operator, and Jeong-Lee-Ryu
[
37
]
for the Bochner-Riesz means for the twisted Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
However, the picture of the Bochner-Riesz problem associated with elliptic operators is far from complete. Motivated by this, we
study the Bochner-Riesz problem for variable-coefficient Schrödinger operators with a scaling-critical magnetic potential.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq+\infty
and let

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})
be the Bochner-Riesz means defined by
(
1.1
) and (
1.4
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
\lambda>0
such that

\|S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(1.6)

if and only if
\delta>\delta_{c}(p,2)=\max\big\{0,2\big|1/2-1/p\big|-1/2\big\}
when
p\not=2
.

The necessary part of (
1.6
) follows from a transplantation theorem due to Kenig-Stanton-Tomas
[
39
]
and the necessary condition for the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Bochner-Riesz means for
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\Delta)
when the total magnetic flux
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
p=2
, one can use the spectral theorem to prove that (
1.6
) holds for
\delta\geq 0
.

To show the sufficiency part of (
1.6
),
we note that the magnetic potential considered here is singular at the origin and decays like the Coulomb potential at infinity, which is quite different from the harmonic oscillator potential in
[
41
]
and the constant magnetic field in
[
37
]
. The potentials considered in their papers belong to
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}
but are unbounded at infinity, so that these Schrödinger operators have only pure point spectrum and have a nice
representation of the kernel due to the Mehler formula. Our magnetic potential is too singular at the origin to be in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}
, while its decay at infinity is not sufficiently fast, leading to a difficult long-range perturbation.
The sufficiency part of (
1.6
) can be proved by making use of the ideas of the celebrated oscillatory integral theory of Stein
[
49
]
and Hörmander
[
34
]
adapted to the singular kernel, which is the so-called variable-coefficient Plancherel theorem of T. H. Wolff
[
53
]
. But we have to overcome the difficulties caused by the singularity of the potential and the Aharonov-Bohm effect of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
.
Let us now describe some key points of our proof.

•

The magnetic Aharonov-Bohm effect is a quantum mechanical phenomenon in which
an electrically charged particle is affected by an electromagnetic potential
\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}
in (
1.3
), despite being
confined to a region in which the magnetic field

{\bf B}(x)=\nabla\times\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}=2\pi\alpha\delta_{0}(x)

vanishes. In physics, the Hamiltonian (
1.1
) describes an infinitely long solenoid of radius
r\to 0
. Assume the total magnetic flux
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}
. The motion of a classical particle outside the solenoid
will not be affected by the vector potential due to the absence of the magnetic field. However, in
quantum mechanics, consider a charged particle traveling around the solenoid; it picks up a
non-trivial phase shift if it travels around the solenoid once. This phenomenon shows the quantum significance of
the vector potential.

•

To understand the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, one needs the diffractive geometry of the conical singular operator of Ford-Wunsch
[
29
]
. By
standard propagation of singularities, away from the solenoid set
\{0\}
, singularities propagate along straight lines in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. However, near the solenoid there
are two other types of generalized geodesics, along which the singularities propagate, passing
through the solenoid, which correspond to the diffractive and geometric waves emanating
from the solenoid upon diffraction. Therefore, in our proof, the difference between the incoming and outgoing directions plays an important role. In particular, when the difference
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
is near
\pi
, the proof becomes more delicate.

•

In contrast to Yang
[
54
,
55
,
56
]
, in which diffractive geometry and the estimates in weighted
L^{2}
spaces are studied, the
L^{p}
-framework estimates on Bochner-Riesz means require
the elaborate properties of the kernel which captures the decay and the oscillation behavior.

•

Mathematically, the incident wave is different from the usual plane wave
e^{ix\cdot\xi}
, which leads to the appearance of the modified factor
e^{\pm i\alpha(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}
in the kernel of Bochner-Riesz means because of the long-range property of the potential
\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}
. In addition, the Heaviside step function
\mathbbm{1}_{I}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})
appears in the kernel of Bochner-Riesz means,
which is another obstacle to efficiently exploit the oscillation behavior of the kernel.

•

In contrast to our previous resolvent estimates in
[
27
]
, we have to study a more difficult kernel
and prove more localized estimates. To prove the sharp result in Theorem
1.1
, we first prove the localized estimates on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
p\geq 6
;
and then prove the localized estimate on
L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which is the key estimate on the line
q=3p^{\prime}
, see
[
47
,
49
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we sketch the construction of the resolvent and spectral measure kernels of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
and we further construct the kernel of the Bochner-Riesz means operator. In Section
3
, we prove the sufficiency part of (
1.6
) by using two key localized propositions, namely Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
below, which will be shown in Section
4
and Section
5
, respectively. Finally, in the appendix, we prove the technical lemmas used in Section
4
and Section
5
.

2.
The kernel of Bochner-Riesz means

In this section, we first sketch the construction of the resolvent and spectral measure kernels for the Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
, which is more convenient for studying the Bochner–Riesz means. We then construct the kernel of the Bochner–Riesz means
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})
, which captures the decay and oscillation properties.

2.1.
The resolvent and spectral measure kernels

In this subsection, we sketch the construction of
the resolvent and the spectral measure kernels for the
Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
, which have been studied in connection with the wave and Klein-Gordon equations in
[
26
,
31
]
.
For the sake of self-containedness, we outline the main steps of the proof and provide some physical diffractive interpretations of the kernel representations.

By using the conic structure of
\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
in (
1.1
), in polar coordinates we can write

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{L_{{{\bf A}}}}{r^{2}},\end{split}

(2.1)

where the operator

\begin{split}L_{{{\bf A}}}&=(i\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+{{\bf A}}(\hat{x}))^{2},\qquad\hat{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\\
&=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+\big(|{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})|^{2}+i\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\big)+2i{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Let
\hat{x}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
, then

\partial_{\theta}=-\hat{x}_{2}\partial_{\hat{x}_{1}}+\hat{x}_{1}\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}},\quad\partial_{\theta}^{2}=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.

Define
A(\theta):[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by

A(\theta)={\bf A}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\cdot(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta).

(2.3)

Then, using the transversality condition (
1.2
), we can write

{\bf A}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=A(\theta)(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\quad\theta\in[0,2\pi).

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}L_{{{\bf A}}}&=-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}+\big(|{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})|^{2}+i\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\big)+2i{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\\
&=-\partial_{\theta}^{2}+\big(|A(\theta)|^{2}+i\,{A^{\prime}}(\theta)\big)+2iA(\theta)\partial_{\theta}\\
&=(i\partial_{\theta}+A(\theta))^{2}.\end{split}

(2.4)

For simplicity, we define the constant
\alpha
by

\alpha=\Phi_{{\bf A}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}A(\theta)\,d\theta,

which represents the total magnetic flux.

For
\lambda>0
, we define the resolvent of the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
by

\big(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)\big)^{-1}=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i\epsilon)\big)^{-1},

(2.5)

where we use the same notation
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
for the Friedrichs self-adjoint extension of the Hamiltonian (
1.1
).

In
[
26
,
31
]
, we observe the conic property of this operator, which allows us to construct the resolvent kernel
inspired by Cheeger-Taylor
[
13
,
14
]
, where the authors studied the diffractive waves on flat cones.

Proposition 2.1

(Resolvent kernel,
[
31
]
)
.

Let
x=r_{1}(\cos\theta_{1},\sin\theta_{1})
and
y=r_{2}(\cos\theta_{2},\sin\theta_{2})
,
then the resolvent kernel has the following expression

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)\big)^{-1}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)}\;d\xi\,A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

(2.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{e^{-i{\bf n}_{s}\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)}\;d\xi\,B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\;ds,

where
{\bf n}_{s}=(r_{1}+r_{2},\sqrt{2r_{1}r_{2}(\cosh s-1)})

and where

\begin{split}&A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\frac{e^{i\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\,A(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}}}{4\pi^{2}}\\
&\times\big(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\pi]}(|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|)+e^{-i2\pi\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{[\pi,2\pi)}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+e^{i2\pi\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{(-2\pi,-\pi]}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big),\end{split}

(2.7)

and

\begin{split}&B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}e^{-i\alpha(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+i\int_{\theta_{2}}^{\theta_{1}}A(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}}\Big(\sin(|\alpha|\pi)e^{-|\alpha|s}\\
&\qquad+\sin(\alpha\pi)\frac{(e^{-s}-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi))\sinh(\alpha s)-i\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)\cosh(\alpha s)}{\cosh(s)-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)}\Big).\end{split}

(2.8)

Remark 2.1
.

From (
2.6
), we see that the resolvent kernel consists of two terms.
The first one is close to the resolvent kernel of
-\Delta
but with
A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
due to the long-range perturbation of the potential
\frac{{{\bf A}}(\hat{x})}{|x|}
.
The second one arises from the diffractive effect, and
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
is singular when
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\to\pi
and
s\to 0
. As explained above, both terms appear
in the kernel of the Bochner-Riesz means.

Remark 2.2
.

In particular, if the total magnetic flux satisfies
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
vanishes and
A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
becomes a constant.
The resolvent is consistent with the resolvent kernel of the Euclidean Laplacian without magnetic potential. This corresponds to the physical fact that magnetic Schrödinger operators are unitarily equivalent.

Remark 2.3
.

The scaling-critical magnetic Schrödinger operators (
1.1
) are essentially equivalent to the Aharonov–Bohm Schrödinger operator
after a gauge transformation. Specifically, from (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), one can observe the kernel identity

{e^{-i\phi(\theta_{1})}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)\big)^{-1}(x,y){e^{i\phi(\theta_{2})}}=\big(\mathcal{L}_{{\alpha}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0)\big)^{-1}(x,y)

where
\phi(\theta)=\int_{0}^{\theta}(A(\theta^{\prime})-\alpha)\,d\theta^{\prime}
and the Aharonov–Bohm Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{{\alpha}}=\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}
with
{\bf A}(\hat{x})
given in (
1.3
).

Remark 2.4
.

Here we refine the
A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
of
[
31
]
by

A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\frac{e^{i\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}A(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}}}{4\pi^{2}}\big(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\pi]}(|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|)+e^{-i2\pi\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{[\pi,2\pi)}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+e^{i2\pi\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{(-2\pi,-\pi]}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big).

Since
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,2\pi)
, we have
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\in(-2\pi,2\pi)
, and thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\pi|\leq\pi\}}e^{z\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\pi)}e^{i(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2j\pi)\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\times\begin{cases}e^{z\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}e^{i(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\alpha}\quad&\text{if}\quad|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|<\pi\\
e^{z\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}e^{i(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}-2\pi)\alpha}\quad&\text{if}\quad\pi<\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}<2\pi\\
e^{z\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}e^{i(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+2\pi)\alpha}\quad&\text{if}\quad-2\pi<\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}<-\pi\\
e^{-z}\big(e^{i\pi\alpha}+e^{-i\pi\alpha}\big)\quad&\text{if}\quad|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|=\pi.\end{cases}

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

We outline the proof here for completeness and we refer to
[
31
,
26
]
for details. The outgoing resolvent can be written as

\big({\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)\big)^{-1}=-\frac{1}{i}\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it{\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}}e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\epsilon)}\;dt,

(2.9)

by observing that for
z=\lambda^{2}+i\epsilon\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\;{\rm Im}(z)>0\}

(s-z)^{-1}=-\frac{1}{i}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ist}e^{izt}\;dt,\quad\forall\;s\in\mathbb{R}.

We construct the Schrödinger propagator

\displaystyle e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{k}(\theta_{1})\overline{\varphi_{k}(\theta_{2})}K_{\nu_{k}}(t;r_{1},r_{2})

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-i\alpha(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+i\int_{\theta_{2}}^{\theta_{1}}A(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-ik(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}K_{\nu_{k}}(t;r_{1},r_{2}),

by making use of the spectral argument and the functional calculus. Here

\nu_{k}=|k+\alpha|,k\in\mathbb{Z}
is the square root of the eigenvalue of the operator
L_{{{\bf A}}}
in (
2.4
) and

\varphi_{k}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\big(\theta(k+\alpha)-\int_{0}^{\theta}A(\theta^{\prime})d\theta^{\prime}\big)}

(2.11)

is the corresponding eigenfunction.
The radial kernel
K_{\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle K_{\nu}(t,r_{1},r_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it\rho^{2}}J_{\nu}(r_{1}\rho)J_{\nu}(r_{2}\rho)\,\rho d\rho

(2.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\epsilon+it)\rho^{2}}J_{\nu}(r_{1}\rho)J_{\nu}(r_{2}\rho)\,\rho d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\frac{e^{-\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}{4(\epsilon+it)}}}{2(\epsilon+it)}I_{\nu}\Big(\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2(\epsilon+it)}\Big),

where we use the Weber identity
[
51
, Proposition 8.7]
and
I_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function

I_{\nu}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{z\cos s}\cos(\nu s)ds-\frac{\sin(\nu\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\cosh s}e^{-s\nu}ds.

2.2.
The kernel of Bochner-Riesz means

Let us recall that the Bochner-Riesz means of order

S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})=\Big(1-\frac{\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}_{+},\quad\lambda>0.

Here we use the distribution notation

, defined by
\chi_{+}^{a}(x)=x_{+}^{a}/\Gamma(a+1)
, where

is the gamma function and

0,\quad&\text{if}\,x<0.\end{cases}

(2.23)

By using Proposition
2.2
, the kernel can be represented as

\begin{split}\Big(1-\frac{\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}_{+}(x,y)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}_{+}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{{{\bf A}}}}}(\rho;x,y)\,d\rho\\
&=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Big(1-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}_{+}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{-i\rho(x-y)\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}A_{\alpha}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{-i\rho{\bf n}_{s}\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}\,B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})ds\right)\,d\rho.\end{split}

(2.24)

More precisely, the kernel representation of the Bochner-Riesz means is given by

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
x=r_{1}(\cos\theta_{1},\sin\theta_{1})
and
y=r_{2}(\cos\theta_{2},\sin\theta_{2})
.
Define

(2.25)

and

d_{s}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=|{\bf n}_{s}|=\sqrt{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\,\cosh s},\quad s\in[0,+\infty).

(2.26)

Then the kernel of the Bochner-Riesz means operator
S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})
can be written as

\begin{split}S_{\lambda}^{\delta}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}})(x,y)=&\Big(1-\frac{\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}{\lambda^{2}}\Big)^{\delta}_{+}(x,y)\\
&=G_{\lambda}(\delta;r_{1},\theta_{1};r_{2},\theta_{2})+D_{\lambda}(\delta;r_{1},\theta_{1};r_{2},\theta_{2}).\end{split}

(2.27)

Here

\begin{split}&G_{\lambda}(\delta;r_{1},\theta_{1};r_{2},\theta_{2})\\

\begin{split}&D_{\lambda}(\delta;r_{1},\theta_{1};r_{2},\theta_{2})\\

where
a_{j}(j=1,2)
are bounded from below near infinity and satisfy

\Big|\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\big)^{N}a_{j}(r)\Big|\leq C_{N}r^{-N},\quad r>0,\,\forall N\geq 0.

(2.30)

Remark 2.6

\Big|\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))(1-\chi(b))\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j+1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}}\,\frac{b}{s^{2}+b^{2}}\;ds\Big|\lesssim\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.19)

If

, the phase function satisfies that

\Big|\partial_{s}\big(2^{j+1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}\big)\Big|\geq 2^{j}r_{1}r_{2},

hence the integration by parts shows

\Big|\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))(1-\chi(b))\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j+1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}}\,\frac{b}{s^{2}+b^{2}}\;ds\Big|\lesssim\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-1}.

Since outside of the support of
\chi
, i.e.,
|b|\geq\epsilon
, we have

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{b}{s^{2}+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|ds\leq\frac{C}{|b|}\leq C_{\epsilon}.

By using the Van der Corput Lemma
2.3
, we have

\Big|\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))(1-\chi(b))\int_{0}^{1}e^{i2^{j+1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}}\,\frac{b}{s^{2}+b^{2}}\;ds\Big|\lesssim\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore we prove (
5.19
).

We next prove (
5.17
). The main difficulty arises when

. As

, due to the periodicity of the sine function, we have
\frac{1}{2}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)\to 0
,
\pi
, or
2\pi
. Without loss of generality, to prove (
5.17
), we only consider the case

\theta_{2}

\theta_{2}\pm 2\pi

Let
\theta=\frac{1}{2}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)
, and define

\begin{split}\tilde{H}=2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\chi(\theta)&(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\\
&\times a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j+1}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}s^{2}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2}\theta}{s^{2}+2\theta^{2}}\;ds.\end{split}

(5.20)

As

, and one can verify that

\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{2}\sin\theta}{s^{2}+2\sin^{2}\theta}-\frac{\sqrt{2}\theta}{s^{2}+2\theta^{2}}\Big)\Big|ds\leq C,\quad\theta\to 0.

The same argument as before, using the Van der Corput Lemma
2.3
, shows

\big|\chi(b)H(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta)\big|\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, it suffices to show (
5.17
) and (
5.16
) with
\chi(b)H
replaced by
\tilde{H}
. We first prove (
5.16
). For
p\geq 2
, we write

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}\;dr_{2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}(\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\;r_{2}\;dr_{2}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{r_{1}dr_{1}}([0,+\infty)}.\end{split}

(5.21)

Using the convolution kernel and Plancherel’s theorem, for

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}(\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\lesssim\|\hat{\tilde{H}}(r_{1},r_{2};\zeta)\|_{L^{\infty}_{\zeta}(\mathbb{R})}\big\|\widehat{f\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}}(r_{2},\zeta)\big\|_{L_{\zeta}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.\end{split}

Lemma 5.3

Let
\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta)
be defined in (

\frac{1}{|\zeta|},\quad&|\zeta|\gg(2^{j}r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The function

given in (

\tilde{H}
in

Therefore, using the fact that

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\;r_{2}\;dr_{2}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\

Now we prove (

\begin{split}\Big\|\tilde{H}_{\ell}(r_{1},r_{2};\cdot)\ast f(\theta_{1})\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}&\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\\

where

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}(\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{\theta_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\\

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tilde{H}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}(\theta_{2})f(r_{2},\theta_{2})\;d\theta_{2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(d\theta_{1}r_{1}dr_{1})}\;r_{2}\;dr_{2}\\

\begin{split}&\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\sum_{2^{\ell}\gg(2^{j}r_{1}r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}2^{-2\ell(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p})}\|(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,2\pi]}f)_{\ell}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\theta_{2}}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\;r_{1}\;dr_{1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\\

If

Lemma 5.4
.

Define

Proof.

Using the change of variables

, we have

I_{j}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta)=\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}\theta^{2}s^{2}}\,\frac{2}{s^{2}+4}\;ds.

Recall the definition of

Now we can bound

\begin{split}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})&\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\big((2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)\big)\Big|\;ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big((2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)\,\frac{b^{2}}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\;ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big((2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)\,\frac{sb^{2}}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}}\Big)\Big|\;ds\Big)\lesssim 1,\end{split}

(6.11)

where we make the change of variables
s\to bs
to avoid the issue arising when both
s,b\to 0
.
Then, by applying the Van der Corput Lemma (Lemma
2.3
) again, we obtain

\begin{split}\Big|2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\int_{0}^{1}e^{i2^{j}\phi(s)}(2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}&a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)\frac{(-s+b^{2})(\alpha s)}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+b^{2}}\;ds\Big|\\
&\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}(2^{j}r_{1}r_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(6.12)

Therefore, we prove Lemma
5.1
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
5.2
:

We first prove (
5.13
). Arguing similarly to Lemma
5.1
,
it suffices to show, when
r_{1}+r_{2}\sim 1
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\big|\partial_{s}\psi_{3,e}(r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2};s)\big|\,ds\lesssim 1,\end{split}

(6.13)

uniformly in
r_{1},r_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
. For our purpose, when
r_{1}+r_{2}\sim 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big[\Big((2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\Big(\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)\cosh(\alpha s)}{\cosh(s)-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)}-\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+2\sin^{2}\big(\tfrac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi}{2}\big)}\Big)\Big]\Big|\,ds\lesssim 1,

(6.14)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{s}\Big[\Big((2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}|{\bf n}_{s}|)

\displaystyle\qquad-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}a(2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2}))\Big)\frac{\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi)}{\frac{s^{2}}{2}+2\sin^{2}\big(\tfrac{\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+\pi}{2}\big)}\Big]\Big|\,ds\lesssim 1,

(6.15)

which can be verified by modifying
[
27
, Lemma 5.1]
and by replacing
|{\bf n}_{s}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
by
(2^{-j}+|{\bf n}_{s}|)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}
.

We next prove (
5.14
). We will use the Morse Lemma to express the phase function as a quadratic form via a change of variables.
Let

\bar{\varphi}(s)=\frac{\phi(s)-(r_{1}+r_{2})}{r_{1}r_{2}}=\frac{\big(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}\cosh s\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{r_{1}r_{2}}-\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{r_{1}r_{2}},

then
\bar{\varphi}(0)=\bar{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)=0
and

\bar{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{r_{1}+r_{2}}\neq 0.

Let

g(s)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)\bar{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(ts)dt,

(6.16)

then we can write

\bar{\varphi}(s)=g(s)s^{2}.

We write

\phi(s)-(r_{1}+r_{2})=r_{1}r_{2}\bar{\varphi}(s)=r_{1}r_{2}g(s)s^{2}.

Choose
\tilde{s}
such that

\frac{\tilde{s}^{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}=g(s)s^{2},

then our aim is to estimate

\displaystyle\big|e^{-i2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2})}\tilde{K}_{D,m}^{3,j}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-H(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}g(s)s^{2}}\psi_{3,m}(s)\;ds-(r_{1}+r_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j}\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{r_{1}+r_{2}}\tilde{s}^{2}}\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})\;d\tilde{s}\Big|\Big).

Since
g(0)=\bar{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)=(r_{1}+r_{2})^{-1}\neq 0
, we make the change of
variables

\tilde{s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g(0)}}g(s)^{\frac{1}{2}}s.

Then we obtain

\frac{d\tilde{s}}{ds}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g(0)}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}g^{-\frac{1}{2}}(s)g^{\prime}(s)s+g^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)\Big):=F(s).

(6.17)

It suffices to estimate

\displaystyle\big|e^{-i2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2})}\tilde{K}_{D,m}^{3,j}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-H(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}g(s)s^{2}}\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F(s)\Big)\;ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\beta(r_{1}+r_{2})\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{1}e^{i2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}g(s)s^{2}}\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F(s)\Big)\;ds\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\Big|\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{i2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}g(s)s^{2}}\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F(s)\Big)\;ds\Big|\Big)

\displaystyle:=I+II.

We first consider
II
. When
s\in[1,+\infty)
, there is no critical point for the phase function and
the derivative of the phase function
\phi=g(s)s^{2}
is monotonic. By using Van der Corput Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle II\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-1}.

(6.18)

We next consider
I
.
Furthermore, we observe that

\bar{\varphi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=0,\implies F^{\prime}(0)=0,

then by the Taylor expansion, we have

F(s)=F(0)+F^{\prime}(0)s+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\theta s)s^{2}=1+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\theta s)s^{2},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 1.

Therefore, we obtain

\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F(s)\Big)=\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F^{\prime\prime}(\theta s)s^{2}.

By using Van der Corput Lemma
2.3
as before, the first term is bounded by

I\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

if we could verify that there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\big(\psi_{3,m}(1)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s}_{0})\big)\Big|+|\frac{1}{2}\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s}_{0})F^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\leq C,\quad\tilde{s}_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g(0)}}g(s)^{\frac{1}{2}}s\big|_{s=1},

(6.19)

and when
s\in[0,1]
and
\tilde{s}\in[0,\tilde{s}_{0}]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\psi_{3,m}(s)-\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})\Big)\Big|+\Big|\frac{d}{ds}\Big(\psi_{3,m}(\tilde{s})F^{\prime\prime}(\theta s)s^{2}\Big)\Big|\,ds\leq C.

(6.20)

which can be verified as in
[
27
, Lemma 5.3]
.
Therefore, as desired, the difference is bounded by

\big|e^{-i2^{j}(r_{1}+r_{2})}\tilde{K}_{D,m}^{3,j}(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})-H(r_{1},r_{2};\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\big|\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{3}{2}+\delta)}\big(1+2^{j}r_{1}r_{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

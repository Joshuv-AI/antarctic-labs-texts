Jones polynomials from matrix elements of tangles in a pseudounitary representation
Dmitry Melnikov

Abstract
In these notes we review the calculation of Jones polynomials using a matrix representation of the braid group and Temperley-Lieb algebra. The pseudounitary representation that we consider allows constructing “states” from the group/algebra matrices and compute the knot invariants as matrix elements, rather than traces. In comparison with a more standard way of computing the invariants through traces, the matrix element method is more interesting and complete from the point of view of applications. As a byproduct of the discussion we prove a general formula for pretzel knots.

In order to compute the Jones polynomials we will use a specific R matrix representation of the braid group:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle R\ =\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}A&&&\\
&A-A^{-3}&A^{-1}&\\
&A^{-1}&0&\\
&&&A\end{array}\right).\end{array}

(2)

This R matrix is a well-known solution of the parameter-independent Yang-Baxter equation
[
5
,
25
]

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle(R\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes R)(R\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\ =\ (\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes R)(R\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes R)\,,\end{array}

(3)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{2}
is a
2\times 2
identity matrix. A representation of the Artin’s braid group can then be obtained from tensor products of
R
and
\mathbbm{1}_{2}
:

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,,

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes R\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle b_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes R\,.

Here each product contains
n-1
terms, so that
b_{k}
are the generators of the Artin’s braid group
B_{n}
on
n
strands.

Given the R matrix one can construct the generators of the Temperley-Lieb algebra. In particular, considering

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle R\ =\ A\,\mathbbm{1}_{4}+A^{-1}\,\mathbb{U}\,,\end{array}

(5)

where

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\mathbb{U}\ =\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&&&\\
&-A^{2}&1&\\
&1&-A^{-2}&\\
&&&0\end{array}\right),\end{array}

(6)

it is straightforward to check that

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{2}\ =\ d\,\mathbb{U}\,,\qquad d\ =\ -A^{2}-A^{-2}\,,\end{array}

(7)

and

\displaystyle(\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U})(\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})\,,

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U})(\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2})(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U})\,,

(8)

which are the defining relations of the Temperley-Lieb algebra. For the Temperley-Lieb algebra
TL_{n}
on
n
strands, one consequently defines the generators as

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,,

\displaystyle u_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle u_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{U}\,.

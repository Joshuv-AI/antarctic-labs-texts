Abstract
. In 2000, Hafner and Stopple proved a conjecture of Zagier which states that the constant term of the automorphic function | Δ ⁡ ( x + i ​ y ) | 2 |\Delta(x+iy)|^{2} i.e., the Lambert series ∑ n = 1 ∞ τ ​ ( n ) 2 ​ e − 4 ​ π ​ n ​ y \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)^{2}e^{-4\pi ny} can be expressed in terms of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. In this article, we study a certain Lambert series associated to Siegel cusp forms and observe a similar phenomenon.



1.
Introduction

Let
\zeta(s)
and
\mu(n)
denote the Riemann zeta function and the Möbius function, respectively. It is well-known that the reciprocal of the Riemann zeta function is the Dirichlet series associated to
\mu(n).
Ramanujan, in his second notebook
[
34
, p. 312]
,
[
5
, eq. (37.3), p. 470]
mentioned the following identity associated to
\mu(n)
:

For a positive real number
x
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left({-\frac{x}{n^{2}}}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{x}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}}{n^{2}x}\right).

However, the above identity is not correct. The corrected version of the above identity was obtained by Hardy and Littlewood
[
18
, p. 156]
. Assuming all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
are simple, they proved that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left({-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left({-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{\rho}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right)^{\rho},

(1.1)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are two positive real numbers with
\alpha\beta=1
and the sum over
\rho
runs through all the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. The convergence of the series (
1
) is quite intricate. In the same paper, Hardy and Littlewood mentioned that they were unable to prove the convergence of this series even after assuming the Riemann hypothesis. However, in an indirect way, they showed the convergence of this series under the assumption of the following bracketing condition on the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, namely,
the non-trivial zeros
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
of
\zeta(s)
are included in the same bracket if they satisfy

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})-\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})|<\exp\left(-\frac{A_{0}\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1})}{\log(\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{1}))}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{A_{0}\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2})}{\log(\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{2}))}\right),

(1.2)

where
A_{0}
is some positive constant. The identity (
1
) also inspired Hardy and Littlewood to obtain the following equivalent criterion for the Riemann hypothesis (RH):

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n}\exp\left({-\frac{x}{n^{2}}}\right)=O\left(x^{-\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\,\,x\rightarrow\infty,

(1.3)

Over the time, the above equivalent criterion for RH has inspired mathematicians to find different generalization of (
1
) and (
1.3
) for
L
-functions associated to several objects. For more details on this topic we refer to
[
1
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
. We would like to mention here that, we also encounter an infinite series similar to the series (
1
) in the main result of this paper.

Let
\Delta(z)
be the Ramanujan delta cusp form. The Fourier series expansion of
\Delta(z)
is given by

\Delta(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau(n)\exp(2n\pi iz),~~z\in\mathcal{H}.

The Fourier coefficients
\tau(n)
is known as Ramanujan tau function. In 1981, Zagier
[
36
, p. 417]
,
[
37
, p. 271]
speculated that the constant term of the automorphic function
\mathcal{F}(z):=y^{12}|\Delta(x+iy)|^{2}
, that is, the following Lambert series

c_{0}(y):=y^{12}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{2}(n)\exp({-4\pi ny}),

(1.4)

has an oscillatory behaviour when
y\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
Moreover, Zagier conjectured that its asymptotic expansion can be written in terms of the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.
Another interesting point he observed that one can numerically obtain non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
using the asymptotic expansion of (
1.4
) and the Ramanujan tau function.
Further, he claimed that the constant term
c_{0}(y)
satisfies the following asymptotic expansion:

\displaystyle c_{0}(y)\sim\mathcal{A}+\sum_{\rho}y^{1-\frac{\rho}{2}}A_{\rho},

where the sum over
\rho
runs through the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
,
\mathcal{A}=\frac{3}{\pi}\langle\Delta,\Delta\rangle
, and the constant
A_{\rho}
depends on
\rho
. Assuming the Riemann hypothesis, the above asymptotic expansion and the oscillatory behaviour of
c_{0}(y)
have been proved by Hafner and Stopple
[
17
]
. A similar phenomenon for Hecke eigenforms for the full modular group as well as for the congruence subgroups have been observed in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. Subsequently, an asymptotic expansion of an infinite series associated to a Hecke-Maass eigenforms has been studied by Banerjee and Chakraborty
[
6
]
. Recently, this problem in the case of Hilbert modular forms has been examined by Agnihotri
[
2
]
. To know more about similar problems inspired from the above conjecture of Zagier, we refer to
[
21
,
22
,
29
,
30
]
.

Siegel modular forms are generalization of elliptic modular forms to higher dimension. They were first introduced by Siegel to study quadratic forms. A Siegel modular forms admits a Fourier-Jacobi expansion and the Fourier-Jacobi coefficients are Jacobi forms. Motivated from the above mentioned conjecture of Zagier, in this paper, we study certain Lambert series associated with the Fourier-Jacobi coefficients of Siegel modular forms. Interestingly, a similar phenomenon has been observed as in the case of elliptic modular forms. We use the analytic properties of certain Dirichlet series associated with Siegel modular forms (studied by Kohnen and Skoruppa
[
23
]
), bounds of the Whittaker function, and a special evaluation of the Meijer
G
-function to prove our result.

In the next section, we provide necessary backgrounds which are essential to define the main problem of our article.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{H}
denote the complex plane and complex upper half-plane, respectively. For two matrices
A
and
B
of appropriate sizes, we define
A[B]:={B}^{t}AB,
where
B^{t}
is the transpose of the matrix
B.
We define
J:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&{I}\\
{-I}&0\end{array}\right),
where
I
denotes the
2\times 2
identity matrix. The Siegel upper half plane
\mathcal{H}_{2}
of degree
2
is defined as

\mathcal{H}_{2}:=\{Z\in M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{C})~|~Z^{t}=Z,\operatorname{Im}(Z)~\text{is positive definite}\}.

The full Siegel modular group
\Gamma_{2}:=Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
of genus
2
is defined as

\Gamma_{2}:=\left\{M\in M_{4\times 4}(\mathbb{Z}),J[M]=J\right\}.

If we write the matrix
M\in\Gamma_{2}
as a block decomposition
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}
, where
A,B,C,D\in M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{Z}),
then we have

\Gamma_{2}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}\bigg|A,B,C,D\in M_{2\times 2}(\mathbb{Z}),A^{t}C=C^{t}A,B^{t}D=D^{t}B,A^{t}D-B^{t}C=I_{2}\right\}.

The group
\Gamma_{2}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{2}
as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}\cdot Z=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}.

Definition 2.1
.

A complex-valued holomorphic function
F:\mathcal{H}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be a Siegel modular form of weight
k\in\mathbb{N}
and degree two if it satisfies

F(M\cdot Z)={\det}(CZ+D)^{k}F(Z),

for all
M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{2}
,
Z\in\mathcal{H}_{2},
and having a Fourier expansion of the form

F(Z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\geq 0\end{subarray}}A_{F}(T)e^{2\pi i(tr(TZ))},

(2.1)

where the summation runs over positive semidefinite half-integral
2\times 2
matrices
T=(t_{ij})
(
i.e.,2t_{ij},\;t_{ii}\in\mathbb{Z}
) and
tr(\cdot)
denotes the trace function.

Further, we say
F
is a Siegel cusp form if and only if the summation in (
2.1
) runs over positive definite half-integral matrices
T.

We denote the space of Siegel modular forms and Siegel cusp forms of weight
k
and degree
2
on
\Gamma_{2}
by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{2})
and
S_{k}(\Gamma_{2}),
respectively.
The Petersson scalar product on
S_{k}(\Gamma_{2})
is defined as

\langle F,G\rangle:=\int_{\Gamma_{2}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{2}}F(Z)\overline{G(Z)}(det~Y)^{k}dZ,

where
Z=X+iY
and
dZ=(det~Y)^{-3}dXdY
is an invariant measure under the action of
\Gamma_{2}
on
\mathcal{H}_{2}.
The space
(S_{k}(\Gamma_{2}),\langle,\rangle)
is a finite dimensional Hilbert space. For more details on the theory on Siegel modular forms, we refer to
[
8
,
32
]
.

2.1.
Fourier-Jacobi expansion

Let
F(Z)\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{2})
be a Siegel modular form with the Fourier series expansion as in (
2.1
).

Write
Z=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&z\\
z&\tau^{\prime}\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
where
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H},~z\in\mathbb{C}.
A matrix
T
in the summation (
2.1
) can be written as
T=\begin{pmatrix}m&r/2\\
r/2&n\\
\end{pmatrix}
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N},r\in\mathbb{Z}
and
4mn-r^{2}\geq 0.

Then the Fourier series expansion (
2.1
) becomes

\displaystyle F(Z)=\sum_{T\geq 0}A_{F}(T)\exp({2\pi i(tr(TZ))})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n,r\\
4mn-r^{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}A_{F}\left(\begin{pmatrix}m&r/2\\
r/2&n\\
\end{pmatrix}\right)\exp({2\pi i(m\tau+rz+n\tau^{\prime})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(\tau,z)\exp({2\pi in\tau^{\prime}}),

where

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(\tau,z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,r\\
4mn-r^{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}A_{F}\left(\begin{pmatrix}m&r/2\\
r/2&n\\
\end{pmatrix}\right)\exp({2\pi i(m\tau+rz)}).

The coefficient
\phi_{n}(\tau,z)
is called the
n
th Fourier-Jacobi coefficients of
F
and is a Jacobi form of weight
k
and index
n
. Jacobi forms are natural generalization of modular forms. Jacobi forms were first systematically studied by Eichler and Zagier and they played a key role in the proof of Saito-Kurokawa conjecture. For more details on the theory of Jacobi forms, we refer to
[
14
]
. It is easy to check that if
F\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{2}),
then its Fourier-Jacobi expansion is given by
F(Z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(\tau,z)\exp({2\pi in\tau^{\prime}})
and each of the Fourier-Jacobi coefficients are Jacobi cusp forms.

2.2.
Dirichlet series associated to Siegel cusp forms

We now define the Rankin-Selberg type Dirichlet series associated to Siegel cusp forms
F_{1},F_{2}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{2})
with the Fourier-Jacobi expansion as follows:

F_{1}(Z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(\tau,z)\exp({2\pi in\tau^{\prime}}),~~~~~F_{2}(Z)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_{n}(\tau,z)\exp({2\pi in\tau^{\prime}})

(2.2)

\displaystyle D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s):=\zeta(2s-2k+4)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle}{n^{s}},

(2.3)

where
\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle
denotes the Petersson scalar product (defined in
[
14
, p. 27, eq. (13)]
) of Jacobi cusp forms
\phi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}.

It is well-known
[
23
, p. 544, Lemma 1]
that the Petersson scalar product of the Fourier-Jacobi coefficients
\phi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
satisfies

\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle=O(n^{k}).

Further, Kohnen
[
25
, p. 718]
,
[
28
, p. 134]
made the following Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture: for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{n},\phi_{n}\rangle=O_{F_{1},\epsilon}\left(n^{k-1+\epsilon}\right).

(2.4)

Kohnen and Sengupta
[
27
]
proved the above conjecture for Hecke eigenform
F\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{2})
which is a Saito-Kurokawa lift. Recently, Kumar and Paul
[
31
]
proved that the above conjecture is true on average for arbitrary degree. Assuming (
2.4
), one can clearly see that the Dirichlet series
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>k
.

In the next section we state the main results of our paper.

3.
Main Results

As already mentioned in the introduction, we are interested to study certain Lambert series associated to Siegel cusp forms. More precisely, for
\alpha>0
, we study the following Lambert series:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha),

(3.1)

where
\phi_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
are defined as in (
2.2
).
Before stating the main result of this article, we define an arithmetic function
a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)
that satisfy the following generating function:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)}{\zeta(2s+1-2k)},

(3.2)

which is absolutely convergent for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>k
. Let
W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)
be the well-known Whittakar function which is one of the solutions of the following second order differential equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}w}{dz^{2}}+\left(\frac{1-4\mu^{2}+4\kappa z-z^{2}}{4z^{2}}\right)w=0.

With the above notation and definitions in our hand, we are ready to state the main result of this article.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two Siegel cusp forms of weight
k
and degree
2
with the Fourier-Jacobi expansion given as in (
2.2
) and satisfying (
2.4
). Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two positive real numbers such that
\alpha\beta=1
. Under the assumption of the simplicity hypothesis of the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2k-2}}{\pi^{3/2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)}{(4\pi n\beta)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}\exp(-2\pi n\beta)W_{\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}-1}(4\pi n\beta)

\displaystyle+R_{k}+\sum_{\rho}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+k-2\right)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+k-2\right)(4\pi\alpha)^{2-k-\frac{\rho}{2}}}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)},

where the sum over
\rho
runs through non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
and satisfy the bracketing condition (
1.2
) and the term
R_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{k}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle=0,\\
\frac{90\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle}{\pi^{2}\alpha^{k}},&\text{ if }\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle\neq 0.\end{cases}

(3.3)

As an application of the above theorem, we have the following asymptotic expansion of the Lambert series defined in (
3.1
).

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be as in Theorem
3.1
.
If
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle\neq 0
, then for
\alpha\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha)\sim\frac{90\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle}{\pi^{2}}.

(3.4)

In the next section, we collect a few results which will be essential for the proof of our main results.

4.
Some Well-known Results

We complete the Dirichlet series
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
associated to the Siegel cusp forms
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
of weight
k
and degree
2
as follows:

D^{*}_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s):=(2\pi)^{-2s}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s-k+2)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s).

The analytic properties of the above completed Dirichlet series is given below.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
23
]

The Dirichlet series
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
can be analytically continued to an entire function if
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle=0
. However, if
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle\neq 0
, then
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=k
with residue
\frac{4^{k}\pi^{k+2}}{(k-1)!}\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle
.
The functional equation of
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
is given by

D^{*}_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)=D_{F_{1},F_{2}}^{*}(2k-2-s).

(4.1)

The above result will play a crucial role in the proof of the Theorem
3.1
. Next, we define a well-known special function known as Meijer
G
-function.

Let
m,n,p,q
be integers with
0\leq m\leq q
,
0\leq n\leq p
. Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}
be
p+q
complex numbers such that
a_{i}-b_{j}\not\in\mathbb{N}
, for
i\in[1,n]
,
j\in[1,m]
. The Meijer
G
-function
[
33
, p. 415]
is defined by the following line integral:

\displaystyle G_{p,q}^{\,m,n}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}a_{1},\cdots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\cdots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{L}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}+w)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}-w)z^{-w}}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}-w)\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}+w)}\mathrm{d}w.

(4.2)

Here, we assume that the line of integration
L
separates the poles of the factors
\Gamma(b_{j}+w)
from the poles of the factors
\Gamma(1-a_{j}-w)
. The above integral converges absolutely if
2(m+n)>p+q

and
|\arg(z)|<(2m+2n-p-q)\frac{\pi}{2}
.
We now state the following special evaluation of the Meijer
G
-function.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
20
, p. 58]

For
|\arg(z)|<\frac{\pi}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle G_{1,2}^{\,2,0}\!\left(\,\begin{matrix}a_{1}\\
b_{1},b_{2}\end{matrix}\;\Big|z\right)=z^{\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}-1}{2}}e^{-z/2}W_{\kappa,\mu}(z),

(4.3)

where
W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)
is the Whittaker function with the variables
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{2}-1)-a_{1}
and
\mu=\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}-b_{2})
.

The next lemma tells us about the behaviour of the Whittaker function for large argument.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
33
, p. 69, eq. (2.11.29)]

As
z\rightarrow\infty
with
|\arg(z)|<3\frac{\pi}{2}
, one has

\displaystyle W_{\kappa,\mu}(z)\sim\exp(-z/2)z^{\kappa}.

Lemma 4.4

(Stirling’s bound for
\Gamma(z)
)
.

[
19
, p. 151, A.4.]

Let
z=\sigma+i{T}
with
p\leq\sigma\leq q.
Then we have

|\Gamma(\sigma+i{T})|\sim\sqrt{2\pi}|{T}|^{\sigma-1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi|{T}|},\quad{\rm as}\quad|{T}|\rightarrow\infty.

(4.4)

Lemma 4.5
.

[
35
, p. 219]

Let
T_{n}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
|T_{n}-\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|>\exp\left(-C\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\rho)}{\log(\operatorname{Im}(\rho))}\right)
for any non-trivial zeros
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
, where
C
is some positive constant. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\zeta(x+iT_{n})|}<\exp(C_{1}T_{n}),

where
0<C_{1}<\pi/4
.

In the next section, we present a proof of the Theorem
3.1
.

5.
Proof of main results

Using the inverse Mellin transform of the gamma function
\Gamma(s)
, we see that the inverse Mellin transform of the function
\frac{\Gamma(s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)(4\pi)^{-s}}{\zeta(2s-2k+4)}
is the infinite series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha).

Therefore, for some large positive constant
c>k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{\Gamma(s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)(4\pi\alpha)^{-s}}{\zeta(2s-2k+4)}\mathrm{d}s.

(5.1)

Here and throughout the article the symbol
(c)
denotes the line integral
c-i\infty
to
c+i\infty
. Now we recall from the Theorem
4.1
that the Rankin-Selberg Dirchlet series
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
can be extended to an entire function if
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle=0
. However, if
\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle\neq 0
, then
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=k
with residue
\frac{4^{k}\pi^{k+2}}{(k-1)!}\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle
. Moreover, from the functional equation of
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
it is clear that the poles of
\Gamma(s)
are neutralized by
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
. Nevertheless, the non-trivial of zeros of
\zeta(2s-2k+4)
will give us infinitely many poles of the integrand function in the strip
k-2<\operatorname{Re}(s)<k-\frac{3}{2}
, and the trivial zeros of
\zeta(2s-2k+4)
are at
k-n
for
n\geq 3
. Thus, we construct a closed rectangular contour
\mathcal{C}
consisting of the end points
c-iT,c+iT,c_{1}+iT,c_{1}-iT
, where
T
is some large positive constant. Here, we consider
c_{1}\in(k-3,k-2)
so that the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(2s-2k+4)
lie inside the contour
\mathcal{C}
and the trivial zeros of
\zeta(2s-2k+4)
lie outside the contour
\mathcal{C}
. Now, applying the Cauchy’s residue theorem, we obtain

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\Gamma{(s)}D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)(4\pi\alpha)^{-s}}{\zeta(2s-2k+4)}\mathrm{d}s=R_{k}+\sum_{|\operatorname{Im}(\rho)|<T}R_{\rho},

(5.2)

where the term
R_{k}
denotes the residual term at
s=k
and
R_{\rho}
denotes the residual term at the non-trivial zero
\rho
of
\zeta(s)
.
The residual term
R_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle R_{k}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle=0,\\
\frac{90\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle}{\pi^{2}\alpha^{k}},&\text{ if }\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle\neq 0.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Here, we have used the fact that
\zeta(4)=\pi^{4}/90
. Now we assume the grand simplicity hypothesis, which states that the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
are simple. This gives us the following evaluation of
R_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle R_{\rho}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+k-2\right)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+k-2\right)(4\pi\alpha)^{2-k-\frac{\rho}{2}}}{\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)}.

(5.4)

Letting
T\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.2
) and utilizing Stirling’s bound (
4.4
) for
\Gamma(s)
and Lemma
4.5
, one can show that both the horizontal integrals will vanish. Finally, using (
5.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},\psi_{n}\rangle\exp(-4\pi n\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)(4\pi\alpha)^{-s}}{\zeta(2s-2k+4)}\mathrm{d}s+R_{k}+\sum_{\rho}R_{\rho},

(5.5)

where the sum over
\rho
runs through the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
. Here, we emphasize that the sum over
\rho
is an infinite sum as we know the existence of infinitely many zeros of
\zeta(s)
from Hardy’s theorem
[
35
, p. 257]
. However, the convergence of this series is quite delicate as we do not know much about the lower bound for
\zeta^{\prime}(s)
. Thus, under the assumption of convergence of the sum in (
5.5
) one can proceed further. Now our main aim is to simplify the following left vertical integral:

\displaystyle V_{k}(\alpha):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)(4\pi\alpha)^{-s}}{\zeta(2s-2k+4)}\mathrm{d}s.

(5.6)

From the functional equation of
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)
, i.e., Theorem
4.1
, one obtains

\displaystyle\Gamma(s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(s)=\frac{(2\pi)^{4s-4k+4}\Gamma(2k-s-2)\Gamma(k-s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(2k-2-s)}{\Gamma(s-k+2)}.

(5.7)

Also, the symmetric form of the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function implies that

\displaystyle\zeta(2s-2k+4)=\frac{\pi^{2s-2k+4}\Gamma\left(k-s-\frac{3}{2}\right)\zeta(2k-2s-3)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(s-k+2)}.

(5.8)

Now substituting (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) in (
5.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle V_{k}(\alpha)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{16^{k-1}\pi^{2k}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(2k-2-s)\Gamma(k-s)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(2k-2-s)}{\Gamma\left(k-s-\frac{3}{2}\right)\zeta(2k-2s-3)}(4\pi\beta)^{s}\mathrm{d}s,

(5.9)

where
\alpha\beta=1
.
To simplify further, we make a change of variable
w=2k-2-s
and upon simplification, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{k}(\alpha)=\frac{\beta^{2k-2}}{\pi^{3/2}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(d_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(w)\Gamma(w-k+2)D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(w)(4\pi\beta)^{-w}}{\Gamma\left(w-k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\zeta(2w+1-2k)}\mathrm{d}w.

(5.10)

One can easily verify that
k<\operatorname{Re}(w)=d_{1}<k+1
as we have assumed
k-3<\operatorname{Re}(s)=c_{1}<k-2
. This allows us to write the infinite series expansions for
D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(w)
and
1/\zeta(2w+1-2k)
as both the series are absolutely and uniformly convergent in the region
\operatorname{Re}(w)>k
.
Let us write

\displaystyle\frac{D_{F_{1},F_{2}}(w)}{\zeta(2w+1-2k)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)}{n^{w}},~~\operatorname{Re}(w)>k.

(5.11)

Now plugging the above series expansion (
5.11
) in (
5.10
) and interchanging the summation and integration, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{k}(\alpha)=\frac{\beta^{2k-2}}{\pi^{3/2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)I_{k}(n,\beta),

(5.12)

where

\displaystyle I_{k}(n,\beta):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(d_{1})}\frac{\Gamma(w)\Gamma(w-k+2)(4\pi n\beta)^{-w}}{\Gamma\left(w-k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\mathrm{d}w.

(5.13)

Now invoking the definition (
4.2
) of the Meijer
G
-function with
m=2,n=0,p=1,q=2
and
a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}-k,b_{1}=0,b_{2}=2-k
, we see that the above integral is the following Meijer
G
-function:

\displaystyle I_{k}(n,\beta)=G_{1,2}^{2,0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{2}-k\\
0,2-k\end{array}\Big|4\pi n\beta\right).

One can easily verify that the above Meijer
G
-function does satisfy all the convergence criteria.
Now employing Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{k}(n,\beta)=(4\pi n\beta)^{\frac{1-k}{2}}W_{k/2,k/2-1}(4\pi n\beta)\exp(-2\pi n\beta).

(5.16)

Substituting (
5.16
) in (
5.12
), the final expression of the left vertical integral becomes

\displaystyle V_{k}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2k-2}}{\pi^{3/2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)(4\pi n\beta)^{\frac{1-k}{2}}W_{\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}-1}(4\pi n\beta)\exp(-2\pi n\beta).

(5.17)

Now applying the asymptotic expansion of the Whittaker function (Lemma
4.3
), one can easily verify the convergence of the above infinite series since the arithmetic function
a_{F_{1},F_{2}}(n)
has polynomial growth and the Whittaker function decays exponentially. Finally,
considering the expression (
5.17
) of the left vertical integral
V_{k}(\alpha)
in (
5.5
), together with residual terms (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.1
, we have started with the assumption on
\alpha
,
\beta
that

. Therefore,

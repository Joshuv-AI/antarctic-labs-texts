Shared values of meromorphic functions on a compact Riemann surface Thanks: The authors thank the journal’s editors for their encouragement to write this paper. The second author thanks the National Science Foundation for support under grant DMS-1601844.
Zhiguo Ding, Michael E. Zieve

Abstract
. We prove a new bound on the number of shared values of distinct meromorphic functions on a compact Riemann surface, explain a mistake in a previous paper on this topic, and give a survey of related questions.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
, and let
p
and
q
be distinct nonconstant meromorphic functions on
X
.
Let
S
be the set of all points
\alpha
in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
for which the sets
p^{-1}(\alpha)
and
q^{-1}(\alpha)
are identical. The main result of
[
3
]
asserts that

(1.1)

(\lvert S\rvert-4)\cdot(\deg p+\deg q)\leq 2g-2.

However, as we will explain, and as the author of
[
3
]
has confirmed via email, the argument in
[
3
]
contains a crucial mistake so that it does not prove (
1.1
); moreover, currently it is not known whether (
1.1
) is always true. We will prove the following inequality, which is stronger than all inequalities in the literature that resemble (
1.1
) (other than (
1.1
) itself):

Theorem 1.2
.

We have

(1.3)

(\lvert S\rvert-2)\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)\leq 2g-2+\deg p+\deg q.

Theorem
1.2
implies the following inequality from
[
13
]
, which more closely resembles (
1.1
):

Corollary 1.4
.

We have

(1.5)

(\lvert S\rvert-4)\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)\leq 2g-2.

In this note we prove these results, explain the step in the proof in
[
3
]
where the mistake occurs, and survey related topics.

2.
Proofs

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.4
. We maintain the notation from the first two sentences of the introduction.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

\deg(p-q)\leq\deg p+\deg q.

Proof.

Every order-
k
pole of
p-q
must be a pole of order at least
k
in at least one of
p
or
q
, so the sum of the orders of the poles of
p-q
is at most the sum of the orders of the poles of
p
and
q
. Since the sum of the orders of the poles of a meromorphic function on
X
equals the degree of the function, the conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
T
be a finite subset of
S
. Replace
p,q,T
by
\mu\circ p
,
\mu\circ q
, and
\mu(T)
for a suitable Möbius transformation
\mu(z)
, in order to assume that
\infty\notin T
.
The Riemann–Hurwitz formula for
p
asserts that

2g-2=-2\deg p+\sum_{\beta\in X}(\mult_{p}(\beta)-1),

where
\mult_{p}(\beta)
is the local multiplicity of
p
near
\beta
. For any
\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
we have
\sum_{\beta\in p^{-1}(\alpha)}\mult_{p}(\beta)=\deg p
, so that

(2.2)

\displaystyle 2g-2

\displaystyle\geq-2\deg p+\sum_{\alpha\in T}\sum_{\beta\in p^{-1}(\alpha)}(\mult_{p}(\beta)-1)

\displaystyle=-2\deg p+\sum_{\alpha\in T}(\deg p-\lvert p^{-1}(\alpha)\rvert)

\displaystyle=-2\deg p+\lvert T\rvert\deg p-\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert.

Likewise

(2.3)

2g-2\geq-2\deg q+\lvert T\rvert\deg q-\lvert q^{-1}(T)\rvert.

Since
p^{-1}(T)=q^{-1}(T)
by hypothesis, the previous two inequalities imply that

(2.4)

2g-2\geq(\lvert T\rvert-2)\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)-\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert.

Since
p^{-1}(T)=q^{-1}(T)
and
\infty\notin T
,
the set
p^{-1}(T)
is contained in the set of zeroes of the meromorphic function
p-q
. This function is nonzero by hypothesis, so it has at most as many zeroes as its degree. Thus Lemma
2.1
implies that

\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert\leq\deg(p-q)\leq\deg p+\deg q.

Combining this with (
2.4
) yields

(2.5)

2g-2\geq(\lvert T\rvert-2)\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)-(\deg p+\deg q).

Thus

(2.6)

\lvert T\rvert\leq 2+\frac{2g-2+\deg p+\deg q}{\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)},

so that in particular
T
is bounded.
It follows that
S
is finite, so we may choose
T
to be
S
in (
2.5
) in order to obtain (
1.3
), which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Combining (
1.3
) with the inequality

\deg p+\deg q\leq 2\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)

yields

\displaystyle(\lvert S\rvert-2)\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)

\displaystyle\leq 2g-2+\deg p+\deg q

\displaystyle\leq 2g-2+2\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q),

and subtracting
2\cdot\max(\deg p,\,\deg q)
from both sides yields (
1.5
).
∎

3.
The mistake in
[
3
]

We now compare the argument in
[
3
]
with our proof of Theorem
1.2
. The argument in
[
3
]
also uses the Riemann–Hurwitz formula to deduce the inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), and proves Lemma
2.1
by a different argument than ours. It then claims that (
1.1
) follows from these results and the inequality

(3.1)

2g-2\geq-2\deg(p-q)+(\deg(p-q)-\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert).

Equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) have the form
\lvert T\rvert\leq A+B\cdot\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert
where
A
and
B
are constants depending on
g
and the degrees of
p
and
q
, with
B>0
. Thus, in order to deduce an upper bound on
\lvert T\rvert
which depends only on
g
and the degrees of
p
and
q
, one needs an upper bound on
\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert
. However, (
3.1
) instead yields a lower bound on
\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert
,
and Lemma
2.1
does not involve
\lvert T\rvert
or
\lvert p^{-1}(T)\rvert
,
so it is not possible to
obtain an upper bound on
\lvert T\rvert
of the desired form by combining (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.1
.

It is reasonable to guess that the author of
[
3
]
was attempting to produce a similar proof to the one we gave for (
1.3
) (except for his different proof of Lemma
2.1
), since
[
3
]
begins by saying that its proof was suggested by reading
[
11
]
, and the argument in
[
11
]
is the specialization to the case
X=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
of the argument used in the same author’s previous paper
[
10
]
to prove a weaker version of Corollary
1.4
.

4.
Related results

We conclude with a quick survey of related literature, in which we continue to use the notation from the first two sentences of the introduction.

•

Another bound is
\lvert S\rvert\leq 2+\sqrt{2g+2}
, which improves the best bound deducible from Corollary
1.4
that depends only on
X
and not on the functions
p
and
q

[
13
]
.

•

Stronger bounds on
\lvert S\rvert
are known for special classes of Riemann surfaces: for instance, if
X
is hyperelliptic then
\lvert S\rvert\leq 5

[
13
]
.

•

Stronger bounds on
\lvert S\rvert
are known when at least one element of
S
has the same preimages
counting multiplicities
under
p
as it does under
q

[
10
,
13
]
.

•

Examples of
p,q,X
where
S
is “large” appear in
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
]
.

•

There are analogous results for holomorphic maps between prescribed compact Riemann surfaces
[
10
]
, and for holomorphic maps
p,q\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
having the same preimages of several hyperplanes
[
9
,
16
]
.

•

Results about “unique range sets” for meromorphic functions on
X
appear in
[
2
]
; by definition, such a set is a finite subset
T
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
for which any two distinct nonconstant meromorphic functions on
X
have distinct preimages of
T
.

•

If there are three disjoint nonempty finite subsets
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that each
T_{i}
has the same preimages under
p
(counting multiplicities) as it does under
q
, then
g\circ p=g\circ q
for some nonconstant rational function
g(z)

[
15
]
.

•

There are related results in case
X
is either the complement of a finite subset of a compact Riemann surface
[
14
]
, an open Riemann surface with a harmonic exhaustion
[
4
,
12
]
, or a higher-dimensional algebraic variety
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Probing proton structure with c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} correlations in ultraperipheral p ​ A pA collisions
Barbara Linek, Agnieszka Łuszczak, Marta Łuszczak, Roman Pasechnik, Wolfgang Schäfer, Antoni Szczurek

Abstract
We study the exclusive diffractive c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} photoproduction in ultraperipheral p ​ A pA collisions. The formalism makes use of off-diagonal generalizations of the unintegrated gluon distribution, the so-called generalized transverse momentum dependent distributions (GTMDs). We present two different formulations. The first one is based directly on gluon GTMD parametrizations in momentum space. Another option is the calculation of the GTMD as a Fourier transform of the dipole-nucleon scattering amplitude N ⁡ ( Y , r → ⟂ , b → ⟂ ) N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp}) . The latter approach requires some extra regularization discussed in the paper. Different dipole amplitudes from the literature are used. Compared to previous calculations in the literature, we integrate over the full phase space and therefore cross sections for realistic conditions are obtained. We present distributions in rapidity of c c or c ¯ \bar{c} , transverse momentum of the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} pair, four-momentum transfer squared as well as the azimuthal correlation between a sum and a difference of the c c and c ¯ \bar{c} transverse momenta. The azimuthal correlations are partially due to the so-called elliptic gluon Wigner distribution. Different models lead to different modulations in the azimuthal angle. The modulations are generally smaller than 5%. They depend on the range of transverse momentum selected for the calculation.



I
Introduction

Figure 1:
Feynman diagrams for the diffractive photoproduction of
q\bar{q}
pairs in nucleus-proton collisions, discussed in the present paper.

The diffractive production of high momentum particles, such as quark-antiquark dijets, serves as a probe of various aspects of the hadron structure
Ashery 2006
. For example, in the diffractive dissociation of photons or pions into dijets, the forward cross section at large jet transverse momenta maps out the unintegrated gluon distribution of the target
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1994a
;
Nikolaev et al. 2001
. Recently, there has been much interest in a generalization of unintegrated (or transverse momentum dependent) parton distributions to a five-dimensional quasi-probability phase space distribution, known as the Wigner distribution
Ji 2003
;
Belitsky et al. 2004
, which depends on both the transverse momentum and impact parameter of a parton in the proton or nucleus. When transformed fully to momentum space these are equivalent to the generalized transverse momentum distributions (GTMDs), see e.g.
Meissner et al. 2009
;
Lorcé and Pasquini 2013
;
Boussarie et al. 2023
. In particular, these distributions encode the dependence on the transverse momentum transfer
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
to the target and, therefore, are of relevance for the description of the forward cone in diffractive processes.

The Wigner distribution depends on the transverse momentum
\vec{k}_{\perp}
of partons as well as the impact parameter
\vec{b}_{\perp}
, including a dependence on the azimuthal angle between the two transverse vectors. In the GTMD approach this translates into a dependence on
\vec{k}_{\perp}
and
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
. This dependence gives rise to a so-called elliptic Wigner function or GTMD
Hagiwara et al. 2016
. In the region of small-
x
which is of interest in this paper, the equivalent color dipole approach for diffractive processes
Kopeliovich et al. 1981
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1992
can be used. Here, the dipole amplitude depending on dipole size
\vec{r}_{\perp}
and impact parameter
\vec{b}_{\perp}
contains the same information as the Wigner function/GTMD. The azimuthal angle correlation discussed above translates then into a dependence on the dipole orientation with respect to the background color field of the target. In inclusive processes this dependence can give rise to flow-like azimuthal correlations, for example in prompt photons production
Kopeliovich et al. 2008
, or in inclusive two-particles’ production
Iancu and Rezaeian 2017
.

It was advocated that the elliptic gluon distributions could be studied in diffractive reactions, such as:

•

Exclusive dijet production in
ep
collisions
Hatta et al. 2016
;
Boer and Setyadi 2021
. For calculations in the color dipole approach, see
Altinoluk et al. 2016
;
Mäntysaari et al. 2019
;
Salazar and Schenke 2019
;

•

Exclusive dijet photoproduction in
pA
and
AA
ultra-peripheral collisions (UPCs)
Hagiwara et al. 2017
;

•

Exclusive
Q\bar{Q}
(with
Q=c,b
) photoproduction in
pA
and
AA
UPCs
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
.

Here, we revisit the exclusive production of
c\bar{c}
pairs in proton-lead collisions at LHC energies. The dominant reaction mechanism here is the diffractive photoproduction of the
c\bar{c}
pair on a proton by a Weizsäcker-Williams photon emitted by the lead nucleus, see the diagrams in Fig.
1
.
One is then interested in the correlations of

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{\perp}={1\over 2}(\vec{p}_{\perp Q}-\vec{p}_{\perp\bar{Q}})\,,

(1)

and the momentum transfer to the proton, which for the exclusive reaction fulfills

\displaystyle\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}=\vec{p}_{\perp Q}+\vec{p}_{\perp\bar{Q}}\,.

(2)

In this work we account only for the contribution where the ion serves as a source of the photon.
Here we exploit the fact, that the photon flux scales with the charge of the ion as
Z^{2}
.
This factor will be missing when the photon is emitted by the proton. On the other hand, the integrated diffractive process on the nucleus will display a dependence
\sigma\propto A^{\alpha}
, with
roughly
\alpha\sim 1.4\div 1.5
(see e.g.
Ashery 2006
;
Nikolaev et al. 2001
). While the diffractive photoproduction on the nucleus may therefore still be a sizeable effect, the extraction of the process discussed in this paper is possible if proton tagging
Staszewski 2023
;
Trzebinski 2019
;
Bossini 2023
is available, due to the very sharp transverse momentum dependence of the electromagnetically scattered proton.

In distinction to dijet production, for the heavy quarks already the quark mass
m_{Q}
can play the role of a hard scale, and also intermediate and small values of
\vec{P}_{\perp}
are tractable. Here, we will calculate cross sections and distributions for realistic kinematic conditions at the LHC. We also discuss some subtleties regarding the correlations in the azimuthal angle

\displaystyle\cos\phi={\vec{P}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over P_{\perp}\Delta_{\perp}}\,.

(3)

At large jet momenta
P_{\perp}
the correlations in
\phi
expected to be dominantly
\propto\cos(2\phi)
and are unambiguously expressed in terms of the elliptic Wigner function/GTMD.
At lower
P_{\perp}
, however, the azimuthal correlations are better understood as coming from the matrix element (or impact factor) at finite
\Delta_{\perp}
. While formally also these correlations can be absorbed into the Wigner function/GTMD, these would correspond to
\delta
-function terms in the latter. We discuss the relation between the different approaches and the alternative formulation in terms of a off-forward gluon density matrix introduced in Ref.
Nemchik et al. 1998
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we explain how to calculate the diffractive cross section of interest and discuss the formalism starting from the dipole representation of the amplitude. Then, in Section
III
we present our numerical results for differential cross sections as functions of various kinematical variables using a variety of the existing gluon GTMD models. Finally, in Section
IV
the main conclusions are summarised.

II
Formalism

II.1
Kinematics and cross section

The cross section for the proton-nucleus reaction can be written in the following form

\displaystyle{d\sigma(pA\to Q\bar{Q}pA;s)\over dx_{Q}dx_{\bar{Q}}d^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}={1\over x_{Q}+x_{\bar{Q}}}f_{\gamma/A}(x_{Q}+x_{\bar{Q}})\,{d\sigma(\gamma p\to Q\bar{Q}p;(x_{Q}+x_{\bar{Q}})s)\over dzd^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}\,,

(4)

with
z=x_{Q}/(x_{Q}+x_{\bar{Q}})
. Here,
x_{Q},x_{\bar{Q}}
are the fractions of the nucleus’ Light-Front plus momentum carried by a heavy quark
Q
and antiquark
\bar{Q}
(with mass
m_{Q}
), respectively. The transverse momenta of quark and antiquark are

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{\perp Q}=\vec{P}_{\perp}+{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}\,,\qquad\vec{p}_{\perp\bar{Q}}=-\vec{P}_{\perp}+{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}\,,

(5)

respectively, so that
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
is the transverse momentum of the
Q\bar{Q}
pair. As the photon is collinear to the incoming nucleus,
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
is also equal, up to a sign, to the transverse momentum transfer to the proton target.

The Weizsäcker-Williams photons carry the momentum fraction

\displaystyle x_{A}=x_{Q}+x_{\bar{Q}}\,.

(6)

For the flux of quasireal photons,

\displaystyle f_{\gamma/A}(x_{A})={dN(x_{A})\over dx_{A}}\,,

(7)

we use the well-known expression (see for example the review
Baur et al. 2002
),

\displaystyle{dN(x_{A})\over dx_{A}}={2Z^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}\over\pi x_{A}}\left[\xi_{jA}K_{0}(\xi_{jA})K_{1}(\xi_{jA})-{\xi_{jA}^{2}\over 2}(K_{1}^{2}(\xi_{jA})-K_{0}^{2}(\xi_{jA}))\right],

(8)

where
Z
correspond to the atomic number of the projectile particle,
\alpha_{\rm em}
is the fine structure constant,
\xi_{jA}=x_{A}m_{p}(R_{j}+R_{A})
involves the target and nucleus radii (
R_{j}
and
R_{A}
, respectively) and effectively excludes the overlap of the projectile and the target in impact parameter space, and
m_{p}
is the proton mass.

Let us also quote a useful expression for the cross section which reads in terms of center-of-mass rapidities
y_{Q},y_{\bar{Q}}
of quarks,

\displaystyle{d\sigma(pA\to Q\bar{Q}pA;s)\over dy_{Q}dy_{\bar{Q}}d^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}=x_{A}{dN(x_{A})\over dx_{A}}\,\Big(z(1-z){d\sigma(\gamma p\to Q\bar{Q}p;x_{A}s)\over dzd^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}\Big)\Big|_{z={x_{Q}\over x_{A}}}\,,

(9)

where

\displaystyle x_{Q}={\sqrt{p_{\perp Q}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}\over s}}\,\exp(y_{Q}),\qquad x_{\bar{Q}}={\sqrt{p_{\perp\bar{Q}}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}\over s}}\,\exp(y_{\bar{Q}})\,.

(10)

We also need the transverse mass of the
Q\bar{Q}
–pair, which square is given by

\displaystyle M_{\perp}^{2}=x_{A}\Big({p^{2}_{\perp Q}+m_{Q}^{2}\over x_{Q}}+{p^{2}_{\perp\bar{Q}}+m_{Q}^{2}\over x_{\bar{Q}}}\Big)\,.

(11)

The rapidity of the
Q\bar{Q}
–pair in the cm-frame can then be calculated from

\displaystyle Y_{\rm pair}={1\over 2}\log\Big({x_{A}\over x_{B}}\Big)=\log\Big({x_{A}\sqrt{s}\over M_{\perp}}\Big)\,,\qquad x_{A}x_{B}s=M^{2}_{\perp}.

(12)

We will be also interested in the laboratory frame rapidities, which are obtained from the shift

\displaystyle y_{Q,\bar{Q}}^{\rm LAB}=y_{Q,\bar{Q}}+{1\over 2}\log\Big({Z\over A}\Big)\,,\quad Y_{\rm pair}^{\rm LAB}=Y_{\rm pair}+{1\over 2}\log\Big({Z\over A}\Big),

(13)

where for the
{}^{208}{\rm Pb}
nucleus, we have
A=208,Z=82
.

II.2
Color dipole representation of the diffractive amplitude

We start our discussion from a basic description of the color dipole approach to diffractive processes
Kopeliovich et al. 1981
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1992
. Here, the cross section for the
\gamma p\to Q\bar{Q}
diffractive dissociation process is written as

\displaystyle{d\sigma(\gamma p\to Q\bar{Q}p;s_{\gamma p})\over dzd^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}=\overline{\sum_{\lambda_{\gamma},\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}}\Big|\int{d^{2}\vec{b}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{r}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}}e^{-i\vec{P}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}}N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})\,\Psi^{\lambda_{\gamma}}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}(z,\vec{r}_{\perp})\,\Big|^{2}\,.

(14)

Above,
z,1-z
are the Light-Front momentum fractions carried by quark/antiquark in the
\gamma\to Q\bar{Q}
transition. The corresponding Light-Front wave function for the
\gamma\to Q\bar{Q}
,

\displaystyle\Psi^{\lambda_{\gamma}}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}(z,\vec{r}_{\perp})={1\over\sqrt{4\pi z(1-z)}}\int{d^{2}\vec{l}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}\,e^{i\vec{r}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{l}_{\perp}}\,\Psi^{\lambda_{\gamma}}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}(z,\vec{l}_{\perp})\,,

(15)

depends on the Light-Front helicities of quarks,
\lambda/2,\bar{\lambda}/2
and photon,
\lambda_{\gamma}
. Its explicit form in transverse momentum space can be found for example in Ref.
Kovchegov and Levin 2012
.

Our main interest is in the dipole scattering amplitude
N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})
. Its energy dependence is encoded through the “rapidity”
Y
. We define the latter as
Y=\ln(x_{0}/x_{\mathrm{I\!P}})
, with
x_{0}=0.01
. Here,

\displaystyle x_{\mathrm{I\!P}}\equiv x_{B}={M_{\perp}^{2}\over s_{\gamma p}}={M_{\perp}^{2}\over x_{A}s}\,,

(16)

where
M_{\perp}
is the transverse mass of the
Q\bar{Q}
system. The dipole amplitude is related to the familiar color dipole cross section (see e.g. the textbook
Barone and Predazzi 2002
) via

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{r}_{\perp})=2\int d^{2}\vec{b}_{\perp}\,N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})\,.

(17)

It is related to an off-diagonal generalization of the unintegrated gluon distribution – a gluon density matrix through the relation
Nemchik et al. 1998
:

\displaystyle N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)\exp[i\vec{q}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big\{\exp\Big[i{1\over 2}\vec{q}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}\Big]+\exp\Big[-i{1\over 2}\vec{q}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}\Big]-\exp[i\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}]-\exp[-i\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}]\Big\}\,.

Quark and antiquark move at impact parameters,

\displaystyle\vec{b}_{\perp Q}=\vec{b}_{\perp}+{\vec{r}_{\perp}\over 2}\,,\qquad\vec{b}_{\perp\bar{Q}}=\vec{b}_{\perp}-{\vec{r}_{\perp}\over 2}\,,

(19)

which allow us to relate the four phase factors in the curly bracket in Eq. (
)
to the four diagrams of Fig.
1
in an obvious fashion. We write the unintegrated gluon density matrix in the following form,

\displaystyle f\Big(Y,{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)={\alpha_{s}\over 4\pi N_{c}}\,{{\mathcal{F}}\Big(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)\over({\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})^{2}({\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})^{2}}\,,

(20)

where
N_{c}=3
for the number of colors in QCD, and we put in evidence the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
and gluon propagators here.

Integrating Eq. (
) over
\vec{b}_{\perp}
, we obtain for the dipole cross section the well-known representation
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1994b

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{r}_{\perp})={2\pi\over N_{c}}\,\int{d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\over\kappa_{\perp}^{4}}\alpha_{s}{\mathcal{F}}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})\,\Big\{2-e^{i\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}}-e^{-i\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}}\Big\}\,,

(21)

so that indeed
\mathcal{F}(x,\vec{\kappa}_{\perp 1},\vec{\kappa}_{\perp 2})
is the proper off-forward generalization of the standard unintegrated gluon distribution, which relates to its collinear counterpart via

\displaystyle xg(x,\mu^{2})=\int{d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\over\pi\kappa_{\perp}^{2}}\,\theta(\mu^{2}-\kappa_{\perp}^{2})\,{\mathcal{F}}(x,\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})\,.

(22)

Below, we will also use the non-perturbative parameter,

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}})={4\pi\over N_{c}}\int{d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\over\kappa_{\perp}^{4}}\alpha_{s}{\mathcal{F}}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})\,,

(23)

which has the interpretation of the dipole cross section for large dipoles.

Now, inserting the representation of the dipole amplitude given in Eq. (
) into Eq. (
14
), we obtain for the diffractive amplitude the following convolution structure:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(Y,\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)\,\Big\{\Psi_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\lambda_{\gamma}}\Big(z,\vec{P}_{\perp}+{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}\Big)+\Psi_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\lambda_{\gamma}}\Big(z,\vec{P}_{\perp}-{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}\Big)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\lambda_{\gamma}}(z,\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})-\Psi_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\lambda_{\gamma}}(z,\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})\Big\}\,.

Here, in the terminology of small-
x
(or BFKL) factorization, one would refer to the structure in brackets as the impact factor for the coupling of two off-shell gluons to the
\gamma\to Q\bar{Q}
amplitude. The two
t
-channel gluons carry the transverse momenta,

\displaystyle\vec{\kappa}_{\perp 1}={\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},\quad\vec{\kappa}_{\perp 2}={\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\,,

(25)

and the impact factor has the property that it vanishes when either of the gluon transverse momenta goes to zero, i.e. for
\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}=\pm\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2
.

Finally, we obtain for our diffractive photoproduction cross section:

\displaystyle{d\sigma(\gamma p\to Q\bar{Q}p;s_{\gamma p})\over dzd^{2}\vec{P}_{\perp}d^{2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}}=e_{f}^{2}\alpha_{\rm em}\,2N_{c}(2\pi)^{2}\Big\{\Big(z^{2}+(1-z)^{2}\Big)\Big|{\vec{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}\Big|^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}\Big|{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}\Big|^{2}\Big\}\,.

(26)

Here,
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
\vec{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
are amplitudes for the sum of quark helicities equal to one or zero, respectively. Explicitly, we find (see e.g. Ref.
Nikolaev et al. 1999
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{\vec{M}}_{0}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\Big\{{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2)^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2)^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\Big\{{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2)^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}

(27)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}/2)^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\Big\}\,.

In order to understand the origin of azimuthal correlations, it is useful to decompose our amplitude. To this end, let us introduce

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{J}}_{0}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{q}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{q}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{q}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}+{\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{q}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{q}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{2\vec{P}_{\perp}\over\vec{P}_{\perp}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{1}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{q}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{q}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}+{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}+\vec{q}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{2\over\vec{P}_{\perp}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\,.

(28)

Then, our matrix elements take the form:

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{J}_{0}}(\vec{P}_{\perp},{1\over 2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\,C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})-\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\vec{\mathcal{J}_{0}}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{k}_{\perp})\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}_{1}}(\vec{P}_{\perp},{1\over 2}\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\,C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})-\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}{\mathcal{J}_{1}}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{k}_{\perp})\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\,.

There emerges a rapidity-dependent form factor,

\displaystyle C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})=\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\,,

(30)

which is a non-perturbative parameter, as is obvious from its form in the forward limit as an integral,

\displaystyle C(Y,0)={1\over 4\pi N_{c}}\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi k_{\perp}^{4}}\,\alpha_{s}{\cal F}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{k}_{\perp},-\vec{k}_{\perp})\,,

(31)

that converges at soft, non-perturbative values of
k_{\perp}
. Indeed, as can be seen from Eq. (
23
), it is directly proportional to the dipole cross section for large dipoles,
\sigma_{0}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}})
.

II.3
GTMD representation

In the literature, often a different momentum-space representation of the diffractive amplitude is used (see, for example, Refs.
Hagiwara et al. 2016
;
Hagiwara et al. 2017
;
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
). Namely, one introduces the Fourier transform of the dipole amplitude (using the normalization and notation of Ref.
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
),

\displaystyle T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})=\int{d^{2}\vec{b}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}{d^{2}\vec{r}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}\,e^{-i\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}}\,e^{-i\vec{k}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}}\,N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})\,.

(32)

Here,
T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})
is often referred to as the generalized transverse momentum distribution (GTMD) of gluons in the proton target. Certainly, just like the gluon density matrix
f\Big(Y,{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{q}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)
, it encodes the same information as the dipole amplitude. What is the relation between these two momentum space distributions?

To answer this question, let us perform the Fourier transform by inserting Eq. (
) into Eq. (
32
), which yields

\displaystyle T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\Big(\delta^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp}-{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2})+\delta^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp}+{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2})\Big)

(33)

\displaystyle-f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)-f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\,,

where
C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})
of Eq. (
30
) multiplies a combination of
\delta
-functions. Now, evidently
T
is essentially equal to the
f
, up to the term containing
\delta
-functions. The latter, however, will not contribute when convoluted with the impact factor in Eq. (
). Formally, we might therefore replace

\displaystyle f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\to-{1\over 2}\,T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\,.

(34)

In practical numerical applications, however, this equivalence is not that obvious. In diffractive interactions the values of
\Delta_{\perp}
are bounded by the diffractive slope
B_{D}
, so that at large values of
k_{\perp}
the delta-functions are irrelevant, and the two gluon distributions,
f
and
T
are equal to each other, up to a factor of
1/2
.

An immediate corollary of the representation in Eq. (
33
) is the sum rule

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\,T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})=0\,,

(35)

which encodes the fact, that
N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})\to 0
for
r_{\perp}\to 0
.
Generally speaking, as evidenced by the presence of
\delta
-function terms, the Fourier transform is a non-convergent integral and does not exist as a function. One therefore needs to regularize the Fourier transform which is often done by inserting a Gaussian cutoff function
Hagiwara et al. 2016
;
Hagiwara et al. 2017
;
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})=\int{d^{2}\vec{b}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}{d^{2}\vec{r}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-i\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}}e^{-i\vec{k}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{r}_{\perp}}\,N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})\,e^{-\varepsilon r_{\perp}^{2}}\,.

(36)

Now, inserting the representation in Eq. (
), we obtain the cutoff-dependent
T
as

\displaystyle T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(Y,\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\Big(\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp}-{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2})+\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp}+{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2})\Big)

(37)

\displaystyle-f_{\varepsilon}\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)-f_{\varepsilon}\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)\,,

where

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp})={1\over 4\pi\varepsilon}\,\exp\Big(-{k_{\perp}^{2}\over 4\varepsilon}\Big)\,,

(38)

is a “smeared out” delta-distribution, and

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\,f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{\kappa}_{\perp}\Big)\,\delta_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}(\vec{k}_{\perp}-\vec{\kappa}_{\perp})

(39)

is a smeared out version of the gluon density matrix. The regularized
T
-matrix also fulfills the sum rule of Eq. (
35
).

Let us finally quote the expressions of matrix elements
\vec{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
in terms of
T
, which read:

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{M}}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\,T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\,\,\Big\{{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{\vec{P}_{\perp}\over\vec{P}_{\perp}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}\,T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})\,\,\Big\{{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}-{1\over\vec{P}_{\perp}^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\Big\}\,.

(40)

In its derivation we made use of the sum rule of Eq. (
35
). Notice, that in effect here we use the impact factor for forward scattering, and all dependence on
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
has been absorbed into the GTMD
T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})
.

Benchmark gluon GTMDs

Let us briefly discuss the different GTMD models used in this work. We follow both approaches presented above, and consider a total of five different models as benchmarks in our analysis of the differential cross section of charm photoproduction in
pA
UPCs.

First, we use two different parametrizations of the off-forward gluon density matrix
f
of Eq. (
20
). Here, we choose to write

\displaystyle f\Big(Y,{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}+\vec{k}_{\perp},{\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}\over 2}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\Big)={\alpha_{s}\over 4\pi N_{c}}\,{{\cal F}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{k}_{\perp},-\vec{k}_{\perp})\over k_{\perp}^{4}}\,\exp\Big[-{1\over 2}B\vec{\Delta}^{2}\Big]\,.

(41)

Such a form has been suggested in Ref.
Ivanov et al. 2006
for the case of vector meson production (for a recent use, see Ref.
Cisek et al. 2023
). The same approach has also been taken in Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2008
. For the diffractive slope, we use
B=4\,\rm{GeV}^{-2}
, and the diagonal unintegrated gluon distribution
\cal F
is taken from two different models: the Golec-Biernat–Wüsthoff (GBW) model
Golec-Biernat and Wüsthoff 1998
, and the Moriggi-Peccini-Machado (MPM) parametrization
Moriggi et al. 2020
.

We also consider models based on the regularized Fourier transform of dipole amplitudes as in Eq. (
36
). The first model is based on a numerical solution of the Balitsky-Kovchegov equation for the dipole
S
-matrix with impact parameter dependence
Balitsky 1996
;
Kovchegov 1999
.
Such solutions have been obtained, for example in Refs.
Golec-Biernat and Stasto 2003
;
Berger and Stasto 2011a
;
Berger and Stasto 2011b
. In our work we use the results of Ref.
Hagiwara et al. 2016
.
This solution is based on exploiting a symmetry first observed in
Gubser 2011
. We label it as HHU below.

The second model, based on the original effective McLerran-Venugopalan model of Ref.
McLerran and Venugopalan 1994
, that has been generalised for the proton target and extended to incorporate inhomogeneities in the transverse-plane distribution of gluons in Ref.
Iancu and Rezaeian 2017
. The latter model has been applied for exclusive diffractive light and heavy quarks’ photoproduction in Refs.
Hagiwara et al. 2017
and
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
, respectively. We denote this model as MV-IR in what follows. A third model is the so-called bSat model of Kowalski and Teaney
Kowalski and Teaney 2003
. The Kowalski-Teaney (KT) model has been adjusted to the proton structure function data and hence it gives a rather realistic dipole amplitude. We use parameters from Table I fit 3 of
Kowalski and Teaney 2003
for the gluon distribution. In this regard, the HHU and MV-IR models can be considered as rather toy models, they do, however, incorporate non-trivial dipole orientation effects, which is not the case for the KT model.

The leading dependence on dipole orientation is quantified by the elliptic part
N_{\epsilon}
of the dipole amplitude in the Fourier expansion,

\displaystyle N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})=N_{0}(Y,r_{\perp},b_{\perp})+2\,\cos(2\phi_{br})\,N_{\epsilon}(Y,r_{\perp},b_{\perp})+\dots\,.

(42)

We translate the isotropic and elliptic parts of the dipole amplitude to GTMDs by the appropriate Fourier-Bessel transforms
Hatta et al. 2016
;
Hagiwara et al. 2017
;
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle T_{0}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}b_{\perp}db_{\perp}J_{0}(\Delta_{\perp}b_{\perp})\int_{0}^{\infty}r_{\perp}dr_{\perp}J_{0}(k_{\perp}r_{\perp})\,N_{0}(Y,r_{\perp},b_{\perp})e^{-\varepsilon r_{\perp}^{2}},

\displaystyle T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}b_{\perp}db_{\perp}J_{2}(\Delta_{\perp}b_{\perp})\int_{0}^{\infty}r_{\perp}dr_{\perp}J_{2}(k_{\perp}r_{\perp})\,N_{\epsilon}(Y,r_{\perp},b_{\perp})e^{-\varepsilon r_{\perp}^{2}}\,.

(43)

The explicit form of the matrix element for the elliptic glue for arbitrary quark mass
m_{Q}
has been found in Ref.
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
. We briefly review its derivation in Appendix
A
.

It is interesting to note that the gluon density matrices, constructed according to the prescription of Eq. (
41
), do also lead to a dipole amplitude that depends on dipole orientation, as can be seen by plugging the expression of Eq. (
41
) into Eq. (
). It gives us the expression

\displaystyle N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})={1\over 4}\Big\{t_{N}\Big(\vec{b}_{\perp}+{\vec{r}_{\perp}\over 2}\Big)+t_{N}\Big(\vec{b}_{\perp}-{\vec{r}_{\perp}\over 2}\Big)-2t_{N}(\vec{b}_{\perp})\Big\}\sigma_{0}(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}})+{1\over 2}t_{N}(\vec{b}_{\perp})\sigma(x_{\mathrm{I\!P}},\vec{r}_{\perp})\,,

(44)

where

\displaystyle t_{N}(\vec{b}_{\perp})=\int{d^{2}\vec{q}_{\perp}\over(2\pi)^{2}}\,\exp(-i\vec{q}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp})\exp\Big[-{1\over 2}Bq_{\perp}^{2}\Big]\,.

(45)

Again, we see that the dipole orientation dependence appears together with the non-perturbative parameter
\sigma_{0}
– the dipole cross section for large dipoles.

We want to stress, that the correlation between
\vec{r}_{\perp}
and
\vec{b}_{\perp}
emerges, although Eq. (
41
) does not contain any correlation between
\vec{k}_{\perp}
and
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
!
The
\vec{r}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{b}_{\perp}
correlation is in fact of a simple geometric origin, as it singles out the contributions of diagrams where only the quark or antiquark interact and probe the matter density at their respective impact parameters. There is no obvious way how to construct an off-forward glue that leads to totally isotropic dipole amplitude. For this to happen, the relevant dipole sizes simply have to be small enough for the matter density to be constant over distances
\sim r_{\perp}
. Then the shifts in the curly brackets do not matter and only the last term in Eq. (
44
) effectively contributes. Of course, in general one would expect Eq. (
20
) to have nontrivial azimuthal correlations between
\vec{k}_{\perp}
and
\vec{\Delta}_{\perp}
, in which case the dipole amplitude will have a genuinely dynamical elliptic piece that can contribute also at hard momenta
\vec{P}_{\perp}
.

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented several differential distributions for the diffractive photoproduction of
c\bar{c}
pairs in the
pA\to p(c\bar{c})A
reaction at LHC energies. Our results have been obtained using different models for gluon GTMDs from the literature. Some GTMDs were obtained via the Fourier transform of the dipole
S
-matrix,
N(Y,\vec{r}_{\perp},\vec{b}_{\perp})
. In this case, the GTMDs are regularized by an extra factor as done already in the literature for exclusive dijet photoproduction. This regularization leads to rather large uncertainties as far as normalization of the cross section is concerned. Therefore, for the distributions derived from dipole amplitudes, one has to focus rather on their shapes than on magnitudes.

In the present work, we go beyond the earlier analysis of Ref.
Reinke Pelicer et al. 2019
considering realistic conditions of proton-lead collisions at the LHC and integrating over the phase space variables (such as quark rapidities and transverse momenta) in the measurable domains. We have paid special attention to azimuthal correlations which were proposed in the literature to test the models of small-
x
dynamics encoded in the so-called elliptic gluon distributions. We find rather small azimuthal angle modulation in
\phi(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})
. The modulations as well as the structure of maxima/minima depends on the GTMD models used in our analysis, so in principle the models can be tested in actual measurements at the LHC. For completeness, we have also presented the predictions for differential distributions in
P_{\perp}
,
\Delta_{\perp}
(transverse momentum of the
c\bar{c}
pair) and rapidity of the
c\bar{c}
pair.

Regarding the possible measurement we need to repeat an important caveat.
First, for the case of open heavy flavour meson production, a thorough analysis of hadronization corrections would be required to investigate if the
sensitivity of our results to the GTMD remains visible at the hadron level.
For the case of dijet production, one expects soft gluon emissions (Sudakov resummation) to have an impact, see e.g. Ref.
Hatta et al. 2021
. We intend to come back to some of these issues in future work.

Here, we collect some steps necessary to analytically perform the azimuthal integration in the convolution of the hard amplitude with the azimuthally asymmetric part of the gluon GTMD.
The gluon GTMD is expanded as

\displaystyle T(Y,\vec{k}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{0}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})+2\,\cos 2(\phi_{k}-\phi_{\Delta})\,T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{0}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})+2\,{2(\vec{k}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})^{2}-k_{\perp}^{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\over k_{\perp}^{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}}\,T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})\,.

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{\vec{M}}_{0}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle 2\,\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\,{2(\vec{k}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})^{2}-k_{\perp}^{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\over k_{\perp}^{2}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}}\,T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})

\displaystyle 2\,\Big(2{\Delta_{\perp}^{i}\Delta_{\perp}^{j}\over\Delta^{2}_{\perp}}-\delta_{ij}\Big)\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\,{k_{\perp}^{i}k_{\perp}^{j}\over k_{\perp}^{2}}\,T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})\,,

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{\vec{M}}_{1}(\vec{P}_{\perp},\vec{\Delta}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\Big(2{\Delta_{\perp}^{i}\Delta_{\perp}^{j}\over\Delta^{2}_{\perp}}-\delta_{ij}\Big)\int{d^{2}\vec{k}_{\perp}\over 2\pi}{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\,{k_{\perp}^{i}k_{\perp}^{j}\over k_{\perp}^{2}}\,T_{\epsilon}(Y,k_{\perp},\Delta_{\perp})\,.

We now concentrate on the integral over azimuthal angles

\displaystyle\vec{I}_{ij}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi_{k}\over 2\pi}{\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}{k_{\perp}^{i}k_{\perp}^{j}\over k^{2}_{\perp}}\,,I^{1}_{ij}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi_{k}\over 2\pi}{1\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}{k_{\perp}^{i}k_{\perp}^{j}\over k^{2}_{\perp}}\,.

This vector integral will be proportional to
\vec{P}_{\perp}
. We therefore write

\displaystyle\vec{I}_{ij}={\vec{P}_{\perp}\over P_{\perp}}\,I^{0}_{ij}(\vec{P}_{\perp},k_{\perp})\,,

(51)

with

\displaystyle I^{0}_{ij}(\vec{P}_{\perp},k_{\perp})={1\over P_{\perp}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi_{k}\over 2\pi}{P^{2}_{\perp}-\vec{P}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{k}_{\perp}\over(\vec{P}_{\perp}-\vec{k}_{\perp})^{2}+m_{Q}^{2}}\,{k_{\perp}^{i}k_{\perp}^{j}\over k^{2}_{\perp}}\,.

(52)

Now we decompose
I^{0,1}_{ij}
in terms of invariant functions of
|\vec{P}_{\perp}|
and
|\vec{k}_{\perp}|
and two orthogonal tensor structures, for which we choose

\displaystyle I^{0,1}_{ij}(\vec{P}_{\perp})=\Big(2{P^{i}_{\perp}P^{j}_{\perp}\over P^{2}_{\perp}}-\delta_{ij}\Big){1\over 2}I^{0,1}_{\epsilon}(P_{\perp},k_{\perp})+\delta_{ij}\,{1\over 2}I^{0,1}_{0}(P_{\perp},k_{\perp})\,.

49

\delta_{ij}

and

\displaystyle P_{\perp}I^{0}_{\epsilon}(P_{\perp},k_{\perp})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi\over 2\pi}{P^{2}_{\perp}-{1\over 2}b\cos\phi\over a-b\cos\phi}\,\cos 2\phi\,

(57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi\over 2\pi}{P^{2}_{\perp}-{1\over 2}a+{1\over 2}(a-b\cos\phi)\over a-b\cos\phi}\,\cos 2\phi\,

\displaystyle(P^{2}_{\perp}-{1\over 2}a)\int_{0}^{2\pi}{d\phi\over 2\pi}{\cos 2\phi\over a-b\cos\phi}

\displaystyle a^{2}-b^{2}=(P^{2}_{\perp}+k^{2}_{\perp}+m^{2})^{2}-4P^{2}_{\perp}k^{2}_{\perp}=(P^{2}_{\perp}-k^{2}_{\perp}-m^{2})^{2}+4P^{2}_{\perp}m^{2}\,.

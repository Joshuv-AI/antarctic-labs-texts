Evaluations of the areal Mahler measure of multivariable polynomials
Matilde N. Lalin, Subham Roy

Abstract
. We exhibit some nontrivial evaluations of the areal Mahler measure of multivariable polynomials, defined by Pritsker [ Pri08 ] by considering the integral over the product of unit disks instead of the unit torus as in the standard case. As in the case of the classical Mahler measure, we find examples yielding special values of L L -functions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11R06; Secondary 11M06, 11R42, 30C10

1.
Introduction

The (logarithmic) Mahler measure of a non-zero rational function
P\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\times}
is a height given by

\mathrm{m}(P):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}},

where the integration is taken over the unit torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|x_{1}|=\cdots=|x_{n}|=1\}
with respect to the Haar measure.

In the case where
P
is a single variable polynomial,
\mathrm{m}(P)
can be expressed in terms of the roots of
P
by means of Jensen’s formula. Namely, if

P(x)=a\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{j})\in\mathbb{C}[x]
, then

(1)

\mathrm{m}(P)=\log|a|+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log^{+}|\alpha_{j}|,

where
\log^{+}|z|=\log\max\{|z|,1\}
.
In the multivariable case,
\mathrm{m}(P)
is more mysterious, and many formulas are known where
\mathrm{m}(P)
is related to special values of the Riemann zeta function,
L
-functions, and other functions of arithmetic significance (for particular
P
’s). For example, Smyth
[
Smy81
,
Boy81
]
proved the formulas

(2)

\mathrm{m}(1+x+y)=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}L(\chi_{-3},2),

(3)

\mathrm{m}(1+x+y+z)=\frac{7}{2\pi^{2}}\zeta(3),

where
L(\chi_{-3},s)
is the Dirichlet
L
-function in the character of conductor 3 and
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function. The connection to these special values has been explained in terms of relations to regulators by Deninger
[
Den97
]
, as well as Boyd
[
Boy98
]
and Rodriguez-Villegas
[
RV99
]
(see also the book of Brunault and Zudilin
[
BZ20
]
for more details). More specifically, the special values
L(\chi_{-3},2)
and
\zeta(3)
in equations (
2
) and (
3
) arise from evaluations of the dilogarithm in the sixth roots of unity and the trilogarithm in
\pm 1
, respectively.

In this note we consider the (logarithmic) areal Mahler measure defined by Pritsker
[
Pri08
]
for
P\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\times}
as

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(P)=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\log|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n}),

where

\mathbb{D}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|\leq 1\}

is the product of
n
unit disks, and the measure is the natural measure in the
A^{0}
Bergman space.

In
[
Pri08
, Theorem 1.1]
, Pritsker proves that for
P(x)=a\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{j})\in\mathbb{C}[x]
, then

(4)

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(P)=\mathrm{m}(P)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha_{j}|<1}\left(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-1\right)=\log|a|+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log^{+}|\alpha_{j}|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha_{j}|<1}\left(|\alpha_{j}|^{2}-1\right),

thus giving a counterpart to Jensen’s formula (
1
). Pritsker proves various inequalities comparing
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(P)
to
\mathrm{m}(P)
and the coefficients of
P
, discusses Kronecker’s Lemma (that characterizes the one-variable polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(P)=0
), considers approximations, exhibits a counterexample to Lehmer’s conjecture in this context, and proves some simple evaluations of multivariable cases. Additional results about the areal Mahler measure can be found in the works of Choi and Samuels
[
CS12
]
and Flammang
[
Fla15
]
.

In this note, we evaluate the areal Mahler measure of some nontrivial multivariable polynomials and rational functions. As mentioned above, some simple evaluations are included in
[
Pri08
]
. More precisely, Pritsker shows that
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}+x_{2})=-\frac{1}{4}
and
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(1+x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}})=0
for
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 0
. We provide many more formulas, most of which involve special values of
L
-functions and other special functions. Our results provide evidence that the areal Mahler measure is also an interesting object deserving of attention. For example, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1
.

We have

(5)

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(1+x+y)=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}L(\chi_{-3},2)+\frac{1}{6}-\frac{11\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}.

Comparing this formula with (
2
), we see the same term involving the
L
-function/dilogarithm, and some extra terms.

It is natural to wonder if one can obtain an elegant areal Mahler measure formula for polynomials of the type
a+bx+cy
, where
a,b,c,
are fixed coefficients, since such formula does exist in the classical case, as proven by Cassaigne and Maillot
[
Mai00
]
.
It seems to be quite difficult to obtain such a formula for the areal Mahler measure in full generality. To illustrate this, we have the following nontrivial statement.

Theorem 2
.

We have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(\sqrt{2}+x+y)=\frac{L(\chi_{-4},2)}{\pi}+\mathcal{C}_{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{3}{8}-\frac{3}{2\pi},

where

\mathcal{C}_{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi^{3}}}{\vphantom{F}}_{4}\kern-0.5ptF_{3}\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{4};\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{4},\frac{5}{4};1\right)-\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}}{72\sqrt{2\pi^{3}}}{\vphantom{F}}_{4}\kern-0.5ptF_{3}\left(\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4},\frac{5}{4};\frac{3}{2},\frac{7}{4},\frac{7}{4};1\right),

is expressed in terms of generalized hypergeometric functions
defined as

{\vphantom{F}}{}_{p}\kern-0.5ptF_{q}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p};b_{1},\ldots,b_{q};z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1})_{n}\cdots(a_{p})_{n}}{(b_{1})_{n}\cdots(b_{q})_{n}}\,\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

where
(a)_{n}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol given by

(a)_{0}=1
, and for
n\geq 1
,

(a)_{n}=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+n-1).

Remark 3
.

While other formulas in this article are given in terms of polylogarithms, it is unclear
that the term
\mathcal{C}_{\sqrt{2}}
can be expressed in this form.

We notice that

\mathrm{m}(\sqrt{2}+x+y)=\frac{L(\chi_{-4},2)}{\pi}+\frac{\log 2}{4}.

This formula can be obtained by specializing the more general expression for
\mathrm{m}(a+bx+cy)
from
[
Mai00
]
.

A motivation to study this particular polynomial (with
a=\sqrt{2}
and
b=c=1
) lies in the fact that it is relatively easy to understand the boundaries of integration upon application of Jensen’s formula, due to the particular properties of the constant
\sqrt{2}
. We see nevertheless that the formula for
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(\sqrt{2}+x+y)
is quite involved.

We also prove the following statement involving a rational function.

Theorem 4
.

We have

(6)

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}\left(y+\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)\right)=\frac{6}{\pi}L\left(\chi_{-4},2\right)-\log 2-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}.

The above formula can be compared to the evaluation due to Boyd
[
Boy92
]

(7)

\mathrm{m}\left(y+\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}L(\chi_{-4},2).

In this case, the term
L(\chi_{-4},2)
involving the Dirichlet
L
-function in the character of conductor 4 comes from evaluating the dilogarithm at
\pm i
. However, unlike the situation of equations (
2
) and (
5
), the dilogarithmic terms in (
6
) and (
7
) do not have the same coefficients.

Variations of Mahler measure such as generalized Mahler measure
[
GO04
]
, multiple and higher Mahler measure
[
KLO08
]
, and zeta Mahler measures
[
BG69
,
Aka09
]
can be adapted to the areal Mahler measure setting. For example, we compute the areal Mahler measure of
x+1
and prove that

(8)

Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|x+1|^{s}dA(x)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(1-2^{1-j})(\zeta(j)-1)s^{j}\right),

which can be compared to the classical case
[
Aka09
,
KLO08
]

Z(s,x+1):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|x+1|^{s}\frac{dx}{x}=\exp\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(1-2^{1-j})\zeta(j)s^{j}\right).

We see that both formulas are very similar, and indeed,

Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1)=\frac{s+1}{(s/2+1)^{2}}Z(s,x+1).

The techniques for proving formulas for the areal Mahler measure and its variants are not unlike the techniques employed in the classical Mahler measure case, and include inventive changes of variables in integrals, as well as connections to polylogarithms and other special functions such as generalized hypergeometric series, and their properties. For the moment we lack the connection to regulators that could potentially allow us to perform these evaluations more systematically. Nevertheless, our results open the door to future considerations of the areal Mahler measure and suggest the search for deeper connections to regulators that could potentially explain such formulas.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present the areal Mahler measure of
x+y
and more generally
x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n}
as a prelude to more involved arguments that follow in subsequent sections. Section
3
contains the definition and basic properties of polylogarithms, which are central to most of our computations. We prove the main areal Mahler measure formulas given by
Theorems
1
,
2
, and
4
in Section
4
. Finally, in Section

5
we extend the definition of generalized, multiple, and higher Mahler measure and the zeta Mahler measure to the areal context, and give examples of evaluations in each of these cases, including formula (
8
).

2.
A basic result

The simplest possibly non-trivial polynomial that we can consider in this context is a linear polynomial in one variable. Equation (
4
) gives us

(9)

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x-\alpha)=\begin{cases}\log^{+}|\alpha|&|\alpha|\geq 1,\\
\frac{|\alpha|^{2}-1}{2}&|\alpha|\leq 1.\end{cases}

Given the above formula, it is natural to pose the question about the areal Mahler measure of
x+y
. The following result is due to Pritsker, but we reprove it here for completeness.

Proposition 5
.

[
Pri08
, Example 5.2]
We have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x+y)=-\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

We first consider the integral over the variable
y
by exploiting formula (
9
). This gives

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x+y)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{2}}\log|x+y|dA(y)dA(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}(|x|^{2}-1)dA(x).

Parametrizing
x=\rho e^{i\theta}
with
0\leq\rho\leq 1
and
-\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi
, the above integral becomes

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x+y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}(\rho^{2}-1)\rho d\rho d\theta=\int_{0}^{1}(\rho^{2}-1)\rho d\rho=\left.\left(\frac{\rho^{4}}{4}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{1}_{0}=-\frac{1}{4}.

∎

We remark that Proposition
5
exhibits a point of difference between the classical case
\mathrm{m}
and the areal case
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}
. Indeed, it is known that the classical Mahler measure of an homogeneous polynomial is the same as the Mahler measure of any dehomogenization, and in particular,
\mathrm{m}(x+y)=\mathrm{m}(x+1)=0
. However, equation (
9
) shows that
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x+1)=0
, while Proposition
5
gives
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x+y)=-\frac{1}{4}
.

This discrepancy is even more general. Indeed, while
\mathrm{m}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})=0
, the areal Mahler measure
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})
is a rational number depending on
m
and
n
as shown in the following result.

Theorem 6
.

We have for
m\geq 1
,

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y)=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{2}.

For
m,n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\binom{m+n-2}{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-3-r}{m-2}2^{r}-\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{m+n-3-r}{n-2}2^{r}

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2^{m+n+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-1-r}{m}2^{r}-\frac{n}{2^{m+n+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{m+n-1-r}{n}2^{r}.

Before proving Theorem
6
, we need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 7
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-r}{b}2^{r}+\sum_{r=0}^{b}\binom{a+b-r}{a}2^{r}=2^{a+b+1}.

Proof.

A version of Chu–Vandermonde identity states that, for
m,n,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
m+n\leq r
, we have

(10)

\sum_{k=n}^{r-m}\binom{r-k}{m}\binom{k}{n}=\binom{r+1}{m+n+1}.

(See for example Equation (25) in
[
Knu97
, 1.2.6]
.)

Since
2^{r}=\sum_{p=0}^{r}\binom{r}{p},
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-r}{b}2^{r}=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-r}{b}\sum_{p=0}^{r}\binom{r}{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{a}\sum_{r=p}^{a}\binom{r}{p}\binom{a+b-r}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{a}\binom{a+b+1}{b+p+1},

where the last equality follows from (
10
).
Similarly,

\sum_{r=0}^{b}\binom{a+b-r}{a}2^{r}=\sum_{q=0}^{b}\binom{a+b+1}{a+q+1}=\sum_{q=0}^{b}\binom{a+b+1}{b-q}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-r}{b}2^{r}+\sum_{r=0}^{b}\binom{a+b-r}{a}2^{r}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{a}\binom{a+b+1}{b+p+1}+\sum_{q=0}^{b}\binom{a+b+1}{b-q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{a+b+1}\binom{a+b+1}{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{a+b+1},

where the penultimate inequality follows from rearranging the terms.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
. By symmetry, this only excludes the case
m=n=1
, which was already treated in Proposition
5
.
By equation (
9
) applied to
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1})=-\frac{m-1}{2}
.
By multiplicity, we get

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})=\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}\left(x_{m}+\frac{y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}}\right)-\frac{m-1}{2}.

Applying the definition of the areal Mahler measure and integrating respect to
x_{m}
by means of (
9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})+\frac{m-1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{m+n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{m+n}}\log\left|x_{m}+\frac{y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}}\right|dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{m})dA(y_{1})\dots dA(y_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{m+n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{m+n-1}\cap\{|y_{1}\cdots y_{n}|\leq|x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}|\}}\left(\left|\frac{y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}}\right|^{2}-1\right)dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{m-1})dA(y_{1})\dots dA(y_{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\pi^{m+n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{m+n-1}\cap\{|y_{1}\cdots y_{n}|\geq|x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}|\}}\log\left|\frac{y_{1}\cdots y_{n}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}}\right|dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{m-1})dA(y_{1})\dots dA(y_{n}).

We now consider the change of variables to polar coordinates
x_{j}=\rho_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
and
y_{k}=\sigma_{k}e^{i\tau_{k}}
, for
j=1,\dots,m-1
and
k=1,\dots,n
, where
0\leq\theta_{j},\tau_{k}\leq 2\pi
, and
0\leq\rho_{j},\sigma_{k}\leq 1
. Since the functions under consideration are independent of
\theta_{j},\tau_{k}
, we can directly integrate respect to those variables. We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})+\frac{m-1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{m+n-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}\leq\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}}\left(\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}}\right)^{2}-1\right)\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}d\rho_{1}\dots d\rho_{m-1}d\sigma_{1}\dots d\sigma_{n}

\displaystyle+2^{m+n-1}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}\geq\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}}\log\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}}\right)\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m-1}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}d\rho_{1}\dots d\rho_{m-1}d\sigma_{1}\dots d\sigma_{n}.

We further consider the change of variables
\alpha_{j}=\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{j}
, and
\beta_{k}=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{k}
for
j=1,\dots,m-1
and
k=1,\dots,n
. This transformation leads to

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})+\frac{m-1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{m+n-2}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\alpha_{m-1}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{1}\leq 1\\
0\leq\beta_{n}\leq\cdots\leq\beta_{1}\leq 1\\
\beta_{n}\leq\alpha_{m-1}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\beta_{n}^{3}}{\alpha_{m-1}}-\alpha_{m-1}\beta_{n}\right)\frac{d\alpha_{1}\dots d\alpha_{m-1}}{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-2}}\frac{d\beta_{1}\dots d\beta_{n}}{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n-1}}

\displaystyle+2^{m+n-1}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\alpha_{m-1}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{1}\leq 1\\
0\leq\beta_{n}\leq\cdots\leq\beta_{1}\leq 1\\
\beta_{n}\geq\alpha_{m-1}\end{subarray}}(\log\beta_{n}-\log\alpha_{m-1})\alpha_{m-1}\beta_{n}\frac{d\alpha_{1}\dots d\alpha_{m-1}}{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{m-2}}\frac{d\beta_{1}\dots d\beta_{n}}{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{n-1}}.

Integrating respect to
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m-2}
as well as
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n-1}
leads to

\displaystyle 2^{m+n-2}\int_{0\leq\beta_{n}\leq\alpha_{m-1}\leq 1}\left(\frac{\beta_{n}^{3}}{\alpha_{m-1}}-\alpha_{m-1}\beta_{n}\right)\frac{(-1)^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\log^{m-2}\alpha_{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\log^{n-1}\beta_{n}d\alpha_{m-1}d\beta_{n}

\displaystyle+2^{m+n-1}\int_{0\leq\alpha_{m-1}\leq\beta_{n}\leq 1}(\log\beta_{n}-\log\alpha_{m-1})\alpha_{m-1}\beta_{n}\frac{(-1)^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}\log^{m-2}\alpha_{m-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\log^{n-1}\beta_{n}d\alpha_{m-1}d\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m+n-1}2^{m+n-2}}{(m-2)!(n-1)!}\int_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1}\left(\frac{\beta^{3}}{\alpha}-\alpha\beta\right)\log^{m-2}\alpha\log^{n-1}\beta d\alpha d\beta

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{m+n-1}2^{m+n-1}}{(m-2)!(n-1)!}\int_{0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1}(\log\beta-\log\alpha)\alpha\beta\log^{m-2}\alpha\log^{n-1}\beta d\alpha d\beta.

The above integral can be decomposed into a sum of similar terms, which can be evaluated from the following general formula (see formula 2.722 in
[
GR07
]
):

(11)

\int x^{j}\log^{k}xdx=x^{j+1}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{r}r!}{(j+1)^{r+1}}\binom{k}{r}\log^{k-r}x+C.

Formula (
11
) allows us to compute

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1}\alpha\beta\log^{a}\alpha\log^{b}\beta=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{a+b}a!b!}{2^{2a+2b+3}}\sum_{r=0}^{b}\binom{a+b-r}{a}2^{r}.

We also have

\displaystyle\int_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1}\frac{\beta^{3}}{\alpha}\log^{m-2}\alpha\log^{n-1}\beta d\alpha d\beta=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{3}\log^{m+n-2}\beta d\beta

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m+n-1}(m+n-2)!}{(m-1)4^{m+n-1}}.

Combining (
12
), (
2
), as well as Lemma
7
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{m-1}{2}+\binom{m+n-2}{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}-\frac{1}{2^{m+n-1}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-3-r}{m-2}2^{r}

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}\binom{m+n-2-r}{n}2^{r}+\frac{m-1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{m+n-2-r}{n-1}2^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{m+n-2}{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}-\frac{1}{2^{m+n-1}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-3-r}{m-2}2^{r}

(14)

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-2}\binom{m+n-2-r}{n}2^{r}-\frac{m-1}{2^{m+n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-2-r}{m-1}2^{r}.

Specializing the above
for
n=1
and
m>1
, we obtain

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y)=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}}-\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover, by comparing with Proposition
5
, this formula is also true for
m=1
.

When
n>1
, expression (
2
) can be made symmetric by exchanging
m
and
n
and taking the average. Applying Lemma
7
again (
12
), this gives the final expression in the case where both
m,n>1
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{m}+y_{1}\cdots y_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\binom{m+n-2}{m-1}\frac{1}{2^{m+n}}

\displaystyle-\frac{m}{2^{m+n+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\binom{m+n-1-r}{m}2^{r}-\frac{n}{2^{m+n+1}}\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\binom{m+n-1-r}{n}2^{r}.

3.
Some preliminary results on dilogarithms and polylogarithms

We recall in this section some basic properties of polylogarithms. For

n_{1},\dots,n_{m}
positive integers, one can define the multiple polylogarithm as the complex function given by the series

\mathrm{Li}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m}):=\sum_{0<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{m}}\frac{z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{m}^{k_{m}}}{k_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots k_{m}^{n_{m}}},

which is absolutely convergent for
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
and
|z_{m}|\leq 1
(respectively
|z_{m}|<1
) if

n_{m}>1
(resp.
n_{m}=1
). This series can also be extended to a multi-valued meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. The number
m
is called the length of the polylogarithm, and the number
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
is called the weight.

The case
m=1
and
n_{1}=1
leads to the Taylor series of the logarithm at 1,

\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k}=-\log(1-z).

When
m=1
and
n_{1}>1
, the evaluation at
z=1
yields
the Riemann zeta function

\mathrm{Li}_{n}(1)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{n}}=\zeta(n).

Some combinations of length 2 polylogarithms can be written in terms of length 1 polylogarithms. This will be useful for us in Theorem
11
, which evaluates the areal higher Mahler measure of
\frac{1-x}{1+x}
.
To achieve this simplification we will use some results due to Nakamura
[
Nak12
]
and Panzer
[
Pan17
]
. Here we state the formulation of
[
LL18
]
.

Theorem 8
.

[
LL18
, Theorem 3]
Let
r,s
be positive integers,
k=r+s
, and let
u,v
be complex numbers such that
|u|=|v|=1
. In addition, we assume that
v\not=1
if
s=1
.
Let

{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}}_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}&k\mbox{ odd,}\\
i\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}&k\mbox{ even.}\end{cases}

Then,

\displaystyle 2{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}}_{k}(\mathrm{Li}_{r,s}(u,v))=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\mathrm{Li}_{k}(\overline{uv})+(-1)^{k+1}\mathrm{Li}_{r}(\overline{u})\mathrm{Li}_{s}(\overline{v})+(-1)^{r-1}\mathrm{Li}_{r}(\overline{u})\mathrm{Li}_{s}(v)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{r-1}\left(\binom{k-1}{r-1}\mathrm{Li}_{k}(\overline{u})+\binom{k-1}{s-1}\mathrm{Li}_{k}(v)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\left(\binom{m-1}{r-1}\mathrm{Li}_{m}(\overline{u})+\binom{m-1}{s-1}(-1)^{k+m}\mathrm{Li}_{m}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\times((-1)^{r}\mathrm{Li}_{k-m}(uv)+(-1)^{s+m}\mathrm{Li}_{k-m}(\overline{uv})).

We close this section by considering a modification of the length one dilogarithm. The Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm is given by

D(z)=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z)-\log|z|\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z))=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(\mathrm{Li}_{2}(z))+\log|z|\arg(1-z).

It is a continuous function in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, which is real analytic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
. It also satisfies

(15)

-2\int_{0}^{\theta}\log|2\sin t|dt=D(e^{2i\theta}).

The following special values will be useful for us

(16)

D(e^{i\pi/3})=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}L(\chi_{-3},2),

and

(17)

D(i)=L(\chi_{-4},2),

where
L(\chi_{-3},s)
and
L(\chi_{-4},s)

denote the Dirichlet
L
-functions on the characters
\chi_{-3}=\left(\frac{-3}{\cdot}\right)
and
\chi_{-4}=\left(\frac{-4}{\cdot}\right)
of conductor 3 and 4 respectively.

\mathbb{D}\cap\left\{\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|\geq 1\right\}

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}\cap\left\{\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|\geq 1\right\}}\log\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|dA(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[4-\pi-2\left(\frac{3}{2}-D(i)\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}\right]=\frac{1}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}D(i).

By combining this with (
40
) and (
17
), the result follows.

∎

5.
The areal versions of generalized, higher, and zeta Mahler measures

In this section we consider areal versions of some variants of the Mahler measure, namely generalized Mahler measure, higher Mahler measure, and zeta Mahler measures.

5.1.
Generalized areal Mahler measure

Generalized Mahler measures were introduced by Gon and Oyanagi
[
GO04
]
who studied their basic properties, computed some examples, and related them to multiple sine functions and special values of Dirichlet
L
-functions. They were also studied in
[
IL13
,
Lal08
]
.

Given nonzero rational functions
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\times}
, the generalized (logarithmic) Mahler measure of
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
is defined by

\mathrm{m}_{\max}(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\max\{\log|P_{1}|,\dots,\log|P_{r}|\}\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}.

It is natural to consider the generalized areal Mahler measure of
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
to be defined by

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},\max}(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\max\{\log|P_{1}|,\dots,\log|P_{r}|\}dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n}).

We have the following result.

Proposition 9
.

We have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},\mathrm{max}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=-\frac{1}{2n}.

We remark that in the classical case, we have
\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{max}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
.

Proof.

As usual, we proceed to apply the definition together with the change of variables
x_{j}=\rho_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
. This gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},\mathrm{max}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\max\{\log|x_{1}|,\dots,\log|x_{n}|\}dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\max\{\log\rho_{1},\dots,\log\rho_{n}\}\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}d\rho_{1}\dots d\rho_{n}.

Notice that the above integral can be written as a sum of
n!
integrals, where each term corresponds to a certain order of the variables
\rho_{j}
. The advantage of considering the ordered variables lies in the fact that the maximum is then easy to describe. Thus, the above becomes

\displaystyle 2^{n}n!\int_{0\leq\rho_{1}\leq\dots\leq\rho_{n}\leq 1}\log\rho_{n}\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}d\rho_{1}\dots d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}n!\int_{0\leq\rho_{2}\leq\dots\leq\rho_{n}\leq 1}\log\rho_{n}\frac{\rho_{2}^{3}}{2}\cdots\rho_{n}d\rho_{2}\dots d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}n!\int_{0\leq\rho_{3}\leq\dots\leq\rho_{n}\leq 1}\log\rho_{n}\frac{\rho_{3}^{5}}{2\cdot 4}\cdots\rho_{n}d\rho_{3}\dots d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}n!\int_{0\leq\rho_{n}\leq 1}\log\rho_{n}\frac{\rho_{n}^{2n-1}}{2\cdots(2n-2)}d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n\left.\frac{\rho_{n}^{2n}}{(2n)^{2}}(2n\log\rho_{n}-1)\right|^{1}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n}.

∎

5.2.
Multiple and higher areal Mahler measures

The multiple Mahler measure was defined in
[
KLO08
]
for nonzero rational functions
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\times}
by

\mathrm{m}(P_{1},\dots,P_{r}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log|P_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\cdots\log|P_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}.

For the particular case in which
P_{1}=\dots=P_{r}=P
, the multiple Mahler measure is called
higher Mahler measure
and is given by

(48)

\mathrm{m}_{r}(P):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log^{r}|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}.

The multiple Mahler measure and the higher Mahler measure were considered by various authors who computed specific formulas and proved various limiting properties
[
KLO08
,
Sas10
,
Sas12
,
BS12
,
BBSW12
,
IL13
,
Bis14
,
BM14
,
LL16
]
.
It is natural to consider the areal versions of these constructions, given by

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h_{1},\dots,h_{r}}(P_{1},\dots,P_{r}):=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\log^{h_{1}}|P_{1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\cdots\log^{h_{r}}|P_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n}).

Proposition 10
.

We have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h_{1},\dots,h_{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\frac{(-1)^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n}}h_{1}!\cdots h_{n}!}{2^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n}+n}}.

We remark that in the classical case, we have
\mathrm{m}_{h_{1},\dots,h_{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
.

Proof.

First we recall equation (
11
), which in particular gives

\int_{0}^{1}x\log^{k}xdx=\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{2^{k+1}}.

Proceeding as usual by setting
x_{j}=\rho_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h_{1},\dots,h_{n}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\log^{h_{1}}|x_{1}|\cdots\log^{h_{n}}|x_{n}|dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\int_{0}^{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}\log^{h_{1}}\rho_{1}\cdots\log^{h_{n}}\rho_{n}\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}d\rho_{1}\dots d\rho_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n}}h_{1}!\cdots h_{n}!}{2^{h_{1}+\cdots+h_{n}+n}}.

∎

We consider a more elaborate case. The following formula is proven in
[
Sas15
,
LL16
]
:

\mathrm{m}_{h}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\frac{|E_{h}|}{2^{h}}\pi^{h}}&h\text{ even},\\
\\
0&h\text{ odd},\end{cases}

where
E_{n}
denotes the
n
th Euler number.

We have the following areal version of the above result.

Theorem 11
.

For
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
even, we have,

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=

\displaystyle-2(h-1)B_{h}(\pi i)^{h-1}+\frac{E_{h}(\pi i)^{h}}{2^{h}}-\frac{E_{h-2}(\pi i)^{h-2}h(h-1)}{2^{h-2}}\log 2

\displaystyle-\frac{4h!}{2^{h}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=2\\
\end{subarray}}^{h-1}(1-2^{1-m})\zeta(m)\frac{E_{h-m-1}(\pi i)^{h-m-1}}{(h-m-1)!}

\displaystyle-2(\pi i)^{h-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=0\end{subarray}}^{h}\binom{h}{m}(1-2^{1-m})(1-2^{1-h+m})B_{m}B_{h-m},

where
B_{n}
and
E_{n}
denote the
n
th Bernoulli number and the
n
th Euler number respectively, and the first sum for
h=2
should be interpreted as equal to zero.

For
h
odd, we have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=0.

Before proceeding to the proof of this result, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 12
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{>2}

|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 1
. We have

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{b>1}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b^{h-1}}\sum_{a=1}^{b-1}\frac{\alpha^{a}}{a}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{1,h-1}(\alpha,\beta),

and for
\alpha\not=1

(50)

\displaystyle\sum_{b>1}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b^{h+1}}\sum_{a=1}^{b-1}a\alpha^{a}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}(\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h}(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h+1}(\alpha\beta)-\mathrm{Li}_{h}(\alpha\beta)+\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h+1}(\beta)).

Proof.

Identity (
49

50

\displaystyle\sum_{b>1}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b^{h+1}}\sum_{a=1}^{b-1}a\alpha^{a}=

\displaystyle\sum_{b\geq 1}\frac{\beta^{b}}{b^{h+1}}\frac{\alpha((b-1)\alpha^{b}-b\alpha^{b-1}+1)}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}(\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h}(\alpha\beta)-\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h+1}(\alpha\beta)-\mathrm{Li}_{h}(\alpha\beta)+\alpha\mathrm{Li}_{h+1}(\beta)).

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},h}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log^{h}\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|dA(x).

We remark that the change of variables

takes the unit disk to the right half plane
\mathbb{H}_{+}
defined by

by definition, the areal measure of

Since the map

\left|x_{1,z_{1}}x_{2,z_{2}}-x_{1,z_{2}}x_{2,z_{1}}\right|dz_{1}dz_{2}=\left|\frac{dx}{dz}\right|^{2}dz_{1}dz_{2}=\left|\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)\right|^{2}dz_{1}dz_{2}=\frac{4dz_{1}dz_{2}}{|z+1|^{4}}.

We further consider the change to polar coordinates

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\log^{h}\rho\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{d\theta}{\left|\rho e^{i\theta}+1\right|^{4}}d\rho

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\rho\log^{h}\rho\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{d\theta}{\left|\rho e^{i\theta}+1\right|^{4}}d\rho+(-1)^{h}\int_{0}^{1}\rho\log^{h}\rho\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{d\theta}{\left|\rho e^{i\theta}+1\right|^{4}}d\rho,

Evaluating the individual terms in the above formula leads to

From (

Since

Finally we get, for
h>2
even,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log^{h}\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|dA(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h!}{2^{h-2}}\zeta(h-1)+\frac{2h!}{\pi i}\left[2(1-2^{1-h})\frac{i^{h}B_{h}\pi^{h}}{h!}-(h-1)\frac{i^{h}B_{h}\pi^{h}}{h!}-i\pi 2^{1-h}\zeta(h-1)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{i^{h+1}E_{h}\pi^{h+1}}{2^{h+1}h!}+\log(2)\frac{i^{h+1}E_{h-2}\pi^{h-1}}{2^{h-1}(h-2)!}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=2\\
m\,\text{odd}\end{subarray}}^{h-1}2(2^{1-m}-1)\zeta(m)\frac{i^{h-m}E_{h-m-1}\pi^{h-m}}{2^{h}(h-m-1)!}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{i^{h}\pi^{h}}{h!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=2\\
m\,\text{even}\end{subarray}}^{h-1}\binom{h}{m}(2^{1-m}-1)(2^{1-h+m}-1)B_{m}B_{h-m}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(h-1)B_{h}(\pi i)^{h-1}+\frac{E_{h}(\pi i)^{h}}{2^{h}}-\log(2)\frac{E_{h-2}(\pi i)^{h-2}h(h-1)}{2^{h-2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{4h!}{2^{h}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=2\\
\end{subarray}}^{h-1}(1-2^{1-m})\zeta(m)\frac{E_{h-m-1}(\pi i)^{h-m-1}}{(h-m-1)!}

(54)

\displaystyle-2(\pi i)^{h-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=0\end{subarray}}^{h}\binom{h}{m}(1-2^{1-m})(1-2^{1-h+m})B_{m}B_{h-m},

where we have used that
B_{n}=E_{n}=0
when
n
is odd (with the exception of
B_{1}
).

Note that above computation fails to converge when
h=2.
Therefore, we need to evaluate the
h=2
case in a different way. Since
\log^{2}\left|\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right|=\log^{2}\left|1-x\right|-2\log\left|1-x\right|\log\left|1+x\right|+\log^{2}\left|1+x\right|,
and
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(1-x\right)=\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(1+x\right),
we have

\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=2\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(1+x\right)-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\left|1-x\right|\log\left|1+x\right|dx.

It only remains to compute the second integral. Following the method in the proof of
[
KLO08
, Theorem 7]
, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\left|1-x\right|\log\left|1+x\right|dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\rho\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\left(\log\left(1-\rho e^{i\theta}\right)\right)\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\left(\log\left(1+\rho e^{i\theta}\right)\right)d\theta\right]d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\rho\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\rho^{k}}{k}\cos(k\theta)\right)\left(-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\rho^{\ell}}{\ell}\cos(\ell\theta)\right)d\theta\right]d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\rho^{k+\ell+1}}{k\ell}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(k\theta)\cos(\ell\theta)d\theta\right]d\rho.

On the other hand, we have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(k\theta)\cos(\ell\theta)d\theta=\begin{cases}\pi&\quad\quad\mbox{if}\ k=\ell,\\
0&\quad\quad\mbox{if}\ k\neq\ell.\end{cases}

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\log\left|1-x\right|\log\left|1+x\right|dx=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k,\ell\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}\rho^{k+\ell+1}}{k\ell}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos(k\theta)\cos(\ell\theta)d\theta\right]d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{2k+1}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k^{2}(k+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}(-1)^{k}\left[\frac{1}{k^{2}}-\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{k+1}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left[\mathrm{Li}_{2}(-1)-2\mathrm{Li}_{1}(-1)-1\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}\left(1+x\right)-\mathrm{Li}_{2}(-1)+2\mathrm{Li}_{1}(-1)+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-2\log 2+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}-2\log 2.

Finally we remark that this value is also obtained by replacing
h=2
in (
5.2
).
∎

5.3.
Areal zeta Mahler measure

The zeta Mahler measure was defined by Akatsuka
[
Aka09
]
for a nonzero rational function
P\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\times}
by

Z(s,P):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left|P\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right)\right|^{s}\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}},

where
s
is a complex variable in a neighborhood of
0
.

The zeta Mahler measure was considered in
[
KLO08
,
Bis14
,
BM18
,
Sas15
,
Rin22
]
.
Its Taylor expansion is the generating series of the higher Mahler measure given in (
48
).

Z(s,P)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}_{k}(P)s^{k}}{k!}.

Akatsuka computed
the zeta Mahler measure

Z(s,x-c)
for a constant
c
. His result in
[
Aka09
, Theorem 2]
implies the following formula for
\rho<1
:

(55)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\rho e^{i\theta}+1|^{s}d\theta={\vphantom{F}}_{2}\kern-0.5ptF_{1}\left(-\frac{s}{2},-\frac{s}{2};1;\rho^{2}\right).

We may consider the areal zeta Mahler measure defined by

Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,P):=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left|P\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\right)\right|^{s}dA(x_{1})\dots dA(x_{n}).

We follow some arguments from
[
KLO08
, Theorem 14]
to prove the next result.

Theorem 13
.

We have

Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(1-2^{1-j})(\zeta(j)-1)s^{j}\right).

Proof.

By definition and the usual change of variables, we have

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|x+1|^{s}dA(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\rho e^{i\theta}+1|^{s}\rho d\theta d\rho.

By applying Akatsuka’s result (
55
), we then have to compute

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}{\vphantom{F}}{}_{2}\kern-0.5ptF_{1}\left(-\frac{s}{2},-\frac{s}{2};1;\rho^{2}\right)\rho d\rho=2\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-s/2)_{n}^{2}}{(n!)^{2}}\rho^{2n+1}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{s/2}{n}^{2}\frac{1}{(n+1)}=\frac{1}{s/2+1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{s/2}{n}\binom{s/2+1}{s/2-n}=\frac{1}{s/2+1}\binom{s+1}{s/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s+1}{(s/2+1)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(s+1)}{\Gamma(s/2+1)^{2}}.

We now apply the Weierstrass product of the Gamma function

\Gamma(s+1)^{-1}=e^{\gamma s}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{s}{k}\right)e^{-s/k},

to obtain

\displaystyle Z_{\mathbb{D}}(s,x+1)=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1+\frac{s}{2k}\right)^{2}}{1+\frac{s}{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(2\log\left(1+\frac{s}{2k}\right)-\log\left(1+\frac{s}{k}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}}{j}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{(2k)^{j}}-\frac{1}{k^{j}}\right)s^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(1-2^{1-j})\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{s^{j}}{k^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(1-2^{1-j})(\zeta(j)-1)s^{j}\right).

∎

As the areal zeta Mahler measure is the generating function of the areal higher Mahler measure, it can be used to compute this last one by taking successive derivatives and evaluating at
s=0
. For example, the first few values are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},1}(x+1)=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},2}(x+1)=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(2)-1}{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},3}(x+1)=

\displaystyle-\frac{3(\zeta(3)-1)}{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},4}(x+1)=

\displaystyle\frac{3(7\zeta(4)+\zeta(2)^{2}-2\zeta(2)-6)}{4}=\frac{3(19\zeta(4)-4\zeta(2)-12)}{8},

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D},5}(x+1)=

\displaystyle-\frac{15(3\zeta(5)+\zeta(3)\zeta(2)-\zeta(3)-\zeta(2)-2)}{2}.

6.
Conclusion

We have obtained formulas for the areal Mahler and its generalizations for various rational functions. In most cases the results are connected to evaluations of polylogarithms leading to special values of functions with arithmetic significance such as the Riemann zeta function, and Dirichlet
L
-functions. In this sense, the results are analogous to what is obtained in the case of the standard Mahler measure.

However, there is a crucial difference between the areal case and the standard case. In the standard case, there is a way to assign a weight to the terms in the formula, that typically results in formulas of homogeneous weight 1. For this, we recall that the length one
n
-th polyogarithm has weight
n
. The logarithm is associated to
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(z)
and it has therefore weight 1. The constant
\pi
arises as an evaluation of the logarithm and therefore has weight 1. Finally, we assign weight one to the Mahler measure itself. Taking the weight multiplicatively, we have, for example, in Smyth’s formula (
2
) that
\mathrm{m}(1+x+y)
has weight 1, while
L(\chi_{-3},2)
has weight 2 (as it is the evaluation of a dilogarithm), while
\pi
has weight 1, giving a total weight of 1 on the right-hand side. In contrast, the terms on the right-hand side of the areal Mahler measure (
5
) do not have a homogeneous weight. While the term
\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}L(\chi_{-3},2)
has weight 1, the term
\frac{1}{6}
has weight 0 and the term
-\frac{11\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}
has weight
-1
. This suggests that if there is a connection between
\mathrm{m}_{\mathbb{D}}
and the regulator, it will be more difficult to describe than in the standard case. It would be nevertheless interesting to explore the possibility of such connection.

None of the formulas considered in this article correspond to rational functions whose Mahler measure is related to special values of other
L
-functions, such as
L
-functions attached to elliptic curves. For example, the following formula was conjectured by Deninger
[
Den97
]
and Boyd
[
Boy98
]
and proven by Rogers and Zudilin
[
RZ14
]
:

\mathrm{m}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}+y+\frac{1}{y}+1\right)=L^{\prime}(E_{15a8},0),

where
L(E_{15a8},s)
denotes the
L
-function associated to the elliptic curve
15a8
.
A natural question and direction of future research would be to evaluate the areal version of the above formula.

Finding a Shortest M M -link Path in a Monge Directed Acyclic Graph
Joy Z. Wan

Abstract

Finding a shortest M M -link path from 1 1 to N N in G G for any given 1 < M < N − 1 1<M<N-1 has many applications. In this paper, we give a contract-and-conquer algorithm for this problem which runs in O ⁡ ( N ​ M ​ ( N − M ) ​ log ⁡ ( N − M ) ) O\left(\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}\right) time and O ⁡ ( N ) O\left(N\right) space. It is the first o ⁡ ( N ​ M ) o\left(NM\right) -time algorithm with linear space complexity, and its time complexity decreases with M M when M ≥ N / 2 M\geq N/2 . In contrast, all previous strongly polynomial algorithms have running time growing with M M . For both O ⁡ ( p ​ o ​ l ​ y ​ ( log ⁡ N ) ) O\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right) and N − O ⁡ ( p ​ o ​ l ​ y ​ ( log ⁡ N ) ) N-O\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right) regimes of M M , our algorithm has running time O ⁡ ( N ⋅ p ​ o ​ l ​ y ​ ( log ⁡ N ) ) O\left(N\cdot poly\left(\log N\right)\right) , which partially answers an open question rased in [ 2 ] affirmatively. Introduction

Many algorithmic problems
[
2
,
3
]

M
-link path in a Monge directed acyclic graph (DAG)
G

1,2,\cdots,N

\left\{\left(i,j\right):1\leq i<j\leq N\right\}
; and the edge-length (or

c\left(i,l\right)+c\left(j,k\right)\geq c\left(i,k\right)+c\left(j,l\right).

In addition, the length

of each edge
\left(i,j\right)
can be evaluated in
constant

-link
s
-

-link
s
-
t

m
-link
s
-
t

As observed by
[
2
,
3
]

-link
1
-
N

time and space by the standard dynamic
programming accelerated by the celebrated SMAWK algorithm
[
1
]
. A
parametric search scheme was also introduced in
[
2
,
3
]
for

M
-link
1
-
N
path in

. When the edge lengths are
restricted to integers, the binary search method yields a
weakly
polynomial
algorithm
[
2
,
3
]
. A
strongly polynomial

algorithm with sub-quadratic complexity was developed in
[
2
]
, which
is referred to the algorithm AST. The algorithm AST follows Megiddo’s
parametric search paradigm
[
7
]
, and runs in
O\left(N\sqrt{M\log N}+N\log N\right)

O\left(N\sqrt{M\log N}\right)
space. For
the
\Omega\left(\log N\right)
regime of

[
2
]
is superior to the
O\left(NM\right)
-time accelerated

O\left(N\cdot poly\left(\log n\right)\right)
-time algorithm was raised as an open question in

[
2
]
.

For the same
\Omega\left(\log N\right)
regime of

, Schieber

[
8
]
gave a

parametric search algorithm with

O\left(N2^{4\sqrt{\left(\log M\right)\left(\log\log N\right)}}\right)

[
2
]
when

, but may be inferior when

M=\Theta\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
. Indeed when

M=\log^{16}N
, Schieber’s algorithm has the same asymptotic time complexity

O\left(N\log^{16}N\right)=O\left(NM\right)
as the accelerated
dynamic programming, while the algorithm AST
[
2
]
has time complexity

O\left(N\log^{8.5}N\right)
and is asymptotically faster by a factor of

\Theta\left(\log^{7.5}N\right)
. Similarly, when

,
Schieber’s algorithm has time complexity
O\left(N\log^{8}N\right)=O\left(NM^{2}\right)
, which is even asymptotically slower by a factor of

\Theta\left(M\right)
than the accelerated dynamic programming.

Schieber
[
8
]
also noted, with no analysis, the linear space
complexity of his recursive algorithm. A careful look into the recursion
reveals that the space complexity hidden within the recursion is more than
linear order. The recursion runs on an auxiliary DAG with modified lengths of

all
edges from the node
1
. These modified lengths have to be
maintained on the recursion stack, and the space needed for maintaining these
modified lengths grows with the recursion depth and certainly exceeds the
linear order. There is no apparent fix for such recursive algorithm to achieve
the linear space complexity.

In addition to the superlinear space complexity, all of the above strongly polynomial
algorithms have time complexity strictly growing with

. However, the
closeness of

to

could be

M
-link
1
-
N

\left(N-1\right)
-link
1
-
N
path. If

, then there are

\left(N-2\right)
-link
1
-
N
paths, each of which can be generated from the

\left(N-1\right)
-link
1
-
N
path by

-link
1
-
N

1
-link
1
-
N
path. If

, then there are
N-2

2
-link
1
-
N

paths, each of which can be generated by

-

-link

n

\overline{f}

\left(j\right)

\Theta\left(n-s+1-m\right)

All

•

f_{s}\left(1,j\right)=c_{s}\left(s,j\right)
for each

•

for each

(1)

The parsimonious dynamic programming exploits the
minimal

recurrence dependence as illustrated in Figure
1
:

•

The entries

for

for all
j\in\left[s+m-1:n-1\right]

•

In turn, the entries
f_{s}\left(m-1,j\right)
for

for all

Recursively, the outputs depend exactly on the
\left(m-1\right)\left(n-s+1-m\right)

for all

Minimal and Maximal Shortest-Path Trees

Suppose
1\leq s<N
and
G_{s}
is a Monge DAG on
\left[s:N\right]
with edge-length function
c_{s}
. A
shortest-path tree

(SPT) in
G_{s}
is a directed tree on
\left[s:N\right]
in which the
tree path from
s
to each node
n
is a shortest
s
-
n
path in
G_{s}
. In
this section, we introduce the notion of minimal SPT and maximal SPT, and
assert that they are also respectively a shallowest SPT and a deepest SPT.

For each
n\in\left[s:N\right]
, let
F_{s}\left(n\right)
be the
minimum length of all
s
-
n
paths in
G_{s}
. All
F_{s}\left(n\right)

for
n\in\left[s:N\right]
satisfy the recurrence:
F_{s}\left(s\right)=0
; and for each
n\in\left[s+1:N\right]
,

F_{s}\left(n\right)=\min_{i\in\left[s:n-1\right]}\left[F_{s}\left(i\right)+c_{s}\left(i,n\right)\right].

Denote
\Pi_{s}\left(s\right):=\left\{0\right\}
; and for
each
n\in\left[s+1:N\right]
denote

\Pi_{s}\left(n\right):=\arg\min_{i\in\left[s:n-1\right]}\left[F_{s}\left(i\right)+c_{s}\left(i,n\right)\right].

Each SPT in
G_{s}
is uniquely defined by a
parent selection

\pi
:

\left[s+1:N\right]\rightarrow\left[s:N-1\right]

\pi\left(n\right)\in\Pi_{s}\left(n\right)

, and vice versa. For each
n\in\left[s:N\right]
, let

\pi_{s}^{\min}\left(n\right)
(resp.,
\pi_{s}^{\max}\left(n\right)
)
be the least (resp., greatest) member in
\Pi_{s}\left(n\right)
. Note
that
\pi_{s}^{\min}\left(s\right)=\pi_{s}^{\min}\left(s\right)=0
. As

F_{s}\left(i\right)+c_{s}\left(i,n\right)
is submodular in
\left(i,n\right)
on the edge lattice of
G_{s}
, both
\pi_{s}^{\min}\left(n\right)
and
\pi_{s}^{\max}\left(n\right)

increase
with

n\in\left[s+1:N\right]
. The
minimal
(resp.,
maximal
) SPT

T_{s}^{\min}
(resp.,
T_{s}^{\max}
) in
G_{s}
is the SPT in
G_{s}

defined by
\pi_{s}^{\min}
(resp.,
\pi_{s}^{\max}
).

For each
n\in\left[s:N\right]
, let
D_{s}\left(n\right)

of numbers of links in all shortest
s
-
n
paths in
G_{s}
, and
d_{s}^{\min}\left(n\right)
(respectively,
d_{s}^{\max}\left(n\right)
) be
the least (respectively, greatest) member in
D_{s}\left(n\right)
.
Clearly,
D_{s}\left(s\right)=\left\{0\right\}
. For each

-
n

with

\displaystyle F_{s}\left(n\right)

\displaystyle=\min_{\left(m,n\right)\in\mathcal{L}_{s}}f_{s}\left(m,n\right)=\min_{m\in\left[n-s\right]}f_{s}\left(m,n\right),

\displaystyle D_{s}\left(n\right)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{m\in\left[n-s\right]}f_{s}\left(m,n\right).

(2)

Lemma 5.2

The procedure
Probe
\left(s,m\right)
runs in

O\left(m\left(r-s-m+1\right)+\left(N-s+1\right)\log\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right).

time and
O\left(N\right)
space.

Proof.
The worst-case running time occurs when the contraction phase is reached. The
contraction phase has
O\left(N-s+1\right)
running time. The total
running time of the other two phases is dominated by the invocations of

PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
and the computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
. We
show that the former’s total running time is

O\left(m\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right)

and the latter’s total running time is

O\left(\left(N-s+1\right)\log\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right),

from which the lemma follows.

Let

k=\left\lceil\log\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right\rceil.

Then the exponential search takes
k
iterations. In the
j
-th iteration with

j\in\left[k\right]
,

n=\min\left\{\left(s+m-1\right)+2^{j},N-M+m\right\};

hence the invocation
PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
has running time

O\left(m\left(n-s+1-m\right)\right)=O\left(m2^{j}\right).

Since

m\sum_{j=1}^{k}2^{j}=2m\left(2^{k}-1\right)<4m\left(r-s-m+1\right),

the invocations of
PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
in the exponential
search is

O\left(m\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right).

The exponential search takes
k
computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
. At the beginning of the binary search,

\displaystyle n^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(s+m-1\right)+2^{k-1},

\displaystyle n^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\left(s+m-1\right)+2^{k}.

As
n^{\prime\prime}-n^{\prime}\leq 2^{k-1}
, the binary search takes at most

k-1
iterations, hence has at most
k-1
computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
and one computation of
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
. Thus, there at most
2k
computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
in total, and
each computation takes
O\left(N-s+1\right)
time. So, the total time by
the computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
is

O\left(\left(N-s+1\right)\log\left(r-s-m+1\right)\right).

The linear space complexity of
Probe
\left(s,m\right)
follows
from that both
PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
and the computation of

d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
require linear space.

6
The Contract-and-Conquer Algorithm

Suppose
G_{1}=G
is a Monge DAG on
\left[N\right]
with edge-length
function
c_{1}=c
, and
2\leq M\leq N-2
. Denote
\Lambda^{\ast}:=\Lambda_{1}\left(M,N\right)
. Computing a path in
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\ast}\left(M,N\right)
is reducible in
linear
time and space to finding a member

\lambda\in\Lambda^{\ast}
. In this section, we present a contract-and-conquer
(C&C) algorithm for finding a member
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\ast}
in linear
space and
O\left(\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}\right)
time.

If
M\left(N-M\right)\leq 4N\log\left(N-M\right)
, then the C&C
algorithm simply invokes
PBF
\left(1,M,N\right)
on
G_{1}
and
returns
\lambda=\delta_{1}\left(M,N\right)
in linear space and

O\left(M\left(N-M\right)\right)=O\left(\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}\right)

time. In the remaining of this section, we assume

M\left(N-M\right)>4N\log\left(N-M\right).

Then
16<M<N-16
. Indeed,
N-M>16
follows from

\left(N-M\right)/\log\left(N-M\right)>4N/M>4,

which further implies

M>4\frac{N}{N-M}\log\left(N-M\right)>4\log\left(N-M\right)>16.

Let

K:=\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{M\left(N-M\right)}{N\log\left(N-M\right)}}\right\rceil.

(6)

Then
2<K<\sqrt{M}
as

2<K\leq\left\lceil\sqrt{\frac{M}{\log\left(N-M\right)}}\right\rceil\leq\left\lceil\sqrt{M}/2\right\rceil<\sqrt{M}.

Hence
K/M>\sqrt{M}>4
. Denote
K^{\prime}:=K-M\operatorname{mod}K
, and
partition
M

evenly
into
K
integers
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{K}

where

m_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cc}\left\lfloor M/K\right\rfloor,&\text{if }1\leq k\leq K^{\prime};\\
\left\lceil M/K\right\rceil,&\text{if }K^{\prime}<k\leq K.\end{array}\right.

Then,
m_{k}\geq 4
for each
1\leq k\leq K
.

The C&C algorithm
implicitly
maintains a Monge DAG
G_{s}
on

\left[s:N\right]
with edge length function
c_{s}
for some
s\geq 1
.
Initially,
s=1
. Each
G_{s}
with
s>1
is generated from
G_{1}
by
successive contractions and is
explicitly
represented by a global
array
h
of size
N
such that
h\left(j\right)=c_{s}\left(s,j\right)
for each
j\in\left[s+1:N\right]
. The C&C algorithm proceeds in at
most
K
successive stages. At the beginning of the stage
k\leq K
, the
following invariant properties are maintained:

•

M=\sum_{i=k}^{K}m_{i}\leq N-s-1
;

•

\Lambda_{s}\left(M,N\right)
in
G_{s}
is
\Lambda^{\ast}
.

These properties hold trivially for the first stage
k=1
where

s=1
. The stage
k
runs as follows depending on whether
k=K
or not.

Suppose
k<K
. As

\displaystyle 4

\displaystyle\leq m_{k}=M-\sum_{i=k+1}^{K}m_{i}\leq M-4,

\displaystyle 4

\displaystyle\leq M\leq N-s-1,

the stage
k
invokes
Probe
\left(s,m_{k}\right)
on
G_{s}
. If
the invocation of
Probe
\left(s,m_{k}\right)
finds a member

\lambda\in\Lambda_{s}\left(M,N\right)=\Lambda^{\ast}
, then the C&C
algorithm
terminates
with the output
\lambda
. Otherwise, the
invocation of
Probe
\left(s,m_{k}\right)

contracts

G_{s}
by updating
s
and
h
and reduces
M
by
m_{k}
. After the
contraction,

•

M=\sum_{i=k+1}^{K}m_{i}\leq N-s-1
;

•

\Lambda_{s}\left(M,N\right)
in
G_{s}
is
\Lambda^{\ast}
by
Theorem
5.1
.

The C&C algorithm then moves on to the stage
k+1
.

Suppose
k=K
. Then the (last) stage
K
simply invokes
PBF
\left(s,M,N\right)
on
G_{s}
, computes
\lambda=\delta_{s}\left(M,N\right)
,
and
terminates
with the output
\lambda
.

The data structures needed by the C&C algorithm are just a few global arrays
indexed of size
N
. The array
h
is used for representing the graph
G_{s}
.
Two arrays
f
and
\overline{f}
are used by the procedure
PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
. The computation of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
and the
invocation of SMAWK algorithm require linear working space. We remark that
there is no need to explicitly maintain the sequence
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{K}
. They can be easily derived from the stage number
k
and
K^{\prime}=K-M\operatorname{mod}K
. Thus, the C&C algorithm has linear space
complexity.

Next, we derive the time complexity of the C&C algorithm.

Theorem 6.1

The C&C algorithm has time complexity
O\left(\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}\right)
.

Proof.
Clearly, the worst-case running time occurs when the stage
K
is reached. For
each
1\leq k\leq k
, let
s_{k}
be the root
s
at the beginning of the
stage
k
. Then,
s_{1}=1
; and for each
k<K
,
s_{k+1}+1
is exactly the
pivot
r
in stage
k
. Thus, for each
k<K
,
s_{k+1}+1\geq s_{k}+m_{k}+1

implying that
s_{k+1}\geq s_{k}+m_{k}
. At the beginning of the last stage

K
,
m_{K}\leq N-s_{K}-1
; hence

s_{K}+m_{K}+1\leq N.

(7)

By Lemma
5.2
, each stage
k<K
has running time

\displaystyle O\left(m_{k}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)\right)+

\displaystyle O\left(\left(N-s_{k}+1\right)\log\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)\right).

The stage
K
has running time

O\left(m_{K}\left(N-s_{K}-m_{K}+1\right)\right).

So, the total running time is

\displaystyle O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}m_{k}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)+m_{K}\left(N-s_{K}-m_{K}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(N-s_{k}+1\right)\log\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)\right)

We show the two sums in the big-
O
notations are bounded respectively by

\displaystyle\frac{M}{K}\left(N-M\right)+N+M,

\displaystyle N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(n-M\right).

By the definition of
m_{k}
for
k\in\left[K\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}m_{k}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)+m_{K}\left(N-s_{K}-m_{K}+1\right)

\displaystyle<\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}m_{k}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}\right)+m_{K}\left(N-s_{K}-m_{K}\right)+2M

\displaystyle\leq\left\lceil\frac{M}{K}\right\rceil\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}\right)+\left\lceil\frac{M}{K}\right\rceil\left(N-s_{K}-m_{K}\right)+2M

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{M}{K}\right\rceil\left(N-M-1\right)+2M

\displaystyle<\frac{M}{K}\left(N-M\right)+N+M.

By the concavity of the logarithm function and the inequality in equation
(
7
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(N-s_{k}+1\right)\log\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)

\displaystyle<N\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\log\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\left(s_{k+1}-s_{k}-m_{k}\right)}{K-1}+2\right)

\displaystyle=N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(\frac{s_{K}+m_{K}-M-1}{K-1}+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(\frac{N-M-2}{K-1}+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(n-M\right).

Finally, by the choice of
K
in equation (
6
),

\frac{M}{K}\left(N-M\right)\leq\frac{M\left(N-M\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{M\left(N-M\right)}{N\log\left(N-M\right)}}}=\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}.

and

\displaystyle N\left(K-1\right)\log\left(n-M\right)\leq N\sqrt{\frac{M\left(N-M\right)}{N\log\left(N-M\right)}}\log\left(N-M\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}.

Therefore, the theorem holds.

The rationale for the choice of
K
, the number of stages, given by equation
(
6
) is now clear from the above proof. This choice is to strike
a balance between the invocations of
PBF
\left(s,m,n\right)
and
the computations of
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
.

7
Conclusion

For the
O\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
and
N-O\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
regimes of
M
, the C&C algorithm has
running time
O\left(N\cdot poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
. It
remains open whether there is
O\left(N\cdot poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
-time algorithm in the regime of
M
where both
M
and
N-M
are at
least
\Omega\left(poly\left(\log N\right)\right)
. It seems quite
possible that with some additional cleverness our algorithm could be made to
run faster. Note that the membership test of
\lambda
in
\Lambda^{\ast}=\Lambda_{1}\left(M,N\right)
via
d_{s}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
and
d_{s}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
in
G_{s}\left(\lambda\right)
can also be done via
d_{1}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
and

d_{1}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
in
G_{1}\left(\lambda\right)
,
and the same asymptotic upper bound
O\left(\sqrt{NM\left(N-M\right)\log\left(N-M\right)}\right)
on the total running time is still valid.
Each time we compute
d_{1}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
d_{1}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)
, we actually produce the parametric shortest-path
tree
T_{1}^{\min}\left(\lambda,N\right)
or
T_{1}^{\max}\left(\lambda,N\right)

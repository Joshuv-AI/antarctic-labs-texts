On the robust learning mixtures of linear regressions
Ying Huang, Liang Chen

Abstract
In this note, we consider the problem of robust learning mixtures of linear regressions. We connect mixtures of linear regressions and mixtures of Gaussians with a simple thresholding, so that a quasi-polynomial time algorithm can be obtained under some mild separation condition. This algorithm has significantly better robustness than the previous result. Keywords: mixtures of linear regressions, mixtures of Gaussians, time complexity, robust learning MSC 2020: 62-08, 62J05, 68W40

Introduction

We consider the following mixtures of linear regressions (MLR) model

z\sim\operatorname{multinomial}(p),x\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\mathbf{I}_{d}\right),y=\left\langle w_{z},x\right\rangle+\eta

(1.1)

where
p\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the proportion of different components satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}=1
,
\mathcal{N}\left(0,\mathbf{I}_{d}\right)
denotes the
d
-dimensional standard Gaussian distribution,
\eta
represents the noise obeying zero-mean
\sigma
-subgaussian distribution
\tau_{\sigma}
(namely,
\mathbf{E}_{\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}e^{t\eta}\leq e^{\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
). The goal is to recover the weights
\left\{w_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{k}
from a dataset
\left\{\left(x_{\ell},y_{\ell}\right)\right\}_{\ell=1}^{N}
which is i.i.d. generated by (
1.1
).

Assumptions.
We make the following assumptions.

(A1) For every
i\in[k],p_{i}\geq p_{\min}
for some
p_{\min}>0
.

(A2) Each
c\leq\left\|w_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq 1
, and for some
\Delta\in(c,1),\left\|w_{i}-w_{j}\right\|_{2}\geq\Delta
for any
i\neq j\in[k]
, where
c\in(0,1)
is some constant.

MLR has been concerned by the statistical learning and theoretical computer science communities for a long time

]
. These studies have different focuses, such as studying the local convergence of the non-convex algorithm
[

,

]
, and constructing an efficient algorithm under the condition of non-degenerate parameter matrix
[
2
,
16
,

]
. This paper mainly focuses on the algorithm design under the general setting
[
12
,
3
,
5
]
, namely, only the separation constant
\Delta
is required. Recently
[
5
]
, proposed a quasi-polynomial time algorithm for MLR, the algorithm depends on a strong algebraic geometry result established by them to extract the information from positive definite tensors. To the best of our knowledge, the time complexity of their algorithm is currently the best in the general setting. However, this algorithm has weak anti-noise ability, that is, it requires
\Delta>\sigma\times{\rm poly}(k)
, which is obviously strict for the case of a large number of components. On the other hand, we know from
[
5
]
that
\Delta/\sigma
cannot be arbitrarily small in a noisy environment, otherwise it will lead to an exponential sample complexity.

In this paper, we hope to find a compromise between the effectiveness, robustness and conditional constraints of the algorithm. We assume that the separation constant
\Delta
is not arbitrarily small,
then an robust algorithm with a time complexity of
\exp(\mathcal{O}(((1+\sigma)/c^{2})^{2}))\times{\rm poly}(1/p_{\min},k,d,1/\epsilon,(dk)^{(\log(1/\epsilon))^{2}})
can be obtained. This is a quasi-polynomial time algorithm for
1/p_{\min},k,d,1/\epsilon
as long as the constants
c,1/\sigma
are not arbitrarily small, where
\epsilon\in(0,1/k)
denotes recovery accuracy. Compared with
[
5
]
, in the noise-free case, we cannot give a fully quasi-polynomial time algorithm for all parameters. However, in the case where the number of components
k
is large and
\sigma
is not very small, our method has a weaker requirement for the separation constant
\Delta
, in other words our method is more robust to noise, even if
\sigma
is larger than
|w_{i}|,i\in[k]
. The key idea of this method is to connect MLR and mixtures of Gaussians through a thresholding, then we can directly apply the recent nice results about the mixture of Gaussians
[
4
]
, the thresholding operation can greatly reduce the influence of noise, and the weights can be recovered with arbitrarily accuracy.

2
Main results

Our thresholding method is based on a simple fact, direct calculation leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

For the weights
\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, the constant
c
defined in
Assumptions
, and
0<\epsilon<1/k
, we have

\bigg|\mathbf{E}_{x\sim N(0,I_{d}),\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\big[x\mid x\in V_{i,\eta}\big]-v_{i}\bigg|\leq\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)

(2.2)

where

v_{i}=\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}w_{i}}{c^{2}|w_{i}|^{2}}

(2.3)

and

V_{i,\eta}\triangleq\big\{x:C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}>w_{i}\cdot x+\eta>C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}-c\epsilon\big\},i=1,2\dots k.

(2.4)

where
C^{\prime}
is some universal constant defined by Theorem 5.1 in
[
4
]
.

Proof:
There is an orthogonal matrix
U
such that
Uw_{i}=|w_{i}|(0,\dots,0,1)^{T}
, then

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbf{E}_{x\sim N(0,I_{d}),\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\big[Ux\mid x\in V_{i,\eta}\big]-Uv_{i}\bigg|

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbf{E}_{y\sim N(0,I_{d}),\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\big[y\mid y_{d}\in V_{d,i,\eta}\big]-\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}|w_{i}|}(0,\dots,0,1)^{T}\bigg|,

where
Ux=y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{d})^{T}
and

V_{d,i,\eta}=\{y_{d}:C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}>|w_{i}|\cdot y_{d}+\eta>C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}-c\epsilon\}.

(2.6)

Since

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbf{E}_{y\sim N(0,I_{d}),\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\big[y\mid y_{d}\in V_{d,i,\eta}\big]

(2.7)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\bigg(\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}|w_{i}|}(0,\dots,0,1)^{T}-(0,\dots,0,\eta)^{T}/|w_{i}|\bigg)\bigg|\leq\mathcal{O}(c\epsilon/|w_{i}|),

and
\tau_{\sigma}
is the zero-mean distribution, we finish the proof.

\Box

For each
i
, let us consider
d
-dimensional random variables

\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d}),x\in V_{i,\eta},\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}\}.

(2.8)

Denote
\mathcal{D}_{i}
the conditional distribution of the above
d
-dimensional random variables, and

\mu_{i}\triangleq\mathbf{E}_{x\sim N(0,I_{d}),\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}\big[x\mid x\in V_{i,\eta}\big]=\mathbf{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}x

(2.9)

is the expectation of
\mathcal{D}_{i}
. Lemma
2.1
shows that the conditional expectation
\mu_{i}
is close to the weights (multiply by a factor) we need to recover. What we want to do is to use the result in
[
4
]
to cluster the data from the mixed distribution
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}
and extract the mean
\mu_{i}
(or
v_{i}
). According to Definition 3.2 in
[
4
]
, we will prove that the mixed distribution
\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\mathcal{D}_{i}
is a
\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon))
-explicitly bounded mixture model with separation
\mathcal{O}(\frac{(1+\sigma)\sqrt{\log(1/\epsilon)}}{c})
. The following estimation shows that the distance between different vectors
v_{i}
is greater than
\mathcal{O}(\frac{(1+\sigma)\sqrt{\log(1/\epsilon)}}{c})
which meets the separation condition.

Lemma 2.2

Suppose
0<\epsilon<1/k
,
|w_{j}-w_{i}|\geq c
and
1\geq|w_{j}|\geq|w_{i}|\geq c
, we have

|v_{i}-v_{j}|\geq\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c}.

(2.10)

Proof:

Let
w_{j}=aw_{i}+by
with
y\perp w_{i},|y|=1
. From
|w_{j}|\geq|w_{i}|
and
|w_{j}-w_{i}|\geq c
, we have

(a^{2}-1)|w_{i}|^{2}+b^{2}\geq 0

(2.11)

and

|1-a|^{2}|w_{i}|^{2}+|b|^{2}\geq c^{2}.

(2.12)

Thus,

\displaystyle|v_{i}-v_{j}|

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}|w_{i}|^{2}|w_{j}|^{2}}\big||w_{j}|^{2}w_{i}-\left|w_{i}\right|^{2}w_{j}\big|

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}\left|w_{i}\right|^{2}\left|w_{j}\right|^{2}}\big|[(a^{2}-1)|w_{i}|^{2}+b^{2}+(1-a)|w_{i}|^{2}]w_{i}-|w_{i}|^{2}by\big|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}\left|w_{i}\right|^{2}\left|w_{j}\right|^{2}}\big|[(1-a)|w_{i}|^{2}]w_{i}-|w_{i}|^{2}by\big|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)c(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}}{c^{2}\left|w_{j}\right|^{2}}\geq\frac{C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)\sqrt{\log(1/\epsilon)}}{c}.

\Box

Since
C^{{}^{\prime}}
is a universal constant, we will omit the constant related to it in the later discussion. Next, we need to prove that
\mathcal{D}_{i}
has some concentration property.

Lemma 2.3

Let
0<\epsilon<1/k
, for each
i
,
\mathcal{D}_{i}
is
\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon))
-explicitly bounded with variance proxy
\mathcal{O}((\frac{1+\sigma}{c})^{2})
(see Definition 3.1 in
[
4
]
), namely, for every even
4\leq s\leq O(\log(1/\epsilon))
,
the polynomial

p(u)=(C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(1+\sigma)^{2}s/c^{2})^{s/2}\|u\|^{s}-\mathbf{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\langle(x-\mu_{i}),u\rangle^{s}

(2.14)

should be a sum-of-squares of degree
s
, where
C^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
is some universal constant.

Proof:

Without loss of generality, let us assume
w_{i}=|w_{i}|(0,\dots,0,1)^{T}
,
\ |w_{i}|\geq c,
then Equ. (
2.8
) can be written as follows

\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}):x_{d}\in V_{d,i,\eta},\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma},x_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),j=1,2,\dots,d\},

(2.15)

where

V_{d,i,\eta}=\{x_{d}:C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}>|w_{i}|\cdot x_{d}+\eta>C^{\prime}(1+\sigma)(\log(1/\epsilon))^{1/2}/c^{2}-c\epsilon\}.

(2.16)

Let
\mu_{d,i}:=\mathbf{E}_{x_{d}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),x_{d}\in V_{d,i,\eta}}x_{d}
.
For
4\leq s\leq\mathcal{O}(\log(1/\epsilon))
and
1\leq j\leq d-1
, we have

\mathbf{E}_{x_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}|x_{j}|^{s}\leq(s/2)^{s/2},j=1,2,\dots,d-1

(2.17)

and

\mathbf{E}_{x_{d}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),x_{d}\in V_{d,i,\eta}}|x_{d}-\mu_{d,i}|^{s}\leq(1+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon))^{s}\max_{4\leq l\leq s}\mathbf{E}_{\eta\sim\tau_{\sigma}}|\eta/|w_{i}||^{l}\leq\mathcal{O}(((1+\sigma)/c)^{s}(s/2)^{s/2}).

(2.18)

Combining Equ.(
2.17
) and Equ.(
2.18
), we finish the proof by using Lemma 3.5 in
[
4
]
.

2.1
, Lemma
2.2

conforms the conditions in Theorem 5.1 in

.

3
Conclusion

We connect mixed linear regression and Gaussian clustering through a simple truncation method, resulting in a quasi-polynomial time algorithm that is both computationally efficient and robust under some mild separation condition. Since the algorithms for Gaussian clustering
[
4
,
10
,
6
]
can be used for more general distribution, it is worth further discussion to design an efficient and stable mixed linear regression algorithms under the wider distribution setting.

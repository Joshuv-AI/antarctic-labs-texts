Cartan subalgebras in W ∗ -algebras
Jean Renault

Abstract
. This article presents a proof of the Feldman-Moore theorem on Cartan subalgebras in W*-algebras based on the non-commutative Stone equivalence between Boolean inverse semigroups and Boolean groupoids. The proof is decomposed into two parts. The first part writes the W*-algebra as the W*-algebra of the Weyl twist of the Cartan subalgebra. The second part is an extension to separable measure inverse semigroups of the realization of a separable measure algebra by a standard measure space.

Introduction

In the theory of operator algebras, a Cartan subalgebra is a maximal abelian self-adjoint subalgebra with additional properties (Definition
2.18
). The main point of
[
33
,
48
]
is that there is a canonical twisted groupoid attached to a Cartan pair of C*-algebras and that the Cartan pair constructed from this twisted groupoid is isomorphic to the initial Cartan pair. Not surprisingly, a similar result holds for Cartan pairs of W*-algebras. In fact, the study of W*-Cartan pairs
[
22
]
preceded and motivated the study of C*-Cartan pairs; moreover, it is easier, because it suffices to consider the partial isometries which normalize the subalgebra rather than arbitrary normalizers. Since the partial isometry normalizers form an inverse semigroup, it is natural to appeal to the theory of inverse semigroups to study the Cartan pair. This is the road followed by A. Donsig, A. Fuller and D. Pitts in
[
16
]
. The close kinship of inverse semigroups and groupoids has been known for a long time and under various forms (see in particular
[
46
,
42
,
52
]
). One of its most basic expressions is that the bisections of a groupoid form an inverse semigroup. One can ask for a converse as in
[
46
, Remark III.2.4]
. This question is studied in
[
41
]
and
[
42
, Section 4.3]
. It turns out that it has a very satisfactory answer, given by M. Lawson in
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
and in his work with D. Lenz
[
39
]
, for a particular class of inverse semigroups, namely the Boolean inverse semigroups (Definition
A.2
). Indeed the category of Boolean inverse semigroups is equivalent to the category of Boolean groupoids (Theorem
A.10
), where a Boolean groupoid is an étale groupoid whose unit space is Boolean (these groupoids are called ample in
[
42
]
). Lawson’s beautiful idea is that Boolean inverse semigroups generalize Boolean algebras, Boolean groupoids generalize Boolean spaces and that Stone’s duality can be extended in this new framework. However, in order to establish a non-commutative Stone duality, Lawson and Lenz had to restrict the class of objects or arrows. The problem is the right definition of the arrows in the category of groupoids. Motivated by the theory of C*-algebras, groupoid correspondences (see
[
49
, Section 2]
) are good candidates but invertible correspondences are groupoid equivalences and not usual isomorphisms. In the present case of Boolean groupoids, the suitable arrows are the multiplier actions, which are a particular case of groupoid correspondences. We give their definition in the Appendix A and refer the reader to the recent preprint
[
53
]
for further information on this notion which appears there under the name of actor. The functors Bis and Germ establishing the equivalence of category are covariant and give an equivalence rather than a dual equivalence. The results of Appendix A are essentially contained in
[
11
]
. Since a W*-Cartan pair gives a twisted Boolean inverse semigroup, the equivalence has to be extended to the twisted categories. The main result of Section 2 (Theorem
2.22
) is a simple transposition of
[
48
, Theorem 5.6]
(see also
[
45
, Theorem 1.2]
). Its final step is even easier since it is the identification of two GNS representations. Comparing with the inverse semigroup road followed in
[
16
]
, one can see the advantage of introducing groupoids and their convolution algebras.

Stone’s duality can be split into three levels: firstly Boolean algebras and Boolean spaces, secondly complete Boolean algebras and Stonian spaces, and thirdly measure algebras and measure spaces. The same holds for the non-commutative Stone equivalence; moreover each level corresponds to a class of operator algebras: firstly Boolean inverse semigroups, Boolean groupoids and C*-algebras, secondly complete Boolean inverse semigroups, Stonian groupoids and AW*-algebras, and thirdly measure inverse semigroups, measure Boolean groupoids and W*-algebras. We are interested in the third level. It is different: strictly speaking, the dual of a measure algebra is a hyperstonian space (
[
15
, Définition 3]
). However the natural objects of measure theory are standard measure spaces and not hyperstonian spaces. When the measure algebra is separable (see for example
[
27
, Section 40]
), it can be realized as the measure algebra of a standard measure space. We show that a similar result holds for measure Boolean inverse semigroups (Theorem
3.8
): if the W*-algebra
W^{*}(G)
of a measure Stonian groupoid
G
has a separable predual, then
G
contains (in the sense of the category of Boolean groupoids) a measure Boolean groupoid
H
which is second countable such that the pairs
(W^{*}(G),L^{\infty}(G^{(0)}))
and
(W^{*}(H),L^{\infty}(H^{(0)}))
are isomorphic, where we have omitted the measures and the possible twists for the sake of legibility. This step is also a crucial element of the original proof of Feldman and Moore (
[
22
, paragraph before Proposition 3.2]
). When we apply this result to the twisted measure Stonian groupoid associated with a Cartan pair, we recover the Feldman-Moore theorem since in this case,
H
admits a reduction to a Borel set of full measure which is an equivalence relation with countable classes.

Here is a brief summary of the content of the paper. Besides this introduction, the paper has three sections. The first section gives the definitions of (twisted) Boolean inverse semigroups and of (twisted) Boolean groupoids, presents briefly the non-commutative Stone equivalence, recalls the construction of the convolution algebra of a twisted étale groupoid, and shows that the full C*-algebra of a twisted Boolean groupoid is universal for the representations of its twisted ample semigroup (Theorem
1.12
). The second section recalls the definition of a Cartan subalgebra both in the C*-algebra and in the W*-algebra contexts. While the study of
[
48
]
is restricted to Hausdorff groupoids, it is completed here by some results valid for non-Hausdorff Boolean groupoids. However, Proposition
2.10
, inspired by
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
and Corollary
2.8
give a severe limitation to a direct extension of
[
48
, Theorem 5.9]
to the non-Hausdorff case. The main result (Theorem
2.22
) of the section is the realization of a W*-Cartan pair as the W*-Cartan pair of its twist. The third section studies briefly measure inverse semigroups and shows that, under a separability assumption, a Stonian measure groupoid can be replaced by a second countable Boolean measure groupoid without changing its W*-algebra (Theorem
3.8
). The Feldman-Moore theorem is given as a corollary (Corollary
3.12
). Two appendices complete the paper for the convenience of the reader. Appendix A establishes the equivalence of the category of Boolean inverse semigroup and of the category of Boolean groupoids, first in the untwisted case (Theorem
A.10
) and then its twisted version (Theorem
A.22
). These constructions and properties, which are well known in the general setting of étale groupoids and inverse semigroups (see for example
[
18
,
12
,
11
]
), take a quite satisfying form in the Boolean case. Appendix B establishes the bijection between the representations of a twisted Boolean inverse semigroup
(\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{S}})
and the representations of the convolution algebra
C_{c}(G,\Sigma)
of its twisted groupoid of germs (Theorem
B.7
). This is a straightforward result which exists in the literature under various forms (often with extraneous assumptions).

The notation about groupoids and inverse semigroups follows essentially that of

1.
Twisted Boolean inverse semigroups and groupoids

1.1.
Boolean inverse semigroups and Boolean groupoids

Definitions and references about inverse semigroups are recalled in Appendix A. We denote by
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
the set of idempotents of the inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
. It is a meet-semilattice for the order
e\leq f
if and only if
ef=e
. One extends this order to
\mathcal{G}
by defining
s\leq t
if and only if there exists
e\in\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
such that
s=te
. Assuming that
\mathcal{G}
has a zero element, we say that a family
(s_{i})
of elements of
\mathcal{G}
is orthogonal if for all
i\not=j
,
s^{*}_{i}s_{j}=s_{i}s^{*}_{j}=0
. The following notion, which plays a crucial role in the present work, is anticipated in
[
46
, Remark III.2.4]
. The related notion of additive inverse semigroup is predominant in
[
42
]
. The terminology of Boolean inverse semigroup is introduced in
[
35
]
, where it is shown that Boolean inverse semigroups are a natural and far-reaching generalization of Boolean algebras. Although various definitions of a Boolean inverse semigroup appear in the literature (see
[
54
, Section 3.1]
), we stick to our original notion because it is the most appropriate in this work.

Definition 1.1
.

An inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
is called
Boolean
if

(i)

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a Boolean algebra;

(ii)

every orthogonal pair
(s,t)
in
\mathcal{G}
has a join
s\vee t
in
\mathcal{G}
.

Although we are mostly concerned with the case when
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
has a unit, we do not make this assumption here. Thus
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a generalized Boolean algebra or a Boolean ring in the usual terminology. We choose this definition to match the corresponding definition for C*-algebras.

Definition 1.2
.

A topological space is called
Boolean
if it is locally compact Hausdorff and totally disconnected.

Definition 1.3
.

A
Boolean groupoid
is an étale groupoid
G
whose unit space
G^{(0)}
is a Boolean space.

A Boolean groupoid is called an ample groupoid by Paterson in
[
42
]
. We shall not use his terminology.

The non-commutative Stone equivalence, proved in Appendix A, is the equivalence of the category of Boolean inverse semigroups, where the arrows are the Boolean inverse semigroup morphisms and the category of Boolean groupoids, where the arrows are the multiplier actions with proper anchor. One associates to a Boolean inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
its groupoid of germs
{\rm Germ}(\mathcal{G})
and to a Boolean groupoid
G
the inverse semigroup of its compact open bisections
{\rm Bis}(G)
. We recall these constructions. The next proposition is proved in Appendix A. The terminology follows
[
32
]
and
[
46
, Definition I.2.10]
.

Proposition A.1.

Endowed with the set-theoretical product, the set of compact open bisections of a Boolean groupoid
G
is a Boolean inverse semigroup, called the ample inverse semigroup of
G
and denoted by
{\rm Bis}(G)
.

Whenever an inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
acts on a topological space

by partial homeomorphisms, one can define the groupoid of germs

of this action (see Paterson
[
42

or Exel
[

, Proposition 4.17]
). Explicitly

, where

is the set of pairs

where
s\in\mathcal{G}
and
x\in{\rm dom}(s)
and
(s,x)\sim(t,y)
if and only if

and there exists

having

in its domain and such that
se=te
. We denote by

the equivalence class of

and

is a Boolean space,

is a Boolean groupoid. We apply this construction to the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
by conjugation:

. If

is the Boolean algebra of compact open sets of the Boolean space

Definition 1.4
.

The
groupoid of germs

{\rm Germ}(\mathcal{G})
of a Boolean inverse semigroup

of the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}

Remark 1.5
.

Our groupoid of germs

is not Paterson’s universal groupoid of

, which is the groupoid of germs of the action of

1.2.
Twists

The non-commutative Stone equivalence extends to twisted Boolean inverse semigroups and twisted Boolean groupoids. The details can be found in Appendix A.

Definition 1.6
.

[
33
, §2]
A
twist
over a groupoid
G
is a central groupoid extension

X\times{\mathbf{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrowtail}}\Sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}G

where
{\mathbf{T}}
is the group of complex numbers of modulus one (by definition, the groupoids of the extension have the common unit space
X
). Then, we say that
(G,\Sigma)
is a
twisted groupoid
.

When
G
is a locally compact groupoid, we implicitly assume that
\Sigma
is a locally compact groupoid, that
X\times{\mathbf{T}}
is a locally closed subset of
\Sigma
and that

is continuous and open. We then say that
(G,\Sigma)
is a locally compact twisted groupoid. If
G
is Boolean, we say that
(G,\Sigma)
is a Boolean twisted groupoid. The notion of open bisection is not suitable for
\Sigma
which is not étale, We replace it by the notion of continuous bisection (Definition
A.13
). It can be checked that the two notions agree when
G
is étale. The set of compact continuous bisections of
\Sigma
is a Boolean inverse semigroup, which we still call the
ample semigroup
of
\Sigma
and denote by
{\rm Bis}(\Sigma)
.

In order to define a twist over a Boolean inverse semigroup, we need to interpret the term of “central extension”. Given a Boolean space

, we denote by
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
the set of continuous functions
h:{\rm supp}(h)\to{\mathbf{T}}
, where
{\rm supp}(h)
is a compact open subset of

is the intersection of the supports of

and

. Note that

is nothing but the ample semigroup

of the trivial group bundle

. It is called the trivial Clifford inverse semigroup over
X
. Every (partial) isomorphism of the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{B}(X)
of compact open subsets of
X
has a trivial extension to a (partial) isomorphism of
{\mathcal{T}}(X)
.

Definition 1.7
.

A
twist
over a Boolean inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
is an inverse semigroup extension

{\mathcal{T}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrowtail}}{\mathcal{S}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}

where
{\mathcal{T}}
is the trivial Clifford inverse semigroup over the dual space of
{\mathcal{G}}^{(0)}
and the action of
{\mathcal{S}}
on
\mathcal{T}
by conjugation is the trivial extension of its action on
{\mathcal{G}}^{(0)}
. Then, we say that
(\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{S}})
is a
twisted Boolean inverse semigroup
.

The proof of the next proposition is given in Appendix A.

Proposition
A.20

Let
(G,\Sigma)

In order to show that every twisted Boolean inverse semigroup

if and only if

x=y\quad{\rm and}\quad\exists\,a,b\in{\mathcal{T}}(X):a(x)=b(x)=1\quad{\rm and}\quad sa=tb

The equivalence class of

for

[resp. by
[[s,x]]

1.3.
Convolution algebras

The construction of the convolution algebra
C_{c}(G,\Sigma)
of a twisted Boolean groupoid
(G,\Sigma)
is a particular case of a construction valid for every twisted locally compact groupoid with a Haar system
\lambda
, which can be found in many places, in particular in
[
50
, Section 1]
. Since
G
is étale,
\lambda^{x}
and
\lambda_{x}
are respectively the counting measures on
G^{x}=r^{-1}(x)
and on
G_{x}=d^{-1}(x)
. When
G
is Hausdorff, the elements of
C_{c}(G,\Sigma)
are compactly supported continuous functions
f:\Sigma\to{\bf C}
which are homogeneous in the sense that
f(\theta\sigma)=\overline{\theta}f(\sigma)
for all
(\theta,\sigma)\in{\mathbf{T}}\times\Sigma
. When
G
is not Hausdorff, they are finite sums of homogeneous compactly supported continuous functions functions defined on
{\mathbf{T}}
-invariant open Hausdorff subsets
U
of
\Sigma
and extended by 0 outside
U
. In all cases, they are finite sums of functions
f=\Delta_{S}(b\circ s)
, where
S
is a compact continuous bisection of
\Sigma
,
b\in C_{c}(G^{(0)})
and
\Delta_{S}(\sigma)=\overline{\theta}
if
\sigma=\theta(Ss(\sigma))
, where
\theta\in{\mathbf{T}}
and 0 if
\sigma
does not belong to
{\mathbf{T}}S
. The convolution product and the involution are respectively given by

f*g(\sigma)=\int_{G}f(\tau)g(\tau^{-1}\sigma)d\lambda^{r(\sigma)}(\tau^{\prime}),\qquad f^{*}(\sigma)=\overline{f(\sigma^{-1})}

where
\tau^{\prime}=p(\tau)
is the image in
G
of
\tau\in\Sigma
. We warn the reader of an abuse of notation in the integral: the function
\tau\mapsto f(\tau)g(\tau^{-1}\sigma)
should be replaced by the function it defines on the quotient
G=\Sigma/{\mathbf{T}}
. We can view

either as a multiplier of
C_{c}(G,\Sigma)
by defining
fb(\sigma)=f(\sigma)b(d(\sigma))
or as an element of
C_{c}(G,\Sigma)
by defining
\tilde{b}(x,\theta)=\overline{\theta}b(x)
and
\tilde{b}(\sigma)=0
if
\sigma
does not belong to
X\times{\mathbf{T}}
. A similar construction defines the Steinberg algebra
A_{R}(G,\Sigma)
of
(G,\Sigma)
, where

is a ring (see
[
52
, Section 3]
and
[
3
,
4
]
for the twisted version).

Let

The main interest of the abstract realization of a Cartan pair given in the previous section, besides being intrinsic, is to reduce the study of a W*-Cartan pair to that of a twisted measure Boolean inverse semigroup (or groupoid). Appendix A
Non-commutative Stone equivalence

Classical Stone duality between Boolean algebras and Boolean spaces admits a generalization to an equivalence between the category of Boolean inverse semigroups and the category of Boolean groupoids. The arrows of the first category are the Boolean inverse semigroup morphisms. The arrows of the second category are the multiplier actions with proper anchor, defined below. The equivalence will be extended to the twisted categories. We refer the reader to
[
34
]
for the theory of inverse semigroups and to
[
46
]
or
[
42
]
for the theory of locally compact groupoids. We recall some basic definitions and properties.

Definition A.1
.

An
inverse semigroup

\mathcal{G}
is a semigroup such that for each

there
exists a unique
t\in\mathcal{G}
such that

and

. One calls

the

and writes

. One denotes

the set of idempotents.

Note that idempotents

are necessarily self-adjoint:

. We write

and

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. The relation

if

for some

is an order relation on
\mathcal{G}
. The poset

is a meet semilattice with

. Assuming that
\mathcal{G}
has a zero element, we say that a family

of elements of

is orthogonal if for all

.

An inverse semigroup

is called

(i)

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
is a Boolean algebra;

(ii)

every orthogonal pair
(s,t)
in
\mathcal{G}
has a join

(also written

) in
\mathcal{G}
.

When

is a Boolean inverse semigroup, we implicitly assume that

is the Boolean algebra

The

An

The definition of the arrows of the category of Boolean groupoids requires some familiar notions of the theory of locally compact groupoids which we recall from

Theorem A.10
.

The constructions of the groupoid of germs of a Boolean inverse semigroup and of the ample semigroup of a Boolean groupoid are functorial and give an equivalence of the category of Boolean inverse semigroups and the category of Boolean groupoids.

Proof.

We recall that
{\rm Germ}(\mathcal{G})
denotes the groupoid of germs of a Boolean inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
and
{\rm Bis}(G)
the inverse semigroup of compact open bisections of a Boolean groupoid
G
. We first show that
{\rm Bis}
and
{\rm Germ}
are functors. Suppose that
H
acts on
G
with proper anchor
\rho^{(0)}
. Let
S
be a compact open bisection of
H

j(S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}SG^{(0)}=\{\eta x:\eta\in S,x\in G^{(0)},\,\rho^{(0)}(x)=d(\eta)\}.

Since, necessarily
\eta=S\rho^{(0)}(x)
,
j(S)
is a section for the domain map of

(\eta x)^{-1}=\eta^{-1}(\eta.x)
shows that
j(S)^{-1}=j(S^{-1})
. Therefore,
j(S)
is also a section for the range map of
G
. Since
j(S)
is the image of the compact open set
S*(\rho^{(0)})^{-1}(s(S))
by the action map

which is continuous and open (according to
[
53

), it is compact and open. Thus

is a map from
{\rm Bis}(H)
to
{\rm Bis}(G)

j(ST)=STG^{(0)}=Sj(T)=SG^{(0)}j(T)=j(S)j(T)
. Therefore,

is an inverse semigroup homomorphism from
{\rm Bis}(H)
to
{\rm Bis}(G)
. Its restriction to the idempotents, which is
(\rho^{(0)})^{-1}
, is a Boolean algebra homomorphism. Let
j
be a homomorphism from a Boolean inverse semigroup
{\mathcal{H}}
into a Boolean inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
. Let
H
and
G
the respective groupoids of germs. The Boolean algebra homomorphism
j^{(0)}
gives a continuous and proper map
\rho^{(0)}:G^{(0)}\to H^{(0)}
. Then we define a multiplier action of
H
on

with proper anchor

by

[s,\rho^{(0)}\circ\varphi_{t}(x)][t,x]=[j(s)t,x]

for

,

and

, where we have used the notation given before Definition
1.4
. The algebraic properties of a multiplier actions are easily verified. Since the restriction of the action map to the product of the open bisections

and

is clearly continuous, so is the action map. We skip the easy but tedious task to check that these constructions respect the composition of arrows.

Let us show that the Boolean groupoid

is isomorphic to the groupoid of germs

of its ample semigroup

. The map

to
Sx
, where

and

, is well defined: if

, then
Sx=Tx
. The map is injective: if
Sx=Ty
, then

. Then

is an open neighborhood of

. If

contained in

, we have

, hence

. The map is surjective: given

, there exists a compact open bisection

containing
\gamma
. Then
\gamma=Sd(\gamma)=f([S,d(\gamma)])
. The definition of the product in

, namely

makes clear that

is a groupoid homomorphism. If

is a compact open bisection of

is a basic open set in
{\rm Germ}(\mathcal{G})
. Therefore,

is a homeomorphism. One deduces that

is an isomorphism of topological groupoids.

Let us show that the ample semigroup of the groupoid of germs
G={\rm Germ}(\mathcal{G})
of a Boolean inverse semigroup
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}
. We recall that for all

is a bisection of

; an element of the second set is of the form

where

can be written

and

, the two sets agree. If

,

is the compact open subset
{\rm dom}(e)
of

corresponding to

. Let us show that the map

is injective. Assume that

. First, this implies that

. For all

\displaystyle e_{i}\leq e_{i}\vee e_{j}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow se_{i}\leq s(e_{i}\vee e_{j})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow se_{i}\vee se_{j}\leq s(e_{i}\vee e_{j})

With

\displaystyle se_{i},se_{j}\leq t

\displaystyle\Rightarrow e_{i},e_{j}\leq s^{*}t

\displaystyle\Rightarrow e_{i}\vee e_{j}\leq s^{*}t

\displaystyle\Rightarrow s(e_{i}\vee e_{j})\leq ss^{*}t\leq t

Let us show that the map

Theorem A.22
.

The constructions of the groupoid of germs of a Boolean inverse semigroup and of the ample semigroup of a Boolean groupoid are functorial and give an equivalence of the category of twisted Boolean inverse semigroups and the category of twisted Boolean groupoids.

Proof.

Let
(H,\Lambda)
and
(G,\Sigma)
be twisted Boolean groupoids and suppose that there is a
{\mathbf{T}}
-equivariant multiplier action of

to
({\rm Bis}(G),{\rm Bis}(\Sigma))

Let

to

The functors Bis and Germ give an equivalence of categories. We have already seen that the twisted Boolean inverse semigroup

\Sigma

Definition B.1

(v)

\pi

\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}

As expected, representations of

A representation

and

Definition B.2
.

We say that a finite family
({T_{j}}^{\prime})_{j\in J}
of bisections of a groupoid

({S_{i}}^{\prime})_{i\in I}

{S_{i}}^{\prime}
is a disjoint union
\vee_{j\in J(i)}{T_{j}}^{\prime}
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
({S_{i}}^{\prime})_{i\in I}
be a finite family of bisections of a groupoid

. We define

(i)

\omega(\gamma)=\{i\in I:\gamma\in{S_{i}}^{\prime}\}

(ii)

\Omega=\{\omega(\gamma):\gamma\in G\}\setminus\emptyset

(iii)

\omega\in\Omega

{T_{\omega}}^{\prime}=\{\gamma\in G:\omega(\gamma)=\omega\}

Then

is a disjoint family of bisections which refines the family
({S_{i}}^{\prime})_{i\in I}
. Moreover, if

is Boolean and the

Left to the reader.
Note that

. Therefore

is a locally closed subset of the open set

∎

Lemma B.4
.

Let
(G,\Sigma)
be a twisted Boolean groupoid with unit space

. Then
every

, where
({T_{j}}^{\prime})_{j\in J}
is a disjoint family in

and

. Moreover, if

has another expression

and

agree on

.

Let

be an arbitrary expression of

. We apply Lemma

to the finite family
({S_{i}}^{\prime})_{i\in I}
. We obtain the finite disjoint family

in

. We pick for each

. Then, we can write

∎

Lemma B.5
.

Let
(G,\Sigma)
be a twisted Boolean groupoid. Let
f=\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_{S_{i}}b_{i}=\sum_{j\in J}\Delta_{T_{j}}c_{j}
be two expressions of
f\in U(G,\Sigma)
. Assume that the family
({T_{j}}^{\prime})_{j\in J}
of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime\prime}
is disjoint and refines the family
({S_{i}}^{\prime})_{i\in I}
. Then we have the equality

\sum_{i\in I}\pi(S_{i})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(b_{i})=\sum_{j\in J}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(c_{j})

for every representation
\pi
of
(G,\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be the kernel of the quotient map
p:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathcal{G}
of the twisted ample semigroup of
(G,\Sigma)
. By assumption, for every
i\in I
, there exists
J(i)\subset J
such that

{S_{i}}^{\prime}=\vee_{j\in J(i)}{T_{j}}^{\prime}
. Then
p(S_{i}d(T_{j}))=p(T_{j})
. Therefore, there exist

such that
S_{i}=\vee_{j\in J(i)}T_{j}t_{(i,j)}
. This implies that
\Delta_{S_{i}}=\sum_{j\in J(i)}\Delta_{T_{j}}t_{(i,j)}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_{S_{i}}b_{i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\big(\sum_{j\in J(i)}\Delta_{T_{j}}t_{(i,j)}\big)b_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in K}\Delta_{T_{j}}t_{(i,j)}b_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\Delta_{T_{j}}\big(\sum_{i:j\in J(i)}t_{(i,j)}b_{i}\big).

Since the family

is disjoint, the uniqueness in Lemma

says that

and

agree on

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\pi(S_{i})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(b_{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\big(\sum_{j\in J(i)}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(t_{(i,j)})\big)\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(b_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in K}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(t_{(i,j)})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(b_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in K}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(t_{(i,j)}b_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(\sum_{i:j\in J(i)}t_{(i,j)}b_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\pi(T_{j})\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(c_{j}).

Theorem B.7
.

Let

be a twisted Boolean inverse semigroup and let

be its groupoid of germs. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the representations

We identify
{\mathcal{S}}={\rm Bis}(\Sigma)
and
\mathcal{G}={\rm Bis}(G)
. Let
\tilde{\pi}
be a representation of

. According to Lemma

defines a representation of the twisted Boolean inverse semigroup
(\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{S}})
. Conversely, let
\pi
be a representation of the twisted Boolean inverse semigroup
(\mathcal{G},{\mathcal{S}})
. We extend
\pi^{(0)}
to

(and to its bidual

), where

is the spectrum of the Boolean algebra
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}
. Every

can be written under the form

where
S_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
b_{i}\in C_{c}(d(S_{i}))

B.6

results from the equality

\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(b)\pi(T)=\pi(T)\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(\alpha_{T^{-1}}(b))

which suffices to check when

\pi(T)\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(\alpha_{T^{-1}}(e))=\pi(T)\pi(T^{-1}eT)=\pi(e)\pi(T)=\tilde{\pi}^{(0)}(e)\pi(T).

The equality

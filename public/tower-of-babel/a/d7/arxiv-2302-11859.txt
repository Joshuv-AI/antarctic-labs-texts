On the product of two 1 1 - q q -summable series Thanks: This project has received funding from the ANR de rerum natura ANR-19-CE40-0018.
Thomas Dreyfus, Changgui Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper we consider a q q -analog of the Borel-Laplace summation process defined by Marotte and the second author, and consider two series solutions of linear q q -difference equations with slopes 0 0 and 1 1 . The latter are q q -summable and we prove that the product of the series is q q -(multi)summable and its q q -sum is the product of the q q -sum of the two series. This is a first step in showing the conjecture that the q q -summation process is a morphism of rings. We prove that the q q -summation does induce a morphism of fields by showing that if the inverse of the q q -Euler series is q q -summable, then its q q -sum is not the inverse of the q q -sum of the q q -Euler series.

Consider a meromorphic linear differential equation. We have the coexistence of divergent formal power series and integral solutions. For instance, the Euler equation
x^{2}\partial_{x}y+y=1
admits the Euler series
f:=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}n!x^{n}
as formal power series solution. On the other hand, there are integral solutions, such as

\mathcal{S}^{d}(f)(x):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{e^{-\xi/x}}{1+\xi}d\xi.

The path of integration has to be understood as the half line in
{\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers of argument
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The latter integral is well defined when the path of integration does not pass through the pole

, that is when
d\not\equiv\pi[2\pi]
. We may prove that the function is analytic on the sector
\arg(x)\in(d-\pi/2,d+\pi/2)
and is asymptotic to
f
is a certain sens.
More generally, given a formal power series solution of a linear differential equation with coefficients that are germs of meromorphic functions at
0

, construct an integral solution using Borel and Laplace transformations in direction
d
. The map

\mathcal{S}^{d}(\partial_{x}f)=\partial_{x}\mathcal{S}^{d}(f)

We may prove that the latter is meromorphic on the Riemann surface of the logarithm and defines a multivalued complex function.

1.1.
Formal transformations

As usual, we will denote by
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space of all formal power series in the variable
x

{\mathbb{C}}
. On the lines of what is done in the classic Borel-Laplace summation theory (see, for example,
[
Bal06
]
), we first recall the following couple
(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1})
of formal

-Borel and
q
-Laplace transforms, which are really ismorphisms of
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector spaces:

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{llll}\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}:&{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]&\to&{\mathbb{C}}[[\xi]]\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,x^{n}&\displaystyle\mapsto&\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,q^{-n(n-1)/2}\,\xi^{n}\end{array}

and

(1.2)

\begin{array}[]{llll}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}:&{\mathbb{C}}[[\xi]]&\to&{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,\xi^{n}&\displaystyle\mapsto&\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\,q^{n(n-1)/2}\,x^{n}\,.\end{array}

By direct computations, one can find the following identities for
j
,
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
:

(1.3)

\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\,x^{j}\,\sigma_{q}^{m}=q^{-j(j-1)/2}\xi^{j}\,\sigma_{q}^{m-j}\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1},\quad\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}\,\xi^{j}\,\sigma_{q}^{m}=q^{j(j-1)/2}x^{j}\,\sigma_{q}^{m+j}\,\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}\,.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
be the sub-space of all power series whose radius of convergence is strictly positive.
Set
{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;1}=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}\left({\mathbb{C}}\{\xi\}\right)
the set of

q
-Gevrey series of order one.

By following
[
Ram92
]
, we say that the entire function
\phi
is called to have a
q
-exponential growth of ordre (at most) one at infinity in the following sense: for some (or any)
R>0
, one can find
C

such that, for every
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}
,

|\xi|\geq R\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\phi(\xi)|\leq C\,e^{(\log|A\xi|)^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,.

The ring of such functions is called
{\mathbb{E}}_{q;1}
.

Let us denote by
\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
the Riemann surface of the logarithm.
Given
r\in(0,\infty)
, let us denote by
\partial^{+}\widetilde{D}_{r}
, the path parameterized by
\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathbb{R}}&\rightarrow&\widetilde{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\\
t&\mapsto&rq^{\mathbf{i}t}\end{array}\right.
.
Define the linear maps
{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}^{d}
as follows:

(1.4)

\begin{array}[]{llll}{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}:&{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi\}&\rightarrow&{\mathbb{E}}_{q;1}\\
&f&\mapsto&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}\,\mathbf{i}}\,\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{+}\widetilde{D}_{r}}e^{(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}\xi}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,f(x)\,\frac{dx}{x}\end{array}

and

(1.5)

\begin{array}[]{llll}{\mathcal{L}}_{q;1}^{d}:&{\mathbb{E}}_{q;1}&\rightarrow&{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi\}\\
&\phi&\mapsto&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}}\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}\xi}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,\phi(\xi)\,\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\,.\end{array}

In the above,
\log
denotes the principal branch of the logarithm on the Riemann surface
\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,

is chosen to be smaller than the radius of convergence of

, and

may be arbitrary.

The integrals appeared in (

) and (

) are related with the well-konwn Gauss integral (

) :

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-a(t+b)^{2}}\,dt=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,.

Using suitable changes of variables, it follows from the above that, for all integer

for all

\int_{\partial^{+}\widetilde{D}_{r}}e^{(\log(x/\sqrt{q}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,x^{n}\,\frac{dx}{x}=\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}\,\mathbf{i}\,q^{-n(n-1)/2}

and

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(\log(\sqrt{q}\,\xi))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,\xi^{n}\,\frac{d\xi}{\xi}=\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}\,q^{n(n-1)/2}\,.

Thus, restricting to the convergent power series spaces

and
{\mathbb{E}}_{q;1}

\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}
and

) and (

and

\psi^{*}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}\,\left(a\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)+b\,\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{b,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)-1\right)\,.

In what follows, we will prove (
2.6
) for all
\zeta>0
, and by analytic continuation principle this permits us to get (
2.6
) for all
\zeta\in\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Let
\xi=u^{2}
with
u>0
, write
\sqrt{\xi}=u
, and note that

\phi(\xi,\zeta)=\frac{u}{(a+q^{-1/4}\,u\,\zeta)(bu+q^{-1/4}\,\zeta)}=\frac{\frac{aq^{1/2}}{abq^{1/2}-\zeta^{2}}}{a+q^{-1/4}\zeta\,u}-\frac{\frac{q^{-1/4}\zeta}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}}{ub+q^{-1/4}\zeta}\,.

Define for every convenient
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that the denominator does not vanish on the path of integration:

I_{1}(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{\frac{aq^{1/2}}{abq^{1/2}-\zeta^{2}}}{a+q^{-1/4}\zeta\,u}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(u/q^{1/4})\,\frac{du}{u}

and

I_{2}(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{\frac{q^{-1/4}\zeta}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}}{ub+q^{-1/4}\zeta}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(u/q^{1/4})\,\frac{du}{u}\,.

A straightforward computation shows that

e_{q^{2}}(\xi/q)\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\log(q)}}\,e^{-(\log(\xi))^{2}/(4\log(q))}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\log(q)}}\,e^{-(\log(u))^{2}/(\log(q))}=\frac{1}{2}e_{q^{1/2}}(u/q^{1/4}).

In view of the relation
\displaystyle\frac{d\xi}{\xi}=2\,\frac{du}{u}

one finds that

\psi^{*}(\zeta)=\left(I_{1}(\zeta)-I_{2}(\zeta)\right).

By using appropriate changes of variables, it follows that, for all convenient
\zeta\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that the denominator does not vanish on the path of integration

I_{1}(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{\frac{aq^{1/2}}{abq^{1/2}-\zeta^{2}}}{a+v}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(v/\zeta)\,\frac{dv}{v}=\frac{a}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}{\mathcal{S}}_{q;2}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)

and

I_{2}(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{\frac{1}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}}{1+bv}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(\zeta v/q^{1/2})\,\frac{dv}{v}.

If we put
w=1/v
, we find

I_{2}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{w}{w+b}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(\zeta/wq^{1/2})\,\frac{dw}{w}

and with
e_{q^{1/2}}(x)=e_{q^{1/2}}(q^{-1/2}x^{-1})
, we obtain that the latter is equal to

\frac{1}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\frac{w}{w+b}\,e_{q^{1/2}}(w/\zeta)\,\frac{dw}{w}=\frac{1-b{\mathcal{S}}^{d}_{q;2}(E_{b,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)}{ab-q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}}.

Therefore, one deduces from the above the expression expected in (
2.6
) for
\psi^{*}
.

General case.

It remains to prove the result for
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S
. Recall that
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{\arg(\sqrt{ab})+\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\}
and for all
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S^{\prime}
,
E_{a,q}E_{b,q}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;(1,2)}^{d}
and for all
x\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
,

(2.7)

\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})(x)=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})(x).

If
S=S^{\prime}
there is nothing to prove. Assume that this is not the case. Let
d_{0}\in\{\arg(\sqrt{ab})+\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\}
that does not belong to
S
. Let
d^{\prime}<d_{0}<d^{\prime\prime}
such that
(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})\cap S^{\prime}=\{d_{0}\}
. Then,
(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})\cap S=\varnothing
.
By Example
1.7
, we deduce that for
\star\in\{a,b\}
,
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{\star,q})
is independent of
d\in(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})
.
Then, the right hand side of (
2.7
), seen as a function of
d
, is independent of
d
in
(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})
.
By Proposition
1.4
,
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})
and
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})
belong to
{\mathbb{O}}_{q;1}^{d}
and it follows by definition that for all
d\in(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})
,
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\times\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})\in{\mathbb{O}}_{q;2}^{d}
and we may apply
{\mathcal{B}}_{q;2}
to it. By Proposition
1.4
, and the proof of the theorem in the case
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S^{\prime}
, for all
d\in(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})\setminus\{d_{0}\}

{\mathcal{B}}_{q;2}(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\times\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q}))={\mathcal{B}}_{q;2}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=f_{2}^{d}.

By Lemma
2.3
, for all
d\in(d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime})\setminus\{d_{0}\}

f_{2}^{d}(\zeta)=\frac{-a\,\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)-b\,\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{b,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)}{q^{-1/2}\zeta^{2}-ab}.

Let
f_{2}^{d_{0}}:={\mathcal{B}}_{q;2}(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d_{0}}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d_{0}}(E_{b,q}))
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})

Then

\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d_{0}}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d_{0}}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d_{0}}(E_{b,q})

∎

2.2.
Variant of the Euler series

Consider now the situation where the two series are of the form
E^{[m]}_{a,q}
,
E^{[n]}_{b,q}
where
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
. Recall that we have set
{S:=\{\arg(-a)+2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cup\{\arg(-b)+2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\}}
. More precisely, let us prove the following.

Corollary 2.6
.

Given
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S
,
E^{[m]}_{a,q}E^{[n]}_{b,q}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;(1,2)}^{d}
and
for all
x\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
, we have

\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E^{[n]}_{b,q})(x)=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[n]}_{b,q})(x).

Proof.

Let us fix
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S
. As we can see in Example
1.8
, we may differentiate
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})
with respect to
a
and find
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q})=\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})
. Therefore we may differentiate
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})
with respect to
a
and find
\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})(x)
. If we show that we may differentiate
\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})
with respect to
a
with

\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q})
, we will deduce with Theorem
2.1
that
\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E_{b,q})
. If we proceed similarly with the
b
derivation we will complete the proof. So it suffices to show that we may differentiate
\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})
with respect to
a
with

\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q})
.

By definition, the derivation in
a
commutes with
\hat{{\mathcal{B}}}_{q;1}
so that
\partial_{a}^{m}f_{1}=\partial_{a}^{m}\hat{{\mathcal{B}}}_{q;1}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\hat{{\mathcal{B}}}_{q;1}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q})
. Let us fix a compact
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}e^{\mathbf{i}(d+\pi)}
. Recall, see (
2.3
) , that we have the expression
{f_{1}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\zeta^{2n}\,(abq^{n}-\zeta^{2})}{(ab)^{n}q^{n^{2}}\,(aq^{n}+\zeta)(bq^{n}+\zeta)}}
. Then, there exists
M>0
such that for all
\zeta\in e^{\mathbf{i}d}
, for all
n\geq 0
, for all
a\in K
,
\left|\frac{\zeta^{2n}\,(abq^{n}-\zeta^{2})}{(ab)^{n}q^{n^{2}}\,(aq^{n}+\zeta)(bq^{n}+\zeta)}\right|<\left|\frac{M\zeta^{2n+2}}{b^{n}q^{n^{2}}(bq^{n}+\zeta)}\right|
. Similarly to the proof of
[
Dre15
]
, Proposition 2.13, (3), we find that
\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\frac{M\zeta^{2n+2}}{b^{n}q^{n^{2}}(bq^{n}+\zeta)}\right|\in{\mathbb{E}}_{q;2}^{d}
. Furthermore, we deduce as in Example
1.8
that
\partial_{a}^{m}f_{1}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{q;2}^{d}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}(\partial_{a}^{m}f_{1})=\partial_{a}^{m}{\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}(f_{1})=\partial_{a}^{m}f_{2}^{d}
.
We now use
(
2.4
) to deal with
f_{2}^{d}
. We need to bound
\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})
and
\frac{1}{(q^{\prime-1}\zeta^{2}-ab)}
uniformly in
a
.
Let us begin by
\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})
. As we can see in Example
1.8
, there exists
M>0
such that for all
a\in K
,

\left|\mathcal{S}_{q}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q})\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}}\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\left|e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}\xi}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,\,\frac{d\xi}{M^{m+1}\xi}\right|.

Setting
x=re^{\mathbf{i}t}
we find that the latter expression is bounded by

\frac{e^{t^{2}/(2\log(q))}}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}M^{m+1}}\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty e^{\mathbf{i}d}}\left|e^{-(\log(\frac{r}{\sqrt{q}\xi}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,\,\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\right|.

Now consider
\frac{1}{(q^{\prime-1}\zeta^{2}-ab)}
in the case
d\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus S^{\prime}
. Let
M^{\prime}>0
such that for all
a\in K
, for all

,
\left|(q^{\prime-1}\zeta^{2}-ab)\right|>M^{\prime}
.
Then, we may bound the function

{f_{2}^{d}(\zeta)=\frac{-a\,\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{a,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)-b\,\mathcal{S}_{q;2}^{d}(E_{b,q^{1/2}})(\zeta)}{(q^{\prime-1}\zeta^{2}-ab)}}
uniformly in

by a function where

may be applied. This shows that we may differentiate and
\partial_{a}^{m}{\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}(f_{2}^{d})={\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}(\partial_{a}^{m}f_{2}^{d})
. For the remaining cases

. By Remark

in that case,
f_{2}^{d}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{q;2}^{d}
proving that

is analytic at

and thus may be correctly bounded.
We have proved that

{\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}{\mathcal{L}}_{q;2}^{d}\hat{{\mathcal{B}}}_{q;1}(\partial_{a}^{m}E_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q}).

Then,

and

\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m]}_{a,q}E_{b,q})=\partial_{a}^{m}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E_{a,q}E_{b,q})
. This was the sufficient fact to conclude the proof.
∎

2.3.
Product theorem

Let us now state and prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f
(resp.
g
) be a series solution of a linear
q
difference equation with slopes
0
and
1
. Then,
fg\in\mathcal{MS}_{q}
. More precisely, let
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;1}^{d}
and
g\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;1}^{d}
. Then
f\times g\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;(1,2)}^{d}
and
for all
x\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
,

\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(fg)(x)=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(f)\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(g)(x).

Proof.

By Theorem
1.9
, there exists a decomposition

f=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{i}E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}

such that
f_{0},\dots,f_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
, and
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
m_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The same holds for
g

g=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}g_{j}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q},

with
g_{0},\dots,g_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
, and
b_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
,
n_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then we find

fg=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}f_{i}g_{j}E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;1}^{d}
and
g\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;1}^{d}
.
The singular directions of
f
(resp.
g
) correspond to the directions
\arg(-a_{i})+2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
with
m_{i}\neq 0
(resp.
\arg(-b_{j})+2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
with
n_{j}\neq 0
). Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be the union of the singular directions of
f
and
g
.
By Corollary
2.6
, when
m_{i},n_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,
E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;(1,2)}^{d}
and for
x\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{*}
,
\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q})(x)=\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q})\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q})(x)
.
By Proposition
1.5
, the map
f\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{q}^{d}(f)
is a morphism of
{\mathbb{C}}\{x\}
-modules so that we have
f_{i}g_{j}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}\left(E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\right)=\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}\left(f_{i}g_{j}E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\right)
,

\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(f)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{i}\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}\left(E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}\right)
, and
\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(g)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}g_{j}\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}\left(E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\right)
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(f)\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(g)(x)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}f_{i}g_{j}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}\left(E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\right)\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}\left(f_{i}g_{j}E^{[m_{i}]}_{a_{i},q}E^{[n_{j}]}_{b_{j},q}\right)\\
&=&\mathcal{S}_{q;(1,2)}^{d}(fg),\end{array}

which shows that
fg\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;(1,2)}^{d}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Inverse of the
q
-Euler series

In this section we study the inverse of the
q
-Euler series
E_{1,q}
. More precisely, we prove that if it is
q
-multisumable in the direction
d=0
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{q}^{0}(E_{1,q}^{-1})\neq{\mathcal{S}}_{q}^{0}(E_{1,q})^{-1}
. In particular, this means that we have no hope to define a morphism of fields.

One can observe that

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{2N\pi}(E_{1,q})(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q)}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}\xi e^{2N\pi\mathbf{i}}}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}}{1+\xi}\,\,\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\,.

This implies that

(3.1)

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{2N\pi}(E_{1,q})(x)={\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(xe^{-2N\pi\mathbf{i}})\,.

Furthermore,
applying the residue theorem as in Example
1.7
yields that

(3.2)

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(x)-{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{2\pi}(E_{1,q})(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(q)}}\,\mathbf{i}\,e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,.

Iterating (
3.2
) several times implies that

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(x)-{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{2N\pi}(E_{1,q})(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(q)}}\,\mathbf{i}\,\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}(2k+1)}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,.

Thus, combining this together with (
3.1
) gives the following identity for all
x\in\tilde{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
:

(3.3)

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(x)={\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(xe^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}N})+\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(q)}}\,\mathbf{i}\,\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}(2k+1)}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,.

Furthermore, replacing
x
with
x\,e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}N}
in the above in (
3.3
) implies immediately that

(3.4)

{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(x)={\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(xe^{2\pi\mathbf{i}N})-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(q)}}\,\mathbf{i}\,\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}e^{\pi\mathbf{i}(2k+1)}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\,.

The following lemma, will permit us to control the sums in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(N,r_{0},\lambda)\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and define

f(N;r_{0},\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{\lambda(N-k+r_{0})^{2}}\,.

For all
r_{1}<r_{2}
, one has the existence of
C,C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
\Re(r_{0})\in[r_{1},r_{2}]

(3.5)

C\left|e^{\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}\right|\leq\left|f(N;r_{0},\lambda)\right|\leq C^{\prime}\left|e^{\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}\right|.

Proof.

Let us fix
r_{1}<r_{2}
. Consider
e^{-\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}f(N;r_{0},\lambda)
with
\Re(r_{0})\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
. On has

e^{-\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}f(N;r_{0},\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}e^{\lambda(N-k+r_{0})^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}.

The degree two polynomial
x\mapsto\lambda x(x-2N-2\Re(r_{0}))
has a minimum at
N+\Re(r_{0})
. Therefore, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
\Re(r_{0})\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
, for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}

1+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left|e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}\right|\leq 1+N\max(e^{\lambda(1-2N-2\Re(r_{0}))},e^{(N-1)\lambda(-N-1-2\Re(r_{0}))})\leq C^{\prime}.

Then,
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}
converges, when
N
goes to infinity proving the upper bound.

On has

\left|e^{-\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}f(N;r,\lambda)\right|\geq\left|1-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}\right|\;\right|.

Again,
\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}\right|\leq N\max(e^{\lambda(1-2N-2\Re(r_{0}))},e^{(N-1)\lambda(-N-1-2\Re(r_{0}))})
, proving that there exists
C>0
, such that for all
\Re(r_{0})\in[r_{1},r_{2}]
, such that when
N
is sufficiently big
{\left|1-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}e^{k\lambda(k-2N-2r_{0})}\right|\;\right|\geq C}
. Up to take a smaller
C
we find that for all
{\Re(r_{0})\in[r_{1},r_{2}]}
, for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

C\left|e^{\lambda(N+r_{0})^{2}}\right|\leq\left|f(N;r_{0},\lambda)\right|
.
∎

Let us now use (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) to give an estimate for
{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(x)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have the existence of
C(r),R>0
such that

\sup_{(r,t)\in(0,R)\times{\mathbb{R}}}\left|\frac{1}{{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(re^{\mathbf{i}t})}\right|\leq C(r)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\log(q)}}.

Proof.

First, we will consider the case where
t\to+\infty
. Choose
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
t\displaystyle\in[N,N+1)
, and set

\lambda:=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\log(q)},\qquad r_{0}:=-\frac{\mathbf{i}}{2\pi}\,\log\left(\frac{x\,e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}}}{\sqrt{q}}\right)-N\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\mathbf{i}{\mathbb{R}}\right)\,.

One can notice that,
for any integer
k
:

e^{-(\log(\frac{re^{\mathbf{i}t}}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}(2k+1)}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}=e^{\lambda(N-k+r_{0})^{2}}\,.

By combining (
3.5
) together with (
3.3
), it follows that we have the existence of
C>0
such that for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
for all
t>0

\left|{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(re^{\mathbf{i}t})\right|\geq\left|\left|{\mathcal{S}}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})(re^{\mathbf{i}t}e^{-2\pi\mathbf{i}N})\right|-C\left|\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(q)}}\,\,e^{-(\log(\frac{re^{\mathbf{i}t}}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}\right|\right|.

When
t
goes to
+\infty
,
e^{-(\log(\frac{re^{\mathbf{i}t}}{\sqrt{q}}e^{-\pi\mathbf{i}}))^{2}/(2\log(q))}=O(e^{\frac{t^{2}}{2\log(q)}})
.
Taking the inverse in the latter expression gives the result for
t>0
sufficiently big. Up to take a bigger constant we deduce the result for all
t>0
.

Finally, one can notice the case of
\arg(x)\to-\infty
may be treated in a similar way, using (
3.4
) instead of (
3.3
).
∎

Let us prove now prove that the Borel transformation of a function with a negative
q
-exponential angular growth is still a function with a negative
q
-exponential angular growth.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
s,s^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
. Let
f
be a function meromorphic on the neighborhood of
0
in the Riemann surface of the logarithm. Assume that there exist
C(R),R>0
such that

\sup_{(r,t)\in(0,R)\times{\mathbb{R}}}|f(re^{\mathbf{i}t})|\,<C(R)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2s^{\prime}\log(q)}}\,.

Then,
f\in{\mathbb{O}}_{q;s}^{0}
and we may consider
{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s}^{0}(f)
. Furthermore, there exist
C^{\prime}(R)\in(0,\infty)
such that

\sup_{(r,t)\in(0,R)\times{\mathbb{R}}}|{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s}^{0}(f)(re^{\mathbf{i}t})|\,<C^{\prime}(R)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\widetilde{s}\log(q)}},\,\quad\widetilde{s}=\frac{1}{s+s^{\prime}}.

Proof.

By definition, we may apply
{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s}^{0}
to
f
. Let
g={\mathcal{B}}_{q;s}^{0}(f)
.
We have

g(re^{\mathbf{i}t})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}\,\mathbf{i}}\,\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{+}\widetilde{D}_{r}}e^{\frac{\left(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q^{s}}re^{\mathbf{i}t}})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}\,f(x)\,\frac{dx}{x}.

To simplify, let us write
C
instead of
C(R)
. If we write
x=re^{\mathbf{i}\theta}
, we find
\frac{dx}{x}=\mathbf{i}d\theta
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{l}|g(re^{\mathbf{i}t})|=\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}\,}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\left(\log(\frac{e^{\mathbf{i}\theta}}{\sqrt{q^{s}}e^{\mathbf{i}t}})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}\,f(re^{\mathbf{i}\theta})\,d\theta\right|\\
\leq\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\left(\log(q^{-s/2})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}e^{\frac{\left(\log(e^{\mathbf{i}(\theta-t)})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}e^{\frac{\log(q^{-s/2})\log(e^{\mathbf{i}(\theta-t)})}{s\log(q)}}Ce^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2s^{\prime}\log(q)}}\,d\theta\right|\\
\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{\frac{\left(\log(q^{-s/2})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}e^{-\frac{(\theta-t)^{2}}{2s\log(q)}}\left|\left(q^{-s/2}\right)^{\frac{\mathbf{i}(\theta-t)}{s\log(q)}}\right|Ce^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2s^{\prime}\log(q)}}\,d\theta\\
\leq\frac{Ce^{\frac{\left(\log(q^{-s/2})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{(\theta-t)^{2}}{2s\log(q)}}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2s^{\prime}\log(q)}}\,d\theta\\
\leq\frac{Ce^{\frac{\left(\log(q^{-s/2})\right)^{2}}{2\log(q)s}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2s\log(q)}}e^{\frac{s^{-2}t^{2}}{2(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1})\log(q)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1})\frac{(\theta-s^{-1}t/(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1}))^{2}}{2\log(q)}}\,d\theta\\
\leq\frac{Ce^{(\log(q^{-s/2}))^{2}/2\log(q)s}e^{-\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s^{2}(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1})}\right)\frac{t^{2}}{2\log(q)}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\log(q^{s})}}\,\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1})\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\log(q)}}\,d\theta.\end{array}

We now remark that

\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s^{2}(s^{-1}+s^{\prime-1})}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s+s^{2}/s^{\prime}}=\frac{s+s^{2}/s^{\prime}-s}{s^{2}+s^{3}/s^{\prime}}=\frac{s^{2}/s^{\prime}}{s^{2}+s^{3}/s^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{s+s^{\prime}}
.
This proves the result.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 3.4
.

For all finite strictly increasing sequence
\vec{s}
of positive rational numbers, there exists no power series
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q;\vec{s}}^{0}
such that

\mathcal{S}_{q;\vec{s}}^{0}(f)=\left(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{0}(E_{1,q})\right)^{-1}
. Therefore, if
E_{1,q}^{-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]_{q}^{0}
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{q}^{0}(E_{1,q}^{-1})\neq{\mathcal{S}}_{q}^{0}(E_{1,q})^{-1}
.

Proof.

To the contrary, assume that
\mathcal{S}_{q;\vec{s}}^{d}(f)=\left(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(\hat{E}_{q})\right)^{-1}
for some suitable increasing sequence
\vec{s}=(s_{1},...,s_{r})
. Let
(\widetilde{s}_{1},...,\widetilde{s}_{r})
its associated sequence as in (
1.4
). By Proposition
1.4
, one has:

\mathcal{S}_{q;\vec{s}}^{d}(f)={\mathcal{L}}_{q;\widetilde{s}_{r}}^{0}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{L}}_{q;\widetilde{s}_{1}}^{0}\circ{\mathcal{S}}^{d}\circ\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s_{1}}(f)\in{\mathbb{O}}_{q;\widetilde{s}_{r}}^{0},

and

{\mathcal{B}}_{q;\widetilde{s}_{1}}\circ\dots\circ{\mathcal{B}}_{q;\widetilde{s}_{r}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{q}^{0}(\hat{E}_{q}(x)\right)^{-1}=\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s_{1}}\left(f\right).

Furthermore, Lemma
3.2
states that

\left(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(\hat{E}_{q}(x))^{-1}\right)=O\left(e^{-(\log(\frac{x}{\sqrt{q}}))^{2}/(\log(q))}\right)\,.

Thus, using several times Lemma
3.3
implies that

\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s_{1}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{q;1}^{d}(\hat{E}_{q}(x))^{-1}\right)=O\left(e^{-{\,x}/({2\tilde{s}\log(q)})}\right),

where
\tilde{s}
is a convenient positive constant.
Since
\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s_{1}}(f)
is a convergent power series, we find that the only possibility is that

{\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;s_{1}}(f)=0}
. This implies
f=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

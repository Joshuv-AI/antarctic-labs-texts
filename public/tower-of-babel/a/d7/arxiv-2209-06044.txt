On the finite generation of valuation semigroups on toric surfaces
Klaus Altmann, Christian Haase, Alex Küronya, Karin Schaller, Lena Walter

Abstract




Abstract.
We provide a combinatorial criterion for the finite generation of a valuation semigroup associated with an ample divisor on a smooth toric surface and a non-toric valuation of maximal rank.
As an application, we construct a lattice polytope such that none of the valuation semigroups of the associated polarized toric variety coming from one-parameter subgroups and centered at a non-toric point are finitely generated.

Keywords.
Newton–Okounkov bodies, toric surfaces, finite generation of valuation semigroups

1. Introduction

Finite generation of semigroups or rings arising from geometric situations has been a question of interest for a long time. As a salient example, we can recall the finite generation of canonical or adjoint rings from birational geometry, which motivated the field through the minimal model program for several decades;
cf.

[
BCHM10
]
. The question of finite generation of valuation semigroups arising from Newton–Okounkov theory appears to be equally difficult in general, with little progress beyond the completely toric situation, but potentially great benefits such as the existence of toric degenerations,
cf.

[
And13
]
, and completely integrable systems,
cf.

[
HK15
]
, to name but a few. In this article, we take a few steps away from the situation where every participant is toric: we consider valuation semigroups associated with torus-invariant divisors on toric surfaces with respect to a
non-toric
valuation.

The main idea behind Newton–Okounkov theory is to attach combinatorial/convex-geometric objects to geometric situations to facilitate their analysis, in other words, to partially replicate the setup of toric geometry in settings without any useful group action. The basis for the theory was developed by Kaveh–Khovanskii
[
KK12
]
and Lazarsfeld–Mustaţă
[
LM09
]
, building on earlier work of Okounkov
[
Oko96
]
, but the subject has seen substantial growth in the last decade. By now applications of Newton–Okounkov theory range from combinatorics and representation theory through birational geometry,
cf.

[
KL17a
,
KL17b
,
KL19
,
KL18a
]
, to mirror symmetry,
cf.

[
RW19
]
, and geometric quantization in mathematical physics.

Given a projective variety
{X}
and a divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
on
{X}
, Newton–Okounkov theory associates to

({X},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
a semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
, the valuation semigroup, and a convex body
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}
, the Newton–Okounkov body of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
. Both the valuation semigroup and the Newton–Okounkov body depend, however, on a maximal rank valuation of the function field of
{X}
coming from an admissible flag
Y_{\bullet}
of subvarieties.

The Newton–Okounkov body
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}
is an asymptotic version of
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
and is, accordingly, a lot easier to determine. Newton–Okounkov bodies on surfaces end up being almost rational polygons;
cf.

[
KLM12
]
. If the section ring of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
is finitely generated, then a suitably general flag valuation will yield a rational simplex as its Newton–Okounkov body;
cf.

[
AKL14
]
. In the case of a toric variety
{X}
with torus-invariant
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
and
Y_{\bullet}
, the associated Newton–Okounkov body recovers the moment polytope of the polarized toric variety.

In this paper, we will focus on the valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
, more concretely, on the question of whether or not it is finitely generated. It is a classical fact that
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is often not finitely generated even if
{X}
is a smooth projective curve.

It is known that
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is a finitely generated semigroup
if
{X}
is a toric variety,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
a torus-invariant divisor, and

Y_{\bullet}
an admissible flag of torus-invariant subvarieties. We
consider the next open question, namely, the case of toric surfaces
and non-torus-invariant flag valuations. Although the divisorial
geometry of toric surfaces themselves is not particularly complicated,
things get out of control once we start blowing up non-toric
points. Blowing up just one point on a toric surface can lead to surfaces with infinitely
many negative curves on them, as recent research of Castravet–Laface–Tevelev–Ugaglia
[
CLTU23
]
illustrates. Blowing up many general points quickly leads to notoriously
difficult situations like Nagata’s conjecture.

1.1. Newton–Okounkov bodies

Assume that
{X}=\TV(\Sigma)
is a smooth projective toric variety
of dimension
n
.
Then ample torus-invariant divisors
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
or their associated
line bundles
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})

can be understood as lattice polytopes

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}
in the character lattice
M\cong\mathbb Z^{n}
of
the torus
\mathbb{T}
acting on
{X}
. Using this description, the starting fan

\Sigma
can be recovered as the normal fan of these polytopes.

Moreover, it is a well-known feature of the toric theory that the vector space

\Gamma({X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}))
has the set of lattice points
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}\cap M
as its
distinguished basis. That is, the polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}
gives

\Gamma({X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}))
not just a dimension but also a shape.

In
[
LM09
]
and
[
KK12
]
, this concept was generalized to
arbitrary projective varieties

X
(still of dimension
n
). If we are given a so-called
admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:{X}=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq Y_{n}

of nested (irreducible) varieties with
\dim Y_{i}=n-i
that are
smooth in the special point

Y_{n}
, then for every ample divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
, there is an associated
convex body
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}

(“Newton–Okounkov body”) in
\mathbb R^{n}
reflecting many properties of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
;
see Section
2.1
for details.

Note that
\mathbb Z^{n}
has ceased to be a character
lattice because there is no longer a torus around. While the
Newton–Okounkov body

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}
depends not on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
but only on its numerical class,
the dependence on the chosen flag is striking.

If, for instance,
{X}
is toric as at the very beginning, then
the construction of
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}
recovers the correspondence
between divisors and polytopes we mentioned above. But
to make this true requires a
toric flag
;
i.e.
,
all subvarieties

Y_{i}
are supposed to be orbit closures.

The fact that toric varieties with toric flags lead to well-known
polytopes is not a one-way street. In fact, if, for a general variety

{X}
, the semigroups
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
are finitely generated, then
they provide a toric degeneration of
{X}
. This was shown in

[
And13
]
. Observe that in general the semigroup

S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
being finitely generated is much stronger than

{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}
being polyhedral. This finite generation of the
valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is the main point of this
paper.

1.2. Results

In
[
IM19
]
the finite generation was shown for complexity one
\mathbb{T}
-varieties with
toric flags. Note that this implies the toric case.
Whenever one is only interested in the Newton–Okounkov body (instead of the semigroup),
there are more results:
The most general one solves the question for surfaces using Zariski decomposition;
cf.

[
LM09
, Theorem 6.4]
.
In particular, the Newton–Okounkov bodies are polyhedral in this case.
Specializing this situation,
[
HKW20
]
has provided
an explicit combinatorial description of
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}

for toric surfaces with certain
non-toric
flags.

In the present paper, partially inspired by
[
AP20
]
, we consider the very same setup of toric surfaces,
namely with the following admissible flag:

Y_{\bullet}:{X}=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq Y_{2},

where
Y_{1}
is the closure of a one-parameter subgroup of the torus,
which is non-torus-invariant and

Y_{2}
is a general smooth point. Then we prove that, in dependence on
Y_{1}
,
the semigroups
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})

can be both finitely generated and not finitely generated.

The main result of the paper (Theorem
6.8
) comes from understanding the relationship between the Newton–Okounkov body of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
and the Newton polygon associated with the
non-toric
flag curve
Y_{1}
. The significance of our contribution lies in the fact that we infer the finite generation of valuation semigroups from asymptotic/convex-geometric data and provide a combinatorial criterion.

In order to state our Theorem, we introduce a bit of terminology. For our flag
Y_{\bullet}
, we consider a non-torus-invariant curve
Y_{1}
given as the closure of the one-parameter subgroup determined by a primitive vector
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\in N
; our flag point is going to be
0\otimes_{\mathbb Z}1
on the torus
\mathbb T\cong\mathbb N\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C^{\times}
. For a strongly convex cone
\con\subseteq NR
and a lattice point
\vect\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
, we say that
\vect
is
strongly decomposable in
\con
if
\vect=\vect^{\prime}+\vect^{\prime\prime}
for suitable
\vect^{\prime},\vect^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
. Given a divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
on
{X}
, we construct strongly convex cones
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\con^{+}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\con^{-}}
associated with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
that are spanned by certain rays of the fan of
{X}
(see Definition
6.7
). With this said, our main result goes as follows.

Theorem

(Theorem
6.8
)
.

Let
{X}
be a smooth toric surface associated with a fan

\Sigma
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
an ample divisor on
{X}
.
The valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is finitely generated
if and only if

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
is not strongly decomposable in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\con^{+}}
and

-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
is not strongly decomposable in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\con^{-}}
.

2. Notation and preliminaries

Let
X
be a two-dimensional smooth projective variety, and assume that we are given an admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:{X}=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq Y_{2},

as in Section
1.1
, and an ample divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
on
{X}
.

2.1. Newton–Okounkov bodies and valuation semigroups

Following
[
LM09
]
, we obtain a rank two
valuation-like function
(or, equivalently, a rank two valuation of the function field of
X
,
see
[
KMR21
]
)

\operatorname{val}_{Y_{\bullet}}\colon\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{{X}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}))\setminus\{0\}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb Z^{2}

as follows:
Let
f
be an equation for
Y_{1}
near
Y_{2}
.
For a non-trivial section
s\in\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{L}})
of a line bundle

{\mathcal{L}}
,
e.g.
,
{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{O}}_{{X}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
, we define

\operatorname{val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(s)=(\operatorname{val}_{1}(s),\operatorname{val}_{2}(s)):=\left(\ord_{Y_{1}}(s),\ord_{Y_{2}}\left(\widetilde{s}|_{Y_{1}}\right)\right),

where

\widetilde{s}:=s/f^{\operatorname{val}_{1}(s)}
is a section of

{\mathcal{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{{X}}(-\operatorname{val}_{1}(s)\cdot Y_{1})
.

The valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
(with respect to the flag
Y_{\bullet}
) is
defined as

\displaystyle S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}):=\{(\ell,\operatorname{val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(s))\,|\,s\in\Gamma({X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}))\setminus\{0\},\ell\in\mathbb N\}\subseteq\mathbb N^{3}.

The Newton–Okounkov body of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
(with respect to the flag
Y_{\bullet}
) is
defined as the set

\displaystyle{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}:=\overline{\bigcupop\displaylimits_{\ell\geq 1}{\frac{1}{\ell}\left\{\operatorname{val}_{Y_{\bullet}}(s)\,|\,s\in\Gamma({X},{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(\ell{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}))\setminus\{0\},\ell\in\mathbb N\right\}}}\subseteq\mathbb R^{2}.

For Newton–Okounkov theory on surfaces, see
[
KL18b
]
.

2.2. Toric setup

Let
N\cong\mathbb Z^{2}
be a two-dimensional lattice with dual lattice
M
and
\Sigma
a
smooth complete fan associated with the toric surface
{X}=\mathbb T\mathbb V(\Sigma)
. We may assume that our ample divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
is toric, hence is represented by a polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}
. Recall that
\mathbb{T}=\spec{(\mathbb C[M])}
is our torus acting on
{X}
; hence
M
becomes its character lattice and
N
the associated lattice of one-parameter subgroups.

Next, fix an admissible flag

Y_{\bullet}:{X}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq Y_{2}
as follows:
Choose a primitive element
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\in N
. The embedding

\iota\colon\overline{N}:=\mathbb Z{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow N

induces a map of fans, hence a toric map
\iota\colon\mathbb P^{1}\rightarrow{X}=\mathbb T\mathbb V(\Sigma)
.
Set
Y_{1}:=\iota(\mathbb P^{1})
and
Y_{2}:=\iota(1\in\mathbb P^{1})
.

Within the torus
\mathbb{T}
, the curve
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}:=Y_{1}
is given by the binomial equation

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f}:=x^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}}-1
with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}\in M
being one of the two primitive elements of

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v^{\bot}_{N}}\subset M_{\mathbb R}
. The associated Newton polytope

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}newt}}}}:=\operatorname{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}newt}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f})

is the line segment
[0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}]
connecting
0
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}
in
M_{\mathbb R}
. This way

\iota\colon\mathbb P^{1}\rightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}
is the normalization map.

For a toric line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
{X}
, the pullback
\iota^{*}\colon\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{L}})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathbb P^{1},\iota^{*}{\mathcal{L}})
corresponds to the projection

\pi\colon M\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow M/\mathbb Z{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}=:\overline{M},

which we will identify with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb Z\cong\overline{M}
.

Observe that this almost fits the setup of
[
IM19
]
as
Y_{1}
is invariant under the codimension one torus given by
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
. We only deviate from
[
IM19
]
by choosing a non-invariant flag point
Y_{2}
.

2.3. Torifying the curve

Note that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}
is also a prime (Cartier) divisor on
X
, which properly
intersects all torus-invariant curves,
i.e.
, all
boundary curves of
{X}
. Therefore,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}
is nef and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{\prime}}:={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}-\operatorname{div}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f})

is its
\mathbb{T}
-invariant representative.

Lemma 2.1
.

The polygon
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}:={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{\prime}}})}
is given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}=\left\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}\in MR\,|\,\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m},\rho\rangle\geq\min\{0,\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}},\rho\rangle\}\text{\rm\ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\right\},

where the rays
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
are identified with their first
(
hence
primitive
)
lattice points.

Proof.

The prime divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{\rho}=\overline{\orb(\rho)}
associated with a ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)

appears in the torus-invariant Weil divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{\prime}}
as often as it
does in the (non-equivariant) principal divisor
-\operatorname{div}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f})
.
Thus,

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}=\left\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}\in MR\,|\,\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m},\rho\rangle\geq\ord_{\rho}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f})\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma(1)\right\},

and it remains to discuss

\ord_{\rho}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f})
.
If
\rho\neq\pm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
, then the
\rho
-orders of the two summands of

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}f}=x^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}}-1
are different, hence

\ord_{\rho}(f)=\min\{\ord_{\rho}(1),\ord_{\rho}(x^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}})\}=\min\{0,\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}},\rho\rangle\}.

If
\rho=\pm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
, then the situation looks locally
like
\ord_{y}(x-1)=0
in
\mathbb A^{2}
.

3.4. Shape of the semigroup

As we did before, let us fix a pair
(\ell,k)
. We know from Section

that we are supposed to collect the values
\ord_{Y_{2}}(\widetilde{s}|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}})
for all possible sections
\widetilde{s}
, where
Y_{2}=\{1\}
is a smooth point on
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}
. In Section
3.1
, we have transferred this setup to
\ord_{1\in\mathbb P^{1}}(\iota^{*}s^{\prime})
, where
s^{\prime}
runs through all global sections represented by the polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}\subseteq MR
.

Proposition
3.8
implies that the pullbacks
\iota^{*}s^{\prime}
run through all
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overline{e(\ell,k)}}
elements of
\pi({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}\cap{M})\subseteq\overline{M}\cong\mathbb Z
. Each element of
\mathbb Z
represents a rational monomial function on
\mathbb P^{1}
. We are supposed to find the orders of vanishing at
1\in\mathbb P^{1}
of all of their linear combinations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
Z\subset\mathbb Z
be a finite subset with
e
elements leading to the
e
-dimensional
vector space

\mathbb C[Z]:=\{f\in\mathbb C[t,t^{-1}]\,|\,\supp(f)\subseteq Z\}.

Then
\ord_{1}\mathbb C[Z]=\{0,1,\ldots,e-1\}=\ord_{c}\mathbb C[Z]
for all
c\in\mathbb C^{*}\subseteq\mathbb P^{1}
.

Proof.

Set
Z=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{e}\}
.
For an element
f\in\mathbb C[Z]
with

f=\lambda_{1}\cdot t^{p_{1}}+\ldots+\lambda_{e}\cdot t^{p_{e}}
and

d\in\mathbb N
, the rows of the matrix
P
given as

{\small\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}cccccc@{}}1&1&&\ldots&1\\
p_{1}&p_{2}&&\ldots&p_{e}\\
p_{1}(p_{1}-1)&p_{2}(p_{2}-1)&&\ldots&p_{e}(p_{e}-1)\\
p_{1}(p_{1}-1)(p_{1}-2)&p_{2}(p_{2}-1)(p_{2}-2)&&\ldots&p_{e}(p_{e}-1)(p_{e}-2)\\
\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}\\
\,\,\,p_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot(p_{1}-(d-1))&p_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot(p_{2}-(d-1))&&\ldots&p_{e}\cdot\ldots\cdot(p_{e}-(d-1))\end{array}\right)}

encode
f(1)=\lambda_{1}\cdot 1+\ldots+\lambda_{e}\cdot 1
,
f^{\prime}(1)=\lambda_{1}\cdot p_{1}\cdot 1+\ldots+\lambda_{e}\cdot p_{e}\cdot 1
,
f^{\prime\prime}(1),\ldots,f^{(d)}(1)
. Let
p
be an arbitrary variable. Then the linear spaces

\textstyle L_{1}(p):=\operatorname{span}Q\left\{1,p,p^{2},\ldots,p^{d}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb Q[p]

and

\textstyle L_{2}(p):=\operatorname{span}Q\left\{0!{p\choose 0},1!{p\choose 1},2!{p\choose 2},\ldots,d!{p\choose d}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb Q[p]\vskip 4.30554pt

coincide because
L_{1}(p)\supseteq L_{2}(p)
and
\dim(L_{1}(p))=\dim(L_{2}(p))
.
In particular,
there exists an invertible lower triangular matrix
F
such that
P=F\cdot V
, where

V:=(p_{j}^{i})_{0\leq i\leq d,1\leq j\leq e}

is the transposed Vandermonde matrix.
If we choose
d=e-1
, the matrices
V
and thus
P
are invertible.
Hence the system of linear equations

P\cdot(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{e})^{T}=0,

which is equivalent to the system
f(1)=f^{\prime}(1)=f^{\prime\prime}(1)=\ldots=f^{(e-1)}(1)=0
, has the unique solution
f=0
, so
\ord_{1}(f)\geq e
is impossible.

On the other hand, replacing the equation
f^{(k)}(1)=0
by
f^{(k)}(1)=1
(
0\leq k\leq e-1
) yields an
f
with
\ord_{1}(f)=k
.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the following statement.

Theorem 3.11
.

The valuation semigroup is given as

S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})=\left\{\left(\ell,k,\delta\right)\in\mathbb N^{3}\,|\,0\leq\delta\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overline{e(\ell,k)}}-1\right\},

and

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overline{e(\ell,k)}}=0
for large
k\gg\ell
.

Proof.

The definition of the valuation semigroup can be reformulated as

S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})=\{(\ell,k,\delta)\in\mathbb N^{3}\,|\,s^{\prime}\in\Gamma({X},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}({\ell},{k})}})\setminus\{0\},\ord_{1\in\mathbb P^{1}}(\iota^{*}s^{\prime})=\delta\}.

Then everything follows from Proposition
3.8
.
∎

5. Criterion for the finite generation of certain valuation semigroups

We provide a criterion for the finite generation of
strictly positive (with respect to their height functions)
semigroups in terms
of their limit polyhedra.

5.1. Semigroups with polyhedral limit

We start with a free abelian group
M
of rank
n
,
i.e.
,
M\cong\mathbb Z^{n}
, and a linear form
h\colon M\rightarrow\hskip-8.00003pt\rightarrow\mathbb Z
which we call a
height function
. This induces
hR\colon MR=M\otimes Z\mathbb R\rightarrow\hskip-8.00003pt\rightarrow\mathbb R
, which we will often denote by
h
as well.

Let
S\subseteq h^{-1}(\mathbb N)
be a semigroup that is
strictly positive
;
i.e.
,
S\cap\ker(h)=\{0\}
. In order to refer to the individual layers of a given height, we will write

\textstyle S_{k}:=S\cap h^{-1}(k);\;\mbox{{i.e.}, we have }S=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{k\in\mathbb N}S_{k}\;\mbox{ with }S_{0}=\{0\}.

This setup allows us to define the enveloping cone

C_{S}:=\overline{\cone S}\subseteq MR

as well as the convex limit figure

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}:=C_{S}\cap hR^{-1}(1)\supseteq S_{1}.

In the case of a valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
, the
height
h\colon\mathbb Z^{3}\rightarrow\hskip-8.00003pt\rightarrow\mathbb Z
is the projection on
\ell
, which then leads to the Newton–Okounkov body
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}=C_{S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})}\cap hR^{-1}(1)\subseteq hR^{-1}(1)\cong\mathbb R^{2}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
S
has a
polyhedral limit
if
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
is a
polytope,
i.e.
, if
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
equals the convex hull of its
(finitely many) vertices.

This property is fulfilled whenever the semigroup
S
is finitely generated. In this case,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
even has rational vertices; it is a rational polytope. However, the following standard example shows that the converse implication does not hold in general.

Example 5.2
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb Z^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb Z,[x,y]\mapstochar\rightarrow x+y
be the summation map. Then

S:=\{0\}\cup(\mathbb Z_{\geq 1}\times\mathbb Z_{\geq 1})
is not finitely generated,
but
C_{S}=\mathbb R^{2}_{\geq 0}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
equals the line segment
connecting the points
[1,0]
and
[0,1]
.

5.2. Equivalent conditions for finite generation

We assume that

S\subseteq M
is a strictly positive (with respect to
h
)
semigroup that has
polyhedral limit
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
.

Definition 5.3
.

We say that a point
p\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}

lifts to the semigroup
S
(
i.e.
, is a valuation point) if there exists some scalar
c\in\mathbb R_{>0}
with
c\cdot p\in S
.

Note that in this case, both
p
and
c
have to be rational;
i.e.
,
p\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}\cap MQ
and
c\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
. Hence, it is not a surprise that the assumption of the next lemma is automatically fulfilled if the semigroup
S
is finitely generated.

Lemma 5.4
.

If all vertices of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
lift to
S
, then they are rational

(
i.e.,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
is a rational polytope
)

and every rational point
p\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}\cap MQ

lifts to
S
.

Proof.

Let
v^{1},\ldots,v^{d}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
be linearly independent (rational) vertices
such that
p
is contained in their convex hull. Then the unique coefficients

\lambda_{i}
in the representation

p=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\,v^{i}
have to be rational, too. Thus, we may choose an
integer
\mu
such that
\mu\cdot\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb N
.
On the other hand,
there is a joint factor
c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq 1}
such that all multiples

c\cdot v^{i}
belong to
S
. This implies

\textstyle\mu c\cdot p=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{d}\mu\lambda_{i}\cdot(c\cdot v^{i})\in S.\qed

Next, we formulate the main point of this section.

Proposition 5.5
.

A semigroup
S
with a
polyhedral limit
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
is finitely generated if and only if
all vertices of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
lift to
S
.

We have already seen that this condition is necessary for the finite
generation.
Now we will show that it is sufficient, too.
Note that, in Example
5.2
, the two vertices of the line
segment
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
indeed do not lift to the semigroup.

Let
S\subseteq M
be a subsemigroup with rational polyhedral limit

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}(S)
(with respect to some height function
h\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb Z
). Assume
that the vertices
and thus, by Lemma
5.4
,
all rational points of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}_{S}
lift to
S
.
Moreover, we may assume that

C_{S}
is a full-dimensional cone.

Proof of Proposition
5.5
.

Assume that
S
is not finitely generated. Then
S
has infinitely many
indecomposable elements;
i.e.
,

{\mathcal{H}}:=S\setminus\big((S\setminus\{0\})+(S\setminus\{0\})\big)

is infinite;
cf.

[
CLS11
, Proposition 1.2.23]
.

By taking a simplicial subdivision we may, w.l.o.g., assume that the cone
C_{S}
is simplicial and given as
C_{S}=\cone(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
. Consider the lattice
\Lambda
generated by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
. As
M/\Lambda
is finite, there must be a coset
m+\Lambda
which contains infinitely many elements of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Here we may choose
m
to be a minimal representative in
C_{S}
:
m\in C_{S}\cap M
so that
m-s_{i}\notin C_{S}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

As the elements in
C_{S}\cap{\mathcal{H}}
were indecomposable in
S
, they certainly are indecomposable in
C_{S}\cap S
. In particular, if we identify
(m+\Lambda)\cap C_{S}
with
\mathbb N^{n}
, we obtain an infinite set of pairwise incomparable elements, in contradiction to Dickson’s lemma
[
CLO15
, Chapter 4, Theorem 5]
.
∎

6. Finite generation criterion

6.1. Characterising the lifting property

The following theorem gives a purely combinatorial criterion to check if
our valuation semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
(in the language
of Section
2.2
) is finitely generated.
Recall the definition

d(k/\ell)=\length(\pi({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}))

from Section
4
on page

4
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The point

(1,k/\ell,d(k/\ell))=(1,q,d(q))

is a valuation point

(
i.e., a multiple of it lies in
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
)
if and only if
there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb N
such that

\pi\colon\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}

is surjective
(
i.e.,

\lambda\cdot d(q)=\overline{e(\lambda,\lambda q)}-1)
and
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})
has endpoints in
\overline{M}
.

Proof.

By definition,
(1,q,d(q))
is a valuation point if and only if
there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb N
such that we have

\lambda\cdot(1,q,d(q))\in S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})\subseteq\mathbb N^{3}
.
By Theorem
3.11
, the latter happens exactly if

0\leq\lambda\cdot d(q)\leq\overline{e(\lambda,\lambda q)}-1,

where

\overline{e(\lambda,\lambda q)}=\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M)
.
In addition, we see that

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot d(q)=\lambda\cdot d(1,q)

\displaystyle=\lambda\cdot\length(\pi({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}))

\displaystyle=\length(\lambda\pi({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}))=\length(\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}))

\displaystyle\geq\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}-1

\displaystyle\geq\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M)-1=\overline{e(\lambda,\lambda q)}-1.

Combining all these inequalities, we obtain the equations

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot d(q)=\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}-1

and

(6.2)

\displaystyle\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}=\#\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M),

where Equation (
6.1
) is equivalent to
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})

having end points in
\overline{M}
, and Equation (
6.2
) to

\pi\colon\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}

being surjective
(
i.e.
,
\pi
meets all possible lattice points in
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}
).
∎

In the following, the
tangent cone
of a polygon
\Theta
at a point
r\in\Theta
is the
cone generated by
\Theta-r
. It is pointed if and only if
r
is a vertex.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that the functional
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb Z
takes its minimum and maximum values over
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}
at the two vertices
\vminus^{\prime}
and
\vplus^{\prime}
, respectively. Denote their tangent cones by
\coneminus^{\prime}
and
\coneplus^{\prime}
.

Then
(1,q,d(q))
is a valuation point if and only if
1\in\pi(\coneminus^{\prime}\cap M)
and
-1\in\pi(\coneplus^{\prime}\cap M)
.

Proof.

If
(1,q,d(q))
is a valuation point, then according to Theorem
6.1
, there exists a
\lambda\in\mathbb N
such that
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})
has vertices in
\overline{M}
and such that
\pi\colon\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M\rightarrow\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}
is surjective. Hence,
1\in\pi\left(\lambda({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}-\vminus^{\prime})\cap M\right)\subset\pi(\coneminus^{\prime}\cap M)
and similarly for
\coneplus^{\prime}
.

For the converse, assume
1\in\pi(\coneminus^{\prime}\cap M)
and
-1\in\pi(\coneplus^{\prime}\cap M)
. We will construct a suitable scaling factor
\lambda\in\mathbb N
for which

\pi\colon\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}\cap M\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}

is surjective and
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})
is a lattice polytope, in order to again apply Theorem
6.1
.
To this end, choose levels
\delta_{\min}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
and
\delta_{\max}\in\mathbb Q_{>0}
such that

\coneminus^{\prime}\cap\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\leq\delta_{\min}\right]\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}-\vminus^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\coneplus^{\prime}\cap\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}\geq\delta_{\max}\right]\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}-\vplus^{\prime}.

We set

\varepsilon_{\min}=\length\left(\coneminus^{\prime}\cap\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}=\delta_{\min}\right]\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon_{\max}=\length\left(\coneplus^{\prime}\cap\left[{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}=\delta_{\max}\right]\right)\,.

Choose a
\lambda\in\mathbb N
with
\lambda>\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\min}}
and
\lambda>\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\max}}
such that
\lambda\vminus^{\prime}
and
\lambda\vplus^{\prime}
are lattice points in
M
. Then the corresponding projection
\pi
is surjective. To show that, we divide the image
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})
into three parts. The first
\lambda\delta_{\min}
lattice points in
\pi(\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)})\cap\overline{M}
are in the image of
\pi
because
\pi
restricted to
\coneminus^{\prime}
is surjective by assumption. The same argument holds for the last
\lambda\delta_{\max}
points since
\pi
is surjective on
\coneplus^{\prime}
. The points in between are hit by projecting the lattice points in
\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}
because all the respective fibers have length greater than
\length({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}})=1
, by construction. Thus
\pi
is surjective and
(1,q,d(q))
is a valuation point, according to Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

The case where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
takes its minimum (or maximum) not at a vertex but at an edge is actually easier to handle. As soon as
\lambda{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta(1,q)}
is a lattice polytope, the edge is an integral multiple of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{M}}

6.2. Strong decomposability

We have seen that it is important to decide the surjectivity of the projection of lattice points in a cone in
MR
. Next, we will translate this surjectivity into a statement in
N
. To this end, we introduce the following notion.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
\con\subseteq NR
be a cone. A lattice point
\vect\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
is

strongly decomposable in
\con
if
\vect=\vect^{\prime}+\vect^{\prime\prime}
for suitable
\vect^{\prime},\vect^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\con\subseteq NR
be a cone and

\vectK\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
a direction.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

We have
1\notin\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{\con^{\vee}},\vectK\rangle
,
where
{\mathcal{H}}_{\con^{\vee}}
denotes the Hilbert basis of
\con^{\vee}
.

(ii)

The direction
\vectK
is strongly decomposable in
\con
.

(iii)

The closure of the one-parameter subgroup

\lambda^{\vectK}(\mathbb C^{*})\subseteq\mathbb{T}

in
\mathbb T\mathbb V(\con)
is singular.

Proof.

(
i
) and (
iii
) are equivalent: The
1
-parameter subgroup
represented by
\vectK
can always be extended to

\lambda^{\vectK}\colon\mathbb C\rightarrow\mathbb T\mathbb V(\con)
. On the dual level of regular
functions, however, this corresponds to

\langle\,\cdot\,,\vectK\rangle\colon\mathbb C[\con^{\vee}\cap M]\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb C[\mathbb N].

The latter map is
surjective if and only if
1\in\langle\con^{\vee}\cap M,\vectK\rangle
.

(
i
)
\Rightarrow
(
ii
): By assumption, there exist primitive lattice points

s^{0},s^{1}\in M\setminus\con^{\vee}
such that the line segment between
them lying on
[\vectK=1]

contains no interior lattice point but intersects
\con^{\vee}
. Note that

\{s^{0},s^{1}\}
is a
\mathbb Z
-basis because
the lattice triangle
\conv(0,s^{0},s^{1})
is unimodular. Then

\operatorname{int}\left(\cone\left(s^{0},s^{1}\right)\right)\supset\con^{\vee}\quad\text{and thus}\quad\cone\left(t^{0},t^{1}\right)\subset\operatorname{int}(\con),

where
\{t^{0},t^{1}\}
denotes the basis dual to
\{s^{0},s^{1}\}
. By the definition of the dual basis, this yields
\langle s^{i},\vectK\rangle=1=\langle s^{i},t^{0}+t^{1}\rangle
for all
i
;
i.e.
, the two linear functionals coincide on the basis. Therefore,
\vectK=t^{0}+t^{1}
.

(
ii
)
\Rightarrow
(
i
): Let
\vectK
be strongly decomposable in
\con
. Then
\vectK=\vect^{\prime}+\vect^{\prime\prime}
for some
\vect^{\prime},\vect^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{int}(\con)\cap N
. Therefore, we have
\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m},\vectK\rangle=\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m},\vect^{\prime}\rangle+\langle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m},\vect^{\prime\prime}\rangle\in\mathbb Z
for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\con^{\vee}}
. Since
\vect^{\prime}
and
\vect^{\prime\prime}
lie in the interior of
\con
, both summands are positive. Thus,
1\notin\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{\con^{\vee}},\vectK\rangle
.
∎

Proposition 6.12
.

Given a fan
\Sigma
and a direction
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
, the valuation
semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is finitely generated for all ample
divisors
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
on
{X}
if and only if
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
and
-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}

are not strongly decomposable in
\con
for all cones

\con\subseteq NR
generated by rays of the given fan
\Sigma
.

Proof.

“
\Leftarrow
”: Theorem
6.8
.
“
\Rightarrow
”:
Assume there exists a cone
\con
built from rays of
\Sigma

such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
is strongly decomposable in
\con
.
We will construct an ample divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}

such that
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is not finitely generated.

In a first step, we build a torus-invariant divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}{b_{\rho}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{\rho}}
whose associated polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}
has a vertex
\vminus^{\prime}
with tangent cone
\coneminus^{\prime}=\con^{\vee}
. For that we choose coefficients
b_{\rho}\in\mathbb Z
such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}}
has positive intersection numbers with all curves corresponding to rays
\rho\nsubseteq\operatorname{int}(\con)
. Then we relax the inequalities at
\vminus^{\prime}
by choosing the coefficients
b_{\rho}\gg 0
for all
\rho\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\con)
. This guarantees that
\con^{\vee}
is the tangent cone of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}
at its vertex
\vminus^{\prime}
.

Now, we want to define an ample torus-invariant divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}})
. As an intermediate step, set
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}^{\prime}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}{a^{\prime}_{\rho}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{\rho}}:={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{\prime}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}}
. Then by construction, the associated polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}^{\prime}})}
contains the Minkowski sum
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}
. Moreover, the defining inequalities of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}
coincide with the ones for the polytope
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}^{\prime}})}
for all rays
\rho\nsubseteq\operatorname{int}(\con)
.

In general,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}^{\prime}
is not nef and in particular not ample because it might have negative intersection with curves associated with remaining rays
\rho\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\con)
. Hence, as a last step, we define a torus-invariant divisor
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}{a_{\rho}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}_{\rho}}
with
a_{\rho}=a^{\prime}_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)\setminus\operatorname{int}(\con)
. For the remaining rays
\rho\subseteq\operatorname{int}(\con)
, choose coefficients
a_{\rho}
small enough such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}
is ample and big enough such that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}}\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}
. Then we have
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D}})}:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta}^{\operatorname{nef}}})
, and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Theta({\ell},{k})}
has a vertex
\vminus^{\prime}
with tangent cone
\coneminus^{\prime}=\con^{\vee}
. Since
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}v_{N}}
is strongly decomposable in
\con
, it follows from Theorem
6.8
that the semigroup
S_{Y_{\bullet}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}D})
is not finitely generated.

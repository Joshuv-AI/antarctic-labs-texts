Contrastive Prompts Improve Disentanglement in Text-to-Image Diffusion Models
Chen Henry Wu, Fernando De la Torre

Abstract
Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved remarkable performance in image synthesis, while the text interface does not always provide fine-grained control over certain image factors. For instance, changing a single token in the text can have unintended effects on the image. This paper shows a simple modification of classifier-free guidance can help disentangle image factors in text-to-image models. The key idea of our method, Contrastive Guidance , is to characterize an intended factor with two prompts that differ in minimal tokens: the positive prompt describes the image to be synthesized, and the baseline prompt serves as a “baseline” that disentangles other factors. Contrastive Guidance is a general method we illustrate whose benefits in three scenarios: (1) to guide domain-specific diffusion models trained on an object class, (2) to gain continuous, rig-like controls for text-to-image generation, and (3) to improve the performance of zero-shot image editors. Code available at: https://github.com/humansensinglab/contrastive-guidance

Introduction

Many recent methods for text-to-image synthesis are built upon diffusion models trained on large-scale datasets (e.g., GLIDE
Nichol et al. 2022
, DALL
\cdot
E 2
Ramesh et al. 2022
, Imagen
Saharia et al. 2022
, and Stable Diffusion
Rombach et al. 2022
).
Besides generating images with unprecedented visual quality, these models also show remarkable generalization ability to novel texts with complex compositions of concepts.

Prompt engineering and guidance
Dhariwal and Nichol 2021
;
Ho and Salimans 2021
are two (overlapping) directions for steering the outputs of these text-to-image models. Prompt engineering focuses on how to design better text as input to the model, while guidance focuses on how the image distribution can be modified by text. However, prompt engineering and previous guidance methods
Dhariwal and Nichol 2021
;
Ho and Salimans 2021
;
Nichol et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022b
still face challenges in fine-grained control over image factors. They can affect many image factors, not only what is intended. Thus, in this paper, we raise the following question.
How can we steer the outputs of text-to-image diffusion models over certain image factors, without affecting other factors?

In this paper, we show that a simple modification of classifier-free guidance can help disentangle image factors in text-to-image diffusion models. Our key idea is to instantiate an intended image factor with two text prompts that differ only in several tokens: the
positive prompt
describes the image to be synthesized, while the
baseline prompt
serves as a “baseline” that disentangles other factors. The difference between the two score functions conditioned on the two prompts is used as guidance. For instance, suppose we already have a diffusion model for high-quality cat images. To synthesize a cat on the beach, one can set the positive prompt as
a portrait photo of a cat on the beach
, and the baseline prompt as
a portrait photo of a cat
. The baseline prompt helps the model focus on
on the beach
, preventing it from contributing to what
a portrait photo of cat
looks like.

Besides the above intuition, we also formalize
Contrastive Guidance
in a probabilistic framework. Specifically, by applying the Bayes rule, we show that the positive and baseline prompts together build a binary generative classifier over the noisy images. This binary generative classifier can be used to guide the denoising process following classifier guidance
Dhariwal and Nichol 2021
. Note that, in classifier guidance, the score function of the classifier is computed by backpropagating through the classifier. Different from them, we show that our generative classifier has a gradient-free solution that corresponds to the score functions of the two prompts, and
Contrastive Guidance
is proportional to this solution up to a constant.

Since our derivation is based on the unified SDE/ODE formulation
Song et al. 2021b
,
Contrastive Guidance
can be plugged into different diffusion models and samplers/schedulers in various scenarios:

•

Guiding Domain Expert (Figure
1
top): Given a
domain expert
trained on high-quality cat faces, how can we combine the domain knowledge of the domain expert with the generalization ability of the text-to-image model? We show that compared with classifier-free guidance
Ho and Salimans 2021
, our
Contrastive Guidance
can better disentangle the domain knowledge and text condition.

•

Continuous, Rig-like Control (Figure
1
middle): How could we gain continuous, rig-like control over the color while maintaining others? It helps synthesize the same object with interpolated color.

•

Improving Image Editing (Figure
1
bottom): Previous works have used text-to-image diffusion models for image editing
Hertz et al. 2022
;
Wu and la Torre 2022
;
Meng et al. 2022
;
Kawar et al. 2022b
. Disentanglement is the key to this application since the user only wants the intended edit (e.g., adding people) without altering other factors (e.g., the car and the background).

Our experiments verify these applications. Furthermore, our analysis demonstrates that our
Contrastive Guidance
is more disentangled than classifier-free guidance
Ho and Salimans 2021
.

Guidance for diffusion models.

Guidance methods modify the output distribution of pre-trained diffusion models, based on additional inputs such as class labels

, text
Nichol et al. 2022
, and corrupted images
Kawar et al. 2022a

;
Rombach et al. 2022

Liu et al. 2022b
combines score estimates with different text inputs. Besides user-specified conditions, several works showed that even guidance based on model outputs
Chen et al. 2022
or representations
Hong et al. 2022
can improve the quality of images. In this paper, we explore how to disentangle image factors with texts to gain fine-grained control.

Image editing with diffusion models.

Recent works have shown that diffusion models are capable of unpaired image-to-image translation

;
Su et al. 2022
;
Choi et al. 2021
;
Wu and la Torre 2022
. A more recent trend of works have explored zero-shot image editing with text-to-image diffusion models

;
Hertz et al. 2022
;
Kawar et al. 2022b
. One of the applications of our
Contrastive Guidance
is to improve the intended edit of some of these zero-shot image editors.

Guidance for other generative models.

Guidance has also been widely studied for GANs
Goodfellow et al. 2014
and autoregressive language models (LMs). For GANs, the conditions for guidance include class labels
Nguyen et al. 2017
;
Nie et al. 2021
text prompts
Patashnik et al. 2021
;
Wu et al. 2022
, corrupted images
Menon et al. 2020
, and de-biasing
Karakas et al. 2022
;
Wu et al. 2022

Krause et al. 2021
uses a small LM with two contrastive prompts to guide a large LM, and contrastive decoding
Li et al. 2022
leverages a small language model to correct mistakes by a large LM.

3
Method

3.1
Background on Diffusion Models

Diffusion models sample data via iterative denoising. Suppose noises are added to the image
\bm{x}
via
\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bm{w}
, where
t\in[0,T]
is the time step, and
\bm{f}
and
g
are pre-defined functions that specify how exactly the noises are added. It has been shown that the denoising problem can be formulated by the following SDE
Song et al. 2021b
:

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\Big[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})\Big]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}},

(1)

where
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})
is the
score function
, the core of diffusion models, which provides information about the denoising direction. Besides the SDE-based formulation, previous works have also proposed non-Markovian
Song et al. 2021a
;
Watson et al. 2022
and ODE-based formulations
Song et al. 2021b
;
Song et al. 2021a
;
Salimans and Ho 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022a
;
Lu et al. 2022
;
Karras et al. 2022
;
Zhang and Chen 2022
. Since the true score function is inaccessible, diffusion models learn to approximate it
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})\approx{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}(\bm{x},t)}
. For instance, we can plug it into the SDE and get the denoising process:

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\Big[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}(\bm{x},t)}\Big]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.

(2)

This connection between diffusion models and differential equations provides a unified formulation for guided image synthesis. Given a condition
c
, one only need to compute the conditional score function
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
and replace
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})
with
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
in the denoising process. For instance, we can plug it into the above SDE and get the conditional denoising process
Song et al. 2021b
:

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\Big[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)\Big]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.

(3)

The true conditional score function
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
is also inaccessible, and each guidance method aims to approximate it. For instance, given a text-to-image diffusion model
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}
and a text
\bm{y}
as the condition, classifier-free guidance (CFG)
Ho and Salimans 2021
models the conditional denoising process as

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\Big(\tau_{t}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})\ -\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad(\tau_{t}-1)\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\varnothing)\Big)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}},\end{split}

(4)

where
\varnothing
is an empty string and
\tau_{t}
is a hyperparameter indicating the guidance strength. It implies that CFG approximates
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)\approx\tau_{t}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})-(\tau_{t}-1)\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\varnothing)
.

3.2
Contrastive Guidance

We introduce
Contrastive Guidance
, which uses text-to-image diffusion models to steer certain image factors while not affecting others. Our key idea is to instantiate a condition
c
with two prompts
(\bm{y}^{+},\bm{y}^{-})
that differ in a few tokens: the
positive prompt

\bm{y}^{+}
describes the intended image, while the
baseline prompt

\bm{y}^{-}
serves as a ‘‘baseline’’ that disentangles other factors.

\bm{y}^{-}

\bm{y}^{+}
as
a portrait photo of a cat with eyeglasses
, and the baseline prompt
\bm{y}^{-}
as
a portrait photo of a cat without eyeglasses
, or simply,
a portrait photo of a cat
. The baseline prompt helps the model focus on
with eyeglasses

In the following, we formalize the above intuition. Our goal is to derive
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
. First, we rewrite
p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
as

\displaystyle p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)\propto p_{t}(\bm{x})p_{t}(c|\bm{x}).

(5)

We will discuss the model to be guided
p_{t}(\bm{x})
later, which has several variations. The key idea of our formulation is to rewrite the term
p_{t}(c|\bm{x})
as a
generative classifier
using the Bayes rule. It is a binary classifier that infers whether the noisy
\bm{x}
is from the positive prompt
\bm{y}^{+}
or the baseline prompt
\bm{y}^{-}
. Formally, we define the generative classifier as

\displaystyle p_{t}(c|\bm{x}):=\frac{p(\bm{y}^{+})p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})^{\gamma_{t}}}{p(\bm{y}^{+})p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})^{\gamma_{t}}+p(\bm{y}^{-})p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{-})^{\gamma_{t}}},

(6)

where the temperature
\gamma_{t}
controls the sharpness of the distribution. In the following, we will combine Eq. (
5
) and Eq. (
6
), using simplified notations:
p^{+}:=p(\bm{y}^{+})
,
p^{-}:=p(\bm{y}^{-})
,
p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x}):=p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
, and
p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x}):=p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{-})
. Using the
log-derivative trick
, we have (see details in Appendix
A.1
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\ \nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)\\
&=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log\Big(p_{t}(\bm{x})\frac{p^{+}p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}}{p^{+}p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}+p^{-}p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}}\Big)\end{split}

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})+\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\frac{\nabla_{\bm{x}}\Big(p^{+}p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}+p^{-}p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}\Big)}{p^{+}p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}+p^{-}p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}}\end{split}

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})+\underbrace{\frac{\gamma_{t}\cdot p^{-}p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}}{p^{+}p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}+p^{-}p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})^{\gamma_{t}}}}_{\lambda_{t}}\cdot\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\Big(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})-\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})\Big)\end{split}

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})+\lambda_{t}\Big(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}^{+}(\bm{x})\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}^{-}(\bm{x})\Big)\end{split}

(10)

\displaystyle\approx{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}(\bm{x},t)}+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\lambda_{t}\big(\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})-\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{-})\big)},

(11)

where
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}
is the text-to-image diffusion model.
We will introduce several choices of the score estimate
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}(\bm{x},t)}
in Section
3.3
, e.g., domain experts, text-to-image models themselves, and image editors. Please see Figure
2
for an illustration of how the term is computed with pretrained text-to-image diffusion models and the two prompt conditions.

(Not) estimating
\lambda_{t}

A remaining question is how to estimate the coefficient
\lambda_{t}
. Since
\gamma_{t}
,
p(\bm{y}^{+})
and
p(\bm{y}^{-})
do not depend on
\bm{x}
, the only terms to estimate are
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
and
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{-})
. We can leverage the fact that each SDE has an ODE with the same marginal density at each time
t

Maoutsa et al. 2020
;
Song et al. 2021b
. Estimating the density of ODEs is tractable by calling an ODE solver
Chen et al. 2018
;
Grathwohl et al. 2019
from
t
to
T
(see Appendix
A.2
). However, this requires calling the ODE solver at all
t
’s, resulting in the total complexity of
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
where
N
is the number of discretization steps. To avoid this, this paper assumes that the temperature
\gamma_{t}
is adaptive such that
\lambda_{t}
does not depend on
\bm{x}
, giving us
\mathcal{O}(N)
complexity. Further, our experiments show that a fixed
\lambda_{t}
for all time
t
provides reasonable results.

Comparison to CFG and negative prompts

It is worth noting that although the derivation of the ultimate solution is complex, the final expression in Eq. (
11
) is simple. In terms of the final expression, our approach can be seen as an extension upon classifier-free guidance (CFG)
Ho and Salimans 2021
, where we employ a non-trivial baseline prompt in place of an empty string. Similarly, negative prompts have become a common practice in most diffusion model applications. Typical negative prompts are phrases describing unwanted outputs such as “low quality” and “NSFW”.
We would like to highlight the idea of “specifying the image factor with a contrastive baseline prompt”, which is why our method achieves better disentanglement than CFG (Section
4
).
In the following sections, we will also demonstrate how this formulation leads to new applications, such as merging a text-to-image diffusion model and a domain-specific diffusion model, rig-like controls, and image editing.

3.3
Applications

Guiding Domain Experts

Text-to-image models sometimes underperform domain-specific models in highly specialized domains (e.g., faces). To combine the best of both worlds, we can use
Contrastive Guidance
to guide domain experts. Suppose we have a domain expert
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},t)}
for high-quality cat faces. To generate a cat with eyeglasses, one can set
\bm{y}^{+}
as
a portrait photo of a cat with eyeglasses
, and
\bm{y}^{-}
as
a portrait photo of a cat without eyeglasses
, or simply,
a portrait photo of a cat
. In this case, the denoising process becomes:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\Big({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\bm{s}_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{x},t)}\ +\\
&\quad{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\lambda_{t}\big(\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})-\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{-})\big)}\Big)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.\end{split}

(12)

The text-only input of text-to-image diffusion models does not allow continuous, rig-like control because it cannot be accurately described by the text.

\bm{y}
is
a photo of a waterfall

\bm{y}^{+}
can be
a beautiful photo of a waterfall
, and the negative prompt
\bm{y}^{-}
can be
a not beautiful photo of a waterfall
. In these cases, the denoising process becomes:

Guiding Image Editors

Zero-shot image editing aims at modifying a real image
\bm{x}
given a source text
\bm{y}
and a target text
\hat{\bm{y}}

Wu and la Torre 2022
. For instance, suppose one wants to change the season of a real image, taken from an aerial view, from autumn to winter. The source text
\bm{y}
can be
an aerial view of autumn scene
, and the target text
\hat{\bm{y}}
can be
an aerial view of winter scene
. Since disentangling image factors is a key to meaningful editing,
Contrastive Guidance
can be helpful to this task. In this paper, we focus on two editors: SDEdit
Meng et al. 2022
and CycleDiffusion
Wu and la Torre 2022
. SDEdit is based on the finding that, when noises are added and then removed up to a suitable noise level, the structure of the image can preserve. For zero-shot image editing, SDEdit first adds noise to the source image
\bm{x}
to get a noisy image
\bm{x}_{t_{\text{SD}}}
. Then, conditioned on the target text
\hat{\bm{y}}
, it denoises
\bm{x}_{t_{\text{e}}}
into a target image
\hat{\bm{x}}
via

\mathrm{d}\hat{\bm{x}}=\Big[\bm{f}(\hat{\bm{x}},t)-g(t)^{2}{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\tilde{\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}}(\hat{\bm{x}},t,\hat{\bm{y}})}\Big]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.

(14)

CycleDiffusion leverages the observation that fixing all random variables in the diffusion process helps produce similar images. Similar to SDEdit, CycleDiffusion also adds noise to get
\bm{x}_{t_{\text{e}}}
and denoises it with Eq. (
14
), but the difference is that the random variable
\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}
in the denoising process in Eq. (
14
) is not randomly sampled. Instead, CycleDiffusion introduces an encoding process that infers the
\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}
that are likely to help reconstruct the source image given the source text. In the denoising process, the encoded
\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}
are applied to Eq. (
14
). This helps improve content preservation during editing.

can strengthen the intended editing for both methods by modifying the denoising process in Eq. (
14

\bm{y}^{+}

6
Conclusions and Discussion

This paper advocates the idea of using text-to-image diffusion models as disentangled guidance for image synthesis. The key idea is to disentangle image factors using two prompts that have minimal differences. We formalize this intuition by defining a generative classifier with the text-to-image model and the two prompts, whose score function is proportional to a simple subtraction term. We have shown the efficacy of
Contrastive Guidance
in several applications: (1) guiding domain experts for domain-specific but generalizable text-to-image synthesis, (2) guiding text-to-image models themselves to gain continuous, rig-like controls, and (3) guiding image editors to improve the intended edit. Our experiments show that
Contrastive Guidance
is both qualitatively and quantitatively better than the baselines.

This paper also leaves several questions for further exploration. For efficiency, we assume that the temperature
\gamma_{t}
is adaptive such that
\lambda_{t}
does not depend on
\bm{x}
; although its effectiveness is validated by our experiments, it should be considered with reservation because the validity may not hold on other domains. Moreover, one may study how the choice of the prompt pairs
(\bm{y}^{+},\bm{y}^{-})
affects the performance. Please see our discussion on errors and biases in Appendix
B.4
.

(19)

(20)

(21)

A.2
Derivation of
\lambda_{t}

In Section
3.2
and Appendix
A.1
, we have derived the score function
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|c)
of the generative classifier
p(\bm{x}|c)
defined by
Contrastive Guidance
. However, a remaining question is how to estimate the coefficient
\lambda_{t}
in Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
25
). In the expression of
\lambda_{t}
,
\gamma_{t}
is a hyperparameter, and
p(\bm{y}^{+})
and
p(\bm{y}^{-})
are priors of text prompts; therefore, to estimate
\lambda_{t}
, we only need to estimate
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
and
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{-})
. In the following, we show how to estimate
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
, and the estimate of
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{-})
can be derived similarly by replacing
\bm{y}^{+}
with
\bm{y}^{-}
.

Recall that
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
is the marginal distribution of
\bm{x}
of the following SDE, with initial state distribution
p_{T}(\bm{x})
:

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\Big[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\Big]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.

(28)

It has been shown that each SDE has a corresponding
probability flow ODE
with the same marginal density at each time
t

Maoutsa et al. 2020
;
Song et al. 2021b
. Specifically, probability flow ODE of the SDE in Eq. (
28
) is

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\Big[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\Big]\mathrm{d}t.

(29)

Since the SDE and the probability flow ODE have the same marginal distribution, we can estimate
p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
by estimating the marginal
p_{t}^{\text{ODE}}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
of the probability flow ODE in Eq. (
29
). Since
p_{T}^{\text{ODE}}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
is the isometric Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(\bm{x};\bm{0},\bm{I})
, the marginal
p_{t}^{\text{ODE}}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
is tractable by integrating the following ODE
Chen et al. 2018
;
Grathwohl et al. 2019
from
t
to
T
.

\mathrm{d}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}\\
\log p_{t}^{\text{ODE}}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\\
\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}\big(\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^{2}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\big)\Big)\end{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}t.

(30)

The generalist text-to-image diffusion model approximates
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})
with
\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})
, and Eq. (
30
) becomes

\mathrm{d}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}\\
\log p_{t}^{\text{ODE}}(\bm{x}|\bm{y}^{+})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^{2}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})\\
\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}\big(\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-\frac{1}{2}g(t)^{2}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})\big)\Big)\end{bmatrix}\mathrm{d}t.

(31)

Although the above estimation is tractable, we need to run it for all
t
’s, resulting in
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
complexity where
N
is the number of discretization steps.
Therefore, although
\lambda_{t}
can be estimated as we show above, this paper tries to avoid such estimation. To this effect, we assume that the temperature
\gamma_{t}
is adaptive such that
\lambda_{t}
does not depend on
\bm{x}
, giving us
\mathcal{O}(N)
complexity.

Generalist Only:

(34)

where
\varnothing
is the null token that stands for unconditional sampling, and
\tau_{t}
is a hyperparameter indicating the strength of guidance.

B.4
Error Analysis

Our text prompts cover objects, poses, environment, attributes, lighting, and sub-population. Among them, we find some failure cases produced by our
Contrastive Guidance
. In terms of objects, our method sometimes fails to model “with AR glasses” and “while another cat is behind it”. In terms of pose, as expected, it does not model ambiguous descriptions consistently, such as “looking left” and “looking right”. In terms of environment, unexpectedly, it fails to model “with storm”. In terms of attributes, it sometimes fails to model low-density attributes such as “closed eyes”, “red eyes”, “purple eyes”, and “asymmetric face”. In terms of lighting, it fails to model “underexposure” and “overexposure”. We also observe some unexpected biases. For instance, “wet cats” tend to be black; “cats in the dark” tend to be gray; “underexposure cats” tend to be black instead of being underexposure; “overexposure cats” tend to be white instead of being overexposure.

Method Details

Generalist Only:
Following the notations in the main text,
Generalist Only
means sampling from the reverse-time SDE:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\tilde{\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})}\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.\end{split}

(36)

where
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\tilde{\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})}
means the score function after classifier-free guidance
Ho and Salimans 2021
. It means that
Generalist Only
samples:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\Big(\tau_{t}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})-(\tau_{t}-1)\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\varnothing)\Big)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}},\end{split}

(37)

where
\varnothing
is the null token that stands for unconditional sampling, and
\tau_{t}
is a hyperparameter indicating the strength of guidance. We encourage readers to read
Ho and Salimans 2021
for more details of classifier-free guidance.

Contrastive Guidance:
When using
Contrastive Guidance
to guide the generalist, we sample from the reverse-time SDE:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\Big({\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\tilde{\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})}+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\lambda_{t}\big(\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})-\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{-})\big)}\Big)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.\end{split}

(38)

where
{\color[rgb]{0.27,0.42,0.81}\tilde{\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})}
means the score function after classifier-free guidance
Ho and Salimans 2021
. It means that
Contrastive Guidance
samples from the following reverse-time SDE:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bigg[\bm{f}(\bm{x},t)-g(t)^{2}\Big(\tau_{t}\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y})-(\tau_{t}-1)\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\varnothing)+{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}\lambda_{t}\big(\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{+})-\bm{s}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{x},t,\bm{y}^{-})\big)}\Big)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t+g(t)\mathrm{d}\bar{\bm{w}}.\end{split}

Appendix D
Guiding Image Editors (Section
5.3
): Experimental Details

For all methods, we follow the setting described in
Wu and la Torre 2022
to guarantee that the best hyperparameter combination is chosen for each method, instead of using a predefined hyperparameter combination. Specifically, for each test sample, we allow each method to enumerate some combinations of hyperparameters, each combination with multiple random trials (detailed below). To select the best hyperparameter combination for each sample, we used the directional CLIP score
\mathcal{S}_{\text{D-CLIP}}
as the criterion (higher is better). For each method, we used the DDIM sampler (
\eta=0.1
) with 100 steps.

SDEdit:
We enumerated the classifier-free guidance of the decoding step as
\{1,1.5,2,3,4,5\}
; we enumerated the encoding step as
\{15,20,25,30,40,50\}
; we ran
15
trials for each combination.

CycleDiffusion:
We set the classifier-free guidance of the encoding step as
1
; we enumerated the classifier-free guidance of the decoding step as
\{1,1.5,2,3,4,5\}
; we enumerated the encoding step as
\{15,20,25,30,40,50\}
; we ran
15
trials for each hyperparameter combination.

Contrastive Guidance for SDEdit:
Since
Contrastive Guidance
helps strengthen the indented editing, we used fewer combinations to enumerate. Specifically, we enumerated the classifier-free guidance of the decoding step as
\{1,1.5,2,3\}
; we enumerated the encoding step as
\{30,40,50\}
; we ran
15
trials for each combination. We set
\lambda_{t}=10
.

Contrastive Guidance for CycleDiffusion:
Since
Contrastive Guidance
helps strengthen the indented editing, we used fewer combinations to enumerate. We set the classifier-free guidance of the encoding step as
1
; we enumerated the classifier-free guidance of the decoding step as
\{1,1.5,2,3\}
; we enumerated the encoding step as
\{30,40,50\}
; we ran
15
trials for each hyperparameter combination. We set

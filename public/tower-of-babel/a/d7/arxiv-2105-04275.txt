Abstract
. In 1999 Khabibullin established the best estimate in Paley problem for a plurisubharmonic function u u of finite lower order 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1 0\leq\rho\leq 1 . For ρ > 1 \rho>1 obtaining a sharp estimate has remained an open question. In this work we solve this problem. We also provide some estimates for the types of the characteristic functions T ⁡ ( r , u ) T(r,u) and M ⁡ ( r , u ) M(r,u) .



Keywords:

plurisubharmonic function, Paley problem, Khabibullin’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Plurisubharmonic functions

A function
u
with values in
[-\infty,\infty)
is called plurisubharmonic (psh) if

(i)

u
is upper semicontinuous and
u\not\equiv-\infty
;

(ii)

for every
r\geq 0
and every
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
it holds that

(1)

u(z)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}u(z+re^{i\theta}w)\,d\theta.

The second condition is equivalent to saying that the mapping
\tau\mapsto u(z+\tau\,w)
is subharmonic in
\mathbb{C}
for any fixed
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The set of psh functions forms a convex cone in the vector space of semicontinuous functions. If
u_{1},u_{2},...
is a decreasing sequence of psh functions, then
u^{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}
is also a psh function (
[
11
, p.225-226]
).
For a function
u
let
M(r,u)\coloneqq\max\{u(z):|z|=r,z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
and
u^{+}(z)=\max\{u(z),0\}
. Define

(2)

T(r,u)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{+}(r\zeta)\,ds_{n}(\zeta)

to be the Nevalinna characteristic of
u
where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
s_{n}
is the rotation-invariant (
[
20
]
, p.12) positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that
s_{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n})=1
. We say a function
u
is of
lower order

\rho>0
if

(3)

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log T(r,u)}{\log r}=\rho.

1.2.
Paley problem

An important issue in the theory of functions of complex variables is estimation of the following quantity

(4)

\vartheta(u)\coloneqq\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u)}{T(r,u)}

for a function
u
defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n},n\geq 1
. Calculation of
\vartheta(u)
, when
u
is an entire function in
\mathbb{C}
of finite or infinite order was first considered by
[
18
, Paley-1932]
. The estimate
\vartheta(u)
was known to Paley for the range
0\leq\rho\leq 1/2
.
The case
\rho>1/2
remained an open question for many years and eventually it became known as Paley problem. This problem was conclusively proved in 1967 by Govorov
[
8
]
. Petrenko
[
19
]
extended Paley problem to meromorphic functions of finite lower order. An analogue estimation of
\vartheta(u)
for subharmonic functions
u
of finite lower order in
\mathbb{R}^{m},m\geq 2
, was obtained by
Dahlberg
[
5
]
. Generalizations for the estimation of (
4
) in terms of the
L^{p}
metric for
1\leq p<+\infty
for subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
were also obtained by Sodin
[
22
]
for
m=2
. Kondratyuk, Tarasyuk, and Vasyl’kiv
[
15
]
obtained respective generalizations for
m>2
. Eventually the problem of Paley was carried over to complex functions of several variables. Khabibullin considered it for meromorphic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n>1
and later posed the problem of finding the best analogue estimate of (
4
) for the class of psh functions and entire functions of severable variables
[
12
,
13
, Khabibullin]
. For
n>1
define

P_{n}(\rho)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle\frac{\pi\rho}{\sin(\pi\rho)}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\\
\pi\rho\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)&:\rho>\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right.

More precisely Khabibullin showed:

Theorem 1
.

[
13
, Theorem 1]

Let
u
be a psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of finite lower order
\rho
. Then

\displaystyle\vartheta(u)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle P_{n}(\rho)&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle 1\\
e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)&:\rho>\displaystyle 1\end{array}\right.

The estimate for
0\leq\rho\leq 1
is best possible.

1.3.
Khabibullin’s conjecture

In connection with Paley problem for psh functions, Khabibullin
[
14
]
has made the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1

(Khabibullin’s conjecture)
.

Let
S(t)
be a nonnegative increasing function on
[0,+\infty)
, and convex with respect to
\log t
. Further let
\rho>1
and
n\geq 2,n\in\mathbb{N}
. If

(7)

\int^{1}_{0}S(tx)(1-x^{2})^{n-2}\,dx\leq t^{\rho}\hskip 8.5359ptt\geq 0

then

(8)

\int^{+\infty}_{0}S(t)\frac{t^{2\rho-1}}{(1+t^{2\rho})^{2}}\,dt\leq\frac{\pi(n-1)}{2\rho}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big).

One can express Conjecture
1
in two other equivalent forms (
[
1
, Baladai–Khabibullin]
), one of which is in terms of nonnegative increasing functions
studied also by the author
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. As remarked by Khabibullin
[
14
]
the validation of this conjecture suffices to conclude that
P_{n}(\rho)
is a sharp estimate for
\vartheta(u)
when
\rho>1
.
However Sharipov
[
21
]
constructs the first counterexample to this hypothesis. Following Sharipov the author extended this negative result in
[
3
]
for
n=2
and any
\rho>1
. In
[
4
]
a counterexample was constructed for the general case
n\geq 2,\rho>1
. By Theorem
1

\vartheta(u)
is uniformly bounded by the constant
e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)
when
\rho>1
. Therefore there must exists a best possible estimate
K_{n}(\rho)\leq e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)
such that
\vartheta(u)\leq K_{n}(\rho)
uniformly for all psh
u
when
\rho>1
. We wish to call
K_{n}(\rho)
the
Khabibullin’s constant
. Now note that Conjecture
1
would be a true statement if for the second integral (
8
) instead the bound
\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{2\rho^{2}}K_{n}(\rho)
is substituted. It is the main goal of this work to establish the exact formula for
K_{n}(\rho)
. More precisely we show that

(9)

K_{n}(\rho)=P_{n}(\rho)+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big)

where
\Phi_{\rho,k}
are certain functions related to
\varphi_{\rho}(t)\coloneqq 1/(1+t^{\rho})
and
\tau_{i},i\in I
run over a certain subset of the zeros of the
n
-th derivative of
\varphi_{\rho}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Discussion

For clarity and completeness we follow the steps of Khabibullin
[
13
]
.
Let
v
be a psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
v(0)=0
and for some
\rho>0
the inequality holds

(10)

T(r,v)\leq r^{\rho}\quad\forall r>0.

Theorem
1
follows from the estimate (see
[
13
, Main Lemma]
)

\displaystyle M(1,v)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle P_{n}(\rho)&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle 1\\
e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)&:\rho>\displaystyle 1.\end{array}\right.

In view of (
2.1
) it is desirable to improve the upper bound on
M(1,v)
when
\rho>1
.
Without loss of generality assume that
v
is nonnegative.
For a fixed
\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
consider the subharmonic (with respect to

w\in\mathbb{C}
) slice function
v_{\zeta}(w)=v(\zeta w)
. By virtue of
[
13
, Lemma 1]
the following estimate is true

(13)

v_{\zeta}(1)\leq 4\mu^{2}\int^{+\infty}_{0}T(t,v_{\zeta})\varphi_{\mu}(t)\,dt\quad\text{where}\quad\mu=\max\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\rho\Big\},\quad\varphi_{\mu}(t)=\frac{t^{2\mu-1}}{(1+t^{2\mu})^{2}}

and

(14)

T(t,v_{\zeta})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{2\pi}_{0}v_{\zeta}(te^{i\theta})\,d\theta

is the Nevanlinna characteristic of the function
v_{\zeta}
. Inequality (
13
) is a direct application of a more general result about subharmonic functions
u
in
\mathbb{C}
for which their Laplacian
\Delta u
vanishes identically in a neighbourhood of the origin (see
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
).
Given an arbitrary
k
-dimensional complex subspace
L_{k}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
we let

T(r,v;L_{k})
be the Nevanlinna
characteristic of the restriction of
v
to
L_{k}
.

T(r,v;L_{k})
is nondecreasing and convex in
\log r
, therefore psh for each
k=1,2,...,n
.
By
[
13
, Lemma 2]
for
L_{k}\subset L_{k+1}

(15)

r\int^{r}_{0}T(t,v;L_{k})t^{2k-1}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2k}T(r,v;L_{k+1})r^{2(k+1)-1}\hskip 8.5359ptr>0.

An iterative application of (
15
) for a chain of complex subspaces
L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset...\subset L_{k}
for
k\leq n
, yields

(16)

\displaystyle t\int^{t}_{0}t_{k-1}\int^{t_{k-1}}_{0}t_{k-2}...\int^{t_{3}}_{0}t_{2}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}t_{1}T(t_{1},v;L_{1})

\displaystyle\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2}...\,dt_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{k-1}(k-1)!}T(t,v;L_{k})t^{2k-1}.

If
G
denotes the operator defined by

G[f](t)\coloneqq\int^{t}_{0}uf(u)\,du

then we have the identity

G^{k}[f](t)=\frac{1}{2^{k-1}(k-1)!}\int^{t}_{0}(t^{2}-u^{2})^{k-1}uf(u)\,du\quad k\geq 1.

We can write (
16
) equivalently as

(17)

G^{k-1}[T(\cdot,v;L_{1})](t)\leq\frac{1}{2^{k-1}(k-1)!}T(t,v;L_{k})t^{2k-2}.

By definition
T(t,v;L_{1})=T(t,v_{\zeta})
and
T(t,v;L_{n})=T(t,v)
. In the special case
k=n
we obtain

(18)

G^{n-1}[T(\cdot,v_{\zeta})](t)\leq\frac{1}{2^{n-1}(n-1)!}T(t,v)t^{2n-2}.

Lemma 1
.

[
17
, Proposition 5.1]

A real valued function
S(t)
, with
S(0)=0
, is increasing on
[0,+\infty)
and convex with respect to
\log t
if and only if there exists an increasing function
s(t)
on
[0,+\infty)
such that
S(t)
can be represented as

(19)

S(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\frac{s(x)}{x}\,dx\quad t\geq 0.

Proposition 1
.

Let
v
be a psh function of finite lower order
\rho>0
. Then there exists a nonnegative increasing function
s(\cdot,v_{\zeta}):[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

(20)

\int^{1}_{0}(1-u)^{n-1}\frac{s(tu,v_{\zeta})}{u}\,du\leq t^{\rho/2}

and

(21)

v_{\zeta}(1)\leq 2\rho\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t,v_{\zeta})}{t}\frac{1}{1+t^{\rho}}\,dt.

Proof.

Note that
T(t,v_{\zeta})
is nondecreasing on
[0,+\infty)
and convex in
\log t
. Then a direct application of Lemma
1
to
T(t,v_{\zeta})
yields

T(t,v_{\zeta})=\int^{t}_{0}\frac{s(x,v_{\zeta})}{x}\,dx\quad t\geq 0

for some increasing function
s(\cdot,v_{\zeta})
. Using this representation we get

\displaystyle G[T(\cdot,v_{\zeta})](t)=\int^{t}_{0}uT(u,v_{\zeta})\,du=\int^{t}_{0}u\Big(\int^{u}_{0}\frac{s(x,v_{\zeta})}{x}\,dx\Big)\,du=\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}(t^{2}-u^{2})\frac{s(u,v_{\zeta})}{u}\,du.

From (
18
) and a change of variables
ut\equiv u
implies

\int^{1}_{0}(1-u^{2})^{n-1}\frac{s(tu,v_{\zeta})}{u}\,du\leq T(t,v).

Substituting
u\equiv u^{2}
and
2s(t^{2}u^{2},v_{\zeta})\equiv s(tu,v_{\zeta})
gives the final form

\int^{1}_{0}(1-u)^{n-1}\frac{s(tu,v_{\zeta})}{u}\,du\leq T(t^{1/2},v)\leq t^{\rho/2}.

This proves inequality (
20
).
On the other hand an integration by parts

\int^{+\infty}_{0}T(t,v_{\zeta})\varphi_{\mu}(t)\,dt=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\Big(\int^{t}_{0}\frac{s(x,v_{\zeta})}{x}\,dx\Big)\varphi_{\mu}(t)\,dt=\frac{1}{2\mu}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t,v_{\zeta})}{t}\frac{1}{1+t^{2\mu}}\,dt

together with
2s(t^{2},v_{\zeta})\equiv s(t,v_{\zeta})

imply for (
13
) the equivalent estimate

v_{\zeta}(1)\leq 2\mu\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t,v_{\zeta})}{t}\frac{1}{1+t^{\mu}}\,dt.

Since
\rho>1
then
\mu=\rho
. This proves inequality (
21
).
∎

Let
\psh_{\rho}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the set of nonnegative functions
v
that are psh in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that inequality
T(r,v)\leq t^{\rho}
is satisfied. Let
\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the set of nonnegative and increasing functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying integral inequality (
20
). Note that

M(1,v)=\max\{v(z)\;:\;z\in\mathbb{C}^{n},|z|=1\}=\max\{v_{\zeta}(1)\;:\;\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}

then by Proposition
1
for any psh function
v
with lower order
\rho>0
it holds that

M(1,v)\leq 2\rho\sup_{v\in\psh_{\rho}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t,v_{\zeta})}{t}\frac{1}{1+t^{\rho}}\,dt=2\rho\sup_{s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t)}{t}\frac{1}{1+t^{\rho}}\,dt.

The last equality follows from the correspondence stated in Lemma
1
and the fact that to each increasing function
T(r)
that is convex in
\log r
one can associate a psh function
v
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
T(r)=T(r,v)
i.e. take
v(z)\coloneqq T(|z|)
.
For a given
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote the last integral by
J_{\rho}(s)
and
J(\rho)\coloneqq\sup_{s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}J_{\rho}(s)
.

3.
Boundedness of the functional
J_{\rho}

3.1.
An upper estimate for
J_{\rho}

Let
Q
be the operator acting on a integrable function
f
by the formula

Q[f](t)\coloneqq\int^{t}_{0}f(x)\,dx\quad t\geq 0.

To operator
Q
corresponds an operator
Q^{-1}
, the inverse operator of
Q
, which acts on a differentiable function
f
by the formula

Q^{-1}[f](t)\coloneqq\frac{df(t)}{dt}.

A repeated integration yields the well known Cauchy integral identity

Q^{k}[f](t)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int^{t}_{0}(t-x)^{k-1}f(x)\,dx\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

In view of this operator we have

(22)

\int^{1}_{0}(1-u)^{n-1}\frac{s(tu)}{u}\,du=\frac{1}{t^{n-1}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-u)^{n-1}\frac{s(x)}{x}\,du=\frac{(n-1)!}{t^{n-1}}Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t).

For
\rho>1
let
\varphi_{\rho}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the function

(23)

\varphi_{\rho}(t)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1+t^{\rho}}\quad t\geq 0.

Lemma 2
.

For all
k=0,1,2,...
the followings hold

(24)

\displaystyle|(Q^{-1})^{k}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)|=O(t^{\rho-k})\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 0

(25)

\displaystyle|(Q^{-1})^{k}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)|=O(t^{-\rho-k})\quad\text{as}\quad t\to+\infty

Proof.

Relation (
24
) follows from the power series expansion
\varphi_{\rho}(t)=1-t^{\rho}+t^{2\rho}-...
as
t\to 0
. Similarly (
25
) follows from the expansion
\varphi_{\rho}(t)=t^{-\rho}-t^{-2\rho}+...
as
t\to+\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For any
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
we have the inequalities

(26)

Q^{k+1}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)\leq\frac{1}{k!}t^{\rho/2+k}\quad k=0,1,2,...

Proof.

Follows immediately from (
22
) and Proposition
1
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For all
k=0,1,2,...
the following limits hold

(27)

\lim_{t\to 0,+\infty}|(Q^{-1})^{k}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)|\cdot Q^{k+1}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)=0

Proof.

By Lemma
3
it follows

Q^{k+1}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)=O(t^{\rho/2+k})

By Lemma
2

\displaystyle|(Q^{-1})^{k}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)|\cdot Q^{k+1}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle O(t^{3\rho/2})&:\text{as}\quad t\to 0\\
O(t^{-\rho/2})&:\text{as}\quad t\to+\infty\end{array}\right.

The limits in (
27
) follow immediately for all
k=0,1,2,...
.
∎

Definition 1
.

For
\rho>1
let
\psi_{\rho}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

(30)

\psi_{\rho}(t)\coloneqq(-1)^{n}(Q^{-1})^{n}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)\quad t\geq 0.

Furthermore let
D_{+}\coloneqq\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\psi_{\rho}(t)\geq 0\}
and
D_{-}\coloneqq\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\psi_{\rho}(t)<0\}
.

Proposition 2
.

If
\rho>1
then for any
n\geq 2

\psi_{\rho}(t)
vanishes at most on a finite number of points. In particular
\psi_{\rho}(t)>0
for all large enough
t>0
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
the equation
(Q^{-1})^{n}[\varphi_{\rho}](\tau)=0
has a solution for all
n\geq 2
and moreover there are at most a finite number of them. If
\sign(\cdot)
denotes the usual sign function taking value
1
on positive numbers,
-1
on negative numbers and
0
otherwise, then the set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}
on which
\sign(Q^{-1})^{n}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)=(-1)^{n+1}
can be expressed as a finite union of open bounded intervals in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In particular
\sign(Q^{-1})^{n}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)=(-1)^{n}
for all sufficiently large
t
. By Definition
1
it follows that
\psi_{\rho}
also vanishes at most on a finite number of points in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

\sign\psi_{\rho}(t)=(-1)^{n}\sign(Q^{-1})^{n}[\varphi_{\rho}](t)=(-1)^{n}\cdot(-1)^{n+1}=-1\quad\forall t\in\Omega.

In particular
D_{-}=\Omega
and
\psi_{\rho}(t)>0
for all large enough
t
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that from the arguments in the proof of the last proposition it follows that
D_{-}
is a bounded set, in particular the closure of
D_{-}
is compact.

Definition 2
.

Let
0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq...\leq\tau_{m}<+\infty
be an enumeration of the zeros of
\psi_{\rho}
. Define the index set
I\coloneqq\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\psi_{\rho}(t)<0,\forall t\in(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i})\}
. Denote by

(31)

\Phi_{\rho,k}(t)\coloneqq(-1)^{n+k}\frac{\Gamma(\rho/2+n)}{\Gamma(n)\Gamma(\rho/2+n-k)}t^{\rho/2+n-k-1}\varphi^{(n-k)}_{\rho}(t)\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,1,...,n-1

and

(32)

\Phi_{\rho,n}(t)\coloneqq\frac{\Gamma(\rho/2+n)}{\Gamma(n)\Gamma(\rho/2+1)}\arctan t^{\rho}.

Proposition 3
.

For any
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
the following inequality holds:

(33)

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(s)\leq\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}(\rho)}{2\rho}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big).

Proof.

Let
\rho>1
then for any
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
by (
30
) and Lemma
4
we have

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(s)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

Since
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
by Lemma
3
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{D_{+}}Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t)\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt\leq\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D_{+}}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

The last integral can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D_{+}}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D_{-}}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

An integration by parts and Lemma
2
imply

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{2}\prod^{n-1}_{k=1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{t^{\rho/2-1}}{1+t^{\rho}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\prod^{n-1}_{k=1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{dt^{\rho/2}}{1+t^{\rho}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\prod^{n-1}_{k=1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)=\frac{P_{n}(\rho)}{2\rho}.

Successive integration by parts implies the following identity in indefinite form

\int t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(\rho/2+n)}{\Gamma(\rho/2+1)}\arctan t^{\rho}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+k}\frac{\Gamma(\rho/2+n)}{\Gamma(\rho/2+n-k)}t^{\rho/2+n-k-1}\varphi^{(n-k)}_{\rho}(t).

Then using (
31
) and (
32
) we can write

\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D_{-}}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})\Big).

Altogether we have for
\rho>1
that

J_{\rho}(s)\leq\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{D_{+}}t^{\rho/2+n-1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\frac{P_{n}(\rho)}{2\rho}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

By a theorem of the author
[
4
, Theorem 1]
there exists
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
such that
J_{\rho}(s)>P_{n}(\rho)/2\rho
. Therefore it follows that

\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big)>0.

Proposition 4
.

There does not exist
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying
J_{\rho}(s)=J(\rho)
whenever
\rho>1
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is some
s\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
J_{\rho}(s)=J(\rho)
. Since
s(x)
is increasing then
by Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem
[
9
, Theorem 9.3.1]

s
is differentiable almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Let
W
be the set of points for which
s
is not differentiable.
Define the function

f(t)=Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s(x)}{x}\Big](t),\;t\geq 0.

By Proposition
2
for any
n\geq 2
the set
D_{-}\neq\emptyset
whenever
\rho>1
and that
\psi_{\rho}(t)
vanishes at most on a finite number of points
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
let

f_{0}
be defined piecewise as

\displaystyle f_{0}(t)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}f(t)(1-\varepsilon\displaystyle\eta(t))&:t\in D_{-}\\
f(t)&:t\in D_{+}\end{array}\right.

where
\eta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is given by

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\sum_{i\in I}\eta_{i}(t)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{i}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\Big[\cos\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{i-1}}\Big)\cos\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{i}}\Big)\Big]^{2n}&:t\in(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i}]\\
0&:t\notin(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i}]\end{array}\right.

with the agreement that if
1\in I
then
\eta_{1}(t)=\cos^{2n}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{1}}\Big)
for all
t\in(0,\tau_{1}]
and zero otherwise. Here the index set
I
and the finite sequence
(\tau_{i})
is given by Definition
2
.
Note that
\eta
is smooth, nonnegative with support in
D_{-}
and uniformly bounded above by one.
Moreover by definition of
\eta
the following vanishing limits are fulfilled

(38)

\lim_{t\to\tau_{i}^{-}}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}[f(t)\eta(t)]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\tau_{i-1}^{+}}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}[f(t)\eta(t)]=0

for
k=1,...,n+1
and for all
\tau_{i}\in\psi^{-1}_{\rho}(0)
. To
f_{0}
we can associate
s_{0}
defined piecewise

\displaystyle s_{0}(t)=t(Q^{-1})^{n}[f_{0}](t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}s(t)-\displaystyle\varepsilon t(Q^{-1})^{n}\displaystyle[f(\cdot)\eta(\cdot)](t)&:t\in D_{-}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus W)\\
s(t)&:t\in D_{+}\cap(\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus W)\end{array}\right.

In view of
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
it is possible to find an
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
s_{0},s^{\prime}_{0}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus W
. And on
W
we can arrange the values of
s_{0}
so that it is nonnegative and preserves monotonicity on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence
s_{0}
is a nonnegative increasing function.
By (
3.1
) and Lemma
3
we have

Q^{n}\Big[\frac{s_{0}(x)}{x}\Big](t)=f_{0}(t)\leq\frac{t^{\rho/2+n-1}}{(n-1)!}

therefore
s_{0}\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. On the other hand since
W
is at most countable, hence of Lebesgue measure zero, then
W
does not contribute to the integral
J_{\rho}(s_{0})
. This in turn implies

J_{\rho}(s_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus W}\frac{s_{0}(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s_{0}(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

which together with representation (
3.1
) yields

J_{\rho}(s_{0})=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt-\varepsilon\int_{D_{-}}(Q^{-1})^{n}\displaystyle[f(\cdot)\eta(\cdot)](t)\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

By Proposition
2
it follows that
D_{-}=\bigcup_{i\in I}(\tau_{i-1},\tau_{i})
. So we can write
\int_{D_{-}}=\sum_{i\in I}\int^{\tau_{i}}_{\tau_{i-1}}
. Integrating by parts in each term of this sum and using the vanishing limits (
38
) implies

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(s_{0})=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt-\varepsilon\int_{D_{-}}f(t)

\displaystyle\eta(t)\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle>\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s(t)}{t}\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=J_{\rho}(s).

However this contradicts that
J_{\rho}(s)
is maximal.
∎

As an immediate corollary we obtain:

Corollary 1
.

There is no
v\in\psh_{\rho}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
,
\rho>1
such that
M(1,v)=J(\rho)
.

3.2.
Constructing a maximizing sequence

In view of Proposition
4
it is then desirable to construct a sequence
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\rho}(s_{k})=J(\rho)
. The method of proof in the last proposition provides us with a concrete method of constructing such a maximizing sequence. Let
s_{0}\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and denote by
f_{0}(t)\coloneqq Q^{n}[s_{0}(x)/x](t)
. By
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can find a positive constant
\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
the sequence of functions

\displaystyle f_{k}(t)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}f_{k-1}(t)&:t\in D_{+}\\
(1-\varepsilon_{k}\eta(t))f_{k-1}(t)&:t\in D_{-}.\end{array}\right.

yields a corresponding sequence
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
by the formula
s_{k}(t)\coloneqq t(Q^{-1})^{n}[f_{k}](t)
. Here
\eta
is the function given by (
3.1
). We start with

(43)

s_{0}(t)\coloneqq\frac{\rho}{2}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2k}\Big)\,t^{\rho/2},\quad t\geq 0.

It is evident that
s_{0}\in\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. To
s_{0}
corresponds

(44)

f_{0}(t)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\,t^{\rho/2+n-1},\quad t\geq 0.

By the recursion formula
f_{k}(t)\coloneqq(1-\varepsilon_{k}\eta(t))f_{k-1}(t)
we obtain

(45)

f_{k}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon_{i}\,\eta(t))\cdot f_{0}(t),\quad t\in D_{-},\,k=1,2,3,...

and correspondingly

(46)

s_{k}(t)=t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon_{i}\,\eta)\cdot f_{0}\Big](t),\quad t\in D_{-},\,k=1,2,3,...

Expanding the product
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon_{i}\,\eta)
and using linearity of the operator
Q^{-1}
we obtain

(47)

\displaystyle s_{k}(t)=s_{0}(t)-t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta\,f_{0}](t)\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}+t\,

\displaystyle(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta^{2}\,f_{0}](t)\,\sum_{i<j}\varepsilon_{i}\,\varepsilon_{j}+...

\displaystyle...+(-1)^{k}t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta^{k}\,f_{0}](t)\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}.

Next we claim that for sufficiently large
k
we can take
\varepsilon_{k}\sim\alpha/k
for some constant
\alpha>0
. From (
47
) the condition
s_{k}\geq 0
is equivalent to the inequality

(48)

\displaystyle 1\geq\frac{t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta\,f_{0}](t)}{s_{0}(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}-

\displaystyle\frac{t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta^{2}\,f_{0}](t)}{s_{0}(t)}\,\sum_{i<j}\varepsilon_{i}\,\varepsilon_{j}+...

\displaystyle...+(-1)^{k+1}\frac{t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta^{k}\,f_{0}](t)}{s_{0}(t)}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}.

By definitions of
f_{0}
and
s_{0}
it follows that each ratio function in the last expression is continuous. As a consequence by Remark
2
the absolute value of each one attains a certain maximum on the closure of
D_{-}
. Additionally for sufficiently large
k
the last term

\Big|\frac{t\,(Q^{-1})^{n}[\eta^{k}\,f_{0}](t)}{s_{0}(t)}\Big|\sim C\,\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}

dominates the whole sum. Here
C>0
is a certain constant possibly depending on
f_{0},s_{0},\eta
and
n
. Without loss of generality assume that
\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{k-1}\leq...\leq\varepsilon_{1}
then for large
k

1\geq C\,\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\geq C\,\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\,(\varepsilon_{k})^{k}

whenever
\varepsilon_{k}\sim\alpha/k
for a certain constant
\alpha>0
. Hence such a choice for
\varepsilon_{k}
is possible. In particular we get that
\sum_{k\geq k^{\prime}}^{N}\varepsilon_{k}\to+\infty
as
N\uparrow+\infty
for
k^{\prime}
itself sufficiently large. Then
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{k}\to+\infty
as
N\uparrow+\infty
. By standard arguments from the theory of infinite series it follows that
\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon_{k}\eta(t))\to 0
as
k\uparrow+\infty
for each
t\in D_{-}
. In particular
\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(t)=0
for every
t\in D_{-}
.
These observations help us show the following result:

Proposition 5
.

Let
s_{0},f_{0}
be given by (
43
) and (
44
) respectively. Then the sequence
(s_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\inc_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as constructed above is a maximizing sequence i.e.
\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\rho}(s_{k})=J(\rho)
.
In particular it holds that

(49)

J(\rho)=\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}(\rho)}{2\rho}+\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big).

Proof.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle J_{\rho}(s_{k})=\int^{+\infty}_{0}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{+}}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt+\int_{D_{-}}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{+}}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt+\int_{D_{-}}(Q^{-1})^{n}[f_{k}](t)\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{+}}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt+\int_{D_{-}}f_{k}(t)\,\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

On the other hand by construction
f_{0}\geq f_{1}\geq f_{2}\geq...
, implying in particular that

\int_{D_{-}}f_{k}(t)\,|\psi_{\rho}(t)|\,dt\leq\int_{D_{-}}f_{0}(t)\,|\psi_{\rho}(t)|\,dt<\int_{0}^{+\infty}f_{0}(t)\,|\psi_{\rho}(t)|\,dt<+\infty.

By Lebesgue’s monotone convergence theorem
[
9
, Corollary 6.8.2]
it follows that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{D_{-}}f_{k}(t)\,\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\int_{D_{-}}\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(t)\,\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=0.

Notice that by construction
s_{k}=s_{0}
on
D_{+}
, therefore we get

\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\rho}(s_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{D_{+}}\frac{s_{k}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt+\int_{D_{-}}f_{k}(t)\,\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=\int_{D_{+}}\frac{s_{0}(t)}{t}\,\varphi_{\rho}(t)\,dt.

The last integral was evaluated in Proposition
3
and equals the estimate in (
33
). Because
\lim_{k\to+\infty}J_{\rho}(s_{k})\leq J(\rho)
then by Proposition
3
the equality (
49
) follows.

∎

Remark 3
.

By (
2.1
) we have the estimate
M(1,v)\leq e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)
for every
n\geq 2
and
\rho>1
. On the other hand by Proposition
5
we obtain
M(1,v)\leq 2\rho\,J(\rho)
where
J(\rho)
is given by the formula (
49
). Since the later is achieved as the limit of a maximizing sequence then
2\rho\,J(\rho)\leq e^{n-1}P_{n}(\rho)
must hold i.e. we have an improvement of the estimate when
\rho>1
.

4.
Main Results

4.1.
Pólya peaks

A main ingredient in the proof of the main result is the utilization of the so called Pólya peaks.
Let
\rho>0
. A sequence
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},...
is said to be a sequence of Pólya peaks for a function
T(r)
if there exist sequences of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{k}),(\xi_{k}),(a_{k}),(A_{k})
satisfying

(50)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\varepsilon_{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\xi_{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}a_{k}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\to+\infty}a_{k}r_{k}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}A_{k}=+\infty

such that the inequalities
r_{k}a_{k}\leq r\leq r_{k}A_{k}
imply

(51)

T(r)\leq(1+\xi_{k})\Big(\frac{r}{r_{k}}\Big)^{\rho+\varepsilon_{k}}T(r_{k}).

Edrei
[
6
]
proved that a sequence of Pólya peaks for
T(r)
always exists whenever
T(r),r\geq 1
is unbounded, nondecreasing, nonnegative continuous function satisfying

(52)

\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log T(r)}{\log r}=\rho<+\infty.

For a nonnegative psh function
u
of lower order
\rho
its Nevalinna characteristic
T(r,u)
(possibly unbounded) is nondecreasing, nonnegative continuous function of
r\geq 0
. In view of definition (
3
) as an immediate corollary we have:

Corollary 2
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of lower order
\rho
, then
T(r,u)
has a sequence of Pólya peaks of order
\rho
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of lower order
\rho
and let
(r_{k})
be the sequence of Pólya peaks for
T(r,u)
. Define

(53)

u_{k}(z)\coloneqq\frac{u(r_{k}z)}{T(r_{k},u)}.

Then
(u_{k})
is a sequence of psh functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

First note that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function
u_{k}
is psh in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
since
T(r_{k},u)>0
and
u(r_{k}z)
is psh in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For a given nonnegative function
v
subharmonic in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the following inequality holds true
M(r,v)\leq 3\cdot 2^{2n-2}\,T(2r,v)
(see
[
13
, (2.2)]
). Because a psh function is subharmonic then applying this to each
u_{k}
yields

0\leq u_{k}(z)=\frac{u(r_{k}z)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq\frac{M(r_{k}\,r,u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq 3\cdot 2^{2n-2}\frac{T(2r_{k}\,r,u)}{T(r_{k},u)},\quad|z|\leq r.

In view of inequality (
51
) for
a_{k}\leq 2r\leq A_{k}
we obtain

\frac{T(2r_{k}\,r,u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq(1+\xi_{k})r^{\rho+\varepsilon_{k}}\leq(1+\xi)\,r^{\rho+\varepsilon}

for some
\xi,\varepsilon>0
. Consequently for
a_{k}\leq 2|z|\leq A_{k}
we get

0\leq u_{k}(z)\leq 3\cdot 2^{2n-2}(1+\xi)\,r^{\rho+\varepsilon}.

When
2|z|\leq a_{k}
then again from definition of
u_{k}
it follows that

0\leq u_{k}(z)\leq\frac{M(a_{k}\,r_{k}/2,u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq\frac{M(\,r_{k}/2,u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq 3\cdot 2^{2n-2}\quad\text{for sufficiently large}\,k.

In both situations
u_{k}
is uniformly bounded on the ball
\mathbb{B}_{k}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\;:\;|z|\leq A_{k}/2\}
for sufficiently large
k
. Now note that for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
we have
K\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{k}
for sufficiently large
k
. This completes the proof.
∎

An important part in the proof of the theorem plays the following result about sequences of subharmonic functions that are uniformly bounded on compact sets. While its original statement
[
10
, Theorem 4.1.9]
is for subharmonic function in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the arguments extend equally also for subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, by identifying
\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Lemma 6
.

[
10
, Theorem 4.1.9]

Let
L^{1}_{\loc}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the space of locally integrable functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. and let
(v_{k})
be a sequence of subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
v_{k}
does not converge to
-\infty
uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
then there exists a subsequence
(v_{n_{k}})
convergent to a subharmonic function
v
in
L^{1}_{\loc}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Moreover the following inequality holds

(54)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}v_{n_{k}}(z)\leq v(z)\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

and the two sides equal and finite almost everywhere.

4.2.
Main Theorems

Theorem 2
.

Let
u:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be a psh function of finite lower order
\rho>1
then
\vartheta(u)\leq 2\rho\,J(\rho)
where
J(\rho)
is given by (
49
). In particular
K_{n}(\rho)=2\rho\,J(\rho)
.

Proof.

We follow the lines in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 1]
. Let
u:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to[-\infty,+\infty)
be a psh function of finite lower order
\rho>1
. Assume without loss of generality that
u\geq 0
. If
T(r,u)
is the Nevanlinna characteristic of
u
then by Corollary
2
it follows that
T(r,u)
has a sequence of Pólya peaks
(r_{k})
of order
\rho
. Let
(u_{k})
be defined as in (
53
). By Lemma
5
the sequence
(u_{k})
is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then by Lemma
6
there is a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})
which converges to a nonnegative subharmonic function
v
in
L^{1}_{\loc}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Moreover
\limsup_{k\to+\infty}u_{n_{k}}(z)\leq v(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If
\limsup_{k\to+\infty}u_{n_{k}}(z)\coloneqq w(z)
we let
w^{*}(z)\coloneqq\limsup_{y\to z}w(y)
denote the upper semicontinuous regularization of
w(z)
. It can easily be checked that
w^{*}(z)
is upper semicontinuous and
w^{*}
is the least function with the property
w^{*}\geq w
. On the other hand
v
is subharmonic, hence upper semicontinuous. We then have the inequalities

(55)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}u_{n_{k}}(z)=w(z)\leq w^{*}(z)\leq v(z)=v^{*}(z).

Convergence of
(u_{n_{k}})
to
v
in
L^{1}
norm implies that
w(z)
and
v(z)
coincide almost everywhere in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Indeed let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a compact set. From the triangle inequality we have

0\leq\int_{K}|w(z)-v(z)|\,d\lambda(z)\leq\int_{K}|w(z)-u_{n_{k}}(z)|\,d\lambda(z)+\int_{K}|u_{n_{k}}(z)-v(z)|\,d\lambda(z)

where
\,d\lambda
is the usual (real) Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Passing in the limit we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K}|w(z)-v(z)|\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{K}|w(z)-u_{n_{k}}(z)|\,d\lambda+\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{K}|u_{n_{k}}(z)-v(z)|\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\int_{K}|w(z)-u_{n_{k}}(z)|\,d\lambda\leq\int_{K}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}|w(z)-u_{n_{k}}(z)|\,d\lambda=0

where the passage of the limit inside the integral is justified by the (reversed) Fatou’s Lemma
[
9
, Lemma 6.8.5]
since
|w(z)-u_{n_{k}}(z)|\leq|w(z)|+C_{K}\leq 2C_{K}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a certain constant
C_{K}
possibly dependent on
K
. Therefore
w(z)=v(z)
almost everywhere on
K
. Since
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is arbitrary then
w(z)=v(z)
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular inequality (
55
) yields
w^{*}(z)=v(z)
almost everywhere.

Now consider any complex line
a+zb\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
where
a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
are fixed and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. By definition of plurisubharmonicity it follows
u_{n_{k}}(a+zb)
is subharmonic in
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Denote
w_{m}(z)\coloneqq\sup_{k\geq m}u_{n_{k}}(a+zb)
for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
(w_{m})
is a nonincreasing sequence of functions and
\lim_{m}w_{m}(z)=w(a+zb)
for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
w^{*}_{m}
denote the upper semicontinuous regularization of
w_{m}
. By
[
11
, Theorem 3.2.2]

w^{*}_{m}
is subharmonic. On the other hand
w^{*}_{m}
is the least function for which
w^{*}_{m}\geq w_{m}
implying
\lim_{m}w^{*}_{m}(z)=w^{*}(a+zb)
. Because
(w^{*}_{m})
is a decreasing sequence of subharmonic functions then the limit
w^{*}(a+zb)
is subharmonic in
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Since the complex line
a+zb\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is arbitrary then by definition
w^{*}
is psh in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. On the other hand two subharmonic functions that coincide almost everywhere are the same.
Therefore
v
is psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By
[
15
, Theorem 4]
the sequence
(u_{n_{k}})
can be chosen so that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}|u_{n_{k}}(r\,\zeta)-v(r\,\zeta)|\,ds_{n}(\zeta)=0,\quad r>0.

This in turn implies

\displaystyle T(r,v)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}v(r\,\zeta)\,ds_{n}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}u_{n_{k}}(r\,\zeta)\,ds_{n}(\zeta)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}\frac{u(r_{n_{k}}r\,\zeta)}{T(r_{n_{k}},u)}\,ds_{n}(\zeta)+o(1)=\frac{T(r_{n_{k}}r,u)}{T(r_{n_{k}},u)}+o(1),\quad\text{as}\;k\uparrow+\infty.

By (
51
) it follows
T(r,v)\leq r^{\rho}
and thus
v\in\psh_{\rho}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Moreover by means of the Poisson kernel (see
[
13
, Theorem 1]
) it is shown that

(56)

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}M(1,u_{k})\leq M(1,v).

Definition of
(u_{k})
implies

\displaystyle M(1,u_{k})=M\Big(1,\frac{u(r_{k}z)}{T(r_{k},u)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{u(r_{k}z)}{T(r_{k},u)}\;:\;|z|=1,z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T(r_{k},u)}\max\{u(z)\;:\;|z|=r_{k},z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}=\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{T(r_{k},u)}.

Hence by (
56
)

(57)

\vartheta(u)=\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u)}{T(r,u)}\leq\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq M(1,v).

On the other hand
M(1,v)\leq 2\rho J(\rho)
. By Proposition
3
it follows that

\vartheta(u)\leq P_{n}(\rho)+2\rho\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i-1})-\Phi_{\rho,k}(\tau_{i})\Big).

Now we demonstrate that this estimate for
\vartheta(u)
is nonimprovable i.e.
K_{n}(\rho)=2\rho\,J(\rho)
. It suffices to show that there is some psh function
u_{0}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of finite lower order
\rho>1
such that all inequalities in (
57
) hold with equality. To this end for
\rho>1
let

(58)

E_{\rho}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{\Gamma(1+j/\rho)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Define
u_{0}(z):=\log|E_{\rho}(z_{1})|
where
z:=(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since
E_{\rho}
is an entire function of order
\rho
it can be shown by direct calculations that
u_{0}
is a psh function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of finite lower order
\rho
. The following asymptotic for the characteristic functions of
u_{0}
hold

(59)

\displaystyle M(r,u_{0})=r^{\rho}+O(1)\quad\text{and}\quad T(r,u_{0})=P^{-1}_{n}(\rho)\,r^{\rho}+O(1).

This implies that

\vartheta(u_{0})=\liminf_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u_{0})}{T(r,u_{0})}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u_{0})}{T(r,u_{0})}=P_{n}(\rho)

and in particular for any Pólya peaks
(r_{k})
of
T(r,u_{0})
it holds that

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{M(r_{k},u_{0})}{T(r_{k},u_{0})}=\vartheta(u_{0}).

By construction of the sequence
(u_{0,k})
from a given Pólya peaks
(r_{k})
we have

u_{0,k}(z)=\frac{u_{0}(r_{k}z)}{T(r_{k},u_{0})}\leq\frac{M(r_{k}r,u_{0})}{T(r_{k},u_{0})},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\;\text{with}\;|z|=r.

By the asymptotic formulae (
59
) we obtain that

u_{0,k}(z)\leq\frac{r_{k}^{\rho}\,r^{\rho}+O(1)}{P^{-1}_{n}(\rho)\,r_{k}^{\rho}+O(1)}

and consequently
\limsup_{k\to+\infty}u_{0,k}(z)\leq P_{n}(\rho)\,r^{\rho}
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
|z|=r
.
From the earlier arguments we have shown that
\limsup_{y\to z}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}u_{0,k}(y)=v_{0}(z)
for some psh function
v_{0}\in\psh_{\rho}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. In particular we obtain that
v_{0}(z)\leq P_{n}(\rho)\,|z|^{\rho}
and consequently
M(1,v_{0})\leq P_{n}(\rho)\,M(1,|z|^{\rho})=P_{n}(\rho)
. Therefore
\vartheta(u_{0})=M(1,v_{0})
.
∎

For a plurisubharmonic function
u
of finite lower order
\rho
let
\rho(r)
denote its refined order i.e.
\lim_{r\to+\infty}\rho(r)=\rho
and
\lim_{r\to+\infty}r\rho^{\prime}(r)\ln r=0
(see
[
16
, B. Ja. Levin, p.32]
). Let
\sigma_{T}
and
\sigma_{M}
be types of the characteristic functions
T(r,u)
and
M(r,u)
respectively with respect to the refined order
\rho(r)
.
Then we obtain the following comparison result.

Theorem 3
.

The following estimates (nonimprovable) hold true

\displaystyle\sigma_{M}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle P_{n}(\rho)\,\sigma_{T}&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle 1\\
K_{n}(\rho)\,\sigma_{T}&:\rho>\displaystyle 1.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By definition of the type
\sigma
of a given function
f
with respect
\rho(r)
we have

\sigma_{M}\coloneqq\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u)}{r^{\rho(r)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{T}\coloneqq\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{T(r,u)}{r^{\rho(r)}}.

By Theorem
2
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
k
the following inequality

\displaystyle\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{T(r_{k},u)}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle P_{n}(\rho)+\varepsilon&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle 1\\
K_{n}(\rho)+\varepsilon&:\rho>\displaystyle 1\end{array}\right.

holds true where
(r_{k})
is the Pólya peaks of
T(r,u)
. Writing

\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{T(r_{k},u)}=\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}\cdot\frac{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}{T(r_{k},u)}

and passing in the limit superior in (
4.2
) yield

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle P_{n}(\rho)\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{T(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}&:0\leq\rho\leq\displaystyle 1\\
K_{n}(\rho)\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{T(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}&:\rho>\displaystyle 1.\end{array}\right.

On the other hand

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{T(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}=\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{T(r,u)}{r^{\rho(r)}}=\sigma_{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{M(r_{k},u)}{r_{k}^{\rho(r_{k})}}=\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{M(r,u)}{r^{\rho(r)}}=\sigma_{M}.

∎

4.3.
Calculations for
n=2

For small values of
n
it is possible to get explicit expression for Khabibullin’s constant
K_{n}(\rho)
. In particular when
n=2
routine calculations show that

\psi_{\rho}(t)=\frac{\rho t^{\rho-2}((\rho+1)t^{\rho}-(\rho-1))}{(1+t^{\rho})^{3}}

and evidently
\psi_{\rho}(t)<0
for
t\in[0,\tau)
and
\psi_{\rho}(t)>0
for
t>\tau
where
\tau
is the non-zero solution of
\psi_{\rho}(t)=0
given by the formula

\tau(\rho)=\Big(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}\Big)^{1/\rho}.

In this case
D_{-}=(0,\tau)
and from the general formula for the indefinite integral we obtain

\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{\rho/2+1}\psi_{\rho}(t)\,dt=-\frac{(\rho+1)^{2}}{4\rho}\Big(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}\Big)^{3/2}-\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\Big[\frac{\rho+1}{2\rho}\Big(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}\Big)^{1/2}-\arctan{\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}}\Big]

whenever
\rho>1
. Hence for
n=2
and
\rho>1
we have

(64)

K_{2}(\rho)=\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{(\rho+1)^{2}}{4\rho}\Big(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}\Big)^{3/2}+\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\Big[\frac{\rho+1}{2\rho}\Big(\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}\Big)^{1/2}-\arctan{\frac{\rho-1}{\rho+1}}\Big].

Note that
\lim_{\rho\downarrow 1}K_{2}(\rho)=3\pi/4
coincides with the sharp estimate when
\rho=1
. Hence
K_{2}(\rho)
is continuous at
\rho=1
. Moreover we get an asymptotic behaviour of
K_{2}(\rho)
given by

\lim_{\rho\uparrow+\infty}\frac{K_{2}(\rho)}{\rho}=\frac{4+\pi}{8}.

Again from the asymptotic analysis we obtain

K_{2}(\rho)=\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\frac{\pi}{2}+o(\rho)\;\text{as}\;\rho\downarrow 1

and

K_{2}(\rho)=\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\rho}{4}+\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\,\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)<2\,\Big(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\Big)\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\;\text{as}\;\rho\uparrow+\infty.

In both case as
\rho
approaches
1
from above or as
\rho
gets arbitrarily large the estimate shows once more that it is smaller then
e\,P_{2}(\rho)
.
Calculating
K_{n}(\rho)
explicitly when
n\geq 3
gets complicated because of the form of
\psi_{\rho}(t)
. It is known that

\psi_{\rho}(t)=(-1)^{n}\frac{p(t^{\rho})}{t^{n}(1+t^{\rho})^{n+1}}\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq 0,n\geq 2

where
p
is a polynomial in
t^{\rho}
of the form

p(x)=c_{n,n-1}x^{n}+c_{n,n-2}x^{n-1}+...+c_{n,1}x^{2}+c_{n,0}x
.
The coefficients
c_{n,k}
are polynomials in
\rho
satisfying a certain recurrence relation (see
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
) which can prove to be helpful in studying the zeros of
p(x)
. These in turn determine the zeros
\tau
of the function
\psi_{\rho}
.

4.4.
Comparing with other estimates for subharmonic functions

Because the set of psh functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a proper subset of the more general class of subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is of interest to compare the estimate
K_{n}(\rho)
for psh functions of lower order
\rho>1
with the estimate for subharmonic functions of the same lower order. But
K_{n}(\rho)
is sharp for the class of psh functions, therefore it must be the case that
K_{n}(\rho)
is no greater than any nonimprovable estimate for subharmonic functions. Indeed we verify that this is the case by making explicit comparison with the estimate obtained by Dahlberg
[
5
]
for subharmonic functions in
\mathbb{R}^{m},m\geq 3
.
For
\rho>0
let the Gegenbauer functions
C_{\rho}^{\gamma}
be given as the solutions of the differential equation

(65)

(1-x^{2})\,\frac{d^{2}f}{dx^{2}}-(2\gamma+1)x\,\frac{df}{dx}+\rho(\rho+2\gamma)f=0,\quad-1<x<1

with the normalization condition

(66)

\lim_{x\uparrow 1}C^{\gamma}_{\rho}(x)=C_{\rho}^{\gamma}(1)=\frac{\Gamma(\rho+2\gamma)}{\Gamma(2\gamma)\,\Gamma(\rho+1)}.

Let
a_{\rho}\coloneqq\sup\{t\;:\;C^{\frac{m-2}{2}}_{\rho}(t)=0\}
and define
u_{\rho}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m},m\geq 3
by the formula:

\displaystyle u_{\rho}(x)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0&:x_{1}\leq a_{\rho}\,r\\
r^{\rho}\,C^{\frac{m-2}{2}}_{\rho}(x_{1}/r)&:x_{1}>a_{\rho}\,r\end{array}\right.

where
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{m})
and
r\coloneqq|x|
. Note that
u_{\rho}
is subharmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Dahlberg
[
5
]
then showed that for a subharmonic function
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{m},m\geq 3
the following estimate is true

(69)

\vartheta(u)\leq\vartheta(u_{\rho}).

Identifying
\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}
then the inequality (
69
) holds also for functions
u
that are subharmonic in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Note that now in the definition of
u_{\rho}
we have
u_{\rho}(x)=r^{\rho}\,C^{n-1}_{\rho}(x_{1}/r)
whenever
x_{1}<a_{\rho}r
and
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{2n}),r=|x|
. We calculate the characteristic functions
M(r,u_{\rho})
and
T(r,u_{\rho})
:

\displaystyle M(r,u_{\rho})=\max\{u_{\rho}(x)\;:\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},|x|=r\}

\displaystyle=\max\{u_{\rho}(x)\;:\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},|x|=r,x_{1}>a_{\rho}r\}

\displaystyle=\max\{r^{\rho}\,C^{n-1}_{\rho}(x_{1}/r)\;:\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2n},|x|=r\}

\displaystyle=r^{\rho}\,C^{n-1}_{\rho}(1).

Applying the formula for Nevalinna characteristic (
2
), now on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
yields

\displaystyle T(r,u_{\rho})=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{2n-1}}u^{+}_{\rho}(r\,\zeta)\,ds_{2n-1}(\zeta)=r^{\rho}\,\int_{\mathbb{S}_{2n-1}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\zeta_{1})\cdot\chi_{\{\zeta_{1}>a_{\rho}\}}\,ds_{2n-1}(\zeta)

where
\zeta:=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},...,\zeta_{2n})\in\mathbb{S}_{2n-1}
and
\chi_{\{\zeta_{1}>a_{\rho}\}}=1
on the set
\{\zeta\in\mathbb{S}_{2n-1}\,:\,\zeta_{1}>a_{\rho}\}
and vanishes elsewhere. Rewriting the last integral in terms of spherical coordinates implies

\displaystyle T(r,u_{\rho})

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\rho}}{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-1})}\,\int^{\pi}_{0}...\int^{\pi}_{0}\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta_{1})\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta_{1}\,\sin^{2n-3}\theta_{2}\,...\,\sin\theta_{2n-2}\,d\theta_{1}...\,d\theta_{2n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\rho}}{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-1})}\,\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta_{1})\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta_{1}\,d\theta_{1}\,\int^{\pi}_{0}...\int^{\pi}_{0}\,\sin^{2n-3}\theta_{2}\,...\,\sin\theta_{2n-2}\,d\theta_{2}...\,d\theta_{2n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-2})}{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-1})}\,r^{\rho}\,\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta_{1})\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta_{1}\,d\theta_{1}

where
A(\mathbb{S}_{k})
is the area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
and
\theta^{*}\coloneqq\arccos a_{\rho}
. As a result we obtain the ratio

\frac{M(r,u_{\rho})}{T(r,u_{\rho})}=C^{n-1}_{\rho}(1)\frac{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-1})}{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-2})}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta\,d\theta\Big)^{-1}.

Because the above quantity is independent of
r
then we get

(70)

\vartheta(u_{\rho})=C^{n-1}_{\rho}(1)\frac{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-1})}{A(\mathbb{S}_{2n-2})}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta\,d\theta\Big)^{-1}.

To evaluate the last integral we make use of a recursion formula for Gegenbauer functions
[
13
]
given by

(71)

C^{n-1}_{\rho}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}\,(n-2)!}\,\frac{d^{n-2}}{dt^{n-2}}C^{1}_{\rho+n-2}(t)

which yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{n-1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}\,(n-2)!}\,\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}\frac{d^{n-2}}{d(\cos\theta)^{n-2}}C^{1}_{\rho+n-2}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{2n-2}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n-2}}{2^{n-1}\,(n-2)!}\,\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}\frac{d^{n-2}}{d\theta^{n-2}}C^{1}_{\rho+n-2}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{n}\theta\,d\theta.

It is possible to find an explicit formula for
C^{1}_{\rho+n-2}(\cos\theta)
. Upon solving the differential equation (
65
) for
\gamma=1
and
\rho\equiv\rho+n-2
we get the expression

(72)

C^{1}_{\rho+n-2}(\cos\theta)=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\,\int^{\theta}_{0}\cos((\rho+n-1)t)\,dt=\frac{\sin((\rho+n-1)\theta)}{(\rho+n-1)\sin\theta},\quad 0<\theta<\pi.

For illustration we consider the cases
n\in\{2,3\}
.

4.4.1.

When
n=2
we have
C^{1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)=\sin((\rho+1)\theta)/((\rho+1)\sin\theta)
and the smallest
\theta>0
for which
C^{1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)=0
is
\theta^{*}=\pi/(\rho+1)
. Then

\displaystyle 2\,(\rho+1)\,\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{1}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi/(\rho+1)}\sin((\rho+1)\theta)\,\sin\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\,\sin\frac{\pi\rho}{\rho+1}-\frac{1}{\rho+2}\,\sin\frac{\pi(\rho+2)}{\rho+1}.

By normalization formula (
66
) we get
C^{1}_{\rho}(1)=\rho+1
, from the formula for the area of the unit sphere
A(\mathbb{S}_{k-1})=2\pi^{k/2}/\Gamma(k/2)
, we obtain
A(\mathbb{S}_{3})=2\pi^{2},A(\mathbb{S}_{2})=4\pi
therefore substituting in (
70
) yields

\vartheta(u_{\rho})=\pi(\rho+1)^{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}\,\sin\frac{\pi\rho}{\rho+1}-\frac{1}{\rho+2}\,\sin\frac{\pi(\rho+2)}{\rho+1}\Big)^{-1}=\pi\rho\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big)\,\frac{\rho+1}{\sin(\pi/(\rho+1))}.

Elementary calculations show that

\frac{\rho+1}{\sin(\pi/(\rho+1))}\geq\frac{3}{\sin(\pi/3)}=2\sqrt{3}>e,\quad\rho\geq 2.

Therefore

\vartheta(u_{\rho})>\pi\rho\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big)\cdot e=e\,P_{2}(\rho),\quad\rho\geq 2.

In view of Remark
3
and Theorem
2
then
\vartheta(u_{\rho})>K_{2}(\rho)
for all
\rho\geq 2
. For the range
1<\rho<2
one can compare directly with the explicit estimate for
K_{2}(\rho)
given in (
64
).

4.4.2.

When
n=3
from the recursion formula (
71
) it follows that

\displaystyle C^{2}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)=-\frac{1}{4\sin\theta}\cdot\frac{d}{d\theta}C^{1}_{\rho+1}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{\cos((\rho+2)\theta)}{\sin^{2}\theta}-\frac{1}{\rho+2}\,\frac{\sin((\rho+2)\theta)\cos\theta}{\sin^{3}\theta}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Big((\rho+3)\,\frac{\sin((\rho+1)\theta)}{\sin^{3}\theta}-(\rho+1)\frac{\sin((\rho+3)\theta)}{\sin^{3}\theta}\Big).

Evaluating the integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta^{*}}C^{2}_{\rho}(\cos\theta)\,\sin^{4}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho+3}{4}\int^{\theta^{*}}_{0}\sin((\rho+1)\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta-\frac{\rho+1}{4}\int^{\theta^{*}}_{0}\sin((\rho+3)\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\,\Big((\rho+3)\frac{\sin(\rho\,\theta^{*})}{\rho}-2\,\sin((\rho+2)\theta^{*})+(\rho+1)\frac{\sin((\rho+4)\theta^{*})}{\rho+4}\Big).

We obtain the estimate

\vartheta(u_{\rho})=3\pi\rho\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\rho}{4}\Big)\frac{(\rho+1)(\rho+3)}{m(\rho,\theta^{*})}=3P_{3}(\rho)\,\frac{(\rho+1)(\rho+3)}{m(\rho,\theta^{*})}

where

m(\rho,\theta^{*}):=(\rho+3)(\rho+4)\sin(\rho\,\theta^{*})-2\rho(\rho+4)\,\sin((\rho+2)\theta^{*})+\rho(\rho+1)\sin((\rho+4)\,\theta^{*}).

A rough approximation for
\theta^{*}
is
\pi/(\rho+2)
for large enough
\rho
. This implies upon substitution of this value in
m(\rho,\theta^{*})
that

m(\rho,\theta^{*})\approx((\rho+3)(\rho+4)-\rho(\rho+1))\sin\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho+2}\pi\Big)=6(\rho+2)\sin\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho+2}\pi\Big).

Hence

\displaystyle\vartheta(u_{\rho})=3P_{3}(\rho)\,\frac{(\rho+1)(\rho+3)}{m(\rho,\theta^{*})}

\displaystyle\approx P_{3}(\rho)\frac{\rho+1}{2\cdot\sin(\rho\pi/(\rho+2))}>e^{2}\,P_{3}(\rho)

for sufficiently large
\rho
.
Once more from Remark
3
and Theorem
2
we have that for
\rho
large enough
\vartheta(u_{\rho})>K_{3}(\rho)
. For small values of
\rho
one has to explicitly compute
K_{3}(\rho)
and make comparisons. But this is more laborious. For larger
n
one can use the recursive relation (
71
), however computations get more complicated by the nature of the involved functions.

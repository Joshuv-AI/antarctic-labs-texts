Periodicity and longtime diffusion for mean field systems in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d}
Eric Luçon, Christophe Poquet

Abstract
. We study in this paper the longtime behavior of some large but finite populations of interacting stochastic differential equations whose (infinite population) limit Fokker-Planck PDE admits a stable periodic solution. We show that the empirical measure for the population of size N N stays close to the periodic solution, but with a random dephasing at the timescale N ​ t Nt that converges weakly to a Brownian motion with constant drift.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The particle system

We are interested in this work in the large time behavior of the following systems of coupled stochastic differential equations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\;\geqslant\;1
)

dX_{i,t}=\left(\delta F(X_{i,t})-K\left(X_{i,t}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j,t}\right)\right)\,\text{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\sigma\,\text{\rm d}B_{i,t},\ i=1,\ldots,N,\ t\;\geqslant\;0.

(1.1)

Here,
N
is the size of the population and
X_{i,t}
represents the state of the individual with index
i
at time
t
. In (
1.1
), the dynamics of
X_{i}
is decomposed into some local dynamics part
\delta F(X_{i,t}){\rm d}t
, where
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a smooth function (we will suppose that
F
and all its derivatives are bounded) and
\delta>0
a scaling parameter, a linear interaction with the rest of the population, governed by a constant interaction matrix
K
, and an additive noise with a constant diffusion matrix
\sigma
. Here
B_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
is a collection of i.i.d. standard Brownian motions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A simplifying hypothesis that we adopt here is to suppose that
K
and
\sigma
are diagonal with positive coefficients (that is both interaction and noise are nondegenerate w.r.t. all components). The system (
1.1
) is of mean-field type, as the interactions occur on the complete graph: the interaction term in (
1.1
) forces each
X_{i}
to align itself to the empirical mean value of the whole system
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j,t}
.

1.2.
The mean-field limit

As the size of the population goes to infinity, standard propagation of chaos results
[
47
]
shows that the natural limit of the particle system (
1.1
) may be given in terms of the following nonlinear McKean-Vlasov process

d\bar{X}_{t}=\delta F(\bar{X}_{t})\,\text{\rm d}t-K\left(\bar{X}_{t}-\mathbf{E}[\bar{X}_{t}]\right)\,\text{\rm d}t+\sqrt{2}\sigma\,\text{\rm d}B_{t}.

(1.2)

The nonlinear character of (
1.2
) lies in the fact that
\bar{X}_{t}
interacts with its own expectation
\mathbb{E}\left[\bar{X}_{t}\right]
.
The proximity between (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) as
N\to\infty
can be easily understood by a standard coupling argument: under mild assumptions on
F
, choosing the same Brownian motions for both (
1.1
) and
(\bar{X}_{1},\ldots,\bar{X}_{N})
solving (
1.2
), Grönwall type arguments lead to the following standard propagation of chaos estimate (for any fixed
T>0
):

\sup_{i=1,\ldots,N}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{i,t}-\bar{X}_{i,t}\right|\right]\;\leqslant\;\frac{C(T)}{\sqrt{N}}.

(1.3)

Note that without further hypotheses on the system, the above constant
C(T)
is exponential in
T
, so that the above estimate is only relevant for horizon time
T
that are logarithmic in
N
.

1.3.
Periodicity and large-time behavior

At the level of the whole particle system, the previous propagation of chaos estimate boils down to the convergence as
N\to\infty
of the empirical measure

\mu_{N,t}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{j,t}}

(1.4)

to
\mu_{t}
, weak solution to the following nonlinear PDE

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(\sigma^{2}\mu_{t}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(K\left(x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}y\mu_{t}({\rm d}y)\right)\mu_{t}\right)-\delta\nabla\cdot\left(F(x)\mu_{t}\right).

(1.5)

Here,
\mu_{t}
is the law of the nonlinear process
\bar{X}_{t}
given by (
1.2
). The point of the present paper is to analyse the proximity of (
1.4
) w.r.t. its mean-field limit (
1.5
) on a time horizon that goes beyond the time scale of order
\log N
. A first step in this direction is of course to understand the behavior as
t\to\infty
of the mean-field system (
1.5
) itself. The long-time dynamics of (
1.2
)-(
1.5
) is a longstanding issue in the literature. In particular, the existence of stable equilibria for (
1.5
) (that is invariant measures for (
1.2
)) has been studied for various choices of dynamics, interaction and regimes of parameters
\delta,K,\sigma
. A prominent example in this direction concerns the case of granular media equation where
F=-\nabla V
for some some confining potential
V
and the interaction is given by a more general potential
W
(we consider here the case
W
quadratic). Uniform in time propagation of chaos results have been studied in the literature for this situation, see e.g.
[
48
,
8
,
15
,
23
]
for further details and references. Note that most of the mentioned results concern situations where the corresponding particle dynamics (
1.1
) is reversible. We present in this paper a non-reversible situation where uniform propagation of chaos does not occur.

We are concerned in this work with the (intrinsically non-reversible) situation when (
1.5
) has a periodic orbit. The emergence of periodicity for mean-field systems is a universal phenomenon, as it reflects a common feature of self-organization in coupled systems. This emergence has been observed in different fields, as chemical reactions (Brusselator model
[
44
]
), neuroscience
[
27
,
28
,
4
,
12
,
24
,
36
,
42
,
37
,
16
,
19
]
or statistical physics (e.g. spin-flip models
[
17
,
21
]
).
An example of particular interest is given by the FitzHugh-Nagumo model
[
43
,
4
]
, as it is commonly used as a prototype for excitability in both neuronal models
[
34
]
and physics
[
5
]
. Here excitability refers to the ability for a neuron to emit spikes in the presence of perturbations (due to some noise and/or external input) while this neuron stays at rest in absence of perturbation. Note that for excitable systems a main point of interest is to understand the influence of noise and interaction in the emergence and stability of periodic solutions. It is now well known that some balance has to be found in the quantity of noise and interaction present in the system in order to obtain oscillations (see
[
34
,
36
,
37
]
for further details).
For the FizHugh-Nagumo case, the long-time dynamics of (
1.5
) has been the subject of several previous works, with aim either the existence of equilibria
[
41
,
42
]
or of periodic solutions
[
36
,
37
]
), depending on the choice of parameters.

We refer here to the companion paper of this work
[
38
]
where, under some hypotheses that we will describe below (see Section
2.3
), the existence of periodic solutions to nonlinear equation such as (
1.5
) is proven, together with stability and regularity results of the solution
\mu_{t}
in a neighborhood of the periodic orbit. This result is obtained via a perturbation argument (i.e. we take the parameter
\delta
in (
1.1
) small), considering (
1.5
) as a slow-fast system (more details on this point will be given in Section
2.3
), relying on normally hyperbolic manifolds arguments
[
6
,
7
]
. The starting point of the present work will be to assume that (
1.5
) possesses a regular periodic solution
\left(\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}\right)_{t\;\geqslant\;0}
with period
T_{\delta}>0
.

The main issue of the present work will be to question the proximity of the empirical measure (
1.4
) w.r.t. the periodic orbit
\Gamma^{\delta}
of the mean-field PDE (
1.5
). The main result will be that, as long as
\mu_{N,0}
is initially sufficiently close to
\Gamma^{\delta}
, on a time scale proportional to
N
,
\mu_{N,tN}
remains close to
\Gamma^{\delta}
whereas its phase (parameterized by the isochron map
\Theta^{\delta}
defined in a neighborhood of
\Gamma^{\delta}
, see Section
2.3
below for precise details) performs a diffusion with constant drift and diffusion coefficient that are explicit in
\Theta^{\delta}
. The proof relies on precise proximity estimates of
\mu_{N,t}
around
\Gamma^{\delta}
expressed in terms of weighted Sobolev norms that are adapted to the linear part of system (
1.1
).

1.4.
Related works

We are interested here in an infinite-dimensional version of a problem that has been widely studied in the literature in the finite dimensional case: the long time behavior of differential equations admitting a stable periodic solution, and perturbed by a Brownian noise of size
\varepsilon
. It is well known that at the timescale
\varepsilon^{-2}t
this system stays with high probability close to periodic solution, but with a random dephasing given by a diffusion
[
49
,
29
]
. The intuition here is that the stability of the limit cycle prevents the perturbed solution from escaping, but the solution is free to diffuse along the tangent space of the limit cycle. Moreover, when one chooses the isochron map to define the phase of the system (the phase dynamics in the neighborhood of the periodic solution being then simply a rotation with constant speed), the dephasing is given by a Brownian motion with a constant drift.

Note that infinite-dimensional versions of this problem where already considered in the case of the Cahn-Allen SPDE with bi-stable symmetric potential
[
14
,
13
]
. In this case the dynamics involves a sable manifold of stationary points instead of a limit cycle, and the system is perturbed by a white noise, but the limit dynamics of the phase after re-scaling is also a diffusion. In contrast to these previous works we do not perturb the mean-field PDE (
1.5
) by adding a macroscopic noise term, we deal with the
natural
microscopic noise already present in the finite population
(X_{i,t})_{i=1,\ldots,N}
in (
1.1
), which becomes macroscopically apparent on a diffusive time scale: when time is proportional to
N
, the dynamics of the empirical measure
\mu_{N,t}
defined in (
1.4
) can be considered as a noisy perturbation of the PDE (
1.5
).

This finite-population effects have already been studied in the particular case of the stochastic Kuramoto model, defined as follows
[
33
,
1
]
:

{\rm d}X_{i,t}=\delta\omega_{i}\,\text{\rm d}t-\frac{K}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sin\left(X_{j,t}-X_{i,t}\right){\rm d}t+\sigma{\rm d}B_{i,t}.

(1.6)

Here, each
X_{i,t}
is a phase living on the circle
\mathbb{S}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi
that is subject to noise, interaction and disorder: the intrinsic dynamics of
X_{i,t}
reduces to a simple rotation with frequency
\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. These choices of intrisic dynamics and interaction term make this model invariant by rotation. From a mathematical perspective, (
1.6
) has the distinct advantage of being explicitly solvable: one can compute the stationary solutions of the corresponding Fokker-Planck PDE and obtain precise stability estimates concerning the manifold of stationary solutions
[
26
]
. Concerning the long-time dynamics of the empirical measure of (
1.6
), in the non-disordered case (
\omega_{i}\equiv 0
, which is the only case when (
1.6
) is reversible), it has been shown in
[
20
,
9
]
that in the supercritical case, that is when the limit PDE admits a non-trivial circle of stationary solutions, the empirical measure of (
1.6
) performs a Brownian motion on this curve on a time scale of order
N
. Note that in this case uniform propagation of chaos for the radial dynamics has been recently obtained in
[
22
]
, and that the empirical measure stays also close to this curve for long times in the case of interactions defined by a Erdös-Renyi graph sufficiently dense
[
18
]
.
Moreover, the presence of nontrivial disorder induces a drift that is observable on a time scale of order
\sqrt{N}

[
35
]
.

One can see the present paper as a natural continuation of these works to more general systems, that in particular no longer live on the compact
\mathbb{S}
but on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In itself, this generalisation is a major complication to the analysis: first, the existence of periodic orbits is no longer intrinsic to the model and therefore requires a proof. We refer to
[
36
]
and the companion paper
[
38
]
where we give rigorous proof of existence of limit cycles for (
1.5
). Secondly, the fact that the state space is no longer compact poses serious technical issues that were not present in the case of the Kuramoto model, particularly when the term
F
is unbounded (and this is the case in many interesting models, such as for FitzHugh-Nagumo of Morris-Lecar). We refer to
[
36
]
where these issues were addressed for the analysis of the existence of periodic solutions for the PDE (
1.5
). Nonetheless, we restrict ourselves in this work to the case where
F
is bounded as well as all its derivatives: the stability results for the periodic solutions we obtained in
[
36
]
in the unbounded case are not sufficient to study the long time dynamics of the empirical measure, while for the bounded case we obtained more satisfying results in the companion paper
[
38
]
, relying on the theory of approximately invariant manifolds
[
7
]
. Note moreover that the existence and regularity of the isochron map is particularly well-covered in the literature in the finite dimension case
[
30
,
32
]
but similar existence and regularity results in infinite dimension is less known in the literature. One of the purpose of the companion paper
[
38
]
is precisely to obtain the desired existence and regularity estimates concerning the isochron in our case.

2.
Setting and main results

2.1.
Reformulating the model

For the analysis of (
1.1
), it will be convenient to distinguish between the empirical mean of the system and the re-centered process: define

\displaystyle Y_{i,t}=X_{i,t}-m_{N,t},\ i=1,\ldots,N

(2.1)

where

m_{N,t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j,t},\ t\;\geqslant\;0.

(2.2)

Reformulating (
1.1
) in terms of
(Y_{i},_{N})
we obtain equivalently

\begin{cases}\,\text{\rm d}Y_{i,t}=&\left(\delta\left(F_{m_{N,t}}(Y_{i,t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{m_{N,t}}(Y_{j,t})\right)-K\ Y_{i,t}\right)\,\text{\rm d}t\\
&+\sqrt{2}\sigma\,\text{\rm d}B_{i,t}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{N}\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\text{\rm d}B_{j,t}\\
dm_{N,t}=&\frac{\delta}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{m_{N,t}}(Y_{j,t})\,\text{\rm d}t+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{N}\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,\text{\rm d}B_{j,t}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Therefore, the knowledge of the empirical measure defined in (
1.4
) is exactly equivalent to the knowledge of

\mu_{N,t}:=\left(p_{N,t},m_{N,t}\right),\ t\;\geqslant\;0,

(2.4)

where

p_{N,t}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{Y_{i,t}},\ t\;\geqslant\;0,N\;\geqslant\;0.

(2.5)

Note here that we make a minor abuse of notations in identifying (
1.4
) with (
2.4
). One obtains from the same standard propagation of chaos estimates that, on each time interval
[0,T]
the couple
\mu_{N,t}
converges weakly to
\mu_{t}=(p_{t},m_{t})
solution to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}p_{t}&=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla p_{t})+\nabla\cdot(p_{t}Kx)-\delta\nabla\cdot\left(p_{t}\left(F_{m_{t}}-\int F_{m_{t}}\,\text{\rm d}p_{t}\right)\right)\\
\dot{m}_{t}&=\delta\int F_{m_{t}}\,\text{\rm d}p_{t}\end{array}\right.,

(2.6)

with
F_{u}(x)=F(x+u)
.
We denote
t\mapsto T^{t}
the semi-flow associated to the system (
2.6
). We will make hypotheses on
F,K,\sigma
such that this system admits a stable limit cycle
(q^{\delta}_{t},\gamma^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}
.

2.2.
Main hypotheses and notations

2.2.1.
Main assumptions

Throughout the paper, we assume that
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
and bounded as well as all its derivatives. Suppose also
\delta>0
and that
K
and
\sigma
are diagonal matrices with positive coefficients:
K=diag(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
,
\sigma=diag(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d})
with
k_{i}>0
and
\sigma_{i}>0
.

2.2.2.
Weighted Sobolev norms

Let
A
be a positive matrix. Denote by

\left|x\right|_{A}=\left(x\cdot Ax\right)^{1/2}

(2.7)

the Euclidean norm twisted by
A
. For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, define

w_{\theta}(x)=\exp\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\left|x\right|_{K\sigma^{-2}}^{2}\right),

(2.8)

and denote by
L^{2}_{\theta}
the
L^{2}
-space with weight
w_{\theta}
that is associated with the scalar product

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)w_{\theta}(x){\rm d}x.

(2.9)

For
\theta>0
, we consider
the scalar products

\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{r}_{\theta}}=\left\langle f,(1-{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast})^{r}g\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{\theta}},

(2.10)

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast}
is the operator

{\mathcal{L}}_{\theta}^{\ast}f=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla f)-\theta Kx\cdot\nabla f,

(2.11)

and we denote
H^{r}_{\theta}
the completion of the space of smooth function
f
satisfying
\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}<\infty
. For
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
the norm
\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\theta}}
is equivalent to the norm (see
[
38
]
for a proof)

\sqrt{\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,\ldots,r\}^{d},\,i_{1}+\ldots+i_{d}\;\leqslant\;r}\left\|\partial^{i_{1}}_{x_{1}}\ldots\partial^{i_{d}}_{x_{d}}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}}^{2}}.

(2.12)

We define
H^{-r}_{\theta}
the dual of
H^{r}_{\theta}
.
We rely on a “pivot” space structure: since the natural injection
H^{r}_{\theta}\rightarrow L^{2}_{\theta}
is dense, we have a dense injection
(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}\rightarrow H^{-r}_{\theta}
. Using the identification of
(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}
with
L^{2}_{-\theta}
, (which means identifying
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(L^{2}_{\theta})^{\prime}\times L^{2}_{\theta}}
with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
), we obtain, for all
u\in L^{2}_{-\theta}\subset H^{-r}_{\theta}
and all
f\in H^{r}_{\theta}
,

\langle u,f\rangle_{H^{-r}_{\theta}\times H^{r}_{\theta}}=\langle u,f\rangle.

Remark moreover that for
u\in H^{-r}_{\theta}
and
f\in H^{r+1}_{\theta}
we have

\left|\langle\nabla u,f\rangle\right|=\left|\langle u,\nabla f\rangle\right|\;\leqslant\;C\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\|f\|_{H^{r+1}_{\theta}},

(2.13)

so that

\|\nabla u\|_{H^{-(r+1)}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C\|u\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(2.14)

The fact that the empirical measure
p_{N,t}
belongs to
H^{-r}_{\theta}
for
r
sufficiently large is provided by the following Lemma (see Appendix
A
for its proof).

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
\theta>0
and
r>d/2
. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the linear form
h\mapsto\left\langle\delta_{x}\,,\,h\right\rangle=h(x)
is continuous on
H^{r}_{\theta}
and we have the following bound: for all
\eta>0
there exists a constant
C_{\eta,r}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\left\|\delta_{x}\right\|_{H^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\eta,r}\exp\left(\frac{\theta\left|x\right|^{2}_{K\sigma^{-2}}}{4-\eta}\right)
.

The space
H_{\theta}^{-r}
is the proper functional space for the process
p_{N,t}
. In order to understand
\mu_{N,t}
given by (
2.4
), define finally the space

\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}:=H_{\theta}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.15)

endowed with the scalar product

\left\langle(f,m)\,,\,(g,m^{\prime})\right\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{r}_{\theta}}:=\left\langle f\,,\,g\right\rangle_{H_{\theta}^{r}}+m\cdot m^{\prime},

(2.16)

and
\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}
the dual of
\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{r}
. Relying on the same “pivot” space structure as above, we have

\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}(\eta,h);(f,m)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}}_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}\times\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{r}}=\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}(\eta,h);(f,m)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}},

(2.17)

where

\mathopen{\hbox{${\big\langle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\langle}$}}(\eta,h);(f,m)\mathclose{\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}\kern-1.94444pt\hbox{${\big\rangle}$}}=\langle\eta,f\rangle+h\cdot m.

(2.18)

It is proved in
[
38
]
, relying on classical arguments
[
31
,
45
]
, that (
2.6
) admits mild solutions in
\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}
.

2.3.
Slow-fast viewpoint and periodic solution for the infinite population system

In this section, we review briefly the results obtained in the companion paper
[
38
]
. The idea, that was already used in
[
36
,
37
]
, to obtain the existence of periodic solutions to (
1.5
) is to see (
2.6
) as a slow-fast system. Indeed, for
\delta=0
, the solution of this system is
(e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}p_{0},m_{0})
, where
{\mathcal{L}}p=\nabla\cdot(\sigma^{2}\nabla p)+\nabla\cdot(pKx)
is simply an Ornstein Uhlenbeck operator. It is well known that in this case
e^{t{\mathcal{L}}}p_{0}
converges exponentially fast to the density of the Gaussian distribution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}K^{-1}
, that we denote
\rho
. So, recalling (
2.6
), when
\delta
is small,
m_{t}
satisfies at first order

\dot{m}_{t}\approx\delta\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(x)q(x-m_{t})\,\text{\rm d}x.

(2.19)

With this heuristic in view, in order to obtain a periodic behavior for the PDE (
2.6
) when
\delta
is small, we will suppose that the ordinary differential equation

\dot{z}_{t}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F(x)\rho(x-z_{t})\,\text{\rm d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{z_{t}}(x)\rho(x)\,\text{\rm d}x

(2.20)

admits a stable periodic solution
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\alpha}]}
. More precisely, we will express the stability hypothesis in terms of Floquet formalism: let us denote
t\mapsto\phi_{t}
the flow defined by (
2.20
), and
\pi_{u+t,u}
the principal matrix solution associated to
\alpha
, that is defined by
\pi_{u+t,u}n=D\phi_{t}(\alpha_{u})\cdot n
, and thus the solution to

\partial_{t}\pi_{u+t,u}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}DF_{\alpha_{u+t}}(x)\rho(x)\,\text{\rm d}x\right)\pi_{u+t,u}.

(2.21)

We suppose that
\pi_{u+t,u}
satisfies the decomposition

\left|\pi_{u+t,u}P^{s}_{u}\right|\;\leqslant\;C_{\alpha}e^{-\lambda_{\alpha}t}\left|n\right|,\text{ and }c_{\alpha}\left|n\right|\;\leqslant\;\left|\pi_{u+t,u}P^{c}_{u}\right|\;\leqslant\;C_{\alpha}\left|n\right|,

(2.22)

where
P^{c}_{u}
,
P^{s}_{u}
are projection satisfying
P^{c}_{u}+P^{s}_{u}=I_{d}
, with
u\mapsto P^{c}_{u}
and
u\mapsto P^{s}_{u}
are smooth, and
c_{\alpha}
,
C_{\alpha}
and
\lambda_{\alpha}
are positive constants.
\pi_{u+t,u}
characterizes the linearized dynamics around
\alpha
, and these hypotheses mean that it is a contraction on a supplementary space of the tangent space to
\alpha
.

As an example, as stated in
[
36
,
38
]
the existence of a stable periodic solution for (
2.20
) occurs in the case of the FitzHugh Nagumo model, defined in dimension
d=2
by

F(x,y)=\left(x-\frac{x^{3}}{3}-y,\frac{1}{c}\left(x+a-by\right)\right),

(2.23)

for an accurate choice of parameters. In particular, taking
a=\frac{1}{3}
,
b=1
and
c=10
, the local dynamics
\dot{x}_{t}=\delta F(x_{t})
has globally attracting fixed-point, while for
\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{K_{1}}
is not too large (for example with value
0.2
) the system defined by (
2.20
) admits a stable periodic solution.
We refer to
[
36
]
for a detailed study of the bifurcations in this model when tuning the value of

and a discussion on the periodic behaviors induced by the common effect of noise and interaction occuring in this model. Of course the function

. For

With these hypotheses, it is proved in
[

]

is expressed using the linearized semi-group
\Phi_{u+t,u+s}
and the projections
\Pi^{\delta,c}_{u},\Pi^{\delta,s}
, which have the same role as

and

respectively for

.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that (
2.20
) admits a stable periodic solution. Then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
r\;\geqslant\;r_{0}
and
\theta\in(0,1]
there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
the system (
2.6
) admits a periodic solution
\left(\Gamma_{t}^{\delta}\right)_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}:=(q^{\delta}_{t},\gamma^{\delta}_{t})_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}
in
\mathbf{H}^{-r+2}_{\theta}
with period
T_{\delta}>0
. Moreover
q^{\delta}_{t}
is a probability distribution for all
t\;\geqslant\;0
, and
t\mapsto\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}
and
t\mapsto\partial^{2}_{t}\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}
are in
C([0,T_{\delta}),\mathbf{H}^{-r+2}_{\theta})
.

Denoting
{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}=\{\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}:\,t\in[0,T_{\delta})\}
and
\Phi_{u+s,u}(\nu)=DT^{s}(\Gamma^{\delta}_{u})[\nu]
, there exists families of projection
\Pi^{\delta,c}_{u}
and
\Pi^{\delta,s}_{t}
that commute with
\Phi
, i.e. that satisfy

\Pi^{\delta,\iota}_{u+t}\Phi_{u+t,u}=\Phi_{u+t,u}\Pi^{\delta,\iota}_{u},\quad\text{for }\iota=c,s.

(2.24)

Moreover
\Pi^{\delta,c}_{t}
is a projection on the tangent space to
{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}
at
\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}
,
\Pi^{\delta,c}_{t}+\Pi^{\delta,s}_{t}=I_{d}
,

, and there exist positive constants
c_{\Phi,\delta}
,
C_{\Phi,\delta}
and
\lambda_{\delta}
such that

c_{\Phi,\delta}\left\|\Pi^{\delta,c}_{u}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left\|\Phi_{u+t,u}\Pi^{\delta,c}_{u}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi,\delta}\left\|\Pi^{\delta,c}_{u}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}},

(2.25)

\left\|\Phi_{u+t,u}\Pi^{\delta,s}_{u}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi,\delta}\,t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-\lambda_{\delta}t}\left\|\Pi^{\delta,s}_{u}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}},

(2.26)

and

\left\|\Phi_{u+t,u}\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi,\delta}\left(1+t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)e^{-\lambda_{\delta}t}\left\|\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-(r+\alpha)}_{\theta}}.

(2.27)

Moreover in
[
38
]
the existence of an isochron map is also proved, as stated in the following Theorem (corresponding to Theorem 1.6 in
[
38
]
). The
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-regularity of this isochron map will be useful when applying Itô’s Lemma at the end of this paper.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the same hypotheses as in Theorem
2.2
, and for
r
and
\delta
as in Theorem
2.2
, there exist a neighborhood
{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\in\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}
of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\delta}
and a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-mapping
\Theta^{\delta}:{\mathcal{W}}^{\delta}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}/T_{\delta}{\mathbb{Z}}
that satisfies, for all

, denoting

\Theta^{\delta}(\mu_{t})=\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)+t\quad\text{mod }T_{\delta},

(2.28)

and there exists a positive constant

such that, for all

with

\left\|\mu_{t}-\Gamma^{\delta}_{\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)+t}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{\Theta,\delta}e^{-\lambda_{\delta}t}\left\|\mu-\Gamma^{\delta}_{\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(2.29)

Moreover
\Theta^{\delta}
satisfies, for all

\left\|D^{2}\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)-D^{2}\Theta^{\delta}\left(\Gamma^{\delta}_{\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)}\right)\right\|_{{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta})}\;\leqslant\;C_{\Theta,\delta}\left\|\mu-\Gamma^{\delta}_{\Theta^{\delta}(\mu)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(2.30)

To simplify the notations we will denote
\Gamma_{t}=\Gamma^{\delta}_{t}
and

in the rest of the article.

in

.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.2
are satisfied, and let
(r,\gamma,\theta)
be fixed as in (

), (
2.34
) and (
2.33
). There exists a

(2)

2.5.

Let

\displaystyle w_{-\gamma}(Y_{i,t})=

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{0}^{t}w_{-\gamma}(Y_{i,s})\left\{-\left(1-\gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\left|K^{\frac{1}{2}}Y_{i,s}\right|^{2}_{K\sigma^{-2}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right){\rm Tr}(K)\right\}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\gamma\delta\mathbf{E}\left[w_{-\gamma}(Y_{i,t})\left\langle K\sigma^{-2}Y_{i,t}\,,\,F_{m_{N,t}}(Y_{i,t})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{m_{N,t}}(Y_{j,t})\right\rangle\right].

is bounded, one obtains that

(3.7)

Using
[

(3.8)

for the constant

(3.9)

\kappa_{2}:=\left(\frac{\bar{\delta}\left\|F\right\|_{\infty}^{2}k_{max}}{(1-\gamma)k_{min}\sigma_{min}^{2}}+{\rm Tr}(K)+1\right)\\

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{-\gamma}(Y_{i,t})\right]\;\leqslant\;\gamma\left(\kappa_{2}-\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{-\gamma}(Y_{i,t})\right]\right)

\displaystyle\,\left\langle p_{N,0},\varphi_{0}\right\rangle

The rest of the proof follows from arguments identical to the ones given for Lemma

4.4.
Proximity to
{\mathcal{M}}_{\delta}
.

Before proving Proposition
4.1
we first remark that the error term
R_{\alpha,s}
defined in (
4.23
) is either quadratic or of order
\frac{1}{N}
, as stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

There exists a constant
C_{R}
such that for all
s,\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\nu=(\eta,h)\in\mathbf{H}^{-(r-1)}_{\theta}
,

\left\|R_{\alpha,s}(\nu)\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-(r+1)}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;C_{R}\left(\frac{1}{N}\left(1+\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{-(r-1)}_{\theta}}\right)+\delta\left\|\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}^{2}\right).

(4.55)

Proof of Lemma
4.8
.

Let
\eta\in H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nu:=(\eta,h)\in\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}
. Then, using (
2.14
)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\sigma^{2}\nabla(q_{\alpha+s}+\eta)\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}\;\leqslant\;C\left(\left\|q_{\alpha+s}\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}}+\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}}\right)\;\leqslant\;C^{\prime}\left(1+\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}}\right),

(4.56)

since
s\mapsto q_{\alpha+s}
is periodic. Similarly, for every
\varphi\in H_{\theta}^{r+1}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla\cdot\eta\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}\right)\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle\eta\,,\,\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\rangle\right|,

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-r}}\left\|\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}.

Writing
F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}(x)-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}DF_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}(x+uh)[h]{\rm d}u
and using that
by assumption,
F
is bounded as well as all its derivatives, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\nabla\cdot\eta\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}\right)\,,\,\varphi\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-r}}\left|h\right|\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r}}\;\leqslant\;C\left\|\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}^{2}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{r+1}}

Hence

\left\|\delta\nabla\cdot\left(\eta\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}\right)\right)\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}\;\leqslant\;\delta C\left\|\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}^{2}.

(4.57)

The next two other terms in the definition (
4.24
) of
R^{1}
can be treated in a same way and lead to the same upper bound.
Moreover, by regularity of
F
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}(x)-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}(x)-DF_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}(x)[h]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}(x+uh)[h,h]{\rm d}u:=U(x)[h,h]
. Then, si
D^{2}F
is uniformly bounded and all its derivatives,

\displaystyle\left\|\delta\nabla\cdot\left(q_{\alpha+s}\left(F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}+h}-F_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}-DF_{\gamma_{\alpha+s}}[h]\right)\right)\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}

\displaystyle=\delta\left\|\nabla\cdot\left(q_{\alpha+s}U(\cdot)[h,h]\right)\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r+1)}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\delta C\left|h\right|^{2},

(4.58)

for some constant
C>0
. The treatment of the last terms in the definitions (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) of
R^{1}
and
R^{2}
is the same and leads to a contribution of order
\delta C\left\|\nu\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}^{2}
.
∎

We have now all the ingredients to prove Proposition

.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let us define the following event, with probability converging to
1
as
N\to\infty
, by Propositions
3.1
and
4.5
,

{\mathcal{C}}_{\varepsilon,N}=\left\{\left\|\mu_{N,0}-\Gamma_{u_{0}}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\right\}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{N}\cap\left\{\sup_{0\;\leqslant\;t\;\leqslant\;Nt_{f}}\left\|p_{N,t}-g_{N}\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}}\;\leqslant\;\kappa_{1}\right\}.

(4.59)

Step 1: approaching the manifold.

For
\varepsilon>0
we consider
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough such that
\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2C_{\Phi}(1+C_{\Gamma})}<\varepsilon
(we will fix the value of
\varepsilon_{0}>0
later). Define for all

h_{n}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2^{n-1}}

(4.60)

Note that

is of order
O(\log(N))

{\mathcal{C}}_{\varepsilon^{\prime},N}
is satisfied for
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
small enough, so that
\left\|\nu_{N,0}^{(1)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;h_{1}
and then proceed by induction to show that we have
\left\|\nu_{N,0}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;h_{n}
and that we have the bound
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\nu_{N,t}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;2C_{\Phi}h_{n}

\left\|\mu_{N,nT+t}-\Gamma_{\Theta\left(\mu^{N}_{nT}\right)+t}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left\|\nu^{n}_{N,t}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}+\left\|\Gamma_{\alpha_{n}+t}-\Gamma_{\Theta\left(\mu^{N}_{nT}\right)+t}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}},

(4.61)

which means, since

\left\|\mu_{N,nT+t}-\Gamma_{\Theta\left(\mu^{N}_{nT}\right)+t}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\left(1+C_{\Gamma}\right)2C_{\Phi}h_{n}\;\leqslant\;\left(1+C_{\Gamma}\right)2C_{\Phi}\varepsilon_{0}\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon,

(4.62)

which is the first point of Proposition
4.1
.

By assumption, one has that
\left\|\nu_{N,0}^{(1)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;h_{1}
. Suppose by inductive hypothesis that
\left\|\nu_{N,0}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;h_{n}
for some
n
and define

t_{n}:=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T],\ \left\|\nu_{N,t}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\geqslant\;2C_{\Phi}h_{n}\right\}

(4.63)

Then,
t_{n}>0
and from Theorem
2.2
, (
4.22
) and Lemma
4.8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nu_{N,t}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi}e^{-\lambda t}h_{n}

\displaystyle+C_{\Phi}C_{R}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\left(1+\frac{e^{-\lambda(t-s)}}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{N}\left(1+\left\|\eta_{N,s}^{(n)}\right\|_{H^{-(r-1)}_{\theta}}\right)+\delta\left\|\nu_{N,s}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}^{2}\right)\right\}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\left\|Z_{N,t\wedge\tau^{n}}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}.

(4.64)

Using the definition of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\varepsilon,N}
and the fact that
\Gamma_{t}
is bounded in
\mathbf{H}^{-r+1}_{\theta}
, we have the uniform a priori bound

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\sup_{n\;\geqslant\;0}\left\|\eta_{N,s}^{(n)}\right\|_{H^{-(r-1)}_{\theta}}\;\leqslant\;\kappa_{1}+\sup_{s\;\geqslant\;0}\left\|g_{N}-q_{s}\right\|_{H_{\theta}^{-(r-1)}}:=\kappa_{5}.

(4.65)

Then, for
t\;\leqslant\;t_{n}

\displaystyle\left\|\nu_{N,t}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi}e^{-\lambda t}h_{n}+C_{\Phi}C_{R}\left(\frac{1}{N}\left(1+\kappa_{5}\right)+\delta h_{n}^{2}\right)\left(T+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}}\right)+N^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\xi}{3}},

(4.66)

by the control on the noise we have obtained in Proposition
4.5
(recall the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{N}
in (
4.36
)). Note that since
n\;\leqslant\;n_{0}
, we have

. Hence, we obtain from the last inequality that

\displaystyle\left\|\nu_{N,t}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi}e^{-\lambda t}h_{n}+C(N,\varepsilon_{0})h_{n},

(4.67)

with
C(N,\varepsilon_{0})\to 0
as
N\to\infty
and
\varepsilon_{0}\to 0
. Hence, choosing
N
sufficiently large and
\varepsilon_{0}\to 0
such that

, we obtain that
t_{n}\;\geqslant\;T
and we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nu_{N,T}^{(n)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}^{-r}_{\theta}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{\Phi}e^{-\lambda T}h_{n}+C(N,\varepsilon_{0})h_{n}\;\leqslant\;\frac{h_{n}}{4C_{P}}+C(N,\varepsilon_{0})h_{n}\;\leqslant\;\frac{3}{8C_{P}}h_{n},

(4.68)

N
large enough and
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough. It remains to show that
\left\|\nu_{N,0}^{(n+1)}\right\|_{\mathbf{H}_{\theta}^{-r}}\;\leqslant\;\frac{h_{n}}{2}

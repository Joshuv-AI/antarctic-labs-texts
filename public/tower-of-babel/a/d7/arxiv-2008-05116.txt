Ultra-peripheral nuclear collisions as a source of heavy quarkonia
B. Z. Kopeliovich 1, M. Krelina 2,3, J. Nemchik 2,4, I. K. Potashnikova 1

Abstract
Heavy quarkonium production in ultra-peripheral nuclear collisions (UPC) is described within the QCD dipole formalism. Realistic quarkonium wave functions in the Q ​ Q ¯ Q\bar{Q} rest frame are calculated by solving the Schrödinger equation with a subsequent Lorentz boost to high energy. We rely on several realistic Q ​ Q ¯ Q\bar{Q} potentials, which allow to describe well the quarkonium masses and decay widths, as well as data on diffractive electroproduction of quarkonia on protons. Nuclear effects are calculated with the phenomenological dipole cross sections fitted to DIS data. The higher twist quark shadowing related to the lowest Q ​ Q ¯ Q\bar{Q} Fock component of the photon, as well as the leading twist gluon shadowing, related to higher components containing gluons, are included. The results for coherent and incoherent photoproduction of charmonia and bottomonia in UPC of heavy nuclei are in good accord with available data from the LHC. They can also be verified in future experiments at electron-ion colliders.

I
Introduction

Ultra-peripheral collisions (UPC) of nuclei at high energies correspond to collisions with impact parameter much larger than the sum of nuclear radii. Correspondingly, the total cross section of UPC is much larger than that of collisions with nuclear overlap. Besides, UPC is the dominant source of heavy vector mesons. Collisions with double rapidity gaps can be interpreted as photon-Pomeron fusion into a heavy vector meson
V=J\!/\!\psi(1S)
,
\Upsilon(1S)
, or their radial excitations.
In impact parameter space the Pomeron is short range exchange, while the radius of photon exchange is infinitely large.

UPC provide a unique access to photon-nucleus interactions. Weizsäcker-Williams photons, originated from one of the nuclei, can interact diffractively with another one. The mechanisms of photo-production of vector mesons on nuclei have been studied pretty well beyond the Glauber approximation, either in the multi-channel approach
Hufner:1997jg
, or employing the color dipole model
Kopeliovich:1991pu
.
Having a comprehensive understanding of photo-production mechanisms, one can switch to description of nuclear UPC, as was done in Ref.
Ivanov:2007ms
.

Within the popular light-front (LF) color dipole approach

Kopeliovich:1991pu
;
Kopeliovich:1993gk
;
Nemchik:1996pp
;
Kopeliovich:1993pw
;
Nemchik:1994fp
;
Nemchik:1994fq
;
Nemchik:1996cw
;
Hufner:2000jb
;
Nemchik:2000de
;
Nemchik:2000dd
;
Kopeliovich:2001xj
;
Kopeliovich:2007wx
,
the effect, known as color transparency (CT), significantly affects heavy quarkonium photo-production off nuclei.
It is controlled by the
formation length

l_{f}
, characterizing evolution of the
Q\bar{Q}
pair, evolving from the small initial size
\sim 1/m_{Q}
, where
m_{Q}
is the heavy quark mass, up to a larger non-perturbative size of the quarkonium.
The corresponding expression for
l_{f}

can be obtained in the nuclear rest frame from the condition that the relative phase shift between the two lowest levels,
V
and
V^{\prime}
becomes of the order of unity
Kopeliovich:1991pu
;
Kopeliovich:2001xj
,

l_{f}=\frac{2\,k}{M_{V^{\prime}}^{2}-M_{V}^{2}}\,

(1)

where
k
is the photon energy
and
M_{V}
and
M_{V^{\prime}}
are the quarkonium masses in
1S
and
2S
states, respectively.

Another important length scale, called
coherence length
(CL)
Kopeliovich:1991pu
;
Kopeliovich:2001xj
, characterizes the phase shift between
Q\bar{Q}
photo-production amplitudes with different longitudinal coordinates of interaction.
It has the following form,

l_{c}=\frac{2\,k}{M_{V}^{2}},

(2)

The amplitudes are coherent, provided that the length interval
\Delta l\ll l_{c}
.
The coherence length
l_{c}
can also be interpreted as a lifetime (or path) of the
Q\bar{Q}
fluctuation of the photon.

In our calculations of nuclear effects we rely, for the sake of simplicity, on the eikonal approximation for in-medium propagation of long-lived
Q\bar{Q}
photon fluctuations, which is relevant at rapidity
y=0
.
However, at forward or backward rapidities at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC),
as well as at Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC),
such an approximation cannot be applied anymore because the coherence length Eq. (
2
) becomes too short at least for one of the colliding nuclei. Here, for the first time, we apply the rigorous path-integral technique
as is described in Sect.
II.4
.

Another source of nuclear suppression, called
gluon shadowing
(GS), in terms of parton model looks like reduction of gluon density in nuclei at small Bjorken
x
. In the infinite momentum frame of the nucleus this occurs due to longitudinal overlap and fusion of gluons, originated from different bound nucleons. This effect is difficult to evaluate, and usually extracted from the global fits to data. The result is unreliable and is known only for the density integrated over impact parameter.

The parton model description is not Lorentz invariant (only observables are), it depends on reference frame. What looks like gluon fusion in the infinite momentum frame of the nucleus, corresponds to usual Glauber-like shadowing of the photon fluctuations in the nuclear rest frame. Namely, gluon reduction corresponds to shadowing of higher Fock components of the photon, which contain one or more gluons (corrected for the effect for the lowest Fock state
|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
)
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
. The transverse size of such fluctuations depends on the hard scale (
m_{Q}
) logarithmically, so is the leading twist effect.

Notice that onset of gluon shadowing requires much smaller
x
in comparison with the
Q\bar{Q}
component. That happens due to a specifically short coherence length for higher Fock states,
l_{c}^{G}\ll l_{c}
. They differ by an order of magnitude
Kopeliovich:2000ra
;
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
.
Since the transverse size of
Q\bar{Q}-G
dipoles fluctuates during propagation through the nucleus,
even at very high energies of the LHC, one cannot rely on the ”frozen” eikonal approximation,

l_{c}^{G}\gg R_{A}
, where
R_{A}
is the nuclear radius.
For this reason, our calculations of the GS effect are performed relying
on the Green function formalism
Kopeliovich:1999am
;
Ivanov:2002kc
;
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
.

In the present paper we incorporate various improvements in theoretical description of quarkonium photo-production off nuclei, performed in Ref.
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
, and apply them also to UPC at RHIC and the LHC. We include
the higher- and leading-twist shadowing corrections corresponding to the
|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
and

|Q\bar{Q}G\rangle
Fock state of the photon, respectively.
The multi-gluon Fock components have too short coherence length to generate a significant shadowing effects even at very high energies of the LHC.
Moreover, as is discussed in Ref.
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
(see also Refs.
Krelina:2018hmt
;
Cepila:2019skb
;
Krelina:2019egg
;
Krelina:2020bxt
), we ignore the frequently used unjustified model of the photon-like
V\to Q\bar{Q}
transition,
which would lead to an exaggerated weight of the
D
-wave in the rest frame quarkonium wave function.

The paper is organized as follows. We present expressions for calculations of differential cross sections
d\sigma/dy
corresponding to coherent (elastic) and incoherent (quasi-elastic) heavy quarkonium production in UPC in Sections
II.1
and
II.2
, respectively.
Incorporation of a small real part of the production amplitude, as well as spin rotation effects
is explained in Sec.
II.3
.
In the following Section
II.4
we discuss the two main effects, which influence on
nuclear effects in UPC:
(i) the corrections for a finite coherence length and
(ii) gluon shadowing.
The former effect is calculated for the first time within the LF dipole approach based on the Green function formalism, leading to the results, that are substantially different from the standard vector dominance model (VDM).
The following Sec.
III
is devoted to comparison of model predictions with available data and to the analysis of particular nuclear effects in coherent and incoherent quarkonium production in UPC.
Finally, the last Sec.
IV
contains a summary and concluding remarks.

II
Quarkonium production cross section in
ultra-peripheral collisions

The large charge
Z
of colliding heavy nuclei gives rise to strong electromagnetic
fields: in a heavy-ion UPC, the photon field of one nucleus can
produce a photo-nuclear reaction in the other. Then the cross section for photo-production of a vector meson
V
by the Weizsäcker-Williams photons
can be written in the rest
frame of the target nucleus
A
as follows
Bertulani:2005ru
:

k\frac{d\sigma}{dk}=\int\,d^{2}\tau\int\,d^{2}b\,\,n(k,\vec{b}-\vec{\tau},y)\,\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}~~+~~\Bigl\{y\rightarrow-y\Bigr\}\,,

(3)

where the rapidity variable
y=\ln\bigl[s/(M_{V}\sqrt{s_{N}}~)\bigr]\approx\ln\bigl[(2kM+M^{2})/(M_{V}\sqrt{s_{N}}~)\bigr]
.

The formula (
3
) is derived in the one-photon-exchange approximation. Here
the variable
\vec{\tau}
is the relative impact parameter of a nuclear collision, and
\vec{b}
is the impact parameter of the photon-nucleon collision
relative to the center of one of the nuclei.
Particularly,
the collision of identical nuclei with the nuclear radius
R_{A}
in UPC
leads to a condition that the impact parameter
\tau>2R_{A}

Bertulani:2005ru
.

The variable

n(k,\vec{b})
in Eq. (
3
) represents the photon flux induced by the
projectile nucleus with Lorenz factor
\gamma
and has the following form,

n(k,\vec{b})=\frac{\alpha_{em}Z^{2}k^{2}}{\pi^{2}\gamma^{2}}\Biggl[K_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{bk}{\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}K_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{bk}{\gamma}\right)\Biggr]\,,

(4)

where
\alpha_{em}=1/137.036
is the fine-structure constant,

K_{0,1}
are the modified Bessel functions of the second kind and the Lorentz factor
\gamma=2\gamma_{col}^{2}-1
with
\gamma_{col}=\sqrt{s_{N}}/2M
.
The first and the second
term in Eq. (
4
) corresponds to the flux of photons transversely and
longitudinally polarized to the ion direction, respectively. The former photon flux
dominates in ultra-relativistic collisions with
\gamma\gg 1
. Consequently, in heavy-ion UPC
at RHIC and the LHC one can safely neglect the second term in Eq. (
4
) treating the photons as almost real due to very small virtuality,
-q^{2}=Q^{2}<1/R_{A}^{2}
.

II.1
Coherent production of quarkonia

To calculate the cross sections for coherent (
coh
)
quarkonium production,
\gamma A\to V\!A
, we use the light-front dipole approach

Kopeliovich:1991pu
, which has been applied to describe
J\!/\!\psi
photo-production
off nucleons
Hufner:2000jb
;
Krelina:2018hmt
;
Cepila:2019skb
and nuclei

Ivanov:2002kc
;
Nemchik:2002ug
. In this approach,
assuming sufficiently large photon energies, corresponding to most of the kinematic regions studied in the present paper when the CL (
2
)

l_{c}\gg R_{A}
,
the nuclear cross section takes a simple asymptotic form,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{coh}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Biggr|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Biggl|\int d^{2}r\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\Psi_{V}^{*}(\vec{r},\alpha)\,\Biggl(1-\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)\,T_{A}(b)\right]\Biggr)\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r},\alpha)\Biggr|^{2}

(5)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Biggl|\int d^{2}r\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\Sigma_{A}^{coh}(r,\alpha,s,b)\,\Biggr|^{2}\,.

Here we rely on the optical approximation, assuming the elastic dipole amplitude pure imaginary, which is rather accurate for heavy nuclei.
Expression (
5
) is frequently called “frozen” approximation, assuming that the transverse separation of the
|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
Fock state of the photon does not change
during propagation through a nuclear medium. It represents the higher twist shadowing correction since the
Q-\bar{Q}
transverse separation diminishes as
1/m_{Q}
.

In Eq. (
5
)

T_{A}(b)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\,\rho_{A}(b,z)
is the nuclear thickness function normalized as
\int d^{2}b\,T_{A}(b)=A
,
where
\rho_{A}(b,z)
is the nuclear density function, for which we employ the realistic Wood-Saxon form with parameters taken from
DeJager:1987qc
;

\Psi_{V}(r,\alpha)
is the LF wave function for heavy quarkonium;

\Psi_{\gamma}(r,\alpha)
is the LF distribution or the wave function
of the
Q\bar{Q}
Fock component of the quasi-real (transversely polarized) photon,
where the
Q\bar{Q}
fluctuation (dipole) has the transverse size
\vec{r}
and the variable
\alpha=p_{Q}^{+}/p_{\gamma}^{+}
is the boost-invariant fraction of the photon momentum carried by a heavy quark (or antiquark).

The universal dipole-nucleon total cross section
\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)
depends on transverse dipole separation
r
and c.m. energy squared
s=M_{V}\,\sqrt{s_{N}}\,\exp[y]
. Energy dependence of the dipole cross section can be alternatively included also via variable
x=M_{V}^{2}/s=M_{V}\,\exp[-y]/\sqrt{s_{N}}
.

Notice that the coherent cross section, Eq. (
5
), is different from the usual Glauber expression
Bauer:1977iq
due to presence of the dipole cross section
Kopeliovich:1981pz
. It effectively includes the
Gribov inelastic shadowing corrections
Gribov:1968jf
;
Kopeliovich:2016jjx
in all orders for the
Q\bar{Q}
Fock component of the photon.

II.2
Incoherent production of quarkonia

Besides “elastic” coherent photo-production
\gamma A\to VA
, where the nucleus remains intact, the vector meson can be produced in a quasi-elastic process
\gamma A\to VA^{*}
, where the nucleus is excited and decays to fragments. Important is that additional meson production is excluded. In this case, one can sum over different products of nuclear excitation and employ the conditions of completeness. Of course, one channel of elastic photo-production must be subtracted. It is instructive to see the result within the Glauber approximation
Hufner:1996dr
,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{inc}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Biggr|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}^{Gl}\propto\exp\left[-\sigma^{VN}_{in}\,T_{A}(b)\right]-\exp\left[-\sigma^{VN}_{tot}\,T_{A}(b)\right]=\exp\left[-\sigma^{VN}_{tot}\,T_{A}(b)\right]\Bigl\{\exp\left[-\sigma^{VN}_{el}\,T_{A}(b)\right]-1\Bigr\}\,.

(6)

Here the inelastic
V-N
cross section
\sigma^{VN}_{in}=\sigma^{VN}_{tot}-\sigma^{VN}_{el}
, where the elastic cross section

\displaystyle\sigma^{VN}_{el}\approx\frac{(\sigma^{VN}_{tot})^{2}}{16\,\pi\,B^{VN}}

(7)

and
B^{VN}
is the slope of the differential elastic
V-N
cross section.

The cross section of incoherent (
inc
) photo-production has the form, analogous to (
6
), but with additional integrations over the dipole size (see derivation in Sect. VII of Ref.
Kopeliovich:2005us
),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{inc}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Bigr|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}r_{1}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha_{1}\,\Psi_{V}^{*}(\vec{r}_{1},\alpha_{1})\,\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r}_{1},\alpha_{1})\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r_{1},s)T_{A}(b)\right]

(8)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int d^{2}r_{2}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha_{2}\,\Psi_{V}^{*}(\vec{r}_{2},\alpha_{2})\,\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r}_{2},\alpha_{2})\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r_{2},s)\,T_{A}(b)\right]

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{\exp\left[\frac{\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r_{1},s)\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r_{2},s)}{16\pi B(s)}\,T_{A}(b)\right]-1\right\}\,.

The elastic cross section of a heavy quarkonium on a nucleon is rather small and the exponential in the last row of Eq. (
8
) can be expanded. Then we arrive at a simple result
Ivanov:2002kc
,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{inc}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Biggr|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{T_{A}(b)}{16\pi B(s)}\,\Biggl|\int d^{2}r\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\Psi_{V}^{*}(\vec{r},\alpha)\,\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r},\alpha)\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)\,T_{A}(b)\right]\,\Biggr|^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{T_{A}(b)}{16\pi B(s)}\,\Biggl|\int d^{2}r\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\,\Sigma_{A}^{inc}(r,\alpha,s,b)\,\Biggr|^{2}.

II.3
Real part of the production amplitude and spin rotation effects

The both Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
) contain a small correction due to
the real part of the
\gamma N\to VN
amplitude applying
the following replacement
Bronzan:1974jh
;
Nemchik:1996cw
;
Forshaw:2003ki
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)\Rightarrow\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)\,\left(1-i\,\frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{\partial\,\ln\,{\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)}}{\partial\,\ln s}\right)\ .

(10)

The advantage of the
S
-wave heavy quarkonia, considered in the present paper, is based on a simple factorization of the radial and spin-dependent components of their wave functions. Here is well defined in the
Q\bar{Q}
rest frame and can be obtained by solving the Schrödinger equation for various realistic interaction potentials between
Q
and
\bar{Q}
proposed in the literature.
In our calculations, we choose two of them, the power-like (POW)
Martin:1980jx
;
Barik:1980ai
and Buchmüller-Tye (BT)
Buchmuller:1980su
potentials, which provide the best description of available data on charmonium electroproduction off protons, as was demonstrated in Ref.
Cepila:2019skb
.
For the dipole cross section, we employ three popular parametrizations - Kopeliovich-Schäfer-Tarasov (KST) from
Ref.
Kopeliovich:1999am
,
Golec-Biernat-Wüsthoff (GBW) from
Refs.
GolecBiernat:1998js
;
GolecBiernat:1999qd
and
Bartels-Golec-Biernat-Kowalski (BGBK) from Ref.
Bartels:2002cj
.

Treating the structure of the
V\to Q\bar{Q}
vertex from Refs.
Ivanov:2002kc
;
Ivanov:2002eq
;
Ivanov:2007ms
;
Krelina:2018hmt
;
Cepila:2019skb
;
Kopeliovich:2022jwe

the Melosh spin transformation
Melosh:1974cu
is incorporated performing following substitutions in Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{coh}(r,\alpha,s,b)\Rightarrow\Sigma_{A}^{coh}(r,\alpha,s,b)\cdot\Bigl[\Sigma^{(1)}(r,\alpha)+\Sigma^{(2)}(r,\alpha)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{inc}(r,\alpha,s,b)\Rightarrow\Sigma_{A}^{inc}(r,\alpha,s,b)\cdot\Bigl[\Sigma^{(1)}(r,\alpha)+\Sigma^{(2)}(r,\alpha)\Bigr],

(11)

where

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(1,2)}(r,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\,K_{0,1}(m_{Q}r)\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dp_{T}\,p_{T}\,J_{0,1}(p_{T}r)\Psi_{V}(\alpha,p_{T})\,\mathcal{R}^{(1,2)}(p_{T})

(12)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{(1)}(p_{T})=\frac{2\,m_{Q}^{2}(m_{L}+m_{T})+m_{L}\,p_{T}^{2}}{m_{T}(m_{L}+m_{T})}\,,\qquad\qquad\quad\mathcal{R}^{(2)}(p_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{Q}^{2}(m_{L}+2m_{T})-m_{T}\,m_{L}^{2}}{m_{Q}\,m_{T}(m_{L}+m_{T})}\,p_{T}\,.

(13)

Here
N=Z_{Q}\,\sqrt{2N_{c}\,\alpha_{em}}/4\,\pi
, where the factor
N_{c}=3
represents the number of colors in QCD,
Z_{Q}=\frac{2}{3}
and
\frac{1}{3}

are the charge-isospin factors for the production of charmonia and bottomonia, respectively, and

J_{0,1}
are the Bessel functions of the first kind.
The variables
m_{T,L}
in the above formulas have the following form,

m_{T}=\sqrt{m_{Q}^{2}+p_{T}^{2}}\,,\qquad m_{L}=2\,m_{Q}\,\sqrt{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\,.

(14)

Note that the new form of Eqs. (
11
)-(
13
)
does not require performing the so-called “
resummation procedure”
proposed in
Ivanov:2002kc
in order to include properly the spin rotation effects in
nuclear photo-production cross sections (
5
) and (
9
).

Fir numerical calculations, following the results from Refs.

Ivanov:2002kc
;
Ivanov:2002eq
;
Ivanov:2007ms
;
Krelina:2018hmt
;
Cepila:2019skb
,
we relied on the charm and the bottom quark masses, corresponding to the values used with the realistic phenomenological models for the
Q-\bar{Q}
interaction potential, such as POW and BT.
Consequently, the LF quarkonium wave functions
\Psi_{V}(\alpha,r)
have been obtained adopting the Lorentz boosting procedure as described in Ref.
Terentev:1976jk
and justified in Ref.
Kopeliovich:2015qna
.

II.4
Corrections for a finite coherence length and the gluon shadowing

As was already mentioned above, the Green function approach allows to include directly the effects of quantum coherence without any restrictions for the magnitude of CL, Eq. (
2
). However, as an alternative and a more simple way,
instead of such a complicated method, one can use expressions (
5
) and (
9
)) for nuclear cross sections in the limit of long CL,
l_{c}\gg R_{A}
,
and then provide additional corrections for a finite CL when
l_{c}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}}R_{A}
.
Such an incorporation of finite-
l_{c}
effects via the effective correction factors (form factors),
F^{coh}(s,l_{c})
and
F^{inc}(s,l_{c})

based on VDM has been suggested in Ref.
Hufner:1996jw
and employed in Ref.
Ivanov:2002kc
for calculations of charmonium photo-production off nuclei.
Then nuclear cross sections corrected to the finite coherence length effects are given as

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{coh}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{coh}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Biggl|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}\cdot F^{coh}\bigl(s,l_{c}(s)\bigr)\,,\qquad\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{inc}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}=\frac{d^{2}\sigma_{A}^{inc}(s,b)}{d^{2}b}\Biggr|_{l_{c}\gg R_{A}}\cdot F^{inc}\bigl(s,l_{c}(s)\bigr)\,.

(15)

In the present paper, as the further improvement, instead of VDM
the corresponding factors
F(s,l_{c})

have been calculated within a more sophisticated Green function formalism and
have the following form,

\displaystyle F^{coh}(s,l_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!d^{2}b\left|\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!dz\,\rho_{A}(b,z)\,F_{1}(s,b,z,l_{c})\,\right|^{2}\bigl/\,\Bigl(...\Bigr)\Bigl|_{l_{c}\to\infty}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle F^{inc}(s,l_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!d^{2}b\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!dz\,\rho_{A}(b,z)\,\Bigl|F_{1}(s,b,z,l_{c})-F_{2}(s,b,z,l_{c})\Bigr|^{2}\bigl/\,\Bigl(...\Bigr)\Bigl|_{l_{c}\to\infty}\,,

(17)

where the functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
read,

\displaystyle F_{1}(s,b,z,l_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int d^{2}r_{1}\,d^{2}r_{2}\,\Psi^{*}_{V}(\vec{r}_{2},\alpha)\,G_{Q\bar{Q}}(z^{\prime},\vec{r}_{2};z,\vec{r}_{1};l_{c})\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r_{1},s)\,\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r}_{1},\alpha)\Bigl|_{z^{\prime}\to\infty}

(18)

\displaystyle F_{2}(s,b,z,l_{c})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{z}dz_{1}\,\rho_{A}(b,z_{1})\,\int\limits_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int d^{2}r_{1}\,d^{2}r_{2}\,d^{2}r\,\Psi^{*}_{V}(\vec{r}_{2},\alpha)

(19)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle G_{Q\bar{Q}}(z^{\prime}\to\infty,\vec{r}_{2};z,\vec{r};l_{c})\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(\vec{r},s)\,G_{Q\bar{Q}}(z,\vec{r};z_{1},\vec{r}_{1};l_{c})\,\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(\vec{r}_{1},s)\,\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r}_{1},\alpha)\,.

Here
\Psi_{\gamma}(\vec{r},\alpha)=K_{0}(m_{Q}r)
, and the Green function

G_{Q\bar{Q}}(z^{\prime},\vec{r}_{2};z,\vec{r}_{1};l_{c})
describes the propagation of an interacting
Q\bar{Q}
pair in a nuclear medium between points with longitudinal coordinates
z
and
z^{\prime}
and with initial and final separations
\vec{r}_{1}
and
\vec{r}_{2}
.
In calculations for the sake of simplicity, we employed the quadratic form for the dipole cross section
\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)=C(s)\,r^{2}
,
and the harmonic oscillatory form for the LF
Q-\bar{Q}
interaction potential in the evolution equation for the Green function (e.g. see Ref.
Kopeliovich:2001xj
).
Another simplification is related to
a constant nuclear density,
\rho_{A}(b,z)=\rho_{0}\Theta(R_{A}^{2}-b^{2}-z^{2})
, which is rather accurate for heavy nuclei used in our analysis. Consequently, for the LF quarkonium wave functions, we obtained the following Gaussian shape
Kopeliovich:2001xj
for the
1S
and
2S
states,

\displaystyle\Psi_{V}(r,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{V}\,a^{2}(\alpha)\,f(\alpha)\,\exp\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(\alpha)r^{2}\Bigr]

(20)

\displaystyle\Psi_{V^{\prime}}(r,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{V^{\prime}}\,a^{2}(\alpha)\,f(\alpha)\,\exp\Bigl[-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(\alpha)r^{2}\Bigr]\Bigl\{1+4h(\alpha)-\beta\,2a^{2}(\alpha)r^{2}\Bigr\}\,,

(21)

where

f(\alpha)=\exp\Bigl[-h(\alpha)\Bigr]=\exp\biggl[-\frac{m_{Q}^{2}}{2a^{2}(\alpha)}+\frac{4\alpha(1-\alpha)m_{Q}^{2}}{2a^{2}(\alpha)}\biggr]\,,

(22)

and the parameter
\beta
, controlling the position of the node, has been determined from the orthogonality condition
\int d^{2}r\,d\alpha\Psi_{V}(r,\alpha)\,\Psi_{V^{\prime}}(r,\alpha)=\delta_{VV^{\prime}}
. We have found
\beta=0.908
and
0.963
for production of
\psi^{\,\prime}
and
\Upsilon^{\,\prime}
, respectively. The function
a^{2}(\alpha)=2\alpha(1-\alpha)\,m_{Q}\,\omega
, where the oscillatory frequency
\omega=(\left.M_{V^{\prime}}\right.-\left.M_{V}\right.)/2\approx 0.3\,\,\mbox{GeV}
.

The above approximations substantially simplify the calculations of the
l_{c}
-correction factors
F^{coh}
and
F^{inc}

since allow to obtain an explicit analytical harmonic oscillatory form for the Green function
fg
,

\displaystyle G_{Q\bar{Q}}(\vec{r_{2}},z_{2};\vec{r_{1}},z_{1};l_{c})=\frac{b(\alpha)}{2\pi i\,{\rm sin}(\Omega\Delta z)}\,{\rm exp}\left\{\frac{i\,b(\alpha)}{{2\,\rm sin}(\Omega\Delta z)}\,\Bigl[(r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})\,{\rm cos}(\Omega\Delta z)-2\vec{r_{1}}\cdot\vec{r_{2}}\Bigr]\right\}\,{\rm exp}\left[-\frac{i\Delta z}{l_{c}}\right]\,,

(23)

with
\Delta z=z_{2}-z_{1}
,
l_{c}=2k\alpha(1-\alpha)/m_{Q}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{b(\alpha)}{k\,\alpha(1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{a^{4}(\alpha)-i\,k\,\alpha\,(1-\alpha)\,C_{eff}(s,\alpha)\,\rho_{A}({b},z_{2})}}{k\,\alpha\,(1-\alpha)}\,.

(24)

Here,
considering the standard saturated shape of the dipole cross section,

\displaystyle\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(\vec{r},s)=\sigma_{0}\,\biggl(1-\exp\Bigl[-\frac{r^{2}}{r_{0}^{2}(s)}\Bigr]\biggr)\,,

(25)

the factor
C_{eff}
in Eq. (
24
) can be expressed in the following form,

\displaystyle C_{eff}(s,\alpha)=C(s)\,\biggl\{1-\exp\biggl[-\,\frac{1}{a^{2}(\alpha)\,r_{0}^{2}(s)}\,\biggr]\biggr\}\,a^{2}(\alpha)\,r_{0}^{2}(s)\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad C(s)=\sigma_{0}/r_{0}^{2}(s)\,.

(26)

Model predictions vs available data

We have calculated rapidity distributions
d\sigma/dy
for
the coherent and incoherent heavy quarkonium photo-production in UPC according to Eq. (
3

Here the
|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
Fock component of the photon was treated in the “frozen” approximation,

l_{c}\gg R_{A}
(see Eqs.(

Moreover,
in order to calculate the nuclear cross sections (
9

\gamma N\to VN
. Here we rely on the standard Regge form,

B_{J\!/\!\psi}(s)=B_{0}+2\,\alpha^{\prime}(0)\ln\big(s/s_{0}\big)
,
where the parameters
\alpha^{\prime}=0.171\,\mbox{GeV}^{-2}

B_{0}=1.54\,\,\mbox{GeV}^{-2}
were fitted in
Cepila:2019skb
to data on
J\!/\!\psi
photo-production with
s_{0}=1\,\mbox{GeV}^{2}
.
The slope for
1S
bottomonium photo-production was fitted to data in
Cepila:2019skb
and found to have a smaller value than for
J\!/\!\psi
,

B_{\Upsilon}(s)\approx B_{J\!/\!\psi}(s)-1\,\mbox{GeV}^{-2}

For the production of radially excited
2S
state of bottomonia, the node effect is negligibly small, and one can safely use the same magnitudes of the slope parameter for both
1S

IV
Conclusions

In this paper we treat the heavy quarkonium [
J\!/\!\psi(1S),\psi^{\,\prime}(2S),\Upsilon(1S),\Upsilon^{\,\prime}(2S)
]
production in heavy-ion UPC
within the light-front QCD dipole approach
in the energy range accessible by experiments at RHIC and the LHC. Here the main observations are the following:

•

The quarkonium wave functions are well defined in the
Q\bar{Q}
rest frame. They have been included in our calculations by solving the Schrödinger equation with several realistic
Q-\bar{Q}
interaction potentials.
Consequently, the corresponding LF wave functions have been generated performing the boosting to the LF frame using the so-called Terent’ev prescription from Ref.
Terentev:1976jk
, which was found in Ref.
Kopeliovich:2015qna
to have a reasonable accuracy in comparison with exact solution.

Here we ignore the model for the photon-like
V\to Q\bar{Q}
transition, frequently used in the literature,
avoiding so too large weight of the
D
-wave component in the
Q\bar{Q}
rest frame, in consistence with solutions of the Schrödinger
equation.

•

The spin-dependent part of the wave function for
S
-wave quarkonia can be safely factorized from the radial component.
Consequently, we
perform explicitly the transformation of two-dimensional heavy (anti)quark spinors from the rest to the LF frame known as the Melosh spin rotation. We derived new formulas for coherent and incoherent nuclear cross sections incorporating such a transformation
(see Eqs. (
11
)-(
13
)).

•

UPC at RHIC and the LHC at the mid rapidities, provide a sufficiently high energy and a long coherence length for the lowest
|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
Fock state of the photon. As far as CL considerably exceeds the nuclear
size, one can rely on the high-energy eikonal approximation for nuclear effects (see Eqs. (
5
) and (
9
)).
The corresponding shadowing correction is small since diminishes with heavy quark mass as
1/m_{Q}^{2}
, so represents the higher twist effect.

•

At forward and/or backward rapidities, the eikonalization of
\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)
cannot be applied anymore and
we included corrections for a finite coherence length which have been calculated for the first time within a rigorous quantum-mechanical description, summing up all possible paths of the quarks.
A proper treatment of finite-
l_{c}
corrections is absent in the most of the recent calculations of UPC.
These corrections lead to a significant modification
of rapidity distributions
d\sigma/dy
at small photon energies when
l_{c}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$<$}}R_{A}
(see Figs.
1
,
5
and
6
).

•

We also included the gluon shadowing corrections related to higher Fock components of the photon containing gluons. Those components have a coherence length much shorter than the lowest

|Q\bar{Q}\rangle
Fock state.
They represents the leading-twist effect since the transverse size of the
Q\bar{Q}-G
dipole is much larger compared to the small-sized
Q\bar{Q}
fluctuation and is almost independent of
m_{Q}
.
The dominant contribution to nuclear shadowing comes from the
|Q\bar{Q}G\rangle
Fock state of the photon.
In calculations of the corresponding shadowing factor one cannot use the standard eikonal approximation even at high energies since the
Q\bar{Q}-G
size fluctuates during propagation through the medium. This is why we applied the Green function formalism.
The higher photon fluctuations with more gluons do not cause a significant shadowing effect (see also Sec. IV in Ref.
Kopeliovich:2022jwe
).

•

We have also studied differences in our predictions employing KST, GBW and BGBK phenomenological parametrizations for the dipole cross section
\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)
in order to estimate
a corresponding measure of the theoretical uncertainty in our current analysis.
We concluded that whereas in charmonium production in UPC, the main source of theoretical uncertainties is related predominantly to our choice of quarkonium wave functions, in the bottomonium case, the main role in the variability of predictions is played by various models for
\sigma_{Q\bar{Q}}(r,s)
.

•

Our predictions for
d\sigma/dy
are in a rather good accord with available data on coherent production of
J\!/\!\psi(1S)
and
\psi^{\,\prime}(2S)
in UPC at the energies of RHIC and LHC (see Figs.
2
and
3
).
They can be tested not only by measurements at the LHC, but also in future experiments at the planned electron-ion colliders.

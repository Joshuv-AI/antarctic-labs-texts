Power boundedness and related properties for weighted composition operators on 𝒮 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Abstract
. Moreover, we characterize power boundedness and topologizablity of C ψ , φ C_{\psi,\varphi} on 𝒮 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) \mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) in terms of ψ , φ \psi,\varphi . 1.
Introduction

Weighted composition operators play an important role in functional analysis and operator theory. Given a set
\Omega
, a self mapping
\varphi
on
\Omega
and a function
\psi
on
\Omega
, beyond the fundamental question of when a weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on a given function space
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
– that is, when the operation
C_{\psi,\varphi}f=\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)
results in a function belonging to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
for every
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
– it is a natural task to characterize operator theoretic properties of
C_{\psi,\varphi}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
by properties of
\psi
and
\varphi
. Obviously, the class of weighted composition operators contains multiplication operators, i.e.
\varphi(x)=x
, as well as composition operators, i.e.
\psi(x)=1
.

In the present article, we consider weighted composition operators on the space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of rapidly decreasing smooth functions. While the space
\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of multipliers for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has been characterized by L. Schwartz
[
22
,
33
]
, the functions
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
for which the corresponding composition operator acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
have been characterized only recently in
[
18
]
. Apart from characterizing the pairs
(\psi,\varphi)
for which
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we also characterize power boundedness and (
m
-)topologizability for weighted composition operators on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in terms of
\psi
and
\varphi
.

In recent years, mean ergodicity, power boundedness, and topologizablity of (weighted) composition operators and multiplication operators on various spaces of (generalized) functions have attracted the attention of a large number of authors. Recall that a continuous linear operator
T
on a locally convex Hausdorff space
E
is power bounded precisely when the set of its iterates
\{T^{n};\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equicontinuous. This notion is closely connected with
T
being mean ergodic, i.e. with the property that for every
x\in E
the sequence of Cesàro means
\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}T^{m}(x)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges. Whenever the Cesàro means converge uniformly on bounded sets,
T
is uniformly mean ergodic. By a classical result of Lorch
[
30
]
, on reflexive Banach spaces, every power bounded operator is mean ergodic, which characterizes reflexivity of Banach spaces, as has been shown in the celebrated work
[
16
]
by Fonf, Lin, and Wojtaszczyk. Bonet, de Pagter, and Ricker
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
proved that Lorch’s result remains true for (semi-)reflexive Hausdorff locally convex spaces. Additionally, by
[
28
, Theorem 2.5]
, on Montel spaces, mean ergodic operators are automatically uniformly mean ergodic. Thus, power bounded operators of
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are already uniformly mean ergodic.

While the interest for power boundedness for operators on locally convex Hausdorff spaces stems from its close relationship to (uniform) mean ergodicity, topologizable operators were introduced by Żelazko in
[
35
]
(see also
[
7
]
). Recall that a continuous linear operator
T
on a locally convex Hausdorff space
E
is topologizable if for every continuous seminorm
p
on
E
there is a continuous seminorm
q
on
E
and a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive numbers such that
(a_{n}T^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous from
E
equipped with
p
into
E
equipped with
q
. This property characterizes those
T
for which there is a unital subalgebra
A
of
\mathcal{L}(E)
(with composition as multiplication) which contains
T
and which admits a locally convex topology making
A
into a topological algebra such that the map
A\times E\rightarrow E,(S,x)\mapsto Sx
is continuous. While the notion of
m
-topologizability (where the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the definition of topologizablity can be chosen as a sequence of powers
(M^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},M>0
) was also introduced by Żelazko
[
34
]
, a renewed interest in this property stems from a recent result of Golińska and Wegner
[
19
]
stating that
m
-topologizable operators on sequentially complete locally convex spaces generate uniformly continuous semigroups of operators. It should be noted that in contrast to Banach spaces, on arbitrary locally convex spaces in general not every continuous linear operator generates a strongly continuous semigroup, see
[
17
]
.

Our results for power boundedness for weighted composition operators
C_{\psi,\varphi}
are sharp when
\psi,\varphi
are univariate polynomials. They allow to provide natural examples of infinite dimensional subspaces of
\mathcal{L}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
consisting entirely of power bounded operators, or – except the zero operator – entirely of non-power bounded operators, respectively. Concrete examples of such infinite dimensional vector spaces are
\{C_{\psi,\varphi}:\varphi(x)=x^{2}+1,\psi\text{ polynomial}\}
(Theorem
5.10
) and
\{C_{\psi,\varphi}:\varphi(x)=(1/2)x,\psi\text{ polynomial}\}
(Proposition
5.4
), respectively. We point out that examples of the second kind cannot occur for operators defined on Banach spaces.

The article is organized as follows. In section
2
we characterize those pairs
(\psi,\varphi)
for which the corresponding weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Under mild additional assumptions on
\psi
and
\varphi
, in section
3
, we give a characterization for the latter property which is easier to check in many situations including composition operators. In section
4
we characterize power boundedness and (

-)topologizability of
C_{\psi,\varphi}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As a concrete example we show power boundedness of
C_{\exp,\exp}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.
\psi=\varphi=\exp
. It should be noted that neither the composition by
\exp
nor the multiplication by
\exp
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. As an application of our findings in section
4
, in section
5
we study power boundedness of weighted composition operators on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
for the case that
\varphi
is a (univariate) polynomial. Among others, we prove that power boundedness of translation operators, i.e.
\varphi(x)=x+b
,
b\neq 0
, can be achieved by multiplication with constants of modulus strictly smaller than 1. We also show that for
\text{deg}(\varphi)\geq 2
, power boundedness of the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
is equivalent to the power boundedness and/or uniform mean ergodicity of the weighted composition operators
C_{\psi,\varphi}
for arbitrary
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
. In the short final section
6
, we apply arguments from section
5
to show that a univariate polynomial
\varphi
is necessarily a translation whenever there is
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
such that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is weakly supercyclic, i.e. there is
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
\{\lambda C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n}f;\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is weakly dense in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. This complements recent results on hypercyclicity of weighted translation operators on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
by Goliński and Przestacki
[
20
]
.

Throughout, we use standard notation from functional analysis

and dynamics of linear operators on locally convex spaces

2.
Weighted composition operators on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

The main purpose of this section is to characterize those pairs
(\psi,\varphi)
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
smooth mapping, for which the corresponding weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where we say that
C_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)

acts on

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
C_{\psi,\varphi}f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Obviously, in this case
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is a linear mapping on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As usual, we say that
\varphi
is a
symbol for

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
C_{\varphi}:=C_{1,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Additionally, we recall that
\psi
is a
multiplier for

\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if
M_{\psi}:=C_{\psi,\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We shall see in Example
2.9
below that neither
C_{\varphi}
acting on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
nor
\psi
being a multiplier for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is necessary for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

We first fix some notation which is valid throughout this paper. As usual, for a smooth function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and a multi index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we write
f^{(\alpha)}(x)=\partial^{\alpha}f(x)=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}f(x)
, where as usual
|\alpha|=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}
. While we also denote the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
|x|
, it will always be clear from the context whether we refer to the length of a multi index or the Euclidean norm of a vector. Next, we recall some notation from
[
13
]
used in the multivariate version of the Faà di Bruno formula. On the set
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
of multi indices, for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})
, we write
\alpha\prec\beta
provided one of the following holds:

(i)

|\alpha|<|\beta|
,

(ii)

|\alpha|=|\beta|
and
\alpha_{1}<\beta_{1}
, or

(iii)

|\alpha|=|\beta|
,
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}
and
\alpha_{k+1}<\beta_{k+1}
for some
1\leq k<d
.

Moreover, for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we define the set

\displaystyle p(\beta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{|\beta|};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2|\beta|d}:\,\text{for some }1\leq s\leq|\beta|,{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad k_{j}=\ell_{j}=0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq|\beta|-s;|k_{j}|>0\text{ for }|\beta|-s+1\leq j\leq|\beta|,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\quad\left.0\prec\ell_{|\beta|-s+1}\prec\cdots\prec\ell_{|\beta|}\text{ are such that }\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}k_{j}=\lambda,\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}|k_{j}|\ell_{j}=\beta\right\}.

It is straightforward to show
|\lambda|\leq|\beta|
whenever
p(\beta,\lambda)\neq\emptyset
. Then, for a smooth function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, a smooth mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
we have

\left(f\circ\varphi\right)^{(\beta)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
1\leq|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x))\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}

(see
[
13
, Remark 2.2]
), where
\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)=(\varphi_{1}^{(\ell_{j})}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{d}^{(\ell_{j})}(x))
and where for
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, as usual
y^{k}=y_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots y_{d}^{k_{d}}
, so that

\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\varphi_{i}^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j,i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\partial^{|\ell_{j}|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\ell_{j,1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\ell_{j,d}}}\varphi_{i}(x)\right)^{k_{j,i}},

with
\ell_{j}=(\ell_{j,1},\ldots,\ell_{j,d}),k_{j}=(k_{j,1},\ldots,k_{j,d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
. It follows immediately from the definition of the set
p(\beta,\lambda)
that
p(\beta,0)=\emptyset
. Thus, employing the usual convention that the sum of summands indexed by the empty set equals zero, for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
we have

\left(f\circ\varphi\right)^{(\beta)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x))\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}.

Abusing notation, for
\beta=0
, we further set
p(0,\lambda)=\emptyset
whenever
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
and
p(0,0)=\{(0,0)\}
(
0\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
) so that, by the usual convention
0!=1
and
0^{0}=1

\sum_{p(0,\lambda)}0!\prod_{j=1}^{|0|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}=\begin{cases}0,&\lambda\neq 0,\\
1,&\lambda=0,\end{cases}

where we also applied the usual convention that the product of factors indexed by the empty set equals one. Thus, for
f
and
\varphi
as above, for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and for every multi index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, by applying Leibniz’ rule and Faà di Bruno’s formula, after reordering, we get

\displaystyle(\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi))^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\beta\leq\alpha\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x)){\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
|\beta|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x)){\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\beta\leq\alpha,|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}},

where we have used that
{\alpha\choose\beta}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\alpha_{j}\choose\beta_{j}}=0
if there are
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\beta_{j}>\alpha_{j}
since
{z\choose m}=\frac{z(z-1)\cdots(z-m+1)}{m!}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

For
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a smooth mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
we denote

(2.1)

F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\beta\leq\alpha,|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{p(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}}{k_{j}!\left(\ell_{j}!\right)^{|k_{j}|}}

and thus

(2.2)

(\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi))^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi(x))F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x).

Now we are ready to prove a first technical lemma which will also be used in section
4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set,
(\psi_{i})_{i\in I}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{I}
and let
\varphi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth mappings,
i\in I
. Assume that there are
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
and
p>0
such that

(2.3)

\forall\,q>0:\,\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q}}|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}}(x)|=\infty.

Then there is
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{p}|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))^{(\alpha)}(x)|=\infty.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be the minimum with respect to the linear ordering
\prec
of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
of the finite set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
for which (
2.3
) holds. Let
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(i_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be sequences in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
I
, respectively, such that
|x_{j+1}|>|x_{j}|+1
and

(2.4)

\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{j}}|F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})|>j.

At this step, we continue the proof by considering two cases. First we assume
(|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
to be unbounded. By making an abuse of notation and identifying
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with a subsequence, we can assume
|x_{j+1}|>|x_{j}|+1
and
|\varphi_{i_{j+1}}(x_{j+1})|>|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|+1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
(l(j))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a strictly increasing sequence of natural numbers such that

(2.5)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l(j)}}|F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})|=\infty.

Fix
\varrho\in\mathscr{D}(B(0,1/2))
,
\varrho^{(\lambda_{0})}(0)=1
and
\varrho^{(\lambda)}(0)=0
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{\lambda_{0}\}
. We define

f(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\varrho(x-\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l(j)}}.

Since the summands which define
f
are smooth functions with mutually disjoint compact supports and because
\lim_{j}l(j)=\infty
, it is standard to check
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, by (
2.2
) and the definition of
F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}|(\psi_{i_{j}}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}))^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}|f^{(\lambda_{0})}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l(j)}}|F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})|

so that by (
2.5
)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{p}|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))^{(\alpha)}(x)|\geq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}|(\psi_{i_{j}}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}))^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|=\infty

as desired.

Next, let us suppose that
(|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. We observe that the choice of
\lambda_{0}
implies the existence of
q>0
and
C\in(0,\infty)
such that, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\lambda\prec\lambda_{0}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x)|)^{q}}|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x)|\leq C.

Next, we fix
f\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f(x)=x^{\lambda_{0}}/\lambda_{0}!
in a neighborhood of the bounded subset
\{\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j});\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, a moment’s reflection reveals that
f^{(\lambda)}\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
\{\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j});\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
whenever
\lambda_{0}\prec\lambda
and obviously, for every
q>0
there is

with

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N},\\
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}\left|f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))\right|(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{q}\leq M.

Now we have by (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}|(\psi_{i_{j}}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}))^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|=(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
0\leq|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|\end{subarray}}f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}\left|f^{(\lambda_{0})}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle-\sum_{\lambda\prec\lambda_{0}}\left|f^{(\lambda)}(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))\right|(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{q}\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{q}}\left|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{j}\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{j}}\left|F_{\alpha,{\lambda_{0}}}^{\varphi_{i_{j}},\psi_{i_{j}}}(x_{j})\right|-\sum_{\lambda\prec\lambda_{0}}MC\geq j-\sum_{\lambda\prec\lambda_{0}}MC

so that again

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{p}|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))^{(\alpha)}(x)|\geq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{p}|(\psi_{i_{j}}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}))^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|=\infty

as desired.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

The weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\mapsto\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)
is correctly defined and continuous.

(iii)

For all
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
and for every
p>0
there exists
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)|<\infty.

Proof.

Obviously, (ii) implies (i), and (i) implies (iii) by Lemma
2.1
applied to
I=\{1\}
and
\psi_{1}=\psi,\varphi_{1}=\varphi
. Additionally, using (
2.2
) it is straightforward to show that (iii) implies (ii).
∎

The evaluation of condition (iii) from the above theorem for concrete
\psi,\varphi
might be quite involved due to the rather complicated expression for
F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}
in (
2.1
). Therefore, we now give necessary and sufficient conditions for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which are easier to evaluate. Since for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
\lambda=0
it holds

F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)=\psi^{(\alpha)}(x)
, Theorem
2.2
(iii) immediately implies the following necessary condition.

Corollary 2.3
.

If the weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\mapsto\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)
is well defined then for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\psi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty.

Theorem
2.2
immediately implies the next sufficient condition for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It will be shown in Theorem
3.3
below that under some mild additional assumptions on
\psi,\varphi
this sufficient condition is also necessary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth. Assume that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\psi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty,

and for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\varphi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty.

Then
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.5
.

For the special case of a multiplication operator, i.e.
\varphi(x)=x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
k_{j},\ell_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we have

\left(\varphi^{(\ell_{j})}(x)\right)^{k_{j}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(\partial^{\ell_{j}}x_{i}\right)^{k_{j}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }\ell_{j}=k_{j}=0,\\
0,&\text{ if }|\ell_{j}|\geq 2,\\
0,&\text{ if }|\ell_{j}|=1,k_{j}\notin\text{span}\{\ell_{j}\},\\
1,&\text{ if }|\ell_{j}|=1,k_{j}\in\text{span}\{\ell_{j}\}.\end{cases}

Therefore, defining

p_{0}(\beta,\lambda):=\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{|\beta|};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|})\in p(\beta,\lambda):\,|\ell_{|\beta|}|\leq 1\text{ and }k_{j}\in\text{span}\{\ell_{j}\}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq|\beta|\}

it follows that

F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\\
\beta\leq\alpha,|\lambda|\leq|\beta|\end{subarray}}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{p_{0}(\beta,\lambda)}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{|\beta|}\frac{1}{k_{j}!}.

In order to continue, let
e_{t}=(\delta_{j,t})_{1\leq j\leq d}
(Kronecker’s
\delta
),
1\leq t\leq d
, be the standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
,
\beta=\sum_{t=1}^{d}\beta_{t}e_{t}
let
I(\beta):=\{1\leq t\leq d:\,\beta_{t}\neq 0\}
so that
\emptyset\neq I(\beta)=\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{\beta}\}
with
1\leq t_{1}<\ldots<t_{\beta}\leq d
. Moreover, we denote the number of elements of
I(\beta)
by
|I(\beta)|
. With this notation, for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
we conclude

\displaystyle p_{0}(\beta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{|\beta|};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|})\in p(\beta,\lambda):\,|\ell_{|\beta|}|=1,k_{j}\in\text{span}\{\ell_{j}\}\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq|\beta|,{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}|k_{j}|\ell_{j}=\beta}\right.

\displaystyle\quad k_{j}=\ell_{j}=0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq|\beta|-|I(\beta)|,\ell_{|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+1}=e_{t_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|}=e_{t_{\beta}},

\displaystyle\quad\left.|k_{j}|>0\text{ for }|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+1\leq j\leq|\beta|,\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}k_{j}=\lambda,\sum_{j=1}^{|\beta|}|k_{j}|\ell_{j}=\beta\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\quad\emptyset,&\text{ if }\beta\neq\lambda,\\
\{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{|\beta|};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2|\beta|d}:\,k_{j}=\ell_{j}=0,1\leq j\leq|\beta|-|I(\beta)|,&\\
\quad\ell_{|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+1}=e_{t_{\beta}},\ell_{|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+2}=e_{t_{\beta}-1},\ldots,\ell_{|\beta|}=e_{t_{1}},&\\
\quad k_{|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+1}=\beta_{t_{\beta}}e_{t_{\beta}},k_{|\beta|-|I(\beta)|+2}=\beta_{t_{\beta}-1}e_{t_{\beta}-1},\ldots,k_{|\beta|}=\beta_{t_{1}}e_{t_{1}}\},&\text{ if }\beta=\lambda,\end{cases}

so that for
\lambda\neq 0

F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)={\alpha\choose\lambda}\psi^{(\alpha-\lambda)}(x)

which also holds true for
\lambda=0
(recall that the above multinomial coefficient is zero whenever
\lambda\nleq\alpha
).

Hence, Theorem
2.2
characterizes those
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which the corresponding multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),f\mapsto\psi f
is correctly defined by the property that for every
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\lambda\leq\alpha
and every
p>0
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|x|)^{q}}|\psi^{(\alpha-\lambda)}(x)|<\infty.

Obviously, this holds precisely when for each
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
there is
r>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-r}|\psi^{(\gamma)}(x)|<\infty.

Thus, for the special case
\varphi(x)=x
, Theorem
2.2
gives the well known characterization of the space of multipliers of
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Additionally, by Corollary
2.3
, if
\varphi
is a non-constant elliptic polynomial,
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, if
d=1
, this equivalence holds whenever
\varphi
is a non-constant univariate polynomial.

Remark 2.6
.

Next, we consider the special case
d=1
in Theorem
2.2
. We define for
\beta,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\backslash\{0\}
with
\lambda\leq\beta

q(\beta,\lambda)=\left\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\beta}:\,\sum_{j=1}^{\beta}i_{j}=\lambda,\sum_{j=1}^{\beta}ji_{j}=\beta\right\},

and for
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})\in q(\beta,\lambda)
we set

L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta}):=\{1\leq j\leq\beta:\,i_{j}\neq 0\}\text{ and }s(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta}):=|L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})|.

Then,
\emptyset\neq L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})\subset\{1,\ldots,\beta\}
and
1\leq s(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})\leq\beta
. It is straightforward that the correspondence
q(\beta,\lambda)\rightarrow p(\beta,\lambda)
which maps
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})
into

(0,\ldots,0,i_{\min L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})},\ldots,i_{\max L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta})};0,\ldots,0,\min L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta}),\ldots,\max L(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta}))

is correctly defined and bijective, where we have (twice)
\beta-s
zeros. With this it follows for
d=1
that for
\psi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\varphi
real valued,
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\lambda\leq\alpha

F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)=\sum_{\beta=\lambda}^{\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{\beta}=\lambda,\\
i_{1}+2i_{2}+\cdots+\beta i_{\beta}=\beta\end{subarray}}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{\beta}\frac{1}{i_{j}!}\left(\frac{\varphi^{(j)}(x)}{j!}\right)^{i_{j}}.

Noticing that
i_{j}=0
for
j>\beta-\lambda+1
the above simplifies to

\displaystyle F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi,\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=\lambda}^{\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\ldots,i_{\beta-\lambda+1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{\beta-\lambda+1}=\lambda,\\
i_{1}+2i_{2}+\cdots+\beta i_{\beta-\lambda+1}=\beta\end{subarray}}\beta!\prod_{j=1}^{\beta-\lambda+1}\frac{1}{i_{j}!}\left(\frac{\varphi^{(j)}(x)}{j!}\right)^{i_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=\lambda}^{\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)B_{\beta,\lambda}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(x),\ldots,\varphi^{(\beta-\lambda+1)}(x)\right),

where
B_{\beta,\lambda}
denotes the corresponding Bell polynomial, i.e.

(2.6)

\forall\,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\beta-\lambda+1}\in\mathbb{R}:\,B_{\beta,\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\beta-\lambda+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2}\ldots,i_{\beta-\lambda+1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{\beta-\lambda+1}=\lambda,\\
i_{1}+2i_{2}+\cdots+(\beta-\lambda+1)i_{\beta-\lambda+1}=\beta\end{subarray}}\beta!\prod_{r=1}^{\beta-\lambda+1}\frac{1}{i_{r}!}\left(\frac{x_{r}}{r!}\right)^{i_{r}}

for
\beta,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
with
\lambda\leq\beta
, and
B_{0,0}=1
,
B_{\beta,0}=0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\varphi
be real valued. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

The weighted composition operator
C_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}),f\mapsto\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)
is correctly defined and continuous.

(iii)

For every
\alpha,\beta,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\alpha\geq\beta\geq\lambda
and for each
p>0
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}\left|\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)B_{\beta,\lambda}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(x),\ldots,\varphi^{(\beta-\lambda+1)}(x)\right)\right|<\infty.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
and Remark
2.6
, (i) and (ii) are equivalent and hold precisely when for every
\alpha,\beta,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\alpha\geq\beta\geq\lambda
and each
p>0
there is
q>0
with

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}\left|\sum_{\beta=\lambda}^{\alpha}{\alpha\choose\beta}\psi^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)B_{\beta,\lambda}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(x),\ldots,\varphi^{(\beta-\lambda+1)}(x)\right)\right|<\infty.

Evaluating the latter condition first for
\lambda=\alpha
, then for
\lambda=\alpha-1
etc. finally shows that (i), (ii), and (iii) are equivalent.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

For a real valued
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
it follows from Corollary
2.7
with
\psi\equiv 1
that the corresponding composition operator
C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if

(2.7)

\forall\,\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\alpha\geq\lambda\,\forall\,p>0\,\exists\,q>0:\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}\left|B_{\alpha,\lambda}\left(\varphi^{\prime}(x),\ldots,\varphi^{(\alpha-\lambda+1)}(x)\right)\right|<\infty.

Since
B_{0,0}\equiv 1
and
B_{\alpha,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\alpha})=x_{\alpha}
, condition (
2.7
) implies

•

there is
k>0
such that
|\varphi(x)|\geq|x|^{1/k}
whenever

, and

•

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are

such that

.

On the other hand, the previous two conditions easily imply condition (

) so that Corollary
2.7
gives an alternate characterization to that in
[

, Theorem 2.3]
. Furthermore, the characterization in
[

, Theorem 2.3]
is also valid for several variables (see
[

), which can be deduced from Theorem

, too, in a similar way.

Example 2.9
.

For the case
\varphi=\psi
, we have that
C_{\varphi,\varphi}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
is continuous if and only if for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and

there exists
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\varphi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty.

Indeed, necessity follows from Corollary
2.3
while sufficiency is due to Corollary

. For
\varphi(x)=\psi(x)=\exp(x)
, we thus have that

is continuous. Observe that neither the composition operator

nor the multiplication operator

acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. We will show in Example

below that

is even power bounded, and therefore uniformly mean ergodic, on

.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth such that for each
p>0

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{p}}{(1+|x|)}<\infty.

Moreover, let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
. By applying Corollary
2.3
for
p+1
we get
q>0
and
C>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{p}|\psi^{(\alpha)}(x)|\leq C\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}{(1+|x|)}.

By hypothesis on
\varphi
, the supremum on the right is finite so the assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth. Then,
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
\partial_{k}\varphi_{i}\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1\leq i,k\leq d
.

Proof.

Let
\partial_{k}\varphi_{i}\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
1\leq i,k\leq d
. By the multivariate Faà di Bruno formula,
f\circ\varphi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence
\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Now, let
C_{\psi,\varphi}
act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We assume that there are
1\leq i,k\leq d
such that
\partial_{k}\varphi_{i}\notin\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
such that

\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}:\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{j}}=\infty.

Thus, there is a sequence
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
|x_{j+1}|>|x_{j}|+1
as well as

\frac{|\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x_{j})|}{(1+|x_{j}|)^{j}}>j

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, we distinguish two cases. In case
(\varphi(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded, by abuse of notation, we identify
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with a subsequence satisfying
|\varphi(x_{j+1})|>|\varphi(x_{j})|+1
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\varrho,g\in\mathscr{D}(B(0,1))
be such that
\varrho=1
in
B(0,1/2)
and
g^{(e_{i})}(0)=1
as well as
g^{(\beta)}(0)=0
for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\backslash\{e_{i}\}
.
It is standard to show that

(2.8)

\psi(x):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\varrho(x-x_{j})}{(1+|x_{j}|)^{j/2}}

belongs to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as does

f(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{g(x-\varphi(x_{j}))}{(1+|x_{j}|)^{j/2}}.

By the multivariate Faà di Bruno formula, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
we conclude

(2.9)

\displaystyle|\left(\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)\right)^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\psi(x_{j})\left(f\circ\varphi\right)^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\psi(x_{j})\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\varphi(x_{j})\right)\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha+e_{k})}(x_{j})|}{(1+|x_{j}|)^{j}}>j,

so that
\psi\cdot(f\circ\varphi)\notin\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
contradicting the hypothesis that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

To finish the proof, we consider the case when
(\varphi(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. We define
\psi
as in (
2.8
) and we consider a function
f\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f(x)=x_{i}
on a neighborhood of
\{\varphi(x_{j}):j\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Then (
2.9
) holds which again gives the desired contradiction.
∎

For smooth and bounded
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by Corollary
2.3
,
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a necessary condition for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, as an immediate corollary of Theorem
2.11
we have the following.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth and bounded. Then,
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
\varphi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

3.
Small decay multipliers

In this section, we shall prove that for a family of weighted composition operators, including composition operators, the sufficient conditions from Corollary
2.4
are also necessary for

to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In order to do so, we first prove the following result which will also be of use in section

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that there are
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and

such that

(3.2)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q}}|\psi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(x)|=\infty

for all
q>0
, or that there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
such that

(3.3)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q}}|\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha)}(x)|=\infty

for all
q>0
. Then there is
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{\max\{p,m+1\}}\max\{|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))(x)|,|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))^{(\alpha)}(x)|\}=\infty.

Proof.

If there are
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
satisfying (
3.2
) for every
q>0
, then the result follows by Lemma
2.1
with
\lambda=0
.

Thus, in order to complete the proof, we may assume that for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
there is
q(\beta,p)>0
such that

(3.4)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q(\beta,p)}}|\psi_{i}^{(\beta)}(x)|<\infty.

We now fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with minimal
|\alpha|
such that (
3.3
) holds for every
q>0
. Clearly,
|\alpha|\geq 1
, and for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\beta\leq\alpha,\beta\neq 0
, there is
q^{\prime}(\beta)>0
such that

(3.5)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}(\beta)}}|\varphi_{i}^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x)|<\infty.

With
m
as in (
3.1
), we choose sequences
(i_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(l_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
I
,
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, such that
l_{j+1}>l_{j}>m+\max\{q(\beta,m):\beta\leq\alpha\}+\max\{q^{\prime}(\beta):0\neq\beta\leq\alpha\}
,
|x_{j+1}|>1+|x_{j}|>1+m
, and
|\varphi_{i_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|>j(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}}
,

.

Next, we distinguish two cases. Suppose that
(|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Take

such that
f\equiv 1
on a neighbourhood of

. By (

) and the boundedness of

we conclude
\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|>0
which implies

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{m+1}|(\psi_{i}\cdot(f\circ\varphi_{i}))(x)|\geq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{m+1}|\psi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})||f(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))|=\infty

proving the assertion in this case.

Now, suppose that
(|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded. By identifying
(\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
with a subsequence, we assume
|\varphi_{i_{j+1}}(x_{j+1})|>1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
|\varphi_{i_{j}}^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|>j(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}}
there is
1\leq k\leq d
such that the
k
-th component
\varphi_{i_{j},k}
of
\varphi_{i_{j}}
satisfies
|\varphi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{j},k}(x_{j})|>\frac{j}{\sqrt{d}}(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g\in\mathscr{D}(B(0,1))
be such that
g(x)=x_{k}
in a neighbourhood of the origin. It is then standard to show that the function

f(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{g(x-\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j}))}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}-m}}

belongs to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\beta\leq\alpha,\beta\neq\alpha
, by the choice of
g
and the multivariate Faà di Bruno formula, we have

\left(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}\right)^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})=\frac{\varphi_{i_{j},k}^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}-m}},

while obviously,
(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}})(x_{j})=0
. Hence, for
0\neq\beta\leq\alpha
we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}^{(\beta)}(x_{j})\left(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}\right)^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}^{(\beta)}(x_{j})|\frac{|\varphi_{i_{j}}^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})|}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}-m}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{q(\beta,m)}}|\psi_{i_{j}}^{(\beta)}(x_{j})|\frac{|\varphi_{i_{j}}^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})|}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{q^{\prime}(\beta)}}<\infty,

where we have used
l_{j}-m>\max\{q(\beta,m):\beta\leq\alpha\}+\max\{q^{\prime}(\beta):0\neq\beta\leq\alpha\}
together with (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}})^{(\alpha)}(x_{j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|\frac{|\varphi^{(\alpha)}_{i_{j},k}(x_{j})|}{(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{l_{j}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+|x_{j}|)^{m}(1+|\varphi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}(x_{j})|\frac{j}{\sqrt{d}}.

Combining the above with (
3.1
), the boundedness of
\left((1+|x_{j}|)^{m}|\psi_{i_{j}}^{(\beta)}(x_{j})\left(f\circ\varphi_{i_{j}}\right)^{(\alpha-\beta)}(x_{j})|\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
for
0\neq\beta\leq\alpha
, and Leibniz’ rule finally give

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{m}|(\psi_{i}(f\circ\varphi_{i}))^{(\alpha)}(x)|=\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

For a smooth mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
a function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be of
small decay with respect to

\varphi
if there is
m>0
such that

\inf_{|x|\geq m}(1+|x|)^{m}(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{m}|\psi(x)|>0.

As an immediate consequence of the previous lemma we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be of small decay with respect to
\varphi
. Then
C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
there is
q>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\psi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|\varphi(x)|)^{q}}|\varphi^{(\alpha)}(x)|<\infty.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.4
the conditions are sufficient for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
while Lemma
3.1
also implies necessity.
∎

4.
Power boundedness and topologizability

In this section we study power boundedness and related properties for weighted composition operators
C_{\psi,\varphi}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
p>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, obviously

\forall\,f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,\|f\|_{p,\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{p}|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|

defines a continuous norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}
on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the set of norms
\{\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}:\,p>0,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\}
defines the standard topology on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Whenever we equip the vector space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
only with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}
we write
\mathscr{S}_{p,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Obviously, every continuous linear mapping from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself is in particular a continuous linear mapping from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
\mathscr{S}_{p,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Next, for a smooth mapping
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set
\varphi_{n}:=\varphi\circ\cdots\circ\varphi
the
n
-th iteration of
\varphi
. Additionally, we set
\varphi_{0}(x)=x
and for
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\psi^{n,\varphi}(x):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\psi(\varphi_{j-1}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular,
\varphi_{1}=\varphi
and
\psi^{1,\varphi}=\psi
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I
be a non-empty set,
(\psi_{i})_{i\in I}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{I}
and let
\varphi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be smooth mappings,
i\in I
, such that the weighted composition operators
C_{\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}}
act on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i\in I
.

(a)

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
p>0
be fixed. Then, the set of continuous linear mappings
\{C_{\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}}:\,i\in I\}
is equicontinuous from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
\mathscr{S}_{p,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
there is
q>0
such that

(4.1)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{i}(x)|)^{q}}\left|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}}(x)\right|<\infty.

(b)

The set of continuous linear mappings
\{C_{\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}}:\,i\in I\}
is equicontinuous on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
and each
p>0
there is
q>0
such that (
4.1
) holds.

Proof.

Clearly, (b) is a direct consequence of (a). In order to prove (a), since
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Fréchet space, by the Uniform Boundedness Principle equicontinuity of
\{C_{\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}}:\,i\in I\}
from
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
\mathscr{S}_{p,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equivalent to the boundedness of
\{C_{\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}}f:i\in I\}
in
\mathscr{S}_{p,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, by Lemma
2.1
and (
2.2
), we obtain the claimed equivalence.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a smooth mapping and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

C_{\psi,\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is power bounded.

(ii)

For all
\alpha,\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\lambda|\leq|\alpha|
and each

there exists
q>0
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(1+|x|)^{p}}{(1+|\varphi_{n}(x)|)^{q}}\left|F_{\alpha,\lambda}^{\varphi_{n},\psi^{n,\varphi}}(x)\right|<\infty.

Proof.

Since
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n}f=C_{\psi^{n,\varphi},\varphi_{n}}f
for every

,

, the assertion follows immediately from Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
4.1
(b).
∎

Remark 4.6
.

We observe that, arguing as in Corollary
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
, the hypothesis for
\psi
to be of small decay with respect to
\varphi
in Theorem
4.5
is only needed for the necessity of (b) in (ii). Condition (a) is necessary for the power boundedness of
C_{\psi,\varphi}
while (a) and (b) together are always sufficient conditions for the power boundedness of
C_{\psi,\varphi}
whether
\psi
is of small decay with respect to
\varphi
or not.

Remark 4.7
.

Obviously,
\psi(x)\equiv 1
is of small decay with respect to every smooth
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
so that Theorem
4.5
is applicable to composition operators. Employing the same arguments as in Remark
2.8
, we derive easily that the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is power bounded if and only if it satisfies the two conditions

(a)

there are
k,l>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds
|\varphi_{n}(x)|\geq|x|^{k}
whenever
|x|\geq l
, and

(b)

for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are
C,q>0
such that
|\varphi_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)|\leq C(1+|\varphi_{n}(x)|)^{q}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For
d=1
, this characterization has been obtained in
[
14
, Proposition 3.9]
. We observe, that when
d=1
, then any polynomial
\varphi
with
\text{deg}(\varphi)\geq 2
satisfies condition (a) above. Indeed, this is easily obtained from
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|\varphi(x)|}{|x|}=\infty
. Combining this observation with
[
14
, Theorem 3.11]
we obtain that condition (b) above is satisfied if and only if
\deg(\varphi)\geq 2
and
\varphi
has no fixed points (and hence the degree of
\varphi
is even).

Remark 4.8
.

Because
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Montel space,
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
combined with
[
28
, Theorem 2.5]
yield that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
as well as its transpose are uniformly mean ergodic whenever
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Example 4.9
.

In Example

we have already seen that for
\psi=\varphi=\exp
the weighted composition operator

acts on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
although neither
\exp
is a symbol for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
nor
\exp\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
. We will now show that

is even power bounded on

, and thus uniformly mean ergodic, by Remark

. To do so, we first notice that

(4.2)

\left(\varphi_{n}\right)^{\prime}(x)=\left(\varphi^{\prime}\right)^{n,\varphi}(x)=\varphi^{n,\varphi}(x)=\varphi_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(x)\cdots\varphi_{n}(x).

Thus, by Corollary

In order to prove (

\forall\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\exists\,n_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}\,\forall\,n\geq n_{\alpha},x\in\mathbb{R}:\,(\varphi^{n,\varphi})^{(\alpha)}(x)\leq\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{n}(x))^{2+\alpha}.

In order to prove that (

the inequality (

) holds for
\alpha=0
and

. Now, assume that the inequality (

) holds for
\alpha=0
and some

. Then, since

for every

\varphi^{n+1,\varphi}(x)=\varphi^{n,\varphi}(x)\varphi_{n+1}(x)\leq\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{n}(x))^{2}\varphi_{n+1}(x)\leq\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{n+1}(x))^{2}

and
n+1
proving the validity of (

) for
\alpha=0
with

.

In order to establish (
4.4
) for
\alpha=1
we first provide some auxiliary estimates. As
\varphi(x)=e^{x}
, for every

,

\varphi_{n}(x)=e^{\varphi_{n-1}(x)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{j}}{j!},

so that

(4.5)

\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2,x\in\mathbb{R}:\,(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{j}\leq j!\varphi_{n}(x).

Besides that, since for

\varphi_{n}(\mathbb{R})=(\varphi_{n-1}(0),+\infty)

we also have

(4.6)

\forall\,n\geq 5,x\in\mathbb{R}:\,n\leq\varphi_{n-2}(0)\leq\varphi_{n-1}(x).

For arbitrary
C>0
and

, choosing

so large that

, by (
4.6
) and (
4.5
), it holds for all

and

(4.7)

nC(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{j}=n(C(j+2)!)\frac{(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{j}}{(j+2)!}\leq(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{2}\frac{(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{j}}{(j+2)!}\leq\varphi_{n}(x).

To show (
4.4
) for
\alpha=1
, we introduce
\Psi_{n}(x):=1+\varphi^{1,\varphi}(x)+\cdots+\varphi^{n-1,\varphi}(x)
. Applying (

\displaystyle(\varphi^{n,\varphi})^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{n})^{\prime}(x)=(\varphi_{1})^{\prime}(x)\cdot\varphi_{2}(x)\cdots\varphi_{n}(x)+\cdots+\varphi_{1}(x)\cdots\varphi_{n-1}(x)\cdot(\varphi_{n})^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{1}(x)\cdots\varphi_{n}(x))(1+\varphi_{1}(x)+\cdots+\varphi_{1}(x)\cdots\varphi_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle=\varphi^{n,\varphi}(x)\Psi_{n}(x).

By inequality (

) for

,
\varphi_{n}(x)\geq 1

implying

for

. Hence,
\Psi_{n}(x)\leq 1+(n-1)\varphi^{n-1,\varphi}(x),x\in\mathbb{R},n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying (
4.6
), (
4.4
) for
\alpha=0
and (

) for

there is
N\geq 2\cdot 5!
such that for all

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{3}\leq\varphi_{n}(x).

Hence, by (

) for

, for all

) for
\alpha=1
.

Now, fix

and assume that for all

(4.9)

(\varphi^{n,\varphi})^{(\beta)}(x)\leq\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{n}(x))^{2+\beta},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\ n\geq n_{\beta}.

We show (

) for

, where

satisfies

and

(4.10)

2^{\alpha-1}n(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{\alpha+2}\leq\varphi_{n}(x),

which is possible by (

) and Leibniz’ rule, for every

Applying the above inequality for

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d^{\alpha-1-\beta}}{dx^{\alpha-1-\beta}}1+(n-1)(\varphi^{n-1,\varphi})^{(\alpha-1-\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 1+(n-1)\varphi_{1}(x)(\varphi_{n-1}(x))^{\alpha-\beta+1}

such that

(5.5)

\forall\,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},|\beta|\leq m,n\in\mathbb{N},|x|\geq k:\,|(\psi\circ\varphi_{n})^{(\beta)}(x)|\leq C_{m}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{q_{m}}.

Next, we fix
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that

\forall\,n\geq n_{1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\max\{3,2^{2m}C_{m}^{2}\}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{2}\leq|\varphi_{n+1}(x)|.

From the continuity of
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n}=C_{\psi^{n,\varphi},\varphi_{n}}
and Corollary
2.3
we obtain the existence of
M_{m},q^{\prime}_{m}>0
with
q_{m}\leq q^{\prime}_{m}
such that

(5.6)

\forall\,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},|\beta|\leq m,1\leq n\leq n_{1},|x|\geq k:\,|(\psi^{n,\varphi})^{(\beta)}(x)|\leq M_{m}(1+|\varphi_{n}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}_{m}}.

Therefore, for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\beta|\leq m
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\geq k
we conclude

\displaystyle|(\psi^{n_{1}+1,\varphi})^{(\beta)}(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|(\psi^{n_{1},\varphi}\cdot(\psi\circ\varphi_{n_{1}}))^{(\beta)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}{\beta\choose\gamma}\left|\left(\psi^{n_{1},\varphi}\right)^{(\gamma)}(x)\right|\cdot\left|\left(\psi\circ\varphi_{n_{1}}\right)^{(\beta-\gamma)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{m}(1+|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}_{m}}\sum_{\gamma\leq\beta}{\gamma\choose\beta}C_{m}|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|^{q_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{m}((1+|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|)^{2})^{q^{\prime}_{m}/2}2^{m}C_{m}(|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|^{2})^{q_{m}/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{m}(1+3|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|^{2})^{q^{\prime}_{m}/2}(2^{2m}C^{2}_{m}|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|^{2})^{q_{m}/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{m}(1+|\varphi_{n_{1}+1}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}_{m}/2}|\varphi_{n_{1}+1}(x)|^{q_{m}/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{m}(1+|\varphi_{n_{1}+1}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}_{m}}

where we have also used that
2|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|\leq 2|\varphi_{n_{1}}(x)|^{2}
for
|x|\geq k
, and
1\leq q_{m}\leq q^{\prime}_{m}
. Thus, (
5.6
) not only holds for
0\leq n\leq n_{1}
but also for
n=n_{1}+1
. Proceeding recursively, we conclude

\forall\,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},|\beta|\leq m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},|x|\geq k:\,|(\psi^{n,\varphi})^{(\beta)}(x)|\leq M_{m}(1+|\varphi_{n}(x)|)^{q^{\prime}_{m}},

i.e. (
5.1
) which proves (i).

Assume that (
5.2
) holds. Refering to this additional hypothesis instead of (
5.4
), the same arguments which led to (
5.5
) yield

(5.7)

\forall\,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},|\beta|\leq m,n\geq n_{0},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|(\psi\circ\varphi_{n})^{(\beta)}(x)|\leq C_{m}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{q_{m}}.

With the aid of (
5.7
) in place of (
5.5
), the validity of (
5.3
) is derived with the same arguments as (
5.1
). This proves (ii).
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a symbol for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
C_{\varphi}
is power bounded. Assume that
|\varphi(x)|>0
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then condition (
5.2
) holds. Indeed, by Remark
4.7
there is
k>0
such that
|\varphi(x)|\geq k^{1/k}
whenever
|x|\geq k
. Since
|\varphi|
has no zeros and
\{x:|x|\leq k\}
is compact, there is
c>0
such that
|\varphi(x)|\geq c
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In particular,
|\varphi|\geq c
on each of the sets
\varphi_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which shows that (
5.2
) holds true.

Moreover, in case of
d=1
, the additional hypothesis (
5.2
) is automatically satisfied for every polynomial
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
for which
C_{\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
without fixed points and
\text{deg}(\varphi)\geq 2
. Indeed, we have either
\varphi(x)>x
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
or
\varphi(x)<x
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\varphi_{n}(x)|=\infty
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. By Remark
4.7
, there is
k>0
such that
|\varphi_{n}(x)|\geq k^{1/k}
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}\backslash[-k,k]
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We now consider the case
\varphi(x)>x,x\in\mathbb{R}
. The arguments for the case
\varphi(x)<x,x\in\mathbb{R}
, are mutatis mutandis the same. In particular,
(\varphi_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is strictly increasing for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Additionally, for each
x\in[-k,k]
there is
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi_{n}(x)>k^{1/k}
for all
n\geq n_{x}
. For every
x\in[-k,k]
, let
\delta_{x}>0
be such that
\varphi_{n_{x}}(y)>k^{1/k}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
with
|x-y|<\delta_{x}
. Since
[-k,k]
is compact and the sequences
(\varphi_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
x\in[-k,k]
, are strictly increasing, there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi_{n}(x)\geq k^{1/k}
for all
x\in[-k,k]
and
n\geq n_{0}
. We conclude that (
5.2
) is true.

Combining Remark
4.7
, Remark
4.6
, Lemma
5.6
, and Remark
5.7
, we immediately derive the following result.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a symbol for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
|\varphi_{n}|^{2}=o(|\varphi_{n+1}|)
uniformly as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, assume that there is
n_{0}
such that
|\varphi_{n_{0}}(x)|>0
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

C_{\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

C_{\psi,\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and/or for every smooth
\psi
of small decay with respect to
\varphi
.

In order to strengthen the above theorem in case
d=1
we first prove another auxiliary result.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
E
be a Montel locally convex space and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

(i)

If
\lambda T
is power bounded for some
\lambda>1
, then
(T^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent to 0 in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)
.

(ii)

If
\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}T^{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)
for some
k>0
, then
\lambda T
is power bounded for every
\lambda\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

If
\lambda>1
and
(\lambda^{n}T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
E
for every
x\in E
then
(T^{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent to 0 for every
x\in E
, and this is equivalent to
(T^{n})_{n}
is convergent to 0 in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)
since
(T^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicontinuous and
E
is Montel. This proves (i). To prove (ii), we proceed by contradiction. Let
0<\lambda<1
and let
x\in E
and let
p
be a continuous seminorm on
E
such that
(\lambda^{n}p(T^{n}(x)))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded. For every
k>0
,
\lambda^{n}<\frac{1}{n^{k}}
eventually, hence
\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}p(T^{n}(x))\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also unbounded.
∎

We can finally prove the main theorem of this subsection which complements Section
5.1
. Note that polynomials
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\text{deg}(\varphi)\geq 2
are symbols for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
by Remark
2.8
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial with
\text{deg}(\varphi)\geq 2
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

\varphi
does not have fixed points (hence,
\varphi
is of even degree).

(b)

C_{\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

(c)

C_{\varphi}
is (uniformly) mean ergodic on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

(d)

\left(C_{\varphi}^{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
0
in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
.

(e)

C_{\varphi}
is Cesàro bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e. the sequence
(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}C_{\varphi}^{m})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
.

(f)

C_{\psi,\varphi}
is power bounded on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
and/or for every smooth
\psi
of small decay with respect to
\varphi
.

(g)

(C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent to 0 in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
and/or for every smooth
\psi
of small decay with respect to
\varphi
.

(h)

(C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is (uniformly) mean ergodic on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
for every
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
and/or for every smooth
\psi
of small decay with respect to
\varphi
.

Proof.

The equivalence among (a), (b), and (c) is
[
14
, Theorem 3.11]
(combined with
[
28
, Theorem 2.5(b)]
), and (d) is equivalent to (b) due to
[
14
, Corollary 3.12]
. Clearly (b) implies (e). If we assume (e), then
\left(\frac{1}{n}C^{n}_{\varphi}\right)
is bounded in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
, and Proposition
5.9
(ii) yields that
C_{\psi_{0},\varphi}
is power bounded for
\psi_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{2}
. Proposition
5.5
implies that
C_{\varphi}
is power bounded and (e) implies (b). The equivalence (b) and (f) holds by Theorem
5.8
. Trivially, (g) implies (b), and if (f) is satisfied, then
C_{2\psi,\varphi}=2C_{\psi,\varphi}
is power bounded, and (g) follows by Proposition
5.9
(i). This shows that (a) to (g) are equivalent. Finally, (f) implies (h) by
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
and
[
28
, Theorem 2.5(b)]
while (h) implies (e) by
[
28
, Theorem 2.5(b)]
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Besides extending the results about composition operators on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
from
[
14
]
to weighted composition operators, showing that the ergodic properties rely only on the symbol, Theorem
5.10
also improves
[
14
, Theorem 3.11 and Corollary 3.12]
for composition operators, by showing that Cesàro boundedness and mean ergodicty are equivalent for composition operators. Analyzing the proof, we can even replace the property of Cesàro boundedness in condition (e) by the boundedness of
\left(\frac{1}{n^{k}}C_{\varphi_{n}}\right)
in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))
for some
k>0
.

6.
Supercyclicity of weighted composition operators with
\varphi
being a univariate polynomial.

In this short section we use the ideas of Lemma
5.6
in order to prove the next result which complements the results from
[
20
]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a polynomial such that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is weakly supercyclic for some
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\varphi(x)=x+b
with
b\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
\psi(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
then
C_{\psi,\varphi}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))\subseteq\text{Ker}(\delta_{x_{0}})
, and
C_{\psi,\varphi}
cannot be weakly supercyclic. If
\varphi(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{1})
for some
x_{0}\neq x_{1}
then
C_{\psi,\varphi}(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}))\subseteq\text{Ker}(\frac{\psi(x_{0})}{\psi(x_{1})}\delta_{x_{1}}-\delta_{x_{0}})
, and again we conclude that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is not weakly supercyclic. Hence
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is not weakly supercyclic if
\varphi
has even degree. If
\varphi
is constant, the range of
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is the span of
\psi
, and again
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is not weakly supercyclic.

When
\varphi(x)=ax+b
and
a\neq 1
,
a\neq 0
, then for
x_{0}:=-\frac{b}{a}
, if we denote by
X
the linear span of
\{\delta_{x_{0}},\delta_{x_{0}}^{(1)}\}\subseteq\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}
, satisfies
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{\prime}(X)\subseteq X
, and the matrix representing the restriction of
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{\prime}
to
X
in the basis
\{\delta_{x_{0}},\delta_{x_{0}}^{(1)}\}
is

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi(x_{0})&\psi^{\prime}(x_{0})\\
0&a\psi(x_{0})\end{array}\right).

Since
a\neq 1
we conclude that
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{\prime}
has two eigenvalues, and then
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is not weakly supercyclic by
[
3
, Proposition I.26]
.

When
\varphi(x)=x
,
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is a multiplication operator with
\{\delta_{x}:\ x\in\mathbb{R}\}
being eigenvectors of
C_{\psi,\varphi}^{\prime}
, and again we conclude by
[
3
, Proposition I.26]
that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
cannot be weakly supercyclic. If
\varphi
has two fixed points again the same argument applies.

To finish, we have to conclude that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
is not weakly supercyclic when
\varphi
is a polynomial of odd degree bigger or equal than 3 with only one fixed point
a
and
\psi(x)\neq 0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We proceed by contradiction. Since non null multiples of weakly supercyclic operators are weakly supercyclic, we can assume
\psi(x_{0})=1
. Under these hypotheses, there is
k>|a|
such that
|x|\geq k
implies
|\varphi(x)|>|x|
and
|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{2}<|\varphi_{n+1}(x)|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
M,q>0
such that
|\psi(x)|\leq M|x|^{q}
for
|x|>k
. We argue as in Lemma
5.6
to get
|\psi^{n,\varphi}(x)|\leq M|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{q}
for all
|x|>k
. From
[
3
, Proposition I.26]
we get that
C_{\psi,\varphi}
restricted to
\text{Ker}(\delta_{a})
is weakly hypercyclic, and, for any
|x|>k
and
f\in\text{Ker}(\delta_{a})
, being a hypercyclic vector

|C_{\psi,\varphi}^{n}f(x)|=|\psi^{n,\varphi}(x)|\cdot|f(\varphi_{n}(x))|\leq M|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{q}|f(\varphi_{n}(x))|\leq M\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|x|^{q}|f(x)|,

and this supremum is finite since
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
, which is a contradiction with the assumption that
f
is a weakly hypercyclic vector.
∎

For the translation operator, i.e.
C_{\varphi}
with
\varphi(x)=x+1
, we refer the reader to
[
20
]
for sufficient conditions on
\psi\in\mathscr{O}_{M}(\mathbb{R})
for
C_{\psi,\varphi}
to be weakly supercyclic on
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
as well as for other linear dynamical properties for these operators.

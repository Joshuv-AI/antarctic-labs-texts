Kuerak Chung, Bumsig Kim, Taejung Kim

Abstract
. We construct a Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg-type quasi-isomorphism for the negative cyclic homology of the category of global matrix factorizations on a smooth separated scheme of finite type over a field. The map is explicit enough to yield a negative cyclic Chern character formula for global matrix factorizations. We also extend these results to the equivariant case of a finite group.

Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Fix a field
k
of characteristic zero and let
X
be a smooth separated scheme of finite type over
k
.
Let
w
be a global function on
X
and let
{\mathbb{G}}
be either the group
{\mathbb{Z}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.
The structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
of
k
-algebras is defined to be concentrated in degree
0
as a
{\mathbb{G}}
-graded sheaf.
We require that the degree of
w
is 2. Hence if
w
is nonzero,

{\mathbb{G}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. If
w=0
, we allow either
{\mathbb{G}}
. Assume that the critical values of

are possibly zero.

We consider a differential
{\mathbb{G}}
-graded (in short dg) category
D_{dg}(X,w)
of

{\mathbb{G}}
-graded matrix factorizations for
(X,w)
. By a matrix factorization
(P,\delta_{P})
for
(X,w)
we mean
a locally free coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module
P
with a
{\mathbb{G}}
-grading and
\delta_{P}
is a curved differential of
P
. By definition,
\delta_{P}

is a degree
1

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-endomorphism of
P

satisfying
\delta_{P}^{2}=w\cdot\mathrm{id}_{P}
.
The Hom space from
P
to
Q
in
D_{dg}(X,w)
is taken to be a dg
k
-module
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}^{\bullet}(I(P),I(Q))
with
the chosen quasi-coherent curved injective replacements
I(P)
,
I(Q)
of
P
,
Q
respectively; see
[
1
]
.
Note that when
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathbb{Z}}
, then
w=0
and
the homotopy category of
D_{dg}(X,w)
is triangulated and equivalent to the derived category
of bounded complexes of coherent sheaves on
X
.
When
w\neq 0
, then
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathbb{Z}}/2

and the homotopy category of

is the derived category
of matrix factorizations on
X
. If
w\neq 0
in every component of
X
, the homotopy category is also equivalent to the singularity category
of the hypersurface
w^{-1}(0)
; see
[
17
, Theorem 2]
and
[
15
, Proposition 2.13]
.

The periodic cyclic homology and the Hochschild homology
of
D_{dg}(X,w)
play the roles of the de Rham cohomology and the Hodge cohomology
of the “space”
(X,w)
, respectively.
The negative cyclic homology
HN_{*}(D_{dg}(X,w))
encapsulates both homology theories, which is canonically isomorphic to

{\mathbb{H}}^{-*}(X,(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}[[u]],-dw+ud));

see
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
.
Here
u
is a formal variable with degree
2
and by definition the exterior derivative
d
has degree
-1
.
Each
(P,\delta_{P})
defines a tautological class in

so-called categorical Chern character
\mathrm{Ch}_{HN}(P)

Under the isomorphism,
\mathrm{Ch}_{HN}(P)
can be
written as an element
\mathrm{ch}_{HN}(P)
of
{\mathbb{H}}^{0}(X,(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}[[u]],-dw+ud))
. In this paper we find a formula for
\mathrm{ch}_{HN}(P)
by Chern-Weil theory and a Čech
resolution, which is a globalization of Brown – Walker’s formula
[
3
]
.

1.2.
A Chern character formula via Chern-Weil theory

We fix a finite open affine covering
\mathfrak{U}:=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
X
and a total ordering of the index set
I
.
Let
(P,\delta_{P})
be a matrix factorization for
(X,w)
as before.
Since each homogeneous part of
P|_{U_{i}}
corresponds to a
finitely generated projective module over a noetherian ring
\Gamma(U_{i},{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,

P|_{U_{i}}
always admits a connection

(1.1)

\nabla_{i}:P|_{U_{i}}\to P|_{U_{i}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i}}}\Omega^{1}_{U_{i}/k}.

It is a
k
-linear degree
-1
map satisfying
\nabla_{i}(pa)=\nabla_{i}(p)a+(-1)^{|p|}p\otimes da
for
p\in P|_{U_{i}}
,

.
We define the associated total curvature

(1.2)

R:=\prod_{i\in I}(u\nabla_{i}^{2}+[\nabla_{i},\delta_{P}])+\prod_{i<j,i,j\in I}(\nabla_{i}-\nabla_{j})

as an element in the Čech complex
\text{\rm\v{C}}(\mathfrak{U},{\mathcal{E}}nd(P)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\Omega_{X/k}^{\bullet}[[u]])
; see (
6.2
) and
Remark
5.6
.
Note that the total degree of each term in the curvature
R
is zero.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
6.1
)
The following Chern character formula holds:

(1.3)

\mathrm{ch}_{HN}(P)={\mathrm{tr}}\exp(-R)

in the ordered Čech cohomology of complexes of sheaves

\check{H}^{0}(\mathfrak{U},(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}[[u]],-dw+ud)).

Here the expression

\exp(-R)=\mathrm{id_{P}}-R+R^{2}/2-\cdots\cdots+(-1)^{\dim X}R^{\dim X}/(\dim X)!

uses the product formula (
4.5
) in the Čech cochain complex and

{\mathrm{tr}}
denotes the supertrace (
5.10
).

When
|I|=1
so that
X
is affine,
formula (
1.3
) is the main result of Brown and Walker
[
3
]
.
When
u=0
,

\mathrm{ch}_{HN}(P)
becomes the image
\mathrm{ch}_{HH}(P)
of the Hochschild homology valued Chern character
\mathrm{Ch}_{HH}(P)
.
Formula (
1.3
) specialized to

matches with those of Căldăraru
[
4
]
for
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathbb{Z}}
; and Platt
[
18
]

and Kim and Polishchuk
[

]
for

isomorphisms). We will also get a localized Chern character formula; see §

1.3.
A chain-level HKR-type map

We will prove Theorem
1.1
by an explicit quasi-isomorphism, which is a globalization of the generalized Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg (in short HKR) map in the
affine case
[
3
, Theorem 5.19]
.
The ordinary HKR map
[
16
, § 1.3.15]
uses the canonical de Rham differential
d
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.
While
P
does not admit a global connection in general, there is a connection on
P|_{U_{i}}
.
This leads us to consider the Čech model
D_{\mathrm{\check{C}}}(X,w)
of
D_{dg}(X,w)
; see §
4.2.2
.
Let
\mathrm{\check{C}}(P)
denote the sheafified version of the ordered Čech
k
-complex
\text{\rm\v{C}}(\mathfrak{U},P)
for
P
; see (
4.2
).
The objects of
D_{\mathrm{\check{C}}}(X,w)
are those of
D_{dg}(X,w)
. The Hom space
{\mathrm{Hom}}(P,Q)
is
defined to be the dg
k
-space

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{\check{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{\check{C}}(P),\mathrm{\check{C}}(Q))

\displaystyle\cong\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}^{\bullet}(P,Q)\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\mathrm{\check{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}))

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{p}\text{\rm\v{C}}^{p}(\mathfrak{U},{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}^{\bullet}(P,Q)).

Its underlying graded
k
-space is the linear space of graded right
\mathrm{\check{C}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-linear sheaf homomorphisms from
\mathrm{\check{C}}(P)
to
\mathrm{\check{C}}(Q)
.
Its differential is given as follows.
Let
\delta_{\mathrm{\check{C}}(P)}=\delta_{P}+d_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\v{C}ech}}
, the sum of the differential
\delta_{P}
of
P
and the Čech differential
d_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\v{C}ech}}
.
Then the differential of
{\mathrm{Hom}}(P,Q)
is defined by sending

f\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(P,Q)
to

\delta_{\mathrm{\check{C}}(Q)}\circ f-(-1)^{|f|}f\circ\delta_{\mathrm{\check{C}}(P)}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(P,Q),

where
|f|
denotes the degree of
f
.

For each matrix factorization
P
for
(X,w)
and
i\in I
,
choose, once and for all, a connection
\nabla_{P,i}
on
P|_{U_{i}}
. Hence we have a zero-th global
Č
ech element

\nabla_{P}:=\prod_{i\in I}\nabla_{P|_{U_{i}}}

of the
k
-sheaf
{\mathcal{H}}om_{k}(\text{\rm\v{C}}(P),\text{\rm\v{C}}(P)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X})
.

When
P={\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, we choose the de Rham differential as a connection on
{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i}}
.
Let
C(D_{\mathrm{\check{C}}}(X,w))
denote the Hochschild complex
of the dg category
D_{\mathrm{\check{C}}}(X,w)
. Using the supertrace
{\mathrm{tr}}
(
5.10
) and the product (
4.5
)
we define a
k[[u]]
-linear map

(1.5)

{\mathrm{tr}}_{\nabla}:C(D_{\mathrm{\check{C}}}(X,w))[[u]]\to\text{\rm\v{C}}(\mathfrak{U},\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k})[[u]]

by sending

(1.6)

{\alpha}_{0}[{\alpha}_{1}|...|{\alpha}_{n}]\mapsto\sum_{(j_{0},...,j_{n})}(-1)^{J}{\mathrm{tr}}\big(\frac{{\alpha}_{0}R_{1}^{j_{0}}[\nabla,{\alpha}_{1}]R_{2}^{j_{1}}[\nabla,{\alpha}_{2}]...R_{n}^{j_{n-1}}[\nabla,{\alpha}_{n}]R_{0}^{j_{n}}}{(n+J)!}\big)

for
{\alpha}_{j}\in\text{\rm\v{C}}(\mathfrak{U},{\mathcal{H}}om_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}(P_{j+1},P_{j}))
with
P_{n+1}=P_{0}
,
where
j_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
J=\sum_{i=0}^{n}j_{i}
; and

\displaystyle[\nabla,{\alpha}_{j}]

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{P_{j}}\cdot{\alpha}_{j}-(-1)^{|{\alpha}_{j}|}{\alpha}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{P_{j+1}};

\displaystyle R_{j}

\displaystyle:=u\nabla_{P_{j}}^{2}+[\nabla_{P_{j}},\delta_{P_{j}}]+\prod_{i_{0}<i_{1}}(\nabla_{P_{j},i_{0}}-\nabla_{P_{j},i_{1}});

see (
5.22
) for unwinded expressions.
Note that when
|I|=1
and
P_{i}
is
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
for

, formula (
1.6
) is the usual HKR map.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem

)
Let

As an application of Theorem

Let
X

We consider the dg category

of
G

(X,w)

We choose a finite open affine covering
\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
U_{i}
is
G

For a finite open affine covering of the

-fixed locus

Let
\nabla_{P|_{U_{i}}}
be a

\nabla_{P|_{X^{g}},i}
on

to be the restriction of
\nabla_{P|_{U_{i}}}
to

followed by the natural map

P|_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}}}\Omega^{1}_{U_{i}/k}|_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}}\to P|_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}}}\Omega^{1}_{X^{g}\cap U_{i}/k}\ .

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

.

(1)

(Theorem
6.9
)
The mixed Hochschild complex
(C(D_{\text{\rm\v{C}}}^{G}(X,w)),b,B)
is naturally quasi-isomorphic to the coinvariant mixed complex

\left(\oplus_{g\in G}(\Gamma(X^{g},\text{\rm\v{C}}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X^{g}/k})),d_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\v{C}ech}}-dw_{g},d)\right)_{G}.

(Formula (

)) For

Specializing to the case where X is affine space, this recovers the results

[

of

and

(

In fact we will establish Theorem

2.1.
Curved dg
k
-categories

2.1.1.
Curved dg algebras

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero.
A
k
-category is a
k
-algebra with several objects.
A
k
-algebra is a
k
-module with a unital associative
k
-linear multiplication.
Let
A
be a graded algebra over
k
and let
h
be a degree
2
element of
A
. Let
d_{A}
be a degree
1
,
k
-linear map such that
(1) the Leibniz rule holds (i.e.,
d_{A}(a_{1}a_{2})=d_{A}(a_{1})a_{2}+(-1)^{|a_{1}|}a_{1}d_{A}(a_{2})
for
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
),
(2)
d_{A}^{2}=[h,?]
(i.e.,
d_{A}^{2}(a)=ha-ah
for
a\in A
), and (3)
d_{A}(h)=0
. Then we call

(A,d_{A},h)
a
curved dg
(in short cdg)

k
-algebra
.
Furthermore, if
h=0
, then we call
(A,d_{A})
a
dg
k
-algebra
.

2.1.2.
Curved dg categories

There is the notion of a
cdg
k
-category

{\mathcal{A}}
as a cdg
k
-algebra with several objects.
It is a graded
k
-category with a differential
d_{x,y}
of
{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)
and a degree
2
element
h_{x}\in{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)
for every
x,y\in{\mathcal{A}}

satisfying the Leibniz rule,
d_{x,y}^{2}(f)=h_{y}\circ f-f\circ h_{x}
for
f\in{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)
, and
d_{x,x}(h_{x})=0
. The element
h_{x}
is called
the
curvature
of
x
. If
h_{x}=0
for all
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we call
{\mathcal{A}}
a
dg
k
-category
.

2.1.3.
Precomplexes

There is a cdg category
\mathrm{Com}_{cdg}(k)
, the category of
precomplexes
of
k
-modules:
an object is a graded
k
-module
C
with a
k
-linear degree
1
map
\delta_{C}

and the Hom space from
C
to
D
has a differential
[\delta_{?},]
.
The curvature of
C
is by definition
\delta_{C}^{2}
. The full subcategory
\mathrm{Com}_{dg}(k)
of all objects with vanishing
curvatures is called the
dg category of
k
-complexes
.

2.1.4.
Cdg functors

A pair
(F,{\alpha})
is called a
quasi-cdg functor

from a cdg category
{\mathcal{A}}
to another cdg category
\mathcal{B}
if

F
is a
k
-linear degree
0
homogeneous functor
and
{\alpha}
is an assignment of a degree
1
element
{\alpha}_{x}\in\mathcal{B}(Fx,Fx)
for each
x\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that

F(d_{x,y}(f))=d_{Fx,Fy}(F(f))+{\alpha}_{y}\circ F(f)-(-1)^{|f|}F(f)\circ{\alpha}_{x}

for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)
.
Furthermore if
F(h_{x})=h_{Fx}+d_{Fx,Fx}({\alpha}_{x})+{\alpha}_{x}^{2}
,
(F,{\alpha})
is called a
cdg functor
.
We often say simply
F
is a (quasi-)cdg functor for a (quasi-)cdg functor
(F,{\alpha})
.
If
{\alpha}_{x}=0
for every
x
, we call
F

strict
.
In particular if
{\mathcal{A}}
,
\mathcal{B}
are dg categories and
F
is a strict cdg functor,
F
is just called a
dg functor
.
Informally a dg functor is a set of several “cochain” maps.
If
(G,\beta):\mathcal{B}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a quasi-cdg functor, then the composition of
(G,{\beta})
and
(F,{\alpha})
is defined as
(G\circ F,G\circ{\alpha}+{\beta}\circ F)
.
Note that the composition is a quasi-cdg functor and the composition of cdg functors becomes a cdg functor.

2.1.5.
The opposite category

The opposite category
{\mathcal{A}}^{op}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is a cdg category whose objects are
the objects of
{\mathcal{A}}
and whose morphisms are
{\mathcal{A}}^{op}(x,y):={\mathcal{A}}(y,x)
with differential
d_{y,x}
.
The composition
g\circ f
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{op}
is
(-1)^{|f||g|}f\circ g
in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
the curvature of
x
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{op}
is
-h_{x}
.

2.1.6.
Left and right (quasi-)cdg modules

A left (resp., right)
quasi-cdg module
over a cdg category
{\mathcal{A}}
is a strict quasi-cdg functor
F:{\mathcal{A}}\ (\text{resp., }{\mathcal{A}}^{op})\to\mathrm{Com}_{cdg}(k)
.
A left (resp., right)
cdg module
over
{\mathcal{A}}
is a strict cdg functor
F:{\mathcal{A}}\ (\text{resp., }{\mathcal{A}}^{op})\to\mathrm{Com}_{cdg}(k)
.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a dg category and
F:{\mathcal{A}}\ (\text{resp., }{\mathcal{A}}^{op})\to Com_{dg}(k)

is a left (resp., right)

over
{\mathcal{A}}
.

The category
q\mathrm{Mod}_{cdg}({\mathcal{A}})
of right quasi-cdg modules over
{\mathcal{A}}
has a natural cdg structure:
The Hom space
{\mathrm{Hom}}(F,G)
of two quasi-cdg modules

is the
{\mathbb{G}}
-graded
k

k
-linear homogenous natural transformations
\mathfrak{n}:F\Rightarrow G
.
The differential
\delta

{\mathrm{Hom}}(F,G)
is given by

and the curvature

of

h_{F}(x)=h_{F(x)}-F(h_{x})
.
The full subcategory
\mathrm{Mod}_{cdg}({\mathcal{A}})
of
q\mathrm{Mod}_{cdg}({\mathcal{A}})
consisting of right cdg modules over
{\mathcal{A}}
has a natural dg structure.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a dg category, we write
\mathrm{Mod}_{dg}({\mathcal{A}})
for
\mathrm{Mod}_{cdg}({\mathcal{A}})
.

2.1.7.
Matrix factorizations

By unwinding the definition, a right quasi-cdg module
M

3.

In this section we recall the definition of categorical Chern characters
and discuss alternative expressions of them which we will use in

3.1.
Chern characters
\mathrm{Ch}_{HH}(P)
and
\mathrm{Ch}_{HN}(P)

For every object

of a dg category
{\mathcal{A}}
, note that
the identity map
1_{P}
is a degree

cocycle of the Hochschild complex of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Hence each

determines the class
[1_{P}]
in

We call
[1_{P}]
the Hochschild homology valued Chern character of

\mathrm{Ch}_{HH}(P)

Likewise, since
1_{P}
is a cocycle of the normalized negative cyclic complex of
{\mathcal{A}}
, we simply define
the negative cyclic homology valued Chern character
\mathrm{Ch}_{HN}(P)
of
P
to be the class
[1_{P}]
in
\overline{HN}_{0}({\mathcal{A}})\cong HN_{0}({\mathcal{A}})
.

For a cdg category
{\mathcal{A}}
and
P\in q\mathrm{Perf}{\mathcal{A}}
we define Chern characters
\mathrm{Ch}^{II}_{HH}(P)
,
\mathrm{Ch}^{II}_{HN}(P)
of the second kind in the same manner
using
1_{P}
in the normalized complexes of the second kind.

3.2.
Alternative expression via direct summands

Let
P
and
N
be objects of a dg category
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Suppose that
P
is a direct summand of
N
, i.e., there are
g:P\to N

and
f:N\to P
degree
0
closed homomorphisms such that

.
Note that
1_{P}
and the idempotent

are homologous

(3.1)

1_{P}\sim\pi

in the Hochschild complex of
{\mathcal{A}}
; see
[
24
]
.
If
N
is simpler than

, then

is often easier to handle than

.

3.2.1.

More generally, every element of
C(\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{A}}}P)
is homologous to an element of
C(\mathrm{End}_{{\mathcal{A}}}N)
in the Hochschild complex of
{\mathcal{A}}
:
Consider the inclusion functor
inc
from
\{N\}
to
\{P,N\}
between the full dg subcategories of

{\mathcal{A}}
consisting only one indicated object, two indicated objects respectively.
As a semifunctor (i.e., a dg functor not necessarily preserving identities between dg categories),

has a left inverse
F
.

\{N\}\xleftarrow{F}\{P,N\}\xleftarrow{inc}\{N\}.

The functor
F:\{P,N\}\to\{N\}
is determined by sending

{\alpha}\mapsto g\circ{\alpha}\circ f
for
{\alpha}\in\mathrm{End}P
,
{\alpha}^{\prime}\mapsto g\circ{\alpha}^{\prime}
for
{\alpha}^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(N,P)

{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\mapsto{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}\circ f
for

Lemma 3.1
.

In the Hochschild complex
(C\{P,N\},b)
,
every element
{\alpha}_{0}[{\alpha}_{1}|....|{\alpha}_{n}]
is homologous to
F({\alpha}_{0})[F({\alpha}_{1})|...|F({\alpha}_{n})]

Proof.

The semifunctor
F
induces a cochain map
F_{*}:(C\{P,N\},b)\to(C\{N\},b)

which is a left inverse of the quasi-isomorphism
inc_{*}:(C\{N\},b)\to(C\{P,N\},b)

induced from

. Therefore for every
m\in C\{P,N\}
,
m
is homologous to
inc_{*}(F_{*}(m))
, since
F_{*}(m)=F_{*}(inc_{*}(F_{*}(m)))
.
∎

A weak Galerkin finite element method for solving the asymptotic lower bound of Maxwell eigenvalue problem
Shusheng Li, Qilong Zhai

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for the Maxwell eigenvalue problem. By restricting subspaces, we transform the mixed form of Maxwell eigenvalue problem into simple elliptic equation. Then we give the WG numerical scheme for the Maxwell eigenvalue problem. Furthermore, we obtain the optimal error estimates of arbitrarily high convergence order and prove the lower bound property of numerical solutions for eigenvalues. Numerical experiments show the accuracy of theoretical analysis and the property of lower bound.



keywords

weak Galerkin finite element method, Maxwell eigenvalue problem, asymptotic lower bound,
error estimate.

Introduction

The computation of eigenvalues in Maxwell equations is crucial in computational electromagnetics. Since Silvester introduced the nodal-based finite element method to electrical engineering
[
23
]
, it has demonstrated several advantages for solving homogeneous waveguide problems. However, when applied to inhomogeneous waveguide problems, this method can yield spurious modes with nonzero eigenvalues. Considerable effort has been dedicated to reducing or eliminating these unwanted numerical artifacts caused by spurious modes
[
8
]
. Rahman and Winkler noted that spurious modes fail to satisfy the zero divergence condition on the electric or magnetic field, and they proposed a penalty function to enforce this condition
[
10
,
22
,
26
]
. Unfortunately, this approach does not completely eliminate the spurious modes and requires users to select an appropriate penalty function parameter.

Another approach to eliminating spurious modes in finite element waveguide problems involves selecting appropriate finite element approximation functions
[
8
,
20
,
21
]
. It is well-established that using
H(\rm{curl;\Omega})
-conforming basis functions (also known as edge elements) for the electric field
[
20
,
21
]
ensure the continuity of tangential field components across interfaces between different media, while allowing normal field components to jump across such interfaces. With the edge element method, spurious modes with nonzero eigenvalues were eliminated. However, the number of spurious modes with zero eigenvalues equal the number of nodal points inside the computational domain, due to the violation of Gauss’s law
[
2
]
. Therefore, to remove these zero eigenvalues, it is necessary to enforce the divergence-free property (Gauss’s law) of the eigenfunction in addition to using the proper finite element space. One effective approach for computing eigenvalues in waveguide problems is to employ

H(\rm{curl;\Omega})
-conforming basis functions to approximate the electric field, while imposing the divergence-free condition using a Lagrange multiplier, as suggested by Kikuchi
[
9
]
.

Meanwhile, higher-order methods like the spectral element method
[
11
,
15
]
have been proposed to solve electromagnetic eigenvalue problems. Despite their high convergence rates, these methods also suffer from the presence of spurious zero eigenvalues. In
[
14
]
, Liu et al. introduced the mixed spectral element method, which incorporates the divergence-free equation from Kikuchi’s scheme into the spectral element method.

There are many methods to study the Maxwell eigenvalue problems. Based on a Helmholtz decomposition of the error, Boffi et al.
[
5
]
presented an
a posteriori
estimator of the error in the
L^{2}
-norm for the numerical approximation of the Maxwell eigenvalue problems using N
{\rm\acute{e}}
d
{\rm\acute{e}}
lec finite elements. Furthermore, based on edge finite elements, Boffi and Gastaldi
[
4
]
proved the optimal convergence of a adaptive scheme. In
[
6
]
, Boffi et al. introduced a residual error indicator for the N
{\rm\acute{e}}
d
{\rm\acute{e}}
lec finite element approximation for Maxwell eigenvalue problems. In addition, based on a domain decomposition method, Liang and Xu
[
12
]
proposed a two-level preconditioned Helmholtz subspace iterative method for solving algebraic eigenvalue problems arising from edge element approximations of Maxwell eigenvalue problems. In certain settings, Boffi et al.
[
7
]
prove the convergence of the discrete eigenvalues using Lagrange finite elements.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce
the Maxwell eigenvalue problem and three equivalent variational forms are given.
In Section 3, we introduce the weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method for the Maxwell eigenvalue problem, then propose the WG numerical scheme.
The error estimates of eigenfunctions and eigenvalues are analyzed in Section 4. Section 5 is devoted to prove the lower bounda of eigenvalues.
Numerical examples are presented to our theoretical analysis in Section 6.

2
The Maxwell eigenvalue problem

In this paper, for simplicity, we consider the following Maxwell eigenvalue problem

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \nabla\times\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}&=k^{2}\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,&\\
\nabla\cdot\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u}&=0,&\quad&\text{in }\Omega,&\\
\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{n}&=0,&\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,&\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is a polygon region in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
, and
\varepsilon_{r}
and
\mu_{r}
denote the relative dielectric permittivity and magnetic permeability tensors, respectively, and
\mathbf{n}
is the
outward unit normal. For convenience, we consider the case where
\varepsilon_{r}
and
\mu_{r}
are constants in this paper.

The standard Sobolev space notation are also used in this paper.
Let
D
be any open bounded
domain with Lipschitz continuous boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
.
We use the standard definition for the Sobolev space
H^{s}(D)
and
their associated inner products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{s,D}
, norms

\|\cdot\|_{s,D}
, and seminorms
|\cdot|_{s,D}
for any
s\geq 0
.
When
D=\Omega
, we shall drop the
subscript
D
in the norm and in the inner product notation. Next, we define the curl operators in 2D:

\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=\partial_{x}v_{2}-\partial_{y}v_{1},\quad\nabla\times w=(\partial_{y}w,-\partial_{x}w)^{T}.

In addition, we define some Sobolev space used in this paper.

\displaystyle H({\rm curl};\Omega)=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}:\nabla\times\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2d-3}\right\},

\displaystyle H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)=\{\mathbf{v}\in H({\rm curl};\Omega):\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},

\displaystyle H_{0}({\rm curl^{0}};\Omega)=\{\mathbf{v}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega):\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=0\},

\displaystyle H({\rm div};\Omega)=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}:\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\},

\displaystyle H({\rm div^{0}};\Omega)=\{\mathbf{v}\in H({\rm div};\Omega):\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\}.

Then we introduce the following Helmholtz decomposition:

H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)=U_{0}\oplus\nabla H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where
U_{0}=\{\mathbf{v}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega):(\mathbf{v},\nabla p)=0,\forall p\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\}=\{\mathbf{v}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega):\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\}
and
\nabla H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=H_{0}({\rm curl^{0}};\Omega)
.

The corresponding variational form of (
2.1
) is: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
such that

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})&=\lambda(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}),&\quad&\forall\mathbf{v}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega),&\\
(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\nabla p)&=0,&\quad&\forall p\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).&\end{aligned}\right.

Here we introduce a mixed form
[
9
]
that is equivalent to (
2.2
): Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{u}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
,
p\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})+(\nabla p,\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{v})&=\lambda(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})&\quad&\forall\mathbf{v}\in H_{0}({\rm curl};\Omega),&\\
(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\nabla q)&=0&\quad&\forall q\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).&\end{aligned}\right.

Using the Helmholtz decomposition, we have the following equivalent form: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{u}\in U_{0}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(2.4)

(\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v})=\lambda(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}),\quad\forall\mathbf{v}\in U_{0}.

In this paper, we only consider form (
2.4
). Therefore, we give the variational form of Maxwell eigenvalue problem (
2.1
): Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{u}\in U_{0}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(2.5)

a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=\lambda b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}),\quad\forall\mathbf{v}\in U_{0},

where

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=

\displaystyle(\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{u},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}),

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}).

3
The weak Galerkin finite element method

In this section, the weak Galerkin finite element numerical scheme of Maxwell eigenvalue problem is given.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a partition of the domain
\Omega
, and the elements
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
are polygons satisfying the regular assumptions specified
in
[
24
]
. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the edges in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and by

{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
the interior edges
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\backslash\partial\Omega
. For
each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
h_{T}
represents the diameter of
T
, and

h=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}
denotes the mesh size. Let
C
be a positive constant which is
independent of the mesh size and
a\lesssim b
denote
a\leq Cb
.

Now we introduce the WG space for the eigenvalue problem (
2.1
).
For a given integer
k\geq 1
, define the WG finite element space

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{\mathbf{v}_{h}=\{\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{b}\}:\mathbf{v}_{0}|_{T}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{d},\mathbf{v}_{b}|_{e}\in[P_{k}(e)]^{d},e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{b}\times\mathbf{n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\},

\displaystyle W_{h}=\{w=\{w_{0},w_{b}\}:w_{0}|_{T}\in P_{k-1}(T),w_{b}|_{e}\in P_{k}(e),e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},w_{b}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

Next, we define the following weak curl operator.

Definition 1
.

For any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
, its weak curl is defined as the polynomial
\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{T}
is the unique polynomial in

[P_{k-1}(T)]^{2d-3}
satisfying

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{q})_{T}=(\mathbf{v}_{0},\nabla\times\mathbf{q})_{T}-\langle\mathbf{v}_{b}\times\mathbf{n},\mathbf{q}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\mathbf{q}\in[P_{k-1}(T)]^{2d-3}.

In addition, we define the following weak gradient operator.

Definition 2
.

For any
p_{h}\in W_{h}
, its weak gradient is defined as the polynomial
\nabla_{w}p_{h}|_{T}
is the unique polynomial in

[P_{k}(T)]^{d}
satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}p_{h},\phi)_{T}=-(p_{0},\nabla\cdot\phi)_{T}+\langle p_{b},\phi\cdot\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall\phi\in[P_{k}(T)]^{d}.

Then we define the subspace
V_{h}^{0}
of
V_{h}
:

V_{h}^{0}=\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}:(\mathbf{v}_{0},\nabla_{w}q)=0,\forall q\in W_{h}\}.

For any
\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, define the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle s(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\gamma(h)\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\l\langle(\mathbf{u}_{0}-\mathbf{u}_{b})\times\mathbf{n},(\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{b})\times\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T},

\displaystyle a_{w}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{u}_{h},\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h})_{T}+s(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})

\displaystyle b_{w}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0}),

where
\gamma(h)=h^{\varepsilon},0<\varepsilon<1,\text{ or }\gamma(h)=-\frac{1}{\log(h)}
.

For further error analysis, we introduce some projection operators used
in this paper. Let
\mathbf{Q}_{0}
be the
L^{2}
projection from
[L^{2}(T)]^{d}

onto
[P_{k}(T)]^{d}
,
Q_{0}
be the
L^{2}
projection from
L^{2}(T)

onto
P_{k-1}(T)
.
Let
\mathbf{Q}_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection from
[L^{2}(e)]^{d}
onto

[P_{k}(e)]^{d}
, and
Q_{b}
denote the
L^{2}
projection from
L^{2}(T)
onto
P_{k}(T)
. In addition, let
\mathbb{Q}_{h}
be the
L^{2}
projection from
[L^{2}(T)]^{2d-3}

onto
[P_{k}(T)]^{2d-3}
.
Combining
\mathbf{Q}_{0}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{b}

together, we can define
\mathbf{Q}_{h}=\{\mathbf{Q}_{0},\mathbf{Q}_{b}\}
. Analogously, denote
Q_{h}=\{Q_{0},Q_{b}\}
.

The following commutativity properties are crucial in error analysis.

Lemma 3
.

[
19
, Lemma 5.1]

For any
\mathbf{v}\in H({\rm curl};\Omega)
, we have

\nabla_{w}\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{v}=\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}),

and for any
q\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, there holds

\nabla_{w}Q_{h}q=\mathbf{Q}_{0}(\nabla q).

Now we give the numerical scheme of (
2.1
) corresponding to (
2.4
).

Weak Galerkin Algorithm 1
.

Find
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h}^{0}
such that
\|\mathbf{u}_{h}\|=1
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle a_{w}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{w}(\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{0}),\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

Define the sum space
V=U_{0}+V_{h}^{0}
.
Now we define the following semi-norm on
V
. For any
\mathbf{v}\in V
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\|_{V}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(\mu_{r}^{-1}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{b})\times\mathbf{n}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big),

where
\mathbf{v}_{0}
is the internal value and
\mathbf{v}_{b}
is the boundary value.

The following Friedrichs inequality
[
3
]
holds on
U_{0}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|\mathbf{u}\|\leq C\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|,\quad\forall\mathbf{u}\in U_{0}.

Therefore, from Lemma
4
,
\|\cdot\|_{V}
is a norm on
U_{0}
.

Lemma 5
.

\|\cdot\|_{V}
defines a norm on
V_{h}^{0}
.

Proof.

We prove that
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}=0
if and only if
\mathbf{v}_{h}=0
.
If
\mathbf{v}_{h}=0
, obviously,
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}=0
.
Next, assume that
\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}=0
. Then we have
\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
(\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{b})\times\mathbf{n}=0
for any
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.
Notice that
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
, then for any
q_{h}\in W_{h}
, there holds

\displaystyle b_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{0},q_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{v}_{0},\nabla_{w}q_{h})_{T}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{v}_{0}),q_{0})_{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{v}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{n},q_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=0.

Specially, let
q_{0}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}
and
q_{b}=0
, then we have
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Let
q_{0}=0
and
q_{b}
is the jump of
\mathbf{v}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{n}
on
e
, then we can get that
\mathbf{v}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{n}
is continuous on
e
.
Notice that
\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
, then there exists a potential function
\phi
such that
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\nabla\phi
in
\Omega
. In addition,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
and
\mathbf{v}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{n}
is continuous, there holds
\phi=0

in
\Omega
. Notice that
\mathbf{v}_{b}\times\mathbf{n}=0
on

\partial\Omega
, and
(\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{b})\times\mathbf{n}=0
for any
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
,
Thus,
\mathbf{v}_{0}\times\mathbf{n}=\nabla\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Therefore,

\phi
must be a constant on
\partial\Omega
. From the uniqueness of the solution of the Laplace equation, if
\phi
is simply connected,
\phi
must be a constant in
\Omega
.
Then
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\nabla\phi=0
and
\mathbf{v}_{b}=0
.
∎

4
Error analysis

By limiting to subspaces, we transform the Maxwell eigenvalue problem into an elliptic eigenvalue problem. Therefore, we can use the framework presented in
[
27
]
and just prove Assumption 1 (A1)-Assumption 7 (A7) in Section 2 of
[
27
]
.

First we verify (A1):

a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
are symmetric, and

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w})\geq\gamma\|\mathbf{w}\|_{V}^{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in U_{0},

\displaystyle a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq\gamma(h)\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}^{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V_{h},

where
\gamma
and
\gamma(h)
are two positive constants for given
h
.

Obviously,
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive and continuous on
U_{0}
. And
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous on
V_{h}^{0}
, we only prove that
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive on
V_{h}^{0}
.

Lemma 6
.

For any
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in V^{0}_{h}
, we have

a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})\geq C\gamma(h)\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}^{2}.

Proof.

It is easy to prove

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{v}_{0}-\mathbf{v}_{b})\times\mathbf{n}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}).

From integration by parts, (
3.1
), Young inequality, trace inequality
[
24
]
and inverse inequality
[
24
]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0})_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})_{T}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0}\times\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h},\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0})_{T}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0},(\mathbf{v}_{b}-\mathbf{v}_{0})\times\mathbf{n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+C\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{v}_{b}-\mathbf{v}_{0})\times\mathbf{n}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{v}_{b}-\mathbf{v}_{0})\times\mathbf{n}\|_{\partial T}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{T}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{v}_{b}-\mathbf{v}_{0})\times\mathbf{n}\|_{\partial T}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}),

which implies that

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}).

Combining the two equations above, we have

\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{V}^{2}\leq C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Before verifying (A2), we define the following two operators

Now we verify (A3): there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathbf{Q}_{h}:V\rightarrow V_{h}^{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{w}_{h}=\mathbf{w}_{h},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{h})=b(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{h}),\quad\forall\mathbf{f}\in V,\mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

Obviously, for any
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{w}_{h}=\mathbf{w}_{h}
, and

b(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{h})=(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0})=(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0})=b(\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{w}_{h}).

Further we need to prove
\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{f}\in V_{h}^{0}
. For any
q_{h}\in W_{h}
, it follows from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{f},\nabla_{w}q_{h})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{f},\nabla_{w}q_{h})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{f},\nabla_{w}q_{h})

\displaystyle=-(\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{f}),q_{0})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{n},q_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=0,

which uses the fact that
\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{n},q_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=0
. Then (A3) is verified.

Denote

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in R(E_{\mu}(K)),\atop\|\mathbf{v}\|_{V}=1}\|\mathbf{v}-Q_{h}\mathbf{v}\|_{V},

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in R(E_{\mu}(K)),\atop\|\mathbf{v}\|_{V}=1}\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{v}\|,

\displaystyle e_{h,\mu}^{\prime}=\|(\Pi_{0}K-\Pi_{0}K_{h}Q_{h})|_{R(E_{\mu}(K))}\|_{X}.

Next we verify (A4) and (A5):

\displaystyle(A4):e_{h,\mu}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,{\rm and}~\delta_{h,\mu}\gamma(h)^{-1}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle(A5):e_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,{\rm and}~\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\gamma(h)^{-1}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0.

Lemma 8
.

Assume
R(E_{\mu}(K))\subset H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, there hold the following estimates

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}\lesssim h^{k},

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\lesssim h^{k+1}.

Proof.

It follows from the trace inequality and the projection inequality
[

]
that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u})\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|(\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{b}\mathbf{u})\times\mathbf{n}\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u})\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\mu_{r}^{-1}\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u})\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}C(h_{T}^{-2}\|\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}+\|\nabla(\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u})\|_{T}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{2k},

and

\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|^{2}\lesssim h^{2(k+1)}.

Then we complete the proof.
∎

&(\varepsilon_{r}\mathbf{u}_{0},\nabla_{w}q_{h})+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}\langle p_{0}-p_{b},q_{0}-q_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=0&\quad&\forall q_{h}\in W_{h}.&\end{aligned}\right.

5
Asymptotic lower bound

In this section, we show that the numerical eigenvalues are the lower bound of the exact eigenvalues.

It only need to verify (A7): Suppose
(\lambda,\mathbf{u})
is an eigenpair of
(\ref{weak form})
,
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})
is an eigenpair of
(\ref{WG-scheme})
, there holds
\varepsilon_{h,\mathbf{u}}\geq\lambda_{h}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|^{2}.

The following estimates are crucial, and the proof can be found in
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 15
.

The exact eigenfunction
\mathbf{u}
of the Steklov eigenvalue problem
(\ref{weak form})
exhibits lower bounds for its convergence rates as follows

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}\gtrsim h^{2k}.

Lemma 16
.

Let
(\lambda,\mathbf{u})
be an eigenpair of
(\ref{weak form})
,
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})
be an eigenpair of
(\ref{WG-scheme})
. For sufficiently small
h
,
\gamma(h)\ll 1
, the following estimate holds

\varepsilon_{h,\mathbf{u}}\geq\lambda_{h}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3
that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{h,\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})-a_{w}(\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}\times\mathbf{Q}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}-s(Q_{h}u,Q_{h}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\gamma(h)h_{T}^{-1}\|\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{b}\mathbf{u}\|_{\partial T}^{2}.

Combining Lemma
15
with the trace inequality
[
24
]
, there holds

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\times\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\times\mathbf{u}\|_{T}^{2}\gtrsim h^{2k},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\gamma(h)h_{T}^{-1}\|\mathbf{Q}_{0}\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{Q}_{b}\mathbf{u}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim\gamma(h)h^{2k}.

Notice that
h
is sufficiently small,
\gamma(h)\ll 1
, then

\varepsilon_{h,\mathbf{u}}\gtrsim h^{2k}.

It follows from Theorem
12
that

\lambda_{h}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|^{2}\lesssim\gamma(h)^{-2}h^{2(k+1)}.

Therefore, for sufficiently small
h
, the following inequality holds

\varepsilon_{h,\mathbf{u}}\geq\lambda_{h}\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\|^{2}.

Then we complete the proof.
∎

From
[
27
, Theorem 2.4]

Theorem 17

Let
(\lambda,\mathbf{u})
be an eigenpair of
(\ref{weak form})
,
(\lambda_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})
be an eigenpair of
(\ref{WG-scheme})

\lambda\geq\lambda_{h}.

Combining Theorems

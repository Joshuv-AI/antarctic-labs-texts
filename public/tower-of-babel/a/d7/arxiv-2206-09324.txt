Seung-Hyeok Kye

Abstract
. A linear map between matrix algebras corresponds to the Choi matrix in the tensor product of two matrix algebras, whose definition depends on the matrix units. Paulsen and Shultz [J. Math. Phys. 54 (2013), 072201] considered the question if one can replace matrix units by another basis of matrix algebras in the definition of Choi matrix to retain the correspondence between complete positivity of maps and positivity of Choi matrices, and gave a sufficient condition on basis under which this is true. In this note, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions, to see that the Paulsen–Shultz condition is also necessary.

15A30, 81P15, 46L05, 46L07

For a given linear map
\phi
from the algebra
M_{n}
of all
n\times n
complex matrices into
M_{m}
of
m\times m
matrices, the

Choi matrix

{\rm C}_{\phi}
is given by

(1)

{\rm C}_{\phi}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{ij}\otimes\phi(e_{ij})\in M_{n}\otimes M_{m},

with the standard matrix units
\{e_{ij}\}
of
M_{n}
.
This had been defined in 1967 by de Pillis
[
2
]
who had shown that
\phi
preserves Hermitianity
if and only if
{\rm C}_{\phi}
is Hermitian.
During the seventies, two results appeared which are now fundamental tools in current
quantum information theory; Jamiołkowski
[
4
]
gave a condition on
{\rm C}_{\phi}
under which
\phi
is a positive linear map,
which is now called
block positivity
; Choi
[
1
]
showed that
\phi
is completely positive if and only if
{\rm C}_{\phi}

is positive (semi-definite). The isomorphism
\phi\mapsto{\rm C}_{\phi}
is now called the Jamiołkowski–Choi isomorphism.

Paulsen and Shultz
[
6
]
asked what happens when we replace matrix units in (
1
)
by another basis
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_{ij}\}
of
M_{n}
.
More precisely, they considered

(2)

{\rm C}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}b_{ij}\otimes\phi(b_{ij})=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)\left(\textstyle\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\otimes b_{ij}\right)\in M_{n}\otimes M_{m}

and asked when the following statement

(3)

\phi:M_{n}\to M_{m}\ {\text{\rm is completely positive}}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ {\rm C}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi}\in M_{n}\otimes M_{m}\ {\text{\rm is positive}}

is still true. For this purpose, they considered the map
C_{\mathcal{B}}
which sends matrix units to
{\mathcal{B}}
;
the
n^{2}\times n^{2}
matrix
[C_{\mathcal{B}}]
which represents the map
C_{\mathcal{B}}
with respect to the matrix units with a fixed order;
the transpose
[C_{\mathcal{B}}]^{T}
of the matrix
[C_{\mathcal{B}}]
;
and finally the linear map
M_{\mathcal{B}}:M_{n}\to M_{n}
given by the
n^{2}\times n^{2}
matrix
[C_{\mathcal{B}}][C_{\mathcal{B}}]^{T}
.
For a given
s\in M_{n}
, we define the map

\operatorname{Ad}_{s}:M_{n}\to M_{n}
by

\operatorname{Ad}_{s}(x)=s^{*}xs,\qquad x\in M_{n}.

Paulsen and Shultz
[
6
]
showed that if
M_{\mathcal{B}}=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
for some
s\in M_{n}
then the statement (
3
) holds
for every
m=1,2,\dots
.
We show that the converse is also true.

We note that the Choi matrix may be written by

(4)

{\rm C}_{\phi}=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)\left(\textstyle\sum_{i,j}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}\right)=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)(|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|),

where
|\omega\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|i\rangle|i\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Very recently, the author
[
5
]

considered the question what happens when we replace
|\omega\rangle
in (
4
) by
another vector. He began with a nonsingular matrix
s=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}s_{ij}{|i\rangle\langle j|}\in M_{n}

to associate the vector
\langle\tilde{s}|=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}s_{ij}\langle i|\langle j|\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
and defined

(5)

{\rm C}^{s}_{\phi}=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)(|\tilde{s}\rangle\langle\tilde{s}|),

to show that
\phi
is completely positive if and only if
{\rm C}^{s}_{\phi}
is positive.
We show that non-singularity of
s
is necessary to retain the correspondence between completely positivity of
\phi

and positivity of
{\rm C}^{s}_{\phi}
.

More generally, we will consider the question what happens when we replace
the matrix
\sum_{i,j}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ij}
in (
4
) by
another matrix
\Sigma\in M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
, to define

(6)

{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)(\Sigma)\in M_{n}\otimes M_{n}.

We abuse notations in (
2
), (
5
) and (
6
).
For a given basis
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_{ij}\}
, we write
\Sigma_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}\otimes b_{ij}
then

{\rm C}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi}
in (
2
) is nothing but
{\rm C}^{\Sigma_{\mathcal{B}}}_{\phi}
defined in (
6
).
The similar comment works for (
5
) and (
6
).

If we take the linear map
\sigma:M_{n}\to M_{n}
with

\Sigma={\rm C}_{\sigma}
then we have the relation

(7)

{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}=({\text{\rm id}}_{n}\otimes\phi)({\rm C}_{\sigma})={\rm C}_{\phi\circ\sigma},

which plays an important role in quantum information theory. See
[
3
, Section 4]
.
Now, we will say that
\Sigma\in M_{n}\otimes M_{n}

satisfies the Choi correspondence
when
complete positivity of a map
\phi:M_{n}\to M_{m}
is equivalent to positivity of
{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}\in M_{n}\otimes M_{m}

for every
m=1,2,\dots.

We see that
\Sigma={\rm C}_{\sigma}
satisfies the Choi correspondence if and only if
the following

(8)

\phi\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{m}]\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \phi\circ\sigma\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{m}]

holds for every
m=1,2,\dots
by (
7
), where
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}={{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{m}]
denotes the convex cone of all completely positive maps
from
M_{n}
into
M_{m}
.

Following
[
3
]
, we use the bilinear pairing
\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(XY^{\text{\rm t}})
for matrices, and define
the bilinear pairing
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle
of two linear maps
\phi,\psi:M_{n}\to M_{m}
by

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\langle{\rm C}_{\phi},{\rm C}_{\psi}\rangle
. The map
\phi^{*}:M_{m}\to M_{n}
is
also defined by
\langle\phi^{*}(x),y\rangle=\langle x,\phi(y)\rangle
.
For a closed convex cone
K
of linear maps, we also define
K^{\circ}
as the convex cone consisting of all linear maps
\phi

satisfying
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle\geq 0
for every
\psi\in K
. It is well known that
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}^{\circ}={{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}
.
Then, we see that
\phi\circ\sigma\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}
holds in (
8
)
if and only if
\langle\phi\circ\sigma,\tau\rangle\geq 0
for every
\tau\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}

if and only if
\langle\phi,\tau\circ\sigma^{*}\rangle\geq 0
for every
\tau\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}
if and only if

\phi\in({{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}\circ\{\sigma^{*}\})^{\circ}
.
Since
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}^{\circ}={{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}
, we see that (
8
) holds if and only if

{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}={{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}\circ\{\sigma^{*}\}
if and only if the following

(9)

{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{m},M_{n}]=\{\sigma\}\circ{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{m},M_{n}]

holds for every
m=1,2,\dots
.
Recall that a linear map
\sigma:M_{n}\to M_{n}
is called a
complete order
isomorphism
if
\sigma
is a bijection and both
\sigma
and

\sigma^{-1}
are completely positive.
When
m=n
, the identity map belongs to
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{n}]
in the condition (
9
),
and so we conclude that
\sigma
is a complete order isomorphism.
Conversely, if
\sigma
is a complete order isomorphism then
(
9
) holds for every
m=1,2,\dots
.
A typical example of complete order isomorphism is the map

\operatorname{Ad}_{s}:M_{A}\to M_{A}
for a nonsingular
s\in M_{A}
, with the inverse map

\operatorname{Ad}_{s^{-1}}
.

Theorem 1.

Suppose that
\Sigma\in M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
with
\Sigma={\rm C}_{\sigma}
for a map
\sigma:M_{n}\to M_{n}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Sigma
satisfies the Choi correspondence,

(ii)

\phi\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{m}]
if and only if
\phi\circ\sigma\in{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{m}]
for every
m=1,2,\dots
,

(iii)

{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{m},M_{n}]=\{\sigma\}\circ{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{m},M_{n}]
for every
m=1,2,\dots
,

(iv)

\sigma
is a complete order isomorphism,

(v)

\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
for a nonsingular matrix
s\in M_{n}
,

(vi)

\Sigma
is a positive rank one matrix whose range vector has the full Schmidt rank,

(vii)

for any basis
\{|\xi_{i}\rangle\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there exists a basis
\{|\eta_{i}\rangle\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\Sigma=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}\rangle\langle\xi_{j}|\otimes|\eta_{i}\rangle\langle\eta_{j}|
,

(viii)

\Sigma=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}\rangle\langle\xi_{j}|\otimes|\eta_{i}\rangle\langle\eta_{j}|
for some bases
\{|\xi_{i}\rangle\}
and
\{|\eta_{i}\rangle\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

In
[
5
]
, we have considered the case when

\Sigma
is a positive rank one matrix with the range vector
|\tilde{s}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and showed that
if
s
is non-singular then
\Sigma
satisfies the Choi correspondence. The above theorem tells us that the converse also holds.

So far, we have seen that the statements (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) are equivalent. It was shown in

[
6
]
that (iv) implies (v). We show here in another way.
Suppose that

\sigma=\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{Ad}_{s_{i}}
is a complete order isomorphism with the
completely positive inverse
\sigma^{-1}=\sum_{j\in J}\operatorname{Ad}_{t_{j}}
. Then we
have

\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{Ad}_{t_{j}s_{i}}=\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{Ad}_{s_{i}}\circ\operatorname{Ad}_{t_{j}}={\text{\rm id}}
.
Because the identity map generates an extreme ray of the convex cone
{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}}[M_{n},M_{n}]
,
there exist positive numbers
\alpha_{(i,j)}
such that

\alpha_{(i,j)}\operatorname{Ad}_{t_{j}s_{i}}={\text{\rm id}}
for
(i,j)\in I\times J
,
and so, we have

\sqrt{\alpha_{(i,j)}}z_{(i,j)}{t_{j}s_{i}}=I
for every
(i,j)\in I\times J
, with complex numbers
z_{(i,j)}
of modulus one.
Therefore, we see that all
s_{i}
coincide up to scalar multiplications.
Furthermore, the invertibility of
\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
implies non-singularity of
s
. This shows that (iv) and (v) are equivalent.
By the relation
{\rm C}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{s}}=|\tilde{s}\rangle\langle\tilde{s}|
in
[
3
]
, we also have (v)
\Longleftrightarrow
(vi).
For a given basis
\{|\xi_{i}\rangle\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, every vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}

is of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}\rangle\otimes|\eta_{i}\rangle
for a family
\{|\eta_{i}\rangle\}
of vectors, which
has the full Schmidt rank if and only if
\{|\eta_{i}\rangle\}
is
a basis. This shows the direction (vi)
\Longrightarrow
(vii). The remaining implications (vii)
\Longrightarrow
(viii)
\Longrightarrow
(vi)
are clear.

We note that
\phi\mapsto{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}
is a bijection if and only if
\phi\mapsto\phi\circ\sigma

is a bijection between the linear mapping space
L(M_{n},M_{m})

if and only if the map
\sigma\in L(M_{n},M_{n})
is invertible. When
\sigma=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
, we also see that
\sigma
is invertible if and only if

s
is nonsingular. Therefore, we see that the Choi correspondence of
\Sigma
implies that

\phi\mapsto{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}
is a bijection.

Considering the condition in
[
6
]
,
we suppose that
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_{j}:j\in J\}
is an ordered family of
n\times n
matrices with the cardinality
n^{2}
, and
define the linear map
C_{\mathcal{B}}:M_{n}\to M_{n}
by

b_{j}=C_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{j})=\sum_{i\in J}x_{ij}e_{i}
for
j\in J
,
with an ordered matrix unit
\{e_{j}:j\in J\}
.
By the relation
C_{\mathcal{B}}^{T}(e_{j})=\sum_{i\in J}x_{ji}e_{i}
, we have

M_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{j})=C_{\mathcal{B}}C_{\mathcal{B}}^{T}(e_{j})=\sum_{i\in J}x_{ji}C_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{i})=\sum_{i\in J}x_{ji}\sum_{k\in J}x_{ki}e_{k}.

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}e_{j}\otimes M_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{k\in J}x_{ji}x_{ki}e_{j}\otimes e_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in J}\left(\sum_{j\in J}x_{ji}e_{j}\right)\otimes\left(\sum_{k\in J}x_{ki}e_{k}\right)=\sum_{i\in J}b_{i}\otimes b_{i}.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_{ij}:i,j=1,2,\dots,n\}
be a family of matrices in
M_{n}
. If we put

\Sigma=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}b_{ij}\otimes b_{ij}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{ij}\otimes M_{\mathcal{B}}(e_{ij})={\rm C}_{M_{\mathcal{B}}},

then
{\rm C}^{\Sigma}_{\phi}
in (
6
) is nothing but
{\rm C}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi}
defined in (
2
).
Therefore, the statement (
3
) holds for every
m=1,2,\dots
if and only if
M_{\mathcal{B}}=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
for a nonsingular
s\in M_{n}

if and only if
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}b_{ij}\otimes b_{ij}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}\rangle\langle\xi_{j}|\otimes|\eta_{i}\rangle\langle\eta_{j}|
for some bases

\{|\xi_{i}\rangle\}
and
\{|\eta_{i}\rangle\}
if and only if there exists a basis
\{|\zeta_{i}\rangle\}
such that
b_{ij}=|\zeta_{i}\rangle\langle\zeta_{j}|

for each
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
. In case when
\{b_{ij}\}
is a basis of
M_{n}
, the matrix
s
in this discussion
is automatically non-singular, by the invertibility of
M_{\mathcal{B}}
. We summarize as follows:

Theorem 2.

For a basis
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b_{ij}\}
of
M_{n}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\phi:M_{n}\to M_{m}
is completely positive if and only if
{\rm C}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi}\in M_{n}\otimes M_{m}
is positive,

(ii)

M_{\mathcal{B}}=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
for a nonsingular matrix
s\in M_{n}
,

(iii)

M_{\mathcal{B}}=\operatorname{Ad}_{s}
for a matrix
s\in M_{n}
,

(iv)

there exists a basis
\{|\zeta_{i}\rangle\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
b_{ij}=|\zeta_{i}\rangle\langle\zeta_{j}|
for
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
.

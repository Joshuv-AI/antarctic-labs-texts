Abstract
. We give a category-theoretic construction of simple and NSOP 1 -like independence relations in locally finitely presentable categories, and in the more general locally finitely multipresentable categories. We do so by identifying properties of a class of monomorphisms ℳ \mathcal{M} such that the pullback squares consisting of morphisms in ℳ \mathcal{M} form the desired independence relation. This generalizes the category-theoretic construction of stable independence relations using effective unions or cellular squares by M. Lieberman, S. Vasey and the second author to the unstable setting.



1.
Introduction

An important tool in the model-theoretic branch of stability theory is that of independence relations. Examples of independence relations are linear independence in vector spaces and algebraic independence in fields. Recent developments
[
11
,
12
,
6
,
7
]
allow for a category-theoretic treatment of these independence relations. More precisely, canonicity theorems have been established. They state that in a given category there can be at most one nice enough independence relation (see e.g. Theorem
2.7
). This then recovers part of the classical
stability hierarchy
, based on which kind of independence relation exists in a given category: in increasing generality we have stable, simple and NSOP
1
-like independence relations (corresponding to the similarly named classes of theories in classical model theory). The difference between stable and simple independence is that the uniqueness property is replaced by
3
-amalgamation. The difference between simple and NSOP
1
-like independence is that the base-monotonicity property is dropped.

In
[
11
,
12
]
a stable independence relation is constructed category-theoretically in locally finitely presentable categories. In
[
11
]
this is done by considering effective unions. These are pullback squares of regular monomorphisms such that the induced morphism from the relevant pushout square is also a regular monomorphism. By replacing the class of regular monomorphisms with some class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms, we get the notion of cellular squares, and
[
12
]
generalizes the above construction of stable independence using cellular squares.

In this paper we construct NSOP
1
-like and simple independence relations category-theoretically, similar to how
[
11
,
12
]
constructs stable independence relations. We do this by essentially replacing the uniqueness property by
3
-amalgamation. Since the uniqueness property comes directly from a pushout being ‘hidden’ in the independent squares, we drop this requirement and work instead with a higher-dimensional requirement that we call
cubic
. This results in pullback squares forming an NSOP
1
-like independence relation. We also identify another propery that corresponds to base-monotonicity, so that property holds exactly when this NSOP
1
-like independence relation is simple.

Main results.

We establish conditions on a class of monomorphisms
\mathcal{M}
in a category
\mathcal{K}
such that the category
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
admits an NSOP
1
-like or simple independence relation. Here
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the category whose objects are those of
\mathcal{K}
and whose morphisms are those from
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is a locally finitely presentable category equipped with a cubic, continuous and accessible class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
has an
\operatorname{NSOP}_{1}
-like independence relation given by pullback squares.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is a locally finitely presentable category equipped with a strongly cubic and continuous class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
has a simple independence relation given by pullback squares.

We then generalize the above theorems to locally multipresentable categories, which are accessible categories that have all connected limits.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is a locally finitely multipresentable category equipped with a multicubic, continuous and accessible class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
has an
\operatorname{NSOP}_{1}
-like independence relation given by pullback squares.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is a locally finitely multipresentable category equipped with a strongly multicubic, continuous and accessible class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
has an simple independence relation given by pullback squares.

The canonicity theorems (Theorem
2.7
) for categorical independence relations immediately apply to simple independence, but for NSOP
1
-like independence we need a technical condition called
the existence axiom for isi-forking
. We prove exactly that in Corollary
6.3
. In fact, we prove more generally that the existence of a weakly stable independence relation is enough (Theorem
6.1
). We can thus summarize the canonicity of the above independence relations as follows.

Corollary 1.5
.

The independence relations in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
are canonical in the following sense. Given a category
\mathcal{K}
and class of monomorphisms
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the conditions in one of these theorems, then any NSOP
1
-like independence relation on
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
is given by pullback squares.

We recall a simplified version of the framework of

Definition 2.1
.

An

is an accessible category with directed colimits and where all morphisms are monomorphisms.

Throughout we will only be interested in AECats with the amalgamation property.

Definition 2.2

We say that a category has the
amalgamation property
or
AP
if any span of morphisms can be completed to a commuting square.

Both
[
6

[
7

deal with independence relations in AECats, where the former is only about simple independence and the latter introduces NSOP
1
-like independence and deals with both. We will base ourselves on the terminology in
[

[
11
,
12
]
where an independence relation is seen as a class of commuting squares. The two approaches can be translated into one another. The former has the advantage of an easier to use independence calculus, whereas the latter has the advantage of admitting a more categorical treatment. We will therefore take the commuting squares perspective, and so we translate the relevant definitions into that perspective. We first define and discuss the various properties for an independence relation that is based on commuting squares, and then we briefly discuss how to compare this to the subobjects version (Remark
2.10
).

4.
Simple independence

Weakly stable independence is base-monotonic because base-monotonicity follows from existence and uniqueness. By essentially replacing the uniqueness property by 3-amalgamation we thus also lose the base-monotonicity property. In this section we give a characterization in terms of factorization systems of when base-monotonicity can be recovered.

Definition 4.1
.

A coherently amalgamable class
\mathcal{M}
of monomorphisms in a locally presentable category
\mathcal{K}
will be called
strongly coherently amalgamable
if there is a class
\mathcal{E}
of epimorphisms such that
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
is a factorization system.

Fact 4.2

(
[

]
)

In a category with cokernel pairs and equalizers of cokernel pairs (e.g. any locally presentable category), the following are equivalent:

(1)

(2)

regular monomorphisms coincide with strong monomorphisms,

(3)

(4)

taking
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{M}
to be the classes of epimorphisms and regular monomorphisms respectively, we have that
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
is a factorization system.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1), (2) and (3) is Propositions 2.7 and 3.8 in
[
9
]
. Then clearly (4) implies (2), while (1) and (3) together imply (4).
∎

Fact 4.3

(
[

If

is a factorization system in any category where
\mathcal{E}
is a class of epimorphisms then
\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}

The following shows that

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a locally finitely presentable coregular category. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{K}
has effective unions (i.e. pullback squares are effective),

(2)

regular monomorphisms are strongly cubic and cofibrantly generated,

(3)

regular monomorphisms are cubic and cofibrantly generated.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2). Let
\mathcal{K}
be a locally finitely presentable coregular category with effective unions. Following
[
3

, regular monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated. Since pullback squares are effective, they form a weakly stable independence which satisfies

is left-cancellable and by general properties of pullbacks, the colimitting cocone satisfies all required properties. Since weakly stable independence is base-monotonic, regular monomorphisms are strongly cubic by Proposition
4.8
.

(2)
\implies
(3). Trivial.

(3)
\implies
(1). Assume that regular monomorphisms are cubic and cofibrantly generated. Following Remark
3.16
we can apply Theorem
1.1
to see that pullback squares form an NSOP

-like independence relation. Following
[

5.
Independence in locally multipresentable categories

We recall the definition and equivalent characterisations of locally multipresentable categories (see e.g.
[
2
, Definition 4.28 and Theorem 4.30]
).

Definition 5.1
.

A
multicolimit
of a diagram
D
is a (possibly empty) set of cocones, such that every cocone factors uniquely through a unique cocone in that set. We call a
\lambda
-accessible category
\mathcal{K}

locally
\lambda
-multipresentable
if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

\mathcal{K}
has all small multicolimits,

(2)

\mathcal{K}
has all small connected limits.

We call
\mathcal{K}

locally multipresentable
if it is locally
\lambda
-multipresentable for some
\lambda
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a strongly coherently amalgamable continuous class of morphisms in a locally
\lambda
-presentable category
\mathcal{K}
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a locally
\lambda
-multipresentable category with AP.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
we have that
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
is
\lambda
-accessible. To show that
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{M}}
has connected limits it suffices to show it has equalizers and wide pullbacks, which easily follows from
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M})
being a proper factorization system (meaning that
\mathcal{E}
consists of epimorphisms).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In the context Lemma
5.2
we have that the multipushout of a span
B\leftarrow A\to C
consists of all its
\mathcal{E}
-pushouts.

We adjust the definitions of coherently amalgamable, squared and cubic to work with multicolimits.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a class of monomorphisms in a locally multipresentable category
\mathcal{K}
that is closed under composition and contains all isomorphisms. We call
coherently multiamalgamable
if it satisfies the following axioms:

(A1)

if
fg\in\mathcal{M}
and
f\in\mathcal{M}
then
g\in\mathcal{M}
(this is sometimes called
coherence
),

(A2’)

the multipushout of two morphisms in
\mathcal{M}
contains at least one square that is entirely in
\mathcal{M}
.

We call
\mathcal{M}

multisquared
if it is coherently multiamalgamable and also satisfies:

(A3)

the pullback of two morphisms in
\mathcal{M}
is in
\mathcal{M}
,

(A4’)

the multipushout of two morphisms in
\mathcal{M}
contains at least one square that is entirely in
\mathcal{M}
and which is also a pullback.

Finally, we call
\mathcal{M}

multicubic
if it is multisquared and the multicolimit of the horn in Definition
3.8
contains a cocone that is entirely in
\mathcal{M}
and where the diagonal square is a pullback.

We can generalize Remark

Example 6.4
.

We give an example of a locally finitely multipresentable category that cannot have a weakly stable independence relation and whose morphisms are all regular monomorphisms, which in turn form a strongly multicubic, continuous and accessible class of monomorphisms (so in particular: coherently multiamalgamable). We note that this also means that Theorem

applies, so we have a simple independence relation and hence the existence axiom for isi-forking, and so this is also an example of the independence between these properties and having a weakly stable independence relation.

Let
\operatorname{\bf TwoGraph}
be the category of two-graphs with graph embeddings. We recall that a

together with a 3-ary edge relation

(so edges are undirected and do not contain duplicate vertices), such that between any four vertices there is an even number of edges. Then

[

(add a relation symbol for the negation of the edge relation and enumerate all two-graphs on four elements). Furthermore, it has AP by
[
15
, Propositions 3.2 and 3.3]

We claim that
\operatorname{\bf TwoGraph}

\mathop{\hbox{\lower 2.0pt\hbox to12.77773pt{\hfil\vrule width=0.3pt,depth=-2.0pt,height=10.0pt\hfil}\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\smile$}}}\limits

[

, Fact 2.8 and Propositions 3.2 and 3.3]
there is, up to isomorphism, a unique countable two-graph

. We apply existence for
\mathop{\hbox{\lower 2.0pt\hbox to12.77773pt{\hfil\vrule width=0.3pt,depth=-2.0pt,height=10.0pt\hfil}\lower 2.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\smile$}}}\limits
to the span

, where

is two-graph with one vertex, to find a two-graph

containing

and

and such that

. Pick some distinct

and

in

. We consider two cases. In the first case we assume that there is no edge between

. By uniqueness of

and

\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{H^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\{d\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

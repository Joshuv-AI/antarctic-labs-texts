Fixed point results for set-contractions on dislocated metric space with a directed graph
Talat Nazir, Zakaria Ali, Shahin Nosrat Jogan, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
Generalized (rational) graph contractions in the framework of a dislocated metric space endowed with a directed graph are investigated. Fixed point results for set-contractions are obtained. We also provide some examples to illustrate our main results. Moreover, the well-posedness of obtained fixed point results are also shown. Our obtained results extend many results in the existing literature. MSC 2020 Classification: 47H09, 47H10, 54C60, 54H25

Introduction to Dislocated Metric Space

Hitzler and Seda
hitzler2000dislocated
introduced the notion of dislocated
metric space and proved a fixed point result in the given space. After this, Rasham et al.
rasham2018multivalued
obtained the multivalued fixed point results for new generalized-dominated contractive mappings on dislocated metric space with application. Wadkare et al.
wadkar2017some
proved some fixed point theorems in dislocated metric space. After which several useful
results were established,
abbas2019nadler
;
aydi2018nadler
;
karapinar2013dislocated
;
kumari2015completion
;
pasicki2015dislocated
introducing the notion of contractions and related fixed point results in dislocated metric spaces.

Definition 1
(Dislocated metric space,
hitzler2000dislocated
)

Let
Y
be a non-empty set and let
\xi:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
be a real-valued non-negative function, satisfying for all
r,s,t\in Y
,

(i)

\xi(r,s)=0\Longrightarrow r=s
;

(ii)

\xi(r,s)=\xi(s,r)
;

(iii)

\xi(r,t)\leq\xi(r,s)+\xi(s,t)
.

Then the pair
(Y,\xi)
is called a dislocated metric space.

Remark 1

Note that the converse of
(i)
is only true in the usual metric space. Furthermore, every (usual) metric space is a dislocated metric space.

One of the simplest examples of a dislocated metric space is
\xi(r,s)=\max\{r,s\}
for all
r,s\in Y
. We build another dislocated metric using the previous example with the discrete metric. As follows:

Example 1

Let
Y=\mathbb{R_{+}}
, we define
\xi(r,s)=\max\{r,s\}+\sigma(r,s)
, where

\displaystyle\sigma(r,s)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }r\neq s,\\
0\text{ if }r=s.\end{cases}

Note that
\xi(0,0)=0
, and
r=s\neq 0\Longrightarrow\xi(r,s)>0
. Proving the other two axioms is routine.

Definition 2

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R^{+}}
. We define the open ball as

\displaystyle B_{\epsilon}(s)=\{t\in X:|\rho(s,t)-\rho(s,s)|<\epsilon\}.

The topology
\tau_{\xi}
on
(Y,\xi)
is defined then by open sets as follows:

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}=\{U\subseteq Y:\forall u\in U\exists\epsilon\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\text{ such that }B_{\epsilon}(u)\subseteq U\}.

A sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subseteq(Y,\xi)
is said to
converge
to
y\in Y\iff\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(y_{n},y)=\xi(y,y)
.

Definition 3

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space. Then a sequence
(y_{n})\subseteq Y
is said to be a Cauchy sequence, if
\lim\limits_{m,n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(y_{n},y_{m})
exists and is finite. Furthermore,
(Y,\xi)
is said to be complete if and only if every Cauchy sequence
(y_{n})
in
Y
converges to a point
y\in Y
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(y_{n},y)=\lim\limits_{m,n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(y_{n},y_{m})=\xi(y,y)
.

Definition 4

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space. A subset
U\subseteq Y
is said to be bounded if there exists a real number
\mathfrak{L}\geq 0
and
u_{0}\in Y
such that
U\subseteq B_{\mathfrak{L}}(u_{0})
, that is,
|\xi(u_{0},u)-\rho(u_{0},u_{0})|<\mathfrak{L}
for all
u\in U
.

Definition 5

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space. A subset
U
of
Y
is said to be closed in
Y
, if
\overline{U}=U
. That is
u\in\overline{U}
if and only if there exists a sequence
(u_{n})
in
U
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(u_{n},u)=\xi(u,u)
.

Following the above definitions, for a dislocated metric space
(Y,\xi)
, let
CB^{\xi}(Y)
be the set of all closed and bounded subsets of the set
Y
. We again provide the definition for the Pompeiu-Hausdorff induced by the dislocated metric
\xi
.

Definition 6

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space and
CB^{\xi}(Y)\neq\emptyset
. For any
U,V\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
, the Pompeiu-Hausdorff
PH
-metric
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}:CB^{\xi}(Y)\times CB^{\xi}(Y)\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
induced by
\xi
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)=\max\biggl\{\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,V),\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(V,U)\biggr\},

where
\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(A,B)=\sup\{\xi(a,B):a\in A\}
and
\xi(a,B)=\inf\{\xi(a,b):b\in B\}
.

We will be using the following results which also state the properties of the
PH
-metric, as proven by Aydi et al. in
aydi2018nadler
, in the proof of our main result.

Lemma 1
(
aydi2018nadler
)

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space,
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq Y
, and
(y_{n})
be a sequence in
Y
and
y\in Y
. Then,

1)

\xi(u,U)=0\Longrightarrow u\in\overline{U}
;

2)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\xi(y_{n},y)=\rho(y,y)\Longrightarrow\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\xi(y_{n},U)-\xi(y,U)|=\rho(y,y)
.

Theorem 1.1
(
aydi2018nadler
)

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space. For
U,V,W\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
,

1)

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,U)=\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,U)=\sup\{\xi(u,U):u\in U\}
;

2)

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,U)
;

3)

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)=0\Longrightarrow U=V
;

4)

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,W)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,W)
.

We prove the following result.

Lemma 2

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space. Then,
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,U)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)
for all
U,V\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
.

Proof

Since, for all
u\in U
, it follows that

\xi(u,U)\leq\xi(u,V)
, and hence

\sup\{\xi(u,U):u\in U\}\leq\sup\{\xi(u,V):u\in U\},

that is,

\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,U)\leq\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,V),

and so

\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,U)\leq\max\{\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(U,V),\mathcal{D}_{\xi}(V,U)\}.

Hence,
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,U)\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)
.

2
Dislocated Metric Space Endowed with Directed Graph

Existence of fixed points in ordered metric spaces has been studied by Ran and Reurings
ran2004fixed
. Recently, many researchers have obtained fixed point results for single- and setvalued mappings defined on partially ordered metrics spaces (see, for example,
beg2013fixed
;
harjani2009fixed
;
nicolae2011fixed
;
nieto2005contractive
;
nieto2007fixed
;
nieto2007existence
;
radenovic2010generalized
). Jachymski and Jozwik
jachymski2007nonlinear
introduced a new approach in metric fixed point theory by replacing the order structure with a graph structure on a metric space.
Abbas et al.
abbas2015fixed
;
abbas2016common
;
abbas2014fixed
obtained the fixed point of multivalued contaraction mappings on metric spaces with a directed graph. Then several useful fixed point results of single and multivalued mappings are appeared in
alfuraidan2014remarks
;
acar2016multivalued
;
aleomraninejad2012some
;
bojor2010fixed
;
bojor2013jachymski
;
bojor2012fixed
;
chifu2012generalized
;
gwozdz2009ifs
;
jachymski2008contraction
. Recently, Latif et al.
latif2019common
established some fixed point results for class of set-contraction mappings endowed with a directed graph.

In this work, we prove fixed
point results for set-valued maps, defined on the family of closed and bounded subsets of a
dislocated metric space endowed with a graph and satisfying generalzied graph
\phi
-contractive conditions. These
results extend and strengthen various known results in
alber1997principle
;
assad1972fixed
;
banach1922operations
;
marin2011functions
;
nadler1969multi
.

Here, the dislocated metric space
(Y,\xi)
is identified with a directed graph
G
such that
G
is weighted, that is, for each
(u,v)\in E(G)
we assign the weight
\xi(u,v)
, where loops have weight
\xi(u,u)\geq 0
for all
u\in V(G)
. Moreover, the
PH
-metric induced by the dislocated metric
\xi
assigns a non-zero weight to every
V,W\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
, that is,
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,W)\geq 0
, and also
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(W,V)=0
implies
W=V
.

Definition 7

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space and
T:CB^{\xi}(Y)\rightarrow CB^{\xi}(Y)
be a set-valued mapping with set-valued domain endowed with a graph
G
. Then
T
is said to be a generalized graph
\phi-
contraction, if the following conditions are satisfied:

1)

An edge (path) between
U
and
V
implies and edge (path) between
T(U)
and
T(V)
, for all
U,V\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
.

2)

There exists an upper semi-continuous and non-decreasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R_{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
with
\phi(t)<t
for each
t>0
such that for all
U,V\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
with
(U,V)\subseteq E(G)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\Big(T(U),T(V)\Big)\leq\phi\Big(M_{T}(U,V)\Big),

where

M_{T}(U,V)=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,T(U)),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,T(V)),\\
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U),T(V)),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U),V),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U),T(V)),\\
\frac{[\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,T(U))+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,T(V))]}{3}\Big\}.

Definition 8

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a dislocated metric space and
S:(Y,\xi)\rightarrow(Y,\xi)
be a set-valued mapping with set-valued domain endowed with a graph
G
. Then
S
is said to be a generalized rational graph
\phi-
contraction, if the following conditions are satisfied:

1)

An edge (path) between
U
and
V
implies an edge (path) between
S(U)
and
S(V)
, for all
U,V\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
.

2)

There exists an upper semi-continuous and non-decreasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R_{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R_{+}}
with
\phi(t)<t
for each
t>0
such that for all
U,V\in(Y,\xi)
with
(U,V)\subseteq E(G)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\Big(S(U),S(V)\Big)\leq\phi\Big(N_{S}(U,V)\Big)

is satisfied, where

\displaystyle N_{S}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{2[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)]},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,S(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,S(U))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}\Big\}.

3
Fixed Point of Graphic Contractions in Dislocated Metric Space

We present our main result in this section.

Theorem 3.1

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a complete dislocated metric space equipped with a directed graph
G
, where
V=Y
and
\Delta\subseteq CB^{\xi}(Y)
. If
T:CB^{\xi}(Y)\rightarrow CB^{\xi}(Y)
is a generalized graphic
\phi-
contraction mapping, then the following statements hold:

1)

If
F(T)=\{U\in CB^{\xi}(Y)\mid T(U)=U\}
is complete, then the weight assigned to
U,V\in F(T)
is zero.

2)

Y_{T}\neq\emptyset
given that
F(T)\neq\emptyset
.

3)

If
Y_{T}\neq\emptyset
and
\widetilde{G}
satisfies property
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, then
T
has a fixed point.

4)

F(T)
is complete if and only if
F(T)
is singleton.

Proof

1
) ∎Let
U,V\in F(T)
, that is,
T(U)=U
and
T(V)=V
and suppose by way of contradiction that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)\neq 0
. Now, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U),T(V))

\displaystyle\leq\phi\big(M_{T}(U,V)\big),

(1)

where

\displaystyle M_{T}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,T(U)),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,T(V)),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U),T(V)),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U),V),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U),T(V))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{[\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,T(U))+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,T(V))]}{3}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V).

Then by (
1
), we have

\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)\leq\phi\big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)\big)<\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V),

which is a contradiction. Hence our result follows, that is,
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)=0
.

2
) Suppose that
U\in F(T)
such that
T(U)=U
. Then we have
(U,T(U))=(U,U)\in\Delta
, hence
U\in Y_{T}
.

3
) Let
U_{0}\in Y_{T}
. It must be
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},T(U_{0}))>0
, since if it was otherwise, then we would have
U_{0}
a fixed point of
T
. Now, since
Y=V(\tilde{G})
there exists
U_{2}\in Y
such that there is an edge between
T(U_{0})=U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{1},T(U_{1}))>0
, that is,
(U_{1},U_{2})\in E(\tilde{G})
, where
T(U_{1})=U_{2}
.
Following, this argument we build a path
P:U_{0}-U_{1}-\cdots-U_{n}
where
\{U_{n}\}\subseteq CB^{\xi}(Y)
is a sequence. The previous argument also builds an iterative sequence by letting
U=U_{0}
and
T(U_{i})=U_{i+1}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. By definition of
T
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U),T^{n+1}(U))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n}))\leq\phi\Big(M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})\Big),

where

\displaystyle M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},T(U_{n-1})),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},T(U_{n})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n})),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U_{n-1}),U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{[\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},T(U_{n-1}))+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},T(U_{n}))]}{3}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1}),

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n})+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n+1})}{3},\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n+1},U_{n+1})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1}),

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n})+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})}{3}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max\Bigl\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Bigr\}\leq M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n}).

That is,
M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})=\max\Bigl\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Bigr\}
.
If
M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})
, then we have a contradiction. Thus, we must have
M_{T}(U_{n-1},U_{n})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})
. It follows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))&=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\\
&=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U_{n-1}),T(U_{n}))\\
&\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})\Big)\\
&=\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U_{n-2}),T(U_{n-1}))\Big)\\
&\leq\phi^{2}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-2},U_{n-1})\Big)\\
&\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots\\
&\leq\phi^{n}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},T(U_{0}))\Big).\end{array}

(2)

Now for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{m}(U))

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))+\dots+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n+k}(U),T^{n+k+1}(U))

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\dots+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{m-1}(U),T^{m}(U))

\displaystyle\leq\phi^{n}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},T(U_{0}))\Big)+\dots+\phi^{n+k}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},T(U_{0}))\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\cdots+\phi^{m-1}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},T(U_{0}))\Big).

It then follows that by taking the upper limits as
n\rightarrow\infty
we have that the sequence
\{T^{n}(U)\}
is a Cauchy. Since
(CB^{\xi}(Y),\mathcal{H}_{\xi})
is a complete dislocated metric space, there exists
U^{*}\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
such that
T^{n}(U)\rightarrow U^{*}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, that is,
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),U^{*})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},U^{*})
.
It follows from inequality (
2
), that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))\leq\phi^{n}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})\big)
, by taking the upper limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
implies that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n}(U),T^{n+1}(U))=0
and due to the uniqueness of limits we obtain
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},U^{*})=0
.
Then by property
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, there exists a subsequence
\{T^{n_{k}}(U)\}
such that there’s an edge between
T^{n_{k}-1}(U)
and
U^{*}
. Now by the triangle inequality
(iii)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(M_{T}(U^{*},T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))\Big)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*}),

(3)

where

\displaystyle M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}))),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(U^{*})),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})),T(U^{*})),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})),U^{*}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{2}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})),T(U^{*}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*})))+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T(U^{*})))}{3}\Big\}.

Case

1
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})
, then by inequality (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(H_{\rho}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})\Big)+H_{\rho}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

and taking the upper limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi(0)+0

and hence
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case

2
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))
, then by inequality (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}))\Big)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

and by taking the upper limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi(0)+0

and hence
T(U^{*})=U^{*}
.

Case

3
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))
, then by inequality (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))\Big)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

and on taking the upper limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))\Big)+0<\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},T(U^{*}))

and hence we must have:
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})=0
.

Case

4
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}+1}(U^{*}),U^{*})
, then by inequality (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}+1}(U^{*}),U^{*})\Big)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

and on taking the upper limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi(0)+0,

and hence we must have
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})=0
.

Case

5
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}+1}(U^{*}),U^{*})
, then by inequality (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}+1}(U^{*}),U^{*})\Big)+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

and on taking the upper limit
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi(0)+0

and hence we must have
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T(U^{*}),U^{*})=0
.

Case

6
: If
M_{T}(T^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(T^{n_{k}+1}(U^{*}),T(U^{*}))
, then by inequality (
3

4
): Suppose by way of contradiction that

Theorem 3.2

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a complete dislocated metric space equipped with a directed graph
G
, where
V=Y
and
\Delta\subseteq CB^{\xi}(Y)
. If
S:CB^{\xi}(Y)\rightarrow CB^{\xi}(Y)
is a generalized rational graphic
\phi-
contraction mapping. Then the following statements hold:

1)

If
F(S)
is complete, then the weight assigned to
U,V\in F(S)
is zero.

2)

Y_{S}\neq\emptyset
given that
F(S)\neq\emptyset
.

3)

If
Y_{S}\neq\emptyset
and
\tilde{G}
satisfies property
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, then
S
has a fixed point.

4)

F(S)
is complete if and only if
F(S)
is singleton.

Proof

∎
1
) Suppose otherwise that
U,V\in F(S)
and that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)>0
. Then since
S(U)=U
and
S(V)=V
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U),S(V))

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(N_{S}(U,V)\Big),

where

\displaystyle N_{S}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{2[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)]},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,S(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,S(U))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}\Big\},

and

\displaystyle N_{S}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}{2[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)]},\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,U)}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,U)}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}.

Then we have
0<\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U),S(V))=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)\leq\phi\Big(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,U)}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)}\Big)
.
Therefore,

(1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V))\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)<\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V),

which is a contradiction, and our result follows.

2
) Let
U\in F(S)
, then since
S(U)=U
, it follows that
(U,S(U))\subseteq E(G)
, and hence
U\in Y_{S}
.

3
) Let
U_{0}\in Y_{S}
. It must be
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},S(U_{0}))>0
, since if it was otherwise, then we would have
U_{0}
a fixed point for
S
. Now, by definition of
S
there exists
U_{2}\in CB^{\xi}(Y)
such that there is an edge between
S(U_{0})=U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{1},S(U_{1}))>0
, that is,
(U_{1},U_{2})\subseteq E(\tilde{G})
, where
S(U_{1})=U_{2}
. Following, this argument we build a path
P:U_{0}-U_{1}-\cdots-U_{n}
, where
\{U_{n}\}\subseteq CB^{\xi}(Y)
is a sequence. The previous argument also builds an iterative sequence by letting
U=U_{0}
and
S(U_{i})=U_{i+1}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. By definition of
S
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),S^{n+1}(U))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U_{n-1}),S(U_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(N_{S}(U_{n-1},U_{n})\Big),

where

\displaystyle N_{S}(U_{n-1},U_{n})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},S(U_{n}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},S(U_{n-1}))]}{2[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})]},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},S(U_{n}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},S(U_{n-1}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},S(U_{n-1}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},S(U_{n-1}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})}\Big\}.

Then

\displaystyle N_{S}(U_{n-1},U_{n})

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n+1})}{2},\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})}{2},\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Big\}.

Now, it follows that if
\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Big\}=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})
, then we have a contradiction. Hence, we must have
\Big\{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n}),\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})\Big\}=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),S^{n+1}(U))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n},U_{n+1})=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U_{n-1}),S(U_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-1},U_{n})\Big)

\displaystyle=\phi\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U_{n-2}),S(U_{n-1}))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\phi^{2}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{n-2},U_{n-1})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle\leq\phi^{n}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},S(U_{0}))\Big).

(4)

Now for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),S^{m}(U))\leq\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),S^{n+1}(U))

\displaystyle+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n+1}(U),S^{n+2}(U))+\cdots+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{m-1}(U),S^{m}(U))

\displaystyle\leq\phi^{n}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},S(U_{0}))\Big)+\phi^{n+1}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},S(U_{0}))\Big)+\cdots+\phi^{m-1}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U_{0},S(U_{0}))\Big).

It then follows that by taking the upper limits as

we have that the sequence
\{S^{n}(U)\}
is a Cauchy. Since
(CB^{\xi}(Y),\mathcal{H}_{\xi})
is a complete dislocated metric space, there exists

as

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),U^{*})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n}(U),S^{n+1}(U))=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},U^{*})
.
It follows from inequality (
4

, by taking the upper limit as

and due to the uniqueness of limits we obtain:
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{*},U^{*})=0
.
Now we are to show that
U^{*}=S(U^{*})
. Assume otherwise that
\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U^{*}),U^{*})>0
. Then by property
\mathcal{\overset{*}{P}}
, there exists a subsequence

such that there’s an edge between
S^{n_{k}-1}(U)
and
U^{*}
. Now by the triangle inequality
(iii)
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\rho}(S(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq H_{\rho}(S^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),S(U^{*}))+H_{\rho}(S^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\phi\Big(N_{S}(S^{n_{k}-1}(U^{*}),U^{*})\Big)+H_{\rho}(S^{n_{k}}(U^{*}),U^{*}),

where

\displaystyle N_{S}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},U^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},S(U^{\ast}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},S(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast}))]}{2[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},U^{\ast})]},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{\ast},S(U^{\ast}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},S(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},U^{\ast})},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U^{\ast},S(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast}))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},S(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast}))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S^{n_{k}-1}U^{\ast},U^{\ast})}\}

.

\displaystyle\leq\phi(0)+0,

a contradiction and hence

.

.

4

|F(S)|>1

such that

\displaystyle 0<\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(S(U),S(V))\leq\phi\Big(N_{S}(U,V)\Big),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(V,S(V))[1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,S(U))]}{1+\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\xi}(U,V),

∎

Poisson equation on Wasserstein space and diffusion approximations for McKean-Vlasov equation Thanks: This work is supported by NNSF of China (No. 12090011, 12071186, 11931004, 61873320).
Yun Li, Fuke Wu and Longjie Xie

Abstract
. We consider the fully-coupled McKean-Vlasov equation with multi-time-scale potentials, and all the coefficients depend on the distributions of both the slow component and the fast motion. By studying the smoothness of the solution of the non-linear Poisson equation on Wasserstein space, we derive the asymptotic limit as well as the quantitative error estimate of the convergence for the slow process. Extra homogenized drift term containing derivative in the measure argument of the solution of the Poisson equation appears in the limit, which seems to be new and is unique for systems involving the fast distribution. AMS 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60J60, 60F05, 35J60, 70K70. Keywords: Poisson equation; diffusion approximation; McKean-Vlasov equation; multi-scale processes.

Our aim in this paper is to derive rigorously the homogenized limit of the non-linear system (
1.1
) as

It turns out that the asymptotic limit for the system (
1.1

in terms of the solution of an non-linear Poisson equation. Namely, we need to consider the following Poisson equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}\Phi(x,\mu,y,\nu)=-H(x,\mu,y,\nu),

(1.2)

where
(x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
are regarded as parameters, and for a test function
\varphi

{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle:={\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}a(\mu,y,\nu)\partial^{2}_{y}\varphi(y,\nu)+b(\mu,y,\nu)\cdot\partial_{y}\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)\cdot\partial_{\nu}\varphi(y,\nu)(\tilde{y})+\frac{1}{2}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}\varphi(y,\nu)(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}),

(1.3)

with
a(\mu,y,\nu):=\sigma\sigma^{*}(\mu,y,\nu)

[
9

[
36
]
) of the test function with respect to the measure argument. Unlike previous works, equation (
1.2
) is totally non-linear. This is exactly due to the dependence on distribution of the fast motion in system (
1.1

{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
can be viewed as the infinitesimal generator of the frozen process
Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}=b(\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\mu,\eta}_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\mu,\eta}_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}^{2},\quad Y_{0}^{\mu,\eta}=\eta,

(1.4)

where

is regarded as a parameter. Furthermore, note that the Poisson equation (
1.2

1.2

H
satisfies the following centering condition (see (
2.12
) below):

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}H(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0,

(1.5)

where
\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)
is the unique invariant measure for the non-linear SDE (
1.4

\nu

\zeta^{\mu}

Under some mild assumptions, we prove that equation (
1.2

\Phi(x,\mu,y,\nu)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}H(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

where
Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu}
satisfies the following decoupled equation associated with the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
1.4

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu}=y+\int_{0}^{t}b\big(\mu,Y_{s}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\mu,\eta}}\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma\big(\mu,Y_{s}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\mu,\eta}}\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{2}_{s}

(1.6)

The smoothness of the solution
\Phi

1.2

Example 1.2

Consider the following McKean-Vlasov type stochastic Langevin equations with two-time-scales potentials in

if its canonical lift
{\mathbb{F}}
is
Fréchet differentiable at some point
X
with
{\mathcal{L}}_{X}=\mu
. By Riezs’ representation theorem, the Fréchet derivative
D{\mathbb{F}}(X)
, viewed as an element of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, can be given by a function
\partial_{\mu}f(\mu)(\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

D{\mathbb{F}}(X)=\partial_{\mu}f({\mathcal{L}}_{X})(X).

The function
\partial_{\mu}f(\mu)(x)
is then called the Lions derivative (
L
-derivative for short) of
f
at
\mu
. Similarly, we can define the higher order derivatives of
f
at
\mu
.

Let
d,d_{1},d_{2}\geqslant 1
,
T>0
and

. We introduce the following spaces of functions.

The space
C_{b}^{m,(k,\ell),2k,(k,k)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))

. Similarly, we define the space

2.
Poisson equations on Wasserstein space

2.1.
McKean-Vlasov equations and associated elliptic equations

Let us first consider the following McKean-Vlasov equation (
without parameters
) in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y^{\eta}_{t}=b(Y^{\eta}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(Y^{\eta}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\quad Y_{0}^{\eta}=\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(2.1)

Without the abuse of notations, we use the same symbol
b
and
\sigma
to denote the drift and diffusion coefficients as in the parameterized equation (
1.4
), and for every
\varphi\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
, we still use
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
to denote the operator

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle:={\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}a(y,\nu)\partial^{2}_{y}\varphi(y,\nu)+b(y,\nu)\cdot\partial_{y}\varphi(y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[b(\tilde{y},\nu)\cdot\partial_{\nu}\varphi(y,\nu)(\tilde{y})+\frac{1}{2}a(\tilde{y},\nu)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}\varphi(y,\nu)(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}),

(2.2)

where
a(y,\nu):=\sigma\sigma^{*}(y,\nu)
. In this situation, the assumption
(
H^{\sigma,b}
)
on the coefficients becomes

(
\hat{H}^{\sigma,b}
):
there exist constants
c_{2}>c_{1}\geqslant 0
such that for every
y_{1},y_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}
and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
,

\displaystyle\|\sigma(y_{1},\nu_{1})-\sigma(y_{2},\nu_{2})\|^{2}+2{\langle}b(y_{1},\nu_{1})-b(y_{2},\nu_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}{\rangle}\leqslant c_{1}{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})^{2}-c_{2}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}.

Let
Y_{t}^{\eta}
be the unique strong solution of the equation (
2.1
). It turns out that the law of
Y_{t}^{\eta}
only depends on
\eta
through its distribution
{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta}=\nu
. Thus, given a measure
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
, it makes sense to consider
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}
as a function of
\nu
without specifying the choice of the lifted random variable
\eta
, and we can define a (non-linear) semigroup
\{P_{t}^{*}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
on
{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
by letting

P_{t}^{*}\nu:={\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}\quad{\text{with}}\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta}=\nu.

We say that a probability measure
\zeta
is an invariant measure of the McKean-Vlasov equation (
2.1
) or the process
Y_{t}^{\eta}
if

P_{t}^{*}\zeta=\zeta,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

Under the assumption
(
\hat{H}^{\sigma,b}
)
, it is known that (see e.g.
[
47
, Theorem 3.1]
) there exists a unique invariant measure
\zeta
for the equation (
2.1
). Moreover, there exist constants
C_{0},\lambda_{0}>0
such that for every
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
,

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{*}\nu-\zeta\|_{TV}+{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(P_{t}^{*}\nu,\zeta)\leqslant C_{0}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t}\,{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\zeta).

(2.3)

We then introduce, for fixed
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}
, the following decoupled equation of the McKean-Vlasov equation (
2.1
):

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}^{y,\nu}=b(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t},\quad Y_{0}^{y,\nu}=y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}},

(2.4)

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta}=\nu
.
Given a function
f(y,\nu)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
, let us define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu):={\mathbb{E}}f(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}),\quad\forall t>0,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}},\nu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}).

(2.5)

We have the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\sigma,b,f\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Then for every
T>0
, the function
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu)
is the unique solution in
C_{b}^{1,2,(1,1)}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
of the non-linear PDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}U(t,y,\nu)-{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)U(t,y,\nu)=0,\quad\forall(t,y,\nu)\in(0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}),\\
&U(0,y,\nu)=f(y,\nu),\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)
is defined by (
2.1
).
If we further assume that
(
\hat{H}^{\sigma,b}
)
holds, then we have
that for every
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}
and
\nu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{2}({\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{y,\nu}},\zeta)\leqslant C_{0}(1+|y|+{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0})){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t},

(2.7)

where
C_{0},\lambda_{0}>0
are positive constants independent of
t
.

In particular,
\zeta
is also the unique invariant for the decoupled equation (
2.4
), and we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu)-{\langle}f(\cdot,\zeta),\zeta{\rangle}|\leqslant C_{0}(1+|y|+{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0})){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t}.

(2.8)

Proof.

The assertion that
{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu)
defined by (
2.5
) is in
C_{b}^{1,2,(1,1)}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and is the unique solution of the equation (
2.6
) follows by
[
8
, Theorem 7.2]
.
To prove (
2.7
), by Itô’s formula we have

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbb{E}}|Y_{t}^{\eta}-Y_{t}^{y,\nu}|^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\big[2{\langle}Y_{t}^{\eta}-Y_{t}^{y,\nu},b(Y_{t}^{\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})-b(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}}){\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\sigma(Y_{t}^{\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})-\sigma(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})\|^{2}\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-c_{2}{\mathbb{E}}|Y_{t}^{\eta}-Y_{t}^{y,\nu}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

By comparsion theorem we get

{\mathcal{W}}_{2}({\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{y,\nu}},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})\leqslant C_{0}(1+|y|+{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0})){\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t}.

This together with estimate (
2.3
) implies (
2.7
).
Moreover, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu)-{\langle}f(\cdot,\zeta),\zeta{\rangle}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}f(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})-{\langle}f(\cdot,\zeta),\zeta{\rangle}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}f(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})-{\mathbb{E}}f(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},\zeta)+{\mathbb{E}}f(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},\zeta)-{\langle}f(\cdot,\zeta),\zeta{\rangle}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For the first term, we have by (
2.3
) that

|I_{1}|\leqslant{\mathcal{W}}_{2}({\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}},\zeta)\leqslant C_{0}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t}{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\zeta).

For the second term, we have by (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leqslant C_{0}{\mathcal{W}}_{2}({\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{y,\nu}},\zeta)\leqslant C_{0}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda_{0}t}(1+|y|+{\mathcal{W}}_{2}(\nu,\delta_{0})).

The proof is finished.
∎

Fix
\lambda>0
below. Consider the following non-linear elliptic equation:

\displaystyle\lambda U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)-{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)=f(y,\nu),

(2.9)

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)
is defined by (
2.1
).
We have:

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
\sigma,b,f\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Then the function

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(y,\nu):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

(2.10)

is the unique solution in
C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
to the equation (
2.9
).

Proof.

The assertion that
U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)
defined by
(\ref{u1})
is in
C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and solves equation (
2.9
) follows by Theorem
2.1
and the integral by part formula. In fact, we have

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t={\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu)\Big|_{0}^{\infty}+\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}{\mathcal{T}}_{t}f(y,\nu){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle=-f(y,\nu)+\lambda U_{\lambda}(y,\nu),

which implies the desired result.
To prove the uniqueness, let
U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
be a solution to equation (
2.9
). Then by Itô’s formula (see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
) we have

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})=U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U_{\lambda}(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+M_{t},

where
M_{t}
is a martingale given by

M_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{y}U_{\lambda})(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}})\cdot\sigma(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}.

By the product formula, we further have

\displaystyle{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda t}U_{\lambda}(Y_{t}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})

\displaystyle=U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U_{\lambda}(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}M_{s}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}U_{\lambda}(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle=U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}f(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}M_{s},

where in the last equality we have used (
2.9
). Taking expectation from both sides and letting
t\to\infty
, we obtain

U_{\lambda}(y,\nu)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\lambda s}f(Y_{s}^{y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right),

which implies the desired result.
∎

Now, we consider the following non-linear Poisson equation in the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(y,\nu)U(y,\nu)=-f(y,\nu).

(2.11)

This equation can be viewed as the limit
\lambda\to 0
of equation (
2.9
). However, in general the integral in (
2.10
) is not well-defined for measurable function
f
if
\lambda=0
. It turns out that a necessary condition for equation (
2.11
) to be well-defined is to assume that
f
satisfies the following centering condition:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}f(y,\zeta)\zeta({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0,

(2.12)

where
\zeta
is the unique invariant measure of the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
2.1
).
In fact, if
U\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
satisfies the equation (
2.11
), then by Itô’s formula we can deduce that

\displaystyle U(Y_{t}^{\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}})=U(\eta,{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta})+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U(Y_{s}^{\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{y}U(Y_{s}^{\eta},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\eta}})\sigma(Y^{\eta}_{s},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{s}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s},

where
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
is defined by (
2.1
). Letting the initial distribution
{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta}
to be the invariant measure
\zeta
of the equation (
2.1
), then we have that for every
t>0
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\eta}}=\zeta
. Taking expectation from both sides of the above equality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}U(y,\zeta)\zeta({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}U(y,\zeta)\zeta({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U(y,\zeta)\zeta({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

2.2.
Poisson equations with parameters

Now, we consider the following parameterized Poisson equation on the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)=-f(x,\mu,y,\nu),

(2.15)

where
(x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
are parameters, and the operator
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
is defined by (
1
). Recall that
\zeta^{\mu}
is the unique invariant measure for the frozen McKean-Vlasov equation (
1.4
).
As in the previous subsection, we assume that
f
satisfies the following centering condition:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0,\quad\forall(x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}).

(2.16)

By regarding
(x,\mu)
as parameters and according to Theorem
2.3
, the unique solution
U
to equation (
2.15
) should satisfy the centering condition (
2.16
) and admit the probabilistic representation

\displaystyle U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}f\big(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}}\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

(2.17)

where
Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu}
and
Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}
satisfy equation (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) with
{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta}=\nu
, respectively.
The main problem addressed here is the smoothness of the solution
U
with respect to the parameters
(x,\mu)
.
We have the following result, which will play a central role in our analysis below.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(
H^{\sigma,b}
)
hold,
\ell,j,k,m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the function
f
satisfies the centering condition (
2.16
), and
U
is defined by (
2.17
).

(i) Assume that
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(n,k),2(m-n),(m-n,m-n)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{j,(n,k),2(m-n),(m-n,m-n)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
with
0\leqslant n\leqslant\ell<m
. Then we have

\displaystyle U\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{j,(\ell,k),2(m-\ell),(m-\ell,m-\ell)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})).

(2.18)

(ii) Assume further that
a,b\in C_{b}^{(m,k),2m,(m,m)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and
f\in C_{b}^{j,(m,k),2m,(m,m)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
, then we have

\displaystyle U\in C_{b}^{j,(m,k),2m,(m,m)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})).

(2.19)

Proof.

We devide the proof into three steps.

(i) The regularities of
U
with respect to the
(y,\nu)
-variables follow by Theorem
2.3
. Our task is to prove the regularities of
U
with respect to
x
and
\mu
.

The derivatives of
U
with respect to the
x
-variable are easy. Since
f
satisfies the centering condition (
2.16
), it is obvious that
\partial^{j}_{x}f
satisfies (
2.16
), too. Thus, we can take derivative directly from both sides of (
2.17
) to get that for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\partial^{j}_{x}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial^{j}_{x}f\big(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}}\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

(2.20)

which in turn implies the desired conclusions for
U
with respect to the
x
-variable.

(ii) The regularities of
U
with respect to the
\mu
-variable is more delicate, since
\mu
also appears in the process
Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu}
as well as the distribution
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}}
.
Taking derivative directly will involve complicated computations. We shall use the equation itself. Note that when
m=1
, the conclusion in (
2.18
) is obvious, we proceed to prove (
2.19
) with
m=1
.
Since
U
is a classical solution to

{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)U(x,\mu,y,\nu)=-f(x,\mu,y,\nu),

we have for every
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
,
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
and
\rho>0
that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\Big(\frac{U(x,\mu,y,\nu)-U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)}{\rho}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{f(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-f(x,\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big({\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\Big)U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)

\displaystyle=:h_{1}^{\phi}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\rho),

where

\displaystyle\Big({\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\Big)U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)

\displaystyle=\Big(b(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-b(\mu,y,\nu)\Big)\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Big(a(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-a(\mu,y,\nu)\Big)\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\big(b(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},\tilde{y},\nu)-b\big(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)\big)\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\big(a(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},\tilde{y},\nu)-a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)\big)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}).

This implies that for every
\rho>0
,
h_{1}^{\phi}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\rho)
satisfies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}h_{1}^{\phi}(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\rho)\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0,

and by Theorem
2.3
(i) we have the representation that

\displaystyle\frac{U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-U(x,\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{1}^{\phi}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\rho){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right).

(2.21)

Note that by the definition of
L
-derivative,

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{f(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-f(x,\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x}),

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{b(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-b(\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x}),

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{a(\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-a(\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x}).

By the continuity of
\partial_{y}^{2}U
and
\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}U
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}h_{1}^{\phi}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\rho)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\Big]\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=:\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}h_{1}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x}).

This further implies that
h_{1}
satisfies the centering condition (
2.16
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}h_{1}(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0.

(2.22)

Thus, taking the limit
\rho\to 0
in (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\rho\to 0}\frac{U(x,\mu\circ(I+\rho\phi)^{-1},y,\nu)-U(x,\mu,y,\nu)}{\rho}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}}h_{1}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\tilde{x})\cdot\phi(\tilde{x})\mu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right).

As a result,
U(x,\mu,y,\nu)
is
L
-differentiable at
\mu
and by regarding
(x,\mu)
as parameters, we arrive at

\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{1}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\tilde{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right).

Meanwhile, note that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}h_{1}
also satisfies the centering condition, thus we have

\displaystyle\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}h_{1}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\tilde{x}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

where

\displaystyle\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}h_{1}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})+\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}).

Consequently, we have

.

(iii) Now we prove (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) with
m=2
. By the assumptions that
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and
f\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{j,(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
, we have

\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{x}),\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{x}),\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{x})\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})).

Combing this with the fact that
\partial_{y}^{2}U(x,\mu,\cdot,\cdot),\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{y})\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
, one can check that for every fixed
x,\tilde{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}
and
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
, we have

\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}h_{1}(x,\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{x})\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})).

As a result of Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})=-h_{1}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})

and

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})=-\partial_{\tilde{x}}h_{1}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x}),

which in turn yields that
\partial^{k}_{\tilde{x}}\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,\cdot,\cdot)(\tilde{x})\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Consequently, we get
U(x,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. In view of (
2.20
), by
\partial_{x}^{j}f(x,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and Theorem
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}(\mu,y,\nu)\partial_{x}^{j}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)=-\partial_{x}^{j}f(x,\mu,y,\nu),

which in turns implies that
\partial_{x}^{j}U(x,\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Thus we have

U\in{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{j,(1,k),2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})).

It remains to show the second order derivatives of
U(x,\mu,y,\nu)
with respect to the
\mu
-variable.
The same argument as above we have

\partial^{2}_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{2}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

where
h_{2}
satisfies the centering condition (
2.16
) and is given by

\displaystyle h_{2}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}}):=\partial^{2}_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\!\!\Big[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\big[\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}).

Consequently, we deduce that

\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}h_{2}(x,\mu,Y_{t}^{\mu,y,\nu},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{t}^{\mu,\eta}})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right),

where

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}h_{2}(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]+\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}\big[\partial_{\mu}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}[\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})]\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{\tilde{x}}}\big[\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\big]\Big]\nu({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y}).

As a result, we obtain

. In the same way, we get
U(x,\cdot,y,\nu)\in C_{b}^{(2,k)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}))
. For general

, the proof follows by the similar argumants, we omit the details here.
∎

Given a function
f(x,\mu,y,\nu)
, we denote

\displaystyle\bar{f}(x,\mu):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

(2.23)

where
\zeta^{\mu}
is the unique invariant measure for the frozen McKean-Vlasov equation (
1.4
). The verification that the averaged coefficient

are smooth usually constitutes a separate problem connected with the smoothness of the invariant measure
\zeta^{\mu}
with respect to the parameter
\mu
. Here,
as a direct application of Theorem
2.4
, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.5
.

Assume that
(
H^{\sigma,b}
)
holds and
\ell,m,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If for every
1\leqslant n<m
,
a,b\in(C_{b}^{(m,k),2m,(m,m)}\cap{\mathbb{C}}_{b}^{(n,k),2(m-n),(m-n,m-n)})({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and

. Then we have

\bar{f}\in C_{b}^{\ell,(m,k)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}))
.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\bar{f}(x,\mu)(\tilde{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

(2.24)

and

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{\mu}\bar{f}(x,\mu)(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial^{2}_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

(2.25)

where
U(x,\mu,y,\nu)
is the unique solution to the Poisson equation

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}U(x,\mu,y,\nu)=-\big[f(x,\mu,y,\nu)-\bar{f}(x,\mu)\big].

(2.26)

Proof.

Since
\zeta^{\mu}
does not depend on the
x
-variable,
we can take derivative directly with respect to
x
from both sides of (
2.23
) to get

\displaystyle\partial^{\ell}_{x}\bar{f}(x,\mu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\partial^{\ell}_{x}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

which in turn implies that
\bar{f}(\cdot,\mu)\in C_{b}^{\ell}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
. We proceed to show the regularities of
\bar{f}
with respect to the
\mu
-variable.
Note that the function

\delta f(x,\mu,y,\nu):=f(x,\mu,y,\nu)-\bar{f}(x,\mu)

always satisfies the centering condition. Thus, under our assumptions there exists a unique solution
U
to the Poisson equation (
2.26
).
Following the same arguments as in (
2.22
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial_{\mu}\delta f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0.

(2.27)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\partial_{\mu}\delta f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\mu}\bar{f}(x,\mu)(\tilde{x})+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

this together with (
2.2
) yields (
2.5
).
Taking derivatives with respect to the
\tilde{x}
and
x
from both sides of (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}\bar{f}(x,\mu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]+\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tilde{x}}[\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})]\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})\big]\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y),

and

\displaystyle\partial_{x}[\partial_{\mu}\bar{f}(x,\mu)(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial_{x}[\partial_{\mu}f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{x}\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{x}\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{x}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\nu)(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\big[\partial_{x}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})]\big]\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y).

Therefore, we deduce
\bar{f}\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}))
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\bigg[\partial^{2}_{\mu}\delta f(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})]+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{y}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}}\Big[\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}[\partial_{\mu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})](\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}b(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial^{2}_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x},\tilde{\tilde{x}})\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\partial_{\mu}a(\mu,\tilde{y},\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{x})\cdot\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\tilde{y}}[\partial_{\nu}U(x,\mu,y,\zeta^{\mu})(\tilde{y})](\tilde{\tilde{x}})\Big]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tilde{y})\bigg]\zeta^{\mu}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}y)=0,

) holds. For general

, the proof follows by induction, we omit the details here.

3.
Fluctuations estimates

Let
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})
and
\bar{X}_{t}
satisfy the McKean-Vlasov equations (
1.1
) and (
1
), respectively. This section is devoted to establish some integral estimates for the fluctuations between
X_{t}^{\varepsilon}
and its homogenized limit
\bar{X}_{t}
.
Given a function
f(t,x,\mu,y,\nu)
on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}})
, we consider the Poisson equation:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{0}\psi(t,x,\mu,y,\nu)=-f(t,x,\mu,y,\nu),

(3.1)

where the operator
{\mathscr{L}}_{0}
is defined by (
1
), and
(t,x,\mu)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})
are regarded as parameters.
We first give the following estimate of the functional law of large number type.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(H
σ,b
)
hold,
F,H,G,c\in C_{b}^{2,(1,1),2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
and
b,\sigma\in C_{b}^{(1,1),2,(1,1)}({\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Then for every
f\in C_{b}^{1,2,(1,1),2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
satisfying (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},{\mathcal{L}}_{X_{s}^{\varepsilon}},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\right|\leqslant C_{0}\,\varepsilon,

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be the solution of the equation (
3.1
). According the assumptions on the coefficients and Theorem
2.4
, we have

\psi\in C_{b}^{1,2,(1,1),2,(1,1)}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}\times{\mathscr{P}}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}))
. Using Itô’s formula, we deduce that

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\partial_{\nu}b(Y^{y,\nu}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})(\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{\eta}}_{t})\cdot\tilde{Z}^{\tilde{\eta}}_{t}(\tilde{y})]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\partial_{\nu}b(Y^{y,\nu}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})(\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t})\cdot\partial_{y}\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})]\rangle\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\partial_{\nu}\sigma(Y^{y,\nu}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})(\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{\eta}}_{t})\cdot\tilde{Z}^{\tilde{\eta}}_{t}(\tilde{y})]+\tilde{\mathbb{E}}[\partial_{\nu}\sigma(Y^{y,\nu}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{Y^{\eta}_{t}})(\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t})\cdot\partial_{y}\tilde{Y}^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})]\|^{2}\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{y}Y^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}e^{-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma)t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

and thus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}\leqslant C_{0}e^{-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma)t}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{y}Y^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}\|^{2p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial^{2}_{y}Y^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}e^{-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-\gamma)t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

which in turn yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}\leqslant C_{0}e^{-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma)t}.

This further implies

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}Z^{\eta}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\quad+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{y}Y^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}\|^{2p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial^{2}_{y}Y^{\tilde{y},\nu}_{t}\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-\gamma){\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+C_{0}e^{-\frac{p}{2}(c_{2}-c_{1}-\gamma)t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

and thus the desired result is obtained. In the same way, we can prove the estimates of
{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{y}\partial_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y})\|^{p}
and

{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial^{2}_{\nu}Y^{y,\nu}_{t}(\tilde{y},\breve{y})\|^{p}
, we omit the details here.
∎

Almost Riemann Solitons with Vertical Potential on Conformal Cosymplectic Contact Complex Riemannian Manifolds
Mancho Manev

Abstract
. Almost Riemann solitons are introduced and studied on an almost contact complex Riemannian manifold, i. ​e. an almost contact B-metric manifold, obtained from a cosymplectic manifold of the considered type by a contact conformal transformation of the Reeb vector field, its dual contact 1-form, the B-metric, and its associated B-metric. The potential of the studied soliton is assumed to be in the vertical distribution, i. ​e. it is collinear to the Reeb vector field. In this way, manifolds from the four main classes of the studied manifolds are obtained. Curvature properties of the resulting manifolds are derived. An explicit example of dimension five is constructed. The Bochner curvature tensor is used (for dimension at least seven) as a conformal invariant to get properties and construct an explicit example in relation to the obtained results.

2.
Almost Contact Complex Riemannian Manifolds and Their Contact Conformal Transformations

We study
almost contact complex Riemannian manifolds
or
accR manifolds
for short,
also known as
almost contact B-metric manifolds
.
Such a manifold, denoted by
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
, is a
(2n+1)
-dimensional differentiable manifold, which is equipped with an almost
contact structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta)
and B-metric
g
. This means that
\varphi
is an endomorphism
of the tangent bundle
TM
,
\xi
is a Reeb vector field and
\eta
is its dual contact 1-form.
Moreover,

g
is a pseudo-Riemannian
metric of signature
(n+1,n)
satisfying the following
algebraic relations:
[
10
]

(2)

\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi\xi=0,\qquad\varphi^{2}=-\iota+\eta\otimes\xi,\qquad\eta\circ\varphi=0,\qquad\eta(\xi)=1,\\
g(\varphi x,\varphi y)=-g(x,y)+\eta(x)\eta(y),\end{array}

where
\iota
is the identity transformation on the set
\Gamma(TM)
of vector fields on
M
.

As consequences of (
2
) are known the following equations

\begin{array}[]{ll}g(\varphi x,y)=g(x,\varphi y),\qquad g(x,\xi)=\eta(x),\qquad g(\xi,\xi)=1,\qquad\eta(\nabla_{x}\xi)=0,\end{array}

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.

The investigated manifold
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
has another B-metric besides
g
.
This is the associated metric
\tilde{g}
of
g
on
M
defined by

\tilde{g}(x,y)=g(x,\varphi y)+\eta(x)\eta(y).

Obviously,
\tilde{g}
as well as
g
satisfies also the last condition in (
2
) and has the same signature.

A classification of accR manifolds containing eleven basic classes
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\dots
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
is given in

[
10
]
. This classification is made with respect
to the tensor
F
of type
(0,3)
defined by

F(x,y,z)=g\bigl(\left(\nabla_{x}\varphi\right)y,z\bigr).

The following identities are valid:

\begin{array}[]{l}F(x,y,z)=F(x,z,y)=F(x,\varphi y,\varphi z)+\eta(y)F(x,\xi,z)+\eta(z)F(x,y,\xi),\\[4.0pt]
F(x,\varphi y,\xi)=(\nabla_{x}\eta)y=g(\nabla_{x}\xi,y).\end{array}

The special class
\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
determined by the condition
F=0
, is the intersection of the basic classes and it is known as the
class of the
cosymplectic accR manifolds
.
Sometimes, in the context of classification and for brevity, these manifolds are called
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifolds.

Let
\left\{e_{i};\xi\right\}

(i=1,2,\dots,2n)
be a basis of

T_{p}M
and let
\left(g^{ij}\right)
be the inverse matrix of the
matrix
\left(g_{ij}\right)
of
g
. Then the following 1-forms
are associated with
F
:

\theta=g^{ij}F(e_{i},e_{j},\cdot),\quad\theta^{*}=g^{ij}F(e_{i},\varphi e_{j},\cdot),\quad\omega=F(\xi,\xi,\cdot).

These 1-forms are known also as the
Lee forms
of the considered manifold. Obviously, the identities

\omega(\xi)=0
and
\theta^{*}\circ\varphi=-\theta\circ\varphi^{2}
are always valid.

In
[
20
]
, it is introduced the so-called contact conformal transformation of the B-metric
g
. It maps
g
into a new B-metric
\bar{g}
using both the B-metrics. Later, in
[
15
]
, this transformation is generalized as a contact conformal transformation that gives an accR structure
(\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
as follows

(3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{\xi}=e^{-w}\xi,\qquad\bar{\eta}=e^{w}\eta,\\[4.0pt]
\bar{g}=e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,g+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\,\tilde{g}+\left(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v}-e^{2u}\sin{2v}\right)\eta\otimes\eta,\end{array}

where
u,v,w
are differentiable functions on
M
. The group of these transformations is denoted by
G
and briefly we call each of the elements of
G
a
G
-transformation
.

Let us note that the
G
-transformations of
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
are a generalization of the
\mathcal{D}
-homothetic deformations
, where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the contact distribution
\ker\eta
. Namely, for a positive constant
t
, a
\mathcal{D}
-homothetic deformation is defined by
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
, p. 125]
for the metric case)

\bar{\xi}=\lambda^{-1}\xi,\qquad\bar{\eta}=\lambda\,\eta,\qquad\bar{g}=-\lambda\,g+\lambda(\lambda+1)\eta\otimes\eta.

It is clear that
\mathcal{D}
-homothetic deformation is a
G
-transformation of the accR structure
(\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}g)
for constants
u=\frac{1}{2}\ln\lambda
,
v=\frac{\pi}{2}
and

.

The structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
determines two mutually orthogonal distributions with respect to

.
They are the horizontal (contact) distribution
\mathcal{H}=\ker\eta
and the vertical distribution
\mathcal{V}=\Span\xi
. They coincide with the respective distributions for the structure
(\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
, i. ​e.
\mathcal{H}=\bar{\mathcal{H}}=\ker\bar{\eta}
and
\mathcal{V}=\bar{\mathcal{V}}=\Span\bar{\xi}
, due to the equalities in the first line of (
3

The corresponding tensors
F
and
\bar{F}
for the accR structures
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
and
(\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
by a
G
-transformation (
3
) are related as follows (e.g.
[
15
]
, see also
[
22
, (22)]
)

(4)

\displaystyle 2\bar{F}

\displaystyle(x,y,z)=2e^{2u}\cos{2v}\,F(x,y,z)+e^{2u}\sin{2v}\left[P(x,y,z)+P(x,z,y)\right]

\displaystyle+(e^{2w}-e^{2u}\cos{2v})\left[Q(x,y,z)+Q(x,z,y)+Q(y,z,x)+Q(z,y,x)\right]

\displaystyle-2e^{2u}\left[\gamma(z)g(\varphi x,\varphi y)+\delta(z)g(x,\varphi y)+\gamma(y)g(\varphi x,\varphi z)+\delta(y)g(x,\varphi z)\right]

\displaystyle+2e^{2w}\eta(x)\left[\eta(y)\D w(\varphi z)+\eta(z)\D w(\varphi y)\right],

where for brevity we use the notations

\begin{array}[]{l}P(x,y,z)=F(\varphi y,z,x)-F(y,\varphi z,x)+F(x,\varphi y,\xi)\eta(z),\\[4.0pt]

\gamma(z)=\cos{2v}\,\alpha(z)+\sin{2v}\,\beta(z),\qquad\delta(z)=\cos{2v}\,\beta(z)-\sin{2v}\,\alpha(z),\end{array}

(5)

In the general case, the relations between the Lee forms of the corresponding manifolds
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
and

(6)

\bar{\theta}=\theta+2n\,\alpha,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{*}=\theta^{*}+2n\,\beta,\qquad\bar{\omega}=\omega+\D w\circ\varphi.

As proved in

, denoted here briefly as
G(\mathcal{F}_{0})
.
The main classes are the only classes of accR manifolds in the Ganchev–Mihova–Gribachev classification, where

is expressed only by the metric
(0,2)
-tensors
g
,
\tilde{g}
,
\eta\otimes\eta

The class

\mathcal{F}_{0}

3.
Almost Riemann Solitons with Vertical Potential on Contact Conformal accR Manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

One says that the B-metric
\bar{g}
generates a
Riemann soliton
with potential
\bar{\vartheta}
and constant
\bar{\sigma}
, denoted
(\bar{g},\bar{\vartheta},\bar{\sigma})
, on an accR manifold
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
, if the following condition is satisfied

(7)

2\bar{R}+\bar{\sigma}\,\bar{g}\owedge\bar{g}+\bar{g}\owedge\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\vartheta}}\bar{g}=0,

where
\bar{R}
is the Riemannian curvature tensor of
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
for
\bar{g}
.
If
\bar{\sigma}
is a differentiable function on
M
, then the generated soliton is called
almost Riemann soliton

(\bar{g},\bar{\vartheta},\bar{\sigma})
on
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
.

In this work, we consider the case when the potential
\bar{\vartheta}
is a vertical vector field, i. ​e.
\bar{\vartheta}
is collinear to
\bar{\xi}
. Then we have the expression
\bar{\vartheta}=\bar{k}\,\bar{\xi}
for a differentiable function
\bar{k}
on the manifold. Obviously, the equality
\bar{k}=\bar{\eta}(\bar{\vartheta})
holds.
We require that the potential
\bar{\vartheta}
does not degenerate at any point on the manifold
(M,\varphi,\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta},\bar{g})
. This means that
\bar{k}
does not vanish anywhere, i. ​e.
\bar{k}\neq 0
.

It is well known the following expression of the Lie derivative in terms of the covariant derivative with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\bar{\nabla}
of
\bar{g}

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}\right)(x,y)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{x}\bar{\xi},y\right)+\bar{g}\left(x,\bar{\nabla}_{y}\bar{\xi}\right).

Similarly, the following formula can be obtained

(8)

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\vartheta}}\bar{g}\right)(x,y)=\bar{g}\left(\bar{\nabla}_{x}\bar{\vartheta},y\right)+\bar{g}\left(x,\bar{\nabla}_{y}\bar{\vartheta}\right).

For a vertical potential we have

\bar{\nabla}_{x}\bar{\vartheta}=\bar{\nabla}\left(\bar{k}\,\bar{\xi}\right)=\bar{k}\bar{\nabla}\bar{\xi}+\D\bar{k}(x)\bar{\xi}.

Then, the latter two equalities imply the formula

(9)

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\vartheta}}\bar{g}\right)(x,y)=\bar{k}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\xi}}\bar{g}\right)(x,y)+\bar{h}_{1}(x,y),

where we use the following notation

(10)

\bar{h}_{1}(x,y)=\D\bar{k}(x)\bar{\eta}(y)+\D\bar{k}(y)\bar{\eta}(x).

Obviously, the
(0,2)
-tensor
\bar{h}_{1}
is symmetric and has the properties

\bar{h}_{1}(\varphi x,\varphi y)=0,\qquad\bar{h}_{1}(\bar{\xi},\bar{\xi})=\tr{\bar{h}_{1}}=2\D\bar{k}(\bar{\xi}).

Therefore, it vanishes on
\mathcal{H}
.
Furthermore,
\bar{h}_{1}
vanishes if and only if
\bar{k}
is a constant.

The following theorem holds for an arbitrary
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold
M
.
It is not necessary to assume that the structure of
M
is obtained by some
G
-transformation.

Theorem 3.2
.

Every
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold admitting an almost Riemann soliton
with vertical potential is flat.

Proof.

Let us consider an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
admitting an almost Riemann soliton
(g,\allowbreak{}\vartheta,\allowbreak{}\sigma)
with vertical potential
\vartheta=k\,\xi
.
Then its curvature tensor for
g
has the following form, similar to (
7
)

R=\frac{1}{2}\sigma\,g\owedge g-\frac{1}{2}g\owedge\mathcal{L}_{\vartheta}g.

Since
\nabla\xi
vanishes on an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold, then we have
\nabla_{x}\vartheta=\nabla_{x}(k\xi)=\D{k}(x)\xi
.
Due to the equality

\left(\mathcal{L}_{\vartheta}g\right)(x,y)=g\left(\nabla_{x}\vartheta,y\right)+g\left(x,\nabla_{y}\vartheta\right)
,
which is the analogue of (
8
) on
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
, we get in this case that

\mathcal{L}_{\vartheta}g=h_{1},
where we use the following notation similar to (
10
)

(11)

h_{1}(x,y)=\D k(x)\eta(y)+\D k(y)\eta(x).

Thus, the curvature tensor of such a manifold
(M,\allowbreak{}\varphi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,\allowbreak{}{}g)
takes the form

(12)

R=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\,g\owedge g-\frac{1}{2}g\owedge h_{1}.

Using (
11
) and (
12
), we get the Ricci tensor and scalar curvatures for
g
and
\tilde{g}
, respectively, given in the following expressions

(13)

\displaystyle\rho=-\left\{2n\,\sigma+\D k(\xi)\right\}g-\frac{1}{2}(2n-1)h_{1},

(14)

\displaystyle\tau=-2n\left\{(2n+1)\sigma+2\D k(\xi)\right\},\qquad\tilde{\tau}=0.

The Riemannian curvature tensor
R
of an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-manifold has the Kähler property

(15)

R(x,y,\varphi z,\varphi w)=-R(x,y,z,w)

since
\varphi
,
\xi
and
\eta
are covariant constant on
M
with respect to
\nabla

[
21
]
.
As consequences of (
15
) and (
11
) we have
\rho(\xi,\xi)=0
and
h_{1}(\xi,\xi)=2\D{k}(\xi)
, respectively, which together with (
13
) imply

(16)

\D{k}(\xi)=-\sigma.

On the other hand, by virtue of (
12
) and (
1
), we obtain

(17)

R(x,y,\varphi z,\varphi w)=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\left(g^{*}\owedge g^{*}\right)(x,y,z,w)-\frac{1}{2}\left(g^{*}\owedge h_{1}^{*}\right)(x,y,z,w),

where we use the notations
g^{*}=g(\cdot,\varphi\cdot)
,
h_{1}^{*}=h_{1}(\cdot,\varphi\cdot)
.
Then, taking into account that
g^{*}
and
h_{1}^{*}
are traceless due to the properties of
\varphi
, equalities (
15
) and (
17
) yield consequently

(18)

R=-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\,g^{*}\owedge g^{*}+\frac{1}{2}g^{*}\owedge h_{1}^{*},\qquad\rho=\sigma\left[g-\eta\otimes\eta\right],\qquad\tau=2n\sigma,\qquad\tilde{\tau}=0.

Comparing the values of
\tau
in (

) and (
14
), we obtain
\D{k}(\xi)=-(n+1)\sigma
, which due to (
16
) gives
\D{k}(\xi)=\sigma=0
.
Therefore, from (
18
) it follows
\rho=0
, which together with
(
13
) imply
h_{1}=0
and then
R=0
, bearing in mind (
12
).
∎

A

(\bar{g},\allowbreak{}\bar{\vartheta},\allowbreak{}\bar{\sigma})

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{R}=-\left[\frac{\bar{\sigma}}{2}+\bar{k}\D{u}(\bar{\xi})\right]\bar{g}\owedge\bar{g}-\bar{k}\left\{\D{v}(\bar{\xi})\,\bar{g}\owedge\tilde{\bar{g}}\right.\left.-\left[\D{u}(\bar{\xi})+\D{v}(\bar{\xi})\right]\bar{g}\owedge\left(\bar{\eta}\otimes\bar{\eta}\right)\right\}\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{\bar{R}=}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{g}\owedge\bar{h}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{k}\,\bar{g}\owedge\bar{h}_{2},\end{array}

Conclusions

Steady-state solutions of geometric flows, including almost Riemann solitons, are still the subject of intense research and interest in differential geometry.
The present paper introduced almost Riemann solitons on almost contact complex Riemannian manifolds and achieved first results in the study the coexistence of these structures on an odd-dimensional manifold.
More precisely, the most important curvature properties of manifolds obtained from manifolds of cosymplectic type by conformal transformations of the considered structures were described.
Since the study of Riemann solitons is still in its beginning, any contribution in this direction may bring new perspectives on the geometry of the manifold.

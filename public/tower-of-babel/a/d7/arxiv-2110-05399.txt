Structure of Chern-Simons Scattering Amplitudes from Topological Equivalence Theorem and Double-Copy


Abstract
We study the mechanism of topological mass-generation for 3d Chern-Simons (CS) gauge theories, where the CS term can retain the gauge symmetry and make gauge boson topologically massive. Without CS term the 3d massless gauge boson has a single physical transverse polarization state, while adding the CS term converts it into a massive physical polarization state and conserves the total physical degrees of freedom. We newly formulate the mechanism of topological mass-generation at S S -matrix level. For this, we propose and prove a new Topological Equivalence Theorem (TET) which connects the N N -point scattering amplitude of the gauge boson’s physical polarization states ( A P a A^{a}_{\text{P}} ) to that of the transverse polarization states ( A T a A^{a}_{\text{T}} ) under high energy expansion. We present a general 3d power counting method on the leading energy dependence of N N -point scattering amplitudes in both topologically massive Yang-Mills (TMYM) and topologically massive gravity (TMG) theories. With these, we uncover a general energy cancellation mechanism for N N -gauge boson scattering amplitudes which predicts the cancellation E 4 → E 4 − N \hskip 0.85358ptE^{4}\hskip-0.85358pt\!\rightarrow\!E^{4-N} at tree level. Then, we compute the four-point amplitudes of A P a A^{a}_{\text{P}} ’s and of A T a A^{a}_{\text{T}} ’s, with which we explicitly demonstrate the TET and establish such energy cancellations. We further extend the double-copy approach and construct the four-point massive graviton amplitude of the TMG theory from the massive gauge boson amplitude of the TMYM theory. With these, we newly uncover striking large energy cancellations E 12 → E 1 E^{12}\!\!\rightarrow\!E^{1} in the four-graviton amplitude of the TMG, and establish its new correspondence to the leading energy cancellations E 4 → E 0 E^{4}\!\rightarrow\!E^{0}\hskip 0.85358pt in the four-gauge boson amplitude of the TMYM. Topological Mass Generation in Chern-Simons Gauge Theories

We consider the 3d topological massive gauge theories including
the Chern-Simons (CS) Lagrangian
with Abelian or non-Abelian gauge symmetry,
where the former may be denoted as
Topologically Massive QED (TMQED)
and the latter as Topologically Massive Yang-Mills (TMYM) theory.
In either case, the CS Lagrangian provides a gauge-invariant
topological mass-term for the 3d gauge bosons.
The 3d TMQED and TMYM Lagrangians have their gauge sectors
take the following forms:

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TMQED}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{m}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}A_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\rho}\,,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\hskip-9.95845pt\hskip-9.95845pt\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TMYM}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}^{2}\!+\widetilde{m}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\text{tr}\!\left(\!\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\mathbf{A}_{\rho}\!-\!\frac{\,\text{i}\hskip 0.85358pt2g\,}{3}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\mathbf{A}_{\nu}\mathbf{A}_{\rho}\!\right)\!,

(2.1b)

where the non-Abelian gauge field

\,\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\!\!=\!A^{a}_{\mu}T^{a}
,
and its field strength

\,\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}\!=\!F^{a}_{\mu\nu}T^{a}
with

\mathbb{F}_{\mu\nu}\!=\!\partial_{\mu}\mathbf{A}_{\nu}\!\!-\!\partial_{\nu}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\!-\!\text{i}g[\mathbf{A}_{\mu},\mathbf{A}_{\nu}]

and
\,T^{a}
denotes the generator of
the non-Abelian group SU(
N
).
The gauge coupling
g
has mass-dimension

\frac{\,{1}\,}{2}
.
The gauge boson acquires a topological mass

\,m\!=\!|\widetilde{m}|
from the CS term, and the ratio

\,\mathfrak{s}={\widetilde{m}}/{m}=\pm 1

corresponds to its spin projection
[
1
]
[
2
]
.
The mass parameter
\widetilde{m}
is related to the CS level

\,n=4\pi\widetilde{m}/g^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\,

[
2
]
[
3
]
.
The CS terms in Eq.(
2.1
) violate
the discrete symmetries
P
,
T
and
CP
.

For the TMQED (
2.1a
), the action

\int\!\hskip-0.7113pt\text{d}^{3}x\hskip 0.99585pt\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TMQED}}\,

is gauge-invariant up to a total derivative
which vanishes at the boundary for trivial topology.
For the TMYM theory (
2.1b
), under the
gauge transformation

\,\mathbf{A}_{\mu}\hskip-1.42262pt\rightarrow\hskip 0.85358pt\mathbf{A}_{\mu}^{\prime}\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip 0.85358ptU^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{\mu}U\!+\!\mbox{$\frac{\,{\text{i}}\,}{g}$}U^{-1}\partial_{\mu}U
,
the action changes by

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\mathrm{TMYM}}\,=\,2\pi nw+\!\int\!\text{d}^{3}x\!\left[\,\text{i}\hskip 0.99585pt\widetilde{m}g^{-1}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\hskip 0.85358pt\text{tr}\!\left(\partial_{\mu}UU^{-1}A_{\rho}\right)\!\,\right]\!,

(2.2a)

\displaystyle w\,=\,\frac{1}{\,24\pi^{2}\,}\!\!\int\!\text{d}^{3}x\!\left[\,\!\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\,\text{tr}(U^{-1}\partial_{\mu}UU^{-1}\partial_{\nu}UU^{-1}\partial_{\rho}U)\!\,\right]\!,

(2.2b)

where
\,w\,
is the winding number which follows from the homotopy group
\Pi_{3}[\mathrm{SU}(N)]\!\cong\!\mathbb{Z}

[
3
]
.
Hence, Eq.(
2.2a
) will not contribute to the path integral
since
\,e^{\text{i}2\pi nw}=1\,
,  and the second term is a
total derivative (similar to the Abelian case).

With the path integral formulation, we can add the covariant
gauge-fixing term and the Faddeev-Popov ghost term:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GF}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\,2\xi\,}(\mathcal{F}^{a})^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{F}^{a}\!=\partial^{\mu}\!A_{\mu}^{a}\,,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FP}}

\displaystyle=\bar{c}^{a}\partial^{\mu}\!\left(\delta^{ab}\partial_{\mu}-gC^{abc}A_{\mu}^{c}\right)\!c^{b},

(2.3b)

where
C^{abc}
is the gauge group structure constant and

(c^{a},\,\bar{c}^{a})
denote the Faddeev-Popov ghost and anti-ghost fields.
Eq.(
2.3
) can be reduced to the Abelian case by
simply setting
\,C^{abc}
=0\,
and
\,A^{a}_{\mu}\!=\!A_{\mu}
.
So, hereafter we need not to specify the Abelian case unless needed.
The quantized CS action

\,\int\!\text{d}^{3}x(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GF}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FP}})\,

is BRST-invariant (Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin), with which we
can derive the relevant BRST identities.

The equation of motion (EOM) for the massive gauge boson
A^{a}_{\mu}

can be derived from the quadratic part of the CS action,

\left[\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+(\xi^{-1}\!-\!1)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\!+\widetilde{m}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\rho\nu}\partial_{\rho}\,\right]\!A^{a}_{\nu}\,=\,0\,,

(2.4)

which describes the propagation of the free field
A^{a}_{\mu}
.
For the on-shell wave solution

\,A^{a}_{\mu}\!\sim\!\epsilon_{\mu}(p)e^{-\text{i}p\cdot x}\,

with
\,p^{\mu}\!A_{\mu}^{a}\!=0
,
the polarization vector should satisfy the
equation

(m\eta^{\mu\nu}-\text{i}\hskip 0.56905pt\mathfrak{s}\hskip 0.56905pt\varepsilon^{\mu\rho\nu}p_{\rho})\,\epsilon_{\nu}(p)\,=\,0\,,

(2.5)

under the on-shell condition
\,p^{2}\!=\!-m^{2}
and with

\,\mathfrak{s}\!=\!{\widetilde{m}}/{m}\!=\!\pm 1
.
The 3d Poincaré group ISO(2,1)
contains the proper Lorentz group
SO(2,1) and translations. The little group
is
\,\mathbb{Z}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}
for massless particles and
SO(2) for massive particles

27

The Poincaré algebra is characterized by two Casimir operators

(P^{2},\,W)=(P_{\mu}P^{\mu},\,P_{\mu}J^{\mu})
,
where

is the Pauli-Lubanski pseudoscalar and the angular

J^{\mu}\,=\,-\text{i}\hskip 0.85358pt\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha}p_{\nu}\frac{\!\partial}{\partial p_{\alpha}}-\mathfrak{s}\frac{\,\,p^{\mu}\!+\!\eta^{\mu}m\,}{\,p\!\cdot\!\eta-m\,}\,,

(2.6)

with
\,\eta^{\mu}\!=\!(1,0,0)\,
.
Thus, in the rest frame it gives

W\!=P\!\cdot\!J=-\mathfrak{s}\hskip 0.85358ptm\,
.
We see that the spin is a pseudoscalar
and takes the values
\,\mathfrak{s}\!=\pm 1

for gauge fields
A^{a}_{\mu}
.
The polarization state with either

\,\mathfrak{s}\!=\!+1\,
or
\,\mathfrak{s}\!=\!-1\,

is physically equivalent.
(More discussions on the gauge boson polarization vector

Note that the 3d massless gauge field can be viewed as a
scalar field of spin-0 with one physical degree of
freedom (DoF)

]
.
Including the CS term does not add any new field,
and the total physical DoF remains as one because
the physical DoF of
A^{a}_{\mu}

should be conserved

.
For the on-shell one-particle state,
the 3d massless gauge boson has a single
(transverse) physical polarization state

\,A^{a}_{\text{T}}=\hskip-0.85358pt\epsilon_{\text{T}}^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}\hskip 0.85358pt
.
As the physical DoF is conserved,
the CS term could only convert the massless
A^{a}_{\text{T}}
state
into a massive physical polarization state

A^{a}_{\text{P}}\!=\!\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}
.

For the on-shell gauge boson in the rest frame with momentum

\,p^{\mu}\!=\!(m,\,0,\,0)\equiv\bar{p}^{\mu}
,
the physical polarization vector
\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(\bar{p})

can be solved from Eq.(
2.5
):

\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(\bar{p})\,=\,\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}$}(0,\,1,\,-\text{i}\hskip 0.56905pt\mathfrak{s})\,,

(2.7)

in agreement with
[
29
]
[
30
]
.
Then, by making a Lorentz boost we can express
\hskip 0.85358pt\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(p)\hskip 0.85358pt

in the moving frame:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(p)\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}\!\!\left(\!\frac{\,\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2}}{m},\,\text{i}\!+\!\frac{\,p_{1}(\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2})}{m(m\!-\!p_{0})},\,\mathfrak{s}\!+\!\frac{\,p_{2}(\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2})}{m(m\!-\!p_{0})}\!\right)\!,

(2.8)

which agrees with
[
30
]

up to an overall factor
i
.
The on-shell physical polarization vector

\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(p)
obeys the conditions

\,\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}\epsilon^{\,*}_{\text{P}\mu}\!\!=\!1

and
\,p_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}_{P}\!\!=\!0
.
We can express the general momentum

in a familiar form

\,p^{\mu}\!=\!E(1,\,\beta s_{\theta},\,\beta c_{\theta})\,
,
where the notations
\,p^{0}\!=\!-p_{0}\!=\!E\hskip 0.85358pt
,

\hskip 0.85358pt(s_{\theta},\,c_{\theta})\!=\!(\sin\theta,\,\cos\theta)
,

\beta\!=\!\hskip-0.85358pt\sqrt{1\!-\!m^{2}\hskip-0.85358pt/E^{2}\,}

and

denotes the angle between the moving direction
and

-axis. With these, we can rewrite the

\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(p)=\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}$}(\bar{E}\beta,\,\bar{E}s_{\theta}\!+\!\text{i}\hskip 0.56905pt\mathfrak{s}\hskip 0.56905ptc_{\theta},\,\bar{E}c_{\theta}\!-\!\text{i}\hskip 0.56905pt\mathfrak{s}s_{\theta})\,,

where

which contains the transverse and longitudinal polarization vectors

includes 1 physical polarization state

and 2 orthogonal unphysical polarization states:

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\text{X}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\epsilon_{\text{X}}^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}=\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}$}\!\left(A^{a}_{\text{T}}-A^{a}_{\text{L}}\right),

(2.13a)

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\text{S}}

\displaystyle\,=\,\epsilon_{\text{S}}^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a}\,,

\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{X}}^{\mu}\!=(\epsilon_{\text{T}}^{\mu}\!-\epsilon_{\text{L}}^{\mu})/\!\sqrt{2\,}\,

\,\epsilon_{\text{S}}^{\mu}\!=p^{\mu}\!/m\,

\,\epsilon_{\text{P}}\!\cdot\epsilon_{\text{X}}^{\,*}=\epsilon_{\text{P}}\!\cdot\epsilon_{\text{S}}^{\,*}=\epsilon_{\text{X}}\!\cdot\epsilon_{\text{S}}^{\,*}=0\,

We see that adding the CS term for gauge boson

dynamically generates a new physical polarization state

In this work, we systematically studied the mechanism of the topological
mass-generations in 3d CS gauge theories and newly formulated it
at the
S
-matrix level. For this, we proposed and proved a new
Topological Equivalence Theorem (TET) for understanding the structure
of the scattering amplitudes of physical gauge bosons (

in the topologically massive gauge theories.
We newly uncovered the nontrivial large energy cancellations
in the
N

the Abelian and non-Abelian CS gauge theories.
We further used an extended double-copy approach to analyze the
structure of the graviton scattering amplitudes in the 3d
topologically massive gravity (TMG) theory, with which we
constructed the massive physical graviton scattering amplitudes
from that of the corresponding massive physical gauge bosons
in the topologically massive Yang-Mills (TMYM) theory.
From these, we newly uncovered
a series of striking large energy cancellations
in the four-point physical graviton scattering amplitudes,
which ensure such massive scattering amplitudes
to have good high energy behaviors
and obey the perturbative unitarity bounds. We summarize these new findings more explicitly as follows.

In section

topological mass-generation in the 3d CS gauge theories.
In such dynamics,
including the CS term (

the gauge boson’s transverse polarization state

(combined

\hskip 0.85358ptA^{a}_{\text{X}}\!\propto\!(A^{a}_{\text{T}}\hskip-0.85358pt-\hskip-1.42262ptA^{a}_{\text{L}})\hskip 1.42262pt
in Eq.(

become an unphysical state.
This topological mass-generation mechanism has essential difference
from the conventional Higgs mechanism
[

Appendix A
Kinematics and Feynman Rules
of 3d CS Gauge Theories

In this Appendix, we present definitions of relevant
kinematic variables for the four-particle scattering process and
the Feynman rules for the 3d topologically massive
Chern-Simons gauge theories.

For the present analysis, we choose the following
metric signature and the rank-3 anti-symmetric tensor:

\eta_{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(-1,1,1)\,,\quad\varepsilon^{012}=-\varepsilon_{012}=1\,.

(A.1)

Thus, we have the momentum on-shell condition

\,p^{2}\!=\!-m^{2}
.

For the
2\!\rightarrow\!2
elastic scattering process,
the momenta in the center-of-mass frame can be defined
as follows:

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=E(1,0,\beta),\quad

\displaystyle p_{2}^{\mu}=E(1,0,-\beta),\quad

\displaystyle p_{3}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=E(1,\beta s_{\theta},\beta c_{\theta}),\quad

\displaystyle p_{4}^{\mu}=E(1,-\beta s_{\theta},-\beta c_{\theta}),

(A.2)

where we have defined
\,\beta\!=\!\sqrt{1\!-\!m^{2}/E^{2}\,}
.
Thus, we further define the Mandelstam variables:

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=-\left(p_{1}\!+\!p_{2}\right)^{2}\!=4E^{2},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=-\left(p_{1}\!-\!p_{4}\right)^{2}\!=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{2}$}\hskip 0.85358pts\hskip 0.85358pt\beta^{2}(1\!+\!c_{\theta})\,,

(A.3)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=-\left(p_{1}\!-\!p_{3}\right)^{2}\!=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\,2\,}$}\hskip 0.85358pts\hskip 0.85358pt\beta^{2}(1\!-\!c_{\theta})\,.

For convenience of our analysis,
we can use the relation

\,E^{2}\!=\!E^{2}\beta^{2}\!+\!m^{2}\,

to define another set of Mandelstam variables

(s_{0},t_{0},u_{0})
:

\displaystyle s_{0}=4E^{2}\beta^{2}\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ t_{0}=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{2}$}s_{0}(1\!+\!c_{\theta})\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u_{0}=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{2}$}s_{0}(1\!-\!c_{\theta})\,.

(A.4)

The summations of
(s,t,u)
and
(s_{0},t_{0},u_{0})
obey
the conditions:

s+t+u=4m^{2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \qquad s_{0}+t_{0}+u_{0}=0\,.

(A.5)

In the rest frame with 3-momentum

\,p^{\mu}\!=(m,\,0,\,0)\equiv\bar{p}^{\mu}
,
we can solve Eq.(
2.5
) and derive the polarization vector:

\epsilon^{\mu}(\bar{p})=\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}$}(0,\,1,\,-\text{i}\mathfrak{s})\,,

(A.6)

where
\,\mathfrak{s}={\widetilde{m}}/{m}=\pm 1
and
\,m=|\widetilde{m}|
.
We note that the in the rest frame the gauge boson polarization
vector has zero time-component and its two possible forms
are not independent due to the relation

\,\epsilon_{2}^{\mu}=\text{i}\hskip 0.85358pt\mathfrak{s}\hskip 0.7113pt\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}
.
Furthermore, by choosing the orthonormal basis

e^{j}_{1}=(1,0)
and
e^{j}_{2}=(0,1)
in a plane,
we can define a polarization basis:

e^{j}_{\pm}\,=\,\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2}}$}(e^{j}_{1}\pm\text{i}\hskip 0.7113pte^{j}_{2}\hskip 0.7113pt)\,=\,\mbox{$\frac{\,{1}\,}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}$}\!\left(1,\,\pm\text{i}\hskip 0.7113pt\right).

(A.7)

Thus, in the rest frame
the spatial part of the polarization vector

\epsilon^{\mu}(\bar{p})

can be decomposed in terms of the basis

\{e^{j}_{\pm}\}
:

\epsilon^{j}(\bar{p})\,=\,\epsilon_{+}e^{j}_{+}+\epsilon_{-}e^{j}_{-}\,,

(A.8)

where the coefficients
\,(\epsilon_{+},\,\epsilon_{-})

satisfy

\,(\epsilon_{+},\,\epsilon_{-})\!=\!(0,\,1)

for
\,\mathfrak{s}\!=\!+1
and

\,(\epsilon_{+},\,\epsilon_{-})\!=\!(1,\,0)

for
\mathfrak{s}=\!-1

[
30
]
.
So, it is clear that in the 3d Chern-Simons gauge theory,
the case of
\,\mathfrak{s}=\!+1
(or,
\mathfrak{s}\!=\!-1
)
only allows one physical polarization state

\epsilon_{-}
(or,
\epsilon_{+}
)
of the gauge boson, as expected.

After taking the Lorentz boost along an arbitrary direction,
the gauge boson momentum can be generally written as

\,p^{\mu}\!=\!E(1,\,\beta s_{\theta},\,\beta c_{\theta})\,
. Thus, we can
Lorentz-boost the rest-frame polarization vector
(
A.6
) to the following general polarization vector:

\epsilon_{\text{P}}^{\mu}(p)\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,}\,}\!\!\left(\!\frac{\,\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2}}{m},\ \text{i}\!+\!\frac{\,p_{1}(\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2})}{m(m\!-\!p_{0})},\ \mathfrak{s}\!+\!\frac{\,p_{2}(\text{i}p_{1}\!\!+\!\mathfrak{s}p_{2})}{m(m\!-\!p_{0})}\!\right)\!,

(A.9)

where
\,\epsilon^{\mu}_{+}\!=\!-(\epsilon^{\mu}_{-})^{*}
.
Thus, by substituting Eq.(
A
) into Eq.(
A.9
),
we derive the following explicit formulas
of the physical polarization vectors:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\frac{\,{\mathfrak{s}}\,}{\sqrt{2}}$}(\bar{E}\beta,\,\text{i}\mathfrak{s},\,\bar{E})\,,\quad

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}^{\mu}=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{\mathfrak{s}}\,}{\sqrt{2}}$}(\bar{E}\beta,\,-\text{i}\mathfrak{s},\,-\bar{E})\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\frac{\,{\mathfrak{s}e^{\text{i}\mathfrak{s}\theta}}\,}{\sqrt{2}}$}(\bar{E}\beta,\,\bar{E}s_{\theta}\!+\!\text{i}\mathfrak{s}c_{\theta},\,\bar{E}c_{\theta}\!-\!\text{i}\mathfrak{s}s_{\theta})\,,\quad

\displaystyle\epsilon_{4}^{\mu}=-\mbox{$\frac{\,{\mathfrak{s}e^{\text{i}\mathfrak{s}\theta}}\,}{\sqrt{2}}$}(\bar{E}\beta,\,-\bar{E}s_{\theta}\!-\!\text{i}\mathfrak{s}c_{\theta},\,-\bar{E}c_{\theta}\!+\!\text{i}\mathfrak{s}s_{\theta})\,,

where we have defined
\,\bar{E}=\!E/m

(A.11a)

(A.11b)

In Eq.(

the pole

for which the EOM (

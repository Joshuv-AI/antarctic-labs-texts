Momentum space entanglement of interacting fermions Preprint: APS/123-QED
Michael O. Flynn, Long-Hin Tang, Anushya Chandran, Chris R. Laumann

Abstract
Momentum space entanglement entropy probes quantum correlations in interacting fermionic phases. It is very sensitive to interactions, obeying volume-law scaling in general, while vanishing in the Fermi gas. We show that the Rényi entropy in momentum space has a systematic expansion in terms of the phase space volume of the partition, which holds at all orders in perturbation theory. This permits, for example, the controlled computation of the entropy of thin shells near the Fermi wavevector in isotropic Fermi liquids and BCS superconductors. In the Fermi liquid, the thin shell entropy is a universal function of the quasiparticle residue. In the superconductor, it reflects the formation of Cooper pairs. Momentum space Rényi entropies are accessible in cold atomic and molecular gas experiments through a time-of-flight generalization of previously implemented measurement protocols.

Fermi Liquids—

Consider the following model of an isotropic Fermi liquid:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=H_{0}+H_{1}

(4)

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k},\sigma}\underbrace{(k^{2}/2m-\epsilon_{F})}_{\xi_{\bm{k}}}f^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\sigma}f_{\bm{k}\sigma}

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{U}{V}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{p}_{1}=\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{p}_{2}}f^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}_{2}\uparrow}f^{\dagger}_{\bm{p}_{2}\downarrow}f_{\bm{p}_{1}\downarrow}f_{\bm{k}_{1}\uparrow}

where
f_{\bm{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}\left(f_{\bm{k}\sigma}\right)
are fermion creation (annihilation) operators with momentum
\bm{k}
and spin
\sigma
,
U
is the interaction strength, and
\epsilon_{F}
is the Fermi energy. Throughout this manuscript, we take
\ket{\psi}
to be the ground state.

Let us warm up by considering
A=\{\bm{k}\sigma\}
a single spin polarized mode. In this case, number conservation dictates that
\rho_{A}
is diagonal in the Fock basis with entries
\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle
and
1-\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle
. The single mode Rényi follows immediately,

\displaystyle S_{n}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\ln[\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle^{n}+(1-\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle)^{n}\Big]

(5)

If the mode lies near the Fermi surface, the occupation
\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle\approx(1\pm z_{k_{F}})/2
where we take
+/-
for
\bm{k}
inside/outside the Fermi surface.
Accordingly, the single mode entropy near the Fermi surface is an elementary function of the quasiparticle residue,
z_{k_{F}}
.

In general, going beyond a single mode is analytically challenging in an interacting state.
As an approximate approach, we start by neglecting all multimode connected correlations.
This amounts to making a Gaussian approximation to the state
\rho_{A}
.
For such states, it is well known that the Rényi entropies follow from the one-body density matrix

\displaystyle G_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime};\bm{k}\sigma}=\langle f^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}f_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle

(6)

restricted to the modes in
A

peschel_calculation_2003
,

\displaystyle S^{G}_{n}(A)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\tr\ln[G|_{A}^{n}+(\mathbb{1}-G|_{A})^{n}\Big]

(7)

For the Fermi liquid, momentum and spin conservation dictate that
G|_{A}
is already diagonal for
\bm{k}\sigma
-space cuts with eigenvalues given by the occupations
\langle n_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle
.
With reference to Eq. (
5
), we find that the Gaussian approximation predicts that the Rényi entropy is simply the sum of the (exact) single mode entropies,

\displaystyle S^{G}_{n}(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma\in A}S_{n}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})

(8)

For general partitions
A
, this approximation is uncontrolled.
For example, if
A
is the entire system, the true entropies vanish while Eq. (
8
) predicts an extensive positive value.
On the other hand,
S^{G}_{n}
is clearly exact for
A
consisting of a single spin-polarized mode.
More generally, short-range interactions in real-space lead to long-range interactions in
\bm{k}\sigma
-space with an interaction strength between modes that scales inversely with the volume
V
, as in Eq. (
15
).
Perturbatively, the associated connected correlations vanish for finite collections of modes; we thus expect that the Gaussian approximation is good for sufficiently small cuts
A
in
\bm{k}\sigma
-space.

More precisely, in the appendix we show

\displaystyle S_{n}(A_{\delta k})-\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma\in A_{\delta k}}S_{n}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})\sim\mathcal{O}((\delta k/k_{F})^{2})

(9)

holds to all orders in perturbation theory in the coupling
U
for thin shells
A_{\delta k}
.
Formally, we obtain this result by relating the various Rényi entropies in Eq. (
9
) to the free energy
F^{(n)}(W)
of systems of interacting Grassmann fermions on pants-like manifolds in
\bm{k}\sigma
-space and imaginary time with varying waist regions
W
(see Fig.
2
).
Comparison of the diagrammatic expansion for
F^{(n)}
on each of those manifolds allows us to show that the terms which contribute to Eq. (
9
) are indeed controlled by
\delta k
at all orders.

Putting Eqs. (
5
), (
8
) and (
9
) together for a cut
A_{\delta k}
near the Fermi surface recovers the universal result quoted in the introduction, Eq. (
2
).

Superconductors—

As the Cooper pairs in a s-wave superconductor are composed of fermions with opposite spin and momentum, it is natural to focus on spin-polarized partitions
A^{\uparrow}
of momentum space.

In this case, spin symmetry dictates that Eqs. (
6
), (
7
), (
8
) still provide the Gaussian approximation to the Renyi entropy.
In particular,
G|_{A^{\uparrow}}
is diagonal and the anomalous correlator
\langle f_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}f_{\bm{k}\sigma}\rangle
vanishes when restricted to
A^{\uparrow}
.
Note that the Gaussian approximation with non-vanishing anomalous correlators in
A
is different from that given in Eq. (
7
) (see, e.g.
peschel_calculation_2003
).

Furthermore, as the discussion around Eq. (
9
) suggests, the relationship between the single mode entropies and that of thin shells holds quite generally. In particular, the argument in the appendix readily extends to the s-wave superconductor after taking into consideration spontaneous symmetry breaking.

In sum, the spin-polarized thin shell Rényi entropies can be computed using Eqs. (
7
)-(
9
) in the s-wave superconductor.

Of course, in order to actually compute
S^{G}_{n}(A^{\uparrow})
, one needs to know the occupation numbers of the
\{\bm{k}\uparrow\}
modes in the shell. BCS theory provides a self-consistent mean field approach to computing these occupations,

\displaystyle\langle f^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}\uparrow}f_{\bm{k}\uparrow}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1-\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}}}\bigg)

(10)

where
|\Delta|
is the gap. Straightforward algebra produces,

\displaystyle S_{n}(\bm{k}\uparrow)

\displaystyle=\ln 2+\frac{1}{1-n}\ln\sum_{r=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{2r}\bigg(\frac{\xi^{2}_{\bm{k}}}{\xi^{2}_{\bm{k}}+|\Delta|^{2}}\bigg)^{r}

(11)

The scaling of
{s_{n}(A^{\uparrow}_{\delta k})}
with
{|\Delta|}
depends on its relative size with the energy scale of the thin shell. In the small gap limit
{|\Delta|\ll v_{F}\delta k}
, we can expand (
11
) in powers of
{|\Delta/\xi_{\bm{k}}|}
or
{|\xi_{\bm{k}}/\Delta|}
to obtain

S_{n}(\bm{k}\uparrow)\approx\begin{cases}C(n)|\Delta\xi_{\bm{k}}^{-1}|^{2},\quad|\xi_{\bm{k}}|>|\Delta|\\
\ln 2-n|\Delta\xi_{\bm{k}}^{-1}|^{-2}/2,\quad|\xi_{\bm{k}}|<|\Delta|\end{cases}

(12)

where
{C(n)=\frac{2^{1-n}}{n-1}\sum_{r=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{2r}r}
.
Summing up all contributions leads to the result in Eq. (
3
), which confirms the following simple intuition.
When
{|\Delta|\gg v_{F}\delta k}
, all modes within the thin shell are strongly hybridized, resulting in the saturation of
{s_{n}(A^{\uparrow}_{\delta k})}
to the maximal value
{\ln 2}
.
On the other hand, when
{|\Delta|\ll v_{F}\delta k}
,
{s_{n}(A^{\uparrow}_{\delta k})}
scales linearly in
{|\Delta|/v_{F}\delta k}
, since only modes within a region
{|\Delta|}
around the Fermi surface are strongly hybridized.

Free Dirac Transitions—

Within BCS theory, the superconducting gap
\Delta
exhibits an essential singularity at
Ug(\epsilon_{F})=0
.
The thin-shell momentum space entropy shown in Fig.
1
inherits this singularity.
It is natural to conjecture that this is connected to the presence of a spectral gap for
U<0
, and that in analogy with real-space entanglement, momentum space entropy can exhibit non-analyticities in response to gap-inducing perturbations.
We test this hypothesis by computing the second Rényi entropy of spin polarized momentum balls around a Dirac point in
D
spatial dimensions.
On tuning the mass
m
, we find a generic non-analyticity

s_{2}(A^{\uparrow})\sim|m|^{D}\ln{|m|}

(13)

in the free theory (see appendix). We leave the extension to the interacting critical theory to future work.

Measurement Protocol—

A series of experiments with ultra-cold bosons have demonstrated that real space Rényi entropy can be measured by preparing copies of a quantum state and interfering them appropriately
islam_measuring_2015
;
kaufman_quantum_2016
. Here we briefly review this protocol, which has been extended theoretically to fermionic systems as well
cornfeld_measuring_2019
;
pichler_thermal_2013
, and generalize it to momentum space.

Begin with two identical copies of a quantum state in a pair of optical lattices; typically these are prepared by independent but identical time evolution in each copy. A beam splitter then interferes the two copies by freezing each of their dynamics and allowing for tunneling between them using an optical superlattice. This operation maps fermions in the first
(a^{\dagger})
and second
(b^{\dagger})
copies as:

a_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}\rightarrow\frac{a_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}+b_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}};\hskip 12.0ptb_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}\rightarrow\frac{b_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}-a_{i,\sigma}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}}

(14)

Microscopy techniques are then used to measure site and spin-resolved particle densities, from which the second Rényi entropy of an arbitrary real space partition is calculated
2
2

2

See
pichler_thermal_2013
for additional comments regarding ordering ambiguities and
cornfeld_measuring_2019
for the generalization to higher Rényi entropies.
.

Prior to measuring particle densities in real space, this protocol does not privilege any particular single-particle basis; it is the measurement basis which determines the partitions that can be accessed. Replacing real-space microscopy with a time-of-flight (TOF) single-atom-resolved measurement
Fang:2016
;
Hodgman:2017
;
Cayla:2018
;
Carcy:2019
;
Preiss:2019
enables the computation of momentum space Rényi entropies in near-term experiments. In TOF, the atoms are released from the optical lattice and absorption imaging is used to reconstruct the initial momenta
bloch_many-body_2008
.

Discussion—

The momentum space entanglement
S_{n}(A)
in the ground state of interacting fermionic systems permits a systematic expansion in the phase space volume of
A
.
The leading contribution for small
A
is given by the sum of the single mode entropies – this provides an explicit relationship between the Rényi entropy and certain low energy properties of the phases for thin shells near the Fermi wavevector.

In the Fermi liquid, the leading term in the shell width
\delta k
only depends on the quasiparticle residue,
z_{k_{F}}
.
An interesting avenue for future work would be to compute the
O(\delta k^{2})
contribution, where we expect the Landau parameters to play a role as they reflect the correlations between
k
-modes.

Similarly, the one dimensional interacting Fermi system realizes a Luttinger liquid where
z_{k_{F}}=0
.
Despite the more dramatic reorganization of the ground state from the non-interacting Fermi sea, we nonetheless expect
s_{2}(A_{\delta k})=2\ln 2+O(\delta k)
.
It would be interesting to check this in a direct multi-mode calculation, building on Ref.
Ibanez-Berganza:2016wi
.

Although we have presented the results in the context of short-ranged interactions, the diagrammatic proof for the phase space expansion is much more general.
In particular, we believe it can be generalized to Coulomb interacting systems and to unconventional superconductors.

Appendix A
Diagrammatic Treatment of Thin Shell Rényi Entropy in Interacting Fermi Systems

For specificity, we consider an isotropic, weakly interacting Fermi system with short-range interactions,

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k},\sigma}\xi_{\bm{k}}f_{\bm{k}\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{\bm{k}\sigma}+\frac{U}{V}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1},\bm{p}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{p}_{2}}\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{p}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{p}_{2}}f_{\bm{k}_{2}\uparrow}^{\dagger}f_{\bm{p}_{2}\downarrow}^{\dagger}f_{\bm{p}_{1}\downarrow}f_{\bm{k}_{1}\uparrow}

(15)

We will briefly discuss generalizations at the end. Our goal is to show that for a thin shell
A_{\delta k}
of thickness
\delta k
near the Fermi surface at
k_{F}
,

\displaystyle S_{n}(A_{\delta k})-\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma\in A_{\delta k}}S_{n}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})\sim O((\delta k/k_{F})^{2})

(16)

That is, the full Rényi entropy in the thin shell is the sum of the single mode Rényi entropies to leading order in
\delta k
.
In order to do this, we will express the relevant Rényi entropies in terms of the free energies of interacting Grassmann fields on several different imaginary time geometries.
We will then prove Eq. (
16
) by showing that the diagrams that contribute to the right hand side are suppressed by phase space factors at all orders in the interaction.

Although we are ultimately interested in entanglement in the ground state of
H
, it is convenient to work at finite temperature,
\rho=e^{-\beta H}
, and take
\beta\to\infty
at the end. The Rényi entropy of a subset
A
of momentum modes of the thermal state is given by

\displaystyle(1-n)S_{n}(A)

\displaystyle=\ln\tr\rho_{A}^{n}-\ln\left(\tr\rho\right)^{n}

(17)

\displaystyle\equiv-\beta(F^{(n)}(A)-F^{(n)}(\emptyset))

(18)

where
\rho_{A}=\tr_{\bar{A}}\rho
is the (unnormalized) reduced density matrix in
A
, and the second term accounts for the normalization of
\rho
.

Each of the two terms defined in Eq. (
18
) can be understood as the free energy
F^{(n)}(W)
of a system of Grassmann fields on an appropriate “pants-like” imaginary time manifold with waist
W
in
\bm{k}\sigma
-space (see Fig.
2
).
Each of the
n
legs has circumference
\beta
while the waist region has circumference
n\beta
.

Let us warm up by recalling the imaginary time representation of the usual free energy
F
of
H
at temperature
\beta^{-1}
:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=e^{-\beta F}=\tr e^{-\beta H}=\int D\bar{\psi}D\psi e^{-S}

(19)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma}\bar{\psi}_{\bm{k}\sigma}\left(\partial_{\tau}+\xi_{\bm{k}}\right)\psi_{\bm{k}\sigma}+\underbrace{\frac{U}{V}\sum_{\bm{k}_{1},\bm{p}_{1},\bm{k}_{2},\bm{p}_{2}}\delta_{\bm{k}_{1}+\bm{p}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{p}_{2}}\bar{\psi}_{\bm{k}_{2}\uparrow}\bar{\psi}_{\bm{p}_{2}\downarrow}\psi_{\bm{p}_{1}\downarrow}\psi_{\bm{k}_{1}\uparrow}}_{\mathcal{V}}

(20)

The Grassmann fields satisfy anti-periodic boundary conditions on a circle of imaginary time of extent
\beta
for all modes
\bm{k}\sigma
:

\displaystyle\psi(0)

\displaystyle=-\psi(\beta)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(0)=-\bar{\psi}(\beta)

(21)

This leads to the non-interacting imaginary-time ordered propagators

\displaystyle G(\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\tau_{2};\bm{k}\sigma\tau_{1})

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\tau}\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}(\tau_{2})\bar{\psi}_{\bm{k}\sigma}(\tau_{1})\rangle=\delta_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}G_{\bm{k}\sigma}(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})

\displaystyle G_{\bm{k}\sigma}(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})

\displaystyle=e^{-\xi_{\bm{k}}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}(\theta(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})-f(\beta\xi_{\bm{k}}))

(22)

where
f(x)=1/(e^{x}+1)
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution and
\theta
is the step function.

The diagrammatic expansion of
F
is given by a sum of connected diagrams according to the linked cluster theorem,

\displaystyle-\beta F

\displaystyle=-\beta F_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}\left\langle T_{\tau}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{l}\mathcal{V}(\tau_{1})\cdots\mathcal{V}(\tau_{l})\right\rangle_{0}^{c}

(23)

We represent this expansion graphically in terms of diagrams in
\bm{k}\sigma
-
\tau
space built out of vertical propagator lines (at fixed
\bm{k}\sigma
) and horizontal dashed interaction vertices (at fixed
\tau
, see Fig.
3
).
A diagram corresponds to a particular Wick contraction of the underlying Grassmann fields and it is connected so long as each vertex
\mathcal{V}(\tau_{i})
can be connected to any other by following the propagator lines.
Momentum conservation implies that each connected diagram makes an extensive contribution to
F
after integration over the loop momenta.
Thus,
F\propto V
as expected.

\displaystyle G^{W}_{\bm{k}\sigma}(\tau_{2},\tau_{1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{-\xi_{\bm{k}}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}(\theta(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})-f(n\beta\xi_{\bm{k}}))&\quad\bm{k}\sigma\in W\\
e^{-\xi_{\bm{k}}(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})}(\theta(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})-f(\beta\xi_{\bm{k}}))\mathbb{I}\left[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\textrm{ in same leg}\right]&\quad\bm{k}\sigma\in\bar{W}\end{array}\right.

Here,
\mathbb{I}
is an indicator function taking values
0
or

. The dependence on the absolute times rather than just their difference reflects the breaking of time translation invariance by the stitching of the legs.

The free energy

again can be computed from the linked cluster theorem,

\displaystyle-\beta F^{(n)}(W)

\displaystyle=-\beta F^{(n)}_{0}(W)+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}\left\langle T_{\tau}\int_{0}^{n\beta}d\tau_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{n\beta}d\tau_{l}\mathcal{V}(\tau_{1})\cdots\mathcal{V}(\tau_{l})\right\rangle_{0,W}^{c}

(26)

The construction of connected diagrams is identical across manifolds with different waists

– their values differ only through the difference in the propagator.
For example, Figs.

VU^{2}\int_{W\cup\bar{W}}\prod_{i=1}^{4}\frac{d^{3}k_{i}}{(2\pi)^{3}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{k}_{1}-\bm{k}_{2}+\bm{k}_{3}-\bm{k}_{4})\int_{0}^{n\beta}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\,\times\\

(27)

Thus, in this representation, we can compare the free energies on different manifolds diagram by diagram at any order simply be comparing which propagators lie in the relevant waists.

Now let us return to our conjecture Eq. (
16
).
In terms of free energies, this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\beta(F^{(n)}(A_{\delta k})-F^{(n)}(\emptyset))-\beta\sum_{\bm{k}\sigma\in A_{\delta k}}(F^{(n)}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})-F^{(n)}(\emptyset))\sim O((\delta k/k_{F})^{2})

(28)

It is straightforward to check that the non-interacting contributions
-\beta F^{(n)}_{0}
to the free energy are identical across all geometries as
\beta\to\infty
and thus cancel in Eq. (
28
).
Let us organize the interaction contributions according to the number
m
of distinct
\bm{k}\sigma
in
A_{\delta k}
which some propagator inside the associated connected diagram carries.

If
m=0
, all propagators in the diagram are leg propagators independent of the underlying geometry (see Fig.
3
b). Such diagrams clearly cancel within each bracket in the LHS of Eq. (
28
).

For
m=1
, exactly one mode in
A_{\delta k}
, say
\bm{q}\sigma
, is active in the diagram (eg. Fig.
3

F^{(n)}(A_{\delta k})
and in
F^{(n)}(\emptyset)
and thus contributes to
S_{n}(A_{\delta k})
. However, the same contribution appears in
F^{(n)}(\{\bm{q}\sigma\})-F^{(n)}(\emptyset)
and thus cancels in Eq. (
28

When integrated over
\bm{q}\sigma\in A_{\delta k}
, these
m=1

\delta k

S_{n}(A_{\delta k})\sim V(k_{F}^{2}\delta k)
.

Diagrams with
m\geq 2
contribute to
S_{n}(A_{\delta k})
as well as several
S_{n}(\{\bm{k}\sigma\})
(see Fig.
3

\{\bm{k}\sigma\}
in the diagram, the requirement that
m

Let us close with a few comments on the generality of the above formal argument.
First, it is clear that the argument made very little use of the actual structure of the interaction and should readily go through for other sufficiently short-range momentum-conserving interactions.
Second, the choice of the non-interacting part of
H
must respect the choice of cut
A
– more precisely, the non-interacting propagator
G_{0}
must not connect
\bm{k}\sigma\in A
to
\bm{k}\sigma^{\prime}\in\bar{A}
in order for Eq. (
A
) to hold.
The result Eq. (
16
) nonetheless holds for spin/orbital-polarized cuts in a system with quadratic off-diagonal terms (eg.
A^{\uparrow}
for a system in a transverse Zeeman field or for a mean-field superconductor).
One simply treats the off-diagonal terms as part of
\mathcal{V}
and introduces corresponding vertices with degree 2 in the diagrams.
While this is not usually the most efficient approach to computing Renyi’s (or other physical properties) in such a system, it serves for the formal argument.
Finally, so long as there is a perturbative resummation scheme to obtain spontaneous symmetry breaking states (as in the BCS superconductor), we expect the result Eq. (
16
) to hold.
In this case, one should consider the diagrammatic calculation in the presence of a weak applied symmetry breaking field in order to break the symmetry explicitly. The weak field is taken to zero after the thermodynamic limit.
This serves to select the particular symmetry breaking direction for the relevant self-energies.

Appendix B
Rényi entropies of Dirac Hamiltonians

In the main text, we analyzed the momentum space entropy of the fermion model (
15
) for both attractive and repulsive couplings. By analogy with real-space entanglement, one might anticipate that momentum space entropy can exhibit a non-analytic response to a gap-inducing perturbation. In the case of the Fermi liquid to superconductor transition, this postulate is somewhat obscured by the fact that the Fermi liquid is gapless over an entire surface in momentum space. It is conceptually simpler and analytically better controlled to test this postulate in the context of fermionic models which are gapless at isolated points in momentum space.

Here we consider a simple model of fermions which is gapless at a Dirac point, and study how suitable entanglement properties of the model are modified by gap-inducing perturbations. These calculations confirm that the second Rényi entropy of orbital-momentum cuts which enclose the Dirac point are non-analytic functions of a mass gap
m
.

The Dirac Hamiltonian in
D=2
spatial dimensions reads

\displaystyle h(\bm{k})=k_{x}\sigma_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y}+m\sigma_{z}

\displaystyle=\left(k_{x},k_{y},m\right)\cdot\bm{\sigma}

(29)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}\left(\hat{n}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\right)

where
\sigma_{i}
are the Pauli matrices and
(k_{x},k_{y})
labels momentum modes around the Dirac point at the origin. In the final equality, we introduced a unit vector
\hat{n}\equiv\left(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta\right)\propto\left(k_{x},k_{y},m\right)
. The eigenstates of
h(\bm{k})
can be written as

|\epsilon_{+}(\bm{k})\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\left(\theta/2\right)\\
\sin\left(\theta/2\right)e^{i\phi}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 12.0pt|\epsilon_{-}(\bm{k})\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\left(\theta/2\right)\\
-\cos\left(\theta/2\right)e^{i\phi}\end{pmatrix}

(30)

with associated eigenvalues
\epsilon_{\pm}(\bm{k})=\pm\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}}
. In the ground state, negative energy states are populated and the ground state density matrix factorizes over momenta:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{\bm{k}}\rho_{\bm{k}},

(31)

\displaystyle\rho_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\equiv\ket{\epsilon_{-}(\bm{k})}\bra{\epsilon_{-}(\bm{k})}

It follows that for any subset of momenta
A
,
S_{n}(A)=0
. Pure momentum-space partitions are therefore insufficient to obtain non-trivial entanglement: we must also trace out an orbital for each
\bm{k}
. This orbital is parameterized by a unit vector
\hat{a}\equiv\left(\sin\alpha\cos\beta,\sin\alpha\sin\beta,\cos\alpha\right)
, and we choose to trace out states that satisfy
\hat{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma}=-1
. The resulting density matrix,
\rho_{\bm{k}}^{\hat{a}+}
, is diagonal with eigenvalues given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\bm{k}}^{\hat{a}+}

\displaystyle=\text{diag}\left\{\lambda(\bm{k}),1-\lambda(\bm{k})\right\}

(32)

\displaystyle\lambda(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\langle\hat{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\rangle}{2}

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\sigma}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{k}}\left(k_{x},k_{y},m\right)

For a collection of momenta
A
, the Rényi entropies of fermions polarized along
\hat{a}
,
S_{n}(A^{\hat{a}+})
, is reduced to sums over independent modes. Specializing to
n=2
,

S_{2}(A^{\hat{a}+})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in A}s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})\to\frac{V}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{\bm{k}\in A}s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})d^{2}k

(33)

where
s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})=-\text{Tr}\left(\rho_{\bm{k}}^{\hat{a}+}\right)^{2}
. A natural choice for the set of momenta
A
is a circle of radius
k_{A}
in momentum space centered on the Dirac point. The gap
m
provides a scale which separates the momenta into regions
A_{m}=\left\{\bm{k}\hskip 6.0pt|0<|\bm{k}|<k_{A}\right\}
and
\overline{A}_{m}
(see Fig.
4
). The contributions of both regions to (
33
) are independent, and the integrand is smooth for
k>m
:

\int_{\bm{k}\in\overline{A}_{m}}s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})d^{2}k\sim f(\alpha)|m|^{2}

(34)

where we have dropped terms independent of
m
and
f
is a function of the polar angle
\alpha
which defines
\hat{a}
. In the region
A_{m}
,
s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})
can be expanded asymptotically:

\int_{\bm{k}\in A_{m}}s_{2}^{\hat{a}+}(\bm{k})d^{2}k\sim C_{0}(\alpha)\left(\frac{|m|}{k_{A}}\right)^{2}+C_{2}(\alpha)\left(\frac{|m|}{k_{A}}\right)^{2}\ln\left[\frac{|m|}{k_{A}}\right]

(35)

Clearly, the contributions (
34
) and (
35
) are non-analytic functions of the mass
m
. The precise form of this non-analyticity depends on the choice of orbital
\hat{a}
, but the leading-order singular contribution is typically of the form
|m|^{2}\ln m
. Similar considerations for models with Dirac points in
D
spatial dimensions also yield a leading order contribution to
s_{2}
of the form
|m|^{D}\ln m
.

Abstract
. Given a compact quantum metric space (A, L), we prove that the domain of L coincides with A if and only if A is finite dimensional. We then show how one can explicitly build many quantum metrics with distinct domains on infinite-dimensional AF algebras. In the last section, we provide a strategy for calculating the distance between certain states in these quantum metrics, which allow us to calculate the distance between pure states in these quantum metrics on the quantized interval and on the Cantor space.

1.
Introduction

Quantum metric spaces were studied by Rieffel
[
12
,
13
]
as the basis for a framework to study various convergence results found, without formal basis, in the mathematical literature. Starting from Connes’ original observation that a spectral triple gives rise to a metric on the state space of the underlying C*-algebra
[
3
]
, and the realization that this metric is a noncommutative version of the Monge-Kantorovich distance, Rieffel defined a quantum compact metric space in a manner which then enables the construction of analogues of the Gromov-Hausdorff distance, within the noncommutative realm
[
14
]
. In addition to the initial quantum Gromov-Hausdorff distance introduced by Rieffel, other analogues have since been introduced, with accompanying variation on the notion of a quantum compact metric space, where the underlying "topological" category varies from order-unit spaces
[
14
]
to C*-algebras
[
7
,
5
]
. There also exist other useful quantum distances in
[
11
,
4
,
15
]
. Later on, thanks to the functional analytic perspective on the Gromov-Hausdorff distance offered by these new constructions, the third author introduced other distances on a similar model between other structures, such as modules over C*-algebras
[
8
]
, dynamics over C*-algebras
[
9
]
, and even spectral triples
[
10
]
.

While convergence of quantum metric spaces has been the focused on the theory of noncommutaitve metric geometry, there is an intrinsic interest in the structure of the noncommutative analogues of the Lipschitz algebras underlying quantum compact metric spaces. For instance in
[
6
]
, the third author proved that a *-morphism between
C^{*}
-algebras which preserves the domain of the quantum metric structure is automatically Lipschitz for the quantum metrics, in a natural way. It thus appears that the noncommutative Lipschitz algebras may contain some important geometric information by themselves. This paper is written in this spirit: we consider the domain of the quantum analogues of Lipschitz algebras as our focus. More specifically, our paper contains two new results on this structural question.

First, in Section
2
, we prove that the domain of the analogue of a Lipschitz seminorm on a unital C*-algebra (see Definition
1.2
) is in fact, the entire C*-algebra, if and only if the C*-algebra is finite dimensional. As a consequence here, we see that when working with infinite-dimensional quantum compact metric spaces, the domain of these seminorms will be dense, but not the entire space, and thus we can expect to find Lipschitz seminorms with different domains. This is the second matter of this paper, where we study examples of quantum metrics with distinct domains in Section
3
, which generalizes a result from
[
1
]
on the Cantor space.

2.
Domain and dimension

The following two results motivates our inquiry into domains of Lip-norms on AF-algebras. Indeed, in Corollary
2.2
, we show that the domain of an
L
-seminorm is equal to the the self-adjoint elements of the C*-algebra if and only if the C*-algebra is finite-dimensional.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Banach space, and let
L
be a seminorm defined on a dense subspace
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}
of
A
such that
K:=\{a\in{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}:\L(a)=0\}
is finite dimensional, and the image of
\{a\in{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}:L(a)\leqslant 1\}
in
A/K
is compact in
A
.

The space
A
is finite dimensional if, and only if,
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}=A
.

Proof.

If
A
is finite dimensional, then the domain
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}
of
L
is also finite dimensional, hence complete, hence closed. Since it is dense in
A
, we conclude that
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}=A
.

Conversely, assume that
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}=A
. Let
B:=A/K
, endowed with the quotient norm form
A
. Let
B_{r}
be the image by the canonical surjection of
\{a\in{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}:L(a)\leqslant r\}
in
B
, for all
r>0
. Since
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}=A
, we observe that
B=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}B_{n}
. By assumption on
L
, the set
B_{n}
is compact, hence closed in
B
for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}
(since
B
is Hausdorff).

Since
B
is complete, by the Baire Category Theorem, there exists
n\in{\mathds{N}}
such that
B_{n}
has nonempty interior. Let
U\subseteq B_{n}
be a nonempty open ball. Since
B_{n}
is compact,
U
is totally bounded. Therefore, the open unit ball of
B
is totally bounded; as
B
is complete, the closed unit ball of
B
is thus compact. Consequently,
B
is finite dimensional. Therefore,
A
is also finite dimensional as claimed, since
A/K=B
and
K
are both finite dimensional.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
(A,L)
is a compact quantum metric space, then
A
is finite dimensional if, and only if,
{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}=sa(A)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that
sa(A)={\operatorname*{dom}\left({L}\right)}
if, and only if,
sa(A)
is finite dimensional, which in turn is equivalent to
A
being finite dimensional.
∎

Therefore, not only do domains of Lip-norms on infinite-dimensional spaces contain the whole space, but this also tells us that it is possible to obtain Lip-norms with different domains. In the next several results, we see an explicit way to do this for infinite-dimensional AF algebras.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A=\overline{\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}A_{n}}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
be a unital AF-algebra equipped with a faithful tracial state
\tau
such that
A_{0}={\mathds{C}}1_{A}
. For each
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, let

E_{n}:A\rightarrow A_{n}

denote the unique
\tau
-preserving conditional expectation onto
A_{n}
(that is,
\tau\circ E_{n}=\tau
for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}
).

If
a\in A
, then
(\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
converges to
0.

Proof.

Let
a\in A
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
N\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
a_{N}\in A_{N}
such that
\|a-a_{N}\|_{A}<\varepsilon
. Let
n\geqslant N
, then

\displaystyle\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|a-a_{N}\|_{A}+\|a_{N}-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/2+\|E_{n}(a_{N})-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon/2+\|E_{n}(a_{N}-a)\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+\|a_{N}-a\|_{A}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon.\qed

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A=\overline{\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}A_{n}}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
be a unital AF-algebra equipped with a faithful tracial state
\tau
such that
A_{0}={\mathds{C}}1_{A}
. For each
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, let

E_{n}:A\rightarrow A_{n}

denote the unique
\tau
-preserving conditional expectation onto
A_{n}
(that is,
\tau\circ E_{n}=\tau
for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}
).

If
a\in A\setminus\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}A_{n}
, then if we set
\beta_{a}(n)=(\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
and
\beta^{\infty}_{a}(n)=(\|a-E_{n}(a)\|^{2}_{A})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}
, it holds that
(\beta_{a}(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
and
(\beta^{\infty}_{a}(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
are sequences in
(0,\infty)
that converge to
0
and using notation from Theorem
1.4

L_{\beta_{a}}(a)=1

and

L_{\beta^{\infty}_{a}}(a)=\infty,

and in particular,
a\in{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L_{\beta_{a}}}\right)}
but
a\not\in{\operatorname*{dom}\left({L_{\beta^{\infty}_{a}}}\right)}.
Moreover,
L_{\beta_{a}}
and
L_{\beta^{\infty}_{a}}
are not equivalent.

Proof.

Since
a\not\in\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}A_{n}
, we have for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}
that
a\neq E_{n}(a)
and so
\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}>0
. Hence
(\beta_{a}(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
is a sequence in
(0,\infty)
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have that
(\beta^{\infty}_{a}(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
and
(\beta^{\infty}_{a}(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}
converge to
0
. Moreover,

L_{\beta_{a}}(a)=\sup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}\frac{\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}}{\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}}=1

and

L_{\beta^{\infty}_{a}}(a)=\sup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}\frac{\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}}{\|a-E_{n}(a)\|^{2}_{A}}=\sup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}\frac{1}{\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}}=\infty

as desired.
∎

By Corollary
2.2
, the previous result holds only holds for any infinite-dimensional AF-algebra equipped with faithful a tracial state. In particular, this holds for

, where

\overline{{\mathds{N}}}=\left\{\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\in{\mathds{R}}:n\in{\mathds{N}}\right\}\cup\{0\}

is a quantized interval and the one-point compactification of

.
Note that

,

(2.1)

{\mathcal{C}}_{n}=\{f\in C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}}):\forall x\leqslant 1/2^{n-1},f(1/2^{n-1})=f(x)\}.

If we consider the polynomial
p_{1}
defined for all
x\in\overline{{\mathds{N}}}
by
p_{1}(x)=x
, then
p_{1}\in C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})\setminus\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{n}
and we have by Theorem
2.4
that

L_{\beta^{\infty}_{p_{1}}}(p_{1})=\infty

but

L_{\beta_{p_{1}}}(p_{1})=1

We also note that

L_{d_{1}}(p_{1})=1

where

L_{d_{1}}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in\overline{{\mathds{N}}}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}

3.
Explicit calculations of some quantum metrics

We continue our study of quantum metrics of Theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A=\overline{\cup_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}A_{n}}
be a unital AF algebra equipped with faithful tracial state
\tau
with
A_{0}={\mathds{C}}1_{A}
. Let
(\beta(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be a sequence in
(0,\infty)
that converges to
0
.

Assume there exists a sequence
({\varphi}_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
such that for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}

{\varphi}_{n}\in sa(A)

and

L_{\beta}({\varphi}_{n})=\frac{1}{\beta(n)}.

If
\mu,\nu\in S(A)
such that
\mu(a)=\nu(a)
for all
a\in A_{n}
, then

\beta(n)|\mu({\varphi}_{n})-\nu({\varphi}_{n})|\leqslant mk_{L_{\beta}}(\mu,\nu)\leqslant 2\beta(n).

In particular, if
\mu({\varphi}_{n})=-\nu({\varphi}_{n})
, then

mk_{L_{\beta}}(\mu,\nu)=2\beta(n).

Proof.

Since
L_{\beta}(\beta(n){\varphi}_{n})=1
,

\displaystyle mk_{L_{\beta}}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|:a\in sa(A),L_{\beta}(a)\leqslant 1\}

\displaystyle\geqslant|\mu(\beta(n){\varphi}_{n})-\nu(\beta(n){\varphi}_{n})|

\displaystyle=|\beta(n)\mu({\varphi}_{n})-\beta(n)\nu({\varphi}_{n})|

\displaystyle=\beta(n)|\mu({\varphi}_{n})-\nu({\varphi}_{n})|

Next, let
a\in A
such that
L_{\beta}(a)\leqslant 1
. Then
\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}\leqslant\beta(n)
. Since
E_{n}(a)\in A_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mu(a)-\nu(a)|

\displaystyle=|\mu(a)-\mu\circ E_{n}(a)+\nu\circ E_{n}(a)-\nu(a)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\mu(a-E_{n}(a))|+|\nu(a-E_{n}(a))|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}+\|a-E_{n}(a)\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta(n)+\beta(n)

\displaystyle=2\beta(n)

Moreover, if
\mu({\varphi}_{n})=-\nu({\varphi})
, then

\beta(n)|\mu({\varphi}_{n})-\nu({\varphi}_{n})|=|(\mu-\nu)(\beta(n){\varphi}_{n})|=2\beta(n),

which implies

mk_{L_{\beta}}(\mu,\nu)=2\beta(n).\qed

\chi_{n}\in\mathrm{span}\{{\varphi}_{0},{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,{\varphi}_{n-1}\}
. Thus,

{\mathcal{C}}_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{{\varphi}_{0},{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,{\varphi}_{n-1}\}.\qed

As mentioned above, the purpose of
({\varphi}_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
is to serve as an orthogonal basis. Here we find the correct
(v_{n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
of Theorem
3.2
to accomplish this task.

Proposition 3.5
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{n})=0
for all
n\in{\mathds{N}}
.

(2)

v=(2^{-n})_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2)
Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. By Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle v_{n}

\displaystyle=\tau_{v}(\chi_{n})

\displaystyle=\tau_{v}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}({\varphi}_{0}-2^{n+1}{\varphi}_{n})+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}{\varphi}_{n-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}(\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{0})-2^{n+1}\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{n}))+\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{n-k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{0})+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{0})\quad\text{ by assumption}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

(2)
\implies
(1) Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. By Expression (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\tau_{v}({\varphi}_{n})

\displaystyle=\tau_{v}\left(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau_{v}(\mathds{1})-\tau_{v}(\chi_{n})-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\tau_{v}(\chi_{j})

Lemma 3.6
.

For each
n,m\in{\mathds{N}}
, it holds that

{\varphi}_{n}{\varphi}_{m}=\begin{cases}{\varphi}_{\max\{m,n\}}&:m\neq n\\
\chi_{n_{c}}&:m=n\end{cases}

Proof.

First, assume
m\neq n
, and without loss of generality
m<n
. If
m=0
, then
{\varphi}_{n}{\varphi}_{0}={\varphi}_{n}
.

Next, if
m>0
, then by Expression (
3.1
),

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{n}{\varphi}_{m}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}\bigg)\bigg(\mathds{1}-\chi_{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\chi_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}-\chi_{m}-\sum_{j=0}^{m}\chi_{j}-\chi_{n}+0+0-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}+\chi_{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}\chi_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}

\displaystyle={\varphi}_{n}.

Second, if
m=n
, we have

\displaystyle{\varphi}_{n}{\varphi}_{n}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}\bigg)\bigg(\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}-\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}-\chi_{n}+\chi_{n}+\chi_{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}+\chi_{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\chi_{j}+\chi_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\chi_{j}

\displaystyle=\chi_{n_{c}}.\qed

Now we see how our choice of faithful state and orthogonal basis come together.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\beta(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be non-increasing sequence of positive real numbers that converges to
0
. For all
n\in{\mathds{N}}
, it holds that

L_{\beta}({\varphi}_{n})=\frac{1}{\beta(n)},

where we use
\tau_{v}
as our faithful tracial state in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. Note that
{\varphi}_{n}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
for all
m\geqslant n+1.
Hence
E_{m}({\varphi}_{n})={\varphi}_{n}
for all
m\geqslant n+1
since
{\varphi}_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{m}
. Therefore,

\frac{\|{\varphi}_{n}-E_{m}({\varphi}_{n})\|_{C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})}}{\beta(m)}=\frac{\|{\varphi}_{n}-{\varphi}_{n}\|_{C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})}}{\beta(m)}=0

for all
m\geqslant n+1.

Next, if
m\leqslant n
, then
E_{m}({\varphi}_{n})=0
since
E_{m}
is the orthogonal projection onto

{\mathcal{C}}_{m}=\mathrm{span}\{{\varphi}_{0},{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,{\varphi}_{m-1}\}

(see Proposition
3.4
),
and
{\varphi}_{n}
is orthogonal to all
{\varphi}_{0},{\varphi}_{1},\ldots,{\varphi}_{m-1}
with respect to
\tau_{v}
by Theorem
3.7
. Therefore,

\frac{\|{\varphi}_{n}-E_{m}({\varphi}_{n})\|_{C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})}}{\beta(m)}=\frac{\|{\varphi}_{n}-0\|_{C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})}}{\beta(m)}=\frac{\|{\varphi}_{n}\|_{C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})}}{\beta(m)}=\frac{1}{\beta(m)}

for all
m\leqslant n.

Hence

\displaystyle L_{\beta}({\varphi}_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{m\in{\mathds{N}},m\leqslant n}\frac{1}{\beta(m)}=\frac{1}{\beta(n)}.\qed

We are now able to calculate exactly the distance between any two pure states on
C(\overline{{\mathds{N}}})
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(\beta(n))_{n\in{\mathds{N}}}
be non-increasing sequence of positive real numbers that converges to
0
. Using
\tau_{v}
as our faithful tracial state in Theorem
1.4
,
it holds for every
x,y\in\overline{{\mathds{N}}}
that

mk_{L_{\beta}}(\delta_{x},\delta_{y})=2\beta(\min\{1-\log_{2}(y),1-\log_{2}(x)\})

with the convention that
\log_{2}(0)=-\infty
,
where
\delta_{x},\delta_{y}
are evaluation at
x,y\in\overline{{\mathds{N}}}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\overline{{\mathds{N}}}
and
x<y
. Let
n\in{\mathds{N}}
such that
y=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}
. Note that

L_{\beta}(\beta(n){\varphi}_{n})=1

by Lemma
3.8
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{n}
. Then

\delta_{x}(f)=f(x)=f(1/2^{n-1})=f(y)=\delta_{y}(f).

Moreover,

\delta_{y}({\varphi}_{n})={\varphi}_{n}(y)=-1=-{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\delta_{x}({\varphi}_{n}).

Thus,

mk_{L_{\beta}}(\delta_{x},\delta_{y})=2\beta(n).

Since
\log_{2}(y)=1-n
and
\log_{2}(x)<\log_{2}(y)
, the proof is complete by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

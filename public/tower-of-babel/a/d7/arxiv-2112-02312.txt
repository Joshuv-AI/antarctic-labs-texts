Indirect Measurement for Optimal Quantum Communication Enhanced by Binary Non-standard Coherent States Preprint: APS/123-QED
Min Namkung, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
It is well known that the Helstrom bound can be improved by generalizing the form of a coherent state. Thus, designing a quantum measurement achieving the improved Helstrom bound is important for novel quantum communication. In the present article, we analytically show that the improved Helstrom bound can be achieved by a projective measurement composed of orthogonal non-standard Schrödinger cat states. Moreover, we numerically show that the improved Helstrom bound can be nearly achieved by an indirect measurement based on the Jaynes-Cummings model. As the Jaynes-Cummings model describes an interaction between a light and a two-level atom, we emphasize that the indirect measurement considered in this article has potential to be experimentally implemented.



I
Introduction

Over several decades, optical communication has been widely used for transmitting and receiving important data among distinct parties. The key feature of the optical communication is to discriminate a message encoded in a light with maximal success probability. Unfortunately, the statistical and physical feature of a light is limited, therefore maximal success probability with conventional measurements could not surpass the shot noise limit (equivalently, the standard quantum limit)
j.proakis
. For this reason, large amount of research effort has been contributed to design an unconventional quantum measurement surpassing the shot noise limit in quantum communication
i.a.burenkov0
.

It is well known that, if one bit message is encoded in one of binary standard coherent states (S-CS), a quantum measurement achieving the Helstrom bound
c.w.helstrom
is described by a projective measurement composed of orthogonal Schrödinger cat states
c.w.helstrom2
;
m.sasaki

r.s.kennedy
;
m.takeoka

In 1973, Dolinar proposed an unconventional measurement with electric feedback, which can achieve the Helstrom bound between binary coherent states
s.j.dolinar

N

;

N

k.nakahira

Recently, some indirect measurements for novel quantum communication have been proposed

It should be noted that, not only the methodology for designing an unconventional measurement, the research for finding a suitable optical light also has been performed

g.cariolaro

In the present article, we propose an optimal measurement for the quantum communication between binary NS-CS in a mathematical manner. We analytically show that the projective measurement can achieve the Helstrom bound and illustrate our result by a projective measurement composed of orthogonal non-standard Schrödinger cat states. II
Preliminaries: Enhanced Binary Quantum Communication

In principle, quantum communication between a sender and an observer can be described as the problem of quantum state discriminaton
g.cariolaro
. The main purpose of the quantum state discrimination is to establish the optimal strategy for discriminating several quantum states with maximal success probability. So far, several quantum state discrimination strategies have been proposed such as minimum error discrimination
j.bae
;
d.ha
;
d.ha2
, unambiguous discrimination
i.d.ivanovic
;
d.dieks
;
a.peres
;
g.jaeger
and fixed rate of inconclusive result
j.fiurasek
;
u.herzog
, as well as suitable measurements for possible implementation
k.banaszek
;
f.e.becerra2
;
k.nakahira2
. However, unambiguous discrimination can be realized only in the ideal case
m.namkung3
, and fixed rate of inconclusive result is not theoretically well developed. For these reasons, the experimental implementation of the quantum communication have been mainly focused on the minimum error discrimination.

Throughout this article, we consider the situation that a sender encodes one bit message
x\in\{0,1\}
into a pure state
|\psi_{x}\rangle
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and send it to an observer. In minimum error discrimination, the observer performs a measurement described by a positive-operator-valued-measure (POVM)
\{M_{0},M_{1}\}
, where
M_{y}
is a POVM element corresponding to a measurement outcome
y\in\{0,1\}
. The minimum error discrimination is to minimize the following error probability,

P_{e}(|\psi_{0}\rangle,|\psi_{1}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})=q_{0}\langle\psi_{0}|M_{1}|\psi_{0}\rangle+q_{1}\langle\psi_{1}|M_{0}|\psi_{1}\rangle,

(1)

where
q_{x}
is the prior probability that
|\psi_{x}\rangle
is prepared. This minimization problem is equivalent to maximize the following success probability,

P_{s}(|\psi_{0}\rangle,|\psi_{1}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})=q_{0}\langle\psi_{0}|M_{0}|\psi_{0}\rangle+q_{1}\langle\psi_{1}|M_{1}|\psi_{1}\rangle.

(2)

The maximal success probability in Eq. (
2
) is called the Helstrom bound
c.w.helstrom
, which is analytically given by,

\displaystyle P_{hel}(|\psi_{0}\rangle,|\psi_{1}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\{M_{0},M_{1}\}}P_{s}(|\psi_{0}\rangle,|\psi_{1}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4q_{0}q_{1}|\langle\psi_{0}|\psi_{1}\rangle|^{2}}}{2}.

For the case that the prior probabilities
\{q_{0},q_{1}\}
are fixed, the Helstrom bound in Eq. (
3
) is determined by inner product of
|\psi_{0}\rangle
and
|\psi_{1}\rangle
. Choosing suitable optical states, quantum communication can be enhanced by improving the Helstrom bound. In the present article, we consider generalized coherent states for possible improvement of the Helstrom bound.

Definition 1
e.m.f.curado
.
If a pure state takes the form:

|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}}\alpha^{n}h_{n}(|\alpha|^{2})|n\rangle,

(4)

then the pure state is a
generalized coherent state
. Here,
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is the set of positive integers,
\{|n\rangle|n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}\}

\mathcal{H}
,

|\alpha|<R
(

\vec{h}:=(h_{0},h_{1},h_{2},\cdots)

h_{n}:[0,R^{2}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}}u^{n}\left\{h_{n}(u)\right\}^{2}=1,

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}}nu^{n}\left\{h_{n}(u)\right\}^{2}\textrm{ is a strictly increasing function of }u,

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{2}}duw(u)u^{n}\left\{h_{n}(u)\right\}^{2}=1,\ \ \textrm{for a function }w:[0,R^{2}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(7)

Here, Eq. (
5
) is for the normalization of Eq. (
4
), Eq. (
6
) is for the consistent definitions of mean photon number in quantum theory and statistical theory, and Eq. (
7
) is for the resolution of an identity operator.

It is noted that a Glauber-Sudarshan coherent state (GS-CS) is a special case of Definition 1.

Definition 2
e.m.f.curado
.
If every real-valued function
h_{n}(u)
takes the form:

h_{n}(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}e^{-\frac{u}{2}},\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}

(8)

then
|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle
is so called GS-CS or
standard coherent state
(S-CS). Otherwise,
|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle
is so called

(NS-CS).

Remark 1.
The tuple
\vec{h}
determines both the explicit form of NS-CS and the Mandel parameter
Q_{M}^{\vec{h}}(u)
,

Q_{M}^{\vec{h}}:=\frac{(\Delta n)^{2}}{\langle n\rangle}-1,

(9)

where
\langle n\rangle
is mean photon number and
\Delta n
is standard deviation of the photon number. It is well known that the Mandel parameter is directly related with physical properties
e.m.f.curado

a.m.perelomov

;

sub-Poissoninan
. Meanwhile, Perelomov coherent state (P-CS) has negative Mandel parameter (

). This implies that P-CS is

. As sub-Poissonianity of NS-CS can improve the Helstrom bound

III
Optimal Measurement for Binary Quantum Communication

In this section, we propose two kinds of measurements for binary quantum communication with NS-CS. First, we analytically show that an optimal measurement for discriminating NS-CS is described by a projective measurement composed of orthogonal non-standard Schrödinger cat states. Moreover, we numerically show that an indirect measurement based on Jaynes-Cummings model is a nearly optimal measurement for discriminating NS-CS.

III.1
On-off Keying (OOK) and Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK) signals

On-off keying (OOK) and binary phase shift keying (BPSK) signals are well known information carrier in quantum communication
i.a.burenkov0
;
g.cariolaro
. In OOK signal

|\alpha_{x},\vec{h}\rangle\in\{|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle,|0,\vec{h}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}|\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\},

(10)

one bit message is encoded depending on the existence of a coherent state. Whereas, in BPSK signal

|\alpha_{x},\vec{h}\rangle\in\{|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle,|-\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}|\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\},

(11)

one bit message is encoded in a phase of a coherent state. Based on NS-CS, the Helstrom bounds of OOK and BPSK signals are given by

\displaystyle P_{hel}(|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle,|0,\vec{h}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4q_{0}q_{1}\left\{h_{0}(\alpha^{2})\right\}^{2}}}{2},

(12)

and

\displaystyle P_{hel}(|\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle,|-\alpha,\vec{h}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4q_{0}q_{1}\left[\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}}(-\alpha^{2})^{n}\left\{h_{n}(\alpha^{2})\right\}^{2}\right]^{2}}}{2},

(13)

respectively. We consider BPSK signal instead of OOK signal, since the Helstrom bound of the BPSK signal is larger than that of the OOK signal. We numerically show this in Fig.2 of Section 3.

In the next section, we propose the optimal measurement for discriminating BPSK signal composed of NS-CS.

III.2
Optimal Projective Measurement with Non-Standard Schrödinger Cat States

It is known that the optimal measurement for discriminating binary GS-CS can be described by a projective measurement composed of two orthogonal Schrödinger cat state
c.w.helstrom2
. Here, the optimal measurement is described by a projective measurement composed of two orthogonal Schrödinger cat states

|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle:=\mathcal{N}_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}}^{(\pm)}(|\alpha_{0}\rangle\pm|\alpha_{1}\rangle),

(14)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}}^{(\pm)}

(15)

4

Theorem 1.
Let us consider a projective measurement defined by

\Pi_{y}=|\pi_{y}\rangle\langle\pi_{y}|,\ \ y\in\{0,1\},

(16)

where the orthonormal vectors
|\pi_{0}\rangle
and
|\pi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle|\pi_{0}\rangle:=\cos(\xi)|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle+e^{i\zeta}\sin(\xi)|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle,

(17)

and

\displaystyle|\pi_{1}\rangle:=\sin(\xi)|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle-e^{i\zeta}\cos(\xi)|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle,

(18)

respectively. For arbitrary
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and prior probabilities
q_{0}
and
q_{1}

and

that the maximal success probability

with respect to the projective measurement composed of
|\pi_{0}\rangle
and
|\pi_{1}\rangle
equals to the Helstrom bound.

Proof.
From Eqs. (
16
) and (
18
), maximal success probability is derived by

\displaystyle P_{dir}(|\alpha_{0},\vec{h}\rangle,|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle|q_{0},q_{1})=q_{0}|\langle\pi_{0}|\alpha_{0},\vec{h}\rangle|^{2}+q_{1}|\langle\pi_{1}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(q_{0}-q_{1})\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle^{2}}{2}\cos(2\xi)+\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle^{2}}}{2}\cos(\zeta)\sin(2\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(q_{0}-q_{1})\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle^{2}}{2}\cos(2\xi)+\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle^{2}}}{2}\sin(2\xi).

(19)

Since
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
are considered,
\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
holds. Moreover, if
\zeta=0
, the inequality Eq. (
19
) becomes an equality.

Suppose that
\xi
takes the form:

\xi=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left\{\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle^{2}}}{(q_{0}-q_{1})\langle\alpha_{0},\vec{h}|\alpha_{1},\vec{h}\rangle}\right\}.

(20)

Then, Eq. (
19
) also takes the form equal to the Helstrom bound.

Remark 2.
The optimal projective measurement proposed in Theorem 1 requires technical capability for implementing the non-standard Schrödinger cat states, whose experimental realization is still unknown. In the next section, we propose another near-optimal measurement, which can be possibly implemented in a near future.

Optimal Indirect Measurement with Jaynes-Cummings Model

III.3.1
Success Probability of Indirect Measurement

Recently, minimum error discrimination between binary pure states can be realized by an indirect measurement

According to a mathematical framework

\Xi:=\{\widetilde{\mathcal{H}},\sigma,\mathrm{P},U\},

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}

III.3.2
Indirect Measurement with Jaynes-Cummings Model

An indirect measurement considered in the present article uses a two-level atom as an auxiliary quantum system
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. We denote
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=\mathrm{span}\{|g\rangle,|e\rangle\}
where
|g\rangle
is the ground state and
|e\rangle
is the excited state of the two-level atom. The initial state of the two-level atom in

\sigma:=|g\rangle\langle g|.

Each projector in Eq. (

\displaystyle|\pi_{0}\rangle:=\cos(\theta)|g\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta)|e\rangle,

(27)

\displaystyle|\pi_{1}\rangle:=\sin(\theta)|g\rangle-e^{i\phi}\cos(\theta)|e\rangle.

In this article, we design the unitary operator

in Eq. (
21
) using Jaynes-Cummings model

(31)

\omega_{L}
is a light frequency,

is a transition frequency of the two-level atom, and

Finally, given that the interaction time is

IV
Conclusions

In the present article, we proposed optimal strategies for quantum communication enhanced by non-standard coherent states.

•

We have analytically shown that optimal measurement for the enhanced quantum communication is described by a projective measurement composed of orthogonal non-standard Schrödinger cat states.

•

We have numerically shown that an indirect measurement based on the Jaynes-Cummings model performs the near-optimal quantum-enhanced communication. Since the Jaynes-Cummings model describes the interaction between the light and the two-level atom, it can be realized in the future.

The success probability for discriminating the BPSK signal can be larger than that of the OOK signal and this justifies the reason of concerning BPSK signal instead of OOK signal. As Jaynes-Cummings model is known to be suitable in the field of quantum computation
j.i.cirac
, our result provides a useful reference in the field of quantum computation and communication.

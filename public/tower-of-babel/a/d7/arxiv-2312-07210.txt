Boundary behavior of limit-Interfaces for the Allen-Cahn equation on Riemannian manifolds with Neumann boundary condition
Martin Man-chun Li, Davide Parise, Lorenzo Sarnataro

Abstract
. We study the boundary behavior of any limit-interface arising from a sequence of general critical points of the Allen-Cahn energy functionals on a smooth bounded domain. Given any such sequence with uniform energy bounds, we prove that the limit-interface is a free boundary varifold which is integer rectifiable up to the boundary. This extends earlier work of Hutchinson and Tonegawa on the interior regularity of such limit-interface. A key novelty in our result is that no convexity assumption of the boundary is required and it is valid even when the limit-interface clusters near the boundary. Moreover, our arguments are local and thus works in the Riemannian setting. This work provides the first step towards the regularity theory for the Allen-Cahn min-max theory for free boundary minimal hypersurfaces, which was developed in the Almgren-Pitts setting by the first-named author and Zhou.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a smooth bounded domain. For each small parameter
\varepsilon>0
, consider the
Allen-Cahn energy functional
arising from the van der Waals-Cahn-Hilliard theory of phase transition
[
6
]
:

E_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}

(1.1)

where
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}
. Here,
W:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative double-well potential with strict minima at
\pm 1
with
W(\pm 1)=0
. If
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a critical point of
E_{\varepsilon}
, by standard elliptic theory
u\in C^{3}(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u+W^{\prime}(u)=0&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
\partial u/\partial\nu=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\nu
is the outward (with respect to
\Omega
) unit normal of
\partial\Omega
. In the literature, (
1.2
) is often known as the
Allen-Cahn equation
. This equation and its parabolic counterpart (see e.g.
[
34
]
) account for important models in phase transitions and evolution of antiphase boundaries
[
3
]
.

There is an extensive literature on the semi-linear elliptic equation (
1.2
). Much of the development was centered around a celebrated conjecture of De Giorgi concerning entire (i.e.
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
) solutions. We refer the interested reader to an excellent recent survey
[
7
]
about this conjecture and the recent developments.

The Allen-Cahn equation is closely related to the theory of minimal hypersurfaces. This connection is particularly important when one considers the asymptotic behavior of solutions to the Allen-Cahn equation as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Heristically, the level sets of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
to the Allen-Cahn equation will converge in some sense to a sharp limit interface which is an embedded minimal hypersurface (which may possess singularity). For minimizing solutions, this was made precise using De Giorgi’s
\Gamma
-convergence theory by Modica
[
31
]
and Kohn-Sternberg
[
24
]
. They further used this idea to show the existence of stable solutions for (
1.2
) in two-dimensional non-convex domains such as a dumbbell. The existence of unstable solutions in two-dimensions was later studied by Kowalczyk
[
25
]
. Sternberg and Zumbrun in
[
37
]
proved the connectivity of the phase boundaries in strictly convex domains.

The interior regularity of (
1.2
) has been treated in a series of important papers by Padilla-Tonegawa
[
32
]
, Hutchinson-Tonegawa
[
21
]
, Tonegawa
[
41
]
and Tonegawa-Wickramasekera
[
42
]
. We remark that these results are foundational in a recent beautiful work of Guaraco
[
17
]
on the min-max theory for minimal hypersurfaces in closed Riemannian manifolds using the Allen-Cahn approach. This approach is especially successful when the ambient space has dimension two and three, as seen in the beautiful work of Chodosh and Mantoulidis
[
29
,
9
,
8
]
.

Although we have by now a rather satisfactory interior theory, much less is known about the boundary behavior of (
1.2
) as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. One major obstacle is to understand the phenomenon of boundary concentration. When
\Omega
is the unit ball, Malchiodi, Ni and Wei
[
28
]
constructed radially symmetric solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.2
) whose interfaces clustered towards
\partial\Omega
. Malchiodi and Wei
[
27
]
removed the symmetry assumption and showed that such boundary clustering can occur for any mean-convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. These results show that the limit-interface can stick to the boundary even when
\Omega
is
convex
.

Our goal is to fill in this gap in the literature and, in subsequent works, to generalize the Allen-Cahn min-max theory to the free boundary setting. More precisely, in this paper we study the boundary behavior of the limit-interface arising from general critical points of the Allen-Cahn energy functional
E_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
), i.e. solutions satisfying (
1.2
), subject only to a uniform energy bound. In particular, we do not assume that
u_{\varepsilon}
is
minimizing
or
stable
. We show that the limit-interface is
integer rectifiable
up to the boundary (i.e. when regarded as an
(n-1)
-varifold
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Moreover, the limit varifold is
stationary with free boundary
, hence a critical point of the area functional with respect to variations preserving the boundary
\partial\Omega
as a set (we note that a different Dirichlet boundary value problem, related to Plateau’s problem, was recently studied by Guaraco and Lynch
[
18
]
) This provides the optimal regularity of the limit-interface up-to-the-boundary, extending the interior regularity result of Hutchinson-Tonegawa
[
21
]
to the free boundary setting.

We now explain in more details what the key difficulties in extending the interior regularity theory to the boundary are and how we overcome these new difficulties. One of the main ingredients in the regularity theory for general critical points is an almost monotonicity formula. The interior monotonicity-type formula was obtained in
[
21
, Proposition 3.4]
. Using a maximum principle argument and a reflection process as in
[
15
]
, Tonegawa
[
39
, Theorem 3.7]
proved a
global
monotonicity formula up to the boundary provided that
\Omega
is
convex
. One of our main results is a boundary monotonicity-type formula, Proposition
4.16
, which differs from that of
[
39
]
in two respects. First, our result is local and does not require the convexity of
\partial\Omega
. Without the convexity assumption, we can only established a weaker pointwise upper bound (c.f. Proposition
4.10
) for the discrepancy function compared to the interior case
[
21
, Proposition 3.3]
and the global case in convex domains
[
39
, Proposition 3.1]
. Nonetheless, our weaker bound, when combined with suitable integral estimates (c.f. Proposition
4.13
), is sufficient to establish the desired local monotonicity formula up to the boundary. We would like to point out that a similar upper bound for the discrepancy function in the interior was also observed with variable chemical potential lying in certain Sobolev spaces
[
40
]
. Second, the monotonicity formula in
[
39
]
contains terms integrated on balls reflected across the boundary as in
[
15
]
, which sometimes make it more difficult to apply. Our monotonicity formula does not involve any reflection and thus can be more readily used in applications. Note that a similar monotonicity formula for free boundary stationary varifolds was obtained by the first-named author with Guang and Zhou in
[
16
]
. It would be possible—though we chose not to pursue it here—to use a reflection technique similar to the one adopted in
[
15
]
and
[
39
]
, and obtain a monotonicity formula involving the energy density on balls reflected across the boundary, resembling that of
[
39
]
.

Without considering reflected balls, another technical difficulty is that our monotonicity type formula only applies to balls that are either disjoint from the boundary or centered exactly at the boundary. This makes the arguments much more delicate when applying the monotonicity formula. For example, in contrast to the interior theory
[
21
]
, we can only establish uniform density ratio upper bound up to the boundary but not the lower bound (c.f. Proposition
5.2
). This is related to the boundary clustering phenomena which is a major obstacle in our setting. Nonetheless, we can still prove the vanishing of the discrepancy measure (c.f. Theorem
5.4
) which allows us to obtain control on the first variation of the limit interface. Given the weaker control on the discrepancy function and the potential boundary clustering phenomena, we carefully modify the arguments in
[
21
]
to establish the up-to-the-boundary integer-rectifiability of the limit varifold.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notations and some preliminary results that will be used throughout the rest of the paper. We then state our assumptions and the main result of this paper (Theorem
3.3
) in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we derive our key monotonicity-type formula and several of its consequences. Much of the work is devoted to proving pointwise and integral estimates on the discrepancy function. Using our monotonicity formula, we then derive at the diffuse level (i.e.
\varepsilon>0
) uniform bounds on the energy density ratio for balls that are either centered at a boundary point or completely disjoint from the boundary. In Section
5
, we establish bounds on the density ratio for the limit measure and prove the vanishing of discrepancy measure, which implies the rectifiability of the limit interface. The last section, Section
6
, shows further that the limit interface is in fact an integral varifold up to the boundary.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the notations and preliminary concepts that will be used throughout the rest of this paper.

2.1.
Ambient space

Let
(\widetilde{M},g)
be a smooth complete
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
without boundary
. We use
\operatorname{dist}_{g}
and
\nabla
to denote respectively the intrinsic distance and Levi-Civita connection on
\widetilde{M}
induced by
g
. For any
x\in\widetilde{M}
, we denote by
\exp_{x}
the exponential map and
\textrm{inj}_{g}(x)
the injectivity radius of
(\widetilde{M},g)
at
x
. We use
B_{r}(x)
to denote the open geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at
x\in\widetilde{M}
. For a given subset
A\subset\widetilde{M}
, we denote by
\mathbf{1}_{A}
its indicator function; by
\overline{A}
and
A^{\circ}
respectively the closure and interior of
A
with respect to the topology on
(\widetilde{M},g)
. Moreover, we set
\operatorname{inj}_{g}(A):=\inf_{x\in A}\operatorname{inj}_{g}(x)
and
U_{r}(A):=\cup_{x\in A}B_{r}(x)
. For any linear transformations
A,B:T_{x}\widetilde{M}\to T_{x}\widetilde{M}
, we define the Frobenius (or Hilbert-Schmidt) inner product and norm by

\langle A,B\rangle_{g}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A_{ij}B_{ij}\quad\text{ and }\quad|A|_{g}:=\sqrt{\langle A,A\rangle}

where
A_{ij},B_{ij}
are matrix components of
A,B
under an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}\widetilde{M}
with respect to
g
. Moreover,
\|A\|_{g}
denotes the operator norm. We shall sometimes use
\cdot
to denote the inner product
g
on the tangent spaces of
\widetilde{M}
or omit the subscript
g
when there is no ambiguity. Vectors and co-vectors are identified via the metric
g
wherever necessary.

Throughout this paper (see Section
3.1
), we assume
(\widetilde{M},g)
has positive injectivity radius
\operatorname{inj}_{g}(\widetilde{M})\geq i_{0}>0
and bounded sectional curvature, i.e.
|\sec_{(\widetilde{M},g)}|\leq k_{0}
, for some constant
k_{0}\geq 0
. The classical Hessian comparison theorem implies (see
[
10
, Lemma 7.1]
) that the distance function
r(\cdot)=\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\cdot,x)
from any
x\in\widetilde{M}
satisfies

\left|\operatorname{Hess}r(X,X)-\frac{1}{r}|X-(X\cdot\nabla r)\nabla r|^{2}\right|\leq\sqrt{k_{0}}

(2.1)

provided that
|X|=1
and
r<\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{0}}}\}
. Taking the trace of (
2.1
) along directions orthogonal to
\nabla r
yields

\left|\Delta r-\frac{n-1}{r}\right|\leq(n-1)\sqrt{k_{0}}.

(2.2)

Moreover, the Bochner formula
[
10
, Proposition 1.47]
implies that for any smooth function
f
on subsets of
\widetilde{M}
,

\left|\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla f|^{2}-|\operatorname{Hess}f|^{2}-\nabla f\cdot\nabla(\Delta f)\right|\leq(n-1)k_{0}|\nabla f|^{2}.

(2.3)

On the other hand, by Rauch comparison theorem, there exists a constant
r_{0}>0
depending only on
n
and
k_{0}
such that for all
r<\min\{r_{0},i_{0}\}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\omega_{n}r^{n}\leq\textrm{Vol}(B_{r}(x))\leq 2\omega_{n}r^{n},

(2.4)

\frac{1}{2}n\omega_{n}r^{n-1}\leq\textrm{Area}(\partial B_{r}(x))\leq 2n\omega_{n}r^{n-1}.

(2.5)

Here,
\omega_{k}
is the volume of the
k
-dimensional unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to the Euclidean metric.

Let
\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,k)
be the space of unoriented
k
-dimensional subspaces of
T_{x}\widetilde{M}
. The orthogonal complement of
S\in\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,k)
is denoted by
S^{\perp}\in\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,n-k)
. For
a\in T_{x}\widetilde{M}
,
a\otimes a:T_{x}\widetilde{M}\to T_{x}\widetilde{M}
is the matrix with components
a_{i}a_{j}
with respect to an orthonormal basis. For
S\in\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,k)
, we identify
S
with the corresponding orthogonal projection of
T_{x}\widetilde{M}
onto
S
. In the case of
k=n-1
, we also identify
S\in\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,n-1)
with the unit vector
\pm N\in T_{x}\widetilde{M}
which is perpendicular to
S
. Note that we may express the relation by
S=I-N\otimes N
where
I
is the identity map on
T_{x}\widetilde{M}
. The correspondence is a homeomorphism with respect to the naturally endowed topologies on
\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,n-1)
and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}/\{\pm 1\}

to denote the space of real-valued continuous functions on
U

to denote the space of functions on
U
which are in

with respect to the volume measure
dv_{g}
on
(\widetilde{M},g)
. For any subset
A\subset\widetilde{M}
, we let
C^{k}(A)
be the space of all functions defined on
A
which can be extended to a
C^{k}
function on an open subset

. We will use

. For any

. A subset

2.4.
Varifolds

We recall some definitions from geometric measure theory and refer the readers to the standard references
[
2
,
36
]
for more details. A nice description of varifolds in Riemannian manifolds without using isometric embedding can be found in
[
35
]
. We shall only consider codimension one varifolds in this paper.

For any open set
U\subset\widetilde{M}
, let
G(U):=\cup_{p\in U}\mathbf{G}_{p}(n,n-1)
. A general varifold in
U
is a Radon measure on
G(U)
. We denote the set of all general varifolds in
U
by
\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
, equipped with the weak topology. For
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
, let
\|V\|
be the weight of
V
, which is the Radon measure on
U
defined by

\|V\|(\phi):=\int_{G(U)}\phi(x)\;dV(x,S),\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(U).

We say
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
is rectifiable if there exists an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measurable, countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable subset
\Sigma\subset U
and a locally
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-integrable function
\Theta
defined on
\Sigma
such that

V(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\phi(x,T_{x}\Sigma)\;\Theta(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\qquad\forall\phi\in C_{c}(G(U)).

Here
T_{x}\Sigma\in\mathbf{G}_{x}(n,n-1)
is the unique approximate tangent space of
\Sigma
at
x
which exists
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\Sigma
. Note that
\Theta^{n-1}(\|V\|,x)=\Theta(x)
for any such
x
. A rectifiable varifold
V
is uniquely determined by its weight
\|V\|=\Theta\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Sigma
through the formula above. For this reason, we naturally say a Radon measure
\mu
on
U
is rectifiable when one can associate a rectifiable varifold
V
such that
\|V\|=\mu
. If
\Theta(x)\in\mathbb{N}
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\Sigma
, we say
V
is integer rectifiable or an integral varifold. The set of all integral varifolds in
U
is denoted by
\mathbf{IV}_{n-1}(U)
. We say that
V
is a unit density varifold if
\Theta(x)=1
for
\|V\|
-a.e.
x
.

For
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
let
\delta V
be the first variation of
V
, namely,

\delta V(X):=\int_{G(U)}\langle\nabla X(x),S\rangle_{g}\;dV(x,S)\qquad\forall X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U).

(2.11)

We also denote the total variation of
\delta V
by
\|\delta V\|
. If
\|\delta V\|

4.
Monotonicity formula and consequences

Let
(\widetilde{M},g)
and
\Omega,U\subset\widetilde{M}
be as given in Section
3.1
. Throughout this section, we assume
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\cap U)
is a solution to

-\varepsilon\Delta u+\frac{W^{\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}=\lambda\quad\text{in $\Omega\cap U$,}

(4.1)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega\cap U$},

(4.2)

where
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,
|\lambda|\leq\Lambda_{0}
,
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}
and
E_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq E_{0}
. We define the energy density
e_{\epsilon}(u)
and discrepancy function
\xi_{\varepsilon}(u)
respectively by

e_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}

(4.3)

and

\xi_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}.

(4.4)

Our first goal is to prove an almost-monotonicity inequality (c.f.
[
17
, Appendix B]
) of the form:

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(e^{c\rho}\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\cap\Omega}e_{\varepsilon}(u)\right)\geq e^{c\rho}\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)\cap\Omega}-(\xi_{\varepsilon}(u))^{+}

for some
c>0
and all sufficiently small
\rho
. Next, we establish up-to-the-boundary estimates, in both pointwise and integral forms, for
u
and
\xi_{\varepsilon}(u)
. Together with the almost-monotonicity inequality, these estimates allow us to control the energy density ratio

I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}e_{\varepsilon}(u)

(4.5)

which will be instrumental in deriving the regularity of the limit interface in later sections.

4.1.
Almost monotonicity formula

Recall that the monotonicity formula for minimal surfaces is obtained by applying the first variation formula for the area functional with (suitably truncated) radial test vector fields. In the Allen-Cahn setting, the almost monotonicity formula (which can be viewed as an
\varepsilon
-version of the monotonicity formula for minimal surfaces) can be obtained by similar arguments. The following
Pohozaev identity
plays the role of the first variation formula in this regard.

Lemma 4.1

(Pohozaev identity)
.

Let
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}\cap U)
be a solution to the semilinear equation
\Delta u=F(u)
in
\Omega\cap U
where
F=G^{\prime}
for some smooth function
G:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. For any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap U}\left[\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+G(u)\right)\operatorname{div}_{g}X-\nabla X(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right]\;dv_{g}

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega\cap U}\left[\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+G(u)\right)(X\cdot\nu)-(\nabla u\cdot X)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right]

\displaystyle d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Proof.

Multiply both sides of the equation
\Delta u=F(u)
by
X\cdot\nabla u
and then integrate by part twice (see
[
43
, Lemma 9]
for a similar calculation).
∎

For the Allen-Cahn equation (
4.1
), we choose
G(t)=\varepsilon^{-2}\widetilde{W}(t)
where

\widetilde{W}(t):=W(t)-\varepsilon\lambda t+\varepsilon\Lambda_{0}C_{0}.

Note that the constant term
\varepsilon\Lambda_{0}C_{0}
is there simply to ensure that
\widetilde{W}(u)\geq 0
under our assumptions. For this choice of
G
and using the Neumann boundary condition (
4.2
), the Pohozaev identity (
4.6
) reads

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap U}\left[\left(\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\widetilde{W}(u)}{\varepsilon}\right)\operatorname{div}_{g}X-\varepsilon\nabla X(\nabla u,\nabla u)\right]\;dv_{g}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega\cap U}\left(\frac{\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\widetilde{W}(u)}{\varepsilon}\right)(X\cdot\nu)\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle.

We define
\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)
,
\widetilde{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u)
and
\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)
as in (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) similarly with
W
replaced by
\widetilde{W}
. We will first establish the almost monotonicity formula for
\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}
from which the corresponding almost monotonicity formula for
I_{\varepsilon,u}
follows.

For the purpose of making the test vector fields compactly supported, we let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be any smooth decreasing cutoff function such that
\varphi(t)=1
for
t\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\varphi(t)=0
for
t\geq 1
. For any
x\in\widetilde{M}
and
0<\rho<\operatorname{inj}_{g}(x)
, the function
\varphi\left(\frac{\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\cdot)}{\rho}\right)
is a smooth function which is compactly supported inside
B_{\rho}(x)
.

From now on, we fix
x\in U\cap\overline{\Omega}
and denote
r(\cdot):=\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\cdot)
. For any
0<\rho<\min\{\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial U),i_{0}\}
, we consider the test vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U)
defined by

X:=\varphi\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)r\nabla r

which is a truncated radial vector field centered at
x
supported inside
B_{\rho}(x)
. When
\rho<\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{0}}}
, a direct computation together with (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) gives

\left|\operatorname{div}_{g}X(x)-\left(n\varphi+\frac{r}{\rho}\varphi^{\prime}\right)\right|\leq(n-1)\sqrt{k_{0}}r\varphi

and

\left|\nabla X(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\left(|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi+\frac{r}{\rho}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla r)^{2}\varphi^{\prime}\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{k_{0}}r|\nabla u|^{2}\varphi

where
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
are both evaluated at
\frac{r}{\rho}
by abuse of notation. Observing the relation

\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)\right]=-\frac{r}{\rho}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right).

and plugging the above estimates into the left hand side of the Pohozaev identity (
4.7
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\rho}

\displaystyle\left[\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\Omega\cap U}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\varphi\right]-\rho^{-(n-1)}\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[\int_{\Omega\cap U}\varepsilon(\nabla u\cdot\nabla r)^{2}\varphi\right]+\rho^{-n}\int_{\Omega\cap U}\widetilde{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u)\varphi

(4.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-(n+1)\sqrt{k_{0}}\left[\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\Omega\cap U}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\varphi\right]-\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap U}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)|\nabla r\cdot\nu|\varphi\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Note that we have used the fact that
r\leq\rho
in the support of
\varphi
and
\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 2\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)
. We would like to consider two separate cases:

•

Case 1:

x\in\Omega\cap U
and
B_{\rho}(x)\Subset\Omega\cap U
;

•

Case 2:

x\in\partial\Omega\cap U

B_{\rho}(x)\Subset U
.

In the first case, there is no boundary term as
B_{\rho}(x)\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
. We obtain the following interior almost-monotonicity inequality (c.f.
[
17
, Appendix B]
), which reduces to
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
when
k_{0}=0
.

Lemma 4.2

(Interior almost-monotonicity inequality)
.

For any
x\in\Omega\cap U
and
0<\rho<\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{0}}}\}
such that
B_{\rho}(x)\Subset\Omega\cap U
, we have

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[e^{(n+1)\sqrt{k_{0}}\rho}\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\right]\geq e^{(n+1)\sqrt{k_{0}}\rho}\rho^{-n}\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}-\widetilde{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u).

(4.9)

Proof.

It follows directly from (
4.8
) by dropping the non-negative term and the boundary term. Finally, we let
\varphi\to\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,1)}
.
∎

For the second case, we need to handle the extra boundary term in (
4.8
). The following lemma allows us to rewrite the boundary term in terms of an interior integral (and its derivative).

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
x\in\partial\Omega\cap U
and
0<\rho<\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{2\kappa_{0}}\}
such that
B_{\rho}(x)\Subset U
, we have

\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq(n+1)C_{2}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)+6\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\right].

(4.10)

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be the radial cutoff function centered at
x
as defined before. For any
0<\rho<\min\{\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial U),\frac{1}{2\kappa_{0}}\}
, we can consider the test vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U)
defined by

X=-\varphi\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)\nabla d

where
d
is the signed distance function from
\partial\Omega
as defined in section
2.2
. Note that
X
is smooth and compactly supported in
B_{\rho}(x)
with
X\cdot\nu=\varphi
along
\partial\Omega
. By (
2.6
), we have the following estimates:

\operatorname{div}_{g}X\leq(n-1)C_{1}\varphi-\frac{1}{\rho}\varphi^{\prime}

-\varepsilon\nabla X(\nabla u,\nabla u)\leq\left(C_{1}\varphi-\frac{1}{\rho}\varphi^{\prime}\right)\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}.

Putting all these into the Pohozaev identity (
4.7
), noting that
r\geq\frac{\rho}{2}
in the support of
\varphi^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 2\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)
, and then letting
\varphi\to\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,1)}
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

We are now ready to establish the almost-monotonicity inequality centered at a boundary point
x\in\partial\Omega
.

Lemma 4.4

(Boundary almost-monotonicity inequality)
.

There exists a constant

c_{1}=c_{1}(n,k_{0},\kappa_{0})\geq 0

so that the following holds: for any
x\in\partial\Omega\cap U
and
0<\rho<\frac{1}{2}\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}\}
such that
B_{\rho}(x)\Subset U
, we have

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[e^{c_{1}\rho}\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\right]\geq\frac{e^{c_{1}\rho}}{1+3\kappa_{0}\rho}\rho^{-n}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}-\widetilde{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u).

(4.11)

Proof.

First of all, note that by (
2.7
), we have the following estimate on the boundary term of (
4.8
) for any
\rho<\frac{1}{2}i_{0}
,

\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)|\nabla r\cdot\nu|\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\frac{\kappa_{0}}{2}\rho^{-(n-2)}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

On the other hand, Lemma
4.3
implies that when
\rho<\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{2\kappa_{0}}\}
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle 6\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\left[\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\right]\geq

\displaystyle\rho^{-(n-2)}\int_{\partial\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u)\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle-[(n+1)C_{1}\rho+6(n-1)]\rho^{-(n-1)}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x)}\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}(u).

Putting it back to (
4.8
), using
\rho\kappa_{0}<\frac{1}{2}
, after letting
\varphi\to\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,1)}
and rearranging terms gives the desired inequality with
c_{1}=\frac{n+1}{4}C_{1}+3(n-1)\kappa_{0}
.
∎

4.2.
Auxiliary estimates

We first derive some a-priori estimates for any solution

to (
4.1
) and (

up to the boundary

\partial\Omega

\varepsilon

We begin with the

Lemma 4.6

(Uniform
C^{0}
estimate)
.

There exist constants

\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon_{2}(\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{2}=c_{2}(\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))\geq 1

such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{2}
, we have

\|u\|_{C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\cap\tilde{U})}\leq 1+c_{2}\varepsilon<2

(4.12)

Proof.

Let
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\tilde{U}
and
\rho<\frac{1}{6}\min\{i_{0},\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{0}}},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial U)\}
. Denote
r(\cdot):=\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\cdot)
. Inside the ball
B_{3\rho}(x)\Subset U
, we fix a smooth radial function
\zeta=\zeta(r)
such that

•

\zeta=1+\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{2}
inside
B_{\rho}(x)
;

•

\zeta=1+C_{0}
on the annulus
B_{3\rho}(x)\setminus B_{2\rho}(x)
; and

•

inside the ball
B_{3\rho}(x)
, we have by (
2.2
)

1+\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{2}\leq\zeta\leq 1+C_{0},\quad|\nabla\zeta|\leq 2C_{0}\rho^{-1},\quad|\Delta\zeta|\leq C(n)C_{0}\rho^{-2}

where
c_{2}
will be chosen later and then
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small so that
c_{2}\varepsilon<\min\{1,C_{0}\}
.

We first claim that
u\leq 1+c_{2}\varepsilon
on
B_{\rho}(x)\cap\Omega
for all sufficiently small
\rho
by a contradiction argument. Suppose we have
\sup_{B_{\rho}(x)\cap\Omega}u>1+c_{2}\varepsilon
. We divide into two cases as before:

•

Case 1:

x\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
and
B_{3\rho}(x)\Subset\Omega\cap U
;

•

Case 2:

x\in\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
and
B_{3\rho}(x)\Subset U
.

For the first case, we consider as in
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
the auxiliary function

which is smooth inside

•

•

\sup_{B_{3\rho}(x)}f>\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{2}>0
.

Therefore,

at an interior maxima

. By maximum principle, at
x_{0}
we have

\displaystyle 0\geq\varepsilon\Delta f=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}W^{\prime}(u)-\lambda-\varepsilon\Delta\zeta

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\left.W^{\prime}(tu+(1-t)\zeta)\right|_{t=0}^{t=1}+\varepsilon^{-1}W^{\prime}(\zeta)-\lambda-\varepsilon\Delta\zeta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}f\int_{0}^{1}W^{\prime\prime}(tu+(1-t)\zeta)\;dt-\lambda-\varepsilon\Delta\zeta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}\kappa}{2}-\lambda-\varepsilon\Delta\zeta,

Note that we have used that
u>\zeta\geq 1
at
x_{0}
and the properties of
W
in section
2.5
. If
c_{2}\geq 1
is sufficiently large, depending only on
\Lambda_{0}
,
C_{0}
,
W
,
n
,
i_{0}
,
k_{0}
,
\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U)
and
\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)
, we arrive at a contradiction.

For the second case, we consider another auxiliary function

f:=u-\zeta+\frac{4C_{0}}{\rho}\chi_{a}\circ d

where
d(\cdot)=\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)
and
\chi
is the cutoff function defined in section
2.6
with
a=\frac{\varepsilon\rho}{16C_{0}}
. One can again verify

•

f\leq-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<-\frac{1}{2}
along the boundary
\partial B_{3\rho}(x)\cap\Omega
;

•

\sup_{B_{3\rho}(x)\cap\Omega}f>\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{2}>0
; and

•

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}\leq-2C_{0}\rho^{-1}<0
along
B_{3\rho}(x)\cap\partial\Omega
.

Hence,
\sup_{B_{3\rho}(x)\cap\Omega}f
is again achieved by an interior maxima
x_{0}\in B_{3\rho}(x)\cap\Omega
. Applying the maximum principle at
x_{0}

u\geq 1
and

by our choice of

) gives

\frac{c_{2}\kappa}{2}\leq\lambda+\varepsilon\Delta\zeta+\frac{1}{2}\max_{t\in[1,1+C_{0}]}W^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{4\varepsilon C_{0}}{\rho}\Delta(\chi_{a}\circ d).

By the definition of
\chi_{a}
in section
2.6
and (
2.6
), we will again obtain a contradiction when

is sufficiently large, depending only on
\Lambda_{0}
,
C_{0}
,

,

,

,

,

and

Lemma 4.8

(Refined uniform

Proposition 4.10

There exists a constant

\varepsilon_{5}=\varepsilon_{5}(\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

Proof.

We consider as in

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}

\partial\Omega
we have

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial\nu}\xi_{G}-\varepsilon\zeta B(\nabla u,\nabla u)-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}

where
B
is the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
with respect to
\nu
(i.e.
B(\tau,\tau):=\langle\nabla_{\tau}\nu,\tau\rangle
). Since
\kappa_{0}
bounds the maximum absolute value of the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
, by Lemma
4.7
and our choice of
\zeta
, we have
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}<0
along
\partial\Omega
by taking
\hat{C}:=10c_{3}^{2}
. Hence, we can assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}\notin\partial\Omega
. Suppose (
4.19
) does not hold, then at an interior maximum
x_{0}
we have by our choice of
a
that

\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\zeta|\nabla u|^{2}\geq\zeta\xi_{G}>\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{-4/5}-2a\hat{C}\epsilon^{-1}R^{-1}=\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{-4/5}.

(4.21)

From this and (
4.14
), we have at
x_{0}
,

\xi_{G}\leq\frac{c_{3}^{2}}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta^{-1}\leq 2c_{3}^{2}\varepsilon^{-1/5}.

(4.22)

Since
x_{0}
is an interior maximum point of
f
, we have
\nabla f=0
and
\Delta f\leq 0
at
x_{0}
. The first order condition says
\zeta\nabla\xi_{G}=-\xi_{G}\nabla\zeta-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\chi_{a}^{\prime}\nabla d
, which together with (
4.22
) implies

\zeta^{1/2}|\nabla\xi_{G}|\leq 4c_{3}^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}+\sqrt{2}\hat{C}c_{3}\varepsilon^{-11/10}R^{-1}\leq C(c_{3})\varepsilon^{-11/10}R^{-1}.

On the other hand, the second order condition at
x_{0}
says

\zeta\Delta\xi_{G}\leq-\xi_{G}\Delta\zeta-2\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla\xi_{G}-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\chi_{a}^{\prime}\Delta d-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\chi_{a}^{\prime\prime}|\nabla d|^{2}.

Using the first order condition, our choice of
\zeta
, together with (
2.6
) and (
4.22
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta\xi_{G}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(-\xi_{G}\zeta^{-1}\Delta\zeta+2\xi_{G}\zeta^{-2}|\nabla\zeta|^{2}\Big)+2\zeta^{-2}\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\chi_{a}^{\prime}(\nabla d\cdot\nabla\zeta)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\zeta^{-1}\chi_{a}^{\prime}\Delta d-\varepsilon^{-1}R^{-1}\hat{C}\zeta^{-1}\chi_{a}^{\prime\prime}|\nabla d|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,c_{3})\varepsilon^{-6/5}R^{-2}+C(c_{3})\varepsilon^{-13/10}R^{-2}

\displaystyle+C(C_{1},c_{3},n)\varepsilon^{-6/5}R^{-1}+C(c_{3})\varepsilon^{-7/5}R^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq C(C_{1},c_{3},n)\varepsilon^{-7/5}R^{-2}.

Combining the estimates above, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
depending only on
C_{1}
,
n
,
\hat{C}
and
c_{3}
, we have at
x_{0}

\textrm{(L.H.S.) of \eqref{E:xi-inequality}}\leq C(C_{1},c_{3},n)\varepsilon^{3/5}R^{-2}+C(W,c_{3})\varepsilon^{-1/5}R^{-1}+C(n,k_{0},c_{3})\varepsilon

(4.23)

On the other hand, by our choice of
G
, together with (
4.18
)

\displaystyle\textrm{(R.H.S.) of \eqref{E:xi-inequality}}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{-1}(G^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}\delta G^{\prime}-C(n,k_{0},W)\varepsilon-C(W)\Lambda_{0}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{-1}(C^{-2}\delta^{4}+C^{-1}\delta^{3})-C(n,k_{0},W)\varepsilon-C(W)\Lambda_{0}

\displaystyle\geq 2C^{-2}\varepsilon^{-1}\delta^{4}-C(n,k_{0},W)\varepsilon-C(W)\Lambda_{0}.

Combining these we arrive at the inequality

\delta^{4}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{4/5}

for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}>0
depending only on
\Lambda_{0}
,
C_{0}
,
W
,
n
,
i_{0}
,
k_{0}
,
\kappa_{0}
and
\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U)
. Therefore, we arrive at a contradiction by taking
\delta=(2C^{\prime\prime})^{1/4}\varepsilon^{1/5}<1
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Therefore, (
4.19
) holds and Proposition
4.10
follows.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

With a different choice of the auxiliary function
G
, resembling the one in
[
22
]
, it is possible to obtain a better estimate of order
\varepsilon^{-3/5}
in Proposition
4.10
.

4.4.
Consequences of the almost monotonicity formula

We now combine the results obtained in the previous subsections to derive some consequences of the almost monotonicity formula (Lemma
4.2
and
4.4
). We first establish a uniform lower bound of the energy density ratio (
4.5
) on balls of radius
\varepsilon\leq r\leq\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4/5})
centered at a boundary point within the transition region
\{|u|\leq 1-b\}
.

Lemma 4.12

(Energy density ratio lower bound I)
.

For any
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants

\varepsilon_{6}=\varepsilon_{6}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{6}=c_{6}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0

such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{4}
,
x\in\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
with
|u(x)|\leq 1-b
, and
\varepsilon\leq r\leq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
, we have

I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\geq c_{6}.

In fact, the same holds for
x\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
provided furthermore that
r<\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

We first assume
x\in\partial\Omega
. Since
|u(x)|\leq 1-b
, we have
|u|\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}
on
B_{\frac{b\varepsilon}{2c_{3}}}(x)\cap\Omega
by (
4.14
) provided that
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{2}
. As a result, by (
2.8
) and (
4.5
) we have for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
that

\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\varepsilon,x)\geq I_{\varepsilon,u}(\varepsilon,x)\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}\varepsilon^{n-1}}\int_{B_{\frac{b\varepsilon}{2c_{3}}}(p)\cap\Omega}\frac{W(u)}{\varepsilon}\geq 4c^{\prime}_{6}

(4.25)

where
c^{\prime}_{6}:=\frac{1}{12}\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}\left(\frac{b}{2c_{3}}\right)^{n}\min_{|s|\leq 1-\frac{b}{2}}W(s)>0
.

Let
c_{6}^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{\omega_{n}}c_{6}^{\prime}
. Assuming
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{5}
is sufficiently small such that
c_{6}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{4/5}<\frac{1}{2}\min\{\operatorname{dist}(\tilde{U},\partial U),i_{0},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}\}
, we can apply the boundary almost monotonicity formula (
4.11
) and the upper bound on discrepancy function (
4.15
) to see that for any
\varepsilon\leq r\leq c_{6}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{4/5}
(note that
\widetilde{\xi}_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq\xi_{\varepsilon}(u)
by definition)

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-c_{1}(r-\varepsilon)}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\varepsilon,x)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}\varepsilon^{-4/5}(r-\varepsilon)

(4.26)

\displaystyle\geq e^{-c_{1}c_{6}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{4/5}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\varepsilon,x)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}c_{6}^{\prime\prime}>2c_{6}^{\prime}

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small such that (
4.25
) holds and
e^{-c_{1}c_{6}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{4/5}}\geq\frac{3}{4}
. Setting
c_{6}:=\min\{c_{6}^{\prime},c_{6}^{\prime\prime}\}
, the asserted lower bound for
I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)
follows from the estimate

\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)+\frac{4}{3}\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}}\Lambda_{0}C_{0}r

(4.27)

which holds for all sufficiently small
r
depending only on
k_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
. The case for interior points
x\in\Omega
can be obtained similarly using the interior monotonicity formula (
4.9
).
∎

In order to prove a uniform lower bound of the energy density ratio on balls of radius
r\geq\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4/5})
, the pointwise upper bound in Proposition
4.10
will not be sufficient for our purpose. We need a more refined integral upper bound for the discrepancy function. It is important to note that the center
x
in the proposition below may or may not lie in
\partial\Omega
, and does not need to be inside the transition region
\{|u|\leq 1-b\}
.

Proposition 4.13

(Integral upper bound for discrepancy function)
.

For any
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants

r_{2}=r_{2}(n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))\in(0,1),

\varepsilon_{7}=\varepsilon_{7}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{7}=c_{7}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>1

such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{7}
,
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\tilde{U}
and
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\leq r\leq r_{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(u)\leq\frac{c_{7}}{r^{7/8}}(I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)+1).

(4.28)

Proof.

Let
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
. Fix
x\in\overline{\Omega}\cap\tilde{U}
and
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\leq r\leq r_{2}
where
r_{2}
is sufficiently small with
r_{2}<\frac{1}{2}\{r_{0},r_{1},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial U)\}
. We divide the domain of integration
B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
into a union of three disjoint subsets as follow:

\mathcal{A}:=\Big((B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\setminus B_{r-2\varepsilon^{9/10}}(x)\Big)\cup\Big((B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\cap U_{2\varepsilon^{9/10}}(\partial\Omega)\Big),

\mathcal{B}:=\left\{y\in(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{A}\;:\;B_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(y)\cap\left\{|u|\leq 1-b\right\}\neq\emptyset\right\},

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{y\in(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{A}\;:\;B_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(y)\cap\left\{|u|\leq 1-b\right\}=\emptyset\right\}.

Note that
r\geq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\geq 2\varepsilon^{9/10}
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
(depending on
c_{6}
). We will estimate the (L.H.S.) of (
4.28
) over each of the three subsets defined above.

We begin with the estimate on
\mathcal{A}
. By (
2.5
) and the bound on ball curvatures, for
r_{2}
small enough (depending only on
n
,
k_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
) we have the following volume estimate

dv_{g}(\mathcal{A})\leq C(n)r^{n-1}\varepsilon^{9/10}.

Combining this estimate with (
4.15
) and
r\geq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
, we have

\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(u)\leq C(n)\varepsilon^{1/10}r^{-1}\leq C(n)c_{6}^{-1/8}r^{-7/8}

which is clearly bounded by the (R.H.S.) of (
4.28
) provided that
c_{7}\geq C(n)c_{6}^{-1/8}
.

We now proceed with the estimate on
\mathcal{B}
. We first need to an estimate of the volume
dv_{g}(\mathcal{B})
by considering the covering of
\mathcal{B}
by balls
\{B_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(z)\}
with centers
z\in\left\{|u|\leq 1-b\right\}\cap(\Omega\cap B_{r-\varepsilon^{9/10}}(x)\setminus\overline{U_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(\partial\Omega)})
. By Vitali covering lemma, we can extract a finite disjoint family of such balls
\{B_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
so that
\mathcal{B}\subset\cup_{i=1}^{N}B_{5\varepsilon^{9/10}}(z_{i})
. Hence, we have from (
2.4
) that the volume estimate

dv_{g}(\mathcal{B})\leq N\cdot C(n)\varepsilon^{9n/10}

(4.29)

holds for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
depending on
r_{0}
. It remains to bound
N
from above. Applying Lemma
4.12
(with
x=z_{i}
), since
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{9/10}\leq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
and
\operatorname{dist}(z_{i},\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon^{9/10}
, we have
I_{\varepsilon,u}(\varepsilon^{9/10},z_{i})\geq c_{6}
. Summing over all
i
, noting that
\{B_{\varepsilon^{9/10}}(z_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
are disjoint balls contained in
B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
, we have

N\omega_{n-1}c_{6}\varepsilon^{9(n-1)/10}\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}e_{\varepsilon}(u)=\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x).

This gives an upper estimate on
N
. Putting it back to (
4.29
), we obtain the volume estimate

dv_{g}(\mathcal{B})\leq C(n,c_{6})\varepsilon^{9/10}r^{n-1}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x).

Using this estimate together with (
4.15
) and
r\geq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
, we have

\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(u)\leq C(n,c_{6})\varepsilon^{1/10}r^{-1}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq C(n,c_{6})r^{-7/8}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)

which is clearly bounded by the (R.H.S.) of (
4.28
) with
c_{7}
sufficiently large depending only on
n
and
c_{6}
.

Finally, it remains to prove the estimate on
\mathcal{C}
. Consider the Lipschitz function

\phi(y):=\min\left\{1,\varepsilon^{-9/10}\operatorname{dist}_{g}\left(y,(\tilde{U}\setminus(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega))\cup\left\{|u|\leq 1-b\right\}\right)\right\}

which clearly satisfies

•

\phi=0
on
(\tilde{U}\setminus(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega))\cup\left\{|u|\leq 1-b\right\}
,

•

\phi=1
on
\mathcal{C}
,

•

0\leq\phi\leq 1
and
|\nabla\phi|\leq\varepsilon^{-9/10}
everywhere.

Taking gradient on both sides of (
4.1
) and then multiplying by
\phi^{2}\nabla u
, we have by Bochner formula (
2.3
) that

\varepsilon\phi^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}-|\operatorname{Hess}u|^{2}+(n-1)k_{0}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)\geq\varepsilon\phi^{2}\langle\nabla\Delta u,\nabla u\rangle=\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Integrating the above on
B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega
and integrating by part (note that
\phi=0
on
B_{r}(x)\cap\partial\Omega
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\left(\varepsilon\phi^{2}|\operatorname{Hess}u|^{2}+\frac{W^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\varepsilon}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\varepsilon\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi\langle\nabla\phi,\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\rangle+C(n,k_{0})\varepsilon\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi\operatorname{Hess}u(\nabla u,\nabla\phi)+C(n,k_{0})\varepsilon\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\varepsilon\phi^{2}|\operatorname{Hess}u|^{2}+2\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\varepsilon|\nabla u|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+C(n,k_{0})\varepsilon\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

where we have used Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities in the last inequality. Since
\operatorname{spt}\phi\subset\left\{|u|>1-b\right\}
, we have
W^{\prime\prime}(u)\geq\kappa
on
\operatorname{spt}\phi
. Absorbing the first term on right to the left hand side and combining this with
|\nabla\phi|\leq\varepsilon^{-9/10}
, we obtain for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small (depending on
n
,
k_{0}
and
\kappa
)

\frac{\kappa}{2\varepsilon}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon^{-4/5}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since
\phi=1
on
\mathcal{C}
and
0\leq\phi\leq 1
, together with
r\geq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
, we have

\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\xi_{\varepsilon}^{+}(u)\leq\frac{4\omega_{n-1}}{\kappa}\varepsilon^{1/5}r^{-1}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq C(n,W,c_{6})r^{-3/4}I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)

which is clearly bounded by the (R.H.S.) of (
4.28
) with
c_{7}
sufficiently large depending only on
n
,
W
and
c_{6}
(note that
r^{-3/4}\leq r^{-7/8}
).
∎

Using Proposition
4.13
, we can extend Lemma
4.12
beyond the scale of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4/5})
.

Proposition 4.14

(Energy density ratio lower bound II)
.

For any
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants

r_{3}=r_{3}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))\in(0,1),

\varepsilon_{8}=\varepsilon_{8}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{8}=c_{8}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0

such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{8}
,
x\in\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
with
|u(x)|\leq 1-b
, and
\varepsilon\leq r\leq r_{3}
, we have

I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\geq c_{8}.

In fact, the same holds for
x\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
provided furthermore that
r<\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.12
, the proposition holds for
\varepsilon\leq r\leq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
with
c_{8}=c_{6}
and
\varepsilon_{8}=\varepsilon_{6}
. The point is to use the almost monotonicity formula in Lemma
4.2
and
4.4
together with Proposition
4.13
to show that
I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)
cannot further decrease too much as
r
increases beyond the scale
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
to some definite radius
r_{3}
to be determined.

We first deal with the case
x\in\partial\Omega
. By the boundary almost monotonicity formula Lemma
4.4
together with (
4.28
), for
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{7}
we obtain for
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\leq\rho\leq\frac{1}{2}\min\{r_{2},i_{0},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\tilde{U})\}
,

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\omega_{n-1}e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)\right)\geq-\frac{c_{7}}{\rho^{7/8}}e^{c_{1}\rho}(\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)+1).

(4.30)

We define
r_{3}
as the supremum of all
r^{\prime}\in\left[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},\frac{1}{2}\min\{r_{2},i_{0},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\tilde{U})\}\right]
such that

e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{6}e^{c_{1}c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}}\text{ for all $\rho\in[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},r^{\prime}]$}.

Note that the left hand side is a continuous function of
\rho
. By Lemma
4.12
, we have
r_{3}>c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
. If
r_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\min\{r_{2},i_{0},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\tilde{U})\}
, then we are done by taking
\varepsilon_{8}=\min\{\varepsilon_{6},\varepsilon_{7}\}
and
c_{8}=\frac{1}{4}c_{6}e^{-c_{1}r_{2}}
(we further reduce
r_{3}
depending on
k_{0}
,
\kappa_{0}
,
C_{0}
and
\Lambda_{0}
according to the estimate (
4.27
)). Otherwise, by continuity we must have

e^{c_{1}r_{3}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r_{3},x)=\frac{1}{2}c_{6}e^{c_{1}c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}}.

(4.31)

By the definition of
r_{3}
and (
4.30
), we have for
\rho\in[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},r_{3}]

\frac{d}{d\rho}\log\left(e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)\right)\geq-C(n,c_{6},c_{7},r_{2})\rho^{-7/8}.

Integrating
\rho
over
[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},r_{3}]
, using (
4.31
) and Lemma
4.12
,

\log\frac{1}{2}\geq\log\left(\frac{e^{c_{1}r_{3}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r_{3},x)}{e^{c_{1}c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},x)}\right)\geq-8C(n,c_{6},c_{7},r_{2})r_{3}^{1/8}.

This gives the desired positive lower bound for
r_{3}
independent of
\varepsilon
and
x
. The proof for the interior case
x\in\Omega
is similar where we use Lemma
4.2
instead.
∎

We now proceed to establish an upper bound for the energy density ratio in terms of the uniform energy bound
E_{0}
, within the same range of radii as in Proposition
4.14
. Note that we do not require the center point
x
to lie inside the transition region
\{|u|\leq 1-b\}
.

Proposition 4.15

(Energy density ratio upper bound)
.

For any
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants

\varepsilon_{9}=\varepsilon_{9}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{9}=c_{9}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>1

such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{9}
,
x\in\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
, and
\varepsilon\leq r\leq r_{3}
, we have

I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq c_{9}(E_{0}+1).

In fact, the same holds for
x\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
provided furthermore that
r<\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

We only give the proof for
x\in\partial\Omega
as the interior case is similar. We first treat the case when
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\leq r\leq r_{3}
. Let
\delta_{0}:=\max\{1,e^{c_{1}r_{3}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r_{3},x)\}>0
. Note that

\delta_{0}\leq C(c_{1},n)r_{3}^{-(n-1)}E_{0}+C(c_{1},n)\Lambda_{0}C_{0}\leq C(c_{1},n,r_{3},\Lambda_{0},C_{0})(E_{0}+1).

(4.32)

Note that
e^{c_{1}r}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq\delta_{0}
clearly implies the desired upper bound of
I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)
. We can assume without loss of generality that
e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)\geq\delta_{0}
for all
\rho\in[r,r_{3}]
. Integrating (
4.30
) over
[r,r_{3}]
, we obtain as in Proposition
4.14
that

\log\left(\frac{e^{c_{1}r_{3}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r_{3},x)}{e^{c_{1}r}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)}\right)\geq-C(c_{7},n,c_{1},r_{3}).

This implies

\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq C(c_{7},n,c_{1},r_{3})\delta_{0}

which is the desired upper bound by (
4.32
).

It remains to treat the case
\varepsilon\leq r\leq c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}
. Integrating the almost monotonicity formula (
4.11
) over
[r,c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}]
and using the pointwise bound (
4.15
), we obtain

e^{c_{1}r}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\leq C(n,c_{1},r_{3},c_{6})+e^{c_{1}c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},x)

which yields the desire result as
\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},x)\leq c_{9}(E_{0}+1)
.
∎

We conclude this section with the following proposition which says that the energy density ratio is almost monotone modulo a term of order
r^{1/8}
for the range of radii in
[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},r_{3}]
.

Proposition 4.16

(Almost monotonicity of energy density ratio)
.

For any
b\in(0,1-\alpha)
, there exist constants

\varepsilon_{10}=\varepsilon_{10}(b,\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

c_{10}=c_{10}(b,E_{0},\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>1

such that for any
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{10}
,
x\in\partial\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
and
c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5}\leq s\leq r\leq r_{3}
, we have

I_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\geq e^{-c_{10}(r-s)}I_{\varepsilon,u}(s,x)-c_{10}r^{1/8};

(4.33)

In fact, the same holds for
x\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
provided furthermore that
r<\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

We again just provide the proof for the case
x\in\partial\Omega
. Using Proposition
4.15
and (
4.30
), for
\varepsilon<\min\{\varepsilon_{7},\varepsilon_{9}\}
, we have for
\rho\in[c_{6}\varepsilon^{4/5},r_{3}]
that

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(\rho,x)\right)\geq-C(c_{1},c_{7},c_{9},E_{0})\rho^{-7/8}.

Integrating
\rho
over
[s,r]
gives

\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(r,x)\geq e^{-c_{1}(r-s)}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon,u}(s,x)-C(c_{1},c_{7},c_{9},E_{0})r^{1/8},

from which (
4.33
) easily follows by (
4.27
) (since
r\leq r^{1/8}
).
∎

5.
Density ratio bounds and rectifiability of the limit varifold

In this section, we assume
u_{i}
,
\varepsilon_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}
are sequences given in Section
3.1
. Recall that the associated Radon measures
\mu_{i}
on
U
and the associated varifolds
V_{i}\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
are defined in (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) respectively. By the compactness of Radon measures, after possibly passing to a subsequence, there exists a Radon measure
\mu
on
U
and a varifold
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
such that

\mu_{i}\to\mu\qquad\text{ and }\qquad V_{i}\to V

in the sense of measures. Note that
\|V_{i}\|=\mu_{i}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\{|\nabla u_{i}|\neq 0\}}
from the definition and observe that the support (as measures on
U
) of both
\mu
and
\|V\|
is contained in
\overline{\Omega}\cap U
. The main result of this section is to show that
V
is rectifiable in
U
and that
\|V\|=\mu
. Note that the rectifiability of
V
in the interior of
\Omega
was established in
[
21
,
17
]
. Our main point here is to prove that the same continues to hold up to the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

5.1.
Density ratio bound

We first begin with a lemma which says that any point in
\operatorname{spt}\mu
can be approximated by a sequence of points
x_{i}
in the transition region
\{|u|\leq\alpha\}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For each
x\in\operatorname{spt}\mu
, there exists (after possibly passing to a subsequence) a sequence
x_{i}\in(\overline{\Omega}\cap U)\cap\{|u_{i}|\leq\alpha\}
such that
x_{i}\to x
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction as in the proof of Proposition 4.1 in
[
21
]
. Let
x\in\operatorname{spt}\mu
. We first assume
x\in\partial\Omega
. Suppose, on the contrary, that there exists some
\overline{r}>0
such that
B_{\overline{r}}(x)\Subset U
and
|u_{i}|>\alpha
on
B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega}
for all large
i
. By shrinking
\overline{r}
such that
B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega}
is connected and passing to a subsequence, we can suppose
u_{i}>\alpha
. By Lemma
4.8
(shrinking
\overline{r}
further if necessary) with a fixed
\eta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have for all large
i
that
|u_{i}-1|\leq c_{4}\varepsilon_{i}^{\eta}
and hence
\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}W(u_{i})=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{i}^{2\eta-1})
on
B_{\overline{r}/3}(x)\cap\Omega
. We have from Bochner formula (
2.3
) that for all large
i
(such that
(n-1)k_{0}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2\varepsilon_{i}}
)

\Delta\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}\right)\geq\frac{\kappa}{2\varepsilon_{i}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}.

Multiplying the above by any nonnegative
\phi\in C^{2}_{c}(B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\overline{\Omega})
and integrating by part, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\Omega}\phi\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}{\kappa}\int_{B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\Omega}(\Delta\phi)\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}{\kappa}\int_{B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\partial\Omega}\left(\varepsilon_{i}\phi B(\nabla u_{i},\nabla u_{i})-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}\right)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{E_{0}}{\kappa}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\|\phi\|_{C^{2}}+\frac{1+2\kappa_{0}}{\kappa}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\|\phi\|_{C^{1}}\int_{B_{\overline{r}}(x)\cap\partial\Omega}e_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

where
B
is the second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
. By the proof of Lemma
3.2
together with the estimates
\varepsilon_{i}^{-1}W(u_{i})=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2\eta-1})
with
\eta>1/2
and letting
i\to\infty
, we have
\mu(B_{\overline{r}/3}(x))=0
, contradicting that
x\in\operatorname{spt}\mu
. The case
x\notin\partial\Omega
is indeed simpler. We further shrink
\overline{r}
such that
B_{2\overline{r}}(x)\Subset\Omega\cap U
and there are no boundary terms when we integrate by part.
∎

As a key step towards proving the rectifiability of the limit varifold
V
, we now establish an up-to-the-boundary locally uniform density ratio upper bound for the limit measure
\mu
. Note that compared to
[
21
, Proposition 4.1]
), we do not claim at this stage that there is a uniform lower bound for the density ratio centered at a boundary point. This is due to the possible complication that the
x_{i}
’s in Lemma
5.1
could be a sequence of interior points approaching a boundary point
x\in\operatorname{spt}\mu\cap\partial\Omega
. This poses new difficulties when we want to apply monotonicity formulas which are either centered at a boundary point (Lemma
4.4
) or stays away from the boundary (Lemma
4.2
).

Proposition 5.2

(Density ratio bounds for the limit measure)
.

There exist constants

\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0,

\theta_{2}=\theta_{2}(E_{0},\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U))>0

such that for any
x\in(\operatorname{spt}\mu\cap\tilde{U})\cap\partial\Omega
, we have for all
0<r<\frac{r_{3}}{2}
,

\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\leq\theta_{2}.

(5.2)

Moreover, for any
x\in(\operatorname{spt}\mu\cap\tilde{U})\cap\Omega
, we have for all
0<r<\frac{1}{2}\min\{r_{3},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)\}
,

\theta_{1}\leq\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\leq\theta_{2}.

(5.3)

Here,
r_{3}
is the constant appearing in Proposition
4.14
with
b=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in(\operatorname{spt}\mu\cap\tilde{U})\cap\partial\Omega
. Fix any
0<r<\frac{r_{3}}{2}
. Since
\mu_{i}\to\mu
as measures, we will have for all large
i

\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\leq\frac{2\mu_{i}(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}=\frac{2}{h_{0}}I_{\varepsilon_{i},u_{i}}(r,x).

Hence, the desired assertion follows from Proposition
4.15
with
\theta_{2}:=\frac{2}{h_{0}}c_{9}(E_{0}+1)
.

For
x\in(\operatorname{spt}\mu\cap\tilde{U})\cap\Omega
. By Lemma
5.1
, after passing to a subsequence, we have a sequence of interior points
x_{i}\in\Omega\cap\tilde{U}
such that
|u_{i}(x_{i})|\leq\alpha
for all
i
and
x_{i}\to x
. Fix any
0<r<\frac{1}{2}\min\{r_{3},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(x,\partial\Omega)\}
. The upper bound
\theta_{2}
is obtained exactly as before. For the lower bound, we observe for all large
i

\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\geq\frac{\mu_{i}(B_{r}(x))}{2\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\geq\frac{\mu_{i}(B_{r/2}(x_{i}))}{2\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}h_{0}}I_{\varepsilon_{i},u_{i}}(r/2,x_{i}).

Hence, the desired assertion follows from Proposition
4.14
with
\theta_{1}:=\frac{c_{8}}{2^{n}h_{0}}
.
∎

Exactly the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
yields the following result.

Proposition 5.3

(Uniform convergence away from limit interface)
.

Either
u_{i}\to 1
or
u_{i}\to-1
locally uniformly on
(\Omega\cap U)\setminus\operatorname{spt}\|V\|
. In particular, we have
M:=\partial^{*}\{u=1\}\subset\operatorname{spt}\|V\|\cap(\Omega\cap U)
. Moreover,
\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}
and
\frac{W(u_{i})}{\varepsilon_{i}}
both converges locally uniformly to
0
on
(\Omega\cap U)\setminus\operatorname{spt}\|V\|
.

5.2.
Vanishing of discrepancy measure

Let
e_{i}:=e_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})
and
\xi_{i}:=\xi_{\varepsilon_{i}}(u_{i})
denote the energy density and discrepancy function as defined in (
4.3
) and (
4.4
). By compactness of Radon measures (on
U
), after possibly passing to a subsequence, we can assume that there exists a Radon measure
|\xi|
on
U
such that

|\xi_{i}|dv_{g}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\Omega\cap U}\to|\xi|

(5.4)

in the sense of measures. We call
|\xi|
the discrepancy measure. Since
|\xi_{i}|\leq e_{i}
, we clearly have
\operatorname{spt}|\xi|\subset\operatorname{spt}\mu\subset\overline{\Omega}\cap U
.

Theorem 5.4

(Vanishing of discrepancy measure)
.

The discrepancy measure
|\xi|
vanishes on
U
, i.e.
|\xi|=0
. In other words, the discrepancy function
\xi_{i}
converges to
0
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega}\cap U)
.

Proof.

We first claim that for all
x\in\operatorname{spt}|\xi|\cap\partial\Omega
, we have

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{|\xi|(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}=0.

(5.5)

We argue by contradiction as in
[
21
, Proposition 4.3]
. Suppose on the contrary that (
5.5
) does not hold, i.e. there exist
\delta_{0}>0
,
R_{0}>0
and
x_{0}\in\operatorname{spt}|\xi|\cap\partial\Omega
such that

|\xi|(B_{r}(x_{0}))\geq\delta_{0}\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}\quad\text{ for all $0<r\leq R_{0}$.}

(5.6)

By the density ratio upper bound (
5.2
), there exists
\theta_{2}>0
such that

\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\leq\theta_{2}\quad\text{ for all $0<r\leq\frac{r_{3}}{2}$.}

(5.7)

Fix
0<t<\min\{R_{0},\frac{r_{3}}{2},r_{1},r_{2},\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}}\}
which will be chosen later. Since
|\xi_{i}|dv_{g}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\Omega\cap U}\to|\xi|
and
\mu_{i}\to\mu
, by (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), for any fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
i>N
such that

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}|\xi_{i}|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\quad\text{ for all $r\in[t^{2},t]$}

(5.8)

and

\frac{\mu_{i}(B_{t}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n-1}t^{n-1}}\leq 2\theta_{2}.

(5.9)

Note that
|\xi_{i}|=\xi_{i}^{+}+\xi_{i}^{-}
. By (
5.8
) and Proposition
4.13
, for all
i
large enough (so that
\varepsilon_{i}<\varepsilon_{7}
and
c_{6}\varepsilon_{i}^{4/5}\leq t^{2}
) we have for all
\rho\in[t^{2},t]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+3\kappa_{0}\rho)\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}-\xi_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}|\xi_{i}|-\frac{2}{\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\xi_{i}^{+}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8}\rho^{-1}-\frac{2c_{7}}{\rho^{7/8}}(I_{\varepsilon_{i},u_{i}}(\rho,x_{0})+1).

Since
x_{0}\in\operatorname{spt}\mu
, by Proposition
4.15
, for all
i
large enough (so that
\varepsilon_{i}<\min\{\varepsilon_{9},t^{2}\}
), we have for all
\rho\in[t^{2},t]

\frac{1}{(1+3\kappa_{0}\rho)\rho^{n}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}-\xi_{i}\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8}\rho^{-1}-C(c_{7},c_{9},E_{0})\rho^{-7/8}.

(5.11)

By the almost monotonicity formula (
4.11
) (note that
\widetilde{\xi}_{i}\leq\xi_{i}
), we have for all
\rho\in[t^{2},t]

\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\omega_{n-1}e^{c_{1}\rho}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon_{i},u_{i}}(\rho,x_{0})\right)\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8}e^{c_{1}\rho}\rho^{-1}-C(c_{7},c_{9},E_{0})e^{c_{1}\rho}\rho^{-7/8}

Integrating over
[t^{2},t]
and using (
4.27
), (
5.9
), we obtain

C(n,c_{1},\Lambda_{0},C_{0})\left(\frac{h_{0}}{\omega_{n-1}}2\theta_{2}+t\right)\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{8}\log\frac{1}{t}-C(c_{7},c_{9},E_{0},c_{1})t^{1/8},

which gives a contradiction if
t
is chosen sufficiently small. Hence, we have proved (
5.5
).

By the theory of differentiation for Radon measures (applied to
|\xi|
and
d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\partial\Omega}
), c.f.
[
36
, P.26]
, we conclude from (
5.5
) that
|\xi|(\partial\Omega)=0
, i.e.
|\xi|
vanishes on
\partial\Omega
. By a similar argument using the interior monotonicity formula (
4.9
), we can show that (
5.5
) also holds for interior points
x\in\operatorname{spt}|\xi|\cap\Omega
. Combining this with the density lower bound in (
5.3
), we have

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{|\xi|(B_{r}(x))}{\mu(B_{r}(x))}=0,

from which again implies
|\xi|=0
inside
\Omega\cap U
as well.
∎

5.3.
Rectifiability of limit varifold

Let
V_{i}
be the varifold associated with
u_{i}
as in (
3.5
). After passing to a subsequence, we can also assume that there exist
u\in BV(\Omega\cap U)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
such that

\lambda_{i}\to\lambda,\quad u_{i}\to u\;a.e.,\quad V_{i}\to V\;\textrm{in the varifold sense.}

Our goal is to show that
V
is in fact rectifiable up to the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Using the vanishing of the discrepancy measure
|\xi|
, we can establish the following result. Recall that
M:=\partial^{*}\{u=1\}
is the reduced boundary of
\{u=1\}
in
\Omega\cap U
with outward normal
\nu_{M}
.

Lemma 5.5

(First variation of limit varifold)
.

There exist a constant

c_{11}=c_{11}(E_{0},\Lambda_{0},C_{0},W,n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},\operatorname{dist}_{g}(\tilde{U},\partial U),dv_{g}(\Omega\cap U),\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega\cap U))>1

such that for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U)
with
\operatorname{spt}X\subset\tilde{U}
, we have

\left|h_{0}\delta V(X)+\lambda\int_{\Omega\cap U}X\cdot Du\right|\leq c_{11}\sup_{\partial\Omega\cap U}|X\cdot\nu|.

(5.12)

In particular, for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}^{tan}_{c}(U)
, we have

\delta V(X)=-\frac{2\lambda}{h_{0}}\int_{M}X\cdot\nu_{M}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(5.13)

Remark 5.6
.

If
\lambda=0
, then (
5.13
) implies that
V
is a free boundary stationary varifold (c.f.
[
16
, Definition 3.1]
).

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(U)
with
\operatorname{spt}X\subset\tilde{U}
. Note that
\delta V_{i}(X)\to\delta V(X)
as
i\to\infty
. On the other hand, by the calculations in
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have

\displaystyle h_{0}\delta V_{i}(X)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap\{|\nabla u_{i}|\neq 0\}}\nabla X\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla u_{i}}{|\nabla u_{i}|}\otimes\frac{\nabla u_{i}}{|\nabla u_{i}|}\right)\xi_{i}-\int_{\Omega\cap\{|\nabla u_{i}|=0\}}(\nabla X\cdot I)\frac{W(u_{i})}{\varepsilon_{i}}

(5.14)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\operatorname{div}_{g}X+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{i}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}}{2}+\frac{W(u_{i})}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\lambda_{i}u_{i}\right)(X\cdot\nu)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Note that the first and second term of the (R.H.S.) above are both bounded by
C(n)\|X\|_{C^{1}}\int_{\tilde{U}\cap\Omega}|\xi_{i}|
, which converges to
0
as
i\to\infty
by Theorem
5.4
. For the third term, since
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda
and
u_{i}\to u\in BV(\Omega\cap U)
a.e., by Dominated Convergence theorem we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}\int_{\Omega}u_{i}\operatorname{div}_{g}X=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{div}_{g}X=-\lambda\int_{\Omega}X\cdot Du+\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}Tu(X\cdot\nu)d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

where
Tu\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
is the trace of
u
on
\partial\Omega
(c.f.
[
13
, Theorem 5.6]
). Moreover, by the boundednesss of the trace operator
T
, we have

\|Tu\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega\cap U)}\leq C(n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0})\|u\|_{BV(\Omega\cap U)}\leq C(n,i_{0},k_{0},\kappa_{0},E_{0},W,dv_{g}(\Omega\cap U)).

Combining all the above and applying Lemma
3.2
for the last term gives (
5.12
).
∎

Theorem 5.7

(Rectifiability of limit varifold)
.

The limit varifold
V\in\mathbf{V}_{n-1}(U)
satisfies
\|V\|=\mu
and is rectifiable.

Proof.

Recall that
\|V_{i}\|=\mu_{i}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\{|\nabla u_{i}|\neq 0\}}
and since
h_{0}\mu_{i}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\{|\nabla u_{i}|=0\}}\leq|\xi_{i}|dv_{g}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}{\Omega\cap U}
which converges to
0
by Theorem
5.4
, we have
\|V_{i}\|\to\mu
and thus
\|V\|=\mu
. From (
5.12
), we know that
V
has locally bounded first variation in
U
. By Allard’s Rectifiability Theorem
[
36
, Theorem 42.4]
, it remains to show that
\|V\|=\mu
has a locally uniform density lower bound on
\operatorname{spt}\|V\|
.

We claim that
V
has free boundary in
\partial\Omega\cap U
as described in Section
2.4
. Recall
M=\partial^{*}\{u=1\}\subset\Omega\cap U
. By the structure theorem for sets of finite perimeter (c.f.
[
13
, Theorem 5.15]
,
\|\partial\{u=1\}\|=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}M
which is a Radon measure on
U
by (
3.3
). Consider the Radon measures
\mu=\|V\|
and
\sigma:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}M
on
U
, the density ratio lower bound (
5.3
) implies (c.f.
[
13
, Section 1.6]
) that
\sigma
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
. Together with Lemma
5.5
, the Radon-Nikodym Theorem implies that

\delta V(X)=-\frac{2\lambda}{h_{0}}\int_{\Omega\cap U}X\cdot\nu_{M}d\sigma=-\frac{2\lambda}{h_{0}}\int_{\Omega\cap U}(X\cdot\nu_{M})D_{\mu}\sigma d\mu

for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}^{tan}(U)
. Therefore,
V
has free boundary in
\partial\Omega\cap U
with
\|V\|
-measurable vector field

A Characterization for Tightness of the Sparse Moment-SOS Hierarchy
Jiawang Nie, Zheng Qu, Xindong Tang, Linghao Zhang

Abstract
. This paper studies the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of relaxations for solving sparse polynomial optimization problems. We show that this sparse hierarchy is tight if and only if the objective can be written as a sum of sparse nonnegative polynomials, each of which belongs to the sum of the ideal and quadratic module generated by the corresponding sparse constraints. Based on this characterization, we give several sufficient conditions for the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy to be tight. In particular, we show that this sparse hierarchy is tight under some assumptions such as convexity, optimality conditions or finiteness of constraining sets.

Introduction

Let
x\coloneqq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be an
n
-dimensional vector variable in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Suppose
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{m}
are subsets of
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
For
\Delta_{i}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n_{i}}\}
, denote the subvector

x_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq(x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{n_{i}}}).

We consider the sparse polynomial optimization problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f(x)\coloneqq f_{1}(x_{\Delta_{1}})+\dots+f_{m}(x_{\Delta_{m}})\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&h_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0,\,g_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0,\,i=1,\ldots,m.\\
\end{array}\right.

In the above, each
f_{i}
is a polynomial and
h_{i},g_{i}
are vectors of polynomials in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.
We remark that for each
i
, the dimensions for the polynomial vectors
h_{i}
and
g_{i}
are not necessarily equal. It is also possible that

or
g_{i}
does not appear for some
i
.
Throughout the paper, a minimizer for (
1.1
)
means it is a global minimizer.
We denote by

the minimum value of (
1.1
) and denote

(1.2)

K_{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{x_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}:h_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0,g_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0\}.

The feasible set
K
of (
1.1
) is

K=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{m}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{\Delta_{i}}\in K_{\Delta_{i}}\}.

General polynomial optimization problems can be solved by

The Moment-SOS hierarchy has strong performance for solving polynomial optimization.
However, a concern in its computational practice is that the sizes

To improve computational efficiency, it is important to exploit sparsity.
In some literature, the sparsity pattern in (

The sets

Contributions

This paper characterizes tightness of the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy,
which is given in (
3.2
)-(
3.3
).
Our main contributions are:

•

We give a sufficient and necessary condition for the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy to be tight.
More precisely, when the optimal value of (
3.2
) is achievable,
we show that
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{\min}
if and only if there exist polynomials

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that (see Section
2
for the meaning of notation below)

(1.4)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\in\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k},\ i\in[m].\end{gathered}}

We remark that the first equation in (
1.4
) is equivalent to

f-f_{\min}\,=\,(f_{1}+p_{1})+\cdots+(f_{m}+p_{m}).

•

Under certain conditions, we show that the tightness of sparse Moment-SOS relaxations can be certified when flat truncations hold for the minimizer of the sparse moment relaxation (
3.3
).
This also gives minimizers for (
1.1
).

•

For convex sparse polynomial optimization problems, we show that the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight under some general conditions. In particular, we show that the sparse relaxations are tight for all relaxation orders, when (
1.1
) is an SOS-convex optimization problem.

•

Based on the characterization, we prove the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight when some classical sufficient optimality conditions hold or when each individual equality constraining variety is finite. In particular, when the RIP holds, we show that this sparse hierarchy is tight if each individual constraining variety is finite.

•

Based on the characterization, we prove the Schmüdgen type sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight under some assumptions. In particular, we show that this sparse hierarchy is tight when the RIP holds and each individual constraining set is finite.

•

We remark that this paper is the first work that characterizes tightness of
the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy.
Also, we give a sparse version of the flat truncation condition to detect tightness and to extract minimizers for (
1.1
).
It is generally hard to check tightness of the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy
[
35
]
.
There are almost no such results in the prior existing work, except for the case that moment matrices corresponding to intersections of blocks are rank one
[
17
]
.
Moreover, we give several sufficient conditions for the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy to be tight.
For the dense case, the analogues of these sufficient conditions are studied in
[
5
,
18
,
30
,
31
]
.
However, the new sufficient conditions given in this paper are not studied in earlier work because the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy requires additional assumptions for tightness.
In Section
4
, we show that these new sufficient conditions are satisfied for many cases; see, for instance, Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
.
We acknowledge that some of our results (e.g., Theorems
3.2
,
3.5
,
4.4
,
4.5
,
5.2
) apply some techniques developed in the first author’s earlier work
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
.

.
In Section

, we give a characterization for the tightness of sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy,

2.1.
Ideals and SOS polynomials

A tuple
h_{i}\coloneqq(h_{i,1},\ldots,h_{i,\ell_{i}})

of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
generates
two ideals respectively in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
and
\mathbb{R}[x]
as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]&\coloneqq&h_{i,1}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]+\cdots+h_{i,\ell_{i}}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}],\\
\operatorname{Ideal}[h_{i}]&\coloneqq&h_{i,1}\mathbb{R}[x]+\cdots+h_{i,\ell_{i}}\mathbb{R}[x].\end{array}

The degree-
2k
truncation of
\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]
is

\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}\coloneqq h_{i,1}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k-\deg(h_{i,1})}+\cdots+h_{i,\ell_{i}}\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k-\deg(h_{i,\ell_{i}})}.

The truncation
\operatorname{Ideal}[h_{i}]_{2k}
is similarly defined.
For
h\coloneqq(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})
, with each
h_{i}
a tuple of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
,
we denote

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{1}}[h_{1}]+\cdots+\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{m}}[h_{m}],\\
\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{1}}[h_{1}]_{2k}+\cdots+\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{m}}[h_{m}]_{2k}.\end{array}\right.

For a set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
, its
real variety
is

V_{\mathbb{R}}(P)\coloneqq\{x_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Delta_{i}}:p(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0\,\,\forall p\in P\}.

The
vanishing ideal
of a subset
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\Delta_{i}}
is

I(V_{i})\coloneqq\{p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]:p(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0\,\,\forall x_{\Delta_{i}}\in V_{i}\}.

Clearly,
P\subseteq I(V_{\mathbb{R}}(P))
.
An ideal
J
is said to be

real radical
if
J=I(V_{\mathbb{R}}(J))
.

A polynomial
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
is said to be a
sum of squares
(SOS)
if there exist polynomials
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

\sigma=p_{1}^{2}+\cdots+p_{s}^{2}
.
The cone of all SOS polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
is denoted as

\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
, and its degree-
2k
truncation is

\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}\coloneqq\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]\cap\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}.

We define
\Sigma[x]
and
\Sigma[x]_{2k}
similarly.
A tuple
g_{i}\coloneqq(g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,s_{i}})

of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
generates
the quadratic modules in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
and
\mathbb{R}[x]
respectively as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]&\coloneqq&\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]+g_{i,1}\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}]+\cdots+g_{i,s_{i}}\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}],\\
\operatorname{QM}[g_{i}]&\coloneqq&\Sigma[x]+g_{i,1}\Sigma[x]+\cdots+g_{i,s_{i}}\Sigma[x].\end{array}

The preorderings of
g_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
and
\mathbb{R}[x]
are respectively

\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]\,\coloneqq\sum_{J\subseteq[s_{i}]}\prod_{j\in J}g_{i,j}\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}],\quad\operatorname{Pre}[g_{i}]\,\coloneqq\sum_{J\subseteq[s_{i}]}\prod_{j\in J}g_{i,j}\Sigma[x].

The product in the above is
1
if
J
is empty.
For an even degree
2k
, we denote the truncation (
g_{i,0}=1
)

\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k}\coloneqq\Big\{\sum_{j=0}^{s_{i}}\sigma_{j}g_{i,j}:\sigma_{j}\in\Sigma[x_{\Delta_{i}}],\deg(\sigma_{j}g_{i,j})\leq 2k\Big\}.

The truncation
\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k}
is defined similarly.
For
g\coloneqq(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
with each
g_{i}

a tuple of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
, we denote

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{1}}[g_{1}]+\cdots+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{m}}[g_{m}],\\
\operatorname{Pre}[g]_{spa}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{1}}[g_{1}]+\ldots+\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{m}}[g_{m}],\\
\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{1}}[g_{1}]_{2k}+\cdots+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{m}}[g_{m}]_{2k},\\
\operatorname{Pre}[g]_{spa,2k}&\coloneqq&\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{1}}[g_{1}]_{2k}+\ldots+\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{m}}[g_{m}]_{2k}.\end{array}\right.

The set
\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}{[h_{i}]}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}{[g_{i}]}
is said to be
archimedean

if it contains a polynomial
q
such that
the set of all points
x_{\Delta_{i}}
satisfying
q(x_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0
is compact.
When
\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}{[h_{i}]}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}{[g_{i}]}
is archimedean,
the set
K_{\Delta_{i}}
must be compact.
The converse is not necessarily true.
However, if
K_{\Delta_{i}}
is compact, i.e., there exists
R>0
such that

\|x_{\Delta_{i}}\|^{2}\leq R
for all
x_{\Delta_{i}}\in K_{\Delta_{i}}
, then

\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}{[h_{i}]}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i},R-\|x_{\Delta_{i}}\|^{2}]
is archimedean.
The following lemma is useful.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f=f_{1}+\dots+f_{m}
be such that each
f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
and let

G=G_{1}+\cdots+G_{m}
be such that each

G_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
.
Then, for a given
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds
f-\gamma\in G

if and only if there exist polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

(2.3)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+\gamma=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\in G_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,m.\end{gathered}}

Proof.

The “if” implication is straightforward.
For the “only if” implication, suppose

f-\gamma\in G,

then there exist
s_{i}\in G_{i}
satisfying

(2.4)

f-\gamma=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}.

For each
i
, let

p_{i}\,\coloneqq\,s_{i}-f_{i}.

Since
f_{i},s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
, we have
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
and

\displaystyle p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}-(f_{1}+\cdots+f_{m})

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}-f=-\gamma.

Therefore,
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+\gamma=0
and
f_{i}+p_{i}=s_{i}\in G_{i}
for all
i
.
So, (
2.3
) holds.
∎

The feasible set of the sparse optimization problem (
1.1
) is

(2.5)

K=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}h_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0,\ g_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0,\\
i=1,\ldots,m\end{array}\right.\right\}.

The Positivstellensatz (see
[

]
) for

is

(2.6)

1+\sigma+\phi=0,

where

and

.
The Positivstellensatz (

.

Let

be the set as in (

Then a sparse version of Positivstellensatz (

)
holds if and only if there exist polynomials

such that

\boxed{\begin{array}[]{c}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}=0,\\
-1+p_{i}\in\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]+\operatorname{Pre}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}],\,\,i\in[m].\end{array}}

Proof.

The conclusion follows from Lemma

with

,

2.2.
Sparse moments

Denote the set of monomial powers in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
as

\mathbb{N}^{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\alpha_{j}=0\,\,\forall j\notin\Delta_{i}\}.

For a degree
d
, denote

\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\Delta_{i}}:|\alpha|\leq d\},

where
|\alpha|\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.
The vector of all monomials in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
in the graded lexicographic order
with degrees up to
d
is denoted as
[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{d}
, i.e.,

(2.8)

[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{d}\,=\,\big(x^{\alpha}\big)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}

the space of all real vectors labeled by
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}
.
A vector
y_{\Delta_{i}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}

is called a truncated multi-sequence (tms) of degree
d
.
For a given
y_{\Delta_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}}
,
the
Riesz functional
generated by
y_{\Delta_{i}}

is the linear functional
{R}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}
acting on
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that

{R}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}(x^{\alpha})\,=\,(y_{\Delta_{i}})_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{for each}\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{d}^{\Delta_{i}}.

This induces the bilinear operation

(2.9)

\langle p,y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle\coloneqq{R}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}(p).

The
localizing vector
and the
localizing matrix
of
p\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
,
generated by
y_{\Delta_{i}}
, are respectively

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{V}_{p}^{\Delta_{i},2k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]&\coloneqq&{R}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}\left(p(x_{\Delta_{i}})[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{k_{1}}\right),\\
L_{p}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]&\coloneqq&{R}_{y_{\Delta_{i}}}\left(p(x_{\Delta_{i}})[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{k_{2}}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]^{T}_{k_{2}}\right).\end{array}

In some literature, the localizing matrix
L_{p}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]
is also denoted as
M_{k-\lceil\deg(p)/2\rceil}(py_{\Delta_{i}})
; see
[
17
,
19
,
24
,
40
]
.
In the above, the Riesz functional is applied entry-wise and

k_{1}\coloneqq 2k-\deg(p),\quad k_{2}\coloneqq\lfloor k-\deg(p)/2\rfloor.

In particular, when
p=1
is the constant polynomial, we get the
moment matrix

M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\coloneqq L_{1}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}].

Recall that
h_{i}=(h_{i,1},\ldots,h_{i,\ell_{i}})
and
g_{i}\coloneqq(g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,s_{i}})
are tuples of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
. We denote

\begin{array}[]{rcl}{V}_{h_{i}}^{\Delta_{i},2k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]&\coloneqq&({V}_{h_{i,1}}^{\Delta_{i},2k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}],\ldots,{V}_{h_{i,\ell_{i}}}^{\Delta_{i},2k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]),\\
L_{g_{i}}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]&\coloneqq&\mbox{diag}\left(L_{g_{i,1}}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}],\ldots,L_{g_{i,s_{i}}}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}]\right).\end{array}

For a given
k
, denote the monomial power set

(2.10)

\mathbb{U}_{k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in[m]}\mathbb{N}_{2k}^{\Delta_{i}}.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}
denote the space of real vectors

y=(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{U}_{k}}.

For given

, we denote the subvector

(2.11)

y_{\Delta_{i}}\,\coloneqq\,(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{2k}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

For the objective
f
in (
1.1
), we denote

(2.12)

\langle f,y\rangle\,\coloneqq\,\langle f_{1},y_{\Delta_{1}}\rangle+\cdots+\langle f_{m},y_{\Delta_{m}}\rangle.

For a degree
t\leq k
, the
2t
-degree truncation of
y_{\Delta_{i}}
is the subvector

(2.13)

y_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}\,\coloneqq\,(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{2t}^{\Delta_{i}}}.

The sparse localizing vectors/matrices are denoted as

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2k}[y]&\coloneqq&{V}_{h_{i}}^{\Delta_{i},2k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}],\\
L_{g_{i}}^{spa,k}[y]&\coloneqq&L_{g_{i}}^{\Delta_{i},k}[y_{\Delta_{i}}],\\
M^{(k)}_{spa}[y]&\coloneqq&\mbox{diag}\left(M_{\Delta_{1}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{1}}],\ldots,M_{\Delta_{m}}^{(k)}[y_{\Delta_{m}}]\right).\end{array}\right.

We refer to
[

,
19
,
21
,
32
]

for more detailed introductions to polynomial optimization.

3.
The characterization for tightness

In this section, we characterize when the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight
for solving (
1.1
).
Let

(3.1)

k_{0}\coloneqq\max_{i\in[m]}\left\{\lceil\deg(f)/2\rceil,\lceil\deg(g_{i})/2\rceil,\lceil\deg(h_{i})/2\rceil\right\}.

For a degree
k\geq k_{0}
, the
k
th order sparse SOS relaxation for (
1.1
) is

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}f_{k}^{spa}\coloneqq&\max&\gamma\\
&\mathrm{s.t.}&f-\gamma\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}.\end{array}\right.

Its dual optimization problem is the sparse moment relaxation

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}f_{k}^{smo}\coloneqq&\min&\langle f,y\rangle=\langle f_{1},y_{\Delta_{1}}\rangle+\cdots+\langle f_{m},y_{\Delta_{m}}\rangle\\
&\mathrm{s.t.}&{V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2k}[y]=0,L_{g_{i}}^{spa,k}[y]\succeq 0\ (i\in[m]),\\
&&M_{spa}^{(k)}[y]\succeq 0,y_{0}=1,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{U}_{k}}.\end{array}\right.

We refer to Subsections
2.1
and
2.2

for the above notation.
Recall that
f_{\min}
denotes the minimum value of (
1.1
).
When the RIP holds, if each
\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}{[h_{i}]}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}{[g_{i}]}
is archimedean,
then
f_{k}^{spa}\rightarrow f_{\min}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,
as shown in
[
17
]
.
When
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{\min}
for some
k
, the hierarchy of (
3.2
)
is said to be tight. Similarly, if
f_{k}^{smo}=f_{\min}
for some
k
,
then the hierarchy of (
3.3
) is tight.
If they are both tight, the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is said to be tight,
or to have finite convergence.

3.1.
Characterization for the tightness

First, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for the sparse SOS hierarchy
of (
3.2
) to be tight.
We remark that the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
)
is tight if and only if the sparse SOS hierarchy of (
3.2
) is tight.
The following is the main theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
), we have:

(i)

For a relaxation order
k\geq k_{0}
, it holds

(3.4)

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}

if and only if there exist polynomials

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
such that

(3.5)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\in\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k},\ i\in[m].\end{gathered}}

The equation in the above is equivalent to that

f-f_{\min}\,=\,(f_{1}+p_{1})+\cdots+(f_{m}+p_{m}).

(ii)

When (
3.5
) holds for some order
k
,
the minimum value
f_{\min}
of (
1.1
)
is achievable if and only if all sparse polynomials
f_{i}+p_{i}

have a common zero in
K
, i.e., there exists
u\in K
such that

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0
for all
i\in[m]
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\gamma=f_{\min}
,

G=\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}
and

G_{i}=\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k},\,\,i=1,\ldots,m.

Then, the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.1
.

(ii) Assume (
3.5
) holds for some order
k
.

(
\Rightarrow
): Suppose
f_{\min}
is achievable for (
1.1
),
then there exists a minimizer
u\in K
such that
f_{\min}=f(u)
. Note

\displaystyle f(x)-f_{\min}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big[f_{\min}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})]

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}.

Since
p_{1}+\dots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}))=0.

Since each
f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0
on
K_{\Delta_{i}}
,
we have

f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=0

for all
i\in[m]
. Therefore,

u\in K
is a common zero of all
f_{i}+p_{i}
.

(
\Leftarrow
): Suppose
u\in K
is a common zero of all
f_{i}+p_{i}
, then

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=f(u)+p_{1}(u_{\Delta_{1}})+\cdots+p_{m}(u_{\Delta_{m}})=0.

Since
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0
,

f(u)=f_{\min}
, so
f_{\min}
is achievable.
∎

Theorem
3.1
gives a sufficient and necessary condition
for the membership (
3.4
),
which implies the tightness
f^{spa}_{k}=f_{\min}
.
When the optimal value of (
3.2
) is achievable,

f^{spa}_{k}=f_{\min}
if and only if (
3.4
) holds.
When (
3.2
) does not achieve its optimal value,
we may not have (
3.4
),
even if the relaxation (
3.2
) is tight.
For such cases, we have the following characterization
for the tightness
f^{spa}_{k}=f_{\min}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The
k
th order sparse SOS relaxation (
3.2
)
is tight (i.e.,
f_{\min}=f^{spa}_{k}
)
if and only if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exist
polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
such that

(3.6)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}+\epsilon\in\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k},\ i\in[m].\end{gathered}}

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
): Suppose (
3.6
) holds for some polynomials

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}
, then

\displaystyle f-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big[f_{\min}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}\Big]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}+p_{i}+\epsilon)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}.

This means that
\gamma=f_{\min}-m\epsilon
is feasible for the
k
th order sparse SOS relaxation
(
3.2
), so
f_{k}^{spa}\geq f_{\min}-m\epsilon
.
Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we get
f_{k}^{spa}\geq f_{\min}
.
On the other hand, we always have
f_{k}^{spa}\leq f_{\min}
,
so
f_{k}^{spa}=f_{\min}
.

(
\Rightarrow
): For an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
, let

f^{\epsilon}_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\,\coloneqq\,f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+\epsilon.

Suppose the
k
th order relaxation (
3.2
) is tight.
Then, every
\gamma<f_{\min}=f_{k}^{spa}

is feasible for (
3.2
).
This is because for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a feasible
\hat{\gamma}

such that
\hat{\gamma}>f_{\min}-m\epsilon
.
So,
f-\hat{\gamma}\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}
and

f-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)=f-\hat{\gamma}+[\hat{\gamma}-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)]\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k},

since
\hat{\gamma}-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)>0
.
Thus,
\gamma=f_{\min}-m\epsilon
is feasible for (
3.2
), and

f(x)-(f_{\min}-m\epsilon)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f^{\epsilon}_{i}-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}.

We apply Lemma
2.1
with
\gamma=f_{\min}
and
f_{i}=f_{i}^{\epsilon}
,
then there exist polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]_{2k}

such that
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0
and for all
i
,

f_{i}^{\epsilon}+p_{i}=f_{i}+p_{i}+\epsilon\in\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]_{2k}+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]_{2k}.

So, (
3.6
) holds.
∎

3.2.
Detecting tightness and extracting minimizers

We now discuss how to detect tightness of sparse Moment-SOS relaxations
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
).
The tightness of (
3.2
) can be detected
if there exists a feasible point
u

such that

; similarly,
the tightness of (
3.3
)
can be detected by

.
This issue is related to how to extract a minimizer
for (

) from the sparse moment relaxations.

For each

, denote the degrees

Suppose

is a minimizer of (
3.3
)
for a relaxation order

.
To extract a minimizer, we typically need to consider a representing measure

.
Suppose there exists a degree

such that

(3.8)

\boxed{\begin{gathered}{y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}=\lambda_{i,1}[u^{(i,1)}]_{2t}+\cdots+\lambda_{i,r_{i}}[u^{(i,r_{i})}]_{2t},}\\
\lambda_{i,1}>0,\ldots,\lambda_{i,r_{i}}>0,\quad\lambda_{i,1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i,r_{i}}=1\end{gathered}}

for some points
u^{(i,j)}\in K_{\Delta_{i}}
. We refer to
(
2.8
), (
2.11
) and (
2.13
)
for the above notation. Denote the set

(3.9)

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose

is a minimizer of (
3.3
) and there exists
t\in[k_{0},k]
such that (
3.8
) holds for all
i\in[m]
.
Let
x^{*}=(x_{1}^{*},\,\cdots,\,x_{n}^{*})
be a point such that each

.
If
f_{t}^{smo}=f_{k}^{smo}
, then

is a minimizer of (

The proof of Theorem
3.3
is given in Subsection

.
For the dense Moment-SOS hierarchy (i.e.,

),
the tightness is guaranteed if there exists a degree

such that (
3.8
) holds.
This is because for the dense case, we have (let

,

and

for notational convenience)

which implies

However, for the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of (

)-(

) holds for all

Example 3.4
.

Consider the following sparse polynomial optimization problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}&f(x)=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\\

.
Then, the sparse optimization problem (
1.1
)
can be split into a union of smaller sized problems that do not have mutually joint variables.

4.
Some sufficient conditions for tightness

This section gives some sufficient conditions for
the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy to be tight.
The sparse hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
)
is tight if and only if (
3.5
) or (
3.6
) holds for some
k
.
This leads to the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

There exist polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
satisfying

(4.1)

\boxed{\begin{array}[]{c}\ p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,\\
f_{i}+p_{i}\geq 0~\text{on}~K_{\Delta_{i}},\,i=1,\ldots,m.\end{array}}

For the dense case (i.e.,
m=1
), Assumption
4.1
holds automatically for
p_{1}=-f_{\min}

if
-\infty<f_{\min}<+\infty
(note that
f_{\min}=+\infty
if
K=\emptyset
).
However, it may not hold for the sparse case. We refer to Example
6.7
for such an exposition.
Besides that, we remark that (
4.1
) does not imply

f_{i}+p_{i}\in\mbox{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]
.
This section explores various conditions for Assumption
4.1
to hold
and for the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
) to be tight.

4.1.
The convex case

We consider the case that (
1.1
) is a convex optimization problem.
For each
i=1,\ldots,m
, we write that

(4.2)

h_{i}=(h_{i,1},\ldots,h_{i,\ell_{i}}),\quad g_{i}=(g_{i,1},\ldots,g_{i,s_{i}}).

Assume each
h_{i}
is linear (or it does not appear),
all
f_{i}
and
-g_{i,j}
are convex,
and
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).
Under certain constraint qualification like Slater’s condition
(i.e., there is a feasible point
x
for (
1.1
) such that all
g_{i}(x)>0
),
there exist Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{i,j}
and
\nu_{i,j}
such that
(
\perp
means that the product is
0
)

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{gathered}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}\nabla g_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\nu_{i,j}\nabla h_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\Big],\\
0\leq\lambda_{i,j}\perp g_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0,\ i=1,\ldots,m,\ j=1,\ldots,s_{i}.\end{gathered}\right.

This is called the first order optimality condition (FOOC).
By Theorem
3.1
, if Assumption
4.1
is satisfied with polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
such that every

is convex,
then the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight under certain conditions; see
[
32
, Chapter 7.2]
.
Interestingly, when (
1.1
) has a minimizer satisfying the FOOC (
4.3
),
such polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
always exist.
This is shown as follows.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that each
h_{i}
is linear (or it does not appear),
all
f_{i}
and
-g_{i,j}
are convex,
and
u
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).
If (
4.3
) holds,
then Assumption
4.1
holds.
Furthermore, if in addition, the sub-Hessian

\nabla_{\Delta_{i}}^{2}\bigg[f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}g_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\bigg]

is positive definite and each
\operatorname{Ideal}_{\Delta_{i}}[h_{i}]+\operatorname{QM}_{\Delta_{i}}[g_{i}]

is archimedean, then the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is tight.

Proof.

For each
i=1,\ldots,m
, let

p_{i}(x)\coloneqq-(x-u)^{T}\big[\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}\nabla g_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\nu_{i,j}\nabla h_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})\big]-f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}}).

Since
f_{i}
,
g_{i}
,
h_{i}
only depend on
x_{\Delta_{i}}
,
we have
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\Delta_{i}}]
. Note

f_{1}(u_{\Delta_{1}})+\cdots+f_{m}(u_{\Delta_{m}})=f_{\min}.

The equation in (
4.3
) and the above choice of
p_{i}
imply

p_{1}+\cdots+p_{m}+f_{\min}=0,

\nabla f_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+\nabla p_{i}(u_{\Delta_{i}})=\sum_{j=1}^{s_{i}}\lambda_{i,j}\nabla g_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}})+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\nu_{i,j}\nabla h_{i,j}(u_{\Delta_{i}}),

so
u_{\Delta_{i}}
satisfies the FOOC for

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x_{\Delta_{i}}}&f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&h_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})=0,\,g_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Since
p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})
is linear,
f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})+p_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}})

is also convex in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
,
hence
u_{\Delta_{i}}
is a minimizer of (
4.4
).
Its minimum value is
0
, so
f_{i}+p_{i}\geq 0
on
K_{\Delta_{i}}
.
Therefore, Assumption
4.1
holds.
The tightness of the hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
)
follows from Theorem
3.1
and
[
5
, Corollary 3.3]

(or
[
32
, Theorem 7.2.5]
).
∎

Theorems
3.3
and
3.5

can be applied to get minimizers of (
1.1
).
Note that a convex optimization problem typically has a unique optimizer,
or otherwise it has infinitely many optimizers.
For this reason, when the conditions (
3.8
)-(
3.9
)
and/or (

)-(
3.16
) hold,
we typically have

for all

.
For such cases, if
y^{*}
is a minimizer of (
3.3
), the point

is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

Next, we consider the special case that the defining polynomials of (
1.1
) are SOS-convex.
Recall that a polynomial
\phi(x)
is SOS-convex
if its Hessian
\nabla^{2}\phi(x)=H(x)^{T}H(x)

for some matrix polynomial
H(x)
.
We refer to
[
32
, Chap. 7]
for SOS-convex polynomials.
For the dense case (i.e.,

), when

and all

are SOS-convex and

is linear (or it does not appear),
the dense Moment-SOS relaxations are tight for all

; see
[
18
]
or
[
32
, Section 7.2]
.
In the following, we show that the sparse Moment-SOS relaxations
(

)-(

) are also tight for all
k\geq k_{0}

and the condition (
3.5
) holds, under the SOS-convexity assumption.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume the feasible set
K\neq\emptyset
and
the minimum value
f_{\min}>-\infty
.
Suppose each
h_{i}
is linear or it does not appear, each
f_{i}
and
-g_{i,j}

are SOS-convex in
x_{\Delta_{i}}
.
Then, we have:

(i)

For all
k\geq k_{0}
,
f^{smo}_{k}=f_{\min}
.
Moreover, if Slater’s condition holds, then
f^{spa}_{k}=f_{\min}
and

(4.5)

f-f_{\min}\in\operatorname{Ideal}[h]_{spa,2k}+\operatorname{QM}[g]_{spa,2k}.

(ii)

For every minimizer
y^{*}
of (
3.3
), the point

x^{*}=(y^{*}_{e_{1}},\ldots,y^{*}_{e_{n}})

is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

Proof.

Suppose
y
is feasible for the relaxation (
3.3
)
and let
v=(y_{e_{1}},\ldots,y_{e_{n}})
. Since they are SOS-convex,
by Jensen’s inequality (see
[
18
]
or
[
32
, Chap. 7]
), we have

f_{i}(v_{\Delta_{i}})\leq\langle f_{i},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle,\quad-g_{i,j}(v_{\Delta_{i}})\leq-\langle g_{i,j},y_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

When

is linear,

.
The feasibility constraint in (

) implies that

, so

is a feasible point for (

Also note that

The above holds for all

that is feasible for (

so

. On the other hand, we always have

, so

.
Therefore, if
y^{*}
is a minimizer of (
3.3
),
then
x^{*}
is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

When the Slater’s condition holds,
the moment relaxation (

) has strictly feasible points.
So, the strong duality holds between
(
3.2
) and (
3.3
) and
(
3.2
) achieves its optimal value.
Therefore,

and (

) holds.
∎

Example 6.7
.

Consider the optimization problem

(6.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}&f(x)=\underbrace{x_{1}^{2}+(x_{1}x_{2}-1)^{2}}_{f_{1}}+\underbrace{(x_{2}x_{3})^{2}+(x_{3}-1)^{2}}_{f_{2}}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}&1-x_{1}^{2}\geq 0,\ 1-x_{2}^{2}\geq 0,\,1-x_{3}^{2}\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\Delta_{1}=\{1,2\}
and
\Delta_{2}=\{2,3\}
.
For fixed
x_{2}\in[-1,1]
, the minimum value of
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})
over
x_{1}\in[-1,1]
is

f_{1,\min}=(x_{2}^{2}+1)^{-1}
,
which is attained at
x_{1}=x_{2}(1+x_{2}^{2})^{-1}
.
Similarly, the minimum value of
f_{2}(x_{2},x_{3})

over
x_{3}\in[-1,1]
is
f_{2,\min}=x_{2}^{2}(x_{2}^{2}+1)^{-1},

which is attained at
x_{3}=(1+x_{2}^{2})^{-1}
.
Hence, we have

f_{\min}=f_{1,\min}+f_{2,\min}=1.

However, Assumption
4.1
fails to hold. Suppose otherwise it holds,
then there exist
p_{1}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},x_{2}]
and
p_{2}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{2},x_{3}]
such that

p_{1}+p_{2}+1=0
and

(6.9)

f_{1}+p_{1}\geq 0\ \mbox{on}\ [-1,1]^{2},\quad f_{2}+p_{2}\geq 0\ \mbox{on}\ [-1,1]^{2}.

Since
x_{2}
is the only joint variable of
x_{\Delta_{1}}
and
x_{\Delta_{2}}
, both
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
depend only on
x_{2}
.
So, by the first condition of (
6.9
), we have

(6.10)

f_{1,\min}+p_{1}(x_{2})=(x_{2}^{2}+1)^{-1}+p_{1}(x_{2})\geq 0\quad\forall x_{2}\in[-1,1].

Since
p_{2}=-1-p_{1}
, the second condition of (
6.9
) implies

(6.11)

f_{2,\min}-1-p_{1}(x_{2})=-(x_{2}^{2}+1)^{-1}-p_{1}(x_{2})\geq 0\quad\forall x_{2}\in[-1,1].

Combining (
6.10
) and (
6.11
), we get

p_{1}(x_{2})=-(x_{2}^{2}+1)^{-1}\quad\forall x_{2}\in[-1,1].

However, the above cannot hold since
p_{1}
is a polynomial.
Therefore, Assumption
4.1
does not hold for (
6.8
), and hence the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of (
3.2
)-(
3.3
) is not tight.
On the other hand, one can verify that

f(x)-f_{\min}=(x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}-1)^{2}+(x_{1}-x_{2}x_{3})^{2},

which shows that the dense Moment-SOS relaxation is tight.

7.1.
Proof of Theorem

Proof.

(Proof of Theorem
3.3
)
Since (
3.8
) holds, we have

M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}]=\rho_{i}[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}^{T}+W_{\Delta_{i}}

for a positive scalar
\rho_{i}>0
and a moment matrix
W_{\Delta_{i}}\succeq 0
.
Let

\rho\coloneqq\min_{1\leq i\leq m}\rho_{i}.

Since
(y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}})_{0}=1
, we have
0<\rho\leq\rho_{i}\leq 1
for every
i\in[m]
, hence

\widehat{W}_{\Delta_{i}}\,\coloneqq\,(\rho_{i}-\rho)[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{t}^{T}+W_{\Delta_{i}}\succeq 0.

Note that
\widehat{W}_{\Delta_{i}}
and
W_{\Delta_{i}}
are also moment matrices
for some tms’s.

For the case
\rho=1
, we have
\widehat{W}_{\Delta_{i}}=W_{\Delta_{i}}=0
for all
i
.
So,
\mathcal{X}_{\Delta_{i}}
consists of the single point
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}
,
and hence
y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}|_{2t}=[x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}]_{2t}
for all

f_{k}^{smo}=\langle f,y^{*}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle f_{i},y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*})=f(x^{*}).

Since each
x_{\Delta_{i}}^{*}\in K_{\Delta_{i}}
,
we know

is a minimizer of (
1.1
).

For the case
0<\rho<1
, define the new tms

\tilde{y}\coloneqq(\tilde{y}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{U}_{t}}
be the tms such that

, then

\displaystyle{V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2t}[y^{*}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho{V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2t}[\hat{y}]+(1-\rho){V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2t}[\tilde{y}],

\displaystyle L_{g_{i}}^{spa,t}[y^{*}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho L_{g_{i}}^{spa,t}[\hat{y}]+(1-\rho)L_{g_{i}}^{spa,t}[\tilde{y}],

\displaystyle M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[\hat{y}_{\Delta_{i}}]+(1-\rho)M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[\tilde{y}_{\Delta_{i}}].

\hat{y}

\displaystyle{V}_{h_{i}}^{spa,2t}[\tilde{y}]

\displaystyle M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[\tilde{y}_{\Delta_{i}}]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho}\left(M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[y^{*}_{\Delta_{i}}]-\rho M_{\Delta_{i}}^{(t)}[\hat{y}_{\Delta_{i}}]\right)\succeq 0.

) with

\langle f,y^{*}\rangle\leq\langle f,\hat{y}\rangle,\quad\langle f,y^{*}\rangle\leq\langle f,\tilde{y}\rangle.

On the other hand, it also holds that

f_{t}^{smo}=\langle f,y^{*}\rangle=\rho\langle f,\hat{y}\rangle+(1-\rho)\langle f,\tilde{y}\rangle.

Since
0<\rho<1
, we must have

f_{t}^{smo}=\langle f,y^{*}\rangle=\langle f,\hat{y}\rangle=\langle f,\tilde{y}\rangle.

Note that

.

f_{k}^{smo}\leq f_{\min}
, we have

f(x^{*})=f_{t}^{smo}\leq f_{\min}\leq f(x^{*}).

This shows that

is a minimizer of (

Conclusions and Discussions

This paper studies the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy of relaxations
(
3.2
)-(
3.3
)
for solving sparse polynomial optimization problem (
1.1
).
We show that this sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight if and only if
(
3.5
) or (
3.6
) holds, i.e.,
the objective can be equivalently written as a sum of sparse nonnegative polynomials,
each of which belongs to the sum of the ideal and quadratic module
generated by the corresponding constraints.
Under Assumption
4.1
, we give some sufficient conditions
for this sparse hierarchy to be tight: optimality conditions for
(
4.6
) or finiteness of individual constraining sets.
We also prove some conditions for Assumption
4.1
to hold.
In particular, we show that the sparse Moment-SOS hierarchy is tight
under some convexity assumptions.

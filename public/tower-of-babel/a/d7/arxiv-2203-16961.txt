Abstract
. This paper considers two commuting smooth transformations on a Banach space, and proves the sub-additivity of the measure theoretic entropies under mild conditions. Furthermore, some additional conditions are given for the equality of the entropies. This extends Hu’s work about commuting diffeomorphisms in a finite dimensional space (Huyi Hu, 1993, Ergod. Th. Dynam. Sys., 13 : 73-100) to the case of systems on an infinite dimensional Banach space.

1.
Introduction

In the study of dynamical systems, entropy is one of the fundamental invariant quantities that is widely used to characterize the complexity of a dynamical system, and the measure theoretic entropy measures the average amount of information and complexity in a system. For a differentiable dynamical system on a compact finite dimensional manifold, there are several well-known results that involves the measure theoretic entropy, such as Ruelle’s inequality, Pesin’s entropy formula and that positivity of the measure theoretic entropy implies the existence of hyperbolic periodic orbits and horseshoes. It is a natural question whether these results in finite dimensional systems hold in infinite dimensional systems.

The Oseledets multiplicative ergodic theorem (MET for short) plays a key role in the theory of differentiable dynamical systems.
Ruelle
[
19
]
proved the MET for operator cocycles on Hilbert spaces. Thieullen
[
20
]
proved it for continuous operator cocycles on Banach spaces. For random dynamical systems, Lian and Lü
[
14
]
proved it for strong measurable operator cocycles on separable Banach spaces; Varzaneh and Riedel
[
21
]
proved the multiplicative ergodic theorem for a strongly measurable semi-invertible operator on fields of Banach spaces, and constructed the stable and unstable manifolds theorem; and Li and Shu
[
11
]
proved Ruelle’s inequality of random dynamical systems in Banach spaces.

For
C^{2}
Fréchet differentiable mappings of Banach spaces,
Blumenthal and Young
[
2
]
recently studied the relation between entropy and volume growth for a natural notion of volume defined on finite dimensional subspaces, and characterized SRB measures as exactly those measures for which entropy is equal to the volume growth on unstable manifolds. This extended a significant result in
[
10
]
for diffeomorphisms of finite dimensional Riemannian manifolds. See
[
13
]
for the existence of SRB measures for a
C^{2}
partially hyperbolic map on a Hilbert space. Another important result in finite dimensional smooth ergodic theory is Katok’s landmark approximation result that positive measure theoretic entropy implies the existence of hyperbolic periodic orbits and horseshoes
[
7
]
. Lian and Young
[
16
]
generalized Katok’s theorem to Fréchet differentiable maps on Hilbert space with injective, compact derivative and admitting a compact invariant set on which the invariant measure is supported, and later to a similar setting for a
C^{2}
smooth semiflow on Hilbert spaces
[
17
]
. Later, Lian and Ma
[
15
]
extended this result to maps in a separable Banach space. These previous research were driven by the study of dissipative parabolic differential equations having global attractors, for instance, 2D Navier-Stokes equations and other reaction diffusion equations.

If
f,g
are two commuting
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on a smooth compact manifold, Hu
[
6
]
proved the sub-additivity of measure theoretic entropies, i.e.,
h_{\mu}(f\circ g)\leq h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)
with respect to any
(f,g)
-invariant measure
\mu
(see Section
2
for the definition). This result does not remain true for measure preserving transformations in general, see an example in the end of
[
6
]
, where the author constructed two commuting measure preserving transformations
f,g
of a compact manifold
M
satisfying that
h_{\mu}(f)=h_{\mu}(g)=0
and
h_{\mu}(fg)>0
with respect to a measure
\mu
.
The main aim of this paper is to generalize the above result to
C^{2}
Fréchet differentiable mappings of Banach spaces. Namely, let
f,g
be two commuting
C^{2}
Fréchet differentiable maps on a Banach space
\mathcal{B}
, we will show that
h_{\mu}(f\circ g)\leq h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)
with respect to an
(f,g)
-ergodic measure
\mu
under some suitable conditions.

Although one may utilize some existing techniques to deal with the lack of local compactness of the phase space, there are some new questions should be addressed in our case of the infinite dimensional Banach spaces. The first one is caused by the non-invertibility of the maps
f
and
g
. The maps may be not onto and has arbitrarily strong contraction in some directions. Even the map
f^{-1}
can be defined in some invariant subset, one cannot expect it to have nice regular properties. The second is that the Lyapunov metric does depend on points, one has difficulties in estimating the norm of vectors since there is no inner product.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we give some preliminary results and the statements of the main results. In Section
3
and Section
4
, we recall the Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem for commuting transformations and establish the Lyapunov norms in our case. In Section
5
we give the detailed of the sub-additivity of entropies. In Section
6
and Section
7
we provide some properties of unstable manifolds and give a condition of the equality of the entropies in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries and statements

Throughout this paper, let
\mathcal{B}
denote a (infinite dimensional) separable Banach space with norm
|\cdot|
, and let
A\subset\mathcal{B}
be a compact subset. Consider two maps
f:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and
g:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
, and denote by
fg
the composition
f\circ g
for simplicity, assume the following properties are satisfied:

(H1)

(i)

fg=gf
, both
f
and
g
are
C^{2}
Fréchet differentiable and injective;

(ii)

denoted by
Df_{x}
and
Dg_{x}
the derivative of
f
and
g
at the point
x\in\mathcal{B}
, they are also injective.

(H2)

(i)

f(A)=A
and
g(A)=A
;

(ii)

the set
A
has finite (upper) box-counting dimension.

(H3)

Assume that

l_{\alpha}(f)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{x\in A}|Df^{n}_{x}|_{\alpha}<0\ \text{,}\ l_{\alpha}(g)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sup_{x\in A}|Dg^{n}_{x}|_{\alpha}<0.

Here
|T|_{\alpha}
is the Kuratowski measure of noncompactness for a linear operator
T
(see definition in below).

Definition 2.1
.

The Kuratowski measure of noncompactness for a linear operator
T:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is defined as

|T|_{\alpha}=\inf_{r>0}\Big\{r:T(B)\ \text{can be covered by finite number of balls with radius}\ r\Big\}

where
B
is the unit ball in
\mathcal{B}
.

Let
h=f\ \text{or}\ g
, since
|T_{1}T_{2}|_{\alpha}\leq|T_{1}|_{\alpha}\cdot|T_{2}|_{\alpha}
for any linear operators
T_{1},T_{2}
on
\mathcal{B}
(e.g., see
[
14
, Section 2]
),
we have that

\sup_{x\in A}|Dh^{n+k}_{x}|_{\alpha}\leq\sup_{x\in A}|Dh^{n}_{x}|_{\alpha}\cdot\sup_{x\in A}|Dh^{k}_{x}|_{\alpha}.

Hence, all the limits in (H3) exist.

Remark 2.1
.

The conditions (H1) and (i) of (H2) are natural properties guaranteeing the existence of Lyapunov exponents in Oseledets multiplicative ergodic theorem on a Banach space (see
[
20
]
). Condition (H3) is discussed in Section
4
, it implies that the tangent space could be decomposed into the unstable, center and stable subspaces with respect to the transformation. For (ii) of (H2) , it is naturally satisfied when
Df_{x}
(or
Dg_{x}
) is the sum of a compact operator and a contraction for each
x\in A
(see
[
18
]
), and it will be used in Sections
5.3
and
7.2.2
.

For every measurable transformation
h:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
, let
\mathcal{M}(h,A)
denote the set of all
h
-invariant Borel probability measures supported on
A
, and put
\mathcal{M}(f,g,A):=\mathcal{M}(f,A)\cap\mathcal{M}(g,A)
. It is proved in
[
6
]
that the set
\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
is not empty. A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
is
(f,g)
-ergodic, if for any measurable set
B
with
\mu(f^{-1}(B)\triangle B)=\mu(g^{-1}(B)\triangle B)=0
, one has
\mu(B)=0
or
1
. Let
\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
denote the set of all
(f,g)
-ergodic measures. Note that if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
is
f
-ergodic or
g
-ergodic then
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
, and the inverse is not true in general.

We recall some basic properties of the sets
\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
and
\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
, and refer the reader to
[
6
, Section 1]
for more details.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
T
and
S
are commuting continuous maps on a compact metric space
X
, then

(1)

(i)

T
and
S
have common invariant measures, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}(T,S,X)\neq\varnothing
;

(ii)

the set
\mathcal{M}(T,S,X)
is convex;

(iii)

\mathcal{M}(TS,S,X)=\mathcal{M}(T,S,X)
.

(2)

(i)

\mathcal{E}(T,S,X)
is the set of all extreme point of
\mathcal{M}(T,S,X)
;

(ii)

for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(T,S,X)
, there exists a unique Borel probability measure
\pi
on
\mathcal{E}(T,S,X)

such that

\mu=\int_{\mathcal{E}(T,S,X)}v_{e}\,d\pi(v_{e})

(iii)

\mathcal{E}(TS,S,X)=\mathcal{E}(T,S,X)
.

(iv)

if
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(T,S,X)
, then for any measurable function
\psi
on
X
with
\psi(f(x))\leq\psi(x)
and
\psi(g(x))\leq\psi(x)
, one has that
\psi
is a constant for
\mu
-a.e.
x
.

Let
h=f,g
or
fg
. Denote by
h_{\mu}(h)
the measure theoretic entropy of
h
with respect to
\mu
(see
[
22
, Chapter 4]
for details).
The assumption (i) of (H1) implies that
h|_{A}
is invertible, and
h_{\mu}(h)=h_{\mu}(h|_{A})
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A sub-bundle
V
is
finite dimensional
Dh
-invariant
with respect to a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

x\mapsto V(x)
is measurable and
\dim V(x)
is finite for
\mu
-a.e.
x
;

(2)

Dh_{x}V(x)=V(h(x))
for
\mu
-a.e
x
,

where
h=f
or
g
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
, assume that the following condition is satisfied:

(H4)

For every finite dimensional sub-bundle
V
which is both
Df
-invariant and
Dg
-invariant with respect to the measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
, then either

\int\log^{+}|(Df_{x}|_{V(x)})^{-1}|d\mu(x)<\infty\ \text{and}\ \int\log^{+}|(Dg_{x}|_{V(x)})^{-1}|d\mu(x)<\infty

or both
\log^{+}|(Df|_{V})^{-1}|
and
\log^{+}|(Dg|_{V})^{-1}|
are not integrable with respect to

.

Remark 2.2
.

The above condition is satisfied for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,g,A)
if
f
and
g
are
C^{2}
diffeomorphisms on some neighborhood of

. It is used in the proof of Corollary
3.2
in Section

, and which is only used in the proof of (e) of Theorem

.

Under the previous assumptions, we have the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a separable Banach space with norm
|\cdot|
. Given a compact subset
A\subset\mathcal{B}
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
. If
f,g:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
satisfy the conditions (H1)-(H4), then we have that

h_{\mu}(fg)\leq h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g).

When does the inequality in the above theorem become an equality? To answer this question, we introduce some notations first. Let
\mathcal{B}_{x}:=\{x\}+\mathcal{B}
denote the tangent space of
\mathcal{B}
at
x\in\mathcal{B}
and choose
\lambda_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

0>\lambda_{\alpha}>\max\{l_{\alpha}(f),l_{\alpha}(g)\}.

(2.1)

Let
h=f
or
g
. Given
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
, denote by
\lambda_{1}(x,h)>\lambda_{2}(x,h)>\cdots>\lambda_{r(x,h)}(x,h)>\lambda_{\alpha}
the distinct Lyapunov exponents of
(h,\mu)
which are bigger than
\lambda_{\alpha}
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.
Define respectively the unstable, center and stable subspace of
\mathcal{B}_{x}
by

E^{u}(x,h)=\bigoplus_{\{i:\lambda_{i}(x,h)>0\}}E_{i}(x,h)\ \text{,}\ E^{c}(x,h)=\bigoplus_{\{i:\lambda_{i}(x,h)=0\}}E_{i}(x,h)

and

E^{s}(x,h)=\bigoplus_{\{i:\lambda_{i}(x,h)<0\}}E_{i}(x,h)\oplus E_{\alpha}(x,h).

See Theorem
3.1
for detailed description of the above notations.
For any
\delta>0
, denote by
w^{u}_{\delta}(x,h)
the local unstable manifold of
h
at the point
x
(see Section
6
for the precise definition).

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a separable Banach space with norm
|\cdot|
. Given a compact subset
A\subset\mathcal{B}
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
. Assume that
f,g:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
satisfy the conditions (H1)-(H4) and
E^{u}(x,f)=E^{u}(x,g)
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.
Then, there exists a number
\hat{\delta}>0
such that

w^{u}_{\delta}(x,f)=w^{u}_{\delta}(x,g)\,\,\mu-a.e.\,x

for every
0<\delta<\hat{\delta}
. Moreover, if either
E^{c}(x,f)=\{0\}
or
E^{c}(x,g)=\{0\}
for
\mu
-almost every
x
, then

h_{\mu}(fg)=h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g).

3.
MET for commuting transformations

In this section, we will construct an Oseledets splitting of the tangent space
\mathcal{B}_{x}
such that the Lyapunov exponents of
Df,Dg
and
D(fg)
are well-defined on the finite dimensional subspace, and can also be controlled on the finite co-dimensional subspace.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
\mu
a Borel probability measure on
X
, and let
Z
be a metric space. We say that a map
\Psi:X\rightarrow Z
is
\mu
-continuous, if there is an increasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of compact subsets of
X
, such that
\mu(\cup_{n}K_{n})=1
and
\Psi|_{K_{n}}
is continuous for each
n
.

Given
f,g,\mathcal{B},A
satisfying the conditions (H1)-(H3) and
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
. Put
h=f
or
g
. By the sub-additive ergodic theorem, the following limit exists for
\mu
-almost every
x
:

l_{\alpha}(x,h):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Dh^{n}_{x}|_{\alpha}.

It follows from (H3) immediately that
l_{\alpha}(x,h)\leq l_{\alpha}(h)<\lambda_{\alpha}
(recall the choice of
\lambda_{\alpha}
in (
2.1
)). We first recall the classical MET for derivative cocycles on Banach space, see
[
20
]
for more details.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B},h,A,\lambda_{\alpha}
and
\mu
be as in above. Then there exists a both
f
-invariant and
g
-invariant Borel subset
\Gamma
with
\mu(\Gamma)=1
such that for every
x\in\Gamma
, there are
r(x,h)
numbers

\lambda_{1}(x,h)>\lambda_{2}(x,h)>\cdots>\lambda_{r(x,h)}(x,h)>\lambda_{\alpha}

and a unique splitting

\mathcal{B}_{x}=E_{1}(x,h)\bigoplus\cdots\bigoplus E_{r(x,h)}(x,h)\bigoplus E_{\alpha}(x,h)

with the following properties:

(a)

for
i=1,\cdots,r(x,h)
,
\dim E_{i}(x,h)=m_{i}(x,h)
is finite and
Dh_{x}E_{i}(x,h)=E_{i}(h(x),h)
, for every
v\in E_{i}(x,h)\setminus\{0\}
we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Dh^{n}_{x}v|=\lambda_{i}(x,h);

(b)

E_{\alpha}(x,h)
is closed and finite co-dimensional, satisfies that

Dh_{x}E_{\alpha}(x,h)

\subset E_{\alpha}(h(x),h)
and

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Dh^{n}_{x}|_{E_{\alpha}(x,h)}|\leq\lambda_{\alpha};

(c)

the map
x\mapsto E_{i}(x,h)
(
\ i=1,\cdots,r(x,h)
,
\alpha
) is
\mu
-continuous;

(d)

let
\pi_{i}(x,h)
(
i=1,\cdots,r(x,h)
,
\alpha
) denote the projection of
\mathcal{B}_{x}
onto
E_{i}(x,h)
via the splitting, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\pi_{i}(h^{n}(x),h)|=0.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\mu
is
h
-ergodic, it is well-known that
r(x,h)
,
\lambda_{i}(x,h)
and

(

-ergodic.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that for every metric space

,
\mu

[

For a given measurable positive function

\psi
is (

-)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\psi\circ h^{n}=0\quad\mu\text{-}a.e.

and we call

\varepsilon

with respect to

\psi(h^{\pm}x)\leq e^{\varepsilon}\psi(x)\quad\mu\text{-}a.e.

where

Let

3.1.
Invariant splitting

We first prove the Oseledet splitting of
f

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
x\in\Gamma
and

, let

\lambda(x,v,f)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|Df^{n}_{x}v|

\lambda(g(x),Dg_{x}v,f)=\lambda(x,v,f)

Proof.

Let
C_{0}=\max\{\sup_{x\in A}|Df_{x}|,\sup_{x\in A}|Dg_{x}|\}<\infty
, and put

m(x,g,E):=m(Dg_{x}|_{E(x,f)})=\inf_{v\in E(x,f)\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{|Dg_{x}v|}{|v|}

We claim that for

\lim_{n\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log m(f^{n}(x),g,E)=0.

(3.1)

We proceed to prove the lemma by assuming that the claim is true. By definition, we have
m(x,g,E)|v|\leq|Dg_{x}v|\leq C_{0}|v|
for every

\displaystyle m(f^{n}(x),g,E)|Df^{n}_{x}v|\leq|Dg_{f^{n}(x)}Df^{n}_{x}v|=|Df^{n}_{g(x)}Dg_{x}v|\leq C_{0}|Df^{n}_{x}v|

It follows from the claim immediately that
\lambda(g(x),Dg_{x}v,f)=\lambda(x,v,f)
for

To finish the proof of the lemma, it suffices to prove the above claim.

Proof of the claim:
Let
\Gamma(m)=\{x\in\Gamma:m(x,f)=m\}

be the normalized measure of

Note that

it is easy to show that

m(f(x),g,E)\leq|Df_{g(x)}|\cdot m(x,g,E)\cdot|(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})^{-1}|
. Consequently, one can show that

\displaystyle(\log m(f(x),g,E)-\log m(x,g,E))^{+}\leq\log C_{0}+\log^{+}|(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})^{-1}|.

(3.2)

To complete the proof of the lemma, it suffices to show that (

. By Lemma

\displaystyle\int\log^{+}|(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})^{-1}|d\mu_{m}(x)<\infty.

(3.3)

For every

and every

with

we have that

\displaystyle\det(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})\leq m^{m/2}|Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)}|^{m-1}|Df_{x}v|

(3.4)

one can see
[
2
, Section 3.2]
for the definition of the determinant of a linear operator on Banach spaces. This together with the fact
|Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)}|\leq C_{0}
yield that

|(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})^{-1}|\leq C_{m}\cdot(\det(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)}))^{-1}

where
C_{m}=m^{m/2}\cdot C_{0}^{m-1}
.

Put
\phi(x)=\log\det(Df_{x}|_{E(x,f)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|(D\widetilde{h}_{x})_{0}(\pi_{x,h}^{cs}(u-v))|_{h(x)}+|(\widetilde{h}_{x}-(D\widetilde{h}_{x})_{0})u-(\widetilde{h}_{x}-(D\widetilde{h}_{x})_{0})v|_{h(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon}|\pi_{x,h}^{cs}(u-v)|_{x}+\delta_{1}|u-v|_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(e^{\varepsilon}+\delta_{1})|u-v|_{x}.

The choice of

(see (

. This yields that

|\widetilde{h}_{x}u-\widetilde{h}_{x}v|_{h(x)}=|\pi_{h(x),h}^{u}(\widetilde{h}_{x}u-\widetilde{h}_{x}v)|_{h(x)}.

5.1.
Local entropy

Suppose

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log\mu(B_{n}(x,\delta,T))

Lemma 5.1

(
[
6
]

for

Let

The following result is an extension of the Brin-Katok’s entropy formula.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
6
]

Let

\displaystyle A_{n}\cap

\displaystyle B_{n}(x_{f},2\rho_{\delta,f},f)\cap B({x}^{\prime},2\delta e^{-2n\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\cap f^{-n}(B_{n}(x_{g},2\rho_{\delta,g},g))\cap(fg)^{-n}(B({x}^{\prime\prime},2\delta e^{-2n\varepsilon}))

is not empty, then for each
x
in it, the intersection is contained in the set

\displaystyle B_{n}(x,4\rho_{\delta,f},f)\cap B(x,4\delta e^{-2n\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\cap f^{-n}(B_{n}(f^{n}(x),4\rho_{\delta,g},g))\cap(fg)^{-n}(B((fg)^{n}(x),4\delta e^{-2n\varepsilon})).

By Lemma
5.5
, the above set is contained in
B_{n}(x,4\delta\ell K_{0},fg)
.
There are at most
|S_{f}|\cdot|S_{g}|\cdot|S_{0}|^{2}
different such intersections. Note that
\mu(A_{n})>1-r
, these intersections cover
A_{n}
and each one is contained in an
(n,4\delta\ell K_{0},fg)
-ball. Thus, we have that

\displaystyle N_{n}(4\delta\ell K_{0},r,fg)

\displaystyle\leq|S_{f}|\cdot|S_{g}|\cdot|S_{0}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{n(h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)+2\varepsilon)}\cdot(2\delta e^{-2n\varepsilon})^{-2D_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq(2\delta)^{-2D_{0}}\cdot e^{n(h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)+(4D_{0}+2)\varepsilon)}.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.2

h_{\mu}(fg)\leq h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)+(4D_{0}+2)\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, one has that

h_{\mu}(fg)\leq h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g).

This completes the proof of Theorem
A
.
∎

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}^{n}_{h^{-n}x}v_{n}=v\

\displaystyle w_{\delta}^{u}(x,h)=\exp_{x}\Big\{v\in\widetilde{B}_{x}(\delta\ell(x)^{-1}):\

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\exists v_{n}\in\widetilde{B}_{h^{-n}x}(\delta_{1}\ell(h^{-n}x)^{-1})\ \text{such that}

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}^{n}_{h^{-n}x}v_{n}=v\Big\}.

Proof.

To simplify the notations, for
\tau=u,cs

\pi^{\tau}_{h^{-k}x,h}

as

\pi^{\tau}_{-k}
,
|\cdot|_{-k}
respectively, and let

\displaystyle R:=\exp_{x}\Big\{v\in\widetilde{B}_{x}(\delta\ell(x)^{-1}):\

\displaystyle\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\exists v_{n}\in\widetilde{B}_{h^{-n}x}(\delta_{1}\ell(h^{-n}x)^{-1})\ \text{such that}

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}^{n}_{h^{-n}x}v_{n}=v\

\displaystyle\text{and}\ |\pi_{h^{-n}(x),h}^{u}v_{n}|_{h^{-n}(x)}=|v_{n}|_{h^{-n}(x)}\Big\}

For each
y\in w_{\delta}^{u}(x,h)
, let

. By Theorem
6.1
, for every

there exists a

such that

. Moreover, one can show that

since

. Thus,

.

On the other hand, take
v\in\widetilde{B}_{x}(\delta\ell(x)^{-1})
, assume that there exist

such that

v_{n}\in\widetilde{B}_{h^{-n}x}(\delta_{1}\ell(h^{-n}x)^{-1}),\ \widetilde{h}^{n}_{h^{-n}x}v_{n}=v\ \text{and}\ |\pi_{-n}^{u}v_{n}|_{-n}=|v_{n}|_{-n}.

v\notin W_{\delta}^{u}(x,h)

such that
\pi^{u}_{x,h}v=\pi^{u}_{x,h}u
but
\pi^{cs}_{x,h}v\neq\pi^{cs}_{x,h}u

Note that

\displaystyle 1\geq\dfrac{|\pi_{-n}^{cs}v_{n}|_{-n}}{|\pi_{-n}^{u}v_{n}|_{-n}}\geq\dfrac{|\pi_{-n}^{cs}(v_{n}-u_{n})|_{-n}-|u_{n}|_{-n}}{|v_{n}|_{-n}},

|u-v|_{x}=|\pi^{cs}_{x,h}(u-v)|_{x}

|\pi_{-n}^{u}v_{n}|_{-n}=|v_{n}|_{-n}

|\pi_{-n}^{u}u_{n}|_{-n}=|u_{n}|_{-n}

By (2) of Corollary

\displaystyle|u-v|_{x}\leq e^{2n\varepsilon}|\pi_{-n}^{cs}(v_{n}-u_{n})|_{-n}=e^{2n\varepsilon}|v_{n}-u_{n}|_{-n}.

\displaystyle|v|_{x}\geq(e^{4\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{n}|v_{n}|_{-n}\ \text{and}\ |u|_{x}\geq(e^{4\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{n}|u_{n}|_{-n}.

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{e^{-2n\varepsilon}|u-v|_{x}-(e^{4\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{-n}|u|_{x}}{(e^{4\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{-n}|v|_{x}}

\displaystyle\geq(\dfrac{e^{4\varepsilon}-\delta_{1}}{e^{2\varepsilon}})^{n}\dfrac{|u-v|_{x}}{|v|_{x}}-\frac{|u|_{x}}{|v|_{x}}.

in (

This contradiction implies that

E^{c}(x,f)=\{0\}
for every
x\in\Gamma
. If the points
y_{i}\in U(x_{0},r_{0})

(i=1,2)
satisfy that

\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))\neq\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2})),

then we have that

\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))\cap\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2}))=\emptyset.

Proof.

Assume on the contrary that there exists a point
{z}^{\prime}\in\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))\cap\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2}))
.
Let
z\in\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2}))
, then by definition of
\Theta(y_{2})
we have that

|z-y_{2}|_{y_{2}}=|\pi^{u}_{y_{2}}(z-y_{2})|_{y_{2}}\leq\ell_{0}\cdot 2\delta\ell_{0}^{-3}\leq 2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}\ell^{-1}(y_{2})

where
\ell_{0},\delta
are the same as in Lemma
7.2
.
This implies that

z\in\exp_{y_{2}}\operatorname{graph}(\sigma_{y_{2}}|_{\widetilde{B}_{y_{2}}^{u}(2\delta\ell_{0}^{-2})})\subset w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(y_{2}).

Hence, there exists
z_{n}\in w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(f^{-n}y_{2})
such that

f^{n}z_{n}=z
for every
n>0
. Note that
|\pi^{u}_{x_{0}}(z-x_{0})|<\delta\ell_{0}^{-3}
,
to show that
z\in\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))
, it suffices to show that

z\in\exp_{y_{1}}\operatorname{graph}(\sigma_{y_{1}}|_{\widetilde{B}^{u}_{y_{1}}(\delta_{3}\ell_{0}^{-2})})
.

Similarly, by assumption of
{z}^{\prime}
, we also have
{z}^{\prime}\in w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(y_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Therefore, for every
n>0
there exists
{z}^{\prime}_{n}\in w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(f^{-n}y_{i})
such that
f^{n}{z}^{\prime}_{n}=z
for
i=1,2
.
For every
n>0
,

\displaystyle|z_{n}-f^{-n}y_{1}|_{f^{-n}y_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell({f^{-n}y_{1}})|z_{n}-f^{-n}y_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{0}e^{n\varepsilon}(|z_{n}-{z}^{\prime}_{n}|+|{z}^{\prime}_{n}-f^{-n}y_{1}|).

Since
z,{z}^{\prime}\in w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(y_{2})
and
{z}^{\prime}\in w^{u}_{2\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(y_{1})
, by Theorem
6.1
we have that

\displaystyle|z_{n}-{z}^{\prime}_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|z_{n}-{z}^{\prime}_{n}|_{f^{-n}y_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}(e^{\lambda^{+}(f)-\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{-n}|z-{z}^{\prime}|_{y_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}(e^{\lambda^{+}(f)-\varepsilon}-\delta_{1})^{-n}(|z-y_{2}|_{y_{2}}+|{z}^{\prime}-y_{2}|_{y_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}e^{-2n\varepsilon}4\delta\ell_{0}^{-2}

by the choice of
\delta_{1}
and
\varepsilon
. Similarly, one can show that

\displaystyle|{z}^{\prime}_{n}-f^{-n}y_{1}|\leq K_{0}e^{-2n\varepsilon}2\delta\ell_{0}^{-2}.

Hence, we have that

|z_{n}-f^{-n}y_{1}|_{f^{-n}y_{1}}\leq 6\delta K_{0}\ell_{0}^{-1}e^{-n\varepsilon}\leq\delta_{3}\ell^{-1}(f^{-n}y_{1})\quad\forall n>0

by the choice of
\delta_{3}
in Theorem
7.2
.
Shrinking
r_{0}
if necessary, we have that

\displaystyle|z-y_{1}|_{y_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{0}(|z-y_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|)

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\ell^{-2}_{0}+2r_{0}\ell_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{3}\ell_{0}^{-2}.

Then, it follows from Lemma
6.1
that
z\in\exp_{y_{1}}\operatorname{graph}(\sigma_{y_{1}}|_{\widetilde{B}^{u}_{y_{1}}(\delta_{3}\ell_{0}^{-2})})
.
Hence, we conclude that
z\in\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))
.
Therefore, we have

\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2}))\subset\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))
.
Similarly, one has
\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2}))\supset\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))
. Thus, we have

\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{1}))=\exp_{x_{0}}(\operatorname{graph}\Theta(y_{2})),

which contradicts with the assumption.
∎

7.2.2.
Construction of the partition

In this section, we will construct a measurable partition
\eta
of a full
\mu
-measure set such that
h_{\mu}(h,\eta)=h_{\mu}(h)
for
h=f
or
g
. See
[
22
, Chapter 4]
for the detailed description of the quantity

.

We first recall some notations. For a measurable partition
\eta
, let
\eta(x)
denote the element of
\eta
containing
x
. For two measurable partitions
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, we say
\eta_{2}
is a refinement of
\eta_{1}

if
\eta_{1}(x)\supset\eta_{2}(x)
for
\mu
-almost every

, and denote it by
\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{2}
. Let further that
\eta_{1}\vee\eta_{2}=\{A\cap B:A\in\eta_{1},B\in\eta_{2}\}
, and
h^{-1}\eta_{1}=\{h^{-1}(A):A\in\eta_{1}\}
where
h=f
or
g
. A measurable partition

is called

-decreasing if
\eta\leq h^{-1}\eta
, and
\eta
is called
(f,g)
-decreasing, if
\eta
are both
f
-decreasing and
g
-decreasing.

Let
H_{\mu}(\eta)
be the entropy of the partition
\eta
, and let
H_{\mu}(\eta_{1}|\eta_{2})
be the conditional entropy of
\eta_{1}
given
\eta_{2}
(see
[
22
, Chapter 4]
for detailed definition). The following results are well-known in the entropy theory of dynamical systems:

Proposition 7.1

(
[
22
]
Chapter 4)
.

Suppose
T:X\rightarrow X
is an invertible measure-preserving transformation of a probability space
(X,\mu)
. If
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
and
\eta
are measurable partitions of
X
. Then, the following properties hold:

(1)

H_{\mu}(\eta_{1}\vee\eta_{2}|\eta)=H_{\mu}(\eta_{1}|\eta)+H_{\mu}(\eta_{2}|\eta_{1}\vee\eta)
;

(2)

If
\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{2}
, then
H_{\mu}(\eta_{1}|\eta_{2})=0
and

H_{\mu}(\eta|\eta_{2})\leq H_{\mu}(\eta|\eta_{1})
;

(3)

H_{\mu}(T\eta_{2}|T\eta_{1})=H_{\mu}(\eta_{2}|\eta_{1})
;

(4)

h_{\mu}(h,\eta)=H_{\mu}(\eta|\bigvee_{k\geq 1}h^{k}\eta)
.

Lemma 7.4

(
[
2
]
Lemma 7.14)

Fix

Lemma 7.5

The partition

(1)

(2)

Take

(7.2)

.

To prove the second statement, we only show that

7.2.3.
Proof of the second statement

In this section, we will first show that
h_{\mu}(f)=h_{\mu}(f,\eta)
and
h_{\mu}(g)=h_{\mu}(g,\eta)
with respect to the partition constructed in the previous section. By a standard argument, the second statement of Theorem
B
follows immediately.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
\eta
be a partition constructed as above. Then

h_{\mu}(f,\eta)=h_{\mu}(f),\ h_{\mu}(g,\eta)=h_{\mu}(g).

Proof.

Define the function
\rho_{\delta}:A\rightarrow(0,1)
as

\rho_{\delta}(x)=\delta\ell_{0}^{-3}e^{-(\lambda+\varepsilon)(\tau_{f}(x)+\tau_{g}(x))}

where
\lambda
is from (
4.12
) and
\tau_{f},\tau_{g}
are the first return time to
\Gamma_{\ell_{0}}
(see Section
5.2
for definitions).
Then, we have
\log\rho_{\delta}\in L^{1}(\mu)
since
\int\tau_{f}(x)+\tau_{g}(x)d\mu<\infty
.
By Lemma
7.4
, there exists a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
A
with
H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})<\infty
and
\mathcal{P}(x)\subset B(x,\rho_{\delta}(x))
for
\mu
-almost every
x\in X
.
Let
\mathcal{P}^{+}=\bigvee_{n,k\geq 0}f^{n}g^{k}\mathcal{P}
.

In the following, we show that
\mathcal{P}^{+}(x)\subset w^{u}_{\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}}(x)
for
\mu
-almost every
x
. By the Poincaré’s recurrence theorem, there exists a subset
{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}}
of
\Gamma_{\ell_{0}}
such that
\mu({\Gamma}^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}})=\mu(\Gamma_{\ell_{0}})
and for every
x\in{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}}
there exist infinity many values of
n
for which
(fg)^{-n}(x)\in\Gamma_{\ell_{0}}
.
Let
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\ell_{0}}=\bigcup_{n,k\geq 0}f^{n}g^{k}{\Gamma}^{\prime}_{\ell_{0}}
, it is clear that
\mu(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\ell_{0}})=1
, and for every

there exist two increasing sequence
\{n_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
,
\{k_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
such that

.

Fix
x\in\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\ell_{0}}
and

. Then,
f^{-n}g^{-k}(y)\in\mathcal{P}(f^{-n}g^{-k}x)
for
n,k\geq 0
.
If
f^{-n}g^{-k}x\in\Gamma_{\ell_{0}}
,
we have

\displaystyle|f^{-n}g^{-k}x-f^{-n}g^{-k}y|_{f^{-n}g^{-k}x}\leq\delta\ell_{0}^{-2}\leq\delta\ell_{0}^{-1}\ell(f^{-n}g^{-k}x)^{-1}.

(7.3)

If
f^{-n}g^{-k}x\notin\Gamma_{\ell_{0}}

be the smallest positive integer such that there exists some

, and let
{k}^{\prime}\geq k
be the smallest positive integer such that

. Such

do exist, since there are

such that

. Clearly, one has that

), (

\displaystyle|f^{-n}g^{-k}x-f^{-n}g^{-k}y|_{f^{-n}g^{-k}x}

\displaystyle\leq e^{({n}^{\prime}-n+{k}^{\prime}-k)\lambda}\cdot|f^{-{n}^{\prime}}g^{-{k}^{\prime}}x-f^{-{n}^{\prime}}g^{-{k}^{\prime}}y|_{f^{-{n}^{\prime}}g^{-{k}^{\prime}}x}

) and (

.

\mathcal{Q}^{+}=\bigvee_{j=0}^{\infty}f^{j}\mathcal{Q}_{g}

we have that

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(f,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(f^{-n}\eta|\eta)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}f^{-j}\mathcal{Q}_{g}|\eta)-\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{j=0}^{n}f^{-j}\mathcal{Q}_{g}|f^{-n}\eta)

the second term above can be shown to be

Remark 7.2
.

We would like to point out that all the statements in Section
7.2
is true if we assume
E^{c}(x,g)=\{0\}
instead of
E^{c}(x,f)=\{0\}
.

Finally, we will prove the second statement of Theorem
B
, we write it as the following theorem.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a separable Banach space with norm
|\cdot|
and
A\subset\mathcal{B}
a compact subset. Assume that
f,g:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
satisfy the conditions (H1)-(H3),
\mu\in\mathcal{E}(f,g,A)
satisfies the condition (H4),

E^{u}(x,f)=E^{u}(x,g)
and
E^{c}(x,f)=\{0\}
(or
E^{c}(x,g)=\{0\}
) for
\mu
-almost every
x
. Then, we have that

h_{\mu}(f)+h_{\mu}(g)=h_{\mu}(fg).

Proof.

Let
\eta
be the partition constructed in Section
7.2.2
, then (recall that
\eta
is
(f,g)
-decreasing) we have that

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(fg)

\displaystyle\geq h_{\mu}(fg,\eta)

\displaystyle=H_{\mu}(f^{-1}g^{-1}\eta|\eta)

\displaystyle=H_{\mu}(f^{-1}g^{-1}\eta\vee g^{-1}\eta|\eta)

\displaystyle=H_{\mu}(g^{-1}\eta|\eta)+H_{\mu}(f^{-1}g^{-1}\eta|g^{-1}\eta)

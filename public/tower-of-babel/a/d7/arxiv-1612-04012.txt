Fubini theorem in noncommutative geometry Note: Research supported by the ARC.
F. Sukochev, D. Zanin

Abstract
We discuss the Fubini formula in Alain Connes’ noncommutative geometry. We present a sufficient condition on spectral triples for which a Fubini formula holds true. The condition is natural and related to heat semigroup asymptotics. We provide examples of spectral triples for which the Fubini formula fails.

4
Physically relevant examples

We supply 3 examples which satisfy the Condition
1.4
. The first example is a sphere — the simplest possible non-flat manifold. The second example is a noncommutative torus. The third and the most technically involved example is the quantum group SU
{}_{q}(2).

The following elementary lemma is needed in all 3 examples. We incorporate the proof for convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

1.

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|l|e^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=t^{-2}+O(t^{-1}),\quad t\to 0.

2.

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{t}+O(1),\quad t\to 0.

3.

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}l^{2}e^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2t^{3}}+O(t^{-2}),\quad t\to 0.

Proof.

Though the second and third equalities can be derived from the Poisson summation formula, this method gives nothing good for the first equality. We provide an elementary proof of the first equality. The proofs of the second and third are similar.

The function
s\to se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}
admits its maximum at the point
s=\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}.
Thus, the function increases on the interval
(0,\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}})
and decreases on the interval
(\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}},\infty).
It follows that

\sum_{l=1}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}\int_{l-1}^{l}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}\int_{l}^{l+1}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds.

Thus,

\Big|\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}-\int_{0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds\Big|\leq\int_{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds\leq\sup_{s>0}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{t\sqrt{2}}.

Similarly,

\Big|\sum_{l=\lceil\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rceil+1}^{\infty}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}-\int_{\lceil\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rceil}^{\infty}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds\Big|\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{t\sqrt{2}}.

It follows that

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=\Big(\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor-1}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}\Big)+\Big(\sum_{l=\lceil\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rceil+1}^{\infty}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}\Big)+\Big(\sum_{l=\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor}^{\lceil\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rceil}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}\Big)=

=\Big(\int_{0}^{\lfloor\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rfloor}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds+O(t^{-1})\Big)+\Big(\int_{\lceil\frac{1}{t\sqrt{2}}\rceil}^{\infty}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds+O(t^{-1})\Big)+O(t^{-1})=

=\int_{0}^{\infty}se^{-s^{2}t^{2}}ds+O(t^{-1})=t^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}se^{-s^{2}}ds+O(t^{-1})=\frac{1}{2t^{2}}+O(t^{-1}).

It follows that

\sum_{l=-\infty}^{0}|l|e^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}le^{-l^{2}t^{2}}=\frac{1}{2t^{2}}+O(t^{-1}).

Adding the last
2
formulae, we conclude the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.1

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\lambda(m,x)=\sum_{p=1}^{4}i^{p-1}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\lambda(m,x_{p})+O(1).

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\lambda(m,x)=\sum_{p=1}^{4}i^{p-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{p}(k)+O(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}x(k)+O(1).

Appendix A
Number-theoretic estimates

The following lemmas are standard in number theory.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
p\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=\frac{2^{-p}\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{p}{2})}m^{p}+O(m^{p-1}),\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=\frac{\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{p}{2})}m^{p}+O(m^{p-1}).

Proof.

We prove the first assertion by induction on
p.
Let
K=[0,1]^{p}
be the unit cube. For brevity, we denote
p-
tuple
(1,\cdots,1)=1.
We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}\int_{K+\mathbf{k}-1}dt=\int_{C_{m}}dt,\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}\int_{K+\mathbf{k}}dt=\int_{B_{m}}dt,

where

C_{m}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}(K+\mathbf{k}-1)\subset\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}:\ |t|\leq m\}\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}(K+\mathbf{k})=B_{m}.

It follows immediately that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1\leq\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}\\
|t|\leq m\end{subarray}}dt\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1.

(A.1)

It is clear that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1\leq p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p-1}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=O(m^{p-1}),

(A.2)

where we used induction with respect to
p
in the last equality. Combining (
A.1
) and (
A.2
), we infer that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{p}\\
|t|\leq m\end{subarray}}dt+O(m^{p-1})=\frac{2^{-p}\pi^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+\frac{p}{2})}m^{p}+O(m^{p-1}).

This concludes the proof of the first equality.

To see the second equality, note that

2^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1\leq 2^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}\\
|\mathbf{k}|\leq m\end{subarray}}1.

The second equality follows now from (
A.2
).
∎

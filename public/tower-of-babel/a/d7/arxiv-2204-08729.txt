Conditions for asymptotic stability of first order scalar differential-difference equation with complex coefficients
Rafał Kapica, Radosław Zawiski

Abstract
We investigate a scalar characteristic exponential polynomial with complex coefficients associated with a first order scalar differential-difference equation. Our analysis provides necessary and sufficient conditions for allocation of the roots in the complex open left half-plane what guarantees asymptotic stability of the differential-difference equation. The conditions are expressed explicitly in terms of complex coefficients of the characteristic exponential polynomial, what makes them easy to use in applications. We show examples including those for retarded PDEs in an abstract formulation. Keywords: first order differential-difference equation with complex coefficients, stability of differential-difference equation, characteristic exponential polynomial of differential-difference equation, retarded differential-difference equation (DDE) 2020 Subject Classification: 30C15, 34K06, 34K20, 34K41

Introduction

In this article we study the asymptotic stability of a scalar linear differential-difference equation (DDE)

x^{\prime}(t)=\lambda x(t)+\gamma x(t-\tau),\quad t\geq 0,

(1)

where
\lambda,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
and
0<\tau
, through the analysis of the corresponding characteristic equation

s-\lambda-\gamma\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-s\tau}=0.

(2)

This problem is frequently related to stability analysis of

x^{\prime}(t)=f\left(x(t),x(t-\tau)\right),\quad t\geq 0,

(3)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth (nonlinear) function about
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, via its linearization given by

x^{\prime}(t)=Ax(t)+Bx(t-\tau),

(4)

where
A=J_{1}f(x_{0},x_{0})
and
B=J_{2}f(x_{0},x_{0})
are partial Jacobian matrices of
f
at
(x_{0},x_{0})
. To be more precise we use the following

Definition 1
.

An equilibrium solution

of (
3
) is exponentially stable if there exist
M,\omega,\delta>0
such that
\|x(t)-x_{0}\|\leq M\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-\omega t}

(t\geq 0)
holds for every solution
x
of (
3
) satisfying an inital condition
\|x(t)-x_{0}\|<\delta

(t\in[-\tau,0])
with the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
.

By the
principle of linearized stability

[
5
]
for the case at hand we have
[
10
]

Fact 2
.

Let the linearization of (
3
) about an equilibrium solution

be expressed by (
4
) and let the corresponding characteristic equation be given by

\det\left[sI-A-B\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-s\tau}\right]=0.

(5)

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

is exponentially stable if
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits s<0
for all characteristic roots

),

(ii)

Equation (

]

). Then (

A
and

.

Our motivation to investigate (

The literature contains two intertwined approaches to stability problem - one based on analysis of some form of (
4
) in time domain and one based on analysis of (
5

\lambda,\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is well understood - see

s-a-c\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-s}=0

. In a more general form

and

[

and references therein. For a thorough exposition of other methods of analysis of the real

\gamma

[
18
]
and references therein.

The case
\lambda,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
is less analysed. In particular, some sufficiency results can be found in
[
1
]
, where the author presents a numerical analysis of (
1
) and stipulates asymptotic stability for every
\tau>0
if
-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\lambda>|\gamma|
. The authors of
[
3
]
provide, based on algorithmic criteria, some sufficiency result for specific values of complex

, proving also the result in
[
1
]
for some cases. Authors of
[

]
built on
[

]
and provide additional sufficient conditions for stability. In
[

the author uses a continuous dependence of the roots of (

. In
[

To the best of authors’ knowledge

Our approach here combines analysis of roots placement depending on

Thus we not only provide a different formulation of stability conditions but our results are based on a new, different proof.

Lemma 3

Let

and let
\{s_{0}\}
be the set of roots of

s-(a+ib)-(c+id)\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-s\tau}=0

z-a-\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-ib\tau}(c+id)\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-z\tau}=0.

Then
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(s_{0})<0
for all

if and only if
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{0})<0
for all
z_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
z_{0}
be a root of (
7
). Then
s_{0}=z_{0}+ib
is a root of (
6
). Conversly, let
s_{0}
be a root of (
6
). Then
z_{0}=s_{0}-ib
is a root of (
7
). As
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(s_{0})=\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z_{0})
the result follows.
∎

We will also use the following result concerning parameter
\beta\in\mathbb{R}

L,\,R:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\quad L(r):=\frac{r}{r^{2}+1},\quad R(r):=\arctan(r)+\beta.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and put

A=\{r\in[0,\infty):L(r)\leq R(r)\},

where real functions

and

are given by (

(i)

if and only if
\beta\geq 0
,

(ii)

if and only if
\beta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)
, wherein the correspondence
(0,\infty)\ni r_{0}\longleftrightarrow\beta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)
is one-to-one,

(iii)

set
A
is empty if and only if
\beta\leq-\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

Let us consider function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\varphi=R-L
. Clearly, we have

\varphi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2r^{2}}{(r^{2}+1)^{2}}>0,\quad\varphi(0)=\beta,\quad\varphi(r)\longrightarrow\beta+\frac{\pi}{2}\quad\rm{as}\quad r\rightarrow\infty.

In particular
\varphi
is strictly increasing and
\varphi([0,\infty))=[\beta,\beta+\frac{\pi}{2})
.

If
\beta\geq 0
, then
\varphi(r)\geq 0
for
r\in[0,\infty)
, i.e.

A=[0,\infty)
. On the other hand if
L(0)\leq R(0)
, then
\beta\geq 0
. This gives assertion (i).

Suppose
\beta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},0\right)
. Hence
\varphi(0)<0
and
\varphi(r)>0
for large enough
r>0
. Then there exists a unique
r_{0}>0
such that
\varphi(r_{0})=0
. This shows that
A=[r_{0},\infty)
.
If now
A=[r_{0},\infty)
for some

, then
\beta<0
by (i). To finish the proof it is enough to notice that for
\beta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
we have
\varphi(r)<0
for

, i.e.
A=\emptyset
.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Equation

has exactly one solution if and only if
\beta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},0]
.∎

Before going to examples we make some comments concerning previous work of other authors with respect to the proof of Theorem
8

8

9

[

As noted in the introduction an analysis of
\tau

[
8
]
. The author obtains there an inequality similar to (
24

\gamma
of (
1

real matrix of a special form (and with

).

The necessary and sufficient condition for stability of first order scalar differential-difference equations with complex coefficients characterised by (
2

9

2
) and a subset of
\mathbb{C}
given by (
9
)-(
11
). As the latter is defined by non-linear inequalities there arises a question whether numerical approximation of the rightmost root isn’t a more practical approach than finding numerically (i.e. approximately) the region given by (
9
)-(
11
), especially given the abundance of literature of computational techniques to approximate characteristic roots.

Analysis of dependence between
\tau
and the crossing of the imaginary axis by the first root in points 2–3 of the proof is well-known. In one of the early works
[
4
]
authors discuss (
2
) with
\lambda,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, in
[
16
]
the authors show a general approach for real polynamial case of (
4
) with multiple delays, what is also shown in
[
12
]
. More recently such analysis is also used in
[
8
]
. A good exposition of such techniques is in
[
18
, Chapter 5.3.2]
. Our calculations in point 2–3 are in fact based on
[
16
]
and we decided to include all of its steps for the reader’s convenience.

The answer to the above question depends, in the authors’ opinion, on the purpose of approaching that problem. If the purpose is an analysis of a given differential-difference equation, considered as a delayed dynamical system, fulfilling assumptions of Corollary
9
, than a numerical check, up to a given accuracy, of at most one of inequalities (
9
)-(
11
) is usually a straightforward procedure.

If, on the other hand, the purpose is a synthesis of a delayed dynamical system that has some
a priori
specified properties, as may be the case of a controller design for such system, then a numerical search for the rightmost root may carry more relevant information.

5
Examples

With the above discussion in mind we present examples concerning only analysis of given differential-difference equations. These examples illustrate how the necessary and sufficient conditions of Theorem
8
can be compared with and improve known literature results.
Note initially that the stability condition discussed in
[
1
]
and later proved in
[
3
]
, that is
-\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\lambda>|\gamma|
, follows immediately from
\lvert\eta\rvert<\lvert a\rvert
(point 3 in the proof of Theorem

concerns the placement of roots of the characteristic equation (

5.1
Example 1

Consider a differential-difference equation

x^{\prime}(t)=i20x(t)+\gamma x(t-0.1),

(42)

where
\mathbb{R}\ni\gamma>0
. Equation (

) is a special case of (

]

\gamma\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-i2}\in\Lambda_{0.1,0}
, where

is given by (

). We thus obtain that (

\gamma<20-5\pi
, what is equivalent to the condition given by
[

5.2
Example 2

Consider the differential-difference equation

x^{\prime}(t)=\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{\pi}{4}\right)x(t)-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+i\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)x(t-1),

(43)

for which the corresponding characteristic equation takes the form

s-\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{\pi}{4}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-i\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-s}=0.

(44)

By Corollary

and (

5.3
Example 3

In
[
11
]
the author considers a semi-linear system version of (
3

\tau=1
. The exemplary system analysed there is transformed to the form of (

\tau=1

x^{\prime}(t)=Bx(t-1),\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&\frac{1}{8}\\

As

With conditions (

5.4
Example 4

Previous examples relate current results to the ones known from the literature and thus demonstrate the technique. The following example shows how the current results can be used in the case of a retarded partial differential equation in an abstract formulation.

Let the representation of our system be

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{z}(t)=Az(t)+A_{1}z(t-\tau)+Bu(t)\\
z(0)=x,\end{array}\right.

(46)

where the state space
X
is a Hilbert space,
A:D(A)\subset X\to X
is a closed, densely defined diagonal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X
,
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is also a diagonal operator and
0<\tau<\infty
is a fixed delay. The input function is
u\in L^{2}(0,\infty;\mathbb{C})
and
B
is the control operator. We assume that
X
posses a Riesz basis
(\phi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
consisting of eigenvectors of
A
, which has a corresponding sequence of eigenvalues
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

A simplified form of (
46
) is analysed in
[
13
]
from the perspective of admissibility which, roughly speaking, asserts whether a solution
z
of (
46
) follows a required type of trajectory. One of the key elements in the approach to admissibility analysis presented in
[
13
]
is to establish when a differential equation associated with the
k
-th component of (
46
), namely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{z}_{k}(t)=\lambda_{k}z_{k}(t)+\gamma_{k}z_{k}(t-\tau)\\
z_{k}(0)=x_{k},\end{array}\right.

(47)

is stable, where
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
is an eigenvalue of
A
,
\gamma_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
is an eigenvalue of
A_{1}
and
x_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
is an initial condition for the
k
-th component of
X
. Then, having stability conditions for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one may proceed with analysis for the whole
X
. Based on Corollary
9
we immediately obtain a genuine approach method of obtaining these stability conditions, namely

Proposition 10
.

For a given delay
\tau\in(0,\infty)
and sequences
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
consider a corresponding set of Cauchy problems of the form (
47
). For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
system (
47
) is stable if and only if

\lambda_{k}=a_{k}+i\beta_{k}\in\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(z)<\frac{1}{\tau}\right\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\gamma_{k}\mathop{\rm e}\nolimits^{-i\beta_{k}\tau}\in\Lambda_{\tau,a_{k}}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

with
\Lambda_{\tau,a_{k}}
defined in (
9
)– (
11
). ∎

Notice that Proposition
10
not only extends
[
13
, Proposition 3.5]
by adding the necessary condition, but it also allows for analysis of unbounded
A
, as it includes e.g. the case when
a_{k}\to-\infty
as
k\to\infty
. This is in fact exactly the case presented in
[
7
]
.

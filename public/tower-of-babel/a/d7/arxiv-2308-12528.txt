Urns with Multiple Drawings and Graph-based Interaction
Yogesh D, Neeraja Sahasrabudhe

Abstract
Consider a finite undirected graph and place an urn with balls of two colours at each vertex. At every discrete time-step, for each urn, a fixed number of balls are drawn from that same urn with probability p p , and from a randomly chosen neighbour of that urn with probability 1 − p 1-p . Based on what is drawn, the urns then reinforce themselves or their neighbours. For every ball of a given colour in the sample, in case of Pólya-type reinforcement, a constant multiple of balls of that colour is added while in case of Friedman-type reinforcement, balls of the other colour are reinforced. These different choices for reinforcement give rise to multiple models. In this paper, we study the convergence of the fraction of balls of either colour across urns for all of these models. We show that in most cases the urns synchronize, that is, the fraction of balls of either colour in each urn converges to the same limit almost surely. A different kind of asymptotic behaviour is observed on bipartite graphs. We also prove similar results for the case of finite directed graphs.



Keywords
- Urn Processes, Synchronization, Stochastic Approximation, Bipartite Graphs, Multiple Drawings.

In this paper, we study a two-colour interacting urn process that combines the ‘multiple drawings’ aspect with graph-based interaction. We consider urns, containing balls of two colours, placed at the vertices of a finite undirected graph. For each urn, a fixed number of balls are drawn from that same urn with probability
p

p\in[0,1]
), and from a randomly chosen neighbour of that urn with probability
1-p

By including interaction with neighbours during the sampling as well as the reinforcement step, we get a dependence on the square of the scaled adjacency matrix which makes this model different from those studied earlier. This can further be generalized by studying sampling and reinforcement that goes beyond nearest-neighbour influence.

We study the effects of the graph structure, the interaction parameter
p
and the reinforcement type on the asymptotic behaviour of the urn system. In this paper, we mainly focus on Friedman-type reinforcement and obtain convergence, synchronization and fluctuation results for the fraction of balls of either colour in the urns. However, we also obtain convergence results for the Pólya-type reinforcement scheme under the added assumption of the regularity of the underlying graph and an assumption on the initial configuration of the urns. For Friedman-type reinforcement, universal synchronization of urns to a deterministic limit of
1/2
is observed when either

By partial synchronization, we mean that urns belonging to a common partition in the bipartite graph synchronize with each other. In this case, we show that for Friedman-type reinforcement, the fraction of balls of white colour in each urn belonging to a particular partition converges to the same limit almost surely. Further, if the limits are

and

on the two partitions, then

We observe that the ‘multiple drawings’ aspect does not have any effect on the convergence and synchronization results, however the limiting variance in the fluctuation theorems depends on both the size of the drawn sample and the interaction parameter

d_{v}>0\;\forall v\in[N]

, and let
D

\textbf{D}_{ij}=d_{i}
for

denote the indicator function of
A

\mathcal{G}
is bipartite, the two disjoint partitions of the graph are denoted by
V

The urn process is a two-phase process, consisting of sampling and reinforcement. At each step each urn samples from itself or from one of its randomly chosen neighbours. The reinforcement process can be characterized by two types of dependence: (1) neighbourhood dependence: reinforcement in each urn depends on other urns in its neighbourhood, and (2) replacement scheme: reinforcement depends on which colour reinforces which colour. We study three types of neighbourhood dependence.

1.

Self-reinforcement: each urn reinforces itself.

2.

Neighbour-reinforcement: each urn reinforces its neighbours in the graph.

3.

Self and neighbour-reinforcement: each urn reinforces itself as well as its neighbours.

Two types of reinforcement schemes are studied.

1.

Pólya-type reinforcement scheme: each colour reinforces itself. That is, for every ball of colour white in the sample,
C
balls of the same colour are reinforced.

2.

Friedman-type reinforcement scheme: each colour reinforces the opposite colour, and not itself. That is, for every ball of colour white in the sample,
C
balls of black colour are reinforced.

Note that due to the multiple drawing aspect of sampling, the replacement matrix of the Pólya-type reinforcement described above is not a diagonal matrix as in the classical Pólya urn model. The above terminology is consistent with
[
5
,
6
]
where multiple drawing urn models have been studied.

We now describe the process by which the configuration of urns evolves. Fix
p\in[0,1]
. At each discrete time-step
t
, the following process takes place.

•

Sampling:
For each urn,
s
number of balls are drawn uniformly at random (with or without replacement) from that same urn with probability
p
, and from a randomly chosen urn from the neighbourhood with probability
1-p
. The sampling event for each urn is assumed to be independent of sampling from every other urn. As we shall see later, the asymptotic behaviour of the urns (convergence of the fraction of balls of a particular colour) is the same regardless of whether sampling is done with replacement or without replacement of balls.

•

Reinforcement
The
N
samples, each consisting of
s
balls, are collated. We study the following types of reinforcement processes.

1.

Pólya-type reinforcement.

(a)

Pólya-type Self-Reinforcement (PTSR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
white/black balls are added to that same urn to which the sample corresponds.

(b)

Pólya-type Neighbour-Reinforcement (PTNR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
white/black balls are added to all the neighbours of that urn.

(c)

Pólya-type Self and Neighbour-Reinforcement (PTSNR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
white/black balls are added to that urn as well as to all the neighbours of that urn.

2.

Friedman-type reinforcement.

(a)

Friedman-type Self-Reinforcement (FTSR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
black/white balls are added to that same urn to which the sample corresponds.

(b)

Friedman-type Neighbour-Reinforcement (FTNR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
black/white balls are added to all the neighbours of that urn.

(c)

Friedman-type Self and Neighbour-Reinforcement (FTSNR) model: For every white/black ball in the drawn sample,
C
black/white balls are added to that urn as well as to all the neighbours of that urn.

Observe that in the case of FTSR with
p=0
, for each urn the balls are sampled from a randomly chosen neigbour and the urn is reinforced. In the case of FTNR with
p=1
, balls are sampled from each urn and the neighbours are reinforced. For large time
t

p=0
and PTNR with
p=1

3
Convergence Results

We begin by showing that for the Friedman-type reinforcement scheme, whenever there is a unique limit point of the O.D.E.
\dot{z}_{t}=h(z_{t})
, it is stable. In other words, we show that the corresponding Jacobian matrices (denoted by
\frac{\partial h}{\partial z}
) are such that their eigenvalues have non-positive real parts.

Lemma 3.1
.

For Friedman-type reinforcement, the real part of each eigenvalue of
\frac{\partial h}{\partial z}
is non-positive, where
h(\cdot)
is as in (
2.6
).

Proof.

Since
h
is a linear function of
z_{t}
, the Jacobian is independent of
z_{t}
and we have

\frac{\partial h}{\partial z}=\begin{cases}-I-(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})&\text{for the FTSR model,}\\
-I-(p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2})&\text{for the FTNR model,}\\
-I-(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(A+I)(I+D)^{-1}&\text{for the FTSNR model}.\end{cases}

Note that the matrices
\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
,
(\textbf{A}+I)(I+\textbf{D})^{-1}
and
pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
are column stochastic, and since the product of two column stochastic matrices is column stochastic, the matrices
p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}
and
(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(\textbf{A}+I)(I+\textbf{D})^{-1}
are also column stochastic. So, it is enough to show that the lemma is satisfied for matrices of the form
-I-X
where
X
is column stochastic.
Let
\lambda
be an arbitrary eigenvalue of
-I-X
. Then
-\lambda-1
is an eigenvalue of the matrix
X
, which is column stochastic. Therefore,
|\lambda+1|\leq 1
. This implies
(Re(\lambda)+1)^{2}+(Im(\lambda))^{2}\leq 1
. Hence,
-2\leq Re(\lambda)\leq 0
.

\Box

Using arguments similar to those in Lemma 2.2 of
[
18
]
for the case of an undirected graph, we get the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{G}}
is a connected undirected graph. The matrix
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
has the following properties.

1.

All eigenvalues of
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
are real and non-negative.

2.

Rank(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
equals
N
, if the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is non-bipartite, and
N-1
if
{\mathcal{G}}
is bipartite.

3.

Assume that the matrix
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
is diagonalizable. If the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is bipartite, then the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
is one.

Proof.

For the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, define the set
\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}
as the set of edges in
{\mathcal{G}}
such that for any
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}
, both
(x,y)
and
(y,x)
are in
\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}
.

1.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
det(xI-\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})=det(\textbf{D}^{1/2}(xI-\textbf{D}^{-1/2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1/2})\textbf{D}^{-1/2})
. So, the eigenvalues of
\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
are the same as those of
\textbf{D}^{-1/2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1/2}
, which is symmetric. Therefore, the eigenvalues of
AD^{-1}
and hence, those of
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
are real. Since
\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
is column stochastic, the eigenvalues of
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
are non-negative.

2.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle x^{T}(\textbf{D}+\textbf{A})x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_{i}x_{i}^{2}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}}x_{i}x_{j}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}}\frac{(x_{i}+x_{j})^{2}}{2}.

Now,
x^{T}(\textbf{D}+\textbf{A})x=0
implies that
x_{i}=-x_{j}
whenever
(i,j)\in\mbox{${\mathcal{E}}$}^{\prime}
. Let
V=\{i\in[N]:x_{i}>0\},W=\{i\in[N]:x_{i}<0\}
and
L=\{i\in[N]:x_{i}=0\}
. For a connected graph,
L
is either empty or whole of
[N]
.

Suppose
V
is non-empty and suppose
v\in V
such that
x_{v}=\alpha
. Then,
x_{i}=\alpha\forall i\in V
, and
x_{i}=-\alpha\forall i\in W
. Thus, the graph can be partitioned into two disjoint vertex sets
V
and
W
such that the edges only connect vertices from
V
to vertices of
W
, and hence the graph is bipartite (
V
and
W
being the two partitions of the graph). In this case, we have
Rank(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})=Rank(\textbf{D}+\textbf{A})=N-1
because the solution of
x^{T}(\textbf{D}+\textbf{A})x=0
is representable in terms of one variable alone. Since the graph cannot be partitioned in the case of a non-bipartite graph, we must have that
L=[N]
, which means that
x=\mathbf{0}
, where
\mathbf{0}
is the zero vector of dimension
N
.
\textbf{D}+\textbf{A}
is full rank in this case.

3.

Since
Rank(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})=N-1
for a bipartite graph, the geometric multiplicity of
0
eigenvalue is equal to one, and since the matrix is assumed to be diagonalizable, algebraic multiplicity of
0
also equals one.

\Box

We now state and prove results for almost sure convergence of
Z_{t}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For the Friedman-type reinforcement scheme, as
t\rightarrow\infty
,
Z_{t}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}
almost surely whenever one of the following holds.

1.

FTSR model

(a)

0<p\leq 1
.

(b)

p=0
and the graph
\mathcal{G}
is non-bipartite.

2.

FTNR model

(a)

0\leq p<1
.

(b)

p=1
and the graph
\mathcal{G}
is non-bipartite.

3.

The model is FTSNR, regardless of the value of
p
.

Proof.

Using the theory of stochastic approximation, we know that it is enough to show that
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}
is the unique stable limit point of the solution of the O.D.E.
\dot{z}_{t}=h(z_{t})
. From (
2.6
), it is clear that
h(Z_{t})=\mathbf{0}
has a unique solution in each of the cases if the matrices
(1+p)I+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
,
p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}+I
and
(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(\textbf{A}+I)(I+\textbf{D})^{-1}+I
are invertible. Since for all these matrices, each column-sum is
2
, it follows that
\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}
is a solution of
h(Z_{t})=\mathbf{0}
in all three cases of Friedman-type reinforcement. Note that from Lemma
3.1
, it follows that if this is a unique solution, it is also stable. Thus, it is enough to prove the invertibility of these matrices under the conditions of the theorem.

1.

(a)

We have
(1+p)I+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}=(1+p)(I+(1-p)/(1+p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
. Since
\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
is a column stochastic matrix,
I+r\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
is invertible for any
r\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\left|r\right|<1
. Since
\Big|\frac{1-p}{1+p}\Big|<1
for
0<p\leq 1
we are done.

(b)

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that the matrix
\textbf{D}+\textbf{A}
is invertible whenever
{\mathcal{G}}
is non-bipartite.

2.

(a)

For
0<p<1
, we have

\displaystyle p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}+I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{p-\sqrt{p^{2}-4(1-p)}}{2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\right)\times

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{p+\sqrt{p^{2}-4(1-p)}}{2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{p-\sqrt{(p+2)^{2}-8}}{2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\right)\times

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{p+\sqrt{(p+2)^{2}-8}}{2}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\right).

Since
0<p<1
, whenever
(p+2)^{2}-8\geq 0
,
\Big|\frac{p\pm\sqrt{{{(p+2)}^{2}}-8}}{2}\Big|<1
. For
{(p+2)^{2}}-8<0
,
\Big|\frac{p\pm\sqrt{(p+2)^{2}-8}}{2}\Big|=\sqrt{1-p}<1
. Thus,
p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}+I
can be written as a product
(I+r_{1}B_{1})(I+r_{2}B_{2})
, where
B_{1},B_{2}
are column-stochastic and
|r_{1}|,|r_{2}|<1
. This implies
p\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}+(1-p)(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}+I
is invertible.

For
p=0
,
I+(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})^{2}=(I+i\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(I-i\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
, and both
(I+i\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
and
(I-i\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
are invertible since from Lemma
3.2
(1), we know that
\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
has real eigenvalues.

(b)

This case is the same as that of FTSR with
p=0
.

3.

Notice that
(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(\textbf{A}+I)(I+\textbf{D})^{-1}=[p\textbf{A}+(1-p)\textbf{A}\ \textbf{D}^{-1}\textbf{A}+pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}](I+\textbf{D})^{-1}
is irreducible for
0\leq p\leq 1
since
\mathcal{G}
is connected. To show that this matrix is aperiodic for
0\leq p\leq 1
, it is enough to show that one of the diagonal terms of this matrix is strictly positive. This is obvious when
0<p\leq 1
(because of the term
pI
). For
p=0
, this is true since
\exists j\in[N]
such that
(1,j)\in\mathcal{E}
, hence we have
(\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\textbf{A})_{1,1}>0
. Since this matrix is also stochastic, it cannot have
-1
as an eigenvalue (because of aperiodicity, there can exist only one eigenvalue with absolute value
1
). Hence,
(pI+(1-p)\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})(\textbf{A}+I)(I+\textbf{D})^{-1}+I
is invertible for
0\leq p\leq 1
.

\Box

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that the underlying graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is regular with degree

Proof.

Since the graph is regular, we have that
d_{i}=d
and
\omega_{i}=\omega\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq N

\displaystyle Z_{t+1}(i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{T_{t}(i)}{T_{t+1}(i)}Z_{t}(i)+\frac{C}{T_{t+1}(i)}\left(\eta\chi_{t+1}(i)+\kappa\sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}\chi_{t+1}(j)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{t}(i)+\frac{1}{\frac{T_{0}}{Cs\omega}+t+1}\left(\eta\frac{E[\chi_{t+1}(i)|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]}{s\omega}-Z_{t}(i)+\kappa\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}E[\chi_{t+1}(j)|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]}{s\omega}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\frac{T_{0}}{Cs\omega}+t+1}\left(\frac{1}{s\omega}\left(\eta\Delta M_{t}(i)+\kappa\sum\limits_{j\in N(i)}\Delta M_{t}(j)\right)\right).

Writing in vector form, we get

\displaystyle Z_{t+1}=Z_{t}+\frac{1}{\frac{T_{0}}{Cs\omega}+t+1}\left(\frac{E[\chi_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]}{s\omega}(\eta I+\kappa\textbf{A})-Z_{t}+\frac{1}{s\omega}\Delta M_{t}(\eta I+\kappa\textbf{A})\right).

(3.2)

Define
X\coloneqq(1-\frac{p\eta}{\omega})I-\frac{1}{\omega}\left(p\kappa+\frac{(1-p)\eta}{d}\right)\textbf{A}-\frac{(1-p)\kappa}{d\omega}\textbf{A}^{2}
and

X
and

are symmetric matrices. For

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{z_{i}}^{2}Y_{ii}}{2}+\sum_{i<j}z_{i}z_{j}Y_{ij}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}&\text{for the Friedman-type reinforcement scheme.}\end{cases}

Using the symmetry of

and for Pólya-type reinforcement, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{r}}=z_{r}X_{r,r}+\sum_{i<r}z_{i}X_{i,r}+\sum_{j>r}z_{j}X_{r,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}X_{j,r}.

Combining this with a similar calculation for Friedman-type reinforcement, we get

\displaystyle\nabla f(Z_{t})=\begin{cases}Z_{t}X&\text{for P\'{o}lya-type reinforcement scheme, }\\
Z_{t}Y-\mathbf{1}&\text{for the Friedman-type reinforcement scheme.}\end{cases}

(3.3)

X
and
Y
have been defined in such a way that
\nabla f(Z_{t})=-h(Z_{t})
for all the six models (after substituting corresponding
\eta,\kappa
and
\omega
for each model). Hence, with
a_{t}=1/\left(\frac{T_{0}}{Cs\omega}+t+1\right)
and
\beta_{t}=-\Delta M_{t}(\eta I+\kappa A)/s\omega
for

A.2
) of the
Appendix
.

Now we verify the three assumptions of theorem
A.1
. Assumption
1
is clearly satisfied for the chosen
\{{a}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Assumption 3 is satisfied since
f
is an analytic function on its entire domain. We now check assumption 2. Let
S_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}a_{i}\gamma_{i}^{2}\beta_{i}
, where
\gamma_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}a_{j}
. Since
\{\Delta M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale difference sequence, we have

and therefore
\gamma_{k}\sim H_{k}

Harmonic number. Since

Hence,

\lambda_{1}=0
. Define

\theta:=\min\{\lambda_{j}:\lambda_{j}\text{ is a non-zero eigenvalue of}\ I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}\}.

(4.1)

In the case of the FTSR model (I) implies that at any given time
t
, the total number of balls in each urn is the same and we denote if by
T_{t}
. Further, for FTSR
\omega_{i}=1
for
i\in[N]
, and therefore we have the following recursion.

Z_{t+1}=\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{t+1}}\right)Z_{t}+\frac{C}{T_{t+1}}\chi_{t+1}.

(4.2)

We show that the fraction of balls of either colour in urns placed on vertices of
V
(resp.
W
) converges to the same random limit almost surely. Define
Z^{v}_{t}=(Z_{t}(1),Z_{t}(2)\ldots Z_{t}(m),0,0\ldots 0)
and
Z^{w}_{t}=(Z_{t}(m+1),Z_{t}(m+2)\ldots Z_{t}(N),0,0\ldots 0)
as
N
-dimensional vectors. For
0<k<N
, let
e_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a row vector with
1
as the entry in the first
k
positions and
0
otherwise (in the rest of the
N-k
positions). Further, define

\bar{d_{v}}=\sum\limits_{i\in V}d_{i}=e_{m}\textbf{D}e_{m}^{\top},\bar{d_{w}}=\sum\limits_{i\in W}d_{i}=(\mathbf{1}-e_{m})\textbf{D}(\mathbf{1}-e_{m})^{\top},\bar{d}=\sum\limits_{i\in[N]}d_{i}=\mathbf{1}D\mathbf{1}^{\top},

\bar{Z}_{t}=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d}}\sum\limits_{i\in[N]}d_{i}Z_{t}(i)=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d}}Z_{t}\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top},\ \bar{Z}^{v}_{t}=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d_{v}}}\sum\limits_{i\in V}d_{i}Z_{t}(i)=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d_{v}}}Z_{t}\textbf{D}e_{m}^{\top}

and

\bar{Z}^{w}_{t}=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d_{w}}}\sum\limits_{i\in W}d_{i}Z_{t}(i)=\dfrac{1}{\bar{d_{w}}}Z_{t}\textbf{D}(\mathbf{1}-e_{m})^{\top}.

The main result of this section, stated in the following theorem shows that urns on vertices in
V
and
W
synchronize almost surely respectively. More precisely, we show that as
t\to\infty
,
Z_{t}(i)-\bar{Z}^{v}_{t}\xrightarrow{a.s.}0
for
i\in V
,
Z_{t}(i)-\bar{Z}^{w}_{t}\xrightarrow{a.s.}0
for
i\in W
, and that
\bar{Z}_{t}^{v}+\bar{Z}_{t}^{w}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For the FTSR model with
p=0
on a bipartite graph, under assumptions (I) and (II), as
t\to\infty
,
Z^{v}_{t}-\bar{Z}^{v}_{t}e_{m}\xrightarrow{a.s.}\mathbf{0}
and
Z^{w}_{t}-\bar{Z}^{w}_{t}(\mathbf{1}-e_{m})\xrightarrow{a.s.}\mathbf{0}
.

We first show that
\bar{Z}_{t}
converges to a unique deterministic limit.

Theorem 4.2
.

For the FTSR model with
p=0
on a bipartite graph, under assumption (I),
\bar{Z}_{t}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1/2
and
\bar{Z}_{t}^{v}+\bar{Z}_{t}^{w}\xrightarrow{a.s.}1
, as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

We write a stochastic approximation scheme for
\bar{Z}_{t}
. Using (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{d}}Z_{t+1}\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{d}}\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{t+1}}\right)Z_{t}\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}+\frac{C}{\bar{d}~T_{t+1}}\chi_{t+1}\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{t}+\frac{C}{\bar{d}T_{t+1}}\left(\chi_{t+1}-sZ_{t}\right)\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{t}+\frac{C}{\bar{d}~T_{t+1}}\left(E[\chi_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]-sZ_{t}\right)\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}+\frac{C}{\bar{d}~T_{t+1}}\Delta\bar{M}_{t+1},

where
\Delta\bar{M}_{t+1}=(\chi_{t+1}-E[\chi_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}])\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
is a bounded martingale difference. Note that

\displaystyle\left(E[\chi_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]-sZ_{t}\right)\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\mathbf{1}-Z_{t}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\textbf{D}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\bar{d}s\bar{Z}_{t}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s(\bar{d}-Z_{t}\textbf{A}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\bar{d}\bar{Z}_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\bar{d}(1-2\bar{Z}_{t}),

since
\textbf{A}\mathbf{1}^{\top}=D\mathbf{1}^{\top}
. From the theory of stochastic approximation, we know that the iterates of (
4.3
) converge almost surely to the stable zeroes of
\dot{x}_{t}=s\bar{d}(1-2x_{t})
. Thus,
\bar{Z}_{t}\to 1/2
almost surely. Since the graph is bipartite, we have that
\bar{d_{v}}=\bar{d_{w}}=\frac{\bar{d}}{2}
. Hence,
\bar{Z}_{t}^{v}+\bar{Z}_{t}^{w}=2\bar{Z}_{t}\to 1
almost surely.

We now prove a useful lemma for the moments of certain linear functions of
\{Z_{t}(i)\}_{i\in[N]}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{N,m}
be the space of all
N\times N
matrices with the property that for each column of the matrix, the sum of the first
m
terms of that column is equal to the sum of the last
N-m
terms. That is,

\mathcal{M}_{N,m}=\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}:e_{m}M=(\mathbf{1}-e_{m})M\},

where
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
denotes the set of all
N\times N
matrices over
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Phi_{t}=Z_{t}Q
such that
Q\in\mathcal{M}_{N,m}
. The following hold under assumptions (I) and (II).

1.

Suppose that, in addition,
Q
is such that

. Then,
E[\Phi_{t}]\sim\frac{1}{t^{\theta}}
, where
\theta
is as defined in (

).

2.

Var(\Phi_{t})\sim\frac{1}{t^{\epsilon}}
for some

.

Proof.

1.

From (

) we get

\displaystyle E[Z_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{t}\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{t+1}}\right)+\frac{Cs(\mathbf{1}-Z_{t}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})}{T_{t+1}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{t+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\right)+\frac{Cs\mathbf{1}}{T_{t+1}}.

This implies

E[\Phi_{t+1}]=Z_{0}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t}H_{i}\right)Q+\sum_{i=0}^{t}\frac{Cs\mathbf{1}}{T_{i+1}}\left(\prod_{j=i+1}^{t}H_{j}\right)Q,

(4.6)

where
H_{j}=I-\frac{Cs}{T_{j+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
for
j\geq 1
. Now, using
\mathbf{1}\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}=\mathbf{1}
, we get that the second term in (

) is zero. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{Cs\mathbf{1}}{T_{i+1}}\left[\prod_{j=i+1}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{j+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\right)\right]Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\left(\mathbf{1}-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+2}}(\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1})\right)\left[\prod_{j=i+2}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{j+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\right)\right]Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\left(1-\frac{2Cs}{T_{i+2}}\right)\mathbf{1}\left[\prod_{j=i+2}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{j+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\right)\right]Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\left[\prod_{j=i+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{2Cs}{T_{j+1}}\right)\right]\mathbf{1}Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}.

Using the diagonalization of
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
, the first term in (
4.6
), can be rewritten as

Z_{0}P\left[\prod_{i=0}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\Lambda\right)\right]P^{-1}Q
. Using Euler’s approximation we have that, for
2\leq j\leq N
,
\prod_{i=0}^{t}\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\lambda_{j}\right)\sim t^{-\lambda_{j}}
. Hence, as

\prod_{i=0}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\Lambda\right)\to e_{1}^{\top}e_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\ldots&0\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(4.7)

Since the first row of
P^{-1}
is the left eigenvector of the matrix

corresponding to the eigenvalue
0
and is of the form
\alpha(\mathbf{1}-2e_{m})
for some

, we get that

e_{1}^{\top}e_{1}P^{-1}Q=\mathbf{0}_{N\times N},

(4.8)

because

. Thus,
E[\Phi_{t}]\to\mathbf{0}
as

, with rate
\frac{1}{t^{\theta}}
, (since

for

).

2.

From (
4.2

Var(Z_{t+1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t})=\frac{C^{2}}{T^{2}_{t+1}}Var(\chi_{t+1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t}).

Note that for

and using the conditional distribution of

from (

\displaystyle E[Var(Y_{t+1}(j)|{\mathcal{F}}_{t})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s(s-1)\sum_{l\in N(j)}E[{Z_{t}(l)}^{2}]+s\sum_{l\in N(j)}E[{Z_{t}(l)}]}{d_{j}}

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{s\sum_{l\in N(j)}E[{Z_{t}(l)}]}{d_{j}}\right)^{2}\eqqcolon\gamma_{t}(j).

Thus, we get

E[Var(Z_{t+1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t})]=\frac{C^{2}}{{T_{t+1}}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{t}(1)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\gamma_{t}(N)\end{pmatrix}\eqqcolon\frac{C^{2}}{{T_{t+1}}^{2}}G_{t}.

(4.10)

From (
4.5
) and (

), we get

\displaystyle Var(Z_{t+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[Var(Z_{t+1}|{\mathcal{F}}_{t})]+Var(E[Z_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}}{{T_{t+1}}^{2}}G_{t}+H_{t}^{\top}Var(Z_{t})H_{t},

where
H_{t}=I-\frac{Cs}{T_{t+1}}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})
. We have

\displaystyle Var(\Phi_{t+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\top}Var(Z_{t+1})Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\top}\left(\frac{C^{2}}{{T_{t+1}}^{2}}G_{t}+H_{t}^{\top}Var(Z_{t})H_{t}\right)Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\top}\left[\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}H_{t-i}^{\top}\right)Var(Z_{1})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}H_{i}\right)+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}\frac{C^{2}}{T_{k+1}^{2}}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t-k-1}H_{t-i}^{\top}\right)G_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}H_{i}\right)\right]Q.

Since
Var(Z_{1})=\frac{C^{2}}{T_{1}^{2}}G(0)
, we have

Var(\Phi_{t+1})=Q^{\top}\left[\sum\limits_{k=0}^{t}\frac{C^{2}}{T_{k+1}^{2}}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t-k-1}H_{t-i}^{\top}\right)G_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}H_{i}\right)\right]Q.

Using diagonalization of
I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle Var(\Phi_{t+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\top}\left[\sum\limits_{k=0}^{t}\frac{C^{2}}{T_{k+1}^{2}}(P^{-1})^{\top}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{t-k-1}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{t-i+1}}\Lambda\right)\right)P^{\top}G_{k}P\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\Lambda\right)P^{-1}\right]Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{\top}(P^{-1})^{\top}\Delta_{t}P^{-1}Q,

where
\Delta_{t}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{t}\frac{C^{2}}{T_{k+1}^{2}}\left[\prod_{i=0}^{t-k-1}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{t-i+1}}\Lambda\right)\right]P^{\top}G_{k}P\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\left(I-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\Lambda\right)
.

For
(p,q)\neq(1,1)
, the
(p,q)^{th}
element of
\Delta_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{t}\frac{C^{2}}{T_{k+1}^{2}}\left[\prod_{i=0}^{t-k-1}\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{t-i+1}}\lambda_{p}\right)\right]s_{k}(p,q)\prod_{i=k+1}^{t}\left(1-\frac{Cs}{T_{i+1}}\lambda_{q}\right),

where

is the
(p,q)^{th}
element of
P^{\top}G_{k}P
. Again, by using the Euler’s approximation, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(p,q)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}t^{-(\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q})}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}\frac{1}{k^{2-(\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q})}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)&\text{ if }\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q}>1,\\
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log t}{t}\right)&\text{ if }\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q}=1,\\
\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-(\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q})}\right)&\text{ if }\lambda_{p}+\lambda_{q}<1.\end{cases}

Since
\Delta_{t}(1,1)
converges to

, there exists a constant

. Since

Before we show that the urns synchronize almost surely on each partition, we will show that the fraction of balls of either colour in each urn attains an almost sure limit. Using the convergence of quasi-martingales (see

]
), we get the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

Z_{t}
admits an almost sure limit.

Proof.

From (
4.3
) and (

), we get
E[\bar{Z}_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]=\bar{Z}_{t}+\frac{2Cs}{T_{t+1}}(1/2-\bar{Z}_{t})
. This implies,

\displaystyle E[\bar{Z}_{t+1}-1/2]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[\bar{Z}_{t}]-1/2+\frac{2Cs}{T_{t+1}}(1/2-E[\bar{Z}_{t}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=0}^{t}\left(1-\frac{2Cs}{T_{k+1}}\right)(\bar{Z}_{0}-1/2)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}}(\bar{Z}_{0}-1/2).

Hence,

\left|E[\bar{Z}_{t}-1/2]\right|\sim\frac{1}{t^{2}}(\left|Z_{0}-1/2\right|).

(4.12)

Using (
4.5
), we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}E[\|E[Z_{t+1}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}]-Z_{t}\|]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cs\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\|\mathbf{1}-Z_{t}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\|]}{T_{t+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cs\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\|\mathbf{1}-\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1}(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})-(Z_{t}-\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1})(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\|]}{T_{t+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cs\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{E[\|\mathbf{1}-2\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1}\|+E[\|(Z_{t}-\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1})(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\|]}{T_{t+1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cs\left\{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\|\mathbf{1}-2\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1}\|}{T_{t+1}}+\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\|(Z_{t}-\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1})(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\|]}{T_{t+1}}\right\}

\displaystyle Cs\left\{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{2\|1\|E[|\bar{Z}_{t}-1/2|]}{T_{t+1}}+\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{E[\|(\bar{Z}_{t}\mathbf{1}-Z_{t})(I+\textbf{A}\textbf{D}^{-1})\|]}{T_{t+1}}\right\}

\bar{Z}_{t}=\frac{Z_{t}D{\mathbf{1}}^{T}}{\bar{d}}

Friedman-type Urns on Directed Graphs

The sampling and reinforcement dynamics described in section

has a natural analogue on directed graphs. In this case, for each urn, balls are sampled balls from the in-neighbourhood of that urn and the reinforcement is done (to self, out-neighbours or both) depending on the configuration of the drawn sample. We define the in-neighbours and out-neighbours of the

We extend the convergence result in Theorem
3.3
to the case of weakly connected directed graphs with no self-loops. Assume that the directed graph
\mathcal{G}
consists of
r
strongly connected components. Without loss of generality (after a renumbering of vertices, if required), assume that these components

between the components, if

Stochastic Approximation Theory

Stochastic approximation has been used extensively in recent times to analyze the behaviour of urn processes (for instance, see
[
1
,
23
]
). A recursion of the form

x_{t+1}=x_{t}+a_{t+1}\left(h(x_{t})+\Delta M_{t+1}+\epsilon_{t+1}\right),\qquad\forall t\geq 0

(A.1)

is called a stochastic approximation scheme if it satisfies the following conditions.

1.

\sum_{t\geq 1}a_{t}=\infty
and
\sum_{t\geq 1}a_{t}^{2}<\infty
.

2.

h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Lipschitz function.

3.

There exists a constant
\delta>0
, such that
E\left[\|\Delta M_{t+1}\|^{2}|\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}\right]\leq\delta(1+\|x_{t}\|^{2})
, almost surely
\forall t\geq 0
, where
\mbox{${\mathcal{F}}$}_{t}={\sigma(x_{0},M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})}
.

4.

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|x_{t}\|<\infty
, almost surely.

5.

\{\epsilon_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
is a bounded sequence such that
\epsilon_{t}\to 0
, almost surely as
t\to\infty
.

The main result (see Chapter 2 of
[
24
]
) implies that the iterates of the recursion for
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying the conditions (1)-(5) above converge almost surely to the stable limit points of the solutions of an ordinary differential equation given by
\dot{x}_{t}=h(x_{t})
. Recall that
x^{*}
is a stable limit point of the O.D.E.
\dot{x}_{t}=h(x_{t})
if the real parts of all the eigenvalues of the Jacobian
\frac{\partial h}{\partial z}(x^{*})
are non-positive.

Further, suppose there exists a differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
h=-\nabla f
. The stochastic approximation scheme can then be written as

x_{t+1}=x_{t}-a_{t+1}(\nabla f(x_{t})+\beta_{t}),

(A.2)

where
\{\beta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
- valued stochastic process. This recursion is also known as the stochastic gradient descent. We now state the main convergence theorem from
[
19
]
. the Let
S
be the set of stationary points of
f(\cdot)
, i.e.,
S=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\nabla f(x)=0\}
. Sequence
\{\gamma_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is defined by
\gamma_{0}=0
and
\gamma_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}a_{i}
.
For
t\in(0,\infty)
and
n\geq 0
,
\tilde{a}(n,t)
is the integer defined as
\tilde{a}(n,t)=\max\{k\geq n:\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{n}\leq t\}

Theorem A.1

(Theorem 2.1
[

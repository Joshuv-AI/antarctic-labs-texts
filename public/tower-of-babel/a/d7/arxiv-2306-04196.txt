L p L^{p} improving properties and maximal estimates for certain multilinear averaging operators
Chu-hee Cho, Jin Bong Lee, Kalachand Shuin

Abstract
. In this article we focus on L p L^{p} estimates for two types of multilinear lacunary maximal averages over hypersurfaces with curvature conditions. Moreover, we give a different proof for the bilinear lacunary spherical maximal functions. To obtain our results, we make use of the L 1 L^{1} -improving estimates of multilinear averaging operators. We also obtain L p L^{p} -improving estimates for certain multilinear averages by means of the nonlinear Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a compact and smooth hypersurface contained in a unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1)
with
\kappa
non-vanishing principal curvatures,
and
\Theta_{j}
be rotation matrices in
\mathbf{M}_{d,d}({\mathbb{R}})
for
j=1,2,\dots,m
.
We assume that
\{\Theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is mutually linearly independent.
Then for
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define

(1.1)

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x+\Theta_{j}y)~\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}(y),

where
F=(f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{m})
and
d\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}
is the normalized surface measure on
\mathcal{S}
.
We also consider another
m
-linear averaging operator defined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x+y_{j})~\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma}(\textsl{y}),\,\,(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})=\textsl{y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{md},

where
\Sigma
is a compact
(md-1)
dimensional smooth hypersurface contained in a unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{md}(0,1)
with
\kappa
non-vanishing principal curvatures.
Note that
\kappa
arising in (
1.1
) satisfies
1\leq\kappa\leq d-1
, while
\kappa
in (
1.2
) is
1\leq\kappa\leq md-1
.
Moreover, we are interested in the following lacunary maximal operators associated with (
1.1
) and (
1.2
):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x-2^{\ell}\Theta_{j}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathcal{S}}(y)\Big|,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x-2^{\ell}y_{j})~~\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\Sigma}(\textsl{y})\Big|.

The purpose of this article is to prove
L^{p}
-improving estimates of multilinear averaging operators defined by (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
Further, using this
L^{p}
-improving estimates we show
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness for
1/p=\sum^{m}_{j=1}1/p_{j}
of the multi-(sub)linear lacunary maximal functions
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{\Sigma}
.

Averaging operators given in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) and related maximal operators arise in many studies in multilinear harmonic analysis.
Since Coifman and Meyer
[
13
]
opened the path of multilinear harmonic analysis in 1975,
there have been significant developments in the area of multilinear harmonic analysis over the last few decades.
Among those achievements, we introduce works of Lacey and Thiele
[
25
,
26
]
in which they proved
L^{p}
-boundedness of the bilinear Hilbert transform given as

BHT_{\alpha}(f,g)(x):=p.v.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x-t)g(x-\alpha t)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t},\quad\alpha\not=0,1.

Their seminal work settled the long standing conjecture of Calderón.
Later, Lacey
[
24
]
studied
L^{p}
- boundedness of bilinear maximal operator

M_{\alpha}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{-t}^{t}|f(x-y)g(x-\alpha y)|~\mathrm{d}y,\quad\alpha\not=0,1,

which is related to the bilinear Hilbert transform.
One may regard averaging operators
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}
as a generalization of
M_{\alpha}
without the supremum because the condition
\alpha\not=0,1
corresponds to the linearly independent condition of
\{\Theta_{j}\}
.

On the other hand,
\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}
given in (
1.2
) is a direct analogue of a spherical averages
A^{t}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x)
for
t=1
, which is defined by

A^{t}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Thus we write
\textsl{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{md-1}}(\mathrm{F})(x)=A^{1}_{\mathbb{S}^{md-1}}(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{m})(x,\dots,x)
.
For studies on
\textsl{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{md-1}}(\mathrm{F})
, we recommend
[
32
,
1
,
36
,
14
]
and references therein.
In the literature,
A^{t}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
have been extensively studied in terms of maximal operators.
For the (sub)linear spherical maximal operator
M^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
defined by

M^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}|A^{t}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x)|:=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|

with
d\sigma
is the normalized surface measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, Stein
[

]
proved that for

, the spherical maximal operator

is bounded in

, if and only if

in 1976.
Later, Bourgain
[
10
]
obtained
L^{p}
boundedness of
M^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
for
p>2
.
Those restricted boundedness of
M^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
can be improved if one considers the lacunary spherical maximal operator, which is given by

M_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|A_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}^{2^{j}}f(x)|
.
Calderón
[
11
]
proved
L^{p}
estimates of the operator
M_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
for

and

.
After then, Seeger and Wright
[

showed

estimates of general lacunary maximal operators

for

, when the Fourier transform of the surface measure

of

satisfies

, for any

.
There are also

estimates for

(we call this

-improving estimates) of the spherical average

[
30
,
38
]
.

Lacey
[
23
]
used the

-improving estimates of spherical averages to prove sparse domination of the corresponding lacunary and full spherical maximal functions.
It is well known that sparse domination of an operator implies vector valued boundedness and weighted boundedness of that operator with respect to Muckenhoupt

weights
[

.
This idea has been extensively used to obtain sparse domination of several linear and sub-linear operators in the field of Harmonic analysis (See
[

The idea of Lacey
[

]
, together with

-improving estimates of certain bilinear averaging operators, can be used to study sparse domination of maximal operators associated with the bilinear operators.
We recommend

and references therein, which contains results of bilinear spherical maximal operator, bilinear maximal triangle averaging operators and bilinear product-type spherical maximal operators, respectively.

Recently, Christ and Zhou
[

studied

\mathcal{M}(f_{1},f_{2})(x):=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|B_{2^{\ell}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{2}f_{j}(x-2^{\ell}\gamma_{j}(t))\eta(t)~\mathrm{d}t\Big|,

In consequence, they have proved

is the normalized surface measure on the circle

.

-estimate of the operator

We first state
L^{1}
-improving and quasi-Banach estimates of the
m
-linear averaging operators
\mathcal{A}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}
.
Note that the following two propositions are derived by simple Fourier analysis and multilinear interpolation, and we will give a proof of the propositions for self-containedness.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(\mathrm{F})
be given in (
1.1
) and
\mathcal{S}
be a compact smooth hypersurface contained in
\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1)
with
\kappa\leq d-1
nonvanishing principal curvatures.
Let
\Theta=\{\Theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be a family of mutually linearly independent rotaion matrices.
Let also
\mathcal{V}^{ij}_{\kappa}=\{z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in[0,1]^{m}:z_{i}=z_{j}=\frac{\kappa+1}{\kappa+2},z_{l}=0,l\not=i,j\}
and
\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa})
be its convex hull.
Then for
(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{p_{m}})\in\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa})
we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

whenever
1\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{\kappa+2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})
be an average given by (
1.2
) over a compact smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
with
\kappa
nonvanishing principal curvatures with
(m-1)d<\kappa\leq md-1
.
Then for
1\leq p_{j}\leq 2
,
j=1,2,\dots,m
and
\frac{m+1}{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<\frac{2d+\kappa}{2d}
, the following
L^{1}
-improving estimates hold:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Moreover, we have for
\frac{m+1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}<\frac{2d+\kappa}{2d}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Let
1\leq p,p_{1},\dots,p_{m}<\infty
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
.
Then for
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
with
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{j}})\subset\mathbb{A}_{n_{j}}:=\{\xi_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:2^{n_{j}-1}\leq|\xi_{j}|\leq 2^{n_{j}+1}\}
,
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
j=1,\dots,m
,
we have

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}(\mathrm{F})\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

where
\delta=\delta(p,\kappa,m,d)>0
and
|n|=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}^{2}}
.

When
p>1
, one can obtain different
L^{p}
-improving estimates for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}
under specific choice of
\{\Theta_{j}\}
and
\mathcal{S}
.
In this case, we do not need any curvature condition on
{\mathcal{S}}
and only the dimension of surfaces matters.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{k}
be a
k
-dimensional
C^{2}
surface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We choose mutually linearly independent
\{\Theta_{j}\}
.
Moreover, we assume that for any choice of
\{j_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
with
2\leq\ell\leq k+1\leq m
, the family
\{\Theta_{j}\}
satisfies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\dim\Big(\text{span}_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\big(\{\Theta_{j_{i}}(y^{\prime},0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{k-1+\ell,d\},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\dim\Big(\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}\{\Theta_{j_{i}}(y^{\prime},0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:y^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq k+1-\ell.

The assumption (
1.9
) yields that dimension of intersection of any subset
\{\Theta_{j_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k+1}
of
\{\Theta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
equals to zero.
The following theorem is one of our main results:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m\geq d\geq 2
and
\mathcal{S}^{k}
be a
k
-dimensional
C^{2}
surface in
\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1)
.
Suppose that
\{\Theta_{j}\}
satisfies (
1.8
) and (
1.9
),
and

(1.10)

\displaystyle\frac{m-d+k}{m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d-k-1}{d}k,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(d-k)k}{d}.

Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{k}}^{\Theta}
is of strong type
(m,\dots,m,\frac{d}{d-k})
.
That is, we have

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}^{k}}^{\Theta}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{\frac{d}{d-k}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In our proof of Theorem
1.3

[
5
]

We give details on the inequality and the proof of Theorem

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1\leq p_{i}^{\circ}\leq\infty
,
\sum^{m}_{i=1}\frac{1}{p_{i}^{\circ}}=\frac{1}{p^{\circ}}
with
p^{\circ}\geq 1

.
Suppose that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}
satisfies the following Sobolev regularity estimates:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(\mathrm{F})\right\|_{L^{p^{\circ}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}^{\circ}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

where
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
with
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{j}})\subset\mathbb{A}_{n_{j}}:=\{\xi_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:2^{n_{j}-1}\leq|\xi_{j}|\leq 2^{n_{j}+1}\}
,
j=1,\dots,m
, and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(p,\kappa,m,d)>0
.
Then the lacunary maximal function
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}
maps

for
(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{p_{m}})\in\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}^{\circ})\cup\{(0,\dots,0)\}
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
,
where
\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}^{\circ})
denotes an interior of the convex hull of
\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa})
and the origin.
In particular, if one considers a lacunary maximal operator associated with
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, then the range of
p
becomes
p>\frac{d+1}{2d}
.

Observe that the multilinear averaging operator (

[
20

.

B_{\theta}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}f(x-y)g(x-\theta y)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),

\theta

On the other hand, one can actually obtain Sobolev regularity estimates for

Thus, another main result of this paper is the following lacunary maximal estimates for
\textsl{A}_{\Sigma}
:

Theorem 1.6

Lemma 2.1

Let

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{{Q}_{\bf{n}}}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x+\Theta_{j}y)\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)~\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)^{p}

3.
A Nonlinear Brascamp-Lieb inequality approach to
L^{p}
-improving estimates for
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}

3.1.
Nonlinear Brascamp-Lieb inequality

Let
f_{j}
be nonnegative integrable funtions,
L_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}
be linear surjections, and
c_{j}\in[0,1]
for
j=1,\cdots,m
.
We also identify a finite dimensional Hilbert sapce
H
and a Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, for instance we let
H={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
H_{j}={\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}
.
Then we can consider the linear Brascamp-Lieb inequality

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(f_{j}(L_{j}x)\Bigr)^{c_{j}}\,~\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}}f_{j}(x_{j})~\mathrm{d}x_{j}\Bigr)^{c_{j}},

where
\mathbf{L}=(H,\{H_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m},\{L_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m})
,
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})
, and
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
is the smallest such constant.
Here, we call
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
a Brascamp-Lieb datum, and
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
a Brascamp-Lieb constant.
There have been studies on nonlinear generalizations of the Brascamp-Lieb inequality.
Bennett, Carbery, and Wright
[
7
]
showed that (
3.1
) holds for
d_{j}=d-1
and
c_{j}=\frac{1}{m-1}
when
L_{j}
’s are smooth submersions supported in a sufficiently small neighborhood.
They also proved that
L_{j}
’s could be
C^{3}
mappings under certain transversality conditions on the submersions.
Later, Bennett and Bez
[
4
]
extended the results of
[
7
]
to general
d_{j}
and
C^{1,\beta}
mappings.
Recently, Bennett, Bez, Buschenhenke, Cowling, and Flock
[
5
]
proved the following nonlinear Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
be a Brascamp-Lieb datum.
Suppose that
B_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}
are
C^{2}
submersions in a neighborhood of a point
x_{0}
and
~\mathrm{d}B_{j}(x_{0})=L_{j}
,
j=1,2,\cdots,m
.
Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{U}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(f_{j}(B_{j}(x))\Bigr)^{c_{j}}\,~\mathrm{d}x\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{j}}}f_{j}(x_{j})~\mathrm{d}x_{j}\Bigr)^{c_{j}}.

Although Theorem
3.1
is stated with
C^{2}
submersions, the proof of
[
5
]
guarantees that the theorem still holds if one takes
C^{1+\theta}
submersions for any
\theta>0
.
It is known
[
6
]
that
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
is finite if and only if the following conditions hold.

(3.3)

\displaystyle\dim(V)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}\dim(L_{j}V)\quad\text{for all subspaces $V$ of ${\mathbb{R}}^{d}$},

(3.4)

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}d_{j}.

These conditions are called the transversality condition and the scaling condition, respectively.
We also present necessary conditions for finiteness of
\mathrm{BL}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{c})
:

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}\ker(L_{j})=\{0\},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}\geq 1.

But, it is not simple to check (
3.3
) for a given Brascamp-Lieb datum.
The following lemma may be useful in such verification.
First, we say a proper subspace
V_{c}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a critical subspace if it satisfies

\dim(V_{c})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}\dim(L_{j}V_{c}).

For a given subspace
V_{c}
, we split the Brascamp-Lieb datum into two parts,
(\mathbf{L}_{V_{c}},\mathbf{c})
and
(\mathbf{L}_{V_{c}^{\perp}},\mathbf{c})
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{V_{c}}

\displaystyle=(V_{c},\{L_{j}V_{c}\}_{1\leq j\leq m},\{L_{j,{V_{c}}}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{V_{c}^{\perp}}

Note that we can rewrite the condition as

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{m-1}{m}\geq\frac{d-k}{d}k-\Big(\frac{d-k}{d}-\frac{1}{m}\Big)n,\quad 0\leq n\leq k-1.

Note that (
3.23
) is reduced to

\frac{m-1}{m}\geq\frac{d-k}{d}k,

for all
0\leq n\leq k-1
when
m\geq d
.
Thus,
(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{p})
is a Brascamp-Lieb datum. Hence, by Proposition
3.3
we prove Theorem
1.3
.

4.
Proof of Theroem
1.5

Recall that the lacunary maximal function
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}
is defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|,

where
\mathcal{S}
has
\kappa
-nonvanishing principal curvatures and
\Theta=\{\Theta_{j}\}
is a family of mutually linearly independent rotation matrices.

Observe that for any fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can write the identity operator
I
as follows

(4.2)

\displaystyle I=P_{<\ell}+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P_{\ell+n}=P_{<\ell}+P_{\ell\leq}.

Then we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Big(P_{<\ell}f_{j}+P_{\ell\leq}f_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{<\ell}f_{j}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{\ell\leq}f_{j}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\alpha!(m-\alpha)!}\sum_{\tau\in S_{m}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha}P_{\ell\leq}f_{\tau(i)}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}P_{<\ell}f_{\tau(i)}\Bigr),

where the second summation runs over the symmetric group
S_{m}
over
\{1,\dots,m\}
.
For
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}=(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{m}
, we define

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha}P_{<\ell}f_{\tau(i)}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{\tau(i)}y)\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\tau(i)}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{\tau(i)}y)\Bigr)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha}f_{\tau(i)}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{\tau(i)}y)\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}P_{<\ell}f_{\tau(i)}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{\tau(i)}y)\Bigr)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\bf{n}}(\mathrm{F})

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{\ell+n_{j}}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\bf{n}}(\mathrm{F})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{\ell+n_{j}}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\bf{n}}(\mathrm{F})
corresponds to
\alpha=0
case in (
4.3
).
Therefore, the lacunary maximal function
\mathcal{M}^{\Theta}_{\mathcal{S}}
can be controlled by a constant mutiple of

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{\tau\in S_{m}}\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big(|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})|+|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})|\Big)+\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F}).

By similarity of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})
and
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})
together with the symmetry on
\tau\in S_{m}
, instead of the first summation it suffices to consider estimates for
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})
, which is given by

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha}P_{<\ell}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{j=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)\Bigr)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

Then the proof will be completed by combination of the following lemmas and induction on
m
-linearity:

Lemma 4.1
.

For
m=2
and
\alpha=1
we have

\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)\leq M_{HL}(f_{1})(x)\times M^{\Theta_{2}}_{\mathcal{S}}(f_{2})(x),

where
\mathrm{F}=(f_{1},f_{2})
and

\displaystyle M^{\Theta_{2}}_{\mathcal{S}}(f_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\int_{\mathcal{S}}f_{2}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{2}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\int_{\mathcal{S}}f_{2}\Big(\Theta_{2}(\Theta^{-1}_{2}x-2^{-\ell}y)\Big)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)|.

Proof.

For
m=2
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}}P_{<\ell}f_{1}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{1}y)\times f_{2}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{2}y)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|

It suffices to show
\sup_{y\in\mathcal{S}}|P_{<\ell}f(x-2^{-\ell}y)|\lesssim M_{HL}(f)(x)
, where
M_{HL}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Since
\phi_{\ell}(x)=2^{\ell d}\phi(2^{\ell}x)
, we have

\displaystyle P_{<\ell}f(x-2^{-\ell}y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(z)2^{\ell d}\phi(2^{\ell}(x-2^{-\ell}y-z))~~\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x+2^{-\ell}z)\phi(y-z)~~\mathrm{d}z.

Since
y
is contained in a compact surface
\mathcal{S}
, we have for any

|P_{<\ell}f(x-2^{-\ell}y)|\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f(x+2^{-\ell}z)|\frac{C_{N}}{(1+|z|)^{N}}~~\mathrm{d}z\leq M_{HL}(f)(x).

∎

Since
M_{HL},M^{\Theta_{2}}_{\mathcal{S}}
are bounded on

for

, we need to handle the summation of
M_{\mathbf{n}}
over
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
.
Note that for
\alpha=2
we have
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)\lesssim M_{HL}(f_{1})(x)\times M_{HL}(f_{2})(x)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{\kappa+2}
.
Then for
(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\cdots,\frac{1}{p_{m}})\in\text{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa})

\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\leq C(1+|\mathbf{n}|^{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}}.

In particular, we have
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{2d}{d+1}
when we consider averages over
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and

for some

\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}.

Proofs of Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
will be given in Section

and note that Lemma

1.13
).
Since

by definition, it follows from interpolation between Lemmas

and

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta^{\prime}|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}},\quad\delta^{\prime}>0,

whenever
\frac{1}{p}<\frac{2(\kappa+1)}{\kappa+2}
and
(1/p_{1},\dots,1/p_{m})
is in an interior of convexhull of
\textrm{conv}(\mathcal{V}_{\kappa})
and
(1/r_{1},\dots,1/r_{m})
.
Since

, this proves the theorem for
m=2
case inside of the convexhull.
Then together with interpolation with trivial

.

For the induction, we assume that Theorem

holds for
N
-linear operators with

.
Note that we already showed that
m=2

By the assumption, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\alpha=1,\dots,m
, we have

\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)\lesssim\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}M_{HL}(f_{\mu})(x)\times\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathcal{S}}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{\nu}y)\Big|~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Moreover, if we assume that Theorem
1.5
holds for
N
-linear operators with

.

Proof.

The first assertion of the lemma follows directly by the proof of Lemma

.
For the second assertion, it is just an

∎

We assume that Theorem

For general

and Young’s inequality.

For the second term, we make use of the vanishing property of
b_{Q}
.
Let
c_{Q}
be the center of
Q
.

\displaystyle P_{\tau}b_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\psi_{\tau}(x-y)-\psi_{\tau}(x-c_{Q}))b_{Q}(y)~~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla_{y}(\psi_{\tau})(x-c_{Q}-t(y-c_{Q})),y-c_{Q}\rangle~~\mathrm{d}t~b_{Q}(y)~~\mathrm{d}y.

Since
\psi
is of Schwartz class, it follows that
\frac{1}{|y-c_{Q}|}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla_{y}(\psi_{\tau})(x-c_{Q}-t(y-c_{Q})),y-c_{Q}\rangle~~\mathrm{d}t
is bounded by a constant multiple of

\frac{2^{\tau(d+1)}}{(1+2^{\tau}|x-c_{Q}-t(y-c_{Q})|)^{N}},

for any
N>0
.
Thus we apply Minkowski’s integral inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{\tau}b_{Q}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{2^{\tau(d+1)p}}{(1+2^{\tau}|x|)^{pN}}~~\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\times\int_{Q}|y-c_{Q}||b_{Q}(y)|~~\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\tau(d+1)}2^{-\tau\frac{d}{p}}s(Q)s(Q)^{d-\frac{d}{p}}\|b_{Q}\|_{p}.

This establishes

\|P_{\tau}b_{Q}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{\tau(1+\frac{d}{p^{\prime}})}s(Q)^{1+\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}}\|b_{Q}\|_{p}.

Therefore we have

(5.15)

\displaystyle\|P_{\tau}b\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{Q}\|P_{\tau}b_{Q}\|_{p}^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2^{\tau}s(Q))^{1+\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl(\sum_{Q}\|b_{Q}\|_{p}^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}=(2^{\tau}s(Q))^{1+\frac{d}{p^{\prime}}}\|b\|_{p}.

The first and the last equalities follow from the disjointness of
Q
’s. This gives a decay estimate when
n_{j}+\ell<i_{j}
.

Lastly, we assume that
\ell>i_{j}
so that for
x\in(C_{\mathcal{S}}Q)^{\complement}
and
z\in Q
, we have
{\rm dist}(x-2^{-\ell}y,z)\geq s(Q)
uniformly in
y\in\mathcal{S}
, because we choose
C_{\mathcal{S}}=5\max(1,\mathop{\rm diam}({\mathcal{S}}))
.
Thus it follows that

\displaystyle P_{n_{j}+\ell}b_{j,\gamma}^{i_{j}}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)=\widetilde{P}_{n_{j}+\ell}b_{j,\gamma}^{i_{j}}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y),

where the kernel of
\widetilde{P}_{\tau}
is given by

\psi_{\tau}(y)\mathbbm{1}_{|y|\geq s(Q)}(y).

Therefore, we obtain with help of (
5.15
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{\mathbf{n}}(b_{1}^{i_{1}},\dots,b_{m}^{i_{m}})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{E})}\lesssim\min_{j=1,\dots,m}\|\widetilde{P}_{n_{j}+\ell}\|_{p\to p}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|b_{j}^{i_{j}}\|_{p_{j}}.

Observe that for any
N>0

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{P}_{n_{j}+\ell}\|_{p\to p}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\psi_{n_{j}+\ell-i_{j}}(x)|\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\geq 1}(x)~~\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 2^{-N(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})}.

Thus we have
\|\widetilde{P}_{n_{j}+\ell}\|_{p\to p}\leq 2^{-\ell+i_{j}}
regardless of
n_{j}\geq 0
.
This proves the lemma.

5.1.4.
Proof of Lemma
5.2

It suffices to show that for any
i_{1}
,
i_{2}

(5.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\min_{i_{1},i_{2}}\min(1,2^{(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},2^{i_{j}-\ell})\lesssim|\mathbf{n}|\min(1,2^{|\mathbf{n}|-|i_{1}-i_{2}|}).

Note that

\displaystyle\min(1,2^{(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},2^{i_{j}-\ell})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}2^{i_{j}-\ell},&i_{j}<\ell;\\
1,&i_{j}-|\mathbf{n}|\leq\ell\leq i_{j};\\
2^{(|\mathbf{n}|-i_{j}+\ell)(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},&\ell<i_{j}-|\mathbf{n}|.\end{array}\right.

When
i_{1}\sim i_{2}
, then the left side of (
5.16
) is bounded by a costant multiple of
|\mathbf{n}|
.
Thus we consider the case of
i_{2}
is greater than
i_{1}+|\mathbf{n}|
.
Since
i_{2}>i_{1}+|\mathbf{n}|
, it follows that for
i_{1}-|\mathbf{n}|\leq\ell\leq i_{1}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\min_{i_{1},i_{2}}\min(1,2^{(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},2^{i_{j}-\ell})\leq 2^{(|\mathbf{n}|-i_{2}+\ell)(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})}\leq 2^{(|\mathbf{n}|-|i_{1}-i_{2}|)(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})}.

Similarly, we have for
i_{2}-|\mathbf{n}|\leq\ell\leq i_{2}

(5.19)

\displaystyle\min_{i_{1},i_{2}}\min(1,2^{(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},2^{i_{j}-\ell})\leq 2^{i_{1}-\ell}\leq 2^{|\mathbf{n}|-|i_{1}-i_{2}|}.

One can obtain the same bounds when
i_{1}>i_{2}+|\mathbf{n}|
by changing roles of
i_{1},i_{2}
in (
5.18
), (
5.19
).
Therefore we conclude that

\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\min_{i_{1},i_{2}}\min(1,2^{(n_{j}+\ell-i_{j})(1+\frac{d}{p_{j}^{\prime}})},2^{i_{j}-\ell})\lesssim|\mathbf{n}|\min(1,2^{|\mathbf{n}|-|i_{1}-i_{2}|}).

5.2.
Proof of Lemma
4.3

By the assumption (
1.13
), for
1\leq r_{1},\dots,r_{m}<\infty
with
1=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{r_{j}}
, we have

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(P_{n_{1}}f_{1},\dots,P_{n_{m}}f_{m})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|P_{n_{j}}f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}},\quad\delta>0.

We make use of the following scaling invariance of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}
:

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{\mathcal{S}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}P_{\ell+n_{j}}f_{j}(x-2^{-\ell}\Theta_{j}y)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathrm{d}x)}=2^{-\ell d}\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(P_{n_{1}}f_{1,-\ell},\dots,P_{n_{m}}f_{m,-\ell})\|_{L^{1}},

where
f_{j,-\ell}(x)=f_{j}(2^{-\ell}x)
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\ell d}\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\Theta}(P_{n_{1}}f_{1,-\ell},\dots,P_{n_{m}}f_{m,-\ell})\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\ell d}2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|P_{n_{j}}f_{j,-\ell}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\ell d}2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}2^{\frac{\ell d}{r_{j}}}\|P_{n_{j}+\ell}f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|P_{n_{j}+\ell}f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}.

We apply Hölder’s inequality to the last line and obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|P_{n_{j}+\ell}f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}^{r_{j}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{r_{j}}}.

Note that
(\sum_{j}\|P_{j}f\|_{p}^{p})^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}
for
p\geq 2
, which gives

\|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-\delta|\mathbf{n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{r_{j}}}.

This proves the lemma.

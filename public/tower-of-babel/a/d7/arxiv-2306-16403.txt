Moment inequalities for sums of weakly dependent random fields
F.Gilles Blanchard, S.Alexandra Carpentier, T.Oleksandr Zadorozhnyi

Abstract
We derive both Azuma-Hoeffding and Burkholder-type inequalities for partial sums over a rectangular grid of dimension d d of a random field satisfying a weak dependency assumption of projective type: the difference between the expectation of an element of the random field and its conditional expectation given the rest of the field at a distance more than δ \delta is bounded, in L p L^{p} distance, by a known decreasing function of δ \delta . The analysis is based on the combination of a multi-scale approximation of random sums by martingale difference sequences, and of a careful decomposition of the domain. The obtained results extend previously known bounds under comparable hypotheses, and do not use the assumption of commuting filtrations.

We will say that the random field is polynomially weakly dependent if Equation (

\varphi_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(r}}\right)=\min(1,cr^{-\alpha})

Under
(\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p))

we obtain a non-asymptotic upper bound for

in

-norm (Burkholder-type inequality), and for

We use the terminology of

random fields obeying Assumption

Doukhan 1994

and in the monograph
Dedecker et al. 2007
which in a certain sense extend the concept of mixing to multi-parameter processes.

The condition (

of similar assumptions made
in dimension 1 in previous literature, and allowing the approximation of a sequence of random variables by a martingale difference,
also related to the notion of mixingale (see
McLeish 1976

of a related nature are available in the literature, in particular for stationary sequences
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
,
Peligrad and Utev 2005

Dedecker and Merlevede 2015
also in dimension

for Banach-valued processes, and
Dedecker 1998
,
Dedecker 2001

for random processes in general dimension.

Specialized in dimension
d=1
, the condition (
1
)
recovers similar conditions involving the
L_{p}
norm of the projection of the past of random process, considered in
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
,
Peligrad and Utev 2005
.
In
Dedecker 1998
,
Dedecker 2001
,
in general dimension
d
the
\sigma
-algebra

is intersected with the past
\sigma
-algebra of
t
in the sense
of lexicographical ordering; this makes the condition in
Dedecker 1998

Dedecker 2001

slightly weaker than
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Furthermore, the condition
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)

implies bounds that improve over those of
Dedecker 2001

as will be discussed later.

In fact, the absence of a natural total order on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d>1

is precisely what makes the generalization to higher dimension challenging and constitutes
the interest of our contribution; in a nutshell, we are able to generalize the dyadic martingale
decomposition used in
Peligrad and Utev 2005

Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and additional arguments.
For a comparison to the theory of strong multidimensional martingales, see Section
3
.

Since under our definition of polynomially/exponentially weakly-dependent processes it holds
\varphi_{p}(0)=1
, and since for any fixed
t
the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{M}}_{t,k}}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

is nonincreasing in
k\geq 0
(by Jensen’s inequality and the fact that the
\sigma

{\mathcal{M}}_{t,k}
are
nested for fixed

), it is natural to think of
M_{p}
as equal to
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}
,
though we do not explicitly require it. Although we do not assume stationarity of the field, the fact
that
M_{p}
is independent of
t
indicates that our results are more geared towards fields that are in some sense
close to being stationary. However, motivated by applications in statistics, it is usual that instead of
a full random field over
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one wants to consider only a process
(X_{t})_{t\in{\mathcal{D}}}
defined over a subdomain
{\mathcal{D}}
, typically a finite hyperrectangular window. Suppose that a version of Assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
is satisfied for this process
but replacing
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by
{\mathcal{D}}
. Then if we extend
X_{t}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by “padding” it with random variables that
are constant (say equal to zero) on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\mathcal{D}}
, we obtain a random field satisfying Assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
and can
apply our results (observe that the extended field is not stationary).

In Section

(3)

3
Discussion and comparison to known results

In this section we compare our approach and bounds to some existing results for
dependent real random fields. In many of such results (see ex.
(
Doukhan, Léon and Portal 1984
,
Rio 2000
,
Dedecker 2001
)
) an exponential inequality for partial sums of bounded random fields is derived from
L_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}}}\right)
bound by optimizing over the value
p\geq 2
. The analysis of the properties of dependent random fields are
commonly based on approximations by
multidimensional martingales.
For this reason we first review briefly the different notions of multidimensional martingales, associated Burkholder-type inequalities for this reference case, and the relation to approximations of dependent random fields. In the following section we also discuss the case of Bernoulli random fields, that is, stationary transforms of an i.i.d. random field.

Notice that the weak dependency condition (
1
) when
d=1
is stronger than
the so-called "mixingale type" condition (see for example
McLeish 1976
), for which the process is not necessarily adapted to the conditioning filtration. The former is mentioned in the works
Dedecker et al. 2007
,
Dehling and Philipp 1982
to
characterize fading correlation between the past and the future of a discrete stochastic process.

3.1
Multiparameter martingale difference fields

Although our interest concerns chiefly dependent random fields, we first review briefly
several notions of multiparameter martingale difference fields as reference situations.
For
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we denote
k\leq_{cw}\ell
if the inequality holds coordinate-wise, i.e.
k_{i}\leq\ell_{i}
for all
i\in\llbracket d\rrbracket
,
k<_{cw}\ell
if the inequality is strict for at least one coordinate, and
k\ll_{cw}\ell
if the inequality is strict for
all
coordinates. Simplifying the general point of view to focus on
canonical filtrations in our setting,
define the
d

marginal
filtrations
({\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}_{i}:={\mathfrak{S}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{X_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})}:t_{k}\leq i}}\right\})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
k\in\llbracket d\rrbracket
; and for
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\wedge}_{t}:=\bigwedge_{k\in\llbracket d\rrbracket}{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}_{t_{k}}
, and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}_{t}:=\bigvee_{k\in\llbracket d\rrbracket}{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}_{t_{k}}
.
Note that
{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}_{t}={\mathcal{M}}_{t,0}
.
The field
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is then called a

(i)

weak
martingale difference field, if
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\wedge}_{t^{\prime}}}}\right]=0
for all
t^{\prime}<_{cw}t
;

(ii)

ortho-martingale
difference field, if
\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}_{t^{\prime}_{k}}\big]=0
for all
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
k\in\llbracket d\rrbracket

and
t^{\prime}_{k}<t_{k}
;

(iii)

strong
martingale difference field, if
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}_{t^{\prime}}}}\right]=0
for all
t^{\prime}\ll_{cw}t
.

The above terminology is in line with the general definitions introduced in the seminal paper
Cairoli and Walsh 1975
, see also
Basu and Dorea 1979
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}^{\wedge}_{t^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{(k)}_{t^{\prime}_{k}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}_{t^{\prime}}
,
we have the straightforward implication
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.
It is also well-known that
(i)
implies
(ii)
under the additional assumption of
commuting

marginal
\sigma
-fields
also known as condition (F4) from
Cairoli and Walsh 1975
, see

Khosnevisan 2002
for a modern account and equivalent formulations
of the assumption. However, we stress that
(iii)
does not imply
commuting marginal
\sigma
-fields in general.

The projective-type dependency assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
, in the edge case
\varphi_{p}(0)=1
and
\varphi_{p}(t)=0
for
t>0
, reduces to the strong martingale difference field assumption
(iii)
. On the other hand, under assumption
(iii)
a Burkholder-type inequality for random fields can be derived as a straightforward consequence of Burkholder’s inequality in dimension
1
. This holds since
(iii)
implies a (one-dimensional) martingale difference condition under the filtration
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}^{<_{\mathrm{lex}}}:={\mathfrak{S}}\{X_{t^{\prime}}:t^{\prime}<_{\mathrm{lex}}t\}
and the one-dimensional Burkholder’s inequality can be applied to the elements of the partial sum ordered in lexicographic order, i.e, we obtain

\big\lVert S_{\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\big\rVert_{p}\leq C_{p}n^{\frac{d}{2}}\max_{t\in\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}}}\right\rVert_{p}

(9)

by this direct argument. Therefore,
in the particular case
(iii)
our results do not bring something new (note also that in that case the optimal
multiplicative constant in the bound does not depend on the dimension
d
, while ours does).
Still, from this point of view the assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
in the general case can be interpreted as a form of limited departure of the random field from a strong martingale difference field.

Concerning the other types of multi-parameter martingales,
in the bivariate case a Burkholder-type inequality was established
by
Gundy and Varopoulos 1976
(Lemma 1 there) under assumptions
(i)
+(F4), and under assumption
(ii)
by
Métraux 1978
.
For general dimension
d
,
Fazekas 2005
established
Burkholder-type inequalities, again under the assumptions
(i)
+(F4). Both
Gundy and Varopoulos 1976
and
Fazekas 2005
use an iterative approach over
the dimension. These results are not recovered by our approach since the
assumptions are different.

Recent developments have used notions of approximation of a random field by ortho-martingales to obtain various results of a related nature

(
Volný 2015
,
El Machkouri and
Giraudo 2016
,
Giraudo 2018
,
Giraudo 2019
)
.
However, we note that these works use the terminology “ortho-martingale” to mean in fact
(i)
+(F4). We insist that we do not
use the assumption (F4) of commuting marginal filtrations in the present work,
so that we see our results as going in a different direction in nature.

3.2
Bernoulli random fields

A Burkholder-type inequality for so-called Bernoulli random fields is given in
El Machkouri, Volný and Wu 2013
. In that work a class of random fields of the form
X_{t}=g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon_{t-s},s\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\right)
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
where
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon_{i}}}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are i.i.d. random variables and
g
is some bounded measurable function is considered. Typical examples which belongs to this class are linear random fields and Volterra random fields (see examples in
Sang and Xiao 2018
and Section 2 in
Giraudo 2019
). Functional central limit theorems
Biermé and Durieu 2014
,
Klicnarová, Volný and
Wang 2016
, a large deviation principle
Sang and Xiao 2018
and a variant of law of the iterated logarithm for partial sums of this class of random fields
Giraudo 2022
have been established. For these types of random fields a following dependence measure (originally introduced in
Wu 2005
) is introduced. Namely, for a stochastic random field
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X_{t}}}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, such that
X_{t}\in L_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}}}\right)
define the coefficient
\delta_{t,p}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}-X_{t}^{*}}}\right\rVert_{p}
, where the coupled process
X^{*}_{t}
is given by
X^{*}_{t}=g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varepsilon^{*}_{t-s},s\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\right)
and
\varepsilon^{*}_{j}=\varepsilon_{j}\mathbb{I}_{j\neq\bm{0}}+\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\bm{0}}\mathbb{I}_{j=\bm{0}}
, with
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\bm{0}}
being
an independent copy of
\varepsilon_{\bm{0}}
.
In this framework, Proposition 1 of
El Machkouri, Volný and Wu 2013
implies following the Burkholder-type inequality for the partial sums of
X_{t}
:

\displaystyle\big\lVert S_{\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\big\rVert_{p}\leq\sqrt{2p}{n^{d/2}}\Delta_{p},

where
\Delta_{p}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\delta_{i,p}<\infty
.

In the one-dimensional case,
a particular situation is when the field
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
causal
with respect to the underlying i.i.d. field
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}
, that is,

X_{t}=g(\varepsilon_{t-s},s\geq 0)
. In this
situation, as pointed out in
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
, if we denote

{\mathcal{F}}_{k}:=\sigma((\varepsilon_{t})_{t\leq k})
, it holds by Jensen’s and stationarity

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{M}}_{t,k}}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{k}|{\mathcal{M}}_{k,k}}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{k}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{k}|{\mathcal{F}}_{0}}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{k}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert g(\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon_{k-1},\ldots)-g(\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon_{k-1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon^{\prime}_{-1},\varepsilon^{\prime}_{-2},\ldots)}}\right\rVert_{p},

so that assumption
WD
can be related to quantities that are tractable in
many models (see
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
for more discussion). Notice that an analogous notion of a "causal field" in higher dimension would be
of the form
X_{t}=g(\varepsilon_{u},u\not\gg_{cw}t)

(with the notation
\gg_{cw}
as introduced in Section
3.1
), a notion that has been considered in past literature (e.g.
Giraudo 2021a
, Corollary 3.5). Observe that in the Bernoulli random field model, the marginal filtrations
for the i.i.d. field
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are commuting, so that tools based on ortho-martingale approximation mentioned in the previous
section can be applied as well (see ex.
Giraudo 2021b
,
Giraudo 2021a
).
In such a setting we can, by the same token as in
dimension 1, relate assumption
WD
to tractable quantities.
To deal with non-causal Bernoulli fields however will require to relax assumption
WD
to the non-adapted case (wherein we condition with respect
to the field
(\varepsilon_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
rather than
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
);
which is out of the scope of the present work.

3.3
L_{p}(\mathbb{P})-
projective criterion

In the work
Dedecker 2001
, a Burkholder-type inequality for random fields is obtained under an
L_{p}-
projective dependence criterion; it takes the form

\displaystyle\big\lVert S_{\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\big\rVert_{p}\leq\sqrt{2p\sum_{t\in\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}b_{t,p/2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X}}\right)},

(10)

where
b_{t,\alpha}(X)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}^{2}}}\right\rVert_{\alpha}+\sum_{k\in V_{t}^{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|k-t}}\right\rvert}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{\alpha}
(we assume the
field is centered for simplicity),

p\geq 2
,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|x-y}}\right\rvert=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|x_{i}-y_{i}}}\right\rvert
,
V_{t}^{1}
denotes the set of all elements which precede
t
in lexicographic order on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\ell}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]
is the conditional expectation with respect to the
\sigma-
algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{V^{\ell}_{t}}
, where
V_{t}^{\ell}
is the subset of elements from
V^{1}_{t}
which are at distance at least
\ell
from
t
.
In the case where
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X}}\right)_{t\in{\mathcal{D}}}
is a strong martingale difference random field then
b_{t,p/2}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}^{2}}}\right\rVert_{p/2}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{t}}}\right\rVert_{p}^{2}
and (
10
) implies (
9
) with a constant
C_{p,d}={\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{p})
.

Let us analyze briefly the estimates obtained via bound (
10
) under the projective weak
dependency assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}^{\prime}(p)

(that is,
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
with
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{t,r}
in place of

{\mathcal{M}}_{t,r}
, see (
2
)).
By construction of the
\sigma-
algebras, it holds
{\mathcal{F}}_{V_{t}^{r}}\subseteq{{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{t,r}}
,
thus under assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}^{\prime}(p)
, by
the generalized Hölder’s inequality
it holds
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{k}\mathbb{E}_{|k-t|}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p/2}\leq\big\lVert X_{k}\big\rVert_{p}\big\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{t,|k-t|}\big]\big\rVert_{p}\leq M_{p}^{2}\varphi_{p}(|k-t|)
.
Hence,
for the right-hand-side of Inequality (
10
) when
p\geq 2
we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{2p\sum_{t\in\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}b_{t,p/2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X}}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq{M_{p}\sqrt{2p\Big(\sum_{t\in\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\Big(1+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\varphi_{p}(r)\Big)\Big)},}

Now, if
\varphi_{p}(r)\lesssim r^{-\alpha}
, plugging this into (
10
) yields
a bound of order
O\big(n^{-\big(\frac{d}{2}\wedge\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)}\big)

for
n^{-d}\big\lVert S_{\llbracket n\rrbracket^{d}}\big\rVert_{p}
, while Corollary
2
entails
a bound in
O\big(n^{-\big(\frac{d}{2}\wedge\alpha\big)}\big)
.
Thus, under assumption
WD
’ the estimate obtained from Corollary
2

improves over that obtained via the bound (
10
)
(in particular extending the range of
\alpha
for which a convergence of
order
O(n^{-d/2})
is granted).
On the other hand, it should be noted that under the stronger
assumption of control of the
\alpha
-mixing coefficient
between the
\sigma
-algebras
{\mathfrak{S}}(X_{t})
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{t,k}
, using Rio’s covariance inequalities
Rio 1993
one can infer both assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}^{\prime}(p)
for a certain
function
\varphi_{p}(k)
but also

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{k}\mathbb{E}_{|k-t|}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}/2\lesssim\varphi_{p}(|k-t|)^{2}
. In this scenario, if
\varphi_{p}(r)\lesssim r^{-\alpha}
,
bound (
10
) and Corollary
2
yield a bound of the same order
Dedecker
.

4
Proof of main result

To prove Theorem
1
it turns out to be more convenient to consider rectangles starting at the origin. We therefore introduce the notation

\llbracket k\rrbracket_{0}:=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}
if
k\geq 1
and
\llbracket k\rrbracket_{0}=\emptyset
if
k=0
, and
below we consider rectangles of the form
{\mathcal{R}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\llbracket N_{i}\rrbracket_{0}
.

For sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
c\in\mathbb{Z}
,
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we will use the standard notation

\displaystyle cA

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{ca:a\in A}}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d};

\displaystyle A+B

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B}}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(If
A=\emptyset
we denote
cA:=\emptyset
,

A+B:=\emptyset
.)

Before we begin the proof proper, we give a few words
of informal overview of its structure. The main principle
of the proof is a decomposition of each term
X_{t}-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]

as a "telescopic" series with terms
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k}(t)}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k-1}(t)}}\right]
, where
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k}(t)

is a suitable filtration generated by blocks
(which we will call
cells
) of the
random field at a dyadic scale
2^{k}
. Each of these terms
can be then summed over the corresponding dyadic cells and
gives rise to a martingale difference "at scale
k
" (i.e.
indexed by dyadic integer vectors);
for each
k
the corresponding dyadic martingale can be controlled by the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality.
This canvas follows the general line of arguments
used by
Peligrad and Utev 2005
,
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
in dimension
d=1
.

Going to higher dimension, we run into the following
additional challenges:

\bullet

There is no natural total order on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In order
to use martingale arguments, we need to carefully define
a specific total order on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to get the martingale structure
while ensuring a form of compatibility of filtrations across scales;
this construction will be explained in detail in Section
4.2
.

\bullet

We need to control

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k}(t)}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k-1}(t)}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

using the
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
assumption,
and this is directly linked
to
\varphi_{p}(d_{k}(t))
, where

d_{k}(t)
is the (supremum) distance
of
t
to the border of the dyadic cell it belongs to. Directly summing
these quantities over cells gives rise to good estimates in dimension 1
(see
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
), but it turns out that the estimates obtained this
way for rectangles in dimension
d\geq 2
are suboptimal.
The reason is that there are “too many” elements
in the cell that are close to its boundary,
making a too large contribution in the
sum over the cell.
This is why, to alleviate this issue, we will at first
exclude the elements which are close to boundaries of a cell at any scale, which we will call the (multiscale) "frame", so that the remaining elements are then sufficiently “separated” from the cell boundaries. The sum of elements in the "frame" will then be dealt with by an inductive argument.
This framed decomposition and the inductive argument will be explained
first, in the coming section.

4.1
Framed decomposition and main inductive step

We prove the theorem by induction on the size of the rectangle
{\mathcal{R}}
.

We consider the case
p<\infty
only; the arguments for the case
p=\infty
are the same for the remainder of the proof.
For
{\mathcal{R}}
reduced to a single element
\bm{0}
, the claim obviously holds. Now, assume the claim is established for any rectangle
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\subsetneq{\mathcal{R}}
.
We use the following construction: for any integer
\delta<2^{k}
let

\Lambda_{k,\delta}:=2^{k}\mathbb{N}_{>0}+\llbracket\delta\rrbracket_{0}.\qquad(\text{If }\delta=0,\text{ then }\Lambda_{k,\delta}:=\emptyset.)

(11)

Let
{\bm{\delta}}=(\delta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a fixed sequence
of integers with
\delta_{k}\leq 2^{k}
,
k\geq 1
. Define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{{\bm{\delta}}}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\Lambda_{k,\delta_{k}}\qquad{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{N}\setminus\Lambda_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}\right)^{d};\qquad{\mathfrak{F}}^{c}_{{\bm{\delta}}}:=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}.

(12)

We call
{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}
the “framed set” and
{\mathfrak{F}}^{c}_{{\bm{\delta}}}
the “frame”. These sets are illustrated
on Figure
2
. We use the decomposition
{\mathcal{R}}=({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})\uplus({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}^{c}_{{\bm{\delta}}})
. By the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}^{c}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}.

(13)

The proof proceeds as follows. The first and main term will be controlled by a multiscale martingale
decomposition and lead to the crucial estimate
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{2}\Psi_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{R}}}}\right)
where

C_{p}=4\sqrt{p}
. The proof of this estimate is postponed to
Proposition
4
below.

{\mathfrak{F}}_{\bm{\delta}}^{c}

{\mathfrak{F}}_{\bm{\delta}}

The second, remainder term will be dealt with via the induction hypothesis. We first decompose
{\mathfrak{F}}_{\bm{\delta}}^{c}
as a disjoint union of
product sets, writing
\Lambda_{\bm{\delta}}^{c}:=\mathbb{N}\setminus\Lambda_{\bm{\delta}}
:

therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{\bm{\delta}}^{c}}}}\right\rVert_{p}=\bigg\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{d}S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i}}\bigg\rVert_{p}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i}}}}\right\rVert_{p}.

(14)

We introduce the following notation. For a finite set
B\subset\mathbb{N}
, and an integer
j\in\llbracket\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|B}}\right\rvert\rrbracket_{0}
, denote

(j:B)
the
(j+1)
-th element of
B
in increasing order. For a finite product set
{\mathbf{A}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}A_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}

define
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{A}}):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\llbracket\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|A_{i}}}\right\rvert\rrbracket_{0}

a

,
and the “compressed” version of the restriction of the process

to

as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\widetilde{X}}^{({\mathbf{A}})}_{t}=X_{t:{\mathbf{A}}},&t\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{A}}),\\

\end{cases}\quad\quad\text{ then it holds }\qquad{\widetilde{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{A}})}^{({\mathbf{A}})}:=\sum_{t\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{A}})}{\widetilde{X}}^{({\mathbf{A}})}_{t}=S_{\mathbf{A}}.

Since
\Delta_{i}
is a product set, so is

, and we can apply the above ”compression principle”.
Using assumption (
3
), the side-length of the rectangle

and

By Lemma
3
below, the “compressed” process
\big({\widetilde{X}}^{({\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i})}_{t}\big)_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfies the same weak-dependency condition (

) as the original process. Applying the induction hypothesis to the process

over
the rectangle
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i})
, we obtain

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{R\cap\Delta_{i}}}}\right\rVert_{p}=\big\lVert{\widetilde{S}}_{{\mathcal{K}}(R\cap\Delta_{i})}^{(R\cap\Delta_{i})}\big\rVert_{p}\leq C_{p}\Psi_{p}({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i})).

We estimate this upper bound using (

\displaystyle\Psi_{p}({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{R}}\cap\Delta_{i}))

(18)

Finally combining Equation (

4.2
Multiscale martingale decomposition

As announced previously, the following estimate for
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}
is crucial.
The main argument resides on a multi-scale martingale decomposition, the principle of which was
used in dimension
d=1
in
Peligrad, Utev and Wu 2007
. However, in dimension
d\geq 2

the conditioning
\sigma
-algebra

{\mathcal{M}}_{t,k}
in Assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
cannot be expressed as a “past” for a total order on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
and extending the argument requires a more involved construction.

Proposition 4
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\llbracket N_{i}\rrbracket_{0}
be a
d
-dimensional rectangle of side-lengths
N_{i}\geq 1
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, and
m({\mathcal{R}}):=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\lfloor\log_{2}N_{i}\rfloor
.
Let
{\bm{\delta}}=(\delta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a fixed nondecreasing sequence
of integers with
\delta_{k}\leq 2^{k}
,
k\geq 1
(put
\delta_{0}=0
), and let
{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}
be as defined in (
12
).

Assume the process
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X_{t}}}\right)_{t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
satisfies the weak dependency assumption

\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
for some with
p\in[2,\infty]
. If
p<\infty
, it holds

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{p}\Psi_{p}({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{R}}),

(19)

where
C_{p}:=4\sqrt{p}
;
and if
p=\infty
,
then it holds

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{SG}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\infty}\Psi_{p}({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{R}}),

(20)

where
C_{\infty}:=10
and we recall that
\Psi_{p}({\bm{\delta}},{\mathcal{R}})
is given by (
4
).

The key element of the proof of
is a tree-like recursive ordering over
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. To define it we introduce the following notation. For
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
define the "dyadic projection at scale
k
" as
\pi_{k}(t):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lfloor 2^{-k}t_{i}\rfloor 2^{k}}}\right)_{1\leq i\leq d}\in 2^{k}\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Observe that
\pi_{0}(t)=t
, and that
\pi_{k}(t)=\bm{0}
for
k\geq\log_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert t}}\right\rVert_{\infty}
.
Let
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
denote the lexicographical order on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. Denote
<_{\mathrm{lex}}
to be the associated strict order relation.
For two elements
t,t^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, define

\kappa(t,t^{\prime})=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\}-1.

(21)

Note that
\kappa(t,t^{\prime})
is always well-defined, since
\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})=\bm{0}
for
k\geq\max(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert t}}\right\rVert_{\infty},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert t^{\prime}}}\right\rVert_{\infty})
, hence
the minimum in Equation (
21
) is over a non-empty set. Furthermore
\kappa(t,t^{\prime})=-1
iff
t=t^{\prime}
. We define the following order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{N}^{d}

t\preceq t^{\prime}\;\;\;\text{ iff }\;\;\;\text{ either }\kappa(t,t^{\prime})=-1\text{ or }\pi_{\kappa(t,t^{\prime})}(t)<_{\mathrm{lex}}\pi_{\kappa(t,t^{\prime})}(t^{\prime}).

(22)

It is straightforward to check that
\preceq
is a total order over
\mathbb{N}^{d}
since
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
is a total order over
\mathbb{N}^{d}
. This order can be described as the co-lexicographical order for the (one-to-one) sequence representation
(\pi_{k}(t))_{k\geq 0}
of
t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, where the base order for the elements of the sequence is the usual lexicographical order. Equivalently, this is the co-lexicographical order on the (infinite) binary representation
((\lfloor t_{i}2^{-k}\rfloor\text{ mod }2)_{i=d,\ldots,1})_{k\geq 0}
, where the vectorization is along (reverse) dimension first, then along scale.
See Figure
3
an illustration of the order
\preceq
.

Figure 3:
Illustration of the total order
\preceq
for
d=2
. The numbers indicate the
total ordering of the cells of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
according to
\preceq
.
The successive “dyadic projections”
\pi_{k}(t)
for a particular point

t
are given (dashed line). For each projection
\pi_{k}(t)
, dotted lines
point to the possible positions of
\pi_{k-1}(t^{\prime})
such that
\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
.

For
t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, let

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)

\displaystyle:=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\prec\pi_{k}(t)\},

(23)

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\preceq\pi_{k}(t)\},

(24)

where
t\prec t^{\prime}
indicates that
t
is strictly less than
t^{\prime}
for the order
\preceq
. Next we need the following Lemma which describes properties of the order
\preceq
.

Lemma 5
.

Let ordering
\leq_{cw}
be the partial order on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that for
t=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}}}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
t^{\prime}=\big(t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{d}\big)\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we say that
t\leq_{cw}t^{\prime}
iff
t_{i}\leq t^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\in\llbracket d\rrbracket
. The following statements hold true:

(0)

For any
k,\ell
such that
k\leq\ell
it holds
\pi_{\ell}\circ\pi_{k}=\pi_{k}\circ\pi_{\ell}=\pi_{\ell}
.

(i)

The partial order
\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}

is compatible with both the total orders
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
and
\preceq
, meaning that

t\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}t^{\prime}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;t\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}t^{\prime}\text{ and }t\preceq t^{\prime}.

(ii)

For any
t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq\ell
it holds
\pi_{k}(t)\preceq\pi_{\ell}(t)
. In particular, in case
\ell=0
it holds
\pi_{k}(t)\preceq t
.

(iii)

All applications
\pi_{k}
are monotone nondrecreasing with respect to
\preceq
:

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\;\;\forall t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\qquad t\preceq t^{\prime}\Rightarrow\pi_{k}(t)\preceq\pi_{k}(t^{\prime}).

(iv)

For an
integer
k
put
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,0}:=\llbracket 2^{k}\rrbracket_{0}^{d}
, and, for
b\in 2^{k}\mathbb{N}^{d}
, put
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{b}}\right\}+{\mathcal{C}}_{k,0}
.
For any
t\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}
,
it holds
\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(b)=b
,

\Pi^{\prec}_{k}(t)=\Pi^{\prec}_{k}(b)
and
\Pi_{k}(t)=\Pi_{k}(b)
.

(v)

For any
t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)

\displaystyle=\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:t^{\prime}\prec\pi_{k}(t)\},

(25)

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\pi_{k}(t)}.

(26)

(vi)

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, it holds

\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)\subseteq\Pi^{\prec}_{k-1}(t)\subseteq\Pi_{k-1}(t)\subseteq\Pi_{k}(t).

(27)

Proof

\mathbf{(0)}
For
\ell\geq k
, the equality
\pi_{k}\circ\pi_{\ell}=\pi_{\ell}
follows directly from the
fact that
\lfloor 2^{\ell-k}\lfloor 2^{-\ell}t\rfloor\rfloor=2^{\ell-k}\lfloor 2^{-\ell}t\rfloor
,
while the equality
\pi_{\ell}\circ\pi_{k}=\pi_{\ell}
follows from
\lfloor 2^{k-\ell}\lfloor 2^{-k}t\rfloor\rfloor=\lfloor 2^{-\ell}t\rfloor
which is easy to check.

\mathbf{(i)}
The implication for the lexicographical order is obvious;
concerning the order
\preceq
, note that obviously all the mappings
\pi_{k}
for
k\geq 0
are non-decreasing for the
partial order
\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}
, i.e.
t\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}t^{\prime}
implies
\pi_{k}(t)\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
, in turn
implying
\pi_{k}(t)\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
for all
k
, which finally entails
t\preceq t^{\prime}
from the definition.

\mathbf{(ii)}
The claim follows directly from
\mathbf{(i)}
since
it follows from the definition that
\pi_{k}(t)\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}\pi_{\ell}(t)

if
k\geq\ell
.

\mathbf{(iii)}
Assume
t\prec t^{\prime}
and let
\kappa=\kappa(t,t^{\prime})\geq 0
. Then by definition
of the order
\preceq
, for
\ell=\kappa+1
it holds
\pi_{\ell}(t)=\pi_{\ell}(t^{\prime})
, and therefore also further for any
\ell>\kappa
,
since
\pi_{\ell}=\pi_{\ell}\circ\pi_{\kappa+1}
by point
\mathbf{(0)}
.
Thus, for
k>\kappa

it holds
\pi_{k}(t)\preceq\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
.
On the other hand, it holds
\pi_{\kappa}(t)<_{\mathrm{lex}}\pi_{\kappa}(t^{\prime})
and
for
\ell<\kappa
,
\pi_{\ell}(t)\neq\pi_{\ell}(t^{\prime})
. For
k\leq\kappa
, put
u:=\pi_{k}(t),u^{\prime}:=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
, then
for any
\ell
we have by point
\mathbf{(0)}
:
\pi_{\ell}(u)=\pi_{\max(\ell,k)}(t)
and
\pi_{\ell}(u^{\prime})=\pi_{\max(\ell,k)}(t^{\prime})
.
It follows that
\kappa(u,u^{\prime})=\kappa
and that
the conditions for
u\prec u^{\prime}
are met.
In both cases we have
\pi_{k}(t)\preceq\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
.

\mathbf{(iv)}
For
u\in 2^{k}\mathbb{N}
, it holds
\lfloor 2^{-k}(u+v)\rfloor=u

iff
v\in\llbracket 2^{k}\rrbracket_{0}
. It follows that for
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
iff
t^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\pi_{k}(t)}
. The claims follow from the definitions of
\pi_{k},\Pi_{k}^{\prec}
and
\Pi_{k}
.

\mathbf{(v)}
For
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, if
t^{\prime}\prec\pi_{k}(t)
then
\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\preceq t^{\prime}\prec\pi_{k}(t)
, from point
\mathbf{(ii)}
. Conversely, if
t^{\prime}\succeq\pi_{k}(t)
, then

\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\succeq\pi_{k}(\pi_{k}(t))=\pi_{k}(t)
, by
\mathbf{(iii)}
.
Hence
t^{\prime}\prec\pi_{k}(t)
iff
\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\prec\pi_{k}(t)
. This establishes (
25
). Concerning (
26
), we have seen above
(see proof of
\mathbf{(iv)}
) that
\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\}={\mathcal{C}}_{k,\pi_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)}
, therefore

\Pi_{k}(t)=\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)\cup\{t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:\pi_{k}(t)=\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\}=\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{k,\pi_{k}(t)}.

\mathbf{(vi)}
It holds
\pi_{k-1}(t)\succeq\pi_{k}(t)
from
\mathbf{(ii)}
.
Then from (
25
), we deduce the inclusion
\Pi_{k}^{\prec}(t)\subseteq\Pi^{\prec}_{k-1}(t)
. The inclusion
\Pi^{\prec}_{k-1}(t)\subseteq\Pi_{k-1}(t)

is immediate from the definitions (
23
), (
24
).
Finally, for any
t^{\prime}\in\Pi_{k-1}(t)
, by definition
\pi_{k-1}(t^{\prime})\preceq\pi_{k-1}(t)
, so
by
\mathbf{(iii)}
and
\pi_{k}\circ\pi_{k-1}=\pi_{k}
, it holds
\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})\preceq\pi_{k}(t)
, hence
t^{\prime}\in\Pi_{k}(b)
, proving the last inclusion.

\square

As announced at the beginning of the proof, the role of excluding elements

Lemma 7

is compatible with the partial coordinate-wise order
\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}
.
This implies in particular that any
t^{\prime}
such that
\pi_{k}(t)\leq_{\mathrm{cw}}t^{\prime}
satisfies

\pi_{k}(\pi_{k}(t))=\pi_{k}(t)\preceq\pi_{k}(t^{\prime})
, and thus cannot belong to
\Pi^{\prec}_{k}(t)
. Therefore, for any
t^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prec}_{k}(t)
, there exists a coordinate
i
such that
t^{\prime}_{i}<\pi_{k}(t_{i})
. In particular,
\pi_{k}(t_{i})>0
, hence
\pi_{k}(t_{i})\in 2^{k}\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. On the other hand, if we assume
t\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\Lambda_{k,\delta})^{d}
then
t_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus(2^{k}\mathbb{N}_{>0}+\llbracket\delta\rrbracket_{0})
. Since
t^{\prime}_{i}<\pi_{k}(t_{i})\leq t_{i}
, it must hold
t_{i}-t^{\prime}_{i}\geq t_{i}-\pi_{k}(t_{i})+1\geq\delta+1
, implying the claim.

\square

We how have all ingredients to establish Proposition
4
.

Proof
[of Proposition
4
]
We use the decomposition (
29
) with
A={\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{\delta}
, so that by the triangle inequality

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert S_{{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}}}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m({\mathcal{R}})+1}\Bigg\lVert\sum_{b\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})}Z_{b,k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})\Bigg\rVert_{p},

(32)

where
Z_{b,k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})
is defined in (
30
), (
31
).

We now estimate the norm of the martingale increments
Z_{b,k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})
using Assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)
. We will denote below
Z_{b,k}=Z_{b,k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})
and
S_{k}=\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}})
to lighten notation.
As a direct consequence of Lemma
7
, for any

it holds
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}^{\prec}(t)\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{t,\delta_{k}+1}
(as defined in Assumption
\bm{\mathrm{WD}}(p)

Therefore, using this property and Jensen’s inequality for
k=0
we get:

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert Z_{b,0}}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{b}-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{b}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b}}\right)\big]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{b}}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{b}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b}}\right)}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{b}-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{b}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert X_{b}-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{b}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{b}|{\mathcal{M}}_{b,1}\big]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{b}}}\right]}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq M_{p}(\varphi_{p}(0)+\varphi_{p}(1)),

(33)

while for
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert Z_{b,k}}}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\bigg\lVert\sum_{t\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)\big]}}\right)\bigg\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Big\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)\big]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]\Big\rVert_{p}+\Big\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prec}_{k-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(t}}\right)\big]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]\Big\rVert_{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t\in{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}\cap{\mathfrak{F}}_{{\bm{\delta}}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Big\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{M}}_{t,\delta_{k}+1}\big]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]\Big\rVert_{p}+\Big\lVert\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}|{\mathcal{M}}_{t,\delta_{k-1}+1}\big]-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[X_{t}}}\right]\Big\rVert_{p}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}}}\right\rvert M_{p}\big(\varphi_{p}(\delta_{k}+1)+\varphi_{p}(\delta_{k-1}+1)\big).

(34)

Note that we can subsume (
33
) into (
34
) by putting formally
\delta_{-1}:=-1
.
By Lemma
6
, the sequence
(Z_{b,k},{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(b))_{b\in S_{k}}
is a martingale
difference sequence (over
b
for fixed
k
), therefore for
p\in[2,\infty)
we apply
the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality for martingales, with optimal constant obtained from Theorem 2.1 of
Rio 2009

(see also Theorem 4.3 in
Pinelis 1994
for a related result under the assumption of
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(b)
-conditionally symmetric distributed martingale difference sequence) which, together with the nonincreasing character of
\varphi_{p}(\cdot)

and nondecreasing character of
(\delta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
implies for
p\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\bigg\lVert\sum_{b\in S_{k}}Z_{b,k}\bigg\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{p}\bigg(\sum_{b\in S_{k}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert Z_{b,k}}}\right\rVert_{p}^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{p}M_{p}\big(\varphi_{p}(\delta_{k}+1)+\varphi_{p}(\delta_{k-1}+1)\big)\bigg(\sum_{b\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}}}\right\rvert^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{p}M_{p}\varphi_{p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\delta_{k-1}+1}}\right)\bigg(\sum_{b\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}}}\right\rvert^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(35)

We now concentrate on the estimate for
\sum_{b\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}}}\right\rvert^{2}
. Put
q_{i}:=\big\lfloor\frac{N_{i}}{2^{k}}\big\rfloor

and
r_{i}:=N_{i}-q_{i}2^{k}
, for
i\in\llbracket d\rrbracket
. Observe that
\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(2^{k}\llbracket q_{i}+1\rrbracket_{0})
;
for
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})
, the set
{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}
is a hyperrectangle with side-lengths:

\ell_{k}(b_{i},N_{i}):=\begin{cases}2^{k}&\text{ if }b_{i}<2^{k}q_{i};\\
r_{i}&\text{ if }b_{i}=2^{k}q_{i}.\end{cases}

Hence, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in\pi_{k}({\mathcal{R}})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|{\mathcal{C}}_{k,b}\cap{\mathcal{R}}}}\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{b\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}(2^{k}\llbracket q_{i}+1\rrbracket_{0})}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{k}(b_{i},N_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=0}^{q_{i}}\ell_{k}(2^{k}j,N_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(q_{i}2^{2k}+r_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\big((N_{i}-r_{i})\min(2^{k},N_{i})+r_{i}\min(2^{k},N_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\big(N_{i}\min(2^{k},N_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|R}}\right\rvert\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|R\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{0,k}}}\right\rvert.

(36)

The claimed estimate for

) and (

) into (

) and straightforward computations.
In the case of

, we can apply the bounded martingale difference inequality

Azuma 1967
stating that the sum

\sum_{b\in S_{k}}Z_{b,k}
is sub-Gaussian such that
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lVert\sum_{b\in S_{k}}Z_{b,k}}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{SG}}\leq\big(\big\lVert\sum_{b\in S_{k}}Z_{b,k}^{2}\big\rVert_{\infty}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

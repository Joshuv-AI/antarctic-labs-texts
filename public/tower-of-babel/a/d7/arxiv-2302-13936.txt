Nb-substitution suppresses the superconducting critical temperature of pressurized MoB 2
J. Lim, S. Sinha, A. C. Hire, J. S. Kim, P. M. Dee, R. S. Kumar, D. Popov, R. J. Hemley, R. G. Hennig, P. J. Hirschfeld, G. R. Stewart, J. J. Hamlin

Abstract
A recent work has demonstrated that MoB 2 , transforming to the same structure as MgB 2 \text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m P6/mmm ), superconducts at temperatures above 30 K near 100 GPa [C. Pei et al. Natl. Sci. Rev., nwad034 (2023)], and Nb-substitution in MoB 2 \text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} stabilizes the P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m P6/mmm structure down to ambient pressure [A. C. Hire et al. Phys. Rev. B 106, 174515 (2022)]. The current work explores the high pressure superconducting behavior of Nb-substituted MoB 2 \text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} (Nb 0.25 Mo 0.75 B 2 ). High pressure x-ray diffraction measurements show that the sample remains in the ambient pressure P ​ 6 / m ​ m ​ m P6/mmm structure to at least 160 GPa. Electrical resistivity measurements demonstrate that from an ambient pressure T c T_{\mathrm{c}} of 8 K (confirmed by specific heat to be a bulk effect), the critical temperature is suppressed to 4 K at 50 GPa, before gradually rising to 5.5 K at 170 GPa. The critical temperature at high pressure is thus significantly lower than that found in MoB 2 \text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} under pressure (30 K), revealing that Nb-substitution results in a strong suppression of the superconducting critical temperature. Our calculations indeed find a reduced electron-phonon coupling in Nb 0.25 Mo 0.75 B 2 , but do not account fully for the observed suppression, which may also arise from inhomogeneity and enhanced spin fluctuations.

<

\times

below
80\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}

At higher pressures, the pressure was determined using the Raman spectrum of the diamond anvil

.
Pressure was measured at
10\text{\,}
and
292\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
during each cooling cycle within an error estimation of 5%.
Two opposing diamond anvils (type Ia, 1/6-carat,
0.15\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
central flats) and a cBN-epoxy, soapstone insulated Re metal gasket were used for the four-probe method (see inset in Fig.
1
).
The diamond anvil cell was then placed inside a customized continuous-flow cryostat (Oxford Instruments).
For each temperature-dependent resistivity measurement, pressure was applied at room temperature.
The sample was then cooled to
1.8\text{\,}\mathrm{K}

\sim
0.25\text{\,}\mathrm{K}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{n}

Further details of the non-hydrostatic high-pressure resistivity techniques are given in Refs.
Matsuoka and Shimizu 2009

High pressure x-ray diffraction measurements were performed on a powdered piece of
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

The x-ray beam had a wavelength of

A MAR345 image plate detector calibrated with a

Neon was used as the pressure medium, and pressure was determined both using an online ruby fluorescence measurement
Chijioke et al. 2005
up to
40\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
as well as the equation of state of Au grains
Fei et al. 2007
loaded into the sample chamber up to
162\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}

Prescher and Prakapenka 2015

Rietveld 1969
and Le Bail
Le Bail et al. 1988
methods using GSAS-II software
Toby and Von Dreele 2013

To better understand the superconducting properties of

The electron-phonon coupling constant,

We use the Perdew–Burke-Ernzerhof functional for the exchange-correlation energy in the DFT calculations

The virtual crystal approximation was used with the optimized norm-conserving pseudopotentials

A

IV
Discussion

One question that still follows from our experiment is why Nb-doped MoB
2

has a significantly lower transition temperatures than MoB
2
over the same
pressure range studied in Ref.
Pei et al.
.
Much of the answer to this question can be gleaned from the literature on
NbB
2
, MoB
2
, and alloyed transition metal diborides.
We will focus on those findings which are most relevant for superconductivity,
starting with the density of states (DOS) near the Fermi level.
When compared with NbB
2
, MoB
2
has a higher DOS near the Fermi level (Table
1
)
and a higher fraction of electrons occupying antibonding states
Vajeeston et al. 2001
;
Muzzy et al. 2002
.
This difference helps to explain why, at ambient/low pressure,
MoB
2
is a less stable diboride, preferring the trigonal
R\bar{3}m
space group symmetry with alternating puckered boron planes instead of the hexagonal
P6/mmm
structure
realized by NbB
2

Vajeeston et al. 2001
.
In addition, MoB
2
has a higher isotropic electron-phonon coupling constant than
NbB
2

Oguchi 2002
;
Naidyuk et al. 2002
;
Heid et al. 2003
;
Shein et al. 2003
;
Shein and Ivanovskii 2006
.
Here, we would like to point out that the calculated electron-phonon coupling for
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at ambient pressure of
\lambda\sim 0.43
in Singh
Singh 2003a
is a result of poorly converged calculations
Singh 2003b
;
Heid et al. 2003
, and our calculated value agrees with Heid
et al.

Heid et al. 2003
.

Another interesting aspect of the present study is that the experimentally realized
suppression of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
is at odds with the
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}
obtained using the Allen-Dynes formula.
The theory and experiment both qualitatively agree that Nb-substitution reduces the
T_{\mathrm{c}}
in MoB
2
at high pressure (Table
1
) compared with
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.
However, there is significant quantitative disagreement in the magnitude of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
between the two results.
Experimentally, we found that
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at 100 GPa exhibits only about 30% of the
T_{\mathrm{c}}
of pure MoB
2
at the same pressure (Table
1
).
In contrast, the Allen-Dynes equation predicts that the Nb-substituted sample should exhibit about 70% of the
T_{\mathrm{c}}
of pure MoB
2
(i.e., for
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}=19.58
- 20.14 K; for
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}=29.17
K ).
In other words, the Allen-Dynes
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}
prediction works reasonably well for pure
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, but it fails to capture the strong reduction in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
for Nb-doped
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

Performing the same calculation for the
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}
of stoichiometric
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at ambient pressure reveals a similar overestimation. However, in that case, the degree of overestimation is difficult to gauge since the experimental literature for stoichiometric
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is rife with inconsistencies.
Some papers report
T_{\mathrm{c}}
’s between 0.62 K and 9 K
Leyarovska and Leyarovski 1979
;
Schirber et al. 1992
;
Kotegawa et al. 2002
;
Takeya et al. 2004
, and many others report an absence of superconductivity down to the lowest temperatures measured
Cooper et al. 1970
;
Yamamoto et al. 2002
;
Escamilla and Huerta 2006
;
Geng et al. 2007
;
Regalado and Escamilla 2007
;
Mudgel et al. 2008
.
There is considerably more evidence for finite
T_{\mathrm{c}}
’s up to 8-11 K in
nonstoichiometric

\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, characterized by increasing the ratio of B to Nb (enabled by Nb-vacancies)
Cooper et al. 1970
;
Gasparov et al. 2001
;
Yamamoto et al. 2002
;
Takeya et al. 2004
;
Escamilla et al. 2004
;
Nunes et al. 2005
;
Takahashi et al. 2005
;
Escamilla and Huerta 2006
;
Regalado and Escamilla 2007
;
Mudgel et al. 2008
or decreasing this ratio via B-vacancies
Hulm and Matthias 1951
;
Ziegler and Young 1953
. Assuming that stoichiometric
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
does not superconduct experimentally, except possibly at minimal temperatures, the Allen-Dynes prediction of
T_{\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{AD}}=
8.86 K becomes a rather severe overestimation.

In light of the sensitivity to inhomogeneity and vacancy formation in
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we point out that
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is also susceptible to metal vacancy formation, which generally lowers the electronic density of states
Shein and Ivanovskii 2006
. Taken together, we cannot rule out the role of inhomogeneities due to vacancies in the alloyed sample. Our calculations show that the tendency for metal vacancy formation in Nb
0.25
M
0.75
B
2
(
E_{\mathrm{vf}}=0.214
eV) is even more likely than in
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
E_{\mathrm{vf}}=1.794
eV). The presence of vacancies on the
4d
-atom site could lower the DOS at the Fermi level, reducing
T_{\mathrm{c}}
. While we do not include these effects in our calculations of the Eliashberg function, we suspect they play a role in the discrepancy between theory and experiment.

Another potential pathology leading to
T_{\mathrm{c}}
predictions larger than experiment could stem from spin fluctuations absent from the present formalism. Several 3
d
transition metals like V and Cr are better known to have significant spin fluctuations
Castaing et al. 1972
;
Bauer et al. 2014
;
Tsutsumi et al. 2020
;
Pei et al. 2021
;
Biswas et al. 2022
. While Nb is generally considered a conventional electron-phonon superconductor, some claim that spin fluctuations effects are essential for estimating
T_{\mathrm{c}}

Bose 2008
;
Tsutsumi et al. 2020
. We have used a modified McMillan formula defined in Eqn. (2) of Ref.
Mazin et al. 2002
to estimate the electron-paramagnon coupling constant required to match the experimental
T_{\mathrm{c}}
of
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(100 GPa), obtaining
\lambda_{\mathrm{sf}}\sim 0.15
. By comparison, to match a
T_{\mathrm{c}}<0.1
K in
\text{NbB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(0 GPa) would require
\lambda_{\mathrm{sf}}>0.26
. These values are comparable to results for Nb in Ref.
Bose 2008
and provide at least a partial explanation for the
T_{\mathrm{c}}
mismatch. Recent theoretical work on the itinerant antiferromagnet CrB
2
suggests that spin fluctuations are suppressed under pressure, giving rise to electron-phonon-mediated superconductivity at higher pressures
Biswas et al. 2022

\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
exhibits analogous behavior in the pressure dependence of

T_{\mathrm{c}}

V
Conclusions

In summary, we have studied the pressure-dependent superconducting transition temperature of
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.25}}}\text{Mo}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{0.75}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
in the same structure as
\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
P6/mmm
).
Electrical resistivity measurements up to
171\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
reveal that
T_{\mathrm{c}}
initially decreases with increasing pressure.
Above
50\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
,
T_{\mathrm{c}}
increases monotonically with a significant broadening of transition width
\Delta T_{\mathrm{c}}
up to the highest pressure.
However, the ambient pressure
T_{\mathrm{c}}
of
8\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
is the highest
T_{\mathrm{c}}
observed up to at least
171\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
.
Synchrotron high-pressure XRD measurements up to
161\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
show that the slope of the
c/a
ratio changes above
50\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}
within the same
P6/mmm
structure, indicating a potential correlation with the change in slope of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
(P).
Our theoretical findings show a reduction of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, due to the weakened electron-phonon coupling, in Nb
0.25
Mo
0.75
B
2
compared to pure MoB
2
at high pressure, in qualitative agreement with the experiment. However, these calculations underestimate the observed suppression of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, suggesting that additional factors, such as inhomogeneity and spin fluctuations, may be present.
High-pressure studies of other substitutions into
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which might enhance electron-phonon coupling, would be interesting to explore, to determine whether
T_{\mathrm{c}}
values comparable to the
32\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
observed in
\text{MoB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at
110\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{P}\mathrm{a}

Pei et al.
can be realized at low or ambient pressure.

An investigation into Lie algebra representations obtained from regular holonomic 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules
Julian Wykowski, Travis Schedler

Abstract
. Beilinson–Bernstein localisation [ BB81 ] relates representations of a Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} to certain 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules on the flag variety of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . In [ Rom21 ] , examples of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} -representations which correspond to 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} were computed. In this expository article, we give a topological description of these and extended examples via the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence. We generalise this to a full characterisation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} -representations which correspond to holonomic 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} with at most 2 regular singularities. We construct further examples with more singularities and develop a computer program for the computation of this correspondence in more general cases.

Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

The inceptive result utilising

[
Kas84
]
and Mebkhout
[
Meb84
]

Beilinson–Bernstein Localisation

We now introduce the second main correspondence theorem we will be using: Beilinson–Bernstein localisation. Let
G
be a semisimple algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
,
T
a maximal torus of
G
and
B\supseteq T
a Borel subgroup of
G
. Write
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
,
\mathfrak{h}=\operatorname{Lie}(T)
and
\mathfrak{b}=\operatorname{Lie}(B)
for the associated Lie algebras. All Borel subgroups are conjugate in
G
, yielding a variety
X=G/B
equipped with a Lie group action

G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}(X)

given by conjugation. The infinitesimal of this action is a Lie algebra morphism

\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\Theta(X))

which induces, by the universal property of the universal enveloping algebra, a map

\Phi:U(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathcal{D}_{X}).

Beilinson and Bernstein showed in
[
BB81
]
that this map is surjective and that its kernel is
\ker(\chi_{0})\cdot U(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\chi_{0}:Z(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbb{C}
is the trivial central character. They went further to generalise this to the case of twisted central characters, which allow for a complete description of all
\mathfrak{g}
-representations. Indeed, given any
\mathfrak{g}
-representation, the centre
Z(\mathfrak{g})
must act as scalars by Schur’s Lemma, and this “central character” factors as

\chi:Z(\mathfrak{g})\xrightarrow{\,\,\gamma\,\,}U(\mathfrak{h})\xrightarrow{\,\,\lambda\,\,}\mathbb{C}

where
\gamma
denotes the Harish-Chandra homomorphism (cf.
[
Hot98
]
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is some dominant and regular weight. We associate to
\lambda
a one-dimensional
B
-representation
\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
, whereon
B
acts via
\lambda\circ\pi
for
\pi:B\to T
the projection; this in turn corresponds to a
G
-equivariant line bundle

\pi_{\lambda}:\frac{G\times\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}}{B}\longrightarrow G/B=X

where
B
acts as
b\cdot(g,v)=(gb^{-1},b\cdot v)
and we denote by
\mathcal{L}(\lambda)
the sheaf of algebraic sections of
\pi_{\lambda}
with
{\mathcal{O}_{X}}
-module structure given by point-wise multiplication. In this way, we may incorporate the twist by
\lambda
into
\mathcal{D}_{X}
, yielding a
sheaf of twisted differential operators

(1.2)

\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}

where
F_{0}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}={\mathcal{O}_{X}}
and

F_{n}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathcal{E}nd_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{L}(\lambda+\rho)):\forall f\in{\mathcal{O}_{X}},[\xi,f]\in F_{n-1}\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\}.

Using the same argument as in the untwisted case, we obtain a map

\Phi_{\lambda}:U(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathcal{D}_{\lambda})

which can be written down explicitly using the
G
-invariant structure on
\mathcal{L}(\lambda+\rho)
. In the case where

is a matric Lie algebra, it amounts to

(1.3)

\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(s)(x)=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e^{ta}\cdot s(e^{-ta}x)\right|_{t=0}

for

and

. The key result in
[
BB81
]
is that the map
\Phi_{\lambda}
is surjective and has kernel
\ker(\chi_{\lambda})\cdot U(\mathfrak{g})
. Now the global sections of any
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}
-module

can be given the structure of a

-representation with central character

. Beilinson–Bernstein localisation gives a strong converse result; we state it here only for regular characters:

\mathcal{D}
-module

5.
Further regular singularities

In this last section, we look at some examples of the many
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representations which arise from holonomic
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
X=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with more than two regular singularities. In general, this category is very large. Indeed—as we saw in Section 2—given a finite set
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\}
of points on
X
, any finite-dimensional complex
\pi_{1}(X\backslash S)
-representation induces a local system on
X\backslash S
of finite rank. Via the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, this in turn gives rise to
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules with (regular) singularities in
S
. In other words, we may construct
\mathcal{D}
-modules on
X
with any prescribed monodromy around the points in
S
.
Let us discuss this in some more detail.

As
X\backslash S
is homeomorphic to the complex plane punctured at
|S|-1
points, it is homotopy equivalent to the wedge sum of
|S|-1
copies of the circle
S^{1}
, so in particular

\pi_{1}(X\backslash S)=\,\mathbin{{\mathop{\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.5pt}{\text{\Huge$*$}}}}\limits}}\mathbb{Z}

is the free group on
|S|-1
generators. A representation of a free product on a set of groups
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
consists of a vector space
V
and a set of actions
\{G_{i}\to\operatorname{GL}(V)\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
which are independent of one another. Consequently, the data of a finite-dimensional
\pi_{1}(X\backslash S)
-representation is equivalent to a choice of positive integer
n
and invertible linear transformations
T_{i}\in\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{C},n)
for each positive integer
i\leq|S|-1
. This gives rise to an
\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus S}
-coherent
\mathcal{D}_{X\setminus S}
-module, or equivalently, a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus S}
-module with a flat connection
\nabla
. If
S
is nonempty, this is necessarily a free module (a trivial vector bundle); note that if
S
is empty then we have a local system on all of
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
which is necessarily trivial. So in either case the representation gives rise to a flat connection on
\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus S}^{n}
. In a punctured complex disk about each point
s_{j}
,
\nabla
must be gauge equivalent (via a holomorphic gauge equivalence) to
d-\frac{A_{j}}{z-s_{j}}
, for some matrix
A_{j}
such that
T_{j}
is conjugate to
\exp(2\pi i\cdot A_{j})
.
It is tempting then to try to write

(5.1)

\nabla=d-\sum_{i=1}^{|S|-1}\frac{A_{i}}{z-s_{i}}-p(z)dz

for some (matrix-valued) polynomial
p(z)
.
However, note that (
5.1
) need not actually be gauge equivalent to
d-\frac{A_{j}}{z-s_{j}}
in a (punctured) complex disc about
s_{j}
, even if
p(z)=0
: in fact
\exp(2\pi iA_{j})
need only be in the
closure
of the conjugacy class of the monodromy of (
5.1
) about
s_{j}
. This can be seen by taking a limit of contour integrals which begin with a base point of the fundamental group, take a path to a point very close to
s_{j}
, take a small loop counterclockwise, and then take the inverse of the first path, as the small loop shrinks to
s_{j}
. (We remark that none of these issues arise in the case
n=1
: here, the monodromy of (5.1) about
s_{j}
really is
\exp(2\pi iA_{j})
, for all
j
.)
Nonetheless, it follows from the Riemann–Hilbert theorem that, for arbitrary
n
, every monodromy representation
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{|S|-1})
can be realised as a connection of the form (
5.1
) with a regular singularity at infinity, for some choices of the
A_{j}
and
p(z)
(with
\exp(2\pi iA_{j})
in the closure of the conjugacy class of
T_{j}
for all
j
).

Note that due to the presence of the
p(z)
term, connections of the form (
5.1
) need not have a regular singularity at infinity, in general. It is therefore natural to restrict to those having the property
p(z)=0
, which has a simple pole at infinity. These connections are called
Fuchsian
.
Hilbert’s 21st problem asked whether every connection is gauge equivalent to a Fuchsian one; its answer was eventually found to be negative
8
8

8

A counterexample was first constructed in
[
Bol90
]
. In
[
Kos92
]
, it is shown that, assuming
|S|\geq 4
and either
n=3
or
n\geq 7
, the codimension within
\operatorname{GL}(\mathbb{C},n)^{|S|-1}
of the variety of monodromy groups which cannot be realised by a Fuchsian connection is
2(n-1)(|S|-1)
.
. However, we shall now restrict our attention to Fuchsian connections to avoid the possibility of an irregular singularity at
\infty
. If we are interested in irreducible connections—needed to form irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-modules and hence irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representations—we can make such an assumption without loss of generality: that irreducible connections on

are always Fuchsian has been shown in
[
Kos92
]
(Theorem 1) and independently in
[
Bol92
]
(Theorem 1).

The connection
\nabla
has a singularity at a point
s_{j}\in S
iff there is
no
local trivialisation of
\nabla
in a punctured complex disc
U
containing
s_{j}
which is holomorphic at
s_{j}
. Different trivialisations are related to one another by gauge transformations, i.e. conjugation by
g
for some

,
so it could happen that, even if
A_{j}
is nonzero,

turns out to be holomorphic after it is conjugated by some such

\nabla|_{X\setminus S}

to a connection with no pole at

of the form (

Let us compute a concrete example of such a representation. We will consider the “simplest” case of three regular singularities; as we shall see, already this case harbours quite a degree of complexity. Write
S=\{0,1,\infty\}
where
0=[0:1]
,
\infty=[1:0]
and

Since the support of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}
is contained entirely in
U_{0}

so the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representation corresponding to
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}

(z-y)\partial_{z}
and
(w-x)\partial_{w}
on the charts
U_{0}
and

or

in terms of our fixed

It is not a coincidence that the Cauchy-Euler operator at a point corresponds to the existence of a singularity of

Appendix: Computation of Beilinson–Bernstein localisation

Calculating the representations which correspond to an
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda-1}
-coherent
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}
-module
\mathcal{M}
on
X=\mathbb{CP}^{1}
can be a laborious task, especially in the case where
\mathcal{M}
has more than two singularities. For this reason, we created a python script which does the necessary calculations. We invite the reader to download it from:

https://github.com/Julek99/DModRep

and we outline briefly in this appendix how to use it. The user has two programs at their disposal —
compute.py
for computation and
graph.py
for graphical representation. A third script,
core.py
, is also included in the file; this script contains the actual mathematical machinery, while the others are merely user interfaces which make use of
core.py
. For this reason, it is essential that
core.py
is kept in the same folder as the other programs.

Each program takes as input a connection
\nabla
defined locally on one of the charts
U_{0}
and
U_{\infty}
which the user has specified. The user is then prompted to give a basis for
\mathcal{M}
, in terms of the coordinates
z
on
U_{0}
or
w
on
U_{\infty}
. The script then calculates the action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-elements on the local sections of
\mathcal{M}
and generates LaTeX code for a graphical representation of this action.

[
Meu+17
]
) installed.

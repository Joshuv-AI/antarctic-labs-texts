Modal Logic with Relations over Paths: a Theoretical Development through Comonadic Semantics
Santiago Figueira, Gabriel Goren-Roig

Abstract
Game comonads provide categorical semantics for comparison games in Finite Model Theory, thus providing an abstract characterisation of logical equivalence for a wide range of logics, each one captured through a specific choice of comonad. Motivated by the goal of applying comonadic tools to the study of data-aware logics such as CoreDataXPath {\rm CoreDataXPath} , in this work we introduce a generalisation of Modal Logic that allows relation symbols of arbitrary arity as atoms of the syntax, which we call Path Predicate Modal Logic or PPML {\rm PPML} . We motivate this logic as arising from a shift in perspective on a previously studied fragment of CoreDataXPath {\rm CoreDataXPath} , called DataGL {\rm DataGL} , and prove that PPML {\rm PPML} recovers DataGL {\rm DataGL} for a specific choice of signature. We argue that this shift in perspective allows the capturing and designing of new data-aware logics. On the other hand, PPML {\rm PPML} enjoys an intrinsic motivation in that it extends Modal Logic to predicate over more general models. Having introduced resource-bounded simulation and bisimulation games for PPML {\rm PPML} together with a proof of the Hennessy-Milner property relating bisimilarity and logical equivalence, we define the PPML {\rm PPML} comonad, which essentially amounts to an unravelling construction on models of PPML {\rm PPML} , and prove that it captures these games, following analogous results in the literature. However, we depart from the literature in our proof strategy, since we draw upon the axiomatic framework of arboreal categories, giving intuition for the axioms involved and supplying detailed verifications. Subsequently, we develop the model-theoretical understanding of PPML {\rm PPML} by making systematic use of the comonadic framework. This includes results such as a tree-model property and an alternative proof of the one-way Hennessy-Milner property using a correspondence between positive PPML {\rm PPML} formulas and canonical models. We also use the comonadic perspective to establish connections with other logics, such as bounded quantifier rank and bounded variable number fragments of First Order Logic on one side and Basic Modal Logic on the other, and show how the PPML {\rm PPML} comonad induces a syntax-free characterisation of logical equivalence for DataGL {\rm DataGL} , our original motivation. With respect to Basic Modal Logic, a functorial assignment from PPML {\rm PPML} unravellings into Kripke trees enables us to obtain polynomial-time reductions from PPML {\rm PPML} problems to their Basic Modal Logic counterparts.

Introduction

One of the main problems of database theory is finding appropriate balances between complexity and expressivity of query languages. In this context,
data-aware logics are languages that reason on data graphs, i.e. finite graphs whose nodes are decorated with a label from a finite alphabet and a data value from an infinite domain.
Formulas in data-aware logics express queries based not only on the graph topology and node labels, as modal logics do, but also with reference to data values. However, instead of directly accessing data values as constants, data-aware logics only allow comparison of data values in a controlled way through specific syntactic constructions

.

Let

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\ \Coloneqq\top\ \mid\ R\ \mid\ \lnot\varphi\ \mid\ \varphi\land\varphi\ \mid\ \Diamond\varphi

2.3
Arboreal Categories and Bisimilarity through Bounded Morphisms

In this section we explain how the
{\rm PPML}
comonad encodes bisimulation games, and hence how we can characterise bisimilarity in categorical terms. To this end, we will use the axiomatic approach of arboreal categories. This lets us access general results in a clean fashion; concretely, we will make use of Propositions 42 and 46 in
[
5
]
.
Arboreal categories and covers constitute a categorical axiomatisation of the situation arising from a game comonad. In
[
5
]
, the authors draw an analogy with computability and computational complexity: in the same way that assigning a program (an intensional description) to a computable function (an extensional object) allows us to assign some complexity measure to the function itself, objects in an arboreal category may serve as intensional descriptions of objects in some ‘extensional’ category that we wish to study—in our case, the category
{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma)
. These intensional descriptions are axiomatically defined to be tree-shaped since they represent processes unfolding in space and time, hence the name ‘arboreal category’.

We proceed as follows.

•

We define arboreal categories and arboreal covers, and give some intuition for them.

•

We prove property (II) above, namely that
\text{\sf{EM}}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
is an arboreal category for each
k\geq 0
. In fact we prove a slightly stronger statement: the
k
-indexed family of
{\rm PPML}
comonads constitutes a resource-indexed arboreal cover of
{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma)
.

•

We show that the abstract bisimulation game associated with each arboreal category
\text{\sf{EM}}(\mathbb{C}_{k})
coincides with the
{\rm PPML}

k
-bisimulation game.

•

We prove that the general notion of open pathwise embedding in an arboreal category reduces in our case to a natural notion of bounded morphism.

•

Putting all of this together, we end the section with Theorem
2.53
, which characterises
{\rm PPML}

k
-bisimilarity using bounded morphisms.

We have anticipated that, intuitively, objects in an arboreal category are ‘tree-shaped’. More precisely, this is enforced by requiring that all objects are path-generated (Def.

One way to talk about the shape of an object is by considering the shape of its poset of subobjects. Then we could postulate that a path is an object whose poset of subobjects is a finite total order. This can be done in any category, but the standard notion of subobject turns out to be inadequate for this task. For instance, even the subobjects of a

3
Model Theory of
{\rm PPML}

In this section, we explore some additional consequences of the comonadic formalism for the model theory of
{\rm PPML}
. We first describe an extension of
{\rm PPML}
with graded modalities, which we call
{\rm PPML}^{\#}
, which of course generalises the graded modalities of
{\rm BML}
(see e.g.
[
20
]
). In the context of logics described through game comonads, this kind of extension is captured by a simpler condition than the existence of spans of open pathwise embeddings—in our case, it is captured by isomorphism of
{\rm PPML}
-unravellings. Considering
{\rm PPML}^{\#}
allows us to obtain immediately a homomorphism counting theorem for
{\rm PPML}
by an application of a general comonadic result, namely
[
19
, Corollary 14]
. It also allows us to state and prove in full strength the tree-model property that
{\rm PPML}
enjoys; this is the second topic covered in this section. Finally, we define a Chandra-Merlin-like correspondence between
{\rm PPML}^{+}
formulas and finite pp-trees, which leads to an alternative proof of the Hennessy-Milner property for
k
-similarity between finite structures.

3.1
Graded Modalities and Homomorphism Counting

Game comonads function as a mathematical framework in two different ways: it can provide general theorems which can be directly instantiated in new game comonads, or, when there is currently no such theorem, it can provide a guideline for producing new results by simple adaptations of previous arguments.
In this section, we combine both methodologies: first, inspired by similar results for many other game comonads (and in particular for the Modal comonad), we prove that isomorphism between the
{\rm PPML}
-unravellings of two pointed structures captures logical indistinguishability for a suitably defined graded extension of
{\rm PPML}
. Then we apply a general theorem of
[
19
]
to obtain a homomorphism-counting or Lovász-type theorem for our logic.

Note that having isomorphic unravellings is a stronger condition than the one featuring in Theorem

Remark 3.7

In general, a game comonad

3.2
The pp-tree-model Property

In the same way one can reason with games or with bisimulations to conclude that a certain property is not expressible within a logic such as
{\rm PPML}
, one can also do so through Corollary
2.54
.

Definition 3.10
.

Following the literature
[
4
,
1
,
6
]
, if
G
is the comonad arising from an arboreal cover of a category
{E}
, we say that
X,Y\in{E}
are
bisimilar
iff there exists a span of open pathwise embeddings
GX\leftarrow Z\to GY
in
\text{\sf{EM}}(G)
. We say that
G
has the
bisimilar companion property
if
GX
is bisimilar to
X
for all
X\in{E}
.

In
[
6
]
comonads (and arboreal covers) that satisfy either idempotence or the bisimilar companion property are referred to as
tame
, and the good properties that these comonads enjoy seem to reflect the fact that they correspond to less expressive logics. First notice that if
G
is idempotent then it satisfies the bisimilar companion property: given
X\in{E}
, just take the span of open pathwise embeddings in
\text{\sf{EM}}(G)
to be
GGX\xleftarrow{\delta_{X}}GX=GX
.
13
13

13

Here we are again using the fact that
\text{\sf{EM}}(G)
can be identified with a full subcategory of
{E}
thanks to idempotence. Contrast this with the language used in e.g.
[
6
, Prop. 5.4]
.

However, the converse is not true, as is exemplified by the comonads for guarded fragments
[
3
]
.
To understand why idempotence is even stronger than the bisimilar companion property, we can make use of the graded logic
{\rm PPML}^{\#}
introduced in Section
3.1
.

Corollary 3.11
.

{(\mathcal{A},a)}\mathrel{\underline{~~}\hskip-8.50006pt{\leftrightarrow}}_{k}^{\#}\mathbb{C}_{k}{(\mathcal{A},a)}
for all
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\in{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma)
. Thus,
if
{(\mathcal{A},a)}
is finitely branching or
\sigma
is finite,
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\equiv_{k}^{\#}\mathbb{C}_{k}{(\mathcal{A},a)}
, and hence also
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\equiv_{k}\mathbb{C}_{k}{(\mathcal{A},a)}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{C}_{k}
is idempotent,
\mathbb{C}_{k}{(\mathcal{A},a)}\cong\mathbb{C}_{k}\mathbb{C}_{k}{(\mathcal{A},a)}
for all
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\in{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma)
, hence the result follows by Corollary
3.6
.
∎

Note that the argument does not depend on the concrete description of open pathwise embeddings as bounded morphisms. Instead, the result follows immediately from the fact that
\mathbb{C}_{k}
is idempotent, using only abstract notions.

Corollary
3.11
allows us to derive many expressivity results about
{\rm PPML}
. One such result says that
{\rm PPML}
enjoys a
pp-tree-model property
which generalises
{\rm BML}
’s tree-model property:

Corollary 3.12
.

A
{\rm PPML}^{\#}
formula
\varphi
is satisfiable if and only if it is satisfied by a pp-tree of finite height. In particular this also holds for
{\rm PPML}
formulas.

Proof.

Let
k
be the modal depth of
\varphi
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\sigma
contains only the relation symbols in
\varphi
together with
E
, hence it is finite. Corollary
3.11
then implies that
\varphi
is satisfiable if and only if it is satisfiable in some
\mathbb{C}_{k}
-coalgebra. Thus any
\varphi
is satisfiable if and only if it is satisfied by a pp-tree of finite height.
∎

More generally, any idempotent game comonad will imply some “coalgebra-model property” for its corresponding logic.

This allows us to prove that many properties are not
{\rm PPML}
-expressible, e.g. the property “in the interpretation of
R
, there is a tuple that is not an
E
-chain” for some
R\in\overline{\sigma}
. This is obviously the case for many structures and yet it cannot be true of any pp-tree; thus it is not expressible in
{\rm PPML}
.

3.3
Canonical Models and the Hennessy-Milner Property

Following the general relationship between coalgebras and conjunctive queries presented in
[
7
]
, we can think of finite pp-trees as reifications of
{\rm PPML}^{+}
formulas

via a Chandra-Merlin-like correspondence
[
18
]
.
In this way, the comonadic formalism leads to an alternative proof of Theorem
2.10
(1) for finite structures.

In adapting the idea from First Order Logic, we must be careful with the fact that in
{\rm PPML}
not all positive formulas are satisfiable, and hence not all of them have a canonical model: we must restrict to the well nested formulas.

Given a finite pp-tree
\mathcal{T}
of height
k
, we wish to construct a
{\rm PPML}^{+}
formula
\nu(\mathcal{T})
such that for all
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\in{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma)
,
{(\mathcal{A},a)}\models\nu(\mathcal{T})
if and only if there exists a morphism
\mathcal{T}\to{(\mathcal{A},a)}
. The construction deviates from that of canonical modal conjunctive queries for
{\rm BML}

[
7
, Section 8.3]
since the presence of relations over paths implies that we cannot write
\nu(\mathcal{T})
recursively in terms of
\nu(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
for each of the subtrees of
\mathcal{T}
. We solve this by a technique analogous to Lemma
3.3
, which involves generalising to formulas
\nu(\mathcal{T},s)
where
s
is a
stem
of
\mathcal{T}
, in the sense of the definition below.

Definition 3.13
.

Given a pp-tree
\mathcal{T}
, we say that an
E
-chain
s
in
\mathcal{T}
is a

of
\mathcal{T}
if it is a prefix of all branches of
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover, given

we define
\mathcal{T}^{w}
as the embedded sub-pp-tree of
\mathcal{T}
containing all points of
\mathcal{T}
that are comparable with
w
in the partial order
(E^{\mathcal{T}})^{*}
.

Notice that given a pp-tree
\mathcal{T}
and

\mathcal{T}_{v}
is the maximal stem of
\mathcal{T}^{v}
. These notions are related to Definition
3.2
: given
\mathcal{A}\in{\sf Struct}(\sigma)
and a chain

in
\mathcal{A}

can be equivalently defined as
(\mathcal{C}_{k+|s|-1}(\mathcal{A},s(1)))^{s}
.

Definition 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a pp-tree and
s
be a stem of
\mathcal{T}
. Suppose that there are only finitely many non-empty interpretations in
\mathcal{T}
, i.e.

is finite.
14
14

14

This condition is necessary since we are working with finitary conjunctions only. In particular, this is trivial when

is finite.

Then we define inductively the formula
15
15

15

We define
\nu(\mathcal{T},s)
only up to a choice of ordering on
\overline{\sigma}
and on the successor sets, but this is immaterial for the current discussion. Also note that the empty conjunction is taken to be syntactically equal to

.

\nu(\mathcal{T},s)\coloneqq\bigwedge\{R\in\overline{\sigma}\mid\mathcal{T},s\models R\}\land\bigwedge_{w\in E^{\mathcal{T}}(\varepsilon(s))}\Diamond\nu(\mathcal{T}^{w},s.w).

Since
\mathcal{T}
is a finite tree, the definition is well-founded, and moreover the resulting formula is clearly in

. When

Lemma
3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a finite pp-tree with finitely many non-empty interpretations. Let

.

.

For every finite pp-tree
\mathcal{T}
with finitely many non-empty interpretations and for all

•

Adding a tuple to an interpretation.

Let

Let

Corollary 3.20

Given finite structures

We now discuss the relationship between the functor

and existing notions of transformations between comonads and their EM categories.

Given

Operations admitting such liftings are shown to enjoy Feferman-Vaught-Mostowski-style or ‘FVM’ compositionality theorems for the logic(s) corresponding to the comonads involved. An interesting application of their formalism is the fact that all logics admitting a comonadic characterisation enjoy an FVM theorem for categorical products, which in the case of

In our case, it is not hard to see that

The Kleisli law

We close with a note on the expressivity of
{\rm PPML}
. Since

shares many of the complexity properties of

5
Conclusions and Future Work

Path Predicate Modal Logic, or
{\rm PPML}
, is a generalisation of Basic Modal Logic which arose from the insight that the ‘equal data’ relationship in
{\rm DataGL}
can be split from the definition of the modal operators and added as an atom of the language. This allows us to express
{\rm DataGL}
not as a bimodal logic but as a unimodal logic of a new kind, and induces a natural representation of the
{\rm DataGL}
bisimulation game as a particular case of the
{\rm PPML}
bisimulation game.

What do we gain? We give two main motivations for
{\rm PPML}
. The first one is that we can think of
{\rm PPML}
as a way of interpreting Basic Modal Logic over general relational structures.
Given a first order signature
\sigma
that contains at least one binary relation, we can select it to function as an accessibility relation and then use
{\rm PPML}
to reason modally about the structure, replacing
\exists
and
\forall
with
\Diamond
and
\Box
. This represents an important relaxation on what kinds of first order signatures admit a modal interpretation. The standard translation into First Order Logic shows how this amounts to a restriction on which bound variables can appear inside first order atoms, and in what order they appear.

Our second motivation for
{\rm PPML}
is to present a framework for capturing and designing simple data-aware logics which is different from (multi-)modal logic, while at the same time retaining a modal-like syntax and semantics. This is seen in the case of
{\rm DataGL}
splitting data-aware modalities into two separate syntactic constructs. More generally, this work constitutes an exploration of comonadic semantics as a framework for studying data-aware logics.

We emphasise that the comonadic formalism allowed a systematic study of multiple properties of interest of a new logic. This perspective on
{\rm PPML}
takes as a fundamental starting point the notion of pp-tree and the
k
-step unravelling construction. From there, multiple lines of thinking open up naturally, such as the characterisation of the expressivity of graded modalities through isomorphism of unravellings, a homomorphism-counting property with respect to the class of finite pp-trees, the pp-tree model property, or the Chandra-Merlin-like correspondence between finite pp-trees and positive
{\rm PPML}
formulas. We even mention in passing a FVM-type theorem for products of structures obtained ‘by free’ from the comonadic formalism (Prop.
4.20
). These are either applications of general results for game comonads or follow analogous results previously established in the literature, although sometimes requiring non-trivial adaptations. Moreover, using a translation technique we obtain a characterisation of the expressivity of
{\rm DataGL}
, and still taking as fundamental the notion of unravelling and the ensuing comonads, both for
{\rm PPML}
and for
{\rm BML}
, we obtain polynomial-time computational reductions from
{\rm PPML}
to
{\rm BML}
. The fundamental algorithmic observation in this context is that unravellings of finite structures are themselves computable in polynomial time for fixed values of
k
.

We close with a discussion of some lines for future work. Of course, we can continue to apply comonadic techniques to the study of
{\rm PPML}
, such as looking into homomorphism preservation properties
[
6
]
or FVM-type properties as explored in
[
24
]
. Here we sketch two lines of research inspired by our two main motivations: developing modal languages for general relational structures, and giving comonadic semantics to data-aware logics.

Multimodal, Polyadic
{\rm PPML}
.

We have presented the theory of
{\rm PPML}
as corresponding to Basic Modal Logic, but modal languages may be constructed more generally by choosing a
modal similarity type
consisting of a finite number of modal operators which moreover may be polyadic, i.e. correspond to accessibility relations of arbitrary finite arity
[
14
]
. Allowing any subset of
\sigma
to be interpreted as accessibility relations for modal exploration is particularly interesting from the motivation of extending the modal lens to arbitrary relational structures. Moreover, in this more general case we may ask what happens when any relation symbol can be used both as accessibility relation for a modality and as an atom at the same time. In
{\rm PPML}
allowing
E
as an atom of the language would not add expressive power at all, since e.g.
\Diamond E
would be equivalent to
\Diamond\top
. However, this is no longer true if we introduce polyadic modalities.

Comonadic semantics for
{\rm CoreDataXPath}
.

As we begin to explore more complex comparison games, such as bisimulation games for
{\rm CoreDataXPath}

[
22
]
, we expect that these games will be captured by a comonad together with a translation technique similar to our treatment of
{\rm DataGL}
. In the case of
{\rm DataGL}
, we translated
{\rm DataGL}
models into relational structures and then applied the comonad
\mathbb{C}_{k}
corresponding to
{\rm PPML}
. For other fragments of
{\rm CoreDataXPath}
the comonad in itself might not correspond to an easily recognizable logic.

As a next step in this direction, recall from Section
4.2.2
the fragment
{\rm CoreDataXPath}_{\varepsilon}(\downarrow^{+})
of
{\rm CoreDataXPath}(\downarrow^{+})
. As we noted, although it is possible to translate this fragment into
\sigma_{\rm CDXP}
-
{\rm PPML}
, this latter logic is strictly more expressive even when restricted to an appropriate class of models. This rules out the possibility of using the
{\rm PPML}
comonad
\mathbb{C}_{k}
to capture indistinguishability for
{\rm CoreDataXPath}_{\varepsilon}(\downarrow^{+})
. Instead, we may obtain a comonadic characterisation of
{\rm CoreDataXPath}_{\varepsilon}(\downarrow^{+})
by recurring to a new comonad.

When we interpret
{\rm CoreDataXPath}_{\varepsilon}(\downarrow^{+})
over tree-shaped models, the
k
-(bi)simulation game for this logic can be stated in terms of a certain two-pebble game, where the two pebbles must be moved in alternation. Here the parameter
k
, which on the side of games corresponds to the maximum number of rounds, represents the number of nested occurrences of
{\downarrow}
in a given formula, including those inside tests.
To obtain a comonad encoding this game, start with the signature
\sigma_{\rm DGL}
and the full subcategory
{\sf ModDGL}_{*}^{\text{tree}}
of
{\sf ModDGL}_{*}
spanned by data trees. Then for each
k\geq 0
we can construct an extension of
\sigma_{\rm DGL}
,
\sigma_{k}\coloneqq\sigma_{\rm DGL}\cup\{E_{0},\dots,E_{k}\}
, where all the new symbols are binary, and a functor
t_{k}:{\sf ModDGL}_{*}^{\text{tree}}\to{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma_{k})
extending the functor from Def.
4.10
with
E_{j}^{t{(\mathfrak{M},w)}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=0}^{j}(E^{t{(\mathfrak{M},w)}})^{i}
. In this context, for each
k\geq 0
there exists a comonad with underlying functor
\mathbb{X}_{k}:{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma_{k})\to{\sf Struct}_{*}(\sigma_{k})
such that winning strategies for Duplicator in the one-way simulation game between the data trees
{(\mathfrak{M},w)},{(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})}\in{\sf ModDGL}_{*}^{\text{tree}}
correspond to Kleisli morphisms
\mathbb{X}_{k}t_{k}{(\mathfrak{M},w)}\to t_{k}{(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})}
.

It is not clear whether it is possible to give a language for
\mathbb{X}_{k}
in such a way that
\mathbb{X}_{k}
becomes ‘its’ game comonad. Further study is needed in order to assess such possibility, for this and other fragments of
{\rm CoreDataXPath}
. In this way it might be possible to obtain new logics closely related to
{\rm CoreDataXPath}
in the same way in which
{\rm PPML}
relates to
{\rm DataGL}
, by extending already known languages into unknown territory.

On the other hand, since we expect that the translation technique will continue to be necessary for data-aware logics, this limits some of the benefits arising from the existence of a related comonad, e.g. we cannot directly interpret coalgebras of these comonads as reifications of positive formulas in our language of interest. This may motivate the development of a proper theory of relative game comonads, by e.g. characterising the logical meaning of the relative coalgebras of a relative comonad
[
10
, Def. 2.11]
.

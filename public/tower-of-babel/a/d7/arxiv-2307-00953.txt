Slow-fast systems with an equilibrium near the folded slow manifold
N. G. Gelfreikh, A. V. Ivanov

Abstract
We study a slow-fast system with two slow and one fast variables. We assume that the slow manifold of the system possesses a fold and there is an equilibrium of the system in a small neighbourhood of the fold. We derive a normal form for the system in a neighbourhood of the pair ”equilibrium-fold” and study the dynamics of the normal form. In particular, as the ratio of two time scales tends to zero we obtain an asymptotic formula for the Poincaré map and calculate the parameter values for the first period-doubling bifurcation. The theory is applied to a generalization of the FitzHugh-Nagumo system.



Keywords: slow-fast systems, period-doubling bifurcation

Note that all fixed points of (

To study the dynamics of (

We add to the system (

\displaystyle S_{-}=\{(\,\xi,\eta,\zeta)\ :\ \xi=0,\ -1<\eta<0,\ \zeta\in\mathbb{R}\,\}.

Since all trajectories of the unperturbed system are closed for

the unperturbed Poincaré map

coincides with the identity map.
It is natural to expect that after a perturbation a trajectory starting at

will hit this section again near the initial point.
Additionally one may expect that eigenvalues of the tangent map
TP(\mu)

should be close to one. However, this conjecture may become false if

To describe this effect we will concentrate our attention on the region

Assuming

, we consider a closed orbit of the unperturbed system (

the orbit of the system (
2

\mathcal{M}^{(0)}
for which

\xi=0,\quad\zeta=\zeta_{0},\quad J=J_{0}.

We suppose that
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(\zeta_{0},J_{0})\subset U_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{O}(\zeta_{0},J_{0})\right)

One may note that a trajectory corresponding to the unperturbed orbit

It starts at the point
\mathcal{M}^{(0)}

At the top of the orbit

Then the trajectory approaches

Taking into account the symmetry of the system, we define an auxiliary Poincaré section

3.2
Fixed points and the period-doubling bifurcation

Since all trajectories of the unperturbed system are closed for
J>0
one may expect that after a small perturbation the image of a point
(\zeta_{0},J_{0})\in S_{-}
under the Poincaré map will be close to the initial point
(\zeta_{0},J_{0})
. The condition for a fixed point reads as

P(\mu)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{0}\\
J_{0}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{0}\\
J_{0}\end{array}\right)

(3.37)

or, taking into account (
3.36
), it can be rewritten

F(\mu)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{0}\\
J_{0}\end{array}\right)=F(-\mu)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{0}\\
J_{0}\end{array}\right).

(3.38)

Due to smooth dependence of (
2
) on
\mu
one may represent the map
F(\mu)
in the form

F(\mu)=\mathrm{id}+\mu F_{1}+\mu^{2}F_{2}+\mu^{3}F_{3}+O(\mu^{4}).

(3.39)

Then the symmetry of the normal form (
3.38
) implies that

F_{1},F_{3}:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{0}\\
J_{0}\end{array}\right)\mapsto\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0\\
0\end{array}\right).

(3.40)

The period-doubling bifurcation occurs when one of the eigenvalues of the tangent map
D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}P
at a fixed point passes through
-1
. Thus, the condition for the period-doubling bifurcation can be written in the form

{\rm det}\big(D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}P(\mu)+I\big)=0

(3.41)

or equivalently due to (
3.36
) as

{\rm det}\big(D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}F(\mu)+D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}F(-\mu)\big)=0.

(3.42)

Substituting (
3.39
) into (
3.42
), one gets

{\rm det}\big(2I+2\mu^{2}D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}F_{2}+O(\mu^{4})\big)=0

or

1+\mu^{2}{\rm Tr}\,D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}F_{2}+O(\mu^{4})=0.

(3.43)

The condition (
3.43
) justifies the necessity of second order approximation
(
3.39
) for the map
F(\mu)
to reveal the cascade of the period-doubling bifurcations.

\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{1}^{(\zeta)}(s)\,ds=k^{-1/2}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0})\left(\frac{1}{4}\ln k^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\ln 4\right)+O\left(\ln k^{-1}\right),

\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{1}^{(J)}(s)\,ds=O\left(k^{-1}J_{0}\right).

Taking into account (
3.5
) and (
3.85
) together with (
3.98
), one gets

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{-3/2}\left[\frac{1}{3}\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0}\right)k(\ln k^{-1}+\ln 4)-\beta_{1}k+O(k^{3/2}\ln k^{-1})\right],

\displaystyle J_{1}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(\zeta_{0})\Bigl(1+O(k)\Bigr)+O(J_{0}k^{-1}).

(3.108)

For the second order terms, due to (

) and (

), we have:

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}^{(2)}-\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{dy}(y_{0}^{(2)})\cdot y_{1}^{(2)}+\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(\zeta)}(s)\,{\rm d}s,

(3.109)

\displaystyle J_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{2}^{(2)}-\frac{dJ_{1}}{dy}(y_{0}^{(2)})\cdot y_{1}^{(2)}+\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(J)}(s)\,{\rm d}s,

where

and

are defined by (

),

Formulae (
3.107
), (
3.94
), (
3.95
) imply

\displaystyle-\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{dy}(y_{0}^{(2)})\cdot y_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A(\zeta_{0})\frac{k^{-1/2}}{2J_{0}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0})\left(1+O(k^{1/2}\ln k^{-1}\right),

(3.110)

\displaystyle-\frac{dJ_{1}}{dy}(y_{0}^{(2)})\cdot y_{1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(k^{-1}).

Application of (

), (

), (

) leads to the following estimates

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(\zeta)}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{k^{1/2}}{J_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-y^{2})^{2}}\right)\quad\Rightarrow\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(\zeta)}(s)\,ds=O\left(\frac{1}{J_{0}}\right),

(3.111)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(J)}(y)

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{k^{1/2}}{(1-y^{2})^{3}}\right)\quad\Rightarrow\int_{y_{0}^{(2)}}^{0}\mathcal{R}_{2}^{(J)}(s)\,ds=O(k^{-1/2}).

Therefore, we have the following asymptotics for the second order approximations

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A(\zeta_{0})\frac{k^{-1/2}}{2J_{0}}\left[\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0}\right]\Bigl(1+O(k^{1/2}\ln k^{-1})\Bigr)+O\left(\frac{1}{J_{0}}\right),

(3.112)

\displaystyle J_{2}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A(\zeta_{0})k^{-3/2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{3}+\alpha_{2}k\zeta_{0}-\gamma\right)\Bigl(1+O(k^{1/2})\Bigr)+O(k^{-1}).

Thus, the Poincaré map

4
Fixed points and period-doubling bifurcations

In this section we derive conditions on the parameters of the normal form which lead to existence of a fixed point of the Poincaré map and its period-doubling bifurcation.
If
(\zeta_{0},J_{0})\in S_{-}
is a fixed point then according to (
3.40
) the following condition should be satisfied:

\zeta_{1}^{(3)}=0,\quad J_{1}^{(3)}=0.

Taking into account (
3.108
), (
3.54
) and definition of the parameter
k
, one may rewrite these conditions as

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{2}\zeta_{0}}{\ln J_{0}^{-1}}\left[1+\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}+\ln 4}{2\ln J_{0}^{-1}}\right]+\beta_{1}\left[\frac{1}{3}+\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}+\ln 4-2}{2\ln J_{0}^{-1}}\right]=O\left(\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}}{\ln^{3/2}J_{0}^{-1}}\right),

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}\gamma+\alpha_{2}\zeta_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{1}-\gamma_{0}=O(J_{0}\ln^{2}J_{0}^{-1}).

(4.120)

Note that the coefficient
\gamma_{0}
in (
4.120
) is the only one which depends on the ratio,
\delta/\varepsilon
, of parameters of the initial problem (
1.6
), namely:

\gamma_{0}=\varkappa\sigma+\nu,\qquad\varkappa=\sqrt{\frac{2}{-D}}F^{\prime\prime}_{x\delta},\quad\nu=\sqrt{\frac{2}{-D}}\frac{D^{\prime}_{x}}{F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}},\quad\sigma=\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon},

where the functions
F^{\prime\prime}_{x\delta},F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}},D,D^{\prime}_{x}
are evaluated at

(x,y,z,\delta)=(0,0,0,0)
.

We solve the first equation with respect to
\zeta_{0}
and substitute the solution into the second one. Then the second condition (
4.120
) can be considered as an equation defining
J_{0}
in terms of the parameter
\sigma
:

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}=-\frac{\beta_{1}}{3\beta_{2}}\ln J_{0}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}+\ln 4-3}{\ln J_{0}^{-1}}\right)+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}}{\ln^{1/2}J_{0}^{-1}}\right),

(4.121)

\displaystyle\sigma=-\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}{3\beta_{2}\varkappa}\ln J_{0}^{-1}\left(1+\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}+\ln 4-3}{\ln J_{0}^{-1}}\right)-\frac{3\gamma+\alpha_{1}+2\nu}{2\varkappa}+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}}{\ln^{1/2}J_{0}^{-1}}\right).

We emphasize here that due to
J_{0}\ll 1
the ratio
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma\gg 1
.

One may also note that due to smooth dependence of solutions on initial conditions asymptotics (
3.112
) and (
3.54
) being differentiated with respect to
(\zeta_{0},J_{0})
remain valid in
U_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{O}(\zeta_{0},J_{0})\right)
. This leads to

{\rm Tr}\,D_{(\zeta_{0},J_{0})}F_{2}=-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}k^{-1/2}}{4J_{0}}\alpha_{2}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}k\zeta_{0})\left(1+O(k^{1/2}\ln k^{-1})\right)+O(k^{-5/2}).

Substituting this into (
3.43
) and taking into account (
4.120
), one gets a condition for the period-doubling bifurcation of the periodic trajectory corresponding to initial point
(\zeta_{0},J_{0})
:

1-\mu^{2}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}\ln^{1/2}J_{0}^{-1}}{6J_{0}}\cdot\left(1+O\Bigl(\frac{\ln\ln J_{0}^{-1}}{\ln^{1/2}J_{0}^{-1}}\Bigr)\right)+O(\mu^{2}\ln^{-5/2}J_{0}^{-1})=0,

where
J_{0}
satisfies (
4.121
).

We solve this equation with respect to
J_{0}
and substitute the solution into (
4.121
). Then, taking into account the relation
\varepsilon=\mu^{2}
, one obtains that the first period-doubling bifurcation occurs at

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}^{*}=-\frac{\beta_{1}}{3\beta_{2}}\left[\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}-\ln\left(\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}{\rm e}^{3}}{24}\right)\right]+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}{\ln^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1}}\right),

\displaystyle J_{0}^{*}=\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}{6}\varepsilon\ln^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}{\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}\right)\right),

(4.122)

\displaystyle\delta^{*}=\begin{aligned} -\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}}{3\beta_{2}\varkappa}\varepsilon\biggl[\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}-&\ln\left(\sqrt{\pi}\frac{\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}{\rm e}^{3}}{24}\right)\biggr]-\\
&-\frac{3\gamma+\alpha_{1}+2\nu}{2\varkappa}\varepsilon+O\left(\varepsilon\frac{\ln\ln\varepsilon^{-1}}{\ln^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1}}\right).\end{aligned}

It is to be noted that (
4.121
), (
4.122
) hold true provided
\varkappa\neq 0
,
\alpha_{2}\neq 0
,
\beta_{1,2}\neq 0
. We apply (
2.16
), (
2.18
), (
2.23
) to obtain:

\beta_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}\sqrt{-D}}\left(G^{\prime}_{1x}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}&G^{\prime\prime}_{1x^{2}}&G^{\prime\prime}_{2x^{2}}\\
0&G^{\prime}_{1x}&G^{\prime}_{2x}\\
F^{\prime}_{y}&G^{\prime}_{1y}&G^{\prime}_{2y}\end{array}\right|+G^{\prime}_{2x}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}&G^{\prime\prime}_{1x^{2}}&G^{\prime\prime}_{2x^{2}}\\
0&G^{\prime}_{1x}&G^{\prime}_{2x}\\
F^{\prime}_{z}&G^{\prime}_{1z}&G^{\prime}_{2z}\end{array}\right|\right),

\alpha_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(-D)^{5/2}}\left(F^{\prime}_{y}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}&F^{\prime\prime}_{xy}&F^{\prime\prime}_{xz}\\
0&F_{y}&F_{z}\\
G^{\prime}_{1x}&G^{\prime}_{1y}&G^{\prime}_{1z}\end{array}\right|+F^{\prime}_{z}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}F^{\prime\prime}_{x^{2}}&F^{\prime\prime}_{xy}&F^{\prime\prime}_{xz}\\
0&F^{\prime}_{y}&F^{\prime}_{z}\\
G^{\prime}_{2x}&G^{\prime}_{2y}&G^{\prime}_{2z}\end{array}\right|\right),

\beta_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(-D)^{3/2}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&F^{\prime}_{y}&F^{\prime}_{z}\\
G^{\prime}_{1x}&G^{\prime}_{1y}&G^{\prime}_{1z}\\
G^{\prime}_{2x}&G^{\prime}_{2y}&G^{\prime}_{2z}\end{array}\right|.

If all coefficients
\varkappa
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\beta_{1,2}
vanish then fixed point does not exist for small values of
J_{0}
. In other cases one needs to perform further asymptotic analysis to obtain conditions for existence of a periodic orbit and its period-doubling bifurcation.

One may also remark that the distance between the fixed point and the fold is

\rho=\delta F^{\prime\prime}_{x\delta}\sqrt{\frac{F^{\prime 2}_{y}+F^{\prime 2}_{z}}{(F^{\prime}_{y}F^{\prime\prime}_{xz})^{2}+F^{\prime\prime 2}_{x^{2}}(F^{\prime 2}_{y}+F^{\prime 2}_{z})}}+O(\delta^{2}).

Thus, the cascade of period-doubling bifurcations occurs when the equilibrium is not very close to the fold, but situated at a distance of the order

O(\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon^{-1})
.

Example: the FitzHugh-Nagumo system

We apply our results to the FitzHugh-Nagumo system (
1.5

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}x^{3}+x^{2}(1-\delta)+x(2\delta-\delta^{2})-y-z\,,

\displaystyle x\,,

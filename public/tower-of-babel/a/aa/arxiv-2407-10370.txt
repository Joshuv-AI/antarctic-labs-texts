Borel complexity of families of finite equivalence relations via large cardinals
Michael C. Laskowski, Danielle S. Ulrich

Abstract
We consider a large family of theories of equivalence relations, each with finitely many classes, and assuming the existence of an ω \omega -Erdős cardinal, we determine which of these theories are Borel complete. We develop machinery, including forbidding nested sequences which implies a tight upper bound on Borel complexity, and admitting cross-cutting absolutely indiscernible sets which in our context implies Borel completeness. In the Appendix we classify the reducts of theories of refining equivalence relations, possibly with infinite splitting.



1
Introduction

For many years, the authors have sought to identify dividing lines for Borel complexity of invariant classes of countable structures. One of the stumbling blocks has been identifying how to handle types in stable theories that have a perfect set of strong types extending it. Indeed, in
[
18
]
, along with Richard Rast, the authors proved that REF(bin), the theory of binary splitting, refining equivalence relations, is not Borel complete (in fact, ‘countable sets of countable sets of reals’ do not Borel embed into
\Mod(REF(bin))
yet the isomorphism relation on pairs of countable models is not Borel. It became apparent that many of the existing tools of Descriptive Set Theory would not be applicable to such theories.

In order to isolate the strong type problem from other phenomena, we concentrate on families of theories whose model theory is extremely tame. For almost all of this paper, we investigate theories of countably many equivalence relations, each with finitely many classes. Any such theory is mutually algebraic, equivalently, every completion is weakly minimal with trivial geometries (see e.g.,
[
11
]
). Moreover, in our examples, for any model
M
,
{\rm acl}(X)=X
for every subset
X\subseteq M
and there is a unique 1-type.
Somewhat surprisingly, we find that even here, understanding the Borel complexity of such a theory is extremely involved – so much so that in some cases we are only able to prove non-Borel completeness by using a large cardinal axiom.

Why should the situation be so complicated? With Proposition 4.4 of
[
11
]
, the first author showed that the
elementary diagram

\ElDiag(M)
of any model of a mutually algebraic theory admits a strong structure theory. From this, it follows easily that for any mutually algebraic theory
T
, the elementary diagram
\ElDiag(M)
has a Borel isomorphism relation and there is no
Borel embedding of ‘countable sets of countable sets of reals’ into the class of countable models of
\ElDiag(M)
. But, passing from
Th(M)
to
\ElDiag(M)
obviates the behavior we were aiming to study. For
M
mutually algebraic, the strong type structure on
\ElDiag(M)
is degenerate.

The Borel complexity of mutually algebraic structures is controlled by the profinite group of elementary permutations of
{\rm acl}^{eq}(\emptyset)
. In previous works, we carefully analyzed
REF(\delta)
, refining equivalence relations in which each
E_{n}
-class is partitioned into
\delta(n+1)

E_{n+1}
-classes. Here, we prove that whereas
REF(\delta)
is not Borel complete, there is a
tame expansion
(see Definition
8.1
) of some countable model that is Borel complete. As well, in the Appendix we prove that no model
M
of the theory of refining equivalence relations has any interesting reducts, from which it follows that no reduct of
REF(\delta)
is Borel complete either.
This last remark is rather peculiar. If one starts with a countable model

M\models REF(\delta)
, neither its theory nor any of the theories of its reducts are Borel complete. Moreover, if one adds constants naming each point and thereby obtaining
\ElDiag(M)
, this expanded structure is not Borel complete. However, the expansion
\ElDiag(M)
does have a Borel complete reduct, say by Theorem 3.2 of

[
12
]
.

By contrast, in a pair of papers
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
, we give a description of the countable models of
CC(\delta)
, the theory of countably many, cross-cutting equivalence relations
E_{n}
, where each
E_{n}
has
\delta(n)<\omega
equivalence relations. On one hand, if

\{\delta(n):n\in\omega\}
is unbounded, then

is Borel complete by Theorem 2.1 of
[
12
]
. By contrast, if
\delta(n)<m
for some fixed
m\in\omega
, then
CC(\delta)
is far from being Borel complete by Theorem 6.2 of
[
13
]
.
With Corollary
7.8
here, we generalize both of these results.
However, so long as
\delta(n)\geq 2
for each
n
, there exist countable models
M\models CC(\delta)
that have Borel complete reducts.

2
Borel complexity and dividing lines from variants of indiscernibility

In their seminal paper
[
4
]
, Friedman and Stanley define and develop a notion of
Borel reducibility
among
first order and infintarily definable classes of structures with universe
\omega
in countable languages. The classes
\Mod(\Phi)
for
\Phi\in L_{\omega_{1},\omega}

are precisely those classes that are
Borel and invariant under permutations of
\omega
.
Such classes include
\Mod(T)
, the countable models of first order theories
T
. For such sentences
\Phi
,
\Psi
, possibly in different languages, a
Borel reduction
is a Borel function
f:\Mod(\Phi)\rightarrow\Mod(\Psi)
that satisfies
M\cong N
if and only if
f(M)\cong f(N)
. One says that
\Phi
is
Borel reducible
to
\Psi
,
\Phi\leq_{B}\Psi
, if there is a Borel reduction
f:\Mod(\Phi)\rightarrow\Mod(\Psi)
.
\Phi
and
\Psi
are
Borel equivalent
if
\Phi\leq_{B}\Psi
and
\Psi\leq_{B}\Phi
.
Among Borel invariant classes there is a maximal class with respect to
\leq_{B}
. We say
\Phi
is
Borel complete
if
\Mod(\Phi)
is in this maximal class. Examples include the (incomplete) theories of graphs, linear orders, groups, and fields.

Many of the classical tools of Descriptive Set Theory do not help in this context, as the isomorphism relation on pairs of
models in
\Mod(T)
is typically not Borel. Here, we adapt various tools from model theory to aid in identifying the Borel complexity of theories.

Indiscernible sequences inside models of a theory
or an infinitary sentence are important in determining the model-theoretic complexity of
its class of models.
However, in the first order context, merely asking for the existence of a model with a non-constant, infinite sequence of
indiscernibles is not a strong requirement. Indeed, a compactness argument shows that any theory
T
admitting an infinite model also admits models containing non-constant indiscernible sequences of any order type.
This extreme freedom may fail for sentences
\Phi\in L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
, but the existence of a non-constant, infinite indiscernible sequence inside some model does not imply that the class of countable models of
\Phi
is complicated.
Indeed, examples of Leo Marcus
[
15
]
and Julia Knight
[
9
]
show that a sentence
\Phi\in L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
can have a unique model
M
, yet
M
contains an infinite, fully indiscernible subset.

However, both weakenings and strengthenings of indiscernibility impact Borel complexity.
Recall that in any structure
M
, a sequence
(a_{n}:n\in\omega)
is indiscernible iff it is nested (see Definition
2.1
) and
{\rm tp}(a_{m}/A_{<m})
does not split over
A_{<n}
for every
n<m<\omega
.
We concentrate on the first of these two conditions. The concept of non-splitting will not play a role in this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Say
M
is any
{\mathcal{L}}
-structure and let
(a_{n}:n\in\omega)
be any sequence of singletons from
M
. As notation, for each
n\in\omega
, let

A_{<n}=\{a_{j}:j<n\}
and let
q_{n}(x)={\rm qftp}(a_{n}/A_{<n})
. Call the sequence nested if
q_{n}(x)\subseteq q_{m}(x)
for every
n\leq m
. Alternatively,
(a_{n}:n\in\omega)
is nested if for each
n
,
(a_{i}:i<n,a_{n})
and
(a_{i}:i<n,a_{n+1})
have the same quantifier-free type.
We say that
\Phi
forbids nested sequences if, for every
M\models\Phi
, every nested sequence from
M
is eventually constant, i.e.,
for some
n
,
a_{m}=a_{n}
for every
m\geq n
. We say
\Phi
admits nested sequences if the negation holds.

This is an extremely restrictive condition on
\Phi
. By compactness, we see that no first order theory
T
with an infinite model forbids nested sequences.
We note two consequences of this property. The first connects to classical notions of
\alpha
-back-and-forth systems, at least when
\alpha=1
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
M,N
are
{\mathcal{L}}
-structures and
\bar{a}\in M^{n}
,

\bar{b}\in N^{n}
. We say
(M,\bar{a})\equiv_{0}(N,\bar{b})
if and only if
{\rm qftp}(\bar{a})={\rm qftp}(\bar{b})
;

For any ordinal
\alpha
,
(M,\bar{a})\equiv_{\alpha+1}(N,\bar{b})
if, for every
a^{*}\in M
there is
b^{*}\in N
such that

(M,\bar{a}a^{*})\equiv_{\alpha}(N,\bar{b},b^{*})
and vice versa; and

For any non-zero limit ordinal
\delta
,
(M,\bar{a})\equiv_{\delta}(N,\bar{b})
if and only if
(M,\bar{a})\equiv_{\alpha}(N,\bar{b})
for every
\alpha<\delta
.

Here, we are only concerned with the case
\alpha=1
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say
M\subseteq^{*}_{1}N
if
M
is a substructure of
N
such that, for all
\bar{a}\in M^{<\omega}
,
(M,\bar{a})\equiv_{1}(N,\bar{a})
.
A 1-embedding
f:M\preceq^{*}_{1}N
is an
{\mathcal{L}}
-embedding for which
f(M)\subseteq^{*}_{1}N
.

It is easily seen that for
M\subseteq N
,
M\subseteq^{*}_{1}N
if and only if for every
\bar{a}\in M^{<\omega}
and every
b\in N
, there is
a^{*}\in M

such that
{\rm qftp}(\bar{a}a^{*})={\rm qftp}(\bar{a}b)
if and only if ‘
M
is relatively
\omega
-saturated in
N
for quantifier-free types.’

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
f:M\preceq^{*}_{1}N
, where
M
forbids nested sequences. Then
f
is onto.

Proof.

We can suppose
M\subseteq_{1}^{*}N
. By way of contradiction, suppose there is some
b\in N\setminus M
. We construct a non-constant, nested sequence
(a_{n}:n\in\omega)\subseteq M

as follows. Suppose
(a_{i}:i<n)\subseteq M
have been chosen. As
f(M)\subseteq^{*}_{1}N
, choose
a_{n}\in M
such that
{\rm qftp}(a_{i}:i\leq n)={\rm qftp}(a_{i}:i<n)b
.
As
b
realizes
{\rm tp}(a_{n}/A_{<n})
for each
n
, we have
q_{n}(x)\subseteq q_{m}(x)
for all
m\geq n
, so the sequence
(a_{n}:n\in\omega)
is nested. Since

,
the sequence is non-constant as well.

In
[
5
]
, Su Gao investigated automorphism groups
\Aut(M)
that admit a compatible, left-invariant, complete metric. Such groups are now called

Gao

has no proper

-elementary extension if and only if every
L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
-embedding

is onto.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
\Phi
forbids nested sequences of types, then
\Aut(M)
is cli for every countable

.

Proof.

By way of contradiction, suppose there were some
M\models\Phi
with a proper,

.
In particular,
M\subseteq_{1}^{*}N
, which contradicts Proposition

is cli for every

We remark that

being cli. Indeed, the examples of Marcus and Knight mentioned above are

In

For each

Notation 2.6

For any

\delta(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-2},x_{n-1})\in\pi^{*}(r)\quad\hbox{if and only if}\quad\delta(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-2},x_{n})\in r

As an example of this usage, for any
{\mathcal{L}}
-structure

\omega

Note that

. Now, given

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose
\Phi\in L_{\omega_{1},\omega}
forbids nested sequences. Then:

1.

Every
\varphi\in\CSS_{\rm ptl}(\Phi)

\mathcal{L}_{(2^{\aleph_{0}})^{+},\omega}

.

\Phi

\Phi
.

Proof.

{\mathcal{L}}
is countable. (4) follows from (3) by Theorem 3.10(2) of
[

So, it suffices to prove (1).
By Proposition 4.6 of

Definition 2.8

For topological groups

With Theorem 5.5 of

Definition 3.1
.

Given a poset
(P,\leq)
and

, let the downward closure of

, be the set of all
p\in P

for some
q\in Q
. We say that

. When

is a singleton we write

instead of

.

Say that
(P,\leq)
is down-finite if for all

is finite.

For
\alpha

\alpha
from
P
, or just an
\alpha

(p_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha)
from
P
with
p_{\beta}<p_{\beta^{\prime}}
for all
\beta<\beta^{\prime}<\alpha
. We define the height of
P
,
{\rm ht}(P)
, to be the supremum of lengths of chains from
P
. When
(P,\leq)

is down-finite, every chain from
P
is of length at most
\omega
, so
{\rm ht}(P)
is always finite or
\omega
.

If

is of bounded height. This includes the case whenever
P
is finite.
For every

, the height of

{\rm ht}_{P}(q)
,
is defined to be

. If

is down-finite, then

for every

(it is always
\leq|P_{\leq q}|
).

The following fact is easy.

Fact 3.2
.

Suppose
(P,\leq)
is down-finite. If

, then for every chain
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\subseteq P_{\leq q}
we must have

and, for every

{\rm ht}(r_{k})=k
. In particular, if
{\rm ht}(q)=n
and
k\leq n
, then some

has

.

Proof.

First note that if
r_{n}\neq q
, then concatenating
q
at the end would give a chain of length

, which is forbidden.
More generally, for any
k\leq n
, the chain
(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
witnesses that
{\rm ht}(r_{k})\geq k
. However, if there were a longer chain
in

we could concatenate
(r_{k+1},\dots,r_{n})
to it, contradicting
{\rm ht}(q)=n
.

Suppose

Suppose not. We construct a family of arbitrarily long pairwise orthogonal finite chains.

Suppose we have found pairwise orthogonal chains

Definition 4.1
.

Let

•

is a countable, down-finite poset;

•

If

•

•

Fact 5.1

\bigwedge(f,f^{\prime})=\{q\in P:{\mathfrak{M}}_{P}\models E_{q}(f,f^{\prime})\}

2.

If

\bigwedge(a,a^{\prime})=\bigwedge(h(a),h(a^{\prime}))

For all

.

satisfy conditions (1), (2), and (3) in the definition of
\sim
. Then
\mbox{qftp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{P}(c)}(\overline{a})=\mbox{qftp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{P}(c)}(\overline{b})
and Clause (4) holds as well.

Proof.

Clearly each
{\bf c}(a_{i})={\bf d}(b_{i})
since
f_{i}
is color-preserving. Next, choose
i,j<i^{*}
. We show that

M\models E_{p}(a_{i},a_{j})
if and only if

for every
p\in P
by splitting into cases. First, if
E_{R}(a_{i},a_{j})
holds, then as
f_{i}=f_{j}
we are done since

f_{i}
preserves quantifier-free types. If
E_{R}(a_{i},a_{j})
fails,
then choose
n
to be
least such that

. Then by (3),

[f_{i}]_{Q_{n}}([a_{i}]_{Q_{n}})=[b_{i}]_{Q_{n}}
and
[f_{i}]_{Q_{n}}([a_{j}]_{Q_{n}})=[b_{j}]_{Q_{n}}
.
It follows that for all

if and only if

. In particular this holds holds for

. By choice of

, we have that
M\models\lnot E_{p_{n}}(a_{i},a_{j})
, so
N\models\lnot E_{p_{n}}(b_{i},b_{j})

as well.
Thus, whenever

, both

and

, so

is preserved in all cases. Thus,
\mbox{qftp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{P}(c)}(\overline{a})=\mbox{qftp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{P}(c)}(\overline{b})
.
Clause (4) follows from this since

and

are inverses.

The existence of an isomorphism

as in Theorem
9.3
,
follows immediately from our second claim.

Claim 2.

\sim
describes a back-and-forth system from
(M,{\bf c})
to
(N,{\bf d})
.

Proof.

We have already established that
\sim
preserves quantifier-free type, and it follows from the definition that
\sim
is symmetric in
M
and
N
, so it is enough to verify the forth condition. So suppose
\overline{a}\sim\overline{b}
via
(f_{i},g_{i}:i<i_{*})
and suppose
a_{i_{*}}
is given. There are four cases.

Case 1.
Suppose
\overline{a}
is empty, i.e.
i_{*}=0
. Then let
f_{0}:(M,{\bf c})\preceq_{1}^{*}(N,{\bf d})
be any
1
-embedding, and this witnesses
a_{0}\sim f_{0}(a_{0})
.

Case 2.
Suppose
E_{R}(a_{i_{*}},a_{i})
for some

. Then let
f_{i_{*}}=f_{i},g_{i_{*}}=g_{i}
. If
f_{i}
was total,
then obviously

, so put
b_{i_{*}}:=f_{i}(a_{i^{*}})
.
On the other hand, if
f_{i}
is not total, then
g_{i}
is total. Then, applying Lemma
9.5
(2) to
g_{i}
, we get that
a_{i^{*}}=g_{i}(b_{i_{*}})
for some

.
In either case, we have
\overline{a}a_{i_{*}}\sim\overline{b}b_{i_{*}}
.

Assuming we are not in Case 1 nor Case 2, let
n
be maximal such that there is

with

. Fix such an

.

Case 3.
If
a_{i_{*}}\in\mbox{dom}(f_{i})
then let
f_{i_{*}}=f_{i},g_{i_{*}}=g_{i}
and let
b_{i_{*}}=f_{i}(a_{i_{*}})
, and then
\overline{a}a_{i_{*}}\sim\overline{b}b_{i_{*}}
.

Case 4.
Suppose
a_{i_{*}}\not\in\mbox{dom}(f_{i})
. Then

is total by Definition
9.7
(1). By Lemma
9.5
(1) we can find
f_{i_{*}}:(M,{\bf c})\preceq_{*}(N,{\bf d})
with

and
[g_{i}]_{Q_{n}}
inverse to each other. Let

and let

. Then this works.

With the proof of Theorem
9.3
in hand,
at the cost of introducing a large cardinal, we obtain
our final result. We first explain what large cardinal we will be using, namely an
\omega
-Erdős cardinal:

Definition 9.8
.

Suppose
\alpha
is an ordinal (we will only use the case
\alpha=\omega
). Then let
\kappa(\alpha)
be the least cardinal
\kappa
with
\kappa\rightarrow(\alpha)^{<\omega}_{2}
(if it exists). In words: whenever
F:[\kappa(\alpha)]^{<\omega}\to 2
, there is some
X\subseteq\kappa(\alpha)
of ordertype
\alpha
, such that
F\mathord{\restriction}_{[X]^{n}}
is constant for each
n<\omega
.

The cardinal
\kappa(\omega)
is a large cardinal: it is always inaccessible and has the tree property. On the other hand, it is absolute to
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{L}
, and is well below the consistency strength of a measurable cardinal. See
[
16
]
for a description of these results.

In
[
17
]
the second author defines the notion of thickness. For every
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}
and for every infinite cardinal
\lambda
we have the thickness
\tau(\Phi,\lambda)
of
\Phi
at
\lambda
, a cardinal invariant of the complexity of
\Phi
. The second author also defines what it means for
(\Phi,\sim_{\alpha\omega})
to have the Schröder-Bernstein property. Then we have:

Theorem 9.9
.

1.

(Follows from Theorem 5.8 of
[
17
]
) For all
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega_{1}\omega}
, if
\Phi\leq_{B}\Psi
then for all
\lambda
,
\tau(\Phi,\lambda)\leq\tau(\Psi,\lambda)
;

2.

(Corollary 5.13 of
[
17
]
) If TAG denotes torsion abelian groups then
\tau(\mbox{TAG},\lambda)=\beth_{1}(\lambda)
whenever
\lambda
is inaccessible or
\aleph_{0}
.

3.

(Theorem 11.8 of
[
17
]
) Suppose
\kappa(\omega)
exists,
\alpha<\kappa(\omega)
, and
(\Phi,\sim_{\alpha\omega})
has the Schröder–Bernstein property. Then for all
\lambda
,
\tau(\Phi,\lambda)\leq\lambda^{<\kappa(\omega)}
. In particular,
\tau(\Phi,\kappa(\omega))\leq\kappa(\omega)
.

Putting these together we get

Corollary 9.10
.

Suppose
\kappa(\omega)
exists, and
\alpha<\kappa(\omega)
, and
(\Phi,\sim_{\alpha\omega})
has the Schröder–Bernstein property. Then TAG is not Borel reducible to
\Phi
, and so
\Phi
is not Borel complete.

Our final theorem follows immediately.

Theorem 9.11
.

Suppose
(P,\leq,\delta)\in{\mathbb{P}}
is minimally unbounded. If
\kappa(\omega)
exists then
T_{P}
is not Borel complete.

Proof.

Suppose
(P,\leq,\delta)\in{\mathbb{P}}
is minimally unbounded and
\kappa(\omega)
exists. We claim that
({\mathcal{C}}(\Phi_{P}),\sim_{1\omega})
has the Schröder-Bernstein property, which suffices by Corollary
9.10
and Lemma
4.3
. Let
\preceq_{1,\omega}
be defined like
\preceq_{1}^{*}
, except replacing quantifier-free type by first-order type. We need to show that for all countable
(M,{\bf c}),(N,{\bf d})\models{\mathcal{C}}(\Phi_{P})
, if there exist

and

then

. This follows at once from Theorem

, using the trivial fact that

is a stronger notion than

.

Appendix A
Classifying the reducts of models of REF

In this Appendix, we concentrate on refining equivalence relations, possibly with infinite splitting. Fix a language
{\mathcal{L}}=\{E_{n}:n\in\omega\}
and let
REF
be the (incomplete) theory
asserting that each
E_{n}
is an equivalence relation;
E_{n+1}
refines
E_{n}
, i.e.,
REF\vdash\forall x\forall y(E_{n+1}(x,y)\rightarrow E_{n}(x,y))
;
and there is a function
\delta:\omega\rightarrow\{2,3,\ldots,\infty\}
specifying the number of classes
E_{n+1}
partitions each
E_{n}
-class into. For the Appendix, we allow infinite splitting.
We classify the reducts of any model
M\models REF
, but first we formally define a reduct of a structure.

Definition A.1
.

Given a non-empty set
M
and two families
{\cal A}=\{D_{i}:i\in I\}
and
{\cal A}^{\prime}=\{D_{j}^{\prime}:j\in J\}
of sets of subsets of
M^{k(j)}
for various
k(j)
,
we say that
{\cal A}
and
{\cal A}^{\prime}
are
\emptyset
-definably equivalent if
every
D_{j}^{\prime}
is
\emptyset
-definable in the structure
(M,D_{i}:i\in I)
, and every
D_{i}
is
\emptyset
-definable in the structure
(M,D_{j}^{\prime}:j\in J\}
.

Evidently, if
{\cal A}
and
{\cal A}^{\prime}
are
\emptyset
-definably equivalent, then the two structures above have the same definable sets, either with or without parameters.

Definition A.2
.

Suppose
M
is an
{\mathcal{L}}
-structure and suppose
\{D_{i}:i\in I\}
is any set of
\emptyset
-definable subsets of
M^{k(i)}
for various
k(i)
.
Any such set defines a reduct of
M
.

Throughout this Appendix, ‘definable’ always means
\emptyset
-definable.

An obvious class of reducts of a model of REF have the form
{\mathcal{L}}_{I}:=\{E_{n}:n\in I\}
for an arbitrary subset
I\subseteq\omega
. Our theorem is that, up to
\emptyset
-definable equivalence,
these are the only reducts of any
M\models REF
.

Theorem A.3
.

Let
M\models REF
be arbitrary and let
{\cal A}=\{D_{i}:I\in I\}
be a set of definable subsets of
M^{k_{i}}
for various
k_{i}
.
Then there is a subset
J\subseteq\omega
such that
{\cal A}
and
\{E_{j}:j\in J\}
are
\emptyset
-definably equivalent.

Towards a proof of Theorem
A.3
, first note that it suffices to prove this when
{\cal A}
is a singleton, i.e., we are given a single definable subset

D(\overline{x})\subseteq M^{\lg(\overline{x})}
. Second, it will simplify the notation to reindex the original language
{\mathcal{L}}
to include two additional equivalence relations.
We insist that
E_{0}
is the equivalence relation on
M
with only one class, i.e.,
M\models\forall x\forall yE_{0}(x,y)
, and we add a new equivalence relation
E_{\omega}

for equality, i.e.,
M\models\forall x\forall y(E_{\omega}(x,y)\leftrightarrow x=y)
.

What we will really prove is the following Theorem, which immediately yields Theorem
A.3
. We phrase it this way to facilitate a delicate induction.

Theorem A.4
.

Let
M\models REF
and let
D(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})\subseteq M^{n}
be definable. Then there is a finite set

F\subseteq\omega+1
such that
\{D,=\}
and
\{E_{j}:j\in F\}
are
\emptyset
-definably equivalent.

The idea of the proof of Theorem
A.4
is as follows. Clearly, any definable
D\subseteq M^{n}
mentions only finitely many
E_{j}
’s, so there is a finite
subset
u\subseteq\omega+1
for which
D
is definable in
M_{u}
, the reduct of
M
to the language
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}:=\{E_{j}:j\in u\}
. Without loss, we may assume

\{0,\omega\}\subseteq u
. This is ‘half’ of what we require. We also need to show that we can ‘whittle away’ unnecessary
E_{j}
’s, still maintaining that
D
is definable,
so that each of the resulting
E_{j}
’s are
\{D,=\}
-definable.

Our toolbox makes heavy use of the fact that for any finite
u\subseteq\omega+1
with
0\in u
, the reduct
M_{u}
of
M
to

{\mathcal{L}}_{u}=\{E_{j}:j\in u\}
is an
\omega
-categorical structure.
First, we conclude that a subset
D\subseteq M_{u}^{k}
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable if and only if
D
is invariant under every
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-automorphism of
M_{u}
.
As well, there are only finitely many 2-types, each of which is isolated. Moreover, we can explicitly describe these 2-types and their isolating formulas:

x=y
is one complete type. If
k=\max(u)<\omega
, then
E_{k}(x,y)\land x\not=y
isolates another complete type.
For each
i\in u
with
i<k
, let
i^{+}
denote the immediate successor of
i
in
u
.
Then for each such
i
, there is a complete 2-type
p_{i}(x,y)
isolated by
E_{i}(x,y)\wedge\neg E_{i+1}(x,y)
.
[Note that since
0\in u
and
E_{0}(x,y)
always holds, this list is exhaustive.]
We introduce one technical concept.

Definition A.5
.

Say
D(\overline{x})
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable with
k=\max(u)
. We say
D
is
u
-irreflexive if
D(\overline{x})\vdash\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{k}(x_{i},x_{j})
.

Lemma A.6
.

Suppose
D(\overline{x})
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable with
k=\max(u)
. Let
w=u\setminus\{k\}
and suppose
D(\overline{x})
is

Then
D(\overline{x})
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{w}
-definable.

Proof.

We show that
D
is invariant under every
{\mathcal{L}}_{v}
-automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(M_{v})
. So choose
\bar{a}
with
D(\bar{a})
holding and let
\bar{b}=\sigma(\bar{a})
.
Since
D
is
w
-irreflexive, we have that
\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{\ell}(a_{i},a_{j})
, where
\ell=\max(w)
. As
\sigma
preserves

, we also have

\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{\ell}(b_{i},b_{j})
. As

refines

, we conclude that
\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{k}(a_{i},a_{j})
and
\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{k}(b_{i},b_{j})
.
Thus,
\sigma
also preserves
E_{k}
on

, so
{\rm tp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{u}}(\bar{a})={\rm tp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{u}}(\bar{b})
.
Since

is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable and
D(\overline{a})
holds, we get
D(\overline{b})
holds as well.

The following Proposition provides the crux of the induction used in proving Theorem
A.4
.

Proposition A.7
.

Suppose
u\subseteq\omega+1
is finite with
0\in u
and
\ell<k
the two largest elements of
u
. Suppose
D(\overline{x})
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable and
u
-irreflexive.
If
E_{\ell}
is not
\{D,E_{k}\}
-definable, then
D
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{u\setminus\{\ell\}}
-definable.

Proof.

Because of our trivial interpretation of
E_{0}
, the Proposition is easy if
|u|=2
, so assume
|u|\geq 3
and let
m<\ell<k
denote the top three elements in
u
.
Let
v=u\setminus\{\ell\}
.
We begin by defining a single
\{D,E_{k}\}
-definable formula
\varphi(x,y)
and we actually prove the stronger statement that if
\varphi
is not equivalent to

,
then
D
is
{\mathcal{L}}_{v}
-definable.

For
\rho\in\Sym(n)
, let

be the same formula

, whose free variables are permuted by

.
Let

\theta(x,y):=\bigwedge_{\rho\in\Sym(n)}\forall z_{2}\dots\forall z_{n-1}[D^{\rho}(x,y,z_{2},\dots,z_{n-1})\leftrightarrow D^{\rho}(y,x,z_{2},\dots,z_{n-1})]

Let
{\rm Edist}_{k}(z_{0},\dots,z_{n-1}):=\bigwedge_{i\neq j}\neg E_{k}(x_{i},x_{j})

\psi(x,y):=\bigwedge_{\rho\in\Sym(n)}\forall z_{1}\dots\forall z_{n-1}\left({\rm Edist}_{k}(x,\overline{z})\wedge{\rm Edist}_{k}(y,\overline{z})\rightarrow[D^{\rho}(x,\overline{z})\leftrightarrow D^{\rho}(y,\overline{z})]\right)

Finally, put

The proof of Proposition

follows immediately from the following three claims.

Claim 1.

E_{\ell}(x,y)\vdash\varphi(x,y)
.

Proof.

Choose any
a,b\in M
with

similar and left to the reader.
Choose any
c_{2},\dots,c_{n-1}
from
M
. By symmetry, it suffices to show that

is preserved when

D^{\rho}=D
.
If both

holds. Since

.
In particular,
\neg E_{k}(a,c_{j})

. It follows that
{\rm tp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{u}}(a,b,c_{2},\dots,c_{n-1})={\rm tp}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{u}}(b,a,c_{2},\dots,c_{n-1})

D

{\mathcal{L}}_{u}
-definable and

holds.
That

Claim 2.
If the
\{D,E_{k}\}

[Here,

E_{i}(x,y)\vdash\varphi(x,y)

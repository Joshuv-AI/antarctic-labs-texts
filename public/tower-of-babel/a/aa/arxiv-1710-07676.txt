Partial inversion of the 2D attenuated X X -ray transform with data on an arc
Hiroshi Fujiwara, Kamran Sadiq, Alexandru Tamasan

Abstract
. In two dimensions, we consider the problem of inversion of the attenuated X X -ray transform of a compactly supported function from data restricted to lines leaning on a given arc. We provide a method to reconstruct the function on the convex hull of this arc. The attenuation is assumed known. The method of proof uses the Hilbert transform associated with A A -analytic functions in the sense of Bukhgeim.

Primary 35J56, 30E20; Secondary 45E05

1.
Introduction

This work concerns a problem of the inversion of the attenuated
X
-ray transform from partial measurements in two dimensional domains. Discriminated by the type of their partial data,
such problems have already been considered both in the non-attenuated and the attenuated case. For example the
180^{0}
angular data in
[
29
,
4
]
, or the partial data case in
[
3
]
; or, in the constant attenuation case in the works
[
22
,
26
,
28
,
16
,
21
]
.

In here we are concerned with yet a different question, where the (fan beam) data is collected on a convex arc. More precisely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a strictly convex domain and
\Lambda
be an arc of its boundary
\varGamma
. The chord
L
joining the endpoints of the arc
\Lambda
partitions the domain in two subdomains
\Omega^{\pm}
, where
\Omega^{+}
denotes the domain enclosed by
\Lambda\cup L
, see Figure
1
below. For a function
f
compactly supported in
\Omega
, we provide a method to reconstruct
f\lvert_{\Omega^{+}}
from its attenuated
X
-ray transform over lines leaning on
\Lambda
. In the attenuated case, the attenuation
a
is assumed known in
\overline{\Omega}
. This is a particular type of the region-of-interest (ROI) inversion problem in tomography
[
9
]
; see
[
5
]
for some limitations on the reconstruction methods of such problems.

Even in the non-attenuated case, if
f
happens to also be supported in
\Omega^{-}
, then its
X
-ray data is incomplete and the classical Radon inversion
[
13
,
27
]
does not apply: while the measurements are affected by the possible nonzero values of
f|_{\Omega^{-}}
, an entire cone of directions through points in
\Omega^{-}
are missing in the data, yielding the reconstruction question nontrivial, even for the restriction
f|_{\Omega^{+}}
.

The source reconstruction problem here is not covered by the inversion from partial data results mentioned above. However, the unique determination of
f\lvert_{\Omega^{+}}
follows from the support theorem in
[
7
]
, provided the attenuation is analytic. One could also adapt the Carleman weight formula in
[
1
]
by finding an explicit Carleman quenching operator as in
[
12
]
. Both such arguments are based on analytic continuation and have not yet yielded a method of reconstruction. The novelty in this work is a reconstruction method, based on the separation of the contribution of
f\lvert_{\Omega^{+}}
from that of
f\lvert_{\Omega^{-}}
, and, in the attenuated case, does not assume analyticity of the attenuation coefficient.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some known properties of the
A
-analytic functions and of the finite Hilbert transform, on which
our reconstruction method is based. We denote by
l_{\infty}
, and by
l_{1}
, the space of bounded, respectively summable, sequences. For
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
, let

\overline{\partial}=\left(\partial_{x}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{y}\right)/2
, and
\partial=\left(\partial_{x}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{y}\right)/2

be the Cauchy-Riemann operators.

A sequence valued map

\Omega\ni z\mapsto{\bf u}(z):=\langle u_{0}(z),u_{-1}(z),u_{-2}(z),...\rangle

in
C(\overline{\Omega};l_{\infty})\cap C^{1}(\Omega;l_{\infty})

is called
\mathcal{L}
-analytic
, if

(5)

\overline{\partial}{\bf u}(z)+\mathcal{L}\partial{\bf u}(z)=0,\quad z\in\Omega,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the left shift operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\langle u_{0},u_{-1},u_{-2},\cdots\rangle=\langle u_{-1},u_{-2},\cdots\rangle.

Note that we use the sequences of non-positive indexes to conform with the original notation in Bukhgeim’s work
[
8
]
.

For completeness we recall the spaces referenced in the results below. For
0<\mu<1
, and
\varGamma
(some part of) the boundary
\partial\Omega
we consider the spaces of sequence valued maps

\displaystyle l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\varGamma)

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bf u}\;:\sup_{\xi\in\varGamma}\sum_{j=1}^{{\infty}}j\lvert u_{-j}(\xi)\rvert<{\infty}\right\},

\displaystyle C^{\mu}(\varGamma;l_{1})

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bf u}:\sup_{\xi\in\varGamma}\lVert{\bf u}(\xi)\rVert_{\displaystyle l_{1}}+\underset{{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi,\eta\in\varGamma\\
\xi\neq\eta\end{subarray}}}{\sup}\frac{\lVert{\bf u}(\xi)-{\bf u}(\eta)\rVert_{\displaystyle l_{1}}}{|\xi-\eta|^{\mu}}<{\infty}\right\}.

We similarly consider
C^{\mu}(\overline{\Omega};l_{1})
, and
C^{\mu}(\overline{\Omega};l_{{\infty}})
.

Analogous to the analytic maps, the
\mathcal{L}
-analytic maps are determined by their boundary values via a Cauchy-like integral formula
[
8
]
. Following
[
10
]
, the Bukhgeim-Cauchy operator
\mathcal{B}
acting on
{\bf u}=\langle u_{0},u_{-1},u_{-2},...\rangle\in l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\varGamma)\cap C^{\mu}(\varGamma;l_{1})

is defined component-wise for
n\leq 0
by

(6)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}{\bf u})_{n}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\varGamma}\frac{u_{n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\varGamma}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-z}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta-z}}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta-z}}{\zeta-z}\right)^{j},\;z\in\Omega;

The explicit form above follows
[
10
]
.

Also similar to the analytic maps, the traces on the boundary of
\mathcal{L}
-analytic maps satisfy some constraints, which can be expressed in terms of a corresponding Hilbert transform introduced in
[
30
]
. More precisely, the Bukhgeim-Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
acting on

{\bf u}\in l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\varGamma)\cap C^{\mu}(\varGamma;l_{1})
is defined
component-wise for
n\leq 0
by

(7)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}{\bf u})_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\varGamma}\frac{u_{n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-\xi}d\zeta+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\varGamma}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-\xi}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta-\xi}}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta-\xi}}{\zeta-\xi}\right)^{j},\;\xi\in\varGamma.

The first integral is in the sense of principal value, e.g.,
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<\mu<1
. Let
{\bf u}=\langle u_{0},u_{-1},u_{-2},...\rangle
be a sequence valued map defined on the boundary
\varGamma
and
\mathcal{B}
be the Bukhgeim-Cauchy operator acting on
{\bf u}
as in (
6
). If
{\bf u}\in l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\varGamma)\cap C^{\mu}(\varGamma;l_{1})
, then
\mathcal{B}{\bf u}\in C^{1,\mu}(\Omega;l_{\infty})\cap C(\overline{\Omega};l_{\infty})
is
\mathcal{L}
-analytic in
\Omega
. If
{\bf u}\in C^{1,\mu}(\Omega;l_{\infty})\cap C(\overline{\Omega};l_{\infty})
is
\mathcal{L}
-analytic in
\overline{\Omega}
, then
{\bf u}(z)=\mathcal{B}\b{(}z)
, for
z\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Moreover, for
{\bf u}
to be the boundary value of an
\mathcal{L}
-analytic function it is necessary and sufficient that

(8)

\displaystyle(I+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}){\bf u}={\bf{0}}.

For the proof of first statement of the Theorem we refer to
[
31
, Theorem 2.2]
. For the proof of the last statement of the Theorem we refer to
[
30
, Theorem 3.2]
.

The method of reconstruction proposed here considers the operator
[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]
, where
H_{t}
is the finite Hilbert transform

(9)

\displaystyle H_{t}g(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{g(s)}{x-s}ds,\quad x\in(-l,l),

with the integral understood in the sense of principal value.
It is well-known, e.g
[
33
]
, that
\mathrm{i}H_{t}
is a bounded operator in
L^{2}(-l,l)
, with spectrum
[-1,1]
, see
[
15
,
25
]
. However,
1
is not in the point spectrum, see e.g.,
[
34
]
for a proof based on Riemann-Hilbert problem. The arguments below use the unitary property in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of the (infinite) Hilbert transform

(10)

\displaystyle Hf(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-{\infty}}^{{\infty}}\frac{f(s)}{x-s}ds.

Proposition 2.1
.

In
L^{2}(-l,l)
,

(11)

\displaystyle Ker[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(-l,l)
be extended by zero to the entire real line.
If
f\in Ker[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}]
, then

|f|=|H_{t}f|=|Hf|
a.e. in
(-l,l)
, and

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(-l,l)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\left\|Hf\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{-l}^{l}|H_{t}f|^{2}dx+\int_{|x|>l}|Hf(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-l}^{l}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{|x|>l}|Hf(x)|^{2}dx.

The latter identity shows that
|Hf(x)|=0
, for
|x|>l
. Thus, for
|x|>l
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{f(s)}{x-s}ds=\frac{1}{x}\int_{-l}^{l}f(s)\sum_{j=0}^{{\infty}}\left(\frac{s}{x}\right)^{j}ds=\sum_{j=0}^{{\infty}}\frac{1}{x^{j+1}}\int_{-l}^{l}f(s)s^{j}ds,

yielding
f
orthogonal on any polynomial. Since polynomials are dense in
L^{2}(-l,l)
,
f=0
.
∎

3.
Partial source reconstruction

In this section, we present the method of reconstruction of
f|_{\Omega^{+}}
. We start with the non-attenuated case,
a=0
. Upon a rotation and translation of the domain
\Omega
, we assume without loss of generality that the arc
\Lambda
lies in the upper half plane with the endpoints on the real axis lying symmetrically about the origin. In particular,
\Omega\cap\{\im{z}=0\}=L=(-l,l)
, for some
l>0
, and
\partial\Omega^{+}=\Lambda\cup L
.

The main idea in our reconstruction method is the recovery of the trace on
L
of an
\mathcal{L}
-analytic function from its trace on
\Lambda
. In the following result, the arc
\Lambda
and the chord
L
are considered without their endpoints.

To simplify the statement, for each
n\leq 0
, let us introduce the functions
F_{n}(z)
defined on
\Lambda\cup L
except at
\pm l
, by

(12)

\displaystyle F_{n}(z):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{u_{n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta+\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-z}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta}-\overline{z}}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}-\overline{z}}{\zeta-z}\right)^{j},\;z\in\Lambda\cup L,

where the first integral is in the sense of principal value. Note that
F_{n}
is known since
u_{n}\lvert_{\Lambda}
are known for all
n\leq 0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a strictly convex bounded domain with
C^{2}
-smooth boundary
\varGamma
,
\Lambda\subset\varGamma
be an arc of its boundary, and
L
be the chord joining the endpoints of
\Lambda
. Let
\Omega^{+}
be the subset of
\Omega
enclosed by
\Lambda\cup L
and
0<\mu<1
. If
{\bf u}\in l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\partial\Omega^{+})\cap C^{\mu}(\partial\Omega^{+};l_{1})
is the boundary value of an
\mathcal{L}
-analytic function in
\Omega^{+}
,
then its trace
{\bf u}\lvert_{L}
on
L
is recovered pointwise from the trace
{\bf u}\lvert_{\Lambda}
on
\Lambda
as follows:
for each
n\leq 0
,
u_{n}\big\lvert_{L}
is the unique solution in
L^{2}(-l,l)
of

(13)

\displaystyle[I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}](u_{n})(x)=

\displaystyle F_{n}(x),\quad x\in L,

where
H_{t}
is the finite Hilbert transform in (
9
), and
the right hand side is determined by the data from (
12
).

Proof.

Since
{\bf u}\in l^{1,1}_{{\infty}}(\partial\Omega^{+})\cap C^{\mu}(\partial\Omega^{+};l_{1})
is the boundary value of an
\mathcal{L}
-analytic function in
\Omega^{+}
, then the necessity part of Theorem
2.1
yields

(14)

\displaystyle[I+\mathrm{i}\mathcal{H}]{\bf u}=0,

where
\mathcal{H}
is the Bukhgeim-Hilbert transform in (
7
).

We consider (
14
) on
L
, where for each
x\in(-l,l)
and
n\leq 0
, the
n
-th component yields

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{u_{n}(s)}{x-s}ds=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{u_{n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-x}d\zeta

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-x}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta}-x}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}-x}{\zeta-x}\right)^{j}

(15)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-x}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta}-x}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}-x}{\zeta-x}\right)^{j}.

Since the last integral in (

) ranges over the reals, it vanishes. The remaining integrals give
F_{n}(x)
, and the expression (
13
) follows.
∎

The equation (
13
) may not have any solution for an arbitrary right hand side in
L^{2}(-l,l)
. However, in the inverse problem, the function
F_{n}
already belongs to the range of
I-\mathrm{i}H_{t}
, so that the solution exists. Moreover, since the range is open, (
13
) is uniquely solvable in a sufficiently small
L^{2}
-neighborhood of
F_{n}
.

We remark that
{\bf u}\lvert_{L}
satisfies further constraints. If we consider (
14
) on
\Lambda
, we obtain for each

and
n\leq 0
:

\displaystyle u_{n}(z)-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{u_{n}(s)}{z-s}ds=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\frac{u_{n}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{\Lambda}\left\{\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta-z}-\frac{d\overline{\zeta}}{\overline{\zeta-z}}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(\zeta)\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta-z}}{\zeta-z}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\pi}\int_{-l}^{l}\left\{\frac{1}{s-z}-\frac{1}{s-\overline{z}}\right\}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(s)\left(\frac{s-\overline{z}}{s-z}\right)^{j}ds,

or, by (

(16)

\displaystyle\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{u_{n}(x)}{x-z}dx+\int_{-l}^{l}\frac{(z-\overline{z})}{|x-z|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{n-j}(x)\left(\frac{x-\overline{z}}{x-z}\right)^{j}dx=\pi\mathrm{i}\left[u_{n}(z)-F_{n}(z)\right],\;\;z\in\Lambda.

The additional constraints (

Since
{\bf u}
is now known on the entire boundary

, we employ the Bukhgeim-Cauchy integral formula (

, and then the source in

\displaystyle f|_{\Omega^{+}}(z)=2\re\{\partial[\mathcal{B}{\bf u}]_{-1}(z)\},\quad z\in\Omega^{+}.

For the sequence valued map

of solutions to the transport equation, particularly as shown in

Next we consider the attenuated case.
Reconstruction in the attenuated case follows by the reduction to the non-attenuated case via the special integrating factor

In the equation above

is orthogonal to

\displaystyle=f(z)e^{-h(z,\theta)}\quad(z,\theta)\in\Omega\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}},

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle e^{-h(z,\theta)}:=\sum_{k=0}^{{\infty}}\alpha_{k}(z)e^{\mathrm{i}k{\varphi}},\quad e^{h(z,\theta)}:=\sum_{k=0}^{{\infty}}\beta_{k}(z)e^{\mathrm{i}k{\varphi}},\quad(z,\theta)\in\overline{\Omega}\times{{\mathbf{S}}^{1}}.

Proof of the Theorem

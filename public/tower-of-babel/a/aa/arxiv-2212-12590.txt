Boundedness of the conformal hyperboloidal energy for a wave-Klein-Gordon model
Philippe G. LeFloch, Jesús Oliver, Yoshio Tsutsumi

Abstract
We consider the global evolution problem for a model which couples together a nonlinear wave equation and a nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation, and was independently introduced by LeFloch and Y. Ma and by Q. Wang. By revisiting the Hyperboloidal Foliation Method, we establish that a weighted energy of the solutions remains (almost) bounded for all times. The new ingredient in the proof is a hierarchy of fractional Morawetz energy estimates (for the wave component of the system) which is defined from two conformal transformations. The optimal case for these energy estimates corresponds to using the scaling vector field as a multiplier for the wave component.

Introduction

Aim of this paper.

We are interested in the global-in-time existence problem for coupled systems of wave-Klein-Gordon equations and we study a typical system that arises as a simple model formally derived from the Einstein equations of general relativity in presence of massive
matter fields. We are interested in spatially compactly supported solutions and in analyzing their decay in time,
and therefore our analysis focus on the interior of a light cone region.
Throughout, we work in Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}:=(\mathbb{R}^{4},\eta)
where

\eta:=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
denotes the Minkowski metric in inertial coordinates denoted by

(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})=(t,x)=(t,x^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
(
i=1,2,3
).
The level sets of the time function
t:\mathbb{R}^{3+1}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
are uniformly spacelike and provide natural hypersurfaces on which the initial value problem can be posed. On the other hand, an alternative standpoint is provided by the slices (with
s\geq s_{0}>1
for notational convenience)

\mathcal{H}_{s}:=\big\{(t,x)\,/\,t>0;\,t^{2}-r^{2}=s^{2}\big\},

(1.1)

in which we used the notation
r^{2}:=|x|^{2}=(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}
. These slices define a foliation by spacelike hypersurfaces of (a subset of) the
interior of the future light cone from the origin.

)

Specifically, we follow the presentation in the monograph

Statement of the main results

2.1
The hyperboloidal foliation

Spacetime slices.

We need some additional notation and, by following
[
23
]
, we introduce the interior of the future light cone with vertex
(1,0,0,0)
, namely

\mathcal{K}:=\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/\,r<t-1\big\},

(2.1)

while we foliate the interior of the cone
\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/r<t\big\}
from the origin
(0,0,0,0)

by the spacelike hypersurfaces of constant hyperboloidal time
s

\mathcal{H}_{s}:=\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/\,t>0,\,t^{2}-r^{2}=s^{2}\big\}.

(2.2)

We have fixed some
s_{0}>1
and for any pair
s_{0}\leq s_{1}<s_{2}
, we introduce the following sub-domain of
\mathcal{K}
bounded by two hyperboloids:

\mathcal{K}_{[s_{1},s_{2}]}:=\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/\,s_{1}^{2}\leq t^{2}-r^{2}\leq s_{2}^{2}\ ,r<t-1\big\}\subset\mathcal{K}.

(2.3)

Within
\mathcal{K}
we single out the
interior domain

\mathcal{K}_{\text{int}}:=\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/\,r<t/2\big\}\ \subset\mathcal{K}.

(2.4)

More precisely, the
wave domain
(i.e. essentially the support of the solutions to the wave equation considered below)
is defined to be the complement in

\mathcal{K}
of the domain
\mathcal{K}_{\text{int}}
, namely

\mathcal{K}_{\text{W}}:=\big\{(t,x^{i})\,/\,(t/2)\leq r\leq t-1\big\}\subset\mathcal{K}.

(2.5)

Vector fields.

We define the
semi-hyperboloidal frame

\underline{\partial}=(\underline{\partial}_{\alpha})=(\underline{\partial}_{0},\underline{\partial}_{1},\underline{\partial}_{2},\underline{\partial}_{3})
to be

\underline{\partial}_{0}:=\partial_{t},\qquad\underline{\partial}_{i}:=\partial_{i}+\frac{x^{i}}{t}\partial_{t},

(2.6)

and we also set
\underline{\partial}_{x}:=(\underline{\partial}_{1},\underline{\partial}_{2},\underline{\partial}_{3})
. Observe that the vector fields
\underline{\partial}_{i}
generate the tangent
space to the hyperboloids of constant
s
. We also further decompose
Span
\{\underline{\partial}_{i}\}_{i=1,2,3}
into radial and angular components

(\underline{\partial}_{r},{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}})
with

\underline{\partial}_{r}:=\partial_{r}+{r\over t}\partial_{t},

(2.7)

while
{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}}
denotes derivatives tangential to the surfaces with constant
t-r
and
r
.

2.2
Functional spaces of interest

Standard energy norms.

On any spacelike hypersurface
\mathcal{H}_{s}
of constant hyperboloidal radius
s=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
,
we use the
L^{2}
norm

\|\,\phi(s)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\phi^{2}\,dx\Big)^{1/2},\qquad s\geq s_{0},

(2.8)

where
dx=dx^{1}dx^{2}dx^{3}
is the standard Euclidean volume form. Observe that
dx
differs from the natural volume form on hyperboloids (which is
dV=(s/t)dx
); the above notation will turn out to be convenient in our analysis.
On any given hypersurface
\mathcal{H}_{s}
, the standard
hyperboloidal energy
of wave and Klein-Gordon fields, respectively, read

\displaystyle\|\,\phi\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}}:=\|\,\frac{s}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,\underline{\partial}_{x}\phi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})},

\displaystyle\phi=\phi(s),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\,\psi\,\|_{E_{\text{KG}}}:=\|\,\frac{s}{t}\partial_{t}\psi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,\underline{\partial}_{x}\psi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,\psi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})},

\displaystyle\psi=\psi(s).

(2.10)

Weighted energy norms.

Next, let us define the so-called optical functions

u:=t-r

and

\underline{u}:=t+r,

which is now used in defining our weights as follows:

\tau_{-}:={\langle u\rangle},\qquad\tau_{+}:={\langle\underline{u}\rangle},\qquad\tau_{0}:=\tau_{-}/\tau_{+}.

(2.11)

Observe that
\tau_{-}
can be regarded as a distance to the light cone, while
\tau_{+}
provides a decay weight in, both, timelike and spacelike directions.
Here, for any function
f
we use the notation
{\langle f\rangle}=(1+f^{2})^{1/2}
for the so-called Japanese bracket.
The, given an arbitrary exponent
a\in[0,1]
, we intrduce the
weighted hyperboloidal energy
of the wave component
\phi=\phi(s)
by

\|\,\phi\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}^{a}}:=\|\,\big(1+a\tau_{+}^{a}\tau_{0}^{\max(a,1/2)}\big)\frac{s}{t}\partial_{t}\phi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,s^{a}\underline{\partial}_{x}\phi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+a\,\|\,\frac{s^{a}}{t}\phi\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})},

(2.12)

where, by definition,
E_{W}^{0}=E_{W}
.
In addition, to deal with a source term
F
in the wave equation, we will use a spacetime weighted norm denoted by the subscript
N_{W}^{a}
, given by

\|\,F\,\|_{N_{W}^{a}[s_{0},s_{1}]}:=\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\|\,\tau_{+}^{a}\frac{s}{t}\,F(s)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}\,ds.

(2.13)

High-order norms.

We now define versions of the norms which incorporate additional decay based on the translations and Lorentz boosts of Minkowski space. In the
(t,x^{i})
coordinates, we define the Lie algebra of translations and boosts as follows (with
\alpha=0,1,2,3
and
i=1,2,3
):

\mathbb{L}:=\big\{\partial_{\alpha}\big\}\cup\big\{L_{i}=t\partial_{i}+x^{i}\partial_{t}\big\}.

(2.14)

Given any multi-indices
I=(I_{\alpha})
and
J=(J_{i})
, we use the standard notation

\partial^{I}=\partial^{I_{0}}_{0}\partial^{I_{1}}_{1}\partial^{I_{2}}_{2}\partial^{I_{3}}_{3}
and

L^{J}=L^{J_{1}}_{1}L^{J_{2}}_{2}L^{J_{3}}_{3}
, and we also set

|I|:=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
and
|J|:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}

We define the
high-order energy norms
of interest on any given hyperboloid
\mathcal{H}_{s}
as

\|\,\phi\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}^{a,k}}:=\sum_{|I|+|J|\leqslant k}\|\,\partial^{I}L^{J}\phi\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}^{a}},\quad\qquad\phi=\phi(s)

(2.15)

as well as, for a source term in the wave equation, the spacetime norm

\|\,F\,\|_{N^{a,k}[s_{0},s_{1}]}:=\sum_{|I|+|J|\leqslant k}\|\,\partial^{I}L^{J}F\,\|_{N_{W}^{a}[s_{0},s_{1}]}.

(2.16)

Similar definitions are used the remaining norms.

Conformal norms.

Finally, let us introduce the Bondi-like derivative operators

\partial^{B}_{u}:=\partial_{t}

and
\partial^{B}_{r}:=\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}
,
which are well-defined except at the center
r=0
. We will work with two choices of conformal weight denoted by
\Omega
, namely

\Omega_{r}:=r,\qquad\Omega_{s}:=s^{2}=u\underline{u}.

(2.17)

For each parameter value
a\in[0,1]
, we associate the

modified weighted energy
of a function
\phi=\phi(s)
in Bondi coordinates defined by

\|\,r\phi\,\|_{E_{r}^{a}}:=\|\,\big(1+a\tau_{+}^{a}\tau_{0}^{\max(a,1/2)}\big)\Big(\frac{s}{t}\frac{\partial^{B}_{u}(r\phi)}{r},{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}}\phi\Big)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,\tau_{+}^{a}\big(\frac{\partial^{B}_{r}(r\phi)}{r},\frac{s}{t}{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}}\phi\big)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}

(2.18)

for the choice of weight
\Omega_{s}
, while for the choice
\Omega_{r}
we set

\|\,s^{2}\phi\,\|_{E_{s}^{a}}:=\|\,\big(1+s^{a-1}\tau_{-}\big)\Big(\frac{s}{t}\frac{\partial^{B}_{u}(s^{2}\phi)}{s^{2}},{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}}\phi\Big)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}+\|\,s^{a-1}\tau_{+}\big(\frac{\partial^{B}_{r}(s^{2}\phi)}{s^{2}},\frac{s}{t}{\,{/\penalty\!\!\!\nabla}}\phi\big)\,\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{s})}.

(2.19)

Observe that a computation using Hardy’s inequality shows the inequalities

\displaystyle\|\,\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}}\lesssim\|\,r\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{r}^{a}},\qquad\|\,\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}}\lesssim\|\,s^{2}\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{s}^{a}},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|\,\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}}\approx\|\,r\phi(s)\,\|_{E^{0}_{r}},\qquad\|\,\phi(s)\,\|_{E_{\text{W}}}\approx\|\,s^{2}\phi(s)\,\|_{E^{0}_{s}}.

Divergence identity.

To any solution

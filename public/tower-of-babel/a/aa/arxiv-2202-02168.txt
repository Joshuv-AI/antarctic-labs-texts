Gravitational chiral anomaly for spin 3/2 field interacting with spin 1/2 field
G. Yu. Prokhorov, O. V. Teryaev, V. I. Zakharov

Abstract
The gravitational chiral quantum anomaly is calculated in the framework of the extended Rarita-Schwinger-Adler (RSA) field theory, which includes the interaction with an additional spin 1/2 field. It is shown that the factor in the gravitational chiral anomaly, normalized to the Dirac field anomaly, is equal to –19. The resulting value distinguishes the RSA theory from the other theories of spin 3/2. A direct verification of the conformality of the RSA theory in the strong interaction limit at the level of one-loop three-point graphs was made.



I
Introduction

Quantum anomalies for fields with spin 1/2 have been well studied over the past years. In particular, there is a famous axial quantum anomaly, which includes gauge and gravitational (also called mixed axial-gravitational) parts

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mu}\hat{j}^{\mu}_{A}\rangle_{S=1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}

(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{384\pi^{2}\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}{R_{\rho\sigma}}^{\kappa\lambda}\,,\,\,\,

where
F_{\mu\nu}
is the gauge field strength tensor,
R_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}
is the curvature tensor and
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative.

In the case of higher spins, the theory turns out to be more complicated and, as a rule, has internal problems, which makes the question of quantum anomalies not completely transparent.

Recently, a new value was obtained for the gauge chiral anomaly in the case of spin 3/2
[
1
,
2
]
. One of the most common ways of constructing a spin 3/2 theory is based on the Rarita-Schwinger field. However, the conventional Rarita-Schwinger field theory has a number of pathologies (see
[
3
]
and references therein). In
[
1
]
the extended Rarita-Schwinger-Adler (RSA) theory was proposed, in which some of the pathologies were overcome by introducing a nontrivial chirally symmetric interaction with an additional spin 1/2 field.

In particular, an important problem with the singularity in the Dirac bracket in the limit of weak gauge fields was solved, which allowed to consistently gauge the theory beyond the supergravity approach. Since the Rarita-Schwinger field theory is a generalized Hamiltonian dynamics, the quantum anticommutator is given by the Dirac bracket, not the Poisson bracket. The existence of a singularity in this bracket does not allow constructing a perturbation theory. The introduction of the interaction with an additional spin 1/2 field in
[
1
]
shifted the pole in the Dirac bracket, which, in turn, allowed to find the gauge chiral anomaly by the famous shift method

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\mu}\hat{j}^{\mu}_{A}\rangle_{RSA}=-\frac{5}{16\pi^{2}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\,.

(2)

Thus, the gauge chiral anomaly turns out to be 5 times larger than for spin 1/2. This numerical factor distinguishes RSA theory from other approaches to the description of Rarita-Schwinger fields, such as supergravity
[
4
]
.

Subsequently, the anomaly (
2
) was generalized to the non-Abelian case and used for the grand unification in
[
5
]
, where fields with spin 3/2 participate in the anomaly cancellation.

In this paper, we continue to analyze the RSA theory and find the gravitational chiral anomaly. For Rarita-Schwinger fields, there is a well-known result for the gravitational chiral anomaly

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mu}\hat{j}^{\mu}_{A}\rangle_{RS}=\frac{-21}{384\pi^{2}\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}{R_{\rho\sigma}}^{\kappa\lambda}\,,

Already in the case of the gauge anomaly (
2

To find the anomalies, we use a new method described in

In this case a three-point function with two vector current operators and one axial current operator defines a gauge chiral anomaly. A three-point function with two stress-energy tensors and an axial current defines a gravitational chiral anomaly. Typical diagrams to be found are shown in Fig.

It was shown that in conformally symmetric theories these three-point functions should have a universal form. The specific choice of the theory affects only the numerical factor in front of the universal function and the key point is the equality of this numerical factor to the coefficient in the quantum anomalies: gauge and gravitational.

III
Anomaly calculation method: universal form of three-point functions

There are many ways to find quantum anomalies. In particular, the anomaly (
2
) in
[
1
]
was derived by the well-known method of shifting in the momentum integrals: before taking the divergence, the three-point function for the mean value of the current allows a shift in the momentum variable under the integral, which at the next stage leads to a finite difference of linearly diverging integrals.

However, it turns out that there is a universal method for calculating anomalies that does not require explicit consideration of divergent integrals. This method is based on an analysis of the form of three-point functions. In
[
8
]
, in this way a gauge chiral anomaly was found, and in
[
9
]
– a gravitational chiral anomaly. We start with a simpler case with a gauge chiral anomaly.

III.1
Gauge chiral anomaly

In
[
8
]
it was shown that for conformally symmetric field theories the three-point connected correlator
\langle T\hat{j}_{V}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}_{V}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle_{c}
has a universal form

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{j}_{V}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}_{V}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}_{A}^{\omega}(z)\rangle_{c}=

\displaystyle=-4\mathscr{B}\frac{I^{\mu}_{\mu^{\prime}}(x-z)I^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}(y-z)}{(x-z)^{6}(y-z)^{6}}\varepsilon^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\lambda\omega}\frac{Z_{\lambda}}{Z^{4}}\,,

(30)

where
\mathscr{B}
is an uncertain coefficient, that depends on a particular choice of the field theory. The notations are introduced

\displaystyle I_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu\nu}-2\frac{x_{\mu}x_{\nu}}{x^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x-z)_{\mu}}{(x-z)^{2}}-\frac{(x-y)_{\mu}}{(x-y)^{2}}\,.

(31)

The Eq. (
30
) was derived using (
19
), in particular, the property
T^{\mu}_{\mu}=0
.

On the other hand, we can consider the gauge chiral anomaly

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\mu}\hat{j}^{\mu}_{A}\rangle=-b\frac{\pi^{4}}{4}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}\,.

(32)

The central point for us is the relationship between the three-point function and the gauge chiral anomaly

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}=b\,.

(33)

To substantiate this relationship of the factors, one needs to take the divergence from (
30
) and multiply it by the external fields.

It is technically convenient to consider a special case when all three points in (
30
) lie on the same axis

\displaystyle x_{\mu}=x\,e_{\mu}\,,\,y_{\mu}=y\,e_{\mu}\,,\,z_{\mu}=z\,e_{\mu}\,,

(34)

where
e^{2}=\pm 1
. In this case (
30
) takes the form

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{j}_{V}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}_{V}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle=\frac{4\mathscr{B}\,e^{2}e_{\vartheta}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\mu\nu\omega}}{\pi^{6}(x-y)^{3}(x-z)^{3}(y-z)^{3}}\,.\quad

(35)

Thus, to calculate the gauge chiral quantum anomaly, it is enough to find the three-point function
\langle T\hat{j}_{V}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}_{V}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle
using the Feynman rules in the coordinate representation. In fact, for this we need to multiply the propagators and find the trace of Dirac matrices. Then it is necessary to check that the three-point function has the form (
30
) or (
35
), and then the factor
\mathscr{B}
in front of the universal function determines the coefficient in the gauge chiral anomaly. In particular, this was done for spin 1/2 in
[
8
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{s=1/2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{6}}\,,

(36)

which reproduces the standard formula (
1
). In Section
IV.1
we will use this method to find the anomaly in the extended spin 3/2 theory.

III.2
Gravitational chiral anomaly

The method described above was generalized in
[
9
]
to the case of a gravitational chiral anomaly.
In this case we have to consider a three-point connected correlator with two stress-energy tensors and one axial current. Then for a conformally symmetric theory it will have the universal form

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(x)\hat{T}^{\sigma\rho}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle_{c}=\frac{1}{(x-z)^{8}(y-z)^{8}}

\displaystyle\times\mathscr{I}_{T}^{\mu\nu,\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(x-z)\mathscr{I}_{T}^{\sigma\rho,\sigma^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}}(y-z){t^{TTA}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}}}^{\omega}(Z)\,,\qquad

(37)

where the notations are introduced

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}^{T}_{\mu\nu,\sigma\rho}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}I^{\alpha}_{\sigma}(x)I^{\beta}_{\rho}(x)\,,

(38)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\mu\nu,\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\mu\alpha}\eta_{\nu\beta}+\eta_{\mu\beta}\eta_{\nu\alpha})-\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle t^{TTA}_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho\omega}(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{A}}{Z^{6}}({\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\mu\nu,\eta}}^{\varepsilon}\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\sigma\rho,\kappa\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{\omega}}^{\eta\kappa\lambda}Z_{\lambda}

\displaystyle-6\,\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\mu\nu,\eta\gamma}\mathscr{E}^{T}_{\sigma\rho,\kappa\delta}{\varepsilon_{\omega}}^{\eta\kappa\lambda}Z^{\gamma}Z^{\delta}Z_{\lambda}Z^{-2})\,.

Here we have the same necessary conditions (
19
) for (
37
) to be valid, as in the case of a gauge chiral anomaly. In particular, the coefficient 6 in (
38
) is a direct consequence of the vanishing of the trace of the stress-energy tensor.

On the other hand, it is known that there is a gravitational chiral anomaly in the gravitational field

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{5}\rangle=a\frac{\pi^{4}}{384\sqrt{-g}}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}{R_{\rho\sigma}}^{\kappa\lambda}\,.

(39)

Of key importance for us is the equality of the factor
\mathscr{A}
to the coefficient
a
in the axial-gravitational anomaly

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=a\,.

(40)

To substantiate this equality, in
[
9
]
it was first shown that despite the fact that the one-point anomalous divergence, like (
39
), vanishes in a flat space-time limit, a similar identity for the three-point function (
37
) is not equal to zero. However, the divergence of (
37
) and (
39
) are proportional to the same numerical factor. Thus, we can establish the correspondence (
40
) and calculate gravitational chiral anomaly from the flat space correlator.

As with the gauge anomaly, calculations can be simplified by assuming that all three points lie on the same axis (
34
).
Then (
30
) takes the form

\displaystyle\langle T\,\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(x)\hat{T}^{\sigma\rho}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle=\mathscr{A}\Big(4(x-y)^{5}

\displaystyle\times(x-z)^{3}(y-z)^{3}\Big)^{-1}e_{\vartheta}\Big(\eta^{\nu\rho}\varepsilon^{\sigma\vartheta\mu\omega}+\eta^{\mu\rho}\varepsilon^{\sigma\vartheta\nu\omega}

\displaystyle+\eta^{\nu\sigma}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\mu\rho\omega}+\eta^{\mu\sigma}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\nu\rho\omega}-6e^{2}(e^{\nu}e^{\rho}\varepsilon^{\sigma\vartheta\mu\omega}

\displaystyle+e^{\mu}e^{\rho}\varepsilon^{\sigma\vartheta\nu\omega}+e^{\sigma}e^{\nu}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\mu\rho\omega}+e^{\sigma}e^{\mu}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\nu\rho\omega})\Big)\,.

(41)

Thus, it is necessary to find a three-point correlator
\langle\hat{T}(x)\hat{T}(y)\hat{j}_{A}(z)\rangle
in an ordinary flat space-time and check that it satisfies (
37
) or (
41
). The coefficient in front of the universal function will give us a factor of the gravitational chiral anomaly. In particular, the anomaly for spin 1/2 was found in this way
[
9
]

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{s=1/2}=\frac{1}{\pi^{6}}\,,

(42)

which matches (
1
). In the next section, we will use the described method to find the gravitational chiral anomaly for the extended theory of spin 3/2.

IV
Calculation of anomalies in the Rarita-Schwinger-Adler theory

IV.1
Gauge chiral anomaly

In contrast to the free Rarita-Schwinger fields, for the extended Rarita-Schwinger-Adler theory (
5
), all of the relations (
19
) are satisfied. Vanishing of the trace of the stress-energy tensor allows us to assume the existence of a conformal symmetry, since in most of the known theories with a conserved dilatation current there is also a conformal symmetry
[
10
,
27
]
. This allows us to use the method described in the previous section to find the anomalies.

We will start by considering the gauge anomaly for which the result (
2
) is already known, to demonstrate the features of the methods.

Operators of the currents can be decomposed depending on the field content

\displaystyle\hat{j}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}^{\mu}+\hat{j}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{A}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{A\bar{\psi}\psi}^{\mu}+\hat{j}_{A\bar{\lambda}\lambda}^{\mu}\,,

(43)

where

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)ab}\bar{\psi}^{a}_{\eta_{1}}\psi^{b}_{\eta_{2}}\,,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{J}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}=-i\varepsilon^{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\nu\mu}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{5}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{(\bar{\lambda}\lambda)ab}\bar{\lambda}_{a}\lambda_{b}\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{(\bar{\lambda}\lambda)}=\gamma^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{A\bar{\psi}\psi}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)ab}\bar{\psi}^{a}_{\eta_{1}}\psi^{b}_{\eta_{2}}\,,\,{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}=-i\varepsilon^{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\nu\mu}\gamma_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{j}_{A\bar{\lambda}\lambda}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu}_{A(\bar{\lambda}\lambda)ab}\bar{\lambda}_{a}\lambda_{b}\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\mathcal{J}}^{\mu}_{A(\bar{\lambda}\lambda)}=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,.

(44)

Following
[
1
]
, we will consider the limit
m\to\infty
. According to the Wick theorem, we have

\displaystyle\langle T\bar{\varphi}_{1}(x)\varphi_{2}(x)\bar{\varphi}_{3}(y)\varphi_{4}(y)\bar{\varphi}_{5}(z)\varphi_{6}(z)\rangle_{c}=

\displaystyle-\langle T\bar{\varphi}_{1}(x)\varphi_{4}(y)\rangle\langle T\varphi_{2}(x)\bar{\varphi}_{5}(z)\rangle\langle T\bar{\varphi}_{3}(y)\varphi_{6}(z)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle T\bar{\varphi}_{1}(x)\varphi_{6}(z)\rangle\langle T\varphi_{2}(x)\bar{\varphi}_{3}(y)\rangle\langle T\varphi_{4}(y)\bar{\varphi}_{5}(z)\rangle\,,\quad

(45)

where we left only connected contributions.
\varphi_{i}
is either a
\psi_{\mu}
field or a
\lambda
field.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{j}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle=-\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\mathcal{J}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{5}}(x-z)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{J}^{\omega\eta_{5}\eta_{6}}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{6}\eta_{3}}(z-y)\mathcal{J}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\nu\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{1}}(y-x)\Big\}

\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\mathcal{J}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\mu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{3}}(x-y)\mathcal{J}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\nu\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{5}}(y-z)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{J}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\omega\eta_{5}\eta_{6}}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{6}\eta_{1}}(z-x)\Big\}\,.

(46)

All the terms with field
\lambda
are to be dropped out: they are either equal to zero due to
\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle=0
, or tend to zero in the limit
m\to\infty
.

The calculations can be simplified by considering the case of codirectional vectors (
34
) (the case with arbitrary points is considered in Appendix
A
). Taking into account the limit
m\to\infty
the Green’s function will be

\displaystyle G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\alpha}(x)\gamma^{\alpha}+f_{\mu\nu\alpha}(x)\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4\pi^{2}x^{3}}\Big(\eta_{\nu\alpha}e_{\mu}+\eta_{\mu\alpha}e_{\nu}-8\frac{e_{\alpha}e_{\mu}e_{\nu}}{e^{2}}+3e_{\alpha}\eta_{\mu\nu}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle f_{\mu\nu\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}x^{3}}e^{\beta}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,,

(47)

where in
x^{3}
there is a power of a number, and in
e^{2}
there is a square of a vector. We also used the properties of Dirac matrices, expanding the product of three matrices. Thus, we need to find a trace with only 6 or 7 gamma matrices. Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{j}^{\mu}(x)\hat{j}^{\nu}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle_{c}=\frac{5e^{2}e_{\vartheta}\varepsilon^{\vartheta\mu\nu\omega}}{\pi^{6}(x-y)^{3}(x-z)^{3}(y-z)^{3}}\,.\quad\,\,\,

(48)

Function (
48
) corresponds to the universal conformally symmetric form (
35
) and the coefficient in the anomaly is

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}=\frac{5}{4\pi^{6}}\,.

(49)

Taking into account (
32
) and (
33
), we get agreement with the original result (
2
).

IV.2
Gravitational chiral anomaly

Now we move on to the gravitational chiral anomaly. In this case, we need to find a three-point function with two operators of the stress-energy tensor and one operator of the axial current (
37
). Now the interaction between the fields
\psi_{\mu}
and
\lambda
plays a significant role, since in the limit
m\to\infty
negative powers
m^{-n}
from the propagators can be canceled by positive powers
m^{n}
from the operators of the stress-energy tensor in the vertices.

First, we decompose the stress-energy tensor (
15
) depending on the field content

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}+\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}+\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}+\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\,,

(50)

where

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\lambda\alpha\beta\rho}\bar{\psi}_{\lambda}\gamma_{5}(\gamma^{\mu}\delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}+\gamma^{\nu}\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha})\partial_{\beta}\psi_{\rho}\,,

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\partial_{\eta}\Big(\varepsilon^{\lambda\alpha\beta\rho}\bar{\psi}_{\lambda}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\alpha}([\gamma^{\eta},\gamma^{\mu}]\delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+[\gamma^{\eta},\gamma^{\nu}]\delta^{\mu}_{\beta})\psi_{\rho}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}\Big(\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\nu}\partial^{\mu}\lambda-\partial^{\mu}\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\nu}\lambda+\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\lambda-\partial^{\nu}\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\mu}\lambda\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}m\Big(\bar{\psi}^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\lambda+\bar{\psi}^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\lambda\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}m\Big(\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\mu}\psi^{\nu}+\bar{\lambda}\gamma^{\nu}\psi^{\mu}\lambda\Big)\,.

(51)

Then the correlator (
37
) splits into 32 terms depending on the set of fields

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}(x)\hat{T}^{\sigma\rho}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A}(z)\rangle_{c}=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+(31\,\text{terms})\,,\quad\,\,\,\,

(52)

where we have omitted some notations for short. A typical diagram is shown in Fig.
1
on the right.

It is clear from the condition
\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle=0
in (
29
) in advance that 12 terms in (
52
) are equal to zero

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=

(53)

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle=0\,,\qquad

since they contain one or more propagators with two fields
\lambda
.
Also, in the
m\to\infty
limit, 11 more terms vanish

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle,\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle\to 0\quad(m\to\infty)\,.\qquad

(54)

Since negative powers from the propagators
\langle\psi\bar{\lambda}\rangle,\langle\lambda\bar{\psi}\rangle\sim 1/m
are not compensated by positive powers from the operators
\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda},\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\sim m
. Thus, in contrast to the gauge anomaly, the terms of interaction with the
\lambda
field start to play a role. Finally, in (
52
), only 9 terms remain

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}(x)\hat{T}_{\sigma\rho}(y)\hat{j}_{\omega}^{A}(z)\rangle_{c}=\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle+\langle\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{T}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}\hat{j}^{A}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}\rangle\,,

(55)

where all the terms except the first one give a finite contribution just due to the cancellation of the powers of
m
. Note that the terms of the order of
1/m^{2}
in the propagator
\langle\psi\bar{\psi}\rangle
can obviously be omitted in advance.

Correlators from (
55
) can be calculated using the Feynman rules. It is convenient to represent all the operators in a split form

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\sigma\tau}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta\xi}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)ab}(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})\bar{\psi}_{\eta a}(x_{1})\psi_{\xi b}(x_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\sigma\tau}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta}_{(\bar{\psi}\lambda)ab}\bar{\psi}_{\eta a}(x_{1})\lambda_{b}(x_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{T}^{\sigma\tau}_{\bar{\lambda}\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta}_{(\bar{\lambda}\psi)ab}\bar{\lambda}_{a}(x_{1})\psi_{\eta b}(x_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{j}^{\sigma}_{A\bar{\psi}\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\mathcal{J}^{\sigma\eta\xi}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)ab}\bar{\psi}_{\eta a}(x_{1})\psi_{\xi b}(x_{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta\xi}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\eta\xi\alpha\beta}\gamma_{5}(\gamma^{\sigma}\delta^{\tau}_{\alpha}+\gamma^{\tau}\delta^{\sigma}_{\alpha})\partial_{\beta}^{x_{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}\varepsilon^{\eta\xi\alpha\beta}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\alpha}([\gamma^{\vartheta},\gamma^{\sigma}]\delta^{\tau}_{\beta}

\displaystyle+[\gamma^{\vartheta},\gamma^{\tau}]\delta^{\sigma}_{\beta})(\partial^{x_{1}}_{\vartheta}+\partial^{x_{2}}_{\vartheta})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta}_{(\bar{\psi}\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}m(\gamma^{\sigma}\eta^{\tau\eta}+\gamma^{\tau}\eta^{\sigma\eta})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\sigma\tau\eta}_{(\bar{\lambda}\psi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2}m(\gamma^{\sigma}\eta^{\tau\eta}+\gamma^{\tau}\eta^{\sigma\eta})\,,

(56)

and
\mathcal{J}^{\sigma\eta\xi}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}
was written out earlier in (
44
). Each of the 9 terms in (
55
) splits into two, due to Wick’s theorem (
45
). In particular, the first term in (
55
) has the form

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}(x)\hat{T}^{\sigma\rho}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A\bar{\psi}\psi}(z)\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\scriptscriptstyle x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\\
{\scriptscriptstyle y_{1},y_{2}\to y}\\
{\scriptscriptstyle z_{1},z_{2}\to z}\end{array}}\bigg(-\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\mathcal{D}^{\mu\nu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}(\partial^{x_{1}},\partial^{x_{2}})G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{5}}(x_{2}-z_{1})\mathcal{J}^{\omega\eta_{5}\eta_{6}}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{6}\eta_{3}}(z_{2}-y_{1})

(61)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{D}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\sigma\rho\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}(\partial^{y_{1}},\partial^{y_{2}})G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{1}}(y_{2}-x_{1})\Big\}-\mathrm{tr}\Big\{\mathcal{D}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\mu\nu\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(\partial^{x_{1}},\partial^{x_{2}})G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{3}}(x_{2}-y_{1})

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{D}_{(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\sigma\rho\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}(\partial^{y_{1}},\partial^{y_{2}})G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{5}}(y_{2}-z_{1})\mathcal{J}_{A(\bar{\psi}\psi)}^{\omega\eta_{5}\eta_{6}}G^{\psi\bar{\psi}}_{\eta_{6}\eta_{1}}(z_{2}-x_{1})\Big\}\bigg)\,,

where the derivatives act on the function regardless of whether it is on the left or on the right. The rest of the terms in (
55
) contain
\lambda
fields, either one or two. In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle T\hat{T}^{\mu\nu}_{\bar{\psi}\psi}(x)\hat{T}^{\sigma\rho}_{\bar{\psi}\lambda}(y)\hat{j}^{\omega}_{A\bar{\psi}\psi}(z)\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\scriptscriptstyle x_{1},x_{2}\to x}\\
{\scriptscriptstyle y_{1},y_{2}\to y}\\

V.1
Interpretation of the factor -19

Thus, the factor in the gravitational chiral anomaly in the RSA theory (

3

2

[

Gauge and gravitational anomalies, e.g. (
3

4

[
4
]

\pm
S. If we switch off the interaction the Adler model describes on mass shell one field of spin 3/2 and two fields of spin 1/2. The same counting should apply when we keep interaction non-vanishing but tend momentum to the infinity. This limit is relevant to evaluation of the chiral anomaly. In this way the coefficient
-19
is readily restored from (
3

\displaystyle-19=-21+2\,.

(89)

A similar conclusion can be reached if we analyze the contribution of the ghosts. In particular, the factor

-20

Now, the
-19

\displaystyle-19=-20+1\,.

Note that, as discussed in
[
1
]

The need to consider the ghostless part of the anomaly can be motivated by the fact that ghosts do not propagate in the RSA theory, and the need to add the contribution of the field with spin 1/2 corresponds to the additional field
\lambda

VI
Conclusion

We have calculated the gravitational chiral anomaly for the extended Rarita-Schwinger-Adler model for spin
3/2
fields and found that it is
-19
times larger than the standard anomaly for spin 1/2. The obtained factor differs from the well-known factor
-21
, for the Rarita-Schwinger field theory. We can associate the
-19
factor with the results for spin 3/2 from supergravity
[
4
]
for free fields, by counting the degrees of freedom and their contributions. Then it is obtained either as
-19=-21+2
or
-19=-20+1
.

We have also calculated the chiral gauge anomaly in RSA theory. The obtained numerical factor coincides with the original result obtained using the shift method
[
1
]
.

At the same time, our derivation of the anomalies is a direct verification of the conformality of the RSA theory in the limit of the infinitely strong interaction
m\to\infty
at the level of one-loop three-point functions. We have explicitly shown, that one-loop three-point functions with two vector currents and one axial current, as well as with two stress-energy tensors and one axial current, satisfy the consequences of conformal symmetry
[
8
,
9
]
.

The method used by us for calculating the gravitational chiral anomaly does not need a transition to a curved space-time. The calculation of a three-point function consists in a simple multiplication of usual propagators in the coordinate space.

The obtained result can be used when considering the theories beyond the Standard Model, where the fields with higher spins should participate in the cancellation of anomalies. Another application relates to the cutting edge field of manifestations of quantum anomalies in the properties of relativistic fluids and in condensed matter, where a number of new effects corresponding to various quantum anomalies have been discovered. However, consideration of these applications is beyond the scope of this work.

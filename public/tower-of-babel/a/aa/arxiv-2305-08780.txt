Intersection cohomology of type-A toric varieties
Andras Szenes and Olga Trapeznikova

Abstract
. Type-A toric varieties may be obtained as GIT quotients with respect to a torus action with weights corresponding to roots of the group S ​ L ​ ( k ) SL(k) for some k > 1 k>1 . These varieties appear in various important applications, in particular, as normal cones to strata in moduli spaces of vector bundles. In this paper, we describe the intersection Betti numbers of these varieties, and those of some associated projective varieties. We present an elegant combinatorial model for these numbers, and, using the work of Hausel and Sturmfels, we show that the relevant intersection cohomology groups are endowed with a canonical product structure.



1.  Introduction

One of the best ways to construct toric varieties is via Geometric
Invariant Theory
toric quotients of vector spaces. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
be
n
weights of a complex
torus of dimension
k-1
, and let
x_{1},...,x_{n}
be the coordinates
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Considering the weights as elements of an additive group, and
using the exponential notation, we can write the corresponding diagonal action
of an element
q\in T
as

q\cdot(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(q^{\alpha_{1}}x_{1},\dots,q^{\alpha_{n}}x_{n}).

The ring of
T
-invariant polynomial functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
then is the ring
of functions of an affine algebraic variety, which we denote by
X=X(\mathfrak{A},0)
,
where
\mathfrak{A}=[\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}]
.
In our constructions, we will always assume that the sequence

\mathfrak{A}
is unimodular, i.e. any
(k-1)
-tuple of these
weights is either linearly dependent, or has determinant
\pm 1
with respect
to the lattice
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{A}
they generate. For the purposes of this introduction,
we will consider the case when
n=k(k-1)
, and
\mathfrak{A}
is the root system of
the group
SL(k)
:

\mathfrak{A}=[\alpha_{ij}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}|\;1\leq i\neq j\leq k].

where
\varepsilon_{i}
,
i=1,\dots k
, are the "coordinate weights" of

(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

A variant of this construction is obtained by fixing a weight
\theta\in\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}
and considering the graded algebra

\oplus_{j=1}^{\infty}S_{j\theta},

where
S_{j\theta}
is the linear span of the monomials in
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{n}]
of weight
j\theta
. The corresponding variety
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
is smooth for generic
\theta
, and projective over
{X}(\mathfrak{A},0)
,
which means, in particular, that there is a canonical proper map

\varphi_{\theta}:X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)\to X(\mathfrak{A},0).

We note that for every
\eta\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{A}
,
there is a well defined line bundle
L_{\eta}
on
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
for each
\theta
, and the line bundle

L_{\theta}\to X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
is the polarization for the projective map
\phi_{\theta}
.

We note that this variety appears in various contexts, in particular as a quiver variety
[
N
]
,
and plays an
important role in the our calculation of the intersection cohomology of the
moduli spaces of semistable bundles
[
MR
,
MozR
]
.

The quotient variety
X
is usually very singular, and the topological
invariants most adapted to this situation are the intersection cohomology
groups
IH^{*}(X)
. The central problem we address in this paper, is
the calculation of the associated Poincaré polynomial

g(k,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{[d/2]}g_{i}t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{[d/2]}\dim IH^{2i}(X)\cdot t^{i},

where
d=n-k=k(k-2)
.

A related problem is the study of the topology of the projective toric variety

\widehat{X}
,
associated to
\mathfrak{A}
as follows. Let
\kappa:\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the tautological
weight of the group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, set
\hat{T}=T\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and consider
the weight data
\widehat{\mathfrak{A}}=[\alpha+\kappa|\,\alpha\in\mathfrak{A}]
for the group
\hat{T}
.
Then we set

, and note that

is the cone over

associated to the line bundle

, i.e. it may be obtained as the

L_{\kappa}
with the zero-section collapsed to a point.

The properties of intersection cohomology imply that

satisfies Poincaré duality,
and thus the corresponding Poincaré polynomial

is palindromic.

Our notation for these polynomials is motivated by the
g
-and-
h
-polynomial calculus of Stanley

[
St
]
, of which our polynomials are examples. This, in particular,
implies that

(1)

g_{i}=h_{i}-h_{i-1}\text{ for }0\leq i\leq[d/2].

2.1. The quotient construction

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{R}\omega_{i}
be a real vector space with a fixed ordered basis, and let

(2)

0\to\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{B}\mathfrak{t}\to 0

be an exact sequence of finite dimensional real vector spaces of dimensions
n-d,n
and
d
. We denote by
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\omega_{i}
the lattice in
\mathfrak{g}
, and assume that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}
intersects
\mathfrak{a}
in a lattice
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}
of full rank; we denote the image
B(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}})
in
\mathfrak{t}
by
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}
. The sequence (
2
) restricted to the lattices is also exact, as well as the dual sequence

0\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}\xrightarrow{A}\mathfrak{a}^{*}\to 0

restricted to the dual lattices
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
.
Denoting the dual basis by
\{\omega^{i}\}
, we have
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{R}\omega^{i}
and
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\omega^{i}
.

We introduce the notation
\alpha_{i}
for the vector
A(\omega^{i})\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
and consider the sequence
\mathfrak{A}=[\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}
]. Note that according to our assumptions, the elements of

\mathfrak{A}
generate
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
over
\mathbb{Z}
.

The complexified torus
T_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
diagonally with weights
(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
. We will be interested in the quotients of this action in the sense of geometric invariant theory. Let

S=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{n}],\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{deg}(x_{i})=\alpha_{i}\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}

be the ring of polynomials graded by the
semigroup
\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}\subset\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
. For
\theta\in\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}
, we denote by
S_{\theta}
the vector space of homogeneous
polynomials in
S
of degree
\theta
. Then
S_{0}
is a finitely generated
subalgebra of homogeneous degree zero polynomials and
S_{\theta}
is a module
over
S_{0}
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}
.

We define the affine toric variety
X(\mathfrak{A},0)
as the affine GIT quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by the torus
T_{\mathfrak{a}}
action:

X(\mathfrak{A},0):=\mathbb{C}^{n}\!\sslash^{0}T_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathrm{Spec}(S_{0}).

For any
\theta\in\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}
, we define the toric variety
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
as the relative projective GIT quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by the
T_{\mathfrak{a}}
-action:

X(\mathfrak{A},\theta):=\mathbb{C}^{n}\sslash^{\theta}{T_{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\mathrm{Proj}(S_{(\theta)}),

where
S_{(\theta)}
is the finitely generated
S_{0}
-algebra
\oplus_{r}t^{r}S_{r\theta}
, which is
\mathbb{N}
-graded by the degree of
t
.

2.2. Gale duality and toric fans

Recall that any toric variety
X
may be associated to a fan
\Sigma
in the lattice
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}\subset\mathfrak{t}
in such a way that each cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
corresponds to an affine subset of
X
(cf.
[
Fulton
, §1.4]
).
In this section we describe the fan which corresponds to the toric variety
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
defined above (for the result, see Proposition
2.2
).

2.2.1. Chambers in the
\mathfrak{A}
-picture

We begin with the definition of some basic concepts related to our weight
sequence
\mathfrak{A}=[\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}]
.

•

For a set or sequence
S
of vectors in a real vector space, denote by

\mathrm{Cone}(S)
the closed cone spanned by the elements of
S
.
By convention, the cone over the empty set is the origin of the vector space.

•

Denote by
\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A})
the set of
basis index
sets
, i.e. the set of those subsets
I\subset\{1,...,n\}
for which the
set
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. We will use the
notation
\alpha^{I}\subset\mathfrak{A}
for the basis associated to
I\in\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A})
.

•

Denote by
\partial\mathfrak{A}
the union of the boundaries of the
simplicial cones spanned by elements of
\mathfrak{A}
:

\partial\mathfrak{A}=\cup\{\partial\mathrm{Cone}(\alpha^{I})|\,I\in\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A})\}.

A connected component of the open set
\mathrm{Cone}(\mathfrak{A})\setminus\partial\mathfrak{A}

in
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
is called a
chamber
, and the set of chambers will be denoted by
Ch(\mathfrak{A})
.

•

We will call
\theta\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}

generic
, if it
lies in one of the chambers of
\mathfrak{A}
.

•

For a chamber
\mathfrak{c}\in Ch(\mathfrak{A})
, we define
\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{c})
to be the set of those
I\in\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A})
for which
\mathrm{Cone}(\alpha^{I})\supset\mathfrak{c}
.

The sequence
\mathfrak{B}=[\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}]
, where be

\beta_{i}=B(\omega_{i})
in
\mathfrak{t}
(cf. (
2
)) is called the

Gale dual
of the sequence
\mathfrak{A}
. This notion is involutive, i.e. the
Gale dual of the sequence
\mathfrak{B}
is
\mathfrak{A}
.

2.2.2. Gale duality and fans

A
fan

\Sigma
on
\mathfrak{B}
is a finite collection of cones of the form
\sigma=\mathrm{Cone}(\beta^{I})
,
I\subset\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, satisfying some additional properties (cf.
[
Fulton
, §1.4]
); in particular, the union of
{\sigma\in\Sigma}
is the cone
\mathrm{Cone}(\mathfrak{B})
. A fan is

if all of its cones are simplicial; further, it is
unimodular
if every maximal cone of
\Sigma
is spanned by a basis of
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}
.

There is a standard construction of toric varieties
\Sigma\mapsto X(\Sigma)

from fans in the lattice
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}
(cf.
[
Fulton
, §1.4]
). The
toric variety
X(\Sigma)
is a
toric orbifold
, i.e. has only finite
quotient singularities, if and only if
\Sigma
is simplicial. In the
unimodular case, simplicial fans give rise to smooth toric varieties.

The torus
T_{\mathfrak{t}}=\mathrm{Hom}(\Gamma_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*},\mathbb{C}^{*})
is embedded in
X(\Sigma)
as an open subset, and its
standard action on itself extends to an action on
X(\Sigma)
.

Notation:
The orbits of the
T_{\mathfrak{t}}
-action on

are in bijection with the cones
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Given

\sigma\in\Sigma
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\sigma)
the corresponding orbit,
and by

the orbit closure of
\mathcal{O}(\sigma)
. Note that given
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
,

\sigma\subset\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if
V({\sigma^{\prime}})\subset V(\sigma)
and

\mathrm{dim}(\sigma)=\mathrm{codim}(V(\sigma)\subset X(\Sigma))
.

Next, we say that the fan
\Sigma_{1}
is a
refinement
of
\Sigma_{2}
if for every cone
\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
there exists a cone
\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}
such that
\sigma_{1}\subset\sigma_{2}
. In this case, we can define a map
\widehat{\psi}_{\Sigma}:\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
by setting
\widehat{\psi}_{\Sigma}(\sigma_{1})
to be the smallest cone in
\Sigma_{2}
that contains
\sigma_{1}
, and this induces a so-called

morphism
\psi:X(\Sigma_{1})\to X(\Sigma_{2})
.

The following lemma describes the fundamental relation between the Gale dual configurations
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

A linear combination
\sum_{i}m_{i}\alpha_{i}
vanishes if and only if there is a linear functional

such that
l(\beta_{i})=m_{i}
.

This relationship is instrumental in the proof of the following important statement.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a sequence in
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and let
\mathfrak{c}
be a chamber in

.

(1)

If

, then its complement

is an element of

, i.e. the set

is a basis of

.

(2)

The set of simplicial cones

.

(3)

Let

, then the GIT quotient

.

(4)

The affine toric variety

.

Remark 2.3
.

Recall that for every scheme

there is a canonical morphism

to the affine scheme

2.2.3. Polar polytopes

Finally, we note that there is a simple way to associate a fan, and thus a

Let

be a

Then we denote by

the fan whose cones are the cones over the proper

(here, we include the empty face). Following the notation from above, we denote by

the associated toric variety. If the center of mass of

is not at the origin, then we replace the polytope

by its shifted copy

.

More generally, if

is a subdivision of the boundary of

i.e.

is a collection of convex polytopes whose union is the

, and the intersection of two polytopes in

(cf. Figure

), then the cones over

form a fan, which we will also denote by

Then

, which induces

Note that while

3.  Intersection cohomology

In this section, we collect a few basic facts about small morphisms and the
intersection cohomology of toric varieties, needed in our paper.

3.1. Small maps

Let
\widetilde{Y}
be a connected nonsingular
variety, and
\psi:\widetilde{Y}\to Y
be a proper surjective map onto a
variety
Y
of the same dimension.
A
stratification
for
\psi
is a decomposition of
Y
into finitely many
locally closed nonsingular subsets
Y=\bigsqcup_{k=0}^{n}S_{k}
such that

\psi^{-1}(S_{k})\to S_{k}
is a topologically locally trivial fibration. The subsets

S_{k}
are called
strata
.

Denote by
d_{k}:=\mathrm{dim}(\psi^{-1}(y_{k}))
the dimension of the fiber of
\psi

over any point
y_{k}\in S_{k}
. The map
\psi
is called
small
, if

(3)

\mathrm{codim}(S_{k})=\mathrm{dim}(Y)-\mathrm{dim}(S_{k})>2d_{k}

for every nondense stratum
S_{k}
for the map
\psi
.

Small maps play an important role in the calculation of the

intersection cohomology
groups of singular varieties
[
GM
]
.
In general, the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(Y)
of an irreducible complex
projective
d
-dimensional variety
Y
is a module over the singular
cohomology ring
H^{*}(Y)
, and satisfies Poincaré duality and the Hard
Lefschetz theorem
[
BBD
, Theorem 5.4.10]
. The latter means that for some
element
\omega\in H^{2}(Y)
(the class of a hyperplane section) and for
0\leq k\leq d
the multiplication by
\omega^{d-k}
mapping
IH^{k}(Y)\to IH^{2d-k}(Y))
is an isomorphism of vector spaces. One can define then, for

0\leq k\leq d
, the
primitive intersection cohomology
of
Y

as

IH_{\mathrm{prim}}^{k}(Y)=IH^{2k}(Y)/\omega\,IH^{2k-2}(Y).

The intersection cohomology of toric varieties is intimately related to the
theory
of convex polytopes
[
deCM
,
St
]
. We will now briefly review this relation.

3.2. Intersection cohomology of toric varieties

Let
P
be a simplicial
d
-dimensional polytope, and denote by
f_{i}
the
number of its
i
-dimensional faces. One associates to
P
its

f
-polynomial

f(P,t)=t^{d}+f_{0}t^{d-1}+f_{1}t^{d-2}+...+f_{d-2}t+f_{d-1},

its

h
-polynomial

(4)

h(P,t)=h_{d}t^{d}+...+h_{1}t+h_{0}:=f(P,t-1),

and its
g
-polynomial

(5)

g(P,t)=h_{0}+(h_{1}-h_{0})t+(h_{2}-h_{1})t^{2}+...+(h_{[d/2]}-h_{[d/2]-1})t^{[d/2]}.

The following theorem calculates the dimension of the intersection cohomology groups of a toric orbifold.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
Fulton
, §5.2]

Let
P
be a simplicial
d
-dimensional rational polytope with
h
-polynomial
h(P,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}h_{k}t^{k}
, and let
X(\Sigma_{P})
be the associated toric orbifold. Then

(6)

\mathrm{dim}H^{2k}(X(\Sigma_{P}))=h_{k}\text{\,\,\, and \,\,\,}H^{2k+1}(X(\Sigma_{P}))=0\text{\,\,\, for \,\,\,}0\leq k\leq d.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that it follows from (
6
) and the definition
(
5
), that the coefficient
h_{k}-h_{k+1}
is the dimension of the
primitive cohomology of the toric variety
X(\Sigma_{P})
in degree
k
, and
thus, in particular, it is non-negative.

When the polytope
P
is not simplicial, the corresponding toric variety

X(\Sigma_{P})
has not only finite quotient singularities. In this case, the
ordinary cohomology groups is not a purely combinatorial invariant, but depends

\displaystyle{2\max_{G\in\mathcal{G}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}_{F}}}\{e(G)\}-e(G_{F})\leq e(\mathbb{K}_{k})=2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}r_{ij},

is a disjoint union of complete graphs. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle{2\max_{G\in\mathcal{G}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}_{F}}}\{e(G)\}-e(G_{F})-s(G_{F})+1\leq 2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}r_{ij}-s(G_{F})+1\leq 2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}r_{ij},

with equality if and only if

is a disjoint union of complete graphs and

. In this latter case,

is connected and complete, and thus

. This corresponds to the empty face

with

, and Theorem

follows.

Now we can finish the proof of Theorem

Since the map

By Lemma

,
\mathcal{G}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}_{\Pi}
is the set of acyclic subgraphs

Proposition 4.12
.

Let

. For any

Proof.

First, we perform a change of variables

in the recursion; by Theorem
4.2
, the polynomial

counts the number of edges in acyclic subgraphs of

with

j\in\{2,3,...,k\}
that has no in-edges.

Now fix a nonempty subset

. We claim that

g(\Pi(r_{ij\in\overline{J}}),t+1)\cdot\prod_{j\in J}p\big(\sum_{i\in\overline{J}}r_{ij},t+1\big)=\sum_{G\in\mathbb{G}^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}}t^{e(G)-k+1},

where the sum is taken over the set
\mathbb{G}^{{}^{\prime}}_{k}
of the connected acyclic subgraphs

with
k

that the vertices
j\in J
of
G
has no in-edges.

Indeed, let
G
be a graph satisfying these conditions.

•

A simple calculation shows that the polynomial

counts all out-edges of
G
from the vertex
j\in J
.

•

We denote by
G\setminus J
the graph obtained from

j\in J

\overline{J}

•

Thus it follows from Theorem

•

Noting that any edge of

Now the recursion follows from the inclusion-exclusion principle.
∎

6.1. Other chambers

It turns out that we could have carried out the arguments of
§
4.2
-§
4.4
for any chamber in the chamber complex

Ch(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}))
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\theta
be a generic point in
\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})
.
Then the morphism of toric varieties

\varphi_{\theta}:X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),\theta)\to X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0)

is small.

Proof.

The proof follows the logic of the proof of Theorem
4.4
. We
repeat the argument with only minor changes, thus omitting the details.

As explained in the proof of Lemma
4.6
, each cone

\mathrm{Cone}(\alpha^{I})
for a basis index set

I\in\mathrm{BInd}(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),\mathfrak{c})
correspond to a
spanning tree in
\mathbb{K}_{k}
; we introduce the notation

\mathrm{Trees}(\theta)
for the set of these trees.

Let
F
be a face of
\Pi
, and let
G_{F}
be the corresponding naked graph
(cf. Lemma
4.5
).
Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{\theta}_{F}
the set of connected subgraphs of

\mathbb{K}_{k}
with
k
vertices that have the same directed cycles as the
naked graph
G_{F}
and that contain at leas one tree from

\mathrm{Trees}(\theta)
.
Then (cf. Theorem
4.9
and Lemma
4.10
) for any
point
y
in
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Cone}(F))\subset X
the Poincaré polynomial of
the fiber
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(y)
is equal to

\sum_{l\geq 0}d_{l}(F)(t^{2}-1)^{l},

where
d_{l}(F)
is the number of graphs in
\mathcal{G}^{\theta}_{F}
with

e(G_{F})+s(G_{F})-1+l
edges.

Repeating the dimension estimates from the proof of Theorem
4.4
,
we arrive at our statement.
∎

6.2. A ring structure on the intersection cohomology

Applying Theorem
3.5
to the small morphism
\varphi_{\theta}
from
Proposition
6.1
, we obtain the following statement.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\theta
and
\varphi_{\theta}
be as in Proposition
6.1
.
There is an isomorphism

\psi_{\theta}:H^{*}(\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0))\xrightarrow{\sim}IH^{*}(X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0))

between the cohomology groups of the fiber
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
and the
intersection cohomology groups of the affine cone
X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0)
.
The isomorphism
\psi_{\theta}
induces a ring structure on the intersection
cohomology of
X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0)
.

The cohomology rings
H^{*}(\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0))
were studied in
[
HSt
]
.
In particular, it was shown that they do not depend on the choice of the
elements
\theta\in\mathfrak{a}_{k}^{*}
(cf. Theorem
6.3
), and thus the
induced ring structure on the intersection cohomology of

X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0)
is canonical.

Theorem 6.3
.

[
HSt
, Theorem 8.3]

Let
\theta
be a generic point in
\mathbb{N}\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})
.
The cohomology ring of the fiber
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
is canonically
isomorphic to the quotient of

\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j},1\leq i<j\leq k]
by the ideal
generated by the polynomials

(20)

p_{D}(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in D_{1},j\in D_{2}\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})^{r_{ij}},

where
D=D_{1}\sqcup D_{2}
runs
over all nontrivial partitions of the set
\{1,2,...,k\}
.

In the remainder of this section, we explain how these relations appear in our
graphical formalism.

We begin by describing the reducible variety
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
.
We introduce the notation
\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
for the subset of graphs in

\mathcal{G}^{\theta}_{\Pi}
with maximal number of edges, which is equal to

\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}
. Using Lemma

4.5
, we can reformulate Lemma 2.1.11 from
[
HLY
]
in terms of
graphs in the following form.

Proposition 6.4
.

(i) The fiber of
\varphi_{\theta}
over a point
y_{\Pi}\in X(\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k}),0)
is a connected reducible variety,
whose irreducible components are toric varieties parametrized by the
elements in
\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
.

(ii) Let
G\in\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
, and denote the sequence of its edges
by
\mathcal{A}_{G}\subset\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})
; then the irreducible
component of
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
associated to
G
is the toric
variety
X(\mathcal{A}_{G},\theta)
, which has dimension

\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}-k+1
.

(iii) For
G_{1},...,G_{m}\in\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
, denote by
G_{1}\cap...\cap G_{m}
the graph obtained by taking all common edges of
G_{1},...,G_{m}
. Then
the
irreducible components

X(\mathcal{A}_{G_{1}},\theta),...,X(\mathcal{A}_{G_{m}},\theta)
are glued
along the toric variety
X(\mathcal{A}_{G_{1}\cap...\cap G_{m}},\theta)
.

Remark 6.5
.

Unlike their cohomology rings, the varieties
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0)
are
not necesserely isomorphic for different
\theta
s. For example, let

\theta_{1}=(3,-1,-1,-1)\text{\,\,\, and \,\,\, }\theta_{\mathrm{rand}}=(2,-3,2,-1)
be two points in the chamber complex
Ch(\mathfrak{A}(1,1,1,1,1,1))
. A
simple calculation shows that
\varphi_{\theta_{1}}^{-1}(0)
has 6
irreducible components that have Poincaré polynomials
1+2t^{2}+2t^{4}+t^{6}
,
while
\varphi_{\theta_{\mathrm{rand}}}^{-1}(0)
has 6 irreducible
components, two of which are isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

To describe the ring structure on
H^{*}(\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}(0))
, we need one
more ingredient: a general statement about relations in the cohomology
ring of compact toric varieties. These are well-known; we will present them in
the formalism of
[
SzV
]
. We will use the notation of
§
2.1
-§
2.2
.

Let
\theta
be a weight in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
. By the Chern-Weil
construction, every polynomial on
\mathfrak{a}
gives rise to a characteristic
class of the toric variety
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
. Thus there is a Chern-Weil
homomorphism

\chi:\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})\to H^{*}(X(\mathfrak{A},\theta))
from the polynomials
on
\mathfrak{a}
to the cohomology of
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
. In particular, for any
vector
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
,
\chi(\alpha)
is an element of

H^{2}(X(\mathfrak{A},\theta))
.

Now let
\mathfrak{A}
be a sequence which lies in an open half space
of the vector space
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, and assume that
\theta
is generic.
Then
X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
is a projective orbifold, and for every
polynomial
Q\in\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{a}^{*})
, one can write explicit formulas
for the intersection numbers
\int_{X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)}\chi(Q)
using the
Jeffrey-Kirwan residue
[
BrV
,
SzV
]
. In this paper, we will only need to
integrate some particularly simple classes, described below.

Proposition 6.6
.

[
SzV
, Proposition 2.3]

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a sequence which lies in an open half space
of
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, and let

\theta
be a generic point in
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
.
Then, for any set of indices
J\subset\{1,...,n\}^{n-d}
we have

\int\limits_{X(\mathfrak{A},\theta)}\prod_{j\in J}\chi(\alpha_{j})=0,

if the
complement of
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\in J}
in
\mathfrak{A}
does not span

\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

Now we apply this statement to our situation.
Let
\theta\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}^{*}
be generic,

G\in\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{A}_{G}\subset\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})

be as in Proposition
6.4
. Given a nontrivial partition
D=D_{1}\sqcup D_{2}
of the set
\{1,2,...,k\}
, denote by
\mathcal{A}[{D}]
the subsequence

[\alpha_{ij}]_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in D_{1},j\in D_{2}\end{subarray}}\subset\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})
,
where the element
\alpha_{ij}\in\mathcal{A}[{D}]
is repeated
r_{ij}
times.

Clearly, the sequence
\mathcal{A}_{G}\cap((\mathfrak{A}(r_{12},...,r_{k-1k})\setminus\mathcal{A}[{D}])

corresponds to a subgraph of
\mathbb{K}_{k}
, which doest not contain any tree
on
k
vertices. Then it follows from Proposition
6.6
that the
product

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in D_{1}\\
j\in D_{2}\end{subarray}}\chi(\alpha_{ij})^{r_{ij}}\in H^{*}(X(\mathcal{A}_{G},\theta))

is zero for any
G\in\mathrm{Top}(\theta)
, hence it is equal to zero in
H^{*}(\varphi^{-1}_{\theta}(0))
. We have thus
reproduced the Hausel-Sturmfels relations given in (
20
).

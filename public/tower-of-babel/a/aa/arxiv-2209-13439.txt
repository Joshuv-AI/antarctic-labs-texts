The volume conjecture for polyhedra implies the Stoker conjecture
Giulio Belletti

Abstract
We show that the Volume Conjecture for polyhedra implies a weak version of the Stoker Conjecture; in turn we prove that this weak version of the Stoker conjecture implies the Stoker conjecture. The main tool used is an extension of a result of Montcouquiol and Weiss, saying that dihedral angles are local coordinates for compact polyhedra with angles ≤ π \leq\pi .



1
Introduction

To what extent dihedral angles determine a hyperbolic polyhedron is a long standing question, first posed in
[
Sto68
, Page 152]
.

Conjecture

(The Stoker conjecture)
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two hyperbolic polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and the same dihedral angle at each corresponding edge. Then
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are isometric.

Over the next half century there have been several results in this direction: as soon as some conditions are required of the polyhedra (for example, acute-angled in
[
And70
]
, simple in
[
RH93
]
or hyperideal in
[
BB02
]
) then the conjecture is known to be true. Moreover
[
Mon13
]
and
[
Wei13
]
independently proved that the conjecture is true locally, i.e. the dihedral angles are local coordinates for the space of polyhedra with fixed
1
-skeleton.

In this paper, we propose a weak version of the Stoker conjecture:

Conjecture

(Weak Stoker conjecture)
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and the same dihedral angle at each corresponding edge. Then
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have the same volume.

Clearly the Stoker conjecture implies the weak Stoker Conjecture; in Corollary
4.4
we prove that in fact the converse also holds.

The interest of the weak Stoker Conjecture is two-fold: firstly, it could be a more tractable problem than the Stoker conjecture; secondly, it is actually a consequence of the Volume Conjecture for polyhedra, providing a connection between a long standing open problem in hyperbolic geometry and quantum invariants.

Since the introduction of quantum invariants in
[
Wit89
]
, many conjectures have been proposed to link their asymptotic behavior to several topological and geometric properties. Among them the subject of the most research has been the plethora of volume conjectures, relating the growth of quantum invariants of objects to the volume of hyperbolic structures on them. The first such conjecture was proposed by Kashaev (
[
Kas97
]
, see also
[
MM01
]
) relating the growth of the Jones polynomial of a knot evaluated at a certain root of unity to the volume of the complement of the knot. More recently, Chen and Yang
[
CY18
]
proposed a volume conjecture for the Turaev-Viro invariant of compact manifolds. Both of these conjectures have extensions relating higher order terms of the asymptotic expansion of a quantum invariant to different quantities such as the Reidemeister torsion. These conjectures have been proven for certain families of knots or manifolds (see for example
[
Oht18
]
and
[
BDKY22
]
for results about the Volume Conjecture of Chen and Yang, and
[
Oht16
]
,
[
OY18
]
,
[
Oht17
]
for results on the Kashaev volume conjecture).

A related series of conjectures connects the quantum invariants of a planar graph
\Gamma
in
S^{3}
to the volume of hyperbolic polyhedra that have
\Gamma
as a
1
-skeleton. The first such conjecture was given in
[
CGvdV15
]
for trivalent planar graphs and simple hyperideal polyhedra; it was extended in
[
KM18
]
and later in
[
Bel20
]
to any polyhedral (i.e. planar and
3
-connected) graph and any hyperbolic polyhedron.

Conjecture

(The volume conjecture for polyhedra)
.

Let
P
be a hyperbolic polyhedron with dihedral angles

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
at the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
, and
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
. Let
col_{r}
be a sequence of
r
-admissible colorings of the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
of
\Gamma
such that

2\pi\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{col_{r}(e_{i})}{r}=\pi-\alpha_{i}.

Then

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\pi}{r}\log\left\lvert Y_{r}(S^{3},\Gamma,col_{r},e^{2\pi i/r})\right\rvert=\mathrm{Vol}(P).

where
Y_{r}\left(S^{3},\Gamma,col,e^{2\pi i/r}\right)
is the Yokota invariant of the graph
\Gamma
with edges colored with
col_{r}
evaluated at
e^{2\pi i/r}
(see Section
3
and
[
Bel20
, Section 2]
for more details and definitions).

In Section
2
we give an overview of hyperbolic polyhedra and we prove the technical results about the space of hyperbolic polyhedra with fixed
1
-skeleton that are needed in the proof of the main result. In Section
3
we give a brief review about the Yokota invariant and the Volume conjecture for polyhedra. Finally in Section
4
we prove the equivalence of the Weak Stoker Conjecture and the Stoker Conjecture. Quantum invariants are only involved in Section
3
; a reader only interested in the geometric results of this paper can safely skip it if so desired.

2
Hyperbolic polyhedra

Definition 2.1
.

A
hyperbolic polyhedron
is a compact intersection of half spaces of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Throughout the literature, other types of hyperbolic polyhedra have also been considered, for example polyhedra with ideal or hyperideal vertices. The results of this paper can be easily extended to
proper generalized hyperbolic polyhedra
(see
[
Bel21
]
) with no ideal vertices, by simply truncating them to obtain a hyperbolic polyhedron of Definition
2.1
. However the techniques here presented cannot be directly applied to the case of polyhedra with some ideal vertices, since it is unknown in this case whether angles are local coordinates; the Stoker conjecture for polyhedra with only ideal vertices is known to be true by
[
Riv96
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

If
\Gamma
is a graph we say that a polyhedron
P
has
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
if there exists a simplicial map
\phi:\Gamma\rightarrow P
sending vertices to vertices injectively and edges to edges.

Remark 2.4
.

A graph
\Gamma
is the
1
-skeleton of a polyhedron if and only if it is planar and
3
-connected (that is to say, it has more than three vertices and cannot be disconnected by removing two edges)
[
Ste22
]
. Such a graph admits a unique embedding in

up to planar isotopy and reflections
[

, Corollary 3.4]
; therefore, even though
\Gamma
is just an abstract graph, it is convenient to treat it as an embedded graph in the sphere. As such, we will often speak of its faces, meaning the faces of the cellularization of
S^{2}
induced by an embedding.

Conjecture 1

(The Stoker conjecture)
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two polyhedra with the same

-skeleton

of
\Gamma
, the dihedral angles of

and

at their corresponding edge are equal, then

and

Conjecture 2

(A weak version of the Stoker conjecture)
.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two polyhedra with the same
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
. If for every edge
e
of
\Gamma
, the dihedral angles of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
at their corresponding edge are equal, then
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have the same volume.

Theorem 2.5
.

Conjecture
1
is equivalent to Conjecture
2
.

Theorem 2.6
.

If Conjecture
3
is true, then the Stoker conjecture is true.

To prove Theorem
2.6
we will need to take a polyhedron

with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
, choose a diagonal

of a non-triangular face, and deform

to make

into an edge. To do this, it is convenient to treat

as a polyhedron with
1
-skeleton equal to

-skeleta.

Definition 2.7
.

Let

be an edge. A polyhedron

has

1
-skeleton

(\Gamma,e)
if there exists a simplicial map
\phi:\Gamma\rightarrow P
sending vertices to vertices injectively, edges different from

to edges of
P
and the edge

to a face of

.

Put in other terms, in a polyhedron with weak
1
-skeleton
(\Gamma,e)
we allow the two faces intersecting in

to lie in the same plane. If this happens, the
1
-skeleton of
P
is actually the graph obtained from
\Gamma
by removing the edge
e
; otherwise, the
1

P
is simply
\Gamma
. Notice that if the
1
-skeleton of

is
\Gamma
, then
P
has weak skeleton
(\Gamma,e)
for any edge

.

Definition 2.8
.

The space of polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
is denoted
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
. The space of polyhedra with weak
1
-skeleton
(\Gamma,e)
is denoted
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
.

Remark 2.9
.

A polyhedron with
F
faces is an intersection of
F
half spaces. The set
\mathcal{H}
of half spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is naturally a manifold; thus both
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
are naturally subsets of the manifold
\mathcal{H}^{F}
. Thus they are topological spaces; we will prove next that they are actually smooth manifolds.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be any
3
-connected planar graph and
\Gamma
be the graph obtained from
\Gamma^{\prime}
by adding an edge
e
to a non-triangular face of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

The space
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
is a manifold; the space
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
is a manifold with boundary whose interior is
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
and whose boundary is
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The proof for
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
is in
[
Mon13
, Proposition 17]
; however since the proof for
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
is adapted from it we briefly sketch it.
The space
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
is an open set of the space of Euclidean polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
(namely, the Euclidean polyhedra with every vertex contained in the open unit ball); therefore if we prove that the latter is a manifold we are done.

Denote with
F=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
the set of faces of
\Gamma
, and with
\mathcal{H}
the set of half spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
is a subset of
\mathcal{H}^{F}
; the inclusion map sends
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
to the
F
-tuple
\left(\Pi_{f_{1}},\dots,\Pi_{f_{n}}\right)
of half-spaces supporting the faces of
P
.

The image of this inclusion is characterized as the tuples
\left(\Pi_{f_{1}},\dots,\Pi_{f_{n}}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{F}
such that:

1.

for every
v\in\Gamma
lying on the faces
f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{k}}
, the corresponding half planes
\Pi_{f_{i_{1}}},\dots,\Pi_{f_{i_{k}}}
intersect in a single point
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

2.

if
j\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
, then
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
is in the interior of
\Pi_{j}
.

The first condition is open if
v
is a trivalent vertex and closed otherwise.
The second condition is clearly open; in particular it implies that the intersection defining a polyhedron must be non-redundant (i.e. if we remove any half space, the polyhedron changes) and that the intersection of half-planes around different vertices are different points of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

These conditions can also be described by equations. The first condition is equivalent to the set of equations

\Psi_{v,j}=\mathrm{det}\left(\partial\Pi_{i_{j}}^{*},\partial\Pi_{i_{j+1}}^{*},\partial\Pi_{i_{j+2}}^{*},\partial\Pi_{i_{j+3}}^{*}\right)=0

for
j\in\{1,\dots,k-3\}
. Put in other words, this equation asks that the dual vectors to the boundaries of
\Pi_{i_{j}},\dots,\Pi_{i_{j+3}}
must be coplanar, which is equivalent to asking that
\partial\Pi_{i_{j}},\dots,\partial\Pi_{i_{j+3}}
intersect in a single point. In
[
Mon13
, Proposition 17]
it is proven that these conditions are linearly independent.

To describe the second condition as an equation we define the
signed distance
of
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
from a half space
H
, denoted
\delta(v,H)
, as the real number having absolute value the Euclidean distance from
v
to
\partial H
, positive sign if
v\in H
and negative sign otherwise. Then condition
2
is

\delta(v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}},\Pi_{j})>0

for all
j\notin{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
. Since these conditions are open, then the space
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
is a manifold.

Consider now the polyhedra with weak
1
-skeleton
(\Gamma,e)
, and suppose (after reindexing if necessary) that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are the two faces of
\Gamma
adjacent to
e
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
is a subset of
\mathcal{H}^{F}
as before, and the tuples
\left(\Pi_{f_{1}},\dots,\Pi_{f_{n}}\right)\in\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
are characterized by the following conditions:

1.

for every
v\in\Gamma
lying on the faces
f_{i_{1}},\dots,f_{i_{k}}
, the corresponding half planes
\Pi_{f_{i_{1}}},\dots,\Pi_{f_{i_{k}}}
intersect in a single point
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
;

2.

if
j\notin\{1,2,i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
, then
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
is in the interior of
\Pi_{j}
;

3.

if
j=1\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and
2\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
or viceversa, then
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
is in the interior of
\Pi_{j}
;

4.

if
j=1\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and
2\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
or viceversa, then
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
belongs to
\Pi_{j}
.

The first and fourth conditions are closed while the second and third are open. We can describe these conditions as equations in a similar manner as before; the first conditions carry over without change. The second condition becomes

\delta(v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}},\Pi_{j})>0

for all
j\notin{1,2,i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
; the third condition is analogous. The fourth condition is instead

\delta(v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}},\Pi_{j})\geq 0

if
j=1\notin\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and
2\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
or viceversa.

To prove that
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
is a manifold, we need to show that the equations of condition
1
and the equalities in condition
4
are independent.

Now notice that if
\delta(v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}},\Pi_{1})=0
, then
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
belongs to
\partial\Pi_{1}
. This can only happen if
2\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
by the aforementioned conditions. Since
\partial\Pi_{1}
and
\partial\Pi_{2}
share three distinct points (the two vertices that are endpoints of
e
and
v_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
) they must coincide; therefore this condition is equivalent to
\Pi_{1}=\Pi_{2}
.

Suppose then that there is a linear dependence for the equations
\Psi_{v,i}=0
and
\Pi_{1}=\Pi_{2}
; this would imply that there is a linear dependence for the analogous system of equations in
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
which is false.

Therefore
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}^{e}
is a smooth manifold whose boundary is
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
and whose interior is
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}
.

∎

Corollary 2.11
.

The space
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
is a manifold; the space

is a manifold with boundary whose interior is
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma}
and whose boundary is
\mathcal{A}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}

We need to show that the group
\textrm{Iso}(\mathbb{H}^{3})

∎

3
The volume conjecture for hyperbolic polyhedra

In this section we briefly talk about the Yokota invariant and the Volume Conjecture for polyhedra. More details about these topics can be found in
[
Bel20
]
.

Take a graph
\Gamma
contained in a manifold
M
, an odd integer
r>2
, a primitive
r
-th root of unity
q
and a coloring
col
of the edges of
\Gamma
with natural even numbers less than
r-2
; then the Yokota invariant
Y_{r}(M,\Gamma,col,q)
is defined in
[
Yok96
]
as a direct generalization of the Kauffman skein bracket of a trivalent graph.

The simplest case to which these invariants apply is that of a planar graph in
S^{3}
; in this case, if the graph is
3
-connected (that is to say, it cannot be disconnected by removing
2
edges and has more than
3
vertices) then it is the
1
-skeleton of a hyperbolic polyhedron. The Volume Conjecture for polyhedra relates the growth of the invariants
Y_{r}\left(S^{3},\Gamma,col,e^{2\pi i/r}\right)
(as
r\rightarrow\infty
) to the volume of a hyperbolic polyhedron with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and dihedral angles determined by
col
in a certain way.

A conjecture relating the growth of the quantum
6j
-symbol to the volume of hyperbolic tetrahedra first appeared in
[
Cos07
]
. Later, various generalizations to the case of trivalent graphs and simple polyhedra were proposed in
[
CGvdV15
]
and
[
KM18
]
; the following version of the conjecture first appeared in
[
Bel20
]
as a direct generalization of these conjectures to non-simple polyhedra.

Conjecture 3

(The volume conjecture for polyhedra)
.

Let
P
be a proper polyhedron with dihedral angles

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}
at the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
, and
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
. Let
col_{r}
be a sequence of
r
-admissible colorings of the edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
of
\Gamma
such that

2\pi\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{col_{r}(e_{i})}{r}=\pi-\alpha_{i}.

Then

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\pi}{r}\log\left\lvert Y_{r}(S^{3},\Gamma,col_{r},e^{2\pi i/r})\right\rvert=\mathrm{Vol}(P).

Remark 3.1
.

Conjecture
3
is stated for proper generalized hyperbolic polyhedra, that is to say, polyhedra with possibly hyperideal vertices and whose truncations are all right-angled; for the terminology see
[
Bel21
]
. In the present paper we are only concerned with hyperbolic polyhedra as in Definition
2.1
, i.e. compact polyhedra. Therefore, it is safe to mentally substitute “proper” for “hyperbolic” in the statement of Conjecture
3
(and any subsequent mention of the term).

Conjecture
3
holds (see
[
CM
]
) for any polyhedron obtained by gluing tetrahedra with at least one hyperideal vertex along some of their truncation faces (in particular, some of these polyhedra are compact but all of them are simple). It was also proved for another, larger family of non-simple polyhedra in
[
Bel20
]
(albeit for a single sequence of colors each). Further numerical evidence for it appears in the appendix of
[
Bel20
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If Conjecture
3
is true, then the weak Stoker Conjecture is true.

Proof.

If
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have the same
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and the same dihedral angles
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
, then we can choose a sequence of colorings
col_{r}
satisfying condition
3
. Then the Volume Conjecture implies that

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\pi}{r}\log\left\lvert Y_{r}(\Gamma,col_{r})\right\rvert=\mathrm{Vol}(P_{1})=\mathrm{Vol}(P_{2}).

∎

4
The weak Stoker conjecture implies the Stoker conjecture

We now proceed to show that the weak version of the Stoker conjecture implies the strong version. The main tool involved, other than Theorem
2.13
, is the Schläfli identity.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
[
Mil94
, Chapter: The Schläfli differential equality]
]
If
P_{t}
is a smooth family of hyperbolic polyhedra with the same
1
-skeleton, dihedral angles
\theta_{1}^{t},\dots,\theta_{n}^{t}
and edge lengths
l_{1}^{t},\dots,l_{n}^{t}
, then

\frac{\partial\textrm{Vol}(P_{t})}{\partial t}_{\big\rvert_{t=t_{0}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}l_{i}^{t_{0}}\frac{\partial\theta^{t}_{i}}{\partial t}_{\big\rvert_{t=t_{0}}}

Proposition 4.2
.

If Conjecture
2
is true, then two polyhedra with the same
1
-skeleton and the same dihedral angles have the same edge lengths.

Proof.

Consider
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
with the same
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and the same dihedral angles
\{\theta_{e}\}_{e\textrm{ edges of }\Gamma}
; fix an edge
e\subseteq\Gamma
. By Theorem
2.6
, for
t
close to
1
there are two families of polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
, denoted with
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
, such that:

•

P_{1}^{1}=P_{1}
and
P_{2}^{1}=P_{2}
;

•

the dihedral angles of both
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
at
e
are equal to
t\theta_{e}
.

•

the dihedral angles of both
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
at an edge
e^{\prime}\neq e
are equal to
\theta_{e^{\prime}}
.

Because of the assumption of the validity of Conjecture
2
,
\textrm{Vol}(P_{1}^{t})=\textrm{Vol}(P_{2}^{t})
for all
t
in a neighborhood of
1
; therefore by the Schläfli identity,
l_{e}^{1}=\frac{\partial\textrm{Vol}(P_{1}^{t})}{\partial\theta_{e}}|_{t=1}=\frac{\partial\textrm{Vol}(P_{2}^{t})}{\partial\theta_{e}}|_{t=1}=l_{e}^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

If Conjecture
2
is true, then two polyhedra with the same
1
-skeleton and the same dihedral angles have congruent faces.

Proof.

Consider two polyhedra
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
with the same
1
-skeleton
\Gamma
and the same dihedral angles
\{\theta_{e}\}_{e\textrm{ edges of }\Gamma}
. Fix faces
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
(of
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
respectively) corresponding to the same face
F
of
\Gamma
. If
F
is triangular, then
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
have the same edge lengths by Proposition
4.2
, and therefore they are congruent. Suppose that
F
is not triangular: we show that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are congruent by showing that their diagonals have the same length. Choose two non-adjacent vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of
F
, and add the edge
\tilde{e}=\overline{v_{1}v_{2}}
to
\Gamma
; we call the new graph
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are both elements of
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma}=\partial\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{\tilde{e}}
, we can think of them as polyhedra with weak
1
-skeleton
(\Gamma^{\prime},\tilde{e})
; in particular their dihedral angle at
\tilde{e}
is equal to
\pi
.

By Theorem
2.6
, for
t
in some interval
(1-\epsilon,1]
there are two families of polyhedra with
1
-skeleton
\Gamma^{\prime}
, denoted with
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
, such that:

•

P_{1}^{1}=P_{1}
and
P_{2}^{1}=P_{2}
;

•

the dihedral angles of both
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
at
\tilde{e}
are equal to
t\pi
.

•

the dihedral angles of both
P_{1}^{t}
and
P_{2}^{t}
at an edge
e\neq\tilde{e}
are equal to
\theta_{e^{\prime}}
.

Therefore, by the same argument of Proposition
4.2
, the length of
\tilde{e}
is the same in
P_{1}
and in
P_{2}
. This shows that every diagonal in
P_{1}
has the same length of the corresponding diagonal in
P_{2}
, which implies that each face of
P_{1}
is congruent to the corresponding face of
P_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If Conjecture
2
is true, then the Stoker conjecture is true.

Proof.

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have the same
1
-skeleton and dihedral angles. Assuming that Conjecture
2
is true, then by Proposition
4.3
,
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
have congruent faces. Then, by
[
RH93
, Theorem 4.1]
, two polyhedra with the same
1
-skeleton and congruent faces are isometric.
∎

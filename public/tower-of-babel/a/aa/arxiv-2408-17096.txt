Particle Flows for Source Localization in 3-D Using TDOA Measurements
Wenyu Zhang, Mohammad Javad Khojasteh, Florian Meyer

Abstract
Localization using time-difference of arrival (TDOA) has myriad applications, e.g., in passive surveillance systems and marine mammal research. In this paper, we present a Bayesian estimation method that can localize an unknown number of static sources in 3-D based on TDOA measurements. The proposed localization algorithm based on particle flow (PFL) can overcome the challenges related to the highly non-linear TDOA measurement model, the data association (DA) uncertainty, and the uncertainty in the number of sources to be localized. Different PFL strategies are compared within a unified belief propagation (BP) framework in a challenging multisensor source localization problem. In particular, we consider PFL-based approximation of beliefs based on one or multiple Gaussian kernels with parameters computed using deterministic and stochastic flow processes. Our numerical results demonstrate that the proposed method can correctly determine the number of sources and provide accurate location estimates. The stochastic flow demonstrates greater accuracy compared to the deterministic flow when using the same number of particles.

Contribution

In this paper, we aim to solve a challenging 3-D multisource localization based on particle-based BP with
da

To address the particle degeneracy of conventional particle-based BP in high-dimensional space, we consider importance sampling based on deterministic and stochastic flow. As deterministic flows, we consider the

The existing auxiliary-based methods

5

With fewer Gaussian kernels than the particles, the computational complexity can be greatly reduced since flow parameters only have to be computed for each Gaussian kernel. Our method can overcome the challenges related to the highly non-linear
tdoa

Simulation results confirm that the number of sources can be determined correctly, and accurate location estimates can be obtained when the number of false alarms is low, and the probability of detection is high. The stochastic Gromov’s flow achieves the best performance considering the localization accuracy and runtime, which exceeds existing state-of-the-art localization methods. Our simulation result promotes further research into stochastic flow since previous work shows a relatively small improvement in the accuracy of the state estimates

III-A

Deterministic
pfl
:
edh
and
ledh
Flow

The Bayesian
pfl

[
17
,
18
]
tries to define a continuous mapping
\Phi:\bm{X}_{0}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\bm{X}_{1}
to migrate particles sampled from the topological space of prior PDF
\bm{x}^{(i)}_{0}\sim\bm{X}_{0}
to the topological space of posterior PDF
\bm{x}^{(i)}_{1}\sim\bm{X}_{1}
. The process is time-dependent w.r.t. a pseudo-time
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

Let
f(\bm{x})
be the prior PDF,
h(\bm{x})\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358ptf(\bm{z}|\bm{x})
be the likelihood function (e.g. (
1
) for
tdoa
model). Following Bayes’ rule, a log-homotopy function is introduced
[
17
,
18
]

as

\phi(\bm{x},\lambda)=\log f(\bm{x})+\lambda\log h(\bm{x}).\vskip 1.42262pt

(10)

Note that the homotopy function defines a continuous and smooth deformation from
\phi(\bm{x},0)\hskip-0.85358pt=\hskip-0.85358pt\log f(\bm{x})
to
\phi(\bm{x},1)\hskip-0.85358pt=\log\pi(\bm{x})
, where
\pi(\bm{x})=f(\bm{x})f(\bm{z}|\bm{x})
is the unnormalized posterior PDF. The drift of the flow

\bm{\zeta}(\bm{x},\lambda)=\text{d}\bm{x}/\text{d}\lambda

is calculated by solving an
ode
and hence used to drive the dynamics of particles deterministically.
Since the drift depends on
\bm{x}
, the direct integral over
\lambda
from 0 to 1 typically has no analytical solution. The Euler method is commonly used for numerical implementation, where particle migration is performed by calculating
\bm{\zeta}(\bm{x},\lambda)
at
N_{\lambda}
discrete values of
\lambda
, i.e.,
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<...<\lambda_{N_{\lambda}}=1
. First,
N_{\mathrm{s}}
particles

\big\{\bm{x}_{0}^{(i)}\big\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}

are drawn from
f({\bm{x}})
. Next, these particles are migrated sequentially across discrete pseudo time steps
l\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\dots,N_{\lambda}\}
,

i.e.,

\bm{x}^{(i)}_{\lambda_{l}}=\bm{x}^{(i)}_{\lambda_{l-1}}+\bm{\zeta}(\bm{x}^{(i)}_{\lambda_{l-1}},\lambda_{l})(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})\hskip 4.2679pt\vskip 2.84526pt

(11)

for all
i\hskip-0.85358pt\in\hskip-0.85358pt\{1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{s}}\}
. In this way, particles

\{\bm{x}_{1}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}

representing the posterior PDF
\pi(\bm{x})
are obtained.

If
\log f(\bm{x})
and
\log h(\bm{x})
are polynomials (e.g.,
f(\bm{x})
and
h(\bm{x})
are Gaussians or in another exponential family), the drift term can be solved exactly
[
20
]
. In particular, let us consider a Gaussian prior
f(\bm{x})={\cal{N}}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu}_{0},\bm{P})
with mean
{\bm{\mu}}_{0}
and covariance matrix
\bm{P}
as well as a linear measurement model
\bm{z}=\bm{H}\bm{x}+\bm{v}
. Here, the measurement noise
\bm{v}
is zero-mean Gaussian with covariance matrix
\bm{R}
. The
edh
flow
[
20
]
is given

by

\bm{\zeta}_{\text{d}}(\bm{x},\lambda)=\bm{A}(\lambda)\bm{x}+\bm{b}(\lambda)\vskip 0.0pt

(12)

where

\displaystyle\bm{A}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}(\lambda\bm{H}\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}+\bm{R})^{-1}\bm{H}

\displaystyle\bm{b}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\bm{I}+2\lambda\bm{A}(\lambda))\big[(\bm{I}+\lambda\bm{A}(\lambda))\bm{P}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{z}+\bm{A}(\lambda){\bm{\mu}}_{0}\big].

For nonlinear measurement models
\bm{z}=\bm{h}(\bm{x})+\bm{v}
, a suboptimal linearization step is employed, i.e.
\tilde{\bm{H}}_{{l-1}}=\frac{\partial\bm{h}}{\partial\bm{x}}\big|_{\bm{x}=\bm{\mu}_{\lambda_{l-1}}}

at a current mean
\bm{\mu}_{\lambda_{l-1}}
. This mean is propagated in parallel to the particles
\{\bm{x}_{\lambda_{l-1}}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}
by using (
11
). Alternatively, linearization can be performed

at each particle, i.e., at
\tilde{\bm{H}}^{(i)}_{{l-1}}=\frac{\partial\bm{h}}{\partial\bm{x}}\big|_{\bm{x}=\bm{x}^{(i)}_{\lambda_{l-1}}}
where
\tilde{\bm{A}}^{(i)}(\lambda)
and
\tilde{\bm{b}}^{(i)}(\lambda)
are computed for each particle respectively. This is known as the
ledh
flow. The
edh
flow is computationally much faster than the
ledh
flow since it only calculates a single pair of global flow parameters
\bm{A}
and
\bm{b}
. However, the PDF of particles after the flow still follows a Gaussian PDF. (Note that the flow in (
12
) can be seen as a sequence of affine transformations applied to the Gaussian prior.) On the other hand, the
ledh
flow provides particles that follow an arbitrary probability PDF since each particle follows an individual affine transform. However, since a different pair of flow parameters is computed for each particle, the computational complexity of the
ledh
flow is
N_{\mathrm{s}}
times higher than
edh
flow.

III-B

Stochastic
pfl
Gromov’s Flow

The migration of particles corresponding to (
10
) can alternatively be describe by a
sde

[
22
]
, i.e.,

\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=\bm{\zeta}_{\mathrm{s}}(\bm{x},\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda+\mathrm{d}\bm{w}_{\lambda}

(13)

where
\bm{\zeta}_{\mathrm{s}}(\bm{x},\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the stochastic drift and
\mathrm{d}\bm{w}
is the
N

\mathrm{d}\bm{w}\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{d}\bm{w}^{\mathrm{T}}=\bm{Q}(\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

[
22
]
.
The stochastic
pfl
outperforms deterministic
pfl
for improving the transient dynamics. In some applications, their random nature can avoid numerical issues, referred to as stiffness of the flow
[
23
]
), related to implementing the flow using discrete values of pseudo-time
\lambda
.

One of the most popular stochastic flow with a closed-form solution is Gromov’s flow, where

\bm{\zeta}_{\mathrm{g}}(\bm{x},\lambda)=-\big(\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\phi\big)^{-1}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\log h

(14)

with the following solution for diffusion matrix

\bm{Q}_{\text{g}}=-\big(\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\phi\big)^{-1}\big(\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\log h\big)\big(\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\phi\big)^{-1}.

(15)

One of the advantages of Gromov’s flow is that it provides an unbiased estimate of the state
[
34
]
.

Given Gaussian prior and linear approximation of measurement model as in the
edh
flow, we have
\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\log h=\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{H}\bm{x})
,
\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\log h=-\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}
and
\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}\nabla_{\hskip-0.85358pt\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{T}}\phi=-\bm{P}^{-1}-\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}
. Then we can rewrite (
14
) and (
15
) as

\bm{\zeta}_{\mathrm{g}}(\bm{x},\lambda)=\Big(\bm{P}^{-1}+\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}\Big)^{-1}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{H}\bm{x})

(16)

\bm{Q}_{\text{g}}=(\bm{P}^{-1}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H})^{-1}(\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H})(\bm{P}^{-1}\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt+\hskip-0.85358pt\hskip-0.85358pt\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H})^{-1}.

(17)

We can still parameterize the drift similarly as
edh
as

\bm{\zeta}_{\text{g}}(\bm{x},\lambda)=\bm{A}_{\text{g}}(\lambda)\bm{x}+\bm{b}_{\text{g}}(\lambda)\vskip 0.0pt

where the parameters of the flow are given by

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{\text{g}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\Big(\bm{P}^{-1}+\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}\Big)^{-1}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}

\displaystyle\bm{b}_{\text{g}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big(\bm{P}^{-1}+\lambda\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{H}\Big)^{-1}\bm{H}^{\mathrm{T}}\bm{R}^{-1}\bm{z}.

III-C

pfl
as Proposal PDF

Although
pfl
methods display very promising characteristics, due to approximations related to the discretization of pseudo time
\lambda
, they are unable to provide an asymptotically optimal representation of the posterior PDF
\pi(\bm{x})
. However, for asymptotically optimal estimation,
pfl
methods can be used to provide samples and a proposal PDF
q(\bm{x})
for importance sampling
[
13
]
.

Direct evaluation of the proposal PDF
q(\bm{x})
related to
pfl
is complicated by the fact that a closed-form expression of
q(\bm{x})
is unavailable. For the numerical evaluation of
q({\bm{x}}^{(i)}_{1})
,
i=1,\dots,N_{\mathrm{s}}
, the following two approaches can be employed:
i) deriving an invertible mapping from the prior to the posterior PDF
[
27
]
and ii) developing a Gaussian proposal PDF where mean and covariance are updated sequentially based on flow equations
[
25
]
.

As presented in
[
27
]
, for an affine deterministic flow, there exists an invertible mapping
\theta:f({\bm{x}}^{(i)}_{0})\rightarrow\pi({\bm{x}}^{(i)}_{1})
with linear operator
\theta
. This motivates a proposal PDF given by
q({\bm{x}}^{(i)}_{1})=f({\bm{x}}^{(i)}_{0})/\tilde{\theta}
where the approximate mapping factor
\tilde{\theta}
is defined

as

\tilde{\theta}=\prod_{l=1}^{N_{\lambda}}\big|\hskip 0.85358pt\mathrm{det}\big[\bm{I}+(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})\hskip 0.85358pt\tilde{\bm{A}}(\lambda_{l})\big]\big|.\vskip 2.84526pt

(18)

Deriving an invertible mapping for stochastic flows remains an open research problem.
As an alternative, following the second approach discussed above, we develop a Gaussian proposal PDF based on stochastic flow, as discussed next.

IV

In this section, we develop a

IV-A

Proposal Evaluation and
gmm
-Based Representation

Proposal evaluation based on deterministic flow within a
gmm
representation of beliefs has been presented in
[
5
]
. Here, we adapt this strategy to the use of a stochastic flow within the
gmm
representation. The primary motivation for using a
gmm
representation is that it can represent non-Gaussian beliefs with potentially complicated shapes. Being able to model complicated beliefs is important in multisensor localization and tracking problems where the dimension of the individual sensor measurements is lower than the dimension of object positions as, e.g., in the scenario shown in Fig.
1
. Moreover, by leveraging a mixture of Gaussian kernels to model beliefs,
pfl
can be performed for each Gaussian kernel in parallel.

Let’s first consider a single Gaussian prior
f(\bm{x})={\cal{N}}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu}_{0},\bm{\varSigma}_{0}\triangleq\bm{P})
. For discrete time
0=\lambda_{0}<\dots<\lambda_{N_{\lambda}}=1
, we can update the mean and covariance by making using of the stochastic flow in (
13
), i.e.,

\bm{\mu}_{\lambda_{l}}=\bm{\mu}_{\lambda_{l-1}}+\bm{\zeta}_{\mathrm{g}}(\bm{\mu}_{\lambda_{l-1}},\lambda_{l})(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})

(19)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt\bm{\varSigma}_{\lambda_{l}}=\hskip 0.85358pt\hskip 0.85358pt

\displaystyle[\bm{I}+(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})\bm{A}_{\text{g}}(\lambda_{l})]\bm{\Sigma}_{\lambda_{l-1}}[\bm{I}+(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})\bm{A}_{\text{g}}(\lambda_{l})]^{\text{T}}

\displaystyle+(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l-1})\bm{Q}_{\text{g}}(\lambda_{l})

(20)

for
l=1,\dots,N_{\lambda}
. We can then evaluate the proposal at the particles as
q(\bm{x}_{1}^{(i)})={\cal{N}}(\bm{x}_{1}^{(i)};\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\varSigma}_{1})
.

Next, we utilize (
19
) and (
20
) in a
gmm
model. More precisely, instead of sampling
\big\{\bm{x}_{0}^{(i)}\big\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{s}}}
from a Gaussian prior

, we model the prior as
gmm

N_{\mathrm{k}}

f(\bm{x})\propto\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{k}}}{\cal{N}}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu}_{0}^{(k)},\bm{\varSigma}_{0}^{(k)})
. This mixture representation makes it possible to approximate arbitrary PDFs. In particular, it enables to closely approximate localization information on a hyperboloid as provided by
tdoa

\big\{\bm{x}_{0}^{(i,k)}\big\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{p}}}
from
{\cal{N}}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu}_{0}^{(k)},\bm{\varSigma}_{0}^{(k)})
and use
\big\{\big\{\bm{x}_{0}^{(i,k)}\big\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{p}}}\big\}_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{k}}}
as the particle set. Hence, the equivalent particle number is

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a BP method for localizing an unknown number of sources in 3-D based on TDOA measurements. Our method relies on
gmm
to represent approximate marginal posterior PDFs, called beliefs, with complicated shapes for accurate localization in 3-D using a nonlinear measurement model. By passing BP messages, represented as weighted particles, over a factor graph, our method can simultaneously detect and localize an unknown number of sources in the presence of
da
uncertainty, false alarms, and clutter. The novelty of our approach lies in leveraging stochastic
pfl
for the efficient update of GMM parameters of beliefs. The considered stochastic
pfl
, referred to as Gromov’s flow, achieves the best accuracy-complexity tradeoff in numerical experiments, compared to previously considered deterministic flows. Future research includes information fusion of multiple types of measurement models with
pfl
and the development of novel stochastic
pfl
with improved transient dynamics. Modeling unresolved measurements
[
37
]
, combining
pfl
with BP-based track-before-detect methods
[
38
]
, or introducing deep learning techniques to refine the BP solution
[
39
]
are also possible venues for future

research.

Boundary value problems for adjoint pairs of operators
Jussi Behrndt

Abstract
. The notion of quasi boundary triples and their Weyl functions from extension theory of symmetric operators is extended to the general framework of adjoint pairs of operators under minimal conditions on the boundary maps. With the help of the corresponding abstract Titchmarsh-Weyl M M -functions sufficient conditions for the unique solvability of the related boundary value problems are obtained and the solutions are expressed via Krein-type resolvent formulae. The abstract theory developed in this manuscript can be applied to a large class of elliptic differential operators.

The main objective of this paper is to extend the notion of quasi boundary triples and their Weyl functions for symmetric operators from
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
17
]
to the general framework of adjoint pairs
of operators, and to develop the abstract theory around this concept;
in particular, the aim is to provide sufficient conditions for boundary parameters and boundary mappings to induce closed extensions with nonempty resolvent sets, and to describe their
resolvents via Krein-type resolvent formulae. Here we shall work under minimal assumptions on the boundary operators in the triple, that is, we require an abstract version of Green’s second identity (G),
a weaker density condition (D) on the range of the boundary mappings than usual, and a certain maximality condition (M). In our results we shall always state explicitely which assumptions
(G), (D), or (M) are needed for the actual statement.

Let us briefly explain and motivate our approach
and the main difference to the concept of ordinary boundary triples for
adjoint pairs of operators. For this, consider two densely defined closed operators

S
and
\widetilde{S}
in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
such that
\widetilde{S}\subset S^{*}
(or, equivalently
S\subset\widetilde{S}^{*}
).
Assume that the operators
T
and
\widetilde{T}
are cores of
S
and
\widetilde{S}
, respectively, that is, their closures coincide with

S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
. The key feature in our theory is the assumption that there exists an auxiliary (boundary)
Hilbert space
{\mathcal{G}}
and boundary mappings
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
such that an abstract Green’s identity

\displaystyle{\rm(G)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(Tf,g)_{\mathfrak{H}}-(f,\widetilde{T}g)_{\mathfrak{H}}=(\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g)_{\mathcal{G}}-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)_{\mathcal{G}}

holds for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
. We emphasize that (G) is not required on the (full) domain of the adjoint operators
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
(as is the case for ordinary boundary triples) and that (G) typically does not
admit an extension onto
{\text{\rm dom\,}}S^{*}
and
{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}^{*}
.
We are mainly interested in the case
\dim{\mathcal{G}}=\infty
(as otherwise
T=S^{*}
and
\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{S}^{*}
, and hence ordinary boundary triples can be used).
In addition to Green’s identity (G) a
density condition (D) or (DD) and a maximality condition (M) in Definition
2.1
is often needed for a fruitful and functioning theory.
We will then study extensions of
\widetilde{S}
and
S
which are restrictions of
T
and
\widetilde{T}
, respectively,
of the form

\begin{split}A_{B}f&=Tf,\qquad{\text{\rm dom\,}}A_{B}=\bigl\{f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T:B\Gamma_{1}f=\Gamma_{0}f\bigr\},\\
\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}}g&=\widetilde{T}g,\qquad{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}}=\bigl\{g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}:\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g\bigr\},\end{split}

where
B
and
\widetilde{B}
are linear operators in
{\mathcal{G}}
. In the general abstract setting the goal is to show that

A_{B}
and
\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}}
are closed operators with nonempty resolvent sets,
as this ensures unique solvability or well-posedness of the abstract Robin-type boundary value problems

(T-\lambda)f=h,\quad B\Gamma_{1}f=\Gamma_{0}f,\quad\text{or}\quad(\widetilde{T}-\mu)g=k,\quad\widetilde{B}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g,

whenever
\lambda\in\rho(A_{B})
and
\mu\in\rho(\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}})
and
h,k\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
After proving an abstract Birman-Schwinger principle (in a symmetrized form)
we find sufficient conditions on the boundary parameter, the mapping properties of the boundary maps,
and the associated Weyl functions such that the resolvents
A_{B}
and
\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}}
can be explicitly
computed in terms of the resolvent of an underlying fixed extension and a perturbation term in the boundary space
{\mathcal{G}}
.
We find it useful for reference purposes to summarize our results for general adjoint pairs in Appendix
A
in the special case that
S=\widetilde{S}
is a densely defined closed symmetric operator. In this situation our results generalize those from
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
18
]
in the sense that we impose a weaker density condition (D) than usual.

The present paper stays on an abstract operator theory level and we have decided to postpone the diverse applications to
future investigations and projects (with the small exceptions Example
2.8
and Example
4.10
, where a strongly elliptic system on a Lipschitz domain following

[
52
]
is discussed). We only indicate briefly as a motivation, that
in the situation of a second order elliptic differential expression
(with smooth coefficients on a domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with a smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
) the adjoints

S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
coincide with the maximal operators associated to
the differential expression and its formal adjoint in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, whereas
T
and
\widetilde{T}
can be chosen, e.g. as the operator realizations defined on
the second order Sobolev space
H^{2}(\Omega)
; in this case a natural choice is
\Gamma_{0}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}

as the Dirichlet trace and
\Gamma_{1},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
as the conormal derivatives mapping into the boundary space
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. It is clear that the operators
A_{B}
and
\widetilde{A}_{\widetilde{B}}
above
are Robin realizations of the elliptic differential expression and its formal adjoint. Besides this standard situation
sketched here many other applications to differential operators can be explored.

2.
Quasi boundary triples for adjoint pairs of operators

Let throughout this section
S
and
\widetilde{S}
be densely defined closed operators in a separable Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
such that

(Sf,g)=(f,\widetilde{S}g),\qquad f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}S,\,g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S},

(2.1)

holds. Note that this is the same as requiring
\widetilde{S}\subset S^{*}
or
S\subset\widetilde{S}^{*}
.
We shall call a pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
of operators with this property an
adjoint pair
or
dual pair
.
In this manuscript we are mainly interested in the situation

\dim\bigl({\text{\rm dom\,}}S^{*}/{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}\bigr)=\dim\bigl({\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}^{*}/{\text{\rm dom\,}}S\bigr)=\infty,

(2.2)

although our results do not formally require this condition.
Note that in the special case
S=\widetilde{S}
the property (
2.1
) shows that
S
is a symmetric operator and (
2.2
) means that at least one of
the defect numbers of
S
is infinite. In the following we shall work with operators
T\subset S^{*}
and

\widetilde{T}\subset\widetilde{S}^{*}
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
which (are typically not closed and) satisfy

\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.34428pt\hskip-2.34428pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.34428pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{5.76332pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.68857pt}{0.20999pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.34428pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.95924pt\hskip-1.95924pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-1.95924pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{4.11667pt}{\hbox{\rule{3.9185pt}{0.15pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 1.95924pt}}\mkern 1.0mu=S^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.22224pt\hskip-2.22224pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mkern-1.0mu\widetilde{T}\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.22224pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.62222pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.44449pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.22224pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.22224pt\hskip-2.22224pt\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.0mu\widetilde{T}\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.22224pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.62222pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.44449pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.22224pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.22224pt\hskip-2.22224pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.0mu\widetilde{T}\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.22224pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.20221pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.44449pt}{0.20999pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.22224pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.22224pt\hskip-2.22224pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.0mu\widetilde{T}\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.22224pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{7.92223pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.44449pt}{0.15pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.22224pt}}\mkern 1.0mu=\widetilde{S}^{*},

or, equivalently
T^{*}=S
and
\widetilde{T}^{*}=\widetilde{S}
. In this situation we
shall say that
T
and
\widetilde{T}
are
cores
of
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively.

Inspired by the notion of quasi boundary triples for symmetric operators in
[
12
,
13
]
and typical applications in elliptic boundary value problems in the non-symmetric situation (see, e.g.
[
52
]
) we extend the definition of
boundary triples for adjoint pairs in the abstract setting from
[

,

]
, see also
[

,

. At the same time we also impose a slightly weaker density condition (D) than usual and it will turn out that this is sufficient for a functioning theory.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
be an adjoint pair of operators in
{\mathfrak{H}}
and assume

T
and
\widetilde{T}
are cores of
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively.
We shall consider
triples
of the form
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
, where

{\mathcal{G}}
is a Hilbert space and

\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}},\qquad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}},

are linear mappings such that

(G)

the abstract
Green’s identity

(Tf,g)_{\mathcal{H}}-(f,\widetilde{T}g)_{\mathcal{H}}=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)_{\mathcal{G}}-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)_{\mathcal{G}}

holds for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
,

(D)

the ranges of
\Gamma_{0}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
are dense,

(M)

the operators
A_{0}:=T\upharpoonright\ker\Gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{A}_{0}:=\widetilde{T}\upharpoonright\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
satisfy

A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}=A_{0}.

(2.3)

If
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is such that (G),

(DD)

the ranges of
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and
(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
are dense,

and (M) hold, then
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is said to be a
quasi boundary triple
for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
.

In the following we shall formulate all results under minimal assumptions on the triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
, tacitly assuming that

\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
is an adjoint pair of operators in
{\mathfrak{H}}
, and
T
and
\widetilde{T}
are cores of
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively. We will refer to condition (G) as
abstract
Green’s identity
,
condition (D) and the stronger condition (DD) as
density conditions
, and (M) is a
maximality condition
. Note that condition (DD) implies that
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}
,
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{1}
,
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
, and

{\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
are all dense in
{\mathcal{G}}
individually and, in particular, condition (DD) implies condition (D). Furthermore, it is clear from (M) that both restrictions

A_{0}=T\upharpoonright\ker\Gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{A}_{0}=\widetilde{T}\upharpoonright\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
are closed operators in
{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Note that for
A_{0}
closed the first condition
A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}
in (
2.3
) already implies the second condition
\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}=A_{0}
, and in the same
way
\widetilde{A}_{0}
closed and
\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}=A_{0}
imply the first condition
A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}
in (
2.3
).
Later in Section
3
and
Section
4
we will typically
assume that the resolvent sets of
\rho(A_{0})
and
\rho(\widetilde{A}_{0})
are nonempty; cf. Lemma
3.1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if (G) holds, then for the operators
A_{0}=T\upharpoonright\ker\Gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{A}_{0}=\widetilde{T}\upharpoonright\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}

one has

(A_{0}f,g)-(f,\widetilde{A}_{0}g)=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)=0

for
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}A_{0}
and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{0}
, and hence the inclusions

\widetilde{A}_{0}\subset A_{0}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{0}\subset\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}

hold without further assumptions;
thus condition (M) is only needed for the inclusions
\widetilde{A}_{0}\supset A_{0}^{*}
and
A_{0}\supset\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}
.
Furthermore, condition (M) implies that the cores
T\subset S^{*}
and

\widetilde{T}\subset\widetilde{S}^{*}
are
also extensions of
\widetilde{S}
and
S
, respectively, since

A_{0}\subset T
,
\widetilde{A}_{0}\subset\widetilde{T}
, lead to

\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{T}^{*}\subset\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}=A_{0}\subset T\quad\text{and}\quad S=T^{*}\subset A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}\subset\widetilde{T}.

(2.4)

We point out that the operators
T
and
\widetilde{T}
in Definition (
2.1
)
are not unique and may also coincide with
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively.
However, in this special case
T=S^{*}
and
\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{S}
the situation simplifies and reduces to the notion of
ordinary boundary triples for adjoint pairs; cf. Proposition
2.6
and, e.g.
[
22
,
50
,
46
,
61
]
.
It is not difficult to see that the mappings
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
are not unique, e.g. if the triple

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
satisfies (G) then also the triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{1},-\Gamma_{0}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1},-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0})\}

satisfies (G). Therefore, by imposing condition (D) for
\Gamma_{1}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
and by requiring that
the operators
A_{1}:=T\upharpoonright\ker\Gamma_{1}
and
\widetilde{A}_{1}:=\widetilde{T}\upharpoonright\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
satisfy condition (M), that is,

A_{1}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{A}_{1}^{*}=A_{1},

(2.5)

the triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{1},-\Gamma_{0}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1},-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0})\}
has the same properties (G), (D), and (M) as the original triple

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
. In particular, if
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}

is a quasi boundary triple and (
2.5
) holds,
then
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{1},-\Gamma_{0}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1},-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0})\}
is also a quasi boundary triple.

The next lemma shows that the density condition (DD) in Definition
2.1

can be concluded from the surjectivity of the maps
\Gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that the triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
satisfies
(G)
and
(M)
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

If
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}={\mathcal{G}}
, then
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
;

(ii)

If
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}={\mathcal{G}}
, then
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
.

In particular, if
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}={\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}={\mathcal{G}}
, then condition
(DD)
in Definition
2.1
is satisfied and

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is a quasi boundary triple for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
.

Proof.

(i) Assume that
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})^{\top}\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
is orthogonal to the range of
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}

and choose
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
such that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g=\varphi^{\prime}
. Then we have

0=(\Gamma_{1}f,\varphi^{\prime})-(\Gamma_{0}f,-\varphi)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,-\varphi)

(2.6)

for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T

and hence the abstract Green’s identity (G) becomes

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)=(\Gamma_{0}f,-\varphi-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g).

In particular, for
f\in\ker\Gamma_{0}={\text{\rm dom\,}}A_{0}
we have

(A_{0}f,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=0

and therefore
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}A_{0}^{*}={\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{0}=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
, that is,
\varphi^{\prime}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g=0
. Now (
2.6
)
reduces to
0=(\Gamma_{0}f,\varphi)
for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and as
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}
we conclude
\varphi=0
. This shows that the range of

(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
.

(ii) can be proved in the same way as (i). It is also clear from (i) and (ii) that under the assumption
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}={\text{\rm ran\,}}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}={\mathcal{G}}
the ranges of both
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
and
(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}

are dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
, that is, condition (DD) in Definition
2.1
holds and hence

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is a quasi boundary triple.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that the triple
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
satisfies
(G)
,
(D)
, and
(M)
.
Then

{\text{\rm dom\,}}S=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}=\ker\Gamma_{0}\cap\ker\Gamma_{1}.

(2.7)

Proof.

We will verify the identity
{\text{\rm dom\,}}S=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
;
the second identity in (
2.7
) can be shown in the same way.
For this, let us consider some fixed
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}S={\text{\rm dom\,}}T^{*}
. From
A_{0}\subset T
and
the maximality condition (M) we obtain

T^{*}\subset A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}\subset\widetilde{T}
(see (
2.4
)) and hence
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{0}=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\subset{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
. The abstract Green’s identity (G) yields

\begin{split}0&=(Tf,g)-(f,T^{*}g)\\
&=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)\\
&=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\\
&=-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\end{split}

for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and as
{\text{\rm ran\,}}\Gamma_{0}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}
by condition (D) it follows that also
g\in\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
. Conversely, for
g\in\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
the abstract Green’s identity
implies

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)=0

for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
. This shows
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T^{*}={\text{\rm dom\,}}S
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that the triple

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
satisfies
(G)
and
(DD)
. Then the mappings

\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{0}\\
\Gamma_{1}\end{pmatrix}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\\
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}\end{pmatrix}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}

(2.8)

are both closable with respect to the graph norm of
T
and
\widetilde{T}
, respectively.
In particular, the individual mappings
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}

are closable.

Proof.

Consider a sequence
f_{n}\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
and
Tf_{n}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
and assume that
\Gamma_{0}f_{n}\rightarrow\varphi
and

\Gamma_{1}f_{n}\rightarrow\varphi^{\prime}
,
n\rightarrow\infty
, for some
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Using (G) it follows that

\begin{split}0&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((Tf_{n},g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{T}g)\bigr)\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((\Gamma_{1}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\bigr)\\
&=(\varphi^{\prime},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\varphi,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\end{split}

and as
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
by (DD) we conclude
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}=0
.
This proves that the first mapping in (
2.8
) is closable and the same
argument applies to the second mapping in (
2.8
).
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
be a quasi boundary triple for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
. Then
the following are equivalent:

(i)

T=S^{*}
and
\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{S}^{*}

(ii)

{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}={\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}={\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

(i)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(ii): Since
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
and
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}
are both dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
by (DD) it remains to show that both ranges are closed. We will provide the argument for
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
; the same reasoning applies to
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}
. Consider a sequence
(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\Gamma_{1}f_{n})^{\top}
, where

f_{n}\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and assume that

\Gamma_{0}f_{n}\rightarrow\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{1}f_{n}\rightarrow\varphi^{\prime}

(2.9)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{G}}
. Note first that by the inclusion
\widetilde{S}\subset T=S^{*}
in (
2.4
) there exists a closed subspace
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}

such that
T=\widetilde{S}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}
. Therefore,
since
{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}=\ker\Gamma_{0}\cap\ker\Gamma_{1}
by Lemma
2.4
it is no restriction
to assume that
f_{n}\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
satisfy

\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{n}\\
Tf_{n}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}k\\
\widetilde{S}k\end{pmatrix}\right)=0,\quad k\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}.

(2.10)

Let
(h,h^{\prime})^{\top}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
be arbitrary and observe that there exist
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}^{*}
and
k\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}
such that

\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\widetilde{S}^{*}g\\
g\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}k\\
\widetilde{S}k\end{pmatrix}.

Using (
2.10
),
\widetilde{S}^{*}=\widetilde{T}
, and the abtract Green’s identity (G) we compute

\begin{split}\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{n}\\
Tf_{n}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right)&=\left(\begin{pmatrix}f_{n}\\
Tf_{n}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-\widetilde{S}^{*}g\\
g\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}k\\
\widetilde{S}k\end{pmatrix}\right)\\
&=(Tf_{n},g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{S}^{*}g)\\
&=(Tf_{n},g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{T}g)\\
&=(\Gamma_{1}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\rightarrow(\varphi^{\prime},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\varphi,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\end{split}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where (
2.9
) entered in the last step. It follows that
(f_{n},Tf_{n})^{\top}
is a weak Cauchy sequence in
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
and
hence weakly bounded and
thus bounded. This implies that there exists a weakly convergent subsequence, again denoted by
(f_{n},Tf_{n})^{\top}
with weak limit

(f,f^{\prime})^{\top}\in\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.34428pt\hskip-2.34428pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.34428pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{5.76332pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.68857pt}{0.20999pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.34428pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.95924pt\hskip-1.95924pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-1.95924pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{4.11667pt}{\hbox{\rule{3.9185pt}{0.15pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 1.95924pt}}\mkern 1.0mu
. By assumption
T=S^{*}
and hence
f^{\prime}=Tf
. Now we conclude

\begin{split}(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)&=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((Tf_{n},g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{T}g)\bigr)\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((\Gamma_{1}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\bigr)\\
&=(\varphi^{\prime},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\varphi,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g),\end{split}

that is,

\left(\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{1}f-\varphi^{\prime}\\
\varphi-\Gamma_{0}f\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g\\
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g\end{pmatrix}\right)=0

for all
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
. As
{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}
is dense in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
by (DD) we obtain

\varphi=\Gamma_{0}f
and
\varphi^{\prime}=\Gamma_{1}f
, in particular,
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})^{\top}\in{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
and therefore

{\text{\rm ran\,}}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}
is closed.

(ii)
\,\Rightarrow\,
(i): Since
T
and
\widetilde{T}
are cores of
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively, it suffices to verify that

T
and
\widetilde{T}
are closed. We will provide the proof for
T
; the same argument can be used to show that
\widetilde{T}
is closed.
Consider a sequence
f_{n}
in
{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
and
Tf_{n}\rightarrow f^{\prime}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
f,f^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Let
(\psi,\psi^{\prime})^{\top}\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and choose
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
such that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g=\psi^{\prime}
and

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g=-\psi
, which is possible by our assumptions. Using (G) we compute

\begin{split}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\Gamma_{0}f_{n}\\
\Gamma_{1}f_{n}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\psi\\
\psi^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right)&=(\Gamma_{1}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\\
&=(T_{n}f,g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{T}g)\rightarrow(f^{\prime},g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)\end{split}

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. This shows that
(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\Gamma_{1}f_{n})^{\top}
is a weak Cauchy sequence in
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and thus weakly bounded and hence bounded. Therefore, there exists a weakly convergent subsequence, again denoted by
(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\Gamma_{1}f_{n})^{\top}

with weak limit
(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})^{\top}\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
. By assumption there exists
h\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
such that
\Gamma_{0}h=\varphi

and
\Gamma_{1}h=\varphi^{\prime}
. Now it follows for
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
that

\begin{split}(f^{\prime},g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((Tf_{n},g)-(f_{n},\widetilde{T}g)\bigr)\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl((\Gamma_{1}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f_{n},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\bigr)\\
&=(\varphi^{\prime},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\varphi,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\\
&=(\Gamma_{1}h,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}h,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\\
&=(Th,g)-(h,\widetilde{T}g)\end{split}

and hence
(h-f,\widetilde{T}g)=(Th-f^{\prime},g)
for all

. This implies
h-f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}^{*}

and
\widetilde{T}^{*}(h-f)=Th-f^{\prime}
. As
\widetilde{T}^{*}=\widetilde{S}\subset T
by (
2.4
) and
h\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
we conclude
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and
Tf=f^{\prime}
.
We have shown that
T
is closed.

The next result is of a slightly different nature: it provides a method to verify that a pair of given operators
T
and
\widetilde{T}

form a core of the adjoints of certain (minimal) operators
S
and
\widetilde{S}
, respectively. To emphasize this different point of view
we shall denote the assumptions on the boundary maps here by (G’), (D’) or (DD’), and (M’).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
be Hilbert spaces and let
T
and
\widetilde{T}
be operators in
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Assume that

\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}

(2.11)

are linear mappings such that

(G’)

the abstract Green’s identity

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)

holds for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
,

(D’)

the ranges of
\Gamma_{0}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
and

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
are dense,

(M’)

the operators
A_{0}:=T\upharpoonright\ker\Gamma_{0}
and
\widetilde{A}_{0}:=\widetilde{T}\upharpoonright\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}
satisfy

A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}=A_{0}.

If, in addition,
\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
and
\ker\Gamma_{0}\cap\ker\Gamma_{1}
are dense in
{\mathfrak{H}}
, then
the operators

\begin{split}Sf:=\widetilde{T}f,&\quad f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}S=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1},\\
\widetilde{S}g:=Tg,&\quad g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}=\ker\Gamma_{0}\cap\ker\Gamma_{1},\end{split}

are closed and form an adjoint pair such that
T
and
\widetilde{T}
are cores of
S^{*}
and
\widetilde{S}^{*}
, respectively. Furthermore, if the mappings in (
2.11
) satisfy the conditions

(G’)
,

(DD’)

the ranges of
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})^{\top}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
and
(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})^{\top}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathcal{G}}
are dense,

and
(M’)
, then

\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is a quasi boundary triple for the adjoint pair
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
.

Proof.

Observe first that by (G’) and the definition of
S
and
\widetilde{S}
we have

(Sf,g)-(f,\widetilde{S}g)=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)=0

for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}S
and and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}
, and hence
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
is an adjoint pair.
We will verify the identity

T^{*}=S

(2.12)

and the same arguments can be used to prove the identity
\widetilde{T}^{*}=\widetilde{S}
.
Note first that from
A_{0}\subset T
and (M’) it follows that
T^{*}\subset A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}\subset\widetilde{T}
.
Therefore, if
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T^{*}
, then
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{0}=\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\subset{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
and (G’) implies

\begin{split}0&=(Tf,g)-(f,T^{*}g)\\
&=(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)\\
&=(\Gamma_{1}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}g)-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\\
&=-(\Gamma_{0}f,\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g)\end{split}

for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
, and hence assumption (D’) shows
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}g=0
.
Now it follows that
T^{*}g=\widetilde{T}g
and
g\in\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
, that is,
T^{*}\subset S
.
For the reverse inclusion let
g\in\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0}\cap\ker\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
. Then it follows from (G’) that

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=0

holds for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
. This implies
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T^{*}
and
T^{*}g=\widetilde{T}g
and hence we obtain
S\subset T^{*}
.
We have shown (
2.12
). It is also clear from (
2.12
) that the operator
S
is closed and
\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 3.06078pt\hskip-3.06078pt\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-3.06078pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.2333pt}{\hbox{\rule{6.12157pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 3.06078pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.34428pt\hskip-2.34428pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.34428pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{5.76332pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.68857pt}{0.20999pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.34428pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.95924pt\hskip-1.95924pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.0muT\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-1.95924pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{4.11667pt}{\hbox{\rule{3.9185pt}{0.15pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 1.95924pt}}\mkern 1.0mu=T^{**}=S^{*}
shows
that
T
is a core for
S^{*}
. In the same way
\widetilde{T}^{*}=\widetilde{S}
implies that
\widetilde{S}
is closed and that

\widetilde{T}
is a core for
\widetilde{S}^{*}
. Finally, note that
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}
is a quasi boundary triple for the adjoint pair

\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
if the conditions (G’), (DD’), and (M’) hold.
∎

We briefly illustrate the abstract theory developed in this section for the case of strongly elliptic systems on Lipschitz domains following the
presentation in
[
52
]
.

Example 2.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a (possibly) unbounded Lipschitz domain with outward unit normal
\nu
and consider a linear second order partial differential expression

{\mathcal{P}}=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}A_{jk}\partial_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\partial_{j}+A,

with matrix-valued coefficient functions
A_{jk},A_{j},A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})
such that
A_{jk},A_{j}
,
j,k=1,\dots,n
, are Lipschitz continuous, and its
formal adjoint

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\partial_{j}A_{kj}^{*}\partial_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}A_{j}^{*}+A^{*}.

We define the operators
T
and
\widetilde{T}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})
by

\begin{split}Tf={\mathcal{P}}f,&\qquad{\text{\rm dom\,}}T=\bigl\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):{\mathcal{P}}f\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})\bigr\},\\
\widetilde{T}g=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g,&\qquad{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}=\bigl\{g\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})\bigr\}.\end{split}

Recall that the Dirichlet trace operator

\tau_{D}:H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})

(2.13)

is bounded and surjective. It follows from
the considerations in

[
52
, Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.4]
that the conormal derivatives

f\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{j}\tau_{D}(B_{j}f)\quad\text{and}\quad g\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{j}\tau_{D}(\widetilde{B}_{j}g),\quad f,g\in H^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),

where
B_{j}f=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{jk}\partial_{k}f
and
\widetilde{B}_{j}g=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{kj}^{*}\partial_{k}g+A_{j}^{*}g
,
can be extended by continuity to mappings

\tau_{N}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}T\rightarrow H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\tau}_{N}:{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}\rightarrow H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})

such that

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=\langle-\tau_{N}f,\tau_{D}g\rangle-\langle\tau_{D}f,-\widetilde{\tau}_{N}g\rangle

(2.14)

holds for all
f\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}T
and
g\in{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{T}
; here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual dual pairing of
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})

and
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})
. Now choose isometric isomorphisms
\iota_{\pm}:H^{\pm 1/2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})\rightarrow L^{2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})

that are compatible with this pairing, so that (
2.14
) turns into

(Tf,g)-(f,\widetilde{T}g)=(-\iota_{-}\tau_{N}f,\iota_{+}\tau_{D}g)-(\iota_{+}\tau_{D}f,-\iota_{-}\widetilde{\tau}_{N}g).

(2.15)

Next define the operators
\widetilde{S}
and
S
as restrictions of
T
and
\widetilde{T}
onto
\ker\tau_{D}\cap\ker\tau_{N}
and
\ker\tau_{D}\cap\ker\widetilde{\tau}_{N}
, respectively.
In a more explicit form we have

\begin{split}\widetilde{S}f&={\mathcal{P}}f,\\
{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{S}&=\bigl\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):{\mathcal{P}}f\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),\tau_{D}f=0,\tau_{N}f=0\bigr\},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}Sg&=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g,\\
{\text{\rm dom\,}}S&=\bigl\{g\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),\tau_{D}g=0,\widetilde{\tau}_{N}g=0\bigr\}.\end{split}

Let us now consider the triple
\{L^{2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),(\iota_{+}\tau_{D},-\iota_{-}\tau_{N}),(\iota_{+}\tau_{D},-\iota_{-}\widetilde{\tau}_{N})\}
. Observe that
(D) and (G) hold by (
2.13
) and (
2.15
). Furthermore, in the present situation we actually have

{\text{\rm ran\,}}\iota_{+}\tau_{D}=L^{2}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m})
. If, in addition, the resolvent set of the Dirichlet realizations

\begin{split}A_{0}f={\mathcal{P}}f,&\quad{\text{\rm dom\,}}A_{0}=\bigl\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):{\mathcal{P}}f\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),\,\tau_{D}f=0\bigr\},\\
\widetilde{A}_{0}g=\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g,&\quad{\text{\rm dom\,}}\widetilde{A}_{0}=\bigl\{g\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}):\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}g\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}),\,\tau_{D}g=0\bigr\},\end{split}

is nonempty, then we also have
A_{0}^{*}=\widetilde{A}_{0}
and
A_{0}=\widetilde{A}_{0}^{*}
(see Lemma
3.1
below), and hence condition (M) holds; cf.
[
52
, Theorem 4.10]
for the case of bounded Lipschitz domains. Now Theorem
2.7
implies that
\{S,\widetilde{S}\}
form an adjoint pair and that

Lemma 3.1

Let
\{{\mathcal{G}},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{0},\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1})\}

(G)

\mkern 1.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox{\hskip 2.36113pt\hskip-2.36113pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\mkern-1.0mu\lambda\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.36113pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.34444pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.72227pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.36113pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 2.36113pt\hskip-2.36113pt\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.0mu\lambda\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-2.36113pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{8.34444pt}{\hbox{\rule{4.72227pt}{0.3pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 2.36113pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.816pt\hskip-1.816pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.0mu\lambda\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-1.816pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{5.84108pt}{\hbox{\rule{3.63199pt}{0.20999pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 1.816pt}}{\hbox{\hskip 1.54517pt\hskip-1.54517pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.0mu\lambda\mkern-1.0mu$}\hskip-1.54517pt\hskip 0.0pt\raisebox{4.17221pt}{\hbox{\rule{3.09035pt}{0.15pt}}}\hskip 0.0pt\hskip 1.54517pt}}\mkern 1.0mu_{0}\in\rho(\widetilde{A}_{0})

and

and, in particular, condition

Definition 3.2
.

Let

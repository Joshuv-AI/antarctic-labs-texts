Fractal Interpolation Function On Products of the Sierpiński Gaskets
S. A. Prasad, S. Verma

Abstract
. In this paper, we aim to construct fractal interpolation function(FIF) on the product of two Sierpiński gaskets. Further, we collect some results regarding smoothness of the constructed FIF. We prove, in particular, that the FIF are Hölder functions under specific conditions. In the final section, we obtain some bounds on the fractal dimension of FIF.

2.
Fractal interpolation function on
SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}

Let
\{X;W_{i},i=1,2,\dots,k\}
be an Iterated Function System (IFS), where
(X,d)
is a complete metric space and
W_{i}:X\to X
are contractive mappings with contraction ratio
\alpha_{i}
respectively.
The IFS generates the mapping
W
from
\mathcal{K}(X)
into
\mathcal{K}(X)
given by

\mathcal{W}(K)=\cup_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}(K),

where
\mathcal{K}(X)
is the collection of all non-empty compact subsets of
X.

The Hutchinson-Barnsley map
\mathcal{W}
defined above is then a contraction
mapping, with respect to the Hausdorff metric
h_{d}
, the contraction ratio
\alpha
of
\mathcal{W}
is equal to
\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}
. Then, by the Banach
contraction principle, there exists a unique nonempty compact subset
K_{*}
such that

K_{*}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}(K_{*})
, The set
K_{*}
is termed as the attractor of the IFS. For further details, the reader is referred to
[
3
]
.

Let
U_{0}=\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
be the vertices of an equilateral triangle on the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
L_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(t+p_{i}),\ i=1,2,3
be three contractions of the plane which constitute an iterated functions system. For
n\in\mathbb{N},
we denote the collection of all words with length
n
by
\{1,2,3\}^{n},
i.e. if
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\{1,2,3\}^{n}
then
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n}
where
\omega_{i}\in\{1,2,3\}.
We define, for
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\{1,2,3\}^{N}
,

L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=L_{\omega_{1}}\circ L_{\omega_{2}}\circ\dots\circ L_{\omega_{N}}.

SG^{\prime}
is the attractor of the system:

SG^{\prime}=L_{1}(SG^{\prime})\cup L_{2}(SG^{\prime})\cup L_{3}(SG^{\prime}).

Similarly, consider
W_{0}=\{q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\}
be the vertices of another equilateral triangle on another
\mathbb{R}^{2}
plane and
K_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{2}(t+q_{i}),i=1,2,3
be three contractions of the second plane constituting another iterated functions system. Then, we have another attractor

SG^{\prime\prime}=K_{1}(SG^{\prime\prime})\cup K_{2}(SG^{\prime\prime})\cup K_{3}(SG^{\prime\prime}).

Hence, we have

SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}=\cup_{i=1,j=1}^{3}L_{i}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{j}(SG^{\prime\prime}).

Iteratively, we get

SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}=\cup_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{n}}L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime}).

Define
V_{N}
by
V_{N}=\{(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{i}),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(q_{j})):\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N},~i,j\in I\}.
The union of images of
V_{0}:=U_{0}\times W_{0}
under these iterations
(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(.),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(.))
with
|w|=N
constitutes the set of N-th stage vertices
V_{N}
of
SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
. Further, define
V_{*}=\cup_{N=1}^{\infty}V_{N}.

Let
\{(y,z_{y}):y\in V_{N}\}\subset SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
be a data set such that
z_{y}
is zero on the boundary of
SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
. We construct an IFS whose attractor is the graph of a function
f:SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f(y)=z_{y}~~~\text{for all}~y\in V_{N}.

Let
X=SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\times\mathbb{R}
and define maps
W_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}:X\rightarrow X
by

W_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s,x)=\Big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),F_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s,x)\Big),~~\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N},

where
F_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}:X\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

F_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s,x)=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s)x+h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t,s),~\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N},

where
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}:SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function such that

•

For
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}i,

\boldsymbol{\tau}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}j
,
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{j},s)=h_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{i},s)
for all
s\in SG^{\prime\prime}.

•

For
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\dots\eta_{N-1}i,

\boldsymbol{\xi}=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\dots\eta_{N-1}j
,
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(t,q_{j})=h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\xi}}(t,q_{i})
for all
t\in SG^{\prime}.

•

For
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}i,

\boldsymbol{\tau}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}j
, and
\boldsymbol{\eta}=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\dots\eta_{N-1}k,

\boldsymbol{\xi}=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\dots\eta_{N-1}l
,
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{j},q_{l})=h_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\xi}}(p_{i},q_{k}),
for all
i,j,k,l\in I.

and for each
\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}
,
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}:SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function with
\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\|_{\infty}<1.

Then
\{X;W_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}\}
is an IFS and admits an invariant set.

Theorem 2.1
.

The IFS
\{X;W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}},\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}\}
defined above has a unique attractor
Gr(f).
The set
Gr(f)
is the graph of a continuous function
f:SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
f(y)=z_{y}~~~\text{for all}~y\in V_{N}.
The function
f
is known as the Fractal Interpolation Function on the product
SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}.

Proof.

We know that
\mathcal{C}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})
the space of all continuous functions forms a Banach space with respect to supnorm. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})=\Big\{g\in\mathcal{C}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}):

\displaystyle g(y)=z_{y},~y\in V_{N},g(p_{i},s)=g(t,q_{i})=0,

\displaystyle\forall\ t\in SG^{\prime},~s\in SG^{\prime\prime},~i\in I\Big\}.

Define a Read-Bajraterevic map
T:\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})
by

(Tg)(t,s)=F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)\big),~~~\forall~(t,s)\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime}),

for every
~\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}.

We first show that
T
is well-defined. For this, we need to check the continuity of
Tg
at the following point :

•

(t,s)\in(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(SG^{\prime}))\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})

•

(t,s)\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times(L_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(SG^{\prime\prime}))

•

(t,s)\in(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(SG^{\prime}))\times(L_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(SG^{\prime\prime})).

From the construction of
SG^{\prime}
and
SG^{\prime\prime},
for any
t\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(SG^{\prime}),
one may get the following relation:
\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}i,

\boldsymbol{\tau}=\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{N-1}j
and
t=L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(p_{j})=L_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(p_{i})
for some
i,j\in I.

Now, since
g(p_{k},s)=0,\forall s\in SG^{\prime\prime},~k\in I,
we have

\displaystyle(Tg)(t,s)

\displaystyle=F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\big)

\displaystyle=F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))g(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))+h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))

\displaystyle=h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{j},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)),

and

\displaystyle(Tg)(t,s)

\displaystyle=F_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(x),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\big)

\displaystyle=F_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\tau\eta}}(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))g(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))+h_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))

\displaystyle=h_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(p_{i},K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)).

With the help of conditions on
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}
, it follows that
Tg
is continuous at
(t,s)\in(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\cap L_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(SG^{\prime}))\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})
. Other cases can be dealt similarily. Further, since
F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is continuous for each
\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}
,
Tg
is continuous, and
Tg\in\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}).
Hence,
T
is well-defined.
Now, let
g,h\in\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}).
Then we have

\displaystyle|(Tg)(t,s)-(Th)(t,s)|

\displaystyle=\Big|F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),g\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)\big)

\displaystyle-F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),h\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s)|\Big|g\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)-h\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}\|_{\infty}\|g-h\|_{\infty}.

This in turn yields

\|Tg-Th\|_{\infty}\leq\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}\|_{\infty}\|g-h\|_{\infty}.

Since
\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}\|_{\infty}<1
,
T
is a contraction. Using the Banach fixed point theorem,
T
has a unique fixed point
f\in\mathcal{C}_{*}(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}).
Since
Tf=f,

\displaystyle f(t,s)=F_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),f\big(L^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K^{-1}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)\big)\big),~~~~~\forall~(t,s)\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime}),

(1)

for every
~\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}.

By using the proof of Theorem
3.8
in
[
12
]
(motivated by Barnsley
[
3
]
) we can show that attractor associated with mentioned IFS is the graph of the above function. Now, we show that it is the graph of FIF
f
. With the help of functional equation (
1
) and
SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}=\cup_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{n}}L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\cup_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}}W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}(Gr(f))=

\displaystyle\cup_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}}\Big\{\big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s),f(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(x),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s))\big):x\in I\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cup_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}}\big\{(t,s,f(t,s)):(t,s)\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Gr(f),

completing the proof.
∎

3.
Smoothness of Fractal Interpolation Functions on
SG\times SG

In this section, we aim to discuss the smoothness property of the FIF
f:SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
obtained through the IFS:

L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{N}}t+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{k}}p_{\omega_{k}};K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(s)=\frac{1}{2^{N}}s+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{k}}q_{\eta_{k}};~~F_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s,x)=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega\eta}}(t,s)x+h_{\omega\eta}(t,s),~\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}.

Targeting to give a simplified presentation, one may introduce the notation
a=\frac{1}{2^{N}}
,
a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{k}}p_{\omega_{k}}
and
b_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2^{k}}q_{\omega_{k}}
. Then we have
L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(t)=at+a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},~K_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(s)=as+b_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
for
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in I^{N}.
In this section, for computational convenience, we consider
\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq 1
for all
(t,s),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}.

We show that for suitable choices of the parameters, the fractal function
f
preserves the Hölder continuity of FIF
h
.

For
t\in SG^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}\in I^{N}
, let

L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(t)=L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}}\circ L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}}\circ\dots\circ L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(t),~~L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime})=L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}}\circ L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}}\circ\dots\circ L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime}).

Define a shift operator
\sigma
by

\sigma(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{3}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}).

Let
\sigma^{k}
denote the
k-
fold autocomposition of
\sigma
such that for
1\leq k\leq m-1
,
L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t)=L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k+1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k+2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(t)
otherwise
L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t)=t.
With the help of the successive iteration and induction, we may prove the following lemma; see also
[
37
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a fractal function corresponding to the aforementioned IFS. For any
(t,s)\in SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{j}\in I^{N},

\displaystyle L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(t)=a^{m}t+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a^{k-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}}~\text{and}~K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(s)=a^{m}s+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a^{k-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}

and

\displaystyle f\big(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(t),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(s)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(s))\Big)f(t,s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(s))\Big)h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(s)\big),

where

\displaystyle L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t)=a^{m-k}t+\sum_{l=1}^{m-k}a^{l-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k+l}}~\text{and}~K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(s)=a^{m-k}s+\sum_{l=1}^{m-k}a^{l-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k+l}}.

Let us now state the next result borrowed from
[
37
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r_{i},q_{i},~~i=1,2,\dots,m,
be given real numbers. Then

\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}-\prod_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}c_{k}^{(i)}\Big)(r_{i}-q_{i}),

where, for all
k=1,2,\dots,m-1,
each
c_{k}^{(i)},i=1,2,\dots m,
is a real number from the set

\{r_{1},r_{2},\dots r_{i-1},r_{i+1},\dots,r_{m},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{i-1},q_{i+1},\dots,q_{m}\}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let the parameter maps
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}
,
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}
(
\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}
) be Hölder continuous functions with constants
K_{h}
,
K_{\alpha}
and Hölder exponents
s_{h}
,
s_{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}}
respectively. Define
\|\alpha\|_{\infty}=\max\{\|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}\|_{\infty}:\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}\}
,
\delta=\frac{\|\alpha\|_{\infty}}{a}
and
s_{\alpha}=\min\{s_{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}}:\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}\}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

If
\|\alpha\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2^{N}}
, then
f
is a Hölder continuous function with exponent
s
, where
s=\min\{s_{h},s_{\alpha}\}.

(2)

If
\|\alpha\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}
, then
f
is a Hölder continuous function with exponent
s-\mu
for some
0<\mu<1.

(3)

If
\|\alpha\|_{\infty}>\frac{1}{2^{N}}
, then
f
is a Hölder continuous function with exponent
\lambda,
where
0<\lambda\leq s-1+\frac{\ln\|\alpha\|_{\infty}}{\ln a}<1.

Proof.

For any
(t,s),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime},
it is possible to find
m\geq 0
such that
t\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime}),~s\in K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(SG^{\prime\prime})
and

a^{m+1}\leq\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq a^{m}.

If
m=0,
we set
L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime})=SG^{\prime}
. Let
t,t^{\prime}\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime}).
Since
t\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime}),~s\in K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(SG^{\prime})
there exists elements
\bar{t}\in SG^{\prime}
,
\bar{s}\in SG^{\prime\prime}
such that the following conditions hold due to Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle t=L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(\overline{t})=a^{m}\overline{t}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}a^{h-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}},~~s=K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(\overline{s})=a^{m}\overline{s}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}a^{h-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}}

(2)

and

\displaystyle f(t,s)=

\displaystyle f(L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(\overline{t}),K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(\overline{s}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(t),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(s))\Big)f(\overline{t},\overline{s})

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s}))\Big)h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big).

From
(\ref{EQN1})
, we may write
\overline{t}
and
\overline{s}
as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{t}=a^{-m}\Big[t-\sum_{l=1}^{m}a^{l-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{l}}\Big]~\text{and}~\overline{s}=a^{-m}\Big[s-\sum_{l=1}^{m}a^{l-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}}\Big].

In view of Lemma
3.1
and the above equation, we get

\displaystyle L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t})=a^{-k}\Big[t-\sum_{l=1}^{m}a^{l-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{l}}\Big]+\sum_{l=1}^{m-k}a^{l-1}a_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k+l}}

and

\displaystyle K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})=a^{-k}\Big[s-\sum_{l=1}^{m}a^{l-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{l}}\Big]+\sum_{l=1}^{m-k}a^{l-1}b_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k+l}}.

Similarly, since
(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m}}(SG^{\prime\prime}),
there exists
(\overline{t^{\prime}},\overline{s^{\prime}})\in SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
such that
t^{\prime},s^{\prime}
and
f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})
have expressions similar to the above. Consequently,

\displaystyle\big|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg\|\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\eta_{k}}\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)\Big)~~f(\overline{t},\overline{s})

\displaystyle-\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k},\eta_{k}}\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big)~~f(\overline{t^{\prime}},\overline{s^{\prime}})\Bigg\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Bigg\|\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s}))\Big)h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}}))\Big)h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Big|\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s}))\Big)\Big|~~\Big\|h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)

\displaystyle-h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\Big|\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s}))\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}}))\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\big\|h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle+2(\|\alpha\|_{\infty})^{m}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Set
s=\min\big\{s_{h},s_{\alpha}\big\}.
Using
\|L_{\sigma^{r}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t})-L_{\sigma^{r}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}})\|_{2}=a^{-r}\|t-t^{\prime}\|_{2}
and Hölder continuity of
h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)-h_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{r}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{r}}\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq~K_{h}\Big\|\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)-\big(L_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{r-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big\|_{2}^{s_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{h}\Big[a^{-(r-1)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\Big]^{s_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{h}a^{-(r-1)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}.

Now, let
K_{\alpha}=\max\{K_{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}}:\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{N}\}
. Since
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
s_{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}}
and Hölder constant
K_{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\eta}}}
, in view of Lemma
3.2
, for
r\geq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)-\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\alpha_{\boldsymbol{\omega}_{k},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{k}}\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty})^{r-2}K_{\alpha}\Big\|\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s})\big)-\big(L_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\omega}_{m})}(\overline{t^{\prime}}),K_{\sigma^{k}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2}\dots\boldsymbol{\eta}_{m})}(\overline{s^{\prime}})\big)\Big\|_{2}^{s_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty})^{r-2}K_{\alpha}\Big[a^{-k}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\Big]^{s_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty})^{r-2}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}a^{-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}(\|\alpha\|_{\infty})^{r-2}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}\frac{a^{1-r}}{1-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{\alpha}\delta^{r-2}}{a(1-a)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}.

Using the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle\big|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\big|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}\big(\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\big)^{r-1}K_{h}a^{-(r-1)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{K_{\alpha}\|h\|_{\infty}\delta^{r-2}}{a(1-a)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}+2\big(\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\big)^{m}\|f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}\big(\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\big)^{r-1}K_{h}a^{-(r-1)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=2}^{m}\frac{K_{\alpha}\|h\|_{\infty}\delta^{r-2}}{a(1-a)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}+2\|f\|_{\infty}\delta^{m}a^{m}.

Since
a^{m+1}\leq\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2},
we have

\displaystyle~\big\|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\big\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{m}\delta^{(r-1)}K_{h}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=2}^{m}\frac{K_{\alpha}\|h\|_{\infty}\delta^{r-2}}{a(1-a)}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}+\frac{2\|f\|_{\infty}\delta^{m}}{a}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4K\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\delta^{r-1},

for a fixed suitable constant
K
.

Case 1:

If
\delta<1
, then we have

\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\delta^{r-1}\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}

and hence we obtain

|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\leq\frac{4K}{1-\delta}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}.

That is,
f
is Hölder continuous with exponent
s.

Case 2:

If
\delta=1
, then

\|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq 4K(m+1)\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}.

Since
\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq a^{m}<1,
we get
m\leq\ln(\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2})/\ln(a).
With the help of a known inequality:

0<-x^{\mu}\ln x\leq\dfrac{1}{\mu e},~\text{for}~0<x\leq 1~\text{ and}~0<\mu<1,

we select a suitable
0<\mu<1
such that

\displaystyle(m+1)\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{\ln(\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2})}{\ln a}\Big)\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle+\frac{-\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{\mu}~\ln(\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2})}{|\ln a|}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s-\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}+\frac{1}{\mu e|\ln a|}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s-\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{1}{\mu e~|\ln a|}\Big)\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s-\mu}.

Hence, we have

|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\leq 4K\Big(1+\dfrac{1}{\mu e|\ln a|}\Big)\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s-\mu}.

Case 3:

If
\delta>1
, then

|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\leq 4K\frac{\delta^{m+1}}{\delta-1}~~\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}.

We now consider a positive number
\lambda
with
0<\lambda<1
such that

\delta^{m+1}\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{s}\leq\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{\lambda}.

Further, one obtains

\lambda\leq s+\frac{(m+1)\ln\delta}{\ln(\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2})}.

Since
a^{m+1}\leq\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2},
we obtain
\dfrac{1}{\ln(\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2})}\leq\dfrac{1}{(m+1)\ln(a)}.
This in turn yields
\lambda\leq s+\dfrac{\ln\delta}{\ln a}=s-1+\frac{\ln\|\alpha\|_{\infty}}{\ln a}<1.
Therefore, we get

|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})|\leq~~\frac{4K}{\delta-1}~~\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{\lambda}.

Thus, the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

4.
Dimension of FIF

In this section, we focus on estimating fractal dimension such as Hausdorff dimension and box dimension of the constructed FIFs. Here, as usual we denote by
\dim_{H}(A)
and
\dim_{B}(A)
the Hausdorff dimension and box dimension of a set
A
respectively. For more details and definitions of these dimensions, we encourage the reader to see the book
[
11
]
of Falconer.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.3
, if
\|\alpha\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2^{N}}
then

2~\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}\leq\dim_{H}(Gr(f))\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}(Gr(f))\leq 1-s+2~\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}.

Proof.

Let us first define the maximum range of the function
f
over
X\subseteq SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
as
R_{f}[X]=\sup_{(t,s),(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\in X}|f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})|.

In view of Theorem
3.3
,
f
satisfies Hölder condition, we have

R_{f}[L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})]\leq\frac{c}{2^{ns}}~~\text{for every}~\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{n},

where
c
is suitable constant depending the Hölder constant of
f
and
s=\min\{s_{h},s_{\alpha}\}.
Suppose that
\delta=\frac{1}{2^{n}}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}.
If
N_{\delta}(Gr(f))
denotes the number of
\delta-
cubes that intersect graph of
f,
then

N_{\delta}(Gr(f))\leq 2.3^{n}.3^{n}+2^{n}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in I^{n}}R_{f}[L_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(SG^{\prime})\times K_{\boldsymbol{\eta}}(SG^{\prime\prime})].

We obtain
N_{\delta}(Gr(f))\leq 2.3^{n}.3^{n}+c2^{n(1-s)}3^{n}3^{n}.

Upper box-dimension of
Gr(f)
can be estimated in the following way

\overline{\lim}_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{\delta}(Gr(f))}{-\log\delta}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(2.3^{n}3^{n}+c2^{n(1-s)}3^{n}3^{n})}{\log 2^{n}}

which produces
\overline{\lim}_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log N_{\delta}(Gr(f))}{-\log\delta}\leq 1-s+2~\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}.

For the lower bound, we begin by defining a mapping
T:Gr(f)\rightarrow SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
by
T\big((t,s,f(t,s))\big)=(t,s).
Then

\displaystyle\|T\big((t,s,f(t,s))\big)-T\big((t^{\prime},s^{\prime},f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}))\big)\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\|(t,s)-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})\|_{2}^{2}+(f(t,s)-f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(t,s,f(t,s))-(t^{\prime},s^{\prime},f(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}))\|_{2},

implies that
T
is Lipschitz. Now, for any
(t,s)\in SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime}
, we can choose
(t,s,f(t,s))\in Gr(f)
such that
(t,s)=T\big((t,s,f(t,s))\big)
which implies the surjectiveness of
T
. Recall (Cf.
[
11
, Corollary 2.4(a)]
) that if
g:A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a Lipschitz function, then
\dim_{H}\big(g(A)\big)\leq\dim_{H}(A).
Using
[
11
, Corollary 7.4]
, it is immediate that
\dim_{H}\big(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})=2~\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}
. In view of the previous two observations, we get

\dim_{H}\big(Gr(f)\big)\geq\dim_{H}\big(T(Gr(f))\big)=\dim_{H}\big(SG^{\prime}\times SG^{\prime\prime})=2~\frac{\log 3}{\log 2},

proving the assertion.
∎

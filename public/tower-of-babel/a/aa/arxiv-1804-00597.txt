Modified Algebraic Bethe Ansatz: Twisted XXX Case
Samuel BELLIARD †‡, Nikita A. SLAVNOV § and Benoit VALLET ‡

Abstract
We prove the modified algebraic Bethe Ansatz characterization of the spectral problem for the closed XXX Heisenberg spin chain with an arbitrary twist and arbitrary positive (half)-integer spin at each site of the chain. We provide two basis to characterize the spectral problem and two families of inhomogeneous Baxter T-Q equations. The two families satisfy an inhomogeneous quantum Wronskian equation.

{\rm U}(1)
symmetry, as the XXZ Heisenberg spin chain on the segment with the most general integrable boundary condition
[
27

23

2
The Yangian of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{gl}_{2}}
and Bethe subalgebra

Let us recall the simplest rational
R
-matrix, which is an element of
\operatorname{End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
:

\displaystyle R(u)=\frac{u}{c}I+P.

Here
c
is a constant,
I=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}E_{ii}\otimes E_{jj}
is the identity operator on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
P=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}
is the permutation operator on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
(E_{ij})_{kl}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
. This
R
-matrix is a solution of the Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle R_{ab}(u-v)R_{ac}(u-w)R_{bc}(v-w)=R_{bc}(v-w)R_{ac}(u-w)R_{ab}(u-v).

Here the
R
-matrix is understood as an element of
\operatorname{End}(V_{a}\otimes V_{b}\otimes V_{c})
with
V=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Namely,
R_{ab}(u)=R(u)\otimes\mathbb{I}
,
R_{bc}(u)=\mathbb{I}\otimes R(u)
,
R_{ac}(u)=P_{bc}R_{ab}(u)P_{bc}
, and
\mathbb{I}
is the identity operator in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

The
R
-matrix (
2
) is
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
-invariant, and thus,

\displaystyle[R_{ab}(u),K_{a}K_{b}]=0,

for any matrix
K\in\operatorname{End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
.

Starting from this
R
-matrix, we can define a quantum group algebra, so called Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
. For this, we introduce a monodromy matrix

\displaystyle T(u)=\left(\begin{matrix}t_{11}(u)&t_{12}(u)\\
t_{21}(u)&t_{22}(u)\end{matrix}\right).

This matrix satisfies the RTT relation

\displaystyle R_{ab}(u-v)T_{a}(u)T_{b}(v)=T_{b}(v)T_{a}(u)R_{ab}(u-v).

(2.1)

Equation (
2.1
) encodes the commutation relations of the entries
t_{ij}(u)
(see Appendix
A
), which generate the Yangian algebra.

The transfer matrix

\displaystyle t(u)=\mathop{\rm Tr}_{a}\big(K_{a}T_{a}(u)\big)

generates a commutative subalgebra of the Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
, i.e.,
[t(u),t(v)]=0
. Its expansion as a series in
u
leads to the conserved charges of the model that depend on the specific representation. To perform the MABA we need to consider a finite dimensional representation. However, this is not necessarily needed for the usual ABA.

Let us define a highest weight representation by
\mathcal{V}(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
, where
\lambda_{i}(u)
are some complex valued functions, and the highest weight vector
|0\rangle
is defined by

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)|0\rangle=\lambda_{i}(u)|0\rangle,\qquad t_{21}(u)|0\rangle=0.

(2.2)

On the other hand, we can define a lowest weight representation
\mathcal{V}(\lambda_{2}(u),\lambda_{1}(u))
, and the lowest weight vector
|\hat{0}\rangle
is defined by

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)|\hat{0}\rangle=\lambda_{3-i}(u)|\hat{0}\rangle,\qquad t_{12}(u)|\hat{0}\rangle=0.

(2.3)

This provides enough tools to consider the usual ABA but not to accomplish the MABA.
In the latter case we need to explicitly fix the representation to be finite dimensional. All of them are given by the highest (lowest) weight representation
[
14
]
.
More precisely, we consider finite dimensional representations with an arbitrary positive (half)-integer spin
s_{i}
for each site of the chain.
In this case the weight vectors and the functions
\lambda_{i}(u)
can be constructed from the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
Lie algebra’s spin
s
finite dimensional representation and the evaluation representation of the Yangian
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
.

The
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
Lie algebra is generated by

\{S^{z},S^{-},S^{+},I\}

that satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[S^{+},S^{-}]=2S^{z},\qquad[S^{z},S^{\pm}]=\pm S^{\pm},\qquad[S^{i},I]=0.

(2.4)

The spin
s
finite dimensional representation of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
Lie algebra is given by the highest (lowest) weight representation. Let us define the
highest weight vector of spin
s
representation by
|s\rangle
. The action of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
generators
are given by

\displaystyle S^{z}|s\rangle=s|s\rangle,\qquad S^{+}|s\rangle=0,\qquad(S^{-})^{2s+1}|s\rangle=0,\qquad I|s\rangle=|s\rangle.

The action of the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
generators on the lowest weight vector
|{-}s\rangle
of spin
s
representation is

\displaystyle S^{z}|{-}s\rangle=-s|{-}s\rangle,\qquad S^{-}|{-}s\rangle=0,\qquad(S^{+})^{2s+1}|{-}s\rangle=0,\qquad I|{-}s\rangle=|{-}s\rangle.

The lowest and the highest vector are related by the formula

\displaystyle|{-}s\rangle=\frac{(S^{-})^{2s}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2s}\sqrt{j(2s-(j-1))}}|s\rangle.

To construct the representation of the Yangian of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
we take the highest (lowest) weight vector as a tensor product of
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
highest (lowest) weight vectors with different spins. They are given by

\displaystyle|0\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|s_{i}\rangle,\qquad|\hat{0}\rangle=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|{-}s_{i}\rangle.

The associated weight functions are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u-\theta_{i}+c\big(s_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\big)}{c},\qquad\lambda_{2}(u)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{u-\theta_{i}-c\big(s_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\big)}{c}.

The evaluation representation is given by the Lax operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ai}(u)=\left(\begin{matrix}\dfrac{u}{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}+S_{i}^{3}&S_{i}^{-}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
S_{i}^{+}&\dfrac{u}{c}+\dfrac{1}{2}-S_{i}^{3}\end{matrix}\right)_{a},

in each site of the spin chain (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
). Due to the commutation relations (
2.4
), the Lax operator satisfies the RTT relation (
2.1
) and the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariance
[
28
]

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}_{ai}(u),K_{a}{\mathcal{K}}_{i}]=0.

Here

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{i}=k_{0}\exp{\big(k^{+}S_{i}^{+}+k^{-}S_{i}^{-}+2k_{3}S_{i}^{3}\big)},

\displaystyle K=k_{0}\exp{\big(k^{+}\sigma^{+}+k^{-}\sigma^{-}+k_{3}\sigma^{3}\big)}=\left(\begin{matrix}\tilde{\kappa}&\kappa^{+}\\
\kappa^{-}&\kappa\end{matrix}\right),

and
\sigma^{\pm}
,
\sigma^{z}
are Pauli matrices.

The monodromy matrix for
N
arbitrary positive (half)-integer spins
\bar{s}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{N}\}
is then given by

\displaystyle T_{a}(u)=\mathcal{L}_{a1}(u-\theta_{1})\cdots\mathcal{L}_{aN}(u-\theta_{N}),

(2.5)

where
\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{N}\}
are inhomogeneity parameters. The monodromy matrix contains the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
Lie algebra generators
\{S^{+},S^{-},S^{z},I\}
realized as
S^{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}S^{\alpha}_{i}
,

\displaystyle T(u)=\left(\frac{u}{c}\right)^{N}+\left(\left(\begin{matrix}\dfrac{I+S^{z}}{2}&S^{-}\\
S^{+}&\dfrac{I-S^{z}}{2}\end{matrix}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\right)\left(\frac{u}{c}\right)^{N-1}+\cdots.

Using the RTT relation we can extract the relation between the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
Lie algebra generators and the
t_{ij}(u)
, in particular we have:

\displaystyle[S^{z},t_{ii}(u)]=0,\qquad[S^{z},t_{12}(u)]=t_{12}(u),\qquad[S^{z},t_{21}(u)]=-t_{21}(u).

We say that the transfer matrix has a
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry if
[S^{z},t(u)]=0
, that is, in the case of a diagonal twist
K
. In fact, if
\mathop{\rm Det}(K)\neq 0
, then due to the global
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariance we can always restore the
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry, by diagonalizing the twist matrix and doing a proper similarity transformation
[
26
]
, to perform usual ABA
4
4

4

In the case of the XXZ models one should consider a more sophisticated face-vertex transformation of the twist (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
30
]
and references therein).
. Here, as we want to study the MABA, we do not use this possibility.

Starting form the highest or the lowest weight vector we can construct two possible basis, that we call Bethe vectors, to span the Hilbert space of the model
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{C}^{2s_{i}+1}
. Let
\bar{u}
denote a set of arbitrary complex parameters
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{M}\}
with cardinality
\#\bar{u}=M
, and let
S=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}2s_{i}
. The representation space of the Yangian can be spanned by

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u})=\prod_{i=1}^{M}t_{12}(u_{i})|0\rangle

(2.6)

with
\#\bar{u}=M
and
M=0,1,\dots,S
. Or equivalently, by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{\hat{M}}(\bar{v})=\prod_{i=1}^{\hat{M}}t_{21}(v_{i})|\hat{0}\rangle

(2.7)

with
\#\bar{v}=\hat{M}
and
\hat{M}=0,1,\dots,S
.

These two basis are related by a morphism of the Yangian. Let us consider the mappings

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(T(u))=T(-u),\qquad\psi_{2}(T(u))=T^{t}(u),

where
t
is the usual transposition in the auxiliary space,
A_{ij}^{t}=A_{ji}
. They define anti-morphism of the RTT relation (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
). Taking the composition of these mappings we obtain an automorphism of the Yangian

\displaystyle\phi(T(u))=\psi_{1}\circ\psi_{2}(T(u))=T^{t}(-u).

(2.8)

This mapping relates the two different bases of the representation space of the Yangian. Indeed we have

\displaystyle\phi\big(\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u})\big)=\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{M}(-\bar{u}),

where we have to apply the rule
\phi(\lambda_{i}(u))=\lambda_{3-i}(-u)
and
\phi(|0\rangle)=|\hat{0}\rangle
to preserve the structure of the action. Thus,
\phi
relates the highest weight and the lowest weight representations. We remark also that
\phi(t(u))=t(-u)
, and therefore the action of the transfer matrix on one of the basis defines the action on the other.

3
Algebraic Bethe ansatz and quantum Wronskian equation

Let us recall the algebraic Bethe ansatz
[
6
,
15
,
16
]
for generalized
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariant integrable models with
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry. For this we introduce new notation. Let us define rational functions

\displaystyle g(u,v)=\frac{c}{u-v},\qquad f(u,v)=1+g(u,v)=\frac{u-v+c}{u-v},

and a shorthand notation for the products of the
f
functions and the
g
functions

\displaystyle g(z,\bar{u})=\prod_{i=1}^{M}g(z,u_{i}),\qquad f(\bar{u},z)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}f(u_{i},z),\qquad f(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i})=\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{M}f(u_{i},u_{j}),

(3.1)

where
\bar{u}_{i}=\{\bar{u}\setminus u_{i}\}
is the set complementary to the element
u_{i}
.

The ABA allows to solve the spectral problem for the transfer matrix

\displaystyle t_{d}(z)=\mathop{\rm Tr}_{a}\big(K_{a}^{(d)}T_{a}(z)\big)=\tilde{\kappa}t_{11}(z)+\kappa t_{22}(z),

with the diagonal twist matrix

\displaystyle K^{(d)}=\left(\begin{matrix}\tilde{\kappa}&0\\
0&\kappa\end{matrix}\right).

(3.2)

In this case we have the
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry regarding to the operator
S^{z}
, i.e.,
[S^{z},t_{d}(z)]=0
and simple eigenstates are given by the highest and lowest weight vectors
\{|0\rangle,|\hat{0}\rangle\}

\displaystyle t_{d}(z)|0\rangle=(\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{1}(z)+\kappa\lambda_{2}(z))|0\rangle,\qquad t_{d}(z)|\hat{0}\rangle=(\kappa\lambda_{1}(z)+\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{2}(z))|\hat{0}\rangle.

(3.3)

We remark that for
K^{(d)}\propto I
, i.e.,
\kappa=\tilde{\kappa}
, that corresponds to the periodic boundary condition, both eigenvalues are the same and the model has additional symmetries (full
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariance).
We should recall that the method applies for any highest weight representation (
2.2
) without specifying the functions
\lambda_{i}(u)
. In particular it also applies for an infinite dimensional representation as in the case of the Bose gas model.

We have seen in the previous section that the space of states can be spanned by Bethe vectors (
2.6
) for a given set of generic variables
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{M}\}
of the cardinality
\#\bar{u}=M
. Due to the
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry the spectrum is decomposed regarding to subspaces of the total spin
S^{z}
. The Bethe vectors (
2.6
) form a basis for the total spin

\displaystyle S^{z}\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u})=\left(\frac{S}{2}-M\right)\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u}).

Then
M
is a given quantum number and we can diagonalize the transfer matrix for each invariant subspace labeled by
M
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
15
]
)
.

The action of the transfer matrix onto the Bethe vector (
2.6
) is given by

\displaystyle t_{d}(z)\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u})=\Lambda_{d}^{M}(z,\bar{u})\mathbb{B}^{M}(\bar{u})+\sum_{i=1}^{M}g(u_{i},z)E_{d}^{M}(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i})t_{12}(z)\mathbb{B}^{M-1}(\bar{u}_{i}),

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}^{M}(z,\bar{u})=\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{1}(z)f(\bar{u},z)+\kappa\lambda_{2}(z)f(z,\bar{u}),

\displaystyle E_{d}^{M}(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i})=-\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{1}(u_{i})f(\bar{u}_{i},u_{i})+\kappa\lambda_{2}(u_{i})f(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i}).

If the set
\bar{u}
satisfies the Bethe equations

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{1}(u_{i})}{\kappa\lambda_{2}(u_{i})}=\frac{f(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i})}{f(\bar{u}_{i},u_{i})}=-\prod_{j=1}^{M}\frac{u_{i}-u_{j}+c}{u_{i}-u_{j}-c},

for
i=0,1,\dots,M
, then the Bethe vectors (
2.6
) are eigenvectors of the transfer matrix and are called on-shell Bethe vectors.

Proof.

Multiplication of the original monodromy matrix by the diagonal twist reduces to the redefinition of the functions
\lambda_{i}(u)
. Since within the framework of ABA these functions are not fixed, the proof then follows from the standard considerations of the ABA
[
15
]
. It is based on the commutation relations of the operators
t_{ij}(u)
(see Appendix
A
) and the action on the highest weight vector (
2.2
).
∎

Using the mapping
\phi
(
2.8
) we can find the transfer matrix action on the Bethe vectors constructed from the lowest weight vector. This gives another parametrization for the spectrum of the transfer matrix.

Corollary 3.2
.

The action of the transfer matrix onto the Bethe vector
\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{\hat{M}}(\bar{v})
is given by

\displaystyle t_{d}(z)\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{\hat{M}}(\bar{v})=\hat{\Lambda}_{d}^{M}(z,\bar{v})\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{\hat{M}}(\bar{v})+\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{M}}g(z,v_{i})\hat{E}_{d}^{\hat{M}}(v_{i},\bar{v}_{i})t_{21}(z)\hat{\mathbb{B}}^{\hat{M}-1}(\bar{v}_{i}),

where

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}_{d}^{\hat{M}}(z,\bar{v})=\kappa\lambda_{1}(z)f(\bar{v},z)+\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{2}(z)f(z,\bar{v}),

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{d}^{\hat{M}}(v_{i},\bar{v}_{i})=-\kappa\lambda_{1}(v_{i})f(\bar{v}_{i},v_{i})+\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{2}(v_{i})f(v_{i},\bar{v}_{i}).

If the set
\bar{v}
satisfies the second family of Bethe equations

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\lambda_{1}(v_{i})}{\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{2}(v_{i})}=\frac{f(v_{i},\bar{v}_{i})}{f(\bar{v}_{i},v_{i})}=-\prod_{j=1}^{\hat{M}}\frac{v_{i}-v_{j}+c}{v_{i}-v_{j}-c}

for
i=0,1,\dots,\hat{M}
, then the on-shell Bethe vectors (
2.7
) are eigenvectors of the transfer matrix.

We can rewrite the two results of the previous section in terms of Baxter Q-polynomials. Let us define the polynomials

\displaystyle q_{+}^{M}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{M}\frac{z-u_{i}}{c}=\big(g(z,\bar{u})\big)^{-1}

and

\displaystyle q_{-}^{\hat{M}}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{\hat{M}}\frac{z-v_{i}}{c}=\big(g(z,\bar{v})\big)^{-1}.

This allows us to rewrite the spectral problem in terms of Baxter T-Q equations

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}(z)q_{+}^{M}(z)=\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{1}(z)q_{+}^{M}(z-c)+\kappa\lambda_{2}(z)q_{+}^{M}(z+c),

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d}(z)q_{-}^{\hat{M}}(z)=\kappa\lambda_{1}(z)q_{-}^{\hat{M}}(z-c)+\tilde{\kappa}\lambda_{2}(z)q_{-}^{\hat{M}}(z+c).

(3.5)

The two characterizations of the spectrum by Baxter T-Q equation satisfy a quantum Wronskian condition.

Theorem 3.3
.

The two Baxter Q-polynomials
q_{+}^{M}(z)
and
q_{-}^{\hat{M}}(z)
are related by the quantum Wronskian equation
[
25
]

\displaystyle W_{d}(z)-W_{d}(z+c)=0,

(3.6)

with

\displaystyle W_{d}(z)=\frac{\tilde{\kappa}q_{+}^{\hat{M}}(z-c)q_{-}^{M}(z)-\kappa q_{+}^{\hat{M}}(z)q_{-}^{M}(z-c)}{\lambda(z)},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}(z)}{\lambda_{2}(z)}=\frac{\lambda(z+c)}{\lambda(z)},\qquad\lambda(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{k=1}^{2s_{i}}\frac{z-\theta_{i}+c\big(s_{i}-k+\frac{1}{2}\big)}{c}.

This implies
\hat{M}+M=S=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}2s_{i}
and
W_{d}(z)=(\tilde{\kappa}-\kappa)
.

Proof.

For a given eigenvalue
\Lambda_{d}(z)

5
Conclusion

In this paper we presented several proofs of the new steps needed to perform the algebraic Bethe ansatz for the models without
{\rm U}(1)
symmetry. We showed that the appearance of the new term in the Baxter T-Q equation follows from the analysis of the product of the modified creation operators and that it is a general property of the null twisted transfer matrix. It is not necessary to consider the action on a weight vector.

Then the Bethe ansatz characterization of the spectral problem of the XXX Heisenberg spin chain with an arbitrary twist and arbitrary positive (half)-integer spin at each site of the chain is fully understood by means of the MABA. We also derived, for arbitrary positive (half)-integer spins, a modified quantum Wronskian equation that relates two different characterizations of the spectral problem. It should be of interest to relate the modified quantum Wronskian equation with Hirota equation
[
17
]
. Moreover finding the numerical solutions of the Bethe equations remains a challenging open problem (see recent development in
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
), and modified quantum Wronskian equation can be used to address it.

The new actions of the modified operators on the weight vectors deserve to be studied in details. They also appear in the context of the separation of variable by introduction of the
B^{\rm good}
operators
[
18
]
(see also recent work of two of the authors
[
8
]
that considers the
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
case from the point of view of the ABA). These actions provide formulas for the development of the modified Bethe vector in terms of the original
t_{12}(u)
creation operator. The multiple action of the modified operators should lead to generalization of the results of
[
6
]
. These multiple action formulas are very useful for the calculation of Bethe vector’s scalar products, form factors, and correlation functions and will be given in a forthcoming publication. In the framework of the MABA for the twisted XXX models, these multiple actions lead to new compact formulas for the scalar products of the modified Bethe vectors and should open a path to prove the modified determinant formulas conjectured in
[
7
]
.

An important step should be to achieve the MABA for the twisted XXX
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
spin chain and to other types of boundary conditions or other higher rank algebras. For a lower block twist, there is no new difficulties, as the first step of the nested Bethe ansatz preserves the
{\rm U}(1)

Appendix A
Commutation relations of the
\boldsymbol{t_{ij}(u)}
and multiple actions

on the Bethe vector

The RTT relation (
2.1
) yields the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle t_{ij}(v)t_{ij}(u)=t_{ij}(u)t_{ij}(v),

\displaystyle t_{ij}(v)t_{ik}(u)=f(u,v)t_{ik}(u)t_{ij}(v)+g(v,u)t_{ik}(v)t_{ij}(u),

\displaystyle t_{ij}(v)t_{kj}(u)=f(v,u)t_{kj}(u)t_{ij}(v)+g(u,v)t_{kj}(v)t_{ij}(u).

They imply the following actions on the products of
M
operators:

\displaystyle t_{ij}(v)t_{ik}(\bar{u})=f(\bar{u},v)t_{ik}(\bar{u})t_{ij}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{M}g(v,u_{i})f(\bar{u}_{i},u_{i})t_{ik}(v)t_{ik}(\bar{u}_{i})t_{ij}(u_{i}),

\displaystyle t_{ij}(v)t_{kj}(\bar{u})=f(v,\bar{u})t_{kj}(\bar{u})t_{ij}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{M}g({u_{i},v})f(u_{i},\bar{u}_{i})t_{{kj}}(v)t_{{kj}}(\bar{u}_{i})t_{{ij}}(u_{i}).

(A.1)

The same commutation relations are valid for the modified operators
\nu_{ij}(u)
.

Appendix B
SoV basis for the modified creation operator
\boldsymbol{\nu_{12}(z)}

Let us construct the Separation of variables basis
[
24
]
of the modified creation operator
\nu_{12}(z)
. We assume that the inhomogeneity parameters
\bar{\theta}
are generic complex numbers and that the product
AB
has non zero entries.

Let us introduce a vector

\displaystyle|y\rangle={\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{1}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{2}\cdots{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{N}|\hat{0}\rangle,

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=a_{0}\exp{\big(a^{+}S_{i}^{+}+a^{-}S_{i}^{-}+2a_{3}S_{i}^{3}\big)},

and the parameters
\{a_{0},a^{+},a^{-},a_{3}\}
are fixed by the equality

\displaystyle A=\sqrt{\mu}\left(\begin{matrix}1&\dfrac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{-}}\\
\dfrac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{+}}&1\end{matrix}\right)=a_{0}\exp{\big(a^{+}\sigma^{+}+a^{-}\sigma^{-}+a_{3}\sigma^{3}\big)}.

Using
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariance we can show that

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)|y\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\kappa^{-}}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})\lambda_{2}(z)|y\rangle.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)|y\rangle=\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{21}AT(z)B){\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{1}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{2}\cdots{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{N}|\hat{0}\rangle={\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{1}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{2}\cdots{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{N}\mathop{\rm Tr}(E_{21}T(z)AB)|\hat{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\hphantom{\nu_{12}(z)|y\rangle}{}=(AB)_{12}\lambda_{2}(z)|y\rangle.

Consider a set of vectors

\displaystyle|Y(\bar{k})\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}\nu_{22}\left(\theta_{i}+c\left(s_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-j\right)\right)|y\rangle,

where
\bar{k}
is a set of
N
integers such that
k_{i}\in\{0,1,\dots,2s_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,N
. Then it follows from the commutation relation (
A.1
) that

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{22}(u-cj)=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}f(z,u-cj)\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{22}(u-cj)\right)\nu_{12}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+g(u-c,z)\left(\prod_{j=2}^{n}f(u-c,u-cj)\right)\nu_{22}(z)\left(\prod_{j=2}^{n}\nu_{22}(u-cj)\right)\nu_{12}(u-c).

Here we have used
f(x-c,x)=0
and
\lambda_{2}\big(\theta_{i}+c\big(s_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\big)\big)=0
. Hence, the action of
\nu_{12}(z)
on
|Y(\bar{k})\rangle
reads

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)|Y(\bar{k})\rangle=\frac{\mu}{\kappa^{-}}(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})\frac{F(z)}{\Lambda_{12}(z,\bar{k})}|Y(\bar{k})\rangle,

where
F(z)
given by (
4.16
) and

\displaystyle\Lambda_{12}\big(z,\bar{k}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}h(z-c,\theta_{i}+c\left(s_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-j\right)\right)\left(\prod_{j=k_{i}+1}^{2s_{i}}h(z,\theta_{i}+c\left(s_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-j\right)\right),

with
h(u,v)=f(u,v)/g(u,v)
.

Remark B.1
.

Using
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
invariance we can show that

\displaystyle\big\langle\hat{0}|{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{1}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{2}\cdots{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}_{N}|Y(\bar{k})\big\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{i}}(AB)_{22}\lambda_{1}\left(\theta_{i}+c\left(s_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-j\right)\right)\neq 0.

Thus, all the vectors under consideration are non zero.

Thus, we have constructed
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}(2s_{i}+1)
different eigenvectors of the modified creation operator with different eigenvalues. As the vectors are independent and the representation is finite (being of dimension
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}(2s_{i}+1)
), this implies that the modified creation operator has simple spectrum and it is invertible. Thus, we have

\displaystyle F(z)(\nu_{12}(z))^{-1}|Y(\bar{k})\rangle=\frac{\kappa^{-}}{\mu(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})}\Lambda_{12}(z,\bar{k})|Y(\bar{k})\rangle.

Therefore,
F(z)(\nu_{12}(z))^{-1}
is a polynomial in
z
of degree
z^{S}
.

Observe that the modified creation operator

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)=\mathop{\rm Tr}(V_{12}T(z)),\qquad V_{12}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{-}}&\dfrac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{(\kappa^{-})^{2}}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
1&\dfrac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{-}}\end{pmatrix}

is a null twisted transfer matrix, because
\mathop{\rm Det}(V_{12})=0
. Since we can consider
\rho_{i}
,
\mu
, and
\kappa^{-}
as free parameters in our construction, the result applies to any null twisted transfer matrix.

Towards inductive proofs in algebraic combinatorics
Ted Dobson

Abstract
. We introduce a new class of transitive permutation groups which properly contains the automorphism groups of vertex-transitive graphs and digraphs. We then give a sufficient condition for a quotient of this family to remain in the family, showing that relatively straightforward induction arguments may possibly be used to solve problems in this family, and consequently for symmetry questions about vertex-transitive digraphs. As an example of this, for p p an odd prime, we use induction to determine the Sylow p p -subgroups of transitive groups of degree p n p^{n} that contain a regular cyclic subgroup in this family. This is enough information to determine the automorphism groups of circulant digraphs of order p n p^{n} .

Primary 05E18, Secondary 05E30

For many problems on vertex-transitive graphs, a natural approach to take for the solution is induction. For example, suppose we want to determine the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
of a Cayley digraph
\Gamma
of a fixed group
G
. First, if the automorphism group is primitive, one can use the Classification of the Finite Simple Groups together with the O’Nan-Scott Theorem, and perhaps determine
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. In practice, there are many results in the literature that list all primitive groups that have certain orders or properties (see
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
, for example). In many cases, these known results are all that is needed to determine
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. Obviously, induction is not used in this analysis. However, if
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
is not primitive, then it has a nontrivial block system
{\mathcal{B}}
, and we may use the Embedding Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.1]
of Kalužnin and Krasner to embed
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
into a permutation group wreath product, say
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
. We have thus reduced our larger problem of what is
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
into three problems with smaller groups. Namely, what are
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, and, how does one put them together to obtain
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
? Ideally, the groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
would be the automorphism groups of smaller vertex-transitive graphs or digraphs. However, examples are known where
G_{1}
is not the automorphism group of a vertex-transitive graph or digraph
[
4
]
, and examples are known where
G_{2}
is not the automorphism group of a vertex-transitive graph or digraph
[
14
, Corollary 4.5.8]
. So while induction can theoretically get us started towards finding
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
, by itself it fails immediately as there is no general way of knowing if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are the automorphism groups of a graph or digraph.

Nonetheless, there are two inductive approaches that are used to study symmetry questions regarding vertex-transitive graphs and digraphs. These are Cheryl Praeger’s normal quotient lemma
[
26
]
and graph covers (see
[
14
, Section 5.5]
and the references there). These techniques are widely used by many authors, but have their own weaknesses. The normal quotient lemma, for example, only applies to a small number of vertex-transitive graphs, namely those whose automorphism group is arc-transitive as well, and also does not apply to bipartite graphs. Graph covers only apply to vertex-transitive graphs with a block system of a subgroup of the automorphism group with a proper intransitive semiregular subgroup (although we comment that Primoz Potočnik and Micael Toledo
[
25
]
have recently removed this restriction provided more information is known). Additionally, graph covers usually produce many different graphs whose quotient is the quotient one began with, and it may not be practical to determine which one of these graphs is the original graph.

In this paper, we introduce a new inductive technique for questions regarding the automorphism group of vertex-transitive digraphs. We introduce a new family of permutation groups which can be conveniently placed in Wielandt’s
k
-closed hierarchy (although not perfectly), and not only contains the automorphism group of all graphs and digraphs, but all
2
-closed groups. For this new family, we give in Theorem
2.3
a sufficient condition for the quotient of a group in this class to be in the class. This can be interpreted as a sufficient condition for induction, as outlined above, to at least have the potential to be used. To demonstrate that this can work in practice, we use this sufficient condition to inductively determine the Sylow
p
-subgroups of transitive groups in this family that contain a regular cyclic subgroup of odd prime-power order. This will give the Sylow
p
-subgroups of transitive groups of circulant digraphs of odd prime-power order. We remark that this is enough information to determine the automorphism groups of circulant digraphs of odd prime-power order, although for length reasons this will be done elsewhere
[
10
]
. In other papers, we will begin to explore applications to the Cayley isomorphism problem
[
12
]
, a generalization of the Polycirculant conjecture
[
13
]
, and classifications of vertex-transitive digraphs of certain orders
[
11
]
.

There is also an important philosophical idea behind this work. The original idea behind this work is that if one can prove results regarding the automorphism group of some combinatorial object, then there must be some permutation group theoretic properties that the object imposes on its automorphism group, and rather than study the automorphism group itself, it may be useful to study permutation groups which have the group theoretic properties of the automorphism group. That is what is done here, although the inspiration
[
2
, Lemma 2]
for the class of groups considered in this paper had to be modified to obtain the sufficient condition given in Theorem
2.3
. This also makes clear where algebraic tools are used and where combinatorial ideas are necessary.

We should also point out that our new class of permutation groups is broad enough to not only contain automorphism groups of vertex-transitive digraphs, but also automorphism groups of other combinatorial objects. In fact, the automorphism group of a transitive “directed and colored
1
-intersecting set system” (see Definition
3.1
) are also contained in our new class of groups (Theorem
3.6
), and combinatorial configurations are contained in the family of
1
-intersecting set systems. Thus this work should also simplify proofs and provide new lines of attack for problems on these combinatorial objects as well. In fact, it was recently shown
[
23
]
that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is a CI-group with respect to the class of symmetric configurations. In
[
12
]
, we will not only greatly generalize this result, but provide a shorter proof of the generalization.

1.
A refinement of the notion of
k
-closed groups

We start with Wielandt’s notion of
k
-closure.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a set and
k\geq 1
an integer. A
k
-ary relational structure
on
X
is an ordered pair
(X,U)
, where
U\subseteq X^{k}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}X
. An

of a
k
-ary relational structure

is a bijection
g\colon X\to X
such that
g(U)=U
. The set of all automorphisms of
X
is a group, called the

of
X
, and denoted
{\rm Aut}(X)
.

A group
G\leq S_{X}
is called
k
-closed
if
G
is the intersection of the automorphism groups of some set of
k
-ary relational structures. The
k
-closure of
G
, denoted
G^{(k)}
, is the intersection of all
k
-closed subgroups of
S_{X}
that contain
G
.

Clearly
G^{(k)}
is
k
-closed. There are other, alternative definitions of a
k
-closed group, and our next goal is to see one of these.

Definition 1.2
.

A
color
k
-ary relational structure
is a
k
-ary relational structure
(X,U)
in which each element of
U
has been assigned a color. An
automorphism
of a color
k
-ary relational structure

is a bijection
g\colon X\to X
such that an element

is colored with color

if and only if
g(u)\in U
is colored with color
i
. The
automorphism group
of
W
, denoted

, is the group of all automorphisms of
W
.

As we may distinguish different
k
-ary relations by coloring them with different colors, a group

is

-closed if and only if it is the automorphism group of a color
k
-ary relational structure.

We now discuss our refinement of Wielandt’s notion of
k
-closed groups for transitive permutation groups, and start with the property that the automorphism groups of digraphs and graphs has which we will use in the proof of our main result. We will need some relatively standard terms in permutation group theory first.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
with orbit

induces a permutation on
{\mathcal{O}}
by restricting the domain of

to

. We denote the resulting permutation in

by

. The group

is the
transitive constituent
of
G
on
{\mathcal{O}}
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
be a set and
G\leq S_{X}
be transitive. A subset
B\subseteq X
is a

of

if whenever
g\in G

g(B)\cap B=\emptyset
or
B
. If

for some

, then

is a

. Any other block is nontrivial. Note that if

is a block of

, then

is also a block of

for every

, and is called a
conjugate block of

. The set of all blocks conjugate to

is a partition of

Definition 1.5
.

Let
X
be a set and

be transitive. If

, then the set of orbits of

is a block system
{\mathcal{B}}
of

. We say

is a

of

Definition 1.6
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with a block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. By
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
we denote the subgroup of

that fixes each

{\mathcal{B}}
set-wise. That is,
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})=\{g\in G:g(B)=B{\rm\ for\ all\ }B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is another block system of

and each block of
{\mathcal{B}}
is (properly) contained within a block of
{\mathcal{C}}
, we write (
{\mathcal{B}}\prec{\mathcal{C}}
)
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
, and say
{\mathcal{B}}

refines

{\mathcal{C}}
. We denote the induced action of

on the block system
{\mathcal{B}}
by

, and the action of an element

on
{\mathcal{B}}
by

. That is,
g/{\mathcal{B}}(B)=B^{\prime}
if and only if

, and

. If
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
, then

has a block system
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
whose blocks are the set of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
whose union are blocks of
{\mathcal{C}}
. In this case, we denote the set of blocks of

contained in

by

. Hence
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}=\{C/{\mathcal{B}}:C\in{\mathcal{C}}\}
.

It is easy to see that
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
is a normal subgroup of

. We are now ready to prove the preliminary results which will be needed for the definition of the new class of permutation groups.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
, and
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
K_{1},K_{2}\leq{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
such that
K_{i}^{B}=1
and
K_{i}^{B^{\prime}}
is trivial or transitive for every
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
,

. Then
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}^{B}=1
, and
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}^{B^{\prime}}
is trivial or transitive for every
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Consequently, there exists a maximal subgroup
K(B)
of
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
such that
K(B)^{B}=1
and
K(B)^{B^{\prime}}
is either trivial or transitive for every
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

Proof.

As
K_{1},K_{2}\leq{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
,
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}\leq{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
. As every element of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
is the identity on
B
,
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}^{B}=1
. Let
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. If
K_{1}^{B^{\prime}}=1
and
K_{2}^{B^{\prime}}=1
, then analogous to

,
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}^{B^{\prime}}=1
. Otherwise, at least one of
K_{1}^{B^{\prime}}
and
K_{2}^{B^{\prime}}
is transitive, in which case
{\langle}K_{1},K_{2}{\rangle}^{B^{\prime}}
is transitive. It is now clear that
K(B)
as in the statement exists. It is simply the subgroup generated by all subgroups
K\leq{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
such that
K^{B}=1
and
K^{B^{\prime}}=1
or is transitive for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Definition 1.8
.

We call the subgroup
K(B)
of
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
the
wreath
stabilizer of
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and denote it by
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
.

The name “wreath” stabilizer is chosen because if
G={\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
with
\Gamma
a vertex-transitive graph, then the wreath stabilizer will give for which blocks

we must have that every element of
B
is adjacent to every element of
B^{\prime}
or no element of
B
is adjacent to every element of
B^{\prime}
. This situation is reminiscent of when

can be written as a nontrivial wreath product. Our next result shows that the wreath stabilizer does act like a stabilizer.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and
g\in G
such that
g(B)=B^{\prime}
. Then
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})=g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)g^{-1}
.

Proof.

As
g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)g^{-1}
fixes every element of
B^{\prime}
, we see
g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)g^{-1}\leq{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})
. Similarly,
g^{-1}{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})g\leq{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
. Thus
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})\leq g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)g^{-1}
, and so
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)=g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})g^{-1}
.
∎

Definition 1.10
.

Let
G
be a transitive group that has a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. Define a relation
\equiv
on
{\mathcal{B}}
by
B\equiv B^{\prime}
if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})
.

Note that

means that
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)^{B^{\prime}}
is transitive.

Lemma 1.11
.

The relation
\equiv
is an equivalence relation on
{\mathcal{B}}
, the set
{\mathcal{E}}
of the unions of the equivalence classes of
\equiv
is a block system of
G
, and
{\mathcal{B}}
is a refinement of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

That
\equiv
is reflexive is trivial. Let
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. As
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})
if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})={\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
,
\equiv
is symmetric. Verifying the transitive property is equally difficult. So
\equiv
is an equivalence relation. To see the set
{\mathcal{E}}
of the unions of the equivalence classes of
\equiv
is a block system, by
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.2]
, we need only observe that
\equiv
is a
G
-congruence. Let
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and

. By Lemma
1.9

{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})
if and only if

{\rm WStab}_{G}(g(B))=g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)g^{-1}=g{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})g^{-1}={\rm WStab}_{G}(g(B^{\prime})).

Hence
B\equiv B^{\prime}
if and only if
g(B)\equiv g(B^{\prime})
and

is a
G
-congruence. Finally, it is clear

refines
{\mathcal{E}}
as each block of

is a union of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Definition 1.12
.

We call
\equiv
the

-restricting equivalence relation of

Let

A transitive group

{\rm WStab}_{G}(B_{0})=1

{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)=1
for all
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, and
B\equiv B^{\prime}
for every
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. We conclude that
\sim
and
\equiv
are different equivalence relations.

Definition 1.23
.

A transitive permutation group
G
is
quasiprimitive
if every nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
is transitive.

Sometimes though, the equivalence relations
\sim
and
\equiv
are the same.

Lemma 1.24
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. If
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})^{B}
is quasiprimitive, then
{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Consequently, the equivalence relations
\equiv
and
\sim
on
{\mathcal{B}}
are the same.

Proof.

We have already seen in Lemma
1.19
that
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)\leq{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B)
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. It is easy to show that
{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B){\unlhd}{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Finally, as
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})^{B}
is quasiprimitive, we see
{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B)^{B^{\prime}}
is trivial or transitive for every
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Hence
{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B)\leq{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\rm PStab}_{{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B)
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Some words about the choice of calling this family
5/2
-closed. With Wielandt’s hierarchy of groups, a
1
-closed transitive group is simply a symmetric group
[
27
, Theorem 5.11]
. As Wielandt showed
[
27
, Theorem 5.10]
that if
\ell\geq k
then
(G^{(k)})^{(\ell)}=G^{(k)}
, every
k
-closed group is
(k+1)
-closed. So as
k
grows, the size of the class of
k
-closed group gets larger. We will see in Theorem
3.6
that every
2
-closed group is
5/2
-closed. Thus, in placing this new class of groups within Wielandt’s hierarchy, it should be to the “right” of
2
-closed, but not to the right of
3
-closed. We then arbitrarily picked the midpoint between
2
and
3
.

Finally, we end this section with a discussion of an obvious question. Are there analogues of
5/2
-closed groups for values of
k\geq 3
? The short answer is definitely a “Yes”. Indeed, the motivation for our definition of
5/2
-closed groups comes from a result on graphs
[
2
, Lemma 2]
, and analogues of this result exist for
k
-closed groups for all
k\geq 3

[
7
, Lemma 12]
. For fixed
k\geq 3
, an analogous definition of
\equiv
on
{\mathcal{B}}
would be that
B\equiv_{k}B^{\prime}
if and only if whenever
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}
are distinct and
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B_{i})={\rm WStab}_{G}(B_{j})
,
1\leq i\leq k-1
, then
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B)={\rm WStab}_{G}(B_{1})
if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{G}(B^{\prime})={\rm WStab}_{G}(B_{1})
. It is straightforward to verify that
\equiv_{k}
is an equivalence relation on
{\mathcal{B}}
, and is a
G
-congruence, as wreath stabilizers are conjugate. We could then define
(2k+1)/2
-closed groups as we defined
5/2
-closed groups by substituting the equivalence classes of
\equiv_{k}
for
\equiv
. However, it is not clear that this definition would be useful. Additionally, it is not clear what should be done for block systems that do not have at least
k
blocks. In particular, in
[
3
, Lemma 12]
an analogue of
[
2
, Lemma 2]
is proven for ternary relational structures which have
2
blocks of prime size
p
. So it is not clear that
\equiv_{k}
by itself will give the appropriate analogue of
[
2
, Lemma 2]
. Consequently, we will not formally define a
(2k+1)/2
-closed group at this time.

2.
Quotients of
5/2
-closed groups

In this section, we will show that under appropriate circumstances the quotient of a
5/2
-closed group is
5/2
-closed. We begin with a basic result about blocks that will be needed.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a transitive group with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. Suppose
G/{\mathcal{B}}
has a (normal) block system
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the set of unions of the blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in a block of
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is a (normal) block system of
G
.

Proof.

Let
C/{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
, and
C
be the union of the set of all blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C/{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
g\in G
such that
g(C)\cap C\not=1
. Then
g
maps some block of
{\mathcal{B}}
in
C
to another block of
{\mathcal{B}}
in
C
, and so
g/{\mathcal{B}}(C/{\mathcal{B}})=C/{\mathcal{B}}
. As
C/{\mathcal{B}}
is a block of
G/{\mathcal{B}}
,
g
maps the set of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
in
C
to the set of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
in
C
. Hence
g(C)=C
and
C
is a block of
G
. Thus
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
G
.

For the “normal” part, let
N\leq G
such that
N/{\mathcal{B}}={\rm fix}_{G/{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}})
. Let
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
N
is transitive on the set of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. As
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})/{\mathcal{B}}=1
,
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})\leq N
, and so
N
is transitive on
C
. Thus
{\mathcal{C}}
is the set of orbits of
N
, and
{\mathcal{C}}
is a normal block system of
G
.
∎

Thus, in the quotient, (normal) block systems of the quotient correspond to (normal) block systems of the group. We call the block system
{\mathcal{C}}
of
G
, as in the preceding lemma, the block system of
G

induced
by
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
. Our main result of this section follows easily from the next result. The preceding result will be used implicitly.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a
5/2
-closed transitive group with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
, with
{\mathcal{E}}
the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
G
. Suppose there is
H\leq G
that is transitive for which
H/{\mathcal{B}}
has a nontrivial normal block system
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
, with
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
H/{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
H^{\prime}\leq G
be maximal such that
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=H/{\mathcal{B}}
. If
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
, then the
{\mathcal{C}}
-fixer block system of
H^{\prime}
is
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
,
{\mathcal{C}}
is a normal block system of
H
. As
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
G
,
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of both
H
and
H^{\prime}
, and as
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=H/{\mathcal{B}}
,
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
. Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
H^{\prime}
. Similarly,
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
is a block system of
H^{\prime}
. Let
K\leq{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{C}})
such that
(K/{\mathcal{B}})^{C/{\mathcal{B}}}
is trivial or transitive for every
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
. We will first show that there exists a subgroup
K^{\prime}\leq{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{C}})
such that
K^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=K/{\mathcal{B}}
and if
(K/{\mathcal{B}})^{C/{\mathcal{B}}}=1
, then
(K^{\prime})^{C}=1
and otherwise that
(K^{\prime})^{C}=K^{C}
.

Let
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
(K/{\mathcal{B}})^{C/{\mathcal{B}}}=1
. Let
k\in K
. Then
k(B)=B
for every
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. Let
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
that contains some
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. Let
C^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
C^{\prime}\subseteq E^{\prime}
. As
{\rm WStab}_{G/{\mathcal{B}}}(C/{\mathcal{B}})={\rm WStab}_{G/{\mathcal{B}}}(C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}})
, we see that
k
fixes setwise each block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
E^{\prime}
. Let
D
be the union of all
E^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
that contain a block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. Then
k
fixes each block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
D
setwise. As
D
is a union of blocks of
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
,
D
is a union of blocks of
{\mathcal{E}}
. As
G
is
5/2
-closed, we see
k|_{E}\in G
for every
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
E\subseteq D
. Hence
k|_{D}\in G
.

Now,
\hat{k}=[k(k|_{D})^{-1}]
has the property that
\hat{k}/{\mathcal{B}}=k/{\mathcal{B}}
and
\hat{k}^{D}=1
. Additionally,
\hat{k}^{C}=k^{C}
for every block of
{\mathcal{C}}
not contained in
D
. Repeating this procedure for every block
C^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
for which
(K/{\mathcal{B}})^{C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}}=1
, we see there is
k^{\prime}\in{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{C}})
such that
k^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=k/{\mathcal{B}}
,
(k^{\prime})^{C}=1
if
k
fixes setwise each block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
, and
(k^{\prime})^{C}=k^{C}
for every other block
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Hence
K^{\prime}={\langle}k^{\prime}:k\in K{\rangle}
has the properties that
K^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=K/{\mathcal{B}}
and if
K/{\mathcal{B}}
fixes every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then
(K^{\prime})^{C}=1
, and otherwise
(K^{\prime})^{C}=K^{C}
. Note this implies that
(K^{\prime})^{C}
is trivial for every block
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
(K/{\mathcal{B}})^{C/{\mathcal{B}}}
is trivial.

Now fix
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
for which
(K^{\prime})^{C}\not=1
. Then
(K^{\prime})^{C}
is transitive on the blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. Let
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
C\subseteq E
. As
G
is
5/2
-closed we have that
{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{B}})|_{E}\leq G
. The group
{\langle}K^{\prime},{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{B}})|_{E}{\rangle}
is then transitive on
C
. Also, as
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
, we see that
{\langle}K^{\prime},{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})|_{E}{\rangle}
is trivial on every block of
{\mathcal{C}}
that
K^{\prime}
is trivial on. Replacing
K^{\prime}
by
{\langle}K^{\prime},{\rm fix}_{H^{\prime}}({\mathcal{B}})|_{E}{\rangle}
and repeating this procedure for every
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
for which
(K^{\prime})^{C}\not=1
, we may assume without loss of generality that
K^{\prime}
is transitive on every block of
{\mathcal{C}}
that
K^{\prime}
is not trivial on.

We now see that
{\rm WStab}_{H^{\prime}}(C)^{C^{\prime}}
is trivial if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{H/{\mathcal{B}}}(C/{\mathcal{B}})^{C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}}
is trivial and
{\rm WStab}_{H^{\prime}}(C)^{C^{\prime}}
is transitive if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{H/{\mathcal{B}}}(C/{\mathcal{B}})^{C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}}
is transitive, and so
C\equiv C^{\prime}
if and only if
C/{\mathcal{B}}\equiv C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
. Thus the
{\mathcal{C}}
-fixer block system of
H^{\prime}
is
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a
5/2
-closed transitive group with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
with
{\mathcal{E}}
the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
G
. Suppose that whenever
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
is a normal block system of any transitive subgroup
H
of
G
then the block system induced by the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
of
H/{\mathcal{B}}
is refined by
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then
G/{\mathcal{B}}
is
5/2
-closed.

Proof.

Let
H\leq G
be transitive. Then
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
H
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
be such that
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
is a normal block system of
H/{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
H^{\prime}\leq G
be the largest subgroup of

such that
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=H/{\mathcal{B}}
, with
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
. As
H^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}=H/{\mathcal{B}}
, we see that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
is the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of

. By hypothesis,
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
. By Lemma

, the
{\mathcal{C}}
-fixer block system of

is
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
. Let

such that

fixes each block of
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
E^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}
set-wise. Then
g\in G
fixes each block of
{\mathcal{C}}
contained in

setwise, and as

is

-closed, we see that
g|_{E^{\prime}}\in G
. Then
g/{\mathcal{B}}|_{E^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}}\in G/{\mathcal{B}}
. As

and

are arbitrary,

is
5/2
-closed by definition.
∎

3.
Combinatorial objects with
5/2
-closed automorphism groups

In this section we explore the natural question of which combinatorial objects have automorphism groups that are
5/2
-closed? While we do not have a characterization of such combinatorial objects, we first give a large class of combinatorial objects whose automorphism groups are
5/2
-closed which contains graphs, digraphs, and configurations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set and
T
a subset of
\cup_{i=1}^{n}X^{i}
for some
n<|X|
. Hence
T
is a set of
k_{i}
-tuples with elements in
X
for some set of integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}<n
. We will call
T
a
tuple system
on
X
. Let
S=\{\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{t}\}:(u_{1},\ldots,u_{t})\in T\}
, so
S
is the set of coordinates of each element of
T
, and we call it the
set system corresponding to
T
. Let
m\geq 0
be an integer. We say a set system
S
is
m
-intersecting if for every
U_{1},U_{2}\in S
with
U_{1}\not=U_{2}
,
|U_{1}\cap U_{2}|\leq m
. A tuple system
T
will also be called
1
-intersecting if its corresponding set system is
1
-intersecting.

Example 3.2
.

The arc set of a finite digraph
\Gamma
is a tuple system, with corresponding set system the set of edges of the underlying simple graph. Also, observe that
A(\Gamma)
is
1
-intersecting, as two different arcs can only share at most one head or tail.

Example 3.3
.

A
configuration
is an ordered triple
(P,L,{\mathcal{I}})
, where
P
is a set whose elements are called
points
,
L
is a set whose elements are called
lines
, and
{\mathcal{I}}
is an
incidence relation
(or subset of
P\times L
). If
(p,\ell)\in{\mathcal{I}}
, we say the point
p
is on, or incident with, the line
\ell
or the line
\ell
contains, or is incident with, the point
p
. The incidence relation of a configuration satisfies several properties, one of which is that two points are on at most one line and two lines contain at most one point in common. So the set of lines of a configuration is a
1
-intersecting set system regardless of the other properties (which can be found in
[
15
, pg. 15]
). The point here is that
1
-intersecting set systems properly contain the set of configurations. We remark that in design theory, a configuration is often referred to as a
1
-design.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
T
be a tuple (set) system. We say
T
is
colored
if each tuple (set) in
T
has been identified with a color (or integer). Hence there is a function
f:T\to{\mathbb{N}}
and
f(t)
is simply the color of the tuple (set)
t\in T
.

We may think of an uncolored tuple (set) system
T
as a colored tuple (set) system simply by assigning the same color to every element of
T
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be colored tuple (set) systems defined on
X
and
Y
, respectively. An
isomorphism
from
T
to
T^{\prime}
is a bijection
f:X\to Y
that preserves tuples (sets) and colors. That is,
f(t)\in T^{\prime}
if and only if
t\in T
and for every color
i
, the image of the set of all tuples (sets) in
T
colored
i
is the set of all tuples (sets) colored
i^{\prime}
in
T^{\prime}
, for some color
i^{\prime}
. An
automorphism
of
T
is an isomorphism from
T
to
T
that fixes each color, and the set of all automorphisms of
T
is the
automorphism group
of
T
, denoted
{\rm Aut}(T)
. A colored tuple (set) system
T
is
point-transitive
if
{\rm Aut}(T)
is transitive on
X
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
T
be a point-transitive colored
1
-intersecting tuple system on
X
. Then
{\rm Aut}(T)
is
5/2
-closed.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
{\rm Aut}(T)
has some transitive subgroup
G
with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
for which the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system
{\mathcal{E}}
of
H
is not
\{X\}
as otherwise
{\rm Aut}(T)
is trivially
5/2
-closed. Thus
{\mathcal{E}}
has at least two blocks. Let
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})\in T
be of color
c
, and
s=\{t_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}
.

Suppose that for some
1\leq i<j\leq k
we have
t_{i},t_{j}\in E\in{\mathcal{E}}
,
t_{i}\not=t_{j}
, and
t_{\ell}\not\in E
for some
1\leq\ell\leq k
. That is, that there are at least two different elements of

in
E
and at least one element of
t
not in
E
. Let

such that
t_{\ell}\in B^{\prime}
, and

such that

. As

is a coordinate of

, and

.

Suppose

,

. Now, for each

(t_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,t_{k}^{\prime})\in T
, where

if

and for any

,

, we have that for all

,

are equal but an arbitrary element of

. Successively choosing

(t_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k})\in T

,

, and are all colored

. As

(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k})\in T
for every

is arbitrary but equal for all

,

and

, and are all colored

.

Now let
g\in{\rm Aut}(T)
such that
g
fixes set-wise all blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in a block

. If
|s\cap E|\geq 2
for some

, then every coordinate of

is contained in
E
. Hence
g|_{E}(t)\in T
and is colored
c
. If
|s\cap E|=0
, then
g|_{E}(t)=t\in T
. Otherwise, we will use the notation of the previous paragraph. As
(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k})\in T
for every

is arbitrary but equal for all

,
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq\ell\leq j
, and are all colored

, we see that

and is colored

. Thus

is
5/2
-closed.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
S
be a
1
-intersecting set system. Just as we identify the edge set of a graph
\Gamma
with the arc set
\{(x,y):xy\in E(\Gamma)\}
to obtain a digraph, we may identify
S
with a tuple system
T
. Namely, we let
T
be the colored tuple system that is the union of all
r!

r
-tuples whose set of coordinates are an element
s
of
S
of length
r
. We color each tuple of
T
the same color as its corresponding set
s
. Note that
{\rm Aut}(S)={\rm Aut}(T)
. Conversely, if
T
is a colored tuple system in which for every
r
-tuple with underlying set
s
we have all
r!

r
-tuples on
s
and all are colored the same color, then we may identify
T
with a colored set system in the obvious manner, and
{\rm Aut}(T)={\rm Aut}(S)
here as well. In this way, we may identify a set system, and in particular, a configuration, with a
1
-intersecting tuple system.

Hence if one proves a group theoretic result that is true for all transitive
5/2
-closed permutation groups, it also holds for all
2
-closed groups, which in our language here is the automorphism group of a colored tuple system, as well as the automorphism groups of configurations!

4.
The
5/2
-closure of a wreath product is the wreath product of the
5/2
-closures

In this section, we will show that some commonly used properties of
2
-closed groups are shared by
5/2
-closed groups. In particular, in Theorem
4.4
we will show that the
5/2
-closure of the wreath product of two permutation groups is
5/2
-closed. The same result for
2
-closed groups is given in
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
. As a consequence, we show in Lemma
4.6
that the
5/2
-closure of a
p
-group is a
p
-group, an analogue of
[
27
, Exercise 5.28]
needed for our main application. We begin with some basic definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
and
H\leq S_{Y}
. Define the
wreath product of
G
and
H
, denoted
G\wr H
, to be the set of all permutations of
X\times Y
of the form
(x,y)\mapsto(g(x),h_{x}(y))
, where
g\in G
and each
h_{x}\in H
.

It is straightforward to show that
G\wr H
is a permutation group.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
and
H\leq S_{Y}
both be transitive. Let
B_{x}=\{x\}\times Y
,
x\in X
, and
{\mathcal{B}}=\{B_{x}:x\in X\}
. Then
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
G\wr H
by
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.7]
. It is called the
lexi-partition of
G\wr H
corresponding to
Y
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G_{1}\leq S_{m}
and
G_{2}\leq S_{n}
be transitive. If
{\mathcal{B}}
is a normal block system of
G_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}=\{\{i\}\times B:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m},B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
is a normal block system of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be the lexi-partition of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
with respect to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
(where we view
G_{2}
as permutating
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
). Then
{\mathcal{C}}\preceq{\mathcal{D}}
. Let
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
C\subseteq D
. Observe that
C
is a block of
{\rm Stab}_{G_{1}\wr G_{2}}(D)^{D}\cong G_{2}
, so
C
is block of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
by
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.10]
. Hence
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
. Then
{\rm fix}_{G_{1}\wr G_{2}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{(i,j)\mapsto(i,g_{i}(j)):g_{i}\in{\rm fix}_{G_{2}}({\mathcal{B}})\}
. We see the set of orbits of
{\rm fix}_{G_{1}\wr G_{2}}({\mathcal{C}})
is
{\mathcal{C}}
, and as
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
,
{\rm fix}_{G_{1}\wr G_{2}}({\mathcal{C}}){\unlhd}G_{1}\wr G_{2}
. Thus
{\mathcal{C}}
is a normal block system of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G_{1}\leq S_{m}
and
G_{2}\leq S_{n}
be transitive. Then
(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}=G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
.

Proof.

We will first show that
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
is
5/2
-closed. As
G_{1}\wr G_{2}\leq G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
, this will give that
(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}\leq G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. So let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a normal block system of some transitive subgroup
H
of
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
H
, and
\gamma\in G_{1}\wr G_{2}
fix
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
and every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
E
. As
{\mathcal{B}}
and
\gamma
are arbitrary, by definition it suffices to show that
\gamma|_{E}\in G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the lexi-partition of
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
with respect to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
(we consider
S_{n}
as permutating the elements of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
). We may assume without loss of generality that
H
is maximal with
{\mathcal{B}}
a block system of
H
. By
[
8
, Lemma 5]
(or
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.7]
), we see that
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
or
{\mathcal{C}}\prec{\mathcal{B}}
. We will consider the two cases separately. Set
G=G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
.

Suppose first that
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
,
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
, and
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
with
B\subseteq C
and
B\subseteq E
. As
{\mathcal{C}}
is a block system of
G
,
{\mathcal{C}}
is also a block system of
H
. By the Embedding Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.1]
, we see there is
L\leq G
that is permutation isomorphic to
(H/{\mathcal{C}})\wr({\rm Stab}_{H}(C)^{C})
. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{C}=\{B\in{\mathcal{B}}:B\subseteq C\}
. As
{\mathcal{B}}
is a normal block system of
H
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{C}
is a normal block system of
{\rm Stab}_{H}(C)^{C}
. By Lemma
4.3
,
{\mathcal{B}}
is a normal block system of
L
. By the assumption that
H
is maximal with
{\mathcal{B}}
a block system, we see
L\leq H
. So
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
. As
\gamma
fixes
B
,
\gamma
fixes
C
, and thus
\gamma^{C}\in G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. Recall
{\rm Stab}_{H}(C)^{C}\leq G_{2}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{C}
is a normal block system of
{\rm Stab}_{H}(C)^{C}
. Additionally,
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=\{E\in{\mathcal{E}}:E\subseteq C\}
is refined by the
{\mathcal{B}}_{C}
-fixer block system of
{\rm Stab}_{H}(C)^{C}
. So
(\gamma^{C})|_{E}\in G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. Hence
\gamma|_{E}\in G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}\prec{\mathcal{B}}
, in which case
{\mathcal{B}}/{\mathcal{C}}
is a normal block system of
H/{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}/{\mathcal{C}}
-fixer block system of
H/{\mathcal{C}}
. As
h|_{C}\leq G
for every
h\in{\rm Stab}_{H}(C)
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\mathcal{E}}/{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. Then
(\gamma/{\mathcal{C}})|_{E/{\mathcal{C}}}\in G_{1}^{(5/2)}
. As there exists
\hat{\gamma}\in G
with
\hat{\gamma}(i,j)=([(\gamma/{\mathcal{C}})|_{E/{\mathcal{C}}}]^{-1}(i),j)
, we see
\hat{\gamma}\gamma\in{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{C}})=1_{S_{m}}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
. We conclude that
\gamma|_{E}\in G
. Hence
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
is
5/2
-closed, and so
(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}\leq G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}
.

To see
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}\leq(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}
, let
H\leq G_{2}
be transitive and
{\mathcal{B}}
a normal block system of
G_{2}
. Then
G_{1}\wr H\leq G_{1}\wr G_{2}
and
{\mathcal{D}}=\{\{i\}\times B:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m},B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
is a normal block system of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
by Lemma
4.3
. For
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, let
D_{B}=\{0\}\times B
. Fix
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Consider
{\rm WStab}_{G_{1}\wr H}(D_{B})
. Note that for any
D\in{\mathcal{D}}
of the form
D=\{i\}\times B^{\prime}
for any
i\not=0
and
B\not=B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
{\rm WStab}_{G_{1}\wr H}(D_{B})^{D}
is transitive. Also,
{\rm WStab}_{G_{1}\wr H}(D_{B})^{D_{B^{\prime}}}=1
if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{H}(B)^{B^{\prime}}=1
. We conclude that if
{\mathcal{E}}
is the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
G_{2}
, then the
{\mathcal{D}}
-fixer block system of
G_{1}\wr H
is
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\{i\}\times E:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}
.

Now
\gamma\in G_{2}
fixes
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
and every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
E
if and only if there is
\hat{\gamma}\in G_{1}\wr H
that fixes
\{0\}\times E
and fixes every block of
{\mathcal{D}}
contained in
\{0\}\times E
(
\hat{\gamma}
can be chosen to be, for example, the map which on
\{0\}\times E
is
(0,e)\mapsto(0,\gamma(e))
and is trivial everywhere else). Additionally,
\gamma|_{E}\in G_{2}
if and only if
\gamma|_{\{0\}\times E}\in G_{1}\wr G_{2}
. We conclude that
G_{1}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}\leq(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}
.

Now let
H\leq G_{1}
be transitive and
{\mathcal{B}}
a normal block system of
H
. Then
H\wr G_{2}\leq G_{1}\wr G_{2}
and
{\mathcal{D}}=\{B\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}:B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
is a normal block system of
H\wr G_{2}
. For
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, let
D_{B}=B\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. Fix
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Consider
{\rm WStab}_{H\wr G_{2}}(D_{B})
. Note
{\rm WStab}_{H\wr G_{2}}(D_{B})^{D_{B^{\prime}}}=1
if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{H}(B)^{B^{\prime}}=1
and
{\rm WStab}_{H\wr G_{2}}(D_{B})^{D_{B^{\prime}}}
is transitive if and only if
{\rm WStab}_{H}(B)^{B^{\prime}}
is transitive. We conclude that if
{\mathcal{E}}
is the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
H
, then the
{\mathcal{D}}
-fixer block system of
H\wr G_{2}
is
{\mathcal{F}}=\{E\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}:E\in{\mathcal{E}}\}
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be the lexi-partition of
G_{1}\wr G_{2}
with respect to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
(so
{\mathcal{C}}=\{\{i\}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}:i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{m}\})
. Now,
\gamma\in H
fixes
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
and every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
E
if and only if there is
\tilde{\gamma}\in H\wr G_{2}
such that
\tilde{\gamma}/{\mathcal{C}}=\gamma
. Then
\gamma|_{E}\in G_{1}^{(5/2)}
if and only if
\tilde{\gamma}|_{E\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}}\in G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}
. We conclude that
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}\leq(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}
, and so
G_{1}^{(5/2)}\wr G_{2}^{(5/2)}\leq(G_{1}\wr G_{2})^{(5/2)}
. The result follows.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
G\leq S_{m}
be a group, and
n
a positive integer. By
G\wr_{n}G
, we mean the group
G\wr G\wr\cdots\wr G
, where the wreath product is performed
n
times.

The next result generalizes
[
27
, Exercise 5.28]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p
be prime,
n
a positive integer, and
P\leq S_{p^{n}}
be a transitive
p
-group. Then
P^{(5/2)}
is a
p
-group.

Proof.

If
n=1
, then
P\cong({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{L}
and has no nontrival normal block systems. Hence
P^{(5/2)}=P
. So we assume
n\geq 2
. As
P
is a transitive
p
-group, it is contained in a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
S_{p^{n}}
which by
[
24
, §2]
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. So we assume
P\leq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. It is clear from the definition of a
5/2
-closed group, that if
H\leq G
are transitive groups, then
H^{(5/2)}\leq G^{(5/2)}
. Inductively applying Theorem
4.4
we see that
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{(5/2)}
is
5/2
-closed. Then
P^{(5/2)}\leq({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{(5/2)}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
P^{(5/2)}
is a
p
-group.
∎

5.
5/2
-closed groups of odd prime-power order that contain a regular cyclic subgroup

In this section, we will give an application of the work done already and determine the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
5/2
-closed permutation groups of degree
p^{n}
that contain a regular cyclic subgroup, where
p
is an odd prime and
n\geq 1
. These turn out to be exactly the same as the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
2
-closed permutation groups of degree
p^{n}
that contain a regular cyclic subgroup
[
10
]
. While the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
2
-closed permutation groups of degree
p^{n}
that contain a regular cyclic subgroup are not in the literature, they have been known for some time. There are two independent proofs of this result. Chronologically, the first is due to Klin and Pöschel
[
16
]
, using the method of Schur. The second, using permutation group theoretic techniques, can be found in
[
2
]
. This application is chosen for this paper for two reasons. First, it is well past time that a proof of the classification of automorphism groups of circulant digraphs of order
p^{n}
appears in the literature (and this will not be completed until
[
10
]
). Second, and from the point of view of this paper most importantly, is that this is the clearest and easiest application of the inductive techniques developed in this paper that the author is aware of.

We begin with a final general result, and then restrict our discussion to transitive permuation groups of degree
p^{n}
that contain a regular cyclic subgroup.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G\leq S_{n}
be transitive with normal block systems
{\mathcal{B}}\prec{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
G
and
{\mathcal{F}}/{\mathcal{B}}
the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}
-fixer block system of
G/{\mathcal{B}}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}
or
{\mathcal{E}}\prec{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

We assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
does not refine
{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
such that
C\cap E\not=\emptyset
. Our assumption ensures that
E
does not contain every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
. Now, if
E
intersects two different blocks of
{\mathcal{F}}
, then there is a block
C^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
C^{\prime}\not\subseteq E
such that the induced action of
{\rm WStab}_{G/{\mathcal{B}}}(C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}})
on
C/{\mathcal{B}}
is transitive. Then
E
contains every block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C
, a contradiction. Hence
E
intersects only one block of
{\mathcal{F}}
, and
{\mathcal{E}}\preceq{\mathcal{F}}
. Note that if
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{F}}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}\preceq{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{E}}
, so
{\mathcal{C}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}
or
{\mathcal{E}}\prec{\mathcal{F}}
.
∎

Define
\tau_{n}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}
by
\tau(i)=i+1\ ({\rm mod\ }p^{n})
. Thus
\tau_{n}
generates
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}})_{L}
. As
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
is cyclic of order
p^{n}
,
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
contains a unique normal subgroup of order
p^{i}
, for each
0\leq i\leq n
, namely
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-i}}{\rangle}
. Thus, for
0\leq i\leq n
, the group
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
has a (possibly trivial) block system
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
of
p^{n-i}
blocks of size
p^{i}
, that is the set of orbits of
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-i}}{\rangle}
. Note that these block systems are the only block systems of
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
, and hence if
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}\leq G\leq S_{p^{n}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
is a block system of
G
, then
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}_{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq n
. Furthermore, the blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
are cosets of the unique subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}
of order
p^{i}
. Thus if
B_{i,j}=j+p^{n-i}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{i}}
, where
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n-i}}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}=\{B_{i,j}:j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n-i}}\}
. Also observe that

{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\prec\ldots\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{n-1}\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{n}.

The next result is a special case of
[
6
, Lemma 23]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
n\geq 2
, and
P\leq S_{p^{n}}
be a transitive
p
-group that contains
\tau_{n}
. If
P\not=\tau_{n}
, then
|{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}})|\geq p^{2}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
n\geq 2
, and
P\leq S_{p^{n}}
be a transitive
p
-group that contains
\tau_{n}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
-fixer block system of
P
. Then
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\preceq{\mathcal{E}_{2}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we see that either
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
or
{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
. We may assume that
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\not={\mathcal{B}}_{2}
as otherwise the result is trivially true as
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
. Suppose
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. As
p
is odd,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
has order
p^{p+1}>p^{3}
. Let
\gamma\in{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})
, and
B_{2}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
. Set
\delta=\gamma^{-1}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-2}}\gamma\tau_{n}^{-p^{n-2}}
. As
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is elementary abelian, we see that
\delta\in{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})
. As
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, we see that
\delta^{B_{2}}\in{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}{\rangle}^{B_{2}}
. Hence
\gamma^{B_{2}}
normalizes
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-2}}{\rangle}^{B_{2}}
. As
N_{S_{p^{2}}}(({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}})_{L})
has a Sylow
p
-subgroup of order

, we see that
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})^{B_{2}}\not\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. As
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})
contains the regular cyclic
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-2}}{\rangle}^{B_{2}}
, it follows by
[
9
, Lemma 4]
that
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{2})^{B_{2}}
does not contain a regular elementary abelian subgroup. Thus if
\gamma^{B_{2}}
is a
p
-cycle on the
p
blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
contained in
B_{2}
, then it must be the case that
\gamma^{B_{2}}
is a
p^{2}
-cycle as otherwise
{\langle}\gamma,\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}{\rangle}^{B_{2}}
is a regular subgroup isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Hence
\gamma^{p}\in{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})
, and
(\gamma^{p})^{B_{2}}=1
if and only if
(\gamma/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})^{B_{2}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}=1
. Thus
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
n\geq 2
, and

be a transitive group that contains
\tau_{n}
. Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}/{\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}
-fixer block system of

,

. Then
{\mathcal{E}}_{i-1}\preceq{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
for every

.

We proceed by induction on

. If

, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}=\{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{2}}\}
and so

. Assume the result holds for all

and let

be a transitive group that contains

. Then
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is a normal block system of

, and

by the induction hypothesis. By Lemma

and the result follows.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

Let
p
be prime and
n\geq 1
an integer. Define a
primary key space

{\bf K}_{p^{n}}
to be the set of all integer vectors
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})
satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

k_{i}<i
for each
1\leq i\leq n
, and

(2)

k_{i-1}\leq k_{i}
for each
2\leq i\leq n
.

A vector in
{\bf K}_{p^{n}}
is called a
primary key
.

Note that the primary key space
{\bf K}_{p^{n}}
is independent of the choice of
p
, and that as
k_{i}<i
, we have
k_{1}=0
. Primary keys were used in
[
22
]
to define partitions of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}
. While we will not prove this here, the partition produced by a primary key when
p
is odd is the set of orbits of the point stabilizer of a Sylow
p
-subgroup of a
2
-closed transitive group which contains
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}})_{L}
. We will use primary keys to construct the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
5/2
-closed transitive groups which contain
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}})_{L}
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
{\bf k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n})
be a primary key. For fixed
k_{i}
, we define a subgroup
P_{i,k_{i}}
by
P_{i,k_{i}}={\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}|_{B_{n-k_{i}}}:B_{n-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}{\rangle}
. We then define a group
\Pi({\bf k})={\langle}P_{i,k_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n{\rangle}
, and call it the subgroup
corresponding
to
{\bf k}
.

As
k_{1}=0
, the group
P_{1,0}={\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{0}}|_{B_{n}}{\rangle}={\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
, where
B_{n}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}
. As the set of orbits of
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}{\rangle}
is the unique block system of
{\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
with blocks of size
p^{n-(i-1)}=p^{n-i+1}
, we see the set of orbits of
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}{\rangle}
is
{\mathcal{B}}_{n-i+1}
. As
k_{i}\leq i-1
, we see that
n-k_{i}\geq n-i+1
, and so
{\mathcal{B}}_{n-i+1}\preceq{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}
. That is, the orbits of
{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}{\rangle}
are contained within blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}
. Thus
\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}|_{B_{n-k_{i}}}
is well-defined for every
B_{n-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}
, and so
P_{i,k_{i}}
is well-defined for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

One more observation about primary keys. As one reads
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\prec\ldots\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
from left to right, the size of the blocks in a block system get larger. As one reads a primary key from left to right, the size of the blocks in
{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}
get smaller or stay the same. In order to cause as little confusion in the literature as possible, we are following Muzychuk’s definition of a primary key
[
22
, Section 2.1]
. It does not seem feasible to always have all things get larger as we go from left to right. The reason for this is that somehow one must communicate the power of
\tau_{n}
together with the size of the blocks we are restricting to. But as the power of
\tau_{n}
gets larger, the size of the blocks we restrict to must get smaller.

We will show that the groups
\Pi({\bf k})
are the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
5/2
-closed groups of degree
p^{n}
that contain
\tau_{n}
. We first give some examples of the groups
\Pi({\bf k})
, and a preliminary result.

Example 5.7
.

Let
{\bf k}=(0,0,\ldots,0)
. Then
\tau_{n}^{p^{k_{i}}}=\tau_{n}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, and
{\mathcal{B}}_{n-0}={\mathcal{B}}_{n}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n}}
, and so
\Pi({\bf k})={\langle}\tau_{n}{\rangle}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
{\bf k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n})
, and define
\delta:{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{n-1}}
by
\delta(B_{1,j})=j
. Then
\delta(\Pi({\bf k})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})=\Pi(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})
.

Proof.

First observe that
\tau(B_{1,j})=B_{1,j+1}
. Hence
\delta(\tau_{n})=\tau_{n-1}
. For
0\leq i\leq n-1
, let
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}^{\prime}
be the block system of
{\langle}\tau_{n-1}{\rangle}
with blocks of size
p^{i}
. As
\Pi({\bf k})={\langle}P_{i,k_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n{\rangle}
, it suffices to show that
\delta(P_{i,k_{i}})=P_{i,k_{i}}^{\prime}
, where
P_{i,k_{i}}^{\prime}={\langle}\tau_{n-1}^{p^{i-1}}|_{B_{n-1-k_{i}}}:B_{n-1-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-1-k_{i}}^{\prime}{\rangle}
, and
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Fix
1\leq i\leq n-1
. As
P_{i,k_{i}}={\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}|_{B_{n-k_{i}}}:B_{n-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}{\rangle}
, we have that
P_{i,k_{i}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}={\langle}(\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})|_{B_{n-k_{i}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}:B_{n-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-k_{i}}{\rangle}
. As
(\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}})^{p^{n-i}}(B_{i})=\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}(B_{i})=B_{i}
and
\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}
is a product of
p^{i-1}
cycles of length
p^{n-i+1}
, we see that
\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is a product of
p^{i-1}
cycles of length
p^{n-i}
. As
\delta(\tau_{n})=\tau_{n-1}
is a
p^{n-1}
-cycle, the power of
\tau_{n-1}
that gives
p^{i-1}
cycles of length
p^{n-i}
is
p^{i-1}
. It is also clear that
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}={\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}^{\prime}
. Hence
\delta({\mathcal{B}}_{i})={\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}^{\prime}
. Then
(\tau_{n}^{p^{i-1}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})|_{B_{n-k_{i}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}=\tau_{n-1}^{p^{i-1}}|_{B_{n-1-k_{i}}}
,
where
B_{n-1-k_{i}}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-1-k_{i}}^{\prime}
, and
\delta(P_{i,k_{i}})=P_{i,k_{i}}^{\prime}
. The result follows.
∎

Example 5.9
.

Let
{\bf k}=(0,1,2,\ldots,n-2,n-1)
. Then
\Pi({\bf k})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, which is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
S_{p^{n}}
by
[
24
, Section 2]
. We proceed by induction on
n
, with the first step of the induction being the primary key
(0)
, which gives
({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{L}
as in Example
5.7
. Inductively assume that the example is correct for the primary key
(0,1,2,\ldots,n-1)
and consider the primary key
{\bf k}=(0,1,2,\ldots,n-1,n)
. By Lemma
5.8
, we have that
P({\bf k})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is the subgroup corresponding to the primary key
{\bf k}^{\prime}=(0,1,\ldots,n-1)
. By the induction hypothesis, we see that
\Pi({\bf k})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. As
{\rm fix}_{\Pi({\bf k})}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})=P_{n,n-1}={\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}|_{B_{1}}:B_{1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{1}{\rangle}
, we conclude that
{\rm fix}_{\Pi({\bf k})}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-1}
. Hence
\Pi({\bf k})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\wr_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Example 5.10
.

Let
n=3
and
{\bf k}=(0,0,2)
. It can be shown by inspecting
\Pi({\bf k})
for all possible primary keys with at most three coordinates that
{\bf k}
is the smallest primary key for which
\Pi({\bf k})
is neither cyclic nor a wreath product. By Example
5.7
and Lemma
5.8
we see that
\Pi({\bf k})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is cyclic. Also, by definition,
{\rm fix}_{\Pi({\bf k})}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})={\langle}\tau_{3}^{p^{2}}|_{B_{2}}:B_{2}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}{\rangle}
and so has order
p^{p}
. Thus
|\Pi({\bf k})|=p^{p+2}
. Let
\Gamma
be a Cayley digraph of order
p^{3}
that contains
\Pi({\bf k})
. Then, using the notation of
[
14
, Section 5.3]
, we see that
\Gamma
is a
(K,L)
-generalized wreath product with
K={\langle}p^{2}{\rangle}
and
L={\langle}p{\rangle}
. In general, for any primary key
{\bf k}
, if
\Pi({\bf k})
is not cyclic, then any Cayley digraph of order
p^{n}
that contains
\Pi({\bf k})
will also be a generalized wreath product (although we will not give a proof of this fact here).

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
p\geq 3
be prime,
n\geq 1
, and
G\leq S_{p^{n}}
that contains a regular cyclic subgroup and is
5/2
-closed. If
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, then
P
is permutation isomorphic to
\Pi({\bf k})
for some primary key
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
. Further,
P
is
5/2
-closed.

Proof.

By
[
14
, Corollary 1.1.10]
, we see that
P
is transitive. As
P\leq G
and
G
is
5/2
-closed, we have that
P^{(5/2)}\leq G
. By Lemma
4.6
, we see
P^{(5/2)}
is a
p
-group and so as
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
,
P^{(5/2)}=P
. It thus suffices to show that a
5/2
-closed
p
-subgroup of
S_{p^{n}}
that contains a regular cyclic subgroup is permutation isomorphic to
\Pi({\bf k})
, for some primary key
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
.

We proceed by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then
P=({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})_{L}
. The result then follows with primary key
(0)
.
Assume the result is true for
n-1\geq 2
, and let
G\leq S_{p^{n}}
be transitive and
5/2
-closed.

We have observed that
P
has
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
as block systems, and these block systems are the only block systems of
P
. If
|P|=p^{n}
, then
P
is a regular cyclic subgroup and the result follows by Example
5.7
with
{\bf k}=(0,0,\ldots,0)
. We thus assume without loss of generality that
|P|>p^{n}
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}/{\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}
be the
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}/{\mathcal{B}}_{i-1}
-fixer block system of
P
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
. As
|P|>p^{n}
and
p
is odd, we have by Lemma
5.2
that
|{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})|>p^{2}
. As
G
is
5/2
-closed, we see
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})={\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}|_{E}:E\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}{\rangle}
. As
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}={\mathcal{B}}_{\ell}
for some
1\leq\ell\leq n-1
, we see
{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})=P_{n,\ell}
.

We next use Theorem
2.3
to show
P/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is
5/2
-closed. Let
H\leq P
be transitive with
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
a normal block system of
H/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
the
{\mathcal{C}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
-fixer block system of
H/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
. By Lemma
5.1
we have that
{\mathcal{C}}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
or
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Suppose
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}\not\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. Let
E_{C}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
C\subseteq E_{C}
. As
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}\not\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
,
{\rm WStab}_{H/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}(C/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})^{C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}\not=1
for some
C^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
C^{\prime}\subseteq E_{1}
, where
E_{1}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
that contains
C
. Let
L={\rm Stab}_{P}(E_{1})^{E_{1}}
. As
H\leq P
,
L
contains
{\rm Stab}_{H}(E_{1})^{E_{1}}
, and
{\rm Stab}_{H}(E_{1})^{E_{1}}
contains
{\rm fix}_{H}({\mathcal{C}})^{E_{1}}
as
E_{1}
is a union of blocks of
{\mathcal{C}}
because
{\mathcal{C}}\prec{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
. As
{\rm WStab}_{H/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}(C/{\mathcal{B}}_{1})^{C^{\prime}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}\not=1
and
C,C^{\prime}\subset E_{1}
, we see that
L
is not a regular cyclic subgroup. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{E_{1}}
be the set of blocks of
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
that are contained in
E_{1}
. By Lemma
5.2
we see that
{\rm fix}_{L}({\mathcal{B}}_{E_{1}})
has order at least
p^{2}
. Then there exists
1\not=\rho\in P
such that
\rho^{E_{1}}\in{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})^{E_{1}}
and
\rho^{E_{1}}\not\in{\langle}\tau_{n}^{p^{n-1}}{\rangle}^{E_{1}}
. As
P
is
5/2
-closed, we see that
\rho|_{E_{1}}\in P
, in which case the
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
-fixer block system of
P
is not
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, a contradiction. This then implies that
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
. As
H
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are arbitrary, by Theorem
2.3
we have that
G/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is
5/2
-closed. By the induction hypothesis, there exists a primary key
{\bf k}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n-1}
such that
P/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}=\Pi({{\bf k}^{\prime}})
.

Let
{\bf k^{\prime}}=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,k_{n-1}^{\prime})
, and
{\bf k}=(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,k_{n-1}^{\prime},\ell)=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n})
. We now show that
{\bf k}
is a primary key. Note that
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=P_{n,\ell}
, and by induction we have that
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}/{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=P_{n-1,k_{n-1}}/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
. By Lemma
5.3
we see that
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\preceq{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
. Hence
k_{n-1}\leq k_{n}
. As
1\leq\ell\leq n-1
,
k_{n}\leq n-1
and so
{\bf k}
is a primary key.

We now show that
\Pi({\bf k})=P
. By the induction hypothesis and Lemma
5.8
we see that
P/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}=\Pi({\bf k})/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
. As
P
is
5/2
-closed, we see that
\tau_{n}^{p^{k_{i}}}|_{B_{n-i}}\in P
for every coordinate
k_{i}
of
{\bf k}
and
B_{n-i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n-i}
. Hence
P_{i,k_{i}}\leq P
for each coordinate
k_{i}
of
{\bf k}
and so
\Pi({\bf k})\leq P
. Let
\gamma\in P
. Then there exists
\delta\in\Pi({\bf k})
such that
\gamma/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}=\delta/{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
. Hence
\delta^{-1}\gamma\in{\rm fix}_{P}({\mathcal{B}}_{1})=P_{n,\ell}\leq\Pi({\bf k})
. Thus
\gamma\in\Pi({\bf k})
and
P=\Pi({\bf k})
. The result follows by induction.
∎

We will show in
[
10
]
that if
{\bf k}
is a primary key such that
\Pi({\bf k})
cannot be written as a nontrivial wreath product, then every minimal transitive subgroup of
\Pi({\bf k})
is a regular cyclic subgroup. By
[
5
, Theorem 33]
this will imply that any permutation group
G
of
S_{p^{n}}
with Sylow
p
-subgroup
\Pi({\bf k})
is either
2
-transitive or
\Pi({\bf k}){\unlhd}G
. The normalizers of such
\Pi({\bf k})
are then calculated in
[
10
]
. This results not only in a classification of
5/2
-closed groups of odd prime power degree which contain a regular cyclic subgroup, but also a classification of
2
-closed groups of odd prime power degree which contain a regular cyclic subgroup. These, in turn, give the automorphism group of every circulant digraph of odd prime power degree, as well as the automorphism group of every configuration of odd prime power order that contains a regular cyclic subgroup.

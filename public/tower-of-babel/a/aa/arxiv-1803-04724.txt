On hyperbolicity and Gevrey well-posedness. Part three: a model of weakly hyperbolic systems.
Baptiste Morisse

Abstract
We consider a model of weakly hyperbolic systems of first-order, nonlinear PDEs. Weak hyperbolicity means here that the principal symbol of the system has a crossing of real-valued eigenvalues, and is not uniformly diagonalizable. We prove the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem in the Gevrey regularity for all Gevrey indices σ \sigma in ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) (1/2,1) . The proof is based on the construction of a suitable approximate symmetrizer of the principal symbol and an energy estimate in Gevrey spaces. We discuss both the generality of the assumption on the structure of the principal symbol and the sharpness of the lower bound of the Gevrey index.

Introduction

In this paper we prove an energy estimate for systems of the form

\partial_{t}u=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
(t+|x-x_{0}|^{2})e(t,x)&0\end{pmatrix}\partial_{x}u+F(t,x,u)u

(1.1)

where
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
F(t,x,u)
is nonlinear in
u
, and
e
is a Gevrey function that is bounded away from zero and compactly supported around
(t,x)=(0,x_{0})
.
This result translates by classical arguments into a
local-in-time
well-posedness result in Gevrey spaces for the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
).

This result could also be extended into a general well-posedness result for a wider class of systems in several spatial dimensions:

\partial_{t}u=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}A_{j}(t,x)\partial_{x_{j}}u+f(t,x,u)

(1.2)

where
x
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the
A_{j}
are in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}
,
f
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the
A_{j}
have some smoothness in time and are Gevrey regular in
x
, the nonlinearity
f
is analytic in all variables, and the principal symbol
A=\sum_{j}A_{j}(t,x)\xi_{j}
experiences a transition from hyperbolicity to ellipticity.
Precisely, in order to extend our result for (
1.1
) into a well-posedness result for (
1.2
), we assume

\bullet

hyperbolicity of the principal symbol
A
, that is the spectrum of

is real.

\bullet

At a distinguished point
(0,x_{0},\xi_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the existence of a real and non semi-simple eigenvalue (semi-simplicity means simplicity as a zero of the minimal polynomial of

).

\bullet

And finally we assume that
A
transitions from hyperbolicity to ellipticity at
(0,x_{0},\xi_{0})
for negative times.
By transition from hyperbolicity to ellipticity we mean the phenomenon studied in
[
Mor18
]
.
Here this transition is

degenerate, we will go back to this point in Section

In a forthcoming version of this paper, we expound on these Assumptions, and handle the general case of weakly hyperbolic systems of the form (
1.2

In the present version of this paper, we work exclusively with the model (
1.1
).
The fact that (
1.1
) is one-dimensional (
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
) does not play any role in our analysis.

Further simplifying into
e\equiv 1
,
F(u)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&u_{1}\end{pmatrix}

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\

Our interest is in the Cauchy problem at

Our present result has a double background: first in

Background: on weakly hyperbolic systems

1.1.1
The classical result of Colombini, Janelli and Spagnolo

We consider here the following second-order, linear scalar equation

The work of Colombini, Janelli and Spagnolo is based on an energy estimate, which uses the particular structure of the wave equation (

\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}=a^{\prime}(t)|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}+2\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}{\rm Re}\,w\partial_{t}w.

As

\displaystyle|a^{\prime}(t)||\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{a^{\prime}}{(a+\varepsilon)^{1-1/k}}\right|\frac{1}{(a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}}\,(a+\varepsilon)|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\left|\left((a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}\right)^{\prime}\right|\frac{1}{(a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}}\,(a+\varepsilon)|\xi|^{2}|w|^{2}.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}E_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left((a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}\right)^{\prime}\right|\frac{1}{(a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}}\,E_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{1/2}|\xi|\,E_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\left((a+\varepsilon)^{1/k}\right)^{\prime}\right|\varepsilon^{-1/k}\,E_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{1/2}|\xi|\,E_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)

In order to optimize the exponential term, we put

1.4
Current result

As mentioned above, generic transitions from hyperbolic to ellipticity involve in one spatial dimension principal symbols of the form (

In order to study these transitions, we must understand the backward-in-time Cauchy problem for such operators.

The function

Under this assumption, we prove an energy estimate for solutions with compact support with regularity

The proof relies on the construction of a suitable symmetrizer

Theorem 1

Here are some remarks concerning our result:

•

Concerning the case

for
d\geq 2
, that is for

\partial_{t}u=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}A_{j}(t,x)\partial_{x_{j}}u+F(t,x,u)u

our method described in the present paper may also apply.
Considering the principal symbol
A(t,x,\xi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq d}A_{j}(t,x)i\xi_{j}
, the analogous of (

) is for the principal symbol to have normal form

A(t,x,\xi)=i|\xi|\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
a(t,x,\omega)&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{with}\quad\omega=\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}

with

.

•

For higher dimensions for the system, our method may also apply for normal forms

\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&1&0&\ldots&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&\ldots&a_{N-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

We would need to adapt consequently our symmetrizer

as

\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&1&0&\ldots&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
a_{1}+\langle\xi\rangle^{-c}&a_{2}&\ldots&\ldots&a_{N-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

and the expected lower bound for the Gevrey index is then

(again, in the presence of source term

).

•

The general case

, with

regularity, could be treated by our method, even in the case where

.
The symmetrizer
S
could be defined in the same way.
The symbol

is still in

, thanks to Glaeser inequality.
The only main difference would be the care of the term
\partial_{t}S
in the energy estimate.
In the case
\partial_{t}a(0,x_{0})=0
, a time Glaeser inequality holds which allows to control
\partial_{t}a
in terms of

.

Finally we note that the main case of fully quasilinear systems

, such as Euler equations with Van der Waals laws or other physical meaningful systems, are out of reach of our current analysis and understanding.
The study of such systems should be a main topic in the future of our research.

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\,\langle{\rm op}(b)D^{\sigma}v_{2},{\rm op}(b)v_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\,\langle D^{-\sigma/2}\left[{\rm op}(b),D^{\sigma}\right]{\rm op}(b)^{-1}D^{-\sigma/2}\,D^{\sigma/2}{\rm op}(b)v_{2},D^{\sigma/2}{\rm op}(b)v_{2}\rangle

(3.9)

4
Pseudo-differential tools

This Section aims to remind a few tools of pseudo-differential calculus and of symbols associated to a general metric of the phase space.
We start first by study the symbol

We then define classes of symbols

4.1
Study of symbol

We define the symbol

Thanks to this notation, we may write the symbol

Lemma 4.1

(Bounds for

)
.

The symbol

satisfies the upper bound

The proof of the upper bound (

Lemma 4.11

(Composition)

About commutators:

Lemma 4.12

(Inversion of

)

For any

Proof.

Our aim is to solve the equation

We proceed by induction on

, using equality (
4.11

Denoting

\omega_{k}(p_{1},p_{2})=2^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=k}\frac{(-i)^{|\beta|}}{\alpha!\beta!}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}p_{1}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}p_{2}

equality (

Note that in particular

For

so that

Let

Then on one side

\displaystyle b\sharp\left(c_{\nu}-c_{\nu-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq k<\nu+1}\omega_{k}(b,c_{\nu}-c_{\nu-1})+S(\lambda^{-(\nu+1)},g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(c_{\nu}-c_{\nu-1})+S(\lambda^{-(\nu+1)},g)

as

, and on the other side thanks to the equation there holds

b\sharp\left(c_{\nu}-c_{\nu-1}\right)=\left(1-b\sharp c_{\nu-1}\right)+S(\lambda^{-(\nu+1)},g)

which leads to

c_{\nu}-c_{\nu-1}=b^{-1}\left(1-b\sharp c_{\nu-1}\right)

which is in
S(b^{-1},g)
.
∎

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|F(u)^{(\tau)}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{\sigma/2})}+\left\|F(u)^{(\tau)}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\right){\rm E_{1}}.

We conclude by

\left|{\rm E_{4}}\right|\leq C_{{\rm 4}}\,{\rm E_{1}}

(4.31)

for some
C_{{\rm 4}}>0
depending essentially on
\left\|F(u)^{(\tau)}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{\sigma/2})}+\left\|F(u)^{(\tau)}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}
.

4.4.4
Conclusion

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
1
, we put together the different constraints between
c
and
\sigma
that appear in the estimates of the energy.
First, combining constraint (
4.15
) and constraint (
4.23
), there holds

\frac{c}{2}=1-\sigma.

This equality between the parameter
c
, used to regularize the weakness in the hyperbolicity of the system, and the Gevrey index
\sigma
already appeared in the seminal paper
[
CJS83
]
.

Next, we gather constraints (
4.18
), (
4.26
), (
4.27
) and (
4.30
).
The last constraint implies immediately the expected lower bound for the Gevrey index

\sigma\geq 1/2.

(4.32)

The three other constraints are weaker, hence do not interfere in the lower bound.
However, as soon as constraint (
4.30
) breaks down (see Remark
4.16
), the lower bound for the Gevrey index is better.
Indeed, constraint (
4.26
) implies the inequality

, equivalent to

\sigma\geq\frac{1}{3}.

(4.33)

Note that both constraints (
4.18
) and (
4.27
) are equivalent, as
\nu
tends to infinity, to
c/2\leq 1
– which is the limitation of
c
imposed by the uncertainty principle for the metric
g
.

We prove Theorem
1
by taking
{\bm{\tau}}>C_{{\rm 2}}+C_{{\rm 3}}+C_{{\rm 4}}
, where the constants are defined respectively in (
4.25
), (
4.28
) and (
4.31
).

5.1
Glaeser-type inequalities

We start by recalling the Faà di Bruno formula on iterated derivatives of composition of functions:

Lemma 5.1

(Faà di Bruno formula)
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be two
C^{\infty}
functions. Then

\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\quad,\quad\frac{\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}(g\circ f)}{\alpha!\beta!}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq|\alpha+\beta|}\frac{g^{(k)}\circ f}{k!}\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})+\cdots+(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})=(\alpha,\beta)\atop(\alpha_{j},\beta_{j})\neq(0,0)}\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}\frac{\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{j}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{j}}f}{\alpha_{j}!\beta_{j}!}.

(5.1)

We recall that for a
d
-tuple
\alpha_{j}=(\alpha_{j}(1),\ldots,\alpha_{j}(d))
, we denote
\alpha_{j}!=\prod_{1\leq p\leq d}\alpha_{j}(p)!
, and
\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{j}}
means
\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{j}(1)}\circ\cdots\circ\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{j}(d)}
.
For further use, we denote

N(\alpha,k)=\left|\left\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\,\Big|\,\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=\alpha\,,\,\alpha_{j}\geq 1\right\}\right|.

(5.2)

By combinatorial arguments, for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
and
k\geq 1
there holds

N(\alpha,k)=\prod_{1\leq j\leq d}\binom{\alpha(j)-1}{k-1}.

By putting
f(y)=y^{n}
and
g(x)=e^{x}
in the Faà di Bruno formula, we obtain

n^{\alpha}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq|\alpha|}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{k}=\alpha\atop\alpha_{j}\geq 1}\binom{\alpha}{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}}.

Next we recall the classical Glaeser inequality (see
[
Gla63
]

Lemma 5.2

(Global Glaeser inequality)
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a non negative
C^{2}
function, such that
\partial_{x}^{2}f
is bounded.
Then

\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\;,\quad|\partial_{x}f(x)|^{2}\leq 2|\partial_{x}^{2}f|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}f(x).

(5.3)

The local result (inequality holds at any point) comes from a global assumption on
f
(non negativity of
f
, boundedness of
\partial_{x}^{2}f
).
The constant
2|\partial_{x}^{2}f|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}
is optimal.
The proof of the Lemma is classical, and is based on the integral Taylor expansion formula.

Local versions of the previous statement, that is with assumptions valid only in an open set of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, also exist.
For any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
, we denote

B_{r}(x_{0})=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,:\,|x-x_{0}|<r\right\}.

In all the following, we consider

a nonnegative,
C^{2}
function.
We give first a sharp version of a local Glaeser’s inequality, used in the present paper.

Lemma 5.3

(Sharp local Glaeser inequality)
.

Assuming that

\min_{x\in\overline{B}_{r}(x_{0})}f(x)>0

then, for any

and any
r^{\prime}<r
, there holds

\forall x\in\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0})\;,\quad|\partial_{x}f(x)|^{p}\leq\frac{\left(|\partial_{x}f|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0}))}\right)^{p}}{\min_{\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0})}f}f(x).

(5.4)

Proof.

The inequality (
5.4
) is straightforward as there holds both

\forall x\in\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0})\;,\quad\min_{\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0})}f\leq f(x)

and

\forall x\in\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0})\;,\quad|\partial_{x}f(x)|^{p}\leq\left(|\partial_{x}f|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{B}_{r^{\prime}}(x_{0}))}\right)^{p}.

∎

g_{X}(X-Y)\leq r^{2}\implies C^{-1}g_{Y}(T)\leq g_{X}(T)\leq Cg_{Y}(T).

(5.11)

2.

The metric

3.

The metric

(5.13)

The metric

is defined by

g_{X}^{\sigma}(Y)=\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}|Y_{1}|^{2}+a_{\natural}(t,X)|Y_{2}|^{2}.

Proof.

We follow here partially the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
CN07
]
.
We remind that
g_{X}(Y)
reads

g_{X}(Y)=\frac{|Y_{1}|^{2}}{a_{\natural}(t,X)}+\frac{|Y_{2}|^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}

with
X=(X_{1},X_{2})
(the first component has to be seen as
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and the second component as
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
).

1.
Assume that there holds

\frac{|X_{2}-Y_{2}|^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq r_{2}^{2}

(5.14)

for some
r_{2}>0
.
This implies in particular that

\displaystyle|Y_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|X_{2}|^{2}+2|X_{2}-Y_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(1+r_{2}^{2})|X_{2}|^{2}

hence
\langle Y_{2}\rangle\leq\sqrt{2}(1+r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}\langle X_{2}\rangle
.
The same way we prove

|X_{2}|^{2}\leq 2r_{2}^{2}|X_{2}|^{2}+2|Y_{2}|^{2}

which leads to
(1-2r_{2}^{2})^{1/2}\langle X_{2}\rangle\leq\sqrt{2}\langle Y_{2}\rangle
as soon as
r_{2}<1/\sqrt{2}
.
We have just proved the following

\frac{|X_{2}-Y_{2}|^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq r_{2}^{2}\implies C_{2}^{-1}\frac{|T_{2}|^{2}}{\langle Y_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq\frac{|T_{2}|^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq C_{2}\frac{|T_{2}|^{2}}{\langle Y_{2}\rangle^{2}}\quad,\quad\forall\,T_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}

with
r_{2}<1/\sqrt{2}
and
C_{2}>0
depending only on
r_{2}
.

Next, we consider the part
\frac{|Y_{1}|^{2}}{a_{\natural}(t,X)}
of the metric
g
.
Assume that there holds

\frac{|X_{1}-Y_{1}|^{2}}{a_{\natural}(t,X)}\leq r_{1}^{2}

(5.15)

for some
X
and
Y
in
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
and
r_{1}>0
, and that (
5.14
) still holds with
r_{2}<1/\sqrt{2}
.
We aim to compare
a_{\natural}(t,X)
and
a_{\natural}(t,Y)
.
As
a(t,x)
is smooth with respect to
x
, there holds

a(t,X_{1})=a(t,Y_{1})+(X_{1}-Y_{1})\partial_{x}a(t,Y_{1})+R

with
|R|\leq|X_{1}-Y_{1}|^{2}\sup\left|\partial_{x}^{2}a(t,x)\right|
.
Note that, as
a
has compact support in
x
, that
\sup\left|\partial_{x}^{2}a(t,x)\right|
is independent of
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
.
Then, using (
5.15
) we get

|R|\leq C_{2}^{\prime}a_{\natural}(t,X)r_{1}^{2}

and

\left|(X_{1}-Y_{1})\partial_{x}a(t,Y_{1})\right|\leq C_{2}^{\prime\prime}a_{\natural}(t,X)^{1/2}r_{1}a_{\natural}(t,Y)^{1/2}

thanks to Lemma
4.2
.
Note that neither
C_{2}^{\prime}
nor
C_{2}^{\prime\prime}
depend on
X
or
Y
.
Then, by positivity of
a
, there holds

\displaystyle a(t,X_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{\natural}(t,Y)+C_{2}^{\prime\prime}a_{\natural}(t,X)^{1/2}r_{1}a_{\natural}(t,Y)^{1/2}+C_{2}^{\prime}a_{\natural}(t,X)r_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2a_{\natural}(t,Y)+\left(C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{2}^{\prime\prime 2}/4\right)r_{1}^{2}a_{\natural}(t,X).

As soon as

C_{2}^{\prime}+C_{2}^{\prime\prime 2}/4<r_{1}^{-2}

a_{\natural}(t,X)\leq C_{2}a_{\natural}(t,Y)

which suffices to prove finally (
5.11
).

2. The uncertainty principle for general metrics in the phase space reads in our case (
5.12
).
As the inequality should hold for all times, in particular for

this leads to

\lambda(0,x,\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{-c/2}\langle\xi\rangle\geq 1\quad,\quad\forall\,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}

hence

.
As
\lambda
is decreasing as time goes by, this inequality is sufficient to ensure (
5.12
) for all times.

3. As
g
is slowly varying, inequality (
5.13
) is satisfied if
g_{X}(X-Y)\leq r^{2}
.
Assume then that
g_{X}(X-Y)>r^{2}
.
As we note that
g^{\sigma}_{X}(X-Y)=a_{\natural}(t,X)\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}g_{X}(X-Y)
, this implies that

\displaystyle g^{\sigma}_{X}(X-Y)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle a_{\natural}(t,X)\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}r^{2}

(5.16)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2-c}r^{2}

by nonnegativity of
a
.

For all
T\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, there holds

\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{Y}(T)}\leq\max\left(\frac{a_{\natural}(t,Y)}{a_{\natural}(t,X)}\,,\,\frac{\langle Y_{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}\right).

To prove (
5.13

\frac{a_{\natural}(t,Y)}{a_{\natural}(t,X)}\leq C^{\prime}\left(1+g_{X}^{\sigma}(X-Y)\right)^{N^{\prime}}

(5.17)

for some
C^{\prime}>0
,

and

\frac{\langle Y_{2}\rangle^{2}}{\langle X_{2}\rangle^{2}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}\left(1+g_{X}^{\sigma}(X-Y)\right)^{N^{\prime\prime}}

for some
C^{\prime\prime}>0
,

.

We proceed as for the point
1.
, proving there is a constant

Here, the control of

\displaystyle|X_{1}-Y_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle g_{X}^{\sigma}(X-Y)

\displaystyle C\langle X_{2}\rangle^{-c}g_{X}^{\sigma}(X-Y)^{1+c/(2-c)}

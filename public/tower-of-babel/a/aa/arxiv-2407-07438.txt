Near-order relation of power means
Jinmi Hwang and Sejong Kim

Abstract
. On the setting of positive definite operators we study the near-order properties of power means such as the quasi-arithmetic mean (Hölder mean) and Rényi power mean. We see the monotonicity of spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean on parameters with respect to the near-order and the near-order relationship between the spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean. Furthermore, the monotonicity of quasi-arithmetic mean on parameters and the convergence of Rényi power mean to the log-Euclidean mean with respect to the near-order have been established. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 47B65, 15B48 Keywords : Near-order, metric geometric mean, spectral geometric mean, Wasserstein mean, quasi-arithmetic mean, Rényi power mean



1.
Introduction

Kubo and Ando
[
19
]
have established the theory of operator means for two variables on the open convex cone of positive definite operators. The crucial property of Kubo-Ando’s operator mean is the monotonicity for Loewner partial order:

A\sigma B\leq C\sigma D\quad
whenever
\quad A\leq C
and
B\leq D

for positive definite operators
A,B,C,D
, where
\sigma
denotes the Kubo-Ando’s operator mean. This is related to the operator monotone function corresponding to the Kubo-Ando’s operator mean.
On the other hand, there are lots of two-variable means which do not fulfill the monotonicity such as the spectral geometric mean
[
6
,
21
]
and Wasserstein mean
[
2
,
13
]
.

One of the important multi-variable means of positive definite matrices is a least squares mean for the Riemannian trace metric, named a Cartan (Karcher) mean. It has been known in
[
16
]
that the Cartan mean coincides with a unique positive definite solution
X
of the Karcher equation

\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\log(X^{1/2}A_{j}^{-1}X^{1/2})=0,

where
A_{j}
’s are positive definite matrices and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is a positive probability vector. This allows us to define the Karcher mean of positive definite operators
[
20
]
. The monotonicity of Karcher mean is a long-standing open problem, but it has been proved in
[
20
]
by using the approach of power means.

New order relation, called a
near-order
, has been recently introduced in
[
5
]
:

A\preceq B\quad
if and only if
\quad A^{-1}\#B\geq I
.

Here,
A^{-1}\#B=A^{-1/2}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2}A^{-1/2}
is the geometric mean of
A^{-1}
and
B
, which is a typical example of the Kubo-Ando’s operator mean. One can easily see that
A\leq B
implies
A\preceq B
. Unlike the Loewner-Heinz inequality, an interesting property of the near-order is as follows:

A\preceq B\quad
implies
\quad A^{p}\preceq B^{p}\quad
for all
p\geq 1
.

One of the main results is the monotonicity of spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean on parameters with respect to the near-order.

Several near-order inequalities of spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean for two variables have been shown in
[
5
,
8
]
, but those of multi-variable means are rare. In this paper we study two kinds of the multi-variable power mean, named the quasi-arithmetic mean and Rényi power mean. For given positive definite operators
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
, the quasi-arithmetic mean
Q_{p}
of order
p\neq 0
is defined by

Q_{p}(\omega;A_{1},\dots,A_{n})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

and the Rényi power mean
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}
for
0\leq t<z\leq 1
is defined as a unique positive definite solution
X
of the equation

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}.

The main results of multi-variable power means with respect to the near-order are the monotonicity of quasi-arithmetic mean on both variables and parameters and the inequalities of Rényi power mean with the quasi-arithmetic mean and log-Euclidean mean.
In particular, the monotonicity of quasi-arithmetic mean provides the following chain: for
0<p\leq q\leq 1

Q_{-1/p}\leq Q_{-1/q}\leq Q_{-1}=\mathcal{H}\preceq Q_{-q}\preceq Q_{-p}\preceq\mathrm{LE}\preceq Q_{p}\preceq Q_{q}\leq\mathcal{A}=Q_{1}\leq Q_{1/q}\leq Q_{1/p},

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{LE}=\lim_{p\to 0}Q_{p}
denotes the log-Euclidean mean.

As an extension of Lie-Trotter formula, new type of multi-variable mean, called a Lie-Trotter mean, has been introduced in
[
12
]
. It is known that multi-variable means between the arithmetic and harmonic means with respect to the Loewner order are Lie-Trotter means, for instance, Karcher mean. We generalize such a consequence to multi-variable means between the arithmetic and harmonic means with respect to the near-order.

2.
Two-variable operator means

Let
B(\mathcal{H})
be the Banach space of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and let
S(\mathcal{H})\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be the closed subspace of all self-adjoint operators. We call
A\in S(\mathcal{H})
positive semi-definite if
\langle x,Ax\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, and positive definite if
\langle x,Ax\rangle>0
for all nonzero
x\in\mathcal{H}
. We denote as
\mathbb{P}\subset S(\mathcal{H})
the open convex cone of all positive definite operators. The group
GL
of all invertible operators transitively acts on
\mathbb{P}
via congruence transformation. On the finite-dimensional setting
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{m}
we write as
\mathbb{H}_{m}
and
\mathbb{P}_{m}
respectively the real vector space of all
m\times m
Hermitian matrices and the open convex cone of all
m\times m
positive definite matrices.

The Thompson metric
d_{T}
on
\mathbb{P}
is defined by

d_{T}(A,B)=\max\{\log M(A/B),\log M(B/A)\},

where
M(B/A)=\inf\{\lambda>0:B\leq\lambda A\}
.
Here,
\leq
denotes the
Loewner partial order
on
S(\mathcal{H})
.
Note from
[
3
,
22
]
that

d_{T}(A,B)=\|\log A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\|

for the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
d_{T}
is a complete metric on
\mathbb{P}
.
The following are well-known fundamental properties for
d_{T}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A,B,C,D\in\mathbb{P}
. The Thompson metric satisfies

(1)

d_{T}(A,B)=d_{T}(A^{-1},B^{-1})=d_{T}(MAM^{*},MBM^{*})
for any
M\in\mathrm{GL}
;

(2)

d_{T}(A+B,C+D)\leq\max\{d_{T}(A,C),d_{T}(B,D)\}
;

(3)

d_{T}(A^{t},B^{t})\leq td_{T}(A,B)
for any
t\in[0,1]
.

The
metric geometric mean
of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
is defined as

A\#_{t}B:=A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^{t}A^{1/2}

(2.1)

for
t\in[0,1]
. We simply write
A\#B=A\#_{1/2}B
, and note that
A\#B
is the unique solution
X\in\mathbb{P}
of the Riccati equation
XA^{-1}X=B
.
By Lemma
2.1
the metric geometric mean satisfies the following convexity for
d_{T}
:

d_{T}(A\#_{s}B,C\#_{t}D)\leq(1-t)\,d_{T}(A,C)+t\,d_{T}(B,D)+|s-t|\,d_{T}(C,D)

(2.2)

for
A,B,C,D\in\mathbb{P}
and
s,t\in[0,1]
. This means that the map

[0,1]\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\ni(t,A,B)\mapsto A\#_{t}B\in\mathbb{P}

is continuous for the Thompson metric. The definition (
2.1
) can be extended to all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The
spectral geometric mean
of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
for
t\in[0,1]
is defined as

A\natural_{t}B=(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}A(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}.

(2.3)

It is first introduced for
t=1/2
by Fiedler and Pták
[
6
]
, and many properties analogous to the metric geometric mean have been studied
[
10
,
18
,
21
]
. Analogous to the metric geometric mean, the definition (
2.3
) can be extended to all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Especially from
[
9
]
, the spectral geometric mean is a geodesic for the semi-metric
d(A,B)=2\|\log(A^{-1}\#B)\|
on
\mathbb{P}
:

d(A\natural_{s}B,A\natural_{t}B)\leq|s-t|\,d(A,B)

for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The
Wasserstein mean
of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
for
t\in[0,1]
is defined as

A\diamond_{t}B=(1-t)^{2}A+t^{2}B+t(1-t)\left[A(A^{-1}\#B)+(A^{-1}\#B)A\right].

This can be written as

A\diamond_{t}B=\left[I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)\right]A\left[I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)\right],

(2.4)

where
A\nabla_{t}B:=(1-t)A+tB
is the weighted arithmetic mean of
A
and
B
. Since the Wasserstein mean
A\diamond_{t}B
is the congruence transformation on
A\in\mathbb{P}
via
I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)
, it can be defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. It is known from
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
that
A\diamond_{t}B
is the unique solution
X\in\mathbb{P}
of the equation

I=(1-t)(A\#X^{-1})+t(B\#X^{-1}).

In the finite-dimensional setting of
\mathbb{P}_{m}
, Wasserstein mean is the least squares mean for Bures-Wasserstein distance:

A\diamond_{t}B=\underset{X\in\mathbb{P}_{m}}{\arg\min}(1-t)d_{W}^{2}(X,A)+td_{W}^{2}(X,B),

where
d_{W}(A,B)=\left[\tr(A+B)-2\tr(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2}\right]^{1/2}
is the Bures-Wasserstein distance of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
.
See
[
10
,
13
,
14
]
for more properties of the Wasserstein mean.

3.
Near-order of positive definite operators

The metric geometric mean satisfies the monotonicity on variables with respect to the Loewner order:

A\leq B\ \text{and}\ C\leq D\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\#_{t}C\leq B\#_{t}D\quad\textrm{for all}\ t\in[0,1].

By the monotonicity, if
A\leq B
then
A^{-1}\#B\geq I.

From this point of view, Dumitru and Franco
[
5
]
introduced a new relation
\preceq
on
\mathbb{P}
, which is called
near-order
.
For
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
,

A\preceq B\quad\text{if and only if}\quad A^{-1}\#B\geq I.

(3.5)

The reflexive, antisymmetric, and modified versions of transitive properties, called the near-transitivity, hold for the near-order.
Moreover, for
A,B\in\mathbb{P}

A\leq B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\log A\leq\log B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\preceq B.

(3.6)

Note that
A\ll B
, called the
chaotic order
, if and only if
\log A\leq\log B
.
Some properties for chaotic order, and monotonicity and convexity properties for near-order have been studied in
[
7
]
and
[
5
]
respectively.

A map
f:S(\mathcal{H})\to S(\mathcal{H})
is operator monotone (monotone increasing) if

f(A)\leq f(B)\quad
whenever
\quad A\leq B
.

Note that the map
X\mapsto X^{p}
for
p\in[0,1]
on
\mathbb{P}
is monotone increasing and the map
X\mapsto X^{p}
for
p\in[-1,0]
is monotone decreasing with respect to the Loewner order
[
1
]
. This is known as the
Loewner-Heinz inequality
.
On the other hand, the following holds for the near-order.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
5
]

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
. Then
A\preceq B
implies

(i)

A^{p}\preceq B^{p}\quad
for
p\geq 1
, and

(ii)

B^{p}\preceq A^{p}\quad
for
p\leq-1
.

The in-betweenness property of the metric geometric mean for the Loewner order is well-known:

A\leq A\#_{t}B\leq B\quad\text{whenever}\quad A\leq B

for any
t\in[0,1]
. Unfortunately this is not satisfied for the spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean.
On the other hand, their in-betweenness properties for the near-order have been shown:

A\preceq A\natural_{t}B\preceq B\quad\textrm{and}\quad A\preceq A\diamond_{t}B\preceq B.

In addition, we summarize the equivalent relations for
A\preceq B
in terms of metric, spectral geometric means and Wasserstein mean.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
5
,
8
]

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

A\preceq B
,

(2)

A^{-1}\#B\geq I
,

(3)

A\#B^{-1}\leq I
,

(4)

A\natural_{t}B\succeq A
for some
t\in(0,1]
,

(5)

A\natural_{t}B\preceq B
for some
t\in[0,1)
,

(6)

A\diamond_{t}B\succeq A
for some
t\in(0,1]
,

(7)

A\diamond_{t}B\preceq B
for some
t\in[0,1)
.

For any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s\leq t
, if
A\leq B
then
A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\geq I
so

A\#_{s}B=A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^{s}A^{1/2}\leq A^{1/2}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})^{t}A^{1/2}=A\#_{t}B.

That is, the geodesic curve
A\#_{t}B
is monotone increasing on
t\in\mathbb{R}
in terms of the Loewner order when
A\leq B
.

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
, characterizations of the monotonicity for the spectral geometric and Wasserstein means on parameters with respect to the near-order have been shown on
\mathbb{P}_{m}
by applying Lemma
3.3
. These can be extended to the infinite-dimensional setting
\mathbb{P}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
8
]

Let
X,Y\in\mathbb{P}
such that
X,Y
commute. Then

X\leq Y
if and only if
XAX\preceq YAY
for any
A\in\mathbb{P}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s<t
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

A\preceq B
,

(ii)

A\natural_{s}B\preceq A\natural_{t}B
,

(iii)

A\diamond_{s}B\preceq A\diamond_{t}B
.

Proof.

The equivalences among (i), (ii) and (iii) have been proved in
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
, except (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s<t
.

We prove that (iii) implies (i). When
s=0
and
t=1
, the conclusion is obviously true. So we need to prove it when
s\neq 0
and
t\neq 1
.

Set
C=A^{-1}\#B
. By assumption (iii),
(I\nabla_{s}C)A(I\nabla_{s}C)\preceq(I\nabla_{t}C)A(I\nabla_{t}C)
for certain
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s<t
so

I\leq\left[(I\nabla_{s}C)^{-1}A^{-1}(I\nabla_{s}C)^{-1}\right]\#\left[(I\nabla_{t}C)A(I\nabla_{t}C)\right].

Taking congruence transformations by
I\nabla_{s}C
and
A^{1/2}
consecutively yield

\begin{split}A^{1/2}(I\nabla_{s}C)^{2}A^{1/2}&\leq I\#A^{1/2}(I\nabla_{s}C)(I\nabla_{t}C)A(I\nabla_{t}C)(I\nabla_{s}C)A^{1/2}\\
&=I\#(A^{1/2}(I\nabla_{s}C)(I\nabla_{t}C)A^{1/2})^{2}=A^{1/2}(I\nabla_{s}C)(I\nabla_{t}C)A^{1/2}.\end{split}

The first equality follows from the fact that
I\nabla_{s}C
and
I\nabla_{t}C
commute. Taking congruence transformation by
A^{-1/2}
we obtain
(I\nabla_{s}C)^{2}\leq(I\nabla_{s}C)(I\nabla_{t}C)
, and hence,
I\nabla_{s}C\leq I\nabla_{t}C
. This is equivalent to
C\geq I
because
s<t
, which means
A\preceq B
.
∎

The following shows the monotonicity of spectral geometric and Wasserstein means on variables with respect to the near-order.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
A,B,C\in\mathbb{P}
such that
(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2}A^{-1}(A^{1/2}CA^{1/2})^{1/2}
is self-adjoint. Then
B\leq C
implies

(1)

A\natural_{t}B\preceq A\natural_{t}C
for any
t\in(0,1]
,

(2)

A\natural_{t}B\succeq A\natural_{t}C
for any
t\in[-1,0)
, and

(3)

A\diamond_{t}B\preceq A\diamond_{t}C
for any
t\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Set
X=A^{-1}\#B
and
Y=A^{-1}\#C
. Then
X\leq Y
by the monotonicity of metric geometric mean with
B\leq C
, and
X^{t}\leq Y^{t}
for
0<t\leq 1
by the Loewner-Heinz inequality. By assumption,

(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2}A^{-1}(A^{1/2}CA^{1/2})^{1/2}=(A^{1/2}CA^{1/2})^{1/2}A^{-1}(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2},

and
XY=YX
. By Lemma
3.3

A\natural_{t}B=X^{t}AX^{t}\preceq Y^{t}AY^{t}=A\natural_{t}C.

Similarly, we can obtain (2) for
t=-s
where
s\in(0,1]
. Moreover,
X\leq Y
yields
I\nabla_{t}X\leq I\nabla_{t}Y
for
t\in(0,1]
, and
XY=YX
yields that
I\nabla_{t}X,I\nabla_{t}Y
commute. By Lemma
3.3

A\diamond_{t}B=[I\nabla_{t}X]A[I\nabla_{t}X]\preceq[I\nabla_{t}Y]A[I\nabla_{t}Y]=A\diamond_{t}C.

∎

Motivated from
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
, Gan and Huang
[
8
]
have shown the near-order relation between the spectral geometric mean and Wasserstein mean of positive definite matrices. It can be extended to the infinite-dimensional setting
\mathbb{P}
:

A\natural_{t}B\preceq A\diamond_{t}B

(3.7)

for
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
and
t\in[0,1]
. Since the near-order is not invariant under congruence transformation, the following is new.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
and
t\in[0,1]
. Then

(1)

A\leq B
implies
A^{-1}\diamond B\geq I
, and

(2)

A^{1/2}(A\natural_{t}B)A^{1/2}\preceq A^{1/2}(A\diamond_{t}B)A^{1/2}
.

Proof.

Note from
[
14
, Remark 3.5]
that
A\leq B
is equivalent to
A^{-1}\natural B\geq I
. By (
3.7
) and applying the near-transitivity in
[
5
]
, we obtain (1).

Since
(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}\leq I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)
,

A^{1/2}(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}A^{1/2}\leq A^{1/2}[I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)]A^{1/2},

and
A^{1/2}(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}A^{1/2}\preceq A^{1/2}[I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)]A^{1/2}
. By Proposition
3.1
(i)

\left[A^{1/2}(A^{-1}\#B)^{t}A^{1/2}\right]^{2}\preceq\left[A^{1/2}[I\nabla_{t}(A^{-1}\#B)]A^{1/2}\right]^{2},

which completes the proof of (2).
∎

The operator fidelity of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
is given by

F(A,B)=(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})^{1/2}.

Indeed,
\tr F(A,B)
for density matrices
A,B
is known as the quantum fidelity.
In general,
F(A,B)\neq F(B,A)
although
\tr F(A,B)=\tr F(B,A)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{P}
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(1)

A^{1/2}\preceq F(A,B)
implies
A^{2^{n-1}}\preceq F(A^{2^{n}},B)
, and

(2)

F(B,A)\preceq B^{1/2}
implies
F(B^{2^{n}},A)\preceq B^{2^{n-1}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
(i), the assumption of (1) implies
A\preceq A^{1/2}BA^{1/2}
, and by definition of the near-order

A^{-1}\#(A^{1/2}BA^{1/2})\geq I.

This is equivalent to
(ABA)^{1/2}\geq A
, and hence,
A\preceq(ABA)^{1/2}=F(A^{2},B)
. So (1) holds for
n=1
.

Assume that
A^{2^{k-1}}\preceq F(A^{2^{k}},B)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Replacing
A^{2^{k}}
by
C
, the assumption can be written as
C^{1/2}\preceq F(C,B)
.
By the preceding argument for
n=1
we get
C\preceq F(C^{2},B)
, that is,
A^{2^{k}}\preceq F(A^{2^{k+1}},B)
. By induction on
n
, we proved (1).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

We recall the
entrywise eigenvalue relation

\leq_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{H}_{m}
.
Let
\lambda_{1}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{m}(A)
, where
\lambda_{i}(A)
’s are real eigenvalues of
A\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
. For
A,B\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
we write as

A\leq_{\lambda}B\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lambda_{i}(A)\leq\lambda_{i}(B)\ \textrm{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq m.

(3.8)

This relation
\leq_{\lambda}
is reflexive and transitive, but not antisymmetric. We write as
A=_{\lambda}B
if and only if
\lambda_{i}(A)=\lambda_{i}(B)
for all
i
.
Note from
[
8
]
that for
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}

A\leq B\ \Longrightarrow\ A\preceq B\ \Longrightarrow\ A\leq_{\lambda}B\ \Longrightarrow\ A\prec_{w\log}B,

(3.9)

where
A\prec_{w\log}B
denotes that
A
is weakly log-majorized by
B
. By definition of the weak log-majorization, one can easily see that
A\prec_{w\log}B
and
B\prec_{w\log}A
imply
A=_{\lambda}B
.

(1)

For
A,B\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
,
A=_{\lambda}B
if and only if
A,B
are unitarily similar. Indeed, since
A,B\in\mathbb{H}_{m}
, they are unitarily diagonalizable, that is,

A=U_{A}D_{A}U_{A}^{*}\quad
and
\quad B=U_{B}D_{B}U_{B}^{*}
,

where
U_{A},U_{B}
are unitary matrices and
D_{A},D_{B}
are diagonal matrices whose main diagonal entries are real eigenvalues of
A,B
respectively. If
A=_{\lambda}B
then there exists a permutation matrix
P
such that
D_{B}=PD_{A}P^{*}
. Thus,

B=U_{B}PD_{A}P^{*}U_{B}^{*}=U_{B}P(U_{A}^{*}AU_{A})P^{*}U_{B}^{*}=VAV^{*},

where
V:=U_{B}PU_{A}^{*}
is a unitary matrix.
The converse is true from
[
11
, Corollary 1.3.4]
.

(2)

It is natural to ask what kinds of
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
yield that the reverse implications of (
3.9
) hold.
For instance, assuming for
A,B\in\mathbb{P}_{m}
that
A\preceq B
implies
A\leq B
, what can we say about
A
and
B
?
The following are sufficient conditions to satisfy the reverse implications of (
3.9
):

(a)

A\leq I\leq B
,

(b)

AB=BA
.

4.
Monotonicity of quasi-arithmetic mean with respect to the near-order

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
, the simplex of all positive probability vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
quasi-arithmetic mean
of order
p\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
is defined by

Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

It is also called the Hölder mean or Pythagorean mean.
Note that for
p=1
and
p=-1

Q_{1}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}=:\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{-1}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{-1}\right)^{-1}=:\mathcal{H}(\omega;\mathbb{A}),

which are the arithmetic and harmonic means, respectively.
Moreover, the quasi-arithmetic mean converges to the log-Euclidean mean as
p
goes to
0
:

\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\exp\left[\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}\log A_{j}\right]=:\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(4.10)

Also, the quasi-arithmetic mean satisfies the duality:

Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=Q_{-p}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})^{-1}.

(4.11)

For
p\geq 1,
the quasi-arithmetic satisfies the following monotonicity on parameters with respect to the Loewner order:

Theorem 4.1
.

[
17
]

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
.
For
1\leq s\leq t<\infty,

Q_{-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq Q_{-s}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq\mathcal{H}(\omega,\mathbb{A})\leq\mathcal{A}(\omega,\mathbb{A})\leq Q_{s}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq Q_{t}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

Unfortunately, the quasi-arithmetic mean is not monotone for
p\in(-1,1)
with respect to the Loewner order.
Also, it is not monotone on variables, that is,
Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\nleq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{B})
if
A_{j}\leq B_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n,
where
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n}),\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
; see
[
17
]
for more information.

On the other hand, we show that the monotonicity of the quasi-arithmetic mean on variables with respect to the near-order.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n}),\mathbb{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
.
If
A_{j}\leq B_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
, then for
0<p\leq 1
or
-1\leq p<0

Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{B}).

(4.12)

Proof.

We first prove (
4.12
) for
0<p\leq 1
.
If
A_{j}\leq B_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, then
A_{j}^{p}\leq B_{j}^{p}
by the Loewner-Heinz inequality.
It implies that
\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}B_{j}^{p}
.
So we obtain

\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\preceq\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}B_{j}^{p}.

Since
\frac{1}{p}\geq 1
, applying Proposition
3.1
(i) to the above relation yields

Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\preceq\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}B_{j}^{p}\right)^{1/p}=Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{B}).

By the similar argument with Proposition
3.1
(ii), we can prove (
4.12
) for
-1\leq p<0
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
with
f(x)\preceq g(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\lim_{x\rightarrow a}f(x)\preceq\lim_{x\rightarrow a}g(x).

Proof.

By assumption,
f(x)^{-1}\#g(x)\geq I
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the metric geometric mean and inversion are continuous for the Thompson metric by (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.1
(1),

\left(\lim_{x\rightarrow a}f(x)\right)^{-1}\#\lim_{x\rightarrow a}g(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow a}\left[f(x)^{-1}\#g(x)\right]\geq I.

Thus, we obtain the desired relation.
∎

The log-majorization relationship among the Cartan, log-Euclidean, and Wasserstein mean has been shown
[
2
]
: for
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}

\Lambda(\omega;\mathbb{A})\prec_{\log}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\prec_{w\log}\Omega(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(4.13)

Since
\Omega(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
, we obtain from (
3.9
)

\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\prec_{w\log}\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(4.14)

We do not provide only stronger relation between the log-Euclidean and arithmetic means, but also prove the monotonicity of the quasi-arithmetic mean for parameter with respect to the near-order.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
.
For
0\leq p\leq q\leq 1,

\mathcal{H}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{-q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{-p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(4.15)

Proof.

We prove (
4.15
) with the following steps.

(1)

We first show
Q_{q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for
0\leq q\leq 1
.
Since the map
X\rightarrow X^{q}
for
0\leq q\leq 1
is operator concave on
\mathbb{P}
,
we get
\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{q}\leq\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}\right)^{p}
so
\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{q}\preceq\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}\right)^{p}
.
Using Proposition
3.1
(i), we obtain

Q_{q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\preceq\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}=\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

Similarly, we get
\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{-q}\preceq\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{-1}\right)^{q}
by the concavity of the map
X\rightarrow X^{q}
for
0\leq q\leq 1
.
Applying Proposition
3.1
(ii), we get

\mathcal{H}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A^{-1}_{j}\right)^{-1}\preceq\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A^{-q}_{j}\right)^{-1/q}=Q_{-q}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(2)

We show
Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for
0\leq p\leq q\leq 1
.
Since
0<\frac{p}{q}\leq 1
, the previous result (1) yields
\displaystyle\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p/q}\right)^{q/p}\preceq\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}
. Replacing
A_{j}
by
A_{j}^{q}
we have

\left(\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{p}\right)^{q/p}\preceq\sum^{n}_{j=1}w_{j}A_{j}^{q}.

Since
\frac{1}{q}\geq 1
we obtain the desired relation by Proposition
3.1
(i).

(3)

Now we prove
Q_{-q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{-p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
for
0\leq p\leq q\leq 1
.
Applying the duality of quasi-arithmetic mean (
4.11
) to the above result (2) with
-1\leq-q\leq-p\leq 0
,

Q_{-q}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=Q_{q}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})^{-1}\preceq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})^{-1}=Q_{-p}(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

(4)

Using Lemma
4.3
and the property (
4.10
), we obtain
\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
and
Q_{-p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
.

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

The quasi-arithmetic mean of order
p
converges to the log-Euclidean mean as
p\to 0
with respect to the near-order, in the sense that for
\mathbb{A}\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega\in\Delta_{n}

\begin{split}Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})&\searrow_{\succeq}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad\textrm{as}\quad p\to 0^{+},\\
Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A})&\nearrow_{\preceq}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad\textrm{as}\quad p\to 0^{-}.\end{split}

Remark 4.6
.

For
1\leq p\leq q
the following chain holds:

Q_{-q}\leq Q_{-p}\leq\mathcal{H}\preceq Q_{-1/p}\preceq Q_{-1/q}\preceq\mathrm{LE}\preceq Q_{1/q}\preceq Q_{1/p}\leq\mathcal{A}\leq Q_{p}\leq Q_{q}.

5.
Boundedness of Rényi power mean with respect to the near-order

The Rényi power mean, introduced in
[
4
]
, is defined as a unique solution
X\in\mathbb{P}
of the equation

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}Q_{t,z}(A_{j},X)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}

(5.16)

for
0\leq t<z\leq 1
. We denote it as
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
, where
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
.

Remark 5.1
.

The function
f:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}
defined by

f(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z},\quad X\in\mathbb{P}

for
0\leq t<z\leq 1
is monotone increasing. Moreover, the Banach fixed point theorem yields that

\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{k}(Z)=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad\textrm{for any}\ Z\in\mathbb{P}.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
,
S\in\mathrm{GL}
, and
\sigma
a permutation on
\{1,\dots,n\}
.
For convenience, we denote

\begin{split}\mathbb{A}^{p}&=(A_{1}^{p},\ldots,A_{n}^{p})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\\
S\mathbb{A}S^{*}&=(SA_{1}S^{*},\ldots,SA_{n}S^{*})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\\
\mathbb{A}_{\sigma}&=(A_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,A_{\sigma(n)})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\\
\omega_{\sigma}&=(w_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,w_{\sigma(n)})\in\Delta_{n},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\mathbb{A}^{(k)}&=(\underbrace{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}},\dots,\underbrace{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}})\in\mathbb{P}^{nk}\\
\omega^{(k)}&=\frac{1}{k}(\underbrace{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}},\dots,\underbrace{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}})\in\Delta_{nk},\end{split}

whose number of blocks is
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
. Then

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})=Q_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
if
A_{j}
’s commute;

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;c\mathbb{A})=c\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
for any
c>0
;

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega_{\sigma};\mathbb{A}_{\sigma})=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
for any permutation
\sigma
on
\{1,\dots,n\}
;

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega^{(k)};\mathbb{A}^{(k)})=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;U\mathbb{A}U^{*})=U\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})U^{*}\quad
for any unitary operator
U
;

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})\geq\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{-1}
;

(7)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\|A_{j}\|\quad
for
1/2\leq t<z\leq 1
.

Proof.

All items (1)-(5) and (7) are already proved in
[
4
, Proposition 4]
.

(6) Let
X=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})^{-1}
. Then
X^{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-1})
, and by the arithmetic-harmonic mean inequality

X=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{-z}\right]^{-1}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}=f(X).

By Remark
5.1

X\leq f(X)\leq\cdots\leq f^{k}(X)
for all
k\geq 1
. Taking limit as
k\to\infty
yields
X\leq\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

For
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\mathbb{P}_{m}^{n}

\det\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\geq\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\det A_{j})^{w_{j}}.

(5.17)

Indeed, by using the joint concavity of
\log\det:\mathbb{P}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
to (
5.16
) for
X=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})

\begin{split}\log\det X&\geq z\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\log\det\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)\\
&=(1-t)\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\log\det A_{j}+t\log\det X.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain the determinantal inequality (
5.17
). Furthermore, the equality in (
5.17
) holds if and only if
A_{1}=\cdots=A_{n}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then for
0\leq t<z\leq 1

(i)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{\frac{1}{1-t}}\preceq Q_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
when
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq I
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{\frac{1}{1-t}}\succeq Q_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
when
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\geq I
.

Proof.

Assume that
X=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\leq I
for
0\leq t<z\leq 1
. Since
\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}\leq A_{j}^{1-t}
for all
j
, we have

X=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\left(A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}X^{\frac{t}{z}}A_{j}^{\frac{1-t}{2z}}\right)^{z}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}A_{j}^{1-t}

(5.18)

and
\displaystyle X\preceq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}A_{j}^{1-t}
. Since
\frac{1}{1-t}\geq 1
, taking the
1/(1-t)
-power on both sides and applying Proposition
3.1
(i) yield
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{\frac{1}{1-t}}\preceq Q_{1-t}(\omega;\mathbb{A})
.

By the similar argument we can prove (ii).
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Theorem
5.4
together with Theorem
4.4
we obtain the near-order relation between the Rényi power mean and log-Euclidean mean:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})^{\frac{1}{1-t}}\succeq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\quad
when
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\geq I
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then for
0\leq t<z\leq 1

\lim_{p\to 0^{-}}\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}\preceq\lim_{p\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p},

(5.19)

when the limits exist. In addition,

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to 0^{+}}\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}\preceq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{1-t})\quad
when
A_{j}\leq I
for all
j
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to 0^{-}}\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}\succeq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{1-t})\quad
when
A_{j}\geq I
for all
j
.

Proof.

Replacing
A_{j}
by
A_{j}^{p}
in Proposition
5.2
(6), we have

\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-p})^{-1}\leq\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})

for

. So
\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-p})^{-1}\preceq\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})
, and hence, by Proposition
3.1
(i)

\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{-p})^{-1/p}\preceq\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}.

Taking limit as
p\to 0^{+}
we obtain (
5.19
) when the limits exist.

We first show

\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}\preceq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{1-t})

(5.20)

for
0<p<1
when
A_{j}\leq I
for all
j
.
Let
X=\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})
. Since
A_{j}^{p}\leq I
for all
j
,
X\leq I
by
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
.
Then from (
5.18
) in the proof of Theorem
5.4
(i)

\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}A_{j}^{(1-t)p}

So
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})\preceq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}A_{j}^{(1-t)p}
, and we obtain (
5.20
) by Proposition
3.1
(i). Taking limit as
p\to 0^{+}
and applying (
4.10
) and Lemma

\mathcal{R}_{t,z}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{p})^{1/p}\succeq Q_{p}(\omega;\mathbb{A}^{1-t})

6.
Final remarks and open question

By Theorem
4.4
we have already proved the weighted arithmetic-
\mathrm{LE}
-harmonic mean inequalities for near-order:

\mathcal{H}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\mathcal{A}(\omega;\mathbb{A})

(6.21)

for
\mathbb{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\omega=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\in\Delta_{n}
.
On the other hand, we can simply prove (
6.21
) by using operator concavity of the logarithmic map
\log:\mathbb{P}\to S(\mathcal{H})
.
Indeed, since

\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\log A_{j}\leq\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}A_{j}\right)

and the left-hand side can be written as
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\log A_{j}=\log\left(\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\right)
, we obtain the second inequality of (
6.21
) by (
3.6
). Replacing
A_{j}
by
A_{j}^{-1}
we get the first inequality of (
6.21
).

A weighted
n
-mean (
n\geq 2
) is a map
G_{n}:=G_{n}(\omega;\cdot):\mathbb{P}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}
satisfying the idempotency, which means that
G_{n}(\omega;X,\dots,X)=X
for all
X\in\mathbb{P}
. The multivariable Lie-Trotter mean is the weighted mean
G_{n}
if it is differentiable and satisfies

\lim_{s\to 0}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}=\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)),

(6.22)

where for
\epsilon>0
,
\gamma_{i}:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{P}
are any differentiable curves with
\gamma_{i}(0)=I
for all
i
. By
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
if the weighted
n
-mean
G_{n}
satisfies the weighted arithmetic-
G_{n}
-harmonic mean inequalities for Loewner order:

\mathcal{H}\leq G_{n}\leq\mathcal{A},

then
G_{n}
is the multivariable Lie-Trotter mean. We can provide the similar result for near-order, but it is a generalization of
[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let the weighted
n
-mean
G_{n}
satisfy the weighted arithmetic-
G_{n}
-harmonic mean inequalities for near-order:

\mathcal{H}\preceq G_{n}\preceq\mathcal{A}.

(6.23)

Then
G_{n}
is the multivariable Lie-Trotter mean.

Proof.

Since the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 4.1]
follows for near-order, the weighted
n
-mean
G_{n}
is differentiable. It is enough to show (
6.22
). Let
\gamma_{i}:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{P}
be any differentiable curves with
\gamma_{i}(0)=I
for all
i
, where
0<\epsilon<1
. Then (
6.23
) implies

\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\gamma_{j}(s)^{-1}\right]^{-1}\preceq G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))\preceq\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\gamma_{j}(s).

For
0<s<1
we have from Proposition
3.1
(i)

\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\gamma_{j}(s)^{-1}\right]^{-1/s}\preceq G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}\preceq\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\gamma_{j}(s)\right]^{1/s}.

Since the weighted arithmetic and harmonic means are the multivariable Lie-Trotter means by
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
, taking limit as
s\to 0^{+}
and applying Lemma
4.3
yield

\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0))\preceq\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}\preceq\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)).

Since the near-order satisfies the antisymmetric property, we obtain (
6.22
). For
-1<s<0
we can get (
6.22
) by similar arguments.
∎

One can naturally ask whether Theorem
6.1
holds even though the condition (
6.23
) is replaced by the entrywise eigenvalue relation or weak log-majorization. Unfortunately, it is not true because the entrywise eigenvalue relation and weak log-majorization do not satisfy the antisymmetric property.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let the weighted
n
-mean
G_{n}
satisfy the weighted arithmetic-
G_{n}
-harmonic mean inequalities for weak log-majorization:

\mathcal{H}\prec_{w\log}G_{n}\prec_{w\log}\mathcal{A}.

If the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}
exists for differentiable curves
\gamma_{i}:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{P}_{m}
with
\gamma_{i}(0)=I
for all
i
, then

\lim_{s\to 0}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}=_{\lambda}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0)).

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
6.1
we obtain

\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0))\prec_{w\log}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}\prec_{w\log}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0))

for
0<s<1
. So
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}=_{\lambda}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0))
when the limit exists. Similarly for
-1<s<0
, we can obtain
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{-}}G_{n}(\omega;\gamma_{1}(s),\dots,\gamma_{n}(s))^{1/s}=_{\lambda}\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\gamma_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(0))
.
∎

It would be an interesting problem to study whether the weak log-majorization relation generalizes to the near-order or entrywise eigenvalue relation. Note that (
6.21
) is a generalization of (
4.14
). From this point of view, we give an open question:

\mathrm{LE}(\omega;\mathbb{A})\preceq\Omega(\omega;\mathbb{A}).

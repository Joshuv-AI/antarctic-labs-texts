Entanglement Buffers
Ye-Chao Liu, Otfried Gühne, Stefan Nimmrichter

Abstract
Quantum entanglement is the essential resource for quantum communication and distributed information processing in a quantum network. However, the remote generation over a network suffers from inevitable transmission loss and other technical difficulties. This paper introduces the concept of entanglement buffers as a potential primitive for preparing long-distance entanglement. We investigate the filling of entanglement buffers with either one Bell state or a stream of Bell states. We illustrate their resilience to non-ideal interactions and transmission loss, making them sometimes more advantageous than other entanglement generation approaches in the quantum network scenario. Additionally, larger entanglement buffers can always enhance these benefits.

However, given that it requires driving fields and environmental damping channels to act globally on the whole system, it is not clear how to realize this approach remotely over a quantum network.

In this paper, we discuss a potential primitive for remote entanglement generation: entanglement buffers.
Similar to dissipative generation, entanglement buffers can accumulate entanglement, independent of initial states and resilient to errors, however, especially suitable for quantum networks.
Given a generic quantum network scenario in which a source broadcasts a stream of maximally entangled states to two or more network parties for a given task, entanglement buffers accumulate the provided entanglement among the parties in local multi-qubit storage systems for later on-demand use. Crucially, they do not rely on the single-shot memory storage of a quantum state with maximum possible fidelity. Instead, the goal is to fill the buffer with,
i.e.
, ’cache’, a maximum (required) amount of entanglement between the parties at the expense of as many source state copies as needed.

In the following, we will introduce the model for an entanglement buffer in a bipartite setting using partial swap operations for the caching of Bell-pair entanglement, as depicted in Fig.
1
. We first assess the entanglement caching of a single Bell pair, highlighting the influence of the initial buffer state, the interaction parameters, and the buffer size. We then focus on the practical benefits of an entanglement buffer in a quantum network scenario, studying how to fill the buffer with a steady amount of entanglement from a stream of Bell pairs, and benchmarking its performance when the interaction strength with the source states is limited and when transmission losses are present. We close with a conclusion and outlook.

Model.—

Consider the bipartite model setting of Fig.

k

logarithmic negativity

E

\rho^{(2k)}_{0}=\rho^{(k)}_{0}\otimes\rho^{(k)}_{0}
with
E=0
.

Every source unit undergoes the same caching protocol in which each of the two qubits interacts subsequently with the
k
buffer qubits on Alice’s and Bob’s side, respectively, via identical unitaries

\displaystyle\hat{S}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta(|01\rangle\langle 01|+|10\rangle\langle 10|)/2}[\mathrm{SWAP}]^{\alpha/\pi}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\beta)(|01\rangle\langle 01|+|10\rangle\langle 10|)/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(|10\rangle\langle 01|+|01\rangle\langle 10|)/2}.

This describes a general partial swap operation up to a global phase, with swap angle
\alpha\in[0,\pi]
and relative phase
\beta\in[0,\alpha]
. A full SWAP is given by
\hat{S}(\pi,0)

\hat{S}(\pi,\pi)

\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\hat{S}_{AA_{j}}(\alpha,\beta)\otimes\hat{S}_{BB_{j}}(\alpha,\beta).

(2)

k

k

The single-copy protocol.—

We begin with the simplest, analytically tractable case of a single source unit, a single caching operation
\hat{U}
, and a buffer of size
k=1
. While in practice, multiple operations on the same source unit could be used to enhance the entanglement transfer to the buffer when the single-pass coupling parameter
\alpha
is weak, in theory, this effectively reduces to a single operation at stronger coupling due to the composition rule
\hat{S}(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})\hat{S}(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})=\hat{S}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})
.

The state of the buffer after the caching operation is

\rho^{(2)}_{1}=\mathrm{tr}_{AB}\left[\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)\rho^{(2)}_{0}\otimes|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\alpha,\beta)\right]\equiv\mathcal{C}[\rho^{(2)}_{0}];

(3)

see Appendix
A
for the Kraus representation of the so defined quantum channel
\mathcal{C}
. We distinguish the two opposite cases of a buffer initialized in the maximally mixed state,
\rho_{0}^{(2)}=\openone/2\otimes\openone/2

\mathcal{C}
turns the buffer state into a mixture of two Bell states and the maximally mixed state,

\rho_{1}^{(2)}=\frac{1-a^{2}}{4}\openone+\frac{a}{2}\left[(a+b)|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|+(a-b)|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|\right],

(4)

with
a=\sin^{2}(\alpha/2)
,
b=\sin^{2}[(\alpha-\beta)/2]
, and
|\psi_{2}\rangle=(|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt{2}

One can show that the cached amount of entanglement is given by

E=\log_{2}\left[1-\frac{1-a(a+2b)-|1-a(a+2b)|}{4}\right],

(5)

which is nonzero for

Our assessment of the single-pair buffer demonstrates that the caching of one ebit of entanglement from a single source unit depends crucially on the interaction strength and the initial buffer state. Before we move on to alleviate this dependence in a multi-copy protocol, we briefly discuss the influence of larger buffer sizes and numbers of caching cycles for a single source pair.
For larger

-pair buffers, subsequent caching operations no longer commute or combine to a single operation in general, as they consist of multiple partial swaps that distribute correlations between all the buffer qubit pairs. The benefit lies in the caching of more entanglement at smaller swap angles

The multi-copy protocol.—

Now we consider protocols for filling the buffer by consuming an arbitrarily long sequence of source Bell-pair units. Each unit is used in a single caching operation and then discarded, transforming the buffer state according to the channel
\mathcal{C}
in (
3
). With repeated applications of this channel, the buffer state undergoes a Markov chain,
\rho_{n}^{(2k)}=\mathcal{C}\rho_{n-1}^{(2k)}=\mathcal{C}^{\circ n}\rho_{0}^{(2k)}

Ciccarello et al. 2022
;
Salvia et al. 2023
. Expanding the channel
\mathcal{C}
into Kraus operators and employing the notation from Refs.
Salvia et al. 2023

\mathcal{L}\rho:=\mathcal{C}[\rho]-\rho
and bring it to Lindblad form.

After sufficiently many steps, the buffer will generally converge towards a steady state
\rho_{\infty}^{(2k)}

\mathcal{L}\rho_{\infty}^{(2k)}=0

\alpha\neq 0

k=1

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\bigl\{

\displaystyle b|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|+\left[(a-b)^{2}+b(1-b)\right]|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|

(6)

\displaystyle+(1-a)b\bigl(|\psi_{3}\rangle\langle\psi_{3}|+|\psi_{4}\rangle\langle\psi_{4}|\bigr)\Bigr\}\,,

Robustness against transmission loss.—

The multi-copy protocol can accumulate and buffer a steady amount of entanglement for later use, even if the source-buffer interaction strength
\alpha
is weak. It crucially assumes the deterministic application of identical unitary caching operations (
2
) with fixed parameters
\alpha,\beta
. In a practical quantum network setting, however, the caching steps will be subject to losses, fluctuations, and noise — calling for a thorough benchmark comparing entanglement buffers and quantum memories in their ability to store entanglement under such conditions. Here, we focus on the single most severe source of error: transmission losses from the source to the buffer qubits.

One advantage of multi-copy over single-shot entanglement storage is immediately apparent: given the same transmission probability
p
on both sides, successful single-shot transfer happens with probability
p^{2}
, whereas multi-copy caching can also accommodate total loss events with probability
(1-p)^{2}
. Single-sided loss events are always detrimental. For example, given full SWAP steps (
\alpha=\pi
), single-sided loss and successful transfer will change the state of 1-pair buffer between
|\psi_{1}\rangle
\langle\psi_{1}|
and
\openone/2\otimes\openone/2
, and two-sided loss never change the states. Then the probability to find a 1-pair buffer filled with 1 ebit after
n
caching steps is

q_{n}=p^{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(1-p)^{2\ell}=\frac{1-(1-p)^{2n}}{2-p}p\xrightarrow{n\gg 1}\frac{p}{2-p},

(8)

with
q_{n}>p^{2}
for any
n\geq 2
.

As a figure of merit for arbitrary
\alpha
, let
q_{n}(E)
be the probability to find the buffer in a state of at least
E
ebits of entanglement, out of all possible caching trajectories consuming
n
source units at a given
p<1
. We estimate this by the relative frequency taken from
M=5000
random samples of
n
-step trajectories (we choose
n=10
to let
(1-p)^{2n}\leq 10^{-6}
).

Liu et al. 2021

Conclusion.—

We have introduced the concept of entanglement buffers, local single- or multi-qubit devices shared by two parties, which accumulate and temporarily store (’cache’) entanglement provided by a source for later on-demand use. Ideally, the buffer could be filled by means of full SWAP operations with source Bell pairs, thereby acting as a quantum memory for Bell states. Crucially, however, one or more ebits of entanglement can also be cached by consuming multiple source copies when only weaker partial SWAP or iSWAP interactions are available. This could facilitate entanglement storage for quantum networking tasks in a wider range of realistic settings with limited control accuracy.

We have also analyzed the impact of transmission loss and found that entanglement buffers can have a higher success rate than single-shot Bell state storage. Future works could explore other realistic imperfections such as fluctuating interaction parameters and dephasing. Moreover, follow-up research should be concerned with the buffering of genuine multi-partite entanglement, extraction and redistribution of entanglement from the buffer, as well as experimental realizations of entanglement buffers.

Appendix A
General partial swap operation and 1-pair caching transformation

Here we recall the Pauli matrix representation of full and partial SWAP and iSWAP operations between a pair of qubits. We then use the results to construct the quantum channel that describes the transformation of a 1-pair buffer state upon caching a single Bell pair.

Given the single-qubit computational basis
(|0\rangle,|1\rangle)
of
\hat{\sigma}_{z}
-eigenstates and the usual Pauli matrices,

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\hat{\sigma}_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{i}\\
-\mathrm{i}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\hat{\sigma}_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(9)

the full two-qubit SWAP and iSWAP gates are defined as

SWAP

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|00\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 00|$}+\mbox{$|11\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 11|$}+\mbox{$|01\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 10|$}+\mbox{$|10\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 01|$}\,,

(10)

iSWAP

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|00\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 00|$}+\mbox{$|11\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 11|$}-i(\mbox{$|01\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 10|$}+\mbox{$|10\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 01|$})\,.

(11)

Here we employ the iSWAP sign convention
(-i)

In practice, the iSWAP is often easier to implement as it amounts to the application of a resonant flip-flop (or XY) interaction between the two spins representing the qubits, whereas the SWAP demands an additional controlled phase rotation (or ZZ-interaction) before or after:

\displaystyle\text{iSWAP}=\hat{R}_{xy}(\pi),\qquad\text{SWAP}=\hat{R}_{xy}(\pi)\hat{P}(-\pi)=\hat{P}(-\pi)\hat{R}_{xy}(\pi)\,,

(12)

with the two commuting unitaries
\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)=\exp\left[-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\hat{\sigma}_{x}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{x}+\hat{\sigma}_{y}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{y})/4\right]

\hat{P}(\beta)=\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\beta(\hat{\sigma}_{z}\otimes\hat{\sigma}_{z}-\openone\otimes\openone)/4\right]

In practice, the interaction time or strength between the qubits may limited to smaller swap angles
\alpha<\pi
, and the phase rotation angle

\displaystyle\hat{S}(\alpha,\beta):=\hat{P}(\beta)[{\rm SWAP}]^{\alpha/\pi}=\hat{P}(\beta-\alpha)\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\

Given that the source pair is in the Bell state

3
),

\rho^{(2)}_{1}=\mathcal{C}[\rho^{(2)}_{0}]=\mathrm{tr}_{AB}\left[\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)\rho^{(2)}_{0}\otimes|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\alpha,\beta)\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\hat{M}_{j}\rho_{0}^{(2)}\hat{M}_{j}^{\dagger},\qquad\text{with}\quad\hat{M}_{j}=\langle\psi_{j}|\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)|\psi_{1}\rangle.

(15)

Here, we have expanded the partial trace over the source pair
AB
in the basis of two-qubit Bell states,
|\psi_{1,2}\rangle=(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
and
|\psi_{3,4}\rangle=(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
. The resulting Kraus operators
\hat{M}_{j}
, which act on the buffer qubit pair
A_{1}B_{1}
, can also be expanded in the Bell basis,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta}(1+\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\alpha}+2\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\beta})\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}+\frac{1}{2}(1+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\beta)})\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{2}|$}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta/2}(1+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha})(\mbox{$|\psi_{3}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{3}|$}+\mbox{$|\psi_{4}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{4}|$})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\beta)})\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{2}|$}+\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta}(2\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\beta}-1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\alpha})\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta/2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha})\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{3}|$}+\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta}(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\alpha})\mbox{$|\psi_{3}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta/2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\alpha})\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{4}|$}-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\beta}(1-\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\alpha})\mbox{$|\psi_{4}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}\,.

(16)

For the simple case of an initially maximally mixed state
\rho_{0}^{(2)}=\openone/4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\hat{M}_{j}\hat{M}_{j}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{1}{4}\sin^{4}(\alpha/2)+\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\alpha/2)\sin^{2}[(\alpha-\beta)/2]+\frac{1}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}

\displaystyle+\left\{\frac{1}{4}\sin^{4}(\alpha/2)-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\alpha/2)\sin^{2}[(\alpha-\beta)/2]+\frac{1}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{2}|$}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left[1-\sin^{4}(\alpha/2)\right]

\displaystyle\bigl(\mbox{$|\psi_{3}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{3}|$}

\displaystyle+\mbox{$|\psi_{4}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{4}|$}\bigr).

Expressed in terms of
a=\sin^{2}(\alpha/2)
and
b=\sin^{2}[(\alpha-\beta)/2]
, this matches (
4
) and yields the logarithmic negativity (
5
) in the main text.

Appendix B
Additional details on the single-copy protocol

Here we present several results concerning the entanglement caching of a single Bell-pair copy. First we introduce a spin representation of caching operations in the limit of weak interactions,
\alpha\to 0
. This will serve as an approximation to describe entanglement caching from a single source pair into
k
-pair buffers by means of many repeated applications of a weak caching unitary. For
k=1
, the description is exact at any
\alpha
.

Letting
\beta=r\alpha
with
0\leq r\leq 1
and making use of the notations in Appendix
A
, the total caching unitary, Eq. (
2
) in the main text, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha\hat{h}_{z}^{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\hat{h}_{xy}^{j}}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\text{with}\qquad\hat{h}_{z}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}(\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{A}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{A_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{B}\hat{\sigma}_{z}^{B_{j}})\,,\qquad\hat{h}_{xy}^{j}=\frac{1}{4}(\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{A}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{A_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{A}\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{A_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{B}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{B_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{B}\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{B_{j}})\,.

(18)

Here, we omit the tensor products for simplicity, and we instead denote the subspace each Pauli matrix acts on by a superscript:
A,B
for the two source qubits, and
A_{j},B_{j}
for the
j
-th qubit pair of the buffer.
In the limit of small
\alpha
, we can expand the caching unitary with help of the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula,

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(1-r)\alpha/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha\sum_{j}\hat{h}_{z}^{j}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\sum_{j}\hat{h}_{xy}^{j}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})}\,,

(19)

omitting the second-order terms
\mathrm{e}^{\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})}
.
This allows us to express the operators acting on the
k
buffer qubits on the
A
-side and on the
B
-side in terms of collective spin operators
\hat{J}^{A,B}
, with

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{x,y,z}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{\sigma}_{x,y,z}\,,\quad\hat{J}_{\pm}=\hat{J}_{x}\pm i\hat{J}_{y}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{\sigma}_{\pm}\,,\quad\hat{\sigma}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\sigma}_{x}\pm i\hat{\sigma}_{y})\,.

(20)

The eigenstates
|m\rangle
of
\hat{J}_{z}
, with
m=-k/2,\ldots,k/2
, are the symmetric Dicke states; in particular
|-k/2\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes k}
and
|k/2\rangle=|1\rangle^{\otimes k}
. The states obey

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}=(-m)\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\,,\qquad\hat{J}_{\pm}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)-m(m\pm 1)}\mbox{$|m\pm 1\rangle$}\,.

(21)

With this, the approximate weak-
\alpha
caching unitary can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)\simeq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha(\hat{H}_{z}^{A}+\hat{H}_{z}^{B})}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\hat{H}_{xy}^{A}+\hat{H}_{xy}^{B})}\,,\qquad\text{with}\qquad\hat{H}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{J}_{z}\,,\qquad\hat{H}_{xy}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}+\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{J}_{+})\,.

(22)

Here we have omitted the irrelevant global phase
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k(1-r)\alpha/2}
.
The operator exponential with
\hat{H}_{xy}
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\hat{H}_{xy}^{A}+\hat{H}_{xy}^{B})}=\left[\cos(|\hat{H}_{xy}^{A}|\alpha)-i\alpha\sinc(|\hat{H}_{xy}^{A}|\alpha)\hat{H}_{xy}^{A}\right]\otimes\left[\cos(|\hat{H}_{xy}^{B}|\alpha)-i\alpha\sinc(|\hat{H}_{xy}^{B}|\alpha)\hat{H}_{xy}^{B}\right]\,,

(23)

introducing the absolute-value operator

\displaystyle|\hat{H}_{xy}|:=\sqrt{\hat{H}_{xy}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 1|$}\otimes\hat{J}_{-}\hat{J}_{+}+\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\otimes\hat{J}_{+}\hat{J}_{-}}\,.

(24)

It satisfies
[H_{xy},|H_{xy}|]=0
and is thus diagonal in the product basis of buffer and source
z
-eigenstates.

Suppose the buffer qubits are initialized in the state
|0\rangle^{\otimes 2k}=|-k/2\rangle|-k/2\rangle
. Then a single Bell pair can at most induce a single Dicke excitation on each side. Abbreviating the Dicke states
|g\rangle\equiv|-k/2\rangle
and
|e\rangle\equiv|-k/2+1\rangle
, the buffer state remains in the four-dimensional subspace spanned by
\{|g\rangle,|e\rangle\}^{\otimes 2}
, where the spin operators on each side reduce to
\hat{J}_{z}=(k/2)|g\rangle\langle g|+(k/2-1)|e\rangle\langle e|
and
\hat{J}_{+}=\hat{J}_{-}^{\dagger}=\sqrt{k}|e\rangle\langle g|
. The combined state of buffer and source pair after application of the weak caching operation reads as

|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,r\alpha)\frac{\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}+\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}}{\sqrt{2}}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}+\left[\cos\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}\alpha}{2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-r)k\alpha/2}\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}-i\sin\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}\alpha}{2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/2}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|e\rangle$}\right]^{\otimes 2}\right)\,,

up to the omitted global phase mentioned above. The reduced buffer state follows by tracing out the source pair,

\displaystyle\rho^{(k)}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+x^{2}}{2}\mbox{$|gg\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle gg|$}+\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{2}\mbox{$|ee\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle ee|$}-\frac{1-x}{2}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha}\mbox{$|gg\rangle\langle ee|$}+h.c.\right]+\frac{x(1-x)}{2}\left[\mbox{$|ge\rangle\langle ge|$}+h.c.\right]\,,

(26)

with
x=\cos^{2}(\sqrt{k}\alpha/2)\in[0,1]
. The entanglement in terms of logarithmic negativity can be given exactly as

\displaystyle E=\log_{2}\left[1+(x-1)^{2}\right]=\log_{2}\left[1+\sin^{4}(\sqrt{k}\alpha/2)\right]\,,

(27)

which is independent of the phase rotation angle
\beta=r\alpha
.
This implies that, for a buffer of sufficiently large size
k=(\pi/\alpha)^{2}
, the caching operation can extract all the entanglement from the source Bell pair.
The same result applies if the buffer is initialized in
\mbox{$|1\rangle$}^{\otimes 2k}=\mbox{$|e\rangle$}
, as this merely swaps the roles of the basis states
\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\leftrightarrow\mbox{$|1\rangle$}
and
\mbox{$|g\rangle$}\leftrightarrow\mbox{$|e\rangle$}
.

More generally, if the buffer is initialized in the product state
\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}^{\otimes 2k}
with
\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}=\cos\theta\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\mbox{$|1\rangle$}
, we can expand it on each side in terms of the Dicke states,

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\phi\rangle$}^{\otimes k}=\sum_{m=-k/2}^{k/2}a_{m}\mbox{$|m\rangle$}\,,\qquad a_{m}=\sqrt{C_{k}^{k/2-m}}(\cos\theta)^{k/2-m}(\sin\theta)^{k/2+m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(k/2+m)\delta}\,,

(28)

where
C_{N}^{K}=N!/K!(N-K)!
is the binomial coefficient.
The caching unitary is block-diagonal with each block coupling
|m\rangle|1\rangle
and
|m+1\rangle|0\rangle
on the
A
-side and on the
B
-side, which facilitates an efficient numerical computation.
For the simplest case of
k=1
, the combined source-buffer state after the caching operation is

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\Psi\rangle$}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{U}(\alpha,r\alpha)\left(\left[\mbox{$|0\rangle$}(\cos\theta\mbox{$|g\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\mbox{$|e\rangle$})\right]^{\otimes 2}+\left[\mbox{$|1\rangle$}(\cos\theta\mbox{$|g\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\mbox{$|e\rangle$})\right]^{\otimes 2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/4}\cos\theta\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/4}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\left(\cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|e\rangle$}-i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}\right)\right]^{\otimes 2}

(29)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/4}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|e\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(1-r)\alpha/4}\cos\theta\left(\cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$|g\rangle$}-i\sin\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$|e\rangle$}\right)\right]^{\otimes 2}\,,

which is valid for arbitrary
\alpha
. The reduced buffer state follows after tracing out the source pair or, alternatively, by directly applying the channel (
15
) to
\rho_{0}^{(2)}=|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|^{\otimes 2}
. The cached entanglement is shown in Fig.
7
as a function of
(\theta,\delta)
for various choices of
\alpha,\beta
, confirming our claim in the main text that the relative phase
\delta
barely influences the caching performance.
Figs.
7
(a)-(d) correspond to iSWAP interactions (
\alpha=\beta
), complementing the results for
\delta=0
shown in Fig.
2
(b).
Figs.
7
(e)-(h) depict the behaviour for increasing
\beta
starting from the SWAP case
\beta=0
, showing that the caching performance deteriorates with
\beta
, and is worst at
\beta=\alpha
in (c).

\alpha,\beta
. The two buffer qubits are initialized in the same pure state
\cos\theta\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta}\sin\theta\mbox{$|1\rangle$}
, and we plot the cached entanglement in ebits of logarithmic negativity as a function of

.

The strong dependence of the cached entanglement on the superposition angle
\theta
in the iSWAP case can be alleviated by considering larger buffers. Fig.

for (a) SWAP-type and (b) iSWAP-type operations. Compared to the 1-pair results in Fig.

.

Finally, we consider the repeated application of a weak caching unitary,
\hat{U}^{n}(\alpha,r\alpha)
. In the limit of small
\alpha
, but finite
n\alpha
, we can commute the exponentials in (
22
) by virtue of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula and incur a correction of the order of

Appendix C
Steady state of the multi-copy protocol

Here we discuss the steady state of a
k
-pair buffer under repeated caching from a Bell pair sequence. Recall that the multi-copy caching protocol is a repeated application of the caching channel
\mathcal{C}
, which defines the Markov chain

\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{(2k)}=\mathcal{C}\rho_{n-1}^{(2k)}=\mathcal{C}^{\circ n}\rho_{0}^{(2k)}\,.

(30)

In Appendix
A
, we have expressed the channel in terms of the Kraus operators
\hat{M}_{j}=\langle\psi_{j}|\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)|\psi_{1}\rangle
and given them explicitly for a 1-pair buffer. The Kraus operators also represent the Lindblad operators of the generator
\mathcal{L}\rho:=\mathcal{C}[\rho]-\rho
describing the discrete time increments of the buffer state.

After sufficiently many such increments, the buffer will generally converge towards a steady state
\rho_{\infty}^{(2k)}
that obeys
\mathcal{L}\rho_{\infty}^{(2k)}=0
.
For
k=1
, we can analytically confirm that it is uniquely determined by
\hat{U}(\alpha,\beta)
, as given in (
6
) in the main text. The corresponding logarithmic negativity (
7
) is also plotted in Fig.
4
(a) of the main text. The results for buffers of size
k=2,3
shown in Fig.
5
were obtained by numerically solving for the kernel of the generator
\mathcal{L}
. What we consistently observe is that, for iSWAP-type caching (
\beta=\alpha
), the buffer is always filled to its maximum
E=k\,
ebit, regardless of
\alpha>0
. In fact, the steady state is given by
k
copies of the Bell state
|\psi_{2}\rangle
, which we can prove for any
k
:

Proposition 1
.

For iSWAP caching of entanglement from a sequence of Bell-
|\psi_{1}\rangle
pairs into a
k
-pair buffer, the
k
-ebit buffer state
\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}
is a steady state for every swap angle
\alpha
.

Proof.

Let us first factorize the partial iSWAP interaction (
\beta=\alpha
) into

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{A}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{A_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{A}\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{A_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{B}\hat{\sigma}_{x}^{B_{j}}+\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{B}\hat{\sigma}_{y}^{B_{j}})/4}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\hat{R}_{xy}^{AA_{j}}\otimes\prod_{j=1}^{k}\hat{R}_{xy}^{BB_{j}}\equiv\hat{U}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}^{(k)}\,,

(31)

where
\hat{U}^{(k)}
acts on the
k+1
qubits of buffer and source either on the
A
-side or on the
B
-side. Expanded in the basis of the source qubit, it can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{(k)}:=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\hat{U}_{j}=\mbox{$|0\rangle\langle 0|$}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}+\mbox{$|1\rangle\langle 1|$}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}+\mbox{$|0\rangle\langle 1|$}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}+\mbox{$|1\rangle\langle 0|$}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\,.

(32)

The expansion "coefficients"
\hat{U}_{ij}^{(k)}
can be obtained iteratively, starting from
k=1
:

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{00}^{(1)}=\langle 0|\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)|0\rangle=\mbox{$|0\rangle\langle 0|$}+\cos(\alpha/2)\mbox{$|1\rangle\langle 1|$}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{-}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{10}^{(1)}=\langle 1|\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)|0\rangle=-\mathrm{i}\sin(\alpha/2)\mbox{$|0\rangle\langle 1|$}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{11}^{(1)}=\langle 1|\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)|1\rangle=\mbox{$|1\rangle\langle 1|$}+\cos(\alpha/2)\mbox{$|0\rangle\langle 0|$}\,,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{+}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{01}^{(1)}=\langle 0|\hat{R}_{xy}(\alpha)|1\rangle=-\mathrm{i}\sin(\alpha/2)\mbox{$|1\rangle\langle 0|$}\,.

(33)

Higher
k
-terms follow by virtue of the recursion relations

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{-}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{P}_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{P}_{2}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}\,.

(34)

We will now derive how the partial iSWAP acts on the buffer state
|\psi_{2}\rangle^{\otimes k}
. Our claim is that

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}=\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}=\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=0\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=0\,.

(35)

These are the only four relevant combinations, because we assume that the source Bell pair is in the state
|\psi_{1}\rangle
. The proof of this claim follows by induction. At
k=1
, we can quickly verify

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(1)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\left(\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}+\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\right)\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{11}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(1)}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\left(\hat{P}_{2}\otimes\hat{P}_{2}+\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\right)\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(1)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\left(\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{-}+\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\otimes\hat{P}_{2}\right)\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=0\,,

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{10}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(1)}+\hat{U}_{11}^{(1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{-}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}+\hat{P}_{2}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\right)\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}=0\,.

(36)

Now suppose our claim (
35
) holds for
k-1
. We can then verify by elementary calculation that each of the four identities also holds for
k
. For example, the first line in (
35
) follows by

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}=

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\hat{U}_{10}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k-1)}+\hat{U}_{11}^{(k-1)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k-1)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\left(\hat{P}_{1}\otimes\hat{P}_{1}+\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\otimes\sigma^{\prime}_{+}\right)\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}+0=\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}\,.

(37)

The other three identities are obtained in a similar manner.
We can thus conclude that the combined source-buffer state
|\psi_{1}\rangle|\psi_{2}\rangle^{\otimes k}
is a fixed point of the partial iSWAP unitary, since

\displaystyle\hat{U}(\alpha,\alpha)\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl\{\mbox{$|00\rangle$}\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}+\mbox{$|11\rangle$}\left[\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}

(38)

\displaystyle+\mbox{$|01\rangle$}\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}+\mbox{$|10\rangle$}\left[\hat{U}_{00}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{10}^{(k)}+\hat{U}_{01}^{(k)}\otimes\hat{U}_{11}^{(k)}\right]\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}\,.

This implies that
\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}^{\otimes k}
is a steady state of the buffer for any
\alpha
-value.

Appendix D
Multi-copy protocol with transmission loss

Here we provide details and additional results on the impact of transmission loss in the multi-copy buffering protocol. First, we compare the 1-ebit success rate
q_{n}
between SWAP and iSWAP caching. In the main text, we found the simple expression (
8
) for the success rate using full SWAP caching of a 1-pair buffer, which exceeds the single-shot success probability
p^{2}
. At the same time, we noticed that iSWAPs are more sensitive to transmission loss with
q_{n}<p^{2}
consistently. The reason lies in the fact that, once the buffer assumes a maximally mixed state (after a single-sided loss event), the full iSWAP caching protocol requires two successful caching operations in a row to refill the buffer, while the full SWAP protocol requires only one. See the diagram in Fig.
9
for a visual representation of both cases.

Concretely, a full iSWAP protocol with single-sided loss transforms between the buffer states
|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{2}|
(filled),
\openone/2\otimes\openone/2
(depleted), and
\mbox{$|00\rangle\langle 00|$}/2+\mbox{$|11\rangle\langle 11|$}/2
(depleted). Consequently, the 1-ebit success rate after
n
steps requires two two-sided successes with an arbitrary number of up to
n-2
two-sided losses in between or thereafter,

q^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-2-k}(1-p)^{2\ell}\right]p^{2}(1-p)^{2k}p^{2}=\frac{1-(1-p)^{2(n-1)}-(n-1)p(2-p)(1-p)^{2(n-1)}}{(2-p)^{2}}p^{2}\xrightarrow{n\gg 1}\frac{p^{2}}{(2-p)^{2}},

(39)

This is smaller than the single-shot success probability
p^{2}
.

For larger buffers, we generally obtain a greater robustness against single-sided losses and thus a greater 1-ebit success rate. The diagram in Fig.
10
illustrates the possible state transformations from a filled to a depleted 2-pair buffer upon full SWAP caching under loss. In the iSWAP case, the diagram looks the same, and the only difference lies in the 1-ebit states (grey-shaded nodes): For SWAPs they are permutations of
|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{1}|\otimes\openone/4
, whereas for iSWAPs they are permutations of
\mbox{$|\psi_{1}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{1}|$}\otimes(\mbox{$|00\rangle\langle 00|$}+\mbox{$|11\rangle\langle 11|$})/4+\mbox{$|\psi_{2}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{2}|$}\otimes(\mbox{$|01\rangle\langle 01|$}+\mbox{$|10\rangle\langle 10|$})/4
.
Both cases are therefore equivalent in terms of success rate, and we compute its asymptotic value as
q_{n\gg 1}\approx 63\,\%
.

Inducing of exotic smooth two fixed point actions on spheres Journal: Journal Name
Piotr Mizerka

Abstract
This paper is concerned with the Smith question [ 28 ] which reads as follows. Is it true that for a finite group acting smoothly on a sphere with exactly two fixed points, the tangent spaces at the fixed points have always isomorphic group module structures defined by differentiation of the action? We show that one can answer this question negatively by using the technique of induction of group representations. We apply our results to indicate new dimensions of spheres admitting actions of specific Oliver groups, which give the negative answer to the Smith question. In particular, for the first time, we indicate some solvable non-nilpotent Oliver groups which yield negative answers to the Smith question.

by the construction of the induced module. This, in connection with
\dim U^{H}=\dim V^{H}=0
, gives us
\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(U))^{G}=\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(V))^{G}=0
. Thus, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(U)-\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(V)\in PO(G)
, which is equivalent, by Proposition
2.6
, to proving that
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}})(p)=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}})(p)
for any
p\in G
of prime power order.

Take
p\in G
of prime power order. If the intersection of the conjugacy class
(p)
of element
p
in
G
with the subgroup
H
is empty, then, by Theorem
1.1
,
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}})(p)=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}})(p)=0
. Assume
H\cap(p)
is nonempty and let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}
be the representatives of all the distinct conjugacy classes of elements in
H
of the set
H\cap(p)
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
,

\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}})(p)=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}})(p)\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}}(h_{1})}{|C_{H}(h_{1})|}+\ldots+\frac{\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}}(h_{m})}{|C_{H}(h_{m})|}=\frac{\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}}(h_{1})}{|C_{H}(h_{1})|}+\ldots+\frac{\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}}(h_{m})}{|C_{H}(h_{m})|}

The above equality is true since
h_{i}
is of prime power order and thus, by our assumption,
\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle U$}}(h_{i})=\chi_{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle V$}}(h_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
∎

We can prove now Theorem
B
announced in the Introduction.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Assume
U
and
V
are two non-isomorphic Smith matched
\mathbb{R}N
-modules such that
W_{U}=\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(U)
and
W_{V}=\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(V)
are
\mathcal{P}
-oriented. Since
U-V\in\widetilde{PO}(N)
, it follows by Lemma
2.7
that
W_{U}-W_{V}\in\widetilde{PO}(G)
. By Theorem
2.4
, the conditions
(2)-(4)
are satisfied for
W_{U}
and
W_{V}
. We show that
W_{U}^{L}=W_{V}^{L}=\{0\}
for any large subgroup of
L\in\mathcal{L}(G)
. We have, by Lemma
2.5
,
L_{N}=L\cap N\in\mathcal{L}(N)
. Thus
U^{L_{N}}=\{0\}
and
V^{L_{N}}=\{0\}
by our assumption. This, in connection with Lemma
2.3
, gives
W_{U}^{L}=W_{V}^{L}=\{0\}
. Obviously,
\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(U))=\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(V))\geq 6
. This completes the proof that
W_{U}
and
W_{V}
are Smith matched. Hence, by the considerations at the beginning of this section, there exists a two fixed point action on some sphere
S
with tangent spaces at the fixed points isomorphic to
W_{U}
and
W_{V}
. Moreover, since
U\not\cong V
as
\mathbb{R}N
-modules, it follows that
W_{U}\not\cong W_{V}
as
\mathbb{R}G
-modules for
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}:\operatorname{RO}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{RO}(G)
is a monomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

If we drop the assumption of
\mathcal{P}
-orientability of
W_{U}
and
W_{V}
, we can formulate a similar assertion. By
[

, the modules
W_{U}^{\prime}=W_{U}\oplus W_{U}
and
W_{V}^{\prime}=W_{V}\oplus W_{U}
are
\mathcal{P}
-oriented. Obviously, these modules are Smith matched. Thus, there exists a two fixed point action of
G
on some sphere
S
with tangent spaces at the two fixed points isomorphic to
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(U\oplus U)
and
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(U\oplus V)
. Obviously, these modules are not isomorphic.

3
Examples for which Conjecture
C
holds

Assume
G
is an Oliver group. Before, we proceed, let us recall the
Laitinen conjecture
.

Conjecture 3.1
.

[
11
, Appendix]

If
\operatorname{prim}(G)\geq 2
, then
\operatorname{Sm}(G)\neq 0
.

Following
[
22
, Answer. 3.9.1]
, the Laitinen conjecture holds in either of the following situations.

1.

G
is of odd order,

2.

G
has a cyclic quotient of composite odd order (in particular,
G
is nilpotent, see
[
22
, p. 25]
),

3.

G
is nonsolvable and
G\neq\operatorname{Aut}(A_{6})
.

Thus, in either of the three cases above, the triviality of Smith sets is easy to verify by means of the Laitinen conjecture. What is interesting is to provide new examples of Oliver groups which do not lie in either of the three classes described above. We apply Lemma
2.3
to give a new example of
G
with nontrivial Smith set such that
G
does not belong to
(a)
,
(b)
or
(c)
. Note that if
G
belongs to either of the classes
(a)
,
(b)
or
(c)
and
N\trianglelefteq G
such that
\operatorname{prim}(N)\geq 2
, then Conjecture
C
holds. Indeed, since
\operatorname{prim}(N)\geq 2
, then if
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}:\operatorname{RO}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{RO}(G)
is a monomorphism, we have
\operatorname{prim}(G)\geq 2
. Thus,
\operatorname{Sm}(G)\neq 0
by the Laitinen conjecture applied for
G
.

Let us call
G
a
gap group
if
\mathcal{P}(G)\cap\mathcal{L}(G)=\emptyset
and there exists an
\mathbb{R}G
-module
V
such that
\dim V^{L}=0
for every
L\in\mathcal{L}(G)
and
V
satisfies the strong gap condition. Following
[
21
]
, we introduce a symbol
\widetilde{PO}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}
for a subgroup of
\widetilde{PO}(G)
consisting of differences
U-V
such that
\dim U^{L}=\dim V^{L}=0
for any
L\in\mathcal{L}(G)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}\neq 0
and
N
is a normal gap subgroup of
G
such that
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}:\operatorname{RO}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{RO}(G)
is a monomorphism. Then
\operatorname{Sm}(G)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Since
N
is a gap group, there exists an
\mathbb{R}G
-module
V
satisfying the gap condition and such that
\dim V^{L}=0
for any
L\in\mathcal{L}(N)
. As in the proofs of Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
B
that
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(V)
satisfies the strong gap condition and
\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}(V))^{L}=0
for any
L\in\mathcal{L}(G)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}(G)\cap\mathcal{L}(G)=\emptyset
since
\mathcal{P}(N)\cap\mathcal{L}(N)=\emptyset
. Indeed if
H\in\mathcal{P}(G)\cap\mathcal{L}(G)
, then it follows by Lemma
2.5
that
H\cap N\in\mathcal{L}(N)
. On the other hand
H\cap N\in\mathcal{P}(N)
. A contradiction. This proves that
G
is a gap group and we cocnlude from
[
21
, Theoem 4.1]
that
\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}\in\operatorname{Sm}(G)
. Since
\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}\neq 0
by our assumption, the assertion follows.
∎

Following
[
21
]
, let us denote for
H\trianglelefteq G
by
\overline{\operatorname{prim}}(G,H)
the number of real conjugacy classes in
G/H
of elements of the form
gH
containing elements of
G
not of prime power order. (note that
\operatorname{prim}(G)=r_{G}
and
\overline{\operatorname{prim}}(G,H)=\overline{r}_{G/H}
in
[
21
]
). Let
G^{nil}
denote the smallest normal subgroup of
G
such that
G/G^{nil}
is nilpotent. The rank of
\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}\neq 0
can be then estimated from below by
\operatorname{prim}(G)-\overline{\operatorname{prim}}(G,G^{nil})
, see
[
21
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Take
G=C_{6}\times A_{4}\times D_{30}\cong C_{30}\rtimes(S_{3}\times A_{4})
, the direct product of cyclic group of order
6
, alternating group of degree
4
and dihedral group of order
30
.
G
is an example of an Oliver group which does not belong to either of the classes
(a)
,
(b)
or
(c)
(the fact that
G
is Oliver follows since
S_{3}\times A_{4}
is Oliver, see
[
21
]
). Assume
a
is the generator of
C_{6}\leq G
and
D_{30}\leq G
has the standard presentation
\langle x,y|x^{15}=1,y^{2}=1,yxy=x^{-1}\rangle
. Put
N=\langle a\rangle\times\{1\}\times\langle x\rangle\cong C_{15}
, the cyclic group of order
30
. Obviously
N
is normal in
G
. Take any
n=(a^{k},1,x^{l})\in N
, where
k=0,...,5
and
l=0,...,14
. Notice that
(n)^{\pm}_{N}=(n)^{\pm}_{G}
. Indeed, for any
n^{\prime}\in(n)_{G}^{\pm}
we can find
m=0,...,14
such that

n^{\prime}=(a^{\pm k},1,(yx^{m})x^{\pm l}(yx^{m})^{-1})=(a^{\pm k},1,yx^{\pm l}y^{-1})=(1,1,y)n^{\pm}(1,1,y)^{-1}

Thus
n^{\prime}\in(n)^{\pm}_{G}
and
(n)^{\pm}_{N}=(n)^{\pm}_{G}
. It follows then by Corollary
1.4
that
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}:\operatorname{RO}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{RO}(G)
is a monomorphism. Moreover,
N=C_{30}
is a gap group (in fact, the gap group with the minimal order, see
[
17
, p. 330]
). We show that
\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}}\neq 0
which would imply that
\operatorname{Sm}(G)\neq 0
by Lemma
3.2
. We have
G^{nil}=\{1\}\times H\times\langle x\rangle
, where
H
is a subgroup of
A_{4}
isomorphic to
C_{2}^{2}
. Hence,
G/G^{nil}\cong C_{6}^{2}
. Using GAP
[
7
]
we compute the primary number of
G
,
\operatorname{prim}(G)=107
and
\beta^{\prime}(G/G^{nil})=\beta^{\prime}(C_{6}^{2})=20
. Thus

\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde{\operatorname{PO}}(G)^{\mathcal{L}})\geq\operatorname{prim}(G)-\beta^{\prime}(G/G^{nil})=87>0

In particular, the assertion of Conjecture
C
for
G
and
N
holds.

Let us show some examples of solvable Oliver groups which are not nilpotent and confirm Conjecture
C
. These groups are

1.

G_{1}=C_{3}\times S_{4}=\operatorname{SmallGroup}(72,42)
,

2.

G_{2}=S_{3}\times A_{4}=\operatorname{SmallGroup}(72,44)
,

3.

G_{3}=(C_{2}^{2}\rtimes C_{3})^{2}\rtimes C_{2}=\operatorname{SmallGroup}(288,1025)
,

4.

G_{4}=A_{4}^{2}\rtimes C_{2}^{2}=\operatorname{SmallGroup}(576,8654)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{N}(G)
the set of normal subgroups
N
of
G
(up to isomorphism) such that
N\not\in\{1,G\}
and
\operatorname{Ind}_{N}^{G}:\operatorname{RO}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{RO}(G)
is a monomorphism. We verify in GAP that
\mathcal{N}(G_{1})=\{C_{3},A_{4},S_{4}\}
,
\mathcal{N}(G_{2})=\{C_{3},S_{3},A_{4}\}
,
\mathcal{N}(G_{3})=\emptyset
,
\mathcal{N}(G_{4})=\{G_{3}\}
. By
[
22
, Answer 4.1.3 (1)]
we have
\operatorname{Sm}(C_{3})=0
, while
\operatorname{Sm}(S_{3})=\operatorname{Sm}(S_{4})=\operatorname{Sm}(A_{4})=0
by
[
22
, Corollary 4.5.4 (3)]
for
\operatorname{prim}(S_{3})=\operatorname{prim}(S_{4})=\operatorname{prim}(A_{4})=0
. The triviality of
\operatorname{Sm}(G_{3})
was proved in
[
21
, Proposition 5.4]
. Thus, the Smith set of every group from
\mathcal{N}(G_{i})
,
i=1,...,6
is trivial, see
[
22
]
. This, in particular, confirms Conjecture
C
for any
N\trianglelefteq G_{i}
with
N\in\mathcal{N}(G_{i})
for
i=1,...,4
.

Similar result holds for the unique nonsolvable group
G
for which the Laitinen conjecture does not hold, that is for
G=\operatorname{Aut}(A_{6})
. We have
\mathcal{N}(G)=\{\operatorname{SmallGroup}(720,765)\}
. Put
N=\operatorname{SmallGroup}(720,765)
. Using GAP, we verify that
N
is a nonsolvable group with
\operatorname{prim}(N)=0
. Thus, by the Laitinen conjecture, we have
\operatorname{Sm}(N)=0
and the assertion of Conjecture
C
holds in this case.

Mori Dream Bonds and ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -actions
Lorenzo Barban, Eleonora A. Romano, Luis E. Solá Conde, Stefano Urbinati

Abstract
. We construct a correspondence between Mori dream regions arising from small modifications of normal projective varieties and ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -actions on polarized pairs which are bordisms. Moreover, we show that the Mori dream regions constructed in this way admit a chamber decomposition on which the models are the geometric quotients of the ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -action. In addition we construct, from a given ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -action on a polarized pair for which there exist at least two admissible geometric quotients, a ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -equivariantly birational ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -variety, whose induced action is a bordism, called the pruning of the variety.

1.
Introduction

The existence of an interplay between birational geometry and geometric invariant theory is a well known fact since the 1980’s. In his work
[
28
]
Reid explains how flips –fundamental birational transformations in modern birational geometry– could be understood in terms of variation of GIT (see also
[
13
]
). On the other hand Thaddeus
[
31
]
showed that the set of all possible linearizations of a torus action has a natural structure of convex cone in the Neron-Severi space of the variety, divided in (convex conic) chambers on whose interior the associated GIT quotient is invariant, and described the birational transformations associated to wall-crossing in this chamber decomposition. The construction
of the wall-crossing transformations in terms of weighted blow-ups has also appeared in
[
5
]
. Moreover, Włodarczyk (see
[
33
]
) used these ideas to address the Weak factorization conjecture of birational transformations by means of the concept of cobordism. Finally, these ideas gave rise to the concept of Mori dream space (MDS for short), introduced by Hu and Keel (cf.
[
14
]
). In a nutshell, the birational geometry of an MDS
X
is governed by the different GIT quotients of an affine variety
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(\Cox(X))
by the action of a torus.

Recently the abovementioned relation was exploited in the classification of certain complex projective varieties admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of a simple type (cf.
[
25
]
), which then was used in
[
26
]
to prove some partial version of the LeBrun-Salamon conjecture. In the paper
[
25
]
the authors studied
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action counterparts of some birational transformations, such as Atiyah flips and special Cremona transformations. These results were then generalized in
[
22
]
to study equivariant birational modifications of smooth projective varieties with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action under some hypotheses. The birational geometry of these varieties is then completely determined in a precise manner by the corresponding GIT-quotients of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action (see for instance
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
). The idea is the following: given a polarized pair
(X,L)
, where
X
is a normal projective variety and
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
, with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action satisfying some hypotheses (see
[
22
, §4]
), one gets a naturally ordered chain of birational transformations among the geometric quotients
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
of
X
:

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(1,2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{2}}
\textstyle{\dots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{r-1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r),}

which are flips (see Example
2.12
,
[
22
, Theorem 3.1]
).
Moreover, given two of these geometric quotients
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
,
\mathcal{G}\!X(j,j+1)
,
i\leq j
, one may find a variety
X^{\prime}
–
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly – birationally equivalent to
X
, admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action whose geometric quotients are
\mathcal{G}\!X(k,k+1)
,
i\leq k\leq j
, and one may obtain
X
from
X^{\prime}
by means of a blow-up and a sequence of flips (
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
).

With this in mind, a natural question arises: given a birational map
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
among normal projective varieties, is it possible to find a variety
X
, endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action such that
Y_{\pm}
are the extremal geometric quotients of
X
and such that
\phi
coincides with the map induced by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action? If this is the case, we say that
X
is a
geometric realization
of the birational map
\phi
. If such a variety
X
exists, we get a natural decomposition of
\phi
in terms of simpler birational transformations, that, moreover, can be read out of the birational geometry of
X
.

In this paper we address this problem, extending the results of
[
22
]
to broader generality. We consider a particular type of polarized pairs
(X,L)
admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action, called
bordisms
, and study how their birational geometry is ruled by the induced birational transformation between the corresponding polarized extremal fixed point components
(Y_{\pm},L_{\pm})
. A bordism is a projective variety admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action whose only invariant divisors are its extremal fixed point components, which are requested to be geometric quotients of
X
. Although at a first glimpse bordisms may seem to be very particular cases of torus actions, we show that they can be constructed upon many examples of torus actions by means of a birational transformation called
pruning
(see Definition
3.3
). Different prunings of the same torus action give rise to
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly birationally equivalent varieties (see Theorem
3.6
). A central definition in this work is the one of
Mori Dream Bond
(
MDB
for short):

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Y_{-}
be a normal projective variety,

L_{-},L_{+}\in\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y_{-})
be two effective Cartier divisors such that
L_{-}
is ample,
R(Y_{-};L_{+})
is finitely generated and the natural map
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}:=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(R(Y_{-};L_{+}))
is a small modification.

We say that
(L_{-},L_{+})
is a
Mori Dream Bond
on
Y_{-}
if the cone
{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y_{-})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
generated by
L_{-},L_{+}
is a Mori Dream Region, that is the multisection ring

R(Y_{-};L_{-},L_{+})=\bigoplus_{a,b\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},aL_{-}+bL_{+})

is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra.

This condition is essentially a local version of the MDS definition. Our main results show that essentially bordisms are torus action counterparts of small modifications defined by an MDB. More concretely we prove the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
be a small modification defined by an MDB
(L_{-},L_{+})
on a normal projective variety
Y_{-}
. Then there exists a normal projective variety
X
admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action which is a bordism and realizes geometrically the map
\phi
.

Let us recall that a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is
equalized at the sink
Y_{-}
and source
Y_{+}
if every point, whose orbit converges either to
Y_{-}
or to
Y_{+}
, has trivial isotropy group (cf. §
2.3
). Moreover we define the
bandwidth
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
as the difference
\delta=\mu_{L}(Y_{+})-\mu_{L}(Y_{-})
, where by
\mu_{L}(Y_{\pm})
we denote the weight of the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on any fiber of
L
over a point of
Y_{\pm}
(cf. §
2.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a normal
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial projective variety, let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of bandwidth
\delta
which is a bordism and is equalized at the sink
Y_{-}
and the source
Y_{+}
, and let
\psi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
be the induced birational map. Denote by
L_{-}=L_{|Y_{-}}
, and by
L_{+}=L_{\mid Y_{-}}-\delta Y_{-\mid Y_{-}}
. Then
(L_{-},L_{+})
is an MDB, whose associated map is
\psi
.

Moreover, in the spirit of
[
15
]
, we show the existence of a chamber decomposition of an MDB; we refer to Definition
5.7
for the notion of chamber model.

Theorem 1.4
.

In the situation of Theorem
1.3
, the rational polyhedral cone
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle L_{-},L_{+}\rangle\subset\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y_{-})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
admits a subdivision
{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{r-1}{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, where every
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=\langle L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i}Y_{-|Y_{-}},L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i+1}Y_{-|Y_{-}}\rangle
is a chamber and
a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}
are the critical values of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
. Moreover, for every
i
the chamber model of
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
is
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
.

We conclude by constructing a natural geometric realization of the standard cubo-cubic Cremona involution of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. We then study its possible prunings, and realize one of them as a geometric realization of an MDB constructed upon the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
along the
4
coordinates points.

Outline.
After introducing some background material about
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions, bordisms, and MDBs (Section
2
), we describe the pruning construction in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
(cf. Theorem
4.1
); in Section

1.3
and
1.4
(see Theorems
5.6
and

respectively). In Section

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We work over the field
{\mathbb{C}}
of complex numbers. Given a normal projective variety
Y
, we denote by
\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(Y)
(resp.
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y)
) the group of Weil (resp. Cartier) divisors on
Y
, and by
\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(Y)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
(resp.
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
) the associated vector space with rational coefficients.
A
small modification
is a birational map
\phi:Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
among normal projective varieties which is an isomorphism in codimension 1. Abusing notation, the strict transform of a Weil divisor
D
in
Y^{\prime}
will still be denoted by
D
. If in addition
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial we call
\phi
a
small
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial modification
(SQM for short). In this case the strict transform via
\phi
allows us to identify
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y^{\prime})_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

By a
polarized pair
we mean a pair
(Y,L)
, where
Y
is a normal projective variety and
L
is an ample line bundle.

2.2.
Mori dream regions and bonds

Given a finite set of effective Cartier divisors
L_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,k
, on a normal projective variety
Y
, we define the
section ring

R(Y;L_{1},\dots,L_{k})
as the multigraded
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

R(Y;L_{1},\dots,L_{k}):=\bigoplus_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}}\HH^{0}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m_{1}L_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}L_{k})).

Note that the product in
R(Y;L_{1},\dots,L_{k})
is induced by the inclusions of each
\HH^{0}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(m_{1}L_{1}+\ldots+m_{k}L_{k}))
into the field of rational functions of
Y
, and so it depends on the particular choice of the effective Cartier divisors
L_{i}
(not only on their linear equivalence class, cf.
[
14
, Remark, p. 341]
).

In the case
k=1
, the finite generation of section rings is a classical problem in birational geometry. If
\{L_{1},\dots,L_{k}\}
is a set of generators of the effective cone of
Y
(in the case such a set exists), the finite generation of the section ring –which is then called the
Cox ring
of
Y
– is equivalent to the birational geometric concept of Mori dream space. In the spirit of
[
14
, Theorem 2.13]
we introduce the following:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a normal projective variety, and let
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}\rangle
be a rational polyhedral cone in
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, where
L_{i}
are effective. The cone
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
Mori dream region
–MDR for short– if the multisection ring

R(Y;L_{1},\ldots,L_{k})
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the meaning of the term Mori Dream Region is not unique in the literature. The one we give and will need for this work is essentially similar to the one introduced in
[
14
, Theorem 2.13]
. We refer to
[
27
, §9.2]
,
[
15
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
17
, §5]
for a complete picture of the different properties required for MDRs.

We introduce a special type of MDR that we will be most interested in:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
Y_{-}
be a normal projective variety, and let

L_{-},L_{+}\in\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(Y_{-})
be two effective Cartier divisors such that:

•

L_{-}
is ample,

•

R(Y_{-};L_{+})
is finitely generated and the natural map
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}:=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(R(Y_{-};L_{+}))
is a small modification.

We say that
(L_{-},L_{+})
is a
Mori Dream Bond
(
MDB
, for short) on
Y_{-}
if the cone
{\mathcal{C}}
generated by
L_{-},L_{+}
is an MDR.

2.3.
Białynicki-Birula decomposition, criticality

In this section, we collect some preliminaries on
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions on polarized pairs
(X,L)
.

Given such a pair, it is known (see
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
and the subsequent Remark) that the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on
X
admits a linearization on the line bundle
L
.
In particular, denoting by
{\mathcal{Y}}
the set of connected components of the fixed point locus, we may assign to every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
a weight
\mu_{L}(Y)\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*}):=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*},{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
, which indicates the weight of the restriction of the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
to any fiber of
L
over a point of
Y
. As usual we will identify
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*})
with
{\mathbb{Z}}
and think of
\mu_{L}(Y)
as integers, and we will call them the
critical values
of the action. Notice that a linearization of
L

induces a linearization of
mL
, for
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, such that
\mu_{mL}(Y)=m\mu_{L}(Y)
, for any
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Considering the weights
\mu_{L}(Y)
, with
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, in an increasing order, we obtain a chain of the form

a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{r}.

We define the
bandwidth

\delta
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action as the difference
a_{r}-a_{0}
, and define the
criticality
of the action as the integer
r
. Moreover, we set

Y_{i}:=\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}},\mu_{L}(Y)=a_{i}}Y.

For every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
we denote by

X^{+}(Y):=\{x\in X\mid\lim_{t\to 0}tx\in Y\},\hskip 8.5359ptX^{-}(Y):=\{x\in X\mid\lim_{t\to\infty}tx\in Y\}

the
Białynicki-Birula cells
of the action.

We are not assuming the smoothness of
X
, therefore we cannot claim that the natural maps
X^{\pm}(Y)\to Y
are affine bundles, as it would follow by the Białynicki-Birula theorem (see for instance
[
25
, Theorem 2.4]
). However, being
X
normal and projective and using
[
30
, Theorem 1]
we may still claim that the varieties
X^{\pm}(Y)
are locally closed and, in particular, that there exists a unique fixed component
Y_{+}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
(resp.
Y_{-}\in{\mathcal{Y}}
) such that
X^{+}(Y_{+})
(resp.
X^{-}(Y_{-})
) is a dense open set in
X
. We will call
Y_{+}
and
Y_{-}
respectively the
source
and the
sink
of the action, and refer to them as the
extremal fixed components
; the rest of fixed point components are called
inner
, and their set will be denoted by
{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}
.
Note that
Y_{\pm}
can also be described as the fixed point components where
\mu_{L}
acquires its extremal values
a_{0},a_{r}
(see for instance
[
25
, Remark 2.12]
).

Moreover we say that a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is
equalized
at
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
if for every point
p\in(X^{-}(Y)\cup X^{+}(Y))\setminus Y
the isotropy group of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action at
p
is trivial. More generally, a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is equalized if it is equalized at every component
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.
In the case in which
X
is smooth, this is equivalent to say that the induced action on the normal bundle of every fixed point component has weights
\pm 1
(see
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
).

2.4.
GIT-quotients of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions and admissible quotients

Throughout this section we will assume that
(X,L)
is a polarized pair together with an action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
of criticality
r
, and we will use for it the notation introduced in Section
2.3
. We will briefly recall the construction of geometric quotients of an action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on a polarized pair
(X,L)
(we refer to
[
22
, §2.2]
and references therein for a detailed explanation of this construction).

The point of view of
[
22
, §2.2]
was based on
[
4
]
, on which the authors show how geometric quotients of
X
are described in terms of two special types of partitions of
{\mathcal{Y}}
, called
sections
. Here we will be interested in a particular example of those quotients, which are defined as the projective spectra of certain subalgebras of
R(X;L)
.

Definition 2.4
.

For any index
i=0,\ldots,r-1
, the partition

{\mathcal{Y}}=\underbrace{\{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\mid\mu_{L}(Y)\leq a_{i}\}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{-}}\sqcup\underbrace{\{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}\mid\mu_{L}(Y)\geq a_{i+1}\}}_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{+}}

is a section in the sense of
[
4
, Definition 1.2]
, determining an open subset:

X^{\mathop{\rm s}\nolimits}(i,i+1):=X\setminus(\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{-}}X^{+}(Y)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{+}}X^{-}(Y)),

which is the set of stable points of a certain linearization of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
L
. Therefore there exists a geometric quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1):=X^{\mathop{\rm s}\nolimits}(i,i+1)\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
. We will call
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
the
i
-th geometric quotient of the polarized pair
(X,L)
. We will refer to
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1),\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r)
as the
extremal geometric quotients
of
(X,L)
, while the others will be called
inner geometric quotients
.

The following remark explains the relation among the GIT-quotients introduced above and the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
R(X;L)
.

Remark 2.5
.

We recall that the geometric quotients can be described as the projective spectra of some finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-subalgebras of
R(X;L)
. Indeed, following
[
22
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
20
, Amplification 1.11, p. 40]
, choose a rational
\tau\in(a_{i},a_{i+1})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then

\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplus_{m\geq 0,\\
m\tau\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{m\tau},

where by
\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{m\tau}
we denote the direct summand of
\HH^{0}(X,mL)
with weight associated to the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action equal to
m\tau
.

Definition 2.6
.

For every
i=0,\ldots,r
, we can define a
semigeometric quotient

\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i)
as the projective spectrum of the finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i):=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{ma_{i}}.

A semigeometric quotient may also be defined in terms of partitions, see for instance
[
22
, Proposition 2.0]
; this presentation allows us to define, for every
i
, natural surjective morphisms:

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(i-1,i)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i)}

The case of our interest will be the
extremal semigeometric quotients
, namely
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,0)
and
\mathcal{G}\!X(r,r)
. There are bijective morphisms
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,0)\to Y_{-}
,
\mathcal{G}\!X(r,r)\to Y_{+}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Since the intersection
\bigcap_{i=0}^{r-1}X^{s}(i,i+1)
is open and non-empty, we obtain a birational map among the geometric quotients

\psi:\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!X(1,2)\dashrightarrow\ldots\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r).

The map
\psi
is called the
birational map associated to the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of criticality
r
. The action is of
B-type
if the compositions
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\to\mathcal{G}\!X(0,0)\to Y_{-}
,
\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r)\to\mathcal{G}\!X(r,r)\to Y_{+}
are isomorphisms.

Remark 2.9
.

The above definition tells us that
1
-dimensional orbits having a limiting point
x_{\pm}\in Y_{\pm}
are uniquely determined by
x_{\pm}
, and that these two correspondences are given by isomorphisms
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\to Y_{-}
,
\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r)\to Y_{+}
. If the variety
X
is smooth, the Białynicki-Birula Theorem implies that the action is of B-type if and only if the sink and the source are subvarieties of codimension
1
, and this was the original definition of B-type action, as presented in
[
25
, Definition 3.1]
. Note that in this case, the birational map
\psi
coincides with the one introduced in
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]
, which associates to every point
p\in Y_{-}
the limit, for
t\to 0
, of the unique orbit having
p
as limit for
t\to\infty
.

Our next goal is to study when the birational map
\psi
is a small modification; to this end, we introduce a new class of geometric quotients, called
admissible
.

Definition 2.10
.

A geometric quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
is
admissible
if
X\setminus(X^{s}(i,i+1)\cup Y_{\pm})
does not contain codimension one subvarieties.

Definition 2.11
.

A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is a
bordism
if it is of
B
-type and, for every inner component
Y
, the closure of the Białynicki-Birula cells
\overline{X^{\pm}(Y)}
does not contain codimension one subvarieties.

Let us notice that Definitions
2.8
,
2.11
generalize the ones given in
[
25
, Definition 3.1, Definition 3.8]
in the smooth case.

Example 2.12
.

A simple example of bordism can be constructed as follows (we refer the interested reader to
[
22
, §5.4]
,
[
12
]
for details). Let
V_{-},V_{+}
be two complex vector spaces of dimension
n
,
k\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
, and consider the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the Grassmannian
G(k,V)
of
k
-dimensional vector subspaces of
V:=V_{-}\oplus V_{+}

, and

along

\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)={\mathbb{P}}_{G(k,V_{-})}({\mathcal{S}}_{-}^{\vee}\otimes V_{+}),\quad\mathcal{G}\!X(k-1,k)={\mathbb{P}}_{G(k,V_{+})}(V_{-}\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{\vee}),

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(k-1,k).}

Following
[

]

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(1,2)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

In this particular example all the geometric quotients

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(i-1,i)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{N}}^{\vee}_{+})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

in

Remark 2.13
.

Given a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
which is a bordism, then the natural birational map
\psi:\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r)
is a small modification.

Remark 2.14
.

Given a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
, every geometric quotient is admissible if and only if for every component
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}
it holds
\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits\overline{X^{\pm}(Y)}\geq 2
.

Lemma 2.15
.

If
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
is not admissible, then either every
\mathcal{G}\!X(k,k+1)
, for
k<i
, or every
\mathcal{G}\!X(m,m+1)
, for
m>i
, is not admissible.

Proof.

By assumption,
X\setminus(X^{s}(i,i+1)\cup Y_{\pm})
contains a divisor. Thus by construction such a divisor will be contained either in the closure of a cell
X^{+}(Y_{j})
, if
j\leq i
, or in the closure of
X^{-}(Y_{j})
, if
j\geq i+1
. Let us prove the statement in the first case; the second one is analogous. By definition, for any

the cell
X^{+}(Y_{j})
will be contained in
X\setminus(X^{s}(m,m+1)\cup Y_{\pm})
, proving that any other quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(m,m+1)
will not be admissible.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

If
\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)
and
\mathcal{G}\!X(r-1,r)
are admissible, then every geometric quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
, for
i=1,\ldots,r-2
is admissible too.

Corollary 2.17
.

A B-type
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is a bordism if and only if every geometric quotient is admissible.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of criticality

, and assume that the action is a bordism. For every index

, we define an

as
e_{i}:\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1))\to\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(X)
,

,
where
\pi_{i}:X^{s}(i,i+1)\to\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
is the geometric quotient map.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
(X,L)
be as in Definition
2.18
. For every
i=0,\ldots,r-1
and every rational function

, we have
e_{i}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f))=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f\circ\pi_{i})
.

Proof.

We have
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f\circ\pi_{i})=\overline{\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f))}+E
, where
E
is a Weil divisor whose support is contained in
X\setminus X^{s}(i,i+1)
. Since
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
is admissible by Corollary

,

.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
(X,L)
be as in Definition
2.18
. Then for any
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1))
such that
D\sim D^{\prime}
, it holds that
e_{i}(D)\sim e_{i}(D^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
D^{\prime}=D+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f)
. Then, using Lemma
2.19
, we obtain:

\begin{split}e_{i}(D^{\prime})&=\overline{\pi_{i}^{-1}(D^{\prime})}=\overline{\pi_{i}^{-1}(D+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f))}=\\
&=\overline{\pi_{i}^{-1}(D)}+\overline{\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits f)}=e_{i}(D)+e_{i}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(f)).\end{split}

∎

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
act on the polarized pair
(X,L)
. Then every Cartier divisor is linearly equivalent to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisor. Moreover, for every
i=0,...,r-1
, the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on
X
is a bordism if and only if the only
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisors are
Y_{\pm}
and the divisors of the form
e_{i}(E)
, for
E\in\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1))
.

Proof.

Since every Cartier divisor is difference of two very ample divisors, we prove that every very ample divisor is linearly equivalent to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant one. Let us consider the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the linear system
|A_{1}|
, with
A_{1}
very ample; the action will have at least a fixed point, which is associated to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisor, hence we conclude.

We now show the second part of the statement, noting that the only if part is obvious.
Let us then assume that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
is a bordism. Note that the divisors of the form
e_{i}(E)
,
E\in\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1))
are clearly
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant. Now let
D
be an irreducible
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisor. If
D
is pointwise fixed by the action, then it is either the sink or the source, by definition of bordism. On the other hand, if
D
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant but not pointwise fixed, then it contains an
(n-2)
-dimensional family of
1
-dimensional orbits (whose union is dense in
D
). Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}p
be the general element of this family, and let
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2}
be the fixed point components of the action containing the sink and the source of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}p
, respectively. It follows that
D\subset X^{-}(Y_{1})\cap X^{+}(Y_{2})
, and from the definition of bordism we conclude that
Y_{1}=Y_{-}
,
Y_{2}=Y_{+}
.
In particular, the general orbit in
D
is contained in
X^{\mathop{\rm s}\nolimits}(i,i+1)
, for every
i
, and we may conclude that
D=e_{i}((D\cap X^{\mathop{\rm s}\nolimits}(i,i+1))\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
.
∎

We conclude this section by recalling the notion of
geometric realization of a birational map
, which was already introduced in
[
23
, Definition 2.10]
:

Definition 2.22
.

Given a birational map
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
between normal projective varieties, a
geometric realization of
\phi
is a normal projective variety
X
, endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of B-type such that the sink and the source are precisely
Y_{-},Y_{+}
and the natural birational map among them, defined in Remark
2.7
, coincides with
\phi
.

As we will later see, the properties we are requiring to a geometric realization guarantee that the birational transformation
\phi
can be accurately described in terms of features of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. In particular, up to the choice of a polarization of
X
, the corresponding GIT quotients will give us a factorization of
\phi
.

In Section
4
we will address the question of constructing geometric realizations of small modifications; more concretely our approach will show the existence of a strong relation among small modifications defined by MDBs and bordisms.

3.
Pruning a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action

In this section we show how to construct, starting from a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a polarized pair
(Z,E)
satisfying certain assumptions,
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant birational maps
Z\dashrightarrow X
to normal projective varieties
X
supporting
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions that are bordisms: this algebro-geometric procedure will be called the
pruning
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
Z
(see Definition
3.3
).

As we have seen in Definition
2.11
, a bordism requires an isomorphism between the extremal fixed point components and the extremal geometric quotients, and the non existence of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisors other than the extremal fixed point components and the extensions (see Lemma
2.21
). At least in the smooth case, a variety with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action can be birationally modified –by means of a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant blow-up (see
[
3
, Definition 3.14]
) along the extremal fixed point components– in order to fulfill the first property. On the other hand, many examples show that the existence of inner
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant divisors is common. For instance, this is always the case when the variety
Z
has an isolated extremal fixed point (cf.
[
25
, Lemma 2.6]
, see also
[
29
, Theorem 4.5]
for particular examples of varieties with this feature). Then one can conclude that, even in the smooth case, performing a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant blow-up does not turn a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a variety into a bordism. Pruning is then a generalization of that procedure, that allows us to birationally and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly modify a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a variety with into a bordism. Intuitively the pruning procedure consists of cutting the segment of weights of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
Z
into a smaller interval and thus removing some fixed components of certain weights:

The first segment represents the weights associated to the fixed components of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(Z,E)
. In order to obtain a bordism, we remove the components with weights smaller than or equal to (resp. greater than or equal to)
a_{h}
(resp.
a_{j+1}
), so that every geometric quotient kept is admissible, and the sink and the source of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the pruning
X
of
Z
are respectively the geometric quotients
Y_{-}:=\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1),Y_{+}:=\mathcal{G}\!Z(j,j+1)
.

Set-up 3.1
.

Throughout this section
(Z,E)
will denote a polarized pair, admitting a nontrivial
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action with critical values
a_{0},\dots,a_{r}
and bandwidth
\delta
. Moreover let us consider
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}\in[a_{0},a_{r}]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
,
with
\rho_{-}<\rho_{+}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

In the situation of Set-up
3.1
, assume furthermore that
\rho_{\pm}
are integers, that
E
is very ample, and that the embedding
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\HH^{0}(Z,E))
is projectively normal.
Then the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

S:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=m\rho_{-}}^{m\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}.

is finitely generated.

Proof.

We will show that the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

\widetilde{S}:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=m\rho_{-}}^{m\rho_{+}}S^{m}\HH^{0}(Z,E)_{k},

is finitely generated, and that the natural homomorphism
\widetilde{i^{*}}:\widetilde{S}\to S
is surjective.

We first prove that the algebra
\widetilde{S}
is finitely generated. To this end, since
E
is ample using
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
we may suppose that
\HH^{0}(Z,E)
is generated by
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
, with
s_{i}\in\HH^{0}(Z,E)_{w_{i}}
for every
i
, where
w_{i}\in[a_{0},a_{r}]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
are the weights of the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\HH^{0}(Z,E)
. The monomials
\prod_{i}s_{i}^{m_{i}}
in
S^{m}(\HH^{0}(Z,E))
belonging to
\widetilde{S}
are those which satisfy the following system of inequalities:

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w_{i}-\rho_{-})m_{i}\geq 0,\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\rho_{+}-w_{i})m_{i}\geq 0,\\
m_{i}\geq 0.\end{cases}

This is a rational polyhedral cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, therefore by Gordan’s Lemma (see, for instance,
[
9
, Proposition 1.2.17]
) its intersection with the lattice of monomials is finitely generated.

Finally, in order to prove that
\widetilde{i^{*}}
is surjective we simply note that the natural map
i^{*}:\Sym(\HH^{0}(Z,E))\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)
is surjective –thanks to the projective normality of
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\HH^{0}(Z,E))
– and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant.
∎

We remark that the Lemma above holds in a greater generality, but we have presented it in this way for the sake of simplicity.

Definition 3.3
.

In the situation of Set-up
3.1
, let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
be the minimum positive integer such that
\rho_{\pm}d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We define the
pruning of
(Z,E)
with respect to
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}
as:

X:=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits S^{(nd)},\quad n\gg 0,

where
S^{(nd)}
is the graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
S^{(nd)}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}S^{(nd)}_{m}
whose graded pieces are defined by

S^{(nd)}_{m}:=\bigoplus_{k=mnd\rho_{-}}^{mnd\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mndE)_{k},\quad m\geq 0.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
S^{(nd)}
is finitely generated for
n\gg 0
by Lemma
3.2
, and that
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits S^{(nd)}=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits S^{(n^{\prime}d)}
for
n,n^{\prime}\gg 0
. Therefore
X
is well-defined and depends only on the pair
(Z,E)
and on the rational numbers
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}
. Furthermore, the pruning of
(Z,E)
with respect to
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}
is equal to the pruning of
(Z,nE)
with respect to
n\rho_{-},n\rho_{+}
, for any
n>0
.

Remark 3.5
.

In the case in which
\rho_{\pm}
belong to the same open interval
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
, then the resulting variety will be a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-fibration over the geometric quotient
\mathcal{G}\!Z(i,i+1)
, whose fibers are the closures of the
1
-dimensional orbits of the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. The sink and the source of the action are two sections of the fibration. Let us notice also that if
Z
is smooth and
\rho_{-}\in(a_{0},a_{1})
,
\rho_{+}\in(a_{r-1},a_{r})
, then the pruning of
Z
with respect to
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}
is precisely the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant blow-up of
Z
along the sink and the source (see
[
3
, Definition 3.14]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

In the situation of Set-up
3.1
, take
\rho_{-}\in(a_{h},a_{h+1})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\rho_{+}\in(a_{j},a_{j+1})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
for some
h,j\in\{0,\dots,r-1\}
. Then the pruning
X
of
(Z,E)
with respect to
\rho_{-},\rho_{+}
is a normal projective variety, endowed with a B-type
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action whose sink and source are, respectively,
\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1),\mathcal{G}\!Z(j,j+1)
. Moreover
X
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly birational to
Z
and, if
\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits\overline{Z^{\pm}(Y)}\geq 2
for every fixed point component
Y
of
Z
with
h<\mu_{E}(Y)<j
, then the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the pruning
X
is a bordism.

The proof of Theorem
3.6
will be divided in several steps. Without loss of generality, using Remark
3.4
, we may assume –by exchanging
E
with a suitable multiple– that
d=n=1
and
\rho_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Note that, by construction, the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on
R(Z;E)
restricts to an action on

S=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=m\rho_{-}}^{m\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}\subset R(Z;E),

providing a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(S)
such that the natural map
Z\dashrightarrow X
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant.

Along the proof, we will use the following notation. For every
m>0
, we will consider the decomposition
S_{m}=S_{m}^{-}\oplus S_{m}^{0}\oplus S_{m}^{+}
, where

S_{m}^{\pm}:=\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{m\rho_{\pm}},\quad S_{m}^{0}:=\bigoplus_{m\rho_{-}<k<m\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}.

For every homogeneous element
f\in S_{m}
,
m>0
, we will denote
D^{+}(f,X):=\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits\left(S_{(f)}\right)\subset X.
Then we define the following open subsets of
X
:

U_{\pm}:=\bigcup_{m>0}\bigcup_{f\in S_{m}^{\pm}}D^{+}(f,X),\quad U_{0}:=\bigcup_{m>0}\bigcup_{f\in S_{m}^{0}}D^{+}(f,X),

and note that, by construction,
X=U_{-}\cup U_{0}\cup U_{+}
, and that
U_{0},U_{\pm}
are
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant.

Step 1
.

The variety
X=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(S)
is normal.

Proof.

We will show that the affine open subsets
D^{+}(f,X)\subset X
are normal, for every
f\in S_{m}^{-}\cup S_{m}^{0}\cup S_{m}^{+}
.

Let us start with the case in which
f\in S_{m}^{0}
.
We claim that

(1)

S_{(f)}=R(Z;E)_{(f)}

The “
\subset
” inclusion is obvious by construction, let us prove the converse. Given an element
\frac{g}{f^{a}}\in R(Z;E)_{(f)}
, with
g,f^{a}\in S_{ma}
,
we can decompose
g=\sum_{k=0}^{ma\delta}g_{k}
, with
g_{k}\in\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}
. There exists a suitable
l\geq 0
– it is enough to take
l\geq ma\rho_{-},ma(\delta-\rho_{+})
– for which

f^{l}g\in S_{m(a+l)}^{0},\mbox{ therefore }\dfrac{f^{l}g}{f^{l+a}}\in\left(\bigoplus_{m>0}S^{0}_{m}\right)_{(f)},

thus we obtain the other inclusion. This tells us that
D^{+}(f,X)
is isomorphic to an open subset
D^{+}(f,Z):=\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits\big(R(Z;E)_{(f)}\big)
of
Z
, hence normal.

Next we prove that
D^{+}(f,X)
is normal for every
f\in S_{m}^{-}
(the proof for
f\in S_{m}^{+}
is analogous). Note that in this case the inclusion
S_{(f)}\subset R(Z;E)_{(f)}
is not an equality in general, but an argument analogous to the one above tells us that:

R(Z,E)_{(f)}=\left(S^{\prime}\right)_{(f)},\qquad\mbox{where }S^{\prime}:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}.

Let us now consider a polynomial ring in one variable
{\mathbb{C}}[y]
, and consider the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on it given by
t\cdot(\sum_{b}c_{b}y^{b})=\sum_{b}c_{b}t^{-b}y^{b}
. We then consider the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
\overline{S}:=S^{\prime}_{(f)}\otimes_{{\mathbb{C}}}{\mathbb{C}}[y]=S^{\prime}_{(f)}[y]
. Note that the variety
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(\overline{S})=D^{+}(f,Z)\times{\mathbb{C}}
is normal, and so it is its categorical quotient by the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action (cf.
[
20
, Chap. 0, §2, (2)]
), which is
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits\big(\overline{S}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}\big)
.

We may then conclude by noting that we have an isomorphism
\varphi:\overline{S}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}\to S_{(f)}
. In fact, every element of
S^{\prime{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
can be written as a finite sum of the form:

\sum_{b=0}^{ma(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-})}\dfrac{g_{b}}{f_{a}}y^{b},\mbox{ where }g_{b}\in\HH^{0}(Z,maE)_{ma\rho_{-}+b}.

The required isomorphism is then given by
\varphi\left(\sum_{b\geq 0}\frac{g_{b}}{f_{a}}y^{b}\right)=\sum_{b\geq 0}\frac{g_{b}}{f_{a}}
.
∎

Step 2
.

The natural
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant map
Z\dashrightarrow X
is birational.

Proof.

It suffices to notice that, as in the proof of Step
1
, the inclusion of graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras
S\subset R(Z;E)
induces isomorphisms
S_{(f)}\simeq R(Z;E)_{(f)}
for every
f\in S_{m}^{0}
,
m>0
. In particular the induced rational map
Z\dashrightarrow X
sends the affine open set
D^{+}(f,Z)\subset Z
isomorphically onto
D^{+}(f,X)\subset X
. Note that this in particular tells us that the open set
U_{0}\subset X
introduced above is the isomorphic image of the open subset
\bigcup_{m>0}\bigcup_{f\in S_{m}^{0}}D^{+}(f,Z)\subset Z
.
∎

Since the algebra
S
is finitely generated by Lemma
3.2
, there exists a positive integer
d^{\prime}
such that
S^{(d^{\prime})}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}S_{d^{\prime}m}
is generated in degree
1
. Therefore
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}:={\mathbb{P}}(S_{d^{\prime}})
, and let us denote by
L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(1)_{|X}
. Since
X
is normal,
L
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-linearizable. For the rest of the section we will consider the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)
.

Step 3
.

The sink and the source of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the pruning
X
of
Z
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1),\mathcal{G}\!Z(j,j+1)
, respectively. The inner fixed components of
X
are isomorphic to the fixed point components of
Z
of weight equal to
a_{h+1},\ldots,a_{j}
. Furthermore, the criticality of the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
is equal to
j-h+1
.

Proof.

Note first that we have a surjective homomorphism of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras:

S=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=m\rho_{-}}^{m\rho_{+}}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{k}\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{m\rho_{-}},

which an inclusion of varieties:

\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1)=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{m\rho_{-}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow X.

By construction,
\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1)
is fixed by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. Moreover, using
[
25
, Remark 2.12]
, the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}={\mathbb{P}}(S_{d^{\prime}})\supset X
defined above has sink
{\mathbb{P}}(\HH^{0}(Z,d^{\prime}E)_{d^{\prime}\rho_{-}})
. Then we may conclude that
\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1)\subset X
is the sink of
X
by noting that
{\mathbb{P}}(\HH^{0}(Z,d^{\prime}E)_{d^{\prime}\rho_{-}})\cap X=\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1)
. In a similar way, one may prove that the source of
X
is isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Z,mE)_{m\rho_{+}}\simeq\mathcal{G}\!Z(j,j+1)
.

In order to compute the inner fixed point components of
X
, we note first that the complement of the extremal fixed point components of
X
is the open set
U_{0}=\bigcup_{m>0}\bigcup_{f\in S_{m}^{0}}D^{+}(f,X)
, which is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly isomorphic to an open set

\bigcup_{m>0}\bigcup_{f\in S_{m}^{0}}D^{+}(f,Z)\subset Z
(see Step
2
), whose fixed point components are the fixed point components of
Z
of
L
-weight
\mu_{L}(Y)\in\{a_{h+1},\dots,a_{j}\}
.

Finally, considering the embedding
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
, the inner fixed point components of
X
are the irreducible components in the intersections
X\cap{\mathbb{P}}(\HH^{0}(Z,d^{\prime}E)_{d^{\prime}a_{i}})
,
i\in\{h+1,\dots,j\}
. In particular, the criticality of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)
is
j-h+1
.
∎

Step 4
.

The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
is of B-type.

Proof.

Using Remark
2.5
and the arguments above one may show that, for every
i=h+1,\ldots,j-1
, it holds
\mathcal{G}\!X(i-h,i-h+1)\simeq\mathcal{G}\!Z(i,i+1)
. Therefore since
\mathcal{G}\!Z(0,1)
and
\mathcal{G}\!Z(r-1,r)
are the sink and the source of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action in
X
by Step
3
, we conclude.
∎

Step 5
.

If
\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits\overline{Z^{\pm}(Y)}\geq 2
for every fixed point component
Y
of
Z
with
h<\mu_{E}(Y)<j
, then the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the pruning
X
is a bordism.

Proof.

For every fixed point connected component
Y
of
Z
of weights
h<\mu_{E}(Y)<j
, using Steps
2
and
3
we conclude
\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits\overline{X^{\pm}(Y)}\geq 2
, i.e. that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
is a bordism.
∎

Using Steps
1
,
2
,
3
,
4

and
5
we conclude the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
Note that the second part of this statement can also be rephrased as follows:

Corollary 3.7
.

In the situation of Set-up
3.1
, if
\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits\overline{Z^{\pm}(Y)}\geq 2
for every fixed point component
Y
with
h<\mu_{E}(Y)<j
, then the pruning
X
is a geometric realization of the small modification
\psi:\mathcal{G}\!Z(h,h+1)\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!Z(j,j+1)
.

4.
From MDBs to bordisms

The purpose of this section is to construct a geometric realization of the small modification
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
associated to an MDB
(L_{-},L_{+})
(recall Definition
2.3
). More precisely, we show the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Y_{-}
be a normal projective variety, and let
(L_{-},L_{+})
be an MDB on
Y_{-}
. We denote by
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
the associated small modification. Then there exists a geometric realization of
\phi
, whose induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is a bordism.

4.1.
Construction of the geometric realization

For the sake of notation, in this section we will denote by

A:=R(Y_{-};L_{-},L_{+})=\bigoplus_{m_{\pm}\geq 0}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},m_{-}L_{-}+m_{+}L_{+})

the finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra associated to the MDB
(L_{-},L_{+})
.
The group

H:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits({\mathbb{Z}}(L_{-},L_{+}),{\mathbb{C}}^{*})

is a
2
-dimensional complex torus, whose character lattice
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)
is
{\mathbb{Z}}(L_{-},L_{+})
, and that acts naturally on the finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
A
. Let
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)^{\vee}
be a
1
-parametric subgroup, such that
\alpha_{\pm}:=\alpha(L_{\pm})>0
; we assume, for simplicity, that
\alpha_{\pm}
are coprime, and denote by
H^{\prime}\subset H
the associated
1
-dimensional subtorus.
We will later show (see Remark
4.11
) that different choices of
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)^{\vee}
yield birationally equivalent geometric realizations.

Remark 4.2
.

The
1
-dimensional torus
H^{\prime}
acts on
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits A
, inducing an
{\mathbb{N}}
-grading on
A
. The algebra
A
endowed with this grading will be denoted by
A^{\alpha}
:

A^{\alpha}:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}A^{\alpha}_{m},\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ where }\hskip 8.5359ptA^{\alpha}_{m}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\\
\alpha(m_{-}L_{-}+m_{+}L_{+})=m\end{subarray}}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},m_{-}L_{-}+m_{+}L_{+}).

Definition 4.3
.

The
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded algebra
A^{\alpha}
is finitely generated by assumption, so we may define
X^{\alpha}:=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits A^{\alpha}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Given
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)^{\vee}
and
H^{\prime}\subset H
as above, we may consider the
1
-dimensional torus

H^{\prime\prime}:=H/H^{\prime},

which acts on
X^{\alpha}
. Indeed the
H
-action on
A^{\alpha}
induces an
H
-action on
X^{\alpha}
, whose kernel is precisely
H^{\prime}
.

Remark 4.5
.

We notice that
A
can be thought of as a local version of the Cox ring construction for MDSs, in the following sense: under the usual MDS-like point of view, one considers the affine variety
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(A)
, from which
Y_{\pm}
(as well as some other birationally equivalent varieties) can be obtained as geometric quotients by the action of the torus
H
. But all these geometric quotients factor via the projection map
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(A)\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(A^{\alpha})
, and so the geometric quotients of
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(A)
via the action of
H
are geometric quotients of
X^{\alpha}
by the action of
H^{\prime\prime}
; the converse of this is obviously true. We summarize this construction by means of the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\alpha}=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits A^{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{-}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces/H^{\prime\prime}}

In the following statements we will use the following
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over
Y_{-}
:

P^{\alpha}:={\mathbb{P}}_{Y_{-}}(\alpha_{+}L_{-}\oplus\alpha_{-}L_{+}).

Lemma 4.6
.

The projective variety
X^{\alpha}
is normal, and there exists a birational map
\Phi:P^{\alpha}\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the ring of sections of the tautological line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1)
. We have:

\begin{split}R(P^{\alpha};{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1))&=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{m_{-}+m_{+}=m}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},m_{-}\alpha_{+}L_{-}+m_{+}\alpha_{-}L_{+})\\
&=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{\alpha(m_{-}\alpha_{+}L_{-}+m_{+}\alpha_{-}L_{+})=m\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},m_{-}\alpha_{+}L_{-}+m_{+}\alpha_{-}L_{+})\\
&=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{\alpha(m_{-}L_{-}+m_{+}L_{+})=m\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}}\HH^{0}(Y_{-},m_{-}L_{-}+m_{+}L_{+}),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}
are coprime. But this
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra is precisely the
(\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+})
-Veronese of
A^{\alpha}
, which is finitely generated.
It follows that
R(P^{\alpha};{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1))
is finitely generated, and that

\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\left(R(P^{\alpha};{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1))\right)\simeq X^{\alpha}.

In particular (see for instance
[
10
, Lemma 7.10]
)
X^{\alpha}
is normal. Furthermore, the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1)
is big (see
[
19
, Lemma 2.3.2. (iv)]
), hence the associated rational map
\Phi:=\varphi_{{\mathcal{O}}_{P^{\alpha}}(1)}:P^{\alpha}\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}
is birational.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Notice that the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle
P^{\alpha}
admits a natural equalized
H^{\prime\prime}
-action with fixed point locus
s_{-}(Y_{-})\sqcup s_{+}(Y_{-})
, where
s_{-},s_{+}
are the sections of
P^{\alpha}
over
Y_{-}
corresponding respectively to the projections of
\alpha_{+}L_{-}\oplus\alpha_{-}L_{+}\to\alpha_{+}L_{-}
,
\alpha_{+}L_{-}\oplus\alpha_{-}L_{+}\to\alpha_{-}L_{+}
.
Moreover the birational map
\Phi:P^{\alpha}\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}
introduced in Lemma
4.6
is
H^{\prime\prime}
-equivariant.

Proposition 4.8
.

The action of
H^{\prime\prime}\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on
X^{\alpha}
is B-type with sink and source
Y_{-}
and
Y_{+}
, respectively, and the induced natural birational map
\psi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
coincides with the small modification
\phi\colon Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
.

Proof.

Consider the birational map
\Phi:P^{\alpha}\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}
introduced in Lemma
4.6
. We first prove that the indeterminacy locus of
\Phi
is contained in
s_{+}(Y_{-})
. We recall that
L_{-}
is ample on
Y_{-}
, thus
\Phi_{\mid s_{-}(Y_{-})}
is well defined; and since by Remark
4.7
we know that
\Phi
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant, it follows that the indeterminacy locus of
\Phi
is
H^{\prime\prime}
-invariant, hence our claim. Therefore,
\Phi_{\mid P^{\alpha}\setminus s_{+}(Y_{-})}\colon P^{\alpha}\setminus s_{+}(Y_{-})\to\mathcal{U}_{-}\subset X^{\alpha}
is an isomorphism where
\mathcal{U}_{-}
is a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant neighborhood of the sink of the action on
X^{\alpha}
; it follows that the sink of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X^{\alpha}
is
s_{-}(Y_{-})\simeq Y_{-}
and is isomorphic to the first geometric quotient of such action.

In order to conclude that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X^{\alpha}
is of B-type, we study an
H^{\prime\prime}
-invariant neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{+}
of the source of
X^{\alpha}
. To do so, we consider the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle
\widetilde{P^{\alpha}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{Y_{+}}(\alpha_{+}L_{-}\oplus\alpha_{-}L_{+})
on
Y_{+}
and show, in a similar way as above, that we can find a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{+}
isomorphic to the complement of a section of
\widetilde{P^{\alpha}}
.
On the other hand, using the arguments above and the construction of the natural birational map
\psi
among the extremal geometric quotients it follows that
\psi
coincides with the small modification
\phi
associated to the MDB
(L_{-},L_{+})
.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

The
H^{\prime\prime}
-action on
X^{\alpha}
is a bordism.

Proof.

Thanks to
[
10
, Lemma 7.10]
, there exists an open subset
U
of
X^{\alpha}
, whose complement has codimension greater or equal than 2, and an open subset
V
of
P^{\alpha}
on which the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant birational map
\Phi_{\mid V}
is an isomorphism.

We know that the
H^{\prime\prime}
-action on
X^{\alpha}
is of B-type by Proposition
4.8
; saying that it is a bordism is to say that the only
H^{\prime\prime}
-invariant divisors in
X^{\alpha}
that are not extensions of divisors in
Y_{-}
are the sink and the source of the action. Let
D
be an
H^{\prime\prime}
-invariant prime divisor on
X^{\alpha}
that is not an extension of a divisor in
Y_{-}
. Since its intersection with
U
is nonempty, we may consider its strict transform
\overline{D}
into
P^{\alpha}
, that will be an
H^{\prime\prime}
-invariant divisor. Then
\overline{D}
coincides with the sink or the source of
P^{\alpha}
, and this implies that
D
is the sink or the source of
X^{\alpha}
.
∎

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.10
.

The
H^{\prime\prime}
-action on
X^{\alpha}
is equalized at the sink
Y_{-}
and the source
Y_{+}
.

Proof.

It suffices to notice that thanks to Remark
4.7
the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
P^{\alpha}
is equalized at the sink and the source and the birational map
\Phi:P^{\alpha}\dashrightarrow X^{\alpha}
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Our construction of a geometric realization depends on the choice of a 1-parameter subgroup
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)^{\vee}
. Given another
\beta\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(H)^{\vee}
as above, the geometric realizations
X^{\alpha}
and
X^{\beta}
are birational. Indeed it suffices to notice that the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundles
P^{\alpha},P^{\beta}
are
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariantly birationally equivalent. However, the geometric quotients of
X^{\alpha}
are independent of the choice of
\alpha
.

5.
From bordisms to MDBs and the induced chamber decomposition

Set-up 5.1
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair endowed with a faithful
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action, where
X
is a normal and
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial projective variety, and
L
is an ample Cartier divisor. Suppose that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is a bordism and it is equalized at the sink and the source. As in Section
2.3
, the critical values of the action will be denoted by
a_{0},\dots,a_{r}
and, without loss of generality, we assume that
\mu_{L}(Y_{-})=0
. We set
\delta:=a_{r}=\mu_{L}(Y_{+})
.

Under Set-up
5.1
, in this section we construct an MDB upon the sink and the source. Moreover we prove the existence of a chamber decomposition of such an MDB, where every chamber model is the geometric quotient of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
. Note that bordisms can be easily constructed via the pruning of a polarized pair
(Z,E)
with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action satisfying some mild conditions (see Section
3
).

Lemma 5.2
.

In the situation of Set-up
5.1
, let
\tau_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
be two rational numbers such that
0\leq\tau_{-}\leq\tau_{+}\leq\delta
, and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
m\tau_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. It holds that:

\bigoplus_{k=m\tau_{-}}^{m\tau_{+}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}=\HH^{0}(X,mL-m\tau_{-}Y_{-}-(m\delta-m\tau_{+})Y_{+}).

Proof.

Let us denote
W:=\HH^{0}(X,mL-m\tau_{-}Y_{-}-(m\delta-m\tau_{+})Y_{+})\subset\HH^{0}(X,mL)
.
Note first that
W
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant, therefore,
W=\bigoplus_{k}(\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}\cap W)
.

We will use
[
24
, Corollary 2.4]
(which follows from
[
6
, Lemma 2.17]
), which determines the multiplicity of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant sections of
\HH^{0}(X,mL)
at the extremal fixed point components of the action. We note first that the proof of this result requires only the smoothness of the variety at the general points of
Y_{\pm}
, and this condition holds in our situation, because
X
is normal and the action is of B-type. The quoted Corollary tells us that a nonzero section
s\in\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{u}
vanishes with multiplicity precisely equal to
u
at
Y_{-}
and
m\delta-u
at
Y_{+}
. This implies that
\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{u}\subset W
if
u\in[m\tau_{-},m\tau_{+}]
and
\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{u}\cap W=0
if
u\notin[m\tau_{-},m\tau_{+}]
, and the claimed equality follows.

∎

We will show in Lemma
5.4
that, for any positive integer
m
greater or equal than a certain
m_{-}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, there exists an isomorphism between global sections of
mL
on
X
of weight
c
, with
c\in[0,\ldots,m\delta]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
, and global sections of
mL_{|Y_{-}}-cY_{-|Y_{-}}
on
Y_{-}
. To this end, we first prove the following:

Lemma 5.3
.

In the situation of Set-up
5.1
, the sink
Y_{-}
and the source
Y_{+}
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial.

Proof.

We prove the result for
Y_{-}
; a similar proof works in the case of
Y_{+}
. Let
D
be a prime divisor in
Y_{-}
, and consider its extension
e_{-}(D)\in\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(X)
. By definition it is the closure
\overline{\pi^{-1}(D)}\subset X
, where
\pi:X^{s}(0,1)\to Y_{-}
. The fact that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is of B-type implies that
e_{-}(D)
can also be written as
\overline{\pi^{\prime-1}(D)}
where
\pi^{\prime}:X^{-}(Y_{-})\to Y_{-}
. Then it follows that
e_{-}(D)\cap Y_{-}=D
. Since
X
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, there exists
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
such that
me_{-}(D)=e_{-}(mD)
is Cartier, and so
mD=me_{-}(D)\cap Y_{-}
is Cartier, as well.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

In the situation of Set-up
5.1
, there exists a positive integer
m_{-}
such that for
m\geq m_{-}
and every
c\in[0,\ldots,m\delta]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
it holds that:

\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{c}\simeq\HH^{0}(Y_{-},mL_{|Y_{-}}-cY_{-|Y_{-}}).

Proof.

Let us first note that, by Lemma
5.2
it follows that, for every
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, and every
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
c\leq m\delta
, we have a commutative diagram with exact columns:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\HH^{0}(X,mL-(c+1)Y_{-})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus\limits_{k=c}^{m\delta}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{c}}}

such that for every

, and every

is surjective, or, equivalently, that the rational map

|mL-cY_{-}|\dashrightarrow|(mL-cY_{-})_{|Y_{-}}|

is surjective.

We start by claiming that there exists
m_{-}
such that for every
m\geq m_{-}
,
\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{c}\neq 0
for every

. In fact, let
C
be the closure of the general
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-orbit in

, which has extremal fixed points in
Y_{-}
,
Y_{+}
, respectively. The generality assumption implies that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on

is faithful, that its extremal points are smooth points of

and, by the Białynicki-Birula decomposition, that

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

, Corollary 3.2]
one has
\HH^{0}(C,mL_{|C})\simeq\HH^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(m\delta))

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the vector space
\HH^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(m\delta))
is
[0,m\delta]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
, the claim follows.

Putting it together with the commutative diagram above, the claim implies that the map
\HH^{0}(X,mL-(c+1)Y_{-})\to\HH^{0}(X,mL-cY_{-})
is not surjective for

and every

, that is, we have a strict inclusion:

|mL-(c+1)Y_{-}|+Y_{-}\subsetneq|mL-cY_{-}|.

In particular (since the projective space
|mL-cY_{-}|
is spanned by
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant elements), there exists a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant effective divisor

whose support does not contain
Y_{-}
. Using Lemma
2.21
It follows that

D_{1}=e_{0}({D^{\prime}_{1}})+aY_{+}\mbox{ for some $a\geq 0$ and some $D^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(Y_{-})$;}

here we are denoting by
e_{0}:\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(Y_{-})=\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1))\to\mathop{\rm Div}\nolimits(X)
the extension map of divisors introduced in Definition
2.18
. By restricting the above equality to
Y_{-}
we get that
D_{1\mid Y_{-}}=D_{1}^{\prime}
, hence

D_{1}=e_{0}({D_{1}}_{|Y_{-}})+aY_{+}\mbox{ for some $a\geq 0$}.

Let us now conclude the proof of the statement by showing that the restriction map
|mL-cY_{-}|\dashrightarrow|(mL-cY_{-})_{|Y_{-}}|
is surjective. Given
D^{\prime}\in|(mL-cY_{-})_{|Y_{-}}|
, Lemma
2.20
tells us that
e_{0}(D^{\prime})\equiv e_{0}({D_{1}}_{|Y_{-}})
which we have proven to be linearly equivalent to

, for some

. Then it follows that
(e_{0}(D^{\prime})+aY_{+})_{|Y_{-}}=D^{\prime}
, and
e_{0}(D^{\prime})+aY_{+}\in|D|
.

Remark 5.5

We conclude this section by showing that the MDB obtained in Theorem
5.6
admits a chamber decomposition, which is induced by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)
. The decomposition of Mori dream regions, which reproduces the behaviour of Mori dream spaces, has been stated by
[
14
, Definition 2.12]
: we refer to
[
15
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
27
, Proposition 9.6]
for the precise statements.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a rational polyhedral cone in
\mathop{\rm CDiv}\nolimits(X)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a chamber if, for any
D_{1},D_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
with finitely generated section ring, it holds that
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(X;D_{1})\simeq\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(X;D_{2})
. We call the variety
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(X;D_{1})
the
chamber model
of
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Theorem 5.8
.

In the situation of Set-up
5.1
, the cone
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle L_{-},L_{+}\rangle
of Theorem
5.6
admits a subdivision

{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{r-1}{\mathcal{C}}_{i},\quad{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=\langle L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i}Y_{-|Y_{-}},L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i+1}Y_{-|Y_{-}}\rangle.

Moreover, for every
i=0,\ldots,r-1
the cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
is a chamber whose model is
\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1)
.

Proof.

The existence of such a subdivision follows immediately by recalling that
a_{i}<a_{i+1}
for every
i=0,\ldots,r-1
. In order to conclude, it suffices to show that, for every
i=0,\ldots,r-1
, the cone
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
is a chamber. Let
D=\beta(L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i}Y_{-|Y_{-}})+\gamma(L_{|Y_{-}}-a_{i+1}Y_{-|Y_{-}})
be a divisor in
{\mathcal{C}}_{i}
, where
\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
. Let
q
be a positive integer such that
q\beta,q\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
q\geq m_{-}
, with
m_{-}
as in Lemma
5.4
. Using again Lemma
5.4
we obtain that

\HH^{0}(Y_{-},qD)\simeq\HH^{0}(X,q(\beta+\gamma)L)_{q(\beta a_{i}+\gamma a_{i+1})},

and since
q\beta a_{i}+q\gamma a_{i+1}\in(q(\beta+\gamma)a_{i},q(\beta+\gamma)a_{i+1})
, using Remark
2.5
and the above isomorphism it holds that

\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(Y_{-};qD)\simeq\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(X,mq(\beta+\gamma)L)_{mq(\beta a_{i}+\gamma a_{i+1})}\simeq\mathcal{G}\!X(i,i+1).

∎

6.
An example: factorization of the cubo-cubic standard Cremona transformation of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}

In this section we will illustrate the concepts of pruning and of the geometric realization of an MDB in an example coming from classical algebraic geometry, namely the cubo-cubic standard Cremona involution of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
. To do so, we will use the language of toric varieties, since the algebraic approach presented in these constructions can be easily implemented in the toric setting. This approach, which was already used in
[
21
, Example 6.1]
, will be studied in more detail in
[
2
]
.

Consider the cubo-cubic standard Cremona involution of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
:

\displaystyle T:{\mathbb{P}}^{3}

\displaystyle\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{3}

\displaystyle(x:y:z:w)

\displaystyle\mapsto(x^{-1}:y^{-1}:z^{-1}:w^{-1})=(yzw:xzw:xyw:xyz).

Let
W\to{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
be the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
along the
4
coordinates points
P_{0}=(1:0:0:0),\ldots,P_{3}=(0:0:0:1)
, and let
E_{i}
, with
i=0,\ldots,3
, be the associated exceptional divisors. Then the map
T
is given by the linear system of cubics which are singular along
P_{0},\ldots,P_{3}
, that is
|3H-2E_{0}-2E_{1}-2E_{2}-2E_{3}|
(cf.
[
18
, §2]
), where
H
denotes the pullback into
W
of the hyperplane section in
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
.

As explained in
[
11
, Example 5.1.6]
, the birational map
T
admits a factorization

\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}^{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{1}}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{2}}
\textstyle{\widetilde{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{3}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}^{3}}

where

•

\psi_{1}:{\mathbb{P}}^{3}\dashrightarrow W
is the inverse of the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
along the four coordinate points
P_{0},P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
;

•

\psi_{2}:W\dashrightarrow\widetilde{W}
is the flip of the strict transform of the six lines joining the coordinate points;

•

\psi_{3}:\widetilde{W}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
is the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
along the four coordinate points.

Since all these varieties are toric, we may have a better understanding of the situation by looking at the associated moment polytopes (Figure
1
).

{\mathbb{P}}^{3},W,\widetilde{W}

A natural geometric realization of the map
T
is given by
X={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, together with the diagonal action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
defined as:

t\big((x_{0}\!:\!x_{1}),(y_{0}\!:\!y_{1}),(z_{0}\!:\!z_{1}),(w_{0}\!:\!w_{1})\big)=\big((x_{0}\!:\!tx_{1}),(y_{0}\!:\!ty_{1}),(z_{0}\!:\!tz_{1}),(w_{0}\!:\!tw_{1})\big),

whose sink and source are respectively
y_{-}=(\infty,\infty,\infty,\infty)
and
y_{+}=(0,0,0,0)
, where we set
\infty:=(0:1)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,

. In fact

is precisely the induced birational map from
{\mathbb{P}}(T_{X,y_{-}})\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(T_{X,y_{+}})
that sends a (general) tangent direction

to

if there exists an orbit with sink and source at

Different polarizations of
X
will give rise to different possible prunings of the

-action and, subsequently, will produce different factorizations of the natural birational map

, then the action on
(X,L)
has bandwidth and criticality equal to four and, without loss of generality, we may assume that the weights with respect to

0,1,2,3,4
. Moreover, it has four geometric quotients

\mathcal{G}\!X(0,1)\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{3},\quad\mathcal{G}\!X(1,2)\simeq W,\quad\mathcal{G}\!X(2,3)\simeq\widetilde{W},\quad\mathcal{G}\!X(3,4)\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{3},

Two classes of level Eulerian posets
Richard Ehrenborg

Abstract
. We present two classes of level Eulerian posets. Both classes contain intervals of rank k + 1 k+1 whose 𝐜𝐝 {\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}} -index is the sum over all 𝐜𝐝 {\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}} -monomials w w of degree k k and the coefficient of the monomial w w is r r to the power of the number of 𝐝 {\mathbf{d}} ’s in w w . We also show that the order complexes of every interval in the first class are homeomorphic to spheres.

1.
Introduction

Level posets were introduced in
[
6
]

as posets where the cover relations between
two adjacent ranks are the same independent of the rank.
The cover relations are naturally encoded by a
0,1
-matrix
M=(m_{i,j})
,
that is, the element
(i,s)
is covered by
(j,s+1)
if and only if
m_{i,j}=1
.
Such posets are naturally infinite and ranked.
See Figure
1
for three examples.

More interestingly, the paper
constructs such a poset which is Eulerian;
see
[
6
, Example 4.7]
.
This level poset contains intervals whose
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index
have all the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-coefficients equal to
1
;
see
[
6
, Example 8.2]
.
This example is the second poset displayed in
Figure
1
.

Recall that the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-index of a poset
P
is
an encoding of the flag
f
-vector of the poset as a polynomial
\Psi(P)

in the non-commutative variables
{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbf{b}}
.
Furthermore, when the poset is Eulerian
the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-index can be expressed in terms of

{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}={\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}
;
see
[
3
,
9
]
.
For a level posets a more theoretical result is proven in
[
6
]
,
namely, that the sum

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{i,j}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\Psi([(i,0),(j,k)])

is a non-commutative rational series in the variables
{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbf{b}}
.
Furthermore, for level Eulerian posets, this series
is a non-commutative rational series in the variables
{\mathbf{c}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}
.

In this paper we present two new classes of level Eulerian posets.
Furthermore, we calculate their
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-series.
Interestingly, both classes contain intervals
where the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-coefficients are
r
to the power of the number
of
{\mathbf{d}}
’s in the associated
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-monomial.
This phenomenon is reflected in the fact that the denominator of the
rational
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series is the polynomial
1-{\mathbf{c}}-r\cdot{\mathbf{d}}
.

For the first class of level Eulerian posets we also show that
all of the intervals are shellable. As a consequence the order complex
of each interval in this poset is homeomorphic to a sphere.

We end the paper with concluding remarks and open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

For a non-negative matrix
M=(m_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}

let
\Bin(M)
be the
0,1
-matrix defined by

\displaystyle\Bin(M)_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&m_{i,j}>0,\\
0&m_{i,j}=0.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we let
J
be the matrix with all the entries equal to
1
.
Note that the size of the matrix
J
will be understood from the context.
We call a non-negative square matrix
M

primitive
if
there is a positive integer
k
such that
\Bin(M^{k})=J
.
The smallest such power
\gamma
is called the
exponent
.

An equivalent way to encode the information of the matrix
M

is the associated digraph, where there is a directed edge

i\longrightarrow j
if and only if
m_{i,j}=1
.
Hence the matrix
M
is primitive
if and only if there exist a positive integer
k
such that
there is a directed path from any vertex to any other vertex with exactly
k
steps.

A poset
P
is
ranked
if there is a function
\rho:P\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

such that for all cover relations
x\prec y
we have
\rho(x)+1=\rho(y)
.
For two elements
x
and
y
in a poset such that
x\leq y
, the
interval

[x,y]
is the set
\{z\in P:x\leq z\leq y\}
.
A poset
P
is
Eulerian
if all non-singleton intervals have the same number
of elements of even rank as the number elements of odd rank.
To verify the Eulerian condition, it is enough to do so for intervals
of even rank; see
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]

or
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
.
We call the condition that all the intervals of length
k
are Eulerian
the
rank
k
Eulerian condition
.

For an
n\times n

0,1
-matrix
M
the associated

level poset
is a poset on the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\times{\mathbb{Z}}

such that the cover relation is given by

(i,s)\prec(j,s+1)
if and only if
m_{i,j}=1
.
Note that the order relation
(i,s)\leq(j,p)
holds if and only if
the equality
\Bin(M^{p-s})_{i,j}=1
holds.
This equivalence also holds in the extreme case when
p=s

since
M^{0}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.
Hence the number of elements of rank
s
in the interval

[(i,0),(j,p)]
is the
(i,j)
entry of the matrix product

\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{p-s})
.
The rank
p
Eulerian condition is therefore the following matrix identity

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{p}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{p-s})=0.

By the comment above, it is enough to verify this condition for even positive integers
p
.
Note that an equivalent formulation of the rank
2
Eulerian condition
is the following identity:

\displaystyle M^{2}=2\cdot\Bin(M^{2}).

This condition is equivalent to that every rank
2
interval is isomorphic to a diamond,
that is, the rank
2
Boolean algebra.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
M
is an
n\times n
primitive
0,1
-matrix with exponent
\gamma
.

(a)

Let
W
be the matrix

\displaystyle W=\sum_{s=1}^{\gamma-1}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s}).

If all of the row sums and the column sums of
W
is

(-1)^{\gamma-1}\cdot n/2-1

then the matrix
M
satisfies the rank
2k
Eulerian condition for
k\geq\gamma
.

(b)

If the matrix
M
satisfies the Eulerian condition of rank
2\gamma

and
(J-\Bin(M^{\gamma-1}))^{2}=0
then
the matrix
M
satisfies the Eulerian condition of rank
2\gamma-2
.

Proof.

For
k\geq\gamma

the sum in the Eulerian condition of rank
2k
can be rewritten as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{2k}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{2k-s})

\displaystyle=2J+W\cdot J+J\cdot W+(-1)^{\gamma}\cdot J^{2}.

This identity follows from the facts
the two terms
s=0
and
s=2k
both yield the matrix
J
,
the terms
1\leq s\leq\gamma-1
yield
W\cdot J
,
the terms
2k-\gamma+1\leq s\leq 2k-1
yield
J\cdot W

and the sum of
2k-2\gamma+1
middle terms yield
(-1)^{\gamma}\cdot J^{2}
.
The matrix on the right-hand side of (
2.1
) equals

(2+2c+(-1)^{\gamma}\cdot n)\cdot J=0

where
c
is the row and column sums of
W
.
This proves statement (a).

To prove statement (b),
note the sum in the rank
2\gamma-2
Eulerian condition can be rewritten as

(2.2)

\displaystyle(-1)^{\gamma-1}\cdot\Bin(M^{\gamma-1})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=0\\
s\neq\gamma-1\end{subarray}}^{2\gamma-2}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{2\gamma-2-s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\gamma-1}\cdot\Bin(M^{\gamma-1})^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s=0\\
s\neq\gamma-1,\gamma,\gamma+1\end{subarray}}^{2\gamma}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{2\gamma-s}),

where we use that
\Bin(M^{s})=J=\Bin(M^{s+2})
for
s\geq\gamma
.
Since
J=\Bin(M^{\gamma})=\Bin(M^{\gamma+1})
,
we reformulate the condition that the square vanishes as follows:

(2.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\gamma}\cdot(\Bin(M^{\gamma-1})-J)^{2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\gamma}\cdot\Bin(M^{\gamma-1})^{2}+\sum_{s=\gamma-1}^{\gamma+1}(-1)^{s}\cdot\Bin(M^{s})\cdot\Bin(M^{2\gamma-s}).

Adding these two identities (
2.2
)
and (
2.3
)
yields the sum of the rank
2\gamma
Eulerian condition
which is equal to
0
. Hence the rank
2\gamma-2
Eulerian condition holds.
∎

3.
The
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
- and
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index and coalgebra techniques

A poset
P
is
graded
if it is ranked and has a unique minimal element
\widehat{0}

and a unique maximal element
\widehat{1}
. We say that
P
has rank
n+1
if

\rho(\widehat{0})=0
and
\rho(\widehat{1})=n+1
.

Let
P
be a graded poset of rank
n+1
.
Let
S=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
We define the flag
f
-vector entry
f_{S}(P)=f_{S}
to be the number of chains
c

in the poset
P
with elements having ranks in the set
S
, that is,

\displaystyle f_{S}

\displaystyle=\{\{\widehat{0}=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}<x_{k+1}=\widehat{1}\}\subseteq P:\rho(x_{i})=s_{i}\text{ for }i=0,1,\ldots,k+1\},

where
s_{0}=0
and
s_{k+1}=n+1
.
For two elements
x
and
y
in the poset
P
such that
x\leq y
,
let the rank difference be denoted by
\rho(x,y)=\rho(y)-\rho(x)
.
The
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-index
is defined as

\displaystyle\Psi(P)=\sum_{c}({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}})^{\rho(x_{0},x_{1})-1}\cdot{\mathbf{b}}\cdot({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}})^{\rho(x_{1},x_{2})-1}\cdot{\mathbf{b}}\cdots{\mathbf{b}}\cdot({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}})^{\rho(x_{k-1},x_{k})-1}\cdot{\mathbf{b}}\cdot({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}})^{\rho(x_{k},x_{k+1})-1},

where the sum is over all chains

c=\{\widehat{0}=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}<x_{k+1}=\widehat{1}\}

in the poset
P
.
Note that the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-index of a graded poset
P
of rank
n+1

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
.

When the poset
P
is Eulerian, the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-index
\Psi(P)

can be expressed in terms of the variables

{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}={\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}
.
This fact was first proved by
Bayer and Klapper
[
3
]
for face lattices
of convex polytopes and
they extended it to Eulerian posets;
see
[
3
, Theorem 4]
.
A direct proof for Eulerian posets
was given by Stanley
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
.

We now introduce the coalgebra techniques of the paper
[
7
]
.
However, in order to work in the setting of formal series, we use the
reformulation of
[
6
, Section 8]
,
that is, we introduce a variable
{\mathbf{t}}
to play the role of the tensor sign.
Let
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\rangle\rangle
and
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\rangle
be the two rings of formal series
in the non-commutative variables
{\mathbf{a}}
and
{\mathbf{b}}
,
respectively,
{\mathbf{a}}
,
{\mathbf{b}}
and
{\mathbf{t}}
.
Let
\Delta:{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\rangle\rangle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\rangle
be a derivation such that

\Delta(1)=0
and
\Delta({\mathbf{a}})=\Delta({\mathbf{b}})={\mathbf{t}}
.
Note that

\Delta({\mathbf{c}})=2{\mathbf{t}}
and
\Delta({\mathbf{d}})={\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{t}}+{\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{c}}
.
Thus the derivation restricts to a linear map
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{d}}\rangle\rangle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{d}},{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\rangle
.
Recall that
for an
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-series
u
, where
u
has no constant term,
we have the identity
1/(1-u)=\sum_{k\geq 0}u^{k}
.
Hence we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{1}{1-u}\right)=\frac{1}{1-u}\cdot\Delta(u)\cdot\frac{1}{1-u}.

For a
0,1
-matrix
M
introduce the series
K_{M}(t)
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{M}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\Bin(M^{k+1})\cdot t^{k}.

Let
\Psi
be the matrix of the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-series
\Psi_{i,j}
from
equation (
1.1
), that is,

\displaystyle\Psi=(\Psi_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}.

Theorem 8.1 in
[
6
]
gives a method
to verify the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-series for a level poset:

Theorem 3.1

(Ehrenborg–Hetyei–Readdy)
.

The
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-series matrix
\Psi_{M}
of a level poset associated to the
0,1
-matrix
M

is the unique solution to the equation system

\displaystyle\Psi_{M}\vrule_{\>{\mathbf{a}}=t,{\mathbf{b}}=0}

\displaystyle=K_{M}(t),

\displaystyle\Delta(\Psi_{M})

\displaystyle=\Psi_{M}\cdot{\mathbf{t}}\cdot\Psi_{M}.

Note that when
\Psi_{M}
is expressed in terms
{\mathbf{c}}
’s and
{\mathbf{d}}
’s,
we have the substitution

\displaystyle\Psi_{M}\vrule_{\>{\mathbf{a}}=t,{\mathbf{b}}=0}=\Psi_{M}\vrule_{\>{\mathbf{c}}=t,{\mathbf{d}}=0}.

For any non-commutative monomial or word
u=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}

the reverse of
u
is defined to be

u^{*}=u_{k}\cdots u_{2}u_{1}
.
For
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{t}}
-monomials
we extend the reversing map
u\longmapsto u^{*}
to

{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\rangle
by linearity. Note that this map on
{\mathbb{Z}}\langle\langle{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{t}}\rangle\rangle
is an anti-isomorphism, that is,

(u\cdot v)^{*}=v^{*}\cdot u^{*}
. Also observe
{\mathbf{c}}^{*}={\mathbf{c}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}^{*}={\mathbf{d}}

and hence this map also reverses
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-monomials and thus also
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-series.

Recall that the transpose of a matrix is the operation of reflecting
the matrix entries over the main diagonal.
We now introduce the operation of reflecting the matrix over its anti-diagonal.
Furthermore since some of the matrices and vectors we work with contain
non-commutative variables,
we will also include reversing these elements.
More formally, if
M=(m_{i,j})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}
is an
m\times n
matrix
then we define
M^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}
to be the
n\times m
matrix
such that

(M^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}})_{i,j}=m_{m+1-j,n+1-i}^{*}
.
We use the flipped letter A to denote this operation:
the letter A for anti-diagonal and this letter is flipped over the antidiagonal.
Note that under this operation the entry
w
in the upper right-hand corner
remains in the same location under this operation and maps to
w^{*}
.
Similarly, the entry in the lower-left hand corner
also remains in the same location.
We also have the following two relations

(3.3)

\displaystyle(M+N)^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}

\displaystyle=M^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}+N^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}},

\displaystyle(M\cdot N)^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}

\displaystyle=N^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}\cdot M^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}.

Note that the last relation would not hold if we did not
include the anti-isomorphism
w\longmapsto w^{*}
in the definition.
Finally, note that for a vector
\vec{v}
the two vectors

\vec{v}^{T}
and
\vec{v}^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}
are reverses of each other.

4.
The first class of level Eulerian posets

We now present the first class of level Eulerian posets
and compute its
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-series.
Let
r
be a nonnegative integer.
To construct the
(2r+2)\times(2r+2)
-matrix
M

we define three
(r+1)\times(r+1)
-matrices:

(A)

Let
A=(a_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq r}

where

a_{i,j}=1
if
i=0

and
a_{i,j}=0
otherwise.

(B)

Let
B=(b_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq r}

where

b_{i,j}=1
if

0\leq i-j\leq 1

and
b_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
Note that
B^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}=B
.

(F)

Let
F=(f_{i,j})_{0\leq i,j\leq r}

where

f_{i,j}=1
if
i=0
or
j=r
,
and
f_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
Again,
F^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}=F
.

We begin with a few relations among these matrices.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following seven relations hold among the four matrices
A
,
B
,
F
and
J
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle A^{2}

\displaystyle=A,

\displaystyle{A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}},

(4.2)

\displaystyle B\cdot J

\displaystyle=2\cdot J-A,

\displaystyle J\cdot B

\displaystyle=2\cdot J-A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}},

\displaystyle J\cdot A

\displaystyle=A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}\cdot J=J,

(4.3)

\displaystyle A\cdot B+B\cdot A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}

\displaystyle=2\cdot F.

Proof.

The two matrices
A
and
A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}
are projections
yielding (
4.1
).
The three relations in (
4.2
)
follows since multiplication with the matrix
J
is straightforward.
Note that
A\cdot B
is the matrix
where the top row is
(2,2,\ldots,2,1)

and the remaining rows are zero.
Similarly,

B\cdot A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}
is the matrix where the last column is

(1,2,2,\ldots,2)^{T}

and the remaining columns are zero.
Adding these two last observations yields
relation (
4.3
).
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For
r\geq 1
the matrix
M
is primitive with exponent
3
and

M
is the adjacency matrix of a level Eulerian poset.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
and
that
J
and
F
are
0,1
-matrices,
the square of the matrix
M
is given by

\displaystyle M^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}A^{2}+BJ&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&AB+BA^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}\\
\hline\cr JA+A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}J&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&JB+{A^{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{90.0}{\small$\forall$}}}^{2}\end{array}\right)=2\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}J&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&F\\
\hline\cr J&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&J\end{array}\right)=2\cdot\Bin(M^{2}).

This identity settles the Eulerian condition of rank

.
It is straightforward to observe that
\Bin(M^{3})=J

and hence
M
is primitive with exponent

.

Note that all of the row and column sums of

is

.

.
Finally, the rank

and the observation

\displaystyle\left(J-\Bin(M^{2})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c}0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&J-F\\
\hline\cr 0&\hskip-5.0pt\vline&0\end{array}\right)^{2}=0.\qed

We now will determine the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index for all of the intervals in the level Eulerian poset associated
with the matrix
M
.
Using that the exponent of the matrix
M
is
3
,
we have that the power series
K_{M}(t)
is given by

Sphericity of the first class

We now state Theorem 6.7 of
[
6
]

that gives an algebraic way to verify that level posets are shellable.
We begin by defining the associated
algebra of walks
.
For a
0,1
-matrix
M=(m_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

the algebra of walks
{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle

is the quotient of the free non-commutative algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}

generated by the set of variables

\{x_{i,j}:m_{i,j}=1\}

quoted out by the ideal generated
by the elements
x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
where
j\neq i^{\prime}
.
That is, a linear basis for this algebra is naturally indexed by
all the walks in the digraph associated to the matrix
M
.
In fact, we introduce the shorthand notation

x_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}
to denoted the product

x_{i_{1},i_{2}}\cdot x_{i_{2},i_{3}}\cdots x_{i_{k-1},i_{k}}
.

Let
I_{<}
be the ideal in
{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle

generated by all the monomials

x_{i,j,k}=x_{i,j}\cdot x_{j,k}

such that
there exists
j^{\prime}<j
such that
m_{i,j^{\prime}}=m_{j^{\prime},k}=1
.
Observe that now we use the natural order on the indices
1,2,\ldots,n
of matrix
M
.
Let
Z_{M}=(z_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be the matrix where

z_{i,j}=x_{i,j}
if
m_{i,j}=1
and
z_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
Our interest is to compute powers of this matrix in the quotient ring

{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle/I_{<}
.

Another way to describe the monomials occurring in the ideal
I_{<}
is that
it consists of all degree
2
monomials that do not
occur in the matrix
Z_{M}^{2}

when calculated in
{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle/I_{<}
.
This fact will be useful in explicit calculations.

Now Theorem 6.7 in
[
6
]
states:

Theorem 6.1

(Ehrenborg–Hetyei–Readdy)
.

If over the ring
{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle/I_{<}
every entry in every power
of the matrix
Z_{M}
is zero or a single monomial
then
<
is a vertex shelling order, implying that
every interval in the level poset is shellable.

Theorem 6.9
in
[
6
]

states that if an Eulerian poset is shellable
then its order complex is homeomorphic to a sphere.
Hence we have the conclusion:

Corollary 6.2

(Ehrenborg–Hetyei–Readdy)
.

If each interval in a level Eulerian poset
is shellable then the order complex of every interval is homeomorphic to a sphere.

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{p})_{i,j}

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the power

The induction basis

Assume now that the result holds for

There are six cases to consider.
Note that in each calculation the two first steps takes place

{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle

{\mathbb{Q}}\langle M\rangle/I_{<}

(i)

r+1\leq i\leq 2r+1,0\leq j\leq r

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{p+1})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\text{ or}\\

(ii)

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{p+1})_{i,2r+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=r}^{2r+1}(Z_{M}^{p})_{i,\ell}\cdot(Z_{M})_{\ell,2r+1}

(iii)

i=0,0\leq j\leq r

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{p+1})_{0,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\text{ or}\\
r+1\leq\ell\leq 2r+1\end{subarray}}(Z_{M}^{p})_{0,\ell}\cdot(Z_{M})_{\ell,j}

\displaystyle=x_{0^{p+1}}\cdot x_{0,j}+\sum_{\ell=r+1}^{2r+1}x_{0^{p-1},\ell-r-1,\ell}\cdot x_{\ell,j}=x_{0^{p+1},j}.

(iv)

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{3})_{i,j}

(v)

\displaystyle(Z_{M}^{p+1})_{0,j}

\displaystyle=(Z_{M}^{p})_{0,j-r-1}\cdot(Z_{M})_{j-r-1,j}+(Z_{M}^{p})_{0,j-r}\cdot(Z_{M})_{j-r,j}

Second subcase,

7.
Concluding remarks

Are there better ways to compute the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series of level Eulerian posets?
Even though the two classes
M
and
N
in this paper are very close, that is,
the Hamming distance between these two matrices is
4
, the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series
verification for the matrix
N
is a lot longer.

Also note that the classes
M
and
N
share many properties, such as
they both have the exponent to be
3
. Also the denominator in their

{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series is the polynomial expression
1-{\mathbf{c}}-r\cdot{\mathbf{d}}
.
Does the exponent
\gamma
yield a bound on the degree of the
denominator of the rational expression of the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series?

Instead of the finding a rational expression for the
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-index series
\Psi
for a
level Eulerian poset, would it be more compact to express the series as a weighted finite
automata? See the book
[
4
, Chapter 1]
for more on
the connection between rational series and weighted finite automata.
Given a
0,1
-matrix
M
indexed by
0\leq i,j\leq n-1
.
Consider the set of states

\{s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1},f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\}
.
We have two types of directed edges

s_{i}\longrightarrow f_{j}
and

f_{i}\longrightarrow f_{j}

and we weight them according to

\displaystyle w(s_{i}\longrightarrow f_{j})

\displaystyle=(K_{M}({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}}))_{i,j},

\displaystyle w(f_{i}\longrightarrow f_{j})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{b}}\cdot(K_{M}({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}}))_{i,j},

where
(K_{M}({\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}}))_{i,j}
is the
(i,j)
entry of
the series
K_{M}
from equation (
3.2
).
Then the
{\mathbf{a}}{\mathbf{b}}
-series
\Psi_{i,j}
is the sum of the total weight
of all the paths from the starting state
s_{i}
to the final state
f_{j}
.
However, even for the first non-trivial example, that is,
when
r=1
in the first class of level Eulerian posets,
it is a non-trivial task to see that the associated weighted automata
generate a
{\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{d}}
-series.

Corollaries
4.4
and
5.5
raises another question:

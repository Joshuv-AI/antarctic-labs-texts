Abstract
. We consider the real eigenvalues of an ( N × N ) (N\times N) real elliptic Ginibre matrix whose entries are correlated through a non-Hermiticity parameter τ N ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \tau_{N}\in[0,1] . In the almost-Hermitian regime where 1 − τ N = Θ ⁡ ( N − 1 ) 1-\tau_{N}=\Theta(N^{-1}) , we obtain the large- N N expansion of the mean and the variance of the number of the real eigenvalues. Furthermore, we derive the limiting empirical distributions of the real eigenvalues, which interpolate the Wigner semicircle law and the uniform distribution, the restriction of the elliptic law on the real axis. Our proofs are based on the skew-orthogonal polynomial representation of the correlation kernel due to Forrester and Nagao.

1.
Introduction

How many eigenvalues of a real random matrix are real? At one extreme, there are real symmetric matrices whose all eigenvalues are real. Without symmetry, Hermiticity fails, and not all eigenvalues are real. Nevertheless, intriguingly, several non-Hermitian random matrices with real entries have real eigenvalues with non-zero probability, contrary to their complex or quaternion counterparts. One of the most fundamental examples of such matrices is a real Ginibre ensemble, a real (
N\times N
) matrix whose entries are i.i.d. Gaussian, for which it was shown in the pioneering work of Edelman, Kostlan, and Shub
[
12
]
that the number of real eigenvalues is of order
\sqrt{N}
.

From the examples of real symmetric and real i.i.d. matrices, it can be heuristically conjectured that as a real random matrix becomes more symmetric, it gets more real eigenvalues. The statement can be made rigorous by considering the following interpolation of the two different random matrices:

(1.1)

X:=\sqrt{\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{2}}\,S+\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau_{N}}{2}}\,A

for a (possibly
N
-dependent) parameter
\tau_{N}
, where
S
and
A
are elements of Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE) and anti-symmetric GOE, respectively. Note that
X
is symmetric if
\tau_{N}=1
and
X
is a Ginibre ensemble if
\tau_{N}=0
, and thus the (non)-Hermiticity of
X
is expressed by
\tau_{N}
. The model is known as a real elliptic Ginibre matrix, which provides a natural bridge between Hermitian and non-Hermitian random matrix theories. The expected number of real eigenvalues
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
of
X
for
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
was obtained by Forrester and Nagao
[
17
]
, which is given by

(1.2)

E_{N,\tau}=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+o(1)).

It in particular shows that
E_{N,\tau}
is an increasing function of
\tau
for any sufficiently large
N
.

The symmetry affects not only the number of real eigenvalues but also its distribution. While the limiting empirical distribution of real eigenvalues of
X
is the uniform distribution on
(-1-\tau,1+\tau)
for
\tau\in[0,1)
, i.e.,

(1.3)

\rho_{\tau}^{s}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{[-1-\tau,1+\tau]}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{2(1+\tau)},

in the limit
\tau\to 1
, the uniform distribution in (
1.3
) does not recover the semicircle distribution, which is obviously the limiting distribution in case
\tau=1
. It suggests that a non-trivial transition happens when
\tau
is close to
1
.

One can expect that such a transition may appear when the number of the real eigenvalues is of order
N
, in which case thus the distribution of real eigenvalues is affected by the semicircle distribution. It is therefore natural to consider the case for which the right side of (
1.2
) is of order
N
, or equivalently
1-\tau_{N}=\Theta(N^{-1})
, which we will call the almost-Hermitian regime. We consider the following questions in this regime:

•

What is the number of real eigenvalues?

•

What is the limiting empirical distribution of the real eigenvalues?

In this paper, we aim to answer these questions.

1.1.
Main contributions

Denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\tau_{N}}
the number of real eigenvalues of
X
in (
1.1
). Our main contributions are as follows: For
N
even and

(1.4)

\tau_{N}=1-\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{N},\qquad(\alpha>0),

we prove

•

asymptotic formulas for the mean and the variance of
\mathcal{N}_{\tau_{N}}
, and

•

a formula for the limiting empirical distribution of real eigenvalues of
X
.

As a corollary, we also prove the convergence of
\mathcal{N}_{\tau_{N}}/N
in probability.

In Theorem
2.1
, we prove that

(1.5)

E_{N,\tau_{N}}=c(\alpha)N+O(1)

with

(1.6)

c(\alpha)=\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha\sqrt{1-s^{2}})\,ds,

where
\operatorname{erf}
is the error function. From the asymptotic behavior of the error function,

(1.7)

\operatorname{erf}(x)\sim\begin{cases}\frac{2x}{\sqrt{\pi}}&\text{as}\quad x\to 0\,,\\
1&\text{as}\quad x\to\infty\,,\end{cases}

we find that
c(\alpha)\sim 1
as
\alpha\to 0
and
c(\alpha)\sim\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}
as
\alpha\to\infty
. The former limit corresponds to the Hermitian case, and the latter matches (
1.2
) since

\frac{1}{N}\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{N}\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{2-(\alpha^{2}/N)}{(\alpha^{2}/N)}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\to\frac{2}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}

as
\alpha\to\infty
. It in particular shows that the regime
1-\tau_{N}=\Theta(N^{-1})
is indeed where the transition for the real eigenvalues happens and our result connects the Hermitian case (
\tau=1
) and the elliptic case (
\tau\in[0,1)
).

For the variance
V_{N,\tau_{N}}
of
\mathcal{N}_{\tau_{N}}
, in our second main result, Theorem
2.3
, we prove that

(1.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{V_{N,\tau_{N}}}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}=r(\alpha)

for some
r(\alpha)
satisfying

(1.9)

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}r(\alpha)=0,\qquad\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}r(\alpha)=2-\sqrt{2}.

Again, our result on the variance connects the Hermitian case and the elliptic case as for a fixed
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
it was obtained in
[
16
,
17
]
that

(1.10)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{V_{N,\tau}}{E_{N,\tau}}=2-\sqrt{2}.

As the mean and the variance are of the same order, it is immediate to obtain the convergence

\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\tau_{N}}}{N}\to c(\alpha)

as
N\to\infty
, in probability.

In Theorem
2.8
, we derive the limiting empirical distribution of the real eigenvalues. Denote by
\rho_{N,\tau_{N}}(x)
the empirical distribution of the real eigenvalues of
X
. Then as
N\to\infty
,

(1.11)

\rho_{N,\tau_{N}}(x)\to\rho_{\alpha}^{w}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{[-2,2]}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})

uniformly on compact subsets of
(-2,2)
. Here
c(\alpha)
is a normalization constant in (
1.6
), which turns
\rho_{\alpha}^{w}
into a probability density function. Applying the asymptotic formula (
1.7
), we find that the density
\rho_{\alpha}^{w}
interpolates the semicircle law

(1.12)

\rho_{sc}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{[-2,2]}(x)\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}

and the uniform distribution
\rho_{1}^{s}
defined in (
1.3
), see Figure
3
.

Our proof for the number of the real eigenvalues is based on a (double) contour integral representation, which follows from the skew-orthogonal polynomial kernel of the associated Pfaffian point process and some basic properties of hypergeometric functions.

For the density of the real eigenvalues, we use a version of the Christoffel–Darboux identity (Lemma
4.2
) for Hermite polynomials with complex variables. We also estimate certain oscillatory integrals, which naturally arise from the Plancherel–Rotach strong asymptotics.

In the elliptic case
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
, the classical Mehler’s formula (also known as the Poisson kernel) for orthogonal polynomials can be applied to derive the large-
N
limit of the correlation kernel. In contrast, the leading mathematical challenge in the proof of the almost-Hermitian case is that it should find seemingly non-trivial analytic expressions of the discrete objects under consideration. For this, we can perform the asymptotic analysis, borrowing some ideas from the theory of special functions.

1.2.
Related works

The Ginibre ensemble was first introduced in
[
22
]
. The limiting empirical spectral distribution of a Ginibre ensemble is the uniform distribution on the unit disc in the complex plane, known as the circular law. For a general (fixed)
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
, the circular law is extended to the elliptic law
[
23
,
36
]
, the uniform distribution on the elliptic disc

(1.13)

K:=\{x+iy:(\tfrac{x}{1+\tau})^{2}+(\tfrac{y}{1-\tau})^{2}\leq 1\},

see Figure
1
.

The formula (
1.2
) was first proved by Edelman, Kostlan, and Shub for a real Ginibre ensemble (
\tau=0
). More precisely, it was shown in
[
12
, Corollary 5.2]
that

(1.14)

E_{N,0}=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[1-\frac{3}{8N}-\frac{3}{128N^{2}}+\frac{27}{1024N^{3}}+\frac{499}{32768N^{4}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{5}})\Big]+\frac{1}{2}.

For a fixed value of
\tau\equiv\tau_{N}\in[0,1)
, Forrester and Nagao
[
17
]
expressed
E_{N,\tau}
in terms of a summation of hypergeometric functions using the theory of skew-orthogonal polynomials previously developed in
[
16
]
for the case
\tau_{N}\equiv 0
from which one can derive (
1.2
). (See also
[
35
,
8
]
for the scaling limits of real Ginibre matrices.)

2.
Main Results

2.1.
Elliptic matrix

In this subsection, we define the elliptic matrix, the primary model we consider in this work.
We consider a sequence
\tau_{N}\in[0,1]
called a
non-Hermiticity
parameter. In the sequel we write
\tau=\lim_{N\to\infty}\tau_{N}
, which is always assumed to be well defined.
Let
\{\xi_{j,N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
(
j=0,1,2
) be sequences of real random variables, which satisfy

(2.1)

\mathbb{E}\xi_{j,N}=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}\xi_{j,N}^{2}=1,\qquad\mathbb{E}\xi_{j,N}^{k}<\infty,\qquad\mathbb{E}\xi_{1,N}\xi_{2,N}=\tau_{N},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We consider a random matrix
X=(x_{jk})_{j,k=1}^{N}
whose entries are given as follows:

•

\{(x_{jk},x_{kj})\}_{j<k}\cup\{x_{jj}\}_{j}
consists of independent random variables;

•

\{x_{jj}\}_{j}
are independent copies of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\xi_{0,N}
;

•

\{(x_{jk},x_{kj})\}_{j<k}
are independent copies of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}(\xi_{1,N},\xi_{2,N})
.

We assume that
\xi_{j,N}
’s are Gaussian, which coincides with
X
in (
1.1
).

2.2.
Main Theorems

Our first main result, Theorem
2.1
, is on the asymptotic expansion of
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
in the almost-Hermitian regime. Recall that the modified Bessel function
I_{\nu}
of the first kind is given by

(2.2)

I_{\nu}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z/2)^{2k+\nu}}{k!\,\Gamma(\nu+k+1)}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\tau_{N}=1-\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{N}
. Then for any positive integer
m\geq 2
,

(2.3)

E_{N,\tau_{N}}=Nc(\alpha)+c_{0}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{c_{l}(\alpha)}{N^{l}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}})

as
N\to\infty
, where

(2.4)

c(\alpha):=e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})+I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})],

(2.5)

c_{0}(\alpha):=-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})+\alpha^{2}I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})],

and for
l\geq 1
,

(2.6)

c_{l}(\alpha):=\sum_{k=0}^{l+1}\sum_{m=l+1-k}^{\infty}q_{m,l+1-k}\,\frac{(-1)^{m-l+k}}{(m-l+k)!}\frac{(2k-3)!!}{(2k)!!}\frac{(2m-1)!!}{m!}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)^{k+m}.

Here
q_{k,s}
is defined by the generating function

(2.7)

\Big(\frac{t\,e^{t}}{e^{t}-1}\Big)^{k+2}\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}q_{k,s}\,t^{s}.

We remark that the definition of
c(\alpha)
in (
2.4
) coincides with that in (
1.6
); see Remark
2.7
.

For a fixed
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
, we have the following full expansion of
E_{N,\tau}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\tau_{N}\equiv\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed.
Then for any positive integer
m
, we have

(2.8)

E_{N,\tau}=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau}{1-\tau}N\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[1+\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{a_{l}(\tau)}{N^{l}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}})\Big]+\frac{1}{2},

as
N\to\infty
, where

(2.9)

a_{l}(\tau):=-\sqrt{1-\tau}\,\frac{(2l-3)!!}{2^{l}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}\,k!}\widehat{p}_{k,l}\,\tau^{k}.

Here
\widehat{p}_{k,l}
is defined by the generating function

(2.10)

\Big(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\frac{2\,e^{t(k+2)}}{e^{t}+1}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\widehat{p}_{k,l}\,t^{l}.

3.
Expected number of real eigenvalues

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
. Our proof is based on the following representation of
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
shown in
[
17
]
: for any
\tau_{N}<1
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{N,\tau_{N}}&=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Gamma(2k+\frac{1}{2})}{(2k)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2};-2k+\tfrac{1}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}),\end{split}

where
{}_{2}F_{1}
is the hypergeometric function defined by the Gauss series

(3.2)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z):=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+s)\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(c+s)s!}z^{s},\quad(|z|<1)

and by analytic continuation elsewhere.

Our strategy for the proof of Theorem
2.1
is summarized as follows:

•

we obtain a contour integral representation of
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
, which holds for any
\tau_{N}<1
(Lemma
3.1
);

•

we treat the integrand in the previous step as a function of variables
N
and
\alpha
, and compute its power series expansion;

•

we derive the large-
N
expansion of
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
by the residue calculus and express its coefficients in terms of the modified Bessel functions.

To analyze the right-hand side of the identity (
3.1
), we begin with the following lemma, which provides a contour integral representation of
E_{N,\tau_{N}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
N>1
and
\tau_{N}\in[0,1)
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{N,\tau_{N}}&=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi}\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{N}(-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}),\end{split}

where
g_{N}:\mathbb{R}_{-}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

(3.4)

g_{N}(x):=\sqrt{\pi}\,\,\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2-\zeta)^{-1}(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-1}}\Big].

Proof.

We first analyze the summand in (
3.1
).
For
|\arg(1-z)|<\pi
and
b,1-c,c-b\not\in\mathbb{N},
the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
has an integral representation

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)&=-\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}\frac{e^{-i\pi c}}{4\sin(\pi b)\sin(\pi(c-b))}\\
&\quad\times\oint_{\mathcal{P}(0,1)}\zeta^{b-1}(1-\zeta)^{c-b-1}(1-\zeta z)^{-a}\,d\zeta,\end{split}

see e.g.,
[
31
, Eq.(15.6.5)]
. Here
\mathcal{P}(0,1)
is a Pochhammer contour entwining
0
and
1
.
It follows from the reflection formula of Gamma function

(3.6)

\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\pi/\sin(\pi z)

that for
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,

\frac{\Gamma(2\kappa+\frac{1}{2})}{(2\kappa)!}\frac{\Gamma(-2\kappa+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(-2\kappa)}\frac{e^{-i\pi/2}}{4\sin(2\kappa\pi)}=\frac{i}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\sec(2\kappa\pi),

which has a removable singularity at each
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
. Combining the equations above, for
x<0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(2k+\frac{1}{2})}{(2k)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2};-2k+\tfrac{1}{2};x)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\oint_{\mathcal{P}(0,1)}\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\zeta)^{-2k-1}(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,d\zeta

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\pi}\,\,\underset{\zeta=1}{\text{Res}}\,\Big[\zeta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\zeta)^{-2k-1}(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

Here the second identity follows from
-2k-1\in\mathbb{Z}
and the standard deformation of the Pochhammer contour.

Taking the sum of the above, we observe that only the second term of the right-hand side below

\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}(1-\zeta)^{-2k-1}=\frac{1-\zeta}{\zeta(\zeta-2)}-\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)^{N-1}\zeta(\zeta-2)},

contributes to the residue.
Thus we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{\Gamma(2k+\frac{1}{2})}{(2k)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2};-2k+\tfrac{1}{2};x)=g_{N}(x).\end{split}

Combining this with the expression (
3.1
), the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

When
\tau_{N}\equiv 0
, it follows from
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;0)=1
and the duplication formula of Gamma function that the expression (
3.1
) is simplified to

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{N,0}=\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{(4k-1)!!}{(4k)!!}&=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(N+\frac{1}{2})}{(N-1)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};N;\tfrac{1}{2}).\end{split}

The formula (
3.8
) also appears in
[
12
, Corollary 5.1, 5.3]
.
For
\tau_{N}\equiv 0
, one can recognize the right-hand side of (
3.3
) in terms of a hypergeometric function using (
3.5
), namely, we have

(3.9)

g_{N}(0)=\frac{\Gamma(N-\frac{1}{2})}{(N-2)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};-N+\tfrac{3}{2};\tfrac{1}{2}).

Note that the regularized hypergeometric function
{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(a,b;c;z):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(c)}{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
satisfies the linear transform

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\sin(\pi(c-b))}{\pi}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,b;c;z)&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}(1-z)^{c-1-b}z^{1-c}\\
&-\frac{1}{\Gamma(c-1)\Gamma(c-b)}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,b;b-c+2;1-z),\end{split}

see
[
31
, Eq.(15.4.6), (15.8.6)]
. Using this, one can observe that Lemma
3.1
for
\tau_{N}\equiv 0
is equivalent to (
3.8
).

It is instructive first to present the proof of Proposition
2.2
, which includes essential ideas for Theorem
2.1
but requires fewer computations.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Using the elementary binomial expansion

\Big(\frac{1-\zeta x}{1-x}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}\Big((1-\zeta)\frac{x}{x-1}\Big)^{k},\qquad\Big(\Big|(1-\zeta)\frac{x}{x-1}\Big|<1\Big),

we write
g_{N}
in (
3.4
) as

(3.11)

g_{N}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{1-x}}\,\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2-\zeta)^{-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-k-1}}\Big]\Big(\frac{x}{x-1}\Big)^{k}.

Moreover, by (
3.5
) and (
3.10
), the residue in the right-hand side of this identity is computed as

\displaystyle\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2-\zeta)^{-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-k-1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-N+k)}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};N-k;\tfrac{1}{2}).

Combining above equations with the identity

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!}\,\Big(\frac{x}{x-1}\Big)^{k}=\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{1-2x}},\qquad(x<0),

we obtain that for
x<0
,

\displaystyle g_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{1-x}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-N+k)}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};N-k;\tfrac{1}{2})\Big(\frac{x}{x-1}\Big)^{k}.

Now let us recall that as
\lambda\to\infty
(
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
)

(3.12)

{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(a,b;c+\lambda;z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(c-b+\lambda)}\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}q_{s}(z)\frac{\Gamma(b+s)}{\Gamma(b)}\,\lambda^{-s-b}+O(\lambda^{-m-b}),

where
m
is any positive integer, see
[
31
, Eq.(15.12.3)]
. Here
q_{0}(z)=1
and
q_{s}(z)
when
s=1,2,\ldots
are defined by the generating function

(3.13)

\Big(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}\Big)^{b-1}e^{t(1-c)}(1-z+ze^{-t})^{-a}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}q_{s}(z)\,t^{s}.

Using (
3.12
), we have

{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};N-k;\tfrac{1}{2})=\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\Gamma(N-k+\frac{1}{2})}\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\widehat{p}_{k,s}\frac{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2})}N^{-s}+O(N^{-m})\Big),

where
\widehat{p}_{k,s}
is defined by (
2.10
).
Then by (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}-N+k)}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,-\tfrac{1}{2};N-k;\tfrac{1}{2})=-\frac{\sqrt{N}}{\pi}\,\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\widehat{p}_{k,s}\,\frac{(2s-3)!!}{2^{s}N^{s}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}})\Big),

which leads to

\displaystyle g_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{N}{1-x}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}\widehat{p}_{k,s}\Big(\frac{x}{x-1}\Big)^{k}\,\Big(\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\frac{(2s-3)!!}{2^{s}N^{s}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}})\Big).

Now Proposition
2.2
follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Now we begin to prove Theorem
2.1
. For each
k=0,1,\ldots,
and
N>1
, let

(3.14)

a_{N,k}(\zeta):=\frac{2}{N^{k+1}}\,\frac{\zeta^{-k-2}(2-\zeta)^{-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-k-1}}.

We will need the following evaluation.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
N
even and
k=0,1,\ldots,N-2
, we have

(3.15)

\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\,a_{N,k}(\zeta)=(-1)^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{q_{k,s}}{(k+1-s)!}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{N^{s}},

where
q_{k,s}
is defined by (
2.7
).

Proof.

To compute the residue of
a_{N,k}(\zeta)
at
\zeta=1
, we first expand
\zeta^{-k-2}
around
\zeta=1
as follows:
for
|\zeta-1|<1
,

\zeta^{-k-2}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{(k+l+1)!}{(k+1)!\,l!}(\zeta-1)^{l}.

Combining this expansion with the definition (
3.2
) of
{}_{2}F_{1}
, we obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\,a_{N,k}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\frac{2}{N^{k+1}}\sum_{l=0}^{N-k-2}(-1)^{l}\frac{(k+l+1)!}{\,l!}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\Big[(-1)^{k}\frac{(k+1)!}{(2N)^{k+1}}+\frac{2}{N^{k}}\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-k-1)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,N+1;N-k;-1)\Big]\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\Big[(-1)^{k}\frac{(k+1)!}{(2N)^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{N^{k}}\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-k-1)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,-k-1;N-k;\tfrac{1}{2})\Big].\end{split}

Here the last identity follows from Pfaff’s transformation

(3.17)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=(1-z)^{-a}{}_{2}F_{1}(a,c-b;c;\tfrac{z}{z-1}).

Moreover, it follows from (
3.12
) and

\frac{\Gamma(-k-1+s)}{\Gamma(-k-1)}=(-1)^{s}\frac{(k+1)!}{(k+1-s)!}

(where we treat the case
k\in\mathbb{N}
in the left-hand side as a removable singularity) that

\frac{1}{N^{k}}\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-k-1)!}{}_{2}F_{1}(1,-k-1;N-k;\tfrac{1}{2})=\sum_{s=0}^{k+1}q_{k,s}\frac{(k+1)!}{(k+1-s)!}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{N^{s}}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\,a_{N,k}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2N)^{k+1}}+(-1)^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k+1}\frac{q_{k,s}}{(k+1-s)!}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{N^{s}}.

Finally, using the definition (
2.7
), we compute
q_{k,k+1}:

q_{k,k+1}=\underset{z=0}{\textup{Res}}\Big[\Big(\frac{e^{z}}{e^{z}-1}\Big)^{k+2}\frac{2}{1+e^{z}}\Big]=2^{-k-1}.

We finish the proof.
∎

Let us write

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d_{s}(\alpha)&:=\frac{(-1)^{s}}{2}\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}q_{k,s}\,\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1-s)!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)^{k}.\end{split}

Then we have the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

(3.19)

\displaystyle d_{0}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})+I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})],

(3.20)

\displaystyle d_{1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{1}{8}e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}[(\alpha^{2}-2)I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})-\alpha^{2}I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})].

Proof.

Using (
2.7
), we first compute

(3.21)

q_{k,0}=1,\qquad q_{k,1}=\frac{k+1}{2}.

By (
2.2
), we have

(3.22)

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}I_{0}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(k!)^{2}}(-1)^{k}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)^{k},

(3.23)

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}I_{1}(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(k-1)!(k+1)!}(-1)^{k+1}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)^{k}.

Then the lemma follows immediately from (
3.22
) and (
3.23
).
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.24)

E_{N,\tau_{N}}=N\,\sqrt{4-\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}}\,\frac{1}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,g_{N}(1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}})

and

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{g_{N}(x)}{\sqrt{N}}&=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{N}}\,\,\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-\frac{3}{2}}(2-\zeta)^{-1}(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-1}}\Big].\end{split}

Let us compute the power series expansion of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}g_{N}(1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}}).

For this computation, we first expand
(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
around
\zeta=1
using the binomial theorem. More precisely, for
x=1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}}
, we have

(3.26)

(1-\zeta x)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\zeta-1)^{k}(\zeta N)^{-k-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2k+1},\quad(|\zeta-1|<|\zeta|\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}).

Therefore we obtain

(3.27)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,g_{N}(1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}})=\sqrt{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\underset{\zeta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\,a_{N,k}(\zeta)\frac{(2k)!}{(k!)^{2}}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2k+1},

where
a_{N,k}
is given by (
3.14
).

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,g_{N}(1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\Big)^{k}\sum_{s=0}^{k}\frac{q_{k,s}}{(k+1-s)!}\frac{(-1)^{s}}{N^{s}}.

Rearranging the terms, for any positive integer
m\geq 2
,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,g_{N}(1-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\frac{d_{s}(\alpha)}{N^{s}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}}),

where
d_{s}(\alpha)
is given by (
3.18
). Now it follows from (
3.24
) and the binomial expansion

\sqrt{4-\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}}=2-\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}\frac{(2s-3)!!\,\alpha^{2s}}{2^{2s-1}s!}\,\frac{1}{N^{s}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}})

that

(3.29)

E_{N,\tau_{N}}=Nc(\alpha)+c_{0}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{c_{l}(\alpha)}{N^{l}}+O(\frac{1}{N^{m}}).

Here
c(\alpha)=2d_{0}(\alpha)
and
c_{l}(\alpha)
’s are given by

(3.30)

c_{0}(\alpha):=2d_{1}(\alpha)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}d_{0}(\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}

and for
l\geq 1
,

c_{l}(\alpha)=2d_{l+1}(\alpha)-\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{l}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(k+1)!}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big)^{2k+1}d_{l-k}(\alpha).

Now Lemma
3.4
completes the proof.

∎

4.
Distributions of real eigenvalues

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
2.8
.

It was obtained in
[
17
]
that the density
\rho_{N}\equiv\rho_{N,\tau_{N}}
of real eigenvalues can be expressed in terms of the Hermite polynomial

H_{k}(x):=(-1)^{k}e^{x^{2}}\tfrac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}e^{-x^{2}}
as

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{N}(x)&=\rho_{N}^{1}(x)+\rho_{N}^{2}(x),\qquad\rho_{N}^{j}=\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\textbf{R}_{N}^{j}(x),\qquad(j=1,2)\end{split}

where

(4.2)

\displaystyle\textbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}x^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)^{2},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textbf{R}_{N}^{2}(x)&:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}x^{2}}H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)\\
&\quad\times\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)\,du.\end{split}

Here we use a different normalization so that the limiting empirical distribution has a compact support, cf. (
1.13
).

The overall strategy to derive the large-
N
limit (
2.16
) of
\rho_{N}
is as follows:

•

we first compute the large-
N
limit of
\rho_{N}(0)
(Lemma
4.1
) by virtue of some basic properties of hypergeometric functions;

•

using a version of the Christoffel-Darboux formula (Lemma
4.2
) we obtain an integral representation of
\rho_{N}^{1}
(Lemma
4.3
), where the previous step is utilized to determine the integration constant;

•

we derive the large-
N
limit of the integral representation using the Plancherel-Rotach asymptotic formula for Hermite polynomials (
4.19
) and some asymptotic analysis on the associated oscillatory integrals (Lemma
4.4
).

We begin with the following lemma, which gives Theorem
2.8
for the specific value
x=0.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\rho_{N}(0)=\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha).

Proof.

Using Hermite numbers

H_{k}(0)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{1-k}{2})}\,2^{k}

and (
3.2
), we have

\rho_{N}(0)=\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[(1-\tau_{N}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\tau_{N}^{N}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tau_{N}^{2})\Big].

By (
3.17
), we have

{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{N+1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tau_{N}^{2})=(1-\tau_{N}^{2})^{-1}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tfrac{\tau_{N}^{2}}{\tau_{N}^{2}-1}).

This leads to

\rho_{N}(0)=a_{N}-b_{N},

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle a_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi(1-\tau_{N}^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle b_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\tau_{N}^{N}}{1-\tau_{N}^{2}}\,\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tfrac{\tau_{N}^{2}}{\tau_{N}^{2}-1}).

Note here that by Theorem
2.1
, we have

(4.7)

\lim_{N\to\infty}a_{N}=\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}.

Therefore it suffices to show that

(4.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}b_{N}=\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erfc}(\alpha).

Notice also that Theorem
2.1
gives rise to

b_{N}\sim\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})\frac{e^{-\alpha^{2}}}{\alpha}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;\tfrac{\tau_{N}^{2}}{\tau_{N}^{2}-1}).

Thus we need to show

(4.9)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2}){}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;-\tfrac{N}{2\alpha^{2}})=\alpha\,e^{\alpha^{2}}\operatorname{erfc}(\alpha).

It follows from
[
31
, (15.4.6), (15.8.2)]
that

\displaystyle\quad\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2}){}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;-\tfrac{N}{2\alpha^{2}})

\displaystyle=\alpha\Big(1+\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}\Big)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\alpha^{2}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1-\tfrac{N}{2},1;\tfrac{3}{2};-\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}).

Then by Stirling’s formula, we have

(4.10)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{N+1}{2}){}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1,\tfrac{1}{2};\tfrac{N}{2}+1;-\tfrac{N}{2\alpha^{2}})=\alpha\,e^{\alpha^{2}}-\alpha^{2}\lim_{N\to\infty}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1-\tfrac{N}{2},1;\tfrac{3}{2};-\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}).

Using the Euler integral formula (see e.g.
[
31
, Eq.(15.6.1)]
): for
c>b>0
,

{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(a,b,c,z)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(b)\Gamma(c-b)}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}}{(1-zt)^{a}}\,dt,

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}{}_{2}\textup{{F}}_{1}(1-\tfrac{N}{2},1;\tfrac{3}{2};-\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}}{N})

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\Big(1+\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{N}t\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}t}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\,dt=\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}}}{\alpha}\operatorname{erf}(\alpha).

This completes the proof.
∎

We shall use the following version of the Christoffel–Darboux identity for Hermite polynomials, see
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
28
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\tau\in(0,1]
, let

(4.11)

\displaystyle F_{N}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(x)^{2}.

Then for any
N\geq 2
, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{N}(x)=\frac{4\tau\,x}{1+\tau}F_{N}(x)-\frac{4(\tau/2)^{N-1}}{1+\tau}\frac{H_{N-2}(x)H_{N-1}(x)}{(N-2)!}.

For the reader’s convenience, let us present the proof of Lemma
4.2
here.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

For
N=2
, it follows from
H_{0}(x)=1,

H_{1}(x)=2x
that the identity (
4.12
) trivially holds.
By the differentiation rule

H_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)=2(N-1)H_{N-2}(x)

and the three-term recurrence relation

H_{N}(x)=2xH_{N-1}(x)-H_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)

of the Hermite polynomials, we have

\tau xH_{N-1}(x)+(N-1)H_{N-2}(x)-\frac{1+\tau}{2}H_{N-1}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\tau}{2}H_{N}(x).

Using this, the induction argument gives

\displaystyle F_{N+1}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=F_{N}^{\prime}(x)+\frac{(\tau/2)^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}\Big(H_{N-1}(x)^{2}\Big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\tau\,x}{1+\tau}F_{N+1}(x)-\frac{4(\tau/2)^{N}}{1+\tau}\frac{H_{N-1}(x)H_{N}(x)}{(N-1)!},

which completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
4.2
, we obtain an integral representation of
\rho_{N}^{1}
, a key ingredient for the latter asymptotic analysis.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\tau_{N}\in(0,1]
, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{N}^{1}(x)=\rho_{N}^{1}(0)&-\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}\\
&\quad\times\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)\,du.\end{split}

Proof.

By (
4.2
), the function
\textbf{R}_{N}^{1}
is expressed in terms of the function
F_{N}
in (
4.11
) as

\textbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}x^{2}}F_{N}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x).

Differentiating the above and using Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\textbf{R}_{N}^{1})^{\prime}(x)=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}x^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x).

Integrating the above, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textbf{R}_{N}^{1}(x)=\textbf{R}_{N}^{1}(0)&-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}\\
&\quad\times\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)\,du,\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

We will need the following lemma on the boundedness of certain oscillatory integrals.

Lemma 4.4
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.15)

\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}u^{2}/8}}{(4-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\cos\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{4}u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}-N\arccos(\tfrac{u}{2})-\tfrac{3}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{u}{2})\Big\}\,du=O(\frac{1}{N})

and

(4.16)

\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}u^{2}/4}}{\sqrt{4-u^{2}}}\sin\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{2}u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}-2N\arccos(\tfrac{u}{2})-2\arcsin(\tfrac{u}{2})\Big\}\,du=O(\frac{1}{N})

uniformly on compact subsets of
(-2,2).

Proof.

We present the proof of the first assertion (
4.15
) as the other one (
4.16
) follows from similar computations.
Let us write

\displaystyle\phi(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}}{4}-\arccos(\tfrac{u}{2}),

\displaystyle a(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}u^{2}/8}}{(4-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\exp\Big\{i(\tfrac{\alpha^{2}}{4}u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}-\tfrac{3}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{u}{2}))\Big\}.

Then the desired asymptotic (
4.15
) is equivalent to

(4.17)

\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{x}a(u)e^{iN\phi(u)}\,du=O(\frac{1}{N}),

where the
O(\frac{1}{N})
term is uniform on compact subsets of
(-2,2).

Since

\phi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\sqrt{4-u^{2}}}{2},

the function
\phi(u)
has critical points only at
u=\pm 2.
Thus we have that for
x\in(0,2),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}a(u)e^{iN\phi(u)}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{iN}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{a(u)}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}\,\frac{d}{du}e^{iN\phi(u)}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{iN}\Big(\frac{a(u)}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}e^{iN\phi(u)}\Big|_{u=0}^{u=x}-\int_{0}^{x}\frac{d}{du}\Big(\frac{a(u)}{\phi^{\prime}(u)}\Big)\,e^{iN\phi(u)}\,du\Big)

since
\phi
has no critical point on
[0,x]
.
Therefore we obtain

(4.18)

\Big|\int_{0}^{x}a(u)e^{iN\phi(u)}\,du\Big|\leq\frac{1}{N}\Big(\Big\|\frac{a}{\phi^{\prime}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}[0,x]}+\Big\|\Big(\frac{a}{\phi^{\prime}}\Big)^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{1}[0,x]}\Big),

which leads to (
4.17
).
One can treat the other case
x\in(-2,0)
in the same way.
∎

Let us recall the Plancherel-Rotach asymptotic formula (see e.g.,
[
10
, Chapter 7]
or
[
11
, Theorem 5]
): for
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
,

(4.19)

H_{N}(\sqrt{2N}\sin\theta)\sim\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\cos\theta}}(2N)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\sin^{2}\theta)N}\cos\Big\{N[-\tfrac{\pi}{2}+\theta+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2\theta]+\tfrac{\theta}{2}\Big\}.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.8
.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

We prove the theorem by using the following steps:

•

for any
x\in(-2,2),

(4.20)

\rho_{N}^{2}(x)\to 0\quad\text{as }N\to\infty;

•

for any
x\in(-2,2),

(4.21)

\displaystyle\rho_{N}^{1}(x)\to\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{2\alpha\sqrt{\pi}}\operatorname{erf}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Combining (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), as
N\to\infty,

\rho_{N}(x)\to\rho_{\alpha}^{w}(x),\quad\text{for }|x|<2.

Since both
\rho_{N}
and
\rho_{\alpha}^{w}
are probability density functions, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\rho_{N}(x)=0,\quad\text{for }|x|\geq 2,

which proves Theorem
2.8
.
Therefore it suffices to show (
4.20
) and (
4.21
).

Let us first show (
4.20
).
By Theorem
2.1
, we have

(4.22)

\frac{1}{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{N}{(N-2)!}\sim\frac{1}{c(\alpha)}\frac{2^{-N+1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{e^{-\alpha^{2}}}{(N-2)!}.

Then by (
4.3
),

(4.23)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{N}^{2}(x)&\sim\frac{e^{-\alpha^{2}}}{c(\alpha)}\frac{2^{-N}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}x^{2}}}{(N-2)!}H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)\,du.\end{split}

We now use (
4.19
) to obtain

(4.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)&\sim-\frac{2}{(4-x^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}(2N)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}x^{2}}\,e^{-\tfrac{N}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha^{2}x^{2}}{8}}\\
&\times\sin\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{4}x\sqrt{4-x^{2}}-N\arccos(\tfrac{x}{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{x}{2})\Big\}\end{split}

and

(4.25)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)&\sim-\frac{2}{(4-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}(2N)^{\frac{N-2}{2}}e^{\frac{N}{2(1+\tau_{N})}u^{2}}\,e^{-\tfrac{N}{2}+\tfrac{\alpha^{2}u^{2}}{8}}\\
&\times\cos\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{4}u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}-N\arccos(\tfrac{u}{2})-\tfrac{3}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{u}{2})\Big\}.\end{split}

Combining (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) with Stirling’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{N}^{2}(x)&\sim\frac{e^{-\alpha^{2}}}{c(\alpha)}\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}x^{2}/8}}{(4-x^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\sin\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{4}x\sqrt{4-x^{2}}-N\arccos(\tfrac{x}{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{x}{2})\Big\}\\
&\times\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{\alpha^{2}u^{2}/8}}{(4-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\cos\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{4}u\sqrt{4-u^{2}}-N\arccos(\tfrac{u}{2})-\tfrac{3}{2}\arcsin(\tfrac{u}{2})\Big\}\,du.\end{split}

Now the claim (
4.20
) follows from the first assertion (
4.15
) of Lemma
4.4
.

Next, we show (
4.21
). For
x\in(-2,2)
, let us write

(4.26)

h_{N}(x):=\frac{2^{-N}}{(N-2)!}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}u^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}u)\,du

and

(4.27)

h(x):=\frac{1}{4\alpha}\,e^{\alpha^{2}}\,\Big(\operatorname{erfc}(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{4-x^{2}})-\operatorname{erfc}(\alpha)\Big).

Combining Lemma
4.3
with (
4.22
), the claim (
4.21
) is equivalent to the convergence

(4.28)

\displaystyle h_{N}(x)\to h(x)\quad\text{as }N\to\infty.

To show this, we first investigate the asymptotic behavior of
{h_{N}^{\prime}(x)}/{h^{\prime}(x)}
as
N\to\infty
.
By differentiation,

(4.29)

h_{N}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2^{-N}}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau_{N}}x^{2}}H_{N-2}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)H_{N-1}(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{2\tau_{N}}}x)

and

(4.30)

h^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{x}{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}e^{\alpha^{2}x^{2}/4}.

Again we use (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) to derive

(4.31)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{N}^{\prime}(x)}{h^{\prime}(x)}\sim 1+\frac{2}{x}\sin\Big\{\tfrac{N+\alpha^{2}}{2}x\sqrt{4-x^{2}}-2N\arccos(\tfrac{x}{2})-2\arcsin(\tfrac{x}{2})\Big\}.

Then (
4.28
) follows from the second assertion (
4.16
) of Lemma
4.4
.
We finish the proof.
∎

5.
Variance of the number of real eigenvalues

In this section, we prove Theorem
2.3
.
Let us define

(5.1)

S_{N}(x,y):=S_{N}^{1}(x,y)+S_{N}^{2}(x,y),

where

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N}^{1}(x,y)&:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2(1+\tau_{N})}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{k}(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}}),\end{split}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{N}^{2}(x,y)&:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{N-\frac{3}{2}}}{1+\tau_{N}}\frac{1}{(N-2)!}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+\tau_{N})}}H_{N-1}(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})\\
&\quad\times\int_{0}^{x}e^{-\frac{u^{2}}{2(1+\tau_{N})}}H_{N-2}(\tfrac{u}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})\,du.\end{split}

The function
S_{N}
is used to form
2\times 2
matrix-valued kernel of the associated Pfaffian point process, see
[
17
]
.

By definition, we have

(5.4)

S_{N}(x,x)=\frac{E_{N,\tau_{N}}}{\sqrt{N}}\rho_{N}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{N}}),\qquad E_{N,\tau_{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{N}(x,x)\,dx.

The variance
V_{N,\tau_{N}}
is expressed in terms of one- and two-point correlation functions of the real eigenvalues, which in turn

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{N,\tau_{N}}=2E_{N,\tau_{N}}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy,\end{split}

see
[
16
]
.

By Theorem
2.1
, we have
E_{N,\tau_{N}}\sim Nc(\alpha).
Observe also that the functions
r(\alpha)
and
c(\alpha)
are related as

r(\alpha)=2-2\,\frac{c(\sqrt{2}\alpha)}{c(\alpha)}.

Then by (
5.5
), it suffices to show that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy=c(\sqrt{2}\alpha).\end{split}

Notice that the integral in (
5.3
) is the same as the one in (
4.3
).
Thus we can use (
4.24
), (
4.25
), and Lemma
4.4
to obtain that for any
x,y\in(-2\sqrt{N},2\sqrt{N})
,

S_{N}^{2}(x,y)=o(\sqrt{N}),\qquad S_{N}^{2}(y,x)=o(\sqrt{N}).

Therefore the term
S_{N}^{2}
does not contribute to the leading order asymptotic of (
5.6
), namely

(5.7)

\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}(x,y)S_{N}(y,x)\,dx\,dy\sim\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}^{1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy.

By (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), all we need to show is the convergence

(5.8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}^{1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!(k+1)!}\alpha^{2k}.

The overall strategy of the proof of (
5.8
) is similar to that in Section
3
albeit the computations are fairly more complicated. For the reader’s convenience, let us summarize the steps of the proof as follows:

•

we first obtain an expression of
\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}^{1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy
which is given in terms of a
double summation
of hypergeometric functions (Lemma
5.1
);

•

we decompose the double summation in the previous step into finite number of single summations (see (
5.15
), (
5.16
)) and obtain a
double contour
integral representation of each of the single summation (Lemma
5.3
);

•

we compute the power series expansion of each of the single summation by the residue calculus (Lemma
5.4
);

•

using a combinatorial identity (Lemma
5.5
) we compile all the contributions from each of the single summation in the large-
N
limit.

See also Remark
5.2
below for a motivation of this approach using the residue calculus.

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N}^{1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy=\textup{I}_{N}(\alpha)+\textup{II}_{N}(\alpha),

where

\displaystyle\textup{I}_{N}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{N}\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}\sum_{l,m=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{{F}}_{1}(l-m+\tfrac{1}{2},m-l+\tfrac{1}{2};-l-m+\tfrac{1}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{2}}{(2l)!(2m)!},

\displaystyle\textup{II}_{N}(\alpha)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{N}\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}\sum_{l,m=1}^{N/2-1}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{{F}}_{1}(l-m+\tfrac{1}{2},m-l+\tfrac{1}{2};-l-m+\tfrac{3}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{2}}{(2l-1)!(2m-1)!}.

Proof.

By (
5.2
), we have

S_{N,1}(x,y)^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{1+\tau_{N}}}\sum_{j,k=0}^{N-2}\frac{(\tau_{N}/2)^{j+k}}{j!k!}H_{j}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{k}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{j}(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{k}(\tfrac{y}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}}).

Observe here that for
j+k
odd, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{1+\tau_{N}}}H_{j}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{k}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})\,dx=0.

For
j+k
even, we use the formula (7.374-5) in
[
25
]
to derive

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{1+\tau_{N}}}H_{j}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})H_{k}(\tfrac{x}{\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}})\,dx=\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{2\tau_{N}}{1+\tau_{N}}u^{2}}H_{j}(u)H_{k}(u)\,du\\
&=\sqrt{2\tau_{N}}\,2^{\frac{j+k-1}{2}}(\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1+\tau_{N}})^{-\frac{j+k+1}{2}}(\tfrac{1-\tau_{N}}{1+\tau_{N}})^{\frac{j+k}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{j+k+1}{2}){}_{2}F_{1}(-j,-k;\tfrac{1-j-k}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})\\
&=(\tfrac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{2})^{-\frac{j+k}{2}}\Gamma(\tfrac{j+k+1}{2}){}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{j-k+1}{2},\tfrac{k-j+1}{2};\tfrac{1-j-k}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}).\end{split}

Here the last identity follows from Euler’s transformation (see e.g.,
[
31
, Eq.(15.8.1)]
)

(5.10)

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}{}_{2}F_{1}(c-a,c-b;c;\tfrac{z}{z-1}).

Then by (
3.6
), we obtain

(5.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N,1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{1+\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:even\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(\tfrac{j-k+1}{2},\tfrac{k-j+1}{2};\tfrac{1-j-k}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{2}}{j!k!},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let us pause here to briefly explain the reason we derive the asymptotics of (
5.11
) using the residue calculus rather than direct computations. For this, note that

(5.12)

\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}S_{N,1}(x,y)^{2}\,dx\,dy\sim\frac{\pi}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:even\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(\tfrac{j-k+1}{2},\tfrac{k-j+1}{2};\tfrac{1-j-k}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{2}}{j!k!}.

As a function of
\alpha
, the right-hand side of the equation (
5.12
) is indeed an even polynomial of degree
4N-8.
More precisely, we have

(5.13)

\frac{\pi}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{j,k=0}^{N-2}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(\tfrac{j-k+1}{2},\tfrac{k-j+1}{2};\tfrac{1-j-k}{2};-\tfrac{\tau_{N}}{1-\tau_{N}})^{2}}{j!k!}=\sum_{l=0}^{2N-4}b_{N,l}\,\alpha^{2l},

where

(5.14)

b_{N,l}=\frac{(-1)^{l}}{\pi N^{l+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j+k:even\\
0\leq j,k\leq N-2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+m=l\\
0\leq n\leq j\\
0\leq m\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{k-j+1}{2}+n)\Gamma(\tfrac{j-k+1}{2}+m)}{\Gamma(k-j+1+n)\Gamma(j-k+1+m)}\binom{j}{n}\binom{k}{m}.

The identity (
5.13
) follows from well-known properties of hypergeometric function which include Pfaff’s transform (
3.17
), a linear transform
[
31
, Eq.(15.8.7)]
and an evaluation in terms of polynomials
[
31
, Eq.(15.2.4)]
as well as basic properties of Gamma function such as the reflection formula (
3.6
). We leave this verification to interested readers.
Using (
5.13
), one may prove Theorem
2.3
by analyzing the large-
N
limit of (
5.14
). Nevertheless, it requires some non-trivial combinatorial identities to evaluate the multiple summations. On the other hand, the residue calculus we will perform plays a role in simplifying the multiple summations in (
5.14
), which makes the evaluation easier. The meaning of this will become clear below.

We shall now focus on the asymptotic analysis for
\textup{I}_{N}
as the other case follows along the same line.
By (
1.4
), we have

\textup{I}_{N}(\alpha)\sim\frac{\pi}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{l,m=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(l-m+\tfrac{1}{2},m-l+\tfrac{1}{2};-l-m+\tfrac{1}{2};-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}}+1)^{2}}{(2l)!(2m)!}.

For each non-negative integer
n
, let us write

(5.15)

\textup{I}_{N,n}(\alpha):=\frac{\pi}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{l=n}^{N/2-1}\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(n+\tfrac{1}{2},-n+\tfrac{1}{2};-2l+n+\tfrac{1}{2};-\tfrac{N}{\alpha^{2}}+1)^{2}}{(2l)!(2l-2n)!}.

Then by letting
l=m-n
, one can see that
\textup{I}_{N}
can be asymptotically decomposed as follows:

(5.16)

\textup{I}_{N}(\alpha)\sim\textup{I}_{N,0}(\alpha)+2\sum_{n=1}^{N/2-1}\textup{I}_{N,n}(\alpha).

In the sequel, we shall use the shorthand notation

\underset{\zeta,\eta=1}{\text{Res}}[f(\zeta,\eta)]:=\underset{\eta=1}{\text{Res}}[\underset{\zeta=1}{\text{Res}}[f(\zeta,\eta)]].

Then we obtain the power series expansion of
\textup{I}_{N,n}
in terms of a double contour integral.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

(5.17)

\textup{I}_{N,n}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{N,k}^{n}\,\alpha^{2k},

where

(5.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{N,k}^{n}&=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{N^{k+1}\,2^{k-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{(2n+2m-1)!!\,(-1)^{k-m}}{m!\,(k-m)!\,(2n-2k+2m-1)!!}\\
&\times\underset{\zeta,\eta=1}{\textup{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-m-2n-1}\,\eta^{-k+m-1+2n}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-1-m-2n}(\eta-1)^{N-1-k+m}}\frac{1}{1-(\zeta-1)^{2}(\eta-1)^{2}}\Big].\end{split}

Proof.

We first express each summand in (
5.15
) in terms of the residue of an elementary function.
By the integral representation (
3.5
) of the hypergeometric function, for
x<0
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(l-m+\tfrac{1}{2},m-l+\tfrac{1}{2};-l-m+\tfrac{1}{2};x)}{(2m)!}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{\Gamma(m-l+\tfrac{1}{2})}\,\underset{\zeta=1}{\text{Res}}\,\Big[\zeta^{m-l-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\zeta)^{-2m-1}(1-\zeta x)^{m-l-\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

This identity together with (
3.6
) leads to

\displaystyle\quad\frac{{}_{2}\textbf{F}_{1}(n+\tfrac{1}{2},-n+\tfrac{1}{2};-2l+n+\tfrac{1}{2};x)^{2}}{(2l)!(2l-2n)!}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(-1)^{n}}{\pi}\,\underset{\zeta,\eta=1}{\text{Res}}\,\Big[\zeta^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\,(1-\zeta)^{-2l+2n-1}(1-\eta)^{-2l-1}\,(1-\zeta x)^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\eta x)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

We now simplify the sum of
(\zeta-1)^{-2l+2n-1}(\eta-1)^{-2l-1}
and expand the factor
(1-\zeta x)^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\eta x)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}
around
\alpha=0
(
x=1-\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}
).
As a rational function of
\zeta
and
\eta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=n}^{N/2-1}(\zeta-1)^{-2l+2n-1}(\eta-1)^{-2l-1}

simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta-1)^{2n-N+1}(\eta-1)^{-N+1}-(\zeta-1)(\eta-1)^{1-2n}}{1-(\zeta-1)^{2}(\eta-1)^{2}}.

Also for
x=1-\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}
, the binomial expansions of
(1-\zeta x)^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}
and
(1-\eta x)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}
give that

\displaystyle\quad(1-\zeta x)^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}(1-\eta x)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2k+2}}{N^{k+1}\,2^{k}}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{(\zeta-1)^{m}}{\zeta^{m+\frac{1}{2}+n}}\frac{(\eta-1)^{k-m}}{\eta^{k-m+\frac{1}{2}-n}}\frac{(2n+2m-1)!!\,(-1)^{k-m}}{m!\,(k-m)!\,(2n-2k+2m-1)!!}

for
|\zeta-1|<|\zeta|\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}
and
|\eta-1|<|\eta|\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}
.
Combining all of the above equations with (
5.15
), the proof is complete.
∎

Next, we compute the residue in (
5.18
).

Lemma 5.4
.

We have

(5.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{N,k}^{n}&=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{(2n+2m-1)!!\,(2k-2m-1-2n)!!}{m!\,(k-m)!\,(m+2n)!\,(k-m-2n)!}\\
&\times\frac{1}{N^{k+1}}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{(N-2-2l)!(N-2-2l-2n)!}{(N-2-2l-m-2n)!(N-2-2l-k+m)!},\end{split}

where
N^{\prime}:=\lfloor\frac{N-m}{2}\rfloor-n-1.

Proof.

Using the expansions

\displaystyle\zeta^{-m-2n-1}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{(m+2n+l)!}{(m+2n)!\,l!}(\zeta-1)^{l},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-(\zeta-1)^{2}(\eta-1)^{2}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\zeta-1)^{2l}(\eta-1)^{2l}

around
\zeta=1
and
\eta=1
,
we have

\displaystyle\quad\underset{\zeta=1}{\text{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-m-2n-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-1-m-2n}}\frac{1}{1-(\zeta-1)^{2}(\eta-1)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(m+2n)!}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{(N-2-2l)!}{(N-2-m-2n-2l)!}(\eta-1)^{2l}.

Therefore the residue in (
5.18
) is computed as

\displaystyle\quad\underset{\zeta,\eta=1}{\text{Res}}\,\Big[\frac{\zeta^{-m-2n-1}\,\eta^{-k+m-1+2n}}{(\zeta-1)^{N-1-m-2n}(\eta-1)^{N-1-k+m}}\frac{1}{1-(\zeta-1)^{2}(\eta-1)^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(m+2n)!}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{(N-2-2l)!}{(N-2-m-2n-2l)!}\,\underset{\eta=1}{\text{Res}}\Big[\frac{\eta^{-k+m-1+2n}}{(\eta-1)^{N-1-k+m-2l}}\Big].

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(m+2n)!(k-m-2n)!}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{(N-2-2l)!(N-2-2l-2n)!}{(N-2-2l-m-2n)!(N-2-2l-k+m)!},

which completes the proof.
∎

To combine each of the residues in (
5.16
), we will need the following combinatorial identity.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any non-negative integer
k
, we have

(5.20)

\binom{2k}{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}\sum_{n=-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\frac{(2n+2m-1)!!\,(2k-2m-2n-1)!!}{\,(m+2n)!\,(k-m-2n)!}.

Proof.

Note that the inner summation of (
5.20
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\frac{(2m+2n)!\,(2k-2m-2n)!}{(m+n)!\,(k-m-n)!\,(m+2n)!\,(k-m-2n)!},

where the summation
\sum^{\prime}_{n}
is taken over all
n
such that
m+2n\geq 0
and
k-m-2n\geq 0
.
Then (
5.20
) is equivalent to

2^{k}\binom{2k}{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\binom{k}{m+n}\binom{2m+2n}{m}\binom{2k-2m-2n}{k-m}.

This identity easily follows from the elementary combinatorics using the double-counting method.
To be more precise, the left-hand side corresponds to the number of cases where
k
out of
2k
balls are chosen, and each is painted black or red (say).
On the other hand, the right-hand side can be interpreted as the total number of cases in which
m+n
pairs are first selected from the
k
pairs of
2
balls each, and
m
balls are selected out of the selected
m+n
pairs of balls and painted black, while
k-m
balls are selected out of the rest
k-m-n
pairs with
2k-2m-2n
balls and painted red. This completes the proof.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Recall that we aim to prove (
5.8
).
By Lemma
5.1
, it is enough to show that

(5.21)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\textup{I}_{N}(\alpha)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\textup{II}_{N}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\alpha^{2k},\qquad a_{k}=(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!(k+1)!}.

We shall present the proof of (
5.21
) for
\textup{I}_{N}.
The other one for
\textup{II}_{N}
is left to the reader as an exercise.

Due to Lemma
5.3
and the decomposition (
5.16
), the convergence (
5.21
) is equivalent to

(5.22)

a_{k}=a_{k}^{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{n},\qquad a_{k}^{n}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}a_{N,k}^{n}.

To show (
5.22
), we first claim that

(5.23)

a_{k}^{n}=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}}\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{m=0}^{k}\frac{(2n+2m-1)!!\,(2k-2m-1-2n)!!}{m!\,(k-m)!\,(m+2n)!\,(k-m-2n)!}.

This follows from Lemma
5.4
and Riemann sum approximation. More precisely, as
N\to\infty
,
the last factor in (
5.19
) has the following asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{N^{k+1}}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{(N-2l-2)!(N-2l-2n-2)!}{(N-2l-m-2n-2)!(N-2l-k+m-2)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=0}^{N^{\prime}}\Big(1-\frac{2l}{N}+O\big(\frac{1}{N}\big)\Big)^{k}\sim\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{k}\,dt=\frac{1}{2(k+1)},

which leads to (
5.23
).

Now observe that by (
5.19
), we have

a_{N,k}^{n}=a_{k}^{n}=0\quad\text{if}\quad n>\lfloor k/2\rfloor.

Then it follows from (
5.23
) and Lemma
5.5
that

\displaystyle a_{k}^{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{n}

\displaystyle=a_{k}^{0}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}a_{k}^{n}=(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{k!(k+1)!}.

This completes the proof.
∎

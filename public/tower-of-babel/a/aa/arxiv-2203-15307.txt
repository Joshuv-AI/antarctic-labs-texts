Higher order moments for SPDE with monotone nonlinearities Thanks: This work is based on the second author’s master’s thesis in applied mathematics, prepared under the supervision of the first and third author at Delft University of Technology.
Manuel V. Gnann, Jochem Hoogendijk, Mark C. Veraar

Abstract
. This paper introduces a new p p -dependent coercivity condition through which L p L^{p} -moments for solutions can be obtained for a large class of SPDEs in the variational framework. If p = 2 p=2 , our condition reduces to the classically coercivity condition, which only yields second moments for the solution. The abstract result is shown to be optimal. Moreover, the results are applied to obtain L p L^{p} -moments of solutions for several classical SPDEs such as stochastic heat equations with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, Burgers’ equation and the Navier-Stokes equations in two spatial dimensions. Furthermore, we can recover recent results for systems of SPDEs and higher order SPDEs using our unifying coercivity condition.

Primary: 60H15, Secondary: 35R50, 47H05, 47J35

1.
Introduction

In this paper we introduce a new coercivity condition through which one can obtain estimates for higher order moments for stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) of the form

(1.1)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=A(t,u(t))\mathrm{d}t+B(t,u(t))\mathrm{d}W(t),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}.

Here
W
is a
U
-cylindrical Brownian motion. We will be concerned with the so-called
variational
or
monotone operator approach
to SPDEs in Hilbert spaces. In particular, we assume that
(V,H,V^{*})
is a Gelfand triple, where
H
is a separable Hilbert space and
V
a reflexive Banach space.

The variational approach for SPDEs was introduced in 1972 by Bensoussan and Temam using time discretizations methods
[
6
]
. Pardoux improved the latter via Lions’ approach for PDEs in
[
20
]
. In this approach, Galerkin approximations are used together with a priori energy estimates to obtain existence and uniqueness. Since then, both Krylov and Rozovskii
[
15
]
and Liu and Röckner
[
16
,
17
]
have extended this approach even further by allowing monotone and locally monotone operators, respectively, as the driving part of the equation.

An advantage of the variational approach is that it directly applies to nonlinear equations. Another key property is that it typically gives global existence and uniqueness at once, and there is often no need to check any further blow-up criteria for the solution. When combined with other approaches this can be very effective (see e.g.
[
4
]
for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations).

Each of the above papers assumes a coercivity condition on
(A,B)
of the form (see Section
2
for explanation on the notation):

(1.2)

2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\|B(t,v)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\leq-\theta\|v\|_{V}^{2}+K\|v\|_{H}^{2}+f(t).

Note that
B(t,\cdot)
is allowed to be defined on the smallest space
V
. In the above mentioned results for the variational approach to (
1.1
) one obtains estimates for

(1.3)

{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|^{p}_{H}\ \ \text{and}\ \ {\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;V)}^{p},

but only for
p=2
. Estimates for
p>2
are not available unless
B
is assumed to be defined on
H
instead of
V
(see
[
17
, Section 5]
).
An attempt to treat more general
p\geq 2
(and even
p<2
) was made in
[
9
]
by Brzeźniak and the third author. Here it also turned out that the classical coercivity condition is not strong enough to obtain finite
L^{p}
-moments. The paper
[
9
]
only considers a simplified setting. Therefore, it was enlightening to see that in
[
19
]
by Neelima and Šiška, some results can be proved in a general monotone setting. However, the
L^{p}
-bounds proved there are only sub-optimal (see Remark
2.6
for details), and the coercivity condition they used seems too restrictive in some cases, which becomes clear further below and in the presented applications.

In the current paper we obtain a complete generalization of the classical monotone operator framework leading to estimates for (
1.3
) for
p>2
. From
[
9
]
it follows that the terms in (
1.3
) are infinite for
p>2
. Therefore, a restriction is necessary. The key ingredient turns out to be the following
p
-dependent coercivity condition:

(1.4)

\displaystyle 2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}+(p-2)

\displaystyle\frac{\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{U}^{2}}{\|v\|_{H}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\theta\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}+K_{c}\|v\|_{H}^{2}+f(t).

Our main result (Theorem
2.4
) states that under (
1.4
) and the usual conditions in the monotone operator framework, one can estimate the norms in (
1.3
). Note that (
1.4
) reduces to (
1.2
) if
p=2
. In Example
2.5
we use a specific choice suggested in
[
9
]
to show that (
1.4
) is optimal. The proof of the main result is elementary, but quite tedious. In some cases we give explicit constants in the obtained estimates for the moments.

An interesting special case occurs if
B(t,v)^{*}v=0
, since then the
p
-dependent term in (
1.4
) vanishes and we get estimates for all
p\geq 2
. This typically occurs for differential operators of odd order with suitable boundary conditions. In some cases we can even let
p\to\infty
to obtain uniform estimates in
\Omega
.

In Section
4
we consider applications to the stochastic heat equation with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, Burgers’ equation, the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations in dimension two, systems, higher order equations, and the
p
-Laplace equation.

2.
Setting and main result

Before we state our main result we fix our notation and terminology. For further details on Gelfand triples and stochastic integration theory we refer to
[
17
]
.

Throughout this paper
(U,(\cdot,\cdot)_{U})
and
(H,(\cdot,\cdot)_{H})
denote real separable Hilbert spaces and
(V,\|\cdot\|_{V})
is a reflexive Banach space embedded continuously and densely in
H
. The dual of
V
(relative to
H
) is denoted by
V^{*}
and the duality pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},{\mathbb{P}})
and filtration
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
will be fixed. The progressive
\sigma
-algebra is denoted by
\mathcal{P}
. Furthermore, suppose that
(W(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a
U
-cylindrical Brownian motion with respect to
({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

2.1.
Assumptions

The main assumptions on the nonlinearities are as follows:

Assumptions 2.1
.

Let

A:[0,T]\times\Omega\times V\to V^{*},\ \ \text{and}\ \ B:[0,T]\times\Omega\times V\to{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)

both be
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(V)
-measurable. Suppose that there exist finite constants

\alpha>1,\ \beta\geq 0,\ p\geq\beta+2,\ \theta>0,\ K_{c},K_{A},K_{B},K_{\alpha}\geq 0

and
f\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T]))
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
a.s.

(H1)

(Hemicontinuity) For all
u,v,w\in V
,
\omega\in\Omega
, the following map is continuous:

\lambda\mapsto\langle A(t,u+\lambda v,\omega),w\rangle.

(H2)

(Local weak monotonicity) For all
u,v\in V
,

\displaystyle 2\langle A(t,u)-A(t,v),u-v\rangle+\|B(t,u)-B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq K(1+\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha})(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\beta})\|u-v\|_{H}^{2}.

(H3)

(Coercivity) For all
v\in V
,
v\neq 0
,

2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}+(p-2)\frac{\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{U}^{2}}{\|v\|_{H}^{2}}\leq-\theta\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}+f(t)+K_{c}\|v\|_{H}^{2}.

(H4)

(Boundedness 1) For all
v\in V
,

\|A(t,v)\|_{V^{*}}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\leq K_{A}(f(t)+\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha})(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\beta}).

(H5)

(Boundedness 2) For all
v\in V
,

\|B(t,v)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\leq f(t)+K_{B}\|v\|_{H}^{2}+K_{\alpha}\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}.

Most conditions are standard and appear in previous works that treat the variational approach to SPDEs (see
[
20
,
14
,
17
]
).
Usually, in these works
\beta=0
. The case
\beta\geq 0
is considered in
[
8
]
where Lévy noise is treated as well. The condition
p\geq\beta+2
is needed for a priori bounds for involving the
L^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(\Omega\times[0,T])
-norm of
\|A(t,u(t))\|
for
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];H))\cap L^{\frac{p\alpha}{2}}(\Omega;L^{\alpha}([0,T];V))
needed in the existence proof. Often it can be avoided by a localization argument.
Our hypothesis (
H
3
) is new and will allow us to obtain estimates for
L^{p}
-moments. It reduces to the classical coercivity assumption if
p=2
. The function
f
can be used to include inhomogeneous terms in
A
and
B
.

After these preparations we can define solutions to (
1.1
).

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
hold and let
u(0):\Omega\to H
be
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
-measurable. An adapted, continuous
H
-valued process
u
is called a solution to (
1.1
) if
u\in L^{\alpha}(0,T;V)
a.s. and
for every
t\in[0,T]
, a.s.,

u(t)=u(0)+\int_{0}^{t}A(s,u(s))\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}B(s,u(s))\mathrm{d}W(s).

Note that due to (
H
4
),
t\mapsto A(t,u(t))\in L^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(0,T;V^{*})
a.s. and thus the above Bochner integral is well-defined. Due to (
H
5
),
t\mapsto B(t,u(t))\in L^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H))
a.s. and thus the stochastic integral is also well-defined.

The following can be checked by elementary arguments involving Young’s inequality and inequalities for convex functions:

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\phi\in L^{\frac{p\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}}(\Omega;L^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(0,T;V^{*}))
and
\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)))

If
(A,B)
satisfies Assumptions
2.1
, then
(A+\phi,B+\psi)
satisfies Assumptions
2.1
with the same
\alpha,\beta
,
p
, and
f
replaced by

\tilde{f}=f+\|\phi\|^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}_{V^{*}}+\|\psi\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}.

2.2.
Main result

The main result of this paper is the following well-posedness result with higher order moments:

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
hold and let
u(0)\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}_{0};H)
. Then (
1.1
) has a unique solution
u
, and there exists a constant
C
depending on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\theta
,
p
,
K_{c}
,
K_{A}
,
K_{B}
,
K_{\alpha}
such that

(2.1)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq Ce^{CT}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big].\end{split}

The proof is given in Section
3
. The main novelty is the a priori estimate (
2.1
). The existence and uniqueness can be obtained by standard Galerkin approximation techniques.
In Corollary
3.4
in case
K_{B}=K_{c}=0
, the
p
-dependence in the estimate (
2.1
) will be made explicit.

The following example is taken from
[
9
]
and implies optimality of Theorem
2.4
with respect to
p
in the sense that if
p
is replaced by some number
q>p
, then it can happen that
{\mathbb{E}}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{q}=\infty
.

Example 2.5

(Optimality)
.

On the torus
\mathbb{T}
consider the equation

(2.2)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\Delta u(t)\mathrm{d}t+2\gamma(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\mathrm{d}W(t),\qquad u(0)=u_{0}.

Here
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
and
W
is a real-valued Wiener process (thus
U={\mathbb{R}}
). In
[
9
]
it is proved that (
2.2
) has a unique solution in
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T})))
if
2\gamma^{2}(p-1)<1
. Indeed, setting
V=H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
,
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,
A=\Delta
, and
B=2\gamma(-\Delta)^{1/2}
, Assumptions
2.1
(
H
1
), (
H
2
), (
H
4
), (
H
5
) hold with
\alpha=2
,
\beta=0
and
f=0
and suitable constants
K
,
K_{A}
and
K_{B}
. To check (
H
3
) note that

\displaystyle 2\langle\Delta v,v\rangle+\|B(v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+(p-2)\frac{|B(v)^{*}v|^{2}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle\Delta v,v\rangle+(p-1)\|B(v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+4\gamma^{2}(p-1)\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq-\theta\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+2\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2},

where
\theta:=2-4\gamma^{2}(p-1)>0
. This proves (
H
3
) and thus the well-posedness follows from Theorem
2.4
.
On the other hand, it follows from
[
9
, Theorem 4.1(ii)]
that there exists an initial datum
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
such that if
q>p
and
\gamma>0
is such that
2\gamma^{2}(p-1)<1
and
2\gamma^{2}(q-1)>1
, then there is a
t>0
such that
{\mathbb{E}}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{q}=\infty
. Moreover, even
{\mathbb{E}}\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{q}=\infty
for all
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remark 2.6
.

In
[
19
]
the following coercivity condition was proposed:

(2.3)

\displaystyle 2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+(p-1)\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}\leq-\theta\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha}+f(t)+K_{c}\|v\|_{H}^{2},\ \ v\in V.

The latter is more restrictive than (
H
3
), since
\frac{\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{U}^{2}}{\|v\|_{H}^{2}}\leq\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}
. Replacing our condition (
H
3
) by (
2.3
), the main result in
[
19
]
states that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\|u(t)\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|^{rp}

\displaystyle\leq C_{r}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big],

where
r\in(0,1)
.
Both estimates are sub-optimal. The result (
2.1
) shows that the supremum can actually be inside the expectation and thus one can take
r=1
. In
[
19
]
the growth condition (
H
5
) on
B
is not explicitly assumed, but as far as we can see (
H
5
) is used in their estimate (13).

Similar results were obtained in
[
8
]
, under a different coercivity condition. A detailed comparison with (
2.3
) can be found in
[
19
, Remark 6.1]
.

3.
Proof of the main result

In
[
17
, Theorem 4.2.5, p. 91]
the following version of Itô’s formula is obtained for
p=2
. The
p>2
version can be obtained from the
p=2
version combined with
the real case by considering
(\|X_{t}\|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{p/2}
and letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
or by applying
[
22
, Theorem 3.2, p. 73]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Itô’s formula for
\|\cdot\|_{H}^{p}
)
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
,
X_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{F}_{0};H)
and
Y\in L^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathbb{P};V^{*})
,
Z\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathbb{P};{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H))
both progressively measurable. If
X\in L^{\alpha}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathbb{P};V)
and for a.e.
t\in[0,T]

{\mathbb{E}}(\|X_{t}\|_{H}^{2})<\infty
, and a.s.

X_{t}=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}Z_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s},\quad t\in[0,T]

is satisfied in
V^{*}
, then X is a continuous
H
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted process and the following holds a.s.:

\displaystyle\|X_{t}\|_{H}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|X_{0}\|_{H}^{p}+p\int_{0}^{t}\|X_{s}\|_{H}^{p-2}Z_{s}^{*}X_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p(p-2)}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|X_{s}\|_{H}^{p-4}\|Z_{s}^{*}X_{s}\|_{U}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|X_{s}\|_{H}^{p-2}\left(2\langle Y_{s},X_{s}\rangle+\|Z_{s}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in[0,T],

where
\|X_{s}\|_{H}^{p-4}
is defined as zero if
X_{s}=0
.

The main step in the proof of Theorem
2.4
is the following new a priori estimate, where we note that the condition
p\geq\beta+2
in Assumptions
2.1
is not needed.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
u
is a solution of equation (
1.1
) with initial condition
u(0)\in L^{p}(\Omega;H)
and
(
H
3
)
,
(
H
4
)
and
(
H
5
)
from Assumptions
2.1
hold with
f\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T]))
. Then, there exists a constant
C
depending on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\theta
,
p
,
K_{c}
,
K_{A}
,
K_{B}
,
K_{\alpha}
such that

(3.1)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}&\leq Ce^{CT}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big].\end{split}

Proof.

Step 0: Stopping time argument
.

For
n\geq 1
consider the following sequence of stopping times:

\tau_{n}=\inf\{t\in[0,T]:\|u(t)\|_{H}\geq n\}\wedge\inf\{t\in[0,T]:\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}ds\geq n\},

where we set
\inf\varnothing=T
. Then
\tau_{n}\to T
a.s. as
n\to\infty
by Definition
2.2
. Since
u
solves (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
, Lemma
3.1
implies the following:

\displaystyle\|u(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}=\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+p\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)

\displaystyle+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\Big(2\langle A(s,u(s)),u(s)\rangle+\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)\frac{\|B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\|_{U}^{2}}{\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}}\Big)\mathrm{d}s.

Using the coercivity assumption (
H
3
), the latter implies

(3.2)

\begin{split}\|u(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}&+\frac{\theta p}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+p\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\\
&\phantom{\leq}+\frac{p}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\left(f(s)+K_{c}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s.\\
\end{split}

Taking expectations in (
3.2
), the stochastic integral cancels and we find

(3.3)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\|u(t\wedge\tau_{n})&\|_{H}^{p}+\frac{\theta p}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+\frac{p}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+\frac{p}{2}K_{c}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) will be used several times to derive new estimates which ultimately lead to (
3.1
).

Step 1: Estimating the supremum term

{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}
.

Taking suprema and expectations in (
3.2
), we obtain the following estimate

(3.4)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+p{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\\
&\quad+\frac{p}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}f(s)\mathrm{d}s+\frac{pK_{c}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\int_{0}^{r\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}2\sqrt{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2p-2}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}2\sqrt{2}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(iii)}}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{1}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(iv)}}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{1}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}(f(s)+K_{B}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}+K_{\alpha}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}s,

where in (i) we have applied the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality with constant

(see

), in (ii) Hölder’s inequality, in (iii) Young’s inequality and in (iv) hypothesis (

\displaystyle(1-p\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{1}){\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+p\Big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Big){\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}f(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+p\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}K_{B}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{K_{c}}{2}\big){\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}\mathrm{d}s+\tfrac{pK_{\alpha}\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s.

Using estimate (

\displaystyle(1-p\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{1}){\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}

\displaystyle\quad+p\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}K_{B}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{K_{c}}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{2}K_{\alpha}K_{c}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big){\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}\mathrm{d}s.

It remains to absorb the integrals of

To this end, let
\varepsilon_{2}>0

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}f(s)\mathrm{d}s&\leq{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{r\in[0,t]}\|u(r\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\\

Setting

&+2\varepsilon_{2}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{2}+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big){\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&+p\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}K_{B}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{K_{c}}{2}+\tfrac{\sqrt{2}K_{\alpha}K_{c}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big){\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p}\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

First choosing

\varepsilon_{2}

(only depending on

Applying Gronwall’s inequality to

By Fatou’s lemma this leads to

{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}\leq Ce^{CT}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)

Step 2: Estimating the

-norm

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

In order to estimate this quantity, by Lemma

By the coercivity condition (

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big((p-2)\frac{\|B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\|_{U}^{2}}{\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}}+\theta\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\Big)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|u(0)\|_{H}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(f(s)+K_{c}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)

Selecting just the term
\theta\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}
and evaluating at

gives

(3.11)

\theta\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\leq\|u(0)\|_{H}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(f(s)+K_{c}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)

Applying the function

to both sides of (

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\frac{\theta^{\frac{p}{2}}}{a_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&\qquad+K_{c}^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\right|^{\frac{p}{2}},\end{split}

where

.
The

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}&\leq T^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}\\
&\leq CT^{\frac{p}{2}}e^{CT}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big).\end{split}

Thus it remains to estimate the

(3.14)

\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Big|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}&B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}C_{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}{{\bf 1}}_{[0,\tau_{n}]}(s)\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{4}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}C_{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{4}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}C_{p}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(iii)}}}{{\leq}}C_{p}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+C_{p}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}},\end{split}

where in (i) we have applied the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, (ii) follows from Hölder’s inequality, and (iii) is a consequence of Young’s inequality. Applying (
H
5
), the

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\big(f(s)+K_{B}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}+K_{\alpha}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\big)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq b_{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+b_{p}K_{B}^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+b_{p}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq b_{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+b_{p}K_{B}^{\frac{p}{2}}T^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+b_{p}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where

Recombining this estimate with (

) and (

), we obtain:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{t}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\Big(C_{p}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}+b_{p}K_{B}^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{p}T^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big){\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+b_{p}C_{p}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+b_{p}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{p}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}(1+T^{\frac{p}{2}})e^{CT}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]+b_{p}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{p}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

Using this and (

\displaystyle\theta^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(1+T^{\frac{p}{2}})e^{CT}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+a_{p}b_{p}2^{\frac{p-2}{2}}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{p}\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Therefore, choosing

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C^{\prime\prime}e^{C^{\prime\prime}T}\Big({\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big).

Since we have estimated all three terms in the above steps, this finishes the proof.

Remark 3.3
.

One can also prove an estimate for the integral of
\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}
. Indeed, by Hölder’s and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{H}^{p-2}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{p-2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+\tfrac{2}{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where the last line is bounded by the left-hand side of (
3.1
).

If
K_{B}=K_{c}=0
in Assumptions
2.1
(
H
3
) and (
H
5
), it is possible to improve the dependency on
p
in estimate (
3.1
). Here the condition
p\geq\beta+2
is not needed.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose
u
is a solution of equation (
1.1
) with initial condition
u(0)\in L^{p}(\Omega;H)
and
(
H
3
)
,
(
H
4
)
,
(
H
5
)
from Assumptions
2.1
hold with
K_{B}=K_{c}=0
and
f\in L^{\frac{p}{2}}(\Omega;L^{1}([0,T]))
. Then there exists a constant
C
only depending on
\alpha,\beta,\theta,K_{A},K_{\alpha}
such that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];H))}+

\displaystyle p^{-1/2}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];V))}

\displaystyle\leq C\big[\|u(0)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}+\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{1}(0,T))}\big].

Moreover, if
B(v)^{*}v=0
for all
v\in V
, then
the above estimates hold for all
p\in[2,\infty]
, and
p^{-1/2}
can be omitted.

The main point is that
C
does not depend on
p
and
T
. In particular, we can let
T\to\infty
in (
3.15
) if
f
is integrable over
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Proof.

Estimate (
3.8
) gives for every
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
:

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\big(1-\phi(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\big){\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,S]}\|u(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}\\
&\qquad\leq\big(1+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big){\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+2\varepsilon_{2}^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{2}+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big){\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}},\\
\end{split}

where
\phi(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=p\sqrt{2}\varepsilon_{1}+(p-2)\varepsilon_{2}\big(\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{2}+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}\theta}\big)
. Choosing

\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}p},\qquad\varepsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{2(p-2)(8p+1+K_{\alpha}\frac{8p}{\theta})}

gives
\phi(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=\frac{1}{4}
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{2}}\leq 16p^{2}(1+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{1}{\theta})+p^{2}+1\leq Ap^{2},

where
A
is a constant depending on
K_{\alpha}
and
\theta
. Therefore, we get:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,S]}\|u(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|_{H}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+K_{\alpha}\tfrac{8p}{\theta}){\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+(Ap^{2}+1)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

Taking
1/p
-th powers, the supremum of (
3.15
) follows since for every
\gamma>0
,

\sup_{p\in[2,\infty)}p^{\gamma/p}=\sup_{p\in[2,\infty)}\big(1+(p-1)\big)^{\gamma/p}\leq\sup_{p\in[2,\infty)}e^{\gamma(p-1)/p}=e^{\gamma}.

Under the additional assumption
B(v)^{*}v=0
, it follows that condition (
H
3
) holds for all
p\in[2,\infty)
. Therefore, we can let
p\to\infty
in (
3.15
).

In order to derive the estimate (
3.15
) for the
V
-term, we use (
3.12
) and the assumption
K_{c}=0
to find that

(3.17)

\begin{split}\frac{\theta^{\frac{p}{2}}}{a_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}&\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&+2^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}},\end{split}

where
a_{p}=2^{p-2}
. If
B(v)^{*}v=0
for all
v\in V
, then the stochastic integral vanishes and thus (
3.17
) already implies the required result.

It remains to prove estimate (
3.15
) for the
V
-norm in the case the stochastic integral in (
3.17
) does not vanish. For this we use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality with
\gamma_{p}=\frac{(2p)^{p/4}}{2}
as in (
3.14
) (see
[
10
, Theorem A]
), giving for all
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big|\int_{0}^{T}B(s,u(s))^{*}u(s)\mathrm{d}W(s)\Big|^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\varepsilon}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+\gamma_{p}\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary. Using assumption (
H
5
), we additionally obtain

\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|B(s,u(s))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\\
&+\leq 2^{\frac{p-2}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+2^{\frac{p-2}{2}}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.\end{split}

Recombining all terms with inequality (
3.17
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{\frac{p}{2}}}{a_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+(1+\gamma_{p}\varepsilon 2^{p-1}){\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\gamma_{p}}{\varepsilon}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}+2^{p-1}K_{\alpha}^{\frac{p}{2}}\gamma_{p}\varepsilon{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Therefore, setting

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2a_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{H}^{p}+(1+\gamma_{p}\varepsilon 2^{p-1}){\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}f(s)\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{2^{\frac{p}{2}}\gamma_{p}}{\varepsilon}{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H}^{p}.

Taking
p
-th powers and observing that the leading term is

, we arrive at the desired inequality.
∎

p>2
seem new. In all cases, the approach to prove them via our Theorem
2.4
also seems new. The variety of the examples will hopefully be enough to explain the reader how to apply our framework to concrete SPDEs.

4.1.
Stochastic heat equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions

We consider a stochastic heat equation with additive noise and Dirichlet boundary conditions.

(4.1)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\Big(\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u(t))+\phi(t)\Big)\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}u(t)+\psi_{k,t}\Big)\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t).

Here the
W_{k}(t)
are real-valued Wiener processes. In what follows, we use:

Assumptions 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a an open set. Let

(V,H,V^{*})=(H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D}),L^{2}(\mathcal{D}),H^{-1}(\mathcal{D}))

and
U=\ell^{2}
.
Suppose that
a^{ij}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D})
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
(b_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T];W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2}))
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Furthermore, we assume that the coefficients are progressively measurable. Define

(4.2)

\sigma^{ij}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{i}b_{k}^{j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{N}

and suppose that the uniform ellipticity condition on
a^{ij}
and
b_{k}^{i}
:

(4.3)

\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(2a^{ij}-\sigma^{ij}\right)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq\theta|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

holds true where
\theta>0
. Furthermore, assume
\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})))
,

\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})))
, and
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))
, where
p\geq 2
.

Equation (
4.1
) can be reformulated as a stochastic evolution equation of the form

\mathrm{d}u(t)=A(t,u(t))\ \mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}(t,u(t))\ \mathrm{d}W_{k}(t),

with the deterministic linear operator
A(t):H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{D})\to H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})
defined by

\langle A(t,u),v\rangle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}a^{ij}(\partial_{i}u\,\partial_{j}v\,\mathrm{d}x+\langle\phi(t),v\rangle\qquad\text{for }u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D}),

and stochastic operators
B_{k}(t):H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
given by

B_{k}(t,v)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}v+\psi_{k,t}\qquad\text{for }v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D}).

It turns out that the
p
-dependent term in the coercivity condition (
H
3
) vanishes. Therefore, the solution admits moment estimates of all orders
p\geq 2
, only limited by the integrability of the additive noise and the initial condition:

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
4.1
are satisfied. Then, a unique variational solution
u
of equation (
4.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
exists and the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\bigg({\mathbb{E}}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg)

where
C
depends on
\theta,p,a^{ij}
and
b_{k}^{i}
for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Assuming that
\mathcal{D}
is bounded and all
b_{k}
are not space dependent, we can use Corollary
3.4
to obtain
p
-independent constants, and even take
p=\infty
. That is, there exists a constant
C
such that for all
p\in[2,\infty]

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(\mathcal{D})))}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{D})))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[\|u(0)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))}+\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})))}+\|\psi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})))}\Big]

where
C
only depends on
\theta,a^{ij},b_{k}^{i}
for all
i,j,k,\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.4
.

A version of Proposition
4.2
holds if we only assume
(b_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D};\ell^{2}))
. However, in this case we can only use
\frac{\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{U}^{2}}{\|v\|_{H}^{2}}\leq\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(U,H)}
which leads to the
p
-dependent coercivity condition

\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(2a^{ij}-(p-1)\sigma^{ij}\right)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq\theta|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

By Remark
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
, it suffices to show Assumptions
2.1
, (
H
1
)-(
H
5
), for
(A,B)
with
\alpha=2
,
\phi=0
,
\psi=0
, and
f=0
.
Hemicontuinty (
H
1
) is immediate from the definition of
A
. For local weak monotonicity (
H
2
), observe that it suffices to prove the inequality for
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
u=0
by linearity.
Using uniform ellipticity (
4.3
), it follows:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+&\|B(t,v)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{2}(\ell^{2},L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))}\\
&=-\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{D}}2a^{ij}\partial_{i}v\,\partial_{j}v\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}b_{k}^{j}\partial_{i}v\,\partial_{j}v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\ =\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(-2a^{ij}+\sigma^{ij})\,\partial_{i}v\,\partial_{j}v\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq-\theta\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}+\theta\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2},\end{split}

that is, (
H
2
) is satisfied with
K=\theta
(if
\mathcal{D}
is bounded one can take
K=0
by Poincaré’s inequality). For coercivity (
H
3
), observe that the first two terms in (
H
3
) form the first line of (
4.4
). Therefore, it remains to derive an expression for
\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}/\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}
, where
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
. Integration by parts gives

(B(t,v)^{*}v)_{k}=\int_{\mathcal{D}}b^{i}_{k}\partial_{i}vv\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\partial_{i}b^{i}_{k}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Using the spatial regularity of
b_{k}^{i}
, we obtain:

\begin{split}\Big\|k\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(b^{i}_{k}\partial_{i}v)v\mathrm{d}x\Big\|_{\ell^{2}}&=\Big\|k\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}b^{i}_{k})v^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big\|_{\ell^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\|\DIV(b)\|_{\ell^{2}}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\|\DIV(b)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b^{ik}\partial_{i}v\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}&+(p-2)\frac{\|(B_{t}(v)^{*}v)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}}\\
&\leq-\theta\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}+C(p-2)\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2},\end{split}

that is, (
H
3
) is satisfied with
f=0
and
K_{c}=C(p-2)
.
For the boundedness condition (
H
4
), let
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})
. Then,

\begin{split}|\langle A(t,u),v\rangle|\leq\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\|a^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})},\end{split}

that is,
\|A(t,u)\|_{H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}\leq\left(\sum\|a^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D})}\right)^{2}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}
, implying (
H
4
) for
\alpha=2
,
\beta=0
, and
K_{A}=\left(\sum\|a^{ij}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D})}\right)^{2}/2
. Similarly, because of (
4.2
),

\|B(t,v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})}^{2}\leq\Big\|\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\sigma^{ij}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}.

Hence condition (
H
5
) holds with
K_{\alpha}=\Big\|\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\sigma^{ij}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D})}
and
K_{B}=0
.
∎

From the above proof it follows that the regularity condition on
b
in Assumption
4.1
can actually be weakened to
b\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})
and
\DIV(b)\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})
, and where the divergence only needs to exist in distributional sense.

4.2.
Stochastic heat equation with Neumann boundary conditions

The second equation we consider is the same stochastic heat equation as before, but now with Neumann boundary conditions on a domain
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For completeness, this equation is:

(4.5)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\Big(\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial_{i}(a^{ij}\partial_{j}u(t))+\phi(t)\Big)\mathrm{d}t+\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}u(t)+\psi_{t,k}\Big)\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t).

Most assumptions and computations will be similar as before, though some special care is needed to derive the coercivity condition in the Neumann setting.

Assumptions 4.5
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain, and consider

(V,H,V^{*})=(H^{1}(\mathcal{D}),L^{2}(\mathcal{D}),H^{1}(\mathcal{D})^{*}).

Suppose that
a^{ij}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathcal{D})
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
and
(b_{k}^{i})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T];W^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2}))
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Furthermore, we assume that the coefficients are progressively measurable. Define

(4.6)

\sigma^{ij}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{i}b_{k}^{j},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{N}

and suppose that the uniform ellipticity condition on
a^{ij}
and
b_{k}^{i}
:

(4.7)

\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\left(2a^{ij}-\sigma^{ij}-(p-2)C_{b}^{2}\right)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\theta|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

holds true where
\theta>0
, and
C_{b}=\|b\cdot n\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D;\ell^{2})}
. Furthermore, assume
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{D}))
,

\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(\mathcal{D})^{*}))\ \ \text{and}\ \ \psi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2}))).

Equation (
4.5
) can be reformulated as a stochastic evolution equation of the form

\mathrm{d}u(t)=A(t,u(t))\ \mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}(t,u(t))\ \mathrm{d}W_{k}(t),

with the deterministic linear operator
A(t):H^{1}(\mathcal{D})\to H^{1}(\mathcal{D})^{*}
defined by

(4.8)

\langle A(t,u),v\rangle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}a^{ij}(t,x)\partial_{i}u\,\partial_{j}v\,\mathrm{d}x+\langle\phi(t),v\rangle\qquad\text{for }u,v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{D}),

and stochastic operators
B_{k}(t):H^{1}(\mathcal{D})\to L^{2}(\mathcal{D})
given by

(4.9)

B_{k}(t,v)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}v+\psi_{k,t}\qquad\text{for }v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{D}).

Unlike section
4.1
, the
p
-dependent term in the coercivity condition (
H
3
) does not vanish completely and enters through the term
b\cdot n|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}
. If
b\cdot n
vanishes at the boundary of
\mathcal{D}
, then the
p
-dependent term solution admits moment estimates of all orders
p\geq 2
, only limited by the integrability of the additive noise and the initial condition. The main result for the Neumann case is:

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose Assumptions
4.5
hold. Then, a unique solution
u
of equation (
4.5
) exists and the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{t}\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{CT}\bigg({\mathbb{E}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\phi(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})^{*}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\ell^{2})}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg)

where
C
depends on
\theta,p,a^{ij}
and
b_{k}^{i}
for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark
4.4
applies in the Neumann case as well, and thus this gives an alternative to (
4.7
) which additionally works without smoothness of
b
.

Before starting the proof of the proposition, we state a lemma that is needed to show the coercivity condition (
H
3
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Consider assumptions
4.5
and
B
as defined in (
4.9
). For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for every nonzero
v\in H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})
one has

(4.10)

\frac{\|B(t,v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)C_{b}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(C_{b}^{2}+D_{b}^{2})\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2},

where
C_{b}^{2}=\|b\cdot n\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D;\ell^{2})}^{2}
and
D_{b}^{2}=\|\DIV(b)\|_{L^{\infty}(D;\ell^{2})}^{2}
, where
n
is the outer normal and
\DIV
denotes the divergence.

Proof.

Observe that
{\rm Tr}(\phi u)=\phi{\rm Tr}(u)
for
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})
and
u\in W^{1,1}(\mathcal{D})
. Indeed, for
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{D}})
this is clear, and the general case follows by approximation and boundedness of
{\rm Tr}:W^{1,1}(\mathcal{D})\to L^{1}(\partial D)
.
Thus, by integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{D}}b^{ik}(\partial_{i}v)vdx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}b^{ik}(\partial_{i}v^{2})dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}b^{ik}{\rm Tr}(v^{2})n_{i}dS+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\partial_{i}b^{ik})v^{2}dx,

where
n
denotes the outer normal of
\mathcal{D}
.
Taking sums over
i
and
\ell^{2}
-norms in
k
for the last term we can write

\displaystyle\Big\|k\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}(\partial_{i}b^{ik})v^{2}dx\Big\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\int_{\mathcal{D}}\|{\rm div}(b)\|_{\ell^{2}}v^{2}dx\leq D_{b}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}.

For the boundary term we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|k\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}b^{ik}{\rm Tr}(v^{2})n_{i}dS\Big\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\int_{\partial\mathcal{D}}\|b\cdot n\|_{\ell^{2}}{\rm Tr}(v^{2})dS\leq C_{b}\|{\rm Tr}(v^{2})\|_{L^{1}(\partial\mathcal{D})}

By
[
18
, Theorem 2.7]
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|{\rm Tr}(v^{2})\|_{L^{1}(\partial D)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|\nabla(v^{2})\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{D})}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{D})}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\|v\nabla v\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{D})}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2},

Therefore, for
v\neq 0

\displaystyle\frac{\Big\|k\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}b^{ik}(\partial_{i}v)vdx\Big\|_{\ell^{2}}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)C_{b}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}+(C_{\varepsilon}C_{b}+D_{b})\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}.

Taking squares we obtain the desired estimate by using
(x+y)^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)x^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}y^{2}
, and by redefining
\varepsilon
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.6
.

We show that Assumptions
4.5
(
H
1
)-(
H
5
) hold, where we set
\phi=0,\psi=0
. Application of Theorem
2.4
gives the result. We see that (
H
1
), (
H
4
) and (
H
5
) are similar to the proof of Proposition
4.2
. To prove (
H
2
), we require an extra step in inequality (
4.4
). Note that the same sequence of inequalities hold, since we only use the uniform ellipticity condition. This condition also follows from the new uniform ellipticity condition in Assumptions
4.5
. By linearity of the operators, it suffices to consider
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{D})
. Using inequality (
4.4
), this results in:

\displaystyle 2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}v\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\theta\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\partial_{i}v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\theta\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}+\theta\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}.

We are left to prove (
H
3
). It only remains to inspect the term
\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|/\|v\|^{2}
where
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{D})
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and invoke lemma (
4.7
) to produce the bound

\begin{split}&2\langle A(t,v),v\rangle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}b_{k}^{i}\partial_{i}v\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}^{2}+(p-2)\frac{\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}}\\
&\leq(-\theta+(p-2)\varepsilon C_{b}^{2})\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(C_{b}^{2}+D_{b}^{2})\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}\\
&\leq(-\theta+(p-2)\varepsilon C_{b}^{2})\|v\|_{H^{1}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2}+(-(1+\varepsilon)C_{b}^{2}+\theta+C_{\varepsilon}(C_{b}^{2}+D_{b}^{2}))\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}^{2},\end{split}

where

. Since

, we can choose

such that

, and this gives (
H
3
). Applying Theorem
2.4
the required statement follows.
∎

4.3.
Stochastic Burgers’ equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions

We consider Burgers’ equation with multiplicative gradient noise, that is,

(4.11)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=\left(\partial^{2}u(t)+u(t)\partial u(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t+\gamma\partial u(t)\mathrm{d}W(t),\quad x\in(0,1),

where
W(t)
is a real-valued Wiener process. Equation (
4.11
) was first studied in
[
7
]
and subsequently in
[
11
]
with space-times white noise. We consider the same setting treated in
[
19
, Example 6.3]
of a one-dimensional gradient noise term. The novelty is that our main abstract theorem allows to treat arbitrary moments in
\Omega
using the classical parabolicity condition.

Assumptions 4.8
.

Let
\gamma\in(-\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2})
,
T>0
, and

(V,H,V^{*})=(H_{0}^{1}(0,1),L^{2}(0,1),H^{-1}(0,1))

and take
U={\mathbb{R}}
.

Now (
4.11
) can be reformulated as a stochastic evolution equation

\mathrm{d}u(t)=A(u(t))\mathrm{d}t+B(u(t))\mathrm{d}W(t),

where
A:H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{D})\to H^{-1}(\mathcal{D})
is given by

(4.12)

\langle A(u),v\rangle=-\int_{0}^{1}\partial u\,\partial v\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}u\,\partial u\,v\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{for }u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1),

and
B\colon H_{0}^{1}(0,1)\to L^{2}(0,1)
is defined by

(4.13)

B(v)=\gamma\partial v\qquad\text{for }v\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1).

Note that in order to align with our abstract framework, we would have to take
B\colon H_{0}^{1}(0,1)\to\mathcal{L}_{2}({\mathbb{R}},L^{2}(0,1))
but we do not distinguish between the through the multiplication operation trivially isomorphic spaces
\mathcal{L}_{2}({\mathbb{R}},L^{2}(0,1))
and
L^{2}(0,1)
.

Since we can allow
p=\infty
in the above, it will turn out that we are able to obtain uniform estimates in
\Omega
for this particular example. This is in correspondence with what has been shown in
[
7
]
, albeit obtained in a different way.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose that Assumptions
4.8
are satisfied. Let
p\in[4,\infty]
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,1))
the equation (
4.11
) has a unique solution
u
, and the following energy estimate holds

\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(0,1)))}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(0,1)))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,1))},

where
C
only depends on
\gamma
.

Proof.

As in previous instances, it suffices to verify Assumptions
2.1
, (
H
1
)-(
H
5
), with
f=0
and
K_{B}=K_{c}=0
, so that the proposition follows from Theorem
2.4
and Corollary
3.4
. Hemicontinuity (
H
1
) is obvious. In order to prove local weak monotonicity (
H
2
), note that for
u,v\in H_{0}^{1}(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{A_burgers}}}{{=}}-\int_{0}^{1}\partial(u-v)\,\partial(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}(u\,\partial u-v\,\partial v)\,(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\,=-\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(u-v)\,\partial(u^{2}-v^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Integration by parts then entails

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(u-v)\,\partial(u^{2}-v^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(u^{2}-v^{2})\,\partial(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{1}\partial(u-v)^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}v\,(u-v)\,\partial(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}v(u-v)\,\partial(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x,

so that

\displaystyle\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}-\int_{0}^{1}v(u-v)\,\partial(u-v)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,1)}\|u-v\|_{L^{4}(0,1)}\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}.

We employ the Sobolev-Gagliardo-Nirenberg and Poincaré inequality to obtain

so that

\displaystyle\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,1)}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}(\varepsilon-1)\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{4}(0,1)}^{4}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(ii)}}}{{\leq}}(\varepsilon-1)\|u-v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\|v\|_{H_{0}^{1}(0,1)}^{2}\|u-v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)},

4.13

\begin{split}&2\langle A(u)-A(v),u-v\rangle+\|B(u)-B(v)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\\

. Noting that

,

, and

4.4.
Stochastic Navier-Stokes equations in 2D

Consider the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations in two space dimensions with multiplicative gradient noise

(4.14)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=(\nu\Delta u(t)-(u,\nabla)u)\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}[(b_{k},\nabla)u]\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t)-(\nabla p)\mathrm{d}t.

Here,
(W_{k}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a collection of independent real Wiener processes indexed by
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The components
b_{k}
are set to be vectors of divergence free vector fields (see Assumptions
4.10
below). Equation (
4.14
) was considered in
[
7
]
using semigroup methods, and later on in many other papers (see
[
4
]
and references therein). For simplicity we do not consider additional forcing terms, but they can be included without difficulty (see Remark
2.3
).

In what follows, we use:

Assumptions 4.10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain. Furthermore, assume
\nu>0
,
T>0
,

(b_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathcal{D};\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}};{\mathbb{R}}^{2\times 2}))
which is progressively measurable, and satisfies
\DIV b_{k}=(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{i}b_{k}^{i\gamma})_{\gamma=1}^{2}=0
in the sense of distributions for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We impose the coercivity condition that there exists
\kappa>0
such that

(4.15)

\Big(2\nu\sum\limits_{i,\gamma=1}^{2}(\xi^{i,\gamma})^{2}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}b_{k}^{i\gamma}b_{k}^{j\gamma^{\prime}}\xi^{i,\gamma}\xi^{j,\gamma^{\prime}}\Big)\geq\kappa\sum\limits_{i,\gamma=1}^{2}(\xi^{i,\gamma})^{2},

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.
Set
U:=\ell^{2}
and define
(V,H,V^{*})
by

V=\{v\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\nabla\cdot v=0\quad a.e.\text{ on }\mathcal{D}\},\quad\|v\|_{V}:=\Big(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|\nabla v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and where
H
is the closure of
V
with respect to the norm

\|v\|_{H}:=\Big(\int_{\mathcal{D}}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Defining the Helmholtz-Leray projection
\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}
as the orthogonal projection

\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}:L^{2}(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to H,

equation (
4.14
) turns into a stochastic evolution equation

(4.16)

\mathrm{d}u(t)=(Lu(t)+F(u(t)))\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}B_{k}(u(t))\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t),

where
L:H^{2,2}(\mathcal{D};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap V\to H
is given by

Lu=\nu\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}(\Delta u),\quad u\in H^{2,2}(\mathcal{D};{\mathbb{R}}^{2})\cap V

and can be extended to a map
L:V\to V^{*}
such that
\|Lu\|_{V^{*}}\leq\|u\|_{V},u\in V
. Furthermore, set
F
to be a nonlinear operator
F:V\to V^{*}
given by

F(u)=-\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}[(u,\nabla)u]=-\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}[\DIV(u\otimes u)],\quad u\in V.

Finally, define
B\colon V\to\mathcal{L}_{2}(U,H)
by

B(u)e_{k}=B_{k}(u)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{HL}}[(b_{k},\nabla)u],\quad u\in V.

Theorem 4.11
.

Suppose Assumption
4.10
holds and let
p\in[2,\infty]
. Then, for any
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;H)
, there exists a unique solution
u
to equation (
4.16
) and there exists a constant

only depending on

such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];H))}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;V))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;H)}

Remark 4.12
.

The special case of periodic boundary conditions in case of rough initial data was recently considered in
[
1
]
, where high order regularity was proved. There the monotone operator setting (in

) was combined with a new approach to SPDEs based on maximal regularity techniques (see
[
2

3
]
). The main difficulty to prove high order regularity for the solution to (

) is that the nonlinearity is

for the space

4.11
.

It suffices to use Theorem

and Corollary

with

Next, we compute

. Finally, the contribution (

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\|B_{k}(u)-B_{k}(v)&\|^{2}_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\mathrm{i})}}{{\leq}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\|[(b_{k},\nabla)(u-v)]\|^{2}_{H}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\mathrm{ii})}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\mathcal{D}}b_{k}^{i\gamma}b_{k}^{j\gamma^{\prime}}\partial_{i}(u-v)^{\gamma}\partial_{j}(u-v)^{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\kappa^{3}}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{4}\|u-v\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{\kappa^{3}}\|v\|_{V}^{2}\|v\|_{H}^{2}\|u-v\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{\kappa^{3}}(1+\|v\|_{V}^{2})(1+\|v\|_{H}^{2})\|u-v\|_{H}^{2},

with a constant

and (i) follows from the Sobolev-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. The above implies that (

and

.

In order to show (

We also note that

&=\underbrace{\int_{\mathcal{D}}\big((b_{k}^{11}\partial_{1}v^{1})v^{1}+(b_{k}^{12}\partial_{2}v^{1})v^{1}\big)\mathrm{d}x}_{\boxed{\text{A}}}+\int_{\mathcal{D}}\big((b_{k}^{21}\partial_{1}v^{2})v^{2}+(b_{k}^{22}\partial_{2}v^{2})v^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

4.5.
Systems of second order SPDEs

The authors of
[
12
]
develop
a
C^{2+\delta}
theory for systems of SPDEs.
This relies on integral estimates for a model system of SPDEs (see
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
). We will show that one of the underlying assumptions, which the authors of
[
12
]
call the
modified stochastic parabolicity condition
, fits naturally in our framework.
Sharpness follows from
[
12
, Example 1.1]
which is based on
[
13
, Section 3]
.

Consider a random field

\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},...,u^{N})^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

described by the following linear system of SPDEs:

(4.21)

\mathrm{d}u^{\alpha}=\left(a^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{ij}u^{\beta}+\phi_{\alpha}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\sigma^{i}_{k,\alpha\beta}\partial_{i}u^{\beta}+\psi_{k,\alpha}\right)\mathrm{d}W_{k}(t)

where the collection
\{W_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are countably many independent Wiener processes.

In this section we use Einstein’s summation convention with

i,j=1,2,...,d;\quad\alpha,\beta=1,2,...,N;\quad k=1,2,...

The assumptions are:

Assumptions 4.13
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
,
d\geq 1
and
N\geq 1
. Let

(V,H,V^{*})=(H^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),H^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),H^{m-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}))

.

for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

for all
1\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N
.
and suppose that the following stochastic modified parabolicity condition is satisfied:

(MSP)

The coefficients
a=(a^{ij}_{\alpha\beta})
and
\sigma=(\sigma^{i}_{k,\alpha\beta})
are said to satisfy the stochastic modified parabolicity (MSP) condition if there are measurable functions
\lambda^{i}_{k,\alpha\beta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda^{i}_{k,\alpha\beta}=\lambda^{i}_{k,\beta\alpha}
such that for

\mathcal{A}^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}=2a^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}-\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\alpha}\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\beta}-(p-2)(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\alpha}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\alpha})(\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{j}_{k,\gamma\beta})

there exists a constant
\kappa>0
with

\mathcal{A}^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\geq\kappa|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times\Omega
.

Suppose that
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}}_{0};H)
and

\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];\\
H^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))),\quad\psi\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})))).

Remark 4.14
.

The above ellipticity condition
\mathcal{A}^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\geq\kappa|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}
is known as the Legendre-Hadamard condition. In case the coefficients depend on the space variable some smoothness is required if one wishes to assume this type of ellipticity. Alternatively, one can consider measurable coefficients with a more restrictive ellipticity condition. For details on these matters we refer to
[
5
]

In the MSP condition one typically takes

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{i}v^{\beta}\partial_{j}v^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x.

Next, we use definition (
4.24
) to consider the term

\begin{split}\|B_{t}(v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\alpha}\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\beta}\partial_{i}v^{\beta}\partial_{j}v^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

Considering

,
for
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
v\neq 0
, we have:

(4.25)

\begin{split}\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}&=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|(B_{t}(v)^{*}v)_{k}|^{2}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}(\partial_{i}v^{\beta})v^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}.\end{split}

Note that the following identity holds:

\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}\partial_{i}v^{\beta}v^{\gamma}=(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})v^{\gamma}\partial_{i}v^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{k,\gamma\beta}^{i}\partial_{i}(v^{\gamma}v^{\beta}).

Integrating both sides of the above expression over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by equation (
4.25
) we find:

\displaystyle\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})(\partial_{i}v^{\beta})v^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum\limits_{\gamma=1}^{N}(v^{\gamma})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum\limits_{\gamma=1}^{N}((\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})\partial_{i}v^{\beta})^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{(i)}}}{{\leq}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}((\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})\partial_{i}v^{\beta})^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})(\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha}-\lambda^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha})\partial_{i}v^{\beta}\partial_{j}v^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x,

where the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is applied at (i). This leads to

\frac{\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})(\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha}-\lambda^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha})\partial_{i}v^{\beta}\partial_{j}v^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\Big).

Therefore, the coercivity condition (
H
3
) can be derived from (MSP) as:

\begin{split}&2\langle A(v),v\rangle+\|(B_{t}(v))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}+(p-2)\frac{\|B_{t}(v)^{*}v\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}}{\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(-2a^{ij}_{\alpha\beta}+\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\alpha}\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\beta}+(p-2)(\sigma^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta}-\lambda^{i}_{k,\gamma\beta})(\sigma^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha}-\lambda^{j}_{k,\gamma\alpha})\right)\partial_{i}v^{\beta}\partial_{j}v^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq-\kappa\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\kappa\|v\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2},\end{split}

which shows that (
H
3
) holds with
\theta=\kappa
,
f=0
, and
K_{c}=\kappa
.
∎

Higher order SPDEs

(4.26)

Assumptions 4.16

We consider the following stochastic version of the

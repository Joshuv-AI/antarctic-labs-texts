Abstract
. Let Π \Pi be a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of GL 3 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) . 1. Introduction

1.1. Context

There is a wide network of conjectures, including the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer and Bloch–Kato conjectures, that describe important arithmetic invariants in terms of special values of (complex)
L
-functions. One of the most successful tools in tackling these conjectures has been Iwasawa theory, in which one seeks to formulate
p
-adic analogues of these conjectures, replacing the complex analytic
L
-function with a
p
-adic
L
-function
. The resulting
p
-adic Iwasawa main conjectures are often more tractable than their complex counterparts, and provide beautiful, deep connections between analysis and arithmetic.

A crucial launching point for Iwasawa theory is proving
existence
of
p
-adic
L
-functions. Coates and Perrin-Riou conjectured the existence of a
p
-adic
L
-function attached to every motive
M
over
\mathbb{Q}
that is ordinary with good reduction at
p
, and whose
L
-function has at least one critical value
[
CPR89
,
Coa89
]
. This itself rests on Deligne’s period conjecture
[
Del79
]
. One may formulate automorphic realisations of these (motivic) conjectures, but proving these remains very difficult.

To illustrate this, consider the fundamental case where
\Pi
is a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation (RACAR) of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then Coates–Perrin-Riou predict that when
\Pi
is unramified and ordinary at
p
, there exists a
p
-adic measure on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
– the
p
-adic
L
-function of
\Pi
– interpolating all its critical
L
-values at
p
. We make this precise in
B
.

For
n=1,2
, existence of
p
-adic
L
-functions has been known for decades (starting from e.g.
[
KL64
,
MSD74
]
). For
n\geq 3
, however, our understanding of the conjecture remains poor, and it is known only in very special cases, e.g. symmetric squares of classical modular forms
[
Sch88
,
Hid90
,
DD97
]
, RACARs of
\mathrm{GL}_{2n}
with Shalika models
[
AG94
,
DJR20
]
, or Rankin–Selberg transfers from
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{n(n+1)}

[
Sch93
,
KMS00
,
Jan
]
. In particular, in
all
known cases,
\Pi
arises as a functorial lift from a group whose
L
-group is a proper subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
: beyond the classical cases of
n=1,2
, there are no examples when
\Pi
has ‘general type’, i.e. doesn’t arise from a smaller group in this way.

In this paper, we prove existence of
p
-adic
L
-functions for
p
-ordinary RACARs of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, making no self-duality or functorial lift assumptions. This provides the first examples of
p
-adic
L
-functions for general type RACARs of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
for any
n\geq 3
.

We briefly highlight some further strengths of our construction:

–

We work in arbitrary cohomological weight, and prove the so-called ‘Manin relations’; that is, we construct a
single

p
-adic measure that sees
L
-values at all critical integers (rather than a separate measure for each different critical integer).

–

We actually prove a more general conjecture of Panchishkin
[
Pan94
]
that refines Coates–Perrin-Riou: rather than Coates–Perrin-Riou’s assumption of Borel-ordinarity at
p
, we impose
p
-ordinarity only along the maximal standard parabolic
P_{1}
with Levi
\mathrm{GL}_{1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}
.

–

We allow arbitrary ramification at
p
. As a result, our construction applies even to a class of RACARs that have infinite slope at
p
for the Borel subgroup (those that are ordinary along
P_{1}
, but that have infinite slope along the other maximal parabolic subgroup
P_{2}
).

We believe that our result is “optimal”, in the sense that
p
-adic
L
-functions for RACARs of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
should only exist as bounded measures when these conditions hold. (Our methods can be adapted to construct finite-order tempered distributions interpolating
L
-values of RACARs which have positive, but sufficiently small, slope for
P_{1}
; this will be pursued elsewhere.)

; they could not prove that their distributions had any uniquely-determined extension from this discrete set to the whole weight space of continuous characters of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Thus their methods did not give a
p
-adic
L
-function in any usual sense (notwithstanding the titles of these papers). This is the fundamental breakthrough in the present work: our methods give uniform control over the denominators of Eisenstein classes, allowing us to construct a uniquely-determined, bounded measure interpolating all critical values.

(Whilst our
p
-adic methods differ totally from those of
[
Mah00
]
and
[
Ger15
]
, these references do contain excellent accounts of a number of the automorphic aspects we require, particularly the local zeta integral at infinity.)

In the special case when
\Pi
is a symmetric square lift of a RACAR of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, a different construction of the

-adic

-function is possible, using Rankin–Selberg convolutions of the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
cusp form with half-integer weight theta-series; see e.g.
[

,
Hid90
,
DD97
]
). However, these methods are completely specific to the case of symmetric-square lifts.

2. Preliminaries: automorphic representations

2.1. Characters

If
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a Dirichlet character, there is a unique finite-order Hecke character
\widehat{\chi}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}/\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{>0}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\widehat{\chi}(\varpi_{\ell})=\chi(\ell)
for all primes
\ell\nmid N
, where
\varpi_{\ell}
is a uniformiser at
\ell
; conversely, every finite-order Hecke character is
\widehat{\chi}
for a unique primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
.

Note that the restriction of
\widehat{\chi}
to
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\times}
is the
inverse
of the composition
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\xrightarrow{\ \chi\ }\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Moreover, if
\ell\mid N
, then
\widehat{\chi}_{\ell}(\ell)=\chi^{(\ell)}(\ell)
, where we have written
\chi=\chi_{\ell}\chi^{(\ell)}
a product of characters of
\ell
-power and prime-to-
\ell
conductor. Given a representation
\Pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})
, we write
\Pi\times\chi
for the representation
\Pi\times[\widehat{\chi}\circ\det]
.

We let
\psi
be the unique character of the additive group
\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\psi(x)=\exp(-2\pi ix)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that the restriction of
\psi
to
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, for a finite prime
\ell
, maps
1/\ell^{n}
to
\exp(2\pi i/\ell^{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\varepsilon(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell},\psi_{\ell})
be the local
\varepsilon
-factor (with respect to the unramified Haar measure
\mathrm{d}x
), as defined in
[
Tat79
]
for example. Then we have

\prod_{\ell\mid N}\varepsilon(\widehat{\chi}_{\ell},\psi_{\ell})=G(\chi)\coloneqq\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(a)\exp(2\pi ia/N)

for
\chi
primitive of conductor
N
. (We do not include the Archimedean root number here.)

2.2. Algebraic representations

If
J
is a split reductive group, and
\mu
a
B_{J}
-dominant weight for some choice of Borel

, then we write

for the unique irreducible algebraic representation of

of highest weight
\mu
. When

, we will drop the

.

Let

denote the diagonal torus of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
(identified with

in the obvious fashion), and

the upper-triangular Borel subgroup. The

-dominant weights for

are of the form

-algebra, then we can realise

as a space of polynomial functions on

where

A weight

is

is isomorphic to a twist of

.

2.4. The

2.4.1. Galois representations

By

Example 2.15
:

We briefly recall the classification of generic representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}

•

If

is supercuspidal, it admits no

-refinements or

-refinements, and hence is never nearly-ordinary for any parabolic.

•

If

Steinberg representation by a character

•

If

•

If

-ordinarity, which we leave to the reader.)

•

If

-refinements are the pairs

-ordinary if in addition

, and
P_{2}
-ordinary if in addition

3. Symmetric spaces and Betti cohomology

3.1. Symmetric spaces

For any split reductive group
J
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we define a symmetric space for
J
by
\mathcal{H}_{J}=J(\mathbb{R})/K_{J,\infty}^{\circ}Z_{J,\infty}^{\circ},
where
(-)^{\circ}
denotes the identity component,
K_{J,\infty}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
J(\mathbb{R})
, and
Z_{\infty}=Z_{J}(\mathbb{R})
. For a neat open compact subgroup
\mathcal{U}\subset J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
, we define

Y^{J}(\mathcal{U})=J(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\left[J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})/\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{H}_{J}\right].

Note that for
J=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the components of
Y^{J}(\mathcal{U})
are indexed by the double quotient

Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{1}}(\det\mathcal{U})=\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}/\det(\mathcal{U})\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\times}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}/\det(\mathcal{U}).

Each component of
Y^{J}(\mathcal{U})
is the quotient of
\mathcal{H}_{J}^{\circ}
by an arithmetic subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Note
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
is the union of two copies of the complex upper half-plane, and has dimension 2 as a real manifold; while
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
is a 5-dimensional real manifold, again with two components.

Remark 3.1
:

Our conventions match the usual ones for modular curves when
J=\mathrm{GL}(2)
. However, note that some papers (such as
[
HT17
]
) use the slightly different quotient
J(\mathbb{Q})\backslash J(\mathbb{A})/UK_{\infty}Z_{\infty}^{\circ}
. For example, if
J=\mathrm{GL}(2)
and
U=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\bmod N\}
, our
Y^{J}(U)
is the usual modular curve
Y_{1}(N)
, while the space considered in
[
HT17
]
is the quotient of
Y_{1}(N)
by an anti-holomorphic involution.

3.2. Betti cohomology and Hecke operators

Let
J,\mathcal{U}
be as above. Given an algebraic representation
V
of
J
(over
\mathbb{Q}
), we have three possible constructions of local systems on
Y^{J}(\mathcal{U})
:

•

a local system
\mathscr{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces, given by the locally constant sections of the projection

J(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\big[\big(J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})\times\mathcal{H}_{J}\big)\times V(\mathbb{Q})\big]/\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow Y^{J}(\mathcal{U}),

with action
\gamma\cdot[(g,z),v]\cdot u=[(\gamma gu,\gamma z),\gamma\cdot v]
. (The functor
V\mapsto\mathscr{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is Pink’s “canonical construction” functor,
[
Pin90
]
).

•

a local system
\mathscr{V}_{\infty}
of
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces, given by the locally constant sections of

J(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\big[\big(J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})\times J(\mathbb{R})\big)\times V(\mathbb{R})\big]/\mathcal{U}K_{J,\infty}^{\circ}Z_{J,\infty}^{\circ}\to Y^{J}(\mathcal{U}),

with action
\gamma\cdot[(g,z),v]\cdot uk_{\infty}z_{\infty}=[(\gamma gu,\gamma z),(k_{\infty}z_{\infty})^{-1}\cdot v]
.

•

a local system of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector spaces
\mathscr{V}_{p}
for any finite prime
p
, given by the locally constant sections of

(3.1)

J(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\big[\big(J(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})\times\mathcal{H}_{J}\big)\times V(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\big]/\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow Y^{J}(\mathcal{U}),

with action
\gamma[(g,z),v]u=[(\gamma gu,\gamma z),u_{p}^{-1}\cdot v]
.

If
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{U}
, then the formation of
\mathscr{V}_{?}
is compatible with the natural projection map

denotes Betti cohomology. We write

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(Y^{J},\mathscr{V}_{?})=\varinjlim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(Y^{J}(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{?})

where

varies over open compact subgroups of

We have direct analogues of these statements for compactly-supported cohomology

It is standard that there are canonical isomorphisms of local systems

For instance, the first of these two is given on sections by

[

3.3.1. Periods and rationality

The representation
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}},\mathscr{V}_{\lambda,\mathbb{Q}}^{\vee}(\mathbb{C}))
has a natural
\mathbb{Q}
-structure given by the cohomology of
\mathscr{V}_{\lambda,\mathbb{Q}}^{\vee}(\mathbb{Q})
. Since the complex representation is admissible and contains
\Pi_{\mathrm{f}}
with multiplicity 1, it follows that there is a number field
E
, the
field of definition
of
\Pi
, such that the
E
-linear representation

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Pi,E)=\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Pi,\mathbb{C})\cap\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}\left(Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}},\mathscr{V}_{\lambda,\mathbb{Q}}^{\vee}(E)\right)

is non-zero and gives an
E
-structure on
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Pi,\mathbb{C})
. (This is the same field
E
as from
Section

2.4
. By the strong multiplicity one theorem for
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
, we may take
E
to be the field generated by the Hecke eigenvalues of
\Pi
at the unramified primes, although we shall not need this.)

From the uniqueness of Whittaker models,
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}})
also has a canonical
E
-structure
\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},E)
, given by the functions
W\in\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}})
which take values in
E\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}
and satisfy

W(g)^{\sigma}=W\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\kappa(\sigma)^{2}\\
&\kappa(\sigma)\\
&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)g\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(E\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/E)$},

where
\kappa:\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
is the cyclotomic character. Hence there exists
\Theta_{\Pi}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that

\phi_{\Pi}\left(\mathcal{W}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},E)\right)=\Theta_{\Pi}\cdot\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{c}}(\Pi,E).

Remark 3.2
:

Having fixed
\zeta_{\infty}
, the period
\Theta_{\Pi}
is uniquely determined modulo
E^{\times}
. We may, however, rescale
\zeta_{\infty}
by an arbitrary non-zero complex scalar, which then also rescales
\Theta_{\Pi}
.

Note that for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
with
n
odd, we obtain only a single Whittaker period, since (
2.1
) is 1-dimensional. This differs from the case of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
for
n
even, where (
2.1
) is 2-dimensional and there are two Whittaker periods (one for each choice of sign at
\infty
).

3.3.2. Integral lattices

Let
V_{\mu}^{J}
be an algebraic representation of a split reductive group
J
, with highest weight vector
v_{\mu}\in V_{\mu}^{J}
. An
admissible lattice
is a lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset V_{\mu}
such that: (1) the map
J_{/\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mu})
extends to a map of
\mathbb{Z}
-group schemes
J_{/\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{L})
, and (2) the intersection of the highest weight space in
V_{\mu}
with
\mathcal{L}
is
\mathbb{Z}\cdot v_{\lambda}
. There are finitely many such lattices (cf.
[
LSZb
, §4.2]
).

We adopt the convenient (but somewhat misleading) notation that
V_{\mu,\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
denotes the
minimal
such lattice in
V_{\mu}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
, whilst
V_{\lambda,\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
denotes the
maximal
such lattice in
V_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
. This ensures that under the branching laws of the next section,
V_{\star,\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
is always mapped into
V_{\star,\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
.

3.3.3. Integral structures

Now let
p
be a prime, and

a prime of
E
. Completing

at

, we may take

Note as above that

The

Remark 3.3
:

Henceforth we will always take

a finite extension containing all embeddings of

or

\phi_{\Pi}/\Theta_{\Pi}:\mathcal{W}_{\psi}(\Pi_{\mathrm{f}},E)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}(Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}},\mathscr{V}_{\lambda}^{\vee}(\mathcal{O}_{L})),

3.4. The cup product pairing

Let
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}^{(p)}\mathcal{U}_{p}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
be a neat open compact subgroup and let

be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra. There is a (perfect) Poincaré duality pairing

(3.6)

\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathcal{U}}:\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}\big(Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{\lambda}^{\vee}(R)\big)\times\frac{\mathrm{H}^{3}(Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{\lambda}(R))}{\mathrm{(torsion)}}\longrightarrow R

given by composing cup product, the natural pairing
V_{\lambda}^{\vee}(R)\otimes V_{\lambda}(R)\to R

N_{P_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\subset\mathcal{U}_{p}\subset\mathcal{J}_{p,1}
, then by adjointness of pullback and pushforward, the Hecke operators

and

4. The subgroup
H

Definition 4.1
.

Let
H=\mathrm{GL}_{2}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}
, and let
\iota:H\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{3}
be the embedding

(4.1)

\left[\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right),z\right]\longmapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b&\\
c&d&\\
&&z\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}:H\to\mathrm{GL}_{1}
be the homomorphisms given by

\nu_{1}(\gamma,z)=\tfrac{\det\gamma}{z}\hskip 20.00003pt\nu_{2}(\gamma,z)=z.

Note
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
gives an isomorphism
H/H^{\mathrm{der}}\cong\mathrm{GL}_{1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}
(this parametrisation of
H/H^{\mathrm{der}}
may seem slightly unnatural, but will give us nicer formulae later), and
\det\circ\mathop{\iota}=\nu_{1}\nu_{2}^{2}
.

4.1. Symmetric spaces

It is important to note that the embedding
\iota:H\to\mathrm{GL}_{3}
does not induce a map on symmetric spaces, since the inclusion
Z_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}\subset\iota(Z_{H})
is strict. To transfer cohomology classes from
Y^{H}
to
Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
, we instead define

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{H}=H(\mathbb{R})/\left(K_{H,\infty}^{\circ}\cdot\iota^{-1}(Z_{G,\infty}^{\circ})\right),

which maps naturally to both
\mathcal{H}_{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{G}
. If
\mathcal{U}\subset H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
is open compact, let

(4.2)

\widetilde{Y}^{H}(\mathcal{U})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\left[H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})/\mathcal{U}\times\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}^{H}\right].

For any open compact
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
, we then have a diagram of maps

(4.3)

Y^{H}(\mathcal{V}\cap H)\leftarrow\widetilde{Y}^{H}(\mathcal{V}\cap H)\xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }Y^{G}(\mathcal{V}),

where the right-hand map is induced by
\iota
. Pulling back under the leftward arrow and pushing forward under the rightward arrow gives a map from the cohomology
Y^{H}
to that of
Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is small enough, the left arrow is a fibre bundle with fibres isomorphic to
Z_{H,\infty}^{\circ}/\iota^{-1}(Z_{G,\infty}^{\circ})\cong\mathbb{R}
. The spaces
Y^{H}
(resp.
\widetilde{Y}^{H}
) have dimension 2 (resp. 3) as real manifolds.

We get local systems on the modified spaces
\widetilde{Y}^{H}(\mathcal{U})
from (
4.2
), defined identically to
Section

3.2
.

4.2. Branching laws

Note
V^{H}_{(r,s;t)}=V_{(r,s)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\otimes V_{(t)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{1}}
is the
H
-representation of highest weight
(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&\\
&y\end{smallmatrix}\right),z)\mapsto x^{r}\cdot y^{s}\cdot z^{t}
. We let
V_{(r,s;t),\mathbb{Z}}^{H}
denote the
minimal
admissible lattice in
V_{(r,s;t)}^{H}
. The following is equivalent to the well-known branching law from
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, e.g.
[
GW09
, §8]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\lambda=(a,0,-a)
. The restriction of
V_{\lambda}
to
H
(embedded via
\iota
) is given by

\iota^{*}\left(V_{\lambda}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{0\leq i,j\leq a}V^{H}_{(j,-i;i-j)}.

For such
\lambda
, we shall fix choices of non-zero morphisms of
H
-representations over
\mathbb{Q}

\operatorname{br}^{[a,j]}:V^{H}_{(j,0;-j)}\to\iota^{*}\left(V_{\lambda}\right)

for each
j
as above. Note
V^{H}_{(j,0;-j)}=V^{H}_{(0,-j;0)}\otimes\|\nu_{1}^{j}\|
, hence our choice yields a pairing

(4.4)

\langle-,-\rangle_{a,j}:V_{\lambda}^{\vee}\times V_{(0,-j)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}\to\mathbb{G}_{a},\hskip 12.0pt(\mu,v)\mapsto\mu\big(\mathrm{br}^{[a,j]}(\|\nu_{1}^{j}\|\otimes[v\otimes 1])\big).

As in
[
LSZb
, Prop 4.3.5]
,
\mathrm{br}^{[a,j]}
maps the (minimal) admissible lattice
V^{H}_{(j,0;-j),\mathbb{Z}}
into the (maximal) admissible lattice
V_{\lambda,\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus the pairing
\langle-,-\rangle_{a,j}
also makes sense integrally.

5. Eisenstein series and classes for

We recall the theory of Eisenstein series and classes attached to adelic Schwartz functions. In particular, we recall the motivic Eisenstein classes of Beĭlinson (see e.g.
[
LSZb
, §7]
) and describe their Betti realisations via adelic Eisenstein series. In this section all symmetric spaces will be for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, so we write simply
Y(\mathcal{U})
(resp.
Y
) for
Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}(\mathcal{U})
(resp.
Y^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
).

If
j\geq 0
, recall
V_{(0,-j)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
denotes the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-representation of highest weight
(0,-j)
. Similarly, in this section only we will drop the superscript and denote this simply
V_{(0,-j)}
.

5.1. Schwartz functions

For a field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, write
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
for the Schwartz space of locally-constant, compactly-supported functions on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2}
with values in
K
, and
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
for the subspace of
\Phi
with
\Phi(0,0)=0
. We also let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2},K)
be the usual space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, write
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2},K)
for the subspace with
\Phi(0,0)=0
, and let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}^{2},K)=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2},K)
(and similarly for
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{2},K)
). We will make specific choices at infinity, depending on an integer
j\geq 0
, and use the notation
\mathcal{S}_{(0)}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
to mean “
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
if
j=0
or
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
otherwise”.

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character, corresponding to a finite-order Hecke character
\widehat{\chi}
. If
{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
, let
R_{\chi}({\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K(\chi))
be its projection to the
\widehat{\chi}^{-1}
-isotypical component, given by

(5.1)

R_{\chi}({\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})(x,y)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{a\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}\widehat{\chi}(a)\ {\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}(ax,ay)\ \mathrm{d}^{\times}a.

We emphasise that this is a projection operator,
not
a twisting operator: if
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are distinct primitive Dirichlet characters, then
R_{\chi_{1}}R_{\chi_{2}}({\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})=0
.

5.2. Eisenstein series

We now review some standard definitions of Eisenstein series from Schwartz functions, both adelically and classically, and relate these definitions.

5.2.1. Adelic Eisenstein series

Let
\Phi=\Phi_{\infty}\cdot{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}^{2},\mathbb{C})
and
\widehat{\chi}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a Hecke character. For
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
and
\Re(s)>0
we define a
Siegel section

(5.2)

f_{\Phi}(g;\widehat{\chi},s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\|\det g\|^{s}\int_{\mathbb{A}^{\times}}\Phi\Big((0,a)g\Big)\widehat{\chi}(a)\|a\|^{2s}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}a.

This admits meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
and gives an element of the family of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-principal series representations
I(\|\cdot\|^{s-1/2},\widehat{\chi}^{-1}\|\cdot\|^{-s+1/2})
, that is,
f_{\Phi}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)g;\widehat{\chi},s\right)=\widehat{\chi}^{-1}(d)\|a/d\|^{s}f_{\Phi}(g;\widehat{\chi}s)
. It thus defines an element of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(B_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}),\mathbb{C})
, for
B_{2}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}
the upper-triangular Borel.

Define (cf.
[
Jac72
, §19]
)

(5.3)

E_{\Phi}(g;\widehat{\chi},s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{\gamma\in B_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})}f_{\Phi}(\gamma g;\widehat{\chi},s),

which converges absolutely and locally uniformly on some right half-plane (for
\Re(s)>1
if
\widehat{\chi}
is unitary), defining a function on the quotient
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
that transforms under the centre by
\widehat{\chi}^{-1}
. It has meromorphic continuation in
s
, analytic if
\Phi(0,0)=\hat{\Phi}(0,0)=0
or if
\widehat{\chi}
is ramified at some finite place.

5.2.2. Classical Eisenstein series

We now introduce classical Eisenstein series.

Definition 5.1
.

(i)

If
{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}\in\mathcal{S}_{(0)}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},K)
and
j\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{E}^{j+2}_{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}(\tau;s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\Gamma(s+\tfrac{j+2}{2})}{(-2\pi i)^{j+2}\pi^{s-\tfrac{j+2}{2}}}\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\backslash(0,0)}\frac{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}(m,n)y^{s-\tfrac{j+2}{2}}}{(m\tau+n)^{j+2}|m\tau+n|^{2s-j-2}},

where
s\in\mathbb{C}
and
\tau\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
. This is a classical real-analytic weight
j+2
Eisenstein series.

(ii)

We extend this to a function on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\times\mathcal{H}
by setting

\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(g_{\mathrm{f}},\tau;s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{E}^{j+2}_{g_{\mathrm{f}}\cdot{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}(\tau;s).

(iii)

Finally, for a Dirichlet character
\chi
we also define

\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(\tau;\chi,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{E}_{R_{\chi}({\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})}^{j+2}(\tau;s),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(g_{\mathrm{f}},\tau;\chi,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{E}_{R_{\chi}({\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})}^{j+2}(g_{\mathrm{f}},\tau;s).

We write
\mathcal{E}^{j+2}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}
for each of these functions, but it will be clear from context which we use.

Remarks
:

The function defined in (i) is denoted
E^{(j+2,{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}})}(\tau;s)
in
[
LPSZ
, Def. 7.1]
. These series always converge absolutely for
\Re(s)\geq 1
unless
j=0
and
\chi
is trivial, and even in this case they converge absolutely if
\widehat{\Phi}(0,0)=0
.

One has the functional equation
\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(\tau;s)=\mathcal{E}_{\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{f}}}^{j+2}(\tau;1-s)
, where
\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{f}}
is the Fourier transform (normalised as in
[
LPSZ
, §8.1]
); this explains the compatibility between our conventions and those of
[
LSZb
, §7]
.
The definition in (ii) ensures the association
{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}
equivariant under
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
.

Via the standard procedure (see
[
Wei71
, §I]
), we now extend
\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}
to a function on
g=g_{\mathrm{f}}g_{\infty}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
. By Iwasawa decomposition,
g_{\infty}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be written uniquely in the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&\\
&z\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)r(\theta),
where
z\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and
r(\theta)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{SO}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we define

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(-;\chi,-):\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})\times\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle(g,s)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\widehat{\chi}_{\infty}^{-1}(z)\cdot|\det(g_{\infty})|^{-s}\cdot y^{\tfrac{j+2}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{exp}[{i(j+2)\theta}]\cdot\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}\left(g_{\mathrm{f}},x+iy;\chi,s\right).

This yields an automorphic form on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
: the
y^{\tfrac{j+2}{2}}
and
e^{i(j+2)\theta}
are always present in extending from
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
Wei71
]
, the
\widehat{\chi}_{\infty}^{-1}(z)
ensures the central character is correct, and
|\det(g_{\infty})|^{-s}
ensures
\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}
factors through
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
.

5.2.3. Comparison of classical and adelic

At infinity, for
j\geq 0
we shall henceforth take

(5.5)

\Phi_{\infty}=\Phi_{\infty}^{j+2}(x,y)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2^{-1-j}(x+iy)^{j+2}\exp(-\pi(x^{2}+y^{2})).

For this choice, by
[
LPSZ
, Prop. 10.1]
we have:

Proposition 5.2
.

If
\Phi=\Phi_{\infty}^{j+2}\cdot{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}^{2},\mathbb{C})
, for some
j\geq 0
and
\Phi_{f}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}}^{2},\mathbb{C})
, and
\chi
is a Dirichlet character, then the adelic and classical Eisenstein series are related by

E_{\Phi}\Big(g_{\mathrm{f}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}y&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right);\widehat{\chi},s\Big)=y^{\tfrac{j+2}{2}}\|\det g_{\mathrm{f}}\|^{s}\cdot\mathcal{E}^{j+2}_{\Phi_{f}}\Big(g_{\mathrm{f}},x+iy;\chi,s\Big)

for
g_{\mathrm{f}}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
and
x+iy\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

When
\Phi_{\infty}=\Phi_{\infty}^{j+2}
, for
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
we have

E_{\Phi}(g;\widehat{\chi},s)=\|\det(g)\|^{s}\mathcal{E}_{{\Phi_{\mathrm{f}}}}^{j+2}(g;\chi,s).

Proof.

We have
E_{\Phi}(gr(\theta),\widehat{\chi},s)=e^{i(j+2)\theta}E_{\Phi}(g,\widehat{\chi},s)
(cf.
[
Bum97
, §3,(7.35)]
); and
E_{\Phi}
left-translates as
\widehat{\chi}^{-1}_{\infty}(z)
under
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&\\
&z\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The result follows from
Proposition

5.2
.

5.2.4. The special value

We shall be chiefly interested in the special value

5.3. Betti–Eisenstein classes

5.3.1. Local systems and realisation maps

Betti local systems:

For
j\geq 0
, we let
\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j)}=\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j)}^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}}
denote the local system on the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
symmetric space associated to the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-representation

of highest weight
(0,-j)

De Rham local systems:
To compare our (Betti)-Eisenstein classes to the classical Eisenstein classes of Harder, we go through a comparison to de Rham cohomology. The local system

comprises the flat sections of a vector bundle
\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j),\mathrm{dR}}
with respect to the Gauss–Manin connection. This is defined over

The pullback of
\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j),\mathrm{dR}}
to the upper half-plane is
\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\otimes\det^{-j}
of the relative de Rham cohomology of

, so has a canonical section
(2\pi i\mathrm{d}z)^{\otimes j}
, for

a co-ordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.

Motivic local systems:

Our Betti–Eisenstein classes will be the Betti realisation of Beĭlinson’s motivic Eisenstein classes. For any level
\mathcal{U}
, there is a
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
-equivariant relative Chow motive

over

attached to the representation

of

; this gives a coefficient system for motivic cohomology. Then we have realisations

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(Y(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j)}(\mathbb{Q})(1)\right)\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{H}^{1}\left(Y(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j)}(\mathbb{Q})\right),

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{dR}}:\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{mot}}\left(Y(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j),\mathrm{mot}}(1)\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}\left(Y(\mathcal{U}),\mathscr{V}_{(0,-j),\mathrm{dR}}\right).

Base-changing to
\mathbb{C}
-coefficients, these are related by

(see e.g.
[

6.1. Sketch

Our goal is to define a vector-valued measure
\Xi^{[a]}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, taking values in the finitely-generated

-module

The values of this measure at locally-algebraic characters of

(of degree at most

\mathrm{GL}_{2}

We sketch the construction of

in the case

, so

is the trivial representation.
Let

, so

.
For

satisfying the

\operatorname{norm}_{n}^{{n+1}}(\xi_{n+1})=U_{p,1}^{\prime}\cdot\xi_{n},

where

is the natural projection map. Since

becomes invertible on the image of the idempotent

, we then define

factors through

For general

6.5. The Manin relations: compatibility in varying

Theorem 6.15

For

Since our classes are built up from the norm-compatible families

and
{}_{c}\xi^{[a,0]}_{n}

L_{1}

Remark 9.5

Y(W,\Phi;\mathrm{id},\chi_{2},s_{1},s_{2})=\chi_{2}(-1)\gamma(\pi\times\chi_{2}^{-1},\psi,s_{1}-s_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2})\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)}W(\iota(g))f_{\Phi}(w_{2}g)|\det g|^{s_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}}\ \mathrm{d}g.

Splitting into an integral over
N_{2}
and an integral over
N_{2}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, the
N_{2}
factor acts on
W
via
\psi
, so the integral over
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
equals

\int_{N_{2}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)}W(\iota(g))|\det g|^{s_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F}f_{\Phi}(w_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)g;\chi_{2},s_{2})\psi(x)\ \mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}g.

Since
w_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and
f_{\Phi}
transforms as
\chi_{2}(-1)
under
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, by
[
LPSZ
, §8.1]
the second integral is precisely
\chi_{2}(-1)W_{\Phi}(g;\chi_{2},s_{2})
. The
\chi_{2}(-1)
’s cancel; we conclude since

\displaystyle Y(W,\Phi;\mathrm{id},\chi_{2},s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\gamma(\pi\times\chi_{2}^{-1},\psi,s_{1}-s_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2})\int_{N_{2}\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)}W(\iota(g))W_{\Phi}(g;\chi_{2},s_{2})|\det g|^{s_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}g

\displaystyle=\gamma(\pi\times\chi_{2}^{-1},\psi,s_{1}-s_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2})Z(W,\Phi;\mathrm{id},\chi_{2},s_{1},s_{2}).\qed

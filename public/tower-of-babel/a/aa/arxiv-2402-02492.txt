Cohomology of ( φ , τ ) (\varphi,\tau) -modules
Hui Gao, Luming Zhao

Abstract
. We construct cohomology theories for ( φ , τ ) (\varphi,\tau) -modules, and study their relation with cohomology of ( φ , Γ ) (\varphi,\Gamma) -modules, as well as Galois cohomology. The method is axiomatic, and can treat the étale case, the overconvergent case, and the rigid-overconvergent case simultaneously. We use recent advances in locally analytic cohomology as a key ingredient.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
K
be a mixed characteristic complete discrete valuation field with perfect residue field of characteristic
p
, and let
{G_{K}}={\mathrm{Gal}}({\overline{K}}/K)
be the Galois group.
In
[
Fon90
]
, Fontaine introduces the
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules to classify
p
-adic representations of
{G_{K}}
. These modules are defined over a concrete “one-variable” ring, and are further equipped with a Frobenius operator
{\varphi}
and a
{\Gamma_{K}}={\mathrm{Gal}}({K_{p^{\infty}}}/K)
-action (here
{K_{p^{\infty}}}
is the cyclotomic extension adjoining all
p
-power roots of unity). Thus, the data of a Galois representation can now be described using just
two
matrices: one for
{\varphi}
and one for
\gamma
(a topological generator of
{\Gamma_{K}}
; for simplicity, the readers can assume
p>2
in the introduction). This very concrete description makes it possible to carry out explicit computations about Galois representations.

Let
V
be a
p
-adic Galois representation of
{G_{K}}
, and let
{\mathbb{M}}
be the associated
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-module. In
[
Her98
,
Her01
]
, Herr defines a three term complex

(1.1)

C_{{\varphi,{\Gamma_{K}}}}({\mathbb{M}}):=\quad[{\mathbb{M}}\xrightarrow{{\varphi}-1,\gamma-1}{\mathbb{M}}\oplus{\mathbb{M}}\xrightarrow{1-\gamma,{\varphi}-1}{\mathbb{M}}].

(Here the second arrow sends
(a,b)
to
(1-\gamma)(a)+(\varphi-1)(b)
).
Herr shows that this complex is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathrm{R}\Gamma({G_{K}},V)
; using this, Herr gives an explicit reproof of Tate’s theorems on cohomology of Galois representations (when
K/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is a finite extension).
In Herr’s work, he only considers étale
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules. In
[
CC98
]
, (rigid)-overconvergent
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules are constructed by Cherbonnier-Colmez; exactly the same formula as (
1.1
) gives rise to Herr complex for these variants of
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules. R. Liu
[
Liu08a
]
shows that these overconvergent complexes still compute Galois cohomology (when
K/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is a finite extension); in addition, Liu also studies cohomology of rigid-overconvergent
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules that are not necessarily associated with Galois representations.
These results are important tools in many aspects of
p
-adic Hodge theory.

In this paper, we study similar cohomological questions for the
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules. The
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules are first introduced by Caruso
[
Car13
]
(building on earlier work of Breuil
[
Bre99
]
), and recently have found many applications in
integral

p
-adic Hodge theory. Indeed, the Breuil–Kisin modules, first considered by Breuil
[
Bre99
]
then constructed in full generality by Kisin
[
Kis06
]
, can be regarded as a special case of
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules. The theory of Breuil–Kisin modules is a
fundamental tool
in every aspect of integral
p
-adic Hodge theory: they are used to construct (various) moduli of Galois representations
[
Kis08
,
EG23
]
, and inspire the construction of several unifying cohomology theories
[
BMS18
,
BMS19
,
BS22
]
.
Recently, overconvergent
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules are constructed in
[
GL20
,
GP21
]
by the first named author jointly with T. Liu and Poyeton respectively, in analogy with the overconvergent
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules constructed by Cherbonnier–Colmez. These overconvergent
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules have found several applications, e.g., in the study of Breuil–Kisin
{G_{K}}
-modules
[
Gao23
]
, and most recently in Hodge–Tate and
{\mathbb{B}_{\mathrm{dR}}^{+}}
-prismatic crystals
[
GMW23
,
GMW
]
.
As there will only be more applications of the
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules, it has thus become urgent to study other
foundational
properties of these modules. This paper grows out with these intended applications in mind, and fills some blanks in the literature.
In addition, our approach even leads to some new results for the cohomology of
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules (which has been such a widely-known theory for circa 20 years); for the curious readers, cf. Rem.
1.14
(
2
) and the historical and comparative Remarks
10.18
and
10.19
.

Before we discuss cohomology theories, we already need to point out that there are several different
variants
of
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules (similar to the
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules). We dub the three main genres with the adjectives: étale, overconvergent and rigid-overconvergent; these are defined over the Laurent ring, the overconvergent Laurent ring, and the Robba ring respectively.
In this introduction, to avoid introducing too many notations, we shall focus on the
rigid-overconvergent
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules
(also called the
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules over the Robba ring
). These are indeed the most useful variants in all the recent applications mentioned in the last paragraph, and have the richest cohomology theory.

To guide the readers (who might already have some experience with cohomology of
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules), we summarize our main discussions in this introduction.
In §
1.2
, we quickly recall
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules.
In §
1.3
(for any
p
), we produce a cohomology theory using
{\varphi}
-operator and group actions; this uses the least machinery and is not too difficult.
In §
1.4
, assuming
p>2
, we can refine results in §
1.3
by producing a 3-term
({\varphi},\tau)
-complex resembling Herr’s complex (
1.1
): however the terms are much more
complicated
in this case, making the
({\varphi},\tau)
-complex very unsatisfying.
Finally in §
1.5
, (for any
p
again), we produce a 3-term complex using
{\varphi}
-operator and
Lie algebra
actions. This is the most satisfying complex, and makes use of recent advances in
locally analytic cohomology
; we do warn that its cohomologies are “bigger” than desired but nonetheless can be “pruned”.

Construction 1.2
.

We now briefly recall the three variants of
(\varphi,\tau)
-modules. All rings here and in the introduction are reviewed in details in §
2
.

(1)

Recall that the
\varphi
-action of an
étale

(\varphi,\tau)
-module is defined over the fraction field of
\mathbf{A}_{K_{\infty}}=(W(k)[[u]][\frac{1}{u}])^{\wedge_{p}}
where
\wedge_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic completion; more concretely, it is the field

\mathbf{B}_{{K_{\infty}}}:=\{\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}a_{i}u^{i}:a_{i}\in K_{0},\lim_{i\rightarrow-\infty}v_{p}(a_{i})=+\infty,\text{ and }{\mathrm{inf}}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{p}(a_{i})>-\infty\}.

Here
v_{p}
is the valuation on
{\overline{K}}
such that
v_{p}(p)=1
.
The
\tau
-action is defined over a bigger filed “
\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{L}
” which we do not recall here, see §
2
.
Indeed, roughly speaking, a
(\varphi,\tau)
-module is a finite free
\mathbf{B}_{{K_{\infty}}}
-vector space
{\mathbf{D}}_{K_{\infty}}
equipped with certain commuting maps
\varphi:{\mathbf{D}}_{K_{\infty}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{D}}_{K_{\infty}}
and
\tau:\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{L}\otimes_{\mathbf{B}_{{K_{\infty}}}}{\mathbf{D}}_{K_{\infty}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathbf{B}}_{L}\otimes_{\mathbf{B}_{{K_{\infty}}}}{\mathbf{D}}_{K_{\infty}}
.
By
[
Car13
, Thm. 1]
, the étale
(\varphi,\tau)
-modules classify all Galois representations of
G_{K}
.

(2)

By
[
GL20
,
GP21
]
, étale
(\varphi,\tau)
-modules are
overconvergent
. That is, (roughly speaking), the
\varphi
-action can be defined over the sub-field:

\mathbf{B}_{K_{\infty}}^{\dagger}:=\{\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}a_{i}u^{i}\in\mathbf{B}_{K_{\infty}},\lim_{i\rightarrow-\infty}(v_{p}(a_{i})+i\alpha)=+\infty\text{ for some }\alpha>0\};

also, the
\tau
-action can be defined over some sub-field
{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}_{L}^{\dagger}\subset{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}_{L}
.

(3)

Let us introduce the following Robba ring (which contains
\mathbf{B}_{K_{\infty}}^{\dagger}
),

\begin{split}\mathbf{B}_{{\mathrm{rig}},K_{\infty}}^{\dagger}:=\{f(u)=\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}a_{i}u^{i},a_{i}\in K_{0},f(u)\text{ converges }\\
\text{ for all }u\in\overline{K}\text{ with }0<v_{p}(u)<\rho(f)\text{ for some }\rho(f)>0\}.\end{split}

Similar to
rigid-overconvergent

({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules (i.e.,
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules over the Robba ring) which are not necessarily étale, one can also define
rigid-overconvergent

({\varphi},\tau)
-modules, now with
\tau
-action defined over another ring
{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}
.

(cf. §
4
)
.

(Let
p
be any prime).
There is an equivalence between the category of

-modules over the Robba ring and the category of
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules over the Robba ring

\mathrm{Mod}_{\varphi,{\Gamma_{K}}}({\mathbb{B}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{\varphi,\tau}(\mathbf{B}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger},{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})

Here we use the blackboard font to denote rings and modules in

-module theory; the algebraic structure of

1.3.
Cohomology I:
{\varphi}+
group

A useful realization is that the Herr complex (
1.1
) can be interpreted using (continuous, as always in this paper) group cohomology. Indeed, the Herr complex
C_{{\varphi},{\Gamma_{K}}}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})
can be interpreted as

\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\Gamma_{K},{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})^{{\varphi}=1}

where
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\Gamma_{K},{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})=[{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}\xrightarrow{\gamma-1}{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}]
(say, when
p>2
) is the (continuous) group cohomology, and
{\varphi}=1
denotes the homotopy fiber of
{\varphi}-1
.
In more concrete terms, we are simply saying that
C_{{\varphi},{\Gamma_{K}}}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})
is the totalization of the double complex

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma-1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi-1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma-1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi-1}

We would like to imitate this in the
({\varphi},\tau)
-module case; however, a naive idea to replace “
\gamma-1
” by “
\tau-1
” would fail, because
\tau
-action on

{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger}
is not stable. Nonetheless, we have a
{\hat{G}}
-action on
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}
; thus we can form the complex

C_{{\varphi},{\hat{G}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}):=\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\hat{G}},\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})^{{\varphi}=1}

Let us explain this complex in more concrete terms (when
p>2
).
The
p
-adic Lie group
{\hat{G}}
is topologically generated by
\gamma
and
\tau
; using a Lazard–Serre resolution, cf. Lem.
3.4
,
\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\hat{G}},\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})
can be explicitly expressed by a three term complex

(1.2)

C_{\gamma,\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}):=\quad[\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}\xrightarrow{\gamma-1,\tau-1}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}\oplus\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}\xrightarrow{\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}-1,\delta-\gamma}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger}]

Here,
\chi
is cyclotomic character, and
\delta=\frac{\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}-1}{\tau-1}
is an element in the Iwasawa algebra of
{\hat{G}}
.
However, one needs to add in the
{\varphi}=1
fiber, and thus it would produce a
four
term complex
C_{{\varphi},\gamma,\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})
.
It turns out it is a correct complex.

Theorem 1.6

(cf. Thm.
10.10
)
.

(Let
p
be any prime).
There is a natural quasi-isomorphism

C_{{\varphi},{\hat{G}}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})\simeq C_{\varphi,{\Gamma_{K}}}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})

If these modules are associated to
V\in{\mathrm{Rep}}_{G_{K}}({\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
, then the complexes are further quasi-isomorphic to

.

Remark 1.7
.

We give some historical remarks; cf. §
10.7
for more.

(1)

In
[
TR11
]
, Tavares Ribeiro defines a similar
C_{{\varphi},{\hat{G}}}
-complex for étale
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules, and shows that it computes Galois cohomology. His proof is “direct” (using devissage and
\delta
-functors), and thus involves many complicated computations (which quite resembles Herr’s original approach
[
Her98
]
).

(2)

In contrast, our proof of Thm.
1.6
is
axiomatic
; it involves certain standard cohomology vanishing computations that work in the étale case as well, and thus recovers Tavares Ribeiro’s results in a very conceptual way.

(3)

The comparison

C_{\varphi,{\Gamma_{K}}}({\mathbb{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{p^{\infty}}}}^{\dagger})\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma({G_{K}},V)

was formerly known by
[
Liu08a
, Prop. 2.7]
, but only when
K/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is a finite extension; Liu’s method uses
\psi
-operator and does
not
work when
K/{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is an infinite extension. In contrast, our conceptual proof (which uses other complexes as well) works for any
K
, and does not use
\psi
-operator.

Remark 1.8
.

We find the 4-term complex
C_{{\varphi},\gamma,\tau}(\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})
unsatisfying in (at least) two ways:

(1)

Firstly,

, it is not obvious to see its cohomology is concentrated in degree

(2)

Secondly and more importantly, the module

Remark 1.10
.

We point our a thorny issue when
p=2
. In this case, the groups
{\mathrm{Gal}}(L/{K_{\infty}})
and
{\mathrm{Gal}}({K_{p^{\infty}}}/K)
are both open subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\times}
and hence are not necessarily pro-cyclic. In theory of
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules, one can first take a finite (and Galois) extension
K^{\prime}/K
inside
{K_{p^{\infty}}}
such that
{K_{p^{\infty}}}/K^{\prime}
is pro-cyclic, then study
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules over
K^{\prime}
, and finally descend to
K
-level. This strategy unfortunately breaks for
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules. Indeed, the uniformizer of
K^{\prime}
is different from
K
, and hence its Kummer tower extension is
completely
different from that of
K
. Indeed, even though it might still be possible to construct certain
explicit
Lazard–Serre resolution as in Lem.
3.4
when
p=2
, the complex is likely to be very complicated and unsuitable to construct simple
({\varphi},\tau)
-complexes as in Thm.
1.9
.

Even though we
fabricated
a 3-term complex in Thm.
1.9
, the module
{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger}
remains at large.
With this in mind, it is tempting to construct
C_{{\varphi},\tau}({\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger},\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},L}^{\dagger})
simply by replacing
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger}
in Eqn. (
1.3
) by
{\mathbf{D}}_{{\mathrm{rig}},{K_{\infty}}}^{\dagger}
.
Item (1) in the next Thm.
1.11
tells us that it is indeed not a whimsical thought; Item (2) then informs us that unfortunately (and somewhat surprisingly), this (natural) idea does
not
work.

Remark 2.3

(
“Integral overconvergent rings”
)
.

We discuss some very subtle (and confusing: at least to the authors)

Note that we have

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}[1/p]=({\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}[{1}/{[\overline{\pi}]}])[1/p]={\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}

(1)

.

(2)

Secondly and more seriously, as we shall see in Prop

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}[{1}/{[\overline{\pi}]}]
satisfies the TS-1 Axiom (due to
[

]
).

(3)

In “retrospect”, the possible “confusion” here comes from the

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{A}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}

{\widetilde{{\mathbf{B}}}}^{[r,+\infty]}
.
This notation system is first introduced (as far as the authors are aware) in
[

, and is also used in the paper
[
GP21
]
.
Note our

resp.

[

In this paper, we have continued to use the notation system of

]

We collect some formulae that we use often; one can easily verify them.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p>2
.

(1)

\gamma\tau\gamma^{-1}=\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}
.

(2)

Define

\delta=\frac{\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}-1}{\tau-1}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\binom{\chi(\gamma)}{n}(\tau-1)^{n-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\tau-1]].

Then we have

(\delta-\gamma)(\tau-1)=(1-\tau^{\chi(\gamma)})(\gamma-1).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p>2
.
Let
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]
be the Iwasawa algebra. We have the following exact sequence

{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\xrightarrow{(\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}-1,\delta-\gamma)}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\oplus{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\xrightarrow{(\gamma-1,\tau-1)}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\xrightarrow{\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\rightarrow 0

Here
\epsilon
is the augmentation map, and all other maps are defined using
right
multiplication.
As a consequence, if
{\widetilde{M}}
is an abelian group with continuous
{\hat{G}}
-action, then
\mathrm{R}\Gamma({\hat{G}},{\widetilde{M}})
is quasi-isomorphic to

C_{\gamma,\tau}({\widetilde{M}})=[\widetilde{M}\xrightarrow{\gamma-1,\tau-1}\widetilde{M}\oplus\widetilde{M}\xrightarrow{\tau^{\chi(\gamma)}-1,\delta-\gamma}\widetilde{M}]

Proof.

Note indeed the first three terms form a Lazard–Serre resolution of the trivial representation
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
, cf. e.g.
[
RJRC22
, Thm. 5.7]
.
To check exactness, the only non-trivial part is exactness at the second term.
Suppose
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\oplus{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]
such that

(3.2)

x(\gamma-1)=y(\tau-1).

It suffices to show
(\tau-1)\mid x
. Note
(\tau-1)\cdot{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]\subset{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[\hat{G}]]
is a two-sided ideal. Thus we can consider (
3.2
) in the quotient ring
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[(\gamma-1)]]
. Then it is obvious
\bar{x}=0
.
∎

3.2.
Locally analytic vectors

We review locally analytic vectors in this subsection, and
higher
locally analytic vectors in the next subsection. For simplicity (enough for our purpose), we always assume

•

G

compact

-adic Lie group.

Notation 3.5

(Locally analytic and pro-analytic vectors)
.

(1)

Let
(W,\|\cdot\|)
be a continuous
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
-Banach representation of
G
.
Let
W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}\subset W
denote the subset of locally analytic vectors. This definition naturally extends to the case where
W
is a LB representation.

(2)

cf.
[
Ber16
, Def. 2.3]
.
Let
W=\varprojlim_{i}W_{i}
be a Fréchet representation of

if its image in

is locally analytic for each

. This definition naturally extends to the case where
W
is a LF representation. We use
W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{pa}$}}
to denote the

Remark 3.6
.

We caution on the (subtle but serious) distinction between
locally analytic vectors
and
pro-analytic vectors
. In summary, our slogan is: for
arithmetic
purposes, we need LF representations and their
pro-analytic vectors
; but for
cohomological
questions, we always only use the LB representations and their
locally analytic vectors
.

(1)

The notion of locally analytic vectors can be defined in a very broad context, e.g., for any barreled locally convex representation as in
[
Tam15
]
(thus including LF representations). However, in general we have a
strict
inclusion:

W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}\subsetneq W^{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{pa}$}}

(2)

Most
locally analytic cohomology
theory (e.g.,
[
Tam15
]
,
[
RJRC22
]
) are developed for general
locally analytic
representations (even over a LF space). However, in general they do
not
work for the pro-analytic representations: intuitively, cohomology in general does not commute with limit.

(3)

We need the pro-analytic vectors for
arithmetic
purposes. For example, for
({\varphi},\Gamma)
-modules resp.
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules over the
Robba ring
, the relevant group actions are only
pro-analytic
.

(4)

As discussed in the introduction, our main theorems in this paper concern cohomology of
({\varphi},\tau)
-modules over the Robba ring, which as mentioned in Item (
3
) are only pro-analytic vectors. An important step in the process is to use the
{\varphi}
-operator to “adjust” to the locally analytic case; cf. Lem.
9.4
which will be
repeatedly
used in the final section §
10
.

(5)

With above items in mind, (and to avoid possible confusions), we have chosen to review theorems on locally analytic cohomology only for
LB (or even just Banach) representations
; cf. Thms.
3.9
and
3.13
.

3.3.
Higher locally analytic vectors

We quickly review the notion of higher locally analytic vectors.
We refer the readers to
[
Pan22
]
,
[
RJRC22
]
and
[
Por24
]
for details.
We start with the notion of higher
analytic
vectors. Consider a
p
-adic Lie group
N
such that there is a homeomorphism (of
p
-adic manifolds)
c:N\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. Let
W
be a
N
-Banach representation over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
(not necessarily a locally analytic representation).
There is an isometry

W\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(N,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\right)\cong\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(N,W\right),

where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(N,W\right)
is the space of
W

functions on
N
. (The analyticity condition here—not just locally analyticity—is the reason we require
N\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
).
We then have

\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(N,W\right)\right)^{N}\simeq W^{N\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}},\quad\text{ via }f\mapsto f(1);

this implies that the functor
W\mapsto W^{N\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}
is left exact. Thus we can define the right derived functors
for
i\geq 1
:

\mathrm{R}_{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}^{i}\left(W\right):=H^{i}\left(G,W\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(G,{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\right)\right).

Now let
G
be a general compact
p
-adic Lie group; there exists an open normal subgroup
G_{1}
such that
G_{n}:=G_{1}^{p^{n-1}}
are subgroups of
G_{1}
and there exists a homeomorphism
c:G_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
such that
c(G_{n})=(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{d}
for all
n
. See the paragraph below
[
BC16
, Prop 2.3]
for details.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
W
be a
G
-Banach representation over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. Define

\mathrm{R}_{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}^{i}\left(W\right):=\varinjlim_{n}\mathrm{R}_{G_{n}\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{an}$}}^{i}\left(W\right)=\varinjlim_{n}H^{i}\left(G_{n},W\widehat{\otimes}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}\left(G_{n},{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\right)\right).

These cohomology groups are called the higher locally analytic vectors of
W
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
W
be a
G
-Banach representation over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
.
Suppose

has no higher locally analytic vectors, that is

\mathrm{R}_{G\text{$\mbox{-}\mathrm{la}$}}^{i}(W)=0,\quad\forall i\geq 1.

H^{i}(G,W)\simeq H^{i}(G,W^{\mathrm{la}})\simeq H^{i}_{\mathrm{an}}(G,W^{\mathrm{la}})\simeq(H^{i}(\mathrm{Lie}G,W^{\mathrm{la}}))^{G}

Proof.

With Thm.
3.9
in mind, the only remaining comparison is between

and
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,W^{\mathrm{la}})
: see
[
RJRC22
, Cor. 1.6]
, or the all encompassing diagram
[
RJRC23
, Thm. 6.3.4]
.
For the comparison of cohomology groups, cf.
[
RJRC22

and
[
RJRC22
, Rem. 5.6]

∎

(A_{\infty},H)
be a Fréchet
H
-ring containing
A
as a subring. Say
(A,A_{\infty},H)
satisfies
Axiom (TS-1-Fréchet)
, if
A_{\infty}
can be written as an inverse limit

A_{\infty}=\varprojlim_{i\geq 0}(A_{i},v_{i})

where
(A_{i},v_{i})
is a sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-Banach algebras equipped with unitary
H
-actions, such that there are continuous and
H
-equivariant ring maps
A\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\beta_{i}}A_{i}
for each
i
, such that the valuations “
increase
” along these maps; namely,

(6.2)

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle v_{i}(\alpha_{i}(x)),\quad\forall x\in A;

(6.3)

\displaystyle v_{i+1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle v_{i}(\beta_{i}(y)),\quad\forall y\in A_{i+1}.

(Note (
6.2
) implies for each
i
, the triple
(A_{i},v_{i},H)
satisfies TS-1).

Proposition 6.7
.

Suppose
(A,A_{\infty},H)
satisfies Axiom TS-1-Fréchet (with
c_{1}
set as 1). Then

H^{1}(H,A_{\infty})=0

Proof.

This is an axiomatization of
[
Col08
, Prop. 10.2]
.

Let
h\mapsto c_{h}
be a continuous cocycle of
H
valued in
A_{\infty}
.
We will construct a sequence of elements
b_{n}\in A_{\infty}
for
n\geq 0
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

v_{i}(b_{n}-b_{n-1})\geq n,\forall i\leq n-2
(here, let
b_{-1}=0
);

(2)

v_{i}(c_{n,h})\geq n+2,\forall i\leq n-1,\forall h\in H
;
here
c_{n,h}:=c_{h}-(1-h)b_{n}
is the modified
cocycle
.

Once constructed, it is clear
b_{n}
converges (in the Fréchet topology) to some
b\in A_{\infty}
that trivializes the cocycle.

When
n=0
, take
b_{0}=0
. In this case, both conditions are vacuous.
Suppose now
b_{n}
is constructed, so we have

v_{n-2}(b_{n}-b_{n-1})\geq n,

and

(6.4)

v_{n-1}(c_{n,h})\geq n+2,\quad\forall h\in H.

Let
H^{\prime}\subset H
be an open subgroup such that

(6.5)

v_{n}(c_{n,\delta})\geq n+4,\quad\forall\delta\in H^{\prime}.

Fix a set of representatives
Q=\{\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{k}\}
for
H/H^{\prime}
.
By Axiom (TS-1) for
A
, there exists some
\alpha\in A^{H^{\prime}}
such that
v(\alpha)>-1
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau_{i}(\alpha)=1
.
Define the weighted summation

b_{Q}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}}

Define

b_{n+1}=b_{n}+b_{Q}

We now check the conditions, (it suffices to check for the maximal index
i
since “
v_{j}\geq v_{j+1}
” by (
6.3
)),

(1)

v_{n-1}(b_{n+1}-b_{n})=v_{n-1}(b_{Q})>(-1)+(n+2)=n+1
, because for each
i=1,\cdots,k
,

•

v_{n-1}(\tau_{i}(\alpha))=v_{n-1}(\alpha)\geq v(\alpha)>-1
, which uses (
6.2
);

•

v_{n-1}(c_{n,\tau_{i}})\geq n+2
by (
6.4
).

(2)

To check the second condition. Fix one
h\in H
, write
hQ=\{\tau_{1}\gamma_{1},\cdots,\tau_{k}\gamma_{k}\}
where
\gamma_{i}\in H^{\prime}
.
Note

\displaystyle c_{n+1,h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{h}-(1-h)(b_{n}+b_{Q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n,h}-(1-h)b_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n,h}+\sum_{i}h\tau_{i}(\alpha)hc_{n,\tau_{i}}-\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}h\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,h}+\sum_{i}h\tau_{i}(\alpha)hc_{n,\tau_{i}}-\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}},\quad\text{ using }\sum_{i}h\tau_{i}(\alpha)=1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(h\tau_{i}(\alpha)\cdot c_{n,h\tau_{i}})-\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}},\quad\text{ combine first two using cocycle condition }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}\gamma_{i}}-\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)c_{n,\tau_{i}},\quad\text{using expression of $hQ$, note $\gamma_{j}(\alpha)=\alpha$, re-order first term }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\tau_{i}(\alpha)\tau_{i}(c_{n,\gamma_{i}}),\quad\text{ using cocycle condition}

By (
6.5
),
c_{n,\gamma_{i}}
and hence
\tau_{i}(c_{n,\gamma_{i}})
has
v_{n}
-valuation
\geq n+4
, thus we can conclude

v_{n}(c_{n+1,h})>n+3

∎

10.

-modules and comparisons

In this section, we prove all the main theorems on cohomology comparisons. Some comparisons simply reduce to comparison of
{\varphi}
-cohomologies, which are fully studied in the previous section §

Indeed, in our approach, we shall first “break down” the roles of the many operators such as

Remark 10.1

(Routes of comparisons)
.

As there are
many
complexes, there are then many ways to pair and compare them.

(1)

In some (easiest) cases, one can use
{\varphi}
-operator
only
to obtain comparisons: see Step 1 in proof of Thm
10.6
.

(2)

In some other cases, one can use “group-operator”
only
to obtain comparisons: see Step 2 in proof of Thm
10.6
and Step 1 in proof of Thm
10.10
.

(3)

For the remaining cases, one need to make use of
{\varphi}
-operator
first
, and then apply “group-operator” techniques. See Step 2 in proof of Thm
10.10
; see also Thm
10.15
.

In many cases, the roles of
{\varphi}
-operator and group-operator cannot be “interchanged” or with their order “switched” (in Item (3) above); see e.g. Rem
10.7
and the (rather confusing) Rem
10.11
.

Remark 10.2

(No
\delta
-functors or
\psi
-operators)
.

Many cohomology comparisons on
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules (and

use
any of them. Note the comparison theorems in

,

In this subsection, we make some extensive historical comparisons on the study of cohomology of

Remark 10.18
.

We discuss the (rather involved) history of Herr complexes of (various)

-modules when they are associated to Galois representations. For simplicity, we only discuss results concerning

(1)

In

(2)

The complexes

Remark 10.19
.

We compare our methods with those in Rem.

.

(1)

The complexes

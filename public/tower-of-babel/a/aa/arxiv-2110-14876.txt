Rigidity of the sharp Bezout estimate on nonnegatively curved Riemann surfaces Thanks: 1 Research partially supported by GDNSF with contract no. 2021A1515010264 and NNSF of China with contract no. 11571215.
Chengjie Yu 1, Chuangyuan Zhang

Abstract
. In this short note, by using a general three circle theorem, we show the rigidity of the sharp Bezout estimate first found by Gang Liu on nonnegatively curved Riemann surfaces.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C55; Secondary 32A10

1.
Introduction

On a complete noncompact Kähler manifold
(M,g)
, the space of holomorphic functions on
M
is denoted as
\mathcal{O}(M)
. A function
f
on
M
is said to be of polynomial growth if there are some positive constants
C
and
d
such that

(1.1)

|f(x)|\leq C(1+r^{d}(x))\ \forall x\in M,

where
r(x)
the distance function to some fixed point. The space of holomorphic functions of polynomial growth is denoted as
\mathcal{P}(M)
, and the space of holomorphic functions on
M
satisfying (
1.1
) for some
C>0
is denoted as
\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)
. For any nonzero
f\in\mathcal{P}(M)
, the degree of
f
is defined as

(1.2)

\deg(f)=\inf\{d>0\ |\ f\in\mathcal{O}_{d}(M)\}.

For
x\in M
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(M)
,
{\rm ord}_{x}(f)
means the vanishing order of
f
at
x
.

The purpose of this short note is to give a proof of the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a noncompact Riemann surface equipped with a complete conformal metric
g
such that the Guassian curvature of
g
is nonnegative. Then, for any nonzero holomorphic function
f
of polynomial growth on
M
,

(1.3)

\sum_{x\in M}{\rm ord}_{x}(f)\leq\deg(f).

Moreover, the equality of (
1.3
) holds for some nonconstant holomorphic function
f
of polynomial growth if and only if
(M,g)
is biholomorphically isometric to
\mathbb{C}
equipped with the standard metric.

The estimate (
1.3
) was first obtained in the unpublished preprint
[
3
]
by Liu. It is clear that the estimate (
1.3
) is sharp because the equality of the estimate holds for any nonzero polynomial on
\mathbb{C}
. In this short note, we give an alternative proof of (
1.3
) using the following general three circle theorem on Riemann surfaces, in a similar spirit of
[
4
]
and characterize the rigidity of the estimate (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a Riemann surface and
\Omega
be an open subset of
M
. Let
u
and
v
be two continuous functions on
\Omega
that are subharmonic and supharmonic respectively. Suppose that
v:\Omega\to(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
is proper and
M_{v}(u,t)=\max_{x\in S_{v}(t)}u(x)
is an increasing function for
t\in(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
where
S_{v}(t)=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ v(x)=t\}
. Then,
M_{v}(u,t)
is a convex function of
t\in(\inf_{\Omega}v,\sup_{\Omega}v)
.

It seems that the original proof in
[
3
]
can not characterize the rigidity of the estimate (
1.3
). In higher dimension, there is an estimate in similar spirit which is not sharp in
[
1
, Proposition 7.2]
.

In algebraic geometry, the Bezout theorem relates the number of roots and the degrees of polynomials. So, an estimate such as (
1.3
) is called a Bezout estimate (See
[
5
, P. 244]
). The Bezout estimate (
1.3
) is stronger than the sharp vanishing order estimate:

(1.4)

{\rm ord}_{x}(f)\leq\deg(f)

for any nonzero
f\in\mathcal{P}(M)
and any
x\in M
. Vanishing order estimate that is not sharp was first obtained by Mok
[
5
]
on complete noncompact Kähler manifolds with certain geometric conditions. In
[
6
]
, Ni obtained the sharp estimate (
1.4
) under the assumptions of nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature and maximal volume growth. The second assumption of Ni’s result was later removed by Chen-Fu-Yin-Zhu
[
2
]
and finally the first assumption of Ni’ result was relaxed to nonnegative holomophic sectional curvature by Liu
[
4
]
. According to all these works, it seems that there must be a higher dimensional analogue of the sharp Bezout estimate (
1.3
).

2.
Proof of the theorem

Although the proof of the general three circle theorem (Theorem
1.2
) is almost the same as that of the classical Hadamard’s three circle theorem on
\mathbb{C}
, we give a proof to it for completeness.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For any
\inf_{\Omega}v<t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}<\sup_{\Omega}v
, let

(2.1)

\tilde{v}=\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{3})-M_{v}(u,t_{1})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}v+\frac{t_{3}M_{v}(u,t_{1})-t_{1}M_{v}(u,t_{3})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}.

Because
M_{v}(u,t_{3})-M_{v}(u,t_{1})\geq 0
,
\tilde{v}
is supharmonic. Moreover, it is clear that

\tilde{v}\geq u

on
S_{v}(t_{1})\cup S_{v}(t_{3})
. Hence, by maximum principle,

\tilde{v}\geq u

in
A_{v}(t_{1},t_{3}):=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ t_{1}<v(x)<t_{3}\}.
Thus, for any
x\in S_{v}(t_{2})
,

(2.2)

\begin{split}u(x)\leq&\tilde{v}(x)\\
=&\frac{M_{v}(u,t_{3})-M_{v}(u,t_{1})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}t_{2}+\frac{t_{3}M_{v}(u,t_{1})-t_{1}M_{v}(u,t_{3})}{t_{3}-t_{1}}\\
=&\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{3})+\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{1}).\\
\end{split}

Hence

(2.3)

M_{v}(u,t_{2})\leq\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{3})+\frac{t_{3}-t_{2}}{t_{3}-t_{1}}M_{v}(u,t_{1}).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Before the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a noncompact Riemann surface equipped a conformal metric
g
and
f
be a nonzero holomorphic function on
M
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
be
n
distinct roots of
f
,
m_{i}={\rm ord}_{p_{i}}(f)
and
\rho=\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}}
. Then,

(2.4)

\lim_{r\to{0^{+}}}\frac{\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)}{\log r}=1.

Here
r_{p}
means the distance function to
p
.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\min\{r(p_{i},p_{j})\ |\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
and
\delta_{0}\in(0,r_{0}]
be such that for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
and
x\in B_{p_{i}}(\delta_{0})
,

(2.5)

c_{1}r_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}}(x)\leq|f(x)|\leq C_{2}r_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}}(x)

for some positive constants
c_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Let
\rho_{0}=\delta_{0}^{N}
where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
. Then, for any
x\in M
such that
\rho(x)=r<\rho_{0}
,
x\in B_{p_{i}}(\delta_{0})
for some
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then, for any
j\neq i
, by the triangle inequality,

(2.6)

\delta_{0}<r(p_{i},p_{j})-r_{p_{i}}(x)\leq r_{p_{j}}(x)\leq r(p_{i},p_{j})+r_{p_{i}}(x)\leq R_{0}+\delta_{0}

where
R_{0}=\max\{r(p_{i},p_{j})\ |\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\}.
Thus,

(2.7)

\frac{r}{(R_{0}+\delta_{0})^{N-m_{i}}}\leq r_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}}(x)=\frac{\rho(x)}{r_{p_{1}}^{m_{1}}(x)\cdots\widehat{r_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}}(x)}\cdots r_{p_{n}}^{m_{n}}(x)}\leq\frac{r}{\delta_{0}^{N-m_{i}}}.

Combining this with (
2.5
), we know that

(2.8)

\frac{c_{1}r}{(R_{0}+\delta_{0})^{N-m_{i}}}\leq|f(x)|\leq\frac{C_{2}r}{\delta_{0}^{N-m_{i}}}.

So, for any
x\in M
with
\rho(x)=r<\rho_{0}
, we have

(2.9)

c_{3}r\leq|f(x)|\leq C_{4}r

where

c_{3}=\min\left\{\frac{c_{1}}{(R_{0}+\delta_{0})^{N-m_{i}}}\ \Bigg|\ i=1,2,\cdots,n\right\}

and

C_{4}=\max\left\{\frac{C_{2}}{\delta_{0}^{N-m_{i}}}\ \Bigg|\ i=1,2,\cdots,n\right\}.

Hence

(2.10)

\frac{C_{4}}{\log r}+1\leq\frac{\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)}{\log r}\leq 1+\frac{c_{3}}{\log r}

for any
r>0
small enough. Then, by the squeezing rule, we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a noncompact Riemann surface equipped with a complete conformal metric
g
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
be
n
distinct points on
M
,
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\rho=r_{p_{1}}^{m_{1}}r_{p_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots r_{p_{n}}^{m_{n}}
. Let
o
be a fixed point on
M
,

R_{0}=\max\{r(o,p_{i})\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then, for any holomorphic function
f
on
M
and any
r>(2R_{0})^{N}
,

(2.11)

M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{N}}-R_{0})\leq M_{\rho}(|f|,r)\leq M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{N}}+R_{0})

where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
and
M_{o}(|f|,r)=\max_{x\in S_{o}(r)}|f(x)|
with
S_{o}(r)=\partial B_{o}(r)
.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality

(2.12)

r_{o}(x)-R_{0}\leq r_{p_{i}}(x)\leq r_{o}(x)+R_{0},\ \forall x\in M,

for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then, for any
x
with
\rho(x)=r>(2R_{0})^{N}
, we have

(2.13)

(r_{o}(x)+R_{0})^{N}\geq\rho(x)=r>(2R_{0})^{N}

which implies that

(2.14)

r_{o}(x)>R_{0}.

Then, by the left hand side of (
2.12
),

(2.15)

(r_{o}(x)-R_{0})^{N}\leq\rho(x)=r.

Thus

(2.16)

r_{o}(x)\leq r^{\frac{1}{N}}+R_{0}

which means that
S_{\rho}(r)\subset B_{o}(r^{\frac{1}{N}}+R_{0})
for any
r>(2R_{0})^{N}
. Therefore, by the principle of maximal modulus for holomorphic functions,

(2.17)

M_{\rho}(|f|,r)\leq M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{N}}+R_{0}).

On the other hand, for any
x\in S_{o}(r)
, by the right hand side of (
2.12
),

(2.18)

\rho(x)\leq(r+R_{0})^{N}.

So,
S_{o}(r)\subset B_{\rho}((r+R_{0})^{N}):=\{x\in M\ |\ \rho(x)\leq(r+R_{0})^{N}\}
and hence

(2.19)

M_{o}(|f|,r)\leq M_{\rho}(|f|,(r+R_{0})^{N})

by the principle of maximal modulus for holomorphic functions again.
Thus

(2.20)

M_{\rho}(|f|,r)\geq M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{N}}-R_{0})

for any
r>R_{0}^{N}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
be
n
distinct roots of
f
with
m_{i}={\rm ord}_{p_{i}}(f)
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Let
\rho=r_{p_{1}}^{m_{1}}r_{p_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots r_{p_{n}}^{m_{n}}
. Then, by the Laplacian comparison, we know that

(2.21)

\Delta r_{p_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{r_{p_{i}}}

for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
in the sense of distribution. So,

(2.22)

\Delta\log r_{p_{i}}\leq 0

on
M\setminus\{p_{i}\}
in the sense of distribution which means that
\log r_{p_{i}}
is a supharmonic function on
M\setminus\{p_{i}\}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. So
\log\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\log r_{p_{i}}
is a supharmonic function on
\Omega=M\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}\}
. Moreover, it is clear that
\log(|f|^{2}+\epsilon)
is a subharmonic function on
M
. Then, by Theorem
1.2
,
\log M_{\rho}(|f|^{2}+\epsilon,r)
is a convex function of
\log r
. Letting
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
, we know that
\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)
is a convex function of
\log r
. Then,
\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)-\log r
is also a convex function of
\log r
. Therefore, for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}
,

(2.23)

\begin{split}&\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{2})-\log r_{2}\\
\leq&\frac{\log r_{3}-\log r_{2}}{\log r_{3}-\log r_{1}}(\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{1})-\log r_{1})+\frac{\log r_{2}-\log r_{1}}{\log r_{3}-\log r_{1}}(\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{3})-\log r_{3}).\end{split}

Letting
r_{1}\to 0^{+}
in the last inequality, and using Lemma
2.1
. we get

(2.24)

\begin{split}\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{2})-\log r_{2}\leq\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{3})-\log r_{3}\end{split}

for any
0<r_{2}<r_{3}
. Thus, letting
r_{2}=1
in (
2.24
), we have

(2.25)

M_{\rho}(|f|,r)\geq M_{\rho}(|f|,1)r

for any
r>1
. Let
o\in M
be a fixed point and
R_{0}=\max\{r(o,p_{i})\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n\}
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
and (
2.25
), for any
r
large enough,

(2.26)

M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{N}}+R_{0})\geq M_{\rho}(|f|,r)\geq M_{\rho}(|f|,1)r.

Hence, for any
r
large enough,

(2.27)

M_{o}(|f|,r)\geq M_{\rho}(|f|,1)(r-R_{0})^{N}.

So

(2.28)

\deg(f)\geq N=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm ord}_{p_{i}}(f).

This completes the proof of the Bezout estimate (
1.3
).

We next come to prove the rigidity of (
1.3
). Let
f
be a nonconstant holomorphic function of polynomial growth such that equality of (
1.3
) holds. Let
d=\deg(f)
. It is then clear from Cheng’s Liouville theorem for harmonic functions that
d\geq 1
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
be all the distinct roots of
f
, and
m_{i}={\rm ord}_{p_{i}}(f)
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then
d=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we know that

(2.29)

\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{d}}-R_{0})}{\log r}\leq\frac{\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)}{\log r}\leq\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{d}}+R_{0})}{\log r}

when
r
is large enough. Moreover, note that

(2.30)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{d}}+R_{0})}{\log r}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,t)}{d\log(t-R_{0})}=1

and

(2.31)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r^{\frac{1}{d}}-R_{0})}{\log r}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,t)}{d\log(t+R_{0})}=1

where we have used the following conclusion proved in
[
4
]
:

(2.32)

\deg(f)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{o}(|f|,r)}{\log r}

for any nonzero
f\in\mathcal{P}(M)
. Thus, by the squeezing rule,

(2.33)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)}{\log r}=1.

Then, by letting
r_{3}\to+\infty
in (
2.23
), we get

(2.34)

\begin{split}\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{2})-\log r_{2}\leq\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r_{1})-\log r_{1}\end{split}

for any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. Combining this with (
2.24
), we know that

\log M_{\rho}(|f|,r)-\log r=C

for some constant
C
. For each
r>0
, let
z_{r}\in S_{\rho}(r)
achieve the maximal modulus of
f
on
S_{\rho}(r)
and

(2.35)

F(x)=\log|f(x)|-\log\rho(x)-C.

Then
F\leq 0
and
F(z_{r})=0
. So,
F
as a subharmonic function on
\Omega
achieves its maximum value on
z_{r}
. By strong maximum principle for subharmonic functions, we know that
F\equiv 0
and hence

(2.36)

\log\rho(x)=\log|f(x)|-C

is harmonic
\Omega
since
\Delta\log|f|=0
on
\Omega
. So

(2.37)

\Delta\log r_{p_{1}}=\Delta\log r_{p_{2}}=\cdots=\Delta\log r_{p_{n}}=0

and the equality of the Laplacian comparison (
2.21
) holds. Thus
(M,g)
is flat and hence is biholomorphically isometric to
\mathbb{C}
or a cylinder. Note that a cylinder admits no nonconstant holomorphic function of polynomial growth. So,
(M,g)
is biholomorphically isometric to
\mathbb{C}
.
∎

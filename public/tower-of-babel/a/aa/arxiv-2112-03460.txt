A Response to Economics as Gauge Theory
Timothy Nguyen

Abstract
We provide an analysis of the recent work by Malaney-Weinstein on “Economics as Gauge Theory” presented on November 10, 2021 at the Money and Banking Workshop hosted by University of Chicago. In particular, we distill the technical mathematics used in their work into a form more suitable to a wider audience. Furthermore, we resolve the conjectures posed by Malaney-Weinstein, revealing that they provide no discernible value for the calculation of index numbers or rates of inflation. Our conclusion is that the main contribution of the Malaney-Weinstein work is that it provides a striking example of how to obscure simple concepts through an uneconomical use of gauge theory.

\mathbf{O}_{t}=\mathbf{O}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}}
are each a foliation of

given by the level sets of
\mathbf{C}_{t}
. The corresponding minimal price basket function
\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}}^{\mathbf{P}_{t}}
for a utility

can be found from the method of Lagrange multipliers by solving the system of equations

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{q_{1}}F_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})&=0\,\\
\partial_{q_{2}}F_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})&=0\\
q_{1}^{t}q_{2}&=u\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(2.32)

where
F_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})=q_{1}+q_{2}+\lambda(q_{1}^{t}q_{2}-u)
. A simple computation shows that the minimizer, as a function of
u
, is given by
q_{t}^{*}=(q_{1,t}^{*},q_{2,t}^{*})
where

\displaystyle q_{1,t}^{*}(u)

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{1}{t+1}}u^{\frac{1}{t+1}}

(2.33)

\displaystyle q_{2,t}^{*}(u)

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{t}{t+1}}u^{\frac{1}{t+1}}.

(2.34)

It is more convenient to express this minimizer in terms of cost rather than utility. We have via Definition

\displaystyle C_{\mathbf{C}_{t}}(u,\mathbf{P}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{t}(q^{*}_{t}(u))

(2.35)

\displaystyle=q_{1,t}^{*}(u)+q_{2,t}^{*}(u)

(2.36)

\displaystyle=\left(t^{\frac{1}{t+1}}+t^{-\frac{t}{t+1}}\right)u^{\frac{1}{t+1}}.

(2.37)

Hence, denoting the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle q_{1,t}^{*}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{tc}{1+t}

(2.38)

\displaystyle q_{2,t}^{*}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{1+t}.

(2.39)

Note that by definition of
c

\mathbf{P}_{t}(q_{t}^{*}(c))=c.

(2.40)

Question:
What is a sensible way to compute the price index at time
t
relative to the (prior) base time
t_{a}=1
given the evolving preferences
\mathbf{O}_{t}
and prices
\mathbf{P}_{t}
?

Answering this question involves two parts: deciding which reference basket of goods to consider for the base time and then deciding upon a fair comparison of goods at a future time. For the first of these, while the computation of a single index number uses a single reference basket, the approach considered here is to consider all possible baskets (as parameterized by the minimal baskets in
\mathrm{im}\,(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{P}_{t}}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}})
), each of which will give rise to their own (not necessarily the same) price index. For the second part, we have the notion of a
welfare map
(see Figure
3
)

\mathcal{W}_{t,t_{a}}:\mathbf{O}_{t_{a}}\to\mathbf{O}_{t}

(2.41)

mapping indifference curves at time
t_{a}
to those at time
t
(two curves related by
\mathcal{W}
are understood to represent equal amounts of welfare). Because an indifference curve can be specified by its minimal price basket, a welfare map can also be expressed as a correspondence between such minimal price baskets:

\mathcal{W}_{t,t_{a}}:\mathrm{im}\,\!\!\left(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{P}_{t_{a}}}_{\mathbf{C}_{t_{a}}}\right)\to\mathrm{im}\,\!\!\left(\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{P}_{t}}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}}\right).

(2.42)

In our present example, the set of minimal price baskets at any given time is especially simple. Based on (
2.38
)–(
2.39
), as the cost
c
varies, the minimal baskets at time
t
sweeps out the line

\mathrm{im}(\mathbf{X}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}})=\{(q_{1},q_{2}):q_{2}=q_{1}/t\}.

(2.43)

In the (unnecessary) bundle setting described above, welfare maps are determined by a choice of parallel transport operator, which can either be the one given by
[
MW21
, Section 7]
, the naive or default “parallel equals constant” parallel transport operator, or else any other parallel transport operator in principle. But we can study these welfare maps directly without such sophisticated machinery as we will now do.

Consider the naive welfare maps
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{naive}}_{t,t_{a}}
given by the naive parallel transport operator. It says that we should treat costs as constant in time, in which case we have
C^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{naive}}_{t,t_{a}}(C)
if and only if the indifference curves
C^{\prime}
and
C
have the same cost with respect to
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{P}_{t}}_{\mathbf{C}_{t}}
and
\mathbf{X}^{\mathbf{P}_{t_{a}}}_{\mathbf{C}_{t_{a}}}
respectively. Indeed, this is exactly expressing the parallel condition with respect to the trivial connection. Rephrasing this in terms of minimal price baskets, we have

\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{naive}}_{t,t_{a}}(q^{*}_{t_{a}}(c))=q^{*}_{t}(c).

(2.44)

Next, consider the welfare map
\mathcal{W}^{MW}_{t,t_{a}}
proposed by Malaney-Weinstein. Then

\mathcal{W}^{MW}_{t,t_{a}}(q^{*}_{t_{a}}(c))=q^{MW}_{t}(c),

(2.45)

for some
q^{MW}_{t}(c)
lying on the line
q_{2}=t^{-1}q_{1}
. We have

q^{MW}_{t}(c)=q^{*}_{t}(\Upsilon_{t,t_{a}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}(c))

(2.46)

because by definition
\Upsilon_{t,t_{a}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}(c)
is the cost adjustment needed to induce parallelism with respect to the Malaney-Weinstein connection.

The resulting COLA price indices can be computed as follows. For the naive welfare map, we have the ratio of prices

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{naive}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{t}(q^{*}_{t}(c))}{\mathbf{P}_{t_{a}}(q^{*}_{t_{a}}(c))}

(2.47)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{c}

(2.48)

\displaystyle=1

(2.49)

for every
c
. This is expected of our naive welfare map: if prices do not change and we naively assume that constant welfare across times equates to constant cost, then the price index stays fixed at
1
.

For the Malaney-Weinstein welfare map, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{MW}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{t}(q^{MW}_{t}(c))}{\mathbf{P}_{t_{a}}(q^{*}_{t_{a}}(c))}

(2.50)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{t}\left(q^{*}_{t}(\Upsilon_{t,t_{a}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}(c))\right)}{\mathbf{P}_{t_{a}}(q^{*}_{t_{a}}(c))}

(2.51)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Upsilon_{t,t_{a}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}(c)}{c}

(2.52)

precisely recovering Corollary 1 of
[
MW21
]
. Note however there is nothing distinguished about the Malaney-Weinstein connection in deriving (
2.52
). Every connection gives a corresponding parallel transport operator, i.e. cost adjustment operator, and so the derivation
(
2.50
)–(
2.52
) holds for any connection
, where
\Upsilon_{t,t_{a}}^{\tilde{\alpha}}
is the corresponding cost-adjustment operator of that particular connection.

In plain English therefore, Corollary 1 of
[
MW21
]
asserts the following:

The most general cost of living adjustment index is given by

the ratio of an arbitrarily chosen adjusted price to a base price.

(2.53)

There is nothing surprising about that statement, except possibly by obscuring it through a choice of price adjustment that invokes infinite dimensional gauge theory. While our above analysis of Corollary 1 was done for our model example, it extends straightforwardly to the general case.

By the general setup of gauge theory, parallel transport operators are equivalent to the choice of a covariant derivative. 4
Conclusion

In this paper, we resolve the conjectures of Malaney-Weinstein by disentangling the gauge theory from the economics. While the application of gauge-theory to economics could potentially be fruitful (and the author would be very enthusiastic about such a positive outcome), the particular attempt of
[
MW21
]
in its current state falls far short of what could be called a success. Namely, the complexity that went into the constructions of
[
MW21
]
far exceeded the gains of what was produced:

•

Corollary 1 is a tautology as can be verified directly from our reformulation (
2.53
).

•

Conjecture 1 asserts that a certain way of relating dynamic preferences is unique in some sense, but that sense is not sufficiently defined and so renders Conjecture 1 false. Even if Conjecture 1 were to become true through an adequate clarification of the “constructible” criterion, further work would be required to establish any theoretical or practical significance.

•

Conjecture 2 is a statement about all possible ways of relating dynamic preferences. It is automatically true as posed from a gauge-theoretic perspective. It too has no theoretical or computational consequences. In fact, because it is a statement about all possible dynamic preferences, as seen by (
3.1
), it is a statement about all possible ways of obtaining a price index. It thus offers no insights into how to calculate particular price indices. Thus, from a practical standpoint, Conjecture 2 is vacuous.

We hope that the technical analysis we have provided here enables those who wish to verify for themselves our conclusions about
[
MW21
]
.

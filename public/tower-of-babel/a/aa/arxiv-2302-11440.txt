Abstract
. We show that, if a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian n n -manifold N N admits a non-constant quasiregular mapping from the Euclidean n n -space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , then the de Rham cohomology algebra H dR ∗ ​ ( N ) H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N) of N N embeds into the exterior algebra ⋀ ∗ ℝ n {\bigwedge}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n} . As a consequence, we obtain a homeomorphic classification of closed simply connected quasiregularly elliptic 4 4 -manifolds.

1.
Introduction

In this article we prove the following result on the de Rham cohomology of quasiregularly elliptic manifolds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N
be a closed quasiregularly elliptic
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Then there exists an embedding of graded algebras
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A connected and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
N
is called
quasiregularly elliptic
if there exists a non-constant quasiregular mapping
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
. A continuous map
f\colon M\to N
between oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds,
n\geqslant 2
, is
quasiregular
if
f
belongs to the Sobolev space
W_{{\operatorname{loc}}}^{1,n}(M,N)
and there exists a constant
K\geqslant 1
for which

(1.1)

\lVert Df\rVert^{n}\leqslant KJ_{f}\quad\text{ a.e. in }M;

here
\lVert Df\rVert
is the operator norm of the weak differential
Df
and
J_{f}
is the Jacobian determinant of
f
.

Theorem
1.1
may be viewed as a quasiconformal analog of a classical result in degree theory:
For a non-zero degree mapping
f\colon M\to N
between orientable closed manifolds, the induced map
f^{*}\colon H^{*}(N)\to H^{*}(M)
in cohomology is an embedding of graded algebras.
This analogy can be taken further by observing that, if there exists a piecewise linear branched covering
\mathbb{T}^{n}\to N
, which is quasiregular with respect to smooth structures of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
N
, then
N
is a quasiregularly elliptic.

Theorem
1.1
has an antecedent in Kangasniemi’s theorem
[
12
]
:
If a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
N
admits a non-constant and non-injective uniformly quasiregular mapping
N\to N
, then there exists an embedding of graded algebras
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Regarding Kangasniemi’s theorem, we recall that a quasiregular self-map is
uniformly quasiregular
if the distortion inequality (
1.1
) holds for all the iterates of the map with the same constant. We also note that such closed manifolds are quasiregularly elliptic; see Martin, Mayer, and Peltonen
[
17
]
.

In terms of known results on the cohomology of closed quasiregularly elliptic manifolds, Theorem
1.1
extends a theorem of Prywes
[
24
]
:
Let
N
be a closed quasiregularly elliptic
n
-manifold. Then
\dim H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\leqslant\dim\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}=\binom{n}{k}
. This dimension bound for the de Rham cohomology theorem is sharp as the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is quasiregularly elliptic. Prywes’s theorem answers a question of Gromov
[
7
, p. 200]
whether there exists closed simply connected manifolds, which are not quasiregularly elliptic. This is indeed the case as, for example,
\#^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
is not quasiregularly elliptic by Prywes’s theorem. We refer to Bonk and Heinonen
[
1
]
for a previous result on distortion dependent bounds on the de Rham cohomology of quasiregularly elliptic manifolds. We also recall a theorem of Peltonen
[
22
]
which shows that connected sums
\mathbb{T}^{n}\#N
, where
N
is not a rational homology sphere, are not quasiregularly elliptic.

We also note in passing that dimension bounds on the de Rham cohomology in Prywes’s theorem do not hold for open quasiregularly elliptic manifolds. Indeed, for any finite set
P\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
there exists a quasiregular mapping
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
for which
f\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus P
; see
[
5
]
. Thus, it is known that the
(n-1)
-cohomology of an open quasiregularly elliptic manifold
N
may have arbitrarily large finite dimension. The dependence of the dimension of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
on the distortion constant is not known in general.

1.1.
Classification of closed simply connected quasiregularly elliptic
4
-manifolds

Our main application of Theorem
1.1
is a classification of closed simply connected quasiregularly elliptic
4
-manifolds.

In dimensions
n=4m
, Theorem
1.1
imposes upper bounds for the middle-dimensional Betti numbers
\beta^{+}_{2m}(N)
and
\beta^{-}_{2m}(N)
given by positive and negative definite subspaces of the intersection form
H^{2m}(N;\mathbb{R})\times H^{2m}(N;\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
of a closed quasiregularly elliptic manifold
N
. In particular, in dimension
n=4
, both Betti numbers
\beta^{+}_{2}(N)
and
\beta^{-}_{2}(N)
are at most three. Therefore the classification of smooth, closed, simply connected, and oriented
4
-manifolds yields that
N
is homeomorphic to one of the manifolds

(1.2)

\#^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})\quad\text{or}\quad\#^{j}\mathbb{C}P^{2}\#^{i}\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}},

where
k,j,i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
\#^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})=\#^{0}\mathbb{C}P^{2}\#^{0}\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}=\mathbb{S}^{4}
.

Piergallini and Zuddas show in
[
23
]
that these manifolds are quasiregularly elliptic. Indeed, by a result Piergallini and Zuddas
[
23
]
, for the manifolds
N
in (
1.2
) there exists a piecewise linear branched covering
\mathbb{T}^{4}\to N
, where
\mathbb{T}^{4}
and
N
have the standard PL structure compatible with the usual smooth structure. Thus composing a such branched covering
\mathbb{T}^{4}\to N
with a locally isometric Riemannian covering map
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{T}^{4}
, we obtain the required quasiregular mapping
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to N
.

Combining Theorem
1.1
with this result of Piergallini and Zuddas we obtain the following classification.

Corollary 1.2
.

A closed simply connected
4
-manifold is quasiregularly elliptic if and only if it is homeomorphic to either
\#^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
or
\#^{j}\mathbb{C}P^{2}\#^{i}\overline{\mathbb{C}P^{2}}
for some
k,j,i\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

Similar classifications of quasiregularly elliptic manifolds, based on general classification theorems for manifolds, are known in dimensions two and three. In dimension two, the result is classically deduced from uniformization of Riemann surfaces and the Stoïlow theorem for quasiregular mappings. In dimension three, the classification follows from Perelman’s solution to the Geometrization conjecture and a growth bound for the fundamental group. This reduction of the classification to the Geometrization conjecture is due to Jormakka
[
11
]
. We recall briefly these results from the point of view of Prywes’s theorem and Theorem
1.1
.

In dimension
n=2
, a closed quasiregularly elliptic
2
-manifold
N
satisfies
\dim H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\leqslant 2
. Since
N
is oriented, we immediately observe that
N
is homeomorphic to either
\mathbb{S}^{2}
or
\mathbb{T}^{2}
by classification of orientable closed surfaces.

For a closed quasiregularly elliptic
3
-manifold
N
, we deduce from Prywes’s theorem that
\dim H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)=\dim H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\leqslant 3
. By elementary algebra, we obtain from Theorem
1.1
that
\dim H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\neq 2
. Thus
N
has the de Rham cohomology of one of the manifolds
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, or
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. This homological result is sharp in the sense that, by Jormakka’s classification theorem
[
11
]
, the only closed quasiregularly elliptic
3
-manifolds are
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, and their quotients.
We find it interesting that, in fact, all closed quasiregularly elliptic manifold in dimensions two and three admit a branched covering from a torus.

In dimension four, to our knowledge, the only known examples of closed simply connected quasiregularly elliptic
4
-manifolds, before the result of Piergallini and Zuddas, were
\mathbb{S}^{4}
,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
\#^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
, and
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
; here the first two are given by elementary maps and the latter two are given by constructions of Rickman
[
28
]
and Luisto–Prywes
[
16
]
, respectively.

1.2.
Quasiregular mappings from a ball

Theorem
1.1
admits a reformulation for quasiregular mappings from a unit ball into closed manifolds. This reformulation in turn yields a cohomological Montel theorem for quasiregular mappings satisfying a doubling condition. For the statements, let
N
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold, let
K\geqslant 1
and
D>1
be constants, and let
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
be a family of all non-constant quasiregular mappings
f\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
satisfying the distortion inequality (
1.1
) with constant
K
and an additional doubling condition

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\leqslant D\int_{B^{n}}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

Here and in what follows,
B^{n}
and
B_{2}^{n}
denote the Euclidean balls of radius
1
and
2
centered at the origin, respectively. In what follows, we also denote

A(f)=\int_{B^{n}}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, for which

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}A(f)=\infty.

Then there exists an embedding of graded algebras
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We conclude that, if
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)
is not a subalgebra of
\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists a uniform upper bound for the area
A(f)
, and hence also for the energy, of mappings
f
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
. This uniform upper bound replaces the degree bound in the Gromov’s compactness theorem for quasiregular maps
[
21
]
and, as a consequence of these two results, we obtain the following cohomological version of Montel’s theorem for quasiregular maps in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
; see Rickman
[
26
, Corollary IV.3.14]
for Rickman’s Montel theorem for quasiregular mappings without an additional doubling assumption.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. If
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)
is not a subalgebra of
\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
is compact with respect to the topology of local uniform convergence. In particular, in this case,
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
is a normal family.

We finish this introduction by stating a more general result on weak limits of normalized Jacobians of mappings in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
. This statement may be viewed as non-dynamical version of Kangasniemi’s absolute continuity theorem for invariant measures for uniformly quasiregular mappings
[
13
]
. For the statement, for each
f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
, let
\nu_{f}
be the measure

\nu_{f}=\frac{1}{A(f)}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

We call
\nu_{f}
the
normalized measure of
f
. Note that, clearly,
\nu_{f}\ll\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

For a closed manifold
N
, which is not a rational homology sphere, we have the following dichotomy for sequences in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
; for the statement, note that a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
need not have a locally uniformly converging subsequence.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere.
Then a sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
has a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
for which either
(f_{j_{i}})
converges to a constant map or the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j_{i}}}
converge vaguely to a measure
\nu\ll\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
in
B_{2}^{n}
.

Theorem
1.5
is sharp in the sense that, for
N=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, there exists a sequence of mappings
(f_{j}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n})
for which the sequence
(\nu_{f_{j}})
of normalized measures converges vaguely to a measure
\nu
, which is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Recall that measures
(\nu_{f_{j}})

converge vaguely to
\nu
if
\int\varphi\nu_{f_{j}}\to\int\varphi\nu
for each
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.5
holds also for covering spaces of closed manifolds which are not rational homology spheres. In particular, Theorem
1.5
holds for quasiregular mappings in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

1.3.
Idea of the proof of Theorem
1.3

Since Theorem
1.1
reduces to Theorem
1.3
, we discuss now the ideas in terms of Theorem
1.3
. To that end, let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold and let

h\colon H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N),\quad c\mapsto h_{c},

be the mapping which associates to each de Rham class
c\in H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)
its unique harmonic representative
h_{c}\in c
. The mapping
h
is typically only a graded linear mapping and not an algebra homomorphism, since harmonic forms typically do not form an algebra. We refer to Kotschick
[
14
]
for a discussion on so-called
formal Riemannian manifolds
, whose harmonic forms form an algebra, and to Kangasniemi
[
12
]
for the role of formal measurable Riemannian metrics in the uniformly quasiregular dynamics.

We define, for each
f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
, a normalized pull-back

f^{\#}\colon H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n}),\quad H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\ni c\mapsto A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}f^{*}(h_{c}),

where
A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}=1
for
k=0
and
\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is the graded algebra

\mathscr{W}^{k}(B_{2}^{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}),&\text{for }0<k\leqslant n\\
\ker(d\colon\Omega^{0}(B_{2}^{n})\to\Omega^{1}(B_{2}^{n})),&\text{for }k=0\end{array}\right.

of Sobolev forms on
B_{2}^{n}
. In particular,
\mathscr{W}^{0}(B_{2}^{n})
consists merely of constant functions on
B_{2}^{n}
. Here
W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the partial Sobolev space of
\frac{n}{k}
-integrable
k
-forms on
B_{2}^{n}
; see Section
3
for details.

It is easy to observe that
f^{\#}
is an injective graded linear mapping and not an algebra homomorphism unless
h
is an algebra homomorphism. Nevertheless, if
(f_{j})
is such a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
that
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and that the sequence
(f_{j}^{\#}|_{H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{k}(B_{2}^{n}))
converges weakly for each
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, then the limit operators yield an embedding of graded algebras.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and
f_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

f_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then
L
extends to an embedding of graded algebras

L\colon H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})

satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}L([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])=1.

Having Theorem
1.7
at our disposal, Theorem
1.3
follows by showing that, for each sequence
(f_{j})
satisfying
A(f_{j})\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence whose normalized pull-backs converge weakly for each
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
,
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, to a linear map
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since these linear maps yield an embedding of algebras
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
, it suffices then to observe that there exists a set of positive measure
E\subset B_{2}^{n}
for which the map

H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to{\bigwedge}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad c\mapsto(L(c))(x_{0}),

is a well-defined embedding of graded algebras for
x_{0}\in E
.

2.
Reduction of Theorem
1.1
to Theorem
1.3

In this section, we discuss the reduction of Theorem
1.1
to Theorem
1.3
. Since both Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
hold trivially if the target manifold is a rational homology sphere, the reduction is based on the following result; see also
[
24
, Section 4]
.

For the statement, we say that a quasiregularly elliptic manifold
N
is
K
-quasiregularly elliptic
if there exists a non-constant quasiregular mapping
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfying the distortion inequality (
1.1
) with constant
K
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N
be a closed
K
-quasiregularly elliptic
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere. Then there exists a constant
D=D(n)>1
for which

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}A(f)=\infty.

We deduce Proposition
2.1
from the following two results. The first result is a lower growth bound for quasiregular mappings into manifolds with non-trivial homology due to Bonk and Heinonen
[
1
]
; see also
[
8
]
for the corresponding result in the setting of quasiregular curves.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 1.11]

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a non-constant quasiregular map satisfying the distortion inequality (
1.1
) with constant
K
. If
N
is not a rational homology sphere, then there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,K)>0
satisfying

\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{-\varepsilon}\int_{B^{n}(r)}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}>0.

The second result is a version of Rickman’s Hunting Lemma
[
25
, Lemma 5.1]
. The following formulation is due to Bonk and Poggi-Corradini
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
2
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\mu
be an atomless Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\infty
and
\mu(B)<\infty
for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there exists a constant
D=D(n)>1
with the property that, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

m\leqslant\mu(2B)\leqslant D\mu(B).

Proposition
2.1
is an immediate consequence of these results.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Since
N
is
K
-quasiregularly elliptic, there exists a non-constant quasiregular map
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfying the distortion inequality (
1.1
) with constant
K
. By Theorem
2.2
, the Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
\mu(E)=\int_{E}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
2.3
. Hence, there exist a constant
D=D(n)>1
and a sequence of balls
(B^{n}(a_{m},r_{m}))
for which

m\leqslant\int_{B^{n}(a_{m},2r_{m})}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\leqslant D\int_{B^{n}(a_{m},r_{m})}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

Now the maps
f_{m}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
,
x\mapsto f(r_{m}x+a_{m})
, form a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
A(f_{m})\to\infty
. The claim follows.
∎

3.
Sobolev algebra
\mathscr{W}^{*}
and normalized pull-backs

We begin by recalling briefly the necessary preliminaries on Sobolev differential forms. A
(k+1)
-form
d\omega\in L_{{\operatorname{loc}}}^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k+1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the
weak exterior derivative
of a
k
-form
\omega\in L_{{\operatorname{loc}}}^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\omega\wedge d\varphi=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\omega\wedge\varphi

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k-1}(B_{2}^{n})
. By
[
10
, Lemma 3.6]
, the exterior derivative commutes with the pullback of a quasiregular map. More precisely, let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold and let
f\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
be a quasiregular map. Then, for
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
, the form
f^{*}(d\alpha)\in L^{\frac{n}{k+1}}_{{\operatorname{loc}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k+1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the weak exterior derivative of
f^{*}\alpha\in L^{\frac{n}{k}}_{{\operatorname{loc}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, i.e.,
d(f^{*}\alpha)=f^{*}(d\alpha)
; here the integrability of
f^{*}\alpha
and
f^{*}(d\alpha)
follow from the point-wise inequality

\left\lvert f^{*}\beta\right\rvert\leqslant(\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\circ f)\lVert Df\rVert^{\ell}

for
\beta\in\Omega^{\ell}(N)
.

Let
W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the Sobolev space of forms
\omega
belonging to
L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
having a weak exterior derivative
d\omega\in L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k+1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose now that
f\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
is a quasiregular map with integrable Jacobian, that is,
J_{f}\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n})
. Then, for a closed form
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
, the pull-back
f^{*}\alpha
belongs to
W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
since
f^{*}\alpha
is weakly closed.

Let
f^{!}\colon\Omega^{*}(N)\to\oplus_{k=0}^{n}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the linear map

\Omega^{k}(N)\ni\alpha\mapsto A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}f^{*}\alpha,

where
A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}=1
for
k=0
; recall that

A(f)=\int_{B^{n}}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

It is easily seen that
f^{!}
commutes with the exterior product and that
f^{!}\circ h=f^{\#}
. We also note that
f^{!}(\mathrm{vol}_{N})
is the normalized measure
\nu_{f}
of
f
.

Next, we record an elementary lemma stating that, if
f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
, then for
k
-forms the operator
f^{!}
is bounded from
\Omega^{k}(N)\to L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the operator norm of
f^{!}
is bounded by
K
and
D
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, and let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
. Then

\lVert f^{!}(\alpha)\rVert_{\frac{n}{k},B_{2}^{n}}\leqslant DK\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}

for every
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
and for
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

The claim holds trivially for
\alpha\in\Omega^{0}(N)
as
\left\lvert f^{!}(\alpha)\right\rvert(x)=\left\lvert\alpha(f(x))\right\rvert
for each
x\in B_{2}^{n}
. Hence, let
0<k\leqslant n
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
. Then

\displaystyle\lVert f^{!}(\alpha)\rVert_{\frac{n}{k},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle=A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\left\lvert f^{*}\alpha\right\rvert^{\frac{n}{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\lVert Df\rVert^{n}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}K^{\frac{k}{n}}A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}\left(\int_{B_{2}^{n}}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\right)^{\frac{k}{n}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}K^{\frac{k}{n}}D^{\frac{k}{n}}\leqslant DK\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We finish this section by showing that limits of exact forms are negligible under sequences
(f_{j}^{!})
, if
(f_{j})
is a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
A(f_{j})\to\infty
; see also
[
24
]
. For a similar observation, based on integration by parts, in the context of entire maps and in quasiregular dynamics see
[
20
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, and let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
A(f_{j})\to\infty
. Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{!}(d\alpha)=0

for
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
,
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k-1}(B_{2}^{n})
, and
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

We may assume that
0\leqslant k<n
. Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
and let
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k-1}(B_{2}^{n})
. By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{!}(d\alpha)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad=A(f_{j})^{-\frac{k+1}{n}}\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{*}(d\alpha)\right\rvert=A(f_{j})^{-\frac{k+1}{n}}\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{*}\alpha\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad=A(f_{j})^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{!}(\alpha)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{C(n)}{A(f_{j})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\left\lvert d\varphi\right\rvert\left\lvert f_{j}^{!}(\alpha)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{C(n)}{A(f_{j})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\lVert d\varphi\rVert_{\frac{n}{n-k},B_{2}^{n}}\lVert f_{j}^{!}(\alpha)\rVert_{\frac{n}{k},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\frac{C(n)}{A(f_{j})^{\frac{1}{n}}}\lVert d\varphi\rVert_{\frac{n}{n-k},B_{2}^{n}}DK\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}.

The claim follows.
∎

4.
Limits of normalized pull-backs are Sobolev-Poincaré limits

We begin by recalling the Poincaré operator of Iwaniec and Lutoborski
[
9
, Section 4]
:
There exists a graded compact linear operator

T\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
\mathrm{id}=dT+Td
. In particular,
dT(\omega)=\omega
for every weakly closed form
\omega\in\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The following lemma is a reformulation of
[
24
, Lemma 4.1]
. We recall the proof for the reader’s convenience. Recall also that, for a quasiregular map
f\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
,

f^{\#}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n}),\;H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\ni c\mapsto A(f)^{-\frac{k}{n}}f^{*}(h_{c})=(f^{!}\circ h)(c).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
f_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

f_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then there exist a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
and a graded linear operator

\widehat{L}\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
T(f_{j_{i}}^{\#})\to\widehat{L}
. In particular,
d\widehat{L}=L
.

Proof.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
be a basis of
\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
. Since

\lVert f_{j}^{\#}(c_{\ell})\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{\ell}},B_{2}^{n}}\leqslant DK\lVert h(c_{\ell})\rVert_{\infty},

where
c_{\ell}\in H^{k_{\ell}}(N)
, for each
\ell=1,\ldots,m
by Lemma
3.1
and
T
is a compact operator, there exist a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
and forms
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{m}\in\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
T(f_{j_{i}}^{\#}(c_{\ell}))\to\tau_{\ell}
. Since
dT(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{\ell}))=f_{j}^{\#}(c_{\ell})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\tau_{\ell}\wedge d\varphi

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}T(f_{j_{i}}^{\#}(c_{\ell}))\wedge d\varphi=(-1)^{k_{\ell}}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}f_{j}^{\#}(c_{\ell})\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k_{\ell}}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}L(c_{\ell})\wedge\varphi

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k_{\ell}}(B_{2}^{n})
. Hence
d\tau_{\ell}=L(c_{\ell})
. We define
\widehat{L}
to be the linear map defined by
c_{\ell}\mapsto\tau_{\ell}
. The claim follows.
∎

Motivated by Lemma
4.1
we introduce the following notion. Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, and let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
. We say that a graded linear operator

\widehat{L}\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

is a
Sobolev–Poincaré limit of
(f_{j}^{\#})
if
d\widehat{L}
is the weak limit of the operators
f_{j}^{\#}
and
\widehat{L}
is the limit of the operators
T(f_{j}^{\#})
.

The weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit commutes with the wedge product in a weak sense; see also
[
24
, Lemma 4.2]
. For the statement, we recall that a sequence
(\mu_{j})
of Radon measures on
B_{2}^{n}

converges vaguely to a Radon measure
\mu
on
B_{2}^{n}
if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
and let the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
have a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Let also
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
satisfy
k_{1}+k_{2}\leqslant n
. Then, for every
c_{1}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{1}}(N)
and
c_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{2}}(N)
, we have
f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})
. Here, the weak convergence is the usual weak convergence in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{1}+k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
and the vague convergence of measures if
k_{1}+k_{2}=n
.

Proof.

Let
c_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{i}}(N)
for
i=1,2
. By Lemma
3.1
, the sequence
(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))=(f_{j}^{!}(h(c_{1})\wedge h(c_{2}))
is bounded in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{1}+k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
\Omega_{0}^{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\frac{n}{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\frac{n}{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}}
-norm if
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-norm. Thus, it suffices to prove that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}(B_{2}^{n})
.

Let
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}(B_{2}^{n})
. By the triangle inequality, it suffices to estimate the terms

\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge(d\widehat{L}(c_{1})-f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1}))\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})\right\rvert

and

\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge(d\widehat{L}(c_{2})-f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\right\rvert.

Since

\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})\in L^{\frac{n}{k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{n-k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\frac{n}{n-k_{1}}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{n-k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}(c_{1})
in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{1}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the first term converges to zero. For the second term, since
dT(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))=f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge(d\widehat{L}(c_{2})-f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge d(\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge(\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(n,\varphi)\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\left\lvert f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\right\rvert.

By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we further have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\left\lvert f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\right\rvert\left\lvert\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\lVert f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\rVert_{\frac{n}{n-k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}\lVert\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(n,k_{1},k_{2})\lVert f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{1}},B_{2}^{n}}\lVert\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(n,k_{1},k_{2},K,D,c_{1})\lVert\widehat{L}(c_{2})-T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}.

Since
T(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2}))\to\widehat{L}(c_{2})
in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the claim follows.
∎

Next, we prove that the exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit commutes with the wedge product provided that the normalizations grow without bound.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and let the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
have a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then

d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})

for every
c_{1}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{1}}(N)
and
c_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{2}}(N)
with
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
satisfying
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
.

Proof.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
satisfy
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
. Let
c_{1}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{1}}(N)
and
c_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{2}}(N)
. Then there exists a form
\tau\in\Omega^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}(N)
for which
h(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=h(c_{1})\wedge h(c_{2})+d\tau
. Since
f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})
in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{1}+k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge(f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})+f_{j}^{!}(d\tau))

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k_{1}-k_{2}}(B_{2}^{n})
by Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
4.2
. The claim follows.
∎

The following result shows that, if the target manifold
N
is not rational homology sphere and
(f_{j})
is a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
A(f_{j})\to\infty
, then a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
of the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
yields a vague limit for the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j}}
. Furthermore, the limiting measure is an absolutely continuous measure; see Sections
8
and
9
for further discussion on the vague convergence of normalized measures.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and let the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
have a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then there exists a non-negative form
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
for which
\nu_{f_{j}}\rightharpoonup\nu_{d\widehat{L}}
vaguely as measures.

Proof.

Since
N
is not a rational homology sphere, there exist, by the Poincaré duality, an index
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
and forms
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
and
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(N)
for which
\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then

\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(N)}{\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}}d\widehat{L}(c)\wedge d\widehat{L}(c^{\prime})

is a well-defined
n
-form in
L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which we consider also as a measure.

Let
\tau\in\Omega^{n-1}(N)
be a form satisfying

\mathrm{vol}(N)h(c)\wedge h(c^{\prime})=\left(\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}\right)(\mathrm{vol}_{N}+d\tau).

Since
\lVert f_{j}^{!}(\mathrm{vol}_{N})\rVert_{1,B_{2}^{n}}\leqslant D
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-norm, it suffices to prove that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{f_{j}}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{d\widehat{L}}

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
. By Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{d\widehat{L}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(N)}{\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi f_{j}^{\#}(c)\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(N)}{\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi f_{j}^{!}(h(c))\wedge f_{j}^{!}(h(c^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi f_{j}^{!}(\mathrm{vol}_{N}+d\tau)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{f_{j}}

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
. Since the forms
\nu_{f_{j}}
are non-negative, so is
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}
.
∎

We finish this section by showing that, when restricted to the closed unit ball
\bar{B}^{n}
, the vague convergence of measures in Theorem
4.4
improves into weak convergence of probability measures.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and let the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
have a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Let
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the limit measure in Theorem
4.4
. Then
\nu_{f_{j}}\llcorner\bar{B}^{n}\rightharpoonup\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\llcorner\bar{B}^{n}
. In particular,
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}(B^{n})=1
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta\in C(\bar{B}^{n})
be a non-negative function. Let
\eta\in C_{b}(B_{2}^{n})
be a non-negative bounded function satisfying
\eta|_{\bar{B}^{n}}=\zeta
. Then
\eta\nu_{f_{j}}\rightharpoonup\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}
vaguely as measures.

Let
0<r<1<R<2
. Let
\psi_{r}\in C_{0}(B^{n})
and
\psi_{R}\in C_{0}(B^{n}(0,R))
satisfy
\chi_{B^{n}(0,r)}\leqslant\psi_{r}\leqslant 1
and
\chi_{B^{n}}\leqslant\psi_{R}\leqslant 1
. Since
\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}
is non-negative, we obtain

\int_{\bar{B}^{n}(0,r)}\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\leqslant\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{r}\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{r}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}

and

\int_{B^{n}(0,R)}\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\geqslant\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{R}\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{R}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}.

Since
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}(B^{n})\geqslant\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}(B^{n})=\eta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}(\bar{B}^{n})\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}\eta\nu_{f_{j}}(B^{n}).

Thus

(4.1)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\zeta\nu_{f_{j}}=\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\zeta\nu_{d\widehat{L}}.

Equality (
4.1
) is then obtained for an arbitrary function
\zeta\in C(\bar{B}^{n})
by writing
\zeta=\max(\zeta,0)-\max(-\zeta,0)
.

Especially, since
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have, for
\zeta=1
, that

\int_{B^{n}}\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\nu_{f_{j}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\nu_{f_{j}}=1.

∎

5.
Weak exterior derivatives of Sobolev–Poincaré limits extend to algebra homomorphisms

In this section, we prove that, if
N
is not a rational homology sphere and
\widehat{L}
is a Sobolev–Poincaré limit induced by a sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
A(f_{j})\to\infty
, then the map
\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
defined by

H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\ni c\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{constant function }h_{c},&\text{for }k=0\\
d\widehat{L}(c),&\text{for }1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1\\
\mathrm{vol}(N)^{-1}(\int_{N}c)\nu_{d\widehat{L}},&\text{for }k=n\end{array}\right.

is a graded algebra homomorphism; here
\nu_{d\widehat{L}}
is the limit
n
-form in Theorem
4.4
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and let the operators
(f_{j}^{\#})
have a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then
\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}
is a graded algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

Clearly
\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}
is a graded linear map. It remains to prove that
\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}
is an algebra homomorphism. Let
c_{1}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{1}}(N)
and
c_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{2}}(N)
,
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
k_{1},k_{2}>0
and that
k_{1}+k_{2}\leqslant n
. If
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
, then, by Lemma
4.3
, we have that

\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})=\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1})\wedge\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{2}).

Suppose now that
k_{1}+k_{2}=n
and let
\tau\in\Omega^{n-1}(N)
be a form satisfying

\mathrm{vol}(N)h(c_{1})\wedge h(c_{2})=\left(\int_{N}c_{1}\wedge c_{2}\right)(\mathrm{vol}_{N}+d\tau).

By Proposition
4.2
, Lemma
3.2
, and Theorem
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1})\wedge\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi f_{j}^{\#}(c_{1})\wedge f_{j}^{\#}(c_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{N}c_{1}\wedge c_{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(N)}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi f_{j}^{!}(\mathrm{vol}_{N}+d\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{N}c_{1}\wedge c_{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(N)}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{f_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{N}c_{1}\wedge c_{2}}{\mathrm{vol}(N)}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\nu_{d\widehat{L}}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
. Thus
\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1})\wedge\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{2})=\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Limits of normalized pull-backs are algebra monomorphisms

In this section, we recall the statement of Theorem
1.7
and give its proof.

Theorem
1.7
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and
f_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

f_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H^{k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then
L
extends to an embedding of graded algebras

L\colon H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})

satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}L([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])=1.

Proof.

We may assume that
N
is not a rational homology sphere. By Lemma
4.1
, the operator
L
is the weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
of the operators
(f_{j_{i}}^{\#})
for some subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
. By Proposition
5.1
, the map
\Phi_{L}=\Phi_{d\widehat{L}}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is an algebra homomorphism and
\Phi_{L}
extends
L
by definition. Moreover, by definition and Corollary
4.5
, we have

\int_{B^{n}}\Phi_{L}([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])=\nu_{d\widehat{L}}(B^{n})=1.

It remains to prove that
\Phi_{L}
is injective. Since
\Phi_{L}([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])\neq 0
, it suffices to show that
\Phi_{L}
is injective on
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
. Let
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
be non-zero. There exists
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(N)
for which
\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}\neq 0
. Since

\Phi_{L}(c)\wedge\Phi_{L}(c^{\prime})=\Phi_{L}(c\wedge c^{\prime})=\frac{\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}}{\mathrm{vol}(N)}\Phi_{L}([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])\neq 0,

we have that
\Phi_{L}(c)\neq 0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we recall the statement of Theorem
1.3
and finish its proof.

Theorem
1.3
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, for which

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}A(f)=\infty.

Then there exists an embedding of graded algebras
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}A(f)=\infty
and
\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
is finite-dimensional, by Lemma
3.1
and the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, there exist a sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
and an operator

L\colon\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}),

which is the weak limit of the sequence
(f_{j}^{\#})
. By Theorem
1.7
, the operator
L
extends to an embedding of graded algebras
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}L([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])=1.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
be a basis of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
. We fix point-wise Borel representatives for
L([\mathrm{vol}_{N}])
,
L(c_{\ell_{1}}\wedge c_{\ell_{2}})
, and
L(c_{\ell_{1}})\wedge L(c_{\ell_{2}})
, where
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1,\ldots,m
. Let

E=\{x\in B^{n}\colon(L([\mathrm{vol}_{N}]))(x)\neq 0\}

and

E_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}=\{x\in B^{n}\colon(L(c_{\ell_{1}}\wedge c_{\ell_{2}}))(x)=(L(c_{\ell_{1}}))(x)\wedge(L(c_{\ell_{2}}))(x)\}

for
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1,\ldots,m
. Since the set
E
has positive
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure and the sets
E_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
have full
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in
B^{n}
, the set
E^{\prime}=\cap_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{m}E\cap E_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
has positive
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

Let
x_{0}\in E^{\prime}
. Now the map

\mathscr{L}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to{\bigwedge}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad c\mapsto(L(c))(x_{0}),

is a graded algebra homomorphism, which is injective on
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)
. If
N
is a rational homology sphere, then
\mathscr{L}
is trivially a monomorphism. If
N
is not a rational homology sphere, the injectivity of
\mathscr{L}
follows from the non-degeneracy of the pairing
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\times H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-*}(N)\to\mathbb{R}
,
(c,c^{\prime})\mapsto\int_{N}c\wedge c^{\prime}
.
∎

7.1.
Remark on the use of harmonic representatives

We defined operators
f^{\#}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
as composition
f^{\#}=f^{!}\circ h
, where
h\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N)
is the section of
\ker d\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
,
\alpha\mapsto[\alpha]
, assigning to each cohomology class its harmonic representative. In fact, we could have taken any section
\xi
of
\ker d\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
,
\alpha\mapsto[\alpha]
, and defined a normalized
\xi
-pullback as composition
f^{\xi}=f^{!}\circ\xi
. The limits of normalized
\xi
-pullbacks are independent of the section
\xi
in the following sense.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, and let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
A(f_{j})\to\infty
. Let
\xi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N)
be a section of
\ker d\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
,
\alpha\mapsto[\alpha]
, and let

L,L^{\prime}\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})

be weak limits of
f_{j}^{\#}
and
f_{j}^{\xi}
, respectively. Then
L=L^{\prime}
.

The proof is a simple modification of the proof of Lemma
4.1
since, for each
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
, the form
\xi(c)-h(c)
is exact. We leave the details to the interested reader.

7.2.
Remark on the non-uniqueness of the embedding
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})

Unless the manifold
N
is a rational homology sphere, the embeddings of graded algebras
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
in Theorem
1.7
are non-unique. Indeed, given a sequence
(f_{j})
for which the sequence
f_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
in the sense of Theorem
1.7
which admits a linear map
Q\in\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
Q^{*}L\neq L
, then
(f_{j}\circ Q)
is a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
(f_{j}\circ Q)^{\#}\rightharpoonup Q^{*}L\neq L
in the sense of Theorem
1.7
.

Even if we restrict ourselves to embeddings
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
associated to sequences
(f_{j}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N)
, which stem from a single quasiregular map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
, the uniqueness of the limit is not guaranteed. For statements of positive and negative results, let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a non-constant quasiregular map and consider the family of all mappings
f_{a,r}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
,
x\mapsto f\circ T_{a,r}
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r>0
, and
T_{a,r}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the mapping
x\mapsto rx+a
.

We begin with an observation that, for the Riemannian covering map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the induced algebra homomorphism
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is unique.

Example 7.2
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(e^{ix_{1}},\ldots,e^{ix_{n}})
, be the standard Riemannian covering map and let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
be the
1
-forms for which
\pi^{*}\theta_{i}=dx_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let also
L_{\pi}\colon H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
the embedding
[\theta_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_{i_{k}}]\to m_{n}(B^{n})^{-\frac{k}{n}}dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots dx_{i_{k}}
, where
m_{n}(B^{n})=\int_{B^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
.

Since
\pi
is a Riemannian covering map, it is
1
-quasiregular. We also have that all the mappings
\pi_{a,r}=\pi\circ T_{a,r}|_{B_{2}^{n}}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
are
1
-quasiregular. It is also easy to deduce that
\pi_{a,r}^{\#}=L_{\pi}
for each
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. Indeed, it suffices to observe that

\displaystyle\pi_{a,r}^{!}(\theta_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A(\pi\circ T_{a,r})^{k/n}}T_{a,r}^{*}\circ\pi^{*}(\theta_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\theta_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{k}m_{n}(B^{n})^{\frac{k}{n}}}T_{a,r}^{*}(dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{n}(B^{n})^{\frac{k}{n}}}dx_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_{i_{k}}

for each multi-index
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
.

We modify now the Riemannian covering mapping
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
in a sequence of Euclidean balls to obtain a quasiregular map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
for which the induced embedding
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is non-unique.

Example 7.3
.

Let
Q\in\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a linear map, which is not the identity. Let also
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a geodesic in
\mathrm{SO}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
from the identity to
Q
. We denote
Q_{t}=\alpha(t)
for each
t\in[0,1]
. Let also
B_{j}=B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})=B^{n}(2^{j}e_{1},j)
be a sequence of mutually disjoint Euclidean balls for which
r_{j}\to\infty
.

We define now
h_{Q}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows. In the complement of the union
\bigcup_{j}B_{j}
, the mapping
h_{Q}
is the identity. For
x\in B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j}/2)
, we set
h_{Q}(x)=a_{j}+r_{j}Q((x-a_{j})/r_{j})
. Finally, if
x\in B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})\setminus B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j}/2)
, we set
h_{Q}(x)=a_{j}+r_{j}Q_{2-2|x-a_{j}|/r_{j}}((x-a_{j})/r_{j})
. Since
\alpha
is a geodesic and each
Q_{t}
is an isometry, the mapping
h_{Q}
is quasiconformal.

Let now
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the quasiregular map
f=\pi\circ h_{Q}
. Then, the sequence
(f_{-je_{1},j})
yields the limiting embedding
L_{\pi}
and the sequence
(f_{a_{j},r_{j}/2})
the limiting embedding
Q^{*}L_{\pi}\neq L_{\pi}
.

8.
Example of a singular limit of normalized measures of quasiregular mappings
B_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}

In this subsection, we show that, for
n\geqslant 2
, there exist a constant
K=K(n)\geqslant 1
and maps
f_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,3}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
for which the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j}}
converge vaguely to the Borel measure
\nu
,

E\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}(E\cap J),

where
J=[-1,1]\times\{0\}^{n-1}
. Since
\nu
is singular with respect to the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, this example shows that Theorem
1.5
does not hold for quasiregular mappings into
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For the construction of the maps
f_{j}
, see also
[
19
, Example 24]
.

Let
\iota\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the stereographic projection and let
F\colon\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the restriction of the map
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{C}
,
(x,re^{i\theta})\mapsto(x,re^{i3\theta})
. We remark that
F(z)=z
for points
z
on the equator
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
and that the map
F
is
K
-quasiregular for
K=K(n)\geqslant 1
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
and for
i=1,\ldots,j
, let
a_{ij}=(-1+(2i-1)/j,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B_{ij}=B^{n}(a_{ij},1/j)
, and
U_{j}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}B_{ij}\subset B^{n}
. Let also
\rho_{ij}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the mapping
x\mapsto j(x-a_{ij})
. Finally, let
\sigma_{ij}
be the Möbius transformation
of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
satisfying
\sigma_{ij}\circ\iota=\iota\circ\rho_{ij}
.

We define now
f_{j}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
to be the mapping
f_{j}(x)=\iota(x)
for
x\notin U_{j}
and
f_{j}(x)=\sigma_{ij}^{-1}(F(\sigma_{ij}(\iota(x))))
for
x\in B_{ij}
and each
i=1,\ldots,j
. Each map
f_{j}
is
K
-quasiregular and, in each ball
B_{ij}
, we have that

\int_{B_{ij}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}=\int_{\sigma_{ij}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})}2+\int_{\sigma_{ij}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n})}1=\mathrm{vol}(\sigma_{ij}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}))+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),

where
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}_{-}^{n}
are the upper and lower hemispheres of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, respectively. Hence

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\leqslant 3j\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\leqslant 3\int_{B^{n}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

Thus
f_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,3}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
.

It remains to show that the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j}}
converge vaguely to
\nu
. Let
\psi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
. Denote

\mu_{f_{j}}:=\left(\int_{U_{j}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\right)^{-1}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{ij}:=\int_{J\cap B_{ij}}\psi\;\mathrm{d}\nu.

Since

\frac{1}{A(f_{j})}\int_{B_{2}^{n}\setminus U_{j}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\leqslant\frac{1}{j}

for each
j
, it suffices to show that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{U_{j}}\psi\mu_{f_{j}}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi\;\mathrm{d}\nu.

Since
\sigma_{ij}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n})=\iota(B^{n}(a_{ij},1/j))
and
\iota
is
\tilde{L}
-bilipschitz in
B^{n}
, where
\tilde{L}=\tilde{L}(n)
, we have that

C^{\prime}(n)\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{L}j}\right)^{n}+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\leqslant\int_{B_{ij}}f_{j}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}\leqslant C^{\prime}(n)\left(\frac{\tilde{L}}{j}\right)^{n}+\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n})

and thus

\left\lvert\int_{B_{ij}}\mu_{f_{j}}-\frac{1}{j}\right\rvert\leqslant C(n)\left(\frac{1}{j}\right)^{n+1}.

Hence, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{U_{j}}\psi\mu_{f_{j}}-\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi\;\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{B_{ij}}\psi\mu_{f_{j}}-\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{J\cap B_{ij}}\psi\;\mathrm{d}\nu\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left\lvert\int_{B_{ij}}\psi\mu_{f_{j}}-\psi_{ij}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left\lvert\int_{B_{ij}}(\psi-j\psi_{ij})\mu_{f_{j}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert j\psi_{ij}\int_{B_{ij}}\mu_{f_{j}}-\psi_{ij}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sup_{B_{ij}}\left\lvert\psi-j\psi_{ij}\right\rvert\int_{B_{ij}}\mu_{f_{j}}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}\left\lvert\int_{B_{ij}}\mu_{f_{j}}-\frac{1}{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sup_{B_{ij}}\left\lvert\psi-j\psi_{ij}\right\rvert C(n)\frac{1}{j}+\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}C(n)\left(\frac{1}{j}\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j}\sup_{B_{ij}}\left\lvert\psi-j\psi_{ij}\right\rvert C(n)+\lVert\psi\rVert_{\infty}C(n)\left(\frac{1}{j}\right)^{n}.

Since
\psi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
, it holds that
\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j}\sup_{B_{ij}}\left\lvert\psi-j\psi_{ij}\right\rvert\to 0
. Thus, we obtain that that the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j}}
converge vaguely to the singular measure
\nu
.

9.
Weak limits of normalized measures

In this section, we prove the following result, which yields Corollary
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
stated in the introduction.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere, i.e.
H^{k}(N)\neq 0
for some
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Then, up to passing to a subsequence, a sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
\sup_{j}A(f_{j})<\infty
converges locally uniformly to a
K
-quasiregular map
f\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
.

Theorem
9.1
implies Corollary
1.4
almost immediately.

Corollary
1.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
. If
H^{*}_{\mathrm{dR}}(N)
is not a subalgebra of
\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
is compact with respect to the topology of local uniform convergence. In particular, in this case,
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
is a normal family.

Proof.

Since the closure
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
of quasiregular mappings in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
in the topology of local uniform convergence consists of
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
and constant mappings
B_{2}^{n}\to N
, it suffices to show that a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
has a subsequence converging either to a mapping in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
or to a constant mapping.

Since
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
is not a subalgebra of
\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}A(f)<\infty

by Theorem
1.3
and that
N
is not a rational homology sphere. Thus, by Theorem
9.1
, each sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
has a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
converging locally uniformly to a (possibly constant) quasiregular map
B_{2}^{n}\to N
in
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
.
We conclude that the family
\overline{\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)}
is compact.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
9.1
, we obtain also Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem
1.5
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 2
, which is not a rational homology sphere.
Then a sequence
(f_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
has a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
for which either
(f_{j_{i}})
converges to a constant map or the normalized measures
\nu_{f_{j_{i}}}
converge vaguely to a measure
\nu\ll\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
in
B_{2}^{n}
.

Proof.

If
\sup_{j}A(f_{j})=\infty

\nu_{f_{j_{i}}}\rightharpoonup\nu_{d\widehat{L}}

Suppose now that

For the rest of this section, we will describe the idea of the proof of Theorem

. This theorem is a particular case of the following version of the Gromov compactness theorem for quasiregular mappings; see

Theorem 9.2
.

Let

be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold,

. Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
for which
\sup_{j}A(f_{j})<\infty

converges locally uniformly to a
K

, on some nodal Riemannian
n

. Furthermore, the main stratum of

is diffeomorphic to
B_{2}^{n}
and all of the bubbles of

are conformal to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Recall that a (pure) nodal

n
-manifold, each point in

into two components, and the local structure of

, each strata is a smooth
n

and all other strata are diffeomorphic to
n

. We refer to
[
21

for further discussion.

In
[
21
]
this convergence theorem is stated for
K
-quasiregular mappings

of fixed degree

between closed manifolds and it was shown that the limit map

[

, Theorem 1.1]
for a precise statement.

In Theorem

, the condition on the degree is replaced by a uniform bound for the energy

. The main difference is that a sequence
(f_{j})
in

may converge to a constant map and hence we may observe a loss of energy

at the limit. Indeed, it suffices to replace
[

, Proposition 3.1]
by the following statement, whose proof is almost verbatim to the proof of

, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 9.3
.

[
21
, Proposition 3.1]

Let

be a closed and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold and let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
satisfying
\sup_{j}A(f_{j})<\infty
. Then, up to passing to a subsequence, there exists a finite set

(1)

2

.

We continue now to the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
9.1
.

Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N)
with
\sup_{j}A(f_{j})<\infty
. Then, by Theorem
9.2
, the sequence
(f_{j})
has a subsequence
(f_{j_{i}})
, which converges to a
K
-quasiregular mapping

, where

is a nodal
n

as its main stratum and (possibly)

Let

be a stratum in

, which is an
n

Appendix B
Quasiregularity of piecewise linear maps

In this appendix, we discuss the quasiregularity of piecewise linear maps. We begin by recalling the definition of a smooth triangulation. Let
N
be a smooth manifold with a piecewise linear structure. A piecewise linear homeomorphism
\kappa\colon P\to N
from a Euclidean polyhedron
P
into
N
is a
piecewise smooth
if there exists a locally finite simplicial complex
\Sigma
having the properties that
P=|\Sigma|
and, for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{\dim\sigma}
of
[0,e_{1},\ldots,e_{\dim\sigma}]
, a smooth map
\lambda\colon U\to N
of rank
\dim\sigma
, and an affine map
A\colon[0,e_{1},\ldots,e_{\dim\sigma}]\to\sigma
satisfying
\lambda|_{[0,e_{1},\ldots,e_{\dim\sigma}]}=\kappa\circ A
. We say that the piecewise linear structure of
N
is compatible with the smooth structure of
N
if there exists a piecewise linear homeomorphism, which is piecewise smooth, from a Euclidean polyhedron into
N
.

We are now ready to state the main result of this appendix.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be closed and oriented piecewise linear Riemannian
n
-manifolds,
n\geqslant 2
. Let
p\colon M\to N
be a non-degenerate orientation preserving piecewise linear map. If the piecewise linear structure is compatible with the smooth structure on both
M
and
N
, then
p
is a quasiregular map.

The following result yields the Sobolev-regularity in Proposition
B.1
. While the result is well-known to experts, we present its proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
M
be a closed piecewise linear smooth
n
-manifold and let
\kappa\colon P\to M
be a piecewise linear homeomorphism, which is piecewise smooth. Let
\Sigma
be a locally finite simplicial complex satisfying
P=|\Sigma|
and let
\rho\colon M\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function having the property that, for each
n
-simplex
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the restriction
\rho|_{\kappa(\sigma)}
has a smooth extension. Then
\rho\in W^{1,\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Since
M
is closed and
\kappa
is a homeomorphism,
\Sigma
has finitely many
n
-simplices
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{j}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that each restriction
\kappa|_{\sigma_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,j
, is a smooth map of rank
n
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,j\}
, there exist a neighbourhood
U_{i}\subset M
of
\kappa(\sigma_{i})
and a smooth function
\psi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\psi_{i}|_{\kappa(\sigma_{i})}=\rho|_{\kappa(\sigma_{i})}
.

Define a differential
1
-form
\alpha\colon M\to TM^{*}
by setting
\alpha_{x}=(d\psi_{i})_{x}
if
x\in\kappa(\mathrm{int}\,\sigma_{i})
and
\alpha_{x}=0
otherwise. Clearly,
\rho\in L^{\infty}(M)
and
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(M,TM^{*})
. Thus, it suffices to prove that
\alpha
is the weak differential of
\rho
.

Let
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-1}(M)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{M}\rho\wedge d\varphi+\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(\int_{\kappa(\sigma_{i})}\psi_{i}\wedge d\varphi+\int_{\kappa(\sigma_{i})}d\psi_{i}\wedge\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{\partial\kappa(\sigma_{i})}\psi_{i}\wedge\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{\kappa(\partial\sigma_{i})}\rho\wedge\varphi

by Stokes’ theorem on manifolds with corners; see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 16.25]
.

For each
i
, let
\delta_{1}^{i},\ldots,\delta_{n+1}^{i}
be the
(n-1)
-faces of
\sigma_{i}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{\kappa(\partial\sigma_{i})}\rho\wedge\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\int_{\kappa(\delta_{j}^{i})}\rho\wedge\varphi.

For each face
\delta_{\ell}^{i}
, there exists a unique face
\delta_{\ell^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}}
such that the faces
\delta_{\ell}^{i}
and
\delta_{\ell^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}}
agree as sets but have opposite orientations. Thus, the terms
\int_{\kappa(\delta_{\ell}^{i})}\rho\wedge\varphi
and
\int_{\kappa(\delta_{\ell^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime}})}\rho\wedge\varphi
cancel each other in the sum. Hence, the whole sum vanishes and the claim follows.
∎

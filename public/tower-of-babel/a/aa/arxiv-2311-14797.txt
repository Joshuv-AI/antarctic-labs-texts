Quantum holographic surface anomalies
Nadav Drukker, Omar Shahpo, Maxime Trépanier

Abstract
Expectation values of surface operators suffer from logarithmic divergences reflecting a conformal anomaly. In a holographic setting, where surface operators can be computed by a minimal surface in A ​ d ​ S AdS , the leading contribution to the anomaly comes from a divergence in the classical action (or area) of the minimal surface. We study the subleading correction to it due to quantum fluctuations of the minimal surface. In the same way that the divergence in the area does not require a global solution but only a near-boundary analysis, the same holds for the quantum corrections. We study the asymptotic form of the fluctuation determinant and show how to use the heat kernel to calculate the quantum anomaly. In the case of M2-branes describing surface operators in the 𝒩 = ( 2 , 0 ) {\cal N}=(2,0) theory in 6d, our calculation of the one-loop determinant reproduces expressions for the anomaly that have been found by less direct methods.

Once we define a surface operator we are faced with the task of computing its

Those are similar to dimensions of local operators and central charges for

\left\langle O(x)O(y)\right\rangle=\frac{c}{|x-y|^{2\Delta_{O}}}\,,

(1.1)

quantum corrections
\Delta_{O}=\Delta_{O}^{\text{cl}}+\delta\Delta_{O}

\displaystyle\log\left\langle O(x)O(y)\right\rangle\sim 2\delta\Delta_{O}\log\epsilon\,.

(1.2)

We include the

irreducible diagrams.

The anomaly coefficients of surface operators play a similar role. They appear
in the expectation value of the surface operator multiplied by particular
conformally covariant local densities. They govern how the observable
transforms under conformal transformations and in explicit calculations are
the prefactors of logarithmic divergences. For a surface operator along
the submanifold
\bar{\Sigma}
with induced metric
\bar{g}

The bars are used to indicate quantities defined in the field theory

In practice, what we would normally find is

\log\left\langle O_{\bar{\Sigma}}\right\rangle\sim\int_{\bar{\Sigma}}d^{2}\tau\sqrt{\bar{g}}\,{\cal A}\,\log\epsilon\,.

3
Determinants on asymptotically
AdS_{3}

Having reviewed the classical M2-brane solution and presented a
convenient form for the metric with explicit asymptotically
AdS_{3}
induced
metric on the brane, we now develop the tools to evaluate determinants
of differential operators on such submanifolds. In turn, we apply this
to the semiclassical M2-brane, where the action for quadratic
fluctuations is evaluated in Appendix
A
.

In the absence of a full classical solution one cannot expect to be able
to fully evaluate the determinant, but we are interested only in the
logarithmically divergent terms that contribute to the anomaly and arise from IR
divergences near the boundary of
AdS
.
We rely on the heat kernel method to evaluate the determinants and
apply them only in the near boundary region to first nontrivial order
beyond pure
AdS_{3}
.

The determinant of a differential operator
L
can be, in principle, calculated
from the heat kernel
K(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})
satisfying

\displaystyle(L_{\sigma}+\partial_{t})K(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=0\,,\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}K(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta^{(3)}(\sigma-\sigma_{0})\,.

(3.1)

The subscript
\sigma
here is meant to emphasize that the differential
operator acts on the point
\sigma
.
If one can solve the heat kernel equation, the determinant of the operator
is then obtained as

\displaystyle\log\det(L)=-\int_{\Sigma}\vol_{\Sigma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\lim_{\sigma\to\sigma_{0}}K(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0}).

(3.2)

For more details on the heat kernel and its applications, see the
review
[
43
]
.

We proceed now to study heat kernels on asymptotically
AdS_{3}
for
the massive scalar laplacian, for vector bundles and for spinors. In the case
of the M2-brane that we are interested in, there are four massless scalars from
the fluctuations in
S^{4}
, four scalars for fluctuations in
AdS_{7}
, which see
the nontrivial geometry of the normal bundle of the M2-brane so should be treated as taking
value in an
SO(4)
-vector bundle, and finally there are 16 fermi fields. See
Appendix
A
for the derivation of the quadratic action.

3.1
Heat kernel asymptotics

In practice, solving the heat kernel equation for an arbitrary kinetic
operator
L
on an arbitrary manifold is impossible.
Fortunately, for the purpose of extracting anomaly
coefficients, we only need the
behavior of the differential operators near the boundary of
AdS
.

We start with a massive scalar laplacian
L=-\Delta+M^{2}

on the asymptotically locally
AdS_{3}
world-volume
given in (
2.15
) and further simplify the calculation
by choosing Riemann normal coordinates for the metric

\bar{g}
about a particular point
(corresponding to
\tau_{0}=(0,0)
and at fixed
z_{0}
), such that

\displaystyle{}g_{zz}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle g_{az}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle g_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\delta_{ab}-R^{2}\left(\frac{1}{3z^{2}}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{acbd}\tau^{c}\tau^{d}+\bar{\Pi}_{ab}\right).

For simplicity we set the
AdS
radius
R=1
in the following.
We proceed by considering the parenthesis on the last line as a perturbation
about the
AdS_{3}
geometry. More precisely,
we treat
(\tau^{a},z)
as homogeneous coordinates
and expand in a power series in them, so

g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}^{(-2)}+g_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}+\cdots

with the index indicating the degree and

\displaystyle{}g_{\mu\nu}^{(-2)}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mu}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z^{2}}(\mathrm{d}z^{2}+\delta_{ab}\mathrm{d}\tau^{a}\mathrm{d}\tau^{b})\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mu}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{acbd}\frac{\tau^{c}\tau^{d}}{z^{2}}+\bar{\Pi}_{ab}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau^{a}\mathrm{d}\tau^{b}\,.

Likewise, the determinant of the metric is expanded as

\displaystyle{}\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z^{3}}\,,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{z}\left(\frac{1}{6}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{cd}\frac{\tau^{c}\tau^{d}}{z^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\tr\bar{\Pi}\right).

We now expand the kinetic operator
L=-\Delta+M^{2}
in the same way as

L=L^{(0)}+L^{(2)}+\cdots

(3.6)

For a massive field, we assume an expansion of the mass term

M^{2}={\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}+\cdots
, and the kinetic operator takes the form

\displaystyle{}L^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}}\partial_{\mu}\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}g^{(2)\,\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle L^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}}{\left(\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}\right)^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}g^{(2)\,\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}g^{(2)\,\mu\nu}+\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}g^{(4)\,\mu\nu}\right)\partial_{\nu}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\,,

where of course
g^{(4)\,\mu\nu}=-g^{(2)\,\mu\rho}g_{\rho\sigma}^{(0)}g^{(2)\,\sigma\nu}
.

Plugging in the metric (
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle{}L^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-z^{3}\partial_{z}\left(\frac{1}{z}\partial_{z}\right)-z^{2}\delta^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\,,

(3.8)

\displaystyle L^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\tr\bar{\Pi}\,z^{3}\partial_{z}+\frac{2z^{2}}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{ab}\tau^{a}\partial_{b}-\left(\frac{z^{2}}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{acbd}\tau^{c}\tau^{d}+z^{4}\bar{\Pi}_{ab}\right)\partial_{a}\partial_{b}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\,.

We emphasize that the kinetic operator
L
acts on the point

\sigma
, and is evaluated at that point. The quantities
(
{\mathcal{M}},\bar{\Pi},\cdots
) appearing in
L^{(2)}
are all evaluated at
\sigma_{0}
.

The heat kernel itself can also be expanded as

K(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=K^{(0)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})+K^{(2)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})+\cdots

(3.9)

To calculate the anomaly we only need those first two terms.
K^{(0)}
is of
homogeneous degree zero, so the volume integral in (
3.2
)
has a quadratic divergence. It may also contribute logarithmic divergences
from the subleading terms in the volume form

\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}
(
3.5
).

K^{(2)}
is quadratic, so like the

y^{2}
terms in the classical action (
2.11
), gives
logarithmic divergences contributing to the conformal anomaly.

The heat kernel equation (
3.1
) then reduces to the
recursive relations

\displaystyle(L^{(0)}+\partial_{t})K^{(0)}=0\,,\qquad(L^{(0)}+\partial_{t})K^{(2)}=-L^{(2)}K^{(0)}\,.

(3.10)

The first equation is the
AdS_{3}
heat kernel equation, its solution is given
by
[
44
]
(also
[
45
,
46
]
)

\displaystyle K^{(0)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}\frac{\rho}{\sinh\rho}\exp\left(-\left(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\right)t-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}\right)\,,

(3.11)

where
\rho
is the geodesic distance in
AdS_{3}
between
\sigma
and
\sigma_{0}

\displaystyle\rho=\mathrm{arccosh}\left(\frac{z^{2}+z_{0}^{2}+|\tau-\tau_{0}|^{2}}{2zz_{0}}\right)\,.

(3.12)

The equation for
K^{(2)}
can be solved by observing that

K^{(0)}(t^{\prime}-t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})\Theta(t^{\prime}-t)

(with
\Theta
the Heaviside step function) is a Green’s function for

L^{(0)}+\partial_{t}
, i.e.

\displaystyle(L^{(0)}+\partial_{t})K^{(0)}(t^{\prime}-t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})\Theta(t^{\prime}-t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}}\delta^{(3)}(\sigma-\sigma_{0})\delta(t^{\prime}-t)\,.

(3.13)

This allows us to express
K^{(2)}
as the integral

\displaystyle{}K^{(2)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}\sigma^{\prime}\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})L^{(2)}_{\sigma^{\prime}}K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\sigma^{\prime},\sigma_{0})

(3.14)

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}}{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}}K^{(0)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})\,,

where the second line ensures that

\lim_{t\to 0}K^{(2)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=-\frac{\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}}{\big(\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}\big)^{2}}\delta^{(3)}(\sigma-\sigma_{0})\,,

(3.15)

so that the boundary conditions at
t=0
(
3.1
) are
compatible with the subleading terms in the measure
\sqrt{g}

(
3.5
).

To evaluate the determinant we need (
3.2
)
the coincident point limit of the heat kernel
K(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})
,
also known as the trace of the heat kernel. For
K^{(0)}
, we
have the explicit expression (
3.11
) from which we get

\lim_{\sigma\to\sigma_{0}}K^{(0)}(t;\sigma,\sigma_{0})=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{3/2}}\exp\left(-\left(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\right)t\right)\,.

(3.16)

For
K^{(2)}
we can use the integral expression (
3.14
) to evaluate
the coincident limit.
K^{(0)}
depends on the coordinates only through the geodesic distance

\rho(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma_{0})
, so it is natural to change coordinates for
AdS_{3}
to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\left(\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}{\rho}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{2})\right)\,.

(3.17)

The change of variables is given by

\displaystyle z=\frac{z_{0}}{\cosh{\rho}-\sinh\rho\cos\theta}\,,\qquad\tau_{a}=\frac{z_{0}\sinh\rho\sin{\theta}\,e_{a}}{\cosh{\rho}-\sinh{\rho}\cos{\theta}}\,,

(3.18)

where
e_{a}=(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)_{a}
.

We now can express
L^{(2)}
(
3.8
)
in these coordinates, but since we act with it on

K^{(0)}
, we only need to keep derivatives with respect to
\rho
. Those are

\displaystyle L^{(2)}f(\rho)=-z_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{R}}\sinh{\rho}\sin^{2}{\theta}}{6(\cosh{\rho}-\sinh{\rho}\cos{\theta})^{3}}\partial_{\rho}+\tr\bar{\Pi}\frac{\coth{\rho}}{\left(\cosh{\rho}-\sinh{\rho}\cos{\theta}\right)^{2}}\partial_{\rho}\right.

(3.19)

\displaystyle+\left.\bar{\Pi}_{ab}\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}\,e_{a}e_{b}}{\left(\cosh{\rho}-\sinh{\rho}\cos\theta\right)^{4}}\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\coth{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\right)\right]f(\rho)+{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}f(\rho)\,.

The integral in (
3.14
) is

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi\sinh^{2}{\rho}\sin{\theta}K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)L^{(2)}K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)\,.

(3.20)

The
\phi
integral is easy to do using

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\phi\,e_{a}e_{b}=\pi\delta_{ab}\,.

(3.21)

The
\theta
integral can be done as well to get

\displaystyle-4\pi

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\sinh^{2}{\rho}\,K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)

(3.22)

\displaystyle\times\left[z_{0}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\left(\partial_{\rho}^{2}+2\coth{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\right)+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{R}}}{6}\frac{\cosh{\rho}\sinh{\rho}-\rho}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}\partial_{\rho}\right)+\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right]K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)\,.

\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle
denotes the average of
{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
over the spherical
coordinates.
We can simplify further the coefficient of
\tr\bar{\Pi}
by using the heat kernel
equation (
3.10
), which in these coordinates is

\displaystyle\left(-\partial_{\rho}^{2}-2\coth{\rho}\,\partial_{\rho}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}+\partial_{t}^{\prime}\right)K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)=0\,,

(3.23)

which removes both first and second order
\rho
derivatives
and we obtain

\displaystyle 4\pi

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\sinh^{2}{\rho}\,K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)

(3.24)

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\,\left(\partial_{t^{\prime}}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\right)-\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6}\bar{\mathcal{R}}\frac{\cosh{\rho}\sinh{\rho}-\rho}{\sinh^{2}{\rho}}\partial_{\rho}-\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right]K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)\,.

The remaining integrals can be evaluated without much difficulty
after plugging in the expression for
K^{(0)}
in (
3.11
).
A more elegant way to evaluate them is to use partial integration to reduce them
to a convolution of heat kernels. Consider first the term proportional to

\tr{\bar{\Pi}}
. We can take out the
t^{\prime}
derivative as

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi z_{0}^{2}}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\sinh^{2}{\rho}K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)\left(\partial_{t^{\prime}}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\right)K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)

(3.25)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi z_{0}^{2}}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\left(\partial_{t^{\prime}}+\partial_{t}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\sinh^{2}{\rho}K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)\,.

Now the
\rho
integral is the convolution of two heat kernels (up to the
factor
4\pi
from the spherical integral),
giving simply
K^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})
.
The
t^{\prime}
dependence completely drops out and the integral over
it gives
t
, leaving

\displaystyle\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\,t(\partial_{t}+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)})K^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})\,.

(3.26)

Second, for the term proportional to
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
, with
an exchange of
t^{\prime}\to t-t^{\prime}
the derivative can be moved to the
other
K^{(0)}
, so we can write it as

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi z_{0}^{2}}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\left(\cosh{\rho}\sinh{\rho}-\rho\right)\partial_{\rho}\left(K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)\right)

(3.27)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi z_{0}^{2}}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\rho\sinh^{2}{\rho}\,K^{(0)}(t-t^{\prime};\rho)K^{(0)}(t^{\prime};\rho)=\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6}\bar{\mathcal{R}}tK^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})\,.

The last term in (
3.24
) is that proportional to

{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
, and assuming
\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle
doesn’t depend on
\rho
(as we show
in the case of interest below), it is directly the convolution of the two heat kernels.

Adding (
3.26
), (
3.27
) and the contribution
from
{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
and plugging the value of the heat kernel at coincident points
(
3.16
)
gives

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{3}\tr\bar{\Pi}\,\left(\frac{3}{2t}+1\right)+\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6}\bar{\mathcal{R}}+\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right]\frac{\exp\left(-(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)})t\right)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}t^{1/2}}\,.

(3.28)

Finally, adding the term which corrects the boundary conditions from
the second line of (
3.14
), it exactly cancels the

3/2t
term and we get

\displaystyle K^{(2)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})=\left[\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6}(\bar{\mathcal{R}}-2\tr\bar{\Pi})-\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right]\frac{\exp\left(-(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)})t\right)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}t^{1/2}}\,.

(3.29)

Note the appearance of the combination
\bar{\mathcal{R}}-2\tr{\bar{\Pi}}
.
The correction to the
AdS_{3}
heat kernel coming from geometric terms is
expected to be proportional to this combination (up to perhaps also a contribution
from terms involving the Weyl tensor), because of the following
consistency check.
Take the surface to be a sphere
\bar{\Sigma}=S^{2}
,
with corresponding classical geometry
AdS_{3}

[
26
]
. In that
case the heat kernel is exactly
K^{(0)}
, so we should find
K^{(2)}=0
.
Indeed, although both
\bar{\mathcal{R}}=2
and
\tr{\bar{\Pi}}=1
are
nonzero for the sphere, the combination
\bar{\mathcal{R}}-2\tr{\bar{\Pi}}

vanishes.

For the evaluation of the determinant (
3.2
),
we need the
t
integral of the trace of the heat kernel,
(
3.16
) and (
3.29
),
which are simple gamma function integrals. Reinstating powers of
R
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,K^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}R^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{6\pi R^{3}}\,,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\,K^{(2)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}R^{2}}}{4\pi R}\left[\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6R^{2}}(2\tr\bar{\Pi}-\bar{\mathcal{R}})+\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right]\,.

(3.31)

3.2
The bosonic determinant

We can use the results above to evaluate the determinant of bosonic
fluctuations of M2-branes describing surface operators. The quadratic
action is derived in Appendix
A
and consists of
eight bosonic and eight fermionic modes. The bosonic modes are
in turn split into the four modes related to the
S^{4}
part
of the geometry and four directions transverse to the
M2-brane world-volume within
AdS_{7}
.

The first four bosonic modes are massless scalars, and their kinetic operator is
simply
L=-\Delta
, see (
A.6
). The path integral over these fluctations
contributes
-\frac{4}{2}\log\det(-\Delta)
to the log of the partition
function, which can be evaluated using (
3.2
). The integrand
contains both a cubic and a simple pole in
z
. Performing the
z
integral, the
cubic pole leads to a quadratic divergence in the cutoff, which we drop (it can
be removed by a local counterterm). The simple pole leads to
\log\epsilon

divergence, and leaves an integral over
\bar{\Sigma}
as in the expression for
the anomaly (
1.4
). The contribution of these four modes to
the anomaly density is therefore

\displaystyle{}{\mathcal{A}}^{S^{4}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{2}\res_{z_{0}\to 0}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\frac{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}+\sqrt{g}^{(-1)}+\cdots}{\sqrt{\bar{g}}}\left(K^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})+K^{(2)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})+\cdots\right)\right]

(3.32)

\displaystyle=-2\res_{z_{0}\to 0}\left[\left(-\frac{1}{2z_{0}}\tr{\bar{\Pi}}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}K^{(0)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})+\frac{1}{z_{0}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}K^{(2)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})+\cdots\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{3}\bar{\mathcal{R}}\,.

The second line has the metric in Riemann normal
coordinates (
3.5
), and in going to the
last line we use the expressions for the integrated heat
kernel (
3.30
) and (
3.31
).

The second set of four bosonic modes parametrise the normal
bundle
N\Sigma
to the world-volume of the M2-brane.
The kinetic operator derived in (
A.6
) acts on the fiber bundle (with
fiber
V=\mathbb{R}^{4}
), and the path integral over the fluctuations contributes a
factor
-\frac{1}{2}\log\det_{V}(-\Delta+M^{2})
to the log of the partition
function. Evaluating this determinant requires a slight generalisation of the
formalism for the scalars.

The heat kernel is still defined by (
3.1
),
with the initial condition multiplied on the right hand side by
the identity matrix
\mathds{1}_{V}
, and the determinant of

L
has the aditional trace

\displaystyle\log\det\nolimits_{V}(L)=-\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{d}^{3}\sigma\sqrt{g}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\tr_{V}{K(t;\sigma,\sigma)}\,.

(3.33)

The class of differential operators we need to consider are laplacians on the
normal bundle along with a mass term. They take the form

\displaystyle L=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}D_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g}g^{\mu\nu}D_{\nu}\right)+M^{2}\,,

(3.34)

with
D_{\mu}
the covariant derivative acting on fluctuations
\zeta^{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\zeta^{m^{\prime}}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\zeta^{m^{\prime}}+\omega^{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}_{\mu}\zeta^{n^{\prime}}\,,

(3.35)

where
\omega_{\mu}^{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
is the spin connection on the
normal bundle. To evaluate it, we pick a vielbein for the metric

G
(
2.14
). We then expand it at
u=0
in
(z,\tau^{a})
as

\displaystyle E=E^{(-1)}+E^{(1)}+\cdots\,,

(3.36)

where
E^{(-1)}
is a vielbein for the
AdS_{7}
metric, for instance

\displaystyle E^{(-1)\,m}=\frac{1}{z}\delta_{\mu}^{m}\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\mu}\,,\qquad E^{(-1)\,m^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{z}\bar{E}_{\mu^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}u^{\mu^{\prime}}\,.

(3.37)

The relevant spin connection is defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu}^{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}=E_{\mu^{\prime}}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\partial_{\mu}E^{\mu^{\prime}n^{\prime}}+\Gamma^{\mu^{\prime}}_{\mu\rho^{\prime}}E^{\rho^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\right).

(3.38)

Importantly, this vanishes using the leading order vielbein
E^{(-1)}
,
so
\omega^{(-1)}=0
. If we expand
L
(
3.34
), to
leading order we recover four copies of the scalar laplacian on

AdS_{3}
(
3.8
). The mass matrix

{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
is diagonal with eigenvalues
3

[
42
]
.
Likewise
K^{(0)}
is a diagonal matrix with entries as in (
3.11
).
Had
\omega^{(-1)}
not vanished, we would have had to solve for the
vector heat kernel, in analogy with the spinors in the next section.
Instead, the nontrivial bundle only affects
L^{(2)}
and
K^{(2)}
.

The first subleading correction is
L^{(2)}
as
in (
3.7
) with the additional contribution from the spin
connection

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{g}^{(-3)}g^{(2)\,\mu\nu}\right)\omega_{\mu}^{(1)\,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}-2g^{(2)\,\mu\nu}\omega^{(1)\,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\,.

(3.39)

Here
\omega^{(1)}
is the subleading correction to the spin connection.

K^{(2)}
is then given by (
3.14
), and we are interested
in the trace of the heat kernel

\tr_{V}{K^{(2)}}
. Using that
{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}
is a diagonal matrix (and so is

K^{(0)}
), while
\omega^{(1)}
is antisymmetric, we see that these
additional contributions simply drop out of the trace

\displaystyle\tr_{V}{K^{(0)}\omega^{(1)}K^{(0)}}=0\,.

(3.40)

We therefore do not even need to write down explicit expressions
for the corrections
E^{(1)}
and
\omega_{\mu}^{(1)\,m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
.
Instead,
K^{(2)}
is a straightforward generalisation of the
scalar case, where the only nontrivial matrix structure is in

{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
. The result is the same as (
3.29
)

\displaystyle\tr_{V}{K^{(2)}(t;\sigma_{0},\sigma_{0})}=\tr_{V}\left[\left(\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{6}(\bar{\mathcal{R}}-2\tr\bar{\Pi})-\left\langle{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}\right\rangle\right)\frac{\exp\left(-(1+{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)})t\right)}{(4\pi)^{3/2}t^{1/2}}\right]\,.

(3.41)

For the M2-brane, the mass matrix
M^{2}
is a function over the
world-volume
\Sigma
derived in (
A.6
).
Its trace over the four transverse
AdS_{7}
modes is

\displaystyle M^{2}_{m^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=-g^{\mu\nu}G^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}R_{\mu^{\prime}\mu\nu^{\prime}\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}G_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\tilde{\makebox[6.83339pt]{\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule width=6.83339pt,height=7.15973pt,depth=-6.72916pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule width=6.83339pt,height=0.25832pt,depth=0.12915pt\hss}\kern 0.5pt{II}\kern 0.5pt}}_{\mu\rho}^{\mu^{\prime}}\tilde{\makebox[6.83339pt]{\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule width=6.83339pt,height=7.15973pt,depth=-6.72916pt\hss}\hbox to0.0pt{\vrule width=6.83339pt,height=0.25832pt,depth=0.12915pt\hss}\kern 0.5pt{II}\kern 0.5pt}}_{\sigma\nu}^{\nu^{\prime}}\,,

(3.42)

where here
R_{\mu^{\prime}\mu\nu^{\prime}\nu}
is the bulk Riemann tensor (as opposed to the
world-volume Riemann tensor
{\mathcal{R}}
).
In order to read
{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}
we rewrite
M^{2}
as follows.
Recall that
\mu
,
\nu
are the M2-brane directions and
\mu^{\prime}
,
\nu^{\prime}

the transverse ones, so we can write

\displaystyle G^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}R_{\mu^{\prime}\mu\nu^{\prime}\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}\,.

(3.43)

The Ricci tensor is fixed by the bulk equations of motion

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}=-6G_{\mu\nu}=-6g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3.44)

and for the components of the Riemann tensor, we relate them
to the world-volume curvature

Rigorous scaling laws for internally heated convection at infinite Prandtl number
Ali Arslan, Giovanni Fantuzzi, John Craske, Andrew Wynn

Abstract
New bounds are proven on the mean vertical convective heat transport, ⟨ w ​ T ⟩ ¯ \smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}} , for uniform internally heated (IH) convection in the limit of infinite Prandtl number. For fluid in a horizontally-periodic layer between isothermal boundaries, we show that ⟨ w ​ T ⟩ ¯ ≤ 1 2 − c ​ R − 2 \mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}\leq\frac{1}{2}-cR^{-2} , where R R is a nondimensional ‘flux’ Rayleigh number quantifying the strength of internal heating and c = 216 c=216 . Then, ⟨ w ​ T ⟩ ¯ = 0 \mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}=0 corresponds to vertical heat transport by conduction alone, while ⟨ w ​ T ⟩ ¯ > 0 \mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}>0 represents the enhancement of vertical heat transport upwards due to convective motion. If, instead, the lower boundary is a thermal insulator, then we obtain ⟨ w ​ T ⟩ ¯ ≤ 1 2 − c ​ R − 4 \mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}\leq\frac{1}{2}-cR^{-4} , with c ≈ 0.0107 c\approx 0.0107 . This result implies that the Nusselt number Nu , defined as the ratio of the total-to-conductive heat transport, satisfies Nu ≲ R 4 \mbox{{Nu}}\lesssim\mbox{{R}}^{4} .Both bounds are obtained by combining the background method with a minimum principle for the fluid’s temperature and with Hardy–Rellich inequalities to exploit the link between the vertical velocity and temperature. In both cases, power-law dependence on R improves the previously best-known bounds, which, although valid at both infinite and finite Prandtl numbers, approach the uniform bound exponentially with R .

One source of difficulty for mathematical studies of IH convection is that the mean thermal dissipation, the mean viscous dissipation, and the mean vertical convective heat flux cannot all be related to each other via

14

15

16

[
2
,
5

2.1  Bounding framework

To bound
\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}
we employ the auxiliary function method
[

,
24
]

\mathcal{V}\{T(t)\}
along solutions of the Boussinesq equations (

\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}=\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle+\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{V}\{T(t)\}}.

If
\mathcal{V}

\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}

Following analysis at finite Prandtl number

2
]

(2.3)

which are parametrized by a positive constant
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a piecewise-differentiable function

with square-integrable derivative. We require

(2.4)

\tau(0)=1,\qquad\tau(1)=0,

so the coefficient multiplying

from above and below. For a lower bound, substitute our choices for

Expanding the square, dropping the nonnegative term

Taking the square root of both sides and substituting for the value of

from (

) gives

, which combined with (

For the upper bound on

, recall that condition

. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle|\tau^{\prime}-\lambda|^{2}\right\rangle}{A^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{1-\varepsilon}_{\delta}\left|\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{1-z}\right|^{2}\textrm{d}z+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln^{2}{\left(\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{1-\delta}_{\delta}\left|\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{1-z}\right|^{2}\textrm{d}z+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right).

Using the inequality

we can further estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle|\tau^{\prime}-\lambda|^{2}\right\rangle}{A^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\int^{1-\delta}_{\delta}\frac{1}{z^{2}}\,{\rm d}z+\int^{1-\delta}_{\delta}\frac{1}{(1-z)^{2}}\,{\rm d}z\right)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)

∎

Lemma 4

Proof of

3

Let

(2.22a)

\displaystyle f(z):=\left[\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{A}{z(1-z)}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\delta)}(z),

(2.22b)

\displaystyle g(z):=\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln\!{\left(\frac{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\delta)}{\varepsilon\delta}\right)}+\frac{1}{z(1-z)}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{(1-\varepsilon,1)}(z).

from (

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\{T\}:=\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle+A\left\langle\frac{wT}{z}\right\rangle-\left\langle f(z)wT\right\rangle,

(2.23b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\{T\}:=\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle+A\left\langle\frac{wT}{1-z}\right\rangle-A\left\langle g(z)wT\right\rangle.

Here,
w
is determined as a function of
T
by solving (

) subject to the boundary conditions in (

). We shall prove that
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are individually non-negative.

First, let us consider
\mathcal{F}
. The Hardy–Rellich inequality
(
2.21
a)
gives

(2.24)

\mathcal{F}\{T\}\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle+\frac{4A}{R}\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle-\left\langle f(z)wT\right\rangle.

Next, we estimate
\langle f(z)wT\rangle
.
Since
T
vanishes at

by virtue of the thermal boundary conditions in (

), we can use the fundamental theorem of calculus and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to estimate
|T(\cdot,z)|\leq\smash{\sqrt{z}(\int^{1}_{0}|\nabla T|^{2}\textrm{d}z)^{1/2}}
. Squaring both sides and taking the horizontal average of which gives
\left\langle T^{2}\right\rangle_{h}\leq z\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle
.
Then, use of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, substitution for
\left\langle T^{2}\right\rangle_{h}
and Youngs inequality gives

\displaystyle\left\langle f(z)wT\right\rangle\leq\int^{1}_{0}|f(z)|\left\langle|wT|\right\rangle_{h}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int^{1}_{0}|f(z)|\left\langle w^{2}\right\rangle^{1/2}_{h}\left\langle T^{2}\right\rangle^{1/2}_{h}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{0}f(z)z^{2}\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}_{h}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle f(z)^{2}z^{4}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\langle f(z)^{2}z^{4}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle.

Upon using the lower bound on
\beta
from (
2.15
) to estimate the last term from above we obtain

(2.25)

\left\langle f(z)wT\right\rangle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{9\delta}\left\langle f(z)^{2}z^{4}\right\rangle\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle.

This can be substituted into (
2.24
) along with our choice of
A
from (
2.13
) to find

(2.26)

\mathcal{F}\{T\}\geq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{9}\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{27\sqrt{3}}{5R}\,-\frac{\left\langle f(z)^{2}z^{4}\right\rangle}{\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right)\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{z^{3}}\right\rangle.

To conclude, we show that the term in parentheses is nonnegative when
\delta
satisfies
\delta\leq\frac{1}{6}
and
(
2.20a,b

.
To do this, we observe that the function

using the estimates

\frac{\left\langle f(z)^{2}z^{4}\right\rangle}{\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{5}{2}}}\int^{\delta}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{6A}{5z}\right)^{2}z^{4}\,{\rm d}z=\bigg(\frac{1}{5}+\frac{9\sqrt{15}}{100}\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{81}{500}\delta^{3}\bigg)\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the assumption that

to estimate the expression in parentheses by its value at

Analogous arguments show that

is nonnegative. Using the Hardy–Rellich inequality

To estimate the last term, we use (in order) the inequality
\left\langle T^{2}\right\rangle_{h}\leq\smash{(1-z)\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle}
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\left\langle g(z)wT\right\rangle\leq\int^{1}_{0}|g(z)|\left\langle|wT|\right\rangle_{h}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int^{1}_{0}|g(z)|\left\langle w^{2}\right\rangle^{1/2}_{h}\left\langle T^{2}\right\rangle^{1/2}_{h}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{0}g(z)(1-z)^{2}\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{(1-z)^{3}}\right\rangle_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle|\nabla T|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle g(z)^{2}(1-z)^{4}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle\frac{w^{2}}{(1-z)^{3}}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}

Using the lower bound on

To conclude the argument we show that the term in parentheses is non-negative. To demonstrate this, we first estimate

Then, we use the assumptions

. Combining these estimates with the upper bound on

\displaystyle\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle g(z)^{2}(1-z)^{4}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{1-\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\ln{\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)}+\frac{3}{2(1-z)}\right)^{2}(1-z)^{4}\,\textrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{3}\left(\frac{1}{5}\ln^{2}{\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)}+\frac{3}{4}\ln{\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)}+\frac{3}{4}\right)

Upper bounds on

Proposition 2

(Bounding framework, IH3)
.

Suppose that the pair
(\beta,\tau)
satisfies the spectral constraint (cf.
definition

1
) and the boundary condition in (

). Further, let

be a nondecreasing function such that

Then,

\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}+\left\langle\tfrac{1}{4\beta}\left|\tau^{\prime}-\lambda-\beta z\right|^{2}-\tau\right\rangle=:U(\tau,\lambda,\beta)

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of
proposition

(3.2)

\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\mathcal{H}_{+}\\

The supremum on the right-hand side can be evaluated using the convex duality argument summarized in

\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}+\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in L^{2}(0,1)\\

3.2  Ansätze

The procedure for the proof of an upper bound on
\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}
is the same as that employed for isothermal boundaries. We construct
\beta>0
,
\tau(z)
and
\lambda(z)
that satisfy the conditions of
proposition

2
, while trying to minimize the corresponding bound
U(\beta,\tau,\lambda)
. Due to the Neumann boundary condition on
T
at
z=0
, we can no longer employ the Poincaré estimates used in
section

2.3
to control the sign-indefinite term in the spectral constraint at the bottom boundary. Instead we modify
\tau(z)
in
(\delta,\frac{1}{2})
to increase slower than logarithmically in

and use results established in
[
26
]
.
The function
\tau(z)
is hence chosen to have the form

(3.4)

\tau(z):=\begin{cases}\delta-z,&0\leq z\leq\delta,\\
\frac{A}{1-\alpha}\left(z^{1-\alpha}-\delta^{1-\alpha}\right)-A\ln{\left(\frac{1-z}{1-\delta}\right)},&\delta\leq z\leq 1-\varepsilon,\\
\frac{A\,B}{\varepsilon}\left(1-z\right),&1-\varepsilon\leq z\leq 1,\end{cases}

where

(3.5)

\displaystyle B=B(\epsilon,\delta,\alpha):=\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{1-\alpha}-\delta^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}+\ln{\left(\frac{1-\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)}.

On the other hand, the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda(z)
is still chosen to be

(3.6)

\lambda(z):=\begin{cases}-1,&0\leq z\leq\delta,\\
0,&\delta\leq z\leq 1.\end{cases}

These piecewise functions, sketched in
fig.

4
, are fully specified by the bottom and top boundary layer widths
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, the constant
A>0
, and the exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
driving the behaviour of
\tau(z)
in the bulk.

For
\beta
we take

(3.7)

\beta:=\left\langle|\tau^{\prime}-\lambda|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle z^{2}\right\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{3}\left\langle|\tau^{\prime}-\lambda|^{2}\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}.

4  Conclusions

We have proven upper bounds on the mean vertical convective heat transport
\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}
for two configurations of infinite-Prandtl-number convection driven by uniform internal heating between no-slip boundaries. In the first case, where both boundaries are held at a constant temperature, we find
\mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}\leq\frac{1}{2}-O(\mbox{{R}}^{-2})
for all sufficiently large
R
(cf.
theorem

1
). This result implies that the outward heat fluxes through the top and bottom are bounded by
\mathcal{F}_{T}\leq 1-O(\mbox{{R}}^{-2})
and
\mathcal{F}_{B}\geq O(\mbox{{R}}^{-2})
, respectively. In the second configuration, where the top boundary remains isothermal but the bottom one is insulating (no-flux condition), we find
\mbox{$\smash{\overline{\left\langle wT\right\rangle}}$}\leq\frac{1}{2}-O(\mbox{{R}}^{-4})
(cf.
eq.

1.8
). In this case, we conclude from (
1.9
) that the Nusselt number is bounded above by
\mbox{{Nu}}\leq O(\mbox{{R}}^{4})
. Explicit suboptimal values for the prefactors in the Rayleigh-dependent terms were also obtained (cf.
remarks

1
and
2
).

All of these results were derived using the background method, which we formulated as a search over quadratic auxiliary functionals of the form (
2.3
) and augmented using a minimum principle for the fluid’s temperature. Similar to previous works on infinite-Prandtl-number Rayleigh–Bénard convection, the background temperature fields used vary linearly in thin boundary layers, and increase either logarithmically (IH1 configuration) or as a power law (IH3 configuration) in the bulk of the fluid layer. This bulk behaviour enables us to use Hardy–Rellich inequalities from
[
14
]
(

One crucial difference between our constructions for the IH1 and IH3 configurations is the leading-order behaviour of the background temperature fields—or, more precisely, of the function
\tau(z)

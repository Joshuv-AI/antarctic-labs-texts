Norm estimates for a broad class of modulation spaces, and continuity of Fourier type operators
Joachim Toft, Christine Pfeuffer, Nenad Teofanov

Abstract
. We consider a broad class of modulation spaces M ⁡ ( ω , ℬ ) M(\omega,\mathscr{B}) , parameterized with weight function ω \omega and a normal quasi-Banach function space ℬ \mathscr{B} of order r 0 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] r_{0}\in(0,1] . Then we prove that f ∈ M ⁡ ( ω , ℬ ) f\in M(\omega,\mathscr{B}) , iff V ϕ ​ f V_{\phi}f belongs to the Wiener amalgam space 𝖶 r ​ ( ω , ℬ ) \mathsf{W}^{r}(\omega,\mathscr{B}) , and ‖ f ‖ M ⁡ ( ω , ℬ ) ≍ ‖ V ϕ ​ f ⋅ ω ‖ ℬ ≍ ‖ V ϕ ​ f ‖ 𝖶 r ​ ( ω , ℬ ) , r ∈ [ r 0 , ∞ ] . \|f\|_{M(\omega,\mathscr{B})}\asymp\|V_{\phi}f\,\cdot\,\omega\|_{\mathscr{B}}\asymp\|V_{\phi}f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r}(\omega,\mathscr{B})},\quad r\in[r_{0},\infty]. We use the results to extend and improve continuity and lifting properties for pseudo-differential and Toeplitz operators with symbols in weighted M ∞ , r 0 M^{\infty,r_{0}} -spaces, with r 0 ≤ 1 r_{0}\leq 1 , when acting on M ⁡ ( ω , ℬ ) M(\omega,\mathscr{B}) -spaces.

Introduction

The aim of the first part of the paper is
to derive various types of quasi-norm
equivalences for
a broad family of quasi-Banach
modulation spaces. Any such topological
vector space,
M(\omega,\mathscr{B})
,
is parameterized by a suitable weight function

\omega
and a quasi-Banach function space

\mathscr{B}
,
and is adapted and feasible for various
kinds of Fourier techniques.
Our family of equivalent norms
is significantly larger, compared to what
has been deduced so far in the context of
modulation
space and coorbit space theories.
(See e. g.

[
4
,
14
,
21
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
28
,
31
,
48
,
49
]
.)
In contrast
to the usual approaches in these theories,
our methods do not rely on (Gabor) frame theory
or Gabor expansions. In fact,
the achieved norm equivalences should be
considered as a
complement
to existing Gabor
frame approach for modulation space theory,
where we supply the theory with quasi-norm
estimates as alternatives to estimates
obtained from Gabor expansions. Especially,
our methods eliminate the need to find
suitable dual window functions to a given
Gabor frame.

The aim of the second part of the paper
is to find convenient and general
continuity properties for pseudo-differential
operators with symbols in suitable modulation
spaces when acting on broad families of
modulation
spaces. These results are significantly more
general than the related results
available in the literature.
(See e. g.
[

The norm equivalences from the first
part serve as cornerstones for achieving
our results, and
it seems that earlier methods are
insufficient for reaching these results.

For an overview of some parts of
our progress, see Table

below.

Our results and ideas might be
applicable in various parts within
analysis, or in a broader perspective,
to various fields in
science and technology, in the
seek of finding suitable
(topological) spaces. In several
such situations it is important
that these spaces are adapted to
Fourier analysis, and in particular
to the Fourier transform and
other related transformations.

The Fourier transform provides a picture
that only
displays non-localized information on
momentum or frequency, while in
different applications, there is a need
for global
representations which simultaneously
depict the behavior in the phase space and
time-frequency shift space.

A common choice of
operators fulfilling such requested
properties is the local Fourier transform,
usually called the short-time Fourier
transform or voice transform in
time-frequency analysis
(see e. g.

[
20
,
24
,
31
]
). In
quantum physics, this transform is
often called the
coherent state transform (cf.

[
20
,
24
,
31
,
41
]
).
It possess several convenient properties,
like continuity
on suitable spaces. It can
also be used for characterizing
topological vector spaces like the
Schwartz space, Gelfand-Shilov spaces,
and their distribution spaces
(see e. g.
[
10
,
15
,
31
,
37
,
38
,
60
]
).

A convenient way to quantify the phase space
or time-frequency content of a
distribution
f
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}

is to estimate its short-time
Fourier transform
V_{\phi}f

in suitable ways. Here
\phi

is a fixed (window) function which
should belong to a convenient
function space, like

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
.
(See
[
40
]

or Sections
1
and

6
–
8

for notations.)
For this reason, Feichtinger
introduced in
[
17
,
20
]
the (classical)
modulation spaces,

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
by imposing a mixed
L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}

norm estimate on the short-time Fourier
transforms of the involved functions
and distributions. Here
\omega
should belong
to
\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, the set of
weight functions on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
,
which are moderate with polynomially
bounded functions, cf. Subsection

1.1
.

Thus the Lebesgue parameters

p
and
q
to some extent quantify the degrees
of asymptotic decays and singularities,
respectively, of the distributions in

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and similar facts
hold true for the weight function
\omega
.
For example in
[
17
,
56
]
it is
observed that

\bigcap_{\omega\in\mathscr{P}}M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\bigcup_{\omega\in\mathscr{P}}M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

(0.1)

In
[
17
,
20
]
, several fundamental
properties for

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}
-spaces were established.
In particular, it is here proved that

M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
increases
with
p
and
q
, decreases with
\omega
,
and is independent of the choice of
window function

\phi\in M^{1}_{(v)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
.

After
[
17
,
20
]
, the theory of
modulation
spaces was further developed in different
ways. In
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
,
Feichtinger and Gröchenig
developed the coorbit space theory
in the framework of Banach spaces,
and in
[

]
,
they showed that these
spaces admit reconstructible sequence
space representations using Gabor
frames.
In
[
28
]
, Galperin and Samarah showed
that this result
still holds
true in the quasi-Banach situation, after
the assumption
p,q\in[1,\infty]

is relaxed into

p,q\in(0,\infty]
. A similar extension
of the whole coorbit space theory in

[
23
,
24
,
25
,
30
]
, to
allow quasi-Banach spaces
was thereafter performed by Rauhut in

[
49
]
. Some recent contributions
to this topic is performed in
[
69
]

by Voigtlaender.

Some extensions to include more general
weight functions, leading to modulation
spaces in the framework of
ultra-distribution, can also be
found, see e. g.

[
31
,
46
,
47
,
54
,
58
,
64
]
.
For example, let

\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
in
(
0.1
)
be replaced by

\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
the set of
all
moderate
weights (without any assumptions
on polynomial bounds).
Then it follows that
(
0.1
)
holds true with the
(Fourier invariant) Gelfand-Shilov space

\Sigma_{1}
and its distribution space

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
, in place of

\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
, respectively
(see e. g.
[
58
]
).
We remark that
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is
significantly smaller (but dense) in

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, while its distribution
space
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is strictly
larger than
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
cf. Subsection
1.2
.

The concept of modulation spaces was also
extended in
[
22
]
, by allowing more
general bound conditions on the
short-time Fourier transform. For example,
here Feichtinger considers modulation
spaces, with our notations denoted
by
M(\omega,\mathscr{B}_{0})
, and
which are parameterized by
the solid Banach function space

\mathscr{B}_{0}
, and the weight function

\omega
.

In several situations, modulation spaces are
convenient to use because it is easy to find
different characterizations for them, especially
norm characterizations. For example,
for
p,q,r\in(0,\infty]
,
the mixed (quasi-)norm space

L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
and
the Wiener space

\mathsf{W}^{r}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}))

are the sets of all measurable
functions
F
on
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
such that

\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}<\infty

and

\|F\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)})}<\infty
, respectively. Here

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}|F(x,\xi)\omega(x,\xi)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\iota\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\big(\|F\,\cdot\,\omega\|_{L^{r}(Q(j,\iota))}\big)^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle Q(j,\iota)

\displaystyle=(j,\iota)+[0,1]^{2d},

when
p,q<\infty
.

By a straight-forward application of
Hölder’s inequality, it follows
that
\mathsf{W}^{r}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}))
decreases with
r
,
its (quasi-)norm increases with
r
,
and

\mathsf{W}^{r_{2}}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}))\subseteq L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\subseteq\mathsf{W}^{r_{1}}(L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})),\quad r_{1}\leq p,q\leq r_{2}.

Taking into account that the latter
embeddings are strict,
one might be surprised that for any

\phi\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\setminus 0
,
indeed

\displaystyle f\in M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad V_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\in L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

(0.2)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad V_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\in\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(L^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})),

and that the (quasi-)norm equivalence

\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}\asymp\|V_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\|_{L^{p,q}}\asymp\|V_{\phi}f\cdot\omega\|_{\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(L^{p,q})},\qquad f\in M^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(0.3)

holds true.
(See e. g.
[
28
,
64
]

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic facts about
weight functions, the short-time Fourier transform
and some spaces of function and distributions which will be used in the sequel.

1.1.
Weight functions

A
weight
or
weight function
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
is a
positive function
\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that
1/\omega\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
If there is a weight
v
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
and a constant
C\geq 1
such that

\omega(x+y)\leq C\omega(x)v(y),\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(1.1)

then the weight
\omega
is called

moderate
, or
v
-moderate
.
By (
1.1
)
we have

C^{-1}v(-x)^{-1}\leq\omega(x)\leq Cv(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.2)

We let
\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be the set of all moderate weights on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

We say that a weight
v
is
submultiplicative
if

v(x+y)\leq v(x)v(y)\quad\text{and}\quad v(-x)=v(x),\qquad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.3)

If
v
is positive and locally bounded and
satisfies the inequality in
(
1.3
),
then
v(x)\leq Ce^{r|x|}
for some positive
constants
C
and
r
, cf.
[
33
]
.

We observe that given a
v
-moderate weight

\omega
, one can find a continuous
v
-
moderate weight
\omega_{0}
such that
\omega\asymp\omega_{0}
(see e. g.

[
58
]
). In addition, a moderate
weight
\omega

is also moderated by a submultiplicative
weight, cf. e. g.
[
24
,
58
]
.
Therefore, if
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
then

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)e^{r|y|},\quad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d},

(1.4)

for some
r>0
.
Here
g_{1}\lesssim g_{2}
means that

g_{1}(x)\leq c\cdot g_{2}(x)

holds uniformly for all
x

in the intersection of the domains of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, and
for some constant
c>0
. We
write
g_{1}\asymp g_{2}

when
g_{1}\lesssim g_{2}\lesssim g_{1}
.

In particular, (
1.2
) shows that
for any
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
there is a constant
r>0
such that

e^{-r|x|}\lesssim\omega(x)\lesssim e^{r|x|},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(1.5)

In the sequel,
v
and
v_{0}
, always stand for
submultiplicative weights if nothing else is stated.

Some parts of our investigations require
more families of weight functions.

.

Let
s,\sigma>0
.

(1)

all
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\omega
is moderate by

,

(2)

The set

(

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\omega(x+y)\lesssim\omega(x)e^{r|y|^{\frac{1}{s}}},\quad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d};

holds for some (every)

.

(3)

(

all

\omega(x+y,\xi+\eta)\lesssim\omega(x,\xi)e^{r(|y|^{\frac{1}{s}}+|\eta|^{\frac{1}{\sigma}})},\quad x,y,\xi,\eta\in\mathbf{R}^{d};

(1.7)

holds for some (every)
r>0
.

1.2.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

In what follows we let

Recall that

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{N}^{d}

Here

and we use the standard terminology
on multi-indices.

We observe that with our choice of the Fourier
transform, the usual
convolution identity for the Fourier transform
takes the forms

\mathscr{F}(f\cdot g)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{F}(f*g)=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\widehat{f}\cdot\widehat{g}

(f*g)(x)=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}f(x-y)g(y)\,dy

Classical modulation

For the introduction of classical

Let

(Lebesgue) measurable set in
\mathbf{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be the set of all

measurable functions on
\Omega

For any
p,q\in(0,\infty]

spaces

L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

and
L^{p,q}_{*,(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

of Lebesgue type, are given by

\displaystyle L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

\displaystyle=\{\,F\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\,;\,\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}<\infty\,\}

(1.27)

and

\displaystyle L^{p,q}_{*,(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}+d_{2}})

\displaystyle=\{\,F\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\,;\,\|F\|_{L^{p,q}_{*,(\omega)}}<\infty\,\},

(1.28)

for some
p,q\in(0,\infty]
,

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
and
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0
are integers which
satisfy

. Here

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\|G_{f,\omega,p}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})},

\displaystyle\quad G_{f,\omega,p}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\|f(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\omega(\,\cdot\,,\xi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})}

\displaystyle,\quad f

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,q}_{*,(\omega)}}

\displaystyle\equiv\|H_{f,\omega,q}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{1}})},

\displaystyle\quad H_{f,\omega,q}(x)

\displaystyle=\|f(x,\,\cdot\,)\omega(x,\,\cdot\,)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbf{R}^{d_{2}})}

\displaystyle,\quad f

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

We put

L^{p,q}=L^{p,q}_{(\omega)}\quad\text{and}\quad L^{p,q}_{*}=L^{p,q}_{*,(\omega)}\quad\text{when}\quad\omega\equiv 1.

We also put
L^{p}_{(\omega)}=L^{p,p}_{(\omega)}
, and observe that

.

1.5.
Invariant quasi-Banach
function spaces

The functional

\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{B}}
, defined on the
vector space
\mathscr{B}
is called a

quasi-norm
of order

r_{0}\in(0,1]
, if the conditions

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{r_{0}}-1}(\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{B}}),

\displaystyle\quad f,g

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{B},

(1.33)

\displaystyle\|\alpha\cdot f\|_{\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle=|\alpha|\cdot\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\quad\alpha

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{C},\quad f\in\mathscr{B}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}

\displaystyle=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f=0,

hold true. A vector space which contains
a quasi-norm (of order
r_{0}
)
is called a quasi-norm space (of order

r_{0}
). A complete quasi-norm space
(of order
r_{0}
) is called a
quasi-Banach space (of order
r_{0}
).
If
\mathscr{B}
is a quasi-Banach space
with quasi-norm satisfying
(
1.33
)
then by
[
3
,
50
]
there is an
equivalent quasi-norm to
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{\mathscr{B}}
of order
r_{0}
, which
additionally satisfies

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}+\|g\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}},\quad f,g\in\mathscr{B}.

(1.34)

From now on we always assume that
the quasi-norm of the quasi-Banach
space
\mathscr{B}
is chosen in such a
way that both
(
1.33
) and
(
1.34
) hold.

The quasi-Banach
space
\mathscr{B}
is called a

quasi-Banach function
space
(or
QBF space
) on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
, whenever
\mathscr{B}
is
contained in

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
It is here
understood that the zero element
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
(which is
an equivalent class), is the zero
element in
\mathscr{B}
.

We always assume that the QBF spaces
are
solid
, which is described
in the following definition.
(See e. g. (A.3) in
[
19
]

Definition 1.12
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a

on

\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

is

(1)

for any

(1.35)

(2)

2.
Some properties of normal QBF spaces

In this section we show some

and

We start with the following,
which explain embedding properties
between
\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}
and

\ell_{\mathscr{B}}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})
on one hand,
and classical Wiener spaces and
discrete Lebesgue spaces on the other
hand.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a normal QBF space
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
with respect to

r_{0}\in(0,1]
and

v_{0}\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, and let

\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.
Then the following is true:

(1)

\ell^{r_{0}}_{(v_{0})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})\hookrightarrow\ell_{\mathscr{B}}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{(1/v_{0})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})
,
and

C^{-1}\|a\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{(1/v_{0})}}\leq\|a\|_{\ell_{\mathscr{B}}}\leq C\|a\|_{\ell^{r_{0}}_{(v_{0})}},\qquad a\in\ell_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Z}^{d}),

(2.1)

where the constant
C
only depends
on
d
and the constant
in (
1.36
);

(2)

\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathscr{B})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\omega,\mathscr{B})
,
and

C^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\omega,\mathscr{B})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathscr{B})},\qquad f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

(2.2)

where the constant
C
only depends
on
\omega
,
d
, and the constant
in (
1.36
).

Proof.

Let

Q=[0,1]^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{r}=[-r,r]^{d},\quad r\geq 0.

The assertion (1) follows if we
prove (
2.1
).
Therefore,
suppose that

a\in\ell^{\infty}_{(1/v_{0})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})
.
Then

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\ell_{\mathscr{B}}}^{r_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}a(j)\chi_{j+Q}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}|a(j)|^{r_{0}}\|\chi_{j+Q}\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}|a(j)v_{0}(j)|^{r_{0}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}\asymp C\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}|a(j)v_{0}(j)|^{r_{0}},

and the second inequality in
(
2.1
) follows.

We also have

\displaystyle\|a\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{(1/v_{0})}}

\displaystyle\asymp\sup_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(|a(j)/v_{0}(j)|\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\mathscr{B}}\right)\leq C\sup_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\||a(j)|\chi_{j+Q}\|_{\mathscr{B}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim C\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}|a(j)|\chi_{j+Q}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}=C\|a\|_{\ell_{\mathscr{B}}},

giving the first inequality in
(
2.1
), and (1)
follows.

The assertion (2) follows if we
prove (
2.2
).
We observe that the map

f\mapsto f\,\omega
is homeomorphic
from
\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}
to
\mathscr{B}
,
and from
\mathsf{W}^{r}(\omega,\mathscr{B})

to
\mathsf{W}^{r}(\mathscr{B})
, for every

r\in(0,\infty]
. This reduces
ourselves to the case when
\omega\equiv 1
.

Since

|f(x)|\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(j+Q)}\chi_{j+Q}(x),

we obtain

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(j+Q)}\chi_{j+Q}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}=\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(\mathscr{B})},

where the last inequality follows
from the fact that
\mathscr{B}
is solid.
This gives the second inequality in
(
2.2
).

It remains to prove the first
inequality in (
2.2
).
By solid properties of the involved
spaces, it is no restriction to
assume that
f\geq 0
.
First we consider the case when

r_{0}=1
.
We have

\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\mathscr{B})}=\|h\|_{\mathscr{B}},

where

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(j+Q)}\chi_{j+Q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{Q}f(y+j)\chi_{Q_{2}}(x-y-j)\,dy\right)\chi_{Q}(x-j).

Here we have taken
y-j
as new
variables of integrations, and
used the fact that

\chi_{Q_{2}}(x-y-j)=1
when

y\in Q
and
x-j\in Q
. By
taking
y+j-x
as new variables
of integrations, and using the fact
that
\chi_{Q_{2}}
is even,
we obtain

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{j-x+Q}f(x+y)\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\,dy\right)\chi_{Q}(x-j)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{Q_{1}}f(x+y)\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\,dy\right)\chi_{Q}(x-j),

where the last inequality follows
from the fact that
y\in Q_{1}
when

y\in j-x+Q
and
x-j\in Q
.
Since
\mathscr{B}
is solid, we obtain

\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\mathscr{B})}=\|h\|_{\mathscr{B}}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{Q_{3}}f(\,\cdot\,+y)\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\,dy\right)\chi_{Q}(\,\cdot\,-j)\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}.

An application of Minkowski’s
inequality now gives

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\mathscr{B})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{3}}\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}f(\,\cdot\,+y)\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\chi_{Q}(\,\cdot\,-j)\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}\,dy

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{Q_{3}}\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}f(\,\cdot\,+y)\cdot\chi_{Q}(\,\cdot\,-j)\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{Q_{3}}\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}\chi_{Q_{2}}(y)\,dy\asymp\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}.

This gives the first
inequality in (
2.2
),
and the result follows in the case

r_{0}=1
.

Next suppose that
r_{0}\in(0,1]

is arbitrary, and let
\mathscr{B}_{0}

be the same as in Definition

1.13
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\mathscr{B})}^{r_{0}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|f\|_{L^{r_{0}}(j+Q)}\chi_{j+Q}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}}=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\|f\|_{L^{r_{0}}(j+Q)}^{r_{0}}\chi_{j+Q}\right\|_{\mathscr{B}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\||f|^{r_{0}}\|_{\mathsf{W}^{1}(\mathscr{B}_{0})}\lesssim\||f|^{r_{0}}\|_{\mathscr{B}_{0}}=\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}}^{r_{0}},

where the inequality follows from
the previous part of the proof.
This gives the first
inequality in (
2.2
),
and the result follows.
∎

As a consequence of
Proposition
2.1
we get

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be a normal QBF space
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
with respect to

and

, and
let

.
Then

\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(\omega v_{0},\ell^{r_{0}}(\mathbf{Z}^{d}))\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\omega/v_{0},\ell^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})),

and

\displaystyle C^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\omega/v_{0},\ell^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{(\omega)}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathsf{W}^{\infty}(\omega v_{0},\ell^{r_{0}})},

(2.3)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\in\mathsf{W}^{r_{0}}(\omega/v_{0},\ell^{\infty}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})),

where the constant
C
only depends
on
d
,
\omega
and the constant
in (
1.36
).

Proof.

If
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

p,q\in(0,\infty]
, then the facts
that
\omega_{j}
,
j=1,2
, are moderate imply

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}^{p}(\omega_{1}\omega_{2},\ell^{q}(\mathbf{Z}^{d}))

\displaystyle=\mathsf{W}^{p}(\omega_{1},\ell^{q}_{(\omega_{2})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d}))

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{W}^{p}(\omega_{2},\ell^{q}_{(\omega_{1})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d}))=\mathsf{W}^{p}(1,\ell^{q}_{(\omega_{1}\omega_{2})}(\mathbf{Z}^{d})),

with equivalent quasi-norms.
By combining these identities with
Proposition
2.1
,

5.
Convolutions and multiplications in
Wiener amalgam and modulation spaces

In this section we deduce convolution and multiplication properties
for Wiener amalgam spaces, which extends analogous results in

[

65

to more general modulation spaces. In the first

convolutions and multiplications. Thereafter we deduce estimates

in background of Wiener amalgam spaces and modulation spaces.

5.1.
Wiener amalgam estimates for
non-commutative convolutions

We observe that the twisted
convolution (as well as the ordinary
convolution) is a mapping of the form

It is obvious that

from

Evidently, the corresponding discrete version of (

6.
Continuity properties for pseudo-differential
operators

In this section we make a review of some facts about
pseudo-differential operators. Thereafter we deduce
continuity for pseudo-differential operators with symbols
in suitable modulation spaces, when acting between
other modulation spaces.

Let
\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
be the set of
d\times d
-matrices with
entries in
\mathbf{R}
,
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and let
A\in\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
be fixed. Then the
pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})

is the linear and continuous operator on
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, given by

(\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\iint\mathfrak{a}(x-A(x-y),\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}\,dyd\xi,\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}

(6.1)

(see e. g. Chapter XVIII in
[
40
]
).
For general
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

pseudo-differential operator
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
is defined as the continuous
operator from
\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
to
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with
distribution kernel

K_{\mathfrak{a},A}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}(\mathscr{F}_{2}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})(x-A(x-y),x-y),\quad x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(6.2)

Here
\mathscr{F}_{2}F
is the partial Fourier transform of
F(x,y)\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
with respect to the
y
variable. This
definition makes sense since the mappings

\mathscr{F}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,y)\mapsto F(x-A(x-y),x-y)

(6.3)

are homeomorphisms on

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, and their duals,
s\geq 1
.
In particular we have the following.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbf{M}(d,\mathbf{R})
,
and
K_{\mathfrak{a},A}
be as in (
6.2
),
when
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Then the map
\mathfrak{a}\mapsto K_{\mathfrak{a},A}
is a homeomorphism on

\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
, which restricts to homeomorphisms on

\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}),\quad\mathcal{S}_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d}).

The standard (Kohn-Nirenberg) representation,
\mathfrak{a}(x,D)=\operatorname{Op}(\mathfrak{a})
, and
the Weyl quantization
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{a})
of
\mathfrak{a}
are obtained by choosing

A=0
and

, respectively, in (
6.1
)
and (
6.2
), where

is the identity matrix.

Remark 6.2
.

Let

. By Fourier’s inversion formula, (

) and the kernel theorem

[

, Theorem 2.2]
for operators from
Gelfand-Shilov spaces to their duals,
it follows that the map

to the set of all linear and continuous operators
from
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

to
\Sigma_{s}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

with
\mathcal{S}_{s}
or
\mathscr{S}
in place of
\Sigma_{s}

at each occurrence.

By Remark
6.2
, it follows that for every

and

\mathfrak{a}_{2}\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

.
Indeed, the relation between

and
\mathfrak{a}_{2}

[

[

Here we note that the operator

on
\Sigma_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

\mathcal{S}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,

\mathscr{S}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})

(cf.
[
8
,
68
]
).

We also recall that
\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})
is a rank-one operator, i. e.

\operatorname{Op}_{A}(\mathfrak{a})f=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(f,f_{2})f_{1},\qquad f\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),

is equal to the

(Cf. e. g.
[
62
]

.

7.

In this section we make a review of some facts about
Toeplitz operators. Thereafter we deduce

in suitable modulation spaces, when acting
between other modulation spaces. As in

[

,
56
,

,
58

the main idea is to
express the Toeplitz operators as

with symbols being a convolution

Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
.
Then the Toeplitz
operator
\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})
,

\mathfrak{a}
, and window functions

\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\mathfrak{a})f_{1},f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=(\mathfrak{a}\cdot V_{\phi_{1}}f_{1},V_{\phi_{2}}f_{2})_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})}

(7.1)

when
f_{1},f_{2}\in\Sigma_{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

8.
Lifting properties
for modulation spaces

In this section we extend the lifting
properties given in
[
36
,
2
]

to the extended class of modulation
spaces under consideration.
In the first part we perform a general
review and explanations concerning
lifting properties on modulation spaces, given
in
[
2
]
. Thereafter
we deduce the extensions of these properties
to our extended class of modulation spaces.

We recall from
[
36
,
2
]

that the topological vector spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}

are said to possess
lifting property if there exists a ”convenient”
homeomorphisms
(a lifting) between them. For example, let

p\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in\mathbf{R}
, and
set

\langle x\rangle=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Then the mappings

f\mapsto\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{s}\cdot f\quad\text{and}\quad f\mapsto\langle\Delta\rangle^{s}f

(8.1)

are homeomorphic from
L^{p}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and
the Sobolev space
H^{p}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively,
into
L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=H^{p}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,
with inverses
f\mapsto\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{-s}\cdot f

and
f\mapsto\langle\Delta\rangle^{-s}f
,
respectively. Here
L^{p}_{s}=L^{p}_{(\omega)}

when
\omega=\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{s}
.

By slightly more cumbersome arguments, one
deduces similar mapping properties for
classical modulation spaces. That is,
suppose that
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,

\omega_{1}(x,\xi)=\langle x\rangle^{s}
,
and
\omega_{2}(x,\xi)=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}
. Then
the mappings in (
8.1
)
are homeomorphisms from

M^{p,q}_{(\omega_{1})}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

and

M^{p,q}_{(\omega_{2})}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, respectively,
into
M^{p,q}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. (See e. g.

[
56
, Theorem 2.2]
.)

We observe that the symbols for the
operators in (
8.1
)
are given by
\langle x\rangle^{s}
and
\langle\xi\rangle^{s}
,
and thereby only depends purely on
the configuration variable
x
or the
momentum variable
\xi
.
The situation becomes drastically more
complicated if the involved operators
depend on both
x
and
\xi
variables,
because of (heavy) interactions between
multiplications and differentiations.

Nevertheless, in
[
2
,
36
]
one
reach lifting results for modulation
spaces, with more general
pseudo-differential
operators and Toeplitz operators,
using delicate combinations of various
methods in time-frequency analysis, micro-local
analysis and spectral theory.

In order to explain these results, we need some
preparations.

8.1.
Classes of smooth
symbols

Next we recall from
[
2
,
8
]

the definition of
spaces of smooth functions
which in several ways are suitable
as symbol classes for pseudo-differential
operators.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

(1)

The class
S^{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

such that

\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{(1/\omega)}}

is finite, for every
\alpha\in\mathbf{N}^{d}
.

(2)

The class
\Gamma^{(\omega)}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

(
\Gamma^{(\omega)}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
) consists of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
such that

\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbf{N}^{d}}\left(\frac{\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{(1/\omega)}}}{h^{|\alpha|}\alpha!^{s}}\right)

is finite, for
some

h>0

(for
every

h>0
).

Evidently, we have

\Gamma^{(\omega)}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq\Gamma^{(\omega)}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\subsetneq S^{(\omega)}(\mathbf{R}^{d}),\quad\text{when}\quad\omega\in\mathscr{P}_{E}(\mathbf{R}^{d}).

On the other hand, one usually
impose that
\omega
should belong
to
\mathscr{P}_{s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
,

\mathscr{P}_{s}^{0}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and

\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
when considering

\Gamma^{(\omega)}_{0,s}(\mathbf{R}^{d})

\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi}(\omega_{0})=\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{b})\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi}(\omega_{0})^{-1}=\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{c})\,.

For
X,Y\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
, let

\omega_{2}(X,Y)=\vartheta(X-Y)\vartheta(X+Y)v_{1}(2Y)\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{3}(X,Y)=\frac{\vartheta(X+Y)}{\vartheta(X-Y)}.

(8.13)

By Theorem
6.4

and Lemma
8.16

it follows that

\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty,1}_{(\omega_{2})}(\mathbf{R}^{2d})
,
and that the mappings

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{b})\,

\displaystyle:\,M(\omega\vartheta,\mathscr{B})\to M(\omega/\vartheta,\mathscr{B})

(8.14)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{c})\,

\displaystyle:\,M(\omega/\vartheta,\mathscr{B})\to M(\omega\vartheta,\mathscr{B})

(8.15)

are continuous.

This gives

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(X-Y+Z,Z)\omega_{2}(X+Z,Y-Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{v(2Z)^{1/2}v_{1}(2Z)}{\vartheta(X-Y+2Z)\vartheta(X-Y)}\Big)\cdot\big(\vartheta(X-Y+2Z)\vartheta(X+Y)v_{1}(2(Y-Z))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v(2Z)^{1/2}v_{1}(2Z)v_{1}(2(Y-Z))\,\vartheta(X+Y)}{\vartheta(X-Y)}

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{\vartheta(X+Y)}{\vartheta(X-Y)}=\omega_{3}(X,Y)\,\qquad X,Y,Z\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}.

Therefore Proposition
8.14

shows that
\mathfrak{b}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{M}^{\infty,1}_{(\omega_{3})}
. Since

\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{b})
is an isomorphism from

M^{2}_{(\vartheta)}
to
M^{2}_{(1/\vartheta)}

with inverse
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{c})
, it follows that

\mathfrak{b}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{c}=1
and that the constant
symbol
1
belongs to

\mathcal{M}^{\infty,1}_{(\omega_{3})}
. By similar
arguments it follows that

\mathfrak{c}{\text{\footnotesize$\#$}}\mathfrak{b}=1
. Therefore the identity
operator
\mathrm{Id}=\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{b})\circ\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{c})
on

M(\omega/\vartheta,\mathscr{B})
factors through

M(\omega\vartheta,\mathscr{B})
,
and thus
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{b})=\operatorname{Tp}_{\phi}(\omega_{0})
is an isomorphism
from
M(\omega\vartheta,\mathscr{B})
to

M(\omega/\vartheta,\mathscr{B})

with inverse
\operatorname{Op}^{w}(\mathfrak{c})
.
This gives the result.

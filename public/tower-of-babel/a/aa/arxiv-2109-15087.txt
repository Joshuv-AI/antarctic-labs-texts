Testing leptogenesis at the LHC and future muon colliders: a Z ′ Z^{\prime} scenario
Wei Liu, Ke-Pan Xie, Zihan Yi

Abstract
If the masses of at least two generations of right-handed neutrinos (RHNs) are near-degenerate, the scale of leptogenesis can be as low as ∼ \sim 100 GeV. In this work, we study probing such resonant leptogenesis in the B − L B-L model at the LHC and future multi-TeV muon colliders via the process Z ′ → N ​ N → ℓ ± ​ ℓ ± + jets Z^{\prime}\to NN\to\ell^{\pm}\ell^{\pm}+{\rm jets} , with Z ′ Z^{\prime} the U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} gauge boson and N N the RHN. The same-sign dilepton feature of the signal makes it almost background-free, while the event number difference between positive and negative leptons is a hint for C ​ P CP violation, which is a key ingredient of leptogenesis. We found that resonant leptogenesis can be tested at the HL-LHC for M Z ′ M_{Z^{\prime}} up to 12 TeV, while at a 10 (30) TeV muon collider the reach can be up to M Z ′ ∼ 30 ​ ( 100 ) M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 30~(100) TeV via the off-shell production of Z ′ Z^{\prime} .

Introduction

The baryon asymmetry of the Universe (BAU) is one of the most mysterious unsolved problems in the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics. Leptogenesis is a very attractive explanation

;

;
Davidson et al. 2008

CP

Davis 1994
;
Fukuda et al. 1998
;
Eguchi et al. 2003
via the Type-I seesaw mechanism
Minkowski 1977

CP
violating effects are related to the RHN mass
M_{N}

;
Pilaftsis 1997
;

;

;

We have shown in the previous subsection that the backgrounds for the same-sign dilepton channel are negligible after imposing a set of selection cuts, which however keep the signal rates almost unchanged. There are also other channels available for the
Z^{\prime}\to NN
process (e.g.
N\to\nu h/\nu Z
), which can further increase the signal significance of the model. Based on this consideration, we make the zero background assumption in this subsection to derive the projected sensitivities for the thermal leptogenesis parameter space of the
B-L
model. The
CP
asymmetry
\epsilon
defined in Eq. (
4
) is related to the asymmetry between the positive and negative same-sign dileptons as
Blanchet et al. 2010

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{N_{+}-N_{-}}{N_{+}+N_{-}}\right|,

(22)

where
N_{\pm}
denotes the event number of the
e^{\pm}e^{\pm}W^{\mp}W^{\mp}
final state. If we observe no asymmetry of the same-sign dileptons at colliders, upper limits can be put on the
CP
asymmetry. This is done by assuming the number of the signal events to follow a Poisson distribution, and hence at 1
\sigma
confidence level
Zyla et al. 2020

\displaystyle N_{+}=\left\langle N_{+}\right\rangle\pm\Delta N_{+}=\left\langle N_{+}\right\rangle\pm\sqrt{\left\langle N_{+}\right\rangle},

(23)

yielding a sensitivities of

\epsilon_{\rm min}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\left\langle N_{+}\right\rangle}}.

(24)

The
CP
asymmetry is detectable if
\epsilon>\epsilon_{\rm min}
.

With Eq. (
24
) in hand, one is able to test resonant leptogenesis at a specific collider environment. Given a set of
(M_{Z^{\prime}},M_{N},g_{B-L})
, the
\epsilon
required by leptogenesis is derived by Eq. (
16
). On the other hand, the corresponding
N_{+}
and hence
\epsilon_{\rm min}
is also available by collider simulation of the
Z^{\prime}\to NN
process. If
\epsilon>\epsilon_{\rm min}
, then this parameter setup is detectable at the collider. The projected detectable parameter space with a fixed
g_{B-L}=0.8
is plotted in Fig.
4
for the HL-LHC (13 TeV, 3 ab
-1
) and two setups of muon colliders (10 TeV, 10 ab
-1
and 30 TeV, 90 ab
-1
) with different colors. As the signal significance is proportional to the
CP
asymmetry
\epsilon
, the reachable regions have similar shapes to the
\epsilon
contours in the left panel of Fig.
1
, except that there is a vertical band-like region in the 10 TeV muon collider case near
M_{Z^{\prime}}\approx 10
TeV due to the enhancement from resonant production of
Z^{\prime}
. The region shaded in dark blue is for
\epsilon>1
or
M_{N}<T_{\rm sph}
that the leptogenesis is not feasible. As we can see, the HL-LHC is able to probe leptogenesis for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 12
TeV with a RHN mass
M_{N}\lesssim 600
GeV, corresponding to the
CP
asymmetry
\epsilon\sim 0.2
. While for the muon colliders, due to the higher production rates and integrated luminosities, smaller
\epsilon\sim 10^{-3}
can be probed and a much higher mass region can be reached. At the 10 TeV muon collider, if the RHN is as light as a few hundreds of GeV, leptogenesis with
M_{Z^{\prime}}
up to 28 TeV can be probed via the off-shell process
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\to Z^{\prime*}\to NN
. The 30 TeV muon collider can cover a wider parameter space via the on-shell
Z^{\prime}Z
or
Z^{\prime}\gamma

M_{Z^{\prime}}
up to 30 TeV, and via the off-shell production for
M_{Z^{\prime}}
up to 100 TeV. We also notice that the parameter space for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim\mathcal{O}(10~{\rm TeV})
and
M_{N}\sim 2

\epsilon
needed for BAU is too small (see the contours in Fig.
1
). Although larger
\epsilon
can be generated from tuning the
\Delta M_{N}
which is a free parameter, the resulting BAU will be greater than the observation value. However, a band in this region can be filled up by the 10 TeV muon collider for
M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 10
TeV, due to the resonant enhancement of the
Z^{\prime}Z/Z^{\prime}\gamma
process. In summary, a considerable fraction of parameter space can be probed at the future muon colliders.

IV
Conclusion

Both the BAU and tiny neutrino mass can be explained simultaneously within the framework of the thermal leptogenesis via the RHNs. If two of the RHNs are nearly degenerate, the scale of leptogenesis can be as low as
\sim
100 GeV and hence accessible at current and future colliders. In this article, we take the
B-L
model as an example to perform a study on the collider probe of the leptogenesis mechanism. In this model, the new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
mediates a scattering process that tends to washout the BAU. Therefore, if both
Z^{\prime}
and
N
are at
\mathcal{O}({\rm TeV})
scale, a sizable
CP
asymmetry
\epsilon
is needed to explain the observed BAU. In other words, successful (resonant) leptogenesis can be realized via TeV scale
Z^{\prime}
,
N
and a sizable
\epsilon
, which are testable at the colliders.

We choose the channel
Z^{\prime}\to NN\to\ell^{\pm}\ell^{\pm}+{\rm jets}
to probe leptogenesis, as this process involves the three key ingredients of the scenario: a new gauge boson
Z^{\prime}
, the RHN
N
, and the
CP
violation
\epsilon
from the asymmetry between
N(\ell^{+}\ell^{+})
and
N(\ell^{-}\ell^{-})
. Three collider setups are considered: 13 TeV LHC with 3 ab
-1
and 10 (30) TeV muon colliders with 10 (90) ab
-1
. At the LHC, the
Z^{\prime}
is produced via the Drell-Yan process; while at the muon colliders,
Z^{\prime}
can be produced in association with a
Z^{\prime}
or
\gamma
, or via the off-shell
s
-channel fusion. We have shown that the backgrounds are safely negligible after a set of selection cuts, and hence the sensitivities for
\epsilon
can be derived under the zero background assumption. Our quantitative study shows that leptogenesis in the
B-L
model can be probed at the LHC for
6~{\rm TeV}\lesssim M_{Z^{\prime}}\lesssim 12
TeV and
M_{N}\sim{\rm TeV}
. At the muon colliders, due to the higher signal rates, the projected probe limits can cover
M_{Z^{\prime}}
up to 30 TeV or even higher. For the 30 TeV muon collider, the leptogenesis can be probed up to
M_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 100
TeV via the off-shell production of
Z^{\prime}
. Our work demonstrates that the muon colliders can serve as a machine to efficiently probe the early Universe dynamics.

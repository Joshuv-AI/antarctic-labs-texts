On Affine Hilbert Functions of Unions of Layers in Finite Grids
S. Venkitesh IIT Bombay, Mumbai, India

Abstract
The affine Hilbert function is a classical algebraic object that has been central, among other tools, to the development of the polynomial method in combinatorics. Owing to its concrete connections with Gröbner basis theory, as well as its applicability in several areas like computational complexity, combinatorial geometry, and coding theory, an important line of enquiry is to understand the affine Hilbert function of structured sets of points in the affine space. In this work, we determine the affine Hilbert function (over the reals) of arbitrary unions of layers of points in a uniform grid (a finite grid with the component sets having equispaced points), where each layer of points is determined by a fixed sum of components for all the points. This extends a result of Bernasconi and Egidi (Inf. Comput. 1999) from the Boolean cube setting to the uniform grid setting. Our proofs follow a similar outline as that of Bernasconi and Egidi. However, there are two bottlenecks that arise in the uniform grid setting. We resolve these by using (i) a classical fact that a symmetric Jordan basis exists for the function space on a uniform grid, and (ii) an extension to multisets of an algebraic interpretation by Friedl and Rónyai (Discrete Math. 2003) of the notion of order shattering . The affine Hilbert function is, in fact, a stronger notion than the finite-degree Zariski closure , which is yet another important tool in the polynomial method toolkit. We conclude by giving an alternate proof of a combinatorial characterization of a variant of finite-degree Zariski closures, for unions of layers in uniform grids, obtained in an earlier work of the author (arXiv, 2021).

1.1
Motivation

Consider the Boolean cube
\{0,1\}^{n}

|x|\coloneqq\mathrm{wt}(x)=\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}.

Let

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in A,\,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\quad\implies\quad(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)})\in A.

It is easy to check that symmetric sets in

Remark 1.1
.

In general, over uniform grids, weight-determined sets and symmetric sets (sets invariant under permutations of coordinates) are different. For instance, take a uniform grid

is a weight-determined set that is not symmetric. Further, the Boolean cube

is a symmetric set that is not weight-determined.

Our result

Our main theorem is an extension of Theorem

1.3
Uniform grid vis à vis Boolean cube: Bottlenecks in extending Theorem
1.2
, and their resolutions

It is interesting to note that the proof of Theorem
1.2
does not readily extend to give Theorem
1.5
. More specifically, there are two main arguments which do not extend to the setting of uniform grids. We list these bottlenecks here, and outline how we solve them.

Let us first fix a few more notations. Let
G
be a uniform grid. Consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{X}]
over the indeterminates
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
. For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we define the monomial
\mathbb{X}^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. Now, for any
i\in[n]
and
a\in\mathbb{N}
, define
Y_{i}^{(a)}=X_{i}(X_{i}-1)\cdots(X_{i}-a+1)
. Further, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define
\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}=Y_{1}^{(\alpha_{1})}\cdots Y_{n}^{(\alpha_{n})}
. Finally, for any
D,E\subseteq[0,N]
, let
\mathrm{Ev}_{D,E}\in M_{\underline{D}\times\underline{E}}(\mathbb{R})
be the evaluation matrix defined by

\mathrm{Ev}_{D,E}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}(\beta)=\alpha!\binom{\beta}{\alpha},\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\underline{D},\,\beta\in\underline{E}.

It follows by definition that
\mathrm{H}_{d}(E)=\rank(\Ev_{[0,d],E})
, for all
E\subseteq[0,N]
and
d\in[0,N]
.

(a)

Consider the statement of Theorem
1.5
for a single layer. In particular, since
G
is unimodal, it claims the following.

Claim.
For any
d\in[0,N]
, if
j\in[d,N-d]
, then
\mathrm{H}_{d}(j)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d}
.

In the Boolean cube setting, this claim follows easily by Wilson’s rank formula
[
Wil90
, Theorem 1]
, in characteristic zero. The proof of Wilson’s rank formula is via induction on the number of indeterminates
n
. It is quite straightforward to check that such an inductive argument would fail in the case of larger uniform grids. This is the first bottleneck.

We remedy this issue by dropping the inductive argument altogether. Instead, we make use of a classical fact about ranked posets. To get to it, let us consider some definitions. Let
(P,\leq)
be a ranked poset with rank function
\rho
, and let
h=\max_{a\in P}\rho(a)
. For every
t\in[0,h]
, let
P_{t}=\{a\in P:\rho(a)=t\}
. For any
A\subseteq P
, let
V(A)
denote the vector space of all functions
A\to\mathbb{R}
. It is then clear that
V(P)=V(P_{0})\oplus\cdots\oplus V(P_{h})
. For any
a\in P
, by abuse of notation, we denote by
a
, the function on
P
that takes the value 1 at
a
, and the value 0 everywhere else. Define the
up operator
on
P
to be the linear map
U:V(P)\to V(P)
given by

U(a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in P,\,a\leq b\\
\rho(b)=\rho(a)+1\end{subarray}}b,\quad\text{for all }a\in P.

For the uniform grid
G
, it is easy to see that the above mentioned claim will follow quickly if we can show that the restriction of the up operator
U:V(\underline{j})\to V(\underline{j+1})
is injective, for all
j\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor-1]
. Incidentally, this is an immediate corollary of a classical result that a
symmetric Jordan basis
exists for
V(G)
.
2
2

2

Note that here, we consider
G
as a ranked poset with rank function
\mathrm{wt}
.
See Canfield
[
RC80
]
, Proctor, Saks, and Sturtevart
[

]

]

(b)

Consider the following technical lemma about ranks of evaluation matrices. We need this lemma in an inductive argument in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, in Section
4

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For
d\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor]
and any

\rank(\Ev_{d,E})=\rank(\Ev_{d,\min E}).

In the Boolean cube setting, this lemma again follows by a simple induction on the number of indeterminates

. And yet again, such an induction fails for larger uniform grids. This is the second bottleneck.

The notion of
order shattering
provides an alternative approach, which remedies this issue. In the Boolean cube setting, Friedl and Rónyai
[

used order shattering and generalized Wilson’s rank formula to determine the rank of
\mathrm{Ev}_{D,w}
, for any
D\subseteq[0,\lfloor n/2\rfloor]
with

. This proves the above lemma by a
dualization
, via the identity:
(\mathrm{Ev}_{D,E})^{t}=\mathrm{Ev}_{n-E,n-D}
, for all
D,E\subseteq[0,n]
.

3

3

Here, for any
F\subseteq[0,n]
, we have
n-F\coloneqq\{n-a:a\in F\}
.
Such an identity is clearly not true over larger uniform grids. Nevertheless, we can extend the notion of order shattering and its properties (in an elementary manner) to uniform grids, and give a proof of the lemma, without appealing to any dualization. The formal details are presented in Section

1.4
An application: the finite-degree Z*-closure of weight-determined sets

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d\in[0,N]
and
A\subseteq G
, the
degree-
d
Zariski (Z-) closure
of
A
, denoted by
\text{Z-cl}_{G,d}(A)
, is defined to be the common zero set, in
G
, of all polynomials with degree at most
d
, that vanish at each point in
A
. Formally introduced by Nie and Wang
[
NW15
]
(for any set of points in the affine space) with the aim to further the polynomial method in combinatorial geometry, it has appeared implicitly much earlier in the literature (see, for instance, Wei
[
Wei91
]
, Heijnen and Pellikaan
[
HP98
]
, Keevash and Sudakov
[
KS05
]
, and Ben-Eliezer, Hod, and Lovett
[
BEHL12
]
).

In an earlier work of the author
[
Ven21
]
, the finite-degree Z-closures were considered in the context of
polynomial covering problems
for weight-determined sets in a uniform grid. Since these polynomial covering problems impose vanishing conditions on polynomial functions on weight-determined sets, it is a natural question to determine the finite-degree Z-closures of weight-determined sets. As noted in
[
Ven21
]
, the finite-degree Z-closure of a weight-determined set need not be weight-determined, and so a variant of the Z-closures is more suitable – the finite-degree Z*-closure – defined exclusively for weight-determined sets.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d\in[0,N]
and
E\subseteq[0,N]
, the
degree-
d
Z*-closure
of
\underline{E}
, denoted by
\text{Z*-cl}_{G,d}(\underline{E})
, is defined to be the maximal weight-determined set contained in
\text{Z-cl}_{G,d}(\underline{E})
. The finite-degree Z*-closure is clearly a weight-determined set. So we can use our identification of weight-determined sets with subsets of
[0,N]
, while describing these closures. Further, in the Boolean cube setting, by definition, the finite-degree Z-closures and Z*-closures coincide for symmetric sets.

For any
d\in[0,N]
, consider the set operator
L_{N,d}:2^{[0,N]}\to 2^{[0,N]}
, defined in
[
Ven21
]
as follows. For any
E=\{t_{1}<\cdots<t_{s}\}\subseteq[0,N]
, let

L_{N,d}(E)=\begin{cases}E&\text{if }s\leq d\\
[0,t_{s-d}]\cup E\cup[t_{d+1},N]&\text{if }s\geq d+1\end{cases}

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, recursively define
L_{N,d}^{k+1}=L_{N,d}\circ L_{N,d}^{k}
. Further, for any
E\subseteq[0,N]
, define
\overline{L}_{N,d}(E)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}L_{N,d}^{k}(E)
.

The combinatorial characterization of finite-degree Z*-closures in
[
Ven21
]
is for a subclass of uniform grids, called
strictly unimodal uniform
(SU
2
) grids. A uniform grid
G
is said to be SU
2
if

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{0}<\cdots<\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{\lceil N/2\rceil}>\cdots>\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{N}.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
Ven21
, Theorem 1.7]
)
.

Let
G
be an
SU
2
grid. For any
d\in[0,N]
and
E\subseteq[0,N]
,

\text{Z*-cl}_{G,d}(E)=\overline{L}_{N,d}(E).

The following fact connects the notions of finite-degree Z*-closures and affine Hilbert functions, for weight-determined sets. This shows that the affine Hilbert function is a stronger notion. The proof of the fact is obvious from, for instance, Nie and Wang
[
NW15
]
and the definitions.

Fact 1.8
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d\in[0,N]
and
E\subseteq[0,N]
, we have
\mathrm{H}_{d}(E)=\mathrm{H}_{d}(\text{Z*-cl}_{G,d}(E))
.

A digression: why consider only uniform grids?

A typical grid that is nonuniform would be of the form
H=\{t_{0}^{(1)}<\cdots<t_{k_{1}-1}^{(1)}\}\times\cdots\times\{t_{0}^{(n)}<\cdots<t_{k_{n}-1}^{(n)}\}
, where the differences
t_{i+1}^{(j)}-t_{i}^{(j)},\,i\in[0,k_{j}-2]
are not equal, for some
j\in[n]
. In this way, a natural way to define the weight of an element
x\in H
is
\mathrm{wt}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{j\in[n]}s_{j}
, where
x_{j}=t_{s_{j}}^{(j)}
, for all
j\in[n]
.
4
4

4

In fact,
H
is a ranked poset with
\mathrm{wt}
as the rank function.

Consider an example: let
G=\{0,1,2\}^{2}
and
H=\{0,1,3\}^{2}
. For both grids, the dimensions are the same, and
N=4
. Consequently,
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{j}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{H}{j}
, for all
j\in[0,4]
. However, the grid
G
is uniform, but the grid
H
is nonuniform.
It is easy to see that
\mathrm{H}_{d}(\{a,2\})>\mathrm{H}_{d}(\{2\})
, for all
a\in[0,4],\,a\neq 2
over
G
, but
\mathrm{H}_{d}(\{a,2\})=\mathrm{H}_{d}(\{2\})
, for all
a\in[0,4],\,a\neq 2
over
H
. In view of Theorem
1.5
, this shows that the affine Hilbert functions over
H
do not depend only on the weights of the points in the set, but also on the coordinates of the points, which is undesirable. So the ‘uniform’ condition ensures that the setting is nice enough for the results to be
combinatorially neat
.

Affine Hilbert function of a single layer

Recall that in a uniform grid
G
, a layer is a weight-determined set of the form
\underline{j},\,j\in[0,N]
. In this section, we will determine the affine Hilbert functions of single layers. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 3.1

(Affine Hilbert function of a single layer)
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid and
d,w\in[0,N]
. Then
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\min\big\{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d},\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}\big\}
.

As mentioned in Subsection
1.3
, the arguments in the Boolean cube setting do not extend to the uniform grid setting, and we will appeal to a classical result that a
symmetric Jordan basis
exists for
V(G)
. Several proofs of existence are known (Canfield
[
RC80
]
, Proctor, Saks, and Sturtevart
[
PSS80
]
, Proctor
[
Pro82
]
), and a constructive proof was given by Srinivasan
[
Sri11
]
. An immediate consequence is the injectivity of the up operator, as mentioned in Subsection
1.3
. The following is the formal statement, in the form that we require it.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
RC80
,
PSS80
,
Pro82
,
Sri11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid and
d\in[0,N-1]
. Consider the matrix
U_{d,d+1}\in M_{\underline{d}\times\underline{d+1}}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

U_{d,d+1}(\alpha,\beta)=\begin{cases}1,&\alpha\leq\beta\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Then
U_{d,d+1}
has full rank, that is,
\rank(U_{d,d+1})=\min\big\{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d},\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d+1}\big\}
.

We will also need another lemma. For
i\in[n]
, let
e_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
be defined by
(e_{i})_{j}=1
if and only if
j=i
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d,w\in[0,N],\,d<w
, we have as functions in
V(w)
,

\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{w-d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\geq\alpha\\
\beta\in\underline{d+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{Y}^{(\beta)},\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\underline{d}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\underline{d}
and
I_{\alpha}=\{i\in[n]:\beta_{i}<k_{i}-1\}
. We then have, as functions in
V(w)
,

\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}X_{i}-\bigg(w-\sum_{i\not\in I_{\alpha}}(k_{i}-1)\bigg)\bigg)=0.

This gives

\displaystyle\bigg(w-\sum_{i\not\in I_{\alpha}}(k_{i}-1)\bigg)\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}X_{i}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}\big(\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}(X_{i}-\alpha_{i})+\alpha_{i}\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha+e_{i})}+\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}\alpha_{i}\bigg).

Now
w-\sum_{i\not\in I_{\alpha}}(k_{i}-1)-\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}\alpha_{i}=w-\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)=w-d>0
. So we get

\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{1}{w-d}\sum_{i\in I_{\alpha}}\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha+e_{i})}=\frac{1}{w-d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\geq\alpha\\
\beta\in\underline{d+1}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{Y}^{(\beta)}.\qed

Repeatedly applying Lemma
3.3
immediately gives us an important corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d,w\in[0,N],\,d<w
,

V_{d}(w)\subseteq\text{span}\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in\underline{d}\}.

For every
w\in[0,N]
, we clearly have
\mathrm{Ev}_{w,w}=\mathrm{diag}_{w}
, and so
\rank(\Ev_{w,w})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}
. Further, Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
together immediately give us the following.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
d,w\in[0,N],\,d<w
, we have

\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}=\frac{1}{(w-d)!}U_{d,d+1}\cdots U_{w-1,w}\mathrm{diag}_{w}.

In particular, if
w\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
,
\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}
has full rank, that is,
\rank(\Ev_{d,w})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d}
. Also in particular, for
d<d^{\prime}<w
, we have

\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}=\frac{1}{(w-d)\cdots(w-d^{\prime}+1)}\mathrm{Ev}_{d^{\prime},w}=\frac{1}{(d^{\prime}-d)!\binom{w-d}{w-d^{\prime}}}\mathrm{Ev}_{d^{\prime},w}.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Since
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\dim V_{d}(w)
, the evaluation matrix of concern to us is
\mathrm{Ev}_{[0,d],w}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}\\
\mathrm{Ev}_{d-1,w}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Ev}_{0,w}\end{bmatrix}
. If
d\geq w
, then we have
\mathrm{Ev}_{[0,d],w}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Ev}_{w,w}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Ev}_{0,w}\end{bmatrix}
, and
\mathrm{Ev}_{w,w}=\mathrm{diag}_{w}
. So
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\rank(\Ev_{[0,d],w})=\rank(\Ev_{w,w})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}
.

Now suppose
d<w
. By Corollary
3.4
, we conclude that
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\rank(\Ev_{d,w})
. If
w\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
, then by Corollary
3.5
, we have
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\min\big\{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{d},\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}\big\}
. Now suppose

. Then
N-w\leq\lfloor N/2\rfloor
. Note that for any
P(\mathbb{X})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{X}]
, we have
P(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\text{span}\big\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{\le d}\big\}
if and only if
P(k_{1}-1-X_{1},\ldots,k_{n}-1-X_{n})\in\text{span}\big\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{\le d}\big\}
. This means

and

are isomorphic vector spaces, and so
\mathrm{H}_{d}(w)=\mathrm{H}_{d}(N-w)

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{N-w}
.
∎

In this section, we consider the notion of

[

Let us first look at the main result required to prove Lemma

6.1
An algebraic lemma, and the proof of Lemma
1.6

For convenience, let us restate Lemma
1.6
.

Lemma
1.6
.

We require the following algebraic lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid, and let
\preceq
be the lexicographic order on
G
induced by the order
1>\cdots>n
on the coordinates. For any
i,j\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor]

i\leq j
, then
\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{i})\subseteq\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{j})
.

We will prove Lemma
6.2
in Subsection
6.2

6.2
, and prove Lemma
1.6
.

Proof of Lemma
1.6
.

Note that for any
E\subseteq F\subseteq[N-d+1,N]
with
\min E=\min F
, it is clear that

\rank(\Ev_{d,\min E})\leq\rank(\Ev_{d,E})\leq\rank(\Ev_{d,F}).

So it is enough to prove
\rank(\Ev_{d,[w,N]})=\rank(\Ev_{d,w})
, for all
w\in[N-d+1,N]
. We will prove this by induction on
w\in[N-d+1,N]
. The assertion is trivial if
w=N
. So now consider any
w\in[N-d+1,N],\,w<N
. We have

\mathrm{Ev}_{d,[w,N]}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}&\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w+1}&\mathrm{Ev}_{[w+2,N]}\end{bmatrix}.

By induction hypothesis, we have
\rank(\Ev_{d,[w+1,N]})=\rank(\Ev_{d,w+1})
, and so there exists an invertible matrix
C\in M_{\underline{[w+1,N]}\times\underline{[w+1,N]}}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\rank(E')=\rank(\Ev_{d,[w,N]})
, where

E^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathrm{Ev}_{d,[w,N]}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}I_{\underline{w}\times\underline{w}}&O\\
O&C\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}&\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w+1}&O\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
, it is enough to show that
\rank\big(\begin{bmatrix}\Ev_{d,w}&\Ev_{d,w+1}\end{bmatrix}\big)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}
. Without loss of generality, let
B\subseteq\underline{d}
such that the rows of
\mathrm{Ev}_{B,w+1}
form a basis of the rowspace of
\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w+1}
. So there exists an invertible matrix
R\in M_{\underline{d}\times\underline{d}}
such that
\rank(E'')=\rank\big(\begin{bmatrix}\Ev_{d,w}&\Ev_{d,w+1}\end{bmatrix}\big)
, where

E^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq R\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}&\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w+1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{B,w}&\mathrm{Ev}_{B,w+1}\\
F&O\end{bmatrix}.

Further, by Theorem
3.1
, we get
|B|=\rank(\Ev_{B,w+1})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w+1}
, and

\rank(E'')=\rank\big(\begin{bmatrix}\Ev_{B,w}&\Ev_{B,w+1}\end{bmatrix}\big)+\rank(F)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w+1}+\rank(F).

Now once again, we note that for any
P(\mathbb{X})\in\mathbb{R}[\mathbb{X}]
, we have
P(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in\text{span}\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)\leq d\}
if and only if
P(k_{1}-1-X_{1},\ldots,k_{n}-1-X_{n})\in\text{span}\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\mathrm{wt}(\alpha)\leq d\}
. This means for any
i\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor]
, the vector spaces
V(i)
and
V(N-i)
are isomorphic, and further,
\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{i})=\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{N-i})
. So by Lemma
6.2
, we have
\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{w+1})\subseteq\text{SM}_{\preceq}(\underline{w})
. This gives

\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in B\}\subseteq\text{span}\big(\text{SM}_{\preceq}(w+1)\big)\subseteq\text{span}\big(\text{SM}_{\preceq}(w)\big),

and so
\{\mathbb{Y}^{(\alpha)}:\alpha\in B\}
is linearly independent in
V(w)
, that is,
\rank(\Ev_{B,w})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w+1}
.

Finally, we have
\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Ev}_{B,w}\\
F\end{bmatrix}=R\cdot\mathrm{Ev}_{d,w}
, and so, by Theorem
3.1
,

\rank(F)=\rank(\Ev_{d,w})-\rank(\Ev_{B,w})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w}-\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{G}{w+1}.

This implies

•

For any
i\in[n]
and
a\in G
, we denote ‘
i\in a
’ if
a(i)\geq 1
.

•

For any
a,b\in G
, we denote ‘
a\leq b
’ if
a(i)\leq b(i)
, for all
i\in[n]
.

•

For any
a,b\in G
, we define
a\cap b\in G
by
(a\cap b)(i)=\min\{a(i),b(i)\}
, for all
i\in[n]
.

•

We denote the zero function on
[n]
(that is, the empty multiset) by
\emptyset
.

Let
\preceq
be the lexicographic order on
G
induced by the order
1>\cdots>n
on the coordinates. For any
b\in G
, let
\tau(b)=\max\{i\in[n]:b(i)\geq 1\}
, and define
b^{*}\in G
by

b^{*}(i)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }i\leq\tau(b),\\
k_{i}-1,&\text{if }i>\tau(b).\end{cases}

Further, for any
b\in G,\,b\neq\emptyset
, let
b^{-}
be the predecessor of
b
with respect to
\preceq
.

We recursively define that
A\subseteq G

order shatters

b\in G
(with respect to
\preceq
) if the following hold:

(a)

if
b=\emptyset
, then
|A|\geq 1
,

(b)

if
b\neq\emptyset
, then there exist
A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A
such that

(i)

a^{\prime}(\tau(b))=b(\tau(b))
, for all
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
.

(ii)

a^{\prime\prime}(\tau(b))<b(\tau(b))
, for all
a^{\prime\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}
.

(iii)

a^{\prime}\cap b^{*}=a^{\prime\prime}\cap b^{*}
, for all
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime},\,a^{\prime\prime}\in A^{\prime\prime}
.

(iv)

|A^{\prime}|+|A^{\prime\prime}|\geq|\Delta b|
.

(v)

A^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime\prime}
both order shatter
b^{-}
.

Further, we denote
\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(A)=\{b\in G:A\text{ order shatters }b\}
.

Let us now collect the two results that we require. The first result is an extension of a result by
[
ARS02
]
, from the Boolean cube setting to uniform grids. The proof is a straightforward extension to uniform grids; therefore, we omit it.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. Then for any
A\subseteq G
,
\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(A)=\text{SM}_{\preceq}(A)
.

The second result is an extension of a result in
[
FR03
]
, from the Boolean cube setting to uniform grids. The proof is a straightforward extension to uniform grids; therefore, we omit it.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
G
be a uniform grid. For any
i,j\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor]
, if
i\leq j
, then

\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{i})=\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{j})\cap\underline{[0,i]}.

Lemma
6.2
then follows immediately from Proposition
6.3
and Proposition
6.4
.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Let
i,j\in[0,\lfloor N/2\rfloor],\,i\leq j
. By Proposition
6.4
, we have
\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{i})=\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{j})\cap\underline{[0,i]}
, which implies
\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{i})\subseteq\text{ord-str}_{\preceq}(\underline{j})
. The proof is then complete by Proposition
6.3
.
∎

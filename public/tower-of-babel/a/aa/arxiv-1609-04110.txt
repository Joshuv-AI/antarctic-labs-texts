Abstract
Absorbing ball in H 1 ​ ( ℧ ) H^{1}(\mho) is obtained for the strong solution to the three dimensional viscous moist primitive equations under the natural assumption Q 1 , Q 2 ∈ L 2 ​ ( ℧ ) Q_{1},Q_{2}\in L^{2}(\mho) which is weaker than the assumption Q 1 , Q 2 ∈ H 1 ​ ( ℧ ) Q_{1},Q_{2}\in H^{1}(\mho) in [ 20 ] . In view of the structure of the manifold and the special geometry involved with vertical velocity, the continuity of the strong solution in H 1 ​ ( ℧ ) H^{1}(\mho) is established with respect to time and initial data. To obtain the existence of the global attractor for the moist primitive equations, the common method is to obtain the absorbing ball in H 2 ​ ( ℧ ) H^{2}(\mho) for the strong solution to the equations. But it is difficult due to the complex structure of the moist primitive equations. To overcome the difficulty, we try to use Aubin-Lions lemma and the continuous property of the strong solutions to the moist primitive equations to prove the the existence of the global attractor which improves the result, the existence of weak attractor, in [ 20 ] .

In order to understand the mechanism of long-term weather prediction, one can take advantage of the historical records and numerical computations to detect the future weather. Alternatively, one should also study the long time behavior mathematically for the equations and models governing the motion. The primitive equations represent the classic model for the study of climate and weather prediction, describing the motion of the atmosphere when the hydrostatic assumption is enforced
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ G, Ha, HW, MT, Ri}{}{}]}.

where the definitions of weak and strong solutions were given and the existence of weak solution was proven, leaving the uniqueness of weak solution as an open problem for now. Local well-posedness of strong solutions was obtained in
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{GMR, TZ}{}{}]}.
If the domains was thin, the global well-posedness of 3D primitive equations was shown in
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{HTZ}{}{}]}.
Taking advantage of the fact that the pressure is essentially two-dimensional in the primitive equations, global well-posedness of the full three-dimensional case was established in

To study the long term behavior of primitive equations, the existence of global attractor was established in

In

The understanding of asymptotic behavior of dynamical system is one of the most important topics of modern mathematical physics. One way to solve the problem for dissipative deterministic dynamical system is to consider its global attractor (see its definition in section 2). Thus, in order to capture the dynamical features of moist primitive equations, Guo and Huang in
[

proved the existence of universal attractor which is weakly closed in

Then,
one natural question arising from it is the existence of global attractor

V
-strong topology, as time goes to
\infty.

As it is stated in
[
20
]
that laking information for the time derivative of the vertical velocity in the moist primitive equations leads to the failure of establishing the existence of the global attractor. How to overcome the difficulty? Inspired by

To overcome the difficulty, the structure of the manifold and the special geometry involved with the vertical velocity are used to obtain the continuity of the strong solution with respect to initial data in

To see the difference between global attractor and universal attractor, we introduce the two definitions in the following. For more details, we refer to
[

,

,

]

Let
(X,d)
be a separable metric space and

be a semigroup satisfying:

(i)\ S(t)S(s)x=S(t+s)x,
for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\in X;

(ii)\ S(0)=I
(Identity in
X
);

(iii)\ S(t)
is continuous in
X
for all
t\geq 0.

Typically,
S(t)
is associated with a autonomous differential equation;
S(t)x
is the state at time
t
of the solution whose initial data is
x.

Definition 2.2

A subset
\mathcal{A}
in
X
is said to be a global attractor if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{A}
is compact in
X;

(ii)
for every
t\geq 0,S(t)\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A};

(iii)
for every bounded set
B
in
X,
the set
S(t)B
converges to
\mathcal{A}
in
X
, when
t\rightarrow\infty,i.e.,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}d(S(t)B,\mathcal{A})=0.

Here, and in the following, for
A
and
B
subsets of
X,d(A,B)
is the semi-distance given by

d(A,B)=\sup\limits_{x\in A}\inf\limits_{y\in B}d(x,y).

Definition 2.3

A subset
\mathcal{A}
in
X
is said to be a universal attractor or weak attractor if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{A}
is bounded and weakly closed in
X;

(ii)
for every
t\geq 0,S(t)\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A};

(iii)
for every bounded set
B
in
X,
the set
S(t)B
converges to
\mathcal{A}
with respect to
X
-weak topology, when
t\rightarrow\infty,i.e.,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}d_{X}^{w}(S(t)B,\mathcal{A})=0

where the distance

is induced by the

-weak topology.

\displaystyle+c|q|_{\xi=1}|_{4}^{4}+c|q|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}++c|Q_{2}|_{\xi=1}||_{2}^{2}+c|Q_{2}|_{2}^{2}.

Combining

and
(3.67)

\displaystyle\frac{d(|T_{\xi}|_{2}^{2}+|q_{\xi}|_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{s}|T|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}+\beta_{s}|q|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2})}{dt}+|\nabla T_{\xi}|_{2}^{2}+|T_{\xi\xi}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+|\nabla q_{\xi}|_{2}^{2}+|q_{\xi\xi}|_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{s}|\nabla T|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}+\beta_{s}|\nabla q|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c+c(|T_{\xi}|_{2}^{2}+|q_{\xi}|_{2}^{2})+c\|v_{\xi}\|_{1}^{2}+c\|v\|_{1}^{2}+c\|q\|_{1}^{2}+c|T|_{\xi=1}|_{4}^{4}+c|T|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+c|q|_{\xi=1}|_{4}^{4}+c|q|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}+c(|Q_{1}|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2}+|Q_{2}|_{\xi=1}|_{2}^{2})+c(|Q_{1}|_{2}^{2}+|Q_{2}|_{2}^{2}).

(3.68)

Then in view of uniform Gronwall lemma,
(3.27),(3.33),(3.38),(3.45)
and

we obtain for arbitrary

\displaystyle|T_{\xi}(t_{0})|_{2}^{2}+|q_{\xi}(t_{0})|_{2}^{2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+1}(\|T_{\xi}(t)\|_{1}^{2}+\|q_{\xi}(t)\|_{1}^{2}+|\nabla T|_{\xi=1}(t)|_{2}^{2}+|\nabla q|_{\xi}(t)|_{2}^{2})dt\leq c,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l_{1}+l_{2}+l_{3}+l_{4}.

\displaystyle|\Delta T|_{2}|\nabla T|_{4}|v|_{4}

\mathrm{\ddot{o}}

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{div}v|d\xi(\int_{0}^{1}T_{\xi}^{2}d\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\int_{0}^{1}|\Delta T|^{2}d\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)dS^{2}

Lemma 4.1

Let

into

From

and Lemma

we obtain for
\eta\in V_{1}=D(A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}),

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}v,\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}v,A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle A_{1}v,A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle-\langle\nabla_{v}v,A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\Big(\int_{\xi}^{1}\mathrm{div}v(x,y,\xi,t)d\xi\Big)v_{\xi},A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\int_{\xi}^{1}\frac{bP}{p}\mathrm{grad}[(1+aq)T]d\xi^{\prime},A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\frac{f}{R_{0}}v^{\bot},A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle.

Using H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}
lder inequality, Agmon inequality and interpolation inequality yields

\displaystyle|\langle A_{1}v,A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A_{1}v|_{2}\|\eta\|_{1},

\displaystyle|\langle(v\cdot\nabla)v,A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c|v|_{\infty}\|v\|_{1}|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\leq c\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}.

By H
\mathrm{\ddot{o}}

\displaystyle-\langle\int_{\xi}^{1}\frac{bP}{p}\mathrm{grad}[(1+aq)T]d\xi^{\prime},A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle

\displaystyle c\int_{S^{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}(|\mathrm{grad}T|+|\mathrm{grad}(Tq)|)d\xi\int_{0}^{1}A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta d\xi\Big)dS^{2}

\displaystyle c\|T\|_{1}|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}T|_{4}|q|_{4}d\xi

\displaystyle+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}q|_{4}|T|_{4}d\xi

\displaystyle c\|T\|_{1}|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}T|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot(|\Delta T|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\mathrm{grad}T|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}})|q|_{4}d\xi

\displaystyle+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}q|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot(|\Delta q|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\mathrm{grad}q|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}})|T|_{4}d\xi

\displaystyle c\|T\|_{1}|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\|T\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|T\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|q|_{4}

\displaystyle+c|A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta|_{2}\|q\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|T|_{4}.

\displaystyle|\langle\Big(\int_{\xi}^{1}\mathrm{div}v(x,y,\xi^{\prime},t)d\xi^{\prime}\Big)v_{\xi},A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta\rangle|\leq c\|v\|_{1}\|v\|_{2}\|\eta\|_{1}.

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}v)\|_{V_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\|v\|_{2}+c\|v\|_{1}\|v\|_{2}+c\|T\|_{1}

\displaystyle+c(\|T\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|T\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|q|_{4}+\|q\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|T|_{4}).

4.1

For any

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c(\|T\|_{2}+\|v\|_{1}+\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|T\|_{1}+|q|_{4}\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|T\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|T\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|Q_{1}|_{2}).

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}A_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}q\|_{V_{2}^{\prime}}\leq c(\|q\|_{2}+\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{1}+\|q\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|Q_{2}|_{2}).

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T\in L^{2}([0,\tau];V_{2}),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{t}A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T\in L^{2}([0,\tau];V_{2}^{\prime})

we infer that

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c|A_{1}u|_{2}\Big(\int_{S^{2}}(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}q_{1}|^{2}d\xi)^{2}dS^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{S^{2}}(\int_{0}^{1}|T|^{2}d\xi)^{2}dS^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+c|A_{1}u|_{2}\Big(\int_{S^{2}}(\int_{0}^{1}|q_{1}|^{2}d\xi)^{2}dS^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{S^{2}}(\int_{0}^{1}|\mathrm{grad}T|^{2}d\xi)^{2}dS^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+c|\mathrm{grad}q_{1}|_{2}(|\mathrm{grad}q_{1}|_{2}+|\Delta q_{1}|_{2})(|T|_{2}^{2}+|T|_{2}|\nabla T|_{2})

\displaystyle+c|\mathrm{grad}T|_{2}(|\mathrm{grad}T|_{2}+|\Delta T|_{2})|q_{1}|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon|\Delta T|_{2}^{2}+c\|T\|_{1}^{2}(\|q_{1}\|_{1}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{2}^{2}).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle k_{8}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+c|\mathrm{grad}q|_{2}(|\mathrm{grad}q|_{2}+|\Delta q|_{2})|T_{2}|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+c|\mathrm{grad}T_{2}|_{2}(|\mathrm{grad}T_{2}|_{2}+|\Delta T_{2}|_{2})(|q|_{2}^{2}+|q|_{2}|\nabla q|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon|\Delta q|_{2}^{2}+c\|q\|_{1}^{2}(\|T_{2}\|_{1}^{4}+\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}).

By
(5.81)
and estimates of
k_{1}-k_{8},
we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\|u\|_{1}^{2}}{dt}+|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon|A_{2}T|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon|A_{3}q|_{2}^{2}

(5.82)

\displaystyle+c\|u\|_{1}^{2}(\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{2}\|_{1}^{4}+1)

\displaystyle+c\|T\|_{1}^{2}(\|q_{1}\|_{1}^{4}+\|q_{1}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+c\|q\|_{1}^{2}(\|T_{2}\|_{1}^{4}+\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}).

Taking an analogous argument as above, from
(5.76)
and
(5.77)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\|T\|_{1}^{2}}{dt}+|A_{2}T|_{2}^{2}

(5.83)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{2}T|_{2}^{2}+\varepsilon|A_{1}u|_{2}^{2}+c\|q\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{2}\|_{1}\|v_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+c\|T\|_{1}^{2}(1+\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+c\|u\|_{1}^{2}(1+\|q_{1}\|_{1}^{4}+\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|T_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2})

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\|q\|_{1}^{2}}{dt}+|A_{3}q|_{2}^{2}

(5.84)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon|A_{3}q|_{2}^{2}+c\|q\|_{1}^{2}(1+\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+c\|u\|_{1}^{2}(\|q_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|q_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|q_{2}\|_{2}^{2}).

Let

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 1+\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|v_{2}\|_{1}^{4}+\|v_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|T_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|q_{1}\|_{1}^{4}+\|q_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|q_{2}\|_{1}^{2}\|q_{2}\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle g_{2}:=1+\|q_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+\|q_{1}\|_{1}^{4}+\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2}

and

\displaystyle g_{3}:=1+\|T_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+\|T_{2}\|_{1}^{4}+\|v_{2}\|_{1}\|v_{2}\|_{2}+\|v_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Obviously, for arbitrary
0\leq a<b<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}(g_{1}(t)+g_{2}(t)+g_{3}(t))dt<\infty.

Thereby, in view of
(5.82)-(5.84)
we get

\displaystyle\frac{d(\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\|T\|_{1}^{2}+\|q\|_{1}^{2})}{dt}\leq c(g_{1}(t)+g_{2}(t)+g_{3}(t))(\|u\|_{1}^{2}+\|T\|_{1}^{2}+\|q\|_{1}^{2}),

which combined with Gronwall lemma implies

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{1}^{2}+\|T(t)\|_{1}^{2}+\|q(t)\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(\|v_{0,1}-v_{0,2}\|_{1}^{2}+\|T_{0,1}-T_{0,2}\|_{1}^{2}+\|q_{0,1}-q_{0,2}\|_{1}^{2})e^{\int_{0}^{t}(g_{1}(s)+g_{2}(s)+g_{3}(s))ds}.

So far, we have shown that for
t>0,\ (v(t),T(t),q(t))
is Lipschitz continuous in
V
with respect to the initial data
(v(0),T(0),q(0)).

6
The global attractor.

In this section, we present our main result, the existence of global attractor, of this paper. To show our main result, we make use of the following theorem from Teman
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{T}{}{}]}.
For more details about the theorem, we can see, e.g.,
[
4
,
14
,
22
,

,

,

,
46
]
and the references therein.

Theorem 6.1

Suppose that
X
is a metric space and semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a family of operators from
X
into itself such that

(i)
for any fixed
t>0,S(t)
is continuous from
X
into itself;

(ii)
for some
t_{0},S(t_{0})
is compact from
X
into itself;

(iii)
there exists a subset
B_{0}
of
X
which is bounded, a subset
U
of
X
is open, such that

B_{0}\subseteq U\subseteq X,
and
B_{0}
is the absorbing set of
U,
i.e. for any bounded subset
B\subset U,
there

is a
t_{0}=t_{0}(B),
such that

\displaystyle S(t)B\subset B_{0},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ t>t_{0}(B).

Then
\mathcal{A}:=\omega(B_{0}),
the
\omega-
limit set of
B_{0}
, is a compact attractor which attracts all the bounded sets of
U,
i.e. for any
x\in U,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}dist(S(t)x,\mathcal{A})=0.

The set
\mathcal{A}
is the maximal bounded attractor in
U
for the inclusion relation.

Suppose in addition that
X
is a Banach space,
U
is a convex and

(iv)\ \forall x\in X,S(t)x:\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto X
is continuous.

Then
\mathcal{A}:=\omega(B_{0})
is also connected.

If
U=X,\mathcal{A}
is the global attractor of the semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
in
X.

Next, we give our main result of the paper and complete the proof of the theorem.

Theorem 6.2

Assume
Q_{1},Q_{2}\in L^{2}(\mho)
and
Q_{1}|_{\xi=1},Q_{2}|_{\xi=1}\in L^{2}(S^{2}).
Then, for
t\geq 0,
the solution operator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
of the
3D
viscous PEs of large-scale moist atmosphere
(1.11)-(1.17):S(t)(v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})=(v(t),T(t),q(t))
defines a semigroup in the space
V.
Furthermore, the results below hold:

(1)
For any
(v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\in V,t\rightarrow S(t)(v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})
is a continuous map from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
into
V.

(2)
For any
t>0,S(t)
is a continuous map in
V.

(3)
For any
t>0,S(t)
is a compact map in
V.

(4)

\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
possesses a global attractor
\mathcal{A}
in
V.
The global attractor in
\mathcal{A}
is compact and

connected in
V
and is the maximal bounded attractor in
V
in the sense of set inclusion

relation;
\mathcal{A}
attracts all bounded subset in
V
in the norm of
V.

To prove Theorem 6.2, we need to check the conditions
(i)-(iv)
in Theorem 6.1. First, condition
(i)
, the continuous dependence on initial data of the solution, is verified in section
5.
Second, condition
(iii)
, the existence of an absorbing ball in
V,
is proved in section
3.
Third, condition
(iv)
, the regularity of the solution, is shown in section
4.
Finally, only the condition
(ii),
compactness of the solution operator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0},
is left to be checked. We will use Aubin-Lions lemma stated below and continuity argument to verify condition
(ii).
For more details of the lemma, we can see

Lemma 6.1

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}_{1}
be Banach spaces such that
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{1}
are reflexive and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\overset{c}{\subset}\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}_{1}.
Define, for
0<\tau<\infty,

\displaystyle X:=\Big\{u\Big|u\in L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathcal{H}_{0}),\ \ \frac{du}{dt}\in L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathcal{H}_{1})\Big\}.

Then
X
is a Banach space equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathcal{H}_{0})}+\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathcal{H}_{1})}.
Moreover,
X\overset{c}{\subset}L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathcal{H}).

Proof of Theorem
6.1.
By the argument above, we only have to check condition
(ii),
the compactness of the solution operator. For any fixed
\tau>0,
let
\mathcal{B}
be a bounded subset of
V
and
A_{\tau}
denote the subset of the space
L^{2}([0,T];H):

\displaystyle A_{\tau}:=\Big\{\Big(A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}v,A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T,A_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}q\Big)\Big|(v_{0},T_{0},q_{0})\in\mathcal{B},(v(t),T(t),q(t))=S(t)(v_{0},T_{0},q(0)),t\in[0,\tau]\Big\}.

For
(v_{0},T_{0},q(0))\in\mathcal{B}
, it has been shown previously that the strong solution
(v,T,q)
satisfies

\displaystyle(A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}v,A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T,A_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}q)\in L^{2}([0,\tau];V),\ \ (\partial_{t}A_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}v,\partial_{t}A_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T,\partial_{t}A_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}q)\in L^{2}([0,\tau];V^{\prime}).

If we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}=V,\mathcal{H}=H,\mathcal{H}_{1}=V^{\prime},

from Lemma
6.1
we infer that
A_{\tau}
is compact in
L^{2}([0,\tau];H).

To prove solution operator
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
is compact in
V,
for any bounded sequence
\{(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{n}\subset\mathcal{B},
we should show there exists a convergent subsequence of
\{S(t)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{\infty}
in
V.

Since
A_{\tau}
is compact in
L^{2}([0,\tau];H),
there exists a function
(v_{*},T_{*},q_{*})\in L^{2}([0,\tau];V)
such that there is a subsequence of
\{S(\cdot)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{\infty},
still denoted as
\{S(\cdot)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{\infty},
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\|S(t)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})-(v_{*}(t),T_{*}(t),q_{*}(t))\|_{1}^{2}dt=0,

which implies that there is a subsequence of
\{S(\cdot)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{\infty},
still denoted as
\{S(\cdot)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})\}_{0}^{\infty}
for simplicity of notation, converging to
(v_{*},T_{*},q_{*})
in
V
a.e. in
(0,\tau):

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S(t)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})-(v_{*}(t),T_{*}(t),q_{*}(t))\|_{1}=0,\ \ a.e.\ t\ in\ (0,\tau).

For any
t\in(0,\tau),
we can choose a
t_{0}\in(0,t)
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S(t_{0})(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})-(v_{*}(t_{0}),T_{*}(t_{0}),q_{*}(t_{0}))\|_{1}=0.

Therefore, for any
t>0,
by the continuity of
S(t)
in
V
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S(t)(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})-S(t-t_{0})(v_{*}(t_{0}),T_{*}(t_{0}),q_{*}(t_{0}))\|_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|S(t-t_{0})S(t_{0})(v_{0,n},T_{0,n},q_{0,n})-S(t-t_{0})(v_{*}(t_{0}),T_{*}(t_{0}),q_{*}(t_{0}))\|_{1}=0,

which implies
S(t)
is a compact map in
V
for any
t>0.
Then by the above argument of this section and Theorem 6.1, we obtain the existence of the global attractor for the moist primitive equations
(1.11)-(1.17).

Simplicial-like Identities for The Paths and The Regular Paths on Discrete Sets


Abstract
Simplicial identities play an important and fundamental role in simplicial homotopy theory. On the other hand, the study of the paths and the regular paths on discrete sets is the foundation for the path-homology theory of digraphs. In this paper, by investigating some weighted face maps and weighted co-face maps on the space of the paths as well as the space of the regular paths, we prove some simplicial-like identities for the paths and the regular paths on discrete sets.

V
be a finite set whose elements are called

. Let
n\geq 0
be a non-negative integer. An

-path
on

v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
where
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
are vertices in
V
(cf.
[
8
, Definition 2.1]
). Here for any
0\leq i<j\leq n
, the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are not assumed to be distinct. Let
\mathbb{R}
be the real numbers. A linear combination

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V}r_{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}}v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n},~~~r_{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}}\in\mathbb{R},

of elementary

-paths on
V
is called an

-path
on
V
(cf.
[
8

[

is of dimension

\Lambda_{0}(V)
is the vector space spanned by all the vertices in

We consider the graded vector space

\displaystyle\Lambda_{*}(V)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}(V)

with the canonical addition

and the canonical (real) scalar multiplication.
Let
n
and
m
be two non-negative integers. We take an
n
-path

\displaystyle\xi=\sum_{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V}r_{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}}v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}

(2.1)

in
\Lambda_{n}(V)
and take an

\displaystyle\eta=\sum_{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in V}t_{u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{n}}u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{m}

\Lambda_{m}(V)

\Lambda_{n+m+1}(V)

\displaystyle\xi*\eta=\sum_{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V;\atop u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in V}r_{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}}t_{u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{m}}v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{m}.

(2.3)

We extend

\Lambda_{*}(V)
.
It is direct to verify the following laws:

(i).

(\lambda\xi)*(\mu\eta)=(\lambda\mu)(\xi*\eta)

.

We call the graded vector space

2.1
The Partial Derivatives and the Partial Differentiations for Paths

Let
v\in V
. In this subsection, we define the partial derivative as well as the partial differentiation on
\Lambda_{*}(V)
, with respect to
v
.

Definition 2
.

For each
n\geq 0
, we define the
partial derivative with respect to
v
to be a linear map

\displaystyle\vec{\frac{\partial}{\partial v}}:~~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\prod_{n+1}\Lambda_{n-1}(V)

by

\displaystyle\vec{\frac{\partial}{\partial v}}=\Big(\frac{\partial_{0}}{\partial v},\frac{\partial_{1}}{\partial v},\ldots,\frac{\partial_{n}}{\partial v}\Big).

(2.4)

Here in (
2.4
), for each
0\leq i\leq n
, the
i
-th coordinate of (
2.4
) is a linear map

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}:~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n-1}(V)

given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=(-1)^{i}\delta(v,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

(2.5)

for any elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
.

Remark 1
:

We give a remark on the notation in (
2.5
). For any

, the notation
\delta(v,u)
is defined to be
1

v=u
and is defined to be
0
if
v\neq u
.

Definition 3
.

For each
n\geq 0
, we define the
partial differentiation with respect to
v
to be a linear map

\displaystyle\vec{d}v:~~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\prod_{n+2}\Lambda_{n+1}(V)

by

\displaystyle\vec{d}v=(d_{0}v,d_{1}v,\ldots,d_{n+1}v).

(2.6)

Here in (
2.6
), for each
0\leq i\leq n+1
, the
i
-th coordinate of (
2.6
) is a linear map

\displaystyle d_{i}v:~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n+1}(V)

given by

\displaystyle d_{i}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=(-1)^{i}v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

(2.7)

for each elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
.

Remark 2
:

We give a remark for Definition
3
.
Letting
i=0

2.7
), we have

\displaystyle d_{0}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=(-1)^{0}vv_{0}\ldots v_{n};

(2.8)

letting
i=n+1
in (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle d_{0}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=(-1)^{n+1}v_{0}\ldots v_{n}v.

(2.9)

Hence for each elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
, by (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9

\vec{d}v
is a vector

\displaystyle\vec{d}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((-1)^{0}vv_{0}\ldots v_{n},(-1)^{1}v_{0}vv_{1}\ldots v_{n},\ldots,

\displaystyle(-1)^{i}v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vv_{i}\ldots v_{n},\ldots,(-1)^{n+1}v_{0}\ldots v_{n}v\Big).

By Definition
2
and Definition

, we have the next three lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v,u\in V
. Let
n\geq 0
. Then for any possible
i
and
j
with
i<j
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}=-\frac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}.

Proof.

Let
v,u\in V
and
n\geq 0
. For any
0\leq j\leq n
and any
0\leq i\leq n-1
, it follows from a direct calculation that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\Big((-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j})\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}(v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{i+j}\delta(v,v_{j})\delta(u,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n},&0\leq i\leq j-1;\\
(-1)^{i+j}\delta(v,v_{j})\delta(u,v_{i+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n},&j\leq i\leq n-1.\end{cases}

Exchanging
u
and
v
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{i+j}\delta(u,v_{j})\delta(v,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n},&0\leq i\leq j-1;\\
(-1)^{i+j}\delta(u,v_{j})\delta(v,v_{i+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n},&j\leq i\leq n-1.\end{cases}

Now we suppose
i<j
. It follows from the above two equations that for any elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=-\frac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}).

By the linear property of
\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}
and
\frac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}
, the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
v,u\in V
. Let
n\geq 0
. Then for any possible
i
and
j
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v=\begin{cases}-d_{j-1}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u},&i<j;\\
\delta(u,v){\rm~id},&i=j;\\
-d_{j}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u},&i>j.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
v,u\in V
and
n\geq 0
. Let
0\leq i,j\leq n+1
. Take an elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
. We consider three cases:

Case 1
.
i<j
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+j}\delta(u,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{i+(j-1)}\delta(u,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{j-1}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}).

Thus by the linear property of
\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v
and
d_{j-1}v\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v=-d_{j-1}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}.

Case 2
.
i=j
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{i}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+i}\delta(u,v)v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}\widehat{v}v_{i}\ldots\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(u,v)v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}.

Thus by the linear property of
\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v=\delta(u,v){\rm~id}.

Case 3
.
i>j
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+j}\delta(u,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(i-1)+j}\delta(u,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-d_{j}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}).

Thus by the linear property of
\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v
and
d_{j}v\circ\frac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v=-d_{j}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u}.

Summarizing all the three cases, we obtain the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
v,u\in V
. Let
n\geq 0
. Then for any possible
i
and
j
with
i\leq j
, we have

\displaystyle d_{i}u\circ d_{j}v=-d_{j+1}v\circ d_{i}u.

Proof.

Let
v,u\in V
and
n\geq 0
. Take an elementary
n
-path
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
on
V
. Then for any possible
i
and

we have

\displaystyle d_{i}u\circ d_{j}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}d_{i}u(v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-1)^{i+j}v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uv_{i}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n},&i<j;\\
v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n},&i=j;\\
-v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n},&i=j+1;\\
(-1)^{i+j}v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{i-2}uv_{i-1}\ldots v_{n},&i>j+1.\end{cases}

Exchanging

\displaystyle d_{i}v\circ d_{j}u(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i+j}v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vv_{i}\ldots v_{j-1}uv_{j}\ldots v_{n},&i<j;\\
v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n},&i=j;\\
-v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n},&i=j+1;\\
(-1)^{i+j}v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}uv_{j}\ldots v_{i-2}vv_{i-1}\ldots v_{n},&i>j+1.\end{cases}

Now we suppose

\displaystyle d_{i}u\circ d_{j}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=-d_{j+1}v\circ d_{i}u(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}).

By the linear property of
d_{i}u\circ d_{j}v

2.2
The Weighted Face Maps and the Weighted Co-Face Maps for Paths

Let
f:V\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an real valued function on
V
which assigns a real number
f(v)
to each vertex
v\in V
. Let
n\geq 0
. In this subsection, we define the
f
-weighted face maps as well as the
f
-weighted co-face maps on
\Lambda_{*}(V)
. Then we prove some simplicial-like identities.

Definition 4
.

For each
0\leq i\leq n
, we define the
i
-th
f
-weighted face map
to be a linear map

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}:~~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n-1}(V).

For simplicity, we use the notation

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{f}:=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}

(2.10)

for the
i
-th
f
-weighted face map.

Definition 5
.

For each
0\leq i\leq n+1
, we define the
i
-th
f
-weighted co-face map
to be a linear map

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v:~~~\Lambda_{n}(V)\longrightarrow\Lambda_{n+1}(V).

For simplicity, we use the notation

\displaystyle d_{i}^{f}:=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v

(2.11)

for the
i
-th
f
-weighted co-face map.

Definition 6
.

For any real functions
f
and
g
on
V
, their inner product with respect to
V
is defined by

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle^{V}:=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)g(v).

Definition 7
.

Taking
f=g
in Definition
6
, for any real function
f
on
V
, the
L^{2}
-norm of
f
is defined by

\displaystyle||f||_{2}=||f||_{2}^{V}:=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}=\sqrt{\sum_{v\in V}|f(v)|^{2}}.

The next proposition gives the explicit formulas for
\partial_{i}^{f}
and
d_{i}^{f}
on the elementary
n
-paths.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f
be a real function on
V
. Let
n\geq 0
and let
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
be an elementary
n
-path on
V
. Then for any
0\leq i\leq n
we have

\displaystyle\partial^{f}_{i}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=(-1)^{i}f(v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v

and for any
0\leq j\leq n+1
we have

\displaystyle d^{f}_{j}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})=\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{j}f(v)v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n}.

Proof.

Let
0\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq j\leq n+1
. By a straight-forward calculation,

\displaystyle\partial^{f}_{i}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{i}\delta(v,v_{i})f(v)v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i}f(v_{i})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v

and

\displaystyle d^{f}_{j}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{j}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{j}f(v)v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n}.

The proposition follows.
∎

The next theorem follows with the help of Subsection
2.1
.

Theorem 2.5

(Main Result I: The simplicial-like identities for paths on discrete sets)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two real functions on
V
. Then for any
n\geq 0
, we have

(i).

\partial_{i}^{f}\circ\partial_{j}^{g}=-\partial_{j-1}^{g}\circ\partial_{i}^{f}
for any
i<j
;

(ii).

\partial_{i}^{f}\circ d_{j}^{g}=\begin{cases}-d_{j-1}^{g}\circ\partial_{i}^{f},&i<j,\\
\langle f,g\rangle{\rm~id},&i=j,\\
-d_{j}^{g}\circ\partial_{i-1}^{f},&i>j;\end{cases}

(iii).

d_{i}^{f}\circ d_{j}^{g}=-d_{j+1}^{g}\circ d_{i}^{f}
for
i\leq j
.

Proof.

(i). Suppose
i<j
. By a straight-forward calculation and Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in V}f(u)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ\dfrac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{u\in V}f(u)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j}}{\partial v}\Big)=-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{j-1}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big).

By the notations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we obtain (i).

(ii). By a straight-forward calculation Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j}v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in V}f(u)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j}v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\circ d_{j}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{j-1}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u},&i<j,\\
\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)\delta(u,v){\rm~id},&i=j,\\
-\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{j}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u},&i>j.\end{cases}

By a similar calculation in (i), we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{j-1}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{j-1}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u},

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle{\rm~id}~=~\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)g(v)\Big){\rm~id}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)\delta(u,v){\rm~id},

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{j}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial v}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{j}v\circ\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial u}.

Therefore, it follows from the above four equations that

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j}v\Big)=\begin{cases}-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{j-1}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big),&i<j,\\
\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)g(v)\Big){\rm~id},&i=j,\\
-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{j}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\dfrac{\partial_{i-1}}{\partial v}\Big),&i>j.\end{cases}

With the help of the notations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we obtain (ii).

(iii). Suppose
i\leq j
. By a straight-forward calculation and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j}v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{i}v\circ d_{j}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{u,v\in V}f(u)g(v)d_{j+1}v\circ d_{i}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j+1}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v\Big).

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j}v\Big)=-\Big(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)d_{j+1}v\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(v)d_{i}v\Big).

By the notations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we obtain (iii).
∎

Lemma 2.7

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

Corollary 2.8

For any

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}=-\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v},~~~~~~~~~~~~\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}d_{i}v\circ d_{j}u=-\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}d_{j}u\circ d_{i}v.

Proof.

The first identity follows from Lemma
2.7
by

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}=\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}\Big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial v}=-\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)=-\sum_{i,j=0}^{n}\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}.

The corollary is proved.

Proposition 2.9

(The anti-commutative properties)

Proof.

\displaystyle\partial^{f}\circ\partial^{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{u\in V}g(u)\frac{\partial_{j}}{\partial u}\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{u\in V}g(u)\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial u}\Big)\circ\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\frac{\partial_{i}}{\partial v}\Big)

The proposition follows.

We prove some Newton-Leibniz-type rules in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.12

.

(2.20)

Proof.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in V;\atop u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in V}r_{v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}}t_{u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{m}}\partial^{f}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}u_{0}u_{1}\ldots u_{m})

\displaystyle+\Big(\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i+1})-\delta(v,v_{i})\delta(u,v_{i+1})\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{d}v,\tilde{d}u)(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\Big(\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(v,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,u)

(3.5)

\displaystyle~~~~~~v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\Big(\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(u,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,v_{i})\epsilon(v,u)

\displaystyle~~~~~~v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n}.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in V
,
n\geq 0
, and
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
be a regular elementary
n
-path on
V
. It follows from a straight-forward calculation that

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial v}\circ\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial u}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\delta(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{i-2}(-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+1})

(3.6)

\displaystyle\text{~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j+1})\epsilon(v_{j},v_{j+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots\widehat{v_{j+1}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{i-1}\delta(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v_{i-2},v_{i+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{i}\delta(v_{i},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial u}\circ\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial v}(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\delta(v,v_{j})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+1})\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}(-1)^{i}\delta(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})

(3.7)

\displaystyle\text{~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=j+1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}\delta(u,v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i},v_{i+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j-1}\delta(u,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v_{j-2},v_{j+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j-1}}\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j}\delta(u,v_{j+1})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\widehat{v_{j+1}}\ldots v_{n}\Big).

Here we abuse the notations by writing

\displaystyle\delta(v,v_{-1})=\delta(v,v_{n+1})=\delta(u,v_{-1})=\delta(u,v_{n+1})=0,

\displaystyle\epsilon(v_{-1},v_{2})=\epsilon(v_{n-2},v_{n+1})=1.

Summing up (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), it follows that

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial v},\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial u}\Big)(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Big(-\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-2},v_{i+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\Big(-\delta(v,v_{j})\delta(u,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+1})\epsilon(v_{j-2},v_{j+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j-1}}\widehat{v_{j}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\delta(v,v_{j})\delta(u,v_{j+1})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+1})\epsilon(v_{j-1},v_{j+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{j}}\widehat{v_{j+1}}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Big(\delta(v,v_{i})\delta(u,v_{i-1})-\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i-1})\Big)\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-2},v_{i+1})

\displaystyle\text{~~~~~~}v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\Big(\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i+1})-\delta(v,v_{i})\delta(u,v_{i+1})\Big)\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+2})

\displaystyle\text{~~~}v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}.

We obtain (
3.4
).

On the other hand, it also follows from a straight-forward calculation that

\displaystyle\tilde{d}v\circ\tilde{d}u(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(u,v_{i})\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(-1)^{j}\epsilon(v,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,v_{j})

(3.8)

\displaystyle\text{~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{i-1}uv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=i+2}^{n+2}(-1)^{j}\epsilon(v,v_{j-2})\epsilon(v,v_{j-1})v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uv_{i}\ldots v_{j-2}vv_{j-1}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{i}\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{i+1}\epsilon(v,u)\epsilon(v,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{d}u\circ\tilde{d}v(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{j}\epsilon(v,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,v_{j})\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{i}\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(u,v_{i})

(3.9)

\displaystyle\text{~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~}v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uv_{i}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=j+2}^{n+2}(-1)^{i}\epsilon(u,v_{i-2})\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vv_{j}\ldots v_{i-2}uv_{i-1}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j}\epsilon(u,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}uvv_{j}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j+1}\epsilon(v,u)\epsilon(u,v_{j})v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vuv_{j}\ldots v_{n}\Big).

Summing up (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), it follows that

\displaystyle(\tilde{d}v,\tilde{d}u)(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\Big(\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle-\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v,u)\epsilon(v,v_{i})v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}\Big(\epsilon(v,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,v_{j})\epsilon(u,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}uvv_{j}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle-\epsilon(v,v_{j-1})\epsilon(v,v_{j})\epsilon(v,u)\epsilon(u,v_{j})v_{0}\ldots v_{j-1}vuv_{j}\ldots v_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\Big(\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(v,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\Big(\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(u,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,v_{i})\epsilon(v,u)v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n}.

We obtain (
3.5
).

Let
f
and
g
be two real functions on
V
We note that

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}^{f}=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial v},~~~~~~\tilde{d}^{f}=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\tilde{d}v

and the same identities hold for
g
as well. Moreover, if we write the anti-commutators as

\displaystyle(\tilde{\partial}^{f},\tilde{\partial}^{g})=\tilde{\partial}^{f}\circ\tilde{\partial}^{g}+\tilde{\partial}^{g}\circ\tilde{\partial}^{f},~~~~~~(\tilde{d}^{f},\tilde{d}^{g})=\tilde{d}^{f}\circ\tilde{d}^{g}+\tilde{d}^{g}\circ\tilde{d}^{f},

then we have

\displaystyle(\tilde{\partial}^{f},\tilde{\partial}^{g})=\sum_{v,u\in V}f(v)g(u)\Big(\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial v},\frac{\tilde{\partial}}{\partial u}\Big)

(3.10)

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{d}^{f},\tilde{d}^{g})=\sum_{v,u\in V}f(v)g(u)(\tilde{d}v,\tilde{d}u).

(3.11)

The next proposition follows from Lemma
3.2
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be real functions on
V
. Let
n\geq 0
and let
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n}
be a regular elementary
n
-path on
V
. Then

\displaystyle(\tilde{\partial}^{f},\tilde{\partial}^{g})(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v,u\in V}f(v)g(u)\Big[\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Big(\delta(v,v_{i})\delta(u,v_{i-1})-\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i-1})\Big)

(3.12)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-2},v_{i+1})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i-1}}\widehat{v_{i}}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\Big(\delta(u,v_{i})\delta(v,v_{i+1})-\delta(v,v_{i})\delta(u,v_{i+1})\Big)

\displaystyle~~~~~~\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})\epsilon(v_{i-1},v_{i+2})v_{0}\ldots\widehat{v_{i}}\widehat{v_{i+1}}\ldots v_{n}\Big]

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{d}^{f},\tilde{d}^{g})(v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v,u\in V}f(v)g(u)\Big[\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\Big(\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(v,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i})\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,u)

(3.13)

\displaystyle~~~~~~v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}vuv_{i}\ldots v_{n}

\displaystyle+\Big(\epsilon(v,v_{i-1})-\epsilon(u,v_{i})\Big)\epsilon(u,v_{i-1})\epsilon(v,v_{i})\epsilon(v,u)

\displaystyle~~~~~~v_{0}\ldots v_{i-1}uvv_{i}\ldots v_{n}\Big].

Proof.

Hypergeometric accelerations with shifted indices


Abstract
Chu and Zhang, in 2014, introduced hypergeometric transforms derived through the application of an Abel-type summation lemma to Dougall’s H 5 5 {}_{5}H_{5} -series. These transforms were applied by Chu and Zhang to obtain accelerated rates of convergence, yielding rational series related to the work of Ramanujan and Guillera. We apply a variant of an acceleration method due to Wilf using what we refer to as shifted indices for Pochhammer symbols involved in our first-order, inhomogeneous recurrences derived via Zeilberger’s algorithm, to build upon Chu and Zhang’s accelerations, recovering many of their accelerated series and introducing many inequivalent series for universal constants, including series of Ramanujan type involving linear polynomials as summand factors, as in Ramanujan’s series for 1 π \frac{1}{\pi} .

3
Hypergeometric accelerations with shifted indices

Building on an acceleration method that was given by Amdeberhan and Zeilberger in 1997
[
1
]
and that relies on the WZ
method, Wilf considered something of a different approach, by applying
Zeilberger’s algorithm
, as opposed to the WZ method, to obtain accelerations
from
inhomogeneous
and first-order difference equations for infinite hypergeometric series, in contrast to series identities
obtained from the difference equation
F(n+1,k)-F(n,k)=G(n,k+1)-G(n,k)
required according to the WZ method. In particular, in the exceptional cases whereby it is possible to obtain a

first-order
recurrence of the form

p_{1}(n)F(n+r,k)+p_{2}(n)F(n,k)=G(n,k+1)-G(n,k)

(32)

through the application of Zeilberger’s algorithm
to a bivariate hypergeometric function
F(n,k)
,
for polynomials
p_{1}(n)
and
p_{2}(n)
,
the difference equation in (
32
) can
be applied, subject to a vanishing condition indicated below,
to obtain series accelerations via the telescoping of the right-hand side of
(
32
), upon summation with respect to
k

[
4
,
28
]
.
Writing
\mathcal{F}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F(n,k)
, we obtain

p_{1}(n)\mathcal{F}(n+r)+p_{2}(n)\mathcal{F}(n)=-G(n,0),

(33)

under the assumption that the series in (
33
)
are convergent, yielding

\mathcal{F}(n)=\frac{-G(n,0)}{p_{2}(n)}-\frac{p_{1}(n)}{p_{2}(n)}\mathcal{F}(n+r).

(34)

Wilf’s acceleration method
[
28
]
is given by the iterative application of the
recursion in (
34
),
for cases whereby the leading coefficients of the polynomials
p_{1}(n)

and
p_{2}(n)

provide an accelerated rate of convergence given by products of consecutive
expressions of the form
-\frac{p_{1}(n)}{p_{2}(n)}
, as clarified in Sections

3.1
and
3.2
below.

Wilf applied the above described acceleration method with the use of the input functions

F(n,k)=\frac{(k!)^{2}}{((n+k+1)!)^{2}}

and
F(n,k)=\frac{(k!)^{3}}{((k+2n+1)!)^{3}}
,
yielding families, according to the free parameter
n
,
of accelerated series for
\pi^{2}
and Apéry’s constant
\zeta(3)=\frac{1}{1^{3}}+\frac{1}{2^{3}}+\cdots
, respectively.
Our recent paper
[
4
]
may be seen as taking Wilf’s ideas a step forward
through the application of
first-order recurrences for
multiparameter
hypergeometric functions
F(n,k)

such as

F(n,k)=\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(n)_{k}^{2}}

(35)

and

F(n,k)=\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(n)_{k}(2n)_{k}}

(36)

for free parameters
a
and
b
. The key idea in this paper is to generalize accelerations of the form considered in

[
4
]
through the use of shifted indices for Pochhammer symbols.
More specifically, given
a hypergeometric function
F(n,k)
that yields series accelerations according to Wilf’s method

[
28
]
, by expressing
F(n,k)
in terms of Pochhammer symbols,
if we instead apply Wilf’s method with respect to the function obtained
by shifting a Pochhammer symbol such as
(\mathcal{G})_{k}
,
for a combination
\mathcal{G}
of parameters and
n
- and
k
-expressions,
by replacing it with
(\mathcal{G})_{k+p}
for a free parameter
p
,
this provides a way of improving upon
families of accelerations obtained via Wilf’s method in
[
4
,
28
]
.

A computational difficulty that arises via our “shifted” variant of Wilf’s method
has to do with searching for closed-form constants
arising from canellations or simplifications of combinations of
\Gamma
-expressions
arising from special values of the left-hand side of
(
34
) together with the accelerated summand.
However, exhaustive computer searches through
combinations of rational parameter values involved
in shifted variants of input functions as in (
35
)–(
36
)
allow us to demonstrate the extent to which
our shifted method builds upon
the work of Chu and Zhang
[
13
]

and related works on Chu-style series as in
[
10
,
11
]
.
We begin by shifting the indices for each of the Pochhammer factors involved in (
35
),
yielding a hypergeometric transform that involves seven free parameters and that
has led us to experimentally discover and prove
new results as in the formulas of Ramanujan type highlighted
among (
5
)–(
8
),
and to recover many of the results due to Chu and Zhang
[
13
]
.

Since our paper is largely motivated by Chu and Zhang’s accelerated
\pi

[

such a way so as to agree with the organization of

In particular, we restrict our attention, for the time being,

[

Similarly, the mathematical constants considered in this paper,

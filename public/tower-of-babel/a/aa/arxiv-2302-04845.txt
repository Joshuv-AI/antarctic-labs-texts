Minimum degree thresholds for Hamilton ( ℓ , k − ℓ ) (\ell,k-\ell) -cycles in k k -uniform hypergraphs

Abstract
Let n > k > ℓ n>k>\ell be positive integers. We say a k k -uniform hypergraph ℋ \mathcal{H} contains a Hamilton ( ℓ , k − ℓ ) (\ell,k-\ell) -cycle if there is a partition ( L 0 , R 0 , L 1 , R 1 , … , L t − 1 , R t − 1 ) (L_{0},R_{0},L_{1},R_{1},\ldots,L_{t-1},R_{t-1}) of V ⁡ ( ℋ ) V(\mathcal{H}) with | L i | = ℓ |L_{i}|=\ell , | R i | = k − ℓ |R_{i}|=k-\ell such that L i ∪ R i L_{i}\cup R_{i} and R i ∪ L i + 1 R_{i}\cup L_{i+1} (subscripts module t t ) are all edges of ℋ \mathcal{H} for i = 0 , 1 , … , t − 1 i=0,1,\ldots,t-1 . In the present paper, we determine the tight minimum ℓ \ell -degree condition that guarantees the existence of a Hamilton ( ℓ , k − ℓ ) (\ell,k-\ell) -cycle in every k k -uniform n n -vertex hypergraph for k ≥ 7 k\geq 7 , k / 2 ≤ ℓ ≤ k − 1 k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1 and sufficiently large n ∈ k ​ ℕ n\in k\mathbb{N} .



1
Introduction

1.1
Background

A central question in graph theory is to establish conditions that ensure a (hyper)graph
\mathcal{H}
contains some spanning (hyper)graph
\mathcal{F}
. Of course, it is desirable to fully characterize those (hyper)graphs
\mathcal{H}
that contain a spanning copy of a given (hyper)graph
\mathcal{F}
. A theorem of Tutte
[
30
]
gives a characterization of all those graphs which contain a perfect matching. However, for some (hyper)graphs
\mathcal{F}
it is unlikely that such a characterization exists. Indeed, for many (hyper)graphs
\mathcal{F}
the decision problem of whether a (hyper)graph
\mathcal{H}
contains
\mathcal{F}
is NP-complete. Garey and Johnson
[
9
]
showed that the decision problem whether a
k
-uniform hypergraph contains a perfect matching or Hamilton path is NP-complete for
k\geq 3
. It is natural therefore to seek simple sufficient conditions that ensure a Hamilton cycle in a
k
-uniform hypergraph. The study of Hamilton cycles is an important topic in graph theory with a long history. In 1952, Dirac
[
5
]
proved that if
\delta(G)\geq n/2
and
n\geq 3
then
G
contains a Hamilton cycle, which is one of the most classical results in graph theory. In recent years, researchers have worked on extending Dirac’s theorem to hypergraphs and we refer to

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
23
,
24
]

for some recent results and to
[
21
,
24
,
32
]

for surveys on this topic.

Given a set
V
of size
n
and an integer
k\geq 2
, we use
\binom{V}{k}
to denote the family of all
k
-element subsets (
k
-subsets, for short) of
V
. A subfamily
\mathcal{H}\subset\binom{V}{k}
is called a
k
-uniform hypergraph
(or
k
-graph
in short). For
\mathcal{H}\subset\binom{V}{k}
, we often use
V(\mathcal{H})
to denote its vertex set
V
and use
\mathcal{H}
to denote its edge set. Define the complement of
\mathcal{H}
as
\overline{\mathcal{H}}:=\binom{V}{k}\setminus\mathcal{H}
. Given
A\subseteq V
, let
\mathcal{H}[A]
denote the sub
k
-graph of
\mathcal{H}
induced by
A
, namely,
\mathcal{H}[A]:=\mathcal{H}\cap\binom{A}{k}
. Define
\mathcal{H}-A:=\mathcal{H}[V(\mathcal{H})\setminus A]
. For
S\in\binom{V}{\ell}
with
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
, define the
link graph
of
S

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}(S):=\{T\colon S\cup T\in\mathcal{H}\}
and let
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(S)
be the cardinality of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{H}}(S)
. The
minimum
\ell
-degree

\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
is the minimum of
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(S)
over all
\ell
-element subsets
S
of
V(\mathcal{H})
. Clearly
\delta_{0}(\mathcal{H})
is the number of edges in
\mathcal{H}
. We refer to
\delta_{1}(\mathcal{H})
as the
minimum vertex degree
of
\mathcal{H}
and
\delta_{k-1}(\mathcal{H})
the
minimum codegree
of
\mathcal{H}
. We often omit the subscript
\mathcal{H}
when the context is clear.

Let
n,k,\ell
be positive integers with
\ell<k
and
(k-\ell)|n
. A
k
-graph is called an
\ell
-cycle
if there is a cyclic ordering of the vertices such that every edge consists of
k
consecutive vertices, every vertex is contained in an edge and two consecutive edges (where the ordering of the edges is inherited from the ordering of the vertices) intersect in exactly
\ell
-vertices. A
(k-1)
-cycle is also called a
tight cycle
. We say a
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
contains a Hamilton
\ell
-cycle if there is a subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
that forms an
\ell
-cycle and covers all vertices of
\mathcal{H}
.

Confirming a conjecture of Katona and Kierstead
[
18
]
, Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
25
,
27
, etc]
showed that for any fixed
k
, every
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(\mathcal{H})\geq n/2+o(n)
contains a Hamilton tight cycle. This is best possible up to the
o(n)
term by a construction given by Katona and Kierstead
[
18
]
. Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
26
]
eventually determined the minimum codegree threshold for Hamilton tight cycles in
3
-graphs for sufficiently large
n
, which is
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.

After a series of efforts
[
19
,
13
,
20
]
, the minimum codegree conditions for the existence of Hamilton
\ell
-cycle were determined asymptotically. Rödl and Ruciński
[
24
, Problem 2.9]
raised the question concerning the
exact
minimum codegree condition for Hamilton
\ell
-cycle. The case
k=3
and
\ell=1
was solved by Czygrinow and Molla
[
4
]
. The threshold for all
k\geq 3
and
\ell<k/2
was determined by Han and Zhao
[
14
]
. The case
k=4
and
\ell=2
was determined by Garbe and Mycroft
[
8
]
. Recently, the case
k\geq 6
,
k
is even and
\ell=k/2
was determined by Hàn, Han and Zhao
[
12
]
.

A
k
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is called an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle
if there is a partition
(L_{0},R_{0},L_{1},R_{1},\ldots,L_{t-1},R_{t-1})
of
V(\mathcal{H})
with
|L_{i}|=\ell
,
|R_{i}|=k-\ell
such that
L_{i}\cup R_{i}
and
R_{i}\cup L_{i+1}
(subscripts module
t
) are all edges of
\mathcal{H}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,t-1
. Similarly, we say
\mathcal{H}
contains a Hamilton
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle if there is a subhypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
that forms an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle and covering all vertices of
\mathcal{H}
. In
[
31
]
, the minimum
\ell
-degree condition for the existence of Hamilton
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle were determined asymptotically for
\ell\geq k/2
.

A
k
-graph
\mathcal{P}
is called an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-path
if there is a partition
(P_{0},P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{t})
of
V(\mathcal{P})
such that
P_{i}\cup P_{i+1}
is an edge of
\mathcal{P}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,t-1
and either
|P_{i}|=\ell
,
|P_{i+1}|=k-\ell
or
|P_{i}|=k-\ell
,
|P_{i+1}|=\ell
. We call
P_{0},P_{t}
the
ends
of this path.

1.2
Main results and relative construction

Given a set
V
of
n
vertices and a partition
V=A\cup B
, let
E_{odd}(A,B)

(E_{even}(A,B))
denote the family of all
k
-element subsets of
V
that intersect
A
in an odd (even) number of vertices. Define
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}(A,B)
to be the
k
-graph with the vertex set
V=A\cup B
and the edge set
E_{odd}(A,B)
. The complement
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}(A,B)
has edge set
E_{even}(A,B)
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{ext}(n,k)
be the family of
k
-graphs containing all hypergraphs
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}(A,B)
when
n/k-|A|
is odd and all
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(A,B)
when
|A|
is odd. Let

\delta(n,k,\ell)=\max_{\mathcal{H}\in\mathcal{H}_{ext}(n,k)}\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H}).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
28
,
29
]
)
.

Given integers
k,\ell
such that
k\geq 3
and
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
n\in k\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq n_{0}
satisfying
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})>\delta(n,k,\ell)
, then
\mathcal{H}
contains a perfect matching.

In the present paper, we mainly prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Main Result)
.

Given integers
k,\ell
such that
k\geq 7
and
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
n\in k\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq n_{0}
satisfying
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})>\delta(n,k,\ell)
, then
\mathcal{H}
contains a Hamilton
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle.

Note that a Hamilton
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle consists of two perfect matchings. Our result can be viewed as a strengthening of Theorem
1.1
for
k\geq 7
. It should be mentioned that the case for
k\geq 6
is even and
\ell=k/2
was already proved by Hàn, Han and Zhao
[
12
]
, they also determined the case when
n\in\frac{k}{2}\mathbb{N}
and
\ell=k/2
.

It is proved in
[
28
]
that no hypergraph in
\mathcal{H}_{ext}(n,k)
contains a perfect matching. We infer that no hypergraph in
\mathcal{H}_{ext}(n,k)
contains a
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle for
1\leq\ell\leq k-1
. It implies that the minimum
\ell
-degree condition in Theorem
1.2
is best possible.

In
[
28
]
, it is showed that

\delta(n,k,k-1)=\begin{cases}n/2-k+2&\mbox{if $k/2$ is even and $n/k$ is odd}\\
n/2-k+3/2&\mbox{if $k$ is odd and $(n-1)/2$ is odd }\\
n/2-k+1/2&\mbox{if $k$ is odd and $(n-1)/2$ is even}\\
n/2-k+1&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(1.1)

It seems hard to compute the precise values of
\delta(n,k,\ell)
for
\ell\leq k-2
, which are only known to be
(1/2+o(1))\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
, see
[
28
]
for details.

1.3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As a common approach to obtain exact results, Theorem
1.2
is proven by distinguishing an
extremal
case from a
non-extremal
case and solve them separately.

Let
\epsilon>0
and suppose that
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are
k
-graphs on
n
vertices. We say that
\mathcal{H}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, and write
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\pm\epsilon n^{k}
, if
\mathcal{H}
can be made a copy of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
by adding and deleting at most
\epsilon n^{k}
edges. If
\mathcal{H}
is a
k
-graph with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})\geq(1/2-o(1))\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
and
o(1)
-close to some
k
-graph in
\mathcal{H}_{ext}(n,k)
, then
\mathcal{H}
must be
o(1)
-close to some
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}(A,B)
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}(A,B)
with
|A|=\lceil n/2\rceil,|B|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
as well (cf.
[
12
]
). In the following we simply write
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
to indicate that there is an implicit partition

of almost equal size.

Theorem 1.3

(Extremal Case)
.

Given integer
k,\ell
with
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
and
k\geq 7
, there exist real
\epsilon>0
and integer
n_{0}=n_{0}(k,\epsilon)
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with

and
n\in k\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is
\epsilon
-close to any
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
and
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})>\delta(n,k,\ell)
, then
\mathcal{H}
contains a Hamilton
(\ell,k-\ell)
-cycle.

Theorem 1.4

(Non-extremal Case)
.

Given real
\epsilon>0
and integers
k,\ell
with
k\geq 3
and
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, there exist
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and real
\gamma>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{H}

-graph on

Proposition 1.1

We need a concentration inequality due to Frankl and Kupavskii.

Theorem 1.5

(Frankl-Kupavskii Concentration Inequality,
[

)

Suppose that

are integers and

. Let
\mathcal{G}\subset\binom{[m]}{k}
be a family, and
\theta=|\mathcal{G}|/\binom{[m]}{k}

\eta
be the random variable equal to the size of the intersection of
\mathcal{G}
with a
t
-matching
\mathcal{B}
of
k
-sets, chosen uniformly at random. Then
\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}[\eta]=\theta t
and, for any positive

, we have

\displaystyle\Pr[|\eta-\theta t|\geq 2\gamma\sqrt{t}]\leq 2e^{-\gamma^{2}/2}.

(1.2)

The following version of the Chernoff bound
for binomial distributions is also needed (see e.g.
[

). Recall that the binomial random variable with parameters

is the sum of

.

Suppose

\Pr(|X-\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}X|\geq a\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}X)\leq 2e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{3}\mathcal{\mathbb{E}}X}

We often write

2
Non-extremal Case

In this section, we deal with the non-extremal case by following the absorbing method initiated by Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
25
]
. We shall use the Frankl-Kupavskii Concentration Inequality to establish the Reservoir Lemma and the Absorbing Lemma.

For disjoint sets
L
,
R
,
C
with
|L|=\ell
,
|R|=k-\ell
,
|C|=2k
, if there exists an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-path on the vertex set
L\cup R\cup C
with ends
L,R
, then we say
C
connects
L,R
, or
C
is a
connector
for
L,R
.

Lemma 2.1

(Reservoir Lemma)
.

For integers
k,\ell
with
k\geq 3
,
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, suppose that
1/n\ll\gamma\ll\epsilon\ll 1/k
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})\geq(1/2-\gamma)\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is not
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
, then there exists a family
\mathcal{C}
with at most
\gamma^{19}n
disjoint
2k
-sets such that every
L,R\in V(\mathcal{H})
with
|L|=\ell,|R|=k-\ell
are connected by at least
\gamma^{23}n
members in
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Absorbing Lemma)
.

For integers
k,\ell
with
k\geq 3
,
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, suppose that
1/n\ll\gamma\ll\epsilon\ll 1/k
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})\geq(1/2-\gamma)\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is not
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
, then there exists an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-path
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathcal{H}
with
|V(\mathcal{P})|\leq\gamma n
such that for all subsets
U\subset V\setminus V(\mathcal{P})
of size at most
k\gamma^{18}n
and
|U|\in k\mathbb{N}
, there exists an
(\ell,k-\ell)
-path
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{H}
with
V(\mathcal{Q})=V(\mathcal{P})\cup U
and, moreover,
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
have the same ends.

Lemma 2.3

(Path-cover Lemma)
.

For integers
k,\ell
with
k\geq 3
,
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
, suppose that
1/n\ll 1/p\ll\alpha\ll\gamma\ll 1/k
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})\geq(1/2-\gamma)\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
. Then there are pairwise disjoint
(\ell,k-\ell)
-paths
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{m}
in
\mathcal{H}
such that
m\leq p
,
|\cup_{1\leq i\leq m}V(\mathcal{P}_{i})|\geq(1-\alpha)n
and each
\mathcal{P}_{i}
has ends
S_{i},T_{i}
with
|S_{i}|=\ell
,
|T_{i}|=k-\ell
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.

Now we ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Given integers
k\geq 3
and
k/2\leq\ell\leq k-1
,
suppose
1/n\ll 1/p,\alpha\ll\gamma\ll\epsilon\ll 1/k
. Let
V
be a set of size
n
and let
\mathcal{H}\subset\binom{V}{k}
with
\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H})\geq(1/2-\gamma)\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}
. Assume that
\mathcal{H}
is not
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is not
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
, we can find an absorbing path
\mathcal{P}_{0}
by Lemma
2.2
with ends
S_{0},T_{0}
and
|V(\mathcal{P}_{0})|\leq\gamma n
. Let
V_{1}=(V\setminus V(\mathcal{P}_{0}))\cup(S_{0}\cup T_{0})
, we claim that
\mathcal{H}[V_{1}]
is not
(\epsilon/2)
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{|V_{1}|,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{|V_{1}|,k}
. Suppose instead that there is a partition of
V_{1}=A\cup B
with
|A|\leq|B|\leq|A|+1
such that
\mathcal{H}[V_{1}]
is
(\epsilon/2)
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{|V_{1}|,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{|V_{1}|,k}
. We add the vertices of
V\setminus V_{1}
arbitrarily and evenly to
A
and
B
, and get a partition of
V(\mathcal{H})=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
with
|A^{\prime}|=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
,
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
, and
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
. Since
|V\setminus V_{1}|\leq\gamma n
, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}
becomes a copy of
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
after adding or deleting at most
\frac{\epsilon}{2}|V_{1}|^{k}+\gamma n\binom{n}{k-1}<\epsilon n^{k}
edges because
\gamma\ll\epsilon
. This means that
\mathcal{H}
is
\epsilon
-close to
\mathcal{B}_{n,k}
or
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n,k}
, a contradiction.

Furthermore, as
|V\setminus V_{1}|\leq\gamma n
, we have

\delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{H}[V_{1}])\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\gamma\right)\binom{n-\ell}{k-\ell}-\gamma n\binom{n-\ell-1}{k-\ell-1}>\left(\frac{1}{2}-k\gamma\right)\binom{|V_{1}|-\ell}{k-\ell}.

Apply Lemma
2.1
on
\mathcal{H}[V_{1}]
and get a family
\mathcal{C}
of order
(k\gamma)^{19}|V_{1}|\leq(k\gamma)^{19}n
. Let
V_{2}:=V\setminus(V(\mathcal{P}_{0})\cup V(\mathcal{C}))
,
n_{2}:=|V_{2}|
, and
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=\mathcal{H}[V_{2}]
. Note that

We follow a similar approach in

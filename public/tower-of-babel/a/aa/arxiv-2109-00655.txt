Regular polytopes, sphere packings and Apollonian sections Thanks: Partially supported by the CNRS and the Austrian Science Fund (FWF), projects F-5503 and P-34763.
Iván Rasskin

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the geometry and the arithmetic of a family of polytopal sphere packings induced by regular polytopes. We prove that every integral polytope is crystallographic and we show that there are 11 crystallographic regular polytopes in any dimension. After introducing the notion of Apollonian section, we study which Platonic crystallographic packings emerge as cross sections of the Apollonian arrangements of the regular 4-polytopes. Additionally, we compute the Möbius spectrum of every regular polytope.

1.
Introduction

Apollonian circle packings and their generalizations are currently active research areas in geometric number theory
[
KK23
,
BKK24
]
. In dimension 2, certain variants of integral Apollonian packings have been explored by substituting the building block with different circle packing modeled on polyhedra
[
GM08
,
Sta15
,
Zha18
,
BBH18
,
CCS20
,
RR21
]
. Although every polyhedron can be employed to construct a packing, not all of them possess an integral structure like the Apollonian one, which allows for packings where the bends (the inverses of the radii) of all the circles are integers. The fundamental question of determining which polyhedra are
integral
in this sense remains wide open
[
KN19
,
CCS20
]
.

Similarly, in dimension
3
, a family of crystallographic/Apollonian-like sphere packings arises by iteratively reflecting an initial sphere packing modeled on a
4
-polytope, as shown in Figure
1
. Integral crystallographic packings modeled on the
4
-simplex
[
Sod36
,
Kon19
]
and the orthoplex
[
Nak14
,
Dia14
,
She19
,
RR24
]
have been extensively studied. However, unlike polyhedra, not every
4
-polytope is
crystallographic
, meaning it may not serve as a suitable model for a crystallographic packing.

Figure 1
.

An integral hypercubic crystallographic packing after 0, 1, 2 and 3 iterations. The numbers are the bends of the spheres.

One of the main questions regarding integral crystallographic packings is the
local-to-global conjecture
, first introduced by Graham, Lagarias, Mallows, Wilks and Yan in
[
Gra+05
]
for Apollonian packings. The conjecture asserts that for any integral Apollonian circle packing, any sufficiently large number that avoids certain local modulo obstructions will be the bend of a circle in the packing. The local-to-global conjecture was disproven by Kertzer, Haag, Stange and Rickards in
[
Ker+24
]
. This question has also been studied in the context of other integral crystallographic packings. For instance, Kontorovich demonstrated in
[
Kon19
]
that the conjecture holds true for the family of integral simplicial crystallographic packings in dimension 3. Among the regular crystallographic sphere packings studied in this paper, two of them, depicted in Figure
2
, exhibit an interesting local-to-global behaviour, as their set of bends appears to have no local obstructions.

Conjecture 1
.

The set of bends of the hypercubic
\mathscr{P}_{\{4,3,3\}}(0,0,1,2)
and 24-cell
\mathscr{P}_{\{3,4,3\}}(0,0,1,2)
crystallographic packings is
\mathbb{N}
.

Figure 2
.
(Left) The integral hypercubic crystallographic packing
\mathscr{P}_{\{4,3,3\}}(0,0,1,2)
. (Right) The integral 24-cell crystallographic packing
\mathscr{P}_{\{3,4,3\}}(0,0,1,2)
.

In this paper, we explore the crystallography and integrality of regular polytopes in any dimension. We present a necessary condition for the entries of the Gramian of the dual of an edge-scribable polytope to be integral (Lemma
2
). This condition offers a straightforward method for proving the nonnintegrality of certain edge-scribable polytopes.

The study of the cross-sections is a classic method for identifying patterns in crystallographic packings
[
Boy74
,
Bar18
]
. We will introduce an algebraic approach based on the concept of
Apollonian section
, which will be useful for identifing the Platonic cross-sections of the regular crystallographic packings in dimension 3. Some of these sections have been used as a geometric framework for deriving results in geometric knot theory
[
RR24
]
.

There is an extensive family of different spectral invariants that have been prove to be useful for studying different algebraic and geometric graph properties. In this vein, Ramírez Alfonsín and the author defined in
[
RR21
]
a spectral invariant of edge-scribable polytopes called
Möbius spectrum
. It is currently unknown if this invariant is complete or if it is related to other invariants. If so, it could be helpful for enumerating edge-scribable polytopes. In particular, in dimension
3
, since all
3
-polytopes are edge-scribable and are completely characterized by their graphs, the Möbius spectrum can be naturally extended to these graphs, usually called
polyhedral graphs
. It would be interesting to investigate if co-spectral polyhedral graphs, with respect to the classical spectrum of the adjacency matrix, have different Möbius spectra. We conclude this paper by computing the Möbius spectrum of all the regular polytopes.

1.1.
Main contributions

(1)

We introduced a necessary condition for integral polytopes (Lemma
2
), enabling us to establish that every integral polytope is crystallographic (Theorem
1
).

(2)

We enumerate the 11 crystallographic regular polytopes in any dimension (Theorem

) and determine their integrality.

(3)

We give a unified Descartes Theorem for the regular
4
-polytopes in terms of their Schläfli symbol (Theorem

(4)

We show that the Platonic crystallographic packings can be obtained as cross sections of the Apollonian arrangements of the regular
4
-polytopes (Theorem
5
) and study the integral invariance of these sections (Theorem
6
).

(5)

We compute the Möbius spectrum of every regular polytope in dimension
d\geq 3
in terms of the number of vertices and the canonical length (Theorem
7

2.1.
Inversive coordinates

An
oriented hypersphere
, or simply
sphere
, of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\{\infty\}
, is the image of a spherical cap of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
under the stereographic projection. Every sphere
S
is uniquely defined by its center
c\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and its bend
b\in\mathbb{R}
(the reciprocal of the
signed
radius), or if
S
is a half-space, by its normal vector
\widehat{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
pointing to the interior and the signed distance
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
between its boundary and the origin. The
inversive coordinates
of
S
are represented by the
(d+2)
-dimensional real vector

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}(S)=\begin{cases}(bc,\dfrac{\overline{b}-b}{2},\dfrac{\overline{b}+b}{2})^{T}&\text{ if }b\not=0,\\
\quad\\
(\widehat{n},\delta,\delta)^{T}&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{cases}

where
\overline{b}=b\|c\|^{2}-\frac{1}{b}
is the
co-bend
of
S
. The co-bend is the bend of
S
after inversion through the unit sphere. The
inversive product
of two spheres
S,S^{\prime}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is the real value

(2)

\displaystyle\langle S,S^{\prime}\rangle=\mathbf{i}(S)^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{d+2}\mathbf{i}(S)

where
\mathbf{Q}_{d+2}
is the diagonal matrix
\mathrm{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)
of size
d+2
. The inversive product encodes the relative position of
S
and
S^{\prime}
according to the following criteria:

(3)

\displaystyle\langle S,S^{\prime}\rangle\begin{cases}<-1&\text{if }S\cap S^{\prime}=\emptyset,\\
=-1&\text{if $\partial S$ and $\partial S^{\prime}$ are tangent and }\mathrm{int}(S)\cap\mathrm{int}(S^{\prime})=\emptyset,\\
=1&\text{if $\partial S$ and $\partial S^{\prime}$ are tangent and }S\subseteq S^{\prime}\text{ or }S^{\prime}\subseteq S,\\
>1&\text{if }\partial S\cap\partial S^{\prime}=\emptyset\text{ and }S\subset S^{\prime}\text{ or }S^{\prime}\subset S.\\
\end{cases}

An arrangement of spheres
\mathcal{A}
in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, possibly infinite, is a
packing
if their interiors are mutually disjoint. The
Gramian
of a finite arrangement
\mathcal{A}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is the matrix
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{A})=(\langle S_{i},S_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
. The group of Möbius transformations of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
preserves the inversive product and acts linearly on the inversive coordinates. It acts as an orthogonal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to
\mathbf{Q}_{d+2}
. In particular, the inversion through a sphere
S
transforms the inversive coordinates through left multiplication with the matrix

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{I}_{d+2}-2\mathbf{i}(S)^{T}\mathbf{i}(S)\mathbf{Q}_{d+2}

where
\mathbf{I}_{d+2}
is the identity matrix of size
d+2
. Consider an arrangement of spheres
\mathcal{A}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d+2})
in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
such that the matrix
\mathbf{A}=(\mathbf{i}(S_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{i}(S_{d+2}))
has full rank. Let
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d+2})^{T}
be the bend vector of
\mathcal{A}
. Then, the inversion through
S
transforms
\mathbf{b}
via left multiplication with the so-called
bend matrix

[
CCS20
]

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}=(\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})^{T}.

2.2.
Polytopal sphere packings

The
polar
of a subset
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is the subset

X^{*}=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\mid\langle u,v\rangle\leq 1\text{ for all }v\in X\}
. The
stereographic sphere
of a point
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

outside

\mathbb{S}^{d}
(i.e. with
\|v\|>1
) is the sphere
S_{v}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
obtained by the stereographic projection of the spherical cap
\{-v\}^{*}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d}
. The inversive coordinates of
S_{v}
can be computed from the Cartesian coordinates
\mathbf{c}(v)
of
v
using the equation

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{i}(S_{v})=(\|v\|^{2}-1)^{-1/2}(\mathbf{c}(v),1).

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a
d
-polytope with vertices outside the unit sphere. The
arrangement projection
of
\mathcal{P}
is defined as the arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
of stereographic spheres of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
(see Figure
4
). A
d
-polytope is termed

if its edges are tangent to the unit sphere. If, in addition, the barycenter of the contact points is the origin, it is referred to as
canonical
(see Figure

[
Spr05
]
. A
d
-polytope is

if it admits an edge-scribed realization. In dimension
d\geq 3
, all the edge-scribed realizations of an edge-scribable
d
-polytope
\mathcal{P}
are equivalent up to Möbius transformations to a unique canonical realization
\mathcal{P}_{0}
(see
[
RR21
]
for more details).

Definition 2.1
.

For every
d\geq 2
, a sphere packing

in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

, where

is a canonical realization.

The combinatorial structure of a polytopal sphere packing

is encoded by the corresponding edge-scribable polytope

\mathcal{P}
are in bijection to the spheres and the tangency relations of

\mathcal{P}
correspond to the

of

of

is defined as the orbit space

where

denotes the group generated by inversions through the dual spheres. All the Apollonian arrangements of

are equivalent up to Möbius transformations
[

]
.

2.3.
Crystallographic and integral polytopes

In their work
[
KN19
]
, Kontorovich and Nakamura introduced the concept of
crystallographic packing
, which generalized families of highly symmetric sphere packings previously studied by Boyd
[
Boy74
]
and Maxwell
[
Max82
]
. These packings are dense infinite sphere packings derived from the limit set of a finitely generated hyperbolic reflection group. According to the Structure Theorem presented in
[
KN19
]
, every crystallographic packing
\mathscr{P}
can be decomposed as the action
\mathscr{P}=\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is a finite sphere packing called the
cluster
,
\langle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\rangle
is a geometrically finite subgroup of the group of Möbius transformations generated by the inversions through a finite arrangement of spheres
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
, called the
co-cluster
, satisfying that every sphere of
\mathcal{C}
is disjoint, tangent or orthogonal to every sphere of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
(see Figure
6
). Therefore, if an Apollonian arrangement
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})=\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
of a polytopal sphere packing is also a packing, then it is crystallographic with
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
as the cluster and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
as the co-cluster. In this case, due to the Möbius uniquenes of polytopal sphere packings,
all
the Apollonian arrangements of
\mathcal{P}
are also packings. Apollonian arrangements of
3
-polytopes are packings, but this is not true in general in higher dimensions
[
RR21
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For every
d\geq 3
, an edge-scribable

is

A crystallographic packing is said to be

\mathbb{Z}
. We extend this definition to the Apollonian arrangements (not necessarily packings) of edge-scribable polytopes.

3.
Geometry and arithmetic of the regular polytopal sphere packings

An edge-scribable polytope
\mathcal{P}
is crystallographic when the dihedral angles of
\mathcal{P}
, viewed as an hyperideal hyperbolic polytope, satisfy the
crystallographic restriction

[
Boy74
]
. This restriction dictates that the period of every rotation obtained as the product of two reflections through the facets is either
2,3,4,6,\infty
, imposing a condition on the dihedral angles. On the other hand, the dihedral angle
\alpha
of two adjacents facets
f
and
f^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}
is equal to the
intersection angle
of the corresponding dual spheres of
S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}
, as defined in
[
RR21a
]
. This angle can be computed from their inversive product by
\langle S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\rangle=\cos(\alpha)
. Therefore, the crystallographic restriction can be reformulated in terms of the inversive product of the dual spheres, as described in Lemma
1
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be an edge-scribable
(d+1)
-polytope with
d\geq 2
. Then
\mathcal{P}
is crystallographic if and only if, for any two dual spheres
S_{f}
,
S_{f^{\prime}}
of any polytopal
d
-sphere packing
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
, we have

|\langle S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\rangle|\in\{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\,|\,n\in\{0,1,2,3\}\}\cup[1,\infty)
.

In
[
CCS20
]
, Chait-Roth, Cui and Stier studied the integrality of polyhedra with few vertices. To establish the integrality of edge-scribable polytopes, we will extend their methods. We consider the group generated by the bend matrices of the dual inversions of any polytopal packing
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
. This group provides a linear representation
\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle<\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{R})
that acts on the set of bends of the Apollonian arrangement
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})=\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Notice that this representation does not depend on the packing but on the choice of the basis of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
, which corresponds to the choice of an affine basis of vertices of
\mathcal{P}
.
If all the bend matrices have integer entries, then
\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle<\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the action of
\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle
on an initial integral packing
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
produces an integral Apollonian arrangement. Alternatively, if the bend matrices have rational entries such that the set of denominators of the entries of all the matrices of
\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle<\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Q})
is bounded, then any arrangement
\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})
where the bends are rationals can be rescaled to obtain an integral arrangement.

For proving nonnintegrality, similar ideas are employed. If there is a bend matrix with an irrational entry or there is a rational representation

\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle<\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Q})
with unbounded denominators, then
\mathcal{P}
is not integral. The authors of
[
CCS20
]
demonstrated the unboundedness of the denominators of the entries for the powers of a matrix of
\langle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}\rangle<\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Q})
with infinite order. The following lemma provides a simpler method, akin to the characterization of integrality of Martin
[
Mar24
]
for Klenian arrengements induced by ideal class groups.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be an edge-scribable
(d+1)
-polytope with
d\geq 2
. If
\mathcal{P}
is integral then, for any two dual spheres
S_{f}
,
S_{f^{\prime}}
of any polytopal
d
-sphere packing
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
, we have
|\langle S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\rangle|\in\{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by contraposition. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
be a polytopal
d
-sphere packing with
d\geq 2
having two dual spheres
S_{f}
,
S_{f^{\prime}}
such that
|\langle S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\rangle|\not\in\{\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Let
\mathbf{M}\in\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{R})
be the product of two bend matrices corresponding to the inversions through
S_{f}
,
S_{f^{\prime}}
with respect to some basis. If
\mathbf{M}
has an irrational entry, then
\mathcal{P}
is not integral. Otherwise,
\mathbf{M}\in\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Q})
and in this case, we can combine the equations (
2
), (
4
), (
5
) with the hypothesis to obtain that

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{M})=d-2+4\langle S_{f},S_{f^{\prime}}\rangle^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}.

Let us now suppose that the denominators of the entries of the powers of
\mathbf{M}
are bounded. Hence, there is
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\mathbf{M}^{n}\in\mathrm{SL}_{d+2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then, the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{r\mathbf{M}^{n}}
is a monic polynomial with integer coefficients. On the other hand, the eigenvalues of
r\mathbf{M}^{n}
are
r\lambda_{1}^{n}\,\ldots,r\lambda_{d+2}^{n}
where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+2}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{M}
. Thus, for every
\lambda_{i}
and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\chi_{r\textbf{M}^{n}}(r\lambda_{i}^{n})=0
, so
r\lambda_{i}^{n}
is an algebraic integer. This implies that every
\lambda_{i}
is also an algebraic integer. Hence, the coefficients of
\chi_{M}
, which include
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{M})
, are in
\mathbb{Z}
, a contradiction. Therefore, the denominators of the entries of the powers of
\mathbf{M}
are unbounded, so
\mathcal{P}
is not integral. ∎

There are the following relations between the Apollonian arrangements of the regular 3- and 4-polytopes

\mathscr{P}_{\{3,3\}},\mathscr{P}_{\{3,4\}},\mathscr{P}_{\{4,3\}}\prec\mathscr{P}_{\{3,3,4\}},\\

For each relation

3.4.
The Möbius spectrum of the regular polytopes

In
[
RR21
]
, the authors introduced an invariant of edge-scribable
d
-polytopes
\mathcal{P}
with
d\geq 3
called the
Möbius spectrum

\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{P})
. This is defined as the multiset of the eigenvalues of the Gramian of any polytopal sphere packing
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
. A similar notion of the Möbius spectrum has been used in
[
Boy74
,
CL15
]
for enumerating Boyd-Maxwell sphere packings. Due to the Möbius uniqueness of edge-scribable polytopes,
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{P})
does not depend on the packing.

In this final section, we compute the Möbius spectrum of every regular polytope
\mathcal{P}
in terms of the number of vertices and the canonical length. We shall use a lemma established in
[
RR21
]
based on the Lorentzian model of the space of spheres. In this model, a sphere
S
of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}
corresponds to a unique vector
\mathbf{x}_{S}
of the Lorentz space
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
satisfying
\langle\mathbf{x}_{S},\mathbf{x}_{S}\rangle=1
. The inversive coordinates and the inversive product of spheres correspond to the Cartesian coordinates and the inner product of the Lorentzian vectors, respectively. The Lorentzian vector
\mathbf{x}_{v}
of a point
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
outside
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is the Lorentzian vector of the stereographic sphere of
v
. Additionally, for each
i=0,\ldots,d
the
Lorenzian barycenter
of a
i
-face
f
of a
d
-polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(including the case
f=\mathcal{P}
) whose vertices are outside
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, is

\mathbf{x}_{f}:=\frac{1}{|V(f)|}\sum_{v\in V(f)}\mathbf{x}_{v}.

Lemma 4

(
[
RR21
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a uniform edge-scribed
d
-polytope with
d\geq 3
. For each face
f
of
\mathcal{P}

(53)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{x}_{f},\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{P}}\rangle=-\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2}.

Theorem 7
.

For every
d\geq 3
, the Möbius spectrum of every regular
d
-polytope
\mathcal{P}
with
n>d
vertices is

(54)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{P})=(\frac{n}{d}(1+\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2})_{(d)},-n\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2},0_{(n-d-1)})

where
\ell_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the canonical length of
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
be a polytopal
(d-1)
-sphere packing where
\mathcal{P}
is a regular
d
-polytope with
d\geq 3
and
n
vertices.
The spheres of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}
correspond to a full-rank collection of
n
unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
. Let
\mathbf{V}
be the matrix of the Cartesian coordinates of these vectors. Since
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})
is a real symmetric matrix, its eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
are real. Moreover, the rank of
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})
is equal to the rank of
\mathbf{V}
, so there are
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d+1}
non-zero eigenvalues and
\lambda_{d+2}=\ldots=\lambda_{n}=0
. By combining the Spectral Theorem for real symmetric matrices with the definition of
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})
, we have the

(55)

\mathbf{UDU}^{-1}=\mathbf{VQ}_{d+1}\mathbf{V}^{T}

where
\mathbf{U}
is an orthogonal matrix,
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
and
\mathbf{Q}=\mathrm{diag}(1,\ldots,1,-1)
of size
d+1
. Then,
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{Gram}(\mathbf{W})
with
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{U}^{T}\mathbf{V}
. This implies that
\mathbf{D}
is the Gramian of a collection of
n
vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
containing an orthogonal basis without light-like vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d,1}
, so this basis must be made of
d
space-like vectors and one time-like vector. Therefore, there are
d
positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
and one negative eigenvalue
\lambda_{d+1}
. The symmetry of
\mathcal{P}
implies that
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{d}
. By adding all the rows of
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})-\lambda_{i}I_{n}
to the last one, we obtain a
n
-vector whose
i
-th entry is equal to

(56)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\mathbf{x}_{v_{i}},\mathbf{x}_{v_{j}}\rangle-\lambda_{i}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{v_{i}},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{x}_{v_{j}}\rangle-\lambda_{i}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{v_{i}},n\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{P}}\rangle-\lambda_{i}=-n\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2}-\lambda_{i}

by Lemma
4
. Therefore,
-n\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2}
is a negative root of the characteristic polynomial of
\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}})
, so
\lambda_{d+1}=-n\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2}
. By combining this with the equation
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Gram}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{P}}))=d\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{d+1}=n
, we obtain that
\lambda_{1}=\frac{n}{d}(1+\ell_{\mathcal{P}}^{-2})
.
∎

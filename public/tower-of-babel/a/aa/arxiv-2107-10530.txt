Diffusion-convection reaction equations with sign-changing diffusivity and bistable reaction term
Diego Berti, Andrea Corli, Luisa Malaguti

Abstract
We consider a reaction-diffusion equation with a convection term in one space variable, where the diffusion changes sign from the positive to the negative and the reaction term is bistable. We study the existence of wavefront solutions, their uniqueness and regularity. The presence of convection reveals several new features of wavefronts: according to the mutual positions of the diffusivity and reaction, profiles can occur either for a single value of the speed or for a bounded interval of such values; uniqueness (up to shifts) is lost; moreover, plateaus of arbitrary length can appear; profiles can be singular where the diffusion vanishes. AMS Subject Classification: 35K65; 35C07, 34B40, 35K57 Keywords: Sign-changing diffusivity, bistable reaction term, diffusion-convection reaction equations, traveling-wave solutions, sharp profiles.

c_{1}^{*}>\bar{c}
(see Figure

). We then prove that if (

) holds then
\varphi_{0}<\alpha
. To this end, since

we observe that

0\leq z_{1}(\varphi_{0})^{2}-z_{1}(0)^{2}=2\int_{0}^{\varphi_{0}}\left(h(\rho)-\sigma\right)z_{1}(\rho)\,d\rho-2\int_{0}^{\varphi_{0}}q(\rho)\,d\rho.

(5.12)

Define

\Delta:=\left(\bar{c}-h(0)\right)\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\left(\bar{c}-h(0)\right)^{2}\alpha+2M}=\Sigma\sqrt{\alpha}+\sqrt{\Sigma^{2}\alpha+2M}.

Since

is convex, by arguing similarly to the proof of
[

, then

turns out to be a strict lower-solution of
\eqref{e:problem0101}_{1}
in

, with

. Hence,

in

From (

Since

in
(\gamma,\alpha)
, if (

) holds then

; hence,

. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

We first observe that, since

Example 6.1

(

Assume first

and

In this case, from the symmetry properties of

and

and (
4.8

c_{3.2}^{*}=-c_{1.1}^{*}.

We have that the function

(a.ii)
, but looking for profiles associated to the speed

1/2
.

Assume now

and (g) with

. Note,

satisfies also (

). Despite the lack of symmetry in the definition of

(6.3)

(6.4)

so that

, for

Example 6.2

(Solutions with plateaus when
\alpha<\gamma
)
.

In the case
\alpha<\gamma
, we show that equation (
1.1
) can admit wavefronts, connecting
1
to
0
, such that
\varphi^{\prime}{}(\xi_{\gamma})=0
for every

(possibly not unique) with

under the notation of Theorem

(c)

) does not hold, that is when either

or

2.2
) we have
\varphi^{\prime}{}(\xi_{\gamma})<0
. We refer to
[
24
, Example 1]
for a similar example in the case

.

For simplicity, we set
\alpha=0
. Define
D
and
g
in
[0,1]
,

D(\varphi):=-\varphi^{2}\ \mbox{ and }\ g(\varphi):=\frac{\sigma+1}{2}\left(\varphi-\gamma\right)\left|\varphi-\gamma\right|^{\sigma-1}\left(1-\varphi\right)^{2},

for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Notice that
g
is not Lipschitz-continuous at point
\gamma
. We want to define a strict (at
0
) semi-wavefront from
1
to
0
. We have
D<0
in
(0,1]
and
D(0)=0
while
g<0
in
[0,\gamma)
,
g>0
in
(\gamma,1)
and

. Direct computations show that the function
z(\varphi):=\varphi\left|\varphi-\gamma\right|^{\frac{\sigma+1}{2}}(1-\varphi)
satisfies, for

,

(6.5)

with

. We have
z>0
in
(0,\gamma)\cup(\gamma,1)
and
z(0)=z(\gamma)=z(1)=0
. Moreover,
\dot{z}(\gamma^{-})=-\infty
and
\dot{z}(\gamma^{+})=\infty
. Also, we have

Let
\varphi_{\gamma,0}
be the solution of

\varphi^{\prime}{}=\frac{z(\varphi)}{D(\varphi)}=\frac{\left(\varphi-1\right)\left|\gamma-\varphi\right|^{\frac{\sigma+1}{2}}}{\varphi},\ \varphi(0)=\varphi_{0}\in(0,\gamma).

(6.6)

G^{\prime}{}<0
in
(0,\gamma)
, where

so that

and
\varphi_{\gamma,0}\in(0,\gamma)
. Analogously, because

also in
(\gamma,1)

as the solution of (
6.6

. The function

such that

and
\varphi_{\gamma,0}
is maximally defined in

and
\varphi_{\gamma,0}

\varphi
, the profile of a strict (at

) semi-wavefront which connects
1
to
0
.

\alpha=1
. We consider then the functions

for some
\tau>0

) wavefront from

to

,

, satisfies (

, such that

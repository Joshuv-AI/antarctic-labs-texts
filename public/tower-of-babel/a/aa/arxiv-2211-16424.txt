The e-property of asymptotically stable Markov semigroups
Ryszard Kukulski 1 and Hanna Wojewódka-Ściążko 1,2

Abstract
. The relations between asymptotic stability and the e-property of Markov semigroups, acting on measures defined on general (Polish) metric spaces, are studied. While usually much attention is paid to asymptotic stability (and the e-property has been for years verified only to establish it), it should be noted that the e-property itself is also important as it, e.g., ensures that numerical errors in simulations are negligible. Here, it is shown that any asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup with an invariant measure such that the interior of its support is non-empty satisfies the eventual e-property. Moreover, we prove that any Markov-Feller semigroup, which is strongly stochastically continuous, and which possesses the eventual e-property, also has the e-property. We also present an example highlighting that strong stochastic continuity cannot be replaced by its weak counterpart, unless a state space of a process corresponding to a Markov semigroup is a compact metric space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 60J25; Secondary 37A30, 46N30, 46E27, 60B10.

Introduction

Markov operators and Markov semigroups acting on
measures come naturally from Markov chains and continuous-time Markov processes, respectively.
We focus here on those whose underlying state space is a general (Polish) metric space.

The research on existence and uniqueness of
stationary distributions
of Markov processes, as well as convergence of the law of these processes to their unique stationaries that is independent of their initial distributions (
asymptotic stability
) has been carried out for years all over the world. In order to prove the above mentioned (and other) ergodic properties, various techniques referring to
equicontinuity
properties of families of Markov operators have been introduced (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
, where ergodic properties of Markov chains are established, or
[
8
,
9
]
, where asymptotic behavior of continuous-time Markov processes are studied). Initially, these type of methods have been developed for Markov processes evolving on compact metric spaces (cf.
[
10
]
) or locally compact Hausdorff topological spaces (cf.
[
11
]
). Further, the so-called
lower-bound technique
for equicontinuous families of Markov operators has been introduced to prove results for processes evolving on general (Polish) metric state spaces
(see, e.g.,
[
2
,
12
]
; cf.
[
13
,
14
]
, where one the first
results concerning asymptotics of Markov operators evolving in Polish metric spaces are obtained). In the literature the concepts like the
e-property

[
5
,
4
,
6
]
, the
eventual e-property

[
3
,
15
]
,
the Cesáro e-property

[
3
]
or even
uniform equicontinuity on balls

[
16
]
have been considered.
Recently, by utilizing a
Schur-like property
for measures, the authors of
[
17
]
have established a rigorous connection between the concepts of equicontinuity for Markov operators acting on measures and their dual operators acting on functions.

Studying the e-property, the natural question arises:
Does asymptotic stability immediately imply the e-property?
. The answer is negative and it is given in
[
18
]
. More precisely, the authors have provided a counterexample of a Markov-Feller operator which is asymptotically stable, but which does not possess the e-property. Simultaneously, they have proved that any asymptotically stable Markov-Feller operator with an invariant probability measure such that the interior of its support is non-empty satisfies the e-property. The result has been later generalised and made tight in
[
19
]
.

One of the main theorems of this paper (Theorem
2.1
) is an equivalent of
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
for semigroups of Markov operators, and so its proof is based on certain ideas derived from
[
18
]
.
The difference is that here the Feller property, asymptotic stability, and non-emptiness of the interior of the support of the unique invariant probability measure are not sufficient to guarantee the e-property (counterexamples are provided in Section
2.2
). Under these assumptions, we can only establish the
eventual e-property
, that is, the e-property that holds from a certain point in time, rather than over the entire time interval
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
(cf.
[
15
]
).
To prove the e-property, an additional assumption of
strong stochastic continuity
(defined as in
[
21
]
) has to be made.
More precisely, in Theorem
2.2
, we demonstrate that any Markov-Feller semigroup that is strongly stochastically continuous, and possesses the eventual e-property, also has the e-property.
Interestingly, the assumption cannot be weakened. Example
2.6
in Section
2.2
illustrates that stochastic continuity in its weaker form, that is, with pointwise convergence in the place of convergence in the supremum norm, does not necessarily imply the desired assertion.

An immediate consequence of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
is that an asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup possesses the e-property if it is also strongly stochastically continuous and the interior of the support of its unique invariant probability measure is nonempty (Corollary
2.4
).

Asymptotic stability, especially if achieved with the exponential rate, is one of the most desired ergodic properties of Markov processes, but it is the e-property that, if additionally met, guarantees that certain numerical errors can be treated as negligible in simulations (cf. also
[
6
]
, where it is proven that an asymptotically stable Markov-Feller operator converges to its stationary uniformly, provided that it satisfies a form of equicontinuity condition). This indicates why the theoretical result established here is important also from the point of view of applications.

It is also worth noting that stochastic continuity is not a highly restrictive requirement when working with Markov semigroups. It is necessary, for instance, to ensure joint measurability (as stated in
[
3
, Proposition 3.4.5]
) or to uniquely characterize a Markov-Feller semigroup through its weak infinitesimal operators (see
[
20
, Theorem 2.5]
).

After having already published the initial version of this paper as a preprint and having submitted it to the journal, the article
[
22
]
on a similar topic has appeared online, also as a preprint. The authors of that article present a slightly more general result by assuming
eventual continuity
of a given Markov semigroup instead of its asymptotic stability. In contrast, here (assuming asymptotic stability) we guarantee the e-property of a Markov semigroup in its strongest version (which we discuss in more detail in Section
1.3
and
Appendix II
).
In addition to this, we focus on providing justifications for the tightness of the results presented in this article. In particular, we give the negative answer to the fifth open question stated in
[
22
]
. The authors of
[
22
]
ask whether the equivalence between the e-property and the eventual e-property of Markov-Feller semigroups is still preserved if only stochastic continuity is assumed (in the place of strong stochastic continuity).
As already mentioned above, Example
2.6
in Section
2.2
proves that it is not true, unless the underlying state space is compact (cf.
Appendix I
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
we collect the notation used throughout the article, as well as some definitions and facts (relating, among others, to Markov semigroups and their ergodic properties) that we refer to later in the paper. Section
2
is devoted to the presentation of the main results, and it is divided into three parts, in which we formulate theorems (Section
2.1
), provide the above mentioned examples, pointing out that asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroups that are not strongly stochastically continuous do not necessarily enjoy the e-property (Section
2.2
), and prove all the results (Section
2.3
). In
Appendix I
we prove that whenever a state space of a Markov semigroup is a compact metric space, the definitions of stochastic continuity and strong stochastic continuity of this semigroup are equivalent. Finally, in
Appendix II
we analyze different definitions
of the e-property, provide examples showing that these definitions are in general not equivalent and prove that under certain conditions (i.e. asymptotic stability and stochastic continuity) they can be used interchangeably.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
(S,\rho)
be a Polish metric space, endowed with a Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathtt{Bor}(S)
. We will write
B(x,r)
for an open ball in
S
centered at
x\in S
and of radius
r>0
. The closure and the interior of a set
A\subset S
shall be denoted by
\mathtt{Cl}(A)
and
\mathtt{Int}(A)
, respectively, while for the boundry of
A
we will write
\partial(A)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
stands for the family of all real-valued, bounded and Borel measurable functions on
S
, equipped with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, and let
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
and
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
denote the subfamilies of
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
consisting of all continuous and all Lipschitz continuous functions, respectively.

1.1.
Spaces of Measures

Let us write
\mathcal{M}(S)
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
for the set of all finite Borel measures, defined on
(S,\mathtt{Bor}(S))
, and its subset consisting of all probability measures, respectively.

For brevity, in what follows, we will write
\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle
for the Lebesgue integral
\int_{S}f(x)\,\mu(dx)
of
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
with respect to a signed Borel measure
\mu
on
S
.

We say that a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}(S)

converges weakly
to
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)
as
n\to\infty

(
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}=\mu
) if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle f,\mu_{n}\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle
for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.
Citing the Portmanteau theorem (cf., e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 13.16]
), in the case of
\mu,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
, the weak convergence of
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
\mu
is equivalent to each of the following conditions:

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle f,\mu_{n}\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle
for all
f\in\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
,

•

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(F)\leq\mu(F)
for all closed sets
F\subset S
,

•

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(G)\geq\mu(G)
for all open sets
G\subset S
,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(A)=\mu(A)
for any
A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)
with
\mu(\partial(A))=0
.

The
support
of any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)
shall be defined, as usual, by

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}\;\mu=\{x\in S:\mu(B(x,r))>0\mbox{ for any }r>0\}.

1.2.
Markov Semigroups

An operator
P:\mathcal{M}(S)\to\mathcal{M}(S)
is called a
Markov operator
if

•

(\lambda\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})P=\lambda\,\mu_{1}P+\mu_{2}P

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(S)
,

•

\mu P(S)=\mu(S)
for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)
.

We say that a Markov operator
P
is
regular
if it possesses a
dual operator
, that is, a linear map (denoted by the same symbol) which acts on
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
and satisfies

\left\langle f,\mu P\right\rangle=\left\langle Pf,\mu\right\rangle\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)\quad\text{and any}\quad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S).

In this work, we will focus on
Markov-Feller operators
, that is, regular Markov operators whose duals leave the space
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
invariant.

It is worth noting that for a given
transition probability function

P:S\times\mathtt{Bor}(S)\to[0,1]
, that is, a map for which

P(x,\cdot):\mathtt{Bor}(S)\to[0,1]
is a probability measure for any fixed
x\in S
and
P(\cdot,A):S\to[0,1]
is a Borel measurable function for any
fixed
A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)
, one may define a regular Markov operator
P:\mathcal{M}(S)\to\mathcal{M}(S)
, along
with its dual operator
P:\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)\to\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mu P(A)=\left\langle P(\cdot,A),\mu\right\rangle\quad\text{for every}\quad A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)\quad\text{and every}\quad\mu\in\mathcal{M}(S),\\
&Pf(x)=\left\langle f,P(x,\cdot)\right\rangle\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in S\quad\text{and every}\quad f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S).\end{aligned}

(1.1)

A family
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is
called a
regular Markov semigroup
if it consists of regular Markov
operators
P(t):\mathcal{M}(S)\to\mathcal{M}(S)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
which form a semigroup (under composition) with the identity transformation
P(0)

as the unity element.
A semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is said to be
Markov-Feller
if

P(t)
has the Feller property for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. It is called

stochastically continuous at zero

if
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}P(t)f(x)=f(x)
for all
x\in S
and all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
(cf.
[
3
,
20
,
24
]
). Clearly, stochastic continuity at zero implies right-continuity at every
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{0}
,
but not left-continuity (see
[
25
, p. 93]
).
We will say that a semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is

stochastically continuous
(left and right) if
\lim_{t\to t_{0}}P(t)f(x)=P(t_{0})f(x)

for all
x\in S
, all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
. If, in
turn,
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|P(t)f-f\|_{\infty}=0
for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, we say,
after
[
21
]
, that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is
strongly
continuous
(or, more precisely,
strongly stochastically continuous
).
From the above definitions one can easily deduce that strong stochastic continuity implies stochastic continuity (left and right) (cf.
[
26
]
), which in turn, obviously, gives stochastic continuity at zero.
A reader interested in a precise distinction between these notions is referred
to Example
2.6
, where we define a stochastically continuous but not
strongly stochastically continuous Markov semigroup, and to

Appendix I
, where we demonstrate that in the case
of a compact metric state space all these notions coincide.

In the end, let us indicate that Markov semigroups are intimately connected with
Markov processes. Given a Markov semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
and some measure

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
, we can define a time-homogeneous
Markov process

\Phi
with transition semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
and initial distribution
\mu
as a family of
S
-valued random variables

\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
,
on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}_{\mu})
, satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\Phi(0)\in A\right)=\mu(A),\\
&\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\Phi(s+t)\in A\,\lvert\,\mathcal{F}(s)\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\Phi(s+t)\in A\,\lvert\,\Phi(s)\right)\quad\text{a.s.},\\
&\mathbb{P}_{\mu}\left(\Phi(s+t)\in A\,\lvert\,\Phi(s)=x\right)=P(t)(x,A)\end{aligned}

(1.2)

for all
A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)
, all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any
x\in S
,
where
\{\mathcal{F}(s)\}_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is the natural filtration of
\Phi
.

1.3.
Ergodic Properties of Markov Semigroups

Let us now recall the most important notions concerning the ergodicity of Markov semigroups, including asymptotic stability, the eventual e-property and the e-property.

A Markov semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is said to be

asymptotically stable
if there exists a unique probability measure
\mu_{*}
such that
\mu_{*}P(t)=\mu_{*}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

(
\mu_{*}

is then called an
invariant measure
of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
) and

\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu P(t)=\mu_{*}
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
.

Let
R
be a sufficiently large subset of the set
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.
According to
[
3
]
, a Markov semigroup

\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the
eventual e-property in
R
at
z\in S
if there exists
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for any
f\in R

\lim_{x\to z}\sup_{t\geq\tau}\,\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert=0,

(1.3)

and it has the
e-property in
R
at
z
as long as the above
holds with the supremum taken over the entire set
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
If (
1.3
) holds for each
z\in S
, then we say that

\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the
(eventual)
e-property
in
R
. A more general definition of the
eventual
e-property in
R
at
z\in S
has been introduced in

[
15
]
, where the author, instead of (
1.3
), only requires
the following:

\forall_{f\in R}\;\forall_{\varepsilon>0}\;\exists_{\delta>0}\;\exists_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\;\forall_{x\in B(z,\delta)}\;\forall_{t\geq\tau}\;\;\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert<\varepsilon.

In what follows, we will adhere to the latter (more general) definition.

In the end, let us indicate that in many papers (such as
[
3
,
18
,
15
,
25
,
22
]
, just to name a few),
R
is assumed to be the set
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
, although it can also be the set of bounded continuous functions with bounded (or compact) support (cf., e.g.,
[
1
,
11
,
4
]
), or even the entire set
\mathrm{C}_{b}(S)
(as in
[
19
]
or in this paper). Definitions of the e-property in different sets
R
are generally non-equivalent. We elaborate more on this in
Appendix II
, where we provide some examples proving this statement. Moreover, we show that the notions of the e-property in some of the sets
R
can be used interchangeably under the assumption that a given Markov semigroup is asymptotically stable and stochastically continuous at zero. Similar observation is derived in the case of a single Markov operator in
[
19
, Remark 2.1 and Lemma 3.4]
(although there only asymptotic stability is required).

2.
Main Results

Within this section we will formulate and establish the main results of this article. The theorems are supported by examples that demonstrate the necessity of the undertaken assumptions.

2.1.
Theorems

The main results of this paper read as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be an asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup, and let
\mu_{*}
be its unique invariant probability measure.
If

\displaystyle\mathtt{Int}\left(\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)\right)\neq\emptyset,

(2.1)

then
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the eventual e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a strongly stochastically continuous Markov-Feller semigroup. If
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the eventual e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, then it has the
e-property
in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
too.

To establish Theorem
2.2
, we employ the following lemma, which
in itself is an interesting result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a strongly stochastically continuous Markov-Feller
semigroup. Then, for every
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the
e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
on the time interval
[0,T]
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to z}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert=0\quad\text{for every}\quad z\in S\quad\text{and every}\quad f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S).

(2.2)

Finally, as an immediate consequence of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we get the following statement:

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a strongly stochastically continuous and asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup, and let
\mu_{*}
be its unique invariant probability measure satisfying (
2.1
).
Then
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.

2.2.
Examples

Before we proceed to the proofs of the above-stated results, let us first present two examples.

Example
2.5
demonstrates that condition (
2.1
), imposed in

[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
on an asymptotically stable
Markov-Feller chain to show that is has the
e-property
, is insufficient for a similar result in the case of a Markov semigroup. More precisely, Example
2.5
illustrates that,
without additional assumptions, beyond those stated in Theorem
2.1
, it
is not possible to establish the e-property. Instead, only the eventual e-property
can be concluded.

Example
2.6
, in turn, highlights the necessity of requiring strong stochastic continuity, rather than just stochastic continuity, to guarantee that an asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup
with unique invariant probability measure satisfying (
2.1
) has the e-property.

Example 2.5
.

Let
S=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and note that
S
, endowed with the Euclidean metric, is a Polish space.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any
A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)
define

\displaystyle P(t)\left(1/n,A\right)=e^{-nt}\delta_{1/n}(A)+\left(1-e^{-nt}\right)\delta_{1}(A),

(2.3)

and let

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P(t)(0,A)&=\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)\delta_{0}(A)+\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)\right)\delta_{1}(A).\end{aligned}

(2.4)

It is not hard to see that, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
P(t):S\times\mathtt{Bor}(A)\to[0,1]
, given by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), is a transition probability function. Thus, we can define the corresponding Markov operator
P(t)
as indicated in (
1.1
). Obviously,
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is then a Markov semigroup. To establish the Feller property it suffices to observe that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lim_{n\to\infty}P(t)f\left(1/n\right)&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(e^{-nt}f\left(1/n\right)+\left(1-e^{-nt}\right)f\left(1\right)\right)\\
&=\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)f(0)+\left(1-\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)\right)f(1)=P(t)f(0).\end{aligned}

Moreover, note that
\delta_{1}
is the unique invariant probability measure of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
such that
\mathtt{Int}(\mathrm{supp}(\delta_{1}))=\{1\}\neq\emptyset
.
Since

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-nt}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and also

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)=0
, we obtain that

\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is asymptotically stable. Let us, however, show
that it does not have the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
. Indeed, by definition,
\delta_{0}P(t)=\delta_{1}
for each
t>0
. On the other hand, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta_{1/n}P\left(1/n\right)=e^{-1}\delta_{1/n}+\left(1-e^{-1}\right)\delta_{1},

which implies that for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert P\left(1/n\right)f(1/n)-f(1)\rvert=e^{-1}\lvert f(0)-f(1)\rvert,

and so
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
fails the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
at 0.
Due to Theorem
2.1
, it however possesses the eventual e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.

As a consequence, referring to Corollary
2.4
, one can deduce that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}+}
is not strongly stochastically continuous (in fact, one can even easily show that it is not stochastically continuous at zero).

Example 2.6
.

Let

S=\{0\}\cup\{n:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{1/n:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and note that, if endowed with the Euclidean metric, it is a Polish space.
The idea is to define a Markov semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
so that it transforms measures in the following way:

\delta_{\frac{1}{n}}\to\delta_{n}\to\delta_{0}\to\delta_{1}\to\delta_{1}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2.

To achieve this, we introduce generators

Q_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\\
0&-1&n&0\\
0&0&-n&n\\
0&0&0&-n\end{pmatrix},\;\;\;n\geq 2,

(2.5)

of embeddable stochastic matrices
\exp(Q_{n}t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, that induce transition operators
P(t):S\times\mathtt{Bor}(A)\to[0,1]
in accordance with

\exp(Q_{n}t)=\begin{pmatrix}P(t)(1,1)&P(t)(0,1)&P(t)(n,1)&P(t)(1/n,1)\\
P(t)(1,0)&P(t)(0,0)&P(t)(n,0)&P(t)(1/n,0)\\
P(t)(1,n)&P(t)(0,n)&P(t)(n,n)&P(t)(1/n,n)\\
P(t)(1,1/n)&P(t)(0,1/n)&P(t)(n,1/n)&P(t)(1/n,1/n)\end{pmatrix},n\geq 2.

(2.6)

As a solution of (
2.6
), and referring to (
1.1
), we see that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
a Markov operator
P(t):\mathcal{M}(S)\to\mathcal{M}(S)
is determined by

\displaystyle\delta_{1}P(t)=\delta_{1},\qquad\delta_{0}P(t)=e^{-t}\delta_{0}+\left(1-e^{-t}\right)\delta_{1},

\displaystyle\delta_{n}P(t)=e^{-nt}\delta_{n}+\frac{n}{n-1}\left(e^{-t}-e^{-nt}\right)\delta_{0}+a_{n,t}\delta_{1},

\displaystyle\delta_{1/n}P(t)=e^{-nt}\delta_{1/n}+nte^{-nt}\delta_{n}+\frac{n^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}\left(e^{-t}+e^{-nt}(t-nt-1)\right)\delta_{0}+b_{n,t}\delta_{1}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}
, where
a_{n,t},b_{n,t}\in\mathbb{R}
are given by

(\delta_{n}P(t))(S)=1
and
(\delta_{1/n}P(t))(S)=1
, respectively.

One can easily check that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, defined as above, is indeed a Markov semigroup, and since
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{1/n}P(t)=\delta_{0}P(t)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, it also possesses the Feller property. Moreover, it is not difficult to see that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is stochastically continuous (left and right) and asymptotically
stable, with the unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{*}=\delta_{1}
such that
\mathtt{Int}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu_{*}))\neq\emptyset
(the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
are thus satisfied). However, since for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}

\delta_{1/n}P(1/n)=e^{-1}\delta_{1/n}+e^{-1}\delta_{n}+\frac{n^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}(e^{-1/n}+\frac{1}{n}e^{-1}-2e^{-1})\delta_{0}+b_{n,1/n}\delta_{1},

we obtain that for all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lvert P(1/n)f(1/n)-P(1/n)f(0)\rvert=\limsup_{n\to\infty}e^{-1}\lvert f(n)-f(0)\rvert.

Hence,
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
does not have the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, and thus, in view of Corollary
2.4
, it is not strongly stochastically continuous.

2.3.
Proofs

Within this section we are going to present the proofs of the main results of this paper, that is, Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
.

Let us begin by formulating and proving two lemmas that will help us establish Theorem
2.1
. The proofs of both these lemmas, as well as the proof of Theorem
2.1
itself, draw on certain ideas employed in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be an asymptotically stable Markov-Feller semigroup, and let
\mu_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
be its unique invariant probability measure.
If condition (
2.1
) holds, then
for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
and any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and an open ball
B\subset\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\left\lvert P(t)f(x)-\left\langle f,\mu_{*}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in B\quad\text{and all}\quad t\geq T_{0}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Due to (
2.1
), we know that there exists an open set
W\subset\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
which is nonempty, and therefore we can define
Y:=\mathtt{Cl}(W)\subset S
. Note that as a subspace of a Polish space,
Y
is Polish too, and thus it is a Baire space, which fact shall be used later in the proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
define

\displaystyle Y_{t}:=\left\{x\in Y:\;\;\left\lvert P(s)f(x)-\left\langle f,\mu_{*}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\;\;\text{for all
}\;\;s\geq t\right\}.

Since
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is assumed to be Feller, that is,
P(s)f
is continuous for any
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we see that the sets
Y_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, are closed in
Y
(as intersections of closed sets).

Let us now take a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers such that
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=\infty
. Referring to the fact that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is, by assumption, asymptotically stable, we obtain that

Y=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Y_{t_{n}}
.
Note that, by definition,
\mathtt{Int}(Y)\neq\emptyset
, whence there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mathtt{Int}(Y_{t_{N}})\neq\emptyset

(2.8)

(otherwise all the sets
Y_{t_{n}}
would be nowhere dense, and so, by the Baire theorem,
Y=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Y_{t_{n}}
would be a boundary set, which would obviously contradict the nonemptiness of
W
).

Finally, we observe that
\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
is closed in
S
, whence
Y_{t_{N}}\subset\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
. Condition (
2.8
) then implies the existence of an open set
B\subset Y_{t_{N}}\subset\mathrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
. Letting
T_{0}:=t_{N}
, we obtain the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
and
A\in\mathtt{Bor}(S)
. If
I
is an infinite set
of indexes, and
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset\mathtt{Bor}(S)
is a family
of pairwise disjoint sets satisfying
A=\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
, then there exists a
countable subset
J
of
I
such that
\mu(A_{i})=0
for all
i\in I\backslash J
.

Proof.

Define
J:=\{i\in I:\;\mu(A_{i})>0\}
, and observe that for all
i\in J
there
exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\mu(A_{i})\in(1/(n+1),1/n]
. Since

\mu(A)<\infty
, for any

n\in\mathbb{N}
only a finite number of indexes
i\in J
can satisfy

\mu(A_{i})\in(1/(n+1),1/n]
. As a consequence,

J
can be at most a countable set.
∎

The proof of Theorem
2.1
then proceeds as follows:

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Fix
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
and
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily. Moreover, let
x_{0}\in S
, and let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S
be an arbitrary sequence converging to
x_{0}
.

By Lemma
2.7
, there exist
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and an open ball
B\subset\textrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
, say
B=B(z,r)
for some
z\in\textrm{supp}\left(\mu_{*}\right)
and some
r>0
, for which (
2.7
) holds with
\varepsilon/4
(in the place of
\varepsilon
). Further, observe that
\gamma:=\mu_{*}(B(z,r))>0
, and choose
\alpha\in(0,\gamma)
.
Using the fact that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is asymptotically stable, and referring to the Portmanteau theorem, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\mu P(t)\left(B(z,r)\right)\geq\mu_{*}\left(B(z,r)\right)=\gamma>\alpha\quad\text{for any}\quad\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S).

(2.9)

Now, fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\displaystyle 4(1-\alpha)^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon,

(2.10)

and let us define the sequences
\{t_{l}\}_{l=1}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

and
\{\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}\}_{l=1}^{k},\{\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}\}_{l=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)

as follows:

(1)

Fix
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\delta_{x_{0}}P(t_{1})(B(z,r))>\alpha,

the existence of which follows from (
2.9
), and note that

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)(B(z,r))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r-1/n\right)\right),

whence we can choose
n_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
for which

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{0}}P(t_{1})(B(z,r-1/n_{*}))>\alpha.

Further, Lemma
2.8
implies that there is
r_{1}\in[r-1/n_{*},r)
satisfying

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(\partial\left(B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)\right)=0.

(2.11)

In view of the above, we can define

\displaystyle\nu_{1}^{x_{0}}(\cdot):=\frac{\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)}{\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)}

(2.12)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{x_{0}}:=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left(\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)-\alpha\nu_{1}^{x_{0}}\right),

(2.13)

which are both Borel probability measures on
S
.
Let us also note that, by the Feller property of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\delta_{x_{i}}P\left(t_{1}\right)=\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right).

(2.14)

(2)

Let
l\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
. Suppose that the Borel probability measures
\nu_{m}^{x_{0}}
and
\mu_{m}^{x_{0}}
are all well-defined for
m\leq l
. Then there exists
t_{l+1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)(B(z,r))>\alpha
. Using arguments similar as in Step 1, we can further conclude that there is
r_{l+1}<r
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)>\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(\partial\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)\right)=0.

This, in turn, allows us to define

\displaystyle\nu_{l+1}^{x_{0}}(\cdot):=\frac{\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)}{\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)}

(2.15)

and

\displaystyle\mu_{l+1}^{x_{0}}:=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left(\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)-\alpha\nu_{l+1}^{x_{0}}\right),

(2.16)

both belonging to
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
.

(3)

Proceeding as in Step 2, we end up with the inductively defined sequences
\{t_{l}\}_{l=1}^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

and
\{\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}\}_{l=1}^{k},\{\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}\}_{l=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
.
Let us indicate that for any
t\geq t_{1}+\ldots+t_{k}
we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta_{x_{0}}P(t)=&\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)P\left(t-t_{1}\right)\\
=&\alpha\nu_{1}^{x_{0}}P\left(t-t_{1}\right)+(1-\alpha)\mu_{1}^{x_{0}}P\left(t-t_{1}\right)=\ldots\\
=&\alpha\nu_{1}^{x_{0}}P\left(t-t_{1}\right)+\ldots\\
&+\alpha(1-\alpha)^{k-1}\nu_{k}^{x_{0}}P\left(t-t_{1}-\ldots-t_{k}\right)\\
&+(1-\alpha)^{k}\mu_{k}^{x_{0}}P\left(t-t_{1}-\ldots-t_{k}\right).\end{aligned}

(2.17)

Combining observation (
2.14
) and the Portmanteau theorem, we see that there exists
i_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{i}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)>\alpha\quad\text{for all}\quad i\geq i_{1},

and thus, for any
i\geq i_{1}
, we can define
\nu_{1}^{x_{i}},\mu_{1}^{x_{i}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
, exactly as in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), respectively, but with
x_{i}
in the place of
x_{0}
.

Using (
2.14
) and the Portmanteau theorem again, we observe that, for every open subset
O
of
S
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}\delta_{x_{i}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(O\cap B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)\geq\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(O\cap B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right).

This, in turn, implies that

\displaystyle\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\delta_{x_{i}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right)=\delta_{x_{0}}P\left(t_{1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(z,r_{1}\right)\right),

and, as a consequence,
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\nu_{1}^{x_{i}}=\nu_{1}^{x_{0}}
and
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{1}^{x_{i}}=\mu_{1}^{x_{0}}
.

For any sufficiently large
i
, we can define
\{\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}\}_{l=1}^{k},\{\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}\}_{l=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
inductively on
l
as follows. Let
l\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, and suppose that there is
i_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
i\geq i_{l}
, the measures
\nu_{1}^{x_{i}},\ldots,\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}
and
\mu_{1}^{x_{i}},\ldots,\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}
are all well-defined Borel probability measures satysfying
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}=\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}
and
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}=\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}
. The Feller property of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
then gives the following:
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}P(t_{l+1})=\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P(t_{l+1})
.
Moreover, due to the choice of
r_{l+1}
, for which

\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(\partial\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)\right)=0
,
and, again, the Portmanteau theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)=\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right).

Repeating the arguments already presented above, we can claim that there exists
i_{l+1}\geq i_{l}
such that for all
i\geq i_{l+1}

\displaystyle\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)>\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{l+1}^{x_{i}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{l}^{x_{i}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(z,r_{l+1}\right)\right)=\mu_{l}^{x_{0}}P\left(t_{l+1}\right)\left(\cdot\cap B\left(x,r_{l+1}\right)\right),

whence
\mu_{l+1}^{x_{i}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
for every
i\geq i_{l+1}
and also

\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\nu_{l+1}^{x_{i}}=\nu_{l+1}^{x_{0}}
,
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{i\to\infty}\mu_{l+1}^{x_{i}}=\mu_{l+1}^{x_{0}}
.

In the final part of the proof, let us observe that for every
i\geq i_{k}
and every
t\geq t_{1}+\ldots+t_{k}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \delta_{x_{i}}P(t)=&\alpha\nu_{1}^{x_{i}}P\left(t-t_{1}\right)+\ldots+\alpha(1-\alpha)^{k-1}\nu_{k}^{x_{i}}P\left(t-t_{1}-\ldots-t_{k}\right)\\
&+(1-\alpha)^{k}\mu_{k}^{x_{i}}P\left(t-t_{1}-\ldots-t_{k}\right).\end{aligned}

(2.18)

Since, for every
l\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and every
i\geq i_{k}
,
\textrm{supp}\left(\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}\right),\textrm{supp}\left(\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}\right)\subset\overline{B\left(z,r_{l}\right)}\subset B(z,r)
, we obtain, by Lemma
2.7
, that for each
l\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, each
i\geq i_{k}
and each
t\geq T_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\lvert\left\langle f,\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}P(t)-\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}P(t)\right\rangle\right\rvert=&\left\lvert\left\langle P(t)f,\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}\right\rangle-\left\langle P(t)f,\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\\
\leq&\left\lvert\left\langle P(t)f-\left\langle f,\mu_{*}\right\rangle,\nu_{l}^{x_{i}}\right\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\langle P(t)f-\left\langle f,\mu_{*}\right\rangle,\nu_{l}^{x_{0}}\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.\end{aligned}

(2.19)

Now, let
\tau:=t_{1}+\ldots+t_{k}+T_{0}
and
I:=i_{k}
. Referring subsequently to (
2.17
), (
2.18
), (
2.19
) and (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\tau}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{i}\right)-P(t)f\left(x_{0}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\geq\tau}\left\lvert\left\langle f,\delta_{x_{i}}P(t)-\delta_{x_{0}}P(t)\right\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\geq T_{0}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(\alpha+\alpha(1-\alpha)+\ldots+\alpha(1-\alpha)^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\alpha)^{k}\left\lvert\left\langle f,\mu_{k}^{x_{i}}P(t)-\mu_{k}^{x_{0}}P(t)\right\rangle\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+2(1-\alpha)^{k}\|f\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad i\geq I,

and thus the proof is completed.
∎

Now, let us proceed with the proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Let
z\in S
. Suppose (contrary to our claim) that there exist
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
and sequences
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, of points in
[0,T]
and
S
, respectively, where the latter converges to
z
, such that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{k}\right)f(z)\right\rvert>\varepsilon\;\;\;\text{for some}\;\;\;\varepsilon>0.

(2.20)

Since the indicated sequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, we can choose its subsequence (for the sake of simplicity of notation also denoted by
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
) which converges monotonically to a certain point in
[0,T]
, say
t_{0}
. Let us now consider the following two cases: (a)
t_{k}\to t_{0}^{+}
and (b)
t_{k}\to t_{0}^{-}
.

(a)

According to (
2.20
), there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\geq K
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varepsilon\leq&\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{k}\right)f(z)\right\rvert\\
\leq&\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}-t_{0}\right)P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)\right\rvert+\left\lvert P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(z\right)\right\rvert\\
&+\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}-t_{0}\right)P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(z\right)-P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(z\right)\right\rvert,\end{aligned}

(2.21)

where the semigroup property of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has also been used above.
Note that, due to the Feller property,
P(t_{0})f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, and therefore the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.21
) converges to zero as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, observe that
u_{k}:=t_{k}-t_{0}\to 0^{+}
, and thus, referring to strong stochastic continuity of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
, we see that the first and the last therm on the right-hand side of (
2.21
) also vanish if
k\to\infty
. The above, however, contradicts (
2.20
).

(b)

According to (
2.20
), there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\geq K
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \varepsilon\leq&\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{k}\right)P\left(t_{0}-t_{k}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)\right\rvert+\left\lvert P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P\left(t_{0}\right)f\left(z\right)\right\rvert\\
&+\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)f\left(z\right)-P\left(t_{k}\right)P\left(t_{0}-t_{k}\right)f\left(z\right)\right\rvert,\end{aligned}

(2.22)

where the semigroup property of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has also been used above. Due to the Feller property, the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.22
) converges to zero as
k\to\infty
.
Let us further observe that

\displaystyle\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)f\left(z\right)-P\left(t_{k}\right)P\left(t_{0}-t_{k}\right)f\left(z\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert P\left(t_{k}\right)\left(f-P\left(t_{0}-t_{k}\right)f\right)\left(z\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f-P\left(t_{0}-t_{k}\right)f\right\|_{\infty},

which expression converges to zero as
k\to\infty
, thanks to the assumed strong stochastic continuity. Using the same arguments, we see that the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.22
) also converges to zero as
k\to\infty
. The above, however, contradicts (
2.20
).

The proof is completed.
∎

Finally, employing Lemma
2.3
, we can establish Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, choose
z\in S
and a sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to it.
Obviously, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert\leq&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert\\
&+\sup_{t\geq T}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert.\end{aligned}

(2.23)

We know, by the assumed eventual e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
, that for every
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)

there exist
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\geq K

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\tau}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert<\varepsilon/2.

(2.24)

Moreover, referring to Lemma
2.3
, we obtain that there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq M

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\left\lvert P(t)f\left(x_{k}\right)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert<\varepsilon/2.

(2.25)

Now, combining (
2.24
) and (
2.25
), we see that for all
k\geq\max\{K,M\}
the right-hand side of (
2.23
) (with
T=\tau
) is smaller than
\varepsilon
, which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix I: Stochastic continuity of Markov semigroups with compact state spaces

The purpose of this appendix is to demonstrate that the notions of stochastic
continuity and strong stochastic continuity of Markov semigroups coincide when the
underlying state space is a compact metric space (Theorem

2.10
).
This statement, however, is not true if we weaken
the assumption on the given space, e.g. if this space is only locally compact (cf. Example
2.6
).

In what follows for all
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
and
\epsilon>0
we will write

\mu_{1}\sim_{f,\epsilon}\mu_{2}

whenever
\lvert\langle f,\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\rangle\rvert<\epsilon
.

Before proceeding to the desired theorem, let us first establish the following lemma (which shall be useful in the proof of Theorem
2.10
):

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
S
be a Polish space, and let
\mu,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
. Moreover, assume that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a
stochastically continuous at zero Markov-Feller semigroup. If
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}=\mu
, then

\forall_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta>0\\
\epsilon>0\\
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)\end{subarray}}\;\;\exists_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{*}\in(0,\theta)\\
\delta>0\\
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\;\;\forall_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in B(t_{*},\delta)\\
n\geq n_{0}\end{subarray}}\quad\mu_{n}P(t)\sim_{f,\epsilon}\mu.

Proof.

Fix
\theta>0
,
\epsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
. Further, choose
\theta_{0}\in(0,\theta)
such that
\mu P(t)\sim_{f,\epsilon/2}\mu
for all
t\leq\theta_{0}
, which can be done thanks to the assumed stochastic continuity at zero of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
.

For any
t\in[0,\theta]
let
n_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
denote the smallest number such that for any
n\geq n_{t}
we have

\mu_{n}P(t)\sim_{f,\epsilon/2}\mu
. Next, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

A_{n}:=\{t\in[0,\theta_{0}]:n_{t}\leq n\}
. Observe that
A_{n}\subset A_{n+1}
and
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}=[0,\theta_{0}]
. Obviously,
[0,\theta_{0}]
is a second category set, whence, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\mathtt{Int}(\mathtt{Cl}(A_{n_{0}}))\neq\emptyset
. In this connection, we can assume that there are some
t_{*}\in(0,\theta_{0})
and
\delta>0
such that
B(t_{*},\delta)\subset\mathtt{Cl}(A_{n_{0}})
.

Fix
t\in B(t_{*},\delta)
and
n\geq n_{0}
. Define
B_{n}:=B(t_{*},\delta)\cap A_{n}
. The set
B_{n}
is dense in
B(t_{*},\delta)
. We can choose a sequence
(t_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset B_{n}
so that
t_{k}\to t^{+}
and an index
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
for which

\mu_{n}P(t_{k_{0}})\sim_{f,\epsilon/2}\mu_{n}P(t)
. Then, we finally get

\left\lvert\left\langle f,\mu_{n}P(t)-\mu\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\left\langle f,\mu_{n}P(t)-\mu_{n}P\left({t_{k_{0}}}\right)\right\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\langle f,\mu_{n}P\left(t_{k_{0}}\right)-\mu\right\rangle\right\rvert<\epsilon,

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.10
.

If
S
is a compact metric space and
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a stochastically continuous at zero Markov-Feller semigroup, then
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is also strongly stochastically continuous.

Proof.

Suppose (contrary to our claim) that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is not strongly stochastically continuous. Then, there are
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
,
\varepsilon>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
converging to
0
such that for all
n\geq N

\left\lvert P\left(t_{n}\right)f\left(x_{n}\right)-f\left(x_{n}\right)\right\rvert>\varepsilon.

(2.26)

By compactness of
S
,
we can choose a subsequence of
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(for the sake of simplicity of notation also denoted by
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
) which is convergent to some
x\in S
and for which
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{x_{n}}P(t_{n})=\mu
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
, where the latter is a consequence of the Prokhorov theorem. Combining it with (
2.26
), we get

\left\lvert\left\langle f,\delta_{x_{n}}P(t_{n})-\mu\right\rangle\right\rvert+\left\lvert\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle-f(x)\right\rvert+\left\lvert f(x)-f\left(x_{n}\right)\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert P\left(t_{n}\right)f\left(x_{n}\right)-f\left(x_{n}\right)\right\rvert>\varepsilon,

hence, after moving to the limit (with
n\to\infty
) on the right and left sides of the above inequality, we obtain

\lvert\langle f,\mu\rangle-f(x)\rvert\geq\varepsilon.

(2.27)

Then, take
\theta>0
such that for all
t\leq\theta
we have
\delta_{x}P(t)\sim_{f,\varepsilon/3}\delta_{x}
, which can be done thanks to the assumed stochastic continuity at zero of
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
. For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
let us introduce
\mu_{n}:=\delta_{x_{n}}P(t_{n})
. By Lemma
2.9
, there are
t_{*}\in(0,\theta)
,
\delta>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu_{n}P(t)\sim_{f,\varepsilon/3}\mu
for all
t\in B(t_{*},\delta)
and all
n\geq n_{0}
. It now suffices to observe that there exists

n_{1}\geq\max\{n_{0},N\}
for which
t_{n_{1}}\leq\min\{t_{*},\delta\}
and
\delta_{x_{n_{1}}}P(t_{*})\sim_{f,\epsilon/3}\delta_{x}P(t_{*})
. Hence, we finally get the following:

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\sim_{f,\epsilon/3}\delta_{x}P\left(t_{*}\right),\qquad\delta_{x}P\left(t_{*}\right)\sim_{f,\epsilon/3}\delta_{x_{n_{1}}}P\left(t_{*}\right),

\displaystyle\delta_{x_{n_{1}}}P\left(t_{*}\right)=\delta_{x_{n_{1}}}P\left(t_{n_{1}}\right)P\left(t_{*}-t_{n_{1}}\right)=\mu_{n_{1}}P\left(t_{*}-t_{n_{1}}\right)\sim_{f,\epsilon/3}\mu,

This, however, gives us
\delta_{x}\sim_{f,\epsilon}\mu
, and thus contradicts (
2.27
). The proof is completed.
∎

Appendix II: Relations between the notions of the e-property in
R
for different sets
R

By
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
let us denote the subfamily of
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
that consists of non-negative Lipschitz continuous functions with bounded supports.

In this appendix, we present an analysis of various definitions of the
e-property
in
R
, where
R\in\{\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S),\mathcal{L}_{b}(S),\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)\}
, which are non-equivalent in general, as demonstrated in the examples below.

Example 2.11
.

Let
S=[1,\infty)
be a given Polish space equipped with the Euclidean metric, and let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a Markov semigroup determined by
\delta_{x}P(t)=\delta_{x+t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\geq 1
. Using the similar reasoning as presented in
[
19
, Remark 2.1]
we can show that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
, but does not have the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
at any point
z\in S
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
S=[1,\infty)
be a given Polish space equipped with the Euclidean metric, and let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a Markov semigroup determined by
\delta_{x}P(t)=\delta_{xe^{t}}
for
t\geq 0
and
x\geq 1
. Take arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
and
z\in S
. Let
L_{f}
be a Lipschitz constant of
f
. There is
x_{0}\geq z
, such that
f(x)=0
for all
x\geq x_{0}
. Therefore, we get

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\,\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert&=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\,\left\lvert f\left(xe^{t}\right)-f\left(ze^{t}\right)\right\rvert\\
&=\sup_{\ln(x_{0}/(z-0.5))\geq t\geq 0}\,\lvert f(xe^{t})-f(ze^{t})\rvert\\
&\leq\sup_{\ln(x_{0}/(z-0.5))\geq t\geq 0}\,L_{f}\,e^{t}\lvert x-z\rvert=\frac{L_{f}x_{0}}{z-0.5}\,\lvert x-z\rvert\end{split}

for any
x\in[z-0.5,z+0.5]
, and thus

\lim_{x\to z}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\,\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert=0.

We therefore see that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
.

To prove that
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
does not have the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
at any
z\in S
, let us define
f\in\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
as follows:

\begin{split}f(x)&=2(x-1),\quad x\in[1,1.5),\\
f(x)&=-2(x-2),\quad x\in[1.5,2),\\
f(x)&=f(x-1),\quad x\geq 2.\\
\end{split}

Further, let
z\in S
be chosen arbitrarily. Consider a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
S
such that
x_{n}=(1+1/(2n))z
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and note that it converges to
z
. Moreover, consider a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq z}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
t_{n}=\ln(n/z)
for
n\geq z
. We then get

\lvert P(t_{n})f(x_{n})-P(t_{n})f(z)\rvert=\left\lvert f\left(x_{n}e^{t_{n}}\right)-f\left(ze^{t_{n}}\right)\right\rvert=\lvert f(n+0.5)-f(n)\rvert=1,

and thus
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
fails the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
at any
z\in S
.

Before we present conditions under which certain definitions of the e-property coincide, let us provide an extension of the Portmanteau theorem.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
S
be a Polish metric space, and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
,
\{\mu_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
. Then
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}=\mu
if and only if
\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle f,\mu_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle
for any
f\in\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
.

Proof.

The direct implication obviously follows from the definition of weak convergence, hence we shall only prove the reverse implication. To do so, we will show that
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F)\leq\mu(F)
for all closed sets
F\subset S
.
For any
\epsilon>0
and an arbitrary closed set
F\subset S
let us define a closed set

F_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\{x\in S:\text{dist}(x,F)\leq\epsilon\}.

Moreover, let us define the functions
f_{F,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
as
f_{F,\epsilon}(x)=1-\text{dist}(x,F)/\epsilon
for
x\in F_{\epsilon}
and
f_{F,\epsilon}(x)=0
for
x\not\in F_{\epsilon}
. Observe that
f_{F,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
if
F
is a bounded subset of
S
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. If
F\subset S
is closed and bounded, then

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\left\langle f_{F,\epsilon},\mu_{t}\right\rangle=\left\langle f_{F,\epsilon},\mu\right\rangle\leq\mu(F_{\epsilon}),

(2.28)

and, in view of the fact that
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu(F_{\epsilon})=\mu(F)
, we obtain the desired inequality.
Now, let us take an arbitrary closed set
F\subset S
. Define a closed ball
B
such that
\mu(B)\geq 1-\epsilon
. Then,

1-\epsilon\leq\left\langle f_{B,\epsilon},\mu\right\rangle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\langle f_{B,\epsilon},\mu_{t}\right\rangle\leq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(B_{\epsilon}),

which, due to (
2.28
), leads to

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F\cap B_{\epsilon})+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F\cap B^{\prime}_{\epsilon})\leq\mu(F\cap B_{\epsilon})+\epsilon.

Eventually, we get
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mu_{t}(F)\leq\mu(F)+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
.
∎

A regular Markov semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
which is stochastically continuous at zero is
right stochastically continuous
, that is,
\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{+}}P(t)f(x)=P(t_{0})f(x)

for all
x\in S
, all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and all
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
. If additionally
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the
e-property
in
\mathcal{L}_{b}(S)
, we conclude that it is stochastically continuous
[
25
, Proposition 3.6.6]
. In the following lemma we show that the same conclusion can be achieved if
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
has the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
be a regular Markov semigroup which is stochastically continuous at zero and which has the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
. Then
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is stochastically continuous.

Proof.

Let
z\in S
,
\epsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
. Since
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is assumed to have the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
, there is
B(z,r)
with some
r>0
such that
\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\rvert\leq\epsilon
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x\in B(z,r)
. Additionally, by the stochastic continuity at zero, we have
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\delta_{z}P(t)=\delta_{z}
, an thus, by the Portmanteau theorem, there exists
\tau>0
such that
\delta_{z}P(t)(B(z,r))>1-\epsilon
for every
t\leq\tau
. Therefore, for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
\tau\geq s-t\geq 0
, we obtain

\begin{split}\lvert P(s)f(z)-P(t)f(z)\rvert=&\lvert\left\langle P(t)f,\delta_{z}P(s-t)\right\rangle-P(t)f(z)\rvert\\
\leq&\left\lvert\int_{B(z,r)}P(t)f(x)\delta_{z}P(s-t)(dx)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert+\|f\|_{\infty}\epsilon\\
\leq&\int_{B(z,r)}\left\lvert P(t)f(x)-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert\delta_{z}P(s-t)(dx)\\
+&\left\lvert P(t)f(z)\delta_{z}P(s-t)(B(z,r))-P(t)f(z)\right\rvert+\|f\|_{\infty}\epsilon\\
\leq&\epsilon+2\|f\|_{\infty}\epsilon.\end{split}

(2.29)

Eventually, by Lemma
2.13
, the function
t\mapsto P(t)f(z)
is continuous for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.
∎

Theorem 2.15
.

A regular stochastically continuous at zero Markov semigroup
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
that is asymptotically stable and has the e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
possesses also the e-property in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
.

Proof.

Assume, by the contradiction, that there is
z\in S
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(S)
,
\epsilon>0
,
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset S
converging to
z
such that

\lvert P(t_{n})f(x_{n})-P(t_{n})f(z)\rvert>\epsilon.

(2.30)

If the sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, let us take its subsequence
\{t_{k_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. As
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is stochastically continuous at zero and has the
e-property
in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
, by Lemma
2.14
, it is also stochastically continuous. Hence, we get
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{z}P(t_{k_{n}})=\delta_{z}P(t_{0})
. Otherwise, let us take a subsequence
\{t_{k_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that is divergent to
\infty
. As
\{P(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is asymptotically stable (with the unique invariant probability measure
\mu_{*}
) we get
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{z}P(t_{k_{n}})=\mu_{*}
.

Let us take an arbitrary
g\in\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
, and let us denote by
\mu
either
\delta_{z}P(t_{0})
or
\mu_{*}
(depending on the case that we consider). We have

\left\lvert P\left(t_{k_{n}}\right)g\left(x_{k_{n}}\right)-\left\langle g,\mu\right\rangle\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert P\left(t_{k_{n}}\right)g\left(x_{k_{n}}\right)-P\left(t_{k_{n}}\right)g(z)\right\rvert+\left\lvert P\left(t_{k_{n}}\right)g(z)-\left\langle g,\mu\right\rangle\right\rvert.

(2.31)

By the assumed e-property in
\mathcal{L}_{bs}(S)
, the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.31
) vanishes. The second one vanishes as well, by the definition of
\mu
. As a consequence, referring to Lemma
2.13
, we get
\text{w}\,\text{-}\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{x_{n_{k}}}P(t_{n_{k}})=\mu
, which contradicts (
2.30
).
∎

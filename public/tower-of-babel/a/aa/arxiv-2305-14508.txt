A Spinorial Hopf Differential for Associative Submanifolds
Gavin Ball, Jesse Madnick

Abstract
Given a CMC surface in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , its traceless second fundamental form can be viewed as a holomorphic section called the Hopf differential. By analogy, we show that for an associative submanifold of a 7-manifold M 7 M^{7} with G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structure, its traceless second fundamental form can be viewed as a twisted spinor. Moreover, if M M is ℝ 7 \mathbb{R}^{7} , T 7 T^{7} , or S 7 S^{7} with the standard G 2 \mathrm{G}_{2} -structure, then this twisted spinor is harmonic. Consequently, every non-totally-geodesic associative 3 3 -fold in ℝ 7 \mathbb{R}^{7} , T 7 T^{7} , and S 7 S^{7} admits non-vanishing harmonic twisted spinors. Analogous results hold for special Lagrangians in ℝ 6 \mathbb{R}^{6} and T 6 T^{6} , coassociative 4 4 -folds in ℝ 7 \mathbb{R}^{7} and T 7 T^{7} , and Cayley 4 4 -folds in ℝ 8 \mathbb{R}^{8} and T 8 T^{8} .



1
Introduction

In minimal surface theory, Hopf’s Theorem states that every immersed constant mean curvature (CMC)
2
-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is congruent to the round sphere. Indeed, for any immersed orientable surface
u\colon\Sigma^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, its second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}
can be split into traceless and trace parts as follows:

\mathrm{I\!I}=\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}+H\,\mathrm{Id}.

(1.1)

Viewing
\Sigma
as a Riemann surface, the complexification
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a smooth section of
\Lambda^{1,0}(\Sigma)\otimes\Lambda^{1,0}(\Sigma)
called the
Hopf differential
, and is a holomorphic section if and only if
H
is constant. Consequently, if
u\colon\mathbb{CP}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an immersed CMC
2
-sphere, then
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic section of
\Lambda^{1,0}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})^{\otimes 2}
, a bundle which admits no non-vanishing holomorphic sections, so that
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}=0
, implying that
u\colon\mathbb{CP}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is congruent to the round sphere.

Generalizations and analogues of the Hopf differential have since been discovered in various other settings. For example, Abresh-Rosenberg
[
1
]
introduced a holomorphic quadratic form for CMC surfaces in
S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and
H^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. As another example, Chern-Wolfson
[
5
]
and Eells-Wood
[
6
]
independently found a holomorphic cubic form for minimal surfaces in
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
and
\mathbb{HP}^{n}
.

In this note, we consider whether there exists a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-geometric analogue of the above ideas. That is, suppose
(M^{7},\varphi)
is a
7
-manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi
. Given an associative submanifold
\Sigma^{3}\subset M^{7}
, can its complexified traceless second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
be viewed as a Hopf differential?

On a
7
-manifold
M
, we recall that a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
is a
3
-form
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
with the property that at each
x\in M
, the symmetric bilinear form
B_{\varphi}\colon\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T_{x}^{*}M)\to\Lambda^{7}(T_{x}^{*}M)
given by
B_{\varphi}(v,w)=(\iota_{v}\varphi)\wedge(\iota_{w}\varphi)\wedge\varphi
is definite. It is well-known that
M
admits a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure if and only if
M
is orientable and spinnable. An
associative submanifold
of
(M,\varphi)
is an oriented
3
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma^{3}\subset M^{7}
satisfying the first-order PDE

\left.\varphi\right|_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}.

If
\varphi
is
closed
(i.e.,
d\varphi=0
) or
nearly-parallel
(i.e.,
d\varphi=\lambda\ast\!\varphi
for some constant
\lambda\neq 0
), then it is well-known that every associative
3
-fold in
(M,\varphi)
is a minimal submanifold (i.e.,
\mathrm{I\!I}=\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}
). The converse was proven in
[
4
]
.

Taken literally, of course, the idea that
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
could be a holomorphic section is absurd. Indeed, since associative submanifolds are
3
-dimensional, they cannot be complex manifolds, and so one cannot speak of holomorphic vector bundles over
\Sigma
. However, we will show that
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies an analogue of the Cauchy-Riemann equations, namely the Dirac equation.

In fact, there are two well-known relationships between associative submanifolds, on the one hand, and the Dirac equation, on the other. First, suppose that
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
is the graph of a smooth function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Identifying
\mathbb{R}^{4}\simeq\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}\simeq\text{Im}(\mathbb{H})
with the quaternions and imaginary quaternions, Harvey and Lawson
[
9
,
\lx@sectionsign
IV.2.A.]
proved that
\Sigma
is associative if and only if
f
satisfies the PDE

\not{D}f=-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\times\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}\times\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{3}},

where
\not{D}f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}i+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}j+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{3}}k
is the Dirac operator, and where
a\times b\times c=\star(a\wedge b\wedge c)
is the standard triple cross product on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Second, suppose
(M^{7},\varphi)
is a
7
-manifold with a closed
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, and let
\Sigma\subset M
denote a compact associative submanifold (without boundary). McLean proved
[
15
]
that under the natural isomorphism between the complexified normal bundle of
\Sigma
and a certain twisted spinor bundle

N\Sigma\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\not{S}(T\Sigma)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{V},

the infinitesimal associative deformations of
\Sigma
correspond to those twisted spinors in the kernel of a Dirac operator
\not{D}
. Analogues and extensions of McLean’s Theorem are established, for example, in
[
2
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
16
]
. A related result is McLean’s second variation of volume formula. To recall it, suppose now that
\varphi
is both closed and co-closed. Then for any compactly-supported variation
(\Sigma_{t})_{t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}
of an associative submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
, letting
\eta\in\Gamma(N\Sigma)
denote the variation vector field, we have

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}\text{Vol}(\Sigma_{t})=\int_{\Sigma}\|\not{D}\eta\|^{2}\,\mathrm{vol},

which again features the Dirac operator
\not{D}
on the normal bundle. This formula has been further studied in, for example,
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
.

Our main result establishes yet another link between associative geometry and spin geometry:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a
7
-manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. Let
\Sigma^{3}\subset M^{7}
be an associative submanifold equipped with a choice of spin structure, let
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)
denote the traceless part of its second fundamental form, and let
\mathrm{I\!I}^{\mathbb{C}}_{0}
be its complexification.

1.

There exists a complex vector bundle
\mathsf{E}\to\Sigma
such that

(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\not{S}(T\Sigma)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{E}.

Consequently,
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
can be viewed as a twisted spinor.

2.

If
M
has constant curvature and
\varphi
is torsion-free or nearly parallel (e.g., if
M=\mathbb{R}^{7}
,
T^{7}
, or
S^{7}
with their standard
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures), then
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a harmonic twisted spinor:

\not{D}\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}=0.

Here,
\not{D}
is the Dirac operator on
\not{S}(T\Sigma)\otimes\mathsf{E}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{7}
,
T^{7}
or
S^{7}
with its standard
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. If
\Sigma\subset M
is an associative submanifold that is not totally geodesic, then
\Sigma
admits a non-vanishing
\mathsf{E}
-twisted harmonic spinor for every spin structure on
\Sigma
.

Corollary
1.2
is remarkable in view of the scarcity of harmonic twisted spinors on compact Riemannian
3
-manifolds
(\Sigma^{3},g)
. Indeed, since the elliptic operator
\not{D}
has Fredholm index 0, the space
\mathrm{Ker}(\not{D})
is generically zero-dimensional. Furthermore, note that a Dirac operator
\not{D}
on a twisted spinor bundle
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}\to\Sigma
depends on the data
(g,\mathfrak{s},h^{\mathsf{E}},\nabla^{\mathsf{E}})
, where
(g,\mathfrak{s})
are the metric and spin structure on
\Sigma
, and where
(h^{\mathsf{E}},\nabla^{\mathsf{E}})
are the Hermitian structure and connection on
\mathsf{E}
, respectively. Angel
[
3
]
and Maier
[
14
]
have shown that for generic choices of
\nabla^{\mathsf{E}}
, the Dirac operator
\not{D}
admits no non-trivial harmonic twisted spinors at all.

Our method also yields analogues of Theorem
1.1
for other calibrated geometries, provided certain modifications are made. For example, if
L^{4}\subset Z^{8}
is a Cayley
4
-fold in an
8
-manifold
(Z^{8},\Phi)
with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure
\Phi\in\Omega^{4}(Z)
, then the bundle
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}L)\otimes NL)^{\mathbb{C}}
can be shown to be isomorphic to a twisted
half
-spinor bundle
\not{S}^{+}\otimes\mathsf{E}
. Moreover, if
\Phi
is flat, then
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a harmonic twisted half-spinor.

In general, further subtleties may arise. For instance, suppose that
L^{4}
is a coassociative
4
-fold in a
7
-manifold
(M^{7},\varphi)
with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure. Then
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}L)\otimes NL)^{\mathbb{C}}
is not isomorphic to a twisted spinor or half-spinor bundle. Nevertheless, there exists a
subbundle
of
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}L)\otimes NL)^{\mathbb{C}}
isomorphic to a twisted half-spinor bundle, say
\not{S}^{+}\otimes\mathsf{E}
. If the
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
M
is torsion-free, then
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a section of that subbundle. Moreover, if
\varphi
is flat, then
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a harmonic twisted spinor.

2
Proof of the Main Result

Let
(M^{7},\varphi)
be a
7
-manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(M)
, let
g_{M}
denote the induced Riemannian metric on
M
, and let
\times\colon\Lambda^{2}(TM)\to TM
denote the vector cross product, i.e.:

\varphi(u,v,w)=g_{M}(u\times v,w).

Let
u\colon\Sigma^{3}\to M^{7}
be an associative submanifold, and let
\mathrm{I\!I}
be its second fundamental form. Decomposing
\mathrm{I\!I}=\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}+H\,\text{Id}
into traceless and trace parts as in (
1.1
), the tensor
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}
is a trace-free
N\Sigma
-valued quadratic form on
\Sigma
:

\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma).

Here, we are using
N\Sigma
to denote the normal bundle of
\Sigma
, and
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)
to denote the vector bundle of trace-free quadratic forms on
\Sigma
.

A oriented orthonormal frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{7})
of
u^{*}(TM)\to\Sigma
will be called an
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-frame
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{ijk}

\displaystyle\text{span}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})

\displaystyle=T\Sigma

\displaystyle\text{span}(e_{4},e_{5},e_{5},e_{7})

\displaystyle=N\Sigma,

where here
\epsilon_{ijk}\in\{-1,0,1\}
is the unique symbol that is antisymmetric in all three indices and satisfies

\displaystyle\epsilon_{123}=\epsilon_{145}=\epsilon_{167}=\epsilon_{246}=\epsilon_{275}=\epsilon_{374}=\epsilon_{365}=1

\displaystyle\epsilon_{ijk}=0\ \text{ if }(i,j,k)\text{ is not a permutation of }(1,2,3),\,(1,4,5),\,\ldots,\,(3,6,5).

We let
F\to\Sigma
denote the principal
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-bundle whose fiber over
x\in\Sigma
consists of the
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-frames at
x
.

Since
\Sigma
is an orientable
3
-manifold, it admits spin structures. Once and for all, we choose a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
\Sigma
. This choice determines a principal
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-bundle
P_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\Sigma
whose total space double covers
F
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\mathfrak{s}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SO}(4)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma}

In
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
, we will show that the complexification of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma
can be identified with a twisted spinor bundle
\not{S}(T\Sigma)\otimes\mathsf{E}
, thereby proving Theorem
1.1
(a). Geometrically,
\mathsf{E}
is a vector bundle associated to the principal bundle
P_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\Sigma
via a particular
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-representation. As such, we need some facts about the representation theories of
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
and
\mathrm{SO}(4)
, to which we now turn.

2.1
Representation Theory of
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
and
\mathrm{SO}(4)

We now recall some basic facts about the representation theory of
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
and
\mathrm{SO}(4)
. First, recall that for each integer
p\geq 0
, the complex vector space
\mathrm{Sym}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{p+1}
is an
\mathrm{SU}(2)
-representation in the following way: for
A\in\mathrm{SU}(2)
and
f\in\mathrm{Sym}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, we define

(A\cdot f)(\mathbf{z}):=f(A^{T}\mathbf{z}).

Therefore, since
\mathrm{Spin}(4)\cong\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
, we have complex
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-representations

U_{p,q}:=\mathrm{Sym}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Sym}^{q}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{(p+1)(q+1)}

for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p,q\geq 0
. It is well-known that each
U_{p,q}
is irreducible, and conversely that every complex irreducible
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-representation is of this form. For future use, we note that the Clebsch-Gordan formula yields the irreducible decompositions

\displaystyle U_{0,1}\otimes U_{p,q+1}

\displaystyle\cong U_{p,q+2}\oplus U_{p,q}

(2.1)

\displaystyle U_{0,1}\otimes U_{p,0}

\displaystyle\cong U_{p,1}

(2.2)

Next, recall that
\mathrm{SO}(4)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(4)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. One can check that the
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-action on
U_{p,q}
descends to an
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-action if and only if
p\equiv q\,(\text{mod }2)
. Moreover, in this case, the complex representation
U_{p,q}
has a natural real structure
c\colon U_{p,q}\to U_{p,q}
, and we may consider its
+1
-eigenspace:

W_{p,q}=\left\{f\in U_{p,q}\colon c(f)=f\right\}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)(q+1)}

so that
W_{p,q}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong U_{p,q}
. It is well-known that each
W_{p,q}
is a real irreducible
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-module. Conversely, every real irreducible
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-module is of the form
W_{p,q}
for some non-negative
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p\equiv q\,(\text{mod }2)
.

2.2
The Second Fundamental Form as a Twisted Spinor

We now use the above
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
- and
\mathrm{SO}(4)
-representations to construct two families of vector bundles over
\Sigma
. First, for any
p,q\geq 0
, we consider the associated complex vector bundle

\mathsf{E}_{p,q}:=P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}U_{p,q}.

Second, if
p\equiv q\ (\text{mod }2)
, we may also consider the associated
real
vector bundle

\mathsf{V}_{p,q}:=F\times_{\mu}W_{p,q}.

We reiterate that
\mathsf{V}_{p,q}
is only defined when
p\equiv q\ (\text{mod }2)
. In this case, we have

\mathsf{E}_{p,q}\cong\mathsf{V}_{p,q}^{\mathbb{C}}

Many vector bundles fundamental to the geometry of associatives
\Sigma\subset M
are, in fact, of the form
\mathsf{E}_{p,q}
or
\mathsf{V}_{p,q}
. For example, the complex spinor bundle, the tangent bundle, and the normal bundle of
\Sigma
are, respectively:

\displaystyle\not{S}:=\not{S}(T\Sigma)

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}

\displaystyle T\Sigma

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{V}_{0,2}

\displaystyle N\Sigma

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{V}_{1,1}.

Moreover, the bundle of trace-free quadratic forms with values in the normal bundle is:

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma\cong\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,3}.

Remark 2.1
.

If
\varphi
is torsion-free or nearly-parallel, then the second fundamental form of an associative
\Sigma\subset M
satisfies the symmetry

\mathrm{I\!I}(u\times v,w)=u\times\mathrm{I\!I}(v,w)+\mathrm{I\!I}(u,w)\times v.

Consequently, the traceless second fundamental form
\mathrm{I\!I}_{0}
is a section of the rank
12
subbundle
\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma
. For the time being, we do not assume that
\varphi
is torsion-free nor nearly-parallel.

In
[
15
]
, McLean observed that the complexified normal bundle of
\Sigma
is isomorphic to a twisted spinor bundle. To see this, we simply notice that

N\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathsf{V}_{1,1}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathsf{E}_{1,1}\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,0}\cong\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,0}.

In general, given a complex vector bundle over
\Sigma
, we wish to know whether it is isomorphic to a twisted spinor bundle. The following lemma addresses this:

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
p,q\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{p,q+1}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{p,q+2}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{p,q}

\displaystyle\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{p,0}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{p,1}.

Proof.

We calculate, using (
2.1
), that

\displaystyle\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{p,q+1}\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{p,q+1}

\displaystyle=(P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}U_{0,1})\otimes(P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}U_{p,q+1})

\displaystyle\cong P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}(U_{0,1}\otimes U_{p,q+1})

\displaystyle\cong P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}(U_{p,q+2}\oplus U_{p,q})

\displaystyle\cong(P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}U_{p,q+2})\oplus(P_{\mathfrak{s}}\times_{\mu}U_{p,q})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{E}_{p,q+2}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{p,q}.

The other isomorphism is proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

As vector bundles with structure group
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
, we have an isomorphism

(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}.

Thus,
\mathrm{I\!I}^{\mathbb{C}}
can be viewed as a twisted spinor.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
with
(p,q)=(1,3)
, we see that

\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}\cong\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,3}\cong(\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,3})^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma\right)^{\mathbb{C}}.

∎

The isomorphism in the preceding corollary can be made explicit by tracing through the following alternative proof. We observe that

\displaystyle\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,0}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\not{S})

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,0}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(\mathsf{E}_{0,2})

\displaystyle\cong(N\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma))^{\mathbb{C}}.

Concretely, for
\sigma\in\not{S}
and
\xi\in\mathsf{E}_{1,0}
, we can view
\sigma\otimes\xi\in\mathsf{E}_{1,1}\cong N\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}
as a complex normal vector. Moreover, for spinors
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in\not{S}
, we can view
\rho_{1}\circ\rho_{2}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\not{S})\cong T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}
as a complex cotangent vector. Altogether, our isomorphism is described on decomposable elements as follows:

\displaystyle N\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\to\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle(\sigma\otimes\xi)\otimes((\rho_{1}\circ\rho_{2})\circ(\rho_{3}\circ\rho_{4}))

\displaystyle\mapsto\sigma\otimes(\xi\otimes\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{3}\rho_{4}).

Here, in writing
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{3}\rho_{4}
, we mean the result of applying the natural map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\not{S}))

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Sym}^{4}(\not{S})\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,4}

\displaystyle q_{1}\circ q_{2}

\displaystyle\mapsto q_{1}q_{2}

to the trace-free quadratic form
(\rho_{1}\circ\rho_{2})\circ(\rho_{3}\circ\rho_{4})
.

2.3
The Twisted Spinor Bundle

Having proved the first part of Theorem
1.1
(as Corollary
2.3
), we now turn to the second part. For this, we need to equip both
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
and
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
with suitable bundle metrics and connections. In this section, we consider
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
. In the following section, we examine
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
and draw conclusions.

2.3.1
Structure on
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

The vector bundle
\mathsf{E}_{1,4}\to\Sigma
is associated to the principal
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-bundle
P_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\Sigma
via the irreducible complex representation
\mu\colon\mathrm{Spin}(4)\to\mathrm{GL}(U_{1,4})
. On
U_{1,4}=\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
, we can exhibit a
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-invariant Hermitian inner product via the tensor product of those on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
. In fact, since
U_{1,4}
is an irreducible representation of the compact Lie group
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
, this Hermitian inner product is the unique
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-invariant one up to scale.

Thus, we obtain an induced Hermitian bundle metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
as follows. If we recast sections
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathsf{E}_{1,4})
as equivariant functions
q\colon P_{\mathfrak{s}}\to U_{1,4}
, say
q=q_{\bar{i}}e_{i}
for some unitary basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{10})
of
U_{1,4}
, then

\langle\tau,\tau\rangle=q_{\bar{i}}\overline{q_{\bar{i}}}.

Next, recall that the principal bundle
P_{\mathfrak{s}}\to\Sigma
has a natural connection, namely the spin connection
\psi\in\Omega^{1}(P_{\mathfrak{s}};\mathfrak{spin}(4))
. Therefore, if
\tau\in\Gamma(\mathsf{E}_{1,4})
is a local section, say
\tau=\tau_{i}\epsilon_{i}
for some (local) functions
\tau_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and local frame
(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{10})
of
\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
, then we may define the covariant derivative

\nabla^{\mathsf{E}}\colon\Gamma(\mathsf{E}_{1,4})\to\Gamma(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})

via

\nabla^{\mathsf{E}}\tau=d\tau_{i}\otimes\epsilon_{i}+\tau_{i}\,\mu_{*}(\psi)_{ij}\otimes\epsilon_{j},

where
\mu_{*}\colon\mathfrak{spin}(4)\to\mathfrak{u}(U_{1,4})
is the
\mathfrak{spin}(4)
-representation on
U_{1,4}
.

Now, having equipped
\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
with a Hermitian bundle metric and connection
\nabla^{\mathsf{E}}
as above, a completely analogous procedure endows the spinor bundle
\not{S}\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,1}
with a Hermitian bundle metric and connection
\nabla^{\not{S}}
. Finally, we may equip
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
with its tensor product metric

\langle\sigma_{1}\otimes\tau_{1},\sigma_{2}\otimes\tau_{2}\rangle:=\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle\langle\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\rangle,

and tensor product connection

\displaystyle\nabla\colon\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})

\displaystyle\to\Gamma((T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})

\displaystyle\nabla(\sigma\otimes\tau)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{\not{S}}\sigma)\otimes\tau+\sigma\otimes\nabla^{\mathsf{E}}\tau.

2.3.2
The Dirac Operator on
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

Let us now recall some relevant definitions, following
[
11
,
\lx@sectionsign
II.5]
and
[
17
,
\lx@sectionsign
12.1]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
E\to(\Sigma,g)
be a complex vector bundle over a Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,g)
with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
. A
Dirac structure on
E
is a triple
(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\nabla,\mathrm{cl})
consisting of a Hermitian bundle metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, a connection
\nabla
, and a
\mathrm{Cl}(T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
-module structure on
E
, denoted

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}\colon T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes E

\displaystyle\to E

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(\alpha\otimes\sigma)

\displaystyle=\alpha\cdot\sigma,

that are pairwise compatible in the following sense:

1.

\nabla
is
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
-compatible.

2.

\langle\alpha\cdot\sigma_{1},\alpha\cdot\sigma_{2}\rangle=|\alpha|^{2}\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle
for all
\alpha\in T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\sigma\in E
.

3.

\nabla(\alpha\cdot\sigma)=(\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}\alpha)\cdot\sigma+\alpha\cdot\nabla\sigma
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma(T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}})
and
\sigma\in\Gamma(E)
.

A
Dirac bundle
is a vector bundle
E\to\Sigma
equipped with a Dirac structure. In this case, its
Dirac operator
is the composition

\not{D}=\mathrm{cl}\circ\nabla\colon\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E).

Diagrammatically,

\not{D}\colon\Gamma(E)\xrightarrow{\nabla}\Gamma(T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes E)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{cl}}\Gamma(E).

We now equip
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
with its standard Dirac structure. For this, recall that
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
already carries the tensor product metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and tensor product connection
\nabla
. Now, for
\alpha\in T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}
,
\sigma\in\not{S}
, and
\tau\in\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
, we define

\displaystyle\text{cl}\colon T^{*}\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})

\displaystyle\to\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\alpha\cdot(\sigma\otimes\tau)

\displaystyle:=(\alpha\cdot\sigma)\otimes\tau.

It is then a standard fact
[
11
,
\lx@sectionsign
II.5]
,
[
17
, p. 643]
that the triple
(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\nabla,\mathrm{cl})
is a Dirac structure on
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
. In particular,
\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}
admits a Dirac operator

\not{D}\colon\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})\to\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}).

2.3.3
A Condition for Harmonicity

We now give a sufficient condition for a twisted spinor
\gamma\in\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})
to satisfy
\not{D}\gamma=0
. For this, we observe that the isomorphism

\displaystyle(T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{0,2}\otimes(\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,3})

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{1,7}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,3}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,1}

allows us to regard the Dirac operator as a composition

\not{D}\colon\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})\xrightarrow{\nabla}\Gamma(\mathsf{E}_{1,7}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,3}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,1})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{cl}}\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}).

The equivariance of
\mathrm{cl}
, together with Schur’s Lemma, now yields the following:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\gamma\in\Gamma(\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4})
be a twisted spinor. If
\nabla\gamma\in\Gamma(\mathsf{E}_{1,7}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,1})
, then
\not{D}\gamma=0
.

Proof.

Consider the bundle map given by the Clifford action

\mathrm{cl}\colon(T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}\to\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4},

and recall the isomorphisms

\displaystyle(T^{*}\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{1,7}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus 2\mathsf{E}_{1,3}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,1}

\displaystyle\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

\displaystyle\cong\mathsf{E}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,3}.

The map
\mathrm{cl}
is
\mathrm{Spin}(4)
-equivariant. Therefore, by Schur’s Lemma, if
\beta\in\mathsf{E}_{1,7}\oplus\mathsf{E}_{1,1}
, then
\mathrm{cl}(\beta)=0
. Taking
\beta=\nabla\gamma
proves the result.
∎

2.4
Proof of Theorem
1.1
(b)

We now return to the bundle
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
. As both
T\Sigma
and
N\Sigma
carry natural bundle metrics, the real vector bundle
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma
inherits a natural bundle metric. Extending by sesquilinearity yields a Hermitian bundle metric on
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
. The isomorphism

F\colon(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\not{S}\otimes\mathsf{E}_{1,4}

of Corollary
2.3
is then a Hermitian bundle isometry.

Now, the Levi-Civita connection on
M
induces tangential and normal connections on
T\Sigma
and
N\Sigma
, which in turn induce a connection on
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma
. After extending by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity, we let
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}
denote the resulting connection on
(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}}
. One can check that the isomorphism
F
also intertwines the connections, in the sense that for any
\sigma\in\Gamma((\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes N\Sigma)^{\mathbb{C}})
,

(\mathrm{Id}\otimes F)(\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}\sigma)=\nabla(F(\sigma)).

As such, we will suppress
F
from the notation. We are now in a position to prove:

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
M
has constant sectional curvature and
\varphi
is torsion-free or nearly parallel. Then
\not{D}(\mathrm{I\!I}^{\mathbb{C}})=0
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.1
,
\mathrm{I\!I}
is a section of
\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\subset N\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)
. Therefore,
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}(\mathrm{I\!I})
is a section of the subbundle
\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\otimes T^{*}\Sigma\subset N\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{2}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)\otimes T^{*}\Sigma.
Abstractly, this subbundle is isomorphic to
\mathsf{V}_{1,7}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\oplus\mathsf{V}_{1,3}.
On the other hand, since
M
has constant sectional curvature, the Codazzi equation implies that
\nabla^{\mathrm{LC}}(\mathrm{I\!I})
is a section of
N\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{3}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma)
. Direct calculation yields

(\mathsf{V}_{1,5}\otimes T^{*}\Sigma)\cap(N\Sigma\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{3}_{0}(T^{*}\Sigma))\cong\mathsf{V}_{1,7},

where the intersection is taken inside
N\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma\otimes T^{*}\Sigma.
Therefore, the complexification
\nabla(\mathrm{I\!I}^{\mathbb{C}})
is section of
\mathsf{E}_{1,7}
. Applying Proposition
2.5
, we conclude that
\not{D}(\mathrm{I\!I}^{\mathbb{C}})=0
.
∎

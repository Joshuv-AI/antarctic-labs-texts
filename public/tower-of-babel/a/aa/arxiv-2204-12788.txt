MCRB-based Performance Analysis of 6G Localization under Hardware Impairments
Hui Chen 1, Sina Rezaei Aghdam 1, Musa Furkan Keskin 1, Yibo Wu 1 3, Simon Lindberg 2

Abstract
Location information is expected to be the key to meeting the needs of communication and context-aware services in 6G systems. User localization is achieved based on delay and/or angle estimation using uplink or downlink pilot signals. However, hardware impairments (HWIs) distort the signals at both the transmitter and receiver sides and thus affect the localization performance. While this impact can be ignored at lower frequencies where HWIs are less severe, modeling and analysis efforts are needed for 6G to evaluate the localization degradation due to HWIs. In this work, we model various types of impairments and conduct a misspecified Cramér-Rao bound analysis to evaluate the HWI-induced performance loss. Simulation results with different types of HWIs show that each HWI leads to a different level of degradation in angle and delay estimation performance.

The presence of hardware impairments such as phase noise, carrier frequency offset, mutual coupling, power amplifier nonlinearity, in-phase and quadrature imbalance, distort the pilot signals. As a result, when algorithms derived from a mismatched model (i.e., without or with limited information about the hardware impairments), the localization performance is unavoidably affected.

III
Localization Algorithm

The maximum likelihood estimation is employed when the observation
{\bf y}
is generated from the same model used by the algorithm. On the other hand, the mismatched maximum likelihood estimation is used when the observation
{\bf y}
is generated from a different model than what is used by the algorithm. In the latter case, we will denote the generative model by
true model
, while the model used by the estimator is called the
mismatched model
.

III-A

MLE

If
{\bf y}\sim f_{\text{TM}}({\bf y}|\alpha,{\bf p})
,
the maximum likelihood estimation of the UE position and channel gain is

\displaystyle[\hat{\bf p}_{\text{MLE}},\hat{\alpha}_{\text{MLE}}]

\displaystyle={\hbox{arg}}\max_{{\bf p},\alpha}\ln f_{\text{TM}}({\bf y}|\alpha,{\bf p}),

(10)

where
\ln f_{\text{TM}}({\bf y}|\alpha,{\bf p})
is the log-likelihood of the true model.
Since
\alpha
appears linearly in the noise-free observation, we can use a plug-in estimate, and solve for the position by a coarse grid search to find an initial estimate
{\bf p}_{0}
, followed by a backtracking line search
[
26
]
. For instance, when
\text{TM}=\textbf{M1}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\bf p}_{\text{MLE}}

\displaystyle={\hbox{arg}}\min_{\bf p}\left\|{\bf y}-\frac{\bar{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}({\bf p})^{\mathsf{H}}{\bf y}}{\|\bar{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}({\bf p})\|^{2}}\bar{\hbox{\boldmath$\eta$}}({\bf p})\right\|^{2}.

(11)

III-B

MMLE

If
{\bf y}\sim f_{\text{TM}}({\bf y}|\alpha,{\bf p})

\displaystyle[\hat{\bf p}_{\text{MMLE}},\hat{\alpha}_{\text{MMLE}}]

\displaystyle={\hbox{arg}}\max_{{\bf p},\alpha}\ln f_{\text{MM}}({\bf y}|\alpha,{\bf p}),

\displaystyle\hat{\bf p}_{\text{MMLE}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}\sum^{{\cal G}}_{g=1}\sum^{K}_{k=1}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\left(\frac{\partial\mu_{g,k}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\right)^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\frac{\partial\mu_{g,k}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{\partial{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial{\bf s}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial\vartheta}{\partial p_{x}}&\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial p_{x}}&0&0\\

\displaystyle\mathrm{AEB}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{([\mathbf{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{-1}]_{1,1})},

(17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{DEB}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{([\mathbf{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{-1}]_{2,2})},

(18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{PEB}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{\hbox{tr}}([\mathrm{CRB}]_{1:2,1:2})},

V-C

The Effect of Individual Impairments

To gain a deeper understanding, we study the impact of hardware impairments on angle and delay estimation, considering different hardware impairments separately. The results are shown in Fig.
3
for (a) PN, (b) CFO, (c) MC, and (d) PAN.
Multiple realizations (multiple hardware realizations with a fixed pilot signal for (a)-(c) and multiple pilot signal realizations for (d)) are performed for each type of the HWI. We can see that different types of the hardware impairments affect angle and delay estimation differently. The PN and PAN affect both angle and delay estimation, however, the effect of PAN is mainly caused by the distortion of the pilot signals, whereas the effect of PN depends on the residual noise level
\sigma_{\text{PN}}
. The CFO and MC have a more significant effect on angle estimation compared to delay estimation since the CFO affects the phase changes across beams and the MC distorts the steering vector.
In addition, within reasonable levels of hardware impairments, the CRBs with perfect knowledge of the impairments (CRB-M1) are close to the CRBs of the mismatched model (CRB-M2).

In Fig.

) as a function of the standard deviation of PN
\sigma_{\text{PN}}
(similar patterns can be seen for CFO and MC) for various values of average transmission power using 100 realizations of PN.
The impairments produce a larger perturbation in the high SNR scenario. As for low SNR, the noise level is high enough and the impairments, under a certain level, will not degrade the PEB too much.

VI
Conclusion

hardware impairments present a crucial roadblock to achieving high performance in
radio-based localization. We modeled different types of hardware impairments and utilize the misspecified Cramér-Rao bound to evaluate the error caused by model-mismatch. The effects of residual PN, residual CFO, residual MC and PAN on angle/delay estimation are evaluated. We found that PN and PAN affect both angle and delay estimation, whereas CFO and MC have a more significant effect on angle estimation.
We also observed that with perfect knowledge of the hardware impairments, the bound is close to the bound of the mismatched model, but will saturate at a certain level in the high SNR regime due to the PAN. In conclusion, dedicated pilot signal design, HWIs estimation and mitigation algorithms are needed for accurate localization in 6G systems.

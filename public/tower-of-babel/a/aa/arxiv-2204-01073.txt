Relatively hyperbolic metric bundles and Cannon-Thurston map
Swathi Krishna

Abstract
. In [ KS20 ] , the authors define the notion of morphisms and pullbacks of metric (graph) bundles. Moreover, they show the existence of the Cannon-Thurston (CT) map for a pullback. In this paper, we prove a combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic metric (graph) bundles and also prove a relatively hyperbolic analogue of the result in [ KS20 ] . Further, we derive some applications of these results.



1.
Introduction

In a seminal work
[
CT85
]
, Cannon and Thurston proved that if
M
is a closed hyperbolic
3
-manifold fibering over the circle with fiber
F
, and
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{M}
denote the universal covers of
F
and
M
respectively, then the inclusion map
\tilde{i}:\widetilde{F}\to\widetilde{M}
extends to a continuous map in the boundary.
This was generalized to the context of geometric group theory by Mahan Mj. Let
X
be a hyperbolic metric space and
G
be a hyperbolic group acting on
X
properly discontinuously and freely by isometries. Let
\Gamma
denote the Cayley graph of
G
and
i:G\to X
be the natural orbit map. Let
\partial{X}
(resp.
\partial{\Gamma}
) denote the Gromov boundary and
\widehat{X}
(resp.
\widehat{\Gamma}
) denote the Gromov compactification of
X
(resp.
\Gamma
). Then he asked the following:

Question 1.1
.

Does
i:\Gamma\to X
extend continuously to
\widehat{i}:\widehat{\Gamma}\to\widehat{X}
?

This was answered in negative by Baker and Riley in
[
BR13
]
(also see
[
MO14
]
). Over the time, existence of Cannon-Thurston map has been proved for many special cases, in hyperbolic as well as relatively hyperbolic setting (see
[
Mj19
]
for the history). Of particular interest to us is the existence of CT map for a hyperbolic group extension proved by Mitra in
[
Mit98a
]
. This was generalized by the introduction of metric (graph) bundles by Mj and Sardar in
[
MS12
]
. They proved a combination theorem for metric (graph) bundles with uniformly hyperbolic fibers, and also that the inclusion map of a fiber in the metric (graph) bundle admits CT.
This was further generalized in
[
KS20
]
using the notion of pullback of metric (graph) bundles.

In this paper, we prove a combination theorem for relatively hyperbolic metric (graph) bundles and the existence of CT map for pullbacks in this case. This also generalize the existence of CT map for relatively hyperbolic group extensions due to Pal in
[
Pal10
]
.
The following is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,B,\pi)
be a metric (graph) bundle satisfying the following.

(1)

(2)

Then

are elements of
\partial Y^{h}
identified under
\partial i_{Y,X}
. Then by Theorem
6.12
(1), there exists
z_{2}\in\partial F_{b}^{h}\setminus\{z_{1}\}
such that
\partial i_{Y}(z_{2})=z
. Therefore, we are done. ∎

We have the obvious corollary.

Corollary 6.15
.

Suppose
1\to(K,K_{1})\to(G,N_{G}(K_{1}))\to(Q,Q_{1})\to 1
is a short exact sequence as in Theorem
6.2
. Suppose
Q^{\prime}<Q
is qi embedded and
G_{1}=\pi^{-1}(Q^{\prime})
.
Then the CT map
\partial K^{h}\to\partial G_{1}^{h}
is surjective.

6.2.2.
Quasiconvexity

Let

1\to K\to G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 1

be a short exact sequence of (resp. relatively) hyperbolic groups. In
[
MR18
]
, the authors show that under certain conditions, a finitely generated infinite index subgroup of
K
is a (resp. relatively) quasiconvex subgroup of
G
. We recall the results here.

Theorem 6.16
.

[
MR18
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
1\to K\to G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 1
be a short exact sequence of hyperbolic groups, where
K
is a (closed) surface group or a free group and
Q
is convex cocompact in Teichmuller space or outer space respectively. Let
H<K
be a finitely generated infinite index subgroup of
K
. Then
H
is a quasiconvex subgroup in
G
.

Theorem 6.17
.

[
MR18
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
K=\pi_{1}(S^{h})
be the fundamental group of a surface with finitely many punctures and let
K_{1},\ldots,K_{n}
be its peripheral subgroups. Let
1\to K\to G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 1
and
1\to K_{i}\to N_{G}(K_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}Q\to 1
be the induced short exact sequences of groups. Suppose
Q
is a convex cocompact subgroup of the pure mapping class group of
S^{h}
. Let
K^{\prime}<K
be a finitely generated infinite index subgroup of
K
. Then
K^{\prime}
is a relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
.

Generalizing Theorem
6.16
, Mj and Sardar proved the following. We keep the setup of Theorem
6.16
.

Theorem 6.18
.

[
MSb
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
Q_{1}<Q
be a quasiconvex subgroup of
Q
and
G_{1}=\pi^{-1}(Q_{1})
. Let
H_{1}<G_{1}
be an infinite index quasiconvex subgroup of
G_{1}
. Then
H_{1}
is quasiconvex in
G
.

We prove a relatively hyperbolic analogue of this result. We first recall the following from
[
MSb
]
.

Proposition 6.19
.

[
MSb
, Proposition 2.8]

Let
G
be a finitely presented group,
H,K<G
, where
H
is finitely generated. Let
\Gamma=\Gamma(G,S)
and
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma(H,S_{1})
be the Cayley graphs, where
S,S_{1}
are finite generating sets of
G,H
respectively. Let
D>0
and
A\subset N_{D}(K)\cap H
be an infinite subset. Suppose there exists
r\geq 1
such that any pair of points in
A
can be joined by a path which lies in an
r
-neighbourhood of
A
in
\Gamma_{1}
. Then there exists a finitely generated infinite subgroup
K^{\prime}\subset H\cap K
such that
A/K^{\prime}
is finite. In particular,
A
is contained in a finite neighbourhood of
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
.

Convention 6.20
.

Let
G
be a group hyperbolic relative to a collection of subgroups and let
H
be a relatively hyperbolic subgroup such that the inclusion admits CT. We denote the CT lamination
\partial^{(2)}_{G^{h}}H^{h}
simply by
\partial^{(2)}_{G}H
.

Lemma 6.21
.

[
MR18
, Lemma 3.5]

Let
(G,{\mathcal{H}})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and
(H,{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})
be a relatively hyperbolic subgroup such that
(H,{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})\to(G,{\mathcal{H}})
admits CT. Then
H
is relatively quasiconvex in
G
if and only if
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H)=\emptyset
.

Lemma 6.22
.

[
Mit99
]

Let
(G,{\mathcal{H}})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and
(H_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{1})
and
(H_{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{2})
be relatively hyperbolic subgroups such that
H_{2}<H_{1}
and
(H_{2},{\mathcal{H}}_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}(H_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{1})
and
(H_{1},{\mathcal{H}}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\to}}(G,{\mathcal{H}})
admit CT map. Then
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H_{2})=\partial^{(2)}_{H_{1}}(H_{2})\cup\partial i^{-1}(\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H_{1}))
.

Theorem 6.23
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}=\{K_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},{\mathcal{H}}=\{N_{G}(K_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Let

1\to(K,{\mathcal{K}})\to(G,{\mathcal{H}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}(Q,Q)\to 1

be a short exact sequence of relatively hyperbolic groups. Let
Q_{1}<Q
be a quasiconvex subgroup of
Q
and let
\pi^{-1}(Q_{1})=G_{1}
. Suppose
H
is an infinite index relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G_{1}
. If
H
is not relatively quasiconvex in
G
, then there exists an infinite index relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
K
which is not relatively quasiconvex in
G
.

Proof.

Since
H
is not relatively quasiconvex in
G
, by Lemma
6.21
,
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H)\neq\emptyset
. Let
\gamma
be a leaf of
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H)
. Since
H
is relatively quasiconvex in
G_{1}
,
\gamma
is a leaf of
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(G_{1})
. By Theorem
6.12
, there exists some
D^{\prime}>0
such that
\gamma
lies in a
D^{\prime}
-neighbourhood of
\Gamma_{K}^{h}
, where
\Gamma_{K}
is the Cayley graph of
K
. Moreover,
\gamma(\pm\infty)
are non-parabolic limit points of
\partial K^{h}
as well as
\partial H^{h}
. This implies that there exists some
D>0
such that
\gamma\cap H
is an infinite subset lying in
N_{D}(K)\cap H
. By Proposition
6.19
, there exists a finitely generated infinite subgroup
K^{\prime}\subset H\cap K
and
R>0
such that
\gamma\cap H
lies in an
R
-neighbourhood of
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is a Cayley graph of
G
. This implies that
\gamma(\pm\infty)\in\Lambda_{H}(K^{\prime})
.

Claim 1:

[K:K^{\prime}]=\infty
. Suppose not. Then
\Lambda_{K}(K^{\prime})=\partial K
. Since
\Lambda_{G_{1}}(K)=\partial G_{1}
, we have that
\Lambda_{G_{1}}(K^{\prime})=\partial G_{1}
. Now, note that
\Lambda_{G_{1}}(K^{\prime})\subset\Lambda_{G_{1}}(H)
. This will imply that
\Lambda_{G_{1}}(H)=\partial G_{1}
, which is a contradiction.

Claim 2:

K^{\prime}
is not relatively quasiconvex in
G
. Suppose not. Then
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(K^{\prime})=\emptyset
and by Lemma
6.22
,
\partial^{(2)}_{H}(K^{\prime})=\emptyset
. Now, by Lemma
6.21
,
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H)\neq\emptyset
and for any leaf
\gamma
of
\partial^{(2)}_{G}(H)
,
(\gamma(-\infty),\gamma(\infty))\in\partial^{(2)}_{G}(K^{\prime})
. This is a contradiction. ∎

Suppose we have the set up of Theorem
6.17
. As a corollary of the above result, we have the following.

Theorem 6.24
.

Let
Q_{1}<Q
be a quasiconvex subgroup of
Q
and let
\pi^{-1}(Q_{1})=G_{1}
. Then any infinite index relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G_{1}
is a relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
.

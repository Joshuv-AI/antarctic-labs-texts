On the stability of type II blowup for the 1-corotational energy supercritical harmonic heat flow Thanks: August 9, 2026


Abstract
. We consider the energy supercritical harmonic heat flow from ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} into the d d -sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} with d ≥ 7 d\geq 7 . Under an additional assumption of 1-corotational symmetry, the problem reduces to the one dimensional semilinear heat equation ∂ t u = ∂ r 2 u + ( d − 1 ) r ​ ∂ r u − ( d − 1 ) 2 ​ r 2 ​ sin ⁡ ( 2 ​ u ) . \partial_{t}u=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{(d-1)}{r}\partial_{r}u-\frac{(d-1)}{2r^{2}}\sin(2u). 1.
Introduction.

We consider the harmonic map heat flow which is defined as the negative gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy of maps between manifolds. Indeed, if
\Phi
is a map from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,T)
to a compact riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with second fundamental form
\Upsilon
, then
\Phi
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\Phi-\Delta\Phi=\Upsilon(\Phi)(\nabla\Phi,\nabla\Phi),\\
\Phi(t=0)=\Phi_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

We assume that the target manifold is the
d
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Then, (
1.1
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\Phi-\Delta\Phi=|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\Phi,\\
\Phi(t=0)=\Phi_{0}.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

We will study the problem (
1.2
) under an additional assumption of 1-corotational symmetry, namely that a solution of (
1.2
) takes the form

\Phi(x,t)=\binom{\cos(u(|x|,t))}{\frac{x}{|x|}\sin(u(|x|,t))}.

(1.3)

Under this ansatz, the problem (
1.2
) reduces to the one dimensional semilinear heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}u&=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{(d-1)}{r}\partial_{r}u-\frac{(d-1)}{2r^{2}}\sin(2u),\\
u(t=0)&=u_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
u(t):r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\to u(r,t)\in[0,\pi]
. The set of solutions to (
1.4
) is invariant by the scaling symmetry

u_{\lambda}(r,t)=u\left(\frac{r}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda^{2}}\right),\quad\forall\lambda>0.

The energy associated to (
1.4
) is given by

\mathcal{E}[u](t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\partial_{r}u|^{2}+\frac{(d-1)}{r^{2}}\sin^{2}(u)\right)r^{d-1}dr,

(1.5)

which satisfies

\mathcal{E}[u_{\lambda}]=\lambda^{d-2}\mathcal{E}[u].

The criticality of the problem is reflected by the fact that the energy (
1.5
) is left invariant by the scaling property when
d=2
, hence, the case
d\geq 3
corresponds to the energy supercritical case.

The problem (
1.4
) is locally wellposed for data which are close in
L^{\infty}
to a uniformly continuous map (see Koch and Lamm
[
Koch and Lamm(2012)
]
) or in
BMO
by Wang
[
Wang(2011)
]
.
Actually, Eells and Sampson
[
Eells and Sampson(1964)
]
introduced the harmonic map heat flow as a process to deform any smooth map
\Phi_{0}
into a harmonic map via (
1.2
). They also proved that the solution exists globally if the sectional curvature of the target manifold is negative. There exist other assumptions for the global existence such as the image of the initial data
u_{0}
is contained in a ball of radius
\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\kappa}}
, where
\kappa
is an upper bound on the sectional curvature of the target manifold
\mathcal{M}
(see Jost
[
Jost(1981)
]
and Lin-Wang
[
Lin and Wang(2008)
]
).
Without these assumptions, the solution
u(r,t)
may develop singularities in some finite time (see for examples, Coron and Ghidaglia
[
Coron and Ghidaglia(1989)
]
, Chen and Ding
[
Chen and Ding(1990)
]
for
d\geq 3
, Chang, Ding and Yei
[
Chang et al.(1992)Chang, Ding, and Ye
]
for
d=2
). In this case, we say that
u(r,t)
blows up in a finite time
T<+\infty
in the sense that

\lim_{t\to T}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=+\infty.

Here we call
T
the blowup time of
u(x,t)
. The blowup has been divided by Struwe
[
Struwe(1996)
]
into two types:

\displaystyle\text{$u$ blows up with type I if:}\quad\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}<+\infty,

\displaystyle\text{$u$ blows up with type II if:}\quad\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=+\infty.

In
[
Struwe(1988)
]
, Struwe shows that the type I singularities are asymptotically self-similar, that is their profile is given by a smooth shrinking function

u(r,t)=\phi\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{T-t}}\right),\quad\forall t\in[0,T),

where
\phi
solves the equation

\phi^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{y}+\frac{y}{2}\right)\phi^{\prime}-\frac{d-1}{2y^{2}}\sin(2\phi)=0.

(1.6)

Thus, the study of Type I blowup reduces to the study of nonconstant solutions of equation (
1.6
).

When
3\leq d\leq 6
, by using a shooting method, Fan
[
Fan(1999)
]
proved that there exists an infinite sequence of globally regular solutions
\phi_{n}
of (
1.6
) which are called ”shrinkers (corresponding to the existence of Type I blowup solutions of (
1.4
)), where the integer index
n
denotes the number of intersections of the function
\phi_{n}
with
\frac{\pi}{2}
. More detailed quantitative properties of such solutions were studied by Biernat and Bizoń
[
Biernat and Bizoń(2011)
]
, where the authors conjectured that
\phi_{1}
is linear stable and provide numerical evidences supporting that
\phi_{1}
corresponds to a generic profile of Type I blow-up. Very recently, Biernat, Donninger and Schörkhuber
[
Biernat et al.(2016)Biernat, Donninger, and Schörkhuber
]
proved the existence of a stable self-similar blowup solution for
d=3
.
Since (
1.2
) is not time reversible, there exist another family of self-similar solutions that are called ”expanders” which were introduced by Germain and Rupflin in
[
Germain and Rupflin(2011)
]
. These ”expanders” have been lately proved to be nonlinearly stable by Germain, Ghoul and Miura
[
Germain et al.(2016)Germain, Ghoul, and Miura
]
. Up to our knowledge, the question on the existence of Type II blowup solutions for (
1.4
) remains open for
3\leq d\leq 6
.

When
d\geq 7
, Bizoń and Wasserman
[
Bizoń and Wasserman(2015)
]
proved that equation (
1.4
) has no self-similar shrinking solutions. According to Struwe
[
Struwe(1988)
]
, this result implies that in dimensions
d\geq 7
, all singularities for equation (
1.4
) must be of type II (see also Biernat
[
Biernat(2015)
]
for a recent analysis of such singularities). Recently, Biernat and Seki
[
Biernat and Seki(2016)
]
, via the matched asymptotic method developed by Herero and Velázquez
[
Herrero and Velázquez(1994)
]
, construct for equation (
1.4
) a countable family of Type II blowup solutions, each characterized by a different blowup rate

\lambda(t)\sim(T-t)^{\frac{\ell}{\gamma}}\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T,

(1.7)

where
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
2\ell>\gamma
and
\gamma=\gamma(d)
is given by

\gamma(d)=\frac{1}{2}(d-2-\tilde{\gamma})\in(1,2]\quad\text{for}\;\;d\geq 7,

(1.8)

where
\tilde{\gamma}=\sqrt{d^{2}-8d+8}
.
The blowup rate (
1.7
) is in fact driven by the asymptotic behavior of a stationary solution of (
1.4
), say
Q
, which is the unique (up to scaling) solution of the equation

Q^{\prime\prime}+\frac{(d-1)}{r}Q^{\prime}-\frac{(d-1)}{2r^{2}}\sin(2Q)=0,\quad Q(0)=0,\;Q^{\prime}(0)=1,

(1.9)

and admits the behavior for
r
large,

Q(r)=\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\dfrac{a_{0}}{r^{\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{r^{2+\gamma}}\right)\quad\text{for some}\;\;a_{0}=a_{0}(d)>0,

(1.10)

(see Appendix in
[
Biernat(2015)
]
for a proof of the existence of
Q
). Note that the case
2\ell=\gamma
only happens in dimension
d=7
. In this case, Biernat
[
Biernat(2015)
]
used the method of
[
Herrero and Velázquez(1994)
]
and formally derived the blowup rate

\lambda(t)\sim\frac{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{|\log(T-t)|}\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T.

(1.11)

He also provided numerical evidences supporting that the case
\ell=1
in (
1.7
) or (
1.11
) corresponds to a generic blowup solution.

In the energy critical case, i.e.
d=2
, Van de Berg, Hulshof and King
[
van den Berg et al.(2003)van den Berg, Hulshof, and King
]
, through a formal analysis based on the matched asymptotic technique of Herrero and Velázquez
[
Herrero and Velázquez(1994)
]
, predicted that there are type II blowup solutions to (
1.4
) of the form

u(r,t)\sim Q\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)}\right),

where

Q(r)=2\tan^{-1}(r)

(1.12)

is the unique (up to scaling) solution of (
1.9
), and the blowup speed governed by the quantized rates

\lambda(t)\sim\frac{(T-t)^{\ell}}{|\log(T-t)|^{\frac{2\ell}{2\ell-1}}}\qquad\text{for}\;\;\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

This result was later confirmed by Raphaël and Schweyer
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
. Note that the case
\ell=1
was treated in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2013)
]
and corresponds to a stable blowup. In particular, the authors in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
,
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2013)
]
adapted the strategy developed by Merle, Raphaël and Rodnianski
[
Raphaël and Rodnianski(2012)
]
,
[
Merle et al.(2011)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
for the study of wave and Schrödinger maps to construct for equation (
1.4
) type II blowup solutions. Their method is based on two main steps:

•

Construction of an adapted approximate blowup profile by solving elliptic equations. From the computation of the tails of the blowup profile, they are able to formally derive the blowup speed.

•

Control of the error by using an energy method involving ”Lyapunov” functionals adapted to the linearized flow around the ground state.
This energy method doesn’t involve neither spectral estimates nor maximum principles.

In this work, by considering
d\geq 7
, we ask whether we can carry out the analysis of
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
for the energy critical case
d=2
, to the construction of blowup solutions for equation (
1.4
) in the case
d\geq 7
. It happens that the asymptotic behavior (
1.10
) is perfectly suitable to replace the explicit profile (
1.12
) for an implementation of the strategy of
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
. The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of type II blowup solutions to (
1.4
) with prescibed behavior)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and
\gamma
be defined as in (
1.8
), we fix an integer

\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\quad\text{such that}\quad 2\ell>\gamma,

and an arbitrary Sobolev exponent

\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{N},\;\;\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}(\ell)\to+\infty\quad\text{as}\;\;\ell\to+\infty.

Then there exists a smooth corotational radially symmetric initial data
u_{0}
such that the corresponding solution to (
1.4
) is of the form:

u(r,t)=Q\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)}\right)+q\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},t\right)

(1.13)

where

\lambda(t)=c(u_{0})(T-t)^{\frac{\ell}{\gamma}}(1+o_{t\to T}(1)),\quad c(u_{0})>0,

(1.14)

and

\lim_{t\to T}\|\nabla^{\sigma}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\forall\sigma\in\left(d/2+3,\mathfrak{s}\right].

(1.15)

Moreover, the case
\ell=1
corresponds to a stable blowup regime.

Remark 1.2
.

Since
\gamma=2
for
d=7
and
\gamma\in(1,2)
for
d\geq 8
, the condition
2\ell>\gamma
means that
\ell\geq 2
for
d=7
and
\ell\geq 1
for
d\geq 8
. Note that the condition
2\ell>\gamma
allows to avoid the presence of logarithmic corrections in the construction of the approximate profile. In other words, the case
2\ell=\gamma
(equivalent to
\ell=1
and
d=7
) would involve an additional logarithmic gain related to the growth of the approximate profile at infinity, which turns out to be essential for the derivation of the speed (
1.11
). Although our analysis could be naturally extended to this case (
\ell=1
and
d=7
) with some complicated computation, we hope to treat this case in a separate work.

Remark 1.3
.

The quantization of the blowup rate (
1.14
) is the same as the one obtained in
[
Biernat and Seki(2016)
]
. Note that the authors of
[
Biernat and Seki(2016)
]
only claim the existence result of a type II blowup solution with the rate (
1.14
) and say nothing about the dynamical description of the solution. On the contrary, our result shows that the constructed solution blows up in finite time by concentration of a stationary state in the supercritical regime. Moreover, our constructed solution is in fact
(\ell-1)
codimension stable in the sense that we will precise shortly.

Remark 1.4
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
2\ell>\gamma
, an integer
L\gg\ell
and
\mathfrak{s}\sim L\gg 1
, our initial data that we are considering has the following form

u_{0}=Q_{b(0)}+\epsilon_{0},

(1.16)

where
Q_{b}
is a deformation of the ground state
Q
.
In addition,
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
corresponds to unstable directions of the flow in the topology
\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}}
in a certain neighborhood of the ground state
Q
. We will prove that for all
\epsilon_{0}\in\dot{H}^{\sigma}\cap\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}}
(for some
\sigma=\sigma(d)>\frac{d}{2}
) small enough, for all
(b_{1}(0),b_{\ell+1}(0),\cdots,b_{L}(0))
small enough, under a specific choice of the unstable directions
(b_{2}(0),\cdots,b_{\ell}(0))
the solution of (
1.4
) with the data (
1.16
) verifies the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
. This implies that our constructed solution is
(\ell-1)
codimension stable. In particular, the case
\ell=1
corresponds to a stable type II blowup regime, which is in agreement with numerical evidences given in
[
Biernat(2015)
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

It is worth to remark that the harmonic heat flow shares many features with the semilinear heat equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u\quad\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.17)

A remarkable fact that two important critical exponents appear when considering the dynamics of (
1.17
):

p_{S}=\frac{d+2}{d-2}\quad\text{and}\quad p_{JL}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty&\quad\text{for}\;\;d\leq 10,\\
1+\frac{4}{d-4-2\sqrt{d-1}}&\quad\text{for}\;\;d\geq 11,\end{array}\right.

correspond to the cases
d=2
and
d=7
in the study of equation (
1.4
) respectively.

When
1<p\leq p_{S}
, Giga and Kohn
[
Giga and Kohn(1987)
]
, Giga, Matsui and Sasayama
[
Giga et al.(2004)Giga, Matsui, and Sasayama
]
showed that all blowup solutions are of type I. Here the type I blowup means that

\limsup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}<+\infty,

otherwise we say the blowup solution is of type II.

When
p=p_{S}
, Filippas, Herrero and Velázquez
[
Filippas et al.(2000)Filippas, Herrero, and Velázquez
]
formally constructed for (
1.17
) type II blowup solutions in dimensions
3\leq d\leq 6
, however, they could not do the same in dimensions
d\geq 7
. This formal result is partly confirmed by Schweyer
[
Schweyer(2012a)
]
in dimension
d=4
. Interestingly, Collot, Merle and Raphaël
[
C. et al.(2016)C., F., and P.
]
show that type II blowup is ruled out in dimension
d\geq 7
near the solitary wave.

When
p_{S}<p<p_{JL}
, Matano and Merle
[
Matano and Merle(2004)
]
(see also Mizoguchi
[
Mizoguchi(2004)
]
) proved that only type I blowup occurs in the radial setting.

When
p>p_{JL}
, Herrero and Velázquez formally derived in
[
Herrero and Velázquez(1994)
]
the existence of type II blowup solutions with the quantized rates:

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim(T-t)^{\frac{2\ell}{(p-1)\alpha(d,p)}},\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N},\;2\ell>\alpha.

The formal result was clarified in
[
Matano and Merle(2009)
]
,
[
Mizoguchi(2007)
]
and
[
C.(2016a)
]
. The collection of these works yields a complete classification of the type II blowup scenario for the radially symmetric energy supercritical case.

In comparison to the case of the semilinear heat equation (
1.17
), it might be possibe to prove that all blowup solutions to equation (
1.4
) are of type I in dimension
3\leq d\leq 6
. However, due to the lack of monotonicity of the nonlinear term, the analysis of the harmonic heat flow (
1.4
) is much more difficult than the case of the semilinear heat equation (
1.17
) treated in
[
Matano and Merle(2004)
]
.

Strategy of the proof.

(i)

Renormalized flow and iterated resonances.
Following the scaling invariance of (

w(y,s)=u(r,t),\quad y=\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},\quad\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(t)},

which leads to the following renormalized flow:

\partial_{s}w=\partial_{y}^{2}w+\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}w-b_{1}\Lambda w-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2w),\quad b_{1}=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}.

(1.23)

Assuming that the leading part of the solution

is given by the ground state profile
Q
admitting the asymptotic behavior (
1.10

\mathscr{L}
defined by (

\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{A}^{*}\mathscr{A},\quad\mathscr{A}f=-\Lambda Q\partial_{y}\left(\frac{f}{\Lambda Q}\right),\quad\mathscr{A}^{*}f=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\partial_{y}\left(y^{d-1}\Lambda Qf\right),

which directly implies

\mathscr{L}(\Lambda Q)=0,

where from a direct computation,

\Lambda Q\sim\frac{c_{0}}{y^{\gamma}}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty\quad\text{with $\gamma$ definied in \eqref{def:gamome}}.

More generally, we can compute the kernel of the powers of

through the iterative scheme

\mathscr{L}T_{k+1}=-T_{k},\quad T_{0}=\Lambda Q,

(1.25)

which displays a non trivial tail at infinity (see Lemma

T_{k}(y)\sim c_{k}y^{2k-\gamma}\quad\text{for}\quad y\gg 1.

(1.26)

(ii)

Tail computation.
Following the approach in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]

w(y,s)=Q_{b(s)}(y),

b_{i}\sim b_{1}^{i},\quad|S_{i}(y)|\lesssim b_{1}^{i}y^{2(i-1)-\gamma},

so that

is in some sense homogeneous of degree

in

at infinity. The construction of

. We plug the decomposition (
1.27

(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq L}
which cancels the leading order terms at infinity.

- At the order
\mathcal{O}(b_{1})
: it is not possible to adjust the law of
b_{1}
for the first term
1

1

(b_{1})_{s}=-c_{1}b_{1}

and obtain from (

- At the order

Hence, we cancel the highest order growth for

We then solve for

- At the order

(b_{k})_{s}T_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\Lambda T_{k}+b_{k+1}\mathscr{L}T_{k+1}+\mathscr{L}S_{k+1}=b_{1}^{k+1}NL_{k}(T_{1},\cdots,T_{k},Q).

(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=0,

to cancel at infinity the leading order growth.
We then solve for
S_{k+1}

(iii)

The universal system of ODEs.
From the above procedure we deduce after

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=0,\quad 1\leq k\leq L,\quad b_{L+1}=0,\\

We localize

As a consequence,

(v)

Control of Sobolev norms.
From (

\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}(s)=\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q|^{2}\gtrsim\int|\nabla^{2\Bbbk}q|^{2}+\int\frac{|q|^{2}}{1+y^{4\Bbbk}},

where
\Bbbk
is given by (

) will simplify our proof. As in
[

[

]

[

\frac{d}{ds}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\right\}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2L+1+2\nu(\delta,\eta)}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}},\quad\nu(\delta,\eta)>0,

which is good enough to control the local norms of

Note that we also need to control lower energies

which is enough to control the nonlinear term. Let us remark that the condition

is always controlled. By the coercivity of

for

1.15

2.
Construction of an approximate profile.

This section is devoted to the construction of a suitable approximate solution to (

) by using the same approach developed in
[
Raphaël and Rodnianski(2012)
]
. Similar approachs can also be found in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2013)
]
,
[
Hillairet and Raphaël(2012)
]
,
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014b)
]
,
[
Schweyer(2012b)
]

[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski

. The construction is mainly based on the fact that the kernel of the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
around

Following the scaling invariance of (
1.4
), we introduce the following change of variables:

w(y,s)=u(r,t),\quad y=\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},\quad\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(t)},

(2.1)

which leads to the following renormalized flow:

\partial_{s}w=\partial_{y}^{2}w+\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}w+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda w-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2w),

(2.2)

where
\lambda_{s}=\frac{d\lambda}{ds}
. Noticing that in the setting (
2.1
), we have

\partial_{r}u(r,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}\partial_{y}w(y,s)

and since we deal with the finite time blowup of the problem (
1.4
), we would naturally impose the condition

\lambda(t)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to T,

for some
T\in(0,+\infty)
. Hence,
\partial_{r}u(r,t)
blows up in finite time
T
.

Let us assume that the leading part of the solution of (
2.2
) is given by the harmonic map
Q
, which is unique solution (up to scaling) of the equation

Q^{\prime\prime}+\frac{(d-1)}{y}Q^{\prime}-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2Q)=0,\quad Q(0)=0,\;Q^{\prime}(0)=1.

(2.3)

We aim at constructing an approximate solution of (
2.2
) close to
Q
. The natural way is to linearize equation (
2.2
) around
Q
, which generates the Schrödinger operator defined by (
1.22
). Let us now recall the main properties of
\mathscr{L}
in the following subsection.

2.1.
Structure of the linearized Hamiltonian.

In this subsection, we recall the main properties of the linearized operator around
Q
, which are fundamental for the construction of the approximate profile. Actually, the properties of the linearized operator are also important to derive the coercivity properties used for the high Sobolev energy estimates. Let us start by recalling the following result from Biernat
[
Biernat(2015)
]
, which gives the asymptotic behavior of the harmonic map
Q
:

Lemma 2.1

(Development of the harmonic map
Q
)
.

Let
d\geq 7
, there exists a unique solution
Q
to equation (
2.3
), which admits the following asymptotic behavior: For any
k\in\mathcal{N}^{*}
,

(i)
(Asymptotic behavior of
Q
)

Q(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\dfrac{a_{0}}{y^{\gamma}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)\right]&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where
\gamma
is defined in (
1.8
),
\tilde{\gamma}=\sqrt{d^{2}-8d+8}
and the constant
a_{0}=a_{0}(d)>0
.

(ii)
(Degeneracy)

\Lambda Q>0,\quad\Lambda Q(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}^{\prime}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{a_{0}\gamma}{y^{\gamma}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)\right]&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

Proof.

The proof of (
2.4
) is done through the introduction of the variables
x=\log y
and
v(x)=2Q(y)-\pi
and consists of the phase portrait analysis of the autonomous equation

v^{\prime\prime}(x)+(d-2)v^{\prime}(x)+(d-2)\sin(v(x))=0.

All details of the proof can be found at pages 184-185 in
[
Biernat(2015)
]
. The proof of (
2.5
) directly follows from the expansion (
2.4
).
∎

The linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
displays a remarkable structure given by the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2

(Factorization of
\mathscr{L}
)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and define the first order operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}w

\displaystyle=-\partial_{y}w+\frac{V}{y}w=-\Lambda Q\partial_{y}\left(\frac{w}{\Lambda Q}\right),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{*}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}}\partial_{y}\big(y^{d-1}w\big)+\frac{V}{y}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\partial_{y}\left(y^{d-1}\Lambda Qw\right),

(2.7)

where

V(y):=\Lambda\log(\Lambda Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\mathcal{O}(y^{2})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
-\gamma+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

We have

\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{A}^{*}\mathscr{A},\quad\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{A}^{*},

(2.9)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
stands for the conjugate Hamiltonian.

Remark 2.3
.

The adjoint operator
\mathscr{A}^{*}
is defined with respect to the Lebesgue measure

\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\mathscr{A}u)wy^{d-1}dy=\int_{0}^{+\infty}u(\mathscr{A}^{*}w)y^{d-1}dy.

Remark 2.4
.

We have

\mathscr{L}(\Lambda w)=\Lambda(\mathscr{L}w)+2\mathscr{L}w-\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}w.

(2.10)

Since
\mathscr{L}(\Lambda Q)=0
, one can express the definition of
Z
through the potential
V
as follows:

Z(y)=V^{2}+\Lambda V+(d-2)V.

(2.11)

Let
\tilde{Z}
be defined by

\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=-\partial_{yy}-\frac{d-1}{y}\partial_{y}+\frac{\tilde{Z}}{y^{2}},

(2.12)

then, a direct computation yields

\tilde{Z}(y)=(V+1)^{2}+(d-2)(V+1)-\Lambda V.

(2.13)

From (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we see that the kernel of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
are explicit:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathscr{A}w=0&\quad\text{if and only if}\quad w\in\text{Span}(\Lambda Q),\\
\mathscr{A}^{*}w=0&\quad\text{if and only if}\quad w\in\text{Span}\left(\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\right).\\
\end{array}\right.

Hence, the elements of the kernel of
\mathscr{L}
are given by

\mathscr{L}w=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad w\in\text{Span}(\Lambda Q,\Gamma),

(2.14)

where
\Gamma
can be found from the Wronskian relation

\Gamma^{\prime}\Lambda Q-\Gamma(\Lambda Q)^{\prime}=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}},

(2.15)

that is

\Gamma(y)=\Lambda Q(y)\int_{1}^{y}\frac{d\xi}{\xi^{d-1}(\Lambda Q(\xi))^{2}},

which admits the asymptotic behavior:

\Gamma(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{dy^{d-1}}+\mathcal{O}(y)&\text{as}\;\;y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{1}{a_{0}\gamma(d-2-2\gamma)y^{d-2-\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{d-\gamma}}\right)&\text{as}\;\;y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.16)

From (
2.14
), we may invert
\mathscr{L}
as follows:

\mathscr{L}^{-1}f=-\Gamma(y)\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Lambda Q(x)x^{d-1}dx+\Lambda Q(y)\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Gamma(x)x^{d-1}dx.

(2.17)

The factorization of
\mathscr{L}
grants us to compute
\mathscr{L}^{-1}
easily. In particular, we have the following:

Lemma 2.5

(Inversion of
\mathscr{L}
)
.

Let
f
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
radially symmetric function and
w=\mathscr{L}^{-1}f
be given by (
2.17
), then

\mathscr{L}w=f,\quad\mathscr{A}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Lambda Q(x)x^{d-1}dx,\quad w=-\Lambda Q\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\mathscr{A}w(x)}{\Lambda Q(x)}dx.

(2.18)

Proof.

From the relation (
2.15
), we compute

\mathscr{A}\Gamma=-\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}.

Applying
\mathscr{A}
to (
2.17
) and using the cancellation
\mathscr{A}(\Lambda Q)=0
, we obtain

\mathscr{A}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Lambda Q(x)x^{d-1}dx.

From the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
, we write

w=-\Lambda Q\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\mathscr{A}w}{\Lambda Q}dx.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

2.2.
Admissible functions.

We define a class of admissible functions which exhibit a relevant behavior both at the origin and infinity.

Definition 2.6

(Admissible function)
.

Fix
\gamma>0
, we say that a smooth function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
is admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
if

(i)\;

f
admits a Taylor expansion to all orders around the origin,

f(y)=\sum_{k=p_{1}}^{p}c_{k}y^{2k+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2p+3});

(ii)\;

f
and its derivatives admit the bounds, for
y\geq 1
,

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial^{k}_{y}f(y)|\lesssim y^{2p_{2}-\gamma-k}.

Remark 2.7
.

Note from (
2.5
) that
\Lambda Q
is admissible of degree
(0,0)
.

One note that
\mathscr{L}
naturally acts on the class of admissible function in the following way:

Lemma 2.8

(Action of
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{L}^{-1}
on admissible functions)
.

Let
f
be an admissible function of degree
(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
, then:

(i)\;

\Lambda f
is admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2})
.

(ii)\,

\mathscr{L}f
is admissible of degree
(\max\{0,p_{1}-1\},p_{2}-1)
.

(iii)\,

\mathscr{L}^{-1}f
is admissible of degree
(p_{1}+1,p_{2}+1)
.

Proof.

(i)-(ii)
This is simply a consequence of Definition
2.6
.

(iii)
We aim at proving that if
f
is admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2})
, then
w=\mathscr{L}^{-1}f
is admissible of degree
(p_{1}+1,p_{2}+1)
. To do so, we use Lemma
2.5
to estimate

- for
y\ll 1
,

\mathscr{A}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}f\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{y^{d}}\int_{0}^{y}x^{2p_{1}+1+d}dx\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2p_{1}+2}),

w=-\Lambda Q\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\mathscr{A}w}{\Lambda Q}dx=\mathcal{O}\left(y\int_{0}^{y}x^{2p_{1}+1}dx\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2(p_{1}+1)+1}),

- for
y\geq 1
,

\mathscr{A}w=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{y^{d-1-\gamma}}\int_{0}^{y}x^{2p_{2}-2\gamma+d-1}dx\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2p_{2}+1-\gamma}),

w=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{y^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{y}x^{2p_{2}+1}\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2(p_{2}+1)-\gamma}).

From the last formula in (
2.18
) and (
2.8
), we estimate

\partial_{y}w=-\partial_{y}\Lambda Q\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\mathscr{A}w}{\Lambda Q}dx-\mathscr{A}w=-\frac{\partial_{y}\Lambda Q}{\Lambda Q}w-\mathscr{A}w=\mathcal{O}(y^{2(p_{2}+1)-\gamma-1}).

Using
\mathscr{L}w=f
, we get

\partial_{yy}w=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\partial_{y}w|}{y}+\frac{|w|}{y^{2}}+|f|\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2(p_{2}+1)-\gamma-2}).

By taking radial derivatives of
\mathscr{L}w=f
, we obtain by induction

|\partial_{y}^{k}w|\lesssim y^{2(p_{2}+1)-\gamma-k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\;y\geq 1.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

The following lemma is a consequence of Lemma
2.8
:

Lemma 2.9

(Generators of the kernel of
\mathscr{L}^{k}
)
.

Let the sequence of profiles

T_{k}=(-1)^{k}\mathscr{L}^{-k}\Lambda Q,\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

(2.19)

then

(i)\;

T_{k}
is admissible of degree
(k,k)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)\;

\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k}
is admissible of degree
(k,k-1)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)
We note from (
2.5
) that
\Lambda Q
is admissible of degree
(0,0)
. By induction and part
(iii)
of Lemma
2.8
, the conclusion then follows.

(ii)
We proceed by induction. For
k=1
, we explicitly compute
T_{1}=-\mathscr{L}^{-1}\Lambda Q
by using Lemma
2.5
and the expansion (
2.5
) to get

\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\quad\partial_{y}^{m}T_{1}(y)=e_{1,m}y^{2-\gamma-m}+\mathcal{O}\left(y^{-\gamma-m}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

By induction, one can easily check that
\partial_{y}^{m}\Lambda f=\Lambda\partial_{y}^{m}f+m\partial_{y}^{m}f
for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Hence,

\partial_{y}^{m}\Big[\Lambda T_{1}-(2-\gamma)T_{1}\Big]=\Lambda\partial_{y}^{m}T_{1}-(2-\gamma-m)\partial_{y}^{m}T_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(y^{-\gamma-m}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

Since
T_{1}
and
\Lambda T_{1}
are admissible of degree
(1,1)
, we deduce that
\Lambda T_{1}-(2-\gamma)T_{1}
is admissible of degree
(1,0)
.

We now assume the claim for
k\geq 1
, namely that
\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k}
is admissible of degree
(k,k-1)
. Let us prove that
\Lambda T_{k+1}-(2(k+1)-\gamma)T_{k+1}
is admissible of degree
(k+1,k)
. We use formula (
2.10
) and definition (
2.19
) to write

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\big(\Lambda T_{k+1}-(2k+2-\gamma)T_{k+1}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Lambda\mathscr{L}T_{k+1}-(2k-\gamma)\mathscr{L}T_{k+1}-\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}T_{k+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k}-\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}T_{k+1}.

(2.20)

From part
(i)
, we know that
T_{k+1}
is admissible of degree
(k+1,k+1)
. From (
2.11
) and (
2.8
), one can check that
\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}T_{k+1}
admits the asymptotic:

\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}T_{k+1}=\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+1})\quad\text{as}\quad y\to 0,

and

\partial_{y}^{j}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}T_{k+1}\right)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2(k+1)-j-\gamma-3})\ll y^{2(k-1)+j-\gamma}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

Together with the induction hypothesis, we deduce that the right hand side of (
2.20
) is admissible of degree
(k,k-1)
. The conclusion then follows by using part
(iii)
of Lemma
2.8
. This ends the proof of Lemma
2.9
.
∎

We close this subsection by defining a simple notion of homogeneous admissible function.

Definition 2.10

(Homogeneous admissible function)
.

Let
L\gg 1
be an integer and
m=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{L})\in\mathbb{N}^{L}
, we say that a function
f(b,y)
with
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
is homogeneous of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}
if it is a finite linear combination of monomials

\tilde{f}(y)\prod_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}^{m_{k}},

with
\tilde{f}(y)
admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2})
in the sense of Definition
2.6
and

(m_{1},\cdots,m_{L})\in\mathbb{N}^{L},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{L}km_{k}=p_{3}.

We set

\text{deg}(f):=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}).

2.3.
Slowly modulated blow-up profile.

In this subsection, we use the explicit structure of the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
to construct an approximate blow-up profile. In particular, we claim the following:

Proposition 2.11

(Construction of the approximate profile)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and
L\gg 1
be an integer. Let
M>0
be a large enough universal constant, then there exist a small enough universal constant
b^{*}(M,L)>0
such that the following holds true. Let a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map

b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L}):[s_{0},s_{1}]\mapsto(-b^{*},b^{*})^{L},

with a priori bounds in
[s_{0},s_{1}]
:

0<b_{1}<b^{*},\quad|b_{k}|\lesssim b_{1}^{k},\quad 2\leq k\leq L,

(2.21)

Then there exist homogeneous profiles

S_{1}=0,\quad S_{k}=S_{k}(b,y),\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

such that

Q_{b(s)}(y)=Q(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}(s)T_{k}(y)+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k}(b,y)\equiv Q(y)+\Theta_{b(s)}(y),

(2.22)

produces an approximate solution to the remormalized flow (
2.2
):

\partial_{s}Q_{b}-\partial_{yy}Q_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}Q_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda Q_{b}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2Q_{b})=\Psi_{b}+\textup{Mod}(t),

(2.23)

with the following property:

(i)
(Modulation equation)

\textup{Mod}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}\Big]\left[T_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{L+2}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{k}}\right],

(2.24)

where we use the convention
b_{j}=0
for
j\geq L+1
.

(ii)
(Estimate on the profiles) The profiles
(S_{k})_{2\leq k\leq L+2}
are homogeneous with

\displaystyle\text{deg}(S_{k})=(k,k-1,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=0\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L.

(iii)
(Estimate on the error
\Psi_{b}
) For all
0\leq m\leq L
, there holds:

- (global weight bound)

\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\Psi_{b}|^{2}+\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{|\Psi_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4(\hbar+m+1)}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

(2.25)

where
B_{1}
,
\hbar
,
\delta
are defined in (
1.20
) and (
1.18
).

- (improved local bound)

\forall M\geq 1,\quad\int_{y\leq 2M}|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\Psi_{b}|^{2}\lesssim M^{C}b_{1}^{2L+6}.

(2.26)

Proof.

We aim at constructing the profiles
(S_{k})_{2\leq k\leq L+2}
such that
\Psi_{b}(y)
defined from (
2.23
) has the
least possible growth
as
y\to+\infty
. The structure of the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
defined in (
1.22
) is the main ingredient for the construction of the approximate profile. This procedure will lead to the leading-order modulation equation

(b_{k})_{s}=-(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}+b_{k+1}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq L,

(2.27)

which actually cancels the worst growth of
S_{k}
as
y\to+\infty
.

\bullet
Expansion of
\Psi_{b}

. From (
2.23
) and (
2.3
), we write

\displaystyle\partial_{s}Q_{b}-\partial_{yy}Q_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}Q_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda Q_{b}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2Q_{b})

\displaystyle=b_{1}\Lambda Q+\partial_{s}\Theta_{b}-\partial_{yy}\Theta_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}\Theta_{y}+\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}\cos(2Q)\Theta_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\Theta_{b}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sin(2Q+2\Theta_{b})-\sin(2Q)-2\cos(2Q)\Theta_{b}\right]:=A_{1}+A_{2}.

Using the expression (
2.22
) of
\Theta_{b}
and the definition (
2.19
) of
T_{k}
(note that
\mathscr{L}T_{k}=-T_{k-1}
with the convention
T_{0}=\Lambda Q
), we write

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}\Lambda Q+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}T_{k}+b_{k}\mathscr{L}T_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\Lambda T_{k}\Big]+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\Big[\partial_{s}S_{k}+\mathscr{L}S_{k}+b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}T_{k}-b_{k+1}T_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\Lambda T_{k}\Big]+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\Big[\partial_{s}S_{k}+\mathscr{L}S_{k}+b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}-b_{k+1}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}\Big]T_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[\mathscr{L}S_{k+1}+\partial_{s}S_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\big[\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k}\big]+b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big[\mathscr{L}S_{L+2}+\partial_{s}S_{L+1}+b_{1}\Lambda S_{L+1}\Big]+\Big[\partial_{s}S_{L+2}+b_{1}\Lambda S_{L+2}\Big].

We now write

\displaystyle\partial_{s}S_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}(b_{j})_{s}\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{j})_{s}+(2j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(2j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle A_{1}=\textup{Mod}(t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{L+1}\left[\mathscr{L}S_{k+1}+E_{k}\right]+E_{L+2},

where for
k=1,\cdots,L
,

E_{k}=b_{1}b_{k}\big[\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k}\big]+b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Big[(2j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{j}},

(2.28)

and for
k=L+1,L+2
,

E_{k}=b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(2j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}.

(2.29)

For the expansion of the nonlinear term
A_{2}
, let us denote

f(x)=\sin(2x)

and use a Taylor expansion to write (see pages 1740 in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
for a similar computation)

\displaystyle A_{2}=\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(i)}(Q)}{i!}\Theta_{b}^{i}+R_{2}\right]=\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}P_{i}+R_{1}+R_{2}\right],

where

P_{i}=\sum_{j=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{j!}\sum_{|J|_{1}=j,|J|_{2}=i}c_{J}\prod_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}^{i_{k}}T_{k}^{i_{k}}\prod_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k}^{j_{k}},

(2.30)

R_{1}=\sum_{j=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{j!}\sum_{|J|_{1}=j,|J|_{2}\geq L+3}c_{J}\prod_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}^{i_{k}}T_{k}^{i_{k}}\prod_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k}^{j_{k}},

(2.31)

R_{2}=\frac{\Theta_{b}^{L+3}}{(L+2)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{L+2}f^{(L+3)}(Q+\tau\Theta_{b})d\tau,

(2.32)

with
J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2L+1}
and

|J|_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}i_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}j_{k},\quad|J|_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}ki_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}kj_{k}.

(2.33)

In conclusion, we have

\Psi_{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{L+1}\left[\mathscr{L}S_{k+1}+E_{k}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}P_{k+1}\right]+E_{L+2}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}(R_{1}+R_{2})

(2.34)

\bullet
Construction of
S_{k}
.

From the expression of
\Psi_{b}
given in (
2.34
), we construct iteratively the sequences of profiles
(S_{k})_{1\leq k\leq L+2}
through the scheme

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S_{1}&=0,\\
S_{k}&=-\mathscr{L}^{-1}F_{k},\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,\end{array}\right.

(2.35)

where

F_{k}=E_{k-1}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}P_{k}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2.

We claim by induction on
k
that
F_{k}
is homogeneous with

\text{deg}(F_{k})=(k-1,k-2,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

(2.36)

and

\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=0\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L+2.

(2.37)

From item
(iii)
of Lemma
2.8
and (
2.36
), we deduce that
S_{k}
is homogeneous of degree

\text{deg}(S_{k})=(k,k-1,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

and from (
2.37
), we get

\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=0\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L+2,

which is the conclusion of item
(ii)
.

Let us now give the proof of (
2.36
) and (
2.37
). We proceed by induction.

- Case
k=2
: We compute explicitly from (
2.28
) and (
2.30
),

F_{2}=E_{1}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}P_{2}=b_{1}^{2}\left[\Lambda T_{1}-(2-\gamma)T_{1}+\frac{(d-1)f^{\prime\prime}(Q)}{2y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}\right],

which directly follows (
2.37
). From Lemma
2.9
, we know that
T_{1}
and
\Lambda T_{1}-(2-\gamma)T_{1}
are admissible of degree
(1,1)
and
(1,0)
respectively. Using (
2.4
), one can check the bound

\forall m,j\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{m}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim y^{-\gamma-2-m}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

(2.38)

Since
T_{1}
is admissible of degree
(1,1)
, we have that

\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{m}(T_{1}^{2})|\lesssim y^{4-2\gamma-m}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

By the Leibniz rule and the fact that
2\gamma-2>0
, we get that

\forall m,j\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{m}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}\right)\right|\lesssim y^{-\gamma-m-(2\gamma-2)}\lesssim y^{-\gamma-m}.

We also have the expansion near the origin,

\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}=\sum_{i=2}^{k}c_{i}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3}),\quad k\geq 1.

Hence,
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}
is admissible of degree
(2,0)
, which concludes the proof of (
2.36
) for
k=2
.

- Case
k\to k+1
: Estimate (
2.37
) holds by direct inspection. Let us now assume that
S_{k}
is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k)
and prove that
S_{k+1}
is homogeneous of degree
(k+1,k,k+1)
. In particular, the claim immediately follows from part
(iii)
of Lemma
2.8
once we show that
F_{k+1}
is homogeneous with

\text{deg}(F_{k+1})=\text{deg}\left(E_{k}+\frac{P_{k+1}}{y^{2}}\right)=(k,k-1,k+1).

(2.39)

From part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.9
and the a priori assumption (
2.21
), we see that
b_{1}b_{k}(\Lambda T_{k}-(2k-\gamma)T_{k})
is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k+1)
. From part
(i)
of Lemma
2.8
and the induction hypothesis,
b_{1}\Lambda S_{k}
is also homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k+1)
. By definition,
b_{1}\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{1}}
is homogeneous and has the same degree as
S_{k}
. Thus,

\left((2j-\gamma)b_{1}-\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}\right)\left(b_{1}\frac{\partial S_{k}}{\partial b_{1}}\right)

is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k+1)
. From definitions (
2.28
) and (
2.29
), we derive

\text{deg}(E_{k})=(k,k-1,k+1),\quad k\geq 1.

It remains to control the term
\frac{P_{k+1}}{y^{2}}
. From the definition (
2.30
), we see that
\frac{P_{k+1}}{y^{2}}
is a linear combination of monomials of the form

M_{J}(y)=\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\prod_{m=1}^{L}b_{m}^{i_{m}}T_{m}^{i_{m}}\prod_{m=2}^{L+2}S_{m}^{j_{m}},

with

J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2}),\quad|J|_{1}=j,\;|J|_{2}=k+1,\;2\leq j\leq k+1.

Recall from part
(i)
of Lemma
2.9
the bound

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{n}T_{m}|\lesssim y^{2m-\gamma-n}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,

and from the induction hypothesis and the a priori bound (
2.21
),

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{n}S_{m}|\lesssim b_{1}^{m}y^{2(m-1)-\gamma-n}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

Together with the bound (
2.38
), we obtain the following bound at infinity,

|M_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{|J|_{2}}y^{2|J|_{2}-\gamma-|J|_{1}\gamma-2-2\sum_{m=2}^{L+2}j_{m}}\lesssim b_{1}^{k+1}y^{2(k-1)-\gamma}.

The control of
\partial_{y}^{n}M_{J}
follows by the Leibniz rule and the above estimates. One can also check that
M_{J}
is of order
y^{2|J|_{2}+|J|_{1}-1}
near the origin. This concludes the proof of (
2.39
) as well as part
(ii)
of Proposition
2.11
.

\bullet
Estimate on
\Psi_{b}
.

From (
2.34
) and (
2.35
), the expression of
\Psi_{b}
is now reduced to

\Psi_{b}=E_{L+2}+\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}(R_{1}+R_{2}),

where
E_{L+2}
,
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are given by (
2.29
), (
2.31
) and (
2.32
).

We start by estimating
E_{L+2}
term defined by (
2.29
). Since
S_{L+2}
is homogeneous of degree
(L+2,L+1,L+2)
and thus so are
\Lambda S_{L+2}
and
b_{1}\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{1}}
. This follows that
E_{L+2}
is homogeneous of degree
(L+2,L+1,L+3)
. Using part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.8
and the relation
d-2\gamma-4\hbar=4\delta
(see (
1.18
)), we estimate for all
0\leq m\leq L

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}E_{L+2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+6}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|y^{2(L+1)-\gamma-2(\hbar+m+1)}|^{2}y^{d-1}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+6}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{4(L-m+\delta)-1}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{(2L+6)-2(L-m+\delta)(1+\eta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

where
\eta=\eta(L)
,
0<\eta\ll 1
.

We now turn to the control of the term
\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}
, which is a linear combination of terms of the form (see (
2.31
))

\tilde{M}_{J}=\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\prod_{n=1}^{L}b_{n}^{i_{n}}T_{n}^{i_{n}}\prod_{n=2}^{L+2}S_{k}^{j_{n}},

with

J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2}),\;|J|_{1}=j,\;|J|_{2}\geq L+3,\;2\leq j\leq L+2.

Using the admissibility of
T_{n}
and the homogeneity of
S_{n}
, we get the bounds

|\tilde{M}_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{L+3}y^{2|J|_{2}+j-1}\lesssim b_{1}^{L+3}y^{2L+6}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to 0,

and

|\tilde{M}_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{|J|_{2}}y^{2|J|_{2}-j\gamma-2-\gamma}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,

where we used the fact that
j\geq 2
and
2-j\gamma<0
, and similarly for higher derivatives by the Leibniz rule. Thus, we obtain the round estimate for all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\left|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\left(\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2|J|_{2}}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|y^{2|J|_{2}-j\gamma-\gamma-2-2(\hbar+m+1)}|^{2}y^{d-1}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}.

The term
\frac{R_{2}}{y^{2}}
is estimated exactly as for the term
\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}
using the definition (
2.32
). Similarly, the control of
\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{|\Psi_{b}|^{2}}{(1+y^{4(\hbar+m+1)})}
is obtained along the exact same lines as above. This concludes the proof of (
2.25
). The local estimate (
2.26
) directly follows from the homogeneity of
S_{k}
and the admissibility of
T_{k}
. This concludes the proof of Proposition
2.11
.
∎

We now proceed to a simple localization of the profile
Q_{b}
to avoid the growth of tails in the region
y\geq 2B_{1}\gg B_{0}
. More precisely, we claim the following:

Proposition 2.12

(Estimates on the localized profile)
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.11
, we assume in addition the a priori bound

|(b_{1})_{s}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}.

(2.40)

Consider the localized profile

\tilde{Q}_{b(s)}(y)=Q(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}\tilde{T}_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\tilde{S}_{k}\quad\text{with}\quad\tilde{T}_{k}=\chi_{B_{1}}T_{k},\;\tilde{S}_{k}=\chi_{B_{1}}S_{k},

(2.41)

where
B_{1}
and
\chi_{B_{1}}
are defined as in (
1.20
) and (
1.21
). Then

\partial_{s}\tilde{Q}_{b}-\partial_{yy}\tilde{Q}_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}\tilde{Q}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b})=\tilde{\Psi}_{b}+\chi_{B_{1}}\,\textup{Mod}(t),

(2.42)

where
\tilde{\Psi}_{b}
satisfies the bounds:

(i)\;
(Large Sobolev bound) For all
0\leq m\leq L-1
,

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}+\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}+\int\frac{|\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4(\hbar+m+1)}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

(2.43)

and

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+L+1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}+\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+L}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+L}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}+\int\frac{|\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4(\hbar+L+1)}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(2.44)

where
\hbar
and
\delta
are defined by (
1.18
).

(ii)
(Very local bound) For all
M\leq\frac{B_{1}}{2}
and
0\leq m\leq L
,

\int_{y\leq 2M}|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}\lesssim M^{C}b_{1}^{2L+6}.

(2.45)

(iii)
(Refined local bound near
B_{0}
) For all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\int_{y\leq 2B_{0}}|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}+\int_{y\leq 2B_{0}}\frac{|\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4(\hbar+m+1)}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}.

(2.46)

Proof.

By a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\tilde{Q}_{b}-\partial_{yy}\tilde{Q}_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}\tilde{Q}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b})

\displaystyle=\chi_{B_{1}}\left[\partial_{s}Q_{b}-\partial_{yy}Q_{b}-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\partial_{y}Q_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda Q_{b}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\sin(2Q_{b})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\Theta_{b}\left[\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}-\left(\partial_{yy}\chi_{B_{1}}+\frac{d-1}{y}\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}}\right)+b_{1}\Lambda\chi_{B_{1}}\right]-2\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}}\partial_{y}\Theta_{b}+b_{1}(1-\chi_{B_{1}})\Lambda Q

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b})-\sin(2Q)-\chi_{B_{1}}(\sin(2Q_{b})-\sin(2Q))\right].

According to (
2.23
) and (
2.42
), we write

\tilde{\Psi}_{b}=\chi_{B_{1}}\Psi_{b}+\hat{\Psi}_{b},

where

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{b}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{b_{1}(1-\chi_{B_{1}})\Lambda Q}_{\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(1)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b})-\sin(2Q)-\chi_{B_{1}}(\sin(2Q_{b})-\sin(2Q))\right]}_{\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(2)}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\Theta_{b}\left[\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}-\left(\partial_{yy}\chi_{B_{1}}+\frac{d-1}{y}\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}}\right)+b_{1}\Lambda\chi_{B_{1}}\right]-2\partial_{y}\chi_{B_{1}}\partial_{y}\Theta_{b}}_{\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(3)}}.

The contribution of the term
\chi_{b_{1}}\Psi_{b}
to the bounds (
2.43
), (
2.44
), (
2.45
) and (
2.46
) follows exactly the same as in the proof of (
2.25
) and (
2.26
). We therefore left to estimate the term
\hat{\Psi}_{b}
. Since all the terms in the expression of
\hat{\Psi}_{b}
are localized in
B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}
except for the first one whose support is a subset of
\{y\geq B_{1}\}
. Hence, the estimates (
2.45
) and (
2.46
) directly follow from (
2.26
) and (
2.25
).

Let us now find the contribution of
\hat{\Psi}_{b}
to the bounds (
2.43
) and (
2.44
). We estimate

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\left|\frac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}(1-\chi_{B_{1}})\Lambda Q\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{\gamma+n}}\mathbf{1}_{y\geq B_{1}},

hence, using the relation
d-2\gamma-4\hbar=4\delta
(see (
1.18
)) and the definition (
1.20
) of
B_{1}
, we estimate for all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(1)}|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\int_{y\geq B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}}{y^{4(\hbar+m+1)+2\gamma}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)+2m\eta}.

For the nonlinear term
\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(2)}
, we note from the admissibility of
T_{k}
and the homogeneity of
S_{k}
that the
T_{k}
terms dominate for
y\geq B_{1}
in
\Theta_{b}
. Thus, for
y\geq B_{1}
,

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{n}\Theta_{b}\right|\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{k}y^{2k-\gamma-n}\mathbf{1}_{y\geq B_{1}}.

(2.47)

Using (
2.47
) and noting that
\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(2)}
is localized in
B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}
, we obtain the round bound

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{y}^{n}\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(2)}\right|\quad\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{k}y^{2(k-1)-\gamma-n}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{y^{\gamma+n}}\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{-(k-1)\eta}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}.

We then estimate for
0\leq m\leq L
,

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{-2(k-1)\eta}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}}{y^{4(\hbar+m+1)+2\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{(2m-2k+2)\eta}.

To control
\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(3)}
, we first note from the definition (
1.21
) and the assumption (
2.40
) that

|\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}|\lesssim\frac{(b_{1})_{s}}{b_{1}}\frac{y}{B_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\lesssim b_{1}\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}.

Using (
2.47
), we estimate for

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\hat{\Psi}_{b}^{(3)}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{2k}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}}{y^{4(\hbar+m+1)+2\gamma-4k+2}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{(2m-2k)\eta}.

Gathering all the bounds yields

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m+1}\hat{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{1}^{(2m-2k)\eta}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)+2\eta(m-L)}.

The control of
\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}
,
\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\hbar+m}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}
and

are obtained along the exact same lines as above. This concludes the proof of (
2.43
) and (
2.44
) as well as Proposition (
2.12
).

2.4.
Study of the dynamical system for
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
.

The construction of the
Q_{b}
profile formally leads to the finite dimensional dynamical system for
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
by setting to zero the inhomogeneous
\textup{Mod}(t)
term given in (
2.24
):

(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=0,\;\;1\leq k\leq L,\;\;b_{L+1}=0.

(2.48)

Unlike the critical case
(d=2)
treated in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
, there is no more logarithmic correction to be taken into account in the system (
2.48
). In particular, the system (
2.48
) admits explicit solutions and the linearized operator near these solutions is explicit.

Lemma 2.13

(Solution to the system (
2.48
))
.

Let

\frac{\gamma}{2}<\ell\ll L,\quad\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

and the sequence

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{1}&=\frac{\ell}{2\ell-\gamma},\\
c_{k+1}&=-\frac{\gamma(\ell-k)}{2\ell-\gamma}c_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell-1,\\
c_{k+1}&=0,\quad k\geq\ell.\end{array}\right.

(2.49)

Then the explicit choice

b_{k}^{e}(s)=\frac{c_{k}}{s^{k}},\quad s>0,\quad 1\leq k\leq L,

(2.50)

is a solution to (
2.48
).

The proof of Lemma
2.13
can be left to the reader since it directly follows from an explicit computation. We claim that the linearized flow of (
2.48
) near the solution (
2.50
) is explicit and exhibits
(\ell-1)
unstable directions. Note that the stability is considered in the sense that

\sup_{s}s^{k}|b_{k}(s)|\leq C_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq L.

In particular, we have the following result which was proved in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
:

Lemma 2.14

(Linearization of (
2.48
) around (
2.50
))
.

Let

b_{k}(s)=b_{k}^{e}(s)+\frac{\mathcal{U}_{k}(s)}{s^{k}},\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell,

(2.51)

and note
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{U}_{\ell})
. Then, for
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
,

(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=\frac{1}{s^{k+1}}\left[s(\mathcal{U}_{k})_{s}-(A_{\ell}\mathcal{U})_{k}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{U}|^{2})\right],

(2.52)

and

(b_{\ell})_{s}+(2\ell-\gamma)b_{1}b_{\ell}=\frac{1}{s^{k+1}}\left[s(\mathcal{U}_{\ell})_{s}-(A_{\ell}\mathcal{U})_{\ell}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{U}|^{2})\right],

(2.53)

where

A_{\ell}=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}\quad\text{with}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{1,1}&=\frac{\gamma(\ell-1)}{2\ell-\gamma}-(2-\gamma)c_{1},\\
a_{i,i}&=\frac{\gamma(\ell-i)}{2\ell-\gamma},\quad 2\leq i\leq\ell,\\
a_{i,i+1}&=1,\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1,\\
a_{1,i}&=-(2i-\gamma)c_{i},\quad 2\leq i\leq\ell,\\
a_{i,j}&=0\quad\textup{ortherwise}\end{array}\right.

Moreover,
A_{\ell}
is diagonalizable:

A_{\ell}=P_{\ell}^{-1}D_{\ell}P_{\ell},\quad D_{\ell}=\textup{diag}\left\{-1,\frac{2\gamma}{2\ell-\gamma},\frac{3\gamma}{2\ell-\gamma},\cdots,\frac{\ell\gamma}{2\ell-\gamma}\right\}.

(2.54)

Proof.

Since we have an analogous system as the one in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
and the proof is essentially the same as written there, we kindly refer the reader to see Lemma 3.7 in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
for all details of the proof.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming technical results.

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We hope that the explanation of the strategy we give in this section will be reader friendly. We proceed in 3 subsections:

- In the first subsection, we give an equivalent formulation of the linearization of the problem in the setting (
1.30
).

- In the second subsection, we prepare the initial data and define the shrinking set
\mathcal{S}_{K}
(see Definition
3.2
) such that the solution trapped in this set satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

- In the third subsection, we give all arguments of the proof of the existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}
(Proposition
3.5
) assuming an important technical result (Proposition
3.6
) whose proof is left to the next section. Then we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Linearization of the problem.

Let
L\gg 1
be an integer and
s_{0}\gg 1
, we introduce the renormalized variables:

y=\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},\quad s=s_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)},

(3.1)

and the decomposition

u(r,t)=w(y,s)=\big(\tilde{Q}_{b(s)}+q\big)(y,s)=\big(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)}+q\big)\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},t\right),

(3.2)

where
\tilde{Q}_{b}
is constructed in Proposition
2.12
and the modulation parameters

\lambda(t)>0,\quad b(t)=(b_{1}(t),\cdots,b_{L}(t))

are determined from the
L+1
orthogonality conditions:

\left<q,\mathscr{L}^{k}\Phi_{M}\right>=0,\quad 0\leq k\leq L,

(3.3)

where
\Phi_{M}
is a fixed direction depending on some large constant
M
defined by

\Phi_{M}=\sum_{k=0}^{L}c_{k,M}\mathscr{L}^{k}(\chi_{M}\Lambda Q),

(3.4)

with

c_{0,M}=1,\quad c_{k,M}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_{j,M}\left<\chi_{M}\mathscr{L}^{j}(\chi_{M}\Lambda Q),T_{k}\right>}{\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q\right>},\quad 1\leq k\leq L.

(3.5)

Here,
\Phi_{M}
is build to ensure the nondegeneracy

\left<\Phi_{M},\Lambda Q\right>=\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q\right>\gtrsim M^{d-2\gamma},

(3.6)

and the cancellation

\left<\Phi_{M},T_{k}\right>=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_{j,M}\left<\mathscr{L}^{j}(\chi_{M}\Lambda Q),T_{k}\right>+c_{k,M}(-1)^{k}\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q\right>=0,

(3.7)

In particular, we have

\left<\mathscr{L}^{i}T_{k},\Phi_{M}\right>=(-1)^{k}\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q\right>\delta_{i,k},\quad 0\leq i,k\leq L.

(3.8)

From (
2.2
), we see that
q
satisfies the equation:

\partial_{s}q-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda q+\mathscr{L}q=-\tilde{\Psi}_{b}-\widehat{\textup{Mod}}+\mathcal{H}(q)-\mathcal{N}(q)\equiv\mathcal{F},

(3.9)

where

\widehat{\textup{Mod}}=-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}-\chi_{B_{1}}\textup{Mod},

(3.10)

and
\mathcal{H}
is the linear part given by

\mathcal{H}(q)=\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}\big[\cos(2Q)-\cos(2\tilde{Q}_{b})\big]q,

(3.11)

and
\mathcal{N}
is the purely nonlinear term

\mathcal{N}(q)=\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\big[\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b}+2q)-\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b})-2q\cos(2\tilde{Q}_{b})\big].

(3.12)

We also need to write the equation (
3.9
) in the original variables. To do so, let the rescaled linearized operator:

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=-\partial_{rr}-\frac{(d-1)}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}},

(3.13)

and the renormalized function

v(r,t)=q(y,s),\quad\partial_{t}v=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(t)}\left(\partial_{s}q-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda q\right)_{\lambda}

then from (
3.9
),
v
satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}v+\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}v=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda},\quad\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(r,t)=\mathcal{F}(y,s).

(3.14)

Note that

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathscr{L}.

3.2.
Preparation of the initial data.

We describe in this subsection the set of initial data
u_{0}
of the problem (
1.4
) as well as the initial data for
(b,\lambda)
leading to the blowup scenario of Theorem
1.1
. Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

\|u_{0}-Q\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\ll 1,\quad\text{for}\quad\frac{d}{2}\leq s\leq\Bbbk.

(3.15)

From the continuity of the flow and a standard argument, we can propagate the smallness assumption (
3.15
) on a small time interval
[0,t_{1})
. Hence, the decomposition (
3.2
),

u(r,t)=\left(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)}+q\right)\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},t\right),\quad\lambda(t)>0,\;b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L}),

(3.16)

can be defined uniquely on the interval

.

The existence of the decomposition (

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)})_{\lambda},\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{1}}(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)})_{\lambda},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{L}}(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)})_{\lambda}\right|_{\lambda=1,b=0}=(\Lambda Q,T_{1},\cdots,T_{L}),

which induces the nondegeneracy of the Jacobian

\left|\left<\frac{\partial}{\partial(\lambda,b_{j})}(\tilde{Q}_{b(t)})_{\lambda},\mathscr{L}^{i}\Phi_{M}\right>_{1\leq j\leq L,0\leq i\leq L}\right|_{\lambda=1,b=0}=\left|\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q\right>\right|^{L+1}\neq 0.

In fact, the decomposition (
3.16
) exists as long as
t<T
and
q
remains small in the energy topology. We now set up the shrinking set for the control of the parameters
(b,\lambda)
and the error
q
. To measure the regularity of the map we use the following coercive norms of

:

\mathscr{E}_{2k}=\int|\mathscr{L}^{k}q|^{2}\geq C(M)\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{m}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4(k-m)}}\quad\text{for}\quad\hbar+1\leq k\leq\Bbbk,

(3.17)

Our construction is build on a careful choice of the initial data for the modulation parameter
b
and the radiation
q
at time

. In particular, we will choose it in the following way:

Definition 3.1

.

Given
\eta
and
\delta
as in (

) and (

\mathcal{V}=P_{\ell}\mathcal{U},

(3.18)

where

Remark 3.4

The introduction of the high Sobolev norm

\left|\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right|+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\left|(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}\right|\lesssim C(M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+l.o.t,

(3.23)

which is computed thanks to the

orthogonality conditions (

) (see lemmas

and

below).

3.3.
Existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
and conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

We claim the following proposition:

Proposition 3.5

(Existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
)
.

There exists
K_{1}\geq 1
such that for
K\geq K_{1}
, there exists
s_{0,1}(K)
such that for all
s_{0}\geq s_{0,1}
, there exists initial data for the unstable modes

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0}))\in\left[-s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)},s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\right]^{\ell-1},

such that the corresponding solution
(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

Let us briefly give the proof of Proposition
3.5
. Let us consider
K\geq 1
and
s_{0}\geq 1
and
(b(s_{0}),q(s_{0}))
as in Definition
3.1
. We introduce the exit time

s_{*}=s_{*}(b(s_{0}),q(s_{0}))=\sup\{s\geq s_{0}\;\text{such that}\;(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)\},

and assume that for any choice of

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0}))\in\left[-s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)},s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\right]^{\ell-1},

the exit time
s_{*}<+\infty
and look for a contradiction. By the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s_{*})
, at least one of the inequalities in that definition is an equality. Owing the following proposition, this can happens only for the components
(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*}))
. Precisely, we have the following result which is the heart of our analysis:

Proposition 3.6

(Control of
(b(s),q(s))
in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
by
(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s))
)
.

There exists
K_{2}\geq 1
such that for each
K\geq K_{2}
, there exists
s_{0,2}(K)\geq 1
such that for all
s_{0}\geq s_{0,2}(k)
, the following holds: Given the initial data at
s=s_{0}
as in Definition
3.1
, if
(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, with
(b(s_{1}),q(s_{1}))\in\partial\mathcal{S}_{K}(s_{1})
for some
s_{1}\geq s_{0}
, then:

(i)
(Reduction to a finite dimensional problem)

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{1}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{1}))\in\partial\left[-\frac{K}{s_{1}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}},\frac{K}{s_{1}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}\right]^{\ell-1}.

(ii)
(Transverse crossing)

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left|s^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\mathcal{V}_{i}(s)\right|^{2}\right)_{\big|_{s=s_{1}}}>0.

Let us assume Proposition
3.6
and continue the proof of Proposition
3.5
. From part
(i)
of Proposition
3.6
, we see that

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*}))\in\partial\left[-\frac{K}{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}},\frac{K}{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}\right]^{\ell-1},

and the following mapping

\displaystyle\Upsilon:[-1,1]^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\partial\left([-1,1]^{\ell-1}\right)

\displaystyle s_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\big(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\to\frac{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}{K}\big(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*})\big)

is well defined. Applying the transverse crossing property given in part
(ii)
of Proposition
3.6
, we see that
(b(s),q(s))
leaves
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
at
s=s_{0}
, hence,
s_{*}=s_{0}
. This is a contradiction since
\Upsilon
is the identity map on the boundary sphere and it can not be a continuous retraction of the unit ball. This concludes the proof of Proposition
3.5
, assuming that Proposition
3.6
holds.

Conclusion of Theorem
1.1
assuming Proposition
3.6

. From Proposition
3.5
, we know that there exist initial data
(b(s_{0}),q(s_{0}))
such that

(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)\quad\text{for all}\quad s\geq s_{0}.

From (
4.58
), (
4.59
), we have

\displaystyle-\lambda\lambda_{t}=c(u_{0})\lambda^{\frac{2\ell-\gamma}{\ell}}\left[1+o(1)\right],

which yields

-\lambda^{1-\frac{2\ell-\gamma}{\ell}}\lambda_{t}=c(u_{0})(1+o(1)).

We easily conclude that
\lambda
vanishes in finite time
T=T(u_{0})<+\infty
with the following behavior near the blowup time:

\lambda(t)=c(u_{0})(1+o(1))(T-t)^{\frac{\ell}{\gamma}},

which is the conclusion of item
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
.

For the control of the Sobolev norms, we observe from (
B.3
) and Definition
3.2
that

\forall\hbar+2\leq m\leq\Bbbk,\quad\int|\partial_{y}^{2m}q|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m}\to 0\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty.

From the relation
d=4\hbar+4\delta+2\gamma
, we deduce that

\forall\sigma\in\left[d/2+3,2\Bbbk\right],\quad\int|\nabla^{\sigma}q|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty,

which yields
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
.

4.
Reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one.

In this section, we aim at proving Proposition
3.6
which is the heart of our analysis. We proceed in three separate subsections:

- In the first subsection, we derive the laws for the parameters
(b,\lambda)
thanks to the orthogonality condition (
3.3
) and the coercivity of the powers of
\mathscr{L}
.

- In the second subsection, we prove the main monotonicity tools for the control of the infinite dimensional part of the solution. In particular, we derive a suitable Lyapunov functional for the
\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}

- In the third subsection, we conclude the proof of Proposition

thanks to the identities obtained in the first two parts.

4.1.

We derive here the modulation equations for

) and the coercivity of the powers of

. Let us start with elementary estimates relating to the fixed direction

Lemma 4.1

(Estimate for
\Phi_{M}
)
.

Given
\Phi_{M}
as defined in (
3.4
), we have the followings:

|c_{k,M}|\lesssim M^{2k}\quad\text{for all}\;\;1\leq k\leq L,

\int|\Phi_{M}|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma},\quad\int|\mathscr{L}\Phi_{M}|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma-4}.

Proof.

Arguing by induction, we assume that

|c_{j,M}|\lesssim M^{2j},\quad 1\leq j\leq k.

Using the fact that
\mathscr{L}^{j}T_{i}
is admissible of degree
(\max\{0,i-j\},i-j)
, we estimate from the definition (
3.5
),

\displaystyle|c_{k+1,M}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M^{d-2\gamma}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}M^{2j}\int|\chi_{M}\Lambda Q\mathscr{L}^{j}(T_{k+1})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M^{d-2\gamma}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}M^{2j}\int_{y\leq M}\frac{y^{d-1}}{y^{\gamma}}y^{2(k+1-j)-\gamma}dy\lesssim M^{2(k+1)}.

Using the estimate for
c_{k,M}
yields

\int|\Phi_{M}|^{2}\lesssim\int|\chi_{M}\Lambda Q|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{L}|c_{j,M}|^{2}\int|\mathscr{L}^{j}(\chi_{M}\Lambda Q)|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma-4},

and

\int|\mathscr{L}\Phi_{M}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{L}|c_{j,M}|^{2}\int|\mathscr{L}^{j+1}(\chi_{M}\Lambda Q)|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma}.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\left|(b_{k})_{s}+(2k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}-b_{k+1}\right|\left|\left<\sum_{j=k+1}^{L+2}\frac{\partial\tilde{S}_{j}}{\partial b_{k}},\mathscr{L}^{L}(\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q)\right>\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left(C(M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right)b_{1}\int_{B_{0}\leq y\leq 2B_{0}}\frac{y^{2L-\gamma}y^{d-1}}{y^{2L+\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\lesssim\left(C(M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right)b_{1}B_{0}^{d-2\gamma}.

The equation (
4.8
) follows by gathering all the above estimates into (
4.9
), dividing both sides of (
4.9
) by
(-1)^{L}\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>
and using the relation (
1.18
). This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

4.2.
Monotonicity.

We derive in this subsection the main monotonocity formula for
\mathscr{E}_{2k}
for
\hbar+1\leq k\leq\Bbbk
. We claim the following which is the heart of this paper:

Proposition 4.4

(Lyapounov monotonicity for the high Sobolev norm)
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left[\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right],

(4.12)

and for
\hbar+2\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}{\lambda^{4m-d}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(b_{1})\right]\right\}\leq\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4m-d+2}}\left[b_{1}^{m-\hbar-1+(1-\delta)-C\eta}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}+b_{1}^{2(m-\hbar-1)+2(1-\delta)-C\eta}\right],

(4.13)

Proof.

The proof uses some ideas developed in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
and
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
. Because the proof of (
4.13
) follows exactly the same lines as for (
4.12
), we only deal with the proof of (
4.12
). Let us start the proof of (
4.12
).

Step 1: Suitable derivatives and energy identity.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the suitable derivatives of
q
and
v
as follows:

q_{2k}=\mathscr{L}^{k}q,\quad q_{2k+1}=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k}q,\quad v_{2k}=\mathscr{L}^{k}_{\lambda}v,\quad v_{2k+1}=\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}^{k}_{\lambda}v,

(4.14)

where
q=q(y,s)
and
v=v(r,t)
satisfy (
3.9
) and (
3.14
) respectively, the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}
are defined by (
1.22
) and (
3.13
),
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
are the first order operators defined by (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), and

\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f=-\partial_{r}f+\frac{V_{\lambda}}{r}f,\quad\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\partial_{r}(r^{d-1}f)+\frac{V_{\lambda}}{r}f,

with
V=\Lambda\log\Lambda Q
admitting the asymptotic behaviors as in (
2.8
).

With the notation (
4.14
), we note that

q_{2k+1}=\mathscr{A}q_{2k},\quad q_{2k+2}=\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{2k+1},\quad v_{2k+1}=\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}v_{2k},\quad v_{2k+2}=\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}v_{2k+1}.

Recall from Lemma
2.2
, we have the following factorization:

\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{A}^{*}\mathscr{A},\quad\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{A}^{*},\quad\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda},\quad\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda},

where

\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=-\partial_{yy}-\frac{d-1}{y}\partial_{y}+\frac{\tilde{Z}}{y^{2}},

(4.15)

and

\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}=-\partial_{rr}-\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\tilde{Z}_{\lambda}}{r^{2}},

(4.16)

with
\tilde{Z}
expressed in terms of
V
as in (
2.13
).

We commute equation (
3.14
) with
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}
and use the notation (
4.14
) to derive

\partial_{t}v_{2\Bbbk-2}+\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-2}=[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v+\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right).

(4.17)

Now commuting this equation with
\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}
yields

\partial_{t}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}=\frac{\partial_{t}V_{\lambda}}{r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}+\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v+\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right),

(4.18)

Since
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathscr{L}
, we then have

\mathscr{L}^{k}_{\lambda}v=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2k}}\mathscr{L}^{k}q,\quad\text{hence,}\quad\int|\mathscr{L}^{k}_{\lambda}v|^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{4k-d}}\int|\mathscr{L}^{k}q|^{2}.

Using the definition (
4.16
) of
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}
and an integration by parts, we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int|\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}v|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle=\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\partial_{t}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{\partial_{t}(\tilde{Z}_{\lambda})}{r^{2}}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle=\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\partial_{t}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+b_{1}\int\frac{(\Lambda\tilde{Z})_{\lambda}}{2\lambda^{2}r^{2}}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\int\frac{(\Lambda\tilde{Z})_{\lambda}}{2\lambda^{2}r^{2}}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}.

Using the definition (
2.7
) of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
and an integration by parts together with the definition (
2.13
) of
\tilde{Z}
, we write

\displaystyle\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{\Lambda V}{y}q_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{\Lambda V(2V+d)-\Lambda^{2}V}{2y^{2}}q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{\Lambda\tilde{Z}}{2y^{2}}q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}=\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda\tilde{Z})_{\lambda}}{2\lambda^{2}r^{2}}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}.

From (
4.17
), we write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}\right)v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-2}+\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\partial_{t}v_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\left[-\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v+\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)\right].

Gathering all the above identities and using (
4.18
) yields the energy identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\right)+2\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right\}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=-\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\int\frac{(\Lambda\tilde{Z})_{\lambda}}{2\lambda^{2}r^{2}}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}-\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}\right)v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-2}+\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\left[[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v+\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\int\left(\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right)\left[\frac{\partial_{t}V_{\lambda}}{r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}+\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v+\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)\right].

We now estimate all terms in (
4.19
). The proof uses the coercivity estimate given in Lemma
A.5
. In particular, we shall apply Lemma
A.5
with

to have the estimate

\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\gtrsim\int\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|q_{2m}|^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4(\Bbbk-1-m)})}+\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int\frac{|q_{2m+1}|^{2}}{y^{6}(1+y^{4(\Bbbk-2-m)})}.

(4.20)

Step 2: Control of the lower order quadratic terms.
Let us start with the second term in the left hand side of (
4.19

2.8
) and (
2.13

|\Lambda\tilde{Z}(y)|+|\Lambda V(y)|\lesssim\frac{y^{2}}{1+y^{4}},\quad\forall y\in[0,+\infty).

4.20

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\int\frac{\Lambda V}{y}q_{2\Bbbk-1}q_{2\Bbbk-2}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\left(\int\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-2}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

Using (
4.21
), (
4.1
) and (
4.20
), we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\int\frac{(\Lambda\tilde{Z})_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{\Lambda\tilde{Z}}{y^{2}}q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-1}}{y^{2}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

For the third term in the right hand side of (
4.19

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+4\int\left(\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}\right)^{2}v^{2}_{2\Bbbk-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{4b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{|\Lambda V|^{2}}{y^{2}}q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-2}

A direct computation yields the round bound

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4}}(|\Lambda V|+|\Lambda^{2}V|).

4.21

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int\left(\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right)\frac{\partial_{t}V_{\lambda}}{r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{Cb_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{|\Lambda V|^{2}}{y^{2}}q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{4}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{Cb_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v\right|+\left|\int\left(\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}+\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\right)\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}]v\right|

(4.22)

whose proof is left to Appendix

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\Big[1+\mathcal{O}(b_{1})\Big]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{Cb_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right).

(4.23)

Step 3: Further use of dissipation.
We aim at estimating all terms in the right hand side of (
4.23

), (
4.20
) and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we write

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\int\frac{q_{2\Bbbk-1}^{2}}{y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{b_{1}(\Lambda V)_{\lambda}}{\lambda^{2}r}v_{2\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{\Lambda V}{y}q_{2\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\int\frac{q_{2\Bbbk-2}^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the last term in (

) and (

We now write

\displaystyle\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\int\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}(\partial_{s}\xi_{L})+\int\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\mathcal{F}_{0}+\int\tilde{\mathscr{L}}q_{2\Bbbk-1}\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\left\|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\mathcal{F}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Injecting all these bounds into (

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-\frac{1}{8}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{Cb_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}(\partial_{s}\xi_{L})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\left[\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{C}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\left\|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\mathcal{F}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

(4.26)

Step 4: Estimates for
\tilde{\Psi}_{b}
term.
Recall from (
2.44
) that we already have the following estimate for

:

\left\|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\tilde{\Psi}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

(4.27)

Step 5: Estimates for
\widehat{\text{Mod}}
term.
We claim the following:

\displaystyle\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\widehat{\text{Mod}}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\widehat{\text{Mod}}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right),

(4.28)

\displaystyle\left(\int\left|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\widetilde{\text{Mod}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim b_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right),

(4.29)

where

\widetilde{\text{Mod}}=\widehat{\text{Mod}}+\partial_{s}\xi_{L}.

Let us prove (
4.28
). We only deal with the first term since the second term is estimated similarly. We recall from (
3.10
) the definition of
\widehat{\text{Mod}}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\text{Mod}}=-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\big[(b_{i})_{s}+(2i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{i}-b_{i+1}\big]\left(\tilde{T}_{i}+\sum_{j=i+1}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\chi_{B_{1}}\right),

where
\tilde{Q}_{b}
is defined as in (
2.41
) and we know from Lemma
2.9
that
T_{i}
is admissible of degree
(i,i)
and from Proposition
2.11
that
S_{j}
is homogeneous of degree
(j,j-1,j)
.

Since
|b_{j}|\lesssim b_{1}^{j}
and
\mathscr{L}\Lambda Q=0
, we use Lemma
2.8
to estimate

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{L}b_{i}^{2}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\Lambda\tilde{T}_{i}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}+\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\Lambda\tilde{S}_{i}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{L}b_{1}^{2i}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}dy}{1+y^{4(\Bbbk-i)+2\gamma}}+\sum_{i=2}^{L+1}b_{1}^{2i}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}dy}{1+y^{4(\Bbbk-i+1)+2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+b_{1}^{2L+4}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}dy}{1+y^{4\hbar+2\gamma}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2},

where we used the algebra
4(\Bbbk-L)+2\gamma-d+1=5-4\delta>1
.

Using the cancellation
\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}T_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq L
and the admissibility of
T_{i}
, we estimate

\sum_{i=1}^{L}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}(\chi_{B_{1}}T_{i})|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{L}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{4(i-\Bbbk)-2\gamma+d-1}dy\lesssim b_{1}^{2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

Using the homogeneity of
S_{j}
, we estimate for
1\leq i\leq L
,

\sum_{j=i+1}^{L+2}\int\frac{1}{1+y^{4}}\left|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=i+1}^{L+2}b_{1}^{2(j-i)}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{4(j-1-\Bbbk)-2\gamma}y^{d-1}dy\lesssim b_{1}^{2},

provided that
\eta\leq\frac{1}{\delta}-1
.

The collection of the above bounds together with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) yields

\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\widehat{\text{Mod}}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right).

The same estimate holds for
\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\widehat{\text{Mod}}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
by following the same lines as above. This concludes the proof of (
4.28
).

We now prove (
4.29
). Let us write

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{Mod}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}+\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}\big[(b_{i})_{s}+(2i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{i}-b_{i+1}\big]\tilde{T}_{i}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\big[(b_{i})_{s}+(2i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{i}-b_{i+1}\big]\chi_{B_{1}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{L+2}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left[(b_{L})_{s}+(2i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}+\frac{d}{ds}\left\{\frac{\left<\mathscr{L}^{L}q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}{\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}\right\}\right]\tilde{T}_{L}+\frac{\left<\mathscr{L}^{L}q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}{\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}\partial_{s}\tilde{T}_{L}.

Proceeding as for the proof of (
4.28
) yields the estimate

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\Lambda\tilde{Q}_{b}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\tilde{T}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sum_{j=i+1}^{L+2}\int\left|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2},

and

\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\tilde{T}_{L}|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

(4.30)

From (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we have the bound

\left|\frac{\left<\mathscr{L}^{L}q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}{\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>}\right|\lesssim B_{0}^{2(1-\delta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}=b_{1}^{-(1-\delta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}.

(4.31)

We also have

\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}(\partial_{s}\chi_{B_{1}}T_{L})|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\int_{B_{1}\leq y\leq 2B_{1}}\frac{y^{d-1}dy}{y^{4(\Bbbk-L)+2\gamma}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

The collection of the above bounds together with Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
yields

\displaystyle\left(\int|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\widetilde{\text{Mod}}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+b_{1}^{(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}b_{1}^{\delta}\left(C(M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+b_{1}^{-(1-\delta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}b_{1}b_{1}^{(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim b_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right),

which is the conclusion of (
4.29
).

Injecting the estimates (
4.27
), (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) into (
4.26
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d}}\Big[1+\mathcal{O}(b_{1})\Big]\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq-\frac{1}{8}\int|\tilde{\mathscr{L}}_{\lambda}v_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left(\frac{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}{M^{2\delta}}+b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}+b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{b_{1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left[\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\left\|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{F}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}(\partial_{s}\xi_{L}).

(4.32)

Step 6: Estimates for the linear small term
\mathcal{H}(q)
.
We claim the following

\int|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{H}(q)|^{2}+\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{H}(q)|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}+\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{H}(q)|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

(4.33)

We only deal with the estimate for the first term because the last two terms are estimated similarly. Let us rewrite from (
3.11
) the definition of
\mathcal{H}(q)
,

\mathcal{H}(q)=\Phi q\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi=\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}\left[\cos(2Q)-\cos(2Q+2\tilde{\Theta}_{b})\right],

where

\tilde{\Theta}_{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}b_{i}\tilde{T}_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}\tilde{S}_{i}(b,y).

From the asymptotic behavior of
Q
given in (
2.4
), the admissibility of
T_{i}
and the homogeneity of
S_{i}
, we deduce that
\Phi
is a regular function both at the origin and at infinity. We then apply the Leibniz rule (
C.2
) with
k=\Bbbk-1
and
\phi=\Phi
to write

\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{H}(q)=\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\Big[q_{2m+1}\Phi_{2\Bbbk-1,2m+1}+q_{2m}\Phi_{2\Bbbk-1,2m}\Big],

where
\Phi_{2\Bbbk-1,i}
with
0\leq i\leq 2\Bbbk-1
are defined by the recurrence relation given in Lemma
C.1
. In particular, we have the following estimate

|\Phi_{k,i}|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{1+y^{\gamma+(k-i)}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{1+y^{1+k-i}},\quad\forall k\geq 1,\;\;0\leq i\leq k.

Hence, we estimate from (
4.20
),

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{H}(q)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\left[\int|q_{2m+1}\Phi_{2\Bbbk-1,2m+1}|^{2}+\int|q_{2m}\Phi_{2\Bbbk-1,2m}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\left[\int\frac{|q_{2m+1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2+2(2\Bbbk-1-2m-1)}}+\int\frac{|q_{2m}|^{2}}{1+y^{2+2(2\Bbbk-1-2m)}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\left[\int\frac{|q_{2m+1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2+4(\Bbbk-1-m)}}+\int\frac{|q_{2m}|^{2}}{1+y^{4+4(\Bbbk-1-m)}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.33
).

Step 7: Estimates for the nonlinear term
\mathcal{N}(q)
.
This is the most delicate point in the proof of (
4.12
). We claim the following

\int|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(4.34)

\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}}{1+y^{2}}+\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}}{1+y^{4}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(4.35)

provided that
\eta
and
1/L
are small enough. We only deal with the proof of (
4.34
) since the same proof holds for (
4.35
).

-
Control for
y<1
.
Let us rewrite from (
3.12
) the definition of
\mathcal{N}(q)
,

\mathcal{N}(q)=\frac{q^{2}}{y}\Phi\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi=\left[-\frac{(d-1)}{y}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b}+2\tau q)d\tau\right].

From (
B.2
) and the admissibility of
T_{i}
, we write

\frac{q^{2}}{y}=\frac{1}{y}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk}c_{i}T_{i}(y)+r_{q}(y)\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\tilde{c}_{i}y^{2i+1}+\tilde{r}_{q}\quad\text{for}\;\;y<1,

(4.36)

where

|\tilde{c}_{i}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},\quad|\partial^{j}_{y}\tilde{r}_{q}(y)|\lesssim y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2\Bbbk-1,\;\;y<1.

Let
\tau\in[0,1]
and

v_{\tau}=\tilde{Q}_{b}+\tau q,

we obtain from Proposition
2.11
and the expansion (
B.2
),

v_{\tau}=\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\hat{c}_{i}y^{2i+1}+\hat{r}_{q},

with

|\hat{c}_{i}|\lesssim 1,\quad|\partial_{y}^{j}\hat{r}_{q}|\lesssim y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk},\;\;0\leq j\leq 2\Bbbk-1,\;\;y<1.

Together with the Taylor expansion of
\sin(x)
at
x=0
, we write

\Phi(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\bar{c}_{i}y^{2i}+\bar{r}_{q},

(4.37)

with

|\bar{c}_{i}|\lesssim 1,\quad|\partial_{y}^{j}\bar{r}_{q}|\lesssim y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-1-j}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2\Bbbk-1,\quad y<1.

From (
4.36
) and (
4.37
), we have the expansion of
\mathcal{N}
near the origin,

\mathcal{N}(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\hat{\tilde{c}}_{i}y^{2i+1}+\hat{\tilde{r}}_{q},

with

|\hat{\tilde{c}}_{i}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},\quad|\partial_{y}^{j}\hat{\tilde{r}}_{q}|\lesssim y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2\Bbbk-1,\;\;y<1.

From the definition of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
(see (
2.6
) and (
2.7
)), one can check that for
y<1
,

|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\hat{\tilde{r}}_{q}|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{2\Bbbk-1}\frac{\partial_{y}^{i}\hat{\tilde{r}}_{q}}{y^{2\Bbbk-1-i}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\sum_{i=0}^{2\Bbbk-1}\frac{y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-i}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}}{y^{2\Bbbk-1-i}}\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

Note from the asymptotic behavior (
2.8
) of
V
that
\mathscr{A}(y)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2})
for
y<1
, which implies

\left|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\hat{\tilde{c}}_{i}y^{2i+1}\right)\right|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}|\hat{\tilde{c}}_{i}|y^{2}\lesssim y^{2}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

We then conclude

\int_{y<1}|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}^{2}\int_{y<1}y|\ln y|^{2\Bbbk}dy\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

-
Control for
y\geq 1
.
Let us rewrite from the definition of
\mathcal{N}(q)
,

\mathcal{N}(q)=Z^{2}\psi,\quad Z=\frac{q}{y},\quad\psi=-(d-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)\sin(2\tilde{Q}_{b}+2\tau q)d\tau.

(4.38)

Note from the definitions of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k}f|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{2k+1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}f|}{y^{2k+1-i}},

from which and the Leibniz rule, we write

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}|\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2\Bbbk-1}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}\mathcal{N}(q)|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2k-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2\Bbbk-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}Z^{2}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2k-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{2\Bbbk-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{i}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2k-2}}.

We aim at using the pointwise estimate (
B.5
) to prove that for
0\leq k\leq 2\Bbbk-1
,
0\leq i\leq k
and
0\leq m\leq i
,

A_{k,i,m}:=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2k-2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(4.39)

which concludes the proof of (
4.34
).

To prove (
4.39
), we distinguish in 3 cases:

-
The initial case:
k=0
.
Since
0\leq m\leq i\leq k
, then
k=i=m=0
. Although this is the simplest case, it gives us a basic idea to handle other cases. From (
4.38
), it is obvious to see that
|\psi|
is uniformly bounded. We write

\displaystyle A_{0,0,0}=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|q|^{4}|\psi|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk+2}}y^{d-1}dy\lesssim\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}\frac{|q|^{4}}{y^{4\Bbbk+3-d}}dy+\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{|q|^{4}}{y^{4\Bbbk+3-d}}dy.

Using (
B.5
), Definition
3.2
,
b_{1}\sim\frac{1}{s}
and the fact that
d=4\hbar+2\gamma+4\delta
(see (
1.18
)), we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}\frac{|q|^{4}}{y^{4\Bbbk+3-d}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|q|^{2}}{y^{2(2\Bbbk-1)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y>1)}\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|q|^{2}}{y^{2(2\ell+2\hbar+3)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y>1)}\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}y^{4\ell+5-4\delta-2\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{2(\ell+\hbar+2)}B_{0}^{4\ell+6-4\delta-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\lesssim Kb_{1}^{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}b_{1}^{2(\ell+1)+2(1-\delta)-K\eta}b_{1}^{-2\ell-3+2\delta+\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim Kb_{1}^{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}b_{1}^{1+\gamma-K\eta}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

For the integral on the domain
y\geq B_{0}
, let us write

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{|q|^{4}}{y^{4\Bbbk+3-d}}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|q|^{2}}{y^{2(2\Bbbk-2\ell-1)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y>1)}\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|q|^{2}}{y^{2(2\ell+2\hbar+1)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y>1)}\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{dy}{y^{4\delta+2\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2(\Bbbk-\ell)}\mathscr{E}_{2(\ell+\hbar+1)}B_{0}^{2-4\delta-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{2(\Bbbk-\ell-\hbar-1)+2(1-\delta)-K\eta}b_{1}^{2\ell+2(1-\delta)-K\eta}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}b_{1}^{1+\gamma-(K+2(1-\delta))\eta}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.39
) when
k=i=m=0
.

-
Case II:
k\geq 1
and
k=i
. We first use the Leibniz rule to write

\forall l\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{l}Z|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{l}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}}{y^{2+2l-2j}},

(4.40)

from which,

\displaystyle A_{k,k,m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{k-m}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}y^{d-1}dy.

We claim that for all
(j,l)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and
1\leq j+l\leq 2\Bbbk-1
,

B_{j,l,0}:=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}y^{d-1}dy\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+1+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)+\frac{(\gamma-1)}{2}},

(4.41)

which immediately follows (
4.39
) for the case when
k=i
.

To prove (
4.41
), we proceed as for the case
k=0
by splitting the integral in two parts as follows:

\displaystyle B_{j,l,0}

\displaystyle=\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+4\hbar+6}}y^{7-4\delta-2\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+4\hbar}}\frac{dy}{y^{4\delta+2\gamma-1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\left\|\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+4\hbar+6}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-4}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\left\|\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{4\Bbbk-2j-2l+4\hbar}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left\|\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{2J_{1}-2j+2J_{2}-2l}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-4}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\left\|\frac{\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}\big)\big(y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}\big)}{y^{2J_{3}-2j+2J_{4}-2l}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad:=B_{j,l,0,J_{1},J_{2}}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-4}+B_{j,l,0,J_{3},J_{4}}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-1},

where
J_{n}(n=1,2,3,4)
satisfy

J_{1}+J_{2}=2\Bbbk+2\hbar+3,\quad J_{3}+J_{4}=2\Bbbk+2\hbar.

We now estimate
B_{j,l,0,J_{1},J_{2}}
.

- If
l
is even, we take

J_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}l+2&\quad\text{if}\quad l\leq 2\Bbbk-4,\\
l&\quad\text{if}\quad l=2\Bbbk-2.\end{array}\right.

This gives

2\hbar+4\leq J_{2}\leq 2\Bbbk-2,\quad 2\hbar+5\leq J_{1}=2\Bbbk+2\hbar+3-J_{2}\leq 2\Bbbk-1.

Using (
B.5
), we have the estimate

\displaystyle B_{j,l,0,J_{1},J_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{j}q|^{2}}{y^{2J_{1}-2j}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\left\|\frac{y^{d-2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q|^{2}}{y^{2J_{2}-2l}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{J_{1}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{J_{2}}\mathscr{E}_{J_{2}+2}}.

- If
l
is odd, we simply take
J_{2}=l+1
which gives

Hence,

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{J_{1}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{J_{2}}\mathscr{E}_{J_{2}+2}}.

Recall from Definition (

b_{1}^{m-2\hbar-2+2(1-\delta)-K\eta}&\quad\text{for}\quad 2\hbar+2\ell+2\leq m\leq 2\Bbbk.\end{array}\right.

- If

- If

Similarly, one prove that

\displaystyle B_{j,l,0}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+2+4(1-\delta)-K\eta}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-4}+b_{1}^{2L-1+4(1-\delta)-K\eta}b_{1}^{2\delta+\gamma-1}

-
Part 1: Reduction to a finite dimensional problem.
Assume that for a given

large and an initial time

large, we have
(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for all

for some

), (

,

Appendix A
Coercivity of the adapted norms.

We give in this section the coercivity estimates for the operator
\mathscr{L}
as well as the iterates of
\mathscr{L}
under some suitable orthogonality condition. We first recall the standard Hardy type inequalities for the class of radially symmetric functions,

\mathcal{D}_{rad}=\{f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\;\text{with radial symmetry}\}.

For simplicity, we write

\int f:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(y)y^{d-1}dy.

and

D^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{m}&\quad\text{if}\;\;k=2m,\\
\partial_{y}\Delta^{m}&\quad\text{if}\;\;k=2m+1.\end{array}\right.

We have the following:

Lemma A.1

(Hardy type inequalities)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{rad}
, then

(i)
(Hardy near the origin)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}}\geq\frac{(d-2-2i)^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2+2i}}-C(d)f^{2}(1),\quad i=0,1,2.

(ii)
(Hardy away from zero for the non-critical exponent) Let
\alpha>0,\alpha\neq\frac{d-2}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2\alpha}}\geq\left(\frac{d-(2\alpha+2)}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2+2\alpha}}-C(\alpha,d)f^{2}(1).

(iii)
(Hardy away from zero for the critical exponent) Let
\alpha=\frac{d-2}{2}
, then

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2\alpha}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2+2\alpha}(1+\log y)^{2}}-C(d)f^{2}(1).

(iv)
(General weighted Hardy) For any
\mu>0
,
k\geq 2
be an integer and
1\leq j\leq k-1
,

\int\frac{|D^{j}f|^{2}}{1+y^{\mu+2(k-j)}}\lesssim_{j,\mu}\int\frac{|D^{k}f|^{2}}{1+y^{\mu}}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{1+y^{\mu+2k}}.

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
, Lemma B.1.
∎

From the Hardy type inequalities, we derive the following coercivity of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
:

Lemma A.2

(Weight coercivity of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
)
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
, there exists
c_{\alpha}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{rad}
:

\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}^{*}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}\geq c_{\alpha}\left(\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2\alpha})}\right),\quad i=0,1,2.

(A.1)

Proof.

We proceed into two steps:

- Step 1: Subcoercive estimate for
\mathscr{A}^{*}
:
We first prove the following subcoercive bound for
\mathscr{A}^{*}
: for
i=0,1,2
and
\alpha\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}^{*}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}\gtrsim\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2\alpha})}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}

\displaystyle\quad-f^{2}(1)-\int\frac{f^{2}}{1+y^{2i+2\alpha+4}}.

(A.2)

From the definition (
2.7
) of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
and the asymptotic of
V
given in (
2.8
), we use an integration by parts to estimate near the origin:

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{|\mathscr{A}^{*}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{1}{y^{2i}}\left|\partial_{y}f+\frac{d}{y}f+\mathcal{O}(|yf|)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}}+d\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{\partial_{y}(f^{2})}{y^{2i+1}}+d^{2}\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\gtrsim\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}}+(2+2i)d\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}}+df^{2}(1)+\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\gtrsim\int_{y\leq 1}\left(\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}}+\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}}\right)-\int_{y\leq 1}y^{2}f^{2}.

Away from the origin, we use (
2.8
) to estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\mathscr{A}^{*}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{1}{y^{2i+2\alpha}}\left(\partial_{y}f+\frac{d-1-\gamma}{y}f\right)^{2}-\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2\alpha+4}}.

We make the change of variable

of Lemma

to write

2.8
),

\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2p}}+\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2p+2}}\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2p}}.

The collection of the above bounds yields the subcoercivity (
A.6
).

- Step 2: Coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
:
Arguing as the proof of (
A.1
), we end up with the existence of
f_{\infty}\neq 0
such that

\int\frac{f_{\infty}^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2p})}\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}f_{\infty}=0.

Hence, from the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
, we have

f_{\infty}=\beta\Lambda Q\quad\text{for some $\beta\neq 0$.}

If
2i+2p>d-2\gamma-2
, we use the orthogonality condition to deduce that

0=\left<f_{\infty},\Phi_{M}\right>=\beta\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{M}\Lambda Q\right>,

thus,
\beta=0
. If
2i+2p\leq d-2\gamma-2
, we use the fact that
\Lambda Q\sim\frac{1}{y^{\gamma}}
as
y\to+\infty
to estimate

\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\Lambda Q|^{2}y^{d-1}dy}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2p})}\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}y^{d-1-2\gamma-2i-2p-2}dy\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}y^{-1}dy=+\infty,

which contradicts with the regularity of
f_{\infty}
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
A.3
.
∎

From the coercivities of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
, we claim the following coercivity for
\mathscr{L}
:

Lemma A.4

(Weighted coercivity of
\mathscr{L}
under a suitable orthogonality condition)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
i=0,1,2
and
M=M(k)
large enough, then there exists
c_{M,k}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{rad}
satisfying the orthogonality

\left<f,\Phi_{M}\right>=0\quad\text{if}\;\;2i+2k>d-2\gamma-4,

where
\Phi_{M}
is defined by (
3.4
),
\hbar
is given in (
1.18
), there holds:

\int\frac{|{\mathscr{L}}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}\geq c_{M,k}\int\left(\frac{|\partial_{yy}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}+\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}+\frac{|f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}\right).

(A.8)

and

\int\frac{|{\mathscr{L}}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}\geq c_{M,k}\int\left(\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k})}+\int\frac{|f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+4})}\right).

(A.9)

Proof.

We proceed into two steps:

-
Step 1: Subcoercivity of
\mathscr{L}
.
We apply Lemma
A.2
to
\mathscr{A}f
with
\alpha=k
and note that

\partial_{y}(\mathscr{A}f)=\mathscr{A}(\partial_{y}f)+\partial_{y}\left(\frac{V}{y}\right)f,

to write

\displaystyle\int\frac{|{\mathscr{L}}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k})}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}(\mathscr{A}f)|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}

(A.10)

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}(\mathscr{A}f)|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}

\displaystyle\qquad\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}+\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\partial_{y}f)|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}-\int\frac{|f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k})}.

(A.11)

Applying Lemma
A.3
to
f
with
p=k+1
and noting that the condition
|2(k+1)+2i-(d-2-2\gamma)|\neq 0
is always satisfied (if not, we have
d=4+2\sqrt{(k+1+i)^{2}+2}\not\in\mathbb{N}
), we have

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}

\displaystyle\quad-\left[f^{2}(1)+\int\frac{f^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+6}}\right].

We apply again Lemma
A.3
to
\partial_{y}f
with
p=k
to estimate

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\partial_{y}f)|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\partial_{yy}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k})}

\displaystyle\quad-\left[|\partial_{y}f(1)|^{2}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+4}}\right].

Injecting these bounds into (
A.11
) yields the subcoercive estimate for
\mathscr{L}
,

\displaystyle\int\frac{|{\mathscr{L}}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int\frac{|\partial_{yy}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}

\displaystyle\quad-\left[f^{2}(1)+|f_{y}(1)|^{2}+\int\frac{|f_{y}|^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+4}}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+6}}\right].

(A.12)

-
Step 2: Coercivity of
\mathscr{L}
.
We argue by contradiction. Assume that (
A.8
) does not hold. Up to a renormalization, there exists a a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{rad}
such that

\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}f_{n}|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}\leq\frac{1}{n},\quad\int\frac{|\partial_{yy}f_{n}|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k})}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f_{n}|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2k+2})}+\int\frac{|f_{n}|^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}=1.

(A.13)

This implies from (
A.12
),

f_{n}^{2}(1)+|\partial_{y}f_{n}(1)|^{2}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f_{n}|^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+4}}+\int\frac{f_{n}^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2k+2i+6})}\gtrsim 1.

(A.14)

From (
A.13
), the sequence
f_{n}
is bounded in
H^{2}_{loc}
. Hence, from a standard diagonal extraction argument, there exists
f_{\infty}\in H^{2}_{loc}
such that up to a subsequence,

f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{2}_{loc},

and from the local compactness of one dimensional Sobolev embeddings:

f_{n}\rightarrow f_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}_{loc},

and

f_{n}(1)\rightarrow f_{\infty}(1),\quad\partial_{y}f_{n}(1)\rightarrow\partial_{y}f_{\infty}(1).

This implies from (
A.13
) and (
A.14
),

f_{\infty}^{2}(1)+|\partial_{y}f_{\infty}(1)|^{2}+\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f_{\infty}|^{2}}{1+y^{2k+2i+4}}+\int\frac{f_{\infty}^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2k+2i+6})}\gtrsim 1,

which means that
f_{\infty}\neq 0
.
On the other hand, from (
A.13
) and the lower semi continuity of norms for the weak topology, we deduce that
f_{\infty}
is a non trivial function in the kernel of
\mathscr{L}
, namely that

\mathscr{L}f_{\infty}=0,

which follows

f_{\infty}=\mu\Gamma+\beta\Lambda Q,

where
\mu
and
\beta
two real numbers.

From (
A.13
) and the lower semicontinuity, we have

\int\frac{f_{\infty}^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}<+\infty.

Recall from (
2.16
) that
\Gamma\sim\frac{1}{y^{d-1}}
as
y\to 0
. This yields the estimate

\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}\gtrsim\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{dy}{y^{2i+2+d-1}}=+\infty,

hence,
\mu=0
.

From (
2.5
), we have
\Lambda Q\sim\frac{1}{y^{\gamma}}
as
y\to+\infty
. If
2i+2k\leq d-2\gamma-4
, we have

\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\Lambda Q|^{2}y^{d-1}dy}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2k+2})}\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}y^{d-1-2i-2k-4-2\gamma}dy\gtrsim\int_{y\geq 1}y^{-1}dy=+\infty,

hence,
\beta=0
. If
2i+2k>d-2\gamma-4
, we use the orthogonality condition to deduce

0=\left<f_{\infty},\Phi_{M}\right>=\beta\left<\Lambda Q,\chi_{M}\Lambda Q\right>,

which yields
\beta=0
, hence
f_{\infty}=0
. The contradiction then follows and the coercivity (
A.8
) is proved. The estimate (
A.9
) simply follows from (
A.8
) and (
A.10
). This finished the proof of Lemma
A.4
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove the coercivity of
{\mathscr{L}}^{k}
under a suitable orthogonality condition. We claim the following:

Lemma A.5

(Coercivity of the iterate of
\mathscr{L}
)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
M=M(k)
large enough, then there exists
c_{M,k}>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{D}_{rad}
satisfying the orthogonal condition

\left<f,\mathscr{L}^{m}\Phi_{M}\right>=0,\quad 0\leq m\leq k-\hbar,

where
\hbar
is defined as in (
1.18
), there holds:

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{2k+2}(f)=\int|\mathscr{L}^{k+1}f|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c_{M,k}\left\{\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{k}f)|^{2}}{y^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.\sum_{m=0}^{k}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{m}f|^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4(k-m)})}+\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{m}f)|^{2}}{y^{6}(1+y^{4(k-m-1)})}\right\}.

(A.15)

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. For
k=0
, we apply Lemma
A.2
to
\mathscr{A}f
with
i=0
and
\alpha=0
, then Lemma
A.3
to
f
with
i=1
and
p=0
to write

\mathscr{E}_{2}(f)=\int|\mathscr{L}f|^{2}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2}}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2}}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{4}}.

Note that we had to use the orthogonality condition
\left<f,\Phi_{M}\right>
when
\hbar=0
. In fact, the case
\hbar=0
only happens when
d=7
. In this case, the condition
2>d-2\gamma-2
is fulfilled when applying Lemma
A.2
with
i=1
and
p=0
.

We now assume the claim for
k\geq 0
and prove it for
k+1
. We have the orthogonality condition

\left<f,\mathscr{L}^{m}\Phi_{M}\right>=0,\quad 0\leq m\leq k+1-\hbar.

Let
g=\mathscr{L}f
, then we have

\left<g,\mathscr{L}^{m}\Phi_{M}\right>=0,\quad 0\leq m\leq k-\hbar.

By induction hypothesis, we write

\displaystyle\int|\mathscr{L}^{k+2}f|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int|\mathscr{L}^{k+1}g|^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{k}g)|^{2}}{y^{2}}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{m}g|^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4(k-m)})}+\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{m}g)|^{2}}{y^{6}(1+y^{4(k-m-1)})}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{k+1}f)|^{2}}{y^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{k+1}\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}^{m}f|^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4(k+1-m)})}+\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{|\mathscr{A}(\mathscr{L}^{m}f)|^{2}}{y^{6}(1+y^{4(k-m)})}.

Note that we have the orthogonality condition
\left<f,\Phi_{M}\right>=0
when
k\geq\hbar-1
. The case
k\leq\hbar-2
implies that

4+4k\leq 4+4\left(\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\delta\right)-8\leq d-2\gamma-4.

Hence, we use the coercivity bound (
A.9
) to derive

\int\frac{|\mathscr{L}f|^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4k})}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{6}(1+y^{4k})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{4}(1+y^{4k+4})},

which concludes the proof of Lemma
A.5
.
∎

Appendix B
Interpolation bounds.

We derive in this section interpolation bounds on
q
which are the consequence of the coercivity property given in Lemma
A.5
. We have the following:

Lemma B.1

(Interpolation bounds)
.

(i)
Weighted bounds for
q_{i}
: for
1\leq m\leq\Bbbk
,

\int|q_{2m}|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{2k-1}\int\frac{|q_{i}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{4m-2i-2})}\leq C(M)\mathscr{E}_{2m}.

(B.1)

(ii)
Development near the origin:

q=\sum_{i=1}^{\Bbbk}c_{i}T_{\Bbbk-i}+r_{q},

(B.2)

with bounds

|c_{i}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},

|\partial_{y}^{j}r_{q}|\lesssim y^{2\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln(y)|^{\Bbbk}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2\Bbbk-1,\;\;y<1.

(iii)
Bounds near the origin for
q_{i}
and
\partial_{y}^{i}q
: for
y\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle|q_{2i}|+|\partial_{y}^{2i}q|

\displaystyle\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq i\leq\Bbbk-1,

\displaystyle|q_{2i-1}|+|\partial_{y}^{2i-1}q|

\displaystyle\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq\Bbbk.

(iv)
Weighted bounds for
\partial_{y}^{i}q
: for
1\leq m\leq\Bbbk
,

\sum_{i=0}^{2m}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m}.

(B.3)

Moreover, let
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
2\leq i+j\leq 2\Bbbk
, then

\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{2j}}\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathscr{E}_{2m}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j=2m,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk,\\
\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2(m+1)}}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j=2m+1,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1.\end{array}\right.

(B.4)

(v)
Pointwise bound far away. Let
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq i+j\leq 2\Bbbk-1
, we have for
y\geq 1
,

\left|\frac{\partial_{y}^{i}q}{y^{j}}\right|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{d-2}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathscr{E}_{2m}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j+1=2m,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk,\\
\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2(m+1)}}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j=2m,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1.\end{array}\right.

(B.5)

Proof.

(i)
The estimate (
B.1
) directly follows from Lemma
A.5
.

(ii)
We claim that for
1\leq m\leq\Bbbk
,
q_{2\Bbbk-2m}
admits the Taylor expansion at the origin

q_{2\Bbbk-2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}T_{m-i}+r_{2m},

(B.6)

with the bounds

|c_{i,m}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},

|\partial_{y}^{j}r_{2m}|\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln(y)|^{m}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad 0\leq j\leq 2m-1,\;\;y<1,

The expansion (
B.2
) then follows from (
B.6
) with
m=\Bbbk
.

We proceed by induction in
m
for the proof of (
B.6
). For
m=1
, we write from the definition (
2.7
) of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
,

r_{1}(y)=q_{2\Bbbk-1}(y)=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}q_{2\Bbbk}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx+\frac{d_{1}}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}.

Note from (
B.1
) that
\int\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}
and from (
2.5
) that
\Lambda Q\sim y
as
y\to 0
, we deduce that
d_{1}=0
. Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we derive the pointwise estimate

|r_{1}(y)|\leq\frac{1}{y^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{y}|q_{2\Bbbk}|^{2}x^{d-1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{y}x^{2}x^{d-1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad y<1.

We remark that there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|q_{2\Bbbk-1}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y\leq 1}|q_{2\Bbbk-1}|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

We then define

r_{2}(y)=-\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{r_{1}}{\Lambda Q}dx,

and obtain from the pointwise estimate of
r_{1}
,

|r_{2}(y)|\lesssim yy^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\int_{a}^{y}\frac{dx}{x}\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}|\ln(x)|\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad y<1.

By construction and the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
, we have

\mathscr{A}r_{2}=r_{1}=q_{2\Bbbk-1},\quad\mathscr{L}r_{2}=\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{2\Bbbk-1}=q_{2\Bbbk}=\mathscr{L}q_{2\Bbbk-2}.

Recall that
\text{span}(\mathscr{L})=\{\Lambda Q,\Gamma\}
where
\Gamma
admits the singular behavior (
2.16
). From (
B.1
), we have
\int\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-2}|^{2}}{y^{4}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}<+\infty
. This implies that there exists
c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

q_{2\Bbbk-2}=c_{2}\Lambda Q+r_{2}.

Moreover, there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|q_{2\Bbbk-2}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{|y|\leq 1}|q_{2\Bbbk-2}|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk},

which follows

|c_{2}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad|q_{2\Bbbk-2}|\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}|\ln(y)|\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad y<1.

Since
\mathscr{A}r_{2}=r_{1}
, we then write from the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
,

|\partial_{y}r_{2}|\lesssim|r_{1}|+\left|\frac{r_{2}}{y}\right|\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}|\ln(y)|\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad y<1.

This concludes the proof of (
B.6
) for
m=1
.

We now assume that (
B.6
) holds for
m\geq 1
and prove it for
m+1
. The term
r_{2m}
is built as follows:

r_{2m-1}=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}r_{2m-2}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx,\quad r_{2m}=-\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{r_{2m-1}}{\Lambda Q}dx,\quad a\in(1/2,1).

We now use the induction hypothesis to estimate

\displaystyle|r_{2m+1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}r_{2m}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{d}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\int_{0}^{y}x^{2m+\frac{d}{2}}|\ln(x)|^{m}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\int_{0}^{y}|\ln(x)|^{m}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}+1}|\ln(y)|^{m}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}.

Here we used the following identity

I_{m}=\int_{0}^{y}[\ln(x)]^{m}dx\lesssim y|\ln(y)|^{m},\quad m\geq 1,\;\;y<1.

Indeed, we have
I_{1}=\int_{0}^{y}\ln(x)dx=y\ln(y)-y\lesssim y|\ln(y)|
for
y<1
. Assume the claim for
m\geq 1
, we use an integration by parts to estimate for
m+1

\displaystyle I_{m+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y}[\ln(x)]^{m}(x\ln(x)-x)^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle\quad=y[\ln(y)]^{m+1}-y[\ln(y)]^{m}-m(I_{m}-I_{m-1})\lesssim y|\ln(y)|^{m+1}.

Using an integration by parts yields
\int_{a}^{y}\frac{[\ln(x)]^{m}}{x}dx=\frac{[\ln(y)]^{m+1}-[\ln(a)]^{m+1}}{m+1}
. Hence, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|r_{2m+2}|=\left|\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{r_{2m+1}}{\Lambda Q}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}+2}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\int_{a}^{y}\frac{|\ln(x)|^{m}}{x}dx

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}+2}|\ln(y)|^{m+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}.

By construction, we have

\mathscr{A}r_{2m+2}=r_{2m+1},\quad\mathscr{L}r_{2m+2}=r_{2m}.

From the induction hypothesis and the definition (
2.19
) of
T_{k}
, we write

\mathscr{L}q_{2\Bbbk-2(m+1)}=q_{2\Bbbk-2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}T_{m-i}+r_{2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}\mathscr{L}T_{m+1-i}+\mathscr{L}r_{2m+2}.

The singularity (
2.16
) of
\Gamma
at the origin and the bound
\int_{y\leq 1}\frac{|q_{2\Bbbk-2(m+1)}|^{2}}{y^{4}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}
allows us to deduce that

q_{2\Bbbk-2(m+1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}T_{m+1-i}+c_{2m+2}\Lambda Q+r_{2m+2}.

From (
B.1
), we see that there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|q_{2\Bbbk-2(m+1)}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y\leq 1}|q_{2\Bbbk-2(m+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

Together with the induction hypothesis
|c_{i,m}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}
and the pointwise estimate on
r_{2m+2}
, we get the bound
|c_{2m+2}|\leq\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}
.

A brute force computation using the definitions of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
and the asymptotic behavior (
2.8
) ensure that for any function
f
,

\partial_{y}^{j}f=\sum_{i=0}^{j}P_{i,j}f_{i},\quad|P_{i,j}|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{j-i}},

(B.7)

and we estimate

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}^{j}r_{2m+2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{|r_{2m+2-i}|}{y^{j-i}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{y^{2m+2-i-\frac{d}{2}}|\ln(y)|^{m+1}}{y^{j-i}}\lesssim y^{2m+2-\frac{d}{2}-j}|\ln(y)|^{m+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}.

This concludes the proof of (
B.6
) as well as (
B.2
).

(iii)
The proof of
(iii)
directly follows from (
B.6
).

(iv)
We have from (
B.7
),

|\partial_{y}^{k}q|\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{|q_{j}|}{y^{k-j}},

and thus, using (
B.1
) and the pointwise bounds given in part
(iii)
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2m}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m}+\sum_{i=0}^{2m-1}\int_{y<1}|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{2m-1}\int_{y>1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{y^{4m-2i}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m}+\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}\int_{y<1}y|\ln y|^{\Bbbk}dy+\sum_{i=0}^{2m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\int_{y>1}\frac{|q_{j}|^{2}}{y^{4m-2j}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m},

which concludes the proof of (
B.3
).

The estimate (
B.4
) simply follows from (
B.3
). Indeed, if
i+j=2m
with
1\leq m\leq\Bbbk
, we have

\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{2j}}=\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2m}.

If
i+j=2m+1
with
1\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1
, we write

\displaystyle\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{2j}}=\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i+2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q|^{2}}{1+y^{4m-2i+4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2(m+1)}}.

(v)
Let
i,j\geq 0
with
1\leq i+j\leq 2\Bbbk-1
, then
2\leq i+j+1\leq 2\Bbbk
and we conclude from (
B.4
) that for
y\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial_{y}^{i}q}{y^{j}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\int_{y}^{+\infty}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{(\partial_{x}^{i}q)^{2}}{x^{2j}}\right)dx\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{d-2}}\left\{\int_{y}^{+\infty}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{i}q|^{2}}{x^{2j+2}}+\int_{y}^{+\infty}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{i+1}q|^{2}}{x^{2j}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{d-2}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathscr{E}_{2m}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j+1=2m,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk,\\
\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2m}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2(m+1)}}&\quad\text{for}\quad i+j+1=2m+1,\;\;1\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1.\end{array}\right.

This ends the proof of Lemma
B.1
.

∎

Appendix C
Proof of (
4.22
).

We give here the proof of (
4.22
). Before going to the proof, we need the following Leibniz rule for
\mathscr{L}^{k}
.

Lemma C.1

(Leibniz rule for
\mathscr{L}^{k}
)
.

Let
\phi
be a smooth function and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathscr{L}^{k+1}(\phi f)=\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}f_{2m}\phi_{2k+2,2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1},

(C.1)

and

\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k}(\phi f)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+1,2m+1}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m}\phi_{2k+1,2m},

(C.2)

where

- for
k=0
,

\displaystyle\phi_{1,0}=-\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{1,1}=\phi,

\displaystyle\phi_{2,0}=-\partial_{y}^{2}\phi-\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{2,1}=2\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{2,2}=\phi,

- for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+1,0}=-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k,0},

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+1,2i}=-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k,2i}-\phi_{2k,2i-1},\quad 1\leq i\leq k,

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+1,2i+1}=\phi_{2k,2i}+\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\phi_{2k,2i+1}-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k,2i+1},\quad 0\leq i\leq k-1,

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+1,2k+1}=\phi_{2k,2k}=\phi,

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+2,0}=\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+1,0}+\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\phi_{2k+1,0},

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+2,2i}=\phi_{2k+1,2i-1}+\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+1,2i}+\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\phi_{2k+1,2i},\quad 1\leq i\leq k,

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+2,2i+1}=-\phi_{2k+1,2i}+\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+1,2i+1},\quad 0\leq i\leq k,

\displaystyle\phi_{2k+2,2k+2}=\phi_{2k+1,2k+1}=\phi.

Proof.

We use the following relation,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(\phi f)=\phi\mathscr{A}f-\partial_{y}\phi f,\quad\mathscr{A}^{*}(\phi f)=\phi\mathscr{A}^{*}f+\partial_{y}\phi f,\quad\mathscr{A}f+\mathscr{A}^{*}f=\frac{d-1+2V}{y}f,

to compute

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(\phi f)

\displaystyle=f_{1}\phi+f(-\partial_{y}\phi),

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\phi f)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}^{*}\mathscr{A}(\phi f)=f_{2}\phi+f_{1}(2\partial_{y}\phi)+f\left(-\partial_{y}^{2}\phi-\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\partial_{y}\phi\right),

which is the conclusion of (
C.1
) and (
C.2
) for
k=0
.

Assume that (
C.1
) and (
C.2
) hold for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let us compute for
k\to k+1
. Using (
C.1
), we write

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k+1}(\phi f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}\mathscr{A}\big[f_{2m}\phi_{2k+2,2m}\big]+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\left[-\mathscr{A}^{*}+\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\right]f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}\big\{f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+2,2m}+f_{2m}(-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m})\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\Big\{f_{2m+2}(-\phi_{2k+2,2m+1})+f_{2m+1}(-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+f_{2m+1}\left(\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m+1}\left(\phi_{2k+2,2m}-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1}+\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m=1}^{k}f_{2m}\left(-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+2,2m}-\phi_{2k+2,2m+1}\right)+f_{2k+3}\phi_{2k+2,2k+2}+f(-\partial_{y}\phi_{2k+2,0}),

which yields the recurrence relation for
\phi_{2k+3,j}
with
0\leq j\leq 2k+3
.

Similarly, we write
\mathscr{L}^{k+2}(\phi f)=\mathscr{A}^{*}\big[\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k+1}(\phi f)\big]
and use the formula (
C.2
) with
k+1
to obtain the recurrence relation for
\phi_{2k+4,j}
with
0\leq j\leq 2k+4
. This concludes the proof of Lemma
C.1
.
∎

Let us now give the proof of (
4.22
). By induction and the definition (
3.13
), we have

[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v=\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{m}\left([\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}]\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-2-m}v\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{m}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-2-m}v\right).

Noting that
\frac{\partial_{t}Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}}=\frac{b_{1}\Lambda Z}{\lambda^{4}y^{2}}
, we make a change of variables to obtain

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(1+y^{2})}\left|[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\int\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\left|\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\mathscr{L}^{m}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-2-m}q\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\left|\mathscr{L}^{m}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-2-m}q\right)\right|^{2}.

For
m=0
, we use (
4.21
) and (
4.20
) to estimate

\int\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\left|\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-2}q\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\int\frac{|q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-4}|}{1+y^{10}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

For
m=1,\cdots,\Bbbk-2
, we apply (
C.1
) with
\phi=\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}=\frac{(d-1)\Lambda\cos(2Q)}{y^{2}}
and note from (
2.4
) that

|\phi_{k,i}|\lesssim\frac{1}{1+y^{2\gamma+2+(2k-i)}}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+y^{4+(2k-i)}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\;\;0\leq i\leq 2k,

which yields

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\left|\mathscr{L}^{m}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-2-m}q\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{2m}\int\frac{q^{2}_{2\Bbbk-4-2m-i}}{(1+y^{10+(4m-2i)})}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

Thus,

\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(1+y^{2})}\left|[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v\right|^{2}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

Similarly, we use (

) to get the estimate

\int\left|\mathscr{A}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v\right|^{2}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{\lambda^{4\Bbbk-d+2}}\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.22
).

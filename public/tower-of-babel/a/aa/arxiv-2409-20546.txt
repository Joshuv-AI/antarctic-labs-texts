Bilateral gamma approximation on Wiener Space
Kalyan Barman, Tomoyuki Ichiba and Palaniappan Vellaisamy

Abstract
. This paper deals with bilateral-gamma ( B ​ G BG ) approximation to functionals of an isonormal Gaussian process. We use Malliavin-Stein method to obtain the error bounds for the smooth Wasserstein distance. As by-products, the error bounds for variance-gamma ( V ​ G VG ), Laplace, gamma and normal approximations are presented. Our approach is new in the sense that the Stein equation is based on integral operators rather than differential operators commonly used in the literature. Some of our bounds are sharper than the existing ones. For the approximation of a random element from the second Wiener chaos to a B ​ G BG distribution, the bounds are obtained in terms of their cumulants. Using this result, we show that a sequence of random variables (rvs) in the second Wiener chaos converges in distribution to a B ​ G BG rv if their cumulants of order two to six converge. As an application of our results, we consider an approximation of homogeneous sums of independent rvs to a B ​ G BG distribution, and mention some related limit theorems also. Finally, an approximation of a U U -statistic to the B ​ G BG distribution is discussed.

Introduction

Approximations related to limit theorems are fundamental issues in probability theory; see, for instance
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
30
]
. The study of approximations related to limit theorems on Wiener space has been a keen interest of researchers in recent years (see
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
21
]
). The first study in this direction is the central limit theorem due to Nualart and Peccati (see
[
24
]
). They proved a necessary and sufficient condition for a sequence of random variables (rvs), belonging to a fixed Wiener chaos, converges in distribution to a Gaussian random variable (rv). This result is known as fourth moment theorem in the literature.

A few years later, Nourdin and Peccati
[
21
]
combined Stein’s method for normal approximation with Malliavin calculus on the Wiener space, called the Malliavin-Stein method, which provides an accuracy of approximation in the fourth moment theorem for functionals on Wiener space.

The Malliavin-Stein method has also been extended to other probability distributions such as product normal
[
3
]
, Laplace
[
15
]
and gamma
[
23
]
distributions. In this direction, Eichelsbacher and Th
\ddot{\text{a}}
le
[
12
]
developed Malliavin-Stein method for variance-gamma (
VG
) approximation on the Wiener space. The Stein equation considered in
[
12
]
is a second-order differential equation. Using the estimates of the solution of the Stein equation, they obtain the bounds for
VG
approximation in terms of Malliavin operators and norms of contractions. However, their bounds contain two indeterminate positive constants (see Equation (4.1) of
[
12
]
). Further, the authors establish a “six moment theorem” for the
VG
approximation of double Wiener-It
\hat{\text{o}}
integrals. Later,
VG
approximations on the Wiener space are also discussed in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
7
]
, in which the limiting distributions are represented as the difference of two centered gamma rvs or the product of two mean zero normal rvs. Also, Gaunt
[
14
]
considered symmetric variance-gamma (
SVG
) approximation of double Wiener-It
\hat{\text{o}}
integrals. Recently, Azmoodeh
et al.

[
5
]
established a six moment theorem for
VG
approximation of double Wiener-It
\hat{\text{o}}
integrals with optimal convergence rate. More recently, Gaunt
[
15
]
obtains some new bounds for the solution of
VG
Stein equation. As an application, the author obtains explicit error bounds for
VG
approximation of double Wiener-It
\hat{\text{o}}
integrals.

The bilateral-gamma (
BG
) distributions are the convolution of two gamma distributions, which have a rich distributional theory and applications (see
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
). This family contains several classical distributions as special or limiting cases, such as normal, product normal, gamma,
SVG
,
VG
, normal variance-mean mixture and Laplace distributions. The problem of
BG
approximation on Wiener space is new and only some special or limiting cases such as gamma, normal, product-normal, Laplace,
VG
and
SVG
approximations have been considered so far in the literature. Therefore, it is of interest to study
BG
approximation for functionals on Wiener space and obtain the error bounds so that the corresponding bounds and limit theorems follow for some special distributions mentioned above.

In this article, we combine Malliavin calculus and Stein’s method to derive bounds for the
BG
approximation of functionals of an isonormal Gaussian process. In particular, we derive bounds for the
BG
approximation of the rvs belonging to the second Wiener chaos, in terms of their cumulants. Our approach of deriving bounds is also new as our Stein equation is based on integral operators, rather than differential operators commonly used in the literature. Also, this approach is rather simpler as can be seen for example in the case of
VG
approximations. It is also shown that some of our bounds are sharper than the existing bounds. Further, we establish that a sequence of rvs in the second Wiener chaos converges in distribution to a
BG
rv if their cumulants of order two to six converge to that of a
BG
rv. We discuss also the approximations of homogeneous sums of independent rvs to a
BG
distribution and show that the limiting distribution belongs to the class of
BG
distributions. Finally, as an application, the convergence of a
U
-statistic to the
BG
distribution is also presented.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce some notations and review some required preliminary results. Section
3
deals with
BG
distribution, and its properties. A Stein equation for a
BG
distribution, and its solution are obtained. In Section
4
, we derive the error in approximation of a functionals of an isonormal Gaussian process to a
BG
distribution. Also, for a sequence of rvs from the second Wiener chaos and its approximation to a
BG
distribution, the error bounds are obtained in terms of their cumulants. As a special case, we mention the results for normal, gamma,
VG
,
SVG
, and Laplace distributions. A comparison of our results with the existing results is also presented. In Section
5
, we consider approximation problem of homogeneous sums of independent rvs to a
BG
distribution. As corollaries, the limit theorems for
SVG
and normal distributions are pointed out. As an example, an approximation of a
U
-statistic to the
BG
distribution is considered.

2.1.
Function spaces and probability metrics

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
be the set of all natural numbers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be the Schwartz space defined by

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}):\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}|x^{m}f^{(n)}(x)|=0,\text{ for all }m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\},

where

, denotes henceforth the

th derivative of

with

and

is the space of infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Note that the Fourier transform (FT) on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is automorphism. If
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, then its FT is given by
\widehat{f}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iux}f(x)dx,~~\text{ }u\in\mathbb{R}
and its inverse FT is given by
f(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iux}\widehat{f}(u)du,~~\text{ }x\in\mathbb{R}
, see
[
27
]
.

Next, we define smooth Waserstein distance (see
[
2
]
or
[
5
]
). Consider the function space

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{r}=\left\{h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}~\big|~h\mbox{ is $r$ times differentiable and }\|h^{(k)}\|\leq 1,~k=0,1,\ldots,r\right\},

where
\|h\|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h(x)|
. Then, for any two random variables
Y
and
Z
,

(2.2)

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{W}_{r}}\left|\mathbb{E}[h(Y)]-\mathbb{E}[h(Z)]\right|:=d_{\mathcal{W}_{r}}(Y,Z)=d_{r}(Y,Z)~(\text{say})

is called the smooth Wasserstein distance. Also, when we choose the function spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\{h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}~\big|~h=\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,x]},~x\in\mathbb{{R}}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{W}

\displaystyle=\left\{h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}~\big|~h\text{ is 1-Lipschitz and }|h(x)-h(y)|\leq|x-y|\right\},

d_{\mathcal{M}_{K}}=d_{K}
(say) and
d_{\mathcal{M}_{W}}=d_{W}
(say) are called the Kolmogorov and Wasserstein distances, respectively (see
[
2
]
). It is known from Appendix A of
[
2
]

\displaystyle d_{{r-1}}(Y,Z)\leq 3\sqrt{2}\sqrt{d_{{r}}(Y,Z)},~r\geq 2,

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle d_{{1}}(Y,Z)\leq\left(3\sqrt{2}\right)^{\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{2^{i-1}}}\left(d_{{r}}(Y,Z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{r-1}}},~r\geq 2.

Also,

d_{K}(Y,Z)\leq c\sqrt{d_{{1}}(Y,Z)},

where
c
is some positive constant. Using (
2.3
), we get

d_{K}(Y,Z)\leq c\left(3\sqrt{2}\right)^{{(1-2^{1-r})}}\left(d_{{r}}(Y,Z)\right)^{2^{-r}},~r\geq 2.

In addition, the order relationship between
d_{r}
and
d_{W}
is

(2.4)

\displaystyle d_{{r}}(Y,Z)\leq d_{{1}}(Y,Z)\leq d_{W}(Y,Z),~r\geq 1.

2.2.
Some basic results on Malliavin calculus

Here, we present some basic properties from Gaussian analysis
and Malliavin calculus, that will be used later. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real separable Hilbert space and
Y=\{Y(h):h\in\mathcal{H}\}
be a centered Gaussian family defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
\mathbb{E}(Y(h))=0
and
Cov(Y(h),Y(g))=\langle h,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. Note that
Y
is called the isonormal Gaussian process over
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\odot m}
denote respectively
m
th tensor product and
m
th symmetric tensor product of
\mathcal{H}
with convention
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 0}=\mathcal{H}^{\odot 0}=\mathbb{{R}}.
For
q\geq 1
, the
q
th degree Hermite polynomial, denoted by
H_{q}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{{R}}
, is defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle H_{q}(x)=(-1)^{q}e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\frac{d^{q}}{dx^{q}}(e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}})=(-1)^{q}\frac{\phi^{(q)}(x)}{\phi(x)},

where
\phi(x)
denotes the density of the standard normal
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
distribution. Then, the
q
th Wiener chaos of
Y
, denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{q}
, defined as the closed linear subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}):=L^{2}(\Omega)
, which is generated by the family
\{H_{q}(Y(h)):h\in\mathcal{H},\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1\}
. We write by convention
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{{R}}.
For
q\geq 1
, the mapping
I_{q}(h^{\otimes q})=H_{q}(Y(h))
can be extended to a linear isometry between
\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}
and the
q
th Wiener chaos
\mathcal{H}_{q}.
For
q=0
, we write
I_{0}(c)=c
,
c\in\mathbb{{R}}
. It is well-known that
L^{2}(\Omega)
can be decomposed into the infinite orthogonal sum of the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{q}
. Hence for any
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we get by Stroock formula

(2.6)

\displaystyle G=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}I_{q}(h^{\odot q})=\mathbb{E}(G)+\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}H_{q}(Y(h)),

where
h^{\odot q}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot q},~q\geq 0,
with
h^{\odot 0}=\mathbb{E}(G).

Let
1\leq p\leq q
be integers and let
\{e_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Given integers
j_{1},\ldots,j_{p},k_{1},\ldots,k_{q}
and
r=0,1,\ldots,p,
the
r
th contraction of the two tensor products
e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{j_{p}}
and
e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}},
is defined as follows.

(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{j_{p}})\otimes_{0}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}})=e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots e_{j_{p}}\otimes e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}},

and, for
r=1,2,\ldots,p,

(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{j_{p}})\otimes_{r}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}})=\bigg[\prod_{l=1}^{r}\langle e_{jl},e_{kl}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigg]e_{j_{r+1}}\otimes\ldots e_{j_{p}}\otimes e_{k_{r+1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}}.

If
r=q=p,
then

(e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{j_{p}})\otimes_{p}(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{q}})=\prod_{l=1}^{p}\langle e_{jl},e_{kl}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle e_{j_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{j_{p}},e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{p}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}}.

Next, let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes q}
, then for every
r=0,1,\ldots,p\wedge q,
the contraction of
f
and
g
of order
r
is the element of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(p+q-2r)}
, defined by

f\otimes_{0}g=f\otimes g
, and for
p=q
,
f\otimes_{p}g=\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}},
and for
1\leq r\leq(p-1),

f\otimes_{r}g=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}=1}^{\infty}\langle f,e_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{i_{r}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}\otimes\langle g,e_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{i_{r}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes r}}.

Note that
f\otimes_{r}g
does not depend on the basis and also not necessarily symmetric. The symmetric version is denoted by
f\tilde{\otimes}_{r}g\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot(p+q-2r)}
. Also, if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot p}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot q},
and
1\leq q\leq p
, then

(2.7)

\displaystyle I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)=\sum_{r=0}^{p\wedge q}\binom{p}{r}\binom{q}{r}I_{p+q-2r}(f\tilde{\otimes}_{r}g),

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(I_{p}(f)I_{q}(g)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p!\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}},&\text{ if }q=p\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
Y
be an isonormal Gaussian process. Let
g:\mathbb{{R}}^{n}\to\mathbb{{R}}
be a

-function such that its partial derivatives have polynomial growth. Then a rv

of the form
G=(Y(h_{1}),Y(h_{2}),\ldots,Y(h_{n}))

be the set of all smooth rvs. The Malliavin derivative of

with respect to

is the element of

defined as

DG=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}g(Y(h_{1}),\ldots,Y(h_{n}))h_{i},

and the
m
th derivative (
m\geq 2
)

is an element of
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\odot m})
, defined by

D^{m}G=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial y_{i_{1}},\ldots,\partial y_{i_{m}}}g(Y(h_{1}),\ldots,Y(h_{m}))h_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes h_{i_{m}}.

For
m\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
denotes the closure of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to the norm defined as

\|G\|^{p}_{m,p}=\mathbb{E}[|G|^{p}]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}[\|D^{i}G\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes i}}],

and
\mathbb{D}^{\infty}:=\cap_{m\geq 1}\cap_{p\geq 1}\mathbb{D}^{m,p}
. That is,

is the domain of

in

. Moreover, for any

.

For
p\geq 1
, the adjoint of the operator

, called the

-th divergence operator, is denoted by
\delta^{p}
. The domain of
\delta^{p}
, denoted by

, is defined as

\displaystyle Dom(\delta^{p})=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p})

\displaystyle~\Big|~|\mathbb{E}[\langle D^{p}G,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}}]|

\displaystyle\leq c_{u}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(G^{2})},~~\text{for some constant $c_{u}>0$}\Big\},

see
[
18
]
. Then the
p
th divergence operator
\delta^{p}:Dom(\delta^{p})\subset L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined, for each

by

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(G\delta^{p}(u))=\mathbb{E}(\langle D^{p}G,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes p}}),~G\in\mathcal{S},

which is sometimes called the duality formula.

It is well-known that the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O-U) semigroup (see Section 2.8 of
[
21
]
) is defined by

(2.10)

\displaystyle P_{t}(G)=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}J_{q}(G),~t\geq 0,

where
J_{q}(G)=\text{Proj}(G|\mathcal{H}_{q})=I_{q}(h^{\odot q})
(

) for any

as in (

). Also, the infinitesimal generator of the O-U semigroup is given by

where the domain of

Dom(L)=\{F\in L^{2}(\Omega):\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{2}\mathbb{E}(J{{}_{q}}(G))^{2}<\infty\}.
It is known from the Proposition 2.8.8 of
[
21
]
that
\delta(DG)=-LG.
Also,
D(f(G))=f^{\prime}(G)DG.

For any

the operator

is called pseudo-inverse of

L^{-1}G\in\text{Dom}L,

Let
f:\mathbb{{R}}\to\mathbb{{R}}
be a differentiable function having bounded derivative. Then, for any

, the Malliavin integration by parts formula (see Theorem 2.9.1 of
[

]

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Gf(H)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f(H))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime}(H)\langle DH,-DL^{-1}G\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Gf(G)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f(G))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime}(G)\langle DG,-DL^{-1}G\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f(G))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime}(G)\Gamma_{2}(G)\right),

where, for a rv

,
\Gamma_{1}(G)=G
, and for every

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}(G)=\langle DG,-DL^{-1}\Gamma_{m-1}(G)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty},

\displaystyle\langle f\otimes_{1}^{(m)}f,f\otimes_{1}^{(p)}f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\odot 2}}=\langle f\otimes_{1}^{(m+p-1)}f,f\otimes_{1}^{(1)}f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\odot 2}}=\langle f\otimes_{1}^{(m+p-1)}f,f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\odot 2}}.

.

[

(i)

The rv

admits the representation

where

(ii)

For every

the

\displaystyle\kappa_{p}(G)=2^{p-1}(p-1)!\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f,j}^{p}=2^{p-1}(p-1)!\langle f\otimes_{1}^{(p-1)}f,f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{\odot 2}}.

2.3.
Malliavin-Stein approach on Wiener space

Finally, we discuss the combination of Stein’s method with Malliavin calculus on the Wiener space. In general, Stein’s method is based on the fact that, any real-valued random variable
Z
follows a distribution
F_{Z}
if and only if there exists an operator
\mathcal{A}
(also called Stein operator) such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{A}f(Z)\right)=0,~~\text{for every}~~f\in\mathcal{F},

where
\mathcal{F}
is a suitably chosen function space. This characterization leads us to the Stein equation

(2.19)

\mathcal{A}f(x)=h(x)-\mathbb{E}h(Z),~x\in\mathbb{R},

where
h
is a real-valued test function. Replacing
x
with a rv
Y
belongs to a fixed Wiener chaos, and taking expectations on both sides of (
2.19
), we get

(2.20)

\mathbb{E}h(Y)-\mathbb{E}h(Z)=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{A}f(Y)\right).

The equality (
2.20
) plays a crucial role in this approach. For a real valued test function
h
, the problem of bounding the quantity
|\mathbb{E}h(Y)-\mathbb{E}h(Z)|
relies on the bounds for the solution of (
2.19
) and behavior of
Y
. Recall that (see (
2.2
))

d_{r}(Y,Z)=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{W}_{r}}\left|\mathbb{E}(h(Y))-\mathbb{E}(h(Z))\right|.

From (
2.20
), it follows that

d_{r}(Y,Z)\leq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{W}_{r})}\left|(\mathbb{E}\mathcal{A}f(Y))\right|,

where
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{W}_{r})=\{f_{h}:h\in\mathcal{W}_{r}\}
is the set of solutions that satisfy (
2.20
). For more details on Malliavin-Stein approach, see the recent review paper
[
6
]
and the references therein.

In particular, if
q\geq 2
be an integer and
G_{n}=I_{q}(f_{n}),

n\geq 1
, such that
f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}
be a sequence of rvs belonging to the
q
-th Wiener chaos such that
\mathbb{E}(G_{n}^{2})=1.
Then, the fourth moment theorem (see Theorem 1 of
[
24
]
) states that
G_{n}\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\to}Z
if and only if
\mathbb{E}(G_{n}^{4})\to 3
, where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and the notation
\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\to}
denotes the convergence in distribution. Using the Malliavin-Stein approach, Nourdin and Peccati proved (see Theorem 5.2.6 of
[
21
]
) that the upper bound of the Kolmogorov distance between
G_{n}
and
Z
is

(2.21)

\displaystyle d_{K}(G_{n},Z)\leq\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}\left(\mathbb{E}(G_{n}^{4})-3\right)}=\sqrt{\frac{q-1}{3q}\kappa_{4}(G_{n})},

where
\kappa_{4}(G_{n})
is the fourth cumulant of
G_{n}
. From (
2.21
), if
\kappa_{4}(G_{n})\to 0
(or equivalently if
\mathbb{E}(G_{n}^{4})\to\mathbb{E}(Z^{4})=3

which implies that the rv

converges to a normal
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
distribution.

3.
Some basic results for bilateral gamma distribution

We start with definition and related results.

3.1.
Definition and properties

Here, we define
BG
distributions and review some of their properties. The
BG
distributions have a rich distributional theory, and contains several classical distributions as special or limiting cases, such as the normal, Laplace,
VG
, normal variance-mean mixtures and etc. (see
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
). Let
X_{i},

i=1,2,
be two independent gamma
Ga(\alpha_{i},p_{i})
rvs with density

f_{i}(x)=\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p_{i}}}{\Gamma(p_{i})}e^{-\alpha_{i}x}x^{p_{i}-1},~x>0,~i=1,2.

Then the distribution of the rv
X=X_{1}-X_{2}
is called the
BG
distribution and

we denote it by
BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2}).
The characteristic function (cf) of a
BG
distribution is

(3.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{bg}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{1}-iz}\right)^{p_{1}}\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{2}+iz}\right)^{p_{2}}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{izu}-1)\nu_{bg}(du)\right),~~z\in\mathbb{R},

where
\nu_{bg}
is the Lévy measure given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\nu_{bg}(du)=\left(\frac{p_{1}}{u}e^{-\alpha_{1}u}\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(u)-\frac{p_{2}}{u}e^{-\alpha_{2}|u|}\mathbf{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(u)\right)du.

Next, we list some special and limiting distributions of the
BG
family (see
[
16
]
).

(i)

When
p_{1}=p_{2}=p
, then
BG(\alpha_{1},p,\alpha_{2},p)
is the
VG
distribution, denoted by
VG(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},p)
.

(ii)

When
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha
and
p_{1}=p_{2}=p
, then
BG(\alpha,p,\alpha,p)
follows
SVG
distribution, denoted by
SVG(\alpha,p)
.

(iii)

The
SVG(\alpha,1)
is called the Laplace distribution, which we denote by
La(\alpha)
.

(iv)

The limiting case as
p\to\infty
, the
SVG(\sqrt{2p}/\alpha,p)
converges in law to a
\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha^{2})
distribution.

(v)

The limiting case as
\alpha_{2}\to\infty
, the
BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
converges in law to

Ga(\alpha_{1},p_{1})
distribution.

Note that the
BG
distributions are infinitely divisible and self-decomposable, see
[
16
]
. Also, it is known that (see
[
16
]
), if
Y_{1}\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
and
Y_{2}\sim BG(\alpha_{1},q_{1},\alpha_{2},q_{2})
, then
Y_{1}+Y_{2}\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1}+q_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2}+q_{2}),
that is, when the scale parameters of the gamma distributions are the same, the
BG
distributions are stable under convolution. Let now
X\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
. The
j
th cumulant (see
[
16
]
) is given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}(X)

\displaystyle=(j-1)!\left(\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{j}}+(-1)^{j}\frac{p_{2}}{\alpha_{2}^{j}}\right),~j\geq 1.

In particular, the first two cumulants (that is, mean and variance) of
X
are

\kappa_{1}(X)=\mathbb{E}(X)=\left(\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha_{1}}-\frac{p_{2}}{\alpha_{2}}\right)\text{ and }\kappa_{2}(X)=\text{Var}(X)=\left(\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha_{1}^{2}}+\frac{p_{2}}{\alpha_{2}^{2}}\right).
Also, from the definition of
BG
rv
X
as
X=X_{1}-X_{2}
, where
X_{1},

X_{2}
are independent gamma
Ga(\alpha_{i},p_{i})
,
i=1,2,
rvs, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{1}^{k-j})\mathbb{E}(X_{2}^{j})

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{k-j}\alpha_{2}^{j}}\frac{\Gamma(p_{1}+k-j)\Gamma(p_{2}+j)}{\Gamma p_{1}\Gamma p_{2}},

since
\mathbb{E}(X_{i}^{n})=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}^{n}}\frac{\Gamma(p_{i}+n)}{\Gamma(p_{i})},

i=1,2,
for
n\geq 1
, see
[
11
]
.

3.2.
A Stein equation for a
BG
distribution

In this section, we derive a Stein equation for the
BG
distribution and solve it using the semigroup approach. Recently, a Stein equation for a
BG
distribution in the form of second order differential equation is given in
[
11
]
. Their approach is based on the density of the
BG
distributions, which is quite involved. However, we derive here a Stein identity in the integral form involving the Lévy measure.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2}).
Then,

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}\left(Xf(X)-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(X+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)=0,~~f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\nu_{bg}
is the Lévy measure given in (
3.3
).

Proof.

Taking logarithms on both sides of (
3.2
), and differentiating with respect to
z
, we have

(3.7)

\phi^{\prime}_{bg}(z)=i\phi_{bg}(z)\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{izu}\nu_{bg}(du).

Let
h_{X}(x)
denote density of
X
. Then

(3.8)

\phi_{bg}(z)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{izx}h_{X}(x)dx~~\text{and}~~\phi^{\prime}_{bg}(z)=i\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}xe^{izx}h_{X}(x)dx.

Substituting (
3.8
) into (
3.7
) and rearranging the integrals, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}xe^{izx}h_{X}(x)dx-\phi_{bg}(z)\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{izu}\nu_{bg}(du)=0

The second integral of (
3.9
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{izu}\nu_{bg}(du)\right)\phi_{bg}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{izu}e^{izx}h_{X}(x)dx\nu_{bg}(du)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{iz(u+x)}\nu_{bg}(du)h_{X}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}ue^{izy}\nu_{bg}(du)h_{X}(y-u)dy

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{izx}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uh_{X}(x-u)\nu_{bg}(du)dx.

Substituting (
3.10
) into (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}xe^{izx}h_{X}(x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{izx}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uh_{X}(x-u)\nu_{bg}(du)dx

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{izx}\left(xh_{X}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}uh_{X}(x-u)\nu_{bg}(du)\right)dx.

Applying FT to (
3.11
), multiplying with
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}),
and integrating over
\mathbb{R},
we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\left(xh_{X}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}uh_{X}(x-u)\nu_{bg}(du)\right)dx=0.

The second integral of (
3.12
) can be seen as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uf(x)h_{X}(x-u)\nu_{bg}(du)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}uf(y+u)h_{X}(y)\nu_{bg}(du)dy

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(X+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right).

Substituting (
3.13
) in (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Xf(X)-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(X+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)=0.

This proves the result.
∎

Note that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f(x):=-xf(x)+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x+u)u\nu_{bg}(du),

is a Stein operator for
BG
distribution. Also, a Stein equation for
BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
distribution is given by

(3.15)

\mathcal{A}f(x)=-xf(x)+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)=h(x)-\mathbb{E}(h(X)),

where
h\in\mathcal{M}
, a class of test functions. We now apply the semigroup approach to find
f_{h}
that satisfies the integral equation (
3.15
) for a given
h\in\mathcal{M}
. This approach for solving Stein equations is developed in Barbour
[
9
]
and Arras and Houdré
[
2
]
generalized it for infinitely divisible distributions with the finite first moment. Henceforth, we consider
\mathcal{F}=\overline{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
, the closure of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{{R}})
. Following Barbour’s approach
[
9
]
, we choose a family of operators
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, as

(3.16)

P_{t}f(x):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)dy,~~f\in\mathcal{F},

where
\hat{f}
is the FT of
f
and

(3.17)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(y):=\frac{\phi_{bg}(y)}{\phi_{bg}(e^{-t}y)},~y\in\mathbb{{R}}.

Since
BG
distributions are self-decomposable (see
[
16
, p.263]
),
\phi_{t}
is also a cf
[
25
, p.90]
of some rv
X_{(t)}
, say. That is, for all
y\in\mathbb{R},
and
t\geq 0,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(y)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iyu}F_{X_{(t)}}(du),

where
F_{X_{(t)}}
is the law of
X_{(t)}
. Using (
3.18
), we get

\displaystyle P_{t}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyxe^{-t}}e^{iyu}F_{X_{(t)}}(du)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iy(u+xe^{-t})}F_{X_{(t)}}(du)dy

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(u+xe^{-t})F_{X_{(t)}}(du),

where the last step follows by inverse FT (see Section
2.1
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a family of operators defined in (
3.16
). Then

(i)

(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
\mathbb{C}_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

Its generator
L
is given by

\displaystyle Lf(x)

\displaystyle=-xf^{\prime}(x)+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x+u)u\nu_{bg}(du),~~f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}f^{\prime}(x),

where
\mathcal{A}
is defined in (
3.14
).

Proof.

(i) First note that
\lim_{t\to 0}\phi_{t}(y)=1
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi_{t}(y)=\phi_{bg}(y).
Then it can be shown that
P_{0}f(x)=f(x)~~\text{and}~~\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{t}(f)(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)F_{X}(dx)=\mathbb{E}(f(X))
.
Now, for any
s,t\geq 0
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\phi_{t+s}(y)=\frac{\phi_{bg}(y)}{\phi_{bg}(e^{-(t+s)}y)}=\frac{\phi_{bg}(y)}{\phi_{bg}(e^{-s}y)}\frac{\phi_{bg}(e^{-s}y)}{\phi_{bg}(e^{-(t+s)}y)}=\phi_{s}(y)\phi_{t}(ye^{-s}).

So, from (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle P_{t+s}(f)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyxe^{-(t+s)}}\phi_{t+s}(y)dy

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyxe^{-(t+s)}}\phi_{s}(y)\phi_{t}(ye^{-s})dy.

Let
\delta
denote the Dirac
\delta
-function defined by

\delta(x-a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i(x-a)y}dy,~x,a\in\mathbb{{R}}.

Then

\displaystyle P_{t}(P_{s}(f))(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{P_{s}}(f)(y)e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ivy}P_{s}(f)(v)dv\right)e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ivy}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(w)e^{iwe^{-s}v}\phi_{s}(w)dw\right)dv\right)e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(w)\phi_{s}(w)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iv(we^{-s}-y)}dv\right)dydw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(w)\phi_{s}(w)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)2\pi\delta(we^{-s}-y)dydw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(w)\phi_{s}(w)e^{ie^{-s}wxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(we^{-s})dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(w)e^{iwxe^{-(t+s)}}\phi_{t+s}(w)dw

\displaystyle=P_{t+s}(f)(x).

(ii) For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle Lf(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(P_{t}f(x)-f(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyxe^{-t}}\phi_{t}(y)dy-\frac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyx}dy\bigg)

(3.23)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyx}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\bigg(e^{iyx(e^{-t}-1)}\phi_{t}(y)-1\bigg)dy,

by dominated convergence theorem. Now by L’Hospital rule and (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\big(e^{iyx(e^{-t}-1)}\phi_{t}(y)-1\big)

\displaystyle=-iyx+\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\phi_{t}^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=-iyx+\frac{y\phi_{bg}^{\prime}(y)}{\phi_{bg}(y)}

(3.24)

\displaystyle=-iyx+iy\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iyu}u\nu_{bg}(du).

Using (
3.24
) in (
3.23
), we get

\displaystyle Lf(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{f}(y)e^{iyx}(iy)\left(-x+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iyu}u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)dy

\displaystyle=-xf^{\prime}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x+u)u\nu_{bg}(du),

where the last equality follows by inverse FT. ∎

Next, we provide the solution of our Stein equation (
3.15
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
and
h\in\mathcal{W}_{r}
, defined in (
2.1
). Then the function
f_{h}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(3.25)

f_{h}(x):=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dx}P_{t}h(x)dt,

solves (
3.15
).

Proof.

Let

g_{h}(x)=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(P_{t}(h)(x)-\mathbb{E}h(X)\bigg)dt.

Then
g_{h}^{\prime}(x)=f_{h}(x).
Also from (
3.20
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}f_{h}(x)

\displaystyle=-xf_{h}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{h}(x+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)

\displaystyle=Lg_{h}(x)

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}LP_{t}(h)(x)dt

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}h(x)dt\text{ (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{nourdin}{}{}, p.68]})}

\displaystyle=P_{0}h(x)-P_{\infty}h(x)

Hence,

is the solution to (

). ∎

Next, we obtain some regularity of

.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
h\in\mathcal{W}_{k+1}
, let
f_{h}
be defined in (
3.25
). Then

(3.26)

\displaystyle\|f_{h}^{(k)}\|\leq\frac{1}{k+1},~k\geq 0,

where
f^{(k)}
,
k\geq 1
, denotes the
k
th derivative of
f
with
f^{(0)}=f
.

Proof.

For
h\in\mathcal{W}_{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\|f_{h}\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dx}P_{t}h(x)dt\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{(1)}(xe^{-t}+y)F_{X_{(t)}}(dy)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{(1)}\|\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}dt\right|

\displaystyle=\|h^{(1)}\|

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Since

is
k
-times differentiable, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{h}^{(1)}\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{(2)}(xe^{-t}+y)F_{X_{(t)}}(dy)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|h^{(2)}\|\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2t}dt\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\|h^{(2)}\|}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Indeed, it follows by induction that

\|f_{h}^{(k)}\|\leq\frac{1}{k+1},~k\geq 1.

This proves the result.
∎

4.
Bilateral gamma approximation on Wiener space

In this section, we obtain error bounds for

, for the smooth Wasserstein distance

. The error bounds for

,

, Laplace, gamma and normal approximations follow as corollaries. Moreover, we give some numerical comparisons of the constants involved in the error bounds between our results and the existing results given by Gaunt
[
14
]
. First, we need the following integration by parts formulas. Similar type of formulas can also be found in the proof of Theorem 4.1 of
[

]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{D}^{2,4}
and
f:\mathbb{{R}}\to\mathbb{{R}}
be a twice differentiable function having bounded first and second derivatives. Then,

(4.1)

\displaystyle(i)~\mathbb{E}\left(Gf^{\prime}(G)\right)=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f^{\prime}(G))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\Gamma_{2}(G)\right),\text{ and}

(4.2)

\displaystyle(ii)~\mathbb{E}\left(Gf(G)\right)=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f(G))+\mathbb{E}(f^{\prime}(G))\mathbb{E}(\Gamma_{2}(G))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\Gamma_{3}(G)\right),

where
\Gamma_{j},~j\geq 1,
is defined as in (
2.13
).

Proof.

(i) Recall from the integration by parts formula given in (

)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime}(G)\Gamma_{2}(G)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(Gf(G)\right)-\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f(G)).

Replacing
f
by
f^{\prime}
in (

) and rearranging the terms, we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(Gf^{\prime}(G)\right)=\mathbb{E}(G)\mathbb{E}(f^{\prime}(G))+\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\Gamma_{2}(G)\right).

(ii) Using the fact
LL^{-1}G=G-\mathbb{E}(G)
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\bigg(\Gamma_{2}(G)-\mathbb{E}(\Gamma_{2}(G))\bigg)f^{\prime}(G)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg(LL^{-1}\Gamma_{2}(G)f^{\prime}(G)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\delta\left(-DL^{-1}\Gamma_{2}(G)\right)f^{\prime}(G)\bigg\}

(since
\delta(DG)=-LG
)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\langle Df^{\prime}(G),-DL^{-1}\Gamma_{2}(G)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigg)\text{ (by \eqref{dualfo})}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\langle DG,-DL^{-1}\Gamma_{2}(G)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\bigg)\text{ (by chain rule)}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\Gamma_{3}(G)\bigg),

which leads to

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{\prime}(G)\Gamma_{2}(G)\bigg)=\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{\prime}(G)\bigg)\mathbb{E}\bigg(\Gamma_{2}(G)\bigg)+\mathbb{E}\bigg(f^{\prime\prime}(G)\Gamma_{3}(G)\bigg)

Putting (
4.3
) in (

).
∎

4.1.
Bounds in terms of
\Gamma

Here, we establish our bounds, for the

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{D}^{2,4}
and
X\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
, and
\Gamma_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, denote the
\Gamma
-operators defined in (
2.13

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}>1+|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\alpha_{12}=\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}-(1+|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|)}\text{ and }\alpha_{13}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}-\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}.

Then,

\displaystyle d_{{3}}(G,X)\leq

Proof.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-p_{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(G-u)e^{-\alpha_{2}u}du\bigg)

\displaystyle=

4.10

4.9

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h(G)-\mathbb{E}h(X)=

\displaystyle\quad-p_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(G-u)e^{-\alpha_{2}u}du\bigg)+\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(G+u)e^{-\alpha_{1}u}du\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{p_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(G-u)e^{-\alpha_{2}u}du\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{p_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(G+u)e^{-\alpha_{1}u}du\right)

(4.11)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{p_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(G-u)e^{-\alpha_{2}u}du\right).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}h(G)-\mathbb{E}h(X)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left(\mathbb{E}X-G\right)f(G)\bigg)+\alpha_{13}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(G+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\left(f^{\prime}(G)\right)+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(p_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(G+u)e^{-\alpha_{1}u}du

\displaystyle-p_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(G-u)e^{-\alpha_{2}u}du\bigg)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg(\left(\mathbb{E}X-G\right)f(G)\bigg)+\alpha_{13}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(G+u)u\nu_{bg}(du)\right)

Definition 5.1

(Homogeneous sums)
.

Let
[N]:=\{1,\ldots,N\}
, where
N\geq 2
, and
Y=\{Y_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of centered independent rvs. For an integer
1\leq q\leq N
, let
f:[N]^{q}\to\mathbb{{R}}
be a symmetric function vanishing on diagonals, that is,
f(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})=0
, whenever
i_{k}=i_{j}
, for some
k\neq j
. The rv

\displaystyle H_{q}(N,f,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\leq N}f(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})Y_{i_{1}}\ldots Y_{i_{q}}

\displaystyle=q!\sum_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}\}\subset[N]^{q}}f(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})Y_{i_{1}}\ldots Y_{i_{q}}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=q!\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{q}\leq N}f(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})Y_{i_{1}}\ldots Y_{i_{q}}

is called the homogeneous sum of order
q
, based on
f
, and on the first
N
elements of
Y
.

Note that
\mathbb{E}(H_{q}(N,f,Y))=0
, and if
\mathbb{E}(Y_{i}^{2})=1
, for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathbb{E}(H_{q}^{2}(N,f,Y))=q!\|f\|_{q}^{2},
where
\|f\|_{q}^{2}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{q}\leq N}f^{2}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q}).

Next recall that (see
[
21
, p.184]
), the quantity

(5.2)

\displaystyle\text{Inf}_{i}(f)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\{i_{2},\ldots,i_{q}\}\subset[N]^{q-1}}f^{2}(i,i_{2},\ldots,i_{q})

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q-1)!}\sum_{1\leq i_{2}<\ldots<i_{q}\leq N}f^{2}(i,i_{2},\ldots,i_{q})

is called the influence of the variable
Y_{i}
. It is known that (see equation (11.2.5) of
[

) if

is a sequence of independent rvs with

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\text{Var}\bigg(H_{q}(N,f,Y)\mid Y_{k},k\neq i\bigg)\bigg]=(q!)^{2}\text{Inf}_{i}(f),

which quantifies the impact of the rv

on the overall fluctuations of

.

Next let
Z=\{Z_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) normal

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{3}(X)\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}-\alpha_{13}\bigg(\kappa_{23}(G_{n})-\kappa_{23}(X)\bigg)

(5.12)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}^{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}}\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}+\alpha_{13}^{2}\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}^{2}+2\alpha_{13}^{2}\kappa_{2}(X)\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}.

Substituting (
5.9
) and (
5.12
) in (
4.39
), and then using the triangle inequality, we get (
5.8
), as desired.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

From (
5.8
), if
\kappa_{j}(G_{n})\to\kappa_{j}(X),~j=2,3,4
, then

\displaystyle d_{{3}}(G_{n},X)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{12}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{120}}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}|}+\frac{\sqrt{|\alpha_{13}|}}{2\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}|}\bigg),

where
\alpha_{12}
and
\alpha_{13}
are defined in (
4.7
).

Using the above two lemmas, we can now deduce an upper bound for
BG
approximation to homogeneous sums of independent rvs.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\{N_{n},f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
,
Y=\{Y_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be the sequences as stated in Lemma
5.2
and
H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y)
be defined as in (
5.1
). Consider the sequence
G_{n}=I_{2}(g_{n})
,
n\geq 1
, where
g_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq N_{n}}f_{n}(i_{1},i_{2})e_{i_{1}}\otimes e_{i_{2}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot 2}
. Let
X\sim BG(\alpha_{1},p_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2})
with
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}>(1+|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|)
and
p_{1}\alpha_{2}=p_{2}\alpha_{1}
(so that
\mathbb{E}(X)=0
). If
\rho=\sup_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{i}|^{3}]<\infty
, then

\displaystyle d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y),X)

\displaystyle\leq 2(30\rho)^{2}\sqrt{\max_{1\leq i\leq N_{n}}\text{Inf}_{i}(f_{n})}+\frac{1}{3}\alpha_{12}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{6}|}}{\sqrt{120}}+\frac{\sqrt{|\alpha_{13}|}}{2\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{5}|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sqrt{\left|\frac{1}{6}\alpha_{13}^{2}-\frac{1}{3\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\right|}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{4}|}+\frac{\sqrt{|\alpha_{13}|}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}|}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{|\kappa_{3}(X)\widetilde{\kappa}_{3}|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\sqrt{|\alpha_{13}|}\sqrt{|\kappa_{23}(G_{n})-\kappa_{23}(X)|}+\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\sqrt{|\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}|}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+|\alpha_{13}||\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}|+\sqrt{2}|\alpha_{13}|\sqrt{|\kappa_{2}(X)\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}|}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{12}\left|\widetilde{\kappa}_{2}\right|,

where
\alpha_{12}
and
\alpha_{13}
are defined in (
4.7
).

Proof.

Let
Z=\{Z_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. normal
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
rvs and
H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Z)
be defined as Definition
5.1
. Then by the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y),X)

\displaystyle\leq d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y),H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Z))+d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Z),X)

(5.14)

\displaystyle=d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y),H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Z))+d_{3}(I_{2}(g_{n}),X),

where the last term of (
5.14
) is followed by (
5.5
). Using (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) in (
5.14
), we get (
5.13
), as desired.
∎

The following corollary gives a new limit theorem for homogeneous sums so that the limiting distribution belongs to the class of
BG
distributions.

Corollary 5.6
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
5.5
hold. If (i)
\max_{1\leq i\leq N_{n}}\text{Inf}_{i}(f_{n})\to 0
, and (ii)
\kappa_{j}(G_{n})\to\kappa_{j}(X),~2\leq j\leq 6
, then

(5.15)

\displaystyle d_{3}(H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y),X)\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

That is,
H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y)\overset{L}{\to}X,
as
n\to\infty
.

The proof is immediate from (
5.13
) as
\widetilde{\kappa}_{j}\to 0,
for
\kappa_{j}(G_{n})\to\kappa_{j}(X),~2\leq j\leq 6
.

As corollaries, we also have the following results for
SVG(\alpha,p)
and
\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha^{2})
distributions. Let
\max_{1\leq i\leq N_{n}}\text{Inf}_{i}(f_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty.
The following limit theorems hold:

(i)

If
\kappa_{j}(G_{n})\to\kappa_{j}(X_{s}),~j=2,4,6

H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y)\overset{L}{\to}X_{s},\text{ as }n\to\infty,

X_{s}\sim SVG(\alpha,p)
,
\alpha>1
,
p>0
.

(ii)

If
\kappa_{2}(G_{n})\to\alpha^{2}
, and
\kappa_{j}(G_{n})\to 0
, for
j=3,6
, then
H_{2}(N_{n},f_{n},Y)\overset{L}{\to}Z_{\alpha},\text{ as }n\to\infty,
where
Z_{\alpha}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha^{2})
,
\alpha>1
.

Remark 5.7
.

(i) Note that the limit theorem for

in (i) above was first proved by Eichelsbacher and Th
\ddot{\text{a}}
le
[

]
.

Example 5.8

(Bilateral-gamma approximation to
U

.

Let

5.16

5.1

\displaystyle f_{n}(i_{1},i_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\binom{n}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n\}}

\displaystyle\text{Inf}_{i}(f_{n})

\text{Inf}_{i}(f_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Next, let

\mathcal{N}(0,1)

W

\hat{\text{o}}
integral of order

. From
[
1

U
-statistic with degeneracy of order

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n}Z_{i_{1}}Z_{i_{2}}

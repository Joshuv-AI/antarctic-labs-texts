Slowly rotating gravastars Preprint: LA-UR-21-22546
Philip Beltracchi, Paolo Gondolo, Emil Mottola

Abstract
We solve Einstein’s equations for slowly rotating gravitational condensate stars (gravastars) up to second order in the rotation by expanding about the spherically symmetric gravastar with de Sitter interior and Schwarzschild exterior matched at their common horizon. Requiring that the perturbations are finite on the null surface reduces the exterior geometry to that of a Kerr black hole, implying that a slowly rotating gravastar cannot be distinguished from a Kerr black hole by any measurement or observation restricted to the macroscopic spacetime exterior to the horizon. We determine the interior solution, the surface stress tensor, and the Komar mass and angular momentum localized on the slowly rotating horizon surface. With the interior equation of state fixed at p = − ρ p=-\rho , finite junction conditions on the null horizon surface necessarily lead to an interior solution with a singular core, where the perturbative expansion breaks down. Comparison to other models and implications for more rapidly rotating gravastars are briefly discussed.

Introduction

Extension of the exterior geometry of a black hole (BH) through the horizon and into the interior involves the physical assumption that the vacuum Einstein equations apply at and inside the horizon. Motivation for considering alternative BH interiors arises first from the curvature singularities in the geometries
of mathematical BHs, which according to the classical singularity theorems are a general feature once some set of conditions involving trapped surfaces and energy conditions applies
Penrose 1965
;
Hawking and Penrose 1970
. In addition to curvature singularities, the BH interiors may show acausal characteristics, such as
the closed timelike curves of the Kerr solution
Hawking and Ellis 1973
.
Such acausal behavior is widely held to be unphysical, especially since it arises on macroscopic scales comparable to the scale of the BH horizon.

A BH horizon is a marginally trapped surface from which matter and information cannot exit classically. This horizon
boundary is the root of severe difficulties BHs pose for quantum theory, most notably the apparent nonconservation of probability and enormous BH entropy implied by the Hawking effect
Hawking 1976
. The resulting “BH information paradox”
’t Hooft 1995
has been the source of perplexity and speculation
for more than four decades, with a wide range of views on its possible resolution
Unruh and Wald 2017
, some of them quite radical, in which no classical spacetime
interior may survive at all
Mathur 2005
.

Another noteworthy property of horizons is the potentially large, unbounded semiclassical stress tensor from vacuum polarization effects that can
occur on them
Christensen and Fulling 1977
;
Mottola and Vaulin 2006
;
Mottola 2010
. If by any mechanism a nonvanishing surface stress tensor is present on the horizon, a globally vacuum solution is inappropriate and the interior may not be singular or have any unphysical or acausal features at all.
Consistency with quantum unitary evolution can then be maintained in this case. Physical surface stresses on a BH horizon, whatever their origin,
are perfectly consistent with the equivalence principle as any physical surface is, and can be described within classical general relativity in terms of a
sharply peaked anisotropic stress tensor—in the infinitely thin surface limit, a Dirac
\delta
-function distribution—localized on the horizon.

An explicit example of surface stresses and a quite different interior solution is provided by the gravitational condensate star (gravastar) in
Mazur and Mottola 2015
, where the Schwarzschild BH exterior is matched to a non-singular de Sitter interior with a positive
vacuum energy but negative pressure,
p\!=\!-\rho
, at a surface located at their respective horizons. A similar proposal based on an analogy to quantum phase transitions in condensed matter was suggested in Ref.
Chapline et al. 2001
. An abrupt change in ground
state vacuum energy at the horizon is characteristic of a quantum phase transition, and would lead to a dark energy de Sitter interior if
\rho=-p>0

Mottola 2010
. Independently
of the microscopic origin of the phase transition boundary layer, the interior static de Sitter and exterior Schwarzschild geometries are “glued” together at their respective horizons at
R_{{}_{S}}=2GM/c^{2}=c/H
, where
M
is the Schwarzschild mass and
H
is related to the de Sitter energy density by
\rho=3H^{2}/(8\pi G)
. The discontinuity
[\kappa]
in the surface gravities at the horizon results in a positive surface
tension
\tau_{s}=[\kappa]/8\pi G=c^{4}(8\pi GR_{{}_{S}})^{-1}

Mazur and Mottola 2015
, and a surface stress tensor
\delta
-function localized on the gravastar physical surface that replaces the mathematical BH horizon.

A variety of other models for “non-singular BHs” or “BH mimickers” have been proposed over the years
Poisson and Israel 1988
;
Frolov et al. 1989
;
Dymnikova 1992
;
Dymnikova 2003
;
Ansoldi 2008
.
In addition to their different interiors, several of these models have also been called “gravastars” by their authors, some with thin shells
Visser and Wiltshire 2004
;
Pani 2015
;
Uchikata and Yoshida 2016
;
Uchikata et al. 2016
, some with shells of quite large macroscopic thickness consisting of stiff matter

;
Chirenti and Rezzolla 2016
or anisotropic matter
Cattoen et al. 2005
;
Chirenti and Rezzolla 2007
;
Chirenti and Rezzolla 2008
. It is important to recognize that these alternate models have a
timelike
outer boundary at radius

R>2GM/c^{2}
,
not
at the null horizon as in
Mazur and Mottola 2015
. This distinction is important
in whether or not the usual Israel junction conditions
Israel 1966
can be used, since these are unsuitable for null hypersurfaces
Barrabès and Israel 1991
.

More importantly, the distinction between timelike and horizon surfaces manifests itself in physical properties and observational signatures that can distinguish such ultracompact objects from mathematical BHs, for example in their tidal deformability. In the era of gravitational wave and multimessenger astronomy it becomes of paramount importance to distinguish these various alternative models, with
different consequences for observations.

At the present time there is
no direct observational evidence of the existence of a BH event horizon. Indirect arguments, based upon accretion models
Broderick and Narayan 2007
;
Broderick et al. 2009
, are limited by the assumptions of the
models
Carballo-Rubio et al. 2018
. The LIGO/LSC gravitational wave data
Abbott et al. 2020
, and even the spectacular
images of M87 obtained by the Event Horizon Telescope
The EHT Collaboration 2019
, are sensitive to the light rings at the innermost stable circular orbits, probing the geometry well outside the BH horizon

;
Cardoso and Pani 2019
. As a result, while some thick shell variants are already ruled out

the gravastar of Ref.
Mazur and Mottola 2015
, defined by an extremely thin shell localized on the horizon, remains consistent with observations
Cardoso and Pani 2019
. The physics of a surface at or near a horizon, and possible interior structure of ultracompact thin-shell models, can be probed by the after-merger
ringdown and/or new phenomena such as “echoes” of the original merger event signal
Cardoso and Pani 2019
;
Cardoso et al. 2016b
,
for which at present, there are suggestions but no conclusive evidence

;
Nielsen et al. 2019

II
The Spherical Gravastar and Horizon Surface Tension

In order to establish notation and conventions we first briefly review the spherically symmetric nonrotating gravastar of Ref.
Mazur and Mottola 2015
. As shown in
Mazur and Mottola 2015
, this gravastar solution can
be obtained from Schwarzschild’s constant density interior solution
Schwarzschild 1916
;
Synge 1960
by a limiting process in which the star radius
R\to R_{{}_{S}}\equiv 2GM/c^{2}
,
the Schwarzschild horizon radius.
The gravastar of
Mazur and Mottola 2015
may be viewed as a universal limit of the gravitational condensate star
model first proposed in
Mazur and Mottola 2001
;
Mazur and Mottola 2004
, where a thin layer of ultrarelativistic
p=\rho
material was interposed, straddling the mutual Schwarzschild
and de Sitter horizons. It is universal in the sense that when the thickness of the intervening surface layer localized on the horizon is taken to zero, the resulting solution
is independent of any assumptions about the intervening layer equation of state, and the surface stress tensor is instead completely determined by the matching of the interior and
exterior spacetimes on their respective horizons. This universal (nonrotating) gravastar has maximal compactness
GM/R=1/2
(up to possible Planck scale corrections), and evades the
Buchdahl bound
R\geq(9/4)GM
, applicable to isotropic fluid spheres
Buchdahl 1959
, by having an anisotropic stress at its surface.
Like the arbitrarily thin shell
model of
Mazur and Mottola 2001
;
Mazur and Mottola 2004
, the gravastar of
Mazur and Mottola 2015
is a low entropy, cold condensed solution, in no conflict with unitary quantum evolution or statistical mechanics, possesses
no enormous entropy, and hence no information paradox
Mazur and Mottola 2001
;
Mazur and Mottola 2004
.

The general static, spherically symmetric line element can be expressed as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)\,dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{h(r)}+r^{2}\big(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta\,d\phi^{2}\big)

(1)

with

two arbitrary functions of
r

\displaystyle f(r)=e^{2\nu_{0}(r)}=\frac{h(r)}{[j(r)]^{2}},\qquad h(r)=1-\frac{2m(r)}{r}

(2)

in terms of the gravitational potential
\nu_{0}(r)
, the mass function

, and Hartle’s function

The general spherically symmetric solution of Einstein’s equations requires the three stress-energy tensor components

III
Linearized Einstein Equations for Slow Rotation

We are interested in small stationary and axisymmetric perturbations due to rotation
about the static spherically symmetric gravastar solution summarized in the previous section.
There are several forms for the general axisymmetric and stationary metric line element in the literature which may be used for this purpose

;
Papapetrou 1966
;
Hartle and Sharp 1967
;
Hartle 1967
;
Bardeen 1970
;
Chandrasekhar and Friedman 1972
;
Chandrasekhar 1983

{\partial}/{\partial t}
and

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2\nu}dt^{2}+e^{2\psi}\big(d\phi-\omega\,dt\big)^{2}+e^{2\alpha}dr^{2}+e^{2\beta}d\theta^{2}

(13)

where the five functions

\displaystyle e^{\psi}=e^{\beta}\sin\theta

fixes this local coordinate freedom, leaving reparametrizations of

Consider now a stationary, axisymmetric, slowly rotating, nearly spherically symmetric solution,

small perturbations about the static solution that are invariant under the transformations

and

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle-e^{2\nu_{0}(r)}\Big[1+2h_{0}(r)+2h_{2}(r)\,P_{2}(\cos\theta)\Big]dt^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{r}{r-2m(r)}\left\{1+\frac{2}{r-2m(r)}\Big[m_{0}(r)+m_{2}(r)\,P_{2}(\cos\theta)\Big]\right\}dr^{2}

\displaystyle+r^{2}\Big[1+2k_{2}(r)\,P_{2}(\cos\theta)\Big]\Big[d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta\,\big(d\phi-\omega(r)dt\big)^{2}\Big].

VI
Joining Exterior to Interior on the Null Horizon Surface

The two Killing vectors
\partial/\partial t
,
\partial/\partial\phi
determine the location of the null hypersurface
r=R_{{}_{H}}
where the gluing of the interior and exterior solutions occurs
by the geometrically invariant condition that the norm of the vector
\ell=\partial/\partial t+\omega\,\partial/\partial\phi
vanishes there,
i.e.

\displaystyle\ell\cdot\ell=e^{2\nu}=f(r)\Big\{1+2h_{0}(r)+2h_{2}(r)\,P_{2}(\cos\theta)\Big\}=0\,.

(41)

If we impose that
h_{0}
and
h_{2}
are finite at
r=2M
in the exterior and at

in the interior, resulting in Eqs. (
32
) and (
40

6
) from the nonrotating gravastar, then the solution of (
41
) is

.

In the coordinates

adapted to the Killing vectors, the induced metric on the horizon hypersurface at

is

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{H}=\Sigma(\theta)\,\Big[d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\!\theta\,\big(d\phi-\omega_{H}\,dt\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\omega_{H}=\omega(R_{{}_{H}})=\frac{2J}{R_{{}_{H}}^{3}\!}

(43)

and using (

40

\displaystyle\Sigma(\theta)=e^{2\beta(R_{{}_{H}},\,\theta)}=R_{{}_{H}}^{2}\Big[1+2k_{2}(R_{{}_{H}})P_{2}(\cos\theta)\Big]\,.

(44)

The induced metric (
42
) is regular on the null hypersurface, and independent of the perturbation functions
h_{2}
,
m_{2}
. The first junction condition is that the induced metric (
42

R_{{}_{H}}
,
k_{2}(R_{{}_{H}})
, and
\omega(R_{{}_{H}})
have the same value on both sides. These conditions also ensure continuity of the inner products of the Killing vectors,
i.e.
,
\partial/\partial t\cdot\partial/\partial t,~~\partial/\partial t\cdot\partial/\partial\phi,
and
\partial/\partial\phi\cdot\partial/\partial\phi
.

The equality of the perturbation function

as

from both sides of the horizon implies that the interior limit

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to R_{{}_{H}}^{-}}k_{2}(r)=\frac{2A_{I}}{R_{{}_{H}}}-\frac{j^{2}W_{2}^{2}}{2R_{{}_{H}}^{4}}

(45)

from Eq. (

and

, and the exterior limit

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to R_{{}_{H}}^{+}}k_{2}(r)=-\frac{4J^{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{4}}

46

\displaystyle A_{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{j^{2}W_{2}^{2}-8J^{2}}{4R_{{}_{H}}^{3}}

(47)

in terms of
W_{2}
and

. Finally, the equality of

when the horizon is approached from both sides imposes

\displaystyle W_{1}=\frac{2J-W_{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{3}}

and fixes the interior integration constant

.

With the finiteness conditions
B_{E}=B_{I}=0
, and the conditions (
32
), (
40
), (
47
), (
48
) on the horizon, the perturbation functions in the interior of the gravastar

, where

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{2J-W_{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{3}}+\frac{W_{2}}{r^{3}}

(49a)

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\delta M_{I}\left(1-\frac{r^{3}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{3}}\right)+W_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{r^{6}-R_{{}_{H}}^{6}}{r^{3}R_{{}_{H}}^{6}}\right)

(49b)

\displaystyle h_{0}

\displaystyle=C+\frac{R_{{}_{H}}^{2}m_{0}}{r(r^{2}-R_{{}_{H}}^{2})}

(49c)

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{2}^{2}(r^{2}-R_{{}_{H}}^{2})}{r^{3}R_{{}_{H}}^{5}}\left(r^{3}+5r^{2}R_{{}_{H}}-rR_{{}_{H}}^{2}-8R_{{}_{H}}^{3}\right)-\frac{2J^{2}(r^{2}-R_{{}_{H}}^{2})^{2}}{r^{2}R_{{}_{H}}^{5}}

(49d)

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{6W_{2}^{2}}{r^{4}}+\frac{R_{{}_{H}}^{2}m_{2}}{r(r^{2}-R_{{}_{H}}^{2})}

(49e)

\displaystyle k_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{W_{2}^{2}}{2r^{4}R_{{}_{H}}^{4}}(r-R_{{}_{H}})(R_{{}_{H}}^{3}+3rR_{{}_{H}}^{2}+3r^{2}R_{{}_{H}}+5r^{3})-\frac{2J^{2}(r^{2}+R_{{}_{H}}^{2})}{r^{3}R_{{}_{H}}^{3}}

We note that (

The reason for this is that the

(exterior)
or

(interior) satisfy an associated Legendre differential equation with argument

or

respectively, with a

\omega^{\prime\,2}

of the Legendre equation correspond to
\!r=\!2M\!=\!R_{{}_{S}}
and

for the exterior solution, and to

r\!=\!1/H\!=\!R_{{}_{H}}
and

for the interior solution.

(

Requiring that the quadrupole perturbations remain finite at these singular points for both the exterior and interior solutions would set all four integration constants

Thus it is impossible to obtain an interior solution for a slowly rotating gravastar with finite values
at the origin and the horizon, matched there to an asymptotically flat finite solution at infinity

of the nonrotating case. We discuss the implications of this and how the divergences might be

and Conclusions

VII
Surface Gravity and Stress Tensor of the Null Horizon Surface

For timelike or spacelike hypersurfaces, one may relate the surface stress-energy to the discontinuity of the extrinsic curvature defined through the surface
normal vector
{\bm{n}}

Israel 1966
. For a null surface where
{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=0
, the extrinsic curvature vanishes, and the second junction conditions
cannot be applied as formulated in
Israel 1966
. In the Barrabès-Israel method
Barrabès and Israel 1991
, the surface stress-energy for a null surface is defined in terms of the
discontinuity of an “oblique” curvature defined in terms of a second transverse vector
{\bm{N}}
. As discussed in Paper I
Beltracchi et al. 2021
, this method
also does not apply to the case at hand here where
j=\sqrt{h/f}
and
\sqrt{-g}
are discontinuous. Instead Paper I
Beltracchi et al. 2021
contains a derivation of the second
junction conditions for a null surface directly from the Einstein tensor density
\sqrt{-g}\,G^{\mu}_{\ \,\nu}
for the horizon of an arbitrary metric of the form (
13
). Following Paper I, the stress tensor localized on the null horizon surface is
Beltracchi et al. 2021

{}^{(\Sigma)}T^{a}_{\ b}\,e^{\alpha+\nu}={\cal S}^{a}_{\ b}\,\delta(r-R_{{}_{H}})

(50)

for
a,b=t,\theta,\phi
, where
{}^{(\Sigma)}T^{a}_{\ b}
is a well-defined Dirac
\delta
-distribution with respect to the volume integration measure
\sqrt{-g}\,dt\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi
,
analogously to (
10
).

Under transformations of the hypersurface coordinates
(t,\theta,\phi)
,
e^{\beta+\psi}{\cal S}^{a}_{\ b}
transforms as a tensor density, while under transformations involving the surface coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
only,
{\cal S}^{A}_{\ B}
is a tensor,
{\cal S}^{t}_{\ t}
is a scalar, and
{\cal S}^{t}_{\ B}
,
{\cal S}^{A}_{\ t}
are vectors, for
A,B=\theta,\phi
. The null-surface stress tensor has nonzero components
Beltracchi et al. 2021

\displaystyle 8\pi{\cal S}^{t}_{\ t}

\displaystyle=-\omega_{H}[\mathcal{J}]

(51a)

\displaystyle 8\pi{\cal S}^{t}_{\ \phi}

\displaystyle=[\mathcal{J}]

(51b)

\displaystyle 8\pi{\cal S}^{\phi}_{\ t}

\displaystyle=-\omega_{H}[\kappa]-\omega_{H}^{2}[\mathcal{J}]

(51c)

\displaystyle 8\pi{\cal S}^{\phi}_{\ \phi}

\displaystyle=[\kappa]+\omega_{H}[\mathcal{J}]

(51d)

\displaystyle 8\pi{\cal S}^{\theta}_{\ \theta}

\displaystyle=[\kappa]

(51e)

where the surface gravity

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,e^{-\alpha-\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\,e^{2\nu}=\frac{1}{2j}\frac{dh}{dr}\left\{1+h_{0}+h_{2}P_{2}-\left(\frac{m_{0}+m_{2}P_{2}}{r-2m}\right)\right\}+\frac{h}{j}\left(h_{0}^{\prime}+h_{2}^{\prime}P_{2}\right)

(52)

(53)

for a function

\displaystyle[F]=F_{+}-F_{-}

(54)

with

(55)

With the interior and exterior perturbations given by the results of the previous two sections, namely the finiteness conditions
B_{E}=B_{I}=0
, and the conditions (

), (

), (

), (

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to R_{{}_{H}}^{-}}\kappa(r)=-\frac{1}{jR_{{}_{H}}}\,\left(1+C-\frac{3\,\delta M_{I}}{R_{{}_{H}}}+\frac{3j^{2}W_{2}^{2}}{2R_{{}_{H}}^{4}}\right)

(56a)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to R_{{}_{H}}^{+}}\kappa(r)=\frac{1}{2R_{{}_{H}}}\,\left(1-\frac{6J^{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{4}}\right)\,.

(56b)

The discontinuity of the surface gravity at the horizon is therefore

\displaystyle[\kappa]=\kappa_{+}-\kappa_{-}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+C}{j}\right)\frac{1}{R_{{}_{H}}}-\frac{3J^{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}^{5}}-\frac{3\,\delta M_{I}}{jR_{{}_{H}}^{2}}+\frac{3jW_{2}^{2}}{2R_{{}_{H}}^{5}}

(58)

approached from the interior and exterior is

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to R_{{}_{H}}^{+}}{\cal J}(r)=\frac{3J}{R_{{}_{H}}^{2}\!}\,\sin^{2}\!\theta

The surface stress components in (

VIII

VIII.2
Komar angular momentum

The Komar angular momentum is expressed similarly to the Komar mass as
Beltracchi et al. 2021

\displaystyle J_{K}(r)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial V_{+}}\!\!\mathcal{J}\,dA=\int_{V}\sqrt{-g}\ T^{t}_{\ \phi}\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi+\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\partial V_{-}}\!\!\mathcal{J}\,dA

which shows that

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm int}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,r^{2}\sin^{3}\!\theta\left(\frac{3jW_{2}}{2r^{2}}\right)=\frac{jW_{2}}{2}

(78)

which is first order in the rotation. Since this is independent of

T^{t}_{\ \,\phi}=0
in the interior, the volume integral term for angular momentum in (

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm volume}=0

(79)

but the constant contribution

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm origin}=\frac{jW_{2}}{2}

(80)

can be ascribed to the volume term from Eq. (
77

\delta
-function contribution at the origin,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\theta d\phi\sqrt{-g}\,\raise 0.5pt\hbox{$\vbox{\hrule\hbox{\vrule height=7.22743pt\kern 7.22743pt\vrule}\hrule}$}\,K^{t}_{(\phi)}\Big|_{\rm origin}\,=2\int d\theta d\phi\sqrt{-g}T^{t}_{\ \,\phi}\Big|_{\rm origin}=2\,J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm origin}\delta(r)

\delta M_{I}=W_{2}=0

The Komar angular momentum function in the exterior is

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm ext}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\,r^{4}\sin^{3}\!\theta\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(\frac{2J}{r^{3}}\right)=J

(83)

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm surface}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{R_{{}_{H}}-\epsilon}^{R_{{}_{H}}+\epsilon}d\theta d\phi dr\sqrt{-g}\,T^{t}_{\ \phi}=2\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{R_{{}_{H}}-\epsilon}^{R_{{}_{H}}+\epsilon}d\theta dr\,e^{\psi+\beta}\,\delta(r-R_{{}_{H}})\,{\cal S}^{t}_{\ \phi}

(84)

to this order in rotation. This shows that the total Komar angular momentum of the gravastar is the sum of contributions from the surface and the origin,

\displaystyle J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm ext}=J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm origin}+J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm surface}=J

(85)

If
W_{2}=0

J_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm origin}=0
and the entire angular momentum
of the slowly rotating gravastar comes from the rotating surface layer localized on the horizon surface.

VIII.3
Moment of inertia

The moment of inertia
I
of the rotating gravastar is

\displaystyle I=\frac{J}{\omega_{H}}=4G^{2}M^{3}=MR_{{}_{H}}^{2}

(86)

which coincides with that of a Kerr BH. This might have been expected from the fact that the external geometry is identical to the Kerr BH geometry. With
W_{2}=0
,
the interior de Sitter condensate carries no angular momentum from (
79
) and (
80
), and all the angular momentum is
carried by the rotating horizon surface from (
84
). Unlike a BH where there is no mass-energy at all at the
horizon to rotate or to give rise to such a moment of inertia, the rotating gravastar has a well-defined stress energy (
61
) on its rotating surface where all the angular momentum resides, and it is this rotating mass-energy that gives rise to (
86
) by straightforward evaluation of
the relevant surface integral (
84
).

The constant

) stress-energy tensor or curvature tensor of the interior solution. Referring to the metric

and exterior were continuously connected in an appropriate horizon limit, or if

by a more detailed theory of the surface layer, we may expect that

would be fixed by the one condition on the Minkowski static time coordinate as

As it is, in our present treatment because of the lightlike null surface intervening where
f(R_{{}_{H}})=0
, the time coordinate of the interior solution
becomes disconnected from the exterior, and

remains a free parameter, which appears in the interior bulk Komar mass (

stress tensor (

It is important to note that our results are contingent on the surface layer being located on the null horizon and infinitesimally thin, following the matching method presented in Paper I
Beltracchi et al. 2021
.
An infinitesimal surface which is located away from the null horizon leads to different results and has been studied by other authors, to which we may explicitly compare our results. Previous works were based on matching the interior Hartle-Thorne perturbed de Sitter
to exterior geometries on a timelike
Pani 2015
;
Uchikata and Yoshida 2016
;
Uchikata et al. 2016
or spacelike
Uchikata and Yoshida 2014
hypersurface

j=1
and do not consider
{\matheul E}_{0}
, but allow

to be nonzero, since they did not require the
perturbations to be finite at the horizon. They set
A_{I}=W_{2}=\delta M_{I}=0
in order to have finiteness of the perturbations at the origin
r=0
.
Comparing our solution in (
35

Uchikata et al. 2016
, we see that the interior solution of Ref.
Uchikata et al. 2016
amounts to
choosing

, with arbitrary
W_{1},B_{I},C
in the present notation. With these choices,
k_{2}(r)
diverges as
r_{{}_{S}}\to 1/H
unless it vanishes identically,
i.e.
also
B_{I}=0
.
The latter option would then set
m_{0},h_{2},k_{2},m_{2}
all to zero identically, and leave only
\omega=W_{1},h_{0}=C
as constants in the interior. But this interior cannot be matched to the uniquely well behaved Kerr exterior on the horizon.

In contrast, finiteness and matching on the null horizon
surface requires (
38
) and leaves a generally nontrivial interior solution for the perturbations, which however may still diverge at the origin if either
A_{I}
or
W_{2}

are nonvanishing. The junction conditions of matching on the horizon fix the relation (
47

A_{I}
and
W_{2}
to zero if

.

The earlier authors
Uchikata and Yoshida 2014
;
Pani 2015
;
Uchikata and Yoshida 2016
;
Uchikata et al. 2016
applied the Israel junction conditions, which do not apply in the limit

, as they produce either a vanishing
or a divergent result depending on whether the extrinsic curvature is computed with both lower contravariant indices, or with one
of its indices raised, due to the fact that the induced metric on the null horizon is degenerate. The relation between
the surface stress tensors of these previous approaches and the present one is given by (

Beltracchi et al. 2021

Another paper
Rutkowski
uses a different perturbation method in Eddington-Finklestein coordinates on rotating gravastars derived directly from the Schwarzchild star limit. This work finds regular perturbative solutions to second order, which fail only at third order. However, the analysis differs from ours in that the surface of the gravastar is allowed to shift away from being null in the rotating case, whereas we have performed the matching on a null surface in both the static and rotating cases.

Our analysis also differs from
Posada 2017
, where a sub-Buchdahl Schwarzschild star (
R<9M/4
) was studied numerically in the Hartle formalism by matching the interior and exterior metrics continuously on a timelike hypersurface and approaching the horizon limit numerically. There it was suggested that the perturbative corrections were everywhere finite in the gravastar limit. However, below the Buchdahl bound the equation for frame dragging solved numerically, Eq. (50) of
Posada 2017
, does not follow from the change of variables given in the definitions in Eqs. (45)-(49) and the original frame-dragging equation in
r
coordinates,
cf.
Eq. (25). Hence the results in
Posada 2017
would have to be recomputed before comparing them to our work, although the same result (

A major result of our analysis is that although one may remove the
\delta
-function contributions at the origin to both the Komar mass function

M_{K}^{\mkern 1.0mu\rm origin}
and angular momentum

, the quadrupole perturbations

,
h_{2}
, and
k_{2}

remain quadratically or cubically divergent at the origin for

. The perturbations

and
k_{2}
in (

) and (
49f

\displaystyle r_{\rm min}\sim\left(\frac{2J^{2}}{R_{{}_{H}}}\right)^{\!\frac{1}{3}}

(89)

and hence the perturbative expansion breaks down for
r\lesssim r_{\rm min}
(or possibly
r\lesssim\sqrt{|W_{2}|}
if one allows for
nonzero
W_{2}
). There are two possible reasons for this breakdown and corresponding possibilities for removing the divergences at
r=0
.

The first possibility is that the perturbative expansion in slow rotation
J=aM
is nonuniform in
r
, and invalid near the origin.
If a diverging
a^{2}/r^{2}
behavior is replaced by any smooth function
F(r/a)
in a more complete solution,
where
F
tends to unity for large values of its argument, but to zero rapidly enough as
r\to 0
, the divergence
at the origin would be removed. For example

\displaystyle F\left(\mbox{\small$\displaystyle\frac{r}{a}$}\right)=\frac{a^{2}r^{2n-2}}{(a^{2}+r^{2})^{n}}

(90)

for
n>1
is finite both for
a=0

r\to 0
for any
a>0

(r/a)^{2n-2}\to 0

near the origin. However, expanding this function to order
a^{2}
yields a leading term,
a^{2}/r^{2}
which diverges as
r\to 0
. Thus
the diverging behavior at

X
Conclusions

In this paper we have derived and solved analytically the Einstein equations for a slowly rotating gravastar by expanding
the spherically symmetric gravastar solution of
Mazur and Mottola 2015
to second order in the angular momentum,
following the methods of Refs.
Hartle 1967
;
Hartle and Thorne 1968
;
Chandrasekhar and Miller 1974
, and assuming that the equation of state of the slowly rotating system is unchanged from that of the nonrotating solution.

In solving the perturbation equations for slow rotation, we applied the lightlike shell junction conditions of Paper I
Beltracchi et al. 2021
to the concrete case of a rotating gravastar. Previous studies in the Hartle-Thorne formalism have
focused on the case of matching on a timelike or spacelike boundary
Pani 2015
;
Uchikata and Yoshida 2016
;
Uchikata et al. 2016
;
Uchikata and Yoshida 2014
;
Reina and Vera 2015
.

The surface stresses and Komar integrals work as expected, with the modifications appropriate for matching on a null hypersurface. The
\delta
-function distribution stress tensor on the horizon surface (
50
)-(
51
)
accounts for the difference between the exterior and interior Komar mass and angular momentum functions.

In the Komar description, the
p=-\rho
equation of state cannot support any angular momentum density and the angular momentum must
either be localized entirely on the horizon or in singular concentration at the origin. With the conditions (
82
) removing any
\delta
-function contribution at the origin, the entire angular momentum
J
of the rotating solution is carried by the physical surface of the gravastar at the horizon.

Matching solutions on the horizon, we determine the interior metric and the surface stress tensor corrections of first and second order in the rotation.
The exterior solution for the perturbations (
30
) yields exactly the same geometry as that of a Kerr BH, expanded to second order in
a=J/M
, but expressed in Hartle-Thorne coordinates (
15
), which are related to
more familiar Boyer-Lindquist coordinates by (
33
). Thus an important conclusion of this work is

•

Matching the interior of a slowly rotating gravastar to an asymptotically flat exterior vacuum solution of Einstein’s equations at their horizons leads to an external geometry
identical
to that of a slowly rotating Kerr BH, notwithstanding a very different interior and the presence of nonzero energy density and stresses (
88
) localized on the physical surface, that takes the place of the Kerr BH horizon.

Since the exterior of the slowly rotating gravastar is precisely that of the Kerr BH to order
a^{2}
, there is no possibility of detecting the difference to this order by any experiment or observation restricted to the large scale external geometry such as by accretion flows or light ring images.
Like a classical Kerr BH, a slowly rotating gravastar with a null surface stress tensor also has “no hair” classically, up to possibly Planck scale corrections very near to the horizon surface. The fact that this surface remains at the horizon, and the moment of inertia (
86
) remains identical to that of a Kerr BH
to this order also implies that a rotating gravastar defined this way will also have the same zero tidal deformability or Love number as a Kerr BH, at least to order
a^{2}
.

If the
\delta
-function surface stress-energies were to be replaced by a microscopically thin shell of radial extent
\Delta r
, several studies
Cardoso et al. 2017
;
Chirenti et al. 2020
indicate that the corrections to this null result may be of order
[\log(\Delta r/M)]^{-1}
, so that even a
\Delta r
of order the Planck length could
potentially produce dimensionless Love numbers of a few tenths of a percent, which could lead to observable tidal effects that can distinguish ultra-compact objects from BHs in future gravitational wave detectors
Uchikata et al. 2016
;
Cardoso et al. 2017
. This question remains open for the universal null shell gravastars considered in this paper.

Assuming that the interior equation of state for a slowly rotating gravastar is unchanged from its nonrotating (
17
)-(
19
) form,
the regular matching of the exterior Kerr and the interior spacetime at the horizon gives Eq. (
47
), which in our calculation implies a
divergence of some perturbation functions at the origin. As explained in Sec.
VI
, since the horizons of the interior and exterior geometries are at singular points of the differential equation governing the quadrupolar
perturbations, the four conditions:

(i)

Finiteness at the origin,

(ii)

Finiteness on the horizon,

(iii)

Finiteness at asymptotic infinity (
i.e.
asymptotic flatness), and

(iv)

Matching (first junction condition) on the horizon,

cannot be simultaneously satisfied if the equation of state in the interior is fixed to be
p=-\rho
.
Thus a second important conclusion of this work is:

•

There is no solution of the Hartle perturbation equations for a slowly rotating
gravastar that is regular both at the horizon and the origin, and can be matched on the horizon to
an asymptotically flat exterior, if the equation of state in the interior is fixed to be
p=-\rho
of the nonrotating gravastar.

The divergences may indicate that the slow rotation expansion is nonuniform and breaks down as
r\to 0
, and/or that the assumption that the equation of state is unchanged from
p=-\rho
is overly restrictive for ultracompact objects such as gravastars,
and should be relaxed. If instead, the equation of state differs from the dark energy one anywhere in the interior, then the perturbation functions inside the gravastar
will also be different and so their matching to the exterior functions at the null surface may become possible, while still remaining finite
at the origin.

The corollary is that the slowly rotating gravastar model generated in this way by the Hartle-Thorne
formalism
Hartle and Thorne 1968
;
Chandrasekhar and Miller 1974
from the spherically symmetric case is necessarily incomplete, and not fully satisfactory. The present work
represents an initial exploration of rotating gravastars with a physical surface localized on the BH horizon, whose results it is hoped
will suggest approaches to a solution of the rotating gravastar interior, beyond the perturbative framework utilized in this paper. This requires a better understanding of the equation of state appropriate for rotating gravitational condensates.

No attempt has been made in this paper to present either a microscopic theory of the surface stresses, which requires
the incorporation of quantum vacuum polarization effects such as those discussed in
Mottola and Vaulin 2006
;
Mottola 2010
,
nor a detailed model of the dynamical formation of rotating gravastars with
p=-\rho
cores from positive pressure matter.
See for example
Beltracchi and Gondolo 2019
for preliminary study of a model of gravastar formation. These important
issues remain open to further investigation.

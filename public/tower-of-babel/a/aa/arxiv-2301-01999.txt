Abstract
We study positivity bounds in the presence of gravity. We first review the gravitational positivity bound at the tree-level, where it is known that a certain amount of negativity is allowed for the coefficients of higher-derivative operators. The size of these potentially negative contributions is estimated for several tree-level, Reggeized gravitational amplitudes which are unitary at high energies and feature the t t -channel pole characteristic of graviton exchange. We also argue for the form of the one-loop Regge amplitude assuming that the branch cut structure associated with the exchange of the graviton and higher-spin particles is reflected. We demonstrate how the one-loop Regge amplitude appears by summing over Feynman diagrams. For our one-loop amplitude proposal, the positivity bounds generically receive a finite contribution from the Regge tower and do not lead to a parametrically small bound on the cut-off scale of the low-energy EFT, consistent with recent studies based on sum rules of the amplitude.

. For simplicity, we restrict our attention to the following form of
K(s,t)
:

\displaystyle K(s,t)=P\left(s^{2}t^{2}+t^{2}u^{2}+s^{2}u^{2}\right)\,,

(26)

where
P
is a positive constant.
By using the Stirling formula,
\Gamma(z)\sim\sqrt{2\pi}\,z^{z-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-z}
, one can easily see that the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{A}(s,t)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s,t)\sim F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)\alpha(s)^{2\alpha(t)+2}\,,

(27)

where

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)=P\left(\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{4}e^{\pi i\alpha(t)}\frac{\Gamma(-\alpha(t))}{\Gamma(1+\alpha(t))}\,.

(28)

Note that
F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)
has poles on the real axis at
\alpha(t)=n
with
n\geq 0
.
The residue in the
t
-plane is

\displaystyle\res_{\alpha(t)=n}\mathcal{A}(s,t)

\displaystyle=-K\left(s,\frac{4n}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha(s))}{n!\Gamma(1+\alpha(s))}\right)^{2}=-K\left(s,\frac{4n}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\frac{1}{\left(n!\right)^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\alpha(s)+j\right)^{2}\,.

(29)

It is clear that the residue at
\alpha(t)=n
is a polynomial in
s
of order
2n+2
, and, in particular at
t=0
, the residue is

\displaystyle-\frac{64Ps^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime 3}}\,.

(30)

The pole expansion in the
t
- and
u
-channel is thus given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s,t)=-\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\left(\alpha(s)+1\right)_{n-1}}{n!}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{K\left(s,4n\alpha^{\prime-1}\right)}{t-4n\alpha^{\prime-1}}-\frac{K\left(s,-s-4n\alpha^{\prime-1}\right)}{t+s+4n\alpha^{\prime-1}}\right]\,,

(31)

where we have used the Pochhammer symbol defined as

\displaystyle(x)_{m}:=\frac{\Gamma(x+m)}{\Gamma(x)}=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(x+j)\,.

(32)

For
s\to\pm i\infty
, in particular, the leading contribution from
t
-channel poles is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s,t)\sim-P\left(\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n!)^{2}}\frac{\alpha(s)^{2n+2}}{t-4n\alpha^{\prime-1}}\,.

(33)

Note that this expression as an infinite sum coincides with the Regge behavior (
27
) with
t
-channel poles expanded since
F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)=-P\left(\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n!)^{2}}\frac{1}{t-4n\alpha^{\prime-1}}\,.

(34)

By using (
29
) with
s\leftrightarrow t
crossing symmetry, we find

\displaystyle\disc_{s}\mathcal{A}(s,t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\delta(\alpha(s)-n)\res_{\alpha(s)=n}\mathcal{A}(s,t)

(35)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}K\left(\frac{4n}{\alpha^{\prime}},t\right)\left(\frac{\left(\alpha(t)+1\right)_{n-1}}{n!}\right)^{2}\delta(s-4n\alpha^{\prime-1})\,,

and then the integral that we would like to evaluate is

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda^{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{s}\mathcal{A}(s^{\prime},t)}{s^{\prime 3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{16P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{\left(\alpha(t)+1\right)_{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(t)

(36)

\displaystyle=-\frac{32P}{\alpha^{\prime 3}t}+\mathcal{O}(t)\,,

where we assume
1/\alpha^{\prime}>\Lambda^{2}
.
The first term on the right-hand side must ensure the cancellation of the graviton
t
-channel pole, and hence we find

\displaystyle\frac{32P}{\alpha^{\prime 3}}\sim M_{\text{Pl}}^{-2}\,.

(37)

Note that we do not obtain any
\mathcal{O}(t^{0})
contributions from the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude (
25
).

Let us next consider the following deformation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(s,t)\to\mathcal{A}(s,t)+\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)\,,

(38)

where
\delta\mathcal{A}
is defined as

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)=-\epsilon K(s,t)\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha(s))\Gamma(1-\alpha(t)\Gamma(1-\alpha(u))}{\Gamma(2+\alpha(s))\Gamma(2+\alpha(t))\Gamma(2+\alpha(u))}\,.

(39)

The positivity of residues requires that the deformation parameter should be bounded as
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1

Arkani-Hamed:2020blm
. Note that
\delta\mathcal{A}
can be expressed in terms of
\mathcal{A}
as follows:

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)=\epsilon\frac{\alpha(s)\alpha(t)\alpha(u)}{(1+\alpha(s))(1+\alpha(t))(1+\alpha(u))}\mathcal{A}(s,t)\,.

(40)

It is hence straightforward to see the Regge behavior of
\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)
is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)\sim\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)\alpha(s)^{2\alpha(t)+2}\,,

(41)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)=\epsilon\frac{\alpha(t)}{1+\alpha(t)}F_{\mathrm{VS}}(t)=\epsilon P\left(\frac{4}{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)^{4}e^{\pi i\alpha(t)}\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha(t))}{\Gamma(2+\alpha(t))}\,.

(42)

Note that
\delta\mathcal{A}
has the same soft behavior as

in the high energy region with

-channel pole appears at

The residue of

\displaystyle\res_{\alpha(s)=n}\delta\mathcal{A}(s,t)=K\left(\frac{4n}{\alpha^{\prime}},t\right)\frac{\left(\alpha(t)\right)_{n-1}\left(\alpha(t)+2\right)_{n-1}}{(n-1)!(n+1)!}\,,

(43)

and the integral is evaluated as

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda^{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{s}\delta\mathcal{A}(s^{\prime},t)}{s^{\prime 3}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16\epsilon P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(n+1\right)\frac{\left(\alpha(t)\right)_{n}\left(\alpha(t)+2\right)_{n}}{n!(n+2)!}+\mathcal{O}(t)

(44)

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\epsilon P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}{}_{2}F_{3}\left(2,\alpha(t),\alpha(t)+2;1,3;1\right)+\mathcal{O}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\epsilon P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}+\mathcal{O}(t)\,.

We can thus obtain the
\mathcal{O}(t^{0})
negative term by deforming the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude by
\delta\mathcal{A}
defined in (
39

One can also obtain the same result by using (

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda^{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{s}\mathcal{A}(s^{\prime},t)}{s^{\prime 3}}

\displaystyle\sim\epsilon\frac{\alpha(t)}{1+\alpha(t)}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{s}\mathcal{A}(s^{\prime},t)}{s^{\prime 3}}

(45)

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\epsilon P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}+\mathcal{O}(t)\,.

From (

) and the bound on

\displaystyle\frac{8\epsilon P}{\alpha^{\prime 2}}\lesssim\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,.

(46)

This bound on the negativity is consistent with the recent paper
Noumi:2022wwf
, in which constraints on Regge amplitudes are considered by using the finite-energy sum rules and IR part of the null constraints.

3.2
Coon amplitude

The Coon amplitude
Coon:1969yw
is a

;
Geiser:2022exp
;

, to date the Coon amplitude has no known worldsheet origin.
Indeed, the unitarity of the Coon amplitude has recently been called into question

.
Nevertheless, we take it as a well-studied amplitude and use it as a building block in a crossing-symmetric amplitude featuring both Regge behavior and a
t
-channel pole characteristic of graviton exchange.

The Coon amplitude has the following product representation,

4
Positivity bounds at one loop

In this section, we first see how the positivity bound at the one-loop level leads to an apparent puzzle that the cut-off scale of the theory is extremely low, under the assumption that the graviton
t
-channel pole is neglected.
Then, we propose how the Reggeization extends to one-loop level.

4.1
Small cut-off scale

Suppose that the approximate gravitational positivity bound (
24
) at tree-level is correct even at the one-loop level.
Then, Ref.
Alberte:2020bdz
found that the left-hand side of (
24
) is computed as

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}\Delta\mathcal{A}(0,0,0)-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\Lambda^{2}}\mathrm{d}{s^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{s}\mathcal{A}_{\text{IR}}(s^{\prime},0,u^{\prime})}{s^{\prime 3}}-\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\Lambda^{2}}\mathrm{d}{u^{\prime}}\,\frac{\disc_{u}\mathcal{A}_{\text{IR}}(s^{\prime},0,u^{\prime})}{u^{\prime 3}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{e^{4}}{4\pi^{2}\Lambda^{4}}-\frac{e^{2}}{180\pi^{2}m^{2}M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\,.

(62)

Note that the second term in the second line corresponds to a gravitational diagram while the first term corresponds to a non-gravitational diagram.
Then, using (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{e^{4}}{4\pi^{2}\Lambda^{4}}-\frac{e^{2}}{180\pi^{2}m^{2}M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\geq-\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}\right),

(63)

which leads to the upper bound on
\Lambda
:

\Lambda\lesssim\sqrt{emM_{\text{Pl}}}\,,\qquad\quad\text{for $m^{2}\ll e^{2}\alpha^{\prime-1}$}\,.

(64)

Similar results are reported for the Standard Model
Aoki:2021ckh
and the dark photon model
Noumi:2022zht
.
In particular, using this argument, it is argued that almost all parameter regions of the dark photon model are excluded
Noumi:2022zht
.

Note that even if a higher-dimensional term is added to quadratic action (
3
), there is no change in the positivity bound (
64
).
For example, we can consider the additional term with mass dimension six,

S_{6}=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}{x}\,\sqrt{-g}\frac{c}{\Lambda^{2}}\phi^{\dagger}\phi F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,,

(65)

where
c
is a constant.
A new vertex (Fig.
3
) and diagrams arise from Eq. (

) cancel out.

After that, it turns out that in the case of graviton-photon

Let us take type IIB theory compactified on Calabi-Yau threefold.

4.2
Reggeization at the loop level

In the previous section, we first argue that the bound (
64
) follows from the gravitational positivity bound (
24
).
Then, we argue that the bound contradicts with the well-established string theory vacua.
This indicates that the derivation of Eq. (
64
) in Sec.
2.2
should be revisited.

In the following, we argue that the Regge amplitude at the loop level changes the argument leading to Eq. (
64
).
In Eq. (
18
), we have used
f\approx f_{\text{tree}}
. Instead, if we keep the quantum correction
f=f_{\text{tree}}+f_{\text{loop}}
, then the term

\displaystyle-\frac{2f_{\text{loop}}^{\prime}(t)}{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}\alpha^{\prime}}

(66)

is added to the right-hand side of Eq. (
21
).
By comparing this with Eq. (
62
), we observe that if the term (
66
) cancels the negative contribution corresponding to the graviton exchange, then it helps with positivity.
This is a resolution of the contradiction in the previous section.
Naively, the cancellation between Eq. (
66
) and
-e^{2}/(180\pi^{2}m^{2}M_{\text{Pl}}^{2})
is unlikely because the UV Regge amplitude should “know" the IR mass scale
m
.
However, here we propose the generalization of the Regge amplitude to the one-loop level, which realizes the cancellation naturally.

At the tree level, we have assumed the Reggeization

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\to F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)\frac{s^{2+\alpha^{\prime}t}}{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}M_{*}^{2\alpha^{\prime}t}}

(67)

for fixed
t
and large
s
.
Here
F_{\mathrm{tree}}
has mass dimension
-2
, and the relation with
f_{\text{tree}}
in Eq. (
18
) is
F_{\mathrm{tree}}\sin(\alpha^{\prime}t)=f_{\text{tree}}
.
This is expected to happen by the exchange of the higher spin particles.
Given this interpretation, the important requirement to the behavior of
F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)
is that

•

F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)
must have poles at
t=n\,\alpha^{\prime-1}
where
n=0,1,\ldots
. Therefore,
F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)
is written as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)=\frac{f_{0}}{t}+\frac{f_{1}}{t-\alpha^{\prime-1}}+\frac{f_{2}}{t-2\alpha^{\prime-1}}+\cdots\,,

(68)

where
f_{i}
is a constant.
Since each residue of the pole must be positive, we obtain
f_{i}>0
.
The presence of the pole at
t=0
is crucial to maintain the positivity bound at the tree-level.
Because of the poles, the amplitude at
t=n\,\alpha^{\prime-1}
is dominated by the diagram corresponding to the exchange of the particle of the mass
n\,\alpha^{\prime-1}
.

Next, we have to impose an extra requirement at the one-loop level. We denote the amplitude as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\to\big[F_{\mathrm{tree}}(t)+F_{\mathrm{loop}}(t)\big]\frac{s^{2+\alpha^{\prime}t}}{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}M_{*}^{2\alpha^{\prime}t}}\,.

(69)

At the one-loop level, contributing diagrams include those with a loop of light particles and tree-level exchange of higher spin particles (see Sec.
4.3
).
Importantly, these diagrams have a branch cut starting from
t=4m^{2}
corresponding to particle production.

•

At the one-loop level, the branch cut must appear at
t=4m^{2}
.
F_{\mathrm{loop}}
is written as

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{loop}}(t)=\tilde{f}_{0}L_{0}(t,m^{2})+\frac{\tilde{f}_{1}\alpha^{\prime-1}}{t-\alpha^{\prime-1}}L_{1}(t,m^{2})+\frac{\tilde{f}_{2}\alpha^{\prime-1}}{t-2\alpha^{\prime-1}}L_{2}(t,m^{2})+\cdots\,,

(70)

where
L_{i}(t,m^{2})
is a loop function that contains the branch cut starting from
t=4m^{2}
(see Sec.
4.3
for the detail), and has an expansion

\displaystyle L_{i}(t,m^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{l_{j}^{(i)}}{m^{2}}\left(\frac{t}{m^{2}}\right)^{j-1}\,,

(71)

up to
\log(m^{2})
. Here
l_{j}
is a dimensionless constant.
On top of the branch cut, there exist poles at
t=k\alpha^{\prime}
where
k=1,2,\ldots
.

Now we discuss the implications of the Regge amplitude on the dispersion relation.
The contribution from Eq. (

5
Discussion

In this paper, we have studied the gravitational positivity bound at the one-loop level.
We first reviewed the gravitational positivity bound at the tree-level, where the
t
-channel pole corresponding to the graviton exchange can be canceled by assuming Regge behavior for the amplitude at high energy.
Next, we saw two examples of unitary, Reggeized gravitational amplitudes for which the potentially negative finite contribution to the positivity bounds can be found.
Finally, we moved to the features of Reggeized amplitudes at one loop. It is known that the one-loop EFT amplitude leads to the parametrically large negative contribution to the positivity bound. We argued for a form of the Reggeized amplitude at the one-loop level based on the analytic structure of the
t
-channel exchange diagram of the graviton and the higher spin particles in the Regge tower.
We also provide an argument that the graviton
t
-channel pole can not be neglected by showing a counterexample to Eq. (
1
) in string theory. These results are consistent with Refs.
Alberte:2021dnj
;
Herrero-Valea:2022lfd
;
deRham:2022gfe
.

As a future direction, we may consider a string theory setup to explicitly check the form of the amplitude at the one-loop of the matter fields.
Ideally, we want to analyze the amplitude in the Calabi-Yau compactification near the conifold point, but this is hard. Instead, we may consider the compactifications on the flat background.
For instance, we may take the non-supersymmetric
\SO(16)\times\SO(16)
heterotic string and compactify it on
T^{6}
.

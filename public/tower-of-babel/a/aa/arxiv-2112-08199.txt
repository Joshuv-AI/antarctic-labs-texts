M-Estimation based on quasi-processes from discrete samples of Lévy processes
Yasutaka Shimizu and Hiroshi Shiraishi

Abstract
We consider M-estimation problems, where the target value is determined using a minimizer of an expected functional of a Lévy process. With discrete observations from the Lévy process, we can produce a “quasi-path” by shuffling increments of the Lévy process; we call it a quasi-process . Under a suitable sampling scheme, the distribution of a quasi-process can converge weakly to one of the true process according to the properties of the stationary and independent increments. Using this resampling technique, we can estimate objective functionals similar to those estimated using the Monte Carlo simulations. It is available as a contrast function. The M-estimator based on these quasi-processes can be consistent and asymptotically normal. Keywords: M-estimation; Lévy processes; resampling; quasi-process; discrete observations. Introduction

On a stochastic basis,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}};\mathbb{F})

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}[e^{it\widetilde{X}_{1}}]=i\mu t-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}t^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itz}-1-itz\mathbf{1}_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\right)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z).

(1.1)

Let
\mathbb{D}_{T}:=D([0,T])\ (0<T\leq\infty)
be a family of càdlàg functions on

(or
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)

generated by the Skorokhod topology, which is metrizable with a suitable metric, called the

, and

can be a Polish space; see Billingsley

1

, Section 12 for details.
Then

is a measurable map
X:(\Omega,\mathcal{F})\to(\mathbb{D}_{T},{\cal D}_{T})
.
Throughout this study, we denote the distribution of

Let

This study aims to statistically infer

This inference problem is often found in some applications in finance and insurance.
For instance, consider a strategy of striking a
perpetual American put option
for a stock price

such that we strike the right when
X_{t}<\vartheta
for a certain threshold
\vartheta>0
, and the strike time is given by a stopping time
\tau^{\vartheta}:=\inf\{t>0\,|\,X_{t}<\vartheta\}
. To search for the optimal
\vartheta
, say
\vartheta_{*}
, it would be natural to consider

\displaystyle\vartheta_{*}:=\arg\max_{\vartheta\in[0,K]}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-r\tau^{\vartheta}}(K-X_{\tau^{\vartheta}})_{+}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau^{\vartheta}<\infty\}}\right],

(1.3)

where
r>0
is the interest rate, and

is the strike price; see Gerber and Shiu
[
8
]
. Here, it is not easy to estimate the objective expectation from the observations of the stock price data
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
because it depends on the entire path of
X
,
and it is an implicit form.

There is another example from insurance risk theory. Assuming that process
X
is a surplus for an insurance contract and considering the present value of a
ruin-related ‘loss’ up to the time of ruin
,

\displaystyle h_{\vartheta}(X)=\int_{0}^{\tau^{\vartheta}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\tau^{\vartheta}:=\inf\{t>0\,|\,X_{t}<f(\vartheta)\},

where

and

[
4
]
and Feng and Shimizu
[
5
]
. Hereafter,

given by (
1.2
) is an optimal parameter for minimizing the expected discounted ‘risk’ for surplus
X
. For instance, consider a
dividend strategy
that pays ratio
\alpha\in(0,1)
when the insurance surplus is over threshold
\vartheta>0
. The expected total dividends up to the ruin is given by

U_{\vartheta}(x)=\alpha\mathbf{1}_{[\vartheta,\infty)}(x)

\displaystyle Ph_{\vartheta}=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}\geq\vartheta,\,t\leq\tau^{\vartheta})\,\mathrm{d}t.

(1.4)

In this quantity, the probability of paying the dividends is small when
\vartheta
is large, although large dividends are paid, and vice versa. Therefore, the expectation can be optimized to a suitable level.

In these examples, we need to estimate the expected functionals (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) from observations of the Lévy process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. However, it is often difficult to estimate these functionals because they include a random time,
\tau^{\vartheta}
, which is often not observable in practice. If we observed many paths of the true process, we could estimate it using the Monte Carlo simulations, but this is impossible in practice.

This paper proposes a new methodology to solve such problems. Contrary to generating paths, we will produce “quasi-paths” from discrete observations of the Lévy process. This idea is similar to bootstrapping. Given discrete samples of
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, say
(X_{t_{0}},X_{t_{1}},\dots,X_{t_{n}})
with
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=T
and
h_{n}\equiv t_{k}-t_{k-1}\ (k=1,2,\dots,n)
, we approximate the path by a step function that jumps at
t_{k}\ (k=1,2,\dots,n)
with jump size
\Delta_{k}X=X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}
; see Section
2
for details. By shuffling the order of the increments, we can create different step functions, which can be regarded as different (discrete) paths from the true distribution.
Using these “quasi-paths”, we can estimate the expected functions of
X
and construct an estimator of
\vartheta_{0}
as an M-estimator. Additionally, we can provide sufficient conditions to ensure that the M-estimator can be consistent and asymptotically normal, which are the main results of this study.

The advantage of this method lies in its versatility. It can be applied to any complex random variable or loss function because it approximates the distribution by a path-base without specifying a concrete model of the Lévy process.
For example, it can estimate moments, extreme value quantities such as maximum/minimum values, and quantiles of
X_{\tau}
for a stopping time
\tau
, which may depend on the whole path of the process. The VaR and expected shortfalls in finance are also important examples in practice. Of course, it is possible to apply not only to vanilla options as described above but also to the exotic options.
Moreover, in many statistical problems, estimators for unknown parameters are usually defined as an “optimizer” of some loss function, so they are widely available for general statistical problems such as maximum likelihood estimation, least squares method,
L^{1}
regularization, and so on.
For example, in machine learning, splitting a decision tree is a problem; it also involves optimizing a loss function that determines the location of the split, which is also within the M-estimation framework. Our method is powerful enough to be applied to all such problems that rely on L’evy processes.

in (

We will make a further paper dealing with more detailed examples using our techniques: Shimizu and Shiraishi

2
Contrast functions via “quasi-processes”

We observed the Lévy process,
X
, starting from
u
equidistantly in time: the observations consist of

\{X_{t_{k}}\}_{k=0,1,\dots,n}
, where

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=T;\quad h_{n}\equiv t_{k}-t_{k-1}.

Let
\mathbb{X}=(\Delta_{1}X,\Delta_{2}X,\dots,\Delta_{n}X)
be a vector of increments with
\Delta_{k}X:=X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}
, and let

\Lambda_{n}:=\left\{i_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\dots&n\\
i_{m}(1)&i_{m}(2)&\dots&i_{m}(n)\end{pmatrix}\,\bigg|\,m=1,2,\dots,n!\right\}

be a family of all the permutations of
(1,2,\dots,n)
.
Because
\Delta_{k}X
’s
(1\leq k\leq n)
are i.i.d. for each
n
,
\mathbb{X}
is
exchangeable
; that is, it follows for any permutation
i\in\Lambda_{n}
that

i(\mathbb{X}):=(\Delta_{i(1)}X,\dots,\Delta_{i(n)}X),\quad i\in\Lambda_{n}.

has the same distribution as
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For given
\mathbb{X}
and
i\in\Lambda_{n}
,
a stochastic process
\widehat{X}^{i,n}=(\widehat{X}^{i,n}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
given by

\displaystyle\widehat{X}^{i,n}_{t}=u+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta_{i(k)}X\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},\infty)}(t)

is said to be a
quasi-process of
X
for a permutation
i\in\Lambda_{n}
.

The path of the quasi-process
\widehat{X}^{i,n}=(\widehat{X}^{i,n}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
belongs to
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
: a right continuous step function that has a jump at
t=t_{k}\ (k=1,2,\dots,n)
with amplitude
\Delta_{i(k)}X
.

For a given number
\alpha_{n}\ (\leq n!)
, let
\{i_{(1)},\dots,i_{(\alpha_{n})}\}
be IID samples withdrawn independently of
X
, uniformly from
\Lambda_{n}
: for a given
m=1,2,\dots,n!
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(i_{(k)}=i_{m}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\quad\mbox{for every $k=1,2,\dots,n!$.}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha_{n}}\delta_{\widehat{X}^{i_{(k)},n}},

(2.1)

where
\delta_{x}
is the delta measure concentrated on
x\in\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
.
A sample
\{i_{(1)},\dots,i_{(\alpha_{n})}\}
is a random sampling from the index set of increments, which can be interpreted as an extension of the bootstrapping.
In particular, as we fix a sequence of permutation sets
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we write

Definition 2.2
.

Given a vector of increments,
\mathbb{X}
, and a number
\alpha_{n}\ (\leq n!)
,
we denote the
minimum contrast estimator
of
\vartheta_{0}
in (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}=\arg\min_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta},

(2.2)

where
h_{\vartheta}(x)=h(x,\vartheta)
for each
x\in\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
.

In some special cases where
Ph_{\vartheta}
is a function of the Lévy measure
\nu
of the form

Ph_{\vartheta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{\vartheta}(z)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z)

for some
H_{\vartheta}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
Ph_{\vartheta}
can be estimated as

\widehat{Ph_{\vartheta}}=\frac{1}{nh_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}H_{\vartheta}^{n}(\Delta_{k}X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}Ph_{\vartheta}

with some
H^{n}_{\vartheta}\to H_{\vartheta}
under some regularities; Comte and Genon-Catalot
[
6
]
; Jacod
[
9
]
; Shimizu
[
13
]
; Kato and Kuris
[
11
]
.
In this case, the quasi-process does not make sense because the estimator is exchangeable with respect to increments
(\Delta_{k}X)_{k=1,2,\dots}
. Our method is particularly advantageous when function
h_{\vartheta}
depends on the path of
X
.
For instance, when considering an example in (
1.4
), we may need a lot of data such as
X_{t}\geq\vartheta
in the past. Using quasi-processes
\widehat{X}^{i,n}
, we can select many “quasi-samples” such that
\widehat{X}^{i,n}_{t}\geq\vartheta
, which gives us a better approximation of
{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}\geq\vartheta)
.

3
Weak convergence for quasi-processes

3.1
Theoretical results

Here, we consider a
high-frequency sampling in the long term
:

(HFLT)

h_{n}\to 0
, and
T\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the sampling scheme (HFLT), it holds for any sequence of permutations
\{i^{n}\}\subset\Lambda_{n}
that

\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n}\leadsto X\quad\mbox{ in $\mathbb{D}_{\infty}$},\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

Note that the process
X
generally has the following decomposition:

X_{t}=u+\mu t+\sigma W_{t}+S_{t},

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma\geq 0
, and
W
are the Wiener process and
S
is the pure-jump Lévy process independent of
W
, with the characteristic exponent

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}[e^{itS_{1}}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{itz}-1-itz\mathbf{1}_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}\right)\,\nu(\mathrm{d}z).

Hereafter, we fix permutation
i^{n}\in\Lambda_{n}
arbitrarily, and let
X^{n}:=\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n}
for simplicity of notation

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}=u+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mu h_{n}+\sigma\Delta_{i^{n}(k)}W+\Delta_{i^{n}(k)}S\right)\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k},\infty)}(t),

where
\Delta_{k}W=W_{t_{k}}-W_{t_{k-1}}
, and so does
\Delta_{k}S
.
Because the limit process
X
is a Lévy process, which is stochastically continuous,
the claim of the statement is equivalent that the following conditions (a) and (b) hold:

(a)

Any finite-dimensional distribution of
X^{n}
converges weakly to the corresponding finite-dimensional distribution of
X
: for any
d
time points
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\dots<s_{d}
, it holds that

(X^{n}_{s_{1}},\dots,X^{n}_{s_{d}})\leadsto(X_{s_{1}},\dots,X_{s_{d}})
as
n\to\infty

because the probability that the Lévy process jumps at a fixed time point is zero;

(b)

The tightness of
X^{n}
in
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathbb{P}}(\varrho(X^{n},X)>M)=0
;

see Billingsley
[
1
]
, Section 13 for details.

For (a), we show the case where
d=2
for simplicity of discussion, and for any
s<t
, we show that

\displaystyle(X^{n}_{s},X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{s})\leadsto(X_{s},X_{t}-X_{s}),\quad n\to\infty,,

(3.1)

which leads to
(X^{n}_{s},X^{n}_{t})\leadsto(X_{s},X_{t})
by the continuous mapping theorem.
Here, when
n
is sufficiently large, we assume that
s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i})
and
t\in[t_{j-1},t_{j})
for
t_{i}\leq t_{j-1}
without loss of generality. Hereafter, the components of
i(\mathbb{X})
are independent and stationary; it suffices for (
3.1
) to show that

\displaystyle X^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle\leadsto X_{s};\qquad X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{s}\leadsto X_{t}-X_{s},

(3.2)

separately. Moreover, for
t_{k_{0}}<s<t_{k_{0}+1}<t

X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Delta_{i^{n}(k_{0}+k)}X\cdot\mathbf{1}_{[t_{k_{0}+k},\infty)}(t)

and
\Delta_{i^{n}(k)}X\,(k=1,2,\dots,n)
are identically distributed, it suffices to show the first convergence in (
3.2
) for any initial value
u\geq 0
. Hence, we show only the first one.

Fixing any
t\in(0,T]
, we can assume that there exists some
t_{k}
such that
t\in(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
and

t_{k}\downarrow t
,
t_{k-1}\uparrow t
as
n\to\infty
.
For
t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k})
,

X^{n}_{t}=^{d}X_{t}+(X_{t_{k-1}}-X_{t}).

because
X^{n}_{t}=^{d}X_{t_{k-1}}
based on the property of independent and stationary increments of
X
.
Hence, we obtain
X^{n}_{t}\leadsto X_{t}
from the stochastic continuity of the Lévy processes:

X_{t_{k-1}}-X_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
as
t_{k-1}\to t
and Slutsky’s lemma; van der Vaart
[
15
]
, Theorem 2.7, (iv).

For the proof of (b): the tightness of
X^{n}
, we use the following lemma obtained from
Theorem 16.9 by Billingsley
[
1
]
and its corollary.

Lemma 3.1
.

A sequence of
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
-valued random elements
\{X^{n}\}_{n}
is uniformly tight when the following conditions (i)–(iii) hold true:

(i)

For any
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\lim_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{t}|\geq a)=0.

(ii)

For any
t>0
,

\lim_{a\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{s\leq t}|X^{n}_{s}-X^{n}_{s-}|\geq a\right)=0

(iii)

For each
{\epsilon},\eta,t>0
, there exists a
\delta_{0}
and
n_{0}
such that, if
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
n\geq n_{0}
, and if
\tau
is a discrete
X^{n}
-stopping time satisfying
\tau\leq t
, then

{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{\tau+\delta}-X^{n}_{\tau}|\geq{\epsilon})\leq\eta.

First, we show (i) for any
t>0
,
t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k})
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
n
large enough because
T\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Further, we suppose that
t_{k-1}\uparrow t
and
t_{k}\downarrow t
as
n\to\infty
, as in the proof of (a).

For any
a>|u|+|\mu|t_{k-1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{t}|\geq a)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sigma\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}W\right|\geq\frac{a-|u|-|\mu|t_{k-1}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S\right|\geq\frac{a-|u|-|\mu|t_{k-1}}{2}\right).

Through infinite divisibility of
W
and
S
and their stochastic continuity, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}W

\displaystyle\big(=^{d}W_{t_{k-1}}\big)\leadsto W_{t};\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S\big(=^{d}S_{t_{k-1}}\big)\leadsto S_{t}.

Hence, we observe that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{t}|\geq a)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\sigma W_{t}\right|\geq(a-|u|-|\mu|t_{k-1})/2\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|S_{t}\right|\geq(a-|u|-|\mu|t_{k-1})/2\right)

\displaystyle\to 0\quad(a\to\infty),

by the tightness of the random variables
\sigma W_{t}
and
S_{t}
.

Second, we show (ii). Fixing
t>0
in the proof of (i), note that, for any
a>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{s\leq t}|X^{n}_{s}-X^{n}_{s-}|\geq 3a\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(|\mu h_{n}|\geq a\right)+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}\sigma|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}W|\geq a\right)

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|\geq a\right).

The first term on the right-hand side clearly goes to zero as
n\to\infty
.
As for the second and third terms, the proofs for those are essentially the same because
W
is also a Lévy process.
Therefore we consider only the third term. Now, it follows by the Markov inequality that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|\geq a\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|^{2}\geq a^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|^{2}\right].

Thanks to, e.g., Proposition 2.3 by Brockwell and Schlemm
[
2
]
, there exist constants
m_{1},m_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|^{2}\right]=m_{1}h_{n}+m_{2}h_{n}^{2}.

(3.3)

Noticing that
t_{k-1}\leq t
for a fixed
t
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq k-1}|\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S|\geq a\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{a^{2}}\left[m_{1}(k-1)h_{n}+m_{2}(k-1)h_{n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m_{1}t}{a^{2}},

which goes to zero as
a\to\infty
. This concludes (ii).

To show (iii), we consider a
X^{n}
-discrete stopping time
\tau
satisfying
\tau\leq t
, and show that

{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}+\delta}-X^{n}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}}|\geq{\epsilon}|\tau=s)\leq\eta,

under the given conditions.

For any
\omega\in\Omega
such that
\tau(\omega)=s\in(0,t]
and any sufficiently small
\delta>0
, there exist some
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that we can take
p
and
q
with
p<q
satisfying that

t_{p-1}\leq s<t_{p}<t_{q-1}<s+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\delta}\leq t_{q}

and that
|s-t_{p-1}|
and
|t_{q}-(s+\delta)|
are small enough for any
n\geq n_{1}
since the partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{k-1}\leq t
becomes finer enough, that is
h_{n}\to 0
, for
n
large enough; that is,
t_{p-1}\uparrow s
and
t_{q}\downarrow s+\delta
.
Then we note that for any
n\geq n_{1}
and the above
\delta
,

|X^{n}_{s+\delta}-X^{n}_{s}|\leq|\mu|\delta+\left|\sigma\sum_{j=p}^{q}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}W\right|+\left|\sum_{j=p}^{q}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S\right|\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}a.s.}.

Since

\sum_{j=p}^{q}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}S=^{d}[S_{s+\delta}-S_{s}]+[S_{s}-S_{t_{p-1}}]-[S_{s+\delta}-S_{t_{q}}],

and

\sigma\sum_{j=p}^{q}\Delta_{i^{n}(j)}W\sim N(0,\sigma^{2}(t_{q}-t_{p-1}));\quad t_{q}-t_{p-1}<\delta,

we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}+\delta}-X^{n}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}}|\geq{\epsilon}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\tau=s})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mu|\delta\geq{\epsilon}/5\}}+{\mathbb{P}}(|N(0,\sigma^{2}\delta)|\geq{\epsilon}/5)+{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{s+\delta}-S_{s}|\geq{\epsilon}/5\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{s}-S_{t_{p-1}}|\geq{\epsilon}/5\right)+{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{s+\delta}-S_{t_{q}}|\geq{\epsilon}/5\right).

For the above
{\epsilon}>0
, there exists a
\delta_{1}>0
with
|\mu|\delta_{1}<{\epsilon}/5
such that

\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mu|\delta\geq{\epsilon}/5\}}=0,\quad{\mathbb{P}}(|N(0,\sigma^{2}\delta)|\geq{\epsilon}/5)<\frac{\eta}{4}

for any
\delta\leq\delta_{1}
. Moreover, it follows from the stochastic continuity of the Lévy process
S
that,
for any
\eta>0
, there exist
\delta_{2}>0
and
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|S_{s+\delta}-S_{s}|\geq{\epsilon}/5)

\displaystyle<\frac{\eta}{4},

for any
\delta\leq\delta_{2}
, and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{s}-S_{t_{p-1}}|\geq{\epsilon}/5\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{\eta}{4};\quad{\mathbb{P}}\left(|S_{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s+\delta}-S_{t_{q}}|\geq{\epsilon}/10\right)<{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{\eta}{4}}

for any
n\geq n_{2}
As a consequence, it follows for any
n\geq n_{0}:=\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}

and
\delta\leq\delta_{0}:=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
that

{\mathbb{P}}(|X^{n}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}+\delta}-X^{n}_{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\tau}|\geq{\epsilon}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\tau=s})<\eta.

Taking the expectation on the both sides, we have (iii). This ends the proof.
∎

4
Main results for M-estimation

4.1
Consistency results

Let
(X^{(\beta)})_{\beta=1,2,\dots,B}
be independent copies of process
X
defined on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
, and denote its empirical measure as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{B}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{\beta=1}^{B}\delta_{X^{(\beta)}}.

(4.1)

In the sequel, we assume that in the empirical measure
{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}
in (
2.1
), the permutation samples are taken such that
\alpha_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Moreover, when
{\cal H}
is a family of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{D}_{\infty}\times\overline{\Theta}\to\mathbb{R}
, we assume that
\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}
is given by (
2.2
) for a given
h_{\vartheta}(x):=h(x,\vartheta)\in{\cal H}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
\Theta
is open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with smooth boundary, and that

\displaystyle{\cal H}\subset C^{0,1}_{b}(\mathbb{D}_{\infty}\times\overline{\Theta}),

(4.2)

Moreover, for
h_{\vartheta}(x)\in{\cal H}
, suppose that there exists
\vartheta_{0}\in\Theta
such that for any
{\epsilon}>0
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}:|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}|>{\epsilon}}Ph_{\vartheta}>Ph_{\vartheta_{0}}.

(4.3)

Then
\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}
is weakly consistent to
\vartheta_{0}
:

\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}\vartheta_{0},\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

It follows for any sequence of permutations
\{i^{n}\}\subset\Lambda_{n}
that

\displaystyle\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h_{\vartheta}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}})h_{\vartheta}|+\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h_{\vartheta}|,

(4.4)

and the last second term goes to zero because, under condition (
4.2
), class
{\cal H}
is
P
-Glivenko-Cantelli
:

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h|=\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h_{\vartheta}|\to 0\quad a.s.,\quad n\to\infty;

see, e.g., Example 19.7 in van der Vaart
[
15
]
.
{\cal H}
is a special case of the
parametric class
.
To show the convergence of the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.4
), we show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}\left|\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha_{n}}\left[h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i_{(k)},n})-h_{\vartheta}(X^{(k)})\right]\right|\right]=0,

(4.5)

for any sequence of
\{i^{n}\}\subset\Lambda_{n}
.

Note the set
\Theta
admits a version of
Sobolev inequality
for embedding
W^{1,p}(\Theta)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Theta})
for
p>d
, that is, for every
f\in W^{1,p}(\Theta)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|f(\vartheta)|^{p}\lesssim\int_{\Theta}\left(|f(\vartheta)|^{p}+|\nabla_{\vartheta}f(\vartheta)|^{p}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta;

see Evans
[
3
]
, Sec.5.6, Theorem 5. Given
h\in{\cal H}
, since
h_{\cdot}(x)\in W^{1,p}(\Theta)
for any
x\in\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
, we can use this inequality to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}\left|\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i_{(k)},n})-h_{\vartheta}(X^{(k)})\right|^{p}\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}\int_{\Theta}\mathbb{E}\left[|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{\ell}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i_{(k)},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{\ell}h_{\vartheta}(X^{(k)})|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta.

(4.7)

and the last summands (expectations) go to zero as
n\to\infty
by Theorem
3.1
and the bounded convergence theorem with the Skorokhood coupling theorem; see, e.g., Theorem 4.30 in Kallenberg
[
10
]
.
Hence we have (
4.5
), and we see that

\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h_{\vartheta}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,\quad n\to\infty.

Together with (
4.3
), we have the consequence from Theorem 5.7 in van der Vaart
[
15
]
.
∎

4.2
Asymptotic normality

For class
{\cal H}
, we denote the
bracketing number
of
{\cal H}
by
N_{[\,]}({\epsilon},{\cal H},L^{r}(P))
, which is the minimum number of
{\epsilon}
-brackets
, including
[l,u]
, that is a family of measurable functions
f
satisfying
l\leq f\leq u
with
\|u-l\|_{L^{r}(P)}<{\epsilon}
, covering
{\cal H}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Considering the same assumptions as in Theorem
4.1
,
h_{\vartheta}\in{\cal H}\cap C^{0,2}_{b}(\mathbb{D}_{T}\times\Theta)
with matrix
V_{\vartheta_{0}}:=P\nabla_{\vartheta}^{2}h_{\vartheta_{0}}
invertible.
Moreover, suppose that there exist a constant
p\,(>d)
and a sequence
\{r_{n}\}
with
r_{n}\uparrow\infty
and
r_{n}^{2}/\alpha_{n}=o(1)
such that, for
k=0,1
and any
i^{n}\in\Lambda_{n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(X)\right\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}=o(r_{n}^{-1}),

(4.8)

as
n\to\infty
.
Then the M-estimator
\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}
is asymptotically normal:

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{n}}(\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}-\vartheta_{0})\leadsto N(0,\Sigma),\quad n\to\infty,

with
\Sigma=V_{\vartheta_{0}}^{-1}P[\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}^{\top}]V_{\vartheta_{0}}^{-1}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that
r_{n}
in Theorem
4.2
is not necessarily unique because, if some
r^{\prime}_{n}
satisfies (
4.8
), then
r_{n}=\log r^{\prime}_{n}\uparrow\infty
also satisfies (
4.8
).
Because we can freely control
\alpha_{n}\,(\leq n!)
, we can attain the
r_{n}
-consistency for various
r_{n}
.
If
r_{n}=r_{n}^{*}
satisfies (
4.8
) and it holds for
\forall\,\{r^{\prime}_{n}\}
with
r^{\prime}_{n}/r_{n}^{*}\to\infty
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}r^{\prime}_{n}\|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(X)\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}>0,\quad k=0,1.

Then, this
r_{n}^{*}
is optimal. In addition, the example in Section
5.1
; see Remark
5.1
, indicates that several
r_{n}
can be found and the optimal rate of convergence is attained by choosing
\alpha_{n}
suitably.

Proof.

We shall use the notation (
4.1
).
Because any function in
{\cal H}
is Lipschitz with respect to
\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}
, it follows from, for example, argument as in Example 19.7 in van der Vaart
[
15
]
, p.271, that the bracketing number of
{\cal H}
is finite. For every
{\epsilon}>0
,

\displaystyle N_{[\,]}({\epsilon},{\cal H},L^{1}(P))\lesssim{\epsilon}^{-d}<\infty,

(4.9)

which implies that class
{\cal H}
is
{\mathbb{P}}
-Donsker
, and therefore,

\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h|=O_{p}(\alpha_{n}^{-1/2}),\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore, it follows from the decomposition (
4.4
) that

\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-P)h_{\vartheta}|\leq\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}})h_{\vartheta}|+O_{p}(\alpha_{n}^{-1/2}),

Under assumption (
4.8
), it follows from the same argument as in (
4.6
)-(
4.7
) that

\sup_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}-{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}})h_{\vartheta}|=o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1}).

Consequently, it holds that

\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}})h|=O_{p}(\alpha_{n}^{-1/2})+o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1})=o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1}).

Considering
|\inf_{n}\alpha_{n}-\inf_{n}b_{n}|\leq\sup_{n}|\alpha_{n}-b_{n}|
and the definition of
\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta_{0}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}})h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}\right|+\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}-\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta}\right|+\left|\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta}-\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}})h|=o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1}),

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta_{0}}+o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1})

Here, we assume that
h_{\vartheta}\in{\cal H}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to
\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}
.
Then the bracketing number satisfies
N_{[\,]}({\epsilon},{\cal H},L^{2}(P))<\infty
by the same argument as in (
4.9
). Then, using the same argument as in the proof of Corollary 5.53 in van der Vaart
[
15
]
with
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
replaced by our
{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}
,
we established that
\sqrt{r_{n}}(\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}-\vartheta_{0})
is bounded in probability.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}-\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}-\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}\right|+\left|\ \inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}-\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}\right|+\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}})h|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{h\in{\cal H}}|({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}})h|=o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1}),

which yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}}\leq\inf_{\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}+o_{p}(r_{n}^{-1}).

(4.10)

Hence,
\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}
is approximately a minimum contrast estimator for the contrast function
{\mathbb{P}}^{*}_{\alpha_{n}}h_{\vartheta}
.
Hereafter, we can obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{n}}(\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}-\vartheta_{0})

\displaystyle=V_{\vartheta_{0}}^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}}\sum_{i\in A_{n}}\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}(X^{(i)})+o_{p}(1),\quad n\to\infty.

using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 5.23 in van der Vaart
[
15
]
with the condition (
4.10
): replace
{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}
with
{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}
;
n{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}(m_{\vartheta_{0}+\widetilde{h}_{n}/\sqrt{n}}-m_{\vartheta_{0}})
with

\sqrt{\alpha_{n}r_{n}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}(h_{\vartheta_{0}+\widetilde{h}_{n}/\sqrt{r_{n}}}-h_{\vartheta_{0}})
;

\mathbb{G}_{n}f
with
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}h_{\vartheta}:=\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha_{n}}^{*}-P)h_{\vartheta}
in the proof.

In particular, because
X^{(i)}
’s are IIDs, we conclude by the central limit theorem that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}}\sum_{i\in A_{n}}\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}(X^{(i)})\leadsto N(0,P[\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}\nabla_{\vartheta}h_{\vartheta_{0}}^{\top}]),\quad\alpha_{n}\to\infty.

∎

5.1
Default-related discounted losses

Here, we consider concrete examples for
h_{\vartheta}
and investigate sufficient conditions that ensure the conditions in Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
.

Suppose the Lévy process
X=(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
with (
1.1
) represents a ‘loss’ process of a company, and we define the time of default as

\tau^{X}:=\inf\{t\in[0,T]\,|\,X_{t}<\xi\}\wedge T,

for constants
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
T\in(0,\infty]
.
\tau^{X}
is interpreted as the
time of default
(resp.
ruin
) with default levels
\xi
in the context of finance (resp. insurance). This section considers a
discounted loss up to default
. For
x=(x_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}\in\mathbb{D}_{T}
,

\displaystyle h_{\vartheta}(x)=h(x,\vartheta)=\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,.

(5.1)

where
r>0
and
U_{\vartheta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are a bounded function with parameter
\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
to be controlled.
Then,
Ph_{\vartheta}
is the expected discounted loss up to default

\displaystyle Ph_{\vartheta}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau^{X}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right];

(5.2)

see Feng and Shimizu
[
5
]
for details on this quantity.

In the sequel, we consider the Skorokhod space
(\mathbb{D}_{T},\varrho_{T})
, where
\varrho_{T}
is the Skorokhod metric given in Section
1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty]
. The functional
\tau^{\cdot}
defined on
\mathbb{D}_{T}
is continuous with respect to
\varrho_{T}
; that is, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{D}_{T}
and
{\epsilon}>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that

\varrho_{T}(x,y)<\delta\quad\Rightarrow\quad|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|<{\epsilon}.

Proof.

Note that the functional

F(x;T_{1},T_{2})=\inf_{T_{1}\leq s\leq T_{2}}x_{s},\quad 0\leq T_{1}<T_{2}\leq T,

in
x\in\mathbb{D}_{T}
is continuous in the sense of the Skorokhod metric
\varrho_{T}
; see, e.g., Embrechts
et al.

[
7
]
, Example A.2.9, and it follows for each
y\in\mathbb{D}_{T}
and
\eta>0
, there exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(y,\eta)>0
such that when
\varrho_{T}(x,y)<\delta
,

\left|F(x;\tau^{y}-{\epsilon},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T})-F(y;\tau^{y}-{\epsilon},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T})\right|<\eta,

which implies that

\inf_{\tau^{y}-{\epsilon}\leq s\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T}}x_{s}<\inf_{\tau^{y}-{\epsilon}\leq s\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T}}y_{s}+\eta.

Since
\inf_{\tau^{y}-{\epsilon}\leq s\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T}}y_{s}<\xi
by the definition of
\tau^{y}
, when
\eta>0
is sufficiently small, we have

\inf_{\tau^{y}-{\epsilon}\leq s\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\tau^{y}+{\epsilon})\wedge T}}x_{s}<\xi,

which is equivalent to
|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|<{\epsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer and
T\in(0,\infty]
.
For bounded function
U_{\vartheta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with parameter
\vartheta\in\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, suppose that the derivatives up to
N
th order with respect to
\vartheta
are uniformly bounded

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R};\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,z)|\leq C,\quad k=0,1,\dots,N,

(5.3)

for constant
C>0
. Moreover, suppose the Lipschitz continuity:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+};\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}}|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,x)-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,y)|\leq L\|x-y\|_{T},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{D}_{T},\ k=1,\dots,N.

(5.4)

Then the functional (
5.1
) satisfies that

h\in C_{b}^{0,k}(\mathbb{D}_{T}\times\Theta),\quad k=1,\dots,N-1.

Proof.

In this proof, we arbitrarily fix
\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}
and simply write
h(x):=h(x,\vartheta)
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{D}_{T}
and any increasing surjection
\lambda:[0,T]\to[0,T]

\displaystyle|h(x)-h(y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau^{y}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}\geq\tau^{y}\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}<\tau^{y}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\tau^{y}}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau^{y}}e^{-rt}\left[U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})-U_{\vartheta}(t,y_{t})\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}\geq\tau^{y}\}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{\tau^{x}}^{\tau^{y}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,y_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}\left[U_{\vartheta}(t,y_{t})-U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}<\tau^{y}\}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}|U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})-U_{\vartheta}(t,y_{t})|\,dt

\displaystyle=C|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+\int_{0}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(T)}}e^{-r\lambda(t)}|U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),x\circ\lambda(t))-U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),y\circ\lambda(t))|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(\mathrm{d}t)}

\displaystyle\leq C|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+\int_{0}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(T)}}e^{-r\lambda(t)}|U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),x\circ\lambda(t))-U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),y_{t})|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(\mathrm{d}t)}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(T)}}e^{-r\lambda(t)}|U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),y\circ\lambda(t))-U_{\vartheta}(\lambda(t),y_{t})|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda(\mathrm{d}t)}

\displaystyle\leq C|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+L\max\{\|x\circ\lambda-y\|_{T},\|\lambda-I\|_{T}\}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\quad+L\max\{\|y\circ\lambda-y\|_{T},\|\lambda-I\|_{T}\}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-rt}\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\leq C|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+r^{-1}L\max\{\|x\circ\lambda-y\|_{T},\|\lambda-I\|_{T}\}

\displaystyle\quad+r^{-1}L\max\{\|y\circ\lambda-y\|_{T},\|\lambda-I\|_{T}\},

where
I
is the identity map of
[0,T]
. In the last inequality, we use that
U_{\vartheta}
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L>0
.
Consider the infimum with respect to
\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}
on both sides to obtain

|h(x)-h(y)|\lesssim|\tau^{x}-\tau^{y}|+\varrho_{T}(x,y).

This inequality with Lemma
5.1
implies that
h
is continuous on
(\mathbb{D}_{T},\varrho_{T})
.

Because
\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h(x,\vartheta)=\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\ (k\leq N)
under this assumption, the same argument is valid for
\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h(\cdot,\vartheta)\ (k\leq N-1)
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose we use the same assumptions as in Lemma
5.2
, and that there exist constants
p>d=\dim(\Theta)
and
q>0
as well as sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}
with
\gamma_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in A_{n}}\|\widehat{X}_{t}^{i,n}-X_{t}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=O(t^{q})}\cdot o(\gamma_{n}^{-1}),\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t\to\infty,}\ n\to\infty,

(5.5)

for each
t>0
, where
o(\gamma_{n}^{-1})
-term is independent of
t
.
Then it follows for each
i^{n}\in A_{n}
and any
p>d
that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(X)\right\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}=o(\gamma_{n}^{-1}),\quad k\leq N-1,

which implies (
4.8
) in Theorem
4.2
.

Proof.

For any integer
k\leq N-1
,
\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}
is the Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
uniformly in
\vartheta
under the assumptions in Lemma
5.2
.
Hence, it follows for any
p\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(\widehat{X}^{i^{n},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}h_{\vartheta}(X)\Big\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\left\|\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,\widehat{X}_{t}^{i^{n},n})-\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}(t,X_{t})\right\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-rt}\|\widehat{X}_{t}^{i^{n},n}-X_{t}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=o(\gamma_{n}^{-1})O\left(1+\int_{t}^{\infty}s^{q}e^{-rs}\,\mathrm{d}s\right),\quad t\to\infty,\ n\to\infty}.

This ends the proof.
∎

We can check the condition (
5.5
) by taking the sampling interval
h_{n}
suitably, as in the following Lemma:

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
h_{n}=n^{-\beta}
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then, the condition (
5.5
) holds with
\gamma_{n}=n^{\beta/p}
for any
p>d
.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{t_{k}}^{i^{n},n}=\mu+\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(l)}X,\quad X_{t_{k}}=\mu+\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{l}X,\quad t_{k}=kh_{n}.

Then

\displaystyle\hat{X}_{t_{k}}^{i^{n},n}-X_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{i^{n}(l)}X-\sum_{l=1}^{k-1}\Delta_{l}X=\sum_{c=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i^{n}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{n,k-1,c}}}(\Delta_{i^{n}(l)}X-\Delta_{l^{\prime}}X),

where
B_{n,k,c}=\{i\in\Lambda_{n}|\#\{\{i(1),\ldots,i(k)\}\cap\{1,\ldots,k\}\}=c\}
,
which is the number of pairs
(i^{n}(l),l^{\prime})
with
i^{n}(l)=l^{\prime}
in
l,l^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
is equal to
c
.
Since the increments of
X
are independent and stationary, we can evaluate the above as

\displaystyle\|\hat{X}_{t_{k}}^{i^{n},n}-X_{t_{k}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{c=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{n,k-1,c}}}(k-1-c)\left\|\mathbf{1}_{\{i^{n}=i\}}(\Delta_{1}X-\Delta_{2}X)\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{k-1}\sum_{i\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{n,k-1,c}}}(k-1-c){\mathbb{P}}(i^{n}=i)\left\|(\Delta_{1}X-\Delta_{2}X)\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})},

(5.6)

where the last equality is due to the independent choice of the permutation
\{i^{n}\}
, in particular
{\mathbb{P}}(i^{n}=i)=1/n!
.
Here we claim that

\#B_{n,k,c}={k\choose c}{n-k\choose k-c}\cdot k!(n-k!),

and then we can apply Vandermonde identity
\sum_{j=0}^{k}{n\choose j}{m\choose k-j}={n+m\choose k}
to obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{c=0}^{k}\sum_{i\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}B_{n,k,c}}}(k-c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{k}{k\choose c}\cdot{{n-k}\choose{k-c}}\cdot k!(n-k)!\cdot(k-c)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{k}{k\choose c}\cdot{{n-k-1}\choose{k-1-c}}(n-k)\cdot k!(n-k)!

\displaystyle={n-1\choose k-1}(n-k)\frac{k!(n-k)!}{n!}n!

\displaystyle=n!k\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)

We can apply this equality, replacing
k
by
k-1
, to evaluate (
5.6
) as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in A_{n}}\|\hat{X}_{t_{k}}^{i,n}-X_{t_{k}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}

\displaystyle\leq n!(k-1)\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)\frac{1}{n!}\left\|(\Delta_{1}X-\Delta_{2}X)\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}

\displaystyle=(k-1)\left(1-\frac{k-1}{n}\right)O({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}h_{n}^{1/p}}).

The last inequality is due to the fact (
3.3
). Since this inequality holds for any
k=1,\dots,n
,
taking
k=n
,
h_{n}=n^{-\beta}
(
0<\beta<1
) and
q>0
, we observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in A_{n}}\frac{\|\hat{X}_{t_{n}}^{i^{n},n}-X_{t_{n}}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{p}({\mathbb{P}})}}}{t_{n}^{q}}=\frac{n-1}{nt_{n}^{q}}O(h_{n}n^{-1/p})=\frac{n-1}{n}o(n^{-\beta/p}),\quad n\to\infty,

which implies (
5.5
) with
\gamma_{n}=n^{-\beta/p}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Note that the rate
\gamma_{n}
in the above proof depends on
p>d
, and by Theorem
4.2
,
we can obtain the rate of convergence
\sqrt{n^{\beta/p}}
for each
p>d
:

\sqrt{n^{\beta/p}}(\widehat{\vartheta}_{n}-\vartheta_{0})\leadsto N(0,\Sigma),\quad n\to\infty,

by controlling the resampling number
\alpha_{n}
such that
\gamma_{n}^{2}/\alpha_{n}=n^{2\beta/p}/\alpha_{n}=o(1)
, where
\Sigma
is given in Theorem
4.2
.
Then, we can access the optimal rate
\sqrt{n^{\beta/d}}
by letting
p
be as close to
d
as possible.

5.2
Dividends up to ruin

In the dividends problem, we can consider a case where
U_{\vartheta}
in (
5.2
) is of the form

U_{\vartheta}(x,t)=\alpha\mathbf{1}_{\{x\geq\vartheta\}}\mathbf{1}_{\{t\leq g(\vartheta)\}},

where
\alpha>0
is a constant and
g
is an increasing function in
\vartheta\in\overline{\Theta}
.
Assuming that a surplus of an insurance company
x=(x_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is given,
the functional

h_{\vartheta}(x)=\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}U_{\vartheta}(t,x_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t

is interpreted as the aggregate dividends paid up to ruin
x_{t}<\xi
or maturity
g(\vartheta)
depending on the parameter
\vartheta
, where the dividend
\alpha
is paid when the surplus
x_{t}
is over the threshold
\vartheta
, and the maturity depends on the threshold. That is, when the threshold level is high (the dividends are hard to pay), the maturity for dividends will be longer, but the maturity will be shorter when the threshold level is low (the dividends are easy to pay).
Because the ruin level is
\xi>0
, the threshold
\vartheta
should be set over
\xi
, and we assume that
\Theta=[\xi,M]
for constant
M>0
.

For technical reasons, we replace the indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{\{u\geq z\}}
with
\varphi_{\epsilon}(u,z)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivatives such that

\varphi_{\epsilon}(u,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&(u\geq z+{\epsilon})\\
0&(u\leq z-{\epsilon})\end{array}\right.

for a ‘small’ constant
{\epsilon}>0
. That is, we can replace the following indicators as

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\{x_{t}\geq\vartheta\}}\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\varphi_{\epsilon}(x_{t},\vartheta)

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{\{t\leq g(\vartheta)\}}\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\varphi_{\epsilon}(\vartheta,g^{-1}(t))

Hereafter, the functional
h
can be approximated by

h_{\vartheta}^{\epsilon}(x)=\alpha\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}\varphi_{\epsilon}(x_{t},\vartheta)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\vartheta,g^{-1}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

This approximation is valid because it follows by the bounded convergence theorem that

\lim_{{\epsilon}\to 0}h_{\vartheta}^{\epsilon}(X)=\alpha\int_{0}^{\tau^{x}}e^{-rt}\lim_{{\epsilon}\to 0}\left[\varphi_{\epsilon}(x_{t},\vartheta)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\vartheta,g^{-1}(t))\right]\,\mathrm{d}t=h_{\vartheta}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{D}_{T}.

Moreover, because the derivatives of
\varphi_{\epsilon}
are uniformly bounded, it follows that for fixed
{\epsilon}>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}U_{\vartheta}^{\epsilon}(x,t)=\alpha\nabla_{\vartheta}^{k}\left[\varphi_{\epsilon}(x_{t},\vartheta)\varphi_{\epsilon}(\vartheta,g^{-1}(t))\right]

satisfies conditions (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) in Lemma
5.2
.
Therefore, Theorem
4.2
is applicable for
h^{\epsilon}_{\vartheta}(X)
with the Lévy process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Further examples will be discussed in a different paper: Shimizu and Shiraishi
[
14
]
.

Electron correlations and charge density wave in the topological kagome metal FeGe
Chandan Setty ⊕,†, Christopher A. Lane ⊕, Lei Chen, Haoyu Hu, Jian-Xin Zhu, Qimiao Si

Abstract
Charge order in kagome metals is of extensive current interest. Recently, a charge density wave was discovered in the magnetic binary kagome metal FeGe Teng et al. 2022 . In analogy to its predecessor, the non-magnetic A A V 3 Sb 5 ( A A =K, Cs, Rb), the in-plane ordering occurs at the M M point. In contrast, however, the system manifestly shows effects of substantial correlations. Here we identify the topological bands crossing the Fermi energy (E F ) in FeGe and characterize the correlation-induced renormalization of these bands. We then derive a charge order from an effective model comprising topological kagome ‘flat’ bands in the presence of a magnetic order. We demonstrate edge states as well as excess out-of-plane magnetic moment associated with the charge order; both are fingerprints of non-trivial band topology and consistent with recent experimental observations. Our results point to FeGe as an ideal platform to realize and elucidate correlated topological physics.

Discussion:

Several points are in order.
While the suppression of the DOS close to the chemical potential due to the charge order enables the observation of edge states in STM, it should be noted that the bulk states need not be fully gapped or even nodal for the observation of edge states.
Even
a modest suppression of the spectral weight at E
F
– 30 %- 40% of the high temperature value with a large residual density of states – is sufficient for the edge spectrum to be discernible. This is consistent with our calculations in Figs
4
where edge states have a large spectral weight despite the lack of a full gap in the charge order phase.

In principle, such topological edge states
should exist in the purely magnetic phase as well (
T_{CO}
<T<T_{m}
). However, both
DFT and DMFT
demonstrate
large density of states of the bulk bands near E
F

in this regime. Correspondingly, edge
states
are
expected to be

smeared
by the
bulk bands crossing E
F
.
This picture is supported by our calculations in Fig.
4
.
In fact, topological surface states are expected to exist even in the normal state above
T_{m}
due to the non-trivial topology of the bands,
albeit mixed with the more dominant bulk states.
Recently such states were observed in photoemission measurements of the ternary kagome compounds
Hu et al. 2021

in both the normal and charge ordered phases.

We have delineated the role of van Hove singularities in the electronic structure.
They are located
\sim-0.6eV
below E
F
in the
nonmagnetic phase, and are pushed closer to E
F

in the magnetic phase. However,
the flat bands
always dominate the DOS.
As seen in the bottom panels
Fig.
1
, and further highlighted in SM (Fig.
6
),
the density of states (DOS) from the van Hove singularities is essentially
completely obscured by the
topological
flat bands.
This
renders the possibility of a strong coupling topological mechanism for the charge order more viable and robust
Setty et al. 2021
.
Finally,
we have illustrated the topological mechanism by focusing on an effective model with only intra-layer coupling.
Generalization of our analysis to
the more realistic
3D case is
left for
future investigations.

In conclusion, the recent discovery of charge order in the topological flat band magnetic kagome metal FeGe opens up a new platform for exploring flat band correlated phases and their topological properties.
While the charge order in FeGe shares many features with its ternary counterparts
A
V
3
Sb
5
(
A
= K, Cs, Rb), it is also distinct in
important ways. The strong correlation
driven band renormalizations, active topological flat bands, appearance of edge states,
and enhancement of the
magnetic
moments below the charge ordering temperature are a few key aspects unique to FeGe.

Beyond
what was discussed in the nonmagnetic setting
Setty et al. 2021
,
namely
correlation driven charge order,
ordering at the
wavevector

M
in the BZ,
and
breaking of
time-reversal symmetry,

we have shown here that our minimal model in a magnetic setting provides a
qualitative understanding of

the most

salient and puzzling features of the charge order in FeGe:
(i) the existence of edge states
and (ii)
development of an excess
magnetic
moment with
the in-plane

\textbf{q}=0
.

The origin of all these exotic properties can be traced to the non-trivial band topology of the magnetic parent phase.
As such, our results will likely have general implications on the interplay of topology and correlations in a broad range of correlated systems.

Projected Hamiltonian in the Wannier basis:

In this section, we derive the effective model in the Wannier basis. As shown in Ref.
Setty et al. 2021
, the lowest two bands of the extended Hubbard model with Coulomb interaction on the kagome lattice can be projected into an effective Hamiltonian defined on the WOs. The projected creation and annihilation operators in the kagome lattice is connected with WOs with a sequence of unitary transformations:

Pc_{{\bf k}}P=\sum_{\mu}(V_{{\bf k}}^{\dagger}U_{H,{\bf k}})_{\mu\nu}b_{{\bf k}\nu},

(7)

where
V_{{\bf k}}
diagonalizes the hopping matrix, and
U_{H,{\bf k}}^{\dagger}
is the Haldane matrix
Soluyanov and Vanderbilt 2012
. The kinetic part of effective Hamiltonian reads:

H_{0}=\sum_{ij,\mu\nu}b^{\dagger}_{i\mu}t_{ij}^{\mu\nu}b_{j\nu},

(8)

where

t^{\mu\nu}_{ij}=\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}T^{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\omega^{*}_{\mu^{\prime}\mu,i^{\prime}-i}\omega_{\nu^{\prime}\nu,j^{\prime}-j},

(9)

with
\omega_{\alpha,\nu,i}=\frac{1}{N}\left(V_{{\bf k}}^{\dagger}U_{H,{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\right)_{\mu\nu}e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r_{i}}}
the real space wave functions of the WOs.

Ginzburg-Landau (GL) free energy:
The GL free energy to fourth order in perturbation is given by

\displaystyle F[\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{i}|\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{2}+\beta_{1}\sum_{i}|\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{4}

(10)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\sum_{i<j}|\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{2}|\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{j}}|^{2}-h^{\prime}\gamma\sum_{i}\Delta_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}\Delta_{-\textbf{Q}_{i}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{i}|\Delta^{\prime}_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{2}+\beta_{1}^{\prime}\sum_{i}|\Delta^{\prime}_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{4}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\beta_{2}^{\prime}\sum_{i<j}|\Delta^{\prime}_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}|^{2}|\Delta^{\prime}_{\textbf{Q}_{j}}|^{2}-h^{\prime}\gamma\sum_{i}\Delta^{\prime}_{\textbf{Q}_{i}}\Delta^{\prime}_{-\textbf{Q}_{i}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle...\quad.

Here the parameters
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta_{i},\beta_{i}^{\prime},\gamma
are constants that depend on details of the electronic structure. The expression for
\gamma
in the flat band limit is given as

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{n}}\frac{6h_{0}}{(h_{0}^{2}+k_{n}^{2})^{2}}.

(11)

where
k_{n}
is the Fermionic Matsubara frequency and
h_{0}
is the static external field. The Matsubara sum can be evaluated to give

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{3h_{0}}{2T_{CO}^{4}}\left[\frac{-1+\tilde{h}_{0}^{-1}\sinh\tilde{h}_{0}}{\tilde{h}_{0}^{2}(1+\cosh\tilde{h}_{0})}\right]

(12)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle h_{0}/T_{CO}.

(13)

This expression for
\gamma
is used in the main text of the Hamiltonian.

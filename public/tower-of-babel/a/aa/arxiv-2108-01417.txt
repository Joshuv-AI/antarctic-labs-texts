On the Structure of the Binary LCD Codes having an Automorphism of Odd Prime Order
Stefka Bouyuklieva

Abstract
The aim of this work is to study the structure and properties of the binary LCD codes having an automorphisms of odd prime order and to present a method for their construction.



1
Introduction

We study the structure of the binary linear codes having an automorphism of an odd prime order
p
, and provide some necessary and sufficient conditions for such code to be a
linear complementary dual
(LCD) code, i.e. the intersection of the code and its orthogonal complement should consists only of the zero vector
\mathbf{0}
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The (Hamming)
distance

d(x,y)
between two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is the number of coordinate positions in which they differ. The (Hamming)
weight
wt
(x)
of a vector
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is the number of its nonzero coordinates.
A linear
[n,k,d]
code
C
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of the
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, and
d
is the
smallest weight among all non-zero codewords of
C
, called the
minimum weight
(or minimum distance) of the code. A matrix whose rows form a basis of
C

is called a
generator matrix
of this code. The
weight enumerator

of a code
C
is given by the polynomial
W_{C}(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}y^{i}
where

A_{i}
is the number of codewords of weight
i
in
C
.
In the binary case, i.e.
q=2
, two codes are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a permutation of coordinates.
The set of all permutations that preserve a linear code
C
of length
n
, forms a subgroup of the symmetric group
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, denoted by
\mathrm{Aut}(C)
and called the automorphism group of the code.

Let
(u,v):\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an inner
product in the linear space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. The orthogonal complement of
C
according to the defined inner product is called the
dual code
of
C
and denoted by
C^{\perp}
, i.e.
C^{\perp}=\{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:(u,v)=0
for all
v\in C\}
. Obviously,
C^{\perp}
is a linear
[n,n-k]
code. If
C\subseteq C^{\perp}
,
C
is termed
self-orthogonal
and if
C=C^{\perp}
,

C
is
self-dual
.

Although the concept of a zero-dimensional intersection of a vector space and its orthogonal complement is very natural in linear algebra, the LCD codes over finite fields were introduced by Massey in his 1992 paper
[
13
]
.
Recently, much work has been done on the study, constructions and classifications of LCD codes with or without additional restrictions over different finite fields and rings
(see, for example,
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
9
]
). It seems, that LCD and self-dual codes are completely different classes of codes, but sometimes the same approaches can be very useful for construction of codes from both classes. The method of constructing and classifying binary self-dual codes having an automorphism of odd prime order is a very powerful tool in the theory of this class of codes
[
10
,
14
]
. Our research is devoted to the following problem: What is the structure of the binary LCD codes having an automorphism of odd prime order
p
, and how to use this structure for construction and classification? Therefore, we first study the structure of binary linear codes invariant under an automorphism of order
p
, the corresponding structure of their dual codes, and the relationship between these structures. The work on this problem began last year and the first results were presented in
[
3
]
, but the authors there consider only primes
p
, for which 2 is a multiplicative root.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we study the structure of binary linear codes having an automorphism of odd prime order. Section
3
is devoted to the linear complementary dual (LCD) codes having such automorphisms. Finally, in Section
4
, we give some construction and classification results for optimal LCD codes having an automorphism of order
p
for
p=7
, 11, and 17.

2
Binary linear codes with an automorphism of odd prime order

Let
C
be a binary linear code of length
n
and
\sigma
be an
automorphism of
C
of odd prime order
p
with
c
independent

p
-cycles. Without loss of generality we can assume that

\sigma=\Omega_{1}\dots\Omega_{c}\Omega_{c+1}\dots\Omega_{c+f}

(1)

where
\Omega_{i}=((i-1)p+1,\dots,ip),i=1,\dots,c
, are the
cycles of length
p
, and
\Omega_{c+i}=(cp+i),i=1,\dots,f
, are
the fixed points. Obviously,
cp+f=n
.

Let
F_{\sigma}(C)=\{v\in C:v\sigma=v\}
and
E_{\sigma}(C)
=
\{v\in C:wt(v|\Omega_{i})\equiv 0\pmod{2},i=1,\dots,c+f\}
,
where
v|\Omega_{i}
is the restriction of
v
on
\Omega_{i}
.
The following lemma is a modification of the general decomposition lemma (Lemma 2) given in
[
10
]
. As Huffman has mentioned there, part of its statement and proof
are a special case of Maschke’s theorem.

Lemma 1

[
10
]

The code C is a direct sum of the subcodes
F_{\sigma}(C)
and
E_{\sigma}(C)
, which are mutually orthogonal,
F_{\sigma}(C)\perp E_{\sigma}(C)
.

The subcodes from Lemma
1
are the main building blocks of the codes we consider. Later in this section we study them in more details.

As
v\in F_{\sigma}(C)
if and only if
v\in C
and
v
is constant on
each cycle, the projection map
\pi:F_{\sigma}(C)\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{c+f}
, defined by

(v\pi)_{i}=v_{j}
for some
j\in\Omega_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,c+f
,
v\in F_{\sigma}(C)
, is a monomorphism. It turns out that the binary codes
F_{\sigma}(C)
and
C_{\pi}=\pi(F_{\sigma}(C))
are isomorphic as linear spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and therefore they have the same dimenstion
k_{\pi}=\dim C_{\pi}=\dim F_{\sigma}(C)
. Moreover,

x\cdot y=p\sum_{i=1}^{c}x_{i}y_{i}+\sum_{i=c+1}^{c+f}x_{i}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}x_{i}y_{i}+\sum_{i=c+1}^{c+f}x_{i}y_{i}=\pi(x)\cdot\pi(y)

for any two vectors
x,y\in F_{\sigma}(C)
,
x=(\underbrace{x_{1},\ldots,x_{1}}_{p},\ldots,\underbrace{x_{c},\ldots,x_{c}}_{p},x_{c+1},\ldots,x_{c+f})
,

y=(\underbrace{y_{1},\ldots,y_{1}}_{p},\ldots,\underbrace{y_{c},\ldots,y_{c}}_{p},y_{c+1},\ldots,y_{c+f})
. By
u\cdot v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
we denote the Euclidean inner product of the binary vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. The following lemma is very helpful for some of the proofs in Section
3
.

Lemma 2

If
C
is a binary linear code invariant under the permutation
\sigma
, given in (
1
), then
\pi(F_{\sigma}(C)\cap F_{\sigma}(C)^{\perp})=C_{\pi}\cap C_{\pi}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

If
x\in F_{\sigma}(C)\cap F_{\sigma}(C)^{\perp}
then
\pi(x)\cdot\pi(y)=x\cdot y=0
for any vector
y\in F_{\sigma}(C)
, so
\pi(x)\in C_{\pi}\cap C_{\pi}^{\perp}
. Conversely, let
\pi(x)\in C_{\pi}\cap C_{\pi}^{\perp}
. Then
x\cdot y=\pi(x)\cdot\pi(y)=0
for all
y\in F_{\sigma}(C)
and therefore
x\in F_{\sigma}(C)\cap F_{\sigma}(C)^{\perp}
.

\Box

Denote by
E_{\sigma}(C)^{*}
the code
E_{\sigma}(C)
with the last
f
coordinates deleted.
For
v\in E_{\sigma}(C)^{*}
we
identify
v|\Omega_{i}=(v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{p-1})
with the
polynomial
v_{0}+v_{1}x+\cdots+v_{p-1}x^{p-1}
from
\mathcal{P}
,
where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of even-weight polynomials in

\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/(x^{p}-1)
. Thus we obtain the map
\varphi:E_{\sigma}(C)^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{c}
. Obviously,
C_{\varphi}=\varphi(E_{\sigma}(C)^{*})
is a
\mathcal{P}
-module, and if
\mathcal{P}
is a field then
C_{\varphi}
is a linear code.
On
\mathcal{P}^{c}
, we use the Hermitian inner product, defined in

[
12
]
, namely

\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{c}u_{j}\overline{v_{j}},

(2)

where
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{c})
,
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{c})\in\mathcal{P}^{c}
, and
\overline{v_{j}}=v_{j}(x^{-1})=v_{j}(x^{p-1})
.

The code
E_{\sigma}(C)^{*}
is equivalent to a quasi-cyclic binary code of length
cp
and index
c
, and we can use the decomposition given in
[
12
]

Let
\mathcal{R}_{p}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/\penalty(x^{p}-1)

x^{p}-1=(x-1)g_{1}(x)\cdots g_{s}(x)h_{1}(x)h_{1}^{*}(x)\cdots h_{t}(x)h_{t}^{*}(x),

(3)

f^{*}(x)
is the reciprocal polynomial of

g_{i}^{*}(x)=g_{i}(x)
,

,
g_{0}(x)=x-1
, and
h_{j}(x)\neq h_{j}^{*}(x)

.
If
G_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/\penalty(g_{i}(x))

H_{j}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x]/\penalty(h_{j}(x))

\mathcal{R}_{p}=G_{0}\oplus G_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus G_{s}\oplus H_{1}\oplus H_{1}^{*}\oplus\cdots\oplus H_{t}\oplus H_{t}^{*}.

(4)

If we denote by
\widehat{f(x)}
the polynomial
\frac{x^{p}-1}{f(x)}

of

G_{0},\ldots,G_{s}
,

are isomorphic to the ideals
\langle\widehat{g_{i}(x)}\rangle

,
\langle\widehat{h_{j}(x)}\rangle

I_{j}=G_{j}

for

.
The following theorem is a modification of
[
11

Theorem 3

The following holds in
\mathcal{R}_{p}

(i)

The ideals

for

\mathcal{R}_{p}

(ii)

e_{i}(x)e_{j}(x)=0

(iii)

(iv)

We also use another slightly different transcription of the factorization (
2

x^{p}-1=\prod_{m}M_{\alpha^{m}}(x)

have the same degree, namely
\mathrm{ord}_{p}(2)

s+2t=\frac{p-1}{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(2)}
and
\deg g_{1}=\cdots=\deg g_{s}=\deg h_{1}=\deg h_{1}^{*}=\cdots=\deg h_{t}=\deg h_{t}^{*}=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(2)

I_{j}
are fields with
2^{\mathrm{ord}_{p}(2)}

We have to mention here that
e_{j}(x)=\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i\in C_{j}}x^{i}
, where
J
is some subset of representatives of 2-cyclotomic cosets modulo
p
, and
e_{j}(x^{-1})=e_{i}(x)
for some

(see
[
11
, Corollary 4.3.15]
). If
e_{i}(x)
is the nonzero idempotent of

then
e_{i}(x)=u(x)\widehat{g_{i}}(x)

e_{i}(x^{-1})=u(x^{-1})\widehat{g_{i}}(x^{-1})=u(x^{-1})x^{-m}\widehat{g_{i}}(x)\in G_{i}
, which gives us that
e_{i}(x^{-1})=e_{i}(x)
,
i=1,\ldots,s
. If
e_{s+j}(x)
is the nonzero idempotent of
H_{j}
then
e_{s+j}(x)=u(x)\widehat{h_{j}}(x)
and so
e_{s+j}(x^{-1})=u(x^{-1})\widehat{h_{j}}(x^{-1})=u(x^{-1})x^{-m}\widehat{h_{j}^{*}}(x)\in H_{j}^{*}
, which gives us that
e_{s+j}(x^{-1})=e_{j}^{*}(x)=e_{s+t+j}(x)
,
j=1,\ldots,t
.

Since
g_{0}(x)=x-1
then
I_{0}=G_{0}\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
\widehat{g_{0}}(x)=1+x+\cdots+x^{p-1}=e_{0}(x)=g_{1}(x)\cdots g_{s}(x)h_{1}(x)h_{1}^{*}(x)\cdots h_{t}(x)h_{t}^{*}(x)
and

\mathcal{P}=G_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus G_{s}\oplus H_{1}\oplus H_{1}^{*}\oplus\cdots\oplus H_{t}\oplus H_{t}^{*}.

Let
M_{j}=\{u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{c})\in C_{\varphi}|\ u_{i}\in I_{j},i=1,\ldots,c\}
,
j=1,\ldots,r
, where
r=s+2t
. Since
I_{j}
is a field,
M_{j}
is a linear code over
I_{j}
of length
c
and dimension
k_{j}\geq 0
. Then the following lemma holds.

Lemma 4

If (
3
) is the decomposition of the polynomial
x^{p}-1
into irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
then

(i)

C_{\varphi}=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{r}
;

(ii)

k=k_{\pi}+m(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r})
, where
m=\mathrm{ord}_{p}(2)
.

Proof.
We can use the decomposition of a quasi-cyclic code, given in
[
12
]
but we prefer to follow the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3]
. Denote by
M
the module
C_{\varphi}
. Then

M=Me(x)=Me_{1}(x)+\cdots+Me_{r}(x)=M_{1}+\cdots+M_{r}.

If
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{c})\in M_{j}\cap\sum_{i\neq j}M_{i}
then
u_{1},\ldots,u_{c}\in I_{j}\cap\sum_{i\neq j}I_{i}=\{0\}
. Hence
u=\mathbf{0}
and
M
is a direct sum of the modules
M_{1},\ldots,M_{r}
. This gives us that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}M=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}M_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim_{I_{j}}M_{j}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}I_{j}=m\sum_{j=1}^{r}\dim_{I_{j}}M_{j}.

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}M=\dim E_{\sigma}(C)
, then
\dim C=\dim F_{\sigma}(C)+\dim E_{\sigma}(C)=k_{\pi}+m\sum_{j=1}^{r}k_{j}
.

\Box

Since
\mathrm{Aut}(C)=\mathrm{Aut}(C^{\perp})
, the automorphism
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(C)
is also an automorphism of the dual code
C^{\perp}
. It turns out that
C^{\perp}=F_{\sigma}(C^{\perp})\oplus E_{\sigma}(C^{\perp})
. Denote
\pi(F_{\sigma}(C^{\perp}))
by
\widehat{C_{\pi}}
, and
\varphi(E_{\sigma}((C^{\perp})^{*}))
by
\widehat{C_{\varphi}}
. The following theorem presents the relationship between the considered subcodes of
C
and
C^{\perp}
.

Theorem 5

If
C
is a binary linear code invariant under the permutation
\sigma
, given in (
1
), then
C_{\pi}^{\perp}=\widehat{C_{\pi}}
and
C_{\varphi}^{\perp}=\widehat{C_{\varphi}}
.

Proof.

Take
u\in C_{\pi}
and
v\in\widehat{C_{\pi}}
. Then
\pi^{-1}(u)\in C
and
\pi^{-1}(v)\in C^{\perp}
, therefore

u\cdot v=\pi^{-1}(u)\cdot\pi^{-1}(v)=0
.
It follows that
v\in C^{\perp}_{\pi}
and so
\widehat{C_{\pi}}\subseteq C_{\pi}^{\perp}
.

If
w\in C_{\pi}^{\perp}
then

0=u\cdot w=\pi^{-1}(u)\cdot\pi^{-1}(w)
.
Hence
\pi^{-1}(w)\perp F_{\sigma}(C)
. If
y\in E_{\sigma}(C)
then

y\cdot\pi^{-1}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{c}w_{i}(y_{(i-1)p+1}+y_{(i-1)p+2}+\cdots+y_{ip})\equiv 0\pmod{2}

and so
\pi^{-1}(w)\perp E_{\sigma}(C)
. Hence
\pi^{-1}(w)\perp C
, which proves that
\pi^{-1}(w)\in C^{\perp}
and
w\in\pi(F_{\sigma}(C^{\perp}))=\widehat{C_{\pi}}
. It follows that
C_{\pi}^{\perp}\subseteq\widehat{C_{\pi}}
, and therefore
C_{\pi}^{\perp}=\widehat{C_{\pi}}
.

Now consider the codes
C_{\varphi}
and
\widehat{C_{\varphi}}
. If
a(x),b(x)\in\mathcal{P}
then

\displaystyle a(x)b(x^{-1})

\displaystyle=(a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{p-1}x^{p-1})(b_{0}+b_{1}x^{p-1}+\cdots+b_{p-1}x)

\displaystyle=a\cdot b+(a\cdot\sigma(b))x+\cdots+(a\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(b))x^{p-1},

where
a\cdot b=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}a_{i}b_{i}
is the inner product of the binary vectors
a
and
b
.
If
v=(v_{1}(x),\ldots,v_{c}(x))
and
w=(w_{1}(x),\ldots,w_{c}(x))
are vectors in
\mathcal{P}^{c}
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}(x)w_{i}(x^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot w_{i}+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma(w_{i}))x+\cdots\\
+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(w_{i}))x^{p-1}.

Let
v\in C_{\varphi}
,
w\in\widehat{C_{\varphi}}
,
v^{\prime}=\varphi^{-1}(v)\in E^{*}_{\sigma}(C)
, and
w^{\prime}=\varphi^{-1}(w)\in E^{*}_{\sigma}(C^{\perp})
. Then
w^{\prime}
,
\sigma(w^{\prime}),\ldots,\sigma^{p-1}(w^{\prime})\in E^{*}_{\sigma}(C^{\perp})
, and so

If
v^{\prime}=(v_{11},\dots,v_{1p},\dots,v_{c1},\dots,v_{cp})
,
w^{\prime}=(w_{11},\dots,w_{1p},\dots,w_{c1},\dots,w_{cp})
,
v_{i}=(v_{i1},\dots,v_{ip})

\displaystyle v^{\prime}\cdot w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot w_{i}=0,

\displaystyle v^{\prime}\cdot\sigma(w^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma(w_{i})=0,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{\prime}\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(w^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(w_{i})=0.

It turns out that

\langle v,w\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}(x)w_{i}(x^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot w_{i}+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma(w_{i}))x+\cdots\\
+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}v_{i}\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(w_{i}))x^{p-1}=0.

This proves that
w\in C^{\perp}_{\varphi}
and so
\widehat{C_{\varphi}}\subseteq C_{\varphi}^{\perp}
.

If

\sum_{i=1}^{c}w_{i}(x)v_{i}(x^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}w_{i}\cdot v_{i}+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}w_{i}\cdot\sigma(v_{i}))x+\cdots+(\sum_{i=1}^{c}w_{i}\cdot\sigma^{p-1}(v_{i}))x^{p-1}=0.

It follows that

. It turns out that

.
This gives us that

.

\Box

Lemma 6

The following holds for the subcodes of
C_{\varphi}
and
\widehat{C_{\varphi}}

; (ii)

Proof.
Recall that

H_{j}=I_{s+j}
,

,

Theorem 8

C
is an LCD code if and only if
E_{\sigma}(C)
and

are LCD codes.

Proof.
We will prove that if

is an LCD code if and only if both

and

are LCD codes.

\Rightarrow)
Let

and

\Leftarrow)
Let

To complete the proof, we take

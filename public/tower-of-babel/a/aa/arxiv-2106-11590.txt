Local measures
\tau_{p}

In this section we remind the notion of a local measure and some well-known facts about local measures.
Consider the group
G=SU(L,K)
. More explicitly,
G
is the group of matrices
A
such that
\begin{cases}AL\bar{A}^{\prime}=L,\\
\det(A)=1.\end{cases}
The Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
of the group
G
is defined by the system
\begin{cases}BL+L\bar{B}^{\prime}=0,\\
Tr(B)=0.\end{cases}
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the elements of
\mathcal{L}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Consider the matrix norm
||A||=max|a_{ij}|_{p}
in the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}_{p}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
B(k)=\{A,||A||<\frac{1}{p^{k}}\}
be a ball in
\mathcal{L}_{p}
of a sufficiently small radius. Denote by
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{(k)}
the image of
B(k)
under the exponential map. The following facts are well-known and can be found, for example, in
[
8
]
:

Proposition 1
.

a)
The group
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{(k)}
is a congruence subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
modulo
p^{k}
for
k\geq 1
and
p\neq 2
or
k\geq 2
and
p=2
.

b)
The manifold
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
splits into a disjoint union of
[G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}:G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{(k)}]
balls of the same volume.

c)
The volume of
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{(k)}
is equal to the volume of
B(k)
starting from
k=1
for
p\neq 2
and from
k=2
for
p=2
.

It follows that

Vol_{\tau_{p}}(G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{N_{p}}{p^{(n+1)^{2}-1}},~p\neq 2;\\
\dfrac{N_{2}}{4^{(n+1)^{2}-1}},~p=2,\end{cases}

where

is the number of elements in
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{(1)}
for

and the order of
G_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}^{(2)}
for

. We need the following version (
[
8
]
) of Hensel’s lemma to compute

:

Theorem 3
.

Let

be a system of

. Denote by
M_{f}(a)
the Jacobi matrix of this system in

. Let

be the greatest invariant factor of

. Suppose that

. Then there exists a solution

Let

be the set of solutions of the system

. Consider the set

So,

Consider a closed Riemannian manifold

.

3

Lemma 2
.

The curvature

of space

equals

.

Proof.

The curvature

can be computed by the formula

where

Definition 1
.

The quotient
Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{\chi(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)}{\chi(\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}))}
is called the Hirzebruch-Mumford volume of the quotient space
\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma
or the Hirzebruch-Mumford covolume of the group
\Gamma
.

It means that
Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{Vol_{\Omega}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)}{n+1}
, since
\chi(\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C}))=n+1
.

So,

Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{Vol_{\Omega}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)}{n+1}=Vol_{\omega_{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)\cdot\dfrac{n!}{(2\pi)^{n}}.

Let
K
be the maximal compact subgroup
S(U(n)\times U(1))
of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We note that

Vol_{\omega}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})=Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)\cdot Vol_{\omega_{2}}(K\backslash G/G_{\mathbb{Z}})=

=Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)\cdot Vol_{\omega_{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})=Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)\cdot Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)\cdot\dfrac{(2\pi)^{n}}{n!}.

So,

Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{n!\cdot Vol_{\omega}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})}{(2\pi)^{n}\cdot Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)}.

Using lemma
1
we obtain:

Lemma 3
.

Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{n!\cdot D^{\frac{n^{2}+3n}{4}}\sqrt{n+1}\cdot Vol_{\tau_{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})}{(2\pi)^{n}\cdot Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)},\textit{ for }d\equiv 3\pmod{4}

and

Vol_{HM}(\mathbb{B}^{n}/\Gamma)=\dfrac{n!\cdot D^{\frac{n^{2}+3n}{4}}\sqrt{n+1}\cdot Vol_{\tau_{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})}{(4\pi)^{n}\cdot Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)},\textit{ for }d\equiv 1\pmod{4}.

So it requires to compute
Vol_{\omega_{1}}(K)
and
Vol_{\tau_{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/G_{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Lemma 5
.

The local volume
\tau_{p}(G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})
for

equals

\tau_{p}(G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n+1}\left(1-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)^{i}p^{-i}\right).

We compute
\beta_{p}
using the article
[
4
]
. By definition,
\beta_{p}=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}p^{-N(n+1)^{2}}\#U(L,\mathcal{O}_{K}/p^{N}\mathcal{O}_{K})
. This limit stabilizes for
N\geq 2\nu_{p}(\det(L))+1
, so in our case
N=1
. By lemma

[U(L,\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K}):SU(L,\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K})]=p(1-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)p^{-1})

\#SU(L,\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K})=\dfrac{\beta_{p}\cdot p^{(n+1)^{2}-1}}{1-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)p^{-1}}
. So it suffices to compute
\beta_{p}
. The Jordan decomposition of the lattice

contains only one component
L_{0}
,
d_{0}=0
(using the notation from
[
4
]
), so
\beta_{p}=p^{-\dim G_{0}}\#G_{0}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(
\mathbb{F}_{p}
– finite field of order

\tau_{p}(G_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=\dfrac{\#SU(L,\mathcal{O}_{K}/p\mathcal{O}_{K})}{p^{(n+1)^{2}-1}}=\dfrac{\beta_{p}}{1-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)p^{-1}}=\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n+1}\left(1-\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)^{n+1}p^{-(n+1)}\right).

∎

Lemma 8
.

Vol_{\omega_{1}}(SU(n))=\dfrac{\sqrt{n}(2\pi)^{\frac{n^{2}+n-2}{2}}}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}i!}

Proof.

It is well-known (

6

, then the volume of the group

equals
\dfrac{2^{\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}}n^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}(2\pi)^{\frac{n^{2}+n-2}{2}}}{1!2!3!\ldots(n-1)!}

Floquet-engineered chiral-induced spin selectivity
Nguyen Thanh Phuc

Abstract
The control of electron spin, which is crucial to the stability of matter, offers new possibilities for manipulating the properties of molecules and materials with potential applications in spintronics and chemical reactions. Recent experiments have demonstrated that the electron transmission through chiral molecules depends on the electron spin orientation, a phenomenon known as chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS). In this study, we show that CISS can be observed in achiral systems driven by an external circularly polarized laser field in the framework of Floquet engineering. By using the Floquet theory for a time-periodically driven system to investigate spin-dependent electron transport in a two-terminal setup, we demonstrate that the spin polarization can approach unity if the light intensity is sufficiently strong, the rate of dephasing is sufficiently small, and the average chemical potential of the two leads is within an appropriate range of values, which is narrow because of the high frequency of the laser field. To obtain a wider range of energies for large spin polarization, a combination of chiral molecules and light-matter interactions is considered and the spin polarization of electrons transported through a helical molecule driven by a laser field is evaluated.

Introduction

The spin angular momentum is a fundamental characteristic of the electron which can regulate the properties of atoms, molecules, and materials.
In accordance with their quantum statistics, two electrons occupying the same orbital must have opposite spins, namely the Pauli exclusive principle.
The exchange energy that determines the energy gap between spin singlet and triplet states is particularly important for understanding the electronic configurations and the accompanying magnetic and optical properties of different atoms and molecules.

Floquet-engineered CISS

As an example of achiral systems, we consider spin-dependent electron transport through a two-dimensional (2D) square lattice (of size
N_{x}\times N_{y}
in the

With the potential gradient pointing along the

\hat{H}_{\text{SO}}=\alpha(\hat{\sigma}_{x}\hat{p}_{y}-\hat{\sigma}_{y}\hat{p}_{x})

An external circularly polarized laser field with the time-dependent vector potential given by

\displaystyle\times\hat{c}_{j_{x},j_{y},s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\Big\}+\sum_{j_{x}=1}^{N_{x}}\sum_{j_{y}=1}^{N_{y}-1}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\Big\{\hat{c}_{j_{x},j_{y}+1,s}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\times\left[t_{y}(t)\delta_{ss^{\prime}}-it_{y}^{\text{SO}}(t)(\sigma_{x})_{ss^{\prime}}\right]\hat{c}_{j_{x},j_{y},s^{\prime}}+\text{h.c.}\Big\},

(1)

where

\displaystyle t_{x}^{(\text{SO})}(t)=

\displaystyle t_{x}^{(\text{SO})}e^{-i\mathcal{A}_{x}\cos\Omega t},

(2)

\displaystyle t_{y}^{(\text{SO})}(t)=

(3)

];

in the two-terminal setup can be expressed as

\displaystyle I_{s}=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{h}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\text{d}\epsilon\sum_{s^{\prime}=\uparrow,\downarrow}\Big[T_{\text{R}s,\text{L}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\epsilon)f_{\text{L}}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle T_{\text{L}s,\text{R}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\epsilon)f_{\text{R}}(\epsilon)\Big],

(4)

where

are the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions for electrons in the left and right leads, respectively.
The transmission probability from the left to the right lead for electrons with initial spin
s^{\prime}
, initial energy

and final spin
s
, final energy
\epsilon+k\hbar\Omega

|k|
photons if

(

and the Fourier components
G^{(k)}
of the retarded Green’s function by

\displaystyle T_{\text{R}s,\text{L}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\epsilon)=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{R}}(\epsilon+k\hbar\Omega)\Gamma_{\text{L}}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\times\left|\sum_{j_{y},j_{y}^{\prime}=1}^{N_{y}}G_{j_{x}=1,j_{y},s^{\prime};j_{x}^{\prime}=N,j_{y}^{\prime},s}^{(k)}(\epsilon)\right|^{2}.

(5)

Similarly, the transmission probability for electrons from the right to the left lead is given by

\displaystyle T_{\text{L}s,\text{R}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\epsilon)=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{L}}(\epsilon+k\hbar\Omega)\Gamma_{\text{R}}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\times\left|\sum_{j_{y},j_{y}^{\prime}=1}^{N_{y}}G_{j_{x}=N,j_{y},s^{\prime};j_{x}^{\prime}=1,j_{y}^{\prime},s}^{(k)}(\epsilon)\right|^{2}.

The wide-band limit is taken by assuming that the broadening functions are energy independent:

\displaystyle c_{s}=\frac{e^{2}}{2h}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{s^{\prime}=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left[T_{\text{L}s,\text{R}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\bar{\mu})+T_{\text{R}s,\text{L}s^{\prime}}^{(k)}(\bar{\mu})\right].

(7)

The spin polarization is defined by

\displaystyle P_{\text{s}}=\frac{c_{\uparrow}-c_{\downarrow}}{c_{\uparrow}+c_{\downarrow}}.

(8)

The Fourier components of the Green’s function can be expressed in terms of the Fourier components of the Floquet states as

\displaystyle G^{(k)}(\epsilon)=\sum_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}\frac{|u_{\alpha,k^{\prime}+k}\rangle\langle u_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}^{+}|}{\epsilon-(\epsilon_{\alpha}+k^{\prime}\hbar\Omega-i\hbar\gamma_{\alpha})},

(9)

where the Fourier components
|u_{\alpha,k}\rangle
of the Floquet state
|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle
are defined by

\displaystyle|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}|u_{\alpha,k}\rangle e^{-ik\Omega t},

(10)

\displaystyle|\psi_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\,e^{(-i\epsilon_{\alpha}/\hbar-\gamma_{\alpha})t}|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle.

(11)

Here, the state

satisfies the time-dependent Schrodinger equation, from which the Floquet state
|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle
is a solution of the eigenvalue equation in the extended Hilbert space with a periodic time coordinate

\displaystyle\left[\hat{H}(t)-i\hat{\Sigma}-i\hbar\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\right]|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=(\epsilon_{\alpha}-i\hbar\gamma_{\alpha})|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle.

(12)

The self-energy
\hat{\Sigma}
, which results from the coupling of electrons to the leads, has the following matrix elements:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{j_{x},j_{y},s;j_{x}^{\prime},j_{y}^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\text{L}}}{2}\delta_{j_{x},1}\delta_{j_{x}^{\prime},1}+\frac{\Gamma_{\text{R}}}{2}\delta_{j_{x},N}\delta_{j_{x}^{\prime},N}\right).

(13)

Due to the Brillouin zone structure of the Floquet spectrum, it is sufficient to compute all eigenvalues in the first Brillouin zone

Since the operator on the left-hand side of Eq. (

\displaystyle\left[\hat{H}(t)+i\hat{\Sigma}-i\hbar\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\right]|u_{\alpha}^{+}(t)\rangle=(\epsilon_{\alpha}+i\hbar\gamma_{\alpha})|u_{\alpha}^{+}(t)\rangle.

(14)

The Floquet states

\displaystyle\langle u_{\alpha}^{+}(t)|u_{\beta}(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\,\delta_{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}|u_{\alpha}(t)\rangle\langle u_{\alpha}^{+}(t)|=

\displaystyle\,1.

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{m}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\text{d}t\,e^{im\Omega t}\hat{H}(t).

III
Conclusion

We have shown that achiral molecules and materials can be used to realize CISS in two-terminal electron transport provided that the system is driven by an external circularly polarized laser field and the average chemical potential of the two leads is within the proper range of values, which is narrow because of the high frequency of the laser.
Because the light-matter interaction can effectively break the time-reversal symmetry in the dynamics of electrons, spin polarization is nonzero without dephasing, in contrast to ordinary CISS in chiral molecules.
Chiral molecules can be combined with light-matter interactions to obtain a wider range of energies for large spin polarization.
For sufficiently strong driving and a slow dephasing rate, which are achievable in current experiments, the spin polarization can get close to unity.
With potential applications in spintronics and chemical reactions, Floquet-engineered CISS provides a potent tool for controlling spin dynamics in a variety of molecules and materials.

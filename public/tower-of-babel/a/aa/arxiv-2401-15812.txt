Building graphs with high minimum degree on a budget
Kyriakos Katsamaktsis, Shoham Letzter

Abstract
We consider the problem of constructing a graph of minimum degree k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 in the following controlled random graph process, introduced recently by Frieze, Krivelevich and Michaeli. Suppose the edges of the complete graph on n n vertices are permuted uniformly at random. A player, Builder, sees the edges one by one, and must decide irrevocably upon seeing each edge whether to purchase it or not. Suppose Builder purchases an edge if and only if at least one endpoint has degree less than k k in her graph. Frieze, Krivelevich and Michaeli observed that this strategy succeeds in building a graph of minimum degree at least k k by τ k \tau_{k} , the hitting time for having minimum degree k k . They conjectured that any strategy using ε ​ n \varepsilon n fewer edges, where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 is any constant, fails with high probability. In this paper we disprove their conjecture. We show that for k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 Builder has a strategy which purchases n / 9 n/9 fewer edges and succeeds with high probability in building a graph of minimum degree at least k k by τ k \tau_{k} . For k = 1 k=1 we show that any strategy using ε ​ n \varepsilon n fewer edges fails with probability bounded away from 0, and exhibit such a strategy that succeeds with probability bounded away from 0.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer,
and
m\in[c_{1}n\log n,c_{2}n\log n]
, where
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are constants.
Let
H
be a graph on
V
with maximum degree at most
10\log n
.
Suppose we draw uniformly at random and with repetition edges from the complete graph on
V
until there are
m
distinct edges.
Then, with high probability, for every
v\in V
such that at least one of the edges drawn is incident to
v
in
H
,
there are at least
k
distinct edges incident to
v
which are not in
H
.

Proof.

Let
L_{v}\subseteq E(H)
be the set of edges incident to
v\in V
, so
\left|L_{v}\right|\leq 10\log n
.
Let
t
be the (random) number of edges we draw with repetition until we see
m
distinct edges.

We claim that with high probability
t\in[m,m+(\log n)^{3}]
.
Indeed, let
X_{i},i\in[m]
, be the random variable counting the number of edges drawn until the
i
-th distinct edge, and set
X_{0}=0
.
Then
X_{i}-X_{i-1}
is a geometric random variable with mean
\frac{1}{1-(i-1)/\binom{n}{2}}
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{m}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[X_{i}-X_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-(i-1)/\binom{n}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(1+\frac{i-1}{\binom{n}{2}}+O\left(\frac{i^{2}}{n^{4}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=m+\Theta\left(\frac{m^{2}}{n^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{m^{3}}{n^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=m+\Theta((\log n)^{2}).

Let
Y
be the number of repeated edges until we see
m
distinct edges. Then the above calculation implies
\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=O((\log n)^{2})
, whence from Markov’s inequality with high probability

.
Therefore
t\in[m,m+(\log n)^{3}]
, with high probability.

To complete the proof of the lemma we now show that, when drawing
t

, with high probability, for every
v
with at least one edge drawn from

, there are at least
k
(distinct) incident edges not in

.
Let

be the drawn edges and

be the event that

.
Let

be the event that

and disjoint from

is at most
k-1
.
The lemma follows from the next claim.

Claim 2.12
.

For every
t\in[m,m+(\log n)^{3}]

Proof.

Fix

,

Because

We have

For

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\!\left[B_{v,j}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\,\binom{t}{\ell}\,\left(\frac{n-1}{\binom{n}{2}}\right)^{\ell}\,\left(1-\frac{n-1-10\log n-(k-1)}{\binom{n}{2}}\right)^{t-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(c_{2}n\log n+(\log n)^{3})^{\ell}\ 2^{\ell}n^{-\ell}\ \left(1-\frac{(2-o(1))}{n}\right)^{(c_{1}-o(1))n\log n}

For

Conditioned on any choice of values for

\frac{\delta n\cdot(n-1)}{\binom{n}{2}}=2\delta.

Hence,

is stochastically dominated by a sum of

The latter gives that, with high probability,

\sum_{i=1}^{Cn}I_{i}\leq 3\delta Cn

Condition on this being the case.

Because the claim assumes that

e_{i}

Y_{i-1}

e_{i}
incident to
Y_{i-1}
.

Let

X_{Cn}^{\prime}=X_{Cn}\cap\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{Cn-1}Y_{i}\right)
be
the set of vertices for which Builder did not purchase the first incident edge in the random graph process, but did purchase a later edge (by step

Since a vertex may move from

to
X_{i}
only if there is an edge incident to
Y_{i-1}

\left|X_{Cn}^{\prime}\right|\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{Cn}I_{i}\leq 6\delta Cn.

Therefore, all but at most
6\delta Cn

are such that Builder bought the first incident edge to them.
Let
E_{1}\subseteq E(B_{Cn})
be the collection of edges that Builder bought and were the first incident edge to one of their endpoints.

We will compare Builder’s graph with the

Recall that

consists of the first edge incident to each vertex in the random graph process, and that

Let

be the subgraph of

Notice that any other edge in

\left|E(O_{1}^{Cn})\setminus E_{1}\right|\leq\left|X_{Cn}^{\prime}\cup Y_{Cn}\right|

Therefore,

\left|E(O_{1}^{Cn})\right|\leq\left|E_{1}\right|+\left|X_{Cn}^{\prime}\right|+\left|Y_{Cn}\right|\leq\left|E_{1}\right|+6\delta Cn+\delta n.

(3)

Similarly,

Hence, with high probability,

using

2.1

Concluding remarks

In this paper we studied the problem of constructing a graph with minimum degree

in the controlled random graph process introduced in
[
11
]
.

Theorem

1.1
disproves
[
11
, Conjecture 7]

.
Determining the optimal budget that

k

Similarly,

Theorems

1.3
and
1.2
disprove
[

for

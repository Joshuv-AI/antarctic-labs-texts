Introduction

Throughout this paper, we denote
(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n\geq 0}(1-aq^{n})
for
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Recall that a partition of a positive integer
n
is a nonincreasing finite sequence of positive integers, known as its parts, whose sum is
n
. We define
p(n)
as the number of partitions of
n
, which can be seen as the coefficients of its generating function given by

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}.

Hardy and Ramanujan
Hardy and Ramanujan (1918)
proved the following asymptotic formula for
p(n)
given by

\displaystyle p(n)\sim\dfrac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2n}{3}}\right),\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

using the celebrated Circle Method. Rademacher
Rademacher (1938)
refined the Hardy-Ramanujan Circle Method and derived the exact formula

\displaystyle p(n)=\dfrac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}(n)\sqrt{k}\dfrac{d}{dn}\left(\dfrac{\sinh(\frac{\pi}{k}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(n-\frac{1}{24})})}{\sqrt{n-\frac{1}{24}}}\right)

where

\displaystyle A_{k}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h<k\\
\gcd(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}\exp\left[\pi i\left(s(h,k)-\dfrac{2nh}{k}\right)\right]

and

\displaystyle s(h,k):=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\dfrac{j}{k}\left(\left\{\dfrac{hj}{k}\right\}-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)

is the Dedekind sum, where
\{t\}
denotes the fractional part of
t
. Recently, Banerjee et al.
Banerjee et al. (2023)
proved the following refined asymptotic formula for
p(n)
given by

\displaystyle\log p(n)\sim\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2n}{3}}-\log n-\log 4\sqrt{3}-\dfrac{0.44\cdots}{\sqrt{n}},\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

from a family of inequalities for
p(n)
, which was used to prove a refine of the inequality of DeSalvo and Pak
DeSalvo and Pak (2015)
, and Chen, Wang and Xie
Chen et al. (2016)
that reads

\displaystyle\left(1+\dfrac{\pi}{24n^{3/2}}-\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\right)p(n-1)p(n+1)<p(n)^{2}<\left(1+\dfrac{\pi}{24n^{3/2}}\right)p(n-1)p(n+1)

for
n\geq 120
.

Let
a_{k}(n)
be the number of partitions of
n
where parts that are multiple of
k
come up with
2
colors. For
k=1
, we have
a_{1}(n)=p_{2}(n)
where
p_{2}(n)
is the total number of partitions of
n
where parts come up with
2
colors and for
k=2
,
a_{2}(n)
also counts the number of cubic partitions (see
Kim (2008)
). The generating function for
a_{k}(n)
is given by
Ahmed et al. (2015)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(n)q^{n}.

Kotesovec
Kotesovec (2015)
found the following asymptotic formula for
a_{2}(n)
given by

\displaystyle a_{2}(n)\sim\dfrac{1}{8n^{5/4}}\exp(\pi\sqrt{n}),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Banerjee et al.
Banerjee et al. (2023)
conjectured that
a_{2}(n)
satisfies the following asymptotic formula

\displaystyle\log a_{2}(n)\sim\pi\sqrt{n}-\dfrac{5}{4}\log n-\log 8-\dfrac{0.79\cdots}{\sqrt{n}},\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(1)

Recently, Mauth
Mauth (2024)
proved (
1
) by finding the exact formula of Rademacher type for
a_{2}(n)
using a result of Zuckerman
Zuckerman (1939)
.

In this paper, we use a result of Sussman
Sussman (2017)
to derive Rademacher-type formulas for
a_{p}(n)
when
p\leq 23
is a prime, and deduce their asymptotic formulas in the spirit of (
1
), which can be considered as a partial extension of Mauth’s work. We give our main results as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\leq 23
be a prime. Then for
n\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle a_{p}(n)

\displaystyle=2\pi\sqrt{p}\left(\dfrac{1+p^{-1}}{24n-p-1}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\equiv j\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}I_{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6m}\sqrt{(1+p^{-1})(24n-p-1)}\right)\dfrac{b_{m}(n)}{m}

\displaystyle+2\pi\left(\dfrac{1+p}{24n-p-1}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
p\mid m\end{subarray}}^{\infty}I_{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6m}\sqrt{(1+p)(24n-p-1)}\right)\dfrac{b_{m}(n)}{m},

where

\displaystyle b_{k}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h<k\\
\gcd(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}\exp\left[-\dfrac{2\pi nhi}{k}+\pi s(h,k)i+\pi s\left(\dfrac{ph}{\gcd(p,k)},\dfrac{k}{\gcd(p,k)}\right)i\right]

and
I_{2}(s)
is the second modified Bessel function of the first kind (see Section
2
).

Corollary 1.2
.

For
p\leq 23
prime, we have

\displaystyle\log a_{p}(n)\sim\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2n(1+p^{-1})}{3}}-\dfrac{5}{4}\log n+\log\dfrac{2\sqrt{3p}(1+p^{-1})^{3/4}}{24^{5/4}}-\dfrac{c_{p}}{24\sqrt{6n}},\quad n\rightarrow\infty

where

\displaystyle c_{p}:=\dfrac{135}{\pi\sqrt{1+p^{-1}}}+\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{(1+p)^{3}}{p}}.

Setting
p=2
in Corollary
1.2
, we deduce the asymptotic formula due to Mauth
Mauth (2024)
given by

\displaystyle\log a_{2}(n)\sim\pi\sqrt{n}-\dfrac{5}{4}\log n-\log 8-\left(\dfrac{15}{8\pi}+\dfrac{\pi}{16}\right)\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}},\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

which immediately yields (
1
).

2

2
Fourier coefficients of a class of
\eta
-quotients

We consider in this section the exact formulas for the Fourier coefficients of the following class of holomorphic functions on the open disk given by

\displaystyle G(q):=\prod_{r=1}^{R}(q^{m_{r}};q^{m_{r}})_{\infty}^{\delta_{r}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n)q^{n},

where
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{R})
is a sequence of
R
distinct positive integers and
\mathbf{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{R})
is a sequence of
R
nonzero integers. The functions
G(q)
can be seen as
\eta
-quotients since
(q;q)_{\infty}=q^{-1/24}\eta(\tau)
, where Dedekind’s eta function, denoted
by
\eta(\tau)
, is defined by the infinite product
\eta(\tau):=q^{1/24}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})
with
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mbox{Im}(z)>0\}
. For useful properties of the Dedekind’s eta function and the definition of
\eta
-quotients, we refer the interested reader to the book
Ono (2004)
. Given a particular
G(q)
with
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\delta_{r}<0
, Sussman
Sussman (2017)
gave a Rademacher-type exact formula for
g(n)
, which is a special case of the work of Bringmann and Ono
Bringmann and Ono (2012)
on the coefficients of harmonic Maass forms. Sussman’s proof follows the original approach of Rademacher
Rademacher (1938)
on the Hardy-Ramanujan Circle Method. In the case where
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\delta_{r}\geq 0
, Chern
Chern (2019)
obtained an analogous formula with an error term using the method of O-Y. Chan
Chan (2005)
.

Before we state Sussman’s result, we need some definitions. For
(h,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
with
\gcd(h,k)=1
, we set

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\delta_{r},\quad\Delta_{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{R}m_{r}\delta_{r},

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(k)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=1}^{R}\dfrac{\gcd(m_{r},k)^{2}}{m_{r}}\delta_{r},\quad\Delta_{4}(k)=\prod_{r=1}^{R}\left(\dfrac{\gcd(m_{r},k)}{m_{r}}\right)^{\delta_{r}/2},\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\widehat{A}_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h<k\\
\gcd(h,k)=1\end{subarray}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{2\pi nhi}{k}-\pi i\sum_{r=1}^{R}\delta_{r}s\left(\dfrac{m_{r}h}{\gcd(m_{r},k)},\dfrac{k}{\gcd(m_{r},k)}\right)\right),

where
s(h,k)
is the Dedekind sum defined in Section
1
. We also set
L
as the least common multiple of
m_{1},\ldots,m_{R}
, and partition the set
\{1,\ldots,L\}
into two disjoint subsets

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{>0}

\displaystyle:=\{1\leq l\leq L:\Delta_{3}(l)>0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\leq 0}

\displaystyle:=\{1\leq l\leq L:\Delta_{3}(l)\leq 0\}.

Theorem 2.1

(
Sussman (2017)
)
.

If
\Delta_{1}>0
and the inequality

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq r\leq R}\dfrac{\gcd(m_{r},l)^{2}}{m_{r}}\geq\dfrac{\Delta_{3}(l)}{24}

(2)

holds for
1\leq l\leq L
, then for positive integers
n>-\Delta_{2}/24
, we have

\displaystyle g(n)=2\pi\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{>0}}\Delta_{4}(l)\left(\dfrac{\Delta_{3}(l)}{24n+\Delta_{2}}\right)^{(\Delta_{1}+1)/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\equiv l\bmod{L}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}I_{\Delta_{1}+1}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6k}\sqrt{\Delta_{3}(l)(24n+\Delta_{2})}\right)\dfrac{\widehat{A}_{k}(n)}{k},

where

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(s):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\frac{s}{2})^{\nu+2m}}{m!\Gamma(\nu+m+1)}

is the
\nu
th modified Bessel function of the first kind, and
\Gamma(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{s-1}\,dt
is the gamma function.

Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2

In this section, we use Theorem
2.1
to prove Theorem
1.1
. As a consequence, we obtain Corollary
1.2
using the asymptotic expansion of the modified Bessel function of the first kind due to Banerjee
Banerjee (2023)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Writing

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{p}(n)q^{n},

we have
\mathbf{m}=(1,p),\mathbf{\delta}=(-1,-1)
and
L=p
. We compute
\Delta_{1}=1
and
\Delta_{2}=-p-1
. Recalling that
p
is a prime, we have that for
l\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(l)=\begin{cases}1+\dfrac{1}{p},&l\neq p\\
1+p,&l=p\end{cases},\qquad\Delta_{4}(l)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{p},&l\neq p\\
1,&l=p\end{cases},

so that
L_{>0}=\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Since
p\leq 23
, we see that condition (
2
) holds for
l\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Applying Theorem
2.1
with
\widehat{A}_{k}(n)=b_{k}(n)
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{p}(n)=2\pi\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{>0}}\Delta_{4}(l)\left(\dfrac{\Delta_{3}(l)}{24n-p-1}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\equiv l\bmod{p}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}I_{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6m}\sqrt{\Delta_{3}(l)(24n-p-1)}\right)\dfrac{b_{m}(n)}{m}

for
n>(p+1)/24
. Plugging in the values of
\Delta_{3}(l)
and
\Delta_{4}(l)
yields the desired result.
∎

We see that the series for
a_{p}(n)
in Theorem
1.1
converges rapidly and that the term
m=1
contributes signficantly to the series. We thus obtain the asymptotic formula

\displaystyle a_{p}(n)\sim 2\pi\sqrt{p}\left(\dfrac{1+p^{-1}}{24n-p-1}\right)I_{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{(1+p^{-1})(24n-p-1)}\right),\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(3)

To prove Corollary
1.2
, we use the following asymptotic formula
(
Banerjee, 2023
, eq. (2.11))

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(s)\sim\dfrac{e^{s}}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{m}d_{m}(\nu)}{x^{m}},\quad s\rightarrow\infty,

(4)

where

\displaystyle d_{m}(\nu)=\dfrac{\binom{\nu-1/2}{m}(\nu+\frac{1}{2})_{m}}{2^{m}}

with

\displaystyle\dbinom{a}{m}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{a(a-1)\cdots(a-m+1)}{m!},&m\in\mathbb{N}\\
1,&m=0\end{cases},

\displaystyle(a)_{m}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a(a+1)\cdots(a+m-1),&m\in\mathbb{N}\\
1,&m=0\end{cases}

for
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

From (
4
) we get

\displaystyle I_{2}(s)=\dfrac{e^{s}}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}\left(1-\dfrac{15}{8s}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{s^{2}}\right)\right).

In view of (
3
), we have

\displaystyle a_{p}(n)\sim 2\sqrt{3p}

\displaystyle\cdot\dfrac{(1+p^{-1})^{3/4}}{(24n-p-1)^{5/4}}

\displaystyle\cdot\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{(1+p^{-1})(24n-p-1)}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{45}{8\pi\sqrt{6n(1+p^{-1})}}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right).

(5)

Since

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(24n-p-1)^{5/4}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(24n)^{5/4}}\left(1+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\sqrt{(1+p^{-1})(24n-p-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2n(1+p^{-1})}{3}}\right)\left(1-\dfrac{p^{-1/2}(1+p)^{3/2}\pi}{24\sqrt{6n}}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right),

we infer from (
5
) that

\displaystyle a_{p}(n)\sim 2\sqrt{3p}

\displaystyle\cdot\dfrac{(1+p^{-1})^{3/4}}{(24n)^{5/4}}\left(1+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right)\cdot\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2n(1+p^{-1})}{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1-\dfrac{p^{-1/2}(1+p)^{3/2}\pi}{24\sqrt{6n}}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right)\left(1-\dfrac{45}{8\pi\sqrt{6n(1+p^{-1})}}+O\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\right),

and taking logarithms yields the desired conclusion.
∎

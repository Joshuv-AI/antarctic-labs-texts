On Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities and A p A_{p} -weights for L L -shape arcs
Charles K. Chui, Lefan Zhong

Abstract
Let Γ \Gamma be an L L -shape arc consisting of 2 line segments that meet at an angle different from π \pi in the complex z z -plane ℂ {\mathbb{C}} . Application of the exterior conformal map ψ \psi from | w | > 1 |w|>1 onto ℂ \ Γ {\mathbb{C}}\backslash\Gamma , with ψ ⁡ ( ∞ ) = ∞ \psi(\infty)=\infty , introduces the level curves Γ n = { z = ψ ⁡ ( w ) : | w | = 1 + 1 n + 1 } \Gamma_{n}=\{z=\psi(w):|w|=1+{1\over{n+1}}\} . Let ψ ∗ \psi^{*} denote the continuous extension of ψ \psi from | w | > 1 |w|>1 to | w | ≥ 1 |w|\geq 1 , so that any family { z n , k : k = 0 , 1 , … , n } \{z_{n,k}:k=0,1,\dots,n\} of points on Γ \Gamma can be written as { z n , k = ψ ∗ ( w n , k ) } \{z_{n,k}=\psi^{*}(w_{n,k})\} , where | w n , k | = 1 |w_{n,k}|=1 . Let ω n ​ ( z ) = Π k = 0 n ​ ( z − z n , k ) \omega_{n}(z)=\Pi^{n}_{k=0}(z-z_{n,k}) . The main objective of this paper is to show that for L L -shape arcs, validation of the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities is equivalent to that of the totality of the A p A_{p} -weight conditions of | ω n ​ ( z ) | |\omega_{n}(z)| on Γ n \Gamma_{n} and a mild separation condition of { z n , k } \{z_{n,k}\} . Since the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities are essential to the study of Lagrange polynomial interpolation of continuous functions at the nodes { z n , k } \{z_{n,k}\} , another objective of this paper is to investigate the behavior of the polynomial interpolants at the Fejér points, defined by { z n , k = ψ ∗ ( e i ⁡ ( 2 ​ k ​ π + θ ) / ( n + 1 ) ) } \{z_{n,k}=\psi^{*}(e^{i(2k\pi+\theta)/(n+1)})\} for any choice of θ \theta . In this regard, we recall that for the interval [-1, 1], the Fejér points { z n , k = ψ ∗ ( e i ⁡ ( 2 ​ k + 1 ) ​ π / ( n + 1 ) ) } \{z_{n,k}=\psi^{*}(e^{i(2k+1)\pi/(n+1)})\} agree with the Chebyshev points and that the Chebyshev points are most commonly used as nodes for Lagrange polynomial interpolation. On the other hand, numerical experimentation demonstrates that for a typical open L L -shape arc Γ \Gamma , the Lebesgue constants tend to ∞ \infty at the rate of O ⁡ ( ( l ​ o ​ g ​ ( n ) ) 2 ) O((log(n))^{2}) , as the polynomial degree n n increases, while the A p A_{p} -weight conditions for the Fejér points { z n , k } \{z_{n,k}\} do not carry over from [-1, 1] to a truly L L -shape arc. Further numerical experiments also demonstrate that the least upper bounds of the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for the canonical Lagrange interpolation polynomials at { z n , k } \{z_{n,k}\} seem to grow at the rate of n β n^{\beta} , for some β > 0 \beta>0 that depends on p > 1 p>1 .

Introduction

Let
\Pi_{n}
denote the space of polynomials with degree at most
n

[
9
]

{{c_{1}}\over{n+1}}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\Big|P_{n}\Big(e^{i{{2\pi k}\over{{n+1}}}}\Big)\Big|^{p}\leq{1\over{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|P_{n}(e^{i\theta})\Big|^{p}d\theta\leq{{c_{2}}\over{n+1}}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\Big|P_{n}\Big(e^{i{{2\pi k}\over{{n+1}}}}\Big)\Big|^{p},

(1.1)

for

and maybe different for other inequalities throughout the discussions in this paper. It is natural to ask if the roots of unity in (1.1) can be replaced by a general family of
\{z_{n,k}:k=0,1,\dots,n\}
on the unit circle. In this regard, it is shown in our earlier work
[
5
]

{{c_{1}}\over{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|P_{n}(z_{n,k})\big|^{p}\leq{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big|P_{n}(e^{i\theta})\big|^{p}d\theta\leq{{c_{2}}\over{n+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big|P_{n}(z_{n,k})\big|^{p},

(1.2)

-weight condition of

\sup_{t_{0}<t_{1}}\Big\{{1\over{t_{1}-t_{0}}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|\omega_{n}((1+{{1}\over{n+1}})e^{it})|^{p}dt\Big\}^{1\over p}\Big\{{1\over{t_{1}-t_{0}}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}|\omega_{n}((1+{{1}\over{n+1}})e^{it})|^{-q}dt\Big\}^{1\over q}\leq c_{2},

(1.3)

where

\min_{0\leq k<n}|z_{n,k+1}-z_{n,k}|\geq{{c_{1}}\over{n+1}}.

(1.4)

This establishes a close relationship of the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities with the

-weights condition, introduced by Muckenhoupt

-

-

-

-

Let
\Gamma
denote an
L

\pi
. That is, the one-dimensional compact and connected set
\gamma

, respectively, in the following discussions. This section is devoted to the proof of the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Let
\Gamma
be an
L
-shape arc in
{\mathbb{C}}
and
1<p<+\infty
. Then
\{{z_{n,k}:k=0,1,\dots,n}\}\subset\Gamma
is an
M
-
Z
family if and only if it satisfies both of the
M
-
A_{p}
and the
c_{0}
-separation conditions. That is, the following two statements are equivalent.

(i)

All polynomials
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
satisfy:

c_{1}\sum^{n}_{k=0}|P_{n}(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\leq\int_{\Gamma}|P_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\leq c_{2}\sum^{n}_{k=0}|P_{n}(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n}).

(2.1)

(ii)

There exist constants
M>0
and
c_{0}>0
, such that

M_{n}:={sup}_{I\subset\Gamma_{n}}{\Bigg({1\over{|I|}}\int_{I}|\omega_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}|d\zeta|\Bigg)^{{1\over p}}\ \Bigg({1\over{|I|}}\int_{I}|\omega_{n}(\zeta)|^{-q}|d\zeta|\Bigg)^{1\over{q}}}\leq M,

(2.2)

and

\min_{0\leq k<j\leq n}{{|z_{n,j}-z_{n,k}|}\over{\min\big(dist(z_{n,j},\Gamma_{n}),dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\big)}}\geq c_{0}>0.

(2.3)

We first remark that if we re-arrange the points
\{z_{n,k}:k=0,1,\dots,n\}

\Gamma
in an increasing order, then it is easy to see that the condition (
2.3
) is equivalent to

\min_{0\leq k<n}|z_{n,k+1}-z_{n,k}|\geq c_{1}\min\big(dist(z_{n,k+1},\Gamma_{n}),dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\big).

(2.4)

Here, for convenience, we have used the same notation for the re-arranged points. To establish the theorem, we need the following three lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

For any polynomial
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
, the integrals of the
L_{p}
-norm over
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{n}
are equivalent to the
L_{p}
norm, in that

c_{1}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|P_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\leq\int_{\Gamma}|P_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\leq c_{2}\int_{\Gamma n}|P_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|.

(2.5)

The above lemma is a re-formulation of Lemma 10 in
[
6
]
.

Next let

be the domain with boundary

and denote by

the Hardy space on

.

Lemma 2.2

Under the

-separation condition in (

),

\sum^{n}_{k=0}|f(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\leq c_{2}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}|dz|,

for all

Proof.
Following the proof of Lemma 5.2 in
[

]

In view of (

), we note that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|f(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\leq{{2}\over{c_{0}}}{1\over{2\pi}}\int_{V_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}|dz|\leq c_{2}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}|dz|.

Lemma 2.3

Let

(2.7)

(ii)

\displaystyle\Big({1\over{2\pi}}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|P_{n,0}(z)|^{p}|dz|\Big)^{1\over p}\Big({1\over{2\pi}}\int_{|w|=1}\Big|{1\over{w-w_{n,0}}}\Big|^{q}|dw|\Big)^{1\over q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}|P(z_{n,0})|(dist(z_{n,0},\Gamma_{n}))^{1\over p}(1-|w_{n,0}|^{2})^{{1-q}\over{q}}

i.e.

{1\over{|B_{n}(w_{n,0})|}}\leq c_{2}\Big|{{dist(z_{n,0},\Gamma_{n})}\over{\Psi_{n}^{\prime}(w_{n,0})(1-|w_{n,0}|^{2})}}\Big|^{1\over p}\leq 4^{1\over p}c_{2}.

Thus, we have

\Big|\prod_{k>1}{{(w_{n,0}-w_{n,k})}\over{(1-{\overline{w_{n,k}}}w_{n,0})}}\Big|\geq(4^{1\over p}c_{2})^{-1}>0.

(2.12)

In particular, it follows, for
w_{n,0}=0
, that

|w_{n,1}|\geq{(4}^{1\over p}c_{2})^{-1}.

Therefore, application of the Koebe Distortion Theorem yields

\big|\Psi_{n}(w_{n,1})-\Psi_{n}(w_{n,0})\big|\geq{1\over 4}\big|\Psi_{n}^{\prime}(w_{n,0})\big||w_{n,1}|/(1+|w_{n,1}|)^{2};

that is,

{{|z_{n,1}-z_{n,0}|}\over{dist(z_{n,0},\Gamma_{n})}}\geq c_{1}

which is (2.4) for
k=0
. The same argument shows that (2.4) holds for any
k\leq n
. In fact, (2.12) can be generalized to

min_{j\neq k}\Big|\prod_{k>1}{{(w_{n,j}-w_{n,k})}\over{(1-{\overline{w_{n,k}}}w_{n,j})}}\Big|\geq c_{1}>0

(2.13)

In view of (2.13), we have, for
h\in H^{p}(U)
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|h(w_{n,k})|^{p}\big(1-|w_{n,k}|^{2}\big)\leq c_{2}\int_{|w|=1}|h(w)|^{p}|dw|.

Hence, for any
H\in E^{p}(D_{n})
, by setting
h(w)=H(\Psi_{n}(w))(\Psi_{n}^{\prime}(w))^{1\over p}
, we may conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}|H(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}|h(w_{n,k})|^{p}{{dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})}\over{|\Psi_{n}^{\prime}(w_{n,k})|}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\sum_{k=0}^{n}|h(w_{n,k})|^{p}(1-|w_{n,k}|^{2})\leq 4c_{2}\int_{|w|=1}|h(w)|^{p}|dw|;

or equivalently,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|H(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\leq 4c_{2}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|H(z)|^{p}|dz|.

(2.14)

For any
f\in L^{p}(\Gamma_{n})
, consider
g(z)=f(z)\omega_{n}(z)
, and let
P_{n}\in\Pi_{n}
, such that
P_{n}(z_{n,k})=(Sg)(z_{n,k})
. Then for
z\in D_{n}
, we have

(Sg)(z)={{\omega_{n}(z)}\over{2\pi i}}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}{{g(\zeta)}\over{\omega_{n}(\zeta)}}{{d\zeta}\over{\zeta-z}}+P_{n}(z).

Note that if
g\in E^{p}(D_{n})
, then this is the same as (2.10), and if
g(z)={1\over{(z-z_{n,0})^{m}}}
, with
m>0
, then
(Sg)(z)=0
so that the formula also holds. In any case, we have

(Sg)(z)=\omega_{n}(z)(Sf)(z)+P_{n}(z)

and

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sf)(z)|^{p}|\omega_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sg)(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}+\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|P_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sg)(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}+{c_{2}}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|P(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sg)(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}+{c_{2}}\big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|(Sg)(z_{n,k})|^{p}dist(z_{n,k},\Gamma_{n})\big)^{1\over p}.

It follows from (2.14), for
H(z)=(Sg)(z)
, that

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sf)(z)|^{p}|\omega_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sg)(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}.

Since
S
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\Gamma_{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|(Sf)(z)|^{p}|\omega_{n}(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|g(z)|^{p}|dz|\big)^{1\over p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\big(\int_{\Gamma_{n}}|f(\zeta)|^{p}|\omega_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}|d\zeta|\big)^{1\over p}.

This shows that the Cauchy Singular Integral operator in uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\Gamma_{n},|\omega_{n}|^{p})
. Thus,
|\omega_{n}|
are
A_{p}
-weights that satisfy (2.2) (see Theorem 4.15 in
[
3
]
).

3.6
Concluding remarks

While the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities provide a useful tool for the assessment of suitable sampling points
\{z_{n,k}:k=0,\cdots,n\}
for polynomial interpolation or approximation, based on comparison of the representing polynomial
P_{n}
at the data samples
P_{n}(z_{n,k})
with the continuous measurement of
P_{n}(z)
for
z\in\Gamma
, there have been few available criteria for selecting such sampling locations. In this paper, suitability of the sampling points on an
L
-shape arc
\Gamma
is characterized by the
M
-
A_{p}
weights based on
\omega_{n}(z):=\Pi_{k=0}^{n}(z-z_{n,k})
(together with certain separation condition on
\{z_{n,k}\}
). In this regard, we believe the main result (Theorem 1), established in Section 2, should be valid for any piece-wise smooth open arc (with finitely many corners). Unfortunately, the proof of this extension seems to be much harder and is therefore delayed to a future work.

On the other hand, from numerical experimentation, it is clear that the behavior of
|\omega_{n}(z)|
for the typical
L
-shape arc is significantly different from that for the interval
[-1,1]
and smooth open arcs
\gamma
. This indicates that the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities might fail to hold for the canonical Lagrange interpolating polynomials at the Fejér points on
L
-shape arcs and more generally for piece-wise smooth open arc with corners, even with adjustment. The growth order of the Lebesgue constants for the adjusted Fejér points on
L
-shape arcs seems to be faster than that of the Chebyshev points on
[-1,1]
. Theoretic development in this direction is also delayed to our future research.

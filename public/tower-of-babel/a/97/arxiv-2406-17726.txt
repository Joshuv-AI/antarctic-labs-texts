Extensions of Panjer’s recursion for mixed compound distributions
Spyridon M. Tzaninis, Apostolos Bozikas

Abstract
. In actuarial practice, the usual independence assumptions for the collective risk model are often violated, implying a growing need for considering more general models that incorporate dependence. To this purpose, the present paper studies the mixed counterpart of the classical Panjer family of claim number distributions and their compound version, by allowing the parameters of the distributions to be viewed as random variables. Under the assumptions that the claim size process is conditionally i.i.d. and (conditionally) mutually independent of the claim counts, we provide a recursive algorithm for the computation of the probability mass function of the aggregate claim sizes. The case of a compound Panjer distribution with exchangeable claim sizes is also studied. For the sake of completeness, our results are illustrated by various numerical examples.

1.
Introduction

Compound distributions are widely used in actuarial science for modelling the aggregate claims amount (denoted as
S
) paid by an insurance company over a fixed period of time. The computation of the probability distribution
P_{S}
of
S
is one of the main topics in the field. More formally, the random variable
S
is defined as the random sum
S:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}X_{n}
, where
X:=\{X_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
represents the individual claim sizes and
N
is the random number of claims. Usually, the sequence
X
is assumed to be independent and identically distributed and independent of
N
. Even under these independence assumptions, the determination of
P_{S}
can be a difficult task, since one usually has to compute higher order convolutions. Consequently, recursive algorithms are often used for the computation of
P_{S}
.

One of the most prominent algorithms in actuarial literature is Panjer’s recursion (see
Panjer (1981)
, Theorem, p. 24), which in the case of discrete claims size distribution simplifies the computation of the probability mass function (abbreviated as pmf) of
S
. Recall that the Panjer class of order
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, denoted by
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;k)
, is a collection of claim number distributions
P_{N}=\{p_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, satisfying
p_{n}=0
for any
n\leq k-1
and

(1)

p_{n}=\left(a+\frac{b}{n}\right)\cdot p_{n-1}\quad\text{ for all }n\geq k+1

(see
Hess et al. (2002)
, p. 283). The members of the aforementioned class have been characterized by
Sundt & Jewell (1981)
for
k=0
,
Willmot (1988)
for
k=1
and
Hess et al. (2002)
for a general
k\geq 2
. Recently,
Fackler (2023)
proposed a reparametrization for the members of the
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;k)
class for
k\geq 0
resulting in a more practical and unified representation of the corresponding probability distributions. More general recursive expressions than (
1
) have also been studied by
Hesselager (1994)
,
Schröter (1990)
,
Sundt (1992)
and
Wang & Sobrero (1994)
. For an extensive review on recursive formulas for actuarial applications, we refer to
Sundt & Vernic (2009)
and the references therein.

In the case of inhomogeneous insurance portfolios, mixtures of claim number distributions are considered for modelling the claim counts.
Gençtürk & Yiğiter (2016)
,
Gómez-Déniz et al. (2004)
,
Mansoor et al. (2020)
,
Tzougas et al. (2019)
and
Wang (2011)
proposed some (compound) mixed negative binomial distributions with different parametrizations using various mixing distributions. On the other hand, the literature for mixed Poisson and compound mixed Poisson distributions is much more enriched, encompassing, among others, the works of
Antzoulakos & Chadjiconstantinidis (2004)
,
Karlis & Xekalaki (2005)
,
Nadarajah & Kotz (2006a)
;
Nadarajah & Kotz (2006b)
,
Sundt & Vernic (2004)
,
Tzougas et al. (2021)
and
Willmot (1986)
;
Willmot (1987)
. In particular, recursive formulas have been obtained by
Willmot (1993)
under the additional assumption that the logarithm of the induced (by the mixing distribution) probability density function can be written as the ratio of two polynomials. Moreover, by adopting the usual independence assumptions,
Hesselager (1996)
obtained a recursive formula for the computation of
P_{S}
.

In actuarial practice, the assumptions of independence among the random variables of
X
and the mutual independence between
X
and
N
are often violated (see among others
Albrecher & Boxma (2004)
,
Albrecher & Teugels (2006)
and
Kolev & Paiva (2008)
). In particular, the mutual independence assumption seems to be unrealistic, especially when considering inhomogeneous portfolios. This motivates us to consider a dependence structure among the random variables of
X
and between
N
and
X
. In contrast to the existing notion of compound (mixed) counting distributions, where the sequence
X
is considered to be i.i.d. and mutually independent of
N
(see
Grandell (1997)
, Chapter 8, and
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2019)
, p. 4), in our case
X
is only conditionally i.i.d. and conditionally mutually independent of
N
. This leads to the concept of
mixed compound distributions
(see Section
3
), which constitute a strong generalization of compound (mixed) distributions.

Under the above framework, this work studies the mixed counterpart of the original
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;0)
class and the corresponding compound distributions, by allowing the parameters of the claim number distribution
P_{N}
to be measurable functions of a random vector, the claim size process
X
to be conditionally i.i.d. and conditionally mutually independent of
N
. The above assumptions allow for a (possible) correlation (positive or negative) between
N
and
X
, as well as correlation among the random variables of
X
. In addition, we consider the case of compound Panjer distributions with exchangeable claims. Henceforth, we denote by
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
the mixed Panjer class, where
\varTheta
is a
d
-dimensional (
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
) random vector (also referred as structural parameter).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
provides a characterization for the members of
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
class in terms of regular conditional probabilities, and a recursive algorithm for the computation of their pmfSection
3
studies the probabilistic aspects of mixed compound distributions. Section
4
presents a recursive algorithm for the computation of
P_{S}
in the case that
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
and the claim size distribution is concentrated on
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Numerical applications are also given in Section
4
, whereas Section
5
concludes the paper.

The class of mixed Panjer claim number distributions

{\mathbb{N}}

stand for the natural and the real numbers, respectively, while

and
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:x\geq{0}\}

denotes the Euclidean space of dimension

. Given a topology
\mathfrak{T}

\varUpsilon

{\mathfrak{B}}(\varUpsilon)

\varUpsilon

\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathfrak{T}
. Also
{\mathfrak{B}}:={\mathfrak{B}}({\mathbb{R}})
and

{\mathbb{R}}
and
(\alpha,\beta)

to

. Given two measurable spaces
(\varOmega,\varSigma)
and
(\varUpsilon,T)
, as well as a
\varSigma
-

\sigma(Z):=\{Z^{-1}[B]:B\in T\}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by

.

Throughout what follows
(\varOmega,\varSigma,P)
is an arbitrary probability space and

is a random variable on
\varOmega

of
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
containing

.

Sundt & Jewell (1981)
proved that the only non-degenerate members of the original Panjer class of claim number distributions
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;0)
are the Poisson, the binomial and the negative binomial distributions (see
Sundt & Jewell (1981)
, Theorem 1). These distributions will be referred as
basic claim number distributions
. Since the members of
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;0)
satisfy the recursive condition (
1
), an interesting question is whether their mixtures also satisfy a recursive relation. The previous question traces back to
Sundt (1992)
, but for a more general family of claim number distributions, where it seems not to have a definite answer.

A way to model the mixtures of claim number distributions is to assume the existence of a random variable (or more generally of a random vector)
\varTheta
on
\varOmega
with values in
D\subseteq[0,\infty)
, such that the conditional distribution of
N
given
\varTheta
satisfies condition
P_{N\mid\varTheta}={\bf K}(\varTheta)

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-almost surely (written a.s. for short), where
{\bf K}(\varTheta)
is a conditional distribution (cf., e.g.,
Lyberopoulos et al. (2019)
, p. 455, for the definition of a conditional distribution). A claim number distribution
P_{N}
that satisfies the previous condition is said to be a
mixed claim number distribution
. This motivates us to provide the following definitions within the class of mixed claim number distributions.

Definitions 2.1
.

Let
\varTheta
be a
d
-dimensional random vector with values in
D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. A random variable
N
is distributed according to:

(a)
the
mixed Poisson distribution
with structural parameter
\xi(\varTheta)
(written
MP
\big(\xi(\varTheta)\big)
for short), where
\xi
is a
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
{\mathfrak{B}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
-measurable function, if

p_{n}(\varTheta):=P(N=n\mid\varTheta)=e^{-\xi(\varTheta)}\cdot\frac{\big(\xi(\varTheta)\big)^{n}}{n!}\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\text{-a.s.}

for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
;

(b)
the
mixed binomial
distribution with structural parameters
z_{1}(\varTheta)
and
z_{2}(\varTheta)
(denoted by
MB
\big(z_{1}(\varTheta),z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
for short), where
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})
- and
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,1)
-measurable functions, respectively, if

p_{n}(\varTheta)=\binom{z_{1}(\varTheta)}{n}\cdot\big(z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n}\cdot\big(1-z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{z_{1}(\varTheta)-n}\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\text{-a.s.}

for any
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,z_{1}(\varTheta)\}

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s.. In particular, if
z_{1}(\varTheta)
or
z_{2}(\varTheta)
is degenerate, simply write
MB
\big(m,z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
) or
MB
\big(z_{1}(\varTheta),p\big)
(
p\in(0,1)
), respectively;

(c)
the
mixed negative binomial
distribution with structural parameters
\rho_{1}(\varTheta)
and
\rho_{2}(\varTheta)
(written
MNB
\big(\rho_{1}(\varTheta),\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
for short), where
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
{\mathfrak{B}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
- and
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,1)
-measurable functions, respectively, if

p_{n}(\varTheta)=\frac{\Gamma\big(\rho_{1}(\varTheta)+n\big)}{n!\cdot\Gamma\big(\rho_{1}(\varTheta)\big)}\cdot\big(\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{\rho_{1}(\varTheta)}\cdot\big(1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n}\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\text{-a.s.}

for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. In particular, if
\rho_{1}(\varTheta)
or
\rho_{2}(\varTheta)
is degenerate, simply write
MNB
\big(r,\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(
r>0
) or
MNB
\big(\rho_{1}(\varTheta),p\big)
(
p\in(0,1)
), respectively.

In what follows, unless stated otherwise,
\varTheta
is a
d
-dimensional random vector with values in
D\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\xi,z_{1},z_{2},\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are as in Definitions
2.1
.

Definition 2.2
.

A claim number distribution
P_{N}
belongs to the class
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
if, the conditional distribution of
N
given
\varTheta
satisfies for every
n\in R_{N}\setminus\{0\}
the condition

(2)

\displaystyle p_{n}(\varTheta)=\bigg(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)}{n}\bigg)\cdot p_{n-1}(\varTheta)\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\text{-a.s.},

where
a
and
b
are real-valued
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-measurable functions.

Remarks 2.3
.

(a)
In the special case
P_{\varTheta}=\delta_{\theta_{0}}
, where
\delta_{\theta_{0}}
denotes the Dirac measure concentrated on
\theta_{0}\in D
, the class
{\bf Panjer}(a(\theta_{0}),b(\theta_{0});0)
coincides with the original Panjer class of claim number distributions.

(b)
Recursive formulas for mixed claim number distributions can be found also in
Gerhold et al. (2010)
, Lemma 5.7, but in a different context from condition (
2
). More precisely, these recursive formulas arise from a change of distributions technique for the mixing distribution.

Since the definition of
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
involves conditioning, it is natural to expect that the notion of regular conditional probabilities (also known as disintegrations) will play a crucial role in order to avoid trivialities and to treat conditioning in a rigorous way. The following definition is a special instance of
Fremlin (2013)
, Definition 452E, appropriately adapted for our purposes (see also
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2021)
, Definition 3.1).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\varUpsilon,T,Q)
be a probability space. A family
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in\varUpsilon}
of probability measures on
\varSigma
is called a
regular conditional probability
(written rcp for short) of
P
over
Q
if

(d1)

for each
E\in\varSigma
the map
y\mapsto P_{y}(E)
is
T
-measurable;

(d2)

\int P_{y}(E)\,Q(dy)=P(E)
for each
E\in\varSigma
.

If
f:\varOmega\to\varUpsilon
is an inverse-measure-preserving function (i.e.,
P\big(f^{-1}[B]\big)=Q(B)
for each
B\in T
), an rcp
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in\varUpsilon}
of
P
over
Q
is called
consistent
with
f
if, for each
B\in T
, the equality
P_{y}\big(f^{-1}[B]\big)=1
holds for
Q
-almost all (written a.a. for short)
y\in B
.

The following result is taken from
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2012)
, and serves as a basic tool for the proofs of the upcoming results. In order to present it, denote by
\circ
the function composition operator.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2012)
, Lemma 3.5)
Let
(\varUpsilon,T,Q)
be a probability space and
f:\varOmega\to\varUpsilon
be an inverse-measure-preserving function. Put
\sigma(f):=\{f^{-1}[B]:B\in T\}
and suppose that an rcp
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in\varUpsilon}
of
P
over
Q
consistent with
f
exists. Then, for each
A\in\varSigma
and
B\in T
the following hold true:

(i)

\mathbb{E}_{P}[g\mid\sigma(f)]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\bullet}}[g]\circ f

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(f)
-a.s., where
g
is a
\varSigma
-
T
-measurable function such that
\int g\,dP
is defined on
{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
;

(ii)

\int_{B}P_{y}(A)\,Q(dy)=\int_{f^{-1}[B]}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\mid\sigma(f)]\,dP=P\big(A\cap f^{-1}[B]\big)
.

Henceforth,
\{P_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in D}
is an rcp of
P
over
P_{\varTheta}
consistent with
\varTheta
. For any
n\in R_{N}
and
\theta\in D
, set
p_{n}(\theta):=P_{\theta}(N=n)
.

Remarks 2.6
.

(a)
The use of rcp’s in applied probability has been criticized mainly due to the fact that their existence is not always guaranteed (cf., e.g.,
Stoyanov (2013)
, Subsection 2.4). However, if
\varSigma
is countably generated and
P
is perfect (see
Fremlin (2013)
, 451A(d), for the definition of a perfect measure), then there always exists an rcp
\{P_{y}\}_{y\in\varUpsilon}
of
P
over
Q
consistent with every inverse-measure-preserving function
f
from
\varOmega
into
\varUpsilon
, provided that
T
is also countably generated (see
Faden (1985)
, Theorems 6 and 3). Note that the most important applications in actuarial science are still rooted in the case that
\varOmega
is a Polish space (cf., e.g.,
Cohn (2013)
, p. 239, for its definition), where such rcp’s always exist. In particular,
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{{\mathbb{N}}}
are typical examples of Polish spaces appearing in applications (see also
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2021)
, Remark 3.2). For an excellent review on rcp’s and their applications, we refer the interested reader to
Chang & Pollard (1997)
.

(b)
Each of the probability measures
P_{\theta}
of the corresponding rcp can be interpreted as a version of the conditional probability
P(\bullet\mid\varTheta=\theta)
, which is well-defined, up to an a.s. equivalence, as a function of
\theta
, while the consistency of
\{P_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in D}
with
\varTheta
can be interpreted as the concentrating property
P(\varTheta\neq\theta\mid\varTheta=\theta)=0
for any
\theta\in D
of conditional probabilities.

(c)
Assume that
\varTheta
is a positive random variable and let
P_{N}
be a mixed claim number distribution. If the map
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
is increasing (resp. decreasing) for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
, then the random variables
N
and
\varTheta
are
P
-positively (resp. negatively) correlated.

In fact, assume first that the map
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
is increasing for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
. By applying Lemma
2.5
, we get that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[N\cdot\varTheta]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\big[\theta\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]\big]
, implying together with
Schmidt (2014)
, Theorem 2.2, and Lemma
2.5
, that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[N\cdot\varTheta]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[N]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}[\varTheta]
; hence
N
and
\varTheta
are positively correlated. The proof for a decreasing map
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
is similar.

It is natural to ask which are the members of
Panjer
(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
, and if they can be characterized in terms of the basic claim number distributions. By applying Lemma
2.5
, the next result yields a characterization for the members of the
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
class via rcp’s, connecting in this way the defining property of the original Panjer class and of its mixed counterpart. This characterization also serves as a useful tool for proving the results presented throughout the paper.

Proposition 2.7
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
;

(ii)

(P_{\theta})_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\theta),b(\theta);0)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
.

Proof.

Fix on arbitrary
n\in R_{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
F\in{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
. Statement (i) is equivalent to

\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}\cdot p_{n}(\varTheta)\big]\,dP=\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigg[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}\cdot\bigg(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)}{n}\bigg)\cdot p_{n-1}(\varTheta)\bigg]

or, according to Lemma
2.5
(i), to

\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{F}\cdot p_{n}(\theta)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\bigg[\mathbbm{1}_{F}\cdot\bigg(a(\theta)+\frac{b(\theta)}{n}\bigg)\cdot p_{n-1}(\theta)\bigg];

hence we equivalently get condition

p_{n}(\theta)=\bigg(a(\theta)+\frac{b(\theta)}{n}\bigg)\cdot p_{n-1}(\theta)\quad\text{for }P_{\varTheta}\text{-a.a. }\theta\in D,

which is equivalent to statement (ii). This completes the proof.
∎

Example 2.9

Remark 2.10

In the case that

Gómez-Déniz et al. (2004)
,
Willmot (1993)
and
Wang (2011)
. However a unified formula for these recursions does not exist in the literature; hence a question that naturally arises is whether we can obtain a recursion of the form

p_{n}=C_{n}\cdot p_{n-1}\quad\text{for any }n\in R_{N}{\setminus}\{0\},

where
C_{n}\in(0,\infty)
, for the members of
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
. The next theorem introduces an appropriate rcp of
P
over
P_{N}
consistent with
N
in order to obtain such a recursive formula.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
. There exists a family
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{R}_{N}}
of probability measures on
\varSigma
defined by means of

\mu_{n}(A):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}\quad\text{for any }A\in\varSigma

being an rcp of
P
over
P_{N}
consistent with
N
, such that for any
n\in{R}_{N}\setminus\{0\}
and
F\in{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
, the condition

p_{n}=C_{n}\cdot p_{n-1}

holds true, where

C_{n}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n-1}}\bigg[a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)}{n}\bigg].

Proof.

Fix on an arbitrary
n\in R_{N}
and define the set-function
\mu_{n}:\varSigma\to(0,\infty)
by

\mu_{n}(A):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}\quad\text{for any }A\in\varSigma.

A standard computation justifies that
\mu_{n}
is a probability measure on
\varSigma
. Let
A\in\varSigma
be fixed, but arbitrary. First note that condition (d1) holds since the map
n\mapsto\mu_{n}(A)
is clearly
\mathcal{P}(R_{N})
-measurable while condition (d2) follows from

P(A)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\mid N]\big]=\int\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}\,dP_{N}=\int\mu_{n}(A)\,dP_{N};

hence the family
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{R}_{N}}
of probability measures is an rcp of
P
over
P_{N}
. The consistency of
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in R_{N}}
follows immediately by the definition of
\mu_{n}
.

As
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
and
\{P_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in D}
is an rcp of
P
over
P_{\varTheta}
consistent with
\varTheta
, we can apply Proposition
2.7
, together with Lemma
2.5
, to get that

(3)

\displaystyle p_{n+1}=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\bigg[\bigg(a(\theta)+\frac{b(\theta)}{n+1}\bigg)\cdot p_{n}(\theta)\bigg].

But since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n}}[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{N=n\}}]}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}[\mathbbm{1}_{F}\cdot p_{n}(\theta)]}{p_{n}}\quad\text{for every }F\in{\mathfrak{B}}(D),

where the second equality follows by Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\bigg[\bigg(a(\theta)+\frac{b(\theta)}{n+1}\bigg)\cdot p_{n}(\theta)\bigg]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n}}\bigg[\bigg(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)}{n+1}\bigg)\bigg]\cdot p_{n}.

The latter together with condition (
3
) completes the proof.
∎

In the next set of examples, we apply Theorem
2.11
in order to obtain recursive formulas for the pmf of a mixed counting distribution that belongs to
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
. In our first example, we revisit the case of the mixed Poisson distribution.

Example 2.12
.

Fix an arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
P_{N}={\bf MP}\big(\xi(\varTheta)\big)
. Since
P_{N}
is an element of
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
with
a(\varTheta)=0
and
b(\varTheta)=\xi(\varTheta)
(see Table
1
), we apply Theorem
2.11
to get

p_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n-1}}\bigg[\frac{\xi(\varTheta)}{n}\bigg]\cdot p_{n-1}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(\xi(\varTheta)\big)^{n}\cdot e^{-\xi(\varTheta)}\big]}{n\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(\xi(\varTheta)\big)^{n-1}\cdot e^{-\xi(\varTheta)}\big]}\cdot p_{n-1}.

Setting
\widehat{u}_{\xi}(t):=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[e^{-t\cdot\xi(\varTheta)}\big]
(
t\geq 0
), the latter equality becomes

(4)

\displaystyle p_{n}=-\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{\widehat{u}_{\xi}^{(n)}(1)}{\widehat{u}_{\xi}^{(n-1)}(1)}\cdot p_{n-1},

where notation
f^{(n)}
stands for the
n
-th order derivative of a real-valued function
f
. Note that the initial value for the recursion is

p_{0}=\widehat{u}_{\xi}(1).

In particular, let
D=(0,\infty)
,
\xi=\text{id}_{D}
, where by
\text{id}_{D}
is denoted the identity map on
D
, and consider the gamma distribution with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
(written
Ga
(\alpha,\beta)
for short) defined as

\textbf{Ga}(\alpha,\beta)(B)=\int_{B}\frac{\beta^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\cdot x^{\alpha-1}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot x}\,\lambda(dx)\quad\text{for any }B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty),

where
\lambda
denotes the restriction to
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)
of the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathfrak{B}}
. Take
P_{\varTheta}=w_{1}\cdot{\bf Exp}(\beta)+w_{2}\cdot{\bf Ga}(\alpha,\beta)
(
\alpha,\beta>0
), where
w_{1},w_{2}\in[0,1]
with
w_{1}+w_{2}=1
. Since

\widehat{u}(t):=\widehat{u}_{\text{id}_{D}}(t)=w_{1}\cdot\frac{\beta}{\beta+t}+w_{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta+t}\Big)^{\alpha}\quad\text{for any }t\geq 0

it follows easily that

\widehat{u}^{(n)}(t)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(\beta+t)^{\alpha+n}}\cdot\Big(w_{1}\cdot n!\cdot\beta\cdot(\beta+t)^{\alpha-1}+w_{2}\cdot(\alpha)_{n}\cdot\beta^{\alpha}\Big)\quad\text{for any }t\geq 0,

where
(x)_{k}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the ascending factorial (cf., e.g.,
Johnson et al. (2005)
, p. 2); hence condition (
4
) yields the recursive formula

(5)

\displaystyle p_{n}=\frac{1}{n\cdot(\beta+1)}\cdot\frac{w_{1}\cdot n!\cdot(\beta+1)^{\alpha-1}+w_{2}\cdot(\alpha)_{n}\cdot\beta^{\alpha-1}}{w_{1}\cdot(n-1)!\cdot(\beta+1)^{\alpha-1}+w_{2}\cdot(\alpha)_{n-1}\cdot\beta^{\alpha-1}}\cdot p_{n-1}

with initial value

(6)

\displaystyle p_{0}=w_{1}\cdot\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}+w_{2}\cdot\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\Big)^{\alpha}.

In the special case
w_{1}=0
and
w_{2}=1
, conditions (
5
) and (
6
) yield

p_{n}=\frac{\alpha+n-1}{n\cdot(\beta+1)}\cdot p_{n-1}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{0}=\Big(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\Big)^{\alpha},

which is the usual negative binomial recursion (see
Panjer (1981)
, p. 23).

Now take
w_{1}=\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}
,
w_{2}=\frac{1}{\beta+1}
and
\alpha=2
. For this particular case we get

P_{\varTheta}(B)=\int_{B}\frac{\beta^{2}}{\beta+1}\cdot(\theta+1)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot\theta}\,\lambda(d\theta)\quad\text{for any }B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)

implying that the structural parameter is distributed according to the Lindley distribution (cf., e.g.,
Ghitany et al. (2008)
for the definition and its basic properties). Conditions (
5
) and (
6
) give

p_{n}=\frac{\beta+n+2}{(\beta+1)\cdot(\beta+1+n)}\cdot p_{n-1}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{0}=\frac{\beta^{2}\cdot(\beta+2)}{(\beta+1)^{3}}.

The next example presents a general recursive formula for the
{\bf MB}\big(m,z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
) distribution. As a special case, we obtain a well-known recursive formula for the negative hypergeometric distribution (cf., e.g.,
Johnson et al. (2005)
, Section 6.2.2, for the definition and its properties) appearing in
Hesselager (1994)
, Example 3.

Example 2.13
.

Letting
P_{N}={\bf MB}\big(m,z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
), we fix on an arbitrary
n\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Since
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
with
a(\varTheta)=-\frac{z_{2}(\varTheta)}{1-z_{2}(\varTheta)}
and
b(\varTheta)=(m+1)\cdot\frac{z_{2}(\varTheta)}{1-z_{2}(\varTheta)}
(see Table
1
), we can apply Theorem
2.11
to get

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{m-n+1}{n}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n-1}}\bigg[\frac{z_{2}(\varTheta)}{1-z_{2}(\varTheta)}\bigg]\cdot p_{n-1}

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{m-n+1}{n}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n}\cdot\big(1-z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{m-n}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n-1}\cdot\big(1-z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{m-n+1}\big]}\cdot p_{n-1}.

In this case, the initial value is given by

p_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(1-z_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{m}\big].

In particular, let
D=(0,1)
, let
z=\text{id}_{D}
and take
P_{\varTheta}={\bf Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta>0
(cf., e.g.,
Schmidt (2012)
, p. 179, for the definition of the beta distribution). An easy computation yields

\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\varTheta^{n}\cdot(1-\varTheta)^{m-n}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\varTheta^{n-1}\cdot(1-\varTheta)^{m-n+1}]}=\frac{\alpha+n-1}{\beta+m-n},

implying, together with condition (
7
), that

p_{n}=\frac{(m-n+1)\cdot(\alpha+n-1)}{n\cdot(\beta+m-n)}\cdot p_{n-1}

with initial value

p_{0}=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{\beta+k}{\alpha+\beta+k}.

Similarly to the previous example, in the next one we treat the case
P_{N}={\bf MNB}\big(r,\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(
r>0
). As a special instance, a well-known recursion for the generalized Waring distribution (cf., e.g.,
Johnson et al. (2005)
, Section 6.2.3, for the definition and its properties) is rediscovered (see
Hesselager (1994)
, Example 4).

Example 2.14
.

Let
P_{N}={\bf MNB}\big(r,\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
, where
r>0
, and fix on arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
with
a(\varTheta)=1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)
and
b(\varTheta)=(r-1)\cdot\big(1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)
(see Table
1
), apply Theorem
2.11
to get

\displaystyle p_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{r+n-1}{n}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{n-1}}\big[\big(1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)\big]\cdot p_{n-1}

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{r+n-1}{n}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{r}\cdot\big(1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n}\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{r}\cdot\big(1-\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{n-1}\big]}\cdot p_{n-1},

while the initial value is given by condition
p_{0}=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\big(\rho_{2}(\varTheta)\big)^{r}\big]
.

In particular, if
D=(0,1)
,
\rho_{2}=\text{id}_{D}
and
P_{\varTheta}={\bf Beta}(\alpha,\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta>0
, then

\mathbb{E}_{P}[\varTheta^{r}\cdot(1-\varTheta)^{n}]=\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+r,\beta+n)}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}

implying, together with condition (
8
), that

p_{n}=\frac{(r+n-1)\cdot(\beta+n-1)}{n\cdot(\alpha+\beta+r+n-1)}\cdot p_{n-1}

with initial value

p_{0}=\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+r,\beta)}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}.

3.
Mixed compound distributions

Let
X:=\{X_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of random variables on
\varOmega
with values in
R_{X}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, and consider the random variable
S
on
\varOmega
taking values in
R_{S}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, defined by

S:=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}&\text{, if }N\geq 1\\
0&\text{, if }N=0\end{cases}.

The sequence
X
and the random variable
S
are known as the
claim size process
and the
aggregate claims size
, respectively. Recall that a sequence
\{Z_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of random variables on
\varOmega
is:

(i)

P
-
conditionally (stochastically) independent given
\varTheta
, if for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq 2

P\big(Z_{i_{1}}\leq z_{i_{1}},Z_{i_{2}}\leq z_{i_{2}},\ldots,Z_{i_{n}}\leq z_{i_{n}}\mid\varTheta\big)=\prod^{n}_{k=1}P\big(Z_{i_{k}}\leq z_{i_{k}}\mid\varTheta\big)\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\mbox{-a.s.}

whenever
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
are distinct members of
{\mathbb{N}}
and
(z_{i_{1}},\ldots,z_{i_{n}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
;

(ii)

P
-conditionally identically distributed given
\varTheta
, if

P\big(F\cap Z_{k}^{-1}[B]\big)=P\big(F\cap Z_{m}^{-1}[B]\big)

whenever
k,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
F\in\sigma(\varTheta)
and
B\in{\mathfrak{B}}
.

For the rest of the paper, we simply write “conditionally” in the place of “conditionally given
\varTheta
” whenever conditioning refers to
\varTheta
, and, unless otherwise stated,
X
is a sequence of
P
-conditionally i.i.d random variables, and
P
-conditionally mutually independent of
N
.

Denote by
\mu
either the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)
or the counting measure on
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and for any
\theta\in D
let

(P_{\theta})_{X_{1}}(B)=\int_{B}f_{\theta}(x)\,\mu(dx)\quad\text{ for any }B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(R_{X}),

where
f_{\theta}:=\big(f_{\theta}\big)_{X_{1}}
is the probability (density) function of
X_{1}
with respect to
P_{\theta}
. Since
\{P_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in D}
is an rcp of
P
over
P_{\varTheta}
consistent with
\varTheta
, it follows by
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Lemma 3.2, that the latter is equivalent to

P_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(B)=\int_{B}f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(x)\,\mu(dx)\quad P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)\text{-a.s.}.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\varTheta
is non-degenerate and if
\int\mathbb{E}^{2}_{P}[X_{1}\mid\varTheta]\,dP<\infty
, then the random variables
X_{n}
,
X_{m}
with
n\neq m
are
P
-positively correlated.

In fact, for any
n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\neq m
, we get

\textrm{Cov}_{P}(X_{n},X_{m})=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{n}\cdot X_{m}\mid\varTheta]\big]-\mathbb{E}^{2}_{P}[X_{1}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[\mathbb{E}^{2}_{P}[X_{1}\mid\varTheta]\big]-\mathbb{E}^{2}_{P}\big[\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{1}\mid\varTheta]\big]>0,

where the first equality follows by
Tzaninis & Macheras (2023)
, Remark 2.1, while the second one follows since
X
is
P
-conditionally i.i.d.; hence
\textrm{Cov}_{P}(X_{n},X_{m})=\text{Var}_{P}\big(\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{1}\mid\varTheta]\big)>0
.

The next proposition provides an expression for the probability distribution of a sum of
P
-conditionally i.i.d. random variables.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
S_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then

P_{S_{n}}(B)=\int_{B}\Big(\int_{D}f^{\ast n}_{\theta}(x)\,P_{\varTheta}(d\theta)\Big)\,\mu(dx)\quad\text{for all }B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(R_{S}),

where
f_{\theta}^{\ast n}
denotes the
n
-th convolution of
f_{\theta}
with itself.

Proof.

Fix on arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any
B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(R_{S})
and
F\in{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
, the consistency of
\{P_{\theta}\}_{\theta\in D}
with
\varTheta
, along with Lemma
2.5
, yields

P\big(S^{-1}_{n}[B]\cap\varTheta^{-1}[F]\big)=\int_{F}(P_{\theta})_{S_{n}}(B)\,P_{\varTheta}(d\theta).

But since
X
is
P
-conditionally i.i.d., it follows by
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Lemma 3.4(ii), that
X
is
P_{\theta}
-i.i.d. for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
, implying that

(9)

\displaystyle(P_{\theta})_{S_{n}}(B)=\int_{B}f_{\theta}^{\ast n}(x)\,\mu(dx);

hence for
F=D
, we get

P_{S_{n}}(B)=\int_{B}\Big(\int_{D}f^{\ast n}_{\theta}(x)\,P_{\varTheta}(d\theta)\Big)\,\mu(dx)

which completes the proof.
∎

The next example shows how Proposition
3.2
can be utilized in order to compute convolutions in the case of
P
-conditionally i.i.d. random variables.

Example 3.3
.

Let
D:=(0,\infty)
and fix on arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
P_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}={\bf Exp}(\varTheta)

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s. and
P_{\varTheta}={\bf Ga}(\alpha,\beta)
with
\alpha,\beta\in(0,\infty)
. Since by
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Lemma 3.4(ii), the claim size process
X
is
P_{\theta}
-independent and
(P_{\theta})_{X_{1}}={\bf Exp}(\theta)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
, we deduce that
(P_{\theta})_{S_{n}}={\bf Ga}(n,\theta)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
. Now, by applying Proposition
3.2
, we get

P_{S_{n}}(B)=\int_{B}\frac{\beta^{\alpha}\cdot x^{n-1}}{\Gamma(n)\cdot\Gamma(\alpha)}\cdot\Big(\int_{D}\theta^{\alpha+n-1}\cdot e^{-(\beta+x)\cdot\theta}\,\lambda(d\theta)\Big)\,\lambda(dx)

for any
B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)
, implying

P_{S_{n}}(B)=\int_{B}\frac{\beta^{\alpha}}{\textrm{B}(\alpha,n)}\cdot\frac{x^{n-1}}{(\beta+x)^{\alpha+n}}\,\lambda(dx),

i.e.,
S_{n}
is distributed according to the generalized Pareto distribution (cf., e.g.,
Willmot (1993)
, p. 118). Note that since each
X_{n}
is
P
-conditionally exponentially distributed and
\varTheta
is gamma distributed, it follows easily that each
X_{n}
is Pareto distributed with parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
(cf., e.g.,
Schmidt (2012)
, p. 180 for the definition of the Pareto distribution).

The following definition is in accordance with the definition of mixed compound Poisson processes in
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2021)
, p. 780 and Lemma 3.3.

Definition 3.4
.

The aggregate claims distribution
P_{S}
is a
mixed compound distribution
, written
{\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
for brevity, if
P_{N}
is a mixed claim number distribution and the sequence
X
is
P
-conditionally i.i.d. and
P
-conditionally mutually independent of
N
.

In particular, if
P_{\varTheta}=\delta_{\theta_{0}}
for some
\theta_{0}\in D
, then
P_{S}
reduces to a
compound distribution
, written
{\bf C}\big((P_{\theta_{0}})_{N},(P_{\theta_{0}})_{X_{1}}\big)
for short (cf., e.g.,
Schmidt (2012)
, p. 109).

Remark 3.5
.

Assume that
\varTheta
is a positive random variable and let
P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
. If the maps
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
and
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[X_{1}]
have the same (resp. different) monotonicity for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
, then the random variables
N
and
X_{1}
are
P
-positively (resp. negatively) correlated.

In fact, assume first that the maps
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
and
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[X_{1}]
have the same monotonicity for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
. By applying Lemma
2.5
we get that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[N\cdot X_{1}]=\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}\big[\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[X_{1}]\big]
, implying together with
Schmidt (2014)
, Theorem 2.2, and Lemma
2.5
, that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[N\cdot X_{1}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[N]\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{1}]
; hence
N
and
X_{1}
are positively correlated. The proof when the maps
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[N]
and
\theta\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{P_{\theta}}[X_{1}]
have different monotonicity, for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta>0
, is similar.

The following characterization of mixed compound distributions in terms of consistent rcp’s allows us to eliminate conditioning via a suitable change of measures, and to convert a mixed compound distribution into a compound one with respect to the probability measures of the corresponding rcp.

Proposition 3.6
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
;

(ii)

(P_{\theta})_{S}={\bf C}\big((P_{\theta})_{N},(P_{\theta})_{X_{1}}\big)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
.

Proof.

Statement (i) is equivalent to the facts
P_{N\mid\varTheta}={\bf K}(\varTheta)

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s.,
X
is
P
-conditionally i.i.d. and
P
-conditionally mutually independent of
N
. Due to
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4(ii), we equivalently get that
(P_{\theta})_{N}={\bf K}(\theta)
, that
N
and
X
are
P_{\theta}
-mutually independent and that
X
is
P_{\theta}
-i.i.d., respectively, for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
, which is equivalent to (ii).
∎

The characterization appearing in Proposition
3.6
allows the extension of well-known results for compound distributions to their mixed counterpart. In the next result, we present a formula for the probability distribution of
S
in the case
P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
.

Corollary 3.7
.

If
P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
, then

P_{S}(B)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{D}p_{n}(\theta)\cdot\int_{B}f_{\theta}^{\ast n}(x)\,\mu(dx)\,P_{\varTheta}(d\theta)\Big)\quad\text{for any }B\in{\mathfrak{B}}(R_{S})

Proof.

Since
P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
, apply Proposition
3.6
to get
(P_{\theta})_{S}={\bf C}\big((P_{\theta})_{N},(P_{\theta})_{X_{1}}\big)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
; hence by
Schmidt (2012)
, Lemma 5.1.1, we obtain

∎

4.
Recursive formulas for mixed compound distributions

Throughout what follows in this section, unless stated otherwise,
P_{S}={\bf MC}\big(P_{N},P_{X_{1}}\big)
with
P_{N}\in\text{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
and
P_{X_{1}}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})=1
.

Since in the collective risk model the random variable
S
represents the total claim amount paid out from an insurance company over a fixed period of time, the determination of its pmf is of great importance. However, even in the classical case where
X
is
P
-i.i.d. and
N,X
are
P
-mutually independent, it is difficult to obtain a closed form for the pmf of
S
, which usually involves the computation of higher-order convolutions, a task that may be proven difficult, or at least time consuming. As a consequence, actuaries usually resort to recursive expressions, which seem to be more convenient and computationally efficient.

In the next result, we obtain a recursive formula for the pmf of
S
by extending the famous Panjer recursion (cf., e.g.,
Schmidt (2012)
, Theorem 5.4.2) to the class
\text{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
. In order to present it, we denote by
m_{N\mid\varTheta}
the conditional probability generating functions of
N
, i.e.,
m_{N\mid\varTheta}(t)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[t^{N}\mid\varTheta\big]
for any
t
with
|t|\leq 1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a family
\{\rho_{x}\}_{x\in{R}_{S}}
of probability measures on
\varSigma
defined by

\rho_{x}(A):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}\quad\text{for any }A\in\varSigma

being an rcp of
P
over
P_{S}
consistent with
S
, such that

g(x)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[m_{N\mid\varTheta}\big(f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)\big)\big]&\text{, if }x=0\\
\sum_{y=1}^{x}D_{x,y}\cdot g(x-y)&\text{, if }x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}\end{cases},

where

D_{x,y}:=\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x-y}}\bigg[\Big(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot\frac{f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)}{1-a(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)}\bigg]

for any
x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\{1,\ldots,x\}
.

In particular, if
f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)=0

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s. then

g(x)=\begin{cases}p_{0}&\text{, if }x=0\\
\sum_{y=1}^{x}D_{x,y}\cdot g(x-y)&\text{, if }x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}\end{cases},

where

D_{x,y}=\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x-y}}\Big[\Big(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot\ f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)\Big]

for any
x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\{1,\ldots,x\}
.

Proof.

Fix on arbitrary
x\in R_{S}
and define the set-function
\rho_{x}:\varSigma\to(0,\infty)
by

\rho_{x}(A):=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{A}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}\quad\text{for any }A\in\varSigma.

By applying similar arguments to those appearing in the proof of Theorem
2.11
, it follows easily that the family
\{\rho_{y}\}_{y\in{R}_{S}}
is an rcp of
P
over
P_{S}
consistent with
S
. Furthermore, first note that condition
g(0)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[m_{N\mid\varTheta}\big(f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)\big)\big]
is an immediate consequence of Corollary
3.7
for
B=\{0\}
. By
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Lemmas 3.4(ii) and 3.3, the sequence
X
is
P_{\theta}
-i.i.d. and
(N,X)
are
P_{\theta}
-mutually independent for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
, respectively. Since
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
, Proposition
2.7
implies that
(P_{\theta})_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\theta),b(\theta);0)
for
P_{\varTheta}
-a.a.
\theta\in D
; hence we may apply
Schmidt (2012)
, Theorem 5.4.2, to get

g_{\theta}(x)=\frac{1}{1-a(\theta)\cdot f_{\theta}(0)}\cdot\sum_{y=1}^{x}\Big(a(\theta)+\frac{b(\theta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot f_{\theta}(y)\cdot g_{\theta}(x-y),

where
g_{\theta}
denotes the pmf of
S
with respect to
P_{\theta}
. The latter, together with Corollary
3.7
and Lemma
2.5
, yields

(11)

g(x)=\sum_{y=1}^{x}\mathbb{E}_{P}\Big[\Big(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot\frac{f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)}{1-a(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)}\cdot g_{\varTheta}(x-y)\Big].

But since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x}}[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}]=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\varTheta^{-1}[F]}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{S=x\}}]}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{P_{\varTheta}}[\mathbbm{1}_{F}\cdot g_{\theta}(x)]}{g(x)}\quad\text{for each }F\in{\mathfrak{B}}(D),

where the second equality follows by Lemma
2.5
, we easily get that

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{P}\Big[\Big(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot\frac{f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)}{1-a(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)}\cdot g_{\varTheta}(x-y)\Big]\\
&\quad=\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x-y}}\Big[\Big(a(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot\frac{f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)}{1-a(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)}\Big]\cdot g(x-y)\end{split}

for any
y\in\{1,\ldots,x\}
. Conditions (
11
) and (
12
) complete the first part of the proof.

In particular, first note that if
f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)=0

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s., then
m_{N\mid\varTheta}\big(f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)\big)=p_{0}(\varTheta)

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s.. The latter, along with conditions (
11
) and (
12
), completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Assume that
P_{X_{1}}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)
. By applying the characterization appearing on Proposition
3.6
in conjunction with
Panjer (1981)
, Theorem on p. 24, we can easily prove the equality

(13)

\displaystyle g(x)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\big[p_{1}(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(x)\big]+\int_{0}^{x}\mathbb{E}_{P}\bigg[\Big(\alpha(\varTheta)+\frac{b(\varTheta)\cdot y}{x}\Big)\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)\cdot g_{\varTheta}(x-y)\bigg]\,\lambda(dy)

for any
x>0
. The integral equation (
13
) has to be solved numerically, however its implementation presents some serious difficulties (see
Gómez-Déniz et al. (2004)
, p. 42). The proof of a recursion for
g
in the spirit of Theorem
4.1
and its implementation is the subject of a forthcoming paper.

Remark 4.3
.

If
X
and
\varTheta
are
P
-independent, it follows by Lemma
2.5
that
P_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}=P_{X_{1}}

P{\upharpoonright}\sigma(\varTheta)
-a.s.. Hence Theorem
4.1
gives

g(x)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{P}\Big[m_{N\mid\varTheta}\big(f_{X_{1}}(0)\big)\Big]&\text{, if }x=0\\
\sum_{y=1}^{x}{D}_{x,y}\cdot g(x-y)&\text{, if }x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}\end{cases},

where
{D}_{x,y}=\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x-y}}\Big[\frac{x\cdot a(\varTheta)+y\cdot b(\varTheta)}{x\cdot(1-a(\varTheta)\cdot f_{X_{1}}(0))}\Big]\cdot f_{X_{1}}(y)
for any
x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\{1,\ldots,x\}
.

Recall that a sequence
Z:=\{Z_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of random variables on
\varOmega
is called
exchangeable
, if the joint distribution of the sequence is invariant under the permutation of the indices (cf., e.g.,
Fremlin (2013)
, 459C). In non-classical Risk Theory, the concept of exchangeability seems to be an appealing way to introduce a dependence structure between the claim sizes. Actually, exchangeability can be a natural assumption in the risk model context (see
Albrecher et al. (2011)
, Remark 2.7). Note that by
Lyberopoulos & Macheras (2022)
, Proposition 4.1, we get that a sequence
Z
is exchangeable under
P
if and only if there exists a random vector
\widetilde{\varTheta}
such that
Z
is
P
-conditionally i.i.d. over
\sigma(\widetilde{\varTheta})
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\{\rho_{x}\}_{x\in{R}_{S}}
be the family of probability measures constructed in Theorem
4.1
. If
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a,b;0)
and
N
,
\varTheta
are
P
-independent then

g(x)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{P}\Big[m_{N}\big(f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)\big)\Big]&\text{, if }x=0\\
\sum_{y=1}^{x}D_{x,y}\cdot g(x-y)&\text{, if }x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}\end{cases},

where
D_{x,y}=\big(a+b\cdot\frac{y}{x}\big)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\rho_{x-y}}\Big[\frac{f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(y)}{1-a\cdot f_{X_{1}\mid\varTheta}(0)}\Big]
for any
x\in R_{S}\setminus\{0\}
and
y\in\{1,\ldots,x\}
.

The proof follows immediately by Theorem
4.1
and therefore it is omitted.

The following example shows that if
P_{N}\in{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
, then the claim number distribution, for example in the case of an Excess of Loss reinsurance, remains in the same class, but with different parameters (cf., e.g.,
Schmidt (2012)
, p. 108).

Example 4.5
.

Let
h
be a
{\mathfrak{B}}(D)
-
{\mathfrak{B}}(0,\infty)
-measurable function and set
v(\theta):=P_{\theta}(X_{1}>h(\theta))\in(0,1)
for any
\theta\in D
. Fix an arbitrary
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and define the function
z_{n}:\varOmega\times D\rightarrow\{0,1\}
by
z_{n}(\omega,\theta):=\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{n}>h(\theta)\}}(\omega)
for any

. For an arbitrary, but fixed
\theta\in D
, set
z(\theta):=\{z_{k}(\theta)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Define the real-valued function
Z_{n}(\varTheta)
on
\varOmega
by
Z_{n}(\varTheta):=z_{n}\circ(id_{\varOmega}\times\varTheta)
and the sequence
Z(\varTheta):=\{Z_{k}(\varTheta)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Clearly

.

5.
Concluding Remarks

When dealing with inhomogeneous insurance portfolios, mixtures of claim number distributions are frequently used to model the claim counts. In actuarial practice, the usual independence assumptions seem to be unrealistic. In particular, the mutual independence between claims sizes and counts appears to be implausible, especially when considering inhomogeneous portfolios. The above led us to the development of this work.

This paper aims to investigate the mixed counterpart of the original
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;0)
class and the corresponding compound distributions by relaxing the usual independence assumptions in two ways. More specifically, we allow the parameters of the claim number and the claim size distributions to be random, and we assume that the claim size process is conditionally i.i.d. and conditionally mutually independent of the claim counts.

Based on the above assumptions and the fundamental concept of regular conditional probabilities, this work provides a characterization for the members of
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
class, as well as two recursive algorithms for
P_{N}
and
P_{S}
, respectively, where the recursion for
P_{S}
also covers the case of a compound Panjer distribution with exchangeable claims. Our results are accompanied by various numerical examples to highlight the practical relevance of this work.

It is worth mentioning that the members of the
{\bf Panjer}(a(\varTheta),b(\varTheta);0)
class may be of particular interest in non-life insurance problems, for example, when insurance data exhibit overdispersion. Finally, this research allows us to consider some possible extensions of actuarial interest beyond the scopes of this paper, such as the study of the mixed counterpart of
{\bf Panjer}(a,b;k)
for
k\geq 1
and the derivation of De Pril’s recursion for the moments of
S
(see
De Pril (1986)
, Theorem, p. 118).

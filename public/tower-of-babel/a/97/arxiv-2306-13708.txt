Coherent-State Ladder Time-Dependent Variational Principle for Open Quantum Systems
David S. Schlegel, Fabrizio Minganti, Vincenzo Savona

Abstract
We present a new paradigm for the dynamical simulation of interacting many-boson open quantum systems. The method relies on a variational ansatz for the n n -boson density matrix, in terms of a superposition of photon-added coherent states. It is most efficient for the simulation of driven-dissipative systems where the state is well described by quantum fluctuations on top of a displaced field, making it suitable for the simulation of several coupled modes with large occupation numbers, that are otherwise very challenging using a Fock-space expansion. We test our method on several examples, demonstrating its potential application to the predictive simulation of interacting bosonic systems and cat qubits.



Introduction.—

Quantum technologies rely on the generation and control of many-body quantum states on platforms that maintain long-lived quantum coherence

;
Bloch et al. 2012
;

While quantum simulators and computers are expected to eventually outperform classical emulation

;

;
Tacchino et al. 2020
;
Cirac 2021
, the certification and verification of quantum protocols still require efficient approximate methods to simulate entangled many-body dynamics

;

.
This task is especially challenging for the many bosonic degrees of freedom characterizing superconducting circuits

;
Kjaergaard et al. 2020

;
Pelucchi et al. 2022
, optomechanical resonators

In quantum optics, several methods have been developed to model weakly-interacting systems
close to their classical limit, based on the Gross-Pitaevskii equation (GPE)
Gross 1961
;
Pitaevskii 1961
;
Carusotto and Ciuti 2013
or the Gutzwiller mean-field approximation

;
Lee et al. 2013
;
Jin et al. 2016
;
Casteels et al. 2018
;
Huybrechts and Wouters 2020
.
Interactions, however, can induce quantum correlations that cannot be captured by a mean-field approximation, nor by methods such as the truncated Wigner approximation or low-order cumulant expansion
Sánchez-Barquilla et al. 2020
;
Vicentini et al. 2018
.
Similarly, driven bosonic systems can operate in a regime where the state inherits a large degree of coherence from the engineered driving fields. A prototypical example are bosonic quantum codes, where drive and dissipation create and stabilize quantum superpositions of coherent states
Ofek et al. 2016
;
Rosenblum et al. 2018
;
Sivak et al. 2023
;
Joshi et al. 2021
.
The prohibitively large Hilbert space of multimode, driven bosonic systems is often an obstacle to the use of conventional simulation techniques based on Fock-space truncation

;
Reed and Simon 1975
.
Novel methods, such as the
shifted Fock basis

Chamberland et al. 2022
, mitigate this problem when simulating the steady state of the system subject to a stationary driving field.
The efficient numerical simulation of the dynamics of driven-dissipative many-body bosonic systems remains, however, a major challenge.

In this Letter, we model the dynamics of a many-body driven-dissipative bosonic system using the time-dependent variational principle
Dirac 1982
;
Frenkel 1934
;
McLachlan 1964
(TDVP). We introduce an ansatz based on a set of unnormalized coherent states called a
coherent-state ladder
. These states are chosen so to have optimal overlap with the corner of the Hilbert space spanned by the system dynamics.

The method naturally accounts for symmetries, such as rotational symmetries in phase space characterizing, e.g., bosonic cat codes
Mirrahimi et al. 2014
;
Ofek et al. 2016
;
Albert et al. 2018
;
Guillaud and Mirrahimi 2019
;
Puri et al. 2020
;
Chamberland et al. 2022
;
Gautier et al. 2023
and other rotation symmetric bosonic codes
Schlegel et al. 2022
;
Grimsmo et al. 2020
, thus permitting an efficient study of time-dependent gate protocols and the occurrence of errors.
The number of basis states enters as a controllable parameter that sets the accuracy of the approximation. It allows simulating coupled interacting bosonic systems of considerable size requiring only moderate computational resources.

Hereunder, we detail the principle of the method and benchmark its performance by applying it to various models of driven-dissipative bosonic systems. Our method outperforms the Fock-space truncation method in all examples, requiring fewer basis states to represent the system’s dynamics accurately.

Method.—

We consider a bosonic open quantum system coupled to a Markovian bath
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
, whose dynamics is governed by the Lindblad master equation

\derivative{\hat{\rho}}{t}=-i\left[\hat{H},\hat{\rho}\right]+\sum\limits_{j}\gamma_{j}\mathcal{D}\left[\hat{L}_{j}\right](\hat{\rho}).

(1)

Here,
\hat{H}
is the Hamiltonian and
\mathcal{D}[\hat{L}_{j}](\hat{\rho})
are the dissipators, with jump-operators
\hat{L}_{j}
and dissipation rates
\gamma_{j}
, defined by
\mathcal{D}[\hat{A}](\hat{\rho})\equiv\hat{A}\hat{\rho}\hat{A}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}\hat{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\rho}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}
.

To implement the TDVP, we adopt the formalism proposed in Ref.
Joubert-Doriol and Izmaylov 2015
. The ansatz for the density matrix is then

\hat{\rho}=\sum_{i,j}^{N}B_{ij}(t)\outerproduct{\phi_i(t)}{\phi_j(t)},

(2)

where the states
\ket{\phi_{i}(t)}
are not in general orthogonal, and are expressed in terms of variational parameters. The matrix elements
B_{ij}
also enters as additional variational parameters. Once a criterion for determining the states
\ket{\phi_{i}(t)}
is introduced, the number
N
will determine the expressive power of the ansatz and the accuracy of the variational approach.

The parametrized states
\ket{\phi_{i}(t)}
that we introduce are based on the
Bargmann
states
\lVert{\alpha}\rangle

Bargmann 1961
;
Segal 1967
;
Pérez-Esteva and Villegas-Blas 2000
, i.e., non-normalized coherent states defined by

\lVert{\alpha}\rangle=e^{\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}}\ket{0}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket{n}=e^{\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\ket{\alpha},

(3)

with
\ket{n}
being Fock states.
Starting from
\lVert{\alpha}\rangle
, the subsequent application of the ladder operator
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
yields the (non-orthonormal) photon-added coherent-state ladder basis
\mathcal{S}_{N}=\{\lVert{\alpha}\rangle,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle,{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{2}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle\dots{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{N}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle\}

Agarwal and Tara 1991
.
For
\lVert{\alpha=0}\rangle
,
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is just the Fock (number) basis.
For
\alpha\neq 0
,
\mathcal{S}_{N}
describes excitations of the state
\lVert{\alpha}\rangle
.

The states
\mathcal{S}_{N}
bring several advantages in the
simulation of the dynamics.
First, they express efficiently the state of a driven system, which is expected to be given by a coherent state plus small quantum fluctuations.
Furthermore, the Bargmann states obey:

\hat{a}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle=\alpha\lVert{\alpha}\rangle,\quad\partialderivative{\ketb{\alpha}}{\alpha}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle.

(4)

From Eq. (
4
) it follows that the tangent space of
\mathcal{S}_{N}
lies in
\mathcal{S}_{N+1}
. In TDVP, the tangent space contains the gradient function, which dictates the updates of the variational parameters, whose evaluation is therefore greatly simplified. Notice also that Bargmann states are holomorphic functions of the coherent-field amplitude
\alpha
, which further simplifies the TDVP formulation using McLachlan’s principle
Broeckhove et al. 1988
;
Kan 1981
.

As depicted schematically in Fig.
1
, we propose to use the McLachlan’s variational principle
McLachlan 1964
;
Broeckhove et al. 1988
;
Raab and Meyer 2000
on a multi-mode bosonic system, by extending the density matrix ansatz in Eq. (
2
) to
M
bosonic modes. Each mode is expressed by its coherent-field amplitude
\alpha_{k}

Joubert-Doriol and Izmaylov 2015
and by the related coherent-state ladder
\mathcal{S}_{N}^{(k)}=\{\hat{a}_{k}^{n}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle,n=0\dots N\}
, where
k=1,\,\ldots,\,M
.
The TDVP translates then into coupled differential equations for the variational parameters
\boldsymbol{B}=\{B_{i\,j}\}
and
\alpha_{k}
(See Supplemental Material for details)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\dot{B}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1},

(5)

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{(k)}\boldsymbol{B}\}}{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{(k)}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}},

(6)

where the matrices
\boldsymbol{S}
,
\boldsymbol{L}
,
\boldsymbol{C}
,
\boldsymbol{\tau}
,
\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{(k)}
and
\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{(k)}
depend on
\alpha_{k}
,
\dot{\alpha}_{k}
, and the model parameters.

Rotational symmetries.—

Our approach conveniently facilitates the inclusion of rotational symmetries, thereby enabling the modeling of rotation-symmetric quantum codes such as cat codes.
As an example, we consider a
\mathds{Z}_{2}
rotational symmetry whose symmetry subspaces can be decomposed in even and odd superpositions of Bargmann states with opposite displacement, yielding the unnormalized even and odd cat states

\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}}\rangle=\lVert{\alpha}\rangle\pm\lVert{-\alpha}\rangle.

(9)

Similar to the previous case, we can construct a cat-ladder basis comprising the two symmetry sectors by applying
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
on the even and odd cat states in Eq. (
9
).
Note that
\partialderivative{\ketb{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^\pm}}{\alpha}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}}\rangle

Equations (
5
) and (
6

A

Conclusions.—

We have introduced an approach for simulating the dynamics of interacting many-boson driven-dissipative systems. The coherent-state ladder approach employs a variational ansatz for the
n
-boson density matrix, where both the matrix elements and the displacement fields serve as time-dependent variational parameters. This approach bridges the gap between quantum and classical realms, making it suitable for simulating systems with large occupation numbers that are beyond the capabilities of Fock-space methods.

We have demonstrated the potential of the method through several examples, showcasing its applicability to interacting bosonic systems and cat qubits. It outperforms Fock-space methods in terms of the number of basis states needed to accurately represent the system’s dynamics.

Our method can straightforwardly be extended to include different parametrizations of the basis, such as squeezed states or higher-order processes, as well as other types of symmetries (See Supplemental Material for details).
It can thus accurately study the system dynamics of multi-mode bosonic systems in the presence of quantum correlations.

The coherent-state ladder thus offers new possibilities for accurately studying system dynamics of a wide range of single- and multi-mode bosonic systems, whose simulation would be otherwise very challenging. It opens new pathways in the simulation of systems of many coupled bosonic qubits to efficiently optimize gate and error-correction protocols
Guillaud and Mirrahimi 2019
;
Puri et al. 2020
;
Chamberland et al. 2022
;
Gautier et al. 2023
.

Derivation of the TDVP equations of motion

In the McLachlan TDVP for density matrices, the equations of motion arise from the minimization of the Frobenius norm of the error
McLachlan 1964
;
Broeckhove et al. 1988

\left\|\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}]\right\|=\text{Tr}\{(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])^{\dagger}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(12)

which is equivalent to the stationary condition
Raab and Meyer 2000

\text{Tr}\{\delta\hat{\rho}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\}=0,

(13)

with an infinitesimal variation in the density matrix
\delta\hat{\rho}
.
For our particular choice of ansatz in Eq. (
2
), infinitesimal changes in the density matrix
\hat{\rho}
translate to the variational paramters as

\delta\hat{\rho}=\sum\limits_{ij}\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{B_{ij}}\delta B_{ij}+\sum\limits_{k}\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k}\delta\alpha_{k}+\sum\limits_{k}\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}\delta\alpha_{k}^{*}.

(14)

Inserting the above expression in Eq. (
13
), and due to the independent nature of the derivatives, we have three stationary conditions:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{B_{ij}}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

\displaystyle=0

(15)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

\displaystyle=0

(16)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

\displaystyle=0

(17)

Since the last two are complex conjugates of one another, they constitute the same condition.
We define the overlap matrix

[\boldsymbol{S}]_{ij}=\innerproduct{\phi_i}{\phi_j}.

(18)

As the coherent-state- and cat-ladder basis states are not orthogonal,
\boldsymbol{S}
is not an orthogonal matrix.
Note that due to this non-orthonormal basis, the projector
\hat{P}
, projecting onto the ansatz basis
\mathcal{S}
, is given by

\hat{P}=\sum\limits_{ij}\ket{\phi_i}[\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}]_{ij}\bra{\phi_j}.

(19)

We can rewrite Eq. (
14
) as

\derivative{\hat{\rho}}{t}=\sum\limits_{cd}\dot{B}_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\phi_d}+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\dot{\phi_c}}{\phi_d}+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\dot{\phi_d}}

(20)

Replacing this expression into Eq. (
15
) yields:

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{B_{ij}}\left(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}]\right)\right\}=0

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{kl}\bra{\phi_l}\boldsymbol{S}_{kl}^{-1}\left[\outerproduct{\phi_i}{\phi_j}\left(\sum\limits_{cd}\dot{B}_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\phi_d}\right.\right.

(22)

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\left.+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\dot{\phi_c}}{\phi_d}+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\dot{\phi_d}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}]\right)\right]\ket{\phi_k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{cd}S_{jc}\dot{B}_{cd}S_{di}+\tau_{jc}B_{cd}S_{di}+S_{jc}B_{cd}\tau_{id}^{*}-L_{ji},

(23)

where we have introduced

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\tau}]_{mn}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k}\innerproduct{\phi_m}{\pdv{\phi_n}{\alpha_k}}\dot{\alpha_{k}},

(24)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{L}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\matrixelement{\phi_i}{\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}]}{\phi_j}.

(25)

We thus have the condition

\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{\dot{B}}\boldsymbol{S}+\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}+\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\dagger}-\boldsymbol{L}=0,

(26)

which gives the equation of motion for the density matrix coefficients,

\boldsymbol{\dot{B}}=\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}.

(27)

For the equation of motion for
\dot{\alpha}_{k}
, we have by inserting the definition of
\dot{\hat{\rho}}
from Eq. (
20
),

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}=0

(28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum\limits_{ab}\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_b}\left\{\sum\limits_{cd}\left(\dot{B}_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\phi_d}\right.\right.\\
&\qquad+\left.\left.B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\dot{\phi_c}}{\phi_d}+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\dot{\phi_d}}\right)-\mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]\right\}\ket{\phi_a}B_{ab}.\end{split}

(29)

Inserting the definition of
\dot{\boldsymbol{B}}
from Eq. (
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

(30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum\limits_{ab}\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_b}\Big\{\sum\limits_{cd}\big([\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}]_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\phi_d}\\
&\qquad+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\dot{\phi_c}}{\phi_d}+B_{cd}\outerproduct*{\phi_c}{\dot{\phi_d}}\big)-\mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]\Big\}\ket{\phi_a}B_{ab}\end{split}

(31)

By using the definition of the projector
\hat{P}
in Eq. (
19
), we have

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum\limits_{ab}\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_b}\Big\{\sum\limits_{cd}\big(\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}\big)\ket*{\dot{\phi}_c}B_{cd}S_{da}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\big(\hat{P}-\hat{\mathds{1}}\big)\mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]\ket{\phi_a}\Big\}B_{ab}\end{split}

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\sum\limits_{abcl}\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_b}[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}]\ket*{\partial_{\alpha_l}\phi_c}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]_{cb}\dot{\alpha}_{l}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_b}[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}]\mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]\ket{\phi_a}B_{ab}\end{split},

(34)

where in the last step we have inserted the definition of
\ket*{\dot{\phi}_c}
.
Importantly, since
\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_m}[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}]\ket*{\partial_{\alpha_l}\phi_n}=0
for
k\neq l
, as a variation of
\alpha_{k}
only affects states in mode
k
, we can drop the sum over
l
in Eq. (
34
).
Defining

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{C_{0}}]_{mn}^{k}

\displaystyle=\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_m}[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}]\ket*{\partial_{\alpha_k}\phi_n},

(35)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}]_{mn}^{k}

\displaystyle=\bra{\partial_{\alpha_k} \phi_m}[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\hat{P}]\mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]\ket{\phi_n},

(36)

we thus obtain

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\left\{{\partialderivative{\hat{\rho}}{\alpha_k^*}}(\dot{\hat{\rho}}-\mathcal{L}[\hat{\rho}])\right\}

(37)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{abc}\boldsymbol{C_{0}}_{bc}^{k}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]_{cb}\dot{\alpha}_{k}-\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}_{ba}^{k}\boldsymbol{B}_{ab}

(38)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{k}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}\dot{\alpha}_{k}-\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{k}\boldsymbol{B}\}.

(39)

Finally, the equation of motion for
\dot{\alpha}_{k}
reads:

\dot{\alpha}_{k}=\frac{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{k}\boldsymbol{B}\}}{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{k}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}}.

(40)

We hence have an implicit ordinary differential equation defined by Eqs. (
5
), (
6
), reading

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\dot{B}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{B}-\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1},

(41)

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{(k)}\boldsymbol{B}\}}{\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{(k)}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}}.

(42)

II
Operator decomposition

We now detail the decomposition of operators and the construction of the overlap matrix for the coherent-state ladder basis and the cat-ladder basis.

II.1
Coherent-state ladder

For the coherent-state ladder with
\mathcal{S}=\{\lVert{\alpha,n}\rangle={{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle,n=0\dots N\}
, we can represent combinations of creation and annihilation operators as:

\begin{split}\hat{a}\lVert{\alpha,n}\rangle&=\hat{a}{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}\lVert{\alpha}\rangle=({{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}\hat{a}+n{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n-1})\lVert{\alpha}\rangle\\
&=\alpha\lVert{\alpha,n}\rangle+n\lVert{\alpha,n-1}\rangle.\end{split}

(43)

Hence with the overlap matrix
\boldsymbol{S}
with elements
S_{mn}=\langle\alpha,m\rVert\alpha,n\rangle
, we have

\langle{\alpha,m}\rVert\hat{a}\lVert{\alpha,n}\rangle=\alpha S_{m,n}+nS_{m,n-1}.

(44)

Representations of other operators are obtained similarly.

We have the following recursion relation for the overlap matrix
\boldsymbol{S}
:

\displaystyle S_{mn}=

\displaystyle\langle{\alpha,m-1}\rVert\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\alpha,n-1}\rangle

(45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&S_{m-1,n-1}+(m-1)(n-1)S_{m-2,n-2}\\
&+|\alpha|^{2}S_{m-1,n-1}+\alpha(m-1)S_{m-2,n-1}\\
&+\alpha^{*}(n-1)S_{m-1,n-2}.\end{split}

(46)

This allows to recursively calculate the matrix elements
S_{mn}
.

II.2
Cat ladder

In the non-orthonormal basis of Bargmann cat states, we can decompose the annihilation operator
\hat{a}
in the subsystem decomposition by calculating its action on a basis state:

\hat{a}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\pm}}\rangle=\alpha\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\mp}}\rangle+n\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n-1}^{\mp}}\rangle,

(47)

and hence in a modular subsystem decomposition
Pantaleoni et al. 2020
the annihilation operator reads

\hat{a}=\hat{X}\otimes(\tilde{b}+\alpha).

(48)

With the inner product matrix

S_{mn}^{\mu\nu}=\langle{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,m}^{\mu}}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\nu}}\rangle,

(49)

where indices
\mu,\;\nu\in\{+,-\}
label the parity of the state, we have

\langle{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,m}^{\mu}}\rVert\hat{a}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\nu}}\rangle=\alpha S_{mn}^{\mu,\bar{\nu}}+nS_{m,n-1}^{\mu,\bar{\nu}},

(50)

where
\bar{\nu}
indicates a flip of parity index
\nu
.
Analogous, we can obtain expressions for the representation of other operators in this basis.

In the cat-ladder basis, the recursion relation to calculate the inner product matrix elements
S_{mn}^{\mu\nu}
is analogous to Eqs. (
45
),(
46
):

\displaystyle S_{mn}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,m-1}^{\bar{\mu}}}\rVert\hat{a}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n-1}^{\bar{\nu}}}\rangle

(51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&S_{m-1,n-1}^{\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}}+(m-1)(n-1)S_{m-2,n-2}^{\mu\nu}\\
&+|\alpha|^{2}S_{m-1,n-1}^{\mu\nu}+\alpha(m-1)S_{m-2,n-1}^{\mu\nu}\\
&+\alpha^{*}(n-1)S_{m-1,n-2}^{\mu\nu}.\end{split}

(52)

With the overlap matrix for the cat-qubit

S_{00}^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}4\cosh{\left(|\alpha|^2\right)}&0\\
0&4\sinh{\left(|\alpha|^2\right)}\end{pmatrix},

(53)

S_{mn}^{\mu\nu}
can be recursively computed for
m,n>0
.

III
Numerical implementation

We here sketch a numerical implementation of the method presented in this paper.
We first consider a single bosonic mode in the cat-ladder basis.
Since calculating a first-order derivative with respect to
\alpha
directly corresponds to the application of
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
and parity conjugation in the chosen basis, we can easily relate derivatives to block shifts in the inner product matrix:

\langle{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,m}^{\mu}}\lVert{\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\nu}}\rangle=\langle{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,m}^{\mu}}\rVert\hat{a}^{\dagger}\lVert{\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,n}^{\bar{\nu}}}\rangle=S_{m,n+1}^{\mu,\nu}.

(54)

We can then calculate the matrices
\mathbf{C}_{0}
and
\mathbf{Y}_{0}
, defined by Eqs. (
35
), (
36
) in terms of block-shifted matrices.
By denoting block-shifts of matrices in the form

[\boldsymbol{A}^{(l)}]_{mn}^{\mu\nu}=[\boldsymbol{A}]_{m+1,n}^{\mu\nu}
,
[\boldsymbol{A}^{(r)}]_{mn}^{\mu\nu}=[\boldsymbol{A}]_{m,n+1}^{\mu\nu}
, and
[\boldsymbol{A}^{(l,r)}]_{mn}^{\mu\nu}=[\boldsymbol{A}]_{m+1,n+1}^{\mu\nu}
for any Matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
with elements
[\boldsymbol{A}]_{mn}^{\mu\nu}
, we can rewrite
\boldsymbol{C_{0}}
and
\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{0}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{S}^{(l,r)}-\boldsymbol{S}^{(l)}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}^{(r)}

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\left(\mathbf{A}^{(l)}_{p}\mathbf{BD}_{p}-\mathbf{S}^{(l)}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{BD}_{p}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}^{(l)}-\mathbf{S}^{(l)}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{L}.

(57)

Here we have expanded the Liouvillian matrix
\boldsymbol{L}
in its operator-sum representation,
\boldsymbol{L}=\sum_{p}\boldsymbol{A}_{p}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{D}_{p}
, with left and right Kraus operator matrices
\boldsymbol{A}_{p}
and
\boldsymbol{D}_{p}
.

Note that matrix
\mathbf{C}_{0}
, which can be interpreted as a quantum-geometric tensor, is not full rank, but only has two diagonal non-zero elements associated with the highest moment of
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
included in the basis (one for each parity sector).
Therefore, for any initial density matrix that has no population for the highest moment included in the basis
\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}
will be zero.
As a result
\dot{\alpha}
will be zero until the density matrix
\boldsymbol{B}
populates the highest moment included in the basis ansatz.
Intuitively, this can be understood in the following way: For our time-dependent variational ansatz, the equations of motion describe a dynamics in which the density matrix
\boldsymbol{B}
evolves into a full-rank matrix. Upon reaching the corner (i.e. the highest moment of
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
included in the basis), the basis re-centers according to the evolution of
\alpha
.
In the numerical simulations, we therefore evolve
\alpha
only if
|\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\}|>\epsilon
, with a small threshold
\epsilon
, otherwise we set
\dot{\alpha}=0
and evolve only
\boldsymbol{B}
.

For multiple bosonic modes, the numerical implementation can be straightforwardly extended. We first note that due to the choice of the basis, the inner product matrix
\boldsymbol{S}
can be expressed as a tensor product of inner product matrices of each bosonic mode, i.e.
\boldsymbol{S}=\otimes_{k}\boldsymbol{S}_{k}
, also reducing the computation of the inverse of
\boldsymbol{S}
to the inverses of
\boldsymbol{S}_{k}
.
Using block-shifts of matrices for mode
k
, we can express left and right derivatives of matrices with respect to
\alpha_{k}
.
Analogous to Eqs. (
55
), (
57
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{0}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{S}^{(l_{k},r_{k})}-\boldsymbol{S}^{(l_{k})}\boldsymbol{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{S}^{(r_{k})}

(58)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{0}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\left(\mathbf{A}^{(l_{k})}_{p}\mathbf{BD}_{p}-\mathbf{S}^{(l_{k})}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{p}\mathbf{BD}_{p}\right)

(59)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{L}^{(l_{k})}-\mathbf{S}^{(l_{k})}\mathbf{S}^{-1}\mathbf{L},

(60)

where here the indices
l_{k}
and
r_{k}
indicate block-shifts associated to mode
k
.

The numerical implementation for the coherent-state ladder basis can be straightforwardly obtained by removing the parity indices
\mu
,
\nu
.

IV
Parametrizing the basis using higher-order processes

In addition to the parametrization of the basis by coherent-state amplitude
\alpha
alone, we can include higher-order processes, such as squeezing and other higher-order processes. For a single bosonic mode, we define the following un-normalized state including up to
K
-photon moments,

\lVert{\alpha^{(1)},\dots,\alpha^{(K)}}\rangle\equiv\prod\limits_{n=1}^{K}e^{\alpha^{(n)}{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}}\ket{0}.

(61)

We can naturally relate derivatives of the parameters
\alpha^{(n)}
to the application of
{{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}
:

\partialderivative{\alpha^{(n)}}\lVert{\alpha^{(1)},\dots,\alpha^{(K)}}\rangle={{}\hat{a}^{\dagger}}^{n}\lVert{\alpha^{(1)},\dots,\alpha^{(K)}}\rangle.

(62)

For a single bosonic mode, the equation of motion for
\dot{\alpha}^{(n)}
then becomes

\dot{\alpha}^{(n)}=[\boldsymbol{C^{-1}}\boldsymbol{Y}]_{n},

(63)

with

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{C}]_{kl}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{(k,l)}[\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{B}]\},

(64)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\{\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{(k)}\boldsymbol{B}\},

(65)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{C_{0}}^{(k,l)}]_{mn}

\displaystyle=\matrixelement{\pdv{\phi_m}{\alpha^{(k)}}}{
[\hat{\mathds{1}} - \hat{P} ]}{\pdv{\phi_n}{\alpha^{(l)}}}

(66)

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{Y_{0}}^{(k)}]_{mn}

\displaystyle=\matrixelement{\pdv{\phi_m}{\alpha^{(k)}}}{[\hat{\mathds{1}} - \hat{P}] \mathcal{{L}}[\hat{\rho}]}{\phi_n}.

(67)

A finite dimensional proof of a result of Hutchings about irrational pseudo-rotations
Patrice Le Calvez

Abstract
. We prove that the Calabi invariant of a C 1 C^{1} pseudo-rotation of the unit disk, that coincides with a rotation on the unit circle, is equal to its rotation number. This result has been shown some years ago by Michael Hutchings (under very slightly stronger hypothesis). While the original proof used Embedded Contact Homology techniques, the proof of this article uses generating functions and the dynamics of the induced gradient flow.



Keywords:
Irrational pseudo-rotation, Calabi invariant, generating function, rotation number, linking number

2.
Radial foliations and Calabi invariant

We will introduce a formal setting to study radial foliations on a disk, based on a “discretization of the angles”, that seems pertinent for studying topological foliations. Classical objects related to disk homeomorphisms, like rotation numbers or linking numbers, will be investigated in this formalism. One can look at Bechara
[
Be
]
for similar questions in a differentiable context.

2.1.
The space of radial foliation

Let
D
be an open disk of the form
D=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,|z|<r\}
, where
r>0
. We will set
D^{*}=D\setminus\{0\}
and will denote
\tilde{D}
the universal covering space of
D^{*}
. We will consider the sets

W=\{(z,z^{\prime})\in D^{*}\times D^{*}\,|\,z\not=z^{\prime}\}

and

\tilde{W}=\{(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{D}\times\tilde{D}\,|\,\tilde{z}\not=\tilde{z}^{\prime}\}.

A
radial foliation
is an oriented topological foliation on
D^{*}
such that every leaf
\phi
is a
ray
, meaning that it satisfies

\alpha(\phi)=\{0\},\enskip\omega(\phi)\subset\partial D=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,|z|=r\}.

In other words,
\phi
tends to
0
in the past and to
\partial D
in the future. We denote
{\mathfrak{F}}
the set of radial foliations. The group
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
of homeomorphisms of
D^{*}
that are isotopic to the identity, acts naturally on
{\mathfrak{F}}
: if
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
, then the foliation
f(\mathcal{F})
, whose leaves are the images by
f
of the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, is a radial foliation. Moreover
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
acts transitively on
\mathfrak{F}
. If
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
is endowed with the
C^{0}
topology, meaning the compact-open topology applied to maps and the inverse maps, the stabilizer
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})
of
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
is a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
. Consequently, the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})/\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})

\displaystyle\to{\mathfrak{F}}

\displaystyle f\,\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})

\displaystyle\mapsto f(\mathcal{F})

is bijective and
{\mathfrak{F}}
can be furnished with a natural
C^{0}
topology. It is the topology, induced from the quotient topology defined on
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})/\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})
by this identification map. Note that this topology does not depend on
\mathcal{F}
.
It is well known that the fondamental groups of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
and
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})
are infinite cyclic and that the morphism
i_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*}),\mathrm{Id})\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*}),\mathrm{Id})
induced by the inclusion map
i:\mathrm{Homeo}_{*,\mathcal{F}}(D^{*})\to\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
is bijective. It implies that
{\mathfrak{F}}
is simply connected.
In the whole article, when
\mathcal{F}
is a radial foliation, the notation
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
means the lift of
\mathcal{F}
to the universal covering space
\tilde{D}
. Note that
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is a trivial foliation. In particular, every leaf
\tilde{\phi}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is an oriented line of
\tilde{D}
that separates
\tilde{D}
into two connected open sets, the component of
\tilde{D}\setminus\tilde{\phi}
lying on the left of
\tilde{\phi}
will be denoted
L(\tilde{\phi})
and the component lying on the right will be denoted
R(\tilde{\phi})
. We get a total order
\preceq_{\mathcal{F}}
on the set of leaves of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
as follows:

\tilde{\phi}\preceq_{\mathcal{F}}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow R(\tilde{\phi})\subset R(\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}).

We can also define a partial order
\leq_{{\mathcal{F}}}
on
\tilde{D}
: denote
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
the leaf of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
that contains
\tilde{z}
and write
\tilde{z}<_{{\mathcal{F}}}\tilde{z}^{\prime}
if
\tilde{z}\not=\tilde{z}^{\prime}
, if
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}=\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
and if the segment of
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
beginning at
\tilde{z}
and ending at
\tilde{z}^{\prime}
inherits the orientation of
\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}
.

2.2.
Topological angles in the universal covering space

Consider the natural projection

\displaystyle\pi:\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\mapsto\dot{k}=k+4\mathbb{Z}.

If
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
is endowed with the topology whose open sets are

\emptyset,\{\dot{1}\},\{\dot{3}\},\{\dot{1},\dot{3}\},\{\dot{1},\dot{2},\dot{3}\},\{\dot{3},\dot{0},\dot{1}\},\{\dot{0},\dot{1},\dot{2},\,\dot{3}\},

and
\mathbb{Z}
with the topology generated
by the sets
2k+1
and
\{2k+1,2k,2k+1\}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
\pi
is a covering map
3
3

3

This topology on
\mathbb{Z}
is usually called the
digital line topology
on
\mathbb{Z}
. Note that both sets

\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}
are non Hausdorff but path connected.

If
k
,
l
are two integers, we will define

\lambda(k,l)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $k=l$,}\\
\#(k,l)\cap 4\mathbb{Z}+(\#\{k,l\}\cap 4\mathbb{Z})/2&\text{ if $k<l$,}\\
-\#(l,k)\cap 4\mathbb{Z}-(\#\{l,k\}\cap 4\mathbb{Z})/2&\text{ if $k>l$.}\end{cases}

Note that for every integers
k,l,m
we have

\lambda(k,l)+\lambda(l,m)=\lambda(k,m).

The quantity
\lambda(k,l)
measures the “algebraic intersection number” of a continuous path
\gamma:[s_{0},s_{1}]\to\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
with
\{\dot{0}\}
, if
\gamma
is lifted to a path
\hat{\gamma}:[s_{0},s_{1}]\to\mathbb{Z}
joining
k
to
l
.

For every
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
, we can define a continuous function
\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}:{\mathfrak{F}}\to\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
as follows:

\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\begin{cases}\dot{0}&\text{ if $\tilde{z}^{\prime}>_{\mathcal{F}}\tilde{z}$,}\\
\dot{1}&\text{ if $\phi_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\succ_{\mathcal{F}}\phi_{\tilde{z}}$,}\\
\dot{2}&\text{ if $\tilde{z}^{\prime}<_{\mathcal{F}}\tilde{z}^{\prime}$,}\\
\dot{3}&\text{ if $\phi_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\prec_{\mathcal{F}}\phi_{\tilde{z}}$.}\end{cases}

The space
\mathfrak{F}
being simply connected, the Lifting Theorem asserts that there exists a continuous function,

\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}:{\mathfrak{F}}\to\mathbb{Z}
, uniquely defined up to an additive constant in
4\mathbb{Z}
, such that
\pi\circ\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}=\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}.

Note that

•

\theta_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}(\mathcal{F})=\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})+\dot{2}
, for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
,

•

\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
and
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}
can be chosen such that
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}(\mathcal{F})=\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})+2
for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
,

•

\theta_{\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}(f(\mathcal{F}))=\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
, for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
and every
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
,

•

\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}
and
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
can be chosen such that
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}(f(\mathcal{F}))=\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
, for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}
are two radial foliations, the numbers

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})

and

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\lambda(\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}),\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))

do not depend on the choice of the lift
\hat{\theta}
.

Suppose that
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
, that
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}({D^{*}})
, that
\tilde{f}
is a lift of
f
and that
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
belong to
\mathfrak{F}
. The following results are immediate:

•

|\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|\leq|\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|
,

•

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})+\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})=\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
,

•

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})+\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})=\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
,

•

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})=-\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})=-\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),f(\mathcal{F}),f(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

\lambda(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),f(\mathcal{F}),f(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.

The second assertion indicates that
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\mapsto\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is symmetric. The next result precises the lack of symmetry of
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\mapsto\lambda(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
. For every
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
, define a function
\delta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}:{\mathfrak{F}}\to\{-1/2,0,1/2\}
as follows:

\delta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}=\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}$,}\\
1/2&\text{ if $\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\succ_{\mathcal{F}}\phi_{\tilde{z}}$,}\\
-1/2&\text{ if $\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\prec_{\mathcal{F}}\phi_{\tilde{z}}$.}\end{cases}

The next result express that
\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\lambda(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is the “algebraic intersection number” of a “well-oriented” continuous path
\gamma:[s_{0},s_{1}]\to\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
joining
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}(\mathcal{F})
to
\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
with
\{\dot{0}\}-\{\dot{2}\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
and every
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{F}
, it holds that

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\lambda(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\delta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\delta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

Recall that

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\lambda(\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}),\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))

and that one can suppose that

\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}(\mathcal{F})=\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})+2,\enskip\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})+2.

Now observe that

\lambda(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\lambda(\tilde{z}^{\prime},\tilde{z},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})$,}\\
0&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{0}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{2}$,}\\
0&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{2}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{0}$,}\\
-1&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{1}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{3}$,}\\
1&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{3}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{1}$,}\\
-1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{1}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{0}$,}\\
1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{0}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{1}$,}\\
-1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{0}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{3}$,}\\
1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{3}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{0}$,}\\
-1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{1}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{2}$,}\\
1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{2}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{1}$,}\\
-1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{2}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{3}$,}\\
1/2&\text{ if $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{3}$ and $\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\dot{2}$.}\end{cases}

The next result will be useful later:

Lemma 2.2
.

For every compact set
\tilde{K}\subset\tilde{W}
and every
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{F}
, there exists
M>0
such that for every
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{K}
it holds that

|\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|\leq M.

Proof.

For every
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
, there exists a neighborhood
\tilde{d}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}\subset\tilde{W}
of
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})
where
\tilde{d}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
and
\tilde{d}^{\prime}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}
are topological closed disks. One can cover
\tilde{K}
by a finite family of such neighborhoods. So it is sufficient to prove the result when
\tilde{K}=\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}
is the product of two topological closed disks.
The map

\displaystyle\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}\times{\mathfrak{F}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\to\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})

being continuous and the space
\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}\times{\mathfrak{F}}
being simply connected, one can lift this last map to a continuous map

\displaystyle\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}\times{\mathfrak{F}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\to\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}).

For every
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the function

(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\mapsto\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})-\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})=\tau(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is continuous and so is bounded on the compact set
\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}
. This last affirmation comes from the fact that for every
l\geq 1
, the set

\left\{(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}\,|\enskip|\tau(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|<2l\right\}
is an open subset of
\tilde{d}\times\tilde{d}^{\prime}
as the preimage of an open set by a continuous map.

Let us conclude with the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

The function
d:{\mathfrak{F}}\times{\mathfrak{F}}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
is an extended distance, where

d(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\sup_{(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}}|\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})|.

Moreover the equality
d(f(\mathcal{F}),f(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=d(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
holds for every
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}({D^{*}})
.

Proof.

One proves immediately that
d
is symmetric and satisfies the triangular inequality. The fact that
d(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})=0
for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
is also obvious. It remains to prove that
d(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\not=0
if
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are distinct radial foliations. In that case, one can find
(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime})\in\tilde{W}
such that
\tilde{z}
and
\tilde{z}^{\prime}
belong to the same leaf of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
and to different leaves of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
. Consequently,
\theta(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})\in\{\dot{0},\dot{2}\}
and
\theta(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\in\{\dot{1},\dot{3}\}
. It implies that

\hat{\theta}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})\not=\hat{\theta}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
if
\hat{\theta}
is a lift of
\theta
.

The equality
d(\tilde{f}(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{f}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=d(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
if
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}({D^{*}})
is a direct consequence of the equality

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),f(\mathcal{F}),f(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))=\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}).

We will call
d
the
winding distance
between two radial foliations.

2.3.
Topological angles in the annulus

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a radial foliation and
f
a homeomorphism of
D^{*}
isotopic to the identity. Let
I=(f_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
be an identity isotopy of
f
in
\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}(D^{*})
and
\tilde{I}=(\tilde{f}_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
the lifted identity isotopy to
\tilde{D}
.
The function

s\in[0,1]\mapsto\theta_{\tilde{f}_{s}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}_{s}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}(\mathcal{F})=\theta_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(f_{s}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))\in\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}

can be lifted to a function

s\in[0,1]\mapsto\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}}(f_{s}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))\in\mathbb{Z}

and the difference between the value at
1
and the value at
0
of this last map is nothing but
\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},\tilde{z},{\mathcal{F}},f^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))
. Of course it depends neither on the choice of
I
, nor on the choice of the lift
\tilde{I}
of
I
. Suppose now that
\tilde{z}
and
\tilde{z}^{\prime}
project onto two different points of
D^{*}
. Note that if
k
is large enough, then for every
s\in[0,1]
, it holds that

\theta_{\tilde{f}_{s}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}_{s}(T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}))}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{1},\enskip\theta_{\tilde{f}_{s}(\tilde{z}),\tilde{f}_{s}(T^{-k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}))}(\mathcal{F})=\dot{3},

and so the functions

s\mapsto\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}(f_{s}^{-1}(\mathcal{F})),\enskip s\mapsto\hat{\theta}_{\tilde{z},T^{-k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})}(f_{s}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}))

are constant, which means that

\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),\mathcal{F},f^{-1}({\mathcal{F}}))=\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},T^{-k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),\mathcal{F},f^{-1}({\mathcal{F}}))=0.

Consequently, if
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
are two radial foliations, then for every
(z,z^{\prime})\in W
, one can define

\displaystyle\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})|,

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\tau}(\tilde{z},T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\lambda(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda(\tilde{z},T^{k}(\tilde{z}^{\prime}),\mathcal{F},{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}),

each sum being independent of the choice of the lifts
\tilde{z},\tilde{z}^{\prime}
of
z,z^{\prime}
.

Suppose that
(z,z^{\prime})\in W
, that
f\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{*}({D^{*}})
and that
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
belong to
\mathfrak{F}
. The following results are immediate

•

|\hat{\tau}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})|\leq\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

|\lambda(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})|\leq\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
,

•

\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z^{\prime},z,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})=\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z,z^{\prime},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})

•

K

the maps

Proof of Theorem

Let

be an irrational pseudo-rotation that coincides with a rotation on

\mathrm{Cal}(f)=0

3

and we want to prove that

3
, keeping the same notations. If

. It is easy to see that
\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I_{|D_{a/b}})
is well-defined, if one refers to the third definition given in the introduction. It can be explicitly computed, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I_{|D_{a/b}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I)+2\int_{1}^{1+a/b-\alpha}(\pi r^{2})(\alpha+r-1)2\pi r\,dr

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I)+4\pi^{2}\int_{1}^{1+a/b-\alpha}r^{3}(\alpha+r-1)dr.

Of course it holds that

\lim_{{a/b}\to\alpha}\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I_{|D_{a/b}})=\widetilde{\mathrm{Cal}}(I_{|\mathbb{D}}).

We have constructed a radial foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
\Delta_{a}^{*}\subset E_{b}
. We define
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}=q_{1}(\mathcal{F})
and denote
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
the lift of
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
to
\tilde{D}_{a/b}

W_{a/b}=\{(z,z^{\prime})\in D_{a/b}^{*}\,|\,z\not=z^{\prime}\}

Lemma 4.1
.

The following bound holds

\mu_{\omega}\times\mu_{\omega}\left(\{(z,z^{\prime})\in W_{a/b}\,|\,\overline{\hat{\tau}}(z,z^{\prime},{\mathcal{F}}_{1},f^{-b}({\mathcal{F}}_{1}))\not=0\}\right)\leq 2\,A(a,b)\,C(a,b).

\tilde{z}^{\prime}\in\tilde{O}(\tilde{z})\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{\check{I}}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})\cap\tilde{\phi}_{\tilde{z}}\not=\emptyset,\enskip z^{\prime}\in O(z)\Longleftrightarrow\check{I}(z^{\prime})\cap\phi_{z}\not=\emptyset.

Note that

and
\tilde{\check{I}}(\tilde{z}^{\prime})

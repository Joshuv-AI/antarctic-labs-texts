Abstract.
This paper is concerned with the global-in-time convergence from bipolar Euler–Poisson system (BEP) to unipolar one (UEP) through the infinity-ion-mass limit by letting the ratio of the mass of ion
m_{i}
over that of electron
m_{e}
goes to infinity.
The global convergence of the limit is obtained for smooth solutions sufficiently close to constant equilibrium states. Furthermore, by applying the stream function method and taking advantage of the anti-symmetric structure of the error system, one obtains the corresponding global-in-time error estimates between smooth solutions of (BEP) and (UEP). It is worth mentioning that due to the strong coupling through the Poisson equation in bipolar system, stream functions for ions and electrons equations should be constructed separately based on asymptotic expansions of solutions, which is very different from the case of unipolar system.

Keywords:
Global-in-time error estimates, stream function, Euler-Poisson system, infinity-ion-mass limits, asymptotic expansion

1.
Introduction

As the limiting system under the non-relativistic limit for the Euler-Maxwell system (See
[
21
,
22
,
26
]
), Euler-Poisson system plays an important role in describing the motions of charged fluids (ions and electrons) in semi-conductors or plasma when the effect of the magnetic field is weak. The periodic problem for the bipolar Euler-Poisson system (BEP) is of the form
[
4
,
5
,
18
]
,

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{\nu}+\mathrm{div}\left(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu}\right)=0,\\
m_{\nu}\partial_{t}\left(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu}\right)+m_{\nu}\mathrm{div}\left(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu}\otimes u_{\nu}\right)+\nabla p_{\nu}(\rho_{\nu})=q_{\nu}\rho_{\nu}\nabla\varphi-m_{\nu}\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu},\\
\triangle\varphi=\rho_{i}-\rho_{e},\\
t=0:(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})=(\rho_{\nu,0},u_{\nu,0}),\end{cases}

for
t>0
and
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a torus in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here for
\nu=e,i
with
e
standing for electrons and
i
ions,
\rho_{\nu}
stand for the particle density and
u_{\nu}
the average velocity of ions and electrons respectively,
\varphi
is the scaled electric potential. The pressure functions
p_{\nu}(\rho_{\nu})

are supposed to be smooth and strictly increasing for all
\rho_{\nu}>0
. The parameters
m_{\nu}
stand for the mass of a single particle and
(q_{e},q_{i})=(-1,1)
stand for the charge of particles.

The (BEP) system is symmetrizable hyperbolic for
\rho_{\nu}>0
if regarding
\nabla\varphi
as a zeroth order term of densities
\rho_{\nu}
by the Poisson equation, hence the existence of local smooth solution is guaranteed by classical hyperbolic theories
[
12
,
13
,
17
]
. For smooth initial data, Alì
[
4
]
proved the global existence of smooth solutions,
which are sufficiently close to the equilibrium state, to equations (
1.1
), see also Li-Yang
[
16
]
and Tong-Tan-Xu
[
25
]
for the decay rate problem. In Wu-Li
[
27
]
and Wu-Wang
[
28
]
, the authors studied the pointwise estimates of solutions. See also Peng-Xu
[
23
]
and Xu-Kawashima
[
30
]
when initial data is in critical Besov spaces.

On the scale of electrons, (BEP) can be simplified based on the fact that the velocity of electrons is much greater than that of ions. Consequently, ions are often regarded as non-moving and becoming a uniform background density for simplicity. In fact, by assuming

\rho_{i}=b(x),\qquad u_{i}=0,

(BEP) becomes the following unipolar Euler-Poisson model for electrons,

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{e}+\mathrm{div}\left(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}\right)=0,\\
m_{e}\partial_{t}\left(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}\right)+m_{e}\mathrm{div}\left(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}\otimes\bar{u}_{e}\right)+\nabla p_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})=-\bar{\rho}_{e}\nabla\bar{\varphi}-m_{e}\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e},\\
\triangle\bar{\varphi}=b(x)-\bar{\rho}_{e},\end{cases}

which has been widely studied, see for instance
[
1
,
8
,
10
,
20
]
. However, the passage from (BEP) to (UEP) is only based on physical observations and lack of rigorous mathematical proof.

In this paper, we will give the rigorous global-in-time convergence analysis from (BEP) to (UEP) in the perspective of mass. Based on the large difference between the mass of electrons and ions, one can assume
m_{e}/m_{i}\rightarrow 0
. In order to derive the (UEP) system (
1.2
), we let
m_{e}=1
be fixed and
1/m_{i}\to 0
, known as the infinity-ion-mass limit, which is another interpretation of the zero-electron-mass limit (
m_{i}
fixed and
m_{e}\to 0
, see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
20
,
31
]
). The infinity-ion-mass limit was introduced in Xi-Zhao
[
29
]
, where the authors obtained the local convergence of this limit for (BEP) without damping mechanism for velocities. We also refer readers to Zhao
[
32
]
for this limit applied for bipolar Euler-Maxwell system.

The global convergence of the infinity-ion-mass limit can be obtained by establishing uniform energy estimates with respect to the small parameter
m_{e}/m_{i}
. When it comes to the global-in-time error estimates between smooth solutions to (BEP) and (UEP), the energy estimates for error variables do not close as the structure has changed. For this difficulty, a stream function method should be applied to get a
L^{2}
dissipative energy estimate for
\rho_{\nu}-\bar{\rho}_{\nu}
. For a conservative equation

\partial_{t}z+\mathrm{div}w=0,

we call
\psi
a stream function associated to this equation if
\psi
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\psi=w+K,\\
&\mathrm{div}\psi=-z,\end{aligned}\right.

with some divergence-free term
K
. The solution of the above system can be established by letting

\psi(t,x)=\psi(0,x)+\int_{0}^{t}(w(\tau,x)+K(\tau,x))d\tau,\qquad\text{with}\,\,\,\,\mathrm{div}\psi(0,x)=-z(0,x).

In our case, the conservative equation can be chosen as the difference between the mass equations of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with
z=\rho_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}
. Then the inner product of the stream function
\psi
with the difference between momentum equations of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can admit a dissipative energy for
\rho_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}
by noticing the strict monotonicity of the pressure function. This method has been successfully used for many fluid dynamical models. We refer readers to the isothermal Euler system with damping in Junca-Rascle
[
11
]
, the M1 model in Goudon-Lin
[
7
]
and the Euler-Maxwell and Euler-Poisson system
[
15
]
, see also
[
14
,
32
]
. However, these results are all relaxation type and are for unipolar systems. Some essential difficulties arise for bipolar models under the infinity-ion-mass limit:

•

The pressure term in the momentum equation for ions vanishes after the passage of the limit, which makes the classical stream function method invalid for the ion equations. Besides, different from relaxation type limits, the dissipative estimates for
\rho_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}
are coupled with
u_{e}-\bar{u}_{e}
, which can not be obtained only based on stream function methods.

•

For bipolar models, the equations for electrons can not be treated independently as the strong coupling effect of
\rho_{e}
and
\rho_{i}
results in the failure of the natural stream function candidate
\nabla(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})
.

In order to handle these difficulties, different treatments are applied for electron and ion equations. For ion equations, due to the vanishing of the dissipative structure for
\rho_{i}-\bar{\rho}_{i}
, we construct the stream function for ion based on some auxiliary equations. For electron equations, the global
L^{2}-
estimates is obtained and energy estimates are closed based on the anti-symmetric structure (see for instance
[
9
,
19
]
) and the stream function technique. To our best knowledge, this seems to be the first global error estimate result for (BEP) concerning bipolar systems under limits other than relaxation limits.

In the following, we let
m_{e}=1
and define the small parameter
\mathbb{\varepsilon}=m_{i}^{-1/2}
. Thus the infinity-ion-mass limit means letting
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
. We denote
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
an integer and
C>0
a generic constant independent of the small parameter
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
and any time. For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
we denote

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}=\dfrac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}\quad{\text{with}}\quad|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}.

For simplicity, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
and
\|\cdot\|_{l}

the usual norms of
L^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

L^{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
H^{l}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}H^{l}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

for all integers
l\geq 1
, respectively. The inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is denoted as
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
. We denote
\nu=i,e
, where
i
stands for ions and
e
electrons.

Now we begin to show the main results of the current paper. Theorem
1.1
concerns the global-in-time uniform estimates of smooth solutions to (BEP) near the constant equilibrium states in classical Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Uniform estimates with respect to
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
)
Let
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
be an integer. There exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
\delta
independent of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
such that for all
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]
, if

\sum_{\nu=i,e}\|\rho_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{s}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|\rho_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{s-1}+\|u_{e,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|u_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}\leq\delta,

then for all
t>0
, the periodic problem (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution
(\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})
satisfying

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=i,e}\|\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(t)-1\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(t)-1\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(t)\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\nu=i,e}\|\nabla\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}+\|u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}+\|\Delta\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\|\rho_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{s}^{2}+\|\rho_{e,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\rho_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-1\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|u_{e,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}^{2}\right).

Combining with some compactness theories, see for instance
[
24
]
, the following Theorem
1.2
concerning the global-in-time convergence of inifinity-ion-mass limit is obtained.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Global convergence) Let
(\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})
be the unique global smooth solution to (BEP) obtained in Theorem
1.1
. If, as
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
,

(\rho_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\rho_{e,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{e,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})\rightharpoonup(\bar{\rho}_{i,0},\bar{\rho}_{e,0},\bar{u}_{e,0},0),\,\,\,\text{weakly in }\,\,H^{s},

then there exist functions
(\bar{\rho}_{i},\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\bar{\varphi})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{s})
, such that, as
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
, up to subsequences,

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle 0\quad\text{strongly in }\,\,\,L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{s}),\quad\forall\,T>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}

\displaystyle(\bar{\rho}_{i},\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\nabla\bar{\varphi}),\quad\text{weakly-* in }\,\,\,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};H^{s}),

where
\bar{\rho}_{i}=\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)
depends only on the space variable
x
, and
(\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\bar{\varphi})
is the unique global smooth solution to the unipolar system (
1.2
) when
m_{e}=1
.

Based on the above two theorems, one obtains the following Theorem
1.3
concerning the global-in-time error estimates between (BEP) and (UEP) in a three-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Global convergence rate) Let
d=3
and
s\geq 3
be integers. Let the conditions in Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
hold. Let
(\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})
be the global smooth solution to (BEP) obtained in Theorem
1.1
and
(\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\bar{\varphi})
be the global solution to (UEP) obtained in Theorem
1.2
. Further assume there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
independent of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
and any time such that

(1.5)

\|\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e,0}-\bar{\rho}_{e,0}\|_{s-1}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i,0}-\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)\|_{s-1}+\|u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e,0}-\bar{u}_{e,0}\|_{s}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i,0}\|_{s}\leq C_{2}\mathbb{\varepsilon},

then for all
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]
, there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
independent of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
and any time, such that the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\|(u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-\bar{u}_{e})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(\tau)-\bar{\rho}_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|(\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-\bar{\rho}_{e})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|\nabla(\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-\bar{\varphi})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\|(u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e}-\bar{u}_{e})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i}(\tau)-\bar{\rho}_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|(\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}+\|\nabla(\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-{\varphi})(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1})d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2},

and

\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\|u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i}(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i}(\tau)\|^{2}_{s-1}d\tau\leq C_{3}\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{4}.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 states preliminaries and reformulations. In Section 3, uniform estimates of (BEP) with respect to
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
are established, based on which the global-in-time convergence towards (UEP) is obtained. Sections 4 focuses on the corresponding convergence rate.

2.
Preliminaries and reformulations

In this section, we show some preliminaries and reformulations of system (BEP). The formal derivation of the limiting equations are also listed.

2.1.
Reformulations and local existence of smooth solutions

We first write (BEP) into a symmetric hyperbolic system. By writing the original momentum equation in (
1.1
) into convective form when
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)>0
, system (
1.1
) becomes

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})=0,\\
\partial_{t}u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+(u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla)u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla h_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})=\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})=0,\\
\partial_{t}u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+(u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\cdot\nabla)u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}+\nabla h_{e}(\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})=-\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\\
\Delta\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}=\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}-\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\end{cases}

where
h_{\nu}
are the enthalpy functions are defined by

h_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho)=\dfrac{p_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho},

which are strictly increasing since
p_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho)>0
. The initial conditions of system (
2.1
) reads

t=0:\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(0,x)=\rho_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x),\ \ u_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(0,x)=u_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x).

Meanwhile, the following restriction is imposed to make
\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
uniquely determined,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}(x)dx=0.

the initial condition of
\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
is defined as follows to avoid appearance of the initial layer:

\Delta\varphi_{0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}=\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i,0}-\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e,0}.

System (
2.1
) admits an equilibrium state

\displaystyle(\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e},\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i},u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e},u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i},\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})=(1,1,0,0,0).

Note that we can take
\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
as a zeroth order term as the
H^{s}
norm of
\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}
can be controlled based on

\|\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}\leq C\|\mathrm{div}\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\|_{s}\leq C(\|\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i,0}-1\|_{s}+\|\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{e,0}-1\|_{s}),

where the Poincaré inequality is used. Now we drop superscript
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
for simplicity and denote

N_{\nu}=\rho_{\nu}-1,\quad U_{\nu}=\left(\begin{matrix}N_{\nu}\\
u_{\nu}\end{matrix}\right).

For
1\leq j\leq d
and
u_{\nu}=(u_{\nu}^{1},\cdots,u_{\nu}^{d})
, we denote

\displaystyle A_{i}^{j}(\rho_{i},u_{i})=\left(\begin{matrix}u_{i}^{j}&\rho_{i}\xi_{j}^{\top}\\
\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}h_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\xi_{j}&u_{i}^{j}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\\
\end{matrix}\right),\qquad A_{e}^{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})=\left(\begin{matrix}u_{e}^{j}&\rho_{e}\xi_{j}^{\top}\\
h_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})\xi_{j}&u_{e}^{j}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\\
\end{matrix}\right),

and

\displaystyle Q_{i}(u_{i},\varphi)=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\varphi-u_{i}\\
\end{matrix}\right),\qquad Q_{e}(u_{e},\varphi)=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
-\nabla\varphi-u_{e}\end{matrix}\right),

where
{\bf{I}}_{d}
is the
d\times d
unit matrix and
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Finally, system (
2.1
) is rewritten into the following form

(2.2)

\partial_{t}U_{\nu}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{\nu}^{j}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})\partial_{x_{j}}U_{\nu}=Q_{\nu}(u_{\nu},\nabla\varphi),

with initial conditions

(2.3)

\displaystyle t=0:\quad(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu},\varphi)=(\rho_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\varphi_{0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

System (
2.2
)-(
2.3
) is symmetrizable hyperbolic when
\rho_{\nu}>0
. Indeed, if we define the symmetrizers as

\displaystyle A_{i}^{0}(\rho_{i})=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})&0\\
0&\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\rho_{i}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\\
\end{matrix}\right),\qquad A_{e}^{0}(\rho_{e})=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})&0\\
0&\rho_{e}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\\
\end{matrix}\right),

which are symmetric and positive definite for
\rho_{\nu}>0
, then the matrices
\tilde{A}_{\nu}^{j}
defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{i}^{j}(\rho_{i},u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i}^{0}(\rho_{i})A_{i}^{j}(\rho_{i},u_{i})=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})u_{i}^{j}&p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\xi_{j}^{\top}\\
p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\xi_{j}&\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\rho_{i}u_{i}^{j}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\\
\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{e}^{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{i}^{0}(\rho_{e})A_{e}^{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})=\left(\begin{matrix}h_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})u_{e}^{j}&p_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})\xi_{j}^{\top}\\
p_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})\xi_{j}&\rho_{e}u_{e}^{j}\mbox{\bf I}_{d}\end{matrix}\right),

are symmetric. By the theory of Lax
[
13
]
and Kato
[
12
]
for the symmetrizable hyperbolic system, one has

Proposition 2.1
.

(Local existence of smooth solutions)
Let
s\geq\dfrac{d}{2}+1
be an integer and the initial data
\left(\rho_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\,u_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)\in H^{s}
with
\rho_{\nu,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\geq 2\underline{\rho}
for some given positive constant
\underline{\rho}>0
independent of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
.
Then there exists
T^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}>0
such that the periodic problem (
2.1
) has a unique smooth solution
\left(\rho^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{\nu},\,u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{\nu},\,\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)
defined on the time interval
[0,T^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}]
, satisfying
\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\geq\underline{\rho}
and

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{\nu}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\nabla\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle C\left(\left[0,\,T^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right];\,H^{s}\right)\cap C^{1}\left(\left[0,\,T^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right];\,H^{s-1}\right).

2.2.
Formal Derivation of the Limiting System.

We now formally derive the limiting system. The formal limits of
(\rho_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\rho_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},u_{e}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}},\varphi^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}})
are denoted by
(\bar{\rho}_{i},\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{i},\bar{u}_{e},\nabla\bar{\varphi})
. Then as
\mathbb{\varepsilon}\to 0
, the formal limiting system reads

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{i}+\mathrm{div}(\bar{\rho}_{i}\bar{u}_{e})=0,\\
\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{i}+(\bar{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{i}+\bar{u}_{i}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{e}+\mathrm{div}(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})=0,\\
\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{e}+(\bar{u}_{e}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{e}+\nabla h_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})=-\nabla\bar{\varphi}-\bar{u}_{e},\\
\Delta\bar{\varphi}=\bar{\rho}_{i}-\bar{\rho}_{e}.\end{cases}

Let
\bar{v}_{i}=e^{t}\bar{u}_{i}
, the second equation in (
2.4
) becomes

\partial_{t}\bar{v}_{i}+(\bar{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla)\bar{v}_{i}=0.

Consider the characteristic path
x(y,0;t)
starting from
y
and satisfying

\frac{d}{dt}x(y,0;t)=\bar{u}_{i}(x(y,0;t),t),

then one obtains

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{v}_{i}(x(y,0;t),t)=0,

namely
\bar{v}_{i}
is a constant along this path. This yields

y=x-\int_{0}^{t}\bar{u}_{i}(x,\tau)d\tau=x-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tau}\bar{v}_{i}(x,\tau)d\tau=x+\bar{u}_{i}(x,t)-e^{-t}\bar{u}_{i}(x,t),

which gives
\bar{u}_{i}(x,t)=e^{-t}u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i,0}(x+(1-e^{-t})u^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}_{i}(x,t))
. Thus by imposing
u_{i,0}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\to 0
, one has
\bar{u}_{i}\to 0
. Hence
\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{i}=0
by the first equation in (
2.4
), namely
\bar{\rho}_{i}
only depends only on the spatial variables. Thus, the equations for electrons in (
2.4
) are decoupled from the bipolar system and become self-contained, which are of the form

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{e}+\mathrm{div}(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})=0,\\
\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{e}+(\bar{u}_{e}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{e}+\nabla h_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})=-\nabla\bar{\varphi}-\bar{u}_{e},\\
\Delta\bar{\varphi}=\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{e},\end{cases}

with initial conditions

(2.6)

t=0:(\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e})=(\bar{\rho}_{e,0},\bar{u}_{e,0}).

3.
Uniform Estimates and Global convergence

In this section, we denote by
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
an integer and
C
a generic positive constant independent of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
and any time. We first prove Theorem
1.1
. In what follows, we will drop the superscript
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
for simplicity and assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. We denote

W=\left(N_{i},\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-1}u_{i},N_{e},u_{e},\nabla\varphi\right)^{\top}.

Without loss of generality, we suppose
T>0
and
(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu},\varphi)
is the local solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]
. We introduce the total energy and the dissipative energy as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=i,e}\|N_{\nu}\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|N_{i}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|u_{e}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|\nabla\varphi(t)\|_{s}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=i,e}\|\nabla N_{\nu}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|u_{e}(t)\|_{s}^{2}.

Moreover, we set

\mathscr{E}_{T}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}(t),

which is assumed to be uniformly sufficiently small with respect to
T
and
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
. Besides, because of the smallness of
\mathscr{E}_{T}
, it is reasonable to assume

(3.1)

\dfrac{1}{2}\leq\rho_{\nu}\leq\dfrac{3}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad h_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho_{\nu})\geq h_{1},

where
h_{1}
is a positive constant independent of the small parameter
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
and any time. One first obtains

Lemma 3.1
.

(
L^{2}-
estimate) It holds

(3.2)

\|W(t)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}(\tau)\|^{2}+\|u_{e}(\tau)\|^{2}\right)d\tau\leq C\|W(0)\|^{2}.

Proof.

The entropy and the corresponding entropy flux for the bipolar Euler system in (
2.1
) are

\begin{cases}\eta_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})=\dfrac{1}{2}\rho_{e}|u_{e}|^{2}+H_{e}(\rho_{e})+\dfrac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\rho_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}+H_{i}(\rho_{i}),\\[5.69054pt]
\psi_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})=\dfrac{1}{2}\rho_{e}|u_{e}|^{2}u_{e}+\rho_{e}h_{e}(\rho_{e})u_{e}+\dfrac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\rho_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}u_{i}+\rho_{i}h_{i}(\rho_{i})u_{i},\end{cases}

in which
H_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho)=h_{\nu}(\rho)
. Then we have

\partial_{t}\eta_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})+\mathrm{div}\psi_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})+\rho_{e}|u_{e}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\rho_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}=-\nabla\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}).

Combining the Poisson equation and the conservative equations we have

\displaystyle-\nabla\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}))+\varphi\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}))-\varphi\partial_{t}(\rho_{e}-\rho_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}))+\varphi\partial_{t}\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}))+\mathrm{div}(\varphi\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi)-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\eta_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})+\dfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{div}\left(\psi_{0}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})+\varphi(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\rho_{i}u_{i}-\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi)\right)+\rho_{e}|u_{e}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\rho_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}=0.

In addition, by the Taylor’s formula, we obtain

H_{\nu}(\rho_{\nu})=H_{\nu}(1)+h_{\nu}(1)N_{\nu}+\dfrac{1}{2}h_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho_{\nu}^{*})N_{\nu}^{2},

where
\rho_{\nu}^{*}
is between
1
and
\rho_{\nu}
. Since
\partial_{t}N_{\nu}=-\mathrm{div}(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu})
, we have

\partial_{t}H_{\nu}(\rho_{\nu})=-h_{\nu}(1)\mathrm{div}(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu})+\dfrac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left(h_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho_{\nu}^{*})N_{\nu}^{2}\right).

Substituting the above equation to (
3
) and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
[0,t]
, also noticing (
3.1
) yields (
3.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

(Higher order estimates) It holds

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|W(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u_{e}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}\right)d\tau\leq C\|W(0)\|_{s}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(\tau)d\tau.

Proof.

For a multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
1\leq|\alpha|\leq s
, applying
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
to both sides of (
2.2
), and making the inner product of the resulting equation with
2A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\mathrm{div}A_{\nu}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>+2\left<J_{\nu}^{\alpha},A_{\nu}^{0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q_{\nu},A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{\nu}^{1}+I_{\nu}^{2}+I_{\nu}^{3},

with the natural correspondence of
I_{\nu}^{1}
,
I_{\nu}^{2}
and
I_{\nu}^{3}
, and
in which

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}A_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}\tilde{A}_{\nu}^{j}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu}),

\displaystyle J_{\nu}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}(A_{\nu}^{j}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}U_{\nu}-\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(A_{\nu}^{j}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})\partial_{x_{j}}U_{\nu})).

In what follows, we will estimate
I_{\nu}^{1}
,
I_{\nu}^{2}
and
I_{\nu}^{3}
term by term. First, since
\partial_{t}\rho_{\nu}=-\mathrm{div}(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu})
, we have

\|\partial_{t}\rho_{\nu}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u_{\nu}\|_{s}.

Since
\mathbb{\varepsilon}<1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{t}A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Similarly, for
1\leq j\leq d
,

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x_{j}}\tilde{A}_{\nu}^{j}(\rho_{\nu},u_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Therefore

(3.6)

|I_{\nu}^{1}|\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

For
I_{\nu}^{2}
, since
I_{\nu}^{2}=2\sum_{j=1}^{d}I_{\nu,j}^{2}
, with

\displaystyle I_{i,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(u_{i}^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{i})-u_{i}^{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{i}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i})\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i}^{j})-\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i}^{j}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<\rho_{i}\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(h_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x_{j}}N_{i})-h_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{i}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}^{j}\right>

\displaystyle+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\rho_{i}\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(u_{i}^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i})-u_{i}^{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>,

and

\displaystyle I_{e,j}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(u_{e}^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{e})-u_{e}^{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{e}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{e}^{j})-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{e}^{j}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<\rho_{e}\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(h_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x_{j}}N_{e})-h_{e}^{\prime}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}N_{e}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}^{j}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<\rho_{e}\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(u_{e}^{j}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{e})-u_{e}^{j}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{e}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>.

Noticing
\nabla h_{\nu}^{\prime}(\rho_{\nu})=h_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{\nu})\nabla N_{\nu}
, by the Moser-type inequalities in Lemma 2.1, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|I_{\nu}^{2}|\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

For
I_{\nu}^{3}
, by using the Moser-type inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{i}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>-\dfrac{2}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left<\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>-\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|^{2},

similarly

\displaystyle I_{e}^{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>-\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\|^{2}.

Substituting (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and the above two estimates into (
3.5
), and adding the resulting equation for
\nu=i,e
yield

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=i,e}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t)+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>.

Now we estimate
2\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>
. Since

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}u_{i}-\rho_{e}u_{e})\right>-\sum_{\nu=i,e}\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{\nu}u_{\nu})-\rho_{\nu}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{\nu}\right>,

in which

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}u_{i}-\rho_{e}u_{e})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi,\mathrm{div}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}u_{i}-\rho_{e}u_{e})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi,\partial_{t}\Delta\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\|^{2},

and

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e})-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{e}\right>\right|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\|\|\nabla N_{e}\|_{s-1}\|u_{e}\|_{s-1}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t),

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}u_{i})-\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>\right|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\|\|\nabla N_{i}\|_{s-1}\|\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-1}u_{i}\|_{s-1}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Substituting these into (
3.8
), adding the resulting equation for all
1\leq|\alpha|\leq s
and integrating over
[0,t]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq|\alpha|\leq s}\left(\sum_{\nu=i,e}\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>+\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|u_{e}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|W(0)\|_{s}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(\tau)d\tau.

Noticing that there exists constants
c_{a}>0
such that

\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu},A_{\nu}^{0}(\rho_{\nu})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}U_{\nu}\right>+\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\varphi\|^{2}\geq c_{a}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}W\|^{2},

further combining (
3.2
) yield (
3.4
).
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

(Dissipation of
\nabla N_{\nu}
) It holds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|\leq s-1}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i}\|^{2}+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>\right)+\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{e}\|^{2}+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{e}u_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{e}\right>\right)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{h_{1}}{2}\|\nabla N_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{h_{1}}{2}\|\nabla N_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta\varphi\|_{s-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\dfrac{u_{i}}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{s}^{2}+C\|u_{e}\|_{s}^{2}+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Proof.

For a multi-index
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\beta|\leq s-1
, multiplying
\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\rho_{i}
to both sides of the momentum equation of ions in (
1.1
), and applying
\partial_{x}^{\beta}
to the resulting equation, we have

\displaystyle p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i})-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}\otimes u_{i}))-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i})

\displaystyle+\left(p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}(p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\nabla N_{i})\right).

Taking the inner product of the above equation with
\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
yields

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left<p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i},\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left<\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>-\left<\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}\otimes u_{i})),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>-\left<\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle+\left<\left(p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}(p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\nabla N_{i})\right),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>.

Now we treat each term in the above equation. First, the estimate for the term containing
\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi)
on the left hand side requires a little more calculations. Let us first remark that

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>+\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi)-\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>,

in which by the Moser-type inequalities, we have

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi)-\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>\right|\leq C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{s-1}\|\nabla N_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t),

and

\displaystyle\left<\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\nabla(\rho_{i})^{2})\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\rho_{i})-\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\rho_{i}\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\nabla(\rho_{i})^{2})\right>+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Hence,

\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}\nabla\varphi),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\nabla(\rho_{i})^{2})\right>+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Moreover, noticing (
3.1
), we have

\left<p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i},\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>\geq\dfrac{h_{1}}{2}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\|^{2}.

Let us move on to the right hand side of (
3.10
). An integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>-\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i})\right\|^{2}.

By applying the Young’s inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Moser-type inequalities, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}|\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}\otimes u_{i})),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla N_{i}\|_{s-1}\left\|\dfrac{u_{i}}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{s}^{2}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t),

\displaystyle|\left<p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}(p_{i}^{\prime}(\rho_{i})\nabla N_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla N_{i}\|_{s-1}^{3}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Next, using the mass equation of ions in (
2.1
), we have

\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>=-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i}\right>=\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i},\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i}\right>=\dfrac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i}\|^{2}.

Thus, combining all these estimates, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{i}\|^{2}+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\right>\right)+h_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{i}\|^{2}-\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\nabla(\rho_{i})^{2})\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\dfrac{u_{i}}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{s}^{2}+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Similarly, applying the same procedure as above to the momentum equation for electrons, we obtain the similar estimate for electrons:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}N_{e}\|^{2}+2\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{e}u_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{e}\right>\right)+h_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla N_{e}\|^{2}+\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\nabla(\rho_{e})^{2})\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|{u}_{e}\right\|_{s}^{2}+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Now it remains to estimate the term
\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla((\rho_{i})^{2}-(\rho_{e})^{2})\right>
. Since

\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi=\partial_{x}^{\beta}\rho_{i}-\partial_{x}^{\beta}\rho_{e},

then

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\nabla((\rho_{i})^{2}-(\rho_{e})^{2})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}((\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e}))\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left<(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi\right>

\displaystyle-\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}((\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e}))-(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})\right>,

in which noticing (
3.1
), we have

\left<(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi\right>\geq\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi\|^{2},

and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Moser-type inequalities, we have

\displaystyle|\left<\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi,\partial_{x}^{\beta}((\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e}))-(\rho_{i}+\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\beta}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})\right>|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi\|\|\nabla\rho_{i}+\nabla\rho_{e}\|_{s-1}\|\rho_{i}-\rho_{e}\|_{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\Delta\varphi\|^{2}+C\mathscr{E}_{T}^{1/2}\mathscr{D}(t).

Hence, combining these estimates, we have

4.
Global convergence rate

In this section, we study the global-in-time convergence rate problems. For simplicity, the superscript
\mathbb{\varepsilon}
is dropped. In this section, we set
d=3
. Let
(\rho_{i},u_{i},\rho_{e},u_{e},\nabla\varphi)
be the solution to bipolar Euler-Poisson system (

), and let their corresponding limit as
(\bar{\rho}_{i}(x),0,\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\nabla\bar{\varphi})
, in which

satisfies the unipolar Euler-Poisson system for electrons (

The complete proof has a total of three steps. First, we introduce
\bar{u}_{i}
as the limit of
\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}u_{i}
to construct an auxiliary equation as the substitute for the missing limiting equation of ion. Then we apply the anti-symmetric structure of the error system to establish some preliminary conclusions. Finally, the stream function method is applied to establish the dissipative estimate for

and
(\rho_{e}-\bar{\rho_{e}})

4.1.
The Limiting Equations

At the beginning, we first list some estimates and some facts about the limiting equations. First, under the conditions in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3

\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)=1
. Indeed,

\|\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)-1\|_{s-1}\ \leq\|\bar{\rho}_{i}(x)-\rho_{i,0}\|_{s-1}+\|\rho_{i,0}-1\|_{s-1}\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon},

for all

. Based on this,
(\bar{\rho}_{e},\bar{u}_{e},\nabla\bar{\varphi})
is in a neighbourhood of a constant equilibrium state. Thus the limit system (
2.5
) becomes

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{e}+\mathrm{div}(\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})=0,\\
\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{e}+(\bar{u}_{e}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{e}=-\nabla\bar{\varphi}-\bar{u}_{e},\\
\Delta\bar{\varphi}=1-\bar{\rho}_{e},\end{cases}

with the same initial condition (
2.6

Proposition 4.1
.

(Estimates for equations for electrons, see
[
10

20
]
) Let

be an integer. There exists positive constants

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\rho}_{e}(t)-1\|_{s}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{e}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{s}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{\rho}_{e}(\tau)-1\|_{s}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{e}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}(\tau)\|_{s}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\bar{\rho}_{e,0}-1\|_{s}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{e,0}\|_{s}^{2}).

Moreover, there exists a positive constant

However, for the limiting equations for ions, after taking the limits, we lose the information of the conservative equations for ions. Consequently, we use the first order profile in the asymptotic expansion to substitute the limiting equations for ions. In order to do this, we first need to establish estimates on
u_{i}

Lemma 4.1
.

(Convergence rate for
u_{i}
) Suppose

(4.3)

\|u_{i}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}d\tau\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{4},\quad\forall\,t>0.

Proof.

For any multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq s-1

to the momentum equation for ion in (
2.1

(4.4)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|^{2}+\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\|^{2}=-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left((u_{i}\cdot\nabla)u_{i}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>+\left<\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\nabla\varphi-\nabla h_{i}(\rho_{i})),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>.

By theorem
1.1
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Moser-type inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left((u_{i}\cdot\nabla)u_{i}\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\nabla u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|^{2}_{s-2}\left\|u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|_{s-1}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\left\langle\mathrm{div}(u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}),(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u_{i})^{2}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|u_{i}^{\mathbb{\varepsilon}}\right\|^{3}_{s-1}.

By Young’s inequality we have

\left|\left<\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\nabla\varphi-\nabla h_{i}(\rho_{i})),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right>\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}u_{i}\right\|^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{4}\left\|\nabla\rho_{i}\right\|_{s-1}^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{s}^{2}.

Lemma 4.4
.

(Estimates for electron variables) It holds

(4.14)

\|(\mathcal{N}_{e},w_{e},\mathcal{F})(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|w_{e}(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{s-1}d\tau\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau.

Proof.

The error system for the electrons are of the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}_{e}+\rho_{e}\mathrm{div}w_{e}+\nabla\mathcal{N}_{e}\cdot u_{e}+\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e}\cdot w_{e}=-\mathcal{N}_{e}\mathrm{div}\bar{u}_{e},\\
\partial_{t}w_{e}+(u_{e}\cdot\nabla)w_{e}+h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\nabla\mathcal{N}_{e}+\mathcal{N}_{e}\nabla h^{\prime}_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})=-(w_{e}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{e}-\mathcal{F}-w_{e}-r(\bar{\rho}_{e},\mathcal{N}_{e}),\end{cases}

where

r(\bar{\rho}_{e},\mathcal{N}_{e})=(h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})-h^{\prime}_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})-h^{\prime\prime}_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})\mathcal{N}_{e})\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e}.

It can be written into the following first-order quasi-linear system:

(4.15)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{V}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})\partial_{x_{j}}\mathcal{V}+L(\bar{\rho}_{e})\mathcal{V}=f,

with

\mathcal{V}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{N}_{e}\\
w_{e}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ A_{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})=\begin{pmatrix}u_{e}^{j}&\rho_{e}\xi_{j}^{\top}\\
h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\xi_{j}&u_{e}^{j}{\bf{I}}_{3}\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ j=1,2,3,

and

L(\bar{\rho}_{e})=\begin{pmatrix}0&(\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e})^{\top}\\
\nabla h^{\prime}_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})&0\end{pmatrix},\quad f=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathcal{N}_{e}\mathrm{div}\bar{u}_{e}\\
-(w_{e}\cdot\nabla)\bar{u}_{e}-\mathcal{F}-w_{e}-r(\bar{\rho}_{e},\mathcal{N}_{e})\end{pmatrix},

where
u_{e}=(u_{e}^{1},u_{e}^{2},u_{e}^{3})
,

is the

is the canonical basis of

.
For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1

to (

in which

g^{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}-\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}A_{j}(\rho_{e},u_{e})\partial_{x_{j}}\mathcal{V}\right)+L(\bar{\rho}_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}-\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(L(\bar{\rho}_{e})\mathcal{V}\right).

Defining the symmetrizer
A_{0}(\rho_{e})
as follows

A_{0}(\rho_{e})=\begin{pmatrix}h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})&0\\
0&\rho_{e}{\bf{I}}_{3}\end{pmatrix},

and taking the inner product of (
4.16
) with
2A_{0}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}
in
L^{2}
yields the following energy equality:

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left<A_{0}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{t}A_{0}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>+\left<B(\mathcal{V},\nabla\mathcal{V})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>

\displaystyle+2\left<A_{0}(\rho_{e})g^{\alpha},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>+2\left<A_{0}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}f,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>

\displaystyle\overset{def}{=}

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\alpha}+J_{2}^{\alpha}+J_{3}^{\alpha}+J_{4}^{\alpha},

with the natural correspondence of
J_{1}^{\alpha}
to
J_{4}^{\alpha}
and the matrix
B(\mathcal{V},\nabla\mathcal{V})
is defined as follows

\displaystyle B(\mathcal{V},\nabla\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{div}(h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})u_{e})&(\nabla p^{\prime}(\rho_{e})-2h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e})^{\top}\\
\nabla p^{\prime}(\rho_{e})-2\rho_{e}\nabla h^{\prime}_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})&\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}u_{e}){\bf{I}}_{3}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\overset{def}{=}\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

For
J_{1}^{\alpha}
, we have

(4.18)

|J_{1}^{\alpha}|\leq\left\|\partial_{t}A_{0}(\rho_{e})\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right\|^{2}\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

For
J_{2}^{\alpha}
, direct calculations give

J_{2}^{\alpha}=\left<B_{11}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right>+\left<B_{22}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right>+\left<(B_{12}+B_{21}^{\top})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right>,

where

(4.19)

\left<B_{11}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right>\leq\left\|B_{11}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right\|^{2}\leq C\delta\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right\|^{2},

and

(4.20)

\left<B_{22}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right>\leq\left\|B_{22}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\|^{2}\leq C\delta\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\|^{2}.

From Taylor Expansion we have,

B_{12}(\rho_{e},\nabla\rho_{e})=B_{12}(\bar{\rho}_{e},\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e})+\frac{\partial B_{12}(\rho_{e}^{a},\nabla\rho_{e}^{b})}{\partial\rho_{e}}\mathcal{N}_{e}+\frac{\partial B_{12}(\rho_{e}^{a},\nabla\rho_{e}^{b})}{\partial(\nabla\rho_{e})}\nabla\mathcal{N}_{e},

and

B_{21}(\rho_{e},\nabla\rho_{e})=B_{21}(\bar{\rho}_{e},\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e})+\frac{\partial B_{21}(\rho_{e}^{c},\nabla\rho_{e}^{d})}{\partial\rho_{e}}\mathcal{N}_{e}+\frac{\partial B_{21}(\rho_{e}^{c},\nabla\rho_{e}^{d})}{\partial(\nabla\rho_{e})}\nabla\mathcal{N}_{e},

with
\rho_{e}^{a}
,
\rho_{e}^{b}
,
\rho_{e}^{c}
and
\rho_{e}^{d}
being between
\rho_{e}
and
\bar{\rho}_{e}
.
Note that,

\frac{\partial B_{12}}{\partial\rho_{e}}=\left(p_{e}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\rho_{e})\nabla\rho_{e}-2h^{\prime\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e})\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e}\right)^{\top},\quad\frac{\partial B_{12}}{\partial(\nabla\rho_{e})}=p_{e}^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{e}){\bf{I}}_{3},

and

\frac{\partial B_{21}}{\partial\rho_{e}}=p_{e}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\rho_{e})\nabla\rho_{e}-2\nabla h^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}),\quad\frac{\partial B_{21}}{\partial(\nabla\rho_{e})}=p_{e}^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{e}){\bf{I}}_{3}.

Also since

\left(B_{12}(\bar{\rho}_{e},\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e})\right)^{\top}=-B_{21}(\bar{\rho}_{e},\nabla\bar{\rho}_{e}),

we have

(4.21)

\left<(B_{12}+B_{21}^{\top})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right>\leq\left\|B_{12}+B_{21}^{\top}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right\|\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\|\leq C\delta\left(\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\|^{2}\right).

Combining (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) we conclude

(4.22)

|J_{2}^{\alpha}|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

For
J_{3}^{\alpha}
, by Moser-type inequality and Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|J_{3}^{\alpha}|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

For
J_{4}^{\alpha}
, by Moser-type calculus inequalities, we have

(4.23)

\displaystyle J_{4}^{\alpha}\leq-\left\langle 2\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\rangle-\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\|^{2}-C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

Noticing the equivalence of
\left<A_{0}(\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right>
and
\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{V}\right\|^{2}
as
A_{0}
is uniformly bounded, integrating (
4.17
) over
[0,t]
and combining (
4.18
), (
4.22
), (
4.2
) and (
4.23
) yields

(4.24)

\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}(t)\right\|^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}(t)\right\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}(\tau)\right\|^{2}d\tau+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right\rangle d\tau\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau.

Now it remains to estimate the last term on the left hand side of the above inequality. Since

\left<\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e}\right>=\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}w_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}w_{e})-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>,

in which

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}w_{e})-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>\right|\leq C\|\nabla\rho_{e}\|_{s-1}\|w_{e}\|_{s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

In addition,

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}w_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>=\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>,

in which

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>\right|\leq C\|\bar{u}_{e}\|_{s}\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t),

and

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{e}-\mathcal{N}_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})\right>+\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{i}u_{i}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|^{2}+C\|\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-1}u_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{s-1}^{2}.

Substituting these estimates into (
4.24
) and summing the resulting equation for all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
yield (
4.14
).
∎

4.3.
Application of the Stream Function Technique

It remains to establish the dissipative estimates for
\mathcal{N}_{i}
and
\mathcal{N}_{e}
, in which the stream function technique is necessary. We first construct the stream functions.

We write equation (
4.7
) into the following

\partial_{t}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}+\mathrm{div}(\bar{u}_{i}-\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})=0.

Subtracting the above equation multiplied by
\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}
from the mass conservative equation for ions leads

\partial_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{i}-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})+\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}u_{i}-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\bar{u}_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})=0.

in which further noticing that
u_{i}=\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}(w_{i}+\bar{u}_{i})
, we have

\partial_{t}(\mathcal{N}_{i}-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})+\mathrm{div}(\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{i}w_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})=0.

Following the methods in constructing stream functions in
[
32
]
, there exist a unique stream function
\psi_{i}
and a remaining function
M_{i}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi_{i}=\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\rho_{i}w_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{N}_{i}\bar{u}_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}+\nabla\times M_{i},\\
\mathrm{div}\psi_{i}=-\mathcal{N}_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1},\quad\nabla\times\psi_{i}=0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{i}dx=0.\end{cases}

By Poincaré inequality, we have

\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\mathrm{div}\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+C\|\nabla\times\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\mathcal{N}_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{4}\|\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}.

Similarly, the error conservative equation for electrons is of the form

\partial_{t}\mathcal{N}_{e}+\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})=0,

which implies that there exist a unique stream function
\psi_{e}
and a remaining function
M_{e}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi_{e}=\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}+\nabla\times M_{e},\\
\mathrm{div}\psi_{e}=-\mathcal{N}_{e},\quad\nabla\times\psi_{e}=0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{e}dx=0.\end{cases}

Applications to the Ion System

Now we apply the stream function technique to the error system. We first treat the ion system. The error equation for the momentum equation for ions reads

\partial_{t}w_{i}+w_{i}+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}(u_{i}\cdot\nabla)u_{i}+\nabla h_{i}(\rho_{i})=\mathcal{F}.

For multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
with
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, applying
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
to both sides of and taking the inner product of the resulting equation with
\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}((u_{i}\cdot\nabla)u_{i}),\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla h_{i}(\rho_{i}),\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{def}{=}

\displaystyle K_{i}^{1}+K_{i}^{2}+K_{i}^{3}+K_{i}^{4},

with the natural correspondence of
K_{i}^{1}
to
K_{i}^{4}
, which are treated term by term in a series of lemmas as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

(Estimate of
K_{i}^{1}
)
For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, it holds

(4.25)

K_{i}^{1}\geq\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathcal{D}(t)+\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2})-\frac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\psi_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{s}.

Proof.

By integration by parts, we have

K_{i}^{1}=\left\langle\partial_{t}(\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{t}\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle,

in which

\left\langle\partial_{t}(\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle.

Direct calculations give

\left|\left\langle\partial_{t}\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\|u_{i}\|_{s}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}w_{i}\|\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{i}\|\leq C(\|\mathbb{\varepsilon}w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\psi_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{s}).

Note that

\displaystyle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}+\bar{u}_{i}\mathcal{N}_{i})\right\rangle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\right\rangle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}(\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\mathcal{D}(t))-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle,

where

\displaystyle-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i})-\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle.

Since
\psi_{i}
is rotation free, by Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\bar{u}_{i}\mathcal{N}_{i}),\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\langle\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t),

and by Moser-type inequalities and the Young’s inequality, we have

\left|\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i})-\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\|\mathcal{N}_{i}\|_{s-1}\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+\dfrac{1}{4\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}.

Thus we have

-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{4\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t).

Consequently

-\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{4\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathcal{D}(t)+\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}).

Combining all these estimates implies (
4.25
).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

(Estimates for
K_{i}^{2}
)
For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, it holds

(4.26)

K_{i}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\|^{2}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)-C\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}(\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x}^{2\alpha}\nabla\Delta\bar{\rho}^{1}_{i}(t,x)|dx\right).

Proof.

First we have

K_{i}^{2}=\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle=\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i}-\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle,

in which by the Moser-type calculus inequalities,

|\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i}-\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle|\leq C\|\mathcal{N}_{i}\|_{s-1}\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{i}\|\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+\frac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|w_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{i}w_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\bar{u}_{i}\mathcal{N}_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\left\langle\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\bar{u}_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}\|\psi_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle,

while by Poincaré inequality and integration by parts we have

\displaystyle|\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\cdot\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{i}|dx\leq C\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x}^{2\alpha}\nabla\Delta\bar{\rho}^{1}_{i}(t,x)|dx

Combining all these estimates yields (
4.26
).
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

(Estimates for
K_{i}^{3}
and
K_{i}^{4}
)
For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, it holds

(4.27)

K_{i}^{3}+K_{i}^{4}\geq h_{1}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\|^{2}-(C\delta+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2})\mathcal{D}(t)-\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}-C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}-\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{s}\|\psi_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}.

Proof.

For
K_{i}^{3}
, by Young’s inequality we have

|K_{i}^{3}|\leq\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}\|u_{i}\|_{s-1}\|u_{i}\|_{s}\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}\leq\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}\|^{2}_{s}\|\psi_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}

For
K_{i}^{4}
, we first notice that by Taylor expansion we have a
\rho_{i}^{h}
between
\rho_{i}
and
1
satisfying

h_{i}(\rho_{i})-h_{i}(1)=h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}.

Hence,

K_{i}^{4}=-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i})\right\rangle=-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathrm{div}(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i})\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{N}_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i})\right\rangle.

By the definition of the stream function, we have

-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathrm{div}(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i})\right\rangle=\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\right\rangle-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\right\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i})-h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h_{1}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\|^{2}-C\delta\mathcal{D}(t),

and by Poincaré inequality

\left|\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|^{2}_{s-1}.

Also we have

\left|\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(h^{\prime}_{i}(\rho_{i}^{h})\mathcal{N}_{i}),\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{N}_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i})\right\rangle\right|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}.

Combining all these estimates, we have (
4.27
).
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

(Dissipative estimates for
\mathcal{N}_{i}
) It holds

(4.28)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s-1}\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle d\tau+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\psi_{i}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times M_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\mathcal{N}_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau+\dfrac{C\delta+c_{0}\mu_{0}}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\psi_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2},

where
\mu_{0}>0
is a sufficiently small constant, of which the value is determined in (
4.35
).

Proof.

Combining Lemmas
4.5
to
4.7
, we have

(4.29)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times\partial^{\alpha}_{x}M_{i}\|^{2}+h_{1}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{N}_{i}\|^{2}-\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}(\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{-2}u_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\nabla\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}))+\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|u_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{C}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\left(\|u_{i}\|_{s}^{2}+\|w_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{i}\|^{2}_{s-1}\right)+C\|\psi_{i}\|_{s-1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x}^{2\alpha}\nabla\Delta\bar{\rho}^{1}_{i}(t,x)|dx.

By Poincaré inequality and Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle|\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle(0)|\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2},

and

\displaystyle|\left\langle\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}w_{i},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle(t)|\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}(t)\|^{2}.

Consequently, integrating (
4.29
) over
[0,t]
, summing the resulting equation for all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
and applying Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s-1}\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle d\tau+\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\psi_{i}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{1}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\|\nabla\times M_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\|\mathcal{N}_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau+\dfrac{C\delta+c_{0}\mu_{0}}{\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\psi_{i}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\partial_{x}^{2\alpha}\nabla\Delta\bar{\rho}^{1}_{i}(\tau,x)|dxd\tau,

where
\mu_{0}>0
is a sufficiently small constant to be determined later.
Recall Lemma
4.2
we obtain (
4.28
).
∎

Applications to the Electron System

The error system for the momentum equation for electrons reads

\partial_{t}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})+\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}u_{e}\otimes u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}\otimes\bar{u}_{e})+\nabla(p_{e}(\rho_{e})-p_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e}))=-(\rho_{e}\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{N}_{e}\nabla\bar{\varphi})-(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}).

Applying
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}
to both sides of the above equation and taking the inner product of the resulting equation with
\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}
, we have

\displaystyle-\left\langle\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla(p_{e}(\rho_{e})-p_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle+\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}\mathcal{F})-\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>

\displaystyle+\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\mathrm{div}(\rho_{e}u_{e}\otimes u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}\otimes\bar{u}_{e}),\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle+\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{e}\nabla\bar{\varphi}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>

\displaystyle\overset{def}{=}

\displaystyle K_{e}^{1}+K_{e}^{2}+K_{e}^{3}+K_{e}^{4}+K_{e}^{5}+K_{e}^{6},

with the natural correspondence of
K_{e}^{1}
to
K_{e}^{6}
, of which the estimates are established in a series of lemmas below.

Lemma 4.9
.

(Reformulations for
K_{e}^{1}
and
K_{e}^{2}
). For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, it holds,

(4.30)

K_{e}^{1}+K_{e}^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\|^{2}+\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>+\|\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\|^{2}-\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})\|^{2}.

Proof.

By integration by parts, we have

K_{e}^{1}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>,

in which

\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>=\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})\|^{2}+\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\right>.

Notice that

\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\right>=\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e},\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\right>-\|\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\|^{2}=-\|\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\|^{2},

combining these identities, we obtain

K_{e}^{1}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>+\|\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\|^{2}-\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e})\|^{2}.

Noticing the definition of the stream function technique, we have

K_{e}^{2}=\left<\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>-\left<\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\|^{2},

which, combining the reformulation for
K_{e}^{1}
, yields (
4.30
).
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

(Estimates for
K_{e}^{3}
). For all
|\alpha|\leq s-1
, it holds,

(4.31)

K_{e}^{3}\geq\dfrac{2}{3}h_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\|^{2}-C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

Proof.

We first notice that by Taylor expansion we have a
\rho_{e}^{p}
between
\rho_{e}
and
\bar{\rho_{e}}
satisfying

p_{e}(\rho_{e})-p_{e}(\bar{\rho}_{e})=p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\mathcal{N}_{e}.

Hence,

\displaystyle K_{e}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\mathcal{N}_{e}),\mathrm{div}(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>+\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\mathcal{N}_{e})-p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>,

in which

\left<p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>\geq\dfrac{2}{3}h_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\|^{2},

and

\left|\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\mathcal{N}_{e})-p^{\prime}_{e}(\rho_{e}^{p})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e},\partial_{x}^{\alpha}N_{e}\right>\right|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

These estimates imply (
4.31
).
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

(Estimates for
K_{e}^{4}
,
K_{e}^{5}
and
K_{e}^{6}

(4.32)

|K_{e}^{4}+K_{e}^{5}+K_{e}^{6}|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

Noticing that by the Poincaré inequality,

\|\psi_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\psi_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\mathrm{div}\psi_{e}\|^{2}+C\|\nabla\times\psi_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2}\leq C\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2},

\displaystyle|K_{e}^{4}|

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\rho_{e}\|_{s-1}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{s-1}\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1},

\displaystyle|K_{e}^{5}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|w_{e}\|_{s-1}^{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1},

\displaystyle|K_{e}^{6}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1}\|\nabla\bar{\varphi}\|_{s-1}\|\mathcal{N}_{e}\|_{s-1}.

All these estimates imply (
4.32
).
∎

Combining Lemmas
4.9
-
4.11

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\|^{2}+\dfrac{d}{dt}\left<\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\rho_{e}u_{e}-\bar{\rho}_{e}\bar{u}_{e}),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>+\|\nabla\times\partial_{x}^{\alpha}M_{e}\|^{2}+\dfrac{2}{3}h_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\|^{2}

\displaystyle C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}+\|w_{e}(t)\|_{s-1}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|w_{e}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}d\tau.

Lemma 4.12
.

It holds

(4.34)

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s-1}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle-\left\langle\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle d\tau\leq C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{F}(\tau)\|_{s-1}^{2}d\tau+C\delta\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}(\tau)d\tau.

Proof.

First we have

\left\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{i}\right\rangle-\left\langle\rho_{e}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\psi_{e}\right\rangle=\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},(\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e}))\right>,

in which

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F},(\rho_{i}-\rho_{e})\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{e}\right>\right|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|\|\rho_{i}-\rho_{e}\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{N}_{e}\|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t).

For the remaining term, by Poincaré inequality we have

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\mathrm{div}(\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e}))\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\nabla\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e})\right>+\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\rho_{i}\mathrm{div}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e})\right>,

in which

\left|\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\nabla\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e})\right>\right|\leq C\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|\|\nabla\rho_{i}\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}_{i}-\mathcal{N}_{e}-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1})\|\leq C\delta\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\rho_{i}\mathrm{div}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{i}-\psi_{e})\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|^{2}-\left<\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\nabla\rho_{i}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\right>-\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\left\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(\varphi-\bar{\varphi}),\rho_{i}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\|^{2}+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{D}(t)+C\delta\mathcal{D}(t)+C\mathbb{\varepsilon}^{2}\|\bar{\rho}_{i}^{1}\|_{s-1}^{2}.

Combining all these estimates and integrating the first equation over

4.34

Fourier transforms of some special functions in terms of orthogonal polynomials on the simplex and continuous Hahn polynomials
Esra Güldoğan-Lekesiz, Rabia Aktaş, I. Area

Abstract
. In this paper Fourier transform of multivariate orthogonal polynomials on the simplex is presented. A new family of multivariate orthogonal functions is obtained by using the Parseval’s identity and several recurrence relations are derived.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 33C50 Secondary 33C45

1.
Introduction

The integral transforms are important mathematical tools used in many fields such as vibration analysis, sound engineering and image processing. They have wide applications in physics, engineering and in other scientific and mathematical disciplines. The study of orthogonal polynomials and their transformations have been the subject of many papers during the last several years.
By the Fourier transform or other integral transforms, some univariate orthogonal polynomials systems which are mapped onto each other exist
[
1
]
. As an example, it is known that Hermite functions which are multiplied Hermite polynomials
H_{n}\left(x\right)
by
\exp\left(-x^{2}/2\right)
are eigenfunctions of Fourier transform
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
; similarly, Koelink
[
2
]
has showed that Jacobi polynomials are mapped onto the continuous Hahn polynomials by using the Fourier transform. Also, in
[
5
]
, it is seen that classical Jacobi polynomials can be mapped onto Wilson polynomials by the Fourier-Jacobi transform. By inspired of Koelink’s paper, in
[
6
]
Masjed-Jamei et al. have introduced two new families of orthogonal functions by using Fourier transforms of the generalized ultraspherical polynomials and the generalized Hermite polynomials. In
[
7
]
, the Fourier transform of Routh-Romanovski polynomials
J_{n}^{\left(u,v\right)}\left(x;a,b,c,d\right)
has been obtained. Furthermore, in
[
3
,
8
]
, four new examples of finite orthogonal functions have been derived by use of the Fourier transforms of the finite classical orthogonal polynomials
M_{n}^{\left(p,q\right)}\left(x\right)
and
N_{n}^{\left(p\right)}\left(x\right)
, and two symmetric sequences of finite orthogonal polynomials and the Parseval’s identity (see
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
for details of the finite orthogonal polynomials and symmetric sequences of finite orthogonal polynomials). Recently, Güldoğan et al.
[
12
]
have obtained new families of orthogonal functions by means of Fourier transforms of bivariate orthogonal polynomials introduced by Koornwinder
[
13
]
and Fernández et al.
[
14
]
.

In the present paper, we study the Fourier transformations of the classical polynomials orthogonal by means of the extension of Jacobi weight function to several variables on the simplex
T^{r}
. When
r=1
, the simplex
T^{r}
becomes the interval
[0,1]
and the corresponding orthogonal polynomials are Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(2x-1\right)
on the interval
[0,1]
. In this case, Koelink
[
2
]
has derived the Fourier transform of certain Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(x\right)
on the interval
[-1,1]
in terms of continuous Hahn polynomials and has discussed some applications. The Fourier transforms of bivariate orthogonal polynomials have been studied by Güldoğan et al.
[
12
]
. By the motivation of these papers, we obtain the Fourier transforms of the orthogonal polynomials on
T^{r}
and we write them in terms of continuous Hahn polynomials. We first state the results for
r=1
and
r=2
to illustrate the results and illuminate how the results on
T^{r}
are obtained, then we give the results on
T^{r}
by induction. By using the obtained Fourier transforms and Parseval’s identity, we derive a new family of orthogonal functions and obtain several recurrence relations for these functions.

The main aim of this work is to derive the Fourier transforms of functions defined in terms of orthogonal polynomials on the simplex and to obtain a new family of multivariate orthogonal functions by the similar method applied in
[
12
]
for bivariate Koornwinder polynomials. While doing these, firstly we define specific special functions so that they are determined with the motivation to use the orthogonality relation of orthogonal polynomials on the simplex in Parseval’s identity created with the help of Fourier transform. In order to give the results on
T^{r}
, we shall proceed by induction and we first discuss the results for
r=1
and
r=2
. The manuscript is organized as follows: in section
2
we introduce some basic definitions and notations. Section
3
includes the Fourier transforms of the multivariate orthogonal polynomials on the
r
-simplex. In section
4
, by using the Parseval’s identity, a new family of multivariate orthogonal functions is defined by induction. Finally, in section
5
we derive several recurrence relations for the new family of multivariate orthogonal functions.

2.
Basic definitions and notations

The univariate Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(x\right)
are defined by the explicit representation

(2.1)

P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(x\right)=2^{-n}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}\binom{n+\alpha}{k}\binom{n+\beta}{n-k}\left(x+1\right)^{k}\left(x-1\right)^{n-k}.

They are orthogonal on the interval
\left[-1,1\right]
with respect to the weight function
w\left(x\right)=\left(1-x\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+x\right)^{\beta}
(cf.
[
15
,
16
]
),

{\displaystyle\int\limits_{-1}^{1}}\left(1-x\right)^{\alpha}\left(1+x\right)^{\beta}P_{n}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(x\right)P_{m}^{\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}\left(x\right)dx\\
=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Gamma\left(\alpha+n+1\right)\Gamma\left(\beta+n+1\right)}{n!\left(\alpha+\beta+2n+1\right)\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta+n+1\right)}\delta_{m,n},\qquad\alpha,\beta>-1,

where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N\cup}\left\{0\right\}
,
\delta_{m,n}
is the Kronecker delta, and
\Gamma(\lambda)
is the Gamma function (cf.
[
17
]
) defined by

(2.2)

\Gamma\left(\lambda\right)={\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}}x^{\lambda-1}e^{-x}dx,\quad\Re\left(\lambda\right)>0.

The
(p,q)
generalized hypergeometric function is defined by (cf.
[
17
]
)

(2.3)

\,{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p}}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{q}}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{x}}\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}}\frac{\left(a_{1}\right)_{k}\left(a_{2}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(a_{p}\right)_{k}}{\left(b_{1}\right)_{k}\left(b_{2}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(b_{q}\right)_{k}}\frac{x^{k}}{k!},

where
\left(a\right)_{k}={\displaystyle\prod\limits_{r=0}^{k-1}}\left(a+r\right)
,
\left(a\right)_{0}=1
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is the Pochhammer symbol, and the beta function is given by (cf.
[
17
]
)

(2.4)

B\left(a,b\right)={\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}}x^{a-1}\left(1-x\right)^{b-1}dx=\frac{\Gamma\left(a\right)\Gamma\left(b\right)}{\Gamma\left(a+b\right)},\qquad\Re\left(a\right),\Re\left(b\right)>0.

The continuous Hahn polynomials can be introduced in terms of hypergeometric series as
[
18
]

(2.5)

p_{n}\left(x;a,b,c,d\right)=i^{n}\frac{\left(a+c\right)_{n}\left(a+d\right)_{n}}{n!}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{-n,\ n+a+b+c+d-1,\ a+ix}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{a+c,\ a+d}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{1}}\right).

These polynomials can be presented as a limiting case of the Wilson polynomials
[
18
]
. Koelink
[
2
]
gave a nice survey of the history this family of orthogonal polynomials. Recently
[
19
]
some applications of continuous Hahn polynomials to quantum mechanics have been presented.

Let
\left|\boldsymbol{x}\right|:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{r}
for
\boldsymbol{x}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Let
V_{n}^{r}
be the linear space of orthogonal polynomials respect to the weight function
W_{\mathbf{\alpha}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\left(1-\left|\boldsymbol{x}\right|\right)^{\alpha_{r+1}}
,
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r+1})
,
\alpha_{i}>-1
, on the simplex
T^{r}=\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}:x_{1}\geq 0,\dots,x_{r}\geq 0,1-\left|\boldsymbol{x}\right|\geq 0\right\}
. The elements of the linear space
V_{n}^{r}
verify the following partial differential equation (cf.
[
20
]
)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}\left(1-x_{i}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}-2\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq r}x_{i}x_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\left(\left(\alpha_{i}+1\right)-\left(\left|\mathbf{\alpha}\right|+r+1\right)x_{i}\right)\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{i}}\\
=-n\left\{n+\left|\mathbf{\alpha}\right|+r+1\right\}P,

which has
\dbinom{n+r-1}{n}
linear independent polynomial solutions of total degree
n
. In here,
\left|\mathbf{\alpha}\right|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{r+1}
. The space
V_{n}^{r}
has several different bases. One of these bases can be introduced as follows. Let
\mathbf{x}_{j}
and
\mathbf{n}^{j}
be defined as

\boldsymbol{x}_{0}=0,\quad\boldsymbol{x}_{j}=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{j}\right),\quad\left|\boldsymbol{x}_{j}\right|=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{j},\quad 1\leq j\leq r,

\mathbf{n}^{j}=\left(n_{j},\dots,n_{r}\right),\quad\left|\mathbf{n}^{j}\right|=n_{j}+\cdots+n_{r},\quad 1\leq j\leq r

and
\mathbf{n}^{r+1}:=0
.

Also, let
\mathbf{n}=\left(n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\right)
,
\left|\mathbf{n}\right|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
,
\mathbf{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r+1}\right)
, and
\mathbf{\alpha}^{j}=\left(\alpha_{j},\dots,\alpha_{r+1}\right)
,
\left|\mathbf{\alpha}^{j}\right|=\alpha_{j}+\cdots+\alpha_{r+1}
,
1\leq j\leq r+1
. In this case, an orthogonal base of the space
V_{n}^{r}
is of form (cf.
[
20
]
)

(2.6)

P_{\mathbf{n}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\left|\boldsymbol{x}_{j-1}\right|\right)^{n_{j}}P_{n_{j}}^{\left(a_{j},b_{j}\right)}\left(\frac{2x_{j}}{1-\left|\boldsymbol{x}_{j-1}\right|}-1\right),

where
P_{n_{j}}^{\left(a_{j},b_{j}\right)}\left(x_{j}\right)
indicates the classical univariate Jacobi polynomial defined in (
2.1
),
a_{j}=2\left|\mathbf{n}^{j+1}\right|+\left|\mathbf{\alpha}^{j+1}\right|+r-j
and
b_{j}=\alpha_{j}
. It follows

(2.7)

{\displaystyle\int\limits_{T^{r}}}W_{\alpha}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)P_{\mathbf{n}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)P_{\mathbf{m}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\mathbf{dx=}h_{\mathbf{n}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}\delta_{\mathbf{n,m}},

where
\mathbf{dx=}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{r}
, and

(2.8)

h_{\mathbf{n}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\Gamma\left(2\left|\mathbf{n}^{j+1}\right|+\left|\mathbf{\alpha}^{j+1}\right|+r-j+1+n_{j}\right)}{n_{j}!\left(2\left|\mathbf{n}^{j+1}\right|+\left|\mathbf{\alpha}^{j+1}\right|+r-j+1+\alpha_{j}+2n_{j}\right)}\\
\times\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\alpha_{j}+n_{j}\right)}{\Gamma\left(2\left|\mathbf{n}^{j+1}\right|+\left|\mathbf{\alpha}^{j+1}\right|+r-j+1+\alpha_{j}+n_{j}\right)}.

3.
Fourier Transforms of Specific Functions

The Fourier transform for a function
g(x)
is defined as (cf.
[
21
]
)

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}\left(g\left(x\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\xi x}g\left(x\right)dx

and the Fourier tranform for a function
g(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})
in
r
variables is defined as (cf.
[
22
]
)

(3.2)

\mathcal{F}\left(g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dots\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{r}x_{r}\right)}g\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\right)dx_{1}\dots dx_{r}.

3.1.
The Fourier transform of Jacobi polynomials on 1-simplex

When
r=1
, the simplex
T^{r}
becomes the interval
[0,1]
and the corresponding orthogonal polynomials in (
2.6
) are the Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(x\right):=P_{n}^{\left(\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}\right)}\left(2x-1\right)
.

Define

(3.3)

g_{1}\left(x;n,a_{1},a_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right)=\left(1+\tanh x\right)^{a_{1}}\left(1-\tanh x\right)^{a_{2}}P_{n}^{\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right)}\left(\Upsilon_{1}\right),

where
a_{1},a_{2},\alpha_{1},
and
\alpha_{2}
are real parameters and

\Upsilon_{1}(x)=\Upsilon_{1}=\frac{1+\tanh x}{2}.

The Fourier transform of this function is

(3.4)

\mathcal{F}\left(g_{1}\left(x;n,a_{1},a_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right)\right)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\xi x}\left(1+\tanh x\right)^{a_{1}}\left(1-\tanh x\right)^{a_{2}}P_{n}^{\left(\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}\right)}\left(\tanh x\right)dx\\
=\frac{2^{a_{1}+a_{2}-1}\left(\alpha_{2}+1\right)_{n}}{n!}\Lambda_{1}^{1}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},n;\xi),

where

\Lambda_{1}^{1}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},n;\xi)=\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{-n,\ a_{2}+\frac{i\xi}{2},\ n+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+1}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{\alpha_{2}+1,\ a_{1}+a_{2}}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{1}}\right)B\left(a_{2}+\frac{i\xi}{2},\ a_{1}-\frac{i\xi}{2}\right),\\

which was proved by Koelink
[
2
]
and it was also rewritten in terms of the continuous Hahn polynomials (
2.5
)
as

\mathcal{F}\left(g_{1}\left(x;n,a_{1},a_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right)\right)=\frac{2^{a_{1}+a_{2}-1}}{i^{n}\left(a_{1}+a_{2}\right)_{n}}B\left(a_{2}+\frac{i\xi}{2},\ a_{1}-\frac{i\xi}{2}\right)\\
\times p_{n}\left(\frac{\xi}{2};\ a_{2},\ \alpha_{1}-a_{1}+1,\ \alpha_{2}-a_{2}+1,\ a_{1}\right),

where
\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{1},a_{2}\right)
,
\mathbf{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right)
and
\ {}_{3}F_{2}
is a special case of the hypergeometric function (
2.3
).

3.2.
The Fourier transform of two-dimensional polynomials on 2-simplex

Let consider the following specific function in terms of the orthogonal polynomials on 2-simplex

(3.5)

g_{2}\left(x_{1},x_{2};n_{1},n_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)=\left(1+\tanh x_{1}\right)^{a_{1}}\left(1-\tanh x_{1}\right)^{a_{2}+a_{3}}\\
\times\left(1+\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{2}}\left(1-\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{3}}P_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)}\left(\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2}\right),

which is selected in this form to use the orthogonality relation for orthogonal polynomials on 2-simplex in Parseval’s identity by the motivation of the Fourier transform of Jacobi polynomials on 1-simplex. Here
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
are real parameters, and

\Upsilon_{1}(x_{1})=\Upsilon_{1}=\frac{1+\tanh x_{1}}{2},\qquad\Upsilon_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=\Upsilon_{2}=\frac{\left(1-\tanh x_{1}\right)\left(1+\tanh x_{2}\right)}{4}.

It is easily seen that the function
g_{2}
can also be expressed in terms of
g_{1}
as follows:

(3.6)

g_{2}\left(x_{1},x_{2};n_{1},n_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)\\
=2^{-n_{2}}\left(1+\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{2}}\left(1-\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{3}}P_{n_{2}}^{\left(\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}\right)}\left(\tanh x_{2}\right)\\
\times g_{1}\left(x_{1};n_{1},a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3}+n_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2n_{2}+1\right).

The corresponding Fourier transform is calculated, under the substitution
\tanh x_{2}=2v-1
, as follows from

\mathcal{F}\left(g_{2}\left(x_{1},x_{2};n_{1},n_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)\right)\\
=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\xi_{1}x_{1}+\xi_{2}x_{2}\right)}g_{2}\left(x_{1},x_{2};n_{1},n_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)dx_{1}dx_{2}\\
=2^{-n_{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\xi_{1}x_{1}}g_{1}\left(x_{1};n_{1},a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3}+n_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2n_{2}+1\right)dx_{1}\\
\times\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\xi_{2}x_{2}}\left(1+\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{2}}\left(1-\tanh x_{2}\right)^{a_{3}}P_{n_{2}}^{\left(\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}\right)}\left(\tanh x_{2}\right)dx_{2}

=\mathcal{F}\left(g_{1}\left(x_{1};n_{1},a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3}+n_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2n_{2}+1\right)\right)\\
\times 2^{a_{2}+a_{3}-n_{2}-{1}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}v^{a_{2}-\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1}\left(1-v\right)^{a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1}P_{n_{2}}^{\left(\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}\right)}\left(2v-1\right)dv\\
=\mathcal{F}\left(g_{1}\left(x_{1};n_{1},a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3}+n_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2n_{2}+1\right)\right)\\
\times 2^{a_{2}+a_{3}-n_{2}-{1}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}v^{a_{2}-\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1}\left(1-v\right)^{a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1}\frac{\left(\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}!}\\
\times\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{-n_{2},n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+1}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{\ \alpha_{3}+1}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{1-v}}\right)dv

(3.7)

=\mathcal{F}\left(g_{1}\left(x_{1};n_{1},a_{1},a_{2}+a_{3}+n_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2n_{2}+1\right)\right)\frac{\left(\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{n_{2}}}{n_{2}!}2^{a_{2}+a_{3}-n_{2}-{1}}\\
\times\sum\limits_{l_{2}=0}^{n_{2}}\frac{\left(-n_{2}\right)_{l_{2}}\left(n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{l_{2}}}{\left(\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{l_{2}}l_{2}!}\int\limits_{0}^{1}v^{a_{2}-\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1}\left(1-v\right)^{a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}-1+l_{2}}dv

that is

(3.8)

\mathcal{F}\left(g_{2}\left(x_{1},x_{2};n_{1},n_{2},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)\right)\\
=\frac{2^{a_{1}+2\left(a_{2}+a_{3}\right)-{2}}\left(2n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2\right)_{n_{1}}\left(\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{n_{2}}}{n_{1}!n_{2}!}\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{1})\Lambda_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{2}),

where

\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{1})=B\left(a_{1}-\frac{i\xi_{1}}{2},n_{2}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{1}}{2}\right)\\
\times\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{-n_{1},n_{1}+2n_{2}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2,\ n_{2}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{1}}{2}}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{2n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2,\ n_{2}+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{1}}\right),

\Lambda_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{2})=B\left(a_{2}-\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2},a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}\right)\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\!\!\begin{array}[]{cc}{\displaystyle{-n_{2},n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+1,a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}}}\\[-0.43057pt]
{\displaystyle{\alpha_{3}+1,\ a_{2}+a_{3}}}\end{array}\Big|\,{\displaystyle{1}}\right),

or, in terms of the continuous Hahn polynomials (
2.5
),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{1}!B\left(a_{1}-\frac{i\xi_{1}}{2},n_{2}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{1}}{2}\right)}{i^{n_{1}}\left(n_{2}+a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}\right)_{n_{1}}\left(2n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+2\right)_{n_{1}}}

\displaystyle\times p_{n_{1}}\left(\frac{\xi_{1}}{2};n_{2}+a_{2}+a_{3},\alpha_{1}-a_{1}+1,n_{2}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}-a_{2}-a_{3}+2,a_{1}\right),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}^{2}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{\alpha},\mathbf{n};\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{2}!B\left(a_{2}-\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2},a_{3}+\frac{i\xi_{2}}{2}\right)}{i^{n_{2}}\left(a_{2}+a_{3}\right)_{n_{2}}\left(\alpha_{3}+1\right)_{n_{2}}}p_{n_{2}}\left(\frac{\xi_{2}}{2};a_{3},\alpha_{2}-a_{2}+1,\alpha_{3}-a_{3}+1,a_{2}\right),

where
\mathbf{a}=\left(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\right)
,
\mathbf{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\right)
,
\mathbf{n}=\left(n_{1},n_{2}\right)
.

3.3.
The Fourier transform of
r
-dimensional polynomials on
r
-simplex

Let us consider

(3.9)

g_{r}\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{r};n_{1},\dots,n_{r},a_{1},\dots,a_{r+1},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r+1}\right)\\
=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(1+\tanh x_{j}\right)^{a_{j}}\left(1-\tanh x_{j}\right)^{|\mathbf{a}^{j+1}|}P_{\mathbf{n}}^{\left(\mathbf{\alpha}\right)}\left(\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2},\dots,\Upsilon_{r}\right),

for
r\geq 1
, where

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{1}(x_{1})=\Upsilon_{1}=\frac{1+\tanh x_{1}}{2},

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=\Upsilon_{r}=\frac{\left(1-\tanh x_{1}\right)\left(1-\tanh x_{2}\right)\cdots\left(1-\tanh x_{r-1}\right)\left(1+\tanh x_{r}\right)}{2^{r}},

for
r\geq 2
and

\mathbf{n}=\left(n_{1},\dots,n_{r}\right),\ \mathbf{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r+1}\right),\quad\text{ and}\ \left|\mathbf{a}^{j}\right|=a_{j}+\cdots+a_{r+1}.

From the latter expression, we can write
g_{r+1}
in terms of
g_{r}

(3.10)

g_{r+1}\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{r+1};n_{1},\dots,n_{r+1},a_{1},\dots,a_{r+2},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r+2}\right)\\

First-order formalism for non-Abelian string
E. Acalapati, H. S. Ramadhan

Abstract
We construct a method of obtaining the Bogomolny equations for general non-Abelian multi-field vortices. This is done by generalizing the well-known first-order formalism to the case of non-Abelian strings. We do not assume any specific gauge group nor the shape of the kinetic term function, but require only that the fields are axially-symmetric and static. With this formalism we reproduce the BPS equations of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(2)\times U(1) Alice strings Chatterjee:2017jsi , and present their corresponding numerical solutions.

Four decades ago, Schwarz found a class of non-Abelian vortices, dubbed the
Alice strings
, where the unbroken symmetries become multi-valued when parallel-transported around it

;

Another exotic feature of this defects is that when the string forms a closed loop it can be endowed with charge that produces long-range field as in the ordinary electrodynamics, but without well-localized source. This strangely-behaved charge is dubbed the
Cheshire charge

Alford:1990mk
;
Bucher:1991bc

;

;

;

The simplest model that contains Alice string in its spectrum is an

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{Z}

Recently, Chatterjee and Nitta found the BPS states of Alice strings in the

II
First-order Formalism for Non-Abelian Multi-field Vortices

Consider the most general Lagrangian containing multi Higgs and gauge fields of co-dimension-2. The gauge fields can either be: Maxwell and
(m-1)
Yang-Mills, or pure
m
-Yang-Mills. The Lagrangian is as follows:

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(|\Phi|,A^{\mu}_{j},X,Y^{(j)}).

(1)

Some explanations are in order. Here,

\Phi\equiv\left(\phi_{1}\ \phi_{2}....\phi_{n}\right)^{T},\ \ A^{\mu}_{j}\equiv\left(A^{\mu}_{1},A^{\mu}_{2},...\right)\equiv\left(a^{\mu},A^{a\mu},...\right),

(2)

with
\phi_{i}
complex scalar fields,
i=1,2,...,n
,
j=1,2,...,m
, and where
a^{\mu}
,
A^{a\mu},...
denote the Maxwell and Yang-Mills fields, respectively. We also define

X\equiv\Tr D_{\mu}\Phi^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\Phi,\ \ \ Y^{(j)}\propto\Tr G_{\mu\nu(j)}G^{\mu\nu}_{~(j)},

(3)

where no summation is implied on
j
, and
G^{\mu\nu}_{j}
denotes the field tensor for either Maxwell
G^{\mu\nu}_{(j)}\equiv\partial^{\mu}a^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}a^{\mu}
or Yang-Mills
G^{\mu\nu}_{(j)}\equiv\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu}-ig_{j}[A^{\mu},A^{\nu}]
with
g_{j}
the coupling constant of the corresponding symmetry group. Details on how many fields are involved depend on the specific models considered. Here,
\mathcal{L_{M}}\equiv\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\mathcal{M}
. The corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations and energy-momentum tensors are

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}D^{\mu}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi}{2|\Phi|}\mathcal{L}_{|\Phi|},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}G^{\mu\nu}_{(j)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{A_{\nu j}},

(4)

and

T_{\mu\nu}=\mathcal{L}_{X}\Tr\left(D_{\mu}\Phi^{{\dagger}}D_{\nu}\Phi+D_{\nu}\Phi^{{\dagger}}D_{\mu}\Phi\right)+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}G_{\mu\lambda(j)}G^{\lambda}_{\ \nu(j)}-g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L},

(5)

respectively.

The ansatz chosen shall depend on the corresponding symmetry group employed. However, as long as axial symmetry and staticity are employed, they can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}\propto f_{i}(r),\ \ A^{\mu}_{j}\propto A_{j}(r).

(6)

In these forms,the kinetic terms will be:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\left(f_{i}^{\prime 2}+\frac{\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}(-1)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{i}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle Y^{(j)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{A_{j}^{\prime 2}}{2g_{j}^{2}r^{2}},

(7)

where
′
denotes the derivative wrt
r
. The alternating sign
\left(-1\right)^{j+1}
will depend on the specific model and the number of non-Abelian gauge fields
A_{j}
involved. The off-diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor will be of the form:

T_{12}=\mathcal{L}_{X}\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\left(f_{i}^{\prime 2}-\frac{\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}(-1)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{i}^{2}\right)\sin 2\theta.

(8)

The first-order formalism relies on two fundamental assumptions:
axial symmetry
and
energy stability under spatial rescaling

Bazeia:2017nas
. The axial symmetry implies

\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\left(f_{i}^{\prime 2}-\frac{\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}(-1)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{i}^{2}\right)=0.

(9)

For ordinary (single-field) vortex, the condition (
9
) simply reduces to

f^{\prime 2}=\frac{a^{2}f^{2}}{r^{2}}.

(10)

For multi-field system, however, the situation becomes tricky. In this work, we assume that each Higgs field is independent,
i.e.,

f_{i}^{\prime 2}=\frac{\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}(-1)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{i}^{2}.

(11)

Note that this may not be the most general equations derived from (
9
). However, in the next section we show that this yields the correct BPS conditions for the known Alice strings. Eq. (
11
) is the
first
of the two Bogomolny equations. With this condition,

X=-2\sum\limits^{n}_{i}f_{i}^{\prime 2}=-2\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\frac{\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}(-1)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}f_{i}^{2}.

(12)

The second fundamental assumption,
i.e.,
the stability under rescaling, is essentially the
Derrick’s theorem
for static defects
Derrick:1964ww
. The argument is as follows. For static axially-symmetric vortices, the total energy is

E=-2\pi\int r\ dr\ \mathcal{L}\left[\Phi_{i},A_{j},X,Y^{(j)}\right].

(13)

Since, under rescaling
r\rightarrow\lambda r
, the kinetic terms rescale as
X\rightarrow\lambda^{2}X
and
Y^{(j)}\rightarrow\lambda^{4}Y^{(j)}
, the energy does also rescale as

E^{(\lambda)}=-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda^{2}}\int r\ dr\ \mathcal{L}\left[\Phi_{i},A_{j},\lambda^{2}X,\lambda^{4}Y^{(j)}\right].

(14)

Derrick’s theorem states that the condition for stability implies

\frac{dE^{(\lambda)}}{d\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=1}=0.

(15)

This results in

\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}_{X}X-2\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}Y^{(j)}=0.

(16)

Taking its derivative wrt to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\frac{f_{i}^{\prime 2}}{r^{2}}\left[r\left(r\mathcal{L}_{X}f_{i}^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}-\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(A_{1}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\left(-1\right)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}f_{i}+\frac{r^{2}f_{i}\mathcal{L}_{|\Phi|}}{2\sqrt{\sum\limits^{n}_{\ell=1}f_{\ell}^{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\frac{A_{j}^{\prime}}{g_{j}^{2}r^{2}}\left[r\left(\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}\frac{A_{j}^{\prime}}{g_{j}r}\right)^{\prime}-2g_{j}\mathcal{L}_{X}\sum\limits^{n}_{i}\left(A_{1}+\left(-1\right)^{i+j}A_{j+1}\right)^{2}f_{i}^{2}\right]=0.

(17)

Here we express
|\Phi|=\sqrt{\sum\limits^{n}_{\ell=1}f_{\ell}^{2}}
. It can be shown that when setting each square bracket to zero it gives us the field equations, as derived from the Euler-Lagrange (
II
). By inserting Eq. (
11
) into (
II
), the first-order equation for
A_{j}
can be extracted. Generally, it will be in the form

\frac{A_{j}^{\prime}}{r}=M\left(f_{i},r\right),

(18)

where
M\left(f_{i},r\right)
in general is an algebraic function of
f_{i}
(and
r
) but not their derivative. Eq. (
18

Bogomolny eqiations.

The BPS energy can be also be calculated directly. Using the condition (
16
), the energy density can be re-written as

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}X+2\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}Y^{(j)}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{L}_{X}\sum\limits^{n}_{i}f_{i}^{\prime 2}+\sum\limits^{m}_{j}\mathcal{L}_{Y^{(j)}}\frac{A_{j}^{\prime 2}}{g_{j}^{2}r^{2}},

where in the last line we have made use of Eq. (
11

W=W(f_{i},A_{j})
such that

W_{f_{i}}\equiv\frac{dW}{df_{i}}\equiv 2r\mathcal{L}_{X}f_{i}^{\prime},\ \ \ W_{A_{j}}\equiv\frac{dW}{dA_{j}}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{Y}\frac{A_{j}^{\prime}}{g_{j}^{2}r}.

(20)

With these definitions, the energy density (
19
) can be writen as

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{r}\frac{dW}{dr},

(21)

which implies

\displaystyle E=2\pi\int rdr\ \rho=2\pi\ W(f_{i},A_{j})\bigg|_{boundary}.

(22)

One generic feature of various Bogomolnyi alternative formalisms
Bazeia:2008tj
;
Bazeia:2007df
;
Adam:2013hza
;
Atmaja:2014fha
;
Atmaja:2015lia
;
Atmaja:2015umo
;
Bazeia:2017nas
is that, unlike the original Bogomolny’s trick
Bogomolny:1975de
, they do not start from a given potential. Instead, the form of the potential is deduced such that it satisfies the Bogomolny equations and the corresponding non-trivial topology suitable for the existence of the defects. In the next section we shall apply these mechanisms to several models of Alice strings and obtaining their corresponding BPS configurations.

Combining all the previous equations, we have the constraint conditions

\displaystyle\Bigl(e\sqrt{2V_{1}}+g\sqrt{2V_{2}}\Bigr)f_{1}-\frac{1}{2}V_{1,f_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}V_{2,f_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(43)

\displaystyle\Bigl(e\sqrt{2V_{1}}-g\sqrt{2V_{2}}\Bigr)f_{2}-\frac{1}{2}V_{1,f_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}V_{2,f_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(44)

Third, the constraints above can be satisfied by taking

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{1}}{\partial f_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e\sqrt{2V_{1}}f_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{\partial V_{1}}{\partial f_{2}}=2e\sqrt{2V_{1}}f_{2},

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{2}}{\partial f_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g\sqrt{2V_{2}}f_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{\partial V_{2}}{\partial f_{2}}=2g\sqrt{2V_{2}}f_{2}.

(46)

These configurations are chosen to guarantee the Schwarz’s integrability condition. The solution for Eq. (
45
) is

\displaystyle V_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2}\left(f_{1}^{2}+f_{2}^{2}-2\xi^{2}\right)^{2},

(47)

where the integration constant
2\xi^{2}
can be identified as the VEV
\langle\Phi\rangle
. In terms of the
\Phi
field it can be written as

V_{1}\left(\Phi\right)=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\left(\Tr\Phi^{{\dagger}}\Phi-2\xi^{2}\right)^{2}.

(48)

Similarly, the solution for Eq. (
46
) is

V_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})=\frac{g^{2}}{2}\left(f_{1}^{2}-f_{2}^{2}+C\right)^{2},

(49)

or

V_{2}(\Phi)=\frac{g^{2}}{4}\Tr\left([\Phi^{{\dagger}},\Phi]+C\right)^{2},

(50)

where
C
is another integration constant. this time, without loss of generality we can set
C=0
. Thus, we will have the following result for the general potential that can generate BPS equations.

\displaystyle V(\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{1}(\Phi)+V_{2}(\Phi),

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2}\Bigl(\Tr\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi-2\xi^{2}\Bigl)^{2}+\frac{g^{2}}{4}\Tr\Bigl([\Phi^{\dagger},\Phi]\Bigr)^{2}.

(52)

Note that we reproduce the same BPS potential as in
Chatterjee:2017jsi
.

To summarize, by means of Eqs. (
34
), (
35
), (
41
), (
42
), (
47
), and (
49
), the BPS equations are

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bigl(a+A\bigr)f_{1}}{r},

\displaystyle f_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bigl(a-A\bigr)f_{2}}{r},

\displaystyle\frac{a^{\prime}}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\bigl(f_{1}^{2}+f_{2}^{2}-2\xi^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle\frac{A^{\prime}}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{2}\bigl(f_{1}^{2}-f_{2}^{2}\bigr).

(53)

IV
Summary

In this work we enlarge the first-order formalism of obtaining Bogomolnyi equations to non-Abelian vortices. Here we neither assume any specific group nor the number of gauge fields involved. The kinetic terms are not restricted to canonical form and may take any arbitrary non-canonical function. We only require axial symmetry and staticity. As in the Abelian case developed by Bazeia
et al.

Bazeia:2017nas
, this formalism relies on
axial symmetry
and
Derrick’s theorem
. The former implies BPS equations for the Higgs fields, while the latter yields the equations for the gauge fields. Both must be consistent in a sense that they should satisfy the constraint equation. In this formalism, the potential is not put by hand. Rather, we start from unspecified potential and move forward by applying the algorithm to obtain the suitable BPS equations. The potential is obtained from the equation(s) of constraint. The “tricky” part in this generalization is that it assumes that each Higgs field is independent on each other. Thus the first Bogomolny equation can be written as in Eq. (
11
). We do not claim that this postulate is the most general we can have, though we do not have the proof to rule it our either, but certainly for the cases considered here it does work.

We apply the formalism to the
U(1)\times SO(3)
gauge theory coupled with charged complex scalar field. Chatterjee and Nitta already derived the BPS equations for such a model
Chatterjee:2017jsi
using the usual Bogomolny’s trick
Bogomolny:1975de
. Here we derive the same BPS equations using the first-order formalism, and obtain the same potential up to some constant. Theoretically, the scope of this work’s formalism is somehow limited, since not many non-Abelian strings are topologically nontrivial. Alice strings are one of the few non-Abelian vortices with genuine topological stability, thus our choice of model studied in this work. Phenomenologically, however, this formalism might shed light on the field of condensed matter and, more especially, in the application of Alice strings in superfluids
Fujimoto:2020dsa
-
Masaki:2021hmk
. We hope to come to this topics in the near future.

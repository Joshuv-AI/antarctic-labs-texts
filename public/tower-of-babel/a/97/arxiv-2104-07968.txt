Zero-cycle groups on algebraic varieties
Federico Binda and Amalendu Krishna

Abstract
. We compare various groups of 0-cycles on quasi-projective varieties over a field. As applications, we show that for certain singular projective varieties, the Levine-Weibel Chow group of 0-cycles coincides with the corresponding Friedlander-Voevodsky motivic cohomology. We also show that over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic, the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus on a smooth projective variety with respect to a reduced divisor coincides with the Suslin homology of the complement of the divisor. We prove several generalizations of the finiteness theorem of Saito and Sato for the Chow group of 0-cycles over p p -adic fields. We also use these results to deduce a torsion theorem for Suslin homology which extends a result of Bloch to open varieties.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this work is to clarify the relation between various groups of 0-cycles
associated to algebraic varieties and prove some new properties of these
groups. One of the questions we want to
address is whether one can isolate a class of algebraic varieties
for which one could describe the a priori non-
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant motivic
cohomology groups (such as the Levine-Weibel Chow group and the Chow group
with modulus) in terms of the — more classical —
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant motivic
cohomology groups
such as the ones
defined by Friedlander, Suslin and Voevodsky. We answer this question
for a projective variety which has either normal crossing singularities or
it is obtained as the double of a smooth variety along a reduced divisor.
The interest in the latter case originates from the fact that it
allows us to describe the Chow group of 0-cycles with
modulus on smooth varieties with reduced modulus in terms of
the singular homology à la Suslin–Voevodsky.
In the following subsections, we describe our main results and their
applications.

1.3.
Finiteness of 0-cycle groups

To motivate our next set of results, suppose that

Such a finiteness result was previously known over finite and algebraically closed

The conjecture of Colliot-Thélène was affirmatively answered by
Saito and Sato
[
48

(see also Bloch’s appendix to

[
11
]
and

[
12
]
for simplified proofs).
It is natural to expect that a similar finiteness result would hold even if

X
is not complete provided we replace

{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}/m
by
{H^{sus}_{0}(X)}/m
.
In the same spirit, one would expect that
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)}/m
is finite for
X

projective, if we interpret
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)}
as a Chow group with compact support for

X\setminus|D|
.

We give positive answers to both questions. Furthermore, we show that the finiteness
result of Saito and Sato also holds for singular schemes.
The question of finiteness of Suslin homology with finite coefficients arose during a
conversation of authors with Colliot-Thélène on his
conjecture.

For a reduced quasi-projective scheme
X
over a field, let
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)
denote the
Levine-Weibel Chow group of
X
(see §
2
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be an integral quasi-projective scheme over
k
which is regular
in codimension one. Let
D\subset X
be an effective Cartier divisor.
We then have the following.

(1)

{H^{sus}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite if
X
is smooth.

(2)

{{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)}/m
is finite if
X
is smooth and complete.

(3)

{{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite if

is complete.

We show that the above finiteness result also holds over finite and
algebraically closed fields (see §
6
).

1.4.
Levine-Weibel Chow group vs. motivic cohomology

Let
k
be a field of exponential characteristic
p\geq 1
. Let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring, and assume that
p
is invertible in
\Lambda
or that
k
admits resolution of singularities.
Let
X
be a reduced quasi-projective
k
-scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
.

Let
H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))
denote the Friedlander-Voevodsky motivic cohomology
of
X
(see §
2
for a reminder of the definition).
It was shown in
[
37
]
that there is a canonical
map

(1.1)

\lambda_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Lambda\to H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d)),

where
\Lambda
is as above. Note that, a priori, the source and the target of
\lambda_{X}
are very different objects: on one side we have a group of cycles, defined by generators and relations (in particular, no topology is involved), while on the other side we have a group defined either as a Hom-group in a motivic category, or as the cdh-hypercohomology of a certain complex of sheaves.

It is well known that this map can not be an isomorphism for arbitrary
varieties: for instance,
H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))
does not
see the difference between
X
and its semi-normalization. On the other
hand, one can easily show for a curve
X
with a cusp singularity
that
{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Lambda
changes after passing to
the semi-normalization of
X
.

On the other hand, it is a result of Voevodsky that for regular
X
, both groups are isomorphic, as a (very) special case of the identification of motivic cohomology with Bloch’s higher Chow groups. It is therefore reasonable to ask if there is a specific class of singular varieties or, rather, a specific class of singularities, for which
\lambda_{X}
is an isomorphism (for instance, we have already observed that semi-normality is a necessary condition).

In this paper, we prove the following positive result when

X
has normal crossings
singularities (see Definition
2.12
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that
k
is an infinite perfect field
and
X
is a normal crossing projective
variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over
k
.
Then
\lambda_{X}
is an isomorphism in the following cases.

(1)

p>1
and
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
.

(2)

m\in k^{\times}
and
\Lambda={{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
.

We prove a similar isomorphism when
X
is the join of a smooth
projective variety along a reduced divisor if
k
is algebraically closed,
see Theorem
3.5
.

Note that
H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))
is not known to have a presentation in terms
of generators and relations in general. In
[
12
, Thm. 7.1]
,
a variant (denoted by
{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)
in this paper)
of the Levine-Weibel Chow group
was defined for
X
as in Theorem
1.6

in terms of generators and relations. It was shown that

{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Lambda
is isomorphic to
H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))
.
Theorem
1.6
implies that under its hypothesis, there is an isomorphism

{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Lambda\xrightarrow{\cong}{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\Lambda.

As a consequence, it follows that

can be defined in terms
of only one type of relations instead of two types of relations used in

[
12
]
.

We end the discussion of our main results with yet another consequence.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d\geq 2

over an infinite
perfect field and let

Let

By the classical moving lemma for Chow groups, one knows that

Corollary 1.7

Let

2.
Preliminaries: cycles groups and
motivic cohomology

We begin by fixing some notations and clarifying our terminology.
Throughout this paper, we shall encounter several different groups of 0-cycles: the groups will often share the same set of generators, but will be subject to different notions of rational equivalence. In a nutshell, our main results will be a comparison between the different notions, by means of suitable moving lemmas, and to deduce new
properties of these 0-cycle group. We shall draw some consequences.
In this preliminary section, we introduce the main players, recalling some definitions already existing in the literature. New groups of cycles, tailored on the specific geometry of the varieties we are interested in, will be defined later in the paper.

4.3.
Kerz-Saito vs. Russell’s Chow groups with modulus

There exists another notion different from the one used in this paper (and specifically used in the previous theorem) of Chow group of 0-cycles with
modulus. This Chow group was introduced by H. Russell
[
47
]
in the study of a higher dimensional analogue of the generalized Jacobian of Rosenlicht-Serre. Russell’s Chow group with modulus is defined as the cokernel of the map

{\rm div}\colon\bigoplus_{\phi_{C}\colon C\to X}G(C,\phi_{C}^{*}(D-D_{\rm red})+(\phi_{C}^{*}(D))_{\rm red})\to{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D).

In particular, in view of Theorem
2.4
, it is clear that it agrees with
H_{0}^{sus}(X\setminus D)
when
D
is reduced and
X
is proper. Theorem
4.4
then exhibits explicitly an example
(at least in characteristic zero)
for which Russell’s Chow group and the Kerz-Saito Chow group do not agree.
Several authors have enquired in the past about this comparison.
Note that in view of
[
6
, Thm. 1.1]
, it follows that Russell’s Chow group does not satisfy Bloch’s formula, even for
D
reduced. In particular,
it may not admit a cycle class map to relative
K
-theory.

6.
The finiteness theorems

In this section, we shall derive Theorem
1.5
from
some general finiteness results for motivic cohomology groups.
Let
k
be a field. Let
m\geq 1
be an integer which is invertible in
k
,
and
p\nmid m
if
k
is a
p
-adic field. Set
\Lambda^{\prime}={{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
.
Let
X
be a smooth projective
k
-scheme.

If
k
is algebraically closed, then it is classically
known that
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}/m\cong\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Otherwise, the structure of
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}/m
is not fully understood.
If
k
is finite, then the main result of unramified class field
theory in geometric case due to Kato-Saito
[
28
]
shows that

{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite. If
k
is a
p
-adic field, a conjecture of
Colliot-Thélène predicted that
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite.
This conjecture was settled by Saito and Sato in
[
48
]

(see also
[
12
, Prop. 9.3]
).

Let us now recall that there is an isomorphism

{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}\cong H^{sus}_{0}(X)
. One therefore concludes that

{H^{sus}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite if
k
is either of the above three types.
Suppose now that
U
is a smooth quasi-projective
k
-scheme
which is not necessarily complete. One can then ask if

{H^{sus}_{0}(X)}/m
is finite. One can similarly ask
if
{{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)}/m
is finite if
D
is an effective Cartier divisor on
a smooth projective scheme
X
over
k
. To our knowledge, no answer is known about
these questions yet. We shall answer these in this section.

6.1.
A general finiteness result

In this subsection, we prove a general result about the finiteness of motivic
cohomology groups of possibly singular schemes.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
k
be either a finite, or a

-adic or an algebraically closed field.

be a smooth projective

Proof.

If

(the latter is Gabber-Kerz improved Milnor

We thus get an exact sequence

{\underset{y\in X_{(1)}}{\bigoplus}}\ {K^{M}_{i+1}(k(y))}/m\xrightarrow{\partial}{\underset{x\in X_{(0)}}{\bigoplus}}\ {K^{M}_{i}(k(x))}/m\to{{\rm CH}^{d+i}(X,i)}/m\to 0.

Suppose now that

p
-adic field with residue characteristic

We first assume

(see

follows from

[

We now assume

Let

6.2.
Proofs of parts (1) and (2) of Theorem

The following result generalizes the finiteness theorems of
Kato-Saito
[
28
]
and Saito-Sato

[
48
]
to non-complete schemes.
The question whether such a finiteness result would hold originated out of
a discussion between Colliot-Thélène and the authors.
In the two results below,
k
is either a finite, or a
p
-adic or an algebraically
closed field.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
U
be a smooth quasi-projective

-scheme of pure dimension

.
Then
{H^{sus}_{0}(U)}/m
is finite.

Proof.

We can assume that
U
is integral.
Let
p
be the exponential characteristic of
k
.
It suffices to show that
({H^{sus}_{0}(U)}/m)[\tfrac{1}{p}]
is finite.
We have seen in §
2.3
that

({H^{sus}_{0}(U)}/m)[\tfrac{1}{p}]
agrees with

({H^{2d}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}(d))}/m)[\tfrac{1}{p}]
.
The theorem is thus equivalent to the statement that

({H^{2d}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}(d))}/m)[\tfrac{1}{p}]
is finite.
By the universal coefficient theorem, we have an exact sequence

(6.8)

0\to({H^{2d}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}(d))}/m)[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to H^{2d}_{c}(U,\Lambda^{\prime}(d))[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to~_{m}H^{2d+1}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}(d)))[\tfrac{1}{p}].

We choose an open embedding
U\subset X
such that
X
is an integral
projective scheme of dimension
d
over
k
. We let
D=X\setminus U

with reduced closed subscheme structure.
We then have an exact sequence

H^{2d}(D,{\mathbb{Z}}(d)))[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to H^{2d+1}_{c}(U,{\mathbb{Z}}(d)))[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to H^{2d+1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}(d)))[\tfrac{1}{p}].

The two end terms are zero by
[

middle term. Using (

),
we are reduced to showing that

H^{2d}_{c}(U,\Lambda^{\prime}(d))[\tfrac{1}{p}]
is finite.
But this follows from the exact sequence

H^{2d-1}(D,\Lambda^{\prime}(d))[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to H^{2d}_{c}(U,\Lambda^{\prime}(d))[\tfrac{1}{p}]\to H^{2d}(X,\Lambda^{\prime}(d))[\tfrac{1}{p}]

and Proposition
6.2
.
∎

The second main result of this section is the following generalization of the
finiteness theorems of Kato-Saito
[

and Saito-Sato

Theorem 6.4

Let

such that
\pi_{*}({\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X^{\prime},D^{\prime}))\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,D)

(see
[
4
, Lem. 3.18]
). We conclude that

{\rm div}(f)={\rm div}(f)_{C}=\pi_{*}({\rm div}(f)_{C^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,D)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

8.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Let
k
be an infinite perfect field
and
X
a projective normal crossing variety of dimension
d\geq 0
over
k
.
We write
D
for
X_{\rm sing}
.

We shall now prove our final result which identifies the
motivic cohomology in the bi-degree
(2d,d)
of
X
to its Levine-Weibel Chow
group. One consequence of this is that it allows us to deduce that the
main result of
[
5
]
, which proved a restriction isomorphism for
relative 0-cycles in a projective and flat family over a Henselian
discrete valuation
ring, recovers the earlier result of
[
12
]
when the family is semi-stable,
i.e., the reduced special fiber is a simple normal crossing divisor.
This question was then raised by some of the authors of
[
12
]
.
The second consequence is that it allows one to describe the
motivic cohomology of a normal crossing variety in terms of 0-cycles
supported on
X_{\rm reg}
and only one type of relations, unlike in the
description given in
[
12
]
which uses two types of relations
(see below).

We shall need one more ingredient: a new Chow group of 0-cycles
introduced in
[
12
, § 2]
.
Let
{\mathcal{R}}^{EKW}_{0}(X,D)\subset{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)
be the subgroup generated
by
{\rm div}(f)
, where
f\in k(C)^{\times}
is a rational function on a curve

C\subset X
such that the pair
(C,f)
satisfies either of the conditions
(1) and (2) below.

(1)

C
is an integral curve not contained in
D
with normalization

\nu:C^{N}\to C\hookrightarrow X
and
f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C^{N},\nu^{*}(D)}
such that

f(x)=1
for all
x\in\nu^{*}(D)
.

(2)

C\subset X
is a snc subcurve and

f\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{C,(C\cap D)}
.

We let
{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)
denote the quotient of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)
by the subgroup

{\mathcal{R}}^{EKW}_{0}(X,D)
. This group was defined in
[
12
, § 2]
, where
it was denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. One of the main computational results of

[
12
]
is the comparison between the group
{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)[\tfrac{1}{p}]
and
the cdh motivic cohomology in bi-degree
(2d,d)
of the normal crossing variety

X
, where
p
is the exponential characteristic of
k
, see
[
12
, Thm. 7.1]
.
There is a canonical surjection

(8.3)

{\rm CH}_{0}^{snc}(X)\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}^{EKW}(X)

since both groups are quotients of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)
.

We now prove Theorem
1.6
. We restate it for convenience.

Theorem 8.4
.

Assume that the condition (
8.1
) holds.
Then the map
\lambda_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}\to H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))

is an isomorphism in the following cases.

(1)

{\rm char}(k)=p>0
and
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
.

(2)

m\in k^{\times}
and
\Lambda={{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
.

Proof.

It is shown in
[
12
, Thm 7.1]
that the maps

(8.4)

\Lambda\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{0}(k(x),\Lambda(0))\to H^{2d}_{c}(X\setminus D,\Lambda(d))\to H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))

for
x\in X\setminus D
induce a map

\gamma_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}\xrightarrow{\simeq}H^{2d}(X,\Lambda(d))
. Moreover, this is an isomorphism.
We remark here that in op. cit., Definition
2.11
is used for
normal crossing varieties which is a stronger notion than the one we use.
However, the reader can easily check that the proof of
[
12
, Thm 7.1]

only uses our weaker notion. It suffices now to show that
there is a canonical surjection
\psi_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}
such that
\gamma_{X}=\lambda_{X}\circ\psi_{X}
.

To show that the identity map of
{\mathcal{Z}}_{0}(X,D)_{\Lambda}
descends to
\psi_{X}
,
we let
C\subset X
be a curve and
f\in k(C)^{\times}
a rational function.
We first observe that if the pair
(C,f)
is of type (1) above, then

C
must be contained in
one and only one irreducible component
Y
of
X
. Moreover, for
this component
Y
, the intersection
E=D\cap Y
must be a simple normal
crossing divisor on
Y
. We now conclude easily from Theorems
1.2

and
8.3
that
\psi_{X}({\rm div}(f))\in{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,D)_{\Lambda}
.
If
(C,f)
is of type (2) above, then we get

\psi_{X}({\rm div}(f))\in{\mathcal{R}}^{LW}_{0}(X,D)_{\Lambda}

by Lemma
7.6
. We have thus shown that
\psi_{X}\colon{\rm CH}^{EKW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}\twoheadrightarrow{\rm CH}^{LW}_{0}(X)_{\Lambda}
is defined.
It is clear from (
8.4
) that

\gamma_{X}=\lambda_{X}\circ\psi_{X}
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

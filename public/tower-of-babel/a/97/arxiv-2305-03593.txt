Emergent world-sheet for the AdS Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude
Luis F. Alday, Tobias Hansen, Joao A. Silva

Abstract
We construct a representation for the first A ​ d ​ S AdS curvature correction to the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude, as an integral over the Riemann sphere. The integrand is that of the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space, with the extra insertion of a linear combination of single-valued multiple polylogarithms of weight three. The integral representation implies an elegant, manifestly single-valued representation for the Wilson coefficients of the low energy expansion.



The idea.
In this letter we study the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude for the scattering of four gravitons on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. This is defined, via the
AdS/CFT
correspondence, as the correlator of four stress-tensor multiplets in Mellin space, to leading order in inverse powers of the central charge. The Borel transform of the Mellin amplitude reduces to the usual Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in the flat space limit, plus a tower of curvature corrections

A(S,T)=A^{(0)}(S,T)+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{R^{2}}A^{(1)}(S,T)+\cdots

(1)

The Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space

A^{(0)}(S,T)=-\frac{\Gamma(-S)\Gamma(-T)\Gamma(-U)}{\Gamma(S+1)\Gamma(T+1)\Gamma(U+1)},

(2)

where
S+T+U=0
, admits a low energy expansion

A^{(0)}(S,T)=\frac{1}{STU}+2\sum_{a,b=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{2}^{a}\sigma_{3}^{b}\alpha_{a,b}^{(0)},

(3)

with
\sigma_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(S^{2}+T^{2}+U^{2})
and
\sigma_{3}=STU
. It turns out
Stieberger:2013wea
that the Wilson coefficients
\alpha_{a,b}^{(0)}
live in the ring of single-valued multiple zeta values (MZVs)
Brown:2013gia
. This is manifest in the representation

A^{(0)}(S,T)=\frac{\exp\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\frac{\zeta^{\text{sv}}(2n+1)(S^{2n+1}+T^{2n+1}+U^{2n+1})}{2n+1}}\right)}{STU},

(4)

where
\zeta^{\text{sv}}(2n+1)=2\zeta(2n+1)
are single-valued zeta values. A direct world-sheet computation leads instead to the following representation as an integral over the Riemann sphere

A^{(0)}(S,T)=\frac{1}{U^{2}}\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2},

(5)

where
z
denotes the complex cross-ratio on the four-punctured sphere and the integration measure is defined as
d^{2}z=dzd\bar{z}/(-2\pi i)
.
It was understood in
Brown:2019wna
;
Schlotterer:2018zce
;
Vanhove:2018elu
that the reason for
\alpha_{a,b}^{(0)}
being in the ring of single-valued MZVs is the single-valued nature
Brown:2018omk
of the integral (
5
).

The focus of this letter will be the first correction
A^{(1)}(S,T)
. In
Alday:2022uxp
;
Alday:2022xwz
the low energy expansion for
A^{(1)}(S,T)
was computed by leveraging Regge boundedness to derive dispersive sum rules and assuming single-valuedness to solve them. The result takes the form

A^{(1)}(S,T)=-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{3}^{2}}+2\sum_{a,b=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{2}^{a}\sigma_{3}^{b}\alpha_{a,b}^{(1)},

(6)

where the Wilson coefficients
\alpha_{a,b}^{(1)}
live in the ring of single-valued MZVs by construction, justified by the expectation that closed string amplitudes like
A^{(1)}(S,T)
arise from world-sheet integrals similar to (
5
). The aim of this letter is to construct such an explicit integral representation for
A^{(1)}(S,T)
, of the form

A^{(1)}(S,T)=B^{(1)}(S,T)+B^{(1)}(U,T)+B^{(1)}(S,U),

(7)

where
B^{(1)}(S,T)
is symmetric and given by

B^{(1)}(S,T)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}G(z,\bar{z}).

(8)

Following the discussion in
Vanhove:2018elu
we construct
G(z,\bar{z})
out of single-valued multiple polylogarithms (SVMPLs). This ensures that the low energy expansion of
A^{(1)}(S,T)
contains only single-valued MZVs. Given that the coincident limit of insertion points in a putative world-sheet correspond to
z\to\{0,1,\infty\}
, we use polylogarithms evaluated at
z
and labelled by words
w
with letters
\{0,1\}
. Furthermore, the structure of the Wilson coefficients
\alpha_{a,b}^{(1)}
found in
Alday:2022uxp
;
Alday:2022xwz
, together with the order of the poles in
A^{(1)}(S,T)
(of fourth order and lower) imply that the SVMPLs that appear have at most weight three. We also present a world-sheet motivation for this fact. It turns out the final integral representation is constructed out of SVMPLs of exactly weight three.

Single-valued multiple polylogarithms.
Multiple polylogarithms (MPLs, also known as harmonic polylogarithms) are functions
L_{w}(z)
of one variable labelled by a word
w
formed in our case by the ’letters’ 0 and 1. They are recursively defined by the relations

\partial_{z}L_{0w}(z)=\frac{1}{z}L_{w}(z),~~~~\partial_{z}L_{1w}(z)=\frac{1}{z-1}L_{w}(z),

(9)

together with
L_{\varnothing}(z)=1
, where
\varnothing
denotes the empty word, and the condition that
\lim_{z\to 0}L_{w}(z)=0
for
w
not of the form
0^{p}
. For
w=0^{p}
we get
L_{0^{p}}(z)=\frac{\log^{p}z}{p!}
. MPLs satisfy shuffle relations

L_{w}(z)L_{w^{\prime}}(z)=\sum_{W\in w\shuffle w^{\prime}}L_{W}(z).

(10)

Evaluated at
z=1
MPLs reduce to MZVs, for instance

L_{0^{s_{1}-1}10^{s_{2}-1}1\cdots 0^{s_{d}-1}1}(1)=\zeta(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d}).

(11)

In the standard notation

\zeta(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{s_{d}}},

(12)

where
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}
is the weight, which agrees with the length
|w|
, and
d
is the depth. When the word starts with
1
,
L_{w}(1)
is generally divergent. A regularised value can be assigned by using the shuffle relations to isolate the divergent contribution, and then setting
L_{1}(1)=\zeta(1)=0
(which is equivalent to setting
\log 0=0
). As a consequence of (
10
) MZVs also satisfy shuffle relations. MPLs have branch points at
z=0,1,\infty
. A single-valued version of MPLs was constructed in
Brown:2004ugm
via a single-valued map
L_{w}(z)\to{\cal L}_{w}(z)
such that
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
is a single-valued weight-preserving linear combination of
L_{w^{\prime}}(z)L_{w^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{z})
and satisfies the same differential relations

\partial_{z}{\cal L}_{0w}(z)=\frac{1}{z}{\cal L}_{w}(z),~~\partial_{z}{\cal L}_{1w}(z)=\frac{1}{z-1}{\cal L}_{w}(z),

(13)

such that
{\cal L}_{\varnothing}(z)=1
,
{\cal L}_{0^{p}}(z)=\frac{\log^{p}|z|^{2}}{p!}
and
\lim_{z\to 0}{\cal L}_{w}(z)=0
for
w
not of the form
0^{p}
. Furthermore they also satisfy the same shuffle relations

{\cal L}_{w}(z){\cal L}_{w^{\prime}}(z)=\sum_{W\in w\shuffle w^{\prime}}{\cal L}_{W}(z).

(14)

Evaluated at
z=1
they give rise to single-valued MZVs. For instance

{\cal L}_{0^{k_{1}-1}10^{k_{2}-1}1\cdots 0^{k_{d}-1}1}(1)=\zeta^{\text{sv}}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}).

(15)

Single-valued MZVs are a subset of the standard MZVs and satisfy several relations, including the same shuffle relations. Furthermore, one can check

\zeta^{\text{sv}}(2n)=0,~~~\zeta^{\text{sv}}(2n+1)=2\zeta(2n+1).

(16)

For higher depths the single-valued map acts in a complicated way, but can be computed for instance with the program HyperlogProcedures
HyperlogProcedures
.

Integrating SVMPLs.
Next we consider the integrals

I_{w}(S,T)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}{\cal L}_{w}(z),

(17)

and compute their low energy expansion.
Vanhove:2018elu
have considered the low energy expansion for the particular case
{\cal L}_{\varnothing}(z)=1
in great detail, and developed a machinery to treat the general case. In particular we will use the following two key results. First, given any SVMPL we can write

\frac{{\cal L}_{w}(z)}{|z|^{2}|1-z|^{2}}=\partial_{z}F_{w}(z),

(18)

where
F_{w}(z)
is given by

F_{w}(z)=\frac{{\cal L}_{0w}(z)-{\cal L}_{1w}(z)}{\bar{z}(1-\bar{z})}.

(19)

This follows from the differential relations that SVMPLs satisfy. The second result regards the integration of SVMPLs. For the case at hand

\int d^{2}z\partial_{z}F(z)=\overline{Res}_{z=\infty}F(z)-\overline{Res}_{0}F(z)-\overline{Res}_{1}F(z).

(20)

This follows from Stokes theorem
Schnetz:2013hqa
. Here the residues around infinity, zero and one are defined by the expansions around these points. For SVMPLs we have

{\cal L}_{w}(z)=\sum_{m,n,k}c_{kmn}(z-\sigma)^{m}(\bar{z}-\bar{\sigma})^{n}\log^{k}|z-\sigma|^{2}.

(21)

The anti-holomorphic residue around a point
z=\sigma
is defined as the coefficient
c_{0,0,-1}
.

Let us now turn to the problem at hand. Following
Vanhove:2018elu
we split the integral above into two contributions

\displaystyle I^{(1)}_{w}(S,T)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}z\frac{(|z|^{-2S}-1)(|1-z|^{-2T}-1)}{|z|^{2}|1-z|^{2}}{\cal L}_{w}(z),

\displaystyle I^{(2)}_{w}(S,T)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}z\frac{|z|^{-2S}+|1-z|^{-2T}-1}{|z|^{2}|1-z|^{2}}{\cal L}_{w}(z).

(22)

Let us consider the first contribution. This is absolutely convergent at
S=T=0
, and hence we can Taylor expand around that point, and exchange summation and integration. This leads to

I^{(1)}_{w}(S,T)=\sum_{p,q=1}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\int d^{2}z\frac{{\cal L}_{0^{p}}(z){\cal L}_{1^{q}}(z){\cal L}_{w}(z)}{|z|^{2}|1-z|^{2}}.

(23)

Using the shuffle product we write

{\cal L}_{0^{p}}(z){\cal L}_{1^{q}}(z){\cal L}_{w}(z)=\sum_{W\in 0^{p}\shuffle 1^{q}\shuffle w}{\cal L}_{W}(z).

(24)

Then, using the two key results above we obtain

I^{(1)}_{w}(S,T)=\sum_{p,q=1}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\hskip-13.0pt\sum_{W\in 0^{p}\shuffle 1^{q}\shuffle w}\hskip-13.0pt\left({\cal L}_{0W}(1)-{\cal L}_{1W}(1)\right).

(25)

This generalises in an obvious way the result by
Vanhove:2018elu
valid for
w=\varnothing
. Let us now turn to the second contribution. Following again
Vanhove:2018elu
we consider

I^{(2),\epsilon}_{w}(S,T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\geq 0\\
p.q=0\end{subarray}}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\sum_{W}\int_{U_{\epsilon}}\partial_{z}F_{W}(z)d^{2}z,

(26)

where
W\in 0^{p}\shuffle 1^{q}\shuffle w
and
U_{\epsilon}=\mathbb{C}\setminus(B_{0}(\epsilon)\cup B_{1}(\epsilon)\cup B_{0}(\epsilon^{-1}))
is the complex plane minus three balls, around zero, one and infinity. Clearly
I^{(2)}_{w}(S,T)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}I^{(2),\epsilon}_{w}(S,T)
. By Stokes theorem this receives only contributions from the three boundaries

\displaystyle I^{(2)}_{w}(S,T)={}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\geq 0\\
p.q=0\end{subarray}}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\sum\limits_{W}\bigg(\hskip-8.0pt\int\limits_{\partial^{+}B_{0}(\epsilon^{-1})}\hskip-8.0ptF_{W}(z)\frac{id\bar{z}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\hskip-8.0pt\int\limits_{\partial^{-}B_{0}(\epsilon)}\hskip-8.0ptF_{W}(z)\frac{id\bar{z}}{2\pi}+\hskip-8.0pt\int\limits_{\partial^{-}B_{1}(\epsilon)}\hskip-8.0ptF_{W}(z)\frac{id\bar{z}}{2\pi}\bigg),

(27)

with
F_{W}(z)=\frac{{\cal L}_{0W}(z)-{\cal L}_{1W}(z)}{\bar{z}(1-\bar{z})}
. Each boundary can be analysed separately. Let us assume for simplicity
w
is not of the form
0^{n}
, and return to this case later. In this case the contribution from the boundary at zero vanishes, and the same is true for the boundary at infinity. The contribution from
\partial^{-}B_{1}(\epsilon)
is slightly more subtle and in order to compute it we need the behaviour of
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
around
z=1
. For
w=1^{n}
we have
{\cal L}_{1^{n}}(1+\epsilon)=\frac{\log^{n}|\epsilon|^{2}}{n!}
. For
w
starting with a
0
we have

{\cal L}_{0w^{\prime}}(1+\epsilon)={\cal L}_{0w^{\prime}}(1)+{\cal O}(\epsilon),

(28)

for finite
{\cal L}_{0w^{\prime}}(1)
given in terms of single-valued MZVs. When the word starts with
1
we can use the shuffle identities to isolate and compute its logarithmic divergences as we approach
z=1
. Assume for instance
w=10w^{\prime}
, then

{\cal L}_{1}(z){\cal L}_{0w^{\prime}}(z)={\cal L}_{w}(z)+\sum_{W\in 1\shuffle w^{\prime}}{\cal L}_{0W}(z),

(29)

which leads to

{\cal L}_{10w^{\prime}}(1+\epsilon)=\log|\epsilon|^{2}{\cal L}_{0w^{\prime}}(1)-\sum_{W\in 1\shuffle w^{\prime}}{\cal L}_{0W}(1)+{\cal O}(\epsilon).

(30)

Applying this idea recursively, we can compute the logarithmic behaviour, around
z=1
, for any word. The contribution from the boundary at
1
will be of the form

I^{(2)}_{w}(S,T)=\text{polar}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p,q\geq 0\\
p.q=0\end{subarray}}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\hskip-15.0pt\sum_{W\in 0^{p}\shuffle 1^{q}\shuffle w}\hskip-15.0pt\left({\cal L}_{0W}(1)-{\cal L}_{1W}(1)\right).

(31)

Combining both contributions we obtain

I_{w}(S,T)=\text{polar}+\sum_{p,q=0}(-S)^{p}(-T)^{q}\hskip-15.0pt\sum_{W\in 0^{p}\shuffle 1^{q}\shuffle w}\hskip-15.0pt\left({\cal L}_{0W}(1)-{\cal L}_{1W}(1)\right).

(32)

This result is valid for arbitrary
w
. The polar contribution arises from logarithmic divergences either around
z=0
, for
w=0^{n}
or around
z=1
, as explained above. For example

\text{polar}(0^{n})=-\frac{1}{S^{n+1}},~~~\text{polar}(1^{n})=-\frac{1}{T^{n+1}}.

(33)

In general, a logarithmic divergence
{\cal L}_{1^{q}}(1+\epsilon)
leads to a polar term
-1/{T^{q+1}}
. Below we will need
I_{w}(S,T)
for weight three. An example of this is

I_{101}(S,T)=\frac{4\zeta(3)}{T}+2(2T-S)\zeta(5)+2(4T^{2}+4ST-S^{2})\zeta(3)^{2}+\cdots

(34)

In particular we see the generic term is an homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
times a (single-valued) MZV of weight
n+4
. This is true for all other cases of weight three, as well as for
\zeta(3)I_{\varnothing}(S,T)
.

Integral representation for
A^{(1)}(S,T)
.
The coefficients in the low energy expansion of
I_{w}(S,T)
are in the ring of single-valued MZVs. Hence, we will attempt to construct an integral representation for
A^{(1)}(S,T)
such that

A^{(1)}(S,T)=B^{(1)}(S,T)+B^{(1)}(U,T)+B^{(1)}(S,U),

(35)

where
B^{(1)}(S,T)
is symmetric and of the form

B^{(1)}(S,T)=\sum_{w}R_{w}(S,T)I_{w}(S,T),

(36)

for some rational functions
R_{w}(S,T)
. From the low energy expansion for
A^{(1)}(S,T)

A^{(1)}(S,T)=-\frac{2}{3}\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{3}^{2}}-\frac{44}{3}\zeta(3)^{2}\sigma_{2}-\frac{537}{8}\zeta(7)\sigma_{3}+\cdots

(37)

we see that the generic term is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
n
times MZVs of weight
n+4
. This suggests
w
in our ansatz should have weight three and the rational functions be the ratio of two homogeneous polynomials of the same degree. This is confirmed by the pole structure of
A^{(1)}(S,T)
with quartic (and lower) order poles. Indeed, note that the insertion of
\log^{3}|z|^{2}
is equivalent to taking
\partial_{S}^{3}
, which increases the degree of the single poles of
A^{(0)}(S,T)
by three.

Next, note that due to the
z\leftrightarrow\bar{z}
symmetry of the integrand, the integral
I_{w}(S,T)
is only sensitive to the symmetric part of
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
. Using the explicit expressions for
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
for weight three, given in the appendix, this implies

I_{011}(S,T)=I_{110}(S,T),~~~I_{001}(S,T)=I_{100}(S,T).

(38)

This reduces the number of independent integrals
I_{w}(S,T)
at weight three from eight to six. Next, we want to construct a function
B^{(1)}(S,T)
which is symmetric under
S\leftrightarrow T
. By a change of variables
z\to 1-z
we see

I_{w}(T,S)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}{\cal L}_{w}(1-z).

(39)

SVMPLs are closed under this transformation. For weight three

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{000}(1-z)

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{111}(z),

(40)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{001}(1-z)

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{110}(z)-2\zeta(3),

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{010}(1-z)

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{101}(z)+4\zeta(3),

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{100}(1-z)

\displaystyle={\cal L}_{011}(z)-2\zeta(3),

so that for instance
I_{001}(T,S)=I_{110}(S,T)-2\zeta(3)I_{\varnothing}(S,T)
and so on. Note furthermore that
I_{\varnothing}(S,T)
is itself symmetric. We then try the following ansatz

\displaystyle B^{(1)}(S,T)={}

\displaystyle R_{0}(S,T)I_{000}(S,T)+R_{0}(T,S)I_{111}(S,T)

(41)

\displaystyle+R_{1}(S,T)I_{010}(S,T)+R_{1}(T,S)I_{101}(S,T)

\displaystyle+R_{2}(S,T)I_{001}(S,T)+R_{2}(T,S)I_{110}(S,T)

\displaystyle+\left(R_{\text{asym}}(S,T)+R_{\text{sym}}(S,T)\right)\zeta(3)I_{\varnothing}(S,T),

where based on the integral representation for
A^{(0)}(S,T)
we propose rational functions of the form
R_{i}(S,T)=U^{-2}P^{(2)}_{i}(S,T)
, for
P^{(2)}_{i}(S,T)
homogeneous polynomials of degree 2. In the above ansatz
R_{\text{asym}}(S,T)=2(R_{1}(T,S)-R_{1}(S,T))-R_{2}(T,S)+R_{2}(S,T)
is an anti-symmetric function fixed so that
B^{(1)}(S,T)
is symmetric. Finally
R_{\text{sym}}(S,T)
cannot be fixed by our procedure, and we set it to zero. In total our ansatz contains only 9 coefficients, which are fixed by matching the low energy expansion with
A^{(1)}(S,T)
. We find the following remarkably simple solution

\displaystyle R_{0}(S,T)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2S}{3(S+T)},

\displaystyle R_{1}(S,T)

\displaystyle=-\frac{S+5T}{6(S+T)},

(42)

\displaystyle R_{2}(S,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(S-T)}{3(S+T)},

\displaystyle R_{\text{asym}}(S,T)

\displaystyle=0.

Structure of poles.

A^{(1)}(S,T)
has a very interesting structure of poles that we would like to reproduce from its integral representation. Let us write

B^{(1)}(S,T)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}G(S,T,z),

(43)

where for the discussion below it is important to include the explicit dependence of the integrand on
S,T
. By a change of variables it is easy to see that

\displaystyle B^{(1)}(U,T)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}|z|^{2}G(U,T,1/z),

\displaystyle B^{(1)}(S,U)

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}|1-z|^{2}G(S,U,\tfrac{z}{z-1}).

(44)

The full expression is then

A^{(1)}(S,T)=\int d^{2}z|z|^{-2S-2}|1-z|^{-2T-2}G_{\text{tot}}(S,T,z),

(45)

with

\displaystyle G_{\text{tot}}(S,T,z)={}

\displaystyle G(S,T,z)+|z|^{2}G(U,T,1/z)

(46)

\displaystyle+|1-z|^{2}G(S,U,\tfrac{z}{z-1}).

Note that single-valued polylogarithms are closed under
z\to 1/z
and
z\to\frac{z}{z-1}
so the above expression can again be expressed in terms of
{\cal L}_{w}(z)
. We are interested in computing the poles at
S=0,1,\dots
. These arise from the region of integration around
z=0
and can be computed by expanding around this point and using polar coordinates. Around
z=0

|1-z|^{-2T-2}G_{\text{tot}}(S,T,z)=\frac{S^{2}}{9T(S+T)}\log^{3}|z|^{2}+\cdots

(47)

The leading order term leads to poles at
S=0

\displaystyle\left.A^{(1)}(S,T)\right|_{S=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{0}}\rho d\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{\pi}\rho^{-2S-2}\frac{S^{2}}{9T(S+T)}\log^{3}\rho^{2}

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3S^{2}T^{2}}+\frac{2}{3ST^{3}}+\text{regular},

which is the correct behaviour for
A^{(1)}(S,T)
around
S=0
. Keeping higher order terms in the expansion (
47
) we can compute poles at
S=1,2,\cdots
, for instance, to next order we obtain

A^{(1)}(S,T)=\frac{-1}{(S-1)^{4}}+\frac{2/3}{(S-1)^{3}}+\frac{2/3}{(S-1)^{2}}-\frac{2\zeta(3)-1}{S-1}+\text{reg.}

(49)

which again agrees with the expected terms. The following comment is in order. From the computation in
Alday:2022uxp
;
Alday:2022xwz
it turns out that quartic and cubic poles follow from the spectrum at leading order, which is the flat space string spectrum. As a result, quartic and cubic poles have a very simple form. It turns out however, that matching these poles is not enough to fix the form of our answer.
Instead, some of the terms in the ansatz (
41
) depend on the
AdS
corrections to the OPE data, which was determined in
Alday:2022xwz
.

A world-sheet perspective.
Although a direct world-sheet computation for string amplitudes on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
is out of reach at the moment, we can understand the main feature of our result from the following world-sheet model. Consider the first
AdS
curvature correction to the flat-space tree-level four-graviton amplitude. Around flat space the
AdS
metric takes the form
g_{\mu\nu}(X)=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{h_{\mu\nu}}{R^{2}}+\cdots
with
h_{\mu\nu}\sim X_{\mu}X_{\nu}
. Hence, one of the contributions to such a curvature correction is given by the integrated insertion of an extra graviton vertex operator with polarisation vector
h_{\mu\nu}

h_{\mu\nu}\sim X_{\mu}X_{\nu}\sim\lim_{P_{g}\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{P_{g}^{\mu}}\partial_{P_{g}^{\nu}}}e^{iP_{g}\cdot X},

(50)

where
P_{g}
is the momentum of the inserted graviton. We are then led to compute

\lim_{P_{g}\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{P_{g}^{\mu}}\partial_{P_{g}^{\nu}}}\int d^{2}u\langle V_{1}(0)V_{2}(1)V_{3}(\infty)V_{4}(z)V_{g}^{\mu\nu}(u)\rangle.

(51)

The superstring amplitude for the scattering of five gravitons can be written in terms of a set of building blocks, see
Schlotterer:2012ny
. A prototype of the integrals to compute is

\lim_{P_{g}\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{P_{g}^{\mu}}\partial_{P_{g}^{\nu}}}{\cal J}(z,\bar{z}),

(52)

where

{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})=\int d^{2}u\frac{|u|^{2a}|1-u|^{2b}|u-z|^{2c}}{|u|^{2}|1-u|^{2}}.

(53)

The aim is to compute
{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})
as an expansion for small
(a,b,c)=\alpha^{\prime}(P_{1}\cdot P_{g},P_{2}\cdot P_{g},P_{4}\cdot P_{g})
to quadratic order. In
Vanhove:2020qtt
this integral was written in terms of products of holomorphic/anti-holomorphic integrals

{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})=-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\kappa_{1}J_{1}(z)J_{1}(\bar{z})+\kappa_{2}J_{2}(z)J_{2}(\bar{z})\right),

(54)

where
\kappa_{1}=\sin(\pi a)\csc(\pi(b+c))\sin(\pi(a+b+c))
,
\kappa_{2}=\sin(\pi b)\sin(\pi c)\csc(\pi(b+c))
and

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}(-u)^{a-1}(1-u)^{b-1}(z-u)^{c}du,

(55)

\displaystyle J_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{z}^{1}u^{a-1}(1-u)^{b-1}(u-z)^{c}du.

These integrals can be solved in terms of hypergeometric functions and expanded for small
a,b,c

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{c}{a}\log z+\cdots,

(56)

\displaystyle J_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}+\frac{b+c}{c}\log(1-z)+\cdots.

Expanding
{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})
for small
a,b,c
we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}-\frac{c}{a}{\cal L}_{0}(z)-\frac{c}{b}{\cal L}_{1}(z)

(57)

\displaystyle+c\left({\cal L}_{0}(z){\cal L}_{1}(z)-\frac{a+c}{2a}{\cal L}^{2}_{0}(z)-\frac{b+c}{2b}{\cal L}^{2}_{1}(z)\right)+\cdots,

so that at
n-th
order in
a,b,c
we obtain SVMPLs of weight
n+1
. This implies

\lim_{P_{g}\to 0}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{P_{g}^{\mu}}\partial_{P_{g}^{\nu}}}{\cal J}(z,\bar{z})=\text{div.}+d\,\zeta_{3}+\sum_{i,j,k=0,1}d_{ijk}{\cal L}_{ijk}(z).

(58)

One shouldn’t worry about the divergent contribution, since this is only a small part of the computation. At finite order we get a combination of SVMPLs of weight three, exactly as in our ansatz. We expect other contributions to behave in the same way.

Discussion.
In this letter we have given an integral representation for the first
AdS
curvature correction to the Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space. It takes the form of the genus zero world-sheet integral for the usual Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude in flat space with the extra insertion of single-valued multiple polylogarithms of weight three. This feature can be understood from a simple world-sheet model. Going to higher orders, it would be interesting to understand if a similar representation still holds, and what is the relevant space of single-valued functions. In the simplest scenario, at the next order SVMPLs of weight six would suffice. A direct world-sheet computation from string theory on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
is out of reach at the moment. However, over the last years there has been promising progress in the construction of vertex operators in the pure spinor formalism
Berkovits:2019rwq
;
Fleury:2021ieo
. Note that our final result is remarkably simple, and it should serve as a guiding target towards defining and computing graviton amplitudes in the pure spinor formalism.

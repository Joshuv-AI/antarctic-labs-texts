Deformations of exterior differential ideals and applications.
Fernando Cukierman, César Massri

Abstract
. We develop some basic facts on deformations of exterior differential ideals on a smooth complex algebraic variety. With these tools we study deformations of several types of differential ideals, leading to several irreducible components of the corresponding moduli spaces.

Introduction.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over the complex numbers. A sheaf of differential ideals
\mathrm{I}\subseteq\Omega_{X}
is a
graded sheaf of coherent ideals of the exterior algebra
\Omega_{X}
such that
d\mathrm{I}\subseteq\mathrm{I}
. We shall study infinitesimal
deformations of
\mathrm{I}
as a differential graded ideal of
\Omega_{X}
. This will follow the well developed
deformation theory; see for example
[
30
]
,
[
48
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
51
]
.

One point to take into account is the slight non-commutativity of
\Omega_{X}
. There exists a general theory of Hilbert schemes over
non-commutative rings in
[
1
]
which amply covers the case of an anti-commutative sheaf of rings like
\Omega_{X}
.
Another less trivial point relevant for us is the condition on the ideals of being
stable under exterior derivative, that is, the integrability condition. Our results will be formally the same as the known tangent-obstruction theory for Hilbert schemes (see e. g.
[
16
]
,
[
1
]
), but working in the Herrera-Lieberman category of differential complexes
[
31
]
,
[
37
]
, which takes the integrability condition into account.

Of special relevance will be the complex
\Omega_{X}/\mathrm{I}
which may be thought of as the De Rham complex of the super-commutative subscheme defined by the ideal

\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
. This or similar complexes appeared before, for instance, in
[
20
]
as the
leaf complex
, and in
[
8
]
in connection with the
characteristic cohomology
.

With these tools at our disposal we are able to study the deformation theory of differential ideals of various special types. This allows the construction of new irreducible components of moduli spaces of differential ideals, and of singular foliations.

Let us summarize the contents of the different sections. In Section 1 we collect several basic definitions and preliminary facts on differential graded modules over
\Omega_{X}
. In Section 2 we work out the basic theory of infinitesimal deformations and obstructions for differential graded ideals. In Section 3 we develop an alternative set-up for working with differential graded ideals on projective varieties, generalizing the Serre correspondence between coherent sheaves and graded modules.
In Sections 4, 5 and 6 we review several known facts and we give some new technical calculations relevant to the geometry of moduli spaces of exterior differential ideals. In Section 5 we prove Theorem
50
on the vanishing of
\mathcal{E}xt
for coherent sheaves, which seems to be new.
Section 7 contains our first application of the previous theory, Theorem (A)
54
, which gives sufficient conditions for the stability of some types of singular Pfaff ideals. More precisely, we consider singular Pfaff ideals
\mathrm{I}=\langle\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{q}\rangle
generated by twisted one-forms
\omega_{i}
that vary in respective irreducible components
\mathcal{F}_{i}
of the space of integrable one-forms. More generally, we study the stability of singular Pfaff ideals of the form
\mathrm{I}=\mathrm{I}_{1}+\dots+\mathrm{I}_{q}
where each
\mathrm{I}_{i}
is a singular Pfaff ideal, general element of a component
\mathcal{F}_{i}
of the space of singular Pfaff ideals.

Theorem (A)
54
is of a general nature, and in order to apply it in examples one needs to verify the surjectivity stated in hypothesis b). This surjectivity follows if the two obstruction spaces defined in (7.2) are zero. In (7.3) and (7.4) we prove that these two spaces are actually zero, under certain more manageable hypothesis. The result obtained is stated in Theorem (B)
68
. In Section 8 we discuss several applications.

1.2.
Pfaff ideals.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible algebraic variety.
Now we focus on exterior differential ideals, that is, differential graded submodules
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
.
For
r\in\mathbb{N}
denote
\mathrm{I}^{r}\subset\Omega^{r}_{X}
the homogeneous component of degree
r
of
\mathrm{I}
. See
[
7
]
for more information.

3 Definition
.

Let
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
be a differential graded ideal.

a) We say that
\mathrm{I}
is
generated in degree one
, or that it is a
Pfaff ideal
,
if it is generated, as a differential graded ideal, by its homogeneous component of degree one
\mathrm{I}^{1}\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}
.
Equivalently,
\mathrm{I}^{r}=\Omega_{X}^{r-1}\wedge\mathrm{I}^{1}+\Omega_{X}^{r-2}\wedge d(\mathrm{I}^{1})
for all
r
.
Notice that, conversely, if
\mathrm{I}^{1}
is a coherent subsheaf of
\Omega_{X}^{1}
, the last formula defines a differential graded ideal
\mathrm{I}
generated by
\mathrm{I}^{1}
.

b) We say that
\mathrm{I}
is an
integrable Pfaff ideal
if it is generated in degree one and
d(\mathrm{I}^{1})\subset\Omega_{X}^{1}\wedge\mathrm{I}^{1}
.
Notice that in this case we have
\mathrm{I}^{r}=\Omega_{X}^{r-1}\wedge\mathrm{I}^{1}
for all
r
.

c) If
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
is a differential graded ideal, we say it is a
singular integrable Pfaff ideal

if
\mathrm{I}
is generated in degree one and
d(\mathrm{I}^{1}|_{U})\subset\Omega_{U}^{1}\wedge\mathrm{I}^{1}|_{U}

for some (Zariski) open dense subset
U\subset X
. Notice that then
\mathrm{I}|_{U}
is an integrable Pfaff ideal on
U
.

d) Similarly, a differential graded
\Omega_{X}
-Module
F
is
generated in degree one

if
F^{r}=\Omega_{X}^{r-1}\wedge F^{1}+\Omega_{X}^{r-2}\wedge d(F^{1})
for all
r
.
The other definitions above extend similarly to
\Omega_{X}
-Modules.

e) If
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
is a differential graded ideal, we say that
\mathrm{I}
is locally-free (resp.
reflexive
) if it is locally-free as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-Module (resp. reflexive, that is, isomorphic to its double dual). Equivalently, if
\mathrm{I}^{r}
is a locally-free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-Module (resp. reflexive) for all
r
.

4 Definition
.

Let
F
be a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-Modules. There exists a non-empty Zariski open
U\subset X
such that
F|_{U}
is locally free, of rank

.
Taking the largest such

\text{Sing}(F)
of

may be characterized as
the union for

of the supports of the coherent sheaves

, see
[
45
]
or
[

(III, Ex. 6.6).
Alternatively,

is the closed set defined by the

[

]

[

[
6

If
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{X}
is a differential ideal, from the exact sequence

of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-Modules, and assuming

and call this set the singular set of the ideal

For instance, if

are one-forms in

\mathrm{I}=\langle\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{q}\rangle

the differential graded ideal generated by

Then

is an integrable Pfaff ideal when

2.
Deformations of exterior differential ideals.

2.1.
Infinitesimal deformations.

6 Definition
.

A
family of differential graded ideals parametrized by a scheme
S
consists of a morphism of schemes
f:\mathcal{X}\to S
and
a coherent subsheaf
\mathrm{I}\subset\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}
which is a differential graded ideal of the differential graded algebra

\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}

\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}/\mathrm{I}
is flat over

In this article we shall focus on families of differential graded ideals as above such that
\mathcal{X}=X\times S

is the canonical projection,
that is, the ambient variety
X
does not deform.
We refer to these as families of differential graded ideals
on
X

In order to work out the infinitesimal deformation theory of exterior differential ideals, let us start with two lemmas.

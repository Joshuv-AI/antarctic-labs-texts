Xizhi Liu, Dhruv Mubayi, Christian Reiher

Abstract
. The feasible region Ω ind ​ ( F ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) of a graph F F is the collection of points ( x , y ) (x,y) in the unit square such that there exists a sequence of graphs whose edge densities approach x x and whose induced F F -densities approach y y . A complete description of Ω ind ​ ( F ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) is not known for any F F with at least four vertices that is not a clique or an independent set. The feasible region provides a lot of combinatorial information about F F . For example, the supremum of y y over all ( x , y ) ∈ Ω ind ​ ( F ) (x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) is the inducibility of F F and Ω ind ​ ( K r ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(K_{r}) yields the Kruskal-Katona and clique density theorems. We begin a systematic study of Ω ind ​ ( F ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) by proving some general statements about the shape of Ω ind ​ ( F ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) and giving results for some specific graphs F F . Many of our theorems apply to the more general setting of quantum graphs. For example, we prove a bound for quantum graphs that generalizes an old result of Bollobás for the number of cliques in a graph with given edge density. We also consider the problems of determining Ω ind ​ ( F ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F) when F = K r − F=K_{r}^{-} , F F is a star, or F F is a complete bipartite graph. In the case of K r − K_{r}^{-} our results sharpen those predicted by the edge-statistics conjecture of Alon et. al. while also extending a theorem of Hirst for K 4 − K_{4}^{-} that was proved using computer aided techniques and flag algebras. The case of the 4-cycle seems particularly interesting and we conjecture that Ω ind ​ ( C 4 ) \Omega_{{\rm ind}}(C_{4}) is determined by the solution to the triangle density problem, which has been solved by Razborov.

1.1.
Feasible regions

Given a graph
G
denote by
V(G)
and
E(G)
the vertex set and edge set of
G
respectively.
Let
v(G)=|V(G)|
,
e(G)=|E(G)|
, and call
\varrho(G)=e(G)/\binom{v(G)}{2}
the

edge density
of
G
.
For two graphs
F
and
G
denote by
N(F,G)
the number of induced copies of
F
in
G
,
and let
\varrho(F,G)={N(F,G)}/{\binom{v(G)}{v(F)}}
be the
induced
F
-density
of
G
.

A
quantum graph

Q
is a formal linear combination of finitely many graphs,
i.e., an expression of the form

Q=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}F_{i}\,,

where
m
is a nonnegative integer, the numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
are real,
and
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}

are graphs. We call
F_{i}
a
constituent
of
Q
if
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
.
Two quantum graphs
Q
,
Q^{\prime}
are equal if they have the same constituents and the same
(nonzero) coefficients for each constituent.
The
complement
of
Q
is
\overline{Q}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\overline{F}_{i}
,
where
\overline{F}_{i}
denotes the complement of
F_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
. A quantum
graph
Q
is
self-complementary
if
Q=\overline{Q}
.
Every graph parameter
f
can be extended linearly to quantum graphs by stipulating

f(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}f(F_{i})
.
In particular,

\displaystyle N(Q,G)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}N(F_{i},G)\quad{\rm and}\quad\varrho(Q,G)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\varrho(F_{i},G)\,.

The main notion investigated in this article is the following.

Definition 1.1

(Feasible region)
.

Let
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}F_{i}
be a quantum graph.

•

A sequence
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
of graphs is
Q
-good
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}v(G_{n})=\infty
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(G_{n})
exists, and
for every
i\in[m]
the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(F_{i},G_{n})
exists.

•

A
Q
-good sequence of graphs
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

realizes

a point
(x,y)\in[0,1]\times{\mathds{R}}
if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(G_{n})=x\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(Q,G_{n})=y.

•

The
feasible region

\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
of (induced)
Q
is the collection of
points
(x,y)\in[0,1]\times{\mathds{R}}
realized by some
Q
-good
sequence
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

We commence a systematic study of the feasible region of quantum graphs
Q
.
As we shall see soon,
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
is determined by its boundary,
so it suffices to consider for every
x\in[0,1]
the numbers

i(Q,x)=\inf\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)\}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad I(Q,x)=\sup\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)\}\,.

Determining the values of
i(Q,x)
and
I(Q,x)
under some constraints is a central topic in extremal combinatorics.
For example, the classical Kruskal-Katona theorem
[
14
,
13
]
implies

I(K_{r},x)=x^{r/2}\quad\text{for all $r\geq 2$ and $x\in[0,1]$}\,.

Turán’s seminal theorem
[
28
]
and supersaturation show that for
every integer
r\geq 3
,

i(K_{r},x)>0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad x>(r-2)/(r-1)\,.

Determining
i(K_{r},x)
for all
x>(r-2)/(r-1)
is highly nontrivial
and was solved for
r=3
by Razborov
[
23
]
, for
r=4
by Nikiforov
[
20
]
,
and for all
r
by the third author
[
25
]
.

Regarding quantum graphs with at least two constituents,
a classical result of Goodman
[
8
]
says that
i(K_{3}+\overline{K}_{3},x)\geq 1/4

and equality holds only for
x=1/2
.
Erdős
[
5
]
conjectured
that
i(K_{r}+\overline{K}_{r},x)\geq 2^{1-\binom{r}{2}}
for
r\geq 4
with equality
for
x=1/2
. This conjecture was disproved by Thomason
[
27
]

for all
r\geq 4
, but even for
r=4
the minimum value of
i(K_{r}+\overline{K}_{r},x)

is still unknown.

For a single graph
F
the function
I(F,x)
is closely related to
the
inducibility

\mathrm{ind}(F)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\max\left\{\varrho(F,G)\colon v(G)=n\right\}

of

introduced by Pippenger and Golumbic
[

]
. In fact,

\mathrm{ind}(F)=\max\{I(F,x)\colon x\in[0,1]\}
, where the maximum exists due to

(see Theorem
1.2
below).

Determining the feasible region
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F)
of a single graph

is a special
case of the more general problem to determine the
graph profile

of a given
finite family of graphs
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\}
.
Here
T(\mathcal{F})\subseteq[0,1]^{k}
is the collection of limit points of

\left((\varrho(F_{1},G_{i}),\ldots,\varrho(F_{k},G_{i}))\right)_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
v(G_{i})\to\infty
.
Besides the clique density theorem, very few results are known about graph profiles
(see
[
10
,
12
,
4
,
9
]
).

Our results are of two flavors.

•

We prove some general results about the shape of
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
.
Our main result here is Theorem
1.2
,
which states that
I(Q,x)
and
i(Q,x)
are continuous and almost everywhere differentiable.

•

We study

for some specific choices of

for which

has been investigated by many researchers.
We focus on quantum graphs whose constituents are complete multipartite graphs and
prove a general upper bound for

.
Prior to this work,
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(F)
for a single graph

was determined only
when
F
is a clique or an independent set. Here we extend this to the case
F=K_{1,2}

and also obtain results for complete bipartite graphs.
Furthermore we study
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(K_{r}^{-})

K_{r}^{-}
arises from the clique

by the deletion of a single edge. As a consequence of our results, we determine the
inducibility

.

1.2.
General results

The following result describes the shape of the feasible region of an arbitrary
quantum graph.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every quantum graph
Q
we have

\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)=\bigl\{(x,y)\in[0,1]\times{\mathds{R}}\colon i(Q,x)\leq y\leq I(Q,x)\bigr\}\,.

Moreover, the boundary functions
i(Q,x)
and
I(Q,x)
are continuous and
almost everywhere differentiable.

In contrast to Theorem
1.2
Hatami and Norin
[
9
]

gave an example of a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs such that the intersection of
the graph profile
T(\mathcal{F})
with some hyperplane has a nowhere differentiable boundary.

For every quantum graph
Q
the feasible regions of
Q
,
-Q
and
\overline{Q}

are closely related. Indeed, using the formulae

\displaystyle N(F,G)=N(\overline{F},\overline{G})\quad{\rm and}\quad\varrho(F,G)=\varrho(\overline{F},\overline{G}),

which are valid for all graphs
F
and
G
, one easily confirms the following
observation.

Fact 1.3
.

Let
Q
be a quantum graph.

(

)

The feasible regions of
Q
and
-Q
are symmetric to each other about
the
x
-axis. Hence,
I(-Q,x)=-i(Q,x)
and
i(-Q,x)=-I(Q,x)
hold for all
x\in[0,1]
.

(

)

The feasible regions of
Q
and
\overline{Q}
are symmetric to
each other about the line
{x=1/2}
.
Thus we have
I(Q,x)=I(\overline{Q},1-x)
and
i(Q,x)=i(\overline{Q},1-x)

for every
x\in[0,1]
.
In particular, if
Q
is self-complementary, then
I(Q,x)=I(Q,1-x)
and
i(Q,x)=i(Q,1-x)
,
i.e. the functions
I(Q,x)
and

are symmetric around
x=1/2
. ∎

If

We proceed with some estimates based on random graphs.
Given a quantum graph

\displaystyle{\rm rand}(Q,x)=\sum_{i\in[m]}\lambda_{i}\frac{\left(v(F_{i})\right)!}{|{\rm Aut}(F_{i})|}x^{e(F_{i})}(1-x)^{e(\overline{F}_{i})}\quad\text{ for every }x\in[0,1]\,,

Construction 1.9

For integers

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{k}{k-1}x}\right).

One checks immediately that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(H^{\star}(n,x))=x

g_{r}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(K_{r},H^{\star}(n,x))

A more explicit description of

is as follows. Clearly
g_{r}(x)=0
holds for

The non-asymptotic problem to determine for given natural numbers
n
and
m

the exact minimum number of
r
-cliques an
n

m

still wide open in general. But for triangles there has recently been spectacular progress by

Easy calculations show that the function
g_{r}(x)
is non-differentiable at the
critical values
x=1-1/q
, where
q\geq r-1
denotes an integer. Moreover,
g_{r}(x)

is piecewise concave between any two consecutive critical values.
An old result of Bollobás
[

(proved long before the clique density theorem)
asserts that the piece-wise linear function interpolating between the critical values
of

is a lower bound on

whose constituents are complete multipartite graphs.

To state this generalization we need the following concepts.
For every positive integer
r\geq 2
and every quantum graph
Q
we
define the

r
-partite feasible region

\Omega_{{\rm ind-r}}(Q)

to be the collection of all points in
[0,(r-1)/r]\times{\mathds{R}}
that can be realized
by a

-good sequence
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

of complete
r
-partite graphs (isolated vertices are not allowed).
For
x\in[0,(r-1)/r]
, let

i_{r}(Q,x)=\inf\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind-r}}(Q)\}\quad{\rm and}\quad I_{r}(Q,x)=\sup\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind-r}}(Q)\}\,.

Optimizing over

we put

m(Q,x)=\inf\left\{i_{r}(Q,x)\colon r\geq\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-x}\right\rceil\right\}\quad{\rm and}\quad M(Q,x)=\sup\left\{I_{r}(Q,x)\colon r\geq\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-x}\right\rceil\right\}

for every quantum graph

and every real

as well as

m(Q,1)=M(Q,1)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(Q,K_{n})\,.

Clearly, we have

i(Q,x)\leq m(Q,x)\leq M(Q,x)\leq I(Q,x)\,.

Next we observe that for every bounded function
f\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathds{R}}
there exist

denotes the

Proposition 2.1
.

For every quantum graph
Q

\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
is closed.

Proof.

Let
(x,y)\in[0,1]\in{\mathds{R}}
be a point belonging to the closure of
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
.
We are to exhibit a
Q
-good sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of graphs realizing

.
For every positive integer

(x_{n},y_{n})\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)

satisfying

and then, using the definition
of
\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)
, we take a graph
G_{n}
such that

v(G_{n})>n,\quad|\varrho(G_{n})-x|<1/n,\quad\text{ and }\quad|\varrho(Q,G_{n})-y|<1/n.

The sequence

thus constructed satisfies

,

, and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(Q,G_{n})=y
.
Since

has only finitely many constituents, some subsequence of
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

is
Q
-good. Every such subsequence realizes

.
∎

Therefore the definitions of

and

rewrite as

\displaystyle i(Q,x)=\min\left\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)\right\}\quad{\rm and}\quad I(Q,x)=\max\left\{y\colon(x,y)\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)\right\}.

Next we show that

is determined by

and

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
Q
be a quantum graph,

and

.
If

and
(x,y_{2})\in\Omega_{{\rm ind}}(Q)

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Fix
y\in[y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Let
\left(G^{\prime}_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a
Q
-good sequence of graphs that
realizes
(x,y_{1})
,
and let
\left(G^{\prime\prime}_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a
Q
-good sequence of graphs that
realizes
(x,y_{2})
.
By a simple probabilistic argument we may assume
V(G^{\prime}_{n})=V(G^{\prime\prime}_{n})=[n]

for every
n\geq 1
.
We shall construct a sequence of graphs
\left(G_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

with

for every

that realizes
(x,y)
.

For fixed
n\geq 1
we consider a finite sequence of graphs

with common vertex set

which interpolates between

in the sense that

\bullet

for

the graph

arises from

by adding
or deleting a single edge,

\bullet

and
\min\{\varrho(G^{\prime}_{n}),\varrho(G^{\prime\prime}_{n})\}\leq\varrho(G^{m}_{n})\leq\max\{\varrho(G^{\prime}_{n}),\varrho(G^{\prime\prime}_{n})\}
for every

.

Due to the first bullet we have
\varrho(Q,G^{m+1}_{n})=\varrho(Q,G^{m}_{n})+o(1)
for every

. Combined with
\varrho(Q,G^{1}_{n})=y_{1}+o(1)
and

∎

Towards the continuity of

we now establish the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every quantum graph
Q
there exist constants
\ell\geq 1
and
C\geq 0
such that
for all
x
,
x^{\prime}
with
0<x\leq x^{\prime}\leq 1
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{I(Q,x^{\prime})}{(x^{\prime})^{\ell}}\leq\frac{I(Q,x)}{x^{\ell}}+C\cdot\left(\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^{\ell}-\left(\frac{1}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\,.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Fix
0<x\leq x^{\prime}\leq 1
, set
\alpha=\left({x^{\prime}}/{x}\right)^{1/2}-1
,
and consider a
Q
-good sequence
\left(G^{\prime}_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
that
realizes
(x^{\prime},I(Q,x^{\prime}))
. Without loss of generality we may assume
v(G^{\prime}_{n})=n

for every
n\geq 1
. Let
G_{n}
be the graph which is the union of
G^{\prime}_{n}
and a set
of
\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor
isolated vertices.
Since

\displaystyle\varrho\left(G_{n}\right)=\frac{\varrho\left(G^{\prime}_{n}\right)\binom{n}{2}}{\binom{n+\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}{2}}\to\frac{x^{\prime}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}=x\quad{\rm as}\quad n\to\infty\,,

we have

(2.2)

I(Q,x)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varrho(Q,G_{n})\,.

To estimate the right side we write
Q=\sum_{i\in P}\lambda_{i}F_{i}+\sum_{j\in N}\lambda_{j}F_{j}

with
\lambda_{i}>0
for
i\in P
and
\lambda_{j}<0
for
j\in N
.
Set
\ell_{i}=v(F_{i})
for every

and
\ell=\max\{\ell_{i}/2\colon i\in P\cup N\}
.
For every
i\in P
the fact that
G^{\prime}_{n}
is a subgraph of

yields

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varrho\left(F_{i},G_{n}\right)\geq\frac{\varrho\left(F_{i},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)\binom{n}{\ell_{i}}}{\binom{n+\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}{\ell_{i}}}\geq\frac{\varrho\left(F_{i},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(1+\alpha)^{\ell_{i}}}=\frac{\varrho\left(F_{i},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell_{i}/2}}\geq\frac{\varrho\left(F_{i},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}.

For
j\in N
we use that every induced copy of
F_{j}
in
G_{n}
is either already
contained in
G^{\prime}_{n}
or involves one of the new isolated vertices, which implies

\varrho\left(F_{j},G_{n}\right)\leq\frac{\varrho\left(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)\binom{n}{\ell_{j}}+\alpha n\cdot\binom{n+\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}{\ell_{j}-1}}{\binom{n+\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}{\ell_{j}}}\leq\varrho(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n})+\frac{\ell_{j}\cdot\alpha}{1+\alpha}+o_{n}(1)\,.

Taking into account that

\varrho(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n})\leq\frac{\varrho\left(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}+\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)

and

\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}=1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{1/2}\leq 1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}

we obtain

\varrho\left(F_{j},G_{n}\right)\leq\frac{\varrho(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n})}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}+(\ell_{j}+1)\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)+o_{n}(1)\,.

Combined with (
2.3
) this entails

\displaystyle\varrho\left(Q,G_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in P}\lambda_{i}\varrho\left(F_{i},G_{n}\right)+\sum_{j\in N}\lambda_{j}\varrho\left(F_{j},G_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in P}\lambda_{i}\frac{\varrho\left(F_{i},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}+\sum_{j\in N}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{\varrho\left(F_{j},G^{\prime}_{n}\right)}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}+(\ell_{j}+1)\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)\right)-o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varrho(Q,G^{\prime}_{n})}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell/2}}-C\cdot\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)-o_{n}(1)\,,

where
C=\sum_{j\in N}(-\lambda_{j})(\ell_{j}+1)\geq 0
.
Now (
2.2
) reveals

\displaystyle I(Q,x)\geq\frac{I(Q,x^{\prime})}{(x^{\prime}/x)^{\ell}}-C\cdot\left(1-\left(\frac{x}{x^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\right)

and upon multiplying both sides by
x^{-\ell}
the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.1

In view of Proposition
5.3

there exists a sequence
\left(G^{\prime}_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
of complete bipartite
graphs such that

(5.1)

V(G^{\prime}_{n})=V(G_{n}),\quad e(G^{\prime}_{n})\leq e(G_{n}),\quad\text{ and }\quad N(K_{s,t},G^{\prime}_{n})\geq N(K_{s,t},G_{n})

hold for every positive integer
n
.
By passing to a subsequence we may assume that the limits
x^{\prime}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(G_{n}^{\prime})

and
y_{2}^{\prime}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho(S_{t},G_{n}^{\prime})
exist. Now (
5.1
)
implies

(5.2)

x^{\prime}\leq x\quad\text{ and }\quad y_{2}^{\prime}\geq y_{r}\,,

and as
\left(G_{n}^{\prime}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

is an
S_{t}
-good sequence of complete bipartite graphs that realizes
(x^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime})

we have
y_{2}^{\prime}\leq I_{2}(S_{t},x^{\prime})
.
Since
I_{2}(S_{t},\cdot)=s_{t}(\cdot)
is increasing on
[0,x^{\star}(t)]
, the first estimate
in (
5.2
) entails
I_{2}(S_{t},x^{\prime})\leq I_{2}(S_{t},x)
.
So altogether we obtain

y_{r}\leq y^{\prime}_{2}\leq I_{2}(S_{t},x^{\prime})\leq I_{2}(S_{t},x)\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.15
.

Proof of Theorem
1.15
.

The case
t=2
already being understood in Theorem
1.12

we may assume that
t\geq 3
. It is clear that
I(S_{t},x)\geq I_{2}(S_{t},x)=s_{t}(x)

holds for
x\in[0,1/2]
and thus we just need to show
I(S_{t},x)\leq s_{t}(x)

for
x\in[0,x^{\star}(t)]
.
Define
f\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathds{R}}
by

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}s_{t}(x)&\quad{\rm for}\quad x\in[0,x^{\star}(t)]\\
s_{t}\left(x^{\star}(t)\right)&\quad{\rm for}\quad x\in[x^{\star}(t),1].\end{cases}

Lemma
5.2
informs us that
f
is concave.
Moreover, we have
f(x)\geq M(S_{t},x)
for all
x\in[0,1]
. Indeed, if
x\in[0,x^{\star}(t)]

this follows from Lemma
5.5
and for
x\in[x^{\star}(t),1]

we can appeal to Theorem
5.4
instead.
Summarizing,
f(x)
is a concave upper bound on
M(S_{t},x)
.
Owing to Theorem
1.11
this
proves
I(S_{t},x)\leq f(x)=s_{t}(x)
for every
x\in[0,x^{\star}(t)]
.
∎

6.
Proofs for complete bipartite graphs

In this section we prove Theorems
1.16

and
1.18
.
The upper bound on
I(K_{s,t},x)
stated in
Theorem
1.16
is an immediate consequence
of the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

If
t\geq s\geq 2
are positive integers,
then for every graph
G
we have

N(K_{s,t},G)\leq\frac{(t-s+1)!}{s!t!}N(S_{t-s+1},G)\cdot\left(e(G)\right)^{s-1}\,.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Notice that for an abstract
K_{s,t}
the number of
ordered partitions
V(K_{s,t})=U_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\dots\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}U_{s}
such that
U_{1}
induces
a star
S_{t-s+1}

and each of
U_{2},\dots,U_{s}
induces an edge is
\binom{t}{t-s+1}(s-1)!s!
.
This is because there are
s\binom{t}{t-s+1}
possibilities for
U_{1}
; moreover, if
i\in[2,s]

and
U_{1},\ldots,U_{i-1}
are already fixed, then there are
(s-i+1)^{2}
possibilities for
U_{i}
.

By double counting it follows that
\binom{t}{t-s+1}(s-1)!s!N(K_{s,t},G)
is at most the
number of
s
-tuples
(U_{1},\dots,U_{s})
of subsets of
G
such that
G[U_{1}]\cong S_{t-s+1}

and
G[U_{i}]\cong K_{2}
for all
i\in[2,s]
, whence

\binom{t}{t-s+1}(s-1)!s!N(K_{s,t},G)\leq N(S_{t-s+1},G)\cdot\left(e(G)\right)^{s-1}\,.

Now it remains to observe
\binom{t}{t-s+1}(s-1)!s!=\frac{s!t!}{(t-s+1)!}
.
∎

We remark that this argument is asymptotically optimal if
G
is a complete bipartite graph.
More precisely, for
x\leq x^{\star}(t-s+1)
the sequence
(B(n,x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
establishes
the equality case in Theorem
1.16
.
This observation concludes the proof of
Theorem
1.16
.

In the remainder of this section we show the following explicit version of
Theorem
1.18
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Every graph
G
on
n
vertices with
{xn^{2}}/{2}
edges
satisfies

\displaystyle N(C_{4},G)\leq\frac{x(1-x)^{2}}{8}n^{4}+2n^{3}\,.

For the proof we need the following well-known result due to Goodman
[
8
]
,
whose short proof we include for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 6.3

(Goodman
[
8
]
)
.

For every real number
x\in[0,1]
, every positive integer
n
,
and every graph
G
on
n
vertices with
xn^{2}/2
edges we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)}e(v)\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}-xn^{3}/2\,,

where
e(v)=e(G[N(v)])
denotes the number of triangles containing the vertex
v
.

Proof of Proposition
6.3
.

Counting the number of pairs
(u,\{v,w\})\in V(G)\times E(G)
with
v,w\in N(u)

in two different ways, we obtain

\sum_{u\in V(G)}e(u)\geq\sum_{vw\in G}\bigl(d(v)+d(w)-n\bigr)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}-e(G)\cdot n\,.\qed

Goodman’s formula has the following consequence, which will assist us in the inductive proof
of Theorem
6.2
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Every graph
G
with
n
vertices and
xn^{2}/2
edges possesses a vertex
v
satisfying

e(v)\geq\frac{d(v)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}}{4}-\frac{(1-x)^{3}n^{3}}{4(n-d(v))}\,.

Proof.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies
\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}\geq x^{2}n^{3}

and because of

\sum_{v\in V(G)}(n-d(v))=(1-x)n^{2}

we also have

\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{n-d(v)}\geq\frac{1}{1-x}\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(G)}\Bigl(\frac{d(v)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}}{4}-\frac{(1-x)^{3}n^{3}}{4(n-d(x))}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{d(v)^{2}}{2}+\frac{(x^{2}-x)n^{3}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)^{2}-xn^{3}/2\,.

Due to Proposition
6.3
the result now follows
by averaging.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We argue by induction on
n
. The base case
n\leq 3
is clear, for there are no
4
-cycles
in graphs with less than four vertices. Now suppose
n\geq 4
and that our claim
holds for every graph on
n-1
vertices.

Given a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
xn^{2}/2
edges we invoke Corollary
6.4

and get a vertex
v\in V(G)
such that

(6.1)

e\geq\frac{d^{2}}{2}+\frac{(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}}{4}-\frac{(1-x)^{3}n^{3}}{4(n-d)}\,,

where
d=d(v)
and
e=e(v)
. We contend that

(6.2)

N(C_{4},G)\leq N(C_{4},G-v)+(d^{2}/2-e)(n-d)\,,

or, in other words, that there are at most
(d^{2}/2-e)(n-d)
induced copies
of
K_{2,2}
in
G
which contain the vertex
v
. The reason for this is that each
such copy contains a pair of non-adjacent members of
N(v)
and a fourth vertex belonging
to
V(G)\smallsetminus N(v)
. Clearly there are at most

d^{2}/2-e
possibilities for such a non-adjacent pair and at most
n-d
possibilities
for the fourth vertex.

Claim 6.5
.

We have

8N(C_{4},G-v)\leq x(1-x)^{2}(n^{4}-4n^{3})+2(xn-d)(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}+16n^{3}\,.

Proof.

The induction hypothesis yields

(6.3)

8N(C_{4},G-v)\leq x^{\prime}(1-x^{\prime})^{2}(n-1)^{4}+16(n-1)^{3}\,,

where
x^{\prime}
is defined by

x^{\prime}=\frac{2|E(G-v)|}{(n-1)^{2}}=\frac{xn^{2}-2d}{(n-1)^{2}}\,.

The function
h(x)=x(1-x)^{2}
has derivatives
h^{\prime}(x)=1-4x+3x^{2}
and
h^{\prime\prime}(x)=-4+6x
.
Therefore we have
\|h^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}=1
and
\|h^{\prime\prime}\|_{[0,1]}=4
,
where
\|\cdot\|_{[0,1]}
denotes the supremum norm with respect to the unit interval.
So Taylor’s formula and (
6.3
) imply

\displaystyle 8N(C_{4},G-v)\leq x(1-x)^{2}(n-1)^{4}

\displaystyle+(1-4x+3x^{2})(x^{\prime}-x)(n-1)^{4}

\displaystyle+2(x^{\prime}-x)^{2}(n-1)^{4}+16(n-1)^{3}\,.

Here

x(1-x)^{2}(n-1)^{4}\leq x(1-x)^{2}(n^{4}-4n^{3}+6n^{2})\leq x(1-x)^{2}(n^{4}-4n^{3})+n^{2}

and due to

(6.4)

x^{\prime}-x=\frac{(2n-1)x-2d}{(n-1)^{2}}

we have
2(x^{\prime}-x)^{2}(n-1)^{4}=2\big|(2n-1)x-2d\big|^{2}\leq 8n^{2}
.
For these reasons it suffices to establish

(6.5)

(1-4x+3x^{2})(x^{\prime}-x)(n-1)^{4}\leq 2(xn-d)(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}+7n^{2}\,.

Now the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\big|(x^{\prime}-x)(n-1)^{4}

\displaystyle-2(xn-d)n^{2}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|(x^{\prime}-x)(n-1)^{2}-2(xn-d)\big|(n-1)^{2}+2|xn-d|\bigl(n^{2}-(n-1)^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:5006}}{\leq}x(n-1)^{2}+4n^{2}\leq 5n^{2}

and together with
\|h^{\prime}\|_{[0,1]}=1
this proves (
6.5
).
Thereby Claim
6.5
is proved.
∎

Now combining (
6.1
), (
6.2
), and Claim
6.5
we obtain

\displaystyle 8N(C_{4},G)

\displaystyle\leq x(1-x)^{2}(n^{4}-4n^{3})+2(xn-d)(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}+16n^{3}

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxxxxxx}-2(1-4x+3x^{2})n^{2}(n-d)+2(1-x)^{3}n^{3}

\displaystyle=x(1-x)^{2}n^{4}+16n^{3}\,,

as desired.
∎

7.
Concluding remarks

7.1.
General questions

As the example
Q=K_{3}+\overline{K}_{3}
shows, for a quantum graph
Q

the function
I(Q,x)
can have at least two global maxima.
We do not know whether this is possible for single graphs
F
as well.

Problem 7.1
.

Does there exist a graph
F
such that the function
I(F,x)
has at least two global maxima?

Two questions of a similar flavor are as follows.

Problem 7.2
.

Does there exist a graph
F
such that for some nontrivial interval
J
we
have
I(F,x)={\mathrm{ind}}(F)
for all
x\in J
?

Problem 7.3
.

Does there exist a graph
F
such that the function
I(F,x)
has a nontrivial local maximum (minimum)?

Recall that for a self-complementary graph
F
the function
I(F,x)
is symmetric
around
x=1/2
. One may thus wonder whether some appropriate self-complementary
graph
F
yields an affirmative solution to Problem
7.1
.
This approach leads to the following question.

Problem 7.4
.

Let
F
be a self-complementary graph.
Is it true that
I(F,x)={\mathrm{ind}}(F)
holds if and only if
x=1/2
?

7.2.
Problems for specific graphs

The smallest problem left open by our results on stars in Section
5

is to determine
I(S_{3},x)
for
x\in[1/2,1]
. In an interesting contrast to the case

S_{2}=K_{3}^{-}
one can show that the clique density construction (see Construction
1.9
)
is not extremal for this problem. For
x\in[4\sqrt{2}-5,1]
the best construction we are
aware of
is the complement of a clique of order
\lfloor(1-x)^{1/2}n\rfloor
, which leads
to the bound

(7.1)

I(S_{3},x)\geq 4(1-(1-x)^{1/2})(1-x)^{3/2}\,.

For
x\in[0.91,0.93]
we have a complicated argument based on the results in
[
24
]

which shows that equality holds in (
7.1
).
In the range
x\in[1/2,4\sqrt{2}-5)
the complement of two
disjoint cliques of order
\lfloor((1-x)/2)^{1/2}n\rfloor
shows that
I(S_{3},x)

is strictly larger than the right side of (
7.1
). We hope to return to this
problem in the near future.

Finally, we would like to emphasize Conjecture
1.17
again:
Is it true that for
x\in[1/2,1]
the graphs in Construction
1.9

minimizing the triangle density maximize the induced
C_{4}
density?

Abstract
. This note aims to study the iteration theory of noncommutative self-maps of bounded matrix convex domains. We prove a version of the Denjoy-Wolff theorem for the row ball and the maximal quantization of the unit ball of ℂ d {\mathbb{C}}^{d} . For more general bounded matrix convex sets, we prove a version of Wolff’s theorem inspired by the results of Abate. Lastly, we use iteration and fixed point theory to generalize the commutative results of Davidson, Ramsey, and Shalit to quotients of the free semigroup algebra by WOT closed ideals.

In Section

2.
Noncommutative functions and domains

Let
E
be a vector space, we set
{\mathbb{M}}(E)=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}(E)
, where
M_{n}(E)=M_{n}\otimes E
. We write
{\mathbb{M}}_{d}={\mathbb{M}}({\mathbb{C}}^{d})
and treat
{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
as the set of all
d
-tuples of matrices of all sizes. Note that
\operatorname{{\mathbf{G}L}}_{n}
acts on
M_{n}(E)
by
S\cdot(A\otimes\xi)=(S^{-1}AS)\otimes\xi
. We will denote this action for
{\mathbf{X}}\in M_{n}(E)
simply by
S^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}S
.

Additionally, for
{\mathbf{X}}\in M_{n}(E)
and
{\mathbf{Y}}\in M_{m}(E)
we will write

{\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&0\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{n+m}(E).

A subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
is called an nc set, if for every
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega
,
{\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega
.

If
E
is an operator space, then we set:

{\mathfrak{B}}_{E}=\bigsqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{{\mathbf{X}}\in M_{n}(E)\mid\|{\mathbf{X}}\|_{n}<1\right\}.

We endow
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
with the row norm and write
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
for
{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{d}}
. We will write
{\mathbb{B}}_{d}={\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(1)
for the unit ball of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and
{\mathbb{D}}
for the disc. A subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
will be called an nc domain if
\Omega
is an nc set, level-wise open, and invariant under unitary similarities.

An nc set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
is called matrix convex if, for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
and
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
an isometry, we have that
V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V\in\Omega(m)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
be an nc domain. We say that a function
f\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}(F)
is an nc function, if

•

f
is graded, i.e,
f(\Omega(n))\subset M_{n}(F)
, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We write
f_{n}=f|_{\Omega(n)}
.

•

f
respects direct sums, i.e,
f({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}})=f({\mathbf{X}})\oplus f({\mathbf{Y}})
.

•

f
respects similarities, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, every
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
, and every
S\in\operatorname{{\mathbf{G}L}}_{n}
, if
S^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}S\in\Omega(n)
, then
f(S^{-1}{\mathbf{X}}S)=S^{-1}f({\mathbf{X}})S
.

It is a remarkable result of Kalyuzhnyi-Verbovetskyi and Vinnikov that a locally bounded nc function is both Gateaux and Frechet differentiable (see also
[
6
]
). Hence, we will say an analytic nc function when we mean locally bounded. The key to proving this result is the nc difference-differential operator. Namely, for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(m)
, there exists a linear map
\Delta f({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\colon M_{n,m}\otimes E\to M_{n,m}\otimes E
, such that for every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}\otimes E
, such that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Omega(n+m)
,

f\left(\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}f({\mathbf{X}})&\Delta f({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})\\
0&f({\mathbf{Y}})\end{pmatrix}.

In fact,
\Delta f({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is the Frechet derivative of a locally bounded nc function at
{\mathbf{X}}
. Moreover, we have the identity

\Delta f({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{X}}-{\mathbf{Y}})=f({\mathbf{X}})-f({\mathbf{Y}}).

For the properties of the nc difference-differential operator, we refer the reader to
[
24
]
.

The nc sets that we will focus on are described in the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{K}}
be a Hilbert space. Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
be an nc domain and
Q\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}(B({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{K}}))
be an nc analytic function. We define
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in\Omega\mid Q({\mathbf{Z}})Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}<I\right\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

For
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(m)
, and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}\otimes B({\mathcal{H}})
, define the nc affine kernel
G({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})={\mathbf{Z}}-Q({\mathbf{X}}){\mathbf{Z}}Q({\mathbf{Y}}^{*})^{*}
. Then,
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
coincides with a generalized noncommutative ball, as defined in
[
13
]
. Domains of the form
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
were originally defined by Agler and McCarthy for
Q
, a matrix of nc polynomials (see, for example,
[
6
,
7
]
). In this form, the sets
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
first appeared in the works of Ball, Marx, and Vinnikov
[
10
,
11
]
.

Similarly, we have a generalized analog of the half-plane, which by the Effros-Winkler separation theorem, includes all matrix convex sets.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a Hilbert space. Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
be an nc domain invariant under pointwise conjugation and
L\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}(B({\mathcal{H}}))
be an injective nc analytic function. We define
{\mathbb{H}}_{L}=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in\Omega\mid\operatorname{Re}(L({\mathbf{Z}}))<I\right\}
. Following
[
11
]
, we also have a representation
\Omega={\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
, where
Q
is obtained from
L
using the Cayley transform.

Note that as observed by Taylor in
[
39
, Section 6]
, for every nc domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
, the algebra
{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
of holomorphic nc functions on
\Omega
is a nuclear Frechet algebra. The topology on
{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
is the topology of uniform convergence on levelwise compacta. Note that the map that takes an nc function to its restriction to the various levels gives a continuous embedding
{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}}(\Omega(n))\otimes M_{n}
, where we write
{\mathcal{O}}(\Omega(n))
for the algebra of holomorphic functions on
\Omega(n)
with the topology of uniform convergence on compacta. Since
{\mathcal{O}}(\Omega(n))\otimes M_{n}
is nuclear for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the product is also nuclear by
[
41
, Proposition 50.1]
. Moreover, since the conditions of respecting direct sums and similarities are closed, we immediately see that
{\mathcal{T}}(\Omega)
is a closed subspace and is also nuclear. Hence, by
[
41
, Proposition 50.2]
, every bounded subset of a nuclear space is precompact, and thus, in particular, in a Frechet space, every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence. In other words, we can apply Montel’s theorem to sequences of nc functions and maps. The obtained convergence is, of course, uniform convergence on compacta on each level. There are stronger versions of Montel’s theorem (see, for example,
[
8
, Theorem 13.14]
and
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
). However, this is the one that will be used almost exclusively throughout this paper. We will apply Popescu’s version of Montel’s theorem for the row ball
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
to obtain a stronger Denjpy-Wolff theorem in this setting.

3.
Noncommutative hyperbolic metrics

Noncommutative hyperbolic metrics and the nc Lempert function were introduced by the first author and Vinnikov in
[
13
]
. For a contraction
T\in B({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{K}})
, we will write
D_{T}=1-TT^{*}
and
D_{T^{*}}=1-T^{*}T
for the defect operators. The nc Lempert function for an nc domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
is defined by

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})=\left[\sup\left\{t\in[0,\infty]\mid\forall s\in[0,t),\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&s{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}\in\Omega\right\}\right]^{-1}

Here
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(m)
, and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}\otimes E
. We also set for
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
.

\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{X}}-{\mathbf{Y}}).

In the case of
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
we have a special form for the nc Lempert function, which is a particular case of the equality appearing in
[
13
, Equation 21]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(m)
, and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}\otimes E
we have

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})=\left\|D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\right\|.

\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\left\|D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}\left(Q({\mathbf{X}})-Q({\mathbf{Y}})\right)D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\right\|

Proof.

Let
t>0
be such that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&t{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
. Then

\begin{pmatrix}Q({\mathbf{X}})&t\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})\\
0&Q({\mathbf{Y}})\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}&0\\
t\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}&Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}\end{pmatrix}<I.

Multiplying the matrices and applying Schur’s complement, we see that

D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}-t^{2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}>\\
t^{2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})}^{-1}Q({\mathbf{Y}})\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}.

Let
T\in B({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{K}})
be a strict row contraction. Writing
D_{T}^{-1}
as a power series in
TT^{*}
, we get that
T^{*}D_{T}^{-1}T=D_{T^{*}}^{-1}-I
. Hence,

D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}>t^{2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}}^{-1}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}.

Thus

\frac{1}{t^{2}}>D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}}^{-1}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}.

Running the argument back, we see that this condition is necessary and sufficient. Hence we conclude that

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})=\left\|D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})D_{Q({\mathbf{Y}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\right\|

The second equality follows from the nc difference-differential formula
[
24
, Theorem 2.10]
.
∎

We have a similar result for generalized half-planes.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{L}(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{L}(m)
, and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
we have

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(I-\operatorname{Re}(L({\mathbf{X}}))\right)^{-1/2}\Delta L({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})\left(I-\operatorname{Re}(L({\mathbf{Y}}))\right)^{-1/2}\right\|.

\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left(I-\operatorname{Re}(L({\mathbf{X}}))\right)^{-1/2}\left(L({\mathbf{X}})-L({\mathbf{Y}})\right)\left(I-\operatorname{Re}(L({\mathbf{Y}}))\right)^{-1/2}\right\|

Remark 3.3
.

As stated in
[
13
]
,
\tilde{\delta}
is symmetric. We include a brief proof of this fact for the sake of completeness. Let
\Omega
be a unitarily invariant nc domain and let
t\in(0,\pi/2)
. For every
{\mathbf{X}},\,{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
, we have

\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&-\sin t\\
-\sin t&-\cos t\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{Y}}&\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}({\mathbf{Y}}-{\mathbf{X}})\\
0&{\mathbf{X}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cos t&-\sin t\\
-\sin t&-\cos t\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}({\mathbf{X}}-{\mathbf{Y}})\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,
\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}})
.

We record the following simple observation, that is, however, using the power of the nc assumption (see also
[
4
, Theorem 5]
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
be a bounded matrix convex nc domain that contains
0
. Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an nc analytic map. If there exists
0<r<1
, such that
f(\Omega)\subset r\Omega
, then
f
has a unique fixed point in
\Omega(1)
. Namely, there exists
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(1)
, such that
f({\mathbf{X}})={\mathbf{X}}
and the only fixed points of
f
are ampliations of
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{n}=f|_{\Omega(n)}
. By the Earle-Hamilton theorem,
f_{n}\colon\Omega(n)\to\Omega(n)
has a unique fixed point. In particular, let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(1)
be the unique fixed point on this level, then
{\mathbf{X}}^{\oplus n}
is fixed by
f_{n}
since
f
respects direct sums. Thus, by uniqueness, the fixed point on level
n
is
{\mathbf{X}}^{\oplus n}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega={\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
. Then for every
n
,
\rho_{{\mathbf{X}}}({\mathbf{Z}})=\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{Z}})
is a seminorm. Furthermore,
\rho_{{\mathbf{X}}}
is a norm if and only if
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is injective.

Proof.

Obvious from Lemma
3.1
and the linearity of
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
.
∎

Note that for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(n)
, we can define a collection of seminorms on
M_{k}(M_{n}(E))
, via
\rho_{k,{\mathbf{X}}}({\mathbf{Z}})=\delta(I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{Z}})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Q
be such that for some
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(n)
,
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is injective. Then
\rho_{k,{\mathbf{X}}}
form an operator space structure on
M_{n}(E)
.

Proof.

By our assumption and the properties of the nc derivative
\Delta Q(I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})
is injective for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, each
\rho_{k}
is a norm by the previous lemma. Hence, we will verify Ruan’s axioms.

Let
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{kn}(E)
and
{\mathbf{W}}\in M_{\ell n}(E)
. By the properties of
\delta
, we have that

\rho_{k+\ell}({\mathbf{Z}}\oplus{\mathbf{W}})=\delta(I_{k+\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{k+\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{Z}}\oplus{\mathbf{W}})=\max\{\rho_{k}({\mathbf{Z}}),\rho_{\ell}({\mathbf{W}})\}.

Now let
\alpha\in M_{\ell,k}
,
\beta\in M_{k,\ell}
, and
Z\in M_{k}(M_{n}(E))
. Write
I=I_{M_{n}(E)}
and observe that since
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is linear and satisfies for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\Delta Q(I_{m}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{m}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})=I_{M_{m}}\otimes\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
, we get that

\Delta Q(I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})((\alpha\otimes I){\mathbf{Z}}(\beta\otimes I))=(\alpha\otimes I)\Delta Q(I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}},I_{k}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{Z}})(\beta\otimes I).

Hence,

\rho_{\ell,{\mathbf{X}}}\left((\alpha\otimes I){\mathbf{Z}}(\beta\otimes I)\right)\leq\|\alpha\|\|\beta\|\rho_{k,{\mathbf{X}}}({\mathbf{Z}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

If
Q\colon{\mathbb{M}}(E)\to{\mathbb{M}}(B({\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{K}}))
is an injective analytic nc function, it is not hard to check that
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is injective for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{M}}(E)
. Indeed, for every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n}(E)
, such that
\Delta Q({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{Z}})=0
,

Q\left(\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{X}}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}Q({\mathbf{X}})&0\\
0&Q({\mathbf{X}})\end{pmatrix}=Q({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{X}}).

Hence, for every point
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
, we have an operator space structure on the “tangent space” at
{\mathbf{X}}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{P}
be an nc function. Then, for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
, the linear map
\Delta f({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})
is a complete contraction.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately from
[
13
, Proposition 3.2]
and the properties of
\delta
and the nc difference-differential operator.
∎

Recall from
[
13
]
that there are several ways to define distances on an nc domain
\Omega
using
\widetilde{\delta}
. The simplest one is a level-dependent distance. Let
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
, we set:

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\inf\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})\mid m\in{\mathbb{N}},\,{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{0},{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}={\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)\}

One can remove the dependence on levels by altering the definition slightly

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\inf\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})\mid m,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},\,I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{0},{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}=I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(\ell n)\}

Lastly, one can define a distance by measuring the lengths of paths connecting the two points.

d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\inf\{\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\gamma(t),\gamma(t))(\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt\mid\gamma\in C^{1}_{pw}([0,1],\Omega(n))\}.

d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\inf\{\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\gamma(t),\gamma(t))(\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt\mid\gamma\in C^{1}_{pw}([0,1],\Omega(\ell n)),\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

Here
C^{1}_{pw}([0,1],\Omega(n))
stands for the piecewise continuously differentiable paths in
\Omega(n)
. Namely
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Omega(n)
, such that there exists finitely many
0=t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{k}=1
, such that
f
is
C^{1}
on every open segment
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
and the one-sided derivatives exist at all the
t_{i}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}(E)
be an open bounded matrix convex nc domain. Let
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
W\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
be isometries. Then, for every
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(n)
,
{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(m)
, and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in M_{n,m}(E)
, we have

\delta(V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V,W^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}W)(V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}W)\leq\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}}).

In particular, if
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
, then for every isometry
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
:

\widetilde{\delta}(V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V,V^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}V)\leq\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}).

Proof.

Note that for
t>0

\begin{pmatrix}V^{*}&0\\
0&W^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&t{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}V&0\\
0&W\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V&tV^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}W\\
0&W^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}W\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, if
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&t{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Omega(n+m)
, then since
\Omega
is matrix convex and
V\oplus W
is an isometry, we conclude that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V&tV^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}W\\
0&W^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}W\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Omega(n+m)
. Hence

A=\left\{t\in[0,\infty]\mid\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{X}}&s{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{Y}}\end{pmatrix}\in\Omega(n+m),\,\text{for all }s\in[0,t)\right\}\subseteq\\
\left\{t\in[0,\infty]\mid\begin{pmatrix}V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V&sV^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}W\\
0&W^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}W\end{pmatrix}\in\Omega(n+m),\,\text{for all }s\in[0,t)\right\}=B.

Since
\delta(V^{*}{\mathbf{X}}V,W^{*}{\mathbf{Y}}W)(V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}W)=\left(\sup B\right)^{-1}
and
\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{Z}})=\left(\sup A\right)^{-1}
, we get the desired inequality.

The second inequality follows immediately from the first.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\Omega
be an open matrix convex domain and let
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
, then

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}),\,d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}).

Proof.

By definition
\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\leq\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
and
d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\leq d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
(see also
[
13
]
). Now let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. We can find
\ell,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{0},{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}=I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(\ell n)
, such that

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})<\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

Let
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\ell n}
be the isometry that embeds
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
as the subspace spanned by the first
n
standard basis vectors. Then
V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{0}V={\mathbf{X}}
,
V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}V={\mathbf{Y}}
, and since
\Omega
is matrix convex for every
1\leq k\leq m-1
,
V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}V\in\Omega(n)
. By Lemma
3.9
we have that for every
1\leq k\leq m
,
\widetilde{\delta}(V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1}V,V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}V)\leq\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})
and thus

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}(V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1}V,V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}V)<\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

This proves that
\widetilde{d}_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
.

Similarly, let
\gamma\in C^{1}_{pw}([0,1],\Omega(\ell n))
be such that
\gamma(0)=I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{X}}
,
\gamma(1)=I_{\ell}\otimes{\mathbf{Y}}
, and

\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\gamma(t),\gamma(t))(\gamma^{\prime}(t))<d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

Let
V
be as above and set
\beta=V^{*}\gamma V\in C^{1}_{pw}([0,1],\Omega(n))
. Then,
\beta(0)={\mathbf{X}}
,
\beta(1)={\mathbf{Y}}
, and
\beta^{\prime}(t)=V^{*}\gamma^{\prime}(t)V
. Thus by Lemma
3.9
,

\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\beta(t),\beta(t))(\beta^{\prime}(t))\leq\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\gamma(t),\gamma(t))(\gamma^{\prime}(t))<d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

Thus
d_{\Omega,\infty}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})=d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\Omega
be an open matrix convex domain. Let
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
and
{\mathbf{A}}\in M_{n}(E)
. Then for every
0<t<1

\delta(t{\mathbf{X}}+(1-t){\mathbf{Y}},t{\mathbf{X}}+(1-t){\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{A}})\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}\max\{\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{A}}),\delta({\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{A}})\}.

\delta(t{\mathbf{X}}+(1-t){\mathbf{Y}},t{\mathbf{X}}+(1-t){\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{A}})\leq\max\{\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{A}}),\delta({\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{A}})\}.

Proof.

Let us define for every
0<t<1
, an isometry
V_{t}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
:

V_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{t}I_{n}\\
\sqrt{1-t}I_{n}\end{pmatrix}

Then

t{\mathbf{X}}+(1-t){\mathbf{Y}}=V_{t}^{*}({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}})V_{t}

Applying Lemma
3.9
and
[
13
, Lemma 3.5]
, we get

\delta(V_{t}^{*}({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}})V_{t},V_{t}^{*}({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}})V_{t})\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}V_{t}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathbf{A}}\\
{\mathbf{A}}&0\end{pmatrix}V_{t}\right)\leq\\
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}\delta({\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}}\oplus{\mathbf{Y}})\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathbf{A}}\\
{\mathbf{A}}&0\end{pmatrix}\right)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}\max\{\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})({\mathbf{A}}),\delta({\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{X}})({\mathbf{A}})\}.

The proof of the second inequality is identical. We only note that
{\mathbf{A}}=V_{t}^{*}({\mathbf{A}}\oplus{\mathbf{A}})V_{t}
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\Omega
be an open matrix convex domain. Then for every
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}\in U(n)
, we have that

\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\leq d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\leq\pi\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}}).

In other words, the metrics
d_{\Omega}
and
\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}
are equivalent.

Proof.

By
[
13
]
,
\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})\leq d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
. Now let
\epsilon>0
and find
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{Z}}_{0},{\mathbf{Z}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{Z}}_{m}={\mathbf{Y}}\in\Omega(n)
, such that

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})<\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\frac{\epsilon}{\pi}.

Now consider the paths
\beta_{k}(t)=t{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}+(1-t){\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1}
and concatenate them to obtain a piecewise smooth path
\beta\colon[0,1]\to\Omega(n)
, such that
\beta(0)={\mathbf{X}}
and
\beta(1)={\mathbf{Y}}
. By Lemma
3.11
we have that

\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\beta_{k}(t),\beta_{k}(t))(\beta_{k}^{\prime}(t))dt\leq\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{2\sqrt{t(1-t)}}=\pi\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{k-1},{\mathbf{Z}}_{k})

Thus,

\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\beta(t),\beta(t))(\beta^{\prime}(t))dt<\pi\widetilde{d}_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we get the result.
∎

Now we collect a few useful properties of the distance

.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\Omega
be a uniformly bounded and open matrix convex domain and
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}},{\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}}\in\Omega(n)
. Then

(i)

For

(ii)

Given
\epsilon>0
, let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Omega(n)
be a piecewise continuously differentiable path, such that
\gamma(0)={\mathbf{X}}
,
\gamma(1)={\mathbf{Y}}
, and

\int_{0}^{1}\delta(\gamma(t),\gamma(t))(\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt<d_{\Omega}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})+\epsilon.

Now note that

The second item follows immediately from Lemma

4.
Noncommutative Denjoy-Wolff theorems

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a holomorphic function that is not an automorphism and has no fixed points. In order to prove the classical Denjoy-Wolff theorem, one applies the Montel theorem to obtain convergent subsequences of the sequence
f_{n}
of iterations of
f
. We can then split the rest of the proof into two pieces. In the first part, we apply the maximum modulus principle to show that a partial limit of the sequence
f_{n}
is either constant or maps
{\mathbb{D}}
into itself. Then one applies the Wolff lemma to show that in the constant case, the value is the Wolff point of
f
and works to rule out the second option. This observation leads us to try and extend the Denjoy-Wolff-type result of Abate and Raissy to the noncommutative setting.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
be an nc domain. We will say that
\Omega
has a simple nc boundary if for every nc holomorphic function
f\colon\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{k}\to\overline{\Omega}
, if
f(\Omega^{\prime})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset
, then
f
is constant.

Example 4.2
.

By the maximum modulus principle
[
35
, Lemma 6.1]
, the row ball
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
has a simple nc boundary.

The following is an extension of the result of Abate and Raissy
[
3
, Theorem 2]
to the noncommutative setting.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
be a uniformly bounded matrix convex domain with a simple nc boundary. Assume that
\Omega(1)
is strictly convex. Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an nc map with no fixed points. Then there exists
\zeta\in\partial\Omega(1)
, such that the sequence of iterates
f^{\circ\,n}
converges to the constant function
\zeta
, where

f^{\circ\,n}=\underbrace{f\circ f\circ\cdots\circ f}_{n\text{ times}}.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 2]
, the sequence of iterates
(f^{\circ\,n})_{1}=f_{1}^{\circ\,n}
converges to the constant function
\zeta
. Now applying Montel’s theorem to
f^{\circ\,n}
, we know that the sequence has convergent subsequences. Let
f^{\circ\,n_{k}}
be a convergent subsequence and
g
its limit. Since
g_{1}=\zeta
, by the fact that
\Omega
has a simple nc boundary,
g
is constant and equals
\zeta
. Hence, every convergent subsequence converges to the same function, and thus,
f^{\circ\,n}
converges to
\zeta
uniformly on every compact.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
be an nc function without fixed points, then the sequence of iterates
f^{\circ\,n}
converges uniformly on subball
r\overline{{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}}
to the constant function
\zeta
, where
\zeta
is the Denjoy-Wolff point of
f_{1}=f|_{{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(1)}
.

Proof.

By Popescu’s Montel theorem
[
28
, Theorem 5.2]
, the sequence
f^{\circ\,n}
has subsequences that converge uniformly on subballs. Hence, we can argue as above, but the convergence is on subballs.
∎

4.1.
Maximal quantization of the unit ball

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
be a bounded open convex set and assume for simplicity that
0\in K
. By the Hahn-Banach separation theorem, for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\setminus K
, there exists
w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that for every
u\in K
,
\operatorname{Re}\langle u,w\rangle<s\leq\operatorname{Re}\langle z,w\rangle
. Since necessarily
s>0
, we may replace
w
by
\frac{1}{s}w
and set
s=1
. Hence,
K
can be identified as an intersection of open half-planes. Let us write
K^{\circ}=\left\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\mid\forall u\in K,\,\operatorname{Re}\langle u,w\rangle\leq 1\right\}
. As in
[
14
]
(in the compact case), we define the maximal matrix convex set that contains
K
by
\operatorname{Max}(K)=\sqcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{Max}(K)(n)
, where

\operatorname{Max}(K)(n)=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{d}(n)\mid\forall w\in K^{\circ},\,\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{w_{j}}Z_{j}\right)<1\right\}.

From now will focus on
\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
. Let
\overline{\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})}
be the levelwise closure of
\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
. Note that by continuity of the functionals,
\overline{\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})}\subset\operatorname{Max}(\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}})
. Conversely, if
Z\in\operatorname{Max}(\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}})
, then for every
0<r<1
,
rZ\in\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
and, thus, these sets coincide. We aim to show that the Denjoy-Wolff theorem holds for
\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
. One of the key ingredients is the maximal modulus principle. We need some preparation to prove a version of the maximum modulus principle for
\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
be an open nc set and
f\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{M}}_{1}
be a locally bounded nc function. If
\operatorname{Re}f({\mathbf{W}})=I
for all
{\mathbf{W}}\in\Omega
, then
f
is constant.

Proof.

Since
f
is levelwise holomorphic and
\Delta f({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}})({\mathbf{Z}})
is the directional derivative at
{\mathbf{W}}
. It suffices to prove that for every
{\mathbf{W}}\in\Omega
and
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
,
\Delta f({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}})({\mathbf{Z}})=0
. Note that since
\Omega
is open there exists
t>0
, such that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathbf{W}}&t{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{W}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Omega
. Hence,

\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}=\operatorname{Re}f\left(\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{W}}&{\mathbf{Z}}\\
0&{\mathbf{W}}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}I&\frac{1}{2}\Delta f({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}})({\mathbf{Z}})\\
\frac{1}{2}\Delta f({\mathbf{W}},{\mathbf{W}})({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}&I\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\operatorname{Max}(\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}})
and assume that
X_{1}=I
. Then
X_{2}=\cdots=X_{d}=0
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Max}(\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}})
is matrix convex, for every unit vector
u\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, we have that
u^{*}{\mathbf{X}}u=(1,\langle X_{2}u,u\rangle,\cdots,\langle X_{d}u,u\rangle)\in\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}}
. Which implies that the numerical ranges of
X_{2},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}
are
\{0\}
and thus these matrices are zero.

∎

The following theorem is a version of the maximum modulus from
[
35
]
for the set
\operatorname{Max}({\mathbb{B}}_{d})
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{k}
be an open unitarily-invariant nc set and let
f\colon\Omega\to\operatorname{Max}(\overline{{\mathbb{B}}_{d}})
be a locally bounded holomorphic function. If for some
{\mathbf{W}}_{0}\in\Omega

Proof.

Assume

, such that
\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{\lambda_{j}}f_{j}({\mathbf{W}}_{0})\right)

. Let

and define

\vdots\\

\operatorname{Re}\langle g({\mathbf{W}})u,u\rangle=1

In particular, the numerical range of

\operatorname{Re}g({\mathbf{W}})=I

to deduce that the restriction of

Now note that if

∎

5.
Noncommutative Vigué and Bedford theorems

In
[
43
]
, Vigué has proved that for a bounded convex domain
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, the set of fixed points of an analytic self-map is a smooth analytic subvariety. Moreover, a smooth analytic subvariety
V\subset U
is the set of fixed points of some analytic self-map if and only if
V
is a holomorphic retract of
U
, i.e., there exists
\psi\colon U\to V
analytic, such that
\psi|_{V}=\mathrm{id}_{V}
. Our goal here is to show that Vigué’s methods extend almost verbatim to the setting of bounded matrix convex sets.

By
[
2
, Proposition 2.1.8]

[
2
, Corollary 2.1.11]
, every bounded convex domain
W\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is both taut and tautly embedded in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, Hence, given a sequence of maps
f_{n}\colon W\to W
that converges uniformly on compacta to
f
, we have that
f(W)\subset W
or
f(W)\subset\partial W
.

Note that if
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
is matrix convex, then for decomposable point in
\Omega
, the restriction to the invariant subspaces and the compression to coinvariant subspaces are in
\Omega
. We say that an nc subset
\Xi\subset\Omega
is (relatively) bifull if it is closed under similarities, restrictions to invariant subspaces, and compressions to coinvariant subspaces (see
[
11
]
). To be more precise,“closed under similarities” means that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
X\in\Xi(n)
, and every
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, such that
S^{-1}XS\in\Omega
, we have that
S^{-1}XS\in\Xi
.

Theorem 5.1

(Noncommutative Vigué’s theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
be a bounded matrix convex set. Let
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset\Omega
be an nc relatively bifull subset, such that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
{\mathfrak{V}}(n)
is a smooth analytic subvariety of
\Omega(n)
. Then,
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the fixed point set of an nc map
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
, if and only if
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the image on an nc holomorphic retraction, i.e., there exists
\psi\colon\Omega\to\Omega
, such that
\psi\circ\psi=\psi
and
\psi(\Omega)={\mathfrak{V}}
.

Proof.

Just like classically, one direction is easy. If there exists an nc holomorphic retraction
\psi\colon\Omega\to{\mathfrak{V}}
, then
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the fixed point set of
\psi
. Moreover, every fixed point set is level-wise smooth by the classical theorem of Vigué and is bifull by basic properties of nc functions.

For the second direction, we can apply Vigué’s proof of his theorem and only verify small details. Let
f
be an nc map, so
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the fixed point set of
f
. Write
f_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{U}
and
f_{n}=f\circ f_{n-1}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Consider the sequence
\varphi_{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}f_{k}
. Since each
\Omega(n)
is convex, each
\varphi_{m}
is a self-map of
\Omega
. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\varphi_{m}
converges level-wise on compacta to an nc function
\varphi
. The fixed point set of
\varphi
contains
{\mathfrak{V}}
. Again, since each
\Omega(n)
is convex and bounded, it is taut, and thus
\varphi
is also a self-map of
\Omega
. Now let
\psi
be a partial limit of the sequence of iterates of
\varphi
. Refining the subsequence, if necessary, we see that
\psi
is a retraction. Arguing as in the proof of
[
43
, Theorem 6.5]
, we see that level-wise
\psi_{n}
maps
\Omega(n)
into
{\mathfrak{V}}(n)
and we are done.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

One can also argue level-wise instead of discussing nuclearity. Since on each level the Montel theorem holds, if we have a sequence of nc functions
f_{n}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
, then we can choose a subsequence
f_{n_{k}^{(1)}}
that converges on the first level and a subsequence of it
f_{n_{k}^{(2)}}
that converges on the second level and so on. Now we set
g_{k}=f_{n_{k}^{(k)}}
, and this sequence converges on all levels. The limit is, of course, an nc function.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded matrix convex set, and let
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be a nc map. Then the fixed point set of
f
contains scalar points. In other words, if
f
has no fixed points on the first level, then
f
has no fixed points.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{V}}
be the collection of fixed points of
f
. By the nc Vigué theorem, we have that
{\mathfrak{V}}
is the image of some nc holomorphic retraction. Since nc maps are graded, we have that
{\mathfrak{V}}(1)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

In
[
12
]
, Bedford proved an important dichotomy for converging iterates of self-maps of a taut domain. The following result is a straightforward generalization of Bedford’s theorem to the setting of uniformly bounded matrix convex domains.

Theorem 5.4

(Noncommutative Bedford theorem)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
be a uniformly bounded matrix convex domain. Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an nc self-map. Let
f^{\circ n}=\underbrace{f\circ f\circ\cdots\circ f}_{n\text{ times}}
be the sequence of iterates of
f
. Suppose the sequence
f^{\circ n_{k}}
converges to
g
level-wise on compacta. In that case, we have that either for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
g(\Omega)\subset\partial\Omega
or there exists an nc analytic subvariety of
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset\Omega
, an nc automorphism
\varphi\colon{\mathfrak{V}}\to{\mathfrak{V}}
and a holomorphic retraction
\psi\colon\Omega\to{\mathfrak{V}}
, such that
g=\varphi\circ\psi
.

Proof.

Due to tautness on every level,
g_{n}(\Omega(n))
is either a subset of
\partial\Omega(n)
or of
\Omega(n)
. Now assume that there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
g_{n_{0}}(\Omega(n_{0}))\subset\partial\Omega(n_{0})
. Let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega(1)
, then
I_{n_{0}}\otimes g({\mathbf{X}})=g(I_{n_{0}}\otimes{\mathbf{X}})\in\partial\Omega(n_{0})
. Since
\Omega
is closed under direct sums and direct summands,
g({\mathbf{X}})\in\partial\Omega(1)
. By tautness
g(\Omega(1))\subset\partial\Omega(1)
. Thus, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
I_{n}\otimes g({\mathbf{X}})\in\partial\Omega(n)
and applying tautness again we conclude that
g_{n}(\Omega(n))\subset\partial\Omega(n)
.

Assume that
g(\Omega)\subset\Omega
. The proof proceeds as the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
. We record the proof here for the sake of completeness. Passing to subsequences, we may assume that
m_{k}=n_{k+1}-n_{k}
and
\ell_{k}=n_{k+1}-2n_{k}
are strictly increasing, and the sequences
f^{\circ m_{k}}
and
f^{\circ\ell_{k}}
converge (using the fact that the space of all level-wise holomorphic nc functions is a Montel space). Denote

\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{\circ m_{k}}=\psi,\,\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{\circ\ell_{k}}=\tau.

Since
f^{\circ m_{k}}\circ f^{\circ n_{k}}=f^{\circ n_{k+1}}=f^{\circ n_{k}}\circ f^{\circ m_{k}}
, we have that
\psi\circ g=g=g\circ\psi
. Similarly,
\tau\circ g=\psi=g\circ\tau
. In particular,
\psi
fixes the image of
g
. Applying the argument above we conclude that
\psi(\Omega)\subset\Omega
and
\tau(\Omega)\subset\Omega
. Set
{\mathfrak{V}}=\operatorname{Fix}(\psi)
and note that
g(\Omega)\subset{\mathfrak{V}}
. For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in\Omega(n)
, we have
\ker Dg_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})=\ker D\psi_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})
since by the chain rule
Dg_{n}(\psi_{n}({\mathbf{Z}}))D\psi_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})=Dg_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})
and
D\psi_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})=D\tau_{n}(g_{n}({\mathbf{Z}}))Dg_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})
. Note that
{\mathfrak{W}}(n)=\psi_{n}^{-1}({\mathfrak{V}}(n))
is an analytic subvariety of
\Omega(n)
cut out by
\psi(\psi({\mathbf{Z}}))-\psi({\mathbf{Z}})
. The dimension of the fiber of
{\mathfrak{W}}(n)
over every point
{\mathbf{Z}}\in g_{n}(\Omega(n))
is precisely
dn^{2}-\dim\ker D\psi_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})=dn^{2}-\dim\ker Dg_{n}({\mathbf{Z}})
. Thus
{\mathfrak{W}}(n)
is of dimension
dn^{2}
, and we conclude that
\psi
is a holomorphic retract with image
{\mathfrak{V}}
. Hence
{\mathfrak{V}}
is a smooth nc analytic subvariety of
\Omega
.

Lastly note that for every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathfrak{V}}
,
\tau(g({\mathbf{Z}}))=g(\tau({\mathbf{Z}}))=\psi({\mathbf{Z}})={\mathbf{Z}}
. Hence
g({\mathfrak{V}})={\mathfrak{V}}
and
g|_{{\mathfrak{V}}}
is an automorphism of
{\mathfrak{V}}
with inverse
\tau|_{{\mathfrak{V}}}
. Set
\varphi=g|_{{\mathfrak{V}}}
and thus
g=\varphi\circ\psi
.
∎

5.1.
Application to the isomorphism problem

Recall that
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
is the operator algebra of bounded nc analytic functions on
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
. Moreover,
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
is the multiplier algebra of the noncommutative reproducing kernel Hilbert space of the nc Szego kernel. This implies that
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
is a dual operator algebra
[
35
]
. The Hilbert space that
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
acts upon can be identified with the full Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{d}

[
10
,
35
]
. Thus,
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
is unitarily equivalent to the weak-* closed subalgebra of operators on the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}_{d}
, generated by the left creation operators (the regular free semigroup algebra in the language of Davidson and Pitts
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
).

Given a subset of bounded nc functions
S\subset H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
we consider the nc analytic subvariety cut out by
S
:

{\mathfrak{V}}(S)=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\mid\forall f\in S,\,f({\mathbf{Z}})=0\right\}.

It is immediate that
{\mathfrak{V}}(S)={\mathfrak{V}}({\mathcal{I}})
, where
{\mathcal{I}}
is the weak-* closed two-sided ideal generated by
S
. Conversely, given a set of points
{\mathfrak{S}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
, we define

{\mathcal{I}}({\mathfrak{S}})=\left\{f\in H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})\mid f|_{{\mathfrak{S}}}=0\right\}.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{I}}({\mathfrak{S}})
is weak-* closed two sided ideal in
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
. In this section, we say that
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
is an nc analytic subvariety if
{\mathfrak{V}}({\mathcal{I}}({\mathfrak{V}}))={\mathfrak{V}}
(in other words,
{\mathfrak{V}}
is Zariski closed in the appropriate sense).

Given a non-empty analytic subvariety
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
, we have that
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})/{\mathcal{I}}({\mathfrak{V}})
is the algebra of bounded nc analytic functions on
{\mathfrak{V}}
. It can also be identified with the left multiplier algebra of an appropriate nc reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
{\mathfrak{V}}
. We will denote this algebra by
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{V}})
.

The following results are necessary for our discussion:

Theorem 5.5

(
[
35
, Theorem 6.12, Corollary 6.14]
)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
and
{\mathfrak{W}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}
be two nc analytic varieties. Let
\alpha\colon H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{V}})\to H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{W}})
be a completely isometric isomorphism. Then there exists nc maps
g\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
and
f\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}
, such that

f\circ g|_{{\mathfrak{W}}}=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{W}}},\quad g\circ f|_{{\mathfrak{V}}}=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{V}}}.

Moreover, composition with
g
implements
\alpha
.

Theorem 5.6

(
[
37
, Theorem 3.12]
)
.

Let
f\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
be an nc map, such that
f(0)=0
. Let
\operatorname{Fix}(f)
be the set of fixed points of
f
. If
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
is a linear subspace, such that
V\cap{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(1)=\operatorname{Fix}(f)(1)
, then for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\operatorname{Fix}(f)(n)=\left(V\otimes M_{n}\right)\cap{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(n)
.

This theorem can be read differently. Recall that for an analytic self-map of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(1)
that fixes the origin, the fixed point set coincides with the fixed subspace of
f^{\prime}(0)
intersected with
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(1)
. Since for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\Delta f(0_{n},0_{n})=f^{\prime}(0)\otimes I_{M_{n}}
is the derivative of
f_{n}
at
0_{n}
. We see that the fixed point set of
f_{n}
is precisely the set of fixed points of
\Delta f(0_{n},0_{n})
intersected with
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(n)
.

Now with these theorems at hand, we can prove the following theorem, which is a strengthening of both
[
37
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
19
, Theorem 4.5]
. Recall that for
S\subset{\mathbb{M}}_{d}
, the matrix span of
S
is
\operatorname{mat-span}(S)=\sqcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{mat-span}(S)(n)
, where

\operatorname{mat-span}(S)(n)=\operatorname{Span}\left\{(I_{d}\otimes T)({\mathbf{X}})\mid{\mathbf{X}}\in S(n),\,T\in{\mathcal{L}}(M_{n})\right\}.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
and
{\mathfrak{W}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}
be two nc analytic varieties. If
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{V}})
and
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{W}})
are completely isometrically isomorphic, then there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an automorphism
F\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}
, such that
{\mathfrak{V}},{\mathfrak{W}}\subset{\mathfrak{B}}_{k}
and
F
maps
{\mathfrak{V}}
onto
{\mathfrak{W}}
.

Proof.

Applying
[
35
, Lemma 8.2]
we can may assume that for
n>>0
, we have that
\operatorname{mat-span}({\mathfrak{V}})(n)={\mathbb{C}}^{d}\otimes M_{n}
and
\operatorname{mat-span}({\mathfrak{W}})(n)={\mathbb{C}}^{e}\otimes M_{n}
. Let
g\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
and
f\colon{\mathfrak{B}}_{e}\to{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
be the nc maps guaranteed by Theorem
5.5
. Set
h=g\circ f
and observe that
{\mathfrak{V}}\subset\operatorname{Fix}(h)
. However, by Corollary
5.3
,
\operatorname{Fix}(h)(1)\neq\emptyset
. Pre- and post-composing with automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
(they induce unitary equivalence on
H^{\infty}({\mathfrak{B}}_{d})
), we may assume that
0\in\operatorname{Fix}(h)(1)
. Thus, by Theorem
5.6
, there exists a subspace
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, such that
\operatorname{Fix}(h)(n)=V\otimes M_{n}
. As mentioned above,
\operatorname{Fix}(h)(n)
coincides with the fixed points of the linear operator
\Delta f(0_{n},0_{n})=f^{\prime}(0)\otimes I_{M_{n}}
. Therefore,
\operatorname{mat-span}({\mathfrak{V}})(n)\cap{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}(n)\subset\operatorname{Fix}(h)(n)
. Thus, by assumption on
{\mathfrak{V}}
,
\operatorname{Fix}(h)(n)={\mathbb{C}}^{d}\otimes M_{n}
, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Applying the same argument to
f\circ g
, we see that
d=e
, and
f
and
g
are automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{d}
that map
{\mathfrak{V}}
onto
{\mathfrak{W}}
and vice versa.
∎

6.
Horospheres in generalized balls

Let us assume that
Q(0)=0
and
Q
is nc analytic. For every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
,

\widetilde{\delta}(0,{\mathbf{Z}})=\left\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}Q({\mathbf{Z}})\right\|.

Let
T=Q({\mathbf{Z}})Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}
, then

\widetilde{\delta}(0,{\mathbf{Z}})^{2}=\left\|T(I-T)^{-1}\right\|=\sup_{x\in\sigma(T)}\frac{x}{1-x}.

Since the function
\frac{x}{1-x}
is monotonically increasing on
[0,1)
,
\widetilde{\delta}(0,{\mathbf{Z}})^{2}=\frac{\|T\|}{1-\|T\|}=\frac{\|Q({\mathbf{Z}})\|^{2}}{1-\|Q({\mathbf{Z}})\|^{2}}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T
be a positive operator with
\|T\|<1
, then
(1-\|T\|)(1-T)^{-1}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since
T\leq\|T\|
, then
1-T\geq 1-\|T\|
. Now multiplying on left and right by
(1-T)^{-1/2}
, we get
1\geq(1-\|T\|)(1-T)^{-1}
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

For every
{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
, then
\widetilde{\delta}(0,{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})
is bounded as
{\mathbf{W}}
approaches a point on the boundary.

Proof.

Note that

\widetilde{\delta}(0,{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})=\\
\sqrt{\frac{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}{\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}}\left\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\right\|.

Note that
\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|\to 1
, as
{\mathbf{W}}
approaches the boundary of
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
. By the preceding lemma applied to
T=Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}Q({\mathbf{W}})
,

D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))(1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}-Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*})D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}\leq\\
D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))(Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}-Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*})D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}.

The right-hand side is bounded in norm, and we are done.
∎

Definition 6.3
.

Let
\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(1)
. We denote by
w
points in
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(1)
. The big and small horospheres of radius
R>0
at
\zeta
are

F_{R}(\zeta)(n)=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}\mid\liminf_{w\to\zeta}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w\otimes I_{n})<R\right\},

E_{R}(\zeta)(n)=\left\{{\mathbf{Z}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}\mid\limsup_{w\to\zeta}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w\otimes I_{n})<R\right\}.

Note that since
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
is unitary invariant and closed under direct sums, for every
{\mathbf{W}}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
,
{\mathbf{W}}\otimes I_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
, since it is up to a canonical shuffle
I_{n}\otimes{\mathbf{W}}={\mathbf{W}}^{\oplus n}\in D_{Q}
. Moreover, since
Q
is nc we have that
Q({\mathbf{W}}\otimes I_{n})=Q({\mathbf{W}})\otimes I_{n}
. For a point
w\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(1)
, we have
w\otimes I_{n}=I_{n}\otimes w
.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
is matrix convex, then for every
R>0
and
\zeta\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(1)
,
E_{R}(\zeta)
is matrix convex.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{Z}}\in E_{R}(\zeta)(n)
and
V\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
an isometry. Then, for
w\in{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}(1)
,
V^{*}(w\otimes I_{n})V=w\otimes I_{m}
and thus, by Lemma
3.9
, we have that
\widetilde{\delta}(V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}V,w\otimes I_{m})\leq\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w\otimes I_{n})
and thus
V^{*}{\mathbf{Z}}V\in E_{R}(\zeta)(n)
. Moreover, if
{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\in E_{R}(\zeta)(n)
and
{\mathbf{Z}}_{2}\in E_{R}(\zeta)(m)
, then

\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1}\oplus{\mathbf{Z}}_{2},w\otimes I_{n+m})=\max\left\{\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1},w\otimes I_{n}),\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}}_{2},w\otimes I_{m})\right\}.

Hence,
E_{R}(\zeta)
is closed under direct sums.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Fix a domain bounded
{\mathbb{D}}_{Q}

(i)

For all

.

(ii)

Example 6.6
.

In the classical case of the ball, we recover the classical definition of a horosphere in the ball. Indeed, if
\zeta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. In the case of the ball
Q({\mathbf{W}})
is the operator
{\mathbf{W}}
, hence for
w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
, we have
Q(w)Q(w)^{*}=\|w\|^{2}
and
Q(w)^{*}Q(w)
is a rank one operator in
M_{d}
.

For
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{d}
,

\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-2}\widetilde{\delta}(z,w)^{2}=\frac{1-\|w\|^{2}}{\|w\|^{2}(1-\|z\|^{2})}(Q(z)-Q(w))(1-Q(w)^{*}Q(w))^{-1}(Q(z)-Q(w))^{*}

Write
v=Q(w)^{*}
, a column vector, and let
P_{v}
be the orthogonal projection on the subspace spanned by
v
. Note that
P_{v}=\frac{1}{\|v\|^{2}}vv^{*}
. Therefore,

(1-Q(w)^{*}Q(w))^{-1}=(1-vv^{*})^{-1}=(1-\|v\|^{2}P_{v})^{-1}=\\
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|v\|^{2n}P_{v}^{n}=I+\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{1-\|v\|^{2}}P_{v}.

Now multiplying by
1-\|Q(w)\|^{2}
we get that

(1-\|Q(w)\|^{2})(1-Q(w)^{*}Q(w))^{-1}=(1-\|Q(w)\|^{2})+\|Q(w)\|^{2}P_{Q(w)^{*}}.

Thus, as

, we get that
\lim_{w\to\zeta}(1-\|Q(w)\|^{2})(1-Q(w)^{*}Q(w))^{-1}=P_{Q(\zeta)^{*}}

\lim_{w\to\zeta}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-2}\widetilde{\delta}(z,w)^{2}=\frac{|1-z_{1}|^{2}}{1-\|z\|^{2}}.

Therefore,
E_{R}(\zeta)(1)=F_{R}(\zeta)(1)
in this case and are the classical horospheres in the ball. Moreover, note that since the row
Q(w)
is scalar, we have that
Q(w)\otimes I_{n}=I_{n}\otimes Q(w)
. Hence we have

(1-\|w\|)^{2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q(w)\otimes I_{n})(1-Q(w)^{*}Q(w)\otimes I_{n})^{-1}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q(w)\otimes I_{n})^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w\to\zeta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\\
(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q(\zeta)\otimes I_{n})P_{\zeta}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q(\zeta)\otimes I_{n})^{*}=(I-Z_{1})(I-Z_{1})^{*}.

Hence,

\lim_{w\to\zeta}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-2}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w\otimes I_{n})^{2}=\|D_{{\mathbf{Z}}}^{-1/2}(I-Z_{1})\|.

In particular, the limit exists. Thus,
E_{R}(\zeta)=F_{R}(\zeta)
. Moreover, for
{\mathbf{Z}}\in E_{r}(\zeta)

(I-Z_{1})(I-Z_{1})^{*}<R^{2}(I-{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{Z}}^{*}).

Thus, we recover Popescu’s definition
[
32
]
of a horosphere in the free ball.

Lemma 6.7
.

For three distinct points
{\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}}\in D_{Q}(n)
. Then,

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})\leq\dfrac{\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{X}})}{\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|-\|Q({\mathbf{X}})\|}+\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}}^{-1/2}D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}\|.

Proof.

By the formula in Lemma
3.1

\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})=\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|\leq\\
\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{Z}})-Q({\mathbf{X}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|+\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{X}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|\leq\\
\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{X}})\|D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|+\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}}^{-1/2}D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}\|.

Since the points are distinct
\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})\neq 0
. Moreover, since
Q({\mathbf{X}})
is a contraction, we have that
\|D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}\|\leq 1
. Hence, we obtain the following inequality:

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})\leq\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{X}})\frac{\|D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|}{\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})}+\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}}^{-1/2}D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}\|.

We need to bound
\frac{\|D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|}{\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})}
. By functional calculus,
\|D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}}
. Hence, we will bound
\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})
from bellow. Again, since
Q({\mathbf{X}})
is a strict contraction,
(1-Q({\mathbf{X}})Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*})^{-1}\geq 1
. Let us write

T=D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{X}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2},

Then,

T^{*}T\geq D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}(Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}-Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*})(Q({\mathbf{X}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}.

Hence,

\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})=\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\|T\|\geq\\
\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\|(Q({\mathbf{X}})-Q({\mathbf{W}}))D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|\geq\\
\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\left(\|Q({\mathbf{W}})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|-\|Q({\mathbf{X}})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|\right)

Now, if
A=Q({\mathbf{W}})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}
, then
A^{*}A=Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}Q({\mathbf{W}})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1}
. Thus, by functional calculus,
\|A\|=\frac{\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|}{\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}}
. Now, for the second summand, we have

\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\|Q({\mathbf{X}})D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|\leq\\
\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\|Q({\mathbf{X}})\|\|D_{Q({\mathbf{W}})^{*}}^{-1/2}\|=\|Q({\mathbf{X}})\|.

In summary,

\sqrt{1-\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|^{2}}\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})\geq\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|-\|Q({\mathbf{X}})\|.

Thus,

\delta({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{W}})^{-1}\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})\leq\dfrac{\delta({\mathbf{Z}},{\mathbf{W}})}{\|Q({\mathbf{W}})\|-\|Q({\mathbf{X}})\|}+\|D_{Q({\mathbf{Z}})^{*}}^{-1/2}D_{Q({\mathbf{X}})^{*}}^{1/2}\|.

∎

Theorem 6.8

(Noncommutative Wolff theorem)
.

Let
0\in\Omega={\mathbb{D}}_{Q}
be a bounded matrix convex set. Let
f\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an nc map with no fixed points. Then there exists
\xi\in\partial\Omega(1)
, such that for every
R>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f^{\circ n}(E_{R}(\xi))\subseteq F_{R}(\xi)
.

Proof.

Let
0<r_{m}<1
be a sequence that monotonically increases to
1
. Let
f_{m}({\mathbf{Z}})=f(r_{m}{\mathbf{Z}})
. By Lemma
3.4
,
f_{m}
has a unique fixed point
w_{m}\in\Omega(1)
. Any accumulation point of
w_{m}
in
\Omega(1)
will be a fixed point of
f
. Hence (passing to a subsequence, if necessary), we may assume that
w_{m}\to\xi\in\partial\Omega
. Fix
R>0
, and let
{\mathbf{Z}}\in E_{R}(\xi)(n)
. Then

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w_{m})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w_{m}\otimes I_{n})\leq\limsup_{w\to\xi}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w\otimes I_{n})<R.

Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Then, for every
m
big enough,

\widetilde{\delta}(0,w_{m})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w_{m}\otimes I_{n})<R-\varepsilon.

By
[
13
, Corollary 3.4]
and the fact that
w_{m}
is a fixed point of
f_{m}^{\circ n}
, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we conclude that for every
m>m_{0}
,

\widetilde{\delta}(f_{m}^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),f_{m}^{\circ n}(w_{m})\otimes I_{n})=\widetilde{\delta}(f_{m}^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})\leq\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w_{m}\otimes I_{n})<\widetilde{\delta}(0,w_{m})(R-\varepsilon).

Now, fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For every
{\mathbf{X}}\in\Omega
,
\lim_{m\to\infty}f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{X}})=f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{X}})
. By the continuity of
\widetilde{\delta}
, we have that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{X}}),f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{X}}))=0
. By Lemma
6.7
,

\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})\leq\\
\frac{\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}))}{\|Q(w_{m})\|-\|Q(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}))\|}+\|D_{Q(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}))^{*}}^{-1/2}D_{Q(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}))^{*}}^{1/2}\|.

Since
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{\|Q(w_{m})\|-\|Q(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}))\|}=\frac{1}{1-\|Q(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}))\|}
, we get that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})\leq 1.

In particular, for
m>>0
,

\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})<(1+\varepsilon)\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}_{m}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\widetilde{\delta}({\mathbf{Z}},w_{m}\otimes I_{n}).

Hence, if we take
0<\delta<\frac{\varepsilon}{R-\varepsilon}
, then again for every
m
big enough,

\widetilde{\delta}(0,w_{m})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})<(1+\delta)(R-\varepsilon)<R.

This implies that

\liminf_{w\to\zeta}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w)^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w\otimes I_{n})\leq\\
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\widetilde{\delta}(0,w_{m})^{-1}\widetilde{\delta}(f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}}),w_{m}\otimes I_{n})<R.

Thus, by definition,
f^{\circ n}({\mathbf{Z}})\in F_{R}(\zeta)
.
∎

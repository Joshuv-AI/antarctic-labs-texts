Abstract
. A vertex coloring of a graph G G is called a 2-distance coloring if any two vertices at distance at most 2 2 from each other receive different colors. Let G G be a planar graph with girth at least 5 5 . We prove that G G admits a 2 2 -distance coloring with Δ + 4 \Delta+4 colors if Δ ≥ 22 \Delta\geq 22 .



1.
Introduction

All graphs in this paper are assumed to be simple, i.e., finite and undirected, with no loops or multiple edges. We refer to
[
16
]
for terminology and notation not defined here. Let
G
be a graph, we use
V(G),E(G),F(G),\Delta(G)
and
g(G)
to denote the vertex, edge and face set, the maximum degree and girth of
G
, respectively. If there is no confusion in the context, we abbreviate
\Delta(G),g(G)
to
\Delta,g
.
A 2-distance coloring is a vertex coloring where two vertices that are adjacent or have a common neighbour receive different colors, and the smallest number of colors for which
G
admits a 2-distance coloring is known as the 2-distance chromatic number
\chi_{2}(G)
of
G
. A detailed survey on 2-distance coloring and related types of coloring is provided by Cranston
[
3
]
.

In 1977, Wegner
[
15
]
posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For every planar graph
G
,
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 7
if
\Delta=3
,
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+5
if
4\leq\Delta\leq 7
, and
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\big\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\big\rfloor+1
if
\Delta\geq 8
,

Wegner also constructed a graph to show that the upper bound for the case
\Delta\geq 8
is indeed the best possible. While the conjecture remains widely open, there have been some partial solution. Thomassen
[
14
]
(independently by Hartke et al.
[
8
]
) proved the conjecture for planar graphs with
\Delta=3
. Moreover, there are upper bounds for
2
-distance chromatic number of planar graphs. For instance, van den Heuvel and McGuinness
[
9
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq 2\Delta+25
, and Molloy and Salavatipour
[
13
]
established the bound
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\big\lceil\frac{5\Delta}{3}\big\rceil+78
.

For planar graphs with girth restrictions, La and Montassier
[
12
]
provided a concise overview of the most recent findings. For instance, Bu and Zhu
[
1
]
showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+5
if
G
is a planar graph with
g\geq 6
, which confirms the Conjecture
1.1
for the planar graphs with girth six. This result was further refined to
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4
when
\Delta\geq 6
in
[
4
]
. Additionally, La
[
11
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+3
under two conditions: either
g\geq 7
and
\Delta\geq 6
, or
g\geq 8
and
\Delta\geq 4
.

When
G
is a planar graph with a girth of at least
5
, Dong and Lin
[
6
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+8
. This result was later improved in
[
7
]
to
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+7
when
\Delta\notin\{7,8\}
. Recently, Deniz
[
5
]
extended this results as
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+7
for every
\Delta
. Dong and Lin
[
7
]
also showed that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4
if
\Delta\geq 150
. More recently, Jin and Miao
[
10
]
proved that
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4
if
\Delta\geq 40
, which is a corollary of a
2
-distance list coloring.

We focus on
2
-distance coloring of the planar graphs with girth at least
5
, and we improve a result of Dong and Lin
[
7
]
and partially extend the result of Jin and Miao
[
10
]
as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
G
is a planar graph with
g\geq 5
and
\Delta\geq 22
, then
\chi_{2}(G)\leq\Delta+4
.

Given a planar graph

, we denote by

and by

.
A

-vertex is a vertex of degree at least

(

-vertex adjacent to

2
-vertices.

For a vertex

For a path

2.1.
The Structure of Minimum Counterexample

Let

be a counterexample to Theorem

\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta\geq 22

,

We will prove that

exists.
Notice that, by minimality,
G-x
has a 2-distance coloring with
\Delta+4

. Obviously,

\delta(G)\geq 2
.

We begin with introducing the concept of light and heavy vertices. A vertex

the number of light vertices adjacent to

.
If

is a vertex with

, then it is called

. It is worth noting that every

-vertex adjacent to a

-vertex is a light vertex, so

for any vertex

.

Let us now outline some structural properties that

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

be a vertex in
G
with

. If
G
has a 2-distance coloring
f
with
\Delta+4
colors such that

is an uncolored vertex, then

always has an available color.

Proof.

Let
v
be a vertex in
G
with
D(v)<\Delta+4+n^{l}(v)
. Denote by
S
the set of light vertices that are adjacent to
v
, and denote by
R
the set of all
2
-neighbours of

that are adjacent to a
3
-vertex. Obviously, we have
R\subseteq S
, also
n_{2}^{3}(v)=|R|
and
n^{l}(v)=|S|
. Consider a 2-distance coloring
f
with
\Delta+4
colors such that
v
is an uncolored vertex, we decolor all vertices in
S
. Since
v
has an available color as
D(v)<\Delta+4+n^{l}(v)
, we give an available color to it. On the other hand, we can easily recolor every vertex in
S
because each of them is a light vertex. Indeed, since we decolor all vertices in
R
, each vertex in
S\setminus R
has an available color. Therefore, we first recolor all vertices in
S\setminus R
, and then the vertices in
R
with available colors. Thus, we obtain a proper 2-distance coloring of
G
with
\Delta+4
colors.
∎

Consider 2-distance coloring of
G
with
\Delta+4
colors, where a vertex

that if

is not heavy, then it always has an available color. We therefore have the following.

Corollary 2.2
.

If

is a light vertex, then any 2-distance coloring

We will now prove that every neighbour of a light vertex is a heavy vertex. This result will be used further to derive some forbidden configurations.

Proposition 2.3
.

If

is a light vertex in

, then each neighbour of

is a heavy vertex.

Proof.

Let

be a light vertex. Assume to the contrary that there exists

such that it is not heavy, i.e.,

. By minimality,

has a 2-distance coloring
f
with
\Delta+4

S
the set of light vertices that are adjacent to

. Obviously,

and

. We first decolor all vertices in

. Now,

has at most
\Delta+3

, so we recolor

with an available color. On the other hand, each vertex in

has an available color by Corollary

, since they are light vertices. Therefore, we give an available color to each vertex of

so that we obtain a proper 2-distance coloring with

∎

David Nualart, Bhargobjyoti Saikia

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to establish the convergence in distribution of the normalized error in the Euler approximation scheme for stochastic Volterra equations driven by a standard Brownian motion, with a kernel of the form ( t − s ) α (t-s)^{\alpha} , where α ∈ ( − 1 2 , 1 2 ) \alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}) .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
60H20,60F05

1.
Introduction

There have been several studies on stochastic Volterra equations starting from the pioneering work by Berger and Mizel
[
1
,
2
]
. We could mention, for instance, the papers by Protter
[
11
]
, Pardoux and Protter
[
10
]
, Cochran, Lee and Potthoff
[
4
]
, Coutin and Decreusefond
[
6
]
, Decreusefond
[
7
]
, Wang
[
14
]
, Zhang
[
17
]
, and the references therein.

In this paper we are interested in the following stochastic Volterra equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{s})\,dW_{s},\qquad t\in[0,T],

where
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
,
\sigma
is a Lipschitz function and

W=\{W_{t},t\geq[0,T]\}
is a standard Brownian motion defined in a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
.
Our aim is to study the fluctuations of the error in the Euler approximation scheme for this equation. It is known that the rate of convergence of the Euler approximation scheme is of the order
n^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
. We refer for this to the work by Li, Huang and Hu
[
9
]
, where they consider the more general
\theta
-Euler and Milstein schemes in the singular case
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<0
, and the papers by
Zhang
[
16
]
and Richard, Tan and Yang
[
13
]
, where the Euler approximation scheme for more general stochastic Volterra equations is studied.
However, to the best of our knowledge, there have been no results on the convergence in distribution of the normalized error, analogous to the case of ordinary stochastic differential equations (see, for instance, the work by Jacod and Protter
[
8
]
).

The Euler scheme approximation for the solution to equation (
1.1
) is defined as

(1.2)

\displaystyle X^{n}_{t}=X_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,dW_{s},

where
\eta_{n}(s)=\frac{\lfloor ns\rfloor}{n}
. That is,
\eta_{n}(s)=\frac{j}{n}
if
\frac{j}{n}\leq s<\frac{j+1}{n}
for some integer
j\geq 0
.
Consider the normalized error defined by

(1.3)

Y^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}).

It is known that
Y^{n}_{t}
is bounded in
L^{p}
, for any
p\geq 2
, uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
and
n\geq 1
(see Lemma
3.1
) for a short proof.
This means that the rate of convergence to zero as
n\to\infty
in
L^{p}
of
X^{n}_{t}-X_{t}
is of the order
n^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
.
The goal of this paper is to study the asymptotic behavior of the normalized error (
1.3
).

We will describe here the approach and the challenging difficulties encountered in handling the limit distribution of the normalized error

Y^{n}_{t}
. This error can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}]\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,dW_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}[\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})-\sigma(X_{s})]\,dW_{s}

(1.4)

\displaystyle:=A^{n}_{t}+Y^{n,1}_{t}.

The term
A^{n}_{t}
has an unexpected asymptotic behavior. Indeed, it only converges at points of the form
t=\eta_{n}(t)

(see Remark 1 below) and its limit in law is

\kappa_{1}\sigma(X_{t})Z_{t}
, where
\kappa_{1}
is a constant defined in (
2.1
) and
Z_{t}
is a
N(0,1)
random variable independent of the Brownian motion
W
. Moreover, (see Proposition
4.3
) the finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{A^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
converge to those of

\{\kappa_{1}\sigma(X_{t})Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
, where
\{Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a centered Gaussian process independent of
W
with covariance

(1.5)

E[Z_{t_{1}}Z_{t_{2}}]=\mathbf{1}_{\{t_{1}=t_{2}\}}.

Notice that the limit in distribution of
A^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)}
does not have any Hölder continuity properties, which creates problems in the global analysis of the error. For this reason, we have split the study of the error in two parts, corresponding to both terms in the above decomposition.
For the term
Y^{n,1}_{t}
, the limit in distribution can be expressed as the solution to a stochastic Volterra equation, with an input given by a stochastic integral term with respect to an independent Brownian motion
B
. A precise statement is given in Theorem
2.1
.
The proof of the convergence of the term
Y^{n,1}_{t}
relies on the application of the asymptotic version of Knight’s theorem. Finally, in Theorem
2.2
we establish the convergence in distribution of the couple of random variables
(A^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)},Y^{n,1}_{t})
, which implies, as a consequence, the limit in law of the normalized error
Y^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)}
given in Corollary
2.1
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the main result. In Section 3 we introduce some preliminaries and in Section 4 we present the proof of the main result. Some technical auxiliary results are included in the Apppendix.

2.
Main result

Let
X=\{X_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
be the solution to the stochastic Volterra equation (
1.1
).
Consider the Euler approximation scheme
X^{n}=\{X^{n}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
defined in (
1.2
).
The following theorem, which deals with the convergence of the processes
A^{n}_{t}
and
Y^{n,1}_{t}
appearing in the decomposition of the normalized error, is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Y^{n}=\{Y^{n}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
be the normalized error in the Euler approximation scheme of equation (
1.1
) defined in (
1.3
).
Suppose that
\sigma
is continuously differentiable and
\sigma^{\prime}
is Hölder continuous of order
\beta
for some
\beta\in(0,1]
.
Consider the decomposition introduced in (
1.4
). Then the following results hold true:

(i)

As
n
tends to infinity, the finite-dimensional distributions of the process

\{A^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
converge in distribution to those of
\kappa_{1}\sigma(X_{t})Z_{t}
, where

(2.1)

\kappa_{1}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}[k^{\alpha}-(k-x)^{\alpha}]^{2}dx}

and
\{Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a zero mean Gaussian process independent of
W
with covariance (
1.5
).

(ii)

As
n
tends to infinity, the finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{Y^{n,1}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
converge in distribution to those of
\{Y^{\infty,1}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
, where
Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
is the solution to the following stochastic Volterra equation

Y^{\infty,1}_{t}=\kappa_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})Y^{\infty,1}_{s}dW_{s},

where
B=\{B_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
W
and

(2.2)

\kappa_{2}=\sqrt{\kappa_{3}+\frac{2\alpha+1}{2(\alpha+1)^{2}}+\kappa_{4}+\kappa_{5}},

with

(2.3)

\kappa_{3}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left[(x+k)^{\alpha}-k^{\alpha}\right]^{2}dx,

(2.4)

\kappa_{4}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(y+k)}[(y+k)^{\alpha}-(y+k-z)^{\alpha}]^{2}dzdy.

and

(2.5)

\kappa_{5}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[(y+k)^{\alpha}-(y+k-z)^{\alpha}][(y+k)^{\alpha}-k^{\alpha}]dzdy.

In the next result we analyze the joint convergence of
A^{n}_{t}
and
Y^{n,1}_{t}
for a fixed
t\in[0,T]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, for any
t\in[0,T]
, the two-dimensional random variable

(A^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)},Y^{n,1}_{t})
converges in distribution to
(\kappa_{1}\sigma(X_{t})Z_{t},Y^{\infty,1}_{t})
, where
Z_{t}
and
Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
are the random variables described in points (i) and (ii) of Theorem
2.1
and
Z_{t}
and
Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
are independent.

As a consequence of theorems
2.1
and
2.2
we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, for any
t\in[0,T]
, the normalized error converges in distribution to

\kappa_{1}\sigma(X_{t})Z_{t}+Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
, where
Z_{t}
and
Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
are the random variables described in points (i) and (ii) of Theorem
2.1
and
Z_{t}
and
Y^{\infty,1}_{t}
are independent.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section we will present some basic properties of the solution to the stochastic Volterra equation (
1.1
) and its associated Euler approximation scheme (
1.2
).
Throughout this paper
C
will denote a generic constant, which may depend on
\alpha
, the coefficient
\sigma
and the terminal time
T
.
Also,
C_{p}
will denote a generic constant, which may depend on
p
,
\alpha
, the coefficient
\sigma
and the terminal time
T
.
Moreover, we will denote by
\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
the increasing family of
\sigma
-algebras generated by the Brownian motion
W
.

Assuming that the coefficient
\sigma
is Lipschitz, there exist a unique solution to equation (
1.1
), such that
for any
p\geq 2
,

(3.1)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E[|X_{t}|^{p}]<C_{p}.

Furthermore, for any
p\geq 2
and for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

(3.2)

E[|X_{t}-X_{s}|^{p}]\leq C_{p}|t-s|^{p(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}.

We refer, for instance, to Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 of
[
9
]
for the proof of these estimates in the case
\alpha<0
. The case
\alpha\geq 0
is easier and can be obtained by similar arguments.

The Euler approximation
X^{n}_{t}
satisfies similar estimates. That means, for any
p\geq 2
, we have

(3.3)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E[|X^{n}_{t}|^{p}]<C_{p}

and for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

(3.4)

E[|X^{n}_{t}-X^{n}_{s}|^{p}]\leq C_{p}|t-s|^{p(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}.

Again, these estimates can be obtained as in the proof of Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 of
[
9
]
, when
\alpha<0
, the case
\alpha\geq 0
being simpler.

The following result provides the rate of convergence in
L^{p}
, for any
p\geq 2
, for the Euler approximation scheme. The case
\alpha<0
and
t=\eta_{n}(t)
was established in Theorem 2.3 of
[
9
]
. For the sake of completeness we include the main ideas of the proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y^{n}_{t}
be the normalized error defined in (
1.3
). Then, there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
, and for any
p\geq 2
, we have

(3.5)

E[|Y^{n}_{t}|^{p}]\leq C_{p}.

Proof.

We can write, using Burkholder and Minkowski’s inequalities, the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
and the estimate (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\|Y^{n}_{t}\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t}\|(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{s})-(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\|_{p}^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\|Y^{n}_{s}\|_{p}^{2}ds+C_{p}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t}[(t-s)^{\alpha}-(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}]^{2}ds.

Lemma
6.3
allows us to write

\|Y^{n}_{t}\|_{p}^{2}\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\|Y^{n}_{s}\|_{p}^{2}ds+C,

which implies the desired estimate by an application of an extended version of Gronwall’s inequality (see, for instance,
[
3
,
15
]
).

\square

As a consequence of (
3.2
) and (
3.5
), we obtain, for any
s,t\in[0,T]
,

(3.6)

E[|X^{n}_{t}-X_{s}|^{p}]\leq C_{p}\left(|t-s|^{p(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}+n^{-p(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\right).

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Recall that
X=\{X_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is the solution to the stochastic Volterra equation (
1.1
) and the normalized
Euler scheme approximation for
X
is defined in (
1.3
).

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
we make the following decomposition of the normalized error

Y^{n}_{t}=A^{n}_{t}+Y^{n,1}_{t}=:A^{n}_{t}+C^{n}_{t}+Z^{n}_{t},

where

(4.1)

A^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}]\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,dW_{s},

(4.2)

C^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}[\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})-\sigma(X^{n}_{s})]\,dW_{s}

and

(4.3)

Z^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}[\sigma(X^{n}_{s})-\sigma(X_{s})]\,dW_{s}.

Then, the proof of Theorem
2.1
will be done in several steps included in the next subsections.

4.1.
Modified error

The next proposition will allow us to replace the three terms
A^{n}_{t}
,
C^{n}_{t}
and
Z^{n}_{t}

defined in (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively, by simpler terms that have the same asymptotic behavior.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A^{n}_{t}
,
C^{n}_{t}
and
Z^{n}_{t}
be defined in (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Then we claim that the following convergences are true

(4.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|A^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}|^{2}]=0,

(4.5)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|C^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}|^{2}]=0

and

(4.6)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|Z^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{t}|^{2}]=0,

where

(4.7)

\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sigma(X_{t})\int^{t}_{0}[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}]dW_{s},

(4.8)

\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\left(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}\right)\,dW_{s}

and

(4.9)

\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\left(X^{n}_{s}-X_{s}\right)\,dW_{s}.

Proof.

The proof will be done in three steps.

Proof of (
4.4
):

Fix
\delta>0
. With the notation

(4.10)

\psi_{n,1}(s,t):=(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha},\quad 0\leq s<t\leq T,

we can write

\displaystyle A^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,dW_{s}-\sigma(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})dW_{s}-\sigma(X_{t})\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{(t-\delta)_{+}}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)\left[\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-\sigma(X_{(t-\delta)_{+}})\right]dW_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left[\sigma(X_{(t-\delta)_{+}})-\sigma(X_{t})\right]\int_{(t-\delta)_{+}}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}\Bigg).

Now, using triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle E[|A^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(s,t)E(\sigma^{2}(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}))ds

\displaystyle\qquad+Cn^{2\alpha+1}\left[E\left(\sigma^{4}(X_{t})\right)E\left[\left(\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}\right)^{4}\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{(t-\delta)_{+}}^{t}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(s)E\left[\left|\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})-\sigma(X_{(t-\delta)_{+}})\right|^{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\qquad+Cn^{2\alpha+1}\left[E\left(\left|\sigma(X_{(t-\delta)_{+}})-\sigma(X_{t})\right|^{4}\right)E\left(\left|\int_{(t-\delta)_{+}}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}\right|^{4}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=:Cn^{2\alpha+1}(A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}).

By the estimate (
3.3
), taking into account that
\sigma
is Lipschitz, we have

L_{1}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}E(\sigma^{2}(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}))<\infty.

Therefore, applying the estimate (
6.4
), we can write

(4.11)

A_{1}\leq L_{1}\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,t)ds\leq L_{1}C_{2}n^{-2}\delta^{2\alpha-1}.

For the term
A_{2}
, using that the random variable
\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}
is Gaussian and applying again the estimate (
6.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{3}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[E\left(\sigma^{4}(X_{t})\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{(t-\delta)_{+}}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,t)ds

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{3}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[E\left(\sigma^{4}(X_{t})\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}C_{2}n^{-2}\delta^{2\alpha-1}.

For the term
A_{3}
, using the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
and inequality (
3.6
), we obtain, assuming
\delta>\frac{1}{n}
,

\displaystyle E\left[\left|\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})-\sigma(X_{(t-\delta)_{+}})\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\sup_{s\in[(t-\delta)_{+},t]}|\eta_{n}(s)-(t-\delta)_{+}|\right]^{2\alpha+1}+\frac{C}{n^{2\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}+\frac{C}{n^{2\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}.

Therefore, using (
6.3
),

(4.13)

A_{3}\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,t)ds\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}n^{-2\alpha-1}.

Finally, for the term
A_{4}
, using the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
and the estimates (
3.1
) and (
6.3
), yields

(4.14)

A_{4}\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,t)ds\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1}n^{-2\alpha-1}.

The estimates (
4.11
), (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) allow us to write

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E\left[|A^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\delta^{2\alpha+1},

where the constant
C
does not depend on
\delta
. Taking into account that
\delta>0
is arbitrary, we conclude the proof of (
4.4
).

Proof of (
4.5
):

We can write

C^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\Psi^{n}_{s}\,\left(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}\right)\,dW_{s},

where
\Psi^{n}_{s}=\int^{1}_{0}\sigma^{\prime}(\theta X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}+(1-\theta)X^{n}_{s})\,d\theta
.
Since
\sigma^{\prime}
is
\beta
-Hölder continuous, using the definition of
\Psi^{n}_{s}
we have

(4.15)

|\Psi^{n}_{s}-\sigma^{\prime}(X^{n}_{s})|\leq C|X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}|^{\beta}.

Now using (
3.4
) and the fact that
|\eta_{n}(s)-s|\leq\frac{1}{n}
we have

(4.16)

E[|\Psi^{n}_{s}-\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})|^{p}]\leq Cn^{-p\beta(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}.

Next coming back to proof of (
4.5
), we can write, using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle E\left[\left|C^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}E\left[\left|\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\Psi^{n}_{s}\right)\left(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}\right)\,dW_{s}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E\left[\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\Psi^{n}_{s}\right)^{2}\left(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}\right)^{2}\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{2\alpha}\left(E[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\Psi^{n}_{s})^{4}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E[(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s})^{4}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Finally, from the estimates (
3.4
) and (
4.16
), we obtain

E\left[\left|C^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}\right|^{2}\right]\leq Cn^{-\beta(2\alpha+1)},

which completes the proof of (
4.5
).

Proof of (
4.6
):

Rewrite
Z^{n}_{t}
as
Z^{n}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\Psi^{n}_{s}(X^{n}_{s}-X_{s})\,dW_{s}
. Then, we can write, using the estimates (
3.5
) and (
4.16
),

\displaystyle E\left[Z^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}E\left[\left|\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\Psi^{n}_{s}\right)\left(X^{n}_{s}-X_{s}\right)\,dW_{s}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(E[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\Psi^{n}_{s})^{4}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(E[(X^{n}_{s}-X_{s})^{4}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-\beta(2\alpha+1)}.

This completes the proof of (
4.6
).

\square

Let us denote by
\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}
the solution to the following stochastic integral equation

(4.17)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}=\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{s}\,dW_{s}.

The next proposition tell us that the limit behavior of the normalized error
Y^{n}_{t}
is analogous to the limit of

\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\widetilde{Y}^{n}
be defined as in (
4.17
). Then, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}]=0.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, we can write

n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}Y^{n}_{t}=R^{n}_{t}+\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\widetilde{Z}^{n}_{t},

where
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|R^{n}_{t}|^{2}]=0
. As a consequence,

\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}-Y^{n}_{t}=R^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})(\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{s}-Y^{n}_{s})\,dW_{s},

which allow us to conclude easily the proof of the proposition.

\square

4.2.
Asymptotic behavior of
\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}

The term
\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}
is singular in the sense that for any fixed
t\geq 0
we cannot guarantee that
\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}
converges in
L^{2}
. However,

\widetilde{A}^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)}
does converges in
L^{2}
, as it is explained in the remark after the proof of the next proposition.
The main result of this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

The finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{\widetilde{A}^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
converge in distribution, as
n\to\infty
, to those of

\{\kappa_{1}Z_{t}\sigma(X_{t}),t\in[0,T]\}
, where
\kappa_{1}
has been defined in (
2.1
)
and
\{Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a zero mean Gaussian process independent of
W
with covariance given by (
1.5
).

Proof.

Let
\psi_{n,1}(s,t)
be defined as in (
4.10
) and set

(4.18)

N_{t}^{n,1}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n,1}(s,t)dW_{s}.

It suffices to show that the finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
converge in distribution to those of

\{Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
.
Notice that
\{N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
is a centered Gaussian process. So, we just need to show that the covariance of
\{N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
converges to the covariance of
\{Z_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
and also that the processes
\{N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)},t\in[0,T]\}
and
W
are not correlated.

Let us first compute the variance of
N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)}
. We have, with the change of variables
ns=x
and
x-j=y
,

(4.19)

\displaystyle E[(N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)})^{2}]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,\eta_{n}(t))ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int^{\frac{j+1}{n}}_{\frac{j}{n}}\left[\left(\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n}-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n}-s\right)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int^{j+1}_{j}\left[(\lfloor nt\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt\rfloor-x)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int^{1}_{0}\left[(\lfloor nt\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt\rfloor-j-y)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}_{k=1}\int^{1}_{0}(k^{\alpha}-(k-y)^{\alpha})^{2}\,dy,

which converges to
\kappa_{1}^{2}
as
n\to\infty
, where
\kappa_{1}
is defined in (
2.1
).
On the other hand, for any interval
[a,b]
, with
0\leq a<b\leq T
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E[N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)}(W_{b}-W_{a})]=0.

Indeed, with the same computations as above, we can write

\displaystyle|E[N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t)}(W_{b}-W_{a})]|

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\left|\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int_{[\frac{j}{n},\frac{j+1}{n}]\cap[a,b]}\left[\left(\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n}-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{\alpha}-\left(\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor}{n}-s\right)^{\alpha}\right]\,ds\right|

\displaystyle=n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}_{k=1}\int^{1}_{0}|k^{\alpha}-(k-y)^{\alpha}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{\max\{\alpha,0\}-\frac{1}{2}},

which converges to zero as
n\to\infty
. Finally, for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
we have

\displaystyle|E[N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t_{2})}N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t_{1})}]|

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\left|\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(t_{1})}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t_{1}))\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t_{2}))ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq n\sum^{\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}}|(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-ns)^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\qquad\times|(\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-ns)^{\alpha}|ds.

The change of variables
ns=x
and
x-j=y
allow us to write

\displaystyle|E[N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t_{2})}N^{n}_{\eta_{n}(t_{1})}]|

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-1}_{j=0}\int_{0}^{1}|(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-j-y)^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\qquad\times|(\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-j)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor-j-y)^{\alpha}|dy

\displaystyle=\sum^{\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor}_{k=1}\int_{0}^{1}|(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor+k)^{\alpha}-(\lfloor nt_{2}\rfloor-\lfloor nt_{1}\rfloor+k-y)^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\qquad\times|k^{\alpha}-(k-y)^{\alpha}|dy,

which converges to zero as
n\to\infty
. This completes the proof of the proposition.

\square

Remark
.
Notice that if we replace
\eta_{n}(t)
by
t
in the proof of Proposition
4.3
, then instead of (
4.19
), we obtain

E[(N^{n,1}_{t})^{2}]=\sum^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}_{k=0}\int^{1\wedge(nt-k)}_{0}\left[(nt-k)^{\alpha}-(nt-k-y)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,dy,

and this term does not seem to converge as
n
tends to infinity due to the fluctuation of
nt-\lfloor nt\rfloor
between
0
and
1
as
n
tends to infinity.

4.3.
Decomposition of the modified normalized error
\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}

Because of the particular behavior of
\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}
, we will consider the decomposition

\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}=\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}+\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}.

From Proposition
4.2
we deduce that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}-Y^{n,1}_{t}|^{2}]=0,

where
Y^{n,1}_{t}
has been introduced in (
1.4
).

The process

\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}=\widetilde{Y}^{n}_{t}-\widetilde{A}^{n}_{t}
satisfies the equation

(4.20)

\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}=\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})(\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}+\widetilde{A}^{n}_{s})\,dW_{s}.

With the notation

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{A}^{n}_{s}\,dW_{s}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\sigma(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)dW_{s},

we can write equation (
4.20
) as

(4.22)

\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}=\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}\,dW_{s}.

4.4.
Asymptotic behavior of
\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}

In this section we will study the limit as
n
tends to infinity of the random process
\{\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
where
\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}

has been introduced in (
4.21
) and
\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}
is defined in (
4.8
).
The main result is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

The finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
converge in law to those of

\left\{\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})dB_{s},t\in[0,T]\right\},

where
B=\{B_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
W
and
\kappa_{2}
is the constant defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

Fix points
t_{1},\dots,t_{Q}\in[0,T]
and real numbers
\rho_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq Q
. Define the martingale

\displaystyle M^{(n)}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{i}}(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{A}^{n}_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{i}}(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s})dW_{s}

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{i}}(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\Theta^{n}_{s}dW_{s},

where

(4.23)

\Theta^{n}_{s}:=\sigma(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)+X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}.

Then, in order to prove the proposition, it suffices to prove that, as
n
tends to infinity,
M^{(n)}_{\tau}
converges in distribution to

\kappa_{2}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\int^{\tau\wedge t_{i}}_{0}(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})dB_{s},

where
B=\{B_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
W
.
By the asymptotic version of Knight’s theorem
(as presented in
[
12
, Chapter XIII, Theorem 2.3]
), it suffices to show that the following two conditions hold:

(C1)
As
n
tends to infinity, the quadratic variation
\langle M^{(n)}\rangle_{\tau}

given by

(4.24)

\langle M^{(n)}\rangle_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(\tau)(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\Theta^{n}_{s}\right|^{2}ds,

converges in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
, to

\kappa_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(\tau)(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})\right|^{2}ds.

(C2)
As
n
tends to infinity, the joint variation

\langle M^{(n)},W\rangle_{\tau}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\rho_{i}\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(\tau)(t_{i}-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\Theta^{n}_{s}ds

converges to zero in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
.

By linearity, to show properties
(C1)
and
(C2)
, it suffices to prove that the following two properties hold true:

(C3)

For any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\Theta^{n}_{s})^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad=\kappa_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

where the convergence holds in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
.

(C4)

For any
t\in[0,T]

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\Theta^{n}_{s}ds=0,

where the convergence holds in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
.

Proof of
(C3)
: We will make use of the notation

(4.25)

\psi_{n,2}(u,s)=(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{n}(s)-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}.

Recall that
\Theta^{n}_{s}
has been defined in (
4.23
).
Then, we can write

\displaystyle\Theta^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}+X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}+\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\,\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}.

The first step in the proof of
(C3)
consists on replacing
\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(u)})
by

\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})
in the expression of
\Theta^{n}_{s}
. That is, if we define

\displaystyle\widehat{\Theta}^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}+X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)}-X^{n}_{s}

\displaystyle=\sigma(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}+\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\,\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u},

then

(4.27)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}n^{2\alpha+1}E[|\widehat{\Theta}^{n}_{s}-\Theta^{n}_{s}|^{2}]=0.

It is easy to check that (
4.27
) will guarantee that if
(C3)
holds for
\widehat{\Theta}^{n}_{s}
, it also holds for
\Theta^{n}_{s}
.
The convergence (
4.27
) is a consequence of the limits

(4.28)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{\eta_{n}(s)}^{s}(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{2\alpha}E[|\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(u)})-\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})|^{2}]du=0

and

(4.29)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)E[|\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(u)})-\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})|^{2}]du=0.

The convergences (
4.28
) and (
4.29
) can be proved using the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
, the estimate (
3.5
) and Lemma
6.3
. Next, we make the following decomposition of
(\widehat{\Theta}^{n}_{s})^{2}
:

(\widehat{\Theta}^{n}_{s})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{6}\Psi^{n,j}_{s},

where

\Psi^{n,1}_{s}=\sigma^{2}(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2},

\Psi^{n,2}_{s}=\left(\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)^{2},

\Psi^{n,3}_{s}=\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\,\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)^{2},

\Psi^{n,4}_{s}=2\sigma(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)\left(\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right),

\Psi^{n,5}_{s}=2\sigma(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\,\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)

and

\Psi^{n,6}_{s}=2\left(\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\,\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right).

Then, condition
(C3)
will be a consequence of the following limits in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,1}_{s}ds

(4.30)

\displaystyle\qquad=\kappa_{3}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,2}_{s}ds

(4.31)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2\alpha+2}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,3}_{s}ds

(4.32)

\displaystyle\qquad=\kappa_{4}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,4}_{s}ds

(4.33)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha+1)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,5}_{s}ds

(4.34)

\displaystyle\qquad=\kappa_{5}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

and

(4.35)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,6}_{s}ds=0,

where
\kappa_{3}
,
\kappa_{4}
and
\kappa_{5}
have been introduced in (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively.

Proof of (
4.30
)
: To simplify the presentation we write

(4.36)

\gamma_{s}:=\mathbf{1}_{[0,t_{1}\wedge t_{2}]}(s)(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}.

Then,

\displaystyle R^{n,1}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,1}_{s}ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=:R^{n,2}_{\tau}+R^{n,3}_{\tau},

where

R^{n,2}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)du\right]ds,

and

R^{n,3}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)duds.

We claim that

(4.37)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E[|R^{n,2}_{\tau}|^{2}]=0.

Proof of (
4.37
)
:
The proof of (
4.37
) is rather involved and will be done in two steps. First we will show that we have convergence to zero in
L^{2}
if we remove the random factor

(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})
and we integrate on any fixed interval
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]\subset[0,\tau]
. Secondly, we will use the small blocks / big blocks argument (see, for instance,
[
5
]
) to show that we have convergence to zero when we include the random weight
(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})
.

Step (i)
: We claim that for any interval
[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]\subset[0,\tau]

(4.38)

R^{n,4}:=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\gamma_{s}\left[\left(\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(u,s)du\,\right]ds\rightarrow 0,

where the convergence holds in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and
\tau
. Notice that

\left(\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(u,s)du=I_{2}\left(\psi_{n,1}^{\otimes 2}(\cdot,s)\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]^{2}}\right),

where
I_{2}
denotes the double stochastic integral with respect to the Brownian motion
W
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle E[|R^{n,4}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=2n^{4\alpha+2}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\langle\psi^{\otimes 2}_{n,1}(\cdot,s_{1})\mathbf{1}^{\otimes 2}_{[0,s_{1}]},\psi^{\otimes 2}_{n,1}(\cdot,s_{2})\mathbf{1}^{\otimes 2}_{[0,s_{2}]}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=4n^{4\alpha+2}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{s_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\psi_{n,1}(u,s_{1})\psi_{n,1}(u,s_{2})du\right)^{2}ds_{1}ds_{2}.

Fix
\delta>0
such that
\delta>\frac{1}{n}
. On the set
\{(s_{1},s_{2}):s_{2}-s_{1}>\delta\}
we have, by the Mean Value Theorem,

|\psi_{n,1}(u,s_{2})|=|(s_{2}-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(s_{2}-u)^{\alpha}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{n}\delta^{\alpha-1}.

As a consequence, using the inequality (
6.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle E[|R^{n,4}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha-1}\delta^{2\alpha-2}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{s_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{s_{2}-s_{1}>\delta\}}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{s_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{s_{2}-s_{1}\leq\delta\}}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha-1}\delta^{2\alpha-2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\gamma_{s}ds\right)^{2}+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{s_{2}-s_{1}\leq\delta\}}ds_{1}ds_{2}.

This implies

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leq\tau\in[0,T]}E[|R^{n,4}|^{2}]\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{s_{2}}\gamma_{s_{1}}\gamma_{s_{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{s_{2}-s_{1}\leq\delta\}}ds_{1}ds_{2}.

Taking into account that
\delta>0
is arbitrary, this completes the proof of (
4.38
).

Step (ii)
: Fix an integer
M\geq 1
and consider a partition
0=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\cdots<\tau_{M}=\tau
, where

\tau_{i}=\frac{i}{M}\tau
for
i=0,1,\dots,M
. With the notation

\Xi^{n,1}_{s}=\left[\left(\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\int^{s}_{0}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(u,s)du\,\right],

we can write

\displaystyle R^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\Xi^{n,1}_{s}ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\Xi^{n,1}_{s}ds

\displaystyle=:R^{n,M,1}_{\tau}+R^{n,M,2}_{\tau},

where

R^{n,M,1}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\tau_{i}})\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\gamma_{s}\Xi^{n,1}_{s}ds

and

R^{n,M,2}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\gamma_{s}\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})-(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\tau_{i}})\right]\Xi^{n,1}_{s}ds

From Step 1, we know that for any fixed
M

(4.39)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E[|R^{n,M,1}_{\tau}|^{2}]=0.

Indeed, for each
0\leq i\leq M-1
, the term
n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{\tau_{i}}^{\tau_{i+1}}\gamma_{s}\Xi^{n,1}_{s}ds
converges to zero in
L^{2}
as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
i=0,\dots,M-1
and
\tau\in[0,T]
, and this convergence also holds in
L^{p}
for any
p\geq 2
, because
\Xi^{n,1}_{s}
belongs to the second Wiener chaos.

Assuming that
n\geq M
, the term
R^{n,M,2}_{\tau}
can be estimated as follows

\|R^{n,M,2}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq C\sup_{u,v\in[0,1],|u-v|\leq\frac{\tau}{M}}\|(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{u})-(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{v})\|_{4}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}\|\Xi^{n,1}_{s}\|_{4}ds.

From Lemma
6.4
we get

\sup_{n\geq 1}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma_{s}\|\Xi^{n,1}_{s}\|_{4}ds<\infty.

Therefore,

(4.40)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq M}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|R^{n,M,2}_{\tau}\|_{2}=0.

In conclusion, (
4.39
) and (
4.40
) imply (
4.37
).

It remains to compute the limit as
n
tends to infinity of the term
R^{n,3}_{\tau}
.
First notice that the term
R^{n,3}_{\tau}
can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle R^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\int_{0}^{s}[(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}]^{2}duds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor ns\rfloor}\int_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}\wedge s}[(s-\frac{i}{n})^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}]^{2}duds.

The change of variables
nu=v
and
ns=x
yields

R^{n,3}_{\tau}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\int_{j}^{(j+1)\wedge n\tau}\gamma_{\frac{x}{n}}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{x}{n}})\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\int_{i}^{(i+1)\wedge x}[(x-i)^{\alpha}-(x-v)^{\alpha}]^{2}dvdx.

With the additional change of variables
x-j=y
and
v-i=z
, we can write

\displaystyle R^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(n\tau-j)}\gamma_{\frac{y+j}{n}}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{y+j}{n}})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor y+j\rfloor}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(y+j-i)}[(y+j-i)^{\alpha}-(y+j-i-z)^{\alpha}]^{2}dzdy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(n\tau-j)}\gamma_{\frac{y+j}{n}}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{y+j}{n}})\sum_{k=j-\lfloor y+j\rfloor}^{j}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(y+k)}[(y+k)^{\alpha}-(y+k-z)^{\alpha}]^{2}dzdy.

Because
y\leq 1
, we have that
\lfloor y+j\rfloor=j
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\gamma_{\frac{j}{n}}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(y+k)}[(y+k)^{\alpha}-(y+k-z)^{\alpha}]^{2}dzdy

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(y+k)}[(y+k)^{\alpha}-(y+k-z)^{\alpha}]^{2}dzdy\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})ds,

where the convergence is in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
.
This completes the proof of (
4.30
).

Proof of (
4.31
)
:
With the notation (
4.36
), we have

\displaystyle Q^{n}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau\wedge t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\Psi^{n,2}_{s}ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}\left(\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}\left(s-\eta_{n}(u)\right)^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)^{2}ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\right)^{2}\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,\left(W_{s}-W_{\eta_{n}(s)}\right)^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\right)^{2}\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,\left[\left(W_{s}-W_{\eta_{n}(s)}\right)^{2}-(s-\eta_{n}(s))\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha+1}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\right)^{2}\,\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})ds

\displaystyle=:Q^{n,1}_{\tau}+Q^{n,2}_{\tau}.

We claim that

(4.41)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E\left[|Q^{n,1}_{\tau}|^{2}\right]=0.

In order to show the convergence (
4.41
), we first replace
\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})
by
\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})
in
Q^{n,1}_{\tau}
and then use
the fact that we are dealing with a discrete martingale sequence.
That is, we make the decomposition

Q^{n,1}_{\tau}=:Q^{n,3}_{\tau}+Q^{n,4}_{\tau},

where

\displaystyle Q^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}\left((\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}-(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)}))^{2}\right)\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\left(W_{s}-W_{\eta_{n}(s)}\right)^{2}-(s-\eta_{n}(s))\right]\,ds

and

Q^{n.4}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\,\left[\left(W_{s}-W_{\eta_{n}(s)}\right)^{2}-(s-\eta_{n}(s))\right]\,ds.

Then, using Minkowski’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|Q^{n,3}_{\tau}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}\|\left((\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}-(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)}))^{2}\right)\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\|_{4}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\|[(W_{s}-W_{\eta_{n}(s)})^{2}-(s-\eta_{n}(s))]\|_{4}ds.

Because
\sigma^{\prime}
is
\beta
-Hölder continuous and
\sigma
has linear growth, we can write using the estimates (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)

\|Q^{n,3}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1-\beta(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha+1}ds\leq Cn^{-\beta(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})},

which shows that

(4.42)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E\left[|Q^{n,3}_{\tau}|^{2}\right]=0.

To handle the term
Q^{n,4}_{\tau}
we write

Q^{n,4}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\xi_{j,n},

where

\xi_{j,n}=\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}(s-\frac{j}{n})^{2\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})\left[(W_{s}-W_{\frac{j}{n}})^{2}-(s-\frac{j}{n})\right]ds.

The random variables
\xi_{j,n}
for

, are
\mathcal{F}_{\frac{j+1}{n}}
-measurable and satisfy

E[\xi_{j,n}\xi_{k,n}]=0
and this implies

(4.43)

E[|Q^{n,4}_{\tau}|^{2}]=n^{4\alpha+2}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}E[\xi_{j,n}^{2}].

By Minkowski’s inequality

(4.44)

\|\xi_{j,n}\|_{2}\leq C\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}(s-\frac{j}{n})^{2\alpha+1}ds\leq Cn^{-2\alpha-1}\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}ds.

Substituting (
4.44

4.43
), we obtain

E[|Q^{n,4}_{\tau}|^{2}]\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\left(\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}ds\right)^{2}\leq C\sup_{0\leq j\leq\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\left(\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}ds\right)\int_{0}^{\tau}\gamma_{s}ds,

which implies

(4.45)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E\left[|Q^{n,4}_{\tau}|^{2}\right]=0.

Therefore, (
4.42
) and (
4.45
) imply (
4.41
).

In order to find the limit of the term
Q^{n,2}_{\tau}
, we first make the following decomposition

Q^{n,2}_{\tau}=Q^{n,5}_{\tau}+Q^{n,6}_{\tau},

where

Q^{n,5}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\gamma_{s}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha+1}[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}-(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)}))^{2}]\,\sigma^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})ds,

and

The
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
\sigma^{\prime}

) and (

(4.46)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}E\left[|Q^{n,5}_{\tau}|^{2}\right]=0.

Then, we make a further decomposition of the term
Q^{6,n}_{\tau}
as follows

Q^{n,6}_{\tau}=Q^{n,7}_{\tau}+Q^{n,8}_{\tau},

where

Q^{n,7}_{\tau}=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{0}[\gamma_{s}-\gamma_{\eta_{n}(s)}](s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha+1}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})ds,

and

We have, using (
6.3
)

\|Q^{n,7}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq C\int^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}_{0}|(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}-(t_{1}-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}(t_{2}-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}|ds,

which implies

(4.47)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|Q^{n,7}_{\tau}\|_{2}=0.

Moreover, the term
Q^{n,8}_{\tau}
can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle Q^{n,8}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\gamma(\frac{j}{n})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})\int_{\frac{j}{n}}^{\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau}(s-\frac{j}{n})^{2\alpha+1}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2\alpha+1}}{2\alpha+2}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}\gamma(\frac{j}{n})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})\left(\frac{j+1}{n}\wedge\tau-\frac{j}{n}\right)^{2\alpha+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\alpha+2)n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor-1}\gamma(\frac{j}{n})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{n^{2\alpha+1}}{2\alpha+2}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}{n}})\gamma(\frac{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}{n})\left(\tau-\frac{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}{n}\right)^{2\alpha+2}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q^{n,8}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha+2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor-1}\gamma(\frac{j}{n})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\frac{j}{n}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha+2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\frac{\lfloor n\tau\rfloor}{n}}\gamma(\eta_{n}(s))(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})ds

where the convergence is in

, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
. The limit in the last equality of the above equation can be proved by arguments similar to those used before based on the estimate (
6.3
) and the Hölder continuity of
\sigma^{\prime}
. From (

4.47

where the convergence is in
L^{2}
, uniformly in
\tau\in[0,T]
. Together with (

6.3
. Finally, applying the Mean Value Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|K^{n,1}_{\tau}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{\alpha-1}n^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}.

Consider now the term

\displaystyle K^{n,2}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}\left(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})\,dW_{u}\right)^{2}\right]\,ds.

We can estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of
K^{n,2}_{\tau}
as follows

\displaystyle\|K^{n,2}_{\tau}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}\left\|\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)[\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})-\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})]\,dW_{u}\right\|_{4}

\displaystyle\times\left\|\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)[\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})+\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})]\,dW_{u}\right\|_{4}ds

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1}\delta^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{T}_{0}\gamma_{s}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)duds\leq C\delta^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}},

where in the first inequality we have used the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
, inequality (
3.2
), together with Burkholder’s inequality for the stochastic integral and in the second inequality we have used Lemma
6.3
.
Define now

\displaystyle K^{n,3}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}))^{2}-(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})]\left(\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}ds

By the same arguments as before, we can write

(4.53)

\|K^{n,3}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq C\left(n^{-\beta(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}+\delta^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

The term

\displaystyle K^{n,4}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=n^{2\alpha+1}\int^{\tau}_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}\gamma_{s}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\left(\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}-\left(\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}\right]ds

\|K^{n,4}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq C\delta^{\alpha-1}n^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, as in the proof of (

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq M}\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\|S^{n,M,2}_{\tau}\|_{2}=0.

In conclusion, (
4.62
) and (
4.63
) imply (
4.59
).

Proof of (
4.60
):

We have

\displaystyle P^{n}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\,\left(\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\Xi^{n,2}_{s}ds,

where
\Xi^{n,2}_{s}=\int^{s}_{\eta_{n}(s)}(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}dW_{u}
. Consider the decomposition

P^{n}_{\tau}=P^{n,1}_{\tau}+P^{n,2}_{\tau},

where

P^{n,1}_{\tau}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\Xi^{n,2}_{s}ds

and

P^{n,2}_{\tau}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})]\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(s)})\Xi^{n,2}_{s}ds.

The
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
\sigma^{\prime}
, Lemma
3.4
and the fact that
\|\Xi^{n,2}_{s}\|_{2}\leq Cn^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
imply that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}E[|P^{n,2}_{\tau}|^{2}]=0.

To handle the term
P^{n,1}_{\tau}
we write

E[|P^{n,1}_{\tau}|^{2}]=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[0,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})})\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{1}}\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{2}}\right]ds_{1}ds_{2}

We claim that the expectation inside the integral vanishes if
|s_{1}-s_{2}|>\frac{1}{n}
. Indeed, if, for instance
s_{1}+\frac{1}{n}<s_{2}
, then
\eta_{n}(s_{1})<\eta_{n}(s_{2})
and

\displaystyle E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})})\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{1}}\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})})\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{1}}E[\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{2}}|\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})}\right]]=0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle E[|P^{n,1}_{\tau}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[0,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})})(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})})\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{1}}\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{2}}\right]ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[0,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\}}\|\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{1}}\|_{4}\|\Xi^{n,2}_{s_{2}}\|_{4}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{[0,t]}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\}}ds_{1}ds_{2},

which tends to zero as
n\to\infty
. This completes the proof of (
4.60
).

Proof of (
4.61
):

Set

F^{n}_{\tau}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\,\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds,

where
\psi_{n,2}(u,s)
is defined in (
4.25
). Fix
\delta>0
such that
\delta<t
and suppose that
n
satisfies
\delta+\frac{1}{n}<t
and
\frac{1}{n}<\delta
. We decompose the term

F^{n}_{\tau}
as follows

\displaystyle F^{n}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\tau}_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}(t-s)^{\alpha}[\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})-\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})]\,\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}^{\tau}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}_{0}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau}^{\tau}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta})\left(\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}\right)ds

\displaystyle=:B^{n,1}_{\tau}+B^{n,2}_{\tau}+B^{n,3}_{\tau}+B^{n,4}_{\tau}.

We have the following estimates. Using Minkowkski’s, Hölder and Burkholder’s inequalities together with Lemma
6.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|B^{n,1}_{\tau}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{\delta+\frac{1}{n}}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)du\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(t^{\alpha+1}-(t-\delta-\frac{1}{n})^{\alpha+1}\right)

(4.64)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\delta+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{(\alpha+1)\wedge 1}.

For the term
B^{n,2}_{\tau}
the
\beta
-Hölder continuity of
\sigma^{\prime}
allows us to write

\displaystyle\|B^{n,2}_{\tau}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{|u-v|\leq\delta+\frac{1}{n}}\|X_{u}-X_{v}\|_{4}^{\beta}n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)}_{0}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)du\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

(4.65)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\delta+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\beta(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}.

For the term
B^{n,3}_{\tau}
, we have

(4.66)

\|B^{n,3}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq Cn^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge t}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\left(\int^{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}_{0}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)du\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}ds.

Applying the Mean Value Theorem and taking into account that
\delta>\frac{1}{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}_{0}\psi_{n,2}^{2}(u,s)du

\displaystyle=\int^{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}_{0}[(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{n}(s)-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}]^{2}du

(4.67)

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-2}\delta^{2\alpha-2}.

Substituting (
4.67
) into (
4.66
), yields

(4.68)

\|B^{n,3}_{\tau}\|_{2}\leq n^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{\alpha-1}.

The estimation of the term
B^{n,4}_{\tau}
is more involved. First, we write

\displaystyle E[|B^{n,4}_{\tau}|^{2}]=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}E\left[\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})-\delta})M_{s_{1}}^{n,3}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})-\delta})M_{s_{2}}^{n,3}\right]ds_{1}ds_{2},

where, for any
s\in[0,\tau]
, we use the notation

M^{n,3}_{s}=\int_{\eta_{n}(s)-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi_{n,2}(u,s)\sigma(X_{\eta_{n}(u)})\,dW_{u}.

We claim that for
|s_{1}-s_{2}|>\delta+\frac{1}{n}
, the expectation

vanishes. Indeed, if, for instance

s_{1}<s_{2}-\delta-\frac{1}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle E\left[\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})-\delta})M_{s_{1}}^{n,3}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})-\delta})M_{s_{2}}^{n,3}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=E\left[\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})-\delta})M_{s_{1}}^{n,3}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})-\delta})E[M_{s_{2}}^{n,3}|\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})-\delta}]\right]=0.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle E[|B^{n,4}_{\tau}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\delta+\frac{1}{n}\}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times E\left[\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{1})-\delta})M_{s_{1}}^{n,3}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{\eta_{n}(s_{2})-\delta})M_{s_{2}}^{n,3}\right]ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{[(\delta+\frac{1}{n})\wedge\tau,\tau]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\delta+\frac{1}{n}\}}\|M_{s_{1}}^{n,3}\|_{4}\|M_{s_{2}}^{n,3}\|_{4}ds_{1}ds_{2}

(4.69)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{[0,t]^{2}}(t-s_{1})^{\alpha}(t-s_{2})^{\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{\{|s_{1}-s_{2}|\leq\delta+\frac{1}{n}\}}ds_{1}ds_{2},

where in the last inequality we used Burkholder’s inequality and Lemma
6.3
.
From the estimates (
4.64
), (
4.65
), (
4.68
) and (
4.69
), letting first
n
tend to infinity and later
\delta
tend to zero, we obtain that
(
4.61
) holds true.

\square

4.5.
Asymptotic behavior of
\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}

We recall that
\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}
satisfies the equation

\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}=\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}\,dW_{s}.

In this section we will prove the following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

The finite-dimensional distributions of the process
\{\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
converges in distribution to those of the process
\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}=\{\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
,
where
\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}
is the solution to the stochastic Volterra equation

\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}_{t}=\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}_{s}\,dW_{s},

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
W
.

Proof.

Fix an integer
m\geq 1
. Set

\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}=\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}\,dW_{s},

where
\beta_{m}(s)=\frac{i}{m}
if
\frac{i}{m}\leq s<\frac{i+1}{m}
. From Section
4.4
we know that, for any fixed
m\geq 1
, the finite-dimensional distributions of the process

\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}
converges in distribution to those of the process
\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,m,1}=\{\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,m,1}_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
that satisfies

\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,m,1}_{t}=\kappa_{2}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha}(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)(X_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s})\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}\,dW_{s},

where
B
is a standard Brownian motion independent of
W
.
Then, the proposition follows immediately form the following convergences:

(4.70)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}|^{2}]=0

and

(4.71)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,1}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{\infty,m,1}_{t}|^{2}]=0.

Let us first show (
4.70
). We have

\displaystyle E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}(X_{s}))^{2}[\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}]^{2}\,\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}|]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{s}|]^{2}\,ds

(4.72)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}|]^{2}\,ds.

Applying Lemma
6.1
and the fact that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{n,m\geq 1}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}|^{2}]<\infty
, we can show that the process
\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}
is Hölder continuous, uniformly in
n
and
m
. Indeed,
for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
,

\displaystyle E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t_{1}}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t_{2}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq CE[|\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{1}}+\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t_{1}}-\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{2}}-\widetilde{C}^{n}_{t_{2}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}-(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}|^{2}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,2}_{\beta_{m}(s)}|^{2}]ds

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(t_{2}-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{\beta_{m}(s)}|^{2}]ds

(4.73)

\displaystyle\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2\alpha+1}.

Substituting (
4.73
) into (
4.72
), we obtain

E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{t}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{t}|^{2}]\leq 2\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\widetilde{Y}^{n,1}_{s}-\widetilde{Y}^{n,m,1}_{s}|]^{2}\,ds+Cm^{-2\alpha-1}.

which implies (
4.70
). The proof of the convergence (
4.71
) is analogous.

\square

Theorem

follows from the next proposition which ensures the asymptotic independence of the random variables

and

.

Proposition 5.1
.

For any
t\in(0,T]
and real numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the following convergence holds true

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\left(\exp\left(i\lambda N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)}+i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}\right)\right)=E\left(\exp\left(i\lambda Z_{t}\right)\right)E\left(\exp\left(i\mu Y^{\infty,1}_{t}\right)\right),

where
N^{n,1}_{t}
has been defined in (
4.18
) and
Y^{n,1}_{t}
is defined in (
1.4
).

Proof.

Set

\Lambda_{n}:=\exp\left(i\lambda N^{n,1}_{\eta_{n}(t)}+i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}\right).

Recall that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

N^{n,1}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}

and

Y^{n,1}_{t}=n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s},

where
\xi^{n}_{s}=\sigma(X^{n}_{\eta_{n}(s)})-\sigma(X_{s})
.
Let
\delta>0
be such that
\delta<t
. Assume that
\frac{1}{n}<\frac{\delta}{2}
. We can decompose the random variable
\Lambda_{n}
as follows

\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda^{(1)}_{n,\delta}+\Lambda^{(2)}_{n,\delta},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(1)}_{n,\delta}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}+i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\exp\left(i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int^{t-\delta}_{0}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right)-1\right]

and

\Lambda^{(2)}_{n,\delta}=\exp\left(i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}+i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right).

We claim that for any fixed
\delta>0
,

(5.1)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E[\Lambda^{(1)}_{n,\delta}]=0.

Indeed, we can write

\displaystyle|E[\Lambda^{(1)}_{n,\delta}]|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}E\left[\left|\int^{t-\delta}_{0}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{t-\delta}_{0}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Mean Value Theorem, taking into account that
s\leq t-\delta
and
\frac{1}{n}<\frac{\delta}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi^{2}_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))

\displaystyle=[(\eta_{n}(t)-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{n}(t)-s)^{\alpha}]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\eta_{n}(t)-s)^{2\alpha-2}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2}\leq(\delta/2)^{2\alpha-2}n^{-2},

and the convergence (
5.1
) follows easily. We now make a further decomposition

\Lambda^{(2)}_{n}=\Lambda^{(3)}_{n,\delta}+\Lambda^{(4)}_{n,\delta},

where

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(3)}_{n,\delta}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}+i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\right)-1\right]

and

\Lambda^{(4)}_{n,\delta}=\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}+i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right).

We claim that

(5.2)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}E[\Lambda^{(3)}_{n,\delta}]=0.

Indeed, we can write

\displaystyle|E[\Lambda^{(3)}_{n,\delta}]|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu|n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}E\left[\left|\int_{t-\delta}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq|\mu|n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{t-\delta}^{t}(t-s)^{2\alpha}E[|\xi^{n}_{s}|^{2}]ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Taking into account that
E[|\xi^{n}_{s}|^{2}]ds\leq Cn^{-2\alpha-1}
, the limit (
5.2
) follows immediately.
Finally, we can write

\displaystyle E[\Lambda^{(4)}_{n,\delta}]=E[E[\Lambda^{(4)}_{n,\delta}|\mathcal{F}_{t-\delta}]]

\displaystyle=E\left[\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\right)E\left[\exp\left(i\lambda n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}(s,\eta_{n}(t))dW_{s}\right)|\mathcal{F}_{t-\delta}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=E\left[\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,\eta_{n}(t))ds\right).

We claim that

(5.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{t-\delta}^{\eta_{n}(t)}\psi_{n,1}^{2}(s,\eta_{n}(t))ds\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\kappa_{1}^{2}\right)

and

(5.4)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|E\left[\exp\left(i\mu n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{\alpha}\xi^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\right)\right]-E\left[\exp\left(i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}\right)\right]\right|=0.

The convergence (
5.3
) follows from Proposition
4.3
and the previous computations. The proof of (
5.4
) is analogous to that of (
4.38
). In conclusion, from (
5.1
), (
4.38
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we can write

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E[\Lambda_{n}]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\kappa_{1}\right)\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\left[\exp\left(i\mu Y^{n,1}_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=E\left[\exp\left(i\lambda Z_{t}\right)\right]E\left[\exp\left(i\mu Y^{\infty,1}_{t}\right)\right],

which completes the proof of the proposition.

\square

we have

\displaystyle E[|\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{1}}-\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{2}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle=n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}-(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}|^{2}E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\qquad+n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(t_{2}-s)^{2\alpha}E\left[(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma)^{2}(X_{s})\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi_{n,1}(u,s)dW_{u}\right)^{2}\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}-(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}|^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)duds

\displaystyle\qquad+Cn^{2\alpha+1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(t_{2}-s)^{2\alpha}\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)duds.

Applying Lemma
6.4
, yields,

\displaystyle E[|\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{1}}-\widetilde{A}^{n,1}_{t_{2}}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|(t_{2}-s)^{\alpha}-(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha}|^{2}ds+C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(t_{2}-s)^{2\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{2\alpha+1}.

The second inequality in the previous display can be deduced from the
L^{2}
norm of the increment on the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
of a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H=\alpha+\frac{1}{2}
. The proof of the inequality (
6.2
) is analogous, but instead of Lemma
6.4
we make use of inequality (
3.2
).
\square

The next results are technical lemmas that have been used several time in the proofs.

Lemma 6.2
.

For any
\alpha\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
and for any
\delta>0
,there are positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
, such that
for all
t\in[0,1]
,

(6.3)

\int^{t}_{0}\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,ds\leq C_{1}\,n^{-2\alpha-1}

and

(6.4)

\int^{(t-\delta)_{+}}_{0}\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,ds\leq C_{2}n^{-2}\delta^{2\alpha-1}.

Proof.

By the Mean Value Theorem, for any
0\leq s<t\leq 1
, we have

(6.5)

\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\leq(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2}.

Let us first show the inequality (
6.3
). We can assume that
t>\frac{1}{n}
, because in the case
t\leq\frac{1}{n}
, the inequality can be easily proved. We decompose the interval
[0,t]
into the union of the intervals
\left[0,\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}{n}\right]
and
\left[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}{n},t\right]
. For the first interval we can write

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}{n}}_{0}\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-2}\int_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-2}\int_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}(t-\frac{i+1}{n})^{2\alpha-2}n^{-2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-2}\frac{(nt-i-1)^{2\alpha-2}}{n^{2\alpha-2}}n^{-3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor-2}(nt-i-1)^{2\alpha-2}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2\alpha+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2\alpha-2}.

Next for any
s\in\left[\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}{n},t\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{2\alpha}+(t-s)^{2\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}Cn^{-2\alpha}&\alpha\geq 0\\
C(t-s)^{2\alpha}&\alpha<0\end{cases}.

Therefore,

(6.7)

\int_{\frac{\lfloor nt\rfloor-1}{n}}^{t}\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}ds\leq Cn^{-2\alpha-1}.

Thus combining (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) we obtain (
6.3
).

The inequality (
6.4
) follows easily from (
6.5
). Indeed, we can assume that
t>\delta
, and in this case

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\delta}\left[(t-\eta_{n}(s))^{\alpha}-(t-s)^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\delta}(t-s)^{2\alpha-2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{\delta}^{t}s^{2\alpha-2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\delta^{2\alpha-1}}{n^{2}(1-2\alpha)}=C_{2}{n^{-2}}\delta^{2\alpha-1}.

\square

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\psi_{n,2}(u,s)
defined as in (
4.25
). There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
s\geq 0
, we have

n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi^{2}_{n,2}(u,s)du\leq C.

Proof.

We have, using the Mean Value Theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}\psi^{2}_{n,2}(u,s)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}[(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(\eta_{n}(s)-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}]^{2}du

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2}(s-\eta_{n}(s))^{2}\int_{0}^{\eta_{n}(s)}(\eta_{n}(s)-\eta_{n}(u))^{2\alpha-2}du

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-3}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor ns\rfloor-1}\left(\eta_{n}(s)-\frac{i}{n}\right)^{2\alpha-2}

\displaystyle=Cn^{-2\alpha-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor ns\rfloor-1}(\eta_{n}-i)^{2\alpha-2}.

Since
2\alpha-2<-1
the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2\alpha-2}
is convergent, and this completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\psi_{n,1}(u,s)
defined as in (
4.10
). There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
s\geq 0
, we have

n^{2\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)du\leq C.

Proof.

We have, using the Mean Value Theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\psi^{2}_{n,1}(u,s)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}[(s-\eta_{n}(u))^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}]^{2}du

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor ns\rfloor}\int_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}\wedge s}[(s-\frac{i}{n})^{\alpha}-(s-u)^{\alpha}]^{2}du

\displaystyle=n^{-2\alpha-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor ns\rfloor}\int_{i}^{(i+1)\wedge ns}[(ns-i)^{\alpha}-(ns-x)^{\alpha}]^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq n^{-2\alpha-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1\wedge(ns-\lfloor ns\rfloor+k)}[(ns-\lfloor ns\rfloor+k)^{\alpha}-(ns-\lfloor ns\rfloor+k-x)^{\alpha}]^{2}dx,

which is uniformly bounded by a constant
C
that does not depend on
n
and
s
.

\square

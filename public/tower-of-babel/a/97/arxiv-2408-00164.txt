Zassenhaus decomposition of half-sided translations and generalizations in 2d conformal field theory
Manish Ramchander

Abstract
We study the half-sided translations associated to Rindler wedge algebras for conformal field theories in 1+1 Minkowski spacetime, generated by an unbounded operator 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , in terms of bilinear forms G , G ′ G,G^{\prime} made from entanglement Hamiltonians of the underlying algebras such that 𝒢 = G + G ′ \mathcal{G}=G+G^{\prime} . We show that despite entanglement Hamiltonians being ill-defined operators on Hilbert space, G , G ′ G,G^{\prime} can be regularized using smooth bump functions to operators G ^ , G ^ ′ \hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime} with well-defined commutators, and use them to do a centered Zassenhaus expansion of exp ⁡ ( i ​ 𝒢 ​ s ) \exp(i\mathcal{G}s) in terms of G ^ \hat{G} and G ^ ′ \hat{G}^{\prime} which is tractable and respects causality. We show that in fact half-sided translations is a special case in a large class of operators 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for which a similar decomposition can be done by defining 𝒪 = O L + O R \mathcal{O}=O_{L}+O_{R} with O L , O R O_{L},O_{R} chosen approriately.

2.1

Quantum fields are postulated to be operator valued distributions, which are symmetric bilinear forms over a dense domain

[
16
]

is divergent as

[
34
]
. Consequently, composite fields can be defined to have finite matrix elements. However, bilinear forms are generally not operators.

Producing operators from fields requires smearing against smooth functions
[

26
]

(

\displaystyle K_{\mathcal{M}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\,T_{00}(x)\,dx

is a well-defined operator even though the smearing function

is unbounded at infinity. As we have been discussing,
this property is not enjoyed by the entanglement Hamiltonians

3.1
Entanglement Hamiltonians as operators?

Let us show that
H_{\mathcal{M}}
and
H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
are not operators by a contradiction proof. We will use the fact that
K
is a well-defined operator with the vacuum
|0\rangle
in its domain, and the OPE of stress tensors to arrive at our result.

Let us start by assuming that
H_{\mathcal{M}}
and
H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
are densely defined operators on domains
D_{\mathcal{M}}
and
D_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
and hence have unique adjoints. As
H_{\mathcal{M}},H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
are integrals of
T_{00}(x)
, they are symmetric operators. Now, note that the domain of
K=H_{\mathcal{M}}-H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
contains
|0\rangle
. In fact
K|0\rangle=0
, as boost is a symmetry. Crucially,
D_{K}\subset D_{\mathcal{M}}\cap D_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
, and therefore,

\displaystyle|0\rangle\in D_{K}\implies|0\rangle\in D_{\mathcal{M}},D_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}.

(30)

In other words,
H_{\mathcal{M}}|0\rangle=H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}|0\rangle
is a state of finite norm. This we will show is false, by computing the norm using the OPE. Because
H_{\mathcal{M}}
is symmetric and
|0\rangle\in D_{\mathcal{M}}
, note that norm squared of
H_{\mathcal{M}}|0\rangle
is
\langle 0|H_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}|0\rangle
which is
4
4

4

As the form is unbounded, it follows that
|\chi\rangle,H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle\in D_{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\langle H_{\mathcal{M}}\chi|H_{\mathcal{M}}\chi\rangle=\langle\chi|H_{M}^{2}|\chi\rangle
. Last step is due to the fact that a symmetric operator
A
coincides with its adjoint
A^{\dagger}
on the domain of
A
.

\displaystyle||H_{\mathcal{M}}|0\rangle||^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\cdot xy\cdot\left\langle 0|T_{00}(x+i\epsilon)T_{00}(y)|0\right\rangle

(31)

where we have put in an
i\epsilon
to appropriately define the product
H_{\mathcal{M}}^{2}
; we will it take to zero at the end of calculation. Writing
T_{00}(x,0)=T(x)+\overline{T}(x)
, and using the OPE, the central charge term gives

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\cdot xy\cdot\frac{1}{(x-y+i\epsilon)^{4}}=\frac{c}{24\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{xdx}{(x+i\epsilon)^{2}}

(32)

which is a divergent integral, both because of

|0\rangle\notin D_{\mathcal{M}}

H_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}

H_{\mathcal{M}},H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
are not operators.
\square

3.1.1
Extension using Reeh-Schlieder theorem

Concerning the norm of states
H_{M}|\chi\rangle
, we can actually derive a stronger result. It was noted in
[
26
]
that norm of
H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle,H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}|\chi\rangle
has a universal ultraviolet divergence. Using the Reeh-Schlieder theorem and the OPE of the stress tensor we can explicitly show that
H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle,H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle
norms are infinite for
\chi
in an arbitrarily large dense set of the vacuum sector of the Hilbert space, with both ultraviolet and infrared divergences.

The key point in the derivation concerns the radius of convergence of the stress tensor OPE, which is determined by the distance to the nearest operator not included in the expansion
[
32
]
. If we identify some states
\chi
such that in computation of
||H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle||^{2}
we can draw a circle around
T(x)
and
T(y)
containing no other insertion as
x,y\to 0
and
x,y\to\infty
, then their norm squares are of the type (
32
).

The Reeh-Schlieder theorem tells us that such states
\chi
can approximate any state in the vacuum sector of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}
be the polynomial algebra associated to an open region
\mathcal{U}
in spacetime, that is, algebra generated using operators of the form
\int_{\mathcal{U}}f(x)\phi(x)dx
. The theorem states that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}|0\rangle
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
for any
\mathcal{U}

[
16
,
26
]
.

Now, for us, provided that
\mathcal{U}
is a bounded region and does not contain
(0,0)
, we can always find an
\alpha_{\mathcal{U}},\beta_{\mathcal{U}}
such that for
0\leq x,y<\alpha_{\mathcal{U}}
and
x,y\geq\beta_{\mathcal{U}}
, the OPE for
T(x)T(y)
converges. For any such
\mathcal{U}
, we can take any
\chi\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{U}}|0\rangle
, and we will find that
||H_{\mathcal{M}}|\chi\rangle||^{2}
diverges because of the
\chi
independent central charge term. For instance, the asymptotic contribution is

\displaystyle\int_{\beta_{\mathcal{U}}}^{\infty}dx\int_{\beta_{\mathcal{U}}}^{\infty}dy\cdot xy\cdot\frac{1}{(x-y+i\epsilon)^{4}}=\int_{\beta_{\mathcal{U}}}^{\infty}dx\;x\frac{3\beta_{\mathcal{U}}+i\epsilon-x}{6(\beta_{\mathcal{U}}+i\epsilon-x)^{3}}\sim\int_{\beta_{\mathcal{U}}}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x}.

(33)

See figure
2
for an illustration. Similarly there is a contribution from near
x=0
.

Let us note two key points in the above discussion which will be important below. The first is that the integrals (
32
), (
33
) are divergent not only because of
x=0
contributions, but also due to infrared contributions coming from the slow decay of

. The second point is the observation that these integrals are marginally divergent. That is, if the integrand had been changed from
x^{-1}\to x^{-1+\kappa}
near the origin, and
x^{-1}\to x^{-1+\kappa}
asymptotically, for any
\kappa>0
, the integrals would have converged. We will next see that
||G|\chi\rangle||^{2}
and
||G^{\prime}|\chi\rangle||^{2}
are not infrared divergent, and their UV divergence can be regularized conveniently because of its marginal nature.

3.2
Forms
G,G^{\prime}
to operators
\hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime}

Let us rewrite
G=H_{\mathcal{M}}-H_{\mathcal{N}},G^{\prime}=H_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}-H_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
obtained from (
20
) using properties of the stress tensor in 2d CFT as follows. From the definition,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\frac{H_{M}-H_{N}}{2\pi}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot T_{00}(x,0)-\int_{a}^{\infty}dx\cdot(x-a)\cdot T_{00}(x,-a)

(34)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot T_{00}(x,0)-\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot T_{00}(x+a,-a).

(35)

Shifting the integration variable, and using the transformation rule
T_{00}(x,t)=T(x-t)+\overline{T}(x+t)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot[T(x)+\overline{T}(x)]-\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot[T(x+2a)+\overline{T}(x)]

(36)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\cdot x\cdot[T(x)-T(x+2a)]

(37)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2a}dx\cdot x\cdot T(x)+2a\int_{2a}^{\infty}dx\cdot T(x).

(38)

Likewise,

\displaystyle G^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{2a}dx\cdot(2a-x)\cdot T(x)+2a\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\cdot T(x)

(39)

These mimic
2aP^{+}
as
x\to\pm\infty
, which is a well-defined operator, and therefore the norm of
G|\chi\rangle,G^{\prime}|\chi\rangle
cannot have a universal infrared divergences. Near
x=0
however, the behaviour is same as
H_{\mathcal{M}}
, and therefore the norms of the states
G|\chi\rangle,G|\chi^{\prime}\rangle
show a universal UV divergence. However, as we saw in the last subsection, this divergence is marginal and can be fixed conveniently.

Let us replace
G,G^{\prime}
by suitable
\hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime}
. It is important to recognize that a change in
G
must be balanced with a change in
G^{\prime}
to maintain
\mathcal{G}=G+G^{\prime}=\hat{G}+\hat{G}^{\prime}
, and because there are two points requiring regulation, i.e.
x=0
and
x=2a
, we will need two functions
\mu
and
\nu
. Let

\displaystyle\hat{G}=2a\int_{2a}^{\infty}dx\cdot T(x)+\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\mu(x)T(x)+\int_{\epsilon}^{2a-\epsilon}dx\cdot x\cdot T(x)+\int_{2a-\epsilon}^{2a}\nu(x)T(x)

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{G}^{\prime}=2a\int^{0}_{-\infty}dx\cdot T(x)+\int_{0}^{\epsilon}[2a-\mu(x)]T(x)+\int_{\epsilon}^{2a-\epsilon}dx\cdot(2a-x)\cdot T(x)+\int_{2a-\epsilon}^{2a}[2a-\nu(x)]T(x)

(41)

for
\epsilon\ll 1
. One possible choice for
\mu(x)
and
\nu(x)
is the minimal regularization

\displaystyle\mu(x)

\displaystyle=x\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right)^{\epsilon},\quad\nu(x)=2a-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\epsilon}}(2a-x)^{1+\epsilon}

(42)

which is sufficient to ensure that norms of
\hat{G}|\chi\rangle,\hat{G}^{\prime}|\chi\rangle
do not display universal UV divergences. This prescription satisfies continuity at
x=\epsilon,2a-\epsilon
, which is an obvious requirement. Note that in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
,
\mu(x)=\nu(x)=x
, which suggests
G,G^{\prime}
by themselves possibly fall just marginally short of being operators. If the divergence was stronger, requiring a different regularization like
\mu(x)\sim x^{2}
, continuity would demand
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
factors in the denominator which would diverge in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.

Let us note two more points in this subsection. First is that the contributions from the additional boundaries introduced in the various integral splits we considered cancel against each other, therefore we don’t need to regularize integrands there. Second point, which we will return to in section
4.4.1
, is that any analytic function used for regularization will have a derivative problem at the points
x=\epsilon,x=2a-\epsilon
. For example,
\mu(x)=x^{1+\epsilon}\epsilon^{-\epsilon}
at
x=\epsilon
is
\epsilon
, but its derivative at
x=\epsilon
is not zero. We can regularize using higher degree polynomials which would have
N
derivatives continuous at
x=\epsilon
, but
N
cannot be infinite. This will be an important point when studying commutators later, which will force us to use non-analytic bump functions for regulation.

What is the nature of the formal operators

We will now compute the commutators for a general class of integrated stress tensors, and later specialize to the half-sided translations. The commutator of stress tensor is obtained by taking products of fields without regularizing them, and therefore

4.1
Commutators of integrals of stress tensor

Let us consider the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\int_{-b_{L}}^{b_{R}}dx\cdot[f(x)T(x)+\overline{f}(x)\overline{T}(x)].

We will split it into two parts,
\mathcal{O}=O_{L}+O_{R}
, where

\displaystyle O_{L}=\int_{-b_{L}}^{-a_{L}}dx\cdot[f(x)T(x)+\overline{f}(x)\overline{T}(x)]+\int_{-a_{L}}^{a_{R}}dx\cdot[L(x)T(x)+\overline{L}(x)\overline{T}(x)]

and

\displaystyle O_{R}=\int^{b_{R}}_{a_{R}}dx\cdot[f(x)T(x)+\overline{f}(x)\overline{T}(x)]+\int_{-a_{L}}^{a_{R}}dx\cdot[R(x)T(x)+\overline{R}(x)\overline{T}(x)].

\displaystyle R(x)+L(x)=f(x),\quad\overline{R}(x)+\overline{L}(x)=\overline{f}(x),\quad x\in(-a_{L},a_{R}).

Further, we would like to require continuity conditions, that

\displaystyle R(-a_{L})=\overline{R}(-a_{L})=L(a_{R})=\overline{L}(a_{R})=0.

(73)

Using causality, these conditions allow us to write
[O_{R},O_{L}]
as integrals over just

\displaystyle[O_{R},O_{L}]=\int_{-a_{L}}^{a_{R}}dx\int_{-a_{L}}^{a_{R}}dy\left(R(x)L(y)[T(x),T(y)]+\overline{R}(x)\overline{L}(y)[\overline{T}(x),\overline{T}(y)]\right).

Below at first we will set

\displaystyle[O_{R},O_{L}]=i\int_{I}dy\int_{I}dx\,L(y)\left(\partial_{x}(R(x)\cdot[T(x)+T(y)])-\frac{c}{24\pi}R^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\right)\delta(x-y)+\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=\int_{I}dy\left(-iL(y)R(x)\cdot[T(x)+T(y)]\delta(x-y)\Big|_{-a_{L}}^{a_{R}}+\frac{icL(y)R(x)}{24\pi}\cdot\delta^{\prime\prime}(x-y)\Big|^{a_{R}}_{-a_{L}}\right).

\displaystyle[O_{R},O_{L}]=i\int_{I}dy\int_{I}dx\cdot L(y)\left(R^{\prime}(x)\cdot[T(x)+T(y)]+R(x)\cdot T^{\prime}(x)-\frac{c}{24\pi}\cdot R^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\right)\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle=i\int_{I}dy\cdot L(y)\left(2R^{\prime}(y)\cdot T(y)+R(y)\cdot T^{\prime}(y)-\frac{c}{24\pi}\cdot R^{\prime\prime\prime}(y)\right).

(80)

\displaystyle i\int_{I}dy\;L(y)\cdot[2R^{\prime}(y)\cdot T(y)+R(y)\cdot T^{\prime}(y)].

5
Zassenhaus expansion for half-sided translations

The Zassenhaus expansion
[
24
]
is ubiquitous in quantum mechanics for writing operators
e^{\lambda(X+Y)}
in terms of exponentials of
X
,
Y
and their commutators in a right sided expansion:

\displaystyle e^{\lambda(X+Y)}=e^{\lambda X}e^{\lambda Y}\cdot\prod_{i=2}^{\infty}e^{\lambda^{n}C_{n}}

(106)

for some
C_{n}
which can be obtained recursively by differentiating the expression repeatedly. In general this is a difficult task, however, there exists an efficient recursive method for their computation due to Casas et al
[
31
]
. Their result is

\displaystyle C_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}f_{1,n-1},\quad\quad\quad n=2,3,4\\
\frac{1}{n}f\left({\left[\frac{n-1}{2}\right],n-1}\right),\quad n\geq 5\end{cases}

(107)

where

\displaystyle f_{1,k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{j!(k-j)!}\text{ad}_{Y}^{k-j}\text{ad}_{X}^{j}Y,

(108)

\displaystyle f_{n,k}=\sum_{j=0}^{[k/n]-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\text{ad}^{j}_{C_{n}}f_{n-1,k-nj},\quad k\geq n.

(109)

where
\text{ad}_{X}Y=[X,Y],\text{ad}_{X}^{k}Y=[X,\text{ad}^{k-1}_{X}Y]
, and
\text{ad}_{X}^{0}Y=Y
.

5.1
Issue with the right sided expansion

We can demand a right sided expansion for half-sided translations, with
X=\hat{G}^{\prime},Y=\hat{G}
and
\lambda=is
,

\displaystyle e^{is\mathcal{G}}=e^{is\hat{G}^{\prime}}e^{is\hat{G}}\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}e^{(is)^{n}C_{n}}

(110)

where
C_{n}
can be obtained using (
127

(0,2a)
that are closely related to (
101
). Can this expression describe half-sided translations? Let us ask this question by considering the conjugation for
\phi(x)
, for
x
spacelike to the interval
(0,2a)
.

\displaystyle A(s,x)=\exp(is\mathcal{G})\phi(x)\exp(-is\mathcal{G})

(111)

Since

is unitary, and
x
is spacelike to
(0,2a)
, by micro causality
\phi(x)
commutes with all
C_{n}
, giving,

\displaystyle A(s,x)=e^{is\hat{G}^{\prime}}e^{is\hat{G}}\cdot\phi(x)\cdot e^{-is\hat{G}}e^{-is\hat{G}^{\prime}}

(112)

but from the discussions of section
3.3

and

can transport
\phi(x)
to regions causally connected with
(0,2a)

,

cannot cross the region 2 of figure

5.2
Derivation of centered Zassenhaus formula

The key to avoid this problem is to transport
\phi(x)
into the overlap region before the commutator terms act on it. This can be done using a centered expansion which will ensure that commutator terms always make their contribution. We therefore should look for the decomposition

\displaystyle e^{\lambda(X+Y)}=e^{\lambda X}\cdot\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}e^{\lambda^{n}D_{n}}\cdot e^{\lambda Y}.

(113)

Unfortunately the centered version of Zassenhaus expansion does not seem to be available in the literature and therefore we will have to derive it. We can take inspiration from
[
31
]
, of proposing an expansion and taking derivatives to get
\{D_{n}\}
. We demand

has an expansion (
113

\displaystyle\quad R_{1}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda X}e^{\lambda(X+Y)}e^{-\lambda Y},\quad F_{1}=\frac{d}{d\lambda}R_{1}\cdot R_{1}^{-1}.

(114)

Note that
R_{1}(\lambda)
is equal to the infinite product
\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}e^{\lambda^{n}D_{n}}
.
Using this, one can define

\displaystyle R_{n}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda^{n}D_{n}}R_{n-1}(\lambda),\quad F_{n}=\frac{d}{d\lambda}R_{n}\cdot R_{n}^{-1}.

(115)

These functions like in
[
31
]
obey the recursive relation

\displaystyle F_{n}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda^{n}\text{ad}_{D_{n}}}(F_{n-1}(\lambda)-nD_{n}\lambda^{n-1})

(116)

which enable us to find
D_{n}
’ s recursively, starting from

. Therefore, let us begin the task.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{1}}{\partial\lambda}=-X\cdot R_{1}-R_{1}\cdot Y+e^{-\lambda X}(X+Y)e^{\lambda(X+Y)}e^{-\lambda Y}

(117)

and now, we use

\displaystyle R_{1}^{\prime}(\lambda)\cdot R_{1}^{-1}=-X-R_{1}YR_{1}^{-1}+e^{-\lambda X}(X+Y)e^{\lambda X}

(118)

\displaystyle=-X-e^{-\lambda X}e^{\lambda(X+Y)}Ye^{-\lambda(X+Y)}e^{\lambda X}+e^{-\lambda X}(X+Y)e^{\lambda X}

(119)

\displaystyle=-X+e^{-\lambda X}\;[-e^{\lambda(X+Y)}Ye^{-\lambda(X+Y)}+X+Y]\;e^{\lambda X}

(120)

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda X}\;[-e^{\lambda(X+Y)}Ye^{-\lambda(X+Y)}+Y]\;e^{\lambda X}

(121)

\displaystyle=-e^{-\lambda\text{ad}_{X}}(e^{\lambda\text{ad}_{X+Y}}-I)Y

(122)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\lambda)^{j}\lambda^{p}}{j!p!}\text{ad}_{X}^{j}\text{ad}_{X+Y}^{p}Y

(123)

Like in
[

\displaystyle F_{n}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}f_{n,k}\lambda^{k}.

From this, we can extract

\displaystyle f_{1,k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j!(k-j)!}\text{ad}_{X}^{j}\text{ad}_{X+Y}^{k-j}Y.

Using the recursive relation (

\displaystyle f_{n,k}=\sum_{j=0}^{[k/n]-1}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j!}\text{ad}_{D_{n}}^{j}f_{n-1,k-nj}

\displaystyle D_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n}f_{1,n-1},\quad\quad\quad n=2,3,4\\
\frac{1}{n}f\left({\left[\frac{n-1}{2}\right],n-1}\right),\quad n\geq 5\end{cases}.

Compared to the right sided expansion in

w=w_{0}-2ag(s)
. We can now ask, what if we were to apply
e^{i\hat{G}^{\prime}s}
on this
\phi
. From (
65
), we already know the final answer would be

\displaystyle\phi(w(s),\overline{w})

\displaystyle=\exp[sh+s(w-2a)\partial_{w}]\cdot\phi(w,\overline{w})

(141)

where the derivative is to be evaluated at
w_{0}-2ag(s)
. Like we did in section
3.3
, using the change of variable
u=\ln(w-2a)
, this simplifies to a translation operation, giving

\displaystyle\phi(w(s),\overline{w})

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\phi(2a-e^{s}[2a-(w_{0}-2ag(s))],\overline{w}).

(142)

This is the result of composing
e^{g(s)D(\epsilon)}
and
e^{i\hat{G}^{\prime}s}
in correct sequence. To include the effect of
e^{i\hat{G}s}
at the beginning, following (
64
), we can do a rescaling
w_{0}\to e^{-s}w_{0}
and cancel
e^{-sh}
. Composing all three therefore gives

\displaystyle\phi(w(s),\overline{w})=

\displaystyle e^{-sh+sh}\phi(w_{0}+2a(1-e^{s})-2ag(s)e^{s}).

(143)

Now finally if we set
g(s)=e^{-s}(s+1)-1
, the answer is

\displaystyle\phi(w(s),\overline{w})

\displaystyle=\phi(w_{0}-2as,\overline{w})

(144)

which is exactly what we will get from
e^{i\mathcal{G}s}
, because
w=x-t
and it decreases as
t
increases.

e^{-sh},e^{sh}

\phi(w(s),\overline{w})
for all

6
Conclusions

In this work we have studied Zassenhaus decomposition of exponential operators for 1+1 dimensional conformal field theories, with main emphasis on half-sided translations. We focused on the observation that one can write
\mathcal{G}=G+G^{\prime}
, where the right side is built out of entanglement Hamiltonians, however,
G,G^{\prime}
are not well-defined operators. We have shown how
G,G^{\prime}
can be replaced by operators
\hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime}
with well-defined nested commutators, and using a central Zassenhaus expansion, we have obtained a decomposition of
e^{is\mathcal{G}}
in terms of
\hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime}
, and their commutators.

One speculative comment that may be relevant here is about
\hat{G},\hat{G}^{\prime}
not being self-adjoint. In section
3.3
we saw that conjugation of a primary
\phi
by these operators does not preserve the operator norm. This is similar to what happens in quantum mechanics for a particle living on half space
(0,\infty)
. The operator
-i\frac{d}{dx}
is not self adjoint on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,\infty)
, and therefore
e^{\frac{d}{dx}}
, the translation operator, is not unitary. This can be seen through the fact that
e^{\frac{d}{dx}}
maps the elements of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,\infty)
to a proper subset by shifting them to right (the map is not onto). The map is in fact an isometry, and while these preserve the inner product, they are not expected to preserve operator norms on conjugation. The situation here is very similar, and it seems to suggest that can
e^{i\hat{G}s},e^{i\hat{G}^{\prime}s}
may be isometries.

As a future direction it would be interesting to explore whether the class of operators permitting decomposition (
69
) can be generalized to include light-ray operators
[
29
]
. One interesting setup to study the decomposition in detail would be for the entanglement Hamiltonian corresponding to a causal diamond, which can be dealt with using the tools developed in this work. This one may be particularly interesting because of its possible connections with entanglement between subregions
(-b_{L},a_{R})
and
(-a_{L},b_{R})
in the limit of shrinking overlap region
(-a_{L},a_{R})
in figure
1
. It would also be interesting to see whether the results can be extended to conformal field theory on AdS
2
for applications to JT gravity, and whether they have deeper connections going back to the split property
[
36
]
.

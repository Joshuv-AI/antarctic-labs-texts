Strichartz Estimates for the ( 𝒌 , 𝒂 ) \boldsymbol{(k,a)} -Generalized Laguerre Operators
Kouichi TAIRA a and Hiroyoshi TAMORI b

Abstract
In this paper, we prove Strichartz estimates for the ( k , a ) (k,a) -generalized Laguerre operators a − 1 ​ ( − | x | 2 − a ​ Δ k + | x | a ) a^{-1}\bigl(-|x|^{2-a}\Delta_{k}+|x|^{a}\bigr) which were introduced by Ben Saïd–Kobayashi–Ørsted, and for the operators | x | 2 − a ​ Δ k |x|^{2-a}\Delta_{k} . Here k k denotes a non-negative multiplicity function for the Dunkl Laplacian Δ k \Delta_{k} and a a denotes a positive real number satisfying certain conditions. The cases a = 1 , 2 a=1,2 were studied previously. We consider more general cases here. The proof depends on symbol-type estimates of special functions and a discrete analog of the stationary phase theorem inspired by the work of Ionescu–Jerison.

Introduction

For the usual Laplacian
\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
), the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\big\|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t\Delta}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]^{2}
satisfies
2/p+n/q=n/2
with
(p,q,n)\neq(2,\infty,2)
. These estimates are called Strichartz estimates and have been widely studied in the past thirty years. Strichartz
[
29
]
proved them for
p=q
by using the Fourier restriction estimates and a duality argument. The most difficult part, that is the end-point case
(p,q)=(2,2n/(n-2))
with
n\geq 3
, was proved by Keel and Tao
[
18
]
.
These are used for well-posedness of linear and non-linear time-dependent Schrödinger equations
[
14
,
34
]
. See also the book
[
31
]
. For the Harmonic oscillator
H_{\rm os}=\frac{-\Delta+|x|^{2}}{2}
, a similar estimates hold:

\big\|{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{\rm os}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p}([-T,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where the region
[-T,T]
cannot be replaced by
\mathbb{R}
essentially due to the existence of
L^{2}
-eigenfunctions.

Given a root system
\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(we assume reducedness and do not assume crystallographic condition for the definition of root system, see
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
), a
[0,\infty)
-valued function on
\mathcal{R}
which is invariant under the finite reflection group
\mathfrak{C}
associated with
\mathcal{R}
is called a non-negative multiplicity function.
For a non-negative multiplicity function
k
and
a>0
, we define the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operator by

\displaystyle H_{k,a}:=\frac{-|x|^{2-a}\Delta_{k}+|x|^{a}}{a}\qquad\text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.

Here
\Delta_{k}
denotes the Dunkl Laplacian (see
[
2
, formula (2.9)]
).
If
k\equiv 0
, the Dunkl Laplacian coincides with the usual Laplacian:
\Delta_{0}=\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}
.

The
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operator
H_{k,a}
is the generator of the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre semigroup which is a holomorphic semigroup introduced by Ben Saïd, Kobayashi and Ørsted
[
2
]
.
For the case
k\equiv 0
and
a=2
(resp.
a=1
), the semigroup is the Hermite semigroup
[
11
,
16
]
(resp. the Laguerre semigroup
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
).
These two semigroups are associated with some realization (called the Schrödinger model) of minimal representations of the metaplectic group
\mathrm{Mp}(n,\mathbb{R})
and a double cover of the indefinite orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}(n+1,2)
.
As unitary representations of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathfrak{C}
, they deformed these two representations with parameters
k
and
a
, and obtained a family of unitary representations.
The
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre semigroups are associated with them.

In
[
2
]
, the Fourier transforms associated with the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre semigroups are introduced and these various properties are studied. Recently, there have been several studies related to these operators such as real Paley–Wiener theorem
[
23
]
,
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness of Fourier multipliers
[
22
]
, Hardy inequality
[
32
]
and wavelet transform
[
3
]
.

The aim of this paper is to prove Strichartz estimates of Schrödinger equations associated with the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operators. This problem is proposed in
[
2
]
and solved in
[
1
]
and
[
24
]
for
a=1
and
a=2
(see also
[
25
]
, where they deal with orthonormal Strichartz estimates). The
(0,2)
-generalized Laguerre operator
H_{0,2}
is just the Harmonic oscillator
H_{\rm os}
and so their results are a generalization of the classical result for
H_{\rm os}
. Here, we deal with more general cases.

For
a=1,2
, the integral kernel of the Schrödinger propagator has a nice expression (due to
[
2
, formula (4.58)]
), which immediately implies the dispersive estimate
[
2
, Proposition 4.26]
. Hence the Strichartz estimates for
a=1,2
are a direct consequence of this estimate and the result in
[
18
]
by Keel and Tao, see also
[
1
,
25
]
. One of the difficulties to extend it to general
a
is a lack of such a nice expression of the Schrödinger propagator. Actually, this is just expressed in terms of an infinite sum of a product of special functions (see (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)). Therefore, we need to control this sum uniformly with respect to some parameters.
To overcome this difficulty, we use a strategy inspired by the proof of Carleman estimates due to Ionescu and Jerison
[
17
]
. To be precise, we reduce estimates of the sum to those of integrals and use the theory of oscillatory integrals such as the stationary phase theorem with several parameters. One difference from
[
17
]
is that we avoid using the dyadic decomposition which is used there many times. Instead, we employ an appropriate scaling and simplify some arguments.
In a sequel work
[
30
]
, we will give another approach based on a deformation of integrals developed in the proof for the Strichartz estimates on flat cones
[
12
]
.

In the last few decades, numerous works have focused on the dispersive estimates or the Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger operators with critical electromagnetic potentials such as Aharonov–Bohm magnetic fields (see
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
) and Laplace–Beltrami operators on conic manifolds (
[
12
,
35
]
). Due to the spherical symmetry of their operator, the integral kernel of their propagator has the form

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\colon\text{eigenvalues on the sphere}}K_{\nu}(t,r_{1},r_{2})H_{\nu}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}),

where
K_{\nu}
is the propagator in the radial direction and
H_{\nu}
is the projection in the spherical direction. To achieve optimal estimates for this integral kernel, one has to use the oscillatory behavior of
K_{\nu}
and
H_{\nu}
, in other words, some cancelation of the sum much like our case. In their paper, it is accomplished through the use of the complex contour deformation and certain functional equations of the special functions.
Therefore, their method appears to be inapplicable when the radial propagator or the spherical projection is not expressed by special functions. Typical scenarios involving these situations arise in the studies of the Schrödinger equation with the degenerate trapping
[
6
]
or the wave equation on the Schwartzshild spacetime
[
7
]
. In their works, the dispersive or Strichartz estimates for initial values with a fixed angular momentum are considered, that is, they studied single modes only and did not consider the sum possibly because of the difficulty to treat the oscillation of the sum.

In this paper, we deduce the asymptotic expansions of the radial direction and the spherical projection first and then sum up them by exploiting their oscillatory behavior. Specifically, we use the properties of the special functions in the first step only. Hence the authors believe that the method employed here specifically in the second step remains applicable even when
K_{\nu}
and
H_{\nu}
are not expressed in terms of special functions as is the case in
[
6
,
7
]
(although we might need more precise analysis of the radial propagators).

The second contribution of this paper is to give a naive application of the stationary phase theorem (see Proposition
2.4
). This is used to prove improved dispersive estimates for
H_{k,a}
(near the diagonal) under the restriction
0<a<2
. Our integral kernel has multiple parameters and it seems important to consider when a similar statement the usual stationary phase theorem holds uniformly with respect to additional parameters and when it is improved. Our Proposition
2.4
addresses intermediate cases between two scenarios where the decay order of an oscillatory integral is improved, see Section
2.3
.

Finally, we also obtain symbol-type estimates of higher-order derivatives for
J
-Bessel functions, which was done in
[
17
]
up to second derivatives and was anticipated to be true for higher-order derivatives there (see
[
17
, Remark after Theorem 9.1]
). It seems that the method used in
[
17
]
via complex counter deformation cannot be applied to the estimates for higher-order derivatives. Here we use an alternative method based on the stationary phase type theorem and partially solve them at the cost of loss of estimates for some parameters.
See Proposition
3.1
for the precise statement and Appendix
A.4
for its proof. We also mention a recent work
[
26
]
, where the precise asymptotic behavior of the Bessel function is given although the authors do not know whether our symbol-type estimates follow from the results in
[
26
]
.

1.1
Main theorem

Let us state our main theorem.
For a nonnegative multiplicity function
k
and
a>0
, we write

\displaystyle\vartheta_{k,a}(x):=|x|^{a-2}\prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{k(\alpha)}

(1.1)

and assume that the homogeneous degree of the measure
\vartheta_{k,a}(x){\rm d}x
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is positive

\displaystyle\sigma_{k,a}:=\frac{n+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}k(\alpha)+a-2}{a}>0.

(1.2)

Then it is shown in
[
2
, Corollary 3.22]
that the operator
H_{k,a}
with domain
W_{k,a}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined in
[
2
, equation (3.29)]
is essentially self-adjoint on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\vartheta_{k,a}(x){\rm d}x)
. We denote the unique self-adjoint extension of
H_{k,a}
by the same symbol
H_{k,a}
.

We write
L^{q}=L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\vartheta_{k,a}(x){\rm d}x)
and
L^{p}(I,L^{q})=L^{p}(I;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\vartheta_{k,a}(x){\rm d}x))
for
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Recall that an exponent pair
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]^{2}
is called
\sigma_{k,a}
-admissible
if

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\sigma_{k,a}}{q}=\frac{\sigma_{k,a}}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(p,q,\sigma_{k,a})\neq(2,\infty,1).

When
n=1
, a nonnegative multiplicity function
k
is a constant function.
In this case, we regard
k
as a nonnegative real number
k(\alpha)

(\alpha\in\mathcal{R})
, and we see
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}k(\alpha)=2k
.

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume one of the following, which implies (
1.2
):

•

n=1
and
a\geq 2-4k
,

•

n\geq 2
and
(0<a\leq 1
or
a=2)
,

•

n\geq 2
,
1<a<2
and
k\equiv 0
.

Let
(p,q),(p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2})\in[2,\infty]^{2}
be
\sigma_{k,a}
-admissible exponents.
Then, for
T>0
, there exists
{C>0}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}u\big\|_{L^{p}([-T,T];L^{q})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(t-s)H_{k,a}}f(s)ds\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}([-T,T];L^{q_{1}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([-T,T];L^{q_{2}^{*}})},

(1.4)

where
r^{*}
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
r
:
r^{*}=r/(r-1)
and in addition
(p_{j},q_{j})\neq\bigl(2,\frac{2\sigma_{k,a}}{\sigma_{k,a}-1}\bigr)
when all of the conditions
n\geq 2
,
1<a<2
and
k\equiv 0
hold.

.

The

case was treated in

]
, where an additional assumption

(see (

(2)

For

(see Theorem

(3)

(4)

In the above estimates, we cannot replace the time interval

by

be an

although

.

Moreover, we can deduce global in time Strichartz estimates for

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the same assumptions and notation as Theorem
1.1
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t|x|^{a}\Delta_{k}}u\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};L^{q})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\left\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(t-s)|x|^{a}\Delta_{k}}f(s){\rm d}s\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R};L^{q_{1}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}(\mathbb{R};L^{q_{2}^{*}})}.

1.2
Key theorem

We write
J_{\lambda}(z)
for the
J
-Bessel function,
\tilde{I}_{\lambda}(w)
for the normalized
I
-Bessel function, and
C^{\nu}_{m}(t)
for
the Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
m
.
These functions are defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\lambda}(w):={}

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}{\rm i}\lambda}\left(\frac{w}{2}\right)^{-\lambda}J_{\lambda}({\rm i}w),

\displaystyle C^{\nu}_{m}(t):={}

\displaystyle\frac{(-2)^{m}}{m!}\frac{\Gamma(m+\nu)\Gamma(m+2\nu)}{\Gamma(\nu)\Gamma(2m+2\nu)}(1-t^{2})^{-\nu+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}^{m}}{{\rm d}t^{m}}\bigl(1-t^{2}\bigr)^{m+\nu-\frac{1}{2}}.

Now we define

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;w;t):={}

\displaystyle\Gamma(b\nu+1)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{w}{2}\right)^{bm}\tilde{I}_{b(m+\nu)}(w)(m+\nu)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(t)

(1.5)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\Gamma(b\nu+1)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{{\rm i}w}{2}\right)^{-b\nu}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}bm{\rm i}}J_{b(m+\nu)}({\rm i}w)(m+\nu)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(t)

for
b>0,\nu\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
and
t\in[-1,1]
, where we interpret
(m+\nu)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(t)
as
{\lim_{\nu\searrow 0}(m+\nu)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(t)}
when
\nu=0
. Moreover, we take a branch of
w^{bm}
such that
w^{bm}\in(0,\infty)
for
w\in(0,\infty)
and
w^{bm}|_{w=0}=0
.
Then the sum in (
1.5
) absolutely converges, and
\mathscr{I}
is a continuous function (see
[
2
, Lemma 4.17 (1)]
or Section
6.1
).

In
[
2
, Theorem C, equations (4.50) and (4.52)]
, it is shown that the integral kernel
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}(x,\allowbreak x^{\prime})
of
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}
for
0<|t|<\pi
is given by

\displaystyle c_{k,a}\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\frac{|x|^{a}+|x^{\prime}|^{a}}{a}\mathrm{cot}(t)}}{({\rm i}\sin(t))^{\sigma_{k,a}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathscr{I}\Bigg(\frac{2}{a},\frac{a(\sigma_{k,a}-1)}{2};-{\rm i}\frac{2|x|^{\frac{a}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{a}{2}}}{a\sin(t)};\xi\cdot\hat{x^{\prime}}\Bigg){\rm d}\mu_{\hat{x}}^{k}(\xi),

(1.6)

where
\hat{x}=x/|x|
,
c_{k,a}
is the constant defined in
[
2
, equation (1.6)]
,
\cdot
denotes the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
{\rm d}\mu_{\hat{x}}^{k}
is the probability measure introduced in
[
2
, equation (2.5)]
. Moreover, for
k\equiv 0
, we have a more explicit expression

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{0,a}}(x,x^{\prime})=c_{0,a}\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\frac{|x|^{a}+|x^{\prime}|^{a}}{a}\mathrm{cot}(t)}}{({\rm i}\sin(t))^{\frac{n+a-2}{a}}}\mathscr{I}\left(\frac{2}{a},\frac{n-2}{2};-{\rm i}\frac{2|x|^{\frac{a}{2}}|x^{\prime}|^{\frac{a}{2}}}{a\sin(t)};\hat{x}\cdot\hat{x^{\prime}}\right).

(1.7)

Theorem
1.1
for

is a consequence of uniform bounds for
\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;w;t)
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\nu\geq 0
.

(i)

Suppose

and

. Then there exists

such that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)|\leq C_{b,\nu,\varepsilon}(1+|y|)^{(1-b)\nu}\qquad\text{for}\quad y\in\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi\in[0,\pi-\varepsilon].

When

, we can take

.

(ii)

Suppose

. Then there exists

such that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)|\leq C_{b,\nu}(1+|y|)^{(2-b)\nu}\qquad\text{for}\quad y\in\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi\in[0,\pi].

In particular, when

, the sum
\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)
is uniformly bounded with respect to
{y\in\mathbb{R}}
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi-\varepsilon]
. Moreover, when

,

or

\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)

.

.

These estimates are sharp for

with respect to the growth in

. In fact,

show
|\mathscr{I}(1,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)|=1

]
.

1.3
Idea of the proof

Here we give an idea of the proof of Theorem
1.4
. We remark that the difficulty lies in the uniformity with respect to the parameters
y
and
\varphi
. For the case
|y|\lesssim 1
, the results are an immediate consequence of the estimates given in
[
2
]
(for rigorous treatment, see Section
6.1
). Hence we consider the case
|y|\gtrsim 1
. Let us assume
y\gtrsim 1
for simplicity.

First, we try to estimate the sum
\mathscr{I}
using optimal estimates for the Bessel functions and the Gegenbauer polynomials. We write

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)=

\displaystyle L_{b,\nu}y^{-b\nu}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+\nu){\rm e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}bm{\rm i}}J_{b(m+\nu)}(y)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(\cos\varphi).

Since the estimates for finite
m
are easy to prove, we only consider the sum over
m\gg 1
.
By the bounds for the Bessel functions
|J_{\mu}(y)|\leq C\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
in (
3.2
) and the Gegenbauer polynomials
{\big|\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)\big|\leq Cm^{2\nu-1}}
in (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\left|y^{-b\nu}\sum_{m\gg 1}^{\infty}(m+\nu){\rm e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}bm{\rm i}}J_{b(m+\nu)}(y)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(\cos\varphi)\right|\leq{}

\displaystyle Cy^{-b\nu}\sum_{m\gg 1}m\cdot m^{-\frac{1}{3}}\cdot m^{2\nu-1}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle Cy^{-b\nu}\sum_{m\gg 1}m^{2\nu-\frac{1}{3}}.

Since the sum
\sum_{m\gg 1}m^{2\nu-\frac{1}{3}}
is not convergent for
\nu\geq-1/3
at all and since the upper bounds for special functions are optimal, such a direct method cannot be applied. Similarly, the estimate based on
|\tilde{I}_{\lambda}(-{\rm i}y)|\leq\Gamma(\lambda+1)^{-1}

is far from a uniform estimate.

The drawback of the above strategy is to use the triangle inequality
|\sum\cdot|\leq\sum|\cdot|
.
To obtain a better estimate of the sum
\mathscr{I}(b,\nu;-{\rm i}y;\cos\varphi)
, we need to make use of some cancellation of the sum instead of using the triangle inequality.
To do this, we write it as a sum of a WKB form

\displaystyle(m+\nu){\rm e}^{-\frac{\pi}{2}bm{\rm i}}J_{b(m+\nu)}(y)\nu^{-1}C^{\nu}_{m}(\cos\varphi)=:\zeta(m,y,\varphi){\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m,y,\varphi)},

where a phase function
S
is a real-valued function and an amplitude
\zeta
does not oscillate as
{m\to\infty}
(uniformly in other parameters
y
,
\varphi
). Roughly speaking, it follows from the classical formula (
2.1
) that we can replace the discrete sum by a sum of integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{m\gg 1}\zeta(m,y,\varphi){\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m,y,\varphi)+2\pi{\rm i}mq}{\rm d}m,\qquad q\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1.8)

The problem on convergence (or uniform boundedness in some parameters) of such an integral has a long history and is related to the stationary phase theorem
[
28
]
. We can anticipate that a uniform estimate for the sum
\mathscr{I}
can be proved by this theorem. On the other hand, we have to be careful to justify it because of the following reasons:

•

To use cancelation of the oscillation via the stationary or non-stationary phase theorems, we need symbol-type (derivative) estimates of
\zeta
. To do this, we have to study those of the special functions
J_{\mu}
and
C_{m}^{\nu}
.

•

The integral (
1.8
) has a lot of parameters and we have to estimate it in a uniform way.

•

The
J
-Bessel function
J_{b(m+\nu)}(y)
has different asymptotic behaviors as
m,y\to\infty
on the regions
m\ll y
,
m\thickapprox y
,
m\gg y
(see Proposition
3.1
) and therefore the WKB form
{\zeta(m,y,\varphi){\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m,y,\varphi)}}
has different properties on each region.

•

The decay rate of
C^{\nu}_{m}(\cos\varphi)
as
m\to\infty
becomes worse as
\varphi\to 0,\pi
(see Proposition
3.3
) and hence estimates for
\varphi=0,\pi
are also worse in general. Nevertheless, at
\varphi=0
with
{0<b<2}
, we can improve the estimate since the first derivative of
S(m,y,0)
vanishes only on regions where
m
is small enough (see Theorem
1.4
(i) and Section
5.2
).

Preliminary

2.1
A sum of monotonic functions

The next lemma is elementary and follows from the piecewise quadrature.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous monotonically decreasing function. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in b\mathbb{N},\,m_{1}\leq m\leq m_{2}}f(m)\leq\int_{m_{1}-b}^{m_{2}}f(x){\rm d}x

for
b>0
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
m_{1}<m_{2}
, where
b\mathbb{N}=\{bn\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

2.2
Discrete oscillatory integrals

In this paper, the following classical formula plays an important role:

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m)}\zeta(m)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m)}\zeta(m){\rm d}m-\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(S(m)+2\pi qm)}\tilde{\zeta}(m){\rm d}m,

(2.1)

where
\tilde{\zeta}(m)=\zeta^{\prime}(m)+{\rm i}S^{\prime}(m)\zeta(m)
(for example, see
[
17
, Proof of Lemma 5.3]
). In this paper, we use this formula for a compactly supported smooth function
\zeta
and a smooth function
S
, so the convergence of the sum does not matter.
From this formula, we can deduce a discrete analogue of the non-stationary phase theorem.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
C_{\alpha}>0
for any nonnegative integer
\alpha
and
M\geq 1
,
0<r\leq 1
,
0<\rho\leq 1
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
.
Suppose that

\supp\zeta\subset[0,M]
,
|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}\zeta(m)|\leq C_{\alpha}M^{k-\rho\alpha}
.
Suppose that a smooth real-valued function
S\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(\partial_{m}S(m),2\pi\mathbb{Z})\geq r,\qquad\big|\partial_{m}^{\alpha+1}S(m)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}M^{-\rho\alpha}|\partial_{m}S(m)|.

Then, for each
N>0
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m)}\zeta(m)\right|\leq C_{N}M^{k+1}(1+rM^{\rho})^{-N},

where
C_{N}>0
depends only on
0<\rho\leq 1
,
N>0
and a finite number of
C_{\alpha}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
17
]
, the fact that
C_{N}
is independent of
r
is important since they avoid to use the stationary phase theorem there. In this paper, we do not use this fact, that is, we use the case
r=1
only in this paper.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

The proof is a slight modification of
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
and we discuss briefly here.

By the assumption and the intermediate value theorem, there exists a unique
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\partial_{m}S(m)\in(2\pi k,2\pi(k+1))
. This implies

r\leq\partial_{m}S(m)-2\pi k\leq 2\pi-r
. By replacing
S(m)
by
S(m)-2\pi k
, we may assume
k=0
and
r\leq\partial_{m}S(m)\leq 2\pi-r
.

From the formula (
2.1
), the problem reduces to the estimates for each integrals appearing in (
2.1
). By the change of variable from
m
to
M^{\rho}m
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m)}\zeta(m){\rm d}m=M^{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S_{M}(m)}\zeta_{M}(m){\rm d}m,

(2.2)

where we set
S_{M}(m)\!=S(M^{\rho}m)
and
\zeta_{M}(m)\!=\zeta(M^{\rho}m)
. Then the assumptions imply
|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}\zeta_{M}(m)|\allowbreak\leq C_{\alpha}M^{k}
,
\big|\partial_{m}^{\alpha+1}S_{M}(m)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}|\partial_{m}S_{M}(m)|
,
\partial_{m}S_{M}(m)\geq rM^{\rho}
and
\supp\zeta_{M}\subset\bigl[0,4M^{1-\rho}\bigr]
. We integrate by parts
N
times in the right-hand side of (
2.2
) (use the identity
({\rm i}S_{M}^{\prime}(m))^{-1}\partial_{m}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S_{M}(m)}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}S_{M}(m)}
and Lemma
A.1
) and obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}S(m)}\zeta(m){\rm d}m\right|\leq M^{\rho}\cdot C_{N}M^{k}(1+rM^{\rho})^{-N}\cdot M^{1-\rho}=C_{N}M^{k+1}(1+rM^{\rho})^{-N},

where the term
M^{1-\rho}
comes from the volume of the support
\supp\zeta_{M}
. The second terms of the right-hand side of (
2.1
) are similarly dealt with as in the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 5.3]
.
∎

2.3
A variant of the stationary phase theorem

Here we give a variant of the stationary phase theorem which is used for an improvement of estimates of
\mathscr{I}
described in Section
5.2
.
We consider the following integral with parameters
\lambda
,
\varphi
:

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\varphi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda S(\mu,\varphi)}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi){\rm d}\mu

and its decay rate with respect to
\lambda\gg 1

If we freeze the parameter
\varphi
, then the stationary phase theorem just implies that if
\mu\mapsto S(\mu,\varphi)
is a Morse function (that is, all critical points are non-degenerate in the sense that
\partial_{\mu}^{2}S\neq 0
there), the optimal decay rate of
I
is
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. To obtain an improved decay, here we consider the following two scenarios:

•

If we assume that
\gamma
vanishes with order
2\nu
at all the critical points of
S
in addition, then the optimal decay rate is improved to be
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}
.

•

If the symbol
\gamma
itself decays like
(1+\lambda)^{-\nu}
, then the decay order becomes
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu}
.

The following proposition interpolates the above two situations in a uniform way with respect to the parameter
\varphi
.
A typical example of such a phase function is
S(\mu,\varphi)=(\mu-\varphi)^{2}
. This simple case could simplify the reading of the proof below.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\nu\geq 0
and
c,C>0
. Suppose that
S\in C^{\infty}\bigl([0,1]^{2};\mathbb{R}\bigr)
and
\gamma(\cdot,\lambda,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])
satisfy

\displaystyle\supp\gamma(\cdot,\lambda,\varphi)\subset[0,1],\qquad|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha}\mu^{2\nu-\alpha}(1+\lambda\varphi\mu)^{-\nu},

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu,\varphi)\big|\geq c,\qquad|\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\varphi}S(\mu,\varphi)|\geq C

uniformly in
\mu\in[0,1]
,
\varphi\in[0,1]
and
\lambda\gg 1
. Suppose that
S(\cdot,\varphi)
has a unique critical point
{\mu_{0}(\varphi)}
which is smooth with respect to
\varphi
and that
\mu_{0}(0)=0
. Then

|I(\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}}
uniformly in
\lambda\gg 1
and
\varphi\in[0,1]
.

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

Without the assumption
\mu_{0}(0)=0
, we obtain
I(\lambda,\varphi)\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
only. The additional decay
\lambda^{-\nu}
comes from the factor
(1+\lambda\varphi\mu)^{-\nu}
in the assumption of
\gamma
and the fact that
\mu_{0}(0)=0
as is mentioned before.

(2)

We observe that the critical point
\mu_{0}(\varphi)
of
S
is close to
0
if
\varphi\sim 0
. There we take advantage of the assumption that the symbol
\gamma
vanishes at zero with order
2\nu
like
|\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\mu^{2\nu}
. On the other hand, if
\varphi
is away from zero, then the critical point
\mu_{0}(\varphi)
is also away from zero. In this case,
\gamma
itself decays like
\lambda^{-\nu}
near the critical point (due to
|\varphi|\gtrsim 1
and
{|\mu_{0}(\varphi)|\gtrsim 1}
), which makes us to prove the improved decay. The difficulty here is to deal with the case where
\varphi
is small enough but depending on
\lambda
.

(3)

The critical point of
S(\cdot,\varphi)
is unique due to the condition
\big|\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu,\varphi)\big|\geq c
in this case.

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

We may assume
\supp\gamma(\cdot,\lambda,\varphi)\subset\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
. In fact, we set

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)=\chi\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu\bigr)\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi),

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\chi(\mu)=1
on
\mu\leq 1
and
\chi(\mu)=0
for
\mu\geq 2
. Using the bound
{|\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\mu^{2\nu}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda S(\mu,\varphi)}\gamma^{\prime}(\mu,\lambda,\varphi){\rm d}\mu\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\nu}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu\bigr){\rm d}\mu\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence, we can replace
\gamma
by
\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}
, where we note that
\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}
satisfies

\displaystyle\supp(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})(\cdot,\lambda,\varphi)\subset\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr],\qquad|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}(\gamma-\gamma^{\prime})(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha}\mu^{2\nu-\alpha}(1+\lambda\varphi\mu)^{-\nu}.

In the following, we assume
\supp\gamma(\cdot,\lambda,\varphi)\subset\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
.

(i)
First, we consider the case
\varphi\in[0,1]
satisfying
\mu_{0}(\varphi)\leq 2^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Since the signature of
\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu,\varphi)
does not change for
\mu\in\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
and
\varphi\in[0,1]
, we have

|\partial_{\mu}S(\mu,\varphi)|=\left|\int_{\mu_{0}(\varphi)}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu^{\prime},\varphi){\rm d}\mu^{\prime}\right|=\int_{\mu_{0}(\varphi)}^{\mu}\big|\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu^{\prime},\varphi)\big|{\rm d}\mu^{\prime}\geq c(\mu-\mu_{0}(\varphi))\geq 2^{-1}c\mu

for
\mu\in\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
. By integrating by parts and using
\supp\gamma(\cdot,\lambda,\varphi)\subset\bigl[\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
, we have

\displaystyle|I(\lambda,\varphi)|=\left|(-{\rm i}\lambda)^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda S(\mu,\varphi)}L^{N}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi){\rm d}\mu\right|\leq\lambda^{-N}\int_{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}|L^{N}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|{\rm d}\mu,

where
L=\partial_{\mu}\circ(\partial_{\mu}S)(\mu,\varphi)^{-1}
and we take
N>\nu+1
. We observe from the assumption and Lemma
A.1
that
\big|L^{N}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi)\big|\lesssim\mu^{2\nu-2N}
, which leads to

\displaystyle|I(\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\int_{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{1}\mu^{2\nu-2N}{\rm d}\mu\lesssim\lambda^{-\nu-\frac{1}{2}}.

(ii)
Next, we consider the case
\varphi\in[0,1]
satisfying
\mu_{0}(\varphi)\in\bigl[2^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\bigr]
.
Differentiating
(\partial_{\mu}S)(\mu_{0}(\varphi),\varphi)=0
with respect to
\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle|\mu_{0}^{\prime}(\varphi)|=\bigl|-(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\varphi}S)(\mu_{0}(\varphi),\varphi)/\partial_{\mu}^{2}S(\mu_{0}(\varphi),\varphi)\bigr|\sim 1,

and hence
\mu_{0}(\varphi)\sim\varphi
for
\varphi\in[0,1]
due to
\mu_{0}(0)=0
. Now the assumption
\mu_{0}(\varphi)\geq 2^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
yields
\varphi\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

By scaling, we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda S(\mu,\varphi)}\gamma(\mu,\lambda,\varphi){\rm d}\mu=\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}S_{\varphi}(\mu)}\gamma_{\varphi}(\mu,\lambda){\rm d}\mu,

where we set

\displaystyle S_{\varphi}(\mu)=\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{-2}S(\mu_{0}(\varphi)\mu,\varphi),\qquad\gamma_{\varphi}(\mu,\lambda)=\lambda^{\nu}\gamma(\mu_{0}(\varphi)\mu,\lambda,\varphi).

We note that
\mu_{0}(\varphi)
is a unique critical point of
S(\mu,\varphi)
.
Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
{\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}+\psi_{3}=1}
,
\supp\psi_{1}\subset\bigl(-\infty,\frac{3}{4}\bigr]
,
\supp\psi_{2}\subset\bigl[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\bigr]
and
\supp\psi_{3}\subset\bigl[\frac{5}{4},\infty\bigr)
. We write

\displaystyle I(\lambda,\varphi)=\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}S_{\varphi}(\mu)}\gamma_{\varphi}(\mu,\lambda)\psi_{j}(\mu){\rm d}\mu=\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\sum_{j=1}^{3}I_{j}(\lambda,\varphi).

First, we deal with
I_{2}(\lambda,\varphi)
.
Now we see
|\partial_{\mu}S(\mu,\varphi)|\geq c|\mu-\mu_{0}(\varphi)|
for
\mu\in[0,1]
and
\varphi\in[0,1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{\mu}S_{\varphi}(\mu)|=\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{-1}\cdot|(\partial_{\mu}S)(\mu_{0}(\varphi)\mu,\varphi)|\geq c|\mu-1|

for
\mu\in\supp\gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\lambda)
and
\varphi\in(0,1]
.
Moreover, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\gamma_{\varphi}(\mu,\lambda)|=\lambda^{\nu}\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{\alpha}|(\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\gamma)(\mu_{0}(\varphi)\mu,\lambda,\varphi)|

\displaystyle\phantom{|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\gamma_{\varphi}(\mu,\lambda)|}{}\lesssim\lambda^{\nu}\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{\alpha}(\mu_{0}(\varphi)\mu)^{2\nu-\alpha}\bigl(1+\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\mu\bigr)^{-\nu}\lesssim\mu^{\nu-\alpha},

\displaystyle\supp(\gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\lambda))\subset\big\{\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{-1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mu\leq\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{-1}\big\},\qquad\big|\partial_{\mu}^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}S_{\varphi}(\mu)\big|\lesssim|\mu_{0}(\varphi)|^{\alpha^{\prime}-1}

Thus the stationary phase theorem (see Lemma
A.4
with
k=2
,
j=0
and
x_{0}=1
) implies

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}|I_{2}(\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\cdot\bigl(\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu},

where we use
\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\gtrsim 1
.

Next, we consider the estimate of
I_{1}(\lambda,\varphi)
. We note that
|\partial_{\mu}S_{\varphi}(\mu)|\gtrsim 1
for
\mu\in\supp\psi_{1}
.
Then the non-stationary phase theorem (see Lemma
A.3
with
\mu=\nu
) implies

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}|I_{1}(\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\cdot\bigl(\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\bigr)^{-\nu-1}\lesssim\varphi\lambda^{-\nu}\cdot\bigl(\lambda\varphi^{2}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu},

where we use
\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\gtrsim 1
.

Finally, we consider the term
I_{3}(\lambda,\varphi)
. We note that
|\partial_{\mu}S_{\varphi}(\mu)|\gtrsim(|\mu|+1)
for
\mu\in\supp\psi_{3}
. Then, by integrating by parts
N(>1)
times, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}|I_{3}(\lambda,\varphi)|\lesssim\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\cdot\bigl(\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\bigr)^{-N}\lesssim\mu_{0}(\varphi)\lambda^{-\nu}\cdot\bigl(\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}-\nu},

where we use
\lambda\mu_{0}(\varphi)^{2}\gtrsim 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.1
Asymptotic expansion of Bessel functions

Here we discuss asymptotic behavior of Bessel functions. For our purpose, we need symbol-type estimates, which is the estimates for higher order derivatives of the amplitudes. Such estimates are studied in

Now we write down the statement here and give its proof in Appendix

.

We define

\displaystyle h_{1}(z)=\sqrt{1-z^{2}}-z\cos^{-1}z,

where
\cos^{-1}z\in[0,\pi/2]
for
0\leq z\leq 1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(i)

There are smooth functions
a_{\pm,y}\colon\bigl[0,y-\frac{1}{2}y^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigr]\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(y)=y^{-\frac{1}{4}}(y-\mu)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\bigl(a_{+,y}(\mu){\rm e}^{{\rm i}yh_{1}(\frac{\mu}{y})}+a_{-,y}(\mu){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}yh_{1}(\frac{\mu}{y})}\bigr)

and for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}a_{\pm,y}(\mu)|\leq C_{\alpha}(y-\mu)^{-\alpha}
, for
y\geq 8
,
\mu\in\bigl[0,y-\frac{1}{2}y^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigr]
.

(ii)

Let
N>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exist
C_{\alpha}>0
and
C_{\alpha N}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}J_{\mu}(y)|\leq\begin{cases}C_{\alpha}y^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{3}}\qquad\text{for}\ y\geq 8,\ \mu\in\bigl[y-2y^{\frac{1}{3}},y+2y^{\frac{1}{3}}\bigr],\\
C_{\alpha N}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\mu-y)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\alpha}\bigl(y^{-1}(\mu-y)^{3}\bigr)^{-N}\\
\qquad\text{for}\ y\geq 8,\ \mu\in\bigl[y+\frac{1}{2}y^{\frac{1}{3}},\infty\bigr).\end{cases}

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

The estimate for

is weaker than the one in
[

at least for
\alpha=0,1,2
. In fact,
[

, Theorem 9.1]
shows that
J_{\mu}(y)

(due to the term

(2)

Another drawback of this proposition compared to
[

is not to give symbol-type estimates of the

In particular, we have a uniform bound

\displaystyle|J_{\mu}(y)|\leq Cy^{-\frac{1}{3}}\qquad y\geq 8,\quad\mu\geq 0.

(3.2)

3.2
Asymptotic expansion of Gegenbauer polynomials

The following proposition is a refinement of
[
17
, Lemma 10.2]

A.6

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\nu\geq 0
.
There are functions
g_{+}(m,\varphi),g_{-}(m,\varphi)
which are smooth respect to
m\in[1,\infty)
and a function
r(m,\varphi)
defined for

such that

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)=\sum_{\pm}g_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi){\rm e}^{\pm{\rm i}m\varphi}+r(m,\varphi)

for

(

\lim_{\nu\searrow 0}\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi))

\displaystyle|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}g_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\nu}m^{2\nu-1-\alpha}(1+m\sin\varphi)^{-\nu},\qquad m\geq 1,

\displaystyle|r(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{N,\nu}m^{-N},\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

for each
N>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, and

with constants

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

In particular, for each
\varepsilon>0
, we have
|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}g_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon}m^{\nu-1-\alpha}
uniformly in
\varphi\in[\varepsilon,\pi-\varepsilon]
.

(2)

We do not impose that
g_{\nu,\pm}
are continuous with respect to
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
. In this paper, we just need uniform estimates of
g_{\nu,\pm}
in
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
.

(3)

In
[

, Lemma 10.2]
, a similar estimate for

divided by

).

Next, we consider the case when
1<a<2
and
k\equiv 0
. Let
0<\varphi_{0}<2\pi
.
Take a finite partition of unity
\{\chi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset C^{\infty}\bigl(\mathbb{S}^{n-1};[0,1]\bigr)
on the sphere such that

\omega,\eta\in\supp\chi_{j}\Rightarrow\omega\cdot\eta\geq\cos\varphi_{0}
.
Due to (
1.7
) and Theorem
1.4
(i), there exists
C>0
such that
\big|\chi_{j}(\hat{x}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}(x,x^{\prime})\chi_{j}\bigl(\hat{x^{\prime}}\bigr)\big|\leq C|t|^{-\sigma_{0,a}}
for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
and

\displaystyle\big\|\chi_{j}(\hat{x}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(t-s)H_{k,a}}(x,x^{\prime})\chi_{j}\bigl(\hat{x^{\prime}}\bigr)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{x^{\prime}})}\leq C|t-s|^{-\sigma_{0,a}}

for
|t|,|s|\leq\frac{\pi}{4}
with
t\neq s
.
Applying Theorem
6.1

U(t):=1_{[-T,T]}(t)\chi_{j}(\hat{x}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}

\big\|\chi_{j}(\hat{x}){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}u\big\|_{L^{p}([-T,T];L^{q})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}
.
Since the number

).

The inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates (

) when

and

5
]
and a complex interpolation since the end-point case

).

Proof of Theorem

Appendix A
Asymptotic behavior of special functions

A.1
Leibniz’s rule

We frequently use the following formula, which directly follows from Leibniz’s rule.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
r
be a smooth function and
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then there exist smooth functions
b_{jN}
such that

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{x}\circ r(x)^{-1}\bigr)^{N}=\frac{1}{r(x)^{N}}\!\sum_{j=0}^{N}b_{jN}(x)\partial_{x}^{j}\qquad\text{with}\quad|b_{jN}(x)|\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{N-j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{k}=N-j,\\
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\!\frac{\big|r^{(\ell_{i})}(x)\big|}{|r(x)|}.

Remark A.2
.

More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial_{x}\circ r(x)^{-1}\bigr)^{N}=\frac{1}{r(x)^{N}}\partial_{x}^{N}+\frac{1}{r(x)^{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{N-j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{k}=N-j,\\
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{k}\geq 1\end{subarray}}C_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ldots\ell_{k}}^{jN}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{r^{(\ell_{i})}(x)}{r(x)}\Bigg)\partial_{x}^{j}

with constants
C_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ldots\ell_{m}}^{jN}>0
.

A.2
Non-stationary phase theorem with a singular amplitude

The following lemma is more or less well known and an easy consequence of integration by parts. We give a proof for completeness of the paper.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\mu>-1
,
\gamma\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty))
which is supported in
x\leq 10
such that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\gamma(x)|\lesssim|x|^{\mu-\alpha}
for
x\in(0,\infty)
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\im f(x)\geq 0
,
f^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
for
{x\in\supp\gamma}
and
f(0)\in\mathbb{R}
. We define

\displaystyle b_{\mu}(\lambda):={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\lambda f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\qquad\text{for}\quad\lambda\gtrsim 1.

Then for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle|\partial_{\lambda}^{\alpha}b_{\mu}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu-1-\alpha},\qquad\lambda\gtrsim 1.

(A.1)

In particular,
\big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\big|\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu-1}
for
\lambda\gtrsim 1
.

Proof.

We only need to prove these estimates for sufficiently large
\lambda
.

First, we prove (
A.1
) for
\alpha=0
. Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1/2
and
{\chi(x)=0}
for
|x|\geq 1
. Define
\chi_{\lambda}(x)=\chi(\lambda x)
and
\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x)=1-\chi_{\lambda}(x)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(x)\chi_{\lambda}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1/\lambda}x^{\mu}{\rm d}x\lesssim{\lambda}^{-\mu-1}.

(A.2)

On the other hand, the integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|=\lambda^{-N}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{N}(\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x)){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|,

where we define
L=\partial_{x}\circ({\rm i}f^{\prime}(x))^{-1}
. By using Leibniz’s rule, Lemma
A.1
and the assumption
f^{\prime}\neq 0
on
\supp\gamma
, we obtain
|L^{N}(\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x))|\lesssim|x|^{\mu-N}
. Hence, for
N>\mu+1
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\int_{\frac{1}{2\lambda}}^{10}x^{\mu-N}{\rm d}x\lesssim\lambda^{-\mu-1}.

(A.3)

The inequalities (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) imply (
A.1
) for
\alpha=0
.

To deal with the case
\alpha\geq 1
, we write

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}^{\alpha}b_{\mu}(\lambda)={\rm i}^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\lambda f(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(f(x)-f(0))^{\alpha}\gamma(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x.

By Taylor’s theorem, we have
\partial_{x}^{N}(\gamma(x)(f(x)-f(0))^{\alpha})=O\bigl(|x|^{\mu+\alpha-N}\bigr)
for
x\in\supp\gamma
. Moreover,
\big|{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\lambda f(0)}\big|=1
due to the assumption
f(0)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence the same argument as the case
\alpha=0
gives (
A.1
) for
\alpha\geq 1
.
∎

A.3
Stationary phase theorem

In this paper, we use the following versions of the stationary phase theorem (the van der Corput lemma). The proof is same as the proof of
[
27
, Lemma 1.1.2]
(see also in
[
28
, p. 334]
). The statement about uniformity in a parameter follows from its proof and Lemma
A.1
. We consider an integral

\displaystyle I(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\qquad\text{for}\quad\lambda\gtrsim 1.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
j\geq 0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{x_{0}\})\cap C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})}
such that
|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\gamma(x)|\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{j-\alpha}
,
|f(x)|\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{k}
and
|f^{\prime}(x)|\gtrsim|x-x_{0}|^{k-1}
for
x\in\supp\gamma\setminus\{x_{0}\}
.
Then
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{j+1}{k}}
for
\lambda\gtrsim 1
.

As a result, we also obtain the following version.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\cap C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
.

(i)

Let
\nu\geq 0
. Suppose that
|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\gamma(x)|\lesssim|x|^{\nu-\beta}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\neq 0
for
x\in\supp\gamma
. Then
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
\lambda\gtrsim 1
.

(ii)

Suppose that
\big|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\gamma(x)\big|\lesssim|x|^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
. If
\supp\gamma
is sufficiently close to
0
, then
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
\lambda\gtrsim 1
.

Remark A.6
.

(1)

The main difference between Lemma
A.4
and this lemma is that we do not assume
f^{\prime}(0)=0
in (i) for example.

(2)

The estimates in (i) and (ii) are optimal. If in addition we assume that
\supp\gamma
is close to
0
for the case (i), then the optimal estimate would be
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+\nu)}
.

Proof of Lemma
A.5
.

The proof of the part (i) is almost same as the case (ii). Thus we only deal with the case (ii).

(ii)
We may assume
f(0)=0
since
\big|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(0)}\big|=1
. Moreover, we may assume that
a:=f^{\prime\prime}(0)
is sufficiently small since our result directly follows from
[
28
, p.334, Corollary]
for
a
away from
0
. For simplicity, we also assume
a>0
and
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)\geq 8
.

First, we consider the case where
a^{3}\lambda\ll 1
. We write
f^{\prime}(x)=ax+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{2}x^{2}+g(x)
with
{g(x)=O\bigl(|x|^{3}\bigr)}
. Then we can take
\supp\gamma
small enough such that
|f^{\prime}(x)|\gtrsim|x|^{2}
for
x\in\supp\gamma
with
|x|\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}
due to the assumptions
a^{3}\lambda\ll 1
and
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
.
Take
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((-2,2);[0,1])
such that
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and set
\chi_{\lambda}(x)=\chi\bigl(\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}}xv)
and
\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}=1-\chi_{\lambda}
. Then

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(x)\chi_{\lambda}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|\lesssim\int_{|x|\leq 2\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}}|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}(1+\frac{1}{2})}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, the integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda f(x)}{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\left(f^{\prime}(x)^{-1}\gamma(x)\overline{\chi_{\lambda}}(x)\right)|{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{|x|\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}}|x|^{-\frac{5}{2}}{\rm d}x\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus we obtain
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Next we consider the case where
a^{3}\lambda\gtrsim 1
. Taking
\supp\gamma
sufficiently close to
0
, we may assume the following three conditions hold: Critical points of
f
(on
\supp\gamma
) are
x=0
or
x=x_{0}(a)
with
{|x_{0}(a)|\in\bigl[\frac{a}{4},\frac{3}{4}a\bigr]}

(
due to the factorization
f^{\prime}(x)=x\bigl(a+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{2}x+h(x)\bigr)
with
h(x)=O\bigl(x^{2}\bigr)
)
. The second condition is
|f^{\prime\prime}(x_{0}(a))|\gtrsim|a|
. The third condition is
|f^{\prime}(x)|\gtrsim|ax|
for
|x|\geq a
.

By scaling, we have

\displaystyle I(\lambda)=a^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(x){\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda a^{3}f_{a}(x)}{\rm d}x,\qquad f_{a}(x):=a^{-3}f(ax),\qquad\gamma_{a}(x):=a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\gamma(ax).

(A.4)

We observe
f_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{6}x^{3}+O\bigl(a|x|^{4}\bigr)
and
\big|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\gamma_{a}(x)\big|\lesssim|x|^{\frac{1}{2}-\beta}
. Moreover, critical points of
f_{a}
are
x=0
or
x=a^{-1}x_{0}(a)
with
\big|a^{-1}x_{0}(a)\big|\in\bigl[\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4}\bigr]
,
|f_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)|\gtrsim 1
and
|f_{a}^{\prime}(x)|\gtrsim|x|
for
|x|\geq 1
. Then we divide the integral (
A.4
) into three parts
I_{1}(\lambda)
,
I_{2}(\lambda)
,
I_{3}(\lambda)
, where the integrand of
I_{j}(\lambda)
is supported close to
0
for
j=1
,
a^{-1}x_{0}(a)^{-1}
for
j=2
and
|f_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)|\gtrsim 1
on the supports of the integrands of
I_{1}(\lambda)
and
I_{2}(\lambda)
for sufficiently small
a
. Then Lemma
A.4
with the large parameter
\lambda a^{3}
gives
|I_{1}(\lambda)|\lesssim a^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigl(\lambda a^{3}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{2})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
|I_{2}(\lambda)|\lesssim a^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigl(\lambda a^{3}\bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
(for the latter case, we use
f^{\prime}(x(a))=0
and use this lemma for the phase function
{f(x)-f(x(a))}
). Moreover, integrating by parts many times, we have
|I_{3}(\lambda)|\lesssim a^{\frac{3}{2}}\bigl(\lambda a^{3}\bigr)^{-N}
for all
N>0
. Taking
{N=\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain
|I_{3}(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and hence
|I(\lambda)|\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

A.4
Asymptotics of the Bessel function

In this appendix, we prove Proposition
3.1
.
First, we seek a nice integral representation approximating the Bessel function
J_{\mu}(y)
.

Lemma A.7
.

There exists
\chi_{1}\in C_{c}^{\infty}\bigl(\bigl(-\frac{3\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}\bigr);[0,1]\bigr)
such that
\chi_{1}(w)=1
for
|w|\leq\frac{2\pi}{3}
and
{\chi_{1}(w)=\chi_{1}(-w)}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(y\sin w-\mu w)}\chi_{1}(w){\rm d}w+R_{0}(\mu,y)\qquad\text{for}\quad\mu\geq 0,\qquad y\geq 1,

(A.5)

where
R_{0}(\mu,y)
satisfies
|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}R_{0}(\mu,y)|\leq C_{N\alpha}(1+y+\mu)^{-N}
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

It is known (see
[
33
, Section 6.21 (3)]
, see also
[
17
, equation (9.7)]
) that the Hankel function can be written as

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{\pi{\rm i}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}+\int_{0}^{\pi{\rm i}}+\int_{\pi{\rm i}}^{\infty+\pi{\rm i}}\right){\rm e}^{y\sinh w-\mu w}{\rm d}w

(A.6)

and
\re H_{\mu}(y)=J_{\mu}(y)
. Since the curve
[-\infty,0]\cup[0,\pi{\rm i}]\cup[\pi{\rm i},\infty+\pi{\rm i}]
is not smooth, we will deform it to remove the corner at
\pi{\rm i}
by using the Cauchy integral formula, where we note that the corner at
0
is not involved with the asymptotics of
J_{\nu}(y)
.
More precisely, we take a curve
{\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}}
such that
\gamma
is smooth and
|\gamma^{\prime}(r)|=1
except at
0
, and

\displaystyle\gamma(r)=\begin{cases}r&\text{for}\quad r\leq 0,\\
{\rm i}r&\text{for}\quad 0\leq r\leq\frac{3}{4}\pi,\\
r-\frac{2}{3}\pi-L+{\rm i}\pi&\text{for}\quad r\geq\frac{3}{4}\pi+L,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\re\gamma(r)\geq 0,\\
\im\gamma(r)\in\bigl[\frac{2\pi}{3},\pi\bigr],\end{cases}\qquad\text{for}\quad\frac{2}{3}\pi\leq r\leq\frac{3}{4}\pi+2L

with a constant
L>0
. Since
\re(y\sinh w-\mu w)=y\sinh(\re w)\cos(\im w)-\mu\re w
and
y,\mu\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\re(y\sinh w-\mu w)|_{w=\gamma(r)}\leq 0\qquad\text{for}\quad r\geq 0.

(A.7)

Now we take
\chi_{1}\in C_{c}^{\infty}\bigl(\bigl(-\frac{3\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}\bigr);[0,1]\bigr)
,
\chi_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}\bigl(\bigl[\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{3\pi}{4}+2L\bigr];[0,1]\bigr)
and
\chi_{3}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\chi_{3}=1
on
[0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(r)=\chi_{1}(-r),\qquad\chi_{1}(r)=1\qquad\text{for}\quad|r|\leq\frac{2\pi}{3},

\displaystyle\chi_{3}(r)=\begin{cases}0&\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{3}{4}\pi+L,\\
1&\text{for}\quad r\geq\frac{3}{4}\pi+2L.\end{cases}

By the Cauchy’s integral formula, we rewrite (
A.6
) as

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}{\rm e}^{y\sinh w-\mu w}{\rm d}w+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(y\sin w-\mu w)}\chi_{1}(w){\rm d}w+\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y),

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y)=\frac{1}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{y\sinh\gamma(r)-\mu\gamma(r)}\chi_{2}(r)\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(r){\rm d}r

\displaystyle\phantom{\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y)=}{}+\frac{{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\pi\mu}}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{-y\sinh w-\mu w}\chi_{3}\left(w+\frac{2}{3}\pi+L\right){\rm d}w.

Since the first term is purely imaginary, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(y)=\re H_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(y\sin w-\mu w)}\chi_{1}(w){\rm d}w+\re\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y),

where we use
\chi_{1}(r)=\chi_{1}(-r)
,
\supp\chi_{1}\subset\bigl(-\frac{3}{4}\pi,\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
and
y\sin(-w)-\mu(-w)=-(y\sin w-\mu w)
. Now we set
R_{0}(\mu,y):=\re\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y)
.
It suffices to prove
|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y)|\leq C_{N\alpha}(1+y+\mu)^{-N}
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu\geq 0
and
y\geq 1
.

We note that
|\partial_{r}(y\sinh\gamma(r)-\mu\gamma(r))|\geq|y\cosh(\re\gamma(r))\cos(\im\gamma(r))-\mu|\gtrsim y+\mu
for
r\in\supp\chi_{2}
, where we use
|\gamma^{\prime}(r)|=1
and
\re\gamma\geq 0
,
\im\gamma\in[\frac{2}{3}\pi,\pi]
on
\supp\chi_{2}
. Moreover,
|\partial_{w}(-y\sinh w-\mu w)|=y\cosh w+\mu\gtrsim y+\mu
. Thus, the integration by parts with (
A.7
) yields
{\big|\partial_{\mu}^{\alpha}\tilde{R}_{0}(\mu,y)\big|\leq C_{N\alpha}(1+y+\mu)^{-N}}
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu\geq 0
and
y\geq 1
.
∎

Remark A.8
.

This lemma might be proved also by integration by parts and by Schläfli’s formula

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(y(\sin w)-\mu w){\rm d}w-\frac{\sin(\pi\mu)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-y(\sinh w)-\mu w}{\rm d}w,

A.5
Asymptotics of the Beta function

For
m\in[1,\infty)
and
\nu>0
, we define

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m):=\nu^{-1}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu)}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(m+2\nu)}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(2\nu)}.

(A.15)

By
[
2
, Fact 4.8, equation (4.30)]
, we have
C_{m}^{\nu}(1)=\frac{\Gamma(m+2\nu)}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(2\nu)}
and hence

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m)=\nu^{-1}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)C_{m}^{\nu}(1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu)}\qquad\text{for}\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

(A.16)

We denote the Beta function by
B(x,y)
. Then it is known that
B(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}
. We may write

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m)=\nu^{-1}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\nu)}\cdot\frac{1}{mB(m,2\nu)}.

(A.17)

Proposition A.10
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
be an integer and
\nu>0
. Then there exists
C_{\nu,\alpha}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}B(m,2\nu)|\leq C_{\nu,\alpha}m^{-2\nu-\alpha},\qquad|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}F_{\nu,1}(m)|\leq C_{\nu,\alpha}m^{2\nu-1-\alpha}

for all
m\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

First, we note that it suffices to prove these estimates for sufficiently large
m
. As is well known (essentially due to Stirling’s formula), we have
B(m,2\nu)\sim\Gamma(2\nu)m^{-2\nu}
as
m\to\infty
.
This implies that the estimates for
F_{\nu,1}(m)
follow from the estimates for
B(m,2\nu)
and (
A.17
). The case
\alpha=0
follows from
B(m,2\nu)\sim\Gamma(2\nu)m^{-2\nu}
as
m\to\infty
. Hence, in the following, we consider the estimates for
\alpha\geq 1
.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
satisfy
\psi(t)=1
for
t\geq 1/2
and
\psi(t)=0
for
t\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Since
B(m,2\nu)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}(1-t)^{2\nu-1}{\rm d}t
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{m}^{\alpha}B(m,2\nu)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}g_{\alpha,1}(t){\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}g_{\alpha,2}(t){\rm d}t,

where we set
g_{\alpha,1}(t)=(\log t)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{2\nu-1}\psi(t)
and
g_{\alpha,2}(t)=(\log t)^{\alpha}(1-t)^{2\nu-1}(1-\psi(t))

First, we deal with the second term. Since
\supp g_{\alpha,2}\subset\{t\leq 1/2\}
and since

for
0<t\leq 1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}g_{\alpha,2}(t){\rm d}t\right|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{m-2}{\rm d}t\lesssim 2^{-m}\lesssim m^{-2\nu-\alpha}

for sufficiently large

.

Next, we consider the first term. We note

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{t}^{\beta}g_{\alpha,1}(t)\big|\leq C_{\alpha}|1-t|^{2\nu-1+\alpha-\beta}\qquad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,1].

Now we write

\displaystyle t^{m-1}={\rm e}^{(m-1)(\log t)}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}(m-1)\tilde{f}(t)},\qquad\tilde{f}(t)=-{\rm i}\log t.

Then
\tilde{f}^{\prime}(t)=-{\rm i}/t\neq 0
and
\im\tilde{f}(t)\geq 0
hold for
t\in\supp\psi\cap(-\infty,1]
.
Now it follows from Lemma
A.3
with
\lambda=m
,
\mu=2\nu-1
and the change of variable
x=1-t
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}t^{m-1}g_{\alpha,1}(t){\rm d}t\right|\lesssim m^{-2\nu-\alpha}

holds for sufficiently large
m\geq 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

A.6
Asymptotics of the Gegenbauer polynomials

In this appendix, we prove Proposition
3.3
using non-stationary phase theorem. The case
{\nu=0}
is easy to deal with (see the proof of Proposition
3.3
below) and hence we focus on the case
{\nu>0}
.

By
[
2
, equation (4.30)]
(see also
[
17
, Section 10]
) and (
A.16
),

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)=

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m)\int_{-1}^{1}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u)^{m}\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}{\rm d}u

(A.18)

for
\nu>0
,
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
, where
F_{\nu,1}
is defined in (
A.15
). To get an asymptotics of
C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)
, we extend
\mathbb{N}^{*}\ni m\mapsto C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)
to a function on
m\in[1,\infty)
(up to a negligible term). However, since the integrant
(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u)^{m}
is not a single-valued function, we have to do it a bit carefully.

First, we deal with the case
\varphi\in\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
.
We consider two branches of logarithmic smooth functions
\log^{+}z\colon\mathbb{C}\setminus\{{\rm i}y\mid y\leq 0\}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\log^{-}z\colon\mathbb{C}\setminus\{{\rm i}y\mid y\geq 0\}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\log^{+}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}\sin\varphi)={\rm i}\varphi
and
\log^{-}(\cos\varphi-{\rm i}\sin\varphi))=-{\rm i}\varphi
for all
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
. We define smooth functions
f_{\pm}
by

\displaystyle f_{\pm}(\varphi,u)=-{\rm i}\log^{\pm}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u)\mp\varphi

for
(\varphi,u)
such that
\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u
belongs to the domain of
\log^{\pm}
. We note that

([0,\pi]\times\mathbb{R}_{\pm})\cup\bigl(\bigl(v[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]\bigr)\times\bigl[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]\bigr)
is included in the domain of
f_{\pm}
.
Then
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{\pm}(\varphi,u)}=(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u)^{m}{\rm e}^{\mp{\rm i}m\varphi}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\pm u>0
.
Now let
\chi_{\pm},\chi_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\chi_{+}+\chi_{0}+\chi_{-}=1
on
\mathbb{R}
,
\supp\chi_{0}\subset\bigl[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]
,
\supp\chi_{+}\subset\bigl[\frac{1}{4},\infty\bigr)
and
\supp\chi_{-}\subset\bigl(-\infty,-\frac{1}{4}\bigr]
. We define

\displaystyle H_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi):=\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{\pm}(\varphi,u)}\chi_{\pm}(u)\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}{\rm d}u,

\displaystyle E_{\nu}(m,\varphi):=\int_{-1}^{1}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u)^{m}\chi_{0}(u)\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}{\rm d}u,

where
H_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi)
is defined for all
m\in[1,\infty)
although
E_{\nu}(m,\varphi)
is defined only for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
whenever
\varphi\in\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
(note that
\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u
may be zero for
u\in\supp\chi_{0}
). By these construction and (
A.18
), we have

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)=

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m)\bigl({\rm e}^{{\rm i}m\varphi}H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)+{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}m\varphi}H_{\nu,-}(m,\varphi)+E_{\nu}(m,\varphi)\bigr)

(A.19)

for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varphi\in\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
.

Next, we consider the case
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
. We define

\displaystyle E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi):=\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{+}(\varphi,u)}\chi_{0}(u)\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}{\rm d}u,\qquad m\in[1,\infty),

\displaystyle\varphi\in\left[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\right]\cup\left[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\right],

where we note that
\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{{\rm i}y\mid y\leq 0\}
whenever
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
and
u\in\supp\chi_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\nu^{-1}C_{m}^{\nu}(\cos\varphi)=

\displaystyle F_{\nu,1}(m)\bigl({\rm e}^{{\rm i}m\varphi}H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)+{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}m\varphi}H_{\nu,-}(m,\varphi)+{\rm e}^{{\rm i}m\varphi}E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)\bigr)

(A.20)

holds for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
.

Now we show that
H_{\nu,\pm}
,
E_{\nu}
and
E_{\nu}^{\prime}
satisfy symbol-type estimates. The basic idea of the proof for
H_{\nu,\pm}
and
E_{\nu}
is to use the non-stationary phase theorem with a singular amplitude (see Lemma
A.3
). To do this, we observe that our phase function
f_{\pm}(\varphi,u)
satisfies
|f_{\pm}^{\prime}(\varphi,u)|\gtrsim|\sin\varphi|
on
{u\in\supp\chi_{\pm}}
(see the proof below). Although
\partial_{u}f_{\pm}(\varphi,u)=0
when
\varphi=0,\pi
, we consider a large parameter
\lambda=m\sin\varphi
rather than
m
and can apply Lemma
A.3
.

Lemma A.11
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
be an integer and
\nu,N>0
.

(i)

There exists
C_{N,\nu}>0
such that
|E_{\nu}(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{N,\nu}m^{-N}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varphi\in\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
.

(ii)

There exists
C_{\alpha,N,\nu}>0
such that
|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha,N,\nu}m^{-\alpha}(1+m\sin\varphi)^{-N}
for
m\in[1,\infty)
and
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
.

(iii)

There exists
C_{\alpha,\nu}>0
such that
|\partial_{m}^{\alpha}H_{\nu,\pm}(m,\varphi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\nu}m^{-\alpha}(1+m\sin\varphi)^{-\nu}
for
m\in[1,\infty)
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
|\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u|\leq\sqrt{\frac{5}{8}}
for
u\in\supp\chi_{0}
and
\varphi\in\bigl(\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3}{4}\pi\bigr)
, we have

|\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)u|^{m}\leq\left(\frac{5}{8}\right)^{\frac{m}{2}}\lesssim m^{-N}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The estimate for
E_{\nu}
directly follows from this inequality.

(ii)
Setting
\psi(u):=\chi_{0}(u)\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}
, we write
E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{+}(\varphi,u)}\psi(u){\rm d}u
.

First, we consider the case
\alpha=0
by using the integration by parts.
Since
\log^{+}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}\sin\varphi)={\rm i}\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle f_{+}(\varphi,u)={\rm i}\int_{u}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\log^{+}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)r){\rm d}r=-\int_{u}^{1}\frac{\sin\varphi(\cos\varphi-{\rm i}(\sin\varphi)r)}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r

(A.21)

and hence
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)=\int_{u}^{1}\frac{(\sin^{2}\varphi)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r
. This implies
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)\geq 0
for
u\in[-1,1]
. In fact, this is trivial for
u\geq 0
. For
u\leq 0
, we write

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{1}\frac{\bigl(\sin^{2}\varphi\bigr)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bigl(\sin^{2}\varphi\bigr)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r+\int_{u}^{0}\frac{\bigl(\sin^{2}\varphi\bigr)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{-u}^{1}\frac{\bigl(\sin^{2}\varphi\bigr)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r,

which is non-negative.

Now we set
L=\partial_{u}\circ({\rm i}\partial_{u}f_{+}(\varphi,u))^{-1}
. By integrating by parts, we have

|E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)|=m^{-N}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{+}(\varphi,u)}L^{N}\psi(u){\rm d}u\right|\leq m^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|L^{N}\psi(u)\big|{\rm d}u

by virtue of
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)\geq 0
. Since
\partial_{u}^{\alpha}f_{+}(\varphi,u)=(-1)^{\alpha-1}(\alpha-1)!(\sin\varphi)^{\alpha}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}u\sin\varphi)^{-\alpha}
, for each
\alpha\geq 2
, we obtain
|\partial_{u}f_{+}(\varphi,u)|^{-1}\lesssim|\sin\varphi|^{-1}
and
|\partial_{u}^{\alpha}f_{+}(\varphi,u)||\partial_{u}f_{+}(\varphi,u)|^{-1}\lesssim 1
for
u\in[-1,1]
and
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
. By Lemma
A.1
, we conclude
\big|L^{N}\psi(u)\big|\lesssim|\sin\varphi|^{-N}
and hence
|E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)|\lesssim m^{-N}|\sin\varphi|^{-N}
for
\varphi\in\bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{4}\bigr]\cup\bigl[\frac{3}{4}\pi,\pi\bigr]
(we note that
\psi
is compactly supported). On the other hand, the inequality
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)\geq 0
also implies
|E_{\nu}^{\prime}(m,\varphi)|\lesssim 1
. Combining these estimates, we have proved (ii) for
\alpha=0
.

Next, we consider the case
\alpha\geq 1
. We note

\displaystyle\partial_{m}^{\alpha}E^{\prime}_{\nu}(m,\varphi)={\rm i}^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}mf_{+}(\varphi,u)}f_{+}(\varphi,u)^{\alpha}\psi(u)du.

As in the case of
\alpha=0
, we can deduce
\big|L^{N}(f_{+}(\varphi,u)^{\alpha}\psi(u))\big|\lesssim|{\sin\varphi}|^{\alpha-N}
, which leads to the part (ii) for
\alpha\geq 1
since
f_{+}(0,u)=f_{+}(\pi,u)=0
.

(iii)
We deal with the case
+
only. There exists
c>0
such that
|{\cos\varphi+{\rm i}u\sin\varphi}|\geq c
for
{u\in\supp\chi_{+}}
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
.
Since
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)=\int_{u}^{1}\frac{(\sin^{2}\varphi)r}{\cos^{2}\varphi+r^{2}\sin^{2}\varphi}{\rm d}r
, which is proved above, we have
\im f_{+}(\varphi,u)\geq 0
for
u\in\supp\chi_{+}
and
\varphi\in[0,\pi]
. Moreover, we have
f_{+}(\varphi,1)=0
.

Now we prove
(iii)
for
\alpha=0
. Set
m^{\prime}=m\sin\varphi
and
g(\varphi,u)=(\sin\varphi)^{-1}f_{+}(\varphi,u)
. Then we write
H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)=\int_{-1}^{1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}m^{\prime}g(\varphi,u)}\chi_{+}(u)\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}{\rm d}u
. Since

\partial_{u}^{\alpha}g(\varphi,u)=(-1)^{\alpha-1}(\alpha-1)!(\sin\varphi)^{\alpha-1}(\cos\varphi+{\rm i}u\sin\varphi)^{-\alpha}

for
\alpha\geq 1
and since

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{u}g(\varphi,u)|\geq c,\qquad|\partial_{u}^{\alpha}g(\varphi,u)||\partial_{u}g(\varphi,u)|^{-1}\leq C_{\alpha}^{\prime}

with a constant
c>0
and
C_{\alpha}^{\prime}>0
. By Lemma
A.3
with
\lambda=m\sin\varphi
and the change of variable
{x=1-u}

|H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)|\lesssim(m^{\prime})^{-\nu}
for

. On the other hand, it follows from

that

. Combining these estimates, we obtain
{|H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)|\lesssim(1+m^{\prime})^{-\nu}=(1+m\sin\varphi)^{-\nu}}
for
m\sin\varphi\geq 1
. For

Finally, we consider the case

. We observe that

\displaystyle\partial_{m}^{\alpha}H_{\nu,+}(m,\varphi)=i^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}m^{\prime}g(\varphi,u)}f_{+}(\varphi,u)^{\alpha}\bigl(1-u^{2}\bigr)^{\nu-1}\chi_{+}(u){\rm d}u

Appendix B
Finite time Strichartz estimates

Here, we show that the Strichartz estimates hold for finite time
T
assuming that they hold for each time
t
with
|t|\leq T_{0}
.
The following argument is more or less well known.
For simplicity, assuming
T_{0}<T<2T_{0}
, (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) hold for
T_{0}
and
T-T_{0}
, we shall show that (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) hold for
T
.

Set
U(t)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}tH_{k,a}}
.
Since
U(t+s)=U(t)U(s)
,
[-T,T]=[-T_{0},T_{0}]\cup[-T,-T_{0}]\cup[T_{0},T]
and
{T<2T_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\|U(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([-T,T];L^{q})}^{p}={}

\displaystyle\|U(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([-T_{0},T_{0}];L^{q})}^{p}+\|U(t)U(-T_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([-T+T_{0},0];L^{q})}^{p}

\displaystyle+\|U(t)U(T_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T-T_{0}];L^{q})}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\|U(-T_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\|U(T_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p}=3\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{p},

where we use the fact that
U(t)
is unitary in the last line. This proves (
1.3
) for
T
.

Set
\Gamma f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}U(t-s)f(s){\rm d}s
.
To see that (
1.4
) holds for
T
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\|\Gamma f\|_{L^{p_{1}}([-T,-T_{0}]\cup[T_{0},T];L^{q_{2}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([-T,T];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}.

(B.1)

We firstly observe

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U(-s)f(s){\rm d}s\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([0,T_{0}];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}

(B.2)

due to the duality of
\|U(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([0,T_{0}];L^{q})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
which in turn follows from (
1.3
) for
T_{0}
. Setting
\Gamma_{1}f(t)=\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U(t-s)f(s){\rm d}s
and
\Gamma_{2}f(t)=\int_{T_{0}}^{t}U(t-s)f(s){\rm d}s
, we have
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{2}
. Since
\Gamma_{1}f(t)=U(t)\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U(-s)f(s){\rm d}s
, the homogeneous estimate
\|U(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{p}([-T,T];L^{q})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
implies

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{1}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}([T_{0},T];L^{q_{2}})}\lesssim\left\|\int_{0}^{T_{0}}U(-s)f(s){\rm d}s\right\|_{L^{2}}\underbrace{\lesssim}_{\eqref{apphomdual}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([0,T_{0}];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}.

On the other hand, setting
g(t)=f(t+T_{0})
, we have
\Gamma_{2}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t-T_{0}}U(t-T_{0}-s)g(s){\rm d}s
. Using (
1.4
) for
T-T_{0}(<T_{0})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{2}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}([T_{0},T];L^{q_{1}})}=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}U(t-s)g(s){\rm d}s\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}([0,T-T_{0}];L^{q_{1}})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([-T+T_{0},T-T_{0}];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}.

Combining them, we conclude
\|\Gamma f\|_{L^{p_{1}}([T_{0},T];L^{q_{1}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([-T,T];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma f\|_{L^{p_{1}}([-T,-T_{0}];L^{q_{1}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}^{*}}([-T,T];L^{q_{2}^{*}})}.

Thus we have proved (
B.1
).

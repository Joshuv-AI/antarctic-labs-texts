On the image of an affine subspace under the inverse function within a finite field
N. Kolomeec, D. Bykov

Abstract
We consider the function x − 1 x^{-1} that inverses a finite field element x ∈ 𝔽 p n x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} ( p p is prime, 0 − 1 = 0 0^{-1}=0 ) and affine 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -subspaces of 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} such that their images are affine subspaces as well. It is proven that the image of an affine subspace L L , | L | > 2 |L|>2 , is an affine subspace if and only if L = q ​ 𝔽 p k L=q\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}} , where q ∈ 𝔽 p n ∗ q\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*} and k | n k\mid n . In other words, it is either a subfield of 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} or a subspace consisting of all elements of a subfield multiplied by q q . This generalizes the results that were obtained for linear invariant subspaces in 2006. As a consequence, we propose a sufficient condition providing that a function A ⁡ ( x − 1 ) + b A(x^{-1})+b has no invariant affine subspaces U U of cardinality 2 < | U | < p n 2<|U|<p^{n} for an invertible linear transformation A : 𝔽 p n → 𝔽 p n A:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} and b ∈ 𝔽 p n ∗ b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*} . As an example, it is shown that the condition works for S-box of AES. Also, we demonstrate that some functions of the form α ​ x − 1 + b \alpha x^{-1}+b have no invariant affine subspaces except for 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} , where α , b ∈ 𝔽 p n ∗ \alpha,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*} and n n is arbitrary.

Introduction

In this work we consider affine subspaces whose images under the function that inverses an element of a finite field are affine subspaces as well.
We denote this function by
\sigma
, i.е.
\sigma:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
such that

\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}x^{-1},&\text{if }x\neq 0\\
0,&\text{if }x=0\end{cases},

where
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is
a finite field
consisting of
p^{n}
elements. Hereinafter
p
is a prime number.

A linear subspace

L
of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is its linear
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace. In other words,
L
is an additive subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
We denote an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
by
a+L
. For any
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
,
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
f:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
we define

a+S=\{a+s:s\in S\},\ aS=\{as:s\in S\}\text{ and }f(S)=\{f(s):s\in S\}.

It is known that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
if and only if
k\mid n
. In this case we assume that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Also,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
.
According to the denotations above, we consider affine subspaces
U
of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
such that
\sigma(U)
are affine subspaces as well. We are interested in
nontrivial
subspaces that satisfy
2<|U|<p^{n}
since any set
S
of cardinality
1
,
2
(for
p=2
) or
p^{n}
is always an affine subspace.

These subspaces are connected with invariant subspaces. If we know all
U
, we can construct all invariant affine subspaces for a function that is affinely equivalent to
\sigma
.
Recall that
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is
an invariant set
of
f
if
f(S)\subseteq S
. Functions
f
and
g
are
affinely equivalent
if and only if
g(x)=A(f(B(x)+b))+a
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, where
A
and
B
are invertible linear transformations,
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Similarly to subspaces, we consider linear transformations assuming that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

The function
\sigma
is interesting for cryptography. For instance, S-box of AES
[
8
,
11
]
is based on
\sigma
. Its invariant linear subspaces were used to investigate group properties of the AES-like round functions
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
An attack using an invariant subspace of several rounds of ciphering was introduced in
[
9
]
. A more general attack which can use an invariant set is also known
[
17
]
.
Note that invariant sets for both of them may be constructed starting with S-boxes, see, for instance,
[
5
,
7
]
.
Note that D. Goldstein et al. and S. Mattarei proved that all invariant linear subspaces of the function
\sigma
are subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, see
[
6
,
16
]
. Thus, we generalize this result in two directions: for affine subspaces and for functions that are affinely equivalent to
\sigma
.

At the same time, if
f(U)
is not an affine subspace for any
2
-dimensional affine subspace
U
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, then
f
is an APN function
[
12
]
. Moreover, only APN functions satisfy this property.
They are very important for cryptography. Also, there are many open questions in this area, see, for instance,
[
4
]
. If
p=2
and
n
is odd, then
\sigma
is an APN function.

Our contribution is the following. In Section
2
, we prove that both
U
and
\sigma(U)
,
|U|>2
, are affine subspaces if and only if
U=q\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, where
q\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
and
k\mid n
(Theorem

). Next, we show how to apply this to construct a function of the form
A(\sigma(x))+b
without invariant affine subspaces, where
A
is an invertible linear transformation and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
. Section
3
contains a sufficient condition for such functions to have no nontrivial invariant affine subspaces (Theorem
3
).
Also, for the function
\alpha\sigma(x)+b
, where
\alpha,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
, we provide a criterion on the existence of invariant affine subspaces excluding the whole
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
(Theorem
4
). Section
4
demonstrates that the results obtained can theoretically prove that there are no nontrivial invariant affine subspaces for S-box of AES.

2
Subspaces that are mapped to subspaces

In this section we find all nontrivial affine subspaces
U
such that
\sigma(U)
is an affine subspace as well.
We start with the following well known property of linear subspaces.

Lemma 1
.

Let
L
be a linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
|L|>2
. Then
\sum_{x\in L}x=0
.

Proof.

Let
s=\sum_{x\in L}x
,
s\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Let
p>2
. Since
(p-1)L=L
, it holds

s=\sum_{x\in L}x=\sum_{x\in L}(p-1)x=(p-1)s.

But
p-1\neq 1
. This implies that
s=0
.

Let
p=2
. Then
\dim L\geq 2
and
L=L^{\prime}\cup(a+L^{\prime})
, where
L^{\prime}
is a
(\dim L-1)
-dimensional linear subspace of
L
,
a\in L
. Therefore,

s=\sum_{x\in L}x=\sum_{x\in L^{\prime}}x+\sum_{x\in L^{\prime}}(a+x)=2^{\dim L-1}a=0.

The lemma is proven.
∎

Actually, it is enough to consider only linear subspaces.

Theorem 1
.

Let
U
be an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
,
|U|>2
and
\sigma(U)
be an affine subspace as well. Then
U
is a linear subspace.

Proof.

Let
U=a+L
and
\sigma(U)=U^{\prime}=a^{\prime}+L^{\prime}
, where
L
and
L^{\prime}
are linear subspaces,
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

First of all, we prove some property of
U
and
U^{\prime}
. Taking into account that
|U|>2
, Lemma
1
provides that

\sum_{y\in\sigma(U)}y=\sum_{y\in L^{\prime}}(a^{\prime}+y)=p^{\dim U}a^{\prime}+\sum_{y\in L^{\prime}}y=0+0=0.

Since
\sigma(U)=\{\sigma(x+s):s\in L\}
for any
x\in U
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in L}\sigma(x+s)=0\text{ for any }x\in U.

(1)

Let us define the following polynomial
h
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
:

\displaystyle h(y)=\sum_{s\in L}\prod_{t\in L\setminus\{s\}}(y+t).

Now we prove by contradiction that
U
is a linear subspace. Suppose that
U
is not a linear subspace. Let
x
be any element of
U
. Therefore,
x\neq s
for any
s\in L
. At the same time,
x+s\neq 0
since
-s\in L
as well. As a result,

\displaystyle h(x)=\sum_{s\in L}\prod_{t\in L\setminus\{s\}}(x+t)=\prod_{t\in L}(x+t)\sum_{s\in L}\sigma(x+s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:solutionsFromU})}}{{=}}0.

Thus, any
x\in U
is a root of
h
. However, the degree of
h
is less than
|U|=|L|
. Also,
h\not\equiv 0
since

h(0)=\prod_{t\in L\setminus\{0\}}(0+t)=\prod_{t\in L\setminus\{0\}}t\neq 0.

In other words,
h
cannot have
|U|
roots. This is a contradiction. Hence,
U
is a linear subspace.
The theorem is proven.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
L
and
\sigma(L)
be linear subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then
a^{2}\sigma(b)\in L
for any
a,b\in L
.

Proof.

Hua’s identity

a-(a^{-1}+(b^{\prime-1}-a)^{-1})^{-1}=ab^{\prime}a

holds for any
a,b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
,
a,b^{\prime}\neq 0,ab^{\prime}\neq 1
. Note that it works not only for fields and can be easily proven.

Let
b^{\prime}=\sigma(b)
.
It is clear that
a^{2}\sigma(b)\in L
if
a=0
,
b^{\prime}=0
or
ab^{\prime}=1
. Next, we consider other cases. It means that
b^{\prime}=b^{-1}
. Since
\sigma(\sigma(L))=L
and both
L
and
\sigma(L)
are linear subspaces, the identity provides that

\displaystyle b-a=b^{\prime-1}-a

\displaystyle\in L,

\displaystyle a^{-1}+(b-a)^{-1}

\displaystyle\in\sigma(L),

\displaystyle a-(a^{-1}+(b-a)^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle\in L.

Hence,
ab^{\prime}a=a^{2}\sigma(b)\in L
as well.
The lemma is proven.
∎

The following theorem describes all considered subspaces.

Theorem 2
.

Let
U
be an affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
|U|>2
. Then
\sigma(U)
is an affine subspace if and only if
U=q\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, where
k\mid n
and
q\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
.

Proof.

First of all,
\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})=\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
for
k\mid n
since it is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Also,
\sigma(q\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})=q^{-1}\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}})
which is a linear subspace. The sufficient condition is proven.

Next, we prove the necessary condition. Let
L=U
and
\sigma(L)
be an affine subspace. By Theorem
1
,
L
is a linear subspace. In fact,
\sigma(L)
is linear as well since
0\in L
and
\sigma(0)=0
. Without loss of generality we assume that
1\in L
since we can consider any
qL
instead of
L
,
q\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
. Indeed,
\sigma(qL)
is a linear subspace since
\sigma(qL)=q^{-1}\sigma(L)
. In light of that, we need only to prove that
L
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

According to Lemma
2
,
a^{2}\sigma(b)\in L
for any
a,b\in L
. But
1\in L
. It means that
\sigma(b)\in L
and
a^{2}\in L
. As a consequence,
L=\sigma(L)
and
a^{2}b,a^{2}\in L
for any
a,b\in L
.

We know that
0,1\in L
,
L
is an additive subgroup and all inversions belong to
L
. The final step is to prove that
bc\in L
for any
b,c\in L
. There are two cases.

Case 1.

p=2
. Let
b,c\in L
. Since
a^{2}\in L
for any
a\in L
, there always exists
a\in L
such that
c=a^{2}
. Thus,
bc=a^{2}b\in L
.

Case 2.

p\neq 2
. For any
b,c\in L
it holds that
2bc=(c+1)^{2}b-b-c^{2}b\in L
. Indeed, all these terms belong to
L
. Since
2\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
L
is a linear subspace,
bc\in L
as well.

Thus,
L
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, i.e.
L=\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, where
k\mid n
. The theorem is proven.
∎

3
Functions without invariant subspaces

Here we show how to apply the results of Section
2
to construct functions without invariant affine subspaces of the form

\sigma_{A,b}(x)=A(\sigma(x))+b,

where
A:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is an invertible linear transformation and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. They are affinely equivalent to
\sigma
.
Let us define

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}})=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}:x\notin\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}},\text{ where }k\mid n\text{ and }k<n\}.

In other words,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}})
contains all elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
which do not lie in its subfields. It is always nonempty.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A:\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
be an invertible linear transformation,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
and

b^{-1}A(b^{-1})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}).

(2)

Then
\sigma_{A,b}
has no invariant affine subspaces
U
such that
2<|U|<p^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
U
be affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
\sigma_{A,b}(U)=U
, where
|U|>2
. Theorem
2
provides that
U=q^{-1}K
and
b+A(qK)=U
for some subfield
K
of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
and
q\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
. This is equivalent to

qb+qA(qK)=K.

(3)

Since
0\in K
, (
3
) provides that
qb=qb+qA(q\cdot 0)\in K
. Thus,
q=b^{-1}u
, where
u\in K\setminus\{0\}
. According to (
3
),

qb+qA(q\cdot u^{-1})=qb+ub^{-1}A(b^{-1}uu^{-1})=qb+ub^{-1}A(b^{-1})\in K.

At the same time, both
qb
and
u
belong to
K
. Hence,
b^{-1}A(b^{-1})\in K
. As a result,
b^{-1}A(b^{-1})\notin\bigcup_{k\mid n,k<n}\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
implies that
U=K=\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
∎

The condition (

(4)

Proof.

Indeed,

4
S-box of AES

The Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is the most popular block cipher
whose S-box
S:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{8}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{8}
(see
[
8
, Figure 7]
) is based on the function
\sigma_{A,b}
for certain invertible linear transformation
A
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
, see
[
8
,
11
]
. The field
\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
is constructed as the quotient ring of polynomials modulo

m(x)=x^{8}+x^{4}+x^{3}+x+1\text{ over }\mathbb{F}_{2}

and
b=x^{6}+x^{5}+x+1=\mathtt{0x63}
. Let us consider its invariant subspaces.

•

Nontrivial affine subspaces.
It can be shown that
t=b^{-1}S(b)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}})
. Indeed,
S(b)=x^{7}+x^{6}+x^{5}+x^{4}+x^{3}+x+1=\mathtt{0xFB}
. It is not difficult to check that
t=x^{7}+x^{6}+x^{3}=\mathtt{0xC8}
.
Also,

\displaystyle t^{2}

\displaystyle=x^{6}+x^{5}+x^{4}+1\neq t,

\displaystyle t^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle=x^{7}+x^{6}+x^{4}+x^{3}+x^{2}+1\neq t,

\displaystyle t^{2^{4}}

\displaystyle=x^{7}+x^{4}+x^{3}+1\neq t.

Hence,
t\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}})
. In other words, Theorem
3
and Remark
2
guarantee that
S
has no nontrivial invariant affine subspaces.

•

Fixed points.
It is known that
S(x)\neq x
for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{8}
by the choice, see
[
11
]
. Therefore,
S
has no invariant affine subspaces of dimension
0
.

•

However,

S(\mathtt{0x73})=\mathtt{0x8F}\text{ and }S(\mathtt{0x8F})=\mathtt{0x73}.

This implies that

\{\mathtt{0x73},\mathtt{0x8F}\}=\{x^{6}+x^{5}+x^{4}+x+1,x^{7}+x^{3}+x^{2}+x+1\}

is its invariant affine subspace of dimension
1
.

Thus, the results obtained allow us to prove theoretically that
S
has no nontrivial invariant affine subspaces. At the same time, it has an invariant affine subspace of dimension
1
.

Enrique González–Jiménez, Álvaro Lozano-Robledo, Benjamin York

Abstract
. For each prime number ℓ \ell and for each imaginary quadratic order of class number one or two, we determine all the possible ℓ \ell -adic Galois representations that occur for any elliptic curve with complex multiplication by such an order over its minimal field of definition, and then we determine all the isomorphism classes of elliptic curves that have a prescribed ℓ \ell -adic Galois representation.

Primary: 11G05, Secondary: 11G15, 14H52, 14K22.

\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}(E))

[
7
]

[

]
). In particular,

[

When

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will set the notation that we will follow in the rest of the paper, define the matrix groups that are the maximal possible images in the CM case, and recall results from previous articles that we will use in later section.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of
K
with discriminant
\Delta_{K}
. Let
f\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
be the order of
K
of conductor
f
. Let
j_{K,f}
be a CM
j
-invariant corresponding to the order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
(thus, every CM
j
-invariant with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
is a Galois conjugate of
j_{K,f}
) and let
N\geq 2
. We define associated constants
\delta
and
\phi
as follows:

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 0\bmod 4
or
N
is odd, let
\delta=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4
, and
\phi=0
.

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 4
and
N
is even, let
\delta=\frac{(\Delta_{K}-1)}{4}f^{2}
, let
\phi=f
.

We define matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

c_{\varepsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon&0\\
\phi&-\varepsilon\\
\end{array}\right),\quad c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right)

where
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
such that
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
We define the Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\{c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\ \det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\},

and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N),c_{1}\right\rangle
. We will sometimes call
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
the ‘‘normalizer’’ of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. In the case of a prime number
N
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is the true group-theoretic normalizer, but this is often not the case for composite values of
N
(see
[
5
, Section 5]
). Finally, we write
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
and regard it as a subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. If
\ell
is a prime, we write
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})=\varprojlim\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, let
N\geq 3
, and let
\rho_{E,N}
be the Galois representation
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))\to\operatorname{Aut}(E[N])\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. Similarly, for a prime
\ell
, we will write
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
for the Galois representation attached to the action of Galois on the
\ell
-adic Tate module
T_{\ell}(E)
. We will denote the
\ell
-adic image by
G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}=\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})})
, with an appropriate choice of basis such that
G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
, where
G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))
is the absolute Galois group of the field of definition
\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
(see Theorem
2.4
, cited from
[
5
]
, which shows that such a choice of basis exists).

2.1.
Labels

In Theorems
2.4
through
2.9
below, we describe certain explicit subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
that correspond to the possible images (up to conjugation) of
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
. We use the following conventions:

G_{\delta,\phi}^{i,t}=G_{\delta,\phi}^{i,t}(\ell^{\infty})

is a subgroup of the Cartan
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
of index
i
, that is,
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty}):G_{\delta,\phi}^{i,t}]=i
. When there are several possible subgroups of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
with index
i
, we use a parameter
t\in\{1,2,3,4\}
to differentiate between subgroups (the choice of numbering, though, was arbitrary).

Alternatively, in the tables, we will refer to the subgroups of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
generated by the subgroups
G_{\delta,\phi}^{i,t}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
and by the matrices
c_{\varepsilon}
or
c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
using a labeling system similar to the labels that are used in the LMFDB (
[
4
]
) for subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. The subgroups will be denoted by a label of the form

\ell\texttt{.}\nu\texttt{.c-n.i.t}

where

•

\ell
is the prime we are considering,

•

\nu
is the
\ell
-adic valuation of
\Delta(\mathcal{O})=\Delta_{K}\cdot f^{2}
,

•

c
is the square class of
u=\Delta_{K}\cdot f^{2}\cdot\ell^{-\nu}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times})^{2}
. If
\ell>2
, then
c
is
s
or
ns
according to whether
u
is a square or a non-square, respectively. If
\ell=2
, then
c
is
s
,
ns3
,
ns5
, or
ns7
according to whether
u\equiv 1,3,5
or
7\bmod 8
, respectively,

•

n
is the level of definition of the subgroup. The level of definition of a subgroup
G\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
is
n\geq 1
is smallest with the property that
\pi_{n}^{-1}(\pi_{n}(G))=G
, where
\pi_{n}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
,

•

i
is the index of the subgroup in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
, and

•

t
is a tie-breaker among subgroups of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
that share
\ell
,
\nu
,
c
,
n
, and
i
. The tie-breaker is defined in
[
7
, Section 2.4]
.

We would like to thank Drew Sutherland for his help in devising the above labeling method.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with
j(E)=1728
, and suppose that
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-Nx
, where
N>0
. If
\zeta_{16}
is a primitive
16
th root of unity, and
\zeta_{8}=\zeta_{16}^{2}
is a primitive
8
th root of unity, then

•

\mathbb{Q}(E[4])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8},\sqrt[4]{N})
, and

•

\mathbb{Q}(E[8])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16},\sqrt{-1+\sqrt{2}},\sqrt[4]{N})

Proof.

Let
\zeta_{16}:=e^{\pi i/8}
, so that
\zeta_{8}=\zeta_{16}^{2}=(1+i)/\sqrt{2}
and
\zeta_{4}=\zeta_{8}^{2}=i
. The Weierstrass model for

can be expressed as

, so we let
\alpha_{1}=0
,
\alpha_{2}=\sqrt{N}
, and
\alpha_{3}=-\sqrt{N}
. Then, let

and
A_{2}=A_{3}=i\sqrt[4]{N}
as in Theorem
2.10
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(E[4])

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}(E[2],i,A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N},i,\sqrt{2},\sqrt[4]{N})=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2},\sqrt[4]{N}),

and since
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8})=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{2})
, we have

\mathbb{Q}(E[4])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8},\sqrt[4]{N}),
as stated. Next, let

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sqrt{2}\sqrt[4]{N}\left(i\sqrt[4]{N}-\sqrt[4]{N})\right)}=\sqrt{\sqrt{2}(i-1)\sqrt{N}}=\sqrt{2}\,\zeta_{16}^{3}\sqrt[4]{N},

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i\sqrt[4]{N}\left(i\sqrt[4]{N}+\sqrt{2}i\sqrt[4]{N}\right)}=\sqrt{(-\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{N}}=i\sqrt{\sqrt{2}+1}\sqrt[4]{N},

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i\sqrt[4]{N}\left(\sqrt{2}\sqrt[4]{N}-\sqrt[4]{N}\right)}=\sqrt{i(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{N}}=\zeta_{8}\sqrt{\sqrt{2}-1}\sqrt[4]{N}.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(E[8])

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}(E[4],\zeta_{8},B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be an elliptic curve defined over a number field

be the image of

by

(1)

. In particular, if
\sqrt{\alpha}\in F(E[N])
, then
F(E^{\alpha}[N])\subseteq F(E[N])
.

(2)

If
\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, then

, and

.

Proof.

Let
N
,
E
,
\alpha
, and
E^{\alpha}
as in the statement. Then,
E
and
E^{\alpha}
are isomorphic over
F(\sqrt{\alpha})
. In particular,
F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E^{\alpha}[N])
, and so

. Moreover, if
\sqrt{\alpha}\in F(E[N])

Now suppose that
\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, and choose any point

. It follows that
(x_{N},y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha})\in E^{\alpha}[N]
. If
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
, then

\sigma(x_{N},y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha})=(\sigma(x_{N}),\sigma(y_{N})/\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha})).

If we define
\chi_{\alpha}\colon G_{F}\to\{\pm 1\}
by
\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)=\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha})/\sqrt{\alpha}
, then:

\sigma(x_{N},y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha})=(\sigma(x_{N}),\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot\sigma(y_{N})/\sqrt{\alpha})=\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot(\sigma(x_{N}),\sigma(y_{N})/\sqrt{\alpha}).

Moreover, if we fix a

-basis

of
E[N]
and a basis

of

, and if we let
\rho_{E,N}(\sigma)=:M(\sigma)\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, then

\rho_{E^{\alpha},N}(\sigma)=(\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot M(\sigma).

Since
\sqrt{\alpha}
is not in
F(E[N])
, we may choose a
\sigma

but acts on

as
\chi_{\alpha}

and therefore:

\sigma(x_{N},y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha})=(\sigma(x_{N}),\sigma(y_{N})/\sigma(\sqrt{\alpha}))=(x_{N},\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha})=\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot(x_{N},y_{N}/\sqrt{\alpha}).

In other words, if
\rho_{E,N}(\sigma)=M(\sigma)=\operatorname{Id}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})

\rho_{E^{\alpha},N}(\sigma)=\chi_{\alpha}(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id}.
Since
\sigma
exists that acts trivially on

and non-trivially on

, and

F(E^{\alpha}[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E^{\alpha}[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N]).

Further, since
\sqrt{\alpha}
is not in
F(E[N])
, it follows that
\operatorname{Gal}(F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])/F)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F)\times\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[N])/F)
and so
\rho_{E^{\alpha},N}(G_{F})
is generated by

and

, i.e.,

G_{E^{\alpha},N}=\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 3.13
.

Let

such that

is
6
th-power-free, and let

•

, and

•

\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3},\sqrt[6]{d},\sqrt[3]{3},\zeta_{9}+\zeta_{9}^{-1})

In particular,

(1)

if and only if

(2)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3]):\mathbb{Q}]=4
if and only if

for some

,

,

(3)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3]):\mathbb{Q}]=6
if and only if
d=t^{2},-3t^{2}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}\smallsetminus\{\pm 1,\pm 3\}
, or

(4)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3]):\mathbb{Q}]=12
otherwise,

and

(5)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9]):\mathbb{Q}]=18
if and only if

(6)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9]):\mathbb{Q}]=36
if and only if
d=t^{3}
, or
9t^{3}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
,
t\neq 1,-3
and
d=3t^{3}
for some
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
,

,

(7)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9]):\mathbb{Q}]=54
if and only if
d=t^{2}
or
-3t^{2}
for

or

(8)

[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9]):\mathbb{Q}]=108

Proof.

Let
\psi_{N}(x)
denote the
N
th division polynomial for
E^{d}
. We find that
\psi_{3}(x)=3x(x^{3}+64d)
. A point

with

has

, so

For
\zeta_{6}
a primitive
6
th root of unity,

, where
\alpha_{i}=4\zeta_{6}^{2i-1}\sqrt[3]{d}

.
Note that

, thus
\sqrt[3]{d}\in\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3])
.
Further,
\alpha_{2}/\alpha_{1}=\zeta_{6}^{2}=\zeta_{3}
, so
\sqrt{-3}\in\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3])
.

Finally, if

, then

, thus

\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3])=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d},\sqrt{-3},\sqrt[3]{d})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt[6]{d}),

and the values of the field degree
[\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[3]):\mathbb{Q}]
follow by inspection. Next, we define

\frac{\psi_{9}(x)}{3\psi_{3}(x)}=F_{9}(x),

where
F_{9}
is a degree
36
polynomial. We perform a change of variables to better find a closed form expression for
\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9])
. Let
x^{3}:=2^{6}dz
, so

for

, and

.

Then

, where

and
g_{9}(z)
are irreducible polynomials of degree
3
and
9
, respectively. We find that

has as its splitting field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})^{+}
, the maximal real subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})
, which is generated as
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})^{+}=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9}+\zeta_{9}^{-1})
.

Next, let
K_{9}^{\prime}\cong K[z]/\langle g_{9}(z)\rangle
, and let
K_{9}
denote the splitting field of
g_{9}(z)
, with field degrees
9
and
18
, respectively. By computation, we find that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{9})^{+}
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{3})
are index 3 subfields of
K_{9}^{\prime}
. Further, for
\gamma\in K_{9}
satisfying

or

, we find that

, and
\gamma

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(E^{d}[9])

\displaystyle=K_{9}(\sqrt{d},\sqrt[3]{d})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3},\sqrt[3]{3},\sqrt[6]{d},\zeta_{9}+\zeta_{9}^{-1})

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F
be a number field, let
E/F
be an elliptic curve, let
N>2
, and let
G_{E,N}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
be the image of
\rho_{E,N}\colon G_{F}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\alpha\in F
such that
-\operatorname{Id}
does not belong to
\operatorname{Im}(\rho_{E^{\alpha},N})=G_{E^{\alpha},N}
, where
E^{\alpha}
is the corresponding quadratic twist of
E
. Then:

(1)

The element
\sqrt{\alpha}
belongs to
F(E[N])
.

(2)

The only prime ideals that can ramify in the extension
F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F
are the primes dividing
N
and the conductor
N_{E/F}
of
E
.

(3)

Let
M/F
be the maximal abelian extension of
F
of exponent
2
that is unramified outside of the infinite places of
F
and the primes dividing
N\cdot N_{E/F}
. Then,
F(\sqrt{\alpha})\subseteq M
.

(4)

There are only finitely many possibilities for
\alpha
up to squares in
(F^{\times})^{2}
.

(5)

Suppose that
F
is a real quadratic field of class number
1
, and let
\wp_{1},\ldots,\wp_{n}
be all the prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
dividing
N\cdot N_{E}
, where
N_{E}
is the norm of the conductor of
E
. Let
\wp_{i}=(\pi_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and some
\pi_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
, and write
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}=\langle-1,u_{F}\rangle
, where
u_{F}
is a fundamental unit. Then,
\alpha
as above belongs to the set

A_{\alpha}=\{\pm u_{F}^{k_{0}}\pi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\pi_{n}^{k_{n}}:k_{0},\ldots,k_{n}\in\{0,1\}\}.

Proof.

Let
E
,
\alpha
, and
E^{\alpha}
be as in the statement, and assume that
-\operatorname{Id}
does not belong to the image
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
. If
\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, then Lemma
3.4
implies that
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
is conjugate to
\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle
, and therefore
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
would contain
-\operatorname{Id}
, a contradiction. Hence
\sqrt{\alpha}
must belong to
F(E[N])
. This proves (1).

By (1), we have inclusions
F\subseteq F(\sqrt{\alpha})\subseteq F(E[N])
. Hence, the criterion of Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich implies that
F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F
can only ramify at primes dividing
N\cdot N_{E/F}
. This proves (2).

Part (3) follows directly from (2), because the extension
F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F
is a quadratic extension, thus abelian of exponent
2
, and the only prime ideals that can ramify are those dividing
N\cdot N_{E/F}
.

Finally,
M/F
is necessarily finite (see
[
9
, Ch. VIII, Prop. 1.6]
). Hence, the set of classes of
\alpha
in
F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}
such that
F(\sqrt{\alpha})\subseteq M
must be finite. This shows (4).

For (5), consider the extension
F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F
for
\alpha\in F
as above. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Since the discriminant of the polynomial
x^{2}-\alpha
is
4\alpha
, this extension may be ramified at primes above
2
and any other prime
\wp
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
such that
\operatorname{ord}_{\wp}(\alpha)=1
. By (2), the only primes that can ramify in
F(\sqrt{\alpha})/F
are the primes dividing
N\cdot N_{E}
, so the prime ideals in the factorization of
(\alpha)
must be contained in
\{\wp_{1},\ldots,\wp_{n}\}
with notation as in the statement of the lemma. Since
F
is assumed to be of class number
1
, then
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is a PID, and
\alpha

\pm u_{F}^{k_{0}}\pi_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\pi_{n}^{k_{n}}
for some
k_{0},\ldots,k_{n}\in\{0,1\}
, as desired.

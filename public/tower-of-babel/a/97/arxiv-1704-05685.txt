Introduction.

Let
(N,h)
be a complete smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
with
\partial N=\emptyset
. We denote spacetime coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
as
(t,x)=(x_{\alpha})
with
0\leq\alpha\leq d
. A wave map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{1+d}\mapsto N
is formally defined as a critical point of the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}(\Phi,\partial\Phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+d}}g^{\alpha\mu}\Big<\partial_{\alpha}\Phi,\partial_{\mu}\Phi\Big>_{h}dtdx,

where
g=\textup{diag}(-1,1,\cdots,1)
is the Minkowski metric on
\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
and
\partial_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\alpha}}
. In the local coordinates on
(N,h)
, the critical points of
\mathcal{L}
satisfy the equation

\square_{g}\Phi^{k}+g^{\alpha\mu}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}(\Phi)\partial_{\alpha}\Phi^{i}\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{j}=0,\quad 1\leq k\leq d,

(1.1)

where
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
are Christoffel symbols associated to the metric
h
of the target manifold
N
, and
\square_{g}
stands for the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
(\mathbb{R}^{1+d},g)
defined by

\square_{g}u=\partial_{tt}-\Delta.

A special case is when the target manifold
N=\Sb{}^{d}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
, equation (
1.1
) becomes

\partial_{t}^{2}\Phi-\Delta\Phi=\Phi(|\nabla\Phi|^{2}-|\partial_{t}\Phi|^{2}).

(1.2)

Under the assumption of 1-corotational symmetry, namely that the solution takes the form

\Phi(x,t)=\binom{\cos(u(|x|,t))}{\frac{x}{|x|}\sin(u(|x|,t))},

equation (
1.2
) reduces to the semilinear wave equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}^{2}u&=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{(d-1)}{r}\partial_{r}u-\frac{(d-1)}{2r^{2}}\sin(2u),\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)\mid_{t=0}&=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
u(t):r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\to u(r,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The set of solutions to (
1.3
) is invariant by the scaling symmetry

\vec{u}(r,t):=\big(u,\partial_{t}u\big)(r,t)\longmapsto\vec{u}_{\lambda}(r,t):=\left(u,\frac{1}{\lambda}\partial_{t}u\right)\left(\frac{r}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda}\right),\quad\forall\lambda>0.

The problem (
1.3
) exhibits a conserved energy

\mathcal{E}(\vec{u})(t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\partial_{r}u|^{2}+\frac{(d-1)}{r^{2}}\sin^{2}(u)\right)r^{d-1}dr=\textup{const.},

(1.4)

which satisfies

\mathcal{E}(\vec{u}_{\lambda})=\lambda^{d-2}\mathcal{E}(\vec{u}).

This means that the wave map problem (
1.3
) is energy subcritical if
d=1
, critical if
d=2
and supercritical if
d\geq 3
.

Implementation of a robust universal energy method to control the solution in the blowup regime through the derivation of suitable Lyapunov functional, which relies on neither spectral estimates nor the maximum principle and may be easily applied to various settings.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of type II blowup solutions to (
1.3
) with prescibed behavior)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and
\gamma
be defined as in (
1.10
), we fix an integer

\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\quad\text{with}\quad\ell>\gamma,

and two numbers
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathfrak{s}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

0<\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\ll 1\quad\text{and}\quad 1\ll\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{s}(\ell)\to+\infty\quad\text{as}\;\;\ell\to+\infty.

Then there exists an open set of initial data of the form

(u_{0},u_{1})=(Q,0)+(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}),\quad(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1})\in\mathcal{O}\subset\left(\dot{H}^{\sigma}\cap\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}}\right)\times\left(\dot{H}^{\sigma-1}\cap\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}-1}\right),

such that the corresponding solution to equation (
1.3
) satisfies

u(r,t)=Q\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)}\right)+\varepsilon\left(\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},t\right)

(1.12)

where

\lambda(t)=c(u_{0},u_{1})(T-t)^{\frac{\ell}{\gamma}}(1+o_{t\to T}(1)),\quad c(u_{0},u_{1})>0,

(1.13)

and

\lim_{t\to T}\|(\varepsilon(t),\lambda\partial_{t}\varepsilon(t))\|_{\dot{H}^{\mu}\times\dot{H}^{\mu-1}}=0,\quad\forall\mu\in\left[\sigma,\mathfrak{s}\right].

(1.14)

Remark 1.2
.

Since
\gamma\in(1,2)
for
d\geq 8
and
\gamma=2
for
d=7
, the condition
\ell>\gamma
requests that
\ell\geq 2
for
d\geq 8
and
\ell\geq 3
for
d=7
. As for the case
\ell=\gamma
, which only happens in the case
d=7
with
\ell=\gamma=2
, we expect that the blowup rate (
1.13
) would involve some logarithmic correction of the form

\lambda(t)\sim\frac{T-t}{|\log(T-t)|^{\nu}}\quad\text{for some}\quad\nu>0.

This logarithmic gain would be related to the growth of the approximate profile at infinity. Although our analysis would be naturally extended to this case, this seems to require some crucial modification in the construction of an approximate profile and this would be treated in a separate work.

Remark 1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
involves a detailed description of the the set of initial data leading to the type II blowup with the quantization of the blowup rate (
1.13
). In particular, given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
L\gg 1
and
\mathfrak{s}\sim L
, our initial data is of the form

\vec{u}_{0}=\vec{Q}_{b(0)}+\vec{q}_{0},

where
\vec{Q}_{b}
is a deformation of the ground state

, and

correspond to possible unstable directions of the flow in the
\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}}\times\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}-1}

\vec{q}_{0}\in\mathcal{O}\subset\Big(\dot{H}^{\sigma}\cap\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}}\Big)\times\Big(\dot{H}^{\sigma-1}\cap\dot{H}^{\mathfrak{s}-1}\Big)

is built on the linearized operator (see Definition
3.1
for its precise description of

) and for all
\big(b_{1}(0),b_{\ell+1}(0),\cdots,b_{L}(0)\big)

such that the solution of (
1.3

) satisfies the conclusion of Theorem

. The control of

Remark 1.4
.

It is worth mentioning that our analysis relies only on the study of supercritical Sobolev norms built on the linearized operator, thus, the finiteness of the

(v)

\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}(s):=\|\vec{q}(s)\|_{\Bbbk}^{2}\gtrsim\|\vec{q}(s)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{\Bbbk}\times\dot{H}^{\Bbbk-1}},

where

\vec{Q}_{b}

which is good enough to control the local norms of

Note that when establishing the formula (

\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}=\|\vec{q}\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}\times\dot{H}^{\sigma-1}},\quad\frac{d}{ds}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d}}\right\}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{1+\frac{\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(2\sigma-d\right)+\epsilon}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d}}\quad\text{for some}\quad\epsilon>0.

\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}(s)\lesssim b_{1}^{\frac{\ell}{\ell-\gamma}(2\sigma-d)}.

Lemma 2.1

(Development of the harmonic map
Q
)
.

Let
d\geq 7
, there exists a unique solution
Q
to equation (
2.3
), which admits the following asymptotic behavior: For any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

(i)
(Asymptotic behavior of
Q
)

Q(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\dfrac{a_{0}}{y^{\gamma}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)\right]&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where
\gamma
is defined in (
1.10
),
\tilde{\gamma}=\sqrt{d^{2}-8d+8}
and the constant
a_{0}=a_{0}(d)>0
.

(ii)
(Degeneracy)

\Lambda Q>0,\quad\Lambda Q(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}^{\prime}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{a_{0}\gamma}{y^{\gamma}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)\right]&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

Proof.

The proof can be found at pages 184-185 in
[
Biernat(2015)
]
.
∎

A remarkable fact is that the linearized operator
\mathscr{L}
admits the following factorization.

Lemma 2.2

(Factorization of
\mathscr{L}
)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and define the first order operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}w

\displaystyle=-\partial_{y}w+\frac{V}{y}w=-\Lambda Q\partial_{y}\left(\frac{w}{\Lambda Q}\right),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{*}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}}\partial_{y}\big(y^{d-1}w\big)+\frac{V}{y}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\partial_{y}\left(y^{d-1}\Lambda Qw\right),

(2.7)

where

V(y):=\Lambda\log(\Lambda Q)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\mathcal{O}(y^{2})&\text{as}\quad y\to 0,\\
&\\
-\gamma+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{\tilde{\gamma}}}\right)&\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.8)

We have

\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{A}^{*}\mathscr{A},\quad\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{A}^{*},

(2.9)

where
\tilde{\mathscr{L}}
stands for the conjugate Hamiltonian.

Remark 2.3
.

The adjoint operator
\mathscr{A}^{*}
is defined with respect to the Lebesgue measure

\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\mathscr{A}u)wy^{d-1}dy=\int_{0}^{+\infty}u(\mathscr{A}^{*}w)y^{d-1}dy.

Remark 2.4
.

The factorization (
2.9
) immediately implies that

\mathscr{L}w=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad w\in\textup{span}\{\Lambda Q,\Gamma\},

where

\Gamma=-\Lambda Q\int_{1}^{y}\frac{dx}{x^{d-1}(\Lambda Q)^{2}},

which admits the asymptotic behavior

\Gamma(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{dy^{d-1}}+\mathcal{O}(y)&\text{as}\;\;y\to 0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{1}{a_{0}\gamma(d-2-2\gamma)y^{d-2-\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{1}{y^{d-\gamma}}\right)&\text{as}\;\;y\to+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

Remark 2.5
.

We have

\mathscr{L}(\Lambda w)=\Lambda(\mathscr{L}w)+2\mathscr{L}w-\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}w.

(2.11)

Since
\mathscr{L}(\Lambda Q)=0
, one can express the definition of
Z
through the potential
V
as follows:

Z(y)=V^{2}+\Lambda V+(d-2)V.

(2.12)

Let
\tilde{Z}
be defined by

\tilde{\mathscr{L}}=-\partial_{yy}-\frac{d-1}{y}\partial_{y}+\frac{\tilde{Z}}{y^{2}},

(2.13)

then, a direct computation yields

\tilde{Z}(y)=(V+1)^{2}+(d-2)(V+1)-\Lambda V.

(2.14)

The factorization of
\mathscr{L}
allows us to compute
\mathscr{L}^{-1}
in an elementary two step processes as follows:

Lemma 2.6

(Inversion of
\mathscr{L}
)
.

Let
f
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
radially symmetric function and
\mathscr{L}w=f
, then

w=-\Lambda Q\int_{0}^{y}\frac{\mathscr{A}w(x)}{\Lambda Q(x)}dx\quad\text{with}\quad\mathscr{A}w=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}f(x)\Lambda Q(x)x^{d-1}dx.

(2.15)

Proof.

See Lemma 2.5 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
.
∎

Knowing
\mathscr{L}^{-1}
, we can easily defined the inversion of
\mathscr{H}
as follows:

\mathscr{H}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathscr{L}^{-1}\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}.

(2.16)

By a direct check, we have

\mathscr{H}^{2k}=(-1)^{k}\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{k}&0\\
0&\mathscr{L}^{k}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}^{2k+1}=(-1)^{k}\begin{bmatrix}0&-\mathscr{L}^{k}\\
\mathscr{L}^{k+1}&0\end{bmatrix},

(2.17)

and

\mathscr{H}^{*2k}=(-1)^{k}\begin{bmatrix}\mathscr{L}^{k}&0\\
0&\mathscr{L}^{k}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}^{*(2k+1)}=(-1)^{k}\begin{bmatrix}0&\mathscr{L}^{k+1}\\
-\mathscr{L}^{k}&0\end{bmatrix}.

(2.18)

2.2.
Admissible functions.

We define a class of admissible functions which display a suitable behavior both at the origin and infinity.

Definition 2.7

(Admissible function)
.

Fix
\gamma>0
, we say that a smooth vector function
\vec{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R})
is admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},\iota)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}
if

(i)\;

\iota
is the position:

\vec{f}=\binom{f}{0}\;\text{if}\;\iota=0,\quad\vec{f}=\binom{0}{f}\;\text{if}\;\iota=1.

(ii)\;

f
admits a Taylor expansion to all orders around the origin,

f(y)=\sum_{k=p_{1}-\iota,k\;\textup{even}}^{p}c_{k}y^{k+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{p+2});

(iii)\;

f
and its derivatives admit the bounds, for
y\geq 1
,

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial^{k}_{y}f(y)|\lesssim y^{p_{2}-\gamma-\iota-k}.

Remark 2.8
.

Note from (
2.5
) that
\Lambda\vec{Q}=\binom{\Lambda Q}{0}
is admissible of degree
(0,0,0)
.

One note that
\mathscr{H}
naturally acts on the class of admissible function in the following way:

Lemma 2.9

(Action of
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{H}^{-1}
on admissible functions)
.

Let
\vec{f}
be an admissible function of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},\iota)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}
, then:

(i)\;

\Lambda\vec{f}
is admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},\iota)
.

(ii)\,

\mathscr{H}\vec{f}
is admissible of degree
(\max\{\iota,p_{1}-1\},p_{2}-1,(\iota+1)\wedge 2))
.

(iii)\,

\mathscr{H}^{-1}\vec{f}
is admissible of degree
(p_{1}+1,p_{2}+1,(\iota+1)\wedge 2))
.

Proof.

The proof directly follows from the definitions of
\Lambda
,
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{H}^{-1}
, and we refer the reader to Lemma 2.8 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
for a similar proof.
∎

Lemma 2.10

Let the sequence of profiles

\mathscr{H}\vec{T}_{k+1}=-\vec{T}_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\vec{T}_{0}=\Lambda\vec{Q},

(i)\;

\vec{T}_{k}

.

(ii)\;

\Lambda\vec{T}_{k}-(k-\gamma)\vec{T}_{k}
is admissible of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(i)
We note from (
2.5
) that
\vec{T}_{0}=\Lambda\vec{Q}
is admissible of degree
(0,0,0)
. By induction and part
(iii)
of Lemma
2.9
, the conclusion simply follows. For item
(ii)
, we refer to Lemma 2.9 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
for an analogous proof.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

From item
(i)
of Lemma
2.10
, we see that the profile
T_{k}
has only one null coordinate, which depends on the index
k
. For simplicity we make use the following notation

\vec{T}_{2i}=\binom{T_{2i}}{0},\quad\vec{T}_{2i+1}=\binom{0}{T_{2i+1}}.

(2.20)

We end this subsection by introducing a simple notion of homogeneous admissible function.

Definition 2.12

(Homogeneous admissible function)
.

Let
L\gg 1
be an integer and
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
. We say that a vector function
\vec{f}(y,b)
is homogeneous of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},\iota,p_{3})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{N}
if it is a finite combination of monomials

\vec{g}(y)\prod_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}^{m_{k}},

with
\vec{g}(y)
admissible of degree
(p_{1},p_{2},\iota)
in the sense of Definition
2.7
and

(m_{1},\cdots,m_{L})\in\mathbb{N}^{L},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{L}km_{k}=p_{3}.

We set

\text{deg}(\vec{f}):=(p_{1},p_{2},\iota,p_{3}).

2.3.
Slowly modulated blow-up profile.

In this subsection, we use the explicit structure of the linearized operator
\mathscr{H}
to construct an approximate blow-up profile. In particular, we claim the following:

Proposition 2.13

(Construction of the approximate profile)
.

Let
d\geq 7
and
L\gg 1
be an odd integer. Let
M>0
be a large enough universal constant, then there exist a small enough universal constant
b^{*}(M,L)>0
such that the following holds true. Let a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
map

b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L}):[s_{0},s_{1}]\mapsto(-b^{*},b^{*})^{L},

with a priori bounds in
[s_{0},s_{1}]
:

0<b_{1}<b^{*},\quad|b_{k}|\lesssim b_{1}^{k},\quad 2\leq k\leq L,

(2.21)

Then there exist homogeneous profiles

\vec{S}_{k}=\vec{S}_{k}(y,b),\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

such that

\vec{Q}_{b(s)}(y)=\vec{Q}(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}(s)\vec{T}_{k}(y)+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\vec{S}_{k}(y,b)\equiv\vec{Q}(y)+\vec{\Theta}_{b(s)}(y),

(2.22)

generates an approximate solution to the remormalized flow (
2.2
):

\partial_{s}\vec{Q}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{Q}_{b}-\vec{F}(\vec{Q}_{b})=\vec{\Psi}_{b}+\vec{\textup{Mod}},

(2.23)

with the following property:

(i)
(Modulation equation)

\vec{\textup{Mod}}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}+(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}\Big]\left[\vec{T}_{k}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{L+2}\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{j}}{\partial b_{k}}\right],

(2.24)

where we use the convention
b_{j}=0
for
j\geq L+1
.

(ii)
(Estimate on the profiles) The profiles
(S_{k})_{2\leq k\leq L+2}
are homogeneous with

\displaystyle\text{deg}(\vec{S}_{k})=(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=\vec{0}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L.

(iii)
(Estimate on the error
\vec{\Psi}_{b}
) The generated error term is of the form

\vec{\Psi}_{b}=\binom{0}{\Psi_{b}},

where
\Psi_{b}
satisfies for all
0\leq m\leq L
,

- (global weight bound)

\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|\nabla^{m+\hbar}\Psi_{b}|^{2}+\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|\Psi_{b}\mathscr{L}^{m+\hbar}\Psi_{b}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

(2.25)

where
B_{1}
,
\hbar
,
\delta
are defined in (
1.20
) and (
1.17
).

- (improved local bound)

\forall M\geq 1,\quad\int_{y\leq M}|\nabla^{m+\hbar}\Psi_{b}|^{2}+\int_{y\leq M}|\Psi_{b}\mathscr{L}^{m+\hbar}\Psi_{b}|\lesssim M^{C}b_{1}^{2L+6}.

(2.26)

Remark 2.14
.

From item
(ii)
of Proposition
2.13
, we make the abuse of notation

\vec{S}_{2i}=\binom{S_{2i}}{0},\quad\vec{S}_{2i+1}=\binom{0}{S_{2i+1}}.

(2.27)

Proof.

We aim at constructing the profiles
(\vec{S}_{k})_{2\leq k\leq L+2}
such that
\vec{\Psi}_{b}(y)
defined from (
2.23
) has the
least possible growth
as
y\to+\infty
. The key to this construction is the fact that the structure of the linearized operator
\mathscr{H}
defined in (
1.22
) is completely explicit in the radial sector thanks to the explicit formulas of the elements of the kernel of
\mathscr{L}
. This procedure will lead to the leading-order modulation equation

(b_{k})_{s}=-(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}+b_{k+1}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq L,

(2.28)

which actually cancels the worst growth of
\vec{S}_{k}
as
y\to+\infty
.

\bullet
Expansion of
\vec{\Psi}_{b}

. From (
2.23
) and (
2.3
), we write

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\vec{Q}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{Q}_{b}-\vec{F}(\vec{Q}_{b})

\displaystyle=b_{1}\Lambda\vec{Q}+\partial_{s}\Theta_{b}+\mathscr{H}\vec{\Theta}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\Theta_{b}-\vec{N}(\vec{\Theta}_{b}):=A_{1}-\vec{N}(\vec{\Theta}_{b}),

where
\vec{N}
is defined as in (
1.24
). Using the expression (
2.22
) of
\vec{\Theta}_{b}
and the definition (
2.19
) of
\vec{T}_{k}
(recall that
\mathscr{H}\vec{T}_{k}=-\vec{T}_{k-1}
with the convention
\vec{T}_{0}=\Lambda\vec{Q}
), we write

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}\Lambda\vec{Q}+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}\vec{T}_{k}+b_{k}\mathscr{H}\vec{T}_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\Lambda\vec{T}_{k}\Big]+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\Big[\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{k}+\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{k}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}\vec{T}_{k}-b_{k+1}\vec{T}_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\Lambda\vec{T}_{k}\Big]+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\Big[\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{k}+\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{k}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{k})_{s}+(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}\Big]\vec{T}_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Big[\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{k+1}+\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{k}+b_{1}b_{k}\big[\Lambda\vec{T}_{k}-(k-\gamma)\vec{T}_{k}\big]+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Big[\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{L+2}+\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{L+1}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{L+1}\Big]+\Big[\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{L+2}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{L+2}\Big].

We now write

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\vec{S}_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}(b_{j})_{s}\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(b_{j})_{s}+(j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle A_{1}=\vec{\textup{Mod}}+\sum_{k=1}^{L+1}\left[\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{k+1}+\vec{E}_{k}\right]+\vec{E}_{L+2},

where for
k=1,\cdots,L
,

\vec{E}_{k}=b_{1}b_{k}\big[\Lambda\vec{T}_{k}-(k-\gamma)\vec{T}_{k}\big]+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Big[(j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{j}},

(2.29)

and for
k=L+1,L+2
,

\vec{E}_{k}=b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{L}\Big[(j-\gamma)b_{1}b_{j}-b_{j+1}\Big]\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{j}}.

(2.30)

Recall from (
3.12
) that the nonlinear term is given by

\vec{N}(\vec{\Theta}_{b})=\binom{0}{N(\Theta_{b,1})}:=\binom{0}{A_{2}}.

Let us denote

f(x)=\sin(2x)

and use a Taylor expansion to write (see pages 1740 in
[
Raphaël and Schweyer(2014a)
]
for a similar computation)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(i)}(Q)}{i!}\left(\sum_{k=2,\textup{even}}^{L-1}b_{i}T_{i}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k,1}\right)^{i}+R_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\left[\sum_{i=2}^{L+2}P_{i}+R_{1}+R_{2}\right],

where

P_{i}=\sum_{j=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{j!}\sum_{|J|_{1}=j,|J|_{2}=i}c_{J}\prod_{k=2,\textup{even}}^{L-1}b_{k}^{i_{k}}T_{k}^{i_{k}}\prod_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k,1}^{j_{k}},

(2.31)

R_{1}=\sum_{j=2}^{L+2}\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{j!}\sum_{|J|_{1}=j,|J|_{2}\geq L+3}c_{J}\prod_{k=2,\textup{even}}^{L-1}b_{k}^{i_{k}}T_{k}^{i_{k}}\prod_{k=2}^{L+2}S_{k,1}^{j_{k}},

(2.32)

R_{2}=\frac{\Theta_{b,1}^{L+3}}{(L+2)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)^{L+2}f^{(L+3)}(Q+\tau\Theta_{b,1})d\tau,

(2.33)

with
J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2L+1}
and

|J|_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}i_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}j_{k},\quad|J|_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{L}ki_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}kj_{k}.

(2.34)

In conclusion, we have

\vec{\Psi}_{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{L+1}\left[\mathscr{H}\vec{S}_{k+1}+\vec{E}_{k}-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\vec{P}_{k+1}\right]+\vec{E}_{L+2}-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}(\vec{R}_{1}+\vec{R}_{2}),

(2.35)

where we write

\vec{P}_{k}=\binom{0}{P_{k}},\quad\vec{R}_{1}=\binom{0}{R_{1}},\quad\vec{R}_{2}=\binom{0}{R_{2}}.

\bullet
Construction of
\vec{S}_{k}
.

From the expression of
\vec{\Psi}_{b}
given in (
2.35
), we construct iteratively the sequences of profiles
(\vec{S}_{k})_{1\leq k\leq L+2}
through the scheme

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vec{S}_{1}&=\vec{0},\\
\vec{S}_{k}&=-\mathscr{H}^{-1}\vec{F}_{k},\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,\end{array}\right.

(2.36)

where

\vec{F}_{k}=\vec{E}_{k-1}-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\vec{P}_{k}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2.

We claim by induction on
k
that
\vec{F}_{k}
is homogeneous with

\text{deg}(\vec{F}_{k})=(k-1,k-2,(k-1)\wedge 2,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

(2.37)

and

\frac{\partial\vec{F}_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=\vec{0}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L+2.

(2.38)

From item
(iii)
of Lemma
2.9
and (
2.37
), we deduce that
\vec{S}_{k}
is homogeneous of degree

\text{deg}(\vec{S}_{k})=(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq L+2,

and from (
2.38
), we get

\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{m}}=\vec{0}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq k\leq m\leq L+2,

which is the conclusion of item
(ii)
.

Let us now give the proof of (
2.37
) and (
2.38
). We proceed by induction.

- Case
k=2
: We compute explicitly from (
2.29
) and (
2.31
),

\vec{F}_{2}=\vec{E}_{1}-\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\vec{P}_{2}=b_{1}^{2}\left[\Lambda\vec{T}_{1}-(1-\gamma)\vec{T}_{1}+\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\vec{P}_{2}\right],

which directly follows (
2.38
). From Lemma
2.10
, we know that
\Lambda\vec{T}_{1}-(1-\gamma)\vec{T}_{1}
are admissible of degree
(1,0,1)
. It remains to check that
\frac{1}{y^{2}}\vec{P}_{2}=\binom{0}{\frac{P_{2}}{y^{2}}}
is admissible of degree
(1,0,1)
. To do so, let us write from the definition (
2.31
),

\frac{P_{2}}{y^{2}}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}.

Using (
2.4
), one can check the bound

\forall m,j\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{m}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim y^{-\gamma-2-m}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

(2.39)

Since
\vec{T}_{1}
is admissible of degree
(1,1,1)
, we have that

\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{m}(T_{1}^{2})|\lesssim y^{-2\gamma-m}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

By the Leibniz rule and the fact that
2\gamma>2
, we get that

\forall m,j\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{m}\left(\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}\right)\right|\lesssim y^{-2-\gamma-m}.

We also have the expansion near the origin,

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(Q)}{y^{2}}T_{1}^{2}=\sum_{i=0,even}^{k}c_{i}y^{i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{k+2}),\quad k\geq 1.

Hence,
\frac{1}{y^{2}}\vec{P}_{2}
is admissible of degree
(1,0,1)
, which concludes the proof of (
2.37
) for
k=2
.

- Case
k\to k+1
: Estimate (
2.38
) holds by direct inspection. Let us now assume that
\vec{S}_{k}
is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k)
and prove that
\vec{S}_{k+1}
is homogeneous of degree
(k+1,k,(k+1)\wedge 2,k+1)
. In particular, the claim immediately follows from part
(iii)
of Lemma
2.9
once we show that
\vec{F}_{k+1}
is homogeneous with

\text{deg}(\vec{F}_{k+1})=\text{deg}\left(\vec{E}_{k}+\frac{\vec{P}_{k+1}}{y^{2}}\right)=(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1).

(2.40)

From part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.10
and the a priori assumption (
2.21
), we see that
b_{1}b_{k}(\Lambda T_{k}-(k-\gamma)T_{k})
is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1)
. From part
(i)
of Lemma
2.9
and the induction hypothesis,
b_{1}\Lambda\vec{S}_{k}
is also homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1)
. By definition,
b_{1}\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{1}}
is homogeneous and has the same degree as
\vec{S}_{k}
. Thus,

\left((j-\gamma)b_{1}-\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}\right)\left(b_{1}\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{k}}{\partial b_{1}}\right)

is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1)
. From definitions (
2.29
) and (
2.30
), we derive

\text{deg}(\vec{E}_{k})=(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1),\quad k\geq 1.

It remains to check that the term
\frac{\vec{P}_{k+1}}{y^{2}}
is homogeneous of degree
(k,k-1,k\wedge 2,k+1)
. From the definition (
2.31
), we see that if
k
is even, then
P_{k+1}=0
and we are done. If
k
is odd, then we see that
\frac{P_{k+1}}{y^{2}}
is a linear combination of monomials of the form

M_{J}(y)=\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\prod_{m=2,\textup{even}}^{L-1}b_{m}^{i_{m}}T_{m}^{i_{m}}\prod_{m=2,\textup{even}}^{L+2}S_{m,1}^{j_{m}},

with

J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2}),\quad|J|_{1}=j,\;|J|_{2}=k+1,\;2\leq j\leq k+1.

Recall from part
(i)
of Lemma
2.10
that
\textup{deg}(\vec{T}_{m})=(m,m,m\wedge 2)
, we then have

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{n}T_{m}|\lesssim y^{m-m\wedge 2-\gamma-n}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,

and from the induction hypothesis and the a priori bound (
2.21
),

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{n}S_{m,1}|\lesssim b_{1}^{m}y^{m-1-m\wedge 2-\gamma-n}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty.

Together with the bound (
2.39
), we obtain the following bound at infinity,

|M_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{|J|_{2}}y^{|J|_{2}-\gamma-|J|_{1}\gamma-2-\sum_{m=2,\textup{even}}^{L+2}j_{m}}\lesssim b_{1}^{k+1}y^{k-1-\gamma}.

The control of
\partial_{y}^{n}M_{J}
follows by the Leibniz rule and the above estimates. The expansion near the origin can be checked by the same way. This concludes the proof of (
2.40
) as well as part
(ii)
of Proposition
2.13
.

\bullet
Estimate on
\vec{\Psi}_{b}
.

From (
2.35
) and (
2.36
), the expression of
\vec{\Psi}_{b}
is now reduced to

\vec{\Psi}_{b}=\vec{E}_{L+2}-\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}(\vec{R}_{1}+\vec{R}_{2}),

where
\vec{E}_{L+2}
is defined by (
2.30
),
\vec{R}_{1}=\binom{0}{R_{1}}
and
\vec{R}_{2}=\binom{0}{R_{2}}
with
R_{1},R_{2}
being given by (
2.32
) and (
2.33
). Note that the first coordinate of
\vec{\Psi}_{b}
is null, so we can write for simplicity

\vec{\Psi}_{b}=\binom{0}{\Psi_{b}}=\binom{0}{E_{L+2}-\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}(R_{1}+R_{2})}.

(2.41)

We start by controlling
\vec{E}_{L+2}
term. Since
\vec{S}_{L+2}
is homogeneous of degree
(L+2,L+1,1,L+2)
and thus so are
\Lambda\vec{S}_{L+2}
and
b_{1}\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{L+2}}{\partial b_{1}}
. This follows that
\vec{E}_{L+2}
is homogeneous of degree
(L+2,L+1,1,L+3)
. Using part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.9
yields

\textup{deg}(\mathscr{H}^{2m+2\hbar+1}\vec{E}_{L+2})=(\max\{1,L-2m-2\hbar+1\},L-2m-2\hbar,0,L+3).

From the relation
d-2\gamma-2\hbar=2\delta
(see (
1.17
)), we estimate for all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}|E_{L+2}\mathscr{L}^{m+\hbar}E_{L+2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+6}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{2L-2\gamma-2(\hbar+m)}y^{d-1}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+6}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{2(L-m+\delta)-1}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{(2L+6)-2(L-m+\delta)(1+\eta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

where
\eta=\eta(L)
,
0<\eta\ll 1
.

We now turn to the control of the term
\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}
, which is a linear combination of terms of the form (see (
2.32
))

\tilde{M}_{J}=\frac{f^{(j)}(Q)}{y^{2}}\prod_{n=2,\textup{even}}^{L-1}b_{n}^{i_{n}}T_{n}^{i_{n}}\prod_{n=2,\textup{even}}^{L+2}S_{n}^{j_{n}},

where we used the abuse notations (
2.20
) and (
2.27
), and

J=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{L},j_{2},\cdots,j_{L+2}),\;|J|_{1}=j,\;|J|_{2}\geq L+3,\;2\leq j\leq L+2.

Using the admissibility of
\vec{T}_{n}
and the homogeneity of
\vec{S}_{n}
, we get the bounds

|\tilde{M}_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{L+3}y^{|J|_{2}+j-1}\lesssim b_{1}^{L+3}y^{L+4}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to 0,

and

|\tilde{M}_{J}|\lesssim b_{1}^{|J|_{2}}y^{|J|_{2}-j\gamma-2-\gamma}\quad\text{as}\quad y\to+\infty,

where we used the fact that
j\geq 2
and
2-j\gamma<0
, and similarly for higher derivatives by the Leibniz rule. Thus, we obtain the round estimate for all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}\left|\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{m+\hbar}\left(\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2|J|_{2}}\int_{y\leq 2B_{1}}y^{2|J|_{2}-2m-2j\gamma-4+2\delta-1}dy

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}.

The term
\frac{R_{2}}{y^{2}}
is estimated exactly as for the term
\frac{R_{1}}{y^{2}}
using the definition (
2.33
). This concludes the proof of (
2.25
). The local estimate (
2.26
) directly follows from the homogeneity of
\vec{S}_{k}
and the admissibility of
\vec{T}_{k}
. This concludes the proof of Proposition
2.13
.
∎

We now proceed to a simple localization of the profile
\vec{Q}_{b}
to avoid the growth of tails in the region
y\geq 2B_{1}\gg B_{0}
. More precisely, we claim the following:

Proposition 2.15

(Estimates on the localized profile)
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.13
, we assume in addition the a priori bound

|(b_{1})_{s}|\lesssim b_{1}^{2}.

(2.42)

Consider the localized profile

\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b(s)}(y)=\vec{Q}(y)+\sum_{k=1}^{L}b_{k}\vec{\mathbf{T}}_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{L+2}\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{k}\quad\text{with}\quad\vec{\mathbf{T}}_{k}=\chi_{B_{1}}\vec{T}_{k},\;\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{k}=\chi_{B_{1}}\vec{S}_{k},

(2.43)

where
B_{1}
and
\chi_{B_{1}}
are defined as in (
1.20
) and (
1.21
). Then

\partial_{s}\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}+b_{1}\Lambda\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}-\vec{F}(\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b})=\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}+\chi_{B_{1}}\,\vec{\textup{Mod}},

(2.44)

where
\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}
satisfies the bounds:

(i)\;
(Large Sobolev bound) For all
0\leq m\leq L-1
,

\displaystyle\|\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}\|_{2m+2\hbar+2}^{2}+\int\left|\nabla^{m+\hbar+1}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\right|^{2}+\int\left|\nabla^{m+\hbar}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\right|^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+2+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta},

(2.45)

and

\displaystyle\|\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}\|_{2L+2\hbar+2}^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2L+2+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(2.46)

where
\hbar
and
\delta
are defined by (
1.17
).

(ii)
(Local bound) For all
M\leq\frac{B_{1}}{2}
and
0\leq m\leq L
,

\|\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}\|_{2m+2\hbar+2,(y\leq M)}^{2}\lesssim M^{C}b_{1}^{2L+6}.

(2.47)

(iii)
(Refined local bound near
B_{0}
) For all
0\leq m\leq L
,

\|\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}\|_{2m+2\hbar+2,(y\leq B_{0})}^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2m+4+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}.

(2.48)

Proof.

The proof is the same as Proposition 2.12 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
because the linear operator
\mathscr{L}
is the same as the one defined in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
. Although the definition of parameters

are slightly different from the ones defined in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
, the reader will have absolutely no difficulty to adapt that proof to the new situation. For that reason, we refer the reader to

for an analogous proof. We would like to mention the fact that the bound (

∎

2.4.
Study of the dynamical system for
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
.

The construction of the
\vec{Q}_{b}
profile formally leads to the finite dimensional dynamical system for
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L})
by setting to zero the inhomogeneous
\vec{\textup{Mod}}
term given in (
2.24
):

(b_{k})_{s}+(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=0,\;\;1\leq k\leq L,\;\;b_{L+1}=0.

(2.49)

The system (
2.49
) admits explicit solutions and the linearized operator near these solutions is explicit. In particular, we have the following.

Lemma 2.16

(Solution to the system (
2.49
))
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
\gamma<\ell\ll L
, and the sequence

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{1}&=\frac{\ell}{\ell-\gamma},\\
c_{k+1}&=-\frac{\gamma(\ell-k)}{\ell-\gamma}c_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell-1,\\
c_{k+1}&=0,\quad k\geq\ell.\end{array}\right.

(2.50)

Then the explicit choice

b_{k}^{e}(s)=\frac{c_{k}}{s^{k}},\quad s>0,\quad 1\leq k\leq L,

(2.51)

is a solution to (
2.49
).

The proof of Lemma
2.16
directly follows from an explicit computation which is left to the reader. We claim that the linearized flow of (
2.49
) near the solution (
2.51
) is explicit and displays
(\ell-1)
unstable directions. Note that the stability is considered in the sense that

\sup_{s}s^{k}|b_{k}(s)|\leq C_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq L.

In particular, we have the following result which was proved in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
:

Lemma 2.17

(Linearization of (
2.49
) around (
2.51
))
.

Let

b_{k}(s)=b_{k}^{e}(s)+\frac{\mathcal{U}_{k}(s)}{s^{k}},\quad 1\leq k\leq\ell,

(2.52)

and note
\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{U}_{\ell})
. Then, for
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
,

(b_{k})_{s}+(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k+1}=\frac{1}{s^{k+1}}\left[s(\mathcal{U}_{k})_{s}-(A_{\ell}\mathcal{U})_{k}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{U}|^{2})\right],

(2.53)

and

(b_{\ell})_{s}+(\ell-\gamma)b_{1}b_{\ell}=\frac{1}{s^{k+1}}\left[s(\mathcal{U}_{\ell})_{s}-(A_{\ell}\mathcal{U})_{\ell}+\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{U}|^{2})\right],

(2.54)

where

A_{\ell}=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell}\quad\text{with}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{1,1}&=\frac{\gamma(\ell-1)}{\ell-\gamma}-(1-\gamma)c_{1},\\
a_{i,i}&=\frac{\gamma(\ell-i)}{\ell-\gamma},\quad 2\leq i\leq\ell,\\
a_{i,i+1}&=1,\quad 1\leq i\leq\ell-1,\\
a_{1,i}&=-(i-\gamma)c_{i},\quad 2\leq i\leq\ell,\\
a_{i,j}&=0\quad\textup{ortherwise}\end{array}\right.

Moreover,
A_{\ell}
is diagonalizable:

A_{\ell}=P_{\ell}^{-1}D_{\ell}P_{\ell},\quad D_{\ell}=\textup{diag}\left\{-1,\frac{2\gamma}{\ell-\gamma},\frac{3\gamma}{\ell-\gamma},\cdots,\frac{\ell\gamma}{\ell-\gamma}\right\}.

(2.55)

Proof.

Since we have an analogous system as the one in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
and the proof is essentially the same as written there, we kindly refer the reader to see Lemma 3.7 in
[
Merle et al.(2015)Merle, Raphaël, and Rodnianski
]
for all details of the proof.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
assuming technical results.

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We hope that the explanation of the strategy we give in this section will be reader friendly. We proceed in 3 subsections:

- In the first subsection, we give an equivalent formulation of the linearization of the problem in the setting (
1.40
).

- In the second subsection, we prepare the initial data and define the shrinking set
\mathcal{S}_{K}
(see Definition
3.2
) such that the solution trapped in this set satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

- In the third subsection, we give all arguments of the proof of the existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}
(Proposition
3.6
) assuming an important technical result (Proposition
3.7
) whose proof is left to the next section. Then we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
Linearization of the problem.

Let
L\gg 1
be an odd integer,
s_{0}\gg 1
and
\ell>\gamma
.
We introduce the following notation

\mathbf{f}=f\chi_{B_{1}}.

We introduce the renormalized variables:

\vec{w}(y,s)=\binom{u_{1}}{\lambda u_{2}}(r,t),\quad y=\frac{r}{\lambda(t)},\quad s=s_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d\tau}{\lambda(\tau)},

(3.1)

and the decomposition

\vec{w}(y,s)=\big(\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b(s)}+\vec{q}\,\big)(y,s),

(3.2)

where
\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}
is defined by (
2.43
) and the modulation parameters

\lambda(s)>0,\quad b(s)=(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s))

are determined from the
L+1
orthogonality conditions:

\left<\vec{q},\mathscr{H}^{*k}\vec{\Phi}_{M}\right>=0,\quad 0\leq k\leq L,

(3.3)

where
\vec{\Phi}_{M}
is a fixed direction depending on some large constant
M
defined by

\vec{\Phi}_{M}=\sum_{k=0}^{L}c_{k,M}\mathscr{H}^{*k}(\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q}),

(3.4)

with

c_{0,M}=1,\quad c_{k,M}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_{j,M}\left<\mathscr{H}^{*j}(\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q}),\vec{T}_{k}\right>}{\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>},\quad 1\leq k\leq L.

(3.5)

Here,
\vec{\Phi}_{M}
is build to ensure the nondegeneracy

\left<\vec{\Phi}_{M},\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>=\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>\gtrsim M^{d-2\gamma},

(3.6)

and the cancellation

\left<\vec{\Phi}_{M},\vec{T}_{k}\right>=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_{j,M}\left<\mathscr{H}^{*j}(\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q}),\vec{T}_{k}\right>+c_{k,M}(-1)^{k}\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>=0,

(3.7)

In particular, we have

\left<\mathscr{H}^{i}\vec{T}_{k},\vec{\Phi}_{M}\right>=(-1)^{k}\left<\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>\delta_{i,k},\quad 0\leq i,k\leq L.

(3.8)

From (
2.2
), we see that
\vec{q}
satisfies

\partial_{s}\vec{q}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda\vec{q}+\mathscr{H}\vec{q}=-\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}-\vec{\mathbf{M}}+\vec{L}(\vec{q})-\vec{N}(\vec{q})\equiv\vec{\mathcal{F}},

(3.9)

where

\displaystyle\vec{\mathbf{M}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{B_{1}}\vec{\textup{Mod}}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\Lambda\vec{\mathbf{Q}}_{b}=\binom{\mathbf{M}_{1}}{\mathbf{M}_{2}},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\vec{L}(\vec{q})

\displaystyle=\binom{0}{\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}\big[\cos(2Q)-\cos(2\mathbf{Q}_{b,1})\big]q_{1}}=\binom{0}{L(q_{1})},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\vec{N}(\vec{q})

\displaystyle=\binom{0}{\frac{(d-1)}{2y^{2}}\big[\sin(2\mathbf{Q}_{b,1}+2q_{1})-\sin(2\mathbf{Q}_{b,1})-2q_{1}\cos(2\mathbf{Q}_{b,1})\big]}=\binom{0}{N(q_{1})}.

(3.12)

We also need to write the equation (
3.9
) in the original variables. To do so, let the rescaled linearized operator:

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=-\partial_{rr}-\frac{(d-1)}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}}\quad\text{with}\quad Z_{\lambda}(r)=Z\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right),

(3.13)

and the renormalized vector function

\vec{v}(r,t)=\binom{q_{1}}{\frac{1}{\lambda}q_{2}}(y,s),\quad r=\lambda y,\quad\frac{dt}{ds}=\lambda.

We compute

\partial_{t}\vec{v}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\binom{\lambda\left(\partial_{s}q_{1}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda q_{1}\right)}{\partial_{s}q_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}Dq_{2}},

then from (
3.9
),
\vec{v}
satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}\vec{v}+\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}\vec{v}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\vec{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda},

(3.14)

where

\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\vec{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}(r,t)=\binom{\lambda\mathcal{F}_{1}}{\mathcal{F}_{2}}(y,s).

Note that

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}v_{1}(r,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathscr{L}q_{1}(y,s),

(3.15)

and by the factorization of
\mathscr{L}
, we can write

\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}\mathscr{A}_{\lambda},

where

\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\partial_{r}(r^{d-1}f)+\frac{V_{\lambda}}{r}f\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}f=-\partial_{r}f+\frac{V_{\lambda}}{r}f\quad\text{with}\quad V_{\lambda}(r)=V\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right).

The reader should keep in mind that
\mathscr{H}_{\lambda}
,
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}
,
\mathscr{A}^{*}_{\lambda}
and
\mathscr{A}_{\lambda}
act on functions depending on variable
r
, while
\mathscr{H},\mathscr{L},\mathscr{A}^{*}
and
\mathscr{A}
act on functions depending on variable
y
.

.

Given

,

and

(3.20)

In particular, the initial data described in Definition

belongs to the following set which shrinks to zero as

Definition 3.2

Given

Remark 3.4
.

Note from the coercive property given in Lemma

3.3.
Existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
and conclusion of Theorem

We claim the following proposition:

Proposition 3.6

(Existence of solutions trapped in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
)
.

There exists
K_{1}\geq 1
such that for
K\geq K_{1}
, there exists
s_{0,1}(K)
such that for all
s_{0}\geq s_{0,1}
, there exists initial data for the unstable modes

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0}))\in\left[-s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)},s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\right]^{\ell-1},

such that the corresponding solution
(b(s),q(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

Let us briefly give the proof of Proposition
3.6
. Let us consider
K\geq 1
and
s_{0}\geq 1
and
(b_{1}(s_{0}),\cdots,b_{L}(s_{0}),\vec{q}(s_{0}))
as in Definition
3.1
. We introduce the exit time

s_{*}=s_{*}(b_{1}(s_{0}),\cdots,b_{L}(s_{0}),\vec{q}(s_{0}))=\sup\{s\geq s_{0}\;\text{such that}\;(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)\},

and assume that for any choice of

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0}))\in\left[-s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)},s_{0}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\right]^{\ell-1},

the exit time
s_{*}<+\infty
and look for a contradiction. By the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s_{*})
, at least one of the inequalities in that definition is an equality. Owing the following proposition, this can happens only for the components
(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*}))
. Precisely, we have the following result which is the heart of our analysis:

Proposition 3.7

(Control of
(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))
in
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
by
(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s))
)
.

There exists
K_{2}\geq 1
such that for each
K\geq K_{2}
, there exists
s_{0,2}(K)\geq 1
such that for all
s_{0}\geq s_{0,2}(k)
, the following holds: Given the initial data at
s=s_{0}
as in Definition
3.1
, if
(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, with
(b_{1}(s_{1}),\cdots,b_{L}(s_{1}),\vec{q}(s_{1}))\in\partial\mathcal{S}_{K}(s_{1})
for some
s_{1}\geq s_{0}
, then:

(i)
(Reduction to a finite dimensional problem)

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{1}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{1}))\in\partial\left[-\frac{K}{s_{1}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}},\frac{K}{s_{1}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}\right]^{\ell-1}.

(ii)
(Transverse crossing)

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\left|s^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\mathcal{V}_{i}(s)\right|^{2}\right)_{\big|_{s=s_{1}}}>0.

Let us assume Proposition
3.7
and continue the proof of Proposition
3.6
. From part
(i)
of Proposition
3.7
, we see that

(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*}))\in\partial\left[-\frac{K}{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}},\frac{K}{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}\right]^{\ell-1},

and the following mapping

\displaystyle\Upsilon:[-1,1]^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\partial\left([-1,1]^{\ell-1}\right)

\displaystyle s_{0}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}\big(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{0}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\to\frac{s_{*}^{\frac{\eta}{2}(1-\delta)}}{K}\big(\mathcal{V}_{2}(s_{*}),\cdots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell}(s_{*})\big)

is well defined. Applying the transverse crossing property given in part
(ii)
of Proposition
3.7
, we see that
(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))
leaves
\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
at
s=s_{0}
, hence,
s_{*}=s_{0}
. This is a contradiction since
\Upsilon
is the identity map on the boundary sphere and it can not be a continuous retraction of the unit ball. This concludes the proof of Proposition
3.6
, assuming that Proposition
3.7
holds.

Conclusion of Theorem
1.1
assuming Proposition
3.7

. From Proposition
3.6
, we know that there exist initial data
(b_{1}(s_{0}),\cdots,b_{L}(s_{0}),\vec{q}(s_{0}))
with
s_{0}\gg 1
such that

(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)\quad\text{for all}\quad s\geq s_{0}.

From (
4.62
), (
4.63
), we have

\displaystyle-\lambda_{t}=c(\vec{u}_{0})\lambda^{\frac{\ell-\gamma}{\ell}}\left[1+o(1)\right],

which yields

-\lambda^{-\frac{\ell-\gamma}{\ell}}\lambda_{t}=c(\vec{u}_{0})(1+o(1)).

We easily conclude that

vanishes in finite time

with the following behavior near the blowup time:

\lambda(t)=c(\vec{u}_{0})(1+o(1))(T-t)^{\frac{\ell}{\gamma}},

which is the conclusion of item
(i)
of Theorem
1.1
.

For the control of the Sobolev norms, we observe from (

) and Definition

\|\vec{q}(s)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{\Bbbk}\times\dot{H}^{\Bbbk-1}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}(s)\to 0\quad\text{as}\;\;s\to+\infty,

which concludes the proof of item

.

4.
Reduction of the problem to a finite dimensional one.

In this section, we aim at proving Proposition
3.7
which is the heart of our analysis. We proceed in three separate subsections:

- In the first subsection, we derive the laws for the parameters
(b_{1},\cdots,b_{L},\lambda)
thanks to the orthogonality condition (
3.3
) and the coercivity of the powers of
\mathscr{H}
.

- In the second subsection, we prove the main monotonicity tools for the control of the infinite dimensional part of the solution. In particular, we derive a suitable Lyapunov functional for the
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
energy as well as the monotonicity formula for the fractional Sobolev norm
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
.

- In the third subsection, we conclude the proof of Proposition
3.7
thanks to the identities obtained in the first two parts.

4.1.
Modulation equations.

We derive here the modulation equations for

. The derivation is mainly based on the orthogonality (

) and the coercivity of the powers of
\mathscr{H}

Lemma 4.1

(Estimate for
\vec{\Phi}_{M}
)
.

Given
\vec{\Phi}_{M}
as defined in (
3.4

c_{2k+1,M}=0,\quad|c_{2k,M}|\lesssim M^{2k}\quad\text{for}\;\;0\leq k\leq\frac{L-1}{2},

\int|\vec{\Phi}_{M}|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma},\quad\int|\mathscr{H}^{*k}\vec{\Phi}_{M}|^{2}\lesssim M^{C}\quad\text{for}\;k\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, we have the following orthogonality:

\big\langle\vec{\Phi}_{M},\mathscr{H}^{i}\vec{T}_{j}\big\rangle=\big\langle\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\big\rangle\delta_{i,j},\quad i\in\mathbb{N},\;1\leq j\leq L.

(4.3)

Remark 4.2
.

Since the second coordinate of
\Phi_{M}
is null, we write

\vec{\Phi}_{M}=\binom{\Phi_{M}}{0}\quad\text{with}\quad\int|\Phi_{M}|^{2}\lesssim M^{d-2\gamma}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Let us start with the proof of (
4.1

Arguing by induction, we assume that

(P_{k})\qquad c_{2j+1,M}=0,\quad|c_{2j,M}|\lesssim M^{2j},\quad 0\leq j\leq k,

and prove that
(P_{k+1})
is true, namely that we prove

c_{2k+3,M}=0,\quad|c_{2k+2,M}|\lesssim M^{2k+2}.

Indeed, by (
3.5
), (
2.17
) and (
2.20
) we write

\displaystyle c_{2k+3,M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\big\langle\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\big\rangle}\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}c_{2j,M}\big\langle\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\mathscr{H}^{2j}\vec{T}_{2k+3}\big\rangle=0.

Similarly, we use
\big\langle\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\Lambda\vec{Q}\big\rangle\sim M^{d-2\gamma}
, the induction hypothesis and
(ii)
of Lemma
2.9
to estimate

\displaystyle|c_{2k+2,M}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M^{d-2\gamma}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}|c_{2j,M}|\left|\big\langle\chi_{M}\Lambda\vec{Q},\mathscr{H}^{2j}\vec{T}_{2k+2}\big\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M^{d-2\gamma}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}M^{2j}\int_{y\leq M}y^{d-1}y^{-\gamma}y^{2(k+1-j)-\gamma}dy\lesssim M^{2(k+1)}.

Thus, the statement
(P_{k+1})
holds true.
Note from (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) that
\mathscr{H}^{*2j}=\mathscr{H}^{2j}
, we then estimate by using
(ii)
of Lemma
2.9
,

\displaystyle\int|\vec{\Phi}_{M}|^{2}

The estimate for

is obtained by a similar way. The orthogonality (

3.7

4.3
to estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}(\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}\big[b_{k,s}+(k-\gamma)b_{1}b_{k}-b_{k}\big]\left(T_{k}\delta_{k\wedge 2,1}+\sum_{j=k+1,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{k}}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\int\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}(\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q)D(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}\sum_{j=k+1,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}\left|\int\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{k}}\right)\right|+\left|\int(\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q)\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}(D(\Theta_{b})_{2})\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}\sum_{j=k+1,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}b_{1}^{j-k}\int_{y\leq 2B_{0}}y^{-2\gamma+j-L-1}+b_{1}^{L}\int_{y\leq 2B_{0}}y^{-2\gamma}+\sum_{k=2,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}b_{1}^{k}\int_{y\leq 2B_{0}}y^{k-2\gamma-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left\{b_{1}^{-2\hbar-2\delta}+b_{1}^{L-2\hbar-2\delta}+b_{1}^{1-2\hbar-2\delta}\right\}\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{-(2\hbar+\delta)}b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(2+\eta)}.

We also write

\displaystyle\big[b_{L,s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}\big]\int\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}(\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q)\left(T_{L}+\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)

\displaystyle=\big[b_{L,s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}\big]\left<\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q,(-1)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\Lambda Q+\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\right>.

Injecting all the above estimates into (
4.10
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left<\mathscr{H}^{L}\vec{q},\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\right>

\displaystyle=\big[b_{L,s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}\big]G(s)+b_{1}^{-(2\hbar+\delta)}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(2+\eta)}\right),

where we write for short

G(s)=\left<\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q,\Lambda Q+(-1)^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\right>\sim b_{1}^{-2\hbar-2\delta}.

(4.12)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\left[\frac{\left<\mathscr{H}^{L}\vec{q},\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>}{G(s)}\right]-\big[b_{L,s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(b_{1}^{4\hbar+4\delta}\left|\left<\mathscr{H}^{L}\vec{q},\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>\frac{d}{ds}G(s)\right|\right)+b_{1}^{\delta}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(2+\eta)}\right).

From (
2.17
) and (
B.1
), we estimate

\left|\left<\mathscr{H}^{L}\vec{q},\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>\right|=\left|\int\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda Q\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}(q_{2})\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\left(\int_{y\sim B_{0}}y^{-2\gamma+2+2\hbar}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{-2\hbar-\delta-1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}.

(4.13)

Note that
\left|\frac{d}{ds}\chi_{B_{0}}\right|\lesssim b_{1}
and has support on
B_{0}\leq y\leq 2B_{0}
, and that
\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}
does not depend on
b_{L}
, we can use the bounds on
b_{1},\cdots,b_{L-1}
given in Lemma
4.3
to obtain the estimate

\left|\frac{d}{ds}G(s)\right|\lesssim b_{1}b_{1}^{-2\hbar-2\delta}.

Hence, we obtain

\frac{d}{ds}\left[\frac{\left<\mathscr{H}^{L}\vec{q},\chi_{B_{0}}\Lambda\vec{Q}\right>}{G(s)}\right]-\big[b_{L,s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}\big]=b_{1}^{\delta}\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}+b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(2+\eta)}\right),

which concludes the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

4.2.
Monotonicity for
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
.

We derive in this subsection the main monotonocity formula for
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
. We claim the following which is the heart of this paper:

Proposition 4.5

(Lyapunov monotonicity for
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
)
.

Given
\hbar
,
\delta
and
\eta
as defined in (
1.17
) and (
1.20
). For
K\geq 1
, we assume that there is
s_{0}(K)\gg 1
such that
(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
for some
s_{1}\geq s_{0}
. Then, the following estimate holds for
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}+\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}+\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)}+\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}{N^{2\gamma-1}}+C(N)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}\right],

(4.14)

where

\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}=\int_{y\leq N}|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}|^{2}+\int_{y\leq N}|(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2},

(4.15)

with
N\gg 1
being a fixed constant.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
, (
3.15
) and equation (
3.14
), we write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\int q_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int q_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right]=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int v_{1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}v_{1}+\int v_{2}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\int\partial_{t}v_{1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}v_{1}+2\int\partial_{t}v_{2}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}+\int v_{1}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}]v_{1}+\int v_{2}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v_{2}

\displaystyle=2\int\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{1})_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}v_{1}+2\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}+\int v_{1}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}]v_{1}+\int v_{2}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v_{2},

(4.16)

where the commutator is defined by

\displaystyle[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{k}]f

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{k}f)-\mathscr{L}^{k}_{\lambda}(\partial_{t}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{m}\left([\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}]\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{k-1-m}f\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{m}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{k-1-m}f\right),

(4.17)

and we recall from (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}=-(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}-\mathbf{M}_{1},\quad\mathbf{M}_{1}=\chi_{B_{1}}(\textup{Mod})_{1}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)\Lambda(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}=-(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}-\mathbf{M}_{2}+L(q_{1})-N(q_{1}),\quad\mathbf{M}_{2}=\chi_{B_{1}}(\textup{Mod})_{2}-\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b_{1}\right)D(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{2}.

(4.19)

\bullet

The error term
\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}_{b}
:
we use (
2.46
) to estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\big)_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}v_{1}+\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\big((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\big)_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left|\int(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left|\int\big((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\big)_{\Bbbk}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}+\int\big((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\big)_{\Bbbk-1}(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

(4.20)

\bullet

The nonlinear term
N(q_{1})
:
we write

\left|\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\big(N(q_{1})\big)_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}\right|=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left|\int N(q_{1})\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\|(N(q_{1}))_{\Bbbk-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.21)

-
Estimate for
y<1
:
By (
3.12
), we can write

N(q_{1})=\frac{q_{1}^{2}}{y}\Phi\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi=-\frac{d-1}{y}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)\sin\big((2\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}+2\tau q_{1}\big)d\tau.

Using the expansion (
B.2
) of
\vec{q}
near the origin, we write

\frac{q_{1}^{2}}{y}=\frac{1}{y}\left(\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}c_{i}T_{i}+r_{1}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}\tilde{c}_{i}y^{i+1}+\tilde{r}_{1},

(4.22)

where

|\tilde{c}_{i}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-1}y^{j}|\partial_{y}^{j}\tilde{r}_{1}|\lesssim y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\quad\text{for}\quad y<1.

Let
\tau\in[0,1]
and

v_{\tau}=(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}+\tau q_{1}.

We obtain from Proposition
2.13
and (
B.2
) the expansion

v_{\tau}=\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}\hat{c}_{i}y^{i+1}+\hat{r}_{1},

where

|\hat{c}_{i}|\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-1}|y^{j}\partial_{y}^{j}\hat{r}_{1}|\lesssim y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad y<1.

By the Taylor expansion of
\sin(x)
at
x=0
, we write

\Phi=\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}\bar{c}_{i}y^{i}+\bar{r}_{1},

(4.23)

where

|\bar{c}_{i}|\lesssim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-1}|y^{j}\partial_{y}^{j}\bar{r}_{1}|\lesssim y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-1}\quad\text{for}\quad y<1.

Thus, we can write from (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) the expansion of
N(q_{1})
near the origin as follows:

N(q_{1})=\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}c_{i}^{\prime}y^{i+1}+r^{\prime}_{1},

(4.24)

where

|c^{\prime}_{i}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-1}|y^{j}\partial_{y}^{j}r^{\prime}_{1}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad y<1.

From the definition of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
, one can check that

|(r^{\prime}_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\frac{\partial_{y}^{i}r_{1}^{\prime}}{y^{\Bbbk-1-i}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\quad\text{for}\;\;y<1.

Using the fact that
\mathscr{A}(y)=\mathcal{O}(y^{2})
for
y<1
, we obtain

\left|\left(\sum_{i=0,\textup{even}}^{\Bbbk-1}c_{i}^{\prime}y^{i+1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|\lesssim y^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

Hence, we derive the estimate

\|\big(N(q_{1})\big)_{\Bbbk-1}\|_{L^{2}(y<1)}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(4.25)

-
Estimate for
y>1
:
Let us rewrite from the definition (
3.12
) of
N(q_{1})
,

N(q_{1})=Z^{2}\psi,\quad Z=\frac{q_{1}}{y},\quad\psi=-(d-1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tau)\sin(2(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}+2\tau q_{1})d\tau.

(4.26)

Note from the definitions of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad|f_{k}|\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}f|}{y^{k-i}},

from which and the Leibniz rule, we write

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}\left|\big(N(q_{1})\big)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}N(q_{1})|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2k-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}Z^{2}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2k-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{i}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2k-2}}.

We aim at using (
B.3
) and (
B.6
) to prove that for
0\leq k\leq\Bbbk-1
,
0\leq i\leq k
and
0\leq m\leq i
,

A_{k,i,m}:=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-m}Z|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2k-2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

(4.27)

from which and (
4.25
), we derive the estimate

\|\big(N(q_{1})\big)_{\Bbbk-1}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim b_{1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

(4.28)

Let us prove (
4.27
). We distinguish in 3 cases:

-
The initial case
k=0
, then
m=i=k=0
.
From (
4.26
), it is obvious to see that
|\psi|
is uniformly bounded. We estimate from (
B.3
) and (
B.8
),

\displaystyle A_{0,0,0}=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|q_{1}|^{4}|\psi|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk+2}}y^{d-1}dy\lesssim\left\|\frac{q_{1}}{y}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

-
Case
k\geq 1
and
k-i=0
. We first use the Leibniz rule to write

\forall l\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\partial_{y}^{l}Z|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{l}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2+2l-2j}},

(4.29)

from which and the uniform bound of
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k,k,m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{k-m}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{k-m}B_{j,l,0}.

where

B_{j,l,0}=\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq j+l\leq\Bbbk-1.

We consider two cases:

- If
0\leq j\leq\frac{L+1}{2}
, we estimate from (
B.8
) and (
B.5
),

\displaystyle B_{j,l,0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2l-2j}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}b_{1}^{2j+2+\frac{2\gamma(j+1)}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{j}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{1-\frac{j}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)},

where we used the following fact

2j+\frac{2\gamma(j+1)}{L}-\frac{j(2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta))}{\Bbbk-\sigma}=\frac{2\gamma}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)>0.

- If
j\geq\frac{L+1}{2}+1
, then
l\leq\Bbbk-1-j\leq\frac{L-3}{2}+\hbar
. We simply change the role of
j
and
l
in the above estimate resulting in the same estimate.

-
Case
k\geq 1
and
k-i\geq 1
.
Let us write from (
4.27
) and (
4.29
),

A_{k,m,i}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{i-m}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k-i}\psi|^{2}}{y^{-2(k-i)}}.

(4.30)

At this stage, we need to precise the decay of
|\partial_{y}^{n}\psi|
to archive the bound (
4.27
). To do so, let us recall that
\vec{T}_{i}
is admissible of degree
(i,i,i\wedge 2)
(see Lemma
2.10
) and
\vec{S}_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
(i,i-1,i\wedge 2,i)
(see Proposition
2.13
). We estimate for
j\geq 1
and
y\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}^{j}(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\partial_{y}^{j}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}b_{2i}T_{2i}+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{L+1}{2}}S_{2i}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{\gamma+j}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{L-1}{2}}\frac{b_{1}^{2i}y^{2i}}{y^{\gamma+j}}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\leq 2B_{1}\}}\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{\gamma+j}}.

Let
\tau\in[0,1]
and
v_{\tau}=(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}+\tau q_{1}
. We use the Faa di Bruno formula to write for
1\leq n\leq\Bbbk-1
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}^{n}\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{m^{*}=n}|\partial_{v_{\tau}}^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}}\sin(v_{\tau})|^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{y}^{i}(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{1}+\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2m_{i}}d\tau

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{|m|_{2}=n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{i}},\quad|m|_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}.

(4.31)

Hence, we need to estimate terms of the form

B_{i,j,n}:=\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2-2n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{i}},

(4.32)

where
(j,l,n)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
m_{i}\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
satisfying

1\leq j+l+n\leq\Bbbk-1,\quad|m|_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}=n.

We consider two cases:

- Case 1:
m_{i}=0
for
L-2\leq i\leq n
(if
n<L-2
then we are in this case as well). We now use (
B.7
) with
p=0
to estimate

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{i}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+b_{1}^{2i}\right)^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+\frac{1}{y^{2i}}\right)^{m_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq y\leq B_{0}\}}+\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(b_{1}^{-C\eta+2\gamma+2i}+b_{1}^{2i}\right)^{m_{i}}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\geq B_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{2n}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq y\leq B_{0}\}}+b_{1}^{2n}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\geq B_{0}\}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle B_{j,l,n}\lesssim\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}+b_{1}^{2n}\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2-2n}}

By the similar estimate as for
B_{i,l,0}
, we derive the bound

B_{j,l,n}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

- Case 2: there exists
i_{*}\in\{L-2,\cdots,n\}
(
n\leq L-3
, this case does not occur) such that
m_{i_{*}}\neq 0
. Since
L\gg 1
and the fact that

0\leq\sum_{i=1,i\neq i_{*}}^{n}im_{i}=n-i_{*}m_{i_{*}}\leq\Bbbk-1-(L-2)m_{i_{*}},

we deduce that

m_{i_{*}}=1,\quad m_{i}=0\quad\text{for}\quad\hbar+2\leq i\neq i_{*}\leq n.

We then write

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{i}}=\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i_{*}}}+|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\hbar+2}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i_{*}}}+|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\hbar+2}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C(L)\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2i}}+b_{1}^{2i}\right)^{m_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2n}}+\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2n-2i_{*}}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq y\leq B_{0}\}}+\left(b_{1}^{2n+2\gamma-C\eta}+b_{1}^{2n-2i^{*}}|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\geq B_{0}\}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle B_{j,l,n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1\leq y\leq B_{0}}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2}}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{2\gamma}}+\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{-2i_{*}}}\right)

\displaystyle+b_{1}^{2n}\int_{y\geq B_{0}}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2l+2-2n}}\left(b_{1}^{2\gamma-C\eta}+b_{1}^{-2i^{*}}|\partial_{y}^{i_{*}}q_{1}|^{2}\right).

Since
i_{*}\geq L-3
, we can use the interpolation bound (
B.5
) to control
\int|\partial_{y}^{i^{*}}q_{1}|^{2}
directly, then the rest terms are controlled by the
L^{\infty}
bound (
B.8
) resulting in

B_{j,l,n}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

This concludes the proof of (
4.27
) as well as (
4.28
).

The small linear term
L(q_{1})
:

\left|\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\big(L(q_{1})\big)_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}\right|=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left|\int L(q_{1})\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\|(L(q_{1}))_{\Bbbk-1}\|_{L^{2}}.

We claim that

(4.33)

Let us rewrite from (

) the definition of

,

L(q_{1})=\Phi q_{1}\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi=\frac{(d-1)}{y^{2}}\left[\cos(2Q)-\cos(2Q+2(\mathbf{\Theta}_{b})_{1})\right],

where

(\mathbf{\Theta}_{b})_{1}=\sum_{i=1,\textup{even}}^{L}b_{i}T_{i}\chi_{B_{1}}+\sum_{i=2,\textup{even}}^{L+2}S_{i}(b,y)\chi_{B_{1}}.

From the asymptotic behavior of

given in (

), the admissibility of
\vec{T}_{i}
and the homogeneity of

, we deduce that

is a regular function both at the origin and at infinity. We then apply the Leibniz rule (

) with

to write

(\Phi q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\big(q_{1}\big)_{m}\Phi_{\Bbbk-1,\Bbbk-1-m},

where

with

are defined by the recurrence relation given in Lemma

. In particular, we have the following estimate

|\Phi_{\Bbbk-1,\Bbbk-1-m}|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2(1-C(L)\eta)}}{1+y^{2\gamma+\Bbbk-1-m}}\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq m\leq\Bbbk-1.

Hence, from the coercivity bound (
B.1
) and
2\gamma-1\geq 1
, we estimate

\displaystyle\int|L(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2(1-\eta)}\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{m}|^{2}}{1+y^{4\gamma-2+2(\Bbbk-m)}}\lesssim b_{1}^{4(1-C(L)\eta)}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

which concludes the proof of (

\displaystyle\left|\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\big(L(q_{1})\big)_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}v_{2}\right|\lesssim\frac{b_{1}^{2(1-C(L)\eta)}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(4.34)

The commutator term:
By (

), we write

\displaystyle\int v_{1}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}]v_{1}+\int v_{2}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v_{2}

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+2}}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int q_{1}\mathscr{L}^{m}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1-m}q_{1}\right)+\sum_{m=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int q_{2}\mathscr{L}^{m}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-2-m}q_{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\left\|\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}(q_{1})_{2m}\right)_{\Bbbk-2-2m}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\left\|\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}(q_{2})_{2m}\right)_{\Bbbk-3-2m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right],

where we used in the last line the fact that
\left|\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\right|\sim b_{1}
from the modulation equation (
4.5
) and the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. We note from (
1.23
) and (

) that

\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}d_{i}y^{2i+1}+\mathcal{O}(y^{2k+3})\quad\text{for}\quad y\to 0,

\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{j}\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{2\gamma+1+j}}\quad\text{for}\quad y\to+\infty.

B.2
with

, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\big(\phi(q_{1})_{2}m\big)_{\Bbbk-2-2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-2-2m}(q_{1})_{2m+j}\phi_{\Bbbk-2-2m,\Bbbk-2-2m-j},

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\big(\phi(q_{2})_{2}m\big)_{\Bbbk-3-2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-3-2m}(q_{2})_{2m+j}\phi_{\Bbbk-3-2m,\Bbbk-3-2m-j},

where we compute from the recurrence formula of Lemma
B.2
,

\phi_{\Bbbk-2-2m,\Bbbk-2-2m-j}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+y^{2\gamma+1+\Bbbk-2-2m-j}},\quad\phi_{\Bbbk-3-2m,\Bbbk-3-2m-j}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+y^{2\gamma+1+\Bbbk-3-2m-j}}.

We then use the coercivity bound of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
given in Lemmas
A.3
and
A.3
to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\left\|\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}(q_{1})_{2m}\right)_{\Bbbk-2-2m}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\left\|\left(\frac{\Lambda Z}{y^{2}}(q_{2})_{2m}\right)_{\Bbbk-3-2m}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-2-2m}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{2m+j}|^{2}}{1+y^{2(2\gamma-1+\Bbbk-2m-j)}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-3-2m}\int\frac{|(q_{2})_{2m+j}|^{2}}{1+y^{2(2\gamma-1+\Bbbk-2m-j-1)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk+1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-2-2m}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}|^{2}}{1+y^{2(2\gamma-1)}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\left[\frac{\Bbbk-1}{2}\right]-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\Bbbk-3-2m}\int\frac{|(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2(2\gamma-1)}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\lesssim\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}{N^{2(2\gamma-1)}}+\left\|\frac{(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}}{1+y^{2\gamma-1}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y\leq N)}+\left\|\frac{(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}}{1+y^{2\gamma-1}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y\leq N)}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int v_{1}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk}]v_{1}+\int v_{2}[\partial_{t},\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}^{\Bbbk-1}]v_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}}{N^{2\gamma-1}}+\left\|\frac{(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}}{1+y^{2\gamma-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(y\leq N)}+\left\|\frac{(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}}{1+y^{2\gamma-1}}\right\|_{L^{2}(y\leq N)}\right],

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}{N^{2\gamma-1}}+C(N)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}\right],

(4.35)

where

is defined by (

The modulation term:
Let us introduce the vector function

\vec{\Upsilon}=C_{\Upsilon}\left(\vec{T}_{L}+\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{L+1}}{\partial b_{L}}+\frac{\partial\vec{S}_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\chi_{B_{1}},

(4.36)

where

(4.37)

The size of the coefficient
C_{\Upsilon}
is computed from (
4.12
) and (
4.13
). The introduction of
\chi_{L}
is to take advantage of the improved modulation equation (
4.9
). Let us write

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathbf{M}_{1})_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}_{\lambda}v_{1}+\int\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(\mathbf{M}_{2})_{\lambda}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}_{\lambda}v_{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\int\mathbf{M}_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int\mathbf{M}_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\int\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\int\left(\mathbf{M}_{1}-\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{1}\right)\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int\left(\mathbf{M}_{2}-\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{2}\right)\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\Sigma_{0}\right]+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[(2\Bbbk-d)\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Sigma_{0}-\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}\right]

where
\mathbf{M}_{1}
and
\mathbf{M}_{2}
are introduced in (
4.18
) and (
4.19

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle=\int\Upsilon_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int\Upsilon_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\Upsilon_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\Upsilon_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\int\Upsilon_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\Upsilon_{2},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\mathbf{M}_{1}-\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{1}\right)\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}q_{1}+\int\left(\mathbf{M}_{2}-\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{2}\right)\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}q_{2},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\Upsilon_{1}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk}\big(\partial_{s}q_{1}+\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{1}\big)+\int\Upsilon_{2}\mathscr{L}^{\Bbbk-1}\big(\partial_{s}q_{2}+\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{2}\big).

We claim that

|\Sigma_{0}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}b_{1}^{\eta(1-\delta)},\quad|\Sigma_{1}|+|\Sigma_{2}|\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}b_{1}^{1+\eta(1-\delta)}+\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(4.38)

from which and

(4.39)

\Sigma_{0}
. By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we write

|\Sigma_{0}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\left(\|(\Upsilon_{1})_{\Bbbk}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\Upsilon_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\|(\Upsilon_{1})_{\Bbbk}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|(\Upsilon_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

\vec{T}_{k}
, the homogeneity of
\vec{S}_{k}
together with the fact that
\mathscr{L}^{\frac{L+1}{2}}T_{L}=0

\displaystyle\|(\Upsilon_{1})_{\Bbbk}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|(\Upsilon_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim C_{\Upsilon}^{2}\left[\int\left|\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{L+1}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)_{\Bbbk}\right|^{2}+\int\left|\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\left(T_{L}+\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim b_{1}^{2\delta-2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\left[b_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{(2-2\delta)(1+\eta)}+b_{1}^{(2-2\delta)(1+\eta)}+b_{1}^{4}b_{1}^{-2\delta(1+\eta)}\right]\quad\lesssim b_{1}^{2\eta(1-\delta)}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(4.40)

This concludes the proof of (
4.38
) for
\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{2}-\partial_{s}\Upsilon_{2}

\displaystyle+\left[(b_{L})_{s}+(L-\gamma)b_{1}b_{L}-\frac{d}{ds}C_{\Upsilon}\right]\left(T_{L}+\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\chi_{B_{1}}-C_{\Upsilon}\frac{d}{ds}\left[\left(T_{L}+\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\chi_{B_{1}}\right].

and

\displaystyle\left\|\big(D(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{2}\big)_{\Bbbk-1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},\quad\left\|\left(\frac{d}{ds}\left(T_{L}+\frac{\partial S_{L+2}}{\partial b_{L}}\right)\chi_{B_{1}}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}.

\Sigma_{1}

) into the identity (
4.16
) yields the formula (
4.14
). This concludes the proof of Proposition
4.5
.
∎

4.3.
Local Morawetz control.

We establish in this subsection the so-called Morawetz type identity in order to control the local term
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\text{loc}}
involved in the formula (
4.14
). In particular, we have the following:

Proposition 4.6

(Local Morawetz control)
.

Let
0<\nu\ll 1
and
A\gg 1
be small and large enough constants, we define

\phi_{A}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\chi_{A}(\xi)\xi^{1-\nu}d\xi,

(4.43)

and

Mc=-\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}.

(4.44)

Then the following bounds hold for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
for
s_{0}
large enough,

|Mc|\leq C(A,M)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

(4.45)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{Mc}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\right]\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left(\frac{\nu}{2N^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}-\frac{C(M)}{A^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}-C(A,M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right),

(4.46)

where the large constants
M
,
N
and
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}
are introduced in (
3.4
) and (
4.15
).

Proof.

The estimate (
4.45
) simply follows from the coercivity bound (
B.1
). We aim at proving the bound

\frac{d}{ds}Mc\geq\frac{\nu}{2N^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}-\frac{C(M)}{A^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}-C(A,M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)},

(4.47)

which immediately implies (
4.46
). Indeed, from
-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\sim b_{1}
and the bound (
4.45
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{Mc}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d}}\right]=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\frac{d}{ds}Mc-\frac{(2\Bbbk-d)\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}\lambda}Mc

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{1}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\left[\frac{\nu}{2N^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}-\frac{C(M)}{A^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}-C(A,M)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right]-C(A,M)\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\Bbbk-d+1}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

Since
b_{1}(s)\leq b_{1}(s_{0})\sim\frac{1}{s_{0}}
, we can take
s_{0}=s_{0}(A)
large such that
b_{1}(s_{0})\leq\frac{1}{A}
, then the estimate (
4.46
) follows.

Let us give the proof of (
4.47
). We first claim the following:

Lemma 4.7

(Morawetz type identity at the linear level)
.

Let
A\gg 1
and
0<\nu\ll 1
, there holds the following:

\displaystyle\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\mathscr{L}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\big](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\quad\gtrsim\frac{\nu}{N^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk,\textup{loc}}-\frac{1}{A^{\nu}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(4.48)

Proof.

The proof follows exactly the same lines as Lemma 3.8 in
[
C.(2016b)
]
because we have the same definition
\phi_{A}
and a similar structure of the linear operator
\mathscr{L}
. Although the potential
\frac{Z}{y^{2}}
is different from the one defined in
[
C.(2016b)
]
, it still satisfies

\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{y}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}\right)\geq-\frac{1}{y^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)^{2}-\kappa(d)\right]\quad\text{for some $\kappa(d)>0$ and $d\geq 7$},

thanks to the asymptotic behavior (
2.4
) and the fact that
\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-(d-1)\geq\frac{1}{4}
for
d\geq 7
. For that reason, we refer the interested reader to
[
C.(2016b)
]
for details of the proof.
∎

We now use the identity (
4.48
) to derive the formula (
4.47
). We compute from the definition of
Mc
and the equation (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}Mc

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla\left(q_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda q_{1}-(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}-\mathbf{M}_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle-\int\frac{1-\nu}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}\left(q_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda q_{1}-(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}-\mathbf{M}_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle+\int\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\left(\mathscr{L}q_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}Dq_{2}+(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}+\mathbf{M}_{2}-L(q_{1})+N(q_{1})\right)_{\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle+\int\frac{1-\nu}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\left(\mathscr{L}q_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}Dq_{2}+(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}+\mathbf{M}_{2}-L(q_{1})+N(q_{1})\right)_{\Bbbk-1}.

(4.49)

From the definitions of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
, the coercivity bound (
B.1
),
-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\sim b_{1}
and the compactness of the support of
\nabla\phi_{A}
and
\Delta\phi_{A}
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla\left(\Lambda q_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}\left(\Lambda q_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\big](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\left(Dq_{2}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|\quad\lesssim C(A)b_{1}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

Again from the compactness of the support of
\nabla\phi_{A}
and
\Delta\phi_{A}
, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the local bound (
2.47
) for
\vec{\mathbf{\Psi}}
to obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla\left((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}\left((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{1}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\big](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\left((\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|\quad\lesssim C(A)\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+3}.

For the small linear term
L(q_{1})
and the nonlinear term
N(q_{1})
, we use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, the bounds (
4.33
) and (
4.28
) to estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\left(L(q_{1})-N(q_{1})\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|\quad\lesssim C(A)b_{1}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

It remains to control the modulation term. We use the fact that
\chi_{B_{1}}T_{i}=T_{i}
for
y\leq 2A\ll B_{1}
and
(T_{i})_{\Bbbk-1}=0
for
1\leq i\leq L
to deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,\textup{even}}^{L}\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla\left(T_{i}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}\left(T_{i}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\big](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1,\textup{odd}}^{L}\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\left(\chi_{B_{1}}T_{i}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}=0.

For the term
\frac{\partial S_{j}}{b_{i}}
for
j\geq i+1
, we recall that
S_{j}
is homogeneous of degree
(j,j-1,j\wedge 2,j)
. Thus,
\left|\frac{\partial S_{j}}{b_{i}}\right|\lesssim C(A)b_{1}
for
y\leq 2A
. We then use Lemma
4.3
to derive the bound for
1\leq i\leq L
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i+1,\textup{even}}^{L+2}\left|\big[(b_{i})_{s}+(i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{i}-b_{i+1}\big]\int\left[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right](q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=i+1,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}\left|\big[(b_{i})_{s}+(i-\gamma)b_{1}b_{i}-b_{i+1}\big]\int\big[\nabla\phi_{A}.\nabla(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}+\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\Delta\phi_{A}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}\big]\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{i}}\right)_{\Bbbk-1}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim C(A,M)\left(b_{1}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}+\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}b_{1}^{L+2+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\right).

Injecting all the above estimates and identity (
4.48
) to (
4.49
) yields the formula (
4.47
). This concludes the proof of Proposition
4.6
.
∎

4.4.
Monotonicity for
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
.

We now in the position to derive the monotonicity formula for
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
. We claim the following.

Proposition 4.8

(Lyapunov monotonicity for
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
)
.

Given
\hbar
,
\delta
and
\eta
as defined in (
1.17
) and (
1.20
). For
K\geq 1
, we assume that there is
s_{0}(K)\gg 1
such that
(b_{1}(s),\cdots,b_{L}(s),\vec{q}(s))\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(s)
for
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
for some
s_{1}\geq s_{0}
. Then, the followings hold for
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d}}\right)\leq\frac{b_{1}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d+1}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}}b_{1}^{\frac{\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}\left[b_{1}^{\frac{1}{L}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\right)}+b_{1}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\right].

(4.50)

Proof.

We compute from definition of
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
and equation (
3.14
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d}}\right]=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int|\nabla^{\sigma}v_{1}|^{2}+\int|\nabla^{\sigma-1}v_{2}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\int\nabla^{\sigma}v_{1}.\nabla^{\sigma}\left(v_{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{1})_{\lambda}\right)+2\int\nabla^{\sigma-1}v_{2}.\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(-\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}v_{1}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=-2\int\nabla^{\sigma-1}v_{2}.\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{Z_{\lambda}}{r^{2}}v_{1}\right)+2\int\nabla^{\sigma}v_{1}.\nabla^{\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}_{1})_{\lambda}\right)+2\int\nabla^{\sigma-1}v_{2}.\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{2})_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\frac{\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}}}{\lambda^{2\sigma-d+1}}\left\{\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right\},

(4.51)

where
Z
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are defined in (
1.23
) and (
3.9
).

- Estimate for the potential term:
From the expansion (
B.2
), we have

\int_{y\leq 1}\left|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

For
y\geq 1
, we note from the asymptotic behavior (
2.4
) that
\left|\partial_{y}^{j}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{2+j}}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We then use the Leibniz rule and interpolation bound (
B.5
) with
\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor+2>\sigma
to obtain the estimates

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}\left|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-2}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor+2-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}},\quad\int_{y\geq 1}\left|\nabla^{\Bbbk-1}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

By interpolation and the bootstrap bounds given in Definition (
3.2
), we have

\int\left|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\leq C\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-2}{\Bbbk-1-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{1}{\Bbbk-1-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}}\leq CK^{2}b_{1}^{\frac{2\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\right)}b_{1}^{\frac{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}{L+\hbar-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}}.

Since
\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\ll 1
, we note that
\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor\geq\frac{d-3}{2}=\gamma+\hbar+\delta-\frac{3}{2}
. We then compute the exponent

\displaystyle\frac{2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}{L+\hbar-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}

\displaystyle=2\left[1+\frac{(1-\delta)(1+\eta)+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-\hbar}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2\left[1+\frac{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}+\eta(1-\delta)}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)\right].

Since
b_{1}(s)\sim\frac{1}{s}\leq\frac{1}{s_{0}}
, we can take
s_{0}=s_{0}(K)
large enough to obtain the bound

\int\left|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\frac{Z}{y^{2}}q_{1}\right)\right|^{2}\leq b_{1}^{\frac{2\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}b_{1}^{2+\frac{2\gamma-1}{2L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\right)}.

(4.52)

We now turn to the estimate for the last term in (
4.51
). We only deal with the
\mathcal{F}_{2}
term since the same estimate holds for
\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Let us recall form (
3.9
),

\mathcal{F}_{2}=-(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}-\mathbf{M}_{2}+L(q_{1})-N(q_{1}).

- Estimate for the error term
(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}
:
we apply (
2.45
) with
j=\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-\hbar
and
j=\lfloor\sigma\rfloor-\hbar
to find that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-2\hbar+2+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}\lesssim b_{1}^{2\gamma+1-C_{L}\eta},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma\rfloor}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{2\lfloor\sigma\rfloor-2\hbar+2+2(1-\delta)-C_{L}\eta}\lesssim b_{1}^{2\gamma+3-C_{L}\eta},

where we used the fact that
2\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor\geq d-3
and
2\lfloor\sigma\rfloor\geq d-1
for
\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\ll 1
and
d=2\gamma+2\hbar+2\delta
from (
1.17
).
Note that
\lfloor\sigma\rfloor-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor=1
and
\sigma-1-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor\geq\sigma-\frac{d}{2}
for
\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|\ll 1
, we have by interpolation

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{b})_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{(2\gamma+1-C_{L}\eta)(\lfloor\sigma\rfloor-\sigma+1)}b_{1}^{(2\gamma+3-C_{L}\eta)(\sigma-1-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor)}

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{2\gamma+1-C_{L}\eta}b_{1}^{2(\sigma-1-\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor)}\lesssim b_{1}^{2}b_{1}^{\frac{2\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}b_{1}^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{2}},

(4.53)

for
\eta
and
\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|
small enough.

- Estimate for the modulation term
\mathbf{M}_{2}
:
From Lemma
4.3
, the admissibility of
\vec{T}_{k}
and homogeneity of
\vec{S}_{k}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\mathbf{M}_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\left[\sum_{k=1,\textup{odd}}^{L}\left(\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\chi_{B_{1}}T_{k}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{j=k+1,\textup{odd}}^{L+2}\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(\chi_{B_{1}}\frac{\partial S_{j}}{\partial b_{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}\left(D(\mathbf{Q}_{b})_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}B_{1}^{L-\sigma+\hbar+\delta}+b_{1}^{L+1+(1-\delta)(1+\eta)}b_{1}^{2(1-\eta)}B_{1}^{\hbar+\delta}\lesssim b_{1}b_{1}^{\frac{\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}b_{1}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},

(4.54)

for
\eta
and
\left|\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right|
small enough.

- Estimate for the small linear term
L(q_{1})
:
From the asymptotic behavior (
2.4
) of
Q
and the definition (
3.11
) of
L(q_{1})
, we have by Leibniz rule

\forall j\in\mathbb{N},\quad\left|\partial_{y}^{j}(L(q_{1}))\right|\lesssim b_{1}^{2(1-C(L)\eta)}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|}{1+y^{2\gamma+j-i}}.

From the bounds (
B.3
) and (
B.5
), we have the estimate

\int|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}L(q_{1})|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-(2\gamma+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor)}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{2\gamma+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}},\quad\int|\nabla^{\Bbbk-2}L(q_{1})|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

By interpolation and the same computation of the exponent as for the potential term, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}L(q_{1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2+\frac{2\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}b_{1}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}.

(4.55)

- Estimate for the nonlinear term
N(q_{1})
:
We claim that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}N(q_{1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim b_{1}^{2+\frac{2\ell}{\ell-\gamma}\left(\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\right)}b_{1}^{\frac{\gamma}{2L}}.

(4.56)

For
y<1
, we use the expansion (
4.24
) to deduce that

\left\|\nabla^{\sigma-1}N(q_{1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y<1)}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

For
y\geq 1
, we shall control
\left\|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}N(q_{1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y\geq 1)}
and
\left\|\nabla^{\lfloor\sigma\rfloor}N(q_{1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(y\geq 1)}
, then obtain the result by interpolation.
From (
4.26
), the Leibniz rule and estimate (
4.31
), we write

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}|\partial_{y}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}N(q_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-k}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}\psi|^{2}}{y^{4+2(j-i)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-k}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{4+2(j-i)}}\prod_{l=1}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2l}}+|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{l}},

where
\mathcal{I}=\{m\in\mathbb{N}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}:\sum_{l=1}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}lm_{l}=[\sigma-1]-j\}
.

From the Hardy inequality (
A.3
) and (
B.5
), we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}}{y^{-k+\sigma-\frac{d}{2}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k+1}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2(-k-1+\sigma)}}+|\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}(1)|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,\cdots,[\sigma-1].

(4.57)

from which we derive for every
m\in\mathcal{I}
,

\displaystyle\frac{y^{2(\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j)}}{y^{\sum_{l}m_{l}(2\sigma-d)}}\prod_{l=1}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2l}}+|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}\right)^{m_{l}}=\prod_{l=1}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-j}\left(\frac{b_{1}^{-C\eta}}{y^{2\gamma+2\sigma-d}}+\frac{|\partial_{y}^{l}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{-2l+2\sigma-d}}\right)^{m_{l}}\lesssim b_{1}^{-C\eta}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{y\geq 1}|\partial_{y}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}N(q_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}}b_{1}^{-C\eta}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}|^{2}|\partial_{y}^{i-k}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{4-2i+2\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-C(2\sigma-d)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\sum_{k=0}^{i}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{I}}b_{1}^{-C\eta}\left\|\frac{|\partial_{y}^{k}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{-2k+2\sigma-d}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i-k}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{4-2i+2k+2\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-C(2\sigma-d)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim b_{1}^{-C\eta}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-(2+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-C(2\sigma-d))}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{2+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-\sigma-C(2\sigma-d)}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{2+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}b_{1}^{2\big(2+\lfloor\sigma-1\rfloor-\sigma\big)\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2L}\right)},

where in the last estimate, we used the bootstrap bound on
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
given in Definition (
3.2
), the smallness
|2\sigma-d|+\eta=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)
from (

i=0,1,2,\quad\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2\alpha})}<+\infty,

then

i=0,1,2,\quad\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}^{*}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}\geq c_{\alpha}\left(\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2\alpha})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2\alpha})}\right),

(A.5)

for some
c_{\alpha}>0
.

Proof.

See Lemma A.2 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
.
∎

We also have the following coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
.

Lemma A.3

(Weight coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
)
.

Let
p\geq 0
and
i=0,1,2
such that
|2p+2i-(d-2-2\gamma)|\neq 0
, where
\gamma\in(1,2]
is defined by (

).
For all

with

\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2p})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2p})}<+\infty,

and

\left<f,\Phi_{M}\right>=0\quad\text{if}\quad 2i+2p>d-2\gamma-2,

(A.6)

where
\Phi_{M}
is defined in (
3.4
), we have

\int\frac{|\mathscr{A}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2p})}\gtrsim\int\frac{|\partial_{y}f|^{2}}{y^{2i}(1+y^{2p})}+\int\frac{f^{2}}{y^{2i+2}(1+y^{2p})}.

(A.7)

Proof.

See Lemma A.3 in
[
Ghoul et al.(2018a)Ghoul, Ibrahim, and Nguyen
]
.
∎

From the coercivity estimates of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
given in Lemmas
A.3
and
A.2
, we can turn to the core of this part: the coercivity of the adapted norm
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
. In particular, we have the following.

Lemma A.4

(Coercivity of
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
)
.

Let
L\gg 1
be an odd integer and
\Bbbk
be defined as in (
1.18
), there exists a constant
c=c(L,M)>0
such that for all radially symmetric vector function
\vec{q}
satisfying

\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{k}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-2-2k})}+\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int\frac{|(q_{2})_{k}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-4-2k)}}<+\infty,

(A.8)

and

\big\langle\vec{q},\mathscr{H}^{*i}\vec{\Phi}_{M}\big\rangle=0\quad\text{for}\quad 0\leq i\leq L,

(A.9)

there holds:

\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{k}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-2-2k})}+\sum_{k=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int\frac{|(q_{2})_{k}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-4-2k)}}\leq c\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(A.10)

Proof.

By (
2.18
), we see that the condition (
A.9
) is equivalent to

\big\langle\mathscr{L}^{i}q_{1},\Phi_{M}\big\rangle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\big\langle\mathscr{L}^{i}q_{2},\Phi_{M}\big\rangle=0\quad\text{for}\;\;0\leq i\leq\frac{L-1}{2}.

(A.11)

Recall that

\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}=\int|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}|^{2}+\int|(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}.

(A.12)

We will write indifferently
q
to denote
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
, and try to control the term of the form

\int|q_{k}|^{2}
with
k=\Bbbk
or
k=\Bbbk-1
. Let us rewrite

q_{k}=\mathscr{A}q_{k-1}\;\text{or}\;q_{k}=\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{k-1},

and apply Lemma
A.3
or Lemma
A.2
with
i=p=0
to find that

\int|q_{k}|^{2}\gtrsim\int\frac{|q_{k-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}.

If
k-1=0
, we are done, if not, we repeat this step again by writing

q_{k-1}=\mathscr{A}^{*}q_{k-2}\;\text{or}\;q_{k-1}=\mathscr{A}q_{k-2},

and so forth. Note that
\frac{1}{y^{2}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}
and that the orthogonal condition in Lemma
A.3
is fulfilled thanks to (
A.11
). Then, applying Lemma
A.3
or Lemma
A.2
with the appropriate values of
i
and
p
would give

\int|q_{k}|^{2}\gtrsim\int\frac{|q_{k-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\gtrsim\cdots\gtrsim\int\frac{|q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2k-2})}\gtrsim\int\frac{|q|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2k-1})}.

This concludes the proof of Lemma
A.4
.
∎

Appendix B
Interpolation bounds.

We derive in this section interpolation bounds on
\vec{q}
which are the consequence of the coercivity property given in Lemma
A.4
. We have the following:

Lemma B.1

(Interpolation bounds)
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
and
\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
satisfy the bootstrap bounds in Definition
3.2
and that
\vec{q}
satisfies the orthogonal condition (
3.3
), there holds:

(i)
Weighted bounds for
\vec{q}
:

\int|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}|^{2}+\int|(q_{2})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{i}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i-2})}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|(q_{2})_{i}|^{2}}{y^{2}(1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i-4})}\leq c(M)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

(B.1)

(ii)
Expansion near the origin: for
y<1
,

\vec{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{\Bbbk}c_{i}\vec{T}_{\Bbbk-i}+\vec{r},\quad|c_{i}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}},

(B.2)

where
\vec{T}_{k}
is defined as in (
2.19
) for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\vec{r}
satisfies the bounds

\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}|y^{i}\partial_{y}^{i}r_{1}|\lesssim y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-2}|y^{i}\partial_{y}^{i}r_{2}|\lesssim y^{\Bbbk-1-\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}.

(iii)
Weighted bounds for
\partial_{y}^{i}\vec{q}
:

\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{2}|^{2}}{1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i-2}}\leq c(M)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

(B.3)

hence,

\|\vec{q}\,\|_{\dot{H}^{\Bbbk}\times\dot{H}^{\Bbbk-1}}^{2}\leq c(M)\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\vec{q}\,\|_{\dot{H}^{\beta}\times\dot{H}^{\beta-1}}^{2}\leq c(M)\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-\beta}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{\beta-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\quad\text{for}\quad\sigma\leq\beta\leq\Bbbk.

(B.4)

Moreover, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
p>0
satisfying
\sigma\leq j+p\leq\Bbbk
, we have

\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2p}}\leq c(M)\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-(j+p)}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{(j+p)-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}.

(B.5)

(iv)
Weighted
L^{\infty}
control: Let
a>0
satisfying
\sigma-\frac{d}{2}\leq p\leq\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}
, then

\left\|\frac{q_{1}}{y^{p}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}-p}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{p-\sigma+\frac{d}{2}}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}.

(B.6)

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
p\geq 0
such that
1\leq j\leq\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}
and
0\leq p\leq\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}
, then

\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y^{p}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-j-p-\frac{d}{2}}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{j+\frac{d}{2}-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}\left(1-\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}\right)+\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}}.

(B.7)

Moreover, if
1\leq j+p\ll L
, then

\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y^{p}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim b_{1}^{2(j+p)+\frac{2\gamma(j+p)}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)}\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}.

(B.8)

Proof.

(i)
The estimate (
B.1
) directly follows from Lemma
A.4
.

(ii)
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\hbar
is an even integer so that
\Bbbk=L+\hbar+1
is also an even integer. By (
2.20
), the expansion (
B.2
) is equivalent to

q_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}}c_{2i}T_{\Bbbk-2i}+r_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}}c_{2i}\phi_{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}-i}+r_{1},\quad q_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}-1}c_{2i+1}T_{\Bbbk-2i-1}+r_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}-1}c_{2i+1}\phi_{\frac{\Bbbk}{2}-i-1}+r_{2},

(B.9)

where we recall that
T_{2k}=T_{2k+1}=\phi_{k}
with
\phi_{k}
being defined as in (
1.32
). We only deal with the expansion of
q_{1}
because the same proof holds for
q_{2}
. We claim that for
1\leq m\leq\frac{\Bbbk}{2}
,
(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2m}
admits the Taylor expansion at the origin

(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}\phi_{m-i}+(r_{1})_{m},

(B.10)

with the bounds

|c_{i,m}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}},

\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}|\partial_{y}^{j}(r_{1})_{m}|\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}-j}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}},\quad\text{for}\quad y<1.

The expansion (
B.2
) for
q_{1}
then follows from (
B.10
) with
m=\frac{\Bbbk}{2}
.

We proceed by induction in
m
for the proof of (
B.10
). For
m=1
, we write from the definition (
2.7
) of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
,

(e_{1})_{1}(y)=(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}(y)=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx+\frac{d_{1}}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}.

Note from (
B.1
) that
\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
and from (
2.5
) that
\Lambda Q\sim y
as
y\to 0
, we deduce that
d_{1}=0
. Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we derive the pointwise estimate

|(e_{1})_{1}(y)|\leq\frac{1}{y^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{y}|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}|^{2}x^{d-1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{y}x^{2}x^{d-1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\quad\text{for}\;y<1.

We remark from (
B.1
) that there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|(e_{1})_{1}(a)|=|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y<1}|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}|^{2}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

We then define

(r_{1})_{1}(y)=-\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{(e_{1})_{1}}{\Lambda Q}dx,

and obtain from the pointwise estimate of
(e_{1})_{1}
,

|(r_{1})_{1}(y)|\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\quad\text{for}\;\;y<1.

By construction and the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
, we have

\mathscr{L}(r_{1})_{1}=\mathscr{A}^{*}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-1}=(q_{1})_{\Bbbk}=\mathscr{L}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}.

Recall that
\text{span}(\mathscr{L})=\{\Lambda Q,\Gamma\}
where
\Gamma
admits the singular behavior (
2.10
). From (
B.1
), we have
\int\frac{|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}<+\infty
. This implies that there exists
c_{1,1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}=c_{1,1}\Lambda Q+(r_{1})_{1}=c_{1,1}\phi_{0}+(r_{1})_{1}.

Moreover, from (
B.1
) there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y<1}|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

which follows

|c_{1,1}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}},\quad|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2}|\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+2}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{2\Bbbk}}\quad\text{for}\;y<1.

Since
\mathscr{A}(r_{1})_{1}=(e_{1})_{1}
, we then write from the definition (
2.6
) of
\mathscr{A}
,

|\partial_{y}(r_{1})_{1}|\lesssim|(e_{1})_{1}|+\left|\frac{(r_{1})_{1}}{y}\right|\lesssim y^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\quad\text{for}\;y<1.

This concludes the proof of (
B.10
) for
m=1
.

We now assume that (
B.10
) holds for
j=1,\cdots,m
for some
m\geq 1
, and prove that (
B.10
) holds for
j=1,\cdots,m+1
. The term
(r_{1})_{m+1}
is built as follows:

(r_{1})_{m+1}=-\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{(e_{1})_{m+1}}{\Lambda Q}dx,

where
a\in(1/2,1)
and

(e_{1})_{m+1}=\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}(r_{1})_{m}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx.

We now use the induction hypothesis to estimate

\displaystyle|(e_{1})_{m+1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{y^{d-1}\Lambda Q}\int_{0}^{y}(r_{1})_{m}\Lambda Qx^{d-1}dx\right|

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{d}}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\int_{0}^{y}x^{2m+\frac{d}{2}}dx\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}+1}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle|(r_{1})_{m+1}|=\left|\Lambda Q\int_{a}^{y}\frac{(e_{1})_{m+1}}{\Lambda Q}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim y^{2m-\frac{d}{2}+2}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}.

By construction, we have

\mathscr{L}(r_{1})_{m+1}=r_{m}.

From the induction hypothesis and the definition (
2.19
) of
T_{k}
, we write

\mathscr{L}(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2(m+1)}=(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}\phi_{m-i}+(r_{1})_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}\mathscr{L}\phi_{m+1-i}+\mathscr{L}r_{m+1}.

The singularity (
2.10
) of
\Gamma
at the origin and the bound
\int_{y<1}\frac{|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2(m+1)}|^{2}}{y^{2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}
implies that

(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2(m+1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i,m}\phi_{m+1-i}+c_{m+1,m}\Lambda Q+(r_{1})_{m+1}.

From (
B.1
), we see that there exists
a\in(1/2,1)
such that

|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2(m+1)}(a)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{y<1}|(q_{1})_{\Bbbk-2(m+1)}|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

from which we derive the bound
|c_{m+1,m}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}
. For the estimate on
\partial_{y}^{j}(r_{1})_{m+1}
, we note that by construction

(r_{1})_{m+2}:=\mathscr{A}(r_{1})_{m+1}=(e_{1})_{m+1},\quad(r_{1})_{m+3}:=\mathscr{L}(r_{1})_{m+1}=(r_{1})_{m},

(r_{1})_{m+4}:=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}(r_{1})_{m+1}=\mathscr{A}(r_{1})_{m}=(e_{1})_{m},\quad(r_{1})_{m+5}:=\mathscr{L}^{2}(r_{1})_{m+1}=(r_{1})_{m-1},\cdots

(r_{1})_{3m+1}:=\mathscr{L}^{m}(r_{1})_{m+1}=(r_{1})_{1},\quad(r_{1})_{3m+2}:=\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{m}(r_{1})_{m+1}=(e_{1})_{1}.

A brute force computation using the definitions of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
and the asymptotic behavior (
2.8
) ensure that for any function
f
, we have

\partial_{y}^{j}f=\sum_{i=0}^{j}P_{i,j}f_{i}\quad\text{with}\quad|P_{i,j}|\lesssim\frac{1}{y^{j-i}}.

(B.11)

Hence, we have for
0\leq j\leq 2m+1
and
y<1
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}^{j}r_{m+1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{|r_{m+1+i}|}{y^{j-i}}\lesssim\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{y^{2m+2-i-\frac{d}{2}}}{y^{j-i}}\lesssim y^{2m+2-\frac{d}{2}-j}\sqrt{\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}}.

This concludes the proof of (
B.10
) as well as (
B.2
).

(iii)
We use (
B.11
), (
B.1
) and the expansion (
B.2
) to estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}}{1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{2}|^{2}}{1+y^{2\Bbbk-2i-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int_{y<1}|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{1}|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int_{y<1}|\partial_{y}^{i}q_{2}|^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\int_{y>1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}(q_{1})|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\int_{y>1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{i}(q_{2})|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2i-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-1}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\int_{y>1}\frac{|(q_{1})_{j}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j}}+\sum_{i=0}^{\Bbbk-2}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\int_{y>1}\frac{|(q_{2})_{j}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk},

which concludes the proof of (
B.3
). The estimate (
B.4
) simply follows from an interpolation. The estimate (
B.5
) follows from (
A.2
) and the interpolation (
B.4
).

(iv)
We apply the Hardy inequality (
A.3
) to
q_{1}
with
\alpha=\sigma-1
, the bound (
B.5
) with

and

, and the expansion (

) to find that

\left\|\frac{q_{1}}{y^{\sigma-\frac{d}{2}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2(\sigma-1)}}+|q_{1}(1)|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}.

Similarly, we have

\left\|\frac{q_{1}}{y^{\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2(\Bbbk-1)}}+|q_{1}(1)|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

An interpolation of the two estimates yields the bound (
B.6
).

For
1\leq j\leq\Bbbk-\frac{d}{2}
, we have from Sobolev and the bound (
B.4
),

\|\nabla^{j}q_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla^{\frac{d}{2}+j}q_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{j+\frac{d}{2}-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}.

(B.12)

We apply (
A.3
) to
\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}
with
\alpha=\Bbbk-j-1
, then use (
B.5
) and (
B.2
) to estimate

\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y^{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\int_{y\geq 1}\frac{|\partial_{y}^{j+1}q_{1}|^{2}}{y^{2\Bbbk-2j-2}}+|\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}(1)|^{2}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}.

We interpolate for
0\leq p\leq\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y^{p}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{\frac{\Bbbk-j-p-\frac{d}{2}}{\Bbbk-\sigma}}\mathscr{E}_{\Bbbk}^{\frac{j+\frac{d}{2}-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}\left(1-\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}\right)+\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}}.

\frac{\Bbbk-j-p-\frac{d}{2}}{\Bbbk-\sigma}=1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right).

Recall from (

\displaystyle\left[\frac{j+\frac{d}{2}-\sigma}{\Bbbk-\sigma}\left(1-\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}\right)+\frac{p}{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}\right](2L+2(1-\delta)(1+\eta))

\left\|\frac{\partial_{y}^{j}q_{1}}{y^{\Bbbk-j-\frac{d}{2}}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(y\geq 1)}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}^{1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)}b_{1}^{2(j+p)+\frac{2\gamma(j+p)}{L}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}}\right)}.

This concludes the proof of (

) and (
B.8
) as well as the proof of Lemma

.

For the estimates of the linear and commutator terms in derivation of the monotonicity formula (

Lemma B.2

(Leibniz rule for

Let

\mathscr{L}^{k+1}(\phi f)=\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}f_{2m}\phi_{2k+2,2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+2,2m+1},

\mathscr{A}\mathscr{L}^{k}(\phi f)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m+1}\phi_{2k+1,2m+1}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}f_{2m}\phi_{2k+1,2m},

where

- for

\displaystyle\phi_{1,0}=-\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{1,1}=\phi,

\displaystyle\phi_{2,0}=-\partial_{y}^{2}\phi-\frac{d-1+2V}{y}\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{2,1}=2\partial_{y}\phi,\quad\phi_{2,2}=\phi,

- for

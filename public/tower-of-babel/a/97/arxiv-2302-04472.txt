A characterization of irreducible Hermitian symmetric spaces of tube type by ℂ ∗ \displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*} -actions
Yingqi Liu

Abstract
. A ℂ ∗ \displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*} -action on a projective variety X \displaystyle X is said to be of Euler type at a nonsingular fixed point x \displaystyle x if the isotropy action of ℂ ∗ \displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*} on T x ​ X \displaystyle T_{x}X is by scalar multiplication. In this paper, it’s proven that a smooth projective variety of Picard number one X \displaystyle X is isomorphic to an irreducible Hermitian symmetric space of tube type if and only if for a general pair of points x , y \displaystyle x,y on X \displaystyle X , there exists a ℂ ∗ \displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*} -action on X \displaystyle X which is of Euler type at x \displaystyle x and its inverse action is of Euler type at y \displaystyle y .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

The study of complex torus actions on algebraic varieties is a classical topic in algebraic geometry. It is an interesting problem to classify algebraic varieties with special
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions.
Let
\displaystyle X
be a smooth projective variety. A
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\displaystyle X
is said to be equalized at a fixed point
\displaystyle x
if any weight of the isotropy action on
\displaystyle T_{x}X
equals to 0 or
\displaystyle\pm 1
. We call the action equalized if it’s equalized at each fixed point. Denote by
\displaystyle X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
the set of fixed points of the
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. An irreducible component of
\displaystyle X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
is called extremal if it intersects with general
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-orbit closures.
In the series of works
[
RW20
]
[
ORSW22
]
[
ORSW21
]
, the authors study equalized
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions on projective manifolds with isolated extremal fixed components. For rational homogenous spaces they proved the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
ORSW21
]
Let
\displaystyle X=G/P
be a rational homogenous space of Picard number one, then
\displaystyle X
admits an equalized
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action with two isolated extremal fixed points if and only if X is isomorphic to one of the followings:

(i) a smooth hyperquadric
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}
.

(ii) the Grassmaninan variety
\displaystyle Gr(n,2n)
.

(iii) the Lagrangian Grassmanian variety
\displaystyle Lag(n,2n)
.

(iv) the spinor variety
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}_{2n}
.

(v) the 27 dimensional
\displaystyle E_{7}
-variety
\displaystyle E_{7}/P_{7}
.

The varieties classified above are exactly irreducible Hermitian symmetric spaces (IHSS for short) of tube type. Recall that an IHSS is called of tube type if its dual, as a bounded symmetric domain, is holomorphically equivalent to a tube domain over a self dual cone.
In
[
Mok02
]
Mok showed that an IHSS is of tube type if and only if for a point
\displaystyle o\in X
the complement of the vectors of maximal rank in
\displaystyle T_{o}(X)
is a hypersurface. Translating in the language of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions, one can regard Theorem 1.1 as a generalization of Mok’s result to rational homogenous spaces.

It is then a natural problem to characterize IHSS of tube type by
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions in a more general context. A
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a projective manifold
\displaystyle X
is said to be of Euler type at a fixed point
\displaystyle x
if the isotropy action of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
on
\displaystyle T_{x}X
is by scalar multiplication, or equivalently if the
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is equalized at
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle x
is an isolated extremal fixed point. In Theorem 1.1, by taking certain conjugates of
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
in
\displaystyle Aut^{0}(X)
one can show that for a general pair of points
\displaystyle x,y
on
\displaystyle X
there exists a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action which is of Euler type at
\displaystyle x
and its inverse action is of Euler type at
\displaystyle y
(see Section 4 for more details). Here a general pair of points on
\displaystyle X
is defined as a pair of points lying in a Zariski open dense subset of
\displaystyle X\times X
. Our main result proves the converse:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be a smooth projective variety of Picard number one, then
\displaystyle X
is isomorphic to an IHSS of tube type if and only if for a general pair of points
\displaystyle x,y
on
\displaystyle X
, there exists a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\displaystyle X
which is of Euler type at
\displaystyle x
and its inverse action is of Euler type at
\displaystyle y
.

1.2.
Outline of the proof

The main ingredient of the proof is the VMRT theory developed by Hwang and Mok. Let
\displaystyle X
be a Fano manifold of Picard number one and let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}
be a fixed irreducible dominant family of minimal rational curves on
\displaystyle X
. The variety of minimal rational tangents (VMRT for short) at a general point
\displaystyle x\in X
is the closed subvariety
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}X
consisting of all tangent directions at
\displaystyle x
of curves in
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}
passing through
\displaystyle x
. A large part of the global geometry of the manifold is controlled by the VMRT
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}X
at a general point
\displaystyle x
. The first step is to show that
\displaystyle X
in Theorem 1.2 is an equivariant compactification of vector group hence
\displaystyle X
can be recovered from its VMRT by Cartan-Fubini type theorem
[
HM01
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Then we follow the methods developed in
[
HM05
]

[
FH12
]
to classify the projective subvariety
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}X
by studying its prolongaion of infinitesimal linear automorphisms.

Definition 1.3
.

(1) Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}l(V)
be a Lie subalgebra, then the k-th prolongation of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
is the space of symmetric multi-linear homomorphisms
\displaystyle A:Sym^{k+1}V\rightarrow V
such that for any fixed
\displaystyle v_{1},...,v_{k}\in V
, the homomorphism
\displaystyle A_{v_{1},...,v_{k}}:V\rightarrow V
defined by:

v\in V\rightarrow A(v,v_{1},...,v_{k})\in V

is in
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}
.

(2) Let
\displaystyle S\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}V
be a projective subvariety. Let
\displaystyle\hat{S}\subseteq V
be its affine cone and
\displaystyle T_{\alpha}(\hat{S})
the tangent space at a smooth point
\displaystyle\alpha\in\hat{S}
. The Lie algebra of infinitesimal linear automorphisms of
\displaystyle\hat{S}
is

\mathfrak{a}ut(\hat{S})=\{g\in End(V)|g(\alpha)\in T_{\alpha}(\hat{S})\,\text{for any smooth point $\displaystyle\alpha\in\hat{S}$}\}=\{g\in End(V)|exp(tg)\cdot\hat{S}\subset\hat{S},\,t\in{\mathbb{C}}\}.

Its k-th prolongation
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}ut(\hat{S})^{(k)}
will be called the k-th prolongation of
\displaystyle S\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}V
.

A crucial step of our proof is the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be a smooth projective variety of Picard number one. Assume for a general pair of points
\displaystyle(x,y)\in X\times X
, there is a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\displaystyle X
which is of Euler type at
\displaystyle x
and its inverse action is of Euler type at
\displaystyle y
, then for the VMRT
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}X
we have:

(1.1)

dim(\mathfrak{a}ut(\hat{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}})^{(1)})=dim(X)

The identity (1.1) is known to be valid for any IHSS. Let
\displaystyle X=G/P
be an IHSS defined by a semi-simple algebraic group
\displaystyle G
and a maximal parabolic subgroup
\displaystyle P
. Denote by
\displaystyle P^{-}
the opposite group of
\displaystyle P
and let
\displaystyle x=eP
,
\displaystyle y=\dot{w}_{0}x
where
\displaystyle w_{0}
is the longest element in the Weyl group. Then there is an equalized
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
\displaystyle X
such that
\displaystyle x
is an isolated extremal fixed point. And
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{a}}ut(\hat{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}})^{(1)}
can be identified with the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle R_{u}(P)
, induced by the
\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of the Lie algebra
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=Lie(G)
. When X is of tube type, then
\displaystyle y
is also an isolated extremal fixed point of the
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action and X is an equivariant compactification of
\displaystyle R_{u}(P)
with open orbit
\displaystyle R_{u}(P)\cdot y
. Thus in this case the nonzero prolongation of
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{a}}ut(\hat{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}})
comes from the vector group compactification on
\displaystyle X
with origin
\displaystyle y
.

Generally assume that
\displaystyle X
satisfies the conditions of Theorem 1.2. Then the vector groups action of
\displaystyle T_{y}X
and
\displaystyle T_{x}X
on themselves can be extended to equivariant compactifications of vector groups on
\displaystyle X
. Choosing a suitable non-degenrate projective embedding of
\displaystyle X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}V
. The actions of
\displaystyle T_{y}X
and
\displaystyle T_{x}X
on
\displaystyle X
can be lifted to linear actions on
\displaystyle V
. Identifying
\displaystyle Lie(T_{y}X),Lie(T_{x}X)
with their images in
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}l(V)
, the adjoint actions of the two Lie subalgebras inside
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{g}}l(V)
will induce the identification:
\displaystyle Lie(T_{y}X)\cong\mathfrak{a}ut(\hat{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}})^{(1)}
.

In
[
FH12
]
Fu and Hwang classified irreducible non-degenrate nonsingular projective subvariety
\displaystyle S\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}V
with nonzero prolongations. Most of them are the VMRT of an IHSS or a nonsingular linear section of some IHSS.
Using a case by case calculation of
\displaystyle dim(\mathfrak{a}ut(\hat{S})^{(1)})
, we show that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}X
is projectively isomorphic to the VMRT of an IHSS. Then by a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant Cartan-Fubini extension theorem, we conclude that
\displaystyle X
is
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-isomorphic to an IHSS. The
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on IHSS was studied in detail in
[
ORSW21
]
, as a corollary it has two isolated extremal fixed points if and only if it is isomorphic to an IHSS of tube type.

The article is organized as follows. In Section 2 we first review the approach in
[
FH20
]
to associate
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

In Section 3 we study the prolongations of projective varieties, we first prove Theorem 1.4 then we calculate the dimension of prolongation for certain projective subvariety with nonzero prolongations. \displaystyle T^{+}(Y),T^{-}(Y)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

\displaystyle X

(2) For each

\displaystyle s_{0}

\displaystyle\eta

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle X_{1}
and
\displaystyle X_{2}
be two Fano manifolds of Picard number 1, different from projective spaces. Let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{2}
be families of minimal rational curves on
\displaystyle X_{1}
and
\displaystyle X_{2}
respectively. Assume that for a general point
\displaystyle x\in X_{1}
, the VMRT
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subset{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}(X_{1})
is irreducible and nonsingular. Let
\displaystyle U_{1}\subset X_{1}
and
\displaystyle U_{2}\subset X_{2}
be connected analytical open subsets. Suppose that there exists a biholomorphic map
\displaystyle\phi:U_{1}\rightarrow U_{2}
such that for a general point
\displaystyle x\in U_{1}
, the differential
\displaystyle d\phi_{x}:{\mathbb{P}}T_{x}(U_{1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}T_{\phi(x)}(U_{2})
sends
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
isomorphically to
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\phi(x)}

\displaystyle\Phi:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
\displaystyle\phi=\Phi|U_{1}
.

The extension theorem enables us to characterize an Euler-symmetric variety by its VMRT. We present a
\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant version for our convenience.

Let
\displaystyle X,X^{\prime}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}T(X)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}T(X^{\prime})
the associated VMRT sturctures on
\displaystyle X
and
\displaystyle X^{\prime}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle X^{\prime}

-actions on
\displaystyle X

\displaystyle X^{\prime}

\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

Proof.

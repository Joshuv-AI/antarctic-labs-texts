Abstract
. We prove the conjecture that the deformation limit of Moishezon manifolds under a smooth deformation over the unit disc in ℂ \mathbb{C} is Moishezon. More generally, for a proper holomorphic submersion over the unit disc with connected compact fibers satisfying the ∂ ∂ ¯ \partial\bar{\partial} -lemma except possibly at one parameter, we show that the very general algebraic dimension is the minimum on the entire disc. The proof builds on Barlet’s theory of cycle spaces and Rao–Tsai’s torsion-freeness method for extending integral Chern classes. Taking this approach as a starting point, we handle the remaining finite-order torsion obstruction by a scalar phase estimate for divisor masses. This gives uniform Gauduchon mass bounds on each component over compact subsets of the base without assuming torsion-freeness. Bishop compactness then establishes properness of every irreducible component of the relative divisor space, and Barlet’s algebraic-dimension theorem yields the conclusion directly. Combined with Rao–Tsai’s theorem, the deformation-limit result also shows that every fiber is Moishezon whenever uncountably many fibers are Moishezon. The Fujiki–Pontecorvo families and Huybrechts’ twistor and Brauer families clarify the scope and necessity of the hypotheses.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Delta:=\{t\in\mathbb{C}:|t|<1\}
be the
unit disc
and write

\Delta^{*}:=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. Let

(1.1)

\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\Delta

be a proper holomorphic submersion with connected compact fibers

X_{t}:=\pi^{-1}(t)
of complex dimension
n
. The
algebraic dimension

a(X)
of a compact connected complex manifold is the transcendence degree
over
\mathbb{C}
of its field of meromorphic functions. It satisfies

0\leq a(X)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
; equality on the right characterizes
Moishezon
manifolds
.

According to Kodaira’s classification of surfaces (e.g.,
[
Si83
,
Bu99
,
Lm99
]
), every compact complex surface with even first Betti number is Kähler. Thus Ehresmann’s theorem
[
Eh47
]
and the deformation invariance of Hodge numbers imply that the limit surface
X_{0}
of compact complex surfaces in Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
(or equivalently Kähler surfaces) is Kähler, while
[
Po13
, Corollary 1.3]
proves that the deformation limit of Moishezon (or equivalently projective) surfaces is projective.

In the higher-dimensional setting, Hironaka’s counterexample
[
Hi62
]
demonstrates that even if all fibers
X_{t}
are projective for
t\in\Delta^{*}
, the central fiber
X_{0}
need not be projective or Kähler.

D. Popovici
[
Po13
]
proved that if all fibers
X_{t}
are projective for
t\in\Delta^{*}
and the central fiber
X_{0}
either satisfies the deformation invariance of the Hodge number
h^{0,1}
or admits a strongly Gauduchon metric, then
X_{0}
is Moishezon.
A Hermitian metric
\omega
on a compact complex
n
-dimensional manifold
is called a
Gauduchon metric
if

\partial\bar{\partial}\omega^{n-1}=0
, and a

strongly Gauduchon metric
if
\partial\omega^{n-1}
is

\bar{\partial}
-exact, as in
[
Po13
]
.
The existence of Gauduchon metrics on any compact complex manifold was established in
[
Ga77
]
. Moreover, if
X
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma, then every Gauduchon metric on
X
is automatically strongly Gauduchon. Here the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma
means
the following: for every pure-type
d
-closed form on a complex manifold, the properties of

d
-exactness,
\partial
-exactness,
\bar{\partial}
-exactness and

\partial\bar{\partial}
-exactness are equivalent.

D. Barlet
[
Ba15
]
further extended these results, showing that if all fibers
X_{t}
are Moishezon for
t\in\Delta^{*}
and
X_{0}
satisfies either of the aforementioned conditions, then
X_{0}
remains Moishezon.
More precisely, his two semicontinuity results can be stated together as
follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Ba15
, Theorems 1.0.2 and 1.0.3]
)
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\Delta
be the family in (
1.1
).

(i)

If
X_{t_{0}}
admits a smooth strongly Gauduchon form, there are an

of

, an integer

, and a
countable union

\Sigma
of irreducible proper analytic subsets of
U

such that

a(X_{t})\geq a\quad(t\in U),\qquad a(X_{t})=a\quad(t\in U\setminus\Sigma).

(ii)

Suppose that
\Delta^{\circ}\subset\Delta
is a dense analytic Zariski
open subset and that every
X_{t}
with
t\in\Delta^{\circ}
satisfies the

\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.
If
h^{0,1}(X_{t})
is constant on
\Delta
, then

a(X_{t})\geq\inf_{u\in\Delta^{\circ}}a(X_{u})\qquad(t\in\Delta).

More recently, S. Rao–I.-H. Tsai obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
RT21
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\Delta
be the family in (
1.1
), and assume
that uncountably many fibers are Moishezon.
For any
t_{0}\in\Delta
, the fiber
X_{t_{0}}
is Moishezon if either

(i)

R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is torsion-free in a neighbourhood of
t_{0}

(equivalently,
h^{0,1}(X_{t})
is constant in a neighbourhood of
t_{0}
,
by
[
RT21
, Proposition 4.12]
); or

(ii)

X_{t_{0}}
admits a strongly Gauduchon metric.

J. Kollár
[
Ko22
]
proposed a conjecture in the case of flat families, asserting that if all fibers
X_{t}
are Moishezon for
t\in\Delta^{*}
and the central fiber
X_{0}
is irreducible with rational singularities in a proper flat morphism over
\Delta
, then
X_{0}
is Moishezon. A brief review of progress on this topic, along with a collection of further stimulating open questions, can be found in
[
Ko22
, Section 1]
.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Upper semicontinuity of algebraic dimension)
.

Assume that there is a point
t_{0}\in\Delta
such that
X_{t}
satisfies
the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for every
t\neq t_{0}
.
Then there are an integer
a_{\mathrm{gen}}\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and a
countable union
\Sigma
of proper closed analytic subsets of
\Delta

such that

a(X_{t})=a_{\mathrm{gen}}\quad(t\in\Delta\setminus\Sigma),\qquad a(X_{t})\geq a_{\mathrm{gen}}\quad(t\in\Delta).

Thus the very general algebraic dimension is the minimum on the entire
disc. In particular, for every
s\in\Delta
and every sufficiently small

\varepsilon>0
with
\{t:|t-s|<\varepsilon\}\Subset\Delta
,

(1.2)

a(X_{s})\geq\inf_{0<|t-s|<\varepsilon}a(X_{t})=a_{\mathrm{gen}}.

The exceptional set
\Sigma
is countable, but need not be closed in
the analytic Zariski topology. Equivalently, each superlevel set

\{t\in\Delta:a(X_{t})\geq k\}
is a countable union of closed analytic
subsets; it need not itself be a closed analytic subset. This is not
upper semicontinuity for the ordinary analytic Zariski topology.

Every Moishezon manifold satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma
(see
[
Pa66
]
and
[
DGMS75
, Corollary 5.23]
), and

a(X_{t})=n
for
t\neq 0
. Theorem
1.3
gives

a(X_{0})\geq n
, hence equality.

Corollary 1.4

(Moishezon deformation limit)
.

If
X_{t}
is Moishezon for every
t\in\Delta^{*}
, then
X_{0}
is
Moishezon.

Actually, we can obtain a more general result:

Theorem 1.5

(Uncountably many Moishezon fibers)
.

If uncountably many fibers of (
1.1
) are Moishezon, then every
fiber is Moishezon.

We outline the proof of Theorem
1.3
in
Section
3
. After an automorphism of the
unit disc, assume that the possibly exceptional parameter is
0
.
The aim is to prove properness of every irreducible component of the
relative divisor space. Vertical components are compact, so we fix a
horizontal component
\Lambda
and its integral divisor class

c\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, where
M
is the common differentiable fiber supplied
by an Ehresmann trivialization. The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma and
base change imply that its holomorphic obstruction

o(c)\in H^{0}(\Delta,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
vanishes on
\Delta^{*}
.
Local total-space line bundles of class
c
then control divisor masses,
and Bishop compactness and Remmert’s theorem show that

\Lambda^{*}\to\Delta^{*}
is proper and surjective. This use of fixed
integral classes builds on Rao–Tsai’s torsion-freeness method

[
RT21
, Propositions 4.12 and 4.13]
: when the direct-image sheaf is
torsion-free, local vanishing of
o(c)
extends globally and yields a
total-space line bundle. Without this hypothesis, the remaining
obstruction is supported at
0
and is annihilated locally by
t^{N}

for some integer
N\geq 0
, by Lemma
2.18
.

The additional step is a scalar phase estimate that handles this
finite-order torsion. For a smooth closed representative
\alpha
of

c
, the relation
t^{N}o(c)=0
yields a smooth absolute
(0,1)
-form
u

with
\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{X}}u=t^{N}\alpha^{0,2}_{\mathcal{X}}
near the central fiber,
by Lemma
3.3
.
For a smooth family of Gauduchon forms
G_{t}
, this gives the mass formula

\operatorname{Vol}_{G_{t}}(Z)=B(t)+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(t^{-N}A(t)\right)\geq 0,\qquad Z\in\Lambda_{t},\quad t\neq 0,

where
A
is complex-valued and
B
is real-valued, both smooth across

0
. Surjectivity over
\Delta^{*}
supplies a divisor for every nonzero
parameter, so nonnegativity of the masses gives a lower bound for

\operatorname{Re}(t^{-N}A(t))
in every angular direction.
The phase Lemma
3.1
, proved by finite Taylor expansion and Fourier
orthogonality, forces
A(t)=O(|t|^{N})
. Thus divisor masses stay bounded
near
0
; density of
\Lambda^{*}
and continuity of cycle integration
extend the bound to the central cycles, as established in
Proposition
3.4
. Bishop compactness, in the form of
Lemma
2.4
, now gives
properness of
\Lambda
over the whole disc. Applying Barlet’s
algebraic-dimension Theorem
2.7
to this
componentwise properness proves Theorem
1.3
, without a
good-filling assumption or a separate comparison of relative Kodaira
images. The estimate controls only the scalar Gauduchon pairing needed
for compactness, not a complete minimal
\bar{\partial}
-solution.

The examples in Section
4
distinguish two notions of
semicontinuity. A. Fujiki–M. Pontecorvo construct a non-Kähler family for
which both the ordinary limsup inequality and the punctured-neighbourhood
infimum inequality fail. Twistor and Brauer families of K3 surfaces show
that the ordinary limsup inequality may fail even when every fiber is
Kähler, while the weaker infimum inequality remains valid. The two
line bundles isolated by Fujiki–Pontecorvo also show why extension of a
prescribed punctured representative is stronger than extension of its
Chern class.

The paper is organized in four sections and an appendix.
Section
2
organizes the tools for this proof:
cycle-space compactness, Gauduchon mass pairings, and the integral-class
obstruction. The complementary good-filling constructions are collected
separately in Section
2.4
.
Section
3
establishes componentwise
properness by phase estimates and proves Theorems
1.3

and
1.5
. Section
4
compares the conclusion with known test
families. Appendix
A
develops applications to
Moishezon families. Standard foundational theorems are stated with the hypotheses
used here, and the steps connecting them to the argument are explained.

Notation 1.6
.

Complex spaces are reduced unless otherwise specified; Douady spaces
and the subspaces they classify may be nonreduced. Cycles retain their

2.1.
Divisor cycle spaces and componentwise compactness

We use Barlet spaces to retain both the supports and the positive
integral multiplicities of divisors; see
[
BM19
]
for a systematic
treatment. The tools below are used in
Section
3.2
to prepare a horizontal
component and in Section
3.4
to conclude
properness and the algebraic-dimension inequality.

2.1.1.
Cycles and their integral classes

Let
X
be a compact complex
n
-dimensional manifold. For

0\leq k\leq n
, an
effective
k
-cycle
is a finite formal sum

Z=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}Z_{i},

where the
Z_{i}
are pairwise distinct irreducible analytic subsets of
complex dimension
k
and the
m_{i}
are positive integers. Its
support

is
|Z|=\bigcup_{i}Z_{i}
. The
Barlet space

\mathcal{C}_{k}(X)
parametrizes these
cycles with their multiplicities, not merely their supports.

The integral homology class of
Z
has a Poincaré-dual class

c(Z)\in H^{2n-2k}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. If a smooth closed form
\eta_{Z}

represents its real image, then

\int_{Z}\alpha=\int_{X}\alpha\wedge\eta_{Z}

for every smooth closed
2k
-form
\alpha
. For a divisor,

c(Z)=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z))
. We retain the integral class even though its
smooth representative detects only its real image.

For the family (
1.1
), Ehresmann’s fibration theorem

[
Eh47
]
and contractibility of the disc give a differentiable
trivialization with common fiber
M
. In particular,
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
is
constant, so integral divisor classes on different fibers can be compared
in
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. This is the
Ehresmann trivialization
used
throughout the paper; it is not a holomorphic trivialization.

2.1.2.
Absolute and relative divisor spaces

Theorem 2.1

(Compactness of divisor components,

[
Ba15
, Theorem 6.0.13]
)
.

Every irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(X)
is compact.

Let
f:\mathcal{X}\to B
be a smooth family of connected compact complex

n
-dimensional manifolds. The
relative Barlet space

\mathcal{C}_{k}(\mathcal{X}/B)
parametrizes effective compact
k
-cycles contained
in the fibers, with the
canonical projection

\mu:\mathcal{C}_{k}(\mathcal{X}/B)\longrightarrow B.

We regard its points as pairs
(b,Z)
, so that the zero cycle, if
included, retains its base point. For the family (
1.1
),
write
\mathscr{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)
.
For a proper
n
-equidimensional map
f:Y\to B
, we similarly write

\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(f)=\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(Y/B)
for its
relative divisor cycle space
.
An
irreducible component
always means a maximal irreducible closed
analytic subset of this space.

Theorem 2.2

(Barlet classification and fundamental cycles,

[
Ma07
, Theorems 1.8–1.9, Main Theorem, and Corollary 3.3]
)
.

Let
M
be a complex space.

(i)

The reduced Barlet space
\mathcal{C}_{k}(M)
has a universal proper
analytic family of compact effective
k
-cycles. A proper analytic
family over a reduced space
S
determines a holomorphic map

S\to\mathcal{C}_{k}(M)
sending
s
to its cycle; conversely, pulling back the
universal family recovers that family, with multiplicities.

(ii)

For a flat proper family of pure
k
-dimensional subspaces,
the fundamental cycles form an analytic family over the reduced base.

An analytic family of cycles retains the integer degrees of its local
multigraphs. It need not be a flat family of analytic subspaces; the
last assertion of the theorem gives the passage from flat families to
cycle families that is used below.

Lemma 2.3

(Constancy of the cycle class)
.

Under an Ehresmann trivialization, the integral homology class of an analytic
family of relative cycles is locally constant. Hence every connected
analytic family of divisors has one Poincaré-dual class in
H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

For the definition and existence of analytic relative fundamental classes,
see
[
Ba80
, § 2 B, Definition 1, and § 2 D, Theorem 2]
.
The integral homology statement is obtained by the
following topological argument.
Along a path in the parameter space, adapted scales sweep an integral bordism
between the endpoint cycles; the constant local degrees retain the
multiplicities. The endpoint cycles are therefore homologous in the
fixed differentiable fiber determined by the trivialization.
∎

2.1.3.
Compactness, proper images, and density

Bishop compactness is used in its cycle-space form, which controls both
the supports and the positive integral multiplicities.

Lemma 2.4

(Cycle compactness from a mass bound)
.

Let
K\Subset\Delta
be compact and let
H
be a fixed Hermitian metric on

\mathcal{X}
. A closed subset
A\subset\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)
whose cycles are
supported in
\pi^{-1}(K)
and have uniformly bounded
H
-volume is
compact.

Proof.

This lemma is well known to experts; we include the proof for the
reader’s convenience.
Properness of
\pi
makes
\pi^{-1}(K)
compact. The compactness criterion
for Barlet spaces states that a family of compact
k
-cycles is relatively
compact when all supports lie in one compact subset and their volumes for
one Hermitian metric are uniformly bounded, by

[
BM19
, Theorem 4.2.72]
.

That theorem is the cycle-theoretic form of Bishop compactness. Its
adapted-scale formulation retains the positive integral multiplicities,
so its conclusion is convergence in the Barlet space rather than only
Hausdorff convergence of supports.

The assumptions of the criterion hold for
A
. Thus
A
is relatively
compact in
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)
. Since
A
is closed and the Barlet
space is Hausdorff,
A
is compact.
∎

Properness then gives analytic images by Remmert’s theorem.

Theorem 2.5

(Proper mapping theorem,

[
Gr60
, § 7, Satz 1]
)
.

Let
f:Y\to B
be a proper holomorphic map of complex spaces. For every
closed analytic subset
Z\subset Y
, its image
f(Z)
is a closed
analytic subset of
B
.

In particular, the image of a proper map to a connected Riemann surface
is the whole surface if it contains a nonempty open subset.
The following density statement applies before properness is known.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Y
be an irreducible reduced complex space and let

h:Y\to B
be a nonconstant holomorphic map to a Riemann surface.
Then
h
is open. If
V_{B}\subset B
is dense and open, then

h^{-1}(V_{B})
is dense in
Y
.

Proof.

This follows directly from
[
Dem12
, Chapter II, Theorem (5.7)]
.
For completeness, we give a proof.
Fix
y\in Y
. No neighbourhood of
y
can be mapped to a point: otherwise

h
would be constant on a nonempty open subset and hence, by the identity
principle on the irreducible reduced space, constant on
Y
. Consequently

h
cannot be constant on every local irreducible branch through
y
, for
then it would be constant on a neighbourhood of
y
. Choose a branch on
which its germ is nonconstant. A generic linear section through
y
gives
a complex curve germ
C\subset Y
through
y
on which
h
is nonconstant.
After normalization, the ordinary open mapping theorem for one complex variable
shows that
h(C\cap W)
contains a neighbourhood of
h(y)
for every
sufficiently small neighbourhood
W
of
y
. Hence
h
is open.
If
h^{-1}(V_{B})
were not dense, some nonempty open
W\subset Y
would map
into
B\setminus V_{B}
. Openness of
h
would give a nonempty open subset
of
B
contained in the nowhere-dense complement, a contradiction.
∎

2.2.
The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma and Gauduchon forms

This subsection supplies the forms and integration identities used
in Section
3.3
. The current

\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma makes it possible to compare a divisor with
a smooth representative of its class. A smooth Gauduchon family then
annihilates the resulting exact correction, while continuity of cycle
integration transfers mass bounds to limiting cycles.

2.2.1.
The smooth and current formulations

Definition 2.8
.

A compact complex manifold
X
satisfies the

\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma
if every
d
-closed pure-type smooth form which
is
d
-exact is
\partial\bar{\partial}
-exact. Equivalently, for every
bidegree,

\ker d\cap\operatorname{im}d=\operatorname{im}\partial\bar{\partial}

inside the space of pure-type forms. The equivalent current formulation
will also be used.

The following current form is the precise consequence needed in the mass
calculation.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
Ba15
, Lemma 5.0.12]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact connected complex manifold satisfying the

\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. If

is a real,

-exact current of type

(1,1)
on
X
, then there is a real
0
-current
\Phi
such that

(2.1)

T=i\partial\bar{\partial}\Phi.

Proof.

Barlet proves that any

-exact
(1,1)
-current can be written

for a complex

-current
\Theta
. When
T
is real, conjugation gives

-\partial\bar{\partial}\overline{\Theta}=T
. Hence

\Phi=(\Theta-\overline{\Theta})/(2i)
is real and satisfies
(
2.1
).
∎

\mathcal{X}
satisfying

\widetilde{G}|_{X_{t}}=G_{t}
. In particular,

\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad Z\longmapsto\int_{Z}\widetilde{G}=\int_{Z}G_{\pi(Z)}

is continuous.

2.3.
The integral-class obstruction and mass bounds

Following the fixed-integral-class approach discussed in the
introduction, we encode the existence of a total-space line bundle by
a holomorphic obstruction section. Base change gives its initial
vanishing, and the exponential sequence gives line bundles where it
vanishes. The crucial remaining information is finite-order
annihilation, not necessarily global vanishing; this is the input for
the phase argument in Section
3.3
.

2.3.1.
Coherence and base change on the

\partial\bar{\partial}
-locus

We first record several basic results from Grauert’s direct image theory used in
Section
3.2
to pass from fiberwise
vanishing to vanishing of a holomorphic obstruction section.

Theorem 2.13

(Coherence, semicontinuity, and base change,

[
Gr60
, Hauptsatz I and § 7, Sätze 3 and 5]
,

[
GR84
, § 10.5.4]
)
.

Let
f:Y\to B
be a proper holomorphic map and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent
analytic sheaf on
Y
.

(i)

(Coherence)
Every
R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{F}
is coherent.

(ii)

(Semicontinuity)
If
B
is a complex manifold and
\mathcal{F}

is flat over
B
, the function

b\mapsto h^{q}(Y_{b},\mathcal{F}|_{Y_{b}})
is upper semicontinuous in the analytic
Zariski topology: for every integer
d\geq 0
, the locus where

h^{q}(Y_{b},\mathcal{F}|_{Y_{b}})\geq d
is a closed analytic subset of
B
.

(iii)

(Base change)
Under the hypotheses of
(ii)
, where
the function
b\mapsto h^{q}(Y_{b},\mathcal{F}|_{Y_{b}})
is locally constant,

R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{F}
is locally free and the natural
base-change map is an isomorphism:

(R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{F})_{b}/\mathfrak{m}_{b}(R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{F})_{b}\longrightarrow H^{q}(Y_{b},\mathcal{F}|_{Y_{b}}).

Here
\mathfrak{m}_{b}
is the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{B,b}
.

In our applications,
f
is a
submersion and
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle, so flatness holds. Coherence
alone does not identify a stalk modulo
\mathfrak{m}_{b}
with fiber
cohomology: that identification is the additional base-change assertion.

Lemma 2.14

(Base change over a disc of

\partial\bar{\partial}
-fibers)
.

Let
U\subset\Delta
be a complex disc such that every

,

,
satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma. Then, for all integers

, the sheaves

are locally free and satisfy base change.

Proof.

For every integer
0\leq k\leq 2n
, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma gives

b_{k}(M)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=k\\
0\leq p,q\leq n\end{subarray}}h^{q}(X_{t},\Omega^{p}_{X_{t}})\qquad(t\in U).

Each summand is upper semicontinuous, while the sum is constant. Shrinking
about any point of

shows that every summand is locally constant.
Theorem
2.13
then makes

locally free
and gives base change, as in

[
Gr60
, Satz 5]
.
∎

2.3.2.
The obstruction section and total-space line bundles

Theorem 2.15

(Cartan’s theorem B,

[
Ca53
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let

be a Stein manifold and let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent analytic sheaf
on

for every

Discs and punctured discs are Stein. The theorem removes higher
cohomology on the base without requiring the total space
\mathcal{X}_{W}

to be Stein.

For clarity, the Leray spectral sequence for a proper map

and a coherent sheaf

starts with

makes the direct images coherent, and

ensures that all columns with

H^{q}(Y,\mathcal{F})\simeq H^{0}(W,R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{F}).

This is the precise cohomological passage used in the lifting argument
below, not an assertion that fiberwise vanishing alone implies vanishing
of a section of a coherent sheaf.

Definition 2.16

(Torsion and torsion-freeness,

Let

be a coherent sheaf on a disc

is

,
and

if this holds at every point.

The finite-order vanishing needed below is established directly in

.

A class

For an open disc

Here

.

Let

be an open disc, possibly

If

Theorems

2.3.3.
Discrete support and finite-order torsion

The next lemma is the key sheaf-theoretic input for
Sections
3.2
and
3.3
:
it controls the obstruction even when the direct-image sheaf is not
torsion-free.

Set
\mathcal{F}:=R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
. The notation
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}o(c)
denotes
the
cyclic subsheaf
of
\mathcal{F}
generated by
o(c)
, namely

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}o(c):=\operatorname{im}\!\left(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\xrightarrow{\,f\mapsto fo(c)\,}\mathcal{F}\right).

Its stalk at
p
is
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,p}o(c)_{p}
.

Lemma 2.18

(Discrete support and finite torsion order;
cf.
[
RT21
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

If
o(c)
vanishes on a nonempty open subset of
\Delta
, then

S(c):=\operatorname{Supp}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\,o(c)\bigr)

is a possibly empty discrete complex-analytic subset. For every

p\in\Delta
, with local coordinate
z=t-p
, there is a least integer

N(p)\geq 0
such that

(2.3)

z^{N(p)}o(c)=0

near
p
. Here
N(p)=0
if and only if
p\notin S(c)
; at a point of

S(c)
one necessarily has
N(p)\geq 1
. In particular,
o(c)=0
on

\Delta
if and only if
S(c)=\varnothing
, equivalently if
N(p)=0

for every
p
.

Proof.

\mathcal{F}
is coherent by Theorem
2.13
, so its cyclic
subsheaf
\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}o(c)
is coherent. Its support is the zero set
of its annihilator ideal sheaf and is therefore analytic. It is proper
because
o(c)
vanishes on a nonempty open set. Since
\Delta
is a
connected complex curve, this proper analytic subset is discrete.

If
p\notin S(c)
, the germ
o(c)_{p}
is zero, hence the section vanishes
on a neighbourhood of
p
. Thus the least exponent there is
N(p)=0
.

Fix
p\in S(c)
and put
R:=\mathcal{O}_{\Delta,p}\simeq\mathbb{C}\{z\}
, where

z=t-p
. The ring
\mathbb{C}\{z\}
consists of convergent power-series germs.
Every nonzero germ has the form
z^{m}u
, where
m\geq 0
is its vanishing
order and
u
is a unit. Choosing the least vanishing order among the
nonzero elements of an ideal shows that every nonzero ideal is
(z^{m})
.
This is the discrete valuation ring property used here.

The cyclic stalk has the presentation

\mathcal{N}_{p}\simeq R/I_{p},\qquad I_{p}:=\{g\in R:g\,o(c)_{p}=0\}.

Here
I_{p}\neq R
because
\mathcal{N}_{p}\neq 0
. Also
I_{p}\neq 0
: otherwise the
coherent kernel of
\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}\to\mathcal{N}
would vanish near
p
, giving

\mathcal{N}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
there, contrary to the isolated support. Hence

I_{p}=(z^{N})
for some
N\geq 1
, and

\mathcal{N}_{p}\simeq R/(z^{N}),\qquad z^{N}\mathcal{N}_{p}=0.

In particular,
\mathcal{N}_{p}
is a finitely generated torsion module, of length

N
. The equality
z^{N}o(c)_{p}=0
is an equality of germs, so

z^{N}o(c)=0
on a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
p
.
Since
I_{p}=(z^{N})
, no smaller power annihilates the generator, so

N=N(p)
. This proves (
2.3
) and the assertions about
the zero exponent.
∎

Rao–Tsai’s supported-section criterion

[
RT21
, Proposition 4.5]
uses the coherent-sheaf support theorem

[
BS76
, Chapter II, Proposition 3.2]
and local freeness on the
punctured base. Here only finite-order annihilation is needed:
the cyclic-subsheaf argument above proves it without local freeness
of the ambient sheaf away from
S(c)
.

The zero-order case deserves separate emphasis. Initial vanishing on
a nonempty open set does not by itself give

on all of
\Delta
:
the remaining germs may be nonzero torsion. If
\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free,
however, (
2.3
) forces every germ to vanish. Then

and

everywhere. Conversely, a particular

can vanish even when
\mathcal{F}
has nonzero torsion.

In Rao–Tsai’s argument
[

,
constancy of

supplies torsion-freeness, so initial

Let
\Gamma

The family

A^{\circ}
is an irreducible component of
A\cap Y^{\circ}
,

\dim A=\dim A^{\circ}
, and
A^{\circ}
contains a nonempty subset which is
open in
A
. In particular, with

\mathscr{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta),\qquad\mu:\mathscr{C}\to\Delta,

there is an irreducible component
\Gamma\subset\mathscr{C}
satisfying

\Gamma_{U}\subset\Gamma\cap\mu^{-1}(U),

and
\Gamma_{U}
contains a nonempty open subset of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Complex spaces are locally Noetherian, and their local irreducible
components are locally finite. In a neighbourhood meeting only finitely
many local components, the intersections of the local branches of

A^{\circ}
with the other components are proper analytic subsets. We may
therefore choose a point
a\in A^{\circ}
which lies on no other local
component of
Y^{\circ}
and at which the germ of
A^{\circ}
is irreducible.

A local irreducible component through
a
is
contained in an irreducible component
A
of
Y
. Hence

A\cap A^{\circ}
is a closed analytic subset of the irreducible space

A^{\circ}
which contains a nonempty open subset. The identity principle
gives
A^{\circ}\subset A
.

If an irreducible closed analytic subset of

A\cap Y^{\circ}
contains
A^{\circ}
, then it is also an irreducible closed
analytic subset of
Y^{\circ}
; maximality of
A^{\circ}
forces equality.
Thus
A^{\circ}
is an irreducible component of
A\cap Y^{\circ}
.

The other irreducible components of
A\cap Y^{\circ}
form a locally finite
family, and their intersections with
A^{\circ}
are proper analytic
subsets. Choose a point of
A^{\circ}
outside their union and shrink a
neighbourhood in
A\cap Y^{\circ}
. The resulting nonempty subset is open
in
A
and is contained in
A^{\circ}
. In particular,

\dim A=\dim A^{\circ}
.

One cannot in general strengthen (
2.5
) to

A\cap Y^{\circ}=A^{\circ}
: for the irreducible curve
t=y^{2}
, the inverse
image of a small simply connected disc away from
t=0
has two components.
Apply the valid containment statement to
Y=\mathscr{C}
and

Y^{\circ}=\mu^{-1}(U)
.
∎

3.
Phase compactness and componentwise properness

We prove Theorem
1.3
by establishing properness of every
irreducible component of the relative divisor space. The integral-class
approach builds on Rao–Tsai’s torsion-freeness method, which extends local vanishing of
the holomorphic obstruction to obtain a total-space line bundle when

R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is torsion-free. Here a scalar phase estimate handles
the remaining finite-order torsion without that assumption and yields
a uniform bound for divisor masses. Bishop compactness then gives
componentwise properness, and Barlet’s Theorem
2.7

yields the algebraic-dimension conclusion. No good-filling assumption
is imposed on the component used in the mass estimate.

Throughout this section assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
.
An automorphism sends the possibly exceptional parameter to
0
.
We keep the original base
\Delta
; only auxiliary neighbourhoods are
shrunk. Put
\mathscr{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)
, let

\mu:\mathscr{C}\to\Delta
be the projection, and fix the smooth
Gauduchon family
G_{t}
of Lemma
2.11
.
For an irreducible component
\Lambda
of
\mathscr{C}
, endowed
with its reduced structure, write
\Lambda_{t}:=\Lambda\cap\mu^{-1}(t)
.
We call
\Lambda

vertical
when
\mu|_{\Lambda}
is constant and

horizontal
otherwise. A discrete image of the connected space

\Lambda
is a single point. Constants and auxiliary discs may depend
on
\Lambda
; no uniformity across different components is required.

3.1.
A scalar phase estimate

We isolate the analytic estimate used to control the possible singular
term in the divisor mass formula. A lower bound for the real part of

z^{-N}A(z)
in every angular direction forces the whole expression to
remain bounded. In the geometric application, this lower bound will
come from the nonnegativity of divisor masses.

Lemma 3.1

(Phase lemma)
.

Let
N\geq 0
be an integer and let
A\in\mathcal{C}^{N}(D_{0},\mathbb{C})
,
where
D_{0}
is a neighbourhood of
0
in
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that there
are a real constant
C
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.1)

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(z^{-N}A(z)\right)\geq-C\qquad(0<|z|<\varepsilon).

Then
A(z)=O(|z|^{N})
, or equivalently
z^{-N}A(z)
is bounded on
a punctured neighbourhood of
0
.

Proof.

For
N=0
, continuity suffices. Assume
N\geq 1
and write
z=x+iy
.
For a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
, use the notation

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\alpha_{2}!
,

(x,y)^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha_{1}}y^{\alpha_{2}}
, and

D^{\alpha}=\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{y}^{\alpha_{2}}
.
For
1\leq m\leq N
, apply the one-variable Taylor formula with integral
remainder
[
KL03
, formula (1)]
to
g(s)=A(sx,sy)
,
0\leq s\leq 1
.
The multinomial chain rule gives

(3.2)

\displaystyle A(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\ \sum_{|\alpha|=j}\frac{D^{\alpha}A(0)}{\alpha!}(x,y)^{\alpha}+R_{m}(x,y),

\displaystyle R_{m}(x,y)

\displaystyle=m\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\frac{(x,y)^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{m-1}D^{\alpha}A(sx,sy)\,ds.

Continuity bounds the derivatives on a small closed disc, so

|R_{m}(x,y)|\leq C_{m}(|x|+|y|)^{m}=O(|z|^{m})
, uniformly in the
angular variable. If all derivatives of order less than
N
vanish
at
0
, take
m=N
to obtain the conclusion.

Otherwise let
k<N
be the least order of a nonzero derivative.
The nonzero homogeneous Taylor polynomial of degree
k
has the
unique expression

P_{k}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{a+b=k}c_{ab}z^{a}\bar{z}^{b}.

Taking
m=k+1
in (
3.2
) yields

A(re^{i\theta})=r^{k}\sum_{a+b=k}c_{ab}e^{i(a-b)\theta}+O(r^{k+1}),

with a remainder uniform in
\theta
. Set

G_{k}(\theta)=\sum_{a+b=k}c_{ab}e^{i(a-b-N)\theta},\qquad F_{k}(\theta)=G_{k}(\theta)+\overline{G_{k}(\theta)}.

It follows that

(3.3)

2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(z^{-N}A(z)\right)=r^{k-N}\bigl(F_{k}(\theta)+E(r,\theta)\bigr),\qquad\sup_{\theta}|E(r,\theta)|\leq C_{E}r.

The
frequencies
of
G_{k}
are the pairwise distinct integers

m_{ab}=a-b-N=2a-k-N\leq k-N<0
; those of
\overline{G_{k}}

are positive. Thus
F_{k}
cannot vanish identically: Fourier
orthogonality would otherwise give

0=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}F_{k}(\theta)e^{-im_{ab}\theta}\,d\theta=c_{ab}

for every
a+b=k
, contradicting
P_{k}\neq 0
.
Moreover,
F_{k}
has no zero-frequency term and hence has mean zero.
Being real, continuous and nonzero, it is negative somewhere.
Choose
\theta_{0}
and
\delta>0
with
F_{k}(\theta_{0})=-\delta
.
Along this fixed ray, (
3.3
) becomes

2\operatorname{Re}\!\left((re^{i\theta_{0}})^{-N}A(re^{i\theta_{0}})\right)=r^{k-N}\bigl(-\delta+O(r)\bigr)\longrightarrow-\infty.

This contradicts (
3.1
) and proves

A(z)=O(|z|^{N})
.
∎

Only finite Taylor expansion and Fourier orthogonality enter this
argument; real analyticity is not required. The lower bound must
hold in every angular direction. We next establish the geometric
nonemptiness that supplies this condition for divisor masses.

3.2.
Preparation on the punctured disc

We first dispose of vertical components and prepare the horizontal
ones away from the exceptional parameter. For a horizontal component,
we show that its integral-class obstruction vanishes on
\Delta^{*}
and
that its restriction there is proper and surjective.
Lemma
2.18
is the key sheaf-theoretic input:
initial vanishing confines the obstruction to a discrete support, and
local freeness on
\Delta^{*}
then confines that support to
0
.
This isolates the possible finite-order torsion that must be treated in
Section
3.3
. Surjectivity
ensures the existence of divisors over every nonzero parameter, which
will supply the lower bound in all angular directions required by the
phase lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

(i)

Every vertical component of
\mathscr{C}
is compact.

(ii)

For every horizontal component
\Lambda
, the restriction

\Lambda^{*}:=\Lambda\cap\mu^{-1}(\Delta^{*})\to\Delta^{*}
is proper and
surjective, and
\Lambda^{*}
is dense in
\Lambda
. Its fixed integral
class

satisfies
o(c)|_{\Delta^{*}}=0
.

Proof.

If
\mu(\Lambda)=\{s\}
, then
\Lambda
lies in the closed analytic

\mu^{-1}(s)=\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(X_{s})
. By its maximality as an
irreducible closed analytic subset of

, it is also an
irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(X_{s})
. Hence
\Lambda
is compact
by Theorem
2.1
.

Suppose now that
\Lambda
is horizontal. Lemma
2.6

makes its projection open and
\Lambda^{*}
dense in
\Lambda
.
Choose a nonempty disc
U\Subset\mu(\Lambda)\cap\Delta^{*}
.
Under an Ehresmann trivialization, Lemma
2.3
gives
one Poincaré-dual class
c
for every divisor in
\Lambda
.
For each
t\in U
there is a divisor
Z\in\Lambda_{t}
, and

c=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}(Z))
has zero image in
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}})
.
The exponential sequence and Lemma
2.14
imply

o(c)|_{U}=0
. Lemma
2.18
now shows that

S(c)
is discrete and that every obstruction germ is annihilated by

is
locally free, hence torsion-free, on

o(c)|_{\Delta^{*}}=0,\qquad S(c)\subset\{0\}.

Only nonemptiness of
\Lambda_{t}
on

is used, not incidence

On a sufficiently small disc
D\Subset\Delta^{*}
,
Lemma
2.17
supplies a total-space line bundle with
Chern class
c
and a smooth curvature representative
\eta
.
Lemma
2.19
gives, for every
t\in D
and
every

\int_{Z}G_{t}=\int_{X_{t}}\eta_{t}\wedge G_{t}.

The right-hand side is smooth
in

and independent of

. A finite cover of any compact

by smaller discs with compact closures in such

gives a uniform mass bound on
\Lambda\cap\mu^{-1}(K)
.
Comparison with a fixed Hermitian metric gives a uniform volume bound.
The cycles have supports in
\pi^{-1}(K)
, and their parameter set is

2.4
makes this set compact.
Thus
\Lambda^{*}\to\Delta^{*}
is proper. Its image is analytic by
Remmert’s Theorem
2.5
and contains

;
hence it is all of
\Delta^{*}
.
∎

3.3.
The mass estimate on an arbitrary component

The remaining issue is to bound divisor masses uniformly near the
exceptional parameter. Lemma
2.18
provides
the crucial link between the sheaf-theoretic obstruction and the analytic
estimate: it supplies a finite exponent
N
with
t^{N}o(c)=0
near
0
,
without assuming torsion-freeness of
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
.
The case
N=0
recovers the unobstructed situation underlying
Rao–Tsai’s torsion-freeness method; the phase estimate handles
N>0
.
We first turn this annihilation relation into a smooth primitive on the
total space, and then express
the mass as a smooth term plus a term to which the phase lemma applies.
Nonnegativity of the masses yields the required lower bound and hence
the uniform estimate. The argument uses only the fixed integral class
of the component and does not require a good filling.

Lemma 3.3

(A primitive from finite-order torsion)
.

Let
c\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, let
D_{0}\subset\Delta
be a disc about
0
,
and suppose
t^{N}o(c)=0
on
D_{0}
for an integer
N\geq 0
.
There are a real smooth
d
-closed two-form
\alpha
representing
the real image of the corresponding total-space class and a smooth
absolute
(0,1)
-form
u
on
\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}}
such that

(3.4)

\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{X}}u=t^{N}\alpha^{0,2}_{\mathcal{X}}.

In particular,
\bar{\partial}_{t}u_{t}=t^{N}\alpha_{t}^{0,2}
on each fiber.

Proof.

An Ehresmann trivialization identifies
c
with a total-space
topological class on
\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}}
. Choose a real smooth closed
representative
\alpha
of its real image. Coherence, Cartan’s
Theorem
2.15
, and the Leray spectral sequence give

H^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}}})\simeq H^{0}(D_{0},R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}).

Under the Dolbeault resolution,
o(c)
corresponds to the class of

\alpha^{0,2}_{\mathcal{X}}
, which is
\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{X}}
-closed since

d\alpha=0
. Multiplication by
t^{N}
on the right corresponds to
multiplication by the holomorphic function
\pi^{*}t^{N}
on the left.
Thus
t^{N}o(c)=0
says that
t^{N}\alpha^{0,2}_{\mathcal{X}}
has zero
Dolbeault class. The fine Dolbeault resolution supplies a global
smooth
u
satisfying (
3.4
),
also on the noncompact manifold
\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}}
. Restriction to a fiber
commutes with
\bar{\partial}
, giving the final assertion.
∎

In the following estimate the integral is taken over a divisor
Z

parametrized by
\Lambda
, not over the parameter space
\Lambda
.
The estimate includes reducible and nonreduced divisors.

Proposition 3.4

(Componentwise mass bound)
.

Let
\Lambda
be any irreducible component of
\mathscr{C}
.
For every compact set
K\Subset\Delta
, there is a constant

C_{\Lambda,K}
such that

(3.5)

0\leq\int_{Z}G_{t}\leq C_{\Lambda,K}\qquad(t\in K,\ Z\in\Lambda_{t}).

No good-filling assumption on
\Lambda
is required.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is vertical, it is compact by
Lemma
3.2
. Continuity of cycle
integration, Lemma
2.12
, gives the bound.
Hence assume
\Lambda
is horizontal, and let
c
be its fixed

3.2
gives the bound on
compact subsets of
\Delta^{*}
and nonemptiness of
\Lambda_{t}
for
every

0

Since
S(c)\subset\{0\}
, Lemma
2.18

gives a disc
D_{0}
about
0
and the finite exponent
N=N(0)\geq 0

with
t^{N}o(c)=0
on
D_{0}
. Here

precisely when the obstruction
vanishes near
0
. This finite-order control, rather than vanishing
of the obstruction itself, is the essential input for the following
construction.
Lemma
3.3
supplies a real
smooth
d
-closed two-form
\alpha

representing the real image of
c
and a smooth absolute
(0,1)
-form

on
\mathcal{X}_{D_{0}}

\bar{\partial}_{\mathcal{X}}u=t^{N}\alpha^{0,2}_{\mathcal{X}}.

For

\beta_{t}=t^{-N}u_{t}+\bar{t}^{-N}\bar{u}_{t},\qquad\eta_{t}=\alpha_{t}-d_{t}\beta_{t}.

The form

is real and
d_{t}
-closed. The fiberwise primitive

(d_{t}\beta_{t})^{0,2}=t^{-N}\bar{\partial}_{t}u_{t}=\alpha_{t}^{0,2},\qquad(d_{t}\beta_{t})^{2,0}=\bar{t}^{-N}\partial_{t}\bar{u}_{t}=\alpha_{t}^{2,0}.

Thus the two unwanted components cancel, so
\eta_{t}
is of type

(1,1)

leaves its real
de Rham class unchanged.
For every

, the current
[Z]-\eta_{t}
is
d_{t}
-exact.
Since

satisfies the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma for

eliminate this

\int_{X_{t}}\eta_{t}\wedge G_{t}=\int_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t}\wedge G_{t}-\int_{X_{t}}\beta_{t}\wedge d_{t}G_{t}.

Only

and its conjugate have top bidegree

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{Z}G_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{X_{t}}\eta_{t}\wedge G_{t}=B(t)+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(t^{-N}A(t)\right),

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t}\wedge G_{t},\qquad A(t):=-\int_{X_{t}}u_{t}\wedge\partial_{t}G_{t}.

Here reality of

gives

\int_{X_{t}}\bar{u}_{t}\wedge\bar{\partial}_{t}G_{t}=\overline{\int_{X_{t}}u_{t}\wedge\partial_{t}G_{t}}
, explaining the

and the real part. Smooth fiber integration shows that

and

are smooth across

,

B
is real, and the expression is independent of

within the
fixed component and fiber.

Choose a disc
0\in D\Subset D_{0}
. For every

,
Lemma
3.2
supplies an effective divisor
in
\Lambda_{t}
. Thus (

) is
nonnegative in every angular direction. Boundedness of

\operatorname{Re}\!\left(t^{-N}A(t)\right)\geq-\tfrac{1}{2}\sup_{\overline{D}}|B|.

For

implies

so (

For

\Lambda

over a sufficiently small punctured disc. No estimate on the full

is needed.

and continuity of cycle integration extend

\Lambda_{0}

\Delta
.
∎

3.4.
Properness and the main theorem

We now convert the mass estimate into properness of every component
over the entire disc by Bishop compactness. Barlet’s local
algebraic-dimension theorem then gives Theorem
1.3
.
We also explain the alternative good-filling conclusion and finish
with the case of uncountably many Moishezon fibers.

Proposition 3.5

(Properness of every component)
.

Every irreducible component of
\mathscr{C}
is proper over
\Delta
.
Every horizontal component maps surjectively onto
\Delta
.

Proof.

Fix an irreducible component
\Lambda
of
\mathscr{C}
and a compact set

K\Subset\Delta
. Proposition
3.4

bounds the
G_{t}
-masses of all its divisors over
K
. Since
G_{t}
is
smooth and strictly positive, comparison on the compact set

\pi^{-1}(K)
with a fixed Hermitian metric bounds their volumes.
The set
\Lambda\cap\mu^{-1}(K)
is closed, so
Lemma
2.4
makes it compact. This is properness
over
\Delta
. For horizontal
\Lambda
, its image is closed and
contains
\Delta^{*}
by Lemma
3.2
;
it therefore equals
\Delta
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The proper holomorphic submersion
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\Delta
is surjective
and
n
-equidimensional, with irreducible total space and base.
Proposition
3.5
verifies properness
of every irreducible component of its relative divisor space.
Theorem
2.7
, namely

[
Ba15
, Proposition 4.0.8]
, now gives an integer

a_{\mathrm{gen}}\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and a countable union
\Sigma

of proper closed analytic subsets of
\Delta
such that

a(X_{t})=a_{\mathrm{gen}}\quad(t\in\Delta\setminus\Sigma),\qquad a(X_{t})\geq a_{\mathrm{gen}}\quad(t\in\Delta).

As
\Sigma
is countable, every nonempty punctured neighbourhood of
every

meets

. Consequently

\inf_{0<|t-s|<\varepsilon}a(X_{t})=a_{\mathrm{gen}}\leq a(X_{s})

whenever the indicated disc is contained in
\Delta
.
This also proves (
1.2
). No separate argument
is needed when
a_{\mathrm{gen}}=0
.
∎

Remark 3.6

(The good-filling route)
.

One can alternatively follow a selected local good filling rather
than use Barlet’s componentwise criterion at the last step.
The local algebraic-dimension
theorem on
\Delta^{*}
first gives its common very general value

a_{\mathrm{gen}}
. If this value is positive,
Proposition
2.23
supplies
\Gamma_{U}
whose
generic Kodaira-image dimension is
a_{U}\geq a_{\mathrm{gen}}
.
Lemma
2.24
embeds it as a local component of
a full global component
\Gamma
.
Proposition
3.5
makes
\Gamma

proper over
\Delta
.

To recover a good filling, consider the addition map for pairs of
nonzero effective relative cycles whose sum belongs to
\Gamma
.
Locally over
\Gamma
this map is proper: the masses of both summands
are bounded by that of the sum, Bishop compactness gives convergent
subsequences, and the monotonicity inequality gives a positive lower
bound for nonzero cycle masses over a compact part of the base.
Neither summand can therefore disappear in the limit. Remmert’s
Theorem
2.5
makes the reducible-cycle locus
closed analytic.
Since
\Gamma_{U}
supplies irreducible members on a nonempty
open subset, the generic member of
\Gamma
is irreducible.
The incidence image is closed analytic by properness and Remmert’s
Theorem
2.5
; it contains
\mathcal{X}_{U}
, hence equals
\mathcal{X}
.
Thus
\Gamma
is a good filling.

One must also retain the local Kodaira-image dimension. On the
general locus of
U
, write
d=\dim\Gamma_{U,t}
and choose the
common open subset
\Omega_{t}
where the local and global parameter
spaces agree. Its complement
R_{t}
in
\Gamma_{U,t}
has dimension
at most
d-1
. The incidence graph over
R_{t}
has dimension at most

d+n-2
, so over a general
x\in X_{t}
it contains no component of
the pure
(d-1)
-dimensional local incidence cycle. That cycle is
therefore recovered, with multiplicities, from its restriction to

\Omega_{t}
. Barlet’s relative Kodaira construction

[
Ba15
, § 2.2]
identifies the local and global incidence cycles
on this open set. Equality of global incidence cycles implies
equality of local ones, giving generic image dimension
q\geq a_{U}
.
Proposition
2.22
concludes

a(X_{0})\geq q\geq a_{U}\geq a_{\mathrm{gen}}
; the case

a_{\mathrm{gen}}=0
is immediate. The proof above retains the
same phase calculation but uses componentwise properness to invoke
Barlet’s theorem directly, so neither these good-filling checks nor
the local–global Kodaira comparison need be repeated.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
h:=\min_{t\in\Delta}h^{1}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}})
. By Grauert’s
semicontinuity Theorem
2.13
, the set

E:=\{t\in\Delta:h^{1}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}})\geq h+1\}

is a proper closed analytic subset of
\Delta
, hence discrete.
At every
s\in\Delta\setminus E
, the Hodge number
h^{0,1}(X_{t})
is
locally constant. Apply
[
RT21
, Theorem 1.4]
after an automorphism
of the unit disc sending
s
to
0
. The theorem requires uncountably
many Moishezon parameters in the whole disc, not necessarily accumulating
at the reference point, so it gives that
X_{s}
is Moishezon.

For each
p\in E
, choose a disc
D\Subset\Delta
with
D\cap E=\{p\}
.
Every fiber over

is Moishezon. Applying

and recentering the base, shows that

∎

4.
Scope, necessity, and test families

The final section tests the conclusion against explicit families.
The Fujiki–Pontecorvo examples explain the role of the cohomological
hypothesis and the difference between a Chern class and a chosen line
bundle. The K3 families then distinguish the infimum inequality proved
here from ordinary upper semicontinuity, even when every fiber is
Kähler.

We distinguish throughout this section between
ordinary upper
semicontinuity
at a parameter
t_{0}
, expressed by

(4.1)

a(X_{t_{0}})\geq\limsup_{t\to t_{0}}a(X_{t}),

and the weaker
punctured-neighbourhood infimum inequality

(4.2)

a(X_{t_{0}})\geq\inf_{0<|t-t_{0}|<\varepsilon}a(X_{t}).

Inequality (
4.1
) may fail in smooth families all of whose fibers are
Kähler. Theorem
1.3
asserts that the very general value is
the global minimum and, consequently, gives the second inequality.
It asserts neither the first inequality nor upper semicontinuity for
the ordinary analytic Zariski topology.

4.1.
The Fujiki–Pontecorvo jump

The Fujiki–Pontecorvo family shows that smoothness alone does not prevent
a downward jump in algebraic dimension.

Let
S
be a half Inoue surface with
b_{2}(S)=m
and let
C
be its unique
twisted anticanonical curve. Fujiki–Pontecorvo study the Kuranishi
family of the pair

g:(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow T.

The base
T
is smooth of dimension
m
and contains a normal-crossings
divisor
A=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}
. The central fiber is
S_{0}=S
.
For
t\in A\setminus\{0\}
, the fiber is a
blown-up half Inoue surface; for
t\notin A
, it is a blown-up elliptic
diagonal Hopf surface. Moreover,

a(S_{t})=0\quad(t\in A),\qquad a(S_{t})=1\quad(t\notin A).

These statements follow from

[
FP10
, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2, and Proposition 1.3]
.

For
m=1
, after shrinking,
T=\Delta
and
A=\{0\}
, so

[
FP10
, Example following Proposition 1.3]
gives

(4.3)

a(S_{0})=0<1=\inf_{t\in\Delta^{*}}a(S_{t}).

Thus both (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) fail at the
central parameter. However, every fiber is a class VII surface with

b_{1}=1
, whereas the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma implies

H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{1,0}_{\bar{\partial}}(X)\oplus H^{0,1}_{\bar{\partial}}(X),\qquad h^{1,0}=h^{0,1},

and hence even
b_{1}=2h^{0,1}
. Thus no fiber satisfies that lemma, and
(
4.3
) shows why the cohomological hypothesis of
Theorem
1.3
cannot be dropped. The punctured fibers are not
Moishezon, since
a(S_{t})=1<2
.

Fujiki–Pontecorvo also record higher-dimensional variants in

[
FP10
, Remark 1.1]
. The products
S_{t}^{n}
and the Douady spaces

S_{t}^{[n]}
have dimension
2n
and algebraic dimension
0
on
A
and

n
off
A
; none is Moishezon.

For the products, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma would descend along the
holomorphic retract
S_{t}^{n}\rightleftarrows S_{t}
, so it still fails. For
the Douady spaces,

b_{1}(S_{t}^{[n]})=b_{1}(S_{t})=1
by the Göttsche formula in

[
DM00
, Theorem 5.2.1.(2)]
, so it also fails. The downward jump can
therefore be arbitrarily large outside our hypotheses. Every punctured
fiber has algebraic dimension
n
only when
m=1
, or on a disc meeting

A
only at its central point.

4.2.
The two Fujiki–Pontecorvo line bundles

This family also distinguishes extension of a specified punctured line
bundle from realization of its Chern class on the total space.

In the one-parameter case,
S_{t}
for
t\neq 0
has a canonical elliptic
fibration
f_{t}:S_{t}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. A fiber decomposes as

F_{t}=C_{t}+E_{t},

where
E_{t}
is the unique
(-1)
-curve. Consider

(4.4)

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\mathcal{O}_{S_{t}}(F_{t})=f_{t}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{t}:=\mathcal{O}_{S_{t}}(E_{t}).

Fujiki–Pontecorvo prove in
[
FP10
, Remark 3.2.(2)]
that neither
prescribed punctured relative bundle extends across the origin.
Indeed, since
h^{0}(S_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t})=2
, an extension
\mathcal{F}
would satisfy

h^{0}(S_{0},\mathcal{F}_{0})\geq 2
by upper semicontinuity. The quotient of two
independent sections would be a nonconstant meromorphic function on

S_{0}
, contrary to
a(S_{0})=0
.

The curve
C_{t}
extends in the Kuranishi family, and

\mathcal{F}_{t}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{S_{t}}(C_{t})\otimes\mathcal{E}_{t};

so an extension of
\mathcal{E}_{t}
would also extend
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, a contradiction.

This concerns only the representatives in (
4.4
), not
every line bundle with the same fiberwise
c_{1}
; it therefore does not
establish
o(c)\neq 0
. Under its Hodge hypothesis, our theorem would yield
a good filling and
a(X_{0})\geq 1
, not extension of either prescribed bundle.

Neither bundle in (
4.4
) is big:
\mathcal{F}_{t}
has Iitaka
dimension one because it defines the elliptic pencil, and a big line bundle
on the surface
S_{t}
would make
S_{t}
Moishezon, contrary to

a(S_{t})=1<2
.

4.3.
Huybrechts’ twistor family

Twistor families distinguish the two semicontinuity inequalities even
when all fibers are Kähler.

Let
(S,L)
be a polarized K3 surface with
\rho(S)=1
, and choose the
Ricci-flat Kähler metric whose Kähler class is proportional to

c_{1}(L)
. Consider the associated twistor family

\mathscr{S}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Every fiber, including those on the real equator, is a K3 surface and
hence Kähler by Y.-T. Siu’s theorem
[
Si83
, Main Theorem]
; all satisfy the

\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma.

For a fixed rational class
\gamma
, type
(1,1)
means orthogonality to
the moving period line. Unless this holds identically, it cuts the
twistor conic in at most two points.

Write
T(S)=\operatorname{NS}(S)^{\perp}\subset H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z})
for the

transcendental lattice
.
Classes orthogonal to the entire positive twistor three-plane remain of
type
(1,1)
on every fiber. Under the rank-one hypothesis no nonzero
rational class does so: it would lie in
T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}
and be of type

(1,1)
on the original fiber, contradicting
\rho(S)=1
.

The rational Hodge structure
T(S)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is irreducible and has signature

(2,19)
. D. Huybrechts’ description therefore applies: the locus where an
additional rational
(1,1)
-class occurs is countable and dense, by

[
Hu22
, Introduction and Proposition 3.2]
; see also

[
Hu23
, § 1.3]
.

A very general twistor fiber has Picard number zero and hence algebraic
dimension zero, whereas the projective fibers are countable and dense.
Thus, on a small coordinate disc about a very general parameter
t_{0}
,

a(\mathscr{S}_{t_{0}})=0<2=\limsup_{t\to t_{0}}a(\mathscr{S}_{t}),\qquad\inf_{0<|t-t_{0}|<\varepsilon}a(\mathscr{S}_{t})=0.

Thus ordinary upper semicontinuity (
4.1
) fails although
every fiber is Kähler, while the weaker inequality
(
4.2
) holds with equality. The dense projective fibers
do not give a stronger infimum bound: their polarizations arise from
different integral classes and do not form one relative good filling.

4.4.
Huybrechts’ Brauer family

In the Brauer family, a persistent genus-one fibration supplies an
explicit proper good filling and a common line bundle, even on
nonalgebraic fibers.

Let
p:S\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a projective elliptic K3 surface with a section.
Huybrechts reviews the Brauer family

\varpi:\mathscr{S}\longrightarrow H^{0,2}(S)\simeq\mathbb{C}

[

construction through the analytic Tate–Shafarevich group is given in

[

Let

be the image of

under the natural map to the analytic Brauer group.
By
[

by the natural projection.

Every fiber is a K3 surface, hence Kähler by
[
Si83
, Main Theorem]
, and has

a(S_{t})\geq 1
by the genus-one fibration

[

a(S_{t})=\begin{cases}2,&t\in T(S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q},\\

To exhibit the good filling, for
(b,t)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Delta
let

. The classifying map

is holomorphic by Theorem
2.2
.
(i)
–
(ii)
,
since

is proper and flat and its fundamental fiber cycles form an
analytic family.

Properness of

makes
\Phi
proper over

\Delta
. By Theorem
2.5
, its image
\Gamma
is
irreducible, analytic, and
\Delta

irreducible and its incidence graph covers
\mathscr{S}_{\Delta}
, so

\Gamma
is a good filling.

Its relative Kodaira map

has one-dimensional image on every fiber,
and the common fiber class is realized by

so its obstruction support is empty. Barlet’s endgame gives

a(S_{0})\geq 1=\inf_{t\in\Delta^{*}}a(S_{t}).

The image of

is countable and

is real-linearly surjective. Its complement is also

and for every small

Thus (

4.2

equality. At

has algebraic

The dense algebraic parameters do not
produce a relative big class: their polarizations need not arise from one

A.1.
Deformation invariance of plurigenera

The first application to a generically fiberwise Moishezon family is the deformation invariance of plurigenera.
For any positive integer

the
m

Similarly, a compact complex variety

.

Z
. Wherever
f_{i}\neq 0
, elementary row and column
operations give

\operatorname{rank}J=1+\operatorname{rank}\bigl(d_{\mathcal{X}/\Delta}(f_{j}/f_{i})\bigr)_{j\neq i}.

Thus, away from the base locus,
Z
is exactly the rank-defect locus
of the relative differential of
\phi
. Since
\phi_{t_{0}}
is an
embedding,
Z\cap X_{t_{0}}=\varnothing
.

By Theorem
2.5
,
\pi(Z)
is a proper closed analytic subset of

\Delta
, hence is discrete. For
U:=\Delta\setminus\pi(Z)
and

s\in U
, the sections have no common zero on
X_{s}
and define a
holomorphic immersion
\phi_{s}:X_{s}\to\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
with

\mathcal{E}|_{X_{s}}\simeq\phi_{s}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N-1}}(1).

The pullback of the Fubini–Study metric has strictly positive curvature,
so
\mathcal{E}|_{X_{s}}
, and hence
\mathcal{L}|_{X_{s}}
, is ample by the Kodaira
embedding theorem. In particular, every
X_{s}
with
s\in U
is
projective. The nonprojective locus is a subset of the closed discrete
set
\pi(Z)
, hence is itself closed analytic in
\Delta
.
Therefore
\mathcal{P}(\pi)
is a dense analytic Zariski open subset.
∎

A.3.
Barlet’s theory of relative codimension-one cycle spaces

As the final (and direct) application of Theorem
1.5
, one has:

Corollary A.6
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\Delta
be a smooth family with uncountably many Moishezon fibers. Then the restriction of

\mu:\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)\rightarrow\Delta

to each irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}_{n-1}(\mathcal{X}/\Delta)
is proper over
\Delta
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.5
and
Lemma
2.20
.
∎

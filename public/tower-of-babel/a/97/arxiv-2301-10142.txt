A novel boundary integral formulation for the biharmonic wave scattering problem Thanks: The first author was supported in part by the NSFC grant 12171201. The second author was supported partially by the NSF grant DMS-2208256.
Heping Dong, Peijun Li

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the cavity scattering problem in an infinite thin plate, where the out-of-plane displacement is governed by the two-dimensional biharmonic wave equation. Based on an operator splitting, the scattering problem is recast into a coupled boundary value problem for the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations. A novel boundary integral formulation is proposed for the coupled problem. By introducing an appropriate regularizer, the well-posedness is established for the system of boundary integral equations. Moreover, the convergence analysis is carried out for the semi- and full-discrete schemes of the boundary integral system by using the collocation method. Numerical results show that the proposed method is highly accurate for both smooth and nonsmooth examples.

1.
Introduction

Scattering problems for biharmonic waves have significant applications in thin plate elasticity. For instance, the ultra-broadband elastic cloaking has been experimentally realized at acoustic frequencies and could serve as a model for seismic cloaks to protect infrastructure from earthquakes
[
5
,
24
]
. Similar to photonic and phononic crystals, platonic crystals (PlaCs) are fabricated by using regularly spaced arrays of holes, called cavities, cut into thin elastic plates
[
9
,
18
]
. The design of PlaCs can be utilized to guide or harness
destructive wave energy for constructive purposes
[
4
,
22
,
25
]
. As an emerging passive vibration control technique, the acoustic black hole (ABH) has recently been developed for vibration and noise reduction of a system
[
20
]
. The basic idea of ABH is to arrange some inhomogeneous medium in a thin beam or plate so that the wave can be trapped from propagating. In the wave motion of these structures, the out-of-plane displacement of the thin plate satisfies the biharmonic wave equation. Although the scattering theory has been well developed for acoustic, elastic, and electromagnetic waves, many scattering problems remain unsolved for biharmonic waves. This paper concerns the biharmonic wave scattering of a time-harmonic plane wave by a cavity in an infinite thin plate. A novel boundary integral formulation is proposed for the boundary value problem and a spectrally accurate collocation method is developed for the boundary integral system.

The method of boundary integral equations has several advantages: the solution is represented by surface distributions which leads to fewer unknowns; the radiation condition is exactly enforced that avoids the truncation error of artificial boundary conditions; the surface geometry can be represented to high precision using appropriate boundary elements. However, in a conventional boundary integral method, these advantages may be offset by two factors: the high cost of evaluating interactions among all the boundary elements and the need to evaluate singular integrals. The former can be resolved by fast evaluation methods such as the panel clustering algorithm
[
8
]
or the fast multipole method
[
7
]
, and the latter has to be treated using special quadrature formulas or analytical expansions. Despite the difficulties, the boundary integral method provides an efficient approach for solving many problems, especially for those imposed in unbounded domains
[
1
,
2
]
.

We consider the cavity scattering problem in an infinite thin elastic plate, where the wave motion is governed by the two-dimensional biharmonic wave equation. As an exterior boundary value problem, it is analogous to the obstacle scattering problem for the acoustic, elastic, or electromagnetic wave equation. Compared with the obstacle scattering problem, the cavity scattering problem is much less investigated. A numerical study was given in
[
22
]
on the boundary integral method for solving the biharmonic wave scattering by smooth cavities embedded in thin plates. In
[
23
]
, boundary integral equations were deduced for cavities with different boundary conditions, and the full discretization of the boundary integral system was discussed by expanding the unknown boundary values in terms of Fourier series. However, the mathematical and numerical analysis are not available on the method of boundary integral equations for solving the biharmonic wave scattering problem.

Motivated by
[
3
]
, we present the first theoretical analysis on the biharmonic wave scattering problem. Based on a splitting of the biharmonic wave operator, two auxiliary functions are introduced to represent the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz wave components, respectively. The scattering problem is recast into a coupled boundary value problem for the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations. The uniquneness is established for the scattering problem by utilizing Rellich’s lemma and the exponentially decaying property of the modified Helmholtz wave component. By the double- and single-layer potentials representing the Helmholtz and modified Helmoltz wave components, respectively, a novel boundary integral formulation is proposed for the coupled boundary value problem. Following
[
3
,
17
]
, we introduce a regularizer to the boundary integral system and split the singular operator into an isomorphic operator plus a compact one. The Riesz–Fredholm theory is then employed to show the well-posedness of the coupled boundary integral system. Moreover, the collocation method is empolyed to examine the semi- and full-discretization of the boundary integral equations. Special quadratures are adopted for the hypersingular and logarithmic singular integrals to carry out the convergence analysis for both schemes. In
[
16
,
13
]
, the quadratures were investigated on the similar hypersingular and logarithmic singular integrals for solving the acoustic obstacle scattering problem. In
[
14
]
, a collocation method was developed and the convergence was analyzed by using the trigonometric interpolation to discretize the principal part of the hypersingular operator. We refer to
[
11
,
19
,
21
]
for studies on general singular integral equations. In addition to the convergence analysis, numerical experiments are provided to demonstrate the high accuracy of the proposed method for both smooth and nonsmooth examples.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In section
2
, the scattering problem is introduced and the uniqueness is proved for the boundary value problem. Section
3
presents the boundary integral equations and discusses the well-posedness of the coupled system. The convergence analysis is carried out in section
4
for the semi- and full-discrete schemes of the system. Section
5
presents numerical experiments to verify the theoretical findings and to demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method. Section
6
concludes the paper with remarks and future work.

2.
Problem formulation

We consider the biharmonic wave scattering problem in a two-dimensional thin plate with a cavity, which is represented by a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with an analytic boundary
\Gamma
. The plate is assumed to be made of a homogeneous and isotropic elastic medium in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
. Let
\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})^{\top}
and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})^{\top}
be the tangential and normal vectors on
\Gamma
, respectively. It is clear to note
\tau_{1}=-\nu_{2}
and
\tau_{2}=\nu_{1}
. The plate is excited by a time-harmonic plane wave

u^{\rm inc}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\kappa x\cdot d},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(2.1)

where
\kappa>0
is the wavenumber and
d=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^{\top}
is the incident direction with
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
being the incident angle.

The out-of-plane displacement of the plate
u
satisfies the two-dimensional biharmonic wave equation

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}u-\kappa^{4}u=0\quad{\rm in}\,\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}.

(2.2)

Assuming that the cavity satisfies the clamped boundary condition, we have

\displaystyle u=\partial_{\nu}u=0\quad{\rm on}\,\Gamma.

(2.3)

The total displacement
u
is composed of the incident wave field
u^{\rm inc}
and the scattered field
v
, i.e.,

u=u^{\rm inc}+v.

It can be verified from (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) that the scattered field
v
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}v-\kappa^{4}v=0\quad{\rm in}\,\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}.

(2.4)

By (
2.3
),
v
satisfies the following boundary conditions on
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle v=-u^{\rm inc},\quad\partial_{\nu}v=-\partial_{\nu}u^{\rm inc}.

(2.5)

In addition,
v
and
\Delta v
are required to satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{r}v-\mathrm{i}\kappa v)=0,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{r}\Delta v-\mathrm{i}\kappa\Delta v)=0,\quad r=|x|.

(2.6)

Consider two auxiliary functions

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}(\Delta v-\kappa^{2}v),\quad v_{\rm M}=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}(\Delta v+\kappa^{2}v).

(2.7)

It is clear to note
v=v_{\rm H}+v_{\rm M}
and
\Delta v=\kappa^{2}(v_{\rm M}-v_{\rm H})
. From (
2.4
), we have

(\Delta-\kappa^{2})(\Delta+\kappa^{2})v=0\quad{\rm in}\,\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D},

which implies that
v_{\rm H}
and
v_{\rm M}
satisfy the Helmholtz equation and the modified Helmholtz equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
, respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Delta v_{\rm H}+\kappa^{2}v_{\rm H}=0,\quad\Delta v_{\rm M}-\kappa^{2}v_{\rm M}=0.

(2.8)

By (
2.5
),
v_{\rm H}
and
v_{\rm M}
satisfy the coupled boundary conditions on
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}+v_{\rm M}=f_{1},\quad\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}+\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm M}=f_{2},

(2.9)

where
f_{1}=-u^{\rm inc}
and
f_{2}=-\partial_{\nu}u^{\rm inc}
. Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) yields that
v_{\rm H}
and
v_{\rm M}
satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{r}v_{\rm H}-\mathrm{i}\kappa v_{\rm H})=0,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{r}v_{\rm M}-\mathrm{i}\kappa v_{\rm M})=0,\quad r=|x|.

(2.10)

Clearly, the scattering problems (
2.4
)–(
2.6
) and (
2.8
)–(
2.10
) are equivalent. Hence, it suffices to consider the scattering problem (
2.8
)–(
2.10
), where
v_{\rm H}
and
v_{\rm M}
are called the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz wave components, respectively.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
v_{\rm M}
be a solution to the scattering problem (
2.8
)–(
2.10
). Denote
D\subset B_{a}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|<a\}
. Then
v_{\rm M}
has the Fourier series expansion

v_{\rm M}(r,\theta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{K_{n}(\kappa r)}{K_{n}(\kappa a)}\hat{v}^{(n)}_{\rm M}(a)e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta},\quad r=|x|>a,

where
\hat{v}^{(n)}_{\rm M}(a)
are the Fourier coefficients of
v_{\rm M}
on
\partial B_{a}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|=a\}
given by

\hat{v}^{(n)}_{\rm M}(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}v_{\rm M}(a,\theta)e^{-\mathrm{i}n\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta

and
K_{n}(z)
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind with order
n
. By
[
26
, p. 511]
,
K_{n}(z)
has no zeros for
|\arg z|\leq\pi/2
, which implies that
K_{n}(\kappa a)\neq 0
. Moreover, we have from
[
26
, §3.7 and §7.23]
that
K_{n}(t)=K_{-n}(t)
and it admits the asymptotic expansion

K_{n}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2t}}e^{-t}\Big\{1+O\big(\frac{1}{t}\big)\Big\},\quad t\to+\infty,\quad t>0,

which shows that
v_{\rm M}
and
\partial_{r}v_{\rm M}
decay exponentially as
r\to\infty
for a fixed
\kappa
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The boundary value problem (
2.8
)–(
2.10
) has at most one solution.

Proof.

It is required to show that
v_{\rm H}=v_{\rm M}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
when
f_{1}=f_{2}=0
. Applying Green’s theorem in
D_{a}:=B_{a}\setminus\overline{D}
and the boundary condition (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{a}}v_{\rm H}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm H}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{a}}\big(v_{\rm H}\Delta\overline{v_{\rm H}}+\nabla v_{\rm H}\cdot\nabla\overline{v_{\rm H}}\big)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}v_{\rm H}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm H}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{a}}\big(-\kappa^{2}|v_{\rm H}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{\rm H}|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{a}}v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{a}}\big(\kappa^{2}|v_{\rm M}|^{2}+|\nabla v_{\rm M}|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}}\mathrm{d}s,

which give

\Im\int_{\partial B_{a}}v_{\rm H}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm H}}\mathrm{d}s=\Im\int_{\Gamma}v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}}\mathrm{d}s=\Im\int_{\partial B_{a}}v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}}\mathrm{d}s.

It follows from the above equation and the Sommerfeld radiation condition (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{a}}|\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}-\mathrm{i}\kappa v_{\rm H}|^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{a}}\big\{|\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}|^{2}+\kappa^{2}|v_{\rm H}|^{2}+2\kappa\Im(v_{\rm H}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm H}})\big\}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\lim_{a\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{a}}\big\{|\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}|^{2}+\kappa^{2}|v_{\rm H}|^{2}+2\kappa\Im(v_{\rm M}\partial_{\nu}\overline{v_{\rm M}})\big\}\mathrm{d}s=0.

(2.11)

By Remark
2.1
and (
2
), we have

\lim_{a\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{a}}\big\{|\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}|^{2}+\kappa^{2}|v_{\rm H}|^{2}\big\}\mathrm{d}s=0.

Using Rellich’s lemma yields
v_{\rm H}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
. It follows from the continuity of
v_{\rm H}
and
\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}
and (
2.9
) that
v_{\rm H}=\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}=0
and
v_{\rm M}=\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm M}=0
on
\Gamma
, which shows
v_{\rm M}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
by Green’s representation theorem (cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
).
∎

3.
Boundary integral formulation

In this section, we propose a novel formulation of boundary integral equations for the scattering problem (
2.8
)–(
2.10
). Based on the Riesz–Fredholm theory, a regularizer is constructed to split the operator equation into the form of an isomorphic operator plus a compact one.

3.1.
Coupled integral equations

Denote by
G_{\rm H}
and
G_{\rm M}
the fundamental solutions to the two-dimensional Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations, respectively. Explicitly, we have

G_{\rm H}(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa|x-y|),\quad G_{\rm M}(x,y)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(\mathrm{i}\kappa|x-y|),

where
H_{0}^{(1)}
is the Hankel function of the first kind with order zero. Using the fundamental solutions, we represent the solutions of (
2.8
) as the double- and single-layer potentials with densities
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}g_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),\quad v_{\rm M}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm M}(x,y)g_{2}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{D}.

(3.1)

It follows from the solution representation (
3.1
), the boundary conditions (
2.5
), and the jump relations of the single- and double-layer potentials, we may deduce the boundary integral equations on
\Gamma
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}g_{1}(x)+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}g_{1}(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm M}(x,y)g_{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)&=-u^{\rm inc}(x),\\
\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)\partial\nu(y)}g_{1}(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)-\frac{1}{2}g_{2}(x)+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm M}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}g_{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y)&=-\frac{\partial u^{\rm inc}(x)}{\partial\nu(x)}.\end{split}

(3.2)

For
x\in\Gamma
, introduce the following integral operators:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}g)(x)=2\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}g(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}g)(x)=2\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\frac{\partial^{2}G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)\partial\nu(y)}g(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}g)(x)=2\int_{\Gamma_{D}}\frac{\partial G_{\rm M}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}g(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}g)(x)=2\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm M}(x,y)g(y)\mathrm{d}s(y).

Since the fundamental solution
G_{\rm M}
decays exponentially, the modified Helmholtz wave component
v_{\rm M}
given in (
3.1
) does not propagate. Hence we may obtain from (
3.1
) that the far-field pattern of the scattered wave
v
is

\displaystyle v_{\infty}(\hat{x})=\varrho\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial e^{-\mathrm{i}\kappa\hat{x}\cdot y}}{\partial\nu(y)}g_{1}(y)\,\mathrm{d}s(y),

(3.3)

where
\varrho=e^{\mathrm{i}\pi/4}/{\sqrt{8\kappa\pi}}
and
\hat{x}=x/r
is the observation direction.

With the help of the integral operators, it is convenient to rewrite (
3.2
) into the operator form

\begin{cases}g_{1}+\mathcal{L}g_{1}+\mathcal{S}g_{2}=2f_{1},\\
\mathcal{T}g_{1}-g_{2}+\mathcal{K}g_{2}=2f_{2}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

Theorem 3.1
.

The coupled boundary integral equations (
3.4
) has at most one solution provided that the wavenumber
\kappa
is not the Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Helmholtz equation in
D
.

Proof.

It is required to show that
g_{1}=g_{2}=0
if
f_{1}=f_{2}=0
. Let

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}(x)=\begin{cases}v_{\rm H}^{i},&x\in D,\\
v_{\rm H}^{e},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D},\end{cases}\qquad v_{\rm M}(x)=\begin{cases}v_{\rm M}^{i},&x\in D,\\
v_{\rm M}^{e},&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}.\end{cases}

Since
v_{\rm H}^{e}
and
v_{\rm M}^{e}
satisfy the boundary value problem (
2.8
)–(
2.10
), it follows from
Theorem
2.2
that they are identically zero in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
. Using the jump relations of the single- and double-layer potentials, we have on
\Gamma
that

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}^{e}-v_{\rm H}^{i}=g_{1},\quad v_{\rm M}^{e}-v_{\rm M}^{i}=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}^{e}-\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm H}^{i}=0,\quad\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm M}^{e}-\partial_{\nu}v_{\rm M}^{i}=-g_{2}.

Since neither
\kappa
nor
\mathrm{i}\kappa
is the Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Helmholtz equation in
D
, we deduce
v_{\rm H}^{i}=v_{\rm M}^{i}=0
in
D
, which implies
g_{1}=g_{2}=0
.
∎

By Theorem
3.1
, the uniqueness holds for the coupled system (
3.4
) if the wavenumber
\kappa
is not the Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Helmholtz equation in
D
. In general, this assumption can be removed by using combined double- and single-layer potentials for the boundary integral formulation
[
1
]
. In this work, we assume that this assumption is satisfied and the coupled system (
3.4
) has at most one solution.

3.2.
Parameterization

Let the analytic boundary
\Gamma
have a parametrization of the form

\Gamma=\{\gamma(t)=(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0\leq t<2\pi\},

where
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are
2\pi
-periodic functions satisfying
|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|>0
for
t\in[0,2\pi)
. Plugging the parameterization of
\Gamma
into
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{S},\mathcal{K}
, and
\mathcal{T}
, we define the parameterized integral operators

\displaystyle(L\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}l(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

\displaystyle(K\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}k(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

\displaystyle(S\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

\displaystyle(R\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}r(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

\displaystyle(H\phi)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}h(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where the integral kernels are given by

\displaystyle l(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa}{2}n(\zeta)\cdot[\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)]\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|},\quad s(t,\zeta)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}H_{0}^{(1)}(\mathrm{i}\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|),

\displaystyle k(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2}n(t)\cdot[\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)]\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(\mathrm{i}\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|},

\displaystyle r(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa^{2}}{2}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)n(t)\cdot n(\zeta),

\displaystyle h(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\tilde{h}(t,\zeta)\Big\{\kappa^{2}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)-\frac{2\kappa H_{1}^{(1)}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa\gamma^{\prime}(t)\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)}{2|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|}H_{1}^{(1)}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)+\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\frac{1}{2}(t-\zeta)}.

Here
n(t):=\big(\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(t),-\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(t)\big)^{\top}
,
n^{\perp}(t):=\gamma^{\prime}(t)=\big(\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(t),\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(t)\big)^{\top}
, and

\tilde{h}(t,\zeta)=\frac{\big[\gamma^{\prime}(t)\cdot(\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta))\big]\big[\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)\cdot(\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta))\big]}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|^{2}}.

The kernels
l(t,\zeta)
,
s(t,\zeta)
,
k(t,\zeta)
,
r(t,\zeta)
, and
h(t,\zeta)
can be decomposed into the following forms:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi(t,\zeta)&=\chi_{1}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)+\chi_{2}(t,\zeta),\\
\chi_{2}(t,\zeta)&=\chi(t,\zeta)-\chi_{1}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big),\end{split}

(3.5)

where
\chi=l,s,k,r,h
, and
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are analytic functions. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}n(\zeta)\cdot\big[\gamma(\zeta)-\gamma(t)\big]\frac{J_{1}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|},~~s_{1}(t,\zeta)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}J_{0}(\mathrm{i}\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|),

\displaystyle k_{1}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa}{2\pi}n(t)\cdot\big[\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)\big]\frac{J_{1}(\mathrm{i}\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|},

\displaystyle r_{1}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\pi}J_{0}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)n(t)\cdot n(\zeta),

\displaystyle h_{1}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\tilde{h}(t,\zeta)\Bigg(\kappa^{2}J_{0}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)-\frac{2\kappa J_{1}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\kappa\gamma^{\prime}(t)\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)}{2\pi|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|}J_{1}(\kappa|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|),

where
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are the Bessel functions of the first kind with order zero and one, respectively. The diagonal entries are given by

\displaystyle l_{1}(t,t)

\displaystyle=0,\quad l_{2}(t,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{{n}(t)\cdot\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)}{|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}},

\displaystyle s_{1}(t,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi},\quad s_{2}(t,t)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}-\frac{C}{\pi}-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\big(\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa}{2}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\big),

\displaystyle r_{1}(t,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\pi}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2},\quad r_{2}(t,t)=\kappa^{2}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\Big\{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}-\frac{C}{\pi}-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\big(\frac{\kappa}{2}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\big)\Big\},

\displaystyle k_{1}(t,t)

\displaystyle=0,\quad k_{2}(t,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{{n}(t)\cdot\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)}{|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}},\quad h_{1}(t,t)=-\frac{\kappa^{2}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}}{4\pi},

\displaystyle h_{2}(t,t)

is compact from
H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
into
H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
. Equivalently, we consider the operator equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\psi+\mathcal{E}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{V})\psi=\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{A}\eta,

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{1}&0\\
0&E_{1}\end{array}\right],\quad\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{1}^{-1}(I+S_{0}T_{0})+S_{0}S_{0}&0\\
0&E_{1}^{-1}(I+T_{0}S_{0})+S_{0}S_{0}\end{array}\right].

It is clear to note that
\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}
is isomorphic and
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{V})
is compact from
H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
into
H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
for
p\geq 0
.

In this section, we adopt the collocation method to study the convergence of the semi- and full-discretization of the boundary integral equation (
4.1
). A related work can be found in
[
3
]
on the convergence analysis of the collocation method for solving the elastic obstacle scattering problem.

4.1.
Semi-discrete scheme

Denote by
X_{n}
the space of
n
-th order trigonometric polynomials. For any
\varphi\in X_{n}
, it has the form

\varphi(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}a_{m}\cos mt+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}b_{m}\sin mt.

Let
P_{n}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]\rightarrow X_{n}
be the interpolation operator, i.e.,
(P_{n}g)(\zeta_{j}^{(n)})=g(\zeta_{j}^{(n)})
, where the interpolation points
\zeta_{j}^{(n)}:=\pi j/n
,

j=0,\cdots,2n-1
. Clearly,
P_{n}
is bounded.

Let
X_{n}^{2}=\{\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})^{\top}:\psi_{1}\in X_{n},\psi_{2}\in X_{n}\}
and define the interpolation operator
\mathcal{P}_{n}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow X_{n}^{2}
by

\mathcal{P}_{n}g=(P_{n}g_{1},P_{n}g_{2})^{\top}\quad\forall\,g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}.

It is clear to note that
X_{n}^{2}
is unisolvent with respect to the points

\{\zeta_{j}^{(n)}\}_{j=0}^{2n-1}
. By
[
15
, Theorem
11.8]
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{n}g-g\|_{q}\leq\frac{C}{n^{p-q}}\|g\|_{p}\quad\forall\,g\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2},

(4.2)

where
0\leq q\leq p,\frac{1}{2}<p
, and
C
is a positive constant depending on
p
and
q
.

Hence
\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})^{\top}
can be approximated by
\psi^{n}=(\psi^{n}_{1},\psi^{n}_{2})^{\top}\in X_{n}^{2}
,
which satisfies the approximate operator equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\psi^{n}+\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{V})]\psi^{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{A})\eta,

(4.3)

where
\psi^{n}

satisfies
\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U})\psi^{n}=\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\psi^{n}
by noting (
3.15
).

The unique solvability of (
4.3
) and the error estimate of the solution are given in the following theorem. Based on the fact that
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{V}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+3}[0,2\pi]^{2}
are bounded for any
p\geq 0
, the proof is similar to that of
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
and is omitted for brevity.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\psi
be the unique solution to (
3.14
). For sufficiently large
n
, the approximate operator equation (
4.3
) has a unique solution
\psi^{n}
, which satisfies

\|\psi^{n}-\psi\|_{p}\leq C\|\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U})\psi-\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\psi\|_{p+2},

where
C
is a positive constant depending on

\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{J}
,
\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{V}
and

\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}
.

4.2.
Full-discrete scheme

Based on the Lagrange basis (cf.
[
15
, eqn.
(11.12)
]
)

\mathfrak{L}_{j}(t)=\frac{1}{2n}\Big\{1+2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\cos k(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})+\cos n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\Big\},\quad j=0,1,\cdots,2n-1,

an approximate solution

\widetilde{\psi}^{n}\in X_{n}^{2}
of (
4.3
) can be written as

\widetilde{\psi}^{n}(t)=\big(\widetilde{\psi}^{n}_{1}(t),\widetilde{\psi}^{n}_{2}(t)\big)^{\top}=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}_{1}(\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\mathfrak{L}_{j}(t),\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}_{2}(\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\mathfrak{L}_{j}(t)\bigg)^{\top}.

Moreover, it satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{U}_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}+\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}_{n}+\mathcal{V}_{n})]\widetilde{\psi}^{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{n})\eta,

(4.4)

where
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n}+\mathcal{D}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{1,n}+\mathcal{N}_{2,n}+\mathcal{D}_{n}
,
\mathcal{J}_{n}=\mathcal{J}_{1,n}+\mathcal{J}_{2,n}
,
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n}+\mathcal{D}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{n}+\mathcal{D}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n}
. The involved quadrature operators are given by

\displaystyle T_{0,n}=T_{0}P_{n},\quad S_{j,n}=S_{j}P_{n},\quad E_{i,n}=E_{i}

(4.5)

for
j=0,1,2
,
i=1,\cdots,9
, and the quadrature operators corresponding the integral operators of the form

\displaystyle(B\phi)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)b_{1}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}b_{2}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}(B_{1}\phi)(t)+(B_{2}\phi)(t)

are defined by

\displaystyle(B_{n}\phi)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)P_{n}[b_{1}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)]\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{n}[b_{2}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)]\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}(B_{1,n}\phi)(t)+(B_{2,n}\phi)(t).

Then we have
\mathcal{N}_{1,n}=\mathcal{N}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{n}
,
\mathcal{N}_{2,n}=\mathcal{N}_{2}\mathcal{P}_{n}
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}E_{1,n}&0\\
0&E_{1,n}\end{array}\right],\quad\mathcal{J}_{1,n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{1,n}&F_{2,n}\\
F_{3,n}&F_{4,n}\end{array}\right],\quad\mathcal{J}_{2,n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}J_{2,n}&0\\
J_{3,n}&J_{1,n}\end{array}\right],

where
F_{j,n}
and
J_{i,n}
are quadrature operators corresponding to the integral operators
F_{j}
and
J_{i}
, respectively. It is clear to note that
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{U}_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}=\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}=\mathcal{N}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}
for any
\widetilde{\psi}^{n}\in X_{n}^{2}
.

Our goal is to examine the convergence of the fully discretized equation (
4.4
). The function
\mathcal{D}\psi
can be split into the following two parts:

(\mathcal{D}\psi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)\alpha(t,\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(t,\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where

\alpha(t,\zeta)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{1}(t,\zeta)&\alpha_{2}(t,\zeta)\\
\alpha_{3}(t,\zeta)&\alpha_{4}(t,\zeta)\end{array}\right],\quad\beta(t,\zeta)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{1}(t,\zeta)&\beta_{2}(t,\zeta)\\
\beta_{3}(t,\zeta)&\beta_{4}(t,\zeta)\end{array}\right]

with
\alpha_{j}(t,t)=\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}(t,t)=\partial^{2}_{tt}\alpha_{j}(t,t)=0,j=1,2,3,4
and
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
being analytic.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
0\leq q\leq p
and
p>1/2
. Then for any
\psi\in X_{n}^{2}
and
\chi\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{q+3}\leq C\frac{1}{n^{p+1-q}}\|\psi\|_{p},\quad\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\chi-\mathcal{D}\chi\|_{q+3}\leq\widetilde{C}\frac{1}{n^{p-q}}\|\chi\|_{p},

(4.6)

where
C
and
\widetilde{C}
are positive constants depending on
p
and
q
.

Proof.

We write the derivative
\frac{\rm d^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}(\mathcal{D}\psi)
in form of

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\psi)(t):=\frac{\rm d^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}(\mathcal{D}\psi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)\widetilde{\alpha}(t,\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widetilde{\beta}(t,\zeta)\psi(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}(t,\zeta)=\partial^{2}_{tt}\alpha(t,\zeta),\quad\widetilde{\beta}(t,\zeta)=2\cot\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\partial_{t}\alpha(t,\zeta)-\alpha(t,\zeta)\frac{1}{2\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}}+\partial^{2}_{tt}\beta(t,\zeta).

By the interpolatory quadrature, the full discretization of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
can be written as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\psi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)\mathcal{P}_{n}\Big\{\widetilde{\alpha}(t,\cdot)\psi\Big\}(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{P}_{n}\Big\{\widetilde{\beta}(t,\cdot)\psi\Big\}(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Noting
p>1/2,0\leq q\leq p,\widetilde{\alpha}(t,t)=0
, and the analyticity of the elements in
\widetilde{\alpha}(t,\zeta)
and
\widetilde{\beta}(t,\zeta)
, we have from
[
15
, Lemma 13.21 and Theorem 12.18]
that

\|\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\psi\|_{q+1}\leq C_{1}\frac{1}{n^{p+1-q}}\|\psi\|_{p},\quad\|\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\chi-\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\chi\|_{q+1}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}\frac{1}{n^{p-q}}\|\chi\|_{p}

for any
\psi\in X_{n}^{2}
and
\chi\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
, where the positive constants
C_{1}
and
\widetilde{C}_{1}
depend on
p
and
q
. Since
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}_{n}\psi=\frac{\rm d^{2}}{{\rm d}t^{2}}(\mathcal{D}_{n}\psi)
, the above inequalities reduce to

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{q+3}\leq C_{2}\frac{1}{n^{p+1-q}}\|\psi\|_{p},\quad\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\chi-\mathcal{D}\chi\|_{q+3}\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}\frac{1}{n^{p-q}}\|\chi\|_{p},

where
C_{2}
and
\widetilde{C}_{2}
are positive constants depending on
p
and
q
.
By
[
15
, Theorem 8.13]
, the proof is completed since the above inequalities hold for any
q
satisfying
0\leq q\leq p
and
p>1/2
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

For any
\varphi\in X_{n}
and
\tilde{\chi}\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]
, we may follow the proofs of

and

and

satisfy the following type of estimates:

,

and

.

Hereafter, the notation
a\preceq b
stands for
a\leq Cb
, where

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p>\frac{3}{2}
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{V}_{n}-\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{V}]\psi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\psi\|_{p}\quad\forall\,\psi\in X_{n}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof is motivated by
[
3
, Theorem 4.3]
. Recalling the boundedness of
\mathcal{N}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p-1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
and using (
4.2
), we have for any
\chi\in H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2},0\leq q\leq p,p>1/2
that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}_{n}-\mathcal{N})\chi\|_{q-1}=\|\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{P}_{n}\chi-\chi)\|_{q-1}\leq C_{1}\|(\mathcal{P}_{n}\chi-\chi)\|_{q}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{n^{p-q}}\|\chi\|_{p},

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive constants depending on
q
and
p,q
, respectively. For any
p>1/2
, it is clear to note that
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}-\mathcal{N}
are uniformly bounded from
H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
to
H^{p-1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
.

For any
\psi\in X_{n}^{2}
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
and
\mathcal{N}_{n}\psi=\mathcal{N}\psi
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{N}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{N}_{n}(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\psi\|_{p+2}+\|(\mathcal{N}_{n}-\mathcal{N})(\mathcal{D}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi)\|_{p+2}+\|(\mathcal{N}_{n}-\mathcal{N})\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\psi\|_{p+3}+\|\mathcal{D}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+3}

\displaystyle\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p}+1/n\|\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+4}\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p}.

Moreover, by Theorem
4.2
, we have for any
p>1/2
that

\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D}
are uniformly bounded from

H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}
to
H^{p+3}[0,2\pi]^{2}
. Combining
(
4.2
) and (
4.6
), and noting the boundedness of
\mathcal{D}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+4}[0,2\pi]^{2}
and the uniform boundedness of

\mathcal{P}_{n}:H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}^{2}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}^{2}\psi\|_{p+2}\leq\|\mathcal{D}^{2}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}^{2}\psi\|_{p+6}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{D}_{n}(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\psi\|_{p+6}+\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})(\mathcal{D}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi)\|_{p+6}+\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+6}

\displaystyle\preceq\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\psi\|_{p+3}+\|(\mathcal{D}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi)\|_{p+3}+1/n\|\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+3}

\displaystyle\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p}+1/n\|\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+4}+1/n\|\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+3}\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p}.

With the help of
\mathcal{N}_{1}\psi\in X_{n}^{2}
, the boundedness of
\mathcal{N}_{2}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
and the uniform boundedness of
\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D}:H^{p-1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
to
H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}:H^{p-1}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p-1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
for
p>\frac{3}{2}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p+2}=\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})(\mathcal{N}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}\psi)\|_{p+2}+\|(\mathcal{D}_{n}-\mathcal{D})\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|\mathcal{N}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p-1}+1/n\|\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{N}_{1}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{1}\psi\|_{p-1}+\|\mathcal{N}_{2}\psi-\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{2}\psi\|_{p-1}+1/n\|\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p-1}

\displaystyle\preceq 1/n^{2}\|\mathcal{N}_{2}\psi\|_{p+1}+1/n\|\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p-1}\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{V}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{V}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{N}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{N}\mathcal{D}\psi\|_{p+2}+\|\mathcal{D}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{n}\psi-\mathcal{D}\mathcal{N}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{D}_{n}^{2}\psi-\mathcal{D}^{2}\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq 1/n\|\psi\|_{p},

which completes the proof by noting that the operators
\mathcal{E}^{-1},\mathcal{P}_{n}:H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
are uniformly bounded.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p>1/2
. Then the following estimate holds:

\|\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{J}_{n}-\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{J}]\psi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\psi\|_{p}\quad\forall\,\psi\in X_{n}^{2}.

Proof.

We claim for any
\varphi\in X_{n}
that

\displaystyle\|F_{j,n}\varphi-F_{j}\varphi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p},\quad\|J_{i,n}\varphi-J_{i}\varphi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

(4.7)

for
j=1,2,3,4
,
i=1,2,3
. In the following, we only show the proof for
j=1
,
i=1
since the other cases can be proved similarly.

First, we have from (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\|F_{1,n}\varphi-F_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2}\leq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\forall\,\varphi\in X_{n},

(4.8)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\|2E_{2,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-2E_{2}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\|E_{2,n}S_{2,n}E_{2,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-E_{2}S_{2}E_{2}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\|S_{0,n}(E_{3,n}S_{1,n}+E_{6,n}S_{2,n})\varphi-S_{0}(E_{3}S_{1}+E_{6}S_{2})\varphi\|_{p+2},

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\|E_{1,n}S_{2,n}(\frac{1}{2}E_{1,n}S_{0,n}+E_{3,n}S_{1,n}+E_{6,n}S_{2,n})\varphi

\displaystyle\quad-E_{1}S_{2}(\frac{1}{2}E_{1}S_{0}+E_{3}S_{1}+E_{6}S_{2})\varphi\|_{p+2}.

It is clear to note from (
4.5
) that
I_{1}=0
. Using (
4.2
) and Remark
4.3
, and following the same techniques as those in
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|S_{2,n}E_{2,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-S_{2}E_{2}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2}=\|(S_{2,n}-S_{2})(E_{2}S_{2}\varphi-P_{n}E_{2}S_{2}\varphi)\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|E_{2}S_{2}\varphi-P_{n}E_{2}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p}\preceq\frac{1}{n^{3}}\|E_{2}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+3}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

By the uniform boundedness of
S_{0,n}-S_{0}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+1}[0,2\pi]^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\preceq\|S_{0,n}E_{3,n}S_{1,n}\varphi-S_{0}E_{3}S_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2}+\|S_{0,n}E_{6,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-S_{0}E_{6}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle=\|(S_{0,n}-S_{0})(E_{3}S_{1}\varphi-P_{n}E_{3}S_{1}\varphi)\|_{p+2}+\|(S_{0,n}-S_{0})(E_{6}S_{2}\varphi-P_{n}E_{6}S_{2}\varphi)\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|E_{3}S_{1}\varphi-P_{n}E_{3}S_{1}\varphi\|_{p+1}+\|E_{6}S_{2}\varphi-P_{n}E_{6}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+1}

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|E_{3}S_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\|E_{6}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+3}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

Analogously, we may show
I_{4}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}
. Combining
I_{j}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p},~j=1,2,3,4
and (
4.8
) leads to the first inequality in (
4.7
).

Next, it follows from (
3.17
) that

\displaystyle\|J_{1,n}\varphi-J_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2}\leq Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3}+Q_{4}+Q_{5}+Q_{6}\quad\forall\,\varphi\in X_{n},

where

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{B}_{3,n}\widetilde{S}_{0,n}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{B}_{3}\widetilde{S}_{0}\varphi\|_{p+2},\qquad~~~~~~Q_{2}=\|\widetilde{S}_{0,n}E_{7,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-\widetilde{S}_{0}E_{7}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=\|4\widetilde{S}_{0,n}E_{3,n}B_{1,n}\varphi-4\widetilde{S}_{0}E_{3}B_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2},\quad~Q_{4}=\|\widetilde{S}_{0,n}E_{1,n}B_{2,n}\varphi-\widetilde{S}_{0}E_{1}B_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2},

\displaystyle Q_{5}

\displaystyle=\|\frac{1}{2}E_{1,n}M_{0,n}S_{0,n}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}E_{1}M_{0}S_{0}\varphi\|_{p+2},~Q_{6}=\|M_{0,n}E_{1,n}S_{2,n}\varphi-M_{0}E_{1}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2}.

Again, we have from Remark
4.3
that

\displaystyle Q_{1}\preceq\|(\widetilde{B}_{3,n}-\widetilde{B}_{3})\widetilde{S}_{0}\varphi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\widetilde{S}_{0}\varphi\|_{p+1}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

The inequality
Q_{2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}
can be obtained by the similar estimate of the second item of
I_{3}
. Noting (
4.2
) and the uniform boundedness of
\widetilde{S}_{0},~\widetilde{S}_{0,n}-\widetilde{S}_{0}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]\rightarrow H^{p+1}[0,2\pi]
, we deduce

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle\preceq\|\widetilde{S}_{0,n}(E_{3,n}B_{1,n}-E_{3}B_{1})\varphi\|_{p+2}+\|(\widetilde{S}_{0,n}-\widetilde{S}_{0})(E_{3}B_{1}\varphi-P_{n}E_{3}B_{1}\varphi)\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\|(E_{3,n}B_{1,n}-E_{3}B_{1})\varphi\|_{p+1}+\|E_{3}B_{1}\varphi-P_{n}E_{3}B_{1}\varphi\|_{p+1}

\displaystyle\preceq\|(B_{1,n}-B_{1})\varphi\|_{p+1}+\frac{1}{n}\|E_{3}B_{1}\varphi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}+\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

Analogously,
Q_{4}
can be estimate as

\displaystyle Q_{4}

\displaystyle\preceq\|(B_{2,n}-B_{2})\varphi\|_{p+1}+\frac{1}{n}\|E_{3}B_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(B_{2,n}-B_{2})\varphi\|_{p+2}+\frac{1}{n}\|E_{3}B_{2}\varphi\|_{p+3}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p}.

Clearly,
Q_{5}=0
by noting
S_{0,n}\varphi=S_{0}\varphi\in X_{n}
. It follows from
[
10
, Theorem A.45]
that
M_{0}
is a bounded operator from
H^{p}[0,2\pi]
into
H^{r}[0,2\pi]
for every
-r\leq p\leq r
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle Q_{6}\preceq\|P_{n}E_{1}S_{2}\varphi-E_{1}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+2}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|E_{1}S_{2}\varphi\|_{p+3}\preceq\frac{1}{n}\|\varphi\|_{p},

which implies the second inequality of (
4.7
).

Combining (
4.5
) and (
3.18
), we complete the proof by noting that the operators
\mathcal{E}^{-1},\mathcal{P}_{n}:H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}\rightarrow H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]^{2}
are uniformly bounded.
∎

The following result concerns the convergence of the full-discrete scheme. Based on the uniform boundedness of the operators
F_{j,n}-F_{j},J_{i,n}-J_{i}:H^{p}[0,2\pi]\rightarrow H^{p+2}[0,2\pi]
from Remark
4.3
and (
4.6
), the proof is similar to that of
[
3
, Theorem 4.5]
and is omitted here for brevity.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\psi
be the unique solution to (
3.14
). For
p>3/2
and sufficiently large
n
, the fully discrete equation (
4.4
) admits a unique solution
\widetilde{\psi}^{n}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\psi}^{n}-\psi\|_{p}

\displaystyle\preceq\|\mathcal{P}_{n}(\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U})\psi-\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{U}\psi\|_{p+2}+\|\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}_{n}+\mathcal{K}_{n})-\mathcal{E}^{-1}(\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{K})]\psi\|_{p+2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{P}_{n}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{n}-\mathcal{E}^{-1}\mathcal{A}]\eta\|_{p+2}.

5.
Numerical experiments

In this section, we present numerical implementation and show some examples to demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method. In particular, we introduce a simple alternative boundary integral formulation by using a combination of the single- and single-layer potentials.

5.1.
Double-single layer potential formulation

As discussed in section 3, the boundary integral formulation is based on a combination of the double- and single-layer potentials. Here we introduce two different approaches to solve the corresponding full-discrete boundary integral equations.

5.1.1.
Approach 1

The first approach is to solve directly the equivalent full-discrete equation of (
3.2
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\Bigg(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
\widetilde{T}_{0}&-I\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}L&S\\
R-H&K\end{array}\right]\Bigg)\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\eta_{1}\\
\eta_{2}\end{array}\right],

where
\widetilde{T}_{0}=T_{0}-M_{0}
. By the decomposition (
3.5
), we apply the simple trapezoidal for the smooth integrals:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta\approx\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}f(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}),

while we employ the quadrature rules via the trigonometric interpolation for the weakly singular integrals (cf.
[
2
,
13
]
]:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)f(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}R_{j}^{(n)}(t)f(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cot\frac{\zeta-t}{2}f^{\prime}(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}T_{j}^{(n)}(t)f(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}),

where the quadrature weights are given by

\displaystyle R_{j}^{(n)}(t)=-\frac{2\pi}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{m}\cos\Big[m(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\Big]-\frac{\pi}{n^{2}}\cos\Big[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\Big],

\displaystyle T_{j}^{(n)}(t)=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}m\cos\Big[m(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\Big]-\frac{1}{2}\cos\Big[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\Big].

5.1.2.
Approach 2

The second approach is to solve the
equivalent full-discrete equation of (
3.14
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{N}_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}+\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{D}_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n}\eta~\Leftrightarrow~A_{n}\widetilde{\psi}^{n}=\eta^{n},

(5.1)

where
A_{n}
is the coefficient matrix of the full-discrete equation. As mentioned in
[
3
, section 5]
, it is more convenient to handle the equivalent full-discrete equation (
5.1
) due to the simple quadrature operators
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

To handle the singular integrals
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, we use the trigonometric interpolation in the quadrature rules

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)\sin(t-\zeta)f(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}V_{j}^{(n)}(t)f(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)\sin^{2}(t-\zeta)f(\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}W_{j}^{(n)}(t)f(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}),

where the integral weights are defined by

\displaystyle V_{j}^{(n)}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2n}\sin(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}-t)+\frac{2\pi}{n}\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}\frac{\sin[m(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}-t)]}{m^{2}-1}+\frac{\pi\sin[n(\zeta_{j}^{(n)}-t)]}{n(n^{2}-1)},

\displaystyle W_{j}^{(n)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{n}\Big\{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{2}{3}\cos(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})+\frac{1}{8}\cos[2(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]\Big\}+\frac{1}{2}R_{j}^{(n)}(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{m=3}^{n-1}\frac{m}{m^{2}-4}\cos[m(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]+\frac{\pi}{2(n^{2}-4)}\cos[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})].

Remark 5.1
.

By a straightforward calculation, we get

\displaystyle W_{j}^{(n)}(t)-R_{j}^{(n)}(t)\sin^{2}(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2n^{2}}\sin[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]\sin[2(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]+\frac{\pi}{n^{2}-n}\sin[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]\sin(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\pi}{n(n-1)(n+1)}\cos[(n-1)(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})]-\frac{\pi}{n(n-2)(n+2)}\cos[n(t-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})].

It is clear to note that
W_{j}^{(n)}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)})-R_{j}^{(n)}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)})\sin^{2}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)}-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\not=0
, which is different from
[
3
, Remark 5.1]
. However, we have
W_{j}^{(n)}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)})-R_{j}^{(n)}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)})\sin^{2}(\zeta_{i}^{(n)}-\zeta_{j}^{(n)})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

5.2.
Single-single layer potential formulation

As a comparison, we introduce a computationally more convenient boundary integral formulation by using the single- and single-layer potentials. Using the fundamental solutions to the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations, we represent the solutions of (
2.8
) in terms of two single-layer potentials

\displaystyle v_{\rm H}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm H}(x,y)g_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),\quad v_{\rm M}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm M}(x,y)g_{2}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Gamma_{D},

(5.2)

where
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are densities.

Using the boundary condition (
2.5
) and the jump relation of the single-layer potentials (
5.2
), we deduce the coupled boundary integral equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}-u^{\rm inc}(x)&=\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm H}(x,y)g_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y)+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\rm M}(x,y)g_{2}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y),\\
-\frac{\partial u^{\rm inc}(x)}{\partial\nu(x)}&=-\frac{1}{2}g_{1}(x)+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm H}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}g_{1}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}g_{2}(x)+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial G_{\rm M}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}g_{2}(y)\mathrm{d}s(y).\end{split}

(5.3)

Similarly, we may obtain the following parametric form of (
5.3
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\psi\overset{\text{def}}{=}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}S^{\rm H}&S^{\rm M}\\
-I+K^{\rm H}&-I+K^{\rm M}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\eta_{1}\\
\eta_{2}\end{array}\right],

where
\eta_{1}=2(f_{1}\circ\gamma)
,
\eta_{2}=2(f_{2}\circ\gamma)|\gamma^{\prime}|
,
\psi_{j}=(g_{j}\circ\gamma)|\gamma^{\prime}|
,
j=1,2
, and

\displaystyle(S^{\sigma}\phi)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}s^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta,\quad(K^{\sigma}\phi)(t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}k^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\zeta

with the kernels being given by

\displaystyle s^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}H_{0}^{(1)}(\kappa_{\sigma}|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|),

\displaystyle k^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}\kappa_{\sigma}}{2}n(t)\cdot[\gamma(\zeta)-\gamma(t)]\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(\kappa_{\sigma}|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|}.

Here
\sigma={\rm H}
or
{\rm M}
,
\kappa_{\rm H}=\kappa
, and
\kappa_{\rm M}={\rm i}\kappa
. The kernels
s^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)
and
k^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)
can be decomposed into

\displaystyle s^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=s_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)+s_{2}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta),

\displaystyle k^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=k_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)+k_{2}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta),

where the functions

\displaystyle s_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}J_{0}(\kappa_{\sigma}|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|),

\displaystyle s_{2}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=m^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)-m_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big),

\displaystyle k_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}}{2\pi}n(t)\cdot\big[\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)\big]\frac{J_{1}(\kappa_{\sigma}|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|)}{|\gamma(t)-\gamma(\zeta)|},

\displaystyle k_{2}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)

\displaystyle=k^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)-k_{1}^{\sigma}(t,\zeta)\ln\Big(4\sin^{2}\frac{t-\zeta}{2}\Big)

are analytic and their diagonal entries are given by

\displaystyle s_{1}^{\sigma}(t,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi},\quad s_{2}^{\sigma}(t,t)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}-\frac{C}{\pi}-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\big(\frac{\kappa_{\sigma}}{2}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\big),

and

\displaystyle k_{1}^{\sigma}(t,t)=0,\quad k_{2}^{\sigma}(t,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{n(t)\cdot\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)}{|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}}.

Compared with (
3.2

5.3

5.3

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the biharmonic wave scattering problem of a cavity embedded in an infinite thin plate. Based on the biharmonic wave operator splitting, the scattering problem of the fourth-order biharmonic wave equation is reduced into a coupled boundary value problem of the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations. With the help of potential theory, a novel boundary integral formulation is proposed for the scattering problem. Based on an appropriate regularizer, the operator equation is split into an isomorphic operator plus a compact one. The well-posedness is established for the coupled system. The convergence analysis is carried out for both the semi- and full-discrete schemes by using the collocation method. To demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed method, numerical experiments are presented for both smooth and nonsmooth cavities. The numerical results show that the proposed method is highly accurate even for nonsmooth examples.

In this work, we examined only the clamped boundary condition. At the edge of cavities, many other boundary conditions can be considered to take into account of different physical behavior
[
6
]
. Clearly, a different boundary condition will lead to a different formulation of boundary integral equations. We are investigating these problems and will report the progress elsewhere in the future.

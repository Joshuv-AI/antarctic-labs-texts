New spectral Bishop–Gromov and Bonnet–Myers theorems and applications to isoperimetry
Gioacchino Antonelli, Kai Xu

Abstract
. We show a sharp and rigid spectral generalization of the classical Bishop–Gromov volume comparison theorem: if a closed Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) of dimension n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 satisfies λ 1 ​ ( − n − 1 n − 2 ​ Δ + Ric ) ⩾ n − 1 , \lambda_{1}\left(-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta+\Ric\right)\geqslant n-1, then vol ⁡ ( M ) ⩽ vol ⁡ ( 𝕊 n ) \operatorname{vol}(M)\leqslant\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n}) , and π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is finite. The constant n − 1 n − 2 \frac{n-1}{n-2} cannot be improved, and if vol ⁡ ( M ) = vol ⁡ ( 𝕊 n ) \vol(M)=\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n}) holds, then M ≅ 𝕊 n M\cong\mathbb{S}^{n} . A sharp generalization of the Bonnet–Myers theorem is also shown under the same spectral condition. The proofs involve the use of a new unequally weighted isoperimetric problem, and unequally warped μ \mu -bubbles. As an application, in dimensions 3 ⩽ n ⩽ 5 3\leqslant n\leqslant 5 , we infer sharp results on the isoperimetric structure at infinity of complete manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and uniformly positive spectral biRicci curvature. Furthermore, the main result of this paper is applied in Mazet’s recent solution of the stable Bernstein problem in ℝ 6 \mathbb{R}^{6} .

Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the results

The aim of this paper is to show a spectral generalization of both the classical Bishop–Gromov volume comparison theorem and Bonnet–Myers theorem, and derive some consequences. We stress that in this paper all manifolds
M
are without boundary.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an
n
-dimensional compact smooth Riemannian manifold with
n\geqslant 3
,
\partial M=\emptyset
, and let
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
,
\lambda>0
. We denote by
\mathrm{Ric}(x):=\inf_{v\in T_{x}M,\,|v|=1}\mathrm{Ric}_{x}(v,v)
the smallest eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor. Assume there is a positive function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(1)

\gamma\Delta u\leqslant u\Ric-(n-1)\lambda u.

Let
\widetilde{M}
be the universal cover of
M
, endowed with the pull-back metric. Then we have:

(1)

A diameter bound

(2)

\diam(\widetilde{M})\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\cdot\Big(\frac{\max(u)}{\min(u)}\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma},

in particular,
\pi_{1}(M)
is finite.

(2)

A sharp volume bound

(3)

\mathrm{vol}(\widetilde{M})\leqslant\lambda^{-n/2}\mathrm{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Moreover, if equality holds in (
3

as in (
1

is isometric to the round sphere of radius

In the shorter range

Let

\gamma\Delta u\leqslant u\Ric-(n-1)\lambda u.

Then

Remark 1

(Smoothing the eigenfunctions)

Observe that in (

\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\Ric\varphi^{2}\geqslant(n-1)\lambda\int_{M}\varphi^{2},\qquad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M),

Theorem

1
has consequences on the isoperimetric structure of spaces with nonnegative Ricci curvature and uniformly positive spectral biRicci curvature. Let us recall that, given orthonormal vectors
u,v\in T_{p}M
,

\mathrm{biRic}(u,v):=\mathrm{Ric}(u)+\mathrm{Ric}(v)-\mathrm{Sect}(u\wedge v).

In dimension
3
, note that the biRicci curvature is half of the scalar curvature.

The biRicci curvature was first introduced and studied by Shen–Ye
[
42
]
. There it was shown that in ambient dimensions up to 5, stable minimal hypersurfaces in manifolds with
\mathrm{biRic}\geqslant n-2
enjoy a uniform diameter bound. This was motivated by and generalizes the 3-dimensional result of Schoen–Yau
[
41
]
. The underlying idea of
[
42
]
is that the stable minimal surface in question satisfies the spectral control (
5
) with
\gamma=1
, and a positive
\lambda>0
. Recently, the study of manifolds with biRicci (and more general intermediate-Ricci) curvature lower bounds has found applications in the solution of the stable Bernstein problem
[
23
]
, and in the study of a generalization of Geroch conjecture
[
13
]
.

Before stating
Theorem

3
, recall that a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
has
linear volume growth
if there is one

(and hence it is true for every

) such that

\limsup_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\vol(B_{r}(p))}{r}<+\infty.

We define the
isoperimetric profile
of

by

(6)

I_{M}(v):=\inf\big\{|\partial^{*}E|:E\subset\subset M,\,\vol(E)=v\big\},\qquad v\in[0,\vol(M)),

where
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of

. Let us denote

\mathrm{biRic}(x):=\inf_{v,w\in T_{x}M\,\text{orthonormal}}\mathrm{biRic}_{x}(v,w),

and let us say that, for constants
\gamma\geqslant 0,\lambda>0
, a manifold satisfies
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\biRic)\geqslant\lambda
outside a compact set
, if there is a compact set

such that

\int\gamma|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\biRic\varphi^{2}\geqslant\lambda\int\varphi^{2},\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M\setminus K).

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

Our proof of the volume bound follows the idea of isoperimetric comparison, due to Bray
[
12
]
. In
[
7
,
12
]
, generalizing the seminal contribution of
[
5
]
, the authors show that in a compact manifold
M^{n}
satisfying
\Ric\geqslant(n-1)\lambda
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the isoperimetric profile defined in (
6
) satisfies

(8)

I^{\prime\prime}_{M}I_{M}\leqslant-\frac{(I^{\prime}_{M})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda,

in the viscosity sense. When
\lambda>0
, an ODE comparison can be applied to (
8
) to obtain a sharp upper bound on the existence interval of
I_{M}
, and therefore an upper bound on
\vol(M)
. In
[
23
]
this argument is generalized to the spectral case in dimension 3: given a function
u
satisfying (
1
), one considers the weighted isoperimetric profile

I(v):=\inf\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}:E\subset\subset M,\,\int_{E}u^{\gamma}=v\right\}.

The same sharp inequality
I^{\prime\prime}I\leqslant-\frac{1}{2}(I^{\prime})^{2}-2\lambda
is shown in
[
23
]
, which eventually leads to a sharp volume bound. In other dimensions, however, the inequality obtained through this argument requires the condition
\gamma<\frac{4}{n-1}
(which is strictly narrower than
\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
when
n>3
), and the volume bound is not sharp.

The main novelty of our approach is to consider the unequally weighted isoperimetric profile

(9)

I(v):=\inf\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}:E\subset\subset M,\int_{E}u^{\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}}=v\right\},

which, if (
1
) is in force, satisfies the sharp inequality

(10)

I^{\prime\prime}I\leqslant-\frac{(I^{\prime})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda.

This leads to the proof of the sharp volume bound (
3
).

The diameter bound is obtained by employing the idea of
\mu
-bubbles originally due to Gromov
[
28
]
. In
[
48
]
, the second-named author considered a warped
\mu
-bubble obtained by minimizing a (suitably defined) energy

E(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u^{\gamma}-\int_{\Omega}hu^{\gamma},

where
u
is the function satisfying (
1
), and
h
satisfies a certain differential inequality

|\nabla h|\leqslant C(n,\gamma)h^{2}+(n-1)\lambda.

Within the narrower range
0\leqslant\gamma<\frac{4}{n-1}
, this argument leads to the diameter bound appearing in
Corollary

1
. We refer to
[
48
, Theorem 1.10]
for more details.

In our case, to obtain
Item

1
of
Theorem

1
for the full range
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, we consider an unequally warped
\mu
-bubble defined by the functional

E(\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u^{\gamma}-\int_{\Omega}hu^{\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}},

where now it turns out that
h
needs to be chosen carefully so that

|\nabla h|u^{\gamma\frac{3-n}{n-1}}<(n-1)\lambda+\frac{h^{2}}{n-1}u^{2\gamma\frac{3-n}{n-1}}.

Under the situation of
Theorem

1
, especially since
u
is bounded below and above uniformly, the latter argument leads to the diameter bound of
\widetilde{M}
stated in (
2
). In particular, this implies
\widetilde{M}
is compact and
\pi_{1}(M)
is finite.

We remark that
Theorem

1
holds in all dimensions. Often in the literature, results that are proved through generalized minimal surfaces are stated up to dimension
n\leqslant 7
, because of singularity formation in higher dimensions. In Appendix
A
, we give a detailed argument showing that our proofs extend to the singular case
n\geqslant 8
as well. The core content is to carry out the stability inequality for variations supported away from the singular set. We argued along the lines of similar approaches that appeared in
[
7
,
11
]
.

Finally, the proof of
Theorem

3
exploits
Theorem

1
and results and idea presented in
[
22
,
53
,
48
,
23
]
. Roughly speaking, within the range of dimension
3\leqslant n\leqslant 5
, and when
0\leqslant\gamma<6-n
(or
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant 2
when
n=4
), the assumption
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{biRic})\geqslant n-2
implies that for every
\varepsilon>0
, one can find a stable warped
\mu
-bubble
\Omega
such that

(11)

B_{\varepsilon^{-1}}(o)\subset\Omega,\qquad-\frac{4}{4-\gamma}\Delta_{\partial\Omega}+\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}-(n-2-\varepsilon)\geqslant 0.

Since
\mathrm{Ric}\geqslant 0
(thus
b_{1}(M)<+\infty
) and
M
has one end, the construction can be made such that
\partial\Omega
is connected. Hence (
11
), together with the volume bound in
Theorem

1
, implies that
|\partial\Omega|\leqslant\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})+o_{\varepsilon}(1)
. The conclusion about
\Omega
, coupled with the fact that
I_{M}
is nondecreasing (since
\Ric\geqslant 0
), essentially implies (
7
).

In higher dimensions

, the present
\mu
-bubble argument does not provide hypersurfaces satisfying the spectral condition (

). Thus, we conclude the introduction by asking the following question about

.

2.
Unequally warped
\mu
-bubbles and diameter bounds

Let
n\geqslant 3
, and let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold, not necessarily compact. Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a positive function. Given a parameter
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, we fix

\alpha:=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}.

Assume that
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
is positive and such that

(12)

-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric u\geqslant(n-1)\lambda u.

Let
\Omega_{-}\subset\Omega_{+}\subset M
be two domains with nonempty boundaries, such that
\overline{\Omega_{+}}\setminus\Omega_{-}
is compact. Suppose
h\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{+}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{-}})
satisfies

(13)

\lim_{x\to\partial\Omega_{-}}h(x)=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{x\to\partial\Omega_{+}}h(x)=-\infty\qquad\text{uniformly.}

For an arbitrary fixed domain
\Omega_{0}
with
\Omega_{-}\subset\subset\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega_{+}
, consider the functional

(14)

E(\Omega):=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega}u^{\gamma}-\int(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})hu^{\alpha},

defined on sets of finite perimeter, where
\partial^{*}\Omega
is the reduced boundary of
\Omega
.
By a slight variant of
[
50
, Proposition 2.1]
there always exists a set of finite perimeter
\Omega
, with
\Omega_{-}\subset\subset\Omega\subset\subset\Omega_{+}
, which is a minimizer of the energy
E
. Assume, for the purpose of the following computations, that
\Omega
has smooth boundary.

Let
\nu
denote the outer unit normal at
\partial\Omega
, and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Let
H,\mathrm{II}
be the mean curvature and second fundamental form of
\partial\Omega
, computed with respect to
\nu
. For a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
we denote
f_{\nu}:=\langle\nabla f,\nu\rangle
. For an arbitrary smooth variation
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
with
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
and variational field
\varphi\nu
at
t=0
, we compute the first variation

(15)

0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\Omega_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\big(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}-hu^{\alpha-\gamma}\big)u^{\gamma}\varphi.

Since
\varphi
is arbitrary we have
H=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}
. Then, computing the second variation,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}E(\Omega_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[-\Delta_{\partial\Omega}\varphi-|\text{II}|^{2}\varphi-\Ric(\nu,\nu)\varphi-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}\varphi

\displaystyle+\gamma u^{-1}\varphi\big(\Delta u-\Delta_{\partial\Omega}u-Hu_{\nu}\big)-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial\Omega}u,\nabla_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle-h_{\nu}u^{\alpha-\gamma}\varphi+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-\gamma-1}u_{\nu}\varphi\Big]u^{\gamma}\varphi.

Setting
\varphi=u^{-\gamma}
, using our main assumption (
12
), and integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}-|\text{II}|^{2}u^{-\gamma}-\Ric(\nu,\nu)u^{-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-2-\gamma}u_{\nu}^{2}+\gamma u^{-1-\gamma}\Delta u-\gamma u^{-1-\gamma}\Delta_{\partial\Omega}u

\displaystyle-\gamma Hu^{-1-\gamma}u_{\nu}-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial\Omega}u,\nabla_{\partial\Omega}(u^{-\gamma})\rangle-h_{\nu}u^{\alpha-2\gamma}+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-2\gamma-1}u_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{H^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda-\gamma(u^{-1}u_{\nu})^{2}

\displaystyle-\gamma H(u^{-1}u_{\nu})+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-\gamma}(u^{-1}u_{\nu})\Big].

Setting
X=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}
and
Y=u^{-1}u_{\nu}
(so
H=X-\gamma Y
), we have

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}XY-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{n-1}Y^{2}-(n-1)\lambda\\
&\qquad\qquad-\gamma Y^{2}-\gamma(X-\gamma Y)Y+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}+(\gamma-\alpha)XY\Big]\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}+\Big(\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}-\alpha\Big)XY+\Big(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\Big)Y^{2}\Big].

Recall that
\alpha=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
and
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
. Therefore, if
h
satisfies

(17)

|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}<\frac{h^{2}u^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda,

then we obtain a contradiction. We are thus ready to prove the following.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
n\geqslant 3
, and let
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
,
\lambda>0
. Assume there is
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\inf(u)>0
,
\sup(u)<+\infty
, and

-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric u\geqslant(n-1)\lambda u.

Then

(18)

\diam(M)\leqslant\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\cdot\Big(\frac{\sup(u)}{\inf(u)}\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}.

In particular,
M
must be compact.

Proof
(n\leqslant 7)
.

For the ease of readability, we give the proof assuming
n\leqslant 7
. The case
n\geqslant 8
, where one needs to deal with the possible singularity of minimizers, is postponed to
Appendix

A
. Set
\alpha:=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
as usual (note that
\alpha-\gamma\leqslant 0
). First, notice that if
h
is a smooth function on
M
such that

(19)

|\nabla h|<\frac{\sup(u)^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{(n-1)\inf(u)^{\alpha-\gamma}}h^{2}+\frac{(n-1)\lambda}{\inf(u)^{\alpha-\gamma}}=:Ch^{2}+D,

then

(20)

\displaystyle|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leqslant|\nabla h|\inf(u)^{\alpha-\gamma}<\frac{h^{2}u^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda.

Moreover, notice that

(21)

CD=\lambda\cdot\left(\frac{\sup(u)}{\inf(u)}\right)^{2\frac{3-n}{n-1}\gamma}.

Suppose by contradiction (
18
) is false. Then there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

(22)

\diam(M)>\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\cdot\Big(\frac{\sup(u)}{\inf(u)}\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{2}+2\varepsilon.

Let us now fix a point
p\in M
realizing
\mathrm{diam}(M)
. Take
\Omega_{-}:=B_{\varepsilon}(p)
, and let
d:M\setminus\Omega_{-}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smoothing of
d(\cdot,\partial\Omega_{-})
such that

d|_{\partial\Omega_{-}}\equiv 0,\quad|\nabla d|\leqslant 1+\varepsilon,\quad d\geqslant\frac{d(\cdot,\partial\Omega_{-})}{1+\varepsilon}.

It can be verified by direct computations that

(23)

h(x):=\sqrt{\frac{D}{C}}\cot\left(\frac{\sqrt{CD}}{1+\varepsilon}d(x)\right)

satisfies (
19
), and thus (
20
). Now we have that

(24)

\mathcal{O}:=\Big\{d>\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\pi}{\sqrt{CD}}\Big\}\supset\Big\{d(\cdot,p)>\varepsilon+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\pi}{\sqrt{CD}}\Big\}\neq\emptyset

due to (
22
). Set
\Omega_{+}:=M\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}
. Thus we have found two domains
\Omega_{-}\subset\subset\Omega_{+}\subset\subset M
and
h(x)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{+}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{-}})
which satisfy (
13
). Let
\Omega
be an unequally warped
\mu
-bubble related to the functional (
14
). Since
n\leqslant 7
, by classical results in Geometric Measure Theory (see
[
32
, Theorem 27.5]
, and
[
32
, Theorem 28.1]
) we have that
\Omega
has smooth boundary. Since (
20
) (hence (
17
)) is in force, we get a contradiction with the minimizing property of
E
, as shown in the computations of the second variation of
E
right before the Lemma.
∎

Proof of
Item

1
of
Theorem

1
.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{M}\to M
be the universal cover of
M
. Set
\widetilde{g}=\pi^{*}g
and
\widetilde{u}:=u\circ\pi
, thus we have

\gamma\widetilde{\Delta}\widetilde{u}\leqslant\widetilde{u}\widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}-(n-1)\lambda\widetilde{u}.

The assertion directly comes from

.

Remark 2

(No universal diameter bound)
.

Suppose
n\geqslant 4
and
\frac{4}{n-1}<\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, which is the range included in
Theorem

1
but not in
Corollary

1
. Thus, we only obtain the diameter bound (
2
) depending on the maximum and minimum of
u
. Here let us show that, even assuming
M
is closed, there is no universal diameter upper bound on
M
(that depends only on
n,\gamma
).

Fix
L\gg 1
. Fix a cutoff function
\eta_{0}
so that
\eta_{0}|_{[-\infty,0)}\equiv 0
and
\eta_{0}|_{[1,\infty)}\equiv 1
. Let
\delta,\mu>0
be constants to be chosen. Let
\eta
be the cutoff function such that:

(1)

\eta\equiv 0
on
(-\infty,-L-\mu]\cup[-\delta,\delta]\cup[L+\mu,+\infty)
and
\eta\equiv 1
on
[-L,-2\delta]\cup[2\delta,L]
,

(2)

on each interval
[-L-\mu,-L]
,
[-2\delta,-\delta]
,
[\delta,2\delta]
,
[L,L+\mu]
, the function
\eta
interpolates between 0 and 1 according to the model function
\eta_{0}
.

Set
\alpha=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
as usual. Choose constants
a,b>0
such that

(25)

-\frac{a^{2}}{n-1}-\Big(\gamma-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}\Big)\frac{b^{2}}{(\gamma-\alpha)^{2}}-(n-1)+ab=0.

The existence of such numbers follows by calculating the discriminant:

\displaystyle\exists\,a,b>0\text{ satisfying \eqref{eq:const_ab}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \ \frac{4}{n-1}\cdot\Big(\gamma-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}\Big)\frac{1}{(\gamma-\alpha)^{2}}<1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \ \gamma>\frac{4}{n-1},

and the second line is exactly our assumption. From the sign of the terms in (
25
), it follows that we can choose
a,b
to be positive.

Now we let
u(r)
solve the differential equation

(26)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\prime}(r)=\eta(r)\sgn(r)\cdot\frac{b}{\gamma-\alpha}u(r),\\
&u(0)=1.\end{aligned}\right.

The solution is positive, even, and smooth on the entire
\mathbb{R}
. Also, note that
u\equiv 1
in
[-\delta,\delta]
, and
u
is constant on
(-\infty,-L-\mu]\cup[L+\mu,+\infty)
. Then let
h=h(r)
solve

(27)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h^{\prime}u^{\alpha-\gamma}=-\frac{h^{2}u^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{n-1}-\Big(\gamma-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}\Big)\frac{(u^{\prime})^{2}}{u^{2}}-(n-1),\\
&h(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Since the solution is an odd function, below we only discuss within the range
r\geqslant 0
(and the case
r<0
follows by symmetry). We claim that there is a choice of
\delta
, such that the solution of (
26
) (
27
) satisfies
h(2\delta)u(2\delta)^{\alpha-\gamma}=-a
. To see this, we set a new function
Q=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}
, and thus (
27
) is converted to

(28)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q^{\prime}=-\frac{Q^{2}}{n-1}-\Big(\gamma-\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}\Big)\frac{\eta^{2}b^{2}}{(\gamma-\alpha)^{2}}-(n-1)-\eta bQ,\\
&Q(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

When
\delta\to 0
the solution satisfies
Q(2\delta)\to 0
. On the other hand, note that
\eta=0
on
[0,\delta]
, thus in this interval we have
Q^{\prime}=-\frac{Q^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)
. Thus there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that, when
\delta\nearrow\delta_{0}
we have
Q(2\delta)\to-\infty
. By continuity, there exists a
\delta
as claimed.

Next, note that
Q\equiv-a
is a solution of (
28
) in
[2\delta,L]
, due to our choice (
25
) and
\eta\equiv 1
. By uniqueness of ODE, for our specific choice of
\delta
the solution of (
28
) must satisfy
Q\equiv-a
on
[2\delta,L]
. Thus for (
27
), we have
h=-au^{\gamma-\alpha}
on
[2\delta,L]
.

Next, by continuity, there is a sufficiently small
\mu
such that the solution of (
27
) exists on
[-L-\mu,L+\mu]
. When
r\geqslant L+\mu
, (
27
) becomes

h^{\prime}u(L+\mu)^{\alpha-\gamma}=-\frac{u(L+\mu)^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{n-1}h^{2}-(n-1),

thus the solution is

h(r)=-(n-1)u(L+\mu)^{-(\alpha-\gamma)}\cot(r_{0}-r),

for some
r_{0}>L+\mu
. To summarize, the equation (
27
) has a solution
h=h(r)
on a maximal interval
(-r_{0},r_{0})\supset[-L,L]
, and
h
blows up like
-(n-1)u(L+\mu)^{-(\alpha-\gamma)}\cot|r_{0}\mp r|
near the endpoints
\pm r_{0}
.

Finally, we let
f(r)
solve the ODE

(29)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-1}u^{\prime},\\
&f(0)=1.\end{aligned}\right.

This equation, along with (
27
), come from analyzing the equality case in the computations from (
15
) to (
17
). Note that
f
is even, since
u
is even and
h
is odd. When
r\geqslant L+\mu
, the ODE becomes

(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{f(r)}=-(n-1)\cot(r_{0}-r)\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ f(r)=c\sin(r_{0}-r)\ \ \text{for some $c>0$.}

For
\varepsilon\ll 1
consider the metric
g=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}f(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. By the asymptotic of
f
near
\pm r_{0}
, this represents a metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with acute cone singularities at the two poles
r=\pm r_{0}
. Note that
\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=-(n-1)f^{\prime\prime}/f
, and
\Ric(e,e)
(where
e
is tangent to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
) grows like
r^{-2}
near the poles. So we may choose
\varepsilon
so small such that
\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})
is the minimal eigenvalue of
\Ric
(see, e.g., the argument at the beginning of
Remark

4
).

From the ODEs (
27
), (
29
) it can be directly verified that

-\gamma\Big[\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{u}+(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\frac{u^{\prime}}{u}\Big]-(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}=(n-1).

This implies
-\gamma\Delta u+u\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=(n-1)u
.

Since
g
is conic and
u
is constant near the pole, we can smooth the metric near the pole, while not decreasing the minimal eigenvalue of
\Ric
. As a consequence, we still have
-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric u\geqslant(n-1)u
in the smoothed metric. This implies
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant n-1
. On the other hand, note that
\diam(M,g)>2L
, which can be made arbitrarily large.

3.
Unequally weighted isoperimetric profile and volume bounds

Let
n\geqslant 3
, and
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Let
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and set

\alpha:=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}.

Set the weighted volume
V_{0}:=\int_{M}u^{\alpha}\in(0,\infty]
, and define the unequally weighted isoperimetric profile

(30)

I(v):=\inf\left\{\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}:\ E\subset\subset M\ \text{has finite perimeter, and}\ \int_{E}u^{\alpha}=v\right\},

for all
v\in[0,V_{0})
. Here
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of
E
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\gamma,\alpha,V_{0},I(v)
be as above, and let
\lambda>0
. Suppose
M^{n}
is complete. Assume
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfies
\inf(u)=1
and

(31)

\gamma\Delta u\leqslant u\Ric-(n-1)\lambda u.

Suppose for a fixed
v_{0}\in(0,V_{0})
, there exists a bounded set
E
with finite perimeter, such that
\int_{E}u^{\alpha}=v_{0}
and
\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}=I(v_{0})
. Then
I
satisfies

(32)

I^{\prime\prime}I\leqslant-\frac{(I^{\prime})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda

in the viscosity sense at
v_{0}
.

Proof
(n\leqslant 7)
.

For the ease of readability, we present the proof here in the case
n\leqslant 7
. When
n\geqslant 8
one again needs to deal with the possible singularity of minimizers, and we postpone the resolution of this to
Appendix

A
.

We recall that (
32
) holds in the viscosity sense at
v_{0}
if the following holds: for every smooth function
\varphi
defined in some neighborhood
\mathcal{I}_{v_{0}}\ni v_{0}
, such that
\varphi(v_{0})=I(v_{0})
and
\varphi(x)\leqslant I(x)
in
\mathcal{I}_{v_{0}}
, we have

(33)

-\varphi^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})\varphi(v_{0})\geqslant(n-1)\lambda+\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(v_{0})^{2}}{n-1}.

The viscosity inequality follows by finding an upper barrier of the isoperimetric profile satisfying (
33
), and the latter is done by computing the second variation of the weighted area. Notice that by classical results in Geometric Measure Theory (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Section 3.10]
), the set
E
in the statement has smooth boundary, since
n\leqslant 7
. For a bounded set
F\subset M
with smooth boundary, define the weighted volume and perimeter

V(F):=\int_{F}u^{\alpha},\qquad A(F):=\int_{\partial F}u^{\gamma}.

For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, let us consider a smooth family of sets
\{E_{t}\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
, such that
E_{0}=E
, the variational vector field
X_{t}
along
\partial E_{t}
at
t=0
is
\varphi\nu
(where
\nu
denotes the outer unit normal of
\partial E_{t}
), and
\nabla_{X_{t}}X_{t}=(\varphi\varphi_{\nu})\nu
at
t=0
. Define
V(t):=V(E_{t})
and
A(t):=A(E_{t})
; thus
V(0)=v_{0}
,
A(0)=I(v_{0})
. Let
H
be the mean curvature of
\partial E
, and denote
\varphi_{\nu}:=\langle\nabla\varphi,\nu\rangle
,
u_{\nu}:=\langle\nabla u,\nu\rangle
for brevity. We compute the first and second variations of the (weighted) volume at the initial time:

(34)

\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)=\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha}\varphi,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}V}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)=\int_{\partial E}(H+\alpha u^{-1}u_{\nu})u^{\alpha}\varphi^{2}+u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu},

where the term
u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu}
in the last equality appears due to our assumption have that the acceleration
\nabla_{X_{t}}X_{t}
is
(\varphi\varphi_{\nu})\nu
. Next, compute the first variation of the (weighted) area:

(35)

\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)=\int_{\partial E}u^{\gamma}\varphi(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})=\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha}\varphi\cdot u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}).

Finally, the second variation of the area is

(36)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}\Big(-\Delta_{\partial E}\varphi-\Ric(\nu,\nu)\varphi-|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\varphi\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle+\Big(-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}\varphi+\gamma u^{-1}\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)\varphi-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial E}u,\nabla_{\partial E}\varphi\rangle\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle+\left(\gamma u^{\alpha-1}u_{\nu}\varphi^{2}+u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu}+Hu^{\alpha}\varphi^{2}\right)u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}),

where the term
u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu}
in the last line again appears from evaluating
\nabla_{X_{t}}X_{t}
, as noted above. Since
E
is a volume-constrained minimizer, we must have
\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)=0
whenever
\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)=0
. Hence
u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})
is constant due to (
35
).

From now on, we fix the choice
\varphi=u^{-\gamma}
in the variation. Since
V(t)
is strictly monotone in
t
in a neighbourhood of 0, we may view
A
as a smooth function in
V
, defined in some neighborhood of
v_{0}
. The value of the constant
u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})
is thus obtained through the chain rule:

(37)

A^{\prime}(v_{0})=\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}V}(v_{0})=\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)}{\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)}=\frac{\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha}\varphi\cdot u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})}{\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha}\varphi}=u^{\gamma-\alpha}\left(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}\right).\

Observe that

(38)

\gamma u^{\alpha-1}u_{\nu}\varphi^{2}=\gamma u^{\alpha-2\gamma-1}u_{\nu},\qquad u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu}=-\gamma u^{\alpha-2\gamma-1}u_{\nu}.

Applying our choice and (
38
) to (
36
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}-\Delta_{\partial E}(u^{-\gamma})-\Ric(\nu,\nu)u^{-\gamma}-|\mathrm{II}|^{2}u^{-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-2-\gamma}u_{\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma u^{-\gamma-1}\Big(\Delta u-\Delta_{\partial E}u-Hu_{\nu}\Big)-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial E}u,\nabla_{\partial E}(u^{-\gamma})\rangle

\displaystyle+\left(Hu^{\alpha-2\gamma}\right)u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}).

Integrating by parts and re-grouping we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}-\Ric(\nu,\nu)u^{-\gamma}+\gamma u^{-\gamma-1}\Delta u+\Big[\gamma(-\gamma-1)+\gamma^{2}\Big]|\nabla_{\partial E}u|^{2}u^{-\gamma-2}

\displaystyle-|\mathrm{II}|^{2}u^{-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-\gamma-2}u_{\nu}^{2}-\gamma Hu^{-\gamma-1}u_{\nu}+Hu^{-\gamma}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}).

For convenience, set
X=u^{\alpha-\gamma}A^{\prime}(v_{0})
,
Y=u^{-1}u_{\nu}
. Thus
H=X-\gamma Y
by (
37
). Using the main condition (
31
), the fact
\gamma(-\gamma-1)+\gamma^{2}\leqslant 0
, the trace inequality
|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\geqslant H^{2}/(n-1)
, we reduce the above inequality to

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial E}-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}+u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{2\gamma XY}{n-1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}Y^{2}}{n-1}-\gamma Y^{2}

\displaystyle-\gamma XY+\gamma^{2}Y^{2}+X^{2}-\gamma XY\Big].

Next, by using the chain rule and the formulae for the derivatives of the inverse function,

A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)\right)^{-2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)-\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)\right)^{-3}\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}V}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0).

Thus, joining (

), (

), (

), and (

) we get, calling

,

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle\cdot A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})\leqslant\int_{\partial E}-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}+u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{2\gamma XY}{n-1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}Y^{2}}{n-1}-\gamma Y^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\gamma XY+\gamma^{2}Y^{2}+X^{2}-\gamma XY\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-Q^{-1}\cdot\Big(\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha-\gamma}A^{\prime}(v_{0})\Big)\cdot\Big(\int_{\partial E}(X+\alpha Y-2\gamma Y)u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}+u^{-\gamma}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{2\gamma XY}{n-1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}Y^{2}}{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\gamma Y^{2}-\gamma XY+\gamma^{2}Y^{2}+X^{2}-\gamma XY-X^{2}-\alpha XY+2\gamma XY\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}u^{-\gamma}\left[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\left(\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}-\alpha\right)XY+\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\right)Y^{2}\right]-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial E}-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}u^{-\gamma}-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}-\frac{A^{\prime 2}(v_{0})}{n-1}u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma},

where in (

\alpha=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
, and
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
. Notice further that

, which is implied by

and

Q^{2}A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})\leqslant-\Big(\frac{A^{\prime 2}(v_{0})}{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda\Big)\int_{\partial E}u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}.

By Holder’s inequality (recall

), we have

(43)

A(v_{0})\int_{\partial E}u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}\geqslant\Big(\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha-\gamma}\Big)^{2}=Q^{2}.

By joining (
42
) and (
43
), we finally obtain

(44)

A(v_{0})A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})\leqslant-\frac{A^{\prime}(v_{0})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda.

Notice that

and

in some neighborhood of

, so

is an upper barrier of

which satisfies (

: indeed, for any test function

in a neighborhood

and

in a smaller neighborhood of

, and thus

and

∎

Lemma 3
.

Suppose
M
is complete, and
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
is positive. Additionally, assume
x\in M
satisfies
u(x)=\inf(u)=1
. Then, if
I
is defined as in (

\limsup_{v\to 0}v^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}I(v)\leqslant n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{1/n},

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

For a small
r_{0}
, both functions
V(r)=\int_{B(x,r)}u^{\alpha}
and
A(r)=\int_{\partial B(x,r)}u^{\gamma}
are smooth and increasing in
(0,r_{0})
. We have the asymptotics

V(r)=\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})r^{n}+O(r^{n+1}),\qquad A(r)=n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})r^{n-1}+O(r^{n}).

This implies that the function

has the asymptotic

The result follows from the straightforward bound

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
V\in(0,+\infty]
, and let
I:[0,V)\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that
I(0)=0
, and
I(v)>0
for every
v\in(0,V)
. Assume that for some
\lambda>0
we have

I^{\prime\prime}I\leqslant-\frac{(I^{\prime})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda,\qquad\text{in the viscosity sense on $(0,V)$},

and

(45)

\limsup_{v\to 0^{+}}v^{-\frac{n-1}{n}}I(v)\leqslant n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{1/n}.

Then
V\leqslant\lambda^{-n/2}\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Proof.

The proof is classical. Let us write it here for completeness. Set
\psi:=I^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
. A direct computation gives that

(46)

\psi^{\prime\prime}\leqslant-\lambda n\psi^{(2-n)/n}\qquad\text{in the viscosity sense on $(0,V)$.}

For every
\zeta>0
, let us consider the model function
I_{\zeta}:(0,V_{\zeta})\to\mathbb{R}
implicitly defined by

(47)

I_{\zeta}\Big(\zeta\int_{0}^{r}\mu(s)^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)=\zeta\mu(r)^{n-1},

where
\mu:[0,\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}]\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\mu(r):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)
. Notice
V_{\zeta}=\frac{\zeta}{\lambda^{n/2}}\frac{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})}{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
, and
I_{\zeta}(0)=I_{\zeta}(V_{\zeta})=0
. A direct computation gives that the function
\psi_{\zeta}:=I_{\zeta}^{\frac{n}{n-1}}
satisfies

(48)

\psi_{\zeta}^{\prime\prime}=-\lambda n\psi_{\zeta}^{(2-n)/n}\qquad\text{on $(0,V_{\zeta})$.}

Denoting by
\psi^{\prime}_{+}(0)
the upper right-hand Dini derivative, by (
45
) we have

\psi^{\prime}_{+}(0)\leqslant n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Moreover, direct computation gives

(\psi_{\zeta})^{\prime}_{+}(0)=n\zeta^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Notice that when
\zeta=n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})=\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, we exactly have
V_{\zeta}=\lambda^{-n/2}\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})
.

Suppose by contradiction that
V>\lambda^{-n/2}\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Then there is
\zeta^{\prime}>n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})
such that
V_{\zeta^{\prime}}<V
. Since
\zeta^{\prime}>n\vol(\mathbb{B}^{n})
we have

(49)

+\infty>(\psi_{\zeta^{\prime}})^{\prime}_{+}(0)>\psi^{\prime}_{+}(0).

Taking into account (
46
), (
48
), and (
49
) we can apply
[
6
, Théorème C.2.2]
, after having used
[
38
, Proposition A.3, (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)]
. Hence we get

\psi(v)\leqslant\psi_{\zeta^{\prime}}(v)\qquad\text{for all $v\leqslant V_{\zeta^{\prime}}$}.

The latter inequality, evaluated at
v=V_{\zeta^{\prime}}
, gives
\psi(V_{\zeta^{\prime}})\leqslant 0
, which is a contradiction with the fact that
I>0
on
(0,V_{\zeta^{\prime}})\subset(0,V)
.
∎

Remark 4

(On the supercritical case)
.

For the values
\gamma\in(\frac{n-1}{n-2},\infty)
, none of the results in
Theorem

1
continue to hold. A counterexample is the following. On
M=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
consider the spherically symmetric metric
g=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}f(r)^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}
, where
f(r)
is a fixed positive periodic nonconstant function, and
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen small with respect to
f
. We have

\mathrm{Ric}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})=-(n-1)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f},\qquad\mathrm{Ric}(e,e)=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}+(n-2)\frac{1-\varepsilon^{2}(f^{\prime})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}f^{2}},

where
e
is an arbitrary unit vector perpendicular to
\partial_{r}
. Hence, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
(depending on
f
), we have
\mathrm{Ric}=\mathrm{Ric}(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})
. Recall that, if
u:=u(r)
, one can directly compute

\Delta u=u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{\varepsilon f}u^{\prime}(\varepsilon f)^{\prime}=u^{\prime\prime}+(n-1)\frac{u^{\prime}f^{\prime}}{f}.

Consider the choice
u:=f(r)^{2-n}
, and set
\gamma_{0}=\frac{n-1}{n-2}
. A direct computation shows

-\gamma_{0}\Delta u+\Ric u=-\gamma_{0}\Delta u+\Ric(\partial_{r},\partial_{r})u=0.

Note that this example appears as a rigidity case of
[
10
, Proposition 3.5]
.

We are now committed to show that, for all
\gamma>\gamma_{0}
, there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

(52)

-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}-\lambda\geqslant 0.

Therefore
(M,g)
satisfies the main condition (
1
) (with
\frac{\lambda}{n-1}
in place of
\lambda
), but it has
\pi_{1}(M)=\mathbb{Z}
. Let
v\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and set
\eta:=v/u
, where
u
is as above. Then
v=u\eta
, and integrating by parts

(53)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\gamma_{0}|\nabla v|^{2}+\Ric v^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\gamma_{0}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+2\gamma_{0}u\eta\langle\nabla u,\nabla\eta\rangle+\gamma_{0}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Ric u^{2}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\gamma_{0}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}+u\eta^{2}(\Ric u-\gamma_{0}\Delta u)=\int_{M}\gamma_{0}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}.

Hence

(54)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla v|^{2}+\Ric v^{2}

\displaystyle=(\gamma-\gamma_{0})\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}+\gamma_{0}\int_{M}u^{2}|\nabla\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant(\gamma-\gamma_{0})\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}+\gamma_{0}\min(u)^{2}\int_{M}\Big|\nabla\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)\Big|^{2}.

Call
\alpha:=\gamma-\gamma_{0}
,
\beta:=\gamma_{0}\min(u)^{2}
. Observe that

c(M):=\inf\left\{\alpha\int_{M}|\nabla v|^{2}+\beta\int_{M}\Big|\nabla\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)\Big|^{2}:v\in C^{\infty}(M),\,\int_{M}v^{2}=1\right\}>0,

since
u
is nonconstant and a minimizer exists. Joining the latter with (
54
) we finally infer that

-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}-c\geqslant 0,

which is the sought (
52
) with
\lambda:=c(M)>0
. ∎

Remark 5

(Smoothing the eigenfunctions)
.

This remark is to prove what claimed in
Remark

1
in the introduction. Given
\lambda>0
and
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, assume the positivity of Dirichlet energy

(55)

\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+\Ric)\geqslant(n-1)\lambda,

on the
n
-dimensional manifold
M
.
We first aim at proving the following:

(56)

\displaystyle\text{If $M$ is compact and \eqref{eqn:PositiveSchrodinger2} holds, then}

\displaystyle\text{$\vol(\widetilde{M})\leqslant\lambda^{-n/2}\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})$, and $\pi_{1}(M)$ is finite.}

By regularizing the function
\mathrm{Ric}
, for each
0<\varepsilon\ll\lambda
there exists
V_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\|V_{\varepsilon}-\mathrm{Ric}\|_{C^{0}}\leqslant\varepsilon
. Note that
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+V_{\varepsilon})\geqslant(n-1)\lambda-\varepsilon
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be the first eigenfunction of
-\gamma\Delta+V_{\varepsilon}
. Notice that
u_{\varepsilon}
is positive, and we have

(57)

-\gamma\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\Ric u_{\varepsilon}\geqslant\Big[(n-1)\lambda-2\varepsilon\Big]u_{\varepsilon}.

Thus, applying
Theorem

1
with (
57
) and taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have proved the desired (
56
).

Let us now deal with the rigidity part. For simplicity, after rescaling, assume
\lambda=1
. Hence, assume we have
\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric)\geqslant n-1
and
\vol(M)=\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Let us show that
M
is isometric to the standard sphere of radius
1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(M)
be the first eigenfunction of
-\gamma\Delta+\mathrm{Ric}
, so that
-\gamma\Delta u+\Ric u=\lambda u
for some
\lambda\geqslant(n-1)
. Normalize
u
so that
\int_{M}u^{2}=1
. Set
\lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\lambda_{1}(-\gamma\Delta+V_{\varepsilon})
, and let
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(M)
be the corresponding first eigenfunction, normalized so that
\int_{M}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}=1
. Note that
|\lambda-\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant\varepsilon
, and
u,u_{\varepsilon}
are all positive.

Let us show that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
C^{1,\beta}
for every
0<\beta<1
. Note that
\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
are uniformly bounded, so there is a subsequence converging to some
\widetilde{u}\in W^{1,2}
strongly in
L^{2}
and weakly in
W^{1,2}
. Using lower semi-continuity, and the fact that

are normalized eigenfunctions, we have:

\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{2}+\Ric\widetilde{u}^{2}\leqslant\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{M}\gamma|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{M}V_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\lambda.

So it must happen that

. Thus

in

. Now, applying standard elliptic regularity, this can be bootstrapped to imply

for every

.

By
Theorem

1
(specifically, (
51
) in its proof) and the rigidity assumption, we have

\displaystyle\vol(M)

\displaystyle\leqslant\min(u_{\varepsilon})^{-\alpha}\int_{M}u_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\leqslant\vol(\mathbb{S}^{n})\big(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\big)=\vol(M)\big(1+o_{\varepsilon}(1)\big).

Passing this to the limit, we get

\vol(M)\leqslant\min(u)^{-\alpha}\int_{M}u^{\alpha}\leqslant\vol(M).

Hence

is constant, and we are reduced to pointwise Ricci bound, from which the rigidity follows from the classical Bishop-Gromov volume comparison.

Finally, the Bonnet-Myers result (
2
) follows from (
57
) and the
C^{1,\beta}
-convergence obtained above.
∎

4.

and spectral
\mathrm{biRic}\geqslant n-2

We aim at showing that when

, a spectral biRicci condition descends to a spectral Ricci condition on properly chosen warped

-bubbles. We will keep track of the sharp constants in the process, and we will use the forthcoming computations to show

5
. We stress that the following computations are inspired by

. Similar computations have appeared in

Let

For an arbitrary fixed domain
\Omega_{0}
with
\Omega_{-}\subset\subset\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega_{+}\subset M

(59)

defined on sets of finite perimeter, where

is the reduced boundary of

is smooth because

Let

\partial\Omega

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[-\Delta_{\partial\Omega}\varphi-|\text{II}|^{2}\varphi-\Ric(\nu,\nu)\varphi-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}\varphi

(61)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}\Big[-\Delta_{\partial\Omega}(\psi u^{-\gamma/2})\psi u^{\gamma/2}-\gamma u^{-1}\psi^{2}\Delta_{\partial\Omega}u-\gamma u^{\gamma/2-1}\psi\langle\nabla_{\partial\Omega}u,\nabla_{\partial\Omega}(\psi u^{-\gamma/2})\rangle\Big]

\displaystyle+\psi^{2}\Big[-|\text{II}|^{2}-\Ric(\nu,\nu)-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}+\gamma u^{-1}\big(\Delta u-Hu_{\nu}\big)-h_{\nu}\Big]

\displaystyle=:P+Q.

|\text{II}|^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\geqslant\mathrm{biRic}-\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}+\frac{6-n}{4}H^{2}.

\displaystyle-|\text{II}|^{2}-\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+\gamma u^{-1}\Delta u

\displaystyle\leqslant-\mathrm{biRic}+\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{6-n}{4}H^{2}+\gamma u^{-1}\Delta u

\displaystyle\leqslant-\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{6-n}{4}H^{2}.

(63)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi^{2}\Big[-\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{6-n}{4}(h-\gamma Y)^{2}-\gamma Y^{2}-\gamma(h-\gamma Y)Y+|\nabla h|\Big]=

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi^{2}\Big[-\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}_{\partial\Omega}+|\nabla h|-\frac{6-n}{4}h^{2}+\frac{4-n}{2}\gamma hY+\left(\frac{n-2}{4}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\right)Y^{2}\Big].

Notice that

for all

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}\Big(-\Delta_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}(\varphi\eta)-\Ric(\nu,\nu)\varphi\eta-|\textrm{II}|^{2}\varphi\eta\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi\eta

\displaystyle+\Big(-\gamma\varphi\eta u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}+\gamma u^{-1}\varphi\eta\nabla^{2}u(\nu,\nu)-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}(u),\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}(\varphi\eta)\rangle\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi\eta

\displaystyle+\big(\gamma u^{\alpha-1}u_{\nu}\varphi^{2}\eta^{2}+u^{\alpha}\varphi\eta(\varphi\eta)_{\nu}+Hu^{\alpha}\varphi^{2}\eta^{2}\big)u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}).

By the same argument as in the proof of
Lemma

2
, we have
u^{\gamma-\alpha}(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})=A^{\prime}(v_{0})
on
\partial^{\text{reg}}E
. Setting
\varphi=u^{-\gamma}
in the variation, we have, running the same computations as in
Lemma

2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}\eta|^{2}+u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\eta\eta_{\nu}A^{\prime}(v_{0})

\displaystyle-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}+u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Big[\frac{n-2}{n-1}X^{2}+\frac{2-n}{n-1}2\gamma XY+\Big(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}-\gamma\Big)Y^{2}\Big].

Next we plug this into the chain rule (
40
) and use the facts
\alpha=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
,
0\leqslant\gamma\leqslant\frac{n-1}{n-2}
, and
u^{\gamma}\geqslant u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}
(recall that we have normalized so that
\min(u)=1
, and
\gamma\geqslant\alpha
). Setting
Q:=\frac{\mathrm{d}V}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{\alpha-\gamma}\eta
we have:

\displaystyle Q^{2}A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}A}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)-Q^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}A}{\mathrm{d}t}(0)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}V}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(0)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}\eta|^{2}+u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\eta\eta_{\nu}A^{\prime}(v_{0})

\displaystyle-(n-1)\lambda u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}+u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Big[\frac{n-2}{n-1}X^{2}+\frac{2-n}{n-1}2\gamma XY\Big]

\displaystyle-Q^{-1}\cdot QA^{\prime}(v_{0})\cdot\Big(\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}(X+\alpha Y-2\gamma Y)u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\eta^{2}+u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\eta\eta_{\nu}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}\eta|^{2}-(n-1)\lambda u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}\eta^{2}-\frac{A^{\prime}(v_{0})^{2}}{n-1}u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{4}-\Big(\frac{A^{\prime}(v_{0})^{2}}{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda\Big)\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{2\alpha-3\gamma}\eta^{2}.

Hence, by using Hölder,

(86)

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(v_{0})\leqslant C\delta^{4}Q^{-2}-\Big(\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{\gamma}\Big)^{-1}\Big(\frac{A^{\prime}(v_{0})^{2}}{n-1}+(n-1)\lambda\Big).

Notice that
I(v_{0})=A(v_{0})=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{\gamma}
, and

Q=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}u^{\alpha-\gamma}\eta\geqslant\widetilde{C}\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}E}\eta\geqslant\widetilde{C}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big(\partial^{\text{reg}}E\cap\{\eta=1\}\big).

Observe that if
\delta\to 0
we have that
\eta\to 1
almost everywhere on
\partial^{*}E
. Thus for every
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\delta_{0}>0
, so that the variation can be arranged such that
C\delta^{4}Q^{-2}\leqslant\varepsilon
for all
\delta<\delta_{0}
. Thus taking (
86
) into account the inequality

(87)

II^{\prime\prime}\leqslant-\frac{(I^{\prime})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda+\varepsilon I

holds in the viscosity sense at
v_{0}
. Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
the proof is concluded.
∎

Remark 8
.

The proof offered above, when specialized to the case
\gamma=\alpha=0
, gives a short and concise proof of the main result of
[
7
]
, i.e.,
[

.

Proof of
Lemma

1
(
n\geqslant 8
).

Let
\Omega
be a minimizer of (
14
). By the Riemannian analogue of
[
32
, Theorem 27.5]
, and
[
32
, Theorem 28.1]
, we have that the regular part of
\partial\Omega
(denoted by
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
) is a smooth hypersurface, while the singular part of
\partial\Omega
(denoted by
\partial^{\text{sing}}\Omega)
has Hausdorff dimension at most
n-8
.

Let
\nu
denote the outer unit normal at
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
. Similar to the argument above, for every
\delta\ll 1
there exists a function
\eta
compactly supported in
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
, such that

(88)

\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\eta|^{2}\leqslant C\delta^{4},

where
C
does not depend on
\delta
. For the case here, the inequality (
82
) is proved by a direct comparison between
\Omega
and
\Omega\cap B(x,r)
.

For
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)
, consider a family of sets
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
, such that
\Omega_{0}=\Omega
and the variational vector field along
\partial\Omega_{t}
is
\varphi\eta\nu
. The first variation becomes

(89)

0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(\Omega_{t})\Big|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\big(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}-hu^{\alpha-\gamma}\big)u^{\gamma}\varphi\eta.

Since
\varphi
is arbitrary, and the support of
\eta
can be taken to exhaust
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
, we have
H=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}-\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu}
on
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
. Then, computing the second variation,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}E(\Omega_{t})\Big|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\Big[-\Delta_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}(\varphi\eta)-|\mathrm{II}|^{2}\varphi\eta-\Ric(\nu,\nu)\varphi\eta-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}\varphi\eta

\displaystyle+\gamma u^{-1}\varphi\eta\big(\Delta u-\Delta_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u-Hu_{\nu}\big)-\gamma u^{-1}\big\langle\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u,\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}(\varphi\eta)\big\rangle

\displaystyle-h_{\nu}u^{\alpha-\gamma}\varphi\eta+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-\gamma-1}u_{\nu}\varphi\eta\Big]u^{\gamma}\varphi\eta.

Setting
\varphi=u^{-\gamma}
, integrating by parts,
and discarding the negative term we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\eta|^{2}-|\mathrm{II}|^{2}u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}-\Ric(\nu,\nu)u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}-\gamma u^{-2-\gamma}u_{\nu}^{2}\eta^{2}+\gamma u^{-1-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Delta u

\displaystyle-\gamma Hu^{-1-\gamma}u_{\nu}\eta^{2}-h_{\nu}u^{\alpha-2\gamma}\eta^{2}+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-2\gamma-1}u_{\nu}\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\eta|^{2}+u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Big[-\frac{H^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda-\gamma(u^{-1}u_{\nu})^{2}

\displaystyle-\gamma H(u^{-1}u_{\nu})+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}+(\gamma-\alpha)hu^{\alpha-\gamma}(u^{-1}u_{\nu})\Big].

Setting
X=hu^{\alpha-\gamma}
and
Y=u^{-1}u_{\nu}
(so
H=X-\gamma Y
), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\eta|^{2}+u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}+\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}XY-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{n-1}Y^{2}-(n-1)\lambda

\displaystyle-\gamma Y^{2}-\gamma(X-\gamma Y)Y+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}+(\gamma-\alpha)XY\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}u^{-\gamma}|\nabla_{\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega}\eta|^{2}+u^{-\gamma}\eta^{2}\Big[-\frac{X^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda+|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}\Big].

By (
88
), if
h
satisfies

(90)

|\nabla h|u^{\alpha-\gamma}<(n-1)\lambda-C\delta^{4}+\frac{h^{2}u^{2\alpha-2\gamma}}{n-1}

(with a different constant
C
, possibly depending also on
\partial^{\text{reg}}\Omega
but independent on
\delta
), then we obtain a contradiction.

Given this, one finishes the proof by modifying the
n\leqslant 7
argument as follows. Let
0<\vartheta<(n-1)\lambda
. Arguing as in (
19
)
\,\sim\,
(
24
), if

(91)

\diam(M)>\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda-\vartheta/(n-1)}}\cdot\Big(\frac{\sup(u)}{\inf(u)}\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}\gamma}\cdot(1+\varepsilon)^{2}+2\varepsilon,

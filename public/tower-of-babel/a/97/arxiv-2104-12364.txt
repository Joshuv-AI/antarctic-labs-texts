Observability for port-Hamiltonian systems
Birgit Jacob, Hans Zwart

Abstract
The class of port-Hamiltonian systems incorporates many physical models, such as mechanical systems in the finite-dimensional case and wave and beam equations in the infinite-dimensional case. In this paper we study a subclass of linear first order port-Hamiltonian systems. In [ 3 ] , it is shown that these systems are exactly observable when the energy is not dissipated internally and when sufficient observations are made at the boundary. In this article we study the observability properties for these systems when internal dissipation of energy is possible. We cannot show the exact observability, but we do show that the Hautus test is satisfied. In general, the Hautus test is weaker than exact observability, but stronger than approximate observability. Hence we conclude that these systems are approximately observable.



I
Introduction

In this paper we investigate the exact observability of port-Hamiltonian systems of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial x}{\partial t}(\zeta,t)

\displaystyle=\left(P_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+G_{0}\right)({\mathcal{H}}({\zeta})x(\zeta,t)),

\displaystyle x(\zeta,0)

\displaystyle=x_{0}(\zeta),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}W_{B,1}&W_{B,0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}({\mathcal{H}}x)(1,t)\\
({\mathcal{H}}x)(0,t)\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}W_{C,1}&W_{C,0}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}({\mathcal{H}}x)(1,t)\\
({\mathcal{H}}x)(0,t)\end{bmatrix},

We refer to Section

This class of systems describes a special class of port-Hamiltonian systems, which however is rich enough
to cover in particular the wave equation, the transport equation and the Timoshenko beam
equation, and also coupled beam and wave equations.
For more information on this class of port-Hamiltonian systems we refer to the first paper on this topic,
[
15
]

[

Provided the port-Hamiltonian system is well-posed, that is, for every initial condition
x_{0}
the port-Hamiltonian system possesses a unique (mild) solution, we aim to characterize

On one hand, exact observability implies the Hautus test and on the other hand, the Hautus test implies approximate observability, see Section

In
[

]
it is shown, that if additionally to the general assumptions of Section

II
Background on exact observability

In this section we assume that
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space
X
and
C
is a linear bounded operator
from
\mathbf{D}(A)
, equipped with the graph norm, to another Hilbert space
Y
. We now consider the observation system

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)

\displaystyle=Ax(t),\quad t\geq 0,\qquad x(0)=x_{0},

(1)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=Cx(t),\quad t\geq 0.

(2)

If the initial condition
x_{0}
is an element of
\mathbf{D}(A)
, then the unique (classical) solution of (
1
)-(
2
) is given by

x(t)=T(t)x_{0},\qquad y(t)=CT(t)x_{0},\qquad t\geq 0,

thanks to the fact that
\mathbf{D}(A)
is a
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
-invariant subspace of
X
. In order to define a (mild) solution of (
1
)-(
2
) for arbitrary initial conditions
x_{0}\in X
an extra condition is required.

Definition II.1

The operator
C
is called an
admissible observation operator for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
, if there is a time
t_{0}>0
and a constant
m>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt\leq m\|x_{0}\|^{2},\quad\mbox{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbf{D}(A).

(3)

There is a rich literature on admissible observation operators, see for example
[
4
]
. If
C
is an admissible observation operator for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
, then (
3
) holds for every
t_{0}>0
, where the constant
m
may depend on
t_{0}
. If the constant
m
can be chosen independent of
t_{0}
, then
C
is called an
infinite-time admissible observation operator for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. If the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable, then the notion of admissibility and infinite-time admissibility are equivalent. Moreover, if
C
is an admissible observation operator for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then
C
is also an admissible observation operator for
(e^{\alpha t}T(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
Admissibility of the observation operator
C
guarantees that the mapping

\Psi_{t_{0}}:\mathbf{D}(A)\rightarrow L^{2}(0,t_{0};Y),\qquad x_{0}\mapsto CT(.)x_{0}

has a continuos extension to a linear bounded operator from
X
to
L^{2}(0,t_{0};Y)
, again denoted by
\Psi_{t_{0}}
. Thus, in this case for every
x\in X
the unique (mild) solution of (
1
)-(
2
) is given by

x(t)=T(t)x_{0},\qquad y=\Psi_{t_{0}}x_{0},\qquad t\in[0,t_{0}].

In this article we are in particular interested in observability, which we define next.

Definition II.2

The observation system (
1
)-(
2
) is called
exact observable in finite time
, if there exists a time
t_{0}>0
and a constant
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt\geq\delta\|x_{0}\|^{2},\quad\mbox{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbf{D}(A),

(4)

the observation system (
1
)-(
2
) is called
exact observable in infinite time
, if there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt\geq\delta\|x_{0}\|^{2},\quad\mbox{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbf{D}(A).

(5)

Further, the observation system (
1
)-(
2
) is called
approximate observable in infinite time
, if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt>0,\quad\mbox{for all }x_{0}\in\mathbf{D}(A),x_{0}\not=0.

If
C
is an admissible observation operator for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
, then the observation system (
1
)-(
2
) is exact observable in finite-time if and only if there is a
t_{0}>0
such that the mapping
\Psi_{t_{0}}
has a linear bounded left-inverse. Further, if the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable, then the notion of observability in finite time and infinite time are equivalent. We recall, that a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is
exponentially stable
, if there exist constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega>0
such that

\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{-\omega t},\qquad t\geq 0.

A
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space

is
strongly stable
, if

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|T(t)x\|=0,\qquad x\in X.

Next we provide an equivalent characterization of exact observability in term of Lyapunov (in)equalities.

Proposition II.1

Let
A
be the generator of a strongly stable
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on a Hilbert space
X
. Then the following statements are equivalent

1.

The observation system (
1
)–(
2
) is exact observable in infinite time.

2.

There exists a linear bounded self-adjoint operator
L
on
X
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle x,Lx\rangle\geq\delta\|x\|^{2}

(6)

for all
x\in X
and some
\delta>0
, and

\displaystyle\langle Ax,Lx\rangle+\langle Lx,Ax\rangle=-\|Cx\|^{2}

(7)

for every
x\in\mathbf{D}(A)
.

3.

There exists a linear bounded self-adjoint operator
L
on
X
satisfying (
6
)
for all
x\in X
and some
\delta>0
, and

\displaystyle\langle Ax,Lx\rangle+\langle Lx,Ax\rangle\geq-\|Cx\|^{2}

(8)

for every
x\in\mathbf{D}(A)
.

Inequality (
8
) is a Lyapunov inequality.

Proof:

The implication
1)\Rightarrow 2)
is well-know. For bounded operators
C\in L(X,Y)
the proof can be found in
[
1
, Exercise 6.10]
. However, the general proof hardly differs. Furthermore, it is clear that
2)\Rightarrow 3)
. So we concentrate on the implication 3)
\Rightarrow
1).

For
x_{0}\in\mathbf{D}(A)
we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle T(t)x_{0},LT(t)x_{0}\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \langle AT(t)x_{0},LT(t)x_{0}\rangle+

\displaystyle\ \langle T(t)x_{0},LAT(t)x_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ -\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}.

In the last step we used (
8
) with
x=T(t)x_{0}
.
Integrating both sides from
t=0
to
t=t_{0}
, gives

\langle T(t_{0})x_{0},LT(t_{0})x_{0}\rangle-\langle x_{0},Lx_{0}\rangle\geq-\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt.

Since the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is strongly stable and using (
6
), we conclude that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|CT(t)x_{0}\|^{2}dt\geq\delta\|x_{0}\|^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

. Further, the Hautus test (HT) is sufficient for exact

. Further, the Hautus test (HT) is

-group
[

]
.
However, in general the Hautus test (HT) is not sufficient for

[

]
. We refer the reader to

[

VI
Conclusions and remarks

In the previous section we have shown that port-Hamiltonian systems of the form (
9
)–(
12
) are exactly observable provided
G_{0}^{*}+G_{0}=0
, and satisfy the Hautus test for any bounded
G_{0}
. Since the Hautus test implies approximate observability we have that this weaker form of observability does hold. Whether or not port-Hamiltonian systems of the form (
9
)–(
12
) are always exactly observable is an open problem. We assert that the answer to that question is yes.

Since controllability and observability are dual properties, similar controllability results hold if we have
n
-dimensional control at the boundary. Thus the (stable) system (
9
)–(
10
) with control

u(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{B,1}&W_{B,0}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}({\mathcal{H}}x)(1,t)\\
({\mathcal{H}}x)(0,t)\end{array}\right]

is exactly controllable when
G_{0}^{*}+G_{0}=0
, and satisfies the (dual) Hautus test always.

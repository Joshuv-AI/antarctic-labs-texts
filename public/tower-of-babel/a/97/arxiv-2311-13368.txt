An Erdős-Stone type result for high-order spectra of graphs
Chunmeng Liu, Jiang Zhou, Changjiang Bu

Abstract
Erdős-Stone Theorem is a well-known result in extremal graph theory which determines the asymptotic behaviour of maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex H H -free graph. In 2009, Nikiforov gave a spectral version of Erdős-Stone Theorem. In this paper, we obtain a tensor’s spectral version of Erdős-Stone Theorem.

Let

For vectors

\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}
is a vector in

whose

\sum_{i_{2},...,i_{m}=1}^{n}a_{ii_{2}...i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}

If there exist

and nonzero vector

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}x^{m-1}=\lambda x^{[m-1]},

then

of

, and

is called an

of

corresponding to

, where

.
A tensor is called nonnegative if its each entry is nonnegative.
A tensor is called symmetric if its entries are invariant under any permutation of their indices.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Hölder’s inequality)
Let

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}}{n}\geq\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}}{\binom{n}{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with

\displaystyle\mu^{(t)}(G)\leq\frac{t}{n}\binom{n}{t}^{\frac{1}{t}}\left(c_{t}(G)\right)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}.

is a complete graph or a

-free graph.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}(G)=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{t}})
be the

-clique tensor of

.
Let a nonnegative vector

be the eigenvector corresponding to the

-clique spectral radius
\mu^{(t)}(G)
and

.
By Lemma

, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu^{(t)}(G)

\displaystyle=x^{T}\mathcal{A}(G)x^{t-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{t}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}=\sum_{\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G)}\frac{1}{(t-1)!}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}

\displaystyle=t\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G),\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{t}}.

(3)

From (
3

, we get

\displaystyle\mu^{(t)}(G)

\displaystyle\leq t\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G),\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\end{subarray}}1^{\frac{t}{t-1}}\right)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G),\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}^{t}x_{i_{2}}^{t}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}

\displaystyle=t\left(c_{t}(G)\right)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G),\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}^{t}x_{i_{2}}^{t}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(4)

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}\in C_{t}(G),\\
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\end{subarray}}x_{i_{1}}^{t}x_{i_{2}}^{t}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{t}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}^{t}x_{i_{2}}^{t}\cdots x_{i_{t}}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{x_{1}^{t}+x_{2}^{t}+\cdots+x_{n}^{t}}{n}\right)\binom{n}{t}^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(5)

Combining inequalities (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mu^{(t)}(G)\leq\frac{t}{n}\binom{n}{t}^{\frac{1}{t}}\left(c_{t}(G)\right)^{\frac{t-1}{t}}.

-free graph, we get

If

-cliques containing each vertex in

Therefore, equality holds if

Proof of Theorem

Thus

\displaystyle ex_{\mu^{(r)}}(n,H)\geq\mu^{(r)}(T_{r}(n)).

For the graph

\displaystyle\mu^{(r)}(T_{r}(n))\geq\frac{rc_{r}(T_{r}(n))}{n}.

, where

\displaystyle\frac{rc_{r}(T_{r}(n))}{n}=\frac{r}{n}\left(n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{r}\right)\geq\frac{r}{n}\left(\frac{n}{r}-1\right)\cdots\left(\frac{n}{r}-1\right)=\frac{r}{n}\left(\frac{n}{r}-1\right)^{r}.

For

\displaystyle ex_{\mu^{(r)}}(n,H)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{r^{r-1}}-\frac{2^{r-1}}{r^{r-2}}\frac{1}{n}\right)n^{r-1}\geq\left(\frac{1}{r^{r-1}}-\epsilon\right)n^{r-1}.

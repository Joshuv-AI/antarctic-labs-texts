Alberto Chiarini, Giovanni Conforti, Giacomo Greco, Zhenjie Ren

Abstract
. We investigate the kinetic Schrödinger problem, obtained considering Langevin dynamics instead of Brownian motion in Schrödinger’s thought experiment. Under a quasilinearity assumption we establish exponential entropic turnpike estimates for the corresponding Schrödinger bridges and exponentially fast convergence of the entropic cost to the sum of the marginal entropies in the long-time regime, which provides as a corollary an entropic Talagrand inequality. In order to do so, we benefit from recent advances in the understanding of classical Schrödinger bridges and adaptations of Bakry–Émery formalism to the kinetic setting. Our quantitative results are complemented by basic structural results such as dual representation of the entropic cost and the existence of Schrödinger potentials.



1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

In two seminal contributions
[
43
,
44
]
E. Schrödinger considered the problem of finding the most likely evolution of a cloud of independent Brownian particles conditionally to observations. This problem is nowadays known as
Schrödinger problem
and may be viewed
[
38
,
35
]
as a more regular and probabilistic proxy for the Optimal transport (Monge-Kantorovich) problem. This observation has motivated recent interest from both the engineering and statistical machine learning communities
[
16
,
41
]
. Moreover, over the past few years, various kinds of Schrödinger problems have been introduced and studied in the literature with different aims and scopes such as, for example, the multiplicative Schrödinger problem
[
40
]
and the mean field Schrödinger problem
[
1
]
. In this article we investigate the
Kinetic Schrödinger Problem
, henceforth
KSP
, with particular emphasis on the long-time and ergodic behaviour of the corresponding Schrödinger bridges. A heuristic formulation of
KSP
is naturally given in terms of the celebrated Schrödinger’s thought experiment. Consider a system of
N\gg 1
independent stationary particles
(X^{1}_{t},\ldots,X^{N}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
evolving according to the Langevin dynamics

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X^{i}_{t}=V^{i}_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathrm{d}V^{i}_{t}=-\nabla U(X^{i}_{t})\mathrm{d}t-\gamma V^{i}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}B^{i}_{t},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,\end{cases}

and assume that two snapshots of the particle system at the initial time
t=0
and at the terminal time
t=T
have been taken. The Schrödinger problem is that of finding the most likely evolution of the particle system conditionally on this information.
In order to turn this heuristic description into a sound mathematical problem, we introduce the empirical path measure

\bm{\mu}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum\delta_{(X^{i}_{\cdot},V^{i}_{\cdot})}

that is a random probability measure on the space of continuous trajectories
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2d}):=\Omega
and consider two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, representing the observed configuration at initial and final time, that is to say

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}_{0}}\approx\mu,\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}_{T}}\approx\nu.

Then, leveraging Sanov’s Theorem
[
22
, Theorem 6.2.10]
, whose message is that the likelihood of a given evolution
\bm{\rho}
is measured through the relative entropy

\mathrm{Prob}\Big[\bm{\mu}^{N}\approx\bm{\rho}\Big]\approx\exp(-N\mathcal{H}(\bm{\rho}|\mathrm{R})),

we finally arrive at the variational problem

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\,\biggl\{\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{P}|\mathrm{R}):\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2d})),\,(X_{0})_{\#}\mathrm{P}=\mu,(X_{T})_{\#}\mathrm{P}=\nu\biggr\}.

(KSPd)

In the above,
\mathrm{R}
is the reference probability measure, that is the law of

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}=V_{t}\mathrm{d}t\\
\mathrm{d}V_{t}=-\,\nabla U(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t-\gamma V_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,\\
(X_{0},V_{0})\sim\mathfrak{m},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the invariant (probability) measure
\mathfrak{m}
is given by

\mathfrak{m}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}v)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-U(x)-\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v,

with
Z
being a normalising constant.
Moreover,
(X_{t},\,V_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
denotes the canonical process on
\Omega
,
\#
is the push-forward and
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathrm{R})
is the relative entropy functional defined on
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
as

\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{P}|\mathrm{R})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{P}}\left[\log\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{P}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}}\right]&\quad\text{if }\mathrm{P}\ll\mathrm{R},\\
+{\infty}&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Given that Schrödinger’s thought experiment is motivated by statistical mechanics and the physical relevance of the Langevin dynamics and its various applications, the study of the kinetic Schrödinger problem appears to be quite natural. Nevertheless, to the best of our knowledge, it seems that there has been no dedicated study so far, with the exception of
[
15
]
. The objective of this paper is to take some steps forward in this direction, in particular by gaining a quantitative understanding of optimal solutions, called Schrödinger bridges.

Turnpike property for Schrödinger bridges

The turnpike property is a general principle in optimal control theory stipulating that solutions of dynamic control problems are made of three pieces: first a rapid transition from the initial state to the steady state, the
turnpike
, then a long stationary phase localised around the turnpike, and finally another rapid transition to reach the final state. In order to link this concept to
KSPd
, we need to rephrase it as a stochastic control problem. This task is easily accomplished thanks to classical results on the representation of path measures with finite entropy, see e.g.
[
26
,
36
]
and we get that
KSPd
is equivalent to

\inf\left\{\mathcal{H}((X_{0},V_{0})_{\#}\mathrm{P}|\mathfrak{m})+\frac{1}{4\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{P}}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\Big]:\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega),\,\,\mathrm{P}\,\text{admissible}\right\},

(1.2)

where a path probability measure
\mathrm{P}
is admissible if and only if under
\mathrm{P}
, there exist a Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
adapted to the canonical filtration and an adapted process
(\alpha^{P}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{P}}[\int_{0}^{T}|\alpha^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t]<+\infty
and the canonical process satisfies

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X_{t}=V_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\\
\mathrm{d}V_{t}=-\nabla U(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t-\gamma V_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\alpha^{P}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{2\gamma}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\\
X_{0}\sim\mu,X_{T}\sim\nu.\end{cases}

(1.3)

For the control problem (
1.2
), the turnpike is the invariant measure
\mathfrak{m}
. Indeed, the natural tendency of the particle system is that of reaching configuration
\mathfrak{m}
and since Schrödinger bridges aim at approximating as much as possible the unconditional dynamics while matching the observed configurations, they should also favour configurations close to
\mathfrak{m}
. Obtaining a quantitative rigorous version of this statement is one of the main objectives of this article and, in view of (
1.3
), it is equivalent to show that Schrödinger bridges satisfy the turnpike property.
In the field of deterministic control, the turnpike phenomenon is rather well understood both in a finite and infinite dimensional setting, see either
[
49
,
48
]
and references therein, or the monographs
[
51
,
52
]
. The understanding of this phenomenon in stochastic control seems to be much more limited: see
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for results on mean field games and
[
17
,
1
]
for results on the classical and mean field Schrödinger problems. The reason why the turnpike property for Schrödinger bridges in the present context cannot be deduced from existing results lies in the hypocoercive
[
50
]
nature of the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation

\partial_{t}f_{t}(x,v)=\gamma\,\Delta_{v}f_{t}(x,v)-\gamma\,v\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{t}(x,v)+\nabla U\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{t}(x,v)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{t}(x,v),

(1.4)

describing the probability density of (
1.1
) with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. It is well known that the problem of quantifying the trend to equilibrium of this PDE is more challenging than for the classical (overdamped) Fokker-Planck equation
and this difficulty is of course reflected in the problem of establishing the turnpike property for the corresponding Schrödinger bridges. In this work, we rely on the important progresses made in the study of the long-time behaviour of (
1.4
) over the last fifteen years using either an analytical approach see e.g.
[
4
,
23
,
31
,
50
]
and references therein, or a probabilistic approach, see e.g.
[
25
,
29
]
, as well as on the new developments around the long-time behaviour of Schrödinger bridges, in order to gain some understanding on
controlled
versions of the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation. Leaving a more accurate comparison between our results and the existing literature to the text below, let us first present a very concise summary of our contributions and explain how this article is structured.

Organisation

1.1
,
1.2

\mu
and
\nu
that we eventually remove thanks to the technical results of Section
4
.

1.1.
The kinetic Schrödinger problem

This article is devoted to the analysis of a stochastic mass transportation problem, that we name
kinetic Schrödinger problem
, owing to the fact that it is obtained from the classical Schrödinger problem by replacing Brownian particles with a system of independent particles following the Langevin dynamics in Schrödinger’s thought experiment. The first formulation
KSPd
, that we proposed on the basis of Sanov’s Theorem, is in terms of an entropy minimisation problem over path probability measures. Besides the change of the reference measure, another difference with respect to classical instances of the Schrödinger problem lies in the fact that it is not the full marginal that is constrained at initial and final time, but only its spatial component. Even though
KSPd
seems to be a more faithful representation of Schrödinger’s thought experiment, also the problem with fully constrained marginals

\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}):=\inf\,\biggl\{\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{P}|\mathrm{R}):\mathrm{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega),\,(X_{0},V_{0})_{\#}\mathrm{P}=\bar{\mu},(X_{T},V_{T})_{\#}\mathrm{P}=\bar{\nu}\biggr\}\,.

(KFSPd)

where
\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is worth studying and we shall work on both problems in the sequel. Through a classical argument
[
26
]
it is possible to reduce the dynamic formulations (cf.
KSPd
and
KFSPd
) to static ones. For example,
KSPd
is equivalent to solving

\inf\,\biggl\{\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}):\pi\in\Pi_{X}(\mu,\,\nu)\biggr\}\,,

(KSP)

where
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\coloneqq\left((X_{0},V_{0}),\,(X_{T},V_{T})\right)_{\#}\mathrm{R}
is the joint law of
\mathrm{R}
at initial and terminal time and the set
\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)
is defined as

\Pi_{X}\left(\mu,\,\nu\right)\coloneqq\left\{\pi\in\mathcal{P}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}\bigr)\mid(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\pi=\mu,\,(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{2}})_{\#}\pi=\nu\right\},

with
\mathrm{proj}_{x_{i}}\bigl((x_{1},\,v_{1}),(x_{2},\,v_{2})\bigr)\coloneqq x_{i}
for any
i=1,2
. In a similar fashion, the static formulation of
KFSPd
is

\inf\,\biggl\{\mathcal{H}(\pi|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}):\pi\in\Pi(\bar{\mu},\,\bar{\nu})\biggr\}\,,

(KFSP)

where
\Pi(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
is the (usual) set of couplings of
\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}
. The equivalence between the static and dynamic formulations is obtained mixing optimal static solutions with the bridges of the reference measure, see
[
37
]
for details. Finally, one can also derive a fluid dynamic (Benamou-Brenier
[
5
]
) formulation as well a stochastic control formulation of both problems. In particular, the latter one, that we sketched at (
1.2
) is the one that motivated us to investigate the turnpike phenomenon. We now proceed to establish some basic though fundamental structural results on the kinetic Schrödinger problems at hand. But before doing so, let us present the assumptions under which our main results hold.

1.1.1.
Assumptions

We state here the assumptions on the potential
U
and on the constraints
\mu,\,\nu,\,\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}
that we use in the sequel. We define
\mathfrak{m}_{X},\,\mathfrak{m}_{V}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to be the respectively the space and velocity marginals of
\mathfrak{m}
, in particular
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V}
.

(H1)

U
is a
C^{\infty}
strongly convex potential with bounded derivatives of order
k\geq 2
.

(H2)

There exist
0<\alpha<\beta
such that

\sqrt{\beta}-\sqrt{\alpha}\leq\gamma\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha\operatorname{Id}_{d}\leq\nabla^{2}U(x)\leq\beta\operatorname{Id}_{d}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

where
\gamma>0
is the friction parameter in (
1.1
).

(H3)

The probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy

\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})<+{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})<+{\infty}\,.

(H4)

\mu,\nu\,\ll\mathfrak{m}_{X}
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}},\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{X})
and are compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(FH3)

The probability measures
\bar{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
satisfy

\mathcal{H}(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m})<+{\infty}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m})<+{\infty}\,.

(FH4)

\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\,\ll\mathfrak{m}
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}},\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})
and are compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

Assumption
(H2)
implies
local
gradient contraction bounds for the semigroup generated by the Langevin dynamics with a certain rate
\kappa>0
(see Proposition
2.2
or
[
4
]
). The exponential rate
\kappa
of Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
below is precisely the one, computed e.g. in
[
39
,
6
]
, at which the synchronous coupling is contractive for the (uncontrolled) Langevin dynamics.

For each of the main results, we will make it explicit which assumptions from the above list are needed.

1.1.2.
Duality

We begin with a duality result, analogous to the Monge-Kantorovich duality of optimal transport and the more recent dual representations of the entropic cost for the classical Schrödinger problem
[
27
]
.
It is worth noticing that, since the stationary Langevin dynamics is not a reversible measure,
\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)
is not symmetric in its arguments. Nevertheless, due to the “physical reversibility” of the dynamics
[
15
]
, that is, reversibility up to a sign flip in the velocities, it is not hard to show that
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric in its arguments.

Proposition 1.1
.

Grant
(H1)
and
(H3)
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)<\infty
and

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{\varphi,\psi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\biggl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{\varphi\oplus\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\biggr\}\,.

(1.5)

Similarly, grant
(H1)
and
(FH3)
it holds
\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})<\infty
and

\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}\left(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\right)=\sup_{\varphi,\psi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\biggl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\nu}-\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{\varphi\oplus\psi}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\biggr\}\,.

(1.6)

1.1.3.
The
fg
-decomposition

Optimal couplings in the Schrödinger problem are characterised by the fact that their density against the reference measure takes a product form, often called
fg
-decomposition
[
37
]
. In
KSPd

f
and
g
have the additional property of depending only on the first and second space variables respectively.

Proposition 1.2
.

Grant
(H1)
,
(H3)
. Then, for all
T>0
,
KSP
and
KSPd
admit unique solutions
\mu^{T},\mathrm{P}^{T}
with
\mu^{T}=((X_{0},V_{0}),(X_{T},V_{T}))_{\#}\mathrm{P}^{T}
and there exist two non-negative measurable functions
f^{T},\,g^{T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\rho^{T}(x,v,y,w)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{T}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}(x,v,y,w)=f^{T}(x)g^{T}(y),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\text{-a.s.}

(1.7)

Moreover,
f^{T},g^{T}
solve the Schrödinger system:

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}(x)=f^{T}(x)\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\big[g^{T}(X_{T})|X_{0}=x\big],\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}(y)=g^{T}(y)\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\big[f^{T}(X_{0})|X_{T}=y\big].\end{cases}

(1.8)

For
KFSP
and
KFSPd
, the uniqueness of solutions (hereafter
\bar{\mu}^{T}
and
\bar{}\mathrm{P}^{T}
respectively) and the
fg
-decomposition are a direct consequence of known results, see e.g.
[
42
]
, whereas the case
KSP
requires some more work. We remark here that for both dual representation of the cost and the
fg
-decomposition the strict convexity of
U
and its smoothness are not really necessary, a bounded Hessian would suffice.

1.2.
Long-time behaviour of the entropic costs

Let us now turn the attention to the ergodic properties of
KSP
and
KFSP
by investigating the long-time behaviour of the entropic cost. To explain the upcoming results, we remark that (H1) implies ergodicity of the Langevin dynamics
[
20
, Theorem 11.14]
and in particular one has the weak convergence

R_{0,T}\rightharpoonup\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}.

Intuitively, this implies that the variational problem
KSP
converges, in a sense to be made precise, to the problem

\min_{\pi\in\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)}\mathcal{H}(\pi\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}),

(1.9)

whose optimal solution and optimal value are easily seen to be
(\mu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V})\otimes(\nu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})
respectively. From the point of view of the particle system, this means that in the long-time limit, initial and final states of the system become essentially independent of one another. Moreover, the initial and final velocities are well approximated by independent Gaussians, and are independent from the spatial variables.
The result below turns this intuition into a solid argument, including a quantitative version of the convergence of the entropic cost towards the sum of the marginal entropies. For the classical Schrödinger problem, an analogous statement can be found in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Grant
(H1)
and
(H3)
. Then

\lim_{T\to{\infty}}\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\,\nu)=\mathcal{H}(\mu\mid\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu\mid\mathfrak{m}_{X})<{\infty}\,.

(1.10)

Moreover as
T\to{\infty}

\mu^{T}\rightharpoonup\left(\mu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V}\right)\otimes\left(\nu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V}\right)\in\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)\,,

(1.11)

weakly and, granted
(H2)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
(depending only on
d,\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
) such that for any
0<\delta\leq 1
, as soon as
T>(\frac{1}{\kappa}\log C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+2\delta)\vee\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\frac{C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}{\delta^{3}}
, it holds

\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu,\nu\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\right\rvert\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\biggr]\,,

(1.12)

and as a consequence the following entropic Talagrand inequality holds

\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu,\nu\right)\leq\Big(1+C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\Big)\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\biggr]\,.

(1.13)

Remark 1.4
.

Equation (
1.11
) implies in particular that
\mu^{T}_{0}\rightharpoonup\mu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V}
and
\mu^{T}_{T}\rightharpoonup\nu\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{V}

.

Theorem 1.5
.

Under the
(H1)
and
(FH3)

\lim_{T\to{\infty}}\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}\left(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\right)=\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)<{\infty}\,.

(1.14)

Moreover as
T\to{\infty}

\bar{\mu}^{T}\rightharpoonup\bar{\mu}\otimes\bar{\nu}\in\Pi\left(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\right)\,,

(1.15)

weakly and, granted
(H2)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
such that for any
0<\delta\leq 1
, as soon as
T>(\frac{1}{\kappa}\log C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+2\delta)\vee\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\frac{C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}{\delta^{3}}

\left\lvert\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}\left(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)\right\rvert\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)\biggr]\,,

\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}\left(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu}\right)\leq\Big(1+C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\Big)\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\mu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\bar{\nu}|\mathfrak{m}\right)\biggr]\,.

(1.17)

The proof of the qualitative statements in the above results rely on
\Gamma

. The key ingredient in the proof of the exponential estimates is a representation formula for the difference

1.6
and
1.7
below.

1.3.
Long-time behaviour of Schrödinger bridges

One of the main contributions of this article are the upcoming quantitative results on the long-time behaviour of Schrödinger bridges, which imply in particular exponential convergence to
\mathfrak{m}
when looking at timescales of order
T
and exponential convergence in
T
to the Langevin dynamics when looking at the Schrödinger bridge over a fixed time-window
[0,t]
.

1.3.1.
Entropic turnpike property

We propose two turnpike results in which distance from equilibrium is measured through the relative entropy
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{m})
and the Fisher information
\mathcal{I}(\cdot)
, see (
1.18
) below. The use of
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{m})
is natural in light of the fact that the costs
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
and
\mathcal{C}^{F}_{T}(\bar{\mu},\bar{\nu})
are also relative entropies, but computed on different spaces. On the other hand, the bound on
\mathcal{I}(\cdot)
is reminiscent of the celebrated Bakry-Émery estimates
[
2
]
. It is worth noticing that entropic turnpike estimates seem to be very rare in the existing literature and even less so are bounds on the Fisher information: we shall elaborate more on this at Remark
1.9
. The key assumption for obtaining (
1.20
) and (
1.23
) is
(H2)
, asking
U
to be strongly convex and such that the difference between the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}U(x)
is controlled by the friction parameter
\gamma
uniformly in

. This assumption is often encountered in works dealing with the long-time behaviour of the (uncontrolled) kinetic Fokker-Planck equation, see e.g.

Proof strategy

A general idea to obtain exponential speed of convergence to equilibrium for hypocoercive equations systematically exploited in
[
50
]
is that of modifying the ”natural” Lyapunov function of the system by adding some extra terms in such a way that proving exponential dissipation becomes an easier task. For the Langevin dynamics, a suitable modification of the natural Lyapunov functional, that is the relative entropy
\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{m})
, is obtained considering

\mu\mapsto a\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m})+\mathcal{I}(\mu)

for a carefully chosen constant
a>0
. Emulating Bakry-Émery
\Gamma
-calculus
[
4
]
it is possible to show that the modified Lyapunov functional decays exponentially along solutions of the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation. Our proof of the turnpike property consists in implementing this abstract idea on the
fg
-decomposition of the entropic interpolation, as we now briefly explain. Indeed, in order to bound
\mathcal{I}(\mu^{T}_{t})
one is naturally led to consider the quantities

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f_{s}^{T}\right\rvert^{2}f_{s}^{T}g_{s}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,,

(1.25a)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla\log g_{s}^{T}\right\rvert^{2}f_{s}^{T}g_{s}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

(1.25b)

However, it is not clear how to obtain a differential inequality ensuring exponential (forward) dissipation of (
1.25a
) and exponential (backward) dissipation of (
1.25b
). But, as we show at Lemma
3.2
, it is possible to find two norms
|\cdot|_{M^{-1}}
and
|\cdot|_{N^{-1}}
, that are equivalent to the Euclidean norm and such that if we define

\varphi^{T}(s)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla\log f_{s}^{T}\right\rvert^{2}_{N^{-1}}f_{s}^{T}g_{s}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\psi^{T}(s)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla\log g_{s}^{T}\right\rvert^{2}_{M^{-1}}f_{s}^{T}g_{s}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,,

(1.26)

then

and

satisfy the desired exponential estimates. To complete the proof, one needs to take care of the boundary conditions. This part is non trivial as it demands to prove certain regularity properties of the
fg
-decomposition and it is accomplished in two steps: we first show at Proposition

a regularising property of entropic interpolations, namely that if

are finite, then the Fisher information

is finite for any

. The proof of this property is based on a gradient bound obtained in
[

and is of independent interest. The second step (Proposition

[

In our setup, due to the lack of a curvature condition, the standard Bakry-Emery machinery does not apply to obtain a commutation estimate for the semigroup of the type

]

For instance, in Theorem 1 of

q^{T}_{n}

\frac{\mathrm{d}q^{T}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}\coloneqq\left(\rho^{T}\wedge n\right)\,\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{K_{n}}}{C_{n}}\,,

(3.21)

where
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{4d}

\mu^{n}\coloneqq(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}q^{T}_{n}

satisfy

and by means of Proposition

it follows that there exists a unique minimizer
\mu^{n,T}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
for
KSP
with marginals
\mu^{n},\,\nu^{n}
, and as soon as
n
diverges it holds

\mu^{n,T}_{t}\rightharpoonup\mu^{T}_{t}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu^{n},\nu^{n}\right)\to\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu,\nu\right)\,.

(3.22)

Then, the thesis in the general case follows from the one under

and the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{I}(\cdot)
and

.
∎

3.3.
Long-time behaviour of the kinetic entropic cost

Throughout the whole section we will always assume
(H1)
and
(H3)
to be true. Let us remark that
(H1)
implies that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
In what follows we are going to prove Theorem
1.3
, but first we need some preparation. The first two claims in Theorem
1.3
will be proved via a
\Gamma
-convergence approach (cf. Proposition
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
) similar to the one used in
[
19
]
for the classical Schrödinger problem. The main difference with
[
19
]
is the lack of compactness for the set
\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)
. On the other hand, the proof of the latter two bounds in Theorem
1.3
will rely on the corrector estimates given in Section
3.2
.

Proposition 3.6

(A
\Gamma
-convergence result)
.

Let
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers converging to
{\infty}
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
consider the functional
\mathcal{H}\left(\cdot\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)
defined on
\Pi_{X}(\mu,\,\nu)
endowed with the weak topology. Then

\Gamma-\lim_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})=\mathcal{H}(\cdot|\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})\,.

Proof.

(
\Gamma
-convergence lower bound inequality) We prove that for any sequence
\left(q_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Pi_{X}\left(\mu,\,\nu\right)
that converges weakly to some
q\in\Pi_{X}(\mu,\,\nu)

\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}\left(q_{n}\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)\geq\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\right)\,.

(3.24)

Note that since
P_{t}
is strongly mixing
[
20
, Theorem 11.14]
for any
\psi,\phi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(x,v)\phi(y,w)\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi\,P_{T_{n}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to{\infty}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\psi(x,v)\phi(y,w)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\,.

From the Portmanteau Theorem, it follows that
\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\rightharpoonup\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}
. Then (
3.24
) follows from the lower semicontinuity of the relative entropy.

(
\Gamma
-convergence upper bound inequality) We prove that for any
q\in\Pi_{X}(\mu,\,\nu)
it holds

\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)\leq\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\right)\,.

(3.25)

We may assume
\mathcal{H}(q|\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})<{\infty}
otherwise the above inequality is trivial. Note that this implies
q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4d})
since
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(cf. (
2.3
)) while

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{proj}_{v_{1}})_{\#}q\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}+2\,\mathcal{W}_{2}((\mathrm{proj}_{v_{1}})_{\#}q,\mathfrak{m}_{V})^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{talagrand}}{\lesssim}1+\,\mathcal{H}((\mathrm{proj}_{v_{1}})_{\#}q|\mathfrak{m}_{V})\leq 1+\mathcal{H}(q|\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})<{\infty}\,,

and similarly for the measure
(\mathrm{proj}_{v_{2}})_{\#}q
.
Then we have

\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)=\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\log p_{T_{n}}\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}q\,,

Thanks to the lower bound given in Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4d})
, we can apply Fatou’s Lemma and get

\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)\leq\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\right)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\log p_{T_{n}}\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}q\,.

(3.26)

Now, for all
t>0
and for all
(x,v),\,(y,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

p_{T_{n}}\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)=P_{T_{n}-t}\left(p_{t}\bigl(\cdot\,,\,(y,w)\bigr)\right)(x,v)\,.

For any
M>0
, we introduce the function
p_{t}^{M}(\cdot\,,\,(y,w))\coloneqq p_{t}(\cdot\,,\,(y,w))\wedge M\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Then, since
P_{T}
is strongly mixing (cf.
[
20
, Theorem 11.14]
), we get

\displaystyle p_{T_{n}}

\displaystyle\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)\geq P_{T_{n}-t}\left(p_{t}^{M}\bigl(\cdot\,,\,(y,w)\bigr)\right)(x,v)\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to{\infty}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}p_{t}^{M}\bigl((x,v),(y,w)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x,v)\,.

Taking the limit as
M\to{\infty}
, by dominated convergence we get that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}p_{t}^{M}\bigl((x,v),(y,w)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x,v)\,{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle M\to{\infty}}}{{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}p_{t}\bigl((x,v),(y,w)\bigr)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}(x,v)=1\,,

where the last equality follows from (
2.6
).
Therefore it holds

\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\log p_{T_{n}}\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)\geq 0\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}-a.s.

which, together with
q\ll\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}
(since
\mathcal{H}\left(q\mid\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m}\right)<{\infty}
), leads to

\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\log p_{T_{n}}\bigl((x,v),\,(y,w)\bigr)\geq 0\,,\hskip 20.00003ptq\text{-a.s.}

Therefore, from (
3.26
) we get inequality (
3.25
). The desired
\Gamma
-convergence follows as a byproduct of (
3.24
) and (
3.25
).
∎

Even though here we are just interested in the
\Gamma
-convergence (as introduced by De Giorgi) on
\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)
equipped with the weak topology, the previous result is actually stronger: indeed we have actually proven the Mosco convergence of the functional
\mathcal{H}\left(\cdot\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}}\right)
since we have considered a constant sequence
q_{n}=q
for the upper bound inequality.

Lemma 3.7

(Equicoerciveness)
.

The family
\left\{\mathcal{H}(\cdot\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})\colon\Pi_{X}(\mu,\nu)\to[0,{\infty}]\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equicoercive, i.e. for any
h\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a (weakly) compact subset
K_{h}\subset\Pi_{X}(\mu,\,\nu)
such that

\bigl\{q\in\Pi_{X}\left(\mu,\,\nu\right)\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{H}(q\mid\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})\leq h\bigr\}\subseteq K_{h}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Proof.

Since
(\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight, a proof of this result is obtained by following the same argument given in
[
24
, Lemma 1.4.3c]
.
∎

The next two results are a consequence of the corrector estimates of Section
3.2
. In the first one we consider the long-time behaviour of the marginals of the solution to
KSP
at times
t=0,T
; in the second one we give a bound for the entropic cost, uniformly in time.

Theorem 3.8
.

Under assumptions
(H1)
,
(H2)
and
(H4)
there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
such that for any
0<\delta\leq 1
and
T>\frac{1}{\kappa}\log C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+2\delta
it holds

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{0}|\mathfrak{m})-\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\right\rvert\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,e^{-2\kappa\,T}\,,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{T}|\mathfrak{m})-\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\right\rvert\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,e^{-2\kappa\,T}\,.

Proof.

We will prove only the first bound since the second one can be proved similarly. Since
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}(\cdot)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{T}_{0}(\cdot,v)\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}(v)
, the log-Sobolev inequality for the Gaussian measure
\mathfrak{m}_{V}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{0}|\mathfrak{m})-

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\biggl[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{T}_{0}\log\rho^{T}_{0}-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{T}_{0}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}\right)\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{T}_{0}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}\biggr]\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\biggl[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\nabla_{v}\sqrt{\rho^{T}_{0}}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{V}\biggr]\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\frac{\nabla_{v}\rho^{T}_{0}}{\rho^{T}_{0}}\right\rvert^{2}\rho^{T}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log(f^{T}P_{T}g^{T})\right\rvert^{2}\rho^{T}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log f^{T}+\nabla_{v}\log P_{T}g^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\rho^{T}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log g_{0}^{T}\right\rvert^{2}\rho^{T}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\lesssim\psi^{T}(0)\overset{\eqref{halfcorrectors}}{\lesssim}\,\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,e^{-2\kappa\,T},

where the equality in the last line follows from the fact that
f^{T}=f^{T}(x)
does not depend on the velocity variable. Finally, since
{\left(\mathrm{proj}_{x}\right)}_{\#}\,\mu^{T}_{0}=\mu
we know that the left hand side term above is positive.
∎

Since it holds
\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})=\mathcal{H}\left((X_{0})_{\#}\mu^{T}|(X_{0})_{\#}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\right)\leq\mathcal{H}\left(\mu^{T}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\right)=\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
, and similarly
\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\leq\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
, the following lower bound is always true

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\geq\frac{\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})}{2}\,.

(3.28)

We now give a corresponding upper bound for sufficiently large times.

Lemma 3.9
.

Under
(H1)
and
(H2)
there exists a constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}>0
such that for any
0<\delta\leq 1
and
T>(\frac{1}{\kappa}\log C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+2\delta)\vee\bigl(\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\frac{C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}{\delta^{3}}\bigr)
it holds

\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\biggr]\,.

(3.29)

Proof.

Firstly, let us assume
(H4)
to hold.
Owing to the bounds
|\nabla_{v}\log g^{T}_{s}|^{2}\lesssim|\nabla\log g^{T}_{s}|^{2}_{M^{-1}}
and
|\nabla_{v}\log f^{T}_{s}|^{2}\lesssim|\nabla\log f^{T}_{s}|^{2}_{N^{-1}}
, from (
3.13
) it follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{0}|\mathfrak{m})+\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{T}|\mathfrak{m})+\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\psi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\varphi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{expmarginals}}{\lesssim}\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+2\,\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,e^{-2\kappa\,T}+\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\psi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\varphi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

We first consider
\int_{T/2}^{T}\varphi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
. For any
s\in[T/2,\,T]
from Corollary
3.5
we have

\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\varphi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}e^{-2\kappa\,s}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,\bigl(e^{-\kappa\,T}-e^{-2\kappa\,T}\bigr)\,.

(3.30)

By reasoning in the same way, this time by using the fact that
s\in[0,T/2]
, we get

\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}\psi^{T}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,\int_{0}^{\frac{T}{2}}e^{-2\kappa\,(T-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,\bigl(e^{-\kappa\,T}-e^{-2\kappa\,T}\bigr)\,.

(3.31)

Therefore there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\biggl[\,\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\delta^{-3}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,\bigl(e^{-\kappa\,T}-e^{-2\kappa\,T}\bigr)\biggr]\,,

which yields our thesis as soon as
T>\frac{1}{\kappa}\log\frac{C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}{\delta^{3}}
for a well chosen
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}>0
.

Now, let us prove the result under
(H2)
. Firstly, notice that we may assume that
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy
(H3)
, otherwise the bound is trivial. The main idea is defining the probability measures
\mu_{n}^{M}
and
\nu_{n}^{M}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, approximating
\mu
and
\nu
, as the measures whose
\mathfrak{m}_{X}
-densities are given by

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{M}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}\coloneqq\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}\wedge n\right)\,\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{K_{n}}}{C^{\mu}_{n}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu^{M}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}\coloneqq\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}}\wedge n\right)\,\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{K_{n}}}{C^{\nu}_{n}}\,,

(3.32)

where
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
C_{n}^{\mu},\,C_{n}^{\nu}
are the normalising constants. Then,
\mu^{M}_{n}
and
\nu_{n}^{M}
satisfy
(H4)
and by means of (
4.2
) it follows

\mathcal{H}(\mu_{n}^{M}|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\overset{n\to{\infty}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{H}(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{H}(\nu_{n}^{M}|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\overset{n\to{\infty}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{H}(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X})\,.

(3.33)

Owing to Lemma
4.4
and (
3.29
) for the approximated

∎

The results given in Theorem
3.8
and Lemma
3.9
will come at hand while proving the exponential convergence in (
1.12

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We start with the proof of (
1.10
) and (

)

(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be any diverging sequence of positive real times. Then, from the optimality of

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T_{n}}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})\leq\limsup_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{H}(\mu^{\infty}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T_{n}})\overset{\eqref{gammalimsupparticolare}}{\leq}\mathcal{H}(\mu^{\infty}|\mathfrak{m}\otimes\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{H}(\mu\mid\mathfrak{m}_{X})+\mathcal{H}(\nu\mid\mathfrak{m}_{X}),

We continue with the proof of (

As a byproduct of the above inequality and Theorem

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu,\nu\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\right\rvert\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\,.

(3.34)

Let us now assume that
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy
(H3)
only.
Firstly, consider the approximating sequence
(\mu^{T}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the optimizer (cf. (
3.21
) and (
3.22
)). Then, by means of (

) under
(H4)
and the lower semicontinuity of the relative entropy we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)-\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu,\nu\right)\overset{\eqref{n:conv:new}}{\leq}\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu^{n}|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu^{n}|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)-\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu^{n},\nu^{n}\right)\biggr]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{exp conv}}{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\delta^{-3}e^{-\kappa\,T}\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{n},\nu^{n})=\delta^{-3}e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\overset{\eqref{eq:Talagrand4}}{\lesssim}\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\biggr]\,.

For the other bound we are going to use the approximation on the marginals (cf. (
3.32
)). Therefore, let us consider
\mu^{M}_{n},\,\nu^{M}_{n}
such that
(H4)
holds. Then, from Lemma
4.4
and the convergence of the relative entropies in (

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{T}

\displaystyle\left(\mu,\nu\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)-\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\leq\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\biggl[\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu^{M}_{n},\nu^{M}_{n})-\,\mathcal{H}(\mu^{M}_{n}|\mathfrak{m}_{X})-\mathcal{H}(\bar{\nu}_{n}^{M}|\mathfrak{m})\biggr]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{exp conv}}{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\mathcal{C}_{T}\left(\mu^{M}_{n},\nu^{M}_{n}\right)\overset{\eqref{eq:Talagrand4}}{\lesssim}\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\liminf_{n\to{\infty}}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu^{M}_{n}|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu^{M}_{n}|\mathfrak{m}_{x}\right)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\,\delta^{-3}\,e^{-\kappa\,T}\,\biggl[\mathcal{H}\left(\mu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)+\mathcal{H}\left(\nu|\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right)\biggr]\,.

3.5.
Convergence over a fixed time-window

In this section we show in Theorem
3.11
that the entropic interpolations for
KSP
and
KFSP
enjoy a turnpike property with respect to the Wasserstein distance.

Notice that a turnpike property in the Wasserstein distance could be deduced from the entropic one (cf. Theorem
1.6
and
1.7
) by means of the Talagrand inequality (
2.2
). However, below we provide a different proof that is of independent interest for two reasons. Firstly, the inequality below holds for any
t\in[0,T]
, while the entropic turnpike is restricted to the sub-interval
[\delta,T-\delta]
. Secondly, the argument in the proof, which uses the optimal control formulation of the Schrödinger problem, will be instrumental for the study of the short-time behaviour of the Schrödinger bridge.

Theorem 3.11

(Wasserstein turnpike)
.

Under hypotheses
(H1)
,
(H2)
and
(H3)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
such that for any
0<\delta\leq 1
, as soon as
T>\frac{1}{\kappa}\log C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}+2\delta
, for any
t\in[0,T]
it holds

\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\,\mathfrak{m})\leq C_{d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\,\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{-\kappa\,[t\wedge(T-t)]}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}\,.

Proof.

Let us firstly assume
(H4)
. We we will prove our result for the distorted Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{M,2}
induced by the metrics
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert_{M}
. Fix
\delta\in(0,1)
and assume
t\in[0,T-\delta]
.
Define
\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{\cdot}
as the marginal flow generated by the uncontrolled process
Z^{\bm{0},T}_{s}\coloneqq(X^{\bm{0},T}_{s},\,V^{\bm{0},T}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
solution of (
1.1
) started at the initial distribution
\mu^{T}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.
Then, since
\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{0}=\mu^{T}_{0}
, it holds

\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\mathfrak{m})\leq\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{t})+\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{t},\mathfrak{m})\overset{\eqref{BEcontW}}{\leq}\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{t})+e^{-\kappa\,t}\,\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{0},\mathfrak{m})\,,

(3.41)

The second term in the right hand side can be handled with the Talagrand inequality:

\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{0},\mathfrak{m})\lesssim\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu^{T}_{0},\mathfrak{m})\overset{\eqref{talagrand}}{\lesssim}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}(\mu^{T}_{0}|\mathfrak{m})}\leq\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}\,,

where the last step holds since
\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)\geq\mathcal{H}\left((\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\mu^{T}|(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\right)
.

Let us now focus on
\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{t})
. We will use a synchronous coupling between these two measures. Therefore we introduce
the process
Z^{\bm{u},T}_{s}\coloneqq(X^{\bm{u},T}_{s},\,V^{\bm{u},T}_{s})\sim\mu^{T}_{s}
, i.e. the solution of (
1.3
) (driven by the same Brownian motion for
Z^{\bm{0},T}_{s}
) when considering the control
{\bm{u}}_{s}=2\gamma\,\nabla_{v}\log g^{T}_{s}(X_{s}^{\bm{u},T},V^{\bm{u},T}_{s})
. Particularly, from (
3.8
) it follows that
\bm{u}
is the optimal control and
Z^{\bm{u},T}_{s}\sim\mu^{T}_{s}
.
For notation’s sake set
Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\coloneqq Z^{\bm{u},T}_{s}-Z^{\bm{0},T}_{s}
. Then it holds

\mathrm{d}Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}=\biggl[b\left(Z^{\bm{u},T}_{s}\right)-b\left(Z^{\bm{0},T}_{s}\right)\biggr]\mathrm{d}s+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\bm{u}_{s}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}s\,,

where
b(z)
denotes the drift of the Langevin dynamics (
1.1
).
By Itô’s Formula we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\lvert Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\right\rvert_{M}^{2}

\displaystyle=2MZ^{\bm{\Delta}}_{s}\cdot\Bigl(b(Z^{\bm{u}}_{s})-b(Z^{\bm{0}}_{s})\Bigr)\mathrm{d}s+2MZ^{\bm{\Delta}}_{s}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\bm{u}_{s}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\cdot MJ_{b}\left(rZ^{\bm{u},T}_{s}+(1-r)Z^{\bm{0},T}_{s}\right)Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s+2MZ^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\bm{u}_{s}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq-2\,\kappa\left\lvert Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\right\rvert_{M}^{2}\mathrm{d}s+2MZ^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\bm{u}_{s}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}s\,,

where the last inequality follows from (
2.9
).
By taking the expectation, and applying Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[\left\lvert Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\right\rvert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq-2\,\kappa\,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[\left\lvert Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\right\rvert_{M}^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[\left\lvert Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\right\rvert_{M}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[\left\lvert(0,\bm{u_{s}})^{T}\right\rvert^{2}_{M}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Therefore it holds

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[{\big|Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\big|}_{M}^{2}\right]}\,\leq-\kappa\,\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[{\big|Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\big|}_{M}^{2}\right]}+\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[\left\lvert(0,\bm{u_{s}})^{T}\right\rvert^{2}_{M}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Recalling that the optimal control is given by
\bm{u}_{s}=2\gamma\,\nabla_{v}\log g^{T}_{s}(X_{s}^{\bm{u},T},V^{\bm{u},T}_{s})
we obtain that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[{\big|Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\big|}_{M}^{2}\right]}\,\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left\lvert\nabla_{v}\log g^{T}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}\rho^{T}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\psi^{T}(s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Therefore, by integrating over
s\in[0,\,t]
we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[{\big|Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{t}\big|}_{M}^{2}\right]}\,=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{R}}\left[{\big|Z^{\bm{\Delta},T}_{s}\big|}_{M}^{2}\right]}\,\mathrm{d}s\overset{\eqref{halfcorrectors}}{\lesssim}\,\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{-\kappa\,(T-t)}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}\,,

and hence it holds

\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\tilde{\mu}^{T}_{t})\lesssim\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{-\kappa\,(T-t)}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}\,.

(3.42)

Then, from (
3.41
) we deduce that for any
t\in[0,T-\delta]
it holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{M,2}(\mu^{T}_{t},\mathfrak{m})\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{-\kappa\,(T-t)}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}+e^{-\kappa\,t}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,e^{-\kappa\,[t\wedge(T-t)]}\,\sqrt{\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)}\,.

By considering the contraction along
P^{\ast}
, the same argument gives us the same bound for
t\in[\delta,T]
and therefore on the whole domain

.

In order to relax the assumption to

4.
From compact support to finite entropy

In this section we discuss two types of approximating sequences that we have used in order to extend our main results from
(H4)
to
(H3)
.

In Section
4.1
we deal with the
Approximation of the optimizer
where we are able to prove the convergence of the entropic cost of the approximated problem to the original entropic cost but not the convergence of the associated entropies of the marginals at time
t=0,T
.
On the other hand in Section
4.2
, we investigate the
Approximation of the marginals
, by approximating directly the marginals and consider the associated Schr̈odinger problems. In this case, we get the convergence of the marginals’ relative entropies, but not the one of the entropic cost.
The two aforementioned strategies produce complementary bounds which can be applied together in order to relax the assumptions from
(H4)
to
(H3)
.

We will deal exclusively with the approximations and proofs for
KSP
and omit those for
KFSP
, since the latter can be treated in the same way.

4.1.
Approximating the optimizer

Fix a couple of marginals
\mu,\,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
(H3)
. We already know that there exists a unique minimizer
\mu^{T}\in\Pi_{X}\left(\mu,\nu\right)
for
KSP
, with
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
-density given by
\rho^{T}
. Now consider an increasing sequence of rectangular compact sets
\left(K_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4d}
whose union gives the whole space. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
define the probability measure
q^{T}_{n}
as the measure whose
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
-density is given by

\hat{\rho}^{T}_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{d}q^{T}_{n}}{\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}}\coloneqq\left(\rho^{T}\wedge n\right)\,\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{K_{n}}}{C_{n}}\,,

where
C_{n}\coloneqq\int_{K_{n}}(\rho^{T}\wedge n)\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
is the normalising constant. Notice that
C_{n}\uparrow 1
by monotone convergence and
\hat{\rho}^{T}_{n}\to\rho^{T}
.
For convenience, we fix in this section some
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
C_{n}\geq 1/2
for any
n\geq\bar{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following properties hold true.

(i)

The marginals
\mu^{n}\coloneqq(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{1}})_{\#}q^{T}_{n}
and
\nu^{n}\coloneqq(\mathrm{proj}_{x_{2}})_{\#}q^{T}_{n}
satisfy
(H4)
.

(ii)

q^{T}_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu^{T}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{H}\left(q^{T}_{n}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\right)\to\mathcal{H}\left(\mu^{T}|\mathrm{R}_{0,T}\right)=\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)
.

Proof.

We start with i).
Since
q^{T}_{n}
has compact support, so do its marginals
\mu^{n},\,\nu^{n}
. Moreover if
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel set, then

\mu^{n}(B)=q^{T}_{n}(B\times\mathbb{R}^{3d})\leq\frac{n}{C_{n}}\,\int_{B\times\mathbb{R}^{3d}}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}=\frac{n}{C_{n}}\,\mathrm{R}_{0,T}(B\times\mathbb{R}^{3d})=\frac{n}{C_{n}}\,\mathfrak{m}_{X}(B)

and therefore
\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\mu^{n}/\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{X}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m}_{X})}\leq\frac{n}{C_{n}}
. The same reasoning applies also to
\nu^{n}
.

The weak convergence in (ii) follows from dominated convergence.

Let us prove point (iii).
Notice that for each
n\geq\bar{n}
it holds

\left\lvert\hat{\rho}^{T}_{n}\,\log\hat{\rho}^{T}_{n}\right\rvert\leq\max\left\{e^{-1},\,(\rho^{T}C_{\bar{n}}^{-1})\,\log(\rho^{T}C_{\bar{n}}^{-1})\right\}\,,

and the above RHS is
\mathrm{R}_{0,T}
-integrable since it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}(\rho^{T}C_{\bar{n}}^{-1})\,\log(\rho^{T}C_{\bar{n}}^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{R}_{0,T}=\,\frac{1}{C_{\bar{n}}}\,\mathcal{C}_{T}(\mu,\nu)+\frac{1}{C_{\bar{n}}}\log\left(\frac{1}{C_{\bar{n}}}\right)<{\infty}\,,

In this section we are going to perform the approximating arguments directly on the fixed marginals. This will not lead to the convergence of the respective kinetic entropic costs, nevertheless it will be useful in proving the bounds where the previous approximating argument fails. The idea is similar to the one performed previously: consider an increasing sequence of compact sets

Abstract
. We construct a large family of positive-definite kernels K : 𝔻 n × 𝔻 n → M ​ ( r , ℂ ) K:\mathbb{D}^{n}\times\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mbox{\rm M}(r,\mathbb{C}) , holomorphic in the first variable and anti-holomorphic in the second, that are quasi-invariant with respect to the subgroup Möb × ⋯ × Möb \mob\times\cdots\times\mob ( n n times) of the bi-holomorphic automorphism group of 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} . The adjoint of the n n - tuples of multiplication operators by the co-ordinate functions on the Hilbert spaces ℋ K \mathcal{H}_{K} determined by K K is then homogeneous with respect to this subgroup. We show that these n n - tuples are irreducible, are in the Cowen-Douglas class B r ​ ( 𝔻 n ) \mathrm{B}_{r}(\mathbb{D}^{n}) and that they are mutually pairwise unitarily inequivalent.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mob
denote the bi-holomorphic automorphism group of the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. A bounded linear operator on a complex separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be homogeneous if the spectrum
\sigma(T)
of
T
is contained in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{D}}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and,
g(T)
is unitarily equivalent to
T
for every
g
in the Möbius group
\mob
. Indeed, since
g\in\mob
is a rational function with pole outside the closed disc
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{D}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, it follows that
g(T)
is well defined whenever the spectrum
\sigma(T)
of
T
is contained in
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{\mathbb{D}}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. In a number of articles
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
12
]
, the class of homogeneous operators has been studied extensively.

The notion of a homogeneous operator has a natural generalization to commuting tuples of operators. Let
\Omega
be a bounded symmetric domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
G
be a subgroup of the bi-holomorphic automorphism group
\aut(\Omega)
of
\Omega
. A commuting
n
- tuple of operators
(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
is said to be homogeneous with respect to
G
if the Taylor joint spectrum of
(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
lies in
\overline{\Omega}
and
g(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
, defined by the holomorphic functional calculus, is unitarily equivalent with
(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
for all
g\in G
. In
[
8
]
, Korányi and Misra described all irreducible homogeneous tuples of operators in the Cowen-Douglas class over the open unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\sum|z_{i}|^{2}<1\}
, see also
[
10
]
. In this generality, the focus has been on the study of commuting tuples of homogeneous operators in the Cowen-Douglas class over an irreducible bounded symmetric domain. In a recent paper, we initiated the study of
n
- tuples of operators homogeneous under the group
\mob^{n}:=\mob\times\cdots\times\mob
. The group
\mob^{n}
is evidently a subgroup of the bi-holomorphic automorphism group of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and acting transitively on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

In the recent paper
[
5
]
, all irreducible
n
- tuples in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
,
r=1,2,3
, homogeneous with respect to the group Möb
n
(respectively,
\Aut({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
) have been listed modulo unitary equivalence. It has been also noted, in the same article, that the representations associated to the
n
- tuples of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
homogeneous with respect to Möb
n
,
r=1,2,3
, must be multiplicity-free.

In general, the classification modulo unitary equivalence of all homogeneous tuples of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
for
r>3
is rather complicated. Recall that if
T_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
are homogeneous operators in
\mathrm{B}_{1}({\mathbb{D}})
and
S
is an irreducible homogeneous operator in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}})
,
r=1
or
2
, then setting
\hat{T}_{i}=I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes T_{i}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I
and
\hat{S}:=I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes S
, we infer that the operators of the form
(\hat{T}_{1},\ldots,\hat{T}_{n-1},\hat{S})
are the only irreducible
n
- tuples of operators (up to unitary equivalence) in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
,
r=1,2
, homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
(cf.
[
5
, Theorem 3.1, Theorem 6.7]
).

Continuing in the same manner, for
r\geq 3
, examples of irreducible
n
- tuples
\boldsymbol{T}\in\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
homogeneous under
\mob^{n}
can be produced by taking tensor product, namely,

( 1.1 )

\boldsymbol{T}=(\hat{T}^{(n_{1})},\ldots,\hat{T}^{(n_{p})}),\,\,\mbox{\rm with}\,\,\hat{T}^{(n_{i})}=I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I,\,\,\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}\in\mathrm{B}_{r_{i}}(\mathbb{D}^{n_{i}})

such that
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=n
,
r_{1}\cdots r_{p}=r
. Here, we use the notation

I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\!:=\!(I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes T_{1n_{i}}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I,\!\cdots,\!I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes T_{n_{i}n_{i}}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I)

where
\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}=(T_{1n_{i}},\ldots,T_{n_{i}n_{i}})\in\mathrm{B}_{r_{i}}(\mathbb{D}^{n_{i}})
.
Now consider the class
\mathcal{T}
of
n
- tuples of operators
\boldsymbol{T}\in\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
obtained as in (
1.1
) and let
\mathcal{T}_{1}
be the subset of
\mathcal{T}
consisting of all operator tuples
\boldsymbol{T}=(\hat{T}^{(n_{1})},\ldots,\hat{T}^{(n_{p})})
with
n_{i}=1
,
1\leq i\leq p
. Then unlike the case when
r=1
or
2
, it was shown in
[
5
, Theorem 7.8]
that there are
n
- tuples
in
\mathrm{B}_{3}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
homogeneous under
\mob^{n}
are not necessarily in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. The specific example for
r=3
given in that paper, however, is in the larger set
\mathcal{T}
. Never the less, we don’t know if
\mathcal{T}
consists of all irreducible
n
- tuples of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
homogeneous under
\mob^{n}
.

It is useful to also give an alternative description of some of these operators as
n
- tuples of multiplication of operators on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. This is the class obtained by setting
n_{i}=1
,
i=1,\ldots,n
and
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{n-1}=1
and
r_{n}=r
. For
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}>0
, let
\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda_{i})}({\mathbb{D}})
be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel
K^{(\lambda_{i})}(z,w)=(1-z\bar{w})^{-\lambda_{i}},\,\,z,w\in\mathbb{D}
and
A^{(\lambda_{n},\mu)}
be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space determined by the positive definite kernel
B^{(\lambda_{n},\mu)}
as in
[
6
, Equation (4.3)]
. Then the adjoint of the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators by the co-ordinate functions on the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda_{1})}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda_{2})}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda_{n-1})}\otimes A^{(\lambda_{n},\mu)}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
, it is irreducible and homogeneous with respect to Möb
n
and are mutually unitarily inequivalent.

Here, we construct a family of irreducible
n
- tuples of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
that is not of the form (
1.1
) if all the
n_{i}
’s are required to be
1
. This, we hope, would be a first step in any attempt to classify homogeneous
n
- tuples in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
,
r>3
. The description of the Hilbert spaces in
[
5
, Section 7]
prompts a natural generalisation of the
\Gamma
map leading to construction of
n
- tuples of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
,
r\geq 3
, in a manner very similar to the one in
[
6
]
.

In Section
3
, for a fixed but arbitrary
r
- tuples of positive real numbers
\mu:=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
, we introduce “the
\Gamma
map” depending
on
\mu
, from
\oplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda_{i}+2\beta_{i})}({\mathbb{D}}))
taking values in Hol
({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
. Here
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
is a fixed but arbitrary
n
- tuple of positive real numbers,
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
such that the cardinality of the set
\Lambda=\{\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
is
r
, where
``\leq"
denotes the graded co-lexicographic ordering on
(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
. Choosing the inner product in the image of
\Gamma
that makes
\Gamma
unitary, we see that the image of
\Gamma
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. The reproducing kernel of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is computed explicitly. We then prove that the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}:=(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
by the co-ordinate functions on
\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is Möb
n
- homogeneous by obtaining the associated multiplier representation of
\mob^{n}
explicitly. We also establish that the adjoint of the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. In Section
4
, we show that the tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
on
\mathcal{H}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is irreducible. In the final section, we prove that
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are unitarily equivalent if and only if
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be the open unit disc in
{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mob
be the set of all bi-holomorphic automorphisms
z\mapsto e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\overline{a}z}
,
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
of
{\mathbb{D}}
. Note that SU
(1,1):=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
\overline{b}&\overline{a}\end{smallmatrix}\right):|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=1\right\}
is a two fold cover of
\mob
. Consequently, the universal covering space
\widetilde{\mob}
of
\mob
is same as that of SU
(1,1)
. Let
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):|z_{1}|,\ldots,|z_{n}|<1\}
and
\mob^{n}:=\mob\times\cdots\times\mob
. It is known that bi-holomorphic automorphisms
\aut({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
of
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
is the semi-direct product
\mob^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, where
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the permutation group on
n
symbols. As we have said before, the group
\mob^{n}
acts transitively on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. In this paper, we study
n
- tuples of multiplication operators homogeneous under the group
\mob^{n}
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
be a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
taking values in
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
possessing a reproducing kernel
K
satisfying the reproducing property: for each
z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
,
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
,

( 2.1 )

\displaystyle\langle f,K(.,z)\xi\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{K}}=\langle f(z),\xi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{r}}.

Note that
K(.,z)\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
for each
z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and as is well-known, the linear span of the set
\{K(.,z)\xi:z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n},\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
. Also, by differentiating both sides of the Equation (
2.1
), we have that

( 2.2 )

\displaystyle\langle f,\bar{\partial}^{l}_{i}K(.,z)\xi\rangle_{{\mathcal{H}}_{K}}=\langle\partial^{l}_{i}f(z),\xi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{r}},~f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{K},~1\leq i\leq n,~l\in\mathbb{N},~\text{and}~\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}.

Recall from
[
3
,
4
]
that the adjoint
\boldsymbol{M}^{*}
of the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}
by the co-ordinate functions on
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
is in the Cowen-Douglas class
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
over
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
if the operator
D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}:{\mathcal{H}}_{K}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{K}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
defined by
D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}f=\left(M_{z_{1}}^{*}f,\ldots,M_{z_{n}}^{*}f\right)
,
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
satisfies the following properties:

•

\dim\ker D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}-zI}=r
,
z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
;

•

\text{ran}D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}-zI}
is closed in
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
;

•

\bigvee_{\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}}\ker D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}-zI}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{K}
.

In this case, there is a hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}:=\{(z,v):v\in\ker D_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}-zI}\}\subset{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
defined over
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
such that the unitary equivalence class of the
n
- tuple
\boldsymbol{M}^{*}
and the (local) equivalence class of the hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}
determine each other. Given two reproducing kernels
K_{1},K_{2}:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\rightarrow\text{M}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
with the property that
\boldsymbol{M}_{i}^{*}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{K_{i}}
,
i=1,2
are in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
, it follows that
M_{1}^{*}
and
M_{2}^{*}
are unitarily equivalent if and only if there exists a holomorphic function
\Phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\rightarrow\text{GL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
such that
K_{1}(z,w)=\Phi(z)K_{2}(z,w)\Phi(w)^{*}
which is same as the fact that the hermitian holomorphic vector bundles
E_{\boldsymbol{M}_{1}^{*}}
and
E_{\boldsymbol{M}_{2}^{*}}
are unitarily equivalent over
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
.

If in addition, the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}=(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
by the co-ordinate functions on
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
is homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
– that is, for each
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n})\in\mob^{n}
,
\phi(\boldsymbol{M})=(M_{\phi_{1}},\ldots,M_{\phi_{n}})
, where
\phi(\boldsymbol{M})
is defined by the usual holomorphic functional calculus, is unitarily equivalent to
\boldsymbol{M}
– then the hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}
is homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
as well. A hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
\pi:E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
is said to be
homogeneous
with respect to a subgroup
G
of the group of bi-holomorphic automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
if for every
\phi\in G
, there is an isometric bundle automorphism
\hat{\phi}:E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}\rightarrow E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}
such that
\phi\circ\pi=\pi\circ\hat{\phi}
. In this paper, the group
G
is fixed once for all to be the subgroup
\mob^{n}
of the group of bi-holomorphic automorphisms of
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
. Moreover, it follows from Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
that the universal covering group
\widetilde{\mob}^{n}
acts on
E_{\boldsymbol{M}^{*}}
uniquely by isometric bundle automorphisms.

Also, we recall from
[
5
, Section 4]
that the homogeneity of
\boldsymbol{M}^{*}\in\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
makes the reproducing kernel
K

quasi-invariant
, that is,

( 2.3 )

\displaystyle K(z,w)=J(\tilde{\phi},z)K(\phi(z),\phi(w))J(\tilde{\phi},w)^{*},~z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n},~\tilde{\phi}\in\widetilde{\mob}^{n},

where
\phi=p(\tilde{\phi})
,
p:\widetilde{\mob}^{n}\rightarrow\mob^{n}
is the universal covering map and for each
\tilde{\phi}\in\widetilde{\mob}^{n}
,
J(\tilde{\phi},.):{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\rightarrow\text{GL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
is a holomorphic mapping. Further, if the function
J
satisfies the “co-cycle” identity

J(\tilde{\phi}\tilde{\psi},z)=J(\tilde{\psi},z)J(\tilde{\phi},\psi(z)),~\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\psi}\in\widetilde{\mob}^{n},~\text{and}~z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n},

we say that
J
is a
co-cycle
.
If
K
is quasi-invariant and J is a co-cycle, then it is easy to verify that the map
U:\widetilde{\mob}^{n}\times{\mathcal{H}}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
defined by

( 2.4 )

\displaystyle(U(\tilde{\phi}^{-1})f)(z):=J(\tilde{\phi},z)f(\phi(z))

is a unitary representation of
\widetilde{\mob}^{n}
onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
.

3.
Construction of Hilbert spaces

In this section, we construct a family of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces consisting of holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
taking values in
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
for a given
r\in\mathbb{N}
. The reproducing kernels associated to these Hilbert spaces are computed explicitly. We then prove that the
n
- tuples of multiplication operators by the co-ordinate functions on these Hilbert spaces are bounded. Finally, the adjoint of these
n
- tuples of multiplication operators are shown to be in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
.

For a given
r\in\mathbb{N}
, fix an element
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
so that the cardinality
|\Lambda|
of the set
\Lambda=\{\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
is
r
. Here we are using the graded co-lexicographic ordering on
(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
.

For each
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
with each
\lambda_{i}>0
, consider the reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda_{i}+2\beta_{i})}({\mathbb{D}})
of holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
with the reproducing kernel

( 3.1 )

\displaystyle K^{(\lambda+2\beta)}(z,w)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-z_{i}\overline{w}_{i})^{-(\lambda_{i}+2\beta_{i})},~z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

Define the mapping
\Gamma_{\beta}:{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}\rightarrow\text{Hol}({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
for
\beta\in\Lambda
as follows

\Gamma_{\beta}(f)=\left({\gamma\choose\beta}\frac{\partial^{\gamma-\beta}f}{(\lambda+2\beta)_{\gamma-\beta}}\right)_{0\leq\gamma\leq\alpha}.

Here,
{\gamma\choose\beta}={\gamma_{1}\choose\beta_{1}}\cdots{\gamma_{n}\choose\beta_{n}}
is the binomial symbol in multi-index and
(\lambda+2\beta)_{\gamma-\beta}
is the Pochhammer symbol in multi-index which by definition is
(\lambda_{1}+2\beta_{1})_{\gamma_{1}-\beta_{1}}\cdots(\lambda_{n}+2\beta_{n})_{\gamma_{n}-\beta_{n}}
.
Note that if
\gamma_{i}<\beta_{i}
for some
i
, then
{\gamma\choose\beta}=0
. It follows from the definition that each
\Gamma_{\beta}
is injective. We define the mapping
\Gamma
on
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
taking values in
\text{Hol}({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
by

( 3.2 )

(f_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\mapsto\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\mu_{\beta}\Gamma_{\beta}(f_{\beta})

where
\mu_{\beta}
with
0\leq\beta\leq\alpha
are all positive real numbers and
\mu_{0}=1
. Note from the definition that
\Gamma
is an injective linear transformation.

Lemma 3.1
.

The map
\Gamma
on
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
taking values in
\text{Hol}({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{r})
defined by the equation (
3.2
) is injective.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda=\{\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
is a total ordered set, we write
\Lambda=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}\}
, where
\beta_{i}<\beta_{j}
if
1\leq i<j\leq r
. Let
f=(f_{\beta_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq r}\in\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
be such that
\Gamma f=0
.

By an induction argument, we now prove that
f_{\beta_{i}}=0
for every
1\leq i\leq r
. Since
\beta_{1}<\beta_{i}
for every
i\geq 2
, it follows from the definition of
\Gamma_{\beta_{i}}
that
\left(\Gamma_{\beta_{i}}f_{\beta_{i}}\right)_{\beta_{1}}=0
for every
i\geq 2
. This implies from the definition of
\Gamma
that
f_{\beta_{1}}=0
.

Now assume that there exists
1\leq j<r
such that
f_{\beta_{k}}=0
for every
1\leq k\leq j
. A similar argument as given in the previous paragraph implies that
f_{\beta_{j+1}}=0
. This proves that
f_{\beta_{i}}=0
for every
1\leq i\leq r
. Therefore the map
\Gamma
is injective.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
denote the image of
\Gamma
. Being injective,
\Gamma
defines an inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
as follows

\left\langle\Gamma((f_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}),\Gamma((g_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha})\right\rangle:=\langle(f_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha},(g_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\rangle

making
\Gamma
a unitary transformation onto
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. We now prove that
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space by first showing that the image Im
(\Gamma_{\beta})
of
\Gamma_{\beta}
is so for each
\beta\in\Lambda
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\beta\in\Lambda
. Then Im
(\Gamma_{\beta})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel

K_{\beta}(z,w)=\left(\!\!\!\left(\frac{{s\choose\beta}{t\choose\beta}}{(\lambda+2\beta)_{s-\beta}(\lambda+2\beta)_{t-\beta}}\partial^{s-\beta}\bar{\partial}^{t-\beta}K^{(\lambda+2\beta)}(z,w)\right)\!\!\!\right)_{0\leq s,t\leq\alpha},~z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}

where
K^{(\lambda+2\beta)}(z,w)
is as in (
3.1
).

Proof.

Let
\{e_{\theta}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}:\theta\in\Lambda\}
be the standard ordered basis for
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. Note that for
f_{\beta}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
,

\displaystyle\langle\Gamma_{\beta}f_{\beta},K_{\beta}(.,w)e_{\theta}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\Gamma_{\beta}f_{\beta},\Gamma_{\beta}\frac{{\theta\choose\beta}}{(\lambda+2\beta)_{\theta-\beta}}\bar{\partial}^{\theta-\beta}K^{(\lambda+2\beta)}(.,w)\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\theta\choose\beta}}{(\lambda+2\beta)_{\theta-\beta}}\partial^{\theta-\beta}f_{\beta}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Gamma_{\beta}f_{\beta}(w),e_{\theta}\rangle

completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel

( 3.3 )

\displaystyle K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)=\sum_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\mu_{\beta}^{2}K_{\beta}(z,w),~z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

At this point, we remark that the Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
differ by their Hilbert space structures since both of them represent the same set as demonstrated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

The Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
are equal as a set.

Proof.

First, we claim that for each

and
0\leq\gamma\leq\alpha
,
\left(\Gamma_{\beta}f\right)_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\gamma)}
. Writing
f=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
with

,
1\leq i\leq n
, we observe that
\partial_{i}^{\gamma_{i}-\beta_{i}}f_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda_{i}+2\gamma_{i})}({\mathbb{D}})
whenever
\gamma_{i}-\beta_{i}\geq 0
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Here, we use the fact that for
0<\mu<\infty
and

,

, where
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\mu)}({\mathbb{D}})
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel
(1-z\bar{w})^{-\mu}
,
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Thus it yields that
\left(\Gamma_{\beta}f\right)_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\gamma)}
verifying our claim since
\{f=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}:f_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda_{i}+2\beta_{i})}({\mathbb{D}}),1\leq i\leq n\}
is a total set in
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
.

Let
g\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
be an arbitrary element. Then there exists
f=(f_{\beta})_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}\in\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
such that
g=\Gamma f
. From the definition of the
\Gamma
and the claim above, it follows that
\Gamma f\in\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
verifying that
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}\subseteq\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
.

For the converse inclusion
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
, we first write
\Lambda=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}\}
, where
\beta_{i}<\beta_{j}
if
1\leq i<j\leq r
(
r
is the cardinality of the set
\Lambda
) since
\Lambda=\{\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
is a total ordered set. From the definition of
\Gamma
, note that
\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta_{r})}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Now we show that
\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+\beta_{r-1})}\oplus\{0\}
is contained in
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Let
f_{\beta_{r-1}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta_{r-1})}
and consider the element
f=(f_{\beta_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq r}
where
f_{\beta_{i}}=0
for

. Then we have that
\Gamma f=\mu_{\beta_{r-1}}\Gamma_{\beta_{r-1}}f_{\beta_{r-1}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Furthermore, it follows from the claim above that
\left(\Gamma_{\beta_{r-1}}f_{\beta_{r-1}}\right)_{\beta_{r}}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta_{r})}

(0,\ldots,0,f_{\beta_{r-1}},0)=\Gamma f-\mu_{\beta_{r-1}}(0,\ldots,0,\left(\Gamma_{\beta_{r-1}}f_{\beta_{r-1}}\right)_{\beta_{r}})\in{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}

since

and
\left(\Gamma_{\beta_{r-1}}f_{\beta_{r-1}}\right)_{\beta_{r-1}}=f_{\beta_{r-1}}

\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta_{r-1})}\oplus\{0\}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Finally, a similar argument with the help of mathematical induction yields that
\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta_{j})}\oplus\{0\}\oplus\cdots\oplus\{0\}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}

which completes the proof.
∎

. From now on, this

- tuple
(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
will be denoted by
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

The adjoint of the
n
-tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
where
r
is the cardinality of the set
\Lambda=\{\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\beta\leq\alpha\}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.4
and a routine generalization of
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
, it follows that the identity map between
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
is invertible. Also, the identity map intertwines the multiplication operators on
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
. Therefore, the adjoint of the tuple of multiplication operators
{\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}}^{*}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is similar to the adjoint of multiplication operators on
\bigoplus_{0\leq\beta\leq\alpha}{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda+2\beta)}
. Now the theorem follows from the fact that the Cowen-Douglas class
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
is invariant under the similarity.
∎

4.
Irreducibility

In this section, we prove that the
n
- tuples of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
are irreducible. The lemma below, modelled after Lemma 5.1 in
[
6
]
, can be proved exactly in the same way as in the original proof, so it is omitted.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}=(M_{z_{1}},\ldots,M_{z_{n}})
by the co-ordinate functions on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with the reproducing kernel
K
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. If there exists an orthogonal projection
X
commuting with
\boldsymbol{M}
then

\Phi_{X}(z)K(z,w)~=~K(z,w)\overline{\Phi_{X}(w)}^{\text{tr}}

for some holomorphic function
\Phi_{X}:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{r\times r}
with
\Phi_{X}^{2}=\Phi_{X}
.

Thus
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is irreducible if and only if there is no non-trivial projection
X_{0}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
satisfying

X_{0}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,0)^{-1}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,w)K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(0,w)^{-1}~=~K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,0)^{-1}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,w)K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(0,w)^{-1}X_{0}

where
K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,w)=K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0,0)^{-\frac{1}{2}}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0,0)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Let

( 4.1 )

\displaystyle\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)=K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,0)^{-1}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(z,w)K^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{0}(0,w)^{-1}

which is called the normalized kernel associated to
K
at origin.

Theorem 4.2
.

The tuple of multiplication operators
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
are irreducible.

Proof.

In view of the discussion above, we show that there is no non-trivial projection commuting with the normalized kernel
\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)
for
z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
associated to the reproducing kernel
K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)
. Recall that
\Lambda=\{\theta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}:\theta\leq\alpha\}
with
|\Lambda|=r
and
\{e_{\theta}:\theta\in\Lambda\}
is the standard ordered basis of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. Denote the set
\{e_{\theta}:\theta\in\Lambda\}
by
\mathcal{E}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\Lambda_{0}^{i}=\{\theta\in\Lambda:\theta_{i}=0\}=\left\{\theta^{(0,i)},\theta^{(1,i)},\ldots,\theta^{(k,i)}\right\}
where
\theta^{(p,i)}\leq\theta^{(q,i)}
if
p\leq q
. Then observe that the set
\mathcal{S}_{(\theta,i)}=\{\tilde{S}_{i}^{j}e_{\theta}:j\geq 0\}
,
\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}
is invariant under
\tilde{S}_{i}
. Since
1\leq i\leq n
is arbitrary but fixed for the rest of the proof, from now on we write
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}
in place of
\mathcal{S}_{(\theta,i)}
. Note that

\Lambda=\bigcup_{\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}}\mathcal{S}_{\theta}.

Let
P
be a permutation on
\mathcal{E}
such that
P
maps
\mathcal{E}
to the ordered basis

P\mathcal{E}=\left\{S_{i}^{j}e_{\theta^{(0,i)}}:j\geq 0\right\}\cup\cdots\cup\left\{S_{i}^{j}e_{\theta^{(k,i)}}:j\geq 0\right\}

with
S_{i}e_{\beta}=e_{\beta+\epsilon_{i}}
where we consider the ordering
S_{i}^{l}e_{\theta^{(p,i)}}<S_{i}^{j}e_{\theta^{(q,i)}}
if
p<q
, or if
l<j
whenever
p=q
.

For
z=(0,\ldots,z_{i},\ldots,0)
and
w=(0,\ldots,w_{i},\ldots,0)
in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, let

A(z_{i},w_{i})=\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)

and observe that

\displaystyle PA(z_{i},w_{i})P^{-1}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}}(1-z_{i}\overline{w}_{i})^{-m(\theta,i)}

\displaystyle\exp(-z_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}^{\ast})B^{-1}_{(\theta,i)}\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})\exp(\bar{w}_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)})\times

\displaystyle B_{(\theta,i)}\exp(z_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}^{\ast})\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})B_{(\theta,i)}^{-1}\exp(-\bar{w}_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}),

where
B_{(\theta,i)}=K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0,0)|_{\mathcal{S}_{\theta}}
,
m(\theta,i)=|\mathcal{S}_{\theta}|-1
,
\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}=\tilde{S}_{i}|_{\mathcal{S}_{\theta}}
and
\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})
is the diagonal operator defined as
\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})S_{i}^{j}e_{\theta}=(1-z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})^{m(\theta,i)-j}e_{\theta+j\epsilon_{i}}
. We denote the expression

\exp(-z_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}^{\ast})B^{-1}_{(\theta,i)}\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})\exp(\bar{w}_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)})B_{(\theta,i)}\exp(z_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)}^{\ast})\tilde{D}_{\theta}(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})B_{(\theta,i)}^{-1}\exp(-\bar{w}_{i}\tilde{S}_{(\theta,i)})

by
A_{\theta}(z_{i},w_{i})
and note that

\displaystyle(1-z_{i}\overline{w}_{i})^{-m(\theta,i)}A_{\theta}(z_{i},w_{i})

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{(\theta,i)}(n)(z_{i}\bar{w}_{i})^{n}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{p\geq 0}b_{(\theta,i)}(p)z_{i}^{p+1}\bar{w}_{i}^{p}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{q\geq 0}c_{(\theta,i)}(q)z_{i}^{q+1}\bar{w}_{i}^{q}+\cdots

where
c_{(\theta,i)}(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{q}a_{(\theta,i)}(l)b_{(\theta,i)}(q-l).
It follows from
[
6
, Lemma 5.4]
that each
b_{(\theta,i)}(p),\,\,p\geq 0,
is a shift on
{\mathbb{C}}^{|\mathcal{S}_{\theta}|}
which in turn implies that each
c_{(\theta.i)}(q),\,\,q\geq 0,
is indeed a shift since each
a_{(\theta,i)}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

For
0\leq l\leq m(\theta,i)
, let
\mathcal{P}_{l}=\left\{p\geq 0:b_{(\theta,i)}(p)_{l,l+1}\neq 0\right\}
which can be seen from
[
6
, Lemma 5.4]
to be a non-empty set. Let
n_{l}=\mbox{min}\,\,\mathcal{P}_{l}
. We then have that

\left(c_{(\theta,i)}(n_{l})\right)_{l,l+1}=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{n_{l}}a_{(\theta,i)}(n)b_{(\theta,i)}(n_{l}-n)\right)_{l,l+1}\neq 0.

Thus for each
1\leq i\leq n,

\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}
and
0\leq l\leq m(\theta,i)
, there exists
r
co-efficient matrices
\displaystyle\oplus_{\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}}c_{(\theta,i)}(n_{l})
of
PA(z_{i},w_{i})P^{-1}
whose
(l,l+1)
-th entry in the
\theta
’th block is non-zero.

Let
X
be a projection commuting with
\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Then note that
Y:=PXP^{-1}
is also a projection commuting with
P\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}P^{-1}
since
P
is a permutation matrix on the orthonormal basis
\mathcal{E}
of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. In particular,
Y
commutes with
\displaystyle\oplus_{\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}}c_{(\theta,i)}(n_{l})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
\theta\in\Lambda_{0}^{i}
and
0\leq l\leq m(\theta,i)
. Thus
Y
must be the identity operator verifying that so is
X
.
∎

5.
Inequivalence

The objective of this section is to show that the operators in the family
\{\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0},\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{>0},\mu_{0}=1\}
are mutually inequivalent. Recall from Theorem
3.12
that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{r}_{>0}
, the adjoint of the
n
- tuple
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. Consequently,
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
gives rise to a hermitian holomorphic vector bundle
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
over
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
with the hermitian structure induced from the reproducing kernel
K^{(\lambda,\mu)}
as is well known (cf.
[
3
]
). Then for each
1\leq i,j\leq n
, the
ij
-th component of the curvature of the bundle
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is defined by the formula

\mathcal{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z)_{ij}=\bar{\partial}_{j}(K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)^{-1}\partial_{i}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)),~z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are unitarily equivalent then
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are unitarily equivalent, it follows (cf.
[
3
]
) that
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
E^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are isomorphic as hermitian holomorphic vector bundles. Consequently, the curvature components of
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
E^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are similar. Note that the metric of the bundle
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
(resp.
E^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
) is
K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)
(resp.
K^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}(z,z)
),
z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
.

A direct computation shows that

( 5.1 )

\mathcal{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{11}(0)=\bar{\partial}_{1}\!\left(\!K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)\partial_{1}K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)\!\right)\!|_{z=0}=\lambda_{1}I+2D_{1}-\tilde{S}_{1}^{*}B^{-1}\tilde{S}_{1}B+B^{-1}\tilde{S}_{1}B\tilde{S}_{1}^{*},

where
B=K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0,0)
and
D_{1}:=\bar{\partial}_{1}\partial_{1}D(|z|^{2})|_{z=0}
. Evaluating
\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0)
on
e_{\theta}
,
0\leq\theta\leq\alpha
, we obtain

\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0)e_{\theta}=\left(\lambda_{1}+2\theta_{1}-\frac{(\theta_{1}+1)^{2}b_{\theta}}{b_{\theta+\epsilon_{1}}}+\frac{\theta_{1}^{2}b_{\theta-\epsilon_{1}}}{b_{\theta}}\right)e_{\theta},

where
b_{\theta}
is the
\theta
’th diagonal entry of
B
. Thus we have

\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0)=r\lambda_{1}+2\displaystyle\sum_{\theta\in\Lambda}\theta_{1}.

Similarly, we have

\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}(0)=r\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+2\displaystyle\sum_{\theta\in\Lambda}\theta_{1}.

Since
\mathcal{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{11}(0)
and
\mathcal{K}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}_{11}(0)
are similar, equating
\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0)
and
\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mathcal{K}_{11}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}(0)
, we obtain
\lambda_{1}=\lambda^{\prime}_{1}
. A similar computation gives us
\lambda_{i}=\lambda^{\prime}_{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq n
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

If
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}
are unitarily equivalent then
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.1
, it is enough to show that
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
whenever
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}
are unitarily equivalent. Since by Theorem
3.12
both
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}
are in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
and they are unitarily equivalent, it follows that the hermitian holomorphic vector bundles
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
E^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}
are isomorphic. Consequently, there exists a holomorphic function
\Phi
on
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
taking values in
\text{GL}(r,{\mathbb{C}})
such that

( 5.2 )

\displaystyle K^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}(z,w)=\Phi(z)K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,w)\Phi(w)^{*},~\text{for all}~z,w\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

Further, since both
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}
are homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
, and
\widetilde{\mob}^{n}
action on
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is unique (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
), it follows that the coycles
J
and
J^{\prime}
associated to
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
E^{(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})}
, respectively, satisfy

( 5.3 )

\displaystyle J(\tilde{g},z)=\Phi(g(z))^{-1}J^{\prime}(\tilde{g},z)\Phi(z),~\tilde{g}\in\widetilde{\mob}^{n},z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

Indeed, note that the expression
\Phi(g(\cdot))^{-1}J^{\prime}(\tilde{g},\cdot)\Phi(\cdot)
gives rise to an action of
\widetilde{\mob}^{n}
on
E^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. In particular, for
g=(g_{z_{1}},\ldots,g_{z_{n}})\in\mob^{n}
with
g_{z_{i}}(w_{i})=\frac{w_{i}-z_{i}}{1-\overline{z}_{i}w_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have from the Equation (
3.12
) that

(1-|z|^{2})^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}D(|z|^{2})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right)=(1-|z|^{2})^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}}\Phi(0)^{-1}D(|z|^{2})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right)\Phi(z),

or equivalently,

\Phi(z)=\exp\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right)D(|z|^{2})^{-1}\Phi(0)D(|z|^{2})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right).

Note from this expression of
\Phi(z)
that
\partial^{p_{1}}_{1}\cdots\partial^{p_{n}}_{n}\Phi(z)|_{z=0}=0
for all
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
with at least one
p_{j}\neq 0
. Consequently, it follows that

\Phi(z)=\Phi(0),~z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}

implying that

( 5.4 )

\displaystyle D(|z|^{2})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right)\Phi(0)=\Phi(0)D(|z|^{2})\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{z}_{i}\tilde{S}_{i}\right).

Now equating the coefficients of
|z_{i}|^{2}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, in the Taylor expansion of both sides of this equation we have that

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{i}\bar{\partial}z_{i}}D(|z|^{2})|_{z=0}\Phi(0)=\Phi(0)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{i}\bar{\partial}z_{i}}D(|z|^{2})|_{z=0},~i=1,\ldots,n.

Since
D(|z|^{2})
is the diagonal operator
D(|z|^{2})e_{\theta}=(1-|z|^{2})^{-\theta}e_{\theta},~0\leq\theta\leq\alpha
, the equation above implies that
\Phi(0)
commutes with the diagonal matrices
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
where for
i=1,\ldots,n
,
D_{i}
is the diagonal matrix whose
\beta
-th diagonal entry is
\beta_{i}
for any
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in\Lambda
. Consequently,
\Phi(0)
also commutes with all the linear combinations of the matrices
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
. Note that there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that the diagonal matrix
c_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}D_{n}
has distinct diagonal entries. Thus
\Phi(0)
is a diagonal matrix. Moreover, by comparing the
\overline{z}_{i}
-th coefficients
\tilde{S}_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, in the Taylor expansion of both sides of the equation (
5.4
), it is seen that
\Phi(0)
commutes with
\tilde{S}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore, it follows from the definition of
\tilde{S}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, there exists a non-zero
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\Phi(0)=cI
where
I
is the
r\times r
identity matrix.

Finally, since
\Phi(0)
satisfies the equation (
5.2
), we have that
K^{\lambda,\mu}(0,0)=K^{(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})}(0,0)
. Therefore, the equality
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
holds from the equation (
3.14
) completing the proof.
∎

We recall the construction of a natural class of irreducible
n
- tuple of operators in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
that are homogeneous with respect to
\mob^{n}
from the Introduction, namely,

( 5.5 )

\boldsymbol{T}=(\hat{T}^{(n_{1})},\ldots,\hat{T}^{(n_{p})}),\,\,\mbox{\rm with}\,\,\hat{T}^{(n_{i})}=I\otimes\cdots\otimes I\otimes\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I,\,\,\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}\in\mathrm{B}_{r_{i}}(\mathbb{D}^{n_{i}})

with
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=n
,
r_{1}\cdots r_{p}=r
and
\boldsymbol{T}^{(n_{i})}=(T_{1n_{i}},\ldots,T_{n_{i}n_{i}})\in\mathrm{B}_{r_{i}}(\mathbb{D}^{n_{i}})
. However, as promised in the Introduction, we now show that the
n
- tuples
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
are not of the form (
5.5
) if we insist that
n_{i}=1,\,1\leq i\leq n
. To establish this, we first prove a lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
\alpha\neq\epsilon_{1}
. Then there exists
1\leq i<j\leq n
and an element
\theta
in
\Lambda
such that
\theta-\epsilon_{i}+\epsilon_{j}
is in
\Lambda
, but
\theta+\epsilon_{j}
is not in
\Lambda
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two cases. In the first case, we prove the existence of such
\theta,i,j
by assuming
\alpha=(|\alpha|,0,\ldots,0)
. In the second case, we assume that
\alpha\neq(|\alpha|,0,\ldots,0)
and prove that such
\theta,i,j
exist.

Case 1:

Suppose
\alpha=(|\alpha|,0,\ldots,0)
. Since
\alpha\neq\epsilon_{1}
, it follows that
|\alpha|>1
. Consider
\theta=\alpha-\epsilon_{1}
,
i=1
and any
j>1
. Then we have
\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{j}\in\Lambda
, but
\theta+\epsilon_{j}\notin\Lambda
.

Case 2:

Assume that
\alpha\neq(|\alpha|,0,\ldots,0)
. There exists
k>1
such that
\alpha_{k}\geq 1
. Consider
\theta=(|\alpha|,0,\ldots,0)
,
i=1
and
j=k
. Since
|\theta+\epsilon_{j}|=|\alpha|+1
, it follows that
\theta+\epsilon_{j}\notin\Lambda
.

We claim that
\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k}\in\Lambda
. On the contrary, assume
\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k}>\alpha
. Note that
|\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k}|=|\alpha|.
Since
\alpha_{k}\geq 1
, we have
0\leq(\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k})_{l}\leq\alpha_{l}
for every
l\geq 2
and therefore, it follows from our assumption that
(\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k})_{l}=\alpha_{l}
for all
l\geq 2
. Also, the inequality
\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k}>\alpha
implies that
\alpha_{1}<(\theta-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{k})_{1}=|\alpha|-1
. Thus

|\alpha|=\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}\alpha_{l}=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{k}<|\alpha|-1+1=|\alpha|,

which is a contradiction. This proves the claim.

The verification of these two cases completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, assume that adjoint of the multiplication operator on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{i}
with the reproducing kernel
K_{i}
is a homogeneous operator in
\mathrm{B}_{r_{i}}({\mathbb{D}})
and denote
M
to be the
n
- tuple of multiplication operators on
\displaystyle\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. If the adjoint of
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
with
r>2
then
M
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
are unitarily inequivalent.

Proof.

Since
M^{*}
is in
\mathrm{B}_{\prod_{i}r_{i}}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
, it follows that if
\prod_{i}r_{i}\neq r
, then
M
and
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
are not unitarily equivalent. Therefore we assume that
\prod_{i}r_{i}=r
.
Note that the adjoint of both the tuples
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
M
are in
\mathrm{B}_{r}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. Thus if
\boldsymbol{M}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
M
are unitarily equivalent, then the corresponding curvature components of
\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\hat{K}
are similar, where
\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
\hat{K}
are normalized kernels of
K^{(\lambda,\mu)}
and
K
at origin, respectively (cf. (
4.1
)).

A direct computation shows that

( 5.6 )

\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{ij}(0)=\bar{\partial}_{j}\partial_{i}\hat{K}^{(\lambda,\mu)}(z,z)|_{z=0}=B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{S}_{j}B\tilde{S}_{i}^{*}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}-B^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{S}_{i}^{*}B^{-1}\tilde{S}_{j}B^{\frac{1}{2}},

for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
, where
B=K^{(\lambda,\mu)}(0,0)
and
\tilde{S}_{i}
is as in the equation (
3.11
). By Lemma
5.3
, there exists a
\theta
in
\Lambda
and
1\leq p\neq q\leq n
such that
\theta-\epsilon_{p}+\epsilon_{q}\in\Lambda
, but
\theta+\epsilon_{q}\notin\Lambda
. This implies that
B^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{S}_{q}B\tilde{S}_{p}^{*}B^{-\frac{1}{2}}e_{\theta}\neq 0
, but
B^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{S}_{p}^{*}B^{-1}\tilde{S}_{q}B^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{\theta}=0
. Therefore, we have
\hat{\mathcal{K}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{pq}(0)\neq 0
.

On the other hand, since
\hat{K}=\displaystyle\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\hat{K}_{i}
, it follows that
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{ij}(0)=\bar{\partial}_{j}\partial_{i}\hat{K}(z,z)|_{z=0}=0
, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. This implies that
\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{pq}(0)

Stable hypersurfaces with constant higher order mean curvature
Leonardo Damasceno, Maria Fernanda Elbert

Abstract
We propose a notion of stability for capillary hypersurfaces with constant higher order mean curvature and we generalize some results of the classical stability theory for CMC capillary hypersurfaces.

Introduction

Let
\displaystyle M^{n+1}
be an oriented Riemannian manifold and
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma^{n}\rightarrow M
be an oriented hypersurface. The higher order mean curvature of order
\displaystyle k
of the hypersurface,
\displaystyle H_{k}
, is defined as the normalized
\displaystyle k
-th symmetric function of the principal curvatures of
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma^{n}\rightarrow M
. We recall that
\displaystyle H=H_{1}
is the mean curvature. Consider a closed domain with smooth boundary
\displaystyle\Omega\subset M
.
In analogy with the constant mean curvature case (CMC), we say that a compact
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurface in
\displaystyle\Omega
is capillary if it is a
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurface with non-empty boundary, whose boundary meets the
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
at a constant angle. In the particular case where the angle is
\displaystyle\pi/2
, the capillary
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurface is called free boundary. In this paper, we are interested in stability questions involving capillary
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\Omega
.

The stability of the minimal or CMC hypersurfaces has been drawing the attention of many mathematicians over the years. Stable hypersurfaces in this case are those for which the second variation of the area is non-negative for a suitable class of variational problems. The existence or not of a boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Sigma
on
\displaystyle\Sigma
and the specific constraints on the variations determine the kind of stability problem we deal with. The stability results for
\displaystyle H_{1}
-hypersurfaces in the literature covers from that applied to closed (without boundary) hypersurfaces to that applied to capillary hypersurfaces, which includes the free boundary hypersurfaces as a particular case (see
[
5
,
6
,
20
,
23
,
28
]
).

We recall that a closed
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurface,
\displaystyle k>1
, of a space form is a critical point for a modified area functional for volume preserving variations and it inherits the concept of stability from this variational problem (see
[
4
]
). We point out that a similar variational characterization of
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces in a general Riemannian manifold is not known. Despite this, in
[
11
]
, the second author and B. Nelli generalized the notion of stability for closed (or fixed boundary)
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces of a general Riemannian manifold for
\displaystyle k>1
.

Inspired by the approach of Alaee, Lesourd and Yau in
[
2
]
when dealing capillary marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS), we propose a stability theory for capillary
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces,
\displaystyle k>1
, that generalizes the capillary CMC (
\displaystyle k=1
) stability theory (see
[
20
,
21
,
23
]
). We also notice that when the hypersurface has empty or fixed boundary, we recover the stability theory proposed in
[
11
]
, and a fortiori, the classical definition of stability for a CMC hypersurface with empty or fixed.

It is worthwhile to say that for
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces,
\displaystyle k>1
, we give two different notions of stability, the
\displaystyle k
-stability and the symmetric
\displaystyle k
-stability, and that for (simply connected) space forms these two notions coincide.

-hypersurfaces of space forms and we prove:

In Section

It is natural to ask if it is possible to generalize other stability results, established for
\displaystyle H_{1}
-hypersurfaces, for
\displaystyle H_{k}
-hypersurfaces. We quote, in particular, some results for capillary

-hypersurfaces supported on special hypersurfaces
\displaystyle\partial\Omega

a geodesic sphere

\displaystyle\displaystyle\emph{II}\left(X,Y\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left<A(X),Y\right>

\displaystyle\displaystyle\emph{II}_{\eta}\left(X,Y\right)=\left<-\overline{\nabla}_{X}\eta,Y\right>,

Let

\sigma_{r}(x_{1},...,x_{n})=\begin{cases}1,&\quad$\displaystyle r=0$\\
\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{r}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}...x_{i_{r}},&\quad$\displaystyle r\in\{1,...,n\}$\\
0,&\quad$\displaystyle r>n.$\end{cases}.

The normalized mean curvature of order
\displaystyle r

\displaystyle H_{r}

\displaystyle r

The functions
\displaystyle S_{r}
can also be deduced from the equation

\det\left(tI-A\right)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\left(-1\right)^{r}S_{r}t^{n-r}.

(1)

\displaystyle\Sigma

-hypersurface

The Newton transformations

-mean curvatures
\displaystyle H_{r}

P_{r}=\begin{cases}I,&\quad$\displaystyle r=0$\\
S_{r}I-AP_{r-1},&\quad$\displaystyle r\geq 1$\end{cases}.

Since

\displaystyle P_{n}=0

be the symmetric elementary polynomial of order

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
\displaystyle r\in\{1,...,n-1\}

(i)

\displaystyle P_{r}e_{i}=S_{r}(A_{i})e_{i}

(ii)

\displaystyle\tr\,(P_{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{r}(A_{i})=(n-r)S_{r}

(iii)

\displaystyle\tr\,(P_{r}A)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}S_{r}(A_{i})=(r+1)S_{r+1}

(iv)

\displaystyle\tr\,(P_{r}A^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}^{2}S_{r}(A_{i})=S_{1}S_{r+1}-(r+2)S_{r+2}.

The next Lemma provide some useful inequalities involving the functions

\displaystyle H_{r-1}H_{r+1}\leq H_{r}^{2}

Stability of capillary
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces

The notion of stability for
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces of a Riemannian manifold with empty (or fixed) boundary is well stated in the literature. The case
\displaystyle r=0
(mean curvature) was established more than 40 years ago (see
[
5
,
6
]

[
4
]
addressed the empty (or fixed) boundary case for
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces of space forms and
\displaystyle r>0

[
11

, the second author and B. Nelli generalized this notion of stability for
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces of a general Riemannian manifold for
\displaystyle r>0

For the capillary case the picture is more complex, since stability is settled only for mean curvature hypersurfaces (
\displaystyle r=0
) of space forms (see
[
21
,
23
]
). The natural generalization of the variational problem for more general ambient spaces and
\displaystyle r>0
has not made progress since the late 1990s. Inspired by the approach of
[
2
]
we propose a notion of stability for capillary
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces that generalizes the works
[
21
]
and
[
23
]
in these two directions: we deal with
\displaystyle r>0
and with a more general ambient space.

A variation of
\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\Phi:\Sigma\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow M

\displaystyle t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=\Phi|_{\Sigma\times\{t\}}

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}=\varphi

\displaystyle(\Sigma,\varphi_{t}^{*}g)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}

\displaystyle\Phi
in
\displaystyle\varphi_{t}

\xi_{t}(p)=\left.\Phi_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right|_{(p,t)}\in\Gamma\left(TM|_{\varphi_{t}(\Sigma)}\right).

If

is the unit normal vector field of
\displaystyle\varphi_{t}

\displaystyle\Phi
at
\displaystyle t

f_{t}=\left<\xi_{t},\eta_{t}\right>\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma).

Since
\displaystyle\varphi_{t}:\Sigma\rightarrow M

\displaystyle\emph{II}_{t}

\displaystyle\left(\emph{II}_{t}\right)_{\eta_{t}}

\displaystyle A_{t}
.
Also, we set
\displaystyle\overline{R}_{\eta_{t}}\left(X\right):=\overline{R}\left(\eta_{t},X\right)\eta_{t}

\displaystyle\overline{R}

\overline{R}(X,Y)Z=\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\overline{\nabla}_{X}Z-\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\nabla}_{Y}Z+\overline{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}Z,\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM).

\displaystyle\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\re\lambda\geq\lambda_{p}
. Also, the associated eigenspace has dimension equal to one and the associated eigenfunction is (strictly) positive.

The proposition below gives useful characterizations of
\displaystyle r
-stability.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Omega\subseteq M
be a positive definite capillary
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurface supported on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega

(i)

\displaystyle\Sigma
is strongly
\displaystyle r
-stable.

(ii)

The principal eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{p}(T_{r})
of (
7
) is non-negative.

(iii)

There exists a positive function
\displaystyle f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f_{0}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Sigma
and
\displaystyle T_{r}f_{0}\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

Proof.

Assume (i) true and let
\displaystyle f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a (strictly) positive eigenfunction of (
7

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}
. Then

\lambda_{p}(T_{r})\int_{\Sigma}f_{0}^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}f_{0}T_{r}f_{0}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|f_{0}\left(\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f_{0}\right)\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}\geq 0,

which proves (ii).

Now assume (ii) is true and let
\displaystyle f_{0}>0
denotes an eigenfunction associated to the principal eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{p}
of (
7

\displaystyle T_{r}f_{0}=\lambda_{p}f_{0}\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\Sigma
and this proves (iii).

Finally assume (iii) is true and let
\displaystyle f_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a positive function such that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f_{0}=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Sigma
and
\displaystyle T_{r}f_{0}=\lambda_{p}f_{0}\geq 0
in
\displaystyle\Sigma
. Now, take
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and set
\displaystyle\tilde{f}=\frac{f}{f_{0}}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. For
\displaystyle f=\tilde{f}f_{0}
we take an admissible normal variation of
\displaystyle\varphi
with variation vector field
\displaystyle f\eta
. Then by using (
6

2.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{F}_{r,\theta}\left[\Sigma_{t}\right]\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}f\left(L_{r}f+q_{r}f\right)\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|f\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f\right)\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{f}^{2}f_{0}\left(L_{r}f_{0}+q_{r}f_{0}\right)+2\tilde{f}f_{0}\left<P_{r}\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla f_{0}\right>+\tilde{f}f_{0}^{2}L_{r}\tilde{f}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|\tilde{f}f_{0}\left(\tilde{f}\left(\frac{\partial f_{0}}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f_{0}\right)+f_{0}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\nu}\right)\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\tilde{f}f_{0}^{2}L_{r}\tilde{f}+2\tilde{f}f_{0}\left<P_{r}\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla f_{0}\right>\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left<\tilde{f}f_{0}^{2}\nabla\tilde{f},P_{r}\nu\right>\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\dive\left(\tilde{f}f_{0}^{2}P_{r}\nabla\tilde{f}\right)-\tilde{f}f_{0}^{2}L_{r}\tilde{f}-2\tilde{f}f_{0}\left<P_{r}\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla f_{0}\right>\,d\mu_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}f_{0}^{2}\left<P_{r}\nabla\tilde{f},\nabla\tilde{f}\right>\,d\mu_{\Sigma}\geq 0.\qed

4
Stability of capillary
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces in space forms

In this section we specialize to stability of
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces immersed on space forms and furnish a particular view of the theory for this setting.
In this paper,
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
denote the simply connected space form of constant sectional curvature
\displaystyle c
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
is equal to
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
if
\displaystyle c=0
,
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n+1}(c)
if
\displaystyle c>0
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n+1}(c)
if
\displaystyle c=0
.

As we mentioned before, in
[
22
, Theorem 4.1]
, H. Rosenberg proved that
\displaystyle P_{r}
is divergence free for hypersurfaces on space forms, i.e.,

\dive P_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}P_{r})e_{i}=0,

where
\displaystyle\left\{e_{i}\right\}
is an arbitrary local frame in
\displaystyle\Sigma
. Thus, the Stokes theorem and Lemma
2.4
imply that

-\int_{\Sigma}fL_{r}f\,d\mu_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\left<P_{r}\nabla f,\nabla f\right>\,d\mu_{\Sigma}-\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|f\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

(8)

for all
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Together with the identity

\overline{R}(X,Y)Z=c\left(\left<X,Z\right>Y-\left<Y,Z\right>X\right),\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(T\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)\right),

we have that
\displaystyle\overline{R}_{\eta}(X)=cX
and
\displaystyle\tr\left(P_{r}\overline{R}_{\eta}\right)=c\tr P_{r}=c(n-r)S_{r}
. Hence, when the ambient space is a space form, (
6
) becomes

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{F}_{r,\theta}\left[\Sigma_{t}\right]\right|_{t=0}=\int_{\Sigma}\left<P_{r}\nabla f,\nabla f\right>-\left(S_{1}S_{r+1}-(r+2)S_{r+2}\right)f^{2}-c\left(n-r\right)S_{r}f^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\\
+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|\alpha_{\theta}f^{2}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

(9)

Given a function
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, the
\displaystyle H^{1}
-norm of
\displaystyle f
is defined by

\left\|{f}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}^{2}=\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}=\int_{\Sigma}f^{2}+\left|\nabla f\right|^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}

and the Sobolev space
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Sigma)
is defined as the closure of
\displaystyle C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
with respect to the norm
\displaystyle\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma)}
. This set endowed with this norm is a Hilbert space, thus one can view (
9
) as a quadratic form associated to a bilinear symmetric form on
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
be a positive definite
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurface. The
\displaystyle r
-index form
of
\displaystyle\varphi
is defined by

\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f_{1},f_{2})=\int_{\Sigma}\left<P_{r}\nabla f_{1},\nabla f_{2}\right>-\tr\left(P_{r}\left(A^{2}+\overline{R}_{\eta}\right)\right)f_{1}f_{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}|{P_{r}\nu}|\alpha_{\theta}f_{1}f_{2}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

(10)

where
\displaystyle f_{1},f_{2}\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
, or equivalently,

\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f_{1},f_{2})=\int_{\Sigma}\left<P_{r}\nabla f_{1},\nabla f_{2}\right>-\left(S_{1}S_{r+1}-(r+2)S_{r+2}+c\left(n-r\right)S_{r}\right)f_{1}f_{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\\
+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|\alpha_{\theta}f_{1}f_{2}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma},

(11)

From the above definition, one can notice that
\displaystyle\Sigma
is strongly
\displaystyle r
-stable if and only if
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,f)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle f\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
and that
\displaystyle\Sigma
is
\displaystyle r
-stable if
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,f)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{F}=\left\{f\in H^{1}(\Sigma)\,|\,\int_{\Sigma}f\,d\mu_{\Sigma}=0\right\}
.

As in the case
\displaystyle r=0
, when considering
\displaystyle\varphi
a capillary
\displaystyle(r+1)
-minimal hypersurface, i.e.
\displaystyle H_{r+1}=0

\displaystyle\varphi
is stable if
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,f)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma)

It is known that if
\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle r
-stable capillary
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurfaces of
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)

\displaystyle\varphi
is
\displaystyle r
-stable.

The
\displaystyle 0
-stability of a totally umbilical hypersurface supported on a horosphere is discussed in
[

.

A
geodesic cap

\displaystyle\Sigma
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
is a geodesic ball of a geodesic sphere of
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)

\displaystyle r
-stable.

Corollary 4.3

Capillary geodesic caps supported on a totally umbilical hypersurface of
\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
are
\displaystyle r
-stable.

A normal vector field
\displaystyle\xi=f\eta
, with
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{F}
, is a
Jacobi field
if
\displaystyle f\in\ker\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}|_{\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)=0
for every
\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{F}
. The next lemma gives a characterization of Jacobi fields on
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{M}^{n+1}(c)
be a positive definite
\displaystyle H_{r+1}
-hypersurface with free boundary in
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
and
\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{F}

(i)

\displaystyle\xi=f\eta
is a Jacobi field on
\displaystyle\Sigma
if and only if
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and

\begin{cases}T_{r}f=-L_{r}f-\tr\left(A^{2}\left(P_{r}+\overline{R}_{\eta}\right)\right)f=\text{constant}&in $\displaystyle\Sigma$\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f=0&on $\displaystyle\partial\Sigma$\end{cases}.

(12)

(ii)

If
\displaystyle\varphi
is
\displaystyle r
-stable and
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,f)=0

\displaystyle f
is a Jacobi field on
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

Proof.

(i)

First suppose that
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
satisfies (
12
) and let
\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{F}
. If
\displaystyle\left(g_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of smooth functions on
\displaystyle\Sigma
such that
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g_{m}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}=0
for all
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle g_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}g
in the
\displaystyle H^{1}(\Sigma)
-sense, it follows from (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\left(L_{r}f+\tr_{\Sigma}\left(P_{r}\left(A^{2}+\overline{R}_{\eta}\right)\right)f\right)g_{m}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f\right)g_{m}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g_{m})=0,

proving that
\displaystyle f\eta
is a Jacobi field on
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

To prove the converse, we will first claim that

\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)=b\int_{\Sigma}g\,d\mu_{\Sigma},\quad\forall g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma),

(13)

where
\displaystyle b
is a constant to be specified. In fact, let
\displaystyle g_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a function such that
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g_{1}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}\neq 0
and define
\displaystyle b=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g_{1})}{\int_{\Sigma}g_{1}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}}
. Given
\displaystyle g\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle g_{2}=-\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g\,d\mu_{\Sigma}}{\int_{\Sigma}g_{1}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}}g_{1}+g\in\mathcal{F}

proving (
13
). Thus,
\displaystyle f
is a weak solution to the first equation in (
12

\displaystyle P_{r}
is positive definite at each point of
\displaystyle\Sigma
, the regularity theory of second-order elliptic self-adjoint linear operator implies that
\displaystyle f\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
, proving that
\displaystyle f
satisfies the equation in the strong sense.

In order to prove that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f=0
on
\displaystyle\partial\Sigma

g=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f,&in $\displaystyle\partial\Sigma$\\
0,&else\end{cases}.

Since
\displaystyle f\eta
is a Jacobi field, we have

0=\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)=\int_{\partial\Sigma}\left|{P_{r}\nu}\right|\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}+\alpha_{\theta}f\right)^{2}\,d\mu_{\partial\Sigma},

proving (i).

(ii)

Let
\displaystyle g\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
\displaystyle\varphi
is
\displaystyle r
-stable, we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}\left(f+\varepsilon g,f+\varepsilon g\right)\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f+\varepsilon g,f+\varepsilon g)=\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,f)+2\varepsilon\,\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(g,g)

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon
and letting

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{r,\theta}(f,g)\geq 0
. The reverse inequality is obtained replacing
\displaystyle\varepsilon
by
\displaystyle-\varepsilon
. ∎

Before proving the next theorem, we need the following definition and the following Lemma.

Definition 5.4
.

The
\displaystyle k
-th
coefficient of umbilicity

\displaystyle\tau_{k}
of a hypersurface
\displaystyle\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow M

\tau_{k}=(k+1)^{2}S_{k+1}^{2}-(n-k)S_{k}\tr\left(P_{k}A^{2}\right).

Lemma 5.5

(Umbilicity Lemma)
.

If
\displaystyle\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{n}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle P_{k}
is positive definite on each point of
\displaystyle\Sigma

\tau_{k}=(k+1)^{2}S_{k+1}^{2}-(n-k)S_{k}\tr\left(P_{k}A^{2}\right)\leq 0

and
\displaystyle\tau_{k}\equiv 0
if and only if
\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=...=\kappa_{n}
.

Notice that for
\displaystyle k=0
,
\displaystyle\tau_{0}=S_{1}^{2}-nS_{0}\tr A^{2}=n\left(nH^{2}-|{A}|^{2}\right)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle\kappa_{1},...,\kappa_{n}\geq 0
and
\displaystyle(n-k)\binom{n}{k}=(k+1)\binom{n}{k+1}
for all
\displaystyle k\in\{0,...,n-1\}
, it follows from Lemma

that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tau_{k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(k+1)^{2}S_{k+1}^{2}-(n-k)S_{k}\left(S_{1}S_{k+1}-(k+2)S_{k+2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(k+1)^{2}\binom{n}{k+1}^{2}H_{k+1}^{2}-(n-k)\binom{n}{k}H_{k}\left(n\binom{n}{k+1}H_{1}H_{k+1}-(k+2)\binom{n}{k+2}H_{k+2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(n-k)\binom{n}{k}\left((n-k)\binom{n}{k}H_{k+1}^{2}-H_{k}\left(\frac{n}{k+1}H_{1}H_{k+1}-(n-k-1)\binom{n}{k+1}H_{k+2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left((n-k)\binom{n}{k}\right)^{2}\left(H_{k+1}^{2}-H_{k}\left(\frac{nH_{1}H_{k+1}-(n-k-1)H_{k+2}}{k+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left((n-k)\binom{n}{k}\right)^{2}\left(H_{k+1}^{2}-H_{1}H_{k}H_{k+1}\right)\leq 0.

\displaystyle\Sigma

which shows that

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle\partial\Sigma

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

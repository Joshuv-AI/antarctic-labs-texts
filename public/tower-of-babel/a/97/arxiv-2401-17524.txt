The Morawetz problem for supersonic flow with cavitation
Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Tristan P. Giron, Simon M. Schulz

Abstract
. We are concerned with the existence and compactness of entropy solutions of the compressible Euler system for two-dimensional steady potential flow around an obstacle for a polytropic gas with supersonic far-field velocity. The existence problem, initially posed by Morawetz [ 33 ] in 1985, has remained open since then. In this paper, we establish the first complete existence theorem for the Morawetz problem by developing a new entropy analysis, coupled with a vanishing viscosity method and compensated compactness ideas. The main challenge arises when the flow approaches cavitation, leading to a loss of strict hyperbolicity of the system and a singularity of the entropy equation, particularly for the case of adiabatic exponent γ = 3 \gamma=3 . Our analysis provides a complete description of the entropy and entropy-flux pairs via the Loewner–Morawetz relations, which, in turn, leads to the establishment of a compensated compactness framework. As direct applications of our entropy analysis and the compensated compactness framework, we obtain the compactness of entropy solutions and the weak continuity of the compressible Euler system in the supersonic regime.

1.
Introduction

The Morawetz problem is concerned with the existence theory of global solutions of
the two-dimensional steady compressible potential flow equations:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\div(\varrho(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u})=0,\\
&\curl\mathbf{u}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathbf{u}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the velocity and
\varrho

is the density, prescribed by the
Bernoulli law
:

(1.2)

\varrho(\mathbf{u})=\big(1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}

for
\gamma>1
(the adiabatic exponent).
In this paper, such a result is achieved by means of a vanishing viscosity method;
that is, such a solution
\mathbf{u}
of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
is constructed as a limit of
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
solving the approximate systems:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\div(\varrho(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})=V_{1}^{\varepsilon},\\
&\curl\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}=V_{2}^{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

where the viscous terms
V_{1}^{\varepsilon}
and
V_{2}^{\varepsilon}

are determined so that
they vanish in the sense of distributions in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0

and some uniform estimates of the corresponding solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
can be obtained.

System (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) is
an archetypal system of conservation laws,
which is of fundamental importance to aerodynamics and fluid mechanics

.
[

,

Together with (

Define the

(1.4)

c(\varrho):=\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},\qquad M:=\frac{|\mathbf{u}|}{c},

and introduce the velocity potential

\mathbf{u}=\nabla\varphi
.
Then the potential flow
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) takes the form:

(1.5)

\div(\varrho(|\nabla\varphi|)\nabla\varphi)=0,

which is of mixed elliptic-hyperbolic type:

(referred to as the

)
and hyperbolic when
|\nabla\varphi|>c
(termed as

);
the curve

.
This paper is devoted to the existence theory of entropy solutions
and the weak continuity of system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
);
the relevance of this work stretches beyond the Morawetz problem,
as the inviscid Euler system serves as a paradigmatic
mixed-type system, sharing many common features with other mixed-type problems in fluid dynamics,
differential geometry, and related fields (see, for example,

The early developments in the analysis of the potential flow system relied
on the methods from the theory of pseudo-analytic functions to solve the quasilinear second-order

These methods mirrored the developments in the theory of minimal surfaces, where system (

While this approach successfully addresses subsonic flows, corresponding to the elliptic equation (

In fact, it was proved in

To address this, an alternative approach was proposed Morawetz

The key challenge lies in establishing the rigorous limit of problems (

.
This necessitates identifying uniform estimates of the approximate solutions and leveraging fine compactness properties of the system.
To circumvent the nonlinear nature of the potential flow system (

)–(

),
Morawetz
[

proposed utilizing compensated compactness, which had previously been applied successfully
in addressing other nonlinear conservation laws (

.
[

,

,

,

,

,
43
,
45

The framework developed by Morawetz in
[

,

requires the viscosity terms

and

to satisfy the two uniform bounds:

(i)

A uniform bound of the form:
|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|\geq\underline{q}
for some constant
\underline{q}>0

the solutions stay uniformly away from stagnation

(ii)

A uniform bound of the form:

,

However, these assumptions may not fully align with physical expectations, as it is generally anticipated

Additionally, apart from the condition away from stagnation, Morawetz’s strategy proposed
in
[

,

relies on the utilization of a hodograph transformation.
Yet, it remains unclear whether a rigorous justification for this transformation

Despite being formulated nearly forty years ago,
the Morawetz problem has remained open and largely unexplored since Morawetz’s own work.
Notable exceptions include the contributions of Chen-Dafermos-Slemrod-Wang

and Huang-Wang-Wang

The former employs a compensated compactness approach in the sonic-subsonic regime,
reflecting the earlier work of Bers

The latter focuses on generating approximate problems admissible in Morawetz’s framework,
successfully excluding the possibility of vacuum occurrence when

,
without relying on Morawetz’s problematic hodograph transformation.
However, a complete existence proof remains elusive in the presence of stagnation,
which cannot be
a priori
excluded for this case.

The present work is concerned with system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
for the case of a gas with
\gamma
-law
for the physically relevant adiabatic exponent
\gamma=3

which in fact corresponds to the case

in the isentropic Euler system (

We establish a compactness framework that accommodates cavitation and
demonstrate that, for any incoming uniform supersonic flow,

Consequently, we succeed to achieve the first complete existence

\gamma>3
will be the subject of future investigations.

Compensated compactness was first introduced by Tartar and
Murat
[
43
,
45
]
;
its first successful application to a system of hyperbolic conservation laws is
due to DiPerna
[
16
,
17
]
,
who established the global existence of an entropy solution of
the Cauchy problem for the one-dimensional isentropic Euler equations:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varrho_{t}+(\varrho u)_{x}=0,\\
&(\varrho u)_{t}+\big(\varrho u^{2}+\tilde{p}(\varrho)\big)_{x}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{p}(\varrho)=\kappa\varrho^{\tilde{\gamma}}
with
\kappa>0

for the cases:
\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{N+2}{N}
for
N\geq 5
integer.
For the interval
1<\gamma\leq\frac{5}{3}
,
the global existence problem was solved in
Ding-Chen-Luo
[
15
]
and Chen
[
4
]
.
The adiabatic exponent range
\gamma\geq 3
was solved in
[
29
]
,
and the remaining case
\frac{5}{3}<\gamma<3
was
closed in Lions-Perthame-Souganidis
[
28
]
.

All these results rely on the cancellation properties of certain nonlinear functionals to obtain compactness
properties (see also
[
5
]
). More precisely, we consider a sequence of functions
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
,
bounded in
L^{\infty}
. Up to a subsequence, it converges weak-* to a limit
\mathbf{u}
;
however, the weak-* convergence is not sufficient to pass to the limit
in nonlinear quantities, say
f(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})
.
Under certain conditions on
f
and sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
,
compensated compactness ideas may be used to show that
\text{w*-}\lim f(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})=f(\mathbf{u})
.
This method is typically applied to deal with the weak limits of approximate solutions
of systems of nonlinear conversation laws. In this context, sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

may be obtained for instance from a viscous system with vanishing viscosity.
It is not usually possible to show directly that the nonlinearities in the system satisfy
the hypotheses of compensated compactness,
even though this is possible sometimes (see
e.g.

[
7
]
).
Instead, with the help of Young measures, for any continuous function
f
,
the weak-* limit of
f(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})
may be characterized as

f(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\,\int f\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}},

where
\{\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}\}_{\mathbf{x}}
is a family of probability measures on the phase-space.
If
f
is shown to be affine on the support of the Young measure,
it then follows that
f(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}f(\mathbf{u})
.
Moreover, if
f
is assumed to be genuinely nonlinear, so that it is nowhere affine,
then it follows that the Young measure concentrates as a Dirac mass
\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}=\delta_{\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})}

whence
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}
almost everywhere.
Then the convergence is sufficiently strong to pass to the limit in the equations.

Hyperbolic systems of nonlinear conservation laws are endowed with
families of entropy pairs (
i.e.
, the pairs of entropy and entropy-flux).
While the original nonlinearity does not typically satisfy the assumptions of compensated compactness,
the key strategy is that compensated compactness arguments can be used with the entropy pairs.
Realizing the above strategy therefore requires a fine understanding of the entropy structure
of the underlying system.

In this paper, we provide a detailed analysis of the entropy structure
for the potential flow system by generating two linearly independent families of entropy pairs.
These families of entropy pairs are subsequently utilized to establish our compensated compactness framework.
The description of the entropy pair families involves a delicate analysis of a Tricomi–Keldysh
equation of the form:

H_{\nu\nu}-\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}=0,

which is of mixed-type: hyperbolic for
M>1
and elliptic when
M<1
(exhibiting
Tricomi degeneracy at
M=1
).
Additionally, the equation is singular at cavitation
\varrho=0
, displaying Keldysh degeneracy.
This analysis constitutes the primary focus of the present work.

2.
The Morawetz Problem and Main Theorems

In this section, we first set up the problem, introduce the key notions required for our analysis,
and then provide the precise statements of our main results and some outline
of our strategies of proof. Finally,
we discuss formal links between the Morawetz problem and the isentropic Euler equations.

2.1.
Problem setup

We are concerned with the existence of an entropy solution
\mathbf{u}=(u,v)
of the nonlinear
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) for the case
\gamma=3
,
i.e.
,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\div(\varrho(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u})=0,\\
&\curl\mathbf{u}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where, denoting
q:=|\mathbf{u}|
to be the
scalar speed
, the density
\varrho
is prescribed by the Bernoulli law:

\varrho(\mathbf{u})=\sqrt{1-q^{2}}.

The problem is posed in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
in the exterior of an obstacle
O
,
a closed connected bounded subset
\Omega:=\mathbb{H}\setminus O
as shown in Figure 2.1.
The obstacle boundary
\partial O
is endowed with a unit normal
vector
\mathbf{n}
pointing into
\Omega
which is filled with the fluid.
A uniform supersonic flow
\mathbf{u}_{\infty}=(q_{\infty},0)
with constant speed
q_{\infty}>0

is given at
x=-\infty
, and a Neumann-type condition is imposed on the obstacle boundary

\partial O
; this is discussed in detail later (see also §
8
).

\mathbf{u}_{\infty}
O
\Omega

Figure 2.1.
The Morawetz problem for two-dimensional steady supersonic flow past an obstacle
O
.

Introduce the
angle function

\vartheta
associated to the velocity field
\mathbf{u}
such that

(2.1)

\mathbf{u}=(q\cos\vartheta,q\sin\vartheta),

and define the
renormalized density

\nu
by the formula:

(2.2)

\nu(\varrho):=\int_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{\tau^{2}}{1-\tau^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then we construct entropy pairs
\mathbf{Q}=(Q_{1},Q_{2})
of
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),
which are functions of the phase space variables
\mathbf{u}
,
via the
Loewner–Morawetz relations
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\begin{matrix}Q_{1}\\
Q_{2}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho q\cos\vartheta&-q\sin\vartheta\\
\varrho q\sin\vartheta&q\cos\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}H_{\nu}\\
H_{\vartheta}\end{matrix}\right),\end{split}

where the
entropy generators

H
are determined by a linear singular mixed-type equation —

the Tricomi–Kelydish equation
:

(2.4)

H_{\nu\nu}-\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}=0;

we refer the reader to §
3.3
for more details on this construction.
We emphasize that the function pairs
\mathbf{Q}
, which are technically
computed in the
(\varrho,\vartheta)
-coordinates
as above, are entropy pairs for the conservative
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
so that
\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{u})
is used throughout the paper.

A particular solution of (
2.4
) is provided by the
special entropy generator
:

(2.5)

H^{*}(\nu,\vartheta)=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2}-\frac{\nu}{\varrho(\bar{\nu})}+\int_{\bar{\nu}}^{\nu}\int_{\bar{\nu}}^{\tau}\frac{M(\nu^{\prime})^{2}-1}{\varrho(\nu^{\prime})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

for suitably chosen constant
\bar{\nu}>0
, provided that
the integral is well-defined (
cf.

[
40
]
).
We define the
special entropy pair

\mathbf{Q}_{*}
to be the entropy pair generated by
H^{*}

via the Loewner–Morawetz relations.
When restricted to the supersonic region, this special generator constitutes
a uniformly convex solution of (
2.4
);
however, it is not periodic with respect to
\vartheta
.

The starting point of the compensated compactness method is to find
a uniformly convex entropy of the system,
with which some uniform gradient estimates are obtained.
It is not clear
a priori
that (
2.4
) possesses uniformly convex solutions
defined up to and including the subsonic region. For this reason,
as well as the invariant regions associated with our setting (
cf.
§
8
),
in this paper, we focus on the supersonic regime.
Note that the supersonic condition
M>1
is equivalent to
q>q_{\mathrm{cr}}
, where the
sonic speed
,
or equivalently
critical speed
, is given by

q_{\mathrm{cr}}:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}};

this constraint is equivalent to
\varrho<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}:=\varrho(q_{\mathrm{cr}})
.
We also define
\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}:=\nu(\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}})
and consider equation (
2.4
) only
for
\nu\in(0,\nu_{*}]
for
\nu_{*}<\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}
,
where this equation is a singular linear wave equation.
Much of the current paper is concerned with generating sufficiently many solutions
of (
2.4
)
to provide the associated entropy pairs as many as possible for the compensated compactness method to be applied.
We note that the vacuum is achieved at the
cavitation speed
:

q_{\mathrm{cav}}:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}},

where, by virtue of the Bernoulli relation, the density is null.

As already mentioned, equation (
2.4
) is a singular
linear second-order wave equation
in the domain of interest, so that two linearly independent fundamental solutions
are expected to generate all possible solutions, which are denoted by
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
.
Furthermore, these fundamental solutions should satisfy the Huygens principle.
To quantify this finite-speed of propagation, we rewrite equation (
2.4
)
in the familiar form:

(2.6)

H_{\nu\nu}-k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}=0.

The Bernoulli relation (
1.2
) implies that
\nu
satisfies

(2.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu^{\prime}(\varrho)=\frac{1}{M^{2}(\rho)}\qquad\text{in }(0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}),\\
&\nu(0)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

whence, by defining the
characteristic speed

k
via

(2.8)

k(\nu(\varrho)):=\int_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{\sqrt{M^{2}(\varrho^{\prime})-1}}{\varrho^{\prime}}\frac{1}{M^{2}(\varrho^{\prime})}\,\mathrm{d}\varrho^{\prime}\qquad\text{for all }\varrho\in(0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}),

we see that

(2.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}=\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}\qquad\text{in }(0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}),\\
&k(0)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

It follows that the entropy generator equation (
2.4
)
can be recast in form (
2.6
) in the supersonic regime.
Moreover, by defining the
light cone
:

\mathcal{K}:=\{(\nu,s)\,:\,|s|\leq k(\nu)\},

we expect that
\supp H^{\mathbf{r}}\cup\supp H^{\mathbf{s}}\subset\mathcal{K}
.

With these notions in hand, we are ready to state our main theorems.

2.2.
Main theorems

Our first main theorem is concerned with the existence of entropy generator kernels in the hyperbolic region,
up to and including cavitation (also see Propositions
4.1
and
5.1
).

Theorem 2.1
.

There exist distributional solutions
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
with
supported in
\mathcal{K}

of the initial value problems in
(0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}})\times\mathbb{R}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu\nu}-\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}H^{\mathbf{r}}_{ss}=0,\\
&H^{\mathbf{r}}|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=\delta_{0},\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} &H^{\mathbf{s}}_{\nu\nu}-\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}H^{\mathbf{s}}_{ss}=0,\\
&H^{\mathbf{s}}|_{\nu=0}=\delta_{0},\\
&\varrho H^{\mathbf{s}}_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=-\delta_{0}^{\prime\prime},\end{aligned}\right.

for which
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}

are respectively called the
regular kernel
and the

of (
2.4
).

Remark 2.1

(Generating Entropy Pairs)
.

We say that a generator
H
is
generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}

if there exist smooth functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
such that

H=H^{\mathbf{r}}*\varphi_{1}+H^{\mathbf{s}}*\varphi_{2},

where the convolution is taken only for the second variable

.

If an entropy pair
\mathbf{Q}
is determined from the Loewner–Morawetz relations
via a generator
H
generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
, we say
that
\mathbf{Q}
is generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
. A suitable generator
H
can also be determined
from
\mathbf{Q}
by inverting the matrix in (
2.3
).

Our goal is then to employ Theorem
2.1
to produce entropy pairs
that are endowed with additional compactness properties.
More precisely, we require the approximate solution
sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
to satisfy
that the sequence of
entropy dissipation measures

\{\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is pre-compact in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
This requirement is critical in applying the compensated compactness method.

To state our main existence result, we must first introduce our notion of solutions.

Definition 2.2

(Entropy Solutions)
.

We say that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}
is an

of (

1.2
)

(i)

is a solution of system (

)–(

(ii)

Let

, where

Theorem 2.2

(Global Existence of Entropy Solutions)

Given the incoming uniform supersonic flow
\mathbf{u}_{\infty}=(q_{\infty},0)

such that

0\leq\varrho(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\varrho_{*},\quad q_{\mathrm{cr}}<q_{*}\leq|\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\qquad\text{a.e.
}\mathbf{x}\in\Omega,

for some
\varrho_{*}>0
and
q_{*}>0
.

We show in §
9
that the entropy solution obtained
in Theorem
2.2

satisfies the boundary condition around
the obstacle boundary
\partial O
:
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial O}\geq 0
in the sense
of weak normal traces (
cf.
Chen-Frid
[
9
]
). Obtaining the more precise boundary
condition
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial O}=0
may be possible
for specific domains, which are beyond the scope of this paper.

As mentioned before, the existence result is proved by combining a vanishing viscosity
method with the following compensated compactness framework:

Theorem 2.3

(Compensated Compactness Framework)
.

Let
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}

be a sequence of functions satisfying
:

(i)

There exists
\varrho_{*}\in(0,\varrho_{\rm cr})
, independent of
\varepsilon>0
, such that

0\leq\varrho(|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})|)\leq\varrho_{*}<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}\qquad\mbox{a.e.~$\mathbf{x}$};

or equivalently, there exists
q^{*}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}}]
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

(2.12)

q_{\mathrm{cr}}<q_{*}\leq|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\qquad\mbox{a.e.~$\mathbf{x}.$}

(ii)

There exist
\vartheta_{*},\vartheta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
,
independent of
\varepsilon>0
,
such that

(2.13)

\vartheta_{*}\leq\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\vartheta^{*}\qquad\mbox{a.e.~$\mathbf{x},$}

where
\vartheta^{\varepsilon}
is the angle function associated to the velocity field
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}.

(iii)

For any entropy pair
\mathbf{Q}
via
the Loewner–Morawetz relations
from a generator
H
generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
,

(2.14)

\{\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}\quad\mbox{is pre-compact in $H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$}.

Then there exist both a subsequence
(
still denoted
)

\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
a function
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}
such that

(2.15)

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\,\to\,\mathbf{u}\qquad\mbox{
{\it a.e.}~and strongly in $L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for all $p\in[1,\infty)$}.

As a direct corollary, we obtain the compactness of entropy solutions
of the Morawetz problem.

Theorem 2.4

(Compactness and Weak Continuity)
.

Let
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}
be entropy solutions of
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) which admit the following bounds
:

(i)

There exists
\varrho_{*}\in(0,\varrho_{\rm cr})
, independent of
\varepsilon>0
, such that

0\leq\varrho(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\varrho_{*}<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}\qquad\mbox{
a.e.~$\mathbf{x}$}.

(ii)

There exist
q^{*}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}}]
and
\vartheta_{*},\vartheta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
,
independent of
\varepsilon>0

such that

q_{\mathrm{cr}}<q_{*}\leq|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}},\quad\vartheta_{*}\leq\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\vartheta^{*}\qquad\mbox{
a.e.~$\mathbf{x}$},

where
\vartheta^{\varepsilon}
is the angle function associated to the velocity field
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}.

Then there exist both a
subsequence
(
still denoted
)

\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

and a function
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}
such that

(i)

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\,\to\,\mathbf{u}
a.e. and strongly in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for all

(ii)

The limit function
\mathbf{u}

is also an entropy solution of system (
1.1
)–(

).

2.3.
Connections with the isentropic Euler equations

As mentioned earlier, the Morawetz problem is deeply connected to the isentropic Euler equations (
1.6
).
In particular, recall that an entropy pair
(E,F)
for system (
1.6
) is a pair of functions
of
(\varrho,u)
such that

E_{t}(\varrho,u)+F_{x}(\varrho,u)=0

for any smooth solution
(\varrho,u)
of system (
1.6
).
When the pressure law is given by a polytropic perfect gas,
i.e.
,

(2.16)

p(\varrho)=\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}-1)^{2}}{4\tilde{\gamma}}\varrho^{\tilde{\gamma}},

the entropy
E
and entropy-flux
F
may be generated by convolving test functions
\phi
with kernels,
denoted for convenience
\chi(\varrho,u,s)
and
\sigma(\varrho,u,s)
, respectively,
so that
(E(\varrho,u),F(\varrho,u))=(\chi(\varrho,u)*_{s}\phi,\,\sigma(\varrho,u)*_{s}\phi)
.
The entropy-flux generator itself is given
by
\sigma(\varrho,u,s)=(u+\frac{\tilde{\gamma}-1}{2}(s-u))\chi(\varrho,u,s)
,
while the entropy generator
\chi
is determined by the Keldysh equation:

(2.17)

\chi_{\varrho\varrho}-\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}-1)^{2}}{4}\varrho^{\tilde{\gamma}-3}\chi_{uu}=0.

This equation admits two linearly independent fundamental solutions,
encoding the choices of admissible initial conditions.

More generally, when considering general pressure laws so that

p(\varrho)
is only
asymptotically
polytropic, a similar Keldysh equation:

(2.18)

\chi_{\varrho\varrho}-\tilde{k}^{\prime}(\varrho)^{2}\chi_{uu}=0

holds for the entropy generator
\chi(\varrho,u,s)
.
A general strategy for solving the equations of this form
was developed in
[
10
]
, and the first step in our approach is concerned with adapting this method.

Returning to the Morawetz problem, notice from (
2.2
) that

\nu(\varrho)=\tanh^{-1}(\varrho)-\varrho
as a closed-form;
however, there is no hope in inverting this formula explicitly.
It follows that the only information available is an asymptotic description
of the characteristic speed
k(\nu)
in the vicinity of the vacuum, a rough statement of which is

(2.19)

k(\nu)=c_{\sharp}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu),\qquad k^{\prime}(\nu)=\frac{1}{3}c_{\sharp}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(1)

for some positive constant
c_{\sharp}
(see Proposition
4.3
).

The leading term of the entropy generator equation (
2.6
)
coincides with the coefficient of the entropy equation for the isentropic Euler equations
(
1.6
)
with a suitable
\gamma
-law. More precisely,

(2.20)

k^{\prime 2}_{\text{Morawetz}}(\nu)\sim(c_{\sharp}\theta/\gamma)^{2}\nu^{-1-\frac{1}{\gamma}},

which coincides with the coefficient of the entropy equation
for
system (
1.6
) when
\tilde{\gamma}=2-\frac{1}{\gamma}
.
We highlight that the case:
\gamma=3
for the Morawetz problem corresponds to
the case:
\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{5}{3}

for the isentropic Euler equations (
1.6
).

We emphasize that our setting is different from that in
[
10
]

for the isentropic Euler equations with general pressure laws.
While it is possible to construct an ad-hoc general pressure
law
\tilde{p}(\nu)
in the sense of
[
10
]
,
matching our
k(\nu)
specified above, this pressure law fails to verify the genuine nonlinearity
requirement in
[
10
]
.
More precisely, coefficient
k
is defined from a given pressure
law

\tilde{p}(\varrho):=\kappa\varrho^{\tilde{\gamma}}(1+P(\varrho))
,
for
\kappa>0
and
\tilde{\gamma}\in(1,3)
with
P
satisfying

|P^{(j)}(\varrho)|\leq C\varrho^{\tilde{\gamma}-1-j}
for
j=1,\dots,4
,
by setting
k^{\prime}(\varrho)=\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{p}^{\prime}(\varrho)}}{\varrho}
.
One is indeed able to reconstruct a pressure function
\tilde{p}(\nu)
of this form
from our coefficient
k(\nu)
defined in (
2.8
) by defining

\tilde{p}(\nu):=\int_{0}^{\nu}\tau^{2}k^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{9}c_{\sharp}^{2}\int_{0}^{\nu}\big(\tau^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{\frac{4}{3}})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{6}c_{\sharp}^{2}\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}\big(1+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{2}{3}})\big),

so that, by setting
\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{5}{3}
,
the perturbation
P(\nu):=6c_{\sharp}^{-2}\nu^{-\frac{5}{3}}p(\nu)-1=\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{2}{3}})

satisfies the boundedness assumptions in
[
10
]
.
However, the pressure functions admissible in the setting in
[
10
]
must also satisfy
the assumptions of strict hyperbolicity and genuine nonlinearity,
i.e.
,

\tilde{p}^{\prime}(\nu)>0
and
2\tilde{p}^{\prime}(\nu)+\nu\tilde{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)>0
for
\nu>0
.
This second requirement is not true in our case since, in the vicinity of the vacuum,

\displaystyle 2\tilde{p}^{\prime}(\nu)+\nu\tilde{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)=4\nu^{2}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}+2\nu^{3}k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)k^{\prime}(\nu)\sim\frac{8}{27}c_{\sharp}^{2}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}\quad\text{as }\nu\to 0,

which is positive; however,
along the sonic curve where
M=1

(
cf.
Remark
4.4
),

\displaystyle 2\tilde{p}^{\prime}(\nu_{\mathrm{cr}})+\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}\tilde{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nu_{\mathrm{cr}})=\Big(4\nu^{2}\frac{(M^{2}-1)}{\varrho^{2}}-2\nu^{3}\frac{M^{2}}{\varrho^{3}}\big(\frac{1}{\varrho^{2}}+(M^{2}-1)\big)\Big)\Big|_{\nu=\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}}\!\!\!\!=\!-2\frac{\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}^{3}}{\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}^{5}},

which is negative.
In turn, one cannot directly apply

[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
,
concerned with the existence of the weak entropy kernel for the isentropic Euler equations,
to obtain the existence of the regular kernel
H^{\mathbf{r}}
.
Meanwhile, the existence of the strong entropy kernel has never been treated for the pressure
laws different from exactly
polytropic gas, so the analysis of the singular kernel
H^{\mathbf{s}}
is different from what has been
covered thus far in the literature.

3.
Hyperbolicity and Entropy Structure

This section aims to formulate system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
as a genuinely nonlinear hyperbolic system in §
3.1

and to contextualize the Loewner–Morawetz relations for entropy pairs
given in (
2.3
)–(
2.4
) in §
3.3
.
The main results of this section are the following:

Proposition 3.1

(Hyperbolicity and Genuine Nonlinearity)
.

For
\gamma=3
, system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
is strictly hyperbolic in region
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}<|\mathbf{u}|<q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}

and is genuinely nonlinear in region
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}\leq|\mathbf{u}|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}
.

Proposition 3.2

(Riemann Invariants)
.

For
\gamma=3
, system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
is endowed with the Riemann invariants
:

(3.1)

W_{\pm}=\vartheta\pm k,

and the mapping
:

\mathbf{u}\mapsto(W_{-}(\mathbf{u}),W_{+}(\mathbf{u}))
is bijective on
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}\leq|\mathbf{u}|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}
.

3.1.
Hyperbolicity and genuine nonlinearity

We first show Proposition
3.1
.

Following
[
11
]
, we first recast
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in polar coordinates.
By recalling the angle function
\vartheta
associated with the velocity field
\mathbf{u}

as introduced in (
2.1
),
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
can be formally rewritten
as

(3.2)

\left(\begin{matrix}q\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{x}+\mathbf{M}\left(\begin{matrix}q\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{y}=0,

where matrix
\mathbf{M}
is uniquely characterized by its normalized right-eigenvectors
\mathbf{R}_{\pm}

and corresponding eigenvalues
\Lambda_{\pm}
, given by the explicit formulas:

(3.3)

\mathbf{R}_{\pm}=\frac{qc}{\sqrt{q^{2}c^{2}+q^{2}-c^{2}}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
\mp\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{qc}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\Lambda_{\pm}=-\frac{\cos\vartheta\pm\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{c}\sin\vartheta}{\sin\vartheta\mp\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{c}\cos\vartheta},

with the local sonic speed
c
defined in (
1.4
):
c(\varrho)=\varrho

for
\gamma=3
.
As per
[
11
, §3]
, further linear transformations may be applied to formally
rewrite (
3.2
) as a nonlinear system
for the unknown vector field
(\varrho,\vartheta)
,
the setting in which we compute entropy pairs (
cf.
§
3.3
).

System (
3.2
),
as well as the proposed variants in
[
11
]
, is
a priori
not equivalent to
the conservative system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
);
indeed we do not expect the solutions to be regular enough to apply the chain rule.
To this end, we define the vector field:

(3.4)

\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})=\left(\begin{matrix}Z_{1}(\mathbf{u})\\
Z_{2}(\mathbf{u})\end{matrix}\right):=\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho(u,v)u\\
v\end{matrix}\right),

where
\varrho
is prescribed by the Bernoulli law (
1.2
)
so that (
1.1
) reads as

(3.5)

\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})_{x}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u}))_{y}=0

with

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u}))=\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho(u,v)v\\
-u\end{matrix}\right).

We note in passing that, for
\gamma=3
, the mapping:
\mathbf{u}\mapsto\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})

in (
3.4
) is well-defined
in region
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}\leq|\mathbf{u}|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}
and is invertible
in subregion
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}<|\mathbf{u}|<q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}
.
Indeed, if the system is constrained to this subregion,
we may reconstruct the velocity field
\mathbf{u}
from
\mathbf{Z}
by the explicit formulas:

(3.6)

\varrho(\mathbf{Z})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{(1-Z_{2}^{2})-\sqrt{(1-Z_{2}^{2})^{2}-4Z_{1}^{2}}},\quad u(\mathbf{Z})=\frac{Z_{1}}{\varrho(\mathbf{Z})},\quad v(\mathbf{Z})=Z_{2}.

The relations above are amount to solving for
\varrho
in terms of
\mathbf{Z}
in the Bernoulli law:

(3.7)

\varrho^{\gamma-1}=1-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\Big(\big(\frac{Z_{1}}{\varrho}\big)^{2}+Z_{2}^{2}\Big),

which may be inverted explicitly for
\gamma=3
,
provided that the condition:
0<\varrho<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}
is imposed.

We are now ready to give the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The proof is divided into two steps.

1.
Hyperbolicity
: First, matrix
\nabla_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{F}
may be computed by comparing
the change of coordinates in (
3.2
)
and the chain rule in (
3.5
):

\nabla_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{J},\qquad\mathbf{J}:=\left(\begin{matrix}q_{Z_{1}}&q_{Z_{2}}\\
\vartheta_{Z_{1}}&\vartheta_{Z_{2}}\end{matrix}\right).

Matrix
\mathbf{J}
is obtained by first calculating the Jacobian for the change of
variables:
(u,v)\mapsto(Z_{1},Z_{2})
via implicitly differentiating (
3.7
),
i.e.
,

\mathbf{K}:=\left(\begin{matrix}u_{Z_{1}}&u_{Z_{2}}\\
v_{Z_{1}}&v_{Z_{2}}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho(u^{2}-c^{2})}&-\frac{uv}{u^{2}-c^{2}}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),

and then multiplying by the corresponding matrix for the mapping:
(q,\vartheta)\mapsto(u,v)
,
i.e.
,

\mathbf{L}:=\left(\begin{matrix}q_{u}&q_{v}\\
\vartheta_{u}&\vartheta_{v}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\vartheta&\sin\vartheta\\
-\frac{1}{q}\sin\vartheta&\frac{1}{q}\cos\vartheta\end{matrix}\right),

whence

\mathbf{J}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{K}.

Notice that, while the matrices above are not well-defined in terms of the coordinates
(Z_{1},Z_{2})

at the vacuum,
they are well-defined on the locus:
\{\varrho=0\}

in terms of either coordinate system
(u,v)
or
(q,\vartheta)
.

It follows from the previous computations that
matrix
\nabla_{\mathbf{Z}}\mathbf{F}
is uniquely characterized
by its normalized right-eigenvectors:

\mathbf{e}_{\pm}=\frac{\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}}{|\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\varrho^{2}c^{-2}(q^{2}-c^{2})}}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho c^{-2}(uv+\Lambda_{\pm}(c^{2}-u^{2}))\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),

and corresponding eigenvalues are given again by
\Lambda_{\pm}
that are distinct and real
when
\varrho>0
and
q>c
,
and are equal on locus
\{\varrho=0\}
when
c=0
.
Thus, the conservative system is strictly hyperbolic in the supersonic region away from the vacuum.

2.
Genuine nonlinearity
: A direct computation yields

\partial_{q}\Lambda_{\pm}=\mp\frac{\frac{qc}{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}\big(1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^{2}\big)}{(c\sin\vartheta\mp\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}\cos\vartheta)^{2}},\quad\partial_{\vartheta}\Lambda_{\pm}=\frac{q^{2}}{(c\sin\vartheta\mp\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}\cos\vartheta)^{2}},

whence

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{Z}}\Lambda_{\pm}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{e}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{J}^{\top}\nabla_{(q,\vartheta)}\Lambda_{\pm})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}}{|\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}|}\big(\nabla_{(q,\vartheta)}\Lambda_{\pm}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\pm}\big)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\gamma+1}{2(c\sin\vartheta\mp\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}\cos\vartheta)^{3}}\frac{q^{3}}{\sqrt{(1+\varrho^{3-\gamma}(q^{2}-c^{2}))(q^{2}-c^{2})}}.

Since the inequality

\frac{q^{3}}{\sqrt{(1+\varrho^{3-\gamma}(q^{2}-c^{2}))(q^{2}-c^{2})}}>0

holds everywhere in the hyperbolic region including at the vacuum for
\gamma=3
, the conservative system (
3.5
)
is genuinely nonlinear in the supersonic region
\{q\geq c\}

including the vacuum states
\{\varrho=0\}
.
∎

3.2.
Riemann invariants

We now show Proposition
3.2
.

As per
[
11
, §4]
, system (
3.2
)
is endowed with Riemann invariants
W_{\pm}
that satisfy the conditions:

\nabla_{(q,\vartheta)}W_{\pm}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\pm}=0
,
i.e.
,

(3.8)

\partial_{\vartheta}W_{\pm}=1,\quad\partial_{q}W_{\pm}=\mp\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{qc}

when

q>c
.
Using the abuse of notation
k(q)
to denote
k
as a function of
q
when composed with
\nu\circ\varrho
, we compute

k^{\prime}(q)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}k}{\,\mathrm{d}\nu}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\nu}{\,\mathrm{d}\varrho}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\varrho}{\,\mathrm{d}q}=-\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{qc}.

It follows from (
3.8
) that the closed-form expression (
3.1
)
for
W_{\pm}
holds, where, by comparing with
[
11
, §5]
for
\gamma=3
,

(3.9)

k(q)=(\sqrt{2}-1)\frac{\pi}{2}-\Big(\sqrt{2}\arcsin\sqrt{2q^{2}-1}-\arcsin\sqrt{2-\frac{1}{q^{2}}}\Big);

a general formula for
k
for all
\gamma>1
may be found in
e.g.

[
23
, §117]
.

The previous formula implies

(3.10)

k^{\prime}(q)=-\frac{1}{q}\sqrt{\frac{2q^{2}-1}{{1-q^{2}}}}<0

for all speeds
q\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},1)
.
Thus,
k:(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})\to\mathbb{R}
is strictly decreasing
and hence invertible on this subdomain. It follows that, for all
q\in[q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}}]
,

(\sqrt{2}-1)\frac{\pi}{2}=k(q_{\mathrm{cr}})\geq k(q)\geq k(q_{\mathrm{cav}})=0.

We are now ready to prove Proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

The proof is divided into two steps.

1.
Invariant coordinates
:
A direct computation shows that

(3.11)

\nabla_{\mathbf{Z}}W_{\pm}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\pm}=(\mathbf{J}^{\top}\nabla_{(q,\vartheta)}W_{\pm})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}}{|\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}|}=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{J}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{\pm}|}\big(\nabla_{(q,\vartheta)}W_{\pm}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\pm}\big)=0,

whence we deduce that functions
W_{\pm}
of (
3.1
) are Riemann invariants for the conservative system (
3.5
).

2.
Invertible coordinate transformation
: We consider the bijectivity of the curvilinear
coordinate transformation
\mathbf{u}\mapsto(W_{+}(\mathbf{u}),W_{-}(\mathbf{u}))
.
This is manifestly well-defined via formulas (
3.1
) and (
3.9
);
it is then a matter of verifying whether they are invertible.

By recalling computation (
3.10
), the velocity vector
\mathbf{u}
may be reconstructed
away from the vacuum and the sonic curve (where
k
is invertible) as follows:

\displaystyle q(W_{-},W_{+})=k^{-1}\big(\frac{1}{2}(W_{+}-W_{-})\big),\qquad\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})=\frac{1}{2}\big(W_{+}+W_{-}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(W_{-},W_{+})=q(W_{-},W_{+})\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\\
\sin\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\end{matrix}\right).

Meanwhile, at the vacuum and on the sonic curve, respectively, we write

\mathbf{u}(W_{-},W_{+})|_{\mathrm{cav}}=q_{\mathrm{cav}}\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\\
\sin\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\end{matrix}\right),\quad\mathbf{u}(W_{-},W_{+})|_{\mathrm{cr}}=q_{\mathrm{cr}}\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\\
\sin\vartheta(W_{-},W_{+})\end{matrix}\right),

and the result follows.
∎

3.3.
Entropy structure

The purpose of this
subsection is to provide a derivation of the entropy generator equation (
2.4
).
We emphasize that the results of this
subsection were already contained in
[
11
]
; we recall them to make the present paper self-contained.
In what follows, to simplify the notation, we omit the
\varepsilon
-superscript.

We recast the approximate system (
1.3
)
in the matrix form, analogous to (
3.2
),
and expand
q
in terms of
\varrho
by using the Bernoulli relation with
q^{\prime}(\varrho)=-\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}
.
As per

[
11
, §3]
, we obtain

(3.12)

\mathbf{A}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{x}+\mathbf{B}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{y}=\left(\begin{matrix}-V_{2}\\
-\frac{q^{2}}{q^{2}-c^{2}}V_{1}\end{matrix}\right),

where

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}\sin\vartheta&-q\cos\vartheta\\
-q\cos\vartheta&-\frac{\varrho q^{3}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\sin\vartheta\end{matrix}\right),\quad\mathbf{B}=\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}\cos\vartheta&-q\sin\vartheta\\
-q\sin\vartheta&\frac{\varrho q^{3}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\cos\vartheta\end{matrix}\right).

Computing the scalar product of system (
3.12
) with
the vector field
(\Phi_{\vartheta},\Phi_{\varrho})^{\top}
for a scalar function
\Phi

of the phase-space variables, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}=-\Phi_{\vartheta}V_{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\Phi_{\varrho}V_{1},

where the pair of functions
\mathbf{Q}=(Q_{1},Q_{2})
is required to satisfy

(3.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{\varrho}Q_{1}=\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}\sin\vartheta\Phi_{\vartheta}-q\cos\vartheta\Phi_{\varrho},\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{\vartheta}Q_{1}=-q\cos\vartheta\Phi_{\vartheta}-\frac{\varrho q^{3}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\sin\vartheta\Phi_{\varrho},

\displaystyle\partial_{\varrho}Q_{2}=-\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}\cos\vartheta\Phi_{\vartheta}-q\sin\vartheta\Phi_{\varrho},\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{\vartheta}Q_{2}=-q\sin\vartheta\Phi_{\vartheta}+\frac{\varrho q^{3}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\cos\vartheta\Phi_{\varrho}.

We emphasize for clarity that the divergence in (
3.14
)
is understood as

\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}=\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{x}+\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{y}.

Along with (
3.15
), the requirement that

\partial_{\vartheta}\partial_{\varrho}Q_{j}=\partial_{\varrho}\partial_{\vartheta}Q_{j}
,
j=1,2
,
implies

\sin\vartheta\Big(\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho q}\Phi_{\vartheta\vartheta}+q\Phi_{\varrho}+\Big(\frac{\varrho q^{3}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\Phi_{\varrho}\Big)_{\varrho}\Big)=0,

which is satisfied,
provided that
\Phi
solves the Tricomi–Keldysh equation

(3.16)

\frac{c^{2}}{\varrho^{2}q^{2}}\Phi_{\vartheta\vartheta}+\Big(\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\Phi_{\varrho}\Big)_{\varrho}=0.

Define
H
to be a solution of the system:

(3.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}=-\Phi_{\vartheta},\\
&H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}=\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}-q^{2}}\Phi_{\varrho}.\end{aligned}\right.

We deduce from (
3.16
) that
H
satisfies the entropy generator
equation (
2.4
);
note also for consistency that equality
\Phi_{\vartheta\nu}=\Phi_{\nu\vartheta}
implies

\Big(H_{\nu\nu}-\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\Big)_{\vartheta}=0,

which is manifestly satisfied, provided that
H
is a solution of (
2.4
).

Conversely, if
H
is a solution of the entropy generator equation (
2.4
),
then, defining
\Phi
such that (
3.17
) is satisfied,
we see by a direct computation that
\Phi
satisfies the Tricomi–Keldysh equation (
3.16
).
Moreover, letting
\mathbf{Q}
be the pair of functions generated from this
H
via the Loewner–Morawetz
relations (
2.3
), it is direct
to check that relations (
3.15
)
are verified and the dissipation equality (
3.14
) holds in the form:

(3.18)

\displaystyle\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}=\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}\big)V_{2}+\big(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)V_{1}.

Finally, we emphasize that formula (
3.18
) holds
for entropy pairs
\mathbf{Q}
understood as functions of the conservative variables of (
3.5
).
Indeed, by setting
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Z}):=\mathbf{Q}(\varrho(\mathbf{Z}),\vartheta(\mathbf{Z}))
and

\mathbf{N}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho_{Z_{1}}&\varrho_{Z_{2}}\\
\vartheta_{Z_{1}}&\vartheta_{Z_{2}}\end{matrix}\right),

a direct computation yields that, for entropy pairs generated via the Loewner–Morawetz
relations (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(\mathbf{Z})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{1}\,\mathbf{N}\mathbf{Z}_{x}+\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{2}\,\mathbf{N}\mathbf{Z}_{y}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{x}+\nabla_{(\varrho,\vartheta)}Q_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\varrho\\
\vartheta\end{matrix}\right)_{y}

\displaystyle=\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}\big)V_{2}+\big(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)V_{1},

by using (
3.14
).

4.
Entropy Generator I: Regular Kernel

In this and the next section, we prove the existence of two
linearly
independent fundamental solutions of
the entropy generator equation (
2.4
) and
identify
a cancellation property that is crucial for establishing
the
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-compactness of the
entropy dissipation
measures (
cf.
§
8.3
).
To achieve this, we exploit the linearity of the equation
and proceed by the Fourier methods, which were employed, for instance,
in
[
10
]
.
That is to say, for an appropriately
chosen
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
we postulate an expansion of the form:

H(\nu,s)=G_{\lambda}(\nu,s)+G_{\lambda+1}(\nu,s)+\text{remainder term},

where

(4.1)

G_{\lambda}(\nu,\vartheta):=[k(\nu)^{2}-\vartheta^{2}]_{+}^{\lambda}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\lambda>-1$},

and the remainder term is a more regular function. It is well-known (
cf.

[
20
]
) that,

\hat{G}_{\lambda}(\nu,\xi)=c_{\lambda}k(\nu)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}J_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $\lambda\in(-1,\infty)$},

with
c_{\lambda}:=\sqrt{\pi}2^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\lambda+1)

and the Bessel function
J_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}
of the first kind.
In the case when
\lambda
is integer-valued, these expressions
simplify to the weighted sums of trigonometric functions; see Appendix
A
.

The goal of this section is to prove the following two results concerning the regular kernel.
The proof of Proposition
4.1
readily generalizes
to a much wider range of
\gamma
,
while Proposition
4.2
is particular to the case:
\gamma=3
.

Proposition 4.1

(Existence of the Regular Kernel)
.

There exists a distributional solution
H^{\mathbf{r}}
, supported in
\mathcal{K}
, of the Cauchy problem
:

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu\nu}-k^{\prime 2}H^{\mathbf{r}}_{ss}=0\qquad\text{in $(0,\nu_{*}]\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&H^{\mathbf{r}}|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=\delta_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

given by the formula:

(4.3)

\displaystyle H^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,s)=a_{0}(\nu)G_{1}(\nu,s)+a_{1}(\nu)G_{2}(\nu,s)+g(\nu,s),

where
a_{j}(\nu),j=0,1
, satisfy that there exists
C>0
independent of
\nu
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle|a_{j}(\nu)|+\varrho(\nu)|a_{j}^{\prime}(\nu)|\leq C\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1$},

and the remainder term
g
is twice continuously differentiable,
with all of its first and second derivatives being
\alpha
-Hölder continuous
in
variable
s
for all
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

Our second main result of this section is concerned with the estimates
required to show the
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
–compactness of the entropy dissipation measures.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a constant
C_{\varphi}>0
independent of
(\nu,s)

such that, for all
(\nu,s)\in[0,\nu_{*}]\times\mathbb{R}
and
j=0,1,2
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{j}_{s}\big(\varrho(\nu)H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu}(\nu,\cdot)+H^{\mathbf{r}}_{ss}(\nu,\cdot)\big)*\varphi(s)\big|\leq C_{\varphi}\varrho(\nu),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\big|\varrho(\nu)\partial^{j}_{s}H^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu}(\nu,\cdot)*\varphi(s)\big|+\big|\partial^{j}_{s}H^{\mathbf{r}}_{s}(\nu,\cdot)*\varphi(s)\big|\leq C_{\varphi}.

We now provide the proofs of Propositions
4.1
–
4.2
.
We start with the analysis of regular kernel coefficients.

4.1.
Computing the regular kernel coefficients

We postulate an expansion of the Fourier transform of the regular kernel
with respect to
variable
s\in\mathbb{R}
in the form:

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},j}(\nu,\xi)+\hat{g}(\nu,\xi),

where

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},j}(\nu,\xi)=\alpha_{j}(\nu)\hat{f}_{j+1}(\xi k(\nu))
for
j=0,1
.

In what follows, for simplicity of notation, we write
\hat{f}
for

\hat{f}(\xi k(\nu))
.
We use the formulas provided in Lemma
A.5
to compute

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},0}_{\nu\nu}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},0}=\,\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}\hat{f}_{1}+\Big(\alpha_{0}k^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{4}\big(\alpha_{0}k^{\prime\prime}k+2\alpha_{0}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k\big)\Big)\xi^{2}\hat{f}_{2},

and impose that

\alpha_{0}k^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{4}\big(\alpha_{0}k^{\prime\prime}k+2\alpha_{0}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k\big)=0,

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{0}^{\prime}}{\alpha_{0}}=2\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{k^{\prime\prime}}{k^{\prime}},

whose closed-form solution is given by

(4.7)

\alpha_{0}(\nu)=c_{0}k(\nu)^{2}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

with
c_{0}
chosen to satisfy the condition:
\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r}}_{\nu}(0,\xi)=1
,

i.e.
,
H_{\nu}^{\mathbf{r}}|_{\nu=0}=\delta_{0}
; see §
4.4
.

For the second term, using again the formulas in Lemma
A.5
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},1}_{\nu\nu}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},1}

\displaystyle=\Big(\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime}-5\big(2\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}+\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime\prime}}{k}-6\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime 2}}{k^{2}}\big)\Big)\hat{f}_{2}

\displaystyle+4\Big(2\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}+\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime\prime}}{k}-6\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime 2}}{k^{2}}\Big)\hat{f}_{1},

whence we impose

(4.8)

4\Big(2\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\frac{k^{\prime}}{k}+\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime\prime}}{k}-6\alpha_{1}\frac{k^{\prime 2}}{k^{2}}\Big)+\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}=0,

that is,

\big(k^{-3}k^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{1}\big)^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{8}k^{-2}k^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},

whose solution is given by

(4.9)

\alpha_{1}(\nu)=-\frac{1}{8}k(\nu)^{3}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\nu}k(\tau)^{-2}k^{\prime}(\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

We therefore have

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},1}_{\nu\nu}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{r},1}=\,\big(\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{5}{4}\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}\big)\hat{f}_{2}-\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}\hat{f}_{1}.

It follows that
the remainder term
\hat{g}
must satisfy the following equation:

(4.10)

\hat{g}_{\nu\nu}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}\hat{g}=\ell\hat{f}_{2},

where

(4.11)

\ell(\nu)=-\big(\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)+\frac{5}{4}\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)\big).

Therefore, we define

(4.12)

R^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,s):=\ell(\nu)k(\nu)^{-5}G_{2}(\nu,s)

such that
\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,\xi)=\ell(\nu)f_{2}(\xi k(\nu))
, whence we impose that the remainder term satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &g_{\nu\nu}-k^{\prime 2}g_{ss}=R^{\mathbf{r}}\qquad\text{in $(0,\nu_{*}]\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&g|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&g_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In turn, the expansion for
H^{\mathbf{r}}
reads as

(4.13)

H^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,s)=a_{0}(\nu)G_{1}(\nu,s)+a_{1}(\nu)G_{2}(\nu,s)+g(\nu,s)

with

(4.14)

a_{0}(\nu):=\alpha_{0}(\nu)k(\nu)^{-3},\qquad a_{1}(\nu):=\alpha_{1}(\nu)k(\nu)^{-5}.

4.2.
Asymptotic analysis of the regular kernel

The purpose of this section is to collect the results
concerning the asymptotic behavior of the characteristic speed function
k(\nu)
in the vicinity of the vacuum.

4.2.1.
Properties of the characteristic speed

For the sake of being self-contained, we provide a brief outline of
the proof for the case:

\gamma=3
;
further details may be found in
[
40
, Appendix C]
.

Proposition 4.3

(Asymptotic Behavior of the Characteristic Speed)
.

The characteristic speed function
k(\nu)
admits the decomposition
:

(4.15)

k(\nu)=c_{\sharp}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}+c_{\flat}\nu+c_{l}\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}+L(\nu)\qquad\text{for $\nu\in[0,\nu_{*}]$},

where
c_{\sharp}=3^{\frac{1}{3}}
,
c_{\flat}
and
c_{l}
are the constants that can be determined.
Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(\nu_{*})>0
such that

(4.16)

|L^{(j)}(\nu)|\leq C\nu^{\frac{7}{3}-j}\qquad\text{for $\nu\in(0,\nu_{*}]$ and $j=0,\cdots,4$}.

Proof.

We write an asymptotic description of
\nu
, defined in (
2.2
), near the vacuum.
Using the Binomial Theorem and the Weierstraß M-test for
\varrho
sufficiently small, we have

\displaystyle\nu(\varrho)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j-1}\int_{0}^{\varrho}\tau^{2j}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\varrho^{3}\Big(1+\varrho^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{3}{2j+5}(-1)^{j+1}\varrho^{2j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\varrho^{3}\big(1-\frac{3}{5}\varrho^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varrho^{4})\big).

In turn, it is direct
to verify that
\nu(\varrho)=\mathcal{O}(\varrho^{3})
, whence

\varrho(\nu)=\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{1}{3}})
.
More precisely, using the Binomial Theorem again, we write

\displaystyle 3^{\frac{1}{3}}\nu(\varrho)^{\frac{1}{3}}=\varrho\big(1-\frac{3}{5}\varrho^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varrho^{4})\big)^{\frac{1}{3}}=\varrho-\frac{1}{5}\varrho^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\varrho^{5})=\varrho-\frac{3}{5}\nu(\varrho)+\mathcal{O}(\varrho^{5}),

which implies

(4.17)

\varrho(\nu)=3^{\frac{1}{3}}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{3}{5}\nu+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}).

It follows from
(
2.9
), the Binomial Theorem, and
the expression for the Mach number
M(\varrho)=\varrho^{-1}\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}
that,
for sufficiently small density,

\displaystyle k^{\prime}(\nu)=

\displaystyle\varrho(\nu)^{-2}\big(1-2\varrho(\nu)^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=3^{-\frac{2}{3}}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}\big(1+3^{\frac{2}{3}}c_{\flat}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{4}{3}})\big),

whence, by integrating the above,
the result with
c_{\sharp}
explicitly determined
can directly be obtained.
By refining the present strategy, it is direct to compute
c_{\flat}
and
c_{l}
explicitly
and to obtain the precise bounds on the remainder
L
.
∎

We remark in passing the following regularity and monotonicity properties of
k
.

Remark 4.4

(Regularity and Monotonicity of the Characteristic Speed)
.

Using representation (
2.8
),

k\in C([0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}])\cap C^{1}((0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}])\cap C^{\infty}((0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}))

and

0<k(\nu)\leq k(\nu_{\mathrm{cr}})\quad\text{for }\nu\in(0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}],\qquad k^{\prime}(\nu)>0\quad\text{for }\nu\in(0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}),

while
k(0)=0=k^{\prime}(\nu_{\mathrm{cr}})
.
Moreover, a direct calculation (
cf.

[
40
, Lemma C.4]
) shows

k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)=-\frac{M^{2}}{\varrho^{2}\sqrt{M^{2}-1}}\big(\varrho^{-2}+(M^{2}-1)\big)<0\qquad\text{for }\nu\in(0,\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}).

Note that the above explodes at the extremities
\nu=0
and
\nu=\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}
, where the hyperbolicity of the system is lost.

From Proposition
4.3
and Remark
4.4
, we derive the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

There exists
C=C(\nu_{*})>0
such that, for all
\nu\in(0,\nu_{*}]
,

(4.18)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\varrho(\nu)\leq C\nu^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad

\displaystyle C^{-1}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq k(\nu)\leq C\nu^{\frac{1}{3}},

\displaystyle C^{-1}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}\leq k^{\prime}(\nu)\leq C\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}},\quad

\displaystyle C^{-1}\nu^{-\frac{5}{3}}\leq-k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)\leq C\nu^{-\frac{5}{3}}.

The following cancellation result is a direct corollary of Proposition
4.3
,
which is a refinement of
[
40
, Lemma 4.16]
for the case:
\gamma=3
:

Corollary 4.6
.

There exists
C=C(\nu_{*})>0
such that, for all
\nu\in(0,\nu_{*}]
,

(4.19)

\Big|\big((2k^{\prime 2}+kk^{\prime\prime})-\frac{10}{9}c_{\sharp}c_{\flat}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}-\tilde{c}\big)^{(j)}(\nu)\Big|\leq C\nu^{\frac{2}{3}-j}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1,2$},

where
\tilde{c}=\frac{28}{9}c_{\sharp}c_{l}+2c_{\flat}^{2}
.

4.2.2.
Coefficients of the regular kernel in the vicinity of the vacuum

Throughout this subsection, we use, with a slight abuse of notation, the shorthand
\mathcal{O}(\nu^{d})

to denote a function
\phi(\nu)
for which
|\phi^{(j)}(\nu)|\leq C\nu^{d-j}
hold for integer
j
, for some constant
C>0
.
We adopt this convention to simplify the calculations contained in the proofs that follow.

Lemma 4.7
.

The following asymptotic expansions hold
:

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(\nu)=C_{\alpha_{0},0}\nu+C_{\alpha_{0},1}\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}+C_{\alpha_{0},2}\nu^{\frac{7}{3}}+r_{\alpha_{0}}(\nu),

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\nu)=C_{\alpha_{1},0}\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}+C_{\alpha_{1},1}\nu^{\frac{7}{3}}+r_{\alpha_{1}}(\nu),

with

\displaystyle C_{\alpha_{0},0}=c_{0}3^{\frac{1}{2}}c_{\sharp}^{\frac{3}{2}}\neq 0,\quad C_{\alpha_{0},1}=C_{\alpha_{0},0}\frac{c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\neq 0,\quad C_{\alpha_{0},2}=C_{\alpha_{0},0}\Big(\frac{11}{8}(\frac{c_{\flat}}{c_{\sharp}})^{2}-\frac{c_{l}}{2c_{\sharp}}\Big),

\displaystyle C_{\alpha_{1},0}=-\frac{5}{4}C_{\alpha_{0},1}\neq 0,\quad C_{\alpha_{1},1}=C_{\alpha_{1},0}\Big(\frac{7}{3}\big(\frac{2C_{\alpha_{0},2}}{5C_{\alpha_{0},1}}-\frac{c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\big)+\frac{3c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\Big),

where

r_{\alpha_{0}}
and
r_{\alpha_{1}}
satisfy the estimates
:

\displaystyle|r^{(j)}_{\alpha_{0}}(\nu)|+|r^{(j)}_{\alpha_{1}}(\nu)|\leq C\nu^{3-j}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1,2,3$}.

In addition,

\ell(\nu)
satisfies the bounds
:

(4.20)

|\ell(\nu)|+\nu|\ell^{\prime}(\nu)|\leq C\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Proof.

Using the previous formula for
\alpha_{0}
and the asymptotic expansion for
k(\nu)
near the vacuum, we obtain

\alpha_{0}(\nu)=c_{0}c_{\sharp}^{2}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}\big(1+\frac{c_{\flat}}{c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{c_{l}}{c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{2})\big)(\frac{c_{\sharp}}{3}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\frac{3c_{\flat}}{c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{5c_{l}}{c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{2})\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the Binomial Theorem,
we expand the terms in the brackets and obtain the result for
\alpha_{0}
, provided that

\nu
is sufficiently small.
Furthermore, we have

\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)=C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}+C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},1}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu)

with

C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}:=\frac{10}{9}C_{\alpha_{0},1},\quad C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},1}=\frac{28}{9}C_{\alpha_{0},2}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\nu)=

\displaystyle-\!\frac{3}{8}C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(1+\frac{3c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{4}{3}})\big)\!\int_{0}^{\nu}\!\tau^{-\frac{2}{3}}\Big(1+\big(\frac{C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},1}}{C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}}-\frac{7c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\big)\tau^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{\frac{4}{3}})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\!\frac{9}{8}C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}\nu^{\frac{5}{3}}\big(1+\frac{3c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{4}{3}})\big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{3}\big(\frac{C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},1}}{C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}}-\frac{7c_{\flat}}{2c_{\sharp}}\big)\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{4}{3}})\Big),

so that

\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)=C_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime},0}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{1}{3}})

with
C_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime},0}:=\frac{10}{9}C_{\alpha_{1},0}
. It follows that

\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{5}{4}\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime}=\big(\underbrace{C_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime\prime},0}+\frac{5}{4}C_{\alpha_{0}^{\prime\prime},0}}_{=0}\big)\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}),

and then the bounds of
\ell(\nu)
are directly obtained.
∎

Then estimates (
4.4
) on
coefficients
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are directly deduced from formulas (
4.14
)
and Lemma
4.7
.

4.3.
Analysis of the remainder term for the regular kernel

It remains to show that there exists a classical solution
g
of equation (
4.10
).
This is the focus of this section, which also provides some estimates on the Hölder regularity
of the remainder term.

Note that the singularity at the vacuum precludes us directly from appealing to
the Cauchy–Lipschitz Theorem to show the existence of a solution
g
of (
4.10
).
Therefore, we study the family of ordinary differential equations posed away from the vacuum for
\varepsilon>0
:

(4.21)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}\quad\,\,&&\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}],\\
&\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}|_{\nu=\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|_{\nu=\varepsilon}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Our strategy is as follows: the Cauchy–Lipschitz Theorem yields the existence
of a unique solution
\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)\in C^{2}([\varepsilon,\nu_{*}])
for each fixed
\varepsilon>0

and each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. We then prove the uniform estimates
of sequence
\{g^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
to pass to the limit strongly
by using the Ascoli–Arzelà Theorem, thereby obtaining a classical solution of (
4.10
).

4.3.1.
Fourier estimates for the remainder term

This subsection is independent from the specific choice of kernel
H^{\mathbf{r}}
.
We also emphasize that, throughout this subsection, the
\varepsilon
-superscript
bears no relation to the sequence of approximate problems.

For each
\varepsilon>0
,

h^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)\in C^{2}([\varepsilon,\nu_{*}])

is the unique solution of the Cauchy problem:

(4.22)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu,\xi)+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)=r^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)\quad&&\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}],\\
&h^{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon,\xi)=0,\\
&h^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\varepsilon,\xi)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

provided by the Cauchy–Lipschitz Theorem,
where
r^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)
is assumed to be continuously differentiable.
The Fourier variable
\xi
is interpreted as a parameter,
and the right-hand side of the above is assumed to take values in
\mathbb{R}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
so that
h^{\varepsilon}
is also real-valued for all such
\xi
.

The following is a version of
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
applicable to our setting.

Lemma 4.8

(Energy Estimate)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
h^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\xi)\in C^{2}([\varepsilon,\nu_{*}])

be the unique solution of (
4.22
).
Then there exists
C>0
independent of
(\varepsilon,\xi,r^{\varepsilon})
such that

(4.23)

h^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\nu,\xi)^{2}+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)^{2}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{3}I^{\varepsilon}_{j}(\nu,\xi)\qquad\text{for }(\nu,\xi)\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}]\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}),

where

(4.24)

\left.\begin{aligned} I^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\nu,\xi)&:=k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-2}\xi^{-2}r^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)^{2},\\
I^{\varepsilon}_{2}(\nu,\xi)&:=\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}k^{\prime}(y)^{-2}r^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\\
I^{\varepsilon}_{3}(\nu,\xi)&:=\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\frac{r_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)^{2}}{|k^{\prime}(\tau)k^{\prime\prime}(y)|+k^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition,
the following estimate holds
:

(4.25)

h^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\nu,\xi)^{2}+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)^{2}\leq C\nu\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}r^{\varepsilon}(\tau,\xi)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}]

for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
with constant
C>0
independent of
(\varepsilon,\xi,r^{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we omit the
\varepsilon
-superscript for clarity of exposition.
We divide the proof into two steps:

1. Multiplying (
4.22
) with
2h^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}
and using the product rule,
we have

(4.26)

\partial_{\nu}\left(h_{\nu}^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}h^{2}\right)=2rh_{\nu}+2\xi^{2}k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}h^{2}\qquad\,\,\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}].

Note that the second term on the right-hand side is negative, since
k^{\prime}(\nu)>0
and
k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)<0
for all
\nu\in(0,\nu_{*}]
.
Using the Cauchy–Young inequality, we have

\partial_{\nu}\left(h_{\nu}^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}h^{2}\right)\leq\nu^{-1}\left(h_{\nu}^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}h^{2}\right)+\nu r^{2}.

Multiplying by the integrating factor
\nu^{-1}
, we obtain

\partial_{\nu}\left(\nu^{-1}\left(h_{\nu}^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}h^{2}\right)\right)\leq r^{2},

which yields (
4.25
) upon integrating over interval
[\varepsilon,\nu]
.

2. We now go through a refined estimate starting from (
4.26
)
by employing
the signed term on the right-hand side.
An integration by parts shows that

\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}rh_{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=r(\nu,\xi)h(\nu,\xi)-\int^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}r_{\nu}h\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then we have

(4.27)

\frac{1}{2}\!\left(h_{\nu}(\nu,\xi)^{2}+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h(\nu,\xi)^{2}\right)\!=r(\nu,\xi)h(\nu,\xi)-\!\int^{\nu}_{\varepsilon}\!r_{\nu}h\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\xi^{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\!k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}h^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Multiplying and dividing the integrand in second term on the right-hand side
by
\xi\!\left(c_{1}|k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}|\!+\!k^{\prime 2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
for any
c_{1}\geq 0
and using the Cauchy–Young inequality,
we find the right-hand side is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}}r(\nu,\xi)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h(\nu,\xi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2c_{2}}\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\left(c_{1}|k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}|+k^{\prime 2}\right)^{-1}r_{\nu}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\left(k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}+\frac{c_{2}}{2}\left(c_{1}|k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}|+k^{\prime 2}\right)\right)\xi^{2}h^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for any choices of
c_{1}\geq 0
and
c_{2}>0
.
Fixing
c_{1}=1
and
c_{2}=2
, and observing that
k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}+|k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}|=0

due to the opposite signs of
k^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime\prime}
(
cf.
Remark
4.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(h_{\nu}(\nu,\xi)^{2}+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h(\nu,\xi)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq I_{1}(\nu,\xi)+\frac{1}{4}\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\left(|k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}|+k^{\prime 2}\right)^{-1}r_{\nu}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}h^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Define
\phi(\nu):=\frac{1}{4}\big(h_{\nu}(\nu,\xi)^{2}+k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}\xi^{2}h(\nu,\xi)^{2}\big)
.
Then

(4.28)

\displaystyle\phi(\nu)\leq I_{1}(\nu,\xi)+\frac{1}{4}I_{3}(\nu,\xi)+4\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\phi(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

An application of Grönwall’s inequality
yields

\phi(\nu)\leq\big(I_{1}(\nu,\xi)+\frac{1}{4}I_{3}(\nu,\xi)\big)+\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\big(I_{1}(\tau,\xi)+\frac{1}{4}I_{3}(\tau,\xi)\big)e^{4\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

It follows that there exists
C_{0}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
and
\xi
,
such that

\phi(\nu)\leq I_{1}(\nu,\xi)+I_{3}(\nu,\xi)+C_{0}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\big(I_{1}(\tau,\xi)+I_{3}(\tau,\xi)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}].

Finally, we observe that
I_{2}(\nu,\xi)=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}I_{1}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
and

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}I_{3}(y,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\xi^{-2}\Big(\int_{\varepsilon}^{\tau^{\prime}}\frac{r_{\nu}(\tau,\xi)^{2}}{|k^{\prime}(\tau)k^{\prime\prime}(\tau)|+k^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq(\nu-\varepsilon)\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\frac{r_{\nu}(\tau,\xi)^{2}}{|k^{\prime}(\tau)k^{\prime\prime}(\tau)|+k^{\prime}(\tau)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{*}I_{3}(\nu,\xi).

Then the result follows.
∎

4.3.2.
Existence and boundedness of the remainder term for the regular kernel

Lemma 4.9
.

There exists
\hat{g}(\cdot,\xi)\in C^{2}([0,\nu_{*}])
that is the solution of the Cauchy problem
:

(4.29)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{g}_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{g}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}\qquad&&\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}],\\
&\hat{g}|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&\hat{g}_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

such that
the uniform estimates hold
:

(4.30)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{g}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\frac{\nu^{\frac{7}{3}-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{4-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1,2$},

where
C>0
is some constant independent of
(\nu,\xi)
.

Proof.

The proof consists of three steps.

1.
Uniform estimates
:
Using the bounds in (
4.20
) and
the asymptotic expansions for the Bessel function contained in
\hat{f}_{2}
,
we obtain that the inhomogeneity on the right-hand side of (
4.10
),

\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,\xi)=A(\nu)\hat{f}_{2}(\xi k(\nu))
, satisfies

(4.31)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,\xi)|\leq\frac{C\nu^{\frac{1}{3}-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{3-j}}\qquad\text{for }j=0,1.

Recall terms
\{I_{j}\}_{j=1}^{3}
in Lemma
4.8
,
and substitute
r^{\varepsilon}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
into the lemmas
in §
4.3.1
.
Then, for
\xi\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq Ck^{\prime-2}\xi^{-2}\nu^{\frac{2}{3}}(1+|\xi k|)^{-6}\leq C\nu^{2}k^{2}|\xi k|^{-8}\leq C\nu^{\frac{8}{3}}|\xi k|^{-8},

while

I_{2}\leq C\xi^{-2}\int_{0}^{\nu}\tau^{2}|\xi k(\tau)|^{-6}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\xi^{-8}\nu\leq C\nu^{\frac{11}{3}}|\xi k|^{-8}.

Finally, we have

I_{3}\leq C\xi^{-2}\int_{0}^{\nu}\tau|\xi k(\tau)|^{-4}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\xi^{-6}\int_{0}^{\nu}\tau^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\nu^{\frac{8}{3}}|\xi k|^{-6}.

It follows that there exists
C>0
independent of
(\nu,\xi,\varepsilon)
such that

(4.32)

|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|^{2}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu^{\frac{8}{3}}|\xi k|^{-6},

which is accurate for

|\xi k|\geq 1
.
Meanwhile, for
|\xi k|<1
, the second estimate of Lemma
4.8
implies

(4.33)

|\hat{g}_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu\int_{0}^{\nu}\tau^{\frac{2}{3}}(1+|\xi k(\tau)|)^{-6}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\nu^{\frac{8}{3}},

so that

|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|\leq C\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}
and,
by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus,
|g^{\varepsilon}|\leq\nu^{\frac{7}{3}}
.
Combining (
4.32
) with (
4.33
), we obtain

|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|^{2}+\xi^{2}k^{\prime 2}|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu^{\frac{8}{3}}(1+|\xi k|)^{-6},

from which we deduce

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq\frac{C\nu^{\frac{7}{3}-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{4-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1$}.

In turn, using equation (
4.10
) to estimate the second derivative,
we have

(4.34)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq\frac{C\nu^{\frac{7}{3}-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{4-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1,2$}.

2.
Equicontinuity of second derivatives
:
We now prove the uniform equicontinuity of the second derivatives
by employing a strategy akin to the one in
[
40
, Lemma 4.35]
.
For
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}]
with
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
,
using equation (
4.21
) yields

\displaystyle|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{2},\xi)|

(4.35)

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{2}|k^{\prime}(\nu_{1})^{2}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}(\nu_{1},\xi)-k^{\prime}(\nu_{2})^{2}\hat{g}(\nu_{2},\xi)|+|\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu_{2},\xi)|.

We bound both terms on the right-hand side of the previous inequality.
The first term may be rewritten and estimated as

\displaystyle\xi^{2}\left|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\partial_{\nu}\big(k^{\prime 2}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{2}\left|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}2k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right|+\xi^{2}\left|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}k^{\prime 2}\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\xi^{2}\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu=C\xi^{2}|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|,

where we have used the uniform bounds (
4.34
).

Meanwhile, using estimates (
4.31
) and the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu_{2},\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}|\partial_{\nu}\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq C\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{\nu_{\mathrm{cr}}}\nu^{-\frac{5}{6}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{\frac{4}{5}}\left(\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1-\frac{4}{5}}\leq C|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|^{\frac{1}{5}}.

Then,
returning to (
4.3.2
), we deduce

(4.36)

|\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{2},\xi)|\leq C_{\xi}|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|^{\frac{1}{5}}

for some constant
C_{\xi}>0
depending on
\xi
, but independent of
\varepsilon
.

3.
Passage to the limit:

Using the Ascoli–Arzelà Theorem with the uniform derivative bounds
of
\hat{g}^{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{g}_{\nu}^{\varepsilon}

and
the uniform equicontinuity estimate (
4.36
),
we find that, for each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
a subsequence
\{\hat{g}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}>0}

such that
\hat{g}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\cdot,\xi)\to\hat{g}(\cdot,\xi)
strongly in
C^{2}([0,\nu_{*}])
.
The limit function
\hat{g}
is manifestly a classical solution of (
4.29
)
and satisfies estimates (
4.30
).
∎

4.3.3.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any

\|\partial_{s}^{j}g(\nu,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\alpha}\nu^{2-\frac{j+\alpha}{3}},\quad\|\partial^{j}_{\nu}g(\nu,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\alpha}\nu^{2-j-\frac{\alpha}{3}}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for $j=0,1,2$},

∎

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}_{\nu\nu}

\displaystyle+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}=\beta_{0}^{\prime\prime}\hat{f}_{-2}+\Big(\frac{1}{2}k\big(\beta_{0}k^{\prime\prime}+2\beta_{0}^{\prime}k^{\prime}\big)+\beta_{0}k^{\prime 2}\Big)\xi^{2}\hat{f}_{-1},

that is,

(5.4)

\beta_{0}(\nu)=d_{0}k(\nu)^{-1}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

with
d_{0}
chosen such that
\lim_{\nu\to 0^{+}}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,\xi)=1
; see §
5.4
. Then we obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}_{\nu\nu}

\displaystyle+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}=\beta_{0}^{\prime\prime}\hat{f}_{-2}.

Meanwhile, using the formulas provided in Lemma
A.6
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},1}_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},1}=

\displaystyle\Big(\beta_{1}^{\prime\prime}k^{2}+6\beta_{1}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k+6\beta_{1}k^{\prime 2}+3\beta_{1}k^{\prime\prime}k\Big)\hat{f}_{-1}

\displaystyle-2\Big(4\beta_{1}k^{\prime 2}+2\beta_{1}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k+\beta_{1}k^{\prime\prime}k\Big)\hat{f}_{-2}.

We therefore set
\beta_{1}
to be the solution of

2\beta_{1}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k+\beta_{1}\big(4k^{\prime 2}+k^{\prime\prime}k\big)=\frac{1}{2}\beta_{0}^{\prime\prime},

that is,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\big(\beta_{1}k^{2}k^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}\big)=\frac{1}{4}\beta_{0}^{\prime\prime}kk^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}},

whose closed-form
solution is

(5.7)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(\nu)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}k(\nu)^{-2}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\nu}\beta_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\tau)k(\tau)k^{\prime}(\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then

\displaystyle\big(\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}+\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},1}\big)_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\big(\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},0}+\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},1}\big)=\ell_{1}\hat{f}_{-1},

where the coefficient is given by the exact formula:

(5.8)

\ell_{1}(\nu)=\beta_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)^{2}+6\beta_{1}^{\prime}(\nu)k^{\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)+6\beta_{1}(\nu)k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}+3\beta_{1}(\nu)k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)k(\nu).

Using again the formulas provided in Lemma
A.6
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},2}_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s},2}=

\displaystyle\big(\beta_{2}^{\prime\prime}k^{4}+6\beta_{2}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k^{3}+6\beta_{2}k^{\prime 2}k^{2}+3\beta_{2}k^{\prime\prime}k^{3}\big)\hat{f}_{0}

\displaystyle+2\big(6\beta_{2}k^{\prime 2}k^{2}+2\beta_{2}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k^{3}+\beta_{2}k^{\prime\prime}k^{3}\big)\hat{f}_{-1}.

Thus, we choose
\beta_{2}
to be the solution of the equation:

2\big(6\beta_{2}k^{\prime 2}k^{2}+2\beta_{2}^{\prime}k^{\prime}k^{3}+\beta_{2}k^{\prime\prime}k^{3}\big)=-\ell_{1},

which can be rewritten as

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\big(\alpha_{2}k^{3}k^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}\big)=-\frac{1}{4}\beta_{1}k^{\prime-\frac{1}{2}},

whose solution is given by the explicit formula

(5.10)

\beta_{2}(\nu)=-\frac{1}{4}k(\nu)^{-3}k^{\prime}(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\nu}\ell_{1}(\tau)k^{\prime}(\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

We define

(5.11)

\displaystyle R^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,s):=\ell_{2}(\nu)k(\nu)^{-1}G_{0}(\nu,s),

(5.12)

\displaystyle\ell_{2}(\nu):=-k(\nu)^{2}\big(\beta_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)^{2}+6\beta_{2}^{\prime}(\nu)k^{\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)+6\beta_{2}(\nu)k^{\prime}(\nu)^{2}+3\beta_{2}(\nu)k^{\prime\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)\big),

such that
\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,\xi)=\ell_{2}(\nu)f_{0}(\xi k(\nu))
.

We therefore define the remainder term
\hat{h}
to be the solution of the Cauchy problem:

(5.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h_{\nu\nu}-k^{\prime 2}h_{ss}=R^{\mathbf{s}}\qquad\text{in $(0,\nu_{*}]\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&h|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&h_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In turn, the expansion for
H^{\mathbf{s}}
reads as

(5.14)

H^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,s)=\beta_{0}(\nu)k(\nu)^{3}G_{-2}(\nu,s)+\beta_{1}(\nu)k(\nu)^{3}G_{-1}(\nu,s)+\beta_{2}(\nu)^{3}G_{0}(\nu,s)+h(\nu,s)

with

(5.15)

b_{0}(\nu):=\beta_{0}(\nu)k(\nu)^{3},\quad b_{1}(\nu):=\beta_{1}(\nu)k(\nu)^{3},\quad b_{2}(\nu):=\beta_{2}(\nu)k(\nu)^{3}.

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(\nu)=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(\nu)

∎

5.3.
Analysis of the remainder term for the singular kernel

We now analyze the remainder term
h(\nu,s)
for the singular kernel.

5.3.1.
Existence and boundedness of the remainder term for the singular kernel

As in §
4.3.2
, we show the existence of
h(\nu,s)
solving the Cauchy problem (
5.13
)
by studying the limit of solutions
\{h^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
of the translated problems:

(5.16)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}\qquad\,\,\text{for $\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}]$},\\
&\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}|_{\nu=\varepsilon}=0,\\
&\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|_{\nu=\varepsilon}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Once again, we stress that the
\varepsilon
-superscript
bears no relation to the sequence
of approximate problems throughout this subsection.

Lemma 5.7
.

There exists
\hat{h}(\cdot,\xi)\in C^{2}([0,\nu_{*}])
satisfying the Cauchy problem:

(5.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{h}_{\nu\nu}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}\hat{h}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}\qquad\,\,\text{for }\nu\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}],\\
&\hat{h}|_{\nu=0}=0,\\
&\hat{h}_{\nu}|_{\nu=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

such that
the uniform estimates hold
:

(5.18)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{h}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\frac{\nu^{2-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{2-j}},\qquad j=0,1,2,

where
C>0
is some constant independent of
(\nu,\xi)
.

Proof.

The proof consists of three steps.

1.
Uniform estimates
:
Using the explicit formulas of Lemmas
A.4
and
A.6
,
and the estimate of
\ell_{2}
provided by Corollary
5.6
, we deduce that the forcing term
\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}
satisfies

(5.19)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\frac{\nu^{-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{1-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1$},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
(\nu,\xi,\varepsilon)
.

Recall terms
\{I_{j}^{\varepsilon}\}_{j=1}^{3}
in Lemma
4.8

and substitute
r^{\varepsilon}=\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Using the asymptotic description of
k(\nu)
, we obtain that, for
\xi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle|I_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}\xi^{-2}(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{-2}\leq C\nu^{2}|\xi k(\nu)|^{-4}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle|I_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\tau^{\frac{4}{3}}(1+|\xi k(\tau)|)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\nu^{3}|\xi k(\nu)|^{-4},

\displaystyle|I_{3}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\xi^{-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\nu}\tau^{-2+\frac{7}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C\xi^{-2}\nu^{\frac{4}{3}}\leq C\nu^{2}|\xi k(\nu)|^{-2}.

By Lemma
4.8
, we see that, for some constant
C>0
independent of
(\nu,\xi,\varepsilon)
,

(5.20)

|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}|h^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu^{2}|\xi k|^{-2},

which is accurate for
|\xi k|\geq 1
.
On the other hand, for
|\xi k|<1
, we use the second estimate in Lemma
4.8
to obtain

(5.21)

|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}|h^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu^{2},

so that
|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|\leq C\nu
,
which implies that

|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}|\leq C\nu^{2}
.
In turn, by combining (
5.20
) with (
5.21
),
we have

|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}|^{2}+k^{\prime 2}\xi^{2}|h^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\nu^{2}(1+|\xi k|)^{-2},

so that

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\frac{\nu^{2-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{2-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1$}.

Finally, using equation (
5.16
) to estimate the second derivative,
we obtain

(5.22)

|\partial^{j}_{\nu}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}(\nu,\xi)|\leq C\frac{\nu^{2-j}}{(1+|\xi k(\nu)|)^{2-j}}\qquad\mbox{for $j=0,1,2$},

where
C>0
is independent of
(\nu,\xi,\varepsilon)
.

2.
Equicontinuity of second derivatives
:
For
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in[\varepsilon,\nu_{*}]
with
\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}
,
we use equation (
4.21
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{2},\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{2}|k^{\prime}(\nu_{1})^{2}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}(\nu_{1},\xi)-k^{\prime}(\nu_{2})^{2}\hat{h}(\nu_{2},\xi)|+|\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu_{2},\xi)|.

We now bound both terms on the right-hand side.
The first term may be rewritten and estimated as

\displaystyle\xi^{2}\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\partial_{\nu}\big(k^{\prime 2}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{2}\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}2k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|+\xi^{2}\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}k^{\prime 2}\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\xi^{2}\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

For the second term, we use
\hat{R}^{\mathbf{r}}(\nu,\xi)=\ell_{2}(\nu)\hat{f}_{0}(\xi k(\nu))
,
the connection formula
\hat{f}_{0}^{\prime}(\xi)=-\frac{\xi}{2}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)

(
cf.
Lemma
A.6
), and Corollary
5.6
to write

\displaystyle|\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{R}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu_{2},\xi)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\big(\ell_{2}(\nu)\hat{f}_{0}(\xi k(\nu))\big)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\ell_{2}^{\prime}(\nu)\hat{f}_{0}(\xi k(\nu))\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|+\bigg|\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\ell_{2}(\nu)\xi k^{\prime}(\nu)\hat{f}_{0}^{\prime}(\xi k(\nu))\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu+C\xi^{2}\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}k^{\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)|\hat{f}_{1}(\xi k(\nu))|\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Employing
\hat{f}_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and the Hölder inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle|\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{1},\xi)-\hat{h}^{\varepsilon}_{\nu\nu}(\nu_{2},\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\xi^{2})\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\xi^{2})\bigg(\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\nu^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{\nu_{1}}^{\nu_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\xi^{2})|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C>0
is independent of
(\nu,\xi,\varepsilon)
.

3.
Passage to the limit
:
Using the uniform equicontinuity estimate (
5.23
),
we invoke the Ascoli–Arzelà Theorem to obtain that,
for each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a subsequence
\{\hat{h}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}>0}

such that
\hat{h}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\cdot,\xi)\to\hat{h}(\cdot,\xi)
strongly in
C^{2}([0,\nu_{*}])
.
The strong convergence ensures that the limit function
\hat{h}
is a classical solution
of (
5.17
) and satisfies the estimates in (
5.18
).
∎

5.3.2.
Hölder continuity of remainder term for the singular kernel

Lemma 5.8
.

For any
\alpha\in[0,1)
, there exists
C_{\alpha}>0
such that the remainder term
h(\nu,s)
satisfies

\|h(\nu,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{\alpha}\nu^{\frac{5-\alpha}{3}}.

Proof.

Using estimates (
5.18
), we have

\|h(\nu,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{\alpha}|\hat{h}(\nu,\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C\nu^{2}k(\nu)^{-1-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\xi|^{\alpha}}{(1+|\xi|)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Then the result follows.
∎

It follows from the previous result that, since
\hat{h}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})

satisfies (
5.17
), the remainder term
h(\nu,s)

is a distributional solution of the Cauchy problem (
5.13
).

5.4.
Proof of Proposition
5.1

We first observe
the initial data of
H^{\mathbf{s}}
at
\nu=0
,
the proof of which follows directly from the asymptotic description of
coefficients
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j=0}^{2}
in Lemma
5.3
.

Lemma 5.9

(Initial Data for the Singular Kernel)
.

\,H^{\mathbf{s}}|_{\nu=0}=\delta_{0}
.

Proof.

Notice that

\hat{H}^{\mathbf{s}}(\nu,\xi)=\beta_{0}\hat{f}_{-2}+\underbrace{\beta_{1}k^{2}}_{=\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{2}{3}})}\hat{f}_{-1}+\underbrace{\beta_{2}k^{4}}_{=\mathcal{O}(\nu^{\frac{4}{3}})}\hat{f}_{0}+\hat{g}.

Since
\hat{f}_{-1},\hat{f}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

by Lemma

6.
Compensated Compactness Framework

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem
2.3
,
based on our detailed entropy analysis in §3–§5.
In doing so, we establish a compactness framework
that leads to the strong convergence of the approximate or exact solutions
to an entropy solution of the limit problem
for system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
);
see §
9
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

The uniform bounds in (
2.12
)–(
2.13
) imply the existence of a subsequence (still denoted)

\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
converging weakly-* to a function
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}
and the existence of a Young measure
\{\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}\}_{\mathbf{x}}

characterizing this limit and its interactions with continuous functions.
The rest of the proof is concerned with upgrading the weak-* convergence
to almost everywhere convergence (taking a further subsequence when necessary)
by reducing the Young measure to the Dirac mass
concentrated at
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})
away from the vacuum. Once the almost everywhere convergence is known,
obtaining the strong convergence in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
is an elementary argument
by using the uniform boundedness of
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and the required interpolation.

We divide the rest of the proof into three steps.

1.
Hyperbolicity, genuine nonlinearity, and Riemann invariant coordinates
:
Recall from Proposition
3.1
(§
3.1
) that,
since the sequence of approximate solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the bounds in (
2.12
)–(
2.13
),
the nonlinear system (
3.5
) written in terms of
\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})

is strictly hyperbolic away from the vacuum;
however, the strict hyperbolicity fails at the vacuum.
Additionally, again by Lemma
3.1
,
the system is genuinely nonlinear,
including the states at the vacuum.

Recall from Proposition
3.2
that

W_{\pm}(\mathbf{u})=\vartheta(\mathbf{u})\pm k(|\mathbf{u}|),

provided by (
3.1
) and (
3.9
),
are Riemann invariants
for the conservative system (
3.5
) for
\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})
.
Furthermore, the change of coordinates:
\mathbf{u}\mapsto(W_{-}(\mathbf{u}),W_{+}(\mathbf{u}))
is bijective
on region
\{q_{\mathrm{cr}}\leq|\mathbf{u}|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\}
.
We henceforth employ the curvilinear coordinate system
(W_{-},W_{+})
in the phase-space and
denote the image measure of
\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}
for this invertible coordinate change by
\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{x}}
,
which is precisely the Young measure generated
by sequence
\{(W_{-}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}),W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}))\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}
.

2.
Reduction of the Young measure
:
Assumption (
2.14
) indicates that the entropy dissipation measures for entropy pairs
generated by
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
satisfy the
H^{-1}
-compactness including at the vacuum state.
In turn, the Young measure satisfies the usual Tartar–Murat commutation relation
for all entropy pairs generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
,
while these kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
provide two linearly independent families of entropy pairs.
Then we can employ the arguments in DiPerna
[
16
]
or Serre
[
41
]
for
the genuinely nonlinear conservative system (
3.5
)
to show that
the support of the family of Young measures generated by the sequence of approximate solutions
is one point in the Riemann invariant coordinates on the phase-plane,
i.e.
,

\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}=\delta_{(W_{-}(\mathbf{x}),W_{+}(\mathbf{x}))}\qquad\text{a.e.~}\mathbf{x},

for some functions
(W_{-},W_{+})\in L^{\infty}
.

3.
Strong convergence of the velocity
:
In turn, using the coordinate transformation:
(W_{-},W_{+})\mapsto\mathbf{u}(W_{-},W_{+})

in Lemma
3.2
, which is well-defined everywhere, including at the vacuum,
we deduce that the original measure
\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}
is also a Dirac mass in the
\mathbf{u}
-phase plane.
By the uniqueness of weak-* limits, we have

\mathscr{Y}_{\mathbf{x}}=\delta_{\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})}\quad\text{a.e.~}\mathbf{x}.

It follows that
there exists a subsequence (still denoted by)
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

such that (
2.15
) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

Observe that the mapping:
\mathbf{u}\mapsto\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{u})
provided by (
3.4
)
is not invertible at the vacuum, since

|u(\mathbf{Z})|=\sqrt{q^{2}_{\mathrm{cav}}-Z^{2}_{2}}
.
However, the sign cannot be determined solely from the entries of
\mathbf{Z}
,
whence
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{Z})
is not well-defined.
We emphasize that this is not a limitation for us, as our compensated compactness framework
is devised
to obtain
\mathbf{u}
directly as a solution of (
3.5
);
that is, it is not the case that we first obtain
\mathbf{Z}

solving (
3.5
)
and then claim that
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{Z})
solves (
1.1
).

Proof of Theorem

.

The proof is divided into four steps.

1. First, assumptions (i)–(ii)

and
H^{\mathbf{s}}

2. For the special entropy pair

we conclude that

is compact for

(

[
36
]

\{\mu^{\varepsilon}_{*}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\quad\mbox{is pre-compact in $W^{-1,p}_{\rm loc}$

The same argument shows that, for any entropy pair
\mathbf{Q}_{H}
determined
from the Loewner–Morawetz relations
via a generator
H
generated by kernels

and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
and satisfying

),

\{{\div_{\mathbf{x}}}\,\mathbf{Q}_{H}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}\qquad\mbox{is pre-compact in $W^{-1,p}_{\rm loc}$ for $p\in(1,\infty)$}.

3. In this step, we apply an idea from Chen
[
6
]
to transfer the compactness of

\{\mu^{\varepsilon}_{*}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

to the dissipation measures for any regular entropy pair,

).

Let
\mathbf{Q}_{H}
be any entropy pair determined from the Loewner–Morawetz relations
via a generator
H
generated by kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
.
It is immediate from Propositions
4.2
and
5.2

that there exists a positive constant

estimates (invariant regions).
Note that it is not clear from equations (
8.2
)
that
0\leq\varrho^{\varepsilon}\leq 1
holds
a priori
,
whence the Bernoulli relation
q(\varrho)=\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}
is not well-defined for the approximate solutions.
For this reason, we consider the auxiliary system:

(8.3)

\begin{cases}\div\big(\varrho^{\varepsilon}\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big)=\varepsilon\div\big((1-\displaystyle\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\big),\\
\div\big(\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon}-\frac{\pi}{2})\big)=\varepsilon\Delta\vartheta^{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

where the speed has been replaced by

(8.4)

\tilde{q}(\varrho)=(1-\varrho_{+}^{2})_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}},

with
(\cdot)_{\pm}
as the positive/negative part of the function.
While the derivative of
\tilde{q}
is a distribution,

\tilde{q}^{2}
is Lipschitz and satisfies the formula:

(8.5)

\tilde{q}(\varrho)\tilde{q}^{\prime}(\varrho)=-\varrho\qquad\,\text{for all }\varrho\in(0,1).

In §
8.2
, we show that a solution of the above auxiliary system is
also a solution of the original system (
8.2
);
that is, it is consistent with the potential flow system.

The invariant regions computed for
(
8.3
) are then used to obtain a dissipation estimate,
after which the method laid out in
[
11
, §10]
yields
the existence of a solution that fulfils the properties required in Theorem
8.1
.
This existence proof is carried out through a standard fixed point argument for the equivalent system

(8.6)

\begin{cases}\Delta\sigma=\varepsilon^{-1}\div\big(\varrho(\sigma)q(\sigma)\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)\big),\\
\Delta\vartheta=\varepsilon^{-1}\div\big(q(\sigma)\mathbf{e}(\vartheta-\frac{\pi}{2})\big),\end{cases}

where

\sigma(\varrho)=\int_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{1-2s^{2}}{1-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=2\varrho-\tanh^{-1}(\varrho)

so that

\sigma^{\prime}(\varrho)=1-\frac{c(\varrho)^{2}}{q(\varrho)^{2}},\quad\textit{i.e.},\quad\Delta\sigma=\div\big((1-\frac{c(\varrho)^{2}}{q(\varrho)^{2}})\nabla\varrho\big).

This approach is standard so that the details are omitted (see
[
11
]
).
Henceforth, we focus exclusively on the required
a priori

L^{\infty}
and dissipation estimates.

The first result concerns the invariant region estimate,
for which we recall the characteristic speed
k
expressed in (
3.9
) as a function of
q
.

Proposition 8.1

(Uniform Upper Bound on the Density)
.

Let
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

be a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
) with the following boundary conditions
:

on the obstacle boundary

\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
,

(8.7)

\begin{cases}\varepsilon\big(1-\frac{c^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}{\tilde{q}^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}\big)\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}=\left|\varrho^{\varepsilon}\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\,\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\right|,\\
\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}=0,\end{cases}

together with the Dirichlet-type condition on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
:

\varrho^{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}=\varrho_{\infty},\qquad\vartheta^{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}=0,

where
\varrho_{\infty}=\sqrt{1-q^{2}_{\infty}}
.
Then there exists
\varrho_{*}\in(0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}})
, depending only
\varrho_{\infty}

but independent of
\varepsilon
, such that, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}
,

0<\varrho^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\varrho_{*}<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}.

We remark that the previous proposition implies the
strict positivity
of the densities
\varrho^{\varepsilon}

and speeds
q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})
for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
.
Furthermore, the invariant regions
imply
\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})=q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})
.
Thus, we deduce that a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
)
is also a solution of (
8.2
) (see Lemma
8.8
).
This means that, at this point, the Bernoulli relation:

\varrho(q)=\sqrt{1-q^{2}}

can be meaningfully interpreted as the equivalent nonlinear constraint:

q(\varrho)=\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}},

with the change of coordinate:
\varrho\mapsto q

and its inverse mapping being justified on the entire domain as
q^{\prime}(\varrho)>0
for
\varrho\in(0,\varrho_{*}]
.
Furthermore, we remark that the
a priori
estimates of Proposition
8.1

imply
q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\geq q(\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}})
and

0<1-\frac{\varrho_{*}^{2}}{1-\varrho_{*}^{2}}\leq 1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\leq 1,

in view of
\varrho_{*}<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.
It follows from the above that the approximate problem (
8.2
)
and the auxiliary problem (
8.3
) are
strongly elliptic
quasilinear systems.
Similar arguments show that the changes of coordinate:
\varrho\mapsto\nu
and
\varrho\mapsto\sigma

are also justified and invertible on the entire domain,
in view of relations
\nu^{\prime}(\varrho)=M^{-2}>0
and
\sigma^{\prime}(\varrho)=1-M^{-2}>0

when the Mach number
M\in(1,\infty)
(
cf.
§
2.1
)
which is equivalent to
\varrho\in(0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}})
.
In summary, away from the critical points (
M=1
) and the stagnation points (
M=0
),
variables
\varrho
,
q
,
\nu
, and
\sigma
differ from each other by composition with a smooth function.
Hence, once the
a priori

L^{\infty}
estimates of Proposition
8.1
are established,
we refer indistinctly to a viscous solution as being parameterized in Cartesian coordinates
(u,v)
,
or in polar coordinates
(q,\vartheta)
,
or any of the following parameterizations
(\varrho,\vartheta)
,
(\nu,\vartheta)
, or
(\sigma,\vartheta)
.

Similar arguments to those in the proof of Proposition
8.1
are used
to obtain the
L^{\infty}
estimates of the unknown angle
\vartheta^{\varepsilon}
.
Note carefully that, as the problem is naturally interpreted to be periodic in
\vartheta
,
one might mistakenly believe that uniform bounds on the angle are unnecessary.
However, the Loewner–Morawetz entropy framework requires that
|\vartheta\nabla\vartheta|^{2}

be integrable, which is not the case when
\vartheta
is merely represented as a piecewise linear
periodic function with jump discontinuities.
In turn, we have the second invariant region result.

Proposition 8.2

(Uniform Estimates of the Angle Functions)
.

Let
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

) with the prescribed boundary conditions
of Propositions

.

\vartheta_{*},\vartheta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}

on
\varrho_{\infty}

\vartheta_{*}\leq\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\vartheta^{*}\qquad\mbox{for a.e.~$\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}$}.

As already mentioned, we then prove the main dissipation estimate needed to show the required compactness
of the entropy dissipation measures.

Proposition 8.3

(Main Dissipation Estimate)
.

Let
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})
be a solution of
the auxiliary system (
8.3
) with the prescribed boundary conditions
of
Propositions

–
8.2
,

,

, such that, for all

(8.8)

\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}}\Big(|\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}{q^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}\big)\frac{1}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}|\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq C_{\mathscr{D}}.

Using the previous result, we are in a position to prove that any solution of

) yields the
H^{-1}
-compactness of

Proposition 8.4

.

Let

) with the prescribed boundary conditions

.

At this stage, one may employ the previous propositions and

) to

.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
\varrho_{\infty}\in(q_{\rm cr},q_{\rm cav})
.
Then the mixed Dirichlet–Neumann problem
:

(8.9)

\begin{cases}\div\big(\varrho^{\varepsilon}q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big)=\varepsilon\div\big((1-\displaystyle\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\big),\\
\div\big(q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon}-\frac{\pi}{2})\big)=\varepsilon\Delta\vartheta^{\varepsilon},\\
\varepsilon\big(1-\frac{c^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}{q^{2}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})}\big)\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}}=\left|\varrho^{\varepsilon}q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\,\mathbf{e}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\right|,\\
\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}}=0,\\
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})|_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}=(\varrho_{\infty},0),\end{cases}

admits a
solution
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})
. Furthermore, this solution satisfies
the conditions of Theorem
8.1
.

We note in passing that the dissipation estimate in Proposition
8.3
implies that
the viscosity terms
V_{1}^{\varepsilon}
and
V_{2}^{\varepsilon}
, defined in (
8.1
) in
terms of the solution of (
8.9
), converge to
\mathbf{0}
strongly in
H^{-1}(\mathscr{D})
.

Throughout this section, except stated otherwise, we use the convention:
\nabla=(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})
;
analogously, all divergences are taken with respect to the physical space variable
\mathbf{x}
.

8.1.
A priori
L^{\infty}
estimates

We consider the invariant regions of the system.
In what follows, we use

W_{\pm}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}):=W_{\pm}(\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}),\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})))
.
We recall from
[
11
, Proposition 4.1]
that, in the case that
\gamma=3
with
\varrho\in(0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}})
,
the Riemann invariants satisfy the elliptic equation:

(8.10)

\frac{\tilde{q}c}{\sqrt{\tilde{q}^{2}-c^{2}}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\pm}\!\cdot\!\nabla W^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}\mp\Delta W^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}=-\varepsilon\frac{2c^{3}}{\varrho^{2}\tilde{q}^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{q}^{2}-c^{2}}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2},

where we have used relation (
8.5
)
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\pm}=(\lambda^{1}_{\pm},\lambda^{2}_{\pm})
with

\lambda^{1}_{\pm}=-\sin\vartheta\pm\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{q}^{2}-c^{2}}}{c}\cos\vartheta,\qquad\lambda^{2}_{\pm}=\cos\vartheta\pm\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{q}^{2}-c^{2}}}{c}\sin\vartheta;

also see §
3.2
.

We show that the maximum value of
W_{+}^{\varepsilon}
and the minimum value of
W_{-}^{\varepsilon}
must
occur on boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
.
Indeed, our choice of the boundary conditions and relations (
3.8
) lead to

(8.11)

\nabla W^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}=\pm\frac{\varepsilon^{-1}}{\sqrt{\tilde{q}^{2}-c^{2}}}|\varrho^{\varepsilon}\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\,\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|\qquad\,\,\,\text{on }\partial\mathscr{D}_{1},

where we have used the relation:

\nabla W_{\pm}=\nabla\vartheta\mp\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{qc}\nabla q=\nabla\vartheta\pm\frac{\sqrt{q^{2}-c^{2}}}{q^{2}}\nabla\varrho,

which is valid for
q\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
.
Using the information on the sign of the normal derivative from (
8.11
),
the Hopf Lemma forbids
W_{+}^{\varepsilon}
to attain a maximum (resp.
W_{-}^{\varepsilon}
to attain a minimum)
on the obstacle boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
.
Nevertheless, equation (
8.10
) is strongly elliptic and contains
no zero-order derivative terms in
W^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
,
whence the Weak Maximum Principle implies that the extremal values of
W^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}
are achieved
on boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}=\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}\cup\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
.
We thereby deduce that these extremal values are attained only on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
,
whence

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}}W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{x})\leq\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}}W^{\varepsilon}_{-}(\mathbf{x})\geq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}).

In what follows, we use the shorthand notation
\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}=\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})
.
From the above, (
3.1
), and (
3.9
), we obtain that, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}
,

(8.12)

k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))=\frac{1}{2}\big(W_{+}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})-W_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{x})-\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W^{\varepsilon}_{-}(\mathbf{x})\big).

We estimate the quantity on the right-hand side of (
8.12
).
Note that the Dirichlet-type condition
(\varrho_{\infty},0)
is imposed on
boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
, from which it follows that, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
and all
\varepsilon>0
,

W_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=-k(q_{\infty}),\qquad W_{+}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=k(q_{\infty}),

where
q_{\infty}:=q(\varrho_{\infty})
.
In turn, equation (
8.12
) and the monotonicity of
k
(
cf.
§
3.2
)
imply the upper bound

(8.13)

k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))\leq k(q_{\infty})\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}.

Lemma 8.6

(Minimum Principle for the Speed)
.

Let
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})
be
a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
), and let
the boundary data satisfy
q_{\infty}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
.
Then there exists
q_{*}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
depending only on
\varrho_{\infty}

such that

\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\geq q_{*}>q_{\mathrm{cr}}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}$}.

Proof.

First, by definition,
\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}
never exceeds
q_{\mathrm{cav}}
.
Suppose that
\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=q_{\mathrm{cav}}
at some point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}
.
Then
\inf_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}q_{\infty}<q_{\mathrm{cav}}
due to the choice of
the boundary data so that we directly see
that
\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\geq\inf_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}q_{\infty}>q_{\mathrm{cr}}

at this specific point.

Furthermore, suppose that
\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}
is such that
\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})<q_{\mathrm{cav}}
.
Define the strictly positive number

\delta_{*}:=k(q_{\infty})
,
and observe from estimate (
8.13
) and our assumptions that

k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\delta_{*}<(\sqrt{2}-1)\frac{\pi}{2}=k(q_{\mathrm{cr}}).

Recall from §
3.2
that
k
is invertible and strictly decreasing in
q

on the restriction of its domain to
(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
, whence

\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\geq k^{-1}(\delta_{*})>q_{\mathrm{cr}}

at all such points
\mathbf{x}
. In turn, we define
q_{*}:=\min\{k^{-1}(\delta_{*}),q_{\infty}\}
,
and the conclusion of the lemma is manifestly verified.
∎

Note that the bound:
\sup_{\mathscr{D}}\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}
follows directly from

\tilde{q}(\varrho)=(1-\varrho^{2}_{+})_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
We are thus in a position to provide the required uniform bound of
sequence
\{\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
.

Lemma 8.7

(Uniform Estimate of the Angle Functions)
.

Suppose that
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

is a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
) with the boundary data

q_{\infty}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
.
Then there exist
\vartheta_{*},\vartheta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
depending only on
\varrho_{\infty}

such that

\vartheta_{*}\leq\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\vartheta^{*}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for all $\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}$}.

Proof.

We obtain directly from the expressions in (
3.1
) and (
3.9
)
for the Riemann invariants that, for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}
,

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2}\big(W_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})+W_{+}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\big)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(W^{\varepsilon}_{-}(\mathbf{x})+\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{x})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})-k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))+k(q_{\infty})\big),

whence, using also that
k
is nonnegative and decreasing in
q
(
cf.
§
3.2
), we obtain the bound

(8.14)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq

\displaystyle\,-k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))+k(q_{\infty})\leq k(q_{\infty})=:\,\vartheta^{*}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2}\big(W_{+}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})+W_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\big)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(W^{\varepsilon}_{+}(\mathbf{x})+\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}W^{\varepsilon}_{-}(\mathbf{x})\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})+k(\tilde{q}^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))-k(q_{\infty})\big),

which implies

(8.15)

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\geq

\displaystyle\,k(q^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))-k(q_{\infty})\geq-k(q_{\infty})=:\,\vartheta_{*},

and the result follows.
∎

It follows that, to prove Propositions
8.1
–
8.2
,
it
suffices to verify that the solutions of the auxiliary system satisfy
the stronger estimate:
\varrho^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})>0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
.
This is the focus of the next section.

8.2.
Consistency of the auxiliary system and strict positivity of
\varrho^{\varepsilon}

Lemma 8.8

(Consistency of the Auxiliary Approximate Problem)
.

Suppose that
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

is a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
).
Then
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})
is a solution of system (
8.9
)
such that, for all
\varepsilon>0
and all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
,

(8.16)

q_{\mathrm{cr}}<q_{*}\leq q^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}},\quad\vartheta_{*}\leq\vartheta^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\leq\vartheta^{*}.

Proof.

In this proof, we omit the
\varepsilon
-superscript to simplify notation.

Suppose that
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

solves (
8.3
).
Then, testing the first equation in (
8.3
) with the negative part

\varrho_{-}^{\varepsilon}=-\min\{\varrho^{\varepsilon},0\}
and using the Divergence Theorem,
and omitting the
\varepsilon
-superscript for ease of notation,
we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}}(1-\frac{c^{2}}{\tilde{q}^{2}})|\nabla\varrho_{-}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{D}}\varrho_{-}\tilde{q}(\varrho)\,\nabla\varrho_{-}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}}\varrho_{-}^{2}\tilde{q}(\varrho)\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)\cdot\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle-\,\varepsilon\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{\tilde{q}^{2}}\big)(\varrho_{\infty})_{-}\,\nabla\varrho_{\infty}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}}|\varrho_{-}^{2}\tilde{q}(\varrho)\,\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0;

the third term on the right-hand side vanishes because
\varrho_{\infty}\geq 0
,
and all the other terms also vanish due to

\supp\tilde{q}(\varrho)\cap\supp\varrho_{-}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}\,:\,\varrho(\mathbf{x})=0\}
.
In view of the lower bound
\tilde{q}\geq q_{*}>q_{\mathrm{cr}}
from Lemma
8.6
,
it follows that there exists
C>0
such that
1-\frac{c^{2}}{\tilde{q}^{2}}\geq C
, whence

\int_{\mathscr{D}}|\nabla\varrho_{-}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0.

Since
\varrho_{\infty}\geq 0
,
it follows from the above that
\varrho_{-}\equiv 0
in
\mathscr{D}
, whence
\varrho\geq 0
in
\mathscr{D}
.

Meanwhile, the lower bound on
\tilde{q}
of Lemma
8.6
implies that

\varrho\leq\varrho(q_{*})<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}<1
.
In turn, it follows that
\tilde{q}(\varrho^{\varepsilon})=q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})=\sqrt{1-(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}
,
whence
(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})
solves
problem (
8.9
) and satisfies the uniform estimates (
8.16
).
∎

In fact, a stronger result is available, now that the weak lower bound
\varrho^{\varepsilon}\geq 0
has been established.

Corollary 8.9
.

For all
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
,
\varrho^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})>0
so that
q^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})<q_{\mathrm{cav}}.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
\varrho^{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0
for some
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\overline{\mathscr{D}}
.
First, our choice of the boundary data on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
implies
\mathbf{x}_{0}\notin\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
.
We now show that the other two possibilities,
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathscr{D}
(an interior point) or
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
,
also yield contradictions.

1.
\mathbf{x}_{0}
is not an interior point of
\mathscr{D}
:
Suppose first that
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathscr{D}
is an interior point.
Recall that
\varrho
solves the original viscous problem (
8.2
),
which is strongly elliptic and reads in the non-divergence form as

\big(1-\frac{\varrho}{q(\varrho)}\big)\nabla\varrho\cdot\mathbf{e}(\vartheta)+\varrho q(\varrho)\nabla\vartheta\cdot\mathbf{e}(\vartheta+\frac{\pi}{2})=-\frac{2\varrho}{(1-\varrho^{2})^{2}}\varepsilon|\nabla\varrho|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{\varrho^{2}}{1-\varrho^{2}}\big)\varepsilon\Delta\varrho.

Since
\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0=\inf_{\mathscr{D}}\varrho
, the version of the Strong Maximum Principle
that requires no sign condition on the lowest-order term implies that
\varrho\equiv 0
is constant,
which contradicts the boundary condition on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
.

2.
\mathbf{x}_{0}
does not belong to boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
:
Suppose that
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
.
Then
\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{0})=0=\inf_{\mathscr{D}}\varrho
is achieved on boundary
\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
,
and thus the version of the Hopf Lemma that requires no sign condition
on the lowest-order term implies either
\nabla\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cdot\mathbf{n}>0
or
\varrho\equiv 0
is constant.
This latter conclusion cannot be true because of the boundary condition on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}
,
whence
\nabla\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cdot\mathbf{n}>0
.
However, by evaluating the boundary condition on
\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}
at point
\mathbf{x}_{0}
, we have

\varepsilon\nabla\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,

which is again a contradiction.
∎

Combining the results in Lemmas
8.6
–
8.8
and
Corollary
8.9
, and setting
\varrho_{*}:=\varrho(q_{*})
,
we complete the proof of Propositions

–
8.2
.

8.3.
Dissipation estimate and
H^{-1}
-compactness

We begin this section by proving Proposition
8.3
, which in effect verifies
that the dissipation estimate
[
11
, Proposition 8.1]
is still valid for the solutions generated
by the viscous problems (
8.9
). Although we write the proof explicitly only for
\gamma=3
,
the proof generalizes to all
\gamma>2
; see
[
40
, Lemma 4.17]
for further details.

Proof of Proposition
8.3
.

The proof is divided into two steps.

1.
Special entropy generator and pointwise estimate
:
For
q_{\infty}\in(q_{\mathrm{cr}},q_{\mathrm{cav}})
, denote
\nu_{\infty}=\nu(\varrho(q_{\infty}))
.
We set
\varrho_{\infty}=\varrho(q_{\infty})
and recall
\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}=\varrho(q_{\mathrm{cr}})
.
In view of the monotonicity provided by (
2.7
), we see that

\nu_{\infty}>0
and
0<\varrho_{\infty}<\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}
.
We now recall the special entropy generator (
2.5
):

H^{*}(\nu,\vartheta)=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2}-\frac{\nu}{\varrho_{\infty}}+\int_{\nu_{\infty}}^{\nu}\int^{\tau}_{\nu_{\infty}}\frac{M(\nu^{\prime})^{2}-1}{\varrho(\nu^{\prime})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

and observe that the double integral is well-defined, since

\int_{\nu_{\infty}}^{\tau}\frac{M(\nu^{\prime})^{2}-1}{\varrho(\nu^{\prime})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}=\int_{\nu_{\infty}}^{\tau}k^{\prime}(\nu^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}\leq C\int_{\nu_{\infty}}^{\tau}(\nu^{\prime})^{-\frac{4}{3}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\prime}\leq C\tau^{-\frac{1}{3}}

for some positive constant
C
depending on
\bar{\nu}
,
and the final right-hand side is integrable. Furthermore, a direct computation via the chain rule and (
2.7
) yields
the equality away from the vacuum:

(8.17)

H^{*}_{\nu}=-\frac{1}{\varrho}+N(\varrho),

where we have defined

(8.18)

N(\varrho):=-\int_{\varrho_{\infty}}^{\varrho}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\varrho^{\prime}}{q(\varrho^{\prime})^{2}}.

Provided
\varrho\in[0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}]
,
where we recall for
\gamma=3
that
\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, we estimate this
term as

\displaystyle|N(\varrho)|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\varrho}^{\varrho_{\infty}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}\right|\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho<\varrho_{\infty}\}}+\left|\int^{\varrho}_{\varrho_{\infty}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}\right|\mathds{1}_{\{\varrho\geq\varrho_{\infty}\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varrho_{\infty}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}+\int_{\varrho_{\infty}}^{\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{1-\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}}{1-\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}^{2}}

and hence

(8.19)

\sup_{\varrho\in[0,\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}]}|N(\varrho)|\leq C,

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
\varrho_{\mathrm{cr}}
.
Observe from (
8.18
) that

(8.20)

N(\varrho_{\infty})=0.

2.
Special entropy pair and dissipation estimate
:
A direct computation via the Loewner–Morawetz relations and (
8.17
)
shows that the special generator
H^{*}
generates the entropy pair
\mathbf{Q}_{*}=(Q_{1*},Q_{2*})
:

\displaystyle Q_{1*}(\nu,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=-q(\vartheta\sin\vartheta+\cos\vartheta)+N(\varrho)\varrho q\cos\vartheta,

(8.21)

\displaystyle Q_{2*}(\nu,\vartheta)

\displaystyle=q(\vartheta\cos\vartheta-\sin\vartheta)+N(\varrho)\varrho q\sin\vartheta.

We then consider sequence
\{\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}_{*}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

for
\mathbf{Q}_{*}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{Q}_{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})
and
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
uniquely
characterized by
(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})
, and observe that
the uniform boundedness of
N(\varrho^{\varepsilon})

and
\vartheta^{\varepsilon}
(
cf.
Proposition
8.2
)
implies that
\{\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}_{*}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly bounded
in
L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D})
.
Furthermore, using relation (
3.18
)
computed in §
3.3

(
cf.

[
11
, §7]
), we have

\displaystyle\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}_{*}=

\displaystyle\,-\varepsilon\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\Delta\vartheta^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon N(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\div\big((1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon\div\big(-\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}+(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}})N(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\big)

(8.22)

\displaystyle\,+\varepsilon\big(|\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}})\frac{1}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}|\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\big).

Integrating the entropy dissipation measures over
\mathscr{D}

and applying the divergence theorem in the plane, and
recalling that
\mathbf{n}
is the inward pointing unit normal, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}}\mathbf{Q}_{*}^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}}\Big(-\vartheta^{\varepsilon}\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}+\big(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\big)N(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}\Big)\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}}\Big(|\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}\big)\frac{1}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon})^{2}}|\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle N^{\varepsilon}_{1}+N^{\varepsilon}_{2}.

The uniform boundedness in
L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D})
of sequence
\{\mathbf{Q}_{*}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

implies that the left-hand side bounded independently of
\varepsilon
, even though the estimate
depends on the size of
\mathcal{H}(\partial\mathscr{D})
.
Using the boundary conditions, we obtain

\displaystyle N^{\varepsilon}_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}}|\varrho^{\varepsilon}\,\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|N(\varrho^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{2}}\Big(\underbrace{-\vartheta_{\infty}\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}+\big(1-\frac{c(\varrho_{\infty})^{2}}{q(\varrho_{\infty})^{2}}\big)N(\varrho_{\infty})\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}}_{=0}\Big)\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x}),

where we have used
\vartheta_{\infty}=N(\varrho_{\infty})=0
from (
8.20
).
Hence,
N^{\varepsilon}_{1}
is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon

due to the uniform bound on
|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|
and the estimate (
8.19
) in Step 1.
We thereby conclude that the second integral
N^{\varepsilon}_{2}
is bounded, independently of
\varepsilon
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma is essential to the proof of Proposition
8.4
.
In what follows,

(\varrho^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}(\mathscr{D})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathscr{D}})

is a solution of the auxiliary system (
8.3
)
satisfying the boundary conditions of Proposition

.

Lemma 8.10

(Compactness Estimates)
.

Let
H
be a smooth entropy generator satisfying that
there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that, for
j=0,1,2
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\vartheta}^{j}\big(\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon})H_{\nu}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big)\big|+\big|\partial^{j}_{\vartheta}\big(H_{\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big)\right|\leq C,

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{j}_{\vartheta}\big(\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon})H_{\nu}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})+H_{\vartheta\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big)\right|\leq C\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon}).

Let
\mathbf{Q}
the corresponding entropy pair generated by
H
via the Loewner–Morawetz relations.
Then the sequence of entropy dissipation measures
\{\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

is pre-compact in
H^{-1}(\mathscr{D})
.

Proof.

We omit the
\varepsilon
-superscript in the following computations to simplify notation.
Recalling relation (
3.18
)
from §
3.3
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon\div\Big(\nabla\vartheta(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta})+(1-\frac{c(\varrho)^{2}}{q(\varrho)^{2}})\nabla\varrho(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta})\Big)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Big(\nabla\vartheta\!\cdot\!\nabla(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta})+(1-\frac{c(\varrho)^{2}}{q(\varrho)^{2}})\nabla\varrho\!\cdot\!\nabla(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta})\Big)

(8.23)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,D_{1}^{\varepsilon}+D_{2}^{\varepsilon}.

1.
Control of
D^{\varepsilon}_{2}
: We focus first on
D^{\varepsilon}_{2}
as it is the more difficult term to bound. Observe that

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{-1}D_{2}^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\Big(2(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}})H_{\nu\vartheta}+\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\varrho H_{\nu\nu\vartheta}+(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}})\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}\Big)\nabla\vartheta\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho

\displaystyle+\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}+\big(\varrho^{2}H_{\nu\nu}+\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta\vartheta}-\frac{q^{2}}{c^{2}}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2},

which, using the entropy equation to rewrite
\partial^{j}_{\vartheta}H_{\nu\nu}=\frac{M^{2}-1}{\varrho^{2}}\partial^{j}_{\vartheta}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}
for
j=0,1
, simplifies to

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{-1}D_{2}^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\,\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)\big(|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}+(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}})\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\big)

(8.24)

\displaystyle+\big(H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}+\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}\big)\frac{2}{\varrho}\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\nabla\vartheta\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho.

By the dissipation estimate of Proposition
8.3
,
we know that the first term on the right-hand side is controlled, provided that
there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

\big|\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon})H_{\nu\vartheta\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})-H_{\vartheta\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\big|\leq C,

which is guaranteed by the assumptions.
The second term on the right-hand side is controlled similarly. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Big|\big(H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}+\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}\big)\frac{2}{\varrho}\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\nabla\vartheta\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho\Big|

\displaystyle=\frac{2q}{\varrho}\sqrt{1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}}\,\big|H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}+\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}\big|\Big|\nabla\vartheta\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho\frac{1}{q}\sqrt{1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}}\,\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\varrho^{-1}\big|H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}+\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}\big|\Big(|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\Big)

for some constant
C>0
depending on
\varrho_{*}
but independent of
\varepsilon
,
where we have used the Cauchy–Young inequality and the boundedness of
1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}
.
Then our assumptions imply that

\left|H_{\vartheta\vartheta\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})+\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon})H_{\nu\vartheta}(\nu^{\varepsilon},\vartheta^{\varepsilon})\right|\leq C\varrho(\nu^{\varepsilon}),

whence the dissipation estimate of Proposition
8.3
imply that there exists
C>0
,
independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

(8.25)

\sup_{\varepsilon}\|D^{\varepsilon}_{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathscr{D})}\leq C,

and hence sequence
\{D^{\varepsilon}_{2}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is
pre-compact in
W^{-1,r}(\mathscr{D})
for all
r\in(1,2)
by the Rellich Theorem,
as
\partial\mathscr{D}
is Lipschitz.

2.
Control of
D^{\varepsilon}_{1}
: The term inside the divergence of
D^{\varepsilon}_{1}
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla\vartheta\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}\big)+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\nabla\varrho\big(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}||\nabla\vartheta|+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)|\varrho H_{\nu}+H_{\vartheta\vartheta}|\varrho^{-1}|\nabla\varrho|

(8.26)

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla\vartheta|+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)|\nabla\varrho|.

Furthermore,
using the boundedness of
q
and
1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}
,
we have

1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\leq Cq^{-1}(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}})\leq Cq^{-1}\sqrt{1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}},

whence it follows from
Proposition
8.3
and the Minkowski inequality that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Big\|\nabla\vartheta\big(\varrho H_{\nu\vartheta}-H_{\vartheta}\big)+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\nabla\varrho\big(H_{\nu}+\frac{1}{\varrho}H_{\vartheta\vartheta}\big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{D})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}\bigg(\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}}\Big(|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}+\big(1-\frac{c^{2}}{q^{2}}\big)\frac{1}{q^{2}}|\nabla\varrho|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(8.27)

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

We deduce that there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that,
for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(8.28)

\|D^{\varepsilon}_{1}\|_{H^{-1}(\mathscr{D})}\leq C\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

3.
Interpolation compactness
:
Observe from the Loewner–Morawetz relations that
sequence
\{\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D})
.
It follows that there exists
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

(8.29)

\sup_{\varepsilon}\|D^{1}_{\varepsilon}+D^{2}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{-1,\infty}(\mathscr{D})}\leq C.

Estimates (
8.25
)–(
8.29
) and the interpolation compactness
lemma of Ding-Chen-Luo
[
15
, §4]
(
cf.
Murat’s Lemma
[
36
]
)
imply that sequence
\{\div_{\mathbf{x}}\mathbf{Q}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is contained in a compact subset of
H^{-1}(\mathscr{D})
.
∎

The second estimate of Lemma
8.10
highlights a key cancellation property,
which can be observed formally from the initial conditions of the kernels
recorded in Theorem
2.1
.

Combining the results of Lemma
8.10
with
Propositions
4.2
and
5.2
,
we have proved Proposition
8.4

and hence also Theorem
8.1
.

In this section, we first combine the compensated compactness

2.3
)
with the approximate solutions
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
constructed and estimated in
§

to show the existence of entropy solutions
on the bounded domains
\mathscr{D}
as
introduced
in §

by considering an exhaustion of the whole domain

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

For each

,
let
\mathscr{D}^{k}
be a domain of identical shape
to
\mathscr{D}
(
cf.
§

and same obstacle (both in shape and size)
of increasing size with

\mathbb{H}

with the shaded obstacle denoted by

Then
\Omega=\bigcup_{k}\mathscr{D}^{k}

\mathscr{D}^{k}\subset\mathscr{D}^{k+1}
for all

.
We divide the proof into two steps.

1. For any fixed domain
\mathscr{D}^{k}
, we first observe that
the approximate solutions
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
generated

8.1
in
\mathscr{D}^{k}

2.3

in
\mathscr{D}^{k}

there exists

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\to\mathbf{u}_{k}\qquad\text{a.e.~and strongly in }L^{p}\text{ for all }p\in[1,\infty).

Now we show that the limit function
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D})
is an entropy solution defined
in
\mathscr{D}^{k}
.
For simplicity of notation, we use

\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}=\varrho(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k})
and
\theta^{\varepsilon}_{k}=\theta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k})

We first show that
\mathbf{u}_{k}
is a distributional solution in
\mathscr{D}^{k}
.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}^{k})
.
Inserting this function in the weak formulation of the viscous problem (
8.9
)
and using the boundary conditions, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k})^{\perp}\!\cdot\!\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=-\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}\nabla\psi\!\cdot\!\nabla\vartheta^{\varepsilon}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

\displaystyle\,C_{\psi}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}^{k}}\varphi\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}^{k}}\varphi|\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}\big(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k})^{2}}\big)\nabla\varphi\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

Note that the final term on the right-hand side of the previous equality may be controlled as in (
9.1
), whence

\varepsilon\Big|\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}\big(1-\frac{c(\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k})^{2}}{q(\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k})^{2}}\big)\nabla\varphi\!\cdot\!\nabla\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\bigg|\leq C_{\varphi}\sqrt{\varepsilon}

vanishes in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Thus, by using the convergence:
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\to\mathbf{u}_{k}

a.e.
and (
9.3
),
the Dominated Convergence Theorem implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{D}^{k}}\varrho_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathscr{D}_{k}}\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{1}^{k}}\varphi\big(\underbrace{\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}-|\varrho^{\varepsilon}_{k}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{n}|}_{\leq 0}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\leq 0.

The weak normal trace condition follows immediately from the above
by using the non-negativity of
\varrho
and the Dubois–Reymond Lemma.

2. In this setup, we prove Theorem
2.2
regarding the existence of
a globally-defined entropy solution on the entire plane
with the incoming supersonic flow at
x=-\infty
,
as a corollary of our weak continuity result.

First, in Step 1,
we have obtained the entropy solutions
\mathbf{u}_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D}^{k})

satisfying Definition
2.2
on each
\mathscr{D}^{k}
:
For each
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}^{k})
,

(9.4)

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\perp}\!\cdot\!\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{H}}\varrho(\mathbf{u}_{k})\mathbf{u}_{k}\!\cdot\!\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=0,

and, for every nonnegative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}^{k})
, the entropy inequality holds:

(9.5)

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{u}_{k})\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq 0,

where
\mathbf{Q}\in\{\mathbf{Q}_{*},\mathbf{Q}_{H}\}
with
\mathbf{Q}_{H}
being an entropy pair generated via
H
generated by
kernels
H^{\mathbf{r}}
and
H^{\mathbf{s}}
and satisfying the convexity conditions in (
2.10
).
Furthermore, since the invariant regions in Theorem
8.1
depend only
on the boundary data (which is the same for all
k
), then, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

0\leq\varrho(\mathbf{u}_{k}(\mathbf{x}))\leq\varrho_{*},\quad q_{\mathrm{cr}}<q_{*}\leq|\mathbf{u}_{k}(\mathbf{x})|\leq q_{\mathrm{cav}}\qquad\,\,\text{for a.e.~}\mathbf{x}\in\mathscr{D}^{k},

where we emphasize that
\varrho_{*}
and
q_{*}
are independent of
k
.

By extending functions
\mathbf{u}_{k}
by
q_{*}
outside of
\mathscr{D}^{k}
,
then sequence
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k}
is uniformly bounded such that the previous bounds hold almost everywhere
in
\mathbb{H}
.
This implies that there exists a subsequence (still denoted by)
\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k}

such that
\mathbf{u}_{k}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{u}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Fix any
l\in\mathbb{N}
. The previous weak-* convergence implies that

\mathbf{u}_{k}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{u}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathscr{D}^{l})
.
Theorem
2.4
implies that there exists a further subsequence (still denoted by)

\{\mathbf{u}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\mathbf{u}_{k}\to\mathbf{u}
almost everywhere and strongly
in
L^{p}(\mathscr{D}^{l})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.
This mode of convergence suffices
to pass to the limit as
k\to\infty
in (
9.4
)–(
9.5
),
where we emphasize that the test functions
\psi
and
\varphi
belong to
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}^{l})
for
l
fixed.

We have therefore shown that, for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
,

\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}_{l})
, and nonnegative function

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathscr{D}^{l})
,
relations (
9.4
)–(
9.5
)
are verified by
\mathbf{u}
.
Since
\{\mathscr{D}^{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing compact exhaustion of the half-plane
\mathbb{H}
,
we deduce that (
9.4
)–(
9.5
)
are verified by
\mathbf{u}
for any test function

\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and any nonnegative test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
.
In addition, the weak normal trace condition follows similarly.

This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Fundamental Solutions of the Tricomi–Keldysh Equation

In this appendix, we present some properties of distribution
G_{\lambda}
that is the fundamental solution
of the Tricomi–Keldysh equation (
cf.

[
20
]
). We also extend the classical definition
and properties of
G_{\lambda}
to the case when
\lambda
is a negative integer;
this is needed for the construction of the kernels in §
4
–§
5
.
Throughout this appendix, we use
\mathscr{F}
to
denote the Fourier transform with respect to variable
s
.

A.1.
Classical definition of
G_{\lambda}
and extension as a distribution

Recall from the classical definition of
G_{\lambda}
given in (
4.1
) that

G_{0}(\nu,s)=\mathds{1}_{[-k(\nu),k(\nu)]}(s),\quad G_{1}(\nu,s)=[k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2}]_{+},\quad G_{2}(\nu,s)=[k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2}]_{+}^{2}.

An elementary computation yields

(A.1)

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{0}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{2\sin(\xi k(\nu))}{\xi}=\,\sqrt{\pi}2^{\frac{1}{2}}k(\nu)^{\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}J_{\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu)),

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{1}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{4k(\nu)}{\xi^{2}}\big(\frac{\sin(\xi k(\nu))}{\xi k(\nu)}-\cos(\xi k(\nu))\big)=\,\sqrt{\pi}2^{\frac{3}{2}}k(\nu)^{\frac{3}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{3}{2}}J_{\frac{3}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu)),

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{2}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{16k(\nu)^{2}}{\xi^{3}}\big(\frac{3\sin(\xi k(\nu))}{\xi^{2}k(\nu)^{2}}-\frac{3\cos(\xi k(\nu))}{\xi k(\nu)}-\sin(\xi k(\nu))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2!\,2^{\frac{5}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}k(\nu)^{\frac{5}{2}}|\xi|^{-\frac{5}{2}}J_{\frac{5}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu)),

where
\hat{G}_{n}(\nu,\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform of
G_{n}
with respect to
its second variable for
n=0,1,2
.
More generally (
cf.

[
20
]
), for
\lambda\in(-1,\infty)
,

(A.2)

\hat{G}_{\lambda}(\nu,\xi)=c_{\lambda}k(\nu)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}J_{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))

with
c_{\lambda}:=\sqrt{\pi}2^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\lambda+1)
.

Note that all the functions on the right-hand sides
of (
A.1
)–(
A.2
) are smooth
in variable
\xi
and grow at most polynomially; that is,
they belong to space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of tempered distributions.
It is therefore natural to consider the tempered distribution
defined by

\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\sqrt{\pi}2^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))\}

as a candidate for
G_{-1}
.
In fact, due to the recurrence relations for the Bessel functions of the first kind
and their asymptotic behavior, the Paley–Wiener–Schwartz Theorem implies that
this distribution is compactly supported.
Furthermore, recall the recurrence relation:

(A.3)

\partial_{z}\hat{G}_{\lambda}(\nu,z)=-\frac{2\lambda+1}{z}\hat{G}_{\lambda}(\nu,z)+\frac{2\lambda}{z}k(\nu)^{2}\hat{G}_{\lambda-1}(\nu,z)\qquad\mbox{for any $z\in\mathbb{C}$},

which holds whenever
\lambda>0
. We now define
G_{-2}
from
G_{-1}
by using the above:

Definition A.1
.

Fix
\nu>0
. We define

(A.4)

\displaystyle G_{-1}(\nu,\cdot):=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\sqrt{\pi}2^{-\frac{1}{2}}k(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))\},

\displaystyle G_{-2}(\nu,\cdot):=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}k(\nu)^{-2}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\xi\partial_{\xi}\hat{G}_{-1}(\nu,\xi)-\hat{G}_{-1}(\nu,\xi)\},

\displaystyle G_{-3}(\nu,\cdot):=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}k(\nu)^{-2}\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\xi\partial_{\xi}\hat{G}_{-2}(\nu,\xi)-3\hat{G}_{-2}(\nu,\xi)\}

in the sense of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma A.2
.

Fix
\nu>0
. The following identities hold:

(A.5)

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{-1}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle c_{-1}k(\nu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))=\frac{\cos(\xi k(\nu))}{k(\nu)},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{-2}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle c_{-2}k(\nu)^{-\frac{3}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{3}{2}}J_{-\frac{3}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))=\frac{1}{2}k(\nu)^{-3}\big(\xi k(\nu)\sin(\xi k(\nu))+\cos(\xi k(\nu))\big),

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{-3}(\nu,\xi)=

\displaystyle c_{-3}k(\nu)^{-\frac{5}{2}}|\xi|^{\frac{5}{2}}J_{-\frac{5}{2}}(|\xi|k(\nu))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}k(\nu)^{-5}\Big(3\cos(\xi k(\nu))+3\xi k(\nu)\sin(\xi k(\nu))-(\xi k(\nu))^{2}\cos(\xi k(\nu))\Big),

where
c_{-n}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\sqrt{\pi}2^{\frac{1}{2}-n}
for
n=1,2,3
,
and the recurrence relation:

(A.6)

\xi\partial_{\xi}\hat{G}_{-n}(\nu,\xi)=(2n-1)\hat{G}_{-n}(\nu,\xi)-2nk(\nu)^{2}\hat{G}_{-n-1}(\nu,\xi),

is understood in the sense of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
for
n=1,2
.
Furthermore, distributions
G_{-n}(\nu,\cdot)
,
n=1,2,3
, are in
space
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
of compactly supported distributions.

Proof.

The first Fourier transform relation for
G_{-1}
holds in view of the exact representation for
the Bessel function
J_{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
The final two relations hold by direct computation via
Definition
A.1

and the trigonometric representation for
\hat{G}_{-1}
,
followed by using the exact representations for the Bessel functions
J_{-\frac{3}{2}}

and
J_{-\frac{5}{2}}
.
The recurrence relation is direct from Definition
A.1

and the fact that
G_{-n}
is a compactly supported distribution is deduced
from the Fourier transform relations and the Paley–Wiener–Schwartz Theorem.
∎

A.2.
Properties of distribution
f_{\lambda}

Definition A.3
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
,
define its rescaling
\zeta(\cdot a)
by

\langle\zeta(\cdot a),\varphi\rangle:=a^{-1}\langle\zeta,\varphi(\cdot a^{-1})\rangle\qquad\,\,\text{for all }\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}).

For
\nu>0
and
n=-3,\dots,0,1,2
,
we define
f_{n}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
to be the
\nu
-independent rescalings:

f_{n}:=k(\nu)^{-2n}G_{n}(\nu,\cdot k(\nu)),

which holds in the sense of distributions.

Then we have

Lemma A.4
.

For
\nu>0
and
n=-3,-2,-1,0,1,2
,

\hat{G}_{n}(\nu,\xi)=k(\nu)^{1+2n}\hat{f}_{n}(\xi k(\nu))\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),

and, for
n=1,2,3
,

\hat{f}_{-n}(\xi)=c_{-n}|\xi|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}J_{-(n-\frac{1}{2})}(|\xi|)\qquad\,\mbox{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{C}$}.

In turn, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{0}(\xi)=\frac{2\sin\xi}{\xi},\quad\hat{f}_{-1}(\xi)=\cos\xi,

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{-2}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\xi\sin\xi+\cos\xi\big),\quad\hat{f}_{-3}(\xi)=\frac{1}{8}\big(3\cos\xi+3\xi\sin\xi-\xi^{2}\cos\xi\big),

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)=\frac{4}{\xi^{2}}\big(\frac{\sin\xi}{\xi}-\cos\xi\big),\quad\hat{f}_{2}(\xi)=\frac{16}{\xi^{3}}\big(\frac{3\sin(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}-\frac{3\cos(\xi)}{\xi}-\sin(\xi)\big),

and
\hat{f}_{-2},\hat{f}_{-3}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
,
while
\hat{f}_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\lambda\geq-1
.

These assertions follow directly from Definition
A.3
and Lemma
A.2
.

We recall the following standard result.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
l>-1
. Then

(A.7)

\hat{f}_{l}^{\prime\prime}(z)+\hat{f}_{l}(z)=\hat{f}_{l+1}(z)=-\frac{2(l+1)}{z}\hat{f}_{l}^{\prime}(z)\qquad\,\,\mbox{for all $z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}$}.

Furthermore, if
l>0
, then

(A.8)

\hat{f}^{\prime}_{l}(z)=-\frac{2l+1}{z}\hat{f}_{l}(z)+\frac{2l}{z}\hat{f}_{l-1}(z)\qquad\mbox{for all $z\in\mathbb{C}$}.

We have the following recurrence formulas for
\hat{f}_{-n}(\xi)
,
which mirror those of the previous lemma.

Lemma A.6
.

For all
\xi\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, the following relations hold
:

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{-1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+\hat{f}_{-1}(\xi)=0,

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{-j}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+\hat{f}_{-j}(\xi)=\hat{f}_{-j+1}(\xi)=\frac{2(j-1)}{\xi}\hat{f}_{-j}^{\prime}(\xi)\qquad\text{for $j=0,2,3$},

and

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{-1}^{\prime}(\xi)=-\frac{\xi}{2}\hat{f}_{0}(\xi),

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{1}^{\prime}(\xi)=-\frac{3}{\xi}\hat{f}_{1}(\xi)+\frac{2}{\xi}\hat{f}_{0}(\xi),

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{0}^{\prime}(\xi)=-\frac{1}{\xi}\hat{f}_{0}(\xi)+\frac{2}{\xi}\hat{f}_{-1}(\xi),

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{-j}^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{2j-1}{\xi}\hat{f}_{-j}(\xi)-\frac{2j}{\xi}\hat{f}_{-j-1}(\xi)\quad\text{for $j=1,2$}.

These formulas follow from direct calculations.

In turn, we deduce the following recurrence relations for distributions
G_{-n}
.

Lemma A.7
.

For
n=1,2
, the following relations hold in the sense of distributions
:

(A.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}G_{-n}(\nu,s)=2nsG_{-n-1}(\nu,s),

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}G_{-n}(\nu,s)=-2nk^{\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)G_{-n-1}(\nu,s),

\displaystyle(k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2})G_{-n-1}(\nu,s)=G_{-n}(\nu,s),

while

\partial_{s}G_{n}(\nu,s)=-2sG_{n-1}(\nu,s)
for
n=0,1
,
\partial_{\nu}G_{0}(\nu,s)=2k^{\prime}(\nu)k(\nu)G_{-1}(\nu,s)
and
(k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2})G_{-1}(\nu,s)=0
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
Finally, the following relations hold in the sense of distributions
:

(A.10)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{s}G_{1}(\nu,s)=-6G_{0}(\nu,s)+4k(\nu)^{2}G_{-1}(\nu,s),

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}G_{0}(\nu,s)=2G_{-1}(\nu,s)-4k(\nu)^{2}G_{-2}(\nu,s),

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}G_{-1}(\nu,s)=-6G_{-2}(\nu,s)+8k(\nu)^{2}G_{-3}(\nu,s),

so that
\supp G_{-n}(\nu,\cdot)\subset[-k(\nu),k(\nu)]
for
n=1,2,3
, and
\nu>0
.

Proof.

We use the fact that the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})

is an automorphism to compute the derivatives via the formulas:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}G_{-n}(\nu,s)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\mathrm{i}\xi\hat{G}_{-n}(\nu,\xi)\}\},

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}G_{-n}(\nu,s)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\{\partial_{\nu}\hat{G}_{-n}(\nu,\xi)\}\}.

The first and second equalities follow then from Lemma
A.2

and the fact that
\mathscr{F}\{x\varphi(x)\}=\mathrm{i}\hat{\varphi}^{\prime}(\xi)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
Recall also that
\mathscr{F}\{x^{2}\varphi(x)\}=-\hat{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, whence

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\{(k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2})G_{-2}(\nu,s)\}\!=\!k(\nu)^{2}\hat{G}_{-2}(\nu,s)+\partial_{\xi}^{2}\hat{G}_{-2}(\nu,\xi)\!=\!\frac{\cos(\xi k(\nu))}{k(\nu)}=\hat{G}_{-1}(\nu,\xi).

We deduce that
(k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2})G_{-2}(\nu,s)=G_{-1}(\nu,s)
in the sense of distributions,
since the Fourier transform is an automorphism of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
.
An identical strategy shows the results for
G_{-3}
and
G_{-1}
,
which verifies the third and fourth equalities.
For the final part, we employ the recurrence relations proved earlier to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}G_{-1}(\nu,s)

\displaystyle=2G_{-2}(\nu,s)+2s\partial_{s}G_{-2}(\nu,s)

\displaystyle=2G_{-2}(\nu,s)-8(k(\nu)^{2}-s^{2})G_{-3}(\nu,s)+8k(\nu)^{2}G_{-3}(\nu,s).

Then the result follows by using the same strategy to obtain analogous relations
for
\partial^{2}_{s}G_{0}
and
\partial^{2}_{s}G_{1}
, and
\partial_{s}G_{1}(\nu,s)=-2sG_{0}(\nu,s)
.
The relation regarding the support of the distributions is clear from the formulas
in (
A.10
) and
the fact that
\supp G_{1}(\nu,\cdot)=\supp G_{0}(\nu,\cdot)=[-k(\nu),k(\nu)]
.
∎

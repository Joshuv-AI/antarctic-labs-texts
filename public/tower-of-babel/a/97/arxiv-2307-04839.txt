Abstract
. The coefficients of the chain polynomial of a finite poset enumerate chains in the poset by their number of elements. It has been a challenging open problem to determine which posets have real-rooted chain polynomials. Two new classes of posets, namely those of all rank-selected subposets of Cohen-Macaulay simplicial posets and all noncrossing partition lattices associated to finite Coxeter groups, are shown to have this property. The first result generalizes one of Brenti and Welker. As a special case, the descent enumerator of permutations of the set { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,\dots,n\} which have ascents at specified positions is shown to be real-rooted, hence log-concave and unimodal, and a good estimate for the location of the peak is deduced.



1.
Introduction

The coefficients of the chain polynomial
f_{P}(x)

of a finite poset (partially ordered set)
P

enumerate chains in
P
by their number of elements.
From a face enumeration point of view,
f_{P}(x)
is
the
f
-polynomial of a flag simplicial complex of
special type, namely the order complex
\Delta(P)

(see
[
34
]
for basic definitions and
terminology on simplicial complexes and their face
enumeration). Thus,

(1)

f_{P}(x)=f(\Delta(P),x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}(\Delta(P))x^{i},

where
f_{i-1}(\Delta(P))
is the number of

i
-element chains in
P
(which are precisely the

(i-1)
-dimensional faces of
\Delta(P)
) and
n

is the largest size of such a chain (which in one
more than the dimension of
\Delta(P)
). For some
purposes, one may focus on the corresponding

h

(2)

h(\Delta(P),x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}(\Delta(P))x^{i}(1-x)^{n-i}

instead. For instance,
f_{P}(x)
has only real
roots if and only if so does

.
The following general question was posed
in
[
5

.

(
[

For which finite posets

There are several motivations behind this question.
For finite distributive lattices, it is known to be

[

and cubical posets with

-vector

This paper contributes an affirmative answer to

2.1.
Polynomials

A polynomial
p(x)=h_{0}+h_{1}x+\cdots+h_{n}x^{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
is called

\bullet

symmetric
, with center of symmetry
n/2
, if

h_{i}=h_{n-i}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
,

\bullet

unimodal
, with a peak at position
k
, if

h_{0}\leq h_{1}\leq\cdots\leq h_{k}\geq h_{k+1}\geq\cdots\geq h_{n}
,

\bullet

log-concave
, if
h^{2}_{i}\geq h_{i-1}h_{i+1}

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

\bullet

real-rooted
, if every root of
p(x)
is
real, or
p(x)\equiv 0
.

Every real-rooted polynomial with nonnegative
coefficients is log-concave and unimodal; see

[
13
,
32
]
for more information about these
concepts.

A real-rooted polynomial
p(x)
, with
roots
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots
, is
said to
interlace
a real-rooted polynomial

q(x)
, with roots
\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\cdots
,
if

\cdots\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\beta_{1}.

We then write
p(x)\preceq q(x)
. By convention,
the zero polynomial interlaces and is interlaced by
every real-rooted polynomial and nonzero constant
polynomials interlace all polynomials
of degree at most one. A sequence

(p_{0}(x),p_{1}(x),\dots,p_{m}(x))
of real-rooted
polynomials is called
interlacing
if

p_{i}(x)\preceq p_{j}(x)
for
0\leq i<j\leq m
. The following statement lists well known
properties of interlacing sequences; see, for
instance,
[
13
, Section 7.8]

[
20
, Chapter 3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(p_{0}(x),p_{1}(x),\dots,p_{m}(x))
be an
interlacing sequence of real-rooted polynomials
with positive leading coefficients.

(a)

Every nonnegative linear combination
p(x)
of

p_{0}(x),p_{1}(x),\dots,p_{m}(x)
is real-rooted.
Moreover,
p_{0}(x)\preceq p(x)\preceq p_{m}(x)
.

(b)

The sequence
(q_{0}(x),q_{1}(x),\dots,q_{m+1}(x))

of partial sums

q_{k}(x)=\sum_{i=k}^{m}p_{i}(x)

for
k\in\{0,1,\dots,m+1\}
is also interlacing.

(c)

The sequence
(t_{0}(x),t_{1}(x),\dots,t_{m+1}(x))

defined by

t_{k}(x)=x\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}p_{i}(x)+\sum_{i=k}^{m}p_{i}(x)

for
k\in\{0,1,\dots,m+1\}
is also interlacing.

Given a polynomial
p(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
of degree
at most
n
, there exist unique symmetric
polynomials
a(x),b(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
with centers
of symmetry
n/2
and
(n-1)/2
, respectively,
such that
p(x)=a(x)+xb(x)
. This expression
is known as the
symmetric decomposition
(or

Stapledon decomposition
) of
p(x)
with
respect to
n
. Then,
p(x)
is said to have a

nonnegative
(respectively,
unimodal

or
real-rooted
)
symmetric
decomposition
with respect to
n
if
a(x)
and

b(x)
have nonnegative coefficients (respectively,
are unimodal or real-rooted); see
[
7
,
14
]

for more information about these concepts. Every
polynomial which has a nonnegative unimodal
symmetric decomposition with respect to
n
is
unimodal, with a peak at position
\lceil n/2\rceil
.

2.2.
Poset combinatorics

Our notation and terminology generally follows that
of
[
37
, Chapter 3]
. Let
P
be a finite
graded poset of rank

\hat{0}
and rank function

We denote by

\beta_{\hat{P}}(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T{\smallsetminus}S|}\alpha_{\hat{P}}(S)

(5)

\alpha_{\hat{P}}(T)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)

for
T\subseteq[n]
. The collections of numbers

(\alpha_{\hat{P}}(T))_{T\subseteq[n]}
and

(\beta_{\hat{P}}(T))_{T\subseteq[n]}
are the

flag
f
-vector
and the

flag
h
-vector
of
\hat{P}
, respectively.

The
order complex
of a finite poset

is
defined as the simplicial complex
\Delta(Q)
which
consists of all chains in
Q
. The
f
-polynomial
and the
h
-polynomial of

are defined by
Equations (
1
)
and (
2
), respectively, when

P
is replaced by
Q
. Since the

-polynomial
is unaffected when maximum or minimum elements are

(6)

f(\Delta(\hat{P}_{T}{\smallsetminus}\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}),x)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}\alpha_{\hat{P}}(S)x^{|S|}=\sum_{S\subseteq T}\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)x^{|S|}(1+x)^{|T{\smallsetminus}S|}

and

(7)

h(\Delta(\hat{P}_{T}),x)=\sum_{S\subseteq T}\alpha_{\hat{P}}(S)x^{|S|}(1-x)^{|T{\smallsetminus}S|}=\sum_{S\subseteq T}\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)x^{|S|}

for every
T\subseteq[n]
, where the second
equality in each case is a consequence of
Equation (
5
).

Example 2.2

Let

be the Boolean lattice

The

Let

Let

Permutations with restricted descent set

This section proves that the polynomials

are real-rooted, as claimed in part (a) of

, and in particular unimodal,
locates their peak and discusses some interesting

[

]

Crucial to the proof will be the polynomials

p^{T}_{n,k}(x)=\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1,k+1}:\,{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T}x^{{\rm des}(w)},

and

such that

that

and

, where

and, as mentioned in

,
A^{T}_{n}(x):=A^{T\cap[n-1]}_{n}(x)
for
T\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
.
We set

when

; these polynomials appeared in
[

]

[

, Section 2.2]
and have

, Section 2]

, Section 3]

For all

We proceed by induction on

We recall that a polynomial

4.
Rank-selected subposets of simplicial
posets

This section proves part (b) of
Theorem
1.2
and gives an application.
We recall that a finite poset
P
with a minimum
element
\hat{0}
is said to be
simplicial

[
33
]

[

, Section III.6]
if the
interval
[\hat{0},y]
is isomorphic to a Boolean
lattice for every

.
The enumerative invariant of a graded simplicial
poset

of rank

which will be essential to
the proof is the

-polynomial
of
P
.
This was defined by Stanley
[
33
]
as

h(P,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}(P)\,x^{i}(1-x)^{n-i},

where
f_{i-1}(P)
is the number of elements of

P
of rank
i
. We then have

(14)

f_{j-1}(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}{n-i\choose j-i}h_{i}(P)

for
j\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and
h(P,x)=h(\Delta,x)
, if
P
is the face poset of an

(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
.
Stanley
[
33
]
(see also

[
34
, Section III.6]
) showed that

has nonnegative coefficients for every
Cohen–Macaulay simplicial poset
P
.

Another essential ingredient for the proof of
Theorem
1.2
is the following statement
(an exercise from
[
34
]
), which expresses
the flag
h
-vector of a graded simplicial poset
in terms of its
h
-vector. We provide a proof for
the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
34
, Exercise III.15]
)

Let
P
be a graded simplicial poset of rank
n
.
Then,

\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}(P)\ \#\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}:\,w(n+1)=k+1,{\rm Des}(w)=[n+1]{\smallsetminus}S\}

for every
S\subseteq[n]
.

Proof.

Let
{\rm Asc}(w):=[n]{\smallsetminus}{\rm Des}(w)
be the set of
ascents of a permutation
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}
. We need
to show that

\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}(P)\ \#\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}:\,w(n+1)=k+1,{\rm Asc}(w)=S\}

for every
S\subseteq[n]
or, equivalently, that

\alpha_{\hat{P}}(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}h_{k}(P)\ \#\{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}:\,w(n+1)=k+1,{\rm Asc}(w)\subseteq T\}

for every
T\subseteq[n]
. Let us write
T=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r}\}\subseteq[n]
, with
1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{r}\leq n
. There are
f_{a_{r}-1}(P)

elements of rank
a_{r}
in
P
and
{a_{r}\choose a_{1},a_{2}-a_{1},\dots,a_{r}-a_{r-1}}
chains of elements of
ranks
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r-1}
in any Boolean
lattice of rank
a_{r}
. Given this and
Equation (
14
), we find that

\displaystyle\alpha_{\hat{P}}(T)

\displaystyle=f_{a_{r}-1}(P){a_{r}\choose a_{1},a_{2}-a_{1},\dots,a_{r}-a_{r-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n-k\choose a_{r}-k}h_{k}(P){a_{r}\choose a_{1},a_{2}-a_{1},\dots,a_{r}-a_{r-1}}.

Thus, it suffices to verify that
{n-k\choose a_{r}-k}{a_{r}\choose a_{1},a_{2}-a_{1},\dots,a_{r}-a_{r-1}}
is
equal to the number of permutations
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}

such that
w(n+1)=k+1
and
{\rm Asc}(w)\subseteq T
,
a task which can safely be left to the reader.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Given Theorem
3.1
, we only need
to show part (b). By Lemma

\displaystyle\beta_{\hat{P}}(S)

]
.

.

The polynomial

to the poset

5.
Noncrossing partition lattices

This section proves Theorem
1.3
.
We first recall the definition of
{\rm NC}_{W}
. Let
W

be a finite Coxeter group with rank

r_{W}
and set of reflections
{\mathcal{T}}
. For
\alpha\in W
we denote by
\ell_{\mathcal{T}}(\alpha)
the smallest

such that
\alpha
can be written as a product of

{\mathcal{T}}
. We define the partial order

\preceq
on

by letting

if
\ell_{\mathcal{T}}(\alpha)+\ell_{\mathcal{T}}(\alpha^{-1}\beta)=\ell_{\mathcal{T}}(\beta)
, in other words if there exists a
shortest factorization of
\alpha
into reflections
which is a prefix of such a shortest factorization
of
\beta
. Then,
{\rm NC}_{W}
is defined as the closed
interval
[e,\gamma]
in
(W,\preceq)
, where
e\in W
is the identity element and
\gamma
is any
Coxeter element of
W
. The noncrossing partition
poset
{\rm NC}_{W}
is a rank-symmetric, graded
lattice with rank function
\ell_{\mathcal{T}}
and rank
r_{W}
;
its combinatorial type is independent of the choice
of
\gamma
. A detailed exposition of noncrossing
partition lattices can be found in

[
1
, Chapter 2]
.

An important role in our first proof of
Theorem
1.3
(a) will be played by the
polynomials

p_{n,k}(x)=\sum_{w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}:\,w(1)=k+1}x^{{\rm des}(w)},

where
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
, discussed in
Section
3
.

First proof of
Theorem
1.3
(a).

Let
r_{W}=n
be the
rank of
W
. We first assume that

is
irreducible. Then, the zeta polynomial of
{\rm NC}_{W}

is given by the formula (see, for instance,

[

, Theorem 3.5.2]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}({\rm NC}_{W},m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(mh+d_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|W|}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left((h-d_{i})m+d_{i}(m+1)\right),

where

is the Coxeter number of

and

are its degrees. Since

.

, where

are the irreducible components
of

In view of Equations (

)
and (

. Since

.
∎

write
\bar{P}=P{\smallsetminus}\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
.
The formula of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
for the
flag
f
-vector of
P
shows that

\displaystyle f_{k-1}(\Delta(\bar{P}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n}{n-1\choose a_{1}}{n-1\choose a_{2}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}}\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}{n-1\choose a_{1}}\cdots{n-2\choose a_{i}-2}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}},

where the first two sums run through all
compositions
(a_{1},a_{2}\dots,a_{k+1})
of
n

with
k+1
parts. Using the fact that

{n-2\choose a_{i}-2}=\frac{a_{i}-1}{n-1}{n-1\choose a_{i}-1}
, changing the order of
summation in the double sum and replacing
a_{i}

with
a_{i}+1
yields that

\displaystyle f_{k-1}(\Delta(\bar{P}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2}\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n}{n-1\choose a_{1}}{n-1\choose a_{2}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}}\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n-1}\frac{a_{i}}{n-1}{n-1\choose a_{1}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{2}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}}.

Changing again the order of summation in the
double sum, since
\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}a_{i}=n-1
,
we find that

\displaystyle f_{k-1}(\Delta(\bar{P}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n}{n-1\choose a_{1}}{n-1\choose a_{2}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}}\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})\vDash n-1}{n-1\choose a_{1}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{2}}\cdots{n-1\choose a_{k+1}}.

We may rewrite this formula as

\displaystyle f_{k-1}(\Delta(\bar{P}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{T\subseteq[n-1],\,|T|=k}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\}\,+

\displaystyle\sum_{T\subseteq[n-2],\,|T|=k}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\},

whence

\displaystyle h(\Delta(P),x)

\displaystyle=h(\Delta(\bar{P}),x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f_{k-1}(\Delta(\bar{P}))\,x^{k}(1-x)^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle=2\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{T\subseteq[n-1],\,|T|=k}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{T\subseteq[n-2],\,|T|=k}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-1-k}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{T\subseteq[n-1]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\}x^{|T|}(1-x)^{n-1-|T|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\subseteq[n-2]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}:{\rm Des}(w)\subseteq T\}x^{|T|}(1-x)^{n-1-|T|}.

Setting
{\rm Des}(w)=S
in each sum, summing over
all
S\subseteq T
and changing the order of
summation yields that

\displaystyle h(\Delta(P),x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{S\subseteq[n-1]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n}:{\rm Des}(w)=S\}\sum_{S\subseteq T\subseteq[n-1]}x^{|T|}(1-x)^{n-1-|T|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{S\subseteq[n-2]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}:{\rm Des}(w)=S\}\sum_{S\subseteq T\subseteq[n-2]}x^{|T|}(1-x)^{n-1-|T|}

and hence that

\displaystyle h(\Delta(P),x)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{S\subseteq[n-1]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n}:{\rm Des}(w)=S\}x^{|S|}

\displaystyle+(1-x)\sum_{S\subseteq[n-2]}\#\{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}:{\rm Des}(w)=S\}x^{|S|}

(16)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{w\in[n-1]^{n}}x^{{\rm des}(w)}+(1-x)\sum_{w\in[n-1]^{n-1}}x^{{\rm des}(w)}.

Considering the cases
|w(1)|\neq w(2)
and
|w(1)|=w(2)
for a word
w\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n}
shows that the
number of words
w\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n}
with
{\rm des}(w)=k
is
equal to the coefficient of
x^{k}
in the
expression (
16
) and the proof
follows.

The exceptional types are handled via
straightforward computations via Sage
[
42
]
.
∎

Second proof of
Theorem
1.3
(a).

We recall that
{\rm NC}_{W}
is isomorphic to the product
of posets
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}{\rm NC}_{W_{i}}
, where
W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{\ell}
are the irreducible components
of
W
. This fact and Lemma
2.3
show
that we may assume that
W
is irreducible.

Let us first consider the case of groups of type
D
.
By Proposition
5.2
, it suffices
to show that
h_{n}(x):=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n}}x^{{\rm des}(w)}
is real-rooted for every
n\geq 2
.
For
k\geq 2
, we denote by
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,k}
the set of
words
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}
such that
(|w(1)|,w(2),\dots,w(k))\in[n-1]^{k}
and note that
{\mathcal{D}}_{n,n}={\mathcal{D}}_{n}
. We define the notion of descent for words

w\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n,k}
just as in the special case
k=n

and set

h_{n,k,j}(x)=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{D}}_{n,k}:\,w(k)=j}x^{{\rm des}(w)}

for
j\in[n-1]
. We will prove that
(h_{n,k,n-1}(x),\dots,h_{n,k,2}(x),h_{n,k,1}(x))
is an
interlacing sequence of real-rooted polynomials for
all
n,k\geq 2
by induction on
k
. This holds for

k=2
since then
h_{n,k,j}(x)=(2j-1)+(2n-2j-1)x

for every
j\in[n-1]
. The inductive step follows
by an application of part (c) of
Lemma
2.1
, since

h_{n,k+1,j}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}h_{n,k,i}(x)\,+\,x\sum_{i=j}^{n-1}h_{n,k,i}(x)

for
j\in[n-1]
. In particular,
h_{n,n+1,1}(x)=xh_{n}(x)
is real-rooted for every
n\geq 2
and
hence so is
h_{n}(x)
.

A similar (and even simpler)
argument shows that
\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}}x^{{\rm des}(w)}

is real-rooted for all
n,r\geq 1
. This covers the
cases of groups of types
A
and
B
.
The exceptional groups can be treated with a case by
case verification.
∎

Symmetric decompositions.

The second part of Theorem
1.3
will
be proven by an application a result of
Jochemko
[
22
]
, after the expressions of
Proposition
5.2
for

h(\Delta({\rm NC}_{W}),x)
are suitably rewritten.
For a polynomial or formal power series
H(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}x^{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[x]]
we use the
notation
{\mathcal{S}}_{r}(H(x))=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{rn}x^{n}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E_{n,r}(x)=\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}}x^{{\rm des}(w)}
.
Then,

x^{n}E_{n,r}(1/x)={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left(x(1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}\right)

for all
n,r\geq 1
.

Proof.

First we relate the polynomials
E_{n,r}(x)
to
the

\tilde{E}_{n,r}(x):=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n,r}}x^{{\rm asc}^{*}(w)},

where
{\mathcal{W}}_{n,r}
is the set of words
w:\{0,1,\dots,n\}\rightarrow[r]
with
w(0)=1
and
{\rm asc}^{*}(w)

is the number of indices
i\in[n]
such that

w(i-1)<w(i)
. We note that

x^{n-1}E_{n,r}(1/x)=\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}}x^{n-1-{\rm des}(w)}=\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}}x^{{\rm asc}^{*}(w)},

where
{\rm asc}^{*}(w)=n-1-{\rm des}(w)
is the number of
strict ascents of
w\in[r]^{n}
. Distinguishing the
cases
w(1)=1
and
w(1)\geq 2
for such a word
and for a word
w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n,r}
we get

\displaystyle x^{n-1}E_{n,r}(1/x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{n-1,r}(x)+\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}:\,w(1)\geq 2}x^{{\rm asc}^{*}(w)},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{n,r}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{n-1,r}(x)+x\sum_{w\in[r]^{n}:\,w(1)\geq 2}x^{{\rm asc}^{*}(w)}.

These equalities imply that

(17)

x^{n}E_{n,r}(1/x)=\tilde{E}_{n,r}(x)+(x-1)\tilde{E}_{n-1,r}(x).

We now recall that

(18)

\tilde{E}_{n,r}(x)={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left((1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}\right).

This formula follows from the identity

(19)

\sum_{m\geq 0}{n+rm\choose n}x^{m}=\frac{\tilde{E}_{n,r}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}},

which can be proved by a standard ‘placing balls
into boxes’ argument (see
[
31
, Corollary 8]

for a
q
-analogue) and the computation

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}{n+rm\choose n}x^{m}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left(\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}\right)={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left(\frac{(1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}}{(1-x^{r})^{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left((1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}\right)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}.

Combining Equations (
17
)
and (
18
) we get

\displaystyle x^{n}E_{n,r}(1/x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left((1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}\right)+(x-1){\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left((1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left((1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}+(x^{r}-1)(1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}_{r}\left(x(1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n+1}\right)

and the proof follows.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

(
[

, by

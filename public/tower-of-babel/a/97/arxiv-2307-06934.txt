Infinitely many monotone Lagrangian tori in higher projective spaces
Soham Chanda, Amanda Hirschi, Luya Wang

Abstract
. In [ 14 ] , Vianna constructed infinitely many exotic Lagrangian tori in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . We lift these tori to higher-dimensional projective spaces and show that they remain non-symplectomorphic. Our proof is elementary except for an application of the wall-crossing formula of [ 11 ] .



1.
Introduction

Given a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, we are interested in studying its monotone Lagrangian tori up to symplectomorphisms of
M
. Here,
monotone
means that the area homomorphism
\sigma:\pi_{2}(M,L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
[D]\mapsto\int_{D}\omega
is a positive scalar of the Maslov homomorphism
\mu:\pi_{2}(M,L)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined in
[
2
]
. We call a Lagrangian torus in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

exotic
if it is monotone and not symplectomorphic to the standard Clifford torus. The earliest example of an exotic torus dates back to
[
6
]
.

Recently, the use of almost toric fibrations has become an important tool in constructing new examples of Lagrangian tori. For example, Vianna has constructed infinitely many exotic tori in
\mathbb{P}^{2}

[
14
]
and in del Pezzo surfaces
[
15
]
. For more details on almost toric fibrations, see
[
13
,
9
,
8
]
. For previous constructions of non-Hamiltonian isotopic Lagrangian tori in higher dimensions, see for example
[
3
,
11
]
and
[
4
]
.

Our exotic examples are lifts
\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}
of the Vianna tori
T_{(a,b,c)}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Here
{(a,b,c)}
is a triple of natural numbers satisfying the Markov equation
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=3abc
. These tori are defined and reviewed in §
2.2
. Any exotic torus
T_{(a,b,c)}
can be obtained from the Clifford torus in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
by a sequence of mutations. We show that the lifted Vianna tori can be obtained from the Clifford torus in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
by a sequence of
solid mutations
, a generalisation of mutations to higher dimension. We study how the disk potentials change under a solid mutation, using as essential input a wall-crossing formula from
[
11
]
. Similar to
[
14
]
, we do not determine the disk potential explicitly. Instead, we show that the associated Newton polytope, defined in §
4.1
, is uniquely determined by
{(a,b,c)}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Newton polytope of the disk potential of
\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}
is a nondegenerate simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One
2
-dimensional face is a triangle with affine edge lengths
a
,
b
and
c
, while the affine length of any other edge is
1
. In particular, if
\{a,b,c\}\neq\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\}
, then there is no symplectomorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that maps the lifted Vianna torus
\overline{T}_{{(a,b,c)}}
to
\overline{T}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any
n\geq 3
,
\mathbb{P}^{n}
admits infinitely many distinct exotic Lagrangian tori.

Remark 1.3
.

The upcoming work
[
7
]
also finds infinitely many non-symplectomorphic monotone Lagrangian tori in complex projective spaces, using a different method.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose

Write
\psi=(\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime\prime})

\psi_{r}(x):=\psi^{\prime\prime}(r,x)

defines an (equivariant) isotopy from

to the identity.
Let
\rho\colon[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth cutoff function with
\rho(t)=t
for

\rho\equiv 1-2\epsilon
on

\rho^{\prime}(t)>0

(1-2\epsilon,1]

This is a diffeomorphism by the choice of
\rho
and

near

It suffices thus to extend the closed embedding

\psi^{(1)}(t,x)=(th(x),\varphi(x))

for

, where

is (equivariantly) isotopic to the identity and

\psi([1-2\epsilon,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})

be a smooth cutoff function, so that

near

Lemma 2.4

(Weinstein neighborhood theorem for solid mutation configurations)
.

Suppose
(L_{i},\mathfrak{T}_{i})\subset(M_{i},\omega_{i})
is a solid mutation configuration for

with
\dim(M_{0})=\dim(M_{1})
. Then there exist neighbourhoods

of

and a symplectomorphism
\psi\colon U_{0}\rightarrow U_{1}
mapping

to
(L_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{1})

Proof.

Fix a basis

\mathfrak{T}_{i}\cap L_{i}

. Let

to be the unique morphism of Lie groups with

maps the Lie algebra of

to the one of

, so
\phi(L_{0}\cap\mathfrak{T}_{0})=L_{1}\cap\mathfrak{T}_{1}
. Extend

to a symplectic bundle isomorphism

, which maps
T\mathfrak{T}_{0}|_{\partial\mathfrak{T}_{0}}
to

.

By the Weinstein neighbourhood theorem,

defines a symplectomorphism

and
U^{\prime}_{1}

, respectively
L_{1}
. Then

to a manifold tangent to

by a Hamiltonian isotopy to assume

maps

∎

2.2.
Lifting Vianna tori

A
Markov triple

(a,b,c)
is a triple of positive integers satisfying the Diophantine equation
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=3abc
. The set of these triples forms the vertices of the
Markov tree
, where
(a,b,c)
is connected to
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
by an edge if and only if
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is a
Markov mutation
of
(a,b,c)
, i.e., of the form
(3bc-a,b,c)
,
(a,3ac-b,c)
or
(a,b,3ab-c)
. By an elementary argument involving Vieta jumping, one sees that the Markov tree is indeed connected and infinite.

For any Markov triple
(a,b,c)
,
[
14
]
constructs a monotone Lagrangian torus
T_{(a,b,c)}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, whose Hamiltonian isotopy class is uniquely determined by
{(a,b,c)}
. To make this precise, let
\mathbb{P}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
be the weighted projective space associated to a Markov triple
{(a,b,c)}
with associated degenerations from
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. By
[
14
]
,
\mathbb{P}^{2}
can be obtained from
\mathbb{P}^{2}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
by performing at most three rational blow-downs.

Definition 2.9

(Vianna tori)
.

The
Vianna torus

T_{{(a,b,c)}}

associated to a Markov triple
{(a,b,c)}
is defined to be the central fiber of the almost toric fibration of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
obtained from the rational blow-down of
\mathbb{P}^{2}(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
.
1
1

1

Note that Vianna denotes
T_{(a,b,c)}
by
T(a^{2},b^{2},c^{2})
instead.

In
[
14
]
, he shows that mutating
T_{(a,b,c)}
results in a torus which is Hamiltonian isotopic to
T_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
, where
{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
is a Markov mutation of
{(a,b,c)}
.

We will lift these tori to monotone Lagrangian tori in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
using symplectic reduction. In Lemma
2.12
, we show that a mutation of
T_{(a,b,c)}
corresponds to a solid mutation of its lift in higher dimensions.

Fix thus
n\geq 3
and let

\mu_{n}\colon\mathbb{P}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n-2}:[z]\mapsto\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}(|z_{3}|^{2},\dots,|z_{n}|^{2})

be the moment map of the standard Hamiltonian
\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
-action acting on the last
n-2
homogeneous coordinates. In particular, it acts freely on
F_{n}:=\mu^{-1}_{n}\Bigl(\left\{\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}e_{i}\right\}\Bigr)
. A computation in local coordinates shows that
F_{n}/\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
equipped with the reduced symplectic form is symplectomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Let
q\colon F_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
be induced by the quotient map. A straightforward computation shows that the preimage of the Clifford torus in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
under
q
is the Clifford torus in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Given a Markov triple
{(a,b,c)}
, we define the
lifted Vianna torus
to be

T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}:=q^{-1}(T_{(a,b,c)}).

We see that
T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}
is a Lagrangian torus by the definition of the symplectic structure on the symplectic reduction. If
n
is clear from the context, we let
\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}:=T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}
.

Remark 2.11
.

As we can also do the reduction inductively, at each step reducing by an
S^{1}
-action, we see that for each
{(a,b,c)}
, we obtain a tower
T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}=:T_{n}\rightarrow T_{n-1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow T_{2}:=T_{(a,b,c)}
, where
T_{j+1}\rightarrow T_{j}
is the restriction of a principal
S^{1}
-bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{j}
. As the Euler class of this
S^{1}
-bundle is a multiple of
[\omega_{\text{FS}}]
, its restriction to
T_{j}
vanishes. Thus
T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}\rightarrow T_{(a,b,c)}
is a trivial
\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
-bundle.

Lemma 2.12
.

If
{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
is a Markov mutation of
{(a,b,c)}
, then
T^{(n)}_{{(a,b,c)}}
and
T^{(n)}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
are solid mutations of each other. In particular, each
T^{(n)}_{(a,b,c)}
is monotone.

Proof.

By Vianna’s construction and
[
11
, Lemma 4.21]
, there exists a mutation configuration
(T_{{(a,b,c)}},D)
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, so that the associated mutation of
T_{{(a,b,c)}}
is
T_{{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}}
. Let
\mathfrak{T}=q^{-1}(D)
. As

is the quotient map of a free
\mathbb{T}^{n-2}
-action,
(T^{(n)}_{{(a,b,c)}},\mathfrak{T})
is a solid mutation configuration in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. It follows from the local model in
[

]
, respectively §

that the solid-mutation of

along

is the lift of the mutation of

along

, and thus Hamiltonian isotopic to
T^{(n)}_{{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}}
. The last assertion follows from Lemma
2.8
.
∎

The first two assertions of Theorem

are immediate from Proposition
4.6
. We now show the last assertion. The
boundary Maslov-2 convex hull

\mho_{\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}}
of
\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}
is the convex hull of
\left\{\partial[u]\,|\,u\colon(\mathbb{D},S^{1})\rightarrow(\mathbb{P}^{n},\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)})\text{ holomorphic with }\mu(u)=2\right\}\subset\pi_{1}(\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)})
. By
[
14
, Remark 4.5]
and the simply-connectedness of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we can identify the Newton polytope of
W_{\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}}
with
\mho_{\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}}
. As the latter is an invariant of the Lagrangian up to symplectomorphism by
[
14
, Corollary 4.3]
, we obtain that
\overline{T}_{(a,b,c)}
is not symplectomorphic to
\overline{T}_{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
for
{(a,b,c)}\neq{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})}
.

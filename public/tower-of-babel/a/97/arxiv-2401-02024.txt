A convergence result for a local planning problem for mean field games and rigorous proof of a Freidlin-Ventchel-type Large Deviations Principle for the 1 + 1 1+1 KPZ equation Thanks: The second author was partially supported by the National Science Foundation grants DMS-1900599 and DMS-2153822, the Office for Naval Research grant N000141712095 and the Air Force Office for Scientific Research grant FA9550-18-1-0494.
Pierre-Louis Lions and Panagiotis E. Souganidis

Abstract
. We prove the convergence of a viscous approximation to an one dimensional local mean field type planning problem with singular initial and terminal measures. Then we use this result to give a rigorous proof to a Freidlin-Ventchel-type Large Deviations Principle for the height of the 1 + 1 1+1 KPZ equation.



1.
introduction

Motivated by the theory of large deviations for large heights and fixed times related to the
1+1
KPZ equation, here we consider, for
\varepsilon,\eta>0
, the parabolic mean field game system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-{\varepsilon}\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}=\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,0)=|x|^{2}/2\eta\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\delta
is the Dirac mass at
x=0
,
and show that, as
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
with
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
which can be arbitrarily small,
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{u}
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{\rho}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1])
where
(\overline{u},\overline{\rho})
is the unique solution of the planning problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\overline{u}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}\overline{u})^{2}=\overline{\rho}\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{t}\overline{\rho}+\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}\overline{u}\overline{\rho})=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{\rho}(\cdot,0)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$},\ \ \ \overline{\rho}(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \overline{u}(0,0)=0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

In addition, the variational problem

\begin{split}I(\varepsilon,\eta)=\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,0)\dfrac{x^{2}}{2\eta}dx+&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{2}\big[\rho^{2}+\dfrac{\beta^{2}}{\rho}\big]dxdt:\\[3.41432pt]
&\partial_{t}\rho+\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}\rho+\partial_{x}\beta=0\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\Big\},\end{split}

(1.3)

which, as we will see below, has
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\beta=\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
as unique minimizer, converges, as
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
with
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
for every
\alpha>0
, to

\overline{I}=\inf\Big\{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{2}\big[\dfrac{\beta^{2}}{\rho}+\rho^{2}\big]dxdt:\;\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\beta=0\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \rho(\cdot,0)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\ \ \rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\Big\},

(1.4)

whose unique minimizer is, at least formally,
(\overline{\rho},\sqrt{\overline{\rho}}\partial_{x}\overline{u})
.

The system (
1.1
) represents an one-dimensional planning mean field game problem in which the player wants to move a Dirac mass at
1
to a Dirac mass at
0
. The facts that these two measures are singular and the Dirac mass at
t=0
is created by the “singular” limit,
\eta\to 0
, in (
1.1
) make the passage to the limit difficult and delicate. In addition, the convergence of the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
requires special attention, since the estimates on
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
only yield convergence in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))
which is not enough to prove the convergence of the
I(\varepsilon,\eta)
’s. The global
L^{2}-
convergence is established directly, using the mean field structure of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), by estimating directly the
\|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))}
, which requires a detailed understanding of the limiting behavior of
some new terms. Note that we are not able to prove directly that the
I(\varepsilon,\eta)
’s converge to
\overline{I}
despite the strong convexity of the functionals involved. Indeed, the singularities due the Dirac masses seem to prevent such a direct approach.

We remark here that (
1.1
) cannot be solved if we start with
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,0)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}
, which in turn will require
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,0)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}
. Indeed, having the former, will require having a Brownian bridge with a square integrable drift, which is known not to exist. Hence, it is necessary to introduce the penalization
x^{2}/2\eta
.

The

KPZ equation

\partial_{t}h-{\kappa}\partial_{x}^{2}h+l(\partial_{x}h)^{2}=\sqrt{D}\xi\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,T),

The nonlinearity arises from the asymmetry between the two phases,

\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\beta+\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}\rho=0\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\ \ \rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}.

(3.2)

Since both
I_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon}
and the constraint are strictly convex, there exists a unique minimizer
(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta},\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta})
of

I_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\inf\big\{I_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\rho,\beta):\rho,\beta\ \text{satisfying}\ \eqref{takis62}\big\}.

(3.3)

It also well known (see
[
29
]
) that
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
with
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta}/\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is the solution of (
1.1
).

Finally, we remark that the minimization problem can also be written as

\begin{split}I_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\inf\big\{&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,0)\dfrac{x^{2}}{2\eta}dx+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\rho^{2}(x,t)+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho\alpha^{2}\big]dxdt:\\[3.41432pt]
&\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho\alpha)+\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}\rho=0\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\ \ \rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\big\}.\end{split}

(3.4)

It is clear that, if we could prove that, as
(\varepsilon,\eta)\to 0
,
I_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{I}
, where

\overline{I}=\min\big\{\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\rho^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}{\alpha^{2}\rho}\big]dxdt\;:\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\alpha\rho)=0\ \ \rho(\cdot,0)=\rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\big\},

(3.5)

we would have that the minimizer
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
converges to the unique minimizer of
\overline{I}
.

Here, we are not able to provide a direct proof of the convergence of the functional, which, of course, follows after we have established the convergence of the solutions.

We begin with a result about a uniform upper bound on
I_{\varepsilon,\eta}
as well as the easy part of what would have been a convergence result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,1)
there exists
C>0
, which is independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
, such that,
if
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
,
then

(i)~\sup\big\{I_{\varepsilon,\eta}:{\varepsilon\leq\eta^{\alpha}}\big\}\leq C\ \ \text{and}\ \ (ii)~\underset{(\varepsilon,\eta)\to(0,0)\ \varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}}{\liminf}I_{\varepsilon,\eta}\geq\overline{I}.

(3.6)

Proof.

To prove (
3.6
)(i), we construct an admissible pair
(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta},\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta})

such that, for some uniform
C>0
,

I_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta},\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta})\leq C.

Let
p
the fundamental solution of the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)
, that is,
p(x,t)=\exp{(-x^{2}/4t)}/\sqrt{2t}
.

In
\mathbb{R}\times(1/2,1)
, we take
\rho(x,t)=p(x,b(t))
with
b
a smooth decreasing function and
b(1)=0
.
It is immediate that

0=\partial_{t}\rho-b^{\prime}\partial_{x}^{2}\rho=\partial_{t}\rho+\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}\rho-(b^{\prime}+\varepsilon)\partial_{x}(\rho\partial_{x}(\log\rho))=\partial_{t}\rho+\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho a)\ \ \text{with}\ \ a(x,t)=\dfrac{b^{\prime}(t)+\varepsilon}{2b(t)}x.

We also have

\int_{1/2}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}dxdt\approx\int_{1/2}^{1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{b}}dt\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{1/2}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho a^{2}dxdt\approx\int_{1/2}^{1}\dfrac{(b^{\prime}+\varepsilon)^{2}}{b}dt.

We choose
b(t)=\varepsilon(1-t)+(1-t)^{\theta}
for
\theta\in(1,2)
. Then, for some
C>0
,

\int_{1/2}^{1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{b}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{1/2}t^{-\theta/2}\leq C\ \text{ and}\ \int_{1/2}^{1}\dfrac{(b^{\prime}+\varepsilon)^{2}}{b}dt\leq\int_{0}^{1/2}t^{\theta-2}dt\leq C.

Finally,

\rho(x,1/2)=\dfrac{1}{2b_{\varepsilon}}\exp[-\dfrac{x^{2}}{4b_{\varepsilon}}]\ \ \text{with}\ \ b_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon/2+1/2^{\theta}.

In
(0,1/2)
, we take
\rho(x,t)=p(x,\gamma+c(t))
with
\gamma>0
and
c
an increasing smooth function with
c(0)=0
. It follows as above that

\partial_{t}\rho+\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho a)=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \ \text{with}\ \ a(x,t)=\dfrac{c^{\prime}(t)+\varepsilon}{\gamma+c(t)}x.

Moreover,

\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{R}x^{2}\rho(x,0)dx=\dfrac{1}{\eta}\int_{R}x^{2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\exp{(-x^{2}/\gamma)}dx\approx\dfrac{\gamma}{\eta}=1\ \ \text{if}\ \ \gamma=\eta.

and

\int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}dxdt\approx\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma+c(t)}}dt\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{0}^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho a^{2}dxdt\approx\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{(c^{\prime}(t)+\varepsilon)^{2}}{\gamma+c(t)}dt.

We choose
c(t)=c_{0}t^{\theta}
for the same
\theta\in(1,2)
as in the construction of
\rho
in
(1/2,1)
.

For
\rho
to be continuous at
t=1/2
, we choose
c_{0}
so that
\eta+c_{0}/2^{\theta}=\varepsilon/2+1/2^{\theta},
and note
that
c_{0}\to 1
in the limit
\eta,\varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, for some
C>0
,

\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\eta+c(t)}}dt=\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{1}{t^{\theta/2}}dt\leq C

and

\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{(c^{\prime}(t)+\varepsilon)^{2}}{\gamma+c(t)}dt=\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{(c_{0}\theta t^{\theta-1}+\varepsilon)^{2}}{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}dt\leq 2\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{(c_{0}\theta t^{\theta-1})^{2}}{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}dt+2\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}dt.

It is clear that

\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{(c_{0}\theta t^{\theta-1})^{2}}{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}dt\leq C,

while

\int_{0}^{1/2}\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\eta+c_{0}t^{\theta}}dt\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\eta^{1-1/\theta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{1+c_{0}s^{\theta}}ds\leq C\dfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\eta^{1-1/\theta}}.

For this last integral to be finite we need to choose
\varepsilon^{2}\leq\eta^{\alpha}
with
\alpha=\frac{\theta-1}{\theta}\in(0,1).

To prove (
3.6
)(ii), let
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
be the minimizer of the strictly convex functional

I_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, that is, for
\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
,

I_{\varepsilon,\eta}=I_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta},\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,0)\dfrac{x^{2}}{2\eta}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\rho^{2}_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\beta^{2}_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\big]dxdt

(3.7)

and

\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\partial_{x}\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \ \rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}.

(3.8)

It follows from (
3.6
)(i) that, for some
C\in(0,\infty)
,

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}_{\varepsilon,\eta}dxdt\leq C\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{\beta^{2}_{\varepsilon,\eta}}{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}}dxdt\leq C.

(3.9)

We also know that, for any measurable subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)
,

\iint_{A}|\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta}|dxdt\leq 2C,

which is an immediate consequence of (
3.20
) and the elementary inequality

|\beta|=\dfrac{|\beta|\rho}{\rho}\leq\dfrac{\beta^{2}}{2\rho}+\dfrac{\rho}{2}.

It follows that the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s converge weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))
to some
\rho
, and the
\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s and
\beta^{2}_{\varepsilon,\eta}/\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s weakly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))
to some
\beta
and
\beta^{2}/\rho
.

It is now clear that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq\eta^{\alpha}}{\liminf}I(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta},\beta_{\varepsilon,\eta})\geq I(\rho,\beta)

and

\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}\beta=0\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$},

and, hence, the claim.

∎

The first basic estimates for
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
are stated and proved in the next proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
,

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}[&\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{2}(x,t)+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)u_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{2}(x,t)]dxdt+{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,0)\frac{x^{2}}{2\eta}dx\\[4.2679pt]
&+\sup_{t\in[0,1]}[{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dx+{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\dfrac{x^{2}}{2(\eta+t)}dx]\leq C.\end{split}

(3.10)

Proof.

Throughout the proof we omit the explicit dependence of
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
on
{\varepsilon,\eta}
and simply write
u
and
\rho.

The first three bounds are immediate from Proposition
3.2
.

The uniform in
t
,
\varepsilon
and
\eta
bound on
{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)dx
follows from the first two bounds and the identity

\dfrac{d}{dt}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)dx={\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}[\dfrac{1}{2}\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dx+\rho^{2}(x,t)]dx.

(3.11)

Finally, for the bound on
{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}}\rho(x,t)x^{2}dx
we observe that, since
\rho\geq 0
,

u
is a supersolution of

w_{t}-\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}w+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}w)^{2}=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ w(x,0)=x^{2}/2\eta,

whose solution is

w(x,t)=\dfrac{x^{2}}{2(\eta+t)}+\varepsilon(\text{ln}(\eta+t)-\text{ln}\;\eta).

(3.12)

It follows that

u\geq w\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times[0,1].

(3.13)

The claim follows from the bound on
\underset{t\in(0,1)}{\sup}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)dx
and the assumption that
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
.

∎

To show the convergence of
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, we need several estimates involving derivatives, which we formulate and prove in the propositions below.

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
\theta\in(0,1/2)
and uniformly for
\varepsilon
and
\eta
such that
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
, the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in
L^{\infty}((\theta,1);L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
, the
\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in
L^{2}((\eta,1);L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
, and, hence, the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in
L^{2}((\eta,1);H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

To simplify the writing, in what follows we drop, when possible, the dependence on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
and we write
u
and
\rho
. Moreover, we denote by
C
constants may change from line to line, are uniform in
\varepsilon
and
\eta
and may depend on
\theta
.

Note that the reason we obtain bounds for
t\in(\theta,1)
instead of
(0,1)
is in order to have estimates that do not depend on
\eta
.

To set things up, we first observe that, since
t\in(\theta,1)
, it follows from Proposition
3.3
that

\underset{t\geq\theta}{\sup}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)|x|^{2}dx\leq C,

and, hence, there exists some
R_{0}>0
such that, for all
R\geq R_{0}
and
t\in(\theta,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}\rho(x,t)|x|^{2}dxdt\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho(x,t)|x|^{2}dxdt\geq\dfrac{1}{2}.

(3.14)

The proof consists of two parts. In the first, we provide, for
R\geq R_{0}\geq 1
, a bound on
\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}u(x,t)dx
by a quantity that depends on
\displaystyle\int_{h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}u_{x}^{2}(x,t)dxdt
, which we bound in the second step.

For every
R\geq R_{0}
, let

\langle u\rangle(t)=\dfrac{1}{2R}\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}u(x,t)dx,

and observe that, in view of the the lower bound on
u
in (
3.13
),

\langle u\rangle(t)\geq R^{2}/6(\eta+t).

Using (
3.14
), Poincare’s inequality and the bound on
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)dxdt
, we find that, for all
R\geq R_{0}
,

\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{2}\langle u\rangle(t)\leq\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho(x,t)&\langle u\rangle(t)dx\leq\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho(x,t)|u-\langle u\rangle(t)|dx+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho(x,t)u(x,t)dxdt\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq C+\Big(\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}\rho^{2}(x,t)dx\;\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}|u(x,t)-\langle u\rangle(t)|^{2}dx\;\Big)^{1/2}\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq C\Big(1+\Big(\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dx\;\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(x,t)dx\;\Big)^{1/2}\Big)\\[3.41432pt]
&=C\Big(1+\phi^{1/2}(t)\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt)^{1/2}\Big),\end{split}

where
\phi(t)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(x,t)dx
and
\phi\in L^{1}((0,1))
.

Since
\rho
and
u
are even in
x
, throughout the argument below all integrals are taken over
[0,R]
instead of
[-R,R]
.

Hence,

\langle u\rangle(t)=\frac{1}{R}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}u(x,t)dx,\ \ \displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt=2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt,

and

\langle u\rangle(t)\leq C\Big(1+\phi(t)^{1/2}\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dx\big)^{1/2}\Big).

(3.15)

Let

F(R,\theta)=\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt

Integrating the
u
-equation in (
1.1
) over
[0,R]\times[h,1]
, using that
R_{0}\geq 1
,
u\geq 0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)dx=1
and (
3.15
) give, after some elementary rearrangement of terms,

\begin{split}F(R,\theta)&\leq 2\Big(1+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}u(x,\theta)dx+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1}\partial_{x}u(R,s)ds\Big)\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq 2R\Big(1+\langle u\rangle(\theta)+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1}\partial_{x}u(R,s)ds\Big)\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq CR\Big(1+\big(\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(R,t)dt\big)^{1/2}+\phi(\theta)^{1/2}\big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,\theta)dx\big)^{1/2}\Big)\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq CR\Big(1+\big(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial R}F(R,\theta)\big)^{1/2}+\phi(\theta)^{1/2}\big(-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\theta}F(R,\theta)\big)^{1/2}\Big),\\[4.2679pt]
\end{split}

and, finally,

F(R,h)^{2}\leq CR^{2}\Big(1+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial R}F(R,h)-\phi(\theta)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial h}F(R,h)\Big).

(3.16)

We now fix
\overline{R}\geq R_{0}
and
\theta_{0}
, write
F_{0}=F(\overline{R},\theta_{0})
, and, for
s>0
, we seek
t_{0}>0
such that the odes

\dot{R}=1\ \ \text{and }\ \ \dot{\theta}=-\phi(\theta)\ \ \text{in}\ \ [0,t_{0}]\ \ \text{and}\ \ R(t_{0})=\overline{R}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \theta(t_{0})=\theta_{0},

(3.17)

have a solution. We do this at the end of the ongoing proof.

Assuming for the moment that (
3.17
) has solution, we observe that (
3.16
) yields that, for some
C>0
, either

\text{either}F\leq\overline{C}\ \ \text{or}\ \ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}F(R(s),\theta(s))\geq\dfrac{1}{C}F^{2}.

The second alternative implies

\dfrac{1}{F(\overline{R},\theta_{0})}-\dfrac{1}{F(R(s),\theta(s))}\geq\dfrac{s}{C},

and, finally,

F(\overline{R},\theta_{0})\leq\dfrac{C}{\theta_{0}}.

(3.18)

We now return to solving (
3.17
), the main issue being the existence
of an interval of existence so that
\theta(t_{0})=\theta_{0}
.

Let
a=\phi\in L^{1}(0,1)
, assume without loss of generality that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a(z)dz\geq 1
and rewrite the
\theta
equation as

\dfrac{d\theta}{a}=-ds,

which leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{\theta_{0}}\dfrac{1}{a(z)}dz=s.

(3.19)

Since, in view of Jensen’s inequality,

\dfrac{1}{\theta_{0}-\theta}\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{\theta_{0}}\dfrac{1}{a(z)}dz\geq\Big({\dfrac{1}{\theta_{0}-\theta}\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{\theta_{0}}a(z)dz}\Big)^{-1},

it follows from (
3.19
) that

s\geq(\theta_{0}-\theta)^{2}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{\theta_{0}}a(z)dz\Big)^{-1}.

Letting
\theta\to 0
, we see that, to have
\theta(t_{0})=\theta_{0}
, it suffices to choose
t_{0}\geq\theta_{0}^{2}/\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{\theta_{0}}{a(z)}dz,

which is possible in view of the assumption that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a(z)dz\geq 1
.

Finally, we choose
R(0)=\overline{R}-t_{0}
.

Having established that the
\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in
L^{2}((\theta,1);L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
, we now turn to the bound on

u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
in
L^{\infty}((\eta,1);L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})),
which will follow from (
3.15
).

Fix
R^{\prime}>R\geq R_{0}
. Since
\displaystyle\int_{\theta/2}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime}}(\partial_{x}u_{x})^{2}(x,s)dxds
is bounded independently of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
, there exists
R^{\prime\prime}\in(R,R^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\theta/2}^{1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(R^{\prime\prime},s)ds\ \ \text{ is bounded independently of $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$}.

(3.20)

We also know from the first estimate in this proof and the fact that
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}dxds\leq C
that

\displaystyle\int_{\theta/2}^{\theta}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime\prime}}\big[(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,s)+\rho^{2}(x,s)\big]dxds\ \ \text{ is bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$}

It follows that there exists some

h\in[\theta/2,\theta]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime\prime}}\rho^{2}(x,h)dx\leq C\ \ \text{and}\ \ \displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime\prime}}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,h)dx\ \ \text{are bounded uniformly in $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$}

(3.21)

which together with (
3.15
) imply that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R{{}^{\prime\prime}}}u(x,h)dx\ \ \text{is bounded independently of $\varepsilon$ and $\eta$}.

(3.22)

Integrating the
u
equation over
[0,R^{\prime\prime}]\times[h,t]
we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime\prime}}u(x,t)dx\leq 1+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R^{\prime\prime}}u(x,h)dx+\varepsilon(\displaystyle\int_{h}^{1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(R^{\prime\prime},s)ds)^{1/2}.

(3.23)

In view of the above observation and (
3.20
) we know that the right hand side of (
3.23
) is bounded independently of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
, so
the same is true for
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R{{}^{\prime\prime}}}u(x,t)dx
, and, hence,
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}u(x,t)dx
.

∎

We prove next a new uniform estimate on
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
, which, given that their limit will be Dirac masses at
t=0
and
t=1
, can be only local in time.

Proposition 3.5
.

For
\varepsilon
and
\eta
such that
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
and any
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, the
\partial_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s and
\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
\eta
in
L^{2}(\theta,1-\theta;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
, and the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
\eta
in
L^{2}(\theta,1-\theta;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we omit the dependence of
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
.

A simple computation, which can be justified by considering approximate problems with regularized
\rho(\cdot,1)
and then passing to the limit, leads, for each
t\in(0,1)
, to the identity

\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}u(x,t)dx=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)+\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}(x,t)\big]dx.

(3.24)

Next we localize the estimate in time staying away from
0
and
1
. For this, we consider a smooth cut-off function
\chi
supported in
(\theta,1-\theta)
and multiply (
3.24
) by
\chi^{2}
.

Integrating in time we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\chi^{2}(t)\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}u(x,t)dxdt=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi^{2}(t)\big[(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)+\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}(x,t)\big]dxdt,

and, hence,

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi^{2}(t)\big[(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)&+\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}(x,t)\big]dxdt=2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(t)\chi^{\prime}(t)\partial_{x}\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}u(x,t)dxdt\\[4.2679pt]
&=-2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(t)\chi^{\prime}(t)\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)dxdt\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(t)\chi^{\prime}(t)\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt+2\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\chi^{\prime})^{2}(t)\rho^{2}(x,t)dxdt,\end{split}

which leads to the claim in view of the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\chi^{\prime})^{2}(t)\rho^{2}(x,t)dxdt\leq C\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(x,t)dxdt.

∎

The first result is about a surprising estimate in view of the fact that

Proposition 3.6
.

For each
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists an, independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta

such that, for all
\alpha\in(0,1/2)

(3.25)

Proof.

As before, when there is no confusion, we drop the dependence on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
.

In the proof we work with
\theta=1/2
and leave it up to the reader to adjust the argument for
\theta\in(0,1).

The starting point is the identity (

)
which can be rewritten as

\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)dx=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)+\rho(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{2}u)^{2}(x,t)\big]dx.

(3.26)

It follows that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)dx\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\big(-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)dx\big)^{2},

and, hence, there exists
C>0
such that, for all

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)dx\leq\dfrac{C}{1-t}.

(3.27)

It follows from Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.5
that there exists
C>0
such that, for each
\varepsilon
and
\eta
, there exists
t_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in(1/4,1/2)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[(\partial_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})+(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})\big]dx\leq C.

and, hence,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dx\leq C.

(3.28)

Integrating (
3.26
) over
(t_{\varepsilon,\eta},t)
we find

\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\displaystyle\int_{t_{\varepsilon,\eta}}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\rho(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})\partial_{x}u(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\rho(x,t)\partial_{x}u(x,t)dx,

and, in view of (
3.27
) and (

), there exists some other

,

\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)dx\leq\dfrac{C}{1-t}.

(3.29)

Next, we recall that, for every
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

[\rho(\cdot,t)]_{C^{0,\alpha}}^{2+\beta}\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\rho)^{2}(x,t)dx,

which together with (
3.29
) imply that

\displaystyle\int_{1/2}^{t}[\rho(\cdot,s)|_{C^{0,\alpha}}^{2+\beta}ds\leq\dfrac{C}{1-t}.

Then, an elementary analysis lemma which we state and prove after the end of the ongoing proof yields (
3.25
).

∎

We state and prove next the auxiliary fact we used at the end of the proof above. For simplicity we change
t
to
1-t
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
a:(0,1)\to[0,\infty)
and
m>2
be such that, for
h,h^{\prime}\in(0,1/2)
with
h<h^{\prime}
,
\int_{h}^{h^{\prime}}a^{m}(s)ds\leq C/(h^{\prime}-h)
. Then
\int_{0}^{1/2}a(s)ds\leq C.

Proof.

Observe that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1/2}a(s)ds=\sum_{n\geq 0}\displaystyle\int_{1/2^{n+1}}^{1/2^{n}}a(s)ds\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\Big[\Big(\displaystyle\int_{1/2^{n+1}}^{1/2^{n}}a^{m}(s)ds\Big)^{1/m}\Big(2^{-{n+1}}\Big)^{m-1/m}\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq C\sum_{n\geq 0}{2^{-{(n+1)/m}}}2^{-(n+1)((m-1)/m)}=C\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{-n((m-1)/m)},\end{split}

and
\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{-n((m-1)/m)}<\infty
if
\frac{m-1}{m}>1/m
, that is,
m>2
.

∎

The next proposition provides
L^{\infty}-
bounds for

and improves their available regularity up to and including

Proposition 3.8
.

As
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
while
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
and every
\theta\in(0,1)
, the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in

L^{\infty}((\theta,1];L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}})(\mathbb{R}).
Moreover, for some uniform

,

\underset{t\in(\theta,1]}{\sup}\|u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}\text{loc}}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,\theta)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}\text{loc}}+C.

(3.30)

Moreover, they converge along subsequences and locally uniformly to a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation
\partial_{t}u+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}=\rho
in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)
satisfying .(
2.31
).

Proof.

In view of Proposition
3.6
and the comment at the beginning of its proof, (
3.30
) follows from the classical parabolic theory once we establish an
L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
bound on
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,\theta)\|.

Proposition
3.5
yields that, for every
\theta\in(0,1)
, the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are uniformly bounded in

L^{2}((\theta,1);H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}((\theta,1);L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Thus, for all
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, there exists
C_{\theta}>0
such that

\underset{t\in(\theta,1-\theta)}{\sup}\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}u(x,t)dx+\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1-\theta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)dxdt\leq C_{\theta}R,

(3.31)

which implies that, for some
t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}\in(\theta/2,\theta)
and
x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}u(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})dx+\displaystyle\int_{-R}^{R}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})\leq C_{\theta}(R+1),

(3.32)

and

u(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0},t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})\leq C_{\theta}.

(3.33)

It also follows from (
3.31
), that, for
m>1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1-\theta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t)}{(1+x^{2})^{m/2}}dxdt\leq C_{\theta},

which also yields some
t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,1}\in(\theta/2,\theta)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{u(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,1})+(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,1})}{(1+x^{2})^{m/2}}dx\leq C_{\theta}.

(3.34)

Finally, since the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are bounded in
L^{2}((t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0},1-\theta);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))
, we observe that

u(x,t)\leq v(x,t)+\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\rho(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}ds,

where
v
solves

\partial_{t}v-\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}v+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}=0\ \ \text{in}\ \ R\times(t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0},1-\theta)\ \quad\ v(\cdot,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})=u(\cdot,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}).

Below, to keep the writing simpler we write
u_{0}
for
u(\cdot,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}).

It follows that, for each progressively measurable
\alpha:(t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0},1-\theta)\to\mathbb{R}
,

u(x,t)\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{t_{\lambda,0}}^{t}\dfrac{1}{2}|\alpha_{s}|^{2}+u_{0}(X_{t})\Big],

(3.35)

where
dX_{s}=\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2\varepsilon}dW_{s}
and
X_{t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}}=x
.

We choose next the control
\alpha=\dfrac{x_{0}-x}{t-t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}}
. Then
dX_{s}=x_{0}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon}dW_{s}
and (
3.35
) yields, for
\tau=t-t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}
,

u(x,t)\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{t-t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}}+\mathbb{E}[u_{0}(x_{0}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon}W_{\tau})].

(3.36)

To conclude we estimate the last term of the right hand side of the inequality above. It follows from (
3.36
) that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[u_{0}(x_{0}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon}(W_{t}-W_{t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}})]&=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon\tau}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\varepsilon\tau}}dy\\[4.2679pt]
&\leq u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon\tau}}\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq 1}|u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)-u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})|e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\varepsilon\tau}}dy.\end{split}

(3.37)

Since
\displaystyle\int_{\theta}^{1}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})dx
is bounded, we have, for some uniform
C_{\theta}>0
,

\sup_{|y|\leq 1}|u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)-u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}))|\leq C_{\theta}.

Moreover, as it was shown above, for some
m>1
,
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{u_{0}(z)}{(1+|z|^{2})^{m/2}}dz\leq C_{\theta}.

It then follows that

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\varepsilon\tau}}\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq 1}|u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)-u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})|e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\varepsilon\tau}}dy\leq C

and

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\nu\tau}}\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq 1}u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2\nu\tau}}dy\leq\int_{|y|\geq 1}\dfrac{u_{0}(x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}+y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{m/2}}\sup_{|z|\geq 1}[{|z|^{m}}\frac{e^{\frac{-|z|^{2}}{\sqrt{2\nu\tau}}}}{\sqrt{2\nu\tau}}\;dy\leq C_{\theta},

and, hence, for some uniform
C>0
,

u(x,t)\leq\dfrac{(x-x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})^{2}}{2(t-t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})}+C_{\theta}.

(3.38)

This upper bound and the strong convergence of the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’ in
L^{2}((0,T);C(\mathbb{R}))
imply that the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0}
’s converge locally uniformly to a viscosity solution of

\partial_{t}u+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}=\rho.

(3.39)

The convergence is established in compact subsets of
(0,1)
. To extend it on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]
, It remains to show that (
3.38
) holds on
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]
, which is possible in view of Proposition
3.6
.

∎

The proof above extends to

u(x,t)\leq\dfrac{(x-x_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})^{2}}{2(t-t_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{\theta,0})}+C+F(x,t),

where, as

,

in

which is not enough to conclude.

Next we investigate the behavior of
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
near

Proposition 3.9
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
with
\varepsilon\;\text{log}\;\eta\to 0
and for any nonnegative compactly supported
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
, which is smooth in its support
[-a,a]
, there exists, an independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
,
e:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\underset{t\to 0}{\lim}e(t)=0
and

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\;\text{log}\;\eta\to 0}{\limsup}\big[\displaystyle\int_{R}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\dfrac{1}{t^{2/3}}\chi(\dfrac{x}{t^{2/3}})dx\big]\leq\displaystyle\int_{R}\dfrac{x^{2}}{2t}\dfrac{1}{t^{2/3}}\chi(\dfrac{x}{t^{2/3}})dx+e(t).

(3.40)

Proof.

Like in previous proofs we write
u
and
\rho
in place of
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
.

Recall (
3.12
) and
observe that
\underset{t\to 0}{\lim}\varepsilon(\log(t+\eta)-\log\eta)=0
uniformly on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
in view of the assumption on
\varepsilon
and
\eta
.

Set

u(x,t)=\dfrac{x^{2}}{2(t+\eta)}+\varepsilon(\log(t+\eta)-\log\eta))+v(x,t).

It is immediate that

\partial_{t}v-\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}v+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}+\dfrac{x}{t+\eta}\partial_{x}v=0\ \text{and}\ v\geq 0\ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \text{and}\ v(\cdot,0)=0.

(3.41)

Given
\chi
as in the statement, set
\chi_{\eta}(x,t)=\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}}\chi(\dfrac{x}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}})
.

Then

\begin{split}\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}dx-&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\partial_{t}\chi_{\eta}dx+\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{t+\eta}\partial_{x}v\chi_{\eta}dx\\[3.41432pt]
&-\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v\chi_{\eta}dx=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\chi_{\eta}\leq\dfrac{C}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}},\end{split}

(3.42)

the last inequality following from the facts that
\chi
is bounded and
\int_{R}\rho(x,t)dx=1
.

Next we analyze several of the terms in the left hand side of (
3.42
) beginning with the last one.

Integrating by parts and using that
|\chi^{\prime}|\leq C
and
|\chi^{\prime\prime}|\leq C
in
[-a,a]
, we find

\begin{split}|\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v\chi_{\eta}dx|&\leq C\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v(\cdot,x)\|_{\infty}+|\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\partial_{x}^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx|\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq C\Big[\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}+|\displaystyle\int_{-a(t+\eta)^{4/3}}^{a(t+\eta)^{4/3}}v\partial_{x}^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx|\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq C\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

It also follows from (
3.41
) that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{R}vdx-\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{R}vdx+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}=1,

and, thus, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho dx=1
,

\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big(\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{R}vdx+\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{R}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}dx\Big)=\dfrac{1}{t+\eta},

which then yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(x,t)dx\leq(t+\eta)\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta},

(3.43)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{s+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}(x,s)dxds\leq\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}.

(3.44)

Using next the imbedding
\|w\|\leq c\|w\|^{1/3}\|\partial_{x}w\|^{2/3}
, we find from (
3.43
) that, for each
t\in(0,1)
,

\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq c\Big((t+\eta)\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\Big)^{1/3}\|\partial_{x}v(\cdot,t)\|^{2/3},

and, therefore, in view of (
3.44
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2}}\dfrac{1}{\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta}}\|v(\cdot,s)\|^{3}ds\leq c\;\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}.

Then,

\begin{split}\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}ds&=\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big[\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2/3}}\dfrac{1}{(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta})^{1/3}}\|v(\cdot,s)\|\big]\big[\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2/3}}(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta})^{1/3}\big]ds\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq c\varepsilon\big(\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big)^{1/3}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{s+\eta}\big(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta}\big)^{1/2}ds)\Big)^{2/3}\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq c\varepsilon\Big(\text{log}\frac{t+\eta}{\eta}\Big)^{2/3}\leq c\varepsilon\text{log}\frac{t+\eta}{\eta},\end{split}

and, thus,

\underset{\varepsilon,\eta,\varepsilon\text{log}\eta\to 0}{\limsup}\big[\varepsilon|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial^{2}_{x}v\chi_{\eta}dxds|\big]\leq 0.

(3.45)

We continue with the second term in (
3.42
) and obtain the following sequence of equalities and inequalities.

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\partial_{t}\chi_{\eta}&=-\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}dx-\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi^{\prime}(\dfrac{x}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}})\dfrac{x}{(t+\eta)^{5/3}}dx\\[3.41432pt]
&=-\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\partial_{x}\chi_{\eta}xdx\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&=-\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)}\Big[\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}dx-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}dx-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}v\chi_{\eta}xdx\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&=\dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}v\chi_{\eta}xdx\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq\dfrac{2}{3}\Big[\dfrac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+2\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\eta}(\frac{x}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}})\big(\dfrac{x^{2}}{(t+\eta)^{2}}\big)dx\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq\dfrac{2}{3}\Big[\dfrac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+2\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi_{\eta}(\dfrac{x}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}})\Big(\dfrac{x}{(t+\eta)^{2/3})}\Big)^{2}dx\Big]\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq\dfrac{2}{3}\Big[\dfrac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+2C\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}}\Big].\end{split}

Finally, since

|\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{t+\eta}\partial_{x}v\chi_{\eta}dx|\leq\dfrac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+2\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x^{2}}{(t+\eta)^{2}}\chi_{\eta}dx\leq\dfrac{1}{8}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v)^{2}\chi_{\eta}dx+C\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{2/3}},

we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v\chi_{\eta})(x,t)dx\leq C\Big[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2/3}}ds+\varepsilon|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial^{2}_{x}v\chi_{\eta}dxds|\Big],

and, hence,

\underset{\varepsilon,\eta,\varepsilon\text{log}\eta\to 0}{\limsup}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\chi_{\eta}(x,t)dx\leq Ct^{1/3},

(3.46)

and the claim follows.

∎

4.
The convergence

We are now ready to prove the main result about the convergence of
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
to
(\overline{u},\rho)
.

Although it is possible to show, based on the interior estimates, the local uniform convergence in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]
of the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s to
\overline{u}
, the arguments so far do not allow to prove the convergence of the
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s to
\overline{\rho}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))
instead of only
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(0,1);L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

We develop here a different argument comparing directly
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and
(\overline{u},\rho)
using the classical mean field games identity. We need, however, to deal with the fact that
\overline{\rho}
is only Lipschitz continuous and not twice differentiable.

We also mention that we can complete the proof assuming that
\varepsilon\text{log}\eta\to 0
. However, since we need to to have
\varepsilon\leq C\eta^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
to obtain the a priori bounds on
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{2}dxdt
and
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}dxdt
, we state next the main result under this assumption.

Theorem 4.1
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\eta\to 0
, while
\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}
for some
A>0
,

u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{u}\ \ \text{locally uniformly in $\mathbb{R}\times(0,1]$},\quad\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{\rho}\ \ \text{in}\ \ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\varepsilon,\eta}\to\overline{I}.

Proof.

The proof of the convergence is based on comparing
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
and the solution
(\overline{u}_{\eta},\overline{\rho}_{\eta})

of the approximate first-order (
2.1
), for which know that, as
\eta\to 0
,
\overline{u}_{\eta}\to\overline{u}
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}\times(0,1[]
and
\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\to\overline{\rho}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1)).

The standard computation for mean field type systems gives

\begin{split}&\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})dx\\[3.41432pt]
&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta}|^{2}+\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\overline{\rho}_{\eta}}{2}|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}|^{2}\big)dxdt+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{R}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}\rho(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})-\partial_{x}^{2}u(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})\big)dx,\end{split}

and, since
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is smooth, after integrating by parts

\begin{split}&\dfrac{d}{dt}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,t)dx\\[3.41432pt]
&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta}|^{2}+\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\overline{\rho}_{\eta}}{2}|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}|^{2}\big)dxdt+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{R}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}-\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}\big)dx.\end{split}

(4.1)

Integrating (
4.1
) over
t\in(0,1)
– recall that both
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and
\overline{u}_{\eta}
are continuous in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
so that integration at
t=0
does not create any problem –we get

\begin{split}&\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,1)dx-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,0)dx\\[3.41432pt]
&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta}|^{2}+\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\overline{\rho}_{\eta}}{2}|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}|^{2}\big)dxdt+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}-\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}\big)dx,\end{split}

(4.2)

and, thus,

\begin{split}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta}|^{2}+&\dfrac{\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\overline{\rho}_{\eta}}{2}|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}|^{2}\big)dxdt\\[3.41432pt]
&=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,1)dx-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,0)dx\\[3.41432pt]
&+\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}-\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\big)dx.\end{split}

(4.3)

The claim about the convergence of the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s and
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s will follow, if we show that that the right hand side of (
4.3
) tends to
0
as
\varepsilon,\eta\to 0
while

\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}
, and we proceed to prove this.

First we observe that, since
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,1)=\overline{\rho}_{\eta}(\cdot,1)=\text{\boldmath$\delta$}
and
u_{\varepsilon.\eta}(x,0)=\overline{u}_{\eta}(x,0)=x^{2}/(2\eta)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,1)dx-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{u}_{\eta})(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta})(x,0)dx=0.

(4.4)

Thus, to conclude we must show that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}-\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\big)dx=0.

(4.5)

In view of the construction of
\overline{u}_{\eta}
, we know that
\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}(x,t)\approx 1/t+\eta
, and, hence, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dx=1
,

\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{u}_{\eta}(x,t)dxdt\approx\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}dt=\varepsilon\big[\text{log}\big(1+\eta)-\text{log}\eta\big],

(4.6)

and

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\varepsilon\big[\text{log}\big(1+\eta)-\text{log}\eta\big]=0.

(4.7)

We turn now to the term
\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\overline{\rho}_{\eta}(x,t)dxdt
. Its analysis is more complicated consisting on splitting the time integration on integrals over
(0,h),(h,1-h)
and
(1-h,1)
and using different arguments for each one.

We begin with the integral over
(1-h,1)
and recall that
\overline{\rho}_{\eta}
’s are supported in
[-r_{\eta,0},r_{\eta,0}]\times[0,1]
, where
r_{\eta,0}
is the minimum of
r_{\eta}
. Moreover, in view of Proposition
3.4
, the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}((\theta,1);H^{1}({J_{\eta}})
with
J_{\eta}=[-r_{\eta,0},r_{\eta,0}]
and
\theta\in(0,1)
.

In view of the uniform bound of the
u
’s in
L^{2}((1/4,1/2);H^{1}({J_{\eta}})
, there exists some
t_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in(1/4,1/2)
such that, for some
C>0
,

\|\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})\|^{2}\leq C.

(4.8)

Next we multiply the
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
equation by
\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}\times(t_{\varepsilon,\eta},1)
to get

\begin{split}\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}&(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,1)dx+\|\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J_{\eta}\times(t_{\varepsilon,\eta},1))}\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq\|\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(1/2,1))}\|\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{(}J_{\eta}\times(t_{\varepsilon,\eta},1))}+\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{J_{\eta}}(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,t_{\varepsilon,\eta})dx.\end{split}

It then follows from (
4.8
) that, for some
C>0
,

\|\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J_{\eta}\times(1/2,1))}\leq\|\varepsilon\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L(J_{\eta}\times(t_{\varepsilon,\eta},1))}\leq C.

Then

\begin{split}\varepsilon|\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\partial_{x}^{2}&u_{\varepsilon,\eta}dxdt|\leq\big(\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}^{2}dxdt\big)^{1/2}dt\big)^{1/2}\big(\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varepsilon^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}dxdt\big)^{1/2}\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq C\big(\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\rho}_{\varepsilon,\eta}^{2}dxdt\big)^{1/2}=\text{o}(1),\end{split}

the last claim following from the fact that the
\overline{\rho}
’s are uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))
.

For the integral over
(h,1-h)
we observe that, since
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is smooth and
\overline{\rho}_{\eta}
is Lipschitz and has compact support,

\begin{split}\varepsilon\large|\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\overline{\rho}_{\eta}(x,t)dxdt\big|&=\varepsilon\big|\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\partial_{x}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}(x,t)dxdt\big|\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq\varepsilon C\Big(\displaystyle\int_{h}^{1-h}\|u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(J_{\eta})}dt+\displaystyle\int_{h}^{1-h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}\overline{\rho}_{\eta})^{2}dxdt\Big),\end{split}

and, hence,

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\lim}\;\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{1-h}^{1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)\overline{\rho}_{\eta}(x,t)dxdt=0\ \ \text{uniformly in $h\in(0,1/2)$},

(4.9)

since, in view of Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
, for each
\theta\in(0,1/2)
,
u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\in L^{2}((\theta,1);H^{1}_{\text{loc}})
and
\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\in L^{2}((\theta,1-\theta);H^{1})
.

To conclude, we need to investigate the behavior of
\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt,
and for this
we recall that, if

u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)=v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)+\dfrac{x^{2}}{2(t+\eta)}+\varepsilon\text{log}\big(\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big),

then

\partial_{t}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}-\varepsilon\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\dfrac{x}{t+\eta}\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}=\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,0)=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\geq 0.

Then,

\begin{split}\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt&=\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt+\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt\\[3.41432pt]
&=\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt+\text{log}\big(\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big).\end{split}

To finish, we need to estimate
\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt
.

Recalling that
\overline{\rho}_{\eta}=r_{\eta}(t)\big(1-\dfrac{x^{2}}{l_{\eta}^{2}(t)}\big)_{+}
, we find

\begin{split}\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt=\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\partial_{x}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt\\[3.41432pt]
&=\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\big[-2v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(l_{\eta}(t),t)\dfrac{r_{\eta}(t)}{l_{\eta}(t)}+2v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(-l_{\eta}(t),t)\dfrac{r_{\eta}(t)}{l_{\eta}(t)}dt+2\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\dfrac{r_{\eta}(t)}{l_{\eta}^{2}(t)}\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|\leq l_{\eta}(t)\}}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dxdt,\end{split}

and, hence, in view of the asymptotic behavior of the
r_{\eta}
’s and
l_{\eta}
’s near
t=0
,

|\varepsilon\;\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt|\leq C\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\dfrac{1}{(t+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}dt.

Finally, we need to estimate the right hand side of this last inequality, which have already done in the proof of Proposition
3.9
.

It follows from the equation satisfied by
v_{\varepsilon,\eta}
that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big(\dfrac{1}{t+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dx\Big)+\dfrac{1}{2(t+\eta)}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,t)dx=\dfrac{1}{t+\eta},

and, hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x,t)dx\leq(t+\eta)\text{log}\big(\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big),

(4.10)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{s+\eta}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}(x,s)dxds\leq\text{log}\big(\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big).

(4.11)

Using next the imbedding
\|w\|\leq c\|w\|^{1/3}\|\partial_{x}w\|^{2/3}
, we find from (
4.10
) that, for each
t\in(0,1)
,

\|v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}\leq c\Big((t+\eta)\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\Big)^{1/3}\|\partial_{x}v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,t)\|^{2/3},

and, therefore, in view of (
4.11
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2}}\dfrac{1}{\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta}}\|v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,s)\|^{3}ds\leq c\;\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}.

Then, for some
c>0
,

\begin{split}\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}&\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{4/3}}\|v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,s)\|_{\infty}ds\\[3.41432pt]
&=\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big[\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2/3}}\dfrac{1}{(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta})^{1/3}}\|v_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,s)\|\big]\big[\dfrac{1}{(s+\eta)^{2/3}}(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta})^{1/3}\big]ds\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq c\varepsilon\big(\text{log}\dfrac{t+\eta}{\eta}\big)^{1/3}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{s+\eta}\big(\text{log}\dfrac{s+\eta}{\eta}\big)^{1/2}ds)\Big)^{2/3}\\[3.41432pt]
&\leq c\varepsilon\Big(\text{log}\frac{t+\eta}{\eta}\Big)^{2/3}\leq c\varepsilon\text{log}\frac{t+\eta}{\eta}.\end{split}

Summarizing the above arguments we find that

|\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt|\leq C\varepsilon\text{log}\big(\dfrac{h+\eta}{\eta}\big),

and, since
\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}
,

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\eta\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}|\varepsilon\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}\overline{\rho}_{\eta}dxdt|\leq\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\eta\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}C\varepsilon\text{log}\big(\dfrac{h+\eta}{\eta}\big)=0.

The final item to show is the convergence of the
I_{\varepsilon,\eta}
’s. This follows from the facts that,

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\eta\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\big)^{2}dxdt=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \underset{\eta\to 0}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\overline{\rho}_{\eta}-\overline{\rho}\big)^{2}dxdt=0,

and the observation that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\eta\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}+\overline{\rho}_{\eta})\Big(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta}-\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}\Big)^{2}dxdt=0

and

\underset{\eta\to 0}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\overline{\rho}_{\eta}\big(\partial_{x}\overline{u}_{\eta}\big)^{2}-\overline{\rho}\big(\partial_{x}\overline{u}\big)^{2}\Big)dxdt=0

yield that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\eta\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}\big(\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon,\eta})^{2}-\overline{\rho}\big(\partial_{x}\overline{u})^{2})^{2}\Big)dxdt=0.

∎

5.
The KPZ asymptotics

As it was discussed in the introduction the original motivation of this work was to provide a complete and rigorous proof to the large deviations-type (LDP) approximation for the KPZ equation for large heights and sharp-wedge initial condition, a topic that has attracted considerable attention in the math physics community.

We begin with a short and informal introduction to the problem.

We consider the following KPZ problem

\partial_{t}h-\partial_{x}^{2}h+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h)^{2}=\sqrt{\theta}\xi\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1)\ \ \ \ h(x,0)=x^{2}/2L.

(5.1)

For any
\rho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,T))
, it is known that

\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{\theta}\xi\approx\rho)\approx\|\rho\|^{2}_{2}=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{2}(x,t)\;dxdt.

Then, in
\{\sqrt{\theta}\xi\approx\rho\}
, the height
h
is approximated by

\partial_{t}h-\partial_{x}^{2}h+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h)^{2}=\rho\ \ \text{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}\times(0,1]\ \ \ h(x,0)=|x|^{2}/L.

(5.2)

The LDP theory says that, for large heights and sharp wedge initial condition,
h_{\theta}
satisfies a large deviation principle if, as
\lambda\to\infty
and
L\to 0
,

h\approx\exp\big[-I(\lambda,L)\big]=\exp\big[-I+\text{o}(1)\big],

where

\begin{split}I(\lambda,L)=&\sup\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\|\rho\|^{2}_{2}\\[3.41432pt]
&=-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{t}h-\dfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}h+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h)^{2})^{2}dxdt:\;h\;\text{ solves \eqref{takis} and $h(0,1)=\lambda$}\Big\}.\end{split}

(5.3)

A variational argument shows that a minimizer in (
5.3
) solves the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{\lambda,L}-\partial^{2}_{x}u^{\lambda,L}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u^{\lambda,L})^{2}=\rho^{\lambda,L}\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1],\\[2.84526pt]
\partial_{t}\rho^{\lambda,L}+\partial^{2}_{x}\rho^{\lambda,L}+\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}u^{\lambda,L}\rho^{\lambda,L})=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times[0,1),\\[2.84526pt]
\rho^{\lambda,L}(\cdot,1)=c(\lambda){\text{\boldmath${\delta}$}}\ \ u^{\lambda,L}(0,1)=\lambda\ \ u^{\lambda,L}(x,0)=|x|^{2}/L.\end{cases}

(5.4)

The terminal value of
\rho^{\lambda,L}
is due to the constraint that
u^{\lambda,L}(0,1)=\lambda
and
c(\lambda)
is an appropriate constant.

The limiting behavior of
I(\lambda,L)
as
\lambda\to\infty
and
L\to 0
is related to the behavior of the solution of the KPZ equation for
\lambda\to\infty
and
L\to 0
.

To understand this behavior it is convenient to introduce the rescaling

u_{\lambda,L}(x,t)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}u^{\lambda,L}(\lambda^{1/2}x,t)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rho_{\lambda,L}(x,t)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\rho^{\lambda,L}(\lambda^{1/2}x,t),

which leads to the forward-backwards system

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u_{\lambda,L}-\partial^{2}_{x}u_{\lambda,L}+\dfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}u_{\lambda,L})^{2}=\rho{\lambda,L}\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[4.2679pt]
&\partial_{t}\rho_{\lambda,L}+\partial^{2}_{x}\rho_{\lambda,L}+\partial_{x}(u_{\lambda,L}\rho_{\lambda,L})=0\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}\times(0,1),\\[4.2679pt]
&u_{\lambda,L}(x,0)=|x|^{2}/L,\;\;\rho_{\lambda,L}(\cdot,1)=\lambda^{-2/3}c(\lambda)\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\ \ \text{and}\ \ u_{\lambda,L}(0,1)=1.\end{split}

(5.5)

We show next how to obtain the.
\lambda\to\infty,L\to 0
behavior of
u_{\lambda,L}
and
\rho_{\lambda,L}
using what was already established in the paper.

We choose
a>0
such that the “physics solution”
\overline{u}_{a}
, which corresponds to (
1.2
) with
\overline{\rho}(\cdot,1)=a\text{\boldmath$\delta$}
, has the property that
\overline{u}_{a}(0,1)=1
. This can be accomplished by the appropriate choices of
r
,
k
and
a
. To simplify the notation, in what follows we assume that
a=1
.

We consider now, for
\varepsilon,\eta>0
such that
\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}
the solution
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
of (
1.1
), and recall that
we have shown that

(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
converges in the appropriate sense to the solution
(\overline{u},\overline{\rho})
of (
1.2
).

Let
A(\varepsilon,\eta)=u_{\varepsilon,\eta}(0,1)
. It follows from the convergence result that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}A(\varepsilon,\eta)=1.

(5.6)

Scaling (
1.1
) by
\mu>0
changes
(u_{\varepsilon,\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta})
to a new solution
(u_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta},\rho_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta})
for
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon\mu^{\alpha}
with
\rho_{\varepsilon,\eta}(\cdot,1)=\mu^{\beta}\text{\boldmath$\delta$}
and
u_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta}(0,1)=A(\varepsilon,\eta)\mu^{\gamma}
.

We choose
\mu=A(\varepsilon,\eta)^{-1/\gamma}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}
. This gives a solution to the KPZ system for
\eta>0
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon\mu^{\alpha}=A(\varepsilon,\eta)^{-\alpha/\gamma}
; note that
\underset{\varepsilon\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\varepsilon^{\prime}/\varepsilon=1.

Finally, since obviously
A(\varepsilon,\eta)
is continuous in
\varepsilon
for each
\eta
, to obtain a solution of (
1.1
), we choose
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{\prime}r
and want to solve for a solution such that
A(\varepsilon^{\prime}r,\eta)^{-\alpha/\gamma}=1
.

Observe next that, for any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
,

\underset{\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\theta A(\varepsilon^{\prime}\theta,\eta)=\theta<1\ \text{and}\ \underset{\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0,\eta\to 0,\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq A\eta^{\alpha}}{\lim}\dfrac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}A(\varepsilon^{\prime}\dfrac{1}{\theta^{\prime}},\eta)=\dfrac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}>1.

Then the claimed existence follows from a standard continuity argument.

Note that

\rho(\cdot,1)=\mu^{\beta}\delta=A(\varepsilon,\eta)^{-\beta/\gamma}\text{\boldmath$\delta$}\to c_{0}\text{\boldmath$\delta$}

which implies that

\underset{\lambda\to\infty}{\lim}\lambda^{-2/3}c(\lambda)\to c_{0}.

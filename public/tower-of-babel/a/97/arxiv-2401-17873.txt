Abstract
Using data samples collected with the BESIII detector operating at the BEPCII storage ring, the cross section of the inclusive process e + ​ e − → η + X e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta+X , normalized by the total cross section of e + ​ e − → hadrons e^{+}e^{-}\to\textmd{hadrons} , is measured at eight center-of-mass energy points from 2.0000 GeV to 3.6710 GeV. These are the first measurements with momentum dependence in this energy region. Our measurement shows a significant discrepancy compared to the existing fragmentation functions. To address this discrepancy, a new QCD analysis is performed at the next-to-next-to-leading order with hadron mass corrections and higher twist effects, which can explain both the established high-energy data and our measurements reasonably well.

, where
h
is an identified hadron under investigation and
X
represents
everything else, provides an effective way to study collinear FFs
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
. A widely measured experimental observable is

\frac{1}{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\textmd{hadrons})}\frac{\textmd{d}\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow h+X)}{\textmd{d}p_{h}},

(1)

where
\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\textmd{hadrons})
represents the total cross section for
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to all possible hadronic final states (referred to as inclusive hadronic events
hereafter), and
p_{h}
denotes the momentum of the identified hadron
h
.
The observable can be interpreted, in terms of the leading order of
\alpha_{s}
, as
\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}[D_{q}^{h}(z,\sqrt{s})+D_{\bar{q}}^{h}(z,\sqrt{s})]
, where

e_{q}
is the fractional charge of the quark
q
, and
D^{h}_{q/\bar{q}}(z,\sqrt{s})
is the FF of quark
q
or antiquark
\bar{q}
at c.m. energy
\sqrt{s}
.
The variable
z\equiv 2\sqrt{p_{h}^{2}c^{2}+M^{2}_{h}c^{4}}/\sqrt{s}
denotes the relative energy of the produced hadron
h
with mass
M_{h}
.

This Letter,
for the first time,
reports a measurement of the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta+X
at eight c.m. energy points from
2.0000
to
3.6710~\mathrm{GeV}
, with a
z
coverage from 0.3 to 0.9.
Such a special energy coverage is expected to be very sensitive to the initial parametrization of FFs, and can test the convergence of fixed order perturbative QCD (pQCD) calculations based on leading twist factorization involving final state hadron production
Deur:2016tte
;
Liu:2019srj
;
Berger:1980qg
.
In addition, a comprehensive QCD analysis is performed by involving both experimental measurements and theoretical calculations of
\eta
production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation.
In the analysis, the data reported in this Letter are implemented into the global study of
\eta
meson FFs, after considering the highest precision pQCD calculation at next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO)
Mitov:2006wy
, the mass corrections
Accardi:2014qda
;
MoosaviNejad:2015lgp
, and the possible contributions from higher twist
Liu:2019srj
;
Berger:1980qg
.
The analysis not only indicates the importance of the BESIII measurement in exploring the
\eta
meson hadronization mechanism, but also serves as a testing ground for the validity of QCD factorization at leading twist for inclusive hadron production and their associated fixed order pQCD calculations.

The data sets used in this Letter were collected with the BESIII
detector
BESIII:2009fln
running at BEPCII
Yu:2016cof
.
Experimentally, the normalized differential cross section characterizing the inclusive
production of identified hadron
h
, as described in Eq. (
1
), can be
determined with

\frac{N_{h}^{\textmd{obs}}}{N_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{obs}}}\frac{1}{\Delta p_{h}}f_{h},

(2)

where
N_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{obs}}
represents the number of observed hadronic events in the

e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation at a given c.m. energy, and
N_{h}^{\textmd{obs}}
denotes the number of

e^{+}e^{-}\to h+X
events within a specific momentum range
\Delta p_{h}
.
The factor
f_{h}
, described later in detail, is a correction factor
accounting for the global detection efficiency and the initial state
radiation (ISR) effects.
Since both
N_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{obs}}
and
N_{h}^{\textmd{obs}}
are obtained from the same data
sample, the integrated luminosity used in the cross section
measurement cancels.

For the first step of this analysis, the hadronic events are identified
using the same selection criteria as described in Ref.
BESIII:2021wib
.
The Bhabha and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma\gamma
events are removed by applying
dedicated requirements on the electromagnetic calorimeter (EMC) shower information.
Subsequently, a series of criteria are applied to select good charged tracks.
Events with less than two good charged tracks are removed to suppress background processes.
For events with two or three good charged tracks, further requirements
are employed to suppress the QED-related backgrounds.
Events with more than three good charged tracks are regarded as hadronic
events directly. Details of the selection of the inclusive hadronic events
can be found in Ref.
BESIII:2021wib
.

Despite comprehensive selection criteria being applied to identify the inclusive hadronic events, there are still residual background events in the data.
The numbers of the QED-related backgrounds are estimated by analyzing the corresponding Monte Carlo (MC) simulation samples.
The
geant4
-based
GEANT4:2002zbu
programs are used to produce these MC samples, where the geometric description of the BESIII detector
Huang:2022wuo

and the interaction between secondary particles and the detector material are included.
The
babayaga3.5

CarloniCalame:2000pz
package is used to generate the
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}
,
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
, and
\gamma\gamma
processes, while
kkmc

Jadach:1999vf
and
evtgen

Lange:2001uf
are utilized for the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
process. The two-photon processes are simulated by dedicated MC generators
BESIII:2021wib
.
The beam-associated background events are estimated using a sideband method
BESIII:2021wib
.

From the selected inclusive hadronic events, the
\eta
candidates are reconstructed via the
\eta\to\gamma\gamma
decay.
Photons are required to have a deposited energy in the EMC of more than
25~\mathrm{MeV}
in the barrel region (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
), and more than
50~\mathrm{MeV}
in the end cap region (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
), where
\theta
is defined with respect to the
z
-axis, which is the symmetry axis of the multi-layer drift chamber.
To exclude showers that originate from charged tracks, the angle subtended by the EMC shower and the position of the closest charged track at the EMC must be greater than 10 degrees as measured from the interaction point.
To suppress electronic noise and showers unrelated to the event, the difference between the EMC time and the event start time is required to be within [0, 700] ns.
Since the yield of
\pi^{0}
mesons is much higher comparing to
\eta
in the inclusive hadronic events
BESIII:2022zit
, the photons originating from
\pi^{0}
are excluded when reconstructing
\eta
.
If a pair of photons in one event has an invariant mass within
(115,~155)~\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
, which covers five times the
\pi^{0}
mass resolution, both photons in that pair are discarded.
A test using the control sample
J/\psi\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
indicates that the exclusion of the
\pi^{0}
photons does not introduce any bias to the invariant-mass distribution of
\eta
.
Moreover, a check using the MC sample of the inclusive hadronic process demonstrates that the exclusion does not induce any peaking background in the
\eta
invariant-mass distribution.
The remaining photons after veto of
\pi^{0}
are paired to reconstruct the
\eta
candidates.
To suppress background due to photon mis-combinations, the helicity variable of the
\eta
candidate, defined as
|E_{\gamma 1}-E_{\gamma 2}|/p_{\gamma\gamma}
where
E_{\gamma 1,2}
represent the deposited energies of photons and
p_{\gamma\gamma}
denotes the momentum of
\eta
candidate, is required to be less than 0.8.
In this analysis, the inclusive hadronic events are simulated with the
luarlw

generator
Andersson:1997xwk
;
Andersson:1999ui
;
BESIII:2021wib
, in which among others the signal processes
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta+X
is contained.

The
\eta
candidates are divided into different momentum intervals with the bin width
\Delta p_{\eta}=0.1~\mathrm{GeV}/c
, approximately 6 times the momentum resolution of
\eta
.
The yield of
\eta
mesons in each momentum bin is determined by an unbinned maximum likelihood fit.
In the fit, the signal is described by the signal shape of
\eta
, which is extracted from the
luarlw
MC sample, convolved with a Gaussian function corresponding to the difference between data and MC.
The background is modeled by a second-order polynomial, except for a few momentum bins where a third-order polynomial is used due to a higher mis-combination background level.
Figure
1
shows the fit result of
\eta
candidates with
p_{\gamma\gamma}\in(0.4,0.5)~\mathrm{GeV}/c
at
\sqrt{s}=2.9000~\mathrm{GeV}
,
where the higher background at the low mass region is caused by the mis-combinations involving the low energy photons.
To extract the signal shape of
\eta
from the
luarlw
MC sample, the truth-level
\eta
mesons decaying to two photons are matched to the reconstructed
\eta
candidates according to the momentum direction.
The reconstructed
\eta
candidate which has the closest momentum direction relative to the truth-level
\eta
is regarded as matched.
In addition, the angle of the momenta between truth-level
\eta
and its matched
\eta
candidate is required to be less than
25
degrees.
The matched
\eta
candidates make up the signal sample of
\eta
and their invariant-mass distributions are used as the signal shapes in the fitting procedure.
The obtained
N_{\eta}^{\textmd{obs}}
in each momentum range of
\eta
at various c.m. energies are summarized in the Supplemental Material
supp
.

The correction factor
f_{h}
, which scales the observable quantity
N_{h}^{\textmd{obs}}/N_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{obs}}
to determine the observable given in Eq. (
1
) in each momentum bin,
is extracted from the inclusive hadronic MC sample and is expressed as:

f_{h}=\frac{\bar{N}_{h}^{\textmd{tru}}\textmd{(off)}}{\bar{N}_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{tru}}\textmd{(off)}}\bigg/\frac{\bar{N}_{h}^{\textmd{obs}}\textmd{(on)}}{\bar{N}_{\textmd{had}}^{\textmd{obs}}\textmd{(on)}}.

(3)

Here,
\bar{N}
denotes the number of events determined from the inclusive hadronic MC sample, either at observable level, similar to the experimental data, with superscript “obs” or
at truth level with superscript “tru”.
The terms “on” and “off” in the parentheses indicate that the corresponding quantities are extracted from the inclusive hadronic MC sample with or without simulating the ISR process, respectively.
In this Letter,
\bar{N}_{\eta}^{\textmd{obs}}\textmd{(on)}
is determined with a similar fit to the
M(\gamma\gamma)
distribution of the
\eta
candidates selected from the inclusive hadronic MC sample.

Extensive comparisons between the
luarlw
generated MC events and the experimental data show that the
luarlw
model can reasonably reproduce the multiplicity and
kinematic quantity of the
\eta
mesons
supp
.
In addition, good agreements are observed in terms of the invariant mass spectra of
\eta
in different momentum bins between experimental data and the inclusive hadronic MC sample.
Thus, the correction applied in this analysis is valid.
The calculated results of
f_{\eta}
in the different
\eta
momentum ranges are presented in the Supplemental Material
supp
.

The systematic uncertainties of the normalized differential cross section are mainly caused by the residual deviations between the signal MC and data samples, the reconstruction efficiency of the
\eta
candidates, the fit scheme of the
M(\gamma\gamma)
spectrum, and the simulation model of the inclusive hadronic events.

The approach described in Ref.
BESIII:2022zit
is applied here to estimate the uncertainty caused by the imperfect simulation of signal events.
Systematic uncertainties of the differential cross section introduced by the determination of
N_{\textmd{bkg}}
are found to be negligible.

For the uncertainty of reconstructing the
\eta
candidates, several factors are considered, including the identification of photons, the exclusion of the
\pi^{0}
photons, and the helicity requirement.
The uncertainty in the photon identification is estimated to be 1% per photon
BESIII:2018rdg
, resulting in 2% uncertainty for each
\eta
meson.
The uncertainty due to the exclusion of the
\pi^{0}
photons is evaluated by varying the nominal invariant-mass range of
\pi^{0}
to
(111,159)~\mathrm{MeV}/c^{2}
.
The differences of the differential cross section are found to be negligible (less than 1%).
To estimate the uncertainty due to the
\eta
helicity requirement, the
\eta
helicity distributions of the
J/\psi\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\eta
events are compared between data and MC simulation.
The average relative difference of 2% is taken as the uncertainty.

To evaluate the uncertainty of the fit scheme, different signal and background description functions are applied to fit the
M(\gamma\gamma)
spectrum.
For the signal, the Crystal Ball function
Skwarnicki:1986xj
is used as an alternative model.
Moreover, the different requirements of the match angle, which are
20
and
30
degrees, are utilized to extract the alternative signal shapes from the signal MC sample.
For the background, the alternative models are obtained by varying the order of the Chebychev polynomial.
All the resulting relative differences in the differential cross sections are combined in quadrature, and taken as the systematic uncertainty.

In this Letter, the dominant systematic uncertainty is introduced by the MC simulation model of the inclusive hadronic events.
According to Eq. (
3
), the generation fractions of the exclusive processes containing the
\eta
mesons, which make up the inclusive process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta+X
, directly affect the correction factors
f_{\eta}
.
To address the corresponding uncertainty, the
hybrid
generator, which was developed in Ref.
Ping:2016pms
and improved in Ref.
BESIII:2021wib
, is used as an alternative model to reproduce the inclusive hadronic events.
The discrepancies observed in the correction factors
f_{\eta}
relative to the nominal ones are regarded as systematic uncertainties.

All these individual systematic uncertainties are regarded as uncorrelated with each other therefore are summed in quadrature. The normalized differential cross sections for the inclusive
\eta
production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation at the eight c.m. energy points are tabulated in the Supplemental Material
supp
and shown in Fig.
2
.

The blue dotted curve in Fig.
2
represents a theoretical prediction performed using the
\eta
FF from the Aidala-Ellinghaus-Sassot-Seele-Stratmann (AESSS) parametrization at the next-to-leading order
Aidala:2010bn
.
The AESSS FFs are extracted using data of
\eta
production in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation with
\sqrt{s}\eqsim
10, 30, and 90 GeV, and
pp
collisions with
\sqrt{s}\eqsim
200 GeV whose energy scales are higher than the typical BESIII c.m. energies.
The AESSS study is based on the well-established de Florian-Sassot-Stratmann (DSS) framework
deFlorian:2007ekg
;
deFlorian:2007aj
for FF extractions.
It consists of a pure next-to-leading order analysis, based on leading-twist pQCD factorization theorems for
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation and
pp
collision processes, where the mass of the
\eta
-meson is considered to be negligible and set to be zero.
The fit in Ref.
Aidala:2010bn
reveals good agreement among data sets taken at different energy scales.
However, according to Fig.
2
, the AESSS fit cannot describe the previous data and the BESIII data at the same time.

The gray line in Fig.
2
shows the calculation with a new extraction of
\eta
FFs
supp
;
Li:2024etc
based on the available
\eta
production data in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation experiments, namely the data sets included in Ref.
Aidala:2010bn
, except for the un-published BaBar data, and the BESIII data presented in this Letter.
The ratio of
\chi^{2}/N_{d.o.f.}
for this fit is 1.52
supp
;
Li:2024etc
which is comparable to that of the AESSS fit (1.91
Aidala:2010bn
) where only the existing
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation data are considered.
For the first time, data at
\sqrt{s}<5~\mathrm{GeV}
are included in such a QCD-based analysis where the analysis framework is extended to NNLO accuracy and the hadron mass corrections and higher twist contributions are considered
supp
;
Li:2024etc
.
The inclusion of BESIII data in the original AESSS framework, namely a refit, leads to a significantly large
\chi^{2}/N_{d.o.f.}
(12.79) which confirms the disagreement between the AESSS fit and the BESIII data.
Each of the three major effects considered in the new fit plays a fundamental role in achieving the good agreement as shown in Fig.
2
.
Hadron mass corrections are well known to be an important effect in the fit of FFs of heavier hadron species, e.g., see discussions in Refs.
Albino:2008fy
;
Albino:2005gd
;
Accardi:2014qda
.
The extension to NNLO has an important effect on the shape of the observable in the lower-
z
region, as one can for example explicitly see in the case of the NNLO parton-to-pion FF fit for
e^{+}e^{-}
production in Fig. 4 of Ref.
Anderle:2016czy
.
That analysis highlighted the importance of the higher accuracy framework in order to perform a reasonable fit including Belle and BaBar data sets which have c.m. energy around 10.5 GeV.
At last, higher twist effects are commonly introduced in PDF analysis when incorporating low energy data, such as JLab data in Ref.
Owens:2012bv
.
They are taken into account as extra fit parameters that parameterize an additional
1/Q^{2}
dependence to the leading twist expression of the observable.
In this analysis, both
1/Q^{2}
and
1/Q^{4}
dependence have to be introduced in order to obtain a good fit.
The analysis is summarized in Supplemental Material
supp
and detailed in Ref.
Li:2024etc
.

In summary, we have measured the normalized differential cross sections of the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\eta+X
processes, using data samples collected from
\sqrt{s}=2.0000
to
3.6710~\mathrm{GeV}
.
The results obtained in this work fill this particular energy region where no such kind of measurements have been reported before.
A QCD-based analysis shows that in order to explain both high and low-energy data, one needs to consider higher-order contributions as well as higher twist effects.
It would be interesting to check if a more flexible approach, such as NNFF
Bertone:2017tyb
or MAPFF
AbdulKhalek:2022laj
, could describe the data over the full energy range.
These new results in the relatively low energy region provide special ingredients for FF studies, moreover, they will help to enhance our understanding of the QCD factorization theorem at the leading twist and beyond.

Propagation reversal on trees in the large diffusion regime
Hermen Jan Hupkes, Mia Jukić

Abstract
In this work we study travelling wave solutions to bistable reaction diffusion equations on bi-infinite k k -ary trees in the continuum regime where the diffusion parameter is large. Adapting the spectral convergence method developed by Bates and his coworkers, we find an asymptotic prediction for the speed of travelling front solutions. In addition, we prove that the associated profiles converge to the solutions of a suitable limiting reaction-diffusion PDE. Finally, for the standard cubic nonlinearity we provide explicit formula’s to bound the thin region in parameter space where the propagation direction undergoes a reversal.



Keywords:
reaction-diffusion equations; lattice differential equations;
travelling waves; propagation reversal; wave pinning; tree graphs.

Introduction

In this work we study the reaction-diffusion-advection lattice differential equation (LDE)

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{i}

\displaystyle=d\Big(ku_{i+1}-(k+1)u_{i}+u_{i-1}\Big)+g(u_{i};a),\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1.1)

As explained below, we view the (real-valued) parameter
k>1
as a branching factor. In addition,
d>0
encodes the
diffusion strength
and the nonlinearity
g
is of bistable type, for example

g(u;a)=u(1-u)(u-a),\qquad a\in(0,1).

(1.2)

This LDE is known
[
13
]
to admit travelling front solutions, i.e. solutions
of the form

u_{i}(t)=\Phi(i-ct),\qquad\qquad\Phi(-\infty)=0,\qquad\qquad\Phi(+\infty)=1.

(1.3)

In this paper we apply a version of the ‘spectral convergence’ technique
to study the behaviour of the pair
(c,\Phi)
in the regime
where
d
is large, the so-called continuum regime. In particular, our results supplement our earlier work
[
9
]
,
where we studied the small and intermediate
d
regime using an
entirely different set of techniques.

Dynamics on
k
-ary trees

Our primary motivation to study (
1.1
) is that
the wavefronts (
1.3
) can be seen as

layer-wave
solutions of bistable reaction-diffusion equations posed on
k
-ary trees. In particular, the sign of the wavespeed
c
determines which of the two stable roots of the nonlinearity
g
can be expected to spread throughout the tree. We refer the reader to our previous work
[
9
]
and a prior paper by Kouvaris, Kori and Mikhailov
[
11
]
for numerical studies to support this claim and and an in-depth discussion of the potential application areas for our results.
Related results for monostable equations can be found in
[
8
]
. Preliminary numerical investigations
show that for general trees, studying (
1.1
)
with the
average
branch-factor
k
still has important
predictive capabilities.

For each fixed
k>1
, the main results in
[
9
]

provide non-empty open sets of parameters
(a,d)
where the expressions

c>0
,
c=0
respectively
c<0
are guaranteed to hold. In fact, upon steadily increasing the diffusion parameter
d>0
for a fixed
a\sim 1
, waves transition from being pinned
(c=0)
, travelling ‘down’ the tree
(c>0)
, being pinned once more
(c=0)

towards finally travelling ‘up’ the tree
(c<0)
. This is illustrated
by the numerical results in Fig.
1
.

These results were achieved by constructing explicit sub- and super-solutions and invoking the comparison principle. Although the boundaries of these regions agree reasonably well with numerical observations for small
d
,
they are - by construction - not ‘asymptotically precise’. In particular, they do not accurately capture the transition region between
c>0
and
c<0
, which appears to converge to a curve as
d
increases. The main purpose of our results here is to increase our understanding of this transition
region where propagation reversal occurs.

Continuum regime

In order to gain some preliminary intuition into the
diffusion-driven propagation reversal discussed above,
we recall from
[
9
]
that
the LDE (
1.1
) can also
be interpreted as a spatial discretization of the
partial differential equation (PDE)

u_{t}(x,t)=\nu u_{xx}(x,t)+\beta u_{x}(x,t)+g\big(u(x,t);a\big),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0.

(1.4)

Indeed, using the correspondence
u_{i}(t)\sim u(ih,t)

and the relation

\nu=\frac{1}{2}(k+1)dh^{2},\qquad\qquad\beta=(k-1)dh,

(1.5)

the standard central difference schemes

u_{x}(x,t)\sim\frac{1}{2h}\big(u(x+h,t)-u(x-h,t)\big),\qquad u_{xx}(x,t)\sim\frac{1}{h^{2}}\big(u(x+h,t)+u(x-h,t)-2u(x,t)\big)

(1.6)

reduce (
1.4
) back to (
1.1
). Vice-versa, expanding the shifted terms in (
1.1
) up to order
O(h^{3})

leads directly to (
1.4
).

For the cubic (
1.2
), the PDE (
1.4
) admits explicit travelling front solutions

u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\big[1+\tanh\big((x-\sigma t)/\sqrt{8\nu}\big)\big],\qquad\qquad\sigma=\sqrt{2\nu}\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\beta.

(1.7)

Upon introducing the appropriate speed-scaling
\sigma=ch

and recalling the coefficients (
1.5
),
we readily obtain the asymptotic prediction

c=\sqrt{(k+1)d}\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)-(k-1)d,

(1.8)

which changes sign at the critical value

d(a,k)=\frac{(k+1)}{(k-1)^{2}}\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}.

(1.9)

The numerical results in Figure
1
illustrate
that this prediction retains its accuracy for intermediate values of
d
, corresponding to values of
k
that are far removed from the critical
regime
k\gtrsim 1
.

Spectral convergence

Our main results provide a rigorous interpretation
and quantification for asymptotic predictions of this type.
This is achieved by using the spectral convergence approach
that was pioneered by Bates and his coworkers in

[
5
]
. The main
feature of this approach is that
Fredholm properties of
linearized operators associated to travelling wave solutions
can be transferred from the spatially continuous setting of
(
1.4
) to the spatially discrete
setting of (
1.1
).
The latter operators can then be used
in a standard fashion to close a fixed-point argument
and construct travelling front solutions to (
1.1
)
that are close to those of their PDE counterpart
(
1.4
).

The main difficulty that needs to be overcome is that
this perturbation is highly singular, since (unbounded) derivatives
are replaced by difference quotients. As a consequence,
one must carefully work with weak limits
and use spectral properties of the (discrete) Laplacian together with
the bistable structure of the nonlinearity
g

to counteract the loss of information that typically occurs
when using the weak topology.

In the present paper, the main additional complication
is the extra convective term appearing in the limiting
PDE (
1.4
) for
k>1
. Indeed,
the coefficients
(\nu,\beta)

introduced in (
1.5
) scale differently
with respect to
h
. In particular, in our analysis
the associated extra terms cannot be seen as ‘small’
and must be handled with care by invoking the
natural directional asymmetry that trees have.
A second main issue is that we want to obtain estimates
that are uniform in terms of the parameters
k
and
a
.
Such control is necessary in order to formulate quantitative results
for the critical curves
d(a,k)
.

Outlook

Let us emphasize that we expect that our techniques
can be applied to a much larger class of problems
than the scalar setting of (
1.1
).
For example, following the framework developed in
[

]

it should be possible to perform a similar analysis
for the FitzHugh-Nagumo system

Indeed, we do not use the comparison principle in this paper,

We also note that the results in

[

,
12
]
.

It is well-known that travelling waves can be used as building blocks to uncover and describe more complex dynamics occurring in spatially extended systems
[
2
]
. We therefore view the current paper as part of a push towards understanding and uncovering the behaviour of systems
in spatially structured environments, which can often naturally be modelled using graphs
[
1
,
18
,
19
,
21
]
. The ability to incorporate such spatial structures into models is becoming more and more important in our increasingly networked world.
Indeed, dynamical systems on networks are being
used in an ever-increasing range of disciplines, including chemical reaction theory
[
6
]
,
neuroscience
[
20
]
, systems biology
[

]
,

[

]
, epidemiology
[

]

and transportation
networks
[

]
.

Main results

In order to state our main result for (
1.1
),
we first formalize the bistability condition that we impose
on the nonlinearity
g
.

(Hg)

The map
(u,a)\mapsto g(u;a)
is
C^{1}
-smooth on
{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,1)
and
for all
a\in(0,1)
we have the identities

\displaystyle g(0;a)

\displaystyle=g(a;a)=g(1;a)=0,

together with the inequalities

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(0;a)

\displaystyle<0,\qquad g^{\prime}(1;a)<0,\qquad g^{\prime}(a;a)>0

and the sign conditions

g(v;a)>0\ \text{for}\ v\in(-\infty,0)\cup(a,1),\quad g(v;a)<0\ \text{for}\ v\in(0,a)\cup(1,\infty).

Fixing
k>0
and turning to travelling waves, it turns out to be convenient to link the diffusion strength
d>0
to a new grid-size parameter
h>0

via the (invertible) relations

h(d,k)=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{d}\sqrt{k+1}},\qquad\qquad d(h,k)=\dfrac{2}{h^{2}(k+1)},

(2.1)

which reduce to
dh^{2}=1
in the symmetric case
k=1
.
To appreciate this, we recall the standard central difference schemes
(
1.6
)
and introduce the associated operators

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\delta_{h}^{0}v(\xi)&:=&\dfrac{v(\xi+h)-v(\xi-h)}{2h},\\[5.69046pt]
\Delta_{h}v(\xi)&:=&\dfrac{v(\xi+h)-2v(\xi)+v(\xi-h)}{h^{2}}.\end{array}

(2.2)

Seeking solutions to (
1.1
) of the form

u_{i}(t)=\Phi\Big(ih(d,k)-ct\Big),

(2.3)

we find that the (rescaled) profile
\Phi

must satisfy the mixed-type functional differential equation (MFDE)

\displaystyle-c\Phi^{\prime}(\xi)=\Delta_{h}\Phi+\dfrac{2(k-1)}{h(k+1)}\partial^{0}_{h}\Phi+g(\Phi(\xi);a)

(2.4)

with
h=h(d,k)
,
to which we couple the standard spatial limits

\lim_{\xi\to-\infty}\Phi(\xi)=0,\qquad\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\Phi(\xi)=1.

(2.5)

On the one hand, this MFDE is covered by the general framework developed by Mallet-Paret in
[
13
]
. This provides the solutions
that lie at the basis of our analysis in this paper.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
13
, Thm. 2.1]

Suppose that (Hg) holds and pick

a\in(0,1)
together with
d>0
and
k>0
.
Then there exist a speed
c=c(a,d,k)
and a non-decreasing profile

that satisfy (
2.4
) with
h=h(d,k)
,
together with the boundary conditions (
2.5
).
Moreover,
c(a,d,k)
is uniquely determined and depends
C^{1}
-smoothly on all parameters when
c(a,d,k)\neq 0
. In this case the profile
\Phi
is
C^{1}
-smooth with
\Phi^{\prime}>0
and unique up to translation.

On the other hand, under the convergence assumption

c+\dfrac{2(k-1)}{h(k+1)}\to\sigma,

(2.6)

one may readily take
the formal

2.4
)
to arrive at the ODE

-\sigma\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi^{\prime\prime}+g\big(\Phi;a\big).

(2.7)

A classic result (see e.g.
[

]
) states that
there is a unique wavespeed
\sigma_{*;a}
for which
(

) with the boundary conditions
(

\Phi_{*;a}
. This waveprofile is unique up to translation and has

The goal of this paper is to link these two viewpoints together from
a spectral convergence perspective, extending earlier

[

The presence of the discrete derivative
\partial^{0}_{h}

in (

In fact, our approach can only keep this (large) term under control

by exploiting the
asymmetry that the parameter

In particular, we need to restrict the values of the detuning parameter

by requiring positive values for the continuum wave speed

To this end, we introduce the set

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{+}&=&\{a\in(0,1):\sigma_{*;a}>0\}\\[5.69046pt]
&=&\{a\in(0,1):\int_{0}^{1}g(s;a)ds<0\},\end{array}

noting that the second characterization can be obtained by integrating (
2.7
) against
\Phi^{\prime}
. With this notation in place,

Theorem 2.2

(see §

)
.

Assume that
(Hg)
holds and pick a compact subset

\sigma_{*;a}=\sqrt{2}(a-1/2),\qquad\qquad\mathcal{A}_{*}^{+}=(\frac{1}{2},1)

3
Fixed point problem

In this section we setup the fixed point problem that
will enable us to extract the bounds (
2.9
).
In particular, we isolate the correct linear and nonlinear parts
and formulate a convergence result for the associated linear operators.
The overall strategy closely resembles the approach originally developed in
[
5
]
and generalized
in
[
10
,
16
]
.

In order to extract the anticipated speed correction
(
2.6
), we recall
the discrete derivatives (
2.2
)
and introduce the combined operator

M_{h,k}v=\Delta_{h}v+\dfrac{2(k-1)}{h(k+1)}\big[\partial_{h}^{0}v-v^{\prime}\big].

(3.1)

This allows us to recast the MFDE (
2.4
)
in the form

-\big[c+\dfrac{2(k-1)}{h(k+1)}\big]\Phi^{\prime}=M_{h,k}\Phi+g(\Phi;a).

(3.2)

We now consider the pair
(c,\Phi)
as a perturbation
from the profile
\Phi_{*;a}
and the
anticipated wavespeed (
2.6
)
by writing

\Phi=\Phi_{*;a}+v,\qquad\qquad c=\sigma_{*;a}-\frac{2(k-1)}{h(k+1)}+\overline{c}

(3.3)

A direct computation shows that solving (
3.2
) is equivalent to finding a solution
(\overline{c},v)
to the problem

\mathcal{L}_{h,k;a}v=\overline{c}\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime}+\mathcal{R}_{A}(\overline{c},v;a)+\mathcal{R}_{B}(h,k;a),

(3.4)

where we have introduced the two nonlinearities

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{R}_{A}(\overline{c},v;a)&=&\overline{c}v^{\prime}+g(\Phi_{*;a}+v;a)-g(\Phi_{*;a};a)-g_{u}(\Phi_{*;a};a)v,\\[5.69046pt]
\mathcal{R}_{B}(h,k;a)&=&M_{h,k}\Phi_{*;a}-\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime\prime}\end{array}

(3.5)

together with the linear operator

\mathcal{L}_{h,k;a}v=-\sigma_{*;a}v^{\prime}-M_{h,k}v-g^{\prime}(\Phi_{*;a};a)v.

(3.6)

The key point is that the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}_{h,k;a}
formally converges to the well-known second order differential operator
\mathcal{L}_{*;a}:H^{2}\to L^{2}
that acts as

[\mathcal{L}_{*;a}v](\xi)=-c_{*;a}v^{\prime}(\xi)-v^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-g^{\prime}(\Phi_{*;a}(\xi);a)v(\xi).

(3.7)

This operator is Fredholm
[
5
]
and satisfies

\mathrm{Ker}\,\mathcal{L}_{*;a}=\mathrm{span}\{\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime}\},\qquad\qquad\mathrm{Range}\,\mathcal{L}_{*;a}=\{f:\langle\Psi_{*;a},f\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\},

(3.8)

where we introduced the adjoint eigenfunction

\Psi_{*;a}(\xi):=\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime}(\xi)e^{-\sigma_{*;a}\xi}/\int\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}e^{-\sigma_{*;a}\xi^{\prime}}\,d\xi^{\prime}

(3.9)

normalized to have

\langle\Psi_{*;a},\Phi_{*;a}^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}}=1.

(3.10)

Our first main contribution is the analogue of
[
10
, Thm. 2.3]
for the current setting and shows
in a sense that the characterization (
3.8
) can be transferred to the operators
\mathcal{L}_{h,k;a}
.

Proposition 3.1

(see §
4
)
.

Suppose that
(Hg)
holds and pick a compact set

\mathcal{A}_{\diamond}\subset\mathcal{A}_{*}^{+}
.
Then there exist constants
K>0

and
h_{0}>0

(3.11)

defined for all
h\in(0,h_{0})
,
k\geq 1

and
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\diamond}
,
so that the following properties hold true
for all such
(h,k,a)
.

(i)

For all
f\in L^{2}
we have the bound

\left|\beta_{h,k;a}f\right|+\left|\left|{\mathcal{V}_{h,k;a}f}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}\leq K\left|\left|{f}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}.

(3.12)

(ii)

For all
f\in L^{2}

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left|\left|{y_{j}}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\mu(\delta)^{2},

(4.46)

the strong convergence

(4.47)

as desired. The bound for

∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Consider the setting of Theorem
2.2

and Proposition
3.2
.
There exists
K>0
so that
for any
0<\mu<1
,
any
0<h<1
and any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\diamond}
,
the estimate

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\left|\left|{\mathcal{R}_{A}(\overline{c},v;a)}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&K\mu^{2}\\[5.69046pt]
\end{array}

(5.1)

holds for each
(\overline{c},v)\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
,
while the estimate

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\left|\left|{\mathcal{R}_{A}(\overline{c}^{(2)},v^{(2)};a)-\mathcal{R}_{A}(\overline{c}^{(1)},v^{(1)};a)}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&K\mu\left|\left|{(\overline{c}^{(2)}-\overline{c}^{(1)},v^{(2)}-v^{(1)})}\right|\right|_{\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}}\\[5.69046pt]
\end{array}

(5.2)

holds for each set of pairs

(\overline{c}^{(1)},v^{(1)})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}

and
(\overline{c}^{(2)},v^{(2)})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mu}
.

Proof.

The first term in
\mathcal{R}_{A}
can be handled
by the elementary estimates

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\left|\left|{\overline{c}v^{\prime}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&\left|\overline{c}\right|\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|_{H^{1}},\\[5.69046pt]
\left|\left|{\overline{c}^{(2)}[v^{(2)}]^{\prime}-\overline{c}^{(1)}[v^{(1)}]^{\prime}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&\left|\overline{c}^{(2)}-\overline{c}^{(1)}\right|\left|\left|{v^{(2)}}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}+\left|\overline{c}^{(1)}\right|\left|\left|{v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}.\end{array}

(5.3)

Writing
\mathcal{N}(v;a)=g(\Phi_{*;a}+v;a)-g(\Phi_{*;a};a)-g_{u}(\Phi_{*;a};a)v
,
we obtain the pointwise bounds

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|\mathcal{N}(v;a)(\xi)|&\leq&C^{\prime}\left|v(\xi)\right|^{2},\\[5.69046pt]
|\mathcal{N}(v^{(1)};a)(\xi)-\mathcal{N}(v^{(2)};a)(\xi)|&\leq&C^{\prime}\big[\left|v^{(1)}(\xi)\right|+\left|v^{(2)}(\xi)\right|\big]\left|v^{(1)}(\xi)-v^{(2)}(\xi)\right|\end{array}

(5.4)

for some
C^{\prime}>0

as a consequence of the a-priori bounds on
\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|_{\infty}
,

\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|^{(1)}_{\infty}
and
\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|^{(2)}_{\infty}
. In particular,
we find

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\left|\left|{\mathcal{N}(v;a)}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&C^{\prime}\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}\left|\left|{v}\right|\right|_{L^{2}},\\[5.69046pt]
\left|\left|{\mathcal{N}(v^{(1)};a)-\mathcal{N}(v^{(2)};a)}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}&\leq&C^{\prime}\big[\left|\left|{v^{(1)}}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}+\left|\left|{v^{(2)}}\right|\right|_{H^{1}}+\left|\left|{v^{(1)}-v^{(2)}}\right|\right|_{L^{2}}\big].\end{array}

(5.5)

The desired bounds follow
readily from these estimates.
∎

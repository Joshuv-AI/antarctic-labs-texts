Chow and augmented Chow polynomials as evaluations of Poincaré-extended 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -indices
Christian Stump

Abstract
. We show that Chow polynomials and augmented Chow polynomials of matroids, and more generally of finite graded posets admitting R R -labelings, are obtained as evaluations of their Poincaré-extended 𝐚𝐛 \mathbf{a}\mathbf{b} -indices. This implies in particular explicit combinatorial γ \gamma -positive expansions for both, providing the first proof of the γ \gamma -positivity not relying on the Kähler package for the Chow ring. We then evaluate this expansion to obtain an explicit closed formula for the braid arrangement.

.

The Chow polynomial

\displaystyle\underline{\textsf{H}}_{M}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathcal{F}}x^{\mathsf{des}(\mathcal{F})}(x+1)^{n-1-2\ \!\!\mathsf{des}(\mathcal{F})}\,,

\displaystyle\textsf{H}_{M}(x)

Remark 1.4

(Generalization to finite graded posets admitting

In work by Ferroni–Matherne–Vecchi, the definitions of the Chow and augmented Chow polynomials are generalized to finite graded posets with

2.
The Poincaré-extended
\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}
-index and its evaluations

Let
P
be a finite
graded poset
of rank
n
.
That is,
P
is a finite poset with unique minimum element
\hat{0}
and unique maximum element
\hat{1}
of rank
n
such that
{\sf rank}(F)
is equal to the length of any maximal chain from
\hat{0}
to
F
.
Its
Möbius function

\mu
is given by
\mu(F,F)=1
for all
F\in P
and
\mu(F,G)=-\sum_{F\leq H<G}\mu(F,H)
for all
F<G
.
Its
Poincaré polynomial
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in P}\mu(\hat{0},F)\cdot(-q)^{{\sf rank}(F)}\,,

its
characteristic polynomial
is

\displaystyle\chi_{P}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in P}\mu(\hat{0},F)\cdot q^{n-{\sf rank}(F)}\,,

and its
reduced characteristic polynomial
is

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{P}(q)

\displaystyle=\chi_{P}(q)/(q-1)=q^{n}\cdot\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(-q^{-1})/(q-1)\,.

A
chain

\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}_{1}<\dots<\mathcal{C}_{k}<\mathcal{C}_{k+1}\}
in
P
is an ordered set of pairwise comparable elements.
We assume throughout this paper that all chains end in the maximum element
\mathcal{C}_{k+1}=\hat{1}
.
A chain is
maximal
if
k=n
and we usually write
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{0}\prec\dots\prec\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
in this case in order to clearly distinguish between general chains ending in
\hat{1}
and maximal chains.
The
chain Poincaré polynomial
and the
reduced chain characteristic polynomial
of such a chain
\mathcal{C}
are

\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P,\mathcal{C}}(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{[\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i+1}]}(q)\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\chi}_{P,\mathcal{C}}(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\overline{\chi}_{[\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i+1}]}(q)\,,

where
[\mathcal{C}_{i},\mathcal{C}_{i+1}]\subseteq P
denotes the interval between two consecutive elements in the chain.
In particular,

\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P,\{\hat{0}<\hat{1}\}}(q)=\mathcal{\sf Poin}_{P}(q),\quad\overline{\chi}_{P,\{\hat{0}<\hat{1}\}}(q)=\overline{\chi}_{P}(q)\,.

Dynamics of weighted shifts on directed trees
Karl-G. Grosse-Erdmann and Dimitris Papathanasiou

Abstract
. We study the dynamical behaviour of weighted shifts defined on sequence spaces of a directed tree. In particular, we characterize their boundedness as well as when they are hypercyclic, weakly mixing and mixing.

47A16, 05C05, 47B37, 46A45

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Weighted shifts in linear dynamics

As Héctor N. Salas famously said, “the class of weighted shifts is a favorite testing ground for operator-theorists”,
[
34
]
. This is particularly true in linear dynamics, where shift operators play a central, and sometimes crucial, role. The reason is that their simple definition allows to compute explicitly the full orbit of a vector, while the class is rather flexible since one may choose both the weights and the underlying space. As a consequence, weighted shifts provide important examples and counter-examples in linear dynamics like a hypercyclic operator whose adjoint is also hypercyclic
[
33
]
, or a frequently hypercyclic operator that is not chaotic
[
4
]
. They are also a key element in constructing hypercyclic operators on arbitrary infinite-dimensional separable Banach spaces
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
. In addition, some natural operators on spaces of analytic functions like the differentiation operator
D
, the multiplication operator
M_{z}
, or the Bergman backward shift are weighted shift operators, see
[
36
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
22
, Sections 4.1, 4.4]
.

On the other hand, some of the counter-examples provided by weighted shifts are rather complicated. For example, one may wonder whether there is not a more elementary operator than the one constructed in
[
7
]
(see also
[
10
]
) that is upper frequently hypercyclic but not frequently hypercyclic. In some cases, weighted shifts fail completely: Menet’s recent deep construction of a chaotic operator that is not frequently hypercyclic
[
29
]
could not have been done with a weighted backward shift, see
[
22
, Corollary 9.14]
.

It is thus of great practical and theoretical interest to enlarge the class of weighted shift operators. Now, these operators are not only determined by their weights and their underlying space, but also by the set of indices for the sequences they act upon: traditionally, these are the set
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers for the unilateral shifts, and the set
\mathbb{Z}
of integers for the bilateral shifts. So, a natural way to extend the notion of weighted shifts is to replace these sets by directed trees.

1.2.
Linear dynamics and universality on trees

Analysis on trees goes back at least to the work of Cartier
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, who introduces and studies harmonic functions on trees. His trees are not directed, but by singling out, as Cartier does, a particular vertex, one may consider them as rooted directed trees, see also
[
1
]
,
[
27
]
. Moreover, he demands that each vertex has only a finite number of children. These trees can be regarded as discrete analogues of the complex unit disk; Cartier also defines the boundary of a tree, which is then the analogue of the complex unit circle. Hardy spaces on trees were subsequently introduced by Korányi, Picardello and Taibleson
[
25
]
. Motivated by work of Bourdon and Shapiro
[
11
]
on the hypercyclicity of composition operators on the Hardy space, Pavone
[
31
]
studies hypercyclicity of composition operators on
L^{p}
-spaces of the boundary of a Cartier tree. In a different direction, Abakumov, Nestoridis and Picardello
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
obtain a universality result on harmonic functions on trees that is analogous to Menshov’s universal trigonometric series
[
28
]
,
[
30
]
.

There is a growing interest in the analysis on trees, see, for example,
[
19
]
and
[
16
]
and the literature cited therein.

1.3.
Weighted shifts on trees

In this paper we study the dynamical behaviour of shift operators on trees. Perhaps surprisingly, weighted shifts on arbitrary directed trees were only introduced and studied quite recently in the work of Jabłoński, Jung and Stochel
[
24
]
; these authors provide a detailed study of their operator theoretic properties on the
\ell^{2}
-space of the tree. Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
]
then initiated the study of the dynamical properties of these operators on arbitrary
\ell^{p}
-spaces; Martínez-Avendaño and Rivera-Guasco
[
27
]
consider equally the unweighted shift on the Lipschitz space of the tree.

In the classical case of the trees
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
, Salas
[
34
]
characterized hypercyclicity and weak mixing on the
\ell^{p}
-spaces, while Costakis and Sambarino
[
17
]
obtained the corresponding result for the mixing property. It is the aim of this paper to solve completely the problem of characterizing when a weighted shift on a directed tree is hypercyclic, weakly mixing or mixing, provided that the underlying space is of type
\ell^{p}
or
c_{0}
. We thereby complete the study started by Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
]
.

Let us add a word of caution. While Jabłoński et al.
[
24
]
consider weighted shifts on the (unweighted)
\ell^{2}
-space, Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
]
has chosen to study (unweighted) shifts on weighted
\ell^{p}
-spaces. It is well known, as explained in
[
36
]
,
[
22
, Section 4.1]
for the trees
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
and mentioned in
[
26
]
for arbitrary trees, that these are merely two different representations of the “same operator”. Motivated by the classical theory, we will consider here both representations in parallel. However, in order to make the transition between the two formulations more transparent we will employ a notation that differs somewhat from that of Martínez-Avendaño, as will be explained in Section
2
.

1.4.
Linear dynamics

We proceed by providing the basic notions from linear dynamics that we will consider. For further details, in particular for the explanation of some additional notions mentioned above, we refer the reader to the books
[
6
]
and
[
22
]
. A bounded (linear) operator
T
on a Banach space
X
is called
hypercyclic
provided there is some
x\in X
whose orbit under
T
,

\Orb(x,T)=\{x,Tx,T^{2}x,\ldots\},

is dense in
X
. If furthermore the operator defined by

(T\oplus T)(x,y)=(Tx,Ty),\quad(x,y)\in X\times X

is hypercyclic, then
T
is called
weakly mixing
. In the context of separable Banach spaces, hypercyclicity is equivalent to
topological transitivity
which requires that for every pair
U
and
V
of non-empty open subsets of
X
there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\varnothing
. If in addition,
T^{k}(U)\cap V\neq\varnothing
for all
k\geq n
, then
T
will be called
mixing
. The main tool for proving the weak mixing and mixing properties of an operator is the following, known as the
Hypercyclicity Criterion
, see
[
22
, Theorem 3.12]
; note, however, that there are hypercyclic operators that are not weakly mixing, see
[
18
]
,
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Hypercyclicity Criterion)
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space and
T
a bounded operator on
X
. Assume that there exist dense subsets
X_{0},Y_{0}
of
X
, an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers, and maps
R_{n_{k}}:Y_{0}\rightarrow X
,
k\geq 1
, such that, for any
x\in X_{0}
and
y\in Y_{0}
,

(i)

T^{n_{k}}x\rightarrow 0
,

(ii)

R_{n_{k}}y\rightarrow 0
, and

(iii)

T^{n_{k}}R_{n_{k}}y\rightarrow y

as
k\to\infty
. Then
T
is weakly mixing.

If the conditions hold for the full sequence
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
then
T
is mixing.

We mention that we will also need a variant of the criterion, see Proposition
5.1
below.

2.
Shifts on trees

2.1.
Directed trees

Let
(V,E)
be a
directed tree
, that is, a connected directed graph consisting of a countable set
V
of vertices and a set
E\subset V\times V\setminus\{(v,v):v\in V\}
of edges that has no cycles and for which each vertex
v\in V
has at most one
w\in V
such that
(w,v)\in E
. The terminology that we use is standard. Thus we can say that
w
is the
parent
of
v
, and the
indegree
of
v
is either 0 if it does not have a parent or 1 if it does. For details we refer to
[
13
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
26
]
; but note that in
[
13
]
and
[
20
]
trees are not directed. We only demand connectedness in order to avoid that the tree splits into a disjoint union of unrelated trees.

A directed tree has at most one vertex without a parent, called the
root
of the tree and denoted
{\tt{root}}
. The parent of a vertex
v\neq{\tt{root}}
is denoted by
\prt(v)
. Any vertex whose parent is
v
is called a child of
v
, and the set of children of
v
is denoted by
\Chi(v)
. More generally, for any
n\geq 1
,

\Chi^{n}(v)=\Chi(\Chi(\ldots(\Chi(v)))),\quad\text{($n$ times)},

with
\Chi^{0}(v)=\{v\}
. The
outdegree
of a vertex
v
is
|\Chi(v)|
, the cardinality of
\Chi(v)
. A
leaf
is a vertex with outdegree
0
.

For two vertices
u,v\in V
, let us write
u\sim v
if there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\prt^{n}(u)=\prt^{n}(v)
, which defines an equivalence relation on
V
. For any
v\in V
, the equivalence class
[v]
of
v
is called the
generation
of
v
. Let
v_{0}\in V
be a fixed vertex; if the directed tree has a root, we pick
v_{0}={\tt{root}}
. We will enumerate the generations with respect to
v_{0}
by defining

\text{Gen}_{n}=\Chi^{n}({\tt{root}}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

if the directed tree has a root, and

\text{Gen}_{n}=\text{Gen}_{n}(v_{0})=\{v\in V:\exists m\geq 0,\prt^{n+m}(v)=\prt^{m}(v_{0})\},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

otherwise. If there is an
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\prt^{n}(v)=\prt^{n}(u)
for each
u\in[v]
then
[v]
has a
common ancestor
. We will say that an unrooted tree has a
free left end
if there is some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\text{Gen}_{k}
is a singleton for all
k\leq n
. In that case, each generation has a common ancestor. We talk of a free
left
end because we imagine trees to be directed from left to right, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
An unrooted directed tree with a free left end

A directed tree
(V,E)
will be called
symmetric
if

|\Chi(u)|=|\Chi(v)|

whenever
u\sim v
. This is equivalent to demanding that the map

|\Chi(\cdot)|:V\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}

depends only on the generation. Let us fix
v_{0}\in V
, with
v_{0}={\tt{root}}
in the rooted case, and let us enumerate the generations with respect to
v_{0}
. For
v\in\text{Gen}_{n}
, we set

\gamma_{n}=|\Chi(v)|,

which is well defined if
(V,E)
is symmetric.

2.2.
Sequence spaces

In this paper we restrict our attention to spaces of type
\ell^{p}
and
c_{0}
. We recall here the definition of weighted versions of these spaces on general index sets. Thus, let
V
be an arbitrary countable set; a possible graph structure on
V
will not play any role in this subsection. Let
\mu=(\mu_{v})_{v\in V}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}
be a family of non-zero (real or complex) numbers, called a
weight
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
weighted
\ell^{p}
-space
of
V
is defined as

\ell^{p}(V,\mu)=\Big\{f\in\mathbb{K}^{V}:\|f\|^{p}:=\sum_{v\in V}|f(v)\mu_{v}|^{p}<\infty\Big\}.

Note that, unlike in
[
26
]
, the weights also carry a power
p
; in addition, it will be convenient for us not to restrict to positive weights. As usual,
\mathbb{K}
denotes either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.

The space
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
is defined similarly. The only reason for considering this space in our paper is that it is the dual of some
\ell^{1}
-space; from a dynamical point of view the space is of little interest because it is non-separable (unless
V
is finite).

In addition we will consider the
weighted
c_{0}
-space
of
V
,

c_{0}(V,\mu)=\{f\in\mathbb{K}^{V}:\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists F\subset V\mbox{ finite},\forall v\in V\setminus F,|f(v)\mu_{v}|<\varepsilon\},

normed by

\|f\|=\sup_{v\in V}|f(v)\mu_{v}|;

it is a closed subspace of
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
.

Setting
e_{v}=\chi_{\{v\}}
for
v\in V
we observe that
\text{span}\{e_{v}:v\in V\}
is dense in
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and in
c_{0}(V,\mu)
. For any element
f\in\mathbb{K}^{V}
, we call
\{v\in V:f(v)\neq 0\}
its
support
.

If
\mu=1
for all
v\in V
, the corresponding unweighted spaces are denoted by

\ell^{p}(V),\,1\leq p\leq\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad c_{0}(V).

Under the natural pairing

(2.1)

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{v\in V}f(v)\overline{g(v)}

we have the following dual spaces:

\ell^{p}(V,\mu)^{*}=\ell^{p^{*}}\!(V,1/\overline{\mu}),\,1\leq p<\infty,

where
p^{*}
is the conjugate exponent, and

c_{0}(V,\mu)^{*}=\ell^{1}(V,1/\overline{\mu}).

2.3.
Shift operators on directed trees

Throughout this subsection, let
(V,E)
be a directed tree. We will consider weighted (forward) shifts and weighted backward shifts on our spaces. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
be a family of non-zero scalars, that is, a weight.

According to
[
24
]
, the
weighted (forward) shift

S_{\lambda}
is defined on
\mathbb{K}^{V}
by

(S_{\lambda}f)(v)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{v}f(\prt(v)),&\mbox{if }v\neq{\tt{root}},\\
0,&\mbox{if }v={\tt{root}},\end{cases}

where
v\in V
; note that the value of
\lambda_{{\tt{root}}}
is not used.

Similarly, by
[
26
]
, the
weighted backward shift

B_{\lambda}
is defined formally on
\mathbb{K}^{V}
by

(B_{\lambda}f)(v)=\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\lambda_{u}f(u),\quad v\in V,

where, as usual, an empty sum is zero. When one considers
B_{\lambda}
on a specific space
X
one needs to ensure that all the series defining
B_{\lambda}f
converge for any
f\in X
.

If
\lambda=1
for all
v\in V
, the corresponding unweighted shifts are denoted by
S
and
B
.

There is an important link between the two types of shifts: backward shifts are the adjoints of forward shifts. In fact, this property has motivated the definition of backward shifts in
[
26
]
. However, the notation in that paper obscures the following simple fact.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be weights.

(a)

If
S_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or on
c_{0}(V,\mu)
, then
S_{\lambda}^{\ast}=B_{\overline{\lambda}}
on
\ell^{p^{\ast}}\!(V,1/\overline{\mu})
or on
\ell^{1}(V,1/\overline{\mu})
, respectively.

(b)

If
B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or on
c_{0}(V,\mu)
, then
B_{\lambda}^{*}=S_{\overline{\lambda}}
on
\ell^{p^{*}}\!(V,1/\overline{\mu})
or on
\ell^{1}(V,1/\overline{\mu})
, respectively.

Proof.

It is readily verified that, with respect to the dual pairing (
2.1
),

\langle S_{\lambda}f,g\rangle=\langle f,B_{\overline{\lambda}}g\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\langle B_{\lambda}f,g\rangle=\langle f,S_{\overline{\lambda}}g\rangle,

where
f
is in the underlying space and
g
in its dual; the rearrangements needed in these calculations can be justified by first treating
|\lambda|
,
|f|
, and
|g|
.
∎

We now characterize when a (forward or backward) shift is a bounded operator. The closed graph theorem shows that for this it suffices that the operator in question is a well-defined map from the space back into the space.

The following result generalizes Jabłoński et al.
[
24
, Proposition 3.1.8]
and Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be weights.

(a)
Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

S_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

there is a constant
M>0
such that
\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}\mu_{u}|^{p}\leq M|\mu_{v}|^{p}
,
v\in V
.

In this case,
\|S_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\in V}\frac{1}{|\mu_{v}|}(\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}\mu_{u}|^{p})^{1/p}
.

(b)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

S_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

there is a constant
M>0
such that
|\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}|\leq M|\mu_{\prt(v)}|
,
v\in V\setminus\{{\tt{root}}\}
.

In this case,
\|S_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\neq{\tt{root}}}\big|\frac{\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}}{\mu_{\prt(v)}}\big|
.

(c)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

S_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
c_{0}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

there is a constant
M>0
such that
|\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}|\leq M|\mu_{\prt(v)}|
,
v\in V\setminus\{{\tt{root}}\}
, and for each
v\in V
,
(\lambda_{u}\mu_{u})_{u\in\Chi(v)}\in c_{0}(\Chi(v))
.

In this case,
\|S_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\neq{\tt{root}}}\big|\frac{\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}}{\mu_{\prt(v)}}\big|
.

Proof.

Parts (a) and (b) follow immediately from the fact that

\|S_{\lambda}f\|_{\ell^{p}(V,\mu)}^{p}=\sum_{v\neq{\tt{root}}}|\lambda_{v}f(\prt(v))\mu_{v}|^{p}=\sum_{w\in V}|f(w)\mu_{w}|^{p}\Big(\frac{1}{|\mu_{w}|^{p}}\sum_{v\in\Chi(w)}|\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}|^{p}\Big)

and

(2.2)

\|S_{\lambda}f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)}=\sup_{v\neq{\tt{root}}}|\lambda_{v}f(\prt(v))\mu_{v}|=\sup_{w\in V}|f(w)\mu_{w}|\Big(\frac{1}{|\mu_{w}|}\sup_{v\in\Chi(w)}|\lambda_{v}\mu_{v}|\Big).

As for (c), applying (
2.2
) to
e_{v}
,
v\in V
, and noting that
S_{\lambda}e_{v}\in c_{0}(V,\mu)
shows that (i) implies (ii). Conversely, if (ii) holds then, by (b),
S_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
; moreover, by the second part of (ii),
S_{\lambda}e_{v}\in c_{0}(V,\mu)
for all
v\in V
. Since
\text{span}\{e_{v}:v\in V\}
is dense in
c_{0}(V,\mu)
and
c_{0}(V,\mu)
is a closed subspace of
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
, (i) follows.
∎

We consider the analogue for backward shift operators, which improves, in particular,
[
26
, Proposition 4.5]
; see also
[
24
, Proposition 3.4.1]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be weights.

(a)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{1}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

\sup_{v\in V\setminus\{{\tt{root}}\}}|\mu_{\prt(v)}|\big|\frac{\lambda_{v}}{\mu_{v}}\big|<\infty
.

In this case,
\|B_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\in V\setminus\{{\tt{root}}\}}|\mu_{\prt(v)}|\big|\frac{\lambda_{v}}{\mu_{v}}\big|
.

(b)
Let
1<p<\infty
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

\sup_{v\in V}|\mu_{v}|^{p^{*}}\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\big|^{p^{*}}<\infty
.

In this case,
\|B_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\in V}|\mu_{v}|(\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\big|^{p^{*}})^{1/{p^{*}}}
.

(c)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
;

(ii)

B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
c_{0}(V,\mu)
;

(iii)

\sup_{v\in V}|\mu_{v}|\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\big|<\infty
.

In this case,
\|B_{\lambda}\|=\sup_{v\in V}|\mu_{v}|\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\big|
as an operator on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
and on
c_{0}(V,\mu)
.

Proof.

(b) (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
M
denote the supremum in (ii). Using Hölder’s inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\Big|\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}f(u)|\,\mu_{v}\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V}|\mu_{v}|^{p}\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\Big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\Big|^{p^{*}}\Big)^{p/p^{*}}\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|f(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq M^{p/p^{*}}\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|f(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\leq M^{p/p^{*}}\|f\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(V,\mu)}.

Thus, we get that
B_{\lambda}
is well-defined and

(2.3)

\|B_{\lambda}\|\leq M^{1/p^{*}}.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). We consider
v\in V
. Let
I:\ell^{p}(\Chi(v),\mu\chi_{\Chi(v)})\to\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
be the canonical isometric embedding. We define

\phi_{v}(f):=\mu_{v}(B_{\lambda}If)(v)=\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\mu_{v}\lambda_{u}f(u),\quad f\in\ell^{p}(\Chi(v),\mu\chi_{\Chi(v)}),

which is a linear functional on
\ell^{p}(\Chi(v),\mu\chi_{\Chi(v)})
. Since

|\phi_{v}(f)|=\|B_{\lambda}If\|_{\ell^{p}(V,\mu)}\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|\|If\|_{\ell^{p}(V,\mu)}=\|B_{\lambda}\|\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\Chi(v),\mu\chi_{\Chi(v)})},

we have that
\phi_{v}\in\ell^{p}(\Chi(v),\mu\chi_{\Chi(v)})^{\ast}=\ell^{p^{*}}\!(\Chi(v),1/\overline{\mu}\,\chi_{\Chi(v)})
with
\|\phi_{v}\|\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|
. In other words,

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\Big|\frac{\mu_{v}\lambda_{u}}{\overline{\mu}_{u}}\Big|^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|.

This implies (ii), and together with (
2.3
) we have also determined
\|B_{\lambda}\|
.

The proof of (a) is essentially the same, noting that
p^{*}=\infty
.

(c) (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Let
B_{\lambda}
be a bounded operator on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
. Note that
B_{\lambda}e_{v}\in c_{0}(V,\mu)
for all
v\in V
; since
\text{span}\{e_{v}:v\in V\}
is dense in
c_{0}(V,\mu)
and
c_{0}(V,\mu)
is a closed subspace of
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
, (ii) follows.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
v\in V
. It follows as in (b), implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii), that

(2.4)

\sup_{v\in V}\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\Big|\frac{\mu_{v}\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\Big|\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|_{c_{0}(V,\mu)\to c_{0}(V,\mu)},

hence (iii).

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
M
denote the supremum in (iii). Then we have for
f\in\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in V}\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}f(u)|\,|\mu_{v}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{v\in V}\Big(|\mu_{v}|\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}\Big|\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\mu_{u}}\Big|\Big)\sup_{u\in\Chi(v)}|f(u)\mu_{u}|

\displaystyle\leq M\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)}.

Thus we get that
B_{\lambda}
is well-defined on
\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)
and

\|B_{\lambda}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)\to\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)}\leq M,

which implies (i). Since, in addition,

\|B_{\lambda}\|_{c_{0}(V,\mu)\to c_{0}(V,\mu)}\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)\to\ell^{\infty}(V,\mu)}

we have together with (
2.4
) that the norm of
B_{\lambda}
on both spaces is
M
.
∎

As far as dynamical properties are concerned we will consider in this paper weighted shifts on unweighted spaces (as do Jabłoński et al.
[
24
]
) and unweighted shifts on weighted spaces (as does Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
]
). One could perhaps argue that it is more natural to put weights on the operator, but the analysis is by far easier when the weights are put on the space because of the simplicity of the orbits of the unweighted shifts
B
and
S
. Luckily, the two kinds of operators are canonically conjugate to one another and thus have the same dynamics. This is a well-known fact in the classical situation, see
[
36
]
and
[
22
, Section 4.1]
, and it is mentioned in
[
26
]
for arbitrary trees.

The conjugacies are in fact given by the following commutative diagrams. Let
\mu=(\mu_{v})_{v\in V}
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
be weights, and let
X
denote one of the spaces
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(V)
. By
X_{\mu}
we denote the space
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(V,\mu)
, respectively. Let
\phi_{\mu}(f)=(\mu_{v}f(v))_{v\in V}
. Then
\phi_{\mu}:X_{\mu}\to X
is an isometric isomorphism.

Now, it is readily verified that

\begin{CD}X_{\mu}@>{S}>{}>X_{\mu}\\
@V{\phi_{\mu}}V{}V@V{}V{\phi_{\mu}}V\\
X@>{S_{\lambda}}>{}>X\end{CD}

is a commutative diagram if the weights
\mu
and
\lambda
are linked by

\lambda_{v}=\frac{\mu_{v}}{\mu_{\prt(v)}},\,v\neq{\tt{root}};

note that, in this diagram,
S
maps
X_{\mu}
into
X_{\mu}
if and only if
S_{\lambda}
maps
X
into
X
.

In order to write
\mu
in terms of
\lambda
we fix a vertex
v_{0}\in V
; it is convenient to take
v_{0}={\tt{root}}
if the tree is rooted. Then

\mu_{v}=\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{\prt^{k}(v)}}{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{\prt^{k}(v_{0})}}\mu_{v_{0}},\,v\in V;

here
m,n\geq 0
are minimal numbers such that

(2.5)

\prt^{n}(v)=\prt^{m}(v_{0}),

and, as usual, empty products are 1. There is no harm to consider larger
m
and
n
, but then there are extra terms in the quotient above that will cancel.

In the same way we have the commutative diagram

(2.6)

\begin{CD}X_{\mu}@>{B}>{}>X_{\mu}\\
@V{\phi_{\mu}}V{}V@V{}V{\phi_{\mu}}V\\
X@>{B_{\lambda}}>{}>X\end{CD}

if the weights
\mu
and
\lambda
are linked by

\lambda_{v}=\frac{\mu_{\prt(v)}}{\mu_{v}},\,v\neq{\tt{root}}

and

(2.7)

\mu_{v}=\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda_{\prt^{k}(v_{0})}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{\prt^{k}(v)}}\mu_{v_{0}},\,v\in V

with
m,n\geq 0
minimal so that (
2.5
) holds.

Of course, more generally, one could also consider arbitrary weighted shifts on arbitrary weighted spaces; but they are likewise conjugate to an unweighted shift on a weighted space and therefore add nothing new.

The conjugacies obtained above contain an interesting special case that is essentially known. Let
B_{\lambda}:X\to X
and
B:X_{\mu}\to X_{\mu}
be conjugate via (
2.7
), and let
c
be a scalar with
|c|=1
. Then, again by (
2.7
),
cB_{\lambda}=B_{c\lambda}:X\to X
is conjugate to
B:X_{\widetilde{\mu}}\to X_{\widetilde{\mu}}
, where the weight
\widetilde{\mu}
satisfies
|\widetilde{\mu}_{v}/\widetilde{\mu}_{v_{0}}|=|\mu_{v}/\mu_{v_{0}}|
for all
v\in V
. But this implies that
X_{\widetilde{\mu}}=X_{\mu}
. In other words,
cB_{\lambda}
and
B_{\lambda}
are similar operators on
X
whenever
|c|=1
. As a consequence, in the complex scalar case, the spectrum and the point spectrum of
B_{\lambda}
are rotation invariant. The same arguments work for any weighted shift on any weighted space. Moreover, one sees that the conjugacy is given by an isometric isomorphism. In the case of Hilbert spaces, such operators are called
circular operators
. For simplicity, let us use this terminology also in the Banach space setting.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be weights. Then any weighted shift operator
B_{\lambda}
and
S_{\lambda}
is a circular operator on any of the spaces
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
c_{0}(V,\mu)
.

In the Hilbert space case, this result is due to Shields
[
36
, Section 2]
for classical weighted shifts, and to Jabłoński et al.
[
24
, Section 3.3]
for shifts on directed trees.

2.4.
Rolewicz operators and symmetric shift operators

In this paper we will consider two special classes of backward shift operators, the multiples of the backward shift
B
, also known as Rolewicz operators, and symmetric shift operators.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
,
\lambda\neq 0
. Then the
Rolewicz operator

\lambda B=B_{(\lambda)_{v\in V}}
is bounded on each space
\ell^{1}(V)
, while it is bounded on any of the spaces
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(V)
if and only if
\sup_{v\in V}|\Chi(v)|<\infty
. These operators are named after S. Rolewicz who was the first to consider the dynamical properties of the operator
\lambda B
for
V=\mathbb{N}
, see
[
32
]
.

A weight
\lambda=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
will be called

if

\lambda_{u}=\lambda_{v}

whenever
u\sim v
. That is, a symmetric weight is a function of the generation. Let us fix
v_{0}\in V
, with
v_{0}={\tt{root}}
in the rooted case, and let us enumerate the generations with respect to
v_{0}
, see Subsection
2.1
. For

we will use the notation

\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{v}.

Notice that this makes sense due to the symmetry of the weight. Thus, from now on, we will be indexing a symmetric weight as
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n}
, where

ranges over
\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the rooted case and over
\mathbb{Z}
in the unrooted case. An interesting class of weighted backward shifts, generalizing the usual unilateral and bilateral ones, occurs when we consider a symmetric weight on a symmetric directed tree.

Example 2.6
.

Let
(V,E)
be a symmetric directed tree and
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n}
a symmetric weight on it. Then
B_{\lambda}
is bounded on
\ell^{1}(V)
if and only if

\sup_{n}|\lambda_{n}|<\infty,

while it is bounded on any of the spaces
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p\leq\infty
, or
c_{0}(V)
, if and only if

\sup_{n}\gamma_{n}|\lambda_{n+1}|^{p^{*}}<\infty,

where
p^{*}=1
when the underlying space is
\ell^{\infty}(V)
or
c_{0}(V)
. We note that for
\ell^{1}(V)
the symmetry of the directed tree is not needed.

If the directed tree

4.
Dynamics of weighted backward shifts - the rooted case

Before we continue, let us state the obvious.

Remark 4.1
.

If a directed tree
(V,E)
has a leaf, then no weighted backward shift can be hypercyclic on any of the spaces considered here. This follows from the fact that, if
v
is a leaf, then
(B_{\lambda}^{n}f)(v)=0
for any element
f
and any
n\geq 1
.

Thus, throughout Sections
4
and
5
we will assume that the directed tree has no leaves.

The dynamics of weighted backward shifts on
\mathbb{N}
is considerably easier to determine than that for shifts on
\mathbb{Z}
. It turns out that the same is true more generally for rooted trees as opposed to unrooted ones.

When trying to characterize hypercyclicity of backward shifts, we were confronted with the need to control the lower bound of a weighted norm when the unweighted
\ell^{1}
-norm is fixed. This is exactly what the reverse Hölder inequality does.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
J
be a finite or countable set. Let
\mu=(\mu_{j})_{j}\in(\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\})^{J}
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\|x\|_{1}=1}\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|

\displaystyle=\inf_{j\in J}|\mu_{j}|,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|x\|_{1}=1}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|^{p^{*}}}\Big)^{-1/p^{*}},\,1<p<\infty,

\displaystyle\inf_{\|x\|_{1}=1}\sup_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|}\Big)^{-1},

where
x\in\mathbb{K}^{J}
,
\|x\|_{1}=\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}|
and
\infty^{-1}=0
.

The same holds when the sequences
x
are required, in addition, to be of finite support.

Proof.

The first claim is easily seen.

Next, let
1<p<\infty
, and suppose that
J
is a finite set. Take
q=\frac{1}{1-p}
, so that
\frac{1}{1/p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Then the reverse Hölder inequality, see
[
23
, Theorem 13]
, tells us that

\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|^{p}\geq\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}|^{p/p}\Big)^{p}\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|\mu_{j}|^{pq}\Big)^{1/q},

and hence

\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\geq\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}|\Big(\sum_{j\in J}|\mu_{j}|^{-p^{*}}\Big)^{-1/p^{*}},

where equality holds for
x=(|\mu_{j}|^{-p^{*}})_{j}
. Dividing by
\|x\|_{1}
we get the second claim for finite sets
J
. The general case is easily deduced from this.

For the third claim: Since

\sum_{j\in J}|x_{j}|=\sum_{j\in J}\Big(|x_{j}\mu_{j}|\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|}\Big)\leq(\sup_{j\in J}|x_{j}\mu_{j}|)\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|},

it follows that the infimum is at least
\big(\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|}\big)^{-1}
. If
J
is finite, then the infimum is attained for

x\in\mathbb{K}^{J}
given by

x_{j}=\frac{1}{|\mu_{j}|}\Big(\sum_{k\in J}\frac{1}{|\mu_{k}|}\Big)^{-1},\quad j\in J.

The general case is easily deduced from this.
∎

One may also arrive at the second claim for finite sets
J
by the method of Lagrange multipliers: one needs to minimize
\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}^{p}|\mu_{j}|^{p}
subject to
x_{j}>0
for all
j\in J
and
\sum_{j\in J}x_{j}=1
.

We can now characterize hypercyclic backward shifts on directed rooted trees, and we first put the weights on the space. As is well known, for the shifts on
\mathbb{N}
, hypercyclicity and weak mixing coincide. The following theorem shows that this property is inherited by shifts on trees.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(V,E)
be a rooted directed tree and
\mu
a weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
, and suppose that the backward shift
B
is a bounded operator on
X
.

(a)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B
is hypercyclic;

(ii)

B
is weakly mixing;

(iii)

there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
, we have as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\mu_{u}|\to 0,

if
X=\ell^{1}(V,\mu)
;

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\to\infty,

if
X=\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
;

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|}\to\infty,

if
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
.

(b)

B
is mixing if and only if condition
(iii)
holds for the full sequence
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
.

Proof.

(a) It suffices to show that (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) and (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). The strategy of the proof is the classical one, the new element being Lemma
4.2
(for both implications).

We do the proof for
X=\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
; the other two cases are similar.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii). We apply the Hypercyclicity Criterion. Let
X_{0}=Y_{0}=\text{span}\{e_{v}:v\in V\}
, which is dense in
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
.

Let
v\in V
. By condition (iii) and Lemma
4.2
, applied to
J=\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)
, there are
g_{v,n_{k}}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}
,
k\geq 1
,
of support in
\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)
such that

(4.1)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|g_{v,n_{k}}(u)|=1

and

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|g_{v,n_{k}}(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Then
g_{v,n_{k}}\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
and
g_{v,n_{k}}\to 0
in
\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
as
k\to\infty
. We can suppose that
g_{v,n_{k}}
is non-negative. (For
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
we choose
g_{v,n_{k}}
to be in addition of finite support so that
g_{v,n_{k}}\in c_{0}(V,\mu)
; see the final assertion in Lemma
4.2
.)

Now define maps
R_{n_{k}}:Y_{0}\rightarrow\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
k\geq 1
, by setting

R_{n_{k}}e_{v}=g_{v,n_{k}},v\in V

and extending linearly to
Y_{0}
. Then, for all
g\in Y_{0}
,

R_{n_{k}}g\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. In addition, since
g_{v,n_{k}}
is non-negative and has support in
\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)
, (
4.1
) implies that

B^{n_{k}}R_{n_{k}}e_{v}=B^{n_{k}}g_{v,n_{k}}=e_{v},v\in V,

so that
B^{n_{k}}R_{n_{k}}
is the identity on
Y_{0}
; note that the second equation is because
B
is the unweighted shift.

Finally, since
V
is rooted, for any
f\in X_{0}
there is some
k
large enough so that

B^{n_{k}}f=0.

Thus
B
satisfies the Hypercyclicity Criterion and is therefore weakly mixing.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
F\subset V
be finite,
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
\delta>0
such that, for any
g=(g(v))_{v\in V}\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
\|g-\sum_{v\in F}e_{v}\|<\delta
implies that
|g(v)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
v\in F
.

Now, by topological transitivity of
B
there is some
f\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
and some
n\geq N
such that

\|f\|<{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\text{\quad}\Big\|B^{n}f-\sum_{v\in F}e_{v}\Big\|<\delta,

which implies that, for all
v\in F
,

c_{v}:=\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|f(u)|\geq\Big|\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}f(u)\Big|=|(B^{n}f)(v)|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Choosing, in Lemma
4.2
,
\Chi^{n}(v)
for
J
and the restriction of
\frac{1}{c_{v}}|f|
to
\Chi^{n}(v)
as
x
we obtain that, for any
v\in F
,

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\Big)^{-1/p^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{v}}\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|f(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\frac{1}{c_{v}}\|f\|<2\varepsilon.

Applying this argument now to an increasing sequence
(F_{k})_{k}
of finite subsets of

with
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}F_{k}=V
, to an increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
of positive integers, and to a positive sequence

tending to 0, condition (iii) follows.

(b) The proof is identical to that of (a). For one implication one has to note that if the Hypercyclicity Criterion holds for the full sequence then the operator is mixing. For the other implication one replaces topological transitivity by the definition of the mixing property.
∎

For what it’s worth let us mention that the characterizing conditions in (iii) can be written in a unified way. If we define the conjugate exponent as

for

where

.

. This is, of course, not the case for general trees. Just consider the directed tree in which the root has exactly two children and any other vertex has exactly one child; then define
\lambda_{v}=1
along one branch and
\lambda_{v}=2
along the other. However, when we impose symmetry on both the directed tree and the weight, then the conditions characterizing hypercyclicity and the mixing property simplify just as in classical case.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
(V,E)
be a symmetric, rooted directed tree and
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
a symmetric weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and suppose that
B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
X
.

(a)
The operator
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic (resp. mixing) on
\ell^{1}(V)
if and only if

\sup_{n\geq 1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|=\infty\quad(resp.\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|=\infty).

(b)
The operator
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic (resp. mixing) on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, or on
c_{0}(V)
if and only if

\sup_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|^{p^{*}}=\infty\quad(resp.\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|^{p^{*}}=\infty),

where
p^{*}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

Proof.

We will do the proof for hypercyclicity on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, since the remaining cases are similar and even simpler. Let us observe that, due to the symmetry of
(V,E)
, if
v\in\text{Gen}_{m}
,
m\geq 0
, then

|\Chi^{n}(v)|=\gamma_{m}\gamma_{m+1}\cdots\gamma_{m+n-1},\quad n\geq 1.

Now, one implication of the claim is obvious since if
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic, then by Theorem
4.4
there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
such that

|\Chi^{n_{k}}({\tt{root}})|\,|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}=\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty.

For the reverse implication, let
(n_{k})_{k}
be an increasing sequence such that

\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty.

We fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We then have for
k
sufficiently large that

\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-j-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}-j}|^{p^{*}}=\frac{\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}}{\gamma_{n_{k}-j}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{n_{k}-j+1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}}.

By Proposition
2.3
, the boundedness of
B_{\lambda}
yields for these
k
that

\gamma_{n_{k}-j}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{n_{k}-j+1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}\leq\|B_{\lambda}\|^{jp^{*}}.

We therefore deduce that, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n_{k}-j-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n_{k}-j}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty.

By an obvious variant of
[
22
, Lemma 4.2]
we obtain an increasing sequence
(m_{k})_{k}
such that, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{m_{k}+j-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{m_{k}+j}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty,

which implies that, for each
v\in\text{Gen}_{j}
,

|\Chi^{m_{k}}(v)|\,|\lambda_{j+1}\cdots\lambda_{m_{k}+j}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty.

By Theorem
4.4
, this shows that
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.
∎

Incidentally, as the proof shows, the symmetry of the directed tree is not needed when
X=\ell^{1}(V)
.

Example 4.8
.

If
(V,E)
is a rooted directed tree with finitely many children for each vertex and
q\geq 1
a real constant, the
Dirichlet shift
according to
[
15
]
is the weighted forward shift
S_{\lambda,q}
on
\ell^{2}(V)
, where

\lambda_{u,q}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Chi(v)|}}\sqrt{\frac{n_{v}+q}{n_{v}+1}}

for
v\in\text{Gen}_{n_{v}}
and
u\in\Chi(v)
. Then
S_{\lambda,q}
is bounded with
\|S_{\lambda,q}\|=\sqrt{q}
. Being a weighted forward shift on a rooted tree, the Dirichlet shift is not hypercyclic. The adjoint of
S_{\lambda,q}
, which in the unilateral case with
q=2
is called the
Bergman backward shift

[
12
]
, is
B_{\lambda,q}
. When
q=1
,
B_{\lambda,q}
is not hypercyclic since
\|B_{\lambda,q}\|=1
. If
q>1
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in V
, we have that

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|\lambda_{q}(v\rightarrow u)|^{2}=\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(n_{v}+q+k)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(n_{v}+k)}\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}|\Chi(\prt^{k}(u))|}.

Now,

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}|\Chi(\prt^{k}(u))|}=1

and

\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(n_{v}+q+k)}{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(n_{v}+k)}=\frac{n_{v}!}{\Gamma(n_{v}+q)}\frac{\Gamma(n_{v}+q+n)}{(n_{v}+n)!}\sim\frac{n_{v}!}{\Gamma(n_{v}+q)}n^{q-1}\rightarrow\infty.

By Theorem
4.4
, we deduce that
B_{\lambda,q}
is mixing in this case.

Returning to the general situation, we see that the behaviour of the numbers

(4.3)

\Lambda_{v,n}=\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|,\;v\in V,n\geq 1

determines hypercyclicity (and the mixing property) of
B_{\lambda}
on
c_{0}(V)
. But they also play a certain role for the other spaces.
Part (a) of the following result is due to Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
, Theorem 5.4]
. An analogous result holds for the mixing property.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
(V,E)
be a rooted directed tree and
\lambda
a weight. Let
B_{\lambda}
be a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

(a)
If
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic, then there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

\Lambda_{v,n_{k}}\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
.

(b)
Suppose that every vertex has at most finitely many children. If there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

\frac{1}{|\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)|^{1/p}}\Lambda_{v,n_{k}}\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
, then
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.

Proof.

(a) This follows from Theorem
4.4
and the monotonicity of
p
-norms (
1\leq p\leq\infty
) on finite-dimensional spaces
\mathbb{K}^{J}
.

(b) This follows from Theorem
4.4
and Hölder’s inequality, which implies that

\Lambda_{v,n}\leq\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}|\Chi^{n}(v)|^{1/p},

with the usual interpretation when
p=1
.
∎

It is easy to see that neither condition in the corollary is a characterization.

Example 4.10
.

Let
(V,E)
be the rooted binary tree, that is, the directed tree with a root for which every vertex has exactly two children. Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

(a) There is a non-hypercyclic weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
on
\ell^{p}(V)
such that, for any
v\in V
,

\Lambda_{v,n}\to\infty\;\text{ as $n\to\infty$}.

An example is the Rolewicz operator
\lambda B
with
\frac{1}{2}<|\lambda|\leq 1
if
p=1
and
\frac{1}{2}<|\lambda|\leq 2^{-1/p^{*}}
if
p>1
, see Corollary
4.5
.

(b) There is a hypercyclic (even mixing) weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, such that, for any
v\in V
,

\frac{1}{|\Chi^{n}(v)|^{1/p}}\Lambda_{v,n}\to 0\;\text{ as $n\to\infty$}.

Let us define the weight
\lambda
such that, for each
v\in V
, one of its two children has weight
a>0
, the other one has weight
b>0
. Let
v\in V
. Since there are
\binom{n}{k}
branches of length
n
that start form
v
and carry exactly
k
times the weight
a
, we have that

\frac{1}{|\Chi^{n}(v)|^{1/p}}\Lambda_{v,n}=\frac{1}{2^{n/p}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a^{k}b^{n-k}=\frac{(a+b)^{n}}{2^{n/p}},

while, similarly,

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}=(a^{p^{*}}+b^{p^{*}})^{n},\quad p>1,

which has to be replaced by
\sup_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|=\max(a,b)^{n}
if
p=1
. Now choose
a
and
b
so that the first expression tends to 0 and the second tends to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 4.11
.

Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
has obtained another sufficient condition for hypercyclicity of backward shifts; his condition can be deduced by another application of Hölder’s inequality on Theorem
4.4
. Indeed, suppose again that every vertex has at most finitely many children. If
B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and if there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)|^{p}}\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}\frac{1}{|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p}}\to 0,

if
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
,

\displaystyle|\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)|\inf_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|\to\infty,

if
X=c_{0}(V)

as
k\to\infty
, then
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic on
X
. The analogous result holds for the mixing property.

Example 4.12
.

There is a natural way to join two classical weighted backward shifts
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
into a single weighted backward shift on a rooted directed tree
V
. Indeed, take
V
to be the tree where only the root has two children, while all other vertices have exactly one child. Then we can identify the two branches of
V
with two copies of
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
, with the two elements 1 being the children of the root, and we put on the branches the shifts
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, respectively. Then, by Theorem
4.4

and
[
34
, Theorem 2.8]
, the resulting weighted shift
B_{\lambda}
on
V
(with
B_{\lambda}f({\tt{root}})
defined arbitrarily) is easily seen to be hypercyclic if and only if the direct sum
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
given by
(T_{1}\oplus T_{2})(x,y)=(T_{1}x,T_{2}y)
is hypercyclic.

It is interesting to note that there are hypercyclic weighted backward shifts
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
so that
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
is not hypercyclic, see
[
34
, unilateral version of Corollary 2.6]
, in which case the joined shift
B_{\lambda}
is not hypercyclic. Of course, if one of the operators
T_{1}
or
T_{2}
is even mixing, then
T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
is hypercyclic and then so is
B_{\lambda}
.

A natural question arises: given any rooted directed tree, does it support a hypercyclic or even mixing weighted backward shift? We will consider this question for arbitrary directed trees in Section
6
.

5.
Dynamics of weighted backward shifts - the unrooted case

We now address the case of unrooted trees. The situation here is substantially more complicated. We first need the following variant of the Hypercyclicity Criterion, see Theorem
1.1
. The usual condition that, for all
x\in X_{0}
,

T^{n_{k}}x\to 0,\quad k\to\infty,

is replaced by one of the form

T^{n_{k}}x_{k}\to 0,\quad k\to\infty,

where
x_{k}\to x
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space and
T
a bounded operator on
X
. Suppose that there exist dense subsets
X_{0},Y_{0}
of
X
, an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers, and maps
I_{n_{k}}:X_{0}\rightarrow X
and
R_{n_{k}}:Y_{0}\rightarrow X
,
k\geq 1
, such that, for any
x\in X_{0}
and
y\in Y_{0}
,

(i)

I_{n_{k}}x\rightarrow x
,

(ii)

T^{n_{k}}I_{n_{k}}x\rightarrow 0
,

(iii)

R_{n_{k}}y\rightarrow 0
, and

(iv)

T^{n_{k}}R_{n_{k}}y\rightarrow y

as
k\to\infty
. Then
T
is weakly mixing.

If the conditions hold for the full sequence
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
then
T
is mixing.

Proof.

Let
U_{1},U_{2},V_{1},V_{2}
be non-empty open subsets of
X
. Find
x_{j}\in U_{j}\cap X_{0}
and
y_{j}\in V_{j}\cap Y_{0}
and set
z_{j,k}:=I_{n_{k}}x_{j}+R_{n_{k}}y_{j}
for
j=1,2
,
k\geq 1
. Then
z_{j,k}\rightarrow x_{j}
and
T^{n_{k}}z_{j,k}\rightarrow y_{j}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, so that
there is some
k\geq 1
such that
T^{n_{k}}(U_{j})\cap V_{j}\neq\varnothing
,
j=1,2
. This shows that
T\oplus T
is topologically transitive (and
T
is mixing if
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
).
∎

By the way, since every weakly mixing operator satisfies the conditions of the Hypercyclicity Criterion, see
[
9
]
, the above version is only formally weaker.

Yet, it is better adapted to our present needs: it allows us to characterize when a weighted backward shift is hypercyclic (or, equivalently, weakly mixing) and when it is mixing, under the assumption that the tree is unrooted. We start again with the unweighted shift.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed tree and
\mu
a weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
, and suppose that the backward shift
B
is a bounded operator on
X
.

(a)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B
is hypercyclic;

(ii)

B
is weakly mixing;

(iii)

there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
, we have as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\inf_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\mu_{u}|\to 0\quad\text{and}\\
&\min\Big(|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|,\inf_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|\mu_{u}|\Big)\to 0\end{aligned}\right\}

if
X=\ell^{1}(V,\mu)
;

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\\
&\frac{1}{|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|^{p^{*}}}+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\to\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\text{ if $X=\ell^{p}(V,\mu)$, $1<p<\infty$};

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\\
&\frac{1}{|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|}+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|}\to\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

if
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
.

(b)

B
is mixing if and only if condition
(iii)
holds for the full sequence
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
.

Proof.

(a) The proof follows the same overall strategy as that of Theorem
4.3
, but it is technically more demanding. As in that proof, we will only consider the case when
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii). This time we apply the Hypercyclicity Criterion in the form of Proposition
5.1
. Let
X_{0}=Y_{0}=\text{span}\{e_{v}:v\in V\}
.

Since the first part of the characterizing condition in (iii) is identical to the condition in Theorem
4.3
(iii) we can again define maps
R_{n_{k}}:Y_{0}\to\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
k\geq 1
, such that, for all
g\in Y_{0}
,

R_{n_{k}}g\to 0\text{ as $k\to\infty$}\quad\text{and}\quad B^{n_{k}}R_{n_{k}}g=g,k\geq 1.

Next, for
v\in V
and
k\geq 1
, let

M_{v,k}:=\frac{1}{|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|^{p^{*}}}+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}};

note that
M_{v,k}<\infty
by the continuity of
B^{n_{k}}
. Then either

(
\alpha
)

\frac{1}{|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|^{p^{*}}}\geq\frac{M_{v,k}}{2}

or

(
\beta
)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}>\frac{M_{v,k}}{2}.

In the case of
(\alpha)
, let us define

f_{v,n_{k}}=e_{v}.

Then we have that
B^{n_{k}}f_{v,n_{k}}=e_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}
and thus

\|B^{n_{k}}f_{v,n_{k}}\|=|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{M_{v,k}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}.

In the case of
(\beta)
there is by Lemma
4.2
, applied to
J=\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))
, some
h_{v,n_{k}}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}
of support in
\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))
such that

(5.1)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|h_{v,n_{k}}(u)|=1

and

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|h_{v,n_{k}}(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\Big(\frac{2}{M_{v,k}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}.

(For
X=c_{0}(V,\mu)
, we choose
h_{v,n_{k}}
to be in addition of finite support so that
h_{v,n_{k}}\in c_{0}(V,\mu)
; see the final assertion in Lemma
4.2
.) We may suppose that
h_{v,n_{k}}(w)\leq 0
for all
w\in V
. We then define

f_{v,n_{k}}=e_{v}+h_{v,n_{k}},

so that

\|f_{v,n_{k}}-e_{v}\|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{M_{v,k}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}

and, by (
5.1
),

B^{n_{k}}f_{v,n_{k}}=\Big(1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}h_{v,n_{k}}(u)\Big)e_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}=0.

In both cases we therefore have that

\|f_{v,n_{k}}-e_{v}\|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{M_{v,k}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|B^{n_{k}}f_{v,n_{k}}\|\leq\Big(\frac{2}{M_{v,k}}\Big)^{1/p^{*}}.

We can then define the maps
I_{n_{k}}:X_{0}\rightarrow\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
k\geq 1
, by setting

I_{n_{k}}e_{v}=f_{v,n_{k}},v\in V

and extending linearly to
X_{0}
. Then we have for all
f\in X_{0}

I_{n_{k}}f\to f\quad\text{and}\quad B^{n_{k}}I_{n_{k}}f\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Thus all the hypotheses of Proposition
5.1
are satisfied, so that
T
is weakly mixing.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Let
F\subset V
be finite,
N\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
. We choose some
\delta>0
with
\delta\leq\varepsilon
such that, for any
h=(h(v))_{v\in V}\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
,
\|h-\sum_{v\in F}e_{v}\|<\delta
implies that
|h(v)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
v\in F
. We also form a subset
G
of
F
by picking exactly one vertex from
F
per generation; that is, if
u\neq v
in
G
then
u\nsim v
, and for each
v\in F
there is some
u\in G
with
u\sim v
.

By topological transitivity of
B
there is now some
f\in\ell^{p}(V,\mu)
and some
n\geq N
such that

(5.2)

\Big\|f-\sum_{v\in G}e_{v}\Big\|<{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\text{\quad}\Big\|B^{n}f-\sum_{v\in F}e_{v}\Big\|<\delta;

by making
n
bigger, if necessary, we may assume that

(5.3)

\Chi^{n}(F)\cap G=\varnothing\quad\text{and}\text{\quad}\prt^{n}(G)\cap F=\varnothing

and

(5.4)

u,v\in F,u\sim v\Longrightarrow\prt^{n}(u)=\prt^{n}(v).

We claim that this implies that, for all
v\in F
,

(5.5)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}>\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^{p^{*}}}

and

(5.6)

\frac{1}{|\mu_{\prt^{n}(v)}|^{p^{*}}}+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\geq\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^{p^{*}}}.

Once this is shown, it suffices to apply the result to an increasing sequence
(F_{k})_{k}
of finite subsets of
V
with
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}F_{k}=V
, to an increasing sequence
(N_{k})_{k}
of positive integers, and to a positive sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
tending to 0 in order to deduce the characterizing condition in (iii).

Thus, let us first show (
5.5
). To this end, let
v\in F
. We restrict
f
to
\Chi^{n}(v)
; then (
5.2
) with (
5.3
) imply that
\|f\chi_{\Chi^{n}(v)}\|<\varepsilon
. We can now argue exactly as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
to deduce, using (
5.2
), that

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(v)}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\Big)^{-1/p^{*}}<2\varepsilon,

and hence (
5.5
).

As for (
5.6
), it follows from (
5.4
) that it suffices, by definition of
G
, to obtain it only for all the vertices in
G
. Thus let
v\in G
. We define

g=f\chi_{\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}-e_{v}.

It follows from (
5.2
) that

(5.7)

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}|g(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}=\|g\|<\varepsilon;

notice that no other
u\in G
,
u\neq v
, belongs to
\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))
. In addition we have that

(B^{n}f)(\prt^{n}(v))=\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}f(u)=1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}g(u).

By (
5.2
) with (
5.3
) this gives us that

(5.8)

|\mu_{\prt^{n}(v)}|\Big|1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}g(u)\Big|<\delta\leq\varepsilon.

We can now assume that
|\mu_{\prt^{n_{k}}(v)}|>2\varepsilon
; for if
|\mu_{\prt^{n}(v)}|\leq 2\varepsilon
, then (
5.6
) holds trivially. But then (
5.8
) implies that

\Big|1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}g(u)\Big|<\frac{1}{2},

which gives us that

c_{v}:=\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}|g(u)|\geq\Big|\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}g(u)\Big|>\frac{1}{2}.

Then
\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}\frac{1}{c_{v}}|g(u)|=1
. Choosing, in Lemma
4.2
,
J=\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))
and for
x
the restriction of
\frac{1}{c_{v}}|g|
to
J
we obtain with (
5.7
) that

\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}\frac{1}{|\mu_{u}|^{p^{*}}}\Big)^{-1/p^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{v}}\Big(\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))}|g(u)\mu_{u}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}<2\varepsilon,

so that (
5.6
) also holds in this case. This completes the proof of (a).

(b) This can be shown exactly as in the proof of (a) noting the final assertion in Proposition
5.1
and the definition of the mixing property.
∎

We shift again the weights from the space to the operator, arriving easily at the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed tree and
\lambda
a weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and suppose that the weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
X
.

(a)
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic;

(ii)

B_{\lambda}
is weakly mixing;

(iii)

there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
, we have as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\sup_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|\to\infty\quad\text{and}\\
&\frac{\max(1,\sup_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(u)\to u)|)}{|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)|}\to\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

if
X=\ell^{1}(V)
;

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\\
&\frac{1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(u)\to u)|^{p^{*}}}{|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)|^{p^{*}}}\to\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

if
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
;

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|\to\infty\quad\text{and}\\
&\frac{1+\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(u)\to u)|}{|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)|}\to\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

if
X=c_{0}(V)
.

(b)

B_{\lambda}
is mixing if and only if condition
(iii)
holds for the full sequence
(n_{k})_{k}=(n)_{n}
.

It is interesting to consider again as our first special case the Rolewicz operators, see Example
2.5
. It is easy to see that these operators are never hypercyclic on the branchless unrooted tree
\mathbb{Z}
. But they may well be hypercyclic if the tree has some branches. And once more the space
\ell^{1}(V)
plays a special role.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed tree. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
be a non-zero number and
\lambda B
the corresponding Rolewicz operator.

(a)
The operator
\lambda B
is never a hypercyclic operator on
\ell^{1}(V)
.

(b)
Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and let
\lambda B
be bounded on
X
. Then
\lambda B
is hypercyclic on
X
if and only if

(i)

there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

|\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)|\,|\lambda|^{n_{k}p^{*}}\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
,

(ii)

if the tree has a free left end, then
|\lambda|<1
.

And
\lambda B
is mixing on
X
if and only if

(i)

for each
v\in V
,

|\Chi^{n}(v)|\,|\lambda|^{np^{*}}\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
,

(ii)

if the tree has a free left end, then
|\lambda|<1
.

Here,
p^{*}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

Proof.

Assertion (a) is obvious from Theorem
5.3
. For (b), by that theorem,
\lambda B
is hypercyclic if and only (i) holds and

(ii’)

for each
v\in V
,
\frac{1}{|\lambda|^{{n_{k}}p^{*}}}+|\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))|\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
. Now, if the tree does not have a free left end then, for any
v\in V
,
|\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
; note that by (i) the tree cannot have leaves. Hence condition (ii’) is satisfied. On the other hand, if the tree has a free left end then there are
v\in V
with
\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))=\{v\}
for all
n\geq 0
; hence condition (ii’) is equivalent to demanding that
|\lambda|<1
. The proof for the mixing property is the same.
∎

If, in particular, every vertex in an unrooted directed tree
(V,E)
has exactly
N\geq 2
children, then the Rolewicz operator
\lambda B
is hypercyclic on
X
(and then mixing) if and only if

\displaystyle|\lambda|>N^{-1/p^{*}}

\displaystyle\text{ if $X=\ell^{p}(V)$, $1<p<\infty$};

\displaystyle|\lambda|>N^{-1}

\displaystyle\text{ if $X=c_{0}(V)$}.

As already mentioned, if
N=1
, that is, when
V=\mathbb{Z}
, no Rolewicz operator is hypercyclic on any of the spaces considered here.

Applying Theorem
5.3
to weighted backward shifts on symmetric unrooted directed trees and with a symmetric weight, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(V,E)
be a symmetric, unrooted directed tree and
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a symmetric weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and suppose that
B_{\lambda}
is bounded on
X
.

(a)
The operator
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic on
X
if and only if there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{j-n_{k}+1}\cdots\lambda_{j}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad|\lambda_{j+1}\cdots\lambda_{j+n_{k}}|\rightarrow\infty\quad

if
X=\ell^{1}(V)
;

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\min\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{j-n_{k}}\cdots\gamma_{j-1}},|\lambda_{j-n_{k}+1}\cdots\lambda_{j}|^{p^{*}}\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ and}\\
&\gamma_{j}\cdots\gamma_{j+n_{k}-1}|\lambda_{j+1}\cdots\lambda_{j+n_{k}}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\text{ if $X=\ell^{p}(V)$, $1<p<\infty$, or $X=c_{0}(V)$};

where
p^{\ast}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

(b)
The operator
B_{\lambda}
is mixing on
X
if and only if, as
n\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{0}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|\rightarrow\infty\quad

if
X=\ell^{1}(V)
;

\displaystyle\left.\begin{aligned} &\min\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{-n}\cdots\gamma_{-1}},|\lambda_{-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{0}|^{p^{*}}\right)\rightarrow 0\text{ and}\\
&\gamma_{0}\cdots\gamma_{n-1}|\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}|^{p^{*}}\rightarrow\infty\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\text{ if $X=\ell^{p}(V)$, $1<p<\infty$, or $X=c_{0}(V)$};

where
p^{\ast}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

Again, the symmetry of the directed tree is not needed if
X=\ell^{1}(V)
.

We next have an interesting consequence of Theorem
5.3
for general weighted shifts; an analogous result holds for the mixing property.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed tree and
\lambda
a weight. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and suppose that the weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
is a bounded operator on
X
.

(a)
Suppose that there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)\to 0

and

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|\to\infty,

if
X=\ell^{1}(V)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\to\infty,

if
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|\to\infty,

if
X=c_{0}(V)
,

as
k\to\infty
, then
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.

(b)
If
(V,E)
has a free left end, then the above conditions characterize when
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.

Proof.

Part (a) is obvious. As for (b), we only consider the case when
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
. Let
v\in V
. Assuming the second condition in Theorem
5.3
(iii) we need to show that

(5.9)

\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\rightarrow v)\rightarrow 0

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
The generation of
v
has a common ancestor, that is, there is some
w\in V
and some
N\geq 0
such that, for all
u\in V
with
u\sim v
,

w=\prt^{N}(u),

and we have for all
n\geq N
that

\Chi^{n}(\prt^{n}(v))=\Chi^{N}(w).

We then deduce with (
4.2
) that, for all
k
with
n_{k}\geq N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(\prt^{n_{k}}(v))}|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(u)\to u)|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}-N}(w)\to w)|^{p^{*}}\sum_{u\in\Chi^{N}(w)}|\lambda(w\to u)|^{p^{*}}

\displaystyle=|\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)|^{p^{*}}\frac{\sum_{u\in\Chi^{N}(w)}|\lambda(w\to u)|^{p^{*}}}{|\lambda(w\to v)|^{p^{*}}}.

This, together with the second condition in Theorem
5.3
(iii), implies (
5.9
).
∎

We remark that assertion (b) in the corollary does not hold without the assumption that
(V,E)
has a free left end. Indeed, by the discussion following Corollary
5.4
, the unweighted backward shift
B
is hypercyclic on
c_{0}(V)
, say, for the unrooted directed tree in which each vertex has exactly two children, while the first condition in (a) is not satisfied.

Finally, Corollary
5.6
and the proof of Corollary
4.9
imply the following sufficient condition for hypercyclicity on particular unrooted trees; an analogous result holds for the mixing property. Recall the definition of
\Lambda_{v,n}
given in (
4.3
).

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed tree and
\lambda
a weight. Let
B_{\lambda}
be a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Suppose that every vertex has at most finitely many children. If there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k}
of positive integers such that, for each
v\in V
,

\lambda(\prt^{n_{k}}(v)\to v)\to 0

and

\frac{1}{|\Chi^{n_{k}}(v)|^{1/p}}\Lambda_{v,n_{k}}\to\infty

as
k\to\infty
, then
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.

Note that Martínez-Avendaño
[
26
, Theorem 6.1]
obtains yet another sufficient condition, see also Remark
4.11
.

6.
Existence of hypercyclic weighted shifts

We finally address the question mentioned at the end of Section
4
: Given any (rooted or unrooted) directed tree, does it support a hypercyclic or even mixing weighted backward shift? Of course, the tree has to be leafless, see Remark
4.1
, but this is the only geometric restriction.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(V,E)
be a leafless directed tree. Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
.

(a)
There exists a mixing weighted backward shift on
X
.

(b)
There exists a hypercyclic non-mixing weighted backward shift on
X
.

Proof.

We will do the proof for the space
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, on an unrooted leafless directed tree since the remaining cases are simpler. Fix a vertex
v_{0}\in V
and enumerate the generations with respect to
v_{0}
.

(a) For any vertex
v\in\text{Gen}_{n}
,
n\leq-1
, we choose weights
\lambda_{u}
,
u\in\Chi(v)
, such that
\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. On the other hand, for any vertex
v\in\text{Gen}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, we fix a child
u_{v}
of
v
and put
\lambda_{u_{v}}=2
, while on the remainder of
\Chi(v)
we choose weights such that
\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq 3^{p^{*}}
. Then
B_{\lambda}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(V)
by Proposition
2.3
, and it is easily seen by the mixing analogue of Corollary
5.6
that
B_{\lambda}
is mixing; the crucial point is that, starting from any
v\in V
, one can choose a branch going to infinity whose weights eventually all have the value 2.

(b) We start as in (a), which gives us
\lambda_{u}
for any
u\in\text{Gen}_{n}
,
n\leq 0
, such that

(6.1)

\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq\frac{1}{2},\quad v\in\bigcup_{n\leq-1}\text{Gen}_{n}.

We construct the remaining weights recursively. For this, fix an enumeration
v_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, of
V\setminus\{v_{0}\}
. Let
N_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
v_{n}\in\text{Gen}_{N_{n}}
for
n\geq 0
; note that
N_{0}=0
.

We now claim that there are sequences
(m_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and
(n_{k})_{k\geq 0}
of non-negative integers and weights
\lambda_{v}
,
v\in\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\text{Gen}_{n}
, such that, for any
k\geq 1
,

(6.2)

m_{k}>n_{k-1}+\max(N_{0},N_{1},\ldots,N_{k}),

(6.3)

n_{k}>m_{k},

(6.4)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{m_{k}}(v_{0})}|\lambda(v_{0}\to u)|^{p^{*}}\leq 1,

(6.5)

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k}}(v_{j})}|\lambda(v_{j}\to u)|^{p^{*}}>2^{k},j=0,\ldots,k;

moreover, for any
v\in\bigcup_{0\leq n<r_{k}}\text{Gen}_{n}
,
k\geq 0
,

(6.6)

\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq 3^{p^{*}},

where
r_{k}:=n_{k}+\max(N_{0},N_{1},\ldots,N_{k})
.

Let us first show that this implies the claim. Since
r_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
,
\lambda_{v}
is defined for all
v\in V
, and (
6.1
) and (
6.6
) imply that the weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(V)
. Now, it follows from (
6.1
) and (
6.5
) with Corollary
5.6
that
B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic, and (
6.4
) shows with Theorem
5.3
that
B_{\lambda}
is not mixing.

Thus it suffices to show recursively that (
6.2
)-(
6.6
) may be satisfied for all
k\geq 0
. For
k=0
, simply take
m_{0}=n_{0}=0
; note that
r_{0}=0
.

Now assume that
m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
,
n_{0},\ldots,n_{k}
, and
\lambda_{u}
,
u\in\bigcup_{0<n\leq r_{k}}\text{Gen}_{n}
have been defined for some
k\geq 0
so that (
6.2
)-(
6.6
) hold.

We choose

m_{k+1}>n_{k}+\max(N_{0},N_{1},\ldots,N_{k+1}),

hence
m_{k+1}>r_{k}
, and we define the weights on
\bigcup_{r_{k}<n\leq m_{k+1}}\text{Gen}_{n}
in such a way that

\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq 3^{p^{*}},\quad v\in\bigcup_{r_{k}\leq n<m_{k+1}}\text{Gen}_{n}

and

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{m_{k+1}}(v_{0})}|\lambda(v_{0}\to u)|^{p^{*}}\leq 1.

Let

(6.7)

\alpha_{j}:=\sum_{u\in\Chi^{m_{k+1}-N_{j}}(v_{j})}|\lambda(v_{j}\to u)|^{p^{*}},\quad j=0,\ldots,k+1;

note that
m_{k+1}-N_{j}>0
and
\Chi^{m_{k+1}-N_{j}}(v_{j})\subset\text{Gen}_{m_{k+1}}
, so the right-hand side is well defined. Then choose

n_{k+1}>m_{k+1}

such that

(6.8)

2^{(n_{k+1}-m_{k+1}+N_{j})p^{*}}\alpha_{j}>2^{k+1},\quad j=0,\ldots,k+1.

Write

s_{k+1}=r_{k+1}-m_{k+1}=n_{k+1}-m_{k+1}+\max(N_{0},N_{1},\ldots,N_{k+1})>0.

Now consider
u\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{k+1}\Chi^{m_{k+1}-N_{j}}(v_{j})\subset\text{Gen}_{m_{k+1}}
. It is not excluded that
u
appears here as a descendant of several
v_{j}
,
0\leq j\leq k+1
. Then fix a lineage
u_{1}\in\Chi(u),u_{2}\in\Chi(u_{1}),\ldots,u_{s_{k+1}}\in\Chi(u_{s_{k+1}-1})
and define
\lambda_{u_{l}}=2
for
l=1,\ldots,s_{k+1}
. Note that
u_{s_{k+1}}\in\text{Gen}_{r_{k+1}}
.

It follows from our construction that any
v\in\bigcup_{m_{k+1}\leq n<r_{k+1}}\text{Gen}_{n}
has at most one child for which we have defined the weight to be 2. Thus we can now choose the undefined weights in
\bigcup_{m_{k+1}<n\leq r_{k+1}}\text{Gen}_{n}
in such a way that, for all
v\in\bigcup_{m_{k+1}\leq n<r_{k+1}}\text{Gen}_{n}
,

\sum_{u\in\Chi(v)}|\lambda_{u}|^{p^{*}}\leq 3^{p^{*}}.

It follows from (
6.7
), (
6.8
) with (
4.2
) and the fact that
s_{k+1}\geq n_{k+1}-m_{k+1}+N_{j}
for
j=0,\ldots,k+1
that

\sum_{u\in\Chi^{n_{k+1}}(v_{j})}|\lambda(v_{j}\to u)|^{p^{*}}>2^{k+1},\quad j=0,\ldots,k+1.

Altogether we have shown that (
6.2
)-(
6.6
) are satisfied for
k+1
.

This finishes the proof.

∎

7.
Outlook

Of course, this is not the end of a story but, as we hope, only a beginning. In future work we intend to study the question of chaos for the operators considered in this paper. Another, and in view of the results in
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
21
]
probably rather technical, problem is that of characterizing frequent hypercyclicity. Let us repeat our hope alluded to in the introduction that weighted shifts on directed trees might provide new and easier counter-examples in linear dynamics; the additional flexibility provided by the geometry of the tree should turn out to be helpful.

Martínez-Avendaño and Rivera-Guasco
[
27
]
have already looked at the hypercyclicity of the backward shift on Lipschitz spaces of directed trees. More generally, one would want a description of the dynamics of weighted backward shifts on very general Banach or Fréchet spaces over a directed tree.

Let us finish with a specific problem. It is yet another well-known result of Salas
[
34
]
that for any unilateral weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
, the operator
I+B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic. This was generalized by Grivaux and Shkarin; in particular, if
T
is an operator on a separable Banach space
X
for which
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}(\text{ker}(T^{n})\cap\text{ran}(T^{n}))
is dense in
X
, then
I+T
is mixing, see
[
6
, Section 2.1]
. Since, for weighted backward shifts on rooted directed trees, the latter set contains all sequences of finite support, we have the following.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
(V,E)
be a rooted directed tree. Then, for any weighted backward shift
B_{\lambda}
on any of the spaces
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, the operator
I+B_{\lambda}
is mixing.

On the other hand, the orbits of the operator
I+2B
on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
are increasing in norm, so that the operator cannot be hypercyclic, while Salas
[
35
]
has found some perturbations of the identity by bilateral weighted shifts that are hypercyclic.

Problem 7.2
.

(a) (Salas
[
35
]
) Characterize the weighted backward shifts
B_{\lambda}
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
I+B_{\lambda}
is hypercyclic.

(b) Do the same for arbitrary unrooted directed trees.

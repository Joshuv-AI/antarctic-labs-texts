Simple limit linear series for curves of compact type


Abstract
We study simple Osserman limit linear series (that is, Osserman limit linear series having a simple basis) on curves of compact type with three irreducible components. For compact type curves with two components, every exact limit linear series is simple. But, for the case of three components, this property is no longer true. We study a certain distributivity property related with the existence of a simple basis and we find characterizations of that property at any fixed multidegree. Also, we find a characterization of simple limit linear series among the exact limit linear series. In our approach, we also study other structure similar to a simple basis and we get a certain inequality for any exact limit linear series.

Introduction

For curves of compact type, Eisenbud and Harris [3] developed the theory of limit linear series as an analogue of linear series. This theory is very powerful for degeneration arguments on curves. The idea is to analyze how linear series degenerate when a family of smooth curves degenerates to a compact type curve. Eisenbud and Harris approached this situation by considering only the possible limit line bundles with nonnegative multidegree and degree
d
on one irreducible component of the curve. Osserman [7] developed a new and more functorial construction for the theory of limit linear series. The basic idea is to consider all possible limit line bundles with nonnegative multidegree. Thus, Osserman limit linear series carry more information about limit line bundles. This new theory has a generalization to higher rank vector bundles Osserman [9].

Esteves and Osserman [4] related limit linear series to fibers of Abel maps via the definition of limit linear series by Osserman [7]. They restricted their work to the case of curves of compact type with two irreducible components and studied exact limit linear series (these contain in particular all limits of linear series on the generic fiber in a regular smoothing family). For any exact limit linear series, they establihed the existence of a simple basis (a certain structure of multidegrees and linearly independent sections), which was a key property in their work. For instance, the simple basis was useful to compute the Hilbert polynomial of certain closed subscheme of a fiber of an Abel map.

For curves of compact type with three irreducible components, Muñoz [5] generalized some of the results in Esteves and Osserman [4] for the special case of exact limit linear series arising as the unique exact extension of a refined Eisenbud-Harris limit linear series. Also, for any exact limit linear series satisfying a certain distributivity property, Muñoz [5] constructed a certain structure similar to a simple basis (Muñoz [5], Proposition 4.4). This structure played a similar role to that of the simple basis in Esteves and Osserman [4], so such a structure was enough for that work. However, we expected that such a structure could be used to construct a simple basis. In the present work, we do that construction (see Proposition
3.23
). In a different context, Dos Santos [2] defines a similar property to the distributivity property (there called the intersection property). He also related that property with the existence of a simple basis.

Our aim in this work is to study simple limit linear series (that is, limit linear series having a simple basis) on curves of compact type with three irreducible components. We find characterizations of the distributivity property at any fixed multidegree. Also, we find a characterization of simple limit linear series among the exact limit linear series. In our approach, we also get a certain inequality for any exact limit linear series.

\underline{d}^{1}=(\tilde{i}-1,d-(\tilde{i}-1)-\tilde{l},\tilde{l}),\ldots,\underline{d}^{\tilde{i}-i}=(i,d-i-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})

\underline{d}^{\tilde{i}-i+1}=(i,d-i-(\tilde{l}+1),\tilde{l}+1),\ldots,\underline{d}^{\tilde{i}-i+l-\tilde{l}}=(i,d-i-l,l)
.

If
\tilde{i}<i
,
\tilde{l}<l
and
i-\tilde{i}<l-\tilde{l}
, we can consider the diagonal path connecting the multidegree
\underline{d}^{0}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})
to
(i,d-i-(\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i}),\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i})
followed by the vertical path connecting
(i,d-i-(\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i}),\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i})
to
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)
, that is

\underline{d}^{1}=(\tilde{i}+1,d-(\tilde{i}+1)-(\tilde{l}+1),\tilde{l}+1),\ldots,\underline{d}^{i-\tilde{i}}=(i,d-i-(\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i}),\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i})
,

\underline{d}^{i-\tilde{i}+1}=(i,d-i-(\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i}+1),\tilde{l}+i-\tilde{i}+1),\ldots,\underline{d}^{l-\tilde{l}}=(i,d-i-l,l)

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\underline{d}^{0}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\geq\underline{0}
and
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)\geq\underline{0}

obtained from sequences of nonnegative multidegrees

of total degree

forming a path connecting
\underline{d}^{0}
to
\underline{d}
and not satisfying any of the conditions (I), (II) and (III) are equal because of the property (3) of the maps

Definition 3.3

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\{(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}},V_{\underline{d}})\}_{\underline{d}}
be a simple limit linear series. Then it is exact.

Proof.
We will first see how the horizontal exactness and the vertical exactness imply the diagonal exactness. In fact, for
i\geq 1
and
l\geq 1
such that
i+l\leq d
, let
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)
,
\underline{d}^{\prime}=(i-1,d-i-l+2,l-1)
and
\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}=(i-1,d-i-l+1,l)
. Suppose
\varphi_{\underline{d},\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}})=V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}^{X_{1},0}
and
\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}})=V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{3},0}
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{\underline{d},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}})=

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d},\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}})(V_{\underline{d}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{\underline{d},\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}^{X_{1},0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}))^{X_{1},0},

where in the last equality we used Remark
2.2
. Thus

\varphi_{\underline{d},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}})=(\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime},\underline{d}^{\prime}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}))^{X_{1},0}=(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{3},0})^{X_{1},0}=V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{2}^{c},0}.

(1)

On the other hand,

\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{2}^{c},0}+\text{dim}\,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})=\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}=r+1
,

so

\text{dim}\,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})=r+1-\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{2}^{c},0}=\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}}-\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{2}^{c},0}=\text{dim}\,V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0},

where the last equality follows from (
1
). Since
\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})\subseteq V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}
, it follows that

\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})=V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}

(2)

Thus, the diagonal exactness follows from (
1
) and (
2
).

It remains to show the horizontal and vertical exactness. We will only prove the horizontal exactness, as the proof of the vertical exactness is analogous. For
i\geq 1
and
l\geq 0
such that
i+l\leq d
, let
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)
. Then
\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}=(i-1,d-i-l+1,l)\geq\underline{0}
.

Let
M
be the set of nonnegative multidegrees of total degree
d
. We define the following subsets of
M
:

D=\{\underline{d}_{0}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\in M\,/\,\,\tilde{l}\leq l\,\,\text{and}\,\,\tilde{i}-i\geq\tilde{l}-l\}
,

E=\{\underline{d}_{0}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\in M\,/\,\,\tilde{l}\geq l\,\,\text{and}\,\,\tilde{i}\geq i\}
and

F=\{\underline{d}_{0}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\in M\,/\,\,\tilde{i}\leq i-1\,\,\text{and}\,\,\tilde{i}-(i-1)\leq\tilde{l}-l\}

Notice that
(D\cup E)\cap F=\emptyset
and
M=(D\cup E)\cup F
.

Since
\{(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}},V_{\underline{d}})\}_{\underline{d}}
is simple, there exist multidegrees
\underline{d}^{1},\ldots,\underline{d}^{m}
and linearly independent sections
s^{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,s^{\alpha}_{r_{\alpha}}\in V_{\underline{d}^{\alpha}}
for each
\alpha
as in Definition
3.3
. We claim that

\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}=\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}},

(3)

for any
\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in F
. In fact, let
\underline{d}^{\alpha}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\in F
. If
\tilde{l}\geq l
, we can obtain
\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}
by considering the vertical path connecting
\underline{d}^{\alpha}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})
to
(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-l,l)
followed by the horizontal path connecting
(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-l,l)
to
\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}=(i-1,d-i-l+1,l)
. Analogously, we can obtain
\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}
by considering the vertical path connecting
\underline{d}^{\alpha}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})
to
(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-l,l)
followed by the horizontal path connecting
(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-l,l)
to
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)
. Since
\tilde{i}\leq i-1
, we conclude that

\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}=\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}
.

If
\tilde{l}<l
, we can obtain
\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}
by considering the diagonal path connecting the multidegree
\underline{d}^{\alpha}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})
to
(\tilde{i}+l-\tilde{l},d-(\tilde{i}+l-\tilde{l})-l,l)

to

(\tilde{i}+l-\tilde{l},d-(\tilde{i}+l-\tilde{l})-l,l)
to

. Since

\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}=\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}
.

This proves the claim (
3
).

Now, we claim that

\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}=\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}},

(4)

for any
\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in D\cup E
. In fact, let
\underline{d}^{\alpha}=(\tilde{i},d-\tilde{i}-\tilde{l},\tilde{l})\in D\cup E

4

3

\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in D

) for the first case.

If

.

4
).

On the other hand, we have that

B_{\underline{d}}:=\{\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\underline{d}}(s^{1}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\underline{d}}(s^{1}_{r_{1}}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\underline{d}}(s^{m}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\underline{d}}(s^{m}_{r_{m}})\}

is a basis of
V_{\underline{d}}
. Now, by the claim (
3
), for each
\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in F
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}=

\displaystyle\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\circ(\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\circ\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}})\circ\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

as
\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\circ\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}=0
. Then, we have

B_{\underline{d},F}:=\{\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}(s^{\alpha}_{z})\,/\,\,\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in F\,\text{and}\,z=1,\ldots,r_{\alpha}\}\subseteq\text{Ker}\,(\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}:V_{\underline{d}}\rightarrow V_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}).

(5)

On the other hand, we have that

B_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}:=\{\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}(s^{1}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}(s^{1}_{r_{1}}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}(s^{m}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}(s^{m}_{r_{m}})\}

4
), this means that

\{\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}(\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}(s^{\alpha}_{z}))\,/\,\,\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in D\cup E\,\text{and}\,z=1,\ldots,r_{\alpha}\}
is linearly independent,

and hence

\left\langle B_{\underline{d},D\cup E}\right\rangle\cap\text{Ker}\,(\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}:V_{\underline{d}}\rightarrow V_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}})=0,

(6)

where
B_{\underline{d},D\cup E}:=\{\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}}(s^{\alpha}_{z})\,/\,\,\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in D\cup E\,\text{and}\,z=1,\ldots,r_{\alpha}\}
. Also, notice that the basis
B_{\underline{d}}
de

can be written as

(7)

.

\text{Ker}\,(\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1},\underline{d}}:V_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}\rightarrow V_{\underline{d}})=\text{Im}\,(\varphi_{\underline{d},\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}}:V_{\underline{d}}\rightarrow V_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}_{1}})

Proposition 3.5
.

For any
i\geq 0
and
l\geq 0
such that

, let

and
l>0
, let
\underline{d}^{\prime}:=(i-1,d-i-l+2,l-1)
. Let
V_{\underline{d}}
and

be
r+1
-dimensional subspaces of

and

r+1\,-

(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{1},0}+V_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}^{X_{3},0})=
dim
V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}\,-

(V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{3}^{c},0}\oplus V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{1}^{c},0})

i>0
, let
\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}:=(i-1,d-i-l+1,l)

and
V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}
be
r+1
-dimensional subspaces of
H^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}})
and
H^{0}(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}})
, respectively, such that

dim
(V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}+V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{3},0})=
dim
V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}^{X_{1},0}\,-

(V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}^{X_{3}^{c},0}\oplus V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime}}^{X_{2}^{c},0})

, let

.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
V_{\underline{d}}
and
V_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}}

as

Using the three previous results, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
\{(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}},V_{\underline{d}})\}_{\underline{d}}
be an exact limit linear series, and suppose that the distributivity holds in

\underline{d}

\{(\mathcal{L}_{\underline{d}},V_{\underline{d}})\}_{\underline{d}}

Proof.
Write

,

and for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,m
, let

V_{\underline{d}^{\alpha}}=(V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{1},0}+V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}+V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{3},0})\oplus\left\langle s^{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,s^{\alpha}_{r_{\alpha}}\right\rangle
.

It follows from Corollary
3.17
that

r_{1}+\ldots+r_{m}=r+1.

(86)

Now, we claim that

V_{\underline{d}}=\left\langle\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\underline{d}}(s^{1}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{1},\underline{d}}(s^{1}_{r_{1}}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\underline{d}}(s^{m}_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{m},\underline{d}}(s^{m}_{r_{m}})\right\rangle,

(87)

for any
\underline{d}=(i,d-i-l,l)
. In fact, as in the statement of Proposition
3.21
, there are six cases to consider. We will only prove the claim for
i\geq 1
,
l\geq 1
and
i+l\leq d-1
, as the remaining cases are analogous. In this case, by Proposition
3.21
, item 4, we get

\displaystyle V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{1},0}+V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{2},0}+V_{\underline{d}}^{X_{3},0}=

\displaystyle\,\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(W^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})+\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime},\underline{d}}(W^{3}_{\underline{d}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle+\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime},\underline{d}}(W^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}})+\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime},\underline{d}}(W^{2}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}})+\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}},\underline{d}}(W^{2}_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}})+\varphi_{\widetilde{\underline{d}},\underline{d}}(W^{3}_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}}),

(88)

where
\widetilde{\underline{d}}:=(i+1,d-i-l-1,l)
. Also, it is easily seen that

and
M^{3}_{\underline{d}}=(M^{3}_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}\cup M^{3}_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}})\cup\{\underline{d}\}
,

that is,

M^{1}_{\underline{d}}\setminus\{\underline{d}\}=M^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}\cup M^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}},

(89)

M^{2}_{\underline{d}}\setminus\{\underline{d}\}=M^{2}_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}}\cup M^{2}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}}

(90)

\text{and}\,\,M^{3}_{\underline{d}}\setminus\{\underline{d}\}=M^{3}_{\underline{d}^{\prime}}\cup M^{3}_{\widetilde{\underline{d}}}.

(91)

\text{and}\,\,\beta^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}}=\{\varphi_{\underline{d}^{\alpha},\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(s^{\alpha}_{z})\,/\,\,\underline{d}^{\alpha}\in M^{1}_{\underline{d}^{\prime\prime\prime}}\,\,\text{and}\,\,z=1,\ldots,r_{\alpha}\}.

Finally, we obtain the following characterization of simple limit linear series among the exact limit linear series.

Corollary 3.24

Proof.

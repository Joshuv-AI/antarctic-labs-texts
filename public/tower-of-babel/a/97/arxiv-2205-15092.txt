Feedback stabilization of a two-fluid surface tension system modeling the motion of a soap bubble at low Reynolds number: The two-dimensional case Thanks: This work is supported by…
Sébastien Court

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to design a feedback operator for stabilizing in infinite time horizon a system modeling the interactions between a viscous incompressible fluid and the deformation of a soap bubble. The latter is represented by an interface separating a bounded domain of ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} into two connected parts filled with viscous incompressible fluids. The interface is a smooth perturbation of the 1-sphere, and the surrounding fluids satisfy the incompressible Stokes equations in time-dependent domains. The mean curvature of the surface defines a surface tension force which induces a jump of the normal trace of the Cauchy stress tensor. The response of the fluids is a velocity trace on the interface, governing the time evolution of the latter, via the equality of velocities. The data are assumed to be sufficiently small, in particular the initial perturbation, that is the initial shape of the soap bubble is close enough to a circle. The control function is a surface tension type force on the interface. We design it as the sum of two feedback operators: one is explicit, the second one is finite-dimensional. They enable us to define a control operator that stabilizes locally the soap bubble to a circle with an arbitrary exponential decay rate, up to translations, and up to non-contact with the outer boundary.



Keywords:
Feedback stabilization, Incompressible viscous fluid, Surface tension, Free boundary problems.

76D55, 76D45, 93B52, 93D15, 35R35.

Introduction

In this paper we consider a model that describes the time evolution of an interface
\Gamma(t)
separating a bounded fluid domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
into two connected components. The fluid is assumed to be viscous and incompressible. This interface represents a soap bubble, and is subject to surface tension forces. Surface tension is the result of intermolecular forces in fluids (air or liquid, see
[
Pro
]
). We consider the low Reynolds number case, that is the inertia forces are assumed to be negligible compared to the other involved forces (viscosity effects, surface tension, electric field, etc…), which is typically the case of blood flows
[
Kun+09
]
containing globules
[
B.15
]
. We will also neglect the temperature effects. Our aim is to stabilize the deformations of this soap bubble such that this latter converges to a circle, with a prescribed exponential decay rate, via the design of a feedback operator acting on the interface. Our contribution falls within the modeling and mathematical aspects of the motion of bubbles out of equilibrium.

1.2
Main result

Note that the volume enclosed by
\Gamma(t)
is constant and prescribed by the one enclosed by the reference circle
\Gamma_{s}
, due to incompressibility. Since any circle of the same volume, strictly included inside
\Omega
, is a stationary state (see Lemma
2.1
), we can decide to stabilize system (
1
) around any of these circles, and thus stabilization
around the sphere
is understood up to elements of the following space

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}=\left\{X_{c}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})\text{ such that }X_{c}(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathring{\Omega}\text{ is a circle of the same radius as }\Gamma_{s}\right\}.

The initial configuration
\Gamma_{0}
is represented via the deformation
X_{0}
of
\Gamma_{s}
, so that
\Gamma_{0}=X_{0}(\Gamma_{s})
. Since we need smallness assumptions on the data, in particular
X_{0}
, in practice we can choose
X_{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
such that
\|X_{0}-X_{c}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}
is minimal, for example by restricting
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
to translations. We refer to Remark
2.1
in section
2.2
for further comments. This amounts to say that
\Gamma_{0}
is
close
to a circle of the same radius as
\Gamma_{s}
. The main result of the present article is Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Choose
X_{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. Let be
X_{0}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f^{\pm}=f_{|\Omega^{\pm}(t)}
such that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))
. For all
\lambda>0
, there exists a finite-dimensional linear operator
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
, depending only on
\lambda
,
\Gamma_{s}
,
\nu
and
\mu
, such that if the quantities
\|X_{0}-X_{c}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}
and
\|e^{\lambda t}f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega))}
are small enough, then the solution of system (
1
) with

g=\left(r(\mathrm{det}\mathfrak{g})^{-1/2}\left(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\big((\tau_{s}\otimes\tau_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-X_{c})\big)+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-X_{c})\right)\right)\circ X^{-1}

satisfies

\left\|e^{\lambda t}(X-X_{c})\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,\infty;\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|X_{0}-X_{c}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},

where
r
denotes the radius of the circle
\Gamma_{s}
,
\tau_{s}
denotes the tangent vector of
\Gamma_{s}
,
C>0
is a constant, and
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the metric tensor of
\Gamma(t)
.

This result implies the asymptotic convergence towards a stationary state corresponding to an immobile circle, up to a translation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Note that in the choice of

, we need two different operators, one explicit, namely
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\big((\tau_{s}\otimes\tau_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-X_{c})\big)

. This feedback stabilization result is in line with many others obtained for other fluid-structure models, for example
[
Ray10
,
Cou14
,
Cou14a
]
. But as far as we know, there are very few mathematical contributions that address control-related questions for models involving surface tension forces: The models considered in
[

,

,

,

,
CGS21
]
deal with free boundary problems, that do not involve jump conditions like in (

1.3
Method

Like in
[
Ray10
,
Cou14
,
Cou14a
]
, our method is based on the feedback stabilization of the linearized system. For deriving the latter, we first need to rewrite system (
1
) in cylindrical domains, in order to uncouple the fluid domains and the state variables, in particular the deformation
X
. Since this surface deformation is initially defined on
\Gamma_{s}
only, we need to define a suitable extension to the whole domain, which leads us to study the non-trivial question of extension of diffeomorphisms from boundaries. This is realized in section
3.1
, and the corresponding proof relies on recent results on the harmonic extensions of diffeomorphisms (see Appendix
A.1
).

Concerning the model, for the sake of simplicity, in the fluid domain we choose to consider the linear stationary Stokes system, which corresponds to a low Reynolds number fluid. Considering fluid models at average or high Reynolds number would introduce other difficulties. For example, in
[
CS07
,
CHS13
]
the authors consider the Euler system, and existence of solutions is obtained with high-order energy estimates, without any operator formulation that could be used for designing a possible feedback operator. On the other hand, addressing the Navier-Stokes system consists classically in deriving an operator formulation of the linearized system, involving the non-stationary Stokes system, for which a
lifting method
is used. For the present model, based on jump conditions, it is not clear how a lifting method would enable us to derive an operator formulation. Still in the case of the non-stationary Stokes equations with transmission conditions, the respective authors of
[
PS10
]
and
[
Den94
]
first reduced the interface to a straight line, and derived existence and uniqueness results via pseudo-differential calculus techniques, which lead to a space-time operator for describing the solution. In practice, such approaches can not be used in a infinite-horizon stabilization problem, moreover dealing with general surfaces. Further, the unique continuation argument used in our case for obtaining approximate controllability and stabilizability of the linearized system does not apply to the case of the non-stationary Stokes system, as the latter would necessitate taking into consideration zeros of spherical harmonics, leading to difficulties that go beyond the scope of this article.

Therefore we adopt the stationary Stokes equations, as we prefer to focus on the time-evolution of the interface displacement, more specifically an operator formulation that involves the time derivative of the interface displacement only. For the linearized system, involving the Stokes system in time-independent domains, we describe the solution via a stationary Poincaré-Steklov operator denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, of Neumann-to-Dirichlet type, mapping the different transmission conditions. The existence of this operator is obtained via the Ladyzhenskaya-Babuška-Brezzi condition. Next the operator formulation for time-evolution of the interface displacement is obtained, involving
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, as follows

\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\mu\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}G\text{ on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,\infty),\quad Z(\cdot,0)=X_{0}-\mathrm{Id}\text{ on }\Gamma_{s},

where
Z=X-\mathrm{Id}
represents the displacement of the interface,
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}:=(n_{s}\otimes n_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
, and
G
represents a control function to be chosen in a feedback form. Since
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
is not coercive (see section
2.3
), we define a first feedback operator as
\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{\tau_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
, where
\nabla^{\tau_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z:=(\tau_{s}\otimes\tau_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
, so that
Z\mapsto-\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z-\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{\tau_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=-\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
is coercive. This operator is explicit, and supported on the tangent to
\Gamma_{s}
, which is compatible with possible practical realization. The resulting operator generates an analytic semi-group of contraction, with compact resolvent. Next we prove approximate controllability for the linear system via a unique continuation argument. Since the spectrum of the operator is discrete, the number of unstable modes is of finite number, and thus we can reduce the problem to a finite-dimensional control problem where approximate controllability implies stailizability by a finite-dimensional feedback operator satisfying a Riccati equation. This feedback operator is re-used for defining another control function that stabilizes locally the nonlinear system, via a fixed-point argument, provided that the perturbations of the steady state are sufficiently small.

One of the main reasons why we restricted our study to dimension 2 is due to theoretical difficulties that arise in dimension 3 when trying to extend diffeomorphisms. Another reason lies in the fact that the linear evolution equation above, involving the operator
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, would be
a priori
more complex in dimension 3. All these reasons are explained in section
7
. Nevertheless, the methodology adopted in the present article for designing in practice the feedback operator is still valid in dimension 3. Throughout the paper we try to keep as much as possible a formalism that is not restricted to dimension 2.

The paper is organized as follows: Notation and functional spaces are defined in section
2
. Comments and important properties of the model are described in section
2.2
. A change of variable is introduced in section
3.1
, enabling us to rewrite in section
3.2
the main system in time-independent domains. Section
4
is devoted to the study of the corresponding linearized system, where in particular in section
4.1
we define operator
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, leading in section
4.2
to an operator formulation. In section
5
we design a linear feedback operator that stabilizes the linear system. In section
6
we deduce another feedback operator that stabilizes the nonlinear system, and thus prove the main result. Questions related with the extension to dimension 3 of the present work are posed in section
7
. Finally, technical proofs of intermediate results are given in the Appendix.

2.2
On the surface tension model and the stationary states

The surface tension force is generated by the mean curvature vector of the surface
\Gamma(t)
. It is related to the Laplace-Beltrami operator via the following relation (see
[
Wil93
, p. 151, Exercise 2]
):

\kappa n=\Delta_{\Gamma(t)}\mathrm{Id}.

Using
[
ADK19
, Theorem 2.6]
, the following energy estimate holds, showing that the surface tension force derives from a potential energy quantified by the area of
\Gamma(t)
:

\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\Gamma(t)|+2\nu\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{+}(t))}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{-}(t))}^{2}\right)=\langle f^{+},u^{+}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega^{+}(t))}+\langle f^{-},u^{-}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega^{-}(t))}+\langle g,u^{\pm}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma(t))}.

This energy is dissipated with the help of the viscosity terms. From
[
Tem83
, page 18]
, the kernel of
\varepsilon
is reduced to the tangent space of the special Euclidean group
SE(2)
, namely the functions of type
u^{\pm}(x)=h^{\pm}+\omega^{\pm}x^{\perp}
, where
h^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\omega^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
are constant. Using
u^{+}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we deduce
h^{+}=0
and
\omega^{+}=0
, which also implies
h^{-}=0
and
\omega^{-}=0
when we have
u^{+}=u^{-}
on
\Gamma(t)
. Therefore, the first Korn’s inequality combined with the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem and the Petree-Tartar lemma yields the following general result:

Lemma 2.0
.

Let
\Omega^{\pm}
be any smooth subdomains of
\Omega
split by a closed smooth curve
\Gamma=\partial\Omega^{-}
such that
\Gamma\subset\mathring{\Omega}
. Then, for all
u^{\pm}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{\pm})
such that
u^{+}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
u^{+}=u^{-}
on
\Gamma
, we have

\|u^{+}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{+})}+\|u^{-}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{-})}\leq C\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{+})}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{-})}^{2}\right),

where
C>0
is independent of
u^{\pm}
.

Since our approach is based on the study of the linearized system that involves
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{I})
(see section
3.2
), we will rather focus on the different differential operators rather than on the mean curvature. The curve
\Gamma_{s}
is considered as a Riemannian manifold, and due to its regularity, we claim that the trace spaces
\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Gamma_{s})
coincide with the definition of Sobolev spaces given on Riemannian manifolds
[
Heb99
, section 2.2]
. For a circle
\Gamma_{s}\subset\mathring{\Omega}
of given radius
r
we adopt the parameterization by arc length

X_{s}:[0,2\pi r)\ni\xi\mapsto\left(r\cos(\xi/r),r\sin(\xi/r)\right)^{T}\in\Gamma_{s}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Denoting
\tau_{s}
the tangent vector of
\Gamma_{s}
, we have

n_{s}\circ X_{s}=\left(\cos(\xi/r),\sin(\xi/r)\right)^{T},\quad\tau_{s}\circ X_{s}=\left(\sin(\xi/r),-\cos(\xi/r)\right)^{T}

Recall the notation for the tangential gradient
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
and the tangential divergence
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}
. In the particular case of a circle, with the parameterization
X_{s}
chosen above, the metric tensor
\mathfrak{g}_{s}
of
\Gamma_{s}
is scalar-valued, equal to
|\nabla_{\xi}X_{s}|_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2}=1
. Therefore, for all
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
, these operators simply write

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\varphi)\circ X_{s}&=&\nabla_{\xi}(\varphi\circ X_{s}),\\
(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}})\circ X_{s}&=&\displaystyle(\mathrm{det}\mathfrak{g}_{s})^{-1/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\left((\mathrm{det}\mathfrak{g}_{s})^{1/2}(\varphi\circ X_{s})\right)=\nabla_{\xi}(\varphi\circ X_{s})=(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\varphi)\circ X_{s}.\end{array}

We will still use the general notation
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
and
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}
, for the sake of consistency with higher dimension. Recall the Frenet-Serret formulas:

\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}n_{s}=-\frac{1}{r}\tau_{s},\quad\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\tau_{s}=\frac{1}{r}n_{s}.

The integrals on
\Gamma_{s}
have to be understood as surface integrals. We recall the Stokes formula on smooth manifolds without boundary, that we will use in this article only for the 1-sphere
\Gamma_{s}
. For all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})
, we have

\langle\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{s}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})}=-\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\varphi,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\psi\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},

where we recall the definition
\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}=\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\circ\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma_{s}
. More generally, for all matrix field
\Sigma\in\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
and vector field
\varphi\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
we have

\langle\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\Sigma,\varphi\rangle_{\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{s}),\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})}=-\langle\Sigma,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\varphi\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(2)

We define
admissible
deformations, summarizing the basic assumptions we consider for mappings
X
, as well as the set of admissible deformations transforming the circle
\Gamma_{s}
into another circle of the same radius and orientation:

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
X\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
is admissible if
X
is invertible, orientation-preserving and volume-preserving, that is that the volume contained by
\Gamma_{s}
is the same as the one contained by
X(\Gamma_{s})
, and if
X(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathring{\Omega}
. Further, we define

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}=\left\{X\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})\mid\ X(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathring{\Omega}\text{ is a circle of the same radius as }\Gamma_{s}\right\}.

The relation
X(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathring{\Omega}
is the condition of non-contact with the outer boundary, that is
X(\Gamma_{s})\cap\partial\Omega=\emptyset
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that any volume-preserving deformation
X\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
that is close enough to the identity and volume-preserving is admissible. Relaxing the non-contact condition, remark that the space
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
contains elements of the special Euclidean group
SE(2)
, made of proper rigid transformations
X_{R}
, namely direct isometries, composed of translations and rotations, as
X_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\ni y\mapsto h+\mathbf{R}y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
h\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbf{R}
is an orthogonal matrix with
\mathrm{det}\mathbf{R}=1
. The space
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
also includes the group
\mathrm{Diff}^{+}(\Gamma_{s})
of direct diffeomorphisms of the circle
\Gamma_{s}
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
can be generated by composing elements of
SE(2)
with elements of
\mathrm{Diff}(\Gamma_{s})
. Finally, we note that we can obtain the circle
X(\Gamma_{s})
globally – as geometric object – from
\Gamma_{s}
simply by composing the latter by a translation of
SE(2)
. But for the sake of completeness we introduce
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
as above for describing all the possible stationary states.

We derive the three following lemmas, that will be used several times throughout the paper. The first one characterizes the stationary states, given by system (
4
):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let be subdomains
\Omega^{\pm}\subset\Omega
like in Lemma
2.0
. If
g\in\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
is the right-hand-side of the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega^{\pm},\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=g&&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{array}\right.

(3)

then necessarily
g=cn
, where
c
is a constant equal to the difference
[p]
of constant pressures. In particular, the admissible mappings
X
such that
X(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathring{\Omega}
splits
\Omega
into two subdomains denoted by
\Omega_{X(\Gamma_{s})}^{\pm}=\Omega^{\pm}
, like in Lemma
2.0
, and satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{X(\Gamma_{s})}^{\pm},\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\Delta_{X(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{Id}&&\text{on }X(\Gamma_{s}),\end{array}\right.

(4)

describe the set
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. That is,
X(\Gamma_{s})
is a circle of the same radius
r>0
as
\Gamma_{s}
. The velocities
u^{\pm}
are equal to zero everywhere, and the pressures
p^{\pm}
are constant, equal to the static pressures
p_{s}^{\pm}
such that
[p_{s}]n_{s}=\mu\Delta_{X(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{Id}=\kappa_{s}n_{s}
(namely the so-called Young-Laplace equation), where
\kappa_{s}=-1/r<0
is the curvature of
X(\Gamma_{s})
, implying that
p_{s}^{-}>p_{s}^{+}
.

Proof.

Taking the scalar product of the first equation of (
3
), and integrating by parts leads to
\|\varepsilon(u^{\pm})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{X(\Gamma_{s})}^{\pm})}=0
, and from Lemma
2.0
, to
u^{\pm}=0
in
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega^{\pm})
. Then we also deduce
\nabla p^{\pm}=0
in the first equation, that yields that
p^{\pm}
are both constant, equal to the static pressures
p_{s}^{\pm}
. Thus
g=\left[2\nu\varepsilon(u)-p\mathrm{I}\right]n=-\left[p_{s}\right]n
, where
\left[p_{s}\right]
is a constant, which completes the first part of the proof. Next, using this result for system (
4
) with
\Omega^{\pm}=\Omega_{X(\Gamma_{s})}^{\pm}
,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{X(\Gamma_{s})}
and
g=\mu\Delta_{X(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{Id}
, the constant
\left[p\right]
obtained previously corresponds to
\left[p\right]n=\mu\Delta_{X(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{Id}
. Actually
\mu\Delta_{X(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{Id}=\kappa n
, where
\kappa
, namely the (mean) curvature of
X(\Gamma_{s})
, is constant, equal to
\kappa_{s}=-1/r
. Therefore
X(\Gamma_{s})
is a circle, and since
X
is assumed to be
admissible
, this condition yields that
X(\Gamma_{s})
and
\Gamma_{s}
have the same radius
r
, and so the same mean curvature
-1/r
. Therefore
X
lies necessarily in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, and conversely, which completes the proof.∎

The stationary states are then obtained from the reference circle
\Gamma_{s}
via transformations of
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. The deformations
X
of
\Gamma_{s}
will be then compared to an element
X_{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, and the corresponding displacements writes
X-X_{c}
. For the sake of simplicity we will rather consider
X-\mathrm{Id}
in what follows, by keeping in mind that
\mathrm{Id}
is to be replaced by any given
X_{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. From there, we will use the notation

Z=X-\mathrm{Id}

for the displacements, keeping in mind that ultimately we shall consider
Z=X-X_{c}
with
X_{c}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. Next we introduce the following differential operator

\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}=(n_{s}\otimes n_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}.

Operator
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
appears in the linearized system (
21
) (see section
3.2
for its derivation). Note that the matrix field
n_{s}\otimes n_{s}
is never invertible.

2.3
The kernel of
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
and the lack of coercivity

The operator
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
appears in the linearization of
(\kappa n)\circ X
(see section
3.2
). The description of the kernel of
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
is then central for the methodology we adopted, namely the wellposedness of the corresponding linearized system (section
4
), and the unique continuation argument (section
5.1
). Unfortunately there are non-trivial mappings
X=Z+\mathrm{Id}
that are smooth, orientation-preserving, volume-preserving, transforming the circle into a Jordan curve, that can be chosen arbitrarily close to the identity, and such that
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=0
.

Indeed,
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\cdot n_{s})n_{s}=0
implies that
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
is tangent to
\Gamma_{s}
. There exists a function
\xi\mapsto\alpha(\xi)
such that
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\alpha\tau_{s}
. Decompose
\alpha
with its Fourier series:

\alpha(\xi)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\big(a_{1,k}\cos(k\xi/r)+a_{2,k}\sin(k\xi/r)\big).

The modes
k=0
and
k=1
are not considered, as they introduce constants that are necessarily equal to
0
, because of the periodicity of
Z
, meaning that
(\mathrm{Id}+Z)(\Gamma_{s})
is a closed curve (
Z(X_{s}(0))=Z(X_{s}(2\pi r))
). Integrating the equality
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\alpha\tau_{s}
leads us to

Z(X_{s}(\xi))=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}-1}\Big(\big(a_{1,k}\cos(k\xi/r)+a_{2,k}\sin(k\xi/r)\big)n_{s}+\big(-a_{2,k}k\cos(k\xi/r)+a_{1,k}k\sin(k\xi/r)\big)\tau_{s}\Big),

up to a constant that corresponds to a translation. Several examples of such displacements are represented in Figure
2
below, corresponding to
k\in\{2,3,4,5,6,7\}
and coefficients
a_{k},b_{k}\in\{0,1\}
.

Figure 2:
Different deformations
X(\Gamma_{s})
of the circle such that
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id})=0
.

In order to define a linear operator whose the kernel is reduced to a trivial set, we need to regularize the operator
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
.

2.4
Regularization

Instead of considering
\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
alone, we add a regularizing term, namely

\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{\tau_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\circ\big((\tau_{s}\otimes\tau_{s})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\big),

so that the linear system studied in section
4
involves the following operator

Z\mapsto-\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z-\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla^{\tau_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=-\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=-\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z.

For the Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}
we recall a rigidity result combined with Gårding-type inequalities.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
Z\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Gamma_{s})
with
s\geq 1
satisfies
\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=0
. Then
Z
is a constant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},

(5)

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})},

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(6)

Furthermore, if
Z\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
, the following estimate holds:

\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(7)

Proof.

See for example
[
Heb99
, section 2.8]
. Estimate (
5
) is deduced from the Poincaré inequality combined with the Petree-tartar lemma. The same inequality applies to high-order derivatives of
Z
, and consequently estimates (
6
) are deduced by interpolation. Finally, estimate (
7
) is classically obtained by using Fourier series on the circle.
∎

We deduce a unique continuation result that is used for proving Proposition
5.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The mappings
X\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
satisfying the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm},\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id})&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s},\end{array}\right.

(8)

are translations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.1
with
\Omega^{\pm}=\Omega_{s}^{\pm}
,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{s}
and
g=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id})
, we deduce
\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id})=cn_{s}
, where
c
is a constant equal to the difference of constant pressures. Recall that the static pressures introduced in Lemma
2.1
satisfy
\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathrm{Id}=[p_{s}]n_{s}
. Therefore we can assume that the constant pressures mentioned above are such that
c=0
, and so
\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id})=0
. Since
\Gamma_{s}
is a compact manifold, it follows that
X-\mathrm{Id}
is a constant, and
X
is a translation.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In the proof of Lemma
2.2
, another argument for deducing
c=0
would have consisted in restricting the deformation
X
to volume-preserving deformations. Since
n_{s}=-r\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathrm{Id}
, we would have deduced
\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}(X-\mathrm{Id}+cr\mathrm{Id})=0
, implying that
X+(cr-1)\mathrm{Id}
is a constant. Assuming that
X
is volume-preserving then leads to
c=0
. This also amounts saying that the difference of static pressures remains the same after deformation by
X
.

The third lemma is an energy estimate that holds for an unsteady linear system:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let be
T>0
and
\lambda>0
. Assume that
Z
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u^{\pm}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+g&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\\
Z(\cdot,0)=Z_{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.\end{array}\right.

(9)

Then the following identity holds almost everywhere in
(0,T)
:

\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}+2\nu\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}^{2}\right)=\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}+\left\langle g;u^{\pm}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(10)

In particular, if
g=0
and
Z_{0}=0
in
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
, then
Z\equiv 0
up to a constant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

Taking the scalar product of the first equation of (
9
) by
u^{\pm}
, and integrating by parts yields

2\nu\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}^{2}\right)=\mu\left\langle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z,\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\right\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}+\left\langle g,u^{\pm}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

The Stokes formula (
2
) shows that
\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}^{\ast}=-\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
, so we deduce

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z,\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\right\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}&=&-\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\partial\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-\lambda\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z,\mu\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\right\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
&=&\displaystyle-\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|^{2}_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}+\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|^{2}_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},\end{array}

and thus (
10
) follows. By integrating in time this identity, with
g=0
, we obtain
\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{0}\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}=0
, which yields
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=0
, and Proposition
2.1
enables us to complete the proof.
∎

Note that the energy estimate (
10
) is also valid for any geometric configuration satisfying the non-contact condition, but we will use it only for
\Gamma_{s}
.

3
Extension of diffeomorphisms and change of variables

In order to rewrite (

3.1
Extension of diffeomorphisms

Let us define an extension of

Proposition 3.1
.

Let be

\tilde{X}

\tilde{X}(\cdot,t)_{|\Omega_{s}^{\pm}}\in\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})

,

is a diffeomorphism from
\Omega_{s}^{-}
onto

, and that satisfies

\|\tilde{X}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\leq C\|X-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})},

(12)

The
\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})

.
Note that the domains

Corollary 3.1
.

Given the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
, the inverse
\tilde{Y}(\cdot,t)
of mapping
\tilde{X}(\cdot,t)_{|\Omega_{s}^{\pm}}
, so that

\tilde{X}(\tilde{Y}(x,t),t)=x,\text{ for }x\in\Omega^{\pm}(t),\ t\in(0,\infty),\qquad\tilde{Y}(\tilde{X}(y,t),t)=y\text{ for }(y,t)\in\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,\infty),

satisfies the following estimate, provided that

is small enough:

\|\nabla\tilde{Y}(\tilde{X})-\mathrm{I}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm}))}\leq C\|X-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(13)

Denoting by
\tilde{Y}_{1}
and

the respective inverses of

and
\tilde{X}_{2}
, extensions of

and

respectively, we have

\|\nabla\tilde{Y}_{1}(\tilde{X}_{1})-\nabla\tilde{Y}_{2}(\tilde{X}_{2})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathbb{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm}))}\leq C\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})},

4.2
The semi-homogeneous system

Using the operator
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, we rewrite system (
21
) with
\lambda=0
as the following abstract evolution equation

\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}G\quad\text{ in }(0,T),\qquad Z(0)=Z_{0},

(29)

with
Z_{0}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Composing (
29
) by
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}
, we obtain an equation dealing with
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
as unknown:

\frac{\partial\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-\mu\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}G\quad\text{ in }(0,T),\qquad\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z(0)=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{0}.

(30)

The interest of this formulation is that the following linear operator is self-adjoint:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\mathcal{A}:&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})=:D(\mathcal{A})&\rightarrow&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})\\
&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z&\mapsto&\mu\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)\end{array}

(31)

For
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{\kappa}(\Gamma_{s})
we consider the norm
\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{\kappa-1}(\Gamma_{s})}
, with
\kappa\geq 1
. Let us derive the fundamental properties of
\mathcal{A}=\mu\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The operator
(\mathcal{A},D(\mathcal{A}))
is self-adjoint, dissipative, and thus infinitesimal generator of an analytic semigroup of contraction on
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})
.

Proof.

The Green’s formula (
2
) shows that
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}^{\ast}=-\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}
. For
Z_{1}
,
Z_{2}\in\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})
we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\langle\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1},\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2}\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}&=&\mu\langle\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1}),(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}})^{\ast}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
&=&-\mu\langle\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1}),\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
&=&-\mu\langle\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1}),\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2}\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.\end{array}

Using Proposition
4.3
, we see that
\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint and dissipative. Consequently, from
[
Ben+07
, Chapter 1, Proposition 2.11]
, the operator
(\mathcal{A},D(\mathcal{A}))
generates an analytic semigroup of contraction.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The resolvent of
\mathcal{A}
is compact.

Proof.

Let us show that there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lambda\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{A}
is invertible. Let be
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})\subset\mathds{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})
and consider the following system:

\lambda\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z-\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W.

(32)

Taking the scalar product of this equation by
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
, and using the Green’s formula (
2
), we obtain

\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}+\mu\langle\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z),\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}=\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

Further, from the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}
, we introduce
u^{\pm}
the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm},\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\left[u\right]=0\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n_{s}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s},\end{array}\right.

(33)

and by integration by parts we deduce

\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2}+2\nu\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{+})}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{-})}\right)=\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(34)

Introduce the bilinear form

\begin{array}[]{rccl}a:&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})\times\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})&\rightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1},\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2})&\mapsto&\lambda\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{1},\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{2}\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\\[5.0pt]
&&&+2\nu\left(\langle\varepsilon(u_{1}^{+}),\varepsilon(u_{2}^{+}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{+})}+\langle\varepsilon(u_{1}^{-}),\varepsilon(u_{2}^{-})\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega^{-})}\right),\end{array}

where
u_{1}^{\pm}
and
u_{2}^{\pm}
are solutions of (
33
) corresponding to
Z=Z_{1}
and
Z=Z_{2}
, respectively. It satisfies

a(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)\geq\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}^{2},

and thus, for
\lambda>0
, it is coercive. Introduce the linear form
b:\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})\ni\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\mapsto\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}
, which is clearly continuous. We consider the variational formulation of (
32
) as follows:

Find
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
such that
a(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z,\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\tilde{Z})=b(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\tilde{Z})
for all
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\tilde{Z}\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
.

(35)

From the Lax-Milgram theorem there exists a unique
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
solution of (
35
), and so satisfying (
32
). Note that it is sufficient to assume
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
for obtaining
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
. Choosing
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\tilde{Z}=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
in (
35
), with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get the following estimate

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(36)

Next, if we assume
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
, let us prove that
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
too. Since
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
, the identity

-\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W-\lambda\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z

yields

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}=\|\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}+\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},

(37)

where we used (
36
). Therefore
\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
, which means that
u^{\pm}_{|\Gamma_{s}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})
in system (
33
), leading to
u^{\pm}\in\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
, and consequently to
-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n_{s}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
. Furthermore, using classical elliptic estimates for Stokes problems with prescribed non-homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (see
[
Gal94
, Lemma 6.1, Chapter IV]
, the estimates in fractional spaces can be obtained by linear interpolation), we estimate

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}=\|\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}&=&\|\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n_{s}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
&\leq&C\left(\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\|\nabla p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right)\\[5.0pt]
&\leq&C\|u^{\pm}_{|\Gamma_{s}}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}=C\|\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\\

&\leq&C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})},\end{array}

where we have used the Poincaré inequality (
5
) and estimate (
37
) above. Next, using (
7
), we deduce

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(38)

We proceed similarly in order to estimate

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\|Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{3}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\|\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}&=&\|\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n_{s}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
&\leq&C\left(\|u\|_{\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\|\nabla p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\right)\\[5.0pt]
&\leq&C\|u^{\pm}_{|\Gamma_{s}}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}=C\|\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}}\\
&\leq&C\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}=C\|\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}.\end{array}

Again, the identity
-\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W-\lambda\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
yields

\|\mathcal{A}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}+\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C(1+\lambda)\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})},

where we have used (
38
), and thus we deduce

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C(1+\lambda)\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{s})}.

(39)

Combining (
38
) and (
39
), by interpolation we obtain

\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C(1+\lambda)^{1/2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}W\|_{\mathds{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})},

which completes the proof.
∎

We deduce wellposedness for system (

).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let be
0<T<\infty
. For
G\in\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})
and

, the following system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0,\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\

admits a unique solution

. Moreover, there exists a constant

, non-decreasing with respect to

, such that

See for example
[

satisfying

)-(

, leading to the announced result.

4.3
The non-homogeneous system

We now address system (
21
) in finite-time horizon, for general right-hand-sides, and any
\lambda\geq 0
.

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=F^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=\divg H^{\pm}&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=u^{-}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n_{s}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+G&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\\
Z(\cdot,0)=Z_{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.\end{array}

(40)

With
0<T<\infty
, we assume that
F^{\pm}\in\mathcal{F}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})
,
H^{\pm}\in\mathcal{U}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
, and
Z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})
. We use a
lifting
method: Let us describe a solution of (
40
) as

u^{\pm}=v^{\pm}+w^{\pm},\quad p^{\pm}=q^{\pm}+\pi^{\pm},

where
(w^{\pm},\pi^{\pm})
are solutions of the following Stokes problems

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg(w^{\pm},\pi^{\pm})=F^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg w^{\pm}=\divg H^{\pm}&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
w^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
w^{+}=w^{-}=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\end{array}

(41)

and
(v^{\pm},q^{\pm})
satisfy

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(v^{\pm},q^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg v^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
v^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
v^{+}=v^{-}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(v,q)\right]n_{s}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+G+\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\\

(42)

Note that the equations of (
41
) are uncoupled, as both Stokes systems can be considered in
\Omega_{s}^{+}
and
\Omega_{s}^{-}
independently. By considering
\overline{w}^{\pm}:=w^{\pm}-H^{\pm}
, we eliminate the non-homogeneous divergence condition, and we reduce (
41
) to standard Stokes problems with non-homogeneous Dirichlet condition:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(\overline{w}^{\pm},\pi^{\pm})=F^{\pm}+2\nu\divg\varepsilon(H^{\pm})\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg\overline{w}^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm},\\
\overline{w}^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\\
\overline{w}^{\pm}=-H^{\pm}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.\end{array}

(43)

It is well-known that for almost every
t\in(0,T)
there exists a unique solution
(\overline{w}^{\pm},\pi^{\pm})
satisfying

\|\overline{w}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|\pi\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})/\mathbb{R}}\leq C\left(\|F^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|\divg\varepsilon(H^{\pm})\|^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|H^{\pm}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s})}\right).

See for example
[
Gal94
, Lemma 6.1, Chapter IV]
. We deduce the same estimate for
(w^{\pm},\pi)=(\overline{w}^{\pm}+H^{\pm},\pi)
. Further, integrating in time this estimate, it follows from the trace theorem the following estimate

\|\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\left(\|F^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|H^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\right).

(44)

On the other side, equation (
42
) admits the following operator formulation

\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\big(G+\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\big)\quad\text{in (0,T),}\quad Z(0)=Z_{0}.

Following Proposition
4.5
and Theorem
4.1
, system (
42
) admits a unique solution, satisfying

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\left(\|Z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}\right).

Combined with (
44
), this estimate yields

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\left(\|Z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|F^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|H^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\right).

(45)

Wellposedness of the linear system (
40
) is stated as follows:

Proposition 4.6
.

For
0<T<\infty
, if
F^{\pm}\in\mathcal{F}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
,
H^{\pm}\in\mathcal{U}_{T}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
,
G\in\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})
and

, then there exists a unique solution
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})
to system (
40
). It satisfies (
45
).

Proof.

Existence is provided by the lifting method described above. For proving uniqueness, we use the linearity of the system, and assume
F^{\pm}=H^{\pm}=G=Z_{0}=0
. Then from Lemma
2.3
we obtain (
10
) with
g=0
, namely the following identity

\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|^{2}_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}+2\nu\left(\|\varepsilon(u^{+})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|\varepsilon(u^{-})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}\right)=\lambda\|\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|^{2}_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}.

The Grönwall’s lemma combined with with
Z_{0}=0
yields
Z\equiv 0
(up to a constant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), and concludes the proof.
∎

5
Feedback operator for the linear system

This section is devoted to the design of a feedback operator that stabilizes system (
21
) in infinite time horizon. Let us first study its controllability properties in finite time horizon.

5.1
Approximate controllability

Let be
0<T<\infty
. We consider system (
21
) with control
G
, with null data and initial condition:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u^{\pm}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+G&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\\
Z(\cdot,0)=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.\end{array}

(46)

Recall the definition of
approximate controllability
and
exact controllability
for linear evolution equations of type (
46
), that we state in our context as follows:

Definition 5.1
.

Define the reachable set as

R(T):=\left\{\text{$Z(\cdot,T)$ such that $Z$ is solution of~\eqref{syslin0}}\mid G\in\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})\right\}.

We say that system (
46
) is approximately controllable if
R(T)
is dense in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}
, or equivalently if
R(T)^{\perp}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We say that (
46
) is exactly controllable if
R(T)=\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})/\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We obtain the following key result:

Proposition 5.1
.

System (
46
) is approximately controllable.

Proof.

Introduce
Z_{T}\in R(T)^{\perp}
, and the following adjoint system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle-\divg\sigma(\phi^{\pm},\psi^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg\phi^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,T),\\
\phi^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\phi^{\pm}=\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial t}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(\phi,\psi)\right]n_{s}=\mu\Delta_{\Gamma_{s}}\zeta&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,T),\\
\zeta(T)=Z_{T}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s},\end{array}

(47)

with
(\phi^{+},\psi^{+},\phi^{-},\psi^{-},\zeta)
as unknowns. Now consider
(u^{+},p^{+},u^{-},p^{-},Z)
the solution of (
46
). Taking the inner product in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm}))
of the left equation in the first line of (
47
) by
u^{\pm}
, by integration by parts we obtain for all
G\in\mathcal{G}_{T}(\Gamma_{s})

\mu\left\langle\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{T},\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z(T)\right\rangle_{\mathds{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle G,\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial t}\right\rangle_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})}\mathrm{d}t.

Since
Z_{T}\in R(T)^{\perp}
, this identity implies that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial t}=0
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma_{s})
. System (
47
) then becomes system (
8
) of Lemma
2.2
, which yields that
\zeta
is a constant of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and therefore
Z_{T}
too, completing the proof.
∎

5.2
Feedback operator

Theorem 5.1
.

For all
\lambda>0
and
Z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})
, there exists a finite-dimensional subspace
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
of
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})
, with orthogonal projection
P_{\lambda}:\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s})\rightarrow\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
, a finite-dimensional space
\Xi\subset\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s})
and a linear operator
\Pi_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)},(\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)})^{\ast}\right)
defining the feedback operator

\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}:=-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}})^{\ast}\Pi_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathbf{H}^{5/2}(\Gamma_{s}),\Xi\right)

such that the solution
Z
of system (
21
) with
G=\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
satisfies
\left\|Z\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C_{0}
, where the constant
C_{0}
depends only on
Z_{0}
. Further, the operator
\Pi_{\lambda}
is the solution of the following finite-dimensional algebraic Riccati equation

\Pi_{\lambda}=\Pi_{\lambda}^{\ast}\succeq 0,\quad\Pi_{\lambda}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}^{\ast}\Pi_{\lambda}-\Pi_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}^{\ast}\Pi_{\lambda}+\mathrm{I}=0,

(48)

where we have introduced
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\mathcal{A}P_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)},\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)})
and
\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{s}),\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)})
.

Proof.

Let us consider the operator formulation of system (
21
), namely

\frac{\partial\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-(\mathcal{A}+\lambda\mathrm{Id})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}G\quad\text{ in }(0,T),\qquad\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z(0)=\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{0},

(49)

where
\mathcal{A}
is defined in (
31
). Recall that from
\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
we can retrieve
Z
(up to a constant) via Proposition
2.1
. We can choose
\lambda
in the resolvent of
\mathcal{A}
, without loss of generality. From Proposition
4.4
, the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete set of complex eigenvalues
(\lambda_{i})_{i\mathbb{N}}
, contained in an angular domain
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\mid\mathrm{arg}(\theta-z)\in(-\alpha,\alpha)\right\}
where
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We can order them such that

\dots<\Re(\lambda_{N+1})<-\lambda<\Re(\lambda_{N})<\cdots<\Re(\lambda_{2})<\Re(\lambda_{1})<0.

Furthermore, the generalized eigenspace associated with each eigenvalue is of finite dimension (see for instance
[
Kat95
, Chapter III, Theorem 6.29 page 187]
). Denoting by
\Lambda(\lambda_{i})
the real generalized eigenspace of
\lambda_{i}
or
(\lambda_{i},\overline{\lambda_{i}})
whether
\Im(\lambda_{i})=0
or not, respectively, we introduce the Hilbert spaces

\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{N}\Lambda(\lambda_{i}),\quad\mathds{H}_{s}^{(\lambda)}=\bigoplus_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\Lambda(\lambda_{i}).

Let us explain what we mean by
real generalized eigenspace
: If
(e_{j}(\lambda_{i}))_{1\leq j\leq m(\lambda_{i})}
is a basis of the complex generalized eigenspace of
\lambda_{i}
, where
m(\lambda_{i})
denotes its multiplicity, then
\Lambda(\lambda_{i})
is generated by the family
\left\{\Re(e_{j}(\lambda_{i})),\Im(e_{j}(\lambda_{i}))\mid 1\leq j\leq m(\lambda_{i})\right\}
. Note that
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
, the space of
unstable modes
, is of finite dimension. Both
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
and
\mathds{H}_{s}^{(\lambda)}
are invariant under
\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
P_{\lambda}
the orthogonal projection on
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
, parallel to
\mathds{H}_{s}^{(\lambda)}
. Projecting equation (
49
), with
\lambda=0
, on
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
yields

\frac{\partial P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-P_{\lambda}\mathcal{A}P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}G\quad\text{ in }(0,T),\qquad P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z(0)=P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z_{0}.

(50)

The approximate controllability of system (
46
) obtained in Proposition
5.1
implies that (
50
) too is approximately controllable. Its reachable set is dense in
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
, and since this space is of finite dimension, it is actually equal to
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
. This means that equation (
50
) is exactly controllable. From there, we use for instance the result of
[
Zab20
, Chapter I, Theorem 2.9, page 35]
stating that there exists a linear operator
K_{\lambda}
defined on
\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}
such that
P_{\lambda}\mathcal{A}P_{\lambda}+P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}K_{\lambda}
is exponentially stable with
\lambda
as a decay rate. Since the same property holds for
\mathcal{A}(\mathrm{Id}-P_{\lambda})
, we merely set
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}=\Re K_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}
and
\Xi=\Re K_{\lambda}(\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)})
. Further, following
[
Son98
]
(more specifically Lemma 8.4.1 page 381 and Theorem 41 page 384), we consider the following infinite time horizon optimal control problem:

\displaystyle\inf_{G\in\Xi}\left\{\mathcal{J}(Z,G)\mid\text{ $Z$ satisfies~\eqref{syslin0}}\right\},

(51)

with
\mathcal{J}(Z,G)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z\|_{\mathds{H}_{u}^{(\lambda)}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|G\|_{\Xi}^{2}\mathrm{d}t
. The first-order optimality conditions for Problem (
51
) lead to
G=-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}^{\ast}(\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}})^{\ast}\Pi_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z=\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\Pi_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z
, where
\Pi_{\lambda}=\Pi_{\lambda}^{\ast}\succeq 0
satisfies the Riccati equation (
48
), finishing the proof.
∎

We deduce an estimate for the stabilized linear system with non-homogeneous right-hand-sides.

Corollary 5.1
.

Assume
Z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})
,
F^{\pm}\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
,
H^{\pm}\in\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})
, and
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})
. Using the feedback operator
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
obtained in Theorem
5.1
, there exists a unique solution
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})
to the following system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=F^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=\divg H^{\pm}&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,\infty),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u^{+}=u^{-}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\quad\text{ and }\quad-\left[\sigma(u,p)\right]n=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+G&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,\infty),\\
Z(\cdot,0)=Z_{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s},\end{array}

(52)

and it satisfies

\begin{array}[]{l}\|u^{+}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|p^{+}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|u^{-}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|p^{-}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\\
\leq C_{s}(1+\lambda)\left(\|Z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|F^{+}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|F^{-}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|H^{+}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|H^{-}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\right),\end{array}

(53)

where the constant
C_{s}>0
depends only on
\Gamma_{s}
.

Proof.

The lifting method of section
4.3
can be used here: Introduce
u^{\pm}=v^{\pm}+w^{\pm}
and
p^{\pm}=q^{\pm}+\pi^{\pm}
, where
(w^{\pm},\pi^{\pm})
satisfy the Stokes problems (
41
) with
(0,T)
replaced by
(0,\infty)
, and where
(v^{\pm},q^{\pm},Z)
satisfies

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(v^{\pm},q^{\pm})=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg v^{\pm}=0&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,\infty),\\
v^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
v^{\pm}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\ \text{ and }\ -\left[\sigma(v,q)\right]n_{s}=\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z+G+\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,\infty),\\
Z(\cdot,0)=Z_{0}&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}.\end{array}

(54)

We formulate system (
54
) as

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-\lambda\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z-\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}(\mu\divg_{\Gamma_{s}}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z)-\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z&=&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\left(G+\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\right),\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z}{\partial t}-(\lambda\mathrm{Id}+\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda})\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}Z&=&\nabla_{\Gamma_{s}}\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{s}}\left(G+\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\right),\end{array}

and since the operator
\lambda\mathrm{Id}+\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
is the infinitesimal generator of an analytic semigroup of negative type, a consequence of
[
Ben+07
, Theorem 3.1 page 143, Part II]
and Proposition
2.1
is the existence of
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})
, satisfying

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\left(\|Z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|\left[\sigma(w,\pi)\right]n_{s}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\right).

Next, the steps of section
4.3
can be repeated to obtain the existence and uniqueness of
Z
, which satisfies

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\leq C\left(\|Z_{0}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|F^{+}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|F^{-}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|H^{+}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|H^{-}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}+\|G\|_{\mathcal{G}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}\right).

(55)

Further,
(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})
are also obtained uniquely as the solutions of the classical Stokes problems with Dirichlet boundary conditions and non-homogeneous divergence condition, namely

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\divg\sigma(u^{\pm},p^{\pm})=F^{\pm}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u^{\pm}=\divg H^{\pm}&&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,\infty),\\
u^{+}=0&&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u^{\pm}=\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z&&\text{on }\Gamma_{s}\times(0,\infty).\end{array}

Up to considering
u^{\pm}-H^{\pm}
, from
[
Gal94
, Lemma 6.1, Chapter IV]
they satisfy the estimate

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|u^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|p^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}&\leq&C\left(\left\|\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}-\lambda Z\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,\infty;\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Gamma_{s}))}+\|H^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\right)\\
&\leq&\left((1+\lambda)\|Z\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\infty}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|H^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\right)\end{array}

which, combined with (
55
), leads to (
53
), and thus the announced result.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem

. We first prove wellposedness of system (

is replaced by

\begin{array}[]{rl}-\divg(\sigma(\hat{u}^{\pm},\hat{p}^{\pm}))=\hat{f}^{\pm}+F(\hat{u}^{\pm},\hat{p}^{\pm},\hat{Z})\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg\hat{u}=\divg H(\hat{u}^{\pm},\hat{Z})&\text{in }\Omega_{s}^{\pm}\times(0,\infty),\\

yields

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|(\hat{u}_{2}^{+},\hat{p}_{2}^{+},\hat{u}_{2}^{-},\hat{p}_{2}^{-},\hat{Z}_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega_{s}^{+},\Omega_{s}^{-},\Gamma_{s})}&\leq&C_{s}(1+\lambda)\Big(\|X_{0}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}+\|\hat{f}^{+}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{+})}+\|\hat{f}^{-}\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\Omega_{s}^{-})}\\

and

is the constant of estimate (

). Let us prove that
\mathcal{N}
is a contraction in
\mathcal{B}
, provided that
\|X_{0}-\mathrm{Id}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{0}(\Gamma_{s})}
and

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\|F(\hat{u}^{\pm},\hat{p}^{\pm},\hat{Z})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}&\leq&C\|\nabla\tilde{Y}(\tilde{X})\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\left(\|\nabla\hat{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}+\|\hat{p}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})}\right)\|\nabla\tilde{X}-\mathrm{I}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{3/2}(\Omega_{s}^{\pm})},\\

We see easily that

7
Comments on a possible extension to dimension 3

Some results obtained in the present paper could certainly and straightforwardly be extended to the three-dimensional case, like the study of the Poincaré-Steklov operator for example, or the design of the feedback operator. Higher-order Sobolev spaces may be considered for guaranteeing the
C^{1}
regularity and stability of Sobolev spaces by product. However, some geometric aspects would deserve a careful investigation. Let us make comments on the difficulties that appear in dimension 3:

•

About the stationary state obtained in Lemma
2.1
: In dimension 2, the interface
\Gamma(t)
is a curve, and its mean curvature is simply called the
curvature
. From the fundamental theorem of curves, this curvature determines entirely
\Gamma(t)
, up to proper rigid deformations. In the case of dimension 3, the interface
\Gamma(t)
is then a surface, and this is the Gaussian curvature which characterizes the metric of the surface. We say that this is an
intrinsic
property of the surface
\Gamma(t)
(cf. the Gauss’s
Theorem Egregium
). More precisely, two surfaces with the same Gauss curvature differ only up to proper rigid deformations, we say that they are
congruent
. The mean curvature which appears in the surface-tension model is only extrinsic in dimension 3, which means that two surfaces with the same mean curvature could not be congruent. However, when restricting the framework to closed surfaces, the Alexandrov’s theorem
[
Ale56
]
(see
[
Ale62
]
for an English translation) provides a positive result: Two closed surfaces with the same mean curvature are identical,
up to essential transformations
. Essential transformations refer to proper rigid deformations and dilatation. In the incompressible case, the volume contained inside the surface is constant, and thus this notion reduces to proper rigid deformations, like in dimension 2.

•

About the linearized system in dimension 3: Simplifications specific to dimension 2 have been made in section
3.2
when linearizing the mean curvature of
\Gamma(t)
for small displacements. The expression so obtained involves the operator
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
. A priori the linear operator which appears in dimension 3 is more complex, and discussions of section
2.3
about the kernel of
\nabla^{n_{s}}_{\Gamma_{s}}
would no longer be relevant.

•

About the extension of diffeomorphisms on the sphere into the ball: This question is less simple in the case of a 2-sphere. In
[
Ye94
, system (8.3), section 8]
, the author gave comments on conditions under which we could extend a diffeomorphism defined on a boundary of a given domain. A sufficient condition is that the set of diffeomorphisms of this boundary preserving the orientation is connected. In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, this sufficient condition is always fulfilled and thus the answer is positive. In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, things are more delicate, and counter-examples to this sufficient condition exist. However, in the case of the sphere, Smale provided a positive answer in
[
Sma59
]
. The result requires a
C^{\infty}
regularity, and we do not know whether it could be used for obtaining an extension with the same properties as in section
3.1
. Further comments on these geometric questions would go beyond the scope of the present article.

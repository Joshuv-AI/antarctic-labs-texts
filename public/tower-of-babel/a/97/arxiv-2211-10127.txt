Classification of radial solutions to − Δ g ​ u = e u -\Delta_{g}u=e^{u} on Riemannian models
Elvise BERCHIO, Alberto FERRERO, Debdip Ganguly, Prasun Roychowdhury

Abstract
. We provide a complete classification with respect to asymptotic behaviour, stability and intersections properties of radial smooth solutions to the equation − Δ g ​ u = e u -\Delta_{g}u=e^{u} on Riemannian model manifolds ( M , g ) (M,g) in dimension N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 . Our assumptions include Riemannian manifolds with sectional curvatures bounded or unbounded from below. Intersection and stability properties of radial solutions are influenced by the dimension N N in the sense that two different kinds of behaviour occur when 2 ≤ N ≤ 9 2\leq N\leq 9 or N ≥ 10 N\geq 10 , respectively. The crucial role of these dimensions in classifying solutions is well-known in Euclidean space; here the analysis highlights new properties of solutions that cannot be observed in the flat case.

1.
Introduction

Let
N\geq 2
and let
M
be an
N
-dimensional
Riemannian model

(M,g)
, namely a manifold admitting a pole
o

and whose metric is given, in polar or spherical coordinates around
o
, by

(1.1)

g={\rm d}r^{2}+(\psi(r))^{2}\,{\rm d}\omega^{2},\qquad r>0,\,\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\,,

for some function
\psi
satisfying suitable assumptions. Here
{\rm d}\omega^{2}
denotes the canonical metric on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
and
r
is by construction the distance between a point of spherical coordinates
(r,\omega)
and the pole
o
.

In this article, we are concerned with radial smooth solutions of

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}u\,=\,e^{u}\quad\text{ in }M\,,

namely smooth solutions of (
1.2
) depending only on the geodesic distance from the pole. Here
\Delta_{g}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
(M,g)
.

The great interest for equation (
1.2
) is motivated by its applications to geometry and physics see e.g.,
[
9
,
14
,
18
]
. For instance, in fluid dynamics, a variant of (
1.2
) appears when studying “Stuart vortices” on a unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in a stationary regime, i.e.

-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,\psi=b+c\,e^{d\psi}\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{2}

where
\psi
represents the stream function,
b,c,d
are real constants and the right hand side of the equation represents a vorticity
\omega
depending exponentially on the streamfunction
\psi
, see
[
11
]
. Related semilinear equations on rotating spheres also arise in the study of stratospheric planetary stationary flows, see
[
10
]
and the discussion therein. When looking for stationary stream functions, the authors of
[
10
]
are led to consider the semilinear elliptic equation

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\,\psi=F(\psi)-2\omega\sin\theta\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{2}

where now
\omega
represents the rotation speed around the polar axis,
F
is a smooth function and
\theta
is the angle of latitude.

The behaviour of solutions to equation (
1.2
), when posed in the Euclidean space, has been fully understood from different points of view: asymptotic behaviour, stability and intersections properties. In the seminal paper
[
21
]
existence and asymptotic behaviour of radial solutions were established by means of a dynamical system analysis based on the so-called Emden transformation of the phase plane. We refer to
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
for the intersection properties of solutions, while the classification of solutions with respect to stability is given in
[
12
,
15
]
. See also
[
13
]
and references therein for more recent results in the case of general nonlinearities. Finally, related results in the weighted case:
-\Delta u=k(x)e^{u}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be found in
[
1
,
7
,
8
]
, extensions to the higher order case are instead given in
[
3
]
and in
[
16
]
.

As already pointed out in
[
21
]
, equation (
1.2
) can be regarded as the limit case as
p\rightarrow+\infty
of the Lane-Emden-Fowler equation:
-\Delta_{g}u=|u|^{p-1}u
in
M
(
p>1
). In the last ten years, there has been an intense study of this equation in non-euclidean frameworks including the hyperbolic space and more general Riemannian models, see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
17
,
20
,
23
,
22
]
and references therein. In these papers existence, multiplicity, asymptotic and stability results were provided. The analysis settled on general manifolds highlights deep relationships between the qualitative behaviour of solutions and intrinsic properties of the manifold itself and, sometimes, it reveals a number of unexpected phenomena, if compared with the euclidean case, see e.g, the introductions of
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
. Another reason for the interest in this kind of research is the fact that some classical tools in the Euclidean case do not work in non-euclidean frameworks, therefore the analysis requires new ideas and alternative approaches that could be useful also in other contexts. As a matter of example, an analogous of the above-mentioned Emden transformation seems not known in non-euclidean settings, therefore different arguments, such as the exploitation of ad hoc Lyapunov functionals, fine asymptotic analysis and blow-up methods, must be exploited, see e.g., Sections
3
-
5
below.

Coming back to (
1.2
), its investigation in non-euclidean settings turns out to be the natural subsequent step in order to complete the scenario of results for this equation; this motivates the present paper. More precisely, we provide a complete classification with respect to asymptotic behaviour, stability and intersections properties of radial smooth solutions to (
1.2
) for manifolds
M
having strictly negative sectional curvatures, possibly unbounded, see assumptions (
A1
)-(
A3
) in Section
2
. It is worth mentioning that, even if in our proofs we take advantage of some arguments already employed in the study of the Lane-Emden-Fowler equation on Riemannian models, the fact of dealing with an exponential nonlinearity brought a number of considerable technical difficulties related, for instance, to the different sign and decay behaviour of solutions in the two cases. Moreover, our analysis made it possible to highlight a number of properties of solutions that cannot be observed in the flat case, see e.g., Remarks
2.3
,
2.8
and
2.10
in Section
2
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we give the precise formulation of the problem and we state our main results about continuation and asymptotic behaviour of solutions (Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
), stability properties (Theorems
2.6
and
2.7
), and intersection properties (Theorem
2.5
and Theorem
2.9
); the remaining sections of the paper are devoted to the proofs. More precisely, in Section
3
we prove Proposition
2.1
while Section
4
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.2
. Section
5
contains a number of technical lemmas that will be exploited to prove Theorems
2.5
,
2.6
,
2.7
and
2.9
. At last, for the sake of the reader, in the Appendix, we briefly recall some well-known facts in the Euclidean case that highlight the role of the dimension
N
in the stability and intersection properties analysis.

2.
Statement of the problem and main results

2.1.
Notations

Let
\psi
be the function introduced in (
1.1
). We assume that
\psi
satisfies

(A1)

\psi\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
,
\psi>0
in
(0,+\infty)
,
\psi(0)=\psi^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
and
\psi^{\prime}(0)=1
;

and

(A2)

\psi^{\prime}(r)>0
for any
r>0
.

We recall that the Riemannian model associated with the choice
\psi(r)=\sinh r
is a well-known representation of the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, see e.g.,
[
19
]
and the references therein, while the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
corresponds to
\psi(r)=r
.

The following list summarizes some notations we shall use throughout this paper.

-

For any
P\in M
we denote by
T_{P}M
the tangent space to
M
at the point
P
.

-

For any
P\in M
and
U_{1},U_{2}\in T_{P}M
we denote by
\langle U_{1},U_{2}\rangle_{g}
the scalar product on
T_{P}M
associated with the metric
g
.

-

For any
P\in M
and
U\in T_{P}M
we denote by
|U|_{g}:=\sqrt{\langle U,U\rangle_{g}}
the norm of the vector
U
.

-

{\rm d}V_{g}
denotes the volume measure in
(M,g)
.

-

\nabla_{g}
denotes the Riemannian gradient in
(M,g)
and in spherical coordinates it is given by

\nabla_{g}u(r,\omega)=\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(r,\omega)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{r}{(\psi(r))^{2}}\,\nabla_{\omega}u(r,\omega)
for any
u\in C^{1}(M)
, where we denoted by
\nabla_{\omega}
the Riemannian gradient in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.

-

\Delta_{g}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
(M,g)
and in spherical coordinates it is given by

\Delta_{g}u(r,\omega)=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial r^{2}}(r,\omega)+(N-1)\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}(r,\omega)+\frac{1}{(\psi(r))^{2}}\,\Delta_{\omega}u(r,\omega)
for any
u\in C^{2}(M)
, where we denoted by
\Delta_{\omega}
the Laplace-Beltrami operator in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.

-

C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
denotes the space of
C^{\infty}(M)
functions compactly supported in
M
.

From the above notations, we deduce that if
u\in C^{2}(M)
is a radial function, then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u(r)=u^{\prime\prime}(r)+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\,u^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{(\psi(r))^{N-1}}[(\psi(r))^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)]^{\prime}.

Since our aim is to study
smooth
radial solutions to (
1.2
), for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we focus our attention on the following initial value problem

(2.2)

\begin{cases}-u^{\prime\prime}(r)-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}u^{\prime}(r)=e^{u(r)}&(r>0)\\
u(0)=\alpha&\\
u^{\prime}(0)=0.&\\
\end{cases}

The existence and uniqueness of a local solution
u(r)
to (
2.2
) in
0\leq r<R
(here
R
denotes the maximal interval of existence) follows by arguing as in Proposition 1 in the Appendix of
[
24
]
. A classical argument allows to prove that actually
R=+\infty
, thus showing that the solution
u=u(r)
to (
2.2
) is globally define in
[0,+\infty)
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Suppose that
\psi
satisfies
assumptions (
A1
)-(
A2
). For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
the local solution to (
2.2
) may be continued to the whole interval
[0,+\infty)
. Moreover, the functions
r\mapsto u^{\prime}(r)
and
r\mapsto e^{u(r)}
are bounded in
[0,+\infty)
,
u^{\prime}(r)<0
for any
r>0
and in particular
u
is decreasing in
[0,+\infty)
.

2.2.
Asymptotic behaviour

In order to study the asymptotic behaviour of global solutions to (
2.2
) we require the additional condition:

(A3)

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=:\Lambda\in(0,\infty].

Clearly, the hyperbolic space
satisfies condition (
A3
) and Riemannian models which are
asymptotically hyperbolic satisfy it as well. Furthermore, such a condition allows for
unbounded
negative sectional curvatures: a typical example in which this can
hold corresponds to the choice
\psi(r)=e^{r^{a}}
for a given
a>1

and
r
large, a case for which (see
[
4
, Section 1.1]
) sectional curvatures in the radial direction diverge like
-a^{2}r^{2(a-1)}
as
r\to+\infty
. In addition, we remark that under assumptions (
A1
)-(
A3
), the
L^{2}
spectrum of
-\Delta_{g}
is
bounded away from zero whereas if
\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=0
then there is no gap in the

L^{2}
spectrum of
-\Delta_{g}
, see e.g.,
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
. Moreover, it can be proved that if the radial
sectional curvature goes to zero as
r\to+\infty
then necessarily
\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=0
, see again
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
, therefore no spectral gap is present and the expected
picture is of Euclidean type.

In the following statement we show that the asymptotic behaviour of solutions of (
2.2
) is related to the behaviour at infinity of the ratio
\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}
and hence, for what remarked above, to the curvatures of the manifold:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
. Suppose that
\psi
satisfies
assumptions (
A1
)-(
A3
). Finally, in the
case
\Lambda=+\infty
we also assume that

(A4)

\displaystyle\left[\log\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\right)\right]^{\prime}=O(1)\quad\text{as }r\rightarrow+\infty\,.

Let
u
be a solution to (
2.2
). Then two cases may occur:

(i)

if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
, then

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}u(r)\in(-\infty,\alpha)\,;

(ii)

if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}\not\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
, then
u
goes to
-\infty
with the following rate:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(r)}{\log\big(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}{\rm d}s\big)}=-1\,;

in particular, when
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(r)}{\log r}=-1.

Remark 2.3
.

As a prototype of function
\psi
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
when
\Lambda=+\infty
consider the function
\psi(r)=re^{r^{2\gamma}}
with
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
. If
\frac{1}{2}<\gamma\leq 1
case
(ii)
occurs while if
\gamma>1
assumption
(i)
holds. Clearly, if
M=\mathbb{H}^{N}
we have that
\Lambda=1
and case
(ii)
occurs. From
[
21
]
we recall that in the flat case solutions diverge to
-\infty
being asymptotically equivalent to
-2\log r
, therefore the effect of curvatures, in general, results in a slower decay of solutions, indeed they may even remain bounded if case
(i)
occurs.

2.3.
Stability results and intersection properties of solutions

Let us start with the definition of stability.

Definition 2.4
.

A solution
u\in C^{2}(M)
to (
1.2
) is stable if

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}v|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}-\int_{M}e^{u}\,v^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}\geq 0\quad\forall v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(M).

If
u
does not satisfy (
2.3
), we say that it is unstable.

It is well known that stability plays an important role in the classification of solutions of elliptic partial differential equations and the analysis of qualitative properties of solutions, see e.g., the seminal paper
[
6
]
. In this section, we provide a complete classification of smooth radial solutions of (
1.2
) with respect to stability and we show that the same conditions determine either the stability or the intersection properties. The relationship between stability properties and intersection properties is clarified by the following result.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
). Then the following statements hold:

(i)

let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two distinct real numbers and let
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
be the corresponding solutions of (
2.2
), i.e.
u_{\alpha}(0)=\alpha
and
u_{\beta}(0)=\beta
. If
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
are stable then they do not intersect;

(ii)

if
u_{\alpha}
is a unstable solution of (
2.2
) for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, then for any
\beta>\alpha
we have that
u_{\beta}
intersects
u_{\alpha}
at least once.

We observe that in the proof Theorem
2.5
-
(ii)
, we actually show the validity of a more general result involving also non-radial smooth solutions of (
1.2
). Indeed, in Lemma
6.2
we prove that if
u
is a smooth unstable solution of (
1.2
) then (
1.2
) does not admit any smooth solution
v
satisfying
v>u
in
M
.

We now state the two main results about the stability of radial smooth solutions of (
1.2
) characterized by dimensions

2\leq N\leq 9
and
N\geq 10
, respectively.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
2\leq N\leq 9
and let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
). For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
denote by
u_{\alpha}
the unique solution to (
2.2
). Then there exist
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

if
\alpha\in(-\infty,\eta]
then
u_{\alpha}
is stable;

(ii)

if
\alpha>\eta
then
u_{\alpha}
is unstable.

Furthermore, we have that
\eta\geq\log(\lambda_{1}(M))
with the strict inequality if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}\notin L^{1}(0,+\infty)
where
\lambda_{1}(M)
denotes the bottom of the spectrum of
-\Delta_{g}
in
M
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
N\geq 10
and let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
) and the additional condition

(A5)

\psi\in C^{3}([0,+\infty)),\quad[\log(\psi^{\prime}(r))]^{\prime\prime}>0\quad\text{for }r>0\,.

Then all solutions to (
2.2
) are stable.

Remark 2.8
.

The fact that
N=10
is a critical threshold for stability is well-known in the Euclidean case, see
[
15
]
and the Appendix. Here a new critical value
\eta
arises which has no analogous in the flat case where solutions are always unstable if
2\leq N\leq 9
and Theorem
2.6
-
(i)
never occurs. In other words, we can say that, by the effect of assumption (
A3
), the critical dimension does not exist if
\alpha\leq\eta
since solutions are stable for all
N\geq 2
.

About assumption (
A5
), it is technical but it includes the most interesting examples. Indeed, it holds in the relevant case of the hyperbolic space, since
[\log(\psi^{\prime}(r))]^{\prime\prime}=(\cosh r)^{-2}
. Models with unbounded curvatures satisfying (
A5
) can be build as well, e.g., by taking
\psi(r)=e^{r^{a}}
with
a>1
for
r\geq 1
, indeed
[\log(\psi^{\prime}(r))]^{\prime\prime}=\frac{a-1}{r^{2}}(ar^{a}-1)>0
for
r\geq 1
.

At last, concerning intersection properties, we have:

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
N\geq 2
, let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
) and let
\eta
be as in the statement of Theorem
2.6
. We have that:

(i)

if
2\leq N\leq 9
and
u_{\alpha}
,
u_{\beta}
are two solutions of (
2.2
) with
\alpha,\beta\in(-\infty,\eta]
then
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
do not intersect;

(ii)

if
2\leq N\leq 9
and
u_{\alpha}
,
u_{\beta}
are two solutions of (
2.2
) with
\alpha,\beta>\eta
then
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
intersect at least once;

(iii)

if
N\geq 10
and the additional condition (
A5
) holds true, then for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the corresponding solutions
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
of (
2.2
) do not intersect.

Remark 2.10
.

In
\mathbb{R}^{N}
it is known, see e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
, that every two smooth radial solutions intersect each other once if
N=2
and infinitely many times if
3\leq N\leq 9
while for
N\geq 10
smooth radial solutions do not intersect. Therefore, the behaviour stated in Theorem
2.9
-
(i)
(with its counterpart Theorem
2.6
-
(i)
) is, as a matter of fact, an effect of non-vanishing curvatures.

3.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

Let
(0,R)
be the maximum interval where the local solution
u
of (
2.2
) is defined and consider the Lyapunov functional

(3.1)

\displaystyle F(r):=\frac{1}{2}(u^{\prime}(r))^{2}+e^{u(r)}\quad\text{for any }r\in(0,R)\,.

It is readily seen that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(r)=u^{\prime}(r)[u^{\prime\prime}(r)+e^{u(r)}]=-(N-1)u^{\prime}(r)^{2}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\leq 0\quad\text{for any }r\in(0,R)\,.

This proves that
F
is decreasing and
0<F(r)\leq e^{\alpha}
for any
r\in(0,R)
, therefore both
u^{\prime}(r)
and
e^{u(r)}
are bounded in
(0,R)
. On the other hand, integrating (
2.1
) from
0
to
r
we deduce

\displaystyle(\psi(r))^{N-1}u^{\prime}(r)=-\int_{0}^{r}(\psi(s))^{N-1}\,e^{u(s)}\>{\rm d}s<0\quad\text{for any }r\in(0,R)\,.

Therefore,
u^{\prime}
is a negative function in
(0,R)
and hence
u
is decreasing in
(0,R)
as claimed.

Finally, we show that
R=+\infty
. Assume by contradiction that
R
is finite and let

\displaystyle l:=\lim_{r\rightarrow R^{-}}u(r)\in[-\infty,\alpha)\,.

If
l=-\infty
we immediately get a contradiction since

\displaystyle u(r)=\alpha+\int_{0}^{r}u^{\prime}(s)\>{\rm d}s\qquad\text{for any }r\in(0,R)

and taking the limit as
r\rightarrow R^{-}
we have that the left hand side goes to
-\infty
while the right hand side remains bounded since
u^{\prime}(r)
is bounded. Being
l
finite, from local existence for solutions of ordinary differential equations with initial value conditions, the solution
u
may be extended in a right neighbourhood of
R
thus contradicting the maximality of
R
. This shows that
R=+\infty
completes the proof of the proposition.

\Box

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

Throughout this section, we will always assume the validity of conditions (
A1
)-(
A3
) and when
\Lambda=+\infty
in (
A3
) we also assume the validity of the additional condition (
A4
). We start by showing that
u^{\prime}
admits a limit at infinity and this limit is zero:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.2
). Then

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u^{\prime}(r)=0\,.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.1
we know that
u
is decreasing in
[0,+\infty)
and hence it admits a limit as
r\to+\infty
. We put

(4.1)

l:=\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)\in[-\infty,\alpha)\,,

where
\alpha=u(0)
.
Let
F
be the function defined in (
3.1
). Since
F
is non-increasing, then
0<F(r)\leq F(0)=e^{\alpha}
for any
r\geq 0
. Hence, there exists
c\in[0,+\infty)
such that
c=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}F(r)
.

By (
4.1
) we deduce that
\bar{l}:=\lim_{r\to+\infty}e^{u(r)}\in[0,+\infty)
so that by (
3.1
) we obtain

(4.2)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u^{\prime}(r)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}-\sqrt{2F(r)-e^{u(r)}}=-\sqrt{2c-\bar{l}}=\gamma\in(-\infty,0]\,.

It remains to prove that
\gamma=0
. Suppose by contradiction that
\gamma<0
.
This implies

(4.3)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left[\alpha+\int_{0}^{r}u^{\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s\right]=-\infty\,.

Letting
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty]
be as in (
A3
), by (
2.2
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) we obtain

(4.4)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u^{\prime\prime}(r)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\left[-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\,u^{\prime}(r)-e^{u(r)}\right]=-(N-1)\Lambda\gamma\in(0,+\infty]\,.

In turn, by (
4.4
) and (
2.2
), we infer that

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u^{\prime}(r)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{r}u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,{\rm d}s=+\infty\,,

in contradiction with (
4.2
). This completes the proof of the lemma.

\Box

By exploiting Lemma
4.1
we prove:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.2
). Then the following limit exists

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}\,.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two parts depending on whether
\Lambda
is finite or not.

The case
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
.
From (

\displaystyle-\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}.

\chi

.

We proceed by contradiction assuming that
\chi

.
By direct computation, we see that

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{e^{u(r)}[({u^{\prime}}(r))^{2}-u^{\prime\prime}(r)]}{({u^{\prime}}(r))^{2}}\quad\text{for any }r>0.

We may assume that
\chi
admits infinitely many local maxima and minima at some points
r_{m}
, with

as

, and
\chi(r_{m})

\displaystyle-u^{\prime}(r_{m})-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r_{m})}{\psi(r_{m})}=\chi(r_{m})\,,

for any

. Now, by (

) and Lemma

The case
\Lambda=+\infty
.
We proceed similarly to the previous case by writing (

(4.7)

\displaystyle-\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}-(N-1)=\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}

and by defining this time
\chi(r):=\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}
in such a way that if

admits a limit as

then the same conclusion occurs for
\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}
and we complete the proof of the lemma also in this case.
By contradiction, assume that
\chi
does not admit a limit as
r\to+\infty
. A simple computation gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}(r)=\chi(r)\left\{u^{\prime}(r)-\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}-\left[\log\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\right)\right]^{\prime}\right\}\qquad\text{for any }r>0.

We may assume that
\chi
admits infinitely many local maxima and minima at some points
r_{m}
with
r_{m}\rightarrow+\infty
as
m\rightarrow+\infty
, and
\chi(r_{m})
does not admit a limit as
m\to+\infty
. In particular, by (
4.8
), we have that

(4.9)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(r_{m})-\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r_{m})}{u^{\prime}(r_{m})}-\left[\log\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r_{m})}{\psi(r_{m})}\right)\right]^{\prime}=0\,.

Hence, evaluating (
4.7
) at
r_{m}
and using (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle-(N-1)-\frac{\psi(r_{m})u^{\prime}(r_{m})}{\psi^{\prime}(r_{m})}+\frac{\psi(r_{m})}{\psi^{\prime}(r_{m})}\left[\log\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r_{m})}{\psi(r_{m})}\right)\right]^{\prime}=\chi(r_{m})\,,

for any
m
.

Finally, by Lemma
4.1
, (
A3
) and (
A4
), we find that
\chi(r_{m})\rightarrow-(N-1)
for
m\rightarrow+\infty
, a contradiction. The proof of the lemma is complete also in this case.

\Box

Our next purpose is to show that the limit in Lemma
4.2
must be
0
under the additional assumption:

(4.10)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)=-\infty\,.

In Lemma

below we discuss the occurrence of (

) and we provide a sufficient condition for (

) in terms of the integrability properties of the ratio
\psi/\psi^{\prime}
.

Before proving in Lemma

that the limit in Lemma

is zero when (

) holds, we state the following result which deals with the behaviour of
\psi
at infinity.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be as in (
A3
), the following statements hold true:

(i)

for any
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty]
we have that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\psi(r)=+\infty

and moreover for any
M>0
and
0<\delta<N-1
we have that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\psi^{-(N-1)+\delta}\in L^{1}(M,\infty)\,;

(ii)

if
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
then there exist
C>0
and
M>0
such that

(4.13)

\psi(r)>Ce^{\frac{\Lambda}{2}\,r}\qquad\text{for any }r>M\,.

Proof.

Let us start with the proof of
(i)
. First of all, we observe that (
A3
) also reads
\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\left[\log(\psi(r))\right]^{\prime}=\Lambda>0
and (
4.11
) follows by writing

\log(\psi(r))=\int_{R}^{r}\left[\log(\psi(s))\right]^{\prime}\,ds+\log(\psi(R))

for some
R>0
and letting
r\to+\infty
.

Let us proceed with the proof of (
4.12
) when
\Lambda=+\infty
. By (
A3
) it follows that there exists
R>0
such that
\psi^{\prime}(r)>M\psi(r)
for any
r>R
, for some positive constant
M>0
. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(\psi(r))^{N-1-\delta}}\,{\rm d}r=\int_{\psi(R)}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{N-1-\delta}\,\psi^{\prime}(\psi^{-1}(s))}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\qquad<\frac{1}{M}\int_{\psi(R)}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{N-1-\delta}\,\psi(\psi^{-1}(s))}\,{\rm d}s=\frac{1}{M}\int_{\psi(R)}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s^{N-\delta}}\,{\rm d}s<+\infty\,.

This proves (
4.12
) and completes the proof of
(i)
when
\Lambda=+\infty
. The validity of (
4.12
) when
\Lambda<+\infty
is an easy consequence of statement
(ii)
.

Let us proceed with the proof of
(ii)
. By (
A3
) we have that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\Lambda-\varepsilon<[\log(\psi(r))]^{\prime}<\Lambda+\varepsilon\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{\varepsilon}\,.

After integration we get

\psi(r_{\varepsilon})e^{(\Lambda-\varepsilon)(r-r_{\varepsilon})}<\psi(r)<\psi(r_{\varepsilon})e^{(\Lambda+\varepsilon)(r-r_{\varepsilon})}\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{\varepsilon}\,.

The proof of (
4.13
) now follows choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{\Lambda}{2}
and
C=\psi(r_{\varepsilon})e^{-\frac{\Lambda}{2}\,r_{\varepsilon}}
in the left inequality above.

\Box

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.2
) and suppose that
u
satisfies (
4.10
). Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}=0\,.

Proof.

If
u^{\prime\prime}
vanishes infinitely many times at infinity, then by Lemma
4.2
we are done. If this does not occur, using again Lemma
4.2
and, recalling Proposition
2.1
and assumptions (
A1
)-(
A2
), we infer that the following limit exists

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime\prime}(r)\psi(r)}=:L\in[-\infty,+\infty]\,.

The proof of the lemma now follows if we prove that
|L|=+\infty
.

We divide the remaining part of the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We show that
L\neq 0
. By contradiction, assume
L=0
. Notice that by Lemma
4.1
, (
4.10
), de l’Hôpital’s rule and (
A3
)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime}(r)e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime\prime}(r)\psi(r)}\,e^{u(r)}\,\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}=0

by which, from (
A3
), we readily get

(4.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{u(r)}\psi(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}=0\,.

Recalling (
4.7
), by (
4.14
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}=-(N-1).

This yields
\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime\prime}(r)\psi(r)}=-\frac{1}{N-1}
, a contradiction.

This completes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2.
We now prove that
L
cannot be finite. Thanks to Step 1, we may assume by contradiction that
L\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Arguing as in Step 1 we get that (
4.14
) also holds in this case and, in turn, we obtain that
L=-\frac{1}{N-1}
. Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>
such that for any
r>r_{\varepsilon}

-(N-1)-\varepsilon\leq\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)\psi(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}\leq-(N-1)+\varepsilon\,,

whence

[-(N-1)-\varepsilon]\,[\log(\psi(r))]^{\prime}\leq[\log(-u^{\prime}(r))]^{\prime}\leq[-(N-1)+\varepsilon]\,[\log(\psi(r))]^{\prime}\,.

Integrating from
r_{\varepsilon}
to
r
, with
r>r_{\varepsilon}
, we deduce that

\log\bigg(\frac{-u^{\prime}(r)}{-u^{\prime}(r_{\varepsilon})}\bigg)\leq[-(N-1)+\varepsilon]\log\bigg(\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi(r_{\varepsilon})}\bigg)

and, in turn, that

-u^{\prime}(r)\leq A_{\varepsilon}(\psi(r))^{-(N-1)+\varepsilon}\,,

where
A_{\varepsilon}=\frac{-u^{\prime}(r_{\varepsilon})}{(\psi(r_{\varepsilon}))^{-(N-1)+\varepsilon}}
is a positive constant. By a further integration, for any
r>r_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle-u(r)+u(r_{\varepsilon})\leq A_{\varepsilon}\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{r}(\psi(s))^{-(N-1)+\varepsilon}\,{\rm d}s\,.

By (
4.12
) we deduce that in both cases
\Lambda=+\infty
and
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
, the function

(\psi(s))^{-(N-1)+\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(r_{\varepsilon},+\infty)

provided that
\varepsilon<N-1
. This means that the right-hand side of (
4.15
) admits a finite limit as
r\to+\infty
and this is absurd since the left-hand side of (
4.15
) blows up to
+\infty
in view of (
4.10
).

\Box

Finally, we determine the exact asymptotic behaviour at infinity for solutions of (
2.2
) satisfying (
4.10
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.2
) and suppose that
u
satisfies (
4.10
). Then

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{-u(r)}}{\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}{\rm d}s}=\frac{1}{N-1}.

Consequently, the following decay estimate holds

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(r)}{\log\big(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}{\rm d}s\big)}=-1.

In particular if
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
then

(4.18)

\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{u(r)}{\log r}=-1

Proof.

By (
2.2
), Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.4
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}-\frac{e^{u(r)}\psi(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}=N-1\,.

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\bigg(\frac{1}{N-1}-\varepsilon\bigg)\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}<\big(e^{-u(r)}\big)^{\prime}<\bigg(\frac{1}{N-1}+\varepsilon\bigg)\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}\,.

Integrating from
r_{\varepsilon}
to
r
, for any
r>r_{\varepsilon}
we get

(4.19)

\displaystyle e^{-u(r_{\varepsilon})}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N-1}-\varepsilon\bigg)\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s<e^{-u(r)}<e^{-u(r_{\varepsilon})}+\bigg(\frac{1}{N-1}+\varepsilon\bigg)\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s.

Now, if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}
is integrable in a neighbourhood of infinity, we reach a contradiction with (
4.10
), therefore
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}
has to be not integrable in a neighbourhood of infinity and we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{e^{-u(r)}}{\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s}=\frac{1}{N-1}.

Then, (
4.16
) readily follows from the above limit by recalling (
A1
). The limit (
4.17
) follows from (
4.19
) with a similar argument.

It remains to prove (
4.18
) when
\Lambda\in(0,+\infty)
. We proceed by considering the limit:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\log\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s\right)}{\log r}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}\frac{r}{\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{r}{\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\cdot\Lambda=1\,,

where we used twice de l’Hôpital rule.

This proves that
\log\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s\right)\sim\log r
as
r\to+\infty
and the proof of (
4.18
) follows from (
4.17
).

\Box

At last, we provide a sufficient condition for (
4.10
) in terms of integrability properties of the ratio
\psi/\psi^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
u
be the unique solution of (
2.2
). Then the two alternatives hold:

(i)

if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
then

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)\in(-\infty,\alpha)\,;

(ii)

if
\frac{\psi}{\psi^{\prime}}\not\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
then

\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)=-\infty\,.

Proof.

The existence of the limit of
u
as
r\to+\infty
is known from Proposition
2.1
as well as the fact that this limit is less than
\alpha
. It remains to prove that the limit is finite in case
(i)
and
-\infty
in case
(ii)
.

Let us start with the proof of
(i)
. Suppose by contradiction that the limit is
-\infty
so that (
4.10
) holds true. Then we can apply Lemma
4.4
and proceed as in the proof of Lemma
4.5
to obtain (
4.19
). The integrability of
\psi/\psi^{\prime}
shows that
u
remains bounded as
r\to+\infty
, a contradiction.

Let us proceed with the proof of
(ii)
. Set
l_{1}:=\lim_{r\to+\infty}u(r)
. Suppose by contradiction that
l_{1}
is finite. We claim that

(4.20)

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\psi(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}=-\infty\,.

From the proof of Lemma
4.2
we know that the function
\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}
admits a limit as
r\to+\infty
, hence, since

e^{-u(r)}\to e^{-l_{1}}\in(0,+\infty)
as
r\to+\infty
, the limit in (
4.20
) exists and it belongs to
[-\infty,0]
, thanks to (
A1
), (
A2
) and Proposition
2.1
.

Let us denote by
l_{2}\leq 0
the limit in (
4.20
) and suppose by contradiction that it is finite.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

l_{2}-\varepsilon<\frac{\psi(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)}<l_{2}+\varepsilon\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{\varepsilon}

and from the left inequality, it follows that

0<\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}<(l_{2}-\varepsilon)u^{\prime}(r)\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{\varepsilon}\,.

Integrating the inequality above we obtain

0<\int_{r_{\varepsilon}}^{r}\frac{\psi(s)}{\psi^{\prime}(s)}\,{\rm d}s<(l_{2}-\varepsilon)[u(r)-u(r_{\varepsilon})]\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{\varepsilon}\,.

Passing to the limit as
r\to+\infty
and recalling that
l_{1}
is finite we infer that
\psi/\psi^{\prime}
is integrable at infinity, in contradiction with the assumption in
(ii)
. This completes the proof of (
4.20
).

Combining (
4.20
) and (
4.7
) with the fact that
l_{1}
is finite we deduce that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}=-(N-1)-\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{e^{u(r)}}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}=+\infty\,.

This implies that for any
M>0
there exists
r_{M}>0
such that

\frac{u^{\prime\prime}(r)}{u^{\prime}(r)}\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}>M\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{M}\,.

Multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
\psi^{\prime}(r)/\psi(r)
and integrating we obtain

\log\left(\frac{|u^{\prime}(r)|}{|u^{\prime}(r_{M})|}\right)>M\log\left(\frac{\psi(r)}{\psi(r_{M})}\right)\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{M}

from which it follows that

|u^{\prime}(r)|>\frac{|u^{\prime}(r_{M})|}{(\psi(r_{M}))^{M}}\,(\psi(r))^{M}\qquad\text{for any }r>r_{M}\,.

Passing to the limit as
r\to+\infty
and recalling (
4.11
), we conclude that
|u^{\prime}(r)|\to+\infty
as

in contradiction with Lemma
4.1
. This completes the proof of
(ii)
.

\Box

.

We start by stating an equivalent characterization of stability in the case of radial solutions of (
1.2
). Since the proof can be obtained by following the proof of
[

, Lemma 5.1]
with obvious changes we omit it.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
) and let

be a radial solution of (

). Then

is stable if and only if

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\chi^{\prime}(r))^{2}\,(\psi(r))^{N-1}\>{\rm d}r-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{u(r)}(\chi(r))^{2}\,(\psi(r))^{N-1}\>{\rm d}r\geq 0,

.

We now give the statement of a series of lemmas that will be exploited in the proofs of the main results about stability and intersection properties.

In the sequel, we denote by
u_{\alpha}
the unique solution of (
2.2
) with
\alpha=u_{\alpha}(0)
and we consider the set

(5.2)

\mathcal{S}:=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}:u_{\alpha}\text{ is stable}\}\,.

Before proceeding, we recall the variational characterization of the bottom of the

spectrum of
-\Delta_{g}
in
M
:

(5.3)

\lambda_{1}(M):=\inf_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}\varphi|_{g}^{2}\,dV_{g}}{\int_{M}\varphi^{2}\,dV_{g}}\,.

It is well known that under assumptions (
A1
)-(

) we have that
\lambda_{1}(M)>0
, see e.g.,

[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
. The bottom of the spectrum is involved in the stability of solutions
u_{\alpha}
of (
2.2
) for sufficiently small values of
\alpha
. Indeed, we prove:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(

) and let
u_{\alpha}
be a solution of (
2.2
) with
\alpha\leq\log(\lambda_{1}(M))
. Then

is stable and in particular, the set

is not empty.

Proof.

Using (

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}v|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(M)}{e^{\alpha}}\int_{M}e^{\alpha}v^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}(M)}{e^{\alpha}}\int_{M}e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}v^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}\qquad\text{for any }v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)

which gives the stability of

if

\Box

The next step is to prove that the set
\mathcal{S}

First, we state three preliminary lemmas; the fact that

Lemma 5.3

Let
\psi
satisfy (

)-(

be such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\alpha_{0}}\,\sup_{r\in[0,R]}\bigg|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\alpha}(\alpha,r)-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\alpha}(\alpha_{0},r)\bigg|=0.

Moreover for any
\alpha\in[a,b]
and

(5.5)

\begin{cases}-v^{\prime\prime}(r)-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}v^{\prime}(r)=e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}v(r)&\\
v(0)=1&\\
v^{\prime}(0)=0.&\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof can be obtained by proceeding along the lines of
[

with suitable changes concerning essentially the fact that the power nonlinearity is replaced here by the exponential nonlinearity. For the sake of completeness, we recall the main steps here below.

For any

and
\alpha\in[a,b]

\displaystyle w(r)=\frac{u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\text{ and }z(r)=w^{\prime}(r)

where

is the solution of problem (

.

Then

(5.6)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}(r)+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}z(r)=-\bigg(\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\bigg)\,.

For any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-\frac{u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}\,e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}+e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}w(r)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|}{|\alpha-\alpha_{0}|}|e^{\xi(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}|+e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}|w(r)|

where, by Lagrange Theorem,
\min\{u_{\alpha}(r),u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\}<\xi(r)<\max\{u_{\alpha}(r),u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\}
any
r\in[0,R]
.
Recalling that for any
\alpha
the functions
u_{\alpha}
are decreasing and using again Lagrange Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\bigg|\leq e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\,\frac{|u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|^{2}}{|\alpha-\alpha_{0}|}+e^{\alpha_{0}}|w(r)|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq|w(r)|\left\{e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\,|u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|+e^{\alpha_{0}}\right\}+e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\,|v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|\,|u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|.

Now, by continuous dependence, for any
\varepsilon>0
we may choose
\delta
small enough in such a way that
\sup_{r\in[0,R]}|u_{\alpha}(r)-u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)|<\varepsilon
and we obtain for

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\bigg|\leq(1+\varepsilon)e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}|w(r)|+C\varepsilon

.
Furthermore, observing that
w(0)=0

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{e^{u_{\alpha}(r)}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}-e^{u_{\alpha_{0}}(r)}v_{\alpha_{0}}(r)\bigg|\leq(1+\varepsilon)e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\int_{0}^{r}|z(s)|{\rm d}s+C\varepsilon.

Combining (
5.6

5.7

\displaystyle|z(r)|\leq K(1+\varepsilon)e^{\alpha_{0}+\delta}\int_{0}^{r}|z(s)|{\rm d}s+KC\varepsilon,

for any

and
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0}-\delta,\alpha_{0}+\delta)\cap[a,b]

Finally, exploiting standard Gronwall-type estimates we obtain

This proves the differentiability with respect to

and shows that the derivative with respect to

Using again the lower semicontinuity of the

-norm, we finally have

\displaystyle 1<\Lambda(M,\bar{\alpha})\leq\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}w|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}{\int_{M}e^{u_{\bar{\alpha}}}|w|^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}w_{k}|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}{\int_{M}e^{u_{\alpha_{k}}}|w_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}=\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Lambda(M,\alpha_{k}),

a contradiction. This ensures that
\Lambda(M,\alpha_{k})>1
definitively and concludes the proof.

\Box

Next we define the operator

Adapting in a non trivial way the proof of

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose that
\psi
satisfies (
A1
), (
A2
) and (
A5

(i)

\mathcal{L}z>0
in
(0,R)

(ii)

in

(iii)

\psi(r)z^{\prime}(r)=O(1)

with

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that there exists

(i)
-
(iii)

Z(r):=(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}}\qquad\text{for any }r>0

Z^{\prime}(r)=\lambda_{1}(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-1}\,\psi^{\prime}(r)\,,\qquad Z^{\prime\prime}(r)=\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-2}\,(\psi^{\prime}(r))^{2}+\lambda_{1}(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-1}\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}Z(r)=\lambda_{1}(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-1}\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-2}(\psi^{\prime}(r))^{2}\,P(\lambda_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=\lambda_{1}(\psi(r))^{\lambda_{1}-1}\,\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)<0

\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}>\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\psi^{\prime}(r)}\right)^{2}\,,\quad\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)>0\qquad\text{for any }r>0\,.

Indeed, the first inequality in (

\psi^{\prime\prime}
increasing and, in turn, by (A1) we finally obtain

(0,+\infty)

Now, combining
(i)
,
(ii)
and (

), we obtain for any

\displaystyle 0<\mathcal{(}\mathcal{L}z(r))Z(r)-\mathcal{(}\mathcal{L}Z(r))z(r)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(z^{\prime\prime}(r)+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\,z^{\prime}(r)\right)Z(r)-\left(Z^{\prime\prime}(r)+(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}\,Z^{\prime}(r)\right)z(r)

In particular, we have the function

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\left[(\psi(r))^{N-2+\lambda_{1}}\,z^{\prime}(r)\psi(r)-\lambda_{1}(\psi(r))^{N-2+\lambda_{1}}z(r)\psi^{\prime}(r)\right]=0

Lemma 5.12

Suppose that

We first observe that by (

\displaystyle W(r)=2\log(\psi^{\prime}(r))-u(r)-2\log(\psi(r))+\log[2(N-2)]\sim-2\log(\psi(r))\to+\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}(\psi(r))^{N-2+\lambda_{1}}\,W(r)=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{-2\log(\psi(r))}{(\psi(r))^{-(N-2+\lambda_{1})}}=\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\,\frac{2}{N-2+\lambda_{1}}\,(\psi(r))^{N-2+\lambda_{1}}=0

6.1.
Proof of Theorem

The proof of statements

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A2
). Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two distinct real numbers and let
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
be the corresponding solutions of (
2.2
), i.e.
u_{\alpha}(0)=\alpha
and
u_{\beta}(0)=\beta
. If
u_{\alpha}
and
u_{\beta}
are stable then they do not intersect.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\alpha>\beta
; we establish that
u_{\alpha}(r)>u_{\beta}(r)
for any
r>0
. If this is not true then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
u_{\alpha}(r_{0})\leq u_{\beta}(r_{0})
. Now using Lagrange Theorem and Lemma
5.3
we deduce that for some
\sigma\in(\beta,\alpha)
we have

\displaystyle v_{\sigma}(r_{0})=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\alpha}(\sigma,r_{0})=\frac{u_{\alpha}(r_{0})-u_{\beta}(r_{0})}{\alpha-\beta}\leq 0

and
v_{\sigma}(0)=1
. Hence there exists
R\in(0,r_{0}]
such that
v_{\sigma}(R)=0
. Then
v_{\sigma}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}v_{\sigma}=e^{u_{\sigma}}v_{\sigma}\text{ in }B_{R}\quad\text{ with }v_{\sigma}=0\text{ on }\partial B_{R}.

Multiplying this equation by
v_{\sigma}
and integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla_{g}v_{\sigma}|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}-\int_{B_{R}}e^{u_{\sigma}}v_{\sigma}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}=0.

Let
w_{\sigma}
be the trivial extension of
v_{\sigma}
to the whole
M
in such a way that
w_{\sigma}\in H^{1}(M)
and

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}w_{\sigma}|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}-\int_{M}e^{u_{\sigma}}w_{\sigma}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}=0.

Since
u_{\alpha}
is a stable solution and
\sigma<\alpha
, by Lemma
5.6
we deduce that
u_{\sigma}
is a stable solution too. This implies that

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in H^{1}(M)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla_{g}v|_{g}^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}{\int_{M}e^{u_{\sigma}}|v|^{2}\,{\rm d}V_{g}}\geq 1,

and
w_{\sigma}
attains the infimum. In particular,
w_{\sigma}
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}w_{\sigma}=e^{u\sigma}w_{\sigma}\text{ in }M.

Now by standard regularity theory
w_{\sigma}\in C^{2}(M)
and satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle-w_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}(r)-(N-1)\frac{\psi^{\prime}(r)}{\psi(r)}w_{\sigma}^{\prime}(r)=e^{u_{\sigma}(r)}w_{\sigma}(r)\text{ for any }r>0.

Moreover, by construction
w_{\sigma}(r)=0
for
r>R
and so, by unique continuation, we conclude that
w_{\sigma}\equiv 0
in
M
. This is a contradiction.

\Box

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\psi
satisfy (
A1
)-(
A3
). If
u\in C^{2}(M)
is an unstable solution of (
2.2
), then there exists no solution
v\in C^{2}(M)
of (
2.2
) such that
v>u
in M.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a solution
v\in C^{2}(M)
of (
2.2
) such that
v>u
. Let
w=v-u
, so

in

. Using the Lagrange Theorem we have

\displaystyle-\Delta_{g}w=e^{v}-e^{u}\geq e^{u}w\text{ in }M\,.

Now taking
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and multiplying in the above inequality by
\frac{\varphi^{2}}{w}
, integrating by parts and using Cauchy Schwarz and Young inequalities, we infer

\displaystyle\int_{M}e^{u}\varphi^{2}{\rm d}V_{g}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{M}\left\langle\nabla_{g}w,\nabla_{g}\left(\frac{\varphi^{2}}{w}\right)\right\rangle_{g}\,{\rm d}V_{g}

Appendix: some (well) known facts in the Euclidean case

Consider the equation

(6.1)

-\Delta u=e^{u}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

and let
u
be a radial regular solution of (
6.1
). Letting
\alpha=u(0)
, then
u
solves (
2.2
) with
\psi(r)=r
. Following
[
21
]
, we consider the function
v(r)=u(r)+2\log r-\log[2(N-2)]
(i.e., (
5.12
) with
\psi(r)=r
) which satisfies the equation

-v^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{N-1}{r}\,v^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2(N-2)}{r^{2}}[e^{v(r)}-1]\quad(r>0)\,.

Now, let
w(t)=v(e^{t})
so that
w
solves the autonomous equation

(6.2)

w^{\prime\prime}(t)+(N-2)w^{\prime}(t)+2(N-2)[e^{w(t)}-1]=0\quad(t\in\mathbb{R})\,.

Following
[
21
]
one can reduce equation (
6.2
) into an
autonomous
system in the plane
(y,z)
admitting the unique stationary point
(0,0)
where we put
z(t)=w(t)
and
y(t)=w^{\prime}(t)
. Clearly, the system is given by

(6.3)

\begin{cases}y^{\prime}(t)=-(N-2)y(t)-2(N-2)[e^{z(t)}-1]\,,\\[5.0pt]
z^{\prime}(t)=y(t)\,.\end{cases}

The behavior and, in turn, the stability of radial solutions to (
6.1
) depend on the nature of the stationary point
(0,0)
of (
6.3
) and, in particular, after linearization at
(0,0)
, on the nature of the eigenvalues of the matrix

(6.4)

\begin{pmatrix}-(N-2)&-2(N-2)\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

We observe that the characteristic polynomial of the matrix (
6.4
) is exactly the polynomial
P
given in (
5.16
). For
3\leq N\leq 9
it admits two complex conjugate eigenvalues while for
N\geq 10
it admits two negative eigenvalues which are coinciding if
N=10
and distinct if
N\geq 11
:

\lambda_{1}=\frac{-(N-2)-\sqrt{(N-2)(N-10)}}{2}\,,\ \ \ \lambda_{2}=\frac{-(N-2)+\sqrt{(N-2)(N-10)}}{2}\quad(N\geq 10)\,.

The above computations highlight a change in the nature of solutions when passing from low (
N\leq 9
) to high dimensions (
N\geq 10
). Starting from this observation, see e.g.,
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
, it can be proved that radial smooth solutions intersect themselves infinitely many times if
3\leq N\leq 9
and do not intersect if
N\geq 10
. Furthermore, for
N\leq 9
all regular solutions of (
6.1
) are unstable while for
N\geq 10
radial regular solutions are stable, see
[
15
]
and reference therein.

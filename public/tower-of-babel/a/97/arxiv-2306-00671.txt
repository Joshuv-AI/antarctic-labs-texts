On the Log Abundance for Compact Kähler threefolds II
Omprokash Das, Wenhao Ou

Abstract
. In this article we show that if ( X , Δ ) (X,\Delta) is a log canonical compact Kähler threefold pair such that K X + Δ K_{X}+\Delta is nef of numerical dimension ν ⁡ ( X , K X + Δ ) = 2 \nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=2 , then K X + Δ K_{X}+\Delta is semi-ample. This result combined with our previous work shows that the log abundance holds for log canonical compact Kähler threefold pairs.



1.
Introduction

The Minimal Model Program (MMP) is an important tool in birational classifications of projective varieties
Z
.
When
\operatorname{dim}Z=3
, the MMP was fully established in the 90’s, and one has the following classification.
For a smooth projective threefold, there is a mildly singular birational model
Z\dashrightarrow X
, such that
either there is a Fano fibration
X\to Y
,
or the canonical class
K_{X}
is nef.
In the second case, the canonical class
K_{X}
induces a fibration
X\to Y
, thanks to the following theorem, known as the abundance theorem (see
[
Miy87
,
Miy88b
,
Miy88a
]
and
[
Kaw92
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a projective threefold with terminal singularities.
If
K_{X}
is nef, then it is semi-ample.

We note that a threefold may be singular throughout this paper.
The MMP was extended to the category of compact Kähler threefolds in
[
HP16
]
and
[
HP15
]
.
Particularly, we can recover the similar bimeromorphic classification.
When
Z
is a smooth compact Kähler threefold, there is a mildly singular bimeromorphic model
Z\dashrightarrow X
such that
either there is a Fano fibration
X\to Y
, or the canonical class
K_{X}
is nef.

It is natural to expect Theorem
1.1
holds for Kähler threefolds.
Before proceeding further, we would like to explain the outline of the complete proof of the abundance theorem for projective threefolds. Assume that
X
is a projective threefolds with mild singularities, and that the canonical divisor
K_{X}
is nef. If the Kodaira dimension of
X
satisfies
\kappa(K_{X})=3
, then the semi-ampleness of
K_{X}
follows from the Kawamata-Shokurov Base-point free theorem.
If
1\leq\kappa(K_{X})\leq 2
, then we let
f:X\dashrightarrow Z
be the Iitaka fibration of
K_{X}
.
In this case, by an argument of Kawamata,
the semi-ampleness of
K_{X}
follows from the MMP and the abundance theorem on general fibers of
f
, which in our case are either a curves or surfaces.
We refer to the proofs of
[
Kaw85
, Theorem 7.3]
and
[
DW19
, Theorem 3.1]
for these lines of arguments.
It remains to study the following two problems.
The first one is the non-vanishing theorem, i.e.
H^{0}(X,mK_{X})\neq 0
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. This was settled by Miyaoka in
[
Miy88a
]
.
The last one is to deal with the situation when
\kappa(K_{X})=0
.
We discuss according to the numerical dimension
\nu(K_{X})
.
If
\nu(K_{X})=3
, then it follows that
\kappa(K_{X})=3
,
which contradicts the assumption.
If
1\leq\nu(K_{X})\leq 2
, then
Miyaoka and Kawamata showed that
\kappa(K_{X})\geq 1
(see
[
Miy88b
,
Kaw92
]
, or
[
Kol92
, Chapter 13 and 14]
).
Thus we can only have
\nu(K_{X})=0
, and the abundance theorem was proved by Nakayama in this case, see
[
Nak04
, Corollary V.4.6]
.

Apart from the non-vanishing theorem, the most difficult part of the abundance theorem is to show that the condition
\nu(K_{X})=2
implies that
\kappa(K_{X})\geq 1
. We will see below that this is even a bigger problem when
X
is just assumed to be compact Kähler.

Now we assume that
X
is a compact Kähler variety of dimension
3
with mild singularities and the canonical bundle
K_{X}
if nef. To prove the abundance conjecture on
X
, we will follow the same general strategy as above. In this case the non-vanishing theorem is proved by Demailly and Peternell in
[
DP03
]
. If
\kappa(K_{X})=3
, then
X
is projective by Moishezon, and we are done by the projective case. When
1\leq\kappa(K_{X})\leq 2
, the semi-ampleness of
K_{X}
is proved in
[
CHP16
]
. The case when
0\leq\nu(K_{X})\leq 1
is also proved in
[
CHP16
]
. The remaining case when
\nu(K_{X})=2
is what we will deal with in this article.
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a lc pair such that
X
is a compact Kähler threefold.
Assume that
K_{X}+\Delta
is nef of numerical dimension
2
.
Then it is semi-ample.

We follow the same strategy as in
[
Kol92
, Chapter 14]
; we note that this is also the same strategy followed by
[
CHP16
,
CHP23
]
. This method involves two technical tools:

(1)

define an orbifold type second Chern class on compact Kähler threefolds with klt singularities, and prove a Bogomolov-Gieseker (BG) type inequality on it.

(2)

compare orbifold type second Chern classes with usual second Chern classes (on a desingularization).

When
X
is projective, an orbifold type second Chern class is defined in Chapter 10 of
[
Kol92
]
via
\mathbb{Q}
-sheaves,
and the related BG type inequality on surfaces with quotient singularities is also proved in the same chapter. Then in dimension
3
, the BG type inequality is proved by taking a hyperplane section and reducing it to the case of surfaces.
In the setting of general Kähler varieties, these techniques are not available and the difficulty of proving the BG type inequality is well known.

Once a BG type inequality is established, the key arguments of proving the abundance theorem from this inequality (in the projective case) is explained in
[
Kol92
, Lemmas 14.3.1-14.3.3]
. One crucial observation is that, in all lemmas of
[
Kol92
]
, the orbifold second Chern class is always represented by an 1-cycle in the Chow group, and this is possible because on a projective orbifold every orbifold coherent sheaf has a
finite
locally free resolution, see Definition 10.4, Lemma 10.5 and Definition 10.6 of
[
Kol92
]
.
Note that this is no longer true on compact analytic orbifolds.
In fact, there are compact Kähler manifolds on which some coherent sheaf doesn’t have a finite locally free resolution, see
[
Voi02a
]
.
Without the cycle representation of the orbifold second Chern class,
we need new tools to compare it to the usual second Chern class on a desingularization of the orbifold, which greatly hinders our ability to adopt the arguments of the proof of
[
Kol92
, Lemmas 14.3.1-14.3.3]
.
We dedicate Section
4
of our article dealing with this problem.
Additionally, we explain the arguments of
[
Kol92
, Chapter 14]
in details with our new tools in Section
6
.

Here we would like to emphasize that, in
[
CHP23
]
, the authors claim that a proof of the BG type inequality on compact Kähler threefolds with klt singularities immediately implies the abundance theorem for the case of
\nu(K_{X})=2
. However, we could not follow their argument.
We believe that the tools to compare the two types of Chern classes as in
[
Kol92
, Lemmas 14.3.1-14.3.3]
are indispensable for the complete proof of abundance theorem.

We also note that the conjectural solution suggested in
[
CHP23
]
is to first prove a BG type inequality on a compact Kähler threefold with klt singularities.
However, we take a different approach here.
For the specific compact Kähler threefold
X
with klt singularities we work with, we first construct a Kähler orbifold
Y
and a bimeromorphic morphism
\pi:Y\to X
with nice properties (see Lemma
6.12
), and then we use Faulk’s orbifold BG type inequality (see
[
Fau22
]
) on
Y
.

Inspired by the techniques developed in this article, seven months after,
the second author proved the BG type inequality on klt compact Kähler varieties of arbitrary dimensions in
[
Ou24
]
.
Another four months after, Guenancia and Pǎun proved the BG type inequality on klt compact Kähler threefolds by a different method in
[
GP24
]
.
Nevertheless, as mentioned above, we believe our article is still relevant, for its new tools on comparison of Chern classes.

Combining the main theorem of
[
DO24
]
with the theorem above, we complete the log abundance for log canonical Kähler threefolds.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a lc pair such that
X
is a compact Kähler threefold.
If
K_{X}+\Delta
is nef,
then it is semi-ample.

As an application of the abundance theorem,
we have the following corollary, as shown in
[
CHP16
, Theorem 1.2]
.
It is one of the steps towards the three dimensional Kodaira problem in
[
Lin17
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a simple compact Kähler threefold with klt singularities.
Then
X
is bimeromorphic to a quotient
Y/G
, where
Y
is a complex torus and
G
is a finite group acting on
Y
.

This article is organized in the following manner.
After reviewing some preliminaries in Section
2
, we will recall the notion of complex orbifolds and prove an orbifold Miyaoka inequality in Section
3
.
In Section
4
, we introduce relative Chern classes, which
compare orbifold Chern classes and usual Chern classes on a desingularization.
We also show that relative second Chern classes can be computed by reducing to surfaces.
In Section
5
, we prove some positivity results on second Chern classes of cotangent sheaves.
With these in hand, we complete the proof of the main theorem in the last three sections.

2.
Preliminaries

We will fix some notation and recall some elementary results in this section.

2.1.
Complex analytic varieties

A
complex analytic variety

is a reduced and irreducible complex space.
For a complex analytic variety
X
, we will denote by
X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}
its smooth locus and by
X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
its singular locus.
We also call a smooth complex analytic variety a complex manifold.
A pair
(X,\Delta)
consists of a complex analytic variety
X
and a divisor
\Delta
with rational coefficients.

When
X
is a normal complex analytic variety and
B
a reduced Weil divisor on
X
, we define
\Omega^{[1]}_{X}(\log\,B)
, the reflexive logarithmic cotangent sheaf as follows.
Let
r\colon Y\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,B)
and let
\Gamma=r_{*}^{-1}B
.
Then the logarithmic cotangent bundle
\Omega_{Y}^{1}(\log\,\Gamma)
is well defined.
We define
\Omega^{[1]}_{X}(\log B):=(r_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{1}(\log\,\Gamma))^{**}
.
We note that such a definition is independent of the resolution
Y
.

A normal complex analytic variety
X
is called
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial if every Weil divisor is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and
(\omega_{X}^{\otimes m})^{**}
is invertible for some integer
m>0
,
where
\omega_{X}:=(\bigwedge^{\operatorname{dim}X}\Omega_{X}^{1})^{**}
is the canonical sheaf.
To mimic the situation of algebraic varieties,
we regard
\omega_{X}
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor and denote it by
K_{X}
.
Then we can consider the sum
aK_{X}+\Delta
for any
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta
and any rational number
a
.
For an integer
l
,
we say that
l(aK_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier if
la
is an integer,
l\Delta
is integral and the reflexive sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{X}(laK_{X}+l\Delta):=(\omega_{X}^{\otimes la}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(l\Delta))^{**}

is invertible.
If
S
is a subvariety in
X
, we use the following convention for the restriction
(aK_{X}+\Delta)|_{S}
of
aK_{X}+\Delta
on
S
.
For any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
F
on
S
,
we write
(aK_{X}+\Delta)|_{S}\equiv F

(resp.
(aK_{X}+\Delta)|_{S}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}F
)
if for some integer
l>0
, both

l(aK_{X}+\Delta)
and
lF
are Cartier, and
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(laK_{X}+l\Delta)|_{S})=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(lF))
(resp.
\mathcal{O}_{X}(laK_{X}+l\Delta)|_{S}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}(lF)
).

Following
[
Gra62
]
, a complex analytic variety
X
is called
Kähler
,
if there exists a closed positive
(1,1)
-form on
X
such that the following holds: for every point
x\in X
, there exists an open neighborhood
x\in U
, a closed embedding
U\to V
into an open subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and a strictly plurisubharmonic
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\omega|_{U\cap X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}=(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}f)|_{U\cap X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}
.
We also refer to
[
Dem85
, Section 1]
for general
(p,q)
-forms on analytic varieties.

For a normal compact Kähler variety
(X,\omega)
, a class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is called
nef
if it can be represented by a smooth form
\alpha
with local potentials such that
for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
f_{\epsilon}
such that
\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}f_{\epsilon}\geq-\epsilon\omega
.
For more explanations on the notation, we refer to
[
DO24
, Definition 1.3]
and the references therein.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper surjective morphism of normal complex analytic varieties.
A divisor
D
is called
f
-
exceptional
if
\operatorname{codim}_{Y}f(\operatorname{Supp}(D))\geq 2
.
The following lemma is a consequence of Hironaka’s flattening theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper surjective morphism between compact complex normal analytic varieties.
Then there exist projective bimeromorphic morphisms
g\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
and
g^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to X
with the following commutative diagram, such that

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{Y}

(1)

Y^{\prime}
is smooth, and

(2)

for any proper bimeromorphic morphism
\varphi\colon W\to X^{\prime}
, every
(f^{\prime}\circ\varphi)
-exceptional divisor is also
(g^{\prime}\circ\varphi)
-exceptional.

Proof.

By
[
Hir75
, Corollary 1]
, there is a projective bimeromorphic morphism
Y_{1}\to Y
such that the natural morphism
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to Y_{1}
is flat, where
X_{1}
is the main component of
X\times_{Y}Y_{1}
.
In particular,
f_{1}
is equidimensional.
Let
Y^{\prime}\to Y_{1}
be a desingularization, and let
X^{\prime}
be the normalization of the main component of
X_{1}\times_{Y_{1}}Y^{\prime}
.
Then the induced morphism
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
is also equidimensional. Let
D\subseteq X^{\prime}
be a prime Weil divisor. Since
f^{\prime}
is equidimensional, either
f^{\prime}(D)=Y^{\prime}
or
f^{\prime}(D)
is a prime Weil divisor on
Y^{\prime}
. In particular, for any proper bimeromorphic morphism
\varphi\colon W\to X^{\prime}
, every
(f^{\prime}\circ\varphi)
-exceptional divisor is in fact
\varphi
-exceptional, and hence it is
(g^{\prime}\circ\varphi)
-exceptional. This completes our proof.
∎

2.2.
Index-one cover

Let
X
be a normal variety and
\mathcal{L}
a reflexive sheaf of rank
1
. Then
\mathcal{L}
is called
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
, if there is a positive integer
m>0
such that
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}
is an invertible sheaf.
The smallest such
m
is called the
Cartier index
of
\mathcal{L}
.

Let
(o\in X)
be a germ of normal complex analytic variety, and
\mathcal{L}
a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier reflexive sheaf of rank 1 on
X
.
Let
m>0
be an integer such that
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}
is locally free.
Shrinking
X
, we assume that there is an isomorphism
\varphi\colon(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Such an isomorphism induces a cyclic cover
f\colon Y\to X
,
which is a finite surjective morphism of degree
m
of normal varieties,
such that
(f^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
Furthermore,
f
is only branched over the locus where
\mathcal{L}
is not locally free, and hence is étale in codimension one.
When
m
is the Cartier index of
\mathcal{L}
, the morphism
f
is called the
index-one cover
of
\mathcal{L}
.
For more explanations, we refer to
[
KM98
, Definition 2.52]
.
In the next two lemmas, we prove some universal properties on cyclic covers.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the notation above, up to isomorphism,
f
is locally unique in the following sense.
If
\varphi^{\prime}\colon(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
is another isomorphism, and if
f^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to X
the the cyclic cover induced by
\varphi^{\prime}
,
then up to shrinking
X
around
o
, there is an isomorphism
Y\cong Y^{\prime}
over
X
.

Proof.

There is some nowhere vanishing holomorphic function
u
on
X
such that
\varphi^{\prime}=u\cdot\varphi
.
Shrinking
X
if necessary, we may assume that
u
has a
m
-th root
v
.
We set
\mathcal{L}^{[i]}=(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes i})^{**}
for all integer
i
.
Then from the construction of
[
KM98
, Definition 2.52]
,
we see that
Y=\mathrm{Spec}_{X}\,\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{L}^{[-i]}
such that the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra structure on
\bigoplus_{0\leq i<m}\mathcal{L}^{[-i]}
is given by

\mathcal{L}^{[-i]}\times\mathcal{L}^{[-j]}\overset{\varphi^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{L}^{[-i-j+m]}.

If we denote this algebra by
\mathcal{A}
, and the one corresponding to
Y^{\prime}
by
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, then the multiplication by
v^{-1}
on
\mathcal{L}
induces an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
This proves that
Y\cong Y^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a reflexive sheaf of rank 1 on a pointed normal complex analytic variety
(o\in X)
.
Let
m,n,d
be positive integers such that
n=dm
and that
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
If
f\colon Y\to X
and
g\colon Z\to X
are the cyclic covers defined as above, with of degree
m
and
n
respectively,
then, by shrinking
X
around
o
if necessary,
Z
is isomorphic to a disjoint union of copies of
Y
.

In particular, every such kind of cyclic cover is locally isomorphic to a disjoint union of copies of the index-one cover of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

We denote by
\varphi\colon(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m})^{**}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\gamma\colon(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes n})^{**}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

the isomorphisms.
Then there is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function
u
on
X
such that

\gamma=u\cdot\varphi^{d}
.
If
u=1
, then we see that
Z
is just the disjoint union of
d
copies of
Y
.

In general, shrinking
X
if necessary, we may assume that
u
has a
d
-th root
v
.
Then we consider the isomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}=v\cdot\varphi
.
It induces a cyclic cover
Y^{\prime}\to X
which is isomorphic to
Y
by Lemma
2.2
.
We notice that
(\varphi^{\prime})^{d}=\gamma
.
Hence
Z
is just a disjoint union of copies of
Y^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

An integral Weil divisor
\Delta
on
(o\in X)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier if and only if the reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\Delta)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
If it is the case, then the Cartier index of
\Delta
is equal to the one of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\Delta)
.
We define the index-one cover of
\Delta
over
(o\in X)
as the index-one cover
f\colon Y\to X
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\Delta)
.
Then the integral divisor
f^{*}\Delta
is Cartier.

2.3.
MMP for complex analytic varieties

Since the log canonical abundance is known for projective threefolds (see
[
Kol92
]
and
[
KMM94
]
),
in this paper, we mainly focus on compact Kähler threefolds which are not algebraic.
Particularly, if
X
is a uniruled non-algebraic compact Kähler threefold with
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial klt singularities,
then the base of the MRC fibration has dimension
2
by
[
DO24
, Lemma 1.19]
.
The following theorems follow from
[
DO24
, Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold dlt pair.
Assume that
X
is non-algebraic and that the base of the MRC fibration for
X
has dimension
2
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D\geq 0
for some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
;

(2)

K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective;

(3)

(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot F\geq 0
for a general fiber
F
of the MRC fibration of
X
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold dlt pair.
If
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective, then it is
\mathbb{Q}
-effective.

The MMP for a three-dimensional compact Kähler pair
(X,\Delta)
was studied in
[
HP16
]
,
[
HP15
]
,
[
CHP16
]
,
[
DO24
]
,
[
DH20
]
and
[
DHP22
]
.
We summarize the following statement.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a dlt pair such that
X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold.
Then the cone theorem, the contraction theorem and the existence of flips hold for
(X,\Delta)
.
Furthermore, any
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP terminates.

Proof.

If
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudoeffective, then
the cone theorem follows from
[
DO24
, Theorem 2.26]
;
if
K_{X}+\Delta
is not pseudoeffective, then
the cone theorem is established in
[
DHP22
, Theorem 5.2]
.
The contraction theorem follows from
[
DHP22
, Corollary 5.3 and Corollary 5.6]
.
For the existence of flips, we refer to
[
CHP16
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Finally, for the termination of flips, see
[
DO24
, Theorem 1.12]
.
∎

We can now introduce the following special MMP (see also
[
KMM94
, Lemma 5.1]
for projective threefolds).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a dlt pair such that
X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold.
Suppose that
K_{X}+\Delta
is not nef,
and that there is an effective divisor
H
such that
K_{X}+\Delta+H
is nef.
Then a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP, trivial with respect to
K_{X}+\Delta+H
, is a sequence of bimeromorphic maps

(X,\Delta)=(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{i},\Delta_{i})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{n},\Delta_{n}),

such that each
(X_{i},\Delta_{i})
is dlt, and that the following properties hold:

(1)

For each

(2)

Proof.

2.4.
Holomorphic foliations

Let

A

of the reflexive tangent sheaf

For a dominant morphism

the pullback foliation

in

via the differential map of

Assume that
X
is a complex manifold and that
\mathcal{F}
a holomorphic foliation on

.
We define

K_{\mathcal{F}}:=(\det\mathcal{F})^{*}

and use the following notation.
For a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

3.
Miayoka’s inequality on complex orbifolds

The goal of this section is to prove a Miyaoka type inequality on compact Kähler threefolds with quotient singularities, see Proposition
3.12
.
Complex analytic varieties with quotient singularities are closely related to complex orbifolds.
Hence, in this section, we will first recall the notion of complex orbifolds and orbifold coherent sheaves.
Then we will introduce blowups of complex orbifolds, which enable us to reduce the study of the second Chern class of orbifold torsion-free sheaves to the one of orbifold vector bundles.
With these tools in hand, we can then prove the main result of the section.

3.1.
Complex orbifolds

Smooth (
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
) orbifolds or V-manifolds were first introduced in
[
Sat56
]
and later on were studied in various contexts.
A complex orbifold is a smooth orbifold that every local chart is a complex manifold and every local group action is holomorphic.
We briefly recall its definition by adapting the notion of
[
CR02
, Section 4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We take the following definitions.

(1)

Let
U
be a connected Hausdorff space. An
orbifold chart
(or a
uniformization system
) of
U
is a triple
(\widetilde{U},G,\pi)
such that
\widetilde{U}
is an open domain in some
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, that
G
is a finite group acting holomorphically on
\widetilde{U}
, and that
\pi\colon\widetilde{U}\to U
is a continuous map inducing a homeomorphism from
\widetilde{U}/G
to
U
.
We denote by
\mathrm{ker}\,G\subseteq G
the maximal subgroup acting trivially on
U
. The chart is called
effective
if
\mathrm{ker}\,G
is the trivial subgroup.
It is called
standard
if the action of
G
is free in codimension one.

(2)

Let
i\colon U^{\prime}\hookrightarrow U
be an open subset, and
(\widetilde{U}^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
an orbifold chart of
U^{\prime}
.
An
injection
(or embedding) from
(\widetilde{U}^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
to
(\widetilde{U},G,\pi)
consists of an open embedding
\varphi\colon\widetilde{U}^{\prime}\to\widetilde{U}
and a group monomorphism
\lambda\colon G^{\prime}\to G
such that
\varphi
is equivariant with respect to
\lambda
, that
\lambda
induces an isomorphism from
\mathrm{ker}\,G^{\prime}
to
\mathrm{ker}\,G
, and that
i\circ\pi^{\prime}=\pi\circ\varphi
.

(3)

Let
X
be a connected second countable Hausdorff space. An
orbifold atlas
on
X
is a collection
\mathcal{U}=\{(\widetilde{U},G,\pi)\}
of orbifold charts of open subsets of
X
which covers
X
and is compatible in the following sense.
For any two orbifold charts
(\widetilde{U},G,\pi)
and
(\widetilde{U}^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{U}
,
of open subsets
U
and
U^{\prime}
respectively, for any point
x\in U\cap U^{\prime}
, there is an open neighborhood
V
of
x
with an orbifold chart
(\widetilde{V},H,\rho)
such that there are injections from
(\widetilde{V},H,\rho)
to
(\widetilde{U},G,\pi)
and
(\widetilde{U}^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
.

(4)

An orbifold atlas
\mathcal{U}
on
X
is a
refinement
of another one
\mathcal{V}
if every orbifold chart of
\mathcal{U}
admits an injection into an orbifold chart of
\mathcal{V}
.

(5)

Two orbifold atlas on
X
are
equivalent
if they admit a common refinement.
An orbifold atlas is
maximal
if it contains any atlas equivalent to it.
From Zorn’s lemma, we see that every equivalent class of orbifold atlas contains a unique maximal element.

(6)

A complex orbifold is a connected second countable Hausdorff space
X
equipped with an equivalent class of orbifold atlas.
We write
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
or simply
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
for such an orbifold structure with an orbifold atlas
\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
.
The underlying spcae
X
is called the
quotient space
of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
The dimension of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
is the complex dimension of any orbifold chart.

(7)

A complex orbifold is called
effective
or
reduced
, if every chart is effective.
It is called
standard
if every chart is standard.

(8)

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
be a complex orbifold.
An
orbifold subvariety

Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
is defined by a collection
\{W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\}
of
G_{i}
-invariant subvarieties which is compatible on the overlaps, in the following sense.
If there are injections of orbifold charts
\varphi_{i}\colon V\to V_{i}
and
\varphi_{j}\colon V\to V_{j}
, then
\varphi_{i}^{-1}(W_{i})=\varphi_{j}^{-1}(W_{j}).

The subvariety
Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
is called smooth if every
W_{i}
is a submanifold of
V_{i}
.
When
Z_{orb}
is smooth with irreducible image
Z
in
X
, we obtain an induced orbifold structure
Z_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(W_{i},G_{i})\}
.

(9)

An orbifold differential form
\sigma
on an orbifold
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
is given by a collection
\{\sigma_{i}\}
of
G_{i}
-invariant differential forms on the collection of charts
\{V_{i}\}
which is compatible on the overlaps.

In
[
Sat56
, Theorem 1]
, Satake proved the de Rham isomorphism theorem for effective oriented

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
orbifolds, in particular for effective complex orbifolds: for every integer
p\geq 0
, there is a natural isomorphism

(3.1)

H^{p}_{\rm dR}(X_{\mathrm{orb}},\mathbb{R})\cong H^{p}(X,\mathbb{R}).

Remark 3.2
.

If
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
is a non effective orbifold, then there is a canonically induced effective orbifold structure
X_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}=\{(V_{i},G^{\prime}_{i})\}
, where
G_{i}^{\prime}=G_{i}/\mathrm{ker}\,G_{i}
.
We note that an orbifold differential form on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an orbifold differential form on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}
, and vice versa.
Therefore, the isomorphism (
3.1
) still holds for non effective orbifold.

Definition 3.3
.

We also use the following notions of orbifold vector bundles and Hermitian metrics, etc.
Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
be a complex orbifold, possibly non effective.

(1)

An orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
is a collection of holomorphic
G_{i}
-linearized vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{i}
on
V_{i}
which is compatible along the overlaps, in the following sense.
For any chart
(V,G,\pi)
in the maximal atlas of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
, there is a
G
-linearized vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
V
satisfying the following properties.
If
(V,G)
is equal to some
(V_{i},G_{i})
, then
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{i}
.
If there is an injection
\varphi\colon(V,G,\pi)\to(V^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})
, then there is an isomorphism
\Psi_{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{E}\cong\varphi^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, if
\iota\colon(V^{\prime},G^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\to(V^{\prime\prime},G^{\prime\prime},\pi^{\prime\prime})
is another injection, then we have
\Psi_{\iota\circ\varphi}=(\varphi^{*}\Psi_{\iota})\circ\Psi_{\varphi}
.

(2)

An orbifold coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
is defined in the same manner.

(3)

An orbifold coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is called torsion-free (resp. reflexive) if every data
\mathcal{E}_{i}
on
V_{i}
is so.

(4)

For an orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, a Hermitian metric
h_{\mathrm{orb}}
on
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is a collection
\{h_{i}\}
of
G_{i}
-invariant Hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{E}_{i}
which are compatible on the overlap.

(5)

For a Hermitian orbifold vector bundle
(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}},h_{\mathrm{orb}})
,
we can define the orbifold Chern classes
\hat{c}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})
using the curvature tensors
c_{i}(h_{\mathrm{orb}})
, which are automatically orbifold differential forms.
They are uniquely determined in the orbifold de Rham cohomology groups of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
, hence by (
3.1
) in the singular cohomology groups of
X
. We note that this does not depend on the choice of metrics.

(6)

The complex orbifold
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
is called
Kähler
if there is a closed positive orbifold
(1,1)
-form
\omega
.
A cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is called an orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
if there is an orbifold Kähler form
\omega
whose cohomology class is
\alpha
.

Remark 3.4
.

Complex orbifolds are closely related to complex analytic varieties with quotient singularities.
We summarize some properties as follows.

(1)

From
[
Car54
, Théorème 1]
, we see that the
quotient space
X
of an effective complex orbifold
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
has a natural structure of complex analytic variety, with quotient singularities.
Furthermore, if
(V_{i},G_{i})
is an orbifold chart of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
, then the quotient map
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to\pi_{i}(V_{i})
is holomorphic.
The holomorphic functions on any open subset
U\subseteq\pi_{i}(V_{i})
are exactly the
G_{i}
-invariant holomorphic functions on
\pi_{i}^{-1}(U)\subseteq V_{i}
.

(2)

Conversely, given a complex analytic variety
X
of dimension
n
with quotient singularities, it admits a unique standard orbifold structure after
[
Pri67
, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2]
.
We call it the
standard orbifold structure
on
X
.

(3)

A same quotient space
X
may have non isomorphic orbifold structures. For example, we take
X=\mathbb{C}
.
Let
(\mathbb{C},\{\mathrm{Id}\},\pi_{1})
and
(\mathbb{C},\{\mathrm{Id},-\mathrm{Id}\},\pi_{2})
be two orbifold charts of
X
, where
\pi_{1}
is the identity map and
\pi_{2}
is the map sending a complex number
z
to
z^{2}
.
Then these two charts induces different orbifold structures on
X
.

(4)

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
be an effective complex orbifold and
X
its quotient space.
Assume that
Y
is a complex analytic space and there are
G_{i}
-equivariant morphisms
q_{i}\colon V_{i}\to Y
,
where the actions of the
G_{i}
’s on
Y
are trivial.
Assume that the
q_{i}
’s are compatible on the overlap.
Then they induces a holomorphic map
f\colon X\to Y
.
Indeed, by the definition of topological quotient spaces, the map is well-defined and continuous.
To prove that it is holomorphic, it is enough to show that for any local holomorphic function
\varphi
on
Y
, the function
\varphi\circ f
is holomorphic.
By definition, each
\varphi\circ q_{i}
is
G_{i}
-invariant and holomorphic.
Hence we can conclude by (1) of Remark
3.4
.

(5)

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
be a standard complex orbifold, and let
X
be its quotient space.
Then there is a correspondence between reflexive coherent sheaves
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and orbifold reflexive sheaves
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Indeed, if
\mathcal{E}
is a reflexive coherent sheaf on
X
, then the reflexive pullbacks
(\pi_{i}^{*}\mathcal{E})^{**}
define an orbifold reflexive sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is a
G_{i}
-equivariant reflexive coherent sheaf on
V_{i}
, then the
G_{i}
-invariant pushforward
((\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i})^{G_{i}}
is a reflexive sheaf on
\pi_{i}(V_{i})\subseteq X
, see for example
[
GKKP11
, Lemma A.4]
.

(6)

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
be a complex orbifold and
X
its quotient space.
Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
of rank one such that
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes b})^{**}
is a line bundle of some
b>0
.
We can define the (
\mathbb{Q}
-)Chern class
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
as
b^{-1}c_{1}(\mathcal{N})
.
In addition, we can defined the orbifold first Chern class
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}
as follows.
We let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be the orbifold reflexive coherent sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
defined by
(\pi_{i}^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**}
, which must be an orbifold line bundle, see for example
[
OSS11
, Chapter 2, Lemma 1.1.15]
.
Then we define
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L})=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{orb}}).

We claim that

b\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=c_{1}(\mathcal{N})=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{N})=b\cdot\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R}),

where we identify
\mathcal{N}
with the orbifold line bundle on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
induced by it.
The first equality is by definition, and the last one follows from the fact that
((\pi_{i}^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**})^{\otimes b}\cong\pi_{i}^{*}\mathcal{N}
.
To see the equality in the middle, we first note that, locally on
X
, the line bundle
\mathcal{N}
is trivial and we can embed
X
into some affine space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.
Thanks to a partition of the unity,
we can hence construct a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{N}|_{X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}
, which extends to an orbifold Hermitian metric on the orbifold line bundle induced by
\mathcal{N}
.
It follows that
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{N})|_{X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}=c_{1}(\mathcal{N})|_{X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}
.
Since
X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
has real codimension at least 4 in
X
, we obtain that

\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{N})=c_{1}(\mathcal{N})\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
This explains that the orbifold first Chern class coincides with the first Chern class for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier reflexive sheaf or a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor.
For this reason, we may use
\hat{c}_{1}
and
c_{1}
interchangeably.

(7)

Let
(V,G,\pi)
be an orbifold chart of
U
and let
b=|G|
.
If
\mathcal{L}
is a torsion-free sheaf on
U
of rank one,
then
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes b})^{**}
is a line bundle.
Indeed,
(\pi^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**}
is a
G
-linearized line bundle on
V
.
For any point
v\in V
, the action of its stabilizer
G_{v}
on the fiber
((\pi^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**})^{\otimes b}_{v}
is trivial (this is known as Kempf’s descending condition, see
[
DN89
, Théorème 2.3]
for the algebraic version).
It follows that there is some
G
-invariant local section of
((\pi^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**})^{\otimes b}
which does not vanish at
v
.
This implies that there is a line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on
U
such that
\pi^{*}\mathcal{M}\cong((\pi^{*}\mathcal{L})^{**})^{\otimes b}
and
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes b})^{**}\cong\mathcal{M}
.
As a consequence, if
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
is a complex orbifold with a finite family of chart, if
\mathcal{L}
is a torsion-free sheaf on the quotient space
X
of rank one,
then
(\mathcal{L}^{\otimes q})^{**}
is a line bundle for the lcm
q
of the cardinalities
|G_{i}|
.

After (5) of Remark
3.4
, we will use the following notion.
Assume that
X
is a complex analytic variety with quotient singularities, and that
\mathcal{E}
a reflexive coherent sheaf on
X
.
If
\mathcal{E}
induces an orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
on the standard orbifold structure, we set

\hat{c}_{i}(\mathcal{E}):=\hat{c}_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}).

The following lemma from
[
Wu23
, Lemma 1 and Proposition 4]

(see also
[
CGN+23
, Construction 6.1]
)
relates Kähler orbifolds and Kähler varieties with quotient singularities.
For the reader’s convenience, we include a proof.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a compact complex analytic variety with quotient singularities, and
let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be an effective orbifold structure on
X
.

If there is a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
,
then there is an orbifold Kähler form on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
of the shape

\omega_{\mathrm{orb}}=pr_{X}^{*}\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi,

for some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
orbifold real function
\varphi
, where
pr_{X}^{*}\omega
is the orbifold form on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
defined by pulling back
\omega
on every orbifold chart.
In particular, the class of
\omega
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is an orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

Conversely, if there is an orbifold Kähler form
\alpha_{\mathrm{orb}}
on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
,
then there is a Kähler form
\alpha
on
X
which has the same class as
\alpha_{\mathrm{orb}}
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

For the first part, we will adapt the method of
[
Dem92
, Lemma 3.5]
.
Let
n=\operatorname{dim}X
.
There is a finite open covering
\{U_{i}\}
of
X
, such that
for each
i
, there is an integer
N_{i}
and a relatively compact open subset
W_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{N_{i}}
with the following properties.
There is a closed embedding
\iota_{i}
from
U_{i}
into
W_{i}
, there is a Kähler form
\Omega_{i}
on a neighborhood the closure
\overline{W_{i}}
, such that

\omega|_{U_{i}}=\iota^{*}\Omega_{i}.

By considering bump functions supported in the
W_{i}
’s, we can obtain a partition of the unity
\{\eta_{i}\}
subordinate to
U_{i}
, so that there is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\rho_{i}
, compactly supported in
W_{i}
, such that
\eta_{i}=\rho_{i}\circ\iota_{i}
.
Since
\Omega_{i}
is positive, by compactness, there are real numbers
A_{i}>0
such that, on every
W_{i}
,

\sqrt{-1}(\rho_{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\rho_{i}-\partial\rho_{i}\wedge\overline{\partial}\rho_{i})\geq-A_{i}\Omega_{i}

Up to refining the covering
\{U_{i}\}
, we can assume that there is an orbifold chart
(V_{i},G_{i})
for each
U_{i}
, such that
V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a relatively compact open neighborhood of the origin.
We denote by
\pi_{i}\colon V_{i}\to W_{i}
the composite map of
V_{i}\to U_{i}\to W_{i}
, which is a holomorphic map.
Let
\theta_{i}=\rho_{i}\circ\pi_{i}
.
Then
\{\theta_{i}\}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
orbifold partition of the unity, subordinate to
\{V_{i}\}
.
The inequality of the previous paragraph implies that, on each
V_{i}
,

(3.2)

\sqrt{-1}(\theta_{i}\partial\overline{\partial}\theta_{i}-\partial\theta_{i}\wedge\overline{\partial}\theta_{i})\geq-A_{i}\cdot\pi_{i}^{*}\Omega_{i}=-A_{i}\cdot pr_{X}^{*}\omega.

For each
i
, we define

w_{i}({x})=\sum_{g\in G_{i}}||g({x})||^{2}\geq 0
for any
{x}\in V_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
where
||\cdot||
is the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then
w_{i}
is
G_{i}
-invariant and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}w_{i}
is a
G_{i}
-invariant Kähler form on
V_{i}
.
We set

\gamma:=\epsilon\cdot\sqrt{-1}\cdot\sum_{i}\theta_{i}\partial\overline{\partial}w_{i}

for some
\epsilon>0
small enough,
so that

(3.3)

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}w_{i}\geq\gamma

on
V_{i}
for all
i
.
By construction,

\gamma
is an orbifold positive
(1,1)
-form on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
We note that each
w_{i}
is bounded on
V_{i}
.
Hence there are constants
C_{i}
such that, for any
x\in V_{i}
, we have

(3.4)

w_{i}(x)\leq C_{i}+\sup_{\ k\neq i,\ V_{k}\ni x}w_{k}(x),

in the following sense.
Let
y
be the image of
x
in
X
.
Then we say that
x\in V_{k}
if and only if
y\in U_{k}
.
If this is the case, then we set
w_{k}(x):=w_{k}(x^{\prime})
, where
x^{\prime}\in V_{k}
is any point lying over
y
.

Let

K=2\sum_{i}A_{i}\exp(C_{i})

and

w=\log\,\left(\sum_{i}\theta_{i}^{2}\exp(w_{i})\right).

Then
w
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
orbifold function on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
By same calculation as in
[
Dem92
, Lemma 3.5]
, the properties of (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) imply that

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}w\geq\gamma-K\cdot pr_{X}^{*}\omega.

We set

\varphi=\frac{1}{K}w
.
It follows that

pr_{X}^{*}\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi\geq\frac{1}{K}\gamma,

which is positive.
This proves the first assertion.

For the converse, we still let
\{U_{i}\}
be the finite open covering of
X

and
(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})
be the orbifold chart of
U_{i}
, as in the first part.
In particular, each
U_{i}
is relative compact in
X
and each
V_{i}
is relatively compact in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then
\alpha_{\mathrm{orb}}
is defined by a Kähler form
\alpha_{i}
on
V_{i}
,
such that
\alpha_{i}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{i}
for some
G_{i}
-invariant
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
strongly plurisubharmonic (psh for short) function, defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{V_{i}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Then
\psi_{i}
descends to a continuous function
\psi^{\prime}_{i}
on
U_{i}
with
\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}^{\prime}\circ\pi_{i}
.

We note that
\psi_{i}^{\prime}
is strongly psh as well by
[
Var89
, Lemma II.3.1.2]
.
By
[
Var89
, Statement II.2.1]
, we deduce that there is a Kähler form
\alpha
on
X
, whose class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is the same as
\alpha_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In the remainder of the paper, for an effective compact complex orbifold
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
with quotient space
X
, we will identify the notion of Kähler classes on
X
and the notion of orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

3.2.
Stable orbifold sheaves

We can extend the notion of slope stability for torsion-free sheaves to the setting of orbifolds.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a compact complex orbifold of dimension
n
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
a torsion-free orbifold coherent sheaf of rank
r>0
.
Let
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
be any class.
We can define the slope of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
with respect to
\alpha^{n-1}
as

\mu_{\alpha^{n-1}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}):=\frac{\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\alpha^{n-1}}{r},

where
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})
is defined as the orbifold first Chern class of the determinant orbifold line bundle
(\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})^{**}
.
The sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is said to be
\alpha^{n-1}
-semistable if for any non zero subsheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, we have
\mu_{\alpha^{n-1}}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\geq\mu_{\alpha^{n-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})
.
It is said to be
\alpha^{n-1}
-stable if the inequality is strict for every non zero saturated proper subsheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

Harder-Narasimhan filtrations and Jordan-Hölder filtrations exist for orbifold coherent sheaves, with respect to orbifold Kähler classes.
To see this, we can adapt the classic argument (see for example
[
Kob14
, Section V.7]
) to the orbifold setting.
We first prove two lemmas.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a compact complex Kähler orbifold of dimension
n\geq 2
.
Fix some nef classes
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-1}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be orbifold coherent torsion-free sheaves on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
of the same rank.
Then
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\Omega\leq\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\Omega
.

Proof.

By continuity and by perturbing
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-1}
, we may assume that they are Kähler classes, and hence orbifold Kähler classes by Lemma
3.5
.
By taking the determinants, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
are orbifold line bundles.
Up to twisting with
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}
, we may assume further that
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is trivial.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an orbifold coherent ideal sheaf
and
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\Omega\leq 0
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a compact complex Kähler orbifold of dimension
n\geq 2
,
and let
X
be its quotient space.
Assume that
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-1}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
are nef classes.
Let
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-1}
and let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be an orbifold coherent torsion-free sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Then the following set of real numbers is bounded from above,

\{\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\Omega\ :\ \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}\mbox{ is an orbifold coherent subsheaf of }\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}\}.

Proof.

For any orbifold coherent sheaf
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, we set
m(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}):=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\Omega
.
Let
\Sigma
be the set of non zero orbifold subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
We define a partial order
\leq
on
\Sigma
, such that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}\leq\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}
if and only if

(1)

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}
,

(2)

and
m(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})\leq m(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime})
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a maximal element of
(\Sigma,\leq)
which has minimal rank.
Such an element always exists, since
\Sigma
satisfies the ascending chain condition with respect to the inclusion.
We will show that
m(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\geq\sup_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}\in\Sigma}m(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})
, which implies the lemma.

For simplicity, we omit the lower script
orb
in the following argument.
Assume by contradiction that there is some
\mathcal{G}\in\Sigma
such that
m(\mathcal{G})>m(\mathcal{F})
.
If
\mathcal{G}\not\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, then
\mathcal{F}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}
.
It follows that
m(\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G})<m(\mathcal{F})
for
\mathcal{F}
is maximal.
There is an exact sequence of orbifold coherent sheaves

0\to\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}\to 0,

and hence
m(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})+m(\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G})=m(\mathcal{F})+m(\mathcal{G})
.
We deduce that

m(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})>m(\mathcal{G})>m(\mathcal{F}).

Therefore, replacing
\mathcal{G}
by
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}
,
we can assume that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.
Furthermore, we may assume that
\mathcal{G}
is maximal among all elements
\mathcal{H}
of
\Sigma
, such that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and that
m(\mathcal{H})>m(\mathcal{F})
.

By Lemma
3.7
, we must have
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{G}<\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{F}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be a maximal element, among the elements of
\Sigma
containing
\mathcal{G}
.
Then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is also a maximal element of
\Sigma
, and
m(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\geq m(\mathcal{G})>m(\mathcal{F})
.
By Lemma
3.7
, either

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\not\subseteq\mathcal{F}

or
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{G}^{\prime}<\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{F}
.
Since
\mathcal{F}
is a maximal element of minimal rank, we deduce that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\not\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.
Thus
m(\mathcal{F}+\mathcal{G}^{\prime})<m(\mathcal{F})
.
By the same argument of the previous paragraph, we obtain that

m(\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}^{\prime})>m(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\geq m(\mathcal{G}).

This is a contradiction,
for
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{G}
is chosen to be maximal at the end of the previous paragraph.
∎

Now we can conclude the existence of Harder-Narasimhan filtrations and Jordan-Hölder filtrations.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a compact complex orbifold of dimension
n
and
\omega
an orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a torsion-free orbifold coherent sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

There is a unique filtration of saturated orbifold subsheaves

0=\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{k}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}

such that the subquotients
\mathcal{F}^{j}_{\mathrm{orb}}/\mathcal{F}^{j-1}_{\mathrm{orb}}
are
\omega^{n-1}
-semistable with strictly decreasing slopes.

(2)

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is
\omega^{n-1}
-semistable.
Then there is a filtration of saturated orbifold subsheaves

0=\mathcal{G}^{0}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{k}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}

such that the subquotients
\mathcal{G}^{j}_{\mathrm{orb}}/\mathcal{G}^{j-1}_{\mathrm{orb}}
are
\omega^{n-1}
-stable with equal slopes.

Proof.

Following the strategy of
[
Kob14
, Theorem V.7.15 and Theorem V.7.18]
, the lemma follows from Lemma
3.8
.
∎

3.3.
Bimeromorphic morphisms of complex orbifolds

We will define the blowup of an orbifold.
The objective of this operation is to reduce the study of orbifold coherent torsion-free sheaf to that of orbifold vector bundle.

Let
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i})\}
be a compact complex orbifold, and let

Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
be an orbifold subvariety of
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
which consists of a family
\{W_{i}\subseteq V_{i}\}
of
G_{i}
-invariant closed subvarieties.
We assume that the image
Z
of
Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
in
Y
is irreducible.

When each
W_{i}
is smooth, we can define the blowup

f_{\mathrm{orb}}\colon X_{\mathrm{orb}}\to Y_{\mathrm{orb}}

of
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
with center
Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
as follows.
Let
\varphi_{i}\colon\widehat{V}_{i}\to V_{i}
be the blowup with center
W_{i}
.
There is an induced action of
G_{i}
on
\widehat{V}_{i}
such that
\varphi_{i}
is
G_{i}
-equivariant.
Then
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(\widehat{V}_{i},G_{i})\}
is a complex compact orbifold and the family of morphisms
\{\varphi_{i}\}
induces the morphism
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
of orbifolds.
We call it the
blowup
of
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
at the smooth center
Z_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
It induces a holomorphic morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of the quotient spaces.

Let
F_{i}\subseteq\widehat{V}_{i}
be the exceptional locus of
\varphi_{i}
.
They define a smooth orbifold subvariety
E_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Each morphsim
F_{i}\to W_{i}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{d}
-bundle, where
d+1
is the codimension of
Z
in
Y
.
For any integer
n
,
we can define the orbifold line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{orb}}}(nE_{\mathrm{orb}}):=\{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{V}_{i}}(nF_{i})\}
.
We note that, for
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{V}_{i}}(-F_{i})
, its restriction on every fiber of
\varphi_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{d}}(1)
, and its restriction on
\widehat{V}_{i}\setminus{F_{i}}
is trivial.
A similar argument as in
[
Voi02b
, Proposition 3.24]
implies that, if
\alpha
is an orbifold Kähler class on
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
, then there is a rational number
b>0
, such that

f^{*}\alpha-b\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{orb}}}(E_{\mathrm{orb}}))

is an orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

Alternatively, let
E\subseteq X
be the exceptional locus of
f\colon X\to Y
, which is equal to the image of
E_{\mathrm{orb}}
in
X
.
Then there is a positive integer
k
such that
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E))=k\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{orb}}}(E_{\mathrm{orb}})
.
We then deduce that

f^{*}\alpha-a\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E))

is an orbifold Kähler class for some rational number
a>0
.

We remark that the relative ampleness of
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{V}_{i}}(-F_{i})
over
V_{i}
above still holds even if the blowup center
W_{i}
is not smooth.
More generally, we can consider a morphism
f_{\mathrm{orb}}\colon X_{\mathrm{orb}}\to Y_{\mathrm{orb}}

such that on each orbifold chart,
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
is defined as a proper bimeromorphic map
\varphi_{i}\colon\widehat{V}_{i}\to V_{i}
.
We call it a
proper bimeromorphic morphism of orbifolds
.
If each
\varphi_{i}
is a composition of blowups at
G_{i}
-invariant centers,
we say that
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
is a
composition of blowups
.
In this case, if there are
k
irreducible components
E_{1},...,E_{k}
of the exceptional locus of the induced morphism
f\colon X\to Y
,
and if
\omega
is an orbifold Kähler class on
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
, then there are rational numbers
a_{1},...,a_{k}>0
such that

(3.5)

f^{*}\alpha-\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{j}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E_{j}))

is an orbifold Kähler class on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

We recall the following statement on resolution of singularities,
which allows us to make a coherent sheaf locally free by taking functorial bimeromorphic transforms.
A construction is called functorial if it commutes with local analytic isomorphisms.
In particular, the functoriality implies the construction is equivariant with respect to any actions of groups.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
X
be a complex analytic variety, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a torsion-free coherent sheaf on
X
.
Then there is a bimeromorphic morphism
r\colon\widetilde{X}\to X

satisfying the following properties.

(1)

r^{*}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{torsion}
is locally free.

(2)

\widetilde{X}
is a smooth complex analytic variety.

(3)

r
is obtained by a sequence of blowups at centers contained in the union of
X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
and the non-locally-free locus of
\mathcal{E}
.

(4)

The construction of
r
is functorial.

Proof.

Since we will show the functoriality of the construction,
we only need to prove the theorem locally.
We can assume that
(o\in X)
is a germ of complex analytic variety.
Then there is an exact sequence of coherent sheaves

(3.6)

\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus p}\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus q}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}\to 0.

The morphism
\varphi
is represented by a
q\times p
matrix
\Theta(x)
with entries as holomorphic functions on
X
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is torsion-free, if
M
is the maximum of the ranks of
\Theta(x)
for
x\in X
, then
q-M
is the rank of
\mathcal{E}
.
Let
m
be the minimum of the ranks of
\Theta(x)
for
x\in X
.
Then
\mathcal{E}
is locally free if and only if
M=m
.
Assume that
m<M
. Then the rank of
\Theta(o)
is equal to
m
up to shrinking
X
around
o
.
We also note that
q-m
is equal to the dimension of the residue of
\mathcal{E}
at
o
,
that is,
q-m=\operatorname{dim}\left(\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathbb{C}(o)\right)
, where
\mathbb{C}(o)\cong\mathbb{C}
is the residue field of
X
at
o
.
We let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
be ideal generated by the determinants of all
(m+1)
-minors of the
\Theta(x)
.
We will prove the theorem in three steps.

Step 1.
In this step, we will show that the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
on
(o\in X)
depends only on
\mathcal{E}
, and is independent of the choice of the exact sequence (
3.6
).
We note that the sheaf
\mathcal{I}
is unchanged if we compose
\varphi
in (
3.6
) by some automorphisms of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus p}
or
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus q}
over
X
.
Hence, we may assume that
\Theta(x)
is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{m}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\Lambda(x)&\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathbf{1}_{m}
is the constant identity square matrix of rank
m
.
Hence
\mathcal{I}
is the matrix generated by the entries of
\Lambda(x)
.
It follows that we can always assume that
m=0
and
q
is equal to the dimension
d
of the residue of
\mathcal{E}
at
o
.

We will next show that
\mathcal{I}
may depend on
\psi
in (
3.6
), but is independent of
\varphi
.
Indeed, we assume that
\theta\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{q}
is another resolution of the kernel of
\psi
.
Then there is a morphism
\alpha\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{p}
such that
\theta=\varphi\circ\alpha
.
This shows that the ideal defined by
\theta
is contained in the one defined by
\varphi
.
Since the rolls of
\varphi
and
\theta
are symmetric, we deduce that these ideals defined by
\varphi
and
\theta
are equal.

Now we will show that
\mathcal{I}
is also independent of the choice of
\psi
.
As shown in the first paragraph of Step 1, we may assume that
q=d
in the exact sequence (
3.6
), where
d
is the dimension of the residue of
\mathcal{E}
at
o
.
Assume that

\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus s}\overset{\varphi^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus d}\overset{\psi^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}\to 0

is another exact sequence.
Since we have assumed that
q=d
in (
3.6
),
there is an endomorphism
\beta
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus d}
such that
\psi^{\prime}=\psi\circ\beta
.
We note that the residue morphisms of
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
at
o
are surjective morphisms of complex vector spaces of the same dimension
d
,
hence they are isomorphisms.
This implies that
\beta
is an isomorphism around
o
.
Thus

\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta^{-1}\circ\varphi}
\textstyle{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi\circ\beta}
\textstyle{\mathcal{E}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

is also exact.
The previous paragraphs then imply that the ideal
\mathcal{I}
defined by
\psi
is the same as the one defined by
\psi^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof of the first Step.

Step 2
.
In this step, we will construct a functorial modification
X_{1}
of
(o\in X)
,
which decreases the difference
M-m
on
X_{1}
.
From the first step, we may assume that
m=0
,
i.e.

\Theta(o)
is the null matrix.
Let
f_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X
be the blowup at the ideal
\mathcal{I}
.
We can pullback the sequence (
3.6
),
and obtain an exact sequence of coherent sheaves

\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}^{\oplus p}\overset{f_{1}^{*}\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}^{\oplus q}\longrightarrow f_{1}^{*}\mathcal{E}\to 0.

We note that
f_{1}^{*}\varphi
is represented by the matrix
\Gamma=\Theta\circ f_{1}
.
Let
o_{1}\in X_{1}
be a point.
Then there is an open neighborhood
U
of
o_{1}
, such that
f_{1}^{*}\mathcal{I}
is generated by some holomorphic function
t
.
As we have assumed that
m=0
, the ideal
\mathcal{I}
is generated by the entries of
\Theta
.
It follows that every entry of the matrix
\Gamma|_{U}
is divisible by
t
, and
t^{-1}\Gamma
is non zero at
o_{1}
.
The matrix
t^{-1}\Gamma
then defines a morphism
\gamma\colon\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}^{\oplus p}\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{1}}^{\oplus q}
, and we denotes its cokernel by
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.
Then the dimension of the residue of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
at
o_{1}
is smaller than the one of
\mathcal{E}
at
o
.
By construction,
there is a natural surjective morphism from
f_{1}^{*}\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, which is generically isomorphic.
It induces a surjective and generically isomorphic
morphism from
\mathcal{E}_{1}:=f_{1}^{*}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{torsion}

to
\mathcal{F}_{1}/\mathrm{torsion}
,
which must be an isomorphism.
Thus we obtain a surjective morphism from
\mathcal{F}_{1}
to
\mathcal{E}_{1}
.
Hence the dimension of the residue of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
at
o_{1}
is smaller than the one of
\mathcal{E}
at
o
.
This completes the proof of the second step.
We note that this construction is functorial since the ideal
\mathcal{I}
depends only on
\mathcal{E}
by the first step.

Step 3.

By repeating the procedure of the second step for finitely many times, and then we take a functorial resolution of singularities in the end,
we obtain the required morphism
r\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

As a corollary, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}={(V_{i},G_{i})}
be a compact complex orbifold, and
\mathcal{E}_{orb}=\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}
an orbifold coherent torsion-free sheaf on

.
Then by successively blowing up, we can obtain a morphism

so that the following properties hold.

(1)

There is an orbifold atlas
X_{\mathrm{orb}}={(\widehat{V}_{i},G_{i})}
so that
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
is induced by the morphisms
\varphi_{i}\colon\widehat{V}_{i}\to V_{i}
, which are successive blowups of
G_{i}
-invariant centers, over the non-locally-free loci of the

.

(2)

The orbifold coherent sheaf
(f_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{orb})/\mathrm{torsion}
, which is defined by the coherent sheaves

on

3.10
to each coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{i}
on

.

3.4.
Miyaoka’s inequality

We will prove the following proposition,
which is a variant of
[
CHP16
, Theorem 7.11]
, and its argument goes back to
[
Miy87
, Theorem 6.1]
.
It will play an important role in the positivity results on cotangent sheaves in Section
5
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler threefold with quotient singularities only.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive sheaf on
X
which induces an orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\rm orb}
on the standard orbifold structure
X_{\rm orb}
of
X
.
Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)

\alpha
is a nef class on
X
such that
\alpha^{2}\not\equiv 0
and
\alpha^{3}=0
.

(2)

\mathcal{E}
is generically nef with respect to
\alpha_{\epsilon}
for all
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, where
\alpha_{\epsilon}=\alpha+\epsilon\omega
.

(3)

\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})=\alpha-\beta
such that
\alpha\cdot\beta\equiv 0
,

Then
\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha\geq 0
.

We recall that
\mathcal{E}
is generically nef with respect to a class
\gamma
if
for any torsion-free quotient
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}
of coherent sheaves, we have
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})\cdot\gamma^{2}\geq 0
.
In the proof of
[
Miy87
, Theorem 6.1]
, one needs to consider second Chern classes of torsion-free sheaves.
To deal with this problem in the setting of orbifolds, we will use the following assertion, which reduce the situation to Chern classes of orbifold vector bundles.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(Y_{\mathrm{orb}},\alpha)
be a compact Kähler orbifold of dimension
n\geq 2
.
Let

Lemma 3.14
.

Let

be a proper bimeromorphic morphism between compact complex orbifolds, and let

be the induced morphism between quotient spaces.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}
be torsion-free orbifold coherent sheaves on

and

, respectively.
Assume that
f_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
on the isomorphic locus of
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Then the following properties hold.

(1)

There is a correspondence between saturated subsheaves
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and saturated subsheaves
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, such that

\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\alpha^{n-1}=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1},

where

and
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is any class.

(2)

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is
\alpha^{n-1}
-(semi)stable if and only if
\mathcal{E^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is
(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}
-(semi)stable.

Proof.

Let
U^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
be the largest open subset over which
f_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an isomorphism and let
U=f(U^{\prime})\subseteq X
.
Then the complement of
U
in
X
has codimension at least
2
.
By assumption, the orbifold coherent sheaves
(f_{\mathrm{orb}})_{*}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
are isomorphic over
U

Thus they have the same reflexive hull which is equal to
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{**}

We first fix a saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
There is a natural morphism
(f_{\mathrm{orb}})_{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}\to((f_{\mathrm{orb}})_{*}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}})^{**}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{**}
.
We then define
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
as the intersection of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and the image of
(f_{\mathrm{orb}})_{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
inside
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{**}
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a saturated subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
Then we can consider the intersection
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and
(f_{\mathrm{orb}})_{*}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}

\mathcal{E}^{**}_{\mathrm{orb}}

We define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
as the saturation in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime}

of the image of

.
Then these constructions are converse with each other.

Since
f_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}
on

,
the image of the class

If

then we have

\displaystyle(f^{*}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})-\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}))\cdot(f^{*}_{\mathrm{orb}}\alpha)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((f^{*}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})-\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}))\smallsmile(f^{*}_{\mathrm{orb}}\alpha)^{n-1})\smallfrown[X^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-1}\smallfrown f_{*}((f^{*}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})-\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{orb}}))\smallfrown[X^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

since

(for example, see Section

Finally, we note that the ranks of

The following lemma is a key ingredient for our openness of stability.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
be an effective compact complex orbifold with quotient space
X
, let
\omega
be an orbifold Kähler class, and let
n
be the dimension of
X
.
Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is a torsion-free orbifold coherent sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
,
and that
C
is a real number.
Then the following subset of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
is finite

\{\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\ :\ 0\neq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}\mbox{ is saturated with }\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\omega^{n-1}\geq C\}.

Proof.

The proof is to descend
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
to the quotient space
X
and then apply the boundedness results of
[
Tom21
]
.
We fix a finite atlas of chart
X_{\mathrm{orb}}=\{(V_{i},G_{i},\pi_{i})\}
.
Let
q
be the lcm of the cardinalities
|G_{i}|
.
Let
U\subseteq X
be the open subset of points over which the actions of
G_{i}
are free,
and let
E_{1},...,E_{s}
be the irreducible components of
X\setminus U
of codimension one.
Then, by taking the preimages
\pi_{i}^{-1}(E_{j})
in the orbifold charts,
each
E_{j}
defines an orbifold subvariety
(E_{j})_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
, of codimension one.
In particular, the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{j}
of
(E_{j})_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an orbifold invertible coherent ideal sheaf.
We set
\hat{c}_{1}(E_{j}):=-\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{I}_{j})
.

Step 1.
In this step, we prove the following statement.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
be a torsion-free coherent sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
of rank
r
,
let
\mathcal{G}
be the coherent sheaf on
X
defined by
((\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i})^{G_{i}}
,
and let
\mathcal{G}_{orb}
be the torsion-free coherent sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
defined by
(\pi_{i})^{*}\mathcal{G}/\mathrm{torsion}
.
Then the rank of
\mathcal{G}
is equal to
r
as well.
Since
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is torsion-free, we obtain a natural morphism from
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, which is an isomorphism over
U
.
Then

\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})+\sum_{j=1}^{s}d_{j}\hat{c}_{1}(E_{j}),

where
d_{j}
is the vanishing order of the natural morphism

\det\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}\to\det\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
along
(E_{j})_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
We claim that
0\leq d_{1},...,d_{s}\leq r(q-1)
.

To prove this assertion, it is sufficient to work locally around a general point
x
of
E_{j}
.
Thus we may assume that, around
x
, the sheaf
\mathcal{G}
is free.
Let
(V,G,\pi)
be an orbifold chart over a neighborhood of
x
such that
\pi^{-1}(\{x\})=\{x^{\prime}\}
is a singleton.
We set
D_{j}=\pi^{-1}(E_{j})
.
Since
x
is a general point of
E_{j}
, we may further assume that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
corresponds to some free coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
V
.
By Lemma
3.18
at the end of this subsection, it is enough to show that, for any local section
\eta
of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}
,
which does not vanish at
x^{\prime}
,
the vanishing order of
\varphi(\eta)
along
D_{j}
is at most
|G|-1\leq q-1
,
where
\varphi\colon\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}
is the natural morphism.
We remark that, if
\eta^{\prime}
is the sum of the
G
-orbit of
\eta
,
then it does not vanish at
x^{\prime}
,
for the action of
G
on the fiber
(\pi^{*}\mathcal{G})_{x^{\prime}}
is trivial.
Furthermore, the vanishing order of
\varphi(\eta^{\prime})
along
D_{j}
is at least the one of
\varphi(\eta)
.
Hence, it is enough to show that, for any local section
\sigma
of
\mathcal{G}
,
which does not vanish at
x
,
the vanishing order of
\varphi(\pi^{*}\sigma)
along
D_{j}
is at most
|G|-1
.
Up to shrinking
V
,
there is a
G
-invariant holomorphic function
u
on
V
which vanishes along
D_{j}
with order equal to
|G|
.
If the vanishing order of
\varphi(\pi^{*}\sigma)
along
D_{j}
is at least
|G|
,
then
u^{-1}\cdot\varphi(\pi^{*}\sigma)
is a
G
-invariant section of
\mathcal{F}
.
Thus it induces a section
\sigma^{\prime}
of
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{F})^{G}
, which is a local section of
\mathcal{G}
around
x
.
Furthermore, if
v
is the local holomorphic function around
x
induced by
u
, then it vanishes along
E_{j}
and we have
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma\cdot v^{-1}
.
We obtain a contradiction since
\sigma
does not vanish at
x
.
This completes the proof of Step 1.

Step 2
.
In this step, we reduce the problem to the case of sheaves on
X
.
We recall that, by Lemma
3.5
,

\omega
is also a Kähler class on the quotient space
X
.
We define

\Sigma_{1}:=\{0\neq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\ :\ \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}\mbox{ is saturated with }\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\omega^{n-1}\geq C\},

\Sigma_{2}:=\{\mbox{coherent sheaves }\mathcal{G}\mbox{ on }X\mbox{ defined by }((\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})^{G_{i}}\mbox{ for }\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\in\Sigma_{1}\}.

Thanks to Step 1, it is sufficient to show that the orbifold first Chern classes of the torsion-free orbifold coherent sheaves
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
induced by the elements
\mathcal{G}
in
\Sigma_{2}
are finite.
We note that
((\det\mathcal{G})^{\otimes q})^{**}
is locally free on
X
, and we have

c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{G})^{\otimes q})^{**})=\hat{c}_{1}(((\det\mathcal{G})^{\otimes q})^{**})=q\cdot\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}),

as shown in (6) and (7) of Remark
3.4
.
Step 1 also implies that the intersection numbers
c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{G})^{\otimes q})^{**})\cdot\omega^{n-1}
, for
\mathcal{G}\in\Sigma_{2}
, are bounded from below by a constant.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be the coherent sheaf on
X
defined by
((\pi_{i})_{*}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})^{G_{i}}
.
Then for any
\mathcal{G}\in\Sigma_{2}
, it is a subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
, saturated over
U
.
We define

\Sigma_{3}=\{\mathcal{H}\ :\ \mathcal{H}\mbox{ is the saturation of }\mathcal{G}\mbox{ in }\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\mbox{ where }\mathcal{G}\in\Sigma_{2}\}.

Since there is a natural morphism from
\mathcal{G}
to its saturation
\mathcal{H}
, which is isomorphic over
U
,
we see that

c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**})=c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{G})^{\otimes q})^{**})+\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}\hat{c}_{1}(E_{j})

for some integers
a_{j}\geq 0
.
Since
\hat{c}_{1}(E_{j})\cdot\omega^{n-1}>0
for all
j=1,...,s
,
the numbers
c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**})\cdot\omega^{n-1}
, for
\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}
, are bounded from below by a constant.
Furthermore, it is sufficient to show that the first Chern classes
c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**})
for
\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}
are finite.

Step 3
.
We completes the proof in this step.
For any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}
of rank
r
, we see that

((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**}\subseteq((\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{\otimes q})^{**}

is a saturated subsheaf,
since the inclusion is saturated in codimension 2,
and since
((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**}
is a line bundle.
In order to apply the results of
[
Tom21
]
, we need to show that,
the
(n-1)
-degree
\operatorname{deg}_{n-1}(\mathcal{L})
of
\mathcal{L}:=((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**}
with respect to
\omega^{n-1}
,
in the sense of
[
Tom21
, Definition 2.5]
,
is bounded from below by a constant,
for all
\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}
of rank
r
.

We fix a desingularization
\rho\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
.
Since
X
has rational singularities, we have
R^{k}\rho_{*}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L})=0
for
k>0
,
and hence
\rho_{!}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}
in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(X)
generated by coherent sheaves.
If
\tau
signifies the homological Todd class as in
[
Tom21
, Section 2.2]
,
then the component
\tau_{n-1}(\mathcal{L})\in H_{2n-2}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\tau_{n-1}(\mathcal{L})=\tau_{n-1}(\rho_{!}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L}))=\rho_{*}(\tau_{n-1}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L})).

Since
\widetilde{X}
is smooth, we have

\tau_{n-1}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L})=(c_{1}(\rho^{*}\mathcal{L})+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\widetilde{X}))\smallfrown[\widetilde{X}]
,
where
[\widetilde{X}]\in H_{2n}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{R})
is the fundamental class.
In particular, by the projection formula, we have

\operatorname{deg}_{n-1}(\mathcal{L})=\tau_{n-1}(\mathcal{L})\smallfrown\omega^{n-1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot\omega^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\widetilde{X})\cdot(\rho^{*}\omega)^{n-1}.

This implies the boundedness on the
(n-1)
-degrees that we require.

Since
\mathcal{L}
is saturated in
((\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{\otimes q})^{**}
,
by
[
Tom21
, Lemma 5.7]
, the set of quotient sheaves

\{((\bigwedge^{r}\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{\otimes q})^{**}/\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}\mbox{ of rank }r\}

is bounded in the sense of
[
Tom21
, Definition 5.1]
.
Using flattening and Noetherian induction (see
[
Tom16
, Section 3]
), we deduce from
[
Tom21
, Proposition 2.6]
that the set of their homological Todd classes is finite.
From the computation above, it follows that the
first Chern classes
c_{1}(((\det\mathcal{H})^{\otimes q})^{**})
for
\mathcal{H}\in\Sigma_{3}
with rank
r
are finite.
Since
r
is an integer between
1
and rank of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
,
this completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
f_{\mathrm{orb}}\colon(X_{\mathrm{orb}},\omega)\to(Y_{\mathrm{orb}},\alpha)
be a proper bimeromorphic morphism between effective compact Kähler orbifolds.
Let
\alpha_{\epsilon}=f^{*}\alpha+\epsilon\omega
for all
\epsilon>0
.
Assume that
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an orbifold coherent torsion-free sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
which is
(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}
-stable.
Then it is
(\alpha_{\epsilon})^{n-1}
-stable for all
\epsilon>0
small enough.

Proof.

We claim that there is a number
C>0
, such that

\mu_{(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\leq\mu_{(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})-C.

for any non zero proper saturated orbifold subsheaf

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{orb}
.
To prove this claim, by considering
(f_{*}\mathcal{G})/\mathrm{torsion}
on
Y_{\mathrm{orb}}
,
and by applying Lemma
3.14
,
we may assume that
f
is the identity map.
In particular,
f^{*}\alpha=\alpha
is a Kähler class.
Then by Lemma
3.15
, there are only finitely many possible values for the slopes
\mu_{\alpha^{n-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})
which are greater than
\mu_{\alpha^{n-1}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})-1
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
are saturated subsheaves of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.
The existence of
C
then follows from the stability of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
.

By developing
(\alpha_{\epsilon})^{n-1}=(f^{*}\alpha+\epsilon\omega)^{n-1}
,
and by Lemma
3.8
,
we deduce that, for all
\epsilon>0
small enough,
for all saturated subsheaves
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}
,
we have

\mu_{(\alpha_{\epsilon})^{n-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})\leq\mu_{(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{orb}})+\frac{1}{3}C.

Moreover, for such
\epsilon
small enough, we have

\mu_{(\alpha_{\epsilon})^{n-1}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})\geq\mu_{(f^{*}\alpha)^{n-1}}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}})-\frac{1}{3}C.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

.

We first remark the following estimate,

\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\cdot(\alpha-\beta)\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}=\frac{2\epsilon\cdot\alpha^{2}\omega+\epsilon^{2}\cdot(\alpha-\beta)\omega^{2}}{3\epsilon\cdot\alpha^{2}\omega+3\epsilon^{2}\cdot\alpha\omega^{2}+\epsilon^{3}\cdot\omega^{3}}\sim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{2\epsilon\cdot\alpha^{2}\omega}{3\epsilon\cdot\alpha^{2}\omega}=\frac{2}{3}.

Therefore, if

We fix some
\epsilon>0

Since
\alpha_{\epsilon}
is a Kähler class on

by Lemma

.
By taking the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of

0=\mathcal{F}^{0}_{\mathrm{orb}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{k}_{\mathrm{orb}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}

such that each subquotient
\mathcal{G}^{j}_{\mathrm{orb}}:=\mathcal{F}^{j}_{\mathrm{orb}}/\mathcal{F}^{j-1}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is

-stable.
Furthermore, if
\delta_{\epsilon,j}
is the slope of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{j}
with respect to
(\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}
, then we have

\delta_{\epsilon,1}\geq\cdots\geq\delta_{\epsilon,k}.

Since
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
is standard,
\mathcal{F}^{k-1}_{\mathrm{orb}}
induces a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}^{k-1}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
, such that
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{k})=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})-\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{k-1})
, see (5) of Remark
3.4
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is generically nef with respect to
\alpha_{\epsilon}

\delta_{\epsilon,k}\geq 0

By applying Lemma
3.11

\mathcal{G}^{k}_{\mathrm{orb}},...,\mathcal{G}^{1}_{\mathrm{orb}}
successively, we obtain an orbifold

with the natural projection

such that each subquotient

where

is the rank of
\mathcal{G}^{j}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, and

is the induced morphism on quotient spaces.
Furthermore, we have
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})\cdot(f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{j})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}.

As a consequence, we get

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}(f^{*}_{\mathrm{orb}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})+\sum_{j<l}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{{}^{\prime}l})\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}+2\sum_{j<l}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{{}^{\prime}l})-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{c}_{1}(f_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})^{2}-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

Applying the Hodge index theorem (see Lemma
3.17
below) on
X^{\prime}
, and noting that
(f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon})^{3}=\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}
, we obtain that

\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}\cdot\left(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}\right)\leq\left(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})\cdot(f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}\right)^{2}=(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{j})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2})^{2}.

Hence we deduce that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\left(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})\cdot(f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\sum_{j}r_{j}\delta_{\epsilon,j}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\epsilon,1}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\sum_{j}r_{j}\delta_{\epsilon,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\epsilon,1}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\cdot\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\epsilon,1}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\cdot(\alpha-\beta)\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is small enough, from the first paragraph, we obtain that

\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}\leq\delta_{\epsilon,1}\cdot\frac{(\alpha-\beta)\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}}{\alpha_{\epsilon}^{3}}\leq\delta_{\epsilon,1}\cdot 1=\delta_{\epsilon,1}.

Since
\sum_{j}r_{j}\delta_{\epsilon,j}=\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}
and since
\delta_{\epsilon,j}\geq 0
for
j=1,...,k
, we deduce that
r_{1}\delta_{\epsilon,1}\leq\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}
.
Hence

\left(\sum_{j}\frac{1}{r_{j}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\prime j})^{2}\right)\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}\leq\frac{1}{r_{1}}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}\leq\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}.

Therefore, we get

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}=\hat{c}_{2}(f_{\mathrm{orb}}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}})\cdot f^{*}\alpha_{\epsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}

By tending
\epsilon
to zero, we conclude that

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\cdot\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha^{2}=0.

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.
Comparison of Chern classes

Consider a standard complex orbifold
X_{\mathrm{orb}}
and its quotient space
X
.
We have seen in (5) of Remark
3.4
that every orbifold vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
induces a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
.
The objective of this section is to compare the orbifold Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}
and the Chern classes of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
is a vector bundle on a desingularization
r\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
such that
(r_{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})^{**}=\mathcal{E}
.

4.1.
Relative de Rham cohomology

Let
M
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifold.
We denote by
\mathscr{D}^{\prime i}
the sheaf of real currents of degree
i
, i.e.
differential
i
-forms with real distribution coefficients.
Then there is a complex

\cdots\to D^{i}(M)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\to}D^{i+1}(M)\to\cdots,

where
D^{i}(M)=\Gamma(M,\mathscr{D}^{\prime i}(M))
is the group of global sections of the sheaf
\mathscr{D}^{\prime i}
.
The cohomology groups of the previous complex are denoted by
H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,\mathbb{R})
.
Then there are natural isomorphisms
H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,\mathbb{R})\cong H^{i}(M,\mathbb{R})
known as the de Rham isomorphisms.
We consider the following complex

\cdots\to D^{i}(M,U)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\to}D^{i+1}(M,U)\to\cdots

where
U\subseteq M
is an open set,
D^{i}(M,U)=D^{i}(M)\oplus D^{i-1}(U)
and the differentials are defined in the following way:

\mathrm{d}(\omega,\eta)=(\mathrm{d}\omega,\omega|_{U}-\mathrm{d}\eta).

We denote the cohomology groups of the previous complex by
H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,U,\mathbb{R})
.
Then there is a long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,U,\mathbb{R})\to H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,\mathbb{R})\overset{\beta}{\to}H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(U,\mathbb{R})\to\cdots

such that
\beta
is the restriction map.
We refer to
[
BT82
, Page 78-79]
for detailed discussion on relative de Rham cohomology.
We note that
[
BT82
]
only treats de Rham cohomology of differential forms.
But the proof of
[
BT82
, Claim 6.48]
also holds for de Rham cohomology of currents in our setting.
By applying the five lemma on the long exact sequences of relative cohomology,
we obtain natural isomorphisms

H^{i}_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}(M,U,\mathbb{R})\cong H^{i}(M,U,\mathbb{R}).

4.2.
Relative Chern classes

We aim to compare orbifold Chern classes and Chern classes on a desingularization.
Similar topics have been studied before, especially for surfaces, see for example
[
Wah93
]
,
[
Bla96
]
and
[
Lan00
]
.
Inspired by their works, we will introduce the relative Chern classes in any dimension.

Let
X
be a normal irreducible complex analytic variety with quotient singularities, and

a desingularization which is an isomorphism over

.
We denote by

the exceptional locus of

Let

be a reflexive sheaf on

Assume that there is a vector bundle

on

Let

which induces an orbifold Hermitian metric

Bla96

On the other hand, by definition,

Let

Then

That is, there is some

Hence it corresponds to the relative de Rham current cohomology class

as the relative cohomology class

(4.1)

c_{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},r):=c_{i}(\tilde{h})-r^{*}c_{i}(h)=c_{i}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}})-r^{*}\hat{c}_{i}(\mathcal{E})\in H^{2i}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{X}^{\circ},\mathbb{R})

Let

be the tangent bundle of

.
Then

.
We set
\hat{c}_{i}(X):=\hat{c}_{i}(T_{X})
.
Then we can define the following relative Chern classes

(4.2)

c_{i}(\widetilde{X},r):=c_{i}(T_{\widetilde{X}},r).

We also define the following classes

c_{1}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},r):=(c_{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}))^{2}-(r^{*}\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{E}))^{2}\mbox{ and }c_{1}^{2}(\widetilde{X},r):=(c_{1}(\widetilde{X}))^{2}-(r^{*}\hat{c}_{1}(X))^{2}

as elements in

, as well as

(4.3)

\ell(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},r):=c_{1}^{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},r)+c_{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}},r)\mbox{ and }\ell(\widetilde{X},r):=c_{1}^{2}(\widetilde{X},r)+c_{2}(\widetilde{X},r).

There are natural isomorphisms

where

Particularly, when

is a surface, we have

4.3.
Borel-Moore homology

As we have seen in the previous subsection, the relative Chern classes lie in some groups of relative cohomology, which are canonically isomorphic to certain groups of Borel-Moore homology.
In this subsection,
we recall some elementary results concerning Borel-Moore homology.
For more details, we refer to
[
Ful98

and the references therein.

Let
X
be a complex analytic space of pure dimension
n

For simplicity, we always assume that it is an open subset of a compact complex analytic space
M
.
Furthermore, if
{X}^{\prime}
is the Euclidean closure of

in

({X}^{\prime},{X}^{\prime}\setminus X)
is homeomorphic to a CW-complex pair.
There are several interpretation of

(1)

(2)

is embedded into an oriented manifold

Lemma 4.3

With the notation above, we have

[

, Chapter 10]
for
\mathbb{Q}
-Chern classes to orbifold Chern classes in our context for projective surfaces.

Proof.

Let

[

There is a finite cover

[

(1)

(2)

\widetilde{X}

(3)

As a consequence, we deduce that

[
Ful98

Note that

The Chern classes in

(see also
[

, Lemma 3.5]
).

Let
\widetilde{Y}\to\widetilde{X}
be an equivariant desingularization, and let

.
We have the following commutative diagram.

\textstyle{\widetilde{Y}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{q}
\textstyle{\widetilde{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let

\mathcal{E}|_{X{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sm}}}}
which extends to an orbifold metric on
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{orb}}

Then by the condition (3) of the global cover,

under

on

On the one hand, we note that

is an orbifold chart of

5.
Positivity of logarithmic cotangent sheaves

The main objective of this section is to establish the following proposition.
We will also study its variant for non-uniruled threefolds,
see Proposition
5.9
in the last subsection, whose proof is simpler.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
Y
be a non-algebraic uniruled compact Kähler threefold with quotient singularities.
Assume the following conditions hold:

(1)

There is a bimeromorphic morphism
\pi\colon Y\to X
to a normal compact Kähler threefold such that
L=k(K_{X}+\Delta)
is Cartier and nef for some reduced boundary
\Delta
and some integer
k>0
.

(2)

L^{2}\not\equiv 0
and
L^{3}=0
.

(3)

\pi^{*}L|_{P}\equiv 0
, in particular
\pi^{*}L\cdot P\equiv 0
, for any
\pi
-exceptional prime divisor
P
.

(4)

If
B=\pi^{-1}_{*}\Delta
, then
K_{Y}+(1-\lambda)B
is pseudoeffective for some
\lambda>0
.

(5)

\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}(\mathrm{log}\,B)
induces an orbifold vector bundle on the standard orbifold structure of
Y
.

Then
\hat{c}_{2}(\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}(\mathrm{log}\,B))\cdot\pi^{*}L\geq 0
.

We remark that the condition that
L^{3}\equiv 0
is automatic under the assumption that
Y
is Kähler and non-algebraic.
This proposition is a consequence of the generic nefness of the logarithmic cotangent sheaf
\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}(\mathrm{log}\,B)
, see Lemma
5.8
below.
A key ingredient is the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth uniruled non-algebraic compact
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Kähler threefold.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation corresponding to the MRC fibration.
Assume that there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudoeffective.
Then
(K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta_{\rm hor})\cdot\omega^{2}\geq 0
for any Kähler class
\omega
on
X
, where
\Delta_{\rm hor}
is the horzontal part of
\Delta
over the base of the MRC fibration.

If we assume that
X
is a projective threefold and the base of its MRC fibration is a surface, then the same conclusion of the previous proposition still holds, and is a special case of
[
Dru17
, Proposition 4.1]
.
Indeed, our proof follows the ideas there, which we sketch as follows.

For simplicity, we assume that the MRC fibration of
X
is an equidimensional morphism
f\colon X\to Y
.
Then from the positivity results of direct images, we may deduce certain positivity on
K_{X/Y}+\Delta_{\rm hor}
.
We remark that
K_{X/Y}-K_{\mathcal{F}}
is an effective divisor, supported in the locus where
f
has non reduced fibers.
To deduce the positivity of
K_{\mathcal{F}}
from the one of
K_{X/Y}
, the proof of
[
Dru17
, Proposition 4.1]
applies
Kawamata’s cyclic covering trick on

has reduced fibers in codimension one.
In such a case, we have

and we can conclude.
In the situation of Proposition
5.2
, Kawamata’s covering trick is not known for compact Kähler varieties.
Therefore, in this section, we will use the basechange method of
[
DO24

, and argue with some elementary geometric computation.

5.1.

We will first prove some results on fibrations whose general fibers are isomorphic to

Lemma 5.3

Let

On the other hand, the natural morphism

is Cartier and nef for some reduced boundary

(2)

(3)

(4)

Proof.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler threefold with quotient singularities.
Assume that

is not uniruled.
Let

be a reduced divisor.
Then for any nef class

and any torsion-free quotient

of coherent sheaves, we have
\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{Q})\cdot\alpha^{2}\geq 0

Proof.

Let

be the subsheaf induced by the inclusion

Then

Assume that

6.
Lower bounds on Euler characteristics

In this section we will work under the following setup.

Setup 6.1
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial compact Kähler threefold, and
\Delta
a reduced boundary on
X

Assume that the following properties hold.

(1)

(X,\Delta)
is lc.

(2)

has terminal singularities outside the support of
\Delta
.

(3)

(4)

There is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

(5)

(6)

If

is dlt near the general points of

Our goal is to prove the following proposition.
When

is projective, this is an important step in
[

, Chapter 14]
, where the algebraic abundance was established.
In the case of Kähler threefolds, the difficulty is that Bogomolov-Gieseker type inequalities for klt Kähler varieties are still unknown.
Our solution is to construct a modification

of
X
, which has quotient singularities only, see Lemma
6.12
.
Afterwards, we can apply orbifold Bogomolov-Gieseker type inequalities on

.

Proposition 6.2
.

With the notations and hypothesis as in Setup
6.1
, we assume further that
K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta
is nef for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
Then there is a constant
\lambda
such that

for all

.
Furthermore, if there is a curve

such that

grows at least linearly in

.

6.1.
Elementary properties

Before going into the proof of Proposition
6.2
, we first show some consequences of the hypothesis in Setup
6.1
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that we are in Setup
6.1
.
Then
L^{2}\cdot\Delta_{i}=0
for any irreducible component
\Delta_{i}
of
\Delta
.
If in addition
K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta
is nef for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,
then
K_{X}^{2}\cdot L=0
.

Proof.

Since
\nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=2
, it follows that

L^{2}\cdot D=L^{3}=0.

Since
L
is nef and
D
is effective, by the property (4),
we deduce that
L^{2}\cdot\Delta_{i}=0
for any irreducible component
\Delta_{i}
of
\Delta
.

Now we assume further that

is nef for any
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
Then
\nu(X,K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta)\leq 2
, and thus

(K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta)^{2}\cdot(D-\epsilon\Delta)=(K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta)^{3}=0

for all
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
Since
(D-\epsilon\Delta)
is effective for all

(K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta)^{2}\cdot\Delta_{i}=0

for every component
\Delta_{i}
of
\Delta
.
The LHS above is a polynomial in

K_{X}^{2}\cdot\Delta_{i}=\Delta^{2}\cdot\Delta_{i}=K_{X}\cdot\Delta\cdot\Delta_{i}=0.

It then follows that

.
∎

2.7
).
Observe that every step of this MMP is in fact
K_{X}
-negative, and hence
X_{1}
has
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities. Moreover,
(X_{1},\Delta_{1})
is lc, and thus
(X_{1},t\Delta_{1})
is klt for any
0\leq t<1
.
We claim that, for all rational number
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\nu(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+\Delta_{1})\ =\ \nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)\ =\ 2,

\displaystyle\kappa(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+\Delta_{1})\ =\ \kappa(X,K_{X}+\Delta).

To see this, fix
0<\epsilon\ll 1
so that
K_{X_{1}}+(1-2\epsilon)\Delta_{1}
is nef.
Then by Theorem
2.5
,
there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+(1-2\epsilon)\Delta_{1}
.
Then clearly
D_{1}+\epsilon\Delta_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta_{1}
and
D_{1}+2\epsilon\Delta_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+\Delta_{1}
.
Since
\operatorname{Supp}\,(D_{1}+\epsilon\Delta_{1})=\operatorname{Supp}\,(D_{1}+2\epsilon\Delta_{1})
, our claim follows from
[
Kol92
, Lemma 11.3.3]
(by using Kähler classes in place of ample divisors).

Now we fix a rational number
0<\mu\ll 1
so that
K_{X_{1}}+(1-2\mu)\Delta_{1}
is nef, and hence
K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}
is nef.
By Theorem
2.5
, there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+(1-2\mu)\Delta_{1}
.
Then
(X_{1},(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}+\eta D_{1})
is klt for some rational number
0<\eta\ll 1
.
Moreover,

(1+\eta)D_{1}+\mu\Delta_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}+\eta D_{1}

and
D_{1}+2\mu\Delta_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X_{1}}+\Delta_{1}
.
Thus by a similar argument as above, we see that

\nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=\nu(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1})=\nu(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}+\eta D_{1}),

\kappa(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=\kappa(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1})=\kappa(X_{1},K_{X_{1}}+(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}+\eta D_{1}).

Hence replacing
(X,\Delta)
by
(X_{1},(1-\mu)\Delta_{1}+\eta D_{1})
, we may assume that
(X,\Delta)
is a klt pair and there is an effective nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{X}+\Delta
such that
D_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Delta_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}
.

Let

be a log resolution of
(X,\Delta)
.
Let
\Gamma
be the reduced sum of all
r
-exceptional divisors and the strict transform of the components of
\Delta
.
Then
E=K_{Z}+\Gamma-r^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 0
, and its support contains the
r
-exceptional locus.
Hence
K_{Z}+\Gamma\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D_{Z}:=r^{*}D+E
, with
(D_{Z})_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Gamma
.

We run a
(K_{Z}+\Gamma)
-MMP and end with a minimal model
(Z,\Gamma)\to(Z^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
such that
(Z^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
is dlt,
K_{Z^{\prime}}+\Gamma^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D_{Z^{\prime}}
is nef,
D_{Z^{\prime}}
is the strict transform of
D_{Z}
,
and
(D_{Z^{\prime}})_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, we see that
\kappa(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=\kappa(Z^{\prime},K_{Z^{\prime}}+\Gamma^{\prime})
, and that
\nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=\nu(Z^{\prime},K_{Z^{\prime}}+\Gamma^{\prime})
.
Indeed, pulling back the divisors
D_{Z^{\prime}}
and

to a common resolution of

,
we see that the two effective nef divisors have the same support,

[

apply.
Therefore,

satisfies the condition of Setup
6.1
.

Now we run a
(K_{Z^{\prime}}+(1-\lambda)\Gamma^{\prime})
-MMP, trivial with respect to

(see Lemma
2.7
) and end with a bimeromorphic map

such that

is nef for any

small enough, where

is the pushforward of
\Gamma^{\prime}
onto
X^{\prime}
.
Moreover, the numerical dimension and the Kodaira dimension of

.
We remark that this MMP is also a

-MMP, trivial with respect to

.
Thanks to Lemma
6.8
and Lemma

also satisfies the conditions of Setup
6.1
.
This completes our proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of the main theorem

In this section we will prove the results stated in the introduction.
We will divide the proof Theorem
1.2
into two cases, depending on whether

is pseudo-effective for some
\lambda>0

In the first case we adapt the methods of
[
Kol92
, Chapter 14]
and
[

, Section 8.B]

Lemma 8.1
.

Theorem
1.2
holds if
K_{X}+(1-\lambda)\Delta
is pseudo-effective for some
0<\lambda\leq 1
.

Proof.

Passing to a dlt model as in
[
DO24
, Theorem 1.29]
we may assume that
(X,\Delta)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pair.
By
[
DO24
, Theorem 1.35]
, it is enough to show that
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 1.

The proof is split into several steps.
In the first two we will replace
(X,\Delta)
by some bimeromorphic model while keeping
\kappa(X,K_{X}+\Delta)
and

\nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)
invariant.

Step 1.

Replacing
(X,\Delta)
with the bimeromorphic model obtained in Lemma
7.1
, we may assume that the following hold:

(1)

X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial,

(2)

(X,\Delta)
is a reduced lc pair and
X
has terminal singularities outside
\operatorname{Supp}\Delta
,

(3)

L=k(K_{X}+\Delta)
is a nef Cartier divisor for some integer
k>0
,
and
L\sim D\geq 0
with
D_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Delta
.

(4)

\nu(X,K_{X}+\Delta)=2
,

(5)

K_{X}+(1-\epsilon)\Delta
is nef for all
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,

From Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
6.2
, we obtain the following properties:

(6)

(K_{X}+\Delta)^{2}\cdot\Delta=L^{2}\cdot\Delta=0
.

(7)

there is a constant
\eta\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}({nL}))\geq\eta

for all
n\geq 0
.
Furthermore, if there is a curve
C\subseteq X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
such that
L\cdot C>0
, then
\chi(X,\mathscr{O}_{{X}}(nL))
grows at least linearly in
n
.

Step 2.

In this step, we will eliminate the components of
\Delta
on which the restrictions of
L
are numerically trivial.
Suppose that
\Delta^{\prime}
is one such irreducible component of
\Delta
, i.e.
L|_{\Delta^{\prime}}\equiv 0
.
We set
\Delta^{\prime\prime}:=\Delta-\Delta^{\prime}
.
Fix some rational number
0<\lambda\ll 1
such that
K_{X}+(1-\lambda)\Delta
is nef.
Let
a
be the coefficient of
\Delta^{\prime}
in
D
.
Then there is some rational number
0<\mu\ll\lambda
such that

(8.1)

\Theta:=\lambda\Delta^{\prime\prime}+\mu\Delta^{\prime}-\frac{\mu}{a}D

is effective with support equal to
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
.

Since
(X,\Delta-\lambda\Delta^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Delta^{\prime})
is klt, we can run a
(K_{X}+\Delta-\lambda\Delta^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Delta^{\prime})
-MMP, trivial with respect to
K_{X}+\Delta
(see Lemma
2.7
).
Then we obtain a bimeromorphic model
f\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
.
Let
\Gamma
,
\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
be the pushforward of
\Delta
,
\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
onto
Y
, respectively.
Then
K_{Y}+\Gamma-\epsilon(\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime}+\mu\Gamma^{\prime})
is nef for all
0<\epsilon\ll 1
,
K_{Y}+\Gamma
is nef of numerical dimension
2
and
(K_{Y}+\Gamma)|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\equiv 0
. We also note that
k(K_{Y}+\Gamma)\sim f_{*}D
and
(f_{*}D)_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Gamma
. Thus we have

(-\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Gamma^{\prime})|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{\epsilon}(K_{Y}+\Gamma-\epsilon(\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime}+\mu\Gamma^{\prime}))|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}

which is nef.
However, we see from (
8.1
) that

-\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Gamma^{\prime}=-f_{*}\Theta-\frac{\mu}{a}f_{*}D.

Thus
(-\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Gamma^{\prime})|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\equiv-(f_{*}\Theta)|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, as
(f_{*}D)|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\equiv 0
by our construction above. The only way the effective divisor
f_{*}\Theta|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
can be anti-nef is that
f_{*}\Theta|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=0
.
In particular,
\operatorname{Supp}(f_{*}\Theta)\cap\Gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
, as
f_{*}\Theta
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
Y
. Since
\operatorname{Supp}(f_{*}\Theta)=\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
by our construction, we have
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\cap\Gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

On the other hand, since
(Y,\Gamma-\lambda\Gamma^{\prime}-\mu\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
is klt,
Y
has rational singularities.
Since
f_{*}D
is nef of numerical dimension
2
, from
[
CHP16
, Lemma 6.7]
it follows that
\operatorname{Supp}(f_{*}D)
is connected.
Since
(f_{*}D)_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm red}}}=\Gamma
by our construction, we deduce that

\Gamma^{\prime}=0\mbox{ and }\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime\prime}.

We remark that
(Y,\Gamma)

satisfies the conditions (1)-(6) of Step 1.
It also satisfies the first part of the condition (7), as

\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}({nk(K_{X}+\Delta)}))=\chi(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}({nk(K_{Y}+\Gamma)}))\mbox{ for all }n\geq 1.

We claim that the only divisor in
X
contracted by
f
is
\Delta^{\prime}
.
Indeed, assume that
E
is a prime divisor contracted by
f
.
Let
W
be a resolution of singularities of the graph of
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
,
and let
p\colon W\to X
and
q\colon W\to Y
be the projection morphisms.
Then we have

p^{*}(K_{X}+(1-\lambda)\Delta)=q^{*}(K_{Y}+(1-\lambda)\Gamma)-E_{1}

and

p^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta-\lambda\Delta^{\prime\prime}-\mu\Delta^{\prime})=q^{*}(K_{Y}+\Gamma-\lambda\Gamma^{\prime\prime})+E_{2},

where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are
q
-exceptional divisors.
From
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.38]
, whose proof still works in the setting of complex analytic varieties,
we see that
E_{2}
is effective and
\operatorname{Supp}(p_{*}E_{2})
contains all the divisors contracted by
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
.
In particular,
E\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(p_{*}E_{2})
.
Moreover, since
K_{X}+(1-\lambda)\Delta
is nef, from the negativity lemma (see
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.39]
),
it follows that
E_{1}
is also effective.
Subtracting the above two equations, we get

(\lambda-\mu)p^{*}\Delta^{\prime}=E_{1}+E_{2}.

By applying
p_{*}
to the above equation and by using the fact that
\Delta^{\prime}
is a prime divisor, we deduce that
E=\Delta^{\prime}
.
This proves our claim.

Now, since
L|_{\Delta^{\prime}}\equiv 0
, it follows that
(K_{Y}+\Gamma)|_{q(p^{-1}_{*}{\Delta^{\prime}})}\equiv 0
.
Combining this with the argument of Lemma
6.9
, we see that,
if
C_{Y}\subseteq Y_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
is a curve with
(K_{Y}+\Gamma)\cdot C_{Y}>0
, then
f
is an isomorphism near the general points of
C_{Y}
.
Hence, the second part of the condition (7) also holds for
(Y,\Gamma)
.

Repeating this procedure for every component of
\Delta
on which the restriction of
L
is numerically trivial,
we may assume that
X
satisfies all the conditions of Step 1, as well as the following one:

(8)

L|_{\Delta_{i}}\not\equiv 0
for any irreducible component
\Delta_{i}
of
\Delta
.

In the following steps, we will argue as in Step 5 of the proof of
[
CHP16
, Theorem 8.2]
.

Step 3.

In this step, we reduce the problem to the case when
\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))
is a constant, independent of
n
.
We remark that the conditions (1) and (2) imply that
X
has klt singularities.
Let
S\subseteq X
be a component of
\Delta
.
Then
S
is Cohen-Macaulay by
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.25]
.
We note that in
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.25]
,
X
is assumed to be quasi-projective.
Nevertheless, the proof still works in our setting.
Indeed, being Cohen-Macaulay is a local property.
Moreover, the only step in
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.25]
which uses the projectivity assumption is to construct a ramified cyclic cover with a branched locus in general position.
Such a construction is still valid locally.

Thus
[
CHP16
, Lemma 8.1]
is applicable here and we have

(8.2)

h^{2}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))=0

for all
n\gg 0
.
Note that, by abuse of notation, here we also use
\Delta
to mean the reduced analytic space structure of the support of
\Delta
.
By
[
CHP16
, Corollary 8.1]
, there are two constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that, for all
n\gg 0,

we have

(8.3)

h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))=C_{1},

(8.4)

h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL-\Delta))=C_{2}.

We recall that

\displaystyle h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))+h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))

\displaystyle-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))+h^{3}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL)).

Thus, from equation (
8.3
) above, we deduce that

(8.5)

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))\geq\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))+h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))-C_{1}

for
n\gg 0
.
Therefore, if
\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}({nL}))
grows at least linearly in
n
, then we get that
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))
also grows at least linearly in
n
, which implies that
\kappa(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 1
.
Hence, by the condition (7),
we may assume that
\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}({nL}))
is a constant independent of
n
for the rest of the proof.
This implies that

(9)

For every curve
C\subseteq X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
, we have
L\cdot C=0
.

Our goal is to show that
h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))
grows at least linearly in
n
.

Step 4.
In this step, we show that
h^{1}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))
grows at least linearly in
n
.
To see this, let
(K_{\Delta}+\Xi)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(K_{X}+\Delta)|_{\Delta}
be defined by adjunction. Then from
[
Fuj00
, Remark 1.2(5)]
it follows that
(\Delta,\Xi)
has slc singularities, in particular,

is demi-normal (see
[
Kol13
, Definition 5.1]

[

, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

\chi(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))=\chi(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta})+\frac{1}{2}(nL|_{\Delta})\cdot(nL|_{\Delta}-K_{\Delta}).

Moreover, from
[
Kol92
, Proposition 16.6]
, we see that
\operatorname{Supp}(\Xi)
is contained in
X_{\operatorname{\textsubscript{\rm sing}}}
.
Hence the condition (9) above implies that

K_{\Delta}\cdot L|_{\Delta}=(K_{\Delta}+\Xi)\cdot L|_{\Delta}=(K_{X}+\Delta)|_{\Delta}\cdot L|_{\Delta}=(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot L\cdot\Delta=0,

where the last equality follows from the condition (6).
By the condition (6) again, we deduce that
(nL|_{\Delta})\cdot(nL|_{\Delta}-K_{\Delta})=0
.
Hence, from equation (
8.6
) it follows that
\chi(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))
is a constant independent of
n
. Now we claim that
L|_{\Delta}=k(K_{\Delta}+\Xi)
semi-ample. To see this observe that by the condition (6) we have
\nu(\Delta,L|_{\Delta})\leq 1
. Thus from the condition (8) and
[
CHP16
, Proposition 5.2]
it follows

is semi-ample.
Again, from the condition (8)

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta})\not\cong\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
for any

; in particular,
h^{0}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))
grows at least linearly in
n
.
Thanks to equation (
8.2
),
we deduce that
h^{1}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))
grows at least linearly in
n
.

Step 5.
We complete the proof in this step.
Consider the exact sequence

From the long exact sequence of cohomology we deduce that

(8.7)

h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL))\geq h^{1}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(nL-\Delta)).

Now since
h^{1}(\Delta,\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}(nL|_{\Delta}))
grows at least linearly in

and

grows at least linearly in

. Combining this along with the condition (7) and equation (
8.5
), we see that

grows at least linearly in

, and hence
\kappa(X,K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 1
. This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Theorem
1.2
holds if
K_{X}+(1-\lambda)\Delta
is not pseudoeffective for any

.

Proof.

We note that
X
is uniruled in this case.
By log abundance for projective threefolds, we may assume that
X
is not algebraic.
Replacing
(X,\Delta)
with the bimeromorphic model of Lemma
7.2
, we may assume that
there is a Mori fiber space
f\colon X\to Y
of relative dimension
1
such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
f
-numerically trivial.
Then by
[
DO24
, Theorem 2.5]
there is an effective divisor
\Gamma
on
Y
such that
K_{X}+\Delta=f^{*}(K_{Y}+\Gamma)
.
Moreover,
(Y,\Gamma)
is klt if
(X,\Delta)
is.

If
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor\neq 0
, then by property (6) of Lemma
7.2
there is a component
S
of
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
which dominates
Y
. Let
S^{n}\to S
be the normalization, then
(S^{n},\Delta_{S^{n}})
is lc, where
K_{S^{n}}+\Delta_{S^{n}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(K_{X}+\Delta)|_{S^{n}}
is defined by adjunction.
Then by log abundance for Kähler surfaces (see
[
DO24
, Theorem 1.33]
),
we deduce that
K_{S^{n}}+\Delta_{S^{n}}
is semi-ample. Let
g:S^{n}\to Y
be the induced surjective morphism, then
K_{S^{n}}+\Delta_{S^{n}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}g^{*}(K_{Y}+\Gamma)
. Hence
K_{Y}+\Gamma
is semi-ample by
[
DO24
, Lemma 1.8]
.
It follows that
K_{X}+\Delta
is semi-ample.

Now assume that
\lfloor\Delta\rfloor=0
.
Then the pair
(X,\Delta)
is klt by property (7) of Lemma
7.2
.
Thus
(Y,\Gamma)
is klt as well.
Hence again by log abundance for Kähler surfaces (see
[
DO24
, Theorem 1.33]
), we deduce that
K_{Y}+\Gamma
is semi-ample, and consequently,
K_{X}+\Delta
is semi-ample.
This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It follows from Lemma

.

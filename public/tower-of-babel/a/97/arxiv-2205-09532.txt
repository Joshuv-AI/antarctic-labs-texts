Dynamics of charge imbalance resolved negativity after a global quench in free scalar field theory
Hui-Huang Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the time evolution of charge imbalance resolved negativity after a global quench in the 1+1 dimensional complex Klein-Gordon theory. We focus on two types of global quenches which are called boundary state quench and mass quench respectively. We first study the boundary state quench where the post-quench dynamic is governed by a massless Hamiltonian. In this case, the temporal evolution of charged imbalance resolved negativity can be obtained first by evaluating the correlators of the fluxed twist field in the upper half plane and then applying Fourier transformation. We test our analytical formulas in the underlying lattice model numerically. We also study the mass quench in the complex harmonic chain where the system evolves according to a massive Hamiltonian after the quench. We argue that our results can be understood in the framework of quasi-particle picture.

2
Charge imbalance resolution of negativity

In this section, we will briefly review the decomposition of entanglement negativity under a global internal symmetry.

Let us first review some basic facts about the symmetry resolution of the entanglement entropy. We assume that our system exhibit a global
U(1)
symmetry generated by a local charge
Q
. If
[\rho,Q]=0
(this can be achieved if
\rho
only acts non-trivially on the eigenspace of
Q
), then we have
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
, which implies that
\rho_{A}
admits charge decomposition according to eigenvalues
q
of local charge
Q_{A}

\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}\mathcal{P}_{q}\rho_{A}=\oplus_{q}p(q)\rho_{A}(q),\quad p(q)={\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{q}\rho_{A}),

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{P}_{q}
is the projection operator which projects the space to the eigenspace corresponding to eigenvalue
q
.
The symmetry resolved Rényi entropies are defined as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log{\textrm{Tr}}[\rho_{A}(q)]^{n}.

(2.2)

It’s convenient to first introduce the charged moments of
\rho_{A}
,

Z_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}(e^{i\mu Q_{A}}\rho_{A}^{n}).

(2.3)

Then it’s sufficient to compute its Fourier transform

Z_{n}(q)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-iq\mu}Z_{n}(\mu)

(2.4)

to obtain the Rényi entropies of the sector with charge
q
as

S_{n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left[\frac{Z_{n}(q)}{Z_{1}(q)^{n}}\right].

(2.5)

Finally, the symmetry resolved entanglement entropy can be obtained by taking the replica limit
S(q)=\lim_{n\rightarrow 1}S_{n}(q)
.

Now we briefly review the symmetry decomposition of entanglement negativity under the
U(1)
charge
Q
. Since the charge
Q
is local, we can write
Q_{A}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
, where
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
are the charges corresponding to sub subsystem
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
respectively. From the relation
[\rho_{A},Q_{A}]=0
, performing a partial transposition with respect to the second region
A_{2}
of subsystem
A
, we obtain

[\rho_{A}^{T_{2}},\mathcal{Q}_{A}]=0,\quad\mathcal{Q}_{A}\equiv Q_{1}-Q_{2}^{T_{2}},

(2.6)

where we have introduced the charge imbalance operator
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
and we will denote its eigenvalues as
\mathrm{q}
to make a distinction with the eigenvalues of
Q_{A}
.
Then
\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}
has a block matrix form, each block was characterized by different eigenvalues
\mathrm{q}
of the imbalance operator
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
. If we write

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}=\oplus_{\mathrm{q}}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}},\quad\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}}{{\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})}.

(2.7)

Then we have

\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}=\oplus_{\mathrm{q}}p({\mathrm{q}})\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}),

(2.8)

where
p({\mathrm{q}})={\textrm{Tr}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})
is the probability of finding
{\mathrm{q}}
as the outcome of measurement of
\mathcal{Q}_{A}
.

The charge imbalance resolved negativity is defined as

\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{1}{2}({\textrm{Tr}}|\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}})|-1).

(2.9)

The total negativity is given by the sum of charge imbalance resolved negativity weighted by the corresponding probability

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{\mathrm{q}}p({\mathrm{q}})\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}}).

(2.10)

It’s useful to define the charge imbalance resolved Rényi negativity

\mathcal{N}_{n}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{N_{n}(\mathrm{q})-1}{2},\quad N_{n}({\mathrm{q}})={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}}({\mathrm{q}}))^{n}]=\frac{1}{p({\mathrm{q}})^{n}}{\textrm{Tr}}[\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}].

(2.11)

Then the charge imbalance entanglement negativity can be obtained by taking the limit

\mathcal{N}({\mathrm{q}})=\lim_{n_{e}\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{N}_{n_{e}}(\mathrm{q}).

(2.12)

The projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}
has the following integral representation

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{q}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu{\mathrm{q}}}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}.

(2.13)

It’s convenient to first introduce the charged Rényi negativity

R_{n}(\mu)={\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}],

(2.14)

and it’s Fourier transformation

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n}(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu\mathrm{q}}R_{n}(\mu)

(2.15)

then the charge imbalance resolved Rényi negativity are related by Fourier transform

\mathcal{N}_{n}({\mathrm{q}})=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n}(\mathrm{q})}{p(\mathrm{q})^{n}},\qquad p(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu{\mathrm{q}}}R_{1}(\mu).

(2.16)

It’s also useful to introduce the charged Rényi logarithmic negativity
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)
, defined as

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=\log{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}].

(2.17)

The charged logarithmic negativity is defined by taking the following replica limit

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\lim_{n_{e}\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{E}_{n_{e}}(\mu).

(2.18)

Then the charge imbalance resolved negativity can be obtained as

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})}{p(\mathrm{q})}-1\right),\quad\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})\equiv\lim_{n_{e}\rightarrow 1}\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2},n_{e}}(\mathrm{q})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\mu}{2\pi}e^{-i\mu\mathrm{q}}e^{\mathcal{E}(\mu)}.

(2.19)

In section
5
, we will use eq. (
2.19
) and eq. (
2.16
) to evaluate the charge imbalance resolved negativity.

3
Boundary state quench

In this paper, we will consider the 1+1 dimensional complex free scalar field theory with the Euclidean action given by

\mathcal{A}=\int d^{2}x(\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}\phi+m^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\phi).

(3.1)

This action exhibit a
U(1)
symmetry, i.e. the phase transformation of the field
\phi\rightarrow e^{i\theta}\phi,\phi^{\dagger}\rightarrow e^{-i\theta}\phi^{\dagger}
leaves the action invariant. The Hamiltonian of this theory is

H=\int dx(\pi^{\dagger}\pi+\partial_{x}\phi^{\dagger}\partial_{x}\phi+m^{2}\phi^{\dagger}\phi)

(3.2)

with
\pi
is the canonical momentum for
\phi
.

We can rewrite this theory in terms of two real scalar fields
\phi^{(1)}
and
\phi^{(2)}
with
\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi^{(1)}+i\phi^{(2)})
.
The Hamiltonian in terms of these variables becomes

H=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1,2}\int d^{2}x[(\pi^{(\alpha)})^{2}+(\partial_{x}\phi^{(\alpha)})^{2}+m^{2}(\phi^{(\alpha)})^{2}].

(3.3)

The Hamiltonian can be diagonalized in terms of particle and anti-particle mode operators
a(p),a^{\dagger}(p)
and
b(p),b^{\dagger}(p)
with the commutation relation
[a(p),a^{\dagger}(q)]=[b(p),b^{\dagger}(q)]=2\pi\delta(p-q)
and all other commutators vanishing. We have

H=\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}e(p)(a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)+b^{\dagger}(p)b(p))

(3.4)

with
e(p)=\sqrt{m^{2}+p^{2}}
, while the conserved charge corresponding to the global
U(1)
symmetry is

Q=\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}(a^{\dagger}(p)a(p)-b^{\dagger}(p)b(p))=\int dx(a^{\dagger}(x)a(x)-b^{\dagger}(x)b(x)).

(3.5)

In the second equal sign of above equation, the conserved charge are expressed as integral of local density in real space. Thus its value in a given subsystem
A
is the same integral restricted to
A
,

Q_{A}=\int_{A}dx(a^{\dagger}(x)a(x)-b^{\dagger}(x)b(x)).

(3.6)

The lattice version of the complex Klein-Gordon field theory is the complex harmonic chain which is equivalent to two decoupled real harmonic chains.
The Hamiltonian of the real harmonic chain made by
L
sites reads

H_{HC}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\left[\pi_{j}^{2}+m^{2}\phi_{j}^{2}+(\phi_{j+1}-\phi_{j})^{2}\right],

(3.7)

where periodic boundary conditions
\phi_{L}\equiv\phi_{0},\pi_{L}\equiv\pi_{0}
are imposed and variables
\pi_{j}
and
\phi_{j}
satisfy standard bosonic commutation relations
[\phi_{i},\phi_{j}]=[\pi_{i},\pi_{j}]=0
and
[\phi_{i},\pi_{j}]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ij}
. The lattice version of the complex scalar field theory is the sum of two of the above harmonic chain. In terms of the variables
\phi^{(1)},\pi^{(1)}
and
\phi^{(2)},\pi^{(2)}
, the Hamiltonian is

H_{CHC}=H_{HC}(\pi^{(1)},\phi^{(1)})+H_{HC}(\pi^{(2)},\phi^{(2)}).

(3.8)

The Hamiltonian eq. (
3.8
) can be diagonalized by introducing the creation and annihilation operators
a_{k},a_{k}^{\dagger}
and
b_{k},b_{k}^{\dagger}
, satisfying
[a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
and
[b_{k},b_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. In terms of these operators, the Hamiltonian eq. (
3.8
) is diagonal

H_{CHC}=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}e_{k}(a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}),\quad e_{k}=\sqrt{m^{2}+4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}\right)}.

(3.9)

While the
U(1)
charge is

Q=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}-b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}).

(3.10)

The conserved charge is local and can also be written in the position space and for a given subsystem
A
reads

Q_{A}=\sum_{j\in A}(a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j}-b_{j}^{\dagger}b_{j}).

(3.11)

3.1
The path integral approach to boundary state quench

In this section, we will focus on the case in which after the quench the mass of the scalar field is zero, so time evolution is governed by a CFT. Let’s first briefly review the imaginary time formalism for the global quenches where the post-quench dynamic is govern by a CFT. This quench setup is called a boundary state quench as the correlation measures after quench can be described by boundary conformal field theory (BCFT), where the gapped initial state are represent by a spacetime boundary.

The expectation value of a product of equal-time local operators in time dependent state
\ket{\psi(t)}
is

\langle\mathcal{O}(t,\{x_{i}\})\rangle=Z^{-1}\bra{\psi_{0}}e^{iHt-\tau_{0}H}\mathcal{O}(\{x_{i}\})e^{-iHt-\tau_{0}H}\ket{\psi_{0}}.

(3.12)

where two factors
e^{-\tau_{0}H}
have been introduced to make the path integral representation of this expectation value absolutely convergent and
Z=\bra{\psi_{0}}e^{-2\tau_{0}H}\ket{\psi_{0}}
is the normalization factor. The above prescription is equivalent to assuming that the initial state has the form
\ket{\psi_{0}}\propto e^{-\tau_{0}H}\ket{B}
, where
\ket{B}
is some conformal boundary state. One could interpret
\tau_{0}
as being proportional to the correlation length of the initial state. Thus, the predictions made by this approach are expected to be valid only in the spacetime scaling limit,
t\gg\tau_{0},|x_{i}-x_{j}|\gg\tau_{0},t/|x_{i}-x_{j}|=\text{finite}
. The above correlator admits a path integral represent

\langle\mathcal{O}(t,\{x_{i}\})\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int[d\phi(x,\tau)]\mathcal{O}(\{x_{i}\},\tau=\tau_{0}+it)e^{-\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}d\tau L}\braket{\psi_{0}|\phi(x,\tau_{2})}\braket{\phi(x,\tau_{1})|\psi_{0}}.

(3.13)

where
L
is the Euclidean Lagrangian corresponding to the post-quench Hamiltonian
H
. We need to identify
\tau_{1}=0
and
\tau_{2}=2\tau_{0}
. We should compute the path integral considering
\tau
real and only at the end of the computation to analytically continue it to
\tau=\tau_{0}+it
.

3.2
Evolution of charged moments of RDM

In this section, we briefly review the strategy of computing the charged Rényi negativity in two-dimensional CFT (see
[
40
]
for more details).
In a generic two-dimensional QFT, we can view the charged moments
Z_{n}(\mu)
(defined in eq. (
2.3
)) as the partition function on the Riemann surface
\mathcal{R}_{n,N}
pierced by an Aharonov-Bohm flux, such that the total phase accumulated by the field upon going through the entire surface is
\mu
. The presence of the flux corresponds to impose an additional twist on the boundary of subsystem
A
. This additional twist fuses with the replica (ordinary) twist field at the endpoints of subsystem
A
and can be implemented by two local fields
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}
named as fluxed twist fields and fluxed anti-twist fields. These fluxed twist fields take into account not only the internal permutational symmetry among the replicas but also the presence of the flux. The partition function on the fluxed Riemann surface is thus proportional to the
2N
-point function of these fluxed twist operators.

We consider the subsystem
A
consists of two disjoint intervals on the real axis,
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
with
A_{1}=[u_{1},u_{2}],A_{2}=[u_{3},u_{4}]
. The charged moments of RDM of vacuum state
\rho_{A}
, i.e.
\textrm{tr}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{i\mu Q_{A}}
is equivalent to a four-point function of the fluxed twist fields

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{i\mu Q_{A}}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{4})\rangle_{\text{strip}}~,\qquad w_{i}=u_{i}+i\tau,

(3.14)

where
w=u+i\tau
with
\tau\in\mathbb{R},\tau\in(0,2\pi)
is the coordinate of the strip.
The fluxed twist fields
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
and fluxed anti-twist field
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}
are primary operators and they have the same dimension
[
23
,
27
]

\Delta_{n,\mu}=\frac{1}{6}\left(n-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi n}.

(3.15)

The charged moments of the partially transposed RDM can be obtained from the correlator above with the fluxed twist field
\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}
at the endpoints of
A_{2}
exchanged while the remaining ones keep the same, giving

{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{3})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{4})\rangle_{\text{strip}}~.

(3.16)

By conformal map
z(w)=e^{\pi w/2\tau_{0}}
, the four point function on the strip are mapped to the upper half plane (UHP), then we can write

{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{i\mu Q_{A}}=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}|z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}z_{4}|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{4})\rangle_{\text{UHP}},

(3.17)

By global conformal symmetry, four-point functions of primary fields on the upper half plane depend on 6 cross ratios
\eta_{i,j}
and have the following structure

\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{3})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{4})\rangle_{\text{UHP}}=c_{n,\mu}^{2}\prod_{a=1}^{4}|z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}|^{-\Delta_{n,\mu}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,3}\eta_{2,4}}{\eta_{1,2}\eta_{1,4}\eta_{2,3}\eta_{3,4}}\right)^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\mathcal{F}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\})

(3.18)

where the cross ratios are defined by
\eta_{i,j}=\frac{(z_{i}-z_{j})(\bar{z}_{i}-\bar{z}_{j})}{(z_{i}-\bar{z}_{j})(\bar{z}_{i}-z_{j})}
and
\mathcal{F}(\{\eta_{j,k}\})
depend on the full operator content of the theory and usually is very complicated. The constants
c_{n,\mu}
are also non-universal and are known for some specific theories.

While for the charged Rényi negativity, we have

\begin{split}{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}\prod_{a=1}^{4}|z_{a}|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(z_{3})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(z_{4})\rangle_{\text{UHP}}\\
=c_{n,\mu}^{2}\prod_{a=1}^{4}\Big|\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}}\Big|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\frac{1}{\eta_{1,2}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\eta_{3,4}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,4}\eta_{2,3}}{\eta_{1,3}\eta_{2,4}}\right)^{\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}/2-\Delta_{n,\mu}}\mathcal{G}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\})\end{split}

(3.19)

Fortunately, in the spacetime scaling regime, we have
\eta_{i,j}\rightarrow 0,1
or
\infty
, and the non-universal functions
\mathcal{F}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\}),\mathcal{G}_{n}(\{\eta_{j,k}\})
are just constant. In the following section we will just omit these non-universal functions and just focus on the universal part.

There is one important comment we must address. To compute the four-point function of fluxed twist fields on the strip, we apply a conformal mapping from the strip to the UHP. This is somewhat similar to the case of computation of the negativity at finite temperature, where it is well-known that if the partial transposition involves an infinite part of an infinite system at finite temperature, using the conformal map from the cylinder to the complex plane is naively wrong
[
56
]
. This is not the case if one, for example, is interested in the negativity between two (adjacent or disjoint) finite intervals
[
57
,
58
]
. In our situation, we are concentrated on the charged Rényi negativity between two finite intervals. Thus it’s indeed free of these troubles.

4
Evolution of charged logarithmic negativity in boundary state quench

In this section, we will consider the dynamics of the charged logarithmic negativity between two intervals after a global quench whose post-quench evolution is governed by a massless Hamiltonian.

4.1
Bipartite system

It’s convenient to first study the case in which
A=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
is the entire system. In this case,
\rho_{A}
corresponds to a pure state. As explained in previous section, in this case, the temporal evolution of the charged Rényi negativity is governed by
\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\rangle
on the strip. The strip two-point function can be computed from the one in the UHP which has the standard form

\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\rangle_{\text{UHP}}=|(z_{1}-\bar{z}_{1})(z_{2}-\bar{z}_{2})\eta_{1,2}|^{-\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}},

(4.1)

where

\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}=\begin{cases}\Delta_{n,2\mu},\quad\text{odd}~n\\
2\Delta_{\frac{n}{2},\mu},\quad\text{even}~n\end{cases}

(4.2)

After mapping the correlator in the UHP to the one in the strip, we find

\begin{split}{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]=\langle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\rangle_{\text{strip}}\\
=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{2\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}\prod_{a=1}^{2}\Big|\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}}\frac{1}{\eta_{1,2}}\Big|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}.\end{split}

(4.3)

In the spacetime scaling limit regime
t\gg\tau_{0},|u_{i}-u_{j}|\gg\tau_{0}
, we have

\log\Big|\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}}\Big|\rightarrow-\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}},\qquad\log\eta_{i,j}\rightarrow\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}(|u_{i}-u_{j}|-\max(2t,|u_{i}-u_{j}|)).

(4.4)

Then it’s straightforward to derive the charged Rényi logarithmic negativity

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}}{2\tau_{0}}\min(2t,l_{1}).

(4.5)

In particular, for
n=1
, the result is

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}h_{1}(2\mu)\min(2t,l_{1}),\qquad h_{1}(\mu)=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}+\frac{\mu}{2\pi}.

(4.6)

The charged logarithmic negativity is obtained by taking replica limit
n_{e}\rightarrow 1
in
\mathcal{E}_{n_{e}}(\mu)

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}h(\mu)\min(2t,l_{1}),\qquad h(\mu)=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{\pi^{2}}+\frac{2\mu}{\pi}.

(4.7)

4.2
Two adjacent intervals

In this case, the quench dynamics of the charged Rényi negativity between two adjacent intervals can be obtained by studying the three point function
\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\rangle
on the strip which can be computed by conformal mapping from the three point function on the UHP. After dropping the non-universal functions, we finally have

\begin{split}{\textrm{Tr}}[(\rho_{A}^{T_{2}})^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}]=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{2}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{3})\rangle_{\text{strip}}\\
=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{\Delta}\prod_{a=1}^{3}\Big|\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}}\Big|^{\Delta_{(a)}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,3}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}-2\Delta_{n,\mu}}}{\eta_{1,2}^{\Delta_{n}^{(2)}}\eta_{2,3}^{\Delta_{n}^{(2)}}}\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

(4.8)

where
\Delta=2\Delta_{n,\mu}+\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}
,
\Delta_{(1)}=\Delta_{(3)}=\Delta_{n,\mu}
and
\Delta_{(2)}=\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}
. Then the CFT prediction for the time evolution of the charged logarithmic Rényi negativity is given by

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{4\tau_{0}}\left[\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2}))-(\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}-2\Delta_{n,\mu})\min(2t,l_{1}+l_{2})\right].

(4.9)

where we have defined
l_{1}=u_{2}-u_{1},l_{2}=u_{3}-u_{2}
.

The temporal evolution of the charged logarithmic negativity is obtained by taking replica limit
n_{e}\rightarrow 1
in
\mathcal{E}_{n_{e}}(\mu)
. The result is

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{4\tau_{0}}\left[h(\mu)(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2}))-h_{2}(\mu)\min(2t,l_{1}+l_{2})\right].

(4.10)

with

h_{2}(\mu)=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}+\frac{\mu}{\pi}.

(4.11)

From eq. (
4.9
), we have

\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{4\tau_{0}}\left[h_{1}(2\mu)(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2}))-h_{3}(\mu)\min(2t,l_{1}+l_{2})\right],

(4.12)

where
h_{3}(\mu)=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}
.

4.3
Two disjoint intervals

The universal part the charged Rényi negativity is

\begin{split}&{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{A}^{n}e^{i\mu\mathcal{Q}_{A}}=\langle\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{1})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{2})\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{n,\mu}(w_{3})\mathcal{T}_{n,\mu}(w_{4})\rangle_{\text{strip}}\\
&=\left(\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\right)^{4\Delta_{n,\mu}}\prod_{a=1}^{4}\Big|\frac{z_{a}}{z_{a}-\bar{z}_{a}}\Big|^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\frac{1}{\eta_{1,2}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}\eta_{3,4}^{\Delta_{n,\mu}}}\left(\frac{\eta_{1,4}\eta_{2,3}}{\eta_{1,3}\eta_{2,4}}\right)^{\Delta^{(2)}_{n,\mu}/2-\Delta_{n,\mu}}.\end{split}

(4.13)

In this case, the CFT prediction for the time evolution of the charged logarithmic Rényi negativity is given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}_{n}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\big[\Delta_{n,\mu}\left(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2})\right)+(\Delta_{n,\mu}^{(2)}/2-\Delta_{n,\mu})\\
&\times(\max(2t,l_{1}+l_{2}+d)+\max(2t,d)-\max(2t,l_{1}+d)-\max(2t,l_{2}+d))\big].\end{split}

(4.14)

where
l_{1}=u_{2}-u_{1},l_{2}=u_{4}-u_{3}
and
d=u_{3}-u_{2}
are the length of the subsubsystem
A_{1},A_{2}
and the distance between them respectively.

The charged logarithmic negativity is easily obtained as

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}\big[h_{1}(\mu)\left(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2})\right)+\frac{1}{2}h_{2}(\mu)\\
&\times(\max(2t,l_{1}+l_{2}+d)+\max(2t,d)-\max(2t,l_{1}+d)-\max(2t,l_{2}+d))\big].\end{split}

(4.15)

As before, we also report the expression of
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)
here since it will be useful in the computation of charge imbalance resolved negativity. The result is

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{2\tau_{0}}[h_{1}(\mu)(\min(2t,l_{1})+\min(2t,l_{2}))\\
&+\frac{1}{2}h_{3}(\mu)(\max(2t,l_{1}+l_{2}+d)+\max(2t,d)-\max(2t,l_{1}+d)-\max(t,l_{2}+d))].\end{split}

(4.16)

We stress that eq. (
4.9
) and eq. (
4.14
) are only valid for CFT in which there is a perfect linear dispersion. However this is not the case for the underlying lattice model, where the excitation has non-linear dispersion. We will discuss how to adapt eq. (
4.9
) and eq. (
4.14
) to describe the dynamics of charged Rényi (logarithmic) negativity in the mass quench protocol of the complex harmonic chain.

5
Charge imbalance resolved negativity

In this section, using the results obtained in the last section, we will compute the dynamics of the charge imbalance resolved negativity between two intervals after a global quench to a conformal Hamiltonian. According to the discussion in section
2
, our strategy is to first compute
\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})
and
p(\mathrm{q})
(cf. eq. (
2.19
) and eq. (
2.16
)) from the charged moments of partially transposed RDM, and then the charge imbalance resolved negativity is given by eq. (
2.19
).

5.1
Bipartite system

\boldsymbol{t<l_{1}/2}

In this time region, we have

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-h(\mu)\frac{\pi t}{\tau_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-h_{1}(2\mu)\frac{\pi t}{\tau_{0}}.

(5.1)

After Fourier transformation, we find

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{q}}e^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}}{4t/\tau_{0}}-\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}}}{2\sqrt{t/\tau_{0}}}{\textrm{Re}}\left[\mathrm{Erfi}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(i\mathrm{q}+2t/\tau_{0})}{2\sqrt{t/\tau_{0}}}\right)\right],

(5.2)

where
\mathrm{Erfi}(x)
is the imaginary error function

\mathrm{Erfi}(x)=\frac{-2i}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{ix}dte^{-t^{2}}\xrightarrow{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e^{x^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}x}.

(5.3)

A very similar expression exists for
p(\mathrm{q})
which we omit here. In the spacetime scaling limit, we have much more concise results

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{2e^{\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}}t/\tau_{0}}{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+4\pi t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}

(5.4)

and the probability distribution is given by

p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{(1+(-1)^{\mathrm{q}})t/\tau_{0}}{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.5)

Thus the charge imbalance resolved negativity is obtained from eq. (
2.19
)

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}}(\mathrm{q}^{2}+t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2})}{(1+(-1)^{\mathrm{q}})(\mathrm{q}^{2}+4t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2})}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.6)

\boldsymbol{t>l_{1}/2}

In this time region, we can simply make the replacement
t\rightarrow l_{1}/2
to obtain corresponding quantities. In particular, the charge imbalance resolved negativity is given by

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi l_{1}}{4\tau_{0}}}(4\mathrm{q}^{2}+l_{1}^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2})}{4(1+(-1)^{\mathrm{q}})(\mathrm{q}^{2}+l_{1}^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2})}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.7)

5.2
Two adjacent intervals

Without loss of generality, we will assume
l_{1}<l_{2}
, the other case can be worked out similarly.

\boldsymbol{t<l_{1}/2<l_{2}/2<(l_{1}+l_{2})/2}

For this early time, we have

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-(2h(\mu)-h_{2}(\mu))\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-(2h_{1}(2\mu)-h_{3}(\mu))\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}.

(5.8)

Then after Fourier transformation, we find

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{q}}e^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}}{3t/\tau_{0}}-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}{\sqrt{3t/\tau_{0}}}{\textrm{Re}}\left[\mathrm{Erfi}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{3}}(2i\mathrm{q}+3t/\tau_{0})}{2\sqrt{t/\tau_{0}}}\right)\right],

(5.9)

Therefore in the spacetime scaling limit (
t\gg\tau_{0}
), we have

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{6e^{\frac{\pi t}{4\tau_{0}}}t/\tau_{0}}{4\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+9\pi(t/\tau_{0})^{2}}.

(5.10)

In the spacetime scaling regime, the probability distribution can be computed similarly and the finial result is

p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{t/\tau_{0}}{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.11)

Then the charge imbalance resolved negativity is given by

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{3e^{\frac{\pi t}{4\tau_{0}}}(\mathrm{q}^{2}+t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2})}{4\mathrm{q}^{2}+9t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.12)

\boldsymbol{l_{1}/2<t<l_{2}/2<(l_{1}+l_{2})/2}

In this case we have

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-(h(\mu)-h_{2}(\mu))\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}-h(\mu)\frac{\pi l_{1}}{4\tau_{0}}.

(5.13)

Then

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{q}}e^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}}{(l_{1}+t)/\tau_{0}}-\frac{\pi(l_{1}+2t)}{8\tau_{0}}}}{\sqrt{(l_{1}+t)/\tau_{0}}}{\textrm{Re}}\left[\mathrm{Erfi}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(2i\mathrm{q}+(l_{1}+t)/\tau_{0})}{2\sqrt{(l_{1}+t)/\tau_{0}}}\right)\right].

(5.14)

In the spacetime scaling limit

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{2e^{\frac{\pi l_{1}}{8\tau_{0}}}(l_{1}+t)/\tau_{0}}{4\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(l_{1}+t)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.15)

The probability distribution is

p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4(l_{1}+2t)/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(l_{1}+2t)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.16)

Then the charge-imbalance resolved negativity is

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+(l_{1}+2t)^{2}}{16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+4(l_{1}+t)^{2}}\frac{(l_{1}+t)e^{\frac{\pi l_{1}}{8\tau_{0}}}}{l_{1}+2t}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.17)

\boldsymbol{l_{2}/2<t<(l_{1}+l_{2})/2}

In this time region, we have

\mathcal{E}(\mu)=h_{2}(\mu)\frac{\pi t}{2\tau_{0}}-h(\mu)\frac{(l_{1}+l_{2})\pi}{4\tau_{0}},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)=-\frac{\pi}{4\tau_{0}}(h_{1}(2\mu)(l_{1}+l_{2})-2th_{3}(\mu)).

(5.18)

Then applying Fourier transformation, we find

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{q}}e^{\frac{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}}{(l-t)/\tau_{0}}-\frac{\pi l}{8\tau_{0}}}}{\sqrt{(l-t)/\tau_{0}}}{\textrm{Re}}\left[\mathrm{Erfi}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(2i\mathrm{q}+(l-t)/\tau_{0})}{2\sqrt{(l-t)/\tau_{0}}}\right)\right].

(5.19)

Here and in the following sections we will always use the definition
l\equiv l_{1}+l_{2}
. In the spacetime scaling limit

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{2e^{\frac{\pi(l-2t)}{8\tau_{0}}}(l-t)/\tau_{0}}{4\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(l-t)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.20)

the probability distribution is

p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4l/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi l^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.21)

the charge imbalance resolved negativity is then easily obtained as

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+l^{2}}{16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+4(l-t)^{2}}\frac{(l-t)e^{\frac{\pi(l-2t)}{8\tau_{0}}}}{l}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.22)

\boldsymbol{t>(l_{1}+l_{2})/2}

In this case, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(\mu)=-[h(\mu)-h_{2}(\mu)]\frac{\pi(l_{1}+l_{2})}{4\tau_{0}}=\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu).\end{split}

(5.23)

Then the charge imbalance resolved negativity is

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=0.

(5.24)

5.3
Two disjoint intervals

The computation of charge imbalance resolved negativity for two disjoint intervals is similar to previous subsection, here we just report the final results (the space time scaling limit has been taken). In this circumstance, the different size relation between
l_{1},l_{2}
and
d
may lead to different behavior. However, for simplicity, we will only consider the case
d<l_{1}<l_{2}
.

\boldsymbol{0<t<d/2<l_{1}/2}

At this early time stage, we have
\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)
, therefore

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=0.

(5.25)

\boldsymbol{d/2<t<l_{1}/2}

In this time period, we have

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4e^{\frac{\pi(2t-d)}{8\tau_{0}}}(6t-d)/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(6t-d)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}},\quad p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{t/\tau_{0}}{\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi t^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.26)

Then the charge imbalance resolved negativity is obtained straightforward as

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{2e^{\frac{\pi(2t-d)}{8\tau_{0}}}(\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+t^{2})}{16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+(6t-d)^{2}}\frac{6t-d}{t}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.27)

\boldsymbol{l_{1}/2<l_{2}/2<t<(l_{1}+d)/2}

In this time region, the following results are obtained (in the spacetime scaling limit)

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4e^{\frac{\pi(2t-d)}{8\tau_{0}}}(l-d+2t)/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(l-d+2t)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}},\quad p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4l/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi l^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.28)

The charge imbalance resolved negativity is given by

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi(2t-d)}{8\tau_{0}}}(16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+l^{2})}{32\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+2(2t+l-d)^{2}}\frac{2t+l-d}{l}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.29)

\boldsymbol{(l_{2}+d)/2<t<(l_{1}+l_{2}+d)/2}

In this time period, we have

\mathcal{Z}_{T_{2}}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4e^{\frac{\pi(d+l-2t)}{8\tau_{0}}}(d+2l-2t)/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi(d+2l-2t)^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}},\quad p(\mathrm{q})=\frac{4l/\tau_{0}}{16\pi\mathrm{q}^{2}+\pi l^{2}/\tau_{0}^{2}}.

(5.30)

The charge imbalance resolved negativity is

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi(d+l-2t)}{8\tau_{0}}}(16\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+l^{2})}{32\mathrm{q}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}+2(d+2l-2t)^{2}}\frac{d+2l-2t}{l}-\frac{1}{2}.

(5.31)

\boldsymbol{t>(l_{1}+l_{2}+d)/2}

In this late time region, we have
\mathcal{E}(\mu)=\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mu)
again, and charge imbalance resolved negativity is zero

\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{q})=0.

(5.32)

5.4
Total negativity

Having obtained the quench dynamics of the charge imbalance resolved negativity, it’s an easy task to check whether we can recover the known results of quench dynamics of entanglement negativity. Using the formula

\sum_{\mathrm{q}=-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{a^{2}}{\mathrm{q}^{2}+a^{2}}=\coth(\pi a)\xrightarrow{a\rightarrow\infty}1

6
Numerical test

In this section, we test our analytical predictions against numerical computation in the complex harmonic chain which is the lattice version of our complex Klein-Gordon field theory. We will use the correlation matrix techniques to obtain the charged Rényi (logarithmic) negativity.

In section
3
, we have already reported the Hamiltonian and the
U(1)
conserved charge of the complex harmonic chain (cf. eq. (
3.9
) and eq. (
3.10
)). From the dispersion relation, it’s easy to see that the Hamiltonian has zero modes for
k=0
and
m=0
, and the group velocities are obtained from the dispersion relation as

v_{p}\equiv\frac{\partial e(p)}{\partial p}=\frac{\sin(p)}{\sqrt{m^{2}+4\sin^{2}(p/2)}},\qquad p\equiv\frac{2\pi k}{L}.

(6.1)

The maximum velocity
v_{\text{max}}\equiv\max_{p}v_{p}
determine s the spreading of entanglement and correlations.

In the global quench protocol, for
t<0
, the system is prepared in the ground state of the Hamiltonian

H_{CHC}(m_{0})=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}e_{0,k}(a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}),\quad e_{0,k}=\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}+4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi k}{L}\right)}.

(6.2)

At
t=0

H_{CHC}(m)
with a different value
m

\ket{\psi(t)}=e^{-iH_{CHC}(m)t}\ket{\psi_{0}}

m=0

m=0

m\neq 0
.

Since the Hamiltonian of a complex harmonic chain is just two copies of a real harmonic chain’s Hamiltonian, we could focus on the real harmonic chain first, and take the double copies into account only at the end of the calculations. In the correlation matrix method of computing entanglement measures, we first need to know the following two-point correlators of the real scalars

\begin{split}\mathbf{X}_{rs}(t)\equiv\bra{\psi_{0}}\phi_{r}(t)\phi_{s}(t)\ket{\psi_{0}},\\
\mathbf{P}_{rs}(t)\equiv\bra{\psi_{0}}\pi_{r}(t)\pi_{s}(t)\ket{\psi_{0}},\\
\mathbf{M}_{rs}(t)\equiv\bra{\psi_{0}}\phi_{r}(t)\pi_{s}(t)\ket{\psi_{0}},\\
\end{split}

(6.3)

where

\phi_{r}(t)=e^{iH_{HC}(m)t}\phi_{r}(0)e^{-iH_{HC}(m)t},\qquad\pi_{r}(t)=e^{iH_{HC}(m)t}\pi_{r}(0)e^{-iH_{HC}(m)t}.

(6.4)

The explicit form of these correlators is given by

&\mathbf{P}_{rs}(t)=\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}P_{k}(t)\cos\left[\frac{2\pi k}{L}(r-s)\right],\\
&\mathbf{M}_{rs}(t)=\frac{i}{2}\delta_{rs}-\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}M_{k}(t)\cos\left[\frac{2\pi k}{L}(r-s)\right],\end{split}

(6.5)

where

\begin{split}&X_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{e_{k}}\left(\frac{e_{k}}{e_{0,k}}\cos^{2}(e_{k}t)+\frac{e_{0,k}}{e_{k}}\sin^{2}(e_{k}t)\right),\\
&P_{k}(t)=e_{k}\left(\frac{e_{k}}{e_{0,k}}\sin^{2}(e_{k}t)+\frac{e_{0,k}}{e_{k}}\cos^{2}(e_{k}t)\right),\\
&M_{k}(t)=\left(\frac{e_{k}}{e_{0,k}}-\frac{e_{0,k}}{e_{k}}\right)\sin(e_{k}t)\cos(e_{k}t).\end{split}

(6.6)

7.1
Quasi-particle picture for entanglement entropy

In free scalar theory, since the particle numbers in each momentum mode are conserved quantities, the time evolution leads to local and quasi-local observables converging to their average values in the Generalized Gibbs Ensemble (GGE) instead of ordinary Gibbs ensemble.

The quasi-particle picture of the time evolution of entanglement after a global quantum quench has been proposed in
[
45
]
, The basic idea is that one can view the initial state as a source of quasi-particle excitations. Pairs of particles emitted from the same point in space are highly entangled whereas particles produced from points far apart are incoherent. We assume that the pairs of quasi-particle are created uniformly with opposite momenta
(p,-p)
. After the production, these quasi-particles travel ballistically with velocity
v_{p}=-v_{-p}
. The entanglement entropy and Rényi entropy of subsystem
A
are proportional to the pairs of entangled quasi-particle shared with its complement at a given time
t
.

In free models, we have

S_{n}(t)=\int\frac{dp}{2\pi}s^{(n)}_{GGE}(p)\min(2|v_{p}|t,l),

(7.1)

with
l
being the length of subsystem
A
and
s^{(n)}_{GGE}(p)
is momentum space density of the Rényi entropies in the GGE thermodynamic state
[
59
]
. Recall that the well-known result of the CFT prediction of the quench to massless dynamics of Rényi entropies

S_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{\pi\Delta_{n,0}}{2\tau_{0}}\min(2t,l).

(7.2)

We can formally obtained the formula eq. (
7.1

7.2

t\rightarrow|v_{p}|t
,
-\frac{\pi\Delta_{n,\mu}}{2\tau_{0}}\rightarrow s^{(n)}_{GGE}(p)
and then integrating over all possible
p

7.2
Quasi-particle picture for charged logarithmic negativity

The complex harmonic chain is a free models and the stationary behavior of local and quasi-local observables is described by the GGE

\rho_{GGE}=Z^{-1}e^{-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{(a)}a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}}\otimes e^{-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{(b)}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}},

(7.3)

where
\lambda_{k}^{(a)},\lambda_{k}^{(b)}
are Lagrange mulitpliers and
Z
is the normalisation constant such that
{\textrm{Tr}}\rho_{GGE}=1
. We have

Z=\prod_{i=a,b}{\textrm{Tr}}e^{-\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{(i)}\hat{n}_{k}^{(i)}}=\prod_{i=a,b}\prod_{k}\sum_{n_{k}^{(i)}=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{k}^{(i)}n_{k}^{(i)}}=\prod_{i=a,b}\prod_{k}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}^{(i)}})^{-1}.

(7.4)

The mode occupation number
\hat{n}_{k}^{(a)}\equiv a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k},\hat{n}_{k}^{(b)}\equiv b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}
are conserved quantities. Therefore we have

\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{n}_{k}^{(i)}\ket{\psi_{0}}={\textrm{Tr}}[\hat{n}_{k}^{(i)}\rho_{GGE}]=(e^{\lambda_{k}^{(i)}}-1)^{-1}.

(7.5)

In the ground state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
, we have
\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{n}_{k}^{(a)}\ket{\psi_{0}}=\bra{\psi_{0}}\hat{n}_{k}^{(b)}\ket{\psi_{0}}\equiv n_{k}
, thus

\lambda_{k}^{(a)}=\lambda_{k}^{(b)}=\log(1+n_{k}^{-1})\equiv\lambda_{k}.

(7.6)

Since the quasi-particle picture is not sensitive to
A_{1}\cup A_{2}
not being a pure state. For our purpose, it’s sufficient to compute the charged moments of
\rho_{GGE}

\begin{split}&{\textrm{Tr}}[\rho_{GGE}^{n}e^{i\mu Q_{A}}]=Z^{-n}\prod_{k}\big|{\textrm{Tr}}[e^{-(n\lambda_{k}-i\mu)a_{k}^{\dagger}a_{k}}]\big|^{2}\\
&=Z^{-n}\prod_{k}\big|\sum_{n_{k}=0}^{\infty}e^{-(n\lambda_{k}-i\mu)n_{k}}\big|^{2}=\prod_{k}\Big|\frac{(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}})^{n}}{1-e^{-(n\lambda_{k}-i\mu)}}\Big|^{2}\\
&=\prod_{k}|(1+n_{k})^{n}-e^{i\mu}n_{k}^{n}|^{-2}.\end{split}

(7.7)

We have find the density of the logarithmic charged moment in momentum space

\epsilon_{n,\mu}(k)=-2\log|(1+n_{k})^{n}-e^{i\mu}n_{k}^{n}|.

(7.8)

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we discuss the temporal evolution of charge imbalance resolved negativity after two types of global quenches. Firstly, we have considered the boundary state quench in which the post-quench dynamics is governed by a conformal Hamiltonian. Evaluating the correlators of the fluxed twist fields in the UHP, we obtained the quench dynamics of charged Rényi negativity. Then the charge resolved negativity is obtained by Fourier transformation. The total negativity can be reconstructed from these charge resolved ones. We also discussed the mass quench in the underlying lattice model and made conjectures based on the quasi-particle picture.

It would be very interesting to investigate the evolution of other charge resolved entanglement measures in the same quenches discussed in this paper, or studying other quench setups such as local joining quench or local operator quenches in field theories and in holographic theories. One may find the breakdown of quasi-particle picture in certain circumstance
[
60
,
61
]
. We will turn to these problems in the future work.

The uniform homotopy category
Sanjeevi Krishnan and Crichton Ogle

Abstract
. This paper gives a uniform-theoretic refinement of classical homotopy theory. Both cubical sets (with connections) and uniform spaces admit classes of weak equivalences, special cases of classical weak equivalences, appropriate for the respective Lipschitz and uniform settings. Cubical sets and uniform spaces admit the additional compatible structures of categories of (co)fibrant objects. A categorical equivalence between classical homotopy categories of cubical sets and spaces lifts to a full and faithful embedding from an associated Lipschitz homotopy category of cubical sets into an associated uniform homotopy category of uniform spaces. Bounded cubical cohomology generalizes to a representable theory on the Lipschitz homotopy category. Bounded singular cohomology on path-connected spaces generalizes to a representable theory on the uniform homotopy category. Along the way, this paper develops a cubical analogue of Kan’s Ex ∞ \mathrm{Ex}^{\infty} functor and proves a cubical approximation theorem for uniform maps.

Classical combinatorial homotopy theory can be refined so as to take into account geometry implicit in the combinatorics.

2.2.
Pseudometric

An
extended

pseudometric
on a set
X
is a function

\rho:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty]

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\rho(x,x)=0
for all
x\in X

(2)

\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
for all
x,y\in X

(3)

\rho(x,z)\leqslant\rho(x,y)+\rho(y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X

An extended pseudometric
\rho
is an
extended metric
if
\rho
nevers takes the value
0
on pairs of distinct points and a
metric
if
\rho
never takes the values
0,\infty
on pairs of distinct points.
Take a
(pseudo)metric space
to mean a set
X
implicitly equipped with an (extended pseudo)metric on it.
We say that a pair
x,y
in a pseudometric space
X
are
\delta
-close
if the extended pseudometric on
X
takes
(x,y)
to a number no greater than
\delta
.
The
diameter
of a pseudometric space
X
is the supremum of the image of the extended pseudometric.
Riemannian manifolds will be regarded as metric spaces whose metrics are the Riemannian distance functions.
Take an
\ell_{p}
-simplicial complex
to mean a simplicial complex equipped with an extended metric as defined in the following example.

Example 2.15
.

An
\ell_{p}
-simplex
is the topological simplex

\nabla_{\ell_{p}}[n]=\{(t_{0},t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{I}^{n+1}\;|\;t_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}=1\}

with metric inherited from the metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
equipped with its
p
-norm.
A
\ell_{p}
-simplicial complex is a simplicial complex
X
regarded as a pseudometric space so that two points
x,y\in X
are
\delta
-close if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}<\delta
for some sequence
x=x_{0},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}=y
of points in
X
such that
x_{i-1},x_{i}
are
\lambda_{i}
-close in a common simplex, naturally identified with a geometric
\ell_{p}
-simplex under a linear homeomorphism, for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

A
\lambda
-(bi)-Lipschitz
function is a function
f:X\rightarrow Y
of pseudometric spaces such that for each
\delta>0
,
f(x),f(y)
are
\lambda\delta
-close if (and only if)
x,y
are
\delta
-close.
A (bi-)Lipschitz function is a
\lambda
-(bi-)Lipschitz function for some
\lambda>0
.

Pseudometric (co)limits are (co)limits of underlying sets equipped with certain universal extended pseudometrics constructed; we refer the reader elsewhere for details

All

2.3.
Uniform

A map
f:X\rightarrow Y
of pseudometric spaces is
uniform
if for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,
f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})
are
\epsilon
-close whenever
x_{1},x_{2}
are
\delta
-close.
A function between pseudometric spaces is uniform if it is Lipschitz, but need not be Lipschitz even if it is uniform.
Pseudometrics can be abstracted up to uniform equivalence as follows.
In fact, it is possible to define two different metrics
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
on the same set
X
such that the identity function
1_{X}
defines uniform maps
X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
and
X_{2}\rightarrow X_{1}
, where
X_{i}
is
X
equipped with
\rho_{i}
.
A
uniformity
on a set
X
is a collection
\mathscr{E}
of reflexive relations
X\rightharpoonup X
, called
entourages
, such that the following axioms hold:

(1)

E^{-1}\in\mathscr{E}
whenever
E\in\mathscr{E}

(2)

for each
E\in\mathscr{E}
, there exists
E^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\in\mathscr{E}
with
\mathop{graph}{(E^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\circ E^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})}\subset\mathop{graph}{(E)}
.

(3)

for all
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E}
, there exists
E\in\mathscr{E}
with
\mathop{graph}{(E)}=\mathop{graph}{(E_{1})}\cap\mathop{graph}{(E_{2})}
.

(4)

A reflexive relation
R:X\rightharpoonup X
lies in
\mathscr{E}
whenever there exists an entourage
E\in\mathscr{E}
with
\mathop{graph}{(R)}\subset\mathop{graph}{(E)}
.

The first axiom abstracts the symmetry axiom of a metric.
The second axiom abstracts the triangle inequality of a metric.
The latter two axioms guarantee that the entourages form a neighborhood basis with respect to a suitable topology.
Pseudometric spaces
X
will be regarded as uniform spaces equipped with the following
uniformity associated to the pseudometric
on
X
in the following example.
A uniform space is
separated
if the intersection of the graphs of all its entourages is the diagonal in
X^{2}
.
A
uniform space
is a set with a uniformity on it.
Every pseudometric space
X
with extended pseudometric
\rho
has an
underlying uniform space
having the same underlying set as
X
and whose uniformity is the smallest uniformity whose entourages include all relations of the form
E_{\epsilon}
with

\mathop{graph}{(E_{\epsilon})}=\{(x,y)\in X^{2}\;|\;\rho(x,y)<\epsilon\}.

Pseudometric spaces will often be implicitly taken to mean their underlying uniform spaces.
In this manner, we regard Riemannian manifolds are examples of uniform spaces.
A
uniform map
will be taken to mean a function between uniform spaces that pulls back entourages to entourages.

Example 2.18
.

Every Lipschitz map is uniform.

Example 2.19
.

Every surjective bi-Lipschitz map is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{U}
.

The
uniform topology
of a uniform space
X
is the completely regular topology on
X
with respect to which a neighborhood basis for each
x\in X
is the collection of all sets of the form
E[x]
for entourages
E
of
X
.
The
underlying space
of a uniform space
X
will be taken to mean the underlying set of
X
equipped with the uniform topology of
X
.
A uniform space is separated if and only if its underlying space is Tychonoff.
Underlying spaces of uniform spaces are completely regular.
Every completely regular space
X
admits a
fine uniformity
, the unique maximal uniformity turning
X
into a uniform space whose uniform topology coincide with the original topology on
X
.
Every uniform map defines a map of underlying spaces.

Corollary 24, §II
[
20
]
.

Each compact Hausdorff space underlies exactly one uniform space.

In this manner, each compact Hausdorff space will be automatically regarded as a uniform space.

2.3.1.
Constructions

Uniform (co)limits
will simply refer to (co)limits in the category of (uniform) spaces and (uniform) maps.
The underlying spaces of uniform colimits are colimits of underlying spaces
[
20

.
The categories of (uniform) spaces and (uniform) maps, while complete and cocomplete, are not Cartesian closed.
This defect can be remedied in a standard way (eg.
[
38

), except that the role of compact Hausdorff spaces in the definition of compactly generated spaces is played by connected, locally connected metric spaces.
Write
\mathscr{T}
for the category of Tychonoff spaces which are colimits of compact, locally connected, second-countable spaces.
The coreflective hull, in an epi-reflective subcategory
\mathscr{R}
of spaces and maps, of a class of compact Hausdorff spaces closed under finite products forms a Cartesian closed coreflective subcategory
[
40

of
\mathscr{R}
.
The following theorem is a consequence.

Proposition 2.20
.

The category
\mathscr{T}
satisfies the following:

(1)

\mathscr{T}
is Cartesian closed

(2)

\mathscr{T}
is a coreflective subcategory of the category of Tychonoff spaces and continuous maps between them.

Let
\mathscr{U}
denote the category of separated uniform colimits of connected, locally connected metric spaces.
The category
\mathscr{U}

\mathscr{U}
-objects.

The category of separated uniform spaces and uniform maps is a reflective subcategory of the category of all uniform spaces and uniform maps.

[
36

Connected, locally connected metric spaces are closed under finite uniform products.

Proposition 2.22
.

The category
\mathscr{U}
satisfies the following:

(1)

\mathscr{U}

(2)

\mathscr{U}

\mathscr{U}

\mathscr{U}
.

(-)^{X}

\mathscr{U}

A
\mathscr{T}

\mathscr{U}
-object.

Proof.

\mathscr{T}

\mathscr{U}

Lemma 2.25
.

The forgetful functor
\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathscr{U}
preserves coproducts and finite products.

Proof.

Every uniform map from a connected, locally connected metric space to an
\mathscr{M}
-coproduct factors through a summand.
The desired coproduct-preservation follows.

The underlying uniform space of a finite
\mathscr{M}
-product, a special case of a finite pseudometric product, is the finite uniform product of underlying separated uniform spaces.
The coreflector from the category of separated uniform spaces and uniform maps to
\mathscr{U}
, a right adjoint, preserves finite products.
The desired finite product-preservation follows.
∎

3.
Homotopy

Homotopy theories for cubical sets and uniform spaces, both classical and geometric, are formalized in §
3.1
and §
3.2
.
The Lipschitz homotopy theory of cubical sets and the uniform homotopy theory of uniform spaces are compared in §
3.3
.
In each of the homotopy theories, the given category
\mathscr{X}
is equipped with
cylinder objects

\odot
, functors
\odot:\mathscr{X}\times\square_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
for which
-\odot[0]\cong 1_{\mathscr{X}}
; a
\odot
-homotopy
between parallel morphisms
\zeta_{\pm}:x\rightarrow y
can defined as some sort of morphism
\eta:x\odot[1]\rightarrow y
for which
\eta(x\odot\delta_{\pm})=\zeta_{\pm}
for each cylinder object
\odot
.
We will repeatedly use the following two formal facts about such homotopies without additional comment.
The first is that a
\odot
-homotopy between both possible projections of the form
o^{2}\rightarrow o
naturally induces a
\odot
-homotopy between any pair of parallel morphisms to
o
.
The second is that for each lax Cartesian monoidal functor
F
between two such categories equipped with respective cylinder objects
\odot_{1},\odot_{2}
for which there exists a natural transformation
(F-)\odot_{2}-\rightarrow F(-\odot_{1}-)
whose component
Fx\odot_{2}[0]\rightarrow F(x\odot_{1}[0])=1_{x}
for all objects
x
, there exists a
\odot_{2}
-homotopy between projections of the form
(Fy)^{2}\rightarrow Fy
natural in
\odot_{1}
-homotopies between projections of the form
y^{2}\rightarrow y
.

5.
Conclusion

Lipschitz homotopy theory naturally extends to the model categorical setting.
Consider a closed Cartesian monoidal presheaf model category
\mathscr{M}
in which all objects are cofibrant.
Further suppose there exists a distinguished
generating diagram

J
for the acyclic cofibrations, a diagram in the arrow category of
\mathscr{M}
for which the fibrations in
\mathscr{M}
are characterized as having suitably natural right lifting properties against the objects in
J

[
3
]
.
The choice of
J
determines a kind of metric structure on
\mathscr{M}
as follows.
An algebraic small object argument builds a fibrant replacement monad
R
for
\mathscr{M}
from
J
as a transfinite colimit of pointed endofunctors.
A weak equivalence in
\mathscr{M}
is exactly a homotopy equivalence after applying
R
.
We can call a weak equivalence
\zeta
in
\mathscr{M}
a
Lipschitz weak equivalence
if for each
\mathscr{M}
-object
o
,
\zeta^{o}
is a homotopy equivalence after applying one of the finite intermediate stages in the construction of
R
.
In the case
\mathscr{M}
is a category of presheaves over a test category
[
6
,
18
]
, a natural question is whether there exists a choice of generating diagram
J
so that the localization by the resulting class of Lipschitz weak equivalences is equivalent to the Lipschitz homotopy category of cubical sets.
The special case where
\mathscr{M}
is the category of simplicial sets, each of which is plausibly sharp in a Lipschitz-theoretic sense (eg.
[
35
]
), is of particular interest in simplicial
\ell_{p}
cohomology.

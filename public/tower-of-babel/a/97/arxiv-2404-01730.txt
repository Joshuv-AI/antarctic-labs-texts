Asymptotics of Language Model Alignment
Joy Qiping Yang, Salman Salamatian, Ziteng Sun, Ananda Theertha Suresh, Ahmad Beirami

Abstract
Let 𝒑 \bm{p} denote a reference generative language model. Let 𝒓 \bm{r} denote a reward model that returns a scalar to capture the degree at which a draw from 𝒑 \bm{p} is preferred. The goal of language model alignment is to alter 𝒑 \bm{p} to a new distribution ϕ \bm{\phi} that results in a higher expected reward while keeping ϕ \bm{\phi} close to 𝒑 . \bm{p}. A popular alignment method is the KL-constrained reinforcement learning (RL) , which chooses a distribution ϕ Δ \bm{\phi}_{\Delta} that maximizes E ϕ Δ ​ 𝒓 ​ ( 𝒚 ) E_{\bm{\phi}_{\Delta}}\bm{r}(\bm{y}) subject to a relative entropy constraint D KL ( ϕ Δ ∥ 𝒑 ) ≤ Δ . D_{\operatorname{KL}}(\bm{\phi}_{\Delta}\|\bm{p})\leq\Delta. Another simple alignment method is best-of- N N , where N N samples are drawn from 𝒑 \bm{p} and one with highest reward is selected. In this paper, we offer a closed-form characterization of the optimal KL-constrained RL solution. We then demonstrate that any alignment method that achieves a comparable trade-off between KL divergence and expected reward must approximate the optimal KL-constrained RL solution in terms of relative entropy. To further analyze the properties of alignment methods, we introduce two simplifying assumptions: we let the language model be memoryless, and the reward model be linear. Although these assumptions may not reflect complex real-world scenarios, they enable a precise characterization of the asymptotic (in the sequence length) behavior of both the best-of- N N alignment, and the KL-constrained RL method, in terms of information-theoretic quantities. We prove that the reward of the optimal KL-constrained RL solution satisfies a large deviation principle, and we fully characterize its rate function. We also show that the rate of growth of the scaled cumulants of the reward is characterized by a proper Rényi cross entropy. Finally, we show that best-of- N N is asymptotically equivalent to KL-constrained RL solution by proving that their expected rewards are asymptotically equal, and concluding that the two distributions must be close in KL divergence.

\displaystyle-\inf_{t\in B^{\circ}}J(t)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\{Y_{n}\in B\}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}\{Y_{n}\in B\}\leq-\inf_{t\in\bar{B}}J(t),

where

The rate function of the Large Deviation Principle (LDP) thoroughly characterizes the probability of rare events in which
Y_{n}
significantly deviates from its mean. Specifically, an LDP offers a complete description of the cumulant generating function of
Y_{n}
by employing Varadhan’s Lemma
(
Dembo & Zeitouni, 1998
, Theorem 4.3.1)
.

We are now ready to state our next result which is an LDP for the reward function for sequences generated from
\bm{\phi}_{\Delta}
, and under Assumptions

-
2
.

Theorem 1

(LDP for aligned reward)
.

Let
Y^{m}\sim\phi_{\delta}^{m}.

The sequence
\{-\frac{1}{m}\log q^{m}(Y^{m})\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an LDP with rate function

J_{\Delta}(t)=D_{\operatorname{KL}}(T(q,p,\beta(t))\|\phi_{\delta}),

(23)

where

\beta(t)=\arg_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\{H(T(q,p,\beta)\|q)=t\}.

Proof sketch of Theorem
1
.

The proof relies on a connection with the LDP of mismatched information along with a change of measure along the aligned family to
\phi_{\delta}^{m}
.
The LDP for the mismatched information is itself proven using a proof technique developed in
(
Salamatian et al., 2019
, Theorem 2)
. More precisely, one first constructs sets in the space of distribution and argue that the empirical type of a sequence falling within these sets constrains the reward. Finally, a careful application of Sanov’s Theorem
(
Dembo & Zeitouni, 1998
, Theorem 6.2.10)
concludes the argument.
∎

As we mentioned above, one practical usecase for the LDP of the reward, is the derivation of the scaled cumulant of function via Varadhan’s lemma.

Corollary 1

(cumulants of the reward under aligned model)
.

For all
\rho\geq 0,
6
6

6

The definition is continuously extended at
\rho=0
.

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\frac{1}{\rho}\log E_{Y^{m}\sim\phi^{m}_{\delta}}e^{\rho\,r(Y^{m})}=-H_{1+\rho}(\phi_{\delta}\|q).

(24)

Note that the same result could have been directly derived from the characterization of the cumulants in Lemma

as well.
This immediately implies that the average reward of a draw from
\phi_{\delta}
concentrates on

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}E_{Y^{m}\sim\phi^{m}_{\Delta}}r(Y^{m})=-H(\phi_{\delta}\|q).

(25)

This characterizes the expected reward that is achieved by the solution to the KL-constrained RL problem.

We remark that even though best-of-

We believe that Theorem

We also empirically validate that the type of best-of-

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we considered the language model alignment problem, and studied two popular alignment techniques:
KL-constrained reinforcement learning
and
best-of-
N.
We established several properties of the solutions of both techniques and established an asymptotic equivalence between the two problems, providing theoretical justification for the remarkable performance of best-of-
N
in practice. While our derivations were obtained under strong assumptions about the string-generating sources, we believe that these results may be extended beyond these classes of sources. In particular, we performed some extra experiments to compare the KL divergence of best-of-
N
solution,
\bm{\pi}_{N}
to that of the optimal KL-regularized RL where the KL divergence budget for both models is
\Delta
, i.e.,
\bm{\phi}_{\Delta}
. We found that for random
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
over alphabet of size
K>1000,
the KL divergence
D_{\operatorname{KL}}(\bm{\pi}_{N}\|\bm{\phi}_{\Delta})
was always bounded by
0.01
for all
N\in[1,1000]
which is striking. Even for
K<10,
we empirically observed that
D_{\operatorname{KL}}(\bm{\pi}_{N}\|\bm{\phi}_{\Delta})<0.5.
We hope future research can extend the theoretical investigation to better understand this phenomenon.

Proofs of Large Deviation Principle (LDP)

Now to prove LDP, we first show some results on the KL-constrained RL.

Theorem 3

(LDP for mismatched information)
.

Let
Y^{m}\sim p^{m}.

The sequence
\{-\frac{1}{m}\log q^{m}(Y^{m})\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an LDP with rate function

J_{0}(t)=D_{\operatorname{KL}}(T(q,p,\beta(t))\|p),

(35)

where

\beta(t)=\arg_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\{H(T(q,p,\beta)\|q)=t\}.

(36)

The proof of Theorem
36
relies on a correspondence between mismatched information, and some sets of distributions, which we will define shortly. This correspondence is implicitly used in
(
Beirami et al., 2018
, proof of Theorem 5)
and made explicit in
(
Salamatian et al., 2019
, Eq. (25)-(27))
. For
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\varphi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}:H(\varphi\|q)-H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)\leq\epsilon\right\}

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(q,\alpha,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\varphi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}:H(\varphi\|q)-H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)\geq-\epsilon\right\}

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(q,\alpha,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\varphi\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}:H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)-H(\varphi\|q)\in[0,\epsilon]\right\}.

(39)

The sets above are extensions of tilted weakly typical sets of order
\alpha

(
Beirami et al., 2018
, Definition 18)
, and capture the set of types which are respectively, more likely, less likely, and as likely according to
q
than
T(q,\alpha)
, where we define

T(q,\alpha):=T(q,u,\alpha),

(40)

where
u=(1/|\mathcal{Y}|,\ldots,1/|\mathcal{Y}|)
is the uniform distribution.
For these sets, we then have the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

For any
\alpha>0
, the following inclusion relations hold, for sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)\right|\leq\epsilon\Rightarrow t(y^{m})\in\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha,2\epsilon),

(41)

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)\right|\leq\epsilon\Rightarrow t(y^{m})\in\mathcal{E}(q,\alpha,2\epsilon),

(42)

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha)\|q)\right|\leq\epsilon\Leftarrow t(y^{m})\in\mathcal{B}(q,\alpha,\epsilon).

(43)

This was proved implicitly in the proofs of Theorems 3 and 5 in
(
Beirami et al., 2018
)
.
We are now equipped to provide the proof of the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
36
.

Note that as
-\frac{1}{m}\log q(Y^{m})

takes values in a compact subset
[0,\log\frac{1}{\zeta}]
of
\mathbb{R}
(see Assumptions
18
-
2

(
Beirami et al., 2018

for a formal discussion), i.e.,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}_{p}^{m}\left(\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(Y^{m})-t\right|<\epsilon\right)=-J(t).

(44)

We proceed with the proof in three separate cases.

Case (a)

t\in(H(q),\log\frac{1}{\zeta})
, which implies
\alpha(t)\in(0,1)
by monotonicity of

for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
(
41
) and (

) respectively imply

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}_{p^{m}}\left(\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(Y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha(t))\|q)\right|\leq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\leq\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}_{p^{m}}(t(Y^{m})\in\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon)),

(45)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}_{p^{m}}\left(\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(Y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha(t))\|q)\right|\leq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\geq\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}_{p^{m}}(t(Y^{m})\in\mathcal{B}(q,\alpha(t),\epsilon)).

Thus, it suffices to show that the RHS of (

) and (
46
) both evaluate to
-D(T(q,p,\alpha(t))\|p)
. This is done via Sanov’s Theorem. Recall that Sanov’s Theorem
(
Dembo & Zeitouni, 1998

states that, for a set of distributions
\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle-\inf_{\gamma\in\mathrm{int}\mathcal{C}}D(\gamma\|p)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}(t(y^{m})\in\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log\mathbb{P}(t(y^{m})\in\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{\gamma\in\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{C}}D(\gamma\|p).

(47)

To obtain the upper bound, we apply this result to the set
\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon/\alpha(t))
. Observing that this holds for any
\epsilon
, and then letting
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, we get that the RHS of (

\displaystyle-\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\inf_{\gamma\in\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon/\alpha(t))}D(\gamma\|p).

\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon)

is a collection of points such that
\gamma(t,\epsilon)\in\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon)
. It follows from compactness that the limit point
\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\gamma(\epsilon,t)\in\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),2\epsilon)=\mathrm{cl}\mathcal{D}(q,\alpha(t),0)
, where we have used that

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}}\hskip 30.00005ptD(\gamma\|p)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }\hskip 15.00002ptH(\gamma\|q)\leq H(T(q,\alpha(t))\|q)

Note that this optimization problem is convex, and thus can be solved analytically by writing the KKT conditions

Analogously, the RHS of (

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}^{m}\left(\left|-\frac{1}{m}\log q(Y^{m})-H(T(q,\alpha(t))\|q)\right|<\epsilon\right)

(50)

Case (b):
We now let

Finally, let

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\log E_{Y^{m}\sim\phi^{m}_{\Delta}}e^{\gamma r(Y^{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t}\;\{\gamma H(T(q,p,\beta(t))\|q)-D(T(q,p,\beta(t))\|\phi_{\Delta})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\beta}\;\{\gamma H(T(q,p,\beta\|q)-D(T(q,p,\beta\|\phi_{\Delta})\},

(54)

where we used the definition of

in Theorem

in (

\displaystyle\;-\sum_{y}T(q,p,\beta)(y)\log\frac{T(q,p,\beta)(y)}{\phi_{\Delta}(y)\cdot q(y)^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{y}T(q,p,\beta)(y)\log\frac{T(q,p,\beta)(y)}{T(q,\phi_{\Delta},\gamma)(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-D_{\operatorname{KL}}(T(q,p,\beta)\|T(q,\phi_{\Delta},\gamma))-\log\sum_{y}\phi_{\Delta}(y)q(y)^{\gamma}.

. ∎

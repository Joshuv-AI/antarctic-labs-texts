Connes distance of 2 ​ D 2D harmonic oscillators in quantum phase space
Bing-Sheng Lin, Tai-Hua Heng

Abstract
We study the Connes distance of quantum states of 2 ​ D 2D harmonic oscillators in phase space. Using the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, we construct a boson Fock space and a quantum Hilbert space, and obtain the Dirac operator and a spectral triple corresponding to a 4 ​ D 4D quantum phase space. Based on the ball condition, we obtain some constraint relations about the optimal elements. We construct the explicit expressions of the corresponding optimal elements and then derive the Connes distance between two arbitrary Fock states of 2 ​ D 2D quantum harmonic oscillators. We prove that these two-dimensional distances satisfy the Pythagoras theorem.

Introduction

In quantum physics, the physical quantities are represented by some operators in a Hilbert space. Usually, these operators are noncommutative with each other.
So it is natural to study physical systems by noncommutative geometry.
In the 1980’s, Connes formulated the mathematically rigorous framework of noncommutative geometry
[
1
]
.
Many kinds of noncommutative spaces are studied by physicists and mathematicians, such as the Moyal plane and fuzzy space
[
2
]
-
[
7
]
.
The phase space in quantum mechanics is also a Moyal-type noncommutative space, because the position and momentum operators satisfy the noncommutative Heisenberg algebras.

Due to the noncommutativity, there is no traditional point in a noncommutative space. This is different from normal commutative spaces. We only have states in noncommutative spaces. So there is no normal distance between two points in a noncommutative space, but we can calculate some kinds of distance measures between the states, such as the Connes distance
[
8
]
.
Many researchers have already studied the Connes distance in some kinds of noncommutative spaces.
Bimonte
et. al.
calculated the distances between the points of a lattice on which the usual discretized Dirac operator has been defined
[
9
]
.
Cagnache
et. al.
have studied the Connes spectral distance in the Moyal plane
[
10
]
. They explicitly computed Connes spectral distance between the pure states which corresponding to eigenfunctions of the quantum harmonic oscillators.
Martinetti
et. al.
have studied the metric aspect of the Moyal plane from Connes’ noncommutative geometry point of view
[
11
]
. They obtained the spectral distance between coherent states of the quantum harmonic oscillator as the Euclidean distance on the plane. They also computed the spectral distance in the so-called double Moyal plane.
D’Andrea
et. al.
have studied the Pythagoras’ theorem in noncommutative geometry
[
12
]
. They showed that for non-pure states it is replaced by some Pythagoras inequalities.
Franco and Wallet also studied metrics and causality on Moyal planes
[
13
]
.
Scholtz and his collaborators have done many works on the studies of Connes spectral distances in Moyal plane and fuzzy sphere
[
14
]
-
[
16
]
. They developed the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, and obtained the distances of harmonic oscillator states and also coherent states.
Kumar
et. al.
used Dirac eigen-spinor method to compute spectral distances in doubled Moyal plane
[
17
]
.
Chakraborty
et. al.
also studied the spectral distance on Lorentzian Moyal plane
[
18
]
.

In the present work, we consider the Connes distance of the quantum states (namely the Fock states) of
2D
harmonic oscillators. Actually, some researchers have already studied the Connes distance between the states of
1D
harmonic oscillators
[
10
,
14
,
16

. But we find that the calculations and results of the Connes distance of
2D
harmonic oscillators are much more complicated than those of
1D
oscillator system. So it is significant to study the explicit formulae of the Connes distance of
2D
harmonic oscillator systems. The method used in the present work is also different from those used in the literatures.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we consider the
4D
quantum phase space and construct a corresponding spectral triple. Using the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, we construct a boson Fock space and a quantum Hilbert space, and obtain the Dirac operator.
In Sec.

Based on these inequalities, we derive some constraint relations about the optimal elements in Sec.

The Connes distance between two adjacent Fock states of

In Sec.

Some discussions and conclusions are given in Sec.

2
4D quantum phase space and spectral triple

Let us consider the
4D
quantum phase space, and the coordinate operators
\hat{x}_{i}
,
\hat{p}_{i}
satisfy the following commutation relation,

[\hat{x}_{i},\,\hat{p}_{j}]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ij}\hbar\qquad(i,j=1,2),

(1)

and others vanish.
We can define the following creation and annihilation operators,

\hat{a}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}(\hat{x}_{i}+\mathrm{i}\hat{p}_{i}),\qquad\hat{a}_{i}^{{\dagger}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}(\hat{x}_{i}-\mathrm{i}\hat{p}_{i}),

(2)

which satisfy the commutation relations
[\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}_{j}^{{\dagger}}]=\delta_{ij}
, and
[\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}_{j}]=[\hat{a}_{i}^{{\dagger}},\hat{a}_{j}^{{\dagger}}]=0
.

In the present work, we will use the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation developed in Ref.
[
19
]
. We can construct the following boson Fock space

\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{span}\left\{|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{1}!n_{2}!}}(\hat{a}_{1}^{{\dagger}})^{n_{1}}(\hat{a}_{2}^{{\dagger}})^{n_{2}}|00\rangle,\quad n_{1},n_{2}=0,1,2,...\right\},

(3)

where
|00\rangle
is the vacuum state,
\hat{a}_{i}|00\rangle=0
. The Fock states
|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
are eigenstates of the number operators
\hat{N}_{i}=\hat{a}_{i}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}_{i}
,
\hat{N}_{i}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=n_{i}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
, and

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{1}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=\sqrt{n_{1}}|n_{1}-1,n_{2}\rangle,\qquad\hat{a}_{1}^{{\dagger}}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=\sqrt{n_{1}+1}|n_{1}+1,n_{2}\rangle,

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{2}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=\sqrt{n_{2}}|n_{1},n_{2}-1\rangle,\qquad\hat{a}_{2}^{{\dagger}}|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=\sqrt{n_{2}+1}|n_{1},n_{2}+1\rangle.

(4)

These Fock states can be regarded as the quantum states of a two-dimensional harmonic oscillator system.

Furthermore, one can construct the following quantum Hilbert space

\mathcal{Q}=\mathrm{span}\left\{|m_{1},m_{2}\rangle\langle n_{1},n_{2}|\right\}.

(5)

In the followings, we will denote the elements
\psi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})
of the quantum Hilbert space by
|\psi)
(The elements
\phi
of the boson Fock space
\mathcal{F}
are denoted by
|\phi\rangle

\psi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})\equiv|\psi)=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\psi^{i,j}_{k,l}|i,j\rangle\langle k,l|.

(6)

The corresponding inner product is

\displaystyle(\phi|\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\phi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i}),\psi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})\right)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\phi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})^{{\dagger}}\psi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\langle m,n|\phi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})^{{\dagger}}\psi(\hat{x}_{i},\hat{p}_{i})|m,n\rangle,

where
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\cdot)
denotes the trace over
\mathcal{F}
.

The quantum phase space (

In general, a noncommutative space corresponds to a spectral triple

(9)

Similar to Ref.
[

After some straightforward calculations, one can obtain the commutator

3
Connes spectral distance and ball condition

The Connes spectral distance between two states
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
is defined as
[
8
]

d(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\equiv\sup_{e\in B}|\omega(e)-\omega^{\prime}(e)|,

(57)

where

B=\left\{e\in\mathcal{A}:\big\|[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]\big\|_{op}\leqslant 1\right\},

(58)

and

\|A\|_{op}\equiv\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{\|A\psi\|}{\|\psi\|},\qquad\|A\|^{2}\equiv(A,A)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(A^{{\dagger}}A).

(59)

The inequality in (
58
) is the so-called ball condition.

Similar to Ref.
[
14
]
, here we only consider the case where the quantum states
\omega
are normal and bounded so that they are representable by density matrices
\rho
.
The action of the state
\omega
on an element
e\in\mathcal{A}
can be written as

\omega(e)=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho e).

(60)

Suppose the quantum states
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
correspond to the density matrices
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
, respectively. We have

d(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho^{\prime}e)|=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta\rho\,e)|=\sup_{e\in B}|(\Delta\rho,e)|,

(61)

where
\Delta\rho=\rho-\rho^{\prime}
, and
(\Delta\rho)^{{\dagger}}=\Delta\rho
.

Now let us calculate the commutator
[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]
for a Hermitian element
e\in\mathcal{A}
.
Using the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}
(
2
), we have

\displaystyle[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\hbar}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&[-\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger},e]&[-\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger},e]\\
0&0&[\hat{a}_{1},e]&[-\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
{}[-\hat{a}_{2},e]&[\hat{a}_{1}^{\dagger},e]&0&0\\
{}[-\hat{a}_{1},e]&[-\hat{a}_{2}^{\dagger},e]&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\hbar}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}&[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}\\
0&0&[\hat{a}_{1},e]&-[\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
{}-[\hat{a}_{2},e]&-[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}&0&0\\
{}-[\hat{a}_{1},e]&[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}&0&0\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\hbar}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&D_{1}\\
-D_{1}^{\dagger}&0\\
\end{array}\right),

where

D_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}&[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&-[\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
\end{array}\right),\quad D_{1}^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e]&[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&-[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}\\
\end{array}\right).

(75)

It is easy to verify that

\left\|[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]^{\dagger}[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]\right\|_{op}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\max\left\{\|D_{1}^{\dagger}D_{1}\|_{op},\|D_{1}D_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{op}\right\}.

(76)

It is known that for any bounded operator
A
, there is
\|A\|_{op}^{2}=\|A^{\dagger}\|_{op}^{2}=\|A^{\dagger}A\|_{op}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\big\|[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]\big\|_{op}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]^{\dagger}[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]\right\|_{op}

(77)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\hbar}\|D_{1}^{\dagger}D_{1}\|_{op}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\|D_{1}D_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{op}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\hbar}\|D_{1}\|_{op}^{2}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\|D_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{op}^{2}.

So for any Hermitian element
e\in B
, using the ball condition in (
58
), one can obtain the following inequality,

\|D_{1}^{\dagger}D_{1}\|_{op}=\|D_{1}D_{1}^{\dagger}\|_{op}=\frac{\hbar}{2}\big\|[\mathcal{D},\pi(e)]\big\|_{op}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(78)

From (
75
), one can obtain

\displaystyle D_{1}^{\dagger}D_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e]&[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&-[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}&[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&-[\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}-[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}-[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]\\
\end{array}\right).

Consider an operator
A
with the matrix elements
A_{ij}
in some orthonormal bases, there is the following Bessel’s inequality
[
16
]

,

\|A\|_{op}^{2}\geqslant\sum_{i}|A_{ij}|^{2}\geqslant|A_{ij}|^{2}.

(87)

So we have

\|D_{1}^{\dagger}D_{1}\|_{op}\geqslant\!\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F},\langle\phi|\phi\rangle=1}\langle\phi|[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]+[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}|\phi\rangle.

(88)

From (
78
) and (
88
), for any Hermitian element
e\in B
, there is the following inequality

\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F},\langle\phi|\phi\rangle=1}\langle\phi|[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]+[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}|\phi\rangle\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(89)

Similarly, we have

\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F},\langle\phi|\phi\rangle=1}\langle\phi|[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]|\phi\rangle\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(90)

From (
75
), we also have

D_{1}D_{1}^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]+[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]&[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}-[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}\\
{}[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]-[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]&[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}\\
\end{array}\right).~~

(91)

Similarly, there are also the following inequalities

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F},\langle\phi|\phi\rangle=1}\langle\phi|[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]+[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]|\phi\rangle\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F},\langle\phi|\phi\rangle=1}\langle\phi|[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}|\phi\rangle\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(92)

4
Optimal element and constraint relations

Consider the
2D
harmonic oscillator states
|p,q\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
, and the corresponding density matrices are
\rho_{p,q}=|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|
and
\rho_{m,n}=|m,n\rangle\langle m,n|
. The Connes distance between these two states is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{p,q},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{p,q}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|

(93)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta\rho\,e)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\langle p,q|e|p,q\rangle-\langle m,n|e|m,n\rangle|,

where
\Delta\rho=\rho_{p,q}-\rho_{m,n}=|p,q\rangle\langle p,q|-|m,n\rangle\langle m,n|
.
The element
e\in B
can be expressed as

e=\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}C^{i,j}_{k,l}|i,j\rangle\langle k,l|.

(94)

Consider the element
e
attaining the supremum in (
93
), which is the so-called optimal element.
Since only Hermitian elements can give the supremum in the Connes
distance function
[
21
]
, there should be
C^{i,j}_{k,l}=(C^{k,l}_{i,j})^{*}
, and
C^{i,j}_{i,j}
are real numbers.
After some straightforward calculations, one can obtain

d(\omega_{p,q},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|C^{p,q}_{p,q}-C^{m,n}_{m,n}|.

(95)

For the element
e
(
109
), using the relations (
2
), we have

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{1},e]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{1}e-e\hat{a}_{1}

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}C^{i,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i}|i-1,j\rangle\langle k,l|-\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}C^{i,j}_{k,l}|i,j\rangle\langle k+1,l|\sqrt{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right)|i,j\rangle\langle k,l|,

and

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right)|i,j\rangle\langle k,l|\right)

(97)

\displaystyle~~\times\left(\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right)^{*}|k,l\rangle\langle i,j|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left|C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right|^{2}\right)|i,j\rangle\langle i,j|+n.d.\,,~~~

” denotes the sum of terms
|k,l\rangle\langle i,j|
with

and/or

, and these terms will not affect the calculations in the following content.

We also have

[\hat{a}_{2},e]=\sum_{i,j,k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(C^{i,j+1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j+1}-C^{i,j}_{k,l-1}\sqrt{l}\right)|i,j\rangle\langle k,l|,

(98)

and

[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left|C^{i,j}_{k,l+1}\sqrt{l+1}-C^{i,j-1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j}\right|^{2}\right)|i,j\rangle\langle i,j|+n.d.\,.

(99)

So there is

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{1},e][\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}+[\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{2},e]=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\left|C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right|^{2}

(100)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|C^{i,j}_{k,l+1}\sqrt{l+1}-C^{i,j-1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j}\right|^{2}\bigg)|i,j\rangle\langle i,j|+n.d.\,.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle[\hat{a}_{1},e]^{\dagger}[\hat{a}_{1},e]+[\hat{a}_{2},e][\hat{a}_{2},e]^{\dagger}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\left|C^{i,j}_{k+1,l}\sqrt{k+1}-C^{i-1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i}\right|^{2}

(101)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left|C^{i,j+1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j+1}-C^{i,j}_{k,l-1}\sqrt{l}\right|^{2}\bigg)|i,j\rangle\langle i,j|+n.d.\,.

From (
89
), (
90
) and (
100
), (
101
), one can obtain the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\left|C^{i,j}_{k+1,l}\sqrt{k+1}-C^{i-1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i}\right|^{2}+\left|C^{i,j+1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j+1}-C^{i,j}_{k,l-1}\sqrt{l}\right|^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\left(\left|C^{i+1,j}_{k,l}\sqrt{i+1}-C^{i,j}_{k-1,l}\sqrt{k}\right|^{2}+\left|C^{i,j}_{k,l+1}\sqrt{l+1}-C^{i,j-1}_{k,l}\sqrt{j}\right|^{2}\right)\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.~~~~~

(102)

For simplicity, we can set
C^{i,j}_{k,l}=0
if
k\neq i
and/or
l\neq j
, and denote
C^{i,j}_{i,j}=c_{i,j}
.
For
i,j=0,1,2,...
, one can obtain the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\left|c_{i,j}\sqrt{i}-c_{i-1,j}\sqrt{i}\right|^{2}+\left|c_{i,j+1}\sqrt{j+1}-c_{i,j}\sqrt{j+1}\right|^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle\left|c_{i+1,j}\sqrt{i+1}-c_{i,j}\sqrt{i+1}\right|^{2}+\left|c_{i,j}\sqrt{j}-c_{i,j-1}\sqrt{j}\right|^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(103)

Since
c_{i,j}=C^{i,j}_{i,j}
are real, so we have

\displaystyle i\left(c_{i,j}-c_{i-1,j}\right)^{2}+(j+1)\left(c_{i,j+1}-c_{i,j}\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle(i+1)\left(c_{i+1,j}-c_{i,j}\right)^{2}+j\left(c_{i,j}-c_{i,j-1}\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(104)

Denote

c_{i,j}-c_{i-1,j}=E_{i,j},\qquad c_{i,j}-c_{i,j-1}=F_{i,j},

(105)

then the inequalities (

(106)

Similarly, from (

), one can obtain the following inequalities,

\displaystyle i\left(c_{i,j}-c_{i-1,j}\right)^{2}+j\left(c_{i,j}-c_{i,j-1}\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle(i+1)\left(c_{i+1,j}-c_{i,j}\right)^{2}+(j+1)\left(c_{i,j+1}-c_{i,j}\right)^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

(107)

or

iE_{i,j}^{2}+jF_{i,j}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},\quad(i+1)E_{i+1,j}^{2}+(j+1)F_{i,j+1}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(108)

So in order to obtain the supremum in (
93
), in general, one only need to consider the so-called optimal elements
e_{o}\in B
which can be expressed as

e_{o}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}c_{i,j}|i,j\rangle\langle i,j|,

(109)

where the coefficients
c_{i,j}
satisfy the constraint relations (
4
), (
4
) or (
106
), (
108
), and the Connes distance (
93
) is obtained by

\displaystyle d(\omega_{p,q},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{p,q}e_{o})-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e_{o})|

(110)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle p,q|e_{o}|p,q\rangle-\langle m,n|e_{o}|m,n\rangle|.

5
Connes distance between harmonic oscillator states

Now let us explicitly calculate the Connes distances between the quantum states of
2D
harmonic oscillators.
First, let us consider the adjacent states
|m+1,n\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
, and the corresponding Connes distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+1,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+1,n}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|=\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+1,n}|.

From (
106
) and (
108
), we have

\displaystyle(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m+1,n+1}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},\quad(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+nF_{m,n}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},\quad(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+nF_{m+1,n}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.\quad

(112)

So in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|=|E_{m+1,n}|
, there should be

(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2},

(113)

or

|c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|=|E_{m+1,n}|=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(m+1)}},

(114)

and then there should be

F_{m,n+1}=F_{m+1,n+1}=0.

(115)

Without loss of generality, we can assume
E_{m+1,n}\geqslant 0
, and

\displaystyle E_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{m+1,n+1}+E_{m+1,n}=c_{m+1,n+1}-c_{m,n}

(116)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}=E_{m+1,n+1}.

So it is easy to see that, if
E_{m+1,n}
satisfy (
113
), there must be

E_{m+1,i}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(m+1)}},\qquad F_{m,i+1}=F_{m+1,i+1}=0,

(117)

where
i=0,1,2,...
. This means that

c_{m,0}=c_{m,1}=...=c_{m,i}=...\,,\qquad c_{m+1,0}=c_{m+1,1}=...=c_{m+1,i}=...\,.

(118)

Furthermore, let us consider the states
|m+k,n\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
,
k>1
, and the corresponding Connes distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|=\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|

(119)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m+k-1,n}+c_{m+k-1,n}-c_{m+k-2,n}

\displaystyle\qquad+...+c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\big(|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m+k-1,n}|+|c_{m+k-1,n}-c_{m+k-2,n}|

\displaystyle\qquad+...+|c_{m+1,n}-c_{m,n}|\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+k}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+k-1}}+...+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}=\zeta_{m;m+k}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}},

where

\zeta_{p;q}=\sum_{i=p+1}^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}=\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},p+1\right)-\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},q+1\right),

(120)

and
\zeta(s,q)
is the Hurwitz zeta function

\zeta(s,q)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(q+i)^{s}}.

(121)

Obviously, there is
\zeta_{i;j}=-\zeta_{j;i}
and
\zeta_{i;i}=0
.

In order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|
, for example, one can choose

\displaystyle c_{0,i}=0,

\displaystyle c_{1,i}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}},

\displaystyle c_{2,i}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle c_{p,i}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}=\zeta_{0;p}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}},

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

(122)

where
p,i=0,1,2...
,
and there is

c_{m+i,n}-c_{m+i-1,n}=E_{m+i,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(m+i)}}\qquad(i=1,2,...,k).

(123)

Suppose
e_{o}
to be the optimal element which attains the supremum in (
119
),
it can be expressed as

e_{o}=\sum_{p,i=0}^{\infty}c_{p,i}|p,i\rangle\langle p,i|=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sum_{p,i=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{0;p}|p,i\rangle\langle p,i|,

(124)

and the Connes distance between the states
|m+k,n\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n}e_{o})-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e_{o})|=|c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}=\zeta_{m;m+k}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left(\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},m+1\right)-\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},m+k+1\right)\right).

Of course, there is
d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m,n},\omega_{m+k,n})
. These distances satisfy

d(\omega_{m+k+l,n},\omega_{m,n})=d(\omega_{m+k,n},\omega_{m,n})+d(\omega_{m+k+l,n},\omega_{m+k,n}).

(126)

These results coincide with the results of the Connes distance between the quantum states of
1D
harmonic oscillators in Refs.
[
10
,
14
,
16
]
.
This means that the Connes spectral distance between the states
|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n\rangle
of
2D
harmonic oscillators is the same as that between the states
|m\rangle
and
|m+k\rangle
of
1D
harmonic oscillators.

Similarly, there is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m,n+k},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+i}}=\zeta_{n;n+k}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}

(127)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\left(\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},n+1\right)-\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2},n+k+1\right)\right).

6
Distance between arbitrary Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|k,l\rangle

Now, let us consider the Connes distance between two arbitrary Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|k,l\rangle
.
First, let us consider the states
|m+1,n+1\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
, and the corresponding Connes distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+1,n+1},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+1,n+1}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|

(128)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+1,n+1}-c_{m,n}|=\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}E_{m+1,n+1}\sqrt{m+1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}F_{m,n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{m+1}+\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}.

In the first inequality, we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})^{2}\leqslant(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}),

(129)

where equality holds if
x_{1}y_{2}=x_{2}y_{1}
.

From (
106
), in order to attain the supremum of
|E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}|
, there should be

(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2},

(130)

and

(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}=(n+1)F_{m,n+1}.

(131)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
E_{m+1,n+1},F_{m,n+1}\geqslant 0
. From (
108
), we have

\displaystyle(m+1)E_{m+1,n+1}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m+1,n+1}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}\leqslant\frac{\hbar}{2}.

(132)

Compare the above inequalities with (
130
), one can obtain

E_{m+1,n}\leqslant E_{m+1,n+1},\qquad F_{m+1,n+1}\leqslant F_{m,n+1}.

(133)

But

E_{m+1,n}+F_{m+1,n+1}=E_{m+1,n+1}+F_{m,n+1}=c_{m+1,n+1}-c_{m,n},

(134)

so there must be

E_{m+1,n}=E_{m+1,n+1},\qquad F_{m+1,n+1}=F_{m,n+1}.

(135)

Furthermore, let us consider the Fock states
|m+2,n+1\rangle
and
|m,n\rangle
, and the corresponding Connes distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+2,n+1},\omega_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+2,n+1}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|=\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+2,n+1}-c_{m,n}|

(136)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+2,n+1}+F_{m+1,n+1}+E_{m+1,n}|.

In order to attain the supremum of
|E_{m+2,n+1}+F_{m+1,n+1}+E_{m+1,n}|
, there must be

\displaystyle(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m+1,n+1}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2},

\displaystyle(m+2)E_{m+2,n+1}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m+1,n+1}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2},

(137)

and then

(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}=(m+2)E_{m+2,n+1}^{2}.

(138)

Without loss of generality, we can assume
E_{m+2,n+1},E_{m+1,n},F_{m+1,n+1}\geqslant 0
.
It is easy to see that, there must be

E_{m+1,n+1}=E_{m+1,n}=\sqrt{\frac{m+2}{m+1}}E_{m+2,n+1}=\sqrt{\frac{m+2}{m+1}}E_{m+2,n},

(139)

F_{m+2,n+1}=F_{m+1,n+1}=F_{m,n+1}.

(140)

So we have

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+2,n+1},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|E_{m+2,n+1}+F_{m+1,n+1}+E_{m+1,n}|

(141)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+2}}\right)E_{m+1,n}\sqrt{m+1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}F_{m+1,n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m+1,n+1}^{2}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Here in the first inequality, we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (
129
).

Finally, let us consider the Connes distance between two arbitrary Fock states
|m,n\rangle
and
|m+k,n+l\rangle
. For simplicity, we assume
k,l\geqslant 0
. The corresponding Connes distance is

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m+k,n+l}e)-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(\rho_{m,n}e)|

(142)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|=\sup_{e\in B}|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m+k,n}+c_{m+k,n}-c_{m,n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{l}(c_{m+k,n+i}-c_{m+k,n+i-1})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(c_{m+j,n}-c_{m+j-1,n})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{l}F_{m+k,n+i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}E_{m+j,n}\right|.

Similarly, in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|
,
there must be

(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2},

(143)

and

E_{m+i,n+j}=\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{m+i}}E_{m+1,n}\qquad(1\leqslant i\leqslant k,0\leqslant j\leqslant l),

(144)

F_{m+i,n+j}=\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{n+j}}F_{m,n+1}\qquad(0\leqslant i\leqslant k,1\leqslant j\leqslant l).

(145)

So we have

\displaystyle d(\omega_{m+k,n+l},\omega_{m,n})=\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{m+i,n}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}F_{m+k,n+j}\right|

(146)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+i}}+\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+j}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\left|\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\zeta_{m;m+k}+\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\zeta_{n;n+l}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in B}\sqrt{(m+1)E_{m+1,n}^{2}+(n+1)F_{m,n+1}^{2}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}.

Here we have used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (
129
) in the above inequality, and the equality holds if

\sqrt{m+1}E_{m+1,n}\zeta_{n;n+l}=\sqrt{n+1}F_{m,n+1}\zeta_{m;m+k}.

(147)

From (
143
) and (
147
), one can derive

E_{m+1,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(m+1)}}\frac{\zeta_{m;m+k}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}=c_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m+1}},

(148)

F_{m,n+1}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2(n+1)}}\frac{\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}=d_{0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}},

(149)

where

c_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\frac{\zeta_{m;m+k}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}},\qquad d_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\frac{\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}}.

(150)

So in order to attain the supremum of
|c_{m+k,n+l}-c_{m,n}|
, for example, one can choose

\displaystyle c_{0,0}=0,\quad c_{1,0}=c_{0},\quad c_{0,1}=d_{0},\quad c_{1,1}=c_{0}+d_{0},\quad...

\displaystyle c_{p,q}=c_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}+d_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j}}=c_{0}\zeta_{0;p}+d_{0}\zeta_{0;q}

\displaystyle~~~~=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2}}\frac{\zeta_{0;p}\zeta_{m;m+k}+\zeta_{0;q}\zeta_{n;n+l}}{\sqrt{\zeta_{m;m+k}^{2}+\zeta_{n;n+l}^{2}}},\quad...

(151)

where
p,q=0,1,2,...
.
Obviously, there is

E_{i+1,j}=c_{i+1,j}-c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{i+1}}c_{0},\qquad F_{i,j+1}=c_{i,j+1}-c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{j+1}}d_{0}.

(152)

The corresponding optimal element
e_{o}
is

7
Discussions and conclusions

In this paper, we study the Connes distance of quantum states of
2D
harmonic oscillators in phase space. By virtue of the Hilbert-Schmidt operatorial formulation, we construct a boson Fock space and a quantum Hilbert space, and obtain the Dirac operator and a spectral triple corresponding to a
4D
quantum phase space.
From the ball condition, we obtain some constraint relations about the optimal elements.
Using these constraint relations, we derive the explicit expressions of the Connes distance between the states of
2D
quantum harmonic oscillators.

We find that the Connes distance between two arbitrary Fock states is not just a simple sum of distances of adjacent Fock states.
Furthermore, these distances also satisfy the Pythagoras theorem.
The calculations and results of the Connes distance of quantum states of
2D
harmonic oscillators are much more complicated than those of the states of
1D
harmonic oscillators.
These results are not just trivial generalizations of the result of
1D
case.
Our method used in the present work is also different from those used in the literatures.
So these results are significant for the study of the Connes distances of physical systems in noncommutative spaces.

Here we only analyse the Connes distance between two pure states.
One can also study the distance between two mixed states.
Furthermore, one can use our method to study the Connes distance between the quantum states of higher-dimensional harmonic oscillators.
But usually the calculations and results will be much more complicated.

We are still exploring the intuitive physical meaning of these distances of physical systems.
Our methods can be used to study other physical systems in other kinds of noncommutative spaces. We hope that our results can help to study the mathematical structures and physical properties of noncommutative spaces.

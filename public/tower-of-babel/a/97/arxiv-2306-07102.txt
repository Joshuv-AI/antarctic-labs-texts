Einstein-type metrics and generalized Ricci solitons on weak f f -K-contact manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
A weak metric f f -structure ( f , Q , ξ i , η i , g ) ​ ( i = 1 , … , s ) (f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)\ (i=1,\ldots,s) , generalizes the metric f f -structure on a smooth manifold, i.e., the complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced with a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor. We study geometry of a weak f f -K-contact structure, which is a weak f f -contact structure, whose characteristic vector fields are Killing. We show that ker ⁡ f \ker f of a weak f f -contact manifold defines a 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -foliation with an abelian Lie algebra. Then we characterize weak f f -K-contact manifolds among all weak metric f f -manifolds by the property known for f f -K-contact manifolds, and find when a Riemannian manifold endowed with a set of orthonormal Killing vector fields is a weak f f -K-contact manifold. We show that for s > 1 s>1 , an Einstein weak f f -K-contact manifold is Ricci flat, then find sufficient conditions for a weak f f -K-contact manifold with parallel Ricci tensor or with a generalized gradient Ricci soliton structure to be Ricci flat or a quasi Einstein manifold. We prove positive definiteness of the Jacobi operators in the characteristic directions and use this to deform a weak f f -K-contact structure to an f f -K-contact structure. We define an η \eta -Ricci soliton and η \eta -Einstein structures on a weak metric f f -manifold (which for s = 1 s=1 , give the well-known structures on contact metric manifolds) and find sufficient conditions for a compact weak f f -K-contact manifold with an η \eta -Ricci soliton structure of constant scalar curvature to be η \eta -Einstein. Introduction

Contact geometry is of growing interest due to its important role in mechanics in explaining physical phenomena.
In addition, many recent articles have been motivated by the question: how interesting Ricci solitons
– self-similar solutions of the Ricci flow equation,
\partial g(t)/\partial t=-2\,\operatorname{Ric}_{g(t)}
–
can be for geometry of contact metric manifolds.
Some of them consider conditions when a contact metric manifold
M(g,f,\xi,\eta)
,
equipped with a Ricci-type soliton structure carries a canonical
(e.g., constant curvature or Einstein-type) metric, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
20
,
21
]
.
The Ricci soliton equation

(1/2)\,\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g,

where
V
is a vector field and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
generalizes Einstein metrics,
\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g
. In the case that
V
is the gradient of a potential function
\sigma
one has a gradient Ricci soliton equation
{\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g
. Almost Ricci solitons (when
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
, see
[
22
]
) are a generalization of quasi-Einstein manifolds (see
[
9
]
) and Ricci solitons.
The following generalized Ricci soliton equation was studied in
[
21
]
:

(1/2)\,\pounds_{V}\,g-c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g-c_{1}V^{\flat}\otimes V^{\flat},

(1)

where
c_{1},c_{2},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
V^{\flat}
is the 1-form dual to the vector field
V
.
If
V=\nabla\sigma
in (
1
), then
using the definition
{\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{\nabla\sigma}\,g
,
we get the
generalized gradient Ricci soliton
equation

{\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}-c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g-c_{1}d\sigma\otimes d\sigma\,,

(2)

and the tensor product notation
(d\sigma\otimes d\sigma)(Y,Z)=d\sigma(Y)\,d\sigma(Z)
is used.
Each equation above gives a generalization of Einstein metric manifolds.
For different values of
c_{1},c_{2}
and
\lambda
, equation (
1
) generalizes
Killing equation (
c_{1}=c_{2}=\lambda=0
), equation for homotheties (
c_{1}=c_{2}=0
), Ricci soliton equation
(
c_{1}=0,\,c_{2}=-1
), vacuum near-horizon geometry equation (
c_{1}=1,\ c_{2}=1/2
), e.g.,
[
16
]
.
Catino et al.
[
8
]
also introduced the notion of an Einstein-type structure on a manifold,

\beta\,{\cal L}_{V}\,g+\alpha\operatorname{Ric}=(\rho\,{\rm Scal}+\lambda)\,g+\mu\,V^{\flat}\otimes V^{\flat}

(with
\alpha,\beta,\mu,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
)
unifying various cases studied recently, such as gradient Ricci solitons, and quasi-Einstein manifolds.
There are no Einstein metrics on some compact manifolds, which motivated the study of generalizations of such metrics.
A contact metric manifold is called
\eta
-Einstein, if

\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\nu\,\eta\otimes\eta,\quad\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{R}.

Under certain conditions, a Sasaki-Ricci flow (an odd-dimensional counterpart of the Kähler-Ricci flow) on a compact manifold converges to an
\eta
-Einstein metric on a contact metric manifold, see
[
29
]
. The notion of
\eta
-Ricci soliton on a contact metric manifold, see
[
11
]
,
generalizes Ricci solitons and
\eta
-Einstein metrics:

(1/2)\,\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\nu\,\eta\otimes\eta,\quad\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.

A metric
f
-structure on a
(2n+s)
-dimensional smooth manifold is a higher dimensional analog of a contact structure,
defined by a (1,1)-tensor

of constant rank

Foliations of simple extrinsic geometry, i.e., vanishing second fundamental form of the leaves,

[

.
The following problem was posed in
[

: find suitable structures on manifolds, which lead to totally geodesic foliations.
The distribution

An interesting case occurs when

(3)

\operatorname{Ric}({{\xi_{i}}},{{\xi_{j}}})\overset{\eqref{E-R1}}{=}-\sum\nolimits_{\,k=1}^{\,2n}g({f}^{2}e_{k},e_{k})\overset{\eqref{2.1}}{=}\sum\nolimits_{\,k=1}^{\,2n}g(Qe_{k},e_{k}).

By the above and the equality
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=2\,n+{\rm\,trace\,}\widetilde{Q}
, (
29
) is true.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

There are no Einstein weak
f
-K-contact manifolds
M^{2n+s}(f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
with
s{>}1
.

Proof.

For a weak
f
-K-contact manifold with
s>1
and vector field
\tilde{\xi}=(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})/\sqrt{2}
, we get

{\rm Ric}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\xi})=(1/2)\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{2}{\rm Ric}(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})\overset{\eqref{E-R1b}}{=}2{\rm\,trace\,}Q.

(33)

If
(M,g)
is an Einstein manifold, then for the unit vector field
\tilde{\xi}
we get
{\rm Ric}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\xi})={\rm Ric}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})={\rm\,trace\,}Q
.
Comparing with (
33
) gives a contradiction:
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=0
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For a weak
f
-K-contact manifold
M^{2n+s}(f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
, the
\xi
-sectional curvature is positive:

K(\xi_{i},{X})=g(Q{X},{X})>0\quad({X}\in{\cal D},\ \|{X}\|=1),

and for the Ricci curvature we get
{\rm Ric}({\xi}_{i},{\xi}_{j})>0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq s
.

Proof.

For any unit vectors
\xi\in\ker f,\ X\in{\cal D}
, from (
27
), we get
K(\xi,X)=g(fX,fX)>0
.
From (
29
), using (
3
) and non-singularity of
f
on
{\cal D}
, we get the following:

\operatorname{Ric}({{\xi}_{i}},{{\xi}_{j}})={\rm\,trace\,}Q=-\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{2n}g(f^{2}e_{k},e_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{2n}g(fe_{k},fe_{k})>0,

where
(e_{k})
is a local orthonormal frame of
{\cal D}
,
thus the second statement is valid.
∎

The next theorem generalizes
[
34
, Proposition 5.1 on p. 283]
and
[
23
, Theorem 4]
for

Theorem 5.1
.

A weak
f
-K-contact manifold
M^{2n+s}(f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
satisfying
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
and
(\nabla\operatorname{Ric})({\xi_{i}},\,\cdot)=0
,
e.g., the Ricci tensor is parallel, is
an Einstein manifold and
s=1
.

Proof.

Differentiating (
29
) and using (
25
) and the conditions, we have

0=\nabla_{Y}\,({\rm Ric}({{\xi_{i}}},{{\xi_{i}}}))=(\nabla_{Y}\,{\rm Ric})({{\xi_{i}}},{{\xi_{i}}})+2\,{\rm Ric}(\nabla_{Y}\,{{\xi_{i}}},{{\xi_{i}}})=-2\,{\rm Ric}({f}Y,{{\xi_{i}}}),

hence
{\rm Ric}(Y,{{\xi_{i}}})=({\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y)
.
Differentiating this, then using

X({\eta^{i}}(Y))=g(\nabla_{X}{\xi_{i}},Y)=-g({f}X,Y)+g(\nabla_{X}Y,{\xi_{i}})

and assuming
\nabla_{X}Y=0
at
x\in M

({\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,g({f}Y,X)=\nabla_{X}\,({\rm Ric}(Y,{{\xi_{i}}}))=(\nabla_{X}\,{\rm Ric})(Y,{{\xi_{i}}})+2\,{\rm Ric}(Y,\nabla_{X}\,{{\xi_{i}}})=-2\,{\rm Ric}(Y,{f}X).

Thus,
{\rm Ric}(Y,{f}X)=({\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,g(Y,{f}X)
.
Therefore,

{\rm Ric}(X,Y)=({\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,g(X,Y)

for any vector fields
X
and
Y
on
M
. By the above and (

),
(M,g)

is
an Einstein manifold and
s=1
.
∎

Corollary 5.3

(see
[
23
]
)
.

A weak K-contact manifold
M^{2n+1}(f,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
with
(\nabla\operatorname{Ric})(\xi,\,\cdot)=0
,
e.g., the Ricci tensor is parallel, and
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
is an Einstein manifold of scalar curvature

(2\,n+1){\rm\,trace\,}Q
.

Remark 5.1
.

For
f
-contact manifolds, we have

\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{\,2n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\,f)\,e_{i}=2\,n\,\bar{\xi},

(34)

see
[
13
, Proposition 2.6]
.
For
f
-K-contact manifolds, (
28
) and (
34
) give
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}(\xi_{i})=2\,n\,\bar{\xi}\ (i=1,\ldots,s)

and
\operatorname{Ric}(\xi_{i},\xi_{i})=2\,n
.
Can one generalize this and (
34
) for weak
f
-contact manifolds ?

Define the skew-symmetric operator
{T}^{\sharp}_{\xi}\ (\xi\in\ker f)
by

g({T}^{\sharp}_{\xi}X,Y)=g({T}(X,Y),\xi)\ (X,Y\in{\cal D})
,
where
T(X,Y)=(1/2)(\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X)^{\bot}
is the integrability tensor of
{\cal D}
.

The self-adjoint partial Ricci curvature tensor, see
[
28
]
, is given by

\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}(X)=\sum\nolimits_{\,i}({R}_{\,X^{\bot},\,\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{i})^{\bot},

and for an
f
-K-contact manifold, we have
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}=s\,{\rm id}^{\bot}
.
By Corollary
5.2
, the tensor
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}
of a weak
f
-K-contact manifold is positive definite.
In
[
28
]
, using the flow of metrics on a
\mathfrak{g}
-foliation, a deformation retraction of
a weak almost
{\cal S}
-structures with positive partial Ricci curvature onto the subspace of the aforementioned classical structures is constructed.
In the following theorem, using Corollary
5.2
and the method of
[
28
]
, we show that a weak
f
-K-contact structure can be transformed into an
f
-K-contact structure.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M^{2n+s}(f(0),Q_{\,0},\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g_{0})
be a weak
f
-K-contact manifold.
Then there exist metrics
g_{t}\ (t\in\mathbb{R})

such that each
(f(t),Q_{\,t},\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g_{t})
is a weak
f
-K-contact structure on
M
with structural tensors defined on
{\cal D}
as

Q_{\,t}=(1/s)\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}_{t},\quad f(t)\,|_{\,{\cal D}}=T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp}(t).

(35)

Moreover,
g_{t}
converges exponentially fast, as
t\to-\infty
, to a limit metric
\hat{g}

with
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}_{\hat{g}}=s\,{\rm id}^{\bot}
that gives an
f
-K-contact structure on
M
.

Proof.

The partial Ricci curvature tensor of a weak
f
-K-contact manifold is positive definite.
Thus, the result is similar to
[
28
, Theorem 1]
.
Consider the “partial Ricci flow” of metrics on
M
,

\partial_{t}\,g_{t}=-2\,(\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot})^{\flat}_{g_{t}}+2\,s\,g^{\bot}_{t},

(36)

where the
(0,2)
-tensor
g^{\bot}
is defined by
g^{\bot}(X,Y):=g(X^{\bot},Y^{\bot})
.
For
t=0
, we get on
{\cal D}
:

\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}=-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}(T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp})^{2}=-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\phi_{i}^{2}=s\,Q,

and find
T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp}\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}=\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp}
, see also
[
28
]
. Thus,

\sum\nolimits_{i}T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp}\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}T_{\xi_{i}}^{\sharp}=-(\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot})^{2}
.
By the above, we obtain the following ODE:

{\partial_{t}}\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}=4\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}(\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}-\,s\,{\rm id}^{\bot})
.
By the theory of ODE’s, there exists a unique solution
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
;
hence, a solution
g_{t}
of (
36
) exists for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and it is unique.
Observe that
(f(t),\xi_{i},\eta^{i},Q_{t})
with
f(t),Q_{t}
given in (
35
)
is a weak
f
-K-contact structure on
(M,g_{t})
.
By uniqueness of a solution, the flow (
36
) preserves the directions of eigenvectors of
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}
,
and each eigenvalue
\mu_{i}>0
of
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}
satisfies ODE

\dot{\mu}_{i}=4\mu_{i}\,(\mu_{i}-s)

with
\mu_{i}(0)>0
.
This ODE has the following solution (function
\mu_{i}(t)
on
M
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
):

\mu_{i}(t)=\frac{\mu_{i}(0)\,s}{\mu_{i}(0)+\exp(4\,s\,t)(s-\mu_{i}(0))}

with
\lim\limits_{t\to-\infty}\mu_{i}(t)=s
.
Thus,
\lim\limits_{t\to-\infty}\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}(t)=s\,{\rm id}^{\bot}
.
Let
\{e_{i}(t)\}
be a
g_{t}
-orthonormal frame of
{\cal D}
of eigenvectors associated with
\mu_{i}(t)
, we then get

{\partial_{t}}e_{i}=(\mu_{i}-s)e_{i}
.
Since
e_{i}(t)=z_{i}(t)\,e_{i}(0)
with
z_{i}(0)=1
, then
{\partial_{t}}\log z_{i}(t)=\mu_{i}(t)-s
.
By the above,
z_{i}(t)=(\mu_{i}(t)/\mu_{i}(0))^{1/4}
. Hence,

g_{t}(e_{i}(0),e_{j}(0))=z_{i}^{-1}(t)z_{j}^{-1}(t)\,g_{t}(e_{i}(t),e_{j}(t))=\delta_{ij}(\mu_{i}(0)\mu_{j}(0)/(\mu_{i}(t)\mu_{j}(t)))^{1/4}.

As
t\to-\infty
,
g_{t}
converges to the metric
\hat{g}
determined by

\hat{g}(e_{i}(0),e_{j}(0))=\delta_{ij}\sqrt{\mu_{i}(0)/s}
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
M^{2n+1}(f(0),Q_{\,0},\xi,\eta,g_{0})
be a weak K-contact manifold.
Then there exist metrics
g_{t}\ (t\in\mathbb{R})

such that each
(f(t),Q_{\,t},\xi,\eta,g_{t})
is a weak K-contact structure on

with structural tensors

Q_{\,t}=\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}_{t}
and

,
defined on
{\cal D}
.

g_{t}
converges exponentially fast, as

, to a limit metric
\hat{g}

with
\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}_{\hat{g}}={\rm id}^{\bot}
that gives a K-contact structure on

.

Remark 5.2
.

By Corollary
5.4
and results on K-contact manifolds, e.g.,
[

, p. 40]

\xi

6
Weak
f
-K-contact structure as a generalized Ricci soliton

Corollary
5.1
motivates to consider
quasi Einstein manifolds
, introduced in
[
10
]
by the condition

\operatorname{Ric}=a\,g+b\,\mu\otimes\mu

for all vector fields
X,Y
,
where

and

are real scalars, and

is a 1-form of unit norm, and later this structure has been studied by several geometers.
If

.

The following generalization of (
2
) was given in
[

{\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma_{1}}-c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g-c_{1}d\sigma_{2}\otimes d\sigma_{2}

for some
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
and real constants

and
\lambda
. For

, (
37
) reduces to (

).

First, we formulate some lemmas.

Lemma 6.1

(see Lemma 3.1 in
[
16
]
or Lemma 4 in
[
23
]
for

)
.

For a weak
f
-K-contact manifold

the following holds:

(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}(\pounds_{X}\,g))(Y,{\xi_{i}})=g(X,Y)+g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,X,Y)+Yg(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,X,{\xi_{i}})

for any
i\leq s
and all smooth vector fields
X,Y
with
Y
orthogonal to
\ker f
.

Proof.

This uses the equalities
\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,{\xi_{i}}=0
and (
27
), and is the same as for
[
23
, Lemma 4]
.
∎

Lemma 6.2

(see, for example,
[
16
]
)
.

Let
(M;g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
\sigma
be a smooth function on
M
. Then the following holds for every vector fields
\xi,Y
on
M:

\pounds_{{\xi}}(d\sigma\otimes d\sigma)(Y,{\xi})=Y({\xi}(\sigma))\,{\xi}(\sigma)+Y(\sigma)\,{\xi}({\xi}(\sigma)).

Recall that the Ricci curvature of any
f
-K-contact manifold satisfies the following condition:

\operatorname{Ric}(X,{\xi})=0\quad(X\in{\cal D},\ \xi\in\ker f).

(38)

Lemma 6.3

(see Lemma 3.3 in
[
16
]
or Lemma 6 in
[
23
]
for
s=1
)
.

Let a weak
f
-K-contact manifold
M^{2n+s}(f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
satisfy (
38
) and admit the generalized gradient Ricci soliton structure.
Then

\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla\sigma=(\lambda+c_{2}{\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,{\xi_{i}}-c_{1}{\xi_{i}}(\sigma)\,\nabla\sigma.

Proof.

By (
29
) and (

) we get

\lambda\,{\eta^{i}}(Y)+c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}({\xi_{i}},Y)=(\lambda+c_{2}{\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y).

(39)

Using (
2
) and (

), we get

{\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}({\xi_{i}},Y)=-c_{1}{\xi_{i}}(\sigma)\,g(\nabla\sigma,Y)+(\lambda+c_{2}{\rm\,trace\,}Q)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y).

(40)

Thus, (
40
) and the condition (
2
) for the Hessian complete the proof.
∎

The following theorem generalizes
[
23
, Theorem 4]
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
with
s=1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let a weak
f
-K-contact manifold
M^{2n+s}(f,Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
with
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
satisfy the generalized gradient Ricci soliton equation
(

\sigma=const
. Furthermore, if
c_{2}\neq 0
, then
(M,g)
is an Einstein manifold and
s=1
.

Proof.

Let
Y\bot\ker f
. Then by Lemma
6.1
with
X=\nabla f
, we obtain

2\,(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}({\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}))(Y,{\xi_{i}})=Y(\sigma)+g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla\sigma,Y)+Yg(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla\sigma,{\xi_{i}}).

(41)

Using Lemma
6.3
in (

\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,{\xi_{i}}=0
and
g({\xi_{i}},{\xi_{i}})=1
, yields

\displaystyle 2\,(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}({\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}))(Y,{\xi_{i}})=Y(\sigma)+a\,g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,{\xi_{i}},Y)

\displaystyle-c_{1}g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}({\xi_{i}}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma),Y)+a\,Y(g({\xi_{i}},{\xi_{i}}))-c_{1}Y({\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2})

\displaystyle=Y(\sigma)-c_{1}g(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}({\xi_{i}}(\sigma)\nabla\sigma),Y)-c_{1}Y({\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2}),

(42)

where

.

6.3
with
Y\in{\cal D}

) it follows that

2\,(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}({\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}))(Y,{\xi_{i}})=Y(\sigma)-c_{1}{\xi_{i}}({\xi_{i}}(\sigma))\,Y(\sigma)+c_{1}^{2}{\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2}Y(\sigma)-c_{1}Y({\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2}).

(43)

Since
{\xi_{i}}
is a Killing vector field, thus
\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}\,g=0

\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}\operatorname{Ric}=0
.
Using the above fact and applying the Lie derivative to equation (

2\,(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}({\rm Hess}_{\,\sigma}))(Y,{\xi_{i}})=-2\,c_{1}(\pounds_{{\xi_{i}}}(d\sigma\otimes d\sigma))(Y,{\xi_{i}}).

(44)

Using (

6.2

Y(\sigma)\big(1+c_{1}{\xi_{i}}({\xi_{i}}(\sigma))+c_{1}^{2}\,{\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2}\big)=0.

By Lemma
6.3
, we get

c_{1}{\xi_{i}}({\xi_{i}}(\sigma))=c_{1}\,{\xi_{i}}(g({\xi_{i}},\nabla\sigma))=c_{1}g({\xi_{i}},\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\nabla\sigma)=c_{1}a-c_{1}^{2}\,{\xi_{i}}(\sigma)^{2}.

Using (

) in (

Y(\sigma)(c_{1}a+1)=0
.

.
Taking the covariant derivative of

7
\eta
-Ricci solitons on compact weak
f
-K-contact manifolds

A weak metric
f
-manifold
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,{\xi_{i}},{\eta^{i}},g)
is said to be
\eta
-Einstein
, if

\operatorname{Ric}=\alpha\,g+\beta\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{i}+(\alpha+\beta)\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{j}

(49)

for some functions
\alpha,\beta\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

By an
\eta
-Ricci soliton
we mean here a weak
f
-contact manifold, whose Ricci tensor satisfies

(1/2)\,\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\nu\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{i}+(\lambda+\nu)\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{j}

(50)

for some functions
\lambda,\nu\in C^{\infty}(M)
and some smooth vector field
V
on
M
.

Remark 7.1
.

For
s=1
, (
49
) and (
50
) give the well-known definitions on contact metric manifolds.
Let
s=1
and
Q={\rm id}
. Then from (
49
) we get an
\eta
-Einstein structure
\operatorname{Ric}=\alpha\,g+\beta\,\eta\otimes\eta
,
e.g.,
[
17
]
;
and
from (
50
) we get an
\eta
-Ricci soliton
\frac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\beta\,\eta\otimes\eta
on an almost contact metric manifold, e.g.,
[
17
]
.
Equation (
50
) gives a generalized
\eta
-Ricci soliton
\frac{1}{2}\,\pounds_{V}\,g+\operatorname{Ric}=\lambda\,g+\nu\,\eta\otimes\eta
on an almost contact metric manifold, e.g.,
[
17
]
;
if also
\nu=0
, then (
50
) gives a Ricci almost soliton;
moreover, for
\lambda=const
it is a Ricci soliton.

For weak
f
-K-manifolds and
V=\bar{\xi}
, the notion of
\eta
-Ricci soliton reduces to
\eta
-Einstein structure.
For this reason, in this section we study an
\eta
-Ricci soliton on a weak
f
-K-contact manifold.

We recall the generalized Pohozaev-Schoen identity due to Gover-Orsted
[
15
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary.
If
E
is a divergence free symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor and
V
is a vector field on
M
, then

\int_{\,M}V({\rm\,trace\,}_{\!g}\,E)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}=\frac{n}{2}\int_{\,M}g(E^{0},\pounds_{V}\,g)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol},

(51)

where
E^{0}=E-\frac{1}{n}\,({\rm\,trace\,}_{\!g}\,E)\,g
and
{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}
is the volume form of
g
.

Denote by
r
the scalar curvature of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
.
The constancy of
r
plays an important role for the triviality of a compact generalized
\eta
-Ricci soliton,
see
[
17
]
. We extend this result for compact weak
f
-K-contact manifolds.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let a compact weak
f
-
K
-contact manifold
M^{2n+s}(f,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},Q,g)
satisfy

r=const
and
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
. If
(g,V,\lambda,\nu)
represents an
\eta
-Ricci soliton, then
M
is
\eta
-Einstein.

Proof.

We take the trace of (
49
) to get the scalar curvature
r=(2n+s)\,\alpha+s\,\beta
.
On the other hand, using (
29
) in (
49
), we obtain
\alpha+\beta={\rm\,trace\,}Q
. From these it follows that

\alpha=\frac{r-s{\rm\,trace\,}Q}{2\,n},\quad\beta=\frac{(2\,n+s){\rm\,trace\,}Q-r}{2\,n}.

(52)

Define a traceless symmetric (0,2)-tensor
E
as

E=\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{r-s{\rm\,trace\,}Q}{2\,n}\,g+\frac{r-(2\,n+s){\rm\,trace\,}Q}{2\,n}\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{i}-({\rm\,trace\,}Q)\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{j}.

(53)

We express (
50
), using (
53
), as

\displaystyle(1/2)\,\pounds_{V}\,g+E

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda-\frac{r-s{\rm\,trace\,}Q}{2\,n}\big)\,g+\big(\nu+\frac{r-(2\,n+s){\rm\,trace\,}Q}{2\,n}\big)\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{i}

\displaystyle+(\lambda+\nu-{\rm\,trace\,}Q)\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{j}.

(54)

Taking covariant derivative of (
53
) along
X
and using (
25
) and
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
, gives

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}\,E)(Y,Z)=(\nabla_{X}\operatorname{Ric})(Y,Z)-\frac{X(r)}{2\,n}\,\big(g(Y,Z)-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\eta^{i}(Y)\,\eta^{i}(Z)\big)

\displaystyle+\Big((s-1){\rm\,trace\,}Q+\frac{(2\,n+s){\rm\,trace\,}Q-r}{2\,n}\Big)\,\big(\bar{\eta}(Z)\,g(fX,Y)+\bar{\eta}(Y)\,g(fX,Z)\big).

(55)

Taking the trace of (
7
) over
X
and
Z
, and since

r=const
,
\operatorname{div}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}=\frac{1}{2}\,d\,r
(twice-contracted 2nd Bianchi identity,
[
19
, p. 135]
),
and
{\rm\,trace\,}f=0
, we get the equality
\operatorname{div}_{g}E=0
:

(\operatorname{div}_{g}E)(Y)=(\operatorname{div}_{g}\operatorname{Ric})(Y)+\frac{s\,(2\,n+1){\rm\,trace\,}Q-r}{2\,n}\,\big(\bar{\eta}(e_{i})\,g(fe_{i},Y)+\bar{\eta}(Y)\,g(fe_{i},e_{i})\big)=0.

Since the tensor
E
is traceless, we get
E^{0}=E
and
V({\rm\,trace\,}_{\!g}E)=0
.
Thus, applying (
51
), we find

\int_{\,M}g(E,\pounds_{V}\,g)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}=0
.
Applying (
7
) and (
29
) in the above integral formula,
and using equalities
g(E,g)={\rm\,trace\,}_{\!g}E=0
and
g(E,\eta^{i}\otimes\eta^{j})=E(\xi_{i},\xi_{j})=0
,
we obtain

\int_{\,M}g(E,E)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}=0.

(56)

From (
56
) we get
E=0
.
Thus, our manifold is
\eta
-Einstein, see (
49
), where
\alpha,\beta
satisfy (
52
).
∎

The following consequence of Theorem
7.1
generalizes
[
17
, Theorem 3.1(i)]
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let a compact weak
K
-contact manifold
M^{2n+1}(f,\xi,\eta,Q,g)
satisfy
r=const
and
{\rm\,trace\,}Q=const
.
If
(g,V,\lambda,\nu)
represents a generalized
\eta
-Ricci soliton on
M
,
then
M
is
\eta
-Einstein.

8
Conclusion

It is shown that a weak
f
-contact structure is a useful tool for studying unit Killing vector fields, totally geodesic foliations and mixed sectional curvature on Riemannian manifolds. Some results for
f
-contact and
f
-K-contact structures
[
4
,
34
]
have been extended to certain weak structures. Conditions are found under which
(i) a weak
f
-K-contact manifold with parallel Ricci tensor or with a generalized gradient Ricci soliton structure is a quasi Einstein or an Einstein manifold;
(ii) a compact weak
f
-K-contact manifold with constant scalar curvature and an
\eta
-Ricci soliton structure is
\eta
-Einstein.
Based on applications of the weak
f
-contact structure in contact geometry considered in the article,
we expect that this structure will also be fruitful in theoretical physics.

In conclusion, we pose several questions.
Is the condition “the
\xi
-sectional curvature is positive” sufficient for a weak
f
-contact manifold to be weak
f
-K-contact?
Does a weak
f
-contact manifold of dimension greater than
3
have some positive
\xi
-sectional curvature?
Is a compact weak
f
-K-contact Einstein manifold an
{\cal S}
-manifold?
When a given weak
f
-K-contact manifold is a mapping torus (see
[
18
]
) of a manifold of lower dimension?
When a weak
f
-contact manifold equipped with a Ricci-type soliton structure,
carries a canonical (for example, with constant sectional curvature or Einstein-type) metric?
One could answer these questions by generalizing some deep results on
f
-contact manifolds for weak
f
-contact manifolds.

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we study the existence and nonexistence of positive solutions for the following coupled elliptic system with critical exponent and logarithmic terms:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1}u+\mu_{1}|u|^{2}u+\beta|v|^{2}u+\theta_{1}u\log u^{2},&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
-\Delta v=\lambda_{2}v+\mu_{2}|v|^{2}v+\beta|u|^{2}v+\theta_{2}v\log v^{2},&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u=v=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(0.1)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
is a bounded smooth domain, the parameters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
and
\beta\neq 0
is a coupling constant. Note that the logarithmic term
s\log s^{2}
has special properties, which makes the problem more complicated. We show that this system has a positive least energy solution for
|\beta|
small and positive large
\beta
if
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
. While the situations for the case
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<0
are quite thorny, in this challenging setting we establish the existence result of positive local minima solutions and nonnegative solutions under various conditions on the parameters. Besides, under some further assumptions, we obtain the nonexistence of positive solutions for both the case where
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
are
negative and the case where they have opposite signs. Comparing our results with those of Chen and Zou (Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 205:515–551, 2012), the logarithmic term
s\log s^{2}
introduces some new interesting phenomenon. Moreover, its presence brings major challenges and make it difficult to use the comparison theorem used in the work of Chen and Zou without new ideas and innovative techniques.
To the best of our knowledge, our paper is the first to give a rather complete picture for the existence and nonexistence results to the coupled elliptic system with critical exponent and logarithmic terms.
Also, we consider the related single equation

-\Delta u=\lambda u+\mu|u|^{2}u+\theta u\log u^{2},~u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)

(0.2)

with
\mu>0
,
\theta<0
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
or
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{1}(\Omega))
and prove the existence of the positive solution under some further suitable assumptions, which is the type of a local minima or a least energy solution.

Schrödinger system, Brézis-Nirenberg problem, Critical exponent, Logarithmic perturbation, Positive solution,

Introduction

Consider the following coupled elliptic system with logarithmic terms

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1}u+\mu_{1}|u|^{2}u+\beta|v|^{2}u+\theta_{1}u\log u^{2},&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
-\Delta v=\lambda_{2}v+\mu_{2}|v|^{2}v+\beta|u|^{2}v+\theta_{2}v\log v^{2},&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u=v=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
is a bounded smooth domain,
\lambda_{i},\theta_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(i=1,2)
,
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
and
\beta\neq 0
is a coupling constant.

System (
1.1
) is closely related to the following time-dependent nonlinear logarithmic type Schrödinger system

\begin{cases}\imath\partial_{t}\Psi_{1}=\Delta\Psi_{1}+\mu_{1}|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\Psi_{1}+\beta|\Psi_{2}|^{2}\Psi_{1}+\theta_{1}\Psi_{1}\log\Psi_{1}^{2},\quad x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\imath\partial_{t}\Psi_{2}=\Delta\Psi_{2}+\ \mu_{2}|\Psi_{2}|^{2}\Psi_{2}+\beta|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\Psi_{2}+\theta_{2}\Psi_{2}\log\Psi_{2}^{2},\quad x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\Psi_{i}=\Psi_{i}(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad i=1,2\\
\Psi_{i}(x,t)=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t>0,\quad i=1,2,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\imath
is the imaginary unit. System (
1.2
) appears in many physical fields, such as quantum mechanics, quantum optics,
nuclear physics, transport and diffusion phenomena, open quantum systems, effective quantum gravity, theory
of superfluidity and Bose-Einstein condensation. We refer the readers to the papers
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
21
,
25
]
for a survey on the related physical backgrounds.

When
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=0
, the system (
1.1
) reduces to the following coupled nonlinear Schrödinger system

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{1}u+\mu_{1}|u|^{2}u+\beta|v|^{2}u,&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
-\Delta v=\lambda_{2}v+\mu_{2}|v|^{2}v+\beta|u|^{2}v,&\quad x\in\Omega,\\
u=v=0,&\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.3)

which appears when looking for standing wave solutions
\Psi_{1}(x,t)=e^{\imath\lambda_{1}t}u(x)
and
\Psi_{2}(x,t)=e^{\imath\lambda_{2}t}v(x)
of time-dependent coupled nonlinear Schrödinger system

\begin{cases}\imath\partial_{t}\Psi_{1}=\Delta\Psi_{1}+\mu_{1}|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\Psi_{1}+\beta|\Psi_{2}|^{2}\Psi_{1},\quad x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\imath\partial_{t}\Psi_{2}=\Delta\Psi_{2}+\ \mu_{2}|\Psi_{2}|^{2}\Psi_{2}+\beta|\Psi_{1}|^{2}\Psi_{2},\quad x\in\Omega,~t>0,\\
\Psi_{i}=\Psi_{i}(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad i=1,2\\
\Psi_{i}(x,t)=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\quad t>0,\quad i=1,2.\end{cases}

(1.4)

This system (
1.4
) originates from many physical models, especially in nonlinear optics and Bose-Einstein condensation. We refer for these to
[
4
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, which also contain information about the physical relevance
of non-cubic nonlinearities.

Note that the nonlinearity and coupling terms of system (
1.3
) are both critical in dimension 4 (that is, the Sobolev critical exponent
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}=4
when
N=4
), and the critical system (
1.3
) was considered by Chen and Zou
[
9
]
for the first time. In
[
9
]
, they proved that there exist
0<\beta_{*}<\beta^{*}
such that system (
1.3
) has a positive least energy solution if
\beta\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\beta_{*})\cup(\beta^{*},+\infty)
when
0<\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
, where
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We would also like to highlight the paper
[
10
,
20
,
29
]
where it is shown, for more general powers, that the dimension has a significant impact on the existence of positive least energy solutions.

Inspired by their work, and noticing that the system (
1.1
) can be viewed as system (
1.3
) with a logarithmic perturbation, we are interested in the existence and nonexistence of positive solution for the system (
1.1
) with logarithmic terms. To the best of our knowledge, there are no results in the literature dealing with (
1.1
).

The logarithmic term has some special properties, which make things more challenging. It is easy to see that

\lim\limits_{u\to 0^{+}}\frac{u\log u^{2}}{u}=-\infty,

(1.5)

that is,
u=o(u\log u^{2})
for
u
very close to 0. Comparing with the critical term
|u|^{2}u
, the logarithmic term
u\log u^{2}
is a lower-order term at infinity. Also, the sign of the logarithmic term changes depending on
u
. Moreover, the presence of logarithmic term makes the structure of the corresponding functional complicated.

Recently, Deng et al.
[
14
]
investigated the existence of positive least energy solutions for the following related single equation

-\Delta u=\lambda_{i}u+\mu_{i}|u|^{2}u+\theta_{i}u\log u^{2},~u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),~\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{4},~i=1,2,

(1.6)

In
[
14
]
, the authors proved that the equation (
1.6
) has a positive least energy solution if
\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mu_{i},\theta_{i}>0
. Also, they obtained the existence and nonexistence results under other different conditions, we refer the readers to
[
14
]
for details. When
\theta_{i}=0
, the equation (
1.6
) reduces to the classical Brézis-Nirenberg problem (see
[
7
]
). It is well known that the classical Brézis-Nirenberg equation has a positive least energy solution if
0<\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
,
\mu_{i}>0
. So, the logarithmic term
\theta_{i}u\log u^{2}
has much more influence than the term
\lambda_{i}u
on the existence solutions. Therefore, we believe that some different phenomena will occur when studying the existence of positive least energy solution for the system (
1.1
).

In this paper, we will study the existence and nonexistence of positive solution for the system (
1.1
) with logarithmic terms. The fact that
\theta_{i}\neq 0
makes the problem quite different and also more
complicated comparing to the case
\theta_{i}=0
(
i=1,2
), and requires us to develop some different ideas and techniques.

Let us first introduce some definitions.
We call a solution
(u,v)
fully nontrivial if both
u\not\equiv 0
and
v\not\equiv 0
; we call a solution
(u,v)
semi-trivial if
u\equiv 0
,
v\not\equiv 0
or
u\not\equiv 0
,
v\equiv 0
; we call a solution
(u,v)
positive (resp. nonnegative) if both
u>0
and
v>0
(resp. both
u\geq 0
and
v\geq 0
).

Define
\mathcal{H}:=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. To find a positive solution to the system (
1.1
), we borrow the ideas from
[
14
]
to define a modified functional
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{1}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{4}-\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}(\log(u^{+})^{2}-1)

(1.7)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{2}-\frac{\mu_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{4}-\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{2}(\log(v^{+})^{2}-1)-\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2},

where
u^{+}:=\max\left\{u,0\right\}
,
u^{-}:=-\max\left\{-u,0\right\}
. It is easy to see that the functional
\mathcal{L}
is well-defined in
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, any nonnegative critical point of
\mathcal{L}
corresponds to a solution of the system (
1.1
).

We say a solution
(u,v)
of (
1.1
) is a least energy solution if
(u,v)
is fully nontrivial and
\mathcal{L}(u,v)\leq\mathcal{L}(\varphi,\psi)
for any other fully nontrivial solution
(\varphi,\psi)
of system (
1.1
). As in
[
18
]
, we consider

\mathcal{N}=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}:u\not\equiv 0,v\not\equiv 0,\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,0)=0\text{ and }\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(0,v)=0\right\}.

(1.8)

It is easy to check that
\mathcal{N}\neq\emptyset
. Then we set

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}:=\inf_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{L}(u,v).

(1.9)

Define

\varSigma_{1}:=\left\{(\lambda,\mu,\theta):\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},\mu>0,\theta>0\right\},

(1.10)

and

\Lambda:=\min_{i=1,2}\left\{\cfrac{\mu_{i}}{\left(\mu_{i}+\theta_{i}e^{\lambda_{i}/\theta_{i}-1}\right)^{2}}\right\}.

(1.11)

Our first existence result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\left(\lambda_{i},\mu_{i},\theta_{i}\right)\in\varSigma_{1}~
for
i=1,2
.

(1)

There exists
\beta_{0}\in\left(0,\min\left\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right\}\right]
such that system (
1.1
) has a positive least energy solution
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{L}(u,v)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
for any
\beta\in\left(-\beta_{0},0\right)
.

(2)

There exists
\beta_{1}\in\left(0,\min\left\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right\}\right]
such that system (
1.1
) has a positive least energy solution
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{L}(u,v)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
for any
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{1}\right)
.

(3)

Let
\beta_{2}
be the larger root of the equation

\beta^{2}-\frac{2}{\Lambda}\beta+\frac{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}{\Lambda}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}=0,

(1.12)

where
\Lambda
is defined in (
1.11
). Then we have
\beta_{2}>\max\left\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right\}
, and system (
1.1
) has a positive least energy solution
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{L}(u,v)=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
for any
\beta>\beta_{2}
.

The Nehari set

Now we establish both lower and upper uniform estimates on the
L^{4}
-norms of elements in the Nehari set that fall below a certain energy level.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in\left(-\sqrt{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}},0\right)\cup\left(0,\beta_{1}\right)
. Then there exist
C_{2}>C_{1}>0
, such that for any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{L}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}
, there holds

C_{1}\leq|u^{+}|_{4}^{4},|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\leq C_{2}.

(2.2)

Here,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
depend only on
\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
,
\theta_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

Take any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{L}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}
.
Firstly, when
-\sqrt{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}<\beta<0
, since the inequality
s^{2}\log s^{2}\leq e^{-1}s^{4}
holds, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}|u^{+}|^{2}_{4}\leq|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log(u^{+})^{2}+\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log(e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}}(u^{+})^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}-1}\right)|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}.

Therefore, we have

|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}\geq\mathcal{S}^{2}\left(\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}-1}\right)^{-2}
. Similarly, we have
|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\geq\mathcal{S}^{2}\left(\mu_{2}+\theta_{2}e^{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\theta_{2}}-1}\right)^{-2}
.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}\geq\mathcal{L}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(u,v)-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\theta_{1}}{4}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log\left(e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}}(u^{+})^{2}\right)+\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla v|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\theta_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}(v^{+})^{2}\log\left(e^{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\theta_{2}}}(v^{+})^{2}\right)+\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}|v^{+}|_{2}^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}\geq\mathcal{L}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(u,v)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\mu_{2}|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}+2\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}\right)+\frac{\theta_{1}}{2}|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{2}|v^{+}|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\beta>-\sqrt{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}
, there exists some positive constants
c_{0},C_{0}>0
such that

c_{0}\left(|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\right)\leq\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\mu_{2}|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}+2\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{0}\left(|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\right).

(2.6)

Therefore, we have

|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}\leq(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\theta_{1}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{2}\text{ and }|v^{+}|_{2}^{2}\leq(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\theta_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{2}.

(2.7)

Recalling the following useful inequality (see
[
26
]
or
[
19
, Theorem 8.14]
)

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\log u^{2}\leq\frac{a}{\pi}|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+\left(\log|u|_{2}^{2}-N(1+\log a)\right)|u|_{2}^{2}\ \ \text{ for }u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\ \text{ and }\ a>0,

(2.8)

where
N=4
is the dimension of
\Omega
. Let
w^{+}=e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{1}}}u^{+}
and
z^{+}=e^{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2\theta_{2}}}v^{+}
. Since
|s^{2}\log s^{2}|\leq Cs^{2-\tau}+Cs^{2+\tau}
for any
\tau\in\left(0,1\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+|\nabla v|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1}}{2}\mathcal{S}^{2}+\frac{\theta_{1}}{4}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}}\int_{\Omega}(w^{+})^{2}\log(w^{+})^{2}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{4}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\theta_{2}}}\int_{\Omega}(z^{+})^{2}\log(z^{+})^{2}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1}}{2}\mathcal{S}^{2}+\frac{\theta_{1}}{4}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}}\left[\frac{a}{\pi}|\nabla w|_{2}^{2}+|w^{+}|_{2}^{2}\log|w^{+}|_{2}^{2}-N(1+\log a)|w^{+}|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta_{2}}{4}e^{-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\theta_{2}}}\left[\frac{a}{\pi}|\nabla z|_{2}^{2}+|z^{+}|_{2}^{2}\log|z^{+}|_{2}^{2}-N(1+\log a)|z^{+}|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1}}{2}\mathcal{S}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}+C|u^{+}|_{2}^{2-\tau}+C|u^{+}|_{2}^{2+\tau}+C|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8}|\nabla v|_{2}^{2}+C|v^{+}|_{2}^{2-\tau}+C|v^{+}|_{2}^{2+\tau}+C|v^{+}|_{2}^{2},

where we fix
a>0
with
\frac{a}{\pi}\theta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{a}{\pi}\theta_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, combining this with (

), we can see that there exists
C_{2}>0
, such that

|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}\leq S^{-2}|\nabla u|_{2}^{4}\leq C_{2}.

(2.10)

Similarly, we have
|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\leq C_{2}
.

Secondly, when
\beta\in(0,\beta_{1})
, since
\theta_{i}>0
for
i=1,2
, we can see from (
2.5
)that

|u^{+}|^{4}_{4}\leq 2\mu_{1}^{-1}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ |v^{+}|^{4}_{4}\leq 2\mu_{2}^{-1}(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1})\mathcal{S}^{2}.

(2.11)

Since
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}|u^{+}|_{4}^{2}\leq|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log(u^{+})^{2}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\beta|u^{+}|_{4}^{2}|v^{+}|_{4}^{2}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log(e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}}(u^{+})^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}-1}\right)|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\beta\sqrt{2\mu_{2}^{-1}\left(\mu_{1}^{-1}+\mu_{2}^{-1}\right)}\mathcal{S}|u^{+}|_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}-1}\right)|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{S}|u^{+}|_{4}^{2}.

which uses the assumption

. Therefore,

|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{2}}{4\left(\mu_{1}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\theta_{1}}-1}\right)^{2}}.

(2.13)

Similarly, we have

|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}\geq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{2}}{4\left(\mu_{2}+\theta_{1}e^{\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\theta_{2}}-1}\right)^{2}}.

(2.14)

This completes the proof.

The following proposition shows that the set
\mathcal{N}
is a natural constraint when
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{1}\right)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{1}\right)
and the energy level
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is achieved by
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
. Then
(u,v)
is a critical point of the functional
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{G}_{1}(u,v):=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,0)\text{ and }\mathcal{G}_{2}(u,v):=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(0,v).

(2.15)

Take
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
. Since the parameters
\mu_{1},\beta,\theta_{1}>0
, we deduce from a direct calculation that

\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)=-2\left(\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{4}+\int_{\Omega}\beta|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2}\right)<0,

Similarly, we have
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,v)<0
. Therefore, it follows that
\mathcal{G}_{i}(u,v)
defines,
locally, a
C^{1}
-manifold of codimension 1 in
\mathcal{H}
for any
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
and
i=1,2
. Now we claim that in a neighborhood of
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
, the set
\mathcal{N}
is a
C^{1}
-manifold of codimension 2 in
\mathcal{H}
. For that purpose, we only need to show that

(\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}(u,v),\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}(u,v))
is a surjective as a linear operator
\mathcal{H}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Notice that

\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}(u,v)(t_{1}u,t_{2}v)=-2\left(\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{4}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2}\right)t_{1}-2\left(\int_{\Omega}\beta|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2}\right)t_{2},

(2.16)

\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}(u,v)(t_{1}u,t_{2}v)=-2\left(\int_{\Omega}\beta|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2}\right)t_{1}-2\left(\mu_{2}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{4}+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{2}\right)t_{2},

(2.17)

and the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\theta_{1}|u^{+}|_{2}^{2}&\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}\\
\beta|u^{+}v^{+}|_{2}^{2}&\mu_{2}|v^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\theta_{2}|v^{+}|_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

(2.18)

has strictly positive determinant. The positivity of the determinant follows from the fact
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0
,
0<\beta<\beta_{1}\leq\min\left\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right\}
and the Hölder’s inequality. Then for any
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exist
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}(w,z),\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}(w,z))=(s_{1},s_{2}),

where
(w,z)=(t_{1}u,t_{2}v)
. Therefore, the claim is true.

We suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}}
is achieved by
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
. Since the set
\mathcal{N}
is a
C^{1}
-manifold of codimension 2 in a neighborhood of
(u,v)\in\mathcal{N}
. Then by the Lagrange multipliers rule, there exist
L_{1},L_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)-L_{1}\mathcal{G}_{1}^{\prime}(u,v)-L_{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}^{\prime}(u,v)=0.

(2.19)

Multiplying the above equation with
\left(u,0\right)
and
\left(0,v\right)
, we deduce from
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(u,0)=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)(0,v)=0
that

L_{1}\left(\mu_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{4}+\theta_{1}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2}\right)+L_{2}\int_{\Omega}\beta|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2}=0.

(2.20)

L_{1}\int_{\Omega}\beta|u^{+}|^{2}|v^{+}|^{2}+L_{2}\left(\mu_{2}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{4}+\theta_{2}\int_{\Omega}|v^{+}|^{2}\right)=0.

(2.21)

Since the system (
2.20
)-(
2.21
) has a strictly positive determinant (since
u,v\not\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{N}
), by the Cramer’s rule, the system has a unique solution
L_{1}=L_{2}=0
, which implies that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,v)=0
and the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}
is a natural constraint in
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Then we have the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

For

, we have

\cfrac{\mu_{i}\mathcal{S}^{2}}{4\left(\mu_{i}+\theta_{i}e^{\lambda_{i}/\theta_{i}-1}\right)^{2}}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\theta_{i}}<\frac{1}{4}\mu_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{2}.

\mathcal{C}_{\theta_{i}}<\frac{1}{4}\mu_{i}^{-1}\mathcal{S}^{2},\quad\text{ for }i=1,2.

On the other hand, for any

for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}|u^{+}|^{2}_{4}\leq|\nabla u|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}|u^{+}|_{4}^{4}+\theta_{i}\int_{\Omega}(u^{+})^{2}\log(e^{\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\theta_{i}}}(u^{+})^{2})

(2.30)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{i}+\theta_{i}e^{\lambda_{i}/\theta_{i}-1}\right)|u^{+}|_{4}^{4},

Bergman spaces and reproducing kernel for the biquaternionic Vekua equation
Víctor A. Vicente-Benítez

Abstract
We analyze the main properties of the Bergman spaces of weak L p L_{p} - solutions for a biquaternionic Vekua equation of the form 𝐃 ​ w ​ ( x ) − 𝐐 A ​ w ​ ( x ) = 0 \mathbf{D}w(x)-\mathbf{Q}_{A}w(x)=0 on bounded domains of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , where the operator 𝐐 A \mathbf{Q}_{A} involves quaternionic conjugation and multiplications, both left and right, by essentially bounded functions. Properties such as completeness, separability, and reflexivity are shown. It is demonstrated that the solutions belonging to the Bergman spaces are locally Hölder continuous and that the evaluation maps are bounded in the L p L_{p} -norm. Consequently, for the case p = 2 p=2 , we obtain a reproducing integral kernel and an explicit formula for the orthogonal projection onto the Bergman space. For 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , the explicit form for the annihilator of the Bergman space in the dual L p ′ L_{p^{\prime}} is presented, along with an orthogonal decomposition for L 2 L_{2} .



Keywords:
Biquaternionic Vekua equation, Bergman spaces, Bergman kernel, quaternionic analysis.

30G20; 30G35; 30H20; 46E22.

1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to develop and analyze the main properties of the Bergman space of
L_{p}
-weak solutions of the biquaternionic Vekua equation

\mathbf{D}w(x)-w(x)a_{1}(x)+\overline{w(x)}a_{2}(x)+a_{3}(x)w(x)+a_{4}(x)\overline{w(x)}=0,\quad x\in G,

(1)

where
w
and
a_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, are biquaternionic-valued functions,
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with a Liapunov boundary, and
\mathbf{D}
is the Moisil-Theodorescu operator (also called Cauchy-Riemann or Dirac operator). The notation
\overline{w}
stands for the quaternionic conjugate of
w
. We assume that
a_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, are essentially bounded on
G
.

Biquaternionic equations of the form (
1
) arise in several elliptic systems of mathematical physics, such as the Dirac and Maxwell systems
[
6
,
19
,
21
]
, div-curl systems
[
10
]
, the study of Beltrami fields
[
23
,
25
]
, and the factorization of Helmholtz and Schrödinger equations
[
3
,
20
,
22
]
. This type of equation was treated first in the complex case by I. Vekua
[
37
]
and L. Bers
[
2
]
(who developed the theory of
pseudoanalytic functions
, called by Vekua
Generalized analytic functions
). The theory of pseudoanalytic functions and
(F,G)
-derivatives (in the sense of Bers) was extended to quaternionic-valued functions in
[
29
,
30
]
. In
[
26
,
27
]
, the theory of fundamental solutions and integral representations was developed for the case when
a_{1}
is a quaternionic constant and
a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0
. Eq. (
1
) is called a
main Vekua equation
when, for a fixed
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
,
a_{j}=\frac{\nabla f}{f}
and
a_{k}=0
for
j\neq k
, where
f
is a
C^{1}
function that does not vanish in the whole domain (the terminology extends the complex case given in
[
24
, Ch. 3]
). The cases for
j\in\{1,4\}
are associated with the factorization of the equations
\operatorname{div}f^{2}\nabla\left(\frac{u}{f}\right)=0
and
-\bigtriangleup u+\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}u=0
, which have been studied in works such as
[
3
,
28
,
19
,
10
]
, focusing on classical and
W^{1,2}
-solutions. In
[
11
]
, a generalization of Hardy spaces for the main Vekua equation in the case
j=4
was studied. The analysis of fundamental solutions for the case
j=3
was presented in
[
34
,
35
]
.

In this work, we analyze the main properties of the weak
L_{p}
- solutions (in the sense of Vekua
[
15
,
37
]
) of (
1
), such as completeness, separability, and reflexivity. In the first part, we investigate the regularity of the solutions to establish a result concerning the boundedness in the
L_{p}
-norm of the evaluation map
w\mapsto w(x)
, where
x\in G
. Consequently, for the case
p=2
, we prove the existence of integral kernels
K_{x}^{k}(t)
satisfying
w_{k}(x)=\langle K_{x}^{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}}
. Unlike the complex case, where the use of the similarity principle is crucial in proving the existence of the reproducing kernel
[
5
]
, there is no result of this nature in the biquaternionic case. Recently, in
[
8
]
, the existence of reproducing integral kernels for an equation with
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
involving
Cl_{0,n}
-valued functions was established. However, this result holds under the hypothesis that
\|a_{3}\|_{L_{\infty}}+\|a_{4}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\frac{1}{M}
, where
M
is the operator-norm of the Theodorescu transform (defined in Subsection
2.2
). Generalizing the ideas presented in
[
36
]
for the bicomplex Vekua equation, we study the regularity of the solutions of (
1
) and establish their Hölder continuity on every ball contained within the domain
G
. Employing a Calderon-Zygmun type inequality, along with the integral operator
\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}=\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{Q}_{A}
, where
\mathbf{T}_{G}
is the Theodorescu transform and
\mathbf{Q}_{A}=wa_{1}+\overline{w}a_{2}+a_{3}w+a_{4}\overline{w}
(this kind of operator was used in other works such as
[
5
,
8
,
36
]
), and the Sobolev embedding theorems, we prove the boundedness of the evaluation map
w\mapsto w(x)
in the
L_{p}
-norm. This result is proven without assuming more than the functions
a_{j}
,
j=1,2,3,4
, are essentially bounded on
G
. As a consequence, for
p=2
, we obtain an integral reproducing kernel for each component function
w_{k}(x)
and a representation for the orthogonal projection of
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
onto the the Bergman space.

The second part of this work is dedicated to establishing the annihilator of the Bergman space for
1<p<\infty
. We compute the transpose (in the sense of
[
4
]
) of the operator
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}
, which is given by
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A^{\star}}
, where
\mathbf{Q}_{A^{\star}}w=w\overline{a_{1}}+\overline{w}a_{4}+\overline{a_{3}}w+a_{2}\overline{w}
. The domain in
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
of the transpose is the Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. From this result, we obtain that the annihilator of the Bergman space is the closure in
L_{p^{\prime}}
of
(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A^{\star}})W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. In particular, this yields an orthogonal decomposition for the space
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. This type of decomposition is useful for solving boundary value problems associated with equations that can be factorized in terms of operators of the type
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}
(see
[
15
,
16
]
for examples in the case when
a_{1}
is a constant and
a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0
). Knowledge of the annihilator is also useful for the problem of establishing the dual of the Bergman space
[
7
,
18
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 presents the basic properties of the algebra of biquaternions, monogenic functions and integral operators. Section 3 establishes the main properties of the Bergman spaces of generalized solutions of Eq. (
1
). Section 4 focuses on the regularity of the solutions and demonstrates the boundedness of the evaluation map with respect to the
L_{p}
-norm. Section 5 is devoted to the construction of the Bergman reproducing kernel and orthogonal projection. Section 6 derives the explicit expression of the annihilator of the Bergman space, provides an orthogonal decomposition of the space
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and discusses some examples.

2
Background on quaternionic analysis

This section provides a summary of the main properties of the algebra of biquaternions and biquaternionic-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, as discussed in
[
15
,
22
]
. We use the notation
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Given Banach spaces
X
and
Y
,
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
denotes the Banach space of bounded linear operators and
\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
the subspace of compact operators. When
X=Y
, we denote
\mathcal{B}(X)=\mathcal{B}(X,X)
and
\mathcal{K}(X)=\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
. Additionally,
\mathcal{G}(X)
denotes the group of invertible operators.

2.1
Quaternions and quaternionic-valued functions

The algebra of the biquaternions (or complex quaternions) is denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The quaternionic units are denoted by
e_{k}
,
k=0,1,2,3
, and satisfy the relations
e_{0}e_{j}=e_{j}e_{0}=e_{j}
and
e_{j}e_{k}+e_{k}e_{j}=2\delta_{j,k}e_{0}
for
j,k=1,2,3
. Additionally, we have the relation with the complex imaginary unit:
\operatorname{i}e_{k}=e_{k}\operatorname{i}
,
k=0,1,2,3
. We identify
e_{0}
with
1
, and a biquaternion
p\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is written as
p=p_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{3}p_{k}e_{k}
, where
\{p_{k}\}_{k=0}^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}
. The complex number
p_{0}
is called the
scalar part
of
p
and is denoted by
\operatorname{Sc}p
. The
vector part
of
p
is
\vec{p}:=\sum_{k=1}^{3}p_{k}e_{k}
(sometimes denoted by
\operatorname{Vec}p
). We identify
\vec{p}
with a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Hence
p=p_{0}+\vec{p}
, and the product of two quaternions
p
and
q
has the form

pq=p_{0}q_{0}+p_{0}\vec{q}+q_{0}\vec{p}-\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}+\vec{p}\times\vec{q},

where
\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}p_{k}q_{k}
and
\vec{p}\times\vec{q}
is the cross product of
\vec{p}
with
\vec{q}
. The algebra
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is non-commutative and contains zero divisors
[
22
, Sec. 2.2]
. Given
p\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, its quaternionic conjugate is defined as
\overline{p}:=p_{0}-\vec{p}
. For
p\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, define
p^{\dagger}:=p_{0}^{*}-\vec{p}^{*}=p_{0}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}p_{k}^{*}e_{k}
,
where
∗
denotes the standard complex conjugation. The operation
p\mapsto p^{\dagger}
is an involution in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
and

\langle p,q\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}:=\operatorname{Sc}(p^{\dagger}q)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}p_{k}^{*}q_{k}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|p|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}:=\sqrt{\langle p,p\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}}

are an inner product (conjugate-linear in the first slot) and a norm in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. For
p,q\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
we have the relation
[
22
, Lemma 2 of Sec. 2.2]

|pq|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant\sqrt{2}|p|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}|q|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}.

(2)

Let
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain. We consider functions of the form
u:G\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
u=u_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{3}u_{k}e_{k}
, where
u_{k}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
k=0,1,2,3
. Let
\mathscr{F}(G)
be a linear space of complex-valued functions (e.g.,
C^{k}(G),L_{p}(G),W^{k,p}(G)
). We say that a biquaternionic-valued function
u
belongs to the biquaternionic space
\mathscr{F}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
if
u_{k}\in\mathscr{F}(G)
,
k=0,1,2,3
. For
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}dV_{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, and for
p=\infty
,
\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}:=\operatorname{esssup}\limits_{x\in G}|u(x)|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
. In particular, for the case
p=2
,
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a complex Hilbert space with the inner product
\langle u,v\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}:=\int_{G}\langle u(x),v(x)\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}dV_{x}
.

Since
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
can be regarded as the product space
(L_{p}(G))^{4}
, and for
1\leqslant p<\infty
,L_{p}(G)
is separable
[
4
, Sec. 4.3]
,
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is also separable. In particular,
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a separable complex Hilbert space. By the same argument, since
L_{p}(G)
is reflexive for
1<p<\infty

[
4
, Th. 4.10]
,
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is also reflexive. The Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, the local spaces
L_{p,loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
W^{k,p}_{loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and
W_{0}^{k,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
are defined in the usual way and equipped with corresponding norms. All these spaces are right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-modules.

The notation
\Omega\Subset G
means that
\Omega
is an open subset of
G
with
\overline{\Omega}\subset G
. We say that
u:G\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
is
b-locally Hölder
in
G
with Hölder exponent
0<\epsilon\leqslant 1
, if
u|_{B}\in C^{0,\epsilon}(\overline{B};\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
for every ball
B\Subset G
, and we denote
u\in C_{bloc}^{0,\epsilon}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

2.2
Monogenic functions and integral operators

The
Moisil-Theodorescu
operator (also called
Cauchy-Riemann
or
Dirac
operator) is defined as

\mathbf{D}:=e_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+e_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+e_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}.

Its action, on the left and on the right, over a function
u\in C^{1}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is given by

\mathbf{D}u=-\operatorname{div}\vec{u}+\operatorname{grad}u_{0}+\operatorname{curl}\vec{u}\quad\mbox{and }\quad u\mathbf{D}=-\operatorname{div}\vec{u}+\operatorname{grad}u_{0}-\operatorname{curl}\vec{u}.

(3)

The following relation holds:

\overline{\mathbf{D}u}=-\overline{u}\mathbf{D},\quad\forall u\in C^{1}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(4)

These relations can be extended for
u\in W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. A function
u\in C^{1}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is called
(left) monogenic
(regular or hyperholomorphic) if
\mathbf{D}u=0
. The set of all monogenic functions on
G
is denoted by
\mathfrak{M}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. From the identity
[
15
, Remark 1.2.6]

\bigtriangleup_{3}u=-\mathbf{D}^{2}u
for
u\in C^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we have that
\mathfrak{M}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset\operatorname{Har}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset C^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. In particular,
\{u_{k}\}_{k=0}^{3}\subset\operatorname{Har}(G)
. We recall the quaternionic Leibniz rule
[
15
, Th. 1.3.2]
:

\mathbf{D}(uv)=(\mathbf{D}u)v+\overline{u}(\mathbf{D}v)-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}u_{k}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{k}},\quad\forall u,v\in C^{1}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(5)

From (
5

\mathfrak{M}(G,\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module. Let
\Gamma=\partial G

G

\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{G}

Cauchy kernel

E(x):=-\frac{\vec{x}}{4\pi|\vec{x}|^{3}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}.

(6)

The
Theodorescu operator
is given by
[

\mathbf{T}_{G}u(x):=-\int_{G}E(y-x)u(y)dV_{y},\quad x\in G.

with
v=\mathbf{D}_{w}u
, point (iii) implies that
u=h+\mathbf{T}_{G}v
, where
h\in\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. From the previous point and Proposition
2
(i),
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Due to point (i), we use the notation
\mathbf{D}u
for the weak
\mathbf{D}
-derivative.

The following lemma is based on ideas from
[
15
, Th. 3.1]
and
[
16
, Th. 3.75 ]
. We include it for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 4

Let
1<p<\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that

\int_{\Gamma}\psi(y)\hat{\nu}(y)\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}u(y)dS_{y}=0,\quad\forall\psi\in W^{1-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(12)

Hence
u=w+g
, where
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
g\in W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\cap\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.
Set
\varphi=\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}u
. Consider the singular Cauchy operator

\mathbf{S}_{\Gamma}\varphi(x)=2\operatorname{P.V.}\int_{\Gamma}E(y-x)\hat{\nu}(y)\varphi(y)dS_{y}
,
x\in\Gamma
. It is known that
\mathbf{S}_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{B}\left(W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\right)
(see
[
32
, p. 421]
), and that the Plemelj-Sokhotski formulas are valid:

\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma^{\pm}}\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\left(\pm\varphi+\mathbf{S}_{\Gamma}\varphi\right),

(13)

where
\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma^{\pm}}\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}
denotes the trace of
\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}
from
G
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{G}
, respectively (see
[
15
, Remark 2.5.11]
). Let
\{q_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{G}
be a dense set, and put
E_{n}(x)=E(x-q_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence
\{E_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. By hypothesis,
\int_{\Gamma}\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}E_{n}(y)\hat{\nu}(y)\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}u(y)dS_{y}=0
, but this is precisely
\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}[\psi](q_{n})=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Since
\{q_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus G
, by Proposition
2
(ii),
\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}[\psi](x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{G}
. Hence
\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma^{-}}C_{\Gamma}\varphi=0
and by (
13
),
\varphi=\mathbf{S}_{\Gamma}\varphi
, which implies that
\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma^{+}}C_{\Gamma}\varphi=\varphi
. Taking,
g=\mathbf{C}_{\Gamma}\varphi\in W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\cap\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
w=u-g\in W_{0}^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we obtain the result.

3
Properties of biquaternionic Vekua equation

Given
p\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, denote
M^{p}q:=qp
for all
q\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
A=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4})\in(L_{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{4}

\mathbf{Q}_{A}w=M^{a_{1}}w+M^{a_{2}}\overline{w}+a_{3}w+a_{4}\overline{w}.

(14)

Note that
\mathbf{Q}_{A}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))
. We consider the Vekua equation

\mathbf{D}w-\mathbf{Q}_{A}w=0,\quad\mbox{ in }G.

(15)

Definition 5

The
Vekua-Bergman space
associated with Eq. (
15
), is the class of all weak solutions
W\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
of (

), denoted by

.

In the case
a_{j}\equiv 0
,
j=1,2,3,4
,
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. We introduce the unbounded operator
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}:\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, together with the operator

\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w:=w-\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{Q}_{A}w\quad\mbox{for }w\in L_{p}(G).

(16)

By Proposition
2
(i),
\mathbf{S}_{A}\in\mathcal{B}(L_{p}(G))
. When
\frac{3}{2}<p<3
, note that
p^{*}=\frac{3p}{p-3}>3
. Thus, taking
3<q<p^{*}
, we have the following compositions:
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\overset{\mathbf{T}_{G}}{\rightarrow}W^{1,p}(G)\hookrightarrow L_{q}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\hookrightarrow L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. By Remark
1
,
\mathbf{T}_{G}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))
. Hence,
\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}
is a Fredholm operator with index

[
31
, Th. 2.22]
.

Proposition 6

The following statements hold.

(i)

\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}(\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))\subset\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and

\mathbf{D}\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w=(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A})w,\quad\forall w\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(17)

(ii)

If
w\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a weak solution of the non-homogeneous equation
(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A})w=v
, with
v\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, then
w\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. In particular,
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset W^{1,p}_{loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.
The contention
\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}(\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))\subset\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
follows from Remark
3
(iii), and equality (
17
) follows from Proposition
2
(i). For the statement (ii), let
h=\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w
. By (
17
),
(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A})w=v
iff
\mathbf{D}h=v
. Remark
3
(v) implies
h=g+\mathbf{T}_{G}v
with
g\in\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, from which we obtain
w=g+\mathbf{T}_{G}[\mathbf{Q}_{A}w+v]
. Since
\mathbf{Q}_{A}w+v\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
g\in\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, by Proposition
2
(i) and Remark

(iv), we conclude that
w\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Corollary 7

w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
iff
\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w\in\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proposition 8

The operator
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}:\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is closed.

Proof.
The case when
A\equiv(0,0,0,0)
is given by Remark
3
(ii). For the general case, let
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
w,v\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that
w_{n}\rightarrow w
and
(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A})w_{n}\rightarrow v
in
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Taking
h_{n}=\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w_{n},h=\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}w\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the continuity of
\mathbf{S}_{G}^{A}
and relation (
17
) imply that
h_{n}\rightarrow h
and
\mathbf{D}h_{n}\rightarrow v
in
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Since
\mathfrak{D}
is closed in
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
,
h\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\mathbf{D}h=v
. By the relations
w=h+\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{Q}_{A}w\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and (

), we obtain that
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}
is closed.

Theorem 9

The Vekua-Bergman space
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a separable and reflexive Banach space. In particular,
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{B})
is a separable Hilbert space.

Proof.
The space
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is closed in
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
(and consequently, it is a Banach space) since it is the null space of the closed operator
\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}
. Since for
1<p<\infty
, the space
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is separable and reflexive, hence
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is also separable and reflexive (see
[
4
]
, Propositions 3.21 and 3.25).

4
Regularity of the solutions

In this section, we focus on analyzing the regularity of the solutions of

(\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A})w=v,\quad\mbox{ with }v\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}),\;\frac{3}{2}<p<\infty.

(18)

Proposition 10

Let

be a solution of (
18

(which does not depend on

(19)

Proof.
Let
U
another open set with

, and
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(G)
be a scalar cut-off function satisfying:
\eta\equiv 1
in
D

\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset U
. By Proposition
6
,
u=w\eta\in W_{0}^{1,p}(G)
. Thus, by formulas
10
and
5
, the following relation holds a.e. in
G

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{D}(w\eta)=\mathbf{T}_{D}\left[(\mathbf{D}u)\eta+\overline{u}\mathbf{D}\eta-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}u\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}_{D}\left[(\mathbf{Q}_{A}w+v)\eta+\overline{u}\mathbf{D}\eta-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}u\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_{k}}\right]

In particular, for a.e.
x\in D
,
u=w
. Taking
M_{G}=\|\mathbf{T}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}),W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle\|w\|_{W^{1,p}(D;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}\leqslant

\displaystyle M_{G}\Big(\left(\|\mathbf{Q}_{A}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))}\|w\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\|v\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}\right)\|\eta\|_{L_{\infty}(G)}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2}\|w\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}\|\mathbf{D}\eta\|_{L_{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+2\|w\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}\|\eta\|_{W^{1,\infty}(G)}\Big)

Proposition 11

Let

Proof.
Let
B\Subset G

B^{\prime}
with

(ii),

Since

(i)

Since

Theorem 14

Suppose
\frac{3}{2}<p<\infty

, there exists

\max_{x\in K}|w(x)|_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}\leqslant C_{K}\|w\|_{L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})},\quad\forall w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

, with

B^{\prime}

1

12
, there exists
r>3

5

In this section, we focus on the case

where

Remark 15

For

\displaystyle w(x)=

where the last equality follows from (
23
). Following
[

36

for the space

(25)

Remark 17

(i)

\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(\cdot,t;a)\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
for all
t\in G
,
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

(ii)

The following reproducing property holds

Since

is separable, it admits a countable orthonormal basis
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

[
9
, Ch. I, Prop. 4.16]
. Since
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(\cdot,t;a)\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
for all
x\in G
,
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, it possesses a Fourier series in terms of
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, and the form of its Fourier coefficients can be obtained in terms of the coefficient
a
.

Proposition 18

For every
t\in G
,
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, the kernel
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)
admits the Fourier series expansion

\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle\varphi_{n}(t),a\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi_{n}(x).

(27)

The series converges in the variable
x
in the
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
-norm and uniformly on compact subsets of
G
.

Proof.
The Fourier series of
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)
is given by
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle\varphi_{n},\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(\cdot,t;a)\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}\varphi_{n}(x)
and converges in
x
with respect to the
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
-norm. The Fourier coefficients have the form

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{n},\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(\cdot,t;a)\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\varphi_{n},\sum_{k=0}^{3}K_{t}^{k}a_{k}\right\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\langle\varphi_{n},K_{t}^{k}\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}a_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\varphi_{n,k}^{*}(t)a_{k}=\langle\varphi_{n}(t),a\rangle_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}},

from which we obtain (
27
). The uniform convergence on compact subsets of
G
is due to Theorem
14
.

Since
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a closed subspace of
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, there exists the bounded orthogonal projection of
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
onto
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})

[
9
, Ch. I, Th. 2.7]
, denoted as
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}
. We refer to it as the
Vekua-Bergman projection
. Similar to the case of the bicomplex Vekua equation
[
36
, Prop. 26]
, the Vekua-Bergman projection can be expressed in terms of the kernel
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)
.

Proposition 19

The Vekua-Bergman projection can be written as

\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}u(x)=\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;u(t))dV_{t},\qquad\forall u\in L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(28)

Proof.
Let
u\in L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. A direct computation shows that

\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;u(t))dV_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\langle K_{x}^{k},u\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k}.

Since
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}u\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, using (
22
) and the fact that an orthogonal projection is a self-adjoin operator
[
9
, Prop. 3.3]
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}u(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\langle K_{x}^{k},\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}u\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\langle\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}K_{x}^{k},u\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\langle K_{x}^{k},u\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k}=\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;u(t))dV_{t}.

The space
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
can be treated as a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-Hilbert space with the
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-inner product

\langle\langle u|v\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\int_{G}u^{\dagger}v,\quad u,v\in L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

(29)

The product (
29
) is right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-linear in the second slot and satisfies
\langle\langle u|v\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\langle\langle v|u\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{\dagger}
. Hence
\langle\langle ua|v\rangle\rangle=a^{\dagger}\langle\langle u|v\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
for all
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that
\langle u,v\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\operatorname{Sc}\langle\langle u|v\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
.

The Bergman space
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is not always a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module. For example,
\mathfrak{V}_{(0,0,a_{3},0)}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module, but even for
A=(\vec{a},0,0,0)
with
\vec{a}
a nonzero constant vector,
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is not a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module.

Theorem 20

If
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module, then there exists a kernel
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)
with
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(\cdot,t)\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
for all
t\in G
, satisfying

w(x)=\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)w(t)dt,\quad\forall w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}),\;x\in G.

(30)

Conversely, if such a kernel exists, then
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module.

Proof.
Fix
x\in G
and let
w\in\mathfrak{V}^{2}_{A}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
with component functions
\{w_{k}\}_{k=0}^{3}\subset L_{2}(G)
. Note that
\operatorname{Sc}(w(x)e_{k})=-w_{k}(x)
, hence

w(x)=w_{0}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{3}\operatorname{Sc}(w(x)e_{k})e_{k}.

Let
K_{x}^{0}(t)
be the kernel satisfying
w_{0}(x)=\langle K_{x}^{0},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
. Using the equality
\overline{\langle\langle u|v\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}}=\langle\langle v|u\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle w(x)=

\displaystyle\langle K_{x}^{0},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}\langle K_{x}^{0},we_{k}\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\overline{\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\textasciiacute\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|we_{k}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\overline{\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|we_{k}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}}\right)e_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\textasciiacute\sum_{k=1}^{3}\left(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|we_{k}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\langle\langle we_{k}|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}\right)e_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}e_{k}\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}e_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}+\frac{1}{2}\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}e_{k}\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}e_{k}.

A direct computation shows that
\sum_{k=1}^{3}e_{k}ae_{k}=-4a_{0}+a
for every
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Consequently,
a+\sum_{k=1}^{3}e_{k}ae_{k}=-2\overline{a}
. Thus,

\displaystyle w(x)=

\displaystyle 2\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}-\overline{\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}-\langle\langle w|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}.

Note that
K_{x}^{0}(t)=\langle\langle K_{t}^{0}|K_{x}^{0}\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=(K_{t}^{0}(x))^{\dagger}
. Thus,
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)=(K_{x}^{0}(t))^{\dagger}=K_{t}^{0}(x)
satisfies (
30
).

Reciprocally, suppose that
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)
satisfies (
30
). Let
K_{x}^{k}(t)
be such that
w_{k}(x)=\langle K_{x}^{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
,
k=0,1,2,3
. Note that
\langle(\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,\cdot))^{\dagger},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=w_{0}(x)=\langle K_{x}^{0},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
for all
w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and hence,
\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)=(K_{x}^{0}(t))^{\dagger}
. On the other hand,

\langle K_{x}^{0},we_{k}\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\operatorname{Sc}(\langle\langle K_{x}^{0}|w\rangle\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}e_{k})=-w_{k}(x)=-\langle K_{x}^{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}.

A direc computation shows that
\langle K_{x}^{0},we_{k}\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=-\langle K_{x}^{0}e_{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
. Hence,
\langle K_{x}^{0}e_{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}=\langle K_{x}^{k},w\rangle_{L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}
for all
w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, from which we conclude that
K_{x}^{0}(t)e_{k}=K_{x}^{k}(t)
for all
x,t\in G
,
k=0,1,2,3
. Consequently,

\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}K_{t}^{k}(x)a_{k}=K_{t}^{0}(x)a_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{3}K_{t}^{0}(x)e_{k}a_{k}=K_{t}^{0}(x)a.

Repeating the same procedure as in the first part, we find
K_{x}^{0}(t)=(K_{t}^{0}(x))^{\dagger}
. Thus, from (
25
) we obtain that

\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t;a)=\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)a.

In consequence, the orthogonal projection
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}
has the form

\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}u(x)=\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)u(t)dV_{t},\quad\forall u\in L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}).

Finally, taking
w\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
a\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
, we get

w(x)a=(\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}[w](x))a=\int_{G}\mathscr{K}_{G}^{A}(x,t)w(t)adV_{t}=\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}[Wa](x).

Hence,
wa\in\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{V}_{A}^{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module.

Consequently, Bergman spaces associated with operators of the type
\mathbf{D}\pm M^{a_{1}(x)}
,
\mathbf{D}\pm M^{a_{2}(x)}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\mathbf{D}\pm a_{4}(x)\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
, generally do not admit a kernel of type (
30
).

6
Annihilators and orthogonal decompositions

Let
X
be a Banach space and
X^{\star}
its dual. The duality pairing between
X
and
X^{\star}
is usually denoted by
(\varphi|x)_{X}:=\varphi(x)
,
x\in X,\varphi\in X^{\star}
. Given a densely defined operator
\mathbf{A}:\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A})\subset X\rightarrow X
, its transpose is the operator
\mathbf{A}^{T}:\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A})\subset X^{\star}\rightarrow X^{\star}
, whose domain consists of the functionals
\varphi\in X^{\star}
for which there exists
\psi\in X^{\star}
satisfying
(\varphi|\mathbf{A}x)_{X}=(\psi|x)_{X}
for all
x\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A})

[
4
, Sec. 2.6]
. Such
\psi
is unique, and we define
\mathbf{A}^{T}\varphi=\psi
. The operator
\mathbf{A}^{T}
is closed in
X^{\star}

[
4
, Prop. 2.17]
.

Remark 21

Let
\mathbf{A}:\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A})\subset X\rightarrow X
be a densely defined operator, and
\mathbf{S}\in\mathcal{B}(X)
. The following relations hold:

\mathbf{S}^{T}\mathbf{A}^{T}\subset(\mathbf{AS})^{T}\quad\mbox{and }(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{S})^{T}=\mathbf{A}^{T}+\mathbf{S}^{T}.

The proof is a straightforward.

Using the Riesz-Fisher representation theorem, it is a straightforward to show that for
1<p<\infty
and every
\Phi\in\left(L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\right)^{\star}
, there exists a unique (a.e. in
G
)
v\in L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\Phi(u)=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{v}u
for all
u\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. The duality pairing between
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is denoted by
(v|u)_{G}:=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{v}u
. Consequently, the transpose of a densely defined operator
\mathbf{A}:\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A})\subset L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
can be realized as an operator
\mathbf{A}^{T}:\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A}^{T})\subset L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
whose domain
\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{A}^{T})
consists of the elements
v\in L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
for which there exists
w\in L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
satisfying
(v|\mathbf{A}u)_{G}=(w|u)_{G}
for all
u\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbf{A})
, and its actions is given by
\mathbf{A}^{T}v=w
. Such operator is closed in
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proposition 22

Let
1<p<\infty
. The following statements hold.

(i)

\mathbf{T}_{G}^{T}=\mathbf{T}_{G}
(as an operator acting in
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
).

(ii)

If
A\in(L_{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{4}
, then
\mathbf{Q}_{A}^{T}=\mathbf{Q}_{A^{\star}}
, where
A^{\star}:=(\overline{a_{1}},a_{4},\overline{a_{3}},a_{2})
.

Proof.

(i)

Take
\varphi,\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Consider

\displaystyle(\psi|\mathbf{T}_{G}\varphi)_{G}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\overline{\psi(x)}\mathbf{T}_{G}\varphi(x)dV_{x}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{G}\overline{\psi(x)}\left[\int_{G}\frac{\vec{y}-\vec{x}}{|\vec{y}-\vec{x}|^{3}}\varphi(y)dV_{y}\right]dV_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{G}\left[\int_{G}\frac{\overline{\psi(x)}(\vec{y}-\vec{x})}{|\vec{y}-\vec{x}|^{3}}dV_{x}\right]\varphi(y)dV_{y}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{G}\overline{\left[\int_{G}\frac{(\vec{x}-\vec{y})\psi(x)}{|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{3}}dV_{x}\right]}\varphi(y)dV_{y}

From this,
(\psi|\mathbf{T}_{G}\varphi)_{G}=(\mathbf{T}_{G}\psi|\varphi)_{G}
. By the continuity of
\mathbf{T}_{G}
and the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
in
L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, we obtain
\mathbf{T}_{G}^{T}=\mathbf{T}_{G}:L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

(ii)

Let
a\in L_{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
u\in L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}),v\in L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Using the fact that
\operatorname{Sc}(pq)=\operatorname{Sc}(qp)=\operatorname{Sc}(\overline{pq})
, we obtain

\displaystyle(v|ua)_{G}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{v}(ua)=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}a(\overline{v}u)=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{(v\overline{a})}u=(v\overline{a}|v)_{G}

\displaystyle(v|au)_{G}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{v}(au)=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{(\overline{a}v)}u=(\overline{a}v|u)_{G},

\displaystyle(v|\overline{u})_{G}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{v}\;\overline{u}=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}\overline{uv}=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}uv=\operatorname{Sc}\int_{G}vu=(\overline{v}|u)_{G}.

Consequently,
(M^{a})^{T}=M^{\overline{a}}
,
(a\mathbf{I})^{T}=\overline{a}\mathbf{I}
, and
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}^{T}=\mathbf{C}
. In the same way,
(M^{a}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{T}v=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}^{T}(M^{a})^{T}v=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}(v\overline{a})=a\overline{v}
and
(a\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{T}v=\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}(\overline{a}v)=\overline{v}a
. Thus,
(M^{a}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{T}=a\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
and
(a\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{T}=M^{a}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}
. From these relations, we conclude that

\mathbf{Q}_{A}^{T}=(M^{a_{1}}+M^{a_{2}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}+a_{3}\mathbf{I}+a_{4}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}})^{T}=M^{\overline{a_{1}}}+a_{2}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}+\overline{a_{3}}\mathbf{I}+M^{a_{4}}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}}=\mathbf{Q}_{A^{\star}},

where
A^{\star}=(\overline{a_{1}},a_{4},\overline{a_{3}},a_{2})
.

Proposition 23

Let
1<p<\infty
. The transpose of the Moisil-Theodorescu operator
\mathbf{D}:\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is given by
\mathbf{D}:W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\rightarrow L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.
By the definition of weak
\mathbf{D}
-derivative, it is clear that
W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\subset\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{D}^{T})
and
\mathbf{D}^{T}v=\mathbf{D}v
for

. On the other hand, suppose
v\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathbf{D}^{T})
and
u\in\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Using the relations
\operatorname{Sc}(pe_{k})=-p_{k}
,

\mathfrak{D}_{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
is a right
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\overline{v}\mathbf{D}u

\displaystyle=(v|\mathbf{D}u)_{G}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}(v|(\mathbf{D}u)e_{k})_{G}e_{k}=(\mathbf{D}^{T}v|u)_{G}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}(v|\mathbf{D}(ue_{k}))_{G}e_{k}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{D}^{T}v|u)_{G}-\sum_{k=1}^{3}(\mathbf{D}^{T}v|ue_{k})_{G}e_{k}=\int_{G}\overline{\mathbf{D}^{T}v}u.

In particular, the equality is valid for all
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, and hence
v\in\mathfrak{D}_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
with
\mathbf{D}^{T}v=\mathbf{D}v
. Consequently,
v=\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{D}^{T}v+g
, with
g\in\mathfrak{M}^{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. By Remarks
3
(iii) and
21
, and Proposition
22
(i),
\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{D}^{T}\subset(\mathbf{D}\mathbf{T}_{G})^{T}=\mathbf{I}
. Thus,

and

. Hence
v=\mathbf{T}_{G}\mathbf{D}^{T}v
and by Proposition
2
(i),
v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Take
\psi\in W^{1-\frac{1}{p},p}(\Gamma;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
and
\Psi\in W^{1,p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})

\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}\Psi=\psi

\displaystyle\int_{G}\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}\overline{v}\widehat{\nu}\psi dS=\int_{G}\left(-\overline{\mathbf{D}v}\Psi+\overline{v}\mathbf{D}\Psi\right)=0

Taking the conjugate, we obtain
\int_{G}\overline{\psi}\widehat{\nu}\operatorname{tr}_{\Gamma}vdS=0

. According to Lemma
4

\displaystyle\int_{G}\overline{(v_{0}+g)}\mathbf{D}u=\int_{G}\overline{(\mathbf{D}v_{0}+\mathbf{D}g)}u=\int_{G}\overline{\mathbf{D}v_{0}}u=\int_{G}\overline{v_{0}}\mathbf{D}u,

from which we concluded that

Let

M^{a}:=\{\varphi\in X^{\star}|\forall x\in M\;(\varphi|x)_{X}=0\}\mbox{ and }N_{a}:=\{x\in X|\forall\varphi\in N\;(\varphi|x)_{X}=0\},

Lemma 25

Let
\mathbf{A}:\operatorname{dom}(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
be a densely defined operator. If
X
is reflexive, then
(\ker\mathbf{A})^{a}
is the closure of the image of
\mathbf{A}^{T}
in the strong topology.

Proof.
Let
R
be the image of
\mathbf{A}^{T}
. By
[
4
, Cor. 2.18]
,
\ker\mathbf{A}=R_{a}
. Thus,
(\ker\mathbf{A})^{a}=(R_{a})^{a}
. Let us show that
(R_{a})^{a}=(R^{a})_{a}
. Since
X
is reflexive, the canonical embedding
J:X\rightarrow X^{\star\star}
, given by
(Jx|\varphi)_{X^{\star}}=(\varphi|x)_{X}=0
for all
\varphi\in X^{\star}
, is an isometric isomorphism. Note that
x\in R_{a}
iff
Jx\in R^{a}
. Indeed, this follows from the relation
(Jx|\varphi)_{X^{\star}}=(\varphi|x)_{X}
for all
\varphi\in R
. Since
J
is an isomorphism,
R^{a}=J(R_{a})
.
Thus,

\varphi\in(R_{a})^{a}\Leftrightarrow(\varphi|x)_{X}=0\mbox{ for all }x\in R_{a}\Leftrightarrow(\Phi|\varphi)_{X^{\star}}=0\mbox{ for all }\Phi\in R^{a}\Leftrightarrow\varphi\in(R^{a})_{a}.

By
[
4
, Prop. 1.9]
,
(R^{a})_{a}=\overline{R}^{X^{\star}}
(the closure in the strong topology), and we conclude that
(\ker\mathbf{A})^{a}=\overline{R}^{X^{\star}}
, as desired.

As a consequence, we obtain the following result.

Example 27

Let
f\in W^{2,\infty}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
be a scalar function such that
\frac{1}{f}\in L_{\infty}(G)
, and consider
A=\left(-\frac{\nabla f}{f},0,0,0\right)
. Denote
\mathbf{D}_{f}:=\mathbf{D}-\mathbf{Q}_{A}=\mathbf{D}+M^{\frac{\nabla f}{f}}
. This is one of the so-called main Vekua operators
[
24
, Ch. 16]
. Hence

\mathbf{D}_{f}^{T}=\mathbf{D}+M^{\overline{\frac{\nabla f}{f}}}=\mathbf{D}+M^{-\frac{\nabla f}{f}}=\mathbf{D}+M^{f\nabla\left(\frac{1}{f}\right)}=\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}.

Note that
\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}=\mathbf{D}-M^{\frac{\nabla f}{f}}
. Thus,
(\mathfrak{V}^{p}_{\left(-\frac{\nabla f}{f},0,0,0\right)}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{a}=\overline{(\mathbf{D}-M^{\frac{\nabla f}{f}})W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})}^{L_{p^{\prime}}}
. Suppose that

satisfies
\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}w\in\mathfrak{D}_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}w=

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}\left(\mathbf{D}w-w\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)+\left(\mathbf{D}w-w\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)\frac{\nabla f}{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigtriangleup w-\mathbf{D}\left(w\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)+(\mathbf{D}w)\frac{\nabla f}{f}-w\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigtriangleup w-(\mathbf{D}w)\frac{\nabla f}{f}-\overline{w}\mathbf{D}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)+2\sum_{j=1}^{3}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)+(\mathbf{D}w)\frac{\nabla f}{f}-w\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigtriangleup w-\overline{w}\left(-\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}-\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}\right)+2\sum_{j=1}^{3}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)-w\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bigtriangleup w+\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}\overline{w}-2\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}\vec{w}+2\sum_{j=1}^{3}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right).

Let
q_{f}=\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}
and note that
q_{\frac{1}{f}}=f\bigtriangleup\left(\frac{1}{f}\right)=2\frac{\nabla f\cdot\nabla f}{f^{2}}-\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}=-2\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)^{2}-\frac{\bigtriangleup f}{f}
. The potential
q_{\frac{1}{f}}
is the Darboux transform of
q_{f}
. Let
R_{j,k}=\frac{1}{f}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{j}}-\frac{1}{f^{2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}
for
j,k=1,2,3
. Observe that the matrix
R=(R_{j,k})_{j,k=1}^{3}
is symmetric and
\sum_{j=1}^{3}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left(\frac{\nabla f}{f}\right)=\sum_{k,j=1}^{3}R_{k,j}w_{j}e_{k}=R\vec{w}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sc}\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}w=

\displaystyle(-\bigtriangleup+q_{f})\operatorname{Sc}w,

(33)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vec}\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}w=

\displaystyle(-\bigtriangleup+q_{\frac{1}{f}})\vec{w}+2R\vec{w}.

(34)

Equation (
33
) is known as a factorization of the Schrödinger operator, see
[
20
]
or
[
24
, Th. 154]
. Consequently,
w=(\mathbf{D}-M^{\frac{\nabla f}{f}})u
with
u\in W_{0}^{1,p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
belongs to
\mathfrak{V}^{p^{\prime}}_{\left(-\frac{\nabla f}{f},0,0,0\right)}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
if
u
is a solution of system (
33
)-(
34
).

Remark 28

For fixed
x,y\in G
, a direct computation shows the inequality

\max\left\{\int_{G}|E(y-x)|dV_{y},\int_{G}|E(y-x)|dV_{x}\right\}\leqslant\operatorname{diam}(G).

By
[

, Th. 6.18]
and (

By the density of

7
Conclusions

The main properties of the Bergman space of weak
L_{p}
-solutions of a class of quaternionic Vekua equations have been established. The regularity of the solutions was analyzed, demonstrating their b-locally Hölder continuity. Furthermore, it was shown that the evaluation map is bounded with respect to the
L_{p}
-norm at each point in the domain. For the case
p=2
, the existence of a reproducing kernel and an explicit formula for the orthogonal projection were demonstrated. Additionally, the explicit form of the annihilator of the Bergman space for
1<p<\infty
was computed, yielding an orthogonal decomposition for
L_{2}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

For
p\neq 2
, an interesting question is to compute the dual
(V_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{\star}
. A conjecture could be that
(V_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{\star}=V_{A}^{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
, which is equivalent to showing that
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})=V_{A}^{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})\oplus(V_{A}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}))^{a}
, or that the Bergman projection
\mathbf{P}_{G}^{A}
can be extended continuously to
L_{p^{\prime}}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
. However, this is not a trivial question, even for the case of monogenic functions
\mathfrak{M}^{p}(G;\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}})
(see
[
7
,
18
]
for the case of the classical analytic and harmonic Bergman space).

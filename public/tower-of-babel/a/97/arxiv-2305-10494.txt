More on Torus Wormholes in 3d Gravity
Cynthia Yan

Abstract




We study further the duality between semiclassical AdS
3
and formal CFT
2
ensembles. First, we study torus wormholes (Maldacena-Maoz wormholes with two torus boundaries) with one insertion or two insertions on each boundary and find that they give non-decaying contribution to the product of two torus one-point or two-point functions at late-time. Second, we study the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
quotients of a torus wormhole such that the outcome has one boundary. We identify quotients that give non-decaying contributions to the torus two-point function at late-time.

We comment on reflection (R) or time-reversal (T) symmetry v.s. the combination RT that is a symmetry of any relativistic field theory. RT symmetry itself implies that to the extent that a relativistic quantum field theory exhibits random matrix statistics it should be of the GOE type for bosonic states and of the GSE type for fermionic states. We discuss related implications of these symmetries for wormholes.

2.2
Lorentzian torus limit

In this section, we examine the late-time behavior of the product of two torus one-point functions. In 2d, the simplest quantity to probe the late-time behavior is the spectral form factor
\braket{Z(\beta+it)Z(\beta-it)}

[
22
]
. This is two thermal partition functions, one with
\beta
analytically continued to
\beta+it
and the other to
\beta-it
. We can do a similar analytical continuation in 3d.

Writing the torus one-point function in operator form, we get

\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}=\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H+isP})=\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)})

(2.20)

where
\tau=i\beta+s
and
\bar{\tau}=-i\beta+s
are complex conjugates since the torus
T^{2}
is a Euclidean torus. Also by definition
H=h+\bar{h}-c/12
and
P=h-\bar{h}
.

The above is one boundary of the Maldacena-Maoz wormhole. The other side
T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})
is another torus that look like the reflection of
T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})
. Thus
\tau^{\prime}=i\beta-s
and
\bar{\tau}^{\prime}=-i\beta-s
. Then the torus one-point function is

\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}=\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H-isP})

(2.21)

For simplicity, we take
s=0
so the product of the two torus one-point function becomes

\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}=\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H})\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H})

(2.22)

Now we can do analytical continuation similar to
\braket{Z(\beta+it)Z(\beta-it)}

[
22
]
, and make the two tori into Lorentzian tori by taking
\beta\mapsto\beta+it
for
T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})
while taking
\beta\mapsto\beta-it
for
T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})
. But this is just saying now

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=i\beta-t\quad\quad\tau^{\prime}=i\beta+t

(2.23)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}

\displaystyle=-i\beta+t\quad\quad\bar{\tau}^{\prime}=-i\beta-t

(2.24)

so we are calculating the quantity

\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}=\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(i\beta-t,-i\beta+t)}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(i\beta+t,-i\beta-t)}=\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-(\beta+it)H})\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-(\beta-it)H})

(2.25)

(
2.7
) then becomes

\overline{\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}=\left(1+(-1)^{s_{1}}\right)\braket{O_{1}}^{L}_{T^{2}(i\beta-t,-i\beta-t)}\braket{O_{1}}^{L}_{T^{2}(i\beta+t,-i\beta+t)}

(2.26)

But now we can see that the Liouville one-point functions on the right-hand-side are on Euclidean tori instead of Lorentzian tori. Another way to present the answer is

\overline{\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-(\beta+it)H})\mathrm{tr}(O_{1}e^{-(\beta-it)H})}=\left(1+(-1)^{s_{1}}\right)\mathrm{tr}_{L}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H-itP})\mathrm{tr}_{L}(O_{1}e^{-\beta H+itP})

(2.27)

On the LHS we start with an average over the formal ensemble of CFTs of a quantity similar to the spectral form factor, but with an operator inserted in each factor. The RHS is the answer for this ensemble average, or equivalently, it is the wormhole contribution in 3d gravity – either way it boils down to a computation in Liouville theory involving an operator insertion with the same dimension. The key point is that on the RHS, the large time parameter
t
multiplies the momentum operator
P
, not the Hamiltonian
H
. Now,
P
is quantized so the RHS is periodic in time with a short period, and in particular does not decay for large
t
.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\Delta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}}

(A.35)

This now matches equation (
A.27
) so we have the discrepancy fixed.

A.3.1
crosscap

In this section we give a way of calculating the disk with a crosscap contribution to the 2-point correlator by first doubling the crosscap configuration to give a cylinder and then take the square root. For a review of JT gravity framework and 2d crosscap see Appendix
.

We start from the contribution to the averaged product of two 2pt functions. This comes from the cylinder

\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}=\int dEdE^{\prime}\underbrace{\frac{\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2E})}{2\pi^{2}}}_{\rho_{0}(E)}\underbrace{\frac{\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2E^{\prime}})}{2\pi^{2}}}_{\rho_{0}(E^{\prime})}e^{-\beta(E+E^{\prime})}|V_{E,E^{\prime}}|^{4}

(A.41)

where

|V_{E,E^{\prime}}|^{2}=\frac{\left|\Gamma\left(\Delta+i(\sqrt{2E}+\sqrt{2E^{\prime}})\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta+i(\sqrt{2E}-\sqrt{2E^{\prime}})\right)\right|^{2}}{2^{2\Delta-1}\Gamma(2\Delta)}

(A.42)

Let
s=\sqrt{2E}
and
s^{\prime}=\sqrt{2E^{\prime}}
we can rewrite the above equation as

\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}=\int dsds^{\prime}ss^{\prime}\frac{\sinh(2\pi s)}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{\sinh(2\pi s^{\prime})}{2\pi^{2}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(s^{2}+s^{\prime 2})}\left(\frac{\prod_{\pm_{1,2}}\Gamma\left(\Delta\pm_{1}is\pm_{2}is^{\prime}\right)}{2^{2\Delta-1}\Gamma(2\Delta)}\right)^{2}

(A.43)

Now make a change of variables

s_{1}+s_{2}=s_{+}\quad\quad s_{1}-s_{2}=s_{-}

(A.44)

Then the above expression becomes

\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}\propto\\
\int ds_{+}ds_{-}(s_{+}^{2}-s_{-}^{2})(e^{2\pi s_{+}}+e^{-2\pi s_{+}}-e^{2\pi s_{-}}-e^{-2\pi s_{-}})e^{-\frac{\beta}{4}(s_{+}^{2}+s_{-}^{2})}\left(\frac{\prod_{\pm_{1,2}}\Gamma\left(\Delta\pm_{1}is_{+}\right)\Gamma\left(\Delta\pm_{2}is_{-}\right)}{2^{2\Delta-1}\Gamma(2\Delta)}\right)^{2}

(A.45)

Using the Stirling approximation

\prod_{\pm}\Gamma(\Delta\pm is_{+})\approx e^{-\pi s_{+}}s_{+}^{2\Delta-1}

(A.46)

So we pick the term
s_{+}^{2}
and
e^{2\pi s_{+}}
in the expansion

\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}\propto\int ds_{+}ds_{-}s_{+}^{4\Delta}e^{-\frac{\beta}{4}(s_{+}^{2}+s_{-}^{2})}\left(\frac{\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\Delta\pm is_{-}\right)}{2^{2\Delta-1}\Gamma(2\Delta)}\right)^{2}

(A.47)

Saddle point of
s_{-}
is at
s_{-}=0
, so (
A.41
) can be approximated by

\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}\approx\int dE\underbrace{\frac{\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2E})}{2\pi^{2}}}_{\rho_{0}(E)}\underbrace{\frac{\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2E})}{2\pi^{2}}}_{\rho_{0}(E)}e^{-\beta 2E}|V_{E,E}|^{4}

(A.48)

We should note that here we only care about the saddle point (i.e. only the integrand of the above integral). Thus we get

\sqrt{\overline{\braket{V(\tau)V(0)}\braket{V(\beta-\tau)V(0)}}}\approx\int dE\underbrace{\frac{\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2E})}{2\pi^{2}}}_{\rho_{0}(E)}e^{-\beta E}|V_{E,E}|^{2}

(A.49)

In

20
]
, crosscap contribution to the 2-point function was calculated directly using propagator

\displaystyle\braket{V(t=-i\tau)V(0)}_{\text{cc},0}

\displaystyle=\int e^{\ell}d\ell\,P_{\text{Disk}}(\tau,\beta-\tau,\ell,\ell)e^{-\Delta\ell}

\displaystyle=\includegraphics[valign,width]{figures/2dcc}

Therefore (

(A.54)

Appendix B
Calculation details

B.1
Calculating product of two torus one-point functions

We start from the torus partition function

Z(\tau,\bar{\tau})=(q\bar{q})^{-c/24}\mathrm{Tr}(q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}})

(B.1)

where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. Based on this the one-point function can be written as

\displaystyle\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}

\displaystyle=(q\bar{q})^{-c/24}\mathrm{Tr}(q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}}O_{1})

(B.2)

\displaystyle=(q\bar{q})^{-c/24}\sum_{h}\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}}

(B.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{p}}}\frac{\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}}{\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}

(B.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p}c_{1pp}\sum_{h\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{p}}}\frac{\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}}{\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}

(B.5)

where we decompose a sum over all operators into a sum over primaries and a sum over all descendents of a primary. Thus, we get an expression for the conformal block

\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau})=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{p}}}\frac{\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}}{\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}

(B.6)

In particular

\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau}^{\prime})=\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};-\bar{\tau}^{\prime})

(B.7)

since complex conjugation gives a minus sign while
\tau\mapsto-\tau
gives another minus sign, these two minus signs cancel.

Thus the averaged product of two torus one-point functions is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q}\overline{c_{1pp}c_{1qq}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{q};\tau^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau}^{\prime})

(B.8)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{p}\overline{c_{1pp}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau}^{\prime})

(B.9)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{p}\overline{c_{1pp}^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};\bar{\tau})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p};-\bar{\tau}^{\prime})

(B.10)

\displaystyle\approx 2\big|\int dh_{p}\rho_{0}(h_{p})C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{p})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\big|^{2}

(B.11)

\displaystyle\approx 2\braket{O_{1}}^{L}_{T^{2}(\tau,-\tau^{\prime})}\braket{O_{1}}^{L}_{T^{2}(-\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\bar{\tau})}

(B.12)

B.2
Calculating product of two torus two-point functions

The two-point function can be written as

\displaystyle\braket{O_{1}(v,\bar{v})O_{2}(0)}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}

\displaystyle=(q\bar{q})^{-c/24}\mathrm{Tr}(q^{L_{0}}\bar{q}^{\bar{L}_{0}}O_{1}O_{2})

(B.13)

\displaystyle=(q\bar{q})^{-c/24}\sum_{h,\bar{h},h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}\braket{h,\bar{h}|e^{i(\tau-v)L_{0}}e^{-i(\bar{\tau}-\bar{v})\bar{L}_{0}}O_{2}e^{ivL_{0}}e^{-i\bar{v}\bar{L}_{0}}|h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}\braket{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}

(B.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}}\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{2}|h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}\braket{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}e^{iv(h^{\prime}-h)}e^{-i\bar{v}(\bar{h}-\bar{h}^{\prime})}

(B.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{p},h_{q}}\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{2}|h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}}\braket{h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{h\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{p}},h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{q}}}\frac{\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{2}|h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}}{\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{2}|h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}}}\frac{\braket{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}}{\braket{h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}e^{iv(h^{\prime}-h)}e^{-i\bar{v}(\bar{h}-\bar{h}^{\prime})}

(B.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{p},h_{q}}c_{1pq}c_{2pq}\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau},\bar{v})

(B.17)

Comparing these two results, we get an expression for the conformal block

\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau},\bar{v})\\
=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{p}},h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{h_{q}}}\frac{\braket{h,\bar{h}|O_{2}|h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}}{\braket{h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}|O_{2}|h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}}}\frac{\braket{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}|O_{1}|h,\bar{h}}}{\braket{h_{q},\bar{h}_{q}|O_{1}|h_{p},\bar{h}_{p}}}e^{i\tau(h-c/24)}e^{-i\bar{\tau}(\bar{h}-c/24)}e^{iv(h^{\prime}-h)}e^{-i\bar{v}(\bar{h}-\bar{h}^{\prime})}

(B.18)

In particular we have

\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau^{\prime},v^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime})=\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};-\tau^{\prime},-v^{\prime})\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};-\bar{\tau}^{\prime},-\bar{v}^{\prime})

(B.19)

Thus the averaged product of two torus two-point functions is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\braket{O_{1}(v,\bar{v})O_{2}(0)}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}(v^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime})O_{2}(0)}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q,r,s}\overline{c_{1pq}c_{2pq}c_{1rs}c_{2rs}}\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau},\bar{v})\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{r},h_{s};\tau^{\prime},v^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{r},\bar{h}_{s};\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime})

(B.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{p,q}\overline{|c_{1pq}^{2}|}\overline{|c_{2pq}^{2}|}\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau},\bar{v})\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau^{\prime},v^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime})

(B.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{p,q}\overline{|c_{1pq}^{2}|}\overline{|c_{2pq}^{2}|}\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};\bar{\tau},\bar{v})\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};-\tau^{\prime},-v^{\prime})\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(\bar{h}_{p},\bar{h}_{q};-\bar{\tau}^{\prime},-\bar{v}^{\prime})

(B.22)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2\big|\int dh_{p}dh_{q}\rho_{0}(h_{p})\rho_{0}(h_{q})C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{q})C_{0}(h_{2},h_{p},h_{q})\mathcal{F}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};\tau,v)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{12}^{g=1}(h_{p},h_{q};-\tau^{\prime},-v^{\prime})\big|^{2}

(B.23)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle 2\braket{O_{1}(v,-v^{\prime})O_{2}(0)}^{L}_{T^{2}(\tau,-\tau^{\prime})}\braket{O_{1}(-\bar{v}^{\prime},\bar{v})O_{2}(0)}^{L}_{T^{2}(-\bar{\tau}^{\prime},\bar{\tau})}

(B.24)

B.3
Averaged product of two torus one-point function in
\Delta\rightarrow 0
limit

Let us begin with our formula of the averaged product of two one-point functions

\overline{\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}\approx 2\big|\int dh_{p}\rho_{0}(h_{p})C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{p})\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\big|^{2}

(B.25)

where
C_{0}
is given by

C_{0}(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=\frac{\Gamma_{b}(2Q)}{\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{b}(Q)^{3}}\frac{\Gamma_{b}(\frac{Q}{2}\pm iP_{1}\pm iP_{2}\pm iP_{3})}{\prod_{k=1}^{3}\Gamma_{b}(Q\pm 2iP_{k})}

(B.26)

Now we take the weight of
O_{1}
to zero i.e.
\Delta=2h=2\alpha(Q-\alpha)\rightarrow 0
. Without loss of generality we take
\alpha=\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, so
\Delta=2\epsilon Q
. More specifically
iP_{1}=-\frac{Q}{2}+\epsilon
so

C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{p})=C_{0}(i(\frac{Q}{2}-\epsilon),P,P)=\frac{\Gamma_{b}(\epsilon)^{2}\Gamma_{b}(\pm 2iP)}{\sqrt{2}\Gamma_{b}(Q)\Gamma_{b}(2\epsilon)\Gamma_{b}(Q\pm 2iP)}

(B.27)

Remember the definition of
\Gamma_{b}
in terms of double gamma function

\Gamma_{b}(P)=\frac{\Gamma_{2}(P|b,b^{-1})}{\Gamma_{2}(\frac{Q}{2}|b,b^{-1})}

(B.28)

where

\Gamma_{2}(w|a_{1},a_{2})=\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}w+\lambda_{2}w^{2}}}{w}\prod_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2},(n_{1},n_{2})\neq(0,0)}\frac{e^{\frac{w}{n_{1}a_{1}+n_{2}a_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{w^{2}}{(n_{1}a_{1}+n_{2}a_{2})^{2}}}}{1+\frac{w}{n_{1}a_{1}+n_{2}a_{2}}}

(B.29)

so as we take
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

\Gamma_{2}(\epsilon|b,b^{-1})=\frac{1}{\epsilon}

(B.30)

and

C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{p})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\epsilon\Gamma_{2}(Q|b,b^{-1})}\frac{\Gamma_{b}(\pm 2iP)}{\Gamma_{b}(Q\pm 2iP)}

(B.31)

Now observe that
\Gamma_{b}
satisfies the following relations

\Gamma_{b}(w+b)=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{b^{bw-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(bw)}\Gamma_{b}(w)\quad\quad\Gamma_{b}(w+b^{-1})=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{b^{-b^{-1}w+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(b^{-1}w)}\Gamma_{b}(w)

(B.32)

so correspondingly

\Gamma_{b}(Q+2iP)=\frac{2\pi b^{1+i2(b-b^{-1})P}\Gamma_{b}(2iP)}{\Gamma(1+2ibP)\Gamma(2ib^{-1}P)}\quad\quad\Gamma_{b}(Q-2iP)=\frac{2\pi b^{1-i2(b-b^{-1})P}\Gamma_{b}(-2iP)}{\Gamma(1-2ibP)\Gamma(-2ib^{-1}P)}

(B.33)

so

\frac{\Gamma_{b}(\pm 2iP)}{\Gamma_{b}(Q\pm 2iP)}=\frac{\Gamma(1\pm 2ibP)\Gamma(\pm 2ib^{-1}P)}{(2\pi)^{2}b^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\sinh(2\pi bP)\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}P)}

(B.34)

where we have used

\displaystyle\Gamma(1+z)\Gamma(1-z)

\displaystyle=z\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{z\pi}{\sin\pi z}

(B.35)

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\Gamma(-z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)}{-z}=-\frac{\pi}{z\sin\pi z}

(B.36)

so

\displaystyle C_{0}(h_{1},h_{p},h_{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\epsilon\Gamma_{2}(Q|b,b^{-1})\sinh^{2}(2\pi bP)\sinh^{2}(2\pi b^{-1}P)}

(B.37)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q}{8\Delta\Gamma_{2}(Q|b,b^{-1})\sinh^{2}(2\pi bP)\sinh^{2}(2\pi b^{-1}P)}

(B.38)

From
[
3
]
equation (2.14) we know that

\rho(P,\bar{P})=\rho_{0}(P)\rho_{0}(\bar{P})=|4\sqrt{2}\sinh(2\pi bP)\sinh(2\pi b^{-1}P)|^{2}

(B.39)

so now we can simplify the product of two one-point functions

\overline{\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}\approx\frac{32Q^{2}}{\Delta^{2}\Gamma_{2}(Q|b,b^{-1})^{2}}\left|\int dP\,\mathcal{F}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\right|^{2}

(B.40)

Now we evaluate
\Gamma_{2}(Q|b,b^{-1})
using the formula

\Gamma_{N}(w|a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})=\Gamma_{N-1}(w|a_{1},\ldots,a_{N-1})\Gamma_{N}(w+a_{N}|a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})

(B.41)

For
N=2

\Gamma_{2}(w|a_{1},a_{2})=\Gamma_{1}(w|a_{1})\Gamma_{2}(w+a_{2}|a_{1},a_{2})

(B.42)

Then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(\epsilon+b+\frac{1}{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{2}(\epsilon+b|b,\frac{1}{b})}{\Gamma(\epsilon+b|b)}=\frac{\Gamma_{2}(\epsilon|b,\frac{1}{b})}{\Gamma(b|b)\Gamma(\epsilon|\frac{1}{b})}

(B.43)

Using

\Gamma(w|a)=\frac{a^{a^{-1}w-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(a^{-1}w)

(B.44)

\displaystyle\Gamma(b|b)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi}}\Gamma(1)=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi}}

(B.45)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\epsilon|\frac{1}{b})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{b}{2\pi}}\Gamma(b\epsilon)

(B.46)

Recall that as
w\rightarrow 0

\Gamma(w)=\frac{1}{w}+O(w^{0})\quad\quad\Gamma_{2}(w|b,b^{-1})=\frac{1}{w}+O(w^{0})

(B.47)

so

\Gamma_{2}(\epsilon+b+\frac{1}{b}|b,\frac{1}{b})=\frac{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\frac{b}{2\pi}\frac{1}{b\epsilon}}=2\pi

(B.48)

Therefore,

\overline{\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}\approx\frac{8Q^{2}}{\Delta^{2}\pi^{2}}\left|\int dP\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{1}}^{g=1}(h_{p};\tau)\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbb{1}}^{g=1}(h_{p};-\tau^{\prime})\right|^{2}

(B.49)

B.4
Saddle-point approximation in 3d

Saddle-point approximation was proven to work in an analogous situation in 2d (for details see Appendix
A.3
). In 2d, we calculate the averaged product of two two-point functions and take the saddle-point approximation. We compare this result with contribution to two-point function from disk with crosscap (see Appendix
A.3.1
) and results are the same. We then assume here that saddle-point approximation can be generalized to 3d even though we do not have a direct exact calculation in this case.

We already know from calculations in previous sections

\overline{\braket{O_{1}(v,\bar{v})O_{2}(0)}_{T^{2}(\tau,\bar{\tau})}\braket{O_{1}(v^{\prime},\bar{v}^{\prime})O_{2}(0)}_{T^{2}(\tau^{\prime},\bar{\tau}^{\prime})}}=\\
2\big|\int dh_{2}dh_{3}\rho_{0}(h_{2})\rho_{0}(h_{3})C_{0}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})C_{0}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})\mathcal{F}_{11}^{g=1}(h_{2},h_{3};\tau,v)\mathcal{F}_{11}^{g=1}(h_{2},h_{3};\tau^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big|^{2}

(B.50)

where

\tau=i\beta\quad\tau^{\prime}=i\beta\quad v=i\frac{\beta}{2}-t\quad v^{\prime}=i\frac{\beta}{2}+t

(B.51)

Recall that

\rho_{0}(h)\approx\exp\left(2\pi\sqrt{\frac{c}{6}(h-\frac{c}{24})}\right)

(B.52)

and recall that the functions

Revealing self-gravity in a Stern-Gerlach Humpty-Dumpty experiment
Mohamed Hatifi, Thomas Durt

Abstract
There is no consensus among today’s physicists about how to describe the gravitational interaction properly in a quantum framework. We propose in this paper an experimental test aimed at revealing the existence of a non-linear self-interaction à la Schrödinger-Newton (S-N). In this test, a mesoscopic spin 1/2 microsphere is freely falling in a Humpty-Dumpty Stern-Gerlach interferometer. After clarifying the role of the scaling of the interaction in function of the amplitudes of the up and down spin components of the microsphere, it is shown that self-gravity induces a measurable phase shift between them, which paves the way to experimental tests. It is also shown that if we consider two distinct microspheres falling in parallel, the entangling power of the S-N interaction is exactly equal to zero.



I
Introduction

Properly quantizing gravity remains one of the most challenging problems of today’s theoretical physics. Recently several experiments were proposed, aimed at testing manifestations of the gravitational interaction in the mesoscopic regime (e.g., through entanglement
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
). It is worth mentioning, however, that there exists no unanimous agreement about how to quantize gravity even in the Newtonian limit
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
;
Anastopoulos and Hu 2018
. Here we focus on a mean-field formulation of Einstein’s general relativity originally proposed by Møller
Moller 1959
and Rosenfeld
Rosenfeld 1963
in which space-time remains classical
while the material source term in Einstein’s equation is the average stress-energy tensor, averaged over quantum degrees of freedom:

R_{\mu\nu}-{1\over 2}g_{\mu\nu}R={8\pi G\over c^{4}}\bra{\Psi}\hat{T}_{\mu\nu}\ket{\Psi},

(1)

with
R_{\mu\nu}
the Ricci tensor,
g_{\mu\nu}
the space-time metric,
G
Newton’s constant,
c
, the velocity of light and
\hat{T}
the stress-energy tensor.
In other words, matter is treated in the mean-field regime.

In the Newtonian limit, a self-gravitational interaction is thus likely to be present, which can be expressed, for instance, in the single-particle case, through the (non-linear) S-N equation
Diósi 1984
;
Penrose 1996
;
Jones 1995

\displaystyle{i}\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi(t,{\bf x})}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\,\Delta\Psi(t,{\bf x})+V_{ext}({\bf x},t)\Psi({\bf x},t)+\int{d}^{3}x^{\prime}|\Psi(t,{\bf x^{\prime}})|^{2}V(|{\bf x-x^{\prime}}|)\Psi(t,{\bf x}),

(2)

where the Newton potential
V(d)=-Gm^{2}/d
contributes to the non-linear S-N potential while
V_{ext}({\bf x},t)
represents the external, linear, potential.

Equation (
2
) can be derived from the self-gravitational contribution to the total energy, which is equal to

\int~d^{3}x\,\int~d^{3}x^{\prime}~|\Psi(t,{\bf x})|^{2}\,|\Psi(t,{\bf x^{\prime}})|^{2}\,\frac{(-)G\,m^{2}}{|{\bf x}-{\bf x^{\prime}}|}

(3)

In this semi-classical approach, the gravitational field is considered to be classical and, contrary to quantum gravity approaches, when the source of the gravitational field is prepared in a coherent superposition of non-overlapping localized states, the gravitational potential/metrics is not a superposition state of the potentials/metrics associated to these localized states. As we explain in section
V.4
, this also contrasts with QED, which is a major source of inspiration for quantum gravity
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
.

Now, gravity is known to differ from other fundamental interactions

and it could be after all that the S-N equation (and its many-particles generalization) is relevant for modeling gravity in the quantum and mesoscopic regimes
Carlip 2008
;
Penrose 2014
. This approach would also contribute to solve the measurement problem and to elucidate the quantum-classical transition
Colin et al. 2017
;
Diósi 1984
;
Penrose 1996
. Here we propose an experimental test to reveal the existence of a gravitational self-interaction
à la
S-N. It is directly inspired by two recent proposals
Bose et al. 2017
;

These proposals were conceived in such a way that the gravitational interaction ultimately induces some (in principle measurable) spin entanglement between the two objects. Their goal
was to reveal the quantum nature of gravitation. Note that in references
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
it was assumed from the beginning that no gravitational self-interaction was present.
In the present paper, we propose on the contrary to test the existence of
a semi-classical interaction
à la S-N
which, among others, is characterized by the existence of a self-interaction at the single object level.

In this case (unique object, as in figure
2
), we show that when a unique spin 1/2 mesoscopic particle interacts with itself due to self-gravity this interaction leads to a dephasing between the spin up and spin down wave packets inside the Stern-Gerlach interferometer. This will lead after recombination to a rotation of the spin, which can, in principle, get revealed by spin tomography after the completion of the Humpty-Dumpty experiment. No self-interaction is assumed to be present in quantum gravity approaches, which explains why in our case, one Humpty-Dumpty interferometer only is sufficient, and not two as in the two aforementioned proposals
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
.

In the case of two objects, the S-N model implies the coexistence of self-interaction and also of two-body interaction. The entangling power predicted in the semi-classical approach when two objects fall in parallel along two Humpty-Dumpty interferometers as plotted in figure
1
is also shown in the present paper to drastically differ from the one predicted in the standard approach
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
(actually it is shown to be equal to zero).

II
Self-interaction of a homogeneous sphere.

Let us consider a solid, rigid object, of mass
m
, and let us denote
\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})
the wave function of its center of mass. The average mass-density at a location
{\bf x^{\prime}}
is then equal to the convolution

\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}F({\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})
where
F({\bf x^{\prime}})
represents the (positive) mass-density of the object at a position
{\bf x^{\prime}}
when its center of mass is located at
{\bf x}=0
. It is normalized to
m
:
\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}F({\bf x^{\prime}})=m
. The gravitational potential created by such a mass-distribution at a location
{\bf x}
is nothing else than
\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}F({\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})(-G/|{\bf x-x^{\prime}}|))
. Accordingly, the full self-interaction energy is equal to

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|\Psi(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}

(4)

with

\displaystyle V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})=\int d^{3}{\bf x}\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}F({\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})(-G/|{\bf x-x^{\prime}}|)F({\bf x}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}{\bf x}\int d^{3}{\bf\tilde{x}^{\prime}}F({\bf\tilde{x}^{\prime}})(-G/|{\bf x-\tilde{x}^{\prime}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}}|)F({\bf x}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}{\bf\tilde{x}}\int d^{3}{\bf\tilde{x}^{\prime}}F({\bf\tilde{x}^{\prime}})(-G/|{\bf\tilde{x}-\tilde{x}^{\prime}}|)F({\bf\tilde{x}}+{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}),

(5)

where we introduced the new variables
{\bf\tilde{x}^{\prime}}={\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}
and
{\bf\tilde{x}}={\bf x}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}
.
For a rigid sphere of radius
R
, we shall consider that the density inside the sphere is homogeneous, in first approximation. The consistency and validity of this approximation are discussed further in the paper (sections
V.3
and
VIII.2
). Then the effective potential
V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})
does depend on the norm of
{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}
only. Its exact expression reads
Iwe 1982
;
Colin et al. 2014

V^{\mathrm{eff}}(d)=\frac{Gm^{2}}{R}~\!\left(-\frac{6}{5}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{2}-\frac{3}{16}\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{3}+\frac{1}{160}\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{5}\right)

(6)

if
d\leq 2R
with
d=|{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{\text{CM}}|
; otherwise for larger distances (
d
larger than twice the radius
R
), one can integrate the internal contributions using Gauss’s theorem:

V^{\mathrm{eff}}(d)=-\frac{Gm^{2}}{d}\quad(d\geq 2R).

(7)

It follows that the center of mass wavefunction (CMWF) will be solution of:

\displaystyle\small\begin{aligned} {i}\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\,\Delta\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})+V_{ext}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)\Psi({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)&\\
&+\int{d}^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}|\Psi(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}(|{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{\text{CM}}|)\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}),\end{aligned}

(8)

where
V^{\mathrm{eff}}(|{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{\text{CM}}|)
has been defined in equations (
6
,
7
).
In particular, in what follows, we will consider the limit where the wave function of the center of mass
\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})
is peaked with a width small compared to the radius
R
. In that case the effective potential defined in (
6
) can be considered as quadratic (see also
Yang et al. 2013
):

V^{\mathrm{eff}}(d)\sim\frac{G\,m^{2}}{R}~\!\left(-\frac{6}{5}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{2}\right)

(9)

hence when
d<<2R
equation (
8
) takes the form

\displaystyle\small\begin{aligned} {i}\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\,\Delta\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})+V_{ext}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)\Psi({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)\\
&+\left[\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\left({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-\langle{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}\rangle\right)^{2}+\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{Q}(t)-\frac{6}{5}\frac{G\,m^{2}}{R}\right]\,\Psi(t,{\bf x_{\text{CM}}}),\end{aligned}

(10)

where
\mathcal{Q}=\langle{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}^{2}\rangle-\langle{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}\rangle^{2}
is the quantum spread in position while the pulsation of the (comoving) harmonic potential
\omega_{s}
is equal to
\sqrt{\frac{G\,m}{R^{3}}}=\sqrt{G\,\rho_{sphere}}
, assumed to be constant. It is worth noting here that, as is shown in sections
V.3
and
VIII.2
, corrections due to the presence of the nuclei which render the mass distribution in the sphere inhomogeneous
Yang et al. 2013
;
Colin et al. 2014
are negligible in the present context.

III
Humpty-Dumpty Stern Gerlach experiment as a test for the Schrödinger-Newton equation

III.1
Self-interaction of a spin 1/2 rigid sphere.

In a Stern-Gerlach Humpty-Dumpty experiment, a neutral spin 1/2 object passes through two successive Stern-Gerlach devices, as plotted in figure
2
. In the first magnetic region, the wave packet gets split into its up and down spin components, and it recombines in the second magnetic region. In between those regions, a phase shift accumulates which can be in principle measured after recombination, for instance, by Stern-Gerlach spin tomography.

The wave function is now a spinorial wave function:

\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})=\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})\ket{+}+\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})\ket{-}

where
\Psi_{\pm}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})
is the (properly normalized to unity) spatial wave function associated to the spin
\pm
component and
|\beta_{+}|^{2}+|\beta_{-}|^{2}=1
.

When there is no overlap between the spin components,

|\Psi(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}=|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}+|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|^{2}
so that the full energy of self-interaction (
4
) now reads

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}

\displaystyle|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}

(11)

The (effective) potential generated by such a superposition state is the sum of 2 potentials:

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}),\text{and}

(12)

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}V^{\mathrm{eff}}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-{\bf x^{\prime}}_{\text{CM}}),

(13)

respectively generated by the masses distributions
\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|\beta_{+}\Psi_{+}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}F({\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})
and
\int d^{3}{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}|\beta_{-}\Psi_{-}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})|^{2}F({\bf x^{\prime}}-{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})
, which are normalised to
|\beta_{+}|^{2}\cdot m
and
|\beta_{-}|^{2}\cdot m

The potential scales like
|\beta_{\pm}|^{2}
, in contrast (as we explain in sections
III.1
and
V.4
) with what could be expected from an analogy with QED
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
, and, not amazingly, with the predictions made in references
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
, where the interactions scale like
m
(or
m|\beta_{\pm}|^{0}
). It is important to distinguish these various scalings in order to avoid deep misunderstandings as in the treatment of the Humpty-Dumpty interferometer developed in Ref.
Großardt 2021
. In that paper, one can read the following comment about an earlier presentation
Hatifi and Durt 2020
of some of the results presented here:
…In a recent preprint, Hatifi and Durt pursue the same idea of a test of the SN equation in a Stern-Gerlach interferometer as described by Bose et al.. (…) their derivation seems fundamentally flaw…The intuition between the considerations in reference
Hatifi and Durt 2020
seems to be a mental split of the wave function into two particles of masses
|\alpha|^{2}m
and
|\beta|^{2}m
, respectively….
Obviously, there is no mental construction in our derivation which is a straightforward consequence of equation (
4
). Moreover, if the analogy with two particles of masses
|\alpha|^{2}m
and
|\beta|^{2}m
would be exact, the scaling would be quartic in the moduli of
\beta_{\pm}
, and not quadratic…

III.3
Temporal evolution

According to the analysis performed in section (
III.1
),
V^{G}
is the self-gravitational potential defined in (
10
) rewritten with convenient weightings for the two spin components (in conformity with equations (
12
,
13
)). If the distance between the centers of the two spin components is larger than twice the radius of the nanosphere (
d_{\pm}\geq 2R
), we shall neglect the overlap between the wave packets associated to the centers of mass of the spin components, which is actually fully justified in the narrow wave packet regime where we operate. Now, with the spin components falling in parallel, the condition
d_{\pm}=|\langle z\rangle_{+}-\langle z\rangle_{-}|\leq 2R
is satisfied when
0\leq t\leq T_{s}
, and
T_{5}-T_{s}\leq t\leq T_{5}
where the separation (recombination) time
T_{s}
can be shown (
34
) to obey
T_{s}=\left(\frac{4\,mR}{g\mu_{B}\,B^{\prime}_{0}}\right)^{1/2}
. Actually, the separation (recombination) time is very short (
T_{s}\sim
0.034 s in the regime considered here, with a free fall of the order of 1 s) so that most of the time the two components do not overlap.
The potential, at the location of the
\pm
spin component, is then the sum of the “near field” (
9
) generated by the same spin component and the “far field” (
7
) generated by the other spin component. The evolution equation (
10
) thus takes the form

\displaystyle\small\begin{aligned} &{i}\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi_{\pm}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\,\Delta\Psi_{\pm}(t,{\bf x}_{\text{CM}})+V_{ext}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)\Psi_{\pm}({\bf x}_{\text{CM}},t)\\
&+\left[|\beta_{\pm}|^{2}(\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\left({\bf x}_{\text{CM}}-\langle{\bf x}_{\text{CM}}\rangle\right)^{2}+\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{Q}(t)-\frac{6}{5}\frac{G\,m^{2}}{R})+|\beta_{\mp}|^{2}\,f_{\mp}(t,{\bf x_{\text{CM}}})\right]\,\Psi_{\pm}(t,{\bf x_{\text{CM}}}),\end{aligned}

(15)

where
f_{\mp}(t,{\bf x_{\text{CM}}})
represents the effective potential between different spin components.

Actually, in the interval
[T_{s},T_{5}-T_{s}]
, the Newton force between the up and down wave packets can be shown to be negligibly small. It is easy to check indeed that even if the spin up and down components would move side by side (
d_{\pm}\approx 2R
) during a time of the order of
T_{5}
their Newtonian attraction is so weak that it would reduce the distance between the wave packets by a tiny fraction (10
-6
) of their size
R
.
Consequently, we shall limit ourselves to the lowest order in the Taylor development of
-\frac{Gm^{2}}{|z_{\pm}-<z_{\mp}>|}
around
<z_{\pm}>
which means that we approximate
f_{\mp}
through a classical Newton-like potential:

f_{\mp}(t)=-{G\,m^{2}}\,\frac{1}{|\langle z\rangle_{+}-\langle z\rangle_{-}|}=-{G\,m^{2}}\,\frac{1}{d_{\pm}}

(16)

Then
f_{\mp}(x,y,z,t)
takes the form of a Newton-like potential (
-\frac{Gm^{2}}{|z_{\pm}-<z_{\mp}>|}
as in (
7
)); moreover it does no longer depend on
x
and
y
.

Even inside magnetic regions of the Stern-Gerlach device, equation (
15
) is thus separable in Cartesian coordinates. This allows us to consider in what follows a description in the (freely falling) comoving frame in which we limit our study to the component of the CMWF along the quantization axis (
Z
) of the Stern-Gerlach interferometer (see figure
2
). Nothing remarkable happens along the free fall axis
X
or along the third axis
Y
: in good approximation, the evolution is the same as for a freely expanding wave packet as shown in appendix (section
VIII.1
). After factoring out its
x
and
y
components, the CMWF can be expressed as a superposition of spin up and down along
Z
states

\large\Psi(z,t)=\sum_{i=\{+,-\}}\,\beta_{i}\,\psi_{i}(z,t)\,\ket{i}
. The probability density assigned to the projection of the position of the center of mass along
Z
reads

|\Psi(z,t)|^{2}=\sum_{i=\{+,-\}}\,|\beta_{i}|^{2}\,|\psi_{i}(z,t)|^{2},

(17)

with
|\beta_{+}|^{2}+|\beta_{-}|^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dz|\psi_{i}(z,t)|^{2}=1
, and the evolution of the projection along
Z
of each spin component is described by a Hamiltonian of the form

\mathcal{H}_{z}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\,m}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}+\lambda\,\frac{g\mu_{B}}{2}\left(B_{0}-B^{\prime}_{0}\,z\right)\otimes\sigma_{z}+V^{G}(z,t)

(18)

where
V^{G}
represents the gravitational self-interaction,
\mu_{B}
is the Bohr magneton,
g\sim 2
is the electronic g-factor and
\lambda
obeys equation (
14
).

After developing the “projected”S-N potential around
\langle z\rangle_{+}
(resp.
\langle z\rangle_{-}
) we get

V_{\pm}^{G}(z,t)=\underbrace{|\beta_{\pm}|^{2}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}\,\omega_{s}^{2}\left(z-\langle z\rangle_{\pm}\right)^{2}+\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}(t)-\frac{6}{5}\,\frac{G\,m^{2}}{R}\right]}_{\textrm{\small Self-interaction $\ket{\pm}\rightarrow\ket{\pm}$}}+\underbrace{|\beta_{\mp}|^{2}\,f_{\mp}(z,t)}_{\textrm{\small Self-interaction $\ket{\mp}\rightarrow\ket{\pm}$}}

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}=\langle z^{2}\rangle_{\pm}-\langle z\rangle_{\pm}^{2}
, and the function
f_{\mp}(z,t)=\int{d}z^{\prime}~|\psi_{\mp}(t,{\bf z^{\prime}})|^{2}~V^{eff}(|{\bf z-z^{\prime}}|)
.
We made use of the fact that
V^{eff}(|{\bf z-z^{\prime}}|)
interpolates, according to (
6
), between the harmonic potential when
d_{\pm}=|\langle z\rangle_{+}-\langle z\rangle_{-}|\leq 2R
and the Newtonian potential if
d_{\pm}\geq 2R
, as in (
7
); it also obeys a scaling quadratic in
|\beta_{\pm}|
.

To conclude, each branch of the superposition is solution of the following non-linear Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi_{\pm}(z,t)}{\partial t}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\,m}\,\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\pm\lambda\frac{g\mu_{B}}{2}\left(B_{0}-B^{\prime}_{0}\,z\right)+V_{\pm}^{G}(z,t)\right]\,\psi_{\pm}(z,t),

(19)

where in good approximation and most of the time
V_{\pm}^{G}(z,t)
is the sum of a quadratic self-interaction of each packet with itself (with weight
|\beta_{i}|^{2}
) with an effective Newtonian interaction towards the other wave packet (with weight
|\beta_{j,j\not=i}|^{2}
). During the (very short) separation and recombination periods, the self-gravitational interaction does not contribute much to the dephasing, because the separation (recombination) time is very short (
T_{s}\sim
0.034 s in the regime considered here, with a free fall of the order of 1 s) and also because during the (short) separation (recombination) process, the Stern-Gerlach potential
Hsu et al. 2011
;
Platt 1992
dominates the self-interaction. We shall thus simply do as if each spin component was isolated during that period.

In accordance with the previous discussion, we have thus

\displaystyle V_{\pm}^{G}(z,t)=\nu_{\pm}^{2}\,\left[\frac{m}{2}\,\omega_{s}^{2}\left(z-\langle z\rangle_{\pm}\right)^{2}+\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}(t)-\frac{6}{5}\,\frac{G\,m^{2}}{R}\right]-(1-\nu_{\pm}^{2})\,\frac{Gm^{2}}{d_{\pm}}

(20)

where

\displaystyle\quad\nu_{\pm}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}&1&\quad\text{if}\quad d_{\pm}=|\langle z\rangle_{+}-\langle z\rangle_{-}|\leq 2R\\
&|\beta_{\pm}|&\quad\text{otherwise that is to say when $T_{s}\leq t\leq T_{5}-T_{s}$ with $T_{s}=\left(\frac{4\,mR}{g\mu_{B}\,B^{\prime}_{0}}\right)^{1/2}\sim 0.034s$}\end{array}\right.

Such an evolution being gaussian, gaussian wave packets remain so in good approximation during the temporal evolution, which seriously facilitates the numerical treatment.

III.4
Ehrenfest’s theorem and self-gravity

Before we compute the phase shift associated to each wave packet, it appears to be useful to develop the potential
V(z,t)
to the second order in
z
around the location of the peak of the gaussian wave packet. To see this, let us consider the Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi}{\partial z^{2}}+V(\braket{z})+\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\Bigr|_{\braket{z}}(z-\braket{z})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial z^{2}}\Bigr|_{\braket{z}}(z-\braket{z})^{2}

(23)

Denoting
\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial z^{2}}\Bigr|_{\braket{z}}=m\omega^{2}
, and identifying

with the expression of

\displaystyle V_{0}

\displaystyle=V(\braket{z})-\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\Bigr|_{\braket{z}}\braket{z}+\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2}\braket{z}^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial V}{\partial z}\Bigr|_{\braket{z}}-m\omega^{2}\braket{z}

(25)

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m\omega^{2}}{2}

(26)

Generally, a Schrödinger equation of the form

where

are complex functions of time. We then get, after straightforward computations, the following system of equations:

\large\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}i\,\frac{dA_{\pm}(t)}{dt}&=&\frac{\hbar}{m}\,A_{\pm}(t)^{2}-2\,\frac{V_{2,\pm}(t)}{\hbar}\\
\\

\\

And we also know from (

\displaystyle\small\frac{d\mathcal{R}\text{e}A_{\pm}}{dt}

\displaystyle=2\,\frac{\hbar}{m}\,\mathcal{R}\text{e}A_{\pm}\,\mathcal{I}\text{m}A_{\pm},

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{I}\text{m}A_{\pm}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-{\hbar\over m}(\mathcal{R}\text{e}A_{\pm}^{2}-\mathcal{I}\text{m}A_{\pm}^{2})+2\,{V_{2,\pm}\over\hbar}

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{R}\text{e}B_{\pm}}{dt}

\displaystyle={\hbar\over m}\,(\mathcal{R}\text{e}A_{\pm}\,\mathcal{I}\text{m}B_{\pm}+\mathcal{I}\text{m}A_{\pm}\,\mathcal{R}\text{e}B_{\pm}),

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{I}\text{m}B_{\pm}}{dt}

hence one can show the following identities

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle z\rangle_{\pm}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\,\langle p\rangle_{\pm}

In particular, (

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\,\langle p\rangle_{\pm}

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle p\rangle_{\pm}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\pm\lambda\frac{g\mu_{B}}{2}\,B^{\prime}_{0}

V.1
Some orders of magnitude.

The main progress in our proposal, as well as in the proposals of references
Bose et al. 2017
;
Marletto and Vedral 2017
, is that they are based on an interferometric effect. Other proposals in the past
Colin et al. 2014
;
Colin et al. 2016
;
Giulini and Großardt 2011
;
van Meter 2011
aimed at measuring the inhibition of the spread of a wave packet due to self-gravity concluded that free-fall times of the order of
10^{4}
seconds are required in order to get an observable effect. A gaussian wave packet gets indeed frozen by self-gravity if its size is of the order of the Lieb radius
\hbar^{2}/Gm^{3}

V.2
One versus Two Humpty-Dumpty devices.

At this level, we may relax the original assumption according to which it was necessary to prepare the initial state in the ground state of the optical trap from which it is released before entering the S-G apparatus. The reason that we advocated for doing so was that we needed a pure gaussian state to begin with. Retrospectively we see that, as it is the additive constant
\frac{6}{5}\,\frac{G\,m^{2}}{\hbar\,R}
in the self-interaction that mainly contributes to the dephasing, a similar dephasing is expected to occur even when the center of mass degrees of freedom are initially prepared in a thermal state.
If the experiment reveals that no dephasing is present, this would mean that there is no gravitational self-interaction (
3
) in nature; then, a double Humpty-Dumpty experiment as already proposed in

;
Marletto and Vedral 2017

but also possibly other models inspired by the electro-magnetic analogy of section
V.4
.
The double Humpty-Dumpty experiment is more difficult to realize than our proposal, not only because two interferometers must be realized in parallel. In order to minimize Casimir-Polders interaction, the authors of
Bose et al. 2017
must impose that the distance between the objects is de facto quite larger than their size (of the order of 100 times larger), so that free fall times of the order of at least 2.5 seconds are necessary (working in the same conditions) to see a dephasing of the order of one radian.

When only one object is present, as in our case, the Casimir-Polders force is equal to zero because there is no electromagnetic self-interaction in nature, as we explained at the beginning of this paper. All that is necessary in our case is to nullify the spatial overlap of the up and down wave packets. Our experimental proposal would thus still be feasible with quite less intense magnetic fields and/or quite a shorter experiment time than those required for realizing the double Humpty-Dumpty proposals

;
Marletto and Vedral 2017

\widetilde{T_{5}}\sim 0.2

V.3
Why in good approximation, the internal density of the object (rigid sphere) can be considered to be constant (homogeneous).

If the size of the wave function of the center of mass of the freely falling object is comparable to the spread of the nucleons inside its crystalline structure, it is not appropriate to treat this object as a sphere of constant density. This is so because the self-interaction of the nucleons must be taken into account. V.4
Developing the electromagnetic analogy.

Compared to all other physical theories, QED has been confirmed with unprecedented accuracy, and it is obviously a vivid source of inspiration for quantum gravity
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
. Moreover, even in the non-relativistic limit, the incorporation of the Coulomb interaction into the non-relativistic Schrödinger equation in two simple cases, such as hydrogen and helium atom, leads to accurate predictions confirmed by experiments so that the corresponding models can serve as a source of inspiration for a toy-model of quantum gravity in the non-relativistic limit, to be contrasted with the S-N interaction (
5
) considered here.

Let us consider first the case of the hydrogen atom.
Replacing the mass
m

G

2

In the single-particle case, however, the Wigner-Poisson equation is clearly not relevant and ruled out by facts, among others, because if we would apply it to quantize electronic energy levels in the hydrogen atom it would drastically modify Bohr’s spectrum, strongly contradicting accurate spectroscopic data accumulated since the 19th century by Rydberg and others
Colin et al. 2014
;
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
. This simple example rules out semi-classical models such as the droplet model originally associated by Schrödinger to the electron

VII
Conclusions

As we explained in the introduction, including a non-linearity at the level of Schrödinger equation marks a strong departure from other proposals aimed at incorporating the gravitational interaction in a quantum framework. One of the motivations for doing so is that it could contribute to solving the measurement problem
Colin et al. 2017
, but other options remain open
Donadi and Bassi 2022
. In reference
Anastopoulos and Hu 2014
, for instance, a QFT approach based on perturbed linear gravity is shown to lead to a linear potential with no self-interaction, similar to the one described in section
V.4
. General relativity being non-linear it is not clear whether its quantized version ought to be linear or not, and it could be that a satisfactory quantum description of gravity will impose to go beyond general relativity and quantum field theory. This explains why we focus here on a realizable experimental proposal: we are convinced that more experimental data about gravity in the quantum regime are needed if we wish to progress in this field
Donadi and Bassi 2022
.

This paper has been written in three steps. Preliminary results were presented for the first time in the Ph.D. thesis of one of us (M.H.) in 2019
Hatifi 2019
. Thereafter we managed to estimate the various contributions contained in the final phase shift using both semi-analytical and numeric methods separately; among others, we estimated in section
VIII.3
the gaussian correction (
49
) to the semi-classical expression of the phase-shift commonly in use in atomic interferometry. These results led to a reprint in 2020
Hatifi and Durt 2020
. In the present paper we definitively clarified some misconceptions
Großardt 2021
regarding the scaling of the self-interaction in function of the amplitudes
\beta_{\pm}
. This paved the way for an estimate of the entangling power of the S-N interaction which is rigorously shown to be null in section
VI
. The present paper contains all these contributions. One of the reasons why it took such a long time to achieve this work is the covid crisis, and its unexpected overload of teaching duties in the case of one of us (T.D.).

Due to the non-linear nature of the S-N interaction, some approximations were necessary in order to be able to tackle the problem but all our approximations are rigorously derived and justified in a fully explicit and transparent fashion throughout the paper. For the rest, our derivations are exact and our results possess an analytical expression. We are thus confident that if one day the experiment described here gets realized, it will undoubtedly constitute a crucial experiment regarding the existence S-N interaction.
As has been shown here, measuring the phase shift
\Delta\varphi(T_{5})
would (see equation (
66
)), indeed, enable us to establish the existence of a self-gravitational interaction
à la
S-N, which is the main result of our paper
5
5

5

Note that realizing a Stern-Gerlach Humpty-Dumpty experiment in the regime considered in the present paper would also make it possible to test other non-standard models like e.g. the de Broglie-Bohm interpretation
Durt 2022
and, possibly, a large range of models of spontaneous localization
Donadi and Bassi 2022
.

Nano-resonators, for which cooling of the center of mass degree of freedom and embedding of NV centers have already been successfully demonstrated in the past
Arcizet et al. 2011
;
Yuan et al. 2015
are promising candidates for testing our ideas, but the most promising candidate can be attributed without a doubt to Folman’s team, because of its expertise in Stern-Gerlach experiments, and its explicit objective to realize a Humpty-Dumpty Stern-Gerlach interferometer with massive objects
Margalit et al. 2021
;
Keil et al. 2021
.

In any case, all these proposals constitute a breakthrough in the sense that they aim at measuring gravitational effects originating from delocalized objects in the mesoscopic regime. It is worth trying to realize them because they could provide the missing clues necessary for properly quantizing gravity. They could also contribute to clarifying the measurement problem and to discriminate between various interpretations of the quantum theory
Donadi and Bassi 2022
;
Durt 2022
. We are confident, in view of all the results presented in this paper, that if one day the experiment described here gets realized, it will constitute a crucial experiment regarding the existence of the S-N interaction.

(83)

VIII.2
Discussion: contributions of self-gravity to the spread and nuclear corrections.

In order to take account of nuclear corrections, we have to impose that
\omega_{s}
is of the order of
\sqrt{G\,({10^{-12}\over 10^{-10}})^{3}\rho_{sphere}}\approx 1
Hz when the width of the wave function is smaller than the size of a nucleon (of the order of
10^{-12}
m
Yang et al. 2013
), and equal to
\sqrt{\frac{G\,m}{R^{3}}}=\sqrt{G\,\rho_{sphere}}
otherwise
Colin et al. 2014
.

Even if we take these corrections into account, the global contribution of self-gravity in (
83
) is actually negligible because either the pulsation of the (comoving) harmonic potential
\omega_{s}
is very small or, when it is large due to nuclear contributions, it is large only during very short times.

Indeed it is easy to check that whenever the width of the wave function is larger than or equal to
10^{-10}
m
Colin et al. 2014
, the contribution of the nucleons vanishes and
\omega_{s}\approx\sqrt{\frac{G\,m}{R^{3}}}=\sqrt{G\,\rho_{sphere}}\approx 10^{-3}
Hz then
(\omega_{s}\,t)^{2}\approx 10^{-6}
for falls of a duration of the order of 1 s.

Otherwise, when for instance
\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}\leq 10^{-12}
m, the expansion of the free packet occurs so fast that the pulsation
\omega_{s}
will get boosted by nucleic contributions during a negligibly short time. For instance, for a mass
m
of
10^{-14}
kg, if we impose that
\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
belongs to the interval
(10^{-13},10^{-15})
m,
\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
will reach a width
\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}\approx 10^{-10}
m after a time of the order of
(m\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}/\hbar)\cdot 10^{-10}
m that belongs to the interval
(10^{-3},10^{-5})
s. During this very short time,
\omega_{s}\,t
will remain very small compared to unity. This explains why the nucleic corrections are negligible being given the typical values of times, masses and so on considered by us in our proposal.

As has been confirmed by accurate numerical computations (see e.g. figure
8
), the difference between
\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}(t)
and their free counterpart
\mathcal{Q}_{0}\left[1+\frac{\hbar^{2}\,t^{2}}{4m^{2}\mathcal{Q}_{0}^{2}}\right]
can thus consistently be neglected.

Indeed, in the limit
\omega_{s}\,t\ll 1
considered above and using equation (
83
) we get that the difference in the width is given by

\mathcal{D}(t)=\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}_{-}(t)}-\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}_{+}(t)}=\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}_{0}}}{2}\,\left(1-2\,|\beta_{-}|^{2}\right)\,(\omega_{s}\,t)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left((\omega_{s}\,t)^{3}\right)

(84)

For example, in our simulations, when we considered
m=5,5.10^{-15}
kg,
\omega_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{G\,m}{R^{3}}}\sim 6.10^{-4}
Hz,
|\beta_{+}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
and
|\beta_{-}|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
, we found for a time
T_{5}
of the order of
3

\mathcal{D}(T_{5})\sim 5,4.10^{-17}

\mathcal{D}(T_{5})
is thus smaller than all the characteristic lengths considered in this study. Actually, the difference between
A_{\pm}(t)
and
A(t)

A(t)

VIII.3
Formal expressions of the integrals contributing to the phase shift

We shall now consider the following integrals that appear in the total phase shift (

\displaystyle I_{1,\pm}(t)=\int~dt^{\prime}\,\frac{\hbar}{4m\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}(t^{\prime})}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2,\pm}(t)=\int~dt^{\prime}\,\frac{m\omega_{s}^{2}}{2\,\hbar}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\pm}(t^{\prime})\,\nu_{\pm}^{2}.

(86)

\bullet\quad
It can be shown that

I_{1,+}(t)-I_{1,-}(t)=0+\mathcal{O}\left((\omega_{s}\,t)^{3}\right)

VIII.4
Entangling power and structure of the interaction.

It has been shown elsewhere by one of us
Durt 2004
that the entangling power of an interaction between two systems
A
and
B
initially prepared in a factorizable state is proportional to the coupling rate of the interaction Hamiltonian to states biorthogonal to this factorizable state. In the same paper, the following theorem has been shown: if a specific Hamiltonian
H_{AB}
never entangles the systems
A
and
B
initially prepared in an arbitrary factorizable state, then
H_{AB}=H_{A}1\!\!1_{B}+1\!\!1_{A}H_{B}
, and there is no effective interaction between
A
and
B
(only effective self-interaction). Now, the interaction Hamiltonian between the systems
A
and
B
in a double Stern-Gerlach device is obviously the sum of the self-interactions between each system plus the mean field interaction generated by each system on its partner. It is easy to show that it reads
H_{AB}=H^{self}_{A}1\!\!1_{B}+1\!\!1_{A}H^{self}_{B}+H_{A}^{<B>}1\!\!1_{B}+1\!\!1_{A}H^{<A>}_{B}
, where
H^{self}_{A(B)}
as well as
H_{A}^{<B>}
(
H_{B}^{<A>}
) are a linear combination (with real amplitudes) of
1\!\!1_{A(B)}
and
\sigma^{Z}_{A(B)}
=
\ket{+^{A(B)}}\bra{+^{A(B)}}-\ket{-^{A(B)}}\bra{-^{A(B)}}
. This means that neither the self-interaction nor the effective interaction resulting from the mean influence of the other system will make it possible to entangle these systems. In a sense, this is a contraposition of another theorem, valid in the linear regime, shown in reference
Durt 2004
: if the total system factorizes, each system feels (up to a global phase) the mean influence of the other one
Durt 2004
;
Gemmer and Mahler 2001
. However, here, each system “feels” the mean influence of the other one, even if the full state is entangled, which is a signature of the S-N interaction, in full conformity with the mean-field approach initiated by Møller
Moller 1959
and Rosenfeld
Rosenfeld 1963
.

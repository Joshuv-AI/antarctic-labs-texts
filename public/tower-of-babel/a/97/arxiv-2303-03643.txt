On singular moduli for higher rank Drinfeld modules
Chien-Hua Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize Dorman’s work to estimate singular moduli for higher rank Drinfeld modules. In particular, we give a lower bound on the valuation of singular moduli for Drinfeld modules with complex multiplication by an imaginary field extension over the rational function field. Furthermore, we compute several examples for rank- 3 3 case.

Introduction

In the theory of arithmetic of elliptic curves, a singular modulus is defined to be the
j
-invariant of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication. In
[
GZ85
]
, Gross and Zagier introduced a systematic method to estimate the difference of two singular moduli:

Theorem

(
[

]

.

Let

,

be two fundamental discriminant of imaginary quadratic fields. Assume further that
d_{1}
and

are coprime to each other. Let

,

be the number of roots of unity in the quadratic orders of discriminant
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
, respectively. Define

J(d_{1},d_{2}):=\left(\prod_{[\tau_{1}],[\tau_{2}]\ {\rm with\ disc}(\tau_{i})=d_{i}}(j(\tau_{1})-j(\tau_{2}))\right)^{\frac{4}{w_{1}w_{2}}}.

Then we have

J(d_{1},d_{2})^{2}=\pm\prod_{x\in\mathbb{Z}\ \textrm{and }n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\textrm{ with }x^{2}+4nn^{\prime}=d_{1}d_{2}}n^{\epsilon(n^{\prime})}.

Here
\epsilon(n^{\prime})
is an explicit map defined in terms of Legendre symbols.

We refer a more detailed explanation of the proofs to
[
Con04
]
. Subsequently, the Gross-Zagier estimation aligns with intriguing topics, including the Hilbert class polynomial for CM elliptic curves
[
BJO06
]
, the number of supersingular reduction primes of an elliptic curve over

[

]

[
GZ86
]
. Additionally, one can also viewed Gross-Zagier singular moduli formula geometrically as valuation of CM cycle on modular stack over

of pairs of elliptic curves (see section 3 in
[

]

2.1
Drinfeld modules

We view

Definition 2.1

A Drinfeld

\phi:A\rightarrow K\{\tau\}

\phi(a):=\phi_{a}

{\rm deg}_{\tau}\phi_{a}=r\cdot{\rm deg}_{T}a

(ii)

Denote

\partial(\sum a_{i}\tau^{i})=a_{0}

\phi

From the definition of Drinfeld

\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r-1}\tau^{r-1}+\Delta\tau^{r},\textrm{ where }g_{i}\in K\textrm{ and }\Delta\in K^{*}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi

be two rank-

Drinfeld

-modules over

. A

A non-zero morphism

{\rm Hom}_{K}(\phi,\psi)
to be the group of all morphisms

over
K
. Also, we denote

. For any field extension

{\rm Hom}_{L}(\phi,\psi)=\{u\in L\{\tau\}\mid u\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}u\text{ \rm for all }a\in A\}.

For

, we omit subscripts and write

{\rm Hom}(\phi,\psi):={\rm Hom}_{\bar{K}}(\phi,\psi)\text{ \rm and }{\rm End}(\phi):={\rm End}_{\bar{K}}(\phi)

Remark 2.3

The composition of morphisms makes

Definition 3.1

\varphi_{T}=T+\tau^{r}

Theorem

Lemma 3.7

\nu(J^{(\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r-1})}(\phi))\geqslant\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\delta_{i}}{q^{r}-1}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#{\rm Iso}_{W/\mu^{n}W}(\phi,\varphi)

Proof.

If
\phi

\varphi
after reduction modulo
\mu

\phi\not\cong\varphi
after reduction modulo
\mu^{n}
for any

Let us assume that
\phi

\varphi

\mu^{k}

We compute

\nu(J^{(\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r-1})}(\phi))\geqslant k(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r-1}).

(3)

On the other hand, from our assumption that

Thus we know that

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#{\rm Iso}_{W/\mu^{n}W}(\phi,\varphi)=k(q^{r}-1).

(4)

Now we can combine (3) and (4) together. From the condition (1) in the definition of
J
-invariant (see section
2.2
), we compute the smallest possible number
(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r-1})
for
\delta_{i}
’s satisfy the equation

The smallest possible is to make
\delta_{r-1}
as large as possible. If
\delta_{r}=1
, then we choose
\delta_{r-1}=q
,
\delta_{1}=1
, and other
\delta_{i}=0
. This gives us

(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r-1})\geqslant q+1.

If

\delta_{i}^{\prime}s
would have

(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r-1})\geqslant\delta_{r-1}=\delta_{r}\cdot q>q+1.

Therefore, the inequality (3) implies

J^{(\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r-1})}(\phi)\geqslant k(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{r-1})\geqslant k(q+1).

Thus we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\nu(J^{(\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{r-1})}(\phi))&\geqslant k(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\delta_{i})=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\delta_{i}}{q^{r}-1}\cdot k(q^{r}-1)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\delta_{i}}{q^{r}-1}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#{\rm Iso}_{W/\mu^{n}W}(\phi,\varphi)\\
\end{array}.

Proof of Theorem

\geqslant k\cdot(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{A}}\delta_{j})\end{array}.

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})})&\geqslant\frac{(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(q-1)}{r_{\rm sep}\cdot(q^{3}-1)\cdot e_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\sum_{m\geqslant 0}\#\mathcal{M}_{m\cdot e_{K,\mathfrak{p}}+1}.\par\end{array}

And we know that the counting number of elements in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
for
n\geqslant 1
an integer is equal to count the number of matrices

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
\pi\sigma(x_{3})&\sigma(x_{1})&\sigma(x_{2})\\
\pi\sigma^{2}(x_{2})&\pi\sigma^{2}(x_{3})&\sigma^{2}(x_{1})\end{array}\right),

whose minimal polynomial is equal to
X^{3}-\Delta
. Here
x_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{H}
, and
x_{i}=\pi^{n-1}x_{i}^{\prime}
with
x_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{H}
for
2\leqslant i\leqslant 3
. Note that
\pi\in A[T]
is the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
, so
\pi
is invariant under the action of
\sigma
. Comparing the coefficients of the minimal polynomial of such matrices with the minimal polynomial
X^{3}-\Delta
of
\sqrt[3]{\Delta}
, we get three equations

{\rm Tr}(x_{1})=0,

x_{1}\sigma(x_{1})+x_{1}\sigma^{2}(x_{1})+\sigma(x_{1})\sigma^{2}(x_{1})=\pi^{2n+1}[\sigma^{2}(x_{2}^{\prime})x_{3}^{\prime}+\sigma(x_{2}^{\prime})\sigma^{2}(x_{3}^{\prime})+x_{2}^{\prime}\sigma(x_{3}^{\prime})],

and

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta&=&{\rm Norm}(x_{1})+\pi^{3n-2}{\rm Norm}(x_{2}^{\prime})+\pi^{3n-1}{\rm Norm}(x_{3}^{\prime})\\
&&-\pi^{2n-1}[\sigma(x_{1})\sigma^{2}(x_{2}^{\prime})x_{3}^{\prime}+x_{1}\sigma(x_{2}^{\prime})\sigma^{2}(x_{3}^{\prime})+\sigma^{2}(x_{1})x_{2}^{\prime}\sigma(x_{3}^{\prime})]\end{array}.

After some simplification, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Tr}(x_{1})=0&(7)\\
&\\
{\rm Tr}(x_{1}\sigma(x_{1}))=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(x_{1}^{2})=\pi^{2n+1}{\rm Tr(x_{2}^{\prime}\sigma(x_{3}^{\prime}))}&(8)\\
&\\
{\rm Norm}(x_{1})+\pi^{3n-2}{\rm Norm}(x_{2}^{\prime})+\pi^{3n-1}{\rm Norm}(x_{3}^{\prime})-\pi^{2n-1}{\rm Tr}(x_{1}\sigma(x_{2}^{\prime})\sigma^{2}(x_{3}^{\prime}))=\Delta&(9)\par\par\end{array}\right..

Hence
\#\mathcal{M}_{n}=\#\{(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[T]^{3}\mid(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime})\textrm{ satisfies equation (4) to (6)}\}

Remark 5.1
.

One can compare the equations (7) to (9) with the Diophantine equation in

Dor91
]

5.1
The case
\Delta=\pi=T

5.1.1
Separable case

We further restrict
q
to be an odd prime power that is coprime to
3
. Before we start the computation, one can check directly that
\mathcal{O}_{K}=A[\sqrt[3]{T}]
. Moreover,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a principal ideal domain, hence there is only one isomorphism class
[\phi]
in
{\rm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\iota)
.

For simplicity, let’s set
\theta=\sqrt[3]{T}
. Let
\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}=\theta+\tau
be the rank-
1
Drinfeld
\mathcal{O}_{K}-
module. From
\phi^{\prime}
, we can construct a Drinfeld
A-
module
\phi
which has CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\phi_{T}&=&\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}\cdot\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}\cdot\phi^{\prime}_{\theta}\\
&=&T+[\theta^{2}+\theta^{q+1}+\theta^{2q}]\tau+[\theta+\theta^{q}+\theta^{q^{2}}]\tau^{2}+\tau^{3}\end{array}.

Hence
{\rm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\iota)=\{[\phi]\}.

Now we show under a suitable choice of
J^{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})}
, the equality in the bound can be reached. Take

J^{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})}=J^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)}=\frac{g_{2}^{q^{2}+q+1}}{g_{3}^{q+1}}.

We have
{\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)})=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}
by direct computation. On the other hand, by counting
T
-degree on both sides of equation (12), one can see that such a solution
(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime},x_{3}^{\prime})
exists only when
n=1
. Moreover, one can pick triples
(0,x_{2}^{\prime},0)
where
x_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[T]
has norm equal to
1
. These triples satisfy equation (7) to (9) when
n=1
, and there are
q^{2}+q+1
many such triples. Thus we have

\sum_{m\geqslant 0}\#\mathcal{M}_{m\cdot e_{K,\mathfrak{p}}+1}=\#\mathcal{M}_{1}\geqslant q^{2}+q+1.

Therefore,

\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}={\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)})\geqslant\frac{(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(q-1)}{3\cdot(q^{3}-1)\cdot 3}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#\mathcal{M}_{n}=\frac{1}{9}\cdot\#\mathcal{M}_{1}\geqslant\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{9}.

The difference between both sides is because of the term “
r_{\rm sep}
” in Remark
4.8
. From Remark
3.19
and the fact that
x^{3}-T\equiv x^{3}\mod\sqrt[3]{T}
has only one root, we actually have

\#S_{1}=\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{1}}{\#\textrm{ of disjoint subsets }\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\mid\partial\circ f=\zeta\mod\sqrt[3]{T}\}\textrm{ in }\mathcal{M}_{1}}=\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{1}}{1},

while our lower bound in Remark
4.8
takes the smallest possible number

\#S_{1}\geqslant\frac{1}{r_{\rm sep}}\#\mathcal{M}_{1}=\frac{\#\mathcal{M}_{1}}{3}.

5.1.2
Inseparable case

We consider the above example in the case when
q
is a
3
-power, i.e.
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
and
K=F(\sqrt[3]{T})
. It is easy to see that
K/F
is a purely inseparable, normal, and imaginary extension over
F
. Set
\theta=\sqrt[3]{T}
. Again, because
\mathcal{O}_{K}=A[\theta]
is a principal ideal domain, there is only one isomorphism class in
{\rm CM}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\iota)
. The Drinfeld
A-
module
\phi
defined by

\begin{array}[]{lll}\phi_{T}&=&T+[\theta^{2}+\theta^{q+1}+\theta^{2q}]\tau+[\theta+\theta^{q}+\theta^{q^{2}}]\tau^{2}+\tau^{3}\end{array}.

which has CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Hence
{\rm{CM}}(\mathcal{O}_{K},\iota)=\{[\phi]\}.

Now we know that
{\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)})=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}
by direct computation. From the same argument as in the separable case, we have

\sum_{m\geqslant 0}\#\mathcal{M}_{m\cdot e_{K,\mathfrak{p}}+1}=\#\mathcal{M}_{1}\geqslant q^{2}+q+1.

Thus we can compute from Corollary
4.7
and get

\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}={\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)})\geqslant\frac{(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(q-1)}{1\cdot(q^{3}-1)\cdot 3}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#\mathcal{M}_{n}\geqslant\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}.

The equality is reached in this case. Thus we actually have

\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#\mathcal{M}_{n}=\#\mathcal{M}_{1}=q^{2}+q+1.

Now we can replace another basic
J
-invariant and apply our estimation again. One can see that even the inequality is sharp for some basic
J
-invariant, the equality does not hold for all basic
J
-invariant. Let us choose the basic
J
-invariant
J^{(q^{2}+q+1,0)}
, then one have

\frac{2(q^{2}+q+1)}{3}={\rm{ord}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(0,q^{2}+q+1)})>\frac{(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})(q-1)}{1\cdot(q^{3}-1)\cdot 3}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\#\mathcal{M}_{n}=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{3}.

5.2
The case
\Delta=T(T+1)
, and
\pi=T

Again, one can check that
\mathcal{O}_{K}=A[\sqrt[3]{T^{2}+T}]
by Theorem 1.1 in
[
Tan01
]
.

Then the set

\mathcal{B}:=\{(0,\beta,\gamma)\mid\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[T]\textrm{ with }{\rm Norm}(\beta)={\rm Norm}(\gamma)=1\textrm{ and }{\rm Tr}(\beta\sigma(\gamma))=0\}

satisfy equation (7) to (9) when
n=1
. Furthermore, we have

\mathcal{B}\supset\{(0,\beta,1)\mid{\rm Norm}(\beta)=1,{\rm Tr}(\beta)=0\}\sqcup\{(0,1,\gamma)\mid{\rm Norm}(\gamma)=1,{\rm Tr}(\gamma)=0\}.

On the other hand, from Katz’s estimation on Soto-Andrade sum (see Theorem 1.1 in
[
MW10
]
), the number
N_{3}(0,1)
of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
with norm equal to
1
and trace equal to
0
is bounded by the following inequality:

|N_{3}(0,1)-\frac{q^{2}-1}{q-1}|\leqslant g.c.d.(3,q-1)\sqrt{q}.

Hence we have

N_{3}(0,1)\geqslant q+1-g.c.d(3,q-1)\sqrt{q},

which implies

\begin{array}[]{lll}\#\mathcal{B}&\geqslant&\#\{(0,\beta,1)\mid{\rm Norm}(\beta)=1,{\rm Tr}(\beta)=0\}+\#\{(0,1,\gamma)\mid{\rm Norm}(\gamma)=1,{\rm Tr}(\gamma)=0\}\\
&\geqslant&2N_{3}(0,1)\\
&\geqslant&2(q+1-g.c.d(3,q-1)\sqrt{q})\end{array}.

Therefore, we get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}(J_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}^{(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})})&\geqslant&\frac{(q+1)(q-1)}{3\cdot(q^{3}-1)\cdot 3}\sum_{m\geqslant 0}\#\mathcal{M}_{m\cdot e_{K,\mathfrak{p}}+1}\\
&&\\
&\geqslant&\frac{(q+1)(q-1)}{9\cdot(q^{3}-1)}\cdot 2(q+1-g.c.d(3,q-1)\sqrt{q})\\
&&\\
&=&\frac{2(q+1)(q+1-g.c.d(3,q-1)\sqrt{q})}{9\cdot(q^{2}+q+1)}\par\end{array}.

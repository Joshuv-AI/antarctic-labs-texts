Existence of closed embedded curves of constant curvature via min-max
Lorenzo Sarnataro, Douglas Stryker

Abstract
. We find conditions under which Almgren-Pitts min-max for the prescribed geodesic curvature functional in a closed oriented Riemannian surface produces a closed embedded curve of constant curvature. In particular, we find a closed embedded curve of any prescribed constant curvature in any metric on S 2 S^{2} with 1 / 8 1/8 -pinched Gaussian curvature.



1.
Introduction

It is a foundational problem in symplectic geometry, dynamical systems, and variational methods to find closed curves of constant geodesic curvature in a closed oriented Riemannian surface. This problem was famously posed by Arnold (see
[
Arn04
, Problem 1981-9]
), and it has inspired an abundance of research using techniques from many fields of mathematics (for a few examples, see
[
Gin87
]
,
[
Gin96
]
,
[
Sch11
]
,
[
AB16
]
,
[
CZ21
]
).

We focus on the problem of finding closed
embedded
curves of constant curvature. From the dynamical perspective, these objects correspond to
simple
closed orbits of the flow of electrons in a constant magnetic field. As a motivating problem, it is a long-standing conjecture of Novikov (see
[
Nov82
, §5]
) that every metric on the 2-sphere admits a closed embedded curve of constant curvature
c
for every
c>0
.

In this paper, we take a variational approach to the existence problem. Namely, we use min-max techniques to produce non-singular critical points of the functional

\mathcal{A}^{c}(\Omega):=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)-c\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega).

The Euler-Lagrange equation for this functional demonstrates that any non-singular critical point is a domain bounded by a closed curve of curvature
c
.

Using the Almgren-Pitts min-max framework, as developed for the
\mathcal{A}^{c}
functional for curves in surfaces by
[
ZZ20
]
and
[
KL18
]
, we obtain the following general existence result. For convenience, we define

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{2},g)
be a smooth closed oriented Riemannian surface and
c>0
. If

(1.1)

c>\mathrm{ct}_{\min K_{g}}(\mathrm{inj}(M,g)),

then there is a nontrivial closed embedded curve of constant curvature
c
in
(M,g)
with
\mathcal{A}^{c}
Morse index 1.

In §
2
, we develop examples that demonstrate the ways in which Theorem
1.1
is sharp. For instance, we show that Theorem
1.1
is sharp for flat tori. It is also sharp for closed hyperbolic surfaces of large injectivity radius and large

1.1
is sharp for the method of proof.

We obtain a stronger result in positive ambient curvature. We summarize the result with a slightly weakened but simplified statement here. We refer the reader to (
8.13

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(S^{2},g)
be a smooth Riemannian 2-sphere with
K_{g}>0
, and let
c>0
. If

(1.2)

\min K_{g}\geq\frac{1}{8}\max K_{g}\ \ \mathrm{or}\ \ c\geq\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\max K_{g}},

then there is a nontrivial closed embedded curve of constant curvature
c
in
(S^{2},g)
with
\mathcal{A}^{c}
Morse index 1.

We expect that Theorem

[

1.2.
Strategy of proof

By the combined work of
[
ZZ20
]
and
[
KL18
]
, Almgren-Pitts min-max for
\mathcal{A}^{c}
produces a set
\Omega
whose boundary is either:

(1)

a closed embedded curve of constant curvature
c
(with respect to
\Omega
),

(2)

a closed almost-embedded curve of constant curvature
c
(with respect to
\Omega
) with at least one self-touching point,

(3)

a network of curves of constant curvature
c
(with respect to
\Omega
) with one stationary node of degree 4.

Our strategy is to find conditions under which (2) and (3) cannot occur.

In case (3), there are two possible configurations, according to whether
\Omega
is disconnected (called “Configuration 1”) or connected (called “Configuration 2”). We refer the reader to Figure
3
for a picture of these configurations. We observe that case (2) can be handled simultaneously with Configuration 2, so it suffices to rule out these two configurations.

Configuration 1:
We construct a competitor sweepout using the second variation of
\mathcal{A}^{c}
and a cut-and-paste procedure similar to
[
KL18
, §4]
, and conclude that any curve in Configuration 1 cannot achieve the first min-max width when
\min K_{g}+c^{2}\geq 0
. Essentially, we find a graphical perturbation of a curve in Configuration 1 that decreases
\mathcal{A}^{c}
, and from this new curve we construct a sweepout by resolving the self intersection in two different ways. Figure
9
demonstrates the basic strategy of this construction (in particular, see steps (3) and (4) in Figure
9
).

Remark 1.7
.

The competitor sweepout we construct is very similar to the construction of
[
CC92
]
in the case of geodesics. We also observe that similar ideas are used in
[
BW22
]
, although the setting of curves differs from the higher dimensional cases they consider in some important ways. Most importantly, the capacity argument of
[
BW22
]
fails for curves.

Configuration 2 (in general):
In this configuration, the second variation formula for
\mathcal{A}^{c}
has a bad term, so we cannot conclude as easily as in Configuration 1. Instead, we rule out this configuration
only
using geometric considerations; namely, we completely ignore the fact that the configuration would have to achieve the min-max width.

We view
\Omega
(which is connected in Configuration 2) as a surface with strictly
c
-convex boundary. We note that there are distinct points
p,\ q
on the boundary that correspond to the same point in
M
(i.e. a self touching point/node). Due to the convexity of the boundary, there is a length minimizing geodesic
\sigma
in
\Omega
from
p
to
q
.

•

In
(M,g)
,
\sigma
is a geodesic loop, so we have
\mathrm{length}(\sigma)\geq 2\,\mathrm{inj}(M,g)
.

•

By a classical comparison geometry result of
[
Dek77
]
,
\mathrm{length}(\sigma)
is bounded from above by the diameter of a closed curve of curvature
c
in the model space of constant Gaussian curvature
\min K_{g}
.

These estimates contradict each other under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 1.8
.

The proof in
[
Sch11
]
also relies on the same geometric considerations, but
[
Sch11
]
uses a weaker upper bound for the length of
\sigma
. More precisely,
[
Sch11
]
observes that
\sigma
is a stable curve and applies Bonnet-Myers when
K_{g}>0
. By replacing this estimate with the estimate of
[
Dek77
]
, we obtain the same conclusions as
[
Sch11
]
under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
when
M=S^{2}
.

Configuration 2 (in positive curvature):
In positive ambient curvature, we look for a non-graphical perturbation to construct a good competitor sweepout for curves in Configuration 2. In particular, we split the figure-eight curve
\partial\Omega
into the union of two embedded loops
\gamma^{1}
and
\gamma^{2}
, and construct the competitor sweepout in three steps.

(1)

We use the curve shortening flow to construct a path between a point curve and
\gamma^{1}
.

(2)

We choose a path of curves between
\gamma^{1}
and
\gamma^{2}
using a min-max procedure for length.

(3)

We choose a path of curves between
\gamma^{2}
and a point curve using constrained minimization for
\mathcal{A}^{c}
.

We refer the reader to Figure
11
for an illustration of this sweepout. Since the curve shortening flow is length nonincreasing, the maximum length of curves in path (1) is
\mathrm{length}(\gamma^{1})
. Using an estimate of
[
Dek77
]
, we obtain a good upper bound on the length of curves in path (2). Finally,
[
KL18
, Lemma 3.2]
yields good
\mathcal{A}^{c}
bounds on path (3).

These estimates give an upper bound for the first
c
min-max width. Combined with the lower bounds from the general case of Configuration 2, we deduce that a curve in Configuration 2 cannot achieve the first
c
min-max width under the conditions of Theorem
1.2
(or more specifically under conditions (
8.13
).

Remark 1.9
.

The are two non-sharp estimates used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(1)

The lower bound for the length of
\gamma^{1}
and
\gamma^{2}
: we ignore the fact that these curves have curvature
c
and not 0.

(2)

The upper bound for the
c
min-max width: we ignore volume terms in the
\mathcal{A}^{c}
value of the competitor sweepout during the curve shortening flow piece (i.e. path (1)).

While implementing these ideas would yield a slight improvement in the minimal curvature pinching for which we can solve the conjecture of Novikov, there is no hope of attacking the conjecture for all metrics of positive curvature along these lines because the sharper estimates would degenerate to our weaker estimates as
c\to 0
. Indeed, Figure
2
demonstrates that our argument only works for
c\ll 1
for metrics with at least
1/16
-curvature pinching.

2.
Examples

We explore a few examples to characterize the sharpness of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Flat tori

Let
\Lambda
be a rank 2 lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Without loss of generality we have
(0,0)\in\Lambda
and (by rotating)
(0,2r)\in\Lambda
is the shortest nonzero vector in
\Lambda
for some
r>0
. Then
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
is a flat torus with injectivity radius
r
.

If
c>1/r
, then
B_{1/c}(x_{0})
is compactly contained in the segment domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
, and its boundary is an embedded loop with constant curvature
c
.

Now suppose
c\leq 1/r
, and let
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
be a closed curve of constant curvature
c
. Then
\gamma
lifts to a circle
C
of radius
1/c
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
1/c\geq r
, there are two points
(x_{1},y_{1}),\ (x_{2},y_{2})\in C
satisfying

x_{1}=x_{2},\ y_{1}=y_{2}-2r.

Hence,
(x_{1},y_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2})
descend to the same point in
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda
, so
\gamma
is not embedded.

In these examples, there is a closed embedded curve of constant curvature
c
if and only if

c>\frac{1}{\mathrm{inj}(\mathbb{R}^{2}/\Lambda)}.

Hence, Theorem
1.1
is sharp for flat tori.

2.2.
Hyperbolic surfaces

We recall two facts about closed hyperbolic surfaces.

First, there is no closed curve of geodesic curvature 1. A simple proof of this fact is contained in
[
Gin96
, Example 3.7]
.

Second, there is a sequence of closed hyperbolic surfaces
(M_{i},g_{i})
with
\mathrm{inj}(M_{i},g_{i})\to\infty
. There are several different constructions of such examples, for instance
[
Bus78
, Theorem A]
(see also
[
BS94
]
).

For a sequence as above, we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\coth(\mathrm{inj}(M_{i},g_{i}))=1.

Hence, Theorem
1.1
is sharp for smooth closed hyperbolic surfaces of large injectivity radius and
c
large.

\mathbb{R}^{3}

0\leq K_{g^{*}}\leq 1
in the weak sense and
\mathrm{inj}(S^{2},g^{*})=\pi

3

We now conclude the proof of Theorem

The result we shall need, which follows from the Corollary to Theorem 1 in

(
[

]
)

Let

is

•

,

\mathrm{length}(\sigma)\leq 2R_{0}(c,k),

where

1/c&k=0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-k}}\coth^{-1}\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{-k}}\right)&k<0.\end{cases}

As in §
6.2
, we note that, in the case of Configuration 2,
\Omega
is a connected region in

-convex boundary

. Let

and

Theorem A.5
.

Suppose (
A.10
) is satisfied, then there exists a sequence
\{\Gamma^{j}\}\subset\mathcal{S}
of sweepouts such that
\max_{t\in[0,1]}\length(\Gamma^{j}_{t})\to W_{0}

Proof.

Let
\{\Gamma^{j}\}\subset\mathcal{S}
be such that
\max_{t\in[0,1]}\length(\Gamma^{j}_{t})\leq W_{0}+1/j
. By applying the curve shortening map, we obtain a new sequence
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{j}_{t}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\Lambda}
, and
\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j}_{t})\leq\length(\Gamma^{j}_{t})
. By (
A.12
),

W_{0}=W^{\Lambda}_{0}\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j}_{t})\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}\length(\Gamma^{j}_{t})\leq W_{0}+1/j,

so

\lim_{j\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j}_{t})=W_{0}.

Assume now for a contradiction that
\{\tilde{\Gamma}^{j}_{t}\}
does not satisfy the second part of the Theorem. Then we can find
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and sequences
\delta_{i}\to 0
,
j_{i}>1/\delta_{i}
,
t_{i}\in[0,1]
such that
\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})>W_{0}-\delta_{i}
but
\dist(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}},G)\geq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Note that

(A.13)

W_{0}-\delta_{i}<\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})=\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})\leq\length(\Gamma^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})\leq W_{0}+\frac{1}{j_{i}}\leq W_{0}+\delta_{i}

Thus, by Property (3) in Theorem

\dist(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}},\Gamma^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}
and

\dist(\Gamma^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}},G)\geq\dist(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}},G)-\dist(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}},\Gamma^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2},

thus contradicting Property (5) in Theorem

\length(\Gamma^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})-\length(\tilde{\Gamma}^{j_{i}}_{t_{i}})\to 0

By applying the procedure in Remark

to

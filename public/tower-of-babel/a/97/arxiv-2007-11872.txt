On the Lefschetz standard conjecture for Lagrangian covered hyper-Kähler varieties
Claire Voisin

Abstract
We investigate the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree 2 2 cohomology of hyper-Kähler manifolds admitting a covering by Lagrangian subvarieties. In the case of a Lagrangian fibration, we show that the Lefschetz standard conjecture is implied by the SYZ conjecture characterizing classes of divisors associated with Lagrangian fibration. In dimension 4 4 , we consider the more general case of a Lagrangian covered fourfold X X , and prove the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree 2 2 , assuming ρ ⁡ ( X ) = 1 \rho(X)=1 and X X is general in moduli. Finally we discuss various links between Lefschetz cycles and the study of the rational equivalence of points and Bloch-Beilinson type filtrations, giving a general interpretation of a recent intriguing result of Marian and Zhao.

Introduction

The Lefschetz standard conjecture
[
18
]
for degree
k
cohomology of a smooth projective variety of dimension

N\geq k
equipped with an ample line bundle
H_{X}
asks whether there exists a
codimension
k
-cycle
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}}\in{\rm CH}^{k}(X\times X)
such that, denoting
[\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}}]\in H^{2k}(X\times X,\mathbb{Q})
the cohomology class of
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}}
,

[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}:H^{2N-k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})

is the inverse of the Lefschetz isomorphism

h_{X}^{N-k}\cup:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2N-k}(X,\mathbb{Q}),

where
h_{X}=c_{1}(H_{X})
.

Remark 0.1
.

The Lefschetz standard conjecture is in fact independent of the choice of polarization
h_{X}
and it can be formulated without any reference to this choice. Indeed, it is equivalent to the existence of a cycle
\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}
such that
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}:H^{2N-k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is an isomorphism. However, its implications rely on the Hodge-Riemann-Lefschetz theory which uses an explicit polarization.

Remark 0.2
.

When
k=2
, it suffices to show that
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}:H^{N,N-2}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(X)

is the inverse of the Lefschetz isomorphism

h_{X}^{N-2}\cup:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{N,N-2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Indeed, the equality of morphisms
of Hodge structures
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}\circ h_{X}^{N-2}\cup=Id
is true on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
once it is true on
H^{2,0}(X)
, and furthermore we have
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})=H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\oplus{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
where the direct sum is orthogonal with respect to the nondegenerate Lefschetz intersection pairing
(\alpha,\beta)_{h_{X}}=\langle\alpha,h_{X}^{N-2}\beta\rangle_{X}
. Once we have a cycle
\mathcal{Z}
satisfying
[\mathcal{Z}]^{*}\circ h_{X}^{N-2}\cup=Id
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
, it is easy to construct
\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}
by adding to

\mathcal{Z}
a decomposable cycle in
{\rm NS}(X)\otimes{\rm NS}(X)
, so that
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}\circ h_{X}^{N-2}\cup=Id
holds on the whole
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

This conjecture is essential for the theory of motives as it allows to realize motivically the Lefschetz decomposition, which in turn implies semisimplicity results for cohomological motives. The Lefschetz standard conjecture also implies the variational Hodge conjecture (see
[
39
]
). Except for hyper-Kähler manifolds that we will discuss in this paper,
the main class of varieties for which the Lefschetz standard conjecture is known is the class of abelian varieties by work of Lieberman
[
21
]
.
In degree

-cycles modulo rational equivalence.

We now turn to the more general case of a Lagrangian covering, with the following definition.

Definition 0.5

A covering of a hyper-Kähler manifold

&B&&\end{matrix}

In practice, we can assume that the map

Example 0.6

If

It is not known if such a covering exists for any projective hyper-Kähler manifold but it exists on

Example 0.7

Let

be a smooth cubic fourfold and

be its Fano variety of lines. This is a hyper-Kähler fourfold by

For any hyperplane

Theorem 0.8

Let

Remark 0.9

The Lefschetz cycle

Corollary 0.10

Let

Proof.

By using the deformations of

, which provides a correspondence

\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}\in{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X^{\prime}\times X^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}]^{*}({h^{\prime}}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X^{\prime}})=c^{\prime}_{1}(l^{\prime}_{X^{\prime}})^{n-1}\sigma_{X^{\prime}},

(9)

where
h^{\prime}=(\psi^{-1})^{*}h
.

By the work of Huybrechts
[
12
]
, there exist cycles
R_{\psi}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X^{\prime}),\,R_{\psi}^{-1}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X^{\prime}\times X)
which act as
\psi^{*}
,
(\psi^{-1})^{*}
on degree
2
cohomology and are constructed by adding corrections
to the graphs of
\psi
and
\psi^{-1}
, and whose action on cohomology is compatible with cup-product. Let

\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime},X}:=R_{\psi}^{-1}\circ\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}\circ R_{\psi}\in{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X\times X).

Then (
9
), together with the compatibility of the action of
[R_{\psi}]
with cup-product which gives
[R_{\psi}^{-1}]^{*}({h}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})={h^{\prime}}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X^{\prime}},\,[R_{\psi}]^{*}((l^{\prime}_{X^{\prime}})^{n-1}\sigma_{X^{\prime}})={l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X}
,
shows that

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime},X}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=c^{\prime}_{1}{l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X},

(10)

Let now

\Gamma_{\rm lef}:=\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime},X}\circ{{}^{t}\Gamma_{l}}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X).

The proof of the Lefschetz standard conjecture in this case concludes, using Remark
0.2
, with the following lemma.

Lemma 1.5
.

One has
[\Gamma_{\rm lef}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=\mu\sigma_{X}
with
\mu\not=0
.

Proof.

One has
[\Gamma_{\rm lef}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{\pi}]^{*}([\Gamma_{\pi^{\prime},X}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X}))
and by (
10
), this is equal to
c^{\prime}_{1}[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{\pi}]^{*}({l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X})
, so we have to prove that

[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{\pi}]^{*}({l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X})\not=0
. It clearly suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\langle[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{\pi}]^{*}({l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X}),h^{2n-2}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle_{X}\not=0.

(11)

But
\langle[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{\pi}]^{*}({l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X}),h^{2n-2}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle_{X}=\langle{l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X},[\Gamma_{\pi}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\overline{\sigma_{X}})\rangle_{X}
, which by Proposition
1.2
, equals

c_{1}\langle{l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X},l^{n-1}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle_{X}
.
Thus the result follows from
\int_{X}(ll^{\prime})^{n-1}\sigma_{X}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\not=0
, which follows from the second Hodge-Riemann relations, since, by (
5
), this is a nonzero multiple of
\int_{X}h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X}\overline{\sigma_{X}}
.
∎

Case
2
:
There is an effective divisor
\Theta
on
X
which satisfies
q(\theta)<0
.
In this case, we claim that

does not

(see also

and this implies that

and

a^{2}q(\theta)+2abq(l,\theta)=0.

As

and

As

It follows from the claim that we must have

, that is, we are in situation (b).

We can now in both cases assume that we are in situation (b) and conclude the proof of Theorem
0.4
. We have
l^{\prime}=(R^{-1})^{*}l
and we know that

is a Lagrangian class on
X
and
R^{-1}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X)
is a correspondence whose action in cohomology preserves the cup-product and is the identity on

1.4
). Let
\Gamma_{\pi}\in{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X\times X)

Then, by Proposition
1.2
, we have

[\Gamma_{\pi}]^{*}(h^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=c_{1}l^{n-1}\sigma_{X}
, hence, if

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{l^{\prime}}]^{*}({h^{\prime}}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=c_{1}{l^{\prime}}^{n-1}\sigma_{X}.

(12)

We can thus apply the same argument as before, replacing

by
\Gamma_{l^{\prime}}
and defining

The analog of Lemma

∎

In the case of dimension
4
, we have the following alternative proof of Theorem
0.4
giving a slightly different statement.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler fourfold admitting a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:X\rightarrow B
. Assume there exists a
uniruled divisor
\Theta\subset X
such that
q(\theta,l)\not=0
. Then

X
satisfies the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree
2
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\Theta}
be a desingularization of
\Theta
and
let
p:\widetilde{\Theta}\rightarrow S
be the maximal rationally connected fibration of
\widetilde{\Theta}
. Then
S
is a surface since

\widetilde{\Theta}
has a nonzero holomorphic
2
-form pulled-back from
X
. We claim that the condition
q(\theta,l)\not=0
implies that

\pi_{\mid\Theta}:{\Theta}\rightarrow B
is surjective. Indeed, if
\pi_{\mid\Theta}:{\Theta}\rightarrow B
is not surjective, then

l^{2}\theta^{2}=0
and this implies
q(l,\theta)=0
by the following argument. The Beauville-Fujiki relations give, for a nonzero rational constant

(\alpha l+\beta\theta)^{4}=c_{X}q(\alpha l+\beta\theta)^{2}=c_{X}(2\alpha\beta q(l,\theta)+\beta^{2}q(\theta))^{2},

and comparing the coefficients in

we get that

is proportional to

, which proves the claim.
We then construct as in
[
43
]
a surface decomposition for
X
using
the “sum map up to isogeny”
\mu:X\times_{B}X\dashrightarrow X
, for which we only need to use a surface

providing a multisection of
\pi
.
The surface decomposition is given by the following construction: let

\Gamma=\widetilde{\Theta}\times_{B}\widetilde{\Theta},\,\,\psi=\mu_{\mid\widetilde{\Theta}\times_{B}\widetilde{\Theta}}:\Gamma\rightarrow X,

and
\phi:=(p,p)_{\mid\widetilde{\Theta}\times_{B}\widetilde{\Theta}}:\Gamma\rightarrow S\times S
.
The claim implies that
\psi
is surjective, hence generically finite since
{\rm dim}\,\Gamma=4
.
It is proved in
[
43
]
that these maps satisfy

\displaystyle\psi^{*}\sigma_{X}=\phi^{*}({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\sigma_{S,1}+{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\sigma_{S,2})

(13)

for some holomorphic forms
\sigma_{S,i}
on

.
As
\psi_{*}\psi^{*}\sigma_{X}=({\rm deg}\,\psi)\sigma_{X}
, (

1.1
.
∎

1.2
More general coverings by Lagrangian varieties

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
0.8
. First of all, we have the following criterion for the Lefschetz standard conjecture to hold in degree
2
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective
2n
-fold with polarization
h_{X}
and
let
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{n+1}(X\times X)
having the property that the intersection pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{X}
is nondegenerate on

[\Gamma]^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr})\subset H^{2n}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.
Then
X
satisfies the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology.

Proof.

We consider the codimension
2
cycle
\Gamma^{\prime}:={\Gamma}\circ{{}^{t}\Gamma}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X)
. We claim that

\displaystyle[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}:h_{X}^{2n-2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}

(14)

is an isomorphism. We note first that
[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}:h_{X}^{2n-2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
has image contained
in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
, so that (
14
) is well defined. This is because
[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}
is a morphism of Hodge structures and by definition of transcendental
cohomology, any Hodge substructure of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
which (after tensoring with
\mathbb{C}
) contains
H^{2,0}(X)
equals
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
. This implies that any morphism of Hodge structures from
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
to a trivial Hodge structure is
0
, hence the composition

[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}:h_{X}^{2n-2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}

is zero, where on the right, we see
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
as the quotient of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
by the space of rational Hodge classes on
X
.
Next, as both spaces in (
14
) have the same dimension, in order to prove the claim, it suffices to show that the intersection pairing

(\alpha,\beta):=\langle[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}\alpha,\beta\rangle_{X}

on
h_{X}^{2n-2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
is nondegenerate. But we have by definition of
\Gamma^{\prime}

\langle[\Gamma^{\prime}]^{*}\alpha,\beta\rangle_{X}=\langle[\Gamma]^{*}\alpha,[\Gamma]^{*}\beta\rangle_{X}

and the pairing defined by the right hand side is nondegenerate by assumption. The claim implies the proposition since
by restricting
\Gamma^{\prime}
to
X\times\Sigma
where
\Sigma\subset X
is a surface complete intersection of hypersurfaces of class
h_{X}
,
we find that
[\Gamma]^{*}:H^{2,0}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(X)
is surjective, so that Proposition
1.1
applies.
∎

Assume now that a hyper-Kähler manifold
X
admits a Lagrangian covering, given by a diagram as in Definition
0.5

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&L&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X\\
&\pi\downarrow&&\\
&B&&\end{matrix}

(15)

of smooth projective varieties, such that
\Phi
is dominant and, for a general point
b\in B
,

\Phi_{\mid L_{b}}
is generically finite to its image, which is a Lagrangian subvariety of
X
.

If
{\rm dim}\,X=2n
, so
{\rm dim}\,L_{b}=n
and
{\rm dim}\,B\geq n
,
as the map
\Phi_{\mid L_{b}}
is generically finite to its image, up to replacing
B
by a complete intersection of ample hypersurfaces, one can assume that
{\rm dim}\,L={\rm dim}\,X
and
\Phi
is dominant generically finite of degree
N
.
We now introduce a relative Poincaré divisor
d\in{\rm CH}^{1}(L\times_{B}L)
, that is, for general
b\in B
,

d_{b}\in{\rm Pic}(L_{b}\times L_{b})
has the property that
the map
y\in L_{b}\mapsto d_{b,y}\in{\rm Pic}(L_{b})
induces an isogeny
{\rm Alb}(L_{b})\rightarrow{\rm Pic}^{0}(L_{b})
. Using the fact that
\Phi_{\mid L_{b}}
is generically finite on its image for a general

, for an ample line bundle
H_{X}
on
X

\Phi^{*}H_{X\mid L_{b}}
is big hence gives a polarization on the Hodge structure on

, hence an isogeny
\eta:{\rm Alb}(X_{b})\rightarrow{\rm Pic}^{0}(X_{b})
. We thus have a natural rational map (which is a morphism over a Zariski open set of

\rho=(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}):L\times_{B}L\dashrightarrow{\rm Alb}(X/B)\times_{B}{\rm Pic}^{0}(X/B)

where
\rho_{1}
is an isogenous version of the relative Albanese map constructed as in the previous section using a multisection of
\pi
and
\rho_{2}=\eta\circ\rho_{1})
, and we can pull-back under

a relative Poincaré divisor on

we get a codimension
n+1

\Gamma_{L}\in{\rm CH}^{n+1}(L\times L)

Denote by
L^{1}H^{2,0}(L)\subset H^{2,0}(L)
the Leray level

L^{1}H^{2,0}(L)={\rm Ker}\,(H^{2,0}(L)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(L_{b})).

The fact that
\Phi:L\rightarrow X

\Phi^{*}\sigma_{X}\in L^{1}H^{2,0}(L)

Lemma 1.9
.

For any
\sigma\in L^{1}H^{2,0}(L)

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{L}]^{*}(({\Phi}^{*}h_{X}^{n-1})\sigma)=\lambda\sigma\,\,{\rm mod}\,\,\pi^{*}H^{2,0}(B)

(16)

for some nonzero rational coefficient

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of (
1

1.2
. The geometric context is slightly different since we were working in Proposition
1.2
with a family of abelian varieties. However, we can work above with the family of abelian varieties

which inherits a holomorphic
2
-form. The proof of (
1
) did not use indeed the fact that the holomorphic

-form is nondegenerate. Furthermore, as noticed in Remark
1.3
the proof given there proves this equality in general,
modulo
\pi^{*}H^{2,0}(B)
.
∎

Using the generically finite map
\Phi:L\rightarrow X
, we get a

n+1
-cycle

\displaystyle\Gamma_{L,X}:=(\Phi,\Phi)_{*}(\Gamma_{L}).

(17)

We have the following result

Proposition 1.10
.

In the situation above, assume that
\Phi_{*}(\pi^{*}H^{2,0}(B))=0
. Then

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{L,X}]^{*}(h_{X}^{n-1}\sigma_{X})=\lambda^{\prime}\sigma_{X}

(18)

for some nonzero coefficient

\displaystyle[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{L,X}]^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})\not=0\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{2n}(X,\mathbb{C}).

We apply (

Pushing forward this equation to

We have

\langle[{{}^{t}\Gamma}_{L,X}]^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X}),h_{X}^{n-1}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle_{X}=\langle h_{X}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X},[\Gamma_{L,X}]^{*}h_{X}^{n-1}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle_{X},

∎

We now prove Theorem
0.8
stated in the introduction, concerning the case of dimension

.

Proof of Theorem
0.8
.

Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler fourfold with
\rho(X)=1
admitting a covering by Lagrangian surfaces

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&L&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}&X\\
&\pi\downarrow&&\\
&B&&\end{matrix},

(20)

where we can assume that
B
is a surface, up to replacing
B
by a complete intersection in
B
.
We perform the construction described above, which provides us with the cycle
\Gamma_{L,X}\in{\rm CH}^{3}(X\times X)
.
Suppose first that
\Phi_{*}(\pi^{*}H^{2,0}(B))\not=0
in
H^{2,0}(X)
. Then, the correspondence
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times B)
given by the Lagrangian cover
(
20
)
where

is a surface,
directly solves the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology of

, since dually

1.1
applies in this case.

We can thus assume that
\Phi_{*}(\pi^{*}H^{2,0}(B))=0
in

, so that Proposition
1.10
applies, and we conclude that the morphism of Hodge structures

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{L,X}]^{*}:h_{X}^{2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\rightarrow H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})

(21)

is not equal to
0
.

Assume now that
X
is very general in moduli, which is part of the assumptions of Theorem
0.8
. Then, by the local surjectivity of the period map, the Mumford-Tate group of the Hodge structure on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
is the whole orthogonal group of the Beauville-Bogomolov quadratic form. We now have

Lemma 1.11
.

If the Hodge structure on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
has maximal Mumford-Tate group, any morphism of Hodge
structure
\phi:H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\rightarrow H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is given by cup-product by an element

h\in{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

We first start with the following easy

Lemma 1.12
.

If
X
is hyper-Kähler of dimension
4
, the Hodge substructure on
the orthogonal complement
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\perp SH^{2}}
of
{\rm Sym}^{2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
in
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})
consists of Hodge classes.

Indeed, the cup-product map

\sigma_{X}\cup:H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X})\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\Omega_{X}^{3})=H^{3,1}(X)

is an isomorphism, hence the Hodge structure on

H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\perp SH^{2}}
has
H^{4,0}=0,\,H^{3,1}=0
.
∎

We conclude that the Hodge structure on
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})

decomposes as

\displaystyle H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})={\rm Sym}^{2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\oplus{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}\oplus K,

(22)

where

consists of Hodge classes coming either from
{\rm Sym}^{2}{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
or from
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})^{\perp SH^{2}}
.
The fact that the Mumford-Tate group of the Hodge structure on

is the sum of the

generated by the Hodge class
c_{BB}
giving the Beauville-Bogomolov

{\rm Sym}^{2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}^{0}
of Hodge niveau

. In particular, there is no nontrivial morphism of Hodge structures from
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
to
{\rm Sym}^{2}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}^{0}
. As there is no nontrivial morphism of Hodge structures from

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
to a trivial Hodge structure, it follows that
the morphism
\phi
takes value in
{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})_{tr}
. As the Mumford-Tate group of the Hodge structure on

is given by cup-product by an element of
{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We now conclude the proof. As

, the divisor class

provided by Lemma

is a nonzero multiple of

. Thus we have by the nonvanishing of

.
It then follows from the second Hodge-Riemann relations that

\langle[\Gamma_{L,X}]^{*}\sigma_{X},[\Gamma_{L,X}]^{*}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\rangle\not=0.

But then the intersection pairing on

2.1
Zero-cycles and the Lefschetz standard conjecture for
H^{2}

We wish to discuss in this section the link between Bloch-Beilinson type filtrations on the group

{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
of
0
-cycles and the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree
2
.
Given a smooth projective variety
X
, we define as usual

F^{1}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)={\rm CH}_{0}(X)_{hom},\,F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X):={\rm Ker}\,({\rm alb}_{X}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X)_{hom}\rightarrow({\rm Alb}\,X)_{\mathbb{Q}}).

Several proposals have been made in order to construct

F^{3}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.
In
[
33
]
, Sh. Saito consider a subgroup that we will denote by

F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
and is (essentially) defined as follows.

\displaystyle F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)=\langle{\rm Im}\,(\Gamma_{*}:F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X))

(23)

\displaystyle{\rm for\,\,all}\,\,\Gamma\,\,{\rm such\,\,that}\,\,0=\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(Y)\rangle.

Remark 2.1
.

In this definition,
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(Y\times X)
and all smooth projective varieties
Y
are considered, but one can also restrict to surfaces

Y
by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety. Then

F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y)=\langle{\rm Im}\,(\Gamma_{*}:F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Z)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)),\,\,{\rm where}\,\,Z\,\,{\rm is\,\,a\,\,surface}\rangle.

Proof.

Indeed, we can assume
{\rm dim}\,Y\geq 2
. Any
0
-cycle
z
of
Y
is supported on a surface

Z\subset Y
which is a smooth complete intersection of ample hypersurfaces. By Lefschetz theorem on hyperplane sections,
{\rm Alb}\,Z={\rm Alb}\,Y
, so if

{\rm alb}_{Y}(z)=0
,
{\rm alb}_{Z}(z)=0
.
∎

The definition of
F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}
given in (
23
) is essentially motivated by the axioms of the Bloch-Beilinson filtration
(see
[
6
]
). Indeed,
suppose
Y
is a surface, and
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(Y\times X)
is a correspondence satisfying the property that

\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(Y)
is zero. Then
one gets by Künneth decomposition on the surface
Y
(see
[
27
]
) that the class
[\Gamma]
of
\Gamma
decomposes as

\displaystyle[\Gamma]=\sum_{i}[\Gamma_{i}\times\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}]+[R_{1}]+[R_{2}]

(24)

for some cycles

\Gamma_{i}
, resp.
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
in
Y
, resp.
X
, such that
{\rm codim}\,\Gamma_{i}+{\rm codim}\,\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}=2
, where the cycle
R_{1}
is supported on
C_{1}\times X
for a curve
C_{1}\subset Y
, and the cycle
R_{2}
is supported on
Y\times C_{2}
for a curve
C_{2}\subset X
.
A decomposable cycle
\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}\times\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
and a cycle
R_{1}
supported on
C_{1}\times X
act trivially on
0
-cycles of degree
0
; the cycle
R_{2}
acts trivially on
0
-cycles of
Y
which are of degree
0
and annihilated by
{\rm alb}_{Y}
, that is, which belong to
F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y)
. Hence we get

\Gamma_{*}=(\Gamma-\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}\times\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}-R_{1}-R_{2})_{*}:F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y)\rightarrow F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X).

As the cycle
\Gamma-\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}\times\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}-R_{1}-R_{2}
is cohomologous to
0
by (
24
), the image
\Gamma_{*}(F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y))
must be contained in the
F^{3}
level of the Bloch-Beilinson filtration.

We now turn to a different definition, introduced in
[
37
]
, also a candidate for the third level of the Bloch-Beilinson filtration.

\displaystyle F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X):=\cap_{\Sigma,\Gamma}{\rm Ker}\,(\Gamma_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)),

(25)

where the intersection is over all smooth projective surfaces
\Sigma
and correspondences
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times\Sigma)
.
Again, this definition is dictated by the Bloch-Beilinson axioms since
\Gamma_{*}
should preserve the Bloch-Beilinson filtration and for a Bloch-Beilinson filtration
F
,
F^{3}{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)=0
for any smooth projective surface.

Remark 2.3
.

It is obvious from the definition that
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(S)=0
for a smooth projective surface
S
.

Let us recall the statement of the Bloch conjecture for
0
-cycles on a surfaces (see
[
6
]
):

Conjecture 2.4
.

(Bloch) Let
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(S\times T)
where
T
and
S
are smooth projective surfaces.
Then if
\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(T)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(S)
is zero,
\Gamma_{*}:F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(S)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(T)
is zero.

We now have the following implications.

Proposition 2.5
.

The inclusion
F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
holds for any
X
if and only if the Bloch conjecture
2.4
holds for
0
-cycles on surfaces.

Proof.

Using Remark
2.1
,
F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is generated by cycles
\Gamma_{*}z
, where

S
is a smooth projective surface,
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(S\times X)

has the property that

\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(S)
is zero, and
z\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)
. Let
w=\Gamma_{*}z
be such a cycle and let now

\Gamma^{\prime}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times\Sigma)
be a correspondence, where
\Sigma
is a smooth projective surface. Then

\Gamma^{\prime}_{*}w=(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z
and the correspondence

\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(S\times\Sigma)

has the property that

(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)^{*}:H^{2,0}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(S)
is zero. Hence if Bloch conjecture
2.4
holds,

(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z=0
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)
. Hence
w\in F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.

Conversely, assume the inclusion
F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
for any smooth projective
X
. Let
S,\,T
be smooth projective surfaces and let
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(S\times T)
such that
\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(T)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(S)
vanishes. Then by definition,

\Gamma_{*}(F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(S))\subset F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(T)

and
F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(T)\subset F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(T)
by our assumption. As
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(T)=0
by Remark
2.3
, we get
\Gamma_{*}(F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(S))=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

The inclusion
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
holds for
X
if
X
satisfies the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology.

Proof.

Assuming the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology on
X
, there exist
a surface
\Sigma
and correspondences

\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times\Sigma),\,\Gamma^{\prime}\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(\Sigma\times X)
such that

\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(X\times X)
acts as the identity on
H^{2,0}(X)
.
For any
z\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
,

\displaystyle z-(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z\in F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)

(26)

since the correspondence

\Delta_{X}-\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma
acts trivially on
H^{2,0}(X)
.
Let now
z\in F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
. Then
\Gamma_{*}z=0
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)
, hence

(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z=0
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
. Hence

z=z-(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z\in F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
by (
26
).
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

The Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology holds on
X
if and only if there exists a
surface
\Sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
such that
j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is surjective.

Proof.

The “only if” direction follows from the arguments given in the previous proof. Indeed, we proved that the Lefschetz standard conjecture implies the equality
(
26
) for
z\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, where the cycle
(\Gamma^{\prime}\circ\Gamma)_{*}z
is supported on a surface

S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
, namely, we can take for
S
the image in
X
of
{\rm Supp}\,\Gamma^{\prime}
by the second projection.
This equality thus says
F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is contained in the image of
j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(S)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F_{dR}^{3}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
. If furthermore
S
contains an ample complete intersection surface, then the map

j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(S)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is also surjective because the Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem implies that
j_{*}:{\rm Alb}\,S\rightarrow{\rm Alb}\,X
is surjective. Hence

j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(S)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F_{dR}^{3}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is surjective.

We now prove the reverse implication. Assume that there is a surface
\Sigma\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
such that
j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{dR}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is surjective. Then for a general point

x\in X
, there exist a smooth projective surface
Y
, a correspondence
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(Y\times X)
,
such that
\Gamma^{*}:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(Y)
is zero, a
0
-cycle
z_{x}\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(Y)
and a
0
-cycle
z^{\prime}_{x}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma)
such that

x=\Gamma_{*}(z_{x})+z^{\prime}_{x}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.

As it follows from Bloch-Srinivas arguments
[
7
]
, these data can be spread over a finite extension of the function
field of
X
, and this produces

(1) a smooth projective variety
\mathcal{Y}
, a dominant morphism
f:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow X
of relative dimension
2
and a
cycle

\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(\mathcal{Y}\times X)
, such that

\Gamma_{x}^{*}:H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(\mathcal{Y}_{x})
is
0
for a general point
x\in X
;

(2) two cycles

\mathcal{Z}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(\mathcal{Y}),\,\,\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times\Sigma),

such that
\mathcal{Z}_{x}\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{Y}_{x})
for a general point
x\in X
, and for some integer
N\not=0

\displaystyle N\Delta_{X}=(f,\Gamma)_{*}(\mathcal{Z})+(Id_{X},j)_{*}\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}+W\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(X\times X)

(27)

where
W\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(X\times X)
is a cycle which is supported on
D\times X
, for some divisor
D\subset X
.
We now examine how the various cycles appearing in (
27
) act on
H^{2,0}(X)
.
One has
[W]^{*}=0
on
H^{2,0}(X)
because
W
is supported on
D\times X
, for some divisor
D\subset X
.
Next we claim that the cycle
A:=(f,\Gamma)_{*}(\mathcal{Z})\in{\rm CH}^{{\rm dim}\,X}(X\times X)
satisfies

[A]^{*}(\eta)=0
for any
\eta\in H^{2,0}(X)
. To see this, we note that

\displaystyle[A]^{*}(\eta)=f_{*}([\mathcal{Z}]\cup\Gamma^{*}\eta),\,\,\,\forall\,\,\eta\in H^{2,0}(X).

(28)

Recalling that
\mathcal{Z}_{x}\in F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{Y}_{x})
, we get that, at least after restriction to
\mathcal{Y}_{U}=f^{-1}(U)
for some Zariski dense open set

U
of
X
,
[\mathcal{Z}]_{\mid\mathcal{Y}_{U}}\in L^{2}H^{4}(\mathcal{Y}_{U},\mathbb{Q})
, where
L^{\bullet}
denotes the Leray filtration associated with the map
f
. On the other hand, as
\Gamma^{*}\eta_{\mid\mathcal{Y}_{x}}=0
, we get that

[\Gamma^{*}\eta]\in L^{1}H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{U},\mathbb{C})
. It follows that

[\mathcal{Z}]\cup\Gamma^{*}\eta_{\mid\mathcal{Y}_{U}}\in L^{3}H^{6}(\mathcal{Y}_{U},\mathbb{C})\subset{\rm Ker}\,(f_{*}:H^{6}(\mathcal{Y}_{U},\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{2}(U,\mathbb{C})).

Thus the class
f_{*}([\mathcal{Z}]\cup\Gamma^{*}\eta)
vanishes in
H^{2}(U,\mathbb{C})
, hence vanishes in
H^{2,0}(X)
since the composite map

H^{2,0}(X)\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{2}(U,\mathbb{C})

is injective by coniveau (see
[
10
]
).
We thus conclude from the claim and (
27
) that for any
\eta\in H^{2,0}(X)
,

\displaystyle N\eta=[(Id_{X},j)_{*}\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}]^{*}\eta\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{2,0}(X).

(29)

But
[(Id_{X},j)_{*}\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}]^{*}\eta=[\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}]^{*}(j^{*}\eta)
, so (
29
) implies that

[\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}]^{*}:H^{2,0}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H^{2,0}(X)
is surjective.
Hence
X
satisfies the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree
2
by Proposition
1.1
.
∎

We conclude this section with the following result that will be used in next section.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X,\,Y
be smooth projective varieties, and let
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times Y)
.
Then for any
z\in F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
,
\Gamma_{*}z=0
in
{\rm CH}^{2}(Y)
.

Proof.

As
z\in F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, the cycle
w:=\Gamma_{*}z\in{\rm CH}^{2}(Y)
has the property that
for any surface
T\subset Y
,
w_{\mid T}=0
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(T)
. One then applies the following
theorem of Joshi
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety and let
w\in{\rm CH}^{2}(Y)
be a cycle such that

w_{\mid T}=0
for any surface
T\subset Y
. Then
w=0
.

∎

2.2
A conjecture on
0
-cycles of hyper-Kähler manifolds

We conclude with the formulation of a conjecture concerning the rational equivalence of points on a smooth projective
hyper-Kähler manifold
X
. Recall from Section
2.1
the filtration

F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\subset F^{2}{\rm CH}_{0}(X):={\rm Ker}\,({\rm alb}_{X}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X)_{hom}\rightarrow{\rm Alb}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}).

The rational equivalence of points on a hyper-Kähler manifold is a very intriguing subject, that started with work of Beauville
and the author
[
5
]
, and continued in
[
13
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
41
]
,
[
42
]
, and more recently in
the paper
[
22
]
which establishes the following remarkable result:

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a smooth projective moduli space of semistable sheaves on a projective
K3
surface
S
. Then
two points
[\mathcal{F}],\,[\mathcal{G}]\in\mathcal{M}
have the same rational equivalence class in
\mathcal{M}
if and only if

c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=c_{2}(\mathcal{G})
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
.

Of course, the condition is necessary, since, assuming for simplicity there is a universal sheaf

\mathcal{F}_{\rm univ}
on
\mathcal{M}\times S
, the Chern class

c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{\rm univ})\in{\rm CH}^{2}(\mathcal{M}\times S)

induces a group morphism
{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
which maps the class of
[\mathcal{F}]
to

c_{2}(\mathcal{F})
. The other direction is far from obvious and is a very intriguing statement. The following conjecture
is motivated by Theorem
2.10
.

Conjecture 2.11
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective manifold whose algebra of holomorphic forms is
generated in degree
\leq 2
(for example, a projective hyper-Kähler manifold). Then for
x,\,y\in X
,

x=y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
if and only if
x=y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.

Theorem
2.10
establishes the conjecture when
X=\mathcal{M}
is as above a smooth projective moduli space of sheaves on a
K3
surface. Indeed, if
[\mathcal{F}]=[\mathcal{G}]
in

{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{M})/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
, then
c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=c_{2}(\mathcal{G})
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(S)
by the definition (
25
) of
F^{3}_{BB}
, and using the existence of the correspondence
c_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{{\rm univ}})
. Thus Theorem
2.10
gives
[\mathcal{F}]=[\mathcal{G}]
in

{\rm CH}_{0}(\mathcal{M})
.

Let us observe that the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree
2
, the generalized Hodge conjecture, and the nilpotence conjecture together imply
Conjecture
2.11
. Let us explain this fact assuming, as this is the case when
X
is hyper-Kähler, that
{\rm dim}\,X=2n
and the algebra of holomorphic forms is generated in degree
2
, with
H^{2,0}(X)=\mathbb{C}\sigma_{X}
. Then

H^{i,0}(X)=0
for
i
odd and
H^{2i,0}(X)
is generated by
\sigma_{X}^{i}
. Denoting by
h_{X}\in{\rm NS}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
a polarizing class on
X
, and assuming the Lefschetz standard conjecture in degree
2
holds for
X
,
there exists a codimension
2
cycle
\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X)
such that

\displaystyle[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2}\sigma_{X})=\sigma_{X}.

(30)

Lemma 2.12
.

One has for any
i\leq n
,

\displaystyle[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}]^{*}h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i}=\lambda_{i}\sigma_{X}^{i},

(31)

for some nonzero rational number
\lambda_{i}
.

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]=[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{2,2}+[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{4,0}+[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{0,4},

(32)

where
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{i,j}\in H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\otimes H^{j}(X,\mathbb{Q})
denotes the
(i,j)
Künneth component of

[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]
. Note that the Künneth components are algebraic in this case, since
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{4,0}
, resp.

[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{0,4}
, identifies to the restriction to
X\times x
, resp.
x\times X
, of
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]
, where
x
is any point of
X
. Furthermore, developing the

i
-th power of (
32
), one sees immediately that

[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}]^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i})=(([\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{2,2})^{i})^{*}(h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i}).

In other words, we can assume that
[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]=[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]^{2,2}
.
Over
\mathbb{C}
, we have the Hodge decomposition

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}\sigma_{X}\oplus H^{1,1}(X)\oplus\overline{\mathbb{C}\sigma_{X}},

and the equation (
30
) then says that

\displaystyle[\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}]=\lambda({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\sigma_{X}\cup{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\overline{\sigma_{X}}+{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\overline{\sigma_{X}}\cup{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\sigma_{X})+R,

(33)

where the residual term
R
belongs to the term
H^{1,1}(X)\otimes H^{1,1}(X)
of the Hodge-Künneth decomposition of
H^{4}(X\times X,\mathbb{C})
.
When we take the
i
-th power of (
33
), only the term

\lambda^{i}({\rm pr}_{1}^{*}\sigma^{i}\cup{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\overline{\sigma}^{i})
acts nontrivially on
h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i}
and it acts on
h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i}
by multiplication by the coefficient
\lambda^{i}\int_{X}h_{X}^{2n-2i}\sigma_{X}^{i}\overline{\sigma_{X}}^{i}
, which proves the result.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

There exists a polynomial

P=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mu_{i}\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}\cdot{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}h_{x}^{2n-2i}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X),

such that

\displaystyle[P]^{*}\sigma_{X}^{i}=\sigma_{X}^{i}

(34)

for
i=0,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{i}:=\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}\cdot{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}h_{x}^{2n-2i}
. We have

[\Gamma_{i}]^{*}\sigma_{X}^{j}=0\,\,{\rm for}\,\,j>i,

[\Gamma_{i}]^{*}\sigma_{X}^{j}=\lambda_{ij}\sigma_{X}^{j}\,\,{\rm for}\,\,j\leq i,

with
\lambda_{j}\not=0
when
j=i
by Lemma
2.12
.
The matrix
M:=(\lambda_{ji})
is thus invertible, and our condition on the column vector
{}^{t}(\mu_{0},\ldots,\mu_{n})
is

M\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

∎

As the cycle

P=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}\cdot{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}h_{X}^{2n-2i}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X)

acts as the identity on
H^{*,0}(X)
,
the cycle
\Delta_{X}-P\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X)
acts as
0
on
H^{*,0}(X)
, so that its image

{\rm Im}\,[\Delta_{X}-P]^{*}:H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})

has Hodge coniveau
\geq 1
. Assuming the generalized Hodge conjecture, there should exist a divisor
D\subset X
and a
cycle
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X)
supported on
D\times X
such that

[\Delta_{X}-P]=[\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}]\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{4n}(X\times X,\mathbb{Q}).

Equivalently, the cycle
\Delta_{X}-P-\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is cohomologous to
0
, and the nilpotence conjecture

[
36
]
predicts that a power

(\Delta_{X}-P-\mathcal{Z}^{\prime})^{\circ N}
vanishes in
{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X)
, so in particular
the endomorphism

\phi:=(\Delta_{X}-P-\mathcal{Z}^{\prime})_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)

is nilpotent.
We observe that, as
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is supported on
D\times X
,
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}_{*}=0
on
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, so

\phi=Id_{{\rm CH}_{0}(X)}-P_{*}.

Now assume
x,\,y\in X
and
x=y
modulo
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
. Then as already noticed,

\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},x}=\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},y}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}^{2}(X),

hence

\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},x}^{i}=\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},y}^{i}
in
{\rm CH}^{2i}(X)
for any
i
, so that
P_{*}x=P_{*}y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.
It thus follows that
\phi(x-y)=x-y
. But
\phi
is nilpotent, so the last condition implies
x-y=0
, as we wanted.

Remark 2.14
.

One may wonder why this proof does not work as well to show (assuming the general conjectures) that for any
0
-cycle
z=\sum_{i}n_{i}x_{i}
of degree
0
,
if
z=0
modulo
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, then
z=0
, a statement that is clearly not expected
to be true since
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is expected to be a direct summand in the
{\rm CH}_{0}
of a surface. The reason is that for a cycle
x-y

P_{*}(x-y)=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}h_{X}^{2n-2i}(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},x}^{i}-\mathcal{Z}_{{lef},y}^{i})

is divisible by
\mathcal{Z}_{{lef},x}-\mathcal{Z}_{{lef},y}
, while
for general
0
-cycles of degree
0
,
P_{*}(z)
is not a priori divisible by
(\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef})_{*}(z)
.

The same argument as above gives the following statement linking the Lefschetz standard conjecture for degree
2
cohomology and Conjecture
2.11
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective manifold with a codimension
2
cycle
\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X)
such that there exists a polynomial
P(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\gamma_{i}y^{i}
with coefficients in
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm CH}^{2n-2i}(X)
, such that for any
x\in X
,

\displaystyle x=P(\mathcal{Z}_{x})\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(X).

(35)

Then
X
satisfies Conjecture
2.11
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
, a
0
-cycle
z\in F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
satisfies
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(z)=0
in
{\rm CH}^{2}(X)
.
If
x=y
modulo
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, we thus get
\mathcal{Z}_{x}=\mathcal{Z}_{y}
in
{\rm CH}^{2}(X)
and thus
(
35
) implies that
x=y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
.
∎

Proposition
2.15
applies when
X=F_{1}(Y)
is the Fano variety of lines of a smooth cubic fourfold
Y
(see
[
3
]
). Indeed in this case the following quadratic formula, which is a particular case of (
35
)

\displaystyle[l]=\alpha S_{l}^{2}+\Gamma.S_{l}+\gamma^{\prime}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(X),

(36)

is proved in

[
38
]
,
for any line
l\subset Y
, where
S_{l}\subset F_{1}(Y)
is the surface of lines meeting
l
.

This suggests that, in the hyper-Kähler case, one can formulate a conjecture even stronger than Conjecture
2.11

and we refer to
[
34
]
for a more precise formulation, namely that the cycle
\Delta_{X}-P-\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
that appears in the argument above is
in fact
0
, and not only nilpotent.

Conjecture 2.16
.

Let
X
be a projective hyper-Kähler manifold with polarization
h_{X}
. Then any Lefschetz cycle
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X)
for
h_{X}
satisfies a polynomial
equation

\displaystyle\Delta_{X}=P(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}})+W\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}^{2n}(X\times X),

(37)

where
P(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}})=\sum_{i}\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef}}^{i}{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\gamma_{i}
for some cycle
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm CH}^{2n-2i}(X)
, and

W
is supported on
D\times X
for some divisor
D\subset X
.

Turning to non hyper-Kähler projective manifolds, an easy example of a variety satisfying Conjecture
2.11
is a product of curves and surfaces. Indeed, if
X=\prod_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}
, where
S_{i}
is either a curve or a surface, and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in X
,
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in X
, then

x=y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
implies, using Definition (
25
), that
x_{i}=y_{i}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(S_{i})
, so
that
x=y
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
. Another class of varieties satisfying the conjecture appears in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
X
be a desingularization of the quotient
K=A/\pm Id
of an abelian variety by the
-1
-involution. Then
X
satisfies Conjecture
2.11
.

Proof.

We first observe that
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is isomorphic by pull-back to
the invariant part of
{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
under the involution
-Id
, so we only have to
show that if
x,\,y\in A
and

\{x\}+\{-x\}=\{y\}+\{-y\}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(A)/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
, then
\{x\}+\{-x\}=\{y\}+\{-y\}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
.
Here we denote as usual by
\{x\}
the
0
-cycle corresponding to the point
x
, so as to distinguish the addition in
A
and the addition in

{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
.
Let
\Theta
be an ample divisor on
A
, determining an isogeny
A\rightarrow\widehat{A}
,

x\mapsto D_{x}=\Theta_{x}-\Theta.

Recalling the Pontryagin product

*
defined on
0
-cycles of
A
by

z*z^{\prime}=\mu_{*}(z\times z^{\prime}),

where
\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A
is the sum map,
we have Beauville’s formulas in

[
2
, Proposition 6]
which give in particular the following equalities, for any
x\in A
and any integer
k
:

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{g-k}}{(g-k)!}D_{x}^{k}=\frac{\theta^{g}}{g!}*\gamma(x)^{*k},

(38)

where

\displaystyle\gamma(x):=\{0_{A}\}-\{x\}+\frac{1}{2}(\{0_{A}\}-\{x\})^{*2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{g}(\{0_{A}\}-\{x\})^{*g}=-{\rm log}(\{x\})\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(A).

(39)

Here the logarithm is taken with respect to the
Pontryagin product
*
and the expansion is finite because
0
-cycles of degree
0
are nilpotent for the Pontryagin
product. Note that
\{x\}*\{-x\}=\{0_{A}\}
, that is,
\{-x\}
is the inverse of
\{x\}
for the Pontryagin product, so that

\gamma(x)=-\gamma(-x)
.

We can assume that
\frac{\theta^{g}}{g!}=d\{0_{A}\}
for some nonzero integer
d
, so that the formula becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{g-k}}{(g-k)!}D_{x}^{k}=d\gamma(x)^{*k}

(40)

for any
k
.
We now add-up formulas (
40
) for
x
and
-x
. For odd
k
we get
0
, using

D_{x}=-D_{-x},\,\,\,\gamma(x)=-\gamma(-x),

and for even
k=2r
we get

\displaystyle 2\frac{\theta^{g-2r}}{(g-2r)!}D_{x}^{2r}=d\gamma(x)^{*2r}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(A).

(41)

We now observe that if
\{x\}+\{-x\}=\{y\}+\{-y\}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(A)/F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
, then, using Lemma
2.8
,

D_{x}^{2}=D_{y}^{2}
in
{\rm CH}^{2}(A)
, since clearly

x\mapsto D_{x}^{2}
is both
-1
-invariant and induced by a codimension
2
self-correspondence of
A
.
So we conclude from (
41
) that, for any
r

\displaystyle\gamma(x)^{*2r}=\gamma(y)^{*2r}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(A).

(42)

Finally, we have (using again nilpotence so that the formal series reduce in fact to finite sums).

\{x\}={\rm exp}(\gamma(x)),\,\,\{-x\}={\rm exp}(-\gamma(x))\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(A)

and similarly for
y
, so that

\displaystyle\{x\}+\{-x\}={\rm exp}(\gamma(x))+{\rm exp}(-\gamma(x))=2\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{\gamma(x)^{*2r}}{(2r)!}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(A),

(43)

and similarly for
y
. Equations (
43
) and (
42
) imply
\{x\}+\{-x\}=\{y\}+\{-y\}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(A)
.
∎

We conclude with the following analogue of Theorem
2.10
in the case of punctual Hilbert schemes.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth surface. Then for any
n
, and any
z,\,z^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{[n]}
,

z
is rationally equivalent to
z^{\prime}
in
\Sigma^{[n]}
if and only if the corresponding
0
-cycles

Z
,
Z^{\prime}
of
\Sigma
are rationally equivalent in
\Sigma
. A fortiori
\Sigma^{[n]}
satisfies Conjecture
2.11
since a
0
-cycle in
F^{3}_{BB}{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{[n]})
is annihilated
by
I_{*}
, where
I\subset\Sigma^{[n]})\times\Sigma
is the incidence correspondence.

Proof.

Note that
{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{[n]})={\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)})
for any
n
, so we will work with
\Sigma^{(n)}
. (It is a standard fact that the quotient singularities of
\Sigma^{(n)}
allow to do intersection theory at least with
\mathbb{Q}
-coefficients. The computations we make should be thought as computations in the invariant part under
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of the Chow groups of
\Sigma^{n}
.)
We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 2.19
.

For any integer
k\geq 0
and any
z\in\Sigma^{(k)}
, the addition

\mu_{z}:\Sigma^{(n)}\rightarrow\Sigma^{(k+n)},

w\mapsto w+z,

induces an injective map

\mu_{z\,*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)})\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n+k)})
.

Proof.

Reasoning by induction on
k
, it clearly suffices to prove the result when
k=1
, so
z
consists of one point
z\in\Sigma
.
The incidence correspondence

\Gamma_{n+1}\in{\rm CH}^{2n}(\Sigma^{(n+1)}\times\Sigma^{(n)})

given by the nested symmetric product (one could work with the Hilbert schemes at this point)
induces a morphism

\Gamma_{n+1\,*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n+1)})\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)}).

One clearly has

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n+1\,*}\circ\mu_{z\,*}=Id_{{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)})}+\mu_{z\,*}\circ\Gamma_{n\,*}.

(44)

A cycle in
{\rm Ker}\,\mu_{z\,*}
is thus in the image of
\mu_{z\,*}
. Using the equations (
44
) for

n-1,\,n-2,\ldots 1
, one proves similarly that
it is in the image of
\mu_{2z\,*},\,\mu_{3z\,*}
and finally that it is zero.
∎

Let now
z,\,z^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{[n]}
be two points such that the corresponding
0
-cycles
Z,\,Z^{\prime}
are rationally equivalent in
\Sigma
.
Then there exist an effective
0
-cycle
W\in\Sigma^{(k)}
and a rational curve in
\Sigma^{(n+k)}
passing through
the two points
\mu_{w}(z)
and
\mu_{w}(z^{\prime})
. Thus
\mu_{w\,*}(z)=\mu_{w\,*}(z^{\prime})
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n+k)})
. By Lemma

2.19
,
z=z^{\prime}
in
{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)})
.
∎

We can also prove Theorem
2.18
by establishing a polynomial formula of the form
(
37
) in this case and applying Proposition
2.15
. Concretely, for the symmetric product
X=\Sigma^{(n)}
, consider the codimension
2
cycle

\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}\in{\rm CH}^{2}(X\times X)
defined as the incidence
correspondence:

\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Sigma^{(n)}\times\Sigma^{(n)},\,Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\not=\emptyset\}.

Proposition 2.20
.

There exist cycles
\gamma_{i}\in{\rm CH}^{2n-2i}(X)
such that
the cycle
P=\sum_{i}\mathcal{Z}_{\rm lef}^{i}{\rm pr}_{2}^{*}\gamma_{i}
satisfies, for any
z\in X
,

\displaystyle z=P(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z})\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(X).

(45)

Proof.

It suffices to check equality (
45
) after pull-back to
\Sigma^{n}
. Let
z=x_{1}+\ldots+z_{n}\in\Sigma^{(n)}
and denote by
\tilde{z}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{n})
the pull-back of
\{z\}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{(n)})
. Then
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}=\sum_{i}x_{i}+\Sigma^{(n-1)}
. In order to compute the self-intersection of this cycle, we pull-back to
\Sigma^{n}
, and denote the resulting cycle by
\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime}
. We get by definition, assuming the points
x_{i}
are distinct

\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j}{\rm pr}_{j}^{*}x_{i}.

Using the fact that cycles
Z_{ij}:={\rm pr}_{j}^{*}x_{i}
satisfy
Z_{ij}Z_{kj}=0
for any
j,\,k
,
we get

(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime})^{n}=\sum_{f}Z_{f},

where
f
runs through the set of applications from
\{1,\ldots,n\}
to itself and

\displaystyle Z_{f}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}Z_{f(i)i}=(x_{f(1)},\ldots,x_{f(n)})\in{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{n}).

(46)

We now stratify the set of maps
f
according to their combinatorics. Up to the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, such a map is characterized by the image of
f
and the partition of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
given by the non-empty preimages
f^{-1}(s)\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The partition
(1,\ldots,1)
where all sets have cardinality
1
produce
the term
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)})
which is the pull-back
\tilde{z}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{n})
of
the point
z=x_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}\in\Sigma^{(n)}
.
The next case is the case of a partition
(2,1,\ldots,1)
for which exactly two points have the same image, the map
f
being injective on the remaining set. The contribution
of these partitions to (
46
) is the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{i\not=j,\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j+1}},\ldots,x_{n}),

(47)

where in each
n
-uple, exactly one point appears twice and another point is missing.
We observe
that (
47
) appears in the development of

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime})^{n-1}\cdot\Delta_{(2,1,\ldots,1)},

(48)

where the codimension
2
cycle
\Delta_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}
is the sum of the partial diagonals

\Delta_{ij}
where
y_{i}=y_{j}
.
Developing (
48
), we get that

\tilde{z}=(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime})^{n}-(\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm lef},z}^{\prime})^{n-1}\cdot\Delta_{(2,1,\ldots,1)}+R_{z}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}_{0}(\Sigma^{n}),

where the cycle
R_{z}
consists in a sum of terms
(x_{f(1)},\ldots,x_{f(n)})
for which the image of the map
f
has cardinality

\leq n-2
. More generally, one checks by induction on the cardinality of
f
that Proposition
2.20
holds, where one can take for the cycles
\gamma_{i}
a combination with rational coefficients of the diagonals

\Delta_{I}
where
|I|=n-i
. Here the cardinality of
I=\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\}
with
\{1,\ldots,n\}=I_{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup I_{k}
is the number

k
and the diagonal
\Delta_{I}\subset\Sigma^{n}
is defined by the equations
y_{j}=y_{k}
when
i,\,k\in I_{l}
for some
l
.
∎

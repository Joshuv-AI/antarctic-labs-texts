Abstract
. We introduce the concept of vector-valued holomorphic mappings on the complex unit disc that factor through a Hilbert space and state the main properties of the space formed by such Bloch mappings equipped with a natural norm: linearization, Bloch transposition, surjective and injective Banach ideal property, Kwapień-type characterization by Bloch domination, and duality.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
30H30, 46E15, 46E40, 47B38

Introduction

Bounded linear operators between Banach spaces that factor through a Hilbert space appeared in Grothendieck’s Résume
[
9
]
, with the name of
h
-integral operators. Their study was encouraged by Lindenstrauss and Pełczyński
[
13
]
, and their duality theory was addressed by Kwapień
[
11
]
. Basic results on this class of operators can be consulted in the monographs by Diestel, Jarchow and Tonge
[
7
, Chapter 7]
, Li and Queffélec
[
12
, Chapter 5]
and Pisier
[
15
, Chapter 2]
.

The extension of this theory to different nonlinear settings has been promoted by some authors. This is the case of completely bounded operators between operator spaces by Pisier
[
16
]
, Lipschitz mappings between metric spaces by Chávez-Domínguez
[
6
]
, multilinear operators between Banach spaces by Fernández-Unzueta and García-Hernández
[
8
]
, and
2
-dominated polynomials by Rueda and Sánchez Pérez
[
17
]
.

We will study in this note the factorization through a Hilbert space of vector-valued Bloch mappings on the complex unit open disc
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
be the space of all holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
. A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is said to be Bloch if there is a constant
c\geq 0
such that
(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq c
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. The normalized Bloch space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the Banach space of all mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
f(0)=0
so that

\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f):=\sup\left\{(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}<\infty,

equipped with the Bloch norm
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
. To simplify, we will write
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
instead of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
. Spaces of Bloch functions have been studied, among others, by Anderson, Clunie and Pommerenke
[
1
]
and Zhu
[
19
]
in the complex-valued case, and by Arregui and Blasco
[
3
,
4
]
in the vector-valued case.

Given two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
the Banach space of all bounded linear operators from
X
into
Y
, equipped with the operator canonical norm. As usual,
X^{*}
stands for
\mathcal{L}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is said to factor through a Hilbert space if there exists a Hilbert space
H
, a mapping
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},H)
and an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(H,X)
such that
f=T\circ g
, that is, the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{T}
\textstyle{\mathbb{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{X}

For such a
f
, we set
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\right\}
, where the infimum runs over all possible factorizations of
f
as above. We denote by
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
the linear space of all mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
for which
f
admits such a factorization.

The key tool to obtain our results is a linearization process of Bloch mappings on
\mathbb{D}
by using a predual space of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, called Bloch-free space over
\mathbb{D}
and denoted by
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
. We will devote Section
1
on preliminaries to recall this process that was introduced in
[
10
]
and applied to study
p
-summing Bloch mappings on
\mathbb{D}
in
[
5
]
. Once this is done, we will present the most important properties of
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}
-spaces in five sections.

In Section
2
, we characterize the factorization through a Hilbert space of a zero-preserving Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
by means of the linear factorization through a Hilbert space of its linearization from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
into
X
as well as of its Bloch transposition from
X^{*}
into
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Using this characterization, we prove in Section
3
that
[\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2},\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}]
is a surjective and injective Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings. In
[
5
]
, the concept of
p
-summing Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
was introduced and studied. We show that every
2
-summing Bloch mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
with
f(0)=0
factors through a Hilbert space.

Section
4
contains the main result of this paper which states a Bloch version of a known theorem of Kwapień
[
11
]
by characterizing holomorphic mappings that factor through a Hilbert space in terms of a Bloch subordination/domination criterion of finite sequences of elements of
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
.

Section
5
presents a convenient norm on the space of the so-called
X
-valued Bloch molecules on
\mathbb{D}
so that the dual of the completion of this last space is identified with
(\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
.

1.
Preliminaries

We first collect some useful results on the linearization of Bloch mappings borrowed from
[
10
]
. For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, a Bloch atom of
\mathbb{D}
is a bounded linear functional
\gamma_{z}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\gamma_{z}(f)=f^{\prime}(z)\qquad(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})).

The elements of
\mathrm{lin}(\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
are called Bloch molecules of
\mathbb{D}
. There exist other standard designations in the literature for Bloch atoms and Bloch molecules as, for example, point evaluations and characters or states, respectively. The Bloch-free Banach space over
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, is the norm-closed linear hull of
\left\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
. As usual, for any
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
T^{*}\colon Y^{*}\to X^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of
T
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
10
]

(i)

For every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function
f_{z}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

f_{z}(w)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})w}{1-\overline{z}w}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}),

belongs to
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})=1=(1-|z|^{2})f_{z}^{\prime}(z)
.

(ii)

The mapping
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
\Gamma(z)=\gamma_{z}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is holomorphic with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=1/(1-|z|^{2})
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

(iii)

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is isometrically isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
, via the evaluation mapping
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
given by

\Lambda(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})\qquad(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),\;\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))).

(iv)

For every holomorphic function
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
h(0)=0
, the composition operator
C_{h}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
C_{h}(f)=f\circ h
, is in
\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\leq||C_{h}||\leq 1
.

(v)

For every holomorphic function
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
such that
h(0)=0
, there exists a unique operator
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
such that
\widehat{h}\circ\Gamma=h^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma\circ h)
. In fact,
\left(\widehat{h}\right)^{*}\equiv C_{h}
.

(vi)

For every complex Banach space
X
and every mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, there exists a unique operator
S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
such that
S_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
||S_{f}||=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

(vii)

The correspondence
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

(viii)

Given
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, the map
f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
f^{t}(x^{*})=x^{*}\circ f
and called Bloch transpose of
f
, is in
\mathcal{L}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
with
\|f^{t}\|=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Furthermore,
f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}
.
\hfill\qed

We will now present the most important properties of vector-valued Bloch mappings on
\mathbb{D}
that factor through a Hilbert space.

2.
Linearization / Bloch transposition

We study the relationships of a map
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with both its linearization
S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and its Bloch transposition
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. Compare with
[
7
, Proposition 7.2]
.

Let us recall that given Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is said to factor through a Hilbert space if there exists a Hilbert space
H
and operators
B\in\mathcal{L}(X,H)
and
A\in\mathcal{L}(H,Y)
such that
T=A\circ B
. The set of all operators
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
admitting such a factorization is denoted by
\Gamma_{2}(X,Y)
, and it becomes a Banach space equipped with the norm
\gamma_{2}(T)=\inf\left\{\left\|A\right\|\left\|B\right\|\right\}
, where the infimum extends over all possible factorizations of
T
as above.

We begin with the following easy observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. Then
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
for all
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

Proof.

If
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then
f=T\circ g
, where
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},H)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(H,X)
for some Hilbert space
H
. We have

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|T(g^{\prime}(z))\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|(1-|z|^{2})\left\|g^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and therefore
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)
. Taking the infimum over all such factorizations of
f
, we obtain
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
factors through a Hilbert space.

(ii)

S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
factors through a Hilbert space.

(iii)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
factors through a Hilbert space.

In this case,
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma_{2}(S_{f})=\gamma_{2}(f^{t})
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then
f=T\circ g
, where
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},H)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(H,X)
for some Hilbert space
H
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have the factorizations
f^{\prime}=S_{f}\circ\Gamma
and
g^{\prime}=S_{g}\circ\Gamma
, where
S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and
S_{g}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),H)
. Hence
S_{f}\circ\Gamma=T\circ S_{g}\circ\Gamma
and this implies that
S_{f}=T\circ S_{g}
since
\Gamma(\mathbb{D})
is a dense linear subspace of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
. Therefore
S_{f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\gamma_{2}(S_{f})\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|S_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)
. Taking the infimum over all factorizations of
f^{\prime}
as above, we have
\gamma_{2}(S_{f})\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If
S_{f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
, then
S_{f}=T\circ S
, where
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),H)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(H,X)
for some Hilbert space
H
. Hence
f^{\prime}=S_{f}\circ\Gamma=T\circ S\circ\Gamma
by Theorem
1.1
. Since
S\circ\Gamma\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},H)
, Lemma 2.9 in
[
10
]
gave us a mapping
h\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},H)
with
h(0)=0
such that
h^{\prime}=S\circ\Gamma
. Since

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|h^{\prime}(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|S(\Gamma(z))\right\|\leq\left\|S\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},H)
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\leq\left\|S\right\|
. Hence
f^{\prime}=T\circ h^{\prime}
and therefore
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|S\right\|
. Taking the infimum over all factorizations of
S_{f}
, we obtain
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\leq\gamma_{2}(S_{f})
, as desired.

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
: Applying first
[
7
, Proposition 7.2]
, and then Theorem
1.1
with
[
7
, Theorem 7.1]
, we get

S_{f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)\Leftrightarrow(S_{f})^{*}\in\Gamma_{2}(X^{*},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\Gamma_{2}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})),

and, in this case,
\gamma_{2}(S_{f})=\gamma_{2}((S_{f})^{*})=\gamma_{2}(f^{t})
.

The last assertion of the statement follows immediately by applying that the correspondence
f\mapsto S_{f}
from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
into
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
is surjective and from what was proved above.
∎

3.
Ideal property

The concept of a Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings was introduced in
[
10
, Definition 5.11]
, inspired by the notion of Banach operator ideal due to Pietsch
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

[\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2},\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}]
is a surjective and injective Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings.

Proof.

We are going to show that
[\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2},\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}]
satisfies the properties of the aforementioned definition. Let
X
be a complex Banach space.

(N1):
(\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X),\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
is a Banach space with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\geq\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
for all
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

Proof:
It follows directly from Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
.

(N2): For every
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
, the mapping
g\cdot x\colon z\mapsto g(z)x
from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
is in
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(g\cdot x)=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
.

Proof:
It is easy to show that
g\cdot x\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
(g\cdot x)^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\cdot x
and
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
. Since
(g\cdot x)^{\prime}=(S_{g}\cdot x)\circ\Gamma
, it follows that
S_{g\cdot x}=S_{g}\cdot x
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=\left\|S_{g\cdot x}\right\|=\left\|S_{g}\cdot x\right\|=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
by Theorem
1.1
, and therefore
S_{g\cdot x}\in\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
, where
\mathscr{F}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
denotes the subspace of
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
formed by all finite-rank operators. Since
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
, we have
S_{g\cdot x}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and thus
g\cdot x\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(g\cdot x)=\left\|S_{g\cdot x}\right\|=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
by Theorem
2.2
. To obtain the reverse inequality, apply (N1).

(N3) Ideal property: If
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
,
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
where
Y
is a complex Banach space, then
T\circ f\circ h\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(T\circ f\circ h)\leq\|T\|\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
.

Proof:
Note that
S_{T\circ f\circ h}=T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
by
[
10
, Proposition 5.13]
, Theorem
2.2
and the ideal property of
[\Gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}]
(see
[
7
, Proposition 7.1]
). Then, by Theorem
2.2
,
T\circ f\circ h\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with

\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(T\circ f\circ h)=\gamma_{2}(T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h})\leq\left\|T\right\|\gamma_{2}(S_{f})||\widehat{h}||\leq\left\|T\right\|\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f).

(I) Injectivity: For any
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, any complex Banach space
Y
and any metric injection
\iota\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, we have
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(\iota\circ f)
whenever
\iota\circ f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},Y)
.

Proof:
Assume
\iota\circ f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},Y)
. Then
\iota\circ S_{f}=S_{\iota\circ f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
by
[
10
, Proposition 5.13]
and Theorem
2.2
. Since the Banach operator ideal
[\Gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}]
is injective by
[
7
, Proposition 7.3]
, it follows that
S_{f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\gamma_{2}(S_{f})=\gamma_{2}(\iota\circ S_{f})
or, equivalently,
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(\iota\circ f)
again by Theorem
2.2
.

(S) Surjectivity: For any
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and any holomorphic function
\pi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\pi(0)=0
such that
\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
is a metric surjection, we have
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f\circ\pi)
whenever
f\circ\pi\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

Proof:
Assume
f\circ\pi\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Since
S_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}=S_{f\circ\pi}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by
[
10
, Proposition 5.13]
and Theorem
2.2
, and the Banach operator ideal
[\Gamma_{2},\gamma_{2}]
is surjective by
[
7
, Proposition 7.3]
, we have
S_{f}\in\Gamma_{2}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\gamma_{2}(S_{f})=\gamma_{2}(S_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi})
, hence
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma_{2}(S_{f})
and thus
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma_{2}(S_{f\circ\pi})=\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f\circ\pi)
by Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Let us recall (see
[
5
]
) that a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
is said to be
2
-summing Bloch if there is a constant
c\geq 0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c\sup_{g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The infimum of such constants
c
, denoted by
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
, defines a complete norm on the linear space, denoted by
\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
, consisting of all
2
-summing Bloch mappings
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
for which
f(0)=0
.

If
f\in\Pi^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
, Theorem 1.6 in
[
5
]
provides us a regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\mu),\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
and a mapping
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{\infty}(\mu))
such that

\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,2}\circ h^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{\infty}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I_{\infty,2}}}{{\rightarrow}}L_{2}(\mu)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T}}{{\rightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}),

where
I_{\infty,2}\colon L_{\infty}(\mu)\to L_{2}(\mu)
is the formal inclusion operator and
\iota_{X}\colon X\to\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}})
is the isometric linear embedding defined by
\left\langle\iota_{X}(x),x^{*}\right\rangle=x^{*}(x)
. Moreover,
\pi^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(h)\right\}
, where the infimum is taken over all such factorizations of
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}
as above.

Since
(\iota_{X}\circ f)^{\prime}=\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}=T\circ I_{\infty,2}\circ h^{\prime}=(T\circ I_{\infty,2}\circ h)^{\prime}
and
(\iota_{X}\circ f)(0)=(T\circ I_{\infty,2}\circ h)(0)=0
, we deduce that
\iota_{X}\circ f=T\circ(I_{\infty,2}\circ h)
. Hence
\iota_{X}\circ f\in\Gamma_{2}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},\ell_{\infty}(B_{X^{*}}))
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(\iota_{X}\circ f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(h)
. By the injectivity of the Banach ideal
[\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2},\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}]
, it follows that
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)=\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(\iota_{X}\circ f)
. Moreover,
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(\iota_{X}\circ f)\leq\pi_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
by taking the infimum over all factorizations of
\iota_{X}\circ f^{\prime}
as above. Thus, we have proved the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Every
2
-summing Bloch mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
with
f(0)=0
factors through a Hilbert space and
\gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\pi_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.
\hfill\qed

4.
Bloch domination

We first introduce a Bloch subordination/domination criterion of finite sequences of elements of
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
(compare with
[
12
, Definition V.3]
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
. We say that
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is Bloch subordinated to
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
or, equivalently, Bloch dominated by
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
, and we write
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
, whenever

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}

for all
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

The following characterization of this criterion will be useful to us later.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is Bloch dominated by
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
.

(ii)

There exists an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{m},\ell_{2}^{n})
with
\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
, represented by a
n\times m
complex matrix
(a_{ij})
, such that
\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\mu_{j}\gamma_{w_{j}}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: We set

S=\left\{(\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\colon g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\right\}\subseteq\ell_{2}^{m}.

Assume
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
. Then the mapping
T_{0}\colon S\to\ell_{2}^{n}
given by

T_{0}\left((\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\right)=(\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n},

is well-defined. Clearly,
T_{0}
is linear and since

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\left((\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\|(\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{m}}

for all
(\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\in S
, then
T_{0}
is continuous with
\left\|T_{0}\right\|\leq 1
. By Hahn–Banach Theorem, there exists
T\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{m},\ell_{2}^{n})
with
\left\|T\right\|=\left\|T_{0}\right\|
such that
\left.T\right|_{S}=T_{0}
. Let
(a_{ij})
be the representation matrix of
T
with respect to the canonical bases of
\ell_{2}^{m}
and
\ell_{2}^{n}
, that is,

T((t_{j})_{j=1}^{m})=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{1j}t_{j},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{nj}t_{j}\right)^{t}

for all
(t_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in\ell_{2}^{m}
. Clearly,
for each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have

\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}(g)=\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\mu_{j}\gamma_{w_{j}}(g)

for all
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, and thus
\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}\mu_{j}\gamma_{w_{j}}
, as required.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Given
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, we have
(\lambda_{i},g^{\prime}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}=T((\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m})
, hence

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\lambda_{i},g^{\prime}(z_{i}))_{i=1}^{n}\right\|^{2}_{\ell_{2}^{n}}\leq\left\|T\right\|^{2}\left\|(\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\right\|^{2}_{\ell_{2}^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\mu_{j}g^{\prime}(w_{j}))_{j=1}^{m}\right\|^{2}_{\ell_{2}^{m}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}.

∎

The following result is a Bloch version of a classical Kwapień’s theorem
[
11
]
by means of Bloch dominated finite sequences of elements of
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
.

As usual,
B_{X}
denotes the closed unit ball of a Banach space
X
. Given a Hausdorff compact topological space
T
,
\mathscr{C}(T,\mathbb{K})
stands for the Banach space of all scalar-valued continuous functions on
T
, endowed with the supremum norm.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

f
factors through a Hilbert space.

(ii)

There exists a constant
c\geq 0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}},

whenever
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
with
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
.

In this case,
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
is the minimum of all constants
c
satisfying the preceding inequality.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Assume
f=T\circ g
, where
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},H)
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(H,X)
for some Hilbert space
H
with inner product
\langle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rangle
.
Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
such that
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
. We claim that for each
v\in H
, the function
h_{v}\colon z\mapsto\langle g(z)\,|\,v\rangle
from
\mathbb{D}
into
\mathbb{C}
belongs to
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Indeed,
h_{v}(0)=\langle g(0)\,|\,v\rangle=\langle 0\,|\,v\rangle=0
and
h_{v}
is Bloch with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(h_{v})\leq\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|v\right\|
since

(1-|z|^{2})\left|h_{v}^{\prime}(z)\right|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\langle g^{\prime}(z)\,|\,v\rangle\right|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|g^{\prime}(z)\right\|\left\|v\right\|\leq\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|v\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Consequently, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|\langle g^{\prime}(z_{i})\,|\,v\rangle\right|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|h_{v}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|h_{v}^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|\langle g^{\prime}(w_{j})\,|\,v\rangle\right|^{2}

for all
v\in H
. Let
(v_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
. Since

\left\|v\right\|^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\left|\langle v\,|\,v_{\alpha}\rangle\right|^{2}

for all
v\in H
, we deduce

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|T(g^{\prime}(z_{i}))\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|T\right\|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T\right\|^{2}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}.

And taking the infimum over all factorizations of
f^{\prime}
, we deduce

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
(ii)
holds. From Theorem
1.1
, we can consider the Hausdorff compact space
(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},w^{*})
. For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, define
\eta_{z}\colon B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\eta_{z}(k)=k^{\prime}(z)
for all
k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
. Clearly,
\eta_{z}\in\mathscr{C}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},\mathbb{C})
with
\left\|\eta_{z}\right\|_{\infty}=1/(1-|z|^{2})
. Consider the set
K_{1}\subseteq\mathscr{C}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},\mathbb{R})
formed by all functions of the form:

\Phi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|\eta_{z_{i}}\right|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|\eta_{w_{j}}\right|^{2},

where
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}>c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}

By concatenation, it is easy to see that
K_{1}
is convex. Condition in the statement (ii) assures that
\sup\left\{\Phi(k)\colon k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\right\}>0
for each
\Phi\in K_{1}
. Hence
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=\emptyset
, where

K_{2}=\left\{\Psi\in\mathscr{C}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},\mathbb{R})\colon\sup\left\{\Psi(k)\colon k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\right\}<0\right\}.

Clearly,
K_{2}
is an open convex subset of
\mathscr{C}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, the Hahn–Banach Separation Theorem and the Riesz Representation Theorem provide a non-zero signed Borel measure
\nu
on
B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and a real number
\alpha
such that

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\Psi(k)\ d\nu(k)<\alpha\leq\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\Phi(k)\ d\nu(k)\qquad(\Psi\in K_{2},\;\Phi\in K_{1}).

Since
r\Psi\in K_{2}
for all
r>0
, it follows that
\alpha\geq 0
. By the definition of
K_{2}
, we obtain

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\Psi(k)\ d\nu(k)\leq 0\qquad(\Psi\in K_{2}).

In particular,

\nu(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})=\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\ d\nu(k)\geq 0,

(in fact,
\nu(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})>0
since
\nu\neq 0
), and therefore
\nu
is a positive measure. Moreover, we have

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\Phi(k)\ d\nu(k)\geq 0\qquad(\Phi\in K_{1}).

Now, note that if
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
and
\left|\mu\right|/(1-|w|^{2})\leq 1
, we have

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\mu\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(w)\right|^{2}\ d\nu(k)\leq\nu(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}),

and therefore there exists

a:=\sup\left\{\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\mu\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(w)\right|^{2}\ d\nu(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\colon(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D},\,\frac{\left|\mu\right|}{1-|w|^{2}}\leq 1\right\}.

Furthermore,
a>0
. Otherwise, for all
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
such that
\left|\mu\right|/(1-|w|^{2})\leq 1
, we would have

\left\|\mu\eta_{w}\right\|_{L_{2}(\nu)}=\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\mu\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(w)\right|^{2}\ d\nu(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0,

that is,
\mu\eta_{w}=0
. In particular,
(1-|w|^{2})\eta_{w}=0
or, equivalently,
\eta_{w}=0
, but
\eta_{w}(f_{w})=f^{\prime}_{w}(w)=1/(1-|w|^{2})\neq 0
, a contradiction. Taking
\widehat{\nu}:=(c^{2}/a^{2})\nu
instead of
\nu
, we obtain

\sup\left\{\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\mu\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(w)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\colon(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D},\,\frac{\left|\mu\right|}{1-|w|^{2}}\leq 1\right\}=c.

Define
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})
by
h(z)=j_{2}(\eta_{z})
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
j_{2}\colon\mathscr{C}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})},\mathbb{C})\to L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})
is the formal inclusion operator. Note that
h\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},L_{2}(\widehat{\nu}))
. By
[
10
, Lemma 2.9]
, there exists a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},L_{2}(\widehat{\nu}))
with
g(0)=0
such that
g^{\prime}=h
. In fact,
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},L_{2}(\widehat{\nu}))
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\leq c
since

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|g^{\prime}(z)\right\|_{L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})}=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|h(z)\right\|_{L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})}=(1-|z|^{2})\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq c

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Consider now the Hilbert space
H:=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(h(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})
and the linear operator
T\colon H\to X
defined by
T(j_{2}(\eta_{z}))=f^{\prime}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. We will now show that
T
is continuous with
\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
. First, we claim that

(\lambda,z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D},\;\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{2}>c^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq c^{2}.

To see this, if
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
satisfies that
\left|\mu\right|/(1-|w|^{2})\leq 1
, then we have

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{2}>c^{2}\frac{\left|\mu\right|^{2}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{2}},

hence the property of integration of the functions
\Phi\in K_{1}
yields

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\mu\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(w)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k),

and taking the supremum over all such
(\mu,w)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
, we conclude that

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq c^{2}.

Our claim implies that

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{2}\leq\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k).

Indeed, if
(\lambda,z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D}
,
b:=\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{2}
and
\varepsilon>0
, then

\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\frac{c^{2}+\varepsilon}{b}\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|^{2}=c^{2}+\varepsilon>c^{2}.

Our claim yields

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\frac{c^{2}+\varepsilon}{b}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq c^{2},

that is,

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq\frac{c^{2}}{c^{2}+\varepsilon}b,

and since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\lambda\right|^{2}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\geq b.

We can adjust
\widehat{\nu}
so that
\widehat{\nu}(B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})})=1
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}j_{2}(\eta_{z_{i}})\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}T(j_{2}(\eta_{z_{i}}))\right\|=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\left(\int_{B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|k^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}\ d\widehat{\nu}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}{1-|z_{i}|^{2}}=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\left(\frac{\overline{\alpha_{i}}}{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}f_{z_{i}}\right)^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}k^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|=\sup_{k\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\eta_{z_{i}}(k)\right|=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\eta_{z_{i}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|j_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\eta_{z_{i}}\right)\right\|_{L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})}=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}j_{2}(\eta_{z_{i}})\right\|_{L_{2}(\widehat{\nu})},

and thus
\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
. Clearly,
f^{\prime}=T\circ g^{\prime}
. Hence
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\leq\left\|T\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\leq c
.
∎

Theorem
4.3
admits the following remarkable reformulation.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

f
factors through a Hilbert space.

(ii)

There exists a constant
c\geq 0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every
n\times n
unitary complex matrix
(a_{ij})
and every
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}a_{ij}f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}.

In this case,
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
is the minimum of all constants
c
satisfying the preceding inequality.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
(i)
holds, then Theorem
4.3
provides a constant
c\geq 0
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
. Let
(a_{ij})
be a
n\times n
unitary complex matrix. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}a_{ij}f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{j}\right|\left|a_{ij}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|a_{ij}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|a_{ij}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume there is a constant
c\geq 0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}b_{ij}f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, any
n\times n
unitary complex matrix
(b_{ij})
and any
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
. It suffices if we show that the assertion
(ii)
of Theorem
4.3
holds. For it, let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
such that
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
. We can suppose
m=n
(otherwise we add some zeros). By Theorem
4.2
, there is a
n\times n
complex matrix
A=(a_{ij})
in
B_{\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{n})}
such that

\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\mu_{j}\gamma_{w_{j}}

for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Then, if
\phi\colon B_{\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{n})}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is the convex functional defined by

\phi(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}\mu_{j}f^{\prime}(w_{j})\right\|^{2}\qquad(B=(b_{ij})\in B_{\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{n})}),

we have

\phi(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\mu_{j}\gamma_{w_{j}}(f)\right\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}(f)\right\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}.

Moreover, by hypothesis, we have

\phi(B)\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}

for any
n\times n
unitary complex matrix
B=(b_{ij})
. Since the extreme points of
B_{\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{n})}
are precisely the unitary complex matrices, then
B_{\mathcal{L}(\ell_{2}^{n})}
coincides with the convex hull of such matrices by the Minkowski–Carathéodory Theorem. Therefore, we deduce that

\phi(A)\leq c^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}},

and this completes the proof.
∎

5.
Duality

Our aim in this last section is to show that the space
(\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
can be canonically identified with the dual of the completion of the space
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
with respect to a convenient norm.

We first recall some concepts introduced in
[
5
]
. In what follows, given a complex Banach space
X
, we will sometimes write
\langle x^{*},x\rangle=x^{*}(x)
when
x\in X
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
. Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, the functional
\gamma_{z}\otimes x\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\left(\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right)(f)=\left\langle f^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle\qquad\left(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\right),

is linear and continuous with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\otimes x\right\|=\left\|x\right\|/(1-|z|^{2})
. We set the linear space

\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X=\mathrm{lin}\left\{\gamma_{z}\otimes x\colon z\in\mathbb{D},\,x\in X\right\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})^{*},

whose elements are called
X
-valued Bloch molecules on
\mathbb{D}
.

Our study of the duality of
(\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
requires to consider the following norm on these molecules.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. Given
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
, define

w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all such representations of
\gamma
in the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
such that
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
with
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
,
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
and
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
.

The concept of Bloch reasonable cross-norm was introduced in
[
5
]
, inspired by the analogue in the theory of tensor product (see, for example,
[
18
]
).

Theorem 5.2
.

w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}
is a Bloch reasonable cross-norm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
for any complex Banach space
X
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
be a representation of
\gamma
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
. Let
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\succ(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
.

Clearly,
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)\geq 0
. Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, since
\lambda\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda\lambda_{i})\gamma_{z}\otimes x_{i}
and
(\lambda\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\lambda\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
, we have

w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\lambda\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left|\lambda\right|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
\lambda=0
, we obtain
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)=0=\left|\lambda\right|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)
. For
\lambda\neq 0
, since the preceding inequality holds for every representation of
\gamma
, we deduce that
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)\leq\left|\lambda\right|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)
. For the converse inequality, note that
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)=w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda^{-1}(\lambda\gamma))\leq|\lambda^{-1}|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)
by the proved inequality, hence
\left|\lambda\right|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)\leq w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)
and thus
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda\gamma)=\left|\lambda\right|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)
.

We now prove the triangular inequality of
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
and let
\varepsilon>0
. We can choose a representation
\gamma_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\succ(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
such that

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Similarly, take a representation
\gamma_{2}=\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=m+1}^{m+l}\succ(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=n+1}^{n+k}
such that

\left(\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{2})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left(\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+l}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left(w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{2})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}
,
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m+l}\succ(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n+k}
and

\left(\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+k}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m+l}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1})+w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{2})+2\varepsilon.

hence
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\leq w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1})+w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{2})+2\varepsilon
, and thus
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})\leq w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{1})+w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{2})
by the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
.

Hence
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}
is a seminorm. To prove that it is a norm, note first that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any
g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
.
Since the first quantity
\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|
does not depend on the representation of
\gamma
because

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)(g\cdot x^{*})=\gamma(g\cdot x^{*}),

taking the infimum over all representations of
\gamma
we deduce that

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|\leq w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)

for any
g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
. Now, if
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)=0
, the preceding inequality yields

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})\gamma_{z_{i}}\right)(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})g^{\prime}(z_{i})=0

for all
g\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}
and
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
. For each
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
, this implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x^{*}(x_{i})\gamma_{z_{i}}=0
, and since
\Gamma(\mathbb{D})
is a linearly independent subset of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
by
[
10
, Remark 2.8]
, it follows that
x^{*}(x_{i})\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, hence
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
since
B_{X^{*}}
separates the points of
X
, and thus
\gamma=0
.

Finally, the norm
w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}
is a Bloch reasonable cross-norm since it holds the two conditions of
[
5
, Definition 2.5]
:

(i)

Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, we have

w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\leq\frac{\left\|x\right\|}{1-|z|^{2}}=\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|\left\|x\right\|.

(ii)

For
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})\right|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}g^{\prime}(z_{i})x^{*}(x_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x^{*}\right\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x^{*}\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(z_{i})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x^{*}\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}\left|g^{\prime}(w_{j})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x^{*}\right\|\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and taking the infimum over all representations of
\gamma
, we obtain that

\left|(g\otimes x^{*})(\gamma)\right|\leq\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma).

Hence
g\otimes x^{*}\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
with
\left\|g\otimes x^{*}\right\|\leq\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x^{*}\right\|
.

∎

We are now ready to state the announced result.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space. Then
(\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*}),\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2})
is isometrically isomorphic to
(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
, via the linear mapping
\Lambda\colon\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
defined by

\Lambda(f)(\lambda\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=\lambda\left\langle f^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle

for
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
. Moreover, its inverse satisfies that

\left\langle(\Lambda^{-1}(\varphi))^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle=\varphi(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)

for
\varphi\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
.

Moreover, on the unit ball of
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
the weak* topology coincides with the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence.

Proof.

Let
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and let
\Lambda_{0}(f)\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X\to\mathbb{C}
be the linear functional given by

\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle

for
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
. Let
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\succ(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
. Note that
\Lambda_{0}(f)\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
with

\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
. Indeed, by using Theorem
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|\left\|x_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and taking the infimum over all the representations of
\gamma
, we deduce that
\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}(\gamma)
, and thus
\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|\leq\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f)
.

Let
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}X
be the completion of the space
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes X
with the norm
w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
. Hence there is a unique continuous mapping
\Lambda(f)
from
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}X
into
\mathbb{C}
that extends
\Lambda_{0}(f)
. Further,
\Lambda(f)
is linear and
\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|=\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|
.

Let
\Lambda\colon\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}X)^{*}
be the map so defined. It is immediate that the mapping
\Lambda_{0}\colon\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})\to(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}X)^{*}
is linear. If
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
\Lambda_{0}(f)=0
, then
\left\langle f^{\prime}(z),x\right\rangle=\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, hence
f^{\prime}(z)=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and therefore
f=0
. This proves that
\Lambda_{0}
is injective. It follows easily that
\Lambda
is also linear and injective.

To prove that
\Lambda
is a surjective isometry, let
\varphi\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}X)^{*}
and define
F_{\varphi}\colon\mathbb{D}\to X^{*}
by

\left\langle F_{\varphi}(z),x\right\rangle=\varphi(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)\qquad\left(z\in\mathbb{D},\;x\in X\right).

An argument similar to that in the proof of Proposition 2.4 in
[
5
]
assures that
there exists a mapping
f_{\varphi}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|
such that
f_{\varphi}^{\prime}=F_{\varphi}
.

We now see that
f_{\varphi}\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
. Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{n}
and
(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}\in(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{D})^{m}
such that
(\lambda_{i},z_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\prec(\mu_{j},w_{j})_{j=1}^{m}
. Let
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, there exists
x_{i}\in X
with
\left\|x_{i}\right\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
such that
\left\langle f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle=\left\|f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|
. Clearly, the function
T\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

T(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\lambda_{i}\left\|f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(z_{i})\right\|,\quad\forall(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

is linear and continuous on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},||\cdot||_{2})
with
\left\|T\right\|=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For all
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
||(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})||_{2}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|T(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)\right|\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|w^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes t_{i}x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|t_{i}x_{i}\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\varphi\right\|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

therefore,

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left\|\varphi\right\|\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
yields

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}\left\|f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i})\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\mu_{j}\right|^{2}}{(1-|w_{j}|^{2})^{2}}.

Therefore,
f_{\varphi}\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
with
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|
by Theorem
4.3
.

Finally, for any
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\otimes_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X
, we get

\Lambda(f_{\varphi})(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z_{i}),x_{i}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\varphi(\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i})=\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{z_{i}}\otimes x_{i}\right)=\varphi(\gamma).

Hence
\Lambda(f_{\varphi})=\varphi
on a dense subspace of
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X
and, consequently,
\Lambda(f_{\varphi})=\varphi
. Moreover,
\gamma^{\mathcal{B}}_{2}(f_{\varphi})\leq\left\|\Lambda(f_{\varphi})\right\|
, and the first assertion in the statement follows. For the second, note that

\langle(\Lambda^{-1}(\varphi))^{\prime}(z),x\rangle=\langle f^{\prime}_{\varphi}(z),x\rangle=\langle F_{\varphi}(z),x\rangle=\varphi(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)

for
\varphi\in(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\widehat{\otimes}_{w_{2}^{\mathcal{B}}}X)^{*}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
.

For the third, let
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
and
f\in\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
. Assume that
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
weak* in
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
.
This implies that

(<f^{\prime}_{i}(z),x>)_{i\in I}=(\Lambda(f_{i})(\gamma_{z}\otimes x))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda(f)(\gamma_{z}\otimes x)=<f^{\prime}(z),x>

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
. Given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
x\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle f_{i}(z)-f(z),x\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[0,z]}\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w)-f^{\prime}(w),x\right\rangle\ dw\right|

\displaystyle\leq|z|\max\left\{\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w)-f^{\prime}(w),x\right\rangle\right|\colon w\in[0,z]\right\}

\displaystyle=|z|\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}_{i}(w_{z})-f^{\prime}(w_{z}),x\right\rangle\right|

for all
i\in I
and some
w_{z}\in[0,z]
, and thus
(\left\langle f_{i}(z),x\right\rangle)_{i\in I}\to\left\langle f(z),x\right\rangle
. Hence
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
in the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence. Therefore, the identity on
\Gamma^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}_{2}(\mathbb{D},X^{*})
is a continuous bijection from the weak* topology to the topology of pointwise
\sigma(X^{*},X)
-convergence. On the unit ball, the former topology is compact and the latter is Hausdorff, and so they are equal.
∎

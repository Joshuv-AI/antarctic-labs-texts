(weakly) ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -prime hyperideals in commutative multiplicative hyperrings
Mahdi Anbarloei

Abstract
. Let H H be a commutative multiplicative hyperring and α , β ∈ ℤ + \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+} . A proper hyperideal P P of H H is called (weakly) ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -prime if ( 0 ∉ x α ∘ y ⊆ P 0\notin x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P ) x α ∘ y ⊆ P x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P for x , y ∈ H x,y\in H implies x β ⊆ P x^{\beta}\subseteq P or y ∈ P y\in P . In this paper, we aim to investigate (weakly) ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -prime hyperideals and then we present some properties of them.

Introduction

Delving into the study of prime ideal generalizations has emerged as a profoundly intriguing and groundbreaking pursuit in the realm of commutative ring theory.
In a recent study
[

-prime ideals which is an intermediate class between prime ideals and

-closed ideals.

or

Some basic definitions concerning multiplicative hyperrings

A hyperoperation

A hypergroupoid

for all

.

Definition 2.1
.

[

]
An algebraic structure

(1)

(2)

(3)

(4)

for all

a\circ(-b)=(-a)\circ b=-(a\circ b)
;

(5)

for all

.

If in (3) the equality holds then we say that the multiplicative hyperring is strongly distributive.

3.
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideals

In this section, we introduce the concept of
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal and give some basic properties of them.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. A proper hyperideal
P
of
H
is called
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime if
x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P
for
x,y\in H
implies
x^{\beta}\subseteq P
or
y\in P
.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the multiplicative hyperring
\mathbb{Z}_{A}
. Let
p
is a prime integer such that
A\cap\langle p\rangle=\varnothing
. Then
\langle p\rangle
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

(i)

Every
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal of
H
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-closed.

(ii)

Every
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal of
H
is primary.

The following example shows that the converse part of statements in Remark
3.3
may not be true, in general.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the multiplicative hyperring
(\mathbb{Z}_{X},+,\circ)
.

(i)

Let
X=\{2,3\}
. Then
P=\langle 6\rangle
is an
(3,2)
-closed hyperideal of
\mathbb{Z}
, but it is not
(3,2)
-prime as
2^{3}\circ 3\subseteq P
where neither
2^{2}=\{8,12\}\nsubseteq P
nor
3\notin P
.

(ii)

Let
X=\{2,4\}
. Then
P=\langle 8\rangle
is a primary hyperideal of the multiplicative hyperring
(\mathbb{Z},+,\circ)
. However, it is not
(4,3)
-prime as the fact that
1^{4}\circ 4\subseteq P
but
1^{3}=\{4,8,16\}\nsubseteq P
and
4\notin P
.

-prime hyperideals

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that
P\neq H
is a hyperideal of
H
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

P
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal in
H
.

(ii)

P=(P:x^{\alpha})
such that
x^{\beta}\nsubseteq P
for
x\in H
.

(iii)

If
x^{\alpha}\circ P^{\prime}\subseteq P
for some hyperideal
P^{\prime}
of
H
and
x\in H
, then
x^{\beta}\subseteq P
or
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) Suppose that
x^{\beta}\nsubseteq P
for
x\in H
. Take any
y\in(P:x^{\alpha})
. So we have
x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P
. Since
P
is an
(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal in
H
and
x^{\beta}\nsubseteq P
, we get
y\in P
which means
(P:x^{\alpha})\subseteq P
. Since the inclusion
P\subseteq(P:x^{\alpha})
always holds, we obtain
P=(P:x^{\alpha})
.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) Let
P^{\prime}
be a hyperideal of
H
and
x\in H
such that
x^{\alpha}\circ P^{\prime}\subseteq P
. If
x^{\beta}\subseteq P
, we are done. If
x^{\beta}\nsubseteq P
, we get the result that
(P:x^{\alpha})=P
by (ii), and so
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
.

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) Let
x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P
for
x,y\in H
. Then
\langle x^{\alpha}\rangle\circ\langle y\rangle\subseteq\langle x^{\alpha}\circ y\rangle\subseteq P
by Proposition 2.15 in
[
17
]
. Put
\langle y\rangle=P^{\prime}
. Hence we have
x^{\alpha}\circ P^{\prime}\subseteq P
. From (iii) it follows that either
x^{\beta}\subseteq P
or
y\in P^{\prime}\subseteq P
, as needed.
∎

Let

(iv)

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) Let

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) Let

(iii)
\Longrightarrow
(iv) Let

\Longrightarrow

-prime hyperideal in

Theorem 3.7
.

If

-prime
\mathcal{C}
-hyperideal of

for

Q=\{x\in H\ |\ x^{\beta}\subseteq P\}

Proof.

The inclusion
\{x\in H\ |\ x^{\beta}\subseteq P\}\subseteq Q
holds. Assume that
x\in Q=rad(P)
and

is the smallest positive integer with

. Then we have
x^{\alpha}\circ x^{n-1}\subseteq P
. Take any

x^{\alpha}\circ y\subseteq P

(\alpha,\beta)
-prime hyperideal of

. Since

is a
\mathcal{C}
-hyperideal of

Q\subseteq\{x\in H\ |\ x^{\beta}\subseteq P\}

Let

Assume that

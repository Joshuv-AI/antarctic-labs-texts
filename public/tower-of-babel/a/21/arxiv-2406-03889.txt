Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the Harnack inequality of nonnegative weak solutions to the doubly nonlinear mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equations. This result is obtained by combining a related comparison principle, a local boundedness estimate, and an integral Harnack-type inequality. Our proof is based on the expansion of positivity together with a comparison argument.

1.
Introduction

Let
E_{T}:=E\times(0,T)
be an open bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
T>0
. In this paper, we discuss the Harnack-type estimate for nonnegative weak solutions to the following doubly nonlinear parabolic equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(|u|^{q-1}u\right)-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\text{in }E_{T},

(1.1)

where
p>1
,
q>0
and the operator
\mathcal{L}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy.

(1.2)

Here, P.V. means the Cauchy principal value and
K(x,y,t):\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is the symmetric kernel function satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\leq K(x,y,t)\equiv K(y,x,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\quad\mathrm{a.e.}\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

for some
\Lambda\geq 1
and
s\in(0,1)
.

To state the definition of weak solutions, we denote by
W_{0}^{1,p}(E)
the Sobolev space with zero boundary values, namely

\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,p}(E):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(E):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash E\right\}.

Moreover, we introduce the tail space

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{m}}{1+|x|^{N+\alpha}}\,dx<+\infty\right\},\quad m>0\text{ and }\alpha>0.

The parabolic nonlocal tail is of the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},R;t_{0}-S,t_{0}):=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{0}-S<t<t_{0}}\left(R^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Note that
\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},R;t_{0}-S,t_{0})
is well-defined for any
v\in L^{\infty}(t_{0}-S,t_{0};L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

Eq. (
1.1
) can be seen as a mixed version of the doubly nonlinear parabolic equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(|u|^{q-1}u\right)-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }E_{T},

(1.3)

which attracts lots of interest both in light of its mathematical structure and its significance to describe many physical phenomena including shallow water flows
[
3
]
and glacier dynamics
[
24
]
, see also
[
36
,
21
]
for other classical applications. It is well-known that Eq. (
1.3
) covers Trudinger’s equation
(q=p-1)
, the evolutionary
p
-Laplace equation
(q=1)
, and the porous medium equation
(p=2)
. There have been fruitful achievements on Eq. (
1.3
), such as the existence of solutions
[
8
,
26
]
, Hölder regularity
[
9
,
39
]
, higher integrability
[
7
,
28
]
, and Harnack type estimate
[
38
,
6
,
11
,
40
]
. We refer the readers to
[
16
,
19
,
27
,
25
]
and reference therein for more related results.

For the doubly nonlinear nonlocal parabolic equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(|u|^{q-1}u\right)+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=0\quad\text{in }E_{T},

(1.4)

the pointwise behavior of weak solutions was shown in
[
4
]
with
p>1
and
q>0
. For what concerns the nonlocal Trudinger equation, Banerjee-Garain-Kinnunen
[
5
]
investigated the local boundedness of weak subsolutions by De Giorgi’s method. Meanwhile, they also provide a crucial algebraic inequality to deal with the nonlocal term in obtaining a reverse Hölder inequality for positive weak supersolutions under the assumption that
p>2
. In particular, a weak Harnack inequality for globally bounded positive weak solutions to the nonlocal Trudinger equation was derived by Prasad
[
32
]
. Taking into account the fractional
p
-Laplace parabolic equation, the local boundedness
(p>1)
and the Hölder regularity
(p>2)
for local weak solutions were explored in
[
14
]
. Moreover,
[
22
]
generalized the regularity results to the region
1<p<\infty
by means of the intrinsic scaling method, but avoids using any comparison principle. Further results can be found in
[
35
,
37
,
12
]
.

Regarding the homogeneous scenario of (
1.1
), i.e.,
q=p-1
, the local boundedness of sign-changing weak solutions for
p\geq 2
was achieved by Nakamura
[
30
]
. Later, the author considered Harnack’s inequality for globally bounded positive weak solutions through some quantitative estimates in
[
29
]
. When
q=1
, Eq. (
1.1
) becomes the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic
p
-Laplace equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\text{in }E_{T},

(1.5)

some regularity properties of sign-changing weak solutions involving the local boundedness, the semicontinuity, and the pointwise behavior were discussed in
[
15
]
. By employing the expansion of positivity, Shang-Zhang studied Harnack’s estimate and the Hölder continuity for weak solutions to (
1.5
) in
[
33
]
and
[
34
]
, respectively. Very recently, Adimurthi-Prasad-Tewary
[
2
]
developed the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of weak solutions to (
1.5
) by suitable comparison estimates.

To the best of our knowledge, there are no results concerning Eq. (
1.1
) for the general case
q\neq p-1
. Motivated by the works
[
6
,
29
]
, our purpose in this paper is to study the Harnack inequality of globally bounded nonnegative weak solutions to problem (
1.1
) under the assumptions that
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
and
p>N
. Different from the homogeneous case, the solutions to Eq. (
1.1
) in the fast diffusion range can not be scaled by multiplying any scale factor. Exactly for this, we adopt the approach called the expansion of positivity and choose suitable geometries to overcome the non-homogeneity. In addition, we remark that the assumptions proposed on
p
and
q
address another difficulty stemming from the nonlocal feature. To investigate the Harnack inequality, we also establish the local boundedness, a comparison principle, and an integral-type Harnack inequality as byproducts.

We now state the notion of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
).

Definition 1.1
.

We identify a function
u
is a weak subsolution (super-) to the doubly nonlinear mixed local and nonlocal parabolic equation (
1.1
) if

\displaystyle u\in C(0,T;L^{q+1}(E))\cap L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}(E))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))

such that there holds the integral inequality

\displaystyle\iint_{E_{T}}-|u|^{q-1}u\partial_{t}\varphi+|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t)\,dt\leq(\geq)0

(1.6)

for all nonnegative test functions
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,q+1}(0,T;L^{q+1}(E))\cap L^{p}(0,T;W_{0}^{1,p}(E))
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))\,dydx.

A function
u
is called a weak solution to (
1.1
) if it is both a weak subsolution and a weak supersolution.

At this stage, we present our results as follows. The first one is about the local boundedness of weak subsolutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<p-1<q
and let
r\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{r}:=N(p-q-1)+rp>0.

(1.7)

Suppose that
u
is a nonnegative, weak subsolution to (
1.1
). For
r>m:=p\frac{N+q+1}{N}
, we further assume that
u
is qualitatively locally bounded. Let
Q_{\rho,s}=K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-s,t_{0}]\subset\subset E_{T}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\underset{Q_{\frac{1}{2}\rho,\frac{1}{2}s}}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}\ u\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{r}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{r}}}+\gamma\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;x_{0},\rho/2;t_{0}-s,t_{0})]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\gamma
is a constant depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
.

Remark 1.3
.

We will discuss the local boundedness result in three cases. The first two cases care about the exponent
r\leq m
, in which
u\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{m}(E_{T})
can be ensured by Sobolev embedding in Lemma
2.7
. For
r>m
, we further assume that the weak subsolutions of (
1.1
) are locally bounded because Lemma
2.7
is generally not true. Notice that there exists some
r\geq 1
satisfying
r\leq m
and
\lambda_{r}>0
, if and only if
\lambda_{m}>0
. Owing to

\lambda_{m}=(N+p)(m-q-1)=\frac{N+p}{N}\lambda_{q+1},

\lambda_{m}>0
asking for
m>q+1
or
\lambda_{q+1}>0
, which is equal to

\displaystyle q<\frac{N(p-1)+p}{(N-p)_{+}}.

Consequently, we can apply Theorem
1.2
for
r=q+1
without assuming a prior that
u
is locally bounded in this case.

From Remark
1.3
, we get a corollary of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
0<p-1<q<\frac{N(p-1)+p}{(N-p)_{+}}
. Then we know that every nonnegative weak subsolution to (
1.1
) is locally bounded.

Based on the boundedness result, we can derive the Harnack inequality in an integral-type, which is a key ingredient to obtain the Harnack inequality.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
0<p-1<q<\min\left\{p^{2}-1,\frac{N(p-1)}{(N-p)_{+}}\right\}
and
\lambda_{q}:=N(p-1-q)+qp>0
. Suppose that
u
is a nonnegative, weak solution to (
1.1
), and the cylinder
Q_{\rho,s}=K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-s,t_{0}]\subset E_{T}
with
\rho\in(0,1]
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\frac{1}{2}\rho,\frac{1}{2}s}}u\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda q}}\left(\inf_{t\in\left[t_{0}-s,t_{0}\right]}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{K_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{q}}}+\gamma\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;x_{0},\rho/2;t_{0}-s,t_{0})]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda_{q}}}\left[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;x_{0},\rho/2;t_{0}-s,t_{0})\right]^{\frac{p(p-1)}{\lambda_{q}}}

with constant
\gamma
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
.

The last theorem is our main result regarding the Harnack inequality of nonnegative weak solutions while we additionally assume that the weak solutions are globally bounded.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p>N
,
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
and let
\rho\in(0,1]
. Suppose that
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
is a nonnegative, continuous, weak solution to (
1.1
), and
u(x_{0},t_{0})>0
. There exist constants
\gamma>1
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
, such that for any

\displaystyle(x,t)\in K_{\rho}\left(x_{0}\right)\times\left(t_{0}-\sigma\left[u\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)\right]^{q+1-p}\rho^{p},t_{0}+\sigma\left[u\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)\right]^{q+1-p}\rho^{p}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}u\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)\leq u(x,t)\leq\gamma u\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right),

provided

\displaystyle K_{8\rho}\left(x_{0}\right)\times\left(t_{0}-\gamma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q+1-p}(8\rho)^{p},t_{0}+\gamma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q+1-p}(8\rho)^{p}\right)\subset E_{T}.

Figure 1.

The range where Theorem
1.6
holds is shown in Figure
1
. The Harnack inequality we established above distinguishes from the normal parabolic Harnack inequality in two aspects. First, we obtain the pointwise information in an intrinsic cylinder since we perform a specific scaling of the equation. Second, the effect of time in this Harnack inequality is weakened, so that it presents a “elliptic” feature.

Remark 1.7
.

In the statement of Theorem
1.6
, we assume that
u
is a continuous function for giving a clear sense to

. In fact, this property can be proved by using De Giorgi-type Lemma
6.2
in the forthcoming context together with Theorem 2.1 in
[

[

]

For a function

E

We also use the shorthand notations

\displaystyle d\mu=d\mu(x,y,t)=K(x,y,t)\,dxdy

\displaystyle U(x,y,t):=|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t)).

2.2.
Technical lemmas

We give an algebraic inequality, see Lemma 2.2 in
[

Lemma 2.1
.

For every

\gamma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}\left||b|^{\alpha-1}b-|a|^{\alpha-1}a\right|\leq(|a|+|b|)^{\alpha-1}|b-a|\leq\gamma\left||b|^{\alpha-1}b-|a|^{\alpha-1}a\right|

for all

.

To work with the term involving the time derivative, we shall use auxiliary functions
\bm{h}_{\pm}
defined as

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{\pm}(w,k):=\pm q\int_{k}^{w}|s|^{q-1}(s-k)_{\pm}\,ds,

(2.1)

for
k,w\in\mathbb{R}
and
q>0
. It is easy to check that
\bm{h}_{\pm}(w,k)\geq 0
. We also write

\displaystyle\bm{h}(w,k):=q\int_{k}^{w}|s|^{q-1}(s-k)\,ds.

The next lemma can be deduced with the help of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.2,
[
9
]
)
.

Let

. There exists a constant

such that for all

, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}(|a|+|b|)^{q-1}|a-b|^{2}\leq\bm{h}(a,b)\leq\gamma(|a|+|b|)^{q-1}|a-b|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma}(|a|+|b|)^{q-1}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}\leq\bm{h}_{\pm}(a,b)\leq\gamma(|a|+|b|)^{q-1}(a-b)_{\pm}^{2}.

What follows is a necessary tool for dealing with the nonlocal term to discuss the integral-type Harnack inequality.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 2.9,
[
5
]
)
.

Let
a,b>0,\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\geq 0
and

. Then there exists a constant

\displaystyle|b-a|^{p-2}(b-a)\left(\tau_{1}^{p}a^{-\varepsilon}-\tau_{2}^{p}b^{-\varepsilon}\right)\geq

\displaystyle\gamma\xi(\varepsilon)\left|\tau_{2}b^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}-\tau_{1}a^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle-\left(\xi(\varepsilon)+1+\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\right)|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|^{p}\left(b^{\alpha}+a^{\alpha}\right),

where
\varepsilon\in(0,p-1)
,

, and

We give a fast geometric convergence lemma from
[

Lemma 2.4

Let

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq Kb^{j}Y_{j}^{1+\delta},\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots

\displaystyle Y_{0}\leq K^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}b^{-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}},

Lemma 2.5

Let constants

Lemma 2.6

2.3.
Time mollification

The exponential mollification in time will be introduced in this subsection to solve the difficulty that weak solutions do not have a time derivation generally. This technique is extracted from
[
20
]
. For any
v\in L^{1}(E_{T})
and
h>0
, define

\displaystyle[\![v]\!]_{h}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{s-t}{h}}v(x,s)\,ds

and

\displaystyle[\![v]\!]_{\bar{h}}(x,t):=\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{T}e^{\frac{t-s}{h}}v(x,s)\,ds.

The following fundamental properties of mollified functions are given in
[
10
, Appendix]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
r\geq 1
. Then the following conclusions hold.

(i)

If
v\in L^{r}(E_{T})
, then
[\![v]\!]_{h}\in L^{r}(E_{T})
and
\|[\![v]\!]_{h}\|_{L^{r}(E_{T})}\leq\|v\|_{L^{r}(E_{T})}
. In addition, we have

[\![v]\!]_{h}\rightarrow v
strongly in
L^{r}(E_{T})
and almost everywhere on
E_{T}
as
h\rightarrow 0
.

(ii)

There almost everywhere on
E_{T}
holds

\displaystyle\partial_{t}[\![v]\!]_{h}=\frac{1}{h}\left(v-[\![v]\!]_{h}\right),\quad\partial_{t}[\![v]\!]_{\bar{h}}=\frac{1}{h}\left([\![v]\!]_{\bar{h}}-v\right).

(iii)

If
v\in L^{r}(E_{T})
, then
[\![v]\!]_{h}
and
[\![v]\!]_{\bar{h}}
belong to
C\left([0,T];L^{r}(E)\right)
.

(iv)

If
Du\in L^{r}(E_{T})
, then
D[\![v]\!]_{h}=[\![Dv]\!]_{h}\rightarrow Dv
strongly in
L^{r}(E_{T})
and almost everywhere on
E_{T}
as
h\rightarrow 0
.

(v)

If
v\in C([0,T];L^{r}(E))
, then
[\![v]\!]_{h}(\cdot,t)\rightarrow v(\cdot,t)
strongly in
L^{r}(E)
and almost everywhere on
E
for any
t\in(0,T]
as
h\rightarrow 0
.

The same statements of
(\rm{{i}}),(\rm{{iv}})
and
(\rm{{v}})
also apply to
[\![v]\!]_{\bar{h}}
.

3.
Comparison principles

In this part, we will consider the continuity with respect to the time variable of weak solutions and establish a comparison principle. We introduce the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}(|u|^{q-1}u)-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+\mathcal{L}u=0&\text{ in }E_{T},\\
u=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash E\times(0,T],\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}&\text{ in }E,\end{array}\right.

where
p>1,q>0
,
\mathcal{L}
is defined as in (
1.2
), and
u_{0}\in L^{q+1}(E)
.

We give the definition of weak solutions to (
3
) as below.

Definition 3.1
.

We say a function

). On the set
[u>k_{j+1}]
, we have

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{u+\widetilde{k}_{j}}{u-\widetilde{k}_{j}}\leq\frac{2u}{u-\widetilde{k}_{j}}\leq\frac{2k_{j+1}}{k_{j+1}-\widetilde{k}_{j}}\leq 2^{j+3},

which in conjunction with Lemma
2.2
gives that

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{+}(u,\widetilde{k}_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}(u+\widetilde{k}_{j})^{q-1}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}2^{-(j+3)(1-q)_{+}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q+1},

(4.3)

where
\gamma>0
depends only on
q
. Besides, on the set
[u>\widetilde{k}_{j}]
,

\displaystyle 1\leq\frac{u+\widetilde{k}_{j}}{u-k_{j}}\leq\frac{2u}{u-k_{j}}\leq\frac{2\widetilde{k}_{j}}{\widetilde{k}_{j}-k_{j}}\leq 2^{j+3}.

Therefore, we have by Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\bm{h}_{+}(u,\widetilde{k}_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(u+\widetilde{k}_{j})^{q-1}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma 2^{(j+3)(q-1)_{+}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{q+1}\chi_{[u>\widetilde{k}_{j}]}

(4.4)

with
\gamma
only depending on
q
. For the last term of (
4.2
), it is easy to find

\displaystyle\frac{|y|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{|x|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{\widehat{\rho}_{j}}{\rho_{j}-\widehat{\rho}_{j}}\leq\frac{2^{j+4}}{1-\sigma}

for every
|x|\leq\widehat{\rho}_{j}\text{ and }|y|\geq\rho_{j}
. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{ess}\,\sup}_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle-s_{j}<t<0}}{{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot,t)}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{j}}\frac{(u(y,t)-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\iint_{Q_{j}}(u(x,t)-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}\zeta^{p}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{(N+sp)j}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+sp}}\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-s_{j}<t<0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{(u(y,t)-k_{0})_{+}^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp}}\,dy\iint_{Q_{j}}(u(x,t)-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{(N+sp)j}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+sp}(\sigma\rho)^{p}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s_{j},0)]^{p-1}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u(x,t)-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}\,dxdt.

(4.5)

Substituting (
4.3
)–(
4.5
) into (
4.2
), we arrive at

\displaystyle 2^{-j(1-q)_{+}}\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\widetilde{s}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\times\{t\}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q+1}\,dx+\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{j}}|\nabla(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{j[1+(q-1)_{+}]}}{(1-\sigma)s}\iint_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{q+1}\,dxdt+\frac{\gamma 2^{pj}}{(1-\sigma)^{p}\rho^{p}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma 2^{(N+sp)j}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+sp}(\sigma\rho)^{p}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s_{j},0)]^{p-1}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}\,dxdt,

(4.6)

where
\widetilde{A}_{j}:=[u>\widetilde{k}_{j}]\cap Q_{j}
, and
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
.
Note that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{A}_{j}|=|[u>\widetilde{k}_{j}]\cap Q_{j}|\leq\frac{2^{(j+2)r}}{k^{r}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt,

(4.7)

along with the assumption
r\geq q+1\geq p
allows us to apply Hölder’s inequality to get

\displaystyle\iint_{\widetilde{A}_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{q+1}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{q+1}{r}}|\widetilde{A}_{j}|^{1-\frac{q+1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma 2^{(r-q-1)j}}{k^{r-q-1}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt.

(4.8)

Similarly, we can derive

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma 2^{(r-p)j}}{k^{r-p}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt,

(4.9)

and

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma 2^{(r-1)j}}{k^{r-1}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt.

(4.10)

Merging estimates (
4.6
), (
4.8
)–(
4.10
) results to

\displaystyle 2^{-j(1-q)_{+}}\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\widetilde{s}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\times\{t\}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q+1}\,dx+\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{j}}|\nabla(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{j(N+p+r)}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+p}\sigma^{p}s}\frac{1}{k^{r-q-1}}\left(1+\frac{s}{\rho^{p}k^{q+1-p}}+\frac{s}{\rho^{p}k^{q}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt,

where we utilized the fact
\sigma\in(0,1)
.
Now, we choose
k
to satisfy

\displaystyle k\geq\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(4.11)

so that

\displaystyle 2^{-j(1-q)_{+}}\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\widetilde{s}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\times\{t\}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q+1}\,dx+\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{j}}|\nabla(u-\widetilde{k}_{j})_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{j(N+p+r)}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+p}\sigma^{p}s}\frac{1}{k^{r-q-1}}\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt.

(4.12)

Since
m=p\frac{N+q+1}{N}
, we deduce from the Sobolev embedding Lemma
2.7
that

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{j+1}}

\displaystyle(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{m}\,dxdt\leq\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{j}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{m}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\iint_{\widetilde{Q}_{j}}|\nabla(u-k_{j+1})_{+}|^{p}\,dxdt\left(\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{-\widetilde{s}_{j}<t<0}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}\times\{t\}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{q+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{j\left[N+p+r+(1-q)_{+}\right]\frac{N+p}{N}}}{(1-\sigma)^{\frac{(N+p)^{2}}{N}}\sigma^{\frac{p(N+p)}{N}}k^{(r-q-1)\frac{N+p}{N}}s^{\frac{N+p}{N}}}\left(\iint_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{N+p}{N}},

where we used (
4.12
) to get the last line. The above estimate implies

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j+1}}\left(u-k_{j+1}\right)_{+}^{m}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma 2^{j\left[N+p+r+(1-q)_{+}\right]\frac{N+p}{N}}}{(1-\sigma)^{\frac{(N+p)^{2}}{N}}\sigma^{\frac{p(N+p)}{N}}k^{(r-q-1)\frac{N+p}{N}}}\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{N+p}{N}},

(4.13)

where
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
.

Note that inequality (
4.13
) holds for exponent
r\geq q+1
. Hereafter, we distinguish three cases:
q+1\leq r\leq m
;
r\leq m
and
r<q+1
;
r>m
to discuss the local boundedness of weak subsolutions to (
1.1
).

4.2.2.
The case
q+1\leq r\leq m

Denote

\displaystyle Y_{j}={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt.

By Hölder’s inequality and (
4.7
), there exists a constant
\gamma
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q_{j+1}|}\left(\iint_{Q_{j+1}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{m}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{r}{m}}|\widetilde{A}_{j}|^{1-\frac{r}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j+1}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{m}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{r}{m}}\left(\frac{2^{(j+2)r}}{k^{r}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{1-\frac{r}{m}}.

Based on the definition
\lambda_{r}=N(p-q-1)+rp
, it reads from (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}\leq\frac{\gamma b^{j}}{(1-\sigma)^{\frac{r(N+p)^{2}}{Nm}}\sigma^{\frac{rp(N+p)}{Nm}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{r}{m}}k^{-\frac{r\lambda_{r}}{Nm}}Y_{j}^{1+\frac{rp}{Nm}},

where
b:=2^{\frac{(N+p)r}{Nm}[N+p+r+(1-q)_{+}]+r}
and
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
. Moreover, Lemma
2.4
guarantees
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
if

\displaystyle Y_{0}={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{0}}u^{r}\,dxdt\leq\gamma^{-1}[\sigma(1-\sigma)]^{\frac{(N+p)^{2}}{p}}\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}k^{\frac{\lambda_{r}}{p}},

which asks for enforcing

\displaystyle k\geq\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda_{r}}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{r}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{0}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{r}}}.

(4.14)

Combining the choices of
k
in (
4.11
) and (
4.14
), it follows form
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\sigma\rho,\sigma s}}\ u\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda_{r}}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{r}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{r}}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(4.15)

where
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
. With taking
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}
, we get the desired boundedness result for
q+1\leq r\leq m
.

4.2.3.
The case
r\leq m\text{ and }r<q+1

Since
r<q+1
, it is not hard to find
0<\lambda_{r}<\lambda_{q+1}
, from which we can deduce that
q+1<m
. Hence, we can exploit estimate (
4.15
) in the first case directly by replacing
q+1
with
r
. For any
\sigma\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\sigma\rho,\sigma s}}\ u\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda_{q+1}}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{q+1}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{q+1}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{q+1}}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(4.16)

Set

\displaystyle M_{\sigma}=\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\sigma\rho,\sigma s}}\ u\quad\text{ and }\quad M_{1}=\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\rho,s}}\ u.

Recalling the definition of
\lambda_{q+1}
,
\lambda_{r}
, we deduce from (
4.16
) that

\displaystyle M_{\sigma}\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}^{1-\frac{\lambda_{r}}{\lambda_{q+1}}}\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda_{q+1}}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{q+1}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{q+1}}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

By Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle M_{\sigma}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}M_{1}+\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda_{r}}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{r}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{r}}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Consider above estimate on the cylinders
Q_{\sigma_{2}\rho,\sigma_{2}s}
and
Q_{\sigma_{1}\rho,\sigma_{1}s}
with
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq 1
, it shows that

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}M_{\sigma_{2}}+\frac{\gamma}{(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})^{\frac{(p+N)^{2}}{\lambda r}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda_{r}}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\sigma_{1}\rho,\sigma_{1}s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda_{r}}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\rho/2;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This enables us to use iteration Lemma
2.5
to arrive at the claim in this case.

4.2.4.
The case
r>m

According to the assumption
\lambda_{r}>0
, we can see
r>q+1
from

. If not, there will hold

, which implies that

due to the embedding Lemma
2.7
generally not true. By iterative inequality (
4.13
) and the definition of
Y_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{j+1}=

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j+1}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{r}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\infty,Q_{0}}^{r-m}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{j+1}}(u-k_{j+1})_{+}^{m}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\|u\|_{\infty,Q_{0}}^{r-m}\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\frac{b^{j}}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(N+p)^{2}}{N}}k^{(r-q-1)\frac{N+p}{N}}}Y_{j}^{1+\frac{p}{N}},

where

. Utilizing Lemma
2.4
, it holds that
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
as

\displaystyle Y_{0}={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{0}}u^{r}\,dxdt\leq\gamma^{-1}\|u\|_{\infty,Q_{0}}^{-(r-m)\frac{N}{p}}[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{(N+p)^{2}}{p}}\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}k^{\frac{(r-q-1)(N+p)}{p}},

which requires us to choose
k
fulling

\displaystyle k\geq\frac{\gamma\|u\|_{\infty,Q_{0}}^{\frac{N(r-m)}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{N+p}{r-q-1}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{0}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}.

(4.17)

Taking the choices of
k
in (
4.11
) and (
4.17
) into account, we deduce from
Y_{j}\rightarrow 0
that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\sup}_{Q_{\sigma\rho,\sigma s}}\ u\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\|u\|_{\infty,Q_{0}}^{\frac{N(r-m)}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}}{[(1-\sigma)\sigma]^{\frac{N+p}{r-q-1}}}\left(\frac{\rho^{p}}{s}\right)^{\frac{N}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}\!\!{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{\rho,s}}u^{r}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{(N+p)(r-q-1)}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}+\left(\frac{s}{\rho^{p}}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\sigma\rho;-s,0)]^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where

depends only on

.

.

5.
Integral-type Harnack inequality

In this section, we will derive Theorem
1.5
which is a direct result from Theorem
1.2
and the following integral-type Harnack inequality

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
,
\rho\in(0,1]
and
K_{2\rho}(\bar{y})\times[s,\tau]\subset\subset E_{T}
. Assume that
u
is a nonnegative weak solution to (
1.1
). Then there exists a constant
\gamma>0
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\inf_{t\in[s,\tau]}\int_{K_{2\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\gamma\left(\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{p-N}}\left[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\bar{y},\rho;s,\tau)\right]^{p-1},

where

\displaystyle\lambda:=\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}=\frac{N}{q}(p-q-1)+p.

Remark 5.2
.

If
u
is only a nonnegative weak supersolution to (
1.1
), there will hold

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{K_{2\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{\tau\}}u^{q}\,dx+\gamma\left(\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{p-N}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\bar{y},\rho;s,\tau)]^{p-1}.

Now, we are in a position to show a valuable estimate for proving Proposition
5.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
,
\rho\in(0,1]
and
K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times[s,\tau]\subset\subset E_{T}
. Let
u
be a nonnegative weak supersolution to (
1.1
). For any
\sigma\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
\gamma>0
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}(u+\kappa)^{-\frac{1+q}{p}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\!\int_{s}^{\tau}\!\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}\!\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}\!(t-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}(|u(x,t)+\kappa|+|u(y,t)+\kappa|)^{-\frac{1+q}{p}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\rho}{(1-\sigma)^{N+p}}\left(\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}},

where

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{N}{q}(p-q-1)+p\quad\textmd{and}\quad\kappa=\left(\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+1-p}}.

(5.1)

Proof.

For simplicity, we may assume
(\bar{y},s)=(0,0)
. Choose

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}(x,t)=t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left([\![u]\!]_{\bar{h}}+\kappa\right)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)

as a test function in (
1.6
), where
\zeta\in C_{0}^{1}\left(K_{\frac{\rho(1+\sigma)}{2}};[0,1]\right)
satisfies
\zeta=1
in
K_{\sigma\rho}
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{2}{(1-\sigma)\rho}
, for
\varepsilon>0
,
\psi_{\varepsilon}
is a Lipschitz function such that
\psi_{\varepsilon}=1
in
(\varepsilon,\tau-\varepsilon),\psi_{\varepsilon}=0
outside
(0,\tau)
, and it is linearly interpolated otherwise. Then we yield that

\displaystyle\iint_{E_{T}}-u^{q}\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}\,dxdt+\iint_{E_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\varphi_{h}(x,t)-\varphi_{h}(y,t))\,d\mu dt\geq 0.

(5.2)

The treatment of the first and second terms in (
5.2
) is similar to that of
[
6
, Lemma 7.3]
, thus we have

\displaystyle-\iint_{E_{T}}u^{q}\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}\,dxdt\leq

\displaystyle q\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}\zeta^{p}(x)\int_{0}^{u}s^{q-1}(s+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,dsdx

and

\displaystyle\iint_{E_{T}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h}\,dxdt\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{q+1-p}{2p}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}|\nabla u|^{p}(u+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1}{p}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-1-q}{p}}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dxdt.

For the nonlocal term, we pass to the limit
h\rightarrow 0
first and utilize Lemma
2.8

(\rm{{i}})
, and then send
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\varphi_{h}(x,t)-\varphi_{h}(y,t))\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U(x,y,t)t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}U(x,y,t)t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{K_{\rho}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}\!U(x,y,t)t^{\frac{1}{p}}\!\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)\!-\!(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]d\mu dt.

Since
p-1<q<p^{2}-1
, it holds that
0<\frac{q+1-p}{p}<p-1
, thus we can employ Lemma
2.3
with
a=u(y,t)+\kappa
,
b=u(x,t)+\kappa
,
\tau_{1}=\zeta(y)
,
\tau_{2}=\zeta(x)
, and
\varepsilon=\frac{q+1-p}{p}
to deduce

\displaystyle U(x,y,t)\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\gamma(p)\xi(\varepsilon)\left|\zeta(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}-\zeta(y)(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+\left(\xi(\varepsilon)+1+\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\right)|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}+(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}\right].

Subsequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}U(x,y,t)t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\gamma\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\left|\zeta(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}-\zeta(y)(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle+\gamma\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}+(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}\right]\frac{|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt,

where
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
. By the non-negativity of weak solutions, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}U(x,y,t)t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\zeta^{p}(y)\right]\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}\cap[u(x,t)\geq u(y,t)]}\frac{U(x,y,t)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\sup_{x\in\rm{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right)\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}u(x,t)^{p-1}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,dxdt.

Gathering the above estimates, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}|\nabla u|^{p}(u+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1}{p}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\left|\zeta(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}-\zeta(y)(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\!\!\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}\zeta^{p}(x)\!\int_{0}^{u}s^{q-1}(s+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,dsdx\!+\!\frac{\gamma}{(1-\sigma)^{p}\rho^{p}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-1-q}{p}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}+(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p}}\right]\frac{|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle+\gamma\left(\sup_{x\in\rm{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash K_{\rho}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right)\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}u(x,t)^{p-1}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\zeta^{p}(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

(5.3)

Estimate of
I_{1}
:
From the hypothesis
0<p-1<q
, one can easily get

\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}\in(0,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{0}^{u}s^{q-1}(s+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\,ds\leq\gamma u^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}.

Thus we apply Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\gamma\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{\tau\}}u^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}\,dx\leq\gamma\rho\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}.

(5.4)

Estimate of
I_{2}
:
Again using Hölder’s inequality and the definition of
\kappa
, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\tau^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-\sigma)^{p}}\frac{\tau}{\rho^{p}}\kappa^{-(q+1-p)}\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\rho}{(1-\sigma)^{p}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}.

(5.5)

Estimate of
I_{3}
:
By exchanging the role of
x
and
y
and by exploiting
\rho\in(0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\rho^{p-sp}}{(1-\sigma)^{p}\rho^{p}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-1-q}{p}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\rho}{(1-\sigma)^{p}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}.

(5.6)

Estimate of
I_{4}
:
Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{|y|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{|x|}{|y-x|}\leq 1+\frac{1+\sigma}{1-\sigma}\leq\frac{2}{1-\sigma}

for any
|x|\leq\frac{(1+\sigma)\rho}{2}
and
|y|\geq\rho
, thus there holds

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma 2^{N+sp}}{(1-\sigma)^{N+sp}\rho^{sp}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-1-q}{p}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma 2^{N+sp}\rho}{(1-\sigma)^{N+sp}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}.

(5.7)

On the other hand, the left-hand side of (
5.3
) can be estimated by utilizing Lemma
2.1
with
\alpha=\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}>0
and the property
\zeta=1
in

, it gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\rho}}\int_{K_{\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\left|\zeta(x)(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}-\zeta(y)(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\left|(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}-(u(y,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{p^{2}-q-1}{p^{2}}}\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(|u(x,t)+\kappa|+|u(y,t)+\kappa|\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{p}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dxdydt.

(5.8)

Combining estimates (

∎

Given Lemma

, we can explore the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
,
\rho\in(0,1]
and
K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times[s,\tau]\subset\subset E_{T}
. Assume that
u
is a nonnegative weak supersolution to (
1.1
). For all
\delta,\sigma\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
\gamma>0
depending only on

such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,dxdt+\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{s}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}(\bar{y})}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-1}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\sup_{t\in[s,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}(\bar{y})\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\frac{\gamma}{\left[\delta^{q+1}(1-\sigma)^{pq}\right]^{\frac{N+p}{q+1-p}}}\left(\frac{\tau-s}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}},

where
\lambda

5.1

Proof.

Let
(\bar{y},s)=(0,0)
. We derive from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,dxdt\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}|\nabla u|^{p}(u+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1}{p}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(5.9)

The estimate of the first integral on the right-hand side of (
5.9
) yields from Lemma
5.3
. Coming to estimate the second integral, there holds by using Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,dt\times\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}\times\{t\}}(u+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\rho\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}.

(5.10)

Still by Hölder’s inequality, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-1}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{-\frac{q+1}{p}}t^{\frac{1}{p}}\,dxdydt\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-p}}t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,dxdydt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(5.11)

The first integral on the right-hand side of (
5.11
) has been provided in Lemma
5.3
. We deal with the second integral as in (
5.10
) and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{(u(x,t)+\kappa)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{p}}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-p}}t^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\rho\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}},

where we used the assumption
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and the radius
\rho\in(0,1]
. Putting the above estimates together, employing Young’s inequality as well as the definition of
\kappa
in (
5.1
), we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\int_{K_{\sigma\rho}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-1}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\rho}{(1-\sigma)^{N+p}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\kappa^{q}\rho^{N}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)(q+1)}{pq}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\rho\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx+\frac{\gamma\rho}{\left[\delta^{q+1}(1-\sigma)^{pq}\right]^{\frac{N+p}{q+1-p}}}\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}},

where the constant
\delta\in(0,1)
depends on

. Diving both sides by
\rho
, we arrive at the claim.
∎

Hereafter, we give the proof of Proposition

.

For
t_{1}>t_{2}
, we may test (
1.6
) by
-\zeta
instead of
\zeta
. To proceed, we set

\displaystyle\int_{K_{2\rho}\times\{t_{2}\}}u^{q}\,dx=\inf_{t\in[0,\tau]}\int_{K_{2\rho}\times\{t\}}u^{q}\,dx=:A.

\displaystyle S_{j}\leq

\displaystyle A+\frac{2^{j+3}}{\rho}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,dxdt+\frac{2^{j+3}}{\rho}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-1}}\,dxdydt

\rho_{j}
, we can get
\rho\leq\rho_{j}<2\rho
. Selecting
\sigma

, and we deduce from Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,dxdt+\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}\int_{\widetilde{K}_{j}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp-1}}\,dxdydt

\displaystyle S_{0}\leq\varepsilon^{j}S_{j}+\gamma(\varepsilon)\left(A+\left(\frac{\tau}{\rho^{\lambda}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}+\tau\rho^{N-p}[\text{Tail}_{\infty}(u;0,\rho;0,\tau)]^{p-1}\right)\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(\varepsilon b)^{i}.

6.
Expansion of positivity

This section aims to obtain the following result regarding the expansion of positivity, which derives the pointwise estimate for weak supersolutions from the measure theoretical condition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
0<p-1\leq q
. Assume that
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
is a nonnegative, weak supersolution to (
1.1
). If for some constants
M>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have

\displaystyle|[u(\cdot,t_{0})\geq M]\cap K_{\rho}(x_{0})|\geq\alpha|K_{\rho}|,

(6.1)

then there exist parameters
\delta,\eta\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda,\alpha
such that

\displaystyle u\geq\eta M\quad\text{ a.e. in }K_{2\rho}(x_{0})\times\left(t_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta M^{q+1-p}\rho^{p},t_{0}+\delta M^{q+1-p}\rho^{p}\right],

provided

\displaystyle K_{8\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta M^{q+1-p}\rho^{p}]\subset E_{T}.

Before proving Proposition
6.1
, we give several preparatory estimates. The first one is a De Giorgi-type lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
q>0
,
p>1
, and let
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
be a nonnegative, weak supersolution to (
1.1
). For some constants
M>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, assume that
\rho\in(0,1]
and
(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\theta)\subset E_{T}
, where
\theta=\delta M^{q+1-p}
. If there is a constant
\nu\in(0,1)
only depending on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\delta
such that

\displaystyle\left|[u\leq M]\cap(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\rho}(\theta)\right|\leq\nu|Q_{\rho}(\theta)|,

then we have

\displaystyle u\geq\frac{1}{2}M\quad\text{a.e. in }(x_{0},t_{0})+Q_{\frac{1}{2}\rho}(\theta).

Proof.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)

Scaling functions

Change the variables

\displaystyle z\rightarrow\frac{x-x_{0}}{\rho},\quad z^{\prime}\rightarrow\frac{y-x_{0}}{\rho},\quad\tau\rightarrow[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{p-q-1}\frac{t-t_{0}}{\rho^{p}}.

Consider the following rescaled function

\displaystyle v(z,\tau):=\frac{u\left(x_{0}+\rho z,t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q+1-p}\rho^{p}\tau\right)}{u(x_{0},t_{0})}\quad\text{ in }\widetilde{Q}_{8}=K_{8}\times(-8^{p},8^{p}).

(7.1)

Via formula calculation, we can check that
v(0,0)=1
and
v
is a bounded continuous nonnegative solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}v^{q}-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widetilde{K}(z,z^{\prime},\tau)|v(z,\tau)-v(z^{\prime},\tau)|^{p-2}(v(z,\tau)-v(z^{\prime},\tau))\,dz^{\prime}=0\quad\text{ in }\widetilde{Q}_{8}

(7.2)

with

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}(z,z^{\prime},\tau)=\rho^{N+p}K(x_{0}+\rho z,x_{0}+\rho z^{\prime},t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q+1-p}\rho^{p}\tau),

satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{p-sp}\Lambda^{-1}}{|z-z^{\prime}|^{N+sp}}\leq\widetilde{K}(z,z^{\prime},\tau)\leq\frac{\rho^{p-sp}\Lambda}{|z-z^{\prime}|^{N+sp}}.

Now, our intention turns to demonstrate there exist
\gamma>1
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
such that

\displaystyle v\geq\gamma^{-1}\quad\text{ in }K_{1}\times(-\sigma,\sigma).

(7.3)

Notice the Harnack inequality on the left-hand side is a straightforward consequence of (
7.3
).

7.2.
The supremum of function
v
in
K_{1}
.

For
\tau\in(0,1)
, define

\displaystyle M_{\tau}:=\sup_{K_{\tau}}v(\cdot,0),\quad N_{\tau}:=(1-\tau)^{-\beta},\quad\text{ where }\beta=\frac{p}{q+1-p}.

Observe that
M_{0}=N_{0}=1
,
N_{\tau}\rightarrow\infty
as
\tau\rightarrow 1
, and
M_{\tau}
is bounded due to
v
is a bounded function. Therefore, due to
M_{\tau}=N_{\tau}
must have roots, we denote the largest one by
\tau_{*}
, specifically,

\displaystyle M_{\tau_{*}}=N_{\tau_{*}}\quad\text{ and }\quad M_{\tau}\leq N_{\tau}\quad\text{ for all }\tau\geq\tau_{*}.

Pick
\bar{\tau}\in\left(\tau_{*},1\right)
such that

\displaystyle N_{\bar{\tau}}=(1-\bar{\tau})^{-\beta}=4(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta},\quad\text{ i.e., }\quad\bar{\tau}=1-4^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(1-\tau_{*}\right),

and let

\displaystyle 2r:=\bar{\tau}-\tau_{*}=(1-4^{-\frac{1}{\beta}})(1-\tau_{*}).

Clearly,
M_{\tau_{*}}
can be achieved at some
\bar{x}\in K_{\tau_{*}}
because
v
is a continuous function. Hence, there holds
K_{2r}(\bar{x})\subset K_{\bar{\tau}},M_{\bar{\tau}}\leq N_{\bar{\tau}}
, and

\displaystyle\sup_{K_{\tau_{*}}}v(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=M_{\tau_{*}}=v(\bar{x},0)\leq\sup_{K_{2r}(\bar{x})}v(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{K_{\bar{\tau}}}v(\cdot,0)=M_{\bar{\tau}}\leq N_{\bar{\tau}}=4(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}.

(7.4)

7.3.
Expanding of positivity of
v
.

Set the cylinder

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{r}(\theta_{*}):=K_{r}(\bar{x})\times(-\theta_{*}r^{p},\theta_{*}r^{p}),\quad\theta_{*}:=(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta(q+1-p)}

with center
(\bar{x},0)
.
By the choices of
\beta
and
r
, we compute

\displaystyle\theta_{*}r^{p}=(1-\tau_{*})^{-p}r^{p}=2^{-p}\left(1-4^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)^{p}=:c,

(7.5)

which implies that

\widetilde{Q}_{2r}\left(\theta_{*}\right)=K_{2r}(\bar{x})\times\left(-2^{p}c,2^{p}c\right).

Obviously,

\displaystyle r=c^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(1-\tau_{*}\right).

(7.6)

We next discuss the estimate of the supremum of
v
in
\widetilde{Q}_{r}\left(\theta_{*}\right)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
v
be defined as in (
7.1
) and
0<p-1<q<\min\left\{p^{2}-1,\frac{N(p-1)}{(N-p)_{+}}\right\}
. There exists a constant
\gamma
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\widetilde{Q}_{r}\left(\theta_{*}\right)}v\leq\gamma(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}.

Proof.

An application of Theorem
1.5
to
v
over the cylinder
\widetilde{Q}_{r}\left(\theta_{*}\right)\subset\widetilde{Q}_{2r}\left(\theta_{*}\right)
leads to

\displaystyle\sup_{\widetilde{Q}_{r}(\theta_{*})}v^{q}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{c^{\frac{N}{\lambda}}}\left(\int_{K_{2r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,0)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}

\displaystyle+\gamma\frac{c}{r^{p}}\left[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\bar{x},r;-2^{p}c,2^{p}c)\right]^{p-1}\!+\!\gamma\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}}\right)^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;\bar{x},r;-2^{p}c,2^{p}c)]^{\frac{p(p-1)}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{c^{\frac{N}{\lambda}}}\left(\int_{K_{2r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,0)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}}\right)^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}+\gamma\frac{c}{r^{p}}+\gamma\left(\frac{c}{r^{p}}\right)^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[(1-\tau_{*})^{-\frac{pq}{q+1-p}}+(1-\tau_{*})^{-p}+(1-\tau_{*})^{\frac{-p(p-N)}{\lambda}}\right],

(7.7)

where
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
. In the last step of (
7.7
), we used (
7.4
) and (
7.6
).

By the definitions of
\beta,\lambda
and the assumption
0<p-1<q
, we compute

\displaystyle-p\geq\frac{-pq}{q+1-p}=-\beta q

(7.8)

and

\displaystyle\frac{-p(p-N)}{\lambda}=\frac{pq(p-N)}{N(q+1-p)-pq}\geq\frac{-pqN}{N(q+1-p)}=-\beta q.

(7.9)

Since
\tau_{*}\in(0,1)
, the claim follows from (
7.7
)–(
7.9
).
∎

With Lemma
7.1
, we present the following result which provides the condition of the expansion of positivity.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
v
be defined as in (
7.1
). Let
p>N
and
0<p-1<q<p^{2}-1
. There exist constants
\delta,\bar{c},\alpha\in(0,1)
just depending on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left[v(\cdot,t)\geq\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]\cap K_{r}(\bar{x})\right|\geq\alpha|K_{r}|\quad\text{ for all }t\in\left[-\delta\theta_{*}r^{p},\delta\theta_{*}r^{p}\right].

Proof.

By using Theorem
1.5
to function
v
over the cylinder
\widetilde{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}r}(\delta\theta_{*})\subset\widetilde{Q}_{r}(\delta\theta_{*})
yields

\displaystyle(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}=

\displaystyle v^{q}(\bar{x},0)\leq\sup_{K_{\frac{1}{2}r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\left(\delta\theta_{*}r^{p}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda}}}\left(\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma(\delta\theta_{*})^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}+\gamma\delta\theta_{*}+\gamma(\delta\theta_{*})^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\left(\delta\theta_{*}r^{p}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda}}}\left(\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma\delta^{\frac{q}{q+1-p}}\left(1-\tau_{*}\right)^{-\beta q}

\displaystyle+\gamma\delta(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta(q+1-p)}+\gamma\delta^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta(q+1-p)\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\left(\delta\theta_{*}r^{p}\right)^{\frac{N}{\lambda}}}\left(\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma\delta^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}

for all
t\in\left[-\delta\theta_{*}r^{p},\delta\theta_{*}r^{p}\right]
. In the above display, we also employed the definition of
\theta_{*}
and the fact
0<(p-N)/\lambda<1
. With taking
\delta
such that
\delta^{\frac{p-N}{\lambda}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}\leq\gamma\left(\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\lambda}},

where
\gamma
depends only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
, since
\theta_{*}r^{p}=c
depends only on
p
and
q
.
In addition, we deduce from Lemma
7.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\chi_{\left[v<\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]}\,dx+\int_{K_{r}(\bar{x})}v^{q}(x,t)\chi_{\left[v\geq\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{c}^{q}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}(2r)^{N}+\gamma^{q}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}|[v\geq\bar{c}\left(1-\tau_{*}\right)^{-\beta}]\cap K_{r}(\bar{x})|

with
\bar{c}\in(0,1)
to be specified. In light of the convexity of
s^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}
, we can derive

\displaystyle(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[\bar{c}^{q}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}(2r)^{N}\right]^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}+\gamma(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q\frac{p}{\lambda}}\left|\left[v\geq\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]\cap K_{r}(\bar{x})\right|^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\bar{c}^{q\frac{p}{\lambda}}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}+\gamma(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q\frac{p}{\lambda}}\left|\left[v\geq\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]\cap K_{r}(\bar{x})\right|^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}.

Fixing
\bar{c}
such that
\gamma\bar{c}^{q\frac{p}{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q}\leq\gamma(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta q\frac{p}{\lambda}}\left|\left[v\geq\bar{c}(1-\tau_{*})^{-\beta}\right]\cap K_{r}(\bar{x})\right|^{\frac{p}{\lambda}}

with
\gamma
depending only on
N,p,s,q,\Lambda
and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))}
. Thus, we complete the proof based on the definitions of
r,\beta
and
\lambda
.
∎

In the next step, we expand the pointwise positivity of

7.4.
A comparison argument

We give the following initial-boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}w^{q}-\mathrm{div}(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w)+\mathcal{L}w=0&\text{ in }K_{4}(\bar{x})\times(-\sigma,1],\\

where

is given by (
1.2
),

is defined as in Lemma

,

to be determined and

Lemma 7.4
.

Let

and

. Let

defined as in (

Since

In view of Lemma

, we only need to obtain the pointwise positivity of

\displaystyle w\geq\gamma^{-1}\quad\text{ in }K_{2}(\bar{x})\times\left[-\tfrac{1}{4}\sigma,\tfrac{1}{4}\sigma\right]

Now, we pick

\displaystyle\sup_{K_{2}(\bar{x})\times\left[-\frac{1}{4}\sigma,\frac{1}{4}\sigma\right]}w^{q}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta c_{0}

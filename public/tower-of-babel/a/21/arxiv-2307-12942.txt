Line bundles on the first Drinfeld covering
James Taylor

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
d
be the
d
-dimensional
Drinfeld symmetric space for a finite extension
F
of
{\mathbb Q}_{p}
. Let
\Sigma^{1}
be a geometrically connected component of the first Drinfeld covering of
d
, and let
{\mathbb F}
be the residue field of the unique degree
d+1
unramified extension of
F
. We show that the natural homomorphism

\widehat{({\mathbb F},+)}\longrightarrow\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]

determined by the second Drinfeld covering is injective.
Here
\widehat{({\mathbb F},+)}
is the group of characters of
({\mathbb F},+)
.
In particular,
\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]\neq 0
. We also show that all vector bundles on
1
are trivial, which extends the classical result that
\Pic({}^{1})=0
.

Keywords.
Picard group, Drinfeld tower

1. Introduction

Let
p
be a prime,
F
a finite extension of
{\mathbb Q}_{p}
, and
L
the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. The
Drinfeld tower
is a system of
d
-dimensional rigid analytic spaces over
L
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\longleftarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\longleftarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\longleftarrow\cdots,

for which the spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
are equipped with compatible actions of
D^{\times}\times\GL_{d+1}(F)
, where
D
is the division algebra with invariant
1/(d+1)
over
F
; see
[
Dri76
,
BC91
,
RZ96
]
. The space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is a fundamental example of a Rapoport–Zink space, and as such is defined as the generic fibre of a
p
-adic formal scheme
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}
which parametrises special formal
\mathcal{O}_{D}
-modules. The spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
over
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
are obtained by considering level structure by the compact open subgroups
1+\Pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D}
of
D^{\times}
, and each
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is a finite étale Galois covering with Galois group
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/(1+\Pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D})
. These spaces play an important role in the representation theory of both
\GL_{d+1}(F)
and
D^{\times}
: this tower has been shown to realise both the local Langlands and the Jacquet–Langlands correspondences for
\GL_{d+1}(F)
in its étale cohomology, see
[
Car90
,
Har97
,
Boy99
,
HT01
]
, and when
F={\mathbb Q}_{p}
and
d=1
, encode part of the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
\GL_{2}({\mathbb Q}_{p})
; see
[
CDN20
, Theorem 0.2]
.

The base space
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is non-canonically a disjoint union over
\mathbb{Z}
of copies of
d
, the
d
-dimensional Drinfeld symmetric space. This is the admissible open subset of
{\mathbb P}^{d,\text{an}}
defined by removing all
F
-rational hyperplanes. The space
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
has been studied by many authors, see
[
Tei90
,
Wan14
,
Pan17
,
Jun22
]
, and recently Junger has shown that, after extending from
L
to
L(\varpi)
, the preimage of each copy of
d
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is a disjoint union of
q-1
copies of
\Sigma^{1}
, a particular geometrically connected Kummer-type cyclic Galois covering of
d
; see
[
Jun22
, Theorem 4.9]
. Here
\varpi
is a
(q-1)^{\mathrm{st}}
root of
-\pi
, for
\pi
a uniformiser of
F
, and the extension
L(\varpi)/L
is equal to the first Lubin–Tate extension of
L
. Little is known about the geometry of the higher covering spaces
({\mathcal{M}}_{n})_{n\geq 2}
.

It is a classical result that
\Pic({}^{1})=0
, and recently this has been generalised to higher dimensions and more general hyperplane arrangements by Junger; see
[
Jun23
, Theorem A]
. Understanding the Picard groups of the covering spaces
({\mathcal{M}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is much more difficult, and almost nothing is known in this context. Previously, we showed that for
d=1
, there is no
p
-torsion in the Picard group of any open subset of
\Sigma^{1}
which is the preimage of a vertex of the Bruhat–Tits tree; see
[
Tay23
, Theorem 3.2]
.

In this paper we show that in any dimension
\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]\neq 0
. More precisely, setting
G\coloneqq\SL_{d+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
and writing
{\mathbb F}
for the residue field of the unique degree
d+1
unramified extension of
F
, we prove the following.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Suppose that
K
contains
L(\varpi)
and a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
. Then the natural homomorphism

\widehat{({\mathbb F},+)}\rightarrow\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]^{G}

determined by the second Drinfeld covering is injective.

Here
\widehat{({\mathbb F},+)}
is the group of characters of
({\mathbb F},+)
. The assumption that
K
contains
L(\varpi)
is simply to ensure that the space
\Sigma^{1}
is defined, and the assumption that
K
contains a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
is to ensure that the homomorphism, which we now describe, is defined. For any Galois covering
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
with abelian Galois group
H
of exponent
e
over a field which contains a primitive
e^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
, there is a decomposition

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi},\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\coloneqq e_{\chi}\cdot f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X},

where
e_{\chi}
is the central primitive idempotent corresponding to the character
\chi
. Furthermore,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\in\Pic(Y)[e]
for any
\chi\in\widehat{H}
, and the association

\widehat{H}\longrightarrow\Pic(Y)[e],\quad\chi\longmapsto{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}

is a group homomorphism (
cf.
Proposition
2.3
). For any
n\geq 1
,
\sigma_{n}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{n+1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
is a Galois covering with Galois group
(1+\Pi^{n}\mathcal{O}_{D})/(1+\Pi^{n+1}\mathcal{O}_{D})
, which is canonically identified with
({\mathbb F},+)
. The homomorphism of Theorem
4.6
is then the homomorphism associated to the abelian Galois covering
\sigma_{1}\colon\Sigma^{2}\rightarrow\Sigma^{1}
, where
\Sigma^{2}
is the preimage of
\Sigma^{1}
in
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
.

Now for the remainder of the introduction we focus on the case where
d=1
and set
\Omega\coloneqq{}^{1}
. Our main interest in Theorem
4.6
is in the following. The work
[
DLB17
]
of Dospinescu and Le Bras describes how certain
D^{\times}
-isotypical parts of the locally analytic representations
\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})^{\prime}
of
\GL_{2}({\mathbb Q}_{p})
are related to the
p
-adic Langlands correspondence and to the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence. We would like to better understand the representations
\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{M}}_{n})^{\prime}
more generally for any
F
and
d\geq 1
. For the representation
\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})^{\prime}
and questions regarding admissibility and topological irreducibility, because
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is a disjoint union of copies of
\Sigma^{1}
, it is sufficient to understand the
D(G)
-module
\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})
, where
D(G)
is the distribution algebra of
G
.

The extension
\sigma_{0}\colon\Sigma^{1}\rightarrow\Omega
is an abelian Galois covering with Galois group
\Gamma\coloneqq({\mathbb F}_{q^{2}}^{\times})^{q-1}\subset{\mathbb F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. Applying the above formalism to this extension and then taking the global sections, we can decompose the
D(G)
-module
\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})
as

\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})=(\sigma_{0,*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{1}})(\Omega)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\psi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(\Omega).

The monumental recent work of Ardakov and Wadsley
[
AW23
]
shows, without any restriction on
F
, that if
\psi\neq 1
, then
{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(\Omega)
is a topologically irreducible coadmissible
D(G)
-module. This follows from
[
AW23
, Theorem A]
together with the proof of
[
AW23
, Corollary B]
and the fact that
\Sigma^{1}
is geometrically connected. The case where
\psi=1
is simply
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}(\Omega)=\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
, and the authors additionally show that this has length
2
as a
D(G)
-module; see
[
AW23
, Corollary 7.5.9]
. We note that unlike
{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}
for
\psi\neq 1
, the line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=\mathcal{O}
is the restriction of some
\GL_{2}(F)
-equivariant vector bundle on
{\mathbb P}^{1,\text{an}}
to
\Omega
, and as such was already well understood as a
D(\GL_{2}(F))
-module; see
[
Orl08
]
. The main idea of
[
AW23
]
is to understand the
G
-equivariant line bundle with connection
{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}
on
\Omega
and then push this to
{\mathbb P}^{1,\text{an}}
and use the
G
-equivariant Beilinson–Bernstein correspondence, see
[
Ard21
, Theorem C]
, to deduce properties of the corresponding
D(G)
-module
{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(\Omega)
.

We would like to use similar techniques to understand the global sections of the higher Drinfeld coverings as
D(G)
-modules. For the second covering, we consider the extension
\sigma_{1}\colon\Sigma^{2}\rightarrow\Sigma^{1}
and want to understand the
G
-equivariant line bundles with connection
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}=e_{\chi}\cdot\sigma_{1,*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}
for any
\chi\neq 1
. In this context Theorem
4.6
says that the underlying line bundle of each
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}
is non-trivial. This is in contrast to what happens for
\sigma_{0}\colon\Sigma^{1}\rightarrow\Omega
, because
\Pic(\Omega)=0
. The analysis used in
[
AW23
]
of
G
-equivariant line bundles with connection works under the assumption that the underlying line bundle is trivial, and Theorem
4.6
shows that none of the line bundles
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}
fit into this formalism.

In this paper we also show that all vector bundles on
\Omega
are trivial (see Corollary
5.4
), which extends and uses the classical result that
\Pic(\Omega)=0
. The key property we use is that
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
is a Prüfer domain, which is unknown to hold in higher dimensions. In the context of the above discussion, this suggests that rather than first understanding
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}=e_{\chi}\cdot\sigma_{1,*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}
as a
G
-equivariant line bundle with connection on
\Sigma^{1}
and then pushing to
\Omega
, a potentially more feasible approach is to instead analyse
\sigma_{0,*}{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}
directly as a
G
-equivariant vector bundle with connection on
\Omega
.

Notation

Throughout we shall use the following notation:
F
is a finite extension of
{\mathbb Q}_{p}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, uniformiser
\pi
, and residue field
{\mathbb F}_{q}
;
K
is a complete field extension of
F
,
L
is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
, and
{\mathbb C}_{p}
is the completion of
\overline{F}
. The integer
d\geq 1
will denote the dimension of the spaces we consider. We set
G=\SL_{d+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
and write
D
for the division algebra over
F
of invariant
1/(d+1)
.

In this section we describe how the pushforward of the structure sheaf of an abelian Galois covering decomposes into line bundles. The approach we take here is influenced by the work of Borevič for Kummer extensions of rings; see

\rho\colon\Gamma^{\text{op}}\rightarrow\Aut_{Y}(X)

\Aut_{Y}(X)
is the group of automorphisms of

f\colon X\rightarrow Y

In this situation the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
has a (left) action of
\Gamma

\Gamma\longrightarrow\Aut_{k}\left(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\right),\quad g\longmapsto\left(\rho(g)^{\sharp}_{f^{-1}(U)}\colon f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\right)_{U\subset Y},

which is well defined as
\rho(g)^{\sharp}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\rho(g)_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
and

\rho(g)^{-1}(f^{-1}(U))=f^{-1}(U)

for any open
U\subset Y
. Therefore, we can consider the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules
(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\Gamma}
defined by

(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\Gamma}(U)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(f^{-1}(U))^{\Gamma}

for any admissible open subset
U
of
Y
, which is a sheaf because
(-)^{\Gamma}
preserves products and equalisers.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a finite group,
a\colon X\times\underline{\Gamma}\rightarrow X
is an action of
\underline{\Gamma}
on
X
, and
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a finite étale morphism of rigid spaces over
K
which is equivariant with respect to the trivial action of
\underline{\Gamma}
on
Y
. Then
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a
Galois covering with Galois group
\Gamma
if the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\Gamma}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules and

p_{X}\times a\colon X\times\underline{\Gamma}\longrightarrow X\times_{Y}X

is an isomorphism of rigid spaces over
K
.

For the remainder of this section, we assume that
\Gamma
is a finite abelian group,
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a Galois covering with Galois group
\Gamma
, and
K
contains a primitive
e(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
, where
e(\Gamma)
is the exponent of
\Gamma
. For each
\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}
, we write
e_{\chi}
for the corresponding central primitive idempotent

e_{\chi}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sumop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi(\gamma^{-1})\gamma\in K[\Gamma].

Definition 2.2
.

For any
\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}
, we define the
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module

{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\coloneqq e_{\chi}\cdot f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Proposition 2.3
.

There is a direct sum decomposition of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi},

and multiplication in
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
induces an isomorphism

{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\vskip-1.5pt}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi\psi}.

In particular, each
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}
is an
e(\Gamma)
-torsion invertible
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module, and the association

\widehat{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\Pic(Y)[e(\Gamma)],\quad\chi\longmapsto{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}

is a group homomorphism.

Proof.

The sheaf
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}[\Gamma]
-module, and the direct sum decomposition of
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
follows from the fact that the
e_{\chi}
are central orthogonal idempotents which sum to
1
. Now suppose that
U
is an affinoid open subset of
Y
, and let

V\coloneqq f^{-1}(U)=U\times_{Y}X\xhookrightarrow{\hphantom{aaa}}X.

Then
\underline{\Gamma}
acts on
V
,
f\colon V\rightarrow U
is equivariant with respect to the trivial action of
\underline{\Gamma}
on
U
, and we have a commutative diagram of isomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\times_{Y}(X\times\underline{\Gamma})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U\times_{Y}(X\times_{Y}X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V\times\underline{\Gamma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(U\times_{Y}X)\times_{U}(U\times_{Y}X)\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}}

Write
A\coloneqq\mathcal{O}(U)
and
B\coloneqq\mathcal{O}(V)
. Since
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is finite,
V
is affinoid, and because
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\Gamma}
is an isomorphism,
A\rightarrow B
is injective and has image
B^{\Gamma}
.
Furthermore, because
B
is finitely generated over
A
, the natural inclusion

B\otimes_{A}B\longrightarrow B\widehat{\otimes}_{A}B

is an isomorphism; see
[
BGR84

.
Therefore, the composition of this inclusion with the global sections of

induces an isomorphism

Now
B
is a right
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)
-comodule algebra for the Hopf algebra
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)
via

\rho\colon B\longrightarrow B\otimes_{K}\mathcal{O}(\Gamma),\quad\rho\colon b\longmapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma(b)\otimes\delta_{\gamma},

and the above isomorphism says exactly that
A\rightarrow B
is an
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)
-Galois extension in the sense of
[
Mon93
, Definition 8.1.1]
. Because
K
contains a primitive
e(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
, the natural map
K[\widehat{\Gamma}]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\Gamma)
is an isomorphism of Hopf algebras over
K
. Therefore, using this identification, we can view
B
as a
K[\widehat{\Gamma}]
-comodule algebra. We have that for
b\in B
,

(2.1)

\rho(b)=\sumop\displaylimits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma(b)\otimes\delta_{\gamma}=\sumop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}b_{\chi}\otimes\chi

for some unique
b_{\chi}\in B
, and for each
\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}
, we define

B_{\chi}\coloneqq\{b_{\chi}\mid b\in B\}.

Because
A\rightarrow B
is
K[\widehat{\Gamma}]
-Galois, these
B_{\chi}
make
B
a strongly graded
\widehat{\Gamma}
-algebra by a result of Ulbrich, see
[
Mon93
, Theorem 8.1.7]
, meaning that

B=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}B_{\chi}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{\chi}\cdot B_{\psi}=B_{\chi\psi}\ \text{ for all }\chi,\psi\in\widehat{\Gamma}.

In fact,
e_{\chi}\cdot B=B_{\chi}
. Indeed, by column orthogonality

\delta_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sumop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}\chi(\gamma^{-1})\chi,

and therefore substituting this into Equation (
2.1
) and comparing the coefficient of
\chi
shows that

e_{\chi}\cdot b=b_{\chi}.

There is a natural surjective morphism of
A
-modules

m_{\chi,\psi}\colon B_{\chi}\otimes_{A}B_{\psi}\longrightarrow B_{\chi}\cdot B_{\psi}=B_{\chi\psi}.

In order to show that this is injective, first note that each
B_{\chi}
is direct summand of
B
and thus is finitely generated projective as an
A
-module. From the decomposition of
B
as the direct sum of the
B_{\chi}
,

(2.2)

\sumop\displaylimits_{\chi\in\widehat{\Gamma}}\rank_{A}(B_{\chi})=\rank_{A}(B)=|\Gamma|.

On the other hand, we have a surjection

B_{\chi}\otimes_{A}B_{\chi^{-1}}\longrightarrow B_{\chi}\cdot B_{\chi^{-1}}=B_{1}=e_{1}\cdot B=A,

and therefore
\rank_{A}(B_{\chi})\rank_{A}(B_{\chi^{-1}})\geq 1
, hence
\rank_{A}(B_{\chi})\geq 1
, and from Equation (
2.2
) above,
\rank_{A}(B_{\chi})=1
. As a consequence,
m_{\chi,\psi}
is a surjective homomorphism between finitely generated rank
1

A
-modules, and as such it is injective.

Now, returning to the global situation, multiplication induces a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules

(2.3)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}.

Because
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
is coherent, then locally over an affinoid open subset
U
as above, this is identified with the morphism of sheaves associated under the associated sheaf construction to the
A
-module homomorphism

e_{\chi}\cdot B\otimes_{A}e_{\psi}\cdot B\longrightarrow B.

We have shown above that this has image
e_{\chi\psi}\cdot B
, and therefore the morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules (
2.3
) above induces an isomorphism

{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\vskip-1.5pt}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi\psi}.\qed

Remark 2.4
.

In fact one can show that if
Y
is connected and
\Gamma_{0}
is the stabiliser of any connected component
X_{0}
of
X
, then
f\colon X_{0}\rightarrow Y
is a Galois extension with Galois group
\Gamma_{0}
, and the homomorphism

\widehat{\Gamma}\longrightarrow\Pic(Y)[e(\Gamma)]

factors as the composition

\widehat{\Gamma}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\widehat{\Gamma_{0}}\longrightarrow\Pic(Y)[e(\Gamma_{0})]\xhookrightarrow{\hphantom{aaa}}\Pic(Y)[e(\Gamma)].

3. Drinfeld symmetric spaces

Let
F
be a finite extension of
{\mathbb Q}_{p}
,
L
the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
, and
K
a complete field extension of
F
. Set
G\coloneqq\SL_{d+1}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
, and let
D
be the division algebra over
F
of invariant
1/(d+1)
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{D}
. We let
\Pi
denote a uniformiser of
\mathcal{O}_{D}
and write
\Nrd\colon D^{\times}\rightarrow F^{\times}
for the reduced norm of
D
. Let
d
be the Drinfeld symmetric space of dimension
d
over
K
, which is the admissible open subset of
{\mathbb P}^{d,\text{an}}_{K}
defined by removing all
F
-rational hyperplanes. This is stable under the natural action of
\GL_{d+1}(F)
on
{\mathbb P}^{d,\text{an}}_{K}
.

The Drinfeld tower is a system of rigid analytic spaces over
L
,

{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\longleftarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\longleftarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\longleftarrow\cdots,

and each space has an action of
\GL_{d+1}(F)\times D^{\times}
for which the transition morphisms are equivariant.
For background material on these spaces, see
[
Dri76
,
BC91
,
RZ96
]

H_{\chi}
is normal,
f^{\prime}\colon\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}\rightarrow\Sigma^{1}
is Galois with Galois group
H/H_{\chi}
, which follows from
[
CHR69
, Theorem 2.2]
and the fact that each property in the definition of a Galois extension checked affinoid locally.

We first note that we have an equality of
\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{1}}
-modules

e_{\chi}\cdot f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}=e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot f^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}}.

Indeed, for any admissible open subset
U
of
\Sigma^{1}
,

(e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot f^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}})(U)=e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}(f^{-1}(U))^{H_{\chi}}

and

(e_{\chi}\cdot f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}})(U)=e_{\chi}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}(f^{-1}(U)).

Setting
B\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}}(f^{-1}(U))
, we have that

\displaystyle e_{\chi}\cdot B

\displaystyle=\{b\in B\mid h(b)=\chi(h)b\text{ for all }h\in H\},

\displaystyle e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot B^{H_{\chi}}

\displaystyle=\{b\in B^{H_{\chi}}\mid h(b)=\chi(h)b\text{ for all }h\in H/H_{\chi}\},

and it is direct to check that these are equal. Therefore, we are reduced to showing that
e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot f^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}}
is non-trivial.

Now because the action of
G
on
\Sigma^{2}
and
\Sigma^{1}
commutes with the action of
H
,
G
acts on
\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}
,
f^{\prime}\colon\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}\rightarrow\Sigma^{1}
is
G
-equivariant, and the
G
-action commutes with the action of
H/H_{\chi}
. Therefore, the covering
f^{\prime}\colon\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}\rightarrow\Sigma^{1}
defines an element of

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\et}(\Sigma^{1},\underline{H/H_{\chi}})^{G}
, see
[
Jun22
, Section 4.1]
, the middle term of the
G
-invariants of the Kummer exact sequence

(4.1)

0\longrightarrow\left(\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})^{\times}/\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})^{\times p}\right)^{G}\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\et}(\Sigma^{1},\underline{H/H_{\chi}})^{G}\longrightarrow\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]^{G}.

Now suppose towards a contradiction that the line bundle
e_{\chi^{\prime}}\cdot f^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}}
is trivial. Then from the exact sequence (
4.1
) above, the space
\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}
is given as
\kappa(v)=\Sigma^{1}(v^{1/p})
for some

v\in\left(\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})^{\times}/\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{1})^{\times p}\right)^{G}.

By Proposition
4.5
, we actually have
v\in K^{\times}/K^{\times p}
, and therefore the base change
\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi}\times_{K}K({v}^{1/p})
is not connected. Over
K({v}^{1/p})
, the intermediate extension

\Sigma^{2}\times_{K}K({v}^{1/p})\longrightarrow(\Sigma^{2}/H_{\chi})\times_{K}K({v}^{1/p})

is Galois and hence surjective, and thus
\Sigma^{2}\times_{K}K({v}^{1/p})
is also not connected. But this gives a contradiction as
\Sigma^{2}
is geometrically connected by Corollary
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

If we do not assume that
K
contains a primitive
p^{\mathrm{th}}
root of
1
, then the techniques used in the proof of Theorem
4.6
can still be used to show that
\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]^{G}\neq 0
. Indeed, if we assume that
\Pic(\Sigma^{1})[p]^{G}=0
, then the same argument but with
H_{\chi}
replaced by any index
p
subgroup
H_{0}
of
H
will still result in a contradiction.

5. Vector bundles on the Drinfeld upper half plane

In this section we provide an elementary proof that all vector bundles on
1
are trivial, which extends and uses the result that all line bundles on
1
are trivial; see
[
Jun23
, Theorem A]
. In the context of Theorem
4.6
, this says that whilst the line bundles
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}
on
\Sigma^{1}
are non-trivial whenever
\chi\neq 1
, the pushforward to
1
will be a trivial vector bundle (of constant rank
q+1
).

Before we state the theorem, we will need the following notions from commutative algebra.

Definition 5.1
.

An integral domain
R
is called a
Prüfer domain
if every finitely generated ideal of
R
is invertible; it is called a
Bézout domain
if every finitely generated ideal of
R
is principal.

We provide a proof of the following result, for which we were unable to find a reference.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
R
is a Bézout domain. Then every finitely generated submodule of a free module is free.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
is finitely generated over
R
, and
M
is contained in a free module
P
. By choosing a basis for
P
, as
M
is finitely generated, we have that
M\subset R^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
. Let
\pi\colon R^{n}\rightarrow R
be the projection to the first factor, and let
I\coloneqq\pi(M)
and
K\coloneqq\ker(\pi\colon M\rightarrow R)
. Now
I
is the homomorphic image of
M
and thus finitely generated; hence
I
is principal and thus free because
R
is a Bézout domain. Therefore, the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow 0

splits, and
M\cong K\oplus I
. Finally,
K
is also finitely generated, being a homomorphic image of
M
, and
K\subset R^{n-1}
, so the result follows by induction.
∎

We note that this property actually characterises Bézout domains among integral domains. Indeed, if
I
is a finitely generated ideal of an integral domain
R
which satisfies the above property, then
I
is free, but also
I\subset R
, so by passing to the fraction field of
R
, we see that
I
must have rank
1
, and thus
I
is principal. This property is analogous to the following property of PIDs (which are exactly the Noetherian Bézout domains): a commutative ring
R
is a PID if and only if every submodule of a free module is free.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak X}
be a smooth connected
1
-dimensional quasi-Stein rigid analytic space, with
\Pic({\mathfrak X})=0
. Then any vector bundle on
{\mathfrak X}
is of the form
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathfrak X}}^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

If
{\mathfrak X}
is as above, the ring
R\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{{\mathfrak X}}({\mathfrak X})
is an integral domain. The global sections functor defines an equivalence of categories between vector bundles on
{\mathfrak X}
and finitely generated projective modules over
R
; see
[
BSX20
, Proposition 1.13]
.
In particular,
\Pic(R)=0
, and we are reduced to showing that any finitely generated projective module over
R
is free. The ring
R
is a Prüfer domain, see
[
BSX20
, Corollary 1.8]
,
and because
\Pic(R)=0
, the ring
R
is furthermore a Bézout domain. Then we can conclude, as for such rings any finitely generated projective module is free, by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Any vector bundle on
1
is of the form
\mathcal{O}_{{}^{1}}^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

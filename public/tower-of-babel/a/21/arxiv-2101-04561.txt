Covering hypergraphs are eulerian
Mateja Šajna, Andrew Wagner

Abstract
An Euler tour in a hypergraph (also called a rank-2 universal cycle or 1-overlap cycle in the context of designs) is a closed walk that traverses every edge exactly once. In this paper, we define a covering k-hypergraph to be a non-empty k k -uniform hypergraph in which every ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -subset of vertices appear together in at least one edge. We then show that every covering k k -hypergraph, for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , admits an Euler tour if and only if it has at least two edges. Keywords: Covering hypergraph; Euler tour; rank-2 universal cycle; 1-overlap cycle; Euler family; interchanging cycle.



1
Introduction

An Euler tour of a graph or hypergraph is a closed walk that traverses every edge exactly once. The complete characterization of graphs that admit an Euler tour, which was conjectured by Euler in 1741
[
6
]
, and proved by Hierholzer and Wiener in 1873
[
7
]
, is a fundamental and accessible result in graph theory. However, analogous results for hypergraphs have only recently begun to be explored.

To our knowledge, Lonc and Naroski were the first to extend the notion of an Euler tour to hypergraphs. In a 2010 paper
[
10
]
they showed that the problem of existence of an Euler tour is NP-complete on the set of
k
-uniform hypergraphs, for any
k\geq 3
, as well as when restricted to a particular subclass of 3-uniform hypergraphs.

A
Steiner triple
(
quadruple
)
system
is a 3-uniform (4-uniform, respectively) hypergraph in which every pair (triple, respectively) of vertices lie together in exactly one edge. Initial results for Euler tours in Steiner triple and quadruple systems used different terminology. Namely, in 2012, Dewar and Stevens
[
5
]
proved that every cyclic Steiner triple system of order greater than three, and every cyclic twofold triple system admits a
rank-2 universal cycle
(that is, Euler tour), and in 2013, Horan and Hurlbert showed that for every admissible order greater than four, there exist a Steiner triple system
[
9
]
and a Steiner quadruple system
[
8
]
with a
1-overlap cycle
(that is, Euler tour).

We define a
covering
k
-hypergraph
, for
k\geq 3
, to be a non-empty
k
-uniform hypergraph in which every
(k-1)
-subset of vertices appear together in at least one edge.

In a 2015 paper
[
2
]
, Bahmanian and Šajna embarked on a systematic study of eulerian properties of general hypergraphs; some of their techniques and results will be used in this paper. In particular, they introduced the notion of an
Euler family
— a collection of closed trails that jointly traverse each edge exactly once — and showed that the problem of existence of an Euler family is polynomial on the class of all hypergraphs. In addition, they proved that every covering 3-hypergraph with at least two edges admits an Euler family.

Most recently, the present authors gave a short proof
[
11
]
to show that every triple system — that is, a 3-uniform hypergraph in which every pair of vertices lie together in the same number of edges — admits an Euler tour as long as it has at least two edges.

The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
H
be a covering
k
-hypergraph, for
k\geq 3
. Then
H
admits an Euler tour if and only if it has at least two edges.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
represents a significant improvement on
[
11
]
, as well as on the above-mentioned result of
[
2
]
.

The framework for the proof of Theorem
1.1
is presented in Section
3
, while the technical details are given in Section
4
. Here, we also introduce our main, new technique of interchanging cycles, which resembles the technique of alternating paths in matching theory.

Preliminaries

We use hypergraph terminology established in
[
1
,
2
]
, which applies to loopless graphs as well. Any graph theory terms not explained here can be found in

]
.

A

is a pair

, where

of

. A hypergraph of order 1 is called

, and a hypergraph with no edges is called

.

Distinct vertices

and

are called

(or

and an edge

are said to be

. The

of

in
H
, denoted
\deg_{H}(v)
, is the number of edges of
H
incident with
v
. A hypergraph
H
is called
k
-uniform
if every edge of
H
has cardinality
k
.

A
covering
k
-hypergraph
, for
k\geq 3
, is a non-empty
k
-uniform hypergraph with the property that every
(k-1)
-subset of vertices lie together in at least one edge.

The
incidence graph
of a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
is a bipartite simple graph
\mathcal{G}(H)
with vertex set
V\cup E
and bipartition
\{V,E\}
such that
vertices
v\in V
and
e\in E
of
\mathcal{G}(H)
are adjacent if and only if
v
is incident with
e
in
H
. The elements of
V
and
E
are called

and
e-vertices
of
\mathcal{G}(H)
, respectively.

A hypergraph
H^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is called a
subhypergraph
of the hypergraph
H=(V,E)
if
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
and
E^{\prime}=\{e\cap V^{\prime}:e\in E^{\prime\prime}\}
for some submultiset
E^{\prime\prime}
of
E
. For any subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
, we define the
subhypergraph of
H
induced by

V^{\prime}
, denoted
H[V^{\prime}]
, to be the hypergraph
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
with
E^{\prime}=\{e\cap V^{\prime}:e\in E,e\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\}
. Thus, we obtain the subhypergraph induced by
V^{\prime}
by deleting all vertices in
V{\backslash}V^{\prime}
from
V
and from each edge of
H
, and subsequently deleting all empty edges.
By
H-V^{\prime}
we denote the subhypergraph of
H
induced by
V{\backslash}V^{\prime}
, and for
v\in V
, we write shortly
H-v
instead of

.
For any subset
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, we denote the subhypergraph
(V,E{\backslash}E^{\prime})
of
H
by
H{\backslash}E^{\prime}
, and for
e\in E
, we write
H{\backslash}e
instead of
H{\backslash}\{e\}
. For any multiset
E^{\prime}
of
2^{V}
, the symbol
H+E^{\prime}
will denote the hypergraph obtained from

by adjoining all edges in
E^{\prime}
.

A
v_{0}v_{k}
-walk
in
H
is a sequence
W=v_{0}e_{1}v_{1}e_{2}\ldots e_{k}v_{k}
such that
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}\in V
;
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\in E
; and
v_{i-1},v_{i}\in e_{i}
with
v_{i-1}\neq v_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. A walk is said to

each of the vertices and edges in the sequence. The vertices
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
are called the

of

. If
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}
are pairwise distinct, then
W
is called a
trail
(
strict trail
in
[
1
,
2
]
); if
v_{0}=v_{k}
and
k\geq 2
, then
W
is
closed
. If
W
is a closed trail and
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}
are pairwise distinct, then
W
is called a
cycle
; more specifically, a cycle
of length
k
, or
k
-cycle
.

A hypergraph
H
is
connected
if, for any pair
u,v\in V(H)
, there exists a
uv
-walk in
H
. A
connected component
is a maximal connected subhypergraph of
H
without empty edges. We call
v\in V(H)
a
cut vertex
of
H
, and
e\in E(H)
a
cut edge
of
H
, if
H-v
and
H{\backslash}e
, respectively, have more connected components than

.

An
Euler family
of a hypergraph

is a collection of pairwise anchor-disjoint and edge-disjoint closed trails that jointly traverse every edge of
H
, and an
Euler tour
is a closed trail that traverses every edge of
H
. Thus, an Euler family of cardinality 1 corresponds to an Euler tour. The closed trails in an Euler family

are called the
components
of
\mathcal{F}
, and an Euler family of

with the smallest number of components is called
minimum.

A hypergraph that is either empty or admits an Euler tour (family) is called
eulerian (quasi-eulerian)
.

Proof of the main result

All of our work in Section
4
will serve to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
H
be a covering 3-hypergraph with at least two edges. Then
H
is eulerian.

Proof.

Let
n
be the order of
H
.
If
n\geq 7
, then
H
is eulerian by Theorem
4.13
, and if
3\leq n\leq 6
, then
H
is eulerian by Lemma
4.14
.
∎

Theorem
3.1
will now be used as the basis of induction in the proof of our main result, which we restate below.

Theorem
1.1

.

Let
H
be a covering
k
-hypergraph, for
k\geq 3
. Then
H
is eulerian if and only if it has at least two edges.

Proof.

By definition, we know
H
is non-empty. The necessity is then clear.

To prove sufficiency, we use induction on
k
. For
k=3
, Theorem
3.1
implies that
H
is eulerian. Suppose that, for some fixed
k\geq 3
, the statement holds: that is, any covering
k
-hypergraph with at least two edges is eulerian.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a covering
(k+1)
-hypergraph with
|E|\geq 2.
Fix any
v\in V
, and let
\mathcal{B}=\{e{\backslash}\{v\}:e\in E,v\in e\}
and
E^{\prime}=\{e\in E:v\not\in e\}
. Then let
\mathcal{C}
be a set obtained from
E^{\prime}
by removing an arbitrary vertex from each
e\in E^{\prime}
. Let
H^{*}=(V{\backslash}\{v\},\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}),
where
\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{C}
denotes the multiset union of
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
. Then
H^{*}
is a covering
k
-hypergraph. Since
|E(H^{*})|=|E(H)|\geq 2
, by the induction hypothesis, we have that
H^{*}
admits an Euler tour
T
. Replacing each edge in
T
by the corresponding edge of
H
, we obtain an Euler tour of
H
.

The result follows by induction.
∎

Let

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G

has a block of order

Proof.

Let
{\cal B}
be the block tree of

adjacent in

if and only if

is a cut vertex of

and

is a block of

containing

. Choose a block

of order

to be the root of the tree

, and observe that any leaf of
{\cal B}
must be a block. If

is a cut vertex of

, consider any block

that is a leaf in the subtree of

rooted at

. Since

is loopless,

has at least two vertices, and since

is a leaf in

, only one of them is a cut vertex of

. Hence each

is either not a cut vertex of

, or else the subtree rooted at

∎

.

Let

be an

Write

END POINT ESTIMATES FOR RIESZ TRANSFORM and Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities
Dangyang He

Abstract
. We consider a class of non-doubling manifolds ℳ \mathcal{M} defined by taking connected sum of finite Riemannian manifolds with dimension N which has the form ℝ n i × ℳ i \mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i} and the Euclidean dimension n i n_{i} are not necessarily all the same. In [ 21 ] , Hassell and Sikora proved that the Riesz transform on ℳ \mathcal{M} is weak type ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) , bounded on L p ​ ( ℳ ) L^{p}(\mathcal{M}) for all 1 < p < n ∗ 1<p<n^{*} where n ∗ = min k ⁡ n k n^{*}=\min_{k}n_{k} and is unbounded for p ≥ n ∗ p\geq n^{*} . In this note we show that the Riesz transform is bounded from Lorentz space L n ∗ , 1 ​ ( ℳ ) L^{n^{*},1}(\mathcal{M}) to L n ∗ , 1 ​ ( ℳ ) L^{n^{*},1}(\mathcal{M}) . This complete the picture by obtaining the end point results for p = n ∗ p=n^{*} . Our approach is based on parametrix construction described in [ 21 ] and a generalisation of Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities first studied by Hardy, Littlewood and Pólya.



1.
Introduction

Riesz transform is an important area of Harmonic Analysis. It provides answer to a very natural and significant questions. Arguably it is also an origin of the whole modern development of Harmonic Analysis especially the theory of singular integrals, which Riesz transform is par excellence example. To explain the significance of Riesz transform question we start with some simple comparison of the two-dimensional wave and Laplace equations. For any two functions
f,g
which map
\mathbb{R}

to itself the corresponding function
F(x,t)=f(x-t)+g(x+t)

satisfy the wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}F-\partial_{x}^{2}F=0
as it is present in D’Alembert’s Formula. As a consequence, potential solutions do not have any regularity and the components

\partial_{t}^{2}F
and
\partial_{x}^{2}F
are not necessarily meaningful.
On the other hand, for Laplace equation if
\partial_{t}^{2}F+\partial_{x}^{2}F=u
for some
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})

then the boundedness of the second-order Riesz transforms

\partial_{t}^{2}/(\partial_{t}^{2}+\partial_{x}^{2})
and

\partial_{x}^{2}/(\partial_{t}^{2}+\partial_{x}^{2})
implies that both derivatives
\partial_{t}^{2}F
and
\partial_{x}^{2}F

are meaningful and in fact they belong to the same
L^{p}

space for all
1<p<\infty
. It is one of the most remarkable and significant observations in the theory of Partial differential equations, especially surprising in the above comparison with the wave equation.
For
p=2
the boundedness of the Riesz transform of any order is a direct consequence of the Plancherel’s Theorem and theory of Lebesgue integration. Whereas the results for
p\neq 2
opened a new chapter in modern Harmonic analysis called singular integrals.

The first order Riesz transform is defined as

R_{i}=\frac{\partial_{i}}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i\leq n

where
\Delta=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}
is the standard Laplacian on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. One of applications of the study of Riesz transform is the definition of Sobolev spaces. In a simple example, the continuity of
R_{i}
on
L^{p}
spaces shows that the following two natural definitions of homogeneous Sobolev spaces are equivalent:

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}}=\|\Delta^{1/2}f\|_{p}\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\partial_{i}f\|_{p}\hskip 10.00002pt1<p<\infty

The first momentum of the theory of the Riesz transform
comes from its one-dimension version, Hilbert transform, which appears in the way:

H(f)=\lim_{y\to 0}(f*Q_{y})

where
Q_{y}
is the conjugate Poisson kernel and the limit is taking in
L^{2}

[
31
, Proposition 3.1]
, and the proof of its boundedness was described by Riesz
[
26
]
based on complex analysis. In higher dimensional setting, the extension of the
L^{p}
boundedness result of Riesz transform was described in the way of Calderón–Zygmund decomposition
[
30
]
.

In
[
28
]
, Strichartz initiated the project to extend the classical results known for the standard Laplace operator to the setting of Laplace-Beltrami operator on the complete Riemannian manifolds. Such generalizations hold automatically for some type of results but in the case of Riesz transform, the question is still not completely solved. Consider the Riesz transform on a complete Riemannian manifold equipped with some measure satisfying doubling condition. That is

(1.1)

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r))\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,r

where
B(x,r)
is the geodesic ball centered at
x
with radius
r
. Coulhon and Duong proved that the Riesz transform on this kind of manifold is bounded in
L^{p}
for
1<p\leq 2
and of weak type
(1,1)
, the assumptions and details can be found in
[
6
]
.

We also consider the case where the doubling condition fails. In
[
4
,
5
,
17
,
21
]
authors focus on a special class of manifolds which is called the connected sums of products of Euclidean spaces and compact manifolds. The main settings can be expressed as follows. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a manifold of connected sum in the form

\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}_{1})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}\times\mathcal{M}_{l})

with
l\geq 2
,
n_{i}\geq 3
and each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is a compact manifold. Also, let
N=n_{i}+\mathop{dim}\mathcal{M}_{i}
to be the topological dimension of
\mathcal{M}
. Denote by
\Delta
the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
\nabla
the gradient on
\mathcal{M}
. The Riesz transform on
\mathcal{M}
is then defined as

R=\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}

then the main question is to determine for which exponent
p
, the Riesz transform
R
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
to
L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
. In
[
21
]
, Hassell and Sikora proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}_{1})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}\times\mathcal{M}_{l})
is a manifold with
l\geq 2
ends, and
n_{i}\geq 3
for each
i
. Then the Riesz transform
\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
defined on
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
if and only if
1<p<\min\{n_{1},\dots,n_{l}\}
. That is,
there exists
C>0
such that

\|\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f)\|_{p}\leq C\|f\|_{p}

if and only if
1<p<n^{*}
where
n^{*}=\min\{n_{1},\dots,n_{l}\}
. In addition the Riesz transform
\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is of
weak type (1,1).

This result improves the work of Carron
[
4
]
and gives an example of a setting in non-doubling manifold when
n_{i}
are not all equal. Together with weak type (1,1) estimates, the
L^{p}
boundedness of the Riesz transform has been described except the estimate for the end point
n^{*}=\min_{k}n_{k}
.

To complete the picture, we wish to give a suitable endpoint result. In
[
6
, Section 5]
, Coulhon and Duong give a counterexample to show that
R
is not bounded in
L^{p}
for
p>n^{*}
in the case where
n_{i}=n_{j}
. From
[
21
, Proposition 6.1]
, we know that in a more general setting where
n_{i}
does not need to equal to
n_{j}
, the strong type estimate cannot hold for
p\geq n^{*}
. In
[
23
, Section 3.7]
, in a different setting, Li gives a counterexample to illustrate that the Riesz transform
R
is not weak type
(p_{0},p_{0})
for some critical value
p_{0}
(In our case
p_{0}=n^{*}
). Therefore, it is natural for us to consider whether the restricted weak type estimates can be established.

To describe the endpoint extension of Theorem
1.1
, we have to recall notions in Lorentz space.
By
f^{*}
we denote the decreasing rearrangement function of
f
, defined by the formula

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s>0;d_{f}(s)\leq t\}

and
d_{f}
is the usual distribution function of
f
i.e.

d_{f}(s)=\mu(\{x\in X\colon|f(x)|>s\}).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
f
be a measurable function defined on a measure space
(X,\mu)
. For
0<p,q\leq\infty
, define

\|f\|_{(p,q)}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&q<\infty\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t),&q=\infty\end{cases}

then we say
f
is in the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(X,\mu)
if
\|f\|_{(p,q)}<\infty
.

We mention that when
q=\infty
, the Lorentz space
L^{p,\infty}
coincides with the weak
L^{p}
space and

\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)=\sup_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{f}(\alpha)^{1/p}

For a detailed discussion of the notion of Lorentz spaces, see
[
12
, section 1.4, Page 48]
.

Now we are in a position to state our main result, which is described in the following theorem. Our approach is based on the setting and techniques in
[
16
,
17
,
21
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}_{1})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}\times\mathcal{M}_{l})
be a manifold with
l\geq 2
ends, and
n_{i}\geq 3
for each
i
. The Riesz transform,
R=\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, is bounded in
L^{n^{*},1}(\mathcal{M})
. That is, there exists some
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\|\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f)\|_{(n^{*},1)}\leq C\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

where
n^{*}=\min_{1\leq k\leq l}n_{k}
.

Moreover,
R
is not bounded from
L^{n^{*},p}\to L^{n^{*},p}
for any
p>1
.

We mention that this result is slightly stronger than the restricted weak type estimate i.e.

\|\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f)\|_{(n^{*},\infty)}\leq C\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

And the unboundedness in
L^{n^{*},p}
indicates it is not weak type
(n^{*},n^{*})
. Compare with
[
23
]
.

Some parts of the proof of boundedness of Riesz transform from
[
21
]
for
1<p<\infty
can be partially simplified if one has only proved weak type
(1,1)
estimate. Since we can use interpolation between Theorem
1.3
and weak
(1,1)
the get the boundedness for
p
between. See Remark
3.3
below.

2.
Manifolds with ends

We start our discussion by introducing the notion of connected sum and manifolds with ends. We refer readers to
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
for more detailed discussion of the notion of manifolds with ends.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M_{i}
where
i=1,2,\dots,l
be a family of complete connected non-compact Riemannian manifolds with the same dimension. Then we call the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
is the connected sum of
M_{1},...,M_{l}
and write it as

\mathcal{M}=M_{1}\#M_{2}\#\dots\#M_{l}

if for some compact subset
K\subset\mathcal{M}
, its compliment
\mathcal{M}\setminus K
is a disjoint union of connected open sets
E_{i}
where
i=1,2,\dots,l
such that each
E_{i}
is isometric to
M_{i}\setminus K_{i}
for some compact set
K_{i}\subset M_{i}
. We call the subsets
E_{i}
the
i
th end of
\mathcal{M}
. In other words, we cut holes,
K_{i}
, on each of the
M_{i}
and then glue
M_{i}\setminus K_{i}
together with the compact set
K
.

In the following, we use
\mathcal{M}
to denote manifold:

\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathcal{M}_{1})\#\dots\#(\mathbb{R}^{n_{l}}\times\mathcal{M}_{l})

which consists of the compact connection
K
and
l
ends
E_{i}=(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i})\setminus K_{i}
. For simplicity, we may assume that each
K_{i}
is the product of
\mathcal{M}_{i}
and a closed ball
\overline{B(x_{i}^{0},\epsilon_{i})}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
for some
x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
and
\epsilon_{i}>0
. Moreover, we define the Riemannian metric on each end to be the product metric. That is
\forall z,z^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i})\setminus K_{i}
, say
z=(x,y)
and
z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
where
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\setminus\overline{B(x_{i}^{0},\epsilon_{i})}
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}
we have

d_{\mathcal{M}}(z,z^{\prime})^{2}=d_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}}(x,x^{\prime})^{2}+d_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}(y,y^{\prime})^{2}

where
d_{\mathcal{M}},d_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}},d_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}
are the corresponding Riemannian distance on
\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},\mathcal{M}_{i}
. For simplicity, in what follow we will use
d(z,z^{\prime})
instead of
d_{\mathcal{M}}(z,z^{\prime})
. We also introduce
\mu_{i}
, the induced Riemannian measure on
E_{i}
and
\mu
, the measure on
\mathcal{M}
with identity
\mu|_{E_{i}}=\mu_{i}
.

Observe that since
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is compact, the Riemannian metric locally behaves like in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
but behaves like
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
globally. That is for any geodesic ball
B(z,r)\subset E_{i}
centered at
z
with radius
r
, we have its volume estimate:

\mu_{i}(B(z,r))\sim\begin{cases}r^{N},&r\leq 1\\
r^{n_{i}},&r\geq 1\end{cases}

Note that the manifolds satisfies the doubling condition if and only if
n_{i}=n_{j}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq l
.

2.1.
Resolvent of the Laplacian

We follow techniques described in
[
20
,
21
,
25
]
and briefly summarize the key idea of the approach and emphasize the crucial estimates and lemmas we use to prove Theorem
1.3
. The starting point is primarily based on the study of the resolvent of the Laplacian. Since the Laplace operator
\Delta
is positive and self-adjoint, the spectral theory guarantees that

(2.1)

\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\nabla\int_{0}^{\infty}(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}dk

Then the idea is to split it into two parts

(2.2)

\nabla\Delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\nabla\int_{0}^{1}(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}dk+\frac{2}{\pi}\nabla\int_{1}^{\infty}(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}dk

and we define

\displaystyle R_{L}=\frac{2}{\pi}\nabla\int_{0}^{1}(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}dk

\displaystyle R_{H}=\frac{2}{\pi}\nabla\int_{1}^{\infty}(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}dk

We call
R_{L}
and
R_{H}
the low and high energy part of
R
. In the view of
[
21
, Proposition 6.1]
, we know

is bounded in
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
. Then by using Theorem
3.2
below with
r=1
, we get

is bounded in
L^{n^{*},1}
. Therefore, instead of proving Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to show the end point result for low energy part,
R_{L}
. The result of
[
21
, Proposition 6.1]
can be stated in the following.

Proposition 2.2
.

The Riesz transform localized to high energies,
R_{H}
, is bounded on
L^{p}({\mathcal{M}})
for
1<p<\infty
. In addition, it is of weak type (1,1), that is, it is bounded from
L^{1}({\mathcal{M}})
to
L^{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}})
.

2.3.
Low energy parametrix

In this section, we briefly introduce the methods used in

At first for each
\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i}

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that each

. Let

by

(\Delta+k^{2})(G_{1}(k)+G_{2}(k)+G_{3}(k))=I+E(k)

which can be calculated explicitly as

\displaystyle E(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\Big(-2\nabla\phi_{i}(z)\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})\big(\nabla_{z}(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i}}+k^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})-\nabla_{z}G_{int}(z,z^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle+\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})v_{i}(z)\big(-(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i}}+k^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})+G_{int}(z,z^{\prime})+(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i}}+k^{2})^{-1}(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\big((\Delta+k^{2})G_{int}(z,z^{\prime})-\delta_{z^{\prime}}(z)\big)\left(1-\sum_{1}^{l}\phi_{i}(z)\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})\right)

Note that
E(k)(z,z^{\prime})
is smooth and compactly supported in the first variable where precisely

\textrm{supp}(E(z,z^{\prime}))\subset\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\textrm{supp}(\nabla\phi_{i})\right)\times\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}^{2}

Moreover if we let
\eta(z)
be the characteristic function of the set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\rm{supp}(\nabla\phi_{i})
. By (
2.5
), we have

(2.12)

|E(k)(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim\begin{cases}\eta(z),&z^{\prime}\in K\\
\eta(z)d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime})^{1-n_{j}}\textrm{exp}\left(-ck\cdot d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime})\right),&z^{\prime}\in E_{j}\end{cases}

Now for each
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
we define function
v_{z^{\prime}}(z)=E(k)(z,z^{\prime})
which is in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
. By Lemma
2.3
, we can set

G_{4}(k)(z,z^{\prime})=-(\Delta+k^{2})^{-1}v_{z^{\prime}}(z)

and thus

(\Delta+k^{2})G(k)=I

which implies that the parametrix
G(k)
is actually the exact resolvent of
\Delta
in low energy.

Moreover, Lemma
2.3
also tells us
\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\times\mathcal{M}_{i}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle|\nabla G_{4}(k)(z,z^{\prime})|\lesssim

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1,&z\in K,z^{\prime}\in K\\
d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime})^{1-n_{j}}\textrm{exp}(-ck\cdot d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime})),&z\in K,z^{\prime}\in E_{j}\\
d(z_{i}^{0},z)^{1-n_{i}}\textrm{exp}(-ck\cdot d(z_{i}^{0},z)),&z\in E_{i},z^{\prime}\in K\\
d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime})^{1-n_{j}}d(z_{i}^{0},z)^{1-n_{i}}\textrm{exp}(-ck\cdot d(z_{j}^{0},z^{\prime}))\textrm{exp}(-ck\cdot d(z_{i}^{0},z)),&z\in E_{i},z^{\prime}\in E_{j}\end{cases}

where
1\leq i,j\leq l
.

3.
Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities and Lorentz spaces

In this section, we prove Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities by using the tools in Lorentz spaces. In section 3.2 and 3.3, we generalize Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya inequality with settings of homogeneous space and inhomogeneous space separately when operator acting between spaces with different ”dimensions”. Finally, we use these results to give a strong type
(p,q)
picture for part of
R_{L}
as an application.

In the case of
\mathbb{R}
, Riesz transform is equivalent with Hilbert transform. In
[
26
]
, Riesz proved that Riesz transform on one-dimension is bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1<p<\infty
which motivated some further studies like the boundedness of the Hilbert transform on half-line:

f\mapsto\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x+y}dy

and the famous Hardy’s inequality:

\|\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}|f(t)|dt\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)}\hskip 10.00002pt1<p<\infty

In
[
18
, Theorem 319]
, authors generalize these results to operators with homogeneous kernel which we call ”
Hardy-Hilbert type
”.

Inequalities originated from
[
18
, Theorem 319]
with new settings have their own interests. The results listed in this section which are related to the proof of Theorem
1.3
are Section 3.1 and Theorem
3.9
.

3.1.
Hardy-Littlewood inequality

In what follows we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Hardy-Littlewood)
.

For all
\mu
measurable function
f,g
, we have

\left|\int_{X}fgd\mu\right|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{*}(t)g^{*}(t)dt

where
f^{*}
and
g^{*}
represent the decreasing rearrangement functions.

The proof can be found in
[
24
, Theorem 2.4]
. Note that Hardy-Littlewood inequality connects the integral-type operator with the Lorentz quasi-norm since the right-hand side can be estimated as

\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{*}(t)g^{*}(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\right)\left(t^{1/p^{\prime}}g^{*}(t)\right)\frac{dt}{t}\leq\|f\|_{(p,q)}\|g\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})}

which plays a crucial role in the proof of our main result. We also mention the
nested
property of Lorentz spaces. That is
\forall 0<p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq r\leq\infty
we have

\|f\|_{(p,r)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(p,q)}

Before stating the next theorem, we first recall that a linear operator on the measure space
(X,\mu)
is of
restricted weak type

(p,q)
if for all measurable subset
A
of
X
with finite measure we have

\|T(\chi_{A})\|_{(q,\infty)}\lesssim\mu(A)^{1/p}

We will use the following interpolation theorem
[
12
, Theorem 1.4.19]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<r<\infty
,
0<p_{0}\neq p_{1}<\infty
, and
0<q_{0}\neq q_{1}\leq\infty
and let
(X,\mu)
,
(Y,\nu)
be
\sigma\text{-}
finite measure spaces. Let
T
be a linear operator defined on the space of simple functions on
X
and taking values in the set of measurable functions on
Y
. Assume that
T
is of restricted weak type
(p_{0},q_{0})
and
(p_{1},q_{1})
. Then for
0<\theta<1
and

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}

we have

\|T(f)\|_{(q,r)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(p,r)}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall f\in L^{p,r}

For the proof we refer readers to
[
12
, Theorem 1.4.19]
. Note that the above result can be improved to strong type estimate, i.e.
\|T(f)\|_{q}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}
, only when
q\geq p
by
nested
property.

Remark 3.3
.

We can regain the boundedness of
R
for
1<p<n^{*}
by the following procedure. First by Theorem
1.1
we know that
R
is weak type
(1,1)
which directly implies
R
is restricted weak
(1,1)
. By Theorem
1.3
and
nested
property of Lorentz spaces, we know that

\|R(f)\|_{(n^{*},\infty)}\lesssim\|R(f)\|_{(n^{*},1)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

which implies
R
is restricted weak
(n^{*},n^{*})
since for any set
A
with
\mu(A)<\infty
we have

\|\chi_{A}\|_{(n^{*},1)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{1/n^{*}}\chi_{A}^{*}(t)\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{1/n^{*}-1}\chi_{[0,\mu(A)]}(t)dt=n^{*}\mu(A)^{1/n^{*}}

Now an application of Theorem
3.2
with
r=p
for each
1<p<n^{*}
, we regain the boundedness of Riesz transform.

3.2.
Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities on homogeneous spaces

In
[
18
, Theorem 319]
, authors consider integral operators with homogeneous kernels on space
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Before we state their original result, we recall that a kernel
K(x,y)
is homogeneous of degree
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
if

K(\lambda x,\lambda y)=\lambda^{\delta}K(x,y)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\lambda>0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y

Theorem 3.4

(Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya)
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
and
K(x,y)
is non-negative defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with homogeneous degree
-1
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,1)x^{-1/p}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(1,y)y^{-1/p^{\prime}}dy=k

then we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)f(x)g(y)dxdy\leq k\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(y)|^{p^{\prime}}dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

Particularly, when
K(x,y)=\frac{1}{x+y}
, the theorem proves the
L^{p}
boundedness of the Hilbert transform on half line. Moreover, for
K(x,y)=\frac{1}{x}\chi_{y\leq x}(x)
, Theorem
3.4
is the famous Hardy’s inequality. In the following, we call an operator ”
Hardy-Hilbert type
” if it is defined via a homogeneous kernel.

For
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 1
, we define measures
d\mu_{j}=r^{d_{j}-1}dr
for
j=1,2
. And we consider multiplicative group
(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})
. For simplicity, we still use
*
to denote the convolution in this group i.e.

(f*g)(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)g(x/y)\frac{dy}{y}=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x/y)g(y)\frac{dy}{y}

To generalize Theorem
3.4
, we will use the following result. See
[
12
, Page from 18]
for more detail.

Theorem 3.5

(Young’s convolution inequality)
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that

1+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}

then for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)
and
g\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)
we have

\|f*g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx/x)}

With Theorem
3.5
in mind, we generalize Theorem
3.4
as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
K(x,y)
is non-negative with homogeneous degree of
-n
where
n=d_{2}/p+d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,1)x^{d_{2}/p-1}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(1,y)y^{d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-1}dy=k<\infty

Then the operator

\mathcal{K}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)f(y)d\mu_{1}(y)

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{1})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2})
with norm at most
k
.

Proof.

Let
g(x)=x^{d_{1}/p}f(x)
and
h(x)=x^{n-d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}}K(x,1)
. Then the operator applies to function
f
is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,y)f(y)y^{d_{1}}\frac{dy}{y}=\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-n}K(x/y,1)\left(y^{d_{1}/p}f(y)\right)\frac{dy}{y}

\displaystyle=x^{d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-n}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x/y)g(y)\frac{dy}{y}=x^{d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-n}(h*g)(x)

Thus we have by Theorem
3.5
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2})}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{p(d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-n)+d_{2}}|(h*g)(x)|^{p}\frac{dx}{x}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|h*g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})}\leq\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})}=\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{1})}

Note that

\|h\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{dx}{x})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{n-d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}-1}K(x,1)dx=k

which completes the proof.

∎

Immediately, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
1<p\leq q
and
1+1/q=1/p+1/r
.
K(x,y)
is non-negative with homogeneous degree of
-n
where
n=d_{1}/{p^{\prime}}+d_{2}/q
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x,1)^{r}x^{\frac{rd_{2}}{q}-1}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(1,y)^{r}y^{\frac{rd_{1}}{p^{\prime}}-1}dy=k

Then

\|\mathcal{K}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2})}\leq k^{1/r}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{1})}

Also, we can extend the above results to higher dimensions.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
1<p\leq q
and
1+1/q=1/p+1/r
.
K(x,y)
is a kernel defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\setminus(0,0)
which is radial and non-negative with homogeneous degree of
-n
where
n=d_{1}/p^{\prime}+d_{2}/q
. Set
K_{0}(|x|,|y|)=K(x,y)
be the function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{0}(s,1)^{r}s^{\frac{rd_{2}}{q}-1}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{0}(1,t)^{r}t^{\frac{rd_{1}}{p^{\prime}}-1}dt=k

Then

\|\mathcal{K}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}

where

\mathcal{K}(f)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}K(x,y)f(y)dy=C_{d_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{0}(|x|,t)f(\theta t)t^{d_{1}-1}dtd\theta

\displaystyle:=C_{d_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(x)d\theta

Since
K
is radial, thus for each
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}
,
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)
is radial. Hence

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(x)|^{q}dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=C_{q,d_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(|s|)|^{q}s^{d_{2}-1}ds\right)^{1/q}=C_{q,d_{2}}\|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(|.|)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2})}

By Corollary
3.7
, we have

\|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(|.|)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2})}\leq k^{1/r}\|f(\theta(.))\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{1})}

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\approx\|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(x)d\theta\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}},dx)}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\|\mathcal{K}_{\theta}(f)(|.|)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{+},d\mu_{2}(x))}d\theta\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(\theta t)|^{p}t^{d_{1}-1}dt\right)^{1/p}d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d_{1}-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(\theta t)|^{p}t^{d_{1}-1}dtd\theta\right)^{1/p}\approx\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}

∎

3.3.
Hardy-Hilbert type inequalities on inhomogeneous spaces

To prove our main results Theorem
1.3
, we need an ”endpoint-type” version of Theorem
3.6
in some different settings. The initial point is to consider ”
Hardy-Hilbert type
” operator
\mathcal{K}
acting between measure spaces
(X,\mu_{1})
and
(X,\mu_{2})
with kernel:

K(x,y)=\begin{cases}x^{-\alpha}y^{-\beta},&x\leq y\\
x^{-\alpha^{\prime}}y^{-\beta^{\prime}},&x>y\end{cases}

where
\alpha+\beta=\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}
satisfying
\alpha>0,0<\beta\leq d_{1}
and
0<\alpha^{\prime}\leq d_{2},\beta^{\prime}>0
. Let
X=[1,\infty)
,
d\mu_{j}
as defined in the last subsection and we want to study the
(p,q)
boundedness of
\mathcal{K}
. That is we aim to find
(p,q)
such that
\mathcal{K}
maps
L^{p}(X,d\mu_{1})
to
L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})
.

Split
\mathcal{K}
into two parts,

\displaystyle R_{1}(f)(x)=x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-\beta}f(y)d\mu_{1}(y)

\displaystyle R_{2}(f)(x)=x^{-\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{1}^{x}y^{-\beta^{\prime}}f(y)d\mu_{1}(y)

Observe that the adjoint operator of
R_{1}
:

R_{1}^{*}(f)(x)=x^{-\beta}\int_{1}^{x}y^{-\alpha}f(y)d\mu_{2}(y)

is exactly
R_{2}
by replacing
\alpha\to\beta^{\prime}
,
\beta\to\alpha^{\prime}
,
d_{1}\to d_{2}
and
d_{2}\to d_{1}
. Thus instead of studying
\mathcal{K}
itself, it is enough to study the boundedness of
R_{1}
. And the results for
R_{2}
follows by duality and replacing indexes.
\mathcal{K}
has also been studied in
[
13
, Proposition 5.1]
and
[
22
, Lemma 5.4]
. We mention that the results in the following do not directly imply Theorem
1.3
, but the ideas are exactly the same.

Theorem 3.9
.

For
0<\beta<d_{1}
,
R_{1}
is bounded from
L^{p,1}(X,d\mu_{1})
to
L^{q,\infty}(X,d\mu_{2})
for
p=\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-\beta}
and
q\geq\frac{d_{2}}{\alpha}
. Moreover,
R_{1}
is of weak type
(1,r)
for all
r\geq\frac{d_{2}}{\alpha+\beta}
for all
0<\beta\leq d_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
x\geq 1
, and
f\in L^{p,1}(X,d\mu_{1})
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq x^{-\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}|f(y)|y^{-\beta}\chi_{[x,\infty)}(y)d\mu_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq x^{-\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(d\mu_{1})}\|F_{x}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(d\mu_{1})}

where
F_{x}(y)=y^{-\beta}\chi_{[x,\infty)}(y)
. Note that

\|F_{x}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(d\mu_{1})}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda d_{F_{x}}(\lambda)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

where
d_{F_{x}}(\lambda)=\mu_{1}\left(\{y\geq x:y^{-\beta}>\lambda\}\right)
.

Since

d_{F_{x}}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{d_{1}}(\lambda^{-d_{1}/\beta}-x^{d_{1}})\chi_{0<\lambda<x^{-\beta}}

thus

\displaystyle\|F_{x}\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(d\mu_{1})}=\left(\sup_{0<\lambda<x^{-\beta}}\lambda^{p^{\prime}}d_{1}^{-1}(\lambda^{-{d_{1}}/\beta}-x^{d_{1}})\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=d_{1}^{-1/{p^{\prime}}}\left(\sup_{0<\lambda<x^{-\beta}}\lambda^{p^{\prime}-d_{1}/\beta}-x^{d_{1}}\lambda^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\leq C_{d_{1},p}

since
p^{\prime}=d_{1}/\beta
. Note that this estimate is uniform in
x
. Thus, we get

(3.1)

|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq C_{d_{1},p}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(d\mu_{1})}x^{-\alpha}

Finally, since
x^{-\alpha}\in L^{q,\infty}(X,d\mu_{2})
whenever
q\geq d_{2}/\alpha
the result of the first part follows.

For the second part, we simply have

(3.2)

|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}|f(y)|y^{-\beta}d\mu_{1}(y)\leq x^{-(\alpha+\beta)}\|f\|_{L^{1}(d\mu_{1})}

and the result follows by
x^{-(\alpha+\beta)}\in L^{r,\infty}(X,d\mu_{2})
whenever
r\geq\frac{d_{2}}{\alpha+\beta}
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is clear from (
3.1
),

\|R_{1}(f)\|_{L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p,1}(X,d\mu_{1})}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall q>d_{2}/\alpha

Then by Theorem
3.2
, we have
R_{1}
is
(p,q)
bounded in the intersection of the open quadrilateral with vertexes
(\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}},0)
,
(1,0)
,
(1,\frac{\alpha+\beta}{d_{2}})
,
(\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}},\frac{\alpha}{d_{2}})
and the set
\{(1/p,1/q)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]:1/q\leq 1/p\}
in the plane
1/p\text{-}1/q
. Moreover, by Marcinkiewicz’s interpolation theorem, the second part tells us
R_{1}
is bounded from
L^{1}(X,d\mu_{1})\to L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})
for all
\frac{d_{2}}{\alpha+\beta}<q<\infty
.

Next we treat the upper boundary of the quadrilateral. Let
\epsilon>0
and set
\frac{1}{p_{\epsilon}}=\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}}+\epsilon
and
\frac{1}{q_{\epsilon}}=\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p_{\epsilon}}-\frac{d_{1}-\alpha-\beta}{d_{2}}
. Then the following lemma gives a weak estimate on that line segment.

Lemma 3.11
.

For
0<\beta\leq d_{1}
,
R_{1}
is of weak type
(p_{\epsilon},q_{\epsilon})
for all
\epsilon>0
such that
(1/p_{\epsilon},1/q_{\epsilon})\in(0,1)\times(0,1)
.

Proof.

Note that
\forall x\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}|f(y)|y^{-\beta}y^{d_{1}-1}dy

\displaystyle\leq x^{-\alpha}\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{d_{1}-\beta p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}-1}\right)^{1/p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim x^{d_{1}/{p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}-\alpha-\beta}\|f\|_{L^{p_{\epsilon}}(X,d\mu_{1})}

Now suppose that for some
\lambda>0
we have

|R_{1}(f)(x)|>\lambda

then we have

\lambda\lesssim x^{d_{1}/{p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}}-\alpha-\beta}\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}

which implies

x^{q_{\epsilon}(\alpha+\beta-d_{1}/{p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}})}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}^{q_{\epsilon}}}{\lambda^{q_{\epsilon}}}\implies x\lesssim\left(\frac{\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}^{q_{\epsilon}}}{\lambda^{q_{\epsilon}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{\epsilon}(\alpha+\beta-d_{1}/{p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}})}}

thus

\mu_{2}\{x\geq 1:|R_{1}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}\lesssim\left(\frac{\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}^{q_{\epsilon}}}{\lambda^{q_{\epsilon}}}\right)^{\frac{d_{2}}{q_{\epsilon}(\alpha+\beta-d_{1}/{p_{\epsilon}^{\prime}})}}=\left(\frac{\|f\|_{p_{\epsilon}}}{\lambda}\right)^{q_{\epsilon}}

∎

Remark 3.12
.

Note that for the case
\beta=d_{1}
,
R_{1}
is
(p,q)
bounded for
1<p<\infty
and
1/q<d_{1}/(d_{2}p)+\alpha/d_{2}
. Indeed, by Hölder’s inequality,

|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq x^{-\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{1})}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-d_{1}p^{\prime}+d_{1}-1}dy\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\approx x^{\frac{d_{1}(1-p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}-\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{1})}

and
\|R_{1}(f)\|_{q}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}
follows.

To verify the negative part of Theorem
3.9
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
0<\beta\leq d_{1}
.

(a).
For
\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}}
,
R_{1}
is not bounded from
L^{p}(X,d\mu_{1})
to
L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})
for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

(b).
For
\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}}<\frac{1}{p}\leq 1
,
R_{1}
is not bounded from
L^{p}(X,d\mu_{1})
to
L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})
for

\frac{1}{q}>\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d_{1}-\alpha-\beta}{d_{2}}

Proof.

(a). Let
p\geq\min(\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-\beta},\infty)
. We consider function
f(y)=y^{\beta-d_{1}}(1+\log y)^{-1}
. We can see that if
\beta<d_{1}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-p(d_{1}-\beta)}(1+\log y)^{-p}y^{d_{1}-1}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-cx}(1+x)^{-p}dx<\infty

where
c=p(d_{1}-\beta)-d_{1}\geq 0
.

And if
\beta=d_{1}
,

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1

However
\forall x\in X
,

R_{1}(f)(x)=x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-1}(1+\log y)^{-1}dy=x^{-\alpha}\int_{\log x}^{\infty}(1+t)^{-1}dt=\infty

which gives a counter example.

(b). Let
1\leq p<\min(\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-\beta},\infty)
. We consider function
f(y)=y^{-\frac{d_{1}+\delta}{p}}
for some
\delta>0
to be determined later. Then

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-d_{1}-\delta+d_{1}-1}dy\lesssim_{\delta}1

and

R_{1}(f)(x)=x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-\beta-\frac{d_{1}+\delta}{p}+d_{1}-1}dy\approx_{\beta,d_{1},\delta,p}x^{d_{1}-\alpha-\beta-\frac{d_{1}+\delta}{p}}

since
p<\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-\beta}<\frac{d_{1}+\delta}{d_{1}-\beta}
.

Hence

(3.3)

\|R_{1}(f)\|_{q}^{q}\approx\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{q(d_{1}-\alpha-\beta)-\frac{q}{p}(d_{1}+\delta)+d_{2}-1}dx

Note that

\frac{1}{q}>\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d_{1}-\alpha-\beta}{d_{2}}\implies 0<\frac{p}{q}\cdot d_{2}-d_{1}+p(d_{1}-\alpha-\beta)

Therefore, by choosing

0<\delta\leq\frac{p}{q}\cdot d_{2}-d_{1}+p(d_{1}-\alpha-\beta)

(
3.3
) is unbounded.

∎

Finally, we introduce the following lemma to complete the picture of the

.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
0<\beta\leq d_{1}
.

(a) If
\alpha+\beta\leq d_{2}
. Then
R_{1}
is bounded from

to

(b) For
\max(\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}},0)<\frac{1}{p}\leq 1

is bounded from
L^{p}(X,d\mu_{1})
to

(a) Note that

\displaystyle R_{1}(f)(x)=x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-\beta}f(y)d\mu_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\beta}\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\big(x^{-\alpha}\chi_{(1,y)}(x)\big)|f(y)|d\mu_{1}(y)

Then by Minkowski’s integral inequality, we have

\|R_{1}(f)\|_{L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})}\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\beta}\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\|x^{-\alpha}\chi_{(1,y)}(x)\|_{L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})}|f(y)|d\mu_{1}(y)

Notice that

\|x^{-\alpha}\chi_{(0,y)}(x)\|_{L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})}^{q}=\int_{1}^{y}x^{-\frac{\alpha d_{2}}{\alpha+\beta}+d_{2}-1}dx\lesssim_{d_{2},\alpha,\beta}y^{\frac{\beta d_{2}}{\alpha+\beta}}

\displaystyle\|R_{1}(f)\|_{L^{q}(X,d\mu_{2})}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\beta}\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}y^{\beta}|f(y)|d\mu_{1}(y)

(b) The case

is obvious and for any

\forall x\geq 1

\displaystyle|R_{1}(f)(x)|\leq x^{-\alpha}\int_{x}^{\infty}|f(y)|y^{-\beta}d\mu_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{p}\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{d_{1}-\beta p^{\prime}-1}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

∎

Introduce notions:

\displaystyle D_{d_{1},d_{2}}^{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]:\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{d_{1}}{d_{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d_{1}-\alpha-\beta}{d_{2}},\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}}<\frac{1}{p}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]:\frac{1}{q}<\frac{\alpha}{d_{2}},\frac{d_{1}-\beta}{d_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{p}\leq 1\right\}

Now putting things together, we have the following.

, we have
\forall z\in E_{i}
,

\displaystyle|T(f)(z)|\lesssim|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\int_{\{z^{\prime}:d(z,z^{\prime})>\epsilon\}}|\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})|\int_{0}^{1}|(\Delta_{i}+k^{2})^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})|dk|f(z^{\prime})|d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\int_{E_{i}}\left(d(z,z^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}(1-\chi_{D})\right)|f(z^{\prime})|d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{*}(t)f^{*}(t)dt=|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}g^{*}(t)\right)\left(t^{1/n^{*}}f^{*}(t)\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}

where
g(z^{\prime})=d(z,z^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}(1-\chi_{D})
.

Recall the notion in Lorentz space,
\forall 0<\alpha<\epsilon^{1-n_{i}}

d_{g}(\alpha)=\mu_{i}\{z^{\prime}:d(z,z^{\prime})>\epsilon,d(z,z^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}>\alpha\}\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{-n_{i}}{n_{i}-1}}

and
d_{g}(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha\geq\epsilon^{1-n_{i}}
.

Hence

\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}\lesssim\sup\limits_{0<\alpha<\epsilon^{1-n_{i}}}\alpha^{1-\frac{n_{i}(n^{*}-1)}{(n_{i}-1)n^{*}}}=\epsilon^{1-n_{i}/n^{*}}

Therefore, we have
\forall z\in E_{i}
,

|T(f)(z)|\lesssim|\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}(z)|\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

Consequently

\|T(f)\|_{(n^{*},1)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

since
\nabla_{z}\phi_{i}
is bounded and compactly supported:

\|\nabla\phi_{i}\|_{(n^{*},1)}\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}d_{\nabla\phi_{i}}(\alpha)^{1/n^{*}}d\alpha\leq\mu_{i}\left(\textrm{supp}(\nabla\phi_{i})\right)^{1/n^{*}}\|\nabla\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}<\infty

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
T_{3}
be the operator defined in (
4.2
). Then there exists some constant

such that

\|T_{3}(f)\|_{(n^{*},1)}\leq C\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

Proof.

Let
z_{i}^{0},z_{j}^{0}
be fixed points in
K_{i},K_{j}
respectively. For simplicity we set

\displaystyle d(z_{i}^{0},K_{i}^{c})=d(z_{j}^{0},K_{j}^{c})=1

Since
T_{3}
is defined on whole
\mathcal{M}
. It suffices to investigate the behaviors of operator defined on
\mathcal{M}

S(f)(z)=\int_{E_{i}}\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})f(z^{\prime})\int_{0}^{1}(\Delta_{i}+k^{2})^{-1}(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})(\nabla_{z}u_{i})(z,k)dkd\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

for
1\leq i\leq l

We first consider the case when
z
is not in the connection
K
. Let
z\in E_{j}
where
1\leq i,j\leq l
. Then by Lemma
2.3
we have

S(f)(z)\lesssim\int_{E_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|\left(\int_{0}^{1}d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}d(z_{j}^{0},z)^{1-n_{j}}e^{-ck(d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})+d(z_{j}^{0},z))}dk\right)d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

Set
r=d(z_{j}^{0},z)
and
r^{\prime}=d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle|S(f)(z)|\lesssim\int_{E_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|\frac{(r^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}r^{1-n_{j}}}{r^{\prime}+r}d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|G(z,z^{\prime})d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

where
G(z,z^{\prime})=\min\big((r^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}r^{1-n_{j}},(r^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}r^{-n_{j}}\big)

(r^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}r^{-n_{j}},&1\leq r^{\prime}\leq r\end{cases}

Therefore

\displaystyle|S(f)(z)|\lesssim r^{-n_{j}}\int_{1\leq r^{\prime}\leq r}(r^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})+r^{1-n_{j}}\int_{r^{\prime}>r}(r^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

Now a similar argument in Theorem
3.9
gives that

|S(f)(z)|\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}\left(r^{-n_{j}}\sup_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{1}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}+r^{1-n_{j}}\sup_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{2}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}\right)

where
g_{1}(z^{\prime})=(r^{\prime})^{2-n_{i}}\chi_{1\leq r^{\prime}\leq r}

g_{2}(z^{\prime})=(r^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}\chi_{r^{\prime}>r}

Note that
\forall\alpha>0

(4.4)

d_{g_{1}}(\alpha)\lesssim\begin{cases}0,&\alpha\geq 1\\
\alpha^{\frac{-n_{i}}{n_{i}-2}},&r^{2-n_{i}}\leq\alpha<1\\

\alpha^{\frac{-n_{i}}{n_{i}-1}},&0<\alpha<r^{1-n_{i}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{1}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}\lesssim r^{2-\frac{n_{i}}{n^{*}}}\hskip 10.00002pt\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{2}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}\lesssim r^{1-\frac{n_{i}}{n^{*}}}

Consequently, we have
\forall z\in E_{j}
,
(1\leq j\leq l)

|S(f)(z)|\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}r^{2-n_{j}-n_{i}/n^{*}}

Finally if

. We simply have

|S(f)(z)|\lesssim\int_{E_{i}}|f(z^{\prime})|(r^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{3}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}

\sup\limits_{\alpha>0}\alpha d_{g_{3}}(\alpha)^{1/(n^{*})^{\prime}}\lesssim 1^{-(n_{i}-n^{*})/n^{*}}=1

Whence

\displaystyle|S(f)(z)|\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}\left(\chi_{K}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\chi_{E_{j}}(z)[d(z,z_{j}^{0})]^{2-n_{j}-n_{i}/n^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{0}^{1}\alpha^{\frac{n_{j}}{2n^{*}-n^{*}n_{j}-n_{i}}}d\alpha\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},1)}

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
p>1
,
R
is not bounded from
L^{n^{*},p}\to L^{n^{*},p}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
and Theorem
3.2
, we know that

\|R_{H}(f)\|_{(n^{*},p)}\lesssim\|f\|_{(n^{*},p)}

Therefore, we only need to consider the low energy part,
R_{L}
. Thanks to
[
21
, Proposition 6.1]
, after a series of simplifications, it suffices to show the operator:

f\mapsto a(z)\int_{E_{i}}b(z^{\prime})f(z^{\prime})d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

where
a(z)
is a function compactly supported on an end, say
E_{j}
, and not identically zero and

b(z^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}(\Delta_{i}+k^{2})^{-1}(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})\phi_{i}(z^{\prime})dk\approx d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}

does not bounded from
L^{n_{i},p}
to
L^{n^{*},p}
for
1\leq i\leq l
.

We prove it by giving a counterexample. Particularly, we consider function

f(z^{\prime})=d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})^{-1}\left(1+|\log[d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})]|\right)^{-\beta}\hskip 10.00002pt1/p<\beta\leq 1

It is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{E_{i}}b(z^{\prime})f(z^{\prime})d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})\approx\int_{E_{i}}d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})^{1-n_{i}}d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})^{-1}\left(1+|\log[d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})]|\right)^{-\beta}d\mu_{i}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-1}(1+|\log(r)|)^{-\beta}dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+|x|)^{-\beta}dx=\infty

sicne
\beta\leq 1
.

Hence, we only need to verify that
f\in L^{n_{i},p}(E_{i})
. Notice that
f
is non-negative ”radial” and decreasing. Set
f_{0}(r)=r^{-1}(1+|\log(r)|)^{-\beta}
then we simply have
f(z^{\prime})=f_{0}(d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime}))
. Moreover, since

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s>0:d_{f}(s)\leq t\}

and

\displaystyle d_{f}(s)=\mu_{i}\{z^{\prime}\in E_{i}:|f(z^{\prime})|>s\}=\mu_{i}\{z^{\prime}\in E_{i}:f_{0}(d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime}))>s\}

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}\{z^{\prime}\in E_{i}:1\leq d(z_{i}^{0},z^{\prime})<f_{0}^{-1}(s)\}\lesssim[f_{0}^{-1}(s)]^{n_{i}}

Therefore

\displaystyle f^{*}(t)\lesssim\inf\{s>0:[f_{0}^{-1}(s)]^{n_{i}}\leq t\}=\inf\{s>0:s\geq f_{0}(t^{1/{n_{i}}})\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(t^{1/{n_{i}}})=t^{-1/{n_{i}}}(1+n_{i}^{-1}|\log(t)|)^{-\beta}

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{(n_{i},p)}^{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{1/{n_{i}}}f^{*}(t)]^{p}\frac{dt}{t}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+n_{i}^{-1}|\log(t)|)^{-\beta p}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+|x|)^{-p\beta}dx<\infty

since
\beta>1/p
. As this argument applies to each end, the proof is complete.

Iterated priority arguments in Descriptive Set Theory
Adam Day, Noam Greenberg, Matthew Harrison-Trainor, Dan Turetsky

Abstract
. We present the true stages machinery and illustrate its applications to descriptive set theory. We use this machinery to provide new proofs of the Hausdorff-Kuratowski and Wadge theorems on the structure of 𝚫 ξ 0 \mathbf{\Delta}^{0}_{\xi} , Louveau and Saint-Raymond’s separation theorem, and Louveau’s separation theorem.



1.
Introduction

Ever since Mostowski
[
Mos47
]
and Addison
[
Add59
]
observed the connections between Kleene’s work on computability and the hyperarithmetic hierarchy
[
Kle43
,
Kle55a
]
and Lusin and Souslin’s study of Borel and analytic sets
[
Lus17
,
Sus17
]
, effective methods have been widely utilised in descriptive set theory. The computable theory gives alternative proofs of classical arguments, for example Addison’s proof of Kondo’s co-analytic uniformisation
[
Kon38
]
, Sack’s proof of the measurability of analytic sets (see
[
Sac90
, Lem.II.6.2]
), or Solovay’s effectivised Galvin-Prikry theorem
[
Sol78
]
. More recently, effective tools were used for the
E_{0}
-dichotomy for Borel equivalence relations (Harrington, Kechris and Louveau
[
HKL90
]
) and the
G_{0}
-dichotomy for Borel graph colourings (Kechris, Solecki and Todorcevic
[
KST99
]
). Perhaps the most prominent interaction of the effective and classical hierarchies appear in Louveau’s separation theorem
[
Lou80
]
, and other work of Loueveau, Saint-Raymond and Debs
[
LSR87
,
LSR88
,
Deb87
]
.

Beyond the basic understanding of the hyperarithmetic hierarchy as a refinement of the Borel one, a number of tools involving computability theory have been used in these studies, for instance computability on admissible sets, and the Gandy-Harrington topology. However, the most unique and central technique of computability, the priority method initiated by Friedberg
[
Fri57
]
and Muchnik
[
Muč56
]
, appeared to have limited application to questions of set theory. Martin’s original proof of Borel determinacy
[
Mar75
]
had a finite-injury aspect to it; recent work by Lecomte and Zeleny
[
LZ14
]
involves an infinite-injury argument. These appeared to be isolated incidents.

This changed very recently, first with work of Day, Downey and Westrick studying topological degrees of discontinuous functions
[
DDW
]
, and then with Day and Marks’s resolution of the decomposability conjecture
[
DM
]
. Both of these used iterated priority arguments, in particular, as formulated by Montalbán’s
true stages
machinery.

Iterated priority arguments originated in works of Harrington (unpublished, see
[
Kni90a
,
Kni90b
]
) and Ash (
[
Ash86
]
, see
[
AK00
]
) in computable structure theory. These are complex priority arguments whose iterated nature reflects the hyperarithmetic hierarchy. They are used to construct computable objects while satisfying some ordinal-height system of requirements and guessing at non-computable information. The idea is to break such a construction down into smaller, more tractable pieces. A typical application is the Ash-Watnick theorem
[
Ash90
]
, which states that if
\delta
is a computable ordinal and
\mathcal{L}
is a
\Delta^{0}_{2\delta+1}
linear ordering, then
\mathbb{Z}^{\delta}\cdot\mathcal{L}
has a computable copy. Here we guess at what
\mathcal{L}
is, while building approximations to an iteration (of length
\delta
) of the Hausdorff derivative of our copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{\delta}\cdot\mathcal{L}
. Another style of iterated priority arguments has been formulated by Lempp and Lerman
[
LL97
,
Ler10
]
, who use sequences of trees of strategies to unfold complicated requirements.

Montalbán
[
Mon14
]
extended Ash’s metatheorem and presented it in a dynamic way, using the concept of true stages. This concept generalises Dekker’s non-deficiency stages, those which provide a correct approximation of the halting problem. Perhaps surprisingly, using a relativised version, true stages turn out to also be of use in constructing functions on Baire space and other Polish spaces, as was demonstrated by Day, Downey and Westrick, and Day and Marks. In some sense, however, the history of such applications goes back several decades: Louveau and Saint-Raymond developed a techinque called the ramification method to prove Borel Wadge determinacy
[
LSR87
,
LSR88
]
. An examination of this technique reveals that it is fundamentally a “worker argument”, Harrington’s formulation of his iterated priority arguments.

The purpose of this paper is to describe the general true stages machinery and illustrate its applications to descriptive set theory by providing new proofs of several results. We start with results which do not actually require a priority argument, but for which the machinery gives smooth proofs regardless: we discuss changes of topology, the Hausdorff-Kuratowski theorem on the structure of
\mathbf{\Delta}^{0}_{\xi+1}
, and Wadge’s theorem on the structure of
\mathbf{\Delta}^{0}_{\lambda}
, for
\lambda
limit. We then show how to use true stages for a priority argument, and give a proof of Louveau’s separation theorem.

These studies have applications to reverse mathematics. The question of the axiomatic strength of results in descriptive set theory was raised by Louveau and Saint-Raymond. In
[
LSR88
]
they showed that Borel Wadge determinacy is provable in second-order arithmetic.
This was quite surprising, since the most straightforward proof relies on Borel determinacy, which is known to require strong axioms
[
Fri71
]
; and since
\bm{\Pi}^{1}_{1}
Wadge determinacy was known to be equivalent to full
\bm{\Pi}^{1}_{1}
determinacy (Harrington
[
Har78
]
); the same holds for
\bm{\Pi}^{1}_{2}
(Hjorth
[
Hjo96
]
). The ramification method allowed Louveau and Saint-Raymond to avoid the reliance on Borel determinacy and rather, reduce the Borel Wadge games to closed games. Similarly, the standard proof of Louveau’s separation theorem uses the Gandy-Harrington topology, and can be carried out using the
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-comprehension axiom system. Our proof shows that in fact, the weaker system
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
(arithmetical transfinite recursion) suffices.

This paper is intended as one of a pair. In the companion paper
[
DGHTT
]
, we give a new and effective classification of all Borel Wadge classes, using the true stages machinery. A corollary is a proof of Borel Wadge determinacy in
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}+\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-
\mathsf{IND}
. One of the steps is a proof of the Louveau and Saint-Raymond separation theorem
[
LSR87
]
for all Borel Wadge classes. In the current paper we give the simpler argument, for the classes
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
, from which we derive the Louveau separation theorem.

1.1.
A note on subscripts

In the hyperarithmetic hierarchy, statements about the finite levels of the hierarchy often have an “off by one” error when generalized to the infinite levels. This is illustrated by (and can be viewed as originating from) the following pair of results:

1.1
Post
[
Pos48
]
.

For
n<\omega
and a set
X\subseteq\omega
,
X\leqslant_{T}\emptyset^{(n)}
if and only if
X\in\Delta^{0}_{n+1}
.

1.2
Ash, see
[
AK00
]
.

For
\omega\leqslant\alpha<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
and a set
X\subseteq\omega
,
X\leqslant_{T}\emptyset^{(\alpha)}
if and only if
X\in\Delta^{0}_{\alpha}
.

Observe that the subscript in the first result contains “
+1
”, while the subscript in the second does not. Ultimately, this comes down to the fact that computable corresponds to
\Delta^{0}_{1}
rather than
\Delta^{0}_{0}
. Similarly, the class of open sets is denoted
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{1}
rather than
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{0}
, which contrasts, for example with the Baire hierarchy of Borel functions. This can be unified, however, by making use of
1+\alpha
. Note that for
\alpha<\omega
,
1+\alpha=\alpha+1
, while for
\alpha\geqslant\omega
,
1+\alpha=\alpha
. Thus the following holds:

1.3
.

For
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
and a set
X\subseteq\omega
,
X\leqslant_{T}\emptyset^{(\alpha)}
if and only if
X\in\Delta^{0}_{1+\alpha}
.

We will make extensive use of this device, generally in the subscripts of
\Delta
’s and
\Sigma
’s.

2.
Change of topology and true Stages

A commonly used technique in descriptive set theory is the enrichment of the topology of a Polish space. For simplicity, we shall restrict ourselves in this paper to Baire space
\mathcal{N}=\omega^{\omega}
. The following is a version of
[
Kec95
, Thm.13.1]
:

2.1
.

If
A\subseteq\mathcal{N}
is Borel, then there is a Polish topology on
\mathcal{N}
, extending the standard one, and which has the same Borel sets, in which
A
is open.

The proof given in
[
Kec95
]
is direct, by induction on the Borel rank of
A
. Alternatively, it can be deduced from a characterisation of classes of Borel sets in terms of generalised homeomorphisms, due to Kuratowski
[
Kur33
]
and Sierpiński
[
Sie33
]
. Recall that a function
f\colon Y\to X
between Polish spaces is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
-measurable if
f^{-1}[U]
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
for every open set
U\subseteq X
. A function
f\colon\mathcal{N}\to X
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi+1}
-measurable if and only if it is Baire class
\xi
(see
[
Kec95
, Thm.24.3]
). Kuratowski and Sierpiński essentially showed:

2.2
.

Let
\xi\geqslant 1
. A set
A\subseteq\mathcal{N}
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
if and only if there is a closed set
E\subseteq\mathcal{N}
and a bijection
f\colon\mathcal{N}\to E
such that:

(i)

f
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
-measurable;

(ii)

f^{-1}
is continuous; and

(iii)

A=f^{-1}[B]
for some open
B\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.

For a detailed account see
[
Wad84
, Thm.IV.C.10]
.
Proposition

2.1
can be directly deduced from
Proposition

2.2
by taking the topology on
\mathcal{N}
which makes
f
a homeomorphism between
\mathcal{N}
and
E
; we know that a closed subset of
\mathcal{N}
is Polish.

An effective proof of
Proposition

2.2
was observed by A. Marks. For any
x\in\mathcal{N}
and ordinal
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{x}
(i.e.,
x
computes a copy of
\alpha
), we let
x^{(\alpha)}
denote the the iteration of the Turing jump operator (the relativised halting problem) along
\alpha
, starting with
x
. Spector showed
[
Spe55
]
that the Turing degree of
x^{(\alpha)}
does not depend on the presentation of
\alpha
as an
x
-computable well-ordering. The degree of
x^{(\alpha)}
is Turing complete for the
\Delta^{0}_{1+\alpha}(x)
sets. Under standard definitions,
\{x^{(\alpha)}\,:\,x\in\mathcal{N}\}
is a
\Pi^{0}_{2}
subset of Cantor space. It can, however, be pulled back to a
\Pi^{0}_{1}
subset of Baire space: for each
x
,
x^{(\alpha)}
is a
\Pi^{0}_{2}(x)
singleton, and each
\Pi^{0}_{2}(x)
singleton in Cantor space is Turing equivalent to a
\Pi^{0}_{1}(x)
singleton in Baire space. This is all uniform in
x
. After this renaming, the function
x\mapsto x^{(\alpha)}
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{1+\alpha}
-measurable with closed image and continuous inverse, and the following holds:

2.3
.

Let
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
. A set
A\subseteq\mathcal{N}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
if and only if there is a
\Sigma^{0}_{1}
set
V\subseteq\mathcal{N}
such that
A=\left\{x\,:\,x^{(\alpha)}\in V\right\}
.

The relativised jump functions
x\mapsto(x,z)^{(\alpha)}
are universal: a function
f\colon\mathcal{N}\to X
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{1+\alpha}
-measurable if and only if there is some oracle (parameter)
z
and some continuous funciton
g\colon\mathcal{N}\to X
such that
f(x)=g((x,z)^{(\alpha)})
. Thus,
Proposition

2.2
can be deduced from its effective version
Proposition

2.3
. We can also deduce an effective version of
Proposition

2.1
. For every oracle
z
and every
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{z}
, let the
(z,\alpha)
-topology
on
\mathcal{N}
be the topology generated by the
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}(z)
sets. It extends the standard topology on
\mathcal{N}
, and has the same Borel sets.
Proposition

2.3
implies the following, which in turn implies
Proposition

2.1
:

2.4
.

For every
z
and every
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{z}
, the
(z,\alpha)
-topology is Polish.

2.1.
Enter true stages

Baire space is the set of infinite paths through the tree
(\omega^{<\omega},\preccurlyeq)
. A metric witnessing that
\mathcal{N}
is Polish is derived from the tree:
d(x,y)=2^{-|\sigma|}
, where
\sigma
is the greatest common initial segment of
x
and
y
on the tree. Our first application of the true stages machinery will be an extension of this idea to the
(z,\alpha)
-topology in an internally coherent fashion. For simplicity of notation, we state the unrelativised version (
z=\emptyset
).

The true stages machinery provides, for each computable ordinal
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
, a partial ordering
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
on
\omega^{\leqslant\omega}
with a variety of useful properties. We will list the properties that we will need as they become relevant. We start with the following four.

TSP(1):

For
\sigma,\tau\in\omega^{\leqslant\omega}
,
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
implies
\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
. For
\alpha=0
,
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{0}\tau\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
.

TSP(2):

(\omega^{\leqslant\omega},\preccurlyeq_{\alpha})
is a tree: for all
\tau\in\omega^{\leqslant\omega}
,
\left\{\sigma\,:\,\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau\right\}
is linearly ordered; the root of the tree is
{\left\langle{}\right\rangle}
(the empty sequence).

TSP(3):

For every
x\in\mathcal{N}
,
\left\{\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\,:\,\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x\right\}
is the unique infinite path of the restriction of
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
to
\left\{\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\,:\,\sigma\prec x\right\}
.

TSP(4):

A set
A\subseteq\mathcal{N}
is
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
if and only if there is a c.e. set
U\subseteq\omega^{<\omega}
such that

A=[U]^{\prec}_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{N}\,:\,(\exists\sigma\in U)\,\,\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x\right\}.

Proposition

2.4
for
z=\emptyset
follows: we let
d_{\alpha}(x,y)=2^{-|\sigma|}
, where
\sigma
is the longest string satisfying
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x,y
. The open sets of the
(\emptyset,\alpha)
-topology are precisely the sets
[U]^{\prec}_{\alpha}
for any
U\subseteq\omega^{<\omega}
.
TSP(1)
implies that this generalises the usual topology. In general, for each oracle
z
, we can relativise the machinery to
z
and obtain, for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}^{z}
, a partial ordering
\preccurlyeq^{z}_{\alpha}
with the same properties, except that in
TSP(4)
we replace c.e. by
z
-c.e. and
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
by
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}(z)
.

As this is an expository paper, we will relegate to the companion paper
[
DGHTT
]
the details of the construction of these partial orderings and the verification of their properties. Montalbán first developed his true stages machinery in
[
Mon14
]
, based on his work with Marcone in
[
MM11
]
on the Veblen functions and the iterated Turing jump. Greenberg and Turetsky then gave an alternative development in
[
GT
]
. Both of these were restricted to
x=0^{\infty}
. Day, Downey and Westrick, and Greenberg and Turetsky independently observed that both of these versions can be extended to all of Baire space.

Let us informally describe the main ideas. The relations
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
are defined by recursion on
\alpha
: we first need to define
\preccurlyeq_{\beta}
for all
\beta<\alpha
. To make the construction work, these relations need to be internally coherent, for example, they are nested and continuous:

TSP(5):

If
\alpha\leqslant\beta
, then
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\beta}\tau
implies
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
;

TSP(6):

If
\lambda
is a limit ordinal, then
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\lambda}\tau
if and only if for all
\alpha<\lambda
,
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
.

The idea is to define, for each
finite
sequence
\sigma
, a finite sequence
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
, which is
\sigma
’s guess of an initial segment of the
\alpha
-jump of infinite sequences
x
extending
\sigma
. This guess is independent of the choice of
x\succ\sigma
. The guess may be correct for some choices of
x
and incorrect for others. Roughly, we let
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x
if
\sigma^{(\alpha)}\prec x^{(\alpha)}
, i.e., when
\sigma
’s guess is correct for
x
; we say that such a
\sigma
is an
\alpha
-true
initial segment of
x
. One of the main ideas, though, is the extension of this relation to a relation between two
finite
sequences
\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
. Again, the idea is to let
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
if
\sigma^{(\alpha)}\preccurlyeq\tau^{(\alpha)}
: the guesses of
\sigma
and
\tau
about the
\alpha
-jump do not contradict each other. We say that
\sigma

appears to be
\alpha
-true
to
\tau
. The terminology “true
stage
” comes from the application to computable constructions, as we discuss below. The idea is that in an
x
-computable construction,
x\!\upharpoonright\!{s}
is the stage
s
information we have, and
(x\!\upharpoonright\!{s})^{(\alpha)}
is our stage
s
guess about
x^{(\alpha)}
;
s
is an
(x,\alpha)
-true stage if
x\!\upharpoonright\!{s}\prec_{\alpha}x
.

This definition of
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
is not quite right. It needs to be modified in order to deal with limit ordinals. In this sketch we will ignore this modification; the definition
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\sigma^{(\alpha)}\preccurlyeq\tau^{(\alpha)}
conveys the main idea. The nested condition
TSP(5)
results from the fact that since the Turing jump operator is defined by recursion on the ordinal, in order to compute
\sigma^{(\beta)}
, we need to first, in some sense, compute
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
for all
\alpha<\beta
.
TSP(6)
reflects the fact that
x^{(\lambda)}
is Turing equivalent to the infinite join
\bigoplus_{\alpha<\lambda}x^{(\alpha)}
.

The main difference between the developments in
[
Mon14
]
and
[
GT
]
is that the former first defines the strings
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
and then modifies the resulting relations
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
, whereas the latter gives a simultaneous inductive definition of both
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
and
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}
.

A single step

Here we sketch out a single step, i.e., the construction of
\sigma^{(\alpha+1)}
from
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
. The reader not interested in peeking under the hood may safely skip down to
TSP(7)
.

For each
x
and
\alpha
,
x^{(\alpha+1)}
is an element of Baire space which is Turing equivalent to
(x^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
, the halting problem relative to
x^{(\alpha)}
. Fix a universal oracle Turing machine
M
which enumerates the jump: for all
x
and
e
,
e\in x^{\prime}
if and only if
M^{x}(e)\!\!\downarrow
, i.e., the machine
M
with oracle
x
halts on input
e
. For a finite
\sigma
, we let
\sigma^{\prime}
be the collection of
e
such that
M^{\sigma}_{|\sigma|}(e)\!\!\downarrow
, i.e., those
e
for which the machine
M
, with oracle
\sigma
, halts on input
e
in at most
|\sigma|
many steps.

For each finite sequence
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
, the sequence
\sigma^{(\alpha+1)}
will encode a finite initial segment of
(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
: for some
p_{\alpha}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
e<p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
,
\sigma^{(\alpha+1)}
tells us whether
e\in(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
or not. The idea is the following: if
e\in(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
then
\sigma
can be confident that
e
is in the jump – for all infinite
x
, if
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x
then
e\in(x^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
. For
e<p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
such that
e\notin(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
,
\sigma
is sufficiently brave to declare that
e\notin(x^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
for
x\succ_{\alpha}\sigma
; it may be correct about some such
x
’s, but not about others. For
e\geqslant p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
,
\sigma
makes no commitment about
(x^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}(e)
.

If
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}\tau
, then as
\sigma^{(\alpha)}\preccurlyeq\tau^{(\alpha)}
, we have
(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}\subseteq(\tau^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
: if the machine
M
with oracle
\sigma^{(\alpha)}
halts on
e
in at most
|\sigma^{(\alpha)}|
many steps, then this also holds when the oracle is extended to
\tau^{(\alpha)}
(and more steps are allowed). We let
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha+1}\tau
if
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}\tau
and
\tau
has no proof that
\sigma
was wrong about
(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}\!\upharpoonright\!{p_{\alpha}(\sigma)}
: if for all
e<p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
, if
e\notin(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
then also
e\notin(\tau^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
.

Fixing
x\in\mathcal{N}
, our goal is to ensure, for each
\alpha
, that:

(
*
):

x^{(\alpha)}=\bigcup\left\{\sigma^{(\alpha)}\,:\,\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x\right\}
.

That is, there are infinitely many
\alpha
-true initial segments of
x
, and as they get longer, they give us more and more information about
x^{(\alpha)}
. Suppose that (
*
) is known for some
\alpha
. To ensure that (
*
) also holds for
\alpha+1
, we need to define
p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
wisely. The main idea is that of the “non-definciency stages” of Dekker, in his construction of a hypersimple set in every c.e. degree
[
Dek54
]
. We fix a enumerations of the jump sets
\sigma^{\prime}
so that if
\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
then the enumeration of
\tau^{\prime}
extends that of
\sigma^{\prime}
; and we let
p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
be the last number enumerated into
(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
. So
\sigma
observes that the number
p=p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
has just entered
(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
; it thinks that no smaller numbers will enter the jump later, but is not willing to give an opinion about larger ones. To verify (
*
), for any
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x
, let
k
be the least element of
(x^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}\setminus(\sigma^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
. By (
*
) for
\alpha
, for sufficiently long
\tau\prec_{\alpha}x
we have
k\in(\tau^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
; the least such
\tau
will have
k=p_{\alpha}(\tau)
and so
\tau\prec_{\alpha+1}x
.

While the actual details are a little different, we can now state another important property of the true stages machinery:

TSP(7):

There are functions
p_{\alpha}\colon\omega^{<\omega}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for all
\sigma,\tau\in\omega^{<\omega}
,
\sigma\prec_{\alpha+1}\tau
if and only if
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}\tau
and for all finite
\rho
satisfying
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}\rho\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
we have
p_{\alpha}(\rho)\geqslant p_{\alpha}(\sigma)
.

(
*
) can also explain
TSP(4)
; we give a sketch.

Sketch of derivation of
TSP(4)
:

In one direction, let
A
be
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
; let
V
be given by
Proposition

2.3
. There is a c.e. set of strings
V_{0}
, closed under taking extensions, that generates

as an open set. By (

),
U=\left\{\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}\,:\,\sigma^{(\alpha)}\in V_{0}\right\}
is as required for

. In the other direction, for each

,
\left\{\sigma\,:\,\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x\right\}
is

-computable, uniformly in

. That is, there is some
\Delta^{0}_{1}

W\subset\omega^{<\omega}\times\mathcal{N}
such that
\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,(\sigma,x^{(\alpha)})\in W

Proposition

then implies that
[\sigma]^{\prec}_{\alpha}
is
\Delta^{0}_{1+\alpha}

3.
Analysis of the ambiguous Borel classes

In this section we show how the true stages machinery allows us to give intuitive proofs of two theorems analysing the structure of the classes

3.1.
The Hausdorff-Kuratowski theorem

The Hausdorff difference hierarchy (sometimes also named after Lavrentiev) is a transfinite extension of a hierarchy of finite Boolean operations.

3.1
.

Let

3.8
.

Let
\alpha
and
\eta\geqslant 1
be computable ordinals. A set

is in

if and only if its characteristic function

has a computable
\alpha
-approximation

for which is there is a computable “witness” function

satisfying:

(i)

If
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
then
o(\tau)\leqslant o(\sigma)
;

(ii)

If
\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\alpha}\tau
and
f(\tau)\neq f(\sigma)
then
o(\tau)<o(\sigma)
;

(iii)

If
o(\sigma)=\eta
then
f(\sigma)=0
.

This notion of a “witness” for the convergence of an approximation is widely used for computable approximations of functions
F\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
(see for example
[
DG20
, Ch.2]
). The idea is that the well-foundedness of
\eta+1
guarantees that the sequence
{\left\langle{f(\sigma)}\right\rangle}_{\sigma\prec_{\alpha}x}
eventually stabilises, i.e., that
{f}
indeed
\alpha
-converges to a limit
F
. In general, the more “mind-changes” the approximation has, the more complicated the function
F
can be. The longer
\eta
is, the “more room” we have for mind-changes.

Proof.

In one direction, let
A
be a
D_{\eta}(\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha})
set; let
{\left\langle{A_{i}}\right\rangle}_{i<\eta}
be the sequence of uniformly
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
sets used to define
A
. For uniformity of notation, let
A_{\eta}=\mathcal{N}
; Then
{\left\langle{A_{i}}\right\rangle}_{i\leqslant\eta}
is increasing and uniformly
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\alpha}
, and
x\in A
if and only if parity
(i)\neq
parity
(\eta)
for the least
i
such that
x\in A_{i}
.

By
TSP(4)
, let
U_{i}
be a sequence of uniformly c.e. subsets of
\omega^{<\omega}
such that

. As in the proof of

are uniformly computable, and each is upwards closed in

. We may take

.

Recall that we are working with a computable copy of
\eta

of
\mathbb{N}
such that
(\mathbb{N},<^{*})\cong\eta
. Let

be the isomorphism. For each
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
, let
O(\sigma)=\{\eta\}\cup\left\{i_{n}\,:\,n<|\sigma|\right\}
(more thematically, we should take

, where as above

is the height of
\sigma

is a finite subset of

for all

for all

Define

as follows. Given

be the least

(in the ordering of

(o(\sigma))\neq

It is not difficult to check that

In the other direction, let

be an
\alpha
-computable approximation of the characteristic function

of

, with a witnessing function

if and only if parity

(this is condition (iii)). So it remains to show that we can modify

This is easily done by setting

and the parity of the ordinals. As mentioned, (iii) for

.

Ershov showed that a function

We can similarly define a notion of an

-approximation with such a witness

We can now prove the effective Hausdorff-Kuratowski theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

.

By

and

Let

Now let

be the Kleene-Brouwer ordering of

3.2.
Wadge’s Theorem

Let
\lambda
be a countable limit ordinal. Wadge described how to construct all sets in the class
\bm{\Delta}^{0}_{\lambda}
starting from the sets in
\bm{\Delta}^{0}_{<\lambda}
and closing under the operation of taking

.

3.10
.

A countable sequence of sets
(A_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
-separated
if there is a sequence
(U_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of pairwise disjoint
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
sets with
A_{n}\subseteq U_{n}
for all
n
.

3.11
Wadge.

Let
\lambda
be a countable limit ordinal. The class
\bm{\Delta}^{0}_{\lambda}
is the smallest collection of sets which:

•

Contains
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
, for all
\xi<\lambda
; and

•

Is closed under unions of
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
-separated sequences, for all
\xi<\lambda
.

3.12
.

Wadge stated his theorem in terms of
partitioned unions
, in which the pairwise disjoint
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
sets of
Definition

3.10
are required to form a partition of
\mathcal{N}
. Every
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi}
-separated union is of course a
\bm{\Sigma}^{0}_{\xi+1}
-partitioned union, so for Wadge’s theorem we may use either notion. The difference between seperated and partitioned unions is important in the analysis of all Borel Wadge classes.

For our proof of
Theorem

3.11
, we require one last property of the true stages machinery. As observed above, this property is a special feature of the development of the machinery in
[
GT
]
. As with

, it reflects the fact that for limit

,
x^{(\lambda)}\equiv_{\textup{\scriptsize T}}\bigoplus_{\alpha<\lambda}x^{(\alpha)}
. Recall the notation introduced in the proof of

3.6
:
|\sigma|_{\alpha}
is the height of

in the tree
(\omega^{<\omega},\preccurlyeq_{\alpha})
.

TSP(9):

Let
\lambda<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
be computable and limit. There is a computable and increasing sequence
{\left\langle{\lambda_{k}}\right\rangle}
, cofinal in
\lambda
, such that for all
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
, letting
k=|\sigma|_{\lambda}
, then
|\sigma|_{\lambda_{k}}=k
, and for all

\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\lambda}\tau\,\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\,\sigma\preccurlyeq_{\lambda_{k}}\tau.

Proof of
Theorem

3.11
.

Let

be the smallest collection of sets with the above closure properties. An induction shows that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\bm{\Delta}^{0}_{\lambda}
, so we will show the converse: given an

, we will demonstrate that
A\in\mathcal{C}
. By working relative to an appropriate oracle, we may assume that
\lambda<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
and

.

Since
\lambda=1+\lambda
,
TSP(4)
gives us two c.e. sets

and

such that

and

. As in previous proofs, we may assume that

and

are disjoint and upwards closed in

Let

is well-founded (as in the proof of

and

We modify

0,&if $\sigma\not\in T$.\end{cases}

For each

let

. By induction on

, we will show that

. The result will then follow since
A=A_{{\left\langle{}\right\rangle}}
. Let
{\left\langle{\lambda_{k}}\right\rangle}
be the sequence given by
TSP(9)
.

Suppose that
s(\sigma)=0
. If
\sigma\in W_{0}
then

; if

then

. In either case, by

Suppose that

. Let

Note that we did not actually need the sets

and

to be c.e. In other words, it was enough that

is
(z,\lambda)
-clopen for some parameter

; we didn’t use the fact that it is effectively

-clopen, i.e.,
\Delta^{0}_{\lambda}(z)
. Nevertheless, the proof can be made entirely effective, and an effective version of Wadge’s theorem is true.

To state it, for a lightface class
\Gamma
and a computable ordinal
\xi
, let
\textup{SU}_{\xi}(\Gamma)
be the collection of unions of all sequences of sets which are uniformly in
\Gamma
and uniformly
\Sigma^{0}_{\xi}
-separated. For a computable limit ordinal
\lambda
define by induction on

the lightface classes
\textup{ISU}^{(\beta)}_{\lambda}
(iterated separated unions):

•

\textup{ISU}^{(<0)}_{\lambda}=\Sigma^{0}_{<\lambda}=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}\Sigma^{0}_{\xi}
;

•

For
\beta>0
,
\textup{ISU}^{(<\beta)}_{\lambda}=\bigcup_{\gamma<\beta}\textup{ISU}^{(\gamma)}_{\lambda}
;

•

\textup{ISU}^{(\beta)}_{\lambda}=\textup{SU}_{<\lambda}(\textup{ISU}^{(<\beta)}_{\lambda})=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}\textup{SU}_{\xi}(\textup{ISU}^{(<\beta)}_{\lambda})
.

Each class
\textup{ISU}^{(\beta)}_{\lambda}
depends on a choice of computable copy of
\beta
; given such a copy, we can give an effective enumeration of the class
\textup{ISU}^{(<\beta)}_{\lambda}
, so we can indeed speak of a sequence of sets being uniformly in this class. The effective version of Wadge’s theorem is:

3.13
.

Let
\lambda<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
be a limit ordinal. Then

\Delta^{0}_{\lambda}=\textup{ISU}^{(<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}})}_{\lambda}.

The proof above effectivises; as in the proof of
Theorem

3.3
, we can make

a computable ranking function into a computable ordinal (by taking the Kleene-Brouwer ordering on

). Then, by effective transfinite recursion on

(from the leaves to the root), we show that
A_{\sigma}\in\textup{ISU}^{(s(\sigma))}_{\lambda}
, uniformly so.

Proof of

.

Fix computable trees

,

such that for

is the projection of

, fix a computable, upwards closed

We define a two player game, in which players I and II alternate turns. On player I’s

. On player II’s
i{}^{\textup{th}}
turn, they play

Suppose that

(y_{1},z_{1})

\dots

. Then
\overline{x}
has an opinion as to whether the real

which player I is building will be in

: the opinion is determined by whether

or not. Whichever it is, player II is responsible for building a real

in
B_{0}
or

, as appropriate, and also a

which witnesses

at those stages which appear
\xi

. This is made precise in the following.

Define a set of indices

as follows:

•

If
\overline{x}\in W
, let

;

•

If

, let
F=\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\overline{x}\!\upharpoonright\!{i}\preccurlyeq_{\xi}\overline{x}\}

Note that

. Also note that since

is upwards closed in

Enumerate

as

. Let

and
\overline{z}=(z_{a_{0}},\dots,z_{a_{k-1}})

, we only collect the numbers played by player II in response to initial segments of

which appear to be correct.

We declare that player I wins if:

•

\overline{x}\in W
and
(\overline{y},\overline{z})\not\in T_{1}
; or

•

\overline{x}\not\in W
and
(\overline{y},\overline{z})\not\in T_{0}
.

Otherwise, the game continues. If the game continues for

As this is an open/closed game, it is determined.

Claim

.1
.

If player II has a winning strategy in the game, then there is a continuous function reducing

(B_{0},B_{1})

Proof.

Suppose that

(y,v)\in[T_{1}]
, and thus

as desired.

If

, let

enumerate those

with

; note that

for such

. The same argument shows that

, as desired.
∎

Now suppose instead that player I has a winning strategy

in the game. In the rest of the proof, we show how to use

to construct a

separator between

and

. Since the game is open for player I, there is a hyperarithmetic winning strategy

as required.

For

and
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
, let

be the sequence
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
which results from player I playing according to

and player II playing
(y\!\upharpoonright\!{|\sigma|},\sigma)
. Note that
|S(y,\sigma)|=|\sigma|+1
. It will be notationally convenient to posit the existence of a string

with

and

.

Roughly, the idea is the following. Given

, we need to decide on which side of the separator we put it (on the
B_{0}
side or the

side). We put it on the

side if there is some “credible”
\sigma

. We need to ensure that if there is such

then

(and similarly, if there is no such

, then
y\notin B_{0}
). The argument for contradiction will be that if
y\in B_{1}
, say

, then we can start from
\sigma
and use

to defeat

. To do this, we will define a sequence
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1},\dots
such that for each

, we can play

and continue to the next

. The notion of credibility of a sequence, which also allows us to continue the inductive construction, is that of a “strongly
\xi
-correct” sequence (
Definition

.3
). The complexity of the separator depends on the complexity of this notion of correctness, which is computed in

.5
. The heart of the argument is

.6
, which ensures that we can take another step in the construction we outlined.

We will use the strategy
S
to pull back the true stage relations onto strings
\sigma
, which we think of as potential second coordinates for player II to play along a given real
y
.

Definition
4.2
.2
.

Let
\alpha\leqslant\xi
and

. For

, we write

when
\sigma\preccurlyeq\tau
and

.

We will now define a sort of absolutely true stage for these new relations, moderated by the need to avoid letting player I win.

Definition

.

,

is

.

Claim
4.2
.4
.

Let

,

, and

(a)

If

\alpha
-correct for

(b)

(c)

(d)

(e)

(and thus

Note that

of

, then for all

is strongly
\alpha
-correct for

is
\alpha
-correct for

.

Claim
4.2
.5
.

Let
\alpha\leqslant\xi
. The relations “

is
\alpha
-correct for

” and “

-correct for

” are:

(i)

\Delta^{0}_{1}(S)
if
\alpha=0
;

(ii)

\Pi^{0}_{\alpha}(S)
if

;

(iii)

\Delta^{0}_{\alpha}(S)
for limit

; and

(iv)

\Pi^{0}_{\alpha-1}(S)
if
\alpha>\omega
is a successor.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
\alpha
. Technically, of course, this is an instance of effective transfinite recursion: for the limit case, we need the relations to be uniformly in their classes.
For every
\alpha
, the complexity of the strong relation follows from the complexity of “
\sigma
is
\alpha
-correct for

” by the fact that the relations
\leqslant_{\alpha}
are uniformly computable (

).

All cases are immediate, except for the limit case (iii). Suppose that
\lambda\leqslant\xi
is a limit ordinal. We use
TSP(9)
; let
{\left\langle{\lambda_{k}}\right\rangle}
be given by that property.

Given
y
and
\sigma
, let
k=|S(y,\sigma)|_{\lambda}
.
We claim that
\sigma
is
\lambda
-correct for
y
if and only if it is strongly
\lambda_{k}
-correct for
y
. One direction follows from
Claim

.4
(c)
. For the other direction, suppose that
\sigma
is strongly
\lambda_{k}
-correct for

. By induction on

, we can show that every
\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\lambda_{k}}\sigma
is strongly
\beta
-correct for
y
.

This is mostly chasing the definitions, using
Claim

.4
. The main point is that for all
\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\lambda_{k}}\sigma
we have
|S(y,\rho)|_{\lambda_{k}}=|S(y,\rho)|_{\lambda}\leqslant k
, and so for all

, if

then
\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\lambda}\tau
, and so
\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\beta+1}\tau
.
∎

Claim
4.2
.6
.

Let

and

. Suppose that

is strongly

-correct for

. If

which is 0-correct for

, then there exists an

which is strongly

-correct for

.

For the purposes of this claim, the empty sequence is a one-element extension of

.

Proof.

The argument is by induction on
\alpha
. For
\alpha=0
we can take
\tau=\sigma
(
Claim

.4
(b)
).

For the successor case, we use

. Suppose that the lemma holds for
\alpha<\xi
, and suppose that
\rho
is strongly
(\alpha+1)
-correct for
y
. Let
p_{\alpha}
be given by

. For brevity, we will write

in place of

. By induction, there are
\tau\succcurlyeq\sigma
which are strongly
\alpha
-correct for
y
. Amongst those

, choose one to minimize

. We claim that this

is strongly
(\alpha+1)
-correct for
y
. Note that since
\rho
is
(\alpha+1)
-correct for
y
and
\tau
is strongly
\alpha
-correct for
y
,
\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\alpha}\tau
(by

.4
(e)
) and so
\rho\trianglelefteq_{\alpha+1}^{y}\tau
(by

.3
).

We must show that every
\nu\trianglelefteq_{\alpha+1}^{y}\tau
is
(\alpha+1)
-correct for
y
. If
\nu\prec\sigma
, then
\nu\preccurlyeq\rho
; since
\preccurlyeq_{\alpha+1}
is a tree (

),
\nu\trianglelefteq_{\alpha+1}^{y}\rho
. As
\rho
is strongly
(\alpha+1)
-correct for

\nu
is
(\alpha+1)
-correct for

. So we may assume that
\sigma\preccurlyeq\nu
.

We need to show that for every

which is strongly
\alpha
-correct for

, it is the case that
\nu\trianglelefteq_{\alpha+1}^{y}\eta
. By

, since

,

. As
\eta
is strongly
\alpha
-correct for

, for every

with
\nu\trianglelefteq_{\alpha}^{y}\eta^{\prime}\trianglelefteq_{\alpha}^{y}\eta

is strongly
\alpha
-correct for

(
Claim

.4
(d)
). Thus such

was a candidate for

, so

, and thus

. By

again,

.

For

. Let
{\left\langle{\alpha_{k}}\right\rangle}
be a cofinal sequence in
\alpha
supplied by that property; let

which is strongly

, fix one minimal with respect to

.
Hence, for all

, if

then

. As

is strongly

is strongly

.

We are now ready to define a separator
U
.

U=\{y:\text{ there is some $\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}$, strongly $\xi$-correct for~$y$, with }S(y,\sigma)\in W\}.

By
Claim

.5
, the set
U
is
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\xi}(S)
. We need to show that
B_{0}\subseteq U
and
B_{1}\subseteq U^{\complement}
.

Suppose, for a contradition, that there is some
y\in U\cap B_{1}
. Fix
v=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots)\in\mathcal{N}
such that
(y,v)\in[T_{1}]
. We define a sequence
\sigma_{0}\prec\sigma_{1}\prec\cdots
of sequences which together with
y
will give a play for II which defeats the strategy
S
. We ensure that:

(i)

Each
\sigma_{i}
is strongly
\xi
-correct for
y
;

(ii)

\sigma_{i+1}(|\sigma_{i}|)=v_{i}
; and

(iii)

for each
i
,

\{\rho\in\omega^{<\omega}\,:\,\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\xi}\sigma_{i}\,\,\,\&\,\,\,S(y,\rho)\in W\}=\{\sigma_{j}\,:\,j\leqslant i\}.

For
\sigma_{0}
, we choose some sequence witnessing that
y\in U
, minimal such; so for all
\rho\triangleleft^{y}_{\xi}\sigma_{0}
in
\omega^{<\omega}
we have

. Note that
\sigma_{0}\neq\pi
. Suppose that
\sigma_{i}
has been chosen. We then consider

; we wish to show that
\tau
is 0-correct for

, i.e., that player I does not win in response to

. Since
\sigma_{i}
is
\xi
-correct for

, it is 0-correct for
y
; player I has not won after we play
(y\!\upharpoonright\!{|\sigma_{i}|},\sigma_{i})
. After playing
(y\!\upharpoonright\!{|\tau|},\tau)
, by (iii) above, the set

of indices used to assess whether I wins is
\{|\sigma_{j}|+1\,:\,j\leqslant i\}
and so the corresponding

is

. Since
(y\!\upharpoonright\!{(i+1)},\overline{z})\in T_{1}
,
\tau
is 0-correct for

as required.

We can therefore appeal to
Claim

.6
. We choose
\sigma_{i+1}
as given by the claim, of minimal length. This ensures (iii) for

(we use the fact that

is upwards closed in
(\omega^{<\omega},\preccurlyeq_{\xi})
).

The other case, that

, is almost identical. In this case we will have

\{\rho\in\omega^{<\omega}\,:\,\rho\trianglelefteq^{y}_{\xi}\sigma_{i}\}=\{\sigma_{j}\,:\,j\leqslant i\}.

The empty sequence is 0-correct for

, and so by
Claim

.6
and
Claim

.4
(f)
, there is some
\sigma\in\omega^{<\omega}
which is strongly

-correct for

. We start with
\sigma_{0}
being such a string of minimal length (again

).
∎

As mentioned, Theorem
4.2
holds not just for the classes
\Sigma^{0}_{1+\xi}
, but for any non-self-dual Borel Wadge class, and our methods can be used to show this. From that, as in
[
LSR88
]
, we can obtain Louveau separation for all non-self-dual Borel Wadge classes. The requirement
\xi<\omega_{1}^{\textup{ck}}
is replaced by the requirement that the Wadge class must have a
\Delta^{1}_{1}
name. Of course, some care must be taken in defining what a name for a Wadge class is, and what the corresponding lightface class is. See the companion paper
[
DGHTT
]
for further details.

4.1.
Reverse mathematics

As mentioned in the introduction, the proofs we gave are sufficiently effective so that they can be made within the system
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
of reverse mathematics. This is in some sense the weakest system which allows a meaningful development of Borel sets (
[
Sim99
, Ch.V]
). The main point is that the true stages machinery is effective, and so can be defined in
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
and all of its properties are provable in
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
. Thus:

4.4
.

Louveau’s separation theorem (
Theorem

4.3
) is provable in
\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
.

We remark that in contrast, Louveau’s original proof in
[
Lou80
]
, which is the proof which appears in standard texts such as
[
Sac90
]
or
[
Mil95
]
, appears to require the stronger system
\Pi^{1}_{1}\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_{0}
.

Ramsey-type problems on induced covers and induced partitions toward the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture
Shuya Chiba, Michitaka Furuya

Abstract
Gyárfás and Sumner independently conjectured that for every tree T T , there exists a function f T : ℕ → ℕ f_{T}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N} such that every T T -free graph G G satisfies χ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ f T ​ ( ω ⁡ ( G ) ) \chi(G)\leq f_{T}(\omega(G)) , where χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) and ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) are the chromatic number and the clique number of G G , respectively. This conjecture gives a solution of a Ramsey-type problem on the chromatic number. For a graph G G , the induced SP-cover number inspc ⁡ ( G ) {\rm inspc}(G) (resp. the induced SP-partition number inspp ⁡ ( G ) {\rm inspp}(G) ) of G G is the minimum cardinality of a family 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of induced subgraphs of G G such that each element of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is a star or a path and ⋃ P ∈ 𝒫 V ⁡ ( P ) = V ⁡ ( G ) \bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}V(P)=V(G) (resp. ⋃ ˙ P ∈ 𝒫 ​ V ​ ( P ) = V ⁡ ( G ) \dot{\bigcup}_{P\in\mathcal{P}}V(P)=V(G) ). Such two invariants are directly related concepts to the chromatic number. From the viewpoint of this fact, we focus on Ramsey-type problems for two invariants inspc {\rm inspc} and inspp {\rm inspp} , which are analogies of the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture, and settle them. As a corollary of our results, we also settle other Ramsey-type problems for widely studied invariants.



Key words and phrases.

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple and undirected.
For terms and symbols not defined in this paper, we refer the reader to
[
11
]
.
For two graphs
G
and
H
,
G
is said to be
H
-free
if
G
contains no copy of
H
as an induced subgraph.
For a family
{\mathcal{H}}
of graphs, a graph

is said to be
{\mathcal{H}}
-free
if
G
is
H
-free for every
H\in{\mathcal{H}}
.
In this context, the members of
{\mathcal{H}}
are called
forbidden subgraphs
.
For two families
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
of graphs, we write
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
if for every
H_{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
,
H_{2}
contains a copy of an element of
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
as an induced subgraph.
The relation “
\leq
” between two families of forbidden subgraphs was introduced in
[
17
]
.
Note that if
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
, then every
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
-free graph is also
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
-free.
Let
K_{n}
,
K_{1,n}
and
P_{n}
denote the
complete graph
of order
n
, the
star
of order
n+1
and the
path
of order
n
, respectively.
Let
\overline{K_{n}}
denote the complement of
K_{n}
, i.e., the graph of order
n
having no edges.

Gyárfás
[
19
]
and Sumner
[
30
]
independently conjectured that for every tree
T
, there exists a function
f_{T}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that every
T
-free graph
G
satisfies
\chi(G)\leq f_{T}(\omega(G))
, where
\chi(G)
and
\omega(G)
are the
chromatic number
and the
clique number
of
G
, respectively.
In graph theory, the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture has been studied as a fundamental and important problem.
The conjecture is true for the case where
T
is a path, a star, and some specific trees, but it is widely open (see a survey
[
28
]
).

Here we focus on Ramsey-type problems.
A classical Ramsey theorem
[
27
]
is equivalent to the following statement:
For a family
{\mathcal{H}}
of graphs, there exists a constant
c({\mathcal{H}})
such that
|V(G)|\leq c({\mathcal{H}})
for every
{\mathcal{H}}
-free graph
G
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},\overline{K_{n}}\}
for an integer
n\geq 1
.
Furthermore, an analogy of the Ramsey theorem for connected graphs is also well-known as a folklore (see
[
11
, Proposition 9.4.1]
):
For a family
{\mathcal{H}}
of connected graphs, there exists a constant
c({\mathcal{H}})
such that
|V(G)|\leq c({\mathcal{H}})
for every connected
{\mathcal{H}}
-free graph
G
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
for an integer
n\geq 1
.
Remark that the
\overline{K_{n}}
-freeness and the
\{K_{1,n},P_{n}\}
-freeness play a similar role in above results.
In particular, the combination of induced stars and induced paths is a natural alternative concept of an independent set when we treat induced subgraphs.

Recently, similar type problems on invariants (other than the order) were obtained as Ramsey-type problems (see, for example,
[
2
,
4
,
6
,
10
,
18
]
).
For a graph-invariant
\mu
, we consider the following condition concerning a (finite) family
{\mathcal{H}}
of graphs:

(P-
\mu
)

There exists a constant
c({\mathcal{H}})
such that
\mu(G)\leq c({\mathcal{H}})
for every connected
{\mathcal{H}}
-free graph
G
.

A Ramsey-type problem of
\mu
is to characterize
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfying (P-
\mu
).

Since there exist infinitely many graphs with sufficiently large chromatic number and sufficiently large girth
[
13
]
, we can easily verify that the following conjecture is equivalent to the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture.

Conjecture 1

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a finite family of connected graphs.
Then
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
(P-
\chi
)
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},T\}
for an integer
n\geq 1
and a tree
T
.

Thus, settling Ramsey-type problems for invariants closely related to the chromatic number, one might expect to obtain some essential information and effective techniques for the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture.
As a study of this policy, for example, Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
9
]
posed a conjecture on a Ramsey-type problem for the cochromatic number, where the
cochromatic number
of a graph
G
is the minimum cardinality of a family of independent sets and cliques of
G
whose union is
V(G)
, and proved that the conjecture is equivalent to the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture.
On the other hand, as far as we know, no Ramsey-type problem for an invariant around the chromatic number was completely settled.
Our main aim in this paper is to approach the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture by settling such Ramsey-type problems.
Remark that Choi, Kim and Park
[
7
]
characterized forbidden structures which are not necessarily induced, bounding some analogies of the chromatic number by a constant.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a family of graphs.
A family
{\mathcal{P}}
of induced subgraphs of
G
is called an
induced
{\mathcal{A}}
-cover
of
G
if
\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}V(P)=V(G)
and each element of
{\mathcal{P}}
is isomorphic to an element of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Note that some elements of an induced
{\mathcal{A}}
-cover of
G
might have common vertices.
An induced
{\mathcal{A}}
-cover
{\mathcal{P}}
of
G
is called an
induced
{\mathcal{A}}
-partition
of
G
if the elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
are pairwise vertex-disjoint.
We mainly consider the case where
{\mathcal{A}}
is equal to the family
{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sp}:=\{K_{1,n}:n\geq 1\}\cup\{P_{n}:n\geq 1\}
, i.e., the family of stars and paths.
An induced
{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sp}
-cover (resp. an induced
{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sp}
-partition) of
G
is called an
induced SP-cover
(resp. an
induced SP-partition
) of
G
.
The minimum cardinality of an induced SP-cover (resp. an induced SP-partition) of
G
, denoted by
{\rm inspc}(G)
(resp.
{\rm inspp}(G)
), is called the
induced SP-cover number
(resp. the
induced SP-partition number
) of
G
.

Let us now compare the induced SP-cover/partition numbers and the chromatic number.
Note that the chromatic number of a graph
G
is equal to the minimum cardinality of an induced
\{\overline{K_{n}}:n\geq 1\}
-cover of
G
, which is also equal to the minimum cardinality of an induced
\{\overline{K_{n}}:n\geq 1\}
-partition of
G
.
As we mentioned in the third paragraph of this section, the combination of induced stars and induced paths is related to the existence of independent sets, or induced
\overline{K_{n}}
’s.
In fact, for an induced SP-cover
{\mathcal{P}}
of
G
, since every element of
{\mathcal{P}}
is a bipartite graph,
\{G[A_{P}],G[B_{P}]:P\in{\mathcal{P}}\}
is an induced
\{\overline{K_{n}}:n\geq 1\}
-cover of
G
, where
\{A_{P},B_{P}\}
is a bipartition of
P
.
Hence we obtain
\chi(G)\leq 2\,{\rm inspc}(G)\leq 2\,{\rm inspp}(G)
for every graph
G
, which implies that the induced SP-cover/partition numbers are directly related concepts to the chromatic number.
In particular, if we restrict induced
{\mathcal{A}}
-covers/partitions to the case where each element of
{\mathcal{A}}
is connected, then small induced SP-covers/partitions are alternatives to small induced
\{\overline{K_{n}}:n\geq 1\}
-covers/partitions, and in this sense those two numbers can be regarded as “connected versions” of the chromatic number.
In this paper, we characterize the finite families
{\mathcal{H}}
of connected graphs satisfying (P-
{\rm inspc}
) or (P-
{\rm inspp}
).

3(m-1)
edges
v_{i}w_{i},v_{i}u^{(n)}_{i},v_{i}u^{(1)}_{i+1}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
.

\bullet

Let
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
be the graph obtained from the union of the paths
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}
by adding
m-1
vertices
v_{i}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
and
3(m-1)
edges
v_{i}u^{(n)}_{i},v_{i}u^{(1)}_{i+1},u^{(n)}_{i}u^{(1)}_{i+1}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
.

\bullet

Let
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
be the graph obtained from the union of the paths
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}
by adding
(m-1)m
vertices
v_{i,j}~(1\leq i\leq m-1,~1\leq j\leq m)
and
2(m-1)m
edges
v_{i,j}u^{(n)}_{i},v_{i,j}u^{(1)}_{i+1}~(1\leq i\leq m-1,~1\leq j\leq m)
.

\bullet

Let
H^{(4)}_{m,n}
be the graph obtained from
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
by deleting
(m-1)(m-2)
vertices
v_{i,j}~(1\leq i\leq m-1,~3\leq j\leq m)
.

\bullet

Let
H^{(5)}_{m,n}
be the graph obtained from
H^{(4)}_{m,n}
by adding
m-1
edges
v_{i,1}v_{i,2}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
.

Then the following lemma holds.
(Here we just want to claim that
{\rm inspc}(H^{(i)}_{m,n})\rightarrow\infty
when
m\rightarrow\infty
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, and
{\rm inspp}(H^{(i)}_{m,n})\rightarrow\infty
when
m\rightarrow\infty
for
i\in\{4,5\}
.
Thus the readers recognizing the facts can skip the proof.)

Lemma 4.2

Let
m
and
n
be integers with
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 3
.

(1)

We have
{\rm inspc}(H^{(1)}_{m,n})\geq\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.

(2)

We have
{\rm inspc}(H^{(2)}_{m,n})\geq\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.

(3)

We have
{\rm inspc}(H^{(3)}_{m,n})\geq m
.

(4)

We have
{\rm inspp}(H^{(4)}_{m,n})\geq m
.

(5)

We have
{\rm inspp}(H^{(5)}_{m,n})\geq m
.

Proof.

(1)

Since any two leaves of
H^{(1)}_{m,n}
have distance at least four, each element of an induced SP-cover of
H^{(1)}_{m,n}
contains at most two leaves of
H^{(1)}_{m,n}
.
Since
H^{(1)}_{m,n}
has
m+1
leaves, this implies that
{\rm inspc}(H^{(1)}_{m,n})\geq\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.

(2)

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an induced SP-cover of
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
.
It suffices to show that
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.
Since
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
is
K_{1,3}
-free,
{\mathcal{P}}
can be regarded as an induced path cover of
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
.
Let
U=\{u^{(1)}_{1},u^{(n)}_{m}\}\cup\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}
and
{\bf X}=\{(P,u)\in{\mathcal{P}}\times U:u\in V(P)\}
.
For each
u\in U
, there exists an element
P
of
{\mathcal{P}}
with
u\in V(P)
.
Hence

\displaystyle|{\bf X}|=\sum_{u\in U}|\{P\in{\mathcal{P}}:u\in V(P)\}|\geq|U|=m+1.

(4.1)

For every vertex
u\in U
, since the neighborhood of
u
is a clique of
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
, no induced path of
H^{(2)}_{m,n}
contains
u
as an internal vertex.
Since a path contains at most two endvertices, this implies that
|V(P)\cap U|\leq 2
for every
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle|{\bf X}|=\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}|V(P)\cap U|\leq 2|{\mathcal{P}}|.

(4.2)

By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have
m+1\leq|{\bf X}|\leq 2|{\mathcal{P}}|
, as desired.

(3)

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an induced SP-cover of
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
.
It suffices to show that
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq m
.
Let
U=\{u^{(1)}_{1}\}\cup\{v_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq m-1,~1\leq j\leq m\}
and
{\bf X}=\{(P,u)\in{\mathcal{P}}\times U:u\in V(P)\}
.
For each
u\in U
, there exists an element
P
of
{\mathcal{P}}
with
u\in V(P)
.
Hence

\displaystyle|{\bf X}|=\sum_{u\in U}|\{P\in{\mathcal{P}}:u\in V(P)\}|\geq|U|=(m-1)m+1.

(4.3)

Note that for an integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq m-1
, if an induced path
P
of
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
contains at least two vertices in
\{v_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
, then
|V(P)|=3
and
P
contains exactly two vertices in
\{v_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
.
Hence if an element
P
of
{\mathcal{P}}
is a path, then
|V(P)\cap U|\leq m
.
(For example, a
u^{(1)}_{1}
-
u^{(n)}_{m}
path
P
of
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
is an induced path of
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
with
|V(P)\cap U|=m
.)
Since any two sets of
\{u^{(1)}_{1}\}
and
\{v_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq m\}~(1\leq i\leq m-1)
have distance at least three in
H^{(3)}_{m,n}
, if an element
P
of
{\mathcal{P}}
is a star, then
|V(P)\cap U|\leq m
.
This implies that

\displaystyle|{\bf X}|=\sum_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}|V(P)\cap U|\leq m|{\mathcal{P}}|.

(4.4)

By (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have
m^{2}-m+1\leq|{\bf X}|\leq m|{\mathcal{P}}|
, and hence
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq m-1+\frac{1}{m}
.
Since
|{\mathcal{P}}|
is an integer, this forces
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq m
.

(4)

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an induced SP-partition of
H^{(4)}_{m,n}
.
It suffices to show that
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq m
.
For an integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq m
, let
R_{i}
be the unique element of
{\mathcal{P}}
containing
u^{(2)}_{i}
.
We remark that
R_{i}
might equal
R_{i^{\prime}}
for some
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq m
.
Let
I=\{i:1\leq i\leq m-1,~R_{i}\neq R_{i+1}\}
, and write
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{h}\}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{h}
where
h=0
if
I=\emptyset
.
If
R_{i_{l}}=R_{i^{\prime}}
for some integers
l
and
i^{\prime}
with
1\leq l\leq h
and
i^{\prime}>i_{l}
, then
R_{i_{l}}
is a path and contains all vertices in
\{u^{(2)}_{j}:i_{l}\leq j\leq i^{\prime}\}
, and hence
R_{i_{l}}=R_{i_{l}+1}
, which contradicts the definition of
i_{l}
.
This, together with the fact that
i_{h}<m
if
I\neq\emptyset
, implies that
R_{i_{1}},R_{i_{2}},\ldots,R_{i_{h}},R_{m}
are pairwise distinct, and so
|\{R_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}|\geq h+1
.
Note that the inequality also holds even if
I=\emptyset
.

Fix an integer
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\}\setminus I
.
By the definition of
I
,
R_{j}=R_{j+1}
, and hence
R_{j}
contains
u^{(2)}_{j}
and
u^{(2)}_{j+1}
.
Since
{\rm dist}_{H^{(4)}_{m,n}}(u^{(2)}_{j},u^{(2)}_{j+1})=n+1\geq 4
,
R_{j}
is a path.
We can easily verify that
\{u^{(n)}_{j},u^{(1)}_{j+1}\}\subseteq V(R_{j})
and
|V(R_{j})\cap\{v_{j,1},v_{j,2}\}|=1
.
This implies that there exists an element
R^{\prime}_{j}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
with
V(R^{\prime}_{j})=\{v_{j,1}\}
or
V(R^{\prime}_{j})=\{v_{j,2}\}
.
Therefore

|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq|\{R_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}\cup\{R^{\prime}_{j}:j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\}\setminus I\}|\geq(h+1)+((m-1)-h)=m,

as desired.

(5)

We proceed by induction on
m
.
Since
H^{(5)}_{2,n}
contains a cycle, we have
{\rm inspp}(H^{(5)}_{2,n})\geq 2
.
Thus we may assume that
m\geq 3
.
We let
{\mathcal{P}}
be an induced SP-partition of
H^{(5)}_{m,n}
.
It suffices to show that
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq m
.
Since
H^{(5)}_{m,n}
is
K_{1,3}
-free,
{\mathcal{P}}
can be regarded as an induced path partition of
H^{(5)}_{m,n}
.

For the moment, suppose that
\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}\not\subseteq V(P)
for any
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
and any integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq m-1
.
Then
{\mathcal{P}}
is an induced SP-partition of
H^{(5)}_{m,n}-\{v_{i,1}v_{i,2}:1\leq i\leq m-1\}~(=H^{(4)}_{m,n})
.
Hence by (4),
|{\mathcal{P}}|\geq{\rm inspp}(H^{(4)}_{m,n})\geq m
, as desired.
Thus we may assume that there exist an element
P^{*}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
and an integer
i
with
1\leq i\leq m-1
such that
\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}\subseteq V(P^{*})
.
Note that a vertex in
V(H^{(5)}_{m,n})\setminus\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}
is adjacent to
v_{i,1}
if and only if the vertex is adjacent to
v_{i,2}
.
Since
P^{*}
is an induced path of
H^{(5)}_{m,n}
, this implies that
P^{*}
consists of two vertices
v_{i,1}
and
v_{i,2}
.
In other words,
{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{P^{*}\}
is an induced SP-partition of
H^{(5)}_{m,n}-\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}
.
If
i\in\{1,m-1\}
, then
H^{(5)}_{m,n}-\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}
is the disjoint union of a path and a copy of
H^{(5)}_{m-1,n}
, and hence by the induction hypothesis,
|{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{P^{*}\}|\geq 1+{\rm inspp}(H^{(5)}_{m-1,n})\geq 1+(m-1)
; if
2\leq i\leq m-2
, then
H^{(5)}_{m,n}-\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2}\}
is the disjoint union of a copy of
H^{(5)}_{i,n}
and a copy of
H^{(5)}_{m-i,n}
, and hence
|{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{P^{*}\}|\geq{\rm inpp}(H^{(5)}_{i,n})+{\rm inpp}(H^{(5)}_{m-i,n})\geq i+(m-i)
.
In either case, we obtain
|{\mathcal{P}}|>|{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\{P^{*}\}|\geq m
, as desired.
∎

5
Proof of main results

In this section, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
and settle Ramsey-type problems for four invariants
{\rm insc}
,
{\rm insp}
,
{\rm inpc}
and
{\rm inpp}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
By Proposition
3.1
, we obtain the “if” parts of (i) and (ii).
Thus we prove that the “only if” parts are true.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a finite family of connected graphs.
Then the value
p=\max\{|V(H)|:H\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
is a well-defined constant depending on
{\mathcal{H}}
.

We first suppose that
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies (P-
{\rm inspc}
).
Then there exists a constant
c=c({\mathcal{H}})
such that
{\rm inspc}(G)\leq c
for every connected
{\mathcal{H}}
-free graph
G
.
Let
n=\max\{p,2c+1,4\}
.
Since
{\rm inspc}(K_{n})={\rm inspc}(S^{*}_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil\geq c+1
by Lemma
4.1
, neither
K_{n}
nor
S^{*}_{n}
is
{\mathcal{H}}
-free.
This implies that
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},S^{*}_{n}\}
.
Furthermore, for each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, it follows from Lemma
4.2
(1)–(3) that
{\rm inspc}(H^{(i)}_{n,n})\geq c+1
, and hence
H^{(i)}_{n,n}
is not
{\mathcal{H}}
-free, i.e.,
H^{(i)}_{n,n}
contains an induced subgraph
A_{i}
isomorphic to an element of
{\mathcal{H}}
.
Since
A_{i}
is connected and
|V(A_{i})|\leq\max\{|V(H)|:H\in{\mathcal{H}}\}=p\leq n
, we have

\bullet

|\{i^{\prime}:1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n,~V(A_{i})\cap V(Q_{i^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset\}|\leq 2
,

\bullet

|\{i^{\prime}:1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n-1,~V(A_{1})\cap\{v_{i^{\prime}},w_{i^{\prime}}\}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq 1
,

\bullet

|\{i^{\prime}:1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n-1,~V(A_{2})\cap\{v_{i^{\prime}}\}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq 1
, and

\bullet

|\{i^{\prime}:1\leq i^{\prime}\leq n-1,~V(A_{3})\cap\{v_{i^{\prime},j}:1\leq j\leq n\}\neq\emptyset\}|\leq 1
.

This implies that
A_{i}
is isomorphic to an induced subgraph of
F^{(i)}_{n}
, and hence
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{F^{(1)}_{n},F^{(2)}_{n},F^{(3)}_{n}\}
.
Consequently,
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},S^{*}_{n},F^{(1)}_{n},F^{(2)}_{n},F^{(3)}_{n}\}
, which proves the “only if” part of (i).

Next we suppose that
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies (P-
{\rm inspp}
).
Then there exists a constant
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
{\rm inspp}(G)\leq c^{\prime}
for every connected
{\mathcal{H}}
-free graph
G
.
Let
n=\max\{p,2c^{\prime}+1,4\}
.
Note that for every graph
H
,
{\rm inspc}(H)\leq{\rm inspp}(H)
.
Hence by similar argument as above, together with Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
(1)(2)(4)(5), we see that
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},S^{*}_{n},\tilde{S}_{n},F^{(1)}_{n},F^{(2)}_{n},F^{(4)}_{n},F^{(5)}_{n}\}
, which proves the “only if” part of (ii).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Note that for every graph
H
,

\displaystyle{\rm inspc}(H)\leq{\rm insc}(H)\leq{\rm insp}(H),~{\rm inspp}(H)\leq{\rm insp}(H),

\displaystyle{\rm inspc}(H)\leq{\rm inpc}(H)\leq{\rm inpp}(H),\mbox{ and }{\rm inspp}(H)\leq{\rm inpp}(H).

Hence, by similar argument in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, together with Proposition
3.2
and Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
(1)(2)(4)(5), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a finite family of connected graphs.

(i)

The family
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
(P-
{\rm insc}
)
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},S^{*}_{n},P_{n}\}
for an integer
n\geq 4
.

(ii)

The family
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
(P-
{\rm insp}
)
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},S^{*}_{n},\tilde{S}_{n},P_{n}\}
for an integer
n\geq 4
.

(iii)

The family
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
(P-
{\rm inpc}
)
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},K_{1,n},F^{(1)}_{n},F^{(2)}_{n}\}
for an integer
n\geq 4
.

(iv)

The family
{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
(P-
{\rm inpp}
)
if and only if
{\mathcal{H}}\leq\{K_{n},K_{1,n},F^{(1)}_{n},F^{(2)}_{n},F^{(4)}_{n},F^{(5)}_{n}\}
for an integer
n\geq 4
.

Now we recall Remark
1
in Section
3
.
Let
G
be a graph.
A family
{\mathcal{P}}
of subgraphs of
G
is called an
isometric path cover
of
G
if
\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}}V(P)=V(G)
and each element of
{\mathcal{P}}
is an isometric path of
G
.
An isometric path cover
{\mathcal{P}}
of
G
is called an
isometric path partition
of
G
if the elements of
{\mathcal{P}}
are pairwise vertex-disjoint.
The minimum cardinality of an isometric path cover (resp. an isometric path partition) of
G
, denoted by
{\rm ispc}(G)
(resp.
{\rm ispp}(G)
), is called the
isometric path cover number
(resp. the
isometric path partition number
) of
G
.
It is known that the isometric path cover number is closely related to the cops-robbers game, and motivated by the fact, there are many researches on the isometric path cover/partition (see
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
22
,
24
,
25
]
).
The isometric path cover number is frequently called the
isometric path number
.
As we mentioned in Remark
1
, Proposition
3.2
(iii)(iv) can be extended to isometric versions.
Furthermore, for every graph
G
, since every isometric path of
G
is also an induced path of
G
, we have
{\rm inpc}(G)\leq{\rm ispc}(G)
and
{\rm inpp}(G)\leq{\rm ispp}(G)
.
Consequently, we have implicitly proved the following theorem.

Tensor meson couplings in AdS/QCD
Shahin Mamedov, Zeynab Hashimli, Shahriyar Jafarzade

Abstract
We study the hadronic and radiative couplings of the f 2 ​ ( 1270 ) f_{2}(1270) meson within the hard- and soft-wall models of AdS/QCD. The results for the tensor meson-nucleon-nucleon coupling ( g f 2 ​ N ​ N g_{f_{2}NN} ) and tensor meson-photon-vector meson coupling ( g f 2 ​ γ ​ ρ g_{f_{2}\gamma\rho} ) are compared to the ones obtained by using the dispersion relations and amplitude methods, respectively. Qualitative agreement with different analyses implies the reliability of the holographic description of spin-2 meson.



I
Introduction

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) describes the strong interaction within the Standard Model of particle physics. The binding of quarks into nucleons (protons and neutrons) and other hadrons, such as mesons composed of a quark-antiquark pair, is accomplished through strong interactions. One of the main challenges of QCD is the lack of a small dimensionless variable that would allow for the perturbative calculation of low-energy observables. Lattice QCD simulations and effective models are alternative approaches to studying low-energy QCD. Phenomenological bottom-up approaches of holographic QCD are one of the effective approaches to low energy QCD which presume that the extra-dimensional fields propagate through a region of anti-de Sitter (AdS) space inspired by the AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena 1998
.

The holographic description of QCD observables was originally constructed to test the hadrons, including pseudoscalar, vector, and axial-vector mesons within the so-called hard-wall model (HW) of AdS/QCD
Erlich et al. 2005
. Three main experimental measurements, such as pion mass and decay constant and the mass of
\rho(770)
, were used to predict other quantities of the mesons within the model.

Later on, the application of holographic QCD is extended to the spin-2 case within the HW model, in which tensor mesons were introduced similarly to gravitons
Katz et al. 2006
. The equation of motion for the profile function of the
f_{2}(1270)
meson was solved in this approach, and its decay into two pions and two photons was evaluated. Higher spin mesons, including
f_{2}(1270)
, were investigated within the SW model in
Karch et al. 2006
.

Nucleons within the HW model were studied in Ref.
Hong et al. 2007
. Further investigations include the coupling between mesons and nucleons, such as the nucleon coupling with the vector meson in Ref.
Maru and Tachibana 2009
, with the axial-vector meson in Ref.
Huseynova and Mamedov 2020
and with the pseudoscalar meson coupling in Ref.
Kwee and Lebed 2008a
. Similar studies can be found in Refs.
Ahn et al. 2009
;
Huseynova and Mamedov 2015
;
Kwee and Lebed 2008b
;
Wang and Ma 2016
and at finite temperature case in Refs.
Mamedov and Taghiyeva 2021
;
Mamedov and Nasibova 2021
.

The resonance
f_{2}(1270)
is the spin-
2
meson with the positive parity (P) and charge conjugation (C) and the total spin number
J=2
. The lightest spin-
2
mesons
\{a_{2}(1320),K_{2}^{\star}(1430),f_{2}(1270),f_{2}(1525)\}
make up a multiplet with quantum numbers
J^{PC}=2^{++}
and has a solid experimental
Workman et al. 2022
, theoretical
Dudek et al. 2014
and phenomenological
Giacosa et al. 2005
foundation compatible with the quark model. Recent analyses of spin-2 mesons can be found e.g., in Refs
Mathieu et al. 2020
;
Nys et al. 2018
;
Bibrzycki and Kaminski 2013
. It has been studied within chiral perturbation theory in
Chow and Rey 1998
. Especially, the mass and two-photon decay of
f_{2}(1270)
estimates within holographic QCD fit the Particle Data Group (PDG)
Workman et al. 2022
very well. Gravitational form factors of nucleons within AdS/QCD approach are computed in Refs.
Abidin and Carlson 2009
;
Abidin and Carlson 2008a
;
Abidin and Carlson 2008b
and extension to the finite temperature see Ref.
Allahverdiyeva and Mamedov 2023
. To what extent can holographic descriptions effectively portray various couplings? We address this question in this paper.

We first study the tensor meson nucleon coupling constant (
g_{f_{2}NN}
) within the hard- and soft-wall models of AdS/QCD and compare it to other known theoretical models, such as Dispersion Relation (DR). Secondly, we estimate the tensor-vector-vector coupling (
g_{f_{2}\rho\rho}
) and the tensor-vector-photon coupling (
g_{f_{2}\gamma\rho}
) and compare the latter one to the results obtained from the CLAS experiment data
Battaglieri et al. 2009
within amplitude methods.

The organization of the paper is as follows: Holographic formulations of mesons and nucleons are presented in Sec.

II
Mesons in Ads/QCD

Background gravity for the AdS/QCD models is the 5D AdS spacetime, and the metric of it in Poincare coordinates has the following form:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\Big(-dz^{2}+dx^{\mu}dx_{\mu}\Big)\quad(\mu=0,1,2,3),

(1)

where extra
z
coordinate extends
0<z\leq z_{m}=323\,\text{GeV}^{-1}
in the HW model and
0<z<\infty
in the SW model. The action of the model we are interested in has four components: free tensor field
h
, vector field
v
, spinor field
N
, and their interactions:

\displaystyle S=S_{h}+S_{v}+S_{N}+S_{int}.

(2)

In the next sections, we shall present the profile functions of these fields and perform calculations of the couplings within both hard- and soft-wall models.

II.1
Tensor and vector mesons in the HW model

Tensor mesons in AdS/QCD were introduced in an analogy with the graviton field in the bulk of the AdS spacetime
Katz et al. 2006
, and here we present this solution briefly. Let us consider the 5D extension of the massive spin-2 field
h_{MN}(x,z)

(M,N=0,1,2,3,5)
. The gauge condition on fifth components of
h_{MN}
implies

\displaystyle h_{MN}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }$M$\,\text{or}\,$N=5$\\
h_{\mu\nu},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(3)

The linear perturbation of gravity around the AdS background is given by the
h_{\mu\nu}(x,z)
field:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\dfrac{1}{z^{2}}(\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu})dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}-\dfrac{1}{z^{2}}dz^{2}\,,

(4)

leading to the following action:

\displaystyle S_{h}^{HW}=-\dfrac{1}{4}\int d^{5}x\dfrac{1}{z^{3}}\Big[(\partial_{z}h_{\mu\nu})(\partial_{z}h^{\mu\nu})+h_{\mu\nu}\Box h^{\mu\nu}+\text{higher order terms}\Big].

(5)

The UV boundary value of
h_{\mu\nu}
corresponds to the wave function of the graviton. The first mode in the Kaluza-Klein (KK) decomposition of
h_{\mu\nu}
will be the lightest tensor meson listed in the introduction, i.e., the
f_{2}(1270)
meson. QCD predicts the existence of multiple isospin singlets spin-2 particles
1
1

1

See for instance Ref.
Vereijken et al. 2023
for the recent detailed discussion about spin-2 isoscalars.
, such as glueballs. Therefore, a comprehensive formulation of the spin-2 particles should contain various bulk spin-2 fields. Each spin-2 particle appears as a KK mode
\Big(h_{\mu\nu}(x,z)=\sum_{n}h_{\mu\nu}^{(n)}(x)h^{HW}_{n}(z)\Big)
where the profile function
h^{HW}_{n}(z)
satisfy the following equation of motion:

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{z}h^{HW}_{n}(z)-\dfrac{3}{z}\partial_{z}h^{HW}_{n}(z)+(m_{n}^{h})^{2}h^{HW}_{n}(z)=0.

(6)

The generic solution to this equation is expressed in terms of Bessel functions of the first kind
J_{2}
and the second kind
Y_{2}

Katz et al. 2006
:

\displaystyle h_{n}^{HW}(z)=N_{n}z^{2}[J_{2}(m_{n}^{h}z)+\beta_{n}Y_{2}(m_{n}^{h}z)].

(7)

UV boundary condition
h^{HW}(0)=0
implies
\beta_{n}=0
and the IR boundary condition

J_{1}(m_{n}^{h}z_{m})=0

f_{2}(1270)
meson is

\displaystyle h^{HW}(z)=3.51\frac{z^{2}}{z_{m}}J_{2}(3.83\frac{z}{z_{m}})\text{ with the normalization: }\int_{0}^{z_{m}}dz\Big(h^{HW}_{n}(z)\Big)^{2}/z^{3}=1.

(8)

The
f_{2}(1270)
mass is predicted by the IR boundary condition of this solution and equals to
3.83/z_{m}=1236
MeV, which is only
3\%
off of the observed mass in
Workman et al. 2022
. However, predictions for the two pion decays are underestimated compared to the PDG
Workman et al. 2022
.

Vector mesons in the HW AdS/QCD are introduced using a 5D vector field composed of the left and right gauge fields. The action for the
V_{M}
vector field is

\displaystyle S_{v}^{HW}=-\frac{1}{4g_{5}^{2}}\int d^{5}x\sqrt{g}V^{MN}V_{MN}=-\dfrac{1}{4g_{5}^{2}}\int d^{5}x(V^{2}_{\mu\nu}-2V^{2}_{\mu 5})/z\,,

(9)

where
V_{\mu\nu}:=\partial_{\mu}V_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V_{\mu}-i[V_{\mu},V_{\nu}]
,
V_{\mu}:=V_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}
and the coupling
g_{5}=\sqrt{4\pi^{2}N_{f}/N_{c}}
was obtained in Ref.
Erlich et al. 2005
. The equation of motion for KK modes
\Big(V_{\mu}(x,z)=\sum_{n}V_{\mu}^{(n)}(x)v^{HW}_{n}(z)\Big)
of the vector field in the HW model has the following form

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{z}v^{HW}_{n}(z)-\dfrac{1}{z}\partial_{z}v^{HW}_{n}(z)+(m^{v}_{n})^{2}v^{HW}_{n}(z)=0\,,

(10)

and solution to this equation is also expressed in terms of Bessel functions:

\displaystyle v^{HW}_{n}(z)=N_{n}z\Big[J_{1}(m^{v}_{n}z)+\beta_{n}Y_{1}(m^{v}_{n}z)\Big].

(11)

One can use boundary conditions
v^{HW\prime}(z_{m})=v^{HW}(0)=0
, which sets
\beta_{n}=0
and quantize the masses of the vector mesons according to the IR condition
J_{0}(m^{V}_{n}z_{m})=0
. Normalization is set by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z_{m}}\dfrac{dz}{z}\Big(v^{HW}_{n}(z)\Big)^{2}=1\,,

(12)

and the ground state profile function of
\rho
meson reads

\displaystyle v^{HW}(z)\equiv v^{HW}_{0}(z)=2.72\dfrac{z}{z_{m}}J_{1}(2.4\dfrac{z}{z_{m}})\,,

(13)

where
z_{m}=(323\text{MeV})^{-1}
is fixed with experimental mass of
\rho(770)
meson.

II.2
Tensor and vector meson profile functions in the SW model

In the SW model, the spin-
S
mesons are described by the rank-
S
tensor field
\phi^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}(x,z)
in the bulk of the 5D AdS space and the quadratic part of the gauge and coordinate invariant action for this field is written in the form
Karch et al. 2006
:

\displaystyle S_{v/h}^{SW}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int d^{5}x\,\exp{-\Big(\Phi(z)-A(z)(2S-1)\Big)}\partial_{N}\phi_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}\partial^{N}\phi^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}.

(14)

Here
A
denotes
A(z)=-\log kz
and the dilaton field
\Phi(z)=k^{2}z^{2}
is introduced to regulate the integral over the extra dimension
z
and to give a meson spectra agreeing with Regge trajectory. The 5D equation of motion for the KK modes
\phi_{n}
of the transverse traceless part of the
\phi_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}
field
\Big(\phi_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}(x,z)=\sum_{n}\phi_{n}(x,z)\phi^{(n)}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}(x)\Big)
can be easily derived from this action by considering
\Box\phi^{(n)}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}(x)=m^{2}_{n}\phi^{(n)}_{\mu_{1}...\mu_{S}}(x)
:

\displaystyle\partial_{z}(e^{-(\Phi(z)-A(z)(2S-1))}\partial_{z}\phi_{n}(z))+m^{2}_{n}e^{-(\Phi(z)-A(z)(2S-1))}\phi_{n}(z)=0.

(15)

By making substitution
\phi_{n}(z)=e^{{-(\Phi(z)-A(z)(2S-1))}/2}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)
in the equation it gives the Schrödinger-like equation form for
\tilde{\phi}

\displaystyle-\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z)+\Big[k^{4}z^{2}+2(S-1)k^{2}+\dfrac{4S^{2}-1}{4z^{2}}\Big]\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)=E\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z).

(16)

Solving the Eq. (
16
) will give the eigenfunctions, which are so-called the profile functions for the spin-
S
mesons, and the corresponding eigenvalues are

\displaystyle E=4n-2S+2\,,

(17)

with the mass spectrum

\displaystyle m_{n,S}^{2}=4k^{2}(n+S).

(18)

1.

For the vector meson (
S=1
) the equation of motion (
16
) gets a form:

\displaystyle-\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z)+\Big[k^{4}z^{2}+\dfrac{3}{4z^{2}}\Big]\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)=m_{n}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z).

(19)

By solving this equation with the substitution
v_{n}^{SW}(z)=\sqrt{kz}e^{k^{2}z^{2}/2}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)
the profile function in terms of Laguerre polynomials (
L_{n}^{1}(x)
) reads:

\displaystyle v^{SW}_{n}(z)=\sqrt{\dfrac{2n!}{(n+1)!}}k^{2}z^{2}L_{n}^{1}(k^{2}z^{2}).

(20)

Note that, parameter
k
is fixed according to the mass of
\rho(770)
which leads
k=0.388\,\text{GeV}
.

2.

For the tensor meson (
S=2
) the equation of motion (
16
) becomes

\displaystyle-\tilde{\phi}^{\prime\prime}_{n}(z)+\Big[2k^{2}+k^{4}z^{2}+\dfrac{15}{4z^{2}}\Big]\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)=m_{n}^{2}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)\,,

(21)

and the substitution
h^{SW}_{n}(z)=\sqrt{(kz)^{3}}e^{k^{2}z^{2}/2}\tilde{\phi}_{n}(z)
leads to the following profile function:

\displaystyle h_{n}^{SW}(z)=(kz)^{4}\sqrt{\frac{2n!}{(n+2)!}}L_{n}^{2}(k^{2}z^{2}),

(22)

which has the normalization:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dze^{-k^{2}z^{2}}\,\Big(h_{n}^{SW}(z)\Big)^{2}/z^{3}=1.

(23)

In the appendix
A
, we show that the solution for the tensor meson profile function in (
22
) is the same as the one derived in
Abidin and Carlson 2009
if one approximates the spin-2 tensor meson as a graviton.

III
Nucleons in Ads/QCD

III.1
Nucleon profile function in the HW model

Two bulk fermion fields,
N_{1}^{HW}
and
N_{2}^{HW}
are required in the AdS space-time to describe the independent left and right chiral components of the boundary nucleon
Hong et al. 2007
. The Lagrangian for these spinors has different signs for the ”5-dimensional mass”
m_{5}
, which is related to the conformal dimension of the spinor:
m_{5}=\dfrac{5}{2}
for
N_{1}^{HW}
and
m_{5}=-\dfrac{5}{2}
for
N_{2}^{HW}
. In light of the normalizability and chirality analyses,
N_{1}^{HW}
describes the left-handed component of the boundary nucleon, while
N_{2}^{HW}
describes the right-handed one. The IR boundary condition eliminates the remaining components of the spinors and gives the mass spectrum of the nucleon
m_{n}
. The bulk action for the Dirac spinors
N_{1}^{HW}
and
N_{2}^{HW}
is given by
Hong et al. 2007
:

\displaystyle S_{N}^{HW}=\int d^{5}x\sqrt{g}\Bigg[\dfrac{i}{2}\overline{N}^{HW}_{1}e^{M}_{A}\Gamma^{A}\nabla_{M}N^{HW}_{1}-\dfrac{i}{2}\Big(\nabla^{\dagger}_{M}\overline{N}^{HW}_{1}\Big)\,e^{M}_{A}\Gamma^{A}N^{HW}_{1}

\displaystyle-m_{5}\overline{N}^{HW}_{1}N^{HW}_{1}

\displaystyle+\Big(N_{1}^{HW}\rightarrow N_{2}^{HW},m_{5}\rightarrow-m_{5}\Big)\Bigg],

(24)

where
e^{A}_{M}=\dfrac{1}{z}\eta^{A}_{M}
is the vielbein and the Dirac matrices
\Gamma^{A}=\Big(\gamma^{\mu},-i\gamma^{5}\Big)
satisfy the Clifford algebra
\Big\{\Gamma^{A},\Gamma^{B}\Big\}=2\eta^{AB}
.
The Lorentz and gauge covariant derivative is:

\displaystyle\nabla_{M}=\partial_{M}+\dfrac{i}{4}\omega^{AB}_{M}\Gamma_{AB},

(25)

where the Lorentz generator
\Gamma^{AB}
is defined as
\Gamma^{AB}:=\dfrac{1}{2i}\Big[\Gamma^{A},\Gamma^{B}\Big]
and the non-vanishing components of the spin connection
\omega^{AB}_{M}
equal to
\omega^{5A}_{M}=-\omega^{A5}_{M}=\dfrac{1}{z}\delta^{A}_{M}
.
Extremizing the action (
24
) with respect to
\overline{N}_{1}^{HW}
, we get 5D Dirac equation for
N_{1}^{HW}
:

\displaystyle(ie^{M}_{A}\Gamma^{A}\nabla_{M}-m_{5})N_{1}^{HW}=0\,,

(26)

with the boundary condition

\displaystyle\Big[\delta\overline{N}^{HW}_{1}e^{5}_{A}\Gamma^{A}N^{HW}_{1}\Big]^{z_{m}}_{\epsilon}=0.

(27)

Similarly, for the field of
N_{2}^{HW}
.
Since the AdS/CFT correspondence relates the classical solution to the bulk equation of motion with the corresponding boundary operator, it is useful to decompose the bulk spinor into left and right components, similar to the 4D chirality projectors, when evaluated at the UV brane:

\displaystyle N_{1}^{HW}(x,z)=N_{1L}^{HW}(x,z)+N_{1R}^{HW}(x,z)\,,\qquad N_{2}^{HW}(x,z)=N_{2L}^{HW}(x,z)+N_{2R}^{HW}(x,z),

(28)

where
N_{1,2L/R}^{HW}=\dfrac{1\mp i\Gamma^{5}}{2}N_{1,2}^{HW}
are chiral spinors. In momentum space, they are written in terms of the left (right) 4D spinors
u_{L,R}(p)
satisfying the 4D Dirac equation

\displaystyle N_{1}^{HW}(p,z)=F^{HW}_{1L}(p,z){u}_{L}(p)+F_{1R}^{HW}(p,z){u}_{R}(p)\,,\qquad N_{2}^{HW}(p,z)=F_{2L}^{HW}(p,z){u}_{L}(p)+F_{2R}^{HW}(p,z){u}_{R}(p).

(29)

We perform KK decomposition for the fermion fields as follows:

\displaystyle N_{1,2L/R}^{HW}(p,z)=\sum_{n}N^{n}_{1,2L/R}(p)F^{n\,HW}_{1,2L/R}(p,z).

(30)

Mode functions for ground state nucleons
F^{HW}_{L,R}(z)\equiv F^{0\,HW}_{L,R}(z)
are the solutions of the Dirac equation (
26
)
Hong et al. 2007

\displaystyle F_{1L}^{HW}(p,z)=c_{1}z^{5/2}J_{2}(m_{n}z)\,,\qquad F_{1R}^{HW}(p,z)=c_{1}z^{5/2}J_{3}(m_{n}z);

(31)

\displaystyle F_{2L}^{HW}(p,z)=-c_{2}z^{5/2}J_{3}(m_{n}z)\,,\qquad F_{2R}^{HW}(p,z)=c_{2}z^{5/2}J_{2}(m_{n}z),

(32)

Maru and Tachibana 2009

\displaystyle|c_{1,2}|=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{z_{m}J_{2}(m_{n}z)}.

IV
Tensor-Nucleon couplings

In order to derive the tensor meson-nucleon coupling constant
g_{f_{2}NN}
within the holographic QCD, we have to write down the interaction Lagrangian
L^{5D}_{f_{2}NN}
for the corresponding fields in the bulk of the 5D AdS space. The interaction Lagrangian between the tensor meson and nucleons in 4D theory is given in Ref.
Yu et al. 2011
:

\displaystyle L_{f_{2}NN}=-2i\frac{g^{(1)}_{f_{2}NN}}{m_{n}}\overline{N}(x)(\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\mu})N(x)h_{\mu\nu}(x)+4\frac{g^{(2)}_{f_{2}NN}}{m_{n}^{2}}\partial^{\mu}\overline{N}(x)\partial^{\nu}N(x)h_{\mu\nu}(x)\,,

(42)

where
\overleftrightarrow{\partial}=\overleftarrow{\partial}-\overrightarrow{\partial}
and
M_{N}
denote the mass of nucleon. It is reasonable to write 4D action in momentum space using Fourier transforms of the nucleon
N(x)
and tensor meson
h_{\mu\nu}(x)
fields:

\displaystyle\overline{N}(x)=\int d^{4}p^{\prime}\overline{N}(p^{\prime})e^{ip^{\prime}x}\,,\qquad N(x)=\int d^{4}pN(p)e^{-ipx}\,,\qquad h_{\mu\nu}(x)=\int d^{4}qh_{\mu\nu}(q)e^{-iqx}.

(43)

The action for the interaction of these fields in momentum space has two terms:

\displaystyle S^{4D}_{\text{int}}=4\frac{g^{(1)}_{f_{2}NN}}{m_{n}}\int d^{4}p^{\prime}d^{4}p(p^{\prime}+p)^{\nu}\overline{N}(p^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}N(p)h_{\mu\nu}(q)+4\frac{g^{(2)}_{f_{2}NN}}{m_{n}^{2}}\int d^{4}p^{\prime}d^{4}pp^{\prime\mu}p^{\nu}\overline{N}(p^{\prime})N(p)h_{\mu\nu}(q),

(44)

which are proportional to
(p^{\prime}+p)^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}
and
p^{\prime\mu}p^{\nu}
in the tensor current of the nucleon. In 5D space-time (
1
), the interaction Lagrangian between the bulk tensor
h_{MN}
and fermionic fields
N
is the extension of the Lagrangian (
42
). To this end, we should replace partial derivatives with the covariant derivatives (
25
) of the spinor field and construct hermitian Lagrangian. Thus, we can write the 5D action

\displaystyle S_{\text{int}}^{5D}=\int d^{5}x\sqrt{g}\,L^{5D}_{f_{2}NN}\,,

(45)

based on the following interaction Lagrangian:

\displaystyle L_{f_{2}NN}^{5D}

\displaystyle=2i\overline{N}(x,z)\Big(\Gamma^{M}\overleftrightarrow{\nabla}^{N}+\Gamma^{N}\overleftrightarrow{\nabla}^{M}\Big)N(x,z)h_{MN}(x,z)

\displaystyle+4\Bigg(N(x,z)^{\dagger}\Gamma^{0}\nabla^{M}\nabla^{N}N(x,z)+\Big(N(x,z)^{\dagger}\Gamma^{0}\nabla^{M}\nabla^{N}N(x,z)\Big)^{\dagger}\Bigg)h_{MN}(x,z)\,,

(46)

where

VI
Conclusion and future directions

We studied the holographic description of the tensor meson by exploring its hadronic and radiative coupling constants. The results are consistent with those obtained from Dispersion Relations and amplitude methods, and the holographic approach still remains effective in describing the couplings of tensor mesons. Our results show that the coupling for tensor meson-nucleon interaction coupling with one derivative term is the dominant one compared to the two-derivative case. The formalism presented here can be extended to describe the tensor glueball, which could be tested in the future Panda experiment
Lutz et al. 2009
. Motivated by the experimental observation of the 4-pion decay of
f_{2}(1270)
(the second dominant decay channel after the two-pion channel), we have computed the coupling (
g^{(i)}_{f_{2}\rho\rho}
) within the holographic model. Radiative decay channels such as
f_{2}(1270)\rightarrow\rho(770)\gamma
, which is obtained via the VMD of the tensor-vector interaction Lagrangian, can be interesting for future experiment measurements, such as the GlueX experiment
Al Ghoul et al. 2016
at Jefferson Lab, to effectively test our prediction for the coupling. We predict the decay width for this channel to be around
0.6\,\text{MeV}
, which is consistent with the prediction presented in Ref.
Jafarzade et al. 2022
. One can study the finite temperature effects
Mamedov and Taghiyeva 2021
;
Mamedov and Nasibova 2021
, the tensor form factors of nucleons given in Ref.
Hagler 2010
;
Aliev et al. 2011
;
Erkol and Ozpineci 2011
and the tensor meson decay constant
Martin Contreras and Vega 2020
within our model.

Uniqueness of a meromorphic function and its linear difference polynomial sharing two sets with finite weights
Goutam Haldar

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the uniqueness property of meromorphic functions together with its linear difference polynomial sharing two sets. Using the polynomial introduced in [FILOMAT 33(18)(2019), 6055-6072], we have improved the result of Li-Chen [Abstract and Applied Analysis, 2014,Article ID 894968] in sense of reducing cardinalities of the main set S S and the associated weights. Some examples have been exhibited to validate our certain claims in the main result.

Introduction, Definitions and Results

Let
f
and

be two non-constant meromorphic functions defined on the set of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
and

and

have the same set of zeros with the same multiplicities and if we do not the multiplicities, then
f
and
g
are said to share the value
a
IM (ignoring multiplicities).

Throughout the paper, we have used the standard notations and definitions of value distribution theory of meromorphic functions introduced in
[
11
]
.
We recall that
T(r,f)
denotes the Nevanlinna characteristic function of the non-constant meromorphic function. Also we denote by
S(r,f)
any quantity satisfying
T(r,f)=o(T(r,f))
as
r\rightarrow\infty
possibly outside a finite set of logarithmic measure and
N(r,a;f)
(
\overline{N}(r,a;f)
) denotes the counting function (reduced counting function) of
a
-points of meromorphic functions
f
. A meromorphic function
a(z)
is said to be a small function of
f
if
T(r,a)=o(T(r,f))
. Let
S(f)
be the set of all small functions of
f
.
For a set
S\subset S(f)
, we define

\displaystyle E_{f}(S)=\bigcup_{a\in S}\{z|f(z)-a(z)=0\},

where each zero is counted according to its multiplicity and

\overline{E}_{f}(S)=\bigcup_{a\in S}\{z|f(z)-a(z)=0\},\;\text{where each zero is counted only once}.

If
E_{f}(S)=E_{g}(S)
, we say that
f
,
g
share the set S CM and if
\overline{E}_{f}(S)=\overline{E}_{g}(S)
, we say
f
,
g
share the set S IM.

Also we note that

We now recall here the uniqueness polynomial

[

(1.4)

P(z)=az^{n}+bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d,

where

,
\text{gcd}(n,m)=1
and

\{0\}

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{4bd}=\displaystyle\frac{n(n-2m)}{(n-m)^{2}}\neq 1
;
a\neq\gamma_{j}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}(2bm\omega_{j}^{2m}+cm\omega_{j}^{m})
, with
\omega_{j}

Then by simple calculation it can easily seen that all the zeros of

Let us define

(1.5)

W(z)=-\displaystyle\frac{az^{n}}{bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d}.

Now,

\displaystyle W(z)-\displaystyle\frac{a}{\gamma_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{j}z^{n}+bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d}{\gamma_{j}(bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a\displaystyle\frac{Q(z)}{\gamma_{j}(bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d)},

where
Q(z)=\gamma_{j}z^{n}+bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d
.

It is to be observed that
Q(\omega_{j})=Q^{{}^{\prime}}(\omega_{j})=Q^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\omega_{j})=0
but
Q^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(\omega_{j})\neq 0
for

.

Therefore,

(1.6)

W(z)-\beta_{j}=-a\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-\omega_{j})^{3}R_{n-3m}(z)}{bz^{2m}+cz^{m}+d},

where
R_{n-3m}(z)
is a polynomial of degree

and

(1.7)

\beta_{j}=\displaystyle\frac{a}{\gamma_{j}},\;j=1,2,\ldots,m.

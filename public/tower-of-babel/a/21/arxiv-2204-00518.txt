Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we study the separable and weak convergence of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces. Furthermore, we prove that the block space
\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Köthe dual of the mixed Morrey space
\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the Fatou property of these block spaces. The boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function is further obtained on the block space
\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As applications, the characterizations of
BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
via the commutators of the fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha}
on mixed Morrey spaces are proved as well as the block space
\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Keywords:
Köthe dual; Mixed Morrey spaces; Block spaces;
BMO({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
; Characterization; Fractional integral operators.

Introduction

In recent years, since the precise structure of mixed-norm function spaces, the mixed-norm function spaces are widely used in the partial differential equations
[
3

5

. In 1961, the mixed-norm Lebesgue space

were studied by Benedek and Panzone
[
7
]
. These spaces are natural generalizations of the classical Lebesgue space
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})(0<p\leq\infty)
. After that, many function spaces with mixed norm were introduced, such as mixed-norm Lorentz spaces
[
8
]
, mixed-norm Lorentz-Marcinkiewicz spaces
[
9
]
, mixed-norm Orlicz spaces
[
10
]
, anisotropic mixed-norm Hardy spaces
[
11
]
, mixed-norm Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
[
12
]
and weak mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces
[
13
]
. More studies can be refereed in
[
14
]
and so on.

In 2019, Nogayama
[
16
,
15
]
introduced mixed Morrey spaces
associated with mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces and Morrey spaces, and further proved that the block space
\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are predual spaces of the mixed Morrey space
\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. In this paper, we will prove that
\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are also Köthe duals of these mixed Morrey spaces. For more studies and a deeper account of developments about the Köthe dual we may consult
[
17
]
and the references therein.

Given
0<\alpha<n
, for a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the fractional integral operators are defined by

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,

and these operators play such a prominent role in real and harmonic analysis
[
1
,
2
]
. By simple calculations,

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)I_{\alpha}g(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)I_{\alpha}f(x)dx

can be obtained.

For a locally integrable function
b
and a measurable function
f
, the commutator of the fractional integral operators is defined by

[b,I_{\alpha}]f(x):=b(x)I_{\alpha}f(x)-I_{\alpha}(bf)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(b(x)-b(y))f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy,

which was introduced by Chanillo in
[
18
]
. For the commutators, a similar formula

(2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)[b,I_{\alpha}]g(x)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)[b,I_{\alpha}]f(x)dx

can be obtained, where
f,g
are measurable functions.

Let us recall the classical results. In 1991, Di Fazio and Ragusa given the characterizations of
BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via the boundedness of
[b,I_{\alpha}]
on classical Morrey spaces
[
19
]
. That is, let
1<p\leq p_{0}<\infty
,
1<q\leq q_{0}<\infty
,
\frac{p}{p_{0}}=\frac{q}{q_{0}}
, and
\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{\alpha}{n}
. Then

b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Rightarrow[b,I_{\alpha}]:\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{q}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Conversely, if
n-\alpha
is an even integer, then

[b,I_{\alpha}]:\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{q}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Rightarrow b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In 2006, Shirai given another characterizations of
BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via the boundedness of
[b,I_{\alpha}]
on classical Morrey spaces
[
20
]
. That is

b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Leftrightarrow[b,I_{\alpha}]:\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{q}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
1<p\leq p_{0}<\infty
,
1<q\leq q_{0}<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{\alpha}{n}
.

In 2019, Nogayama proved characterizations of
BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via the operator
[b,I_{\alpha}]
on mixed Morrey spaces
[
16
]
. That is

b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Leftrightarrow[b,I_{\alpha}]:\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
1<\frac{1}{p_{0}}<\frac{\alpha}{n}
,
\frac{n}{p_{0}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
,
\frac{n}{q_{0}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}
,
\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{\alpha}{n}
and
\frac{\vec{p}}{p_{0}}=\frac{\vec{q}}{q_{0}}
.

In fact, the results of
[
16
]
can be regarded as a generation of
[
19
]
. In the paper, we generalize the results of
[
20
]
on mixed Morrey spaces. We point out that

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{p_{0}}{p}\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{p}{p_{0}}\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)\geq\frac{p_{0}}{p}\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)=\frac{p_{0}}{pq_{0}}.

Thus, it’s easy to see that the results of
[
19
]
can be regard as the improved results of
[
20
]
. But, for mixed Morrey spaces, we can only prove that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\alpha\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{0}}{p_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right).

In other words, the results of
[
16
]
are not the improvements of Theorem
6.1
. Furthermore, when
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}
and
\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}
, the results of Theorem
6.1
are better than
[
16
]
.

2
Main definitions

We begin this section with the definition of some maximal functions in the following.

For a locally integrable function
f
, the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator is defined by, for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

M(f)(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|dy,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
x
, and the sharp maximal operator is defined by

M^{\sharp}(f)(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-f_{Q}|dy,

where
f_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}f
and the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
x
.

The definition of ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces is presented as follows, which were introduced by Sawano et al.
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A (quasi-)Banach space
\mathbf{X}\subset\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with (quasi-)norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}}
is called a ball (quasi-)Banach function space if

(i)

|g|\leq|f|
almost everywhere implies that
\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}}
;

(ii)

0\leq f_{m}\uparrow f
almost everywhere implies that
\|f_{m}\|_{\mathbf{X}}\uparrow\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}};

(iii)

If
|Q|<\infty
, then
\chi_{Q}\in\mathbf{X}
;

(iv)

If
f\geq 0
almost everywhere and
|Q|<\infty
, then

\int_{Q}f(x)dx\leq C_{Q}\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}};

for some positive constants
C_{Q}
,
0<c_{Q}<\infty
, depending on
Q
but independent of
f
.

Remark 2.1
.

If ”cube” is replaced by ”ball” in the preceding definition, it’s also valid. In particular, if we replace any cubes
Q
by any measurable sets
E
in Definition
2.1
, it is (quasi-)Banach function spaces (see
[
24
, Definition 1.1 of Chapter 1]
).

The definition of the associate space of a ball (quasi-)Banach function space can be found in
[
24
, chapter 1]

as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

For any ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces
\mathbf{X}
, the associate space (also called the Köthe dual)
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}
is defined by setting

\mathbf{X}^{\prime}:=\left\{f\in\mathfrak{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}:=\sup_{g\in\mathbf{X},\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx<\infty\right\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}
is called the associate norm of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}}.

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

In other literatures (for example
[
31
]
) the Banach function spaces and the associate spaces are called the Köthe spaces and the Köthe dual respectively. Thus, the associate spaces of ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces are called the Köthe dual of ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces.

(ii)

It is easy to prove that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx\leq\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}.

(iii)

Due to
[
32
, Lemma 2.6]
, if
\mathbf{X}
is a ball Banach function space, then

\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}}=\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime\prime}},

where
\mathbf{X}^{\prime\prime}=(\mathbf{X}^{\prime})^{\prime}
.

(iv)

According to the Definition
2.2
and (iv), it is easy to know that

(3)

\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}=\||f|\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}=\sup_{g\in\mathbf{X},\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx

and

(4)

\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}}=\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime\prime}}=\||f|\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime\prime}}=\||f|\|_{\mathbf{X}}.

Furthermore, by (
3
) and (
4
), for
f\in X^{\prime}
and
g\in\mathbf{X}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbf{X}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx=\sup_{\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}=1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|=\sup_{\|g\|_{\mathbf{X}}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx.

Indeed, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx,

and for
h=sgn(fg)|g|
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)h(x)dx=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)h(x)dx\right|=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)g(x)|dx.

We still recall the notion of the convexity of ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces, which can be found in

Definition 2.3

Let
\mathbf{X}

Obviously, if

are also ball (quasi-)Banach function spaces. Now, let us recall

Definition 2.4

Let

[\omega]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\omega(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\omega(x)^{\frac{1}{1-p}}dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

A weight

Definition 2.5

Let

(i)

3
Weak convergence of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces

In this section, we discuss the separable and weak convergence of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<\vec{p}<\infty
. Then the mixed-norm Lebesgue space
L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is separable space.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<\vec{p}<\infty
and
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

then there exists a subset

Before we give our proofs, the following lemma is necessary. According to

Proof of Theorem

.

From Lemma

We write

Proof of Theorem

.

According to

According to Theorem
3.1
, we assume that

\{\mathcal{F}_{k,1}(g_{1})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

\{\mathcal{F}_{k,2}(g_{2})\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{m,m}(g)-\mathcal{F}_{m^{\prime},m^{\prime}}(g)|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f_{k_{m}}(x)-f_{k_{m^{\prime}}}(x)||(g(x)-g_{i}(x)|dx

The proof is completed.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi_{Q_{k+1}\backslash Q_{k}}(x)}{(2^{k}r)^{n}}\|b\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\chi_{Q(x_{0},r)}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Applying Lemma
5.2
, we write

\displaystyle\|m_{k}\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|\chi_{Q_{k+1}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{(2^{k}r)^{n}}\|b\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\chi_{Q(x_{0},r)}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\chi_{Q_{k+1}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\cdot\|\chi_{Q_{k+1}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\chi_{Q_{k+1}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\cdot\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\chi_{Q(x_{0},r)}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{(2^{k}r)^{n}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{|Q(x_{0},2^{k+1}r)|}{\|\chi_{Q_{k+1}}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\cdot\frac{\|b\|_{L^{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\chi_{Q(x_{0},r)}\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{(2^{k}r)^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{2^{nk}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\cdot|Q_{k+1}|^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}}.

It is obvious that
\frac{1}{p_{0}}>0
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2^{nk}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}<C_{n,p_{0},\vec{p}}<\infty,

where
C_{n,p_{0},\vec{p}}
only depends on
n,\penalty\ p_{0}
and
\vec{p}
. Then, for any
(p_{0}^{\prime},\vec{p}\,^{\prime})
-block
b
,

\|Mb\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,p_{0},\vec{p}}.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then there exists a decomposition such that
f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}b_{k}(x)
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|Mf\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\|Mb_{k}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,p_{0},\vec{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|\leq 2C_{n,p_{0},\vec{p}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{p}\,^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let
b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
p=\min\{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}\}
. From Hölder’s inequality, we have

f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Thus,
[b,I_{\alpha}]f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{q_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
.
According to Lemma
6.1
, there exist
1<s<\infty
such that

[b,I_{\alpha}]f\in L^{s}_{\omega}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),

where
\omega=[M(\chi_{Q(0,1)})]^{\varepsilon}
with
0<\epsilon<1
. Thus, the assumption of Lemma
6.6
is satisfied.
We use Lemma
6.6
, Lemma
6.4
and Lemma
6.5
to write

\displaystyle\left\|[b,I_{\alpha}]f\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|M([b,I_{\alpha}](f))\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|M^{\sharp}\left([b,I_{\alpha}](f)\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\left\|I_{\alpha}(|f|)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|I_{r\alpha}(|f|^{r})^{\frac{1}{r}}\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=C\|b\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left(\left\|I_{\alpha}(|f|)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|I_{r\alpha}(|f|^{r})\right\|^{\frac{1}{r}}_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}/r}^{q_{0}/r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Assume that
[b,I_{\alpha}]
is bounded from
\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We use the same method as Janson
[
30
]
. Choose
0\neq z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\notin Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n})
. Then for
x\in Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n})
,
|x|^{n-\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n}))
. Hence,
|x|^{n-\alpha}
can be written as the absolutely convergent Fourier series:

(12)

|x|^{n-\alpha}\chi_{Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n})}(x)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}e^{2im\cdot x}\chi_{Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n})}(x)

with
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|a_{m}|<\infty
.

For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, let
Q=Q(x_{0},t)
and
Q_{z_{0}}=Q(x_{0}+z_{0}t,t)
. Let
s(x)=\overline{sgn(\int_{Q^{\prime}}(b(x)-b(y))dy)}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|}\frac{1}{|Q_{z_{0}}|}\int_{Q}\left|\int_{Q_{z_{0}}}(b(x)-b(y))dy\right|dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|Q|}\frac{1}{|Q_{z_{0}}|}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{z_{0}}}s(x)(b(x)-b(y))dydx

\displaystyle=t^{-2n}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{z_{0}}}s(x)(b(x)-b(y))dydx.

If
x\in Q
and
y\in Q_{z_{0}}
, then
\frac{y-x}{t}\in Q(z_{0},2\sqrt{n})
. Hence, (
12
) shows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|

\displaystyle=t^{-n-\alpha}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{z_{0}}}s(x)(b(x)-b(y))|x-y|^{\alpha-n}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{t}\right)^{n-\alpha}dydx

\displaystyle=t^{-n-\alpha}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{z_{0}}}s(x)(b(x)-b(y))|x-y|^{\alpha-n}e^{-2im\cdot\frac{y}{t}}dy\times e^{2im\cdot\frac{x}{t}}dx

\displaystyle=t^{-n-\alpha}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\int_{Q}[b,I_{\alpha}](e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q_{z_{0}}})(x)\times s(x)e^{2im\cdot\frac{x}{t}}dx.

By the definition of Köthe dual spaces,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|\leq t^{-n-\alpha}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\left\|[b,I_{\alpha}](e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q_{z_{0}}})\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{o}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|s\cdot e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{q}\,^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

It is easy to calculate

\left\|s\cdot e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{q}\,^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\vec{q}\,^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim t^{n-\frac{n}{{q_{0}}}}.

Hence,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|=t^{-\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\alpha}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\left\|[b,I_{\alpha}](e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q_{z_{0}}})\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

According to the hypothesis

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\alpha}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\left\|e^{-2im\cdot\frac{\cdot}{t}}\chi_{Q_{z_{0}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|[b,I_{\alpha}]\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\alpha+\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{m}\left\|[b,I_{\alpha}]\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|a_{m}|\|[b,I_{\alpha}]\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|[b,I_{\alpha}]\|_{\mathcal{M}_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\vec{q}}^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Thus, we have

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b(y)|dx\leq\frac{2}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q_{z_{0}}}|dx\leq C\|[b,I_{\alpha}]\|_{L^{\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\vec{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

This prove
b\in BMO(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

(i) Let
U_{\vec{p}}^{p_{0}}

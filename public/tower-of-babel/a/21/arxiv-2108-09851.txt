A Framework for Computing Greedy Clique Cover
Ahammed Ullah

Abstract
Structural parameters of graph (such as degeneracy and arboricity) had rarely been considered when designing algorithms for (edge) clique cover problems. Taking degeneracy of graph into account, we present a greedy framework and two fixed-parameter tractable algorithms for clique cover problems. We introduce a set theoretic concept and demonstrate its use in the computations of different objectives of clique cover . Furthermore, we show efficacy of our algorithms in practice.

1.1
Our Contributions.

We introduce a set theoretic concept (we call it
candidate clique sets
) closely related to family of sets representing vertices of an intersection graph
[
34
]
and use it to design greedy algorithms and FPT algorithms.

We define a greedily computable
clique cover
and show that it admits desirable characterizations. Combining
candidate clique sets
with different objectives of
clique cover
, we derive characterizations useful in designing and analyzing algorithms.

Using our derived characterizations, we describe a basic greedy framework that can compute a
minimal
(inclusion-wise)
clique cover
in
O(m^{2})
time.

We make use of
candidate clique sets
and related characterizations to improve the time bound of our greedy framework; for graphs of constant maximum degree, this results in a family of linear time algorithms. We make the improved greedy framework amenable to utilize degeneracy of input graph; this also results in a family of linear time algorithms, for graphs of constant degeneracy.

We present two FPT algorithms for
ECC-d
with respect to parameter
(d,k)
. One of our FPT algorithm is based on
candidate clique sets
, and the other FPT algorithm is based on maximal clique enumeration (listing all maximal cliques in graph
[
13
]
). For graphs of constant degeneracy, time bounds of our FPT algorithms have single exponential dependence on
k
.

For graphs satisfying
2^{k}>d
, our maximal clique enumeration based FPT algorithm improves upon the time bound of the search tree algorithm of
[
19
]
by a factor of
3^{k(2^{k}-d)}
.

Our greedy framework and FPT algorithm based on
candidate clique sets
demonstrate a general design paradigm for different objectives of
clique covers
. For example, we easily obtain a search tree algorithm for the
assignment-minimum clique cover
(
clique cover
with a minimum total number of vertices across all cliques) [
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
36
]
] with depth, number of branches, and space use all bounded by polynomials in input size.
Prior search tree algorithm designed for this objective of
clique cover
requires bounds exponential in input size for depth, number of branches, and space use
[
12
]
.

Our algorithms show excellent performance in practice. We show comparison results of different metrics of our algorithms against the state of the art, on random graphs, as well as on real-world graphs.

Definition 3.1
(Minimum Clique Cover)

A
minimum clique cover
of a graph
G
is a
clique cover
with smallest number of cliques among all
clique covers
of
G
.

For the example graph of Figure
1
, a
minimum clique cover
is
\mathcal{C}=\{\{1,3,5\},\{2,3,4\},\{3,4,5,6\}\}
. In this case all the cliques of the
minimum clique cover
are maximal cliques. But having a
clique cover
consisting only of maximal cliques is neither necessary nor sufficient for a
minimum clique cover
.

Definition 3.2
(Minimal Clique Cover)

A
minimal clique cover

\mathcal{C}
of a graph
G
is a
clique cover
of
G
such that no other
clique cover
of
G
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is an inclusion-wise minimal set of cliques that covers all the edges of
G
.

A
minimum clique cover
is also a
minimal clique cover
. Note that from a
minimal clique cover
it may be possible to remove vertices (or edges), given that such removals still maintain a
clique cover
of
G
. Any such removal of vertices (or edges) from a
minimal clique cover
would not affect number of cliques in the
clique cover
.

In the example graph of Figure 1,
\mathcal{C}=\{\{1,3,5\},\{2,3,4\},\{4,5,6\},\{3,6,5\}\}
is a
minimal clique cover
of the graph, and size of the
clique cover
is four. From the clique
\{3,6,5\}
, we can remove vertex 5 (hence edges
\{3,5\}
and
\{6,5\}
), but the size of the
clique cover
would still be four.

It is easy to construct a
minimal clique cover
by covering every edge with a clique containing only the edge. But, computing a
minimal clique cover
with some desirable characterizations does not seem to be straightforward. Therefore, we define a type of
clique cover
that admits desirable characterizations and can be efficiently turned into a
minimal clique cover
, preserving the characterizations.

Definition 3.3
(Locally Minimal Clique Cover)

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{k}\}
be a
clique cover
of a subset of edges of
G=(V,E)
, and
\{x,y\}
be an uncovered edge of
G
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\prime}}
be a clique cover of the edges covered in
\mathcal{C}
together with the edge
\{x,y\}
constructed as follows.

(i) If edge
\{x,y\}
can be covered by a clique contained in
\mathcal{C}
, then we set
\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\prime}}
to be a clique cover in which
\{x,y\}
is covered with exactly one of the cliques of
\mathcal{C}
and rest of the cliques of
\mathcal{C}
are included.

(ii) Otherwise, we create a new clique
C_{k+1}
containing only
\{x,y\}
, and we set
\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\prime}}
to be
\mathcal{C}\cup\{C_{k+1}\}
.

We call a
clique cover

\mathcal{C}

locally minimal
if
\mathcal{C}
is obtained from an empty clique cover using aforesaid construction; i.e., for every expansion of the cliques contained in
\mathcal{C}
, we either have used (i) whenever applicable, or (ii) otherwise.

Note that a
locally minimal clique cover
only ensures local minimality with respect to an uncovered edge of interest; it does not ensure minimality with respect to the edges already included in the clique cover. Nevertheless, it allows characterizations of
clique covers
desirable for designing and analyzing algorithms.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{k}\}
be a clique cover of a subset of edges of
G=(V,E)
. If
\mathcal{C}
is
locally minimal
, then each vertex
x
appears at most
|N(x)|
times in
\mathcal{C}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is
locally minimal
, for an uncovered edge
\{x,y\}
covered during the expansion of clique cover at hand, we can trace use of the two rules described in Definition
3.3
. Let
C^{{}^{\prime}}
be the clique cover immediately before covering the edge
\{x,y\}
. Before the expansion of
C^{{}^{\prime}}
for
\{x,y\}
, none of the cliques in
C^{{}^{\prime}}
contained both
x
and
y
: if
x
and
y
are both contained in a clique of
C^{{}^{\prime}}
, then clearly
\{x,y\}
is not uncovered. Number of uncovered edges
\{x,y\}
incident on a vertex
x
is at most
|N(x)|
. Therefore, number of such expansions of a
clique cover
for a vertex
x
is at most
|N(x)|
. Hence the vertex
x
appears at most
|N(x)|
times in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.1

The converse of characterization in Lemma
3.1
does not hold; i.e., each
x\in V
can appear at most
|N(x)|
times in
\mathcal{C}
, but

Lemma 3.2

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{k}\}
be a clique cover of a subset of edges of

\mathcal{C}
is

, then
C_{i}\not\subseteq C_{j},\forall i,j\in[1,k],i\neq j

is not locally minimal.

WLOG assume
C_{i}
is being created first, and consider the first uncovered edge
\{x,y\}

is being created. Since

is not

.

Remark 3.2

The converse of characterization in Lemma
3.2
does not hold; i.e., it is possible that no clique in
\mathcal{C}
is contained in another clique, but
\mathcal{C}
may not be

Lemma 3.3

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{k}\}
be a clique cover of a subset of edges of
G=(V,E)
. If
\mathcal{C}
is

, then
\forall\{C_{i},C_{j}\}\in\mathcal{C},i\neq j,\exists x\in C_{i},y\in C_{j}

We prove the contrapositive, i.e., if for some
\{C_{i},C_{j}\}\in\mathcal{C}
with

\forall x\in C_{i}
and
\forall y\in C_{j}
,
x\neq y
,
\{x,y\}\in E
, then
\mathcal{C}
is not locally minimal.

WLOG assume
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is being created first, and consider the first uncovered edge
\{y,z\}
for which
\mathcal{C}_{j}
is being created. Clearly,
\forall x\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
,
\{x,z\}\in E
and
\{x,y\}\in E

3.3
applies for

, instead rule (ii) of Definition

\mathcal{C}
is not

Remark 3.3

Clique cover
satisfying Lemma
3.2
does not imply it satisfies Lemma
3.3
. But,
clique cover
satisfies Lemma
3.3
implies no clique is contained in another clique, i.e., Lemma

Note that it is the combination of the characterizations in Lemma

desirable than each standalone characterization. For example, it is easy to satisfy the characterizations in Lemma

ensures that a single clique covers the complete graph. We can also glean more information on

D\not\subseteq F

Definition 3.4
(Intersection Graph Basis)

Let
X=\{1,2,...,k\}
, and consider a family of

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},...,F_{n}\}
, with
F_{i}\subseteq X
. The

of
\mathcal{F}
has vertices
F_{i},i\in[1,n]
, and an edge joins
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
when
F_{i}\cap F_{j}\neq\phi
, with
i\neq j
. Every graph
G=(V,E)
arises as the intersection graph of some family of sets; i.e., there is an assignment of a set

to each vertex

of

such that for
\{i,j\}\in V
, and

, the following holds:
\{i,j\}\in E\Leftrightarrow F_{i}\cap F_{j}\neq\phi
. Computing

of

is equivalent to computing a set

such that

is the intersection graph of a family of subsets of

[
14
]
. Such a set

with minimum cardinality is called an intersection graph basis of

[
18
]
. Every element

corresponds to a clique such that

is the vertex set of the clique.

Definition 3.5

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{k}\}

Consider the example graph in Figure

5.1.1
Assignment-Minimum Clique Cover
.

An objective of
clique cover
that has found application in problems requiring statistical data visualization is called
assignment-minimum clique cover
[
[
12
]
,
[
20
]
,
[

]
]. The objective of this
clique cover
is to find a
clique cover

C
of
G
such that among the
clique covers
of
G
,
C
has a minimum total number of vertices contained in the cliques of
C
, i.e.,
\sum_{C_{l}\in\mathcal{C}}|C_{l}|
is minimum. Prior search tree algorithm for this objective of
clique cover
requires bounds exponential in input size for depth, number of branches, and space use
[
12
]
. Next, we describe straightforward modifications of Algorithm
4
that solves this
clique cover
problem exactly, but with polynomial bounds on depth, number of branches, and space use.

For the
assignment-minimum clique cover
, we would run modifications of Algorithm
4
with parameter
k
set to
m
, since no
clique cover
would require more than
m
cliques. We would discard steps 9-11 and steps 17-19, since we would need to explore all
clique covers
with at most
m
cliques. Let
\mathcal{C}^{AM}
denote a
clique cover
with minimum total number of vertices among the
clique covers
explored in the search tree. Initially,
\mathcal{C}^{AM}
would be empty. In step 2 of Algorithm
4
, while returning, we would compare
\mathcal{C}^{AM}
with
\mathcal{C}
, and if
\mathcal{C}
has smaller total number of vertices than
\mathcal{C}^{AM}
, then we would set
\mathcal{C}^{AM}
to be
\mathcal{C}
. This algorithm has space bound
O(m\Delta)
(Lemma
5.2
), depth bound
O(dm)
(Lemma
5.3
), and
O(d^{2})
number of branches (Lemma
5.4
).

shows the correctness proof for Algorithm

. Next, we show the fixed-parameter tractability of Algorithm
9
.

Since
|N_{d}(x)\cap N(y)|\leq d

3^{\frac{d}{3}}

3^{\frac{d}{3}}
. Therefore, total number of nodes in the search tree is at most
3^{\frac{dk}{3}}
, i.e., time bound of Algorithm

O^{*}(3^{O(dk)})
.

At each node of the search tree of Algorithm
9
, we need to enumerate maximal cliques of a subgraph containing
|N_{d}(x)\cap N(y)|=O(d)
vertices. Assuming maximal clique enumeration algorithm uses space proportional to the size of input graph, in a search tree with at most
k

9
is
O(m+d^{2}k)
.

Using the Moon-Moser bound for the search tree algorithm of
[
19
]

O^{*}(3^{O(k2^{k})})

2^{k}>d
, Algorithm
9
improves upon the time bound of the search tree algorithm of
[
19
]
by a factor of
3^{k(2^{k}-d)}
.

We can also compare the algorithms using time bounds of maximal clique enumeration algorithms. Using the time bound of
[
13
]
, we get
O^{*}{}(d^{k}3^{O(dk)})
and
O^{*}(2^{k^{2}}3^{O(dk)})

9
and search tree algorithm of
[
19
]
respectively. For
2^{k}>d
, this results in
(\frac{2^{k}}{d})^{k}
factor improvement of time bound by Algorithm
9
on the time bound of the search tree algorithm of
[
19
]
.

Note that our FPT algorithm based on maximal clique enumeration (Algorithm
9
) has better dependency on
d
in the time bound than our FPT algorithm based on
candidate clique sets
(Algorithm
4

O^{*}(3^{O(dk)})
vs.
O^{*}(d^{O(dk)})
. Therefore, we expect Algorithm
9
to perform better than Algorithm

7
Conclusions

We have presented a set theoretic concept and its use as a design paradigm for computing different objectives of
clique covers
. We have shown that integration of structural parameters of graph along with natural parameter can lead to design of better algorithms for
clique cover
problems, both in asymptotic time bound and in practice. We have provided detailed comparison results of different metrics of our algorithms against the state of the art, substantiating excellent performance of our algorithms.

Motivated by real-world applications, recently, a number of
clique cover
problems have been studied
[
9
]
that consider edge weights of graph. We believe (alluding to the polynomial bounds we have shown for the
assignment-minimum clique cover

[
12
]
) our set theoretic concept and its use in utilizing structural parameters of graph would prompt further studies of these edge weighted
clique cover
problems.

Eighth–Order Foldy–Wouthuysen Transformation
Ulrich D. Jentschura

Abstract
The calculation of higher-order binding corrections to bound systems is a fundamental problem of theoretical physics. For any nonrelativistic expansion, one needs the Foldy–Wouthuysen Transformation which disentangles the particle and the antiparticle degrees of freedom. This transformation is carried out here to eighth order in the momenta, or, to eighth order in the momentum operators, which is equivalent to the eighth order of the fine-structure constant. Matrix elements of the eighth-order terms are evaluated for F 5 / 2 F_{5/2} and F 7 / 2 F_{7/2} states in hydrogenlike ions and compared with the Dirac–Coulomb energy levels.

II
Direct Calculation

II.1
Unitary Transformation

We start from the well-known Dirac Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{D}}

coupled to a general electromagnetic field,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm D}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi}+\beta\,m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(m+e\,\Phi)\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&\;\;\;\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}\\
\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}&\;\;\;(-m+e\,\Phi)\,\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\vec{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\vec{\sigma}\\
\vec{\sigma}&0\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&0_{2\times 2}\\
0_{2\times 2}&-\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\end{array}\right)\,.

The Dirac Hamiltonian couples upper and lower components of the Dirac bispinor.
The aim of the (unitary) Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation is
to separate the upper (particle) from the lower (antiparticle)
degrees of freedom, through an iterative
procedure. One writes the Hamiltonian
as a sum of even terms (
\mathcal{E}
) and odd terms (
\mathcal{O}
)
in bispinor space (see also Chap. 11 of Ref.
JeAd2022book
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm D}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{O}\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=

\displaystyle\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\left(H+\beta\,H\,\beta\right)\,,\qquad\mathcal{O}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\left(H-\beta\,H\,\beta\right)\,,

(10b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=

\displaystyle\;\beta m+e\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(m+e\,\Phi)\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&\;\;\;0_{2\times 2}\\
0_{2\times 2}&\;\;\;(-m+e\,\Phi)\,\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\;\;\;\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}\\
\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}&0\end{array}\right)\,.

The aim is to eliminate the odd terms
\mathcal{O}
through
unitary transformations. These transformations,
necessarily, in order to preserve
the physical interpretation of the operators,
need to conserve parity
[see the remarks following Eq. (7.33) of
Ref.
ObSiTe2017
and the comprehensive
discussion in Ref.
JeNo2014jpa
].
In short, it has been shown
in Ref.
JeNo2014jpa
that, if one uses
a unitary transformation which breaks parity,
the Dirac Hamiltonian can be “disentangled”
into what seems to be “particle” and “antiparticle”
Hamiltonians, but the operators inside the
disentangled (diagonal) Hamiltonian have changed
their physical interpretation.
Spurious terms
Ob2001
which could otherwise break
particle-antiparticle symmetry in gravitational
fields were shown to be absent in
Ref.
JeNo2014jpa
, if the parity-conserving,
standard Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation is used
(see also Ref.
Je2020physics
).
One defines the Hermitian operator
S
and the
unitary operator
U=\exp(\mathrm{i}S)
as follows,

S=-\mathrm{i}\,\beta\,\frac{\mathcal{O}}{2m}\,,\qquad S=S^{\mathrm{+}}\,,\qquad U=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S}\,.

(11)

The odd operator
\mathcal{O}
defined in Eq. (
10
)
is proportional to the kinetic momentum
\vec{\pi}
,
and hence, the Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation eliminates
the odd terms order by order in an expansion in the
momenta. The transformed Hamiltonian is
written in terms of nested commutators,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm FW}=

\displaystyle\;\exp(\mathrm{i}S)\,\left(H-\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\right)\exp(-\mathrm{i}S)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;H+\left[\mathrm{i}\,S,H-\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\right]+\frac{1}{2!}\,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}\,S,H-\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\right]\right]

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{3!}\,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}\,S,H-\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\right]\right]\right]

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{4!}\,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}S,\left[\mathrm{i}\,S,H-\mathrm{i}\,\partial_{t}\right]\right]\right]\right]+\ldots\,.

(12)

where one notes the identity
[\mathrm{i}S,H-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}]=\mathrm{i}[S,H]-\partial_{t}S
.

Though the intensive use of computer algebra
generalized to the symbolic commutation relations

III.1
Coulomb Field Coupling

One of the important applications of the
Hamiltonian (
21

which are relevant to one-electron
ions in the central field of a nucleus

Z

In this case, one has the relations

\displaystyle e\,A^{0}=

\displaystyle\;V=-\frac{Z\alpha}{r}\,,\qquad\vec{A}=\vec{0}\,,\qquad\vec{B}=\vec{0}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad e\,\vec{E}=

\displaystyle\;-e\,\vec{\nabla}A^{0}=-\vec{\nabla}V\,,\qquad\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}\,.

The scalar potential and electric field

One ends up with the following leading term,
where the subscript

H^{[2]}_{C}=\frac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2m}+V=\frac{\vec{p}^{\,2}}{2m}-\frac{Z\alpha}{r}\,.

This is the Schrödinger–Coulomb Hamiltonian in

JeAd2022book

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}e\,[\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{E}]=

\displaystyle e\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{E}\}=

\displaystyle\;-\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\nabla}V\}=-\mathrm{i}[\vec{p}^{\,2},V]\,.

For the Coulomb field,
the well-known leading relativistic correction to the
Foldy–Wouthuysen Hamiltonian reads as follows,

H^{[4]}_{C}=-\frac{\vec{p}^{\,4}}{8m^{3}}+\frac{1}{8m^{2}}\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+\frac{1}{4m^{2}}\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})=-\frac{\vec{p}^{\,4}}{8m^{3}}+\frac{\pi\,(Z\alpha)}{2m^{2}}\;\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r})+\frac{Z\alpha}{4m^{2}\,r^{3}}\;\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{L}\,,

(31)

where
\vec{L}
is the orbital angular momentum operator.

The sixth-order corrections attain the following form,

H^{[6]}_{C}=\frac{\vec{p}^{\,6}}{16m^{5}}-\frac{3\{\,\vec{p}^{\,2},\vec{\nabla}^{2}V\,\}}{64m^{4}}\;-\frac{3\{\,\vec{p}^{\,2},\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})\}}{32m^{4}}\;+\frac{5}{128m^{4}}\,\left[\vec{p}^{\,2},[\vec{p}^{\,2},V]\right]+\,\frac{(\vec{\nabla}V)^{\,2}}{8m^{3}}\,.

(32)

With
\partial_{t}\vec{E}=\vec{0}
, one has for the eighth-order
corrections,

\displaystyle H^{[8]}_{C}=

\displaystyle\;K_{C}+D_{C}+Q_{C}+L_{C}\,,\qquad K_{C}=-\frac{5}{128m^{7}}\vec{p}^{\,8}\,,\qquad D_{C}=0\,,

(33a)

\displaystyle Q_{C}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{7[\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})]\,[\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})]}{192m^{5}}+\frac{3\,\;[\vec{p}^{\,2},V]\,[\vec{p}^{\,2},V]}{64m^{5}}-\frac{[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},(\vec{\nabla}V)^{2}\,]\,]}{24m^{5}}\,,

(33b)

\displaystyle L_{C}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{5[\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})]\,]\,]\,]}{1024m^{6}}+\frac{\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\{\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})\,]\,]\,\}\,\}}{32m^{6}}\,

\displaystyle\;+\frac{1}{48m^{6}}\,\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p},\vec{\nabla}^{2}V+2\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot(\vec{\nabla}V\times\vec{p})\}\}\}\}\,.

(33c)

III.2
Application:
{{F_{5/2}}}
States

We shall compare the eighth-order
corrections to the bound-state energy
obtained from Eq. (
33a
) to the
bound-state energies of the Dirac–Coulomb problem.
It is well known that the Dirac–Coulomb
problem can be solved exactly (Chap. 8
of Ref.
JeAd2022book
), with the result

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{D}}=

\displaystyle\;m\,\left(1+\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{(n_{r}+\gamma)^{2}}\right)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\,,

\displaystyle n_{r}=

\displaystyle\;n-j-\tfrac{1}{2}\,,\qquad\gamma=\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}+(Z\alpha)^{2}}\,.

(34)

For
nF_{5/2}
states, the presence of
large spin-orbit coupling implies the emergence
of nontrivial corrections to the energy
from the corresponding higher-order terms
in Eq. (
33a
). One expands as follows,

E_{\mathrm{D}}(nF_{5/2})=m-\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}m}{2n^{2}}+(Z\alpha)^{4}m\left(\frac{3}{8n^{4}}-\frac{1}{6n^{3}}\right)\\
-(Z\alpha)^{6}m\left(\frac{1}{216n^{3}}+\frac{1}{24n^{4}}-\frac{1}{4n^{5}}+\frac{5}{16n^{6}}\right)\\
+(Z\alpha)^{8}m\Bigl(-\frac{1}{3888n^{3}}-\frac{1}{432n^{4}}-\frac{1}{432n^{5}}\\
+\frac{5}{48n^{6}}-\frac{5}{16n^{7}}+\frac{35}{128n^{8}}\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(Z\alpha)^{10}\,.

(35)

Within the Foldy–Wouthuysen method, the eighth-order
terms comprise several effects,
namely, the combined effect of third-order
perturbative terms
E^{[8]}=E^{[8]}_{c}
generated by the
fourth-order Hamiltonian
H^{[4]}
, the
mixed fourth- and sixth-order terms
E^{[8]}_{m}

and the diagonal element of eighth order

E^{[8]}_{d}

E^{[8]}=E^{[8]}_{c}+E^{[8]}_{m}+E^{[8]}_{d}\,.

(36)

The terms will be further examined in the following.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the reduced Green function,

G^{\prime}=\left(\frac{1}{E_{S}-H_{S}}\right)^{\prime}\,,

(37)

where
E_{S}
is the Schrödinger–Coulomb
energy
E_{S}=-\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}m}{2n^{2}}

and
H_{S}=H^{[2]}_{C}
is the
Schrödinger–Coulomb Hamiltonian.
Then,

\displaystyle E^{[8]}_{c}=

\displaystyle\;\langle H^{[4]}\,G^{\prime}\,(H^{[4]}-\langle H^{[4]}\rangle)\,G^{\prime}\,H^{[4]}\rangle\,,

(38a)

\displaystyle E^{[8]}_{m}=

\displaystyle\;2\langle H^{[4]}\,G^{\prime}\,H^{[6]}\rangle\,,

(38b)

\displaystyle E^{[8]}_{d}=

\displaystyle\;\langle H^{[8]}\rangle=\langle K_{C}\rangle+\langle Q_{C}\rangle+\langle L_{C}\rangle\,.

(38c)

After lengthy algebra, we obtain the results,

\displaystyle\frac{\langle K_{C}\rangle_{nF_{5/2}}}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{2}{693n^{3}}+\frac{65}{1386n^{5}}-\frac{1549}{5544n^{7}}+\frac{35}{128n^{8}}\,,

(39a)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle Q_{C}\rangle_{nF_{5/2}}}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{4}{31185n^{3}}+\frac{131}{99792n^{5}}-\frac{23}{13860n^{7}}\,,

(39b)

\displaystyle\frac{\langle L_{C}\rangle_{nF_{5/2}}}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{281}{249480n^{3}}+\frac{5975}{399168n^{5}}-\frac{599}{13860n^{7}}\,,

(39c)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{[8]}_{d}(nF_{5/2})}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{1033}{249480n^{3}}+\frac{25219}{399168n^{5}}-\frac{8989}{27720n^{7}}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{35}{128n^{8}}\,,

(39d)

For absolutely clarity, we should emphasize the
all matrix elements are calculated
with nonrelativistic Schrödinger–Pauli
two-component reference-state wave functions
(Chap. 6 of Ref.
JeAd2022book
).
Summing up the results for the combined third-order
perturbation theory term, and the mixed term,
one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{E^{[8]}_{c}(nF_{5/2})}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{169721}{23950080n^{3}}-\frac{19}{1728n^{4}}+\frac{4560727}{41912640n^{5}}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{955}{3024n^{6}}-\frac{1052}{693n^{7}}+\frac{21}{16n^{8}}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{[8]}_{m}(nF_{5/2})}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{262729}{23950080n^{3}}+\frac{5}{576n^{4}}-\frac{3652871}{20956320n^{5}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{40}{189n^{6}}+\frac{28271}{18480n^{7}}-\frac{21}{16n^{8}}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{[8]}(nF_{5/2})}{(Z\alpha)^{8}m}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{1}{3888n^{3}}-\frac{1}{432n^{4}}-\frac{1}{432n^{5}}+\frac{5}{48n^{6}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{5}{16n^{7}}+\frac{35}{128n^{8}}\,.

(42)

The latter terms confirm Eq. (
35
).

IV
Conclusions

In this article, we have extended the
treatment of the Foldy–Wouthuysen
transformation to eighth order,
based on the scaling of the operators
outlined in Eq. (
3
).
The results are obtained
by a straightforward application of the
elimination of odd operators by repeated
unitary transformations of the
form outlined in Eq. (
11
).
The sixth-order terms
[Eqs. (
II.1
) and (
II.2
)]
are obtained in full agreement with the literature
(Refs.
ZaPa2010
;
HiLePaSo2013
;
PaYePa2016
;
HaZhKoKa2020
;
ZhMeQi2023
).
For the eighth-order terms, we give
results in Eqs. (
II.1
) and (
II.2
).
We have applied our general results
to the relativistic bound Coulomb problem in Sec.
III
.
An application to
nF_{5/2}
and
nF_{7/2}

states, which present large spin-orbit couplings,
confirms, analytically, that the
Dirac–Coulomb bound-state energy
can be obtained, within the Foldy–Wouthuysen
formalism, as a sum of combined third-order
perturbative effects generated by the
leading relativistic corrections [Eq. (
38a
)],
mixed fourth-order and sixth-order Hamiltonian terms [Eq. (
38b
)],
and diagonal elements of the eighth-order Hamiltonian [Eq. (
38c
)].
The latter terms are obtained as diagonal elements
of our eighth-order terms for the Coulomb field,
evaluated on Schrödinger–Pauli wave functions
(Chap. 6 of Ref.
JeAd2022book
).
As outlined in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013
,
the results are important in a wider context,
in view of the fact that the
Foldy–Wouthuysen Hamiltonian determines (part of)
the matching coefficients in the Hamiltonian
of Nonrelativistic Quantum Electrodynamics (NRQED).

However, the Hamiltonians derived
here and in Ref.
ZhMeQi2023
are
not equivalent for time-dependent
problems.
Let us consider a binding Coulomb field
added to an external plane-wave laser field
(in the length gauge), polarized along the
z

with

(50)

where

is the peak laser field during a
laser period, and
\omega_{L}
is the laser angular
frequency. The vector potential still vanishes,
so that the relation
\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}
is retained.
The spatially homogeneous, but time-dependent, laser field is

\vec{E}_{L}(t)=\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}\,E_{L}\,\cos(\omega_{L}t)
.
The total
\vec{E}
field (Coulomb
+
laser field) fulfills

e\vec{E}=\vec{\nabla}\frac{Z\alpha}{r}+e\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}E_{L}\,\cos(\omega_{L}\,t)

The commutator
[\,\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{E},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\partial_{t}\vec{E}\,]
vanishes,
but the term

H^{[8]}\sim\mathcal{D}\sim\frac{e}{48m^{5}}\,\{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{p})^{3},\vec{\sigma}\cdot\partial_{t}\vec{E}_{L}(t)\}\,,

(51)

from the
\mathcal{D}
term in our Eq. (
II.2
),
generates a contribution proportional to

\sin(\omega_{L}\,t)
, in view of the
time derivative of the laser field.
Its sinusoidal (as opposed to cosinusoidal)
time dependence cannot be compensated
by any term proportional to the laser field
itself,
\vec{E}_{L}(t)\propto\cos(\omega_{L}t)
, i.e., the sinusoidal term cannot be compensated
by any other term in
H^{[8]}
which is
free from time derivatives of the electric
field. Hence, the Hamiltonians derived
here and in Ref.
ZhMeQi2023
cannot
be completely equivalent for time-dependent problems.

The eighth-order Hamiltonians derived
here and in Ref.
ZhMeQi2023

be equivalent up to a unitary transformation,
in a somewhat distant analogy to the

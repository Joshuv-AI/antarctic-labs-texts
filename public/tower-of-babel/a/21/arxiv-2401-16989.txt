On a weighted anisotropic eigenvalue problem
Nunzia Gavitone, Rossano Sannipoli

Abstract
. In this paper we deal with a weighted eigenvalue problem for the anisotropic ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We study the main properties of the first eigenvalue and a reverse Hölder type inequality for the corresponding eigenfunctions. MSC 2020: 35B51, 35J62. Keywords : Anisotropic p p -Laplacian, Comparison results, Reverse Hölder inequality.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open, bounded and connected set and let
p,q
be such that
1<p
, and
1<q<p^{*}
, where
p^{*}=np/(n-p)
if
p<n
and
p^{*}=\infty
if
p\geq n
. In this paper we study the following variational problem

(1.1)

\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m|u|^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}},

where
H
is a sufficiently smooth norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a positive function. Obviously
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
depends also by
m
, but to simplify the notation we will omit its dependence.

The Euler-Lagrange equation associated with the minimization problem (
1.1
) is the following
weighted eigenvalue problem for the anisotropic
(p,q)-
Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary condition

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)=\lambda m(x)\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}|u|^{q-2}u&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\vskip 5.69046pt,\end{cases}

where
\|u\|_{q,m}=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)}
is the weighted Lebesgue norm of
u
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is the so-called anisotropic
p
-Laplacian operator defined as follows

(1.3)

\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)=\mathrm{div}(H(\nabla u)^{p-1}H_{\xi}(\nabla u)).

We stress that when
p=q
and
m(x)\equiv 1
, (
1.1
) is the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
of the anisotropic
p
-Laplacian and it has been studied by many authors (see for instance
[
8
]
,
[
19
]
and the references therein). In particular in
[
8
]
it is proved that
\lambda^{H}_{p}(\Omega)
is simple for any
p
, the corresponding eigenfunctions have a sign and that a suitable Faber-Krahn inequality holds.

When
H=\mathcal{E}
is the usual Euclidean norm,
\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)
is the well-known
p
-Laplace operator and the eigenvalue problem (
1.2
) reduces to the following

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}|u|^{q-2}u&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The spectrum of (
1.4
) and the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, when
p\neq q
and
m\equiv 1
, have been studied for instance in the case
p=2
in
[
10
]
, for any
p
in
[
25
,
31
,
32
,
24
]
and in
[
20
]
where the authors study also the weighted case.
It is known that
\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is not simple, in general, for any
1<q<p^{*}
. Indeed in
[
26
]
the authors prove the simplicity for any
1<q\leq p
, while for
p<q<p^{*}
,
\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(\Omega)
could not be necessary simple. This fact has been observed for instance in
[
10
]
, in the case
p=2
, and for any
p
in
[
30
]
and
[
27
]
, where the authors prove that the simplicity fails if
\Omega
is a sufficiently thin spherical shell.

In this paper we study the main properties of
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
and of the corresponding eigenfunctions. In particular our aim is to prove a reverse Hölder inequality for them.

In the Euclidean case if
p=q=2
and
m\equiv 1
, in
[
15
,
16
]
, Chiti proved the following inequality for the first eigenfunctions
v
corresponding to the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplace operator
\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(\Omega)\equiv\lambda(\Omega)

(1.5)

\|v\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C(r,s,n,\lambda(\Omega))\|v\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)},\quad 0<s<r,

and the equality case is achieved if and only if
\Omega
is a ball. In
[
3
]
, the authors prove (
1.5
) for the first eigenfunctions of the
p
-Laplacian. Moreover, in
[
1
]
, the authors extend the result to the weighted case, and the inequality reads as follows

(1.6)

\|v\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,m)}\leq C(p,r,s,n,\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(\Omega))\|v\|_{L^{s}(\Omega,m)},\quad 0<s<r.

In the general case
p\neq q
, in the Euclidean case, a Chiti type inequality is proved in the case
m\equiv 1
in
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
when
p=2
and for any
p
respectively. More precisely in
[
13
]
the authors prove the following inequality

(1.7)

\|v\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C(p,q,s,n,\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(\Omega))\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},\quad q<s.

The goal of this paper is to prove a Chiti type inequality in the spirit of (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) for the first eigenfunctions of the general weighted eigenvalue problem (
1.2
). In particular our main theorem is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded and connected set. Let
1<q\leq p
, and let
u
be an eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue (
1.1
). Then the following statements hold

i)

There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,r,\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega))
such that

(1.8)

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,m)}\leq C\,\,\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)},\quad q\leq r;

ii)

There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,r,\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega))
such that

(1.9)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,m)}\quad 1\leq r<\infty.

The equality cases hold if and only if
\Omega
is a Wulff shape.

We stress that this result gives, in particular, a Chiti type inequality for the eigenfunctions corresponding to the first weighted eigenvalue of the anisotropic
p
-Laplacian and extend (
1.7
) to the weighted case. The proof is based on symmetrization techniques and a comparison between the eigenfunctions corresponding to the first eigenvalue (
1.1
) and the first eigenfunctions of a suitable symmetrical eigenvalue problem.

The structure of the paper is the following.
In Section 2 we fix some notation, we recall some basic properties of the Finsler norms and we give a brief overview about convex symmetrization. In Section 3 we study the main properties of
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
and a Faber-Krahn type inequality. In the last Section we prove Theorem
1.1
by using symmetrization arguments.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

Throughout this article,
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, while
\cdot
is the standard Euclidean scalar product for
n\geq 2
. Moreover we denote by
|\Omega|
the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
B_{R}
the Euclidean ball centered at the origin with radius
R
and by
\omega_{n}
the measure of the unit ball.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, open set and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set. We recall now the definition of the perimeter of
\Omega
in
E
in the sense of De Giorgi, that is

P(\Omega;E)=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}\varphi\>dx:\;\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;||\varphi||_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

The perimeter of
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
P(\Omega)
and, if
P(\Omega)<\infty
, we say that
E
is a set of finite perimeter. Some references for results relative to the sets of finite perimeter are for example
[
29
]
. Moreover, if
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary, we have that

P(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial\Omega).

2.1.
The anisotropic norm

Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
,
n\geq 2
, be a
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
convex function which is
1
-homogeneous, i.e.

(2.1)

H(t\xi)=|t|H(\xi)\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R},

and such that

(2.2)

\gamma|\xi|\leq H(\xi)\leq\delta|\xi|,

for some positive constants
\gamma\leq\delta
.

These properties guarantee that
H
is a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed by (
2.2
) we have that
H(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
. It is homogeneous by (
2.1
) and the triangular inequality is a consequence of the convexity of the function
H
: if
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

\frac{H(x+y)}{2}=H\bigg(\frac{x}{2}+\frac{y}{2}\bigg)\leq\frac{H(x)}{2}+\frac{H(y)}{2}.

Because of (
2.1
), we can assume that the set

K=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:H(\xi)\leq 1\}

is such that
|K|=\omega_{n}
, where
\omega_{n}
is the measure of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We can define the support function of
K
as

(2.3)

H^{\circ}(x)=\sup_{\xi\in K}\left<x,\xi\right>,

where
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
H^{\circ}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
is a convex, homogeneous function in the sense of (
2.1
). Moreover
H
and
H^{\circ}
are polar to each other, in the sense that

H^{\circ}(x)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\left<x,\xi\right>}{H(\xi)}

and

H(x)=\sup_{\xi\neq 0}\frac{\left<x,\xi\right>}{H^{\circ}(\xi)}.

H
is the support function of the set

K^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:H^{\circ}(x)\leq 1\}.

The set
\mathcal{W}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:H^{\circ}(x)<1\}
is the so-called Wulff shape centered at the origin. We set
k_{n}=|\mathcal{W}|
. More generally we will denote by
\mathcal{W}_{R}(x_{0})
the Wulff shape centered in
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the set
R\mathcal{W}+x_{0}
, and
\mathcal{W}_{R}(0)=\mathcal{W}_{R}
.

The following properties hold for
H
and
H^{\circ}
:

(2.4)

H_{\xi}(\xi)\cdot\xi=H(\xi),\;\;\;\;H^{\circ}_{\xi}(\xi)\cdot\xi=H^{\circ}(\xi),

(2.5)

H(H^{\circ}_{\xi}(\xi))=H^{\circ}(H_{\xi}(\xi))=1\,\,\,\,\,\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

(2.6)

H^{\circ}(\xi)H_{\xi}(H^{\circ}_{\xi}(\xi))=H(\xi)H^{\circ}_{\xi}(H_{\xi}(\xi))=\xi\,\,\,\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and
E
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we can give a generalized definition of perimeter of
E
with respect to the anisotropic norm as follows (see for instance
[
6
]
)

P_{H}(E,\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega}H(\nu)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where
\partial^{*}E
is the reduced boundary of
E
(for the definition see
[
21
]
),
\nu
is its Euclidean outer normal and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is the
(n-1)-
dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Clearly, if
E
is open, bounded and Lipschitz, then the outer unit normal exists almost everywhere and

(2.7)

P_{H}(E,\mathbb{R}^{n}):=P_{H}(E)=\int_{\partial E}H(\nu)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

By (
2.2
) we have that

\gamma P(E)\leq P_{H}(E)\leq\delta P(E).

In
[
5
]
it is shown that if
u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)
, then for a.e.
t>0

(2.8)

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\{u>t\}}H(\nabla u)\,dx=P_{H}(\{u>t\},\Omega)=\int_{\partial^{*}\{u>t\}\cap\Omega}\frac{H(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Moreover an isoperimetric inequality for the anisotropic perimeter holds (for instance see
[
12
,
18
,
2
,
23
]
)

(2.9)

P_{H}(E)\geq nk^{\frac{1}{n}}_{n}|E|^{1-\frac{1}{n}}.

2.2.
Convex symmetrization

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded, connected set.
Let
f:\Omega\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
be a measurable function. The decreasing rearrangement
f^{*}
of
f
is defined as follows

f^{*}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu(t)<s\}\,\,\,\,\,\,s\in[0,|\Omega|],

where

\mu(t)=|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>t\}|,

is the distribution function of
f
. We recall that the Schwarz symmetrand of
f
is a radially spherically function defined as follows

f^{\sharp}(x)=f^{*}(\omega_{n}|x|^{n})\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball centered at the origin such that
|\Omega^{\sharp}|=|\Omega|
.
The convex symmetrization
f^{\star}
of
f
instead, is a function symmetric with respect to
H^{\circ}
defined as follows

f^{\star}(x)=f^{*}(k_{n}(H^{\circ}(x))^{n})\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in\Omega^{\star},

where
\Omega^{\star}
is a Wulff shape centered at the origin and such that
|\Omega^{*}|=|\Omega|
(see
[
2
]
).
We stress that both
f^{\star}
and
f^{\sharp}
are defined by means the decreasing rearrangement
f^{*}
but they have different symmetry.
In particular it is well known that the functions
f
,
f^{*}
,
f^{\sharp}
and
f^{\star}
are equimeasurable, i.e.

|\{f>t\}|=|\{f^{\sharp}>t\}|=|\{f^{*}>t\}|=|\{f^{\star}>t\}|\,\,\,\,\,t\geq 0.

As a consequence, if
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
p\geq 1
, then

(2.10)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|=\|f^{\sharp}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}=\|f^{*}\|_{L^{p}([0,|\Omega|])}=\|f^{\star}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\star})}.

Regarding the norm of the gradient, a generalized version of the well known Pólya-Szegö inequality holds and it states (see for instance
[
2
]
)

Theorem 2.1
.

(Pólya-Szegö principle)
If
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for
p\geq 1
, then we have that

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega^{\star}}H(\nabla u^{\star})^{p}\,dx.

where
\Omega^{\star}
is the Wulff Shape such that
|\Omega^{\star}|=|\Omega|
.

The equality case, proved in the euclidean case by
[
11
]
, was studied in
[
22
]
. We will state it for sake of completeness

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
u
be a non-negative function in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
1<p<+\infty
, such that

|\{|\nabla u^{\star}|=0\}\cap\{0<u^{\star}<\esssup u\}|=0.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}H(\nabla u^{\star})^{p}\,dx

if and only if
u=u^{\star}
a.e in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, up to translations.

Clearly Theorem
2.2
can be adapted in the case of a
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
function.

We conclude this section by recalling some known properties about rearrangements that we will use in the proof of the main theorem.
The following result is the well- known Hardy-Littlewood inequality (see
[
28
]
)

(2.11)

\int_{\Omega}|f(x)g(x)|\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds.

So, if we consider
g
as the characteristic function of the set
\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\}
, for some measurable function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
t\geq 0
, then we get

(2.12)

\int_{\{u>t\}}f(x)\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}f^{*}(s)\,ds,

where, again,
\mu(t)
is the distribution function of
u
.
Finally we recall the definition of dominated rearrangements (see for instance
[
4
]
and
[
17
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f,g\in L^{1}(\Omega)
be nonnegative functions. We say that
g
is dominated by
f
and we write
g\prec f
if the following two statements hold

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}g^{*}(t)\,dt\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}f^{*}(t)\,dt
;

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}g^{*}(t)\,dt=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}f^{*}(t)\,dt
.

In
[
4
]
the authors prove the following result

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f,g,h
be positive and such that
hf,hg\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Let
F
be a convex, nonnegative function such that
F(0)=0
. If
hg\prec hf
Then

\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}h^{*}F(g^{*})\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{|\Omega|}h^{*}F(f^{*})\,dt.

Moreover, if
F
is strictly convex the equality holds if and only if
f^{*}\equiv g^{*}
a.e. in
[0,|\Omega|]
.

3.
The (p,q)-anisotropic Laplacian

In this section we study the main properties of (
1.1
) and of the corresponding minimizers.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
be an open, bounded and connected set. Let
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function and
p,q
be such that
1<p<\infty
, and
1<q<p^{*}
, where
p^{*}=np/(n-p)
, if
p<n
, and
p^{*}=\infty
, if
p\geq n
. A function
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.2
) corresponding to
\lambda
if

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla v))^{p-1}H_{\xi}(\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\lambda\|v\|_{q,m}^{p-q}\int_{\Omega}m(x)\,|v|^{q-2}\,v\,\varphi\,dx,

for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
By standard argument of calculus of variations it is not difficult to prove the following result

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, be an open, bounded and connected set and let
p,q
and
m
be as above. Then
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, defined in (
1.1
), is strictly positive and it is actually a minimum. Moreover any minimizer is a weak solution to the problem (
1.2
), with
\lambda=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
and it has constant sign.

As regard the simplicity, we have

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, be an open, bounded and connected set and let
p
and
m
be as above and let
1<q\leq p
. Then
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is simple, that is there exists a unique corresponding eigenfunction up to multiplicative constants.

The proof of the previous result is contained in
[
27
]
, where the authors consider a more general class of quasilinear operators.
Finally we have the following

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded and connected set. Let
p
and
m
be as above and let
1<q\leq p
. Any nonnegative function
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, which is a weak solution to the problem (
1.2
), for some
\lambda>0
, is a first eigenfunction, that is
\lambda=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The proof follows by standard arguments and a general Picone inequality contained in
[
9
]
.

Let
v
be a non-negative weak solution to the problem (
1.2
) corresponding to
\lambda
. By the strong maximum principle we have that
v>0
in
\Omega.
Let
u
be the first positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
such that

(3.2)

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)}=\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)}.

Then

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}(H(\nabla u))^{p}\,dx=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\left(\int_{\Omega}m(x)\,u^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Being
v
a weak positive solution to (
1.2
) corresponding to
\lambda
, we can chose
\varphi=\displaystyle\frac{u^{q}}{v^{q-1}}
as test function in (
3.1
) obtaining

(3.4)

\int_{\Omega}\left((H(\nabla v)\right)^{p-1}H_{\xi}(\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{u^{q}}{v^{q-1}}\right)\,dx=\lambda\|m^{\frac{1}{q}}v\|_{q}^{p-q}\int_{\Omega}m(x)\,u^{q}\,dx\\
=\lambda\left(\int_{\Omega}m(x)\,u^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}},

where last equality follows by (
3.2
).
In the left-hand side, we can apply the general Picone inequality (see Proposition 2.9 in
[
9
]
) and we have

\int_{\Omega}\left(H(\nabla v)\right)^{q}\left(H(\nabla u)\right)^{p-q}\,dx\geq\lambda\left(\int_{\Omega}m(x)\,u^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

By the Hölder inequality, the normalization (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we get that

\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\geq\lambda
, that implies
u=v
.
∎

3.1.
The case
\Omega=\mathcal{W}_{R}

In this subsection we study the problem (
1.2
) when
\Omega
is a Wulff shape. In this case the eigenfunctions inherit some symmetry properties.
Let be
\Omega=\mathcal{W}_{R}
and let
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
be a positive function such that
m(x)=m^{\star}(x)
. Then problem (
1.2
) becomes

(3.5)

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)=\lambda m^{\star}(x)\|v\|_{q,m^{\star}}^{p-q}|v|^{q-2}v&\mbox{in}\ \mathcal{W}_{R}\\
v=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\mathcal{W}_{R}\vskip 5.69046pt.\end{cases}

The following result holds

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
1<q\leq p
. Let
v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
be a first positive eigenfunction to the problem (
3.5
). Then there exists a decreasing function
\rho(r)
,
r\in[0,R]
, such that
\rho\in C^{\infty}((0,R))\cap C^{1}([0,R])
,
\rho^{\prime}(0)=0
and
v(x)=\rho(H^{o}(x))
.

Proof.

By the simplicity we can assume that
\|v\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{W}_{R},m^{\star})}=1
.
Let
B_{R}
be the ball centered at the origin with radius
R>0
, and let us consider the weighted p-Laplace eigenvalue problem in
B_{R}

(3.6)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}z=\lambda\tilde{m}(|x|)\|z\|_{q,m^{\star}}^{p-q}|z|^{q-2}z&\mbox{in}\ B_{R}\\
z=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial B_{R},\end{cases}

where
\tilde{m}(r)=m^{*}(k_{n}r^{n})
,
0\leq r\leq R
.
Let
z
be the positive eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(B_{R})
to the problem (
3.6
), such that
\|z\|_{L^{q}(B_{R},\tilde{m})}=\|v\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{W}_{R},m^{\star})}=1
.
Then uniqueness guarantees that
z
is radially symmetric, which means that there exists a positive one dimensional function
\rho_{p}:r\in[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

such that
z(x)=\rho_{p}(|x|)
, and
\rho_{p}
solves the following problem

(3.7)

\begin{cases}-(p-1)|\rho^{\prime}_{p}|^{p-2}\rho^{\prime\prime}_{p}+\frac{n-1}{r}|\rho^{\prime}_{p}|^{p-1}=\lambda^{\mathcal{E}}_{p,q}(B_{R})\tilde{m}|\rho_{p}|^{q-2}\rho_{p},&r\in(0,R)\\
\rho^{\prime}_{p}(0)=\rho_{p}(R)=0.\end{cases}

In particular, integrating equation (
3.7
), it is possible to see that
\rho^{\prime}_{p}
is zero only when
r=0
and consequently that
\rho_{p}
is strictly decreasing in
[0,R]
.
Now we can come back to the anisotropy. Indeed if we consider
w=\rho_{p}(H^{\circ}(x))
then using properties (
2.4
)-(
2.6
) and the regularity of
H
, by construction, we obtain that
w(x)
is a solution to problem (
3.5
), which is positive and radial with respect to the anisotropic norm. The simplicity and Theorem
3.3
imply that
v=w
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We stress that the proof of the previous result shows that the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
coincides with the first eigenvalue of problem (
3.6
).

3.2.
A Faber-Krahn type inequality

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open, bounded and connected set and let
1<q\leq p
. Then

(3.8)

\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\geq\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega^{\star}),

where
\Omega^{\star}
is the Wulff shape such that
|\Omega^{\star}|=|\Omega|
. The equality case holds if and only if
\Omega=\Omega^{\star}
and
m=m^{\star}
a.e. in
\Omega
, up to translations, where
m^{\star}
is the convex symmetrization of
m
.

Proof.

We observe that
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega^{\star})
has the following variational characterization

(3.9)

\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega^{\star})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\star}),\\
w\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}H(\nabla w)^{p}\,dx}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}m^{\star}|w|^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}.

The Faber-Krahn inequality is a straightforward application of the Pólya-Szegö principle and the Hardy-Littlewood inequality. Indeed if
u
is a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, then

(3.10)

\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}m{u}^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\geq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}H(\nabla u^{\star})^{p}\,dx}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}m^{\star}(u^{\star})^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\geq\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega^{\star}).

Let us now consider the equality case. From (
3.10
), Pólya-Szegö principle and Hardy-Littlewood inequality we get

1\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}H(\nabla u^{\star})^{p}\,dx}=\frac{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}m^{\star}(u^{\star})^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}mu^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq 1.

It follows that

(3.11)

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{\star}}H(\nabla u^{\star})^{p}\,dx,

and

(3.12)

\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}mu^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}=\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}}m^{\star}(u^{\star})^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

The thesis follows from (
3.11
), Theorem
2.2
and (
3.12
).
∎

4.
A Chiti type inequality

In this section we prove a reverse Hölder inequality for the eigenfunctions corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
. We first prove the following proposition as in the spirit of the Talenti result contained in
[
34
]
(see also
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
33
]
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open, bounded and connected set,
1<q\leq p
, and let
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function. Let
u
be a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
.
Then we have that

(4.1)

(-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(s))^{p-1}\leq n^{-p}k_{n}^{-\frac{p}{n}}\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(u^{*})^{q-1}(r)\,dr,\qquad s\in[0,|\Omega|].

In particular the equality case holds if and only if
\Omega=\Omega^{\star}
and
m=m^{\star}
a.e. in
\Omega
, up to translations, where
m^{\star}
is the convex symmetrization of
m
.

Proof.

The function
u
is a weak solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)m\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}u^{q-1}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega\vskip 5.69046pt.\end{cases}

Let us integrate both sides of the equation in (
4
) on the super level sets of
u
. Then integrating by parts we get

-\int_{\{u=t\}}H(\nabla u)^{p-1}H_{\xi}(\nabla u)\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\,\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}\int_{\{u>t\}}mu^{q-1}\,dx.

Since on
\{u=t\}
we have that
\nu=-\nabla u/|\nabla u|
and using property (
2.4
), we have that the left hand side of the previous equation becomes

-\int_{\{u=t\}}H(\nabla u)^{p-1}H_{\xi}(\nabla u)\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{H(\nabla u)^{p}}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

By Hölder inequality and since
H
is 1-homogeneous, we get

\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{H(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\bigg(\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{H(\nabla u)^{p}}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

Since

\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{H(\nabla u)}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\{u>t\}}H(\nabla u)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=P_{H}(\{u>t\}),

and

-\mu^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

applying the isoperimetric inequality (
2.9
) we have

(-\mu^{\prime}(t))^{1-p}\int_{\{u=t\}}\frac{H(\nabla u)^{p}}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq n^{p}k_{n}^{\frac{p}{n}}\mu(t)^{p-\frac{p}{n}}

Since
\mu^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{{u^{*}}^{\prime}(\mu(t))}
we have

(-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(\mu(t)))^{p-1}\leq n^{-p}k_{n}^{-\frac{p}{n}}\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}\mu(t)^{\frac{p}{n}-p}\int_{\{u>t\}}m(x)u^{q-1}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Using (
2.12
) and calling
s=\mu(t)
we have

(-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(s))^{p-1}\leq n^{-p}k_{n}^{-\frac{p}{n}}\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|u\|_{q,m}^{p-q}\,s^{\frac{p}{n}-p}\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(u^{*})^{q-1}\,dr.

An application of the Hardy-Littlewood inequality gives the desired result.
∎

The main tool we use in order to prove Theorem
1.1
is a suitable comparison result between
u
and an eigenfunction
z
of a suitable eigenvalue problem.
More precisely, let
\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}
be the Wulff shape centered at the origin such that
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue to the following symmetric problem

(4.2)

\begin{cases}-\mathcal{L}_{p,q}(z)=\mu m^{\star}\|z\|_{q,m^{\star}}^{p-q}z^{q-1}&\mbox{in}\ \Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}\\
z=0&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}\vskip 5.69046pt,\end{cases}

We stress that the Faber-krahn inequality (
3.8
) implies that

(4.3)

|\Omega|\geq|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|,

and hence
m^{\star}
is well defined in
\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}
.

Moreover if
z
is a positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
, we observe that repeating the same argument as before, by Proposition
3.4
, for any
1<q\leq p
we have

(4.4)

(-{z^{*}}^{\prime}(s))^{p-1}=n^{-p}k_{n}^{-\frac{p}{n}}\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|z^{*}\|_{q,m^{*}}^{p-q}\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(z^{*})^{q-1}(r)\,dr.

The following proposition gives a comparison result between the eigenfunctions
u
and
z
when they are normalized with respect the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be an open, bounded and connected set,
1<q\leq p
and let
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function. Let
u
be a positive solution to the problem (
1.2
) corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
and let
z
be a positive eigenfunction to the problem (
4.2
) corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
such that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda})}

Then

u^{*}(s)\geq z^{*}(s),\qquad\forall s\in[0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|],

where
u^{*}
and
z^{*}
are respectively the decreasing rearrangements of
u
and
z
.
The equality case holds if and only if
\Omega=\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}
and
m=m^{\star}
a.e. in
\Omega
, up to translations, where
m^{\star}
is the convex symmetrization of
m
.

Proof.

First of all we stress that, if
|\Omega|=|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|
, then there is nothing to prove, since Faber-Krahn inequality implies that
u^{*}(s)=z^{*}(s)
.

Moreover we have
u^{*}(|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|)>z^{*}(|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|)=0
. Then, the following definition is well posed

s_{0}=\inf\{s\in[0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]\,:\;u^{*}(t)\geq z^{*}(t),\,\forall t\in[s,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]\}.

By definition
u^{*}(s_{0})=z^{*}(s_{0})
and we want to prove that
s_{0}=0
. We proceed by contradiction supposing that
s_{0}>0
. Then under this assumption,
u^{*}
and
z^{*}
coincide in
0
and
s_{0}
and we have

(4.5)

\begin{cases}u^{*}(s)<z^{*}(s)&s\in(0,s_{0})\\
u^{*}(s)\geq z^{*}(s)&s\in(s_{0},|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|).\end{cases}

By (
4.1
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have that

-{u^{*}}^{\prime}(t)\leq-{z^{*}}^{\prime}(t),\quad\text{ for every }t\in(0,s_{0}).

Integrating between
(0,s)
, with
s\in(0,s_{0})
, being
u^{*}(0)=z^{*}(0)
, we get

u^{*}(s)\geq z^{*}(s),\qquad\forall s\in(0,s_{0}),

which is in contradiction with the definition of
s_{0}
. Hence
s_{0}=0
and the proof is completed.
∎

As an immediately consequence of the previous result we get the following scale-invariant inequality for any
r>0

(4.6)

\frac{\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,m)}}{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\geq\frac{\|z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda},m^{\star})}}{\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\lambda}^{\star})}}.

When the functions
u
and
z
are normalized with respect the weighted
L^{q}
-norm, we get the following comparison result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded and connected set,
1<q\leq p
and let
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a positive function. Let
u
be a positive solution to the problem (
1.2
) corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
and let
z
be a positive eigenfunction to the problem (
4.2
) corresponding to
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)
such that

(4.7)

\int_{\Omega}m\,u^{q}\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}m^{\star}z^{q}\,dx.

Then we have

(4.8)

\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}\,(u^{*})^{r}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}\,(z^{*})^{r}\,dt,\quad s\in[0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|],\quad q\leq r

where
u^{*}
,
m^{*}
and
z^{*}
are respectively the decreasing rearrangements of
u
,
m
and
z
, and
m^{\star}
is the convex symmetrization of
m
.
The equality case holds if and only if
\Omega=\Omega^{\star}
,
z=u=u^{*}
and
m=m^{\star}
a.e.
\Omega
, up to translations.

Proof.

If
|\Omega|=|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|
the conclusion is trivial. Let be
|\Omega|>|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|
, since
u
and
z
verify (
4.7
), by (
4.6
) it holds that

u^{*}(0)=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|z\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda})}=z^{*}(0),

If
u^{*}(0)=z^{*}(0)
, then Proposition
4.2
and the normalization (
4.7
) imply that
u^{*}(s)=z^{*}(s)
for every
s\in[0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]
and than the claim follows trivially.

Let
u^{*}(0)<z^{*}(0)
. Since
u^{*}(|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|)>z^{*}(|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|)
, we can consider

s_{0}=\sup\{s\in(0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|):u^{*}(t)\leq z^{*}(t)\text{ for }t\in[0,s]\}.

Obviously
0<s_{0}<|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|
,
u^{*}(s_{0})=z^{*}(s_{0})
and
u^{*}\leq z^{*}
in
[0,s_{0}]
.
We want to show that
u^{*}>z^{*}
in
[s_{0},|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]
. Indeed, if we suppose by contradiction that there exists
s_{1}>s_{0}
such that
u^{*}(s_{1})=z^{*}(s_{1})
and
u^{*}(s)>z^{*}(s)
for
s\in(s_{0},s_{1})
we can construct the following function

w^{*}(s)=\begin{cases}z^{*}(s)&s\in[0,s_{0}]\cup[s_{1},|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]\\
u^{*}(s)&s\in[s_{0},s_{1}].\end{cases}

It is straightforward to check that

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla w)^{p}\,dx=n^{p}k^{p}_{n}\int_{\Omega}(-{w^{*}}^{\prime}(k_{n}H^{\circ}(x)^{n}))^{p}H^{\circ}(x)^{p(n-1)}\,dx.

Applying Coarea Formula and considering the change of variables
s=k_{n}t^{n}
, we get

\int_{\Omega}H(\nabla w)^{p}\,dx=n^{p}k_{n}^{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|}s^{p-\frac{p}{n}}(-{w^{*}}^{\prime}(t))^{p}\,dt.

Thanks to the normalization (
4.7
) and the definition of
w
, we have that

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)}=\|z\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda},m^{\star})}\leq\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda},m^{\star})},

then by (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) we have that

(4.9)

(-{w^{*}}^{\prime}(s))^{p-1}\leq n^{-p}k_{n}^{-\frac{p}{n}}\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|w^{*}\|_{q,m^{*}}^{p-q}s^{\frac{p}{n}-p}\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(r)({w^{*}})^{q-1}(r)\,dr.

Multiplying (
4.9
) by
-w^{\prime}
, rearranging the terms and integrating between
0
and
|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|
, we get

\begin{split}n^{p}k_{n}^{\frac{p}{n}}&\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|}s^{p-\frac{p}{n}}(-{w^{*}}^{\prime}(s))^{p}\,ds\leq\\
&\leq\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)\|w^{*}\|_{q,m^{*}}^{p-q}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|}(-{w^{*}}^{\prime}(s))\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(r)({w^{*}})^{q-1}(r)\,dr\,ds.\end{split}

An integration by parts allows us to conclude that

\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}H(\nabla w)^{p}\,dx}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}m^{\star}w^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}=\frac{\displaystyle n^{p}k_{n}^{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|}s^{p-\frac{p}{n}}(-w^{\prime}(s))^{p}\,ds}{\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|}m^{*}(s)({w^{*}})^{q}(s)\,ds\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega)=\lambda^{H}_{p,q}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}).

By the minimality and the simplicity of
\lambda^{H}_{p,q}
, and the definition of
w^{*}
, it must be
w^{*}(s)=z^{*}(s)
for every
s\in[0,|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|]
, but this is a contradiction since in
(s_{0},s_{1})
we have that
u^{*}(s)>z^{*}(s)
.

In this way we have proved that there exists a unique point
s_{0}
where
u^{*}
and
z^{*}
can cross each other, and such that

(4.10)

\begin{cases}u^{*}(s)\leq z^{*}(s)&s\in[0,s_{0}]\\
u^{*}(s)\geq z^{*}(s)&s\in[s_{0},|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|].\end{cases}

If we extend
z^{*}
to be zero in
[|\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}|,|\Omega|]
, by (
4.7
) and (
4.10
) then we have that for every
s\in[0,|\Omega|]

(4.11)

\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(t)({u^{*}(t)})^{q}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{s}m^{*}(t)({z^{*}(t)})^{q}\,dt.

Indeed (
4.10
) implies that the function

G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}{m^{*}}(t)((z^{*}))^{q}-(u^{*})^{q}\,dt,\qquad s\in[0,|\Omega|]

has a maximum in
s_{0}
and cannot be negative in any point. This proves (
4.11
). Finally inequality (
4.8
) follows easily by (
4.11
) by using Proposition
2.4
being
m^{\star}(u^{\star})^{q}\prec m^{\star}z^{q}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The proof of statement i) follows directly from (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), indeed we have

\bigg(\int_{\Omega}mu^{r}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\bigg(\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}m^{\star}z^{r}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}=\frac{\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}m^{\star}z^{r}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}}{\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda}}m^{\star}z^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}mu^{q}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore we have

\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega,m)}\leq C\,\,\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,m)},\quad q\leq r<+\infty;

with

C=\displaystyle\frac{\|z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda},m^{\star})}}{\|z\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\star}_{\lambda},m^{\star})}}.

The proof of statement ii) follows immediately by Proposition
4.2
with

C=\frac{\|z\|_{\infty}}{\|z\|_{L^{r}(\Omega^{\star},m^{\star})}}
and the theorem is completely proved.
∎

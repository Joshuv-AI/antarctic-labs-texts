Introduction

The new results in this paper are first, that an alternative ring with

Definitions 2.1
.

Let

(1)

The

(x,y,z)

(2)

(3)

It is well known and is a theorem of E. Artin,

This fact will be used throughout this paper.

(4)

The

Note that in an alternative ring the associator is skew symmetric in its
3

for

(5)

The
center
of

is denoted
C

C=\{c\in N\mid(c,R)=0\}.

In the remainder of this section
R
is an alternative ring

which is
not associative
.

N
denotes the nucleus of
R
and

its center.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x\in R\smallsetminus C,
and let

be a non-zero commutator.
Then

(1)

and

(2)

Proof.

(1) We have
v+vx=v({\bf 1}+x)=(x,y({\bf 1}+x)),
and
vx=(x,yx).

By hypothesis (i) of Theorem A, these commutators are non-zero.

(2) Let
\alpha:=(v+vx)^{2}=v^{2}+v^{2}x+vxv+(vx)^{2}.
Then
\alpha\in C.

We have
\alpha x=v^{2}x^{2}+(v^{2}+(vx)^{2})x+(vx)^{2}.
Letting

a:=v^{2},b:=v^{2}+(vx)^{2}-(v+vx)^{2}
and

we see that

and
ax^{2}+bx+c=0,
with
{a\neq 0\neq c.}

Suppose first that

Suppose next that

(2) that

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\in R\smallsetminus C,
such that
u_{i}^{2}\in C,

and
u_{\ell}u_{s}=-u_{s}u_{\ell},
for all distinct
\ell,s.

Let

V:=C+Cu_{1}+\dots+Cu_{n},

be the subspace of
R
spanned by
{\bf 1},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}.

(1)

If
p\in R
satisfies

pu_{\ell}+u_{\ell}p:=d_{\ell}\in C,\quad\text{for all }\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\},

then the element

(3.1)

\textstyle{m:=p-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}/2u_{i}^{2})u_{i},}

satisfies
mu_{\ell}+u_{\ell}m=0,
for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

(2)

If
R\neq V,

then there exists
p\in R\smallsetminus V
such that the element
m
of equation (
3.1
)
satisfies
mu_{\ell}+u_{\ell}m=0,
for all
\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\},
and
m^{2}\in C.

Proof.

(1)
We show that
mu_{1}+u_{1}m=0,
the proof for
u_{2},\dots,u_{n}

is identical.

mu_{1}+u_{1}m=pu_{1}+u_{1}p-d_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}(d_{i}/2u_{i}^{2})(u_{i}u_{1}+u_{1}u_{i})=0

(2)
We show that there exists
p\in R\smallsetminus V,
such that

p^{2}\in C,
and such that
p
satisfies
(*).

Let
x\in R\smallsetminus V.
By Lemma
3.1
,
x
satisfies a quadratic,
and hence a monic quadratic
equation
x^{2}-bx+c=0,
over
C.
Let
p:=x-b/2.
Then
p\notin V,

and
p^{2}\in C.
Let
u\in\{u_{1},\dots u_{n}\}.
Then both
p+u
and
p-u
satisfy
a monic quadratic equation over
C.
That is

\displaystyle(p+u)^{2}=c_{1}(p+u)+c_{2}

\displaystyle(p-u)^{2}=c_{3}(p-u)+c_{4}.

Adding we get

(c_{1}+c_{3})p+(c_{1}-c_{3})u+c_{5}=0,\quad\text{where }c_{5}=c_{2}+c_{4}-2p^{2}-2u^{2}\in C.

Now
c_{1}+c_{3}=0,
since

and then
c_{1}-c_{3}=0,
since

We thus get that

pu+up=c_{2}-p^{2}-u^{2}\in C.

Let now
m
be as in equation (
3.1
). For

set
{\alpha_{i}:=(d_{i}/2u_{i}^{2})\in C.}
Note that

m^{2}=p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}^{2}u_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}(pu_{i}+u_{i}p)\in C.

∎

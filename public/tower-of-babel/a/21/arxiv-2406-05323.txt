F F -purity and the F F -pure threshold as invariants of linkage
Vaibhav Pandey

Abstract
. The generic link of an unmixed radical ideal is a prime ideal. We show that the squarefreeness of the initial ideal and F F -purity are, however, not preserved along generic links. On the flip side, for several important cases in liaison theory, including generic height three Gorenstein ideals and the maximal minors of a generic matrix, we show that the squarefreeness of the initial ideal, F F -purity, and the F F -pure threshold are each preserved along generic links by identifying a property of unmixed ideals which propagates along generic links. We use this property to establish the F F -regularity of the generic links of such ideals. Finally, we study the F F -pure threshold of the generic residual intersections of a complete intersection ideal and answer a related question of Kim–Miller–Niu.

Introduction

Given a proper, unmixed ideal

Since the beginning of the modern study of linkage, or liaison theory, by Peskine and Szpiro

For example, height and the Cohen-Macaulay property are invariants of linkage, and the canonical module of the link is ‘dual’ to that of

2.
Background

2.1.
Linkage

While the notion of linkage holds more generally over Cohen–Macaulay rings (and most of the important results hold true over Gorenstein rings), for the purpose of this paper, and for simplicity of exposition, we restrict our attention to the situation where the ambient ring is polynomial (see the references in this subsection for the general statements).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring, and let
I
and
J
be proper
R
-ideals. We say that

and

are

(or

generated by a regular sequence such that

J=\mathfrak{a}:I\qquad\text{and}\qquad I=\mathfrak{a}:J,

and use the notation
I\sim_{\mathfrak{a}}J
. Furthermore we say that the link is

if we have
\height(I+J)\geq\height(I)+1
(where
\height(-)
denotes height).

It is clear that the ideal

is contained in

and

and

and

are unmixed. Further, the heights of the ideals

are equal. Moreover, when the link is geometric, the ideal

is the intersection of

and

The linked ideal is ‘dual’ to the given ideal in a sense made precise by the following foundational result of linkage theory:

.

[

2.2.
Frobenius splittings and the
F
-pure threshold

For a reduced Noetherian ring
R
of prime characteristic
p>0
, the (
e

R
is the map
F^{e}:R\to R
with
F^{e}(r)=r^{p^{e}}
. To avoid any confusion between the domain and codomain, we instead use the notation
F^{e}_{*}(R)
for the codomain and
F^{e}_{*}(r)
for its elements. So
F^{e}_{*}(R)
is the same ring as
R
with the
R
-module structure obtained from the Frobenius map:

r.F^{e}_{*}(s)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F^{e}_{*}(r^{p^{e}}s).

The ring
R
is
F
-finite
if
F^{e}_{*}(R)
is a finite
R
-module for some (equivalently, every)
e>0
. A finitely generated algebra
R
over a field
K
is
F
-finite if and only if
F_{*}(K)
is a finite field extension of
K
—a fairly mild condition. The
F
-finite ring

is
F
-pure
if the Frobenius endomorphism
F:R\to F_{*}(R)
with
F(r)=F_{*}(r^{p})
is pure, that is, for any
R
-module
M
, the map
F\otimes 1:R\otimes_{R}M\to F_{*}(R)\otimes_{R}M
is injective. Note that if
R
is an
F
-pure ring, the Frobenius map
F:R\to F^{e}_{*}(R)
splits as a map of
R
-modules so that the
R
-module
F_{*}(R)
admits a free
R
-summand. An
F
-finite ring

is
strongly
F
-regular
if it has a sufficiently large number of Frobenius splittings, made precise as follows: For every element
c\in R^{\circ}
, there exists an integer
q=p^{e}
such that the
R
-linear map
R\longrightarrow F^{e}_{*}(R)
sending
1
to
F^{e}_{*}(c)
splits as a map of
R
-modules. Clearly, a strongly
F
-regular ring is
F
-pure.

The rings under consideration in this paper are
\mathbb{N}
-graded; since the various competing notions of
F
-regularity (like weakly
F
-regular,
F
-regular, and strongly

-regular) coincide in the graded case due to
[

The following result will help us in showing that several ideals considered in this paper define

-pure rings.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
32

Let

{\mathfrak{a}}\subsetneq I

In particular, if the ring

Definition 2.9
.

Let

be a polynomial ring over a field of characteristic

and let

denote its homogeneous maximal ideal. For a homogeneous proper ideal

\nu_{I}(p^{e})=\max\{r\in\mathbb{N}\;|\;I^{r}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}\},

Moreover, if

It follows that the sequence of real numbers

F
-pure threshold
of

The notion of

These facts follow from the work of Hara and Yoshida

Lemma 2.10
.

The

-pure threshold of a homogeneous ideal

Let

Therefore

(1)

For any integer

, let

denote the
n
-th generic link of
I
in a polynomial extension
R[Y]
. The pair
(R[Y],L_{n}(I))
has
\mathbf{P}
.

(2)

If the ideal
I
is not generated by a regular sequence, then
(\underline{\alpha}):I
is a link of
I
in the polynomial ring
R
. The pair
(R,(\underline{\alpha}):I)

\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

We first prove item
(1)
. Assume that the ideal
I
has height
g>0
and proceed by induction on
n
. Since the
n
-th generic link of
I
is the generic link of the

-th generic link, i.e.,

\ L_{n}(I)=L_{1}(L_{n-1}(I)),

it suffices to prove the assertion for
n=1
. Note that the generic link is a homogeneous ideal for each
n>0
.

Let the elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
inside the ideal
I
be as given by
\mathbf{P}
. Extend it to find generators
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g},\alpha_{g+1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
of

and fix this generating set. We proceed to find homogeneous elements
\underline{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g}
inside the ideal
L_{1}(I)
and a term order
<_{1}
in the polynomial ring
R[Y]
, where
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(Y_{i,j})
is a

matrix of indeterminates, such that the pair
(R[Y],L_{1}(I))
has
\mathbf{P}
. Recall that

L_{1}(I)=Y[\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{g}\,\alpha_{g+1}\cdots\alpha_{r}]^{T}R[Y]:(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})R[Y].

Define the following variable order in
R[Y]
:

Y_{1,1}>Y_{2,2}>\cdots>Y_{g,g}>\text{ the remaining }Y_{i,j}>x_{k},

where the order
<
on the indeterminates
x_{k}
is the one given in the property
\mathbf{P}
and the order on “the remaining
Y_{i,j}
” is arbitrary. Given a monomial
u\in R[Y]
, we write
u_{x}\in R
for the image of
u
under the map of
R

R[Y]
to
R
sending each
Y_{i,j}
to
1
. Consider the following term order
<_{1}
on
R[Y]
: For monomials
u

, we define

u<_{1}v\iff\begin{cases}u/u_{x}\mbox{ is lexicographically smaller than }v/v_{x},\\
u/u_{x}=v/v_{x}\mbox{ and }u_{x}<v_{x}.\end{cases}

That is, the term order
<_{1}
extends the given term order
<
of

in a lexicographical manner ‘along the diagonal’.

Let
\beta_{1},\ldots\beta_{g}
denote the entries of the matrix
Y[\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{r}]^{T}
. Then,

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{1})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{1,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{1,2}\alpha_{2}+\ldots+Y_{1,r}\alpha_{r})=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{1,1}\alpha_{1})=Y_{1,1}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{1}),

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{2})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{2,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{2,2}\alpha_{2}+\ldots+Y_{2,r}\alpha_{r})=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{2,2}\alpha_{2})=Y_{2,2}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{2}),

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{g})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{g,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{g,2}\alpha_{2}+\ldots+Y_{g,r}\alpha_{r})=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{g,g}\alpha_{g})=Y_{g,g}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{g}).

Since the pair
(R,I)
has
\mathbf{P}
, we know that for the elements
\underline{\alpha}=\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
, each initial term
\ini_{<}(\alpha_{i})
is squarefree and each pair of initial terms
\ini_{<}(\alpha_{i}),\ini_{<}(\alpha_{j})
is mutually coprime for

. It immediately follows from the above display that the same assertions also hold true for the elements
\underline{\beta}=\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g}
of

.

We now prove item
(2)
. Since

is not generated by a regular sequence, the proper (homogeneous)

\underline{\alpha}:I
is a link of the unmixed ideal

. The pair
(R,(\underline{\alpha}):I)

\underline{\alpha}\subseteq(\underline{\alpha}):I.\qed

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the above lemma holds true without requiring the ideal
I
to be unmixed. Furthermore, it also holds true if the phrase “each initial term
\ini_{<}(\alpha_{i})
is squarefree” is deleted from the property
\mathbf{P}
or if the sequence of elements
\underline{\alpha}
is shorter. However, the applications that we have in mind require
\mathbf{P}
to be as stated.

In the remainder of this section, we show that if the pair
(R,I)
has
\mathbf{P}
, then important properties of the ideal
I
like the initial ideal being squarefree,
F
-purity, and the

-pure threshold remain invariant under linkage. In fact, we get that the
F
-pure threshold (or the log canonical threshold) of each generic link of
I
, and hence that of
I
itself, attains its maximal value. That is, the
F
-pure threshold of the
n
-th generic link is equal to the height—an invariant of linkage!

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
. Let
I
be an unmixed homogeneous
R
-ideal. Assume that the pair
(R,I)
has the property
\mathbf{P}
.

(1)

If the field
K
has positive characteristic, then

\fpt(I)=\height(I).

Furthermore,
R/I
is an
F
-pure ring.

(2)

If the field
K
has characteristic zero, then

\lct(I)=\height(I).

Furthermore,
R/I
has log canonical singularities.

(3)

The initial ideal of
I
is squarefree.

Proof.

Note that item
(2)
immediately follows from item
(1)
since
F
-pure rings in characteristic
p>0
are log canonical in characteristic zero (
[
13
, Theorem 3.9]
) and the log canonical threshold of an ideal is the limit of the
F
-pure thresholds of its characteristic
p>0
models. In view of this, assume that
K
has characteristic
p>0
. We begin by proving item
(1)
. Assume throughout that the ideal
I
has height
g>0
and let
\underline{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
be as stated in property
\mathbf{P}
.

Since
\underline{(\alpha)}\subseteq I
and, by Lemma
2.10
, the
F
-pure threshold of an ideal is bounded above by its height, we have

\fpt(\underline{(\alpha)})\leq\fpt(I)\leq g.

We claim that
\fpt(\underline{(\alpha)})=g
. This would give us

g=\fpt(\underline{(\alpha)})\leq\fpt(I)\leq g,

so that we have equality throughout.

We now show that
\fpt(\underline{(\alpha)})=g
. The initial term of

f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{g}\in\underline{(\alpha)}^{g}

is squarefree as the pair
(R,I)
has
\mathbf{P}
. For any
e>0
,
{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
is a monomial ideal. So, we have

(1)

f^{p^{e}-1}=(\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{g})^{p^{e}-1}\in((\underline{\alpha}^{[p^{e}]}):\underline{(\alpha)})\smallsetminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}.

Hence

(\underline{\alpha})^{g(p^{e}-1)}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}.\quad\text{However}\quad(\underline{\alpha})^{g(p^{e}-1)+1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}

by the pigeonhole principle. So we conclude that

\nu_{({\underline{\alpha}})}(p^{e})=\max\{r\;|\;(\underline{\alpha})^{r}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}\}=g(p^{e}-1)

for each
e>0
, so that

\fpt(\underline{\alpha})=g,

as required; we thus have

\fpt(I)=\height(I).

The
F
-purity of the ring
R/I
follows from Lemma
2.10
; alternatively, it also follows from Equation
1
in view of Lemma
2.8
.

We now prove item
(3)
. Since the
R/I
is
F
-pure, we get that
I
is a radical ideal. Consider the polynomial

f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{g}\in I^{g}.

Then the initial term

\ini_{<}(f)=\prod_{i=1}^{g}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{i})\in\ini_{<}((I^{g})\subseteq\ini_{<}((I^{(g)})

and is squarefree, as noted above. All the requirements of Theorem
2.11
are met; we conclude that
I
has a squarefree initial ideal.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field

. Fix an integer

and let

be an unmixed homogeneous

-ideal such that the pair

.

(1)

If the field

Furthermore, the link

defines an

(2)

If the field

Furthermore, the link

defines a log canonical singularity (in a polynomial extension of

).

The initial ideal of

is squarefree.

Proof.

The generic link

is an ideal of a polynomial extension

of

. The ideals

and

are linked in

so

. Crucially, since the pair

has the property

(and

), by Lemma

, the pair

.
∎

We now specialize to a link of

in the ambient polynomial ring for which the conclusions of Theorem

hold:

In
[
24
, Question 4.5 (a)]
, Kim–Miller–Niu ask if the equality of the log canonical thresholds of a variety

and that of its generic link implies the equality of the log canonical threshold of
X
and those of the higher generic links of
X
. In view of Corollary

, the examples listed below provide evidence in favor of an affirmative answer: For each pair
(R,I)
in the examples below, the log canonical threshold of the variety and that of its

-th generic link are equal for each

.

Remark 3.8
.

In each of the examples discussed in the next section, the elements
\underline{\alpha}
discussed in the property
\mathbf{P}
form a part of a minimal generating set of the ideal of interest. Hence the
n
-th (homogeneous) generic link that we get in each case is actually the
n
-th
minimal
(homogeneous) generic link. Minimal linkage is generally preferable over arbitrary linkage: The minimal link of an almost complete intersection ideal defines a Gorenstein ring. For other favorable properties of minimal links, see
[
19
, Remark 2.7]
.

4.
Consequences of the Property
\mathbf{P}

4.1.
F
-regularity of the generic link of generic height
3
Gorenstein ideals

Let
I
be a height
3
ideal in a polynomial ring
R
such that
R/I
is a Gorenstein ring. By the Buchsbaum–Eisenbud structure theorem
[
5
]
, the generators of
I
are given by the size
2n
pfaffians of a
(2n+1)\times(2n+1)
skew-symmetric matrix. If the entries of this matrix
X
are indeterminates, then
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
is said to be a ‘generic’ height
3
Gorenstein ideal in the ring
R=K[X]
. Note that any other such ideal can be obtained by specializing the entries of
X
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The pair
(K[X],\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X))
has
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

Let

\underline{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[1,2,\ldots,2n],[1,2,\ldots,n,n+2,\ldots,2n+1],[2,3,\ldots,2n+1]

denote the pfaffians of
X
with the given rows and columns and fix the lexicographical order in
K[X]
induced by the variable order

x_{1,2n+1}>x_{1,2n}>\cdots>x_{1,2}>x_{2,2n+1}>\cdots>x_{2,3}>x_{3,2n+1}>\cdots>x_{2n,2n+1}

which moves from right to left along any row of the matrix
X
and then vertically downwards. The initial terms

\displaystyle\ini_{<}([1,2,\ldots,2n])

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i,2n+1-i},

\displaystyle\ini_{<}([1,2,\ldots,n,n+2,\ldots,2n+1])

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i,2n+2-i},

\displaystyle\ini_{<}([2,3,\ldots,2n+1])

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}x_{i,2n+3-i}

are precisely as required for the property
\mathbf{P}
to hold. Since the ideal
\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
has height
3
, we are done.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a
(2n+1)\times(2n+1)
skew-symmetric matrix of indeterminates and
I=\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
be the ideal of submaximal pfaffians in
K[X]
; set
n\geq 0
. Then

\fpt(L_{n}(I))=3.

In particular,
\fpt(I)=3
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Proposition
4.1
in view of Corollary
3.6
.
∎

Proposition
4.1
has a remarkable consequence: The generic link of a generic height
3
Gorenstein ideal defines an
F
-regular ring.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x_{i,j})
be a
(2n+1)\times(2n+1)
skew-symmetric matrix of indeterminates,
K
a field, and
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K[X]
. Let
\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
denote the ideal generated by the size
2n
pfaffians of
X
.

(1)

If
K
is an
F
-finite field of characteristic
p>0
, the generic link of
R/\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
is
F
-regular.

(2)

If
K
has characteristic
0
, the generic link of
R/\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
has rational singularities.

Proof.

Assertion
(2)
follows from
(1)
since
F
-regular rings are
F
-rational and
F
-rational rings have rational singularities by
[
35
, Theorem 4.3]
. We therefore concentrate on the case where the characteristic of
K
is positive.

Let
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(Y_{i,j})
be a generic
3\times(2n+1)
matrix of indeterminates and
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R[Y]
. The proof will proceed by induction on
n
. Note that if
n=1
, the ideal
\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)=\operatorname{Pf}_{2}(X)
is the homogeneous maximal ideal of a polynomial ring in
3
variables. So the
F
-regularity of the generic link is a special case of
[
32
, Theorem 5.1]
. For the remainder of the proof, assume
n\geq 2
. Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{2n+1}
denote the pfaffians of
X
with

\Delta_{2n}=[1,3,\ldots,2n+1]\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{2n+1}=[2,3,\ldots,2n+1].

We have,

J=Y[\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{2n+1}]^{T}S:(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{2n+1})S,

and
S/J
is the generic link of
R/\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)
.

We first show that the ring
(S/J)_{x_{1,2}}
is
F
-regular. To prove the inductive step
n>1
, we show that the localization
(S/J)_{x_{1,2}}
is a faithfully flat extension of the generic link
S^{\prime}/J^{\prime}
of the size
2n-2
pfaffians of a generic skew-symmetric
(2n-1)\times(2n-1)
matrix
X^{\prime}
, with
S^{\prime}=K[X^{\prime}]
.

Let
\alpha_{i}
denote the
i
-th entry of the matrix
Y[\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{2n+1}]^{T}
for
1\leq i\leq 2n+1
; set
\alpha_{i}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha_{i}/x_{1,2}
for
i=1,2,3
and
\Delta_{j}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Delta_{j}/x_{1,2}
for
j=1,\ldots,2n+1
. Consider the linear change of coordinates,

x^{\prime}_{i-2,j-2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}x_{i,j}+\frac{x_{1,j}x_{2,i}-x_{1,i}x_{2,j}}{x_{1,2}}\qquad i,j=3,\ldots,2n+1

and set
X^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x^{\prime}_{i,j})
, a generic skew-symmetric
(2n-1)\times(2n-1)
matrix. By

[
2
, Lemma 1.3]
, there exists an invertible matrix
E
, with entries in
R_{x_{1,2}}
, such that

E^{T}XE=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}&\mbox{\Large 0}\\
\hline\cr\mbox{\Large 0}&\begin{matrix}0&x^{\prime}_{1,2}&\cdots&x^{\prime}_{1,2n-1}\\
-x^{\prime}_{1,2}&0&\cdots&x^{\prime}_{2,2n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-x^{\prime}_{1,2n-1}&-x^{\prime}_{2,2n-1}&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\end{array}\right).

A routine, albeit tedious, calculation gives

\alpha_{i}^{\prime}=Y_{i,1}^{\prime}\Delta_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+Y_{i,2n-1}^{\prime}\Delta_{2n-1}^{\prime}\qquad i=1,2,3,

where
\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{2n-1}^{\prime}
are the size
2n-2
pfaffians of
X^{\prime}
and the elements
Y^{\prime}_{i,j}
are algebraically independent over the field
K
; note that

\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(X)=\operatorname{Pf}_{2n}(E^{T}XE)=\operatorname{Pf}_{2n-2}(X^{\prime}).

Let
Y^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(Y^{\prime}_{i,j})
be a
3\times(2n-1)
(generic) matrix; set
R^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K[X^{\prime}]
,
S^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R[Y^{\prime}]
, and

J^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y^{\prime}[\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{2n-1}^{\prime}]S^{\prime}:(\Delta_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\Delta_{2n-1}^{\prime})S^{\prime}.

Then we get an isomorphism

(S/J)_{x_{1,2}}\cong S^{\prime}/J^{\prime}[x_{1,3},\ldots,x_{1,2n+1},x_{2,3},\ldots,x_{2,2n+1},Y_{i,j}|i=1,2,3;\;j=2n,2n+1],

as desired (where
\Delta_{i}\mapsto\Delta_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,2n-1
). Therefore, the ring
(S/J)_{x_{1.2}}
is
F
-regular by induction.

We now construct an
S/J
-linear splitting of the map

S/J\to F_{*}(S/J)\qquad\text{where}\qquad 1\mapsto F_{*}(x_{1,2})

in order to establish the
F
-regularity of the generic link
S/J
. After reindexing, let

\Delta_{1}=[1,\ldots,2n],\;\Delta_{2}=[1,\ldots,n,n+2,\ldots,2n+1],\;\text{and}\;\Delta_{3}=[2,\ldots,2n+1]

denote the pfaffians of
X
with the given rows and columns and let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
denote the first three entries of the matrix
Y[\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{2n+1}]^{T}
. By Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
4.1
, we get that the initial term of the polynomial

f\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{1}\beta_{2}\beta_{3}

is squarefree with respect to the term order
<_{1}
in Lemma
3.3
(constructed by extending the term order
<
in Proposition
4.1
). Furthermore, we have,

f^{p-1}\in J^{[p]}:J

by Lemma
2.8
. Note that
x_{1,2}
does not divide the initial term
\ini_{<_{1}}(f)
. In view of this, consider the polynomial

g\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y_{i,j},x_{k,l}\notin\ini_{<_{1}}(f)\\
(k,l)\neq(1,2)\end{subarray}}Y_{i,j}x_{k,l}f

and note that
g^{p-1}\in J^{[p]}:J
. The Frobenius trace map

\Tr(F_{*}(x_{1,2}^{p-2}g^{p-1})):F_{*}(S/J)\to S/J\quad\text{sends}\quad F_{*}(x_{1,2})\mapsto 1

to give the required splitting. The
F
-regularity of the generic link
S/J
now follows from
[
15
, Theorem 5.9(a)]
.
∎

, we did
not
need its generators to establish its
F
-regularity (or its
F
-pure threshold, or the squarefreeness of its initial ideal).

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
I
be a generic height
3
Gorenstein ideal of
R=K[\underline{x}]
over an
F
-finite field
K
of characteristic
p>0
and
L^{k}(I)
the
k
-th universal link of
I
in a transcendental extension
S
of
R
. Then
S/L^{k}(I)
is an
F
-rational ring for each
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

Due to
[
19
, Corollary 2.15]
, the even universal links
S/L^{2k}(I)
(respectively the odd universal links
S/L^{2k+1}(I)
) are essentially a deformation of
R/I
(respectively that of
S/L^{1}(I)
). That is, the universal links are obtained by a finite sequence of deformations and localizations at prime ideals beginning from
R/I
or
S/L^{1}(I)
, in the respective cases. Note that generic pfaffian rings are
F
-regular and the generic links of generic height
3
Gorenstein ideals are
F
-regular by Theorem
4.3
. Since the universal link is a localization of the corresponding generic link at a prime ideal, the assertion follows as
F
-regular rings are
F
-rational and
F
-rationality deforms (and is a local property) due to
[
15
, Theorem 4.2 (h)]
.
∎

4.2.
Minors of a generic matrix: A curious dichotomy

While the generic link of the maximal minors of a generic matrix is
F
-regular
[
32
, Theorem 5.6]
, in this section, we show that the generic link of the non-maximal (even submaximal) minors is usually not even
F
-injective; the point is that they do not posses the property
\mathbf{P}
. Let
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(x_{ij})
be an
m\times n
matrix of indeterminates for positive integers
m\leq n
and
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K[X]
. Let
I_{t}(X)
denote the
R
-ideal generated by the size
t
-minors of the matrix
X
.

Proposition 4.6
.

The pair
(K[X]),I_{m}(X))
has
\mathbf{P}
.

Proof.

Let

\underline{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[1,m],[2,m+1],\ldots,[n-m+1,n]

denote the size
m
minors of
X
with the given adjacent columns and fix the lexicographical order in
K[X]
induced by the variable order

x_{1,1}>x_{1,2}>\dots>x_{1,n}>x_{2,1}>\dots>x_{2,n}>\dots>x_{m,n}.

For
1\leq i\leq n-m+1
, by determinant expansion along the first row of
X
, we get

\ini_{<}\big([i,m+i-1]\big)=x_{1,i}x_{2,i+1}\dots x_{m,m+i-1},

which is the product of the variables in the main diagonal of the minor
[i,m+i-1]
. Since the determinantal ideal
I_{m}(X)
has height
n-m+1
, we are done.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In view of Corollary
3.6
, Proposition
4.6
simultaneously recovers the
F
-pure threshold (respectively log canonical threshold) of the ideal of maximal minors calculated in
[
29
, Theorem 1.2]
(respectively
[
11
, Theorem 5.6]
,
[
23
]
) and the log canonical threshold of the generic links of maximal minors calculated in
[

, Theorem 1]
by a simpler method and extends these calculations to the
n
-th generic link for each

.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
I
be an unmixed homogeneous
R
-ideal of height
g
for which each generator has degree
d
. Then the (top)
a
-invariant of the first universal link of
I
is
d(g-1)-n
.

Proof.

Let
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})\subset R
,
Y:=(Y_{i,j})
be a
g\times k
matrix, and
S:=R_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}(Y_{i,j})
. Let

L^{1}(I)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y[f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}]^{T}S:(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})S=(\underline{\alpha}):IS

denote the (first) universal link of
I
. Note that, since the ideal
I
is generated in a single degree, the factor ring
S/L^{1}(I)
is the localization of a graded ring at its homogeneous maximal ideal. Hence its top local cohomology module

H^{n-g}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}(S/L^{1}(I))

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
S/L^{1}(I)
-module in a natural manner. By
[
27
, Lemma 2.3]
, the graded canonical module of the universal link is

\omega_{S/L^{1}(I)}\cong IS/(\underline{\alpha})[-n+\sum_{i=1}^{g}\deg(\alpha_{i})].

Since the indeterminates
Y_{ij}
are units in
S
and so have degree zero, it follows that the graded canonical module
\omega_{S/L^{1}(I)}
is generated in degree

d-(-n+gd)=n-d(g-1).

Therefore the
a
-invariant

a(S/L^{1}(I))=d(g-1)-n

is as claimed.
∎

Example 4.9
.

Let
X
be a
3\times 7
matrix of indeterminates and
R=K[X]
. The pair
(R,I_{2}(X))
clearly does not have the property
\mathbf{P}
: The ideal
I_{2}(X)
has height
12
and does not contain any sequence of elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{12}
as required for
\mathbf{P}
to hold since that would require atleast
24
indeterminates.

By Lemma
4.8
, the
a
-invariant of the (first) universal link is

a(R(Y)/L^{1}(I_{2}(X)))=2(12-1)-21=1>0.

Since a graded
F
-injective ring has a non-positive
a
-invariant, it follows that the universal link
L^{1}(I_{2}(X))
does not define an
F
-injective ring. As the universal link is a localization of the generic link at a prime ideal and since
F
-injectivity is a local property
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
, it also follows that the generic link
L_{1}(I_{2}(X))
is
not

F
-injective. Therefore it is not
F
-pure. This observation stands in sharp contrast to the recent result
[
32
, Theorem 5.6]
which says that the generic link of the maximal minors of a generic matrix is
F
-regular.

Since a squarefree initial ideal implies
F
-injectivity (see
[
25
, Corollary 4.11(2)]
), it follows that the generic link
L_{1}(I_{2}(X))
does
not
have a squarefree initial ideal. However, the generic determinantal ideal of minors of any size has a squarefree initial ideal since the minors form a Gröbner basis with respect to a diagonal term order by
[
36
]
. We conclude that (generic) linkage does
not
preserve the squarefreeness of the initial ideal.

In addition, since generic determinantal rings (of minors of any size) are
F
-regular (
[
16
, §7]
) and hence have rational singularities (
[
35
, Theorem 4.3]
), we recover the fact that (generic) linkage does
not
preserve rational singularities (see
[
31
, Corollary 3.4]
). Finally, note that by
[
24
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 5.6]
),

\fpt(L_{1}(I_{2}(X)))=10.5<12=\height(I_{2}(X)).

We now show that the property
\mathbf{P}
does not hold for the (maximal) minors of a Hankel matrix, which are linear specializations of the maximal minors of a generic matrix:

Example 4.10
.

Let
H
be a
t\times n
Hankel matrix with
t\geq 2
and
n\geq 2t+2
. Let
I_{t}(H)
denote the ideal generated by the size
t
minors of
H
in the polynomial ring
R=K[H]
. By Lemma
4.8
, the
a
-invariant of the universal link

a(R(Y)/L^{1}(I_{t}(H)))=t(n-t)-(n+t-1)=(n-1)(t-1)-t^{2}\geq t^{2}-t-1>0.

By the same arguments as in Example
4.9
, we conclude that the generic link
L_{1}(I_{t}(H))
does not define an
F
-injective ring and does not have a squarefree initial ideal. However, the Hankel determinantal ring
R/I_{t}(H)
is
F
-rational if the field
K
has characteristic
p
with

, and therefore has rational singularities (see
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
). Furthermore, the ideal

has a squarefree initial ideal as its generators form a Gröbner basis with respect to the usual lexicographical ordering on the variables by
[
7
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Note that the case

gives us that the generic link of the rational normal curve is not
F
-injective and does not have a squarefree initial ideal for any
n\geq 6
.

5.
Generic residual intersections of a complete intersection inherit
\mathbf{P}

In this section, we show that the property
\mathbf{P}
is inherited by the generic residual intersections of an ideal generated by a regular sequence. This observation is quite surprising in light of the discussion so far since residual intersections do not preserve height unless they are links.

5.1.
Brief recall of residual intersections

The notion of residual intersections essentially goes back to Artin and Nagata
[
1
]
. Let
X
and
Y
be two irreducible closed subschemes of a Noetherian scheme
Z
with
\codim_{Z}X\leq\codim_{Z}Y=s
and
Y\nsubseteq X
, then
Y
is called a residual intersection of
X
if the number of equations needed to define
X\cup Y
as a subscheme of
Z
is the smallest possible, namely
s
. However, in order to include the case where
X
and
Y
are reducible with
X
possibly containing some component of
Y
, the following algebraic definition is more suited:

Definition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring and
I
an
R
-ideal. Given an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subsetneq I
generated by
s
elements, set
J=\mathfrak{a}:I
. If
\height(J)\geq s\geq\height(I)
, then
J
is an
s
-residual intersection
of
I
(or
R/J
is an
s
-residual intersection of
R/I
).

If furthermore
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}={\mathfrak{a}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in V(I)
with
\height{{\mathfrak{p}}}\leq s
, then
J
is called a
geometric

s
-residual intersection of
I
.

Residual intersections are natural generalizations of linkage: If
I
is unmixed of height
g
, then the
g
-residual intersections of
I
are precisely its links and the geometric
g
-residual intersections are precisely its the geometric links by Proposition
2.4
. Furthermore, as in the case of links, we may define the ‘most general’ residual intersections if the following technical requirement is met:

Definition 5.2
.

We say that an ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring satisfies the condition
G_{s}
if
\mu(I_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\leq\height({\mathfrak{p}})
for all prime ideals
{\mathfrak{p}}
containing
I
such that
\height({\mathfrak{p}})\leq s-1
(where
\mu(-)
denotes the minimal number of generators). We say that
I
satisfies
G_{\infty}
if it satisfies
G_{s}
for each
s
. It is clear that this is equivalent to the condition that
\mu(I_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\leq\height({\mathfrak{p}})
for each prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
containing
I
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring and
I
be an ideal of height
g>0
satisfying
G_{s+1}
, where
s\geq g
. Choose any generating set
\underline{f}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
of
I
and let
Y
be an
s\times n
matrix of indeterminates. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be the ideal generated by the entries of the matrix
Y[f_{1}\dots f_{n}]^{T}
. Then we set

\RI(s;I)=\RI(s;\underline{f})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathfrak{a}R[Y]:IR[Y]

and call this ideal a
generic
s
-residual intersection
of
I
.

Generic residual intersections are geometric residual intersections
[
21
, Theorem 3.3]
. As is the case with generic linkage, the generic residual intersections are essentially independent of the generating set for the ideal
[
22
, Lemma 2.2]
and specialize to any particuar
s
-residual intersection of the given ideal.

5.2.
F
-pure threshold of the generic residual intersections of a complete intersection

We layout the setup for this subsection: Let
\underline{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
be a homogeneous regular sequence, with each
\alpha_{i}
having the same degree, in the polynomial ring
K[\underline{x}]
such that the pair
(K[\underline{x}],\underline{\alpha})
has
\mathbf{P}
. Fix an integer
s\geq g
and let
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(Y_{i,j})
be a matrix of indeterminates of size
s\times g
. Let

\RI(s;\underline{\alpha})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y[\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{g}]^{T}R[Y]:(\underline{\alpha})R[Y]

denote the generic
s
-residual intersection of the ideal
(\underline{\alpha})
; set
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y[\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{g}]^{T}
. Since an ideal generated by a regular sequence satisfies
G_{\infty}
, the residual intersection
\RI(s;\underline{\alpha})
exists for each
s\geq g
.

We now show that the generic residual intersections of complete intersections inherit
\mathbf{P}
. We need the generators of the residual intersection to do this, which were calculated in
[
21
, Example 3.4]
(see also
[
4
]
).

Theorem 5.4
.

If the pair
(R,\underline{\alpha})
has
\mathbf{P}
, then
(R[Y],\RI(s;\underline{\alpha}))
also has
\mathbf{P}
for each
s\geq|\underline{\alpha}|
.

Proof.

Since
\underline{\alpha}=\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{g}
is a regular sequence, by the structure theorem for the residual intersections of a complete intersection ring (
[
21
, Example 3.4]
), we have

RI(s,\underline{\alpha})=(\text{entries of the matrix $M$})R[Y]+I_{g}(Y)R[Y]

is a homogeneous
R[Y]
-ideal of height
s
. We shall construct a term order
<_{1}
in
R[Y]
such that the pair
(R[Y],\underline{\beta})
satisfies
\mathbf{P}
with

\underline{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{M_{1,1},\ldots,M_{g-1,1}\}\bigcup\{[i,i+1,\ldots,g-i+1]\;|\;1\leq i\leq s-g+1\}.

That is,
\underline{\beta}
consists of the first
g-1
entries of the matrix
M
and all the
g
-minors of the matrix
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(Y_{i,j})
with adjacent rows. Clearly, the set
\underline{\beta}
has

(g-1)+(s-g+1)=s=\height(\RI(s;\underline{\alpha}))

elements, as required; hereafter, we call these elements
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{g-1},\beta_{g},\ldots,\beta_{s}
with the indexing according to the above display. We now construct the required term order.

Define the following variable order in
R[Y]
:

\displaystyle Y_{s,g}>Y_{s,g-1}>\cdots>Y_{s,1}>

\displaystyle Y_{g-1,g}>Y_{g-1,g-1}>\cdots>Y_{g-1,1}>

\displaystyle Y_{g-2,g-1}>Y_{g-2,g}>Y_{g-2,g-2}>\cdots>Y_{g-2,1}>

\displaystyle Y_{g-3,g-2}>Y_{g-3,g}>Y_{g-3,g-1}>\cdots>Y_{g-3,1}>

\displaystyle Y_{1,2}>Y_{1,g}>Y_{1,g-1}>\cdots>Y_{1,1}>x_{i.j}.

That is, we begin the variable order with the last row of the matrix
Y
, move from right to left, and then vertically up along the rows of
Y
for (the bottom)
s-g
rows, thereafter we follow the same order for the remaining rows except that the
(i,i+1)
entry appears first in the ordering of the
i
-th row. In addition, the ordering on the indeterminates
x_{i,j}
is the one given in the property
\mathbf{P}
for the pair
(R,\underline{\alpha})
. Given a monomial
u\in R[Y]
, we write
u_{x}\in R
for the image of
u
under the map of
R
-algebras from
R[Y]
to
R
sending each
Y_{i,j}
to
1
. Consider the following term order
<_{1}
on
R[Y]
: For monomials
u
and
v
in
R[Y]
, we define

u<_{1}v\iff\begin{cases}u/u_{x}\mbox{ is lexicographically smaller than }v/v_{x},\\
u/u_{x}=v/v_{x}\mbox{ and }u_{x}<v_{x}.\end{cases}

Note that the initial terms of the first
g-1
entries of the matrix
M
are

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{1})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{1,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{1,2}\alpha_{2}+\cdots+Y_{1,g}\alpha_{g})=Y_{1,2}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{2}),

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{2})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{2,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{2,2}\alpha_{2}+\cdots+Y_{2,g}\alpha_{g})=Y_{2,3}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{3}),

\displaystyle\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{g-1})

\displaystyle=\ini_{<_{1}}(Y_{g-1,1}\alpha_{1}+Y_{g-1,2}\alpha_{2}+\cdots+Y_{g-1,g}\alpha_{g})=Y_{g-1,g}\ini_{<}(\alpha_{g}).

Further, by determinant expansion along the last row of
Y
, the initial terms of the remaining
s-g+1
elements of
\underline{\beta}
,

\ini_{<_{1}}(\beta_{i})=\ini_{<_{1}}([i,i+1,\cdots,g+i-1])=Y_{i,1}Y_{i+1,2}\ldots Y_{g+i-1,g},\quad 1\leq i\leq s-g+1,

are just the products of the variables occurring in the main diagonal of the matrix
[i,i+1,\cdots,g+i-1]
. Since
(R,\underline{\alpha})
has
\mathbf{P}
by hypothesis, we get that for the elements
\underline{\beta}=\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{s}
of
R[Y]
, each initial term is squarefree and each pair of initial terms is mutually coprime, as required for
(R[Y],\RI(s;\underline{\alpha}))
to inherit
\mathbf{P}
.
∎

While the
F
-pure threshold of the generic link of an ideal is atleast as large as that of the ideal itself by
[
31
, Proposition 3.7]
, it is clearly bounded above by the height of the ideal due to Lemma
2.10
. On the other hand:

Corollary 5.5
.

If the pair
(R,\underline{\alpha})
has
\mathbf{P}
, then, for each
s\geq|\underline{\alpha}|
, we have

\lct({\RI(s;\underline{\alpha}))}=\fpt({\RI(s;\underline{\alpha}))}=s\geq|\underline{\alpha}|=\fpt{(\underline{\alpha})}=\lct{(\underline{\alpha})}.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
5.4
using Theorem
3.5
. The outer equality is
[
14
]
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Corollary
5.5
with

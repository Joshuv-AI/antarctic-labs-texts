Union vertex-distinguishing edge colorings
Teeradej Kittipassorn, Preechaya Sanyatit

Abstract
The union vertex-distinguishing chromatic index χ ∪ ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G) of a graph G G is the smallest natural number k k such that the edges of G G can be assigned nonempty subsets of [ k ] [k] so that the union of the subsets assigned to the edges incident to each vertex is different. We prove that χ ∪ ′ ​ ( G ) ∈ { ⌈ log 2 ⁡ ( n + 1 ) ⌉ , ⌈ log 2 ⁡ ( n + 1 ) ⌉ + 1 } \chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\in\left\{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+1\right\} for a graph G G on n n vertices without a component of order at most two. This answers a question posed by Bousquet, Dailly, Duchêne, Kheddouci and Parreau, and independently by Chartrand, Hallas and Zhang.

Introduction

An edge coloring of a graph is
vertex-distinguishing
if the set of colors of the edges incident to each vertex is different.
The pioneers of this concept include Harary and Plantholt
[
16
]
, Aigner, Triesch and Tuza
[
2
]
, and Burris and Schelp
[
11
]
.
It has been widely studied for arbitrary edge colorings
[
1
,
10
]

and also for proper edge colorings
[
7
,
4
,
6
]
.
The weaker notion where only adjacent vertices must be distinguished
has also been considered for both arbitrary edge colorings
[
15
,
18
]

and for proper edge colorings
[
24
,
17
,
5
]
.
Many variants have been investigated where the set of colors is replaced by the multiset of colors
[
9
,
12
,
20
]
, the sum
[
19
,
21
,
14
]
, the difference
[
23
]
or the product
[
22
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the generalization where the colors are nonempty subsets of
[k]=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
and we distinguish each vertex by the union of the colors of the edges incident to it.
Given a graph
G
on
n
vertices and a natural number
k
, an edge coloring
c:E(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
is
union vertex-distinguishing
if its
union vertex coloring

c_{\cup}:V(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
given by

c_{\cup}(v)=\bigcup_{uv\in E(G)}c(uv),

is injective, i.e.
c_{\cup}(u)\not=c_{\cup}(v)
for all distinct vertices
u
and
v
.
The
union vertex-distinguishing chromatic index

\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)
of
G
is the smallest natural number
k
such that
G
admits a union vertex-distinguishing edge coloring.
This concept was introduced by Bousquet, Dailly, Duchêne, Kheddouci and Parreau
[
8
]
, and independently by Chartrand, Hallas and Zhang
[
13
]
under the name
strong royal coloring
.

We shall only consider graphs without a component of order at most two otherwise a union vertex-distinguishing edge coloring does not exist.
Observe that
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\geq\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
since, in
c_{\cup}:V(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
, there must be at least as many colors as vertices, i.e.
2^{k}-1\geq n
.
On the other hand, Bousquet, Dailly, Duchêne, Kheddouci and Parreau
[
8
]
proved that there are only three possible values for

\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\in\left\{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+1,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+2\right\}.

Paths, complete binary trees, cycles (except
C_{3},C_{7}
) and hypercubes satisfy
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
,
while complete graphs (except
K_{2^{k}}
for all
k
) and graphs with large minimum degree satisfy
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+1
(see
[
8
,
13
,
3
]
).
However, no graphs
G
with
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+2
have been found.
Bousquet, Dailly, Duchêne, Kheddouci and Parreau
[
8
]
asked whether they exist, while
Chartrand, Hallas and Zhang
[
13
]
conjectured that they do not exist.
We prove this conjecture.

Theorem 1
.

\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\in\left\{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+1\right\}
for any graph
G
on
n
vertices without a component of order at most two.

To prove Theorem
1
, it is enough to prove the following result.
This reduction was made by Bousquet, Dailly, Duchêne, Kheddouci and Parreau
[
8
]
.
A
1
-star
is a graph obtained from a star on at least three vertices by subdividing each edge at most once.

Theorem 2
.

\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(F)=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
for any forest of
1
-stars
F
on
n
vertices.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, we shall prove Theorem
2
and deduce Theorem
1
from it.
We then conclude with some remarks and open problems.

2
Proofs of the main results

First, we show how to deduce Theorem
1
from Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices without a component of order at most two.
We claim that
G
has a spanning forest of
1
-stars
F
.
Indeed, consider a minimal spanning forest of
G
without a tree of order at most two, and so the deletion of any edge creates a tree of order at most two.
We shall show that any tree
T
in the forest is a
1
-star.
If there is a vertex
v
in
T
of degree at least three, then
T
is a
1
-star since, for each neighbor
u
of
v
, the component of
u
in
T-uv
has order at most two.
So we may assume that all vertices in
T
have degree at most two, i.e.
T
is a path.
Then
\lvert V(T)\rvert\leq 5
otherwise the deletion of the middle edge does not create a tree of order at most two.
Therefore,
T
is a
1
-star.

Let
k=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
.
By Theorem
2
, there exists an edge coloring
c:E(F)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
whose union vertex coloring
c_{\cup}
is injective.
We extend
c
to
c^{\prime}:E(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}([k+1])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
by

c^{\prime}(e)=\begin{cases}c(e)&\text{for }e\in E(F),\\
\{k+1\}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then its union vertex coloring
c^{\prime}_{\cup}
is also injective since
c^{\prime}_{\cup}(v)\cap[k]=c_{\cup}(v)
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Therefore,
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\leq k+1
.
∎

To prove Theorem
2
, the key idea is to partition
\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
into collections of arbitrary sizes with appropriate properties.
We would like each collection to be the image of the vertices of each
1
-star under the union vertex coloring.
We choose the properties of a collection of size
m
in such a way that, for any
1
-star of order
m
, there exists an edge coloring whose union vertex coloring maps the vertices of the
1
-star onto the collection.

A collection of distinct sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}
is an
m
-star
if

•

for
m\geq 4
,
A_{1}=A_{2}\cup A_{4}
and
A_{2i+1}\subset A_{2i}\subset A_{1}
for each
1\leq i\leq\frac{m}{2}
,

•

for
m=3
,
A_{1}=A_{2}\cup A_{3}
,

•

for
m=2
,
A_{2}\subset A_{1}
,

•

for
m=1
, no condition.

We also allow
m
to be
1,2
as we shall prove the existence of such a partition by induction.
A collection of sets is an
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
-forest
if it can be partitioned into an
m_{1}
-star, an
m_{2}
-star,
\dots
, and an
m_{r}
-star.

Theorem 3
.

If
m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}=2^{k}-1
then

\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
is an
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
-forest.

Now, we show how to deduce Theorem
2
from Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
F
be a forest of
1
-stars of orders
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r}\geq 3
with
n=m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}
,
and let
k=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
.
Since
m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}\leq 2^{k}-1
, by Theorem
3
,
the collection
\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
can be partitioned into an
m_{1}
-star, an
m_{2}
-star,
\dots
, an
m_{r}
-star and a
(2^{k}-1-n)
-star.
To prove that
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(F)\leq k
, it is enough to find an edge coloring of
F
whose union vertex coloring maps the
1
-star of order
m_{i}
onto the
m_{i}
-star for each
i
.

It remains to show that, given an
m
-star
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
with
m\geq 3
, the edges of any
1
-star of order
m
can be assigned nonempty subsets of
[k]
so that the unions of the subsets assigned to the edges incident to each vertex, are
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}
.
Consider a
1
-star of order
m
obtained from a star with vertices
v,v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m-l-1}
where
v
is the center, by subdividing the edges
vv_{1},vv_{2},\dots,vv_{l}
with vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{l}
respectively.
To obtain
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}
as the unions at the vertices

v,u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2},\dots,u_{l},v_{l},v_{l+1},v_{l+2},\dots,v_{m-l-1}

respectively,
we assign the subsets
A_{2},A_{3},\dots,A_{m}
to the edges

vu_{1},u_{1}v_{1},vu_{2},u_{2}v_{2},\dots,vu_{l},u_{l}v_{l},vv_{l+1},vv_{l+2},\dots,vv_{m-l-1}

respectively. Indeed, the union vertex coloring
c_{\cup}
satisfies

\displaystyle c_{\cup}(v)

\displaystyle=A_{2}\cup A_{4}\cup\dots\cup A_{2l}\cup A_{2l+2}\cup A_{2l+3}\cup\dots\cup A_{m}=A_{1},

\displaystyle c_{\cup}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=A_{2i}\cup A_{2i+1}=A_{2i}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq l,

\displaystyle c_{\cup}(v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}A_{2i+1}&\text{for }1\leq i\leq l,\\
A_{i+l+1}&\text{for }l+1\leq i\leq m-l-1.\end{cases}

by the properties of the
m
-star.
∎

To prove Theorem
3
, we shall construct new stars from existing ones. For instance, we show that if we double an
m
-star by adding a new element to every set then the new collection can be partitioned into an
i
-star and a
(2m-i)
-star for any odd number
i
.
For a collection
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{P}([k-1])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
, we write
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\{A\cup\{k\}:A\in\mathcal{A}\}\subset\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{P}([k-1])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
is an
m
-star then

(i)

\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is an
(i,2m-i)
-forest for each odd number
1\leq i\leq m
,

(ii)

\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cup\{\{k\}\}
is a
(2m+1)
-star, and

(iii)

\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a
2m
-star.

Proof.

(i)
Suppose first that
m\geq 6
is even.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}\}
be such that
A_{1}=A_{2}\cup A_{4}
and
A_{2j+1}\subset A_{2j}\subset A_{1}
for each
1\leq j\leq\frac{m}{2}
.
Then the first
i
sets form an
i
-star if
i\not=3
, and the remaining
2m-i
sets in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
can be ordered to form a
(2m-i)
-star as follows:

A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A^{\prime}_{5},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m-2},A^{\prime}_{m-1},A_{i+1},A_{i+2},\dots,A_{m-2},A_{m-1},A^{\prime}_{m},A_{m}

where
A^{\prime}_{i}=A_{i}\cup\{k\}
.

For
i=3
, the sets
A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A_{2}
form a
3
-star and

A^{\prime}_{1},A_{1},A_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A^{\prime}_{5},A^{\prime}_{6},A^{\prime}_{7},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m-2},A^{\prime}_{m-1},A_{4},A_{5},\dots,A_{m-2},A_{m-1},A^{\prime}_{m},A_{m}

form a
(2m-3)
-star.

Suppose now that
m\geq 5
is odd. Take the above partition for
\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert=m+1
and delete the final two sets
A^{\prime}_{m+1},A_{m+1}
from the second star.

For the remaining case
m\leq 4
, we partition
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
as follows:

•

for
m=4
and
i=1
,
A_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A_{4},A_{2},A_{3}
,

•

for
m=4
and
i=3
,
A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A_{2}
and
A^{\prime}_{1},A_{1},A_{3},A^{\prime}_{4},A_{4}
,

•

for
m=3
and
i=1
,
A_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A_{3}
,

•

for
m=3
and
i=3
,
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
and
A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A^{\prime}_{3}
,

•

for
m=2
and
i=1
,
A_{2}
and
A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},A_{1}
,

•

for
m=1
and
i=1
,
A_{1}
and
A^{\prime}_{1}
.

(ii)
Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{m}\}
be an
m
-star for
m\geq 2
.
Then the sets in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cup\{\{k\}\}
can be ordered to form a
(2m+1)
-star as follows:

A^{\prime}_{1},A_{1},A_{m},A^{\prime}_{2},A_{2},A^{\prime}_{3},A_{3},\dots,A^{\prime}_{m-1},A_{m-1},A^{\prime}_{m},\{k\}.

For
m=1
, the sets
A^{\prime}_{1},A_{1},\{k\}
form a
3
-star.

(iii)
Take the ordering in
(ii)
and delete the final set
\{k\}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We proceed by induction on
k
.
The result is trivial for
k=1
.
Suppose that
m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}=2^{k}-1
.
Since there are an odd number of odd
m_{i}
’s, without loss of generality, we may assume that
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{2s+1}
are odd and
m_{2s+2},m_{2s+3},\dots,m_{r}
are even.
Rewrite

\displaystyle 2^{k-1}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}}{2}+\frac{m_{3}+m_{4}}{2}+\dots+\frac{m_{2s+1}-1}{2}+\frac{m_{2s+2}}{2}+\frac{m_{2s+3}}{2}+\dots+\frac{m_{r}}{2}

\displaystyle=m^{\prime}_{1}+m^{\prime}_{2}+\dots+m^{\prime}_{s+1}+m^{\prime}_{s+2}+m^{\prime}_{s+3}+\dots+m^{\prime}_{r-s}

where the summands in the second line are defined to be the
m^{\prime}_{i}
’s in the third line.
By the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{P}([k-1])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
is an
(m^{\prime}_{1},m^{\prime}_{2},\dots,m^{\prime}_{r-s})
-forest, i.e. there is a partition
\mathcal{P}([k-1])\setminus\{\varnothing\}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{A}_{r-s}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is an
m^{\prime}_{i}
-star for each
i
.
By Lemma
4
, we have

(i)

\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{i}
is an
(m_{2i-1},m_{2i})
-forest for each
1\leq i\leq s
,

(ii)

\mathcal{A}_{s+1}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{s+1}\cup\{\{k\}\}
is an
m_{2s+1}
-star, and

(iii)

\mathcal{A}_{i}\cup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{i}
is an
m_{i+s}
-star for each
s+2\leq i\leq r-s
.

Since the above collections form a partition of
\mathcal{P}([k])\setminus\{\varnothing\}
, it is an
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
-forest.
∎

3
Concluding remarks

What if we are allowed to color the edges with the empty set as well?
It turns out our argument can be adapted to show that every graph can be optimally colored, i.e.
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)=\left\lceil\log_{2}n\right\rceil
for any graph
G
on
n
vertices without a component of order at most two.
Indeed, in the proof of Theorem
1
, we color the edges outside the spanning forest of
1
-stars with
\varnothing
instead of
\{k+1\}
, and Theorem
3
is modified to state that if
m_{1}+m_{2}+\dots+m_{r}=2^{k}
then

\mathcal{P}([k])
is an
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r})
-forest.

We now know that
\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(G)\in\left\{\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil,\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil+1\right\}
.
It would be interesting to characterize the graphs that can be optimally colored.
This is open even for trees (see
[
8
,
13
]
).
We think that forests can be optimally colored.

Conjecture 5
.

\chi^{\prime}_{\cup}(F)=\left\lceil\log_{2}\left(n+1\right)\right\rceil
for any forest
F
on
n
vertices.

Another direction forward could be to study natural variants including the case where the edge coloring must be proper and the case where only adjacent vertices must be distinguished.

Giovanna Andreucci, Matteo Focardi, Emanuele Spadaro

Abstract
. We establish a quasi-monotonicity formula for an intrinsic frequency function related to solutions to thin obstacle problems with zero obstacle driven by quadratic energies with Sobolev W 1 , p W^{1,p} coefficients, with p p bigger than the space dimension. From this we deduce several regularity and structural properties of the corresponding free boundaries at those distinguished points with finite order of contact with the obstacle. In particular, we prove the rectifiability and the local finiteness of the Minkowski content of the whole free boundary in the case of Lipschitz coefficients.



1.
Introduction

In this article we consider a class of
lower dimensional obstacle problems with variable coefficients which has been extensively considered in the literature.
In order to state the results, we introduce the following notation:
for any subset
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we set

E^{+}:=E\cap\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\,x_{n+1}>0\big\}E^{\prime}:=E\cap\big\{x_{n+1}=0\big\}.

For any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we will write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover,
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
denotes the open ball centered
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with radius
r>0
, and
\overline{B_{r}}(x)
its closure
(we omit to write the point
x
if the origin).

We consider the problem of minimizing a variable coefficient quadratic (
Dirichlet
) energy with an unilateral constraint:

\min_{v\in\mathscr{A}}\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle{\rm d}x,

(1.1)

where the class of competing functions is given by

\mathscr{A}:=\Big\{v\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+}):\,v=g\;\textup{in }\,(\partial B_{1})^{+}v\geq 0\;\textup{in }\,B_{1}^{\prime}\Big\},

with
g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}((\partial B_{1})^{+})
such that
\mathcal{A}
is not empty (the boundary conditions are meant in the sense of traces).
We assume the following hypotheses:

(H1)

\mathbb{A}\in W^{1,p}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)})
,
p\in(n+1,\infty]
(in particular, by Morrey’s embedding, we have
\mathbb{A}\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)})
,

\alpha:=1-\frac{n+1}{p}
);

(H2)

\mathbb{A}(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{i,j=1}^{n+1}
is a symmetric, bounded and coercive matrix,
i.e. for every
x\in B_{1}^{+}
,
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

a_{ij}(x)=a_{ji}(x)
, and

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\,,

for some
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
,

By a well-known result due to Ural’tseva
[
38
]
, the minimizers of (
1.1
) are
C^{1,\beta}
regular for some
\beta>0
and satisfy an elliptic partial differential equation in the half ball
B_{1}^{+}
, whereas on the flat part of the boundary
B_{1}^{\prime}
the unilateral constraint
u\geq 0
leads to a free boundary problem. The Euler–Lagrange equation of (
1.1
) is

\begin{cases}\textup{div}\big(\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u(x)\big)=0&\text{in }\;B_{1}^{+},\\
u\geq 0,\quad\mathbb{A}\nabla u\cdot e_{n+1}\leq 0,\quad u(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\cdot e_{n+1})=0&\text{in }\;B_{1}^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The condition
u(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\cdot e_{n+1})=0
in
B_{1}^{\prime}
is the so called
Signorini complementary
boundary condition.
The behaviour of
u
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
is not prescribed and is characterized by the so called
free boundary

\Gamma(u)
,
which is the relative boundary in
B_{1}^{\prime}
of the contact set
{B_{1}^{\prime}\cap}\{u=0\}
where the solution saturates the constraint.

This problem has been widely studied in the last decades and it has become
a very active field of research after the seminal papers
by Athanasopoulos and Caffarelli
[
4
]
and Athanasopoulos, Caffarelli and Salsa
[
5
]
.
The key idea introduced in

]
is the use of
Almgren’s frequency function

in the study of both the regularity of the solution
u
and the properties of the free boundary

\Gamma(u)
.
This has been the turning point for a long series of results for the constant coefficient case,
leading to a detailed analysis of the free boundary (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
7
,
8

12
,
13

The regularity of the solutions of the variable coefficients case has been

[

[

in the case of smooth coefficients, whereas the problem (

The optimal regularity of the solutions and the regularity of a subset of the free boundary (points with selected orders of contact)

In this paper we continue the analysis for quadratic energies with matrix field with Sobolev regularity. In particular, we address the question of the global structure of the free boundary.
In details, we need to consider only the points with finite order of contact: to this aim we write

\Gamma(u):=\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}\cup\Gamma^{\infty},

with

For

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a solution to (

) under the hypotheses (H1) and (H2).
The subset of points of the free boundary with finite order of contact

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u)\setminus\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}M_{i}\right)=0.

Furthermore, there exists a set
\Sigma(u)\subset\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u)

with Hausdorff dimension at most

such that for every

N_{u}(x,0^{+})\in\{2m,2m-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2m+1\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\,.

In the statement above,

In addition, if

, completely analogous to the case of the classical scalar Signorini problem for the Dirichlet energy as shown in
[

14
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a solution to (

Furthermore, there exists a set

N_{u}(x,0^{+})\in\{2m,2m-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2m+1\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\,.

1.1.
New insights and main difficulties

The main ideas for this work stem from
[
13
]
. Starting from the groundbreaking papers by Naber and Valtorta
[
31
,
32
]
, it is well-known that a monotone quantity of the type of the energy ratio for harmonic maps can be used to describe the structure of singularities: indeed, if the monotone quantity is able to detect homogeneous blowups at singular points and satisfies a suitable rigidigy property, then general covering and rectifiability arguments lead to the estimate of the measure (actually the Minkowski content) and the rectifiability of the singular set.

This principle has been exploited in
[
13
]
in the case of thin and fractional obstacle problems with constant coefficients, using suitable variants of Almgren’s frequency functions, which revelaed itself to be a key tools for this class of problems since
[
5
]
.

The main difficulties here are due to extension of such approach to the case of variable coefficients. Indeed, the monotonicity of the frequency is closely related to the linearity of the equations and is valid only for harmonic functions, while in the general case one should find a suitable linear approximation of the local geometry prescribed by the matrix
\mathbb{A}
.
This is clearly an issue for general nonlinear problems and understanding this question for low regularity matrix fields
\mathbb{A}
is a first step towards such program.

We circumvent this difficulty by introducing an intrinsic frequency adapted to the coefficients matrix
\mathbb{A}
(as opposed to the natural frequency for variable coefficients, see Section
5
for more details).
Actually, we need to use three different forms of the frequency, which although different can be suitably compared at the right scales.
In particular, we show a quasi-monotonicity formula for a Dirichlet type frequency for solutions to variable coefficient thin obstacle problems. This idea has been used for the analysis of the classical obstacle problem in
[
18
,
3
]
. In the current setting, we couple it with the fundamental insight provided by Simon and Wickramasekera in the framework of
2
-valued minimal graphs, that quasi-monotonicity of the frequency is actually equivalent to a doubling condition on the relevant quantities provided Schauder estimates hold (cf.
[
35
, Lemma 6.1]
).

A comment deserves the restriction to Sobolev coefficients as opposite to the more general Hölder setting, for which the analysis of regular and singular points is contained in
[
23
,
24
]
. In the derivation of the basic estimate on the oscillation of the frequency, as well as for the monotonicity, we differentiate the matrix of coefficients and the gradient of the solutions, and therefore we need enough regularity for
\mathbb{A}
.

2.
Preliminaries on the thin obstacle problem

Here we recall the hypotheses on the thin obstacle problem we address.
We consider quadratic energies of the form

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla v(x),\nabla v(x)\rangle\,dx,

where the matrix field
\mathbb{A}
satisfies the hypotheses:

(H1)

\mathbb{A}\in W^{1,p}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)})
,
p\in(n+1,\infty]
(in particular, by Morrey’s embedding, we have
\mathbb{A}\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{1},\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)})
,

\alpha:=1-\frac{n+1}{p}
);

(H2)

\mathbb{A}(x)=(a_{ij}(x))_{i,j=1}^{n+1}
is a symmetric, bounded and coercive matrix,
i.e. for every
x\in B_{1}^{+}
,
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

a_{i,j}(x)=a_{j,i}(x)
, and

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\,.

for some
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.

Following
[
37
, Remark 1]
, by a means of a change of variables, it is not restrictive to additionally assume that

(H3)

a_{i,n+1}(x^{\prime},0)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
for every
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
.

Under these hypotheses, we can extend all the functions on
B_{1}
by even symmetry: with a slight abuse of notation, set

\mathbb{A}(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=\mathbb{A}(x^{\prime},-x_{n+1}),\quad u(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=u(x^{\prime},-x_{n+1})\qquad\forall\;x\in B_{1}^{+}.

In this way it is equivalent to formulate the problem on
B_{1}
with the symmetry condition:

\min_{v\in\mathcal{A}}\int_{B_{1}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla v(x),\nabla v(x)\rangle\,dx,

(2.1)

in the class of functions

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{v\in g+H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}):v(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})=v(x^{\prime},-x_{n+1}),\quad v(x^{\prime},0)\geq 0\right\},

with
g\in H^{1}(B_{1})
even symmetric and such that
g\geq 0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
.

By a result due to Ural’tseva
[
38
, Theorem 3.1]
the solution of the thin obstacle problem under assumptions (H1), (H2) and (H3) have Hölder continuous first derivative up to
B_{1}^{\prime}
(for the optimal Hölder exponent see
[
27
,
28
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
38
]
)
.

For every
g\in H^{1}(B_{1})
, even symmetric with
g\geq 0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
, there exists a unique
solution
u\in\mathcal{A}
to the thin obstacle problem (
2.1
).
Moreover,
u\in H_{\textup{loc}}^{2}\cap C_{\textup{loc}}^{1,\beta}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B_{1}^{\prime})
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
,
and

\|u\|_{H^{2}(B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{+}\cup B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime})}+\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{+}\cup B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime})}\leq C\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

(2.2)

where
C=C(p,n,\beta,\|\mathbb{A}\|_{W^{1,p}})>0
.

The Euler–Lagrange equation satisfied by the solution
u
to the thin obstacle problem are then the following:

\begin{cases}\textup{div}\big(\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u(x)\big)=0&\text{for }\;x\in B_{1}\setminus\big\{(x^{\prime},0)\,:\,u(x^{\prime},0)=0\big\},\\
\textup{div}\big(\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u(x)\big)\leq 0&\text{in the sense of distribution in }\,B_{1}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Moreover, in view of the assumptions (H2) and (H3), the Signorini complementary condition
in (
1.2
) then reads as

u\,\partial_{n+1}u=0\qquad\textup{on }\;B_{1}^{\prime}.

In the sequel
u
will always denote a solution to the thin obstacle problem (
2.1
), unless otherwise stated.

3.
The frequency function

In this section we introduce a suitable version of Almgren’s frequency function.
Let
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a decreasing
C^{1,1}
function such that
\phi^{\prime}(t)<0
if
\frac{1}{2}<t<1
and

\phi(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }\;0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
>0&\text{for }\;\frac{1}{2}<t<1,\\
0&\text{for }\;1\leq t.\\
\end{cases}

For the sake of simplicity we assume that

\phi(t)=2(1-t)\qquad\forall\;t\in\left[\frac{5}{8},\frac{7}{8}\right].

(3.1)

We define the frequency function of a function
u
at a point
x_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
by

I_{u}(x_{0},r):=\frac{r\,D_{u}(x_{0},r)}{H_{u}(x_{0},r)}\quad\forall\;r<1-|x_{0}|,

where the Dirichlet energy is given by

D_{u}(x_{0},r):=\int\phi\Big(\textstyle{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{r}}\Big)\,|\nabla u(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x,

and the “boundary”
L^{2}
norm of
u
is given by

H_{u}(x_{0},r):=-\int\phi^{\prime}\Big(\textstyle{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{r}}\Big)\,\frac{u^{2}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|}\,{\rm d}x.

Note that the frequency function is well-defined as long as
H_{u}(x_{0},r)>0
.
In what follows, when
u
is a solution to (
2.1
), we shall tacitly assume that the latter condition is satisfied when writing
I_{u}(x_{0},r)
.
Indeed,
H_{u}(x_{0},r)>0
if
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
by minimality of
u
and the uniqueness of minimizers.
Otherwise
u\equiv 0
on
B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}(x_{0})
, in turn implying

u\equiv 0
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
. This contradicts the fact that
x_{0}

is a free boundary point. Analogously, if
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
then

D_{u}(x_{0},r)>0
.

Additionally, for later convenience we introduce the following quantities

G_{u}(x_{0},r):=-r^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{r}}\Big)u(x)\nabla u(x)\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}}{\rm d}x\,,

and

E_{u}(x_{0},r):=-\int\phi^{\prime}\Big(\textstyle{\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{r}}\Big)\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{r^{2}}\Big(\nabla u(x)\cdot\frac{x-x_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}\Big)^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

(3.2)

In particular, note that
E_{u}(x_{0},r)H_{u}(x_{0},r)-G_{u}^{2}(x_{0},r)\geq 0
by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.

Finally, for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
and
r>0
, the rescalings
of a solution
u
are given by

\displaystyle u_{x_{0},r}(y):=\frac{r^{\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}}}{H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0},r)}u(x_{0}+r\,y)\quad\quad\forall\;y\in B_{\frac{1-|x_{0}|}{r}},

(3.3)

so that

H_{u_{x_{0},r}}(\underline{0},1)=1.

We shall always omit to write the base point

x_{0}
in the notation of
I_{u}
,
D_{u}
,
H_{u}
,
E_{u}
,
G_{u}
when
x_{0}=\underline{0}
.

By a simple corollary of Theorem
2.1
we have the following compactness: if
(u_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are such that

\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big(D_{u_{j}}(1)+H_{u_{j}}(1)\big)<+\infty,

then
u_{j}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(B_{s})
for every
s<1
.
Therefore, if
u_{j}
are solutions to the thin obstacle problem (
2.1
) satisfying the hypotheses (H1)-(H3) (holding uniformly in
j
for varying matrix fields
\mathbb{A}_{j}
, with the same constants
p,\lambda,\Lambda
), then by Theorem
2.1
there exists a function
u\in C^{1,\beta}(B_{1}^{+})
and a subsequence
(u_{j^{\prime}})\subset(u_{j})
such that

u_{j^{\prime}}\to u\qquad\textup{in}\quad C^{1,\gamma}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B_{1}^{\prime})\quad\forall\;\gamma\in(0,\beta),

where
\beta
is the constant in Theorem
2.1
.

In particular, the following compactness result holds for the rescaling of solutions.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of solutions to the thin obstacle problem (
2.1
)
for varying matrix fields
\mathbb{A}_{j}
under assumptions (H1)-(H3) holding uniformly in
j
, with
x_{j}\in\Gamma(u_{j})\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Assume that

\sup_{j}I_{u_{j}}(x_{j},\varrho_{j})<\infty\qquad\textup{ for some $\varrho_{j}\downarrow 0$.}

Then, there exists a subsequence

(j^{\prime})
such that
x_{j^{\prime}}\to x_{\infty}\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
and
a function
v_{\infty}
such that

v_{j^{\prime}}:=(u_{j^{\prime}})_{x_{j^{\prime}},\varrho_{j^{\prime}}}
satisfy as
j^{\prime}\to\infty

\displaystyle v_{j^{\prime}}\to v_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad C^{1,\gamma}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B_{1}^{\prime})\quad\forall\;\gamma\in(0,\beta),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\underline{0}\in\{x\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}:\,v_{\infty}(x)=|\nabla v_{\infty}(x)|=0\}\,.

(3.5)

Moreover,
v_{\infty}
is a solution the thin obstacle problem for the constant coefficients quadratic energy having density
\xi\mapsto\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{\infty})\xi,\xi\rangle
.

Proof.

From the assumption on the frequency, clearly the functions
v_{j}:=(u_{j})_{x_{j},\varrho_{j}}
satisfy

\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big(D_{v_{j}}(1)+H_{v_{j}}(1)\big)<+\infty.

Therefore, by compactness there exists a subsequence
(j^{\prime})
such that the points
x_{j^{\prime}}
converge to some
x_{\infty}\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
, and
v_{j^{\prime}}
converge to a limiting function
v_{\infty}
in the sense of (
3.4
).
Since
v_{j^{\prime}}(\underline{0})=|\nabla v_{j^{\prime}}(\underline{0})|=0
(because
x_{j^{\prime}}\in\Gamma(u_{j^{\prime}})
),
by the convergence (
3.4
) we infer also (
3.5
).

Finally, the fact that
v_{\infty}
is a solution to a thin obstacle problem with fixed coefficients
\mathbb{A}(x_{\infty})
follows either by a
\Gamma
-convergence result or by passing into the limit in the weak formulation of the Euler–Lagrange equations (
2.3
) characterizing the solutions, thanks to the convergence (
3.4
) and the continuity of the matrix field
\mathbb{A}
.

3.1.
Doubling estimates

To establish quasi-monotonicity of the frequency at distinguished points of the free boundary,
we follow a perturbative approach developed for the classical obstacle in
[
18
]
.
This approach is coupled with a fundamental insight present in the work of Simon and Wickramasekera
[
35
]
on minimal immersion, who highlighted that the doubling condition is equivalent to the quasi-monotonicity of the frequency.

More in details, we shall consider
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
satisfying the following hypothesis:

(H4)

x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
such that

\mathfrak{m}(x_{0}):=\sup_{r\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}I_{u}(x_{0},r)<\infty

(3.6)

As shown in Appendix
A
condition (
3.6
)
is equivalent to assume that
u
has a finite order of contact with the null obstacle at

x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
(cf. the Introduction).

A first step towards the monotonicity of the frequency is to establish a lower bound.
A more refined version of the ensuing result will follow after showing
the quasi-monotonicity of the frequency (cf. (
3.32
)).
All the constants that will appear in the results below can depend on the parameters
p,\lambda,\Lambda
of (H1)-(H3), even though it will never be explicitly highlighted.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
with this property. If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions (H1)-(H3), for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
satisfying (H4) with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, then

(3.7)

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that there exist solutions
u_{j}
and points
x_{j}\in\Gamma(u_{j})\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
for some
\mathfrak{m}_{0}<\infty

as in the statement, such that for a suitable choice of radii
r_{j}\downarrow 0
we have that

I_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{j}}.

For
j
sufficiently large
I_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, and therefore by
Corollary
3.1
, up to a subsequence not relabeled and keeping
the notation introduced there, we conclude that the functions
v_{j}:=(u_{j})_{x_{j},2r_{j}}

converges in
C^{1}(B_{1})
to some function
v_{\infty}
that minimizes

v\mapsto\int_{B_{1}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{\infty})\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx,

among all functions
v\in v_{\infty}+H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
with
v(x^{\prime},0)\geq 0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, by strong convergence, we infer that
H_{v_{\infty}}(1)=H_{(u_{j})_{x_{j},2r_{j}}}(1)=1

and

I_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\lim_{j}I_{(u_{j})_{x_{j},2r_{j}}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\lim_{j}I_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})=0.

Therefore,
D_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=0
and thus
v_{\infty}\equiv 0
on
B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
,
a contradiction being
v_{\infty}
analytic on
B_{1}\setminus B_{1}^{\prime}
.
∎

Next we prove the above mentioned doubling estimates.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
with this property. If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions
(H1)-(H3), for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
satisfying (H4) with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, then

C^{-1}\leq\frac{H_{u}(x_{0},2r)}{H_{u}(x_{0},r)}\leq C,\qquad 1\leq\frac{D_{u}(x_{0},2r)}{D_{u}(x_{0},r)}\leq C\qquad\forall\;r\in(0,\varrho].

(3.8)

Proof.

First note that by the very definition
D_{u}(x_{0},r)\leq D_{u}(x_{0},2r)
.
Assume by contradiction that there exist solutions
u_{j}
, points

x_{j}\in\Gamma(u_{j})\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
for some
\mathfrak{m}_{0}<\infty

as in the statement and radii
r_{j}\downarrow 0
such that

\lim_{j}\frac{H_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})}{H_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})}\in\{0,\infty\},\quad\text{and/or}\quad\lim_{j}\frac{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})}{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})}=\infty.

For
j
sufficiently large
I_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
thus by Corollary
3.1
, up to a subsequence not relabeled,
v_{j}:=(u_{j})_{x_{j},2r_{j}}

converges in
C^{1}(B_{1})
to some function
v_{\infty}
that minimizes

v\mapsto\int_{B_{1}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{\infty})\nabla v,\nabla v\rangle dx,

among all functions
v\in v_{\infty}+H^{1}_{0}(B_{1})
with
v(x^{\prime},0)\geq 0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
.
In particular, we conclude that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{H_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})}{H_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})}=\frac{H_{v_{\infty}}(1)}{H_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}\in[C^{-1},C],

and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},2r_{j})}{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})}=\frac{D_{v_{\infty}}(1)}{D_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})}\in[1,C],

for some constant
C>0
(depending on
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
) because of the doubling estimates satisfied by
v_{\infty}
which is a solution to an obstacle problem with constant coefficients and frequency
I_{v_{\infty}}(1)
bounded by
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
(e.g., cf.
[
13
, Corollary 2.8]
). This gives the desired contradiction.
∎

In a similar fashion, the frequency computed at nearby points can be compared at radii that are bigger than the distance between the points.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
with this property. If

\displaystyle\frac{H_{u}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x_{0},t)},\quad\frac{D_{u}(x,t)}{D_{u}(x_{0},t)}\in[C^{-1},C].

(3.9)

In particular, the frequency function of

is well-defined at every point
x\in B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}^{\prime}(x_{0})
at the scales

(3.10)

Remark 3.5
.

We stress that the conclusions of Lemma
3.4
hold even for points
x
not necessarily in the free boundary.

Proof.

The proof proceeds analogously to the previous ones by contradiction: assume there exist functions
u_{j}
, points
z_{j}\in\Gamma(u_{j})\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
with
\mathfrak{m}(z_{j})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}<\infty
and
x_{j}\in B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{r_{j}}}{{2}}}(z_{j})
contradicting one of the two sets of inequalities in (
3.9
) for some sequence

t_{j}\in[r_{j},\varrho_{j}]
with
\varrho_{j}\downarrow 0
.
As above, we can apply Corollary
3.1
, thus
(up to passing to a subsequence not relabeled) there exists
v_{\infty}

such that
v_{j}:=(u_{j})_{z_{j},t_{j}}\to v_{\infty}
in
C^{1,\beta}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{2})
with
v_{\infty}
solution to the thin obstacle problem with matrix fields
\mathbb{A}(x_{\infty})
.
Clearly, we may also assume that
t_{j}^{-1}(x_{j}-z_{j})\to y_{\infty}\in\bar{B}^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
(note that

).

Assume now that the first set of inequalities in (

) is contradicted,
i.e.

By the convergence of

to

Thus

.
Given that

is analytical in

,
by unique continuation we conclude that
v_{\infty}\equiv 0
in
B_{2}
, against
the assumption
H_{v_{\infty}}(1)=\lim_{j}H_{v_{j}}(1)=1
.

In case the second set of inequalities in (

) is contradicted,
i.e.

\lim_{j}\frac{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},t_{j})}{D_{u_{j}}(z_{j},t_{j})}\in\{0,\infty\}.

we have on one hand that
D_{v_{\infty}}(1)=\lim_{j}D_{v_{j}}(1)=\lim_{j}I_{u_{j}}(z_{j},t_{j})\in[C,\mathfrak{m}_{0}]
, where

is the constant of the lower bound found in Lemma
3.2

and
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
is the upper bound for the
\mathfrak{m}(z_{j})
.
On the other hand we have that

D_{v_{\infty}}(y_{\infty},1)=\lim_{j}D_{v_{j}}(t_{j}^{-1}(x_{j}-z_{j}),1)=\lim_{j}t_{j}\frac{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},t_{j})}{H_{u_{j}}(z_{j},t_{j})}=\lim_{j}I_{u_{j}}(z_{j},t_{j})\frac{D_{u_{j}}(x_{j},t_{j})}{D_{u_{j}}(z_{j},t_{j})}\,.

Therefore, under the contradiction assumption we infer that

and taking into account the analiticity of the solutions the last

, which is a contradiction.

Finally, as a byproduct of the first estimate in (

)
the frequency function is well-defined for

, provided that

.
Moreover, (

) follows straightforwardly from (

):

\displaystyle\Big|I_{u}(x_{0},t)-I_{u}(x,t)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|I_{u}(x_{0},t)\left(1-\frac{D_{u}(x,t)}{D_{u}(x_{0},t)}\cdot\frac{H_{u}(x_{0},t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\right)\Big|\leq C.\qed

As an immediate consequence, the doubling estimates hold not only for the points on the free boundary, but also for nearby points at suitable scales. This information will be crucial to bound error terms in the almost monotonicity formulas in the sequel (cf. (

), (

)).

3.2.
Almost monotonicity of the frequency

By means of the doubling estimates established in Proposition
3.3
, and arguing similarly to
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
, we can conclude almost monotonicity of the frequency
I_{u}(x_{0},\cdot)
under the condition

\mathbb{A}(x_{0})=\textup{Id}
.
In fact, we establish quasi-monotonicity for all points
x_{0}
and radii
r\geq(\mathfrak{a}(x_{0}))^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, recall that
\alpha=1-\frac{n+1}{p}
, provided that

\mathfrak{a}(x_{0}):=|\mathbb{A}(x_{0})-\textup{Id}|.

Notice that
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
if and only if
\mathbb{A}(x_{0})=\textup{Id}
.
We start off proving a couple of preliminary results.
In what follows, differentiation with respect to the radius shall be denoted by a prime and we write

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D}(x,t):=G_{u}(x,t)-D_{u}(x,t)\,,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t):=tD_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-(n-1)D_{u}(x,t)-2tE_{u}(x,t)\,.

(3.13)

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
with this property. If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions (H1)-(H3),
for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
satisfying (H4) with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
, then for
every
r\in(0,\varrho]
,
x\in B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}(x_{0})
, and

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{D}(x,t)|\leq C

\displaystyle\big([\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}(|x-x_{0}|+t)^{\alpha}+\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\Big(D_{u}(x,t)+t^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t)D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t)\Big)\,,

(3.14)

and

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)|\leq C\big([\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}(|x-x_{0}|+t)^{\alpha}+\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})\big)D_{u}(x,t)\,.

(3.15)

Proof.

Without loss of generality we suppose
x_{0}=\underline{0}
.
Fix a point
x\in B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
, and set
\mathbb{B}(z):=\mathbb{A}(z)-\textrm{Id}
for every
z\in B_{1}^{\prime}
.
To infer (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) we consider the equation satisfied by
u
and test it
with a suitable function.

We start noticing that

\displaystyle\epsilon_{D}(x,t)

\displaystyle=G_{u}(x,t)-D_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla\Big(u(z)\phi\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big)\Big){\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\int\mathbb{B}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla\Big(u(z)\phi\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big)\Big){\rm d}z,

(3.16)

where in the last equality we use that
u
is a solution to (
2.3
) (tested with
u(z)\phi\big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\big)
) and Signorini ambiguous boundary conditions.
In order to prove (
3.14
), we use (
3.16
): for
\varrho
sufficiently small, by the Hölder inequality we get

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{D}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\big([\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}(|x|+t)^{\alpha}+\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\Big(D_{u}(x,t)-t^{-1}\int_{B_{t}(x)\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}}(x)}\phi^{\prime}\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big)|u(z)||\nabla u(z)|{\rm d}z\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\big([\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}(|x|+t)^{\alpha}+\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})\big)\Big(D_{u}(x,t)+t^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t)D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,2t)\Big)\,,

(3.17)

with
C=C(\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty})
.
We conclude the estimate in (
3.14
) in view of Corollary
3.6

(cf. (
3.11
)).

To prove (
3.15
) we argue similarly, and test the equation with
the function
w
defined as the even extension across
B_{1}^{\prime}
of the restriction to
B_{1}^{+}

of
\phi\big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\big)\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)
. Note that
w
is an
admissible test in view of the
H^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{\pm}\cup B_{1}^{\prime})
regularity of
u
. Additionally, (H3) and Signorini’s ambiguous boundary conditions imply that
(\mathbb{A}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot e_{n+1})\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)={a_{n+1,n+1}(z)\partial_{n+1}u(z)=}0
on
B_{1}^{\prime}
.
In view of this we compute explicitly using the divergence theorem

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla w{\rm d}z=\int\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla w\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=D_{u}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}\int\phi\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big)\nabla(|\nabla u(z)|^{2})\cdot(z-x)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{t}\int\phi^{\prime}\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big){\textstyle{\frac{(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x))^{2}}{|z-x|}}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{2}D_{u}(x,t)+\frac{1}{t}\int\phi^{\prime}\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\Big)\Big(-{\textstyle{\frac{|\nabla u(z)|^{2}}{2}|z-x|+\frac{(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x))^{2}}{|z-x|}}}\Big){\rm d}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{2}D_{u}(x,t)+\frac{t}{2}D_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-tE_{u}(x,t)=-\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t).

(3.18)

To establish (
3.15
) we can then proceed similarly as above:

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)|\leq

\displaystyle\quad+t\int\phi\big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}}\big)|\nabla^{2}u(z)||\nabla u(z)|\,{\rm d}z+\int_{B_{t}(x)\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}}(x)}|\nabla u(z)|^{2}{\rm d}z\Big)

Lemma 3.8
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
and
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}

and

First note that by scaling and a direct differentiation we easily get

\displaystyle H_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{t}\,H_{u}(x,t)-2\,t^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\Big({\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\Big)u(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-x}{|z-x|}}{\rm d}z

3.14

)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}

|R_{u}(x,t)|\leq C\,t^{\alpha-1}I_{u}(x,t)\qquad\forall\;t\in\big[\max\{(\mathfrak{a}(x_{0}))^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}},r\},\varrho\big]\,.

(3.24)

r<\varrho

[r,\varrho]\ni t\mapsto e^{Ct^{\alpha}}I_{u}(x,t)\quad\text{is non-decreasing}.

(3.25)

Proof.

Without loss of generality we prove the result in case

We take advantage of formulas (

)-(

and (

\displaystyle\frac{I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)}{I_{u}(x,t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}+\frac{D_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)}{D_{u}(x,t)}-\frac{H_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:variationDprime}}}{{=}}2\,\frac{E_{u}(x,t)}{D_{u}(x,t)}-2\frac{G_{u}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}+\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{tD_{u}(x,t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{D_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)}\big(E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-G_{u}(x,t)D_{u}(x,t)\big)+\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{tD_{u}(x,t)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:variationG}}}{{=}}\frac{2}{D_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)}\big(E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-G_{u}^{2}(x,t)\big)

\displaystyle+\,\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{tD_{u}(x,t)}+2\,\frac{\epsilon_{D}(x,t)G_{u}(x,t)}{D_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)}.

(3.26)

Thus, for
t\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
we conclude equality (
3.23
), i.e.

\displaystyle I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)

\displaystyle-\frac{2t}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}\big(E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-G_{u}^{2}(x,t)\big)=\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}+2t\frac{G_{u}(x,t)}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}\epsilon_{D}(x,t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:variationG}}}{{=}}\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}+2t\frac{\epsilon_{D}^{2}(x,t)}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}+2I(x,t)\frac{\epsilon_{D}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}=:R_{u}(x,t).

(3.27)

To estimate
R_{u}
we note that, if
t\geq\max\{(\mathfrak{a}(x_{0}))^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}},r\}
and

x\in B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}(x_{0})
, then

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\Big|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:variationD prime errore}}}{{\leq}}Ct^{\alpha-1}I_{u}(x,t)\,,

in turn implying that

\displaystyle\frac{t\epsilon_{D}^{2}(x,t)}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}+I_{u}(x,t)\frac{|\epsilon_{D}(x,t)|}{H_{u}(x,t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:variationD errore}}}{{\leq}}Ct^{\alpha-1}I_{u}(x,t),

with
C=C(\mathfrak{m}(x_{0}),[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha})
, where we use the local uniform upper bound
on
I_{u}(x,t)
given by Lemma
3.4
. Therefore, estimate (
3.24
) follows straightforwardly.

In particular, if
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
, then (
3.24
)
holds true for all
t\in[r,\varrho]
, and thus (
3.25
) follows by direct
integration of (
3.23
) by taking into account estimate (
3.24
) and
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that implies
E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-G_{u}^{2}(x,t)\geq 0
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is also convenient to highlight a different expression of the derivative of
the frequency function for later purposes: from (
3.27
) we get

I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)=\frac{2t}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}\big(E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-D_{u}^{2}(x,t)\big)+\widetilde{R}_{u}(x,t)\,,

\widetilde{R}_{u}(x,t):=\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}-2I_{u}(x,t)\frac{\epsilon_{D}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\,.

(3.29)

In particular, if (H4) is satisfied in
x_{0}
,
x\in B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}(x_{0})
and

, then

|\widetilde{R}_{u}(x,t)|\leq C\,t^{\alpha-1}I_{u}(x,t)\,.

(3.30)

An additive quasi-monotonicity formula is then easily deduced.

Corollary 3.11
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
and
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}
with this property. If
u

) under assumptions (H1)-(H3), for every

[\max\{(\mathfrak{a}(x_{0}))^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}},r\},\varrho]\ni t\mapsto I_{u}(x,t)+Ct^{\alpha}\quad\text{is non-decreasing}.

In particular, if

, then

(3.32)

Proof.

The proof of (

) is straighforward from inequality (
3.23
), estimate (
3.24
) in Proposition
3.9
and (
3.10
) in Lemma
3.4
.

5
, Lemma 1]

Remark 3.12
.

The
H^{2}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1})
regularity of a solution
u
has been exploited to infer the quasi-monotonicity property of the frequency function
I_{u}(x_{0},\cdot)
at points
x_{0}
as in the statement of Corollary
3.11
in order to estimate the error term
\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}
(cf. Lemma
3.7
).
Different approaches, such as that in
[

,
24
]
, lead to quasi-monotonicity formulas holding in the less restrictive Hölder regularity scale for the matrix field. Despite this, in
order to establish rectifiability of free boundary points with finite order of contact we shall crucially use the

).

4.
Main estimates on the frequency

4.1.
Oscillation estimate of the frequency

We introduce the following notation for the radial variations of the frequency
at a point
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})<\infty
and
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
:

\Delta^{r}_{\rho}(x_{0}):=I_{u}(x_{0},r)+Cr^{\alpha}-\big(I_{u}(x_{0},\rho)+C\rho^{\alpha}),\qquad 0<\rho<r,

(4.1)

for
C>0
the constant in Corollary
3.11
, so that

\Delta^{r}_{\rho}(x_{0})\geq 0
for
r,\rho
sufficiently small.
The result in the ensuing Proposition
4.2
shows how the spatial
oscillation of the frequency in two nearby points at a given scale is in turn
controlled by the radial variations at comparable scales.
We establish first a technical result. To this aim it is convenient to define the parameter

\theta:=\min\{[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}}},1\}.

(4.2)

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
and
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}
with this property. If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions (H1)-(H3), for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
satisfying (H4) with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
and
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
, then for all
r_{1}\leq\varrho
,
r_{0}\in(\frac{\theta}{16}r_{1},r_{1})
,
and
x\in{B_{\frac{\theta}{32}r_{1}}^{\prime}(x_{0})}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x)\setminus B_{r_{0}}(x)}\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-

\displaystyle I_{u}(x,r_{0})\,u(z)\big)^{2}{\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}\,{\rm d}z\leq CH_{u}(x,2\,r_{1})\,\Delta^{2r_{1}}_{r_{0}}(x).

(4.3)

Proof.

By translation, it suffices to prove the lemma for
x_{0}=\underline{0}
. We start off with the following computation that uses Remark
3.10
:

\displaystyle-\int\phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,t)\,u(z)\big)^{2}\frac{1}{|z-x|}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=t^{2}E_{u}(x,t)-2\,t\,I_{u}(x,t)\,G_{u}(x,t)+I_{u}^{2}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{H_{u}(x,t)}\,\big(E_{u}(x,t)H_{u}(x,t)-D_{u}^{2}(x,t)\big)-2t\epsilon_{D}(x,t)\,I_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:Iuprime bis}}}{{=}}\frac{t}{2}\,H_{u}(x,t)\big(I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-\overline{R}_{u}(x,t)\big),

(4.4)

where

\overline{R}_{u}=\widetilde{R}_{u}+\frac{4}{H_{u}(x,t)}\,\epsilon_{D}(x,t)\,I_{u}(x,t)=\frac{\epsilon_{D^{\prime}}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}+2I_{u}(x,t)\frac{\epsilon_{D}(x,t)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\,.

with
\widetilde{R}_{u}
function in (
3.29
).
In particular, the above equalities show that the last factor in (
4.4
) is nonnegative, being nonnegative the term on the first line of (
4.4
) itself. Therefore,
we may use the elementary integral estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x)\setminus B_{r_{0}}(x)}f(z){\rm d}z

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)f(z)\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}t\quad\text{ for all $0<r_{0}\leq r_{1}$},

(4.5)

that holds true for any measurable function
f\geq 0
, in order to deduce

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x)\setminus B_{r_{0}}(x)}\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\,u(z)\big)^{2}{\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:fubini}}}{{\leq}}-\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\,u(z)\big)^{2}{\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,t)\,u(z)\big)^{2}\,{\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(I_{u}(x,t)-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\big)^{2}u^{2}(z){\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:I mon}}}{{\leq}}\,\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\frac{t}{2}\,H_{u}(x,t)\big(I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-\overline{R}_{u}(x,t)\big)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{C}{r_{0}}\,\big((I_{u}(x,2r_{1})-I_{u}(x,r_{0}))^{2}+(2r_{1})^{2\alpha}\big)\,\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}H_{u}(x,t)\,{\rm d}t,

(4.6)

where in the last estimate we have used Corollary
3.11
because
x\in{B_{\frac{\theta}{32}r_{1}}^{\prime}\subset B_{\nicefrac{{r_{0}}}{{2}}}^{\prime}}
.

From Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.8
we get that
H_{u}(x,t)\leq CH_{u}(x,2r_{1})
for all

t\in[r_{0},2r_{1}]
.
Furthermore, we can use (
3.30
) in Remark
3.10
to estimate
|\overline{R}_{u}(x,t)|
for all
x\in B_{\frac{\theta}{32}r_{1}}(x_{0})
and
t\in[r_{0},2r_{1}]
.
Thus, by taking into account that
I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-\overline{R}_{u}(x,t)\geq 0
(cf. (
4.4
)),
from (
4.6
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x)\setminus B_{r_{0}}(x)}

\displaystyle\big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\,u(z)\big)^{2}{\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\leq CH_{u}(x,2r_{1})\int_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}\big(I_{u}^{\prime}(x,t)-\overline{R}_{u}(x,t)\big)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+C\,H_{u}(x,2r_{1})\Big(\big(I_{u}(x,2r_{1})-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\big)^{2}+(2r_{1})^{\alpha}-r_{0}^{\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\,H_{u}(x,2r_{1})\,\big(I_{u}(x,2r_{1})-I_{u}(x,r_{0})\big)+CH_{u}(x,2r_{1})\big((2r_{1})^{\alpha}-r_{0}^{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\,H_{u}(x,2r_{1})\,\Delta_{r_{0}}^{2r_{1}}(x),

where we used that
r_{0}\in(\frac{\theta}{16}r_{1},r_{1})
and
I_{u}(x,t)\leq C
for all
t\in(r_{0},2r_{1})
and
x\in B^{\prime}_{\frac{\theta}{32}r_{1}}
by Lemma
3.4
.
∎

We are now ready to prove a spatial oscillation estimate on the frequency function in terms of the radial oscillation computed between suitable radii in all points belonging to a neighbourhood of a point
x_{0}

with
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on

\mathfrak{m}_{0}
and
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}
with this property. If
u
is a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions (H1)-(H3), then for every
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
satisfying (H4) with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
and
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
, for all
R,\rho>0
with
R>\frac{32}{\theta}
and
R\rho<\varrho
, we have

\big|I_{u}(x_{1},R\rho)-I_{u}(x_{2},R\rho)\big|\leq C\,\Big(\big(\Delta^{4R\rho}_{\nicefrac{{R\rho}}{{4}}}(x_{1})\big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\big(\Delta^{R\rho}_{\nicefrac{{R\rho}}{{4}}}(x_{2})\big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+(R\rho)^{\alpha}\Big),

(4.7)

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in B^{\prime}_{\rho}(x_{0})
(where
\theta
is defined in (
4.2
)).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we show the conclusion for
x_{0}=\underline{0}
. Moreover, we set
t=R\rho
.
Note that, under the assumption
R>\frac{32}{\theta}
, if
t<\varrho
is sufficiently small, then
I_{u}(x,t)\leq C
with the constant
C
depending on
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
by Lemma
3.4
.

The proof is based on estimating the tangential derivative
of the frequency function
x\mapsto I_{u}(x,t)
for
x\in B^{\prime}_{\rho}
by taking advantage of the
H^{2}_{\textup{loc}}
regularity of
u
.
Thus, we start off noticing that the functions
x\mapsto H_{u}(x,t)

and
x\mapsto D_{u}(x,t)
are differentiable and, for every

e\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
e\cdot e_{n+1}=0
,
we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{e}H_{u}(x,t)=-2\,\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,u(z)\,\partial_{e}u(z)\,\frac{1}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z,

(4.8)

and setting
\mathbb{B}(z):=\mathbb{A}(z)-\mathbb{A}(\underline{0})(=\mathbb{A}(z)-\textup{Id})

\displaystyle\partial_{e}D_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\int\phi\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla(\partial_{e}u)(z)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=2\int\phi\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}\big)(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla(\partial_{e}u)(z)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=-2\,t^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\partial_{e}u(z)\,\mathbb{A}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-x}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-2\int\phi\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\mathbb{B}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla(\partial_{e}u)(z)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=-2\,t^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\partial_{e}u(z)\,\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-x}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z+\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)\,,

(4.9)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=-2\,t^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\mathbb{B}(z)\partial_{e}u(z)\,\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-x}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle-2\int\phi\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\mathbb{B}(z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla(\partial_{e}u)(z)\,{\rm d}z.

(4.10)

The third equality in (
4.9
) follows from the divergence theorem
applied to the vector field
V(z):=\phi\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\partial_{e}u(z)\mathbb{A}(z)\nabla u(z)
,
note that
V\in C^{\infty}(B_{t}(x)\setminus B_{1}^{\prime},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
,

V
has compact support and the divergence of
V
does not concentrate on
B_{1}^{\prime}
.
Recalling the
H^{2}_{\textup{loc}}
-estimates in Theorem
2.1
and the doubling estimates in Corollary
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle|\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C|e|t^{-1}(|x|+t)^{\alpha}D_{u}(x,2t)+C(|x|+t)^{\alpha}D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t)\|u\|_{H^{2}(B_{t}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq C|e|t^{-1}(|x|+t)^{\alpha}\big(D_{u}(x,2t)+D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t)D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,2t)\big)

\displaystyle\leq C|e|t^{-1}(|x|+t)^{\alpha}D_{u}(x,t)\,.

(4.11)

We choose
e:=x_{2}-x_{1}
and set

\mathcal{E}_{i}(z):=\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x_{i})-I_{u}(x_{i},t)\,u(z)\quad\text{for $i=1,2$,}

\Delta I:=I_{u}(x_{1},t)-I_{u}(x_{2},t)\Delta\mathcal{E}(z):=\mathcal{E}_{1}(z)-\mathcal{E}_{2}(z).

Then, we have that
\partial_{e}u(z)=\Delta I\;u(z)+\Delta\mathcal{E}(z)
.
Thus, from (
4.8
) we infer that

\displaystyle\partial_{e}H_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\Delta I\cdot H_{u}(x,t)-2\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\Delta\mathcal{E}(z)\;\frac{u(z)}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z\,,

while from (
4.9
) and (
3.12
) we conclude

\displaystyle\partial_{e}D_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\,\Delta I\cdot(D_{u}(x,t)+\epsilon_{D}(x,t))+\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)

\displaystyle-2\,t^{-1}\int\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\Delta\mathcal{E}(z)\;\nabla u(z)\cdot\frac{z-x}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z\,.

In particular, by a direct computation we deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{e}I(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{H_{u}^{2}(x,t)}\,\big(H_{u}(x,t)\,\partial_{e}D_{u}(x,t)-D_{u}(x,t)\,\partial_{e}H_{u}(x,t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{H_{u}(x,t)}\int-\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\,\Delta\mathcal{E}(z)\;\Big(\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,t)\,u(z)\Big)\,\frac{1}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{t}{H_{u}(x,t)}\cdot(2\Delta I\cdot\epsilon_{D}(x,t)+\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)).

(4.12)

We estimate (
4.12
) (recall that
t=R\rho
and
x\in B_{\rho}^{\prime}
).
First notice that thanks to (
3.14
) we may conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{t}{H_{u}(x,t)}|2\Delta I\cdot\epsilon_{D}(x,t)|\leq C|\Delta I|t^{\alpha}(I_{u}(x,t)+I_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x,t))\leq Ct^{\alpha}\,,

(4.13)

for some
C>0
.
Furthermore, by (
4.11
) we get that

\displaystyle\frac{t}{H_{u}(x,t)}|\epsilon_{\partial_{e}D}(x,t)|\leq Ct^{\alpha}I_{u}(x,t)\leq Ct^{\alpha}\,,

(4.14)

where we used that
|e|\leq 2\rho\leq t
.
Note that, since
x\in B_{\rho}^{\prime}
,
by elliptic regularity of
u
(cf. Theorem
2.1
) we infer that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{t}^{+}(x)}

\displaystyle|\nabla u(z)\cdot(z-x)-I_{u}(x,t)\,u(z)|

\displaystyle\leq t\sup_{z\in B_{t+\rho}^{+}}|\nabla u(z)|+I_{u}(x,t)\|u\|_{C^{0}(B_{t+\rho})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,t^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{2t+2\rho})}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\,H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big(2t+2\rho\big),

where we use (
3.21
) in Lemma
3.8
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{e}I_{u}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq C\,t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\frac{H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\big(2t+2\rho\big)}{H_{u}(x,t)}\int-\phi^{\prime}\big(\textstyle{\frac{|z-x|}{t}}\big)\big(|\mathcal{E}_{1}(z)|+|\mathcal{E}_{2}(z)|\big)\frac{1}{|z-x|}\,{\rm d}z+C\,t^{\alpha}\,.

(4.15)

In order to estimate the integral term in (
4.15
), we notice that

B_{t}(x)\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}}(x)\subset B_{t+2\rho}(x_{i})\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}-2\rho}(x_{i})\quad\forall\,x\in B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime},\;\text{for }\;i=1,2;

therefore

\displaystyle\int_{B_{t}(x)\setminus B_{\frac{t}{2}}(x)}|\mathcal{E}_{i}(z)|\,\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x|}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\leq{\textstyle{\frac{2\,(t+2\rho)}{t}}}\int_{B_{t+2\rho}(x_{i})\setminus B_{\frac{t}{2}-2\rho}(x_{i})}|\mathcal{E}_{i}(z)|\,\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x_{i}|}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\leq C\,t^{\frac{n}{2}}\Big(\int_{B_{t+2\rho}(x_{i})\setminus B_{\frac{t}{2}-2\rho}(x_{i})}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}(z)\,\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x_{i}|}}\,{\rm d}z\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

(4.16)

where we choose
R>8
and we use the direct computation

\int_{B_{t+2\rho}(x_{i})\setminus B_{\frac{t}{2}-2\rho}(x_{i})}\textstyle{\frac{1}{|z-x_{i}|}}\,{\rm d}z\leq C\,t^{n},

with

a dimensional constant.
If
R>\frac{32}{\theta}
, then we are in the position to apply Lemma
4.1

(with
r_{0}=\nicefrac{{t}}{{2}}-2\rho
and
r_{1}=t+2\rho

(4.17)

5.
Intrinsic frequency

In this section we introduce an elementary change of variables in order to make a generic free boundary point
x_{0}
satisfy
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
for a different, related thin obstacle problem (cf.
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
for the thin obstacle, and
[
17
]
in the case of the classical obstacle problem).
In such a way we define an intrinsic frequency function for which the conclusions of Proposition
4.4
hold even without the matrix
\mathbb{A}(x_{0})
being the identity at free boundary points.

Given a solution
u
of (
2.3
), and
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)\cap B_{1}^{\prime}
, consider the function

u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}:\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{1})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(x):=u(\Phi_{x_{0}}(x))\,,

where
\Phi_{x_{0}}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the
affine map
\Phi_{x_{0}}(x):=x_{0}+\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})
.
In particular, changing variables by means of
\Phi_{x_{0}}
leads to

\int_{B_{1}}\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u(x),\nabla u(x)\rangle\,{\rm d}x=\det\big(\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})\big)\,\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}\big(u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})},\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{1})\big)\,,

(5.1)

where

\mathscr{E}_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(v,U):=\int_{U}\langle\mathbb{C}_{x_{0}}(x)\nabla v(x),\nabla v(x)\rangle{\rm d}x\,,

(5.2)

for every open set
U\subseteq\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{1})
,
and
\mathbb{C}_{x_{0}}(x):=\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})\mathbb{A}(\Phi_{x_{0}}(x))\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})
.
Note that
\mathbb{C}_{x_{0}}(x_{0})=\text{Id}
.
Therefore,
u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}
turns out to be the solution of
the thin obstacle problem for the energy in (
5.2
)
among all functions in
v\in g(\Phi_{x_{0}})+H^{1}_{0}\big(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{1})\big)
that are even across the corresponding hyperplane

\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(\{x_{n+1}=0\})=\{x_{n+1}=0\}
(thanks to hypothesis (H3)), and such that

v|_{\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{1}^{\prime})}\geq 0
.
Moreover, there is a bijection of the free boundaries:

\Gamma(u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})})=\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(\Gamma(u))
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
2.3
), and let

x_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
r>0
be such that
\Phi_{x_{0}}(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{x_{n+1}=0\})\subset B_{1}^{\prime}
.
Being
u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}
solution to the thin obstacle problem corresponding to the matrix field
\mathbb{C}_{x_{0}}(\cdot)
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
,
we consider the related frequency function

I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x,s)=\frac{s\,D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x,s)}{H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x,s)}\,,\qquad x\in\{x_{n+1}=0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}),\quad s<r-|x-x_{0}|.

(5.3)

In passing, note that if
x_{0}
satisfies assumption
\mathfrak{a}(x_{0})=0
, then
u
coincides with

u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}
, and correspondingly
I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}
coincides with
I_{u}
at all points in
B_{1}^{\prime}
and admissible radii.
For later purposes it is convenient to point out explicit formulas for the Dirichlet energy

D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x,s)=\int\phi\Big(\textstyle{\frac{|\Phi^{-1}_{x_{0}}(y)-x|}{s}}\Big)\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{0})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}\,{\rm d}y,

(5.4)

and for the “boundary”

norm of

H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x,s)=-\int\phi^{\prime}\Big(\textstyle{\frac{|\Phi^{-1}_{x_{0}}(y)-x|}{s}}\Big)\,\frac{u^{2}(y)}{|\Phi^{-1}_{x_{0}}(y)-x|}\frac{1}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}\,{\rm d}y.

(5.5)

We call
I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x_{0},r)
the intrinsic function at a free boundary point
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
and set

N_{u}(x_{0},r):=I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(x_{0},r).

Having fixed a point
x_{0}
of the free boundary with finite frequency, we compare the intrinsic frequency function and the (standard) Dirichlet based one at points of the free boundary with finite frequency close to
x_{0}
. Thus, for every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
we set

Proposition 5.1
.

For every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
there exist constants
\varrho,C>0
depending on the ellipticity constant
\lambda
,
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
and

u

) under assumptions (H1)-(H3), for every

,
if

and

\displaystyle\big|I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)-N_{u}(x_{1},r)\big|\leq C|x_{0}-x_{1}|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}N_{u}(x_{1},r)\,.

(5.6)

Proof.

We start off noticing that by (H1)

|\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1})-x_{0}|=|\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1})-\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{0})|\leq\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|x_{1}-x_{0}|\,,

where the square root of the ellipticity constant

Therefore,
I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)
is well defined provided that

are small (cf. Lemma
3.4
).

To prove the inequality in (
5.6
) it is convenient to recall formulas (

)-(

D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)=\int{\textstyle{\phi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(y-x_{1})|}{r}}\Big)\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{0})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}}\,{\rm d}y\,,

To estimate the difference between the Dirichlet energies we introduce the sets

\displaystyle U_{r}(x)

\displaystyle:=\Big((x+\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x)B_{r})\cup({x_{1}}+\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})B_{r})\Big)\setminus

\displaystyle\qquad\setminus\Big((x+\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x)B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}})\cap({x_{1}}+\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}})\Big)

for all
x\in B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
. Then, we argue as follows

\displaystyle D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)-D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)

\displaystyle=\int{\textstyle{\phi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})(y-x_{1})|}{r}}\Big)\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{1})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\quad-\int{\textstyle{\phi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(y-x_{1})|}{r}}\Big)\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{0})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\quad+\int{\textstyle{\phi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(y-x_{1})|}{r}\Big)\Big(\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{1})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}-\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{0})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}\Big)}}\,{\rm d}y

(5.7)

Since

\displaystyle|D^{(1)}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq C[\phi]_{0,1}|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})-\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})|\int_{U_{r}(x_{1})}{\textstyle{\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{1})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}}}\,{\rm d}y

(5.8)

and, analogously,

\displaystyle|D^{(2)}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|{\textstyle{\text{Id}-\frac{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}\mathbb{A}^{-1}(x_{1})\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}\Big|\int_{U_{r}(x_{1})}{\textstyle{\frac{\langle\mathbb{A}(x_{1})\nabla u(y),\nabla u(y)\rangle}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}}}\,{\rm d}y

(5.9)

for some constant
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,[\phi]_{0,1},[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha})>0
.
For
\rho
sufficiently small we apply iteratively Corollary
3.6
to
u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}
to conclude

|D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)-D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}D_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)\,.

(5.10)

To estimate the difference of the

(recall that

, having assumed

\displaystyle H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)-H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)

\displaystyle=\int{\textstyle{\psi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(y-x_{1})|}{r}\Big)\,\frac{u^{2}(y)}{r\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}}}\,{\rm d}y-\int{\textstyle{\psi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})(y-x_{1})|}{r}\Big)\,\frac{u^{2}(y)}{r\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}}}\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\int\Big({\textstyle{\psi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})(y-x_{1})|}{r}\Big)-\psi\Big(\frac{|\mathbb{A}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})(y-x_{1})|}{r}\Big)\Big)\,\frac{u^{2}(y)}{r\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}}}\,{\rm d}y

(5.11)

\displaystyle|H^{(2)}(r)|\leq\Big|{\textstyle{\frac{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{1})}{\det\mathbb{A}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(x_{0})}}}-1\Big|H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)\leq C|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)\,,

(5.12)

with

\displaystyle|H^{(1)}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq C|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r\big)\,.

|H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)-H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x_{1}),r)|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{\nicefrac{{\alpha}}{{2}}}H_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{1})}}(x_{1},r)\,,

Proposition 5.2

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\mathcal{L}}}r^{-n-1}\int_{B_{r}(p)}{\textup{dist}}^{2}(y,{\mathcal{L}}){\rm d}(\Phi_{x_{0}})_{\#}\mu_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\mathcal{L}}}r^{-n-1}\int_{\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(B_{r}(p))}{\textup{dist}}^{2}(\Phi_{x_{0}}(y),\Phi_{x_{0}}({\mathcal{L}})){\rm d}\mu_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(y)

if

.
Since,

satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition

in

we deduce that

\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}^{2}(p,r)

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n+1}\Lambda\,\beta_{\mu_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}^{2}(p,Rr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{B_{Rr}(p)}\big(\Delta_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}\big)^{(2R+4)r}_{\frac{1}{2}(R-5)r}(x){\rm d}\mu_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(x)+(Rr)^{\alpha}\mu_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}(B_{Rr}(p))\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\Phi_{x_{0}}(B_{Rr}(p))}\big(\Delta_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}\big)^{(2R+4)r}_{\frac{1}{2}(R-5)r}(\Phi_{x_{0}}^{-1}(x))\,{\rm d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{r^{n-1}}(Rr)^{\alpha}\mu(\Phi_{x_{0}}(B_{Rr}(p))\,,

where we denote by
\Delta_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}
the quantity defined
in (
4.1
) by means of
I_{u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})}}
,
R\geq(1+2\lambda^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\vee\frac{64}{\theta_{0}}
, and

is sufficiently small (cf. Proposition

).

Eventually, Proposition
5.1
provides
the conclusion as
{\rm spt}\,\mu\subseteq\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}^{2}(p,r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{B_{R^{2}r}(p)}\Xi^{R_{2}r}_{R_{1}r}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)+(Rr)^{\alpha/2}\mu(B_{R^{2}r}(p))\Big)\,,

for
R_{2}>\max\{2R^{2},2R+4\}
and

,
and

).
∎

6.
The measure and the structure of the free boundary

We recall the definition of homogeneous and almost homegeneous solutions to the (standard)
thin obstacle problem:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}:=\Big\{w\in H^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle:\,w(x)=|x|^{\lambda}\,w\big({\textstyle{\frac{x}{|x|}}}\big),\,\lambda\geq\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},

\displaystyle\text{ $w|_{B_{1}}$ solves \eqref{e:prob} with $\mathbb{A}\equiv{\rm Id}$}\Big\},

Given a solution
u
to (
2.1
) with
\mathbb{A}
satisfying (H1)-(H3), we set

\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u):=\left\{x\in\Gamma(u)\;:\;\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}N_{u}(x,r)<+\infty\right\}.

(6.1)

Note that, for every
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
we have that
\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\subseteq\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u)
.
For any point
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u)
, we set

J_{u}(x_{0},t):=e^{Ct^{\alpha}}N_{u}(x_{0},t),

for all
t>0
such that
J_{u}(x_{0},t)
is monotone, namely for all
t\in(0,\varrho)
with
\varrho>0

a constant depending on
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{0}
as in the statement of Proposition
3.9
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\eta>0
and let
u:B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a solution to thin obstacle problem (
2.1
). Assume that
x_{0}\in{\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u)\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
and
r\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})
is such that
J_{u}(x_{0},r)
is defined. Then,
u
is called

\eta
-almost homogeneous of (
2.1
) in
B_{r}(x_{0})
if

J_{u}(x_{0},\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}})-J_{u}(x_{0},\nicefrac{{r}}{{4}})\leq\eta.

The following lemma justifies this terminology.

Lemma 6.2
.

For every
{\varepsilon}>0
and
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
, there exist
\eta,\varrho>0
with the following property:
if
u
is a
\eta
-almost homogeneous solution in
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
r\leq\varrho
and
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{w\in{\mathcal{H}}}\big\|(u_{\mathbb{A}(x_{0})})_{x_{0},r}-w\big\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}\leq{\varepsilon}.

(6.2)

Proof.

The proof follows by a contradiction argument similar to
[
13
, Lemma 5.5]
.
Assume that for
{\varepsilon}>0
we could find sequences
r_{l}
of numbers and
u_{l}
of
\frac{1}{l}
-almost homogeneous solutions in
{B_{r_{l}}(x_{l})}
, such that

\inf_{l}\inf_{w\in{\mathcal{H}}}\big\|\big((u_{l})_{{\mathbb{A}(x_{l})}}\big)_{x_{l},r_{l}}-w\big\|_{H^{1}(B_{1})}\geq{\varepsilon}\,,

(6.3)

with
x_{l}\in\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u_{l})\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{m}(x_{l})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
.
By Proposition
3.1
there exists a subsequence (not relabeled) of
v_{l}=\big((u_{l})_{{\mathbb{A}(x_{l})}}\big)_{x_{l},r_{l}}
converging to a solution
v_{\infty}
of the thin obstacle problem in
B_{1}
for the standard Dirichlet energy. Moreover, we can assume that the points
x_{l}
converge to
x_{\infty}\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
.
From Proposition
3.3
we infer that

H_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})=\lim_{l}H_{v_{l}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\geq C\lim_{l}H_{v_{l}}(1)>0\,,

so that
v_{\infty}
is not zero.
On the other hand, we have that

I_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})-I_{v_{\infty}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}})=\lim_{l}(I_{v_{l}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})-I_{v_{l}}(\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}))=\lim_{l}(J_{u}(x_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}})-J_{u}(x_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{4}}))=0.

This implies that
v_{\infty}
is a solution with constant frequency and thus is homogeneous (see for instance
[
13
, Proposition 2.7]
), contradicting (
6.3
).
∎

A rigidity property of the type shown in
[
13
, Proposition 5.6]
holds in the case of non smooth coefficients as well. We call spine
S(w)
of a function
w\in{\mathcal{H}}
the maximal subspace of invariance
of
u
,

S(w):=\Big\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\;:\;w(x+y)=w(x)\quad\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\Big\}.

We recall that the maximal dimension of the spine of a function
w
in
{\mathcal{H}}
is at most
n-1
(cf.
[
13
, Section 5.2]
), and we set
{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{top}}
for the set of homogeneous solutions
w

with
\dim S(w)=n-1
; whereas
{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{low}}:={\mathcal{H}}\setminus{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{top}}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

For every
\tau>0
and
\mathfrak{m}_{0}>0
, there exists
\eta,\varrho>0
with this property.
If
u
is a
\eta
-almost homogeneous solution in
B_{r}(x_{0})
,
r\leq\varrho
and
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
with
\mathfrak{m}(x_{0})\leq\mathfrak{m}_{0}
,
then the following dichotomy holds:

(i)

either for every point
x\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}^{\prime}(x_{0})
we have

\displaystyle\left|J_{u}(x,\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}})-J_{u}(x_{0},\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}})\right|\leq\tau,

(6.4)

(ii)

or there exists a linear subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}
of dimension
n-2
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B_{\nicefrac{{r}}{{2}}}^{\prime}(x_{0}),\\
J_{u}(y,\nicefrac{{r}}{{8}})-J_{u}(y,\nicefrac{{r}}{{16}})\leq\eta\end{cases}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\textup{dist}}(y,x_{0}+V)\leq\tau r.

(6.5)

Proof.

The proof proceeds by contradiction and follows the strategy in
[
13
, Proposition 5.6]
.
Let
\tau>0
be a given constant and assume that there exist
r_{l}
and a sequence
(u_{l})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}
-almost homogeneous solutions in
B_{r_{l}}
(this clearly holds up to horizontal translations) such that

(i)

there exists
x_{l}\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u_{l})\cap B_{\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}}}^{\prime}
for which

\displaystyle\left|J_{u}(x_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}})-J_{u}(\underline{0},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}})\right|>\tau,

(6.6)

(ii)

for every linear subspace
V\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}

of dimension
n-2
there exists
y_{l}\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u_{l})\cap B_{\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}}}^{\prime}(x_{0})

(a priori depending on
V
) such that

\displaystyle J_{u}(y_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{8}})-J_{u}(y_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{16}})\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}\quad\text{and}\quad{\textup{dist}}\big(y_{l},V\big)>\tau{r_{l}}.

(6.7)

We consider the rescaled functions
v_{l}:=(u_{l})_{\underline{0},r_{l}}
. By the compactness result in Corollary
3.1

v_{l}
converge, up to a subsequence, to a not zero solution to the thin obstacle problem with constant coefficients
v_{\infty}
. In particular
v_{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}
thanks to Lemma
6.2
.

If
v_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{top}}
, then (
6.6
) is contradicted. Indeed, up to choosing a further subsequence, we can assume that
z_{l}:=r_{l}^{-1}x_{l}\to z_{\infty}\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
; moreover,
z_{\infty}
is a critical point for
v_{\infty}
, because both
v_{l}(z_{l})=|\nabla v_{l}(z_{l})|=0
and the convergence is
C^{1}
, and by a simple change of variables we have that

\displaystyle\left|I_{v_{\infty}}(z_{\infty},\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})-I_{v_{\infty}}(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|J_{u}(x_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}})-J_{u}(0,\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{2}})\right|\geq\tau,

which is a contradiction to the constancy of the frequency at critical points of homogeneous solutions
v_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{top}}
(see
[
13
, Lemma 5.3]
).

On the other hand, if
v_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{\textup{low}}
, we show a contradiction to the second condition in (
6.7
) with
V
any
(n-2)
-dimensional subspace containing
S(v_{\infty})
.
Indeed, let
y_{l}
be as in (
6.7
) for such a choice of
V
. By compactness, up to passing to a subsequence (not relabeled),
z_{l}:=r_{l}^{-1}y_{l}\to z_{\infty}
for some
z_{\infty}\in\bar{B}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
with
{\textup{dist}}(z_{\infty},V)\geq\tau>0
.
Arguing as before, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|I_{v_{\infty}}\big(z_{\infty},\nicefrac{{1}}{{8}}\big)-I_{v_{\infty}}\big(z_{\infty},\nicefrac{{1}}{{16}}\big)\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|J_{u}(y_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{8}})-J_{u}(y_{l},\nicefrac{{r_{l}}}{{16}})\right|\leq\lim_{l\to\infty}\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}=0\,,

where the last inequality is given by the
\nicefrac{{1}}{{l}}
-almost homogeneity of the functions
u_{l}
cf. the first condition in (
6.7
)).
Using
[
13
, Proposition 2.7, Lemma 5.2]
it follows that
z_{\infty}\in S(v_{\infty})
, from which we infer a contradiction as
z_{\infty}\in S(v_{\infty})\subseteq V
and
{\textup{dist}}(z_{\infty},V)\geq\tau
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof is now a simple consequence of the results established in the previous sections. Indeed, we can follow verbatim
[
13
, Section 6]
(see also
[
16
, §5.3]
).
Recall that

\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u):=\left\{x\in B_{1}^{\prime}:\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}N_{u}(x,r)<+\infty\right\}.

By a simple rescaling argument, if
x_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
r<{\textup{dist}}(x_{0},\partial B_{1})
, then the function
u_{r}(y):=u(x_{0}+ry)
solves a thin obstacle problem (
1.1
) with
\mathbb{A}
satisfying (H1) - (H3) and

\Gamma^{\textup{finite}}(u_{r})\cap B^{\prime}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m}_{0}\geq\frac{3}{2}}\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u_{r}).

Therefore, it is enough to show that
\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
is rectifiable. To this aim we fix
\rho_{0}>0
such that the conclusions of all propositions in the previous sections hold for points
x\in\Gamma^{\mathfrak{m}_{0}}(u)\cap B^{\prime}_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
and radii
\rho\leq\rho_{0}
.
We can then follow the proof of
[
13
, Section 6]
applied to the intrinsic frequency
N_{u}
starting at

: indeed, the proof uses only the lower bound of the frequency (cf. Corollary

), the estimate of the spatial oscillation of the frequency in terms of the mean-flatness (cf. Proposition

) and the rigidity of Proposition

, together with the Reifenberg-type rectifiability criteria provided in the work by Naber and Valtorta
[

.

Finally, we note that the proof of the rectifiability also gives the local finiteness of the measure of each

, which we will use for the proof of

1.2
in the next section.

7.
Finiteness of the frequency for
\mathbb{A}\in W^{1,\infty}

In this section we prove the finiteness of the intrinsic frequency
N_{u}
at all free boundary points for a solution
u
of (
2.3
) assuming that the matrix field
\mathbb{A}
satisfies
(H1) with
p=\infty
, (H2), and (H3) (see also
[
20
]
). Given this for granted, Theorem
1.2
is then an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

We first establish several auxuliary results under the simplifying assumptions that the
base point is the origin and that
\mathbb{A}(\underline{0})={\rm Id}
in the spirit of
[
17
, Section 3.2]
.
Consider then the function
\mu:B_{1}\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\mu(x):=\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nu(x),\nu(x)\rangle\;\mbox{ if }x\neq 0\;\mbox{ and }\;\mu(\underline{0})=1,

where
\nu(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}
. Recalling that
\mathbb{A}
is Lipschitz continuous we infer that

\mu\in C^{0,1}(B_{1})
, and

{\lambda\leq\mu(x)\leq\Lambda,\quad\mbox{for every }x\in B_{1},}

(7.1)

where
\lambda,\,\Lambda
are the ellipticity constants in (H2)
(for a proof see
[
17
, Lemma 3.10]
).

Here, for the sake of simplicity, we follow the computations in
[
17
]

which use Almgren original frequency function (cf.
[
2
]
) tailored for
Lipschitz coefficients. Let us define the functions

\mathscr{E}_{u}(r):=\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathscr{H}_{u}(r):=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\mu u^{2}{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n},

(7.2)

and the energy driven frequency function

\mathscr{I}_{u}(r):=\frac{r\mathscr{E}_{u}(r)}{\mathscr{H}_{u}(r)}.

(7.3)

It is useful for the sequel to observe that

\mathscr{E}_{u}(r)=\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nu\rangle{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\,.

(7.4)

This equality follows by computing the divergence of the vector field
u\mathbb{A}\nabla u
, by taking into account
(
2.3
), and by exploiting the Signorini’s ambiguous conditions together with (H3).

In order to establish the monotonicity of
\mathscr{I}_{u}
we start with the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
satisfies (H1) with
p=\infty
, (H2), and
\mathbb{A}(\underline{0})={\rm Id}
,
let
\mu
be as above. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
n
and on
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,1}
such that for every
r\in(0,1)
and
x\in B_{r}
, we have that

\left|\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}(x)-(n+1)\mu(x)\right|\leq Cr.

Proof.

Fixed a point
x\in B_{r}
, let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
, be the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathbb{A}(x)

and
\left\{e_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be the corresponding orthonormal base of eigenvectors.
Set
y_{i}:=r\,e_{i}
,
then,

\begin{split}|\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}(x)-&(n+1)\mu(x)|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(\lambda_{i}-\mu(x))\right|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\Big(\langle\mathbb{A}(x)e_{i},e_{i}\rangle-\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nu(x),\nu(x)\rangle\Big)\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(\Big|\langle\mathbb{A}(x)e_{i},e_{i}\rangle-\langle\mathbb{A}(y_{i})e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\Big|+\Big|\langle\mathbb{A}(y_{i})e_{i},e_{i}\rangle-\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nu(x),\nu(x)\rangle\Big|\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(\big|\langle\left(\mathbb{A}(x)-\mathbb{A}(y_{i})\right)e_{i},e_{i}\rangle\big|+\big|\mu(y_{i})-\mu(x)\big|\right)\\
&\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(|e_{i}|^{2}+1)|x-y_{i}|\leq Cr,\end{split}

where we used the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbb{A}
and
\mu
, and that
x,\,y_{i}\in\overline{B}_{r}
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

From Lemma
7.1
we deduce that

\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}(x)-(n+1)\mu(x)\geq-Cr\,,

in turn implying for every
x\in B_{1}

\mu^{-1}(x)\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}(x)\geq-Cr\mu^{-1}(x)+(n+1).

(7.5)

First, we compute the derivative of
\mathscr{E}_{u}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
2.1
) under assumptions (H1)-(H3) with
p=\infty
, and

\mathbb{A}(\underline{0})={\rm Id}
, and let
\mu
be as above. Then, there exists a constant
C>0

depending on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
, and
[\mathbb{A}]_{0,1}
such that
for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in(0,1)

\mathscr{E}_{u}^{\prime}(r)=2\int_{\partial B_{r}}\mu^{-1}\langle\mathbb{A}\nu,\nabla u\rangle^{2}{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}+E_{r},

(7.6)

with

E_{r}\geq-C\mathscr{E}_{u}(r)+\frac{n-1}{r}\mathscr{E}_{u}(r).

Proof.

By the coarea formula and
[
17
, Lemma 3.4]
applied to the Lipschitz vector field

{\bf F}(x):=\frac{\mathbb{A}(x)x}{r\mu(x)}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathscr{E}_{u}^{\prime}(r)=&\int_{\partial B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}\\
=&2\int_{\partial B_{r}}\mu^{-1}\langle\mathbb{A}\nu,\nabla u\rangle^{2}{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\mu^{-1}\nabla\mathbb{A}\colon\mathbb{A}x\otimes\nabla u\otimes\nabla u{\rm d}x\\
&+\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\textup{div}\left(\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A}x\right){\rm d}x-\frac{2}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla^{T}\left(\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A}x\right)\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x\\
=:&2\int_{\partial B_{r}}\mu^{-1}\langle\mathbb{A}\nu,\nabla u\rangle^{2}{\rm d}{\mathcal{H}}^{n}+R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}.\end{split}

(7.7)

We now estimate the
R_{i}
’s. We start with
R_{1}
. By using the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbb{A}
,
(
7.1
) and (H2) we get

\displaystyle|R_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}\left|\mu^{-1}\partial_{i}a_{j,l}a_{i,k}x_{k}\;\partial_{j}u\;\partial_{l}u\right|{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-1}\int_{B_{r}}\sum_{i,j,k,l}|\partial_{i}a_{j,l}||a_{i,k}\partial_{j}u\partial_{l}u|{\rm d}x\leq C\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x=C\mathscr{E}_{u}(r).

(7.8)

By computing explicitly the divergence,
R_{2}
rewrites as

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\sum_{i,j}^{n+1}\partial_{i}\left(\mu^{-1}a_{ij}x_{j}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\sum_{i,j}^{n+1}\partial_{i}(\mu^{-1}a_{ij})x_{j}{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\mu^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq-C\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle\mu^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb{A}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq-C\mathscr{E}_{u}(r)+\frac{n+1}{r}\mathscr{E}_{u}(r),

(7.9)

where we used the Lipschitz continuity of
\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A}
,
(
7.1
)
,
and (
7.5
). Analogously, for
R_{3}
we have

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,{(\nabla^{T}(\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A})x)\nabla u}\rangle{\rm d}x-\frac{2}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A}\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq-C\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x-\frac{2}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\left(\mu^{-1}\mathbb{A}-{\rm Id}\right)\nabla u\rangle-\frac{2}{r}\int_{B_{r}}\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq-C\mathscr{E}_{u}(r)-\frac{2}{r}\mathscr{E}_{u}(r),

(7.10)

where we used the Lipschitz continuity of

Proposition 7.4

Let

Proposition 7.5

Proposition 7.7

\displaystyle H_{u}(r)

7.1.
Free boundary of nonlinear thin obstacle problems

The results proven above can be applied to the case of nonlinear thin obstacle problems studied in
[
1
]
, i.e. to the class of problems

\min_{u\in\mathcal{A}}\;\int_{B_{1}^{+}}f\left(\nabla u\right){\rm d}x,

(7.18)

where the energy density
f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and is of the form

f(p)=h(|p|)

(7.19)

for every
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and the matrix
D_{p}^{2}f(p)
is uniformly coercive
on compact subsets, i.e. fulfills the following local ellipticity condition: for every

M>0
there exists
\lambda=\lambda(M)>0
such that

\langle D_{p}^{2}f(p)\xi,\xi\rangle\geq\lambda|\xi|^{2}

(7.20)

for every
|p|\leq M
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

As shown in
[
1
]
, if the function
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad h^{\prime\prime}(t)=1+O(t)\qquad\textup{for }t\to 0^{+},

then the solutions to the variational problem in (
7.18
) are

C^{1,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1})
.
Here we show that, if in addition

h^{\prime\prime}(t)=1+O(t^{2})\qquad\textup{for }t\to 0^{+},

(7.21)

and, for the sake of simplicity,
h\in C^{\infty}
,
then we may apply Theorem
1.2
to infer all the results on
the free boundary regularity in that statement.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
u\in W^{1,\infty}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+})
be a solution to (
7.18
) in
\mathcal{A}
with
h\in C^{\infty}
under the assumptions (
7.19
), (
7.20
) and (
7.21
),
then
u\in C^{1,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1})
, and the free boundary
\Gamma(u)

is
(n-1)
-rectifiable and its Minkowski content is locally finite, i.e. for every
K\subset\subset B_{1}^{\prime}
there exists a constant
C(K)>0
such that

\mathcal{L}^{n+1}\big(\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Gamma(u)\cap K)\big)\leq C(K)r^{2},

for every
r\in(0,1)
.

Moreover, there exists a set
\Sigma(u)\subset\Gamma(u)

with Hausdorff dimension at most
n-2
such that for every

x\in\Gamma(u)\setminus\Sigma(u)

N_{u}(x,0^{+})\in\{2m,2m-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2m+1\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\,.

Proof.

The solution to (
7.18
) is
C^{1,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{\textup{loc}}(B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1})

by
[
1
]
and by standard elliptic regularity
u\in C^{\infty}(B_{1}^{+})
(thanks to the simplifying assumption
h\in C^{\infty}
).
Moreover,
u
can be characterized as the weak solution to the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\textup{div}(\nabla_{p}f(\nabla u))=0\mbox{ in }B^{+}_{1}\\
&u\,\partial_{n+1}f(\nabla u)=0\mbox{ on }B^{\prime}_{1}\\
&-\partial_{n+1}f(\nabla u)\geq 0\mbox{ on }B^{\prime}_{1}\\
&u\geq 0\mbox{ on }B^{\prime}_{1}\\
&u=g\mbox{ on }(\partial B_{1})^{+}\end{cases}.

(7.22)

In particular, we deduce from the first equation in (
7.22
) that for every
\phi\in C^{1}(B_{1}^{+})
with support non-intersecting
(\partial B_{1})^{+}

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\langle\nabla_{p}f(\nabla u),\nabla\phi(x)\rangle\,dx=0\,.

(7.23)

We can write assumption (
7.21
) in the form
h^{\prime\prime}(t)=1+\omega(t)
with

|\omega(t)|\leq C\,t^{2}
for
t
sufficiently small. Integrating, we infer that

h^{\prime}(t)=t\big(1+\tilde{\omega}(t)\big)\,,\qquad\tilde{\omega}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(s)\,ds\,,

and
\tilde{\omega}\in C^{1}

with
\tilde{\omega}(0)=\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(0)=0
and

\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\omega(s)\,ds+\frac{1}{t}\,\omega(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(t)|\leq C\,t,

for
t
sufficiently small. The first variations (
7.23
) reads then for every

\phi\in C^{1}(B_{1}^{+})
with support non-intersecting
(\partial B_{1})^{+}

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\langle\big(1+\tilde{\omega}(|\nabla u(x)|)\big)\nabla u(x),\nabla\phi(x)\rangle\,dx=0\,,

which is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the linear thin obstacle problem driven by the quadratic energy

\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\theta(x)\,|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx,\qquad\theta(x):=1+\tilde{\omega}(|\nabla u(x)|).

Note that
1\leq\theta(x)\leq 1+C|\nabla u(x)|
, therefore
\theta
is
locally bounded on
B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}
. Thus, if we prove that the function

\theta
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}
,
we can apply Theorem
1.2
and conclude all the results about the
structure of
\Gamma(u)
.

To this aim, we notice that for nontrivial solutions
u
in
B_{1}^{+}
we have

\nabla\theta(x)=\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(|\nabla u(x)|)\,D^{2}u(x)\frac{\nabla u(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|}\quad\textup{if $|\nabla u(x)|\neq 0$.}

Extend for simplicity
u
by even reflection to the whole
B_{1}
(without renaming the function
u
) and let
d:B_{1}\to[0,\infty)
be the distance from the free boundary
\Gamma(u)
.
By (
7.22
) the function
u
satisfies the nonlinear elliptic equation

\textup{div}\left(\nabla_{p}f(\nabla u)\right)=0\qquad\mbox{ on }B_{1}\setminus\{(x^{\prime},0):u(x^{\prime},0)=0\}\,,

and therefore the following classical elliptic estimates hold locally in
B_{1}\setminus\{(x^{\prime},0):u(x^{\prime},0)=0\}
:

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\,d(x)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{d(x)}(x))}\leq C\,d(x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

\displaystyle|D^{2}u(x)|\leq C\,d(x)^{-2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{d(x)}(x))}\leq C\,d(x)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

Recalling that by assumption

\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(|\nabla u(x)|)\leq C|\nabla u(x)|\,,

we then conclude for points outside the contact set, i.e.
B_{1}\setminus\{(x^{\prime},0):u(x^{\prime},0)=0\}
, that

|\nabla\theta(x)|\leq\frac{|\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(|\nabla u(x)|)|}{|\nabla u(x)|}\,{|D^{2}u(x)|\,|\nabla u(x)|}\leq Cd(x)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,d(x)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\leq C\,.

(7.24)

Moreover, if
x\in\Gamma(u)
then
|\nabla u(x)|=0
, so that
for every
y\in B_{1}^{+}\cup B_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle|\theta(x)-\theta(y)|

\displaystyle=|\tilde{\omega}(|\nabla u(y)|)|\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(t|\nabla u(y)|)||\nabla u(y)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla u(y)|^{2}\leq C|x-y|,

using the optimal regularity of
u
.
Finally, if
x
belongs to the relative interior of
\{(x^{\prime},0):u(x^{\prime},0)=0\}
in
B^{\prime}_{1}
, we use the odd riflection across the hyperplane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
as in
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
to infer that (
7.24
) holds as well.

In conclusion,
\theta
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
B_{1}^{+}\cup B^{\prime}_{1}
.

∎

Appendix A
Order of contact

We introduce the definition of lower and upper order of contact at zero in a point.

Definition A.1
.

Let
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
x_{0}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the lower and upper orders of contact with
0
of
v
at
x_{0}
are
defined respectively as

\displaystyle\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0}):=\sup\Big\{\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}:\,\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{H_{v}(x_{0},\rho)}{\rho^{n+2\vartheta}}<\infty\Big\},

(A.1)

\displaystyle\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0}):=\inf\Big\{\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}:\,\liminf_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{H_{v}(x_{0},\rho)}{\rho^{n+2\vartheta}}>0\Big\}\,.

(A.2)

Few elementary properties of
\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
and
\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})

are resumed in the ensuing list: for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have

(1)

-\infty\leq\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})\leq\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})\leq\infty
,

(2)

\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\sup\Big\{\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}:\,\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{H_{u}(x_{0},\rho)}{\rho^{n+2\vartheta}}=0\Big\}\,,

(3)

\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\inf\Big\{\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}:\,\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{H_{u}(x_{0},\rho)}{\rho^{n+2\vartheta}}=\infty\Big\}\,.

Additionally, we compare the latter notions with those used by Koch, Rüland and Shi
[
28
]
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
u\in\mathscr{A}
be a solution to (
1.1
) under the hypotheses (H1) and (H2), and
x_{0}\in\Gamma(u)
with

. Then, on setting
A_{\rho}(x_{0}):=B_{\rho}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\nicefrac{{\rho}}{{2}}}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\liminf_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\Big(\fint_{A_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{2}{\rm d}x\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\ln\rho}=:\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})\,,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\limsup_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\frac{\ln\Big(\fint_{A_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{2}{\rm d}x\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}{\ln\rho}=:\overline{\kappa}(x_{0})\,.

(A.4)

Proof.

We shall only prove the equality in (
A.3
), the other in (
A.4
) being completely analogous.
We first note that by Lemma

\frac{1}{2}\,\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{-1}\,\rho\,H_{u}(x_{0},\rho)\leq\int_{A_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:L2 vs H}}}{{\leq}}C\,\rho\,H_{u}(x_{0},\rho),

(A.5)

for points on the free boundary with
\mathbb{A}(x_{0})={\rm Id}
.
Assume
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
, then for every
{\varepsilon}>0
there are
\rho_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)

and
\rho_{j}\downarrow 0
such that for all

\fint_{A_{\rho}(x_{0})}u^{2}{\rm d}x\leq\rho^{2(\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})-{\varepsilon})}\,,

and for all

\fint_{A_{\rho_{j}}(x_{0})}u^{2}{\rm d}x\geq\rho_{j}^{2(\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})+{\varepsilon})}\,.

From the former inequality and (
A.5
)
we infer that
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})-{\varepsilon}\leq\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
, and thus

\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})\leq\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
.
Instead, from the latter inequality and (
A.5
) we deduce that

\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})+2{\varepsilon}>\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
, thus
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})\geq\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
.
Therefore,
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})=\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})
.

If
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})=-\infty
then there is
\rho_{j}\downarrow 0
such that for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is

j_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all

\fint_{A_{\rho_{j}}(x_{0})}u^{2}{\rm d}x\geq\rho_{j}^{-2i}\,,

and thus

, in turn implying
\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=-\infty
.

If
\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})=\infty
then for every

there is
\rho_{i}\in(0,1)

from which we conclude that

.

In conclusion,
\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\underline{\kappa}(x_{0})
in all possible instances.

For solutions to the thin obstacle problem the points with finite frequency are points with finite order of contact.

Lemma A.3

Let

Moreover, if
\displaystyle{\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}I_{u}(x_{0},r)}<\infty
then the limsup is actually a limit and

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}I_{u}(x_{0},r)=\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})\in[\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}},\infty)\,.

(A.7)

Proof.

Without loss of generality we take
x_{0}=\underline{0}
, and set
\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+}):=\displaystyle{\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}I_{u}(\underline{0},r)}
.

We start off proving (
A.6
). Without loss of generality we assume
\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})<\infty
, the inequality being trivial otherwise. Hence, the doubling of both
H_{u}(\underline{0},\cdot)
and
D_{u}(\underline{0},\cdot)
hold thanks to Proposition
3.3
.
Then, we use the equality in (
3.22
), namely

\displaystyle H_{u}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{n}{r}\,H_{u}(r)+2G_{u}(r)\,,

and (
3.14
) with
x=x_{0}=\underline{0}
and
\kappa=0
, to infer that

\displaystyle\Big|H_{u}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{n}{r}\,H_{u}(r)-2D_{u}(r)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\alpha}\Big(D_{u}(r)+\frac{1}{r^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}H_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(r)D_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(r)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\alpha}D_{u}(r)\Big(1+I_{u}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(r)\Big)\,,

in turn implying

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\ln\Big(\frac{H_{u}(r)}{r^{n}}\Big)-\frac{2}{r}I_{u}(r)\Big|\leq Cr^{\alpha-1}I_{u}(r)(1+I_{u}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(r))=Cr^{\alpha-1}(I_{u}(r)+I_{u}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(r))\,.

(A.8)

Then, for every
{\varepsilon}>0
there is

such that
I_{u}(r)\leq\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})+{\varepsilon}
for every

We use (

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\ln\Big(\frac{H_{u}(r)}{r^{n}}\Big)\leq\frac{2}{r}(\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})+{\varepsilon})+Cr^{\alpha-1}\,.

Hence, by direct integration we get that for all
0<r<s<r_{\varepsilon}

0<\frac{H_{u}(s)}{s^{n+2(\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})+{\varepsilon})}}e^{-\frac{C}{\alpha}s^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{H_{u}(r)}{r^{n+2(\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})+{\varepsilon})}}e^{-\frac{C}{\alpha}r^{\alpha}}\,.

From this and the very definition of
\overline{\vartheta}(\underline{0})
in (
A.2
) we have

\overline{\vartheta}(\underline{0})\leq\overline{I_{u}}(0^{+})+{\varepsilon}
for every
{\varepsilon}>0
, which implies (
A.6
).

In oder to prove (
A.7
) we combine the results in (
A.6
)
with those in Proposition
3.9
(cf. (
3.25
)) to infer that the
\limsup
of the frequency is actually a limit, so that the latter rewrites as

(A.9)

Therefore, arguing as above, by the inequality in (
3.32

3.11
and
(
A.8

from which we conclude by integration that for all
0<r<s<r_{\varepsilon}

Hence, we deduce that

{\varepsilon}>0
,

it is established there that for the solutions to the variable coefficients thin obstacle problem:

\underline{\vartheta}(x_{0})=\overline{\vartheta}(x_{0})

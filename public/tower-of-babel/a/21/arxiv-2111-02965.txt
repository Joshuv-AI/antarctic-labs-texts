The stable rank of ℤ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Z}[x] is 3 3
Luc Guyot

Abstract
. Let ℤ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Z}[x] be the ring of univariate polynomials over ℤ \mathbb{Z} and denote by sr ⁡ ( ℤ ⁡ [ x ] ) \operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x]) its stable rank in the sense of Bass. Grunewald, Mennicke and Vaserstein proved that sr ⁡ ( ℤ ⁡ [ x ] ) = 3 . \operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])=3. As the inequality sr ⁡ ( ℤ ⁡ [ x ] ) ≤ 3 \operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\leq 3 follows immediately from Bass’s stable range theorem, the above identity is equivalent to the existence of a non-stable unimodular row of size 3 3 . This note addresses minor errors found in the existing proof of the latter fact in the literature. Using the same methods, we show that the unimodular row ( 3 , x + 1 , x 2 + 16 ) (3,x+1,x^{2}+16) is not stable.

Rings are assumed to be commutative and unital. Let
R
be a ring.
A row
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in R^{n}
is
unimodular
if
\sum_{i}Rr_{i}=R
. We denote by
\operatorname{Um}_{n}(R)
the set of unimodular rows of size
n
.
A row
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n+1})\in\operatorname{Um}_{n+1}(R)
(
n>0
) is
stable
if there is

(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in R^{n}
such that

(r_{1}+s_{1}r_{n+1},\dots,r_{n}+s_{n}r_{n+1})

belongs to
\operatorname{Um}_{n}(R)
.
An integer
n>0
lies in the
stable range
of
R
if every row in
\operatorname{Um}_{n+1}(R)
is stable.
If
n
lies in the stable range of
R
, then so does
k
for every
k>n

[
MR87
, Lemma 11.3.3]
.
The
Bass stable rank
\operatorname{sr}(R)
of
R
is the least integer in the stable range of
R
.
This rank is key in several direct sum cancellation results
[
Mag02
, Theorems 4.26 and 4.28]
;
stably free
R
-modules of stably free rank at least
\operatorname{sr}(R)
are free
[
Mag02
, Corollary 4.23]
.
Bass’s stable rank is also used to simplify the computation of the algebraic
K
-groups
K_{n}(R)
through
surjectivity and injectivity theorems. For instance, the value of
\operatorname{sr}(R)
is an upper bound on the order of
the matrices than can be used to represent elements of
K_{1}(R)

[
Mag02
, Theorem 10.3]
.

Grunewald, Mennicke and Vaserstein proved the following:

Theorem A
.

(
[
GMV94
, Proposition 1.9]
)

\operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])=3
.

The inequality
\operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\leq 3
is provided by Bass’s upper bound on the stable rank of finite-dimensional rings.
Indeed, we have
\operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\leq\dim_{\text{Krull}}(\mathbb{Z}[x])+1
by Bass’s stable range theorem
[
MR87
, Corollary 6.7.4]
and

\dim_{\text{Krull}}(\mathbb{Z}[x])=2

[
MR87
, Proposition 6.5.4]
.

The authors of
[
GMV94
, Proposition 1.9]
claimed that the row
(21+4x,12,x^{2}+20)
is unimodular but not stable,
which would therefore establish Theorem
A
.
However, it is unknown if the previous row is stable: because of a typographical error, one should read

(21+2x,12,x^{2}+20)
instead of
(21+4x,12,x^{2}+20)
.
Besides
[
GMV94
, Proof of Proposition 1.9, page 191]

uses the false statement “
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(\mathcal{O},f\mathcal{O})\neq 1
” where
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{-5}
and
f=2
, see Remark
3.3
.
Thus none of the previous unimodular rows has been proven to be unstable.

In Section
2
, we present a proof of Theorem
A
which addresses these shortcomings.
Our proof follows closely the lines of the original.
It consists in exhibiting a quotient
R
of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)\neq 1
.This suffices to show that
\operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])>2
. Indeed, if
\operatorname{sr}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\leq 2
, then the natural map

\operatorname{SK}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[x])\rightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
would be surjective (Corollary
1.6
below),
which is impossible as
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[x])=1

[
BHS64
, Theorem 1]
.

In Section
3
, we show in addition:

Proposition B
.

The unimodular row

(3,x+1,x^{2}+16)

of
\mathbb{Z}[x]
is not stable.

Bass’s stable rank, and surjective homomorphisms

In this section, we prove Corollary

We also introduce the relative special Whitehead group
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)
, the group
\operatorname{K}_{2}(R)
and an exact sequence,
called the
relative sequence
, which binds these groups.
This sequence is used to show the existence of an isomorphism
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)\simeq\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)

1.1.
Bass’s stable rank

Recall that rings are supposed to be commutative and unital.
The Bass stable rank can be characterized in terms of a lifting property for unimodular rows of quotient rings.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq n
.

(ii)

For every ideal
I\subseteq R
, the map
\operatorname{Um}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Um}_{n}(R/I)
sending
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
to
(r_{1}+I,\dots,r_{n}+I)
is surjective.

The proof is straightforward, hence left to the reader.
Specializing
n
to
1
, we obtain:

Proposition 1.2
.

(
[
EO67
, Lemma 6.1]
)
Let
R
be a ring. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 1
.

(ii)

For every ideal
I\subseteq R
, the natural map
R^{\times}\rightarrow(R/I)^{\times}
is surjective.

The condition
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 1
received special attention in
[
EO67
]
and
[
Vas84
]
.
We shall see with Proposition
1.4
below that the condition
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
can also be interpreted in terms
of surjective group homomorphisms.
We denote by
\operatorname{Jac}(R)
, the
Jacobson radical of
R
, that is, the intersection of the maximal ideals of
R
.
We record the following proposition for later use.

Proposition 1.3
.

[
MR87
, Proposition 11.3.11 and Lemma 11.4.6]

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. Then we have

\operatorname{sr}(R/I)\leq\operatorname{sr}(R)
and equality holds if
I\subseteq\operatorname{Jac}(R)
.

1.2.
Rings of stable rank at most
2

Rings of stable rank at most
2
enjoy the following characterization.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
R
be a ring. Then then following are equivalent:

(i)

\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
.

(ii)

The natural map
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\overline{R})
is surjective for every quotient

\overline{R}
of
R
.

(iii)

The natural map
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\overline{R})
is surjective for every quotient

\overline{R}
of
R
and every
n\geq 2
.

Our proof of Proposition
1.4
relies on

Lemma 1.5
.

(
[
GMV94
, Lemma 6.2]
,
[
EO67
, Corollary 8.3]
)
Let
R
be a ring. Let
(a,b,c)\in\operatorname{Um}_{3}(R)
and denote by
\overline{r}
the image of
r\in R

in
R/Rc
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The row
(a,b,c)
is stable.

(ii)

Every matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\overline{a}&\overline{b}\\
\overline{d}&\overline{e}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R/Rc)
has a lift in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Let
A=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{a}&\overline{b}\\
\overline{d}&\overline{e}\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R/Rc)
.
Since
(a,b,c)
is stable, we can find
(r,s)\in\operatorname{Um}_{2}(R)
such that
\overline{r}=\overline{a}
and
\overline{s}=\overline{b}
.
Let
u,v\in R
be such that
\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\
u&v\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
.
Then

\begin{pmatrix}\overline{a}&\overline{b}\\
\overline{u}&\overline{v}\end{pmatrix}A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\overline{w}&1\end{pmatrix}
for some
w\in R
. Therefore

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
w&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\
u&v\end{pmatrix}
is a lift of
A
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. By assumption, we can find
(r,s)\in\operatorname{Um}_{2}(R)
such that
\overline{r}=\overline{a}
and
\overline{s}=\overline{b}
, i.e., we have

r=a+\lambda c
and
s=b+\mu c
for some
\lambda,\mu\in R
.
It follows immediately that
(a,b,c)
is stable.
∎

Let us denote by
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R)
the subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
generated by the
elementary matrices
, i.e.,
the matrices which differ from the identity matrix by a single off-diagonal entry.

Proof of Proposition
1.4
.

Clearly, we have
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
. The implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
is given by Lemma
1.5
.
Hence it only remains to show that
(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

Assume that
(i)
holds and let
n\geq 2
,
A\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\overline{R})
where
\overline{R}=R/I
for some ideal
I
of
R
.
If
n=2
, then it follows from Lemma
1.5
that
A
has a lift in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
.

Indeed, write
A=\begin{pmatrix}a+I&b+I\\
d+I&e+I\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\overline{R})
.
Putting
c:=1-(ae-bd)\in I
, then
(a,b,c)\in\operatorname{Um}_{3}(R)
and

\widetilde{A}=\begin{pmatrix}a+Rc&b+Rc\\
d+Rc&e+Rc\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R/Rc)
.
By hypothesis, we have
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
, so that Lemma
1.5
applies and
provides us with a lift of
\widetilde{A}
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
which is also a lift of
A
.

We can now assume that
n>2
and proceed by induction on
n
.
Since
\operatorname{sr}(\overline{R})\leq 2
by Proposition
1.3
, and because the first row of
A
is unimodular, we can find
E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{E}_{n}(\overline{R})

such that
A=E\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&A^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}E^{\prime}
with
A^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(\overline{R})
.
By induction hypothesis, the matrix
A^{\prime}
has a lift in
\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(R)
.
Clearly, the matrices
E
and
E^{\prime}
lift to
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
. Thus
A
has a lift in
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
.
∎

The next result refers to the special Whitehead group
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
of a ring
R
.
We define this group as follows.
Let
\operatorname{E}(R)\Doteq\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{E}_{n}(R)
and
\operatorname{SL}(R)\Doteq\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
be the ascending unions for which
the embeddings
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{E}_{n+1}(R)
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}(R)
are defined
through
A\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
.
Then
\operatorname{E}(R)
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(R)

[
Mag02
, Whitehead Lemma 9.7]
and the
special Whitehead group

\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
of
R
is the quotient group
\operatorname{SL}(R)/\operatorname{E}(R)
.

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Proposition
1.4
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
R
be a ring satisfying
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
. Let
\overline{R}
be a quotient of
R
.
Then the natural map
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{1}(\overline{R})
is surjective.

Following
[
Coh66
]
, we call a ring
R
a
\operatorname{GE}_{2}
-ring
if
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)=\operatorname{E}_{2}(R)
.

The next corollaries are of independent interest.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
R
be a
\operatorname{GE}_{2}
-ring satisfying
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
.
Then every quotient of
R
is a
\operatorname{GE}_{2}
-ring.

Proof.

Let
\overline{R}
be a quotient of
R
and let
A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\overline{R})
. By Proposition
1.4
, the matrix
A
is the image of some matrix in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
.
Since
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)=\operatorname{E}_{2}(R)
by assumption, we infer that
A\in\operatorname{E}_{2}(R)
.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

Corollary
1.7
provides a straightforward proof of
[

, Theorem A]
.

Corollary 1.9
.

[

, Theorem 3.6]

Let

be a ring such that any proper quotient of

has stable rank

. Then
\operatorname{sr}(R)\leq 2
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that rings of stable rank

are
\operatorname{GE}_{2}
-rings. The result follows by

.
∎

1.3.
The groups
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)
,
\operatorname{K}_{2}(R)
and the relative sequence

In this section, we define the relative special Whitehead group
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)
for
I
an ideal of a ring
R
and the group
\operatorname{K}_{2}(R)
from algebraic K-theory.
We describe then the
relative sequence
, an exact sequence relating them to one another.
This sequence will come in handy for the K-theoretical computations of Section
2
.

Let
R
be a ring. Let
n\geq 2
and denote by
I_{n}
the
n\times n
identify matrix.
For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, let
\epsilon_{ij}
be the
n\times n
matrix whose
(i,j)
entry is
1
and whose other entries are zero.
For
a\in R
and
i\neq j
, we set
e_{ij}(a)=I_{n}+a\epsilon_{ij}
and call any such matrix an
elementary matrix
.

Recall that
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R)
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
generated by the elementary matrices.
Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. We denote by
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R,I)
the normal subgroup of
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R)

which is normally generated by the matrices
e_{ij}(a)
with
a\in I
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.
We denote by
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R,I)
the kernel of the natural map
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R/I)
.

The
relative special Whitehead group
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)
is defined as follows.
Let
\operatorname{E}(R,I)\Doteq\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{E}_{n}(R,I)
and
\operatorname{SL}(R,I)\Doteq\bigcup_{n}\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R,I)
be the ascending unions for which
the embeddings
\operatorname{E}_{n}(R,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{E}_{n+1}(R,I)
and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}(R,I)
are defined
through
A\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
.
Then
\operatorname{E}(R,I)
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(R,I)

[
Mag02
, Relative Whitehead Lemma 11.1]

and
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)\Doteq\operatorname{SL}(R,I)/\operatorname{E}(R,I)
.

We now turn to the definition of
\operatorname{K}_{2}(R)
. The
Steinberg group

\operatorname{St}_{n}(R)

is the group with generators
x_{ij}(r)
, with
i\neq j
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
r\in R
subject to the defining relations:

(1)

\begin{split}x_{ij}(r)x_{ij}(s)=x_{ij}(r+s),\\
[x_{ij}(r),x_{kl}(s)]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,\text{if }i\neq l,j\neq k,\\
x_{il}(rs),\text{ if }i\neq l,j=k.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

Removing the restrictions
i\leq n
and
j\leq n
on the generators
x_{ij}(r)
, the same presentation defines the
Steinberg group

\operatorname{St}(R)
.
Because the elementary matrices
e_{ij}(r)
obey the above standard relations and generate
\operatorname{E}(R)
,
the map
x_{ij}(r)\mapsto e_{ij}(r)
induces a surjective group homomorphism

\operatorname{St}(R)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{E}(R)
. The kernel of this homomorphism is
\operatorname{K}_{2}(R)
.

Finally, we introduce the so-called
relative sequence
:

(2)

\operatorname{K}_{2}(R/I)\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R/I)

where the second and third arrows are induced respectively by the inclusion
\operatorname{SL}(R,I)\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(R)
and the natural map
\operatorname{SL}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}(R/I)
.
For the connecting homomorphism
\partial_{1}
,
we refer the reader to
[
Mag02
, Theorem 13.20 and Example 13.22]
where this sequence is shown to be exact.

.

[

\operatorname{SK}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[x])=1

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a ring

R/I\simeq\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}

(3)

\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R,I)\rightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)

In the exact sequence (

Proof of Proposition

We shall prove that the unimodular row

[
GMV94

\operatorname{SL}_{2}(R)
that defines a
non-trivial element of
\operatorname{SK}_{1}(R)
for a suitable quotient
R

\mathbb{Z}[x]

[
BMS67

\operatorname{SK}_{1}(S)

the ring of integers of a totally imaginary number field, in terms of power residue symbols.
The following definitions are required to state the latter theorem.

Let

\mu_{m}
the group of
m

Let
S

.
For

\mathfrak{a}

\mathfrak{p}
ranges in the set of prime ideals of

\mathfrak{a}
and where
\left(\frac{b}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{m}
is the unique element of
\mu_{m}
satisfying

Theorem 3.1
.

[
BMS67
, Theorems 3.6 and 4.1]

Let
S

Let

denote the number of roots of unity in
S
. If
I

S
, define the divisor

of
m

\operatorname{ord}_{p}(r)=j_{p}(I)
where

m

is the nearest integer in the interval

PRIMALITY OF WEAKLY CONNECTED COLLECTIONS OF CELLS AND WEAKLY CLOSED PATH POLYOMINOES
CARMELO CISTO, FRANCESCO NAVARRA, ROSANNA UTANO

Abstract
. In this paper we study the primality of weakly connected collections of cells, showing that the ideal generated by inner 2-minors attached to a weakly connected and simple collection of cells is the toric ideal of the edge ring of a weakly chordal bipartite graph. As an application of this result we characterize the primality of the polyomino ideals of weakly closed paths, a new class of non simple polyominoes.

1.
Introduction

The study of the ideals of
t
-minors of an
m\times n
matrix of indeterminates is a central topic in Commutative Algebra. The determinantal ideals are studied in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, the ideals of adjacent 2-minors are studied in
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
13
]
as well as the ideals generated by an arbitrary set
of
2
-minors in a
2\times n
matrix in
[
8
]
. The class of polyomino ideals generalizes the class of the ideals generated by 2-minors of
m\times n
matrices of indeterminates. Roughly speaking, polyominoes are finite collections of squares of the same size joined edge to edge. In
[
19
]
A.A. Qureshi establishes a connection of polyominoes to Commutative Algebra, attaching to a polyomino
\mathcal{P}
the ideal generated by all inner 2-minors of
\mathcal{P}
. This ideal is called the
polyomino ideal
of
\mathcal{P}
and it is denoted by
I_{\mathcal{P}}
. We say that a polyomino
\mathcal{P}
is prime if the polyomino ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is prime. One of the most exciting challenges is to characterize the primality of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
depending upon the shape of
\mathcal{P}
. In
[
10
]
and in
[
20
]
it is proved that the simple polyominoes, which are roughly speaking the polyominoes without holes, are prime. Nowadays the study is applied to multiply connected polyominoes, which are polyominoes with one or more holes. In
[
12
]
and
[
22
]
, the authors prove that the polyominoes obtained by removing a convex polyomino from a rectangle in
{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
are prime. In
[
14
]
the authors introduce a very useful tool to provide a characterization of prime polyominoes. They define a particular sequence of inner intervals of
\mathcal{P}
, called a
zig-zag walk
, and they prove that if
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is prime then
\mathcal{P}
does not contain zig-zag walks. Using a computational method, they show that for polyominoes consisting of at most fourteen cells the non-existence of zig-zag walks in
\mathcal{P}
is a sufficient condition in order for
I_{\mathcal{P}}
to be prime. Therefore, they conjecture that non-existence of zig-zag walks in a polyomino characterizes its primality. In order to support this conjecture, in
[
5
]
the authors introduce a new class of polyominoes, called closed paths, and they prove that having no zig-zag walks is a necessary and sufficient condition for their primality. Moreover they describe some classes of prime polyominoes, which can be considered as a generalization of closed paths. In
[
15
]
the authors give some conditions so that the set of generators of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
forms a reduced Gröbner basis with respect to some suitable degree reverse lexicographic monomial orders and they have proved that in these cases the polyomino is prime. Using this method, they prove the primality of two classes of thin polyominoes, which are polyominoes not containing the square tetromino. In addition, in
[
21
]
the Hilbert series of simple thin polyominoes is studied.

In this paper we study the primality of the weakly connected collections of cells and of a new class of non-simple polyominoes, called
weakly closed paths
. In Section
2
we introduce the preliminary notions and some useful tools. Inspired by
[
20
]
, in Section
3
we define a bipartite graph
G(\mathcal{P})
attached to a weakly connected and simple collection
\mathcal{P}
of cells of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, we prove that
G(\mathcal{P})
is weakly chordal and that the ideal generated by inner 2-minors of
\mathcal{P}
is the toric ideal attached to the edge ring of
G(\mathcal{P})
. This result generalizes Theorem 3.10 of
[
20
]
and we conjecture that absence of zig-zag walks in a weakly connected collection of cells characterizes its primality. Applying the previous result and using the same demonstrative approach introduced in
[
5
]
, in Section
4
we obtain that the new class of multiply connected polyominoes, called
weakly closed paths
, satisfies the conjecture, which states that a polyomino ideal is prime if and only if the polyomino does not contain zig-zag walks.

Remark 3.1
.

Arguing as in Section 1 of

Let

Definition 4.1
.

A finite non-empty collection of cells

The difference between a closed path, introduced in

These polyominoes, as well as the closed paths, are particular thin polyominoes, which are polyominoes not containing the square tetromino.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a non-simple polyomino with a unique hole
\mathcal{H}
. Let

be the set of the maximal edge intervals of
\mathcal{P}
and
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be the set of the maximal horizontal edge intervals of
\mathcal{P}
. Let
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{h_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be the set of the variables associated respectively to

and
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the hole of
\mathcal{P}
and

be another variable. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a subset of

and we define the following map:

\displaystyle\alpha:V(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow K[\{v_{i},h_{j},w\}:i\in I,j\in J]

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\longmapsto v_{i}h_{j}w^{k}

where
a\in V_{i}\cap H_{j}
,

if

, and

if

. The toric ring, denoted by

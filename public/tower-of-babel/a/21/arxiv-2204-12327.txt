L p L^{p} -boundedness of Pseudo differential operators on rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type Thanks: Second author is supported by a Ph.D fellowship of the CSIR(India).
Sanjoy Pusti, Tapendu Rana

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study L p L^{p} -boundedness property of the pseudo differential operator associated with a symbol, on rank one Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type, where the symbol satisfies Hörmander-type conditions near infinity.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 43A85; Secondary 22E30

1.
Introduction

A differential operator
p(x,D)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}
can be represented as

p(x,D)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}p(x,\xi)\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\,d\xi,

where
p(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{F}f
is the Fourier transform of
f
defined by

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx.

Let
a(x,\xi)
be a general function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
not necessarily a polynomial in the
\xi
variable. Consider the operator
a(x,D)
defined by

a(x,D)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\,d\xi.

This operator
a(x,D)
is called pseudo differential operator associated with the ‘symbol’
a(x,\xi)
. Pseudo differential operators have many applications in the theory of partial differential operators. If the symbol
a(x,\xi)
is independent in
x
variable, say
a(x,\xi)=m(\xi)
, then the associated operator

m(D)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}m(\xi)\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\,d\xi,

is called multiplier operator. Boundedness of the multiplier operator is well studied on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
m:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies the following Hörmander-Mihlin differential inequalities:

\left|\frac{d^{j}}{d\xi^{j}}m(\xi)\right|\leq A_{j}|\xi|^{-j},

(1.1)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and for all
0\leq j\leq[\frac{n}{2}]+1
. Then the multiplier operator
m(D)
is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),1<p<\infty
.

Now let
G
be a real rank one noncompact connected
semisimple Lie group with finite center. Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of G,
and let
\mathbb{X}=G/K
be the associated symmetric space.
Corresponding to a multiplier function
m
, the associated multiplier operator
T_{m}
on
\mathbb{X}
is defined by

T_{m}(f)(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{K}m(\lambda)\widetilde{f}(\lambda,k)e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda,\,\,g\in G,

(1.2)

where
\widetilde{f}
is the (Helgason) Fourier transform of
f
and
c(\lambda)
is the Harish-Chandra
c
-function. The Boundedness of the multiplier operator on the symmetric space has been studied by Clerc and Stein
[
5
]
, Stanton and Tomas
[
22
]
, Anker and Lohoué
[
2
]
, Taylor
[
25
]
, Anker
[
1
]
and Giulini, Mauceri, Meda
[
12
]
. Let us elaborate. The pioneer work was done in
[
5
]
. It was observed there that if
T_{m}
to be a continuous operator on
L^{p}(G/K)
, then
m
is necessarily holomorphic and bounded inside the strip
S_{p}^{\circ}
, where

S_{p}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\mid\,|\Im\lambda|\leq\left|\frac{2}{p}-1\right|\rho\right\}\quad\text{ for }p\in[1,\infty].

Conversely the authors in
[
5
]
also gave a sufficient conditions when
G
is complex. Similar results were obtained later when
G
is real rank one
[
22
]
and when
G
is a normal real form
[
2
]
. In 1990, Anker (
[
1
]
) improved and generalized previous results of
[
5
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
25
]
by proving the following multiplier theorem on
\mathbb{X}=G/K
:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Anker
)

Let
1<p<\infty
,
v=|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|
and
N=[v\dim\mathbb{X}]+1
. Let
m:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
extends to an even holomorphic function on
S_{p}^{\circ}
,
\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial\lambda^{i}}m\,(i=0,1,\cdots,N)
extends continuously to
S_{p}
and satisfies

\sup_{\lambda\in S_{p}}(1+|\lambda|)^{-i}\left|\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial\lambda^{i}}m(\lambda)\right|<\infty,

for
i=0,1,\cdots,N
.
Then the associated multiplier operator

T_{m}

is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{X})
.

For technical reasons, it was necessary for the author to assume regularity assumptions on the boundary. Nonetheless, Anker mentioned that it should be possible to allow
m
to have a singularity at the boundary points
\pm i\rho_{p}:=i\left|\frac{2}{p}-1\right|\rho
; and
m
will be still a
L^{p}
multiplier on
\mathbb{X}
. Later Ionescu
[
16
]
improved the theorem above by replacing the continuity of the multiplier
m
on the boundary with that of singularity condition at
\pm i\rho_{p}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
Ionescu
)

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
m:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
extends to an even holomorphic function on
S_{p}^{\circ}
and satisfies

\left|\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\lambda^{j}}m(\lambda)\right|\leq A_{j}\left[|\lambda+i\rho_{p}|^{-j}+|\lambda-i\rho_{p}|^{-j}\right],\lambda\in S_{p},

for
j=0,1,\cdots,\left[\frac{\dim\mathbb{X}+1}{2}\right]+1
.
Then the associated multiplier operator

T_{m}

is a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{X})
.

The boundedness of multiplier operator for fixed
K
-types on
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
has also been studied in
[
21
]
. Now one is naturally led to the following questions : what happens if we replace the multiplier
m(\lambda)
by a symbol
\sigma(x,\lambda)
? and if the corresponding operator associated with the symbol should be bounded, then what are the conditions
\sigma(x,\lambda)
should satisfy? In this paper we introduce and study the boundedness of pseudo differential operators in the setting of noncompact symmetric spaces of rank one. In preparation for the statement of our main results, let us recall the analogous theory of pseudo differential operator in Euclidean setting and also let us introduce some more notations:

Let
\mathcal{S}^{m}
be the symbols defined to be the set of all smooth functions
a:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies,

\left|{\partial^{\beta}_{x}}{\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}}a(x,\xi)\right|\leq{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\,{(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|}},

(1.3)

for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all multi indices
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Then we have the following theorem
[
23
]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
23
, Chap VI, Theorem 1]
)

Let
a\in\mathcal{S}^{0}
and
a(x,D)
be the pseudo differential operator associated with the symbol
a
. Then
a(x,D)
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to itself, for
1<p<\infty
.

Moreover, a genralized version of this theorem is true for a family of symbols
\{a_{\tau}:\tau\in\Lambda\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
\{a_{\tau}:\tau\in\Lambda\}
be a family of symbols in
\mathcal{S}^{0}
such that

\left|{\partial^{\beta}_{x}}{\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}}a_{\tau}(x,\xi)\right|\leq{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\,{(1+|\xi|)^{-|\alpha|}},

(1.4)

where
C_{\alpha,\beta}
does not depend on
\tau
. Then for
1<p<\infty
, there is a constant
C_{p}>0
independent of
\tau\in\Lambda
, such that

\|a_{\tau}(x,D)(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(1.5)

for all
\tau\in\Lambda
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof of this theorem (Theorem
1.4
) is exactly similar to Theorem
1.3
and therefore we omit the proof.

Remark 1.5
.

We would like to mention that for pseudo differential operator one cannot weaken the regularity assumption on the symbol
a(x,\xi)
, having singularity near
\xi=0
. Particularly, if the symbol
a
satisfies the following simpler looking condition

\left|{\partial^{\beta}_{x}}{\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}}a(x,\xi)\right|\leq{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\,{|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}},

(1.6)

then the corresponding operator may not be bounded. We refer the interested reader to the discussion
[
23
, page 267]
.

Now suppose
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a suitable function. We define the pseudo differential operator associated with the symbol
\sigma
by

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{K}\sigma(x,\lambda)\tilde{f}(\lambda,k)e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda\,dk,

(1.7)

for any smooth compactly supported function
f
on
\mathbb{X}
. Our aim of this paper is to study boundedness of the pseudo differential operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
(
\Psi
DO) on rank one symmetric spaces. We divide the study into two cases: when
p=2
and when
p\not=2
.
First we state our result for the case when
p\not=2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,\infty)
and
\mathbb{X}=G/K
. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that
for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
,
\lambda\mapsto\sigma(x,\lambda)
is an even holomorphic function on
S_{p}^{\circ}
and satisfies the differential inequalities:

\left|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{{\partial s}^{\beta}}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\sigma(xa_{s},\lambda)\right|\leq{C_{\alpha,\beta}}{(1+|\lambda|)^{-\alpha}},

(1.8)

for all
\alpha=0,1,\cdots,\left[\frac{\dim\mathbb{X}+1}{2}\right]+1;\beta=0,1,2
; for all
x\in G,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in S_{p}
. Then the operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{p}{(\mathbb{X})}
to itself.

Remark 1.7
.

(1)

Let us compare our result with the Euclidean case. As mentioned before in multiplier case, the holomorphic extension property of the symbol
\sigma
is a new and necessary condition for the pseudo differential operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
to be bounded on
L^{p}(G/K)
. It is the boundary behavior of
\sigma
which is similar to the Euclidean case. Here we have considered Hörmander-type conditions near infinity.

(2)

When
\sigma
is a multiplier, then one can write
\Psi_{\sigma}
as a convolution operator with a
K
-biinvariant kernel. This is the fundamental difference between the multiplier case and our situation. Particularly, in multiplier theory the author in
[
16
]
used a transference theorem, which is comparable to the Herz majorizing principle. For higher rank case the author used the same principle to estimate the
L^{p}
norm of multiplier operator (see
[
17
, Lemma 4.3]
). But in our case one cannot apply the methods above directly, to find the
L^{p}
norm estimate of
\Psi_{\sigma}
.

(3)

We also have established a connection between the
L^{p}
boundedness of (the local part of ) pseudo differential operator on
G/K
with bounded Euclidean pseudo differential operators (see
\lx@sectionsign

4
). From the Euclidean counterpart, we derived the derivative condition on the
``x"
variable of
\sigma(x,\lambda)
.

Next we consider the case when
p=2
. For
p=2
, the boundedness of the multiplier operator follows easily due to Plancherel theorem in both Euclidean spaces and symmetric spaces. But in the same way we could not prove
L^{2}
boundedness for the pseudo differential operator associated with a symbol. In this case we get only partial results, in the
K
-biinvariant setting. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that
x\mapsto\sigma(x,\lambda)
is a
K
-biinvariant function and let
\Psi_{\sigma}
be the “radial” pseudo differential operator associated with
\sigma
. That is,

\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma(x,\lambda)\widehat{f}(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(x)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda,

(1.9)

for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G//K)
, where
\widehat{f}
is the spherical Fourier transform of the
K
-biinvariant function
f
and
\phi_{\lambda}
is the elementary spherical function. Suppose
\dim\mathbb{X}=d=m_{1}+m_{2}+1
, then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that
x\mapsto\sigma(x,\lambda)
is
K
-biinvariant and

\sigma(x,\lambda)=\sigma(x,-\lambda)\text{ for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Assume that
\sigma
satisfies the following differential inequalities:

\left|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{{\partial t}^{\beta}}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{{\partial\lambda}^{\alpha}}\sigma(a_{t},\lambda)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\lambda|)^{-\alpha-d-1},

(1.10)

for all
\lambda,t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha,\beta=0,1,2
. Then the operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{2}(G//K)\cap L^{p}(G//K)
to
L^{2}{(G//K)}
for any
1\leq p<2
.

We improve this result in the following subcases:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\frac{3}{2}<p<2
and
\mathbb{X}=G/K
. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function such that
x\mapsto\sigma(x,\lambda)
is
K
-biinvariant and satisfies

\left|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{{\partial s}^{\beta}}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\sigma(xa_{s},\lambda)\right|\leq{C_{\alpha,\beta}}{(1+|\lambda|)^{-\alpha-d-1}},

for all
\alpha=0,1,\cdots,\dim\mathbb{X}+2
;
\beta=0,1,2
; for all
x\in G,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in S_{p}
. Then the operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{p}(G//K)
to
L^{2}{(G//K)}
.

We have the following drawbacks in the last two theorems:

(1)

We had to take more decay condition on the symbol
\sigma
than the required
\mathcal{S}^{0}
condition.

(2)

Also we did not get the required
L^{2}-L^{2}
bound.

All of these drawbacks are due to technical difficulties. We could not use direct semisimple theory to the proof; instead, we prove the theorems using boundedness of Euclidean pseudo differential operator. We overcome these drawbacks in complex symmetric spaces (in the
K
-biinvariant case). The following theorem is an analogue of
Calderón and Vaillancourt theorem
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
, where the assumption on the symbol is weaker than usual
S^{0}
condition.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be an arbitrary rank complex symmetric space.
Let
\sigma:\mathbb{X}\times\mathfrak{a}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a
C^{\infty}
function, such that
x\mapsto\sigma(x,\lambda)
is
K
-biinvariant,
\lambda\rightarrow\sigma(x,\lambda)
is
W
-invariant and satisfies the following inequalities:

\left|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{{\partial H}^{\beta}}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{{\partial\lambda}^{\alpha}}\sigma(H,\lambda)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}

(1.11)

for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*},H\in\mathfrak{a}
and for all multi index
\alpha,\beta

with
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq[\text{rank of }\mathbb{X}/2]+1
.
Then the pseudo differential operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}{(G//K)}
.

For the boundedness of the pseudo differential operators on nilpotent Lie groups and stratified Lie groups, we refer to
[
3
,
9
,
10
]
and references there in.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we describe the necessary preliminaries regarding semisimple Lie groups and harmonic analysis on Riemannian symmetric spaces. These are standard and can be found, for example, in
[
11
,
13
,
14
]
. To make the article self-contained, we shall gather only those results used throughout this paper.
Let
G
be a noncompact connected semisimple real rank one Lie group with finite center, with its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\theta
be a Cartan involution of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k+p}
be the associated Cartan decompostion. Let
K=\exp\mathfrak{k}
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
and let
\mathbb{X}=G/K
be an associated symmetric space with origin
\textbf{0}=\{K\}
. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a maximal abelian subspace of
\mathfrak{p}
. Since the group
G
is of real rank one, dim
\mathfrak{a}=1
. Let
\Sigma
be the set of nonzero roots of the pair
(\mathfrak{g,a})
, and let
W
be the associated Weyl group. For rank one case, it is well known that either
\Sigma=\{-\alpha,\alpha\}
or
\{-2\alpha,-\alpha,\alpha,2\alpha\}
, where
\alpha
is a positive root and the Weyl group
W
associated to
\Sigma
is {-Id, Id}, where Id is the identity operator. Let
\mathfrak{a}^{+}=\{H\in\mathfrak{a}:\alpha(H)>0\}
be a positive Weyl chamber, and let
\Sigma^{+}
be the corresponding set of positive roots. In our case,
\Sigma^{+}=\{\alpha\}
or
\{\alpha,2\alpha\}
. For any root
\beta\in\Sigma
, let
\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
be the root space associated to
\beta
. Let

\mathfrak{n}=\sum_{\beta\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta},

and let

\overline{\mathfrak{n}}=\theta(\mathfrak{n}).

Also let

N=\exp\mathfrak{n},\overline{N}=\exp\overline{\mathfrak{n}}.

The group
G
has an Iwasawa decomposition

G=K(\exp\mathfrak{a})N,

and a Cartan decomposition

G=K(\exp\mathfrak{a}^{+})K.

This decomposition is unique. For each
g\in G
, we denote
H(g)\in\mathfrak{a}

and
g^{+}\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
are the unique elements such that

g=k\exp H(g)n,k\in K,n\in N,

(2.1)

and

g=k_{1}\exp(g^{+})k_{2},k_{1},k_{2}\in K.

(2.2)

We also have another Iwasawa decomposition

G=\overline{N}(\exp\mathfrak{a})K.

Let
H_{0}
be the unique element in
\mathfrak{a}
such that
\alpha(H_{0})=1
and through this we identify
\mathfrak{a}
with
\mathbb{R}
as
t\leftrightarrow tH_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{+}=\{H\in\mathfrak{a}\mid\alpha(H)>0\}
is identified with the set of positive real numbers. We also identify
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and its complexification
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
respectively by
t\leftrightarrow t\alpha
and
z\leftrightarrow z\alpha
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
A=\exp\mathfrak{a}=\left\{a_{t}:=\exp(tH_{0})\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}
and
A^{+}=\left\{a_{t}\mid t>0\right\}
.
Let
m_{1}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and
m_{2}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
and

\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
are the root spaces corresponding to
\alpha
and
2\alpha
. As usual then
\rho=\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}+2m_{2})\alpha
denotes the half sum of the positive roots.
By abuse of notation we will denote
\rho(H_{0})=\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}+2m_{2})
by
\rho
.

Let
dg,dk,dn
and
d\overline{n}
be the Haar measures on the groups
G,K,N
and
\overline{N}
respectively. We normalize
dk
such that
\int_{K}dk=1
. We have the following integral formulae corresponding to the Iwasawa and Cartan
decomposition respectively, which holds for any integrable function
f
:

\int_{G}f(g)dg=\int_{\overline{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{K}f(\overline{n}a_{t}k)e^{2\rho t}dkdtd\overline{n},

(2.3)

and

\int_{G}f(g)dg=\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\int_{K}f(k_{1}a_{t}k_{2})\Delta(t)\,dk_{1}\,dt\,dk_{2}.

(2.4)

where
\Delta(t)=(2\sinh t)^{m_{1}+m_{2}}(2\cosh t)^{m_{2}}
.

2.1.
Iwasawa meets Cartan:

We will use the Iwasawa decomposition of
G=\overline{N}AK
and its connection with the Cartan decomposition. This idea was also used by
[
24
]
and later in this direction Ionescu proved the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
17
, Lemma 3]
)

If
\overline{v}\in\overline{N}
and
r\geq 0
, then

[\overline{v}a_{r}]^{+}=r+H(\overline{v})+E(\overline{v},r),

(2.5)

where

0\leq E(\overline{v},r)\leq 2e^{-2r}.

(2.6)

Let
P(\overline{v})=e^{-\rho(H(\overline{v}))}
; then for any
\epsilon_{0}>0
we have (see
[
17
, (3.11)]
)

\int_{\overline{N}}P(\overline{v})^{1+\epsilon_{0}}d\overline{v}=C_{\epsilon_{0}}<\infty.

(2.7)

Also we have (see
[
14
, Theorem 6.1, Chapter II, p. 180]
)

e^{2\alpha(H(\overline{v}))}=\left[1+c_{0}|X|^{2}\right]^{2}+4c_{0}|Y|^{2},

where
X
and
Y
are the coordinates of
\overline{v}
in
\overline{N}
corresponding to the root spaces
\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-2\alpha}
, and
c_{0}^{-1}=\frac{1}{4}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})
. Therefore,

H(\overline{v})\geq 0,\,\,\text{for all }\overline{v}\in\overline{N}.

(2.8)

We recall the abelian group
A
acts as a dilation on
\overline{N}
, by the mapping

\displaystyle\overline{n}\rightarrow a\overline{n}a^{-1}\in\overline{N}.

(2.9)

Moreover if
\delta_{r}
be a dilation of
\overline{N}
, defined by

\delta_{r}(\overline{n}):=\exp(rH_{0})\overline{n}\exp(-rH_{0}),

then the following is true

\int_{\overline{N}}\mathfrak{h}(\delta_{r}(\overline{n}))\,d\overline{n}=e^{2\rho r}\int_{\overline{N}}\mathfrak{h}(\overline{n})\,d\overline{n},

(2.10)

for any integrable function
\mathfrak{h}
on
\overline{N}
.

2.2.
Fourier transform

For a sufficiently nice function
f
on
\mathbb{X}
, its (Helgason) Fourier transform
\widetilde{f}
is a function defined on
\mathbb{C}\times K
given by

\widetilde{f}(\lambda,k)=\int_{G}f(g)e^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}dg,\>\>\>\>\>\>\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\>\>k\in K,

(2.11)

whenever the integral exists (
[
14
, p. 199]
).

It is known that if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
then
\widetilde{f}(\lambda,k)
is a continuous function of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, for almost every
k\in K
. If in addition
\widetilde{f}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times K,|c(\lambda)|^{-2}~d\lambda~dk)
then the following Fourier inversion holds,

f(gK)=|W|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times K}\widetilde{f}(\lambda,k)~e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}~|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda~dk,

(2.12)

for almost every
gK\in\mathbb{X}
(
[
14
, Chapter III, Theorem 1.8, Theorem 1.9]
), where
c(\lambda)
is the Harish Chandra’s
c
-function given by

c(\lambda)=\frac{2^{\rho-i\lambda}\Gamma(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}+1}{2})\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\Gamma(\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{2})\Gamma(\frac{m_{1}+2}{4}+\frac{i\lambda}{2})}.

It is normalized such that
c(-i\rho)=1
.

A function

We denote the set of all

.
Let

be the algebra of

-invariant differential operators on

for some complex eigenvalue

They are parametrized by

. The algebra

For any

\phi_{\lambda}(x)=\int_{K}e^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(xk)}\,dk\text{ for all }x\in G.

The spherical transform

of a suitable

-biinvariant function

\widehat{f}(\lambda)=\int_{G}f(x)\phi_{\lambda}(x^{-1})\,dx.

It is easy to check that for suitable

-biinvariant function

We now list down some well known properties of the elementary spherical functions which are important for us (

(1)

(3)

.

The function

The Abel transform of a suitable

Decomposition of the
\Psi

Due to distinct behavior of spherical function near the origin and away from it, we now split
\Psi_{\sigma}
into two parts. The idea of such decomposition employed here was first introduced by Clerc and Stein
[

, where they proved multiplier theorem for complex

. Suppose
\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]

\eta^{\circ}(t)=1-\eta(t)

where

\Psi_{\sigma}^{loc}f(x)=\int_{G}f(y)\eta^{\circ}(y^{-1}x)\mathcal{K}(x,y)dy,

(3.5)

and

\Psi_{\sigma}^{glo}f(x)=\int_{G}f(y)\eta(y^{-1}x)\mathcal{K}(x,y)dy.

In the upcoming two sections, we separately deal with the operators

4.
Analysis on the local part of
\Psi_{\sigma}

In the multiplier case, one can write the local part of the multiplier operator as convolution with a compactly supported
K
-biinvariant function
\mathcal{K}_{m}
(say). Next they relate the convolution with

to Euclidean multiplier and then the boundedness of multiplier on

In the case of multiplier operator, writing it as a convolution operator plays a crucial role. However in pseudo differential operator, one cannot use the theory of multiplier, due to an extra variable

in the symbol

\mathbb{R}+i(\rho_{p}-\rho/[\overline{w}a_{-r}]^{+})
, it follows

\begin{split}\left\langle\mathfrak{E}^{+}_{0,\epsilon}\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h}\right\rangle&=\int_{r=0}^{\infty}\int_{\overline{N}}\int_{\overline{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{f}(\overline{m}a_{s})\mathfrak{h}(\overline{m}\,\delta_{s}(\overline{w})\,a_{s+r})\eta(\overline{w}a_{r})\\
&\hskip-34.14322pt\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\vartheta_{\epsilon}(\overline{m}\,\delta_{s}(\overline{w})\,a_{s+r},\lambda+i(\rho_{p}-\frac{\rho}{[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}}))\,e^{(i\lambda-\frac{2\rho}{p}+\frac{\rho}{[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}})[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}}\,d\lambda\right)e^{2\rho(s+r)}ds\,d\overline{m}\,d\overline{w}\,dr\,.\end{split}

Now by using integration by parts and Lemma
2.8
, we get

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\vartheta_{\epsilon}(\overline{m}\,\delta_{s}(\overline{w})\,a_{s+r},\lambda+i(\rho_{p}-\frac{\rho}{[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}}))\,e^{i\lambda[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}}\,d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\left([\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}\right)^{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\lambda}{(1+|\lambda|)^{l-\frac{d-1}{2}}}.

By taking
l>\frac{d+1}{2},
and substituting the expression
[\overline{w}a_{r}]^{+}
, it follows

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{E}^{+}_{0,\epsilon}\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{r=0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\overline{N}}\mathfrak{F}(s)\,e^{\frac{2\rho s}{p}}\,\mathfrak{H}(s+r)\,e^{\frac{2\rho(s+r)}{p^{\prime}}}\eta(\overline{w}a_{r})\frac{e^{-\frac{2}{p}\rho H(\overline{w})}}{(r+H(\overline{w})+E(\overline{w},r))^{l}}d\overline{w}\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle+C\int_{r=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\overline{N}}\mathfrak{F}(s)\,e^{\frac{2\rho s}{p}}\,\mathfrak{H}(s+r)\,e^{\frac{2\rho(s+r)}{p^{\prime}}}\eta(\overline{w}a_{r})\frac{e^{-\frac{2}{p}\rho H(\overline{w})}}{(r+H(\overline{w})+E(\overline{w},r))^{l}}\,d\overline{w}\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathfrak{f}\|_{L^{p}(\overline{N}A)}\,\|\mathfrak{h}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\overline{N}A)}.

In the last inequality, we used Hölder’s inequality along with (
2.7
). This completes the proof of the lemma and consequently the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of the Theorem
1.8
.

: Let
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]

[1,\infty)

1

[2,\infty)

2.5
, (
2.15
) and evenness of

\displaystyle\eta(t)\Psi_{\sigma}f(a_{t})e^{\rho t}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)}e^{i\lambda t}\,\widehat{f}(\lambda)d\lambda+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)a(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)}e^{i\lambda t}\,\widehat{f}(\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)}e^{i\lambda t}\,\mathcal{F}\mathcal{A}f(\lambda)d\lambda+2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)a(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)}e^{i\lambda t}\,\mathcal{F}\mathcal{A}f(\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Psi}_{a_{1}}(\mathcal{A}f)(t)+\tilde{\Psi}_{a_{2}}(\mathcal{A}f)(t),

where

a_{1}(t,\lambda)=2\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)}\,\text{ and }\,a_{2}(t,\lambda)=2\eta(t)\frac{\sigma(t,\lambda)a(t,\lambda)}{c(-\lambda)},

(6.1)

and
\tilde{\Psi}_{a_{1}},\tilde{\Psi}_{a_{2}}
are the Euclidean pseudo differential operator associated with symbols
a_{1},a_{2}
respectively.
Now from (
1.10
),(
2.19
) and (
2.20
) it follows that
a_{1},a_{2}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are smooth functions and satisfies the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{{\partial t}^{\beta}}\,\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{{\partial\lambda}^{\alpha}}a_{i}(t,\lambda)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\lambda|)^{-\alpha},

(6.2)

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and

Using the results of pseudo differential operators in Euclidean spaces (Theorem
1.3
), it follows that
\tilde{\Psi}_{a_{i}}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
to itself. Therefore for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G//K)
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{2}^{\infty}|\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(a_{t})|^{2}e^{2\rho t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}|\eta(t)\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(a_{t})|^{2}e^{2\rho t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

(6.3)

Now we choose
M>0,
such that
|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\geq 1
for all

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\lambda=2\int_{0}^{M}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\lambda+2\int_{M}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}^{2}+\int_{M}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|^{2}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\,d\lambda

\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\right).

(6.4)

Therefore from (

\left(\int_{2}^{\infty}|\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(a_{t})|^{2}e^{2\rho t}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{X})}\right).

Next from (

\displaystyle|\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(a_{t})|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}\|\|{\phi_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{X}|)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(t,\lambda)|\,|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{X})}.

Complex symmetric space:
Let
\mathbb{X}
be an arbitrary rank complex symmetric space. The elementary spherical functions on complex symmetric space
\mathbb{X}
are given by the expression

\phi_{\lambda}(H)=c(\lambda)\frac{\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)e^{-is\lambda(H)}}{\phi(H)},\,\text{ for }H\in\mathfrak{a},\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*},

(6.17)

where
\phi{(H)}
is defined by the formula

\phi(H)=\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)e^{s\rho(H)},\,\text{ for }H\in\mathfrak{a},

(6.18)

(see
[
8
, p. 907 and p. 910]
).
It is known that the Haar measure on
A
corresponding to the polar decomposition of
G
is given by
\phi(H)^{2}dH
for
H\in\mathfrak{a}
.

Thus the spherical transform of a suitable
K
-biinvariant function
f
is given by

\widehat{f}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathfrak{a}}f(H)\phi_{\lambda}(H)\phi(H)^{2}dH,\,{\text{ for }}\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}.

(6.19)

We define the
K
-biinvariant pseudo differential operator
\Psi_{\sigma}
associated to
\sigma
by,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(H)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\sigma(H,\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(H)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda.

(6.20)

Now we shall prove Theorem
1.10
:

Proof of the Theorem
1.10
:
.

From (
6
) and (
6.17
) we have

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=c(\lambda)\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)\int_{\mathfrak{a}}f(H)\phi(H)e^{-is\lambda(H)}dH

\displaystyle=c(\lambda)\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)\int_{\mathfrak{a}}f(H)\phi(H)e^{-is\lambda(H)}dH

\displaystyle=c(\lambda)\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)\mathcal{F}g(s\lambda),\,\text{ for }\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*},

where the function
g
on
\mathfrak{a}
is defined as

g(H)=f(H)\phi(H),\,\text{ for }H\in\mathfrak{a},

and
\mathcal{F}g
denotes the Euclidean Fourier transform of
g
. Since
\phi
satisfies

\phi(sH)=\det(s)\phi(H),

for
H\in\mathfrak{a},s\in W
, we have

g(sH)=\det(s)g(H),\,\text{ for }H\in\mathfrak{a},s\in W.

(6.21)

Therefore,

\mathcal{F}g(s\lambda)=\det(s)\,\mathcal{F}g(\lambda),\,\text{ for }\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*},s\in W.

(6.22)

Consequently the spherical transform of
f
can be written as

\widehat{f}(\lambda)=c(\lambda)|W|\,\mathcal{F}g(\lambda),\,\text{ for }\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}.

(6.23)

Now from the definition (
6.20
) of
\Psi_{\sigma}
we get,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(H)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\,\sigma(H,\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(H)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\,\sigma(H,\lambda)\phi_{-\lambda}(H)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=|W|\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\mathcal{F}g(\lambda)c(\lambda)\sigma(H,\lambda)c(-\lambda)\frac{\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)e^{is\lambda(H)}}{\phi(H)}|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{|W|}{\phi(H)}\sum_{s\in W}\det(s)\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\mathcal{F}g(\lambda)\sigma(H,\lambda)e^{is\lambda(H)}d\lambda.

Now by the change of variable
\lambda\mapsto s^{-1}\lambda
and (
6.22
) we get

\phi(H)\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(H)=|W|^{2}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\mathcal{F}g(\lambda)\sigma(H,\lambda)e^{i\lambda(H)}d\lambda.

That is, that

\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(H)\,\phi(H)=|W|^{2}\widetilde{\Psi}_{\sigma}(g)(H),

(6.24)

where
\widetilde{T}_{\sigma}
is the Euclidean pseudo differential operator associated with the symbol
\sigma(H,\lambda)
.
Using Calderón and Vaillancourt theorem
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
, we have
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\sigma}:L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}
bounded. Therefore we have

\int_{\mathfrak{a}}|\Psi_{\sigma}(f)(H)|^{2}\phi(H)^{2}dH=|W|^{4}\int_{\mathfrak{a}}|\widetilde{\Psi}_{\sigma}(g)(H)|^{2}dH\leq C\int_{\mathfrak{a}}|g(H)|^{2}dH=\int_{\mathfrak{a}}|f(H)|^{2}\phi(H)^{2}dH.

This shows that

\|\Psi_{\sigma}(f)\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

This completes the proof.

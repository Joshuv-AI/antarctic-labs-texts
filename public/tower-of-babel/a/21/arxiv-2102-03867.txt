Abstract
We introduce the notion of positional marked pattern, which is a permutation τ \displaystyle\tau with one element underlined. Given a permutation σ \displaystyle\sigma , we say that σ \displaystyle\sigma has a τ \displaystyle\tau -match at position i \displaystyle i if τ \displaystyle\tau occurs in σ \displaystyle\sigma in such a way that σ i \displaystyle\sigma_{i} plays the role of the underlined element in the occurrence. We let p ​ m ​ p τ ​ ( σ ) \displaystyle pmp_{\tau}(\sigma) denote the number of positions i \displaystyle i which σ \displaystyle\sigma has a τ \displaystyle\tau -match. This defines a new class of statistics on permutations, where we study such statistics and prove a number of results. In particular, we prove that two positional marked patterns 1 ​ 2 ¯ ​ 3 \displaystyle 1\underline{2}3 and 1 ​ 3 ¯ ​ 2 \displaystyle 1\underline{3}2 give rise to two statistics that have the same distribution. We studied a generalization of quadrant marked mesh patterns. Here, we make a connection between quadrant marked mesh pattern and classical permutation patterns. Let
\displaystyle\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{n}\in S_{n}
and
\displaystyle\tau=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\ldots\tau_{k}\in S_{k}

\displaystyle\sigma
contains pattern
\displaystyle\tau
if there is a subsequence
\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{2}}\ldots\sigma_{i_{k}}
of
\displaystyle\sigma
such that
\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle\tau
have the same relative order. That is,
\displaystyle\sigma_{i_{j}}<\sigma_{i_{k}}
if and only if
\displaystyle\tau_{j}<\tau_{k}
. For example, permutation
\displaystyle 15324
contains pattern
\displaystyle 123
as the subsequence
\displaystyle 134
(and
\displaystyle 124
) has the same relative order as pattern
\displaystyle 123
. We now consider the pattern
\displaystyle MMP(1,0,1,0)
. An element in a permutation matches
\displaystyle MMP(1,0,1,0)
when there is at least one point to the top right and at least one point to the bottom left of the interested point. An equivalent way to think about
\displaystyle MMP(1,0,1,0)
for any permutation
\displaystyle\sigma
is to count the number of elements in
\displaystyle\sigma
that can be the midpoint of the pattern
\displaystyle 123
in
\displaystyle\sigma
. One can ask similar questions but for different patterns and positions. For example, one may consider the number of elements in
\displaystyle\sigma
that can be the starting point of the pattern
\displaystyle 2314
.

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle[k]
with one of the elements underlined. Given any positional marked pattern
\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\pi(\tau)
denote the element in
\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle u(\tau)

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{n}\in S_{n}
and a positional marked pattern
\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\sigma
has a
\displaystyle\tau
-match at position
\displaystyle\ell
if
\displaystyle\sigma
contains the pattern
\displaystyle\pi(\tau)
in such a way that the
\displaystyle\ell
-th element in
\displaystyle\sigma
plays the role of the underlined elements in
\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle pmp_{\tau}(\sigma)
denote the number of positions
\displaystyle\ell
such that
\displaystyle\sigma
has a
\displaystyle\tau
-match at position
\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle\tau_{1}
and
\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\sigma
in
\displaystyle S_{n}
avoiding

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

and
\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\tau_{1}
and
\displaystyle\tau_{2}
are
\displaystyle PMP
-Wilf-equivalent

\displaystyle pmp_{\tau_{1}}
and
\displaystyle pmp_{\tau_{2}}
have the same distribution over
\displaystyle S_{n}
for all
\displaystyle n
. Our main goal is to classify

-Wilf-equivalent classes for positional marked patterns.

\displaystyle P_{n,\tau_{1},s}(x)=\sum_{\sigma}x^{pmp_{\tau_{1}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\sigma
at position

\displaystyle\sigma\in S_{n}

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle 1,2,\ldots,n-1

Lemma 23
.

\displaystyle P_{n,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)
satisfies the following recursive formulas:

P_{n,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}P_{n-1,\tau_{1},s-1,\ell}(x)+(s-1-t)xP_{n-1,\tau_{1},s-1,t}(x)+\sum_{j=t+1}^{s}P_{n-1,\tau_{1},j,t}(x)+P_{n-1,\tau_{1},t,t}(x)

for
\displaystyle 1\leq s\leq n-1
, and

P_{n,\tau_{1},s,n}(x)=P_{n-1,\tau_{1},s}(x)

for
\displaystyle 1\leq s<n-1
.

By convention, Let
\displaystyle P_{n,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)=0
for infeasible value of
\displaystyle s,t

Proof.

First, consider the formula for
\displaystyle P_{n,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)
when
\displaystyle 1\leq t<s\leq n-1.

\displaystyle\ell
be the position of
\displaystyle 2
in
\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\sigma\in S_{n}

\displaystyle\sigma
at position
\displaystyle s
and 1 at position
\displaystyle t

\displaystyle 1\leq\ell\leq t-1

\displaystyle 1
from
\displaystyle\sigma
without effecting a
\displaystyle\tau_{1}
-match at other positions. That is because
\displaystyle\sigma
does not have a
\displaystyle\tau_{1}
-match at the position of
\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle\sigma
has a
\displaystyle\tau_{1}
-match at other positions and matching involves 1, it can still have a
\displaystyle\tau_{1}
-match by using 2 instead of 1.

After removing and reducing, the position of 1 is
\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle s-1

2.

\displaystyle t+1\leq\ell\leq s-1

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle s-1

\displaystyle\ell=s

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

After removing and reducing, the position of
\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle t
and
\displaystyle s-1

\displaystyle\sum_{j=t}^{s-1}P_{n-1,\tau_{1},j,t}(x)

\displaystyle\ell=n

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{n,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{t-1}P_{n-1,\tau_{1},s-1,\ell}(x)+(s-1-t)xP_{n-1,\tau_{1},s-1,t}(x)+\sum_{j=t}^{s-1}P_{n-1,\tau_{1},j,t}(x)+P_{n-1,\tau_{1},s,t}(x)

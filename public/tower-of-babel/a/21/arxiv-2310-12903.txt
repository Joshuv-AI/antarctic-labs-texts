Homogenization of a nonlinear elliptic problem with imperfect rough interface
Sara Monsurrò, Carmen Perugia, Federica Raimondi

Abstract
This paper deals with an elliptic problem with a nonlinear lower order term set in an open bounded cylinder of ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , divided into two connected components by an imperfect rough interface. More precisely, we assume that at the interface the flux is continuous and proportional, via a nonlinear rule, to the jump of the solution. According to the amplitude of the interface oscillations and the proportionality coefficient, we derive different homogenized problems. † † Keywords: Homogenization, nonlinear elliptic problems, rough interface † † MSC: 35B27, 35J60, 35J66, 35Q92

Introduction

This paper concerns a nonlinear elliptic problem with a lower order term defined in an open bounded cylinder
Q=\omega\times]-\ell,\ell[
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 2
,
\ell>0
and
\omega
bounded smooth domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. The cylinder is divided into two connected components
Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}
and
Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}
by a rough surface
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon
being a positive parameter tending to zero. This common interface is represented by the graph of a quickly oscillating function
\varepsilon
-periodic in the first
N-1
variables, having oscillations’ amplitude of order
\varepsilon^{k}
, with
k>0
. This means that, as
\varepsilon
goes to zero, the interface turns into a flat surface.

Given
f\in L^{2}(Q)
, we consider the following problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle-\textnormal{div}(D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})=f&\textnormal{in}\;Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle(D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}=(D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{-}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}&\textnormal{on}\;\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle(D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{+}\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}=-\varepsilon^{\gamma}h_{2}([u_{\varepsilon}])&\textnormal{on}\;\Gamma^{\varepsilon},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=0&\textnormal{on}\;\partial Q,\end{array}\right.

where
\nu_{\varepsilon}
is the unit outward normal to
Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}
and
[\cdot]
is the jump of the solution across
\Gamma^{\varepsilon}
.
We assume that the matrix
D^{\varepsilon}
is
\varepsilon
-periodic, uniformly elliptic and bounded, and the nonlinear functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
satisfy some natural regularity and growth conditions. For more details about the setting of the problem
we refer the reader to Section
2
.

We are interested in studying the asymptotic behaviour, as
\varepsilon
tends to zero, of the solutions of problem (
1.1
). The values of the parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
in the jump condition and the parameter
k
in the amplitude of the oscillations play a crucial role and give rise to three different limit cases denoted by A), B) and C). In all of them, we obtain a homogenized elliptic equation with a nonlinear lower order term.

In cases A) and B), the limit domain is a cylinder made up of two components
Q^{+}
and
Q^{-}
separated by a flat interface
\Gamma_{0}
. In particular, in case A), we obtain an effective imperfect transmission problem where the
flux is proportional, by means of a nonlinear function, to the jump of the solution via different constants whose values depend on
k
and
\gamma
. In case B), one obtains a homogenized problem equivalent to two independent problems set in
Q^{+}
and
Q^{-}
, with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{0}
.

Finally, in case C), we find a homogenized Dirichlet elliptic problem in the whole cylinder
Q
.

Remark 2.4
.

We observe that in the three limit problems we always get a homogenized elliptic equation with a nonlinear lower order term. The main differences are due to the interfacial contribution. In particular, in case A), we obtain an effective imperfect transmission problem where the

Proposition 3.3

Under the hypotheses of Proposition

\displaystyle\left\|w^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0,

We proceed into three steps.
In the first two steps we show (

Step 1.

To this aim, we restrict the function

One has

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{Q^{+}}\left|\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}-w^{+}\right|^{2}\,dx=\int_{Q^{+}}\left|\chi_{Q^{+}\setminus S_{\varepsilon}}\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}+\chi_{S_{\varepsilon}}\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}-w^{+}\right|^{2}\,dx\\[14.22636pt]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{Q^{+}}\left|w^{+}_{\varepsilon}-w^{+}\right|^{2}\,dx+\int_{Q^{+}}\left|\chi_{S_{\varepsilon}}\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,dx\right).\end{array}

In view of (
3.5
), the first integral on the right hand side of (

) converges to zero. Concerning the second integral, by Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{+}}\left|\chi_{S_{\varepsilon}}\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2}\,dx\leq\left|S_{\varepsilon}\right|^{\frac{1}{(2^{*}/2)^{\prime}}}\left\|\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}(Q^{+})}.

Thus, this integral goes to zero too, in view of (

) and (

Step 2.

Here we prove (

).

Let us observe that

(3.14)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left\|w^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\leq\left\|w^{+}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\displaystyle\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.\end{array}

By (

), the first term on the right hand side of (

) tends to zero.
Let us prove that the second term converges to zero as well. One has

(3.15)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\leq\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\left\|w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.\end{array}

Let us first show that

(3.16)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\left\|\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\leq\left\|\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\left\|\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)+w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0.\end{array}

Indeed, by (

), (

) and (

) the difference

\displaystyle\left\|\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0,

up to a subsequence.

Furthermore, by (

) applied to the function
(\check{w}^{+}_{\varepsilon}-w^{+})
, one also has

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left\|\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-\check{w}_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)+w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0.

Combining (

Moreover, by the continuity of trace arguments, as observed in

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left\|w^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0.

Putting together (

) and (

(3.20)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{+}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}\bar{g}\right)-w^{+}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0.\end{array}

The previous arguments, together with (

Step 3.
The proof of (

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\left\|w^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-w^{-}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle\leq\left\|w^{-}_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot,\varepsilon^{k}g\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)-w_{\varepsilon}^{-}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+\displaystyle\left\|w_{\varepsilon}^{-}\left(\cdot,0\right)-w^{-}\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.\end{array}

On the other hand, by (

4
Existence and uniqueness result

Here, we aim to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution of problem (
2.14
), for any fixed
\varepsilon
, together with some uniform a priori estimates.

Let us start by proving that all the terms in (
2.14
) make sense.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under assumptions (
A
g
),
(\textbf{A}2)
and
(\textbf{A}3)
, suppose that
r
and
\bar{r}
are such that

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle i)\ \text{ if }N=2,\quad r,\,\bar{r}\in[1,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle ii)\ \text{ if }N>2,\quad r=\dfrac{2(N-1)}{N-2},\,\bar{r}=2^{*}=\dfrac{2N}{N-2},\end{array}\right.

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle r\geq q_{2}\,r^{\prime},\qquad\bar{r}\geq q_{1}\bar{r}^{\prime},

where
r^{\prime}
and
\bar{r}^{\prime}
are the coniugate exponents of
r
and
\bar{r}
, respectively.

Then, for any fixed
\varepsilon
, the following maps are bounded and continuous:

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle i)\ H_{1}:w_{\varepsilon}\in W_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow H_{1}(w_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\frac{\bar{r}}{q_{1}}}(Q),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle ii)\ H_{2}:w_{\varepsilon}\in W_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow H_{2}(w_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\frac{r}{q_{2}}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon}),\end{array}

where
H_{1}(w_{\varepsilon})(x)=h_{1}(w_{\varepsilon}(x))
,
H_{2}(w_{\varepsilon})(x)=h_{2}(w_{\varepsilon}^{+}(x)-w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x))
.

Moreover for every
w_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\varepsilon}
in
W_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we get

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle h_{1}(w_{\varepsilon})\,v_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(Q),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle h_{2}(w^{+}_{\varepsilon}-w^{-}_{\varepsilon})(v^{+}_{\varepsilon}-v^{-}_{\varepsilon})\in L^{1}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon}).\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let us consider the lower order term. If
N>2
, the embeddings
H^{1}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})\subset L^{2^{*}}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})
are continuous. Hence, we get
w_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2^{*}}(Q)
. By
(\textbf{A}2)_{1,3}
and the property of the Nemytskii operator associated to the function
h_{1}
we get that
H_{1}(w_{\varepsilon})(x)=h_{1}(w_{\varepsilon}(x))
is a bounded and continuous map from
W_{0}^{\varepsilon}
to
L^{\frac{2^{*}}{q_{1}}}(Q)
. By (
2.4
) the first relation in (
4.4
) holds for
N>2
.
For
N=2
we argue in a similar way as above by using the continuous embeddings
H^{1}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})\subset L^{\bar{r}}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})
for any
\bar{r}\in[1,+\infty)
and again (
2.4
) for
N=2
.

For the interface term, let

j:w_{\varepsilon}\in W^{\varepsilon}_{0}\,\to\,w_{\varepsilon}^{+}-w_{\varepsilon}^{-}\in L^{r}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})

and

j_{2}:\overline{w}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{r}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})\,\to\,j_{2}(\overline{w}_{\varepsilon})\in L^{\frac{r}{q_{2}}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})

be defined by
j_{2}(\overline{w}_{\varepsilon})(x)=h_{2}(\overline{w}_{\varepsilon}(x))
. By the linear continuity of the inclusions
H^{1}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{+})\subset L^{r}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})
and
H^{1}(Q_{\varepsilon}^{-})\subset L^{r}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})
,
j
is bounded and continuous. Moreover, assumption
(\textbf{A}3)_{1,4}
and the property of the Nemytskii operator associated to the function

imply that
j_{2}
is bounded and continuous from
L^{r}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})
to
L^{\frac{r}{q_{2}}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})
. Hence,
H_{2}\left(w_{\varepsilon}\right)=\left(j_{2}\circ j\right)(w_{\varepsilon})
is a bounded and continuous map from
W^{\varepsilon}_{0}
into
L^{\frac{r}{q_{2}}}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})
. Thus, by (
2.7
) and (
4.2
) the second relation in (
4.4
) is true.
∎

At this point, we prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under assumptions
(\textbf{A}_{g})
,
(\textbf{A}1)
,
(\textbf{A}2)
,
(\textbf{A}3)
and given
f\in L^{2}(Q)
, for any fixed
\varepsilon
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{\varepsilon}_{0}
of problem (
2.14
) satisfying

(4.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle i)\ \|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}\leq C,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle ii)\ \|u^{+}_{\varepsilon}-u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}},\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}\right.

with
C
positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same arguments of Theorem 3.1 in
[
13
]
. We start proving, for any fixed
\varepsilon
, the existence of a solution of the variational problem (
2.14
) by using the Minty-Browder theorem for the operator
\mathcal{G}:u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{\varepsilon}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}(u_{\varepsilon})\in(W^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{\prime}
where

(4.6)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right),\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{(W^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{\prime},W^{\varepsilon}_{0}}={\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}+{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}h_{2}(u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-u_{\varepsilon}^{-})(\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{-})\,d\sigma}+{\displaystyle\int_{Q}h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}-{\displaystyle\int_{Q}f\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}.

The continuity of
\mathcal{G}
follows directly from assumption
(\textbf{A}1)
and Proposition
4.1
. Moreover hypotheses
(\textbf{A}1)
,
(\textbf{A}2)_{2}
and
(\textbf{A}3)_{2}
, assure the monotonicity of
\mathcal{G}
. It remains to prove the coercivness of
\mathcal{G}
. To this aim let us observe that by
(\textbf{A}1)
,
(\textbf{A}3)_{3}
, Remark
2.2
, (
2.9
) and (
2.13
), it holds

(4.7)

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{G}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right),u_{\varepsilon}\rangle\geq\alpha\left\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}+C\left\|u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-u_{\varepsilon}^{-}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}-\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W^{\varepsilon}_{0}}-C_{2}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{\varepsilon}_{0}},\end{array}

with
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
positive constants. The above inequality yields that the operator
\mathcal{G}
is coercive. Hence, by the Minty-Browder theorem,
\mathcal{G}
is surjective, so that there exists at least one function
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{\varepsilon}_{0}
such that
\mathcal{G}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
. This means that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of problem (
2.14
).
As for the uniqueness, let us suppose that problem (
2.14
) admits another solution
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
. Choosing as test function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
in (
2.14
) written in correspondence of the solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
, respectively, we get

\begin{array}[]{c}{\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}+{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}h_{2}(u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-u_{\varepsilon}^{-})((u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+})-(u_{\varepsilon}^{-}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-}))\,d\sigma}\\[14.22636pt]
{\displaystyle+\int_{Q}h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}={\displaystyle\int_{Q}f(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}.\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{c}{\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}h_{2}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-})((u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+})-(u_{\varepsilon}^{-}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-}))\,d\sigma}\\[14.22636pt]
{\displaystyle+\int_{Q}h_{1}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}={\displaystyle\int_{Q}f(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}\end{array}

Then, we subtract and obtain

(4.8)

\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}\\[14.22636pt]
{\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}\left[h_{2}(u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-u_{\varepsilon}^{-})-h_{2}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-})\right]((u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+})-(u_{\varepsilon}^{-}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-}))\,d\sigma}\\[11.38109pt]
+{\displaystyle\int_{Q}\left[h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})-h_{1}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\right](u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx}=0.\end{array}

By assumption
(\textbf{A}1)
, the monotonicity of

and

) gives

\displaystyle\nabla(u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{ in }Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}.

Then, by using Poincaré inequality (

which ends the proof of uniqueness.

In order to prove uniform estimates in (
4.5
), let us take
u_{\varepsilon}
as test function in (
2.14
) and obtain

{\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\,dx}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}h_{2}(u^{+}_{\varepsilon}-u^{-}_{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon}^{+}-u_{\varepsilon}^{-})\,d\sigma}+{\displaystyle\int_{Q}h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\,dx}={\displaystyle\int_{Q}fu_{\varepsilon}\,dx}.

By
(\textbf{A}1)
,
(\textbf{A}3)_{3}
and Remark

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\alpha\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}+C\,\varepsilon^{\gamma}\|u^{+}_{\varepsilon}-u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma^{\varepsilon})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(Q)},\end{array}

∎

5.1
Proof of Theorem
2.3

At first we consider cases A) and B). To this aim, observe that given

\displaystyle\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\varphi\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{2^{*}}(Q).

Now, let us take

as test function in the variational equality (

(5.3)

{\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}+\varepsilon^{\gamma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}h_{2}(u^{+}_{\varepsilon}-u^{-}_{\varepsilon})(\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{+}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{-})\,d\sigma}+{\displaystyle\int_{Q}h_{1}(u_{\varepsilon})\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}={\displaystyle\int_{Q}f\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx}.

We would like to pass to the limit in (

By Corollary

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int_{Q\setminus\Gamma^{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\,dx=\displaystyle\int_{Q}\chi_{Q^{+}_{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\psi_{1}\,dx+\int_{Q}\chi_{Q^{-}_{\varepsilon}}D^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\psi_{2}\,dx\\[14.22636pt]

Moreover, (

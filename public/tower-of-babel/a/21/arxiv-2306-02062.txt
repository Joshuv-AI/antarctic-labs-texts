Synchronization analysis of high order layered complex networks
Yujuan Han, Wenlian Lu, Tianping Chen

Abstract
In this letter, we propose high order layered complex networks. The synchronization is discussed in detail. The relations of synchronization, individual coupling matrices and the intrinsic function of the uncoupled system are given. As special cases, synchronization of monolayer networks and multiplex networks discussed in literature can be obtained.

In this paper, we propose more general multi-layer networks, called high order layered networks, and study synchronization of these networks. More precisely, we investigate the following high order layered coupled networks

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=f(x_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}}(t))+c_{1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{I_{1}}l^{(1)}_{i_{1}j}x_{j,i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}}(t)

II
Model description and background

Let us consider a setup in which the dynamics of nodes in high order layered networks is linearly coupled. The coupling among nodes are assumed to be undirected.

We call complex network (
1
) the
first order layered network
and multiplex network (
I
) the
second order layered network
.

Notice that each layer of multiplex networks is a first order layered network. If we take each layer as a “node” whose own dynamics follows model (
1
) and
b_{kl}
as the coupling strength between the
k
th and
l
th “nodes”, these linearly coupled “nodes” will generate the multiplex network (
I
). This implies that the second order layered network is in fact a network composed of identical first order layered networks.

Inductively, we can obtain a
m
th order layered network
by regarding the
(m-1)
th order layered network as a “node” and linearly coupling these “nodes”. And we call the network topology among the
(m-1)
th order layered networks the
m
th order subnetwork
. In sequence, we can define the
k
th order subnetwork
of the
m
th order layered network,
k=m-1,\cdots,1
(see Fig.
).

Suppose the
m
th order layered network have
I_{k}
nodes in its
k
th order subnetwork,
k=1,\cdots,m
. Denote by
L^{(k)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{k}\times I_{k}}
the coupling matrix of the
k
th order subnetwork with zero-sum rows, i.e.,
[L^{(k)}]_{ij}\geq 0
for
i\neq j
and
[L^{(k)}]_{ii}=-\sum_{j=1}^{I_{k}}[L^{(k)}]_{ij}
, and
c_{k}
the coupling strength among nodes in the
k
th order subnetwork.

Next, we will introduce the model of high order layered network inductively. Firstly, we consider the first order layered network. Denote by
x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}(t)
the state of the
i_{1}
th node in the first order layered network. Then the dynamics of the first order layered network satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{i_{1}}^{(1)}(t)}{dt}=f(x_{i_{1}}^{(1)}(t))+c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{1}}l_{i_{1},j}^{(1)}x_{j}^{(1)}(t),~i_{1}=1,\cdots,I_{1}.

(4)

Denote
x^{(1)}(t)=[x^{(1)}_{1}(t)^{T},\cdots,x^{(1)}_{I_{1}}(t)^{T}]^{T}
,

f(x^{(1)}(t))=[f(x^{(1)}_{1}(t))^{T},\cdots,f(x^{(1)}_{I_{1}}(t))^{T}]^{T}
. System (
4
) can be written in the matrix form

\displaystyle\frac{dx^{(1)}(t)}{dt}=f(x^{(1)}(t))+c_{1}(L^{(1)}\otimes E_{n})x^{(1)}(t)

(5)

where
\otimes
is the Kronecker product and
E_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix. Consider that the second order layered network is obtained by linearly coupling
I_{2}
identical first order layered networks. Denote by
{x}^{(2)}_{i_{2}}(t)
the state of the
i_{2}
th first order layered network, whose uncoupled dynamics follows model (
5
). Hence, the dynamics of the second order layered network satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{i_{2}}^{(2)}(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle f(x^{(2)}_{i_{2}}(t))+c_{1}(L^{(1)}\otimes E_{n})x^{(2)}_{i_{2}}(t)

\displaystyle+c_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{2}}l_{i_{2}j}^{(2)}x_{j}^{(2)}(t)

Denote
x^{(2)}(t)=[x^{(2)}_{1}(t)^{T},\cdots,x^{(2)}_{I_{2}}(t)^{T}]^{T}
,

f(x^{(2)}(t))=[f(x^{(2)}_{1}(t))^{T},\cdots,f(x^{(2)}_{I_{2}}(t))^{T}]^{T}
. Then the dynamics of second order layered networks can be written in the matrix form

\displaystyle\frac{dx^{(2)}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=f(x^{(2)}(t))+c_{1}(E_{I_{2}}\otimes L^{(1)}\otimes E_{n})x^{(2)}(t)

\displaystyle+c_{2}(L^{(2)}\otimes E_{I_{1}}\otimes E_{n})x^{(2)}(t)

(6)

Inductively, we obtain the dynamics of the
m
th order layered network as follows

\displaystyle\frac{dx^{(m)}(t)}{dt}=f(x^{(m)}(t))+\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}(E_{I_{m}}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{I_{k+1}}\otimes L^{(k)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\otimes E_{I_{k-1}}\cdots\otimes E_{I_{1}}\otimes E_{n})x^{(m)}(t)

(7)

where
x^{(m)}(t)=[x^{(m)}_{1}(t)^{T},\cdots,x^{(m)}_{I_{m}}(t)^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{nI_{1}\cdots I_{m}}
.

Notice that the
m
th order layered network has a total number of
(I_{m}\cdots I_{2}I_{1})
nodes in the network (see red nodes in Fig.
). Let us consider the dynamics of these node, which are denoted by
v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}}
with

being the node index in the
k

k=1,\cdots,m

v_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}}

x_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

Similar to synchronization in multiplex networks, different forms of synchronization phenomenon can emerge in high order layered networks.

In this paper, synchronization of high order layered networks is defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Consider the
m
th order layered network (
I
).

1.

For any
1\leq k\leq m
, network (
I
) is said to reach
k
th order synchronization if for any
1\leq p,q\leq I_{k}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k-1},p,i_{k+1},\cdots,i_{m}}(t)-x_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k-1},q,i_{k+1},\cdots,i_{m}}(t)\|

\displaystyle=0

(8)

holds for any fixed
i_{s}=1,\cdots,I_{s},s=1,\cdots,k-1,k,\cdots,m
.

2.

Network (
I
) is said to realize complete synchronization if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}}(t)-x_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{m}}(t)\|=0

(9)

holds for any
1\leq i_{k},j_{k}\leq I_{k}
,
k=1,\cdots,m
,

Throughout this paper, we suppose
f
satisfies the following QUAD condition. The role of the QUAD condition in synchronization of complex networks was investigated in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

Assumption 1
.

The continuous function
f(x,t):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to satisfy QUAD condition if there exists a constant
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle(x-y)^{\top}\left[f(x)-f(y)\right]\leq\alpha(x-y)^{\top}(x-y)

(10)

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Assumption 2
.

All coupling matrices
L^{(k)}=[l^{(k)}_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{k}\times I_{k}}
,
k=1,\cdots,m
are symmetric. For any
k
, the eigenvalues of
L^{(k)}
are
0=\lambda_{1}^{(k)}\geq\lambda_{2}^{(k)}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{I_{k}}^{(k)}
.

III
Main Results

In this section, we provide a unified approach to analyse synchronization of the network (
I
). The following theorem reveals how the synchronization depends on the eigenvalues of the coupling matrices
L^{(k)}
and the coupling strengths
c_{k}
,
k=1,\cdots,m
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. For any
k=1,\cdots,m
, the
m
th order layered network (
I
) can reach
k
th order synchronization if
c_{k}\lambda_{2}^{(k)}+\alpha<0
, where
\alpha
is the constant defined in Assumption 1.

Proof.

Here we mainly consider the case
m=3
. The proof for case
m>3
can be given in a similar argument. For any fixed
1\leq i_{2}\leq I_{2}
and
1\leq i_{3}\leq I_{3}
, we define

\displaystyle\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)=\frac{1}{I_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{1}}x_{j,i_{2},i_{3}}(t).

(11)

Define a Lyapunov funciton

\displaystyle V(X(t))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}.

(12)

Because of
\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{I_{1}}(x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t))=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}\frac{d\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)}{dt}=0

(13)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}f(\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t))=0.

(14)

Differentiating
V(X(t))
and by (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{dV(X(t))}{dt}=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}f(x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t))

\displaystyle~~~~~~+c_{1}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{1}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}l_{i_{1}j}^{(1)}x_{j,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+c_{2}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{2}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}l_{i_{2}j}^{(2)}x_{i_{1},j,i_{3}}(t)

\displaystyle~~~~~~+c_{3}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{3}}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}l_{i_{3}j}^{(3)}x_{i_{1},i_{2},j}(t)

\displaystyle~~~~=V_{1}(t)+V_{2}(t)+V_{3}(t)+V_{4}(t)

(15)

From (
14
) and Assumption 1, we have

\displaystyle V_{1}(t)\leq\alpha\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)\|^{2}_{2}

(16)

By Assumption 2 that
L^{(1)}
is symmetric and has all row sums zero, we have

\displaystyle V_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{1}}l_{i_{1}j}^{(1)}[x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]^{\top}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\cdot[x_{j,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\lambda_{2}^{(1)}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-\bar{x}^{(1)}_{\cdot,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}

(17)

By Assumption 2 that
L^{(2)}
is symmetric, semi-negative and has all row sums zero, we have

\displaystyle V_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=c_{2}\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{I_{2}}l_{i_{2}j}^{(2)}x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)^{\top}x_{i_{1},j,i_{3}}(t)\leq 0

(18)

Similarly, we can derive
V_{4}(t)\leq 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{dV(X(t))}{dt}\leq 2(c_{1}\lambda_{2}^{(1)}+\alpha)V(t)

(19)

which implies for any fixed
1\leq i_{2}\leq I_{2}
and
1\leq i_{3}\leq I_{3}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{p,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-x_{q,i_{2},i_{3}}(t)\|=O\left(e^{2(c_{1}\lambda_{2}^{(1)}+\alpha)t}\right),~1\leq p,q\leq I_{1}

(20)

i.e. the 1st order synchronization can be realized if
c_{1}\lambda_{2}^{(1)}+\alpha<0
.
Similarly, we can derive that for any fixed
1\leq i_{1}\leq I_{1}
and
1\leq i_{3}\leq I_{3}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{i_{1},p,i_{3}}(t)-x_{i_{1},q,i_{3}}(t)\|=O\left(e^{2(c_{2}\lambda_{2}^{(2)}+\alpha)t}\right),~1\leq p,q\leq I_{2}

(21)

and for any fixed
1\leq i_{1}\leq I_{1}
and
1\leq i_{2}\leq I_{2}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},p}(t)-x_{i_{1},i_{2},q}(t)\|=O\left(e^{2(c_{3}\lambda_{2}^{(3)}+\alpha)t}\right),~1\leq p,q\leq I_{3}.

(22)

From (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
), we have that the
k
th order synchronization can be realized if
c_{k}\lambda_{2}^{(k)}+\alpha<0
,
k=1,2,3
. This completes the proof.
∎

Combing (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
), we have that for any
1\leq i_{k},j_{k}\leq I_{k}
,
k=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle\|x_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}(t)-x_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}}(t)\|=O\left(e^{\max_{k}2(c_{k}\lambda_{2}^{(k)}+\alpha)t}\right)

(23)

This implies that complete synchronization of the 3rd order layered network can be realized if
\max_{k=1,2,3}(c_{k}\lambda_{2}^{(k)}+\alpha)<0
, which leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose Assumptions 1 and 2 are satisfied. Then the
m
th order layered network (
I
) will realize complete synchronization if
\max_{k=1,\cdots,m}(c_{k}\lambda_{2}^{(k)}+\alpha)<0
.

It is clear that the multiplex network (
I
) is a special case of model (
I
) with
m=2
,
L^{(1)}=A
and
L^{(2)}=B
. Therefore, by Theorem
1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Consider the multiplex network (
I
). Suppose Assumption 1 is satisfied and coupling matrices
A
and
B
are symmetric. The eigenvalues of
A
are
0=\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{N}
. The eigenvalues of
B
are
0=\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{K}
. Then the multiplex network will realize i) intra-layer synchronization if
c\mu_{2}+\alpha<0
, ii) inter-layer synchronization if
d\nu_{2}+\alpha<0
, and iii) complete synchronization if
c\max\{\mu_{2},\nu_{2}\}+\alpha<0
.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigated synchronization of the high order layered network (
I
) and explored relations between synchronization and the eigenvalues of the coupling matrices. An effective theoretical analysis was provided. Numerical examples confirmed our analytic results.

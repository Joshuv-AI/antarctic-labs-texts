A golden ratio primal-dual algorithm for structured convex optimization
Xiaokai Chang 1, Junfeng Yang 2

Abstract
We design, analyze and test a golden ratio primal-dual algorithm (GRPDA) for solving structured convex optimization problem, where the objective function is the sum of two closed proper convex functions, one of which involves a composition with a linear transform. GRPDA preserves all the favorable features of the classical primal-dual algorithm (PDA), i.e., the primal and the dual variables are updated in a Gauss-Seidel manner, and the per iteration cost is dominated by the evaluation of the proximal point mappings of the two component functions and two matrix-vector multiplications. Compared with the classical PDA, which takes an extrapolation step, the novelty of GRPDA is that it is constructed based on a convex combination of essentially the whole iteration trajectory. We show that GRPDA converges within a broader range of parameters than the classical PDA, provided that the reciprocal of the convex combination parameter is bounded above by the golden ratio, which explains the name of the algorithm. An 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / N ) {\mathcal{O}}(1/N) ergodic convergence rate result is also established based on the primal-dual gap function, where N N denotes the number of iterations. When either the primal or the dual problem is strongly convex, an accelerated GRPDA is constructed to improve the ergodic convergence rate from 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / N ) {\mathcal{O}}(1/N) to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / N 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}(1/N^{2}) . Moreover, we show for regularized least-squares and linear equality constrained problems that the reciprocal of the convex combination parameter can be extended from the golden ratio to 2 2 and meanwhile a relaxation step can be taken. Our preliminary numerical results on LASSO, nonnegative least-squares and minimax matrix game problems, with comparisons to some state-of-the-art relative algorithms, demonstrate the efficiency of the proposed algorithms.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbb{R}^{q}
be finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, each endowed with an inner product and the induced norm denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
, respectively.
In this paper, we consider structured convex optimization problem of the form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}f(Kx)+g(x),

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
are extended real-valued closed proper convex functions
Rockafellar1970Convex
, and
K\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}
is a linear operator from
\mathbb{R}^{q}
to
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
Problem (
1
) arises from numerous applications, including signal and image processing, machine learning, statistics, mechanics and economics, to name a few, see, e.g.,
Chambolle2011A
;
Bouwmans2016Handbook
;
Yang2011Alternating
;
Hayden2013A
;
Bertsekas1982Projection

and the references therein.
The saddle point or primal-dual form of (
1
) reads

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}~~\mathcal{L}(x,y):=g(x)+\langle Kx,y\rangle-f^{*}(y),

(2)

where
f^{*}(y)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\langle y,u\rangle-f(u)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, is the Legendre-Fenchel conjugate of
f
.
Saddle point problems are ubiquitous in optimization as they provide a very convenient way to represent many nonsmooth problems. In particular, (
2
) is intrinsically related to the Fenchel dual problem of (
1
), which is given by

\displaystyle\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}-f^{*}(y)-g^{*}(-K^{\top}y),

(3)

where
K^{\top}
denotes the matrix transpose or adjoint operator of
K
.

We note that in this paper we restrict the decision variable
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{q}
, yet all the analysis can be extended to any finite dimensional real Euclidean spaces, e.g., the matrix space
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with trace inner product and the induced Frobenius norm.
In the rest of this section, we define some notation, make our assumptions, review some algorithms for solving (
1
)-(
3
) that are closely related to this work, and summarize our contributions and the organization of this paper.

1.1
Notation and assumptions

The matrix and vector transpose operation is denoted by superscript “
\top
”.
The operator norm of
K
is denoted by
L
, i.e.,
L:=\|K\|=\|K^{\top}\|=\sup\{\langle Kx,y\rangle:\|x\|=\|y\|=1,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{q},\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}
. Throughout this paper, we denote the golden ratio by
\phi
, i.e.,
\phi={\sqrt{5}+1\over 2}
.
Let
h
be any extended real-valued closed proper convex function defined on a finite dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The effective domain of
h
is denoted by
\text{dom}(h):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:h(x)<+\infty\}
, and the subdifferential of
h
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is denoted by
\partial h(x):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\,h(y)\geq h(x)+\langle\xi,y-x\rangle\text{~for all~}y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}
. Furthermore, for
\lambda>0
, the proximal point mapping of
\lambda h
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Prox}_{\lambda h}(x):=\arg\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big\{h(y)+{1\over 2\lambda}\|y-x\|^{2}\Big\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Since
h
is closed proper and convex, for any
\lambda>0
,
{\rm Prox}_{\lambda h}
is uniquely well defined everywhere.
The indicator function of a set
C
is denoted by
\iota_{C}(x)
, i.e.,
\iota_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and
+\infty
if otherwise.
The relative interior of
C
is denoted by
\text{ri}(C)
.
The identity operator or identity matrix is denoted by
I
, whose domain or order is clear from the context.
The zero vector or matrix is simply denoted by
0
. The composition of two operators is denoted by “
\circ
”.
The stagnation of two column vectors
u
and
v
is also denoted by
(u;v)
, i.e.,
(u;v)=(u^{\top},v^{\top})^{\top}
.
The sequence of positive natural numbers is denoted by
{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
.
Other notation will be specified later.

Throughout the paper, we make the following blanket assumptions.

Assumption 1.1

Assume that the set of solutions of (
1
) is nonempty and,
in addition, there exists
\tilde{x}\in\text{ri}(\text{dom}(g))
such that
K\tilde{x}\in\text{ri}(\text{dom}(f))
.

Under Assumption
1.1
, it follows from
(
Rockafellar1970Convex
, Corollaries 28.2.2 and 28.3.1)
that

{\bar{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
is a solution of (
1
) if and only if there exists
{\bar{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
is a saddle point of
{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{L}}({\bar{x}},y)\leq{\mathcal{L}}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\leq{\mathcal{L}}(x,{\bar{y}})
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and furthermore, such
\bar{y}
is an optimal solution of the dual problem (
3
).
Throughout this paper, we denote the set of solutions of (
2
) by
{\mathcal{S}}
, which is nonempty under Assumption
1.1
and
characterized by

{\mathcal{S}}:=\{({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}:\;0\in\partial g({\bar{x}})+K^{\top}{\bar{y}}\text{~~and~~}0\in\partial f^{*}({\bar{y}})-K{\bar{x}}\}.

(4)

In many applications including signal and image processing and machine learning, the two component functions in (
1
) enforce, respectively, data fitting and regularization. In such cases,
f
and
g
usually preserve simple structures so that their proximal point mappings can be evaluated efficiently. Examples of such functions are abundant, see, e.g.,
(
Beck2017book
, Chapter 6)
. We therefore make the following assumption.

Assumption 1.2

Assume that the proximal point mappings of the component functions
f
and
g
either have closed form formulas or can be evaluated efficiently.

1.2
Related algorithms

To solve (
1
)-(
3
) simultaneously, one may resort to the well known alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM)
GM75
;
GabM76
;
Jonathan1992On
;
Lions1979Splitting
,
the primal-dual algorithm (PDA)
Chambolle2011A
;
He2012Convergence
;
Pock2011Diagonal
;
Esser2010General
and their accelerated and generalized variants
Liu2018Acceleration
;
Malitsky2018A
.
Since the literature on numerical algorithms for solving (
1
)-(
3
) has become so vast, a thorough overview is far beyond the focus of this paper. Instead, we next review only some primal-dual type algorithms that are most closely related to our work.

A main feature of primal-dual type algorithms is that both the primal and the dual variables are updated at each iteration, and thus the primal and the dual problems are solved simultaneously. Among others, ADMM
GM75
;
GabM76
has been well studied in the literature
Lions1979Splitting
;
Jonathan1992On
;
Fazel2013Hankel
and widely used in practice
Boyd2010Distributed
. The main difficulty encountered by ADMM when applied to (a reformulation of) (
1
) is that a subproblem of the form
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\frac{1}{2}\|Kx-b_{n}\|^{2}+g(x)
needs to be solved at each iteration for some
b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
varying with the iteration counter
n
. We note that even though
{\rm Prox}_{g}
is easy to evaluate, this problem, which involves a linear operator
K
, has to be solved iteratively in general. On the other hand, when
f(Kx)
has a least-squares structure, i.e.,
f(Kx)={1\over 2}\|Kx-b\|^{2}
for some
b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, ADMM can be applied to the equivalent problem
\min_{x,y}\{f(Kx)+g(y):\;x=y\}
. In this case, the
x
-subproblem appears as a least-squares problem and is equivalent to solving a linear system of equations with coefficient matrix of the form
I+\rho K^{\top}K
for some
\rho>0
, which could be expensive or even prohibitive for large scale problems.

The simplest primal-dual type algorithm for solving (
1
)-(
3
), which does not require to solve any subproblem iteratively or any linear system of equations, is probably the classical Arrow-Hurwicz method
Uzawa58
, which, started at
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, iterates as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{n}={\rm Prox}_{\tau g}(x_{n-1}-\tau K^{\top}y_{n-1}),\\
y_{n}={\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(y_{n-1}+\sigma Kx_{n}),\end{array}\right.

for
n\geq 1
. Here
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
are step size parameters. As a conjugate function,
f^{*}
is always closed and convex. Furthermore,

f^{*}
is also proper since
f
is proper and convex. Thus,
{\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}
is uniquely well defined everywhere. The iterative scheme (
1.2
) is also known as primal dual hybrid gradient method in image processing community, see
ZhC08cam
;
Esser2010General
;
Chambolle2011A
. The main computational cost per iteration is the evaluation of two proximal point mappings and two matrix-vector multiplications. The convergence of the Arrow-Hurwicz method was studied in
Esser2010General
with very small step sizes, and
{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{N})
rate of convergence, measured by primal-dual gap, was obtained in
Chambolle2011A
;
Nedic2009Subgradient
when the domain of
f^{*}
is assumed to be bounded. However, the Arrow-Hurwicz method does not converge in general, see
He2012Convergence
for a divergent example.

Chambolle and Pock
Chambolle2011A
;
Pock2011Diagonal
adopted an extrapolation step after obtaining
x_{n}
in (
1.2
) to obtain
{\bar{x}}_{n}=x_{n}+\delta(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
for some
\delta\in[0,1]
, which is then used to replace
x_{n}
in (
1.2
) in the update of
y_{n}
. The resulting scheme is nowadays widely accepted as PDA and appears as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{n}={\rm Prox}_{\tau g}(x_{n-1}-\tau K^{\top}y_{n-1}),\\
{\bar{x}}_{n}=x_{n}+\delta(x_{n}-x_{n-1}),\\
y_{n}={\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(y_{n-1}+\sigma K{\bar{x}}_{n}).\end{array}\right.

In the case
\delta=1
, the convergence of (
1.2
) was established in
Chambolle2011A
under the condition
\tau\sigma L^{2}<1
, where
L=\|K\|
.
Note that, for
\delta=1
the scheme (
1.2
) is referred to as a split inexact Uzawa method by
Esser, Zhang and Chan
Esser2010General
, where the connection of PDA with preconditioned or linearized ADMM has been revealed, see also
Chambolle2011A
;
Shefi2014Rate
. Later, it was shown in
He2012Convergence
that PDA (
1.2
) can be viewed as a weighted proximal point method applied to the variational inequality (VI) representation of the optimality conditions of (
2
).
The overrelaxed, inertial and accelerated versions of (
1.2
) were investigated in
Chambolle2016ergodic
. See also
Chambolle2018STOCHASTIC
for a stochastic variant of PDA.

The effectiveness of the PDA scheme (
1.2
) is essentially guaranteed by the extrapolation or inertial step
{\bar{x}}_{n}=x_{n}+\delta(x_{n}-x_{n-1})
since the Arrow-Hurwicz method (
1.2
), which corresponds to
\delta=0
, fails to converge in general. For a fixed
\delta\in[0,1]
, the scheme (
1.2
) can be written abstractly as
(x_{n},y_{n})=T(x_{n-1},y_{n-1})
for some suitably defined mapping
T
.
Therefore, PDA can be viewed as a one-step fixed point iterative method.
Recently, Malitsky
Malitsky2019Golden
proposed a fully adaptive forward-backward type splitting algorithm, called golden ratio algorithm, for solving mixed VI problem, where a novel convex combination technique is introduced. It is shown that the golden ratio algorithm preserves
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic convergence and R-linear convergence under an error bound condition.
The mixed VI problem is to find
z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\theta(z)-\theta(z^{*})+\langle z-z^{*},F(z^{*})\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(12)

where
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is a closed proper convex function, and
F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a monotone mapping.
Given an initial point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
{\bar{z}}_{0}=z_{0}
, a basic version of the golden ratio algorithm
Malitsky2019Golden
applied to (
12
) iterates for
n\geq 1
as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\bar{z}_{n}&=&{\phi-1\over\phi}z_{n-1}+{1\over\phi}{\bar{z}}_{n-1},\\
z_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau\theta}(\bar{z}_{n}-\tau F(z_{n-1})),\end{array}\right.

(13)

where
\tau>0
is a step size parameter and
\phi
is the golden ratio.
By induction,
\bar{z}_{n}
is a convex combination of
\{z_{i}:i=0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
As a result, the update from
z_{n-1}
to
z_{n}
in (
13
) is essentially dependent on the whole iteration trajectory, which is vastly different from the one-step iterative scheme (
1.2
).
Note that the primal-dual problem (
2
) is equivalent to (
12
) with
\mathbb{R}^{m}=\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, endowed with the natural inner product
\langle(x,y),(u,v)\rangle:=\langle x,u\rangle+\langle y,v\rangle
for
(x,y),(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

\theta(z):=g(x)+f^{*}(y)\text{~~and~~}F(z):=(K^{\top}y,-Kx).

(14)

Given the special structure of
\theta
and
F
in (
14
), the golden ratio algorithm (
13
) breaks into

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}\bar{x}_{n}&=&{\phi-1\over\phi}x_{n-1}+{1\over\phi}{\bar{x}}_{n-1},\;\;x_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau g}(\bar{x}_{n}-\tau K^{\top}y_{n-1}),\\
\bar{y}_{n}&=&{\phi-1\over\phi}y_{n-1}+{1\over\phi}{\bar{y}}_{n-1},\;\;y_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau f^{*}}(\bar{y}_{n}+\tau Kx_{n-1}).\end{array}\right.

(15)

Apparently, (
15
) is a Jacobian type algorithm, which fails to fully utilize the latest available information.
Furthermore, the primal and dual step sizes are identically
\tau
, which offers less flexibility.
Given the nice convergence properties and the promising numerical results of golden ratio algorithm
Malitsky2019Golden
,
it is desirable to construct a Gauss-Seidel type golden ratio PDA for the saddle point problem (
2
), which is able to fully take advantage of the problem structure. This motivated the current work.

1.3
Contributions

We adapt the convex combination technique
Malitsky2019Golden
into the Arrow-Hurwicz scheme (
1.2
) to
construct a Gauss-Seidel type golden ratio PDA (abbreviated as GRPDA), which not only preserves nice convergence properties but also performs favorably in practice. Our main contributions are summarized below.

•

Using the convex combination technique
Malitsky2019Golden
, we construct a GRPDA with fixed step size parameters
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
. Under the condition
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi\in(1,\phi]
, global convergence and
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic rate of convergence measured by primal-dual gap function are established.
Since
\tau\sigma L^{2}>1
is permitted, GRPDA converges in a broader range of parameters compared to PDA, which typically requires
\tau\sigma L^{2}<1
.
It is further explained that GRPDA is an ADMM-like method with a proximal term
\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{n-1}\|_{M}^{2}
, where
M=\frac{1}{\tau}I-\sigma K^{\top}K
, plus an extra linear term. As a consequence of the extra linear term, the weighting matrix
M
is allowed to be indefinite.

•

When either
g
or
f^{*}
is strongly convex, an accelerated GRPDA is constructed to improve the ergodic convergence rate from
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
to
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N^{2})
.

•

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. For regularized least-squares problem, i.e.,
f(\cdot)={1\over 2}\|\cdot-b\|^{2}
, or linear equality constrained problem, i.e.,
f(\cdot)=\iota_{\{b\}}(\cdot)
, we show via spectral analysis that the permitted range of
\psi
can be extended from
(1,\phi]
to
(1,2]
and meanwhile a relaxation step can be taken as well, where the relaxation parameter lies in
(0,3/2)
. The analysis for these two special cases is based on the fixed point theory of averaged operators.

•

We carry out numerical experiments on LASSO, nonnegative least-squares and minimax matrix game problems, with comparisons to some state-of-the-art algorithms, to demonstrate the favorable performance of the proposed algorithms.

Preliminaries

In this section, we summarize some useful facts and identities and define the primal-dual gap function, which will be useful in our analysis.

Fact 2.1

For any extended real-valued closed proper convex function
h
defined on an Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
it holds that
p={\rm Prox}_{\lambda h}(x)
if and only if

\displaystyle\langle p-x,y-p\rangle\geq\lambda\big(h(p)-h(y)\big),~~\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Fact 2.2

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be two nonnegative real sequences. If there exists an integer
N>0
such that

a_{n+1}\leq a_{n}-b_{n}
for all
n>N
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}
exists and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}b_{n}=0
.

The proofs of Facts
2.1
and
2.2
are easily derived and thus are omitted.
The following elementary identities will be used in our analsis. For any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there hold

\displaystyle\langle x-y,x-z\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x-z\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|y-z\|^{2},

(16)

\displaystyle\|\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\|x\|^{2}+(1-\alpha)\|y\|^{2}-\alpha(1-\alpha)\|x-y\|^{2}.

(17)

We next define the primal-dual gap function that will be used in our analysis. Let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\mathcal{S}}
be any saddle point of the primal-dual problem (
2
). Then, there hold
K\bar{x}\in\partial f^{*}(\bar{y})
and
-K^{\top}\bar{y}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
, which are equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}P(x):=g(x)-g(\bar{x})+\langle K^{\top}\bar{y},x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0,\quad~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{q},\\
D(y):=f^{*}(y)-f^{*}(\bar{y})-\langle K\bar{x},y-\bar{y}\rangle\geq 0,\quad~\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}.\end{array}\right.

The primal-dual gap function is defined by

\displaystyle G(x,y):=P(x)+D(y)\geq 0,~\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}.

(19)

This primal-dual gap function is also used in, e.g.,
Malitsky2018A
;
Chambolle2016ergodic
. Note that, for fixed
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
P(x)
and
D(y)
, and thus
G(x,y)
, are convex. Although
P(\cdot)
,
D(\cdot)
and
G(\cdot,\cdot)
depend on
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, we do not indicate this dependence in our notation since it is always clear from the context.

3
Golden ratio primal-dual algorithm

In this section, we present our GRPDA with fixed step sizes and establish its convergence and
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic convergence rate.

3.1
GRPDA with fixed step sizes

Recall that
\phi
represents the golden ratio and
L=\|K\|
is the operator norm of
K
.
Below, we introduce our GRPDA with fixed step sizes
\tau>0
and
\sigma>0
.

Algorithm 3.1
(GRPDA with fixed step sizes)

Step 0.

Let
\tau,\sigma>0
and
\psi\in(1,\phi]
be such that
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi
.
Choose
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
,
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Set
z_{0}=x_{0}
and
n=1
.

Step 1.

Compute

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}z_{n}&=&\frac{\psi-1}{\psi}x_{n-1}+\frac{1}{\psi}z_{n-1},\\
x_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau g}(z_{n}-\tau K^{\top}y_{n-1}),\\
y_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(y_{n-1}+\sigma Kx_{n}).\end{array}\right.

Step 2.

Set
n\leftarrow n+1
and return to Step 1.

The GRPDA (
Step 1.
) and the PDA (
1.2
) are quite similar, both of which are modifications of the Arrow-Hurwicz scheme (
1.2
). The difference is that PDA (
1.2
) adopts an inertial or extrapolation technique, while GRPDA (
Step 1.
) uses a convex combination
z_{n}=\frac{\psi-1}{\psi}x_{n-1}+\frac{1}{\psi}z_{n-1}
.
To our knowledge, this convex combination technique with
\psi
upper bounded by the golden ratio
\phi
was initially introduced by Malitsky
Malitsky2019Golden
to solve monotone mixed VI problem (
12
). A direct adaptation of
(
Malitsky2019Golden
, Eq. (10))
to the primal-dual problem (
2
) would give the algorithm (
15
), which is a Jacobian type algorithm and has less flexibility since the primal and the dual step sizes are required to be identical.
In contrast, GRPDA (
Step 1.
) is a combination of
Malitsky2019Golden
with the Arrow-Hurwicz scheme (
1.2
), and it is of Gauss-Seidel type since it utilizes the latest available information. Compared with the direct adaptation (
15
), the primal and the dual step sizes in (
Step 1.
) are not necessarily identical, which offers more flexibility in practice. Moreover, compared with PDA (
1.2
), which requires
\tau\sigma L^{2}<1
, the condition
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi\in(1,\phi]
required by GRPDA (
Step 1.
) permits larger step sizes. Apparently, GRPDA has the same per iteration cost as those of the Arrow-Hurwicz method and the PDA.

3.2
Connection with ADMM and PDA

It is well known that the classical PDA can be interpreted as a preconditioned or linearized ADMM with positive definition proximal term, see
Chambolle2011A
;
Shefi2014Rate
;
Esser2010General
.
As the main difference between GRPDA and PDA is that the extrapolation step in (
1.2
) is replaced by the convex combination in (
Step 1.
), it is natural to connect GRPDA with the ADMM. To be specific, we define the augmented Lagrangian function of

\min_{x,w}\{g(x)+f(w):Kx=w\}
, a reformulation of (
1
), as

{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\sigma}(x,w,y):=g(x)+f(w)+\langle y,Kx-w\rangle+{\sigma\over 2}\|Kx-w\|^{2},

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the Lagrange multiplier and
\sigma>0
is the penalty parameter.
Then, by using the Moreau decomposition
y={\rm Prox}_{\lambda f}(y)+\lambda{\rm Prox}_{{1\over\lambda}f^{*}}({y\over\lambda})
for any
\lambda>0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and following
Chambolle2011A
;
Shefi2014Rate
;
Esser2010General
, it is easy to show that GRPDA (
Step 1.
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}z_{n}&=&\frac{\psi-1}{\psi}x_{n-1}+{1\over\psi}z_{n-1},\\
x_{n}&=&\arg\min\limits_{x}\left\{{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\sigma}(x,w_{n-1},y_{n-1})+\ell(x,z_{n},x_{n-1},w_{n-1})+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{n-1}\|^{2}_{M}\right\},\\
w_{n}&=&\arg\min\limits_{w}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\sigma}(x_{n},w,y_{n-1}),\\
y_{n}&=&y_{n-1}+\sigma(Kx_{n}-w_{n}),\end{array}\right.

where
\ell(x,z_{n},x_{n-1},w_{n-1})={1\over\tau}\langle x,z_{n}-x_{n-1}\rangle-\sigma\langle Kx,Kx_{n-1}-w_{n-1}\rangle
,
M={1\over\tau}I-\sigma K^{\top}K
and
w_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is arbitrary. Here the proximal term
\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{n-1}\|^{2}_{M}
is used to cancel out
{\sigma\over 2}\|Kx\|^{2}
in the
x
-subproblem. Since, by following
Chambolle2011A
;
Shefi2014Rate
;
Esser2010General
, the derivation of (
3.2
) from (
Step 1.
) is standard, we omit the details.
By discarding the linear term
\ell(x,z_{n},x_{n-1},w_{n-1})
in (
3.2
) and using the Moreau decomposition to carry out a similar reduction, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\bar{y}}_{n-1}&=&2y_{n-1}-y_{n-2},\\
x_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau g}\big(x_{n-1}-\tau K^{\top}{\bar{y}}_{n-1}\big),\\
y_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(y_{n}+\sigma Kx_{n}),\end{array}\right.

which is a form of PDA with
\delta=1
.
Note that PDA requires
\tau\sigma L^{2}<1
to guarantee the positive definiteness of
M
. In comparison, by introducing the linear term
\ell(x,z_{n},x_{n-1},w_{n-1})
, GRPDA (
3.2
) allows larger step sizes since
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi\in(1,\phi]
suffices for global convergence. In this case,
M
is permitted to be indefinite. This broader convergence region of GRPDA is beneficial in practice.

3.3
Convergence results

We next establish the convergence and
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic convergence rate of GRPDA (
Step 1.
). For convenience, we let
\beta:=\sigma/\tau
in the rest of this section. First, we present a useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
\{(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
from any initial point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
z_{0}=x_{0}
, and let

be defined as in (

. Then, it holds for any

\displaystyle\tau G(x_{n},y_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle x_{n+1}-z_{n+1},\bar{x}-x_{n+1}\rangle+{1\over\beta}\left\langle y_{n}-y_{n-1},\bar{y}-y_{n}\right\rangle+\psi\left\langle x_{n}-z_{n+1},x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\rangle

(30)

\displaystyle+\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle.

Proof. It follows from (
Step 1.
) and Fact
2.1
that

\displaystyle\langle x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}+\tau K^{\top}y_{n},~\bar{x}-x_{n+1}\rangle\geq\tau\big(g(x_{n+1})-g(\bar{x})\big),

(31)

\displaystyle\big\langle{1\over\beta}(y_{n}-y_{n-1})-\tau Kx_{n},~\bar{y}-y_{n}\big\rangle\geq\tau\big(f^{*}(y_{n})-f^{*}(\bar{y})\big).

(32)

Similar to (
31
), it holds that

\displaystyle\langle x_{n}-z_{n}+\tau K^{\top}y_{n-1},~x_{n+1}-x_{n}\rangle\geq\tau\big(g(x_{n})-g(x_{n+1})\big).

(33)

Elementary calculations show that the addition of (
31
), (
32
) and (
33
) gives

\displaystyle\langle x_{n+1}-z_{n+1},~\bar{x}-x_{n+1}\rangle+{1\over\beta}\big\langle y_{n}-y_{n-1},~\bar{y}-y_{n}\big\rangle+\left\langle x_{n}-z_{n},~x_{n+1}-x_{n}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),~x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle-\tau\langle K^{\top}\bar{y},~x_{n}-\bar{x}\rangle+\tau\langle K\bar{x},~y_{n}-\bar{y}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\tau\big(f^{*}(y_{n})-f^{*}(\bar{y})\big)+\tau\big(g(x_{n})-g(\bar{x})\big),

which is the same as (
30
) by considering
x_{n}-z_{n}=\psi(x_{n}-z_{n+1})
(implied by the first equality in (
Step 1.
)) and following the definition of
G(\cdot,\cdot)
in (
19
). This completes the proof.

\Box

Now, we are ready to establish the convergence of GRPDA (
Step 1.
).

Theorem 3.1

Let
\{(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
from any initial point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
z_{0}=x_{0}
, and let

be defined as in (
19
) with any fixed

. Then,
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to a solution of (
2
), i.e., an element of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Proof. From Lemma
3.1
and (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-\bar{y}\|^{2}+2\tau G(x_{n},y_{n})

(34)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n-1}-\bar{y}\|^{2}+2\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),~x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\psi\|z_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}+(\psi-1)\|x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}-\psi\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}-{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|^{2}.

Since
x_{n+1}={\psi\over\psi-1}z_{n+2}-{1\over\psi-1}z_{n+1}
, it follows from (
17
) that

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+2}-\bar{x}\|^{2}-{1\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{\psi\over(\psi-1)^{2}}\|z_{n+2}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+2}-\bar{x}\|^{2}-{1\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\psi}\|x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}\|^{2},

where the second equality is due to
z_{n+2}-z_{n+1}={\psi-1\over\psi}(x_{n+1}-z_{n+1})
.
By plugging (
35
) into (
34
) and combining terms, we obtain

\displaystyle{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+2}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-\bar{y}\|^{2}+2\tau G(x_{n},y_{n})

(36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n-1}-\bar{y}\|^{2}+2\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),~x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\psi\|z_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}+(\psi-1-\frac{1}{\psi})\|x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}-\psi\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}-{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n-1}-\bar{y}\|^{2}+2\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),~x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle-\psi\|z_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}-\psi\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}-{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|^{2},

where the second “
\leq
” follows from
\psi-1-\frac{1}{\psi}\leq 0
as
\psi\leq\phi
.
Recall that
\beta=\sigma/\tau
. Then,
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi
is equivalent to
\tau<\sqrt{{\psi/\beta}}/L
.
Let
\tau=\zeta\sqrt{{\psi/\beta}}/L
with

. It follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
L=\|K^{\top}\|
that

\displaystyle 2\tau\langle K^{\top}(y_{n}-y_{n-1}),~x_{n}-x_{n+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\zeta\sqrt{{\psi/\beta}}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta\Big(\psi\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|^{2}\Big),

which together with (
36
) leads to

\displaystyle 2\tau G(x_{n},y_{n})+a_{n+1}\leq a_{n}-b_{n},

(37)

with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}a_{n}&=&{\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{n+1}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n-1}-\bar{y}\|^{2},\\
b_{n}&=&\psi\|z_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}+(1-\zeta)\big(\psi\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{n}-y_{n-1}\|^{2}\big).\end{array}\right.

Since
G(x_{n},y_{n})\geq 0
,
a_{n}\geq 0
and
b_{n}\geq 0
, it follows from Fact
2.2
that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}
exists and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}b_{n}=0
.
By the definition of
b_{n}
, this implies

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|z_{n+1}-x_{n}\|=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n+1}-y_{n}\|=0.

Furthermore, we also have
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-z_{n}\|=0
since
x_{n}-z_{n}=\psi(x_{n}-z_{n+1})
.

It follows from (
3.3
) and the existence of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}
that the sequences
\{z_{n+1}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\{y_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, and thus

\{(z_{n+1},y_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, are bounded. Since
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n}-z_{n}\|=0
,
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is also bounded.
Let
\{(x_{n_{k}+1},y_{n_{k}})\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a subsequence of
\{(z_{n+1},y_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, which is convergent to
(x^{*},y^{*})
.
Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n_{k}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n_{k}+1}=x^{*}
.
Similar to (
31
) and (
32
), for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
, there hold

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\langle x_{n_{k}+1}-z_{n_{k}+1}+\tau K^{\top}y_{n_{k}},~x-x_{n_{k}+1}\rangle\geq\tau\big(g(x_{n_{k}+1})-g(x)\big),\\
\big\langle{1\over\beta}(y_{n_{k}}-y_{n_{k}-1})-\tau Kx_{n_{k}},~y-y_{n_{k}}\big\rangle\geq\tau\big(f^{*}(y_{n_{k}})-f^{*}(y)\big).\end{array}\right.

By driving

, taking into account that both
g
and
f^{*}
are closed (and thus lower semicontinuous) and cancelling out
\tau>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle K^{\top}y^{*},~x-x^{*}\rangle\geq g(x^{*})-g(x)\text{~~and~~}-\langle Kx^{*},~y-y^{*}\rangle\geq f^{*}(y^{*})-f^{*}(y),

which hold for any
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
. This implies that
(x^{*},y^{*})\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
Since all the discussions remain valid for any
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
can be replaced by
(x^{*},y^{*})

in the definition of

in the first place. As such, we have

since

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|z_{n_{k}+1}-x^{*}\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|x_{n_{k}}-x^{*}\|=0\text{~~and~~}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n_{k}-1}-x^{*}\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|y_{n_{k}}-x^{*}\|=0.

Since
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is monotonically nonincreasing, it follows that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0
and thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}z_{n+1}=x^{*}\text{~~and~~}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}y_{n}=y^{*}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

Remark 3.1

Our analysis is motivated by Malitsky
Malitsky2019Golden
. In particular, to get rid of the term
\|x_{n+1}-z_{n+1}\|^{2}
in (

), it is required that
\psi-1-{1\over\psi}\leq 0
, and apparently the golden ratio is an upper bound of such
\psi
. As long as
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi
is satisfied, larger

allows larger step sizes
\tau
and

On the other hand,

3.4
Ergodic convergence rate

For convex-concave saddle point problems, many algorithms exhibit
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic rate of convergence, see
Chambolle2016ergodic
;
Monteiro2011Complexity
;
Nemirovski2006Prox-Method
. It is shown by Nemirovski
Nemirovski2006Prox-Method
that this rate is in fact tight.
We next establish the same ergodic rate of convergence for GRPDA, where the measure is the primal-dual gap function defined in (
19
).

Theorem 3.2

Let
\{(x_{n},y_{n},z_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
from any initial point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{q}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
z_{0}=x_{0}
, and let
G(\cdot,\cdot)
be defined as in (
19
) with any fixed
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Let
N\geq 1
be any integer and define

\displaystyle X_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum\nolimits_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}~~\mbox{and}~~Y_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum\nolimits_{n=1}^{N}y_{n}.

(41)

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle G(X_{N},Y_{N})\leq\frac{1}{2\tau N}\Big({\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{2}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{0}-\bar{y}\|^{2}\Big).

Proof. It follows from (
37
) and (
3.3
) that
2\tau G(x_{n},y_{n})\leq a_{n}-a_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Sum this up for
n=1,\ldots,N
, we obtain
2\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}G(x_{n},y_{n})\leq a_{1}-a_{N+1}\leq a_{1}
.
Since
G(x,y)=P(x)+D(y)
and
P(x)
and
D(y)
are, respectively, convex in
x
and
y
, it follows from (
41
) that

\displaystyle G(X_{N},Y_{N})=P(X_{N})+D(Y_{N})\leq{1\over N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}P(x_{n})+{1\over N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}D(y_{n})={1\over N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}G(x_{n},y_{n}).

The conclusion follows since
a_{1}={\psi\over\psi-1}\|z_{2}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+{1\over\beta}\|y_{0}-\bar{y}\|^{2}
.

\Box

4
Accelerated GRPDA

When either
g
or
f^{*}
is strongly convex, it was shown in
Chambolle2011A
that one can adaptively choose the primal and the dual step sizes, as well as the inertial parameter, so that PDA achieves a faster
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N^{2})
convergence rate. Similar results have been achieved in
Malitsky2018A
for PDA with variable step sizes from linesearch.
In this section, we show that the same is true for GRPDA provided that the parameters are chosen adaptively. Similar to
Chambolle2011A
;
Malitsky2018A
, the ratio
\beta=\sigma/\tau
plays an important role.

Under the regularity condition specified in Assumption
1.1
, the “
\min
” and the “
\max
” in the primal dual problem (
2
) can be switched
(
Rockafellar1970Convex
, Corollary 31.2.1)
, and thus (
2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}~~f^{*}(y)-\langle K^{\top}y,x\rangle-g(x),

(42)

As such, by switching the roles of
(g,K,x,q)
and
(f^{*},-K^{T},y,p)
, (
42
) is reducible to (
2
). Therefore, we will only
treat the case when
g
is strongly convex for succinctness, and it is completely analogues when
f^{*}
is strongly convex.
In this section, we thus make the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1

Assume that
g
is
\gamma
-strongly convex, i.e., for some
\gamma>0
it holds that

\displaystyle g(y)\geq g(x)+\langle u,y-x\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},~~\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{q},\;\forall\,u\in\partial g(x).

Our accelerated GRPDA is presented below. Recall that
\phi
denotes the golden ratio.

Algorithm 4.1
(Accelerated GRPDA when
g
is
\gamma
-strongly convex)

Step 0.

Let
\psi_{0}=1.3247...
be the unique real root of
\psi^{3}-\psi-1=0
.
Choose
\psi\in(\psi_{0},\phi)
,
\beta_{0}>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and set

z_{0}=x_{0}
,
\varphi=\frac{1+\psi}{\psi^{2}}
,
\tau_{0}=\frac{1}{L}\sqrt{{\psi\over\beta_{0}}}
and
n=1
.

Step 1.

Compute

\displaystyle z_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\psi-1\over\psi}x_{n-1}+\frac{1}{\psi}z_{n-1},

(43)

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Prox}_{\tau_{n-1}g}(z_{n}-\tau_{n-1}K^{\top}y_{n-1}).

(44)

Step 2.

Compute

\displaystyle\omega_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\psi-\varphi}{\psi+\varphi\gamma\tau_{n-1}},

(45)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{n-1}(1+\omega_{n}\gamma\tau_{n-1}),

(46)

\displaystyle\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\varphi\tau_{n-1},~~\frac{\psi}{\tau_{n-1}\beta_{n}L^{2}}\Big\},

(47)

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Prox}_{\beta_{n}\tau_{n}f^{*}}(y_{n-1}+\beta_{n}\tau_{n}Kx_{n}).

(48)

Step 3.

Set
n\leftarrow n+1
and return to Step 1.

Remark 4.1

We present the following remarks on Algorithm
4.1
.

1.

In Algorithm
4.1
,
\omega_{n}
is used to update
\beta_{n}
, which plays a key role in establishing the

convergence rate. The condition

, where

2.

To achieve the

\tau_{n}

It follows from

3.

Assume

,

for all

Therefore, Algorithm
4.1
reduces to Algorithm

, i.e., GRPDA with fixed step sizes

in Algorithm

Proof. Since

Further considering

all the relations in (

5
GRPDA for two special cases

This section is devoted to GRPDA for two special cases of the structured convex optimization problem (
1
), which are specified in the following assumption.

Assumption 5.1

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be given.
Assume that either
f(\cdot)=\iota_{\{b\}}(\cdot)
, the indicator function of the singleton
\{b\}
, or

f(\cdot)={1\over 2}\|\cdot-b\|^{2}
.

The two cases specified in Assumption
5.1
correspond respectively to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\{g(x):Kx=b\}\text{~~and~~}\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\frac{1}{2}\|Kx-b\|^{2}+g(x),

which are abundant in practice. For example, linear inverse problems, which include many signal and image processing applications, usually enforce data fitting via
f(Kx)
, which takes the form
\frac{1}{2}\|Kx-b\|^{2}
for noisy data and
\iota_{\{b\}}(Kx)
, or equivalently
Kx=b
, for ideal noiseless data.
A well known application is compressive signal/image sensing
Dono2006Compressed
;
Needell2013compressed
, which recovers sparse or compressible signals from a small number of linear measurements via solving the basis pursuit and/or the LASSO problems
Tibshirani1996Regression
;
Chen2001Atomic
.

The aim of this section is to propose a relaxed GRPDA when
f
satisfies Assumption
5.1
, which is a modification of Algorithm
3.1
in two aspects: (i) extending the permitted range of
\psi
from
(1,\phi]
to
(1,2]
, and (ii) introducing a relaxation step.
In the rest of this section, we always assume that
f
satisfies Assumption
5.1
.
Then, for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\sigma>0
, it is easy to show that
{\rm Prox}_{\sigma f^{*}}(u)=\eta u+\varrho b
, where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(\eta,\varrho):=(\eta(\sigma),\varrho(\sigma))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1,-\sigma)&\hbox{\quad if $f(\cdot)=\iota_{\{b\}}(\cdot)$,}\\
{(1,\sigma)\over 1+\sigma}&\hbox{\quad if $f(\cdot)={1\over 2}\|\cdot-b\|^{2}$.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

In both cases, it holds that
\eta=\eta(\sigma)\in(0,1]
for any
\sigma>0
.

5.1
GRPDA as fixed point iteration

The iterative scheme GRPDA (

) applied to (

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{n-1}&=&\eta(y_{n-2}+\sigma Kx_{n-1})+\varrho b,\\

Note that, since

Our choice of (

\min\Big\{1,~~\frac{1}{4(1-\frac{1}{\psi})}\Big\},&~~\psi_{1}=1.\end{array}\right.

By
\psi_{1}\in(0,\psi)
and
\psi>1
, it is elementary to show that
\sqrt{v}<1-u
and
\sqrt{v}<1+u
.
Consequently, we have
\lambda\in(-1,1)
.

•

Case (ii). If
t\neq 0
, we have
|\lambda_{1}-1|^{2}=\eta\psi_{1}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\psi}\Big)
from (
88
). Then, (
87
) implies

(4\eta-2-2s)\Big(1-{1\over\psi}\Big)=\Big(s+1-{2\over\psi}\Big)(s-1)+t^{2},

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(s+1-\frac{2}{\psi}\Big)^{2}+t^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4\eta-2-2s)\Big(1-{1\over\psi}\Big)+\Big(s+1-{2\over\psi}\Big)\Big(2-{2\over\psi}\Big)

(90)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\Big(1-{1\over\psi}\Big)\Big(\eta-{1\over\psi}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{2}{\psi}\Big)^{2}+4\eta\Big(1-\frac{1}{\psi}\Big)-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{2}{\psi}\Big)^{2}+4\Big(1-\frac{1}{\psi}\Big)-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\Big(1-\frac{1}{\psi}\Big)^{2},

where the inequality follows from
\eta\in(0,1]
and
\psi>1
.
Since
\psi\in(1,2]
, a simple geometric argument based on (

) shows that
|\lambda|=\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}\leq 1
.

Combining Cases (i) and (ii), we have shown that
|\lambda|\leq 1
for any
\tau,\sigma>0
and
\psi\in(1,2]
such that

\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi
. This completes the proof.

\Box

5.2
Relaxed GRPDA

Now, we are ready to present a relaxed GRPDA when

satisfies Assumption

The relaxation parameters lie in

Algorithm 5.1
(Relaxed GRPDA)

Step 0.

Let
\tau,\sigma>0
and
\psi\in(1,2]
be such that
\tau\sigma L^{2}<\psi
,
(\eta,\varrho)=(\eta(\sigma),\varrho(\sigma))
be defined in (
5
), and

\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq(0,\frac{3}{2})
be such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{n}(1-\frac{2\rho_{n}}{3})=+\infty
.
Choose
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
and
y_{-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Set
z_{0}=x_{0}
and
n=1
.

Step 1.

Compute

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widetilde{y}_{n-1}&=&\eta(y_{n-2}+\sigma Kx_{n-1})+\varrho b,\\
\widetilde{z}_{n}&=&\frac{\psi-1}{\psi}x_{n-1}+{1\over\psi}z_{n-1},\\
\widetilde{x}_{n}&=&{\rm Prox}_{\tau g}(\widetilde{z}_{n}-\tau K^{\top}\widetilde{y}_{n-1}).\end{array}\right.

Step 2.

Take a relaxation step

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}y_{n-1}&=&y_{n-2}+\rho_{n}(\widetilde{y}_{n-1}-y_{n-2}),\\
z_{n}&=&z_{n-1}+\rho_{n}(\widetilde{z}_{n}-z_{n-1}),\\
x_{n}&=&x_{n-1}+\rho_{n}(\widetilde{x}_{n}-x_{n-1}).\end{array}\right.

Step 3.

Set
n\leftarrow n+1
and return to Step 1.

5.1
is established in the following theorem.
The key of its proof is to observe, based on Theorem
5.1
and
(
Bauschke2011book
, Proposition 4.32)

the operator
\mathbb{S}\circ\mathbb{T}
is
2/3
-averaged

7
Conclusions

In this paper, motivated by the recent work
Malitsky2019Golden
, we proposed, analyzed and tested a golden ratio primal-dual algorithm (GRPDA), which can be viewed as a new remedy to the classical Arrow-Hurwicz method. Different from the PDA in
Chambolle2011A
, which uses an extrapolation or inertial step, the proposed GRPDA uses a convex combination technique originally introduced in
Malitsky2019Golden
.
Global iterate convergence and
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N)
ergodic rate of convergence, measured by the primal dual gap function, are established.
When either
g
or
f^{*}
is strongly convex, we managed to modify the algorithm so that it enjoys a faster
{\mathcal{O}}(1/N^{2})
ergodic convergence rate.
For two widely used special cases, i.e., regularized least-squares problem and linear equality constrained problem,
we further show via spectral analysis that the algorithmic parameter
\psi
can be enlarged from
(1,\phi]
to
(1,2]
, which allows larger primal and dual step sizes.
Moreover, in the fixed point perspective the iterative mapping is
2/3
-averaged and thus a relaxation step can be taken with parameter
\rho\in(0,2/3)
.
Our preliminary numerical results on LASSO, nonnegative least-squares and minimax matrix game problems show that the proposed algorithms perform favorably. In particular, GRPDA with fixed step sizes is comparable with PDA in general, and the relaxed and accelerated variants can achieve superior performance.

Some interesting issues remain to be investigated. For example, how to modify the proposed algorithms so that they are suitable to the cases when the operator norm
\|K\|
is hard to evaluate, how to adapt the proposed algorithms to more general settings, including the general linearly constrained separable convex optimization problem
\min_{x,y}\{f(x)+g(y):Ax+By=b\}
, or when the operator
K
is nonlinear. Moreover, for large scale finite sum problem, which are abundant in machine learning, it is interesting to design and analyze their stochastic and/or incremental counterparts. We leave these issues for further investigations.

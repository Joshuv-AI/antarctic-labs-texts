Arithmetic properties of orders in imaginary quadratic fields
Ho Yun Jung, Ja Kyung Koo, Dong Hwa Shin, Dong Sung Yoon

Abstract
Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field. For an order 𝒪 \mathcal{O} in K K and a positive integer N N , let K 𝒪 , N K_{\mathcal{O},\,N} be the ray class field of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} modulo N ​ 𝒪 N\mathcal{O} . We deal with various subjects related to K 𝒪 , N K_{\mathcal{O},\,N} , mainly about Galois representations attached to elliptic curves with complex multiplication, form class groups and L L -functions for orders.

Introduction

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field.
In 1935, H. Söhngen (
[
26
]
)
first dealt with ray class fields for orders in
K

which generalizes the usual ray class fields for the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
Later in
[
28

Stevenhagen paid attention to the ray class fields for orders
in order to have explicit description of Shimura’s reciprocity law (Proposition
8.1
).
Furthermore, Cho (
[
4
]
) constructed
the fields in view of certain Diophantine equations by extending the ideas in
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
of discriminant
D_{\mathcal{O}}
.
We denote by
I(\mathcal{O})
the group of
proper fractional
\mathcal{O}
-ideals in the sense of
[
6
,
\lx@sectionsign
7.A]
.
It is well known that every fractional
\mathcal{O}
-ideal is proper
if and only if it is invertible.
We say that a nonzero
\mathcal{O}
-ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is prime to
a positive integer
\ell
if
\mathfrak{a}+\ell\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}
.
Let
P(\mathcal{O})
be the subgroup of
I(\mathcal{O})
consisting of
principal fractional
\mathcal{O}
-ideals.
For a positive integer
N
, we define the subgroups of
I(\mathcal{O})
and
P(\mathcal{O})
as

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{O},\,N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathfrak{a}~|~\mathfrak{a}~\textrm{is a nonzero proper $\mathcal{O}$-ideal
prime to $N$}\rangle,

\displaystyle P_{N}(\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nu\mathcal{O}~|~\nu\in\mathcal{O}\setminus\{0\}~\textrm{and}~\nu\equiv 1\ (\textup{mod}\ N\mathcal{O})\rangle,

(1)

respectively.
In particular, we have
I(\mathcal{O},\,1)=I(\mathcal{O})

(cf.
[
6
, Exercise 7.7]
) and
P_{1}(\mathcal{O})=P(\mathcal{O})

because the field of fractions of
\mathcal{O}
is
K
.
Then the associated quotient group

\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O})=I(\mathcal{O},\,N)/P_{N}(\mathcal{O})

is isomorphic to
a generalized ideal class group
modulo
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}N\mathcal{O}_{K}
, where

\ell_{\mathcal{O}}=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}]
is the conductor of
\mathcal{O}

(cf.
[
20
, Theorem 3.1.8]
).
And the existence theorem of class field theory
asserts that there
is a unique abelian extension
K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}

for which the Artin map
for the modulus
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}N\mathcal{O}_{K}
induces
an isomorphism of the generalized ideal class group
onto
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathcal{O},\,N}/K)

(cf.
[
6
,
\lx@sectionsign
8]
or
[
9
, V.9]
).
We call this extension field

of
K

the
ray class field of
\mathcal{O}
modulo
N\mathcal{O}
.
In particular,

K_{\mathcal{O},\,1}
is just the ring class field
H_{\mathcal{O}}

of order
\mathcal{O}
and
K_{\mathcal{O}_{K},\,N}
is the
ray class field
K_{(N)}
modulo
(N)=N\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

When
D_{\mathcal{O}}\neq-3,\,-4

E_{\mathcal{O}}

with

Then it can be shown that if

And, in the first main theorem (Theorem

Theorem A
.

Assume that
D_{\mathcal{O}}\neq-3,\,-4
.

(i)

If
D\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)
, then

\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathcal{O}}[2])
is the maximal real subfield of
K_{\mathcal{O},\,2}
.

(ii)

If
D\equiv 1\ (\textup{mod}\ 4)

\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathcal{O}}[2])=K_{\mathcal{O},\,2}

(iii)

\mathbb{Q}(E_{\mathcal{O}}[N])

generated by the coordinates of

This fact distinguishes Theorem

of Bourdon-Clark-Pollack ([3, Lemma 8.4]) and Clark-Pollack ([5, Theorem 4.2]).

The generalized ideal class group
isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O})

Throughout this paper, we let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field,
\mathcal{O}
be an order in

and
N
be a positive integer.
We denote the conductor and the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}

by
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}
and
D_{\mathcal{O}}
, respectively.
As far as we know, Schertz’s book
[

is the only reference for the fact that

\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O})
modulo

is isomorphic
to a generalized ideal class group modulo
\ell_{\mathcal{O}}N\mathcal{O}_{K}

In this section, we shall explain this fact in modern terms
by adopting the ideas of
[
6
,
\lx@sectionsign
7]
for

,
which will be definitely helpful to develop our main theorems.

For a positive integer
\ell
, we denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{the monoid of nonzero proper $\mathcal{O}$-ideals prime to $\ell$},

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{the subgroup of $I(\mathcal{O})$
generated by $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)$},

\displaystyle P_{N}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nu\mathcal{O}~|~\nu\in\mathcal{O}\setminus\{0\},~\textrm{$\nu\mathcal{O}$ is prime to $\ell$}~\textrm{and}~\nu\equiv 1\ (\textup{mod}\ N\mathcal{O})\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)/P_{N}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell).

Recall that

and
P_{1}(\mathcal{O},\,1)=P_{1}(\mathcal{O})=P(\mathcal{O})
, and so

\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathcal{O},\,1)=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{O})
.
Furthermore, since

, we have

\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O},\,N)=\mathcal{C}_{N}(\mathcal{O})
.
And, for simplicity, we just write

,

,
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)
for

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O},\,1)
,

,

, respectively.
For

, we denote its norm by

,
namely,
\mathrm{N}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{a})=|\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}|
.

Proof..

See

Remark 2.3

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

.

Proof..

The inclusion

Now, let
\mathfrak{a}\in I(\mathcal{O},\,\ell)\cap\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O})

Since

And we get from the fact

\mathrm{N}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{a})\mathrm{N}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{c})=\mathrm{N}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathfrak{b})

(4)

(ii). Since
\mathfrak{b}
is prime to
\ell

Thus we obtain by (

which implies again by Lemma

is prime to

Therefore we achieve the converse inclusion

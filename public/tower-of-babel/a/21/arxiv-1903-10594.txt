Completeness theorem for the system of eigenfunctions of the complex Schrödinger operator ℒ c = − d 2 / d x 2 + c x 2 / 3 \mathscr{L}_{c}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}+cx^{2/3}
Sergey Tumanov

Abstract
The completeness of the system of eigenfunctions of the complex Schrödinger operator ℒ c = − d 2 / d x 2 + c x 2 / 3 \mathscr{L}_{c}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}+cx^{2/3} on the semi-axis in L 2 ​ ( ℝ + ) L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}) with Dirichlet boundary conditions is proved for all c c : | arg ⁡ c | < π / 2 + θ 0 |\arg c|<\pi/2+\theta_{0} , where θ 0 ∈ ( π / 10 , π / 9 ) \theta_{0}\in(\pi/10,\pi/9) is defined as the only solution of a certain transcendental equation.



Introduction

We consider the operator

\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+cx^{\alpha}

on the semi-axis in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
Dirichlet boundary conditions. The constant
c\in\mathbb{C}
.

It is well-known (
[
1
]
, lemma V.6.1) that the resolvent
R(\lambda)=(\mathscr{L}^{*}_{c,\alpha}-\lambda)^{-1}
is vanishing outside
the closed sector
\Lambda=\{\arg\lambda\in[0,-\arg c]\}
— the
numerical range of
\mathscr{L}^{*}_{c,\alpha}
. It’s also known
[
2
]
that the operator
\mathscr{L}^{*}_{c,\alpha}
is of the order of
2\alpha/(2+\alpha)
.
If
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is ortogonal to all eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
, then
the vector function
R(\lambda)f=(\mathscr{L}^{*}_{c,\alpha}-\lambda)^{-1}f
with values in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

is an entire function with the order of growth
2\alpha/(2+\alpha)
(see §4
[
3
]
).
It follows
from the Phragmen–Lindelöf principle that
if the central angle of the sector
\Lambda
is less than
2\pi\alpha/(2+\alpha)
, i.e.
|\arg c|<2\pi\alpha/(2+\alpha)
, then
R(\lambda)f\equiv 0
,
thus
f\equiv 0
. This prooves the completeness of the system of eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
.

These ideas originated from the work of Keldysh
[
4
]
. Of course, this approach does not provide information about the behavior
of
R(\lambda)f
inside
\Lambda
where a priori
R(\lambda)f
blows up exponentially. But it turns out that if instead of the vector function

R(\lambda)f
we consider the scalar entire function
(R(\lambda)f)(x)
fixing an arbitrary point
x\geq 0
, then this function may be vanishing
in a wider sector than
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Lambda
. This observation turns out to be decisive for the proof of the completeness
theorem under weaker conditions on the argument of
c
.

The proposed approach is likely to make it possible to positively solve the following problem: to prove that
for each
\alpha\in(0,2)
there is
\varepsilon>0
such that the eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}

form a complete system for all
c
:
|\arg c|<2\alpha\pi/(2+\alpha)+\varepsilon
. In this paper we only consider the case of
\alpha=2/3
.

So, our main theorem is dedicated to the operator

\mathscr{L}_{c}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+cx^{2/3}

(1)

and is stated as follows:

Theorem 1
.

There is
\theta_{0}\in(\pi/10,\pi/9)
such that for all
c\in\mathbb{C}
:
|\arg c|<\pi/2+\theta_{0}
the system of eigenfunctions of the operator
\mathscr{L}_{c}

is complete in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Our source of inspiration was the article by Savchuk and Shkalikov
[
2
]
, in which for operators on the semi-axis of the form

\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+cx^{\alpha}

(2)

a hypothesis was put forward: there is
\alpha_{0}<2/3
, such that the system of eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{i,\alpha}=-d^{2}/dx^{2}+ix^{\alpha}
is complete in

L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for all
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0},2/3]
.

The completeness problem for
\mathscr{L}_{i,\alpha}
has not yet been solved even for
\alpha=2/3
and in 2015 it was noted by
Y. Almog
[
5
]
as one of the actual open problems. Of course our theorem solves it for
\alpha=2/3
.

§1 Some spectral properties of the operator
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}

Let

,
|\arg c|<\pi
,
\alpha>0
. The operator
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with Dirichlet boundary conditions is determined by the differential expression:

l_{c,\alpha}(y)=-\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}+cx^{\alpha}y,\quad x\in[0,+\infty)

and the domain

\mathfrak{D}(\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha})=\bigl\{y\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\,\bigl|\bigr.\,y\in W_{2,\loc}^{2},\ l_{c,\alpha}(y)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\ y(0)=0\bigr\}.

Proposition 1
.

For
|\arg c|<\pi
, the operator
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
is closed with a compact inverse.
Its eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
are simple (root subspaces are one-dimensional), and have the form

\lambda_{n}=c^{2/(\alpha+2)}t_{n}
, where
t_{n}>0
does not depend on
c
, and
with
n\to\infty
:

t_{n}\sim\left[(n-1/4)\frac{\sqrt{\pi}(\alpha+2)\Gamma(1/\alpha+1/2)}{\Gamma(1/\alpha)}\right]^{2\alpha/(\alpha+2)}.

For arbitrary
f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
the inverse operator is determined as follows:

(\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}^{-1}f)(x)=\frac{1}{\mathscr{W}}v(x)\int\limits_{0}^{x}u(\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi+\frac{1}{\mathscr{W}}u(x)\int\limits_{x}^{+\infty}v(\xi)f(\xi)\,d\xi,

(3)

where
u
and
v
are nontrivial solutions of the homogeneous equation
l_{c,\alpha}(y)=0

with properties:
u(0)=0
,
v(+\infty)=0
. Here
\mathscr{W}=\mathscr{W}(v,u)\neq 0
is the Wronskian.

For
s
–numbers of the inverse operator
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}^{-1}
the equality is true:

s_{n}(\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}^{-1})=1/|\lambda_{n}|=|c|^{-2/(\alpha+2)}/t_{n}=O(n^{-2\alpha/(\alpha+2)}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.
Let
z=c^{1/(\alpha+2)}
,
|\arg z|<\pi/(\alpha+2)
. Consider the operator
\mathscr{A}_{z,\alpha}=z^{-2}\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
,
that is in the form of Davies:

\mathscr{A}_{z,\alpha}=-z^{-2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+z^{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

the work
[
6
]
implies the existence of a compact inverse
\mathscr{A}_{z,\alpha}^{-1}
for all
z
as well as simplicity, reality, positivity and independence
from
z
of its eigenvalues
t_{n}=z^{-2}\lambda_{n}
.
The same properties have

s
–numbers of
\mathscr{A}_{z,\alpha}^{-1}
:
s_{n}(\mathscr{A}_{z,\alpha}^{-1})=1/t_{n}
, so
s_{n}(\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}^{-1})=(t_{n}|z|^{2})^{-1}=1/|\lambda_{n}|
.

To calculate the asymptotic behavior of
t_{n}
we set
z=1
and study in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
:

-y^{\prime\prime}(x)+x^{\alpha}y(x)=t\,y(x),\quad y(0)=0.

Substituting the independent variable and the parameter:
\zeta=xt^{-1/\alpha}
,
m=t^{1/\alpha+1/2}
we get

y^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)=m^{2}(\zeta^{\alpha}-1)y(\zeta),\quad y(0)=0,

the asymptotics of the nonnegative eigenvalues
m_{n}
is then calculated as in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
:

\displaystyle m_{n}\sim\frac{\pi(n-1/4)}{\int\limits_{0}^{1}\sqrt{1-\zeta^{\alpha}}\,d\zeta},\quad n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{1}\sqrt{1-\zeta^{\alpha}}\,d\zeta=\frac{\Gamma(1/\alpha)\sqrt{\pi}}{(\alpha+2)\Gamma(1/\alpha+1/2)}.

The solution
v
of the homogeneous equation
l_{c,\alpha}(y)=0
have the following WKB approximation as

:

v(x)\sim\frac{C_{2}}{x^{\alpha/4}}\exp\bigl(-\frac{2}{\alpha+2}c^{1/2}x^{\alpha/2+1}\bigr),\quad v^{\prime}(x)\sim-C_{2}c^{1/2}x^{\alpha/4}\exp\bigl(-\frac{2}{\alpha+2}c^{1/2}x^{\alpha/2+1}\bigr).

It is significant to show that
u
and
v
form a fundamental system of solutions (FSS) of the corresponding
homogeneous equation, and
\mathscr{W}(v,u)\neq 0
. Otherwise these solutions
are linearly dependent and

v(0)=0
, thus

\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}|v^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\,dx+c\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}x^{\alpha}|v(x)|^{2}\,dx=0,

which is not possible as
|\arg c|<\pi
.

One can easily verify that the expression (
3
)
defines the inverse operator for
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
.

The existence of a bounded inverse implies the closure of
\mathscr{L}_{c,\alpha}
.
\Box

. As the order of growth of the norms
\|r\|_{L_{2}(0,x)}

and
\|r^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{2}(0,x)}
by
\lambda
is not higher than 2, then

\frac{d}{dx}(R(\lambda)f)(x)\Bigl|_{x=0}\Bigr.=\frac{1}{C_{0}\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}\int\limits_{0}^{+\infty}\mathcal{Y}_{0}(\xi,\lambda)f(\xi)\,d\xi=\frac{1}{C_{0}}\frac{\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)}{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}

is an entire function of the order
\leq 2
.

Part I
. For the above
f
we use the Levinson method and show that there is
\theta_{0}\in(0,\pi/6)
such that for
0<4\gamma=\arg c<\pi/2+\theta_{0}

holds
\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)\equiv 0
.

For
\arg c=4\gamma<\pi/2
we apply Phragmen–Lindelöf principle (PL) to the function
\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
of the order
\leq 2

in the sector
\arg\lambda\in(0,4\gamma)
. Taking into account the lemma
2
we obtain

\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\equiv 0
.

Let
4\gamma\geq\pi/2
, moreover,
\pi/8\leq\gamma<\pi/6

\psi_{0}=\psi_{0}(\gamma)=\pi/2-3\gamma\in(0,\gamma]
. Here it will be convenient for us to

\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
as a function of
\mu
(
18

We turn to the estimate (
31
) which holds for
\arg\mu=\psi\in(0,\gamma)
.
We show that if for a given
\psi_{0}\in(0,\gamma]
the value
\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0}))]<0
then PL can be applied to
\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)
, hence

\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\equiv 0
. Suppose
\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0}))]<0
as
\psi=\psi_{0}
.

Let
\pi/8<\gamma<\pi/6
, it implies
\psi_{0}\in(0,\gamma)
. Due to the continuous dependence of

on
\psi
,
the condition
\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0})]<0
is valid in some interval
\psi\in(\psi_{0}-\varepsilon,\psi_{0}+\varepsilon)
. The central angles of each sector

\arg\mu\in(-3\gamma,\psi_{0}-\varepsilon)
and
\arg\mu\in(\psi_{0},\gamma)

are strictly less than
\pi/2
. Due to the lemma
2
the function

\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)/\mathcal{F}(\lambda)

\arg\mu\in(\gamma,2\pi-3\gamma)
, due to (
31

\arg\mu\in(\psi_{0}-\varepsilon,\psi_{0}+\varepsilon)

\pi/8<\gamma<\pi/6
.

Let
\gamma=\pi/8

0<\varepsilon<\pi/8
so that
\psi_{1}=\psi_{0}-\varepsilon=\pi/8-\varepsilon\in(0,\gamma)

and
\arg\mu\in(\psi_{1},\gamma)

Next we estimate
\gamma
to satisfy
\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0})]<0

Denote
\theta=\gamma-\psi_{0}=4\gamma-\pi/2=\pi/6-4\psi_{0}/3
,
0\leq\theta<\pi/6

\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0})]

as a function of
\theta

\psi_{0}=\pi/8-3\theta/4

From (

|Z_{0}|=\tau_{0}=\csc 4\gamma
.
For geometrical reasons
\re Z_{0}=1
,
\im Z_{0}=\tan\theta
.

Since
Z_{0}\in(Z^{*},Z^{**})
, then
\re S_{5}(Z_{0})<0
. For
z=1+iy
,
y>0
and the principle branch of the square root,
\arg\sqrt{z(1-z)}\in(-\pi/4,0)
.
For the principle branch of the square root:

\arg i\int\limits_{1}^{1+iy}\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)}\,d\zeta=\arg\Bigl(-\int\limits_{0}^{y}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt\Bigr)\in(3\pi/4,\pi).

Since

\re S_{1}(1)<0
and
\re S_{5}(Z_{0})<0

\displaystyle S_{1}(1)=e^{2i\psi_{0}}i\pi/8,

\displaystyle S_{5}(Z_{0})=e^{2i\psi_{0}}i\int\limits_{1}^{1+i\tan\theta}\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)}\,d\zeta=-e^{2i\psi_{0}}\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt,

\displaystyle\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0})]=\re\Bigl[e^{2i\psi_{0}}\bigl(i\frac{\pi}{8}+\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt\bigr)\Bigr]=

(35)

\displaystyle=-\sin 2\psi_{0}\,\bigl(\frac{\pi}{8}+\im\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt\bigr)+\cos 2\psi_{0}\,\re\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt.

Noting that
\psi_{0}=\pi/8-3\theta/4

Thus there is

Part II
. We show that the condition
\mathcal{G}(0,\lambda)\equiv 0
implies
f\equiv 0
— a contradiction with our original assumption.

Consider
\varphi_{1}(x,\lambda)
,
\varphi_{2}(x,\lambda)
— two solutions of the homogeneous equation (

\varphi_{1}(0,\lambda)=\varphi_{2}^{\prime}(0,\lambda)=0
,
\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(0,\lambda)=\varphi_{2}(0,\lambda)=1

is an entire function of

\lambda
of the order of growth
1/2
. Each function behaves like
O(e^{t|\im\lambda^{1/2}|})
uniformly in

as
|\lambda|\to\infty

[

,
15
]

For an arbitrary FSS
u
,
v
of the equation (
16
) and arbitrary
g\in L_{2}(0,x)
we denote

\displaystyle(K(\lambda)g)(t)=\frac{1}{\mathscr{W}(v,u)}v

\displaystyle(t,\lambda)\int\limits_{0}^{t}u(\xi,\lambda)g(\xi)\,d\xi-

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathscr{W}(v,u)}u(t,\lambda)\int\limits_{0}^{t}v(\xi,\lambda)g(\xi)\,d\xi.

One can check that
y(t)=(K(\lambda)g)(t)\in W_{2}^{2}[0,x]
is a solution to the Cauchy problem

-y^{\prime\prime}(t)+(ct^{2/3}-\lambda)y(t)=g(t)

Let

Now let

,

,

For

Part III
. We will estimate
\theta_{0}
.

We show first that
\theta_{0}<\pi/9
. Due to the equality (

\theta_{0}
is the only solution of the equation:

\tan 2\psi\,\bigl(\frac{\pi}{8}+\im\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt\bigr)=\re\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\theta}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt,

(36)

with
\psi=\pi/2-3\gamma\in(0,\pi/8)

Separating the real and the imaginary parts of the root, we get for

\re\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sqrt{\frac{t\sqrt{t^{2}+1}+t^{2}}{2}}\,dt,\ \im\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt=-\int\limits_{0}^{x}\sqrt{\frac{t\sqrt{t^{2}+1}-t^{2}}{2}}\,dt,

\sqrt{\frac{t\sqrt{t^{2}+1}+t^{2}}{2}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{t}{2}},

\re\int\limits_{0}^{\tan\pi/9}\sqrt{t^{2}-it}\,dt>\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\tan^{3/2}\frac{\pi}{9}>\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}(\pi/9)^{3/2}.

For

\theta

where the elementary equality

Now we show that

\re[S_{1}(1)-S_{5}(Z_{0})]=\re\Bigl(e^{2i\psi}i\bigl(\frac{\pi}{8}-\int\limits_{1}^{1+i\tan\theta}\sqrt{\zeta(1-\zeta)}\,d\zeta\bigr)\Bigr),

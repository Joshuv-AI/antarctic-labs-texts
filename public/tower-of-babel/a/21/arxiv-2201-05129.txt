Rewriting with Acyclic Queries: Mind Your Head
Gaetano Geck ORCID:0000-0002-8946-9440, Jens Keppeler, Thomas Schwentick ORCID:0000-0002-1062-922X, Christopher Spinrath

Abstract
. The paper studies the rewriting problem, that is, the decision problem whether, for a given conjunctive query Q Q and a set 𝒱 \mathcal{V} of views, there is a conjunctive query Q ′ Q^{\prime} over 𝒱 \mathcal{V} that is equivalent to Q Q , for cases where the query, the views, and/or the desired rewriting are acyclic or even more restricted. It shows that, if Q Q itself is acyclic, an acyclic rewriting exists if there is any rewriting. An analogous statement also holds for free-connex acyclic, hierarchical, and q-hierarchical queries. Regarding the complexity of the rewriting problem, the paper identifies a border between tractable and (presumably) intractable variants of the rewriting problem: for schemas of bounded arity, the acyclic rewriting problem is NP -hard, even if both Q Q and the views in 𝒱 \mathcal{V} are acyclic or hierarchical. However, it becomes tractable if the views are free-connex acyclic (i.e., in a nutshell, their body is (i) acyclic and (ii) remains acyclic if their head is added as an additional atom).

Under which circumstances is it guaranteed that a structurally simple rewriting exists, if there exists a rewriting at all?

(2)

What is the complexity to decide whether such a rewriting exists and to compute one?

Structurally simple queries.

We note that the requirement in

5.2.

The Existence of Hierarchical and q-hierarchical Rewritings

In
Section 5
, we have shown that every
acyclic
query has an
acyclic
rewriting if it has a rewriting at all.
This gives us a guarantee that there is a rewriting that has the same complexity benefits for query evaluation as the original query.

It is natural to ask whether other, stronger properties transfer in the same fashion.
In this section, we consider this question for hierarchical and q-hierarchical queries.

The following example illustrates that, as for acyclic rewritings, even if a hierarchical rewriting for a hierarchical query exists, the canonical rewriting is not necessarily hierarchical.

However, a hierarchical rewriting exists, for instance

Related Work

We already mentioned that our notion of cover partitions is similar to various notions from the literature. We mention three of them here.

In
[
AC19
]
algorithms for finding exact rewritings with a minimal number of atoms and (maximally) contained rewritings are presented.
For this purpose triples

Namely,

corresponds to the set

In our characterisation we can assume

(and, thus, omit it in the specification of a cover description) thanks to the view application
{\alpha}
.
We note that

can be assumed to be one-to-one for (equivalent)

-rewritings
[
AC19
, Theorem 3.15]
.
Furthermore, for (equivalent) rewritings, only candidates whose body is a proper subset of the canonical rewriting’s body are considered (cf.
[
AC19
, Section 3.2.3]
).
Note that the existence of body homomorphisms
\psi_{i}
mapping the

into

(in particular, the unification of variables in the head of a view).
This is, however, too restrictive for our purposes (cf., for instance,
subsection 5.1
) which is why

and

are part of a cover description.
We emphasise that Condition
(2)
is equivalent to the

[
AC19
, Definition 3.12]
.
An analogue of the implication
(a)

\Rightarrow

[

(called

) and they are allowed to overlap, i.e. they do not have to form a partition.
In contrast, we consider non-maximal sets, for instance in the proof of

For (maximally) contained rewritings, an analogue of

\Rightarrow

Interestingly, the proof of this result (and the associated algorithm) exploits triples with minimal sets

We discuss the relation of this minimality constraint with our results below in more detail.
An analogue of

y\in{{\textnormal{vars}}(V_{i})}
and the input views are hierarchical.
The same is true for the set
\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
(viewed as a Boolean query here).
Moreover,
W
and
Q^{\prime}
are also hierarchical since the views and the query, and, hence, the
V_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
do not share any variable.

Correctness.

In the following, we show that
{Q}
has a
{\mathcal{V}}
-rewriting if and only if
C_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{{Q}}
for
Q^{\prime}
is not minimal.

For the only if-direction assume that
Q
has a
{\mathcal{V}}
-rewriting. Then there is a consistent cover partition
\mathcal{C}
for
{Q}
. Note that by construction each cover description
({\mathcal{A}},V_{j},{\alpha},\psi)
in
\mathcal{C}
can be turned into a cover description
({\mathcal{A}}^{u},V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},{\alpha}\cup\{v_{j}\mapsto u\},\psi\cup\{u\mapsto u\})
for
Q^{\prime}
and, furthermore into a cover description
({\mathcal{A}}^{u},W,{\alpha}^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
for
Q^{\prime}
where
{\alpha}^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
coincide with
{\alpha}\cup\{v_{j}\mapsto u\}
and
\psi\cup\{u\mapsto u\}
on their domain, respectively, and are the identity on all other variables.
Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be the collection of cover descriptions we obtain by transforming every cover description in
\mathcal{C}
to a cover description as described above and the additional cover description
(\{S(u)\},W,\{v_{n+1}\mapsto u\},{\textnormal{id}})
for
Q^{\prime}
.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is a cover partition for
{Q}
and
{Q}
does not contain an
S
-atom, the atom sets of the cover descriptions in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
form a partition of
\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}={{\textnormal{body}}({Q})}^{u}\cup\{S(u)\}
.
But then
(\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\textnormal{id}},{\textnormal{id}})
is not minimal for
Q^{\prime}
because
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
consists of at least two cover descriptions.

For the if-direction assume that
C_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{{Q}}
is not minimal for
Q^{\prime}
. Let

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}=({\mathcal{A}}_{1}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{1},\psi_{1}),\ldots,({\mathcal{A}}_{k}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{k},\psi_{k})

be a collection of cover descriptions witnessing that
C_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{{Q}}=(\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\textnormal{id}},{\textnormal{id}})
is not minimal for
Q^{\prime}
.
From
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
we will derive a cover partition
{\mathcal{C}}
for
{Q}
witnessing that
{Q}
is indeed
{\mathcal{V}}
-rewritable.
For this purpose, we first analyse to which atoms in
{\alpha}_{i}(W)
the atoms of a set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
are mapped to and then associate views
V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
with (subsets of) the sets
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
.

We can assume that the
{\alpha}_{i}
fulfil the quantified variable disjointness.
Note, that
k\geq 2
since
C_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{{Q}}
is not minimal, and
u
is a bridge variable of each set
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
since it occurs in every atom in
\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
. Therefore, no atom in
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
can be mapped into the copy of
{\alpha}_{i}(\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}})
in
\alpha_{i}(W)
because the variable
u
is not a head variable of
W
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
is a subset of
{\alpha}_{i}({{\textnormal{body}}(V_{1})}^{v_{1}})\cup\ldots\cup{\alpha}_{i}({{\textnormal{body}}(V_{n})}^{v_{n}})\cup\{{\alpha}_{i}(S(v_{n+1}))\}
.

Since the sets
{\alpha}_{i}({{\textnormal{body}}(V_{j})}^{v_{j}})
do not share any variable that is not in the
{{\textnormal{head}}({\alpha}_{i}(W))}
, each cover description in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
can be partitioned into cover descriptions

({\mathcal{B}}_{i,1}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{i},\psi_{i}),\ldots,({\mathcal{B}}_{i,n}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{i},\psi_{i})

for
Q^{\prime}
where
{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}={\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}\cap{\alpha}_{i}({{\textnormal{body}}(V_{j})}^{v_{j}})
and, in case
S(u)\in{\mathcal{A}}_{i}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
, a cover description
(\{S(u)\},W,{\alpha}_{i},\psi_{i})
.
For the sake of readability we assume that
{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}\neq\emptyset
holds for all
i,j
.
If not, the respective cover descriptions can just be removed from the sequence.

Note that the view
W
in a cover description
({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{i},\psi_{i})
can be replaced by
V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}
, because
{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}\subseteq{\alpha}_{i}({{\textnormal{body}}(V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}})})
by definition.
Hence, each cover description
({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},W,{\alpha}_{i},\psi_{i})
can be transformed into a cover description
({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},{\alpha}_{i,j},\psi_{i,j})
where
\alpha_{i,j}
and
\psi_{i,j}
are the restriction of
{\alpha}_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
resp. on
{{\textnormal{vars}}(V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}})}
.
A cover description
({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},V_{j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}},{\alpha}_{i,j},\psi_{i,j})
can, in turn, be transformed into a cover description
({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j},V_{j},\widehat{{\alpha}}_{i,j},\widehat{\psi}_{i,j})
for
{Q}
where
{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}
is the atom set with
{\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{v_{j}}={\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$+$}}

{\mathcal{C}}
be the collection of cover descriptions we obtain by

and

{{\textnormal{body}}({Q})}
.
Thus

Applications of structurally simple queries.

It is well known that many problems are tractable for acyclic conjunctive queries but (presumably) not for conjunctive queries in general.
Notably, the evaluation, minimisation, and the containment problem are tractable for acyclic queries
[
Yan81
,
CR00
,
GLS01
]
but
NP
-complete for the class of conjunctive queries
[
CM77
]
.

The class of free-connex conjunctive queries plays a central role in the enumeration complexity of conjunctive queries.
In
[
BDG07
]
, Bagan, Durand and Grandjean showed that the result of a free-connex acyclic conjunctive query can be enumerated with constant delay after a linear time preprocessing phase.
Moreover, they also showed that the result of an acyclic conjunctive query without self-joins that is not free-connex cannot be enumerated with constant delay after a linear time preprocessing, unless
n\times n
matrices can be multiplied in time
O(n^{2})
.

Hierarchical queries play a central role in different contexts.
On the one hand, Dalvi and Suciu
[
DS07
]
showed that the class of hierarchical Boolean conjunctive queries without self-joins characterises precisely
the Boolean CQs without self-joins that can be
answered in polynomial time on probabilistic
databases. This has been extended by Fink and Olteanu
[
FO14
]
to the notion of non-Boolean queries and queries with negation.
On the other hand, Koutris and Suciu
[
KS11
]

studied hierarchical join queries in the context of query evaluation on massively parallel architectures.
We refer to
[
FO16
]
for further applications of hierarchical queries.

The notion of q-hierarchical queries has played a central role in the evaluation of conjunctive queries under single tuple
updates
[
BKS17
]
.
In
[
BKS17
]
it is shown that the result of a q-hierarchical conjunctive query
can be evaluated (by answering a Boolean CQ in constant time,
enumerating the result of a non-Boolean CQ with constant delay,
or outputting the number of results in constant time) with constant update time
after a linear time preprocessing. Moreover, they showed that the result of a conjunctive query without self-joins that is not q-hierarchical cannot be evaluated with constant update time after a linear time preprocessing, unless some algorithmic conjectures on Online Matrix-Vector multiplication (see
[
HKNS15
]
for more information about the conjecture) do not hold.
The notion of q-hierarchical queries is also related to factorised databases
[
Kep20
]
.
The notion of factorised databases has already been considered in
various contexts
[
OZ15
,
BKOZ13
,
SOC16
]
.
A further recent source of information on structurally simple queries is
[
KNOZ20
]
.

9.
Conclusion

We studied rewritability by acyclic queries or queries from
{\mathsf{CCQ}}
,
{\mathsf{HCQ}}
, or
{\mathsf{QHCQ}}
. Based on a new characterisation of (exact) rewritability, we showed that acyclic queries have acyclic rewritings, if they have any CQ rewriting.
The same holds for the other three query classes.

We showed that for acyclic queries and views the decision problem, whether an acyclic rewriting exists, is intractable, even for schemas with bounded arity, but becomes tractable if views have a bounded arity (even with unbounded schema arity) or are free-connex acyclic.

We leave the case of free-connex acyclic views and unbounded schemas open.
Another interesting open question is the complexity of rewriting problems
{{\textsc{Rewr}}({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}})}
with
{\mathbb{R}}\subsetneq{\mathbb{Q}}
, e.g.
{{\textsc{Rewr}}({\mathsf{ACQ}},{\mathsf{ACQ}},{\mathsf{QHCQ}})}
.
So far we have only
NP
-hardness results for problems
{{\textsc{Rewr}}({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathsf{ACQ}})}
and
{{\textsc{Rewr}}({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathsf{HCQ}})}
where
{\mathbb{V}}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}
encompass all acyclic or all hierarchical queries, respectively.

Finally, it would be interesting to study whether our results can be extended to other classes of queries that can be evaluated efficiently like conjunctive queries of bounded treewidth.

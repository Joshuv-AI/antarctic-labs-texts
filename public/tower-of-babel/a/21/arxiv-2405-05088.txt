Local Tameness and Ends of Groups
Rita Gitik

Abstract
. We investigate connections between local tameness of a group and a number of its ends.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20F65; Secondary: 20E06, 57M07, 57M10

Keywords: locally tame group, ends of groups, graph of groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
H
be a subgroup of a group
G
given by a presentation
G=\langle X|R\rangle
.
Let
K
be the standard presentation
2
-complex of
G
, i.e.
K
has one vertex,
K
has an edge, which is a loop, for every generator
x\in X
, and
K
has a
2
-cell for every relator
r\in R
. The Cayley complex of
G
, denoted by
Cayley_{2}(G)
, is the universal cover of
K
. Denote by
Cayley_{2}(G,H)
the cover of
K
corresponding to
a subgroup
H
of
G
.

Definition 1
.

cf.
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
.
A finitely generated subgroup
H
of a finitely presented group
G
is tame in
G
if for any finite subcomplex
C
of
Cayley_{2}(G,H)
and for any component
C_{0}
of
Cayley_{2}(G,H)-C
the group
\pi_{1}(C_{0})
is finitely generated.

Note that Definition 1 makes sense for any subgroup
H
and for any presentation of a group
G
, however it was shown in
[
10
]
that tameness of a finitely generated subgroup
H
is independent of a finite presentation of a group
G
. As we are interested in investigating a property of a group, rather than a property of a presentation, we will work with finitely presented groups, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

It is not known if there exists a finitely generated subgroup
H
of a finitely presented group
G
such that
H
is not tame in
G
. Moreover, it is not known if the trivial subgroup is tame in any finite presentation of a group. However, an infinitely generated subgroup might not be tame in a finitely presented group.

Example 1
.

Let
F=\langle x,y\rangle
be a free group of rank two, and let
F^{\prime}
be its commutator subgroup. The complex
Cayley_{2}(F,F^{\prime})
is one-dimensional and is homeomorphic to a lattice in the Euclidean plane, consisting of the horizontal lines
\{y=n,n\in\mathbf{Z}\}
and the vertical lines
\{x=n,n\in\mathbf{Z}\}
. As the fundamental group of the complement of any finite connected subcomplex
C
in
Cayley(F,F^{\prime})
is infinitely generated,
F^{\prime}
is not tame in
F
.

The following definition was given in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2
.

A group
G
is locally tame if all finitely generated subgroups of
G
are tame in
G
.

A finitely generated subgroup can be tame in an infinite presentation of a group. Moreover, an infinite presentation of a group can be locally tame.

Example 2
.

Let
F=\langle X\rangle
be an infinitely generated free group and let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
F
. Note that
Cayley_{2}(F,H)
is one-dimensional. It has a finite subcomplex, called the core, which carries
H
, and the complement of the core in
Cayley_{2}(F,H)
is a forest. Hence for any finite subcomplex of
Cayley_{2}(F,H)
the fundamental group of any component of
Cayley_{2}(F,H)-C
is finitely generated. Therefore any free group is locally tame.

In this paper we discuss connections between local tameness and a number of ends of a group.

It was shown in
[
4
]
and
[
9
]
that any finitely generated group has either
0,1,2
, or infinitely many ends.

Remark 1
.

A finitely presented group has
0
ends if and only if it is finite, so it is locally tame because its Cayley complex is finite.

Remark 2
.

A finitely generated group has two ends if and only if it is virtually infinite cyclic, i.e. it contains an infinite cyclic subgroup of finite index.

It was shown in
[
7
]
that virtually locally tame groups are locally tame and an infinite cyclic group is locally tame. Hence any finitely presented group with
2
ends is locally tame.

The main results of this paper are the following theorems. The proofs are given in Section 3.

Theorem 1
.

A finitely presented group with infinitely many ends is locally tame if all finitely presented groups with one end are locally tame.

Theorem 2
.

A finitely presented group
G
is locally tame if there exists a finitely presented group
B
such that the free product
G\ast B
is locally tame.

Remark 3
.

It was shown in
[
2
]
that a free product of two finitely generated groups has infinitely many ends unless both groups have order
2
, in which case it has
2
ends.

2.
Graphs of Groups

The following result was proven in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a possibly infinite graph of groups with all edge groups finitely generated.

If the presentation of
G
given by that graph of groups structure is finitely generated then all the vertex groups of
G
are finitely generated.

If the presentation of
G
given by that graph of groups structure is finitely presented then all the vertex groups of
G
are finitely presented.

The next proposition is the converse of Proposition 1. It follows from the definition of the fundamental group of a graph of groups.

Proposition 2
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with all edge groups finitely generated.

If all the vertex groups of
G
are finitely generated then the presentation of
G
given by that graph of groups structure is finitely generated.

If all the vertex groups of
G
are finitely presented then the presentation of
G
given by that graph of groups structure is finite.

Remark 4
.

Note that Proposition 1 uses a strong assumption on the existence of a presentation of
G
with good properties. Without this assumption the result is false. Indeed, a finitely presented amalgamated free product over a finitely generated subgroup of two groups which do not have finite presentation was constructed in
[
1
]
.

3.
Ends of Groups

It was shown in
[
12
]
that a finitely generated group with infinitely many ends is either an amalgamated free product of finitely generated groups with finite amalgamating subgroup or an HNN-extension of a finitely generated group over a finite subgroup.

Dunwoody proved in
[
3
]
that a finitely presented group with infinitely many ends is a fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with all edge groups finite and all vertex groups finitely generated with at most one end. He also proved (private communication) that a finitely presented group with infinitely many ends is a fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with all edge groups finite and all vertex groups finitely presented with at most one end. The proof of the stronger result, apparently, did not appear in print.

Recall that a group is Noetherian if all its subgroups are finitely generated.

The following result was proved by the author in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with Noetherian edge groups such that the presentation of
G
given by the graph of groups structure is finite. If all the vertex groups of
G
are locally tame then
G
is locally tame.

Proof of Theorem 1

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with finite edge groups and finitely presented locally tame vertex groups.

Proposition 2 implies that the presentation of
G
given by that graph of groups structure is finite. As finite groups are Noetherian, Proposition 3 implies that
G
is locally tame.

The accessibility theorem of Dunwoody, quoted above, states that a finitely presented group with infinitely many ends is a fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with all edge groups finite and all vertex groups finitely presented with at most one end.

Remark 1 states that all finitely presented groups with
0
ends are locally tame. Therefore, if all finitely presented groups with one end are locally tame then all finitely presented groups with infinitely many ends are locally tame.
\square

We use the following notation. Let
G
be a group generated by a set
X
and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
.
Let
\{Hg\}
be the set of right cosets of
H
in
G
.

The coset graph of
G
with respect to
H
, denoted
Cayley(G,H)
, is the oriented graph whose vertices are the cosets
\{Hg\}
, the set of edges is
\{Hg\}\times(X\cup X^{-1})
, and for
x\in X\cup X^{-1}
the edge
(Hg,x)
begins at the vertex
Hg
and ends at the vertex
Hgx
.

Denote the Cayley graph of
G
by
Cayley(G)
. Note that
Cayley(G,H)
is the quotient of
Cayley(G)
by the left multiplication by
H
. Also note that the
1
-skeleton of
Cayley_{2}(G)
is
Cayley(G)
, and the
1
-skeleton of
Cayley_{2}(G,H)
is
Cayley(G,H)
.

Let
G
be generated by a disjoint union of sets
X_{i},1\leq i\leq n
.
We call a connected subcomplex of
Cayley_{2}(G,H)
an
X_{i}
-component, if all the edges in the
1
-skeleton of that subcomplex have the form
(Hg,x)
with
x\in X_{i}\cup X_{i}^{-1}
.

Proof of Theorem 2

Let
G
be a finitely presented group and let
B
be a finitely presented group such that
G\ast B
is locally tame. Let
G
be generated by a finite set
X
and
B
generated by a finite set
Y
. We assume that
X
and
Y
are disjoint. Let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
G
and
C
be a finite subcomplex of
Cayley(G,H)
.

Consider
Cayley(G\ast B,H)
. The normal form theorem for free products of groups implies that all
Y
-components are homeomorphic to
Cayley(B)
, hence are simply connected. The normal form theorem for free products of groups also implies that all
X
-components of
Cayley(G\ast B,H)
, except one, are homeomorphic to
Cayley(G)
, hence are simply connected. The remaining
X
-component of
Cayley(G\ast B,H)
is homeomorphic to
Cayley(G,H)
, hence
Cayley(G,H)-C
is homeomorphic to an
X
-component of
Cayley(G\ast B,H)-C
.

Proposition 2 implies that the free product
G\ast B
is finitely presented. By assumptions, the free product
G\ast B
is locally tame, hence
Cayley(G\ast B,H)-C
has a finitely generated fundamental group. As all components of
Cayley(G\ast B,H)-C
, except one, are simply connected, it follows that the fundamental group of
Cayley(G\ast B,H)-C
is isomorphic to the fundamental group of
Cayley(G,H)-C
. Hence the fundamental group of

Cayley(G,H)-C
is finitely generated, proving that
G
is locally tame.
\square

4.
Open Questions

Remark 5
.

The group
F_{2}\times F_{2}
is a source of counterexamples. Is it locally tame? Lemma
7
in
[
7
]
implies that the trivial subgroup is tame in
F_{2}\times F_{2}
. It is proven in
[
2
]
that the direct product of two finitely generated groups has one end, so
F_{2}\times F_{2}
has one end.

Recall that a group has f.g.i.p. (finitely generated intersection property) if the intersection of any two of its finitely generated subgroups is finitely generated.

Remark 6
.

Is the group
F_{2}\times F_{1}
locally tame? It is shown in
[
11
]
that
F_{2}\times F_{1}
does not have f.g.i.p.. Indeed, let
F_{2}
be generated by the elements
x
and
y
and let
F_{1}
be generated by the element
t
. Let
K
be a subgroup of
F_{2}\times F_{1}
generated by the elements
x
and
ty
. Then
F_{2}\cap K
is the normal closure of
x
in
F_{2}
, hence it is not finitely generated.

Remark 7
.

Let
A
and
B
be finitely presented locally tame groups. Is it true that a finitely generated subgroup of
A\times B
is tame in
A\times B
if its intersections with
A
and
B
are finitely generated.

Remark 8
.

A positive answer to the question stated in the previous remark will imply that
the direct product of two finitely presented locally tame groups is locally tame if any finitely generated subgroup intersects each factor in a finitely generated subgroup.

Remark 9
.

Let a finitely presented group
G
be a direct product
G=N\times L
, where
L
is finite and
N
is finitely presented. Remark 10 implies that
G
is locally tame if and only if
N
is locally tame.

Remark 10
.

The following result was proved in
[
7
]
. Let
K_{0}
be a finite index subgroup of a finitely presented group
K
. A finitely generated subgroup
H
of
K
is tame in
K
if and only if
H\cap K_{0}
is tame in
K_{0}
. It follows that virtually locally tame groups are locally tame and finite index subgroups of locally tame groups are locally tame.

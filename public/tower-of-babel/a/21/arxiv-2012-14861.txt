Linearized trinomials with maximum kernel
Paolo Santonastaso, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
Linearized polynomials have attracted a lot of attention because of their applications in both geometric and algebraic areas. Let q q be a prime power, n n be a positive integer and σ \sigma be a generator of Gal ⁡ ( 𝔽 q n : 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q}) . In this paper we provide closed formulas for the coefficients of a σ \sigma -trinomial f f over 𝔽 q n {\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}} which ensure that the dimension of the kernel of f f equals its σ \sigma -degree, that is linearized polynomials with maximum kernel . As a consequence, we present explicit examples of linearized trinomials with maximum kernel and characterize those having σ \sigma -degree 3 3 and 4 4 . Our techniques rely on the tools developed in [ 24 ] . Finally, we apply these results to investigate a class of rank metric codes introduced in [ 8 ] , to construct quasi-subfield polynomials and cyclic subspace codes, obtaining new explicit constructions to the conjecture posed in [ 37 ] .

7.1
Rank metric codes

Rank metric codes were introduced by Delsarte
[
10
]
in 1978 and they have been intensively investigated in recent years because of their applications; we refer to
[
35
]
for a recent survey on this topic.
The set of
m\times n
matrices
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times n}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
may be endowed with a metric, called
rank metric
, defined by

d(A,B)=\mathrm{rk}\,(A-B).

A subset
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times n}
equipped with the rank metric is called a
rank metric code
(shortly, a
RM
-code).
The minimum distance of
\C
is defined as

d=\min\{d(A,B)\colon A,B\in\C,\,\,A\neq B\}.

Denote the parameters of a RM-code
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m,n}
with minimum distance
d
by
(m,n,q;d)
.
We are interested in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-
linear
RM-codes, i.e.
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{m\times n}
.
Delsarte showed in
[
10
]
that the parameters of these codes must obey a Singleton-like bound, i.e.

|\C|\leq q^{\max\{m,n\}(\min\{m,n\}-d+1)}.

When equality holds, we call
\C
a
maximum rank distance
(
MRD
for short) code.
Examples of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear MRD-codes were first found in
[
10
,
14
]
.
We say that two
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear RM-codes
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
are equivalent if there exist
X\in\mathrm{GL}(m,q)
,
Y\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
, and
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
such that

\C^{\prime}=\{XC^{\sigma}Y\colon C\in\C\}.

The
left
and
right
idealisers of
\C
are defined in
[
21
]
as
L(\C)=\{A\in\mathrm{GL}(m,q)\colon A\C\subseteq\C\}
and
R(\C)=\{B\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)\colon\C B\subseteq\C\}
. They are invariant under the equivalence of rank metric codes, and have been investigated in
[
22
]
; further invariants have been introduced in
[
15
,
27
]
.

When
m=n
, an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear rank metric code of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times n}
can be seen an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
(or
\mathcal{L}_{n,q,\sigma}
) where
d(f(x),g(x))=\mathrm{rk}(f(x)-g(x))=n-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(f(x)-g(x)))
for any
f(x),g(x)\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
In
[
8
]
, the authors study those rank metric codes of
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with both the idealisers isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
In particular, in
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
they proved that any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear rank metric code of
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with both the idealisers isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
is equivalent to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
-span of some linearized monomials. Then they classify these codes when
n\leq 9
,
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
, introducing a new class of MRD-codes

\C_{3,n}=\langle x,x^{q},x^{q^{3}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}},

when
n=7
and
q
is odd, and
n=8
and
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
For
n=7
the property of
\C_{3,n}
of being MRD or not may be obtained as a consequence of (c) of Theorem
2.1
, whereas the case

may be obtained by using Proposition

.
Moreover we can determine the number of minimum weight codewords of

where
\sigma
is a generator of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
, when
\C_{3,n,\sigma}
is not an MRD-code and
n\in\{7,8\}
.
Note that the minimum distance of
\C_{3,n,\sigma}
is greater than or equal to
n-3
and the number of minimum weight codewords of an MRD-code (that is those of weight
n-2
) depends only on its parameters, see
[
31
]
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power,
n
be a positive integer and let
\sigma
is a generator of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Denote by
D
the number of codewords of weight
n-3
in
\C_{3,n,\sigma}
.

•

If
n\in\{4,5\}
then
D=0
, and if
n=6
then
D=(q^{6}-1)(q^{3}+1)
.

•

If
n=7
, then
D
is either
0
when
q
is odd, or
\frac{(q^{7}-1)^{2}}{q-1}
when
q
is even. In particular
\C_{3,7,\sigma}
is an MRD-code if and only if
q
is odd.

•

If
n=8
, then
D
is either
0
when
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, or
\frac{(q^{8}-1)^{2}}{q-1}
when
q\equiv 0\pmod{3}
, or
2\frac{(q^{8}-1)^{2}}{q-1}
when
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. In particular
\C_{3,8,\sigma}
is an MRD-code if and only if
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

Proof.

The number of codewords in
\C_{3,n,\sigma}
having weight
n-3
coincides with the number of polynomials
\C_{3,n,\sigma}
with kernel of dimension
3
.
So, by Theorem
1.3
(see also Theorem
2.1
) the first point follows.
Let
n=7
. By Proposition
6.1
, the polynomials in
\C_{3,7,\sigma}
with kernel of dimension
3
exists only when
q
is even and are of the following form

\eta(x^{\sigma^{3}}-bx^{\sigma}-ax),

with
\mathrm{N}_{q^{7}/q}(a)=1
,
b=-a^{\sigma(1+\sigma^{3}+\sigma^{6})}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{7}}^{*}
.
Similarly, for the case
n=8
by Proposition
6.1
the polynomials having kernel of dimension
3
in
\C_{3,8,\sigma}
exists only if
q\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}
and have the following shape

\eta(x^{\sigma^{3}}-bx^{\sigma}-ax),

with
\mathrm{N}_{q^{8}/q}(a)=1
,
b=-\frac{\alpha}{a^{\sigma^{3}+\sigma^{6}}}
, where
\alpha
is a root of
x^{2}+x+1
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{8}}^{*}
.
The assertion then follows by noting that the equation
x^{2}+x+1=0
has one root if
q\equiv 0\pmod{3}
and it has two distinct roots if
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.
∎

Now consider
\sigma
a generator of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
,
d
a positive integer with
d\leq n
and let

\C_{d,n,\sigma}=\langle x,x^{\sigma},x^{\sigma^{d}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}.

The minimum distance of
\C_{d,n,\sigma}
is greater than or equal to
n-d
.
Denote by
D
the number of codewords of weight
n-d
in
\C_{d,n,\sigma}
.

Similarly to
\C_{3,n}
, as a corollary of Proposition
6.2
we determine the number of codewords in
\C_{4,n,\sigma}
with weight
n-4
.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
q
be a prime power,
n
be a positive integer,
\sigma
be a generator of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and let

\C_{4,n,\sigma}=\langle x,x^{\sigma},x^{\sigma^{4}}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}.

•

If
5\leq n\leq 11
and
n\neq 8
then
D=0
, and if
n\in\{8,12\}
then
D=\frac{(q^{n}-1)^{2}}{q^{4}-1}
.

•

If
n=13
then

D=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{if}\,\,q\not\equiv 0\pmod{3},\\
\frac{(q^{n}-1)^{2}}{q-1},&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

•

If
n=15
then

D=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{if}\,\,q\,\,\text{is odd},\\
\frac{(q^{n}-1)^{2}}{q-1},&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Using Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
and
5.1
and arguing as in Corollary
7.1
we obtain results on
D
for
\C_{d,n,\sigma}
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
q
be a prime power,
n
be a positive integer,
\sigma
be a generator of
\mathrm{Gal}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

•

If
n\leq d(d-1)
and
d\nmid n
, then
D=0
.

•

If

and

, then

.

•

If

then

If

,

.

7.2
Quasi-subfield polynomials

A
quasi-subfield polynomial
is defined as a polynomial of the form
x^{q^{d}}-\lambda(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x]
which divides
x^{q^{n}}-x
and for which
\log_{q}(\deg(\lambda(x)))<d^{2}/n
.
Those polynomials were recently introduced in
[
19
]
in order to solve the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP).

As for the family of subspace polynomials introduced in
[
24
]
, we show that the polynomials in Theorem
5.1
with
\sigma\colon x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mapsto x^{q}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
are quasi-subfield polynomials.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
L(x)=x^{q^{d}}-bx^{q}-ax\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
, with
n=(d-1)d+d-1
. If

•

q
is a power of 2,

•

d
is a power of 2,

•

\mathrm{N}_{q^{n}/q}(a)=1
,

•

b=a^{-q^{d}\frac{q^{d(d-1)}-1}{q^{d}-1}}
,

then
L(x)
is a quasi-subfield polynomial.

Proof.

Clearly,
\log_{q}(\deg(\lambda(x)))=1
and
d^{2}>n=d^{2}-1
, so that the condition
\log_{q}(\deg(\lambda(x)))<d^{2}/n
is satisfied. By Theorem
5.1

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\ker(L(x)))=d
, i.e.
L
splits completely over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
and so it divides
x^{q^{n}}-x
.
∎

In
[
19
]
, the authors developed an algorithm to solve ECDLP over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
using a quasi-subfield polynomial.
The complexity of the algorithm presented in
[
19
]
in which
n=d^{2}-1
and with the use of
L(x)=x^{q^{d}}-bx^{q}-ax\in\mathcal{L}_{d^{2}-1,q}
as in Proposition
7.4
, is less than the complexity of a brute force algorithm, but greater than the generic algorithms (Pollard Rho or Baby-Step-Giant-Step).

See also
[
12
,
13
]
.

7.3
Cyclic subspace codes

Let
k
be a non-negative integer with
k\leq n
, the set of all
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, viewed as
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space, forms a
Grassmanian space
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
. A
constant dimension subspace code
is a subset
C
of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
endowed with the metric defined as follows

d(U,V)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V),

where
U,V\in C
.
The interest in subspace codes has recently increased because of their application to error correction in random
network coding, see
[
20
]
.
One of the most studied family of subspace codes was introduced in
[
11
]
and is knows as
cyclic subspace codes
.
Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
, the
cyclic shift
of
V
by
\alpha
is defined as
\alpha V=\{\alpha v:v\in V\}
. Note that a cyclic shift of
V
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
of the same dimension of
V
. So, a subspace code
C\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
is said to be
cyclic
if for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
and every
V\in C
then
\alpha V\in C
.
Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
, the
orbit
of
V
is the set
\{\alpha V:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}
, and its cardinality is
(q^{n}-1)/(q^{t}-1)
, for some
t
which divides
n
, see e.g.
[
29
, Theorem 1]
. In particular, every orbit of a subspace
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
defines a cyclic subspace code of size
(q^{n}-1)/(q^{t}-1)
, for some
t\mid n
. Assume
k>1
.
It is easy to see that a cyclic subspace code generated by an orbit of a subspace
V
with size
(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)
(that is the maximum possible) has minimum distance at most
2k-2
. In
[
37
]
the following conjecture arises.

Conjecture 7.5
.

For every positive integers
n,k
such that
k\leq n/2
, there exists a cyclic code of size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and minimum distance
2k-2
.

Ben-Sasson et al.
[
4
]
used subspace polynomials to generate cyclic subspace codes with size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
. Precisely, they proved that Conjecture
7.5
holds true for any given
k
and infinitely many values of
n
. Such result was then improved in
[
29
]
.
However, the relation between
k
and
n
was not immediately clear, as
n
depends on the degree of the irreducible factors of
x^{q^{k}}+x^{q}+x
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Finally, Conjecture
7.5
was solved in
[
33
]
for most of the cases, by using
Sidon spaces
.

In this section we construct cyclic subspace codes via subspace polynomials with the same parameters of the codes of Conjecture
7.5
, in which the relation of
n
and
k
is given explicitly, covering some cases of Conjecture
7.5
not covered before.

In order to do this, we need the concept of
subspace polynomial
(see
[
4
, Definition 2]
), that is a monic
q
-polynomial with maximum kernel.

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
, the polynomial
P_{V}(x)=\prod_{v\in V}(x-v)
is the unique subspace polynomial whose set of roots is
V
. Clearly, if
P(x)
is a subspace polynomial having
q
-degree
k
then
P(x)=P_{V}(x)
, where
V
is the kernel of
P
.

Definition 7.6
.

[
4
, Definition 3]

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and
P_{V}(x)=x^{q^{k}}+\sum_{j=0}^{i}\alpha_{j}x^{q^{j}}\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
, such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. We define the gap of
V
as
gap(V)=k-i
.

The gap of a subspace polynomial plays an important role for determining the distance between two subspace. Indeed, the following holds.

Proposition 7.7
.

[
4
, Corollary 2, Lemma 5 and Corollary 3]

1.

If
U,V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
, then
d(U,V)\geq 2\min(gap(U),gap(V))
.

2.

If
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, then
P_{\alpha V}(x)=\alpha^{q^{k}}P_{V}(\alpha^{-1}x)
. In particular,
V
and
\alpha V
have the same gap.

3.

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
and
P_{V}(x)=x^{q^{k}}+\sum_{j=0}^{i}\alpha_{j}x^{q^{j}}
. If
\alpha_{h}\neq 0
for some
h\in\{1,\ldots,i\}
with
\gcd(h,n)=t
, then
V
has at least
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{t}-1}
distinct cyclic shifts.

Now we are able to exhibit two families of cyclic subspace codes.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
k
be a positive integer such that
k-1
is a power characteristic of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, with
n=k(k-1)+1
,
such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{n}/q}(a)=(-1)^{k-1}
and
b=-a^{qf_{1}}
where
f_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q^{ik}
. Let
V
be the kernel of
P(x)
, with
P(x)=x^{q^{k}}-bx^{q}-ax
. Then
C=\{\alpha V:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{N}}^{*}\}
is a cyclic subspace code in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(N,k)
, with size
\frac{q^{N}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
, for every
N=nh
with
h\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Let
P(x)=x^{q^{k}}-bx^{q}-ax
, with
n,k,a
and
b
be as in the assumptions. By (c) of Theorem
2.1
, the polynomial
P(x)
splits completely over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, so that
P(x)=P_{V}(x)
. By 3. of Proposition
7.7
, the code
C=\{\alpha V:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(n,k)
has size
\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
. Moreover, 1. and 2. of Proposition
7.7
, the minimum distance of
C
is
2k-2
. Clearly, the result holds in each extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.
∎

Similarly, as a corollary of Theorem
5.1
and Propositions
6.1
one can prove the following.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
k
and
q
be powers of
2
.
Let
n=k^{2}-1
and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}

such that
\mathrm{N}_{q^{n}/q}(a)=(-1)^{k-1}
and
b=a^{-\frac{q^{k^{2}}-q}{q^{k}-1}}
. Let
V
be the kernel of
P(x)=x^{q^{k}}-bx^{q}-ax
. Then
C=\{\alpha V:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{N}}^{*}\}
is a cyclic subspace code in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(N,k)
, with size
\frac{q^{N}-1}{q-1}
and minimum distance
2k-2
, for every
N=nh
with
h\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
n=8
,
q\equiv 0,2\pmod{3}
,
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{8}}
with
\mathrm{N}_{q^{8}/q}(a)=1
,
b=\frac{-\alpha}{a^{q^{6}+q^{3}}}
, where
\alpha
is a root of
x^{2}+x+1
. Let
V
be the kernel of
P(x)=x^{q^{3}}-bx^{q}-ax
. Then
C=\{\alpha V:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{N}}^{*}\}

with

.

Conjecture
7.5
was solved in
[
33
, Corollary 35.]
for the following parameters:

•

Any
q,n
and
k
with
k
up to the largest divisor of
n
that is smaller than
n/2
;

•

For any
q\geq 3
and even

.

So that the results in
[
33
]
do not cover the cases in which
n
is a prime number.
Therefore, as a consequence of Proposition
7.8
, we have the following result.

Corollary 7.11
.

Let
q=p^{h}
and
k=p^{\ell}+1
, where
p
is a prime number and
h,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. If
n=(k-1)k+1
is a prime then the parameters
(q,n,k)
of the construction in Proposition
7.8
are new solutions of Conjecture
7.5
.

If
k=p^{\ell}+1
, then
n=p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}+1
.
Up to our knowledge, it is not known whether or not there infinitely many prime
p
such that
n
is a prime.
This problem is related to a well-known conjecture, see Bunyakovsky conjecture
[
6
]
.
If
\ell=1
or
\ell=2
, the values of

and

, respectively,

.

8
Conclusions and open problems

In this paper we have provided closed formulas for the coefficients of a
\sigma
-trinomial
f
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
ensuring that
f
has maximum kernel.
As a consequence, we construct explicit examples of
\sigma
-linearized trinomials with maximum kernel and characterize those having
\sigma
-degree
3
and
4
.
Then we applied these results to rank metric codes, quasi-subfield polynomials and cyclic subspace codes.
We strongly believe that these results may be applied in further contexts.
Indeed, we are currently working on applying these results for the list decodability of certain rank metric codes, see
[
34
]
.
However still many open problems remain and we conclude the paper by listing some of them.

•

By the results contained in
[
2
]
, when
q
is large enough, there always exists a
\sigma
-linearized polynomial with maximum kernel of form

-x^{\sigma^{d}}+a_{d-2}x^{\sigma^{d-2}}+\ldots+a_{0}x\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q,\sigma}.

Thus, there could be room for
\sigma
-linearized polynomials with maximum kernel and having some coefficients of high
\sigma
-degree equal to zero.

•

When
n=d^{2}-1
, there are others
\sigma
-linearized trinomials with maximum kernel different from those found in Theorem
5.1
, as suggested by Proposition
6.1
. Determine the remaining family of
\sigma
-linearized polynomials with maximum kernel when
n=d^{2}-1
.

•

More generally, is it possible to manipulate the equations in Theorem
1.4
to get some more examples of linearized trinomials with maximum kernel also when
g\in\{2,\ldots,d-2\}
and
d\geq 4
?

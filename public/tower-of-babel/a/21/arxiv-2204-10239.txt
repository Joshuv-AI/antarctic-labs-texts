Linear-quadratic stochastic Volterra controls II: Optimal strategies and Riccati–Volterra equations
Yushi Hamaguchi, Tianxiao Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we study linear-quadratic control problems for stochastic Volterra integral equations with singular and non-convolution-type coefficients. The weighting matrices in the cost functional are not assumed to be non-negative definite. From a new viewpoint, we formulate a framework of causal feedback strategies. The existence and the uniqueness of a causal feedback optimal strategy are characterized by means of the corresponding Riccati–Volterra equation.



Keywords:
Linear-quadratic control; stochastic Volterra integral equation; Riccati–Volterra equation.

: 60H20; 45A05; 93E20; 93B52.

Introduction

Linear-quadratic (LQ) control problems are special classes of optimal control problems described by a linear state dynamics and a quadratic cost functional. In the continuous time setting, the state dynamics is assumed to be governed by a controlled differential/integral equation. In this paper, we consider the following controlled linear stochastic Volterra integral equation (SVIE):

\begin{split}&X(t)=x(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(t,s)X(s)+B(t,s)u(s)+b(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(t,s)X(s)+D(t,s)u(s)+\sigma(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\\
&\hskip 227.62204ptt\in(t_{0},T),\end{split}

(1.1)

where
u
is a control process,
x
is a given deterministic function called the free term (which is also called the forcing term),
W
is a Brownian motion,
A,B,C
and
D
are matrix-valued deterministic coefficients, and
b
and
\sigma
are vector-valued stochastic inhomogeneous terms. The cost functional is given by the following quadratic functional:

J(t_{0},x;u):=\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\left\{\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}Q(t)&S(t)^{\top}\\
S(t)&R(t)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}X(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}X(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}q(t)\\
\rho(t)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}X(t)\\
u(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\right\}\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

(1.2)

where
Q,S
and
R
are matrix-valued deterministic functions, and
q
and
\rho
are vector-valued adapted processes. The LQ control problem for an SVIE, which we call an
LQ stochastic Volterra control problem
, is to minimize the quadratic cost functional
J(t_{0},x;u)
over all control process
u
subject to the state dynamics (
1.1
).

The controlled SVIE (
1.1
) is a Volterra-type extension of a controlled linear stochastic differential equation (SDE):

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X(s)=\{A(s)X(s)+B(s)u(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\{C(s)X(s)+D(s)u(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X(t_{0})=x,\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
x
being a constant. LQ control problems for SDEs were first studied by Wonham
[
23
]
in 1968 and followed by many researchers; see
[
25
, Chapter 6]
and
[
18
]
for systematic studies and the recent developments of LQ control theory for SDEs. In this context, there are at least two different frameworks, namely, the
open-loop framework
and the
closed-loop framework
. On the one hand, in the open-loop framework, the problem is to find, for each fixed input condition
(t_{0},x)
, a control process
\hat{u}
such that

J(t_{0},x;\hat{u})\leq J(t_{0},x;u)

for any other control processes
u
. Such a control process is called an open-loop optimal control. The open-loop optimal control is characterized by a coupled system of an SDE and a backward SDE (BSDE) (see
[
18
, Section 2.3]
). On the other hand, in the closed-loop framework, the problem is to find an optimal “strategy” which a controller uses to select a control action based on his/her state. More precisely, in the LQ control problem for SDE (
1.3
) with the cost functional (
1.2
), consider a matrix-valued deterministic function
\Xi
and a stochastic inhomogeneous term
v
which are independent of the choice of input conditions
(t_{0},x)
. The pair
(\Xi,v)
is called a closed-loop strategy (which is also called a state-feedback strategy). Then for each input condition
(t_{0},x)
, consider the following closed-loop system of the controlled SDE (
1.3
):

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X^{t_{0},x}(s)=\{A(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\{C(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X^{t_{0},x}(t_{0})=x,\\
u^{t_{0},x}(s)=\Xi(s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+v(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\end{cases}

(1.4)

or equivalently the SDE

\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{d}X^{t_{0},x}(s)=\{(A(s)+B(s)\Xi(s))X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(s)v(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\{(C(s)+D(s)\Xi(s))X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(s)v(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ s\in[t_{0},T],\\
X^{t_{0},x}(t_{0})=x.\end{cases}

We note that the above system is an equation for the state process
X=X^{t_{0},x}
, and the control process
u=u^{t_{0},x}
is obtained as the outcome of the strategy
(\Xi,v)
by inserting the solution
X^{t_{0},x}
into the expression
u^{t_{0},x}=\Xi X^{t_{0},x}+v
. In order to clarify the dependency of the outcome
u^{t_{0},x}
on the closed-loop strategy
(\Xi,v)
and the input condition
(t_{0},x)
, we write
u^{t_{0},x}=(\Xi,v)[t_{0},x]
. The problem in the closed-loop framework is to find a closed-loop strategy
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{v})
such that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(\Xi,v)[t_{0},x])

for any other closed-loop strategy
(\Xi,v)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)
. In this case, the pair
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{v})
is called a closed-loop optimal strategy. The closed-loop optimality is closely related to the solvability of a Riccati (differential) equation and a BSDE (see
[
18
, Section 2.4]
). It is worth to mention that if
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{v})
is a closed-loop optimal strategy, then the outcome
\hat{u}^{t_{0},x}=(\hat{\Xi},\hat{v})[t_{0},x]
is an open-loop optimal control for every input condition
(t_{0},x)
. Therefore, each closed-loop optimal strategy can be seen as a state-feedback representation of an open-loop optimal control.

Optimal control problems of (non-linear) SVIEs were first studied by Yong
[
24
]
. By means of the maximum principle, he characterized the open-loop optimal control by the so-called Type-II backward stochastic Volterra integral equation (Type-II BSVIE) which is a Volterra-type extension of a BSDE. Since then, several researchers have tried to solve optimal control problems for SVIEs in the open-loop framework; see
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. On the other hand, in the special case of SVIEs with completely monotone and convolution-type kernels, several kinds of feedback representations of the optimal controls were investigated by
[
1
,
4
,
6
]
. Specifically, Abi Jaber, Miller and Pham
[
1
]
studied LQ stochastic Volterra control problems with completely monotone and convolution-type kernels. Based on an infinite-dimensional approach, they obtained a kind of a linear feedback representation of the optimal control; see also
[
2
]
for the study on the associated integral operator Riccati equation. We emphasize that the approaches of
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
]
heavily rely on the special structure of the completely monotone and convolution-type kernels, and they cannot be applied to the non-convolution-type SVIE (
1.1
).

The purpose of this paper is to formulate and investigate the closed-loop framework of LQ stochastic Volterra control problems with general (singular and non-convolution-type) coefficients. In this framework, a difficulty comes from the definition of the “strategy”. Indeed, as discussed by Pritchard and You
[
13
]
in the deterministic LQ Volterra control problems, the class of state-feedback strategies of the form
u^{t_{0},x}(t)=\Xi(t)X^{t_{0},x}(t)+v(t)
is not sufficient to capture the Volterra structure of the state dynamics (see also
[
12
]
for the study of deterministic LQ Volterra control problems). In our previous paper
[
11
]
, inspired by the so-called causal projection approach of
[
12
,
13
]
, we introduced the notion of
causal feedback strategies
for the linear controlled SVIE (
1.1
). This is a feedback strategy involving not only the state process
X(t)
, but also the
forward state process
defined by

\Theta(s,t)=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(s,r)X(r)+B(s,r)u(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(s,r)X(r)+D(s,r)u(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r)

for
(s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T):=\{(s,t)\,|\,t_{0}<t<s<T\}
. The forward state
\Theta(s,t)
can be seen as the causal projection of the original controlled SVIE (
1.1
) which is determined by information of
X
and
u
up to the current time
t
. A causal feedback strategy consists of a triplet
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
of matrix-valued deterministic functions
\Xi
and
\Gamma
and a stochastic inhomogeneous term
v
which leads to the following closed-loop system: for each input condition
(t_{0},x)
,

\begin{cases}X^{t_{0},x}(t)=x(t)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+B(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+b(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(t,s)X^{t_{0},x}(s)+D(t,s)u^{t_{0},x}(s)+\sigma(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(t_{0},T),\\
\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{A(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+B(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+b(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}r\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\{C(s,r)X^{t_{0},x}(r)+D(s,r)u^{t_{0},x}(r)+\sigma(s,r)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(r),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T),\\
u^{t_{0},x}(t)=\Xi(t)X^{t_{0},x}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(s,t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+v(t),\ t\in(t_{0},T).\end{cases}

(1.5)

We say that a pair
(X^{t_{0},x},\Theta^{t_{0},x})
satisfying the above system a
causal feedback solution
of the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) at
(t_{0},x)
corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
.
This framework is different from that of
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
]
and more reasonable in view of the (generalized) flow property and the time-consistency of the state dynamics. For more detailed theory on the causal feedback strategies and the associated causal feedback solutions, see our previous paper
[
11
]
. In order to clarify the dependency of the outcome
u^{t_{0},x}
on the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
and the input condition
(t_{0},x)
, we write
u^{t_{0},x}=(\Xi,\Gamma,v)[t_{0},x]
. Our problem is to find a causal feedback strategy
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})
such that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(\Xi,\Gamma,v)[t_{0},x])

for any other causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,v)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)
. In this case, we call the triplet
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})
a
causal feedback optimal strategy
.

The main contributions of this paper are the following two points:

(i)

We show that the existence of a causal feedback optimal strategy is equivalent to the “regular solvability” of a
Riccati–Volterra equation
(
5.1
) together with an additional condition for the solution to a
Type-II extended BSVIE
(Type-II EBSVIE) (
5.4
). The causal feedback optimal strategy and the associated value functional are expressed by the solutions of these equations. See
5.3
.

(ii)

We show that the existence of a “strongly regular solution” of the Riccati–Volterra equation (
5.1
) is equivalent to the uniform convexity of the cost functional. These two equivalent conditions imply the existence and the uniqueness of the causal feedback optimal strategy. See
6.5
.

Furthermore, we found the following interesting fact:

Fact.

If the control does not enter the drift part, that is, if
B=0
, then the causal feedback optimal strategy
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})
is of a state-feedback form in the sense that
\hat{\Gamma}=0
. Moreover, if in addition the inhomogeneous terms
b,\sigma,q
and
\rho
are zeros, then it is of a Markovian state-feedback form in the sense that
\hat{\Gamma}=0
and
\hat{v}=0
. See
5.7
.

This is a surprising consequence since, even in the homogeneous case, the state process is highly non-Markovian and being non-semimartingale due to the Volterra structure. Very recently, a similar fact was also found in an independent work of Wang, Yong and Zhou
[
19
]
by a different method. In
[
19
]
, they considered an LQ stochastic Volterra control problem (involving a terminal cost) in the open-loop framework, where the coefficients
A,B,C
and
D
are non-convolution-type but assumed to be regular (i.e. bounded and differentiable), the inhomogeneous terms
b,\sigma,q
and
\rho
are zeros,
S=0
, and the weighting matrices
Q
and
R
are assumed to be non-negative and strictly positive definite, respectively. By a dynamic programming method and a decoupling technique, they derived a causal feedback represention of the open-loop optimal control by means of a path-dependent Riccati equation which is different from our Riccati–Volterra equation (
5.1
).

Besides the fact that the coefficients of the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) are non-convolution-type and singular, our cost functional (
1.2
) is also quit general compared to
[
1
,
2
,
19
]
since we do not a priori impose any non-negativity conditions on the matrix-valued functions
Q
and
R
. In particular, under standard non-negativity assumptions for
Q
and
R
which are similar to
[
1
,
2
,
19
]
, we see that the Riccati–Volterra equation is strongly regularly solvable, which implies that there exists a unique causal feedback optimal strategy (see
6.7
). Our results (i) and (ii) mentioned above are extensions of the known results
[
16
,
17
]
of LQ control problems for SDEs (see also
[
18
, Section 2.4]
) to our LQ stochastic Volterra control problems. Type-II EBSVIEs were introduced and investigated in our previous paper
[
11
]
. They are extensions of a class of Type-II BSVIEs introduced by Yong
[
24
]
to the framework of causal feedback solutions of controlled SVIEs. The Riccati–Volterra equation (
5.1
) is a coupled system of Riccati-type Volterra integro-differential equations which appears for the first time in the literature. This is closely related to
Lyapunov–Volterra equations
which were also introduced in our previous paper
[
11
]
.

2
LQ stochastic Volterra control problems

We define the set of input conditions by
\mathcal{I}:=\{(t_{0},x)\,|\,t_{0}\in[0,T),\,x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
and control processes by

Assumption 1
.

•

The coefficients:

B\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell})
,
C\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})

,

,

.

•

The inhomogeneous terms:
b\in L^{2,1}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\sigma\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

Remark 2.1
.

In the standing assumption, the coefficients and the inhomogeneous terms of the controlled SVIE (
1.1
) are singular and of non-convolution-types. For example,
A(t,s)
is allowed to diverge as

, and the same is true for
B,C,D,b
and
\sigma

[
5
]
(where the coefficients are of non-convolution-types, but

and
D
are essentially regular, and the inhomogeneous terms
b
and
\sigma
do not appear) and
[
1
,
2
]
(where the coefficients are singular, but they are of convolution-types with completely monotone kernels, and the inhomogeneous terms are deterministic). It is also worth to mention that the assumptions for the coefficients

and

being in

fit into the framework of the so-called
\star
-Volterra kernels introduced in
[
10
]
. Furthermore, we do not impose any non-negativity conditions on the weighting matrices
Q
and
R
at this time.

The LQ stochastic Volterra control problem is stated as follows.

Problem (SVC)
.

For each
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
, find a control process
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
satisfying

J(t_{0},x;\hat{u})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)}J(t_{0},x;u)=:V(t_{0},x).

(2.1)

Definition 2.2

For each
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}

\hat{u}\in\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
satisfying (
2.1

open-loop optimal control
at
(t_{0},x)

of Problem (SVC).

Remark 2.5

We emphasize that the causal feedback strategy

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the causal feedback optimal strategy defined as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

A causal feedback strategy
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
is called a
causal feedback optimal strategy
of Problem (SVC) if

and any causal feedback strategy

The following lemma provides equivalent formulations of the causal feedback optimal strategy.

Lemma 2.7
.

For each
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})
is a causal feedback optimal strategy of Problem (SVC);

(ii)

it holds that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(\Xi,0,v)[t_{0},x])

for any
(\Xi,v)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times d})\times\mathcal{U}(0,T)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
;

(iii)

it holds that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(0,\Gamma,v)[t_{0},x])

for any
(\Gamma,v)\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times d})\times\mathcal{U}(0,T)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
;

(iv)

it holds that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},v)[t_{0},x])

for any
v\in\mathcal{U}(0,T)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
;

(v)

it holds that

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;u)

for any
u\in\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
and any input condition
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

From the definition of the causal feedback optimality, (i) implies (ii),(iii),(iv) and (v). The implications (ii)
\Rightarrow
(v), (iii)
\Rightarrow
(v) and (v)
\Rightarrow
(i) are also trivial. We only need to show the implication (iv)
\Rightarrow
(v).

Assume that (iv) holds. Let
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}(t_{0},T)
be arbitrary, and denote by
(X,\Theta)
the corresponding state pair. Define
v(t):=0
for
t\in(0,t_{0}]
and
v(t):=u(t)-\hat{\Xi}(t)X(t)-\int^{T}_{t}\hat{\Gamma}(s,t)\Theta(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s
for
t\in(t_{0},T)
. Clearly,
v
is in
\mathcal{U}(0,T)
and satisfies
(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},v)[t_{0},x]=u
. By (iv), we have

J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},\hat{v})[t_{0},x])\leq J(t_{0},x;(\hat{\Xi},\hat{\Gamma},v)[t_{0},x])=J(t_{0},x;u).

Thus, (v) holds. This completes the proof.
∎

From the above lemma, if

(\Xi,\Gamma,v)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
and any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
.

.

For any

, let

) at the zero input condition

\displaystyle J^{0}(0,0;(-R^{-1}S,0,v)^{0}[0,0])

\begin{cases}\bar{P}^{(1)}_{i}(t)=F^{(1)}[\Xi_{i+1};\bar{P}_{i}](t)+\bar{Q}^{(1)}_{i}(t),\ t\in(0,T),\\
\bar{P}^{(2)}_{i}(s,t,t)=\bar{P}^{(2)}_{i}(t,s,t)^{\top}=F^{(2)}[\Xi_{i+1},\Gamma_{i+1};\bar{P}_{i}](s,t)+\bar{Q}^{(2)}_{i}(s,t),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),\\
\dot{\bar{P}}^{(2)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t)+F^{(3)}[\Gamma_{i+1};\bar{P}_{i}](s_{1},s_{2},t)+\bar{Q}^{(3)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t)=0,\ (s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T),\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{(1)}_{i}(t):=\bar{\Xi}_{i}(t)^{\top}(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t))\bar{\Xi}_{i}(t),\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{(2)}_{i}(s,t):=\bar{\Gamma}_{i}(s,t)^{\top}(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t))\bar{\Xi}_{i}(t),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{(3)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t):=\bar{\Gamma}_{i}(s_{1},t)^{\top}(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t))\bar{\Gamma}_{i}(s_{2},t),\ (s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T).

Therefore, by the representation formula (
3.10
), for any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
, we have

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(1)}_{i}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(2)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{\langle\bar{Q}^{(1)}_{i}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(t),X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(t)\rangle+2\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(2)}_{i}(s,t)X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int^{T}_{t}\!\!\int^{T}_{t}\langle\bar{Q}^{(3)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(s_{2},t),\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(s_{1},t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr],

where
(X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1},\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1})
is the causal feedback solution to the homogeneous controlled SVIE (
2.3
) at

corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi_{i+1},\Gamma_{i+1},0)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. From the definition of
(\bar{Q}^{(1)}_{i},\bar{Q}^{(2)}_{i},\bar{Q}^{(3)}_{i})
and the fact that
R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t)\geq\lambda I_{\ell}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, we get

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(1)}_{i}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle\bar{P}^{(2)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\langle(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t))\Bigl(\bar{\Xi}_{i}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\bar{\Gamma}_{i}(s,t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr),

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\bar{\Xi}_{i}(t)X^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\bar{\Gamma}_{i}(s,t)\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{i+1}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Therefore, for any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
and any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the following chain of inequalities holds:

\begin{split}&\hskip 14.22636pt\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}_{1}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}_{1}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\\
&\geq\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}_{i}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}_{i}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\\
&\geq\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(1)}_{i+1}(t)x(t),x(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\langle P^{(2)}_{i+1}(s_{1},s_{2},t_{0})x(s_{2}),x(s_{1})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\\
&\geq\alpha\int^{T}_{t_{0}}|x(t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(6.6)

Let
t_{0}\in[0,T)
be fixed. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, define a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

(\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}x)(t):=P^{(1)}_{i}(t)x(t)+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}P^{(2)}_{i}(t,s,t_{0})x(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(t_{0},T),

for

. By
3.5
,
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}
is self-adjoint. The chain of inequalities (
6.6
) implies that

\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{1}\geq\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}\geq\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i+1}\geq\alpha I_{L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for any
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, where
I_{L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})}
denotes the identity operator on
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, for each
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, the sequence
\{\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded and monotone sequence of self-adjoint operators, and hence it is strongly convergent. In other words, for any
x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the sequence
\{\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}x\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convergent sequence on
L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, for each
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator norm
\|\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
of
\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}
is estimated as
\|\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\max\{|\alpha|,\|\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
. Noting that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in L^{2}(t_{0},T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
\|x\|_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\langle\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{1}x,x\rangle_{L^{2}(t_{0},T)}|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{t\in(0,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|P^{(1)}_{1}(t)|+\Bigl(\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{T}_{0}\sup_{t\in[0,s_{1}\wedge s_{2}]}|P^{(2)}_{1}(s_{1},s_{2},t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\!\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}<\infty,

we obtain the uniform boundedness:

\sup_{t_{0}\in[0,T),i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mathcal{P}^{t_{0}}_{i}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty.

The above observations and the dominated convergence theorem yield the following assertions:

•

For any
M=(M_{1},\dots,M_{d_{1}})\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence

converges in
L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
. Similarly, the sequence
M^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}=(P_{i}{\rtimes}M)^{\top}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, converges in
L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d})
.

•

For any
M=(M_{1},\dots,M_{d_{1}})\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
and

with
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}

converges for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. Furthermore,
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|M^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}N\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}<\infty
.

Therefore, noting that
R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)(t)\geq\lambda I_{\ell}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following assertions:

•

The sequence

converges for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. Furthermore,
\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\|\Xi_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}<\infty
.

•

The sequence

\Gamma_{i}(s,t)=-(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i-1}{\rtimes}D)(t))^{-1}(B^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i-1})(s,t),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),\ i\geq 2,

converges in

•

The sequences

\displaystyle F^{(1)}[\Xi_{i};P_{i}](t)=(C^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}C)(t)+\Xi_{i}(t)^{\top}(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}C)(t)+(C^{\top}{\ltimes}P_{i}{\rtimes}D)\Xi_{i}(t)

converge for a.e.

. Furthermore, the uniform estimates

•

The sequences

converges to an element

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\Xi_{i}(t)=-(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t))^{-1}(S(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}C)(t))=:\check{\Xi}(t)\ \text{for a.e.\ $t\in(0,T)$},

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\Gamma_{i}(s,t)=-(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t))^{-1}(B^{\top}{\ltimes}P)(s,t)=:\check{\Gamma}(s,t)\ \text{in $L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$},

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}Q^{(2)}[\Xi_{i},\Gamma_{i}]=Q^{(2)}[\check{\Xi},\check{\Gamma}],\ \lim_{i\to\infty}F^{(2)}[\Xi_{i},\Gamma_{i};P_{i}]=F^{(2)}[\check{\Xi},\check{\Gamma};P]\ \text{in $L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$},

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}Q^{(3)}[\Gamma_{i}]=Q^{(3)}[\check{\Gamma}],\ \lim_{i\to\infty}F^{(3)}[\Gamma_{i};P_{i}]=F^{(3)}[\check{\Gamma};P]\ \text{in $L^{2,2,1}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\square_{3}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})$}.

Consequently,
P=(P^{(1)},P^{(2)})\in\Pi(0,T)
satisfies the Lyapunov–Volterra equation

\begin{cases}P^{(1)}(t)=F^{(1)}[\check{\Xi};P](t)+Q^{(1)}[\check{\Xi}](t),\ t\in(0,T),\\
P^{(2)}(s,t,t)=P^{(2)}(t,s,t)^{\top}=F^{(2)}[\check{\Xi},\check{\Gamma};P](s,t)+Q^{(2)}[\check{\Xi},\check{\Gamma}](s,t),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),\\
\dot{P}^{(2)}(s_{1},s_{2},t)+F^{(3)}[\check{\Gamma};P](s_{1},s_{2},t)+Q^{(3)}[\check{\Gamma}](s_{1},s_{2},t)=0,\ (s_{1},s_{2},t)\in\square_{3}(0,T).\end{cases}

By inserting the formulae

\displaystyle\check{\Xi}(t)=-(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t))^{-1}(S(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}C)(t)),\ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle\check{\Gamma}(s,t)=-(R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t))^{-1}(B^{\top}{\ltimes}P)(s,t),\ (s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

into the above Lyapunov–Volterra equation, we see that
P
satisfies the Riccati–Volterra equation (
5.1
). Furthermore, since
R(t)+(D^{\top}{\ltimes}P{\rtimes}D)(t)\geq\lambda I_{\ell}
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
with

, the solution is strongly regular. This completes the proof.
∎

\mathbb{A}(t,s):=\begin{pmatrix}A(t,s)&B(t,s)\\
\Xi(t)A(t,s)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(r,t)A(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r&\Xi(t)B(t,s)+\int^{T}_{t}\Gamma(r,t)B(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r\end{pmatrix},\ (t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T),

respectively. From the general theory on deterministic Volterra equations (see
[
7
, Chapter 9]
or
[
10
]
), the kernel
\mathbb{A}
has a resolvent
\mathbb{F}
in
L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{(d+\ell)\times(d+\ell)})
, and
\bar{\mathbb{X}}
is given by the variation of constants formula:

\bar{\mathbb{X}}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}x(t)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(t)+\bar{v}(t)\end{pmatrix}+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{F}(t,s)\begin{pmatrix}x(s)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(s)+\bar{v}(s)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(t_{0},T).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}(s,t)

\displaystyle=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(A(s,r),B(s,r))\bar{\mathbb{X}}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(A(s,r),B(s,r))\Bigl\{\begin{pmatrix}x(r)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(r)+\bar{v}(r)\end{pmatrix}+\int^{r}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{F}(r,\theta)\begin{pmatrix}x(\theta)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(\theta)+\bar{v}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Bigl\{(A(s,\theta),B(s,\theta))+\int^{t}_{\theta}(A(s,r),B(s,r))\mathbb{F}(r,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\begin{pmatrix}x(\theta)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(\theta)+\bar{v}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}(\tilde{G}_{1}(s,t,\theta),\tilde{G}_{2}(s,t,\theta))\begin{pmatrix}x(\theta)\\
x^{\Xi,\Gamma}(\theta)+\bar{v}(\theta)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

for
(s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(t_{0},T)
, where
\tilde{G}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{1}(s,t,\theta):=A(s,\theta)+\int^{t}_{\theta}\{A(s,r)F_{1,1}(r,\theta)+B(s,r)F_{2,1}(r,\theta)\}\,\mathrm{d}r,

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{2}(s,t,\theta):=B(s,\theta)+\int^{t}_{\theta}\{A(s,r)F_{1,2}(r,\theta)+B(s,r)F_{2,2}(r,\theta)\}\,\mathrm{d}r,

for
T>s\geq t\geq\theta>0
, with the notation
\mathbb{F}(t,s)=\begin{pmatrix}F_{1,1}(t,s)&F_{1,2}(t,s)\\
F_{2,1}(t,s)&F_{2,2}(t,s)\end{pmatrix}
,
(t,s)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. Define

G_{1}(s,t,\theta):=\tilde{G}_{1}(s,t,\theta)+\tilde{G}_{2}(s,t,\theta)\Xi(\theta),\ G_{2}(s,t,\theta):=\tilde{G}_{2}(s,t,\theta)

For
T>s\geq t\geq\theta>0
. It is easy to see that the estimates (
A.1
) hold for
i=1,2
, and the representation of
\bar{\Theta}=\mathbb{E}[\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}]
in (
A.2
) holds. Noting that
\mathbb{E}[X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t)]=\bar{X}(t)=\bar{\Theta}(t,t)
, we get the representation for
\mathbb{E}[X^{t_{0},x}_{0}]
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
(\Xi,\Gamma)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times d})\times L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times d})
be fixed. Let
M_{1}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{\prime}\times d})
and
M_{2}\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{\prime}\times d})
with
\ell^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that, for any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
,

M_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}[X^{t_{0},x}_{0}(t)]+\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)\mathbb{E}[\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0}(s,t)]\,\mathrm{d}s=0\ \text{for a.e.\ $t\in(t_{0},T)$},

(A.3)

where
(X^{t_{0},x}_{0},\Theta^{t_{0},x}_{0})
is the causal feedback solution to the homogeneous controlled SVIE (
2.3
) at the input condition
(t_{0},x)
corresponding to the causal feedback strategy
(\Xi,\Gamma,0)\in\mathcal{S}(0,T)
. Then it holds that

M_{1}(t)=0\ \text{for a.e.\ $t\in(0,T)$ and}\ M_{2}(s,t)=0\ \text{for a.e.\ $(s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)$}.

Proof.

First, we prove that
M_{1}(t)=0
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
. By the assumption, together with
A.2
, for any
x\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=M_{1}(t)\Bigl\{x(t)+\int^{t}_{0}G_{1}(t,t,\theta)x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{t\wedge\theta}_{0}G_{2}(t,t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)\Bigl\{x(s)+\int^{t}_{0}G_{1}(s,t,\theta)x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\int^{t\wedge\theta}_{0}G_{2}(s,t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=M_{1}(t)x(t)+\int^{T}_{0}\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{M_{1}(t)G_{1}(t,t,\theta)+\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)G_{1}(s,t,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\triangle_{2}(0,T)}(t,\theta)+M_{2}(\theta,t)\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\triangle_{2}(0,T)}(\theta,t)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\int^{t\wedge\theta}_{0}\Bigl\{M_{1}(t)G_{2}(t,t,r)+\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)G_{2}(s,t,r)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r,\ (t,\theta)\in(0,T)^{2}.

Noting the estimate (
A.1
), it is easy to see that
\mathcal{M}\in L^{2}((0,T)^{2};\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{\prime}\times d})
. Considering
x(t)=N\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[\tau,\tau+1/N]}(t)x
,
t\in(0,T)
, with
\tau\in(0,T)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
\tau+1/N<T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
being arbitrary, and then integrating with respect to
t\in(\tau,\tau+1/N)
, we get

N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}M_{1}(t)x\,\mathrm{d}t+N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)x\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

and hence

N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}M_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we have
\lim_{N\to\infty}N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}M_{1}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M_{1}(\tau)
for a.e.
\tau\in(0,T)
. On the other hand, for any
\tau\in(0,T)
,

N\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}|\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\Bigl(\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{\tau+1/N}_{\tau}|\mathcal{M}(t,\theta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/2}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Therefore, we get
M_{1}(t)=0
for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
.

Next, we show that
M_{2}(s,t)=0
for a.e.
(s,t)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. By the assumption (
A.3
), together with
A.2
and
M_{1}=0
, for any
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
,

\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)\Bigl\{x(s)+\int^{t}_{t_{0}}G_{1}(s,t,\theta)x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int^{T}_{t_{0}}\!\!\int^{t\wedge\theta}_{t_{0}}G_{2}(s,t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,x(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s=0

for a.e.
t\in(t_{0},T)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
1/N<\min\{T-\tau_{1},\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\}
be fixed. Consider
(t_{0},x)\in\mathcal{I}
with
t_{0}=\tau_{2}
and
x(t)=\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{[\tau_{1},\tau_{1}+1/N]}(t)x
,
t\in(\tau_{2},T)
. Then for a.e.
t\in(\tau_{2},\tau_{2}+1/N)
,

\Bigl\{\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}M_{2}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\!\!\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}G_{2}(s,t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}x=0,

and thus

\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}M_{2}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\!\!\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0,

where

\mathcal{N}(t,r):=\int^{T}_{t}M_{2}(s,t)G_{2}(s,t,r)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ (t,r)\in\triangle_{2}(0,T).

Noting the estimate (
A.1
), it is easy to see that
\mathcal{N}\in L^{2}(\triangle_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{\ell^{\prime}\times d})
. Integrating both sides with respect to
t\in(\tau_{2},\tau_{2}+1/N)
and multiplying
N^{2}
, we get

N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}M_{2}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\!\,\mathrm{d}t+N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

On one hand, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}M_{2}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\!\,\mathrm{d}t=M_{2}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

for a.e.
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. On the other hand, for any
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\Bigl|N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}|\mathcal{N}(t,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}|\Gamma(\theta,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}|\mathcal{N}(t,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\Bigl(\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}|\Gamma(\theta,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}|\mathcal{N}(t,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\!\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/2}\,\Bigl(N^{2}\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}|\Gamma(\theta,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\!\,\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr)^{1/2}.

We have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}|\mathcal{N}(t,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\!\,\mathrm{d}t=0

for any
\tau_{2}\in(0,T)
and, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem,

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}|\Gamma(\theta,r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\!\,\mathrm{d}\theta=|\Gamma(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})|^{2}<\infty

for a.e.
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\int^{\tau_{2}+1/N}_{\tau_{2}}\!\!\int^{\tau_{1}+1/N}_{\tau_{1}}\int^{t}_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{N}(t,r)\Gamma(\theta,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}t=0

for a.e.
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. Consequently, we have
M_{2}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=0
for a.e.
(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\triangle_{2}(0,T)
. This completes the proof.
∎

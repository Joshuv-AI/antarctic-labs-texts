On Pfaffian and determinant of one type of skew centrosymmetric matrices
Fatih YILMAZ Tomohiro SOGABE, Emrullah KIRKLAR

Abstract
This paper is dedicated to compute Pfaffian and determinant of one type of skew centrosymmetric matrices in terms of general number sequence of second order. Key words: Pfaffian; determinant; skew centrosymmetric matrix



1
Introduction

The
determinant
is one of the basic parameters in matrix theory.
For an
n
-square matrix
A
, it is defined by

\det(A)=\underset{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\sum}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\prod}}a_{i\sigma(i)}.

The
Pfaffian
which is

intimately related to determinant,
was introduced by Cayley, denoted by
Pf(A),
is defined by

\mathrm{Pf}(A)=\underset{\pi\in S_{n}}{\sum}\mathrm{sgn}(\pi)_{2n}\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\prod}}a_{\pi(2i-1)\pi(2i)}.

The Pfaffian of a skew symmetric matrix is a quantity closely related to the
determinant. Cayley’s Theorem says that the square of the Pfaffian of a
matrix is equal to the determinant of the matrix. In other words, for an
n
-square skew-symmetric matrix
A
,

\det(A)=[\mathrm{Pf}(A)]^{2}.

A matrix
A
is a
centrosymmetric matrix
if
A=JAJ^{-1}
where
J

is anti-diagonal matrix whose anti-diagonal entries are one and others are
zero. If
A=-AJA^{-1},
it is said to be
skew-centrosymmetric matrix
. This matrix family have wide applications in many fields of science such
as, numerical solution of certain differential equations, digital signal
processing, information theory, statistics, linear systems theory, some
Markov processes and so on (see
[
1
, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
).

This paper highlights the close connections among the Pfaffian, the
determinant and general number sequences of second order.

2
Pfaffian and Skew centrosymmetric matrices

Definition 1

Let us define
n
-square matrices
A_{n}=[a_{i,j}]
and
B_{n}=[b_{i,j}]
in
the given form

[a_{i,j}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}a,&\text{for }j=i+1\\
-a,&\text{for }i=j+1\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ }[b_{i,j}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(-1)^{i+1}b,&\text{for }i+j=n+1\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

where
1\leq i,j\leq n.

Definition 2

Let us define
2\times 2
-block matrices as below.

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{k}&B_{k}\\
(-1)^{k}B_{k}&A_{k}\end{array}\right)\text{ and \ \ }\mathcal{G}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{k}&-B_{k}\\
(-1)^{k+1}B_{k}&A_{k}\end{array}\right).

For example, for
n=10
, the
n
-square (
n=2k,

k
is any positive
integer) skew centrosymmetric matrix
\mathcal{F}_{n}
will be in the
following form:

\mathcal{F}_{10}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc|ccccc}0&a&0&0&0&0&0&0&0&b\\
-a&0&a&0&0&0&0&0&-b&0\\
0&-a&0&a&0&0&0&{b}&0&0\\
0&0&-a&0&a&0&-b&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&-a&0&b&0&0&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0&0&-b&0&a&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&b&0&-a&0&a&0&0\\
0&0&{-b}&0&0&0&-a&0&a&0\\
0&b&0&0&0&0&0&-a&0&a\\
-b&0&0&0&0&0&0&0&-a&0\end{array}\right).

Definition 3

Let us consider couple-recurrences given below.

\displaystyle f_{n}=bg_{n-1}+a^{2}f_{n-2}\text{ \ \ for \ }f_{1}=b,

\displaystyle g_{n}=-bf_{n-1}+a^{2}g_{n-2}\text{ \ \ for \ }g_{1}=-b.

Then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 4

For
n=2k,

f_{k}=\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\text{ \ \ and \ \ }g_{k}=\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{G}_{n}),

where
f_{-1}=0,

f_{0}=1
and
g_{-1}=0,

g_{0}=1.

Proof.
Let us prove the theorem by using mathematical induction method. For
k=1
,

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&B_{1}\\
-B_{1}&A_{1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&b\\
-b&0\end{array}\right)\text{ and }\mathcal{G}_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&-B_{1}\\
B_{1}&A_{1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-b\\
b&0\end{array}\right).

In this case,

f_{1}=\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{F}_{2})=b,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }g_{1}=\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{G}_{2})=-b.

Assume that the recurrences hold for all
t\leq k.
Then they hold for
k=t+1
.

\mathcal{F}_{2t+2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A_{t+1}&B_{t+1}\\
\hline\cr(-1)^{t+1}B_{t+1}&A_{t+1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{c|cccc|c}$0$&$a$&$0$&$\cdots$&$0$&$b$\\
\hline\cr$-a$&&&&&$0$\\
$0$&&$A_{t}$&$-B_{t}$&&$\vdots$\\
$\vdots$&&$(-1)^{t+1}B_{t}$&$A_{t}$&&$0$\\
$0$&&&&&$a$\\
\hline\cr$-b$&$0$&$\cdots$&$0$&$-a$&$0$\end{tabular}\right).

(1)

From the expansion formula along with
2t+2
column of (
1
), it
follows that

\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{F}_{2t+2})=b\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{G}_{2t})+a\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{M}_{2t})=bg_{t}+a\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{M}_{2t}),

(2)

where

\mathcal{M}_{2t}=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{cc|cccc}$0$&$a$&$0$&$\cdots$&$\cdots$&$0$\\
$-a$&$0$&$a$&$0$&$\cdots$&$0$\\
\hline\cr$0$&$a$&&&&\\
$\vdots$&$0$&&$A_{t-1}$&$B_{t-1}$&\\
$\vdots$&$\vdots$&&$(-1)^{t-1}B_{t-1}$&$A_{t-1}$&\\
$0$&$0$&&&&\end{tabular}\right)

(3)

From the expansion formula along with 1st row of (
3
), it follows that

\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{M}_{2t})=a\mathrm{Pf}(\mathcal{F}_{2t-2})=af_{t-1}.

(4)

From (
2
) and (
4
), we have

f_{t+1}=bg_{t}+a^{2}f_{t-1}.

The recurrence for
g_{t+1}
can be obtained in a similar manner.

Corollary 5

f_{n}=(-1)^{n-1}bf_{n-1}+a^{2}f_{n-2}
with
f_{-1}=0
and
f_{1}=1.

3
Determinant of the skew centrosymmetric matrix

In this section, we consider determinant of the matrix
\mathcal{F}_{n}
(
n=2k
). It is a well-known fact
[
3
]
for block matrices:

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\end{array}\right|=\det(AD-BC)

if it verifies
AC=CA
. Taking into account this property, determinant of
the matrix
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is

\left|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right|=\left|\mathcal{T}_{k}\right|=\det\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-a^{2}+b^{2}&0&a^{2}&&\\
0&-2a^{2}+b^{2}&0&\ddots&\\
a^{2}&0&\ddots&\ddots&a^{2}\\
&\ddots&\ddots&-2a^{2}+b^{2}&0\\
&&a^{2}&0&-a^{2}+b^{2}\end{array}\right)_{k\times k}.

Sogabe and El-Mikkawy
[
8
]
considered a fast block diagonalization of
k
-tridiagonal matrices using permutation matrices. Exploiting this method,
we can rearrange the matrix
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

(
i
) For
\mathbf{k}
is odd

For this let us define the following matrices:

H_{\frac{k-1}{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-2a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ for }i=j\\
a^{2},\text{ for }i=j+1\text{ and }j=i+1\\
0,\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.\text{ }

and

K_{\frac{k+1}{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=1\text{ and }i=j=\frac{k+1}{2}\\
-2a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=2(1)\frac{k-1}{2}\\
a^{2},\text{ for }i=j+1\text{ and }j=i+1\\
0,\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Then,

P^{T}\mathcal{F}_{k}P=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}H_{\frac{k-1}{2}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&K_{\frac{k+1}{2}}\end{array}\right),\text{ }

where permutation matrix
P
is determined by using method in
[
8
]
.
Obviously,

\det(P^{T}\mathcal{T}_{k}P)=\det\mathcal{T}_{k}=\det\mathcal{F}_{n}=\det(H_{\frac{k-1}{2}})\det(K_{\frac{k+1}{2}}).

(
ii
)

For
\mathbf{k}
is even

Let us define

N_{\frac{k}{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=\frac{k}{2}\\
-2a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=1(1)\frac{k}{2}-1\\
a^{2},\text{ for }i=j+1\text{ and }j=i+1\\
0,\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and

Q_{\frac{k}{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=1\\
-2a^{2}+b^{2},\text{ }i=j=2(1)\frac{k}{2}\\
a^{2},\text{ for }i=j+1\text{ and }j=i+1\\
0,\text{ otherwise}\end{array}\right..

Then,

P^{T}\mathcal{F}_{k}P=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}N_{\frac{k}{2}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&Q_{\frac{k}{2}}\end{array}\right)\text{ }.

Obviously,

\det(P^{T}\mathcal{T}_{k}P)=\det\mathcal{T}_{k}=\det\mathcal{F}_{n}=\det(N_{\frac{k}{2}})\det(Q_{\frac{k}{2}}).

It can be seen that
\det(N_{\frac{k}{2}})=\det(Q_{\frac{k}{2}})
.

El-Mikkawy
[
9
]
obtained determinant of tridiagonal matrix. That is,

v_{i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}d_{1}&a_{1}&0&\ldots&0\\
b_{2}&d_{2}&a_{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&b_{3}&d_{3}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&a_{i-1}\\
0&\ldots&0&b_{i}&d_{i}\end{array}\right|,

where
v_{i}=d_{i}v_{i-1}-b_{i}a_{i-1}v_{i-2}
for
v_{0}=1
and
v_{-1}=0
.
By exploiting
[
9
]
and Laplace expansion:

For
k
is even

\det(N_{\frac{k}{2}})=\det(Q_{\frac{k}{2}})=(-a^{2}+b^{2})w_{\frac{k}{2}-1}-a^{4}w_{\frac{k}{2}-2}.

For
k
is odd

\displaystyle\det(K_{\frac{k+1}{2}})=\left(-a^{2}+b^{2}\right)^{2}w_{\frac{k-3}{2}}-2a^{4}(-a^{2}+b^{2})w_{\frac{k-5}{2}}+a^{8}w_{\frac{k-7}{2}},

\displaystyle\det(H_{\frac{k-1}{2}})=w_{\frac{k-1}{2}},

where

w_{i}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}-2a^{2}+b^{2}&a^{2}&\ldots&0\\
a^{2}&-2a^{2}+b^{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&a^{2}\\
0&\ldots&a^{2}&-2a^{2}+b^{2}\end{array}\right|.

Here
w_{i}=(-2a^{2}+b^{2})w_{i-1}-a^{4}w_{i-2}
for
w_{0}=1
and
w_{-1}=0
.

Consequently, for
n=2k
,

i
)

If
k
is odd,

\det\mathcal{F}_{n}=\det\mathcal{T}_{k}=w_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\left(\left(-a^{2}+b^{2}\right)^{2}w_{\frac{k-3}{2}}-2a^{4}(-a^{2}+b^{2})w_{\frac{k-5}{2}}+a^{8}w_{\frac{k-7}{2}}\right).

ii
)

If
k
is even,
\det\mathcal{F}_{n}=\det\mathcal{T}_{k}=\left((-a^{2}+b^{2})w_{\frac{k}{2}-1}-a^{4}w_{\frac{k}{2}-2}\right)^{2}.

A quantitative closing Lemma and Partner orbits on Riemannian manifolds with negative curvature Thanks: The authors are partially supported by the German Research Foundation (DFG), CRC TRR 191, Symplectic structures in geometry, algebra and dynamics.
Michela Egidi, Gerhard Knieper

Abstract
. In this paper we prove a quantitative closing Lemma for manifolds of negative sectional curvature. As an application we study partner and pseudo-partner orbits for self-crossing closed geodesic.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we provide a quantitative closing Lemma for closed Riemannian manifolds of negative sectional curvature.
More precisely, let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
whose sectional curvature
K
is bounded
by
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}<\infty

and let
\phi^{t}:SM\to SM
be the geodesic flow on the unit tangent bundle
SM
of
M
. The metric
d
induced by
g
on
M
yields a metric
d_{1}
on
SM
given by

d_{1}(v,w)=\max_{t\in[-1,1]}d(c_{v}(t),c_{w}(t))

where
c_{v},c_{w}
are geodesics in
M
with initial vector
v
and
w
, respectively.
Under these assumptions we obtain the following quantitative version of the closing Lemma (see Theorem
3.14
in Section
3
).

Theorem 1
.

There exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big)
and
t_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1

such that for all
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, all
T\geq t_{0}
and all
w\in SM
with
d_{1}(w,\phi^{T}(w))\leq\delta

there exist
u\in SM
,
T^{\prime}>0
, with
\phi^{T^{\prime}}(u)=u
and

|T-T^{\prime}|\leq 2C\delta,

where the constant
C
is given by

C=\frac{4\pi}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+3\Big).

Furthermore,

d_{1}(\phi^{s}(w),\phi^{s}(u))\leq(5C+1)\delta\qquad\forall\,s\in[0,T].

A first consequence of the closing Lemma is the existence of so called partner orbits of closed geodesics with small self-crossing angle.
A closed geodesic
c_{w}:[0,T]\to M
with
c_{w}(T_{1})=c_{w}(0)
for
0<T_{1}<T
has a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
and we denote by

\sphericalangle_{p}(w,-\dot{c}_{w}(T_{1}))
its crossing angle.

Partner orbits have been studied by physicist to
explain universal behaviour of the spectrum of the associated quantized systems (see
[
1
]
for more details).
These partner orbits are new closed geodesics with slightly smaller length whose image lies in a very small neighbourhood of the original closed geodesic.
For surfaces they have been first studied by Sieber and Richter
[
11
,
10
]
and such pairs are sometimes referred to as Sieber-Richter pairs.
A rigorous mathematical treatment has been provided for closed surfaces of constant negative curvature by Huynh and Kunze in
[
7
]
and Huynh in
[
6
]
. While in
[
7
]
and
[
6
]

the authors used an algebraic approach, we will use more geometric techniques which work for Riemannian manifolds with variable negative curvature
and arbitrary dimension (see Theorem
4.1
in Section
4
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}
and let
T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1
be the one from Prop.
3.8
.
Then for all closed geodesic
c_{w}:[0,T] \to M
of period
T

with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
and crossing angle
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
T_{1},T-T_{1}\geq T_{0}
there exists a closed orbit
c_{u}:[0,T^{\prime}] \to M
of period
T^{\prime}<T
satisfying

T-T^{\prime}\leq\Big(\frac{18}{\kappa_{1}}+\frac{16\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}^{2}}\Big)\varepsilon,

and such that

d(c_{u}(s),c_{w}[0,T])\leq\left(\frac{25}{\kappa_{1}}+\frac{24\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}^{2}}\right)\varepsilon

for all
s\in[0,T^{\prime}]
.

For surfaces we can improve the estimate of the action difference obtained in Theorem
2
(see Theorem
5.3
in Section
5
).
In particular, if the pinching constant of the Gaussian curvature is at least
\frac{1}{2}
the action difference is of order
\varepsilon^{2}
in the crossing angle
\varepsilon
.
This result confirms the findings of Sieber and Richter
[
11
,
10
]
, and agrees with the result of Huynh and Kunze
[
7
]
for surfaces of constant negative Gaussian curvature.
More precisely, we obtain:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian surface with Gaussian curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}
and let
T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1
be as in Proposition
3.8
.
Then for all closed geodesic
c_{w}\colon[0,T]\rightarrow M
of period
T
with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
with crossing angle
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

and
T_{1},T-T_{1}\geq T_{0}
,
there exists a closed geodesic
c_{u}\colon[0,T^{\prime}]\rightarrow M
of period
T^{\prime}<T
such that

C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\leq T-T^{\prime}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big),\\
C_{3}\varepsilon^{2}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big[\frac{1}{2},1\big],\end{array}\right.

where
C_{i}
are suitable positive constants depending on the curvature bounds and the injectivity radius of
M
.

A second consequence of the closing Lemma is the existence of so called pseudo-partner orbits for closed geodesic with large crossing angle,
that is the existence of a pair of two closed geodesics remaining close to the loops of the original self-crossing geodesic.
Their lengths are comparable, although smaller, with the the length of the loops
(see Theorem
6.1
in Section
6
).

Precisely we prove the following:

Theorem 4
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}
and let
T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1
be the one from Prop.
3.8
.
Then for all closed geodesic
c_{w}:[0,T] \to M
of period
T

with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
such that
\sphericalangle_{p}(w,\dot{c}_{w}(T_{1}))=\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

and
T_{1},T_{2}:=T-T_{1}\geq T_{0}

there exists a pair of closed geodesics
c_{u_{1}}
and
c_{u_{2}}
of period
\hat{T}_{1}
and
\hat{T}_{2}
,
satisfying

0\leq T_{1}-\hat{T}_{1}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon\;\;\text{and}\;\;0\leq T_{2}-\hat{T}_{2}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon.

Furthermore,

d(c_{u_{1}}(s),c_{w}(s))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for
s\in[0,T_{1}]
, and

d(c_{u_{2}}(s),c_{w}(s+T_{1}))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for
s\in[0,T_{2}]
.
In particular,

d(c_{u_{1}}(\hat{T}_{1}),c_{u_{2}}(0))\leq\frac{32}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we recall important facts about Hadamard manifolds
and we prove some trigonometric estimates which we will need in the sequel. In Section
3
we obtain the quantitative version of the Anosov closing Lemma formulated in Theorem
1
.
In Section
4
we derive the existence of Sieber-Richter pairs for any dimension, together with the estimate for the action difference stated in Theorem
2
,
while in Section
5
we analyse Sieber-Richter pairs for surfaces and obtain an improved estimate for their action difference (see Theorem
3
).
Finally in Section
6
we prove the existence of pseudo-partner orbits (see Theorem
4
).

2.
Basic facts on Hadamard manifolds

In this section we recall certain standard facts on Hadamard manifolds, i.e, complete simply connected Riemannian manifolds of non-positive curvature which we will need in the sequel.
We start with the construction of the boundary at infinity and a discussion on Busemann functions. We then prove some basic estimates for geodesic triangle under the assumption of
negative pinched curvature and we conclude this section with a quantitative version of the Anosov closing Lemma, on which the construction of partner orbits will be based.

For the rest of this section,
X
denotes a Hadamard manifold of dimension
d\geq 2
.

2.1.
Boundary at infinity

To introduce the boundary at infinity of
X
, we follow
[
4
,
3
]
and we start by defining an equivalence relation on the set of geodesics in
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

Two geodesic rays
c_{1},c_{2}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
are called
asymptotic

if
\sup_{t\geq 0}d(c_{1}(t),c_{2}(t))<\infty
. This is an equivalence relation; we write
\partial X
for the set of equivalence classes and call its elements
points at infinity
. We denote the equivalence class of a geodesic ray (or geodesic)
c
by
c(\infty)
.

Given
v\in SX
, let
c_{v}
be the unique geodesic with
\dot{c}_{v}(0)=v
. The following is proved in
[
3
, Propositions 1.5 and 1.14]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given any
p\in X
and
\xi\in\partial X
, there is a unique geodesic ray
c=c_{p,\xi}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
with
c(0)=p
and
c(\infty)=\xi
.
Equivalently, the map
f_{p}\colon S_{p}X\to\partial X
defined by
f_{p}(v)=c_{v}(\infty)
is a bijection.

Following Eberlein
and O’Neill
[
4
]
we equip
\partial X
with a topology that makes it a compact metric space homeomorphic to
S^{n-1}
. Fix
p\in X
and let
f_{p}\colon S_{p}X\to\partial X

be the bijection
v\mapsto c_{v}(\infty)
from Lemma
2.2
. The topology (sphere-topology) on
\partial X
is defined such that
f_{p}

becomes a homeomorphism.
Since the map
f_{q}^{-1}f_{p}\colon S_{p}X\to S_{q}X
is a homeomorphism for all
q\in X

[
3
]
, the topology is independent of the reference point
p
.

The topologies on
\partial X
and
X

extend naturally
to
\bar{X}:=X\cup\partial X

by requiring that the map

\varphi\colon B_{1}(p)=\{v\in T_{p}X:\|v\|\leq 1\}\to\bar{X}

defined by

\varphi(v)=\begin{cases}\exp_{p}\left(\frac{v}{1-\|v\|}\right)&\|v\|<1\\
f_{p}(v)&\|v\|=1\end{cases}

is a homeomorphism. This topology, called the cone topology, was also introduced by Eberlein
and O’Neill in
[
4
]
. In
particular,
\bar{X}
is homeomorphic to a closed ball in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The relative topology on
\partial X
coincides with the sphere topology, and the relative topology on
X
coincides with the manifold topology.

Every geodesic
c\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
determines two distinct points
c(-\infty)
and
c(+\infty)
on
\partial X
. We will use the notation

(2.1)

v^{-}:=c_{v}(-\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad v^{+}:=c_{v}(+\infty).

We also write

(2.2)

\partial^{2}X:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in(\partial X)^{2}:\xi\neq\eta\}

and consider the
endpoint map

(2.3)

\displaystyle E\colon SX

\displaystyle\to\partial^{2}X,\qquad v

\displaystyle\mapsto(v^{-},v^{+}).

By
[
3
, Proposition 1.7]
, every pair of distinct points on
\partial X
is joined by exactly one geodesic, so the map
E
is onto.
Note that
E
is continuous by the definition of the topology on
\partial X
.

2.2.
Busemann functions

Given
v\in SX
, the function
b_{v}:X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

b_{v}(q):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(d(q,c_{v}(t))-t\right)

is called the
Busemann function
associated to
v
. Busemann functions are of class
C^{2}
, as proved by Heintze and Im Hof in (the proof of)
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
, see also the references therein for an
unpublished result of Eberlein. For a discussion on the regularity of Busemann functions in the more general context of no conjugate points see
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
p\in X
and
\xi\in\partial X
, let
v\in S_{p}X
be the unique unit tangent vector at
p
such that
c_{v}(\infty)=\xi
.
We call
b_{\xi}(q,p):=b_{v}(q)
the Busemann function based at
\xi

and normalized by
p
(
b_{\xi}(p,p)=0
).

The zero set of the Busemann function
b_{\xi}(q,p)
given by

H_{\xi}(p):=\{q\in X\mid b_{\xi}(q,p)=0\}

is called the horosphere through
p
centred at
\xi
.
The Busemann function fulfils the following
cocycle property
:

(2.4)

b_{\xi}(p,q)=b_{\xi}(p,r)+b_{\xi}(r,q)\text{ for all }\xi\in\partial X\text{ and }p,q,r\in X.

Note also that

(2.5)

b_{\xi}(p,q)=-b_{\xi}(q,p)\text{ for all }\xi\in\partial X\text{ and }p,q\in X.

Furthermore,
\mathrm{grad}b_{\xi}(q,p)=-\dot{c}_{q,\xi}(0)
and is, in particular, independent of
p
.
For
q\in X
and
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
we denote by

\sphericalangle_{q}(\xi,\eta)=\sphericalangle_{q}(\dot{c}_{q,\xi}(0),\dot{c}_{q,\eta}(0))=\cos\langle\mathrm{grad}b_{\xi}(q,p),\mathrm{grad}b_{\eta}(q,p)\rangle

the angle of
\xi,\eta
as seen from
q
(visibility angle).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Hadamard manifold. Then for any pair of points
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
the visibility angle
f(q)=\sphericalangle_{q}(\xi,\eta)
defines a
C^{1}
function
on
q\in X
. If the sectional curvature
K
of
X
satisfies
-\kappa^{2}\leq K
for some
\kappa\geq 0
, then the norm of the gradient of
f
is bounded by
\kappa
.
In particular,
f
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\kappa
.

Proof.

Since Busemann functions are
C^{2}
, it follows from the definition of the visibility angle that
f
is a
C^{1}
function.
Since the Hessian of the Busemann function is positive semi-definite and bounded from above by
\kappa
, we obtain the estimate for the gradient of
f
.
∎

2.3.
Some trigonometrical estimates for pinched negatively curved Hadamard manifolds

In this subsection we present some results related to geodesic triangles, angles, and orthogonal projections in
a Hadamard manifold
X
with bounded sectional curvature
K
.

In the following, we denote by
\Delta(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
a geodesic triangle with vertices
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
.
We may also use the notation
\Delta_{\bullet}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
to specify in which manifold the triangle lives.
The angles with vertices
p_{i}
are denoted by
\alpha_{i}
and the lengths of the sides opposite to
\alpha_{i}
by
\ell_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.

We start by recalling the law of cosine and sine in the case of non-constant negative curvature.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Hadamard manifold with
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}<\infty
,
and let
\Delta(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\subset X
as described above.
Then

(2.6)

\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})\geq\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})-\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})\cos(\alpha_{3}),

and

(2.7)

\cosh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{3})\leq\cosh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{1})\cosh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{2})-\sinh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{2})\cos(\alpha_{3}).

Moreover, if
\alpha_{3}=\frac{\pi}{2}
we have

(2.8)

\sin(\alpha_{i})\leq\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{i})}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}\qquad i=1,2,

and

(2.9)

\sin(\alpha_{i})\geq\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{i})}{\sinh(\kappa_{2}\ell_{3})}\qquad i=1,2.

Equality in all of the above holds if
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}
.

The above inequalities give us the next lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a Hadamard manifold with
K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
\kappa_{1}>0
and let
\Delta(p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})
be as in the beginning of this subsection.
Then

(2.10)

\frac{2\alpha_{3}^{2}}{\pi^{2}} \leq 2\sin^{2}(\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2})\leq\frac{\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})-1}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})}.

Furthermore assume that
\alpha_{3}=\frac{\pi}{2}
,then

(2.11)

\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\leq\frac{1}{\sin(\alpha_{2})}

and

(2.12)

\sin(\alpha_{1})\leq\frac{\cot(\alpha_{2})}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}.

Proof.

We start by proving (
2.10
). The addition theorem for hyperbolic functions yields

\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})=\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})+\cosh(\kappa_{1}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1})).

Therefore, by the law of cosine (
2.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\cosh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})

\displaystyle\geq\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})(1-\cos(\alpha_{3}))+\cosh(\kappa_{1}(\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}))

\displaystyle\geq\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})(1-\cos(\alpha_{3}))+1.

Using
1-\cos(\alpha_{3})=2\sin^{2}(\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2})
and the inequality
\sin\alpha\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\alpha
for all
\alpha\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]

we obtain (
2.10
).

We now turn to the proof of (
2.11
) and (
2.12
). The law of cosine (
2.6
) with
\alpha_{3}=\pi/2
and (
2.8
) yield

\displaystyle\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}{\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})}=\frac{\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}{1+\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}{1+\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}=\frac{1+\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}{1+\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})},

which proves (
2.11
). Equation (
2.12
) is a consequence of (
2.8
) and the estimate just proved:

\displaystyle\sin^{2}(\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})}{\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}=\frac{\cosh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1})-1}{\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})}-1}{\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}=\frac{1-\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos^{2}(\alpha_{2})}{\sin^{2}(\alpha_{2})\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}=\frac{\cot^{2}(\alpha_{2})}{\sinh^{2}(\kappa_{1}\ell_{3})}.

∎

The corollary below gives relations among sides, angles, and distances from a vertex to the opposite side in a geodesic triangle, assuming
that one of the angles is large enough and the lengths of the sides starting at its vertex are bounded from below.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
X
be a Hadamard manifold with
K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
\kappa_{1}>0
,
\Delta(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
be as in at the beginning of this subsection,
and assume
\alpha_{3}\in[\pi-\varepsilon,\pi]
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.
Set
a(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\arcosh\left(\frac{1}{\cos(\varepsilon)}\right)
and denote further by
c_{1}
the geodesic
connecting
p_{3}
and
p_{2}
, by
c_{2}
the geodesic connecting
p_{3}
and
p_{1}
,
and by
c_{3}
the geodesic connecting
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.
Then

(2.13)

c_{1}([0,\ell_{1}]),c_{2}([0,\ell_{1}])\in\left\{q\in X\,|\,d(q,c_{3})\leq a\big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)\right\}.

Assume furthermore that
\ell_{i}\geq R_{i}
for some
R_{i}>0
,
i=1,2
, then

(2.14)

\ell_{3}\geq R_{1}+R_{2}-2a\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big),

and

(2.15)

\sin(\alpha_{i})\leq\tan(\varepsilon)\sinh^{-1}(\kappa_{1}R_{j})\quad i,j\in\{1,2\},i\neq j.

Proof.

We first observe that the functions
t\mapsto d(c_{1}(t),c_{3})
and
t\mapsto d(c_{2}(t),c_{3})
are convex and therefore

\max_{q\in c_{1}\cup c_{2}}d(q,c_{3})=d(p_{3},c_{3}).

Since
\alpha_{3}\geq\pi/2
, the foot-point projection
p
of
p_{3}
onto
c_{3}
is contained in the geodesic segment between
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
.
Consider the angles

\varphi_{1}=\sphericalangle_{p_{3}}(p_{1},p)
and
\varphi_{2}=\sphericalangle_{p_{3}}(p_{2},p)
.
Since at least one of the angles
\varphi_{i}
is bigger or equal than

\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2}
, and
\frac{\pi}{2} -\frac{\varepsilon}{2} \leq\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, Eq. (
2.11
) in Lemma
2.6
implies

\displaystyle d(p_{3},c_{3})

\displaystyle=d(p_{3},p)\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\arcosh\left(\frac{1}{\sin(\varphi_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\arcosh\left(\frac{1}{\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{3}}{2}\right)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\arcosh\left(\frac{1}{\cos\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}\right)=a\big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big),

proving (
2.13
). The equation (
2.14
) is a direct consequence of the triangle inequality:

\ell_{3}=d(p_{1},p)+d(p,p_{2})\geq R_{1}-a(\varepsilon/2)+R_{2}-a(\varepsilon/2).

We now turn to the proof of (
2.15
). Eq. (
2.12
) applied to
\Delta(p_{i},p_{3},p)
,
i=1,2
, yields

\sin(\alpha_{i})\leq\frac{\cot(\varphi_{i})}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}R_{j})}

for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
with
i\neq j
. Since
\pi-\varepsilon\leq\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\leq\pi
and
\varphi_{i}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, we obtain

\varphi_{2}\geq\pi-\varepsilon-\varphi_{1}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon
and similarly
\varphi_{1}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon
.
Therefore, we obtain

\cot(\varphi_{i})\leq\cot(\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon)=\tan\varepsilon,

which implies the claim.
∎

The next lemma tells us that the angle between points at infinity is small if their corresponding vectors are close w.r.t. to the metric on
SX
defined by

d_{1}(v,w)=\max_{t\in[-1,1]}d(c_{v}(t),c_{w}(t)),

where the metric
d
is induced by the Riemannian metric on
X
. Note that by definition the metric is flip-invariant, i.e.
d_{1}(v,w)=d_{1}(-v,-w)
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X
be a Hadamard manifold with sectional curvature
K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
\kappa_{1}>0
and let
SX
be endowed with the metric
d_{1}
defined above.
Let
v,w\in SX
such that
d_{1}(v,w)\leq\delta
for
\delta\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big]
.
Then for
p=\pi w
we have

\sphericalangle_{p}(c_{v}(+\infty),c_{w}(+\infty))\leq f(\delta)\;\text{and } \;\sphericalangle_{p}(c_{v}(-\infty),c_{w}(-\infty))\leq f(\delta),

where
f(\delta)=2\arcsin\Big(\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\delta)}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}(1-\delta))}\Big)
.

Remark 2.9
.

Since the function
f(\delta)

is convex and attains its maximum at
\frac{1}{2}
with value
\pi
,
it holds

f(\delta)\leq 2\pi\delta\qquad\forall\delta\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big].

Proof.

By the flip-invariance of
d_{1}
it is enough to prove
\sphericalangle_{p}(c_{v}(+\infty),c_{w}(+\infty))\leq f(\delta)
.
Let
s>1
and set
\alpha(s):=\sphericalangle_{c_{w}(0)}(c_{w}(+\infty),c_{v}(s))
,
\beta:=\sphericalangle_{c_{w}(0)}(c_{v}(1),c_{w}(1))
and

\tilde{\alpha}(s)=\sphericalangle_{c_{w}(0)}(c_{v}(1),c_{v}(s))
. Then

\sphericalangle_{p}(c_{v}(+\infty),c_{w}(+\infty))=\lim_{s\to\infty}\alpha(s)\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}\tilde{\alpha}(s)+\beta.

It is enough to show that
\beta+\tilde{\alpha}(s)
is bounded by
f(\delta):=2\arcsin\Big(\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\delta)}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}(1-\delta))}\Big)
.

By assumption we have
d(c_{v}(0),c_{w}(0))\leq\delta
and
d(c_{v}(1),c_{w}(1))\leq\delta
.
Let
q
be the foot-point projection of
c_{v}(1)
onto
c_{w}
and consider the triangle
\Delta(c_{w}(0),q,c_{v}(1))
.
Then we have
\beta=\sphericalangle_{c_{w}(0)}(c_{v}(1),q)
and by (
2.8
) we have

\sin(\beta)\leq\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}d(c_{v}(1),q))}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}d(c_{w}(0),c_{v}(1)))}.

By the triangle inequality we have

d(c_{w}(0),c_{v}(1))\geq d(c_{w}(0),c_{w}(1))-d(c_{w}(1),c_{v}(1))\geq 1-\delta

and therefore we obtain

\sin(\beta)\leq\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}\delta)}{\sinh(\kappa_{1}(1-\delta))},

which implies
\beta\leq\frac{1}{2}f(\delta)
.

For the angle
\eta:=\sphericalangle_{c_{v}(1)}(c_{w}(0),c_{v}(0))
, we have

\eta\leq\frac{1}{2}f(\delta)
,
with a similar argument as before considering the triangle
\Delta(c_{w}(0),c_{v}(1),q_{1})
, where
q_{1}
is the foot-point projection of
c_{w}(0)
onto
c_{v}
.

Consider now the triangle
\Delta(c_{w}(0),c_{v}(s),c_{v}(1))
with angles
\tilde{\alpha}(s)
,
\eta_{1}
and
\gamma
at the points
c_{w}(0),c_{v}(1)
and
c_{v}(s)
, respectively.
Since
\eta_{1}
is the complement of the angle
\eta
and
\tilde{\alpha}(s)+\eta_{1}+\gamma\leq\pi
, we have
\pi-\eta+\tilde{\alpha}(s)+\gamma\leq\pi
,
which implies
\tilde{\alpha}(s)\leq\eta
.

We conclude

\beta+\tilde{\alpha}(s)\leq f(\delta)

for all
s>1
and the claim follows.
∎

3.
A quantitative closing lemma

Let
M=X/\Gamma
be a closed
d
-dimensional manifold,
d\geq 2
, with sectional curvature

-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for some
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}<\infty
,
where
X
is its universal cover and
\Gamma
is the group of deck transformations.
The map
\pr\colon X\rightarrow M
denotes the canonical projection,
while we denote with
\pi:SM\rightarrow M
the foot-point projection defined by
\pi(v)=p
for all
v\in S_{p}M
.
Furthermore, we will denote by
\phi^{t}
the geodesic flow on
M
, i.e. the map
\phi^{t}\colon SM\rightarrow SM
,
\phi^{t}(v)=\dot{c}_{v}(t)
,
where
c_{v}
is the unique geodesic with
c_{v}(0)=\pi(v)
and
\dot{c}_{v}(0)=v
.
By abuse of notation, we will also denote by
\pi
and
\phi^{t}
the foot-point projection from
SX
onto
X
and the geodesic flow on
X
, respectively.

The class of closed geodesics on
M
is in one-to-one correspondence with the conjugacy classes in the group
\Gamma
of deck transformations.
We here recall some elements of this correspondence, which we will use in the next sections.

Given a closed geodesic
\bar{c}\colon\mathbb{R}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\to M
, the length
\ell>0
is such that
\bar{c}(t+\ell)=\bar{c}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and thus for every lift
c
of
\bar{c}
there is a unique
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that

(3.1)

c(t+\ell)=\gamma c(t)\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Definition 3.1
.

If
c
,
\gamma
, and
\ell>0
are such that (
3.1
) holds, then we say that
\gamma
is the
axial isometry
of
(c,\ell)
, and
c
is an
axis
of
\gamma
.

If
c
is an axis of some
\gamma
, then it follows immediately that
\bar{c}={\pr}\circ c
is a closed geodesic on
M
. Moreover, every axial isometry
\gamma
of
c
fixes
c(\pm\infty)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given any geodesic
c
on
X
such that
\bar{c}={\pr}\circ c
is closed, the set of
\gamma\in\Gamma
fixing
c(-\infty)
and
c(\infty)
is an infinite cyclic subgroup.

The preceding discussion lets us go from closed geodesics to deck transformations. We will also need to go in the other direction.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\id
, the

of

|\gamma|:=\inf\{d(q,\gamma q):q\in X\}.

.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\id

. Moreover, if

is a geodesic joining
q_{0}
and
\gamma q_{0}
, then
c(t+|\gamma|)=\gamma c(t)
for all

, so

is a closed geodesic.

\displaystyle\geq\pi-4\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}-3\theta-b^{\prime}e^{-\kappa_{1}(t-2a(\theta))}\frac{2}{\kappa_{1}}\theta,

where the estimate
\sphericalangle_{x_{2}}(\gamma^{-1}x_{1},\gamma^{-1}y_{2})\leq b^{\prime}e^{-\kappa_{1}(t-2a(\theta))}\frac{2}{\kappa_{1}}\theta
follows as in (

)

.
In particular, one can choose

such that

for all

. Let

be the orthogonal projection of

onto the geodesic ray connecting

and

yields the requested upper bound

which yields

Hence

\delta_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\theta_{0}
.
Further, let
T\geq t_{0}
and set
\theta_{\delta}=2\pi\delta
for all
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
. Then
\theta_{\delta}\leq\theta_{0}
for
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
.

As before, we prove that
\gamma_{*}^{-1}\phi^{T}(v_{0})\in A_{\theta_{\delta}}(v_{0})
.
We have

0=\tilde{d}(\gamma p,\tilde{c}(T))=\tilde{d}(p,\gamma^{-1}\tilde{c}(T))=\tilde{d}(p,\pi\gamma_{*}^{-1}\phi^{T}v_{0}),

and

(\gamma^{-1}_{*}\phi^{T}(v_{0}))^{+}\in\mathbf{F}_{\theta_{\delta}}(v_{0})\;\text{and}\;(\gamma^{-1}_{*}\phi^{T}(v_{0}))^{-}\in\mathbf{P}_{\theta_{\delta}}(v_{0})

for all
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
by Lemma
2.8
and Remark
2.9
, which proves the claim.

By Proposition
3.11
and Remark
3.12
there exists an axis
c
of
\gamma
of length
T^{\prime}<T
satisfying

0\leq T-T^{\prime}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\theta_{\delta}=\frac{16\pi}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\delta=:4\tilde{C}\delta,

and such that

\tilde{d}(c(t),\tilde{c}(t))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\theta_{\delta}=6\tilde{C}\delta\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

For
s\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\tilde{d}(c(T+s),\tilde{c}(T+s))

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{d}(c(T+s),\gamma c(s))+\tilde{d}(\gamma c(s),\gamma\tilde{c}(s))+\tilde{d}(\gamma\tilde{c}(s),\tilde{c}(T+s))

\displaystyle\leq T-T^{\prime}+6\tilde{C}\delta+\delta=(10\tilde{C}+1)\delta.

Similarly, for

4.
Construction of partner orbits

Using the results of Section
3
we can now prove the existence of partner orbits of self-crossing geodesics, together with the fact that they have smaller period
and that they remain close to the original geodesic.

We recall that a closed geodesic
c_{w}
has a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
with crossing angle
\varepsilon

if there is a time
T_{1}\in(0,T)
such that

c_{w}(0)=c_{w}(T_{1})
and
\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,-\dot{c_{w}}(T_{1}))=\varepsilon
.
We will also refer to such geodesics as closed geodesic with self-crossing at
p
at time
T_{1}
to emphasise the time at which the
intersection takes place.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}

\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}

T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1

3.8
.

\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, all
T_{1},T_{2}\geq T_{0}

T=T_{1}+T_{2}

with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
and crossing angle
\varepsilon
there exists a closed orbit
c_{u}:[0,T^{\prime}] \to M

and such that for some

c_{w}:[0,T]\to M
be as in the statement of the theorem

c_{w,1}\colon[0,T_{1}]\to M
,
c_{w,1}(t)=c_{w}(t)

and
c_{w,2}\colon[0,T_{2}]\rightarrow M

T_{2}:=T-T_{1}
the associated geodesic loops. Consider the geodesic loop
\bar{c}_{w,2}:[0,T_{2}]\to M

and
c_{w,2}

\gamma_{2}(\tilde{c}_{w,2}(0))=\tilde{c}_{w,2}(T_{2})

with initial point

\bar{c}_{w,2}

\tilde{c}_{2}(0)=p_{1}

\tilde{c}_{1}=\tilde{c}_{w,1}

\sphericalangle_{p_{1}}(\dot{\tilde{c}}_{1}(T_{1}),\dot{\tilde{c}}_{2}(0))=\varepsilon
. Furthermore,

\sphericalangle_{p_{2}}((\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1})_{\ast}v_{0},\dot{\tilde{c}}_{2}(T_{2}))=\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,-\dot{c}_{w,2}(0))=\varepsilon.

Finally, let

5.
Construction of partner orbits for surfaces

In the case that the manifold
M
has dimension
2
,
it is possible to improve the estimate for the action difference
in Theorem
4.1
by means of Lemma
2.10
.
This new estimate is consistent with the result in
[
7
]
.
To this end, we need an estimate from above for the length of closed geodesic loops in
M
,
and one from above and below for the difference between the lengths of sides in a geodesic triangle.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a compact
d
-dimensional manifold,
d\geq 2
, with sectional curvature
-\kappa\leq K\leq 0
, for some
\kappa>0
.
Assume that the injectivity radius
\mathrm{inj}(M)=\frac{\rho}{2}
is positive.
Let
c:[0,r]\rightarrow M
be a geodesic loop, i.e.
c(0)=c(r)
and assume
\sphericalangle(\dot{c}(0),-\dot{c}(r))\leq\varepsilon>0
.
Then

(5.1)

r\geq\frac{1}{\kappa} \arcosh\left(\frac{b}{1-\cos(\varepsilon)}+1\right)

with
b=2(\cosh(\kappa\rho)-1)
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{c}:[0,r]\rightarrow X
be a lift of
c
and let
\dot{\tilde{c}}(0)=v\in S_{p}X
.
Then there exits
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
\gamma(p)=\tilde{c}(r)
. Furthermore,

\sphericalangle(\gamma_{*}(v),-\dot{\tilde{c}}(r))\leq\varepsilon
and
\gamma\tilde{c}(t)
is the geodesic with initial condition
\gamma_{*}(v)
.
Since by the assumption on the injectivity radius we have

d(\tilde{c}(\frac{r}{2}),\gamma(\tilde{c}(\frac{r}{2}))\geq\rho
. Using (
2.7
) in Lemma
2.5
we obtain

\displaystyle\cosh(\kappa\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\cosh(\kappa d(\tilde{c}(\frac{r}{2}),\gamma(\tilde{c}(\frac{r}{2}))\leq\cosh^{2}(\kappa\frac{r}{2})-\sinh^{2}(\kappa\frac{r}{2})\cos(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=1+\sinh^{2}(\kappa\frac{r}{2})(1-\cos(\varepsilon))

\displaystyle=1+\frac{1}{2}(\cosh(\kappa r)-1)(1-\cos(\varepsilon))

which yields

\frac{2}{ 1-\cos(\varepsilon)}(\cosh(\kappa\rho)-1)\leq\cosh(\kappa r)-1

and therefore

\frac{1}{\kappa} \left(\arcosh\left(\frac{b}{1-\cos(\varepsilon)}+1\right)\right)\leq r

where
b=2(\cosh(\kappa\rho)-1)
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be the universal cover of a compact manifold
M=X/\Gamma
of dimension
d\geq 2
with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
with
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\Delta(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
be a geodesic triangle in
X
such that
\sphericalangle_{p_{3}}(p_{1},p_{2})=\pi-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi)
and such that
T_{i}=d(p_{3},p_{i})
satisfies (
5.1
)
with
T_{i}
instead of
r
for
i=1,2
. Let
T^{\prime}=d(p_{1},p_{2})
and
T=T_{1}+T_{2}
, then

-\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}\log\Big(1-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{8(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}\Big)\leq T-T^{\prime}\\
\leq-\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}-\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}b}\Big)^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}\Big).

Proof.

The proof is essentially an application of Lemma
2.5
.
We start with the upper bound. By (
2.6
) in Lemma
2.5
, the addition theorem for hyperbolic cosine,
and the fact that
1-\cos(\varepsilon)=2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)
we estimate

(5.2)

\begin{split}\cosh(\kappa_{1}T^{\prime})&\geq\cosh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\cosh(\kappa_{1}T_{2})-\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{2})\cos(\pi-\varepsilon)\\
&=\cosh(\kappa_{1}(T_{1}+T_{2}))+(\cos(\varepsilon)-1)\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{2})\\
&=\cosh(\kappa_{1}(T_{1}+T_{2}))-2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{2}).\end{split}

Moreover

(5.3)

\begin{split}\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{2})}{\cosh(\kappa_{1}T)}&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(e^{\kappa_{1}T_{1}}-e^{-\kappa_{1}T_{1}})(e^{\kappa_{1}T_{2}}-e^{-\kappa_{1}T_{2}})}{e^{\kappa_{1}T}+e^{-\kappa_{1}T}}\\
&=\frac{(1-e^{-2\kappa_{1}T_{1}})(1-e^{-2\kappa_{1}T_{2}})}{2(1+e^{-2\kappa_{1}T})}\leq\frac{1}{2}.\end{split}

Using (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) and since
e^{x}/2\leq\cosh(x)\leq(e^{x}+1)/2
, we then estimate

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\kappa_{1}T^{\prime}}+1}{e^{\kappa_{1}T}}\geq\frac{\cosh(\kappa_{1}T^{\prime})}{\cosh(\kappa_{1}T)}\geq 1-\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2).

Using the assumption that
T_{i}
satisfies (
5.1
) for
i=1,2
and again the fact that
1-\cos(\varepsilon)=2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)
, we obtain

(5.5)

e^{\kappa_{2}T_{i}}\geq\cosh(\kappa_{2}T_{i})\geq\frac{b}{1-\cos(\varepsilon)}+1=\frac{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)},

so that

e^{-\kappa_{1}T}\leq\exp\Big(\frac{2\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\log\big(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\big)\Big)=\Big(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\Big)^{\frac{2\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}.

Therefore from (
5.4
) we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\kappa_{1}(T^{\prime}-T)}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)-e^{-\kappa_{1}T}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)-\Big(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\Big)^{\frac{2\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}-\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}b}\Big)^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}

and consequently

\displaystyle T^{\prime}-T\geq\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}-\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}b}\Big)^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}\Big),

which proves the upper bound.

For the lower bound we proceed similarly using (
2.7
) in Lemma
2.5
:

\displaystyle\cosh(\kappa_{2}T^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\cosh(\kappa_{2}T)+(\cos(\varepsilon)-1)\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{2}T_{2})

\displaystyle=\cosh(\kappa_{2}T)-2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)\sinh(\kappa_{1}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{2}T_{2}).

Since
T^{\prime}\leq T
we have

\frac{\cosh(\kappa_{2}T^{\prime})}{\cosh(\kappa_{2}T)}=\frac{e^{\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}}+e^{-\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}}}{e^{\kappa_{2}T}+e^{-\kappa_{2}T}}=e^{\kappa_{2}(T^{\prime}-T)}\frac{1+e^{-2\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}}}{1+e^{-2\kappa_{2}T}}\geq e^{\kappa_{2}(T^{\prime}-T)}.

Moreover, using (
5.5
) we estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{2}T_{1})\sinh(\kappa_{2}T_{2})}{\cosh(\kappa_{2}T)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-e^{-2\kappa_{2}T_{1}})(1-e^{-2\kappa_{2}T_{2}})}{2(1+e^{-2\kappa_{2}T})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Big(1-\Big(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{2}}{2\Big(1+\Big(\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\Big)^{4}\Big)}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}},

since the function
\frac{(1-x^{2})^{2}}{1+x^{4}}
is decreasing for
x\in(0,1)
and

\frac{2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}{b+2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)}\in(0,\frac{2}{b+2})
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi)
.
Therefore

\displaystyle e^{\kappa_{2}(T^{\prime}-T)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\cosh(\kappa_{2}T^{\prime})}{\cosh(\kappa_{2}T)}\leq 1-2\sin^{2}(\varepsilon/2)\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}}\Big)\varepsilon^{2},

since
\sin(x)\geq\frac{2}{\pi}x
for
x\in(0,\pi/2)
, and consequently

T^{\prime}-T\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}\log\Big(1-\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{8(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}\Big).

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem 3 stated in the introduction.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
M
be a
2
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}
and let
T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1
as in Proposition
3.8
.
Then for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, all
T_{1},T_{2}\geq T_{0}

and all closed geodesics
c_{w}\colon[0,T]\rightarrow M
of period
T=T_{1}+T_{2}
with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
and crossing angle
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}>0
, depending on the curvature bounds and on the injectivity radius of
M
, and a closed geodesic
c_{u}\colon[0,T^{\prime}]\rightarrow M
of period
T^{\prime}<T
such that

C_{1}\varepsilon^{2}\leq T-T^{\prime}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big),\\
C_{3}\varepsilon^{2}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big[\frac{1}{2},1\big],\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
c_{w}
be as in the statement and denote by
c_{w,1}\colon[0,T_{1}]\rightarrow M
,

c_{w,1}(t)=c_{w}(t)
and
c_{w,2}\colon[0,T_{2}]\rightarrow M
,
c_{w,2}(t)=c_{w}(T_{1}+t)
,
where
T_{2}:=T-T_{1}
, its geodesic loops. Let also
\bar{c}_{w,2}
such that
\bar{c}_{w,2}(t)=c_{w}(T-t)
,
and
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
be the deck transformation associated to
\tilde{c}_{1}=\tilde{c}_{w,1}
,
\tilde{c}_{w,2}
,
respectively, which denote the lifts of
c_{w,1}
and
c_{w,2}
.
Following the beginning of the proof of Theorem
4.1
consider the geodesic
\tilde{c}_{2}(0)=\gamma_{2}^{-1}\tilde{\alpha}(t)

and set
p_{0}:=\tilde{c}_{1}(0)
,
v_{0}:=\dot{\tilde{c}}_{1}(0)
,
and
p_{2}:=\tilde{c}_{2}(T_{2})

\gamma_{1}(p_{0})=p_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}^{-1}(p_{1})=p_{2}

\sphericalangle_{p_{1}}(\dot{\tilde{c}}_{1}(T_{1}),\dot{\tilde{c}}_{2}(0))=\varepsilon
.
Moreover,

\sphericalangle_{p_{2}}((\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1})_{\ast}v_{0},\dot{\tilde{c}}_{2}(T_{2}))=\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,-\dot{c}_{w,2}(0))=\varepsilon.

Denote by
\tilde{c}_{3}\colon[0,\hat{T}]\rightarrow M
the geodesic connecting
p_{0}
and
p_{2}

\hat{T}=d(p_{0},p_{2})
.
Let also
\theta_{1}:=\sphericalangle_{p_{0}}(v_{0},\dot{\tilde{c}}_{3}(0))

Considering again the vector

and arguing as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
,
we obtain that
\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{2\varepsilon}(v_{0},\hat{T})

3.10

yields the existence of an axis

To estimate the action difference
|T-T^{\prime}|

T^{\prime}=|\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}|

Parametrising

such that
d(p_{0},c)=d(p_{0},c(0))
, we have

\displaystyle d(p_{2},c(T^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=d(\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}(p_{0}),\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}(c(0)))

\displaystyle=d(p_{0},c(0))\leq a(\rho(2\varepsilon))\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\big)\varepsilon,

by Lemma
3.6
.
Further we have
\frac{\pi}{2}=\sphericalangle_{c(0)}(p_{0},c(T^{\prime}))=\sphericalangle_{c(T^{\prime})}(p_{2},c(2T^{\prime}))
which in turn gives

\sphericalangle_{c(T^{\prime})}(p_{2},c(0))=\frac{\pi}{2}
, due to dimension
2
. This means that
c[0,T^{\prime}]
is the orthogonal projection of
\tilde{c}_{3}[0,\hat{T}]
onto
c
.
Hence, Lemma
2.10
(applicable because of dimension 2) with
r_{1}=r_{2}=d(p_{0},c(0))

\sinh\big(\kappa_{2}\frac{\hat{T}}{2}\big)\leq\cosh(\kappa_{2}d(p_{0},c(0))\sinh\big(\kappa_{2}\frac{T^{\prime}}{2}\big),

and since
\hat{T}\geq T^{\prime}=|\gamma_{2}^{-1}\gamma_{1}|

\displaystyle e^{\kappa_{2}\frac{\hat{T}-T^{\prime}}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\kappa_{2}\hat{T}/2}(1-e^{-\kappa_{2}\hat{T}})}{e^{\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}/2}(1-e^{-\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}})}=\frac{\sinh(\kappa_{2}\hat{T}/2)}{\sinh(\kappa_{2}T^{\prime}/2)}

\displaystyle\leq\cosh\big(\kappa_{2}a(\rho(2\varepsilon))\big).

5.2

and of
\log(1-x)

depending on
\kappa_{1}
,

\displaystyle T-T^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(T-\hat{T})+(\hat{T}-T^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\kappa_{2}}\log(1+D\varepsilon^{2})-\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}}\log\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}-\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}b}\Big)^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{4\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big)\\
C_{3}\varepsilon^{2}&\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{2}}\in\big[\frac{1}{2},1\big].\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=(T-\hat{T})+(\hat{T}-T^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}\log\Big(1-\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{4(b+2)^{2}}{(b+2)^{4}+2^{4}}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}\Big)

6.
Construction of pseudo-partner orbits

We now turn to the study of closed geodesics
c_{w}:[0,T] \to M
, where
M
is again a manifold of arbitrary dimension, of period
T

having a large crossing angle at
c_{w}(0)=c_{w}(T_{1})
for
T_{1}\in(0,T)
. We will express this fact assuming that
its complement angle
\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,\dot{c}_{w}(T_{1}))=\varepsilon
is small enough.
Indeed, if the latter angle is small,
the crossing angle
\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,-\dot{c}_{w}(T_{1}))
is necessarily between
\pi-\varepsilon
and
\pi
, and therefore large.
Under this assumptions we show that a pair of closed geodesics exists which stay
close to the two loops generating the original closed geodesic and whose length are comparable but slightly smaller than the length of the loops. This pair of geodesics is often referred to as pseudo-partner orbits.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
-\kappa_{2}^{2}\leq K\leq-\kappa_{1}^{2}
for
0<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{\pi}{16}\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}}
and let
T_{0}=t_{0}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})>1
be the one from Prop.
3.8
.
Then for all closed geodesic
c_{w}:[0,T] \to M
of period
T

with a self-crossing at
p=c_{w}(0)
at time
T_{1}
such that
\sphericalangle_{p}(w,\dot{c}_{w}(T_{1}))=\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

and
T_{1},T_{2}:=T-T_{1}\geq T_{0}

there exists a pair of closed geodesics
c_{u_{1}}
and
c_{u_{2}}
of period
\hat{T}_{1}
and
\hat{T}_{2}
,
satisfying

0\leq T_{1}-\hat{T}_{1}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon\;\;\text{and}\;\;0\leq T_{2}-\hat{T}_{2}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon.

Furthermore,

d(c_{u_{1}}(s),c_{w}(s))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for
s\in[0,T_{1}]
, and

d(c_{u_{2}}(s),c_{w}(s+T_{1}))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for
s\in[0,T_{2}]
.
In particular,

d(c_{u_{1}}(\hat{T}_{1}),c_{u_{2}}(0))\leq\frac{32}{\kappa_{1}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\right)\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
c_{w}:[0,T]\to M
be as in the statement of the theorem
and denote by
c_{w,1}\colon[0,T_{1}]\to M
,
c_{w,1}(t)=c_{w}(t)

and
c_{w,2}\colon[0,T_{2}]\rightarrow M
,
c_{w,2}(t)=c_{w}(T_{1}+t)
, where
T_{2}:=T-T_{1}
the associated geodesic loops. Let
\tilde{c}_{w,1}
be a lifts of
c_{w,1}
to the universal cover
X
and set
\tilde{c}_{w,1}(0)=:p_{0}\in X
,
\dot{\tilde{c}}_{w,1}(0):=v_{0}
, and

\tilde{c}_{w,1}(T_{1})=:p_{1}
.
Let
\gamma_{1}
be the deck transformation with
\gamma_{1}(p_{0})=p_{1}
. Then

\sphericalangle_{p_{1}}((\gamma_{1})_{*}v_{0},\dot{\tilde{c}}_{w,1}(T_{1}))=\sphericalangle_{\pi w}(w,\dot{c}_{w,1}(T_{1})))=\varepsilon

by assumption, which implies that
\phi^{T_{1}}(v_{0})\in(\gamma_{1})_{*}A_{\varepsilon}(v_{0})
.

Therefore
v_{0}\in A_{\varepsilon}(v_{0})\cap\phi^{-T_{1}}(\gamma_{1})_{*}A_{\varepsilon}(v_{0})
and by Proposition
3.11
and Remark
3.12
there exists an axis
c_{1}
of
\gamma_{1}
of length
\hat{T}_{1}
such that

(6.1)

0\leq T_{1}-\hat{T}_{1}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon,

and such that

(6.2)

d(c_{1}(s),\tilde{c}_{w,1}(s))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for all
s\in[0,T_{1}]
.

A similar construction using a lift of the geodesic loop
c_{w,2}
and the associated deck transformation
\gamma_{2}
we obtain the existence
of an axis
c_{2}
of
\gamma_{2}
of length
\hat{T}_{2}
satisfying

(6.3)

0\leq T_{2}-\hat{T}_{2}\leq\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon,

and such that

(6.4)

d(c_{2}(s),\tilde{c}_{w,2}(s))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

for all
s\in[0,T_{2}]
.

Projecting the two axis

and
c_{2}
onto

and
c_{u_{2}}=\pr\circ c_{2}(s)

of period
\hat{T}_{1}
and
\hat{T}_{2}
, respectively, whose periods satisfy (
6.1
) and (
6.3
),
and such that

d(c_{u_{1}}(s),c_{w}(s))\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon,\qquad s\in[0,T_{1}],

and

Moreover, using these estimates and the triangle inequality we obtain

\displaystyle d(c_{u_{1}}(\hat{T}_{1}),c_{u_{2}}(0))

\displaystyle\leq d(c_{u_{1}}(\hat{T}_{1}),c_{w,1}(\hat{T}_{1}))+d(c_{w,1}(\hat{T}_{1}),c_{w,1}(T_{1}))

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+d(c_{w,2}(0),c_{u_{2}}(0))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon+(T_{1}-\hat{T}_{1})+\frac{12}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{24}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon+\frac{8}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon=\frac{32}{\kappa_{1}}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\kappa_{1}}+1\Big)\varepsilon.

which yields the last estimate. ∎

Miura type transformations for integrable lattices in 3D
I. T. Habibullin, A. R. Khakimova, A. U. Sakieva

Abstract
The article studies a class of integrable semidiscrete equations with one continuous and two discrete independent variables. Miura type transformations are obtained that relate the equations of the class. A new integrable chain of this type is found, for which the Lax pair is presented. Integrable in the sense of Darboux reductions of the chain are discussed, for small order reductions complete sets of integrals are constructed. Continuum limits for the chain are discussed. A method for finding particular solutions of chains based on integrable in sense of Darboux reductions is proposed. The effectiveness of the method is illustrated by an example.



Keywords:
integrability, differential-difference lattices, Miura transformation, continuum limit, Lax pair, local conservation laws, explicit solutions.

Nonlinear integrable differential-difference equations with one continuous and two discrete variables are intermediate objects between the class of integrable nonlinear equations of dimension 1 + 2, such as the Toda chain, the Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation, the Davey-Stewartson equation, etc. (see

Such chains can find application in studying the nonlinear dynamics of localized magnetic inhomogeneities in such magnetically ordered substances as ferromagnets, antiferromagnets with weak ferromagnetism, magnetoelastic and magnetoelectric interactions

Equations of the type under consideration have potential applications in the problem of describing dislocations in media with a microstructure, as well as in the nonlinear theory of elastic and nonelastic deformations accompanied with deep reconstruction of an

All known integrable differential-difference equations with one continuous and two discrete independent variables can be reduced to the following form

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=f(u^{j}_{n,x},u^{j+1}_{n},u^{j}_{n},u^{j}_{n+1},u^{j-1}_{n+1}),

(1.1)

where the sought function
u=u^{j}_{n}(x)
depends on the continuous
x
and discrete
n
and
j
. The subscript
x
denotes the derivative
u_{x}=\frac{d}{dx}u

Below we give a list of currently known examples of integrable equations of the form (
1.1
):

1)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}+e^{u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j+1}_{n}}-e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}},

2)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}+e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n+1}}-e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}-e^{u^{j}_{n}-u^{j+1}_{n}}+e^{u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j+1}_{n}},

3)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}\frac{{(u^{j}_{n+1})^{2}}}{{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}u^{j+1}_{n}},

4)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}\frac{u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j+1}_{n}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}},

5)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}\frac{u^{j}_{n+1}(u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j+1}_{n})}{u^{j+1}_{n}(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n})},

6)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}\frac{(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n+1})(u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j+1}_{n})}{(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n})(u^{j}_{n}-u^{j+1}_{n})},

7)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}+e^{{-u^{j+1}_{n}+u^{j}_{n}}+u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j-1}_{n+1}},

8)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}-e^{u^{j+1}_{n}}+e^{u^{j}_{n}}+e^{u^{j}_{n+1}}-e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}},

9)

u^{j}_{n+1,x}=u^{j}_{n,x}+(u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n})(u^{j+1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}+u^{j-1}_{n+1}).

Equation 1) was derived in

1.1

A distinctive feature of nonlinear chains with three independent variables is that they allow reductions in the form of systems of differential-difference equations with complete sets of characteristic integrals. Such systems of equations are called Darboux integrable. As a rule it is possible to find in an explicit form general solution to Darboux integrable systems at least for the case of small orders. This circumstance allows one finding localized particular solutions for the lattices in 3D via the Darboux integrable reductions. In our articles

One can check that the equations from the list above are not related to each other by point changes of variables. However, the equations could be transformed from one to the other using more complex irreversible Miura type transformations, which would be useful in finding explicit solutions to the equations (see, for instance,
[

The results of working with Miura type transformations for the class of lattices (
1.1

Let us briefly discuss on the content of the article. In §2 we suggest an algorithm for searching differential or/and difference substitutions generated by local conservation laws. In §3 we apply the algorithm to equations 1)–9). We have established connections between the equations. In §4 we give the Lax pair for the chain 9) and calculate for it the continuum limits of two types. We also consider examples of the small order Darboux integrable reductions admitted by the chain. For the reduced systems the complete sets of the characteristic integrals are constructed. An explicit particular solution to chain 9) is presented obtained via the reduced equation.

2
Local conservation laws and Miura type transformations.

To find the Miura transformation that converts an equation of a given class (
1.1
) into some other equation of the same class, we use the following scheme. Let’s first represent the equation as a local conservation law of the form:

(D_{n}-1)A=(D_{j}-1)B

(2.1)

or the form

D_{x}A=(D_{j}-1)B,

(2.2)

where
A
and
B
are functions depending on the sought function
u=u^{j}_{n}(x)
, as well as on its derivatives with respect to
x
and shifts with respect to discrete variables
j
and
n
. Here
D_{n}
and
D_{j}
denote operators for shifting a function argument in
n
and
j
, for example,
D_{n}y(n)=y(n+1),\quad D_{j}y(j)=y(j+1)
, and
D_{x}
stands for the operator of the total differentiation with respect to
x
. If the representation (
2.1
) takes place, we define a new function
v=v^{j}_{n}(x)
by introducing a potential, i.e. assuming

(D_{j}-1)v^{j}_{n}=A.

(2.3)

Then substituting instead of the function
A
its representation (
2.3
) into relation (
2.1
) we arrive at the equality

(D_{j}-1)(D_{n}-1)v^{j}_{n}=(D_{j}-1)B,

(2.4)

from which, applying the operator inverse to
(D_{j}-1)
, we find another important relation connecting the variables
u^{j}_{n}
and
v^{j}_{n}
:

(D_{n}-1)v^{j}_{n}=B+C.

(2.5)

Since we do concentrate only on autonomous transformations we assume that
C
is a constant. In the examples considered below, it is usually possible to completely exclude the variable
u^{j}_{n}
from the pair of relations (
2.3
), (
2.5
). In such a case, we arrive at some equation for the new function
v^{j}_{n}
.

The second way to look for the desired substitution uses representation (
2.2
). Here we introduce a new function
{\omega}^{j}_{n}
due to the relation:

D_{x}{\omega}^{j}_{n}=B.

(2.6)

Now the equation yields a relation

D_{x}A=D_{x}\left(D_{j}-1\right){\omega}^{j}_{n}.

(2.7)

Integrating the latter, we find, in addition to (
2.6
), the following constraint that the variable
{\omega}^{j}_{n}
should satisfy,

\left(D_{j}-1\right){\omega}^{j}_{n}=A+\hbox{const}.

(2.8)

To obtain the lattice equation for
{\omega}^{j}_{n}
it is necessary to get rid the variable
u^{j}_{n}
by means of the relations (
2.6
) and (
2.8
).

As one can see from the examples below, this approach very often leads to success. In particular, it is in this way that we managed to find Miura type transformations for each of the equations from the list under consideration.

3
Searching for the transformations

In this section we apply the algorithm discussed above to the lattices 1)-9). It is interesting to note that the set of equations falls into two non-overlapping subclasses. Most of the equations belong to the first subclass, namely 1), 3), 4), 7), 8), 9). Only three equations fall into the second class: 2), 5), 6).

3.1
Connection between equations 1) and 9)

Obviously equation 1) can be rewritten as follows

(D_{n}-1)u^{j}_{n,x}=(D_{j}-1)e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}.

(3.1)

According to the rule above we introduce a new variable
v^{j}_{n}
by setting

u^{j}_{n,x}=v^{j+1}_{n}-v^{j}_{n}=(D_{j}-1)v^{j}_{n},

(3.2)

then formulas (
3.1
), (
3.2
) imply

(D_{j}-1)(D_{n}-1)v^{j}_{n}=(D_{j}-1)e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}

or, the same

(D_{n}-1)v^{j}_{n}=e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}+C.

(3.3)

Let us differentiate (
3.3
) with respect to
x
and replace derivatives
u^{j-1}_{n+1,x}
,
u^{j}_{n,x}
due to (
3.2

v^{j}_{n+1,x}-v^{j}_{n,x}=(v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j+1}_{n}+v^{j}_{n})(v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}-C),

(3.4)

that is reduced to 9) by a point transformation
x=-\bar{x}
,
v^{j}_{n}=u^{j}_{n}-Cn

3.2
Connection between equations 2) and 5)

Now we derive a substitution generated by the equation 5). To this end we first simplify 5) by the change of variables
u^{j}_{n}=e^{v^{j}_{n}}

v^{j}_{n+1,x}=v^{j}_{n,x}\frac{e^{v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j+1}_{n}}-1}{e^{v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}-1}.

Then we represent the obtained equation in the form of the local conservation law:

(D_{n}-1)\ln{v^{j}_{n,x}}=(D_{j}-1)\ln\left(e^{v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}-1\right).

(3.5)

Let us introduce a new dependent variable
{\omega}^{j}_{n}
as

\left(D_{j}-1\right){\omega}^{j}_{n}=\ln v^{j}_{n,x}

(3.6)

or

v^{j}_{n,x}=e^{{\omega}^{j+1}_{n}-{\omega}^{j}_{n}}.

Then from (
3.5
), (
3.6
) we obtain the equation

\left(D_{j}-1\right)\left(D_{n}-1\right){\omega}^{j}_{n}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\left(e^{v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}-1\right),

which implies:

\left(D_{n}-1\right){\omega}^{j}_{n}=\ln\left(e^{v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}-1\right)+C.

(3.7)

One can easily verify that the variable
v^{j}_{n}
is completely eliminated from formulas (
3.6
), (
3.7

3.7

{\omega}^{j}_{n+1,x}-{\omega}^{j}_{n,x}=\frac{e^{v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}\left(v^{j-1}_{n+1,x}-v^{j}_{n,x}\right)}{e^{v^{j+1}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}-1}.

(3.8)

The right hand side of the formula is greatly simplified due to equalities (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and as a result the equation reduces to the form

{\omega}^{j}_{n+1,x}-{\omega}^{j}_{n,x}=\left(1+e^{{\omega}^{j}_{n}-{\omega}^{j}_{n+1}+C}\right)\left(e^{{\omega}^{j}_{n+1}-{\omega}^{j-1}_{n+1}}-e^{{\omega}^{j+1}_{n}-{\omega}^{j}_{n}}\right).

(3.9)

Up to the linear transformation
{\omega}^{j}_{n}=-u^{j}_{n}+Cj
equation (
3.9

3.3
Connection between equations 3) and 1)

Let us turn to equation 3). Taking the logarithm of both sides of the equation we get an identity

\ln u^{j}_{n+1,x}-\ln u^{j}_{n,x}=2\ln u^{j}_{n+1}-\ln u^{j-1}_{n+1}-\ln u^{j+1}_{n},

(3.10)

that implies a local conservation law of the form

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j}_{n}}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}{u^{j}_{n}}.

(3.11)

Having introduced a new variable

h^{j}_{n}
by setting

\left(D_{j}-1\right)h^{j}_{n}=\ln\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j}_{n}},

(3.12)

we deduce from (
3.11
) the equality

\left(D_{j}-1\right)\left(D_{n}-1\right)h^{j}_{n}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}{u^{j}_{n}},

(3.13)

that gives rise to

\left(D_{n}-1\right)h^{j}_{n}=\ln\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}{u^{j}_{n}}+C,

(3.14)

where
C
is assumed to be constant. Now we differentiate (
3.14
) respect to
x
and find

h^{j}_{n+1,x}-h^{j}_{n,x}=\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}-\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j}_{n}},

which is easily simplified due to (
3.12
). As a result we obtain the lattice

h^{j}_{n+1,x}-h^{j}_{n,x}=e^{h^{j}_{n+1}-h^{j-1}_{n+1}}-e^{h^{j+1}_{n}-h^{j}_{n}}.

The latter coincides with the discrete Toda lattice 1) up to the linear change of the independent discrete variables
j=-\bar{j},n=\bar{j}+\bar{n}
.

3.4
Connection between equations 3) and 7)

We note that for equation 3) one can derive one more substitution. To this end we use the equation
(
3.10
) rewritten as

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln{u^{j}_{n,x}}=\left(2D_{n}-D_{n}D^{-1}_{j}-D_{j}\right)\ln u^{j}_{n}.

(3.15)

Assuming that
\ln u^{j}_{n}=\left(D_{n}-1\right)v^{j}_{n}
, we obtain

u^{j}_{n}=e^{v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}.

(3.16)

Then combining (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) we arrive at

u^{j}_{n,x}=e^{2v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j+1}_{n}}

(3.17)

and by differentiating (
3.16
) we find a similar relation

u^{j}_{n,x}=\left(v^{j}_{n+1,x}-v^{j}_{n,x}\right)e^{v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}}.

(3.18)

Comparing the last two representations for
u^{j}_{n,x}
one can get the equation

v^{j}_{n+1,x}-v^{j}_{n,x}=e^{-v^{j+1}_{n}+v^{j}_{n}+v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j-1}_{n+1}},

(3.19)

which is nothing but equation 7).

3.5
Connection between equations 4) and 1)

Now we study equation 4) in the list. It can easily be represented as a local concervation law of the form

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln u^{j}_{n,x}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\left(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}\right).

(3.20)

We introduce a new unknown
s^{j}_{n}
by taking

\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln s^{j}_{n}=\ln u^{j}_{n,x}.

(3.21)

Then from (
3.20
) it follows that

\left(D_{j}-1\right)\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln s^{j}_{n}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\left(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}\right).

By applying to the latter equation the operator
\left(D_{j}-1\right)^{-1}
we obtain the second part of the searched substitution:

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln s^{j}_{n}=\ln\left(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}\right)+\ln C,\quad C=\hbox{const}.

(3.22)

Let’s simplify (
3.22
) a little, after which, applying the operator
D_{x}
to both its sides, we obtain a nonlinear lattice

\frac{s^{j}_{n+1,x}}{s^{j}_{n}}-\frac{s^{j}_{n+1}s^{j}_{n,x}}{\left(s^{j}_{n}\right)^{2}}=C\left(\frac{s^{j}_{n+1}}{s^{j-1}_{n+1}}-\frac{s^{j+1}_{n}}{s^{j}_{n}}\right),

that is reduced to the discrete Toda lattice 1) by the point transformation

s^{j}_{n}=e^{-u^{j}_{n}},\quad x\mapsto{Cx}
.

3.6
Connection between equations 6) and 2)

Let us look for the Miura type transformations generated by equation 6). By taking logarithm of both sides of 6) we get a relation

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln u^{j}_{n,x}=\left(D_{n}-D_{j}\right)\ln\left(u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}\right)+\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\left(u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}\right).

Afterwards by using the decomposition
D_{n}-D_{j}=D_{n}-1-\left(D_{j}-1\right)
one can rewrite the relation as a local conservation law of the form:

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}.

(3.23)

Then we introduce a new variable
h^{j}_{n}
by the rule

\left(D_{j}-1\right)\ln h^{j}_{n}=\ln\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}.

(3.24)

Applying the reasoning above to (
3.23
), (
3.24
) one can derive:

\left(D_{n}-1\right)\ln h^{j}_{n}=\ln\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}+\ln C.

(3.25)

Let us rewrite Miura transformation (
3.24
), (
3.25
) in a more convenient form

\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}=\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n}},\qquad C\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}=\frac{h^{j}_{n+1}}{h^{j}_{n}},

(3.26)

which implies that

\frac{1}{C}\frac{u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}=\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n+1}}.

(3.27)

Differentiating (

\frac{h^{j}_{n+1,x}}{h^{j}_{n+1}}-\frac{h^{j}_{n,x}}{h^{j}_{n}}=\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1,x}-u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}-\frac{u^{j-1}_{n,x}-u^{j}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}=\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}-C\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n+1}}-\frac{u^{j-1}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}+\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n}}.

(3.28)

We simplify some of the terms in (
3.28
) due to the lattice 6) and get:

\displaystyle\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}-\frac{u^{j-1}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{j-1}_{n,x}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}\left(\frac{u^{j-2}_{n+1}-u^{j-1}_{n+1}}{u^{j-2}_{n+1}-u^{j-1}_{n}}-1\right)=

\displaystyle=C\frac{h^{j}_{n}}{h^{j-1}_{n+1}}\frac{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j-1}_{n+1}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}=C\frac{h^{j}_{n}}{h^{j-1}_{n+1}}\left(1-\frac{u^{j-1}_{n+1}-u^{j}_{n}}{u^{j-1}_{n}-u^{j}_{n}}\right)=C\frac{h^{j}_{n}}{h^{j-1}_{n+1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{C}\frac{h^{j}_{n+1}}{h^{j}_{n}}\right).

Finally we obtain a lattice

\frac{h^{j}_{n+1,x}}{h^{j}_{n+1}}-\frac{h^{j}_{n,x}}{h^{j}_{n}}=\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n}}-C\frac{h^{j+1}_{n}}{h^{j}_{n+1}}+C\frac{h^{j}_{n}}{h^{j-1}_{n+1}}-\frac{h^{j}_{n+1}}{h^{j-1}_{n+1}},

which is related to the lattice 2) by a point transformation

h^{j}_{n}=C^{n}e^{-p^{j}_{n}}
:

p^{j}_{n+1,x}-p^{j}_{n,x}=e^{p^{j-1}_{n+1}-p^{j}_{n+1}}-e^{p^{j-1}_{n+1}-p^{j}_{n}}-e^{p^{j}_{n}-p^{j+1}_{n}}+e^{p^{j}_{n+1}-p^{j+1}_{n}}.

3.7
Connection between equations 8) and 1)

Let us look for a substitution that would connect lattices 8) and 1). At first we rewrite equation 8) in the form of a local conservation law:

\left(D_{n}-1\right)u^{j}_{n,x}=\left(D_{j}-1\right)\left(e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}-e^{u^{j}_{n}}\right).

From the latter one can derive the second part of the Miura type transformation

\left(D_{n}-1\right)p^{j}_{n,x}=e^{u^{j-1}_{n+1}}-e^{u^{j}_{n}}+C_{1}.

(3.32)

The expression

e^{u^{j}_{n}}=e^{C_{2}}e^{p^{j+1}_{n}-p^{j}_{n}}.

(3.33)

Let us eliminate the variables

from (

4
On a novel integrable chain of the form (1.1)

In this section, we focus on a detailed study of the found chain 9): we represent the Lax pair, calculate the continuum limit, and study Darboux-integrable reductions. For reduced systems of small dimensions, characteristic integrals are found in both directions
n
and
x
. We also briefly discuss on the method of constructing particular solutions of the chain 9) by means of its reduction.

Let us begin with the Lax pair. It is checked straightforwardly that system of linear equations

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\psi}^{j}_{n+1}=\left(v^{j}_{n}-v^{j}_{n+1}\right){\psi}^{j+1}_{n}+{\psi}^{j}_{n},

(4.1)

Now we estimate the difference derivatives with respect to the variables

\displaystyle v^{j-1}_{n+1}-v^{j-1}_{n}

\displaystyle=u(t+\delta^{2},z-\delta,y)-u(t,z-\delta,y)=

4.3
Darboux integrable reductions of the lattice 9)

Chain 9) admits the following cutting off boundary condition

{v^{j}_{n}(x)|}_{j=j_{0}}=C,\quad C=\hbox{const}

(4.9)

preserving the integrability property. Imposing this kind of the conditions on two points
j=0
and
j=N
we obtain a finite field reduction of lattice 9):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &v^{-1}_{n}=C_{0},\\
&v^{j}_{n+1,x}-v^{j}_{n,x}=\left(v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j}_{n}\right)\left(-v^{j+1}_{n}+v^{j}_{n}+v^{j}_{n+1}-v^{j-1}_{n+1}\right),\qquad 0\leq{j}\leq{N-1},\\
&v^{N}_{n}=C_{1},\end{aligned}

admitting the Lax representation of the form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\psi}^{j}_{n+1}=\left(v^{j}_{n}-v^{j}_{n+1}\right){\psi}^{j+1}_{n}+{\psi}^{j}_{n},\qquad{0}\leq{j}\leq{N-1},\\
{\psi^{N}_{n+1}}={\psi}^{N}_{n},\end{cases}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\psi}^{0}_{n,x}=\left(v^{0}_{n}-C_{0}\right){\psi}^{0}_{n},\\
{\psi}^{j}_{n,x}=\left(v^{j}_{n}-v^{j-1}_{n}\right){\psi}^{j}_{n}-{\psi}^{j-1}_{n},\qquad{1}\leq{j}\leq{N}\end{cases}

(4.12)

deduced from (
4.1

{\psi}^{-1}_{n}={\psi}^{N+1}_{n}=0
,
v^{-1}_{n}=C_{0}
,
v^{N}_{n}=C_{1}
.
Let us concentrate on the finite field system (

(4.15)

Example 2

4.4
Explicit solutions to the chain 9)

The presence of complete sets of integrals in both characteristic directions
x
and
n
makes it possible to completely separate the variables. Indeed, according to the definition, each solution
v=(v^{0},v^{1},\dots,v^{N-1})
of system (
4.10
) simultaneously satisfies two systems of equations, namely the system of ordinary differential equations

I_{1}={\varphi}_{1}(x),\quad I_{2}={\varphi}_{2}(x),\quad...,\quad I_{N}={\varphi}_{N}(x)

(4.17)

and the system of ordinary discrete equations

J_{1}={\psi}_{1}(n),\quad J_{2}={\psi}_{2}(n),\quad...,\quad J_{N}={\psi}_{N}(n),

(4.18)

where
{\varphi}_{1}(x),{\varphi}_{2}(x),...,{\varphi}_{N}(x)
are arbitrary functions of
x
and similarly
{\psi}_{1}(n),{\psi}_{2}(n),...,{\psi}_{N}(n)
are arbitrary functions of the variable
n
.

In this case common solution
\left\{v^{j}_{n}(x)\right\}^{N-1}_{j=0}
of the systems provides a solution to the system (
4.10
). We remark that solution of the reduced system (
4.10
) obviously can be prolonged to a particular solution of the original lattice 9). However for arbitrary
N
system (
4.17
) is rather difficult to solve. Here we discuss only a comparatively simple case
N=1
.
Let us derive a formula describing general solution to the lattice (
4.13
). Due to the formula
DI=I
one can conclude that
I={\varphi}(x)
i.e. function
v_{n}(x)
is a solution to the equation

v_{n,x}-\left(v_{n}-C_{0}\right)^{2}-{\varepsilon}\left(v_{n}-C_{1}\right)={\varphi}(x),\quad\varepsilon=C_{0}-C_{1}.

(4.19)

Since
\varphi(x)
is arbitrary we can assume that
\varphi(x)
is represented as

\varphi(x)=z_{x}-(z-C_{0})^{2}-\varepsilon(z-C_{1})

for some function
z=z(x)
that now is considered as a new functional parameters. Let us change the variables in (
4.19
) by taking
v_{n}=z+s
and get a new equation with unknown
s
:

s_{x}-s^{2}-s\left(\varepsilon+2\left(z-C_{0}\right)\right)=0,

(4.20)

which has a form of the Bernoulli equation. Let us replace
s=\frac{1}{g}
and arrive at a linear equation

g_{x}=-g\left(\varepsilon+2\left(z-C_{0}\right)\right)-1.

(4.21)

We first solve the corresponding homogeneous equation

g_{x}=-g\left(\varepsilon+2\left(z-C_{0}\right)\right).

We pass from the functional parameter
z=z(x)
to a new parameter
p(x)
in such a way:

p_{x}=\varepsilon+2(z-C_{0}).

Then the homogeneous equation

g_{x}=-gp^{\prime}

is easily integrated
g=Ce^{-p}
. Now supposing
C=C(x)
we return to the equation (
4.21
) and find
C_{x}=-e^{p}
. We change the parameter in the equation again by setting
e^{p}={\omega}_{x}
, where
\omega
is a new parameter. As a result we get
C(x)=-\omega+K(n)
, where
K(n)
is a constant of integration that might depend on
n
. Therefore we have

g=\frac{K(n)-\omega}{{\omega}_{x}}\quad\mbox{so that}\quad s=\frac{{\omega}_{x}}{K(n)-\omega}.

To write down the final form of
v_{n}(x)
we need in expression of
z
in terms of
\omega
. To this end we exclude parameter
p(x)
from the relations

z=C_{0}+\frac{1}{2}(p_{x}-\varepsilon),\quad e^{p}={\omega}_{x}

and find

z=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\omega}_{xx}}{{\omega}_{x}}+\frac{C_{0}+C_{1}}{2}.

Now we substitute the found expressions into
v_{n}=s+z
and obtain general solution to the lattice (
4.13
):

v_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\omega}_{xx}}{{\omega}_{x}}+\frac{{\omega}_{x}}{K(n)-\omega}+\frac{C_{0}+C_{1}}{2},

(4.22)

where
\omega=\omega(x)
is an arbitrary function of
x
,
K(n)
is an arbitrary function of
n
.
Due to the fact that the termination conditions
v^{-1}_{n}=C_{0}
,
v^{1}_{n}=C_{1}
are fully consistent with dynamics, by virtue of the original three-dimensional chain 9) the solution of the reduced system (
4.13
) extends in an obvious way to the solution of the chain 9):

\displaystyle v^{j}_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}C_{0}&\mbox{for}\quad j\leq-1,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{{\omega}_{xx}}{2{\omega}_{x}}+\frac{{\omega}_{x}}{K(n)-\omega}+\frac{C_{0}-C_{1}}{2}}&\mbox{for}\quad j=0,\\
C_{1}&\mbox{for}\quad j\geq 1.\end{cases}

Conclusions

In the article integrable chains with three independent variables
x
,
n
,
j
are considered. A list of currently known examples is given. Differential and difference substitutions linking equations of the list with each other are presented. A new integrable chain is found that is investigated in more detail. For this model the Lax pair is presented, the continuum limits are computed. Degenerate cutoff condition is pointed out which reduces the chain to a Darboux integrable system of differential-difference equations when it is imposed on two different points
(x,n,j=0)
and
(x,n,j=N)
. For the reductions of small orders characteristic integrals are found. A method for constructing explicit particular solutions to the chain via reductions is proposed.

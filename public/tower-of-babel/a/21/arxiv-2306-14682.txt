A new variant of the Erdős-Gyárfás problem on K 5 K_{5}
Gennian Ge, Zixiang Xu, Yixuan Zhang

Abstract
Motivated by an extremal problem on graph-codes that links coding theory and graph theory, Alon recently proposed a question aiming to find the smallest number t t such that there is an edge coloring of K n K_{n} by t t colors with no copy of given graph H H in which every color appears an even number of times. When H = K 4 H=K_{4} , the question of whether n o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{o(1)} colors are enough, was initially emphasized by Alon. Through modifications to the coloring functions originally designed by Mubayi, and Conlon, Fox, Lee and Sudakov, the question of K 4 K_{4} has already been addressed. Expanding on this line of inquiry, we further study this new variant of the generalized Ramsey problem and provide a conclusively affirmative answer to Alon’s question concerning K 5 K_{5} .

Introduction

The symmetric difference is a fundamental operation in set theory and combinatorics. It is defined as the set of elements that are present in either of the two given sets but not in their intersection. Kleitman
[
23
]
established a celebrated result in extremal combinatorics, which determines the maximum cardinality of any subset
A\subseteq[n]
such that the size of the symmetric difference, determined by the elements in
A
, is bounded by a positive integer. There have been many interesting variants and applications around Kleitman’s Theorem in recent years
[
1
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
25
,
28
]
.

Recently, a graph-theoretic version of the restricted symmetric difference problem has also received widespread attention
[
2
,
4
]
. Formally, Alon
[
2
]
defined a notion of symmetric difference on graphs and introduced a concept called
graph codes
. More precisely, the symmetric difference of two graphs
G_{1}=(V,E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V,E_{2})
on the same vertex set
V
is the graph
(V,E_{1}\oplus E_{2})
where
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
is the symmetric difference defined to be the set of all edges that belong to exactly one of the two graphs. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a family of graphs on the vertex set
[n]
which is closed under isomorphism. We say that a collection
\mathcal{F}
of graphs on
[n]
is an
\mathcal{H}
-code
if
it contains no two members whose symmetric difference is a graph in
\mathcal{H}
. Then a natural question is studying the maximum possible cardinality of an
\mathcal{H}
-code, denoted by
D_{\mathcal{H}}(n)
. Let
d_{\mathcal{H}}(n):=\frac{D_{\mathcal{H}}(n)}{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}
be the maximum possible fraction of the total number of graphs on
[n]
in an
\mathcal{H}
-code. If
\mathcal{H}
only contains a single graph
H
, we also write them as
D_{H}(n)
and
d_{H}(n)
for simplicity. As the symmetric differences determined by a family of graphs on
[n]
are evidently much more complicated than that determined by a collection of subsets of
[n]
, it appears to be impossible to expect the establishment of concise and elegant conclusions analogous to Kleitman’s Theorem. Therefore, an initial goal is to study the asymptotic behaviors of the functions
d_{\mathcal{H}}(n)
for various families
\mathcal{H}
of graphs.

Question 1.1
.

For a collection of graphs
\mathcal{H}
, determine the order of growth of
d_{\mathcal{H}}(n)
when
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Regarding this question, there have already been some interesting results in the past. For instance, a breakthrough of Ellis, Filmus and Friedgut
[
14
]
indicates that if
\mathcal{H}
consists of all graphs with independence number at most
2
, then
d_{\mathcal{H}}(n)=\frac{1}{8}
for all
n\geq 3
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{H}
is the family of all graphs with independence number at most
3
, Berger and Zhao
[
6
]
recently proved that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(n)=\frac{1}{64}
for all
n\geq 4
. Alon, Gujgiczer, Körner, Milojević and Simonyi
[
4
]
also studied this function for families of graphs with special propeties, including the families of all disconnected graphs, all graphs that are not 2-connected, all non-Hamiltonian graphs and all graphs that contain or do not contain a spanning star.

Very recently, Alon
[
2
]
conducted a comprehensive investigation of this extremal problem, focusing on various graphs, including cliques, stars
K_{1,t}
, and matchings
M_{1,t}
consisting of
t
edges. Several results obtained were asymptotically tight, namely,
d_{K_{1,2k}}(n)=\Theta_{k}(\frac{1}{n^{k}})
and
d_{M_{1,2k}}(n)=\Theta_{k}(\frac{1}{n^{k}})
. Notably, the study of cliques poses a particular interest as it is closely associated with another problem originally proposed by Gowers
[
20
]
in 2009.

Conjecture 1

(Gowers
[
20
]
)
.

For every
\delta>0
, there exists

such that if
\mathcal{A}
is any collection of at least
\delta\cdot 2^{\binom{n}{2}}
graphs with vertex set

, then
\mathcal{A}
contains two distinct graphs

is a clique.

Let

denote the family of all cliques on at most

[

]
also proved that

. While for a single clique

,

[

]
then built a connection between this problem and a new coloring problem, which can be viewed as a variant of the Erdős-Gyárfás problem.
The Erdős-Gyárfás problem itself is a very important generalized Ramsey problem, which asks the minimum number of colors

[

[

. Mubayi
[
26

developed an elegant subset ranking coloring and successfully showed

. Later, the subset ranking coloring became a milestone work in the study of this problem, suitable modifications provide a lot of nice lower bounds on

for different parameters

and
q

[
7
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
27
]
. In particular, Conlon, Fox, Lee and Sudakov
[

]
completely proved that

for any integer

, which yields that

is the smallest value of

for which

is polynomial in

, together with
f(n,p,p)=\Omega(n^{\frac{1}{p-2}})
proven in
[
15
]
.

Back to the problem on
\mathcal{H}
-codes, Alon
[
2
]
originally emphasized that the problem is particularly interesting when

because he found that, if one can show the existence of an edge coloring of

by

. It was pointed out by Hunter and Mubayi that the existence of such coloring function has already been shown by Cameron and Heath

in which every copy of a given graph

Question 1.2
.

For a given graph
H
with

being even, determine the order of growth of

In this paper, we further explore Question

and show the following upper bound regarding

when

.

Theorem 1.3
.

f(n,K_{5})\leq e^{O((\log{n})^{2/3}\log\log{n})}
.

By a simple application of the celebrated Lovász Local Lemma
[
5
,
16
]
, we can show the following general upper bound.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any positive integer
p\geq 4
with
p\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
, we have

f(n,K_{p})=O(n^{\frac{4(p-2)}{p(p-1)}}).

Note that the exponent
\frac{4(p-2)}{p(p-1)}
tends to

as

becomes larger, thus we believe that the following conjecture holds.

Conjecture 2
.

For any positive integer
p\geq 4
with
p\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
,
f(n,K_{p})=n^{o(1)}
.

As for lower bounds on

with positive integer

, if for any

-coloring of
K_{n}
, there is a monochromatic copy of
K_{p}
, then we can find a copy of colored
K_{p}
in which the single color appears an even number of times. By the best known results on the multi-color Ramsey numbers on cliques

[
29
]
(also see

]
), we have that
f(n,K_{p})\geq\Omega_{p}(\frac{\log{n}}{\log{\log{n}}})
.

Indeed, we have a better lower bound

. To see this, we take advantage of a result of Fox and Sudakov
[

, who showed that if

in which every color appears an even number of times if

. A slightly weaker lower bound
f(n,K_{5})\geq\Omega(\frac{\log{n}}{\log{\log{\log{n}}}})
could be obtained from the main result in
[
24
]
.

Note added.
Heath and Zerbib
[

also independently proved that

. Moreover, they noted that

Lemma 2.1

(Lovász Local Lemma)

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{k}
be a sequence of events such that each event occurs with probability at most
p

ep(D+1)<1

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
t=cn^{\frac{4(p-2)}{p(p-1)}}
with a sufficiently large constant

. Consider the random
t

K_{n}
, where each edge receives the color

independently. Say that a copy of
K_{p}
is

K_{p}

\mathbb{P}[K_{p}\ \textup{is\ bad}]\leq\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq\binom{p}{2}/2\atop\binom{p}{2}/i\ \textup{is\ even }}t^{i}\cdot\bigg(\frac{i}{t}\bigg)^{\binom{p}{2}}\leq O(t^{-\frac{p(p-1)}{4}}).

Note that every copy of

is independent of all but at most

many other

3.1
Description of the coloring functions

We will employ a simpler and slightly different version of the edge-coloring function

Let

can be presented as a binary vector of length

\bm{v}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{\alpha}
and

, we can partition this vector into

blocks and write

as

\bm{v}=\big(\bm{v}_{1}^{(d)},\bm{v}_{2}^{(d)},\ldots,\bm{v}_{a_{d}}^{(d)}\big),

where
\bm{v}_{i}^{(d)}\in\{0,1\}^{r_{d}}

. In particular when

implies that

and
\bm{v}_{i}^{(0)}\in\{0,1\}

\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\alpha})
. Moreover, if
d=3
, then
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{1}^{(3)}
.

Next we define several auxiliary families of functions. Let
\bm{v}:=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\alpha}\}
and
\bm{w}:=\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{\alpha}\}
be two vertices in
\{0,1\}^{\alpha}
. For any
d\in\{0,1,2\}
, we define the functions
\eta_{d}
as

\eta_{d}(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\left\{\begin{matrix}\bigg(i,\left\{\bm{v}_{i}^{(d)},\bm{w}_{i}^{(d)}\right\}\bigg)&\textup{if }&\bm{v}\neq\bm{w}\\
0&\textup{if }&\bm{v}=\bm{w}\\
\end{matrix}\right.
,

where
i
is the minimum index such that
\bm{v}_{i}^{(d)}\neq\bm{w}_{i}^{(d)}
. Furthermore, for
\bm{v},\bm{w}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{\alpha}
and for each
d\in\{0,1,2\}
, define

\xi_{d}(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\bigg(\eta_{d}(\bm{v}_{1}^{(d+1)},\bm{w}_{1}^{(d+1)}),\ldots,\eta_{d}(\bm{v}_{a_{d+1}}^{(d+1)},\bm{w}_{a_{d+1}}^{(d+1)})\bigg).

Then let
c(\bm{v},\bm{w})=(\xi_{2}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),\xi_{1}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),\xi_{0}(\bm{v},\bm{w}))
. In particular, we will repeatedly take advantage of
\xi_{2}(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\eta_{2}(\bm{v},\bm{w})
in the proof of our main result.

We then assume the vectors in
\{0,1\}^{\beta}
are lexicographically ordered. For an ordered pair of vertices
\bm{v}\preceq\bm{w}
and

, define

\delta_{i}(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\left\{\begin{matrix}+1&if&\bm{v}_{i}^{(2)}\prec\bm{w}_{i}^{(2)}\\

\end{matrix}\right.

We further define

\Delta(\bm{v},\bm{w})=\big(\delta_{1}(\bm{v},\bm{w}),\ldots,\delta_{\beta}(\bm{v},\bm{w})\big)

Finally, let

Therefore, we can see the number of colors used in function

. The number of colors used in

\leq e^{O(\beta^{2}\log{\beta})}
, as shown in

5
Concluding remarks

Based on the similarity to the classical Erdős-Gyárfás problem and the recent developments
[
12
,
13
,
26
]
, we are led to speculate that Conjecture
2
holds true. Our investigation has produced supporting evidence, such as Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. However, this new conjecture presents additional challenges, as we not only discuss the number of colors assigned to each
K_{p}
, but also need to consider their distribution carefully. For instance, when
p=8
, the potential distribution of colored
8
-cliques becomes notably intricate, surpassing what can be managed manually (although leveraging appropriate computer assistance could be beneficial). Consequently, we think that solely relying on existing approaches is insufficient for proving Conjecture
2
. However, this new problem does not impose a requirement for each
K_{p}
to receive a large number of colors, opening the possibility of employing an entirely different approach that could prove advantageous.

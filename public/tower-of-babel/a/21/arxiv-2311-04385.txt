Partial fraction expansions and zeros of Hankel transforms
Yong-Kum Cho, Seok-Young Chung, Young Woong Park

Abstract




Abstract.
It is proved by the method of partial fraction expansion
and Sturm’s oscillation theory that the zeros of certain Hankel transforms are
all real, simple and distributed one by one between consecutive zeros of Bessel functions.
As an application, we obtain a list of sufficient conditions as well as necessary conditions on parameters
for which
{}_{1}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions belong to the Laguerre-Pólya class.

Keywords.
Bessel functions, Fourier transforms, Hankel transforms,

Laguerre-Pólya class, partial fraction expansions, transference principle.

2020 MSC.
30D10, 33C10, 34C10, 44A15.

1
Introduction

This paper deals with partial fraction expansions for the ratios of Hankel transforms to Bessel functions
and its applications to the theory of zeros of Hankel transforms, which extend those analogous results of
Hurwitz and Pólya
[
24
]
concerning Fourier cosine and sine transforms.

The Hankel transform under consideration is defined by

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)J_{\nu}(zt)\sqrt{zt}\,dt

(1.1)

for
\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},
where
J_{\nu}(z)
stands for the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu

and
f(t)
is a real-valued function supported in the unit interval.
It will be assumed throughout that
\nu
is real with
\,\nu>-1.

From a complex analysis point of view, it is advantageous to consider
the
normalized Hankel transform
defined by

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\nu+1/2}f(t)\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(zt)\,dt\quad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

(1.2)

where the kernel
\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(z)
stands for the entire function given by

\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}(z/2)^{2m}}{m!\,(\nu+1)_{m}}=\Gamma(\nu+1)(z/2)^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(z).

Due to the readily-verified relation

\,\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,(z/2)^{\nu+1/2}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z),

(1.3)

it is evident that
\,J_{\nu}(z),\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(z)
share zeros in common for
\,z\neq 0\,

and so do Hankel transforms
\,\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z),\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z).
A distinctive aspect is that the normalized Hankel transform

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
, if exists, is a real entire function.

In the special cases
\,\nu=\pm 1/2,
(
1.1
) reduces to

U(z)=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\cos zt\,dt,\quad V(z)=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\sin zt\,dt,

(1.4)

except multiplicative factor
\sqrt{2/\pi\,},
the Fourier cosine and sine transforms, respectively.
1
1

1

For this reason, the Hankel transform (
1.1
) is often referred to as the Fourier-Bessel or generalized Fourier transform.

In a simplified version, it is shown by Hurwitz and Pólya
[
24
]
that
U(z),V(z)
have an infinity of zeros which are all real and simple
when
f(t)
is positive and increasing for
\,0<t<1,
unless it is a step function.
In addition,
U(z),V(z)
have exactly one zero in each of intervals

\big((m-1/2)\pi,\,(m+1/2)\pi\big),\quad\big(m\pi,\,(m+1)\pi\big),\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

separately, and have no zeros elsewhere.

The idea of proof is based on partial fraction expansions

\displaystyle\frac{U(z)}{\,z\cos z\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{U(0)}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}U\left[(m-1/2)\pi\right]}{(m-1/2)\pi}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\,\left[\frac{1}{z-(m-1/2)\pi}+\frac{1}{z+(m-1/2)\pi}\right],

(1.5)

\frac{V(z)}{\,z\sin z\,}=\frac{V^{\prime}(0)}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}V(m\pi)\left(\frac{1}{z-m\pi}+\frac{1}{z+m\pi}\right),

(1.6)

both of which are valid if
f(t)
is integrable. By observing that the Fourier coefficients

\,U\left[(m-1/2)\pi\right],\,V(m\pi)\,
alternate in sign when
f(t)
is positive and increasing,
Hurwitz and Pólya obtained the stated results by inspecting partial sums of (
1
), (
1.6
)
(see section 3 for the detail).

In this paper we aim at investigating the nature and distribution of zeros of Hankel transforms in an analogous manner.
For this purpose, we shall establish partial fraction expansions of the form

\frac{\,\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)\,}{z^{\lambda}J_{\mu}(z)}=\frac{b}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-j_{\mu,m}}+\frac{1}{z+j_{\mu,m}}\right)

in the range
\,-1<\mu<\nu+2,
where
\big(j_{\mu,m}\big)
denotes all positive zero of
J_{\mu}(z)
and

\lambda
is chosen so as to determine

\,b,c_{m}\,
explicitly. By exploiting these expansions, we shall modify the method of Hurwitz and Pólya
to prove that the zeros of
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
are distributed one by one between consecutive zeros of
J_{\mu}(z)
,
provided that
c_{m}
keeps constant sign.

For general
\mu,\nu
in the range
\,-1<\mu<\nu+2,

it is seemingly impossible to determine whether
c_{m}
keeps constant sign.
In the particular case
\,\mu=\nu,
however, we shall give a set of
sufficient conditions on
f(t)
deduced on the basis
of Sturm’s comparison theorems and its modification.

A great deal of special functions encountered in mathematical physics arise as the images of
Hankel transforms of type (
1.1
) or (
1.2
). In this paper we shall focus on

{}_{1}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions of the form

\Phi(z)={}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
b,\,c\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,z^{2}}{4}\right]\quad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

which can be identified as the images of (
1.2
) under certain circumstances.
In application of our results, we shall focus on determining the region of

\,(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\,
for which
\Phi(z)
belongs to the Laguerre-Pólya class, that is, the class of all real entire functions
g(z)

having only real zeros, if any, which are representable in the product form

g(z)=Az^{\ell}e^{-\alpha z^{2}+\beta z}\prod_{m=1}^{\omega}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sigma_{m}}\right)e^{\frac{z}{\,\sigma_{m}\,}},\quad 0\leq\omega\leq\infty,

(1.7)

where
\,A,\alpha,\beta\,
are real with
\,\alpha\geq 0,

\ell
is a nonnegative integer and
\big(\sigma_{k}\big)

is a sequence of non-zero reals with
\,\sum_{m=1}^{\omega}1/\sigma_{m}^{2}<\infty\,
(see
[
6
]
,
[
26
]
).

The problem of identifying the exact set of positive parameters for which
\Phi(z)
belongs to the Laguerre-Pólya class
is a long-standing open problem in the theory of entire functions (see
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
26
]
).

To describe briefly, we shall assign to each fixed
\,a>0\,
an unbounded hyperbolic region
\mathcal{P}_{a}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\,

having the property that
\Phi(z)
has only complex zeros for each
\,(b,c)\in\mathcal{P}_{a}.
In the complement of
\mathcal{P}_{a},

we apply our results on Hankel transforms to specify the range of
(b,c)

for which
\Phi(z)
belongs to the Laguerre-Pólya class (see Figure
7.2
in a particular case). Although there
still remains a large part of the complement of
\mathcal{P}_{a}
left undetermined, our result provides a considerable improvement
of the known parameter patterns for the Laguerre-Pólya class available in the literature.

We organize the present paper as follows.

In section 2, we establish the aforementioned partial fraction expansions
based on Cauchy’s residue theorem. In section 3, we revisit
the method of Hurwitz and Pólya. In sections 4, 5,
we describe the nature and distribution of zeros of Hankel transforms and identify
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
as
a member of the Laguerre-Pólya class under the assumption that
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(j_{\mu,m})
alternates in sign.
In section 6, we modify Sturm’s comparison theorems to give sufficient conditions on
f(t)
for the sign alternation of

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(j_{\nu,m}).
In the last section 7, we consider
{}_{1}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions of the above type
and deal with the problem of identifying parameters for the Laguerre-Pólya class.

2
Partial fraction expansions

It is classical that Bessel function
J_{\nu}(z)
of order
\,\nu>-1\,

has an infinity of zeros which are all real and simple.
In addition, the zeros are symmetrically distributed about the origin and it is standard
to denote all positive zeros by
\left(j_{\nu,m}\right)
arranged in ascending order of magnitude (
[
28
, §15.2-15.22]
).

Our principal result states as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\,\nu>-1,\,-1<\mu<\nu+2\,
and
f(t)
be an integrable function, defined for
\,0<t<1,
subject to the condition

\int_{0}^{1}t^{\nu+1/2}|f(t)|dt<\infty\quad\text{when}\quad\nu<-1/2.

(2.1)

Put
\,\lambda=\nu-\mu+3/2\,
and
\,\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\equiv\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{0,\,\pm j_{\mu,1},\,\pm j_{\mu,2},\,\cdots\right\}
.
If
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
denotes the Hankel transform of
f(t)
defined by (
1.1
), then

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)\,}{\,z^{\lambda}J_{\mu}(z)\,}=\frac{b}{z}-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-j_{\mu,m}}+\frac{1}{z+j_{\mu,m}}\right)\quad(z\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\,\,b\,\,=\frac{\,\Gamma(\mu+1)}{2^{\nu-\mu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\nu+1/2}f(t)dt,}\\
&{a_{m}=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)}{\,j_{\mu,m}^{\lambda}J_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.}\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, the series of (
2.2
) converges absolutely for each
\,z\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\,
and
the convergence is uniform on each compact subset of
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
.

2.1
Inequalities of Bessel functions

As one of the key analytical tools in what follows, we shall establish
the following inequalities for Bessel functions of real order.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\nu
is real with
\,\nu>-1,
then there exists a positive constant
\,c_{\nu}>0,
depending only on
\nu
, such that
for all
\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\},

\displaystyle\left|J_{\nu}(z)\right|\leq c_{\nu}\,\frac{\exp\left(|{\rm{Im}}\,z\right|)}{\sqrt{|z|}}\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\quad 1}&{\big(\nu\geq-1/2\big),}\\
&{1+|z|^{\nu+1/2}}&{\big(\nu<-1/2\big).}\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

A version of Hankel’s asymptotic formula
[
28
, §7.21]
reads

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(z)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}\,}\Bigg\{\cos\Big(z-\frac{\nu\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\left[1+O\left(z^{-2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\sin\Big(z-\frac{\nu\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\left[\frac{4\nu^{2}-1}{8z}+O\left(z^{-3}\right)\right]\Bigg\}

(2.3)

as
\,z\to\infty,
provided that
\,|\arg\,z|<\pi.
By obvious estimates

\left|\cos\Big(z-\frac{\nu\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\right|\leq\exp(|{\rm{Im}}\,z|),\,\,\left|\sin\Big(z-\frac{\nu\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\right|\leq\exp(|{\rm{Im}}\,z|),

the asymptotic formula (
2.1
) implies that we can find
\,r>1\,
so that

|J_{\nu}(z)|\leq\frac{\exp\left(|{\rm{Im}}\,z\right|)}{\sqrt{|z|}}\quad\text{for all $z$ with}\quad|z|>r,

(2.4)

provided that
\,{\rm Re}\,z\geq 0.
For
z
with
\,|z|>r,\,{\rm Re}\,z<0,
if we consider rotating clockwise through the angle

\pi
and recall the relation
\,J_{\nu}(z)=e^{-\nu\pi i}J_{\nu}(e^{\pi i}z),
it is easy to see that the estimate (
2.4
)
continues to be valid. Since the function
\,z\mapsto J_{\nu}(z)\,\sqrt{|z|}\,\exp\left(-|{\rm{Im}}\,z\right|)\,
is continuous
for
\,z\neq 0,
it must be bounded in the annulus
\,\{z:1\leq|z|\leq r\}\,
and hence

|J_{\nu}(z)|\leq d_{\nu}\,\frac{\exp\left(|{\rm{Im}}\,z\right|)}{\sqrt{|z|}}\quad\text{for all $z$ with}\quad|z|\geq 1

(2.5)

for some positive constant
d_{\nu}
which depends only on
\nu
.

Let
\,\nu\geq-1/2.
By applying the known inequality
[
28
, §3.31, (1)]

\left|J_{\nu}(z)\right|\leq\frac{\,|z|^{\nu}\exp(|{\rm{Im}}\,z|)}{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,

and noting the fact that
\,|z|^{\nu+1/2}\leq 1\,
when
\,|z|\leq 1,
we deduce

|J_{\nu}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{\,2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\frac{\exp\left(|{\rm{Im}}\,z\right|)}{\sqrt{|z|}}\quad\text{for all $z$ with}\quad|z|\leq 1.

(2.6)

On combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
), the stated estimate follows with

c_{\nu}=\max\left(d_{\nu},\,\frac{1}{\,2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\right).

In the case
\,-1<\nu<-1/2,
we use
[
28
, §3.31, (2)]
to estimate

\displaystyle\left|J_{\nu}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\,|z|^{\nu}\exp(|{\rm{Im}}\,z|)}{2^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu+1)}\left[1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{4(\nu+1)(\nu+2)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\,(2\nu+3)^{2}\,}{2^{\nu+2}\Gamma(\nu+3)}\frac{\,|z|^{\nu+1/2}\exp(|{\rm{Im}}\,z|)}{\sqrt{|z|}}

(2.7)

for
\,|z|\leq 1\,
and the desired estimate follows on combining (
2.5
), (
2.1
) with

c_{\nu}=\max\left(d_{\nu},\,\frac{\,(2\nu+3)^{2}\,}{2^{\nu+2}\Gamma(\nu+3)}\right).

∎

In dealing with the convergence matter, we shall need uniform lower bounds
for the values of derivatives of Bessel functions at positive zeros.

Lemma 2.2
.

For real
\,\mu>-1,
the following estimates hold true.

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{j_{\mu,m}}\,J_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)\right|\geq\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\,}\quad\text{if $\,|\mu|\leq 1/2,\,\,m=1,2,\cdots,$}}\\
&{\frac{1}{\mu\sqrt{2\pi}}\quad\text{if $\,\mu>1/2,\,\,m=1,2,\cdots,$}}\\
&{\sqrt{\frac{15}{8\pi}\,}\quad\text{if $\,\mu<-1/2,\,\,m=2,3,\cdots.$}}\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

On making use of the known expressions
[
28
, §3.4]

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{-1/2}(z)&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}\,}\cos z,\quad J_{1/2}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}\,}\sin z,\\
J_{3/2}(z)&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}\,}\left(\frac{\sin z}{z}-\cos z\right),\end{split}

(2.8)

the estimates in the cases
\,|\mu|=1/2\,
are trivially verified and hence it suffices to
prove the estimates in the remaining cases.

The function
\,u(x)\equiv\sqrt{x}\,J_{\mu}(x)\,
solves the differential equation

u^{\prime\prime}+\phi(x)u=0,\quad x>0,\quad\text{where $\,\,\phi(x)=1+\frac{1/4-\mu^{2}}{x^{2}}\,.$}

(2.9)

We note that
\phi(x)
decreases to the value
1
when
\,|\mu|<1/2\,
and increases
to the value
1
when
\,|\mu|>1/2\,
with a unique zero at
\,x=\sqrt{\mu^{2}-1/4\,}.

(i) Assuming
\,|\mu|<1/2,
we consider the auxiliary function

g(x)=\phi(x)[u(x)]^{2}+[u^{\prime}(x)]^{2},\quad x>0

for which
\,g^{\prime}(x)=\phi^{\prime}(x)[u(x)]^{2}\,

due to the differential equation (
2.9
).

According to Hankel’s asymptotic formula
[
28
, §7.21]
, we have

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\cos\chi_{\mu}+O\left(x^{-1}\right),

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}J_{\mu}(x)+\sqrt{x}\,J_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\sin\chi_{\mu}+O\left(x^{-1}\right)

as
\,x\to\infty,
where
\,\chi_{\mu}=x-(\mu/2+1/4)\pi.
On combining with the trivial behavior

\,\phi(x)=1+O\left(x^{-2}\right),
we find that

g(x)=\frac{2}{\pi}+O\left(x^{-1}\right)\quad\text{as $\,\,x\to\infty.$}

It is thus found that
g(x)
decreases to the value
\,2/\pi\,
and hence we deduce that

\,g(j_{\mu,1})>g(j_{\mu,2})>\cdots\,\to\,2/\pi.
Since

g(j_{\mu,m})=\big[u^{\prime}(j_{\mu,m})\big]^{2}=j_{\mu,m}\big[J_{\mu+1}(j_{\mu,m})\big]^{2},

we obtain the uniform lower bound

\sqrt{j_{\mu,m}}\,\big|J_{\mu+1}(j_{\mu,m})\big|\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\,},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

(ii) In the case
\,|\mu|>1/2,
we consider the auxiliary function

h(x)=[u(x)]^{2}+\frac{1}{\phi(x)}[u^{\prime}(x)]^{2},\quad x>\sqrt{\mu^{2}-1/4\,}.

As readily calculated with the aid of (
2.9
), we have

h^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{[\phi(x)]^{2}}[u^{\prime}(x)]^{2},\,\,\frac{1}{\phi(x)}=1+O\left(x^{-2}\right)\quad\text{as $\,x\to\infty,$}

and thus, by the same reasoning as above,
h(x)
decreases to the value
2/\pi
.
Evaluating at
\,x=j_{\mu,m},
we deduce the lower bound

\sqrt{j_{\mu,m}}\,\big|J_{\mu+1}(j_{\mu,m})\big|\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}\,}\sqrt{\phi(j_{\mu,m})\,,}

provided that
\,j_{\mu,m}>\sqrt{\mu^{2}-1/4}.

(a)

In view of Lorch’s estimate for the first positive zero (
[
16
]
)

j_{\mu,1}^{2}>(\mu+1)(\mu+5),\quad\mu>-1,

we observe that
\,j_{\mu,1}>\mu\,
when
\,\mu>1/2.
Since
\phi(x)
is increasing and

\,\phi(x)>1/(4\mu^{2})\,
for all
\,x>\mu,
we find that if
\,\mu>1/2,
then

\sqrt{j_{\mu,m}}\,\big|J_{\mu+1}(j_{\mu,m})\big|\geq\frac{1}{\mu\sqrt{2\pi}},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

(b)

In the case
\,\mu<-1/2,
while it is known that
\,j_{\mu,1}\to 0\,
as
\,\mu\to-1,
we have

\,j_{\mu,2}\geq j_{1,1}\,
(see
[
2
]
). Since
\,j_{1,1}>\sqrt{12}\,
according to the above Lorch’s estimate
and
\,\phi(\sqrt{12})>15/16,
we find that

\sqrt{j_{\mu,m}}\,\big|J_{\mu+1}(j_{\mu,m})\big|\geq\sqrt{\frac{15}{8\pi}},\quad m=2,3,\cdots.

On collecting the above case estimates, we complete the proof.
∎

2.2
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Our proof of Theorem
2.1

will be based on Cauchy’s residue theorem and preceding inequalities of Bessel functions.
For the sake of convenience, we shall divide our proof into four different stages.

1.
We fix a point
\,z\in\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\,
and define

\displaystyle\psi(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(w\right)}{\,w^{\lambda}\left(w-z\right)J_{\mu}(w)\,}=\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\Psi(w,t)\,dt,

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad\Psi(w,t)=\frac{J_{\nu}(wt)\sqrt{wt}}{\,w^{\lambda}\left(w-z\right)J_{\mu}(w)\,}.

In view of the limiting behavior

\Psi(w,t)\,\sim\,-\frac{\Gamma(\mu+1)}{\,2^{\nu-\mu}\,\Gamma(\nu+1)\,z}\frac{t^{\nu+1/2}}{w}\quad\text{as}\quad w\to 0

(2.10)

and the fact that the zeros of
J_{\mu}(w)
are all simple, it is
evident that the function
\psi(w)
is meromorphic with simple poles at

\,z,\,0,\,\pm j_{\mu,1},\,\pm j_{\mu,2},\,\cdots.\,

By making use of (
2.10
), it is trivial to calculate the residues

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}\left[\psi\left(w\right);w=z\right]=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(z\right)}{z^{\lambda}J_{\mu}(z)},\quad\mathrm{Res}\left[\psi\left(w\right);w=0\right]=-\frac{b}{z},

where it is assumed with no loss of generality that
\,b\neq 0.

By using the relation

\,J_{\mu}^{\prime}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)=-J_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right),

it is also easy to calculate

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}\left[\psi\left(w\right);w=j_{\mu,m}\right]=\frac{a_{m}}{z-j_{\mu,m}}.

We note that if
j
denotes any positive zero of

J_{\mu}(w)
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{w\to-j}(w+j)\Psi(w,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\,J_{\nu}(-jt)\sqrt{-jt}}{(-j)^{\lambda}(j+z)J_{\mu+1}(-j)\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,e^{(\nu-\lambda-\mu-1/2)\pi i}\,J_{\nu}(jt)\sqrt{jt}\,}{j^{\lambda}(j+z)J_{\mu+1}(j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,J_{\nu}(jt)\sqrt{jt}\,}{j^{\lambda}(j+z)J_{\mu+1}(j)},

(2.11)

where we have used the relation
\,J_{\mu}(-w)=e^{\mu\pi i}J_{\mu}(w)\,
(
[
28
, §3.62]
), whence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Res}\left[\psi\left(w\right);w=-j_{\mu,m}\right]=\frac{a_{m}}{z+j_{\mu,m}}.

For each positive integer
n
, let
R_{n}
be the rectangle
with vertices at
\,\pm X_{n}\pm Yi,
where
\,X_{n}=\left(n+\mu/2+1/4\right)\pi\,

and
\,Y>2|z|.
Due to McMahon’s asymptotic formula
[
19
]
which states

j_{\mu,n}=\left(n+\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\pi+O\big(n^{-1}\big)\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(2.12)

there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that
\,2|z|<j_{\mu,n}<X_{n}<j_{\mu,n+1}\,
when
\,n\geq n_{0}.
By the residue theorem,
hence, if
\,n\geq n_{0},
then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{R_{n}}\psi(w)dw

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)}{z^{\lambda}J_{\mu}(z)}-\frac{b}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}a_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-j_{\mu,m}}+\frac{1}{z+j_{\mu,m}}\right).

(2.13)

2.
We shall now prove that the integrals of
\psi(w)
along the upper and lower sides of
R_{n}

tend to zero as
\,Y\to\infty\,
for each fixed
\,n\geq n_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For real
\,\nu>-1,\,\mu>-1,
there exists a positive constant
A
, depending only on
\mu,\nu
, such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\,J_{\nu}(wt)\sqrt{wt}\,}{J_{\mu}(w)}\right|\leq A\,|w|^{1/2}\cdot\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\quad 1\qquad\,\,\,\big(\nu\geq-1/2\big),}\\
&{t^{\nu+1/2}\,\,\quad\,\big(\nu<-1/2\big)}\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

for all
w
with
\,\left|{\rm{Im}}\,w\right|\geq 1\,
and for all
\,0<t<1.

Proof.

By making use of the inequality

\left|\cos\Big(z-\frac{\mu\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}\exp(|{\rm Im\,}w|),

valid when
\,|{\rm Im\,}w|\geq 1,
if we apply Hankel’s asymptotic formula (
2.1
) in the same manner as before,
then we can find
\,r>1\,
such that

\left|J_{\mu}(w)\right|\geq\frac{1}{6}\frac{\exp(|{\rm Im\,}w|)}{\sqrt{|w|}}\quad\text{if}\,\,\,|w|>r,\,|{\rm Im\,}w|\geq 1.

Since the function

3
Hurwitz-Pólya theorems

This section aims to revisit some of the results obtained by Hurwitz and Pólya
[
24
]
on Fourier transforms
in terms of Hankel transforms for the sake of completeness and subsequent applications.

Concerning Fourier transforms
U(z),V(z)
as defined in (
1.4
),
if we apply the partial fraction expansion formula (
2.1
) with
\,\mu=\pm 1/2,

it is immediate to derive (
1
), (
1.6
) and the additional expansions

\frac{\,U(z)\,}{\,\sin z\,}=\frac{U(0)}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}U(m\pi)\left(\frac{1}{z-m\pi}+\frac{1}{z+m\pi}\right),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{V(z)}{z\cos z}

\displaystyle=V^{\prime}(0)+z\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}V\left[(m-1/2)\pi\right]}{[(m-1/2)\pi]^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left[\frac{1}{z-(m-1/2)\pi}+\frac{1}{z+(m-1/2)\pi}\right].

(3.2)

valid under the assumption that
f(t)
is integrable.

We remark that (
3.1
) coincides with
[
24
, (38)]
but (
3
) is not listed in
[
24
]
. Thus (
2.1
) recovers
all expansions obtained by Hurwitz and Pólya, with the additional formula (
3
),
as special cases. Since the choice of
\mu
is free in the range
\,-1<\mu<\nu+2,

more expansions are also available.

As discovered by Hurwitz and developed formally by Pólya, it turns out that
these partial fraction expansions provide effective means of investigating
the existence and nature of zeros as well as their distributions.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Hurwitz and Pólya
[
24
]
)

Let
G(z)
be an even entire function having zeros only at
\,\pm\alpha_{m},\,0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots.

Suppose that
F(z)
is an even entire function such that
\,\left|F(\alpha_{m})\right|>0\,

for all
m
and

\frac{F(z)}{\,zG(z)\,}=\frac{b}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-\alpha_{m}}+\frac{1}{z+\alpha_{m}}\right)

(3.3)

for all
\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\left\{0,\,\pm\alpha_{1},\,\pm\alpha_{2},\cdots\right\},

where
\,b\in\mathbb{R}\,
and
\,c_{m}>0\,
for each
m
.

(i)

If
\,b>0,
then
F(z)
has an infinity of zeros which are all real and
it has exactly one zero in each of the intervals

\big(0,\,\alpha_{1}\big),\,\,\big(\alpha_{m},\,\alpha_{m+1}\big),\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

and no positive zeros elsewhere.

(ii)

If
\,b<0\,
and
F(z)
is subject to the additional assumption

\,|F(iy)|>0\,
for all
\,y\in\mathbb{R},
then
F(z)
has an infinity of zeros which are all real and
it has exactly one zero in each of the intervals

\big(\alpha_{m},\,\alpha_{m+1}\big),\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

and no positive zeros elsewhere.

This important result is an abstract formulation of what Hurwitz and Pólya observed
(
[
24
, §5]
). To reproduce their ideas of proof in the present setting,
let us consider the
N
th partial sum of (
3.3
)

\displaystyle S_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{N}c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-\alpha_{m}}+\frac{1}{z+\alpha_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{2N}(z)}{\,z\left(z^{2}-\alpha_{1}^{2}\right)\cdots\left(z^{2}-\alpha_{N}^{2}\right)\,},

where
P_{2N}(z)
is an even polynomial of degree
2N
.
As
\,S_{N}(x),\,x>0,
takes every real value in each open interval between
two consecutive poles in a monotonically decreasing manner, it is evident that

S_{N}(z)
has exactly one positive zero in
\,\big(\alpha_{m},\,\alpha_{m+1}\big),\,m=1,2,\cdots,N-1,

whatever
b
is.

If
\,b>0,
then
S_{N}(z)
has exactly one positive zero in

\,\big(0,\,\alpha_{1}\big)\,
by the same reason and thus all of
2N
zeros of
S_{N}(z)

are located in view of symmetry.

In the case
\,b<0,
if we consider the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle S_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\frac{\,(b+2c_{m})z^{2}-b\alpha_{m}^{2}\,}{z^{2}-\alpha_{m}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\,b+2(c_{1}+\cdots+c_{N})\,}{z}\quad\text{as $\,|z|\to\infty,$}

we infer that
S_{N}(z)
has one positive zero in
\,\big(\alpha_{N},\,\infty\big)\,
only when

b+2(c_{1}+\cdots+c_{N})<0.

Otherwise,
S_{N}(z)
has two complex zeros which must be purely imaginary because
P_{2N}(z)
is even.
Consequently,
S_{N}(z)
has either
2N
real zeros or
2N-2
real zeros and two purely imaginary
zeros.

If we transfer the above analyses to the function
\,F(z)/zG(z)
by letting
\,N\to\infty,

since the possibility that the zeros move towards

\alpha_{m}
’s are excluded due to the hypothesis
\,\left|F(\alpha_{m})\right|>0\,

for all
m
, we may conclude that if
\,b>0,
then

F(z)
has only real zeros distributed in the pattern of (i) and if
\,b<0,
then

F(z)
has real zeros distributed in the pattern of (ii) and possibly two purely imaginary zeros.
Since it is assumed that
\,|F(iy)|>0\,
for all
\,y\in\mathbb{R},

F(z)
can not have purely imaginary zeros and the assertion follows.

Remark 3.1
.

Without assuming that

\,\left|F(\alpha_{m})\right|>0\,
for all
m
,
an inspection on the proof indicates that
F(z)
still has
an infinity of zeros but no complex zeros. Since the zeros of
S_{N}(z)
could move towards
the end-points in the limiting process, each
\alpha_{m}
could be a zero of
F(z)

and it is not difficult to infer that
F(z)
has at most two zeros in each interval

\,\left(0,\,\alpha_{1}\right],\,\left(\alpha_{m},\,\alpha_{m+1}\right]\,

in the case (i) and
\,\left[\alpha_{m},\,\alpha_{m+1}\right)\,

in the case (ii), where
\,m=1,2,\cdots.

4
Zeros of Hankel transforms

By applying the preceding Hurwitz-Pólya theorem on the basis of
partial fraction expansion formula (
2.1
), we
are about to obtain information on the zeros of Hankel transforms.
Before proceeding further, let us state a simple consequence of (
2.1
) which will be
used to determine the multiplicity of each zero. As usual, we shall denote
the Wronskian of
f(z),g(z)
by

W[f,g](z)=f(z)g^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(z)g(z).

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle W\big[\mathbb{J}_{\mu},\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\big](z)=8z(\mu+1)\mathbb{J}_{\mu}^{2}(z)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)\,}{\mathbb{J}_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)}\,\frac{1}{\left(z^{2}-j_{\mu,m}^{2}\right)^{2}},

(4.1)

valid for all
\,z\in\mathbb{C}.

If we multiply by
z
and differentiate both sides of (
2.1
),
where termwise differentiation is permissible due to the uniform convergence
of the derived series on every compact subset of
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
, we obtain
the Wronskian formula (
4.1
) on
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
.
Since the singularities at
\pm j_{\mu,m}
are easily seen to be removable,
the formula remains valid for all
\,z\in\mathbb{C}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\,\nu>-1,\,-1<\mu<\nu+2\,
and
f(t)
be a positive integrable function, defined for
\,0<t<1,

subject to the condition (
2.1
). Put

\sigma_{m}=(-1)^{m+1}\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right),\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

If
\left(\sigma_{m}\right)
keeps constant sign for all
m
, then
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)

has only an infinity of real simple zeros whose positive zeros
are distributed as follows.

(i)

If
\,\sigma_{m}>0\,
for all
m
, then each of the intervals

\,\big(j_{\mu,m},\,j_{\mu,m+1}\big),\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

contains exactly one zero with no positive zeros elsewhere.

(ii)

If
\,\sigma_{m}<0\,
for all
m
, then each of the intervals

\,\big(0,\,j_{\mu,1}\big),\,\,\big(j_{\mu,m},\,j_{\mu,m+1}\big),\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

contains exactly one zero with no positive zeros elsewhere.

For the proof, we first note that
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
is entire with

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(iy)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m+\nu+1/2}f(t)dt\right)\frac{(y/2)^{2m}}{\,m!\,(\nu+1)_{m}\,}>0

for all
\,y\in\mathbb{R}\,
and
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
is even.
Owing to the inequalities (
[
28
, §15.22]
)

0<j_{\mu,1}<j_{\mu+1,1}<j_{\mu,2}<j_{\mu+1,2}<\cdots\quad(\mu>-1)

and the positivity
\,\mathbb{J}_{\mu+1}(x)>0\,
for
\,0<x<j_{\mu+1,1},
we have

{\rm{sgn}}\,\Big[\mathbb{J}_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)\Big]=(-1)^{m+1},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

In the case (i), if we notice the inequalities

b\equiv-\int_{0}^{1}t^{\nu+1/2}f(t)dt<0,\quad c_{m}\equiv 2(\mu+1)\frac{\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)}{\,j_{\mu,m}^{2}\mathbb{J}_{\mu+1}\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)\,}>0

for each
m
and rewrite formula (
2.1
) into the form

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(z\right)}{z\mathbb{J}_{\mu}(z)}=\frac{b}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-j_{\mu,m}}+\frac{1}{z+j_{\mu,m}}\right),

then the stated results are immediate consequences of Theorem
3.1
except the simplicity of zeros.
Similarly, the results in the case (ii) can be proved, except the simplicity of zeros, with obvious modifications.

Regarding the simplicity of each zero, we observe that Wronskian formula
(
4.1
) implies the inequalities
\,W\big[\mathbb{J}_{\mu},\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\big](x)>0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
in the case (i) and

\,W\big[\mathbb{J}_{\mu},\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\big](x)<0\,
for all
\,x>0\,
in the case (ii). If

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(\widehat{x})=0=\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)^{\prime}(\widehat{x})\quad\text{for some}\quad\widehat{x}>0,

then the Wronskian of
\mathbb{J}_{\mu},\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)
vanishes at
\widehat{x}
, which leads to a contradiction in any case.
It is thus shown that any positive zero of
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(x)
must be simple and
Theorem
4.1
is now completely proved.∎

5
Laguerre-Pólya class

Our purpose here is to identify the Hankel transform as an
entire function of the Laguerre-Pólya class under the same conditions for sign changes.

1.

For an integrable function
f(t)
satisfying (
2.1
),
it is elementary to find by integrating termwise
that
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
is an even entire function with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)&{\,\,=\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\beta_{m}(f)\,}{\,m!\,(\nu+1)_{m}\,}\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)^{m},\quad\text{where}}\\
\beta_{m}(f)\,\,&{\,\,=\,\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m+\nu+1/2}f(t)dt,\quad m=0,1,2,\cdots.}\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

Concerning the (growth) order of
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
, let

b_{m}=\frac{(-1)^{m}\beta_{m}(f)}{4^{m}m!(\nu+1)_{m}},\quad m=0,1,\cdots,

the coefficient of
z^{2m}
. By using generalized Stirling’s formula
[
23
, 5.11.7]

\,\Gamma(m+c)\,\sim\,\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{-m}m^{m+c-1/2}\,
as
\,m\to\infty,

where
c
is any real number, and
\,(\nu+1)_{m}=\Gamma(m+\nu+1)/\Gamma(\nu+1),

it is easy to see that

\log(1/|b_{m}|)\,\sim\,2m\log m\quad\text{as $\,m\to\infty\,$}

and consequently
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
has the order

\limsup\frac{2m\log 2m}{\,\log(1/|b_{m}|)\,\,}=1

(see
[
17
]
for the definition and
[
1
]
for analogous arguments).

2.
Suppose now that
f(t)
satisfies the additional assumptions of Theorem
4.1
so that

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
becomes a real entire function having only
an infinity of real simple zeros. Let

\big(\zeta_{m}\big)
be the sequence of all positive zeros arranged in ascending order
of magnitude. Since the positive zeros of
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z),J_{\mu}(z)
are shown to be interlaced and

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j_{\mu,m}}=+\infty,\quad\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j_{\mu,m}^{2}}=\frac{1}{4(\mu+1)},

we find that

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\zeta_{m}}=+\infty\quad\text{but}\quad\quad\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\zeta_{m}^{2}}<\infty.

In view of Hadamard’s factorization theorem (
[
17
, §4.2]
), it is thus shown that the Hankel transform
\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)
belongs to the
Laguerre-Pólya class, to be denoted by
\mathcal{LP}
hereafter, and has genus one.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\,\nu>-1,\,-1<\mu<\nu+2.
If
f(t)
is a positive integrable function satisfying (
2.1
)
such that the sequence
\,\big\{\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)\big\}\,
alternates in sign for
\,m=1,2,\cdots,
then

\,\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\in\mathcal{LP}\,
with

\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)=\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(0)\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\zeta_{m}^{2}}\right).

(5.2)

While the theorem is of considerable interest in the theory of entire functions,
we present an application of (
5.2
) after
Euler and Rayleigh
[
28
, §15.5, 15.51]
. By differentiating
logarithmically and expanding each term in geometric series, it is immediate to deduce the relation

-\frac{d}{dz}\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(\sqrt{z})=\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(\sqrt{z})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}z^{k},\quad\Delta_{k}:=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\,\zeta_{m}^{2k+2}\,},

valid for all
\,|z|<\zeta_{1}.
If we expand both sides into power series,
with the aid of (
5.1
) and the Cauchy product formula, and equate the coefficients, we can
compute
(\Delta_{k})
explicitly in an inductive manner.
For example,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\,\zeta_{m}^{2}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{1}(f)}{\,4(\nu+1)\beta_{0}(f)\,},

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\,\zeta_{m}^{4}\,}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,(\nu+2)[\beta_{1}(f)]^{2}-(\nu+1)\beta_{0}(f)\beta_{2}(f)\,}{\,16(\nu+1)^{2}(\nu+2)\left[\beta_{0}(f)\right]^{2}\,}.

(5.4)

We refer to
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
25
]
and further references therein for the related sums of zeros
of Bessel functions and various applications.

3.
It is well known that each entire function of the Laguerre-Pólya class
\mathcal{LP}

arises as the uniform limit of real polynomials having only real zeros. By Rolle’s theorem,
hence, it is simple to prove that
\mathcal{LP}
is closed under differentiation.
By exploiting the method of partial fractions, we shall now prove that certain subclass of
\mathcal{LP}

is closed under more general differential operators, which will be useful in later applications.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G(z)
be an even entire function of the form

G(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,(-1)^{m}\gamma_{m}\,}{m!}\,z^{2m}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

(5.5)

where
(\gamma_{m})
is a sequence of non-zero reals subject to the condition

\gamma_{m}=O\left(\frac{1}{m!}\right)\quad\text{as $\,m\to\infty.$}

(5.6)

If
\,G\in\mathcal{LP},
then
\,G_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{LP}\,
for each
\,\alpha\geq 0,
where

G_{\alpha}
is defined by

G_{\alpha}(z)=\left(z\frac{d}{dz}+\alpha\right)G(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

Since the case
\,\alpha=0\,
is known, we shall only prove the
case
\,\alpha>0\,
although the proof for the case
\,\alpha=0\,
is not much different.

The hypothesis (
5.6
) implies that
G(z)
has order not exceeding one.
Let
\big(\zeta_{m}\big)
denote the sequence of all distinct positive zeros of
G(z)
,
arranged in ascending order of magnitude,
and
\ell_{m}
the multiplicity of
\zeta_{m}
. Since
G(z)
does not vanish at the origin, the order
restriction implies

G(z)=\gamma_{0}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\zeta_{m}^{2}}\right)^{\ell_{m}},\quad\text{where}\quad\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell_{m}}{\,\zeta_{m}^{2}\,}<\infty.

(5.7)

As readily verified,
G_{\alpha}(z)
is an even entire function with

G_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,(-1)^{m}\gamma_{m}\,(2m+\alpha)\,}{m!}\,z^{2m}.

We note that
G_{\alpha}(z)
also has order not exceeding one because the term
\,\log|2m+\alpha|\,

does not give any contribution in calculating order.
Concerning the zeros of
G_{\alpha}(z)
, we first observe that
\,G_{\alpha}(\zeta_{m})=0\,

only when
\,\ell_{m}\geq 2.
Since the
j
th derivative of
G_{\alpha}(z)
is given by

G_{\alpha}^{(j)}(z)=zG^{(j+1)}(z)+(\alpha+j)G^{(j)}(z),\quad j=1,2,\cdots,

it is evident that
\zeta_{m}
has multiplicity
\ell_{m}-1
in such a case.

By taking the logarithmic derivative of
z^{\alpha}G(z)

with the representation of (
5.7
) and simplifying, we obtain

\frac{\,G_{\alpha}(z)\,}{zG(z)}=\frac{\alpha}{z}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\ell_{m}\left(\frac{1}{z-\zeta_{m}}+\frac{1}{z+\zeta_{m}}\right).

(5.8)

Since the coefficients are all positive, according to Remark
3.1
after
the proof of Theorem
3.1
, this partial fraction expansion indicates that

G_{\alpha}(z)
has only an infinity of real zeros and each of the intervals

\big(0,\,\zeta_{1}\big],\,\,\big(\zeta_{m},\,\zeta_{m+1}\big],\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

contains at most two zeros. In addition, (
5.8
) gives

W\big[G_{\alpha},G\big](x)=4x[G(x)]^{2}\,\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell_{m}\zeta_{m}^{2}}{\,(x^{2}-\zeta_{m}^{2})^{2}\,}>0

for all
\,x>0,\,x\neq\zeta_{m},\,m=1,2,\cdots,
and thus
each positive zero of
G_{\alpha}(z)
is simple if it does not
coincides with some
\zeta_{m}
.

Let
\big(\sigma_{m}\big)
denote the
sequence of all positive zeros of
G_{\alpha}(z)
, repeated
up to multiplicities and arranged in the manner

\,0<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma_{2}\leq\cdots.

Due to its nature explained as above, it is evident that

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\,\sigma_{m}^{2}\,}\leq\frac{1}{\,\sigma_{1}^{2}}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell_{m}}{\,\zeta_{m}^{2}\,}<\infty.

(5.9)

By applying the Hadamard theorem, we conclude that
\,G_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{LP}\,
with

G_{\alpha}(z)=\alpha\gamma_{0}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z^{2}}{\sigma_{m}^{2}}\right).

∎

Remark 5.1
.

In dealing with even entire functions of
\mathcal{LP}
,
it is customary and often advantageous to consider the subclass
\,\mathcal{LP}^{+}\subset\mathcal{LP},

which consists of all real entire functions
g(z)
representable in the form

g(z)=Az^{\ell}e^{\beta z}\prod_{m=1}^{\omega}\left(1+\frac{z}{\tau_{m}}\right),\quad 0\leq\omega\leq\infty,

(5.10)

where
\,\beta\geq 0,\,A\in\mathbb{R},

\ell
is a nonnegative integer, and
\big(\tau_{m}\big)

is a sequence of positive reals satisfying
\,\sum_{m=1}^{\omega}1/\tau_{m}<\infty.

As readily observed,
\,g\in\mathcal{LP}^{+}\,
if and only if

\,G\in\mathcal{LP},
where
G
is an even extension of
g
defined by

\,G(z)=g(-\delta z^{2})\,
for some
\,\delta>0.
As a consequence,
Theorem
5.2
may be rephrased as follows: If
\,g\in\mathcal{LP}^{+}\,
and takes the form

g(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\,\gamma_{m}\,}{m!}\,z^{m}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

(5.11)

where
(\gamma_{m})
is a sequence of non-zero reals satisfying (
5.6
),
then

\left(z\frac{d}{dz}+\alpha\right)g\in\mathcal{LP}^{+}\quad\text{for all}\quad\alpha\geq 0.

Corollary 5.1
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, we have

\left(z\frac{d}{dz}+\alpha\right)\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\in\mathcal{LP}\quad\text{for all}\quad\alpha\geq 0.

6
Sturm’s method for the sign pattern

This section focuses on investigating whether the sequence

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\mu,m}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\nu+1/2}f(t)\mathbb{J}_{\nu}\left(j_{\mu,m}t\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j_{\mu,m}^{\nu+3/2}}\int_{0}^{j_{\mu,m}}f\left(\frac{t}{j_{\mu,m}}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt

(6.1)

alternates in sign in the particular case
\mu=\nu
. Our investigation will be based on
a version of Sturm’s comparison theorems which states as follows.
(It is practically due to Sturm
[
27
]
but we refer to
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
28
]
for the
present version and further backgrounds with applications.)

For
\,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in C([a,b]),
let
u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)
be
C^{1}
solutions of

u_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\phi_{1}(t)u_{1}=0,\quad u_{2}^{\prime\prime}+\phi_{2}(t)u_{2}=0\quad(a<t<b),

respectively, such that
\,u_{1}(a)=u_{2}(a)=0\,
and
\,u_{1}^{\prime}(a+)=u_{2}^{\prime}(a+)>0.

If
\,\phi_{2}(t)>\phi_{1}(t)\,
for all
\,a<t<b,
then
\,u_{1}(t)>u_{2}(t)\,
for all
t
between

a
and the first zero of
u_{2}(t)
, and hence the first zero of
u_{2}(t)
on
(a,b]
is on the
left of the first zero of
u_{1}(t)
.

In what follows we shall set
\,j_{\nu,m}\equiv j_{m}\,
for each
m
to simplify notation.

6.1
The case
\,|\nu|<1/2

As readily verified, the function
\,u(t)=t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t),\,t>0,
satisfies the same differential equation
as defined in (
2.9
), that is,

u^{\prime\prime}+\phi(t)u=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\phi(t)=1+\frac{(1/4-\nu^{2})}{t^{2}}.

In addition, we note that
u(t)
is strictly positive on
\,\big(0,j_{1}\big),\,\big(j_{2k},\,j_{2k+1}\big)\,

and strictly negative on
\,\big(j_{2k-1},\,j_{2k}\big)\,
for each
\,k=1,2,\cdots.

For a fixed
k
, if we consider the functions
u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)
defined by

u_{1}(t)=u\left(j_{2k}+t\right),\quad u_{2}(t)=-u\left(j_{2k}-t\right),

it is readily verified that
u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)
satisfy

\displaystyle u_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\phi\left(j_{2k}+t\right)

\displaystyle u_{1}=0,\quad u_{2}^{\prime\prime}+\phi\left(j_{2k}-t\right)u_{2}=0,

\displaystyle u_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=u_{2}(0)=0,

\displaystyle u_{1}^{\prime}(0)=u_{2}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{j_{2k}^{\nu+3/2}}{2(\nu+1)}\mathbb{J}_{\nu+1}\left(j_{2k}\right)>0.

Since
\phi(t)
is strictly decreasing for
\,t>0\,
when
\,|\nu|<1/2,

it follows from the aforementioned Sturm’s theorem that

\,j_{2k+1}-j_{2k}>j_{2k}-j_{2k-1}\,
and
\,u_{1}(t)>u_{2}(t)\,
for
\,0<t<j_{2k}-j_{2k-1},

which implies in turn that

g\left(j_{2k}+t\right)u_{1}(t)>g\left(j_{2k}-t\right)u_{2}(t),\quad 0<t<j_{2k}-j_{2k-1},

for any positive function
g(t)
increasing on the interval
\,\big[j_{2k-1},\,j_{2k+1}\big].

As a consequence, we find by integrating that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{j_{2k+1}-j_{2k}}g(j_{2k}+t)u_{1}(t)dt

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{j_{2k}-j_{2k-1}}+\int_{j_{2k}-j_{2k-1}}^{j_{2k+1}-j_{2k}}\right)(\cdots)\,dt

\displaystyle>\int_{0}^{j_{2k}-j_{2k-1}}g(j_{2k}-t)u_{2}(t)dt.

On changing variables appropriately, the last inequality leads to

\int_{j_{2k}}^{j_{2k+1}}g(t)u(t)dt>-\int_{j_{2k-1}}^{j_{2k}}g(t)u(t)dt,

which implies by the additivity of integrals the inequality

\int_{j_{2k-1}}^{j_{2k+1}}g(t)u(t)dt>0.

(6.2)

In the same manner, if we replace
u_{1}(t),u_{2}(t)
by the functions

\widetilde{u_{1}}(t)=-u\left(j_{2k+1}+t\right),\quad\widetilde{u_{2}}(t)=u\left(j_{2k+1}-t\right)

and proceeds as above, it is not difficult to deduce the inequality

\int_{j_{2k}}^{j_{2k+2}}g(t)u(t)dt<0.

(6.3)

By setting
\,j_{0}=0,
this inequality continues to be valid for
\,k=0.

Let
f(t)
be positive, increasing and integrable for
\,0<t<1.

By applying (
6.2
) with
\,g(t)=f(t/j_{2m+1}),
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{j_{2m+1}}f\left(\frac{t}{j_{2m+1}}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt=\left(\int_{0}^{j_{1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{j_{2k-1}}^{j_{2k+1}}\right)(\cdots)\,dt>0.

Similarly, by applying (
6.3
) with
\,g(t)=f(t/j_{2m}),
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{j_{2m}}f\left(\frac{t}{j_{2m}}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{j_{2k-2}}^{j_{2k}}(\cdots)\,dt<0.

In view of (
6
), hence, we have proved the following sign pattern.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\,|\nu|<1/2.
If
f(t)
is positive, increasing and integrable for
\,0<t<1,
then

{\rm{sgn}}\,\big[\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\nu,m}\right)\big]=(-1)^{m+1},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

Remark 6.1
.

When
\,|\nu|=1/2
, it is shown by Pólya
[
24
]
that if
f(t)
is positive, increasing and integrable, then
the same sign pattern holds true unless
f
belongs to the so-called exceptional case,
the class of all step functions on
[0,1]
having finitely many jump discontinuities
at rational points.

6.2
The case
\,|\nu|>1/2

We shall modify Makai
[
21
]
by considering the function

w(t)=t^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\nu}{|\nu|}\right)}\,\mathbb{J}_{\nu}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2|\nu|}}\right)\qquad(t>0),

which is easily seen to be a solution of the differential equation

w^{\prime\prime}+\varphi(t)w=0,\quad\text{where}\quad\varphi(t)=\frac{1}{4\nu^{2}}\,t^{\frac{1}{|\nu|}-2}.

(6.4)

We note that
\varphi(t)
is strictly decreasing for
\,t>0\,
in the present case.

Let us use the notation
\,{\mathbf{j}}_{m}:=j_{m}^{2|\nu|}\,
so that
w(t)
has zeros at
each
{\mathbf{j}}_{m}
. As in the previous case, if we fix a positive integer
k
and consider

w_{1}(t)=-w\left({\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}+t\right),\quad w_{2}(t)=w\left({\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}-t\right),

then it is straightforward to calculate, by using (
6.4
), that

\displaystyle w_{1}^{\prime\prime}+\varphi\left({\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}+t\right)

\displaystyle w_{1}=0,\quad w_{2}^{\prime\prime}+\varphi\left({\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}-t\right)w_{2}=0,

\displaystyle w_{1}(0)

\displaystyle=w_{2}(0)=0,

\displaystyle w_{1}^{\prime}(0)=w_{2}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{j_{2k+1}^{2+\nu-|\nu|}}{\,4|\nu(\nu+1)|\,}\,\mathbb{J}_{\nu+1}\left(j_{2k+1}\right)>0,

which implies by Sturm’s theorem that

\,{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+2}-{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}>{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}-{\mathbf{j}}_{2k}\,
and also
\,w_{1}(t)>w_{2}(t)\,
for
\,0<t<{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}-{\mathbf{j}}_{2k}.

Proceeding as before, we find that

\int_{{\mathbf{j}}_{2k}}^{{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+2}}g(t)w(t)dt<0,\quad k=1,2,\cdots,

(6.5)

for any positive function
g(t)
increasing on
\,\big[{\mathbf{j}}_{2k},\,{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+2}\big].
Set
\,{\mathbf{j}}_{0}=0
. In the case
\nu>1/2
, it is easy to see that (
6.5
)
remains valid for
\,k=0.
In the case
\,-1<\nu<-1/2
, we use the fact

\,w_{1}(t)>w_{2}(t)\,
for
0<t<{\mathbf{j}}_{1}
to observe

\int_{0}^{2{\mathbf{j}}_{1}}g(t)w(t)dt<0.

Since
\,w(t)<0\,
for
\,2{\mathbf{j}}_{1}<t<{\mathbf{j}}_{2},
it is now evident that

\int_{0}^{{\mathbf{j}}_{2}}g(t)w(t)dt<0,

whence (
6.5
) remains valid for
\,k=0.
In a like manner, we have

\int_{{\mathbf{j}}_{2k-1}}^{{\mathbf{j}}_{2k+1}}g(t)w(t)dt>0,\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

(6.6)

If
f(t)
is a positive function for
\,0<t<1\,
and
\,x>0,
then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}f\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2|\nu|}\int_{0}^{x^{2|\nu|}}t^{(3/2-3|\nu|)/2|\nu|}f\left(\frac{t^{1/2|\nu|}}{x}\right)\,w(t)dt

whenever the integral converges. We note that the function

h(t,x)=t^{(3/2-3|\nu|)/2|\nu|}f\left(\frac{t^{1/2|\nu|}}{x}\right),\quad 0<t<x^{2|\nu|},

is increasing in
t
if and only if
\,t^{3/2-3|\nu|}f(t)\,
is increasing for
\,0<t<1.

By applying (
6.5
), together with the additional case
\,k=0,
and (
6.6
) with

\,g(t)=h\left(t,j_{2m+1}\right)\,
or
\,h\left(t,j_{2m}\right),
we find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{j_{2m+1}}f\left(\frac{t}{j_{2m+1}}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt>0,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0}^{j_{2m}}f\left(\frac{t}{j_{2m}}\right)\left[t^{\nu+1/2}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(t)\right]dt<0,\quad m=1,2,\cdots,

provided that
\,t^{3/2-3|\nu|}f(t)\,
is positive and increasing
for
\,0<t<1.

In summary, it is proved that the following sign pattern holds true.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\,|\nu|>1/2\,
and
f(t)
be a positive integrable function satisfying (
2.1
) such that
\,t^{3/2-3|\nu|}f(t)\,
is increasing
for
\,0<t<1,
then

{\rm{sgn}}\,\big[\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)\left(j_{\nu,m}\right)\big]=(-1)^{m+1},\quad m=1,2,\cdots.

Remark 6.2
.

As it may be expected, the sufficient conditions for the sign changes presented in
both Lemmas
6.1
,
6.2
are far from being optimal.
For example, if we take
\,f(t)=2(\nu+1)\,t^{\nu+1/2},
a special case of (
2.28
),
then its Hankel transform

\,\mathbb{H}_{\nu}(f)(z)=\mathbb{J}_{\nu+1}(z)\,
is subject to the sign pattern

{\rm{sgn}}\,\big[\mathbb{J}_{\nu+1}\left(j_{\nu,m}\right)\big]=(-1)^{m+1}\quad\text{for all $m$ and $\,\nu>-1.$}

Nevertheless, both lemmas indicate that this sign pattern holds true only in the range

\,-1<\nu\leq 1,\,\nu\neq-1/2.

On combining the above lemmas with Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
,
we obtain the following theorem which constitutes one of our main results.

Theorem 6.1
.

For
\,\nu>-1,
suppose that

While we shall present applications in the next section,

As to the existence of zeros, the following results have been established in our recent work

Proposition 7.1

Regarding the region

of parameters, it is simple to observe

Theorem 7.1
.

For each
\,a>0,
if

\,\Phi\notin\mathcal{LP}\,
and

Proof.

As readily calculated, the entire function

What matters is the nature of zeros in the case

By integrating termwise, it is readily verified that

7.3
A transference principle

In summary, it has been shown so far that
\,\Phi\in\mathcal{LP}\,
in the case when
\,a>0\,
and
parameters
b,c
satisfy one of the following conditions:

(1)\,\text{$\,b=a\,$ or $\,c=a.$}\quad(2)\,\,(b,c)\in\mathcal{S}_{a}.\quad(3)\,\,(b,c)\in\big(\mathcal{Z}_{a}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{a}^{*}\big).

As it may be expected from Theorem
5.2
, which asserts that
certain class of
\mathcal{LP}
is closed under the operation
\,z\cdot d/dz+\alpha,\,\alpha\geq 0,

it is possible to extend the range of parameters with the aid of the following.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
\Phi(z)
belongs to
\mathcal{LP},
then the function of type

{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a+m\\
b+n,\,c+\ell\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,z^{2}}{4}\right]

(7.3)

also belongs to
\mathcal{LP},
where
m,n,\ell
are integers subject to the condition

m\geq 0,\quad-b<n\leq m,\quad-c<\ell\leq m.

Proof.

As an even entire function of order one, it is easy to verify that
any entire function of the above type falls under the scope of
Theorem
5.2
. For
\,a\neq 0,\,b\neq 1,\,c\neq 1,
if we consider the differential operators

D=z\frac{d}{dz},\quad D_{b}=z\frac{d}{dz}+2(b-1),\quad D_{c}=z\frac{d}{dz}+2(c-1),

(7.4)

then it is routine to calculate

\displaystyle D\big[\Phi(z)\big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\,az^{2}\,}{2bc}\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a+1\\
b+1,\,c+1\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,z^{2}}{4}\right],

\displaystyle D_{b}\big[\Phi(z)\big]

\displaystyle=2(b-1)\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
b-1,\,c\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,z^{2}}{4}\right],

\displaystyle D_{c}\big[\Phi(z)\big]

\displaystyle=2(c-1)\,{}_{1}F_{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a\\
b,\,c-1\end{array}\biggr|-\frac{\,z^{2}}{4}\right].

Since (
7.3
) can be obtained from
\Phi(z)
by a successive applications of the operations (
7.4
),
where we eliminate the multiplicative factor in each application,
the desired result follows by Theorem
5.2
.
∎

For
\,a=7/2,
the region
\Delta
of parameter-pair
(b,c)
for which

\,\Phi\in\mathcal{LP}\,
consists of line segments, vertical and horizontal rays,
integer-lattice points and rectangles
\mathcal{X}_{a},\mathcal{X}_{a}^{*}.

If
\,(b,c)\in\mathcal{P}_{a}\setminus\{(4,7),\,(7,4)\},
then
\,\Phi\notin\mathcal{LP}
with infinitely
many complex zeros.

•

By Theorem
7.1
, if
\,(b,c)\in\mathcal{P}_{a}\setminus\big\{(4,7),(7,4)\big\},
where

\mathcal{P}_{a}=\big\{(b,c):b>7/2,\,\,b+c\geq 11,\,\,(2b-7)(2c-7)\geq 7\big\},

then
\,\Phi\notin\mathcal{LP}\,
and
\Phi(z)
has an infinity of complex zeros.

•

By Proposition
7.2
,
\Delta
contains four vertical rays
\,b=7/2-k,\,c>0,
and
four horizontal rays
\,b>0,\,c=7/2-k,
where
\,k=0,1,2,3.
Similarly, by Proposition
7.3
,

\Delta
contains the lattice points

\displaystyle\left\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}:1\leq m\leq 4,\,1\leq n\leq 7\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cup\,\left\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}:1\leq m\leq 7,\,1\leq n\leq 4\right\}.

•

By Theorem
7.3
,
\Delta
contains eight line segments defined by

b+c=k,\,b>0,\,c>0,\quad\text{where $\,k=1,\cdots,8.$}

•

By Theorem
7.4
,
\Delta
contains the rectangles

\mathcal{X}_{a}=\big(0,\,9/2\big]\times\big(0,\,15/4\big],\quad\mathcal{X}_{a}^{*}=\big(0,\,15/4\big]\times\big(0,\,9/2\big].

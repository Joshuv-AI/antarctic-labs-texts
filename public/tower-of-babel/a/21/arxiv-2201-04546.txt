Noise Improvements in Quantum Simulations of sQED using Qutrits Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-22-002-SQMS-T
Erik J. Gustafson

Abstract
We present an argument for the advantages of using qudits over qubits for scalar Quantum Electrodynamics in ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) d. We measure the mass gap using an out of time correlator as a function of noise coming from an amplitude damping error channel and a generalized Pauli channel decoherence channel for both qubits and qutrits. For the same error in determination of the mass, the qutrit simulations can tolerate 10 to 100x larger gate noise than a qubit simulations. We find that 20 per-cent accuracy on the mass gap could be possible in the near future with a qutrits but is infeasible using qubits.

III
Systematics

There are many systematic errors that are noticeable for any NISQ era QFT simulation. These systematic errors will come from two places: the Fourier transform of the time series data, and Trotterization. These first two types of errors are also going to exist in fault tolerant computers, while systematic and coherent errors from gate operations will only be a NISQ era problem.

Given the corrrelator in Eq. (
11
), a Fourier transform will take this time series data and provide an energy spectrum that should be sharply peaked around the desired state of interest. In practice this is done by taking a finite number of time steps at a finite resolution of
\delta t

\delta E_{FFT}=2\pi/(\delta tN_{steps}),

(18)

where
\delta t
is time resolution and
N_{steps}
is the number of sample in time. The time resolution,
\delta t
, directly imposes an upper bound on the observable energy.
N_{steps}
imposes a finite resolution on the energy given a choice of
\delta t

It should be reiterated, the resolution in Eq. (
18

\hat{U}^{\pm}
will impose ancillary excitations and decreased resolution of the peaks.

In practice implementing

\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{z}(\delta t)=e^{-i\delta t(g^{2}/2+1)\sum_{i}(\hat{L}^{z}_{i})^{2}},

\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{zz}(\delta t)=e^{-i\delta t\sum_{i}(\hat{L}^{z}_{i}\hat{L}^{z}_{i+1})},

(21)

\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{x}(\delta t)=e^{-i\delta t\sum_{i}(\hat{U}^{x}_{i})}.

(22)

The Trotterization of the time evolution operator is going to take us away from the correlator in Eq. (

where

and

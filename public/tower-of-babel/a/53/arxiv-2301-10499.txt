A Provable Splitting Approach for Symmetric Nonnegative Matrix Factorization
Xiao Li, Zhihui Zhu, Qiuwei Li, Kai Liu

Abstract
The symmetric Nonnegative Matrix Factorization (NMF), a special but important class of the general NMF, has found numerous applications in data analysis such as various clustering tasks. Unfortunately, designing fast algorithms for the symmetric NMF is not as easy as for its nonsymmetric counterpart, since the latter admits the splitting property that allows state-of-the-art alternating-type algorithms. To overcome this issue, we first split the decision variable and transform the symmetric NMF to a penalized nonsymmetric one, paving the way for designing efficient alternating-type algorithms. We then show that solving the penalized nonsymmetric reformulation returns a solution to the original symmetric NMF. Moreover, we design a family of alternating-type algorithms and show that they all admit strong convergence guarantee: the generated sequence of iterates is convergent and converges at least sublinearly to a critical point of the original symmetric NMF. Finally, we conduct experiments on both synthetic data and real image clustering to support our theoretical results and demonstrate the performance of the alternating-type algorithms.

I-A

Main Contributions

In this paper, we compute the symmetric NMF by considering a variable splitting method, which reformulates our problem to a penalized nonsymmetric NMF. This new nonsymmetric reformulation enables us to design efficient alternating-type algorithms for solving the original symmetric NMF.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

•

Motivated by the splitting property exploited in ANLS and HALS algorithms, we split the quadratic form on
\boldsymbol{U}
in the symmetric NMF into two different factors and transform symmetric NMF to a penalized nonsymmetric NMF, i.e.,

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{U}_{0}\boldsymbol{V}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{F}+\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{2}

\displaystyle<2\lambda+\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{X}),

where the second line utilizes (
15
). We complete the proof by invoking
1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

2
indicates that instead of directly solving the symmetric NMF (
2
), one can turn to solve (
3
) with a properly chosen penalty parameter
\lambda
. The latter has similar form to the general nonsymmetric NMF (

) obeying splitting property, which paves the way for designing efficient alternating-type algorithms.

III
Fast Algorithms for Symmetric NMF

In the last section, we have shown that the symmetric NMF (
2
) can be transformed to problem (
3
) which admits splitting property, enabling us to design efficient alternating-type algorithms to solve the original symmetric NMF. In this section, we exploit the splitting property and design fast algorithms for solving problem (
3
) by adopting ANLS, HALS, and accelerated HALS. Moreover, we provide strong convergence guarantees that the sequence of iterates generated by our algorithms is convergent and converges to a critical point of the original symmetric NMF (
2
). This is obtained by exploiting
2
and the property that the objective function
f
in (
3
) is strongly convex with respect to
\boldsymbol{U}
(or
\boldsymbol{V}
) when the other variable
\boldsymbol{V}
(or
\boldsymbol{U}
) is fixed.

III-B

HALS-type Method for Symmetric NMF

As we stated before, due to the nonnegative constraint, there is no closed-from solution for (
16
), although one may utilize some efficient algorithms for solving it. However, there does exist a closed-form solution when

. HALS
[
cichocki2007hierarchical
]
exploits this observation by splitting the pair of variables
(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{V})
into columns
(\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{u}_{r},\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{v}_{r})
and then optimizing over
column by column

3

\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{V}^{\mathrm{T}}=\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}+\sum_{j\neq i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\boldsymbol{v}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}
and denote

\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}=\boldsymbol{X}-\sum_{j\neq i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\boldsymbol{v}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}

the factorization residual
\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{V}^{\mathrm{T}}
excluding the contribution of
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}
. Now if we minimize the objective function

in (

\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
, then it is equivalent to

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\natural}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\boldsymbol{v}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{v}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle=\max\left(\frac{(\overline{\boldsymbol{X}}+\lambda{\bf I})\boldsymbol{v}_{i}}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda},0\right).

Similar closed-form solution also holds when optimizing in terms of
\boldsymbol{v}_{i}

\boldsymbol{U}
or

Remark 2
.

SymHALS updates each column of
\boldsymbol{U}
and
\boldsymbol{V}
one time during each iteration, while A-SymHALS updates each column multiple times by refining previous solutions.
Thus, on the one hand, A-SymHALS has higher computational complexity than SymHALS in each iteration, but less than SymANLS since the latter requires to solve relatively computationally expensive nonnegative least squares in each iteration. On the other hand, A-SymHALS is supposed to converge faster than SymHALS since the former decreases more function value in each iteration, while SymANLS can decrease the most function value among them in each iteration. Therefore, A-SymHALS can be viewed as an effective approach to balance the trade-off between convergence speed and computational complexity of each iteration within SymHALS and SymANLS. We will compare these three algorithms in
Section

IV
. But before this, in the next Section we provide convergence analysis for the three algorithms and show that all of them will converge to a critical point of the original symmetric NMF problem (
2
).

III-D

Convergence Results

By exploiting the strong convexity of the objective function in (
3
) when restricted to block
\boldsymbol{U}
(or
\boldsymbol{V}
) and the guarantee of
2
, we establish the convergence result of the three proposed alternating algorithms.

Theorem 3

(Convergence of the proposed algorithms to a critical point of the symmetric NMF (
2
))
.

In (
3
), set

\lambda>\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{2}+\left\|\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{U}_{0}\boldsymbol{U}_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}\right\|_{F}-\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{X})\right)=:\overline{\lambda}.

(18)

Suppose
Algorithm

1
,
Algorithm

2
, and
Algorithm

3
are initialized with
\boldsymbol{V}_{0}=\boldsymbol{U}_{0}
. Let
\{(\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
be the sequence of iterates generated by any of the three algorithms. Then,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k})=(\boldsymbol{U}^{\star},\boldsymbol{V}^{\star}),

where the limit point
(\boldsymbol{U}^{\star},\boldsymbol{V}^{\star})

\boldsymbol{U}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{V}^{\star}

and
\boldsymbol{U}^{\star}
is a critical point of (

The proof of
3
consists of showing the convergence requirement of algorithms in
2

Remark 3
.

First note that the algorithm can be proved to converge to
(\boldsymbol{U}^{\star},\boldsymbol{V}^{\star})
for any positive
\lambda
with the argument in Section
V
, but we need
\lambda
to be relatively large as in (

) to ensure
\boldsymbol{U}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{V}^{\star}
.
We emphasize that the specific structure within (
3
) enables
3
to get rid of both the assumption on the boundedness of the iterates
\{(\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k})\}_{k\geq 0}
and the requirement of an additional proximal term, which are usually required for convergence analysis though are not necessary in practice
[

23
]
. For example, the previous work
[
29
]
provides convergence guarantee for the standard ANLS when used to solve the general nonsymmetric NMF (

1
)
[
15

22

(\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k})
is away from zero through all iterations. Though such an assumption can be satisfied by explicitly imposing additional constraints, it leads to a slightly different problem. By contrast, our convergence result when applied to SymHALS and A-SymHALS overcomes this issue because of the additional penalty term in (

).

Remark 4
.

The convergence of SymANLS and SymHALS can be established by following the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz (KL) convergence analysis framework
[
23
,
24
]
since the additional penalty term in (
3
) can be used for establishing the so-called sufficient decrease property of the two algorithms. However, the multiple update scheme of A-SymHALS makes its convergence analysis more complicated than the previous two algorithms. As a result, one cannot directly apply this framework for A-SymHALS. To overcome this technical difficulty, we generalize the KL analysis framework such that it becomes compatible with the multiple update scheme used in A-SymHALS, which is of independent interest; see Section
V
for detailed analysis.

III-E

An Adaptive Updating Formula for
\lambda

3
guarantees convergence of the three algorithms for any relatively large penalty parameter
\lambda
. Though
\lambda
is fixed in Algorithms 1-3 for simplicity, it can be updated through the entire process. In this subsection, we provide an adaptive strategy for updating the parameter
\lambda
along with the iterations of our algorithms. Towards that end, first note that
\|\boldsymbol{U}_{k}\pm\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\geq 0
always holds, which gives

\|\boldsymbol{U}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\geq 2\left|\langle\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\rangle\right|.

This further implies

\frac{\|\boldsymbol{U}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}}{2\left|\langle\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\rangle\right|}\geq 1.

(19)

The relation in (
19
) motivates us to use the following
adaptive
strategy for updating
\lambda
:

\boxed{\quad\lambda_{k+1}=\lambda_{k}\cdot\frac{\|\boldsymbol{U}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}}{2\left|\langle\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\rangle\right|},\quad k\geq 0,\quad}

(20)

where the initial regularization parameter
\lambda_{0}>0
can be selected as a very small number, e.g.,
\lambda_{0}=10^{-5}
. Note that
\boldsymbol{U}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{V}_{k}
will not tend to be orthogonal as we are minimizing
\|\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{V}\|_{F}^{2}
. Therefore, the formula (
20
) is well defined as the denominator is bounded alway from zero as the iteration proceeds.

To understand this adaptive strategy, we note that by (
19
), the parameter
\lambda_{k}
keeps increasing at each iteration until

\frac{\|\boldsymbol{U}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}}{2\left|\langle\boldsymbol{U}_{k},\boldsymbol{V}_{k}\rangle\right|}=1.

The above happens only when
2
2

2

It can also happen when
\boldsymbol{U}_{k}=-\boldsymbol{V}_{k}
, but since the algorithm minimizes
\|\boldsymbol{U}-\boldsymbol{V}\|_{F}^{2}
, this case in practice is likely impossible.

\boldsymbol{U}_{k}=\boldsymbol{V}_{k}
, which indeed is the goal we want to achieve. On the one hand, the two factors can reach consensus without requiring
\lambda_{k}
to go to infinitely large, as guaranteed by
3
. On the other hand, we note that the algorithm may already converge and
\boldsymbol{U}^{\star}=\boldsymbol{V}^{\star}
even with
\lambda_{k}\leq\overline{\lambda}
, where
\overline{\lambda}
is the lower bound in (
18
). There is no contradiction to
3
as (
18
) is a sufficient but not necessary condition to guarantee convergence and consensus. In fact, this is one of the advantages of using this adaptive strategy since a smaller penalty term can allow the algorithms focus more on the data fidelity term. We refer to
Figure

1
in next Section for an illustration of the practical performance of the adaptive strategy (
20
).

It is worth mentioning that our idea of updating the penalty parameter
\lambda
has the potential to be used widely in other Augmented Lagrangian-based algorithms and splitting algorithms since these type of methods usually use squared Euclidean distance to penalize violation of the constraints.

Definition 1

Definition 2

of

The above KL property (also known as KL inequality) states the regularity of

around its critical point
\overline{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
[

shows that our function of interest

\operatorname{dist}\left(\boldsymbol{W}_{k},\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{W}_{0})\right)\leq\delta
and
f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})<f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})+\eta
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Furthermore, from
3
, we have
f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})>f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})
for all
k\geq 0
. Hence, from
9
one has

\left[f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})\right]^{\theta}\leq C_{3}\operatorname{dist}({\bf 0},\partial f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k}))\ \forall k\geq k_{0}.

(25)

In the subsequent analysis, we restrict to
k\geq k_{0}
.
Construct a concave function
x^{1-\theta}
for some
\theta\in[0,1)
with domain
x>0
. Obviously, by the concavity, we have

x_{2}^{1-\theta}-x_{1}^{1-\theta}\geq(1-\theta)x_{2}^{-\theta}(x_{2}-x_{1}),\forall x_{1}>0,x_{2}>0.

By replacing
x_{1}
by
f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})
and
x_{2}
by
f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})
and using the sufficient decrease property in
2
, we have
(we will hide all absolute and independent constants in
\overline{C}
to simplify notation)

\begin{split}&\left(f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})\right)^{1-\theta}-\left(f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})\right)^{1-\theta}\\
&\geq\overline{C}\frac{f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})-f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1})}{\left(f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})\right)^{\theta}}.\\
&\geq\overline{C}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{j+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{j}\|_{F}^{2}}{\operatorname{dist}({\bf 0},\partial f(\boldsymbol{W}_{k}))}\\
&\geq\overline{C}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{j+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{j}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}\|_{F}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}}\\
&\geq\overline{C}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}},\end{split}

(26)

where the second inequality uses
2
and (
25
), the third inequality is due to
6
, and
\overline{C}
in the last line is

\overline{C}=\frac{\lambda(1-\theta)}{8lrC_{3}(2B_{0}+\lambda+\|\boldsymbol{X}\|_{F})}>0.

Due to
\frac{a}{t}=\frac{a}{t}+t-t\geq 2\sqrt{a}-t
for all
a\geq 0,t>0
, we have

\begin{split}&\frac{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}}\\
&\geq 2\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\\
&\quad-\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\end{split}

(27)

Combining (
26
) and (
27
) and summing them up from
\widetilde{k}\geq k_{0}
to
m\rightarrow\infty
yields

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=\widetilde{k}}^{\infty}\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\overline{C}}\left(f(\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}})-f(\boldsymbol{W}^{\star})\right)^{1-\theta},\end{split}

(28)

which immediately implies

\sum_{k=\widetilde{k}}^{\infty}\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}<\infty.

Thus, we conclude that
\{\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence and hence is convergent. It immediately follows that the limit points set
\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{W}_{0})=\{\boldsymbol{W}^{\star}\}
is a singleton and
\boldsymbol{W}^{\star}=(\boldsymbol{U}^{\star},\boldsymbol{V}^{\star})
is a critical point of (
3
) due to
8
.

As for convergence rate, it follows from (
28
) that

\begin{split}&\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}^{\star}\|_{F}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=\widetilde{k}}^{\infty}\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\\
&+\alpha\left(\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}}-\boldsymbol{W}_{\widetilde{k}-1}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\end{split}

(29)

for some constant
\alpha>0
, where the last line uses (
25
) and
6
. Denoting by

P_{\widetilde{k}}=\sum_{k=\widetilde{k}}^{\infty}\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{W}_{k+1}-\boldsymbol{W}_{k}^{L-1}\|_{F}^{2}},

we obtain

P_{\widetilde{k}}\leq P_{{\widetilde{k}}-1}-P_{\widetilde{k}}+\alpha\left[P_{\widetilde{k}-1}-P_{\widetilde{k}}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}.

(30)

The above recursion about the sequence
\{P_{\widetilde{k}}\}_{\widetilde{k}\geq k_{0}}

has exactly the same form with
[
32
, eq. (12)]
. Hence the convergence rate can be obtained by following the same arguments after
[
32
, eq. (12)]
. This completes the proof.
∎

VI
Conclusion

In order to design efficient alternating-type algorithms for the symmetric NMF, we transfer this problem to a penalized nonsymmetric NMF. We have proved that solving the nonsymmetric reformulation returns a solution for the original symmetric NMF when the penalty term is relatively large, in sharp contrast to the classical result for the methods of Lagrangian multiplier that suggests it happens only when the penalty term tends to infinity. Furthermore, we have proved that various alternating-type algorithms—when used to efficiently solve the nonsymmetric reformulation—admit strong convergence guarantee in the sense that the generated sequence is convergent at least at a sublinear rate and it converges to a critical point of the original symmetric NMF.
An interesting question would be whether it is possible to further improve the lower bound on the penalty parameter
\lambda
that ensures convergence, and even to the extreme case that whether the convergence to a critical point of the original symmetric NMF is guaranteed for any positive
\lambda
. In additions, it would also
be of great interest to extend both algorithmic strategy and theoretical guarantee for multidimensional cases, such as symmetric tensor factorization and symmetric nonnegative tensor factorization.

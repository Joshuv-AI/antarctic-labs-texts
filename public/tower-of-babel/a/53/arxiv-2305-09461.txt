Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we will study a class of linear integral operators with the nonnegative kernels on higher-dimensional product spaces, the norms of the operators can be obtained by integral of the product of the kernel function and finitely many basic functions. As application, we obtain the sharp constant for linear Hilbert operator on Higher-dimensional product spaces.

Introduction

How to compute the sharp constants of some inequalities is a very important question in harmonic analysis. For a class of linear integral operators with the nonnegative kernels, which satisfy some homogeneity and the rotational invariance condition,Beckner
[
3
]
obtained the following conclusion which is also denoted by Stein-Weiss Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
K
is a nonnegative kernel defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
continuous on any domain that
excludes the point
(0,0)
, homogeneous of degree
-n
.

K(\delta u,\delta v)=\delta^{-n}K(u,v)

(1)

and

K(Ru,Rv)=K(u,v)

(2)

for any
R\in\mathrm{S}O(n)
. Then
K
defines an integral operator

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x,y)f(y)dy

which maps
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

i.e.
,

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(3)

holds for
1<p<\infty
, where the optimal constant is given by

C=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(e_{1},y)|y|^{-\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}}dy,

(4)

where
e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
is a unit vector in the first coordinate direction in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mathrm{S}O(n)
denotes a set of the rotation transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The inequality (
1
) was given by Stein and Weiss
[
1
]
, then Beckner
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
pointed out that the constant
C
is the best constant and gave the sharp constant for the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality with some power weights £¬more information readers can refer to
[
5
,
6
]
. Our main result is inspired by Lemma
1
. For more information about higher-dimensional product spaces, the reader can refer to
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Now , we give the definition of a class of linear integral operator with nonnegative kernel on higher-dimensional product spaces.

Definition 1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},1\leq i\leq m
, and
f
be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
.Then operator
T_{m}
with the kernel
K
is defined by

T_{m}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}}K(x_{1},y_{1})\cdots K(x_{m},y_{m})f(y_{1},y_{2}\cdots y_{m})dy_{1}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{m}.

(5)

It is not difficult to find that our work is actually to generalize the Stein-Weiss Lemma in higher-dimensional product space. Now, we give our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},1\leq i\leq m
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})
, then we have

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})}.

(6)

Moreover,

\|T_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})}=C,

(7)

where

C=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})\cdots K(e_{m},y_{m})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}\cdots|y_{m}|^{-\frac{n_{m}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{m}.

(8)

To prove above Theorem, we first provide some definitions and Lemmas which will be used in the following.Some Lemmas are well known in many classic books so we omit their proofs.

Auxiliary lemmas

Definition 2

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})K(e_{2},y_{2})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}.

The inequality (
22
) implies that

\|T_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})K(e_{2},y_{2})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

(23)

Next,we will prove the reverse inequality.In fact, it suffices to prove

\|T_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\geq\|\tilde{T}_{H}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})},

where
H
is defined as(
19
).
For any function
h
:
\mathbb{G}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, we define the corresponding function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as

f(y_{1},y_{2})=\omega_{n_{1}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{n_{2}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}h(|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|).

Clearly ,
f
is a radial nonnegative function and it easily implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{1}-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{2}-1}}K(t_{1}x_{1}^{\prime},t_{1}y_{1}^{\prime})K(t_{2}x_{2}^{\prime},t_{2}y_{2}^{\prime})f(t_{1}y_{1},t_{2}y_{2})d\sigma(y_{1})d\sigma(y_{2})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n_{1}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{n_{2}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}A(t_{1},t_{2})t_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}t_{2}^{-\frac{1}{p}}h(t_{1},t_{2}).

According to (
11
) and (
24
) , we have

\displaystyle\|T_{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}

\displaystyle\quad=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{1}-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n_{2}-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{G}^{2}}\omega_{n_{1}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\omega_{n_{2}-1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}A(t_{1},t_{2})t_{1}^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}t_{2}^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}h(t_{1},t_{2})t_{1}^{n_{1}-1}t_{2}^{n_{2}-1}dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{p}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times d\sigma(x_{1}^{\prime})d\sigma(x_{2}^{\prime})t_{1}^{n_{1}-1}t_{2}^{n_{2}-1}dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{G}^{2}}A(t_{1},t_{2})h(t_{1},t_{2})t_{1}^{\frac{n_{1}}{p^{\prime}}}t_{2}^{\frac{n_{2}}{p^{\prime}}}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\right)^{p}t_{1}^{n_{1}-1}t_{2}^{n_{2}-1}dt_{1}dt_{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Combining (
14
),(
18
) with the homogeneity of
-n
, we obtain

A(t_{1},t_{2})t_{1}^{\frac{n_{1}}{p}}t_{2}^{\frac{n_{2}}{p}}=H(\frac{t_{1}}{t},\frac{t_{2}}{t})t_{1}^{\frac{n_{1}}{p}}t_{2}^{\frac{n_{2}}{p}}.

Consequently, it follows that

\|T_{2}(f)\|_{L^{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}=\|\widetilde{T}_{H}(h)\|_{L^{P}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})}.

(25)

Since operator
\widetilde{T}_{H}
satisfies all the conditions of Corollary
10
, we have

\|\widetilde{T}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}=\|H\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})K(e_{2},y_{2})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

(26)

Since
H
is nonnegative , we conclude from (
25
) that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{T}_{H}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|h\|\end{subarray}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})}\neq 0,h\geq 0}\frac{\|\widetilde{T}_{H}(h)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})}}{\|h\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}^{2})}}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|f\|\end{subarray}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\neq 0}\frac{\|T_{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}}

\displaystyle=\|T_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}.

It immediately follows from (
26
) and (
27
) that

\|T_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})K(e_{2},y_{2})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

(28)

Both the inequalities (
23
) and (

) , yield

\|T_{2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}K(e_{1},y_{1})K(e_{2},y_{2})|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{2}}{p}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

So far, we have completed the proof of the Theorem

.

4
Sharp constant for linear Hilbert operator

In this section, we will consider linear Hilbert operator which can be found in
[
10
]
. Applying Theorem
1
, we can easily obtain the sharp constant on higher-dimensional product spaces.

Definition 5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},1\leq i\leq m
, and f be a nonnegative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
. Then the linear Hilbert operator
T^{*}_{m}
on higher-dimensional product spaces is defined by

T^{*}_{m}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}\frac{f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(|x_{i}|^{n_{i}}+|y_{i}|^{n_{i}})}dy_{1}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{m}.

(29)

Theorem 2
.

Let
1<p<\infty,m\in\mathbb{N},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}},1\leq i\leq m
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})
, then we have

\|T^{*}_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})}\leq A.

Morever,

\|T^{*}_{m}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}})}=A,

where

A=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n_{i}}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n_{i}}{2})n_{i}}m\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{p})\Gamma(\frac{1}{p}).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Obviously, the operator
T^{*}_{m}
satisfies the condition of Theorem
1
, so we have

\displaystyle T^{*}_{m}f(x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}}\frac{f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(|x_{i}|^{n_{i}}+|y_{i}|^{n_{i}})}dy_{1}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}\frac{|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}\cdots|y_{m}|^{\frac{n_{m}}{p}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1+|y_{i}|^{n_{i}})}dy_{1}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{m}.

We merely give the proof with the case
m=2
, and the same is true for the general case
m>2
.
By the polar coordinates integral formula,we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}}\frac{|y_{1}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}|y_{2}|^{-\frac{n_{1}}{p}}}{(1+|y_{1}|^{n_{1}})(1+|y_{2}|^{n_{2}})}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{n_{1}-1}\omega_{n_{2}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{r_{1}^{\frac{n_{1}}{p}-1}r_{2}^{\frac{n_{2}}{p}-1}}{(1+r_{1}^{n_{1}})(1+r_{2}^{n_{2}})}dr_{1}dr_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n_{1}-1}}{n_{1}}\frac{\omega_{n_{2}-1}}{n_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}s_{2}^{-\frac{1}{p}}}{(1+s_{1})(1+s_{2})}ds_{1}ds_{2}.

Define

F(\beta):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{1}^{-\beta}}{1+s_{1}}\frac{s_{2}^{-\beta}}{1+s_{2}}ds_{1}ds_{2},

then we have

\displaystyle F(\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{1}^{-\beta}}{1+s_{1}}\frac{s_{2}^{-\beta}}{1+s_{2}}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+s_{1})s_{1}^{\beta}}\frac{1}{(1+s_{2})s_{2}^{\beta}}ds_{1}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+s_{1})s_{1}^{\beta}}ds_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+s_{2})s_{2}^{\beta}}ds_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t_{1})^{-\beta}t_{1}^{\beta-1}dt_{1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t_{2})^{-\beta}t_{2}^{\beta-1}dt_{2}

\displaystyle=2B(1-\beta,\beta)

\displaystyle=2\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right).

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle T^{*}_{2}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{n_{1}-1}}{n_{1}}\frac{\omega_{n_{2}-1}}{n_{2}}2\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{1}}{2}\right)n_{1}}\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n_{2}}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n_{2}}{2}\right)n_{2}}2\Gamma\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{p}\right).

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

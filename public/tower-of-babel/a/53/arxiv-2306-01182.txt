A Yee-like finite-element scheme for Maxwell’s equations on unstructured grids
B. Radu † and H. Egger †,∗

Abstract
. A novel finite element scheme is studied for solving the time-dependent Maxwell’s equations on unstructured grids efficiently. Similar to the traditional Yee scheme, the method has one degree of freedom for most edges and a sparse inverse mass matrix. This allows for an efficient realization by explicit time-stepping without solving linear systems. The method is constructed by algebraic reduction of another underlying finite element scheme which involves two degrees of freedom for every edge. Mass-lumping and additional modifications are used in the construction of this method to allow for the mentioned algebraic reduction in the presence of source terms and lossy media later on. A full error analysis of the underlying method is developed which by construction also carries over to the reduced scheme and allows to prove convergence rates for the latter. The efficiency and accuracy of both methods are illustrated by numerical tests. The proposed schemes and their analysis can be extended to structured grids and in special cases the reduced method turns out to be algebraically equivalent to the Yee scheme. The analysis of this paper highlights possible difficulties in extensions of the Yee scheme to non-orthogonal or unstructured grids, discontinuous material parameters, and non-smooth source terms, and also offers potential remedies.



Keywords:

Maxwell’s equations, explicit time stepping, finite element methods, mass-lumping, Yee-like schemes

AMS subject classifications: 35Q61, 65M60

1.
Introduction

We consider the efficient numerical simulation of electromagnetic wave propagation through linear non-dispersive lossy media. As the basic mathematical model, we use Maxwell’s equations in second-order form, i.e.

(1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{tt}{E}+\sigma\partial_{t}{E}+\operatorname{curl}(\nu\operatorname{curl}{E})

\displaystyle=f,\qquad\text{in }\Omega,\,t>0,

(2)

\displaystyle n\times(\nu\operatorname{curl}E)

\displaystyle=g,\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\,t>0,

Here
\varepsilon
and
\sigma
denote the electric permittivity and conductivity, and
\nu
the magnetic reluctivity of the medium; further
E
is the electric field intensity,
f
,
g
describe the excitation by volume or surface currents, and
\Omega
is the computational domain.

One of the standard approaches for solving (
1
)–(
2
) numerically is the Yee scheme and its relatives, e.g., the finite difference time domain (FDTD) method and the finite-integration technique (FIT); see
[
38
]
,
[
34
,
35
]
, and
[
36
,
37
]
for details.
The common algebraic form of all these methods reads

(3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon}\frac{\mathsf{E}^{n+1}-2\mathsf{E}^{n}+\mathsf{E}^{n}}{\tau^{2}}+\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\frac{\mathsf{E}^{n+1}-\mathsf{E}^{n-1}}{2\tau}+\mathsf{K}_{\nu}\mathsf{E}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{f}^{n}+\mathsf{g}^{n}.

This allows for an efficient realization by explicit time stepping, whenever the mass matrices
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
are diagonal. On structured orthogonal grids and for homogeneous material distributions, such constructions are possible and lead to second-order accurate approximations in space and time; see e.g.
[
6
,
35
,
38
]
. These results remain valid for non-uniform orthogonal grids
[
28
]
; further see
[
6
,
7
]
for related higher-order methods.
The extension of the Yee scheme to discontinuous material parameters or source terms and to non-orthogonal or even unstructured grids is, however, not straightforward; see
[
17
,
20
,
32
,
33
]
and
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
for some attempts in the latter case. To the best of our knowledge, a rigorous convergence analysis of Yee-like schemes in such situations is not available up to date.

An alternative approach that allows the construction of stable schemes for structured as well as unstructured grids, and also for discontinuous material parameters, is offered by finite element discretizations. A rigorous error analysis for various methods can be developed; see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
; we refer to
[
6
,
18
]
for an overview of different approaches, their analysis, and further references.
Together with the leap-frog time-stepping scheme, the finite element approximation of (
1
)–(
2
) again leads to algebraic systems of the form (
3
).
In contrast to the finite difference schemes mentioned above, the mass matrices
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
are, however, usually not diagonal, and the realization of (
3
) thus requires the solution of linear systems in every time step. Therefore, the use of implicit time-stepping schemes has been advocated in the literature
[
22
,
26
]
.

This inherent disadvantage of finite element approximations can be overcome by mass-lumping, which aims at replacing
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}
in (
3
) by diagonal or block-diagonal matrices. For orthogonal grids, corresponding schemes have been proposed in
[
7
]
and modifications for unstructured grids have been considered in
[
11
,
12
]
; we also refer to
[
6
]
for a detailed discussion. A full convergence analysis for a second order method has been given recently in
[
10
,
31
]
, and in
[
19
]
higher order approximations of Maxwell’s equations were obtained based on staggered grid complexes.
Another approach for generating non-conforming finite element approximations with block-diagonal mass matrices is provided by discontinuous-Galerkin methods
[
8
,
16
]
. For low-order approximations however, these methods suffer from a substantial increase in the number of degrees of freedom; we refer to
[
15
]
for comparison with mass-lumping schemes in the context of elastodynamics.

Main contributions

In this paper, we propose and analyze a fully discrete finite element approximation for (
1
)–(
2
) which can be considered as a natural extension of the Yee scheme to unstructured grids. This method involves only one degree of freedom for most edges and it can be realized efficiently as an explicit time-stepping scheme; moreover, a full convergence analysis is possible.
The approach and its analysis are based on the following key ideas, already presented in
[
9
]
:
First, a finite element method is considered involving two degrees of freedom on each edge. Mass lumping is achieved by appropriate numerical quadrature and a corresponding choice of basis functions, and a full error analysis can be developed based on standard arguments.
In a second step, the method is then reduced on the algebraic level to a scheme that involves only one degree of freedom for most edges. While a direct variational characterization of this scheme is no longer possible, its close relation to the first method nevertheless allows to develop a rigorous error analysis.
Compared to
[
9
]
, some non-trivial modifications are required in the construction and the analysis of the proposed methods to deal with non-trivial conductivities
\sigma
and inhomogeneous problem data
f
,
g
.
On orthogonal grids and for special situations, the reduced method becomes equivalent to the traditional Yee scheme. The analysis of this paper therefore also offers some recipes for extensions of the Yee scheme to non-orthogonal grids and discontinuous parameters and problem data.
The algebraic form of the numerical scheme obtained after reduction also shares similarities with the approaches of
[
4
,
5
]
, which will be briefly discussed at the end of the manuscript.

2.
Notation and basic assumptions

We consider a three-dimensional setting and assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain.
Throughout the paper, we assume that

(A1)

\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a geometrically-conforming non-overlapping partition of
\Omega
into tetrahedral elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
; the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is shape-regular and quasi-uniform, i.e.,
\gamma h_{K}^{3}\leq|K|\leq h_{K}^{3}
and
\delta h\leq h_{K}\leq h
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
\gamma,\delta>0
.

As usual,
h_{K}
and
|K|
denote the diameter and the volume of the element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
h=\max_{K}h_{K}
is the global mesh size; see
[
13
]
for further details.
All results are presented in detail for the three-dimensional setting and unstructured grids, but the translation to two dimensions and structured grids is possible and more or less straightforward; see
[
31
, Chapter 5]
and Section
6
.

The material parameters and problem data are required to be sufficiently regular and to satisfy the usual physical bounds. Moreover, material discontinuities shall be resolved by the mesh.
For ease of presentation, we thus assume that

(A2)

\varepsilon
,
\sigma
,
\nu\in P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
0\leq\varepsilon,\sigma,\nu\leq\overline{c}
and
\varepsilon,\nu\geq\underline{c}
for some constants
\underline{c},\overline{c}>0
.

(A3)

f:[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
g:[0,T]\to L^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
are smooth functions of time, with
f(0)=0
and
g(0)=0
, and the initial conditions are
E(0)=\partial_{t}E(0)=0
.

We write
P_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\{v:v|_{K}\in P_{k}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
for the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
k
on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Under these assumptions, the existence of a unique solution to (
1
)–(
2
) can be established by standard arguments; see e.g.
[
21
,
23
]
.
Also, more general conditions could be treated with minor modifications to our analysis.

Function spaces

We use standard symbols
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
H^{1}(\Omega)
, and
H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)
for the spaces of square-integrable function with square integrable weak gradients and curls, respectively; see
[
27
]
for details.
The norm of a space
X
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and we will often write
\langle a,b\rangle=\int_{\Omega}a\cdot b\,dx
and
\langle a,b\rangle_{\partial}=\int_{\partial\Omega}a\cdot b\,ds(x)
for the scalar products of two functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)^{3}
, respectively.
For the error analysis, we use
H^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in H^{k}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
, to denote spaces of piecewise smooth functions, and we write
\|v\|_{H^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}=(\sum_{K}\|v\|_{H^{k}(K)}^{2})^{1/2}
for the corresponding norms.
We further denote by
L^{p}(0,T;X)
the Bochner spaces of functions with values in
X
whose
p
-th power is integrable in time. The functions in
W^{k,p}(0,T;X)
further have weak derivatives in
L^{p}(0,T;X)
.
For brevity, we will sometimes write
L^{p}(X)
and
W^{k,p}(X)
, and omit explicit reference to the time interval.

Space discretization

For the spatial approximation of the electric field
E
, we first consider Nédélec finite elements of type II, i.e.

(4)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega):v_{h}|_{K}\in P_{1}(K)^{3}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

This amounts to the space of piecewise linear vector-valued functions with tangential continuity across element boundaries; see
[
1
,
27
,
29
]
.
Let us recall that functions in
V_{h}
have two degrees of freedom for every edge, and the canonical interpolation operator for the space is given by
\Pi_{h}:H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}\to V_{h}
, with

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\Pi_{h}v\cdot\tau_{e}\,p_{e}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}v\cdot\tau_{e}\,p_{e}\,ds\qquad\forall p_{e}\in P_{1}(e),\ e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.

Here
\mathcal{E}_{h}=\{e_{ij}:i<j\}
is the set of edges
e_{ij}=(v_{i},v_{j})
and
\tau_{e}
is the unit tangential vector on
e=e_{ij}
pointing from vertex
v_{i}
to
v_{j}
with
i<j
.

For a given subset
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
of edges, we define the corresponding subspace

(6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{h}=\{v_{h}\in V_{h}:v_{h}\cdot\tau_{e}\in P_{0}(e)\ \forall e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\}\subset V_{h},

which consists of functions in
V_{h}
having only constant tangential trace, and therefore only one degree of freedom for edges
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
. We thus call
\widetilde{V}_{h}
the
reduced space
in the following.
The canonical interpolation operator for
\widetilde{V}_{h}
is given by
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}:H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3}\cap H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)\to\widetilde{V}_{h}
with

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{e}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v\cdot\tau_{e}\,p_{e}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{e}v\cdot\tau_{e}\,p_{e}\,ds\qquad\forall p_{e}\in P_{k_{e}}(e),\ e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},

and with degree
k_{e}=0
for
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
and
k_{e}=1
for
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
.
For any choice of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we have the inclusions

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega)\subset\widetilde{V}_{h}\subset\mathcal{N\mkern-2.0muC\mkern-2.0mu}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H(\operatorname{curl};\Omega).

Here
\mathcal{N}_{0}
and
\mathcal{N\mkern-2.0muC\mkern-2.0mu}_{1}
denote the lowest order Nédélec elements of type I and II. This ensures good approximation properties for both spaces; see
[
1
,
30
,
29
]
for details.
For the choice
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}
or
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}=\emptyset
, one of the two inclusions becomes an identity.

Mass lumping

For the approximation of some of the integrals arising in the finite element approximation of (
1
)–(
2
), we use numerical integration by the vertex rule; this will allow for mass-lumping later on.
For ease of notation, we introduce

(8)

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle_{h}=\sum\nolimits_{T}\tfrac{|T|}{4}\sum\nolimits_{v_{i}\in T}a(v_{i})\cdot b(v_{i}).

Here
a,b
are assumed to be piecewise smooth vector-valued functions over the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Let us note that the quadrature is exact if
a\cdot b\in P_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

Time discretization

Let
\tau=T/N
and
t^{n}=n\tau
be a sequence of uniformly spaced time steps. Further let
(a^{n})_{n\geq 0}\subset X
be a sequence in some vector space
X
. Then

(9)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}a^{n}:=\frac{a^{n+1}-2a^{n}+a^{n-1}}{\tau^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{\tau}a^{n-1/2}:=\frac{a^{n}-a^{n-1}}{\tau},

are used to denote the standard central difference quotients approximating the second and first derivative at time
t=t^{n}
and
t=t^{n-1/2}=t^{n}-\frac{1}{2}\tau
, respectively.

3.
Main results

For the numerical approximation of (
1
)–(
2
) with homogeneous initial conditions, see assumption (A3), we now consider the following fully discrete scheme.

Method 3.1
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}:V_{h}\to\widetilde{V}_{h}
denote the appropriate projection.
Find
E_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}
,
0\leq n\leq N
, with
E_{h}^{0}=E_{h}^{1}=0
and such that

(10)

\displaystyle\langle(\varepsilon+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma)\partial_{\tau\tau}E_{h}^{n},v_{h}\rangle_{h}+\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}E_{h}^{n-1/2},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}\rangle_{h}+\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}E_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle+\langle g(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\Omega},\qquad\forall\ 1\leq n<N.

The solution (sequence) will be abbreviated by the symbol
E_{h}=(E_{h}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N}
.

Independently of the choice of the set of edges
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
, on which the number of degrees of freedom is reduced, the implementation of the method leads to a finite-dimensional recursion of the form

(11)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}\frac{\mathsf{E}^{n+1}-2\mathsf{E}^{n}+\mathsf{E}^{n-1}}{\tau^{2}}+\widehat{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\frac{\mathsf{E}^{n}-\mathsf{E}^{n-1}}{\tau}+\mathsf{K}_{\nu}\mathsf{E}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{f}^{n}+\mathsf{g}^{n}.

The well-posedness of the discretization scheme then follows immediately from the regularity of the matrix
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
, which is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.3
below.
In Section
5
, we further show that an appropriate choice of basis functions for the space
V_{h}
, adopted to the numerical quadrature, leads to a block-diagonal mass matrix
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
, such that time stepping in (
11
) can be realized efficiently.

The algebraic form (
11
) reveals that Method
3.1
is based on an explicit time-stepping scheme and a restriction on the time step size
\tau
is, therefore, required to ensure discrete stability and convergence with
h,\tau\to 0
.
We thus assume that

(A4)

the time step
\tau>0
is chosen to satisfy for all
{v}_{h}\in{V}_{h}
the inequality

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\tau}^{2}}{4}\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}{v}_{h},\operatorname{curl}{v}_{h}\rangle+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\left|\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{v}_{h},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{v}_{h}\rangle-\langle\sigma{v}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle\right|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\varepsilon{v}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle_{h}.

For conductivity
\sigma=0
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, this assumption reduces to the usual CFL condition as used, e.g., in
[
6
,
18
]
.
Under our assumptions on the mesh and the model parameters, one can verify that
\tau\leq Ch
for some appropriate constant
C>0
is sufficient to guarantee condition (A4); see Section
6
.
In practice, an appropriate time step
\tau
satisfying (A4) can be found by performing a few vector iterations.

To guarantee good approximation properties, we further need some restriction on the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
of edges, on which the polynomial order is reduced.
We thus require that

(A5)

\sigma
is continuous across edges
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
inside
\Omega
, and
\sigma=0
for all
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
on
\partial\Omega
.

This condition simply means that we stay with two degrees of freedom on edges where the conductivity
\sigma
is either discontinuous or non-trivial at the boundary.
The reason for this restriction will become clear from the error analysis given in the next section and its necessity will be illustrated by numerical tests.

For ease of notation, we write
\|u_{h}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(X)}=\max_{0\leq k\leq N-1}\|u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{X}
in the following statements, and we write
u^{n+1/2}=u(t^{n+1/2})
for functions
u
that are continuous in time.
We further denote by
\widehat{u}^{\,n+1/2}=\frac{1}{2}(u^{n+1}+u^{n})
the average at intermediate time steps.
This allows us to present our first main result as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
E
be a sufficiently smooth solution of (
1
)–(
2
) and let (A1)–(A5) hold.
Then Method
3.1
is well-defined and the discrete solution
E_{h}=(E_{h}^{n})_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}E-\partial_{\tau}E_{h}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+\|\operatorname{curl}(E-\widehat{E}_{h})\|_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(E)h+C^{\prime}(E)\tau^{2},

with constants

\displaystyle C(E)

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\operatorname{curl}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\partial_{tt}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\Omega))},

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(E)

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{tttt}E\|_{L^{1}(L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{ttt}E\|_{L^{1}(L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{tt}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}.\qquad

The implementation leads to a time-stepping scheme (
11
), and for an appropriate choice of a basis, the matrix
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
is block-diagonal, while
\widehat{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}
and
\mathsf{K}_{\nu}
are sparse.

The convergence results is proven in Section
4
, while the algebraic structure of the scheme is derived in Section
5
.
Let us note that the assertions hold, in particular, for the choice
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}=\emptyset
, for which the projection
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
drops out and the method as well as its implementation become somewhat simpler; see
[
31
]
for details.

The Yee-like scheme

The reason for introducing the projection
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
in Method
3.1
lies in the following important observation, which is summarized as our second main result and leads to the reduced Yee-like scheme announced in the introduction.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
be valid and
E_{h}=(E_{h}^{n})_{n}
denote the solution of Method
3.1
.
Further define
\widetilde{E}_{h}^{n}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E_{h}^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}E-\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{E}_{h}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+\|\operatorname{curl}(E-\widehat{\widetilde{E}}_{h})\|_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(E)h+C^{\prime}(E)\tau^{2},

with constants
C(E)
and
C^{\prime}(E)
of the same form as in Theorem
3.2
.
Moreover, the coefficients of the solution
\widetilde{E}_{h}
can be computed by the time-stepping scheme

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}(-\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n-1/2}-\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n})+\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}+\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n},

and for an appropriate choice of basis for
\widetilde{V}_{h}
, the matrices
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}
,
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}
are sparse and the vectors
\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}
can be cheaply assembled from
\mathsf{f}^{n}
and
\mathsf{g}^{n}
in (
11
).

Remark 3.4
.

If
\sigma=0
,
we may choose
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}
and obtain an explicit time-stepping method for Maxwell’s equations with exactly one degree of freedom per edge.
For orthogonal grids and homogeneous data
f,g\equiv 0
, the presented approach becomes equivalent to the Yee scheme; see
[
9
]
for details.

The two assertions of Theorem
3.3
are again proven in the following two sections.

Remark 3.5
.

Let us emphasize that the matrix
\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
in (
12
) has a sparse inverse, but it is not a sparse matrix by itself.
In contrast to (
11
), which corresponds to Method
3.1
, we can not give a variational characterization of the scheme (
12
) in closed form.
This poses a severe challenge for the analysis of this method which can be overcome only by a somewhat non-standard analysis.

4.
Proof of convergence rates

In this section, we establish the convergence rates stated in Theorem
3.2
and
3.3
. To be able to do so, we require a couple of auxiliary results, which are stated first.

4.1.
Projection operators

For later reference, we collect some well-known properties of projection operators arising in our error analysis below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let (A1) hold,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
, and
\Pi_{h}
,
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
be defined as in Section
2
. Then

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|{E}-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{E}\|_{L^{2}(K)}&\leq Ch\|{E}\|_{H^{1}(K)},\\
\|\operatorname{curl}({E}-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{E})\|_{L^{2}(K)}&\leq Ch\|\operatorname{curl}{E}\|_{H^{1}(K)},\end{split}

for all
E\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
with a constant
C
depending only on
\gamma,\delta
in assumption (A1).
The same estimates also hold for the projection operator
\Pi_{h}
.

The proof of the assertions follows from the arguments given in
[
1
, Sec. 2.5]
.
In our analysis, we will also make use of the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection
\pi^{0}_{\omega}:L^{2}(\omega)\to P_{0}(\omega)
to constants for certain subsets
\omega\subset\Omega
, which is defined by

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}\pi^{0}_{\omega}v\,dx=\int_{\omega}v\,dx.

The same symbol will also be used for the projection of vector-valued functions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let (A1) hold and
\omega=K
for
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
or
\omega=\bigcup_{K\cap e=e}K
for
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Then the projection error can be estimated by

(15)

\displaystyle\|v-\pi^{0}_{\omega}v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq Ch\|v\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\omega)
with constant
C
depending only on
\gamma,\delta
in assumption (A1).

The proof of this assertion is based on the Poicaré–Friedrichs inequality
[
14
, Thm. 1.1]
and standard scaling arguments; also see
[
3
, Ch. 4]
.
Here we use that the sets
\omega
appearing in the lemma are uniformly star-shaped with respect to balls of size
h
, which follows from assumption (A1) on the mesh, and hence
C
is universal.

We will further write
\pi^{0}_{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
for the projection to piecewise constants over the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, defined by
(\pi^{0}_{h}v)|_{K}=\pi^{0}_{K}(v|_{K})
, and note that

(16)

\displaystyle\|v-\pi^{0}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq Ch\|v\|_{H^{1}(K)},

which follows immediately from the assertion of the previous lemma.

4.2.
Properties of the quadrature rule

As a second ingredient, we now state some elementary facts about the quadrature rule introduced in Section
2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let (A1) hold and
\alpha\in P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
\alpha\geq 0
. Then

(17)

\displaystyle c\,\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle\leq\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}\leq C\,\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

with uniform constants
c,C>0
depending only on the bounds in assumption (A1).

Proof.

We consider a single element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and abbreviate
\langle a,b\rangle_{K}=\int_{K}a\cdot b\,dx
and
\langle a,b\rangle_{h,K}=\frac{|K|}{4}\sum_{i}a(v_{i})\cdot b(v_{i})
. By mapping to the reference element, using the finite dimensionality of
P_{1}(K)^{3}
, and noting that
\alpha\geq 0
is piecewise constant, we get

\displaystyle c\,\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{K}\leq\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h,K}\leq C\,\langle\alpha v_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{K},

with constants
c,C
that only depend on the shape regularity of the element
K
.
The assertion of the lemma then follows by summation over all elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
∎

As a direct consequence of the previous result, we obtain the following assertions:

Corollary 4.4
.

Let (A1) hold.
Then
\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}=\langle v_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}
defines a norm on
V_{h}
and

(18)

\displaystyle c_{1}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|v_{h}\|_{h}^{2}\leq c_{2}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Further let
\alpha\in P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
0<\underline{\alpha}\leq\alpha\leq\overline{\alpha}
.
Then
\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}
defines a continuous and elliptic symmetric bilinear form on
V_{h}
, more precisely

\displaystyle c_{1}\underline{\alpha}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\langle\alpha u_{h},u_{h}\rangle_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}\leq c_{2}\overline{\alpha}\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in V_{h}.

These properties immediately imply the well-posedness of Method
3.1
.
As a next ingredient for our analysis, we analyze the quadrature error.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let assumption (A1) hold and let

\displaystyle\delta_{h}(\alpha u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}-\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle

denote the quadrature error for some
\alpha\in P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
Then

\displaystyle|\delta_{h}(\alpha\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u},{v}_{h})|\leq Ch\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{3},v_{h}\in V_{h},

with constant
C
depending only on
\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and the constants in assumption (A1).

Proof.

We define the local error
\delta_{h,K}(\alpha u_{h},v_{h})\coloneqq\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h,K}-\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{K}
, and split

\displaystyle|\delta_{h,K}(\alpha\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u},{v}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha\delta_{h,K}(\pi^{0}_{h}{u},{v}_{h})|+|\alpha\delta_{K}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u}-\pi^{0}_{h}{u},v_{h})|=(i)+(ii),

where we used that
\alpha
is piecewise constant.
Since the quadrature rule integrates linear polynomials exactly, we obtain
(i)=0
.
The second term can again be bounded elementwise. We may therefore omit
\alpha
and obtain

\displaystyle|\delta_{K}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u}-\pi^{0}_{h}{u},{v}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq(1+c_{1})\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u}-\pi^{0}_{K}{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\|{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|{u}-\pi^{0}_{h}{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\|{u}-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\big)\|{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}h\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(K)}\|{v}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

Here we used the assertions of Corollary
4.4
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the first step, and the projection error estimates of Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
in the last.
Scaling by the constant
\alpha
and summation over all elements leads to the assertion.
∎

4.3.
Estimates for the loss term

We now present a particular approximation property, which explains why the degree of approximation can be reduced on the edges in the set
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
satisfying assumption (A5) without decreasing the accuracy.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\lambda_{i}\in P_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
denote the barycentric coordinates defined by
\lambda_{i}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for all vertices
v_{j}
of the mesh.
To every edge
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we define

(19)

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\nabla\lambda_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi_{ji}=-\lambda_{j}\nabla\lambda_{i}.

These basis functions are linearly independent and
V_{h}=\operatorname{span}\{\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{ji}:e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\}
.
Hence the functions defined in (
19
)
comprise a basis for
V_{h}
.

The assertion follows immediately from the considerations in
[
1
,
29
]
.
In the subsequent analysis, we will also make use of the following norm equivalence.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let (A1) hold and let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, i.e.,
v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\mathsf{v}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
for appropriate coefficients
\mathsf{v}_{ij},\mathsf{v}_{ji}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Further let
\omega(e_{ij})=\bigcup_{K\cap e_{ij}=e_{ij}}K
denote the patch of elements containing the edge
e_{ij}
.
Then

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}^{2}\coloneqq\sum\nolimits_{e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\mathsf{v}_{ij}^{2}\|\Phi_{ij}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega(e_{ij}))}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}^{2}\|\Phi_{ji}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\omega(e_{ij}))}

defines a norm on
V_{h}
which is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. More precisely, one has

\displaystyle c_{1}^{\prime}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}\leq c_{2}^{\prime}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}

with uniform constants
c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime}>0
depending only on the bounds in assumption (A1).

The result again follows by scaling arguments and the equivalence of norms on finite-dimensional spaces.
Using these observations, we can now prove the following.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let (A1)–(A2) and (A5) hold.
Then for any
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{3}
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}u,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{v}_{h}-{v}_{h}\rangle\leq C\,h\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

with a uniform constant
C
depending only on the bounds in the assumptions.

Proof.

We start by considering a special test function
v_{h}=\Phi_{ij}
, where
\Phi_{ij}
is one of the basis functions introduced in Lemma
4.6
.
We then split

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}u,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma\pi_{\omega}^{0}u,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}\rangle+\langle\sigma(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}u-\pi^{0}_{\omega}u),\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}\rangle

(21)

\displaystyle=(i)+(ii),

where
\pi^{0}_{\omega}:L^{2}(\omega)\to P_{0}(\omega)
is the
L^{2}
-projection onto constants on the support
\omega=\omega(e_{ij})
of the basis function
\Phi_{ij}
.
If
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, we have
\Phi_{ij}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}
, which means that
(i)=0
in this case.
If
e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, on the other hand, then we deduce from assumptions (A2) and (A5) that
\sigma
is constant on the patch
\omega=\omega(e_{ij})
.
We can thus find a vector
b_{1}\in P_{1}(\omega)^{3}
such that

\operatorname{curl}b_{1}=\sigma\pi^{0}_{\omega}u
,
and evaluate

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle=\langle\operatorname{curl}b_{1},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}\rangle_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\langle b_{1},\operatorname{curl}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij})\rangle_{\omega}+\langle b_{1},n\times(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij})\rangle_{\partial\omega}=(iii)+(iv).

By elementary computations, see Lemma
5.1
below, one can verify that

(22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}=\nabla(\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}),\text{ and hence }\operatorname{curl}(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij})=0,

which in turn implies
(iii)=0
.
By definition of the basis functions, the tangential components of
\Phi_{ij}
and also that of
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}
vanish on
\partial\omega(e_{ij})
, unless
e_{ij}
is a boundary edge, which is excluded by assumption (A5).
Hence
(iv)=0
, and as a consequence, we see that
(i)=0
.
Since we assumed
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we may further bound

\displaystyle(ii)\leq C\,h\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}\|\Phi_{ij}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

by employing the estimates of Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
.
In summary, we thus have

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}u,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\Phi_{ij}-\Phi_{ij}\rangle\leq C\,h\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}\|\Phi_{ij}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

The same estimate is obtained for the basis functions
\Phi_{ji}
.
By Lemma
4.6
, any test function in
V_{h}
can be expanded as
v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}}\mathsf{v}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
, and by splitting and summing over all elements, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}u,\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}{v}_{h}-{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq Ch\sum\nolimits_{e_{ij}}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\omega(e_{ij}))}\left(\mathsf{v}_{ij}\|\Phi_{ij}\|_{L^{2}(\omega(e_{ij}))}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}\|\Phi_{ji}\|_{L^{2}(\omega(e_{ij}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\Big(\sum\nolimits_{e_{ij}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\omega(e_{ij}))}\Big)^{1/2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{h}\leq c^{\prime\prime}h\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Here we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the second step, the finite overlap of the patches, and the norm equivalence of Corollary
4.4
in the last.
∎

4.4.
Discrete stability

We now derive discrete stability estimates for solutions of Method
3.1
.
To simplify the presentation, we introduce the short-hand notation

\displaystyle\|{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\langle\alpha{v}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle,\qquad\|{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{h,\alpha}\coloneqq\langle\alpha{v}_{h},{v}_{h}\rangle_{h},

for non-negative piecewise constant parameters
\alpha\in P_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
Furthermore, we use

(23)

\displaystyle\widehat{u}_{h}^{\,n+1/2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{h}^{n}+u_{h}^{n+1})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{\tau}\widehat{u}_{h}^{\,n}=\tfrac{1}{2\tau}(u_{h}^{n+1}-u_{h}^{n-1}).

As a final ingredient, we introduce a discrete energy functional, defined by

(24)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{h}(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\|\partial_{\tau}u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\varepsilon}^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{u}_{h}^{\,n+1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{4}\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}-\tfrac{\tau}{2}\left(\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}-\|\partial_{\tau}u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}\right).

We begin with some elementary auxiliary observations.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let (A1)–(A2) and (A4) hold and
u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n+1}\in V_{h}
be given.
Then

(25)

\displaystyle\tfrac{2}{3}\mathscr{E}_{h}(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n+1})\leq\|\partial_{\tau}u_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\varepsilon}^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{u}_{h}^{\,n+1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}\leq 2\mathscr{E}_{h}(u_{h}^{n},u_{h}^{n+1}).

Proof.

The result follows directly from the definition of the energy functional and the CFL condition (A4), which was specifically tailored to obtain this result.
∎

We can now establish the required stability estimate for solutions of Method
3.1
.

Lemma 4.10

(Discrete stability)
.

Let (A1)–(A2) and (A4) hold.
Further let
\xi_{h}^{n}
,
{r}_{h}^{n}\in V_{h}
,
n\geq 0
be given such that

(26)

\displaystyle\langle(\varepsilon+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma)\partial_{\tau\tau}\xi_{h}^{n},v_{h}\rangle_{h}+\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}\rangle_{h}+\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}\xi_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h}\rangle=\langle r_{h}^{n},v_{h}\rangle,\qquad

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
n\geq 0
.
Then for all time steps
0\leq n<N
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{n},\xi_{h}^{n+1})\leq\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{0},\xi_{h}^{1})+2\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{n}\tau({r}_{h}^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}).

Proof.

We begin by setting
v_{h}=\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}:=\frac{1}{2\tau}(\xi_{h}^{n+1}-\xi_{h}^{n-1})
in the identity (
26
).
For the first term on the left-hand side, this results in

(27)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\partial_{\tau\tau}\xi_{h}^{n},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n})_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\varepsilon}^{2}-\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2}\|_{h,\varepsilon}^{2}\big).

For the third term in (
26
), we see in a similar manner that

(28)

\displaystyle(\nu\operatorname{curl}\xi_{h}^{n},\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\tau}\Big(\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n+1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}-\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n-1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad-\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{4}\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n+1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}+\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{4}\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2}\|^{2}_{\nu}\Big).

The loss terms involving
\sigma
have to be treated more carefully. Here we use that

(29)

\displaystyle\langle\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}\xi_{h}^{n},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle_{h}+\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma\partial_{\tau\tau}\xi_{h}^{n},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle_{h}-\langle\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma\partial_{\tau\tau}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\xi_{h}^{n},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle_{h}+\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\tau}{2}\tfrac{1}{2\tau}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}-\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}+\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n-1/2}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}\Big)+\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\|_{h,\sigma}^{2}.

By summing up the identities (
27
)–(
29
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{n},\xi_{h}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{n},\xi_{h}^{n+1})+2\tau\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\|_{\sigma}^{2}=\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{n-1},\xi_{h}^{n})+2\tau\langle{r}_{h}^{n},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,n}\rangle.

The assertion of the theorem then follows by induction over
n
.
∎

4.5.
Error estimates

Let us start with proving the estimate of Theorem
3.2
.
Following standard practice, we split

(30)

\displaystyle E(t^{n})-E_{h}^{n}=-(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E(t^{n})-E(t^{n}))+(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E(t^{n})-E_{h}^{n})\eqqcolon-\eta^{n}+\xi_{h}^{n},

into an interpolation error
\eta^{n}
and a discrete error component
\xi_{h}^{n}
.
The use of the particular projection
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
in this splitting will become important below.
With the interpolation error estimates of Lemma
4.1
, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\max\limits_{0\leq n<N}\Big(\|\partial_{\tau}\eta^{n+1/2}

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\eta}^{\,\,n+1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left(\|\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\operatorname{curl}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}\right).

For estimating the discrete error
\xi_{h}^{n}
, we first note that
\xi_{h}^{0}=0
.
Moreover,
E_{h}^{1}=0
by assumption, and by Taylor expansion, we further see that

\displaystyle E(\tau)

\displaystyle=E(0)+\tau\partial_{t}E(0)+\tfrac{\tau^{2}}{2}\partial_{tt}E(0)+\tfrac{\tau^{3}}{6}\partial_{ttt}E(s_{3}),

for some
0<s_{3}<\tau
.
From assumption (A3) and using equation (
1
), one can see that
E(0)=\partial_{t}E(0)=\partial_{tt}E(0)=0
, and hence

\xi_{h}^{1}=\frac{\tau^{3}}{6}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{ttt}E(s_{3})
.
Alternatively, we could also get
\xi_{h}^{1}=\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{tt}E(s_{2})
for some
0<s_{2}<\tau
by truncating the Taylor series earlier.
In summary, this yields

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tfrac{2}{3}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{0},\xi_{h}^{1})&\leq\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\;1/2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|\xi_{h}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\|\operatorname{curl}\xi_{h}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\tau^{4}\Big(\|\partial_{ttt}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}+\|\partial_{tt}E\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

For the last step, we used the formulas for
\xi_{h}^{0}
and
\xi_{h}^{1}
derived above, and the stability estimates
\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}}
and
\|\operatorname{curl}\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}}
for the projection.

As a next step, let us observe that the continuous solution
E(t)
of (
1
)–(
2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon\partial_{tt}{E}(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\sigma\partial_{t}{E}(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}{E}(t^{n}),\operatorname{curl}v_{h}\rangle=\langle f(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle+\langle g(t^{n}),v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}

for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
n>0
.
By combination with (
10
), one can then see that the discrete error
\xi_{h}^{n}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E(t^{n})-E_{h}^{n}
thus satisfies the error equation (
26
) with

\displaystyle\langle{r}_{h}^{n},v_{h}\rangle=\langle{r}_{h,t}^{n},v_{h}\rangle+\langle{r}_{h,s}^{n},v_{h}\rangle+\langle{r}_{h,q}^{n},v_{h}\rangle+\langle{r}_{h,p}^{n},v_{h}\rangle,

where the four partial residuals are defined by

\displaystyle\langle{r}_{h,t}^{n},v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varepsilon(\partial_{\tau\tau}E^{n}-\partial_{tt}E^{n}),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\sigma(\partial_{\tau}\widehat{E}^{n}-\partial_{t}E^{n}),v_{h}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle{r}_{h,s}^{n},v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varepsilon\partial_{tt}\eta^{n},v_{h}\rangle+\langle\sigma\partial_{t}\eta^{n},v_{h}\rangle+\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}\eta^{n},\operatorname{curl}v_{h}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle{r}_{h,q}^{n},v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{h}((\varepsilon+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\sigma)\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau\tau}E^{n},v_{h})+\delta_{h}(\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{n-1/2},v_{h}),

\displaystyle\langle{r}_{h,p}^{n},v_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\sigma\partial_{\tau}E^{\,n-1/2},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}-v_{h}\rangle,

which represent the temporal, spatial, quadrature, and projection errors, respectively.
In the third term, we again used
\delta_{h}(\alpha u_{h},v_{h})=\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle_{h}-\langle\alpha u_{h},v_{h}\rangle
to abbreviate the quadrature error.
We now estimate the four residuals independently.

First residual.

By summation over the time steps, we get

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle{r}_{h,t}^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle=\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau

\displaystyle\langle\varepsilon(\partial_{\tau\tau}E^{k}-\partial_{tt}E^{k}),\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle\sigma(\partial_{\tau}\widehat{E}^{k}-\partial_{t}E^{k}),\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle=(i)+(ii).

The first term in this expansion can be estimated by Taylor expansion, giving

\displaystyle|(i)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\nolimits_{k}c\tau^{2}\|\partial_{tttt}E\|_{L^{1}(t^{k-1},t^{k};L^{2})}\|\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{4}\|\partial_{tttt}E\|_{L^{1}(L^{2})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{56}\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{4}\|\partial_{tttt}E\|_{L^{1}(L^{2})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

With similar arguments, the second term can be bounded by

\displaystyle|(ii)|

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{4}\|\partial_{ttt}E\|_{L^{1}(L^{2})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1})

Second residual.

For the spatial errors, we use

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle{r}_{h,s}^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle\varepsilon\partial_{tt}\eta^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle+\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle\sigma\partial_{t}\eta^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}\eta^{k},\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle=(iii)+(iv)+(v).

By the interpolation error estimates of Lemma
4.1
and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|(iii)+(iv)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}(\|\partial_{t}E\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\partial_{tt}E\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))})+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

The factor
\tfrac{1}{28}
in the last term was simply chosen small enough for later on.
For the remaining term in the second residual, we use summation by parts to arrive at

\displaystyle|(v)|\leq\langle\operatorname{curl}

\displaystyle\eta^{n},\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\;n+1/2}\rangle-\langle\operatorname{curl}\eta^{1},\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\;1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{k}\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}\partial_{\tau}\eta^{k-1/2},\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\;k-1/2}\rangle=(vi)+(vii)+(viii).

By the interpolation error estimates and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|(vi)+(vii)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\operatorname{curl}{E}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\tfrac{1}{56}\|\operatorname{curl}\widehat{\xi}_{h}\|^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\operatorname{curl}{E}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

By Taylor expansion and arguments similar to before, we further obtain

\displaystyle|(viii)|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\operatorname{curl}\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

The prefactors in these estimates were again simply chosen sufficiently small.

Third residual.

Using Lemma
4.5
and Taylor expansion in time, we can estimate the residuals caused by the quadrature errors via

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle{r}_{h,q}^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}(\|\partial_{tt}E\|^{2}_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\partial_{t}E\|^{2}_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))})+\tfrac{1}{56}\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}\|^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}(\|\partial_{tt}E\|^{2}_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))}+\|\partial_{t}E\|^{2}_{L^{1}(H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}))})+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

Fourth residual.

For the projection errors, we again use Lemma
4.8
and obtain

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle{r}_{h,p}^{k},\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{k}\tau\langle\sigma\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{\,k-1/2},\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}-\partial_{\tau}\widehat{\xi}_{h}^{\,k}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{56}\|\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}\|^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\|\partial_{t}E\|_{L^{1}(H^{1})}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}_{h}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1}).

Let us note that the appearance of the projection
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
in the residual
r_{h,p}^{k}
was essential here, in order to be able to apply Lemma
4.8
.

Conclusion.

By summation of all the individual estimates for the respective residuals and application of Lemma
4.10
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\xi_{h}^{n},\xi_{h}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{E}(\xi_{h}^{0},\xi_{h}^{1})+2\left(7\cdot\tfrac{1}{28}\max_{0\leq k<N}\mathscr{E}(\xi_{h}^{k},\xi_{h}^{k+1})+C(E)h^{2}+C^{\prime}(E)\tau^{2}\right).

Taking the maximum over all
n
, using (
25
), and the bound (
31
) for
\mathscr{E}(\xi_{h}^{0},\xi_{h}^{1})
, we thus obtain the required estimates for the discrete error component. By combination with the interpolation error estimate, we arrive at the estimate of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Estimate of Theorem
3.3

We now split the corresponding error by

\displaystyle E(t^{n})-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E_{h}^{n}=-(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E(t^{n})-E(t^{n}))+(\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E(t^{n})-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E_{h}^{n})=:-\eta^{n}+\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{n}

The first term is the same as before, and for the discrete error, we can use the following arguments: By the commuting diagram property of the projectors, we see that
\operatorname{curl}\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{n}=\operatorname{curl}\xi_{h}^{n}
, which allows using the bounds of the previous proof to handle the
\operatorname{curl}
-terms in the estimate of the discrete error
\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{n}
.
For the
L^{2}
-terms, we use

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{\xi}_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}(\Pi_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}-\partial_{\tau}E_{h}^{n+1/2})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Pi_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}-\partial_{\tau}E_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\widetilde{\Pi}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}-\partial_{\tau}E_{h}^{n+1/2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Pi_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}-\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}\|_{L^{2}}).

In the first inequality, we made use of the bound
\|\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}
, which follows by the usual scaling arguments.
The first term in the above estimate only involves the discrete error
\partial_{\tau}\xi_{h}^{n+1/2}=\widetilde{\Pi}\partial_{\tau}E^{n+1/2}-\partial_{\tau}E_{h}^{n+1/2}
, which was already analyzed before, and the second term can be bounded by the interpolation error estimates for the two projectors.
This then already yields the estimate of Theorem
3.3
.
∎

5.
Algebraic properties

We now discuss the implementation of the proposed methods and the algebraic properties stated in Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
.
Let us recall the basis functions

\displaystyle\Phi_{ij}=\lambda_{i}\nabla\lambda_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi_{ji}=-\lambda_{j}\nabla\lambda_{i}

associated with the edges
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. By basic computations, one can see the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}}\mathsf{v}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}\in V_{h}
be given.
Then

(32)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\mathsf{v}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}+\sum_{e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathsf{v}_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji})(\Phi_{ij}+\Phi_{ji}).

Hence
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}
with coefficients given by
\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}\coloneqq\mathsf{v}_{ij}
,
\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ji}\coloneqq\mathsf{v}_{ji}
for the edges
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
and
\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}=\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ji}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\mathsf{v}_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji})
for the edges
e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
.

For the following considerations, we always assume that the degrees of freedom are sorted edge-wise.
This allows us to make the following statements.

Lemma 5.2
.

The equation (
10
) is equivalent to the algebraic system (
11
) with

\displaystyle[\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}]_{ij,kl}

\displaystyle=\langle(\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2)\Phi_{kl},\Phi_{ij}\rangle_{h},\qquad

\displaystyle[\mathsf{f}^{n}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle f(t^{n}),\Phi_{ij}\rangle,

\displaystyle[\mathsf{K}_{\nu}]_{ij,kl}

\displaystyle=\langle\nu\operatorname{curl}\Phi_{kl},\operatorname{curl}\Phi_{ij}\rangle,\qquad

\displaystyle[\mathsf{g}^{n}]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\langle g(t^{n}),\Phi_{ij}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}.

Both matrices are sparse, and
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
is block diagonal, with one block per vertex of the mesh coupling all degrees of freedom not vanishing at this vertex.
Furthermore

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}=\mathsf{Q}^{\top}\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\mathsf{Q}\qquad\text{with}\qquad[\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}]_{ij,kl}=(\sigma\Phi_{kl},\Phi_{ij})_{h},

and the projection matrix
\mathsf{Q}
is block diagonal with
2\times 2
blocks of the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

for the edges
e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
and
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, respectively.

The assertions follow immediately from the properties of the basis functions; for details, see
[
9
,
31
]
.
This already proves the second claim of Theorem
3.2
. ∎

Algebraic reduction

We next derive the reduced scheme (
12
). To do so, we start with the following observation, which again follows from elementary arguments.

Lemma 5.3
.

The function
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}
can be expressed equivalently as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}=\sum_{e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}\Phi_{ij}+\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ji}\Phi_{ji}+\sum_{e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij},\qquad\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}=\Phi_{ij}+\Phi_{ji},

with
\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}=\mathsf{v}_{ij}
,
\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ji}=\mathsf{v}_{ji}
for
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathsf{v}_{ij}+\mathsf{v}_{ji})
for
e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, and the collection
\{\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{ji}:e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\}\cup\{\widetilde{\Phi}_{ij}:=\Phi_{ij}+\Phi_{ji}):e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\}
is a basis for
\widetilde{V}_{h}
.

Also for the reduced space, the degrees of freedom are sorted edgewise.
Then the relation between the coefficients
\mathsf{v}_{ij}
,
\widehat{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}_{ij}
can be expressed as follows.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
be the block-diagonal (prolongation) matrix with blocks

(33)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

for edges
e_{ij}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
and
e_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, respectively.
Then
\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{P})^{-1}\mathsf{P}^{\top}
and
\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{P}
is diagonal with entries
1/2
and
1
, respectively.
Furthermore,

(34)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{v}}=\mathsf{P}\,\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}=\mathsf{R}\,\mathsf{v}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{R}=(\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{P})^{-1}\mathsf{P}^{\top}.

This lemma allows us to express the coefficients of
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}v_{h}
in the basis of
\widetilde{V}_{h}
by the coefficients of the expansion in the basis of
V_{h}
.
With the help of this result, we can now derive the algebraic form (
12
) of the reduced scheme of Theorem
3.3
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(\mathsf{E}^{n})_{n}
be a solution of (
11
). Then
\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{E}^{n}
satisfies (
12
) with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\mathsf{R}^{\top},\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}=\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\mathsf{P},\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}=\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{K}_{\nu}\mathsf{P}

and right-hand sides

(35)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\mathsf{f}^{n}\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\mathsf{g}^{n},

where
\mathsf{f}^{n},\mathsf{g}^{n}
are defined as in Lemma
5.2
.
Hence, the projection
\widetilde{E}_{h}^{n}=\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}E_{h}^{n}
of the solution of Method
3.1
is given by (
12
).

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}
is regular, the scheme (
11
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}\mathsf{E}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\left(-\widehat{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\mathsf{E}^{n-1/2}-\mathsf{K}_{\nu}\mathsf{E}^{n}+\mathsf{f}^{n}+\mathsf{g}^{n}\right).

We further multiply this equation from the left by
\mathsf{R}^{\top}=\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{P})^{-1}
and note that
\mathsf{P}\mathsf{R}=\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{P})^{-1}\mathsf{P}^{\top}=\mathsf{Q}
, which follows from the definition of the matrices.
We see

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}=\mathsf{R}^{\top}\mathsf{P}^{\top}\mathsf{M}_{\sigma}\mathsf{P}\mathsf{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{K}_{\nu}=\mathsf{Q}^{\top}\mathsf{K}_{\nu}\mathsf{Q}=\mathsf{R}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}\mathsf{R},

where we used the algebraic form of the commuting diagram property of the projection
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}
, see (
22
).
Using the definitions of
\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}
, and
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}
, we thus conclude that

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}=\partial_{\tau\tau}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{E}^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{R}\mathsf{M}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}(-\mathsf{R}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\mathsf{R}\,\partial_{\tau}\mathsf{E}^{n-1/2}-\mathsf{R}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{E}^{n}+\mathsf{f}^{n}+\mathsf{g}^{n})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}(-\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n-1/2}-\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n})+\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}+\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}

This already yields the algebraic form given in (
12
) and concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The choice (
35
) for the right-hand sides
\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}
in the reduced method makes use of the vectors
\mathsf{f}^{n}
,
\mathsf{g}^{n}
with two degrees of freedom for each edge.
An alternative and more direct choice would be

(36)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{F}}^{n}:=\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{f}}^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}^{n}:=\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}^{n},

which now only depends on the vectors
\widetilde{\mathsf{f}}^{n}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}^{n}
assembled on the reduced finite element space
\widetilde{V}_{h}
.
This modification can be included in our analysis, if one replaces assumption (A5) by choosing
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
such that

(A5
∗
)

\sigma
and
f
are continuous across edges
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
lying in the interior
\Omega
, and
\sigma=0
,
g=0
for all edges
e\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

This allows us to apply a variant of Lemma
4.8
which covers the additional consistency error introduced by the representation of

; the boundary term
g
is already fully covered by the original assumption (A5) since it vanishes along all edges where no reduction is applied.
The time-stepping scheme resulting from this modified choice of the right-hand sides can be stated as

(37)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}=\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\varepsilon+\tau\sigma/2}^{-1}(-\widetilde{\mathsf{M}}_{\sigma}\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n-1/2}-\widetilde{\mathsf{K}}_{\nu}\widetilde{\mathsf{E}}^{n}+\widetilde{\mathsf{f}}^{n}+\widetilde{\mathsf{g}}^{n}),

and used as an alternative to (
12
).
In our numerical tests, we will compare the two versions (
12
) and (
37
), in particular, also highlighting the importance of the additional conditions in assumption (A5
∗
) for the latter.

7.
Discussion

In this paper, we proposed and analyzed finite-element schemes which can be seen as a natural extension of the Yee scheme to unstructured grids and inhomogeneous lossy media.
While the number of degrees of freedom can be reduced to one for almost all edges, the necessity incorporation of two degrees of freedom at interfaces or boundaries with material jumps has been illustrated.
A full convergence analysis of the schemes could be provided and optimal convergence could be proven under reasonable assumptions.
Alternative extensions of the Yee scheme to triangular and tetrahedral elements based on dual cell complexes have been proposed previously in
[
4
,
5
,
19
]
.
While in some settings, the algebraic form of these methods is similar or even identical to ours, the constructions in these papers follow a rather geometric approach which complicates the error analysis.
The results obtained in our paper may be useful to gain further insight also into these related methods.

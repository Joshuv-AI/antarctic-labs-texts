C 2 C^{2} estimates for k k -Hessian equations and a rigidity theorem
RUIJIA ZHANG

Abstract
. We derive a concavity inequality for k k -Hessian operators under the semi-convexity condition. As an application, we establish interior estimates for semiconvex solutions to the k k -Hessian equations with vanishing Dirichlet boundary conditions and obtain a Liouville-type result. Additionally, we provide new and simple proofs of Guan-Ren-Wang’s work [ 12 ] on global curvature estimates for k k -curvature equations.

1.
introduction

In this paper, we consider the following
k
-Hessian equations with Dirichlet boundary

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)&=f(x,u,\nabla u)\quad&\text{in}\ &\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&\text{on}\ &\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f
is a positive function in
\overline{\Omega}\times R\times R^{n}
,
u
is a function defined in
\overline{\Omega}
. Here we denote by
\nabla u
and

the gradient of
u
and the Hessian of
u
, respectively.

\sigma_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined as

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(x)=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}...<i_{k}\leqslant n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

Let
\lambda
be the eigenvalues of
D^{2}u
. Then the
k
-Hessian operator of
u
is defined by
\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda(D^{2}))
.

Research on the
k
-Hessian equation

(1.2)

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=f(x,u,\nabla u)

is a vital area within PDE and geometric analysis. We note that (
1.2
) simplifies to a semilinear equation when
k=1
. For
2\leqslant k\leqslant n

estimates for the Monge-Ampére equations, as referred to in
[
10
,
17
]
. For general

, Chou-Wang
[
7
]
established the Pogorelov estimates for
k
-convex solutions of the
k
-Hessian equation

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)&=f(x,u)\quad&\text{in}\ &\Omega\\
u&=0\quad&\text{on}\ &\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f
is independent of
\nabla u
. They proved that

\displaystyle(-u)^{\beta}\Delta u\leqslant C

for some constants
\beta>1
and
C>0
depending on
n
,
k
,
\inf f
,
\|f\|_{C^{1,1}}
,
\|u\|_{C^{1}}
but independent of
\Omega
. Following
[
3
]
, we define the
k
-convex function by :
For a domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a function

is called k-convex (or

-admissible) if the eigenvalues
\lambda(X)
of Hessian

are in
\Gamma_{k}
(defined by (

. We note that for all
k
-convex functions

, the
k
-Hessian operator is elliptic, i.e.,

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{k}^{ij}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial u_{ij}}

is positive-definite. In the following text, we consider the
k
-Hessian equation in this realm.

We extend Theorem 1.5 by Chou-Wang
[

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(D^{2}u(x))\geqslant-A,\quad\text{for all }x\in\Omega

Assume that

Remark 1.1

Using this class of interior estimates, we further derive a rigidity theorem for entire solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=1.

It has long been a question of concern whether an entire function

in
n

1.4
) is quadratic. For
k=1
, the Liouville property of harmonic functions implies rigidity results for solutions with bounded Hessian. For

[

provided an alternative geometric proof.
For

[
4

proved that the entire solution to

is quadratic if

\displaystyle D^{2}u\geqslant\delta-\sqrt{\frac{2}{n(n-1)}}I

for any

, the authors also guess that the rigidity theorem holds true under the semi-convexity assumption

\displaystyle D^{2}u\geqslant-AI

with arbitrarily large constant

. Warren
[
24

constructed a class of nontrivial solutions to (

[
20

obtained the rigidity theorem for

k
-Hessian equation remains open.
A function

is said to have quadratic growth if there exists constants

and sufficient large

(1.5)

\displaystyle u(x)\geqslant c|x|^{2}-b,\quad\text{for }\ |x|\geqslant R.

In this paper, we confirm Chang-Yuan’s conjecture under the quadratic growth condition for general

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
2<k\leqslant n-1

be any semi-convex entire function in

with

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=1

and exhibits quadratic growth. Then

is a quadratic polynomial.

Assuming a quadratic growth condition, Bao-Chen-Guan-Ji

showed that strictly convex entire solutions to (

[

]
extended the convexity assumption to

-convexity, and Chu-Dinew
[
8
]
further relaxed the condition to

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k+1}>-A

for some

(see also
[

]
).
In the following text, we note that using the lemma

if
\sigma_{k+1}>-A

\lambda_{i}>-C(n,k,\sigma_{k},A)
, which means that Theorem
1.2
implies all these results.

To establish the curvature estimates and derive the rigidity theorem, the main difficulty to overcome is how to deal with the third-order derivatives. It is well known that
\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}
is concave in the cone
\Gamma_{k}
, which is very useful to deal with these terms. It should be noted that Ren and Wang
[
18
]
established the concavity inequality of the Hessian operator for
k=n-1
. They proved that

Theorem 1.3
.

[
18
]

Given two positive constants
\delta<1
,
\varepsilon
, for any index
i
, if
\kappa_{i}\geqslant\delta\kappa_{1}
and the positive constant
K
depending only on
\delta
and
\varepsilon
is sufficient large, then we have

(1.8)

\kappa_{i}[K(\sigma_{n-1})_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{n-1}^{pp,qq}h_{ppi}h_{qqi}]-\sigma^{ii}_{n-1}h_{iii}^{2}+(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{j\neq i}\sigma_{n-1}^{jj}h_{jji}^{2}\geqslant 0.

In
[
18
]
, they also pointed out that the inequality (
1.8
) is not true even if
i=1
,
\sigma_{k}=1
and
k_{1}
are large enough for
k<n-1
. In addition, Guan and Qiu
[

established the interior

\sigma_{2}
-Hessian equation under the assumption
\sigma_{3}\geqslant-A
for some constant

\sigma_{3}\geqslant-A
and
\kappa_{1}
large enough

(1.9)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i\neq j}u_{ii1}u_{jj1}+C\frac{(\dot{\sigma}_{2}^{ii}u_{ii1})^{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\geqslant(1+\delta_{0})\frac{\dot{\sigma}_{2}^{11}u_{111}^{2}}{u_{11}}

for some constant
C>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
. (

) is basically the similar form of (
1.8
) and is derived by using Chen’s
[

]
work on the optimal concavity of the

operators with the eigenvector decomposition method. We note that the method makes sense only for

since the Hessian operator is a known matrix. It is difficult to generalize this method to other cases. Moreover, it reminds us that the concavity inequality of the general

-Hessian operator may also hold under some additional convexity assumption. we develop a method based on
[

by Guan-Ren-Wang and

Lemma 1.1
.

Assume
\{\lambda_{i}\}\in\Gamma_{k},

\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n},
and
\lambda_{n}>-A
for a constant
A>0
. Then there exists some constant
C>0
depending on
n
,
k
,
\sigma_{k}
,
A
and small constant
\delta_{0}>0
depending on
k
such that if
\lambda_{1}\geqslant C
, then the following inequality holds

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\sum_{p\neq q}\frac{\sigma_{k}^{pp,qq}\xi_{p}\xi_{q}}{\sigma_{k}}+K\frac{(\sum_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ii}\xi_{i})^{2}}{\sigma_{k}^{2}}+2\sum_{i>1}\frac{\sigma_{k}^{ii}\xi_{i}^{2}}{(\lambda_{1}+A+1)\sigma_{k}}\geqslant(1+\delta_{0})\frac{\dot{\sigma}_{k}^{11}\xi_{1}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}\sigma_{k}}

for some sufficient large
K>0
(depending on
\delta_{0}
) where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
is an arbitrary vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The lemma
2.2
implies that if
\sigma_{k+1}>-A,

\lambda_{i}>-C(n,k,\sigma_{k},A)

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Symmetric function

Here we state some algebraic properties of the elementary symmetric function
\sigma_{m}(\lambda)
,
m=1,\cdots,n
, where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
. We recall
\sigma_{m}(\lambda)
defined by

\sigma_{m}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leqslant n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{m}},\quad m=1,2,\cdots,n.

The Garding cone
\Gamma_{m}
is an open symmetric convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with vertex at the origin, given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m}=\{\left(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\forall j=1,\cdots,m\}.

Clearly
\sigma_{m}(\lambda)=0
for
\lambda\in\partial\overline{\Gamma}_{m}
and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}\subset\cdots\subset{\Gamma}_{m}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma_{1}.

In particular,
\Gamma^{+}=\Gamma_{n}
is called the positive cone,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{+}=\{\left(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\lambda_{1}>0,\cdots,\lambda_{n}>0\}.

Always assume
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}
. We collect some properties of
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
where
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
:

(1)

\sum_{p=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{p}}\lambda_{p}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}
.

(2)

\sum_{p=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{p}}=(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}.

(3)

\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{n}}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{1}}>0.

(4)

\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{1}}\lambda_{1}\geqslant\frac{k}{n}\sigma_{k}.

(5)

q_{k}:=\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k-1}}
is concave, i.e.,
\partial^{2}_{\xi}q_{k}\leqslant 0
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(6)

For any
s<k
,
\sigma_{s}>\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{s}
.

We point out that we will denote
C
,
c
or
C_{i}
and
c_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
as some positive constants throughout the subsequent sections, and these constants may change from line to line.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
\{\lambda_{i}\}\in\Gamma_{k}
and
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}
. Then
\lambda_{k}>0
and
|\lambda_{n}|<(n-k)\lambda_{k}.

Proof.

From Property (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{1}\cdots\partial\lambda_{k-1}}=\lambda_{k}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}>0.

Suppose that only
\lambda_{n}
is negative, then
(n-k)\lambda_{k}>|\lambda_{n}|
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
\{\lambda_{i}\}\in\Gamma_{k}
,
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}
, and
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
are bounded from above and below. If
\sigma_{k+1}>-A
for any positive constant A, then
\lambda
is semiconvex, i.e.,
\lambda_{\min}>-C(n,k,\sigma_{k},A)
.

Proof.

There exists constant
C>0
, such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{n}}\lambda_{n}^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}<C\sigma_{1}+(k+1)A.

Then by Property (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{n}^{2}<nC\sigma_{1}+n(k+1)A.

Combining
(n-k+1)\sigma_{k-1}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}
and Property (
6
), Lemma
2.1
, there exists constant
C(n,k)>0

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(n,k)|\lambda_{n}|^{k+1}\leqslant C(n,k)|\lambda_{n}|^{k}\lambda_{1}<C\lambda_{1}+n(k+1)A.

Thus, we get
|\lambda_{n}|<C(n,k,\sigma_{k},A)
by Lemma
2.1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\{\lambda_{i}\}\in\Gamma_{k}
and
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}
. If
\lambda_{n}>-A
, then
\lambda_{k}<C(\sigma_{k},n,A).

Proof.

Assume that
\lambda_{k-1}>A
, otherwise
\lambda_{k}\leqslant A
, then we are done. Since

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}\leqslant i_{2}...\leqslant i_{k}\leqslant n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}>0.

We have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}\lambda_{k}\leqslant\sigma_{k}-C(n,k)\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}\lambda_{n}.

Thus

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{\sigma_{k}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}+2C(n,k)|\lambda_{n}|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{n\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}+2C(n,k)A

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c(n,k)\sigma_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}+C(n,k,A).

Here we use property (
6
) in the second inequality and the last inequality follows from Maclaurin’s inequality. Thus, there exists some constant
C>0
, such that
\lambda_{k}\leqslant C
, which depends on the bounds of
\sigma_{k}
,
n
and
A
.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\{\lambda_{i}\}\in\Gamma_{k}
and
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}
, then
\sigma_{k-1}\geqslant C\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
.

Proof.

Using Newton inequality and Property (
6
), we have

(2.5)

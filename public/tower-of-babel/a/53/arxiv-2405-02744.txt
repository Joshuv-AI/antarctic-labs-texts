Equivariant geometry of singular cubic threefolds, II
Ivan Cheltsov, Lisa Marquand, Yuri Tschinkel, Zhijia Zhang

Abstract
. We study linearizability of actions of finite groups on cubic threefolds with nonnodal isolated singularities.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 14E07, 14L30

Introduction

Among the central problems in birational geometry is the
linearizability
problem, as
well as the closely related

rationality problem
.
The first is about identifying regular actions of finite groups
G
on algebraic varieties which are equivariantly birational to actions of
G
on
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
, where
V
is a representation of
G

G
is the trivial group, i.e., identifying varieties birational to projective space. These questions can be asked over the complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
, or arbitrary ground fields. One of the distinguishing features of this research is the rich interplay between arithmetic and geometric aspects.

In this paper, we focus on
linearizability
and

of actions of finite groups on singular
cubic threefolds
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}

G
-action on

is stably linearizable if the action on

is linearizable, with

acting trivially on the second factor.

We extend our investigations of the

in

6
]

[

]
to cover the remaining cases of isolated singularities.
We rely on the recent classification of such singularities in

In detail, we only consider situations when the

-action is linearizable via projection from this point. Under this assumption, in the nonnodal case,

2.
The Defect

In this section, we compute the defect

d(X):=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathrm{Cl}(X)-1,

where
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
is the class group,
for cubic threefolds
X
with specified combinations of singularities, using the projection method. The proofs follow closely those in
[
17
, §4]
, however, we feel that the presentation will be useful for the reader. We always project from the worst singularity
q\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)
, the singular locus of
X
.

Projection method

We review the projection method outlined in
[
14
]
(see also
[
17
]
): fix

q\in\mathrm{Sing}(X)

and choose coordinates so that
q=[1:0:0:0:0]
. Then
X
is given by

(2.1)

x_{1}f_{2}(x_{2},\dots,x_{5})+f_{3}(x_{2},\dots,x_{5})=0,

where
f_{2},f_{3}
are homogeneous, of degree 2 and 3, respectively.
Projection from
q
gives a birational map
X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
, factoring as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}_{q}X\cong\mathrm{Bl}_{C_{q}}{\mathbb{P}}^{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}^{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}

This yields

\displaystyle Q_{q}:=\{f_{2}(x_{2},\dots,x_{5})=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3},

\displaystyle S_{q}:=\{f_{3}(x_{2},\dots,x_{5})=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3},

\displaystyle C_{q}:=Q_{q}\cap S_{q}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{3}.

The curve
C_{q}
parameterizes lines contained in
X
passing through
q
. Recall that by
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
, the singularities of
C_{q}
correspond to that of
X
away from
q
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
18
, Theorem 1.2]

Consider a singular point
p\in C_{q}
, and assume
S_{q}
is smooth.

(1)

If
Q_{q}
is smooth at
p
, then
X
has a unique singular point on the line
\langle p,q\rangle
other than
q
, and the singularity has the same type.

(2)

If
Q_{q}
is singular at
p
, then the only singular point of
X
on
\langle p,q\rangle
is
q
, and the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{q}X
has a singular point of the same type as
p\in C
on
\phi^{-1}(q)|_{E}
for
\phi
as above.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with singularities as above. Then:

(1)

If
q
is a
{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
-point, then
Q_{q}
is the union of two planes and

d(X)=(\#\text{ irreducible components of }C_{q})-2;

(2)

If

is an
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
-point with

then
Q_{q}
is a quadric cone and

d(X)=(\#\text{ irreducible components of }C_{q})-1.

(3)

If
q
is an
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
-point with

then in addition

passes through the cone point of

and has an
{\mathsf{A}}_{n-2}
-singularity there.

Proof.

The statements about

are a combination of
[
17
, Claim A.10, A.11]
. The formula for the defect is
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be a cubic threefold. Then

, when

-singularities, for

(2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+m{\mathsf{A}}_{1})
-singularities, for

(3{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1})

Projecting from

-singularities. It follows that

is an irreducible

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

be a cubic with
(4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{2})

Then

(1)

(2)

the line containing the
2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}

Let

{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
-point. By

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a cubic with
2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities. Then

(1)

d(X)=3
, and the extra generators of

are planes,

(2)

there is a unique plane
\Pi\subset X
containing all four nodes,

(3)

each
{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
-point is contained in two planes, each containing two other nodes,

(4)

the line containing the
2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
-points is disjoint from
\Pi
.

Proof.

We project from an
{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
-point
q\in X
. By
[

, Proposition 4.8, corrected version]
, we see that

, where
A_{1},A_{2}
are two distinct lines in the ruling of the quadric cone
Q_{q}
, and
B_{1},B_{2}
are two hyperplane sections of
Q_{q}
not passing through the cone point and tangent to each other at

.
The computation of
d(X)
follows from Lemma
2.2
.

We see that there are two planes containing

; namely, the cones over
A_{1},A_{2}
with vertex

: indeed,

has a node at each of the points

, and thus by, Theorem

Note that the plane

Finally, let

.
Indeed, suppose that

.
Then there exists a hyperplane section

that contains

. Note that

must split as a union of

and two other planes such that both of them contain

, and each of them contains two nodes of

3.
Automorphisms

In this section, we explain how to classify automorphisms
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
of singular cubics
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
, with
s
singular points. By convention, the action on the variety is from the right, and the action on the function field from the left. In coordinates, given

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, we put

\sigma({\bf x})=\sigma((x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}))=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\cdot M_{\sigma},

where
M_{\sigma}\in\mathsf{GL}_{5}
is the corresponding matrix.

Throughout, we assume that
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
does not fix any singular points,
since otherwise, the action is linearizable. Let
H
be the maximal subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
which fixes all singular points.
The
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-action preserves the singular locus,
yielding an exact sequence

0\to H\to\mathrm{Aut}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathfrak{S}}_{s}.

The image of
\rho
reflects the singularity type, for example, when
X
has
(2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1})
-singularities, the image is contained in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{2}
.

The algorithm for classifying
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
involves the following steps:

•

Find a
normal form
of
X
, based on the singularity type. This amounts to fixing appropriate coordinates and simplifying the equation.

•

Determine possible images of
\rho
, and find all lifts to
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, depending on parameters in the equation of
X
.

•

For each lift, determine
H
.

Here, we explain the process in a simple case, when
X
is the unique cubic with

3{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
-singularities, see
[
1
, Theorem 5.4]
:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle X:=\{x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+x_{4}^{3}+x_{5}^{3}=0\}

be the unique cubic threefold with
3{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
-singularities.
Then

\mathrm{Aut}(X)=\langle\tau_{a,b},\eta,\sigma_{(45)},\sigma_{(123)},\sigma_{(12)}\rangle\simeq({\mathbb{G}}^{2}_{m}(k)\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3})\rtimes{\mathfrak{S}}_{3},

where

\displaystyle\tau_{a,b}:(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mapsto(ax_{1},bx_{2},a^{-1}b^{-1}x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}),\quad a,b\in k^{\times},

\displaystyle\eta:(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\zeta_{3}x_{4},\zeta_{3}^{2}x_{5}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{(45)}:(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{4}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{(123)}:(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{3},x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{(12)}:(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{2},x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}).

In particular,
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
generate the first
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
-factor and
\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(123)}
generate the second
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
-factor in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

Proof.

Observe that
\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(123)}\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, and that
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acts transitively on the three singular points. It remains to find the subgroup
H\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
fixing all three singular points.
Based on the form of the equation, we see that
an
h\in H
takes the form

(\mathbf{x})\mapsto(s_{1}x_{1},s_{2}x_{2},s_{3}x_{3},a_{1}x_{4}+a_{2}x_{5},a_{3}x_{4}+a_{4}x_{5}),

for
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in k
. There exists an exact sequence

0\to H^{\prime}\to H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}\mathsf{PGL}_{2},

where
\psi
is the projection of the
H
-action onto
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{x_{4},x_{5}}
, given by the coordinates
x_{4},x_{5}
.
The equation of
X
implies that
\psi(H)
leaves invariant three points defined by

\{x_{4}^{3}+x_{5}^{3}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{x_{4},x_{5}}.

The maximal subgroup of
\mathsf{PGL}_{2}
leaving these three points invariant is
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
. To show that
\psi(H)={\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, one can check that
\eta,\sigma_{(45)}\in H
and their images in
\mathsf{PGL}_{2}(k)
generate
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
. On the other hand, elements in
\tau\in H^{\prime}
are diagonal of the form

\tau:(\mathbf{x})\mapsto(s_{1}x_{1},s_{2}x_{2},s_{3}x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}).

One can check that

∎

4.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with
{\mathsf{A}}{\mathsf{D}}{\mathsf{E}}

\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-equivariant resolution of singularities; it can be achieved via a sequence of blowups, where at each step we blow up the necessarily

Here we consider the induced
G

and
\mathrm{Cl}(X)

is linearizable, then the

\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X})
is a

[
6

.
If the cohomology groups

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X})),\text{ or }{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X})^{\vee})

\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{X})
fails to be a stably permutation module. We call this the
(\bf{H1})
-obstruction to linearizability.
This is also an obstruction to

, i.e., linearizability of

We refer the reader to
[
6
,
5
, Section 2]
for applications.

The following proposition shows that the only possible combinations of nonnodal singularities with

-obstructions are

2{\mathsf{A}}_{5},\quad 2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad 3{\mathsf{D}}_{4}.

Proposition 6.6

is a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree 6, realized as

is
H
-equivariant. The

-action on

and the 2-dimensional torus action. The

.

The following
G

for general values of parameters of the corresponding families:

(1)

2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
, and
G=\langle\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(12)(34)}\rangle
, from Proposition
7.1
,

(2)

2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
,
d(X)=2
, and
G=\langle\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(12)(34)}\rangle
, from Proposition

,

(3)

2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
, and
G=\langle\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(12)(34)}\rangle
,
from Proposition
7.6
,

(4)

4{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
, and
G=\langle\sigma_{(12)},\sigma_{(12)(34)}\rangle
in the cases when
r_{2}=r_{3}
from Proposition
7.9
,

(5)

4{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
, and
G=\langle\eta,\sigma_{(234)},\sigma_{(12)(34)}\rangle\simeq C_{3}\times{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}

.

Proof.

In Cases (1)–(4), we are in the situation of Proposition
5.16
: the involution
\sigma_{(12)}
gives rise to a Burnside symbol of the form

(7.13)

(\langle\sigma_{(12)}\rangle,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(S),(1))\in\mathrm{Burn}_{3}(G),

where
S\subset X
is a cubic surface. The residual
Y
-action on

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(Y,\mathrm{Pic}(\tilde{S}))=({\mathbb{Z}}/2)^{2},

by
[
4
]
,
i.e., the symbol is incompressible. Moreover, linear actions do not contribute such symbols.

In Case (5), we have an incompressible symbol

(C_{3},{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(S^{\prime}),(\zeta_{3})),

where
S^{\prime}
is the Cayley cubic surface (unique cubic surface with 4 nodes). The
{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}
-action on

is birational to the linear
{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
This symbol is incompressible, appears in the class
[X\righttoleftarrow G]

with multiplicity one,
and distinguishes the given
G
-action on

from a linear action,
as in
[
6
, Remark 6.4]
.
∎

8.
Five Singular points

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with five singular points. Under our assumptions, we need to consider the following combinations of singularities:

2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\quad 2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\quad 3{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\quad 5{\mathsf{A}}_{2},\quad 2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1}.

We adapt the argument in
[
5
, §6]
, which handles
5{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities. First note that if the singularities are not in linearly general position, then there is a distinguished
G
-fixed singular point, and the
G
-action on
X
is linearizable. Thus we can assume that the singular points of
X
are

p_{1}=[1:0:0:0:0],\quad p_{2}=[0:1:0:0:0],\quad p_{3}=[0:0:1:0:0],

p_{4}=[0:0:0:1:0],\quad p_{5}=[0:0:0:0:1].

Automorphisms
\mathrm{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{4},5)
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
respecting these points fit into the exact sequence:

0\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{4}(k)\to\mathrm{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{4},5)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\to}}{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}\to 0.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with at most
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
-singularities and
\mathrm{Sing}(X)=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}\}
. Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
be a finite subgroup acting intransitively on
\mathrm{Sing}(X).
Then the
G
-action on
X
is linearizable.

Proof.

If
G
fixes a singular points, it is linearizable via projection. It suffices to show linearizability when
\rho(G)=C_{2}\times C_{3}
or
C_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, i.e., when
G
preserves the set
\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
and
\{p_{3},p_{4},p_{5}\}.
In these cases, we can find an element
\sigma\in G
such that
\rho(\sigma)=(1,2)(3,4,5)
. By conjugation under the torus action, one may assume that
\sigma
permutes the coordinates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}
as the cycle
(1,2)(3,4,5)
. The
\langle\sigma\rangle
-invariant cubic threefolds with only
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
-singularities are given by

(8.1)

x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}+a(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{5})+\\
b(x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}+x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{3}x_{5}+x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}+x_{2}x_{4}x_{5})=0,

for some
a,b\in k^{\times}
(via
Magma
). Notice that if
a=0
, the cubic has nonisolated singularities, and if
b=0
,
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are
{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
-points.
Based on the form
of (
8.1
), one can see that the embedding
{\mathbb{G}}^{4}_{m}(k)\subset G
is trivial, i.e.,
G
does not contain diagonal elements.

If
\rho(G)=C_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, then there exists
\tau\in G
such that
\rho(\tau)=(3,4)
and
\tau
had order
2.
Thus
\tau
takes the form

\rho((x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}))=(a_{1}x_{1},a_{2}x_{2},x_{4},x_{3},a_{3}x_{5}),\quad a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}=\pm 1.

The only possibility for leaving (
8.1
) invariant is
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=1
.

Therefore, we conclude that
G=C_{2}\times C_{3}
or
C_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, acting via corresponding permutations on the coordinates. Then
G
pointwise fixes the line
l\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
through
[1:1:0:0:0]
and
[0:0:1:1:1]
. Let
\iota
be the standard Cremona transformation on
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
. Observe that
\iota
birationally transforms
X
to a smooth quadric threefold
Q
, and
\iota(l)=l
. The intersection
l\cap Q
contains
G
-fixed points, which implies the assertion.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with singularities of type

2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\quad 2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\text{ or }\,\,3{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+2{\mathsf{A}}_{1}.

Then the
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-action on
X
is linearizable.

Now we consider the cases when
G\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Sing}(X)
: it follows that the only possible singularity type is
5{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with
5{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
-singularities such that
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
does not fix any singular points. Then up to isomorphism,
X
is given by

(8.2)

x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{2}x_{5}+x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}+x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}\\
+a(x_{1}x_{2}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}+x_{1}x_{3}x_{5}+x_{2}x_{3}x_{5}+x_{2}x_{4}x_{5})=0,

with
a=\zeta_{3}
or
\zeta_{3}^{2}
, and
\mathrm{Aut}(X)={\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
is generated by

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle:(\mathbf{x})\mapsto(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x_{1}),

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle:(\mathbf{x})\mapsto(a^{2}x_{2},ax_{1},a^{2}x_{4},ax_{3},x_{5}).

Proof.

By assumption, there exists an element
(1,2,3,4,5)\in\rho(G)
. Up to conjugation by the torus action, we may assume that
G
contains
\sigma
as is given in the assertion. The condition that
X
has 5
{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
-singularities at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{5}
and that
X
is left invariant by
\sigma
forces
X
to have equation (
8.2
).
By computation, one can check that
(1,2)\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
does not lift to
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, and
G
contains no torus action. On the other hand,
X
is left invariant by
\tau
. It follows that
\mathrm{Aut}(X)={\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
.
∎

In this case, the
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-action on
X
is not linearizable: the image
\iota(X)
under the standard Cremona transformation is a smooth quadric threefold. This
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
-action on a smooth quadric is (conjecturally) not linearizable, by a work in progress
[
15
]
.

Singularity type
2{\mathsf{D}}_{4}+3{\mathsf{A}}_{1}

Assume that
p_{1},p_{2}
are the
{\mathsf{D}}_{4}
-points and
p_{3},p_{4},p_{5}
the
{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-points.
Following the proof of Lemma
8.1
,
if
G
does not fix a singular point, then
X
is given by

x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}=x_{1}x_{2}(x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}),

with the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
-action permuting
x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}
and the infinite dihedral group
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\infty}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k)\rtimes C_{2}
acting on
x_{1},x_{2}
. Applying the Cremona transformation
\iota
, based at the 5 singular points, we obtain the smooth quadric

x_{1}x_{2}=x_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{5}+x_{3}x_{5}.

The
G
-action does not have fixed points, by our assumptions.

We can apply the Burnside formalism. Consider

G\simeq C_{2}^{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\subset{\mathfrak{D}}_{\infty}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3},

where one generator of
C_{2}^{2}
switches
x_{1},x_{2}
and
the other multiplies
x_{1},x_{2}
by
-1
.
The first gives rise to the symbol

(C_{2},C_{2}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(S),(1))\in\mathrm{Burn}_{3}(C_{2}^{2}),

with residual action
on the quadric
S
, given by
x_{1}^{2}=x_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{5}+x_{3}x_{5}
. By
[
16
, Example 9.2]
, this is an incompressible symbol, and the
G
-action on the quadric threefold is not linearizable.

9.
Six singularities

The relevant cases are

(9.1)

2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1},\quad 2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}.

By Propositions
2.7
and
2.4
, we may assume that the
4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-points are

p_{1}=[1:1:1:0:0],\qquad p_{2}=[-1:1:1:0:0],

p_{3}=[1:-1:1:0:0],\qquad p_{4}=[1:1:-1:0:0],

and the two
{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
or
{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
-points are

p_{5}=[0:0:0:1:0],\qquad p_{6}:=[0:0:0:0:1].

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold with six singularities which are not all
{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-points. Then the
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
-action on
X
is linearizable.

Proof.

In both cases of (
9.1
), the four
{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities are necessarily in a

G
-stable plane
\Pi\subset X
, and the two points with worse singularities define a
G
-stable line
l\subset X
, which is disjoint from
\Pi
.
Arguing as in
[
6
, Lemma 1.1]
, we obtain linearization.
∎

Normal forms in these two cases in (
9.1
) are not needed for the study of linearizability.
Nevertheless, we present them, for completeness.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
X
be a cubic threefold singular at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}.

•

If
X
has singularity type
2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
, then
X
is given by

(9.2)

(a_{1}x_{4}+a_{2}x_{5})(x_{1}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})+(a_{3}x_{4}+a_{4}x_{5})(x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})+\\
+(a_{5}x_{1}+a_{6}x_{2}+a_{7}x_{3})x_{4}x_{5}=0,

for general
a_{1},\ldots,a_{7}\in k
satisfying

(9.3)

\displaystyle a_{1}^{2}a_{6}^{2}+a_{1}a_{3}a_{5}^{2}+a_{1}a_{3}a_{6}^{2}-a_{1}a_{3}a_{7}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}a_{5}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle a_{2}^{2}a_{6}^{2}+a_{2}a_{4}a_{5}^{2}+a_{2}a_{4}a_{6}^{2}-a_{2}a_{4}a_{7}^{2}+a_{4}^{2}a_{5}^{2}=0.

•

If
X
has singularity type
2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
, then
X
is given by

(9.4)

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})x_{4}+(x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2})x_{5}+x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}=0.

Proof.

Singularities at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}
impose linear conditions on the vector space
{\mathrm{H}}^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{4},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}}^{4}(3)).
In particular, every cubic threefold singular at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{6}
is of the form (
9.2
), with general parameters
a_{1},\ldots,a_{7}
. Assume that
p_{5},p_{6}
are
{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
-points. This implies that the quadratic terms locally at
x_{4}=1
and
x_{5}=1
define a degenerate quadratic form in four variables. This gives the nonlinear conditions (
9.3
). A general solution to this system of equations in
a_{1},\ldots,a_{7}
defines a cubic with
2{\mathsf{A}}_{2}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities via (
9.2
).

If
X
has
2{\mathsf{A}}_{3}+4{\mathsf{A}}_{1}
-singularities, from Proposition
2.7
, we know that it contains the five planes spanned by points

\Pi_{1}\supset\{p_{2},p_{3},p_{5}\},\quad\Pi_{2}\supset\{p_{1},p_{4},p_{5}\},\quad\Pi_{3}\supset\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\},

\Pi_{4}\supset\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{6}\},\quad\Pi_{5}\supset\{p_{3},p_{4},p_{6}\}.

This imposes further linear conditions
a_{2}=a_{3}=0.
Substituting into (
9.3
), we also have
a_{1}a_{6}=a_{4}a_{5}=0
. When
a_{1}
or
a_{4}=0
, the cubic will be reducible, thus
a_{5}=a_{6}=0.
Moreover,
a_{7}\neq 0
, since otherwise
X
has nonisolated singularities. By scaling
x_{4}
and
x_{5},
we obtain the form (
9.4
).
∎

Sanju Mandal, Partha Pratim Roy, Molla Basir Ahamed

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Suppose
\mathcal{H}
be the class of functions
f
which are holomorphic in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then
\mathcal{H}
is a locally convex topological vector space endowed with the topology of uniform convergence over compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}
such that
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
. Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the subclass of all functions in
\mathcal{A}
which are univalent.

Let

(1.2)

\displaystyle F_{f}(z):=\log\dfrac{f(z)}{z}=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}(f)z^{n},\;\;z\in\mathbb{D},\;\;\log 1:=0,

be a logarithmic function associated with
f\in\mathcal{S}
. The numbers
\gamma_{n}:=\gamma_{n}(f)
are called the logarithmic coefficients of
f
. Although the logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
play a critical role in the theory of univalent functions, it appears that there are only a limited number of exact upper bounds established for them. As is well known, the logarithmic coefficients play a crucial role in Milin’s conjecture (
[
11
]
, see also
[
5
, p.155]
). We note that for the class
\mathcal{S}
sharp estimates are known only for
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, namely

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|\leq 1,\;\;|\gamma_{2}|\leq\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{e}=0.635\ldots

Estimating the modulus of logarithmic coefficients for
f\in\mathcal{S}
and various sub-classes has been considered recently by several authors. We refer to the articles
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
7
,
15
]
and references therein.

A function
f(z)
is said to be starlike in
\mathbb{C}
if it maps the open unit disk
|z|<1
conformally onto a region that is starlike with respect to the origin. The class of functions that are starlike with respect to symmetric points, which is denoted as
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
, was introduced by Sakaguchi in
1959

[
14
]
. A function
f
that belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
is characterized by the following conditions:

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\left(\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)-f(-z)}\right)>0,\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

A function
f(z)
is said to be convex in
\mathbb{C}
if it maps the open unit disk
|z|<1
conformally onto a region that is convex. We consider another class, which is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{S}
, that is, a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is said to be convex with respect to symmetric points if, and only if,

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\left(\dfrac{(zf^{\prime}(z))^{\prime}}{(f(z)-f(-z))^{\prime}}\right)>0,\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

The functions that are members of
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
are identified as close-to-convex, and consequently, they are univalent. The class of functions that are starlike with respect to symmetric points also includes the classes of convex functions and odd starlike functions with respect to the origin. This study is dedicated to providing the sharp bound for the second Hankel determinant, whose entries are the logarithmic coefficients. We commence by presenting the definitions of Hankel determinants in the case where
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

The Hankel determinant
H_{q,n}(f)
of Taylor’s coefficients of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
represented by (
1.1
) is defined for
q,n\in\mathbb{N}
as follows:

\displaystyle H_{q,n}(f):=\begin{vmatrix}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{n+q-1}\\
a_{n+1}&a_{n+2}&\cdots&a_{n+q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n+q-1}&a_{n+q}&\cdots&a_{n+2(q-1)}\end{vmatrix}.

Kowalczyk and Lecko
[
8
]
recently proposed a Hankel determinant whose elements are the logarithmic coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{S}
, realizing the extensive use of these coefficients. This determinant is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle H_{q,n}(F_{f}/2)=\begin{vmatrix}\gamma_{n}&\gamma_{n+1}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+q-1}\\
\gamma_{n+1}&\gamma_{n+2}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+q}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\gamma_{n+q-1}&\gamma_{n+q}&\cdots&\gamma_{n+2(q-1)}\end{vmatrix}.

The study of Hankel determinants with logarithmic coefficients of starlike or many other functions has been done extensively, their sharp bounds have been established. Recently the second Hankel determinants with logarithmic coefficients have been examined for selected subclasses of starlike functions, convex functions, univalent function, strongly starlike and strongly convex functions(see
[
3
,
8
,
9
]
and references therein). Recently, the sharp bounds of
H_{2,1}(F_{f}/2)
are established in
[
3
]
for the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
and
\mathcal{K}_{S}
. However, for further details on other aspects of the Hankel determinant, we suggest referring to the article
[
13
]
and its cited sources. The significance of logarithmic coefficients makes the proposed problem worth considering and interesting. The results enlarge the scope of knowledge on logarithmic coefficients.

The main objective of this paper is to continue the previous research work by examining the exact bound of the Hankel determinant
H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}-\gamma^{2}_{3}
for two class of functions: starlike and convex functions with respect to symmetric points. Differentiating (
1.2
) and using (
1.1
), a simple computation shows that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}a_{2},\vskip 4.2679pt\\
\gamma_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(a_{3}-\dfrac{1}{2}a^{2}_{2}\right),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
\gamma_{3}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(a_{4}-a_{2}a_{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}a^{3}_{2}\right),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
\gamma_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(a_{5}-a_{2}a_{4}+a^{2}_{2}a_{3}-\dfrac{1}{2}a^{2}_{3}-\dfrac{1}{4}a^{4}_{2}\right),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
\gamma_{5}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(a_{6}-a_{2}a_{5}-a_{3}a_{4}+a_{2}a^{2}_{3}+a^{2}_{2}a_{4}-a^{3}_{2}a_{3}+\dfrac{1}{5}a^{5}_{2}\right).\end{cases}

It is known that for the Koebe function
f(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
, the logarithmic coefficients are
\gamma_{n}=1/n
, for each positive integer
n
. Since the Koebe function appears as an extremal function in many problems of geometric theory of analytic functions, one could expect that
\gamma_{n}=1/n
holds for functions in
\mathcal{S}
. But this is not true in general, even in order of magnitude. The problem of computing
the bound of the logarithmic coefficients is also considered in
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
15
,
12
]
for several subclasses of close-to-convex functions. In 2021, Zaprawa
[
18
]
obtained the sharp bounds of the initial logarithmic coefficients
\gamma_{n}
for functions in the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
and
\mathcal{K}_{S}
.

However, the primary aim of this paper is to find the sharp bound of

\displaystyle H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}-\gamma^{2}_{3}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{288}\left(a^{6}_{2}-6a^{4}_{2}a_{3}-12a^{3}_{2}a_{4}+72a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}+18a^{2}_{2}a^{2}_{3}-36a^{2}_{2}a_{5}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-36a^{3}_{3}-72a^{2}_{4}+72a_{3}a_{5}\right)

when
f
is a member of the classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
and
\mathcal{K}_{S}
, which respectively refer to starlike functions and convex functions with respect to symmetric points. In addition, we give examples of functions to illustrate these results.

2.
Main results

Suppose
\mathcal{B}_{0}
be the class of Schwarz function
i.e
analytic function
w:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that
w(0)=0
. A function
w\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
can be written as a power series

\displaystyle w(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}.

For two functions
f
and
g
which are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, we say that the function
f
is subordinate to
g
in
\mathbb{D}
and written as

f(z)\prec g(z)
there exists a Schwarz function
w\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
such that
f(z)=g(w(z))
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. To prove our results, the following lemma for Schwarz functions will play a key role.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
6
]

Let
w(z)=c_{1}z+c_{2}z^{2}+c_{3}z^{3}+\ldots
be a Schwarz function. Then

\displaystyle|c_{1}|\leq 1,\;\;|c_{2}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2},\;\;|c_{3}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;|c_{4}|\leq 1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2}.

2.1.
Second Hankel determinant of logarithmic coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
:

We obtain the following sharp bound for
H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)
for functions in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
. Then

\displaystyle|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq\dfrac{(1272+113\sqrt{678})}{32856}.

The inequality is sharp.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{S}
be of the form
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then by the definition of subordination there exits a Schwarz function
w(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{2zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)-f(-z)}=\dfrac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)}.

By equating the coefficients on each side of (
2.1
), we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{2}=c_{1},\\
a_{3}=c_{2}+c^{2}_{1},\vskip 4.2679pt\\
a_{4}=\dfrac{1}{2}(c_{3}+3c_{1}c_{2}+2c^{3}_{1}),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
a_{5}=\dfrac{1}{2}(c_{4}+2c^{2}_{2}+5c^{2}_{1}c_{2}+2c_{1}c_{3}+2c^{4}_{1}).\end{cases}

In view of (
1.3
) and (
2.2
), a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}-\gamma^{2}_{3}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{288}\left(a^{6}_{2}-6a^{4}_{2}a_{3}-12a^{3}_{2}a_{4}+72a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}+18a^{2}_{2}a^{2}_{3}-36a^{2}_{2}a_{5}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-36a^{3}_{3}-72a^{2}_{4}+72a_{3}a_{5}\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{288}\left(c^{6}_{1}+30c^{4}_{1}c_{2}-6c^{3}_{1}c_{3}+72c^{2}_{1}c^{2}_{2}+18c^{2}_{1}c_{4}+36c^{3}_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad-18c^{2}_{3}+36c_{2}c_{4}\right)

Using the Lemma
2.1
into (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle 288|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|

\displaystyle\leq|c_{1}|^{6}+30|c_{1}|^{4}|c_{2}|+6|c_{1}|^{3}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)+72|c_{1}|^{2}|c_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+18|c_{1}|^{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2})+36|c_{3}|^{3}+18\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)^{2}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad+36|c_{2}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2}).

Suppose that
x=|c_{1}|
and
y=|c_{2}|
. Then it follows from (
2.1
) that

(2.5)

\displaystyle 288|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq M(x,y),

where

\displaystyle M(x,y)

\displaystyle:=x^{6}+30x^{4}y+6x^{3}\left(1-x^{2}-\dfrac{y^{2}}{1+x}\right)+72x^{2}y^{2}+18x^{2}(1-x^{2}-y^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+36y^{3}+18\left(1-x^{2}-\dfrac{y^{2}}{1+x}\right)^{2}+36y(1-x^{2}-y^{2}).

In view of Lemma
2.1
, the region of variability of a pair
(x,y)
coincides with the set

\displaystyle\Omega=\{(x,y):0\leq x\leq 1,0\leq y\leq 1-x^{2}\}.

The goal is to establish the maximum value of
M(x,y)
in the region
\Omega
. Therefore, the critical point of
M(x,y)
satisfies the conditions

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=6x^{5}-30x^{4}+18x^{2}-36x+120x^{3}y+108xy^{2}+\dfrac{18xy^{2}(4-x)}{(1+x)}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{6y^{2}(6-6x^{2}+x^{3})}{(1+x)^{2}}-\dfrac{36y^{4}}{(1+x)^{3}}=0

and

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y}=30x^{4}-36x^{2}+108x^{2}y+36+\dfrac{12y(6x^{2}-x^{3}-6)}{(1+x)}+\dfrac{72y^{3}}{(1+x)^{2}}=0.

There are no solutions of
M(x,y)
inside the interior of
\Omega
, hence it is not possible for the function to attain a maximum value within this region. Since
M(x,y)
is a continuous function on a compact set
\Omega
, its maximum value must occur at some point on the boundary of
\Omega
. On the boundary of
\Omega
, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle M(x,0)=x^{6}-6x^{5}+6x^{3}-18x^{2}+18\leq 18\;\;\mbox{for}\;0\leq x\leq 1,

\displaystyle M(0,y)=18y^{4}-36y^{2}+36y+18\leq 36\;\;\mbox{for}\;0\leq y\leq 1

and

\displaystyle M(x,1-x^{2})=37x^{6}-90x^{4}+18x^{2}+36\leq\dfrac{12(1272+113\sqrt{678})}{1369}\;\;\mbox{for}\;0\leq x\leq 1.

Therefore, we see that

\displaystyle\max_{(x,y)\in\Omega}M(x,y)=\dfrac{12(1272+113\sqrt{678})}{1369}

and it fllows from (
2.5
) that

(2.6)

\displaystyle|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq\dfrac{(1272+113\sqrt{678})}{32856}.

To prove the equality in (
2.6
) sharp, for

\displaystyle A=8696056

\displaystyle+741393\sqrt{678}

\displaystyle+81\sqrt{11507173440+1965308656\sqrt{678}+83777409(\sqrt{678})^{2}},

we consider the following function

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{z}{1-\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}+\frac{32\times 74^{2/3}}{27A^{1/3}}+\frac{2^{1/3}A^{1/3}}{27\times 37^{2/3}}}\right)z}

\displaystyle=z+\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}+\frac{32\times 74^{2/3}}{27A^{1/3}}+\frac{2^{1/3}A^{1/3}}{27\times 37^{2/3}}}\right)z^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}+\frac{32\times 74^{2/3}}{27A^{1/3}}+\frac{2^{1/3}A^{1/3}}{27\times 37^{2/3}}}\right)^{2}z^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}+\frac{32\times 74^{2/3}}{27A^{1/3}}+\frac{2^{1/3}A^{1/3}}{27\times 37^{2/3}}}\right)^{3}z^{4}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sqrt{\frac{8}{27}+\frac{32\times 74^{2/3}}{27A^{1/3}}+\frac{2^{1/3}A^{1/3}}{27\times 37^{2/3}}}\right)^{4}z^{5}+\ldots,\;\;z\in\mathbb{D};

It can be easily shown that the function

. A simple but tedious computations show that

, where

Hence equality hods in (
2.6
). This completes the proof

Example 2.1
.

\displaystyle f_{2}(z)=\dfrac{z}{1-z^{2}}=z+z^{3}+z^{5}+z^{7}+\ldots

2.2.
Second Hankel determinant of logarithmic coefficients of
f\in\mathcal{K}_{S}
:

We obtain the following sharp bound of
H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)
for functions in the class
\mathcal{K}_{S}

.

Let

. Then

\displaystyle|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq\dfrac{13}{1080}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{K}_{S}

,

. Then by the definition of subordination there exits a Schwarz function

such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{2(zf^{\prime}(z))^{\prime}}{(f(z)-f(-z))^{\prime}}=\dfrac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)}.

Comparing the coefficients on both side of (
2.7
), we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}c_{1},\vskip 4.2679pt\\
a_{3}=\dfrac{1}{3}(c_{2}+c^{2}_{1}),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
a_{4}=\dfrac{1}{8}(c_{3}+3c_{1}c_{2}+2c^{3}_{1}),\vskip 4.2679pt\\
a_{5}=\dfrac{1}{10}(c_{4}+2c^{2}_{2}+5c^{2}_{1}c_{2}+2c_{1}c_{3}+2c^{4}_{1}).\end{cases}

By utilizing (
1.3
) and (
2.8
), an easy calculation gives that

\displaystyle H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}-\gamma^{2}_{3}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{288}\left(a^{6}_{2}-36a^{3}_{3}-72a^{2}_{4}-6a^{4}_{2}a_{3}-12a^{3}_{2}a_{4}+72a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad+18a^{2}_{2}a^{2}_{3}-36a^{2}_{2}a_{5}+72a_{3}a_{5}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{276480}\left(175c^{6}_{1}+2508c^{4}_{1}c_{2}-180c^{3}_{1}c_{3}-432c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}+5640c^{2}_{1}c^{2}_{2}\right.

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left.\quad+1440c^{2}_{1}c_{4}+3328c^{3}_{2}-1080c^{2}_{3}+2304c_{2}c_{4}\right).

Applying the triangle inequality to (
2.2
) and using Lemma

\displaystyle 276480|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|

\displaystyle\leq 175|c_{1}|^{6}+2508|c_{1}|^{4}|c_{2}|+180|c_{1}|^{3}\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+432|c_{1}||c_{2}|\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)+5640|c_{1}|^{2}|c_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+1440|c_{1}|^{2}(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2})+3328|c_{2}|^{3}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\quad+2304|c_{2}|(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-|c_{2}|^{2})+1080\left(1-|c_{1}|^{2}-\dfrac{|c_{2}|^{2}}{1+|c_{1}|}\right)^{2}.

Suppose that
x=|c_{1}|
and

. Then it follows from (

) that

(2.11)

\displaystyle 276480|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq N(x,y),

\displaystyle N(x,y)

2.1

\displaystyle|H_{2,2}(F_{f}/2)|\leq\dfrac{13}{1080}.

In order to show the equality in (

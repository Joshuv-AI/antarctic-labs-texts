1.
Introduction

Let
F
be the finite field of order
q
. For
n
a positive integer and
0\leq r\leq n
, we let
\M(n,F)
to be the vector space of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
F
, and
\M(n,r,F)
to be the subset of
\M(n,F)
consisting of rank
r
matrices. For
\alpha\in F
and
A\in\M(n,F)
, we let

Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}=\left\{X\in\M(n,r,F)\mid\tr(AX)=\alpha\right\}.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, we define the
k
-th partial trace
, or in short the
k
-trace
of a matrix as the sum of its first
k
diagonal entries. It is clear that for
X\in\M(n,F)
, the
k
-trace of the matrix
X
is precisely
\tr(AX)
where
A
is the diagonal matrix with the first
k
diagonal entries being
1
, and the last
(n-k)
diagonal entries being
0
. We let
Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
be the set of
n\times n
matrices of rank
r
whose
k
-trace is
\alpha
. The main result of this paper is a computation of the cardinality of
Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
. It is easy to see that the sets
Z_{A,r}^{\alpha}
and
Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
, where
k
is the rank of
A
, have the same cardinality (see Proposition
2.1
).

Let
\psi:F\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a fixed non-trivial additive character of
F
. For
A\in\M(n,F),
consider

(1.1)

\sum_{X\in\M(n,r,F)}\psi(\tr(AX)).

In the case
r=n
, the sum above runs over
\GL(n,F)
and can be viewed as an analogue for
\GL(n,F)
of the classical Gauss sums.
In
[
6
]
, Eichler computed the above sum (
1.1
) when
r=n
, and
A=I_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix, and used it to compute the cardinality of
Z_{I_{n},n}^{\alpha}
. Eichler’s result was reproved by Lamprecht in
[
10
]
and more recently by Kim in
[
9
]
using different methods. See also Theorem 1 in
[
7
]
where a similar result is proved. In a more recent work
[
11
]
, Li and Hu computed the sum (
1.1
) for
r=n
and arbitrary
A\in\M(n,F)
generalizing the earlier works of Eichler, Lamprecht and Kim. In
[
3
]
, Buckhiester counted the number of
n\times n
matrices of rank
r
and trace
\alpha
over
F
. This cardinality was also counted independently by Prasad in
[
12
]
and was used to compute the dimension of a certain representation of
\GL(2n,F)
. In
[
1
]
, Balasubramanian, Khurana and Dangodara computed the cardinality of
n\times n
matrices of rank
r
and
1
-trace
\alpha
over
F
, and used it to calculate the dimension of a certain representation of
\GL(2n,F)
. More recently, a similar type of calculation was done by Gorodetsky and Hazan in
[
8
]
, generalizing the work of Prasad, to compute the dimension of a certain representation of
\GL(kn,F)
. Motivated by these works, in this article, we count the number of
n\times n
matrices of rank
r
and
k
-trace
\alpha
over
F
for
1\leq k\leq n
. This calculation is motivated by an application to the computation of the dimension formula for a certain twisted Jacquet module of a cuspidal representation of
\GL(2n,F)
. We will discuss the dimension formula in a forthcoming paper.

For the sake of clarity, we mention below the statement of the main theorem. Let
f_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
denote the cardinality of
Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
.
The cardinality of
\M(n,r,F)
is denoted
by
a(n,r,q)
(see (
2.1
)). The Gaussian binomial coefficient (
q
-binomial coefficient) which counts the number of
r
-dimensional linear subspaces of the
n
-dimensional space
F^{n}
is denoted as
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}
.

Theorem 1.1

(Main Theorem)
.

We have

\displaystyle f_{n,r,k}^{1}

\displaystyle=(a(n,r,q)-g_{n,r,k})/q,

\displaystyle f_{n,r,k}^{0}

\displaystyle=(a(n,r,q)+(q-1)g_{n,r,k})/q,

where

g_{n,r,k}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}q^{\tbinom{i}{2}+k(r-i)}\,\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\,a(n-k,r-i,q).

The range of summation in the expression for
g_{n,r,k}
above can be taken to be
\max\{0,r-(n-k)\}\leq i\leq\min\{r,k\}
, as the terms outside this range are zero. We also remark that

g_{n,r,k}=f_{n,r,k}^{0}-f_{n,r,k}^{1}
equals the sum (
1.1
) when rank
(A)=k
. The result of the above Theorem can be succinctly stated in terms of the polynomial generating functions

A_{n}(T),g_{n,k}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
defined by

A_{n}(T)=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}a(n,r,q)T^{r},\qquad g_{n,k}(T)=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}g_{n,r,k}T^{r}.

We have

g_{n,k}(T)=A_{n-k}(q^{k}T)\,(1-T)(1-qT)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}T).

In fact, we first obtain this polynomial identity in Theorem
3.4
, and use it to obtain the expression for
g_{n,r,k}
given in Theorem
1.1
. The polynomial generating functions
f^{\alpha}_{n,k}(T)=\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}T^{r}
are given by:

f^{1}_{n,k}(T)=(A_{n}(T)-g_{n,k}(T))/q,\qquad f^{0}_{n,k}(T)=(A_{n}(T)+(q-1)g_{n,k}(T))/q.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout, we let
F
denote the finite field of order
q
, and we denote by
\M(n,F)
the set of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
F
. For
0\leq r\leq n
, we let
\M(n,r,F)
denote the subset of
\M(n,F)
consisting of matrices of rank
r
. For
1\leq k\leq n
, and
\alpha\in F
, define

Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}=\left\{X=(x_{ij})\in\M(n,r,F)\mid\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{ii}=\alpha\right\}.

We write
f_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
for the cardinality of
Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
. If
k=n
, to simplify notation, we will denote
Y_{k,r,k}^{\alpha}=Y_{k,r}^{\alpha}
and
f_{k,r,k}^{\alpha}=f_{k,r}^{\alpha}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
n,r
be non-negative integers. For
A\in\M(n,F)
and
\alpha\in F
, let

Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}=\left\{X\in\M(n,r,F)\mid\tr(AX)=\alpha\right\}.

If
\Rank(A)=k
, then

|Z_{A,r}^{\alpha}|=f_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Since
A\in\M(n,k,F)
, there exists
g_{1},g_{2}\in\GL(n,F)
such that
g_{1}Ag_{2}^{-1}=B
, where

B=\begin{bmatrix}I_{k}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.

Consider the bijective map

\phi\colon\M(n,r,F)\to\M(n,r,F),\quad\phi(X)=g_{2}Xg_{1}^{-1}.

Since

\tr(B\phi(X))=\tr(Bg_{2}Xg_{1}^{-1})=\tr(g_{1}^{-1}B{g_{2}}X)=\tr(AX),

it follows that
\phi
carries

Z_{A,r}^{\alpha}
bijectively to

Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
.
∎

Before we proceed further, we set up some more notation and recall some more results that we need. We write
a(n,q)
(respectively
a(n,r,q)
) for the cardinality of
\M(n,F)
(respectively
\M(n,r,F)
). We let
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}
denote the Gaussian binomial coefficient and
(T;q)_{n}
for the
q
-Pochhammer symbol. For the sake of clarity, we record their definitions below.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
n
,
r
be non-negative integers with
r\leq n
. The
q
-binomial coefficient is defined as

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{r}_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[r]_{q}![n-r]_{q}!}

where
[n]_{q}!=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}
and
[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
.
It is known that
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}
is a polynomial of degree
r(n-r)
in
q
which evaluates at
q=1
to
the usual binomial coefficient
\tbinom{n}{r}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. We define

(T;q)_{n}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-Tq^{i}),\quad n>0.

We take
(T;q)_{0}=1
.

We recall the
q
-binomial theorem which goes back to Cauchy. A proof can be found in
[
14
, §1.8]
.

Theorem 2.4

(
q
-binomial theorem)
.

We have

(T;q)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{r}_{q}(-1)^{r}q^{\tbinom{r}{2}}T^{r}.

We note that substituting
q=1
in the
q
-binomial theorem recovers the usual binomial theorem.

Proposition 2.5
.

The number
a(m\times n,r,q)
of
m\times n
matrices of rank
r
over
F
equals

a(m\times n,r,q)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{r}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{m}{r}_{q}|\GL(r,F)|=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{(q^{m}-q^{i})(q^{n}-q^{i})}{(q^{r}-q^{i})}.

In particular,

(2.1)

a(n,r,q)=a(n\times n,r,q)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{r}_{q}^{2}|\GL(r,F)|=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{r-1}\frac{(q^{n}-q^{i})^{2}}{(q^{r}-q^{i})}.

Proof.

A rank
r
linear map
T:F^{n}\to F^{m}
is uniquely determined by the triple of i) kernel
(T)
, ii) image
(T)
, and iii) the isomorphism
F^{n}/\text{kernel}(T)\to\text{image}(T)
. These can be chosen in i)
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{n-r}_{q}
, ii)
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{m}{r}_{q}
, and iii)
|\GL(r,F)|
ways respectively.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
0\leq r\leq n
. Then

f_{n,r,k}^{0}+(q-1)f_{n,r,k}^{1}=a(n,r,q).

Proof.

It is clear that
\M(n,r,F)
can be written as the disjoint union
\displaystyle\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in F}Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
. Thus, we have
\displaystyle a(n,r,q)=\sum_{\alpha\in F}f_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
. Let
\alpha\neq 0\in F
. It is enough to show that
f_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}=f_{n,r,k}^{1}
. Let

Z=\begin{bmatrix}P&Q\\
R&S\end{bmatrix}\in Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha},

where
P\in\M(k,F)
and so
\tr(P)=\alpha\neq 0
. Clearly the map
\phi\colon Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}\rightarrow Y_{n,r,k}^{1}
defined by
\phi(Z)=KZ
, where

K=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha^{-1}I_{k}&0\\
0&I_{n-k}\end{bmatrix}\in\GL(n,F)

is a bijection. The result follows.
∎

For
\alpha\in F
, recall that
f_{k,r}^{\alpha}=f_{k,r,k}^{\alpha}
. The following result is due to Prasad where he computes
f_{k,r}^{0}-f_{k,r}^{1}
. We can use this result in combination with Proposition
2.6
to explicitly compute the numbers
f_{k,r}^{0}
and
f_{k,r}^{1}
.

Lemma 2.7

(Prasad)
.

g_{k,r,k}=f_{k,r}^{0}-f_{k,r}^{1}=(-1)^{r}q^{\tbinom{r}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k}{r}_{q}.

Proof.

For a proof, we refer the reader to Lemma 2 in
[
12
]
.
∎

We can reformulate Lemma
2.7
, using generating functions and the
q
-binomial theorem as follows.

Lemma 2.8
.

The polynomial generating function
g_{k,k}(T)=\displaystyle\sum_{r\geq 0}g_{k,r,k}T^{r}\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
equals:

(2.2)

g_{k,k}(T)=(1-T)(1-qT)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}T)=(T;q)_{k}.

3.
main theorem

In this section, we prove the main result of this paper. We continue with the same notation as in the previous section.

Lemma 3.1
.

f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}
satisfies the recursion

f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}=f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r,k}q^{2r}+f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-1,k}q^{2r-2}(2q^{n-r+1}-1-q)+f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-2,k}q^{2r-3}(q^{n-r+1}-1)^{2}

for
n>k
.

Proof.

Let
X\in Y_{n,r,k}^{\alpha}
. Then,
X
is of the form

X=\begin{bmatrix}D&v\\
w&x\end{bmatrix}

where
x\in F
,
v\in F^{n-1}
,
w
is a row vector of length
(n-1)
, and
D\in Y^{\alpha}_{n-1,s,k}
for some
s\in\{r,r-1,r-2\}
. We denote the
n\times 1
column vector
\begin{bmatrix}v\\
x\end{bmatrix}
by
\tilde{v}
. Let
W
denote the row space of
D
, and let
V
denote the column space of the

n\times(n-1)
matrix
\begin{bmatrix}D\\
w\end{bmatrix}
.
We consider the cases
s=r
,
s=r-1
, and
s=r-2
one by one.

In case
s=r
, the condition for
X
to have rank
r
is that
w\in W
and
\tilde{v}\in V
. Thus, given
D\in Y_{k,r}^{0}
, we get
q^{r}
choices each, for the vectors
w
and
\tilde{v}
such that
X\in Y_{k+1,r,k}^{0}
. Thus, there are in total
q^{2r}
choices for the pair
(w,\tilde{v})
so that
X\in Y^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}
.

In case
s=r-1
, then
X
has rank
r
if either i)
w\in W
and
\tilde{v}\notin V
, or ii)
w\notin W
and
\tilde{v}\in V
. The number of choices for
w
and
\tilde{v}
are
q^{r-1}
and
(q^{n}-q^{r-1})
in case i), and

(q^{n-1}-q^{r-1})q^{r}
in case ii). Thus, there are in total
(2q^{n+r-1}-q^{2r-2}-q^{2r-1})
choices for the pair
(w,\tilde{v})
so that
X\in Y^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}
.

In case
s=r-2
, the condition for
X
to have rank
r
is that
w\notin W
and
\tilde{v}\notin V
. The number of choices for
w
and
\tilde{v}
are
(q^{n-1}-q^{r-2})
and
(q^{n}-q^{r-1})
respectively. Thus, there are
(q^{n-1}-q^{r-2})(q^{n}-q^{r-1})
choices for the pair
(w,\tilde{v})
so that
X\in Y^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}
.

It follows that

f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}=f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r,k}q^{2r}+f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-1,k}q^{2r-2}(2q^{n-r+1}-1-q)+f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-2,k}q^{2r-3}(q^{n-r+1}-1)^{2}.

∎

For
1\leq k\leq n
and
\alpha\in F
, consider the polynomial generating function
f^{\alpha}_{n,k}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
defined by

(3.1)

f^{\alpha}_{n,k}(T)=\sum_{r\geq 0}f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}T^{r}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
n>k
, we have

f_{n,k}^{\alpha}(T)=(1-T)(1-qT)f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(q^{2}T)+2q^{n}T(1-T)f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(qT)+q^{2n-1}T^{2}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(T).

Proof.

Using the recursive expression for
f^{\alpha}_{n,r,k}
of Lemma
3.1
in (
3.1
), we get:

\displaystyle f_{n,k}^{\alpha}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\geq 0}\bigg(f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r,k}(q^{2}T)^{r}+Tf^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-1,k}(q^{2}T)^{r-1}(2q^{n-r+1}-1-q)

(3.2)

\displaystyle+qT^{2}f^{\alpha}_{n-1,r-2,k}(q^{2}T)^{r-2}(q^{n-r+1}-1)^{2}\bigg).

In terms of the index
t=r-1
, the second term in the above sum can be rewritten as

2Tq^{n}\sum_{t\geq 0}(qT)^{t}f^{\alpha}_{n-1,t,k}-(1+q)T\sum_{t\geq 0}(q^{2}T)^{t}f^{\alpha}_{n-1,t,k}=\\
2Tq^{n}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(qT)-(1+q)Tf_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(q^{2}T).

Similarly, in terms of the index
s=r-2
,
the third term in the sum can be rewritten as

\sum_{s\geq 0}\left(q^{2n-1}T^{2}f_{n-1,s,k}^{\alpha}T^{s}+T^{2}qf_{n-1,s,k}^{\alpha}(q^{2}T)^{s}-2q^{n}T^{2}f^{\alpha}_{n-1,s,k}(qT)^{s}\right)\\
=T^{2}q^{2n-1}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(T)+qT^{2}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(q^{2}T)-2q^{n}T^{2}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(qT).

Therefore, (
3.2
) can be written as

f_{n,k}^{\alpha}(T)=(1-T)(1-qT)f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(q^{2}T)+2Tq^{n}(1-T)f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(qT)+T^{2}q^{2n-1}f_{n-1,k}^{\alpha}(T).

∎

Since
a(n,r,q)=f^{0}_{n,r,0}
, it follows that the polynomial generating function

\displaystyle A_{n}(T)=\sum_{r\geq 0}a(n,r,q)T^{r}\in\mathbb{Z}[T],

of the numbers
a(n,r,q)
(for fixed
n
), satisfies the recurrence:

(3.3)

A_{n}(T)=A_{n-1}(q^{2}T)(1-T)(1-qT)+2q^{n}T(1-T)A_{n-1}(qT)+q^{2n-1}T^{2}A_{n-1}(T),\quad n>0.

Let
g_{n,r,k}=f_{n,r,k}^{0}-f_{n,r,k}^{1}
. Consider the polynomial generating function:

g_{n,k}(T)=\sum_{r\geq 0}g_{n,r,k}T^{r}=f_{n,k}^{0}(T)-f_{n,k}^{1}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T],

where
f^{\alpha}_{n,k}(T)
is as defined in (
3.1
).
It follows from Lemma
3.2
that

g_{n,k}(T)
also obeys the same recursion as
f_{n,k}^{\alpha}(T)
:

(3.4)

g_{n,k}(T)=(1-T)(1-qT)g_{n-1,k}(q^{2}T)+2q^{n}T(1-T)g_{n-1,k}(qT)+q^{2n-1}T^{2}g_{n-1,k}(T).

For
n=k
, the polynomial
g_{n,k}(T)
equals
(T;q)_{k}
by Lemma
2.8
. In the next Lemma, we use (
3.4
) to determine

g_{n,k}(T)
for
n=k+1
and
n=k+2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\frac{g_{k+1,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}=1+(q-1)Tq^{k}=A_{1}(q^{k}T).

and

\frac{g_{k+2,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}=1+(q^{2}-1)\left[(Tq^{k})(q+1)+(Tq^{k})^{2}(q^{2}-q)\right]=A_{2}(q^{k}T).

Proof.

Using the fact that
g_{k,k}(T)=(T;q)_{k}
, Equation (
3.4
) becomes

g_{k+1,k}(T)=(1-T)(1-qT)(q^{2}T;q)_{k}+2T(1-T)q^{k+1}(qT;q)_{k}+T^{2}q^{2k+1}(T;q)_{k}.

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{g_{k+1,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{k}T)(1-q^{k+1}T)+2Tq^{k+1}(1-q^{k}T)+T^{2}q^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=1+Tq^{k}(q-1)=A_{1}(q^{k}T),

because
A_{1}(T)=1+(q-1)T
.

Similarly,
g_{k+2,k}(T)
equals

A_{1}(q^{k+2}T)(q^{2}T;q)_{k}(1-T)(1-qT)+2q^{k+2}T(1-T)(qT;q)_{k}A_{1}(q^{k+1}T)+T^{2}q^{2k+3}A_{1}(qT)(T;q)_{k}

Therefore,
\tfrac{g_{k+2,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}
equals

A_{1}(q^{k+2}T)(1-q^{k}T)(1-q^{k+1}T)+2Tq^{k+2}A_{1}(q^{k+1}T)(1-q^{k}T)\\
+q^{3}A_{1}(q^{k}T)(q^{k}T)^{2}.

Using
A_{1}(T)=1+(q-1)T
, this simplifies to

\frac{g_{k+2,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}=1+(q^{2}-1)\left[(Tq^{k})(q+1)+(Tq^{k})^{2}(q^{2}-q)\right]=A_{2}(q^{k}T),

because
A_{2}(T)=1+(q+1)^{2}(q-1)T+(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)T^{2}
.
∎

This suggests the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

g_{n,k}(T)=(T;q)_{k}\;A_{n-k}(q^{k}T).

Proof.

We use induction on
n
. The base case is
k=n
which is true by Lemma
2.8
. Assume inductively that the theorem is true for
g_{n-1,k}(T)
. We have

\displaystyle g_{n,k}(T)

\displaystyle=(1-T)(1-qT)g_{n-1,k}(q^{2}T)+2q^{n}T(1-T)g_{n-1,k}(qT)+q^{2n-1}T^{2}g_{n-1,k}(T)

\displaystyle=(1-T)(1-qT)(q^{2}T;q)_{k}A_{n-k-1}(q^{k+2}T)+2q^{n}T(1-T)(qT;q)_{k}A_{n-k-1}(q^{k+1}T)

\displaystyle\qquad+q^{2n-1}T^{2}(T;q)_{k}\;A_{n-k-1}(q^{k}T).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{g_{n,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}

\displaystyle=(1-q^{k}T)(1-q^{k+1}T)A_{n-k-1}(q^{k+2}T)+2Tq^{n}(1-q^{k}T)A_{n-k-1}(q^{k+1}T)

\displaystyle+A_{n-k-1}(q^{k}T)T^{2}q^{2n-1}.

In terms of the recurrence relation (
3.3
) for
A_{m}(Y)
where
Y=q^{k}T
and

, we see that the above expression is just

A_{n-k}(q^{k}T).

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem

From Theorem

and Theorem

, it follows that

4.
The case of rectangular matrices

The problem of determining the cardinality of

Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}=\{X\in\M(n,r,F)\colon\tr(AX)=\alpha\},

has a natural generalization to
m\times n
matrices. Let

\M(m\times n,F)
be the
vector space of
m\times n
matrices with entries in
F
, and let

\M(m\times n,r,F)
denote the subset of
\M(m\times n,F)
consisting of matrices of rank
r
. For a
n\times m
matrix
A
over
F
, let
Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}
again denote (with a little abuse of notation)

Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}=\{X\in\M(m\times n,r,F)\colon\tr(AX)=\alpha\}.

The problem is to determine the cardinality of
Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}
.
If
k
denotes rank
(A)
, then just as in Proposition
2.1
, the cardinality of
Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}
is the same as the cardinality of the set

Y^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k}=\{X\in\M(m\times n,r,F):X_{11}+\dots+X_{kk}=\alpha\}.

Clearly the transpose operation gives a bijection between
Y^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k}

and
Y^{\alpha}_{n\times m,r,k}
, so we assume
m\leq n
without loss of generality. We recall from Proposition
2.5
that

|\M(m\times n,r,F)|=a(m\times n,r,q)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{m}{r}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}|\GL(r,F)|.

We define:

\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k}

\displaystyle=|Y^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k}|

\displaystyle g_{m\times n,r,k}

\displaystyle=f^{0}_{m\times n,r,k}-f^{1}_{m\times n,r,k}

\displaystyle g_{m\times n,k}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{m}g_{m\times n,r,k}T^{r}

\displaystyle A_{m\times n}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a(m\times n,r,q)T^{r}.

Moreover, as in Proposition
2.6
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle a(m\times n,r,q)

\displaystyle=f^{0}_{m\times n,r,k}+(q-1)f^{1}_{m\times n,r,k},

\displaystyle f_{m\times n,r,k}^{1}

\displaystyle=(a(m\times n,r,q)-g_{m\times n,r,k})/q,

\displaystyle f_{m\times n,r,k}^{0}

\displaystyle=(a(m\times n,r,q)+(q-1)g_{m\times n,r,k})/q.

Therefore, it suffices to determine the quantities
g_{m\times n,r,k}
, or effectively the polynomial generating function
g_{m\times n,k}(T)
:

Theorem 4.1
.

g_{m\times n,k}(T)=(T;q)_{k}\;A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(q^{k}T).

In other words

(4.2)

g_{m\times n,r,k}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\,q^{\tbinom{i}{2}+k(r-i)}\,a((m-k)\times(n-k),r-i,q).

The range of summation in (
4.2
) can be taken to be
\text{max}\{0,k+r-m\}\leq i\leq\text{min}\{r,k\}
, as the terms outside this range are zero. We will prove Theorem
4.1
by
induction on
m
. The base case is
m=k
which is treated in the next lemma. It states the non-obvious fact that
g_{k\times n,r,k}
is independent of
n
for all
n\geq k
:

Lemma 4.2
.

g_{k\times n,r,k}=g_{k,r,k}=(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{r}_{q},\quad\forall\,n\geq k.

Proof.

If
A\in Y^{\alpha}_{k,i,k}

i.e.,
A_{k\times k}

has rank
i
and trace
\alpha
, then the number of matrices
X=[A_{k\times k}\mid B_{k\times n-k}]
having rank
r
equals
q^{(n-k)i}
times
a((k-i)\times(n-k),r-i,q)
, where
a((k-i)\times(n-k),r-i,q)=|\M((k-i)\times(n-k),r-i,F)|
.
Therefore,

f^{\alpha}_{k\times n,r,k}=\sum_{i}f^{\alpha}_{k,i,k}\cdot q^{(n-k)i}\,a((k-i)\times(n-k),r-i,q).

It follows that

g_{k\times n,r,k}=\sum_{i}g_{k,i,k}\cdot q^{(n-k)i}\,a((k-i)\times(n-k),r-i,q)\\
=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}\cdot q^{(n-k)i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k-i}{r-i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-k}{r-i}_{q}|\GL(r-i,F)|,

where we have used Lemma
2.7
. Using the identity
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k-i}{k-r}_{q}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{r}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{r}{i}_{q}
, we see that

\tilde{g}_{k\times n,r,k}:=\frac{g_{k\times n,r,k}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{r}_{q}}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{r}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-k}{r-i}_{q}|\GL(r-i,F)|q^{(n-k)i},

for a fixed
r
, only depends on
n-k
for all
k,n
with
n\geq k\geq r
. We will prove the assertion in the statement by induction on
n
. The base case
n=k
is true for all
k
. We inductively assume that the assertion holds for
n-1
. Since
\tilde{g}_{k\times n,r,k}=\tilde{g}_{(k-1)\times(n-1),r,(k-1)}
which by the inductive hypothesis equals

\frac{g_{(k-1)\times(n-1),r,k-1}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k-1}{r}_{q}}=\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k-1}{r}_{q}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k-1}{r}_{q}}=(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}},

we have shown that

\frac{g_{k\times n,r,k}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{r}_{q}}=(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}},

as required.
∎

Proof.

(of Theorem
4.1
)

Having proved the base case
m=k
, of the result, we
assume inductively that the theorem is true for
g_{m\times n,k}(T)
.
Let

X=\begin{bmatrix}B_{m\times n}\\
w_{1\times n}\end{bmatrix}
be an element of
Y^{\alpha}_{(m+1)\times n,r,k}
. Then, either

with

B
, or
B\in Y^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r-1,k}
with
w
not in the row space of

. Thus

In terms of the generating function

f^{\alpha}_{(m+1)\times n,k}(T)=f^{\alpha}_{m\times n,k}(qT)+q^{n}Tf^{\alpha}_{m\times n,k}(T)-Tf^{\alpha}_{m\times n,k}(qT).

We may rewrite this as

(4.3)

Since

We make another observation from

Since

Using the inductive hypothesis in (

), we get:

g_{(m+1)\times n,k}(T)=(1-T)(qT;q)_{k}A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(q^{k+1}T)+q^{n}T(T;q)_{k}A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(q^{k}T).

We may rewrite this as:

\frac{g_{(m+1)\times n,k}(T)}{(T;q)_{k}}=(1-q^{k}T)A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(q(q^{k}T))+q^{n-k}(q^{k}T)A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(q^{k}T).

Using (

A different proof of Theorem

The induction in the proof of Theorem

was on the parameter
m
with

being fixed. We now develop a recurrence for

which does not fix any of the parameters

.

Lemma 4.3
.

The quantities

and

\displaystyle f^{\alpha}_{(m+1)\times(n+1),r+1,k+1}

\displaystyle g_{(m+1)\times(n+1),r+1,k+1}

)

has rank

or 2)
U
has rank

is not the zero vector, b) the first column of
U
is the zero vector. Let

is

.

In case 1a), the condition for

to be in

with

, for each of the

.

In case 2a), the condition for

to be in

is that

is a vector not in the row space of
U
, and

. The number of choices for
w
is
(q^{n}-q^{r-1})

.

In case 2b), the condition for

to be in

.

5.
Some properties of
g_{m\times n,r,k}

An interesting question is to determine how the sizes

|Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}|
depend on
\alpha
. For example, if
A\neq 0
then
the set
\{X\in\M(m\times n,F)\colon\tr(AX)=0\}
is a codimension one linear subspace of the vector space
\M(m\times n,F)
, and hence has cardinality
q^{mn-1}
. This cardinality is also the sum

\sum_{r=0}^{m}|Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}|=\sum_{r=0}^{m}f^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k},

and hence
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}(f^{0}_{m\times n,r,k}-f^{1}_{m\times n,r,k})=\sum_{r=0}^{m}g_{m\times n,r,k}
must be zero. This can be seen as follows:
\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{m}g_{m\times n,r,k}
is the evaluation of polynomial

g_{m\times n,k}(T)=(T;q)_{k}A_{(m-k)\times(n-k)}(T),

at
T=1
, and
(T;q)_{k}=(1-T)(1-qT)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}T)
evaluates to
0
at
T=1
for
k>0
.

We recall that

f^{\alpha}_{m\times n,r,k}=f^{1}_{m\times n,r,k}
for all
\alpha\in F\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, the quantity

(5.1)

h_{m\times n,r,k}=\frac{g_{m\times n,r,k}}{a(m\times n,r,q)},

is a measure of equidistribution of the function
\alpha\mapsto|Z^{\alpha}_{A,r}|
where rank
(A)=k
. Some natural questions arise here:

(1)

How does the sign of
h_{m\times n,r,k}
depend on the parameters
m,n,r,k
?

(2)

Can we find an upper bound on
|h_{m\times n,r,k}|
?

For fixed
m,n,r,k
what is the asymptotic behaviour of
|h_{m\times n,r,k}|
as
q\to\infty
?

We will answer these questions. A non-obvious property of
h_{m\times n,r,k}
is that it is symmetric in
r
and
k
:

Lemma 5.1

(Delsarte
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

h_{m\times n,r,k}=h_{m\times n,k,r}.

Remark 5.2
.

Before going to our proof of this result, we quickly explain Delsarte’s proof.
Delsarte showed in
[
4
, p.240]
and
[
5
, eqn. (16)]
, that
h_{m\times n,r,k}
can be expressed in the terms of the
basic hypergeometric function

{}_{2}\phi_{2}
(the definition of
{}_{2}\phi_{2}
is as in references [1], [13] of
[
5
]
):

(5.2)

h_{m\times n,r,k}={}_{2}\phi_{2}((\begin{smallmatrix}q^{-r},&q^{-k}\\
q^{-m},&q^{-n}\end{smallmatrix});q;q)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-r};q)_{j}(q^{-k};q)_{j}q^{j}}{(q^{-m};q)_{j}(q^{-n};q)_{j}(q;q)_{j}}

It is clear that the above expression is symmetric in
r
and
k
. We can rewrite this expression as the
alternating sum:

h_{m\times n,r,k}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{q^{j(m+n+1-r-k)}\,a(r\times k,j,q)}{(q;q)_{j}\,a(m\times n,j,q)}.

We now return to our proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Proof.

Dividing the expression (
4.2
) for
g_{m\times n,r,k}
by
a(m\times n,r,q)
we get

h_{m\times n,r,k}=\sum_{i=0}^{\text{min}\{r,k\}}(-1)^{i}q^{\tbinom{i}{2}+k(r-i)}\frac{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{m-k}{r-i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-k}{r-i}_{q}|\GL(r-i,F)|}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{m}{r}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}|\GL(r,F)|}

The ratio in the right hand side can be simplified as

\frac{\dfrac{(q^{k-i+1};q)_{i}}{(q;q)_{i}}\cdot\dfrac{(q^{m+i+1-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}}{(q^{m-k+1};q)_{k}(q;q)_{r-i}}\cdot\dfrac{(q^{n+i+1-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}}{(q^{n-k+1};q)_{k}(q;q)_{r-i}}\cdot q^{\binom{r-i}{2}}(q;q)_{r-i}(-1)^{r-i}}{\dfrac{(q^{m-r+1};q)_{r}}{(q:q)_{r}}\cdot\dfrac{(q^{n-r+1};q)_{r}}{(q:q)_{r}}\cdot q^{\binom{r}{2}}(q;q)_{r}(-1)^{r}}.

This can be rewritten as

\frac{(q^{k-i+1};q)_{i}(q^{r-i+1};q)_{i}(q^{m+i+1-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}(q^{n+i+1-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}}{(-1)^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}+i(r-i)}(q;q)_{i}(q^{n-r+1};q)_{r}(q^{m-r+1};q)_{r}(q^{m-k+1};q)_{k}(q^{n-k+1};q)_{k}}

Therefore
h_{m\times n,r,k}
equals

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\text{max}\{0,r+k-m\}}^{\text{min}\{r,k\}}\!\!\!\!\frac{\dfrac{q^{(k-i)(r-i)}}{(q;q)_{i}}(q^{k-i+1};q)_{i}(q^{r-i+1};q)_{i}(q^{m+1+i-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}(q^{n+1+i-r-k};q)_{r+k-i}}{(q^{m-r+1};q)_{r}(q^{m-k+1};q)_{k}(q^{n-r+1};q)_{r}(q^{n-k+1};q)_{k}},

which is clearly symmetric in
r
and
k
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For
q>2
, the terms in the alternating sum
(
4.2
)

g_{m\times n,r,k}=\sum_{i=\mathrm{max}\{0,k+r-m\}}^{\mathrm{min}\{r,k\}}\!\!\!\!(-1)^{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{k}{i}_{q}\,q^{\tbinom{i}{2}+k(r-i)}\,a((m-k)\times(n-k),r-i,q),

are decreasing in absolute value.

Proof.

The absolute value of the ratio of the
(i+1)
-th term to the
i
-th term in the sum above simplifies to

(5.4)

\frac{(1-\dfrac{1}{q^{k-i}})(1-\dfrac{1}{q^{r-i}})}{\dfrac{(q^{i+1}-1)(q^{m-k-r+i+1}-1)}{q}(q^{n-k-r+i+1}-1)},

for
i
in the range

\text{max}\{0,k+r-m\}\leq i\leq\text{min}\{r,k\}-1.

In this range the numerator of (
5.4
) is clearly less than
1
, and hence it suffices to show that the denominator is
\geq 1
. There are two possible cases:
k+r>m
or
k+r\leq m
.

In case
k+r>m
, then
i\geq k+r-m\geq 1
, and hence

(q^{n-k-r+i+1}-1)\geq(q^{m-k-r+i+1}-1)\geq(q-1)\geq 1,

and

(q^{i+1}-1)/q\geq(q^{2}-1)/q>1,

which shows that the denominator is
\geq 1
.

If
k+r\leq m
, then

(q^{n-k-r+i+1}-1)\geq(q^{m-k-r+i+1}-1)\geq(q^{i+1}-1)\geq 1,

and

\frac{(q^{i+1}-1)(q^{m-k-r+i+1}-1)}{q}\geq\frac{(q^{i+1}-1)^{2}}{q}\geq\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q}>1,

which shows that the denominator is
\geq 1
, when
q>2
. If
q=2
, the denominator is
\geq 1
unless
n=m=r+k
and
i=0
.

Suppose
q=2
and
m=n=r+k
. Writing (
4.2
) as

g_{m\times n,r,k}=e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2}-\dots+(-1)^{r}e_{r},

we have shown
\frac{e_{i+1}}{e_{i}}<1
for
i\geq 1
. As for
\frac{e_{1}}{e_{0}}
, we note that

\frac{e_{1}}{e_{0}}=2(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}})(1-\frac{1}{2^{r}}),

is
<1
only if
(k-1)(r-1)=0
. In other words,
\frac{e_{1}}{e_{0}}>1
if and only if
m=n=r+k
and
r,k\geq 2
. In this case

e_{0}-e_{1}<g_{m\times n,r,k}<e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2}.

We have

\frac{e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2}}{|\GL(r,F)|\,2^{kr}}=1-\frac{(2^{k}-1)(2^{r}-1)}{2^{m-1}}+\frac{(2^{k}-1)(2^{r}-1)(2^{k-1}-1)(2^{r-1}-1)}{2^{2m-4}\,27}\\
<1-\frac{25(2^{k}-1)(2^{r}-1)}{2^{m-1}27},

where we have used the fact that
(2^{k-1}-1)(2^{r-1}-1)<2^{k+r-2}=2^{m-2}
. Since
k+r=m
and
k,r\geq 2
, the minimum value of
(2^{k}-1)(2^{r}-1)
is
3(2^{m-2}-1)
, and hence

1-\frac{25(2^{k}-1)(2^{r}-1)}{2^{m-1}27}<\frac{-7}{18}+\frac{25}{18\cdot 2^{m-2}}<\frac{-1}{24},

where we have used the fact that
m=r+k\geq 4
. Thus
e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2}<0
, which shows that
g_{m\times n,r,k}<0
in this case.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

For
q>2
, we have:

(5.5)

\mathrm{sgn}(h_{m\times n,r,k})=(-1)^{\mathrm{max}\{r+k-m,0\}}

(If we do not assume
m\leq n
, then
m
should be replaced by
\mathrm{min}\{m,n\}
in this assertion.)

If
q=2
, the result still holds unless
m=n=r+k
and
r,k\geq 2
, in which case
\mathrm{sgn}(h_{m\times n,r,k})=-1
.

Proof.

Since
h_{m\times n,r,k}=g_{m\times n,r,k}/a(m\times n,r,q)
, it suffices to show that
g_{m\times n,r,k}
has the asserted sign. If
q>2
, then by Lemma
5.3
, the terms of the alternating sum (
4.2
) are decreasing in absolute value, and hence the sign of
g_{m\times n,r,k}
is the sign of the first (non-zero) term in the alternating sum. This is the term with index
i=\ell
where
\ell=\text{max}\{0,k+r-m\}
. Thus, sgn
(g_{m\times n,r,k})=(-1)^{\ell}
.

If
q=2
, we have shown in the last part of the proof of Lemma
5.3
, that (
5.5
) is violated if and only if
m=n=r+k
and
r,k\geq 2
.
∎

In fact, Lemma
5.3
can be used to give an upper bound on the absolute value of
h_{m\times n,r,k}
:

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
q>2
, and let
\ell=\mathrm{max}\{0,k+r-m\}
.

(5.6)

|h_{m\times n,r,k}|\leq q^{-kr-\ell(n-m)+\binom{\ell}{2}}\,\frac{(\frac{1}{q^{k}};q)_{\ell}\,(\frac{1}{q^{r}};q)_{\ell}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{r+k-\ell}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{k+r-\ell}}{(\frac{1}{q^{\ell}};q)_{\ell}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{k}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{r}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{r}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{k}}.

In particular,

(5.7)

|h_{m\times n,r,k}|=O(q^{-kr-\ell(n-m)+\binom{\ell}{2}})\quad\text{ as $q\to\infty$}.

Proof.

We note that all the Pochhammer symbols in the right hand side of (
5.6
) lie in the interval
(0,1]
and tend to
1
as
q\to\infty
: this is because for natural numbers
a<b
we have

(\tfrac{1}{q^{b}};q)_{a}=(1-\tfrac{1}{q^{b}})(1-\tfrac{1}{q^{b-1}})\cdots(1-\tfrac{1}{q^{b-a+1}}).

Therefore, the assertion (
5.7
) readily follows from (
5.6
). As for the assertion in (
5.6
), we again use the fact that the expression (
5.3
) is an alternating sum of decreasing terms (by Lemma
5.3
), and hence
|h_{m\times n,r,k}|
is bounded above by the absolute value of the first (nonzero) term of the sum, which is the term with index
i=\ell
.
Using the identity
(q^{a};q)_{b}=(\frac{1}{q^{a+b-1}};q)_{b}(-1)^{b}q^{\binom{b}{2}+ab}

for natural numbers
a,b
, we can express the absolute value of the
i
-th term of (
5.3
) as

(\tfrac{1}{q})^{\left(rk-\binom{i}{2}+i(i-(r+k-m))+i(i-(r+k-n))\right)}\,\frac{(\frac{1}{q^{k}};q)_{i}\,(\frac{1}{q^{r}};q)_{i}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{r+k-i}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{k+r-i}}{(\frac{1}{q^{i}};q)_{i}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{k}\,(\frac{1}{q^{m}};q)_{r}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{r}\,(\frac{1}{q^{n}};q)_{k}}.

Taking
i=\ell
, we obtain (
5.6
).

∎

We remark that the exponent

(5.8)

E=kr+\ell(n-m)-\binom{\ell}{2}

of
\tfrac{1}{q}
in (
5.7
) is positive: if
\ell=0
, we have

E=kr>0
. If
\ell=k+r-m
, then we proceed as follows: by symmetry of
E
in
r,k
, we may assume
r\leq k
without loss of generality. We can express

E=\frac{r(2k-r+1)}{2}+\binom{m-k}{2}+\ell(m-k)+\ell(n-m),

which is non-negative because
n\geq m\geq k\geq r\geq 0
.

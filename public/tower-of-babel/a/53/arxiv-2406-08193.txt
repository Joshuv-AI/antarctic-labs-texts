Minimal Communication-Cost Statistical Learning
Milad Sefidgaran ∤ {}^{\>\nmid} Abdellatif Zaidi † \>{}^{\dagger} ∤ \>{}^{\nmid} Piotr Krasnowski ∤ {}^{\>\nmid}

Abstract
A client device which has access to n n training data samples needs to obtain a statistical hypothesis or model W W and then to send it to a remote server. The client and the server devices share some common randomness sequence as well as a prior on the hypothesis space. In this problem a suitable hypothesis or model W W should meet two distinct design criteria simultaneously: (i) small (population) risk during the inference phase and (ii) small ‘complexity’ for it to be conveyed to the server with minimum communication cost. In this paper, we propose a joint training and source coding scheme with provable in-expectation guarantees, where the expectation is over the encoder’s output message. Specifically, we show that by imposing a constraint on a suitable Kullback-Leibler divergence between the conditional distribution induced by a compressed learning model W ^ \widehat{W} given W W and the prior, one guarantees simultaneously small average empirical risk (aka training loss), small average generalization error and small average communication cost. We also consider a one-shot scenario in which the guarantees on the empirical risk and generalization error are obtained for every encoder’s output message.

Introduction

The development of distributed or decentralized machine learning solutions has witnessed a rapid increase over the last years, in particular, due to abundant applications in various areas. Examples include the federated learning (FL) of

Often, the client devices in these architectures (also simply referred to as “clients”) process their available training data samples locally and then send the output hypotheses or models to a central node or server. The process is repeated until a given loss function is minimized over the training data set; and, typically, this induces a large communication overhead. For this reason, the search for efficient model compression techniques is of paramount importance, especially in bandwidth-constrained settings such as model transmission over a finite-capacity wireless channel.

Existing approaches to model training and compression are mostly based on a “separation” principle. That is, a first processing stage during which the client learns a suitable (local) model or hypothesis on the basis of the available training dataset followed by an independent second processing stage during which the client produces a compressed version of the obtained model that it conveys to the server. Problem setup

We consider a point-to-point setup for joint local training and remote source coding, as depicted in Fig.

Data.
Let

Learning algorithm.
In this work, we consider a general stochastic learning framework. Suppose that the learning algorithm

Loss function and risks.

Overall, given

\displaystyle\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}\left[K\right]=\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}\left[\mathcal{E}(S,\mathcal{A}(S))\right].

(3)

In our framework, in addition to the chosen index

The recovered model

ii.

the generalization error of the model
\mathcal{D}(K,W_{\epsilon})

Note that by studying the generalization error and the empirical risk, we indirectly provide an analysis of the population risk of the model

III
In-expectation performance

In this section, we propose a source encoder based on the Ordered Random Coding (ORC) method and provide an in-expectation guarantee for the performance of the joint training and source coding scheme. ORC is a variant of MRC, with “near-optimal” in-expectation communication cost, that is proposed in
[
25

to overcome the communication cost of MRC, which grows with
N

P_{W|S}

P_{W|S}
is degenerate. As a result, both “vanilla” MRC and ORC fail in our case.

IV
One-shot performance

with scale 1. Denote their ordered sequence by
\tilde{G}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{G}_{N_{W}}
,
i.e.,
\tilde{G}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{G}_{N_{W}}
. We define the encoder using the ORC method introduced in
[
25
]
. That is,
\mathcal{E}(S,W)=K
is chosen according to the following rule:

\displaystyle K=\argmax_{n\leq N_{W}}\left\{\log\rho_{W}\left(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[n]\right)+\tilde{G}_{n}\right\}.

(11)

Now, we analyze the performance of this encoder.

Part i.
Similar to the related proofs in
[
23
,
25
,
26
]
, this proof is also inspired by the ideas introduced in
[
31
]
.

Using the definition of
\Delta_{\mathbf{U}}(W,K)
and the Lipschitz continuity assumption, to prove this part, it is sufficient to show that for every
(S,W)
, with probability at least
1-2\sqrt{b_{W}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\right\|{\leq}\frac{2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\hat{W}\sim P_{\hat{W}|W}}\left[\|W-\hat{W}\|^{2}\right]b_{W}}}{1{-}\sqrt{b_{W}}}.

Due to
[
25
, Theorem 3.1.]
, the distribution of
\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]
is the same as the one picked using MRC introduced in
[
23
,
32
]
. Hence, for this section, we consider the following MRC encoder: Given
(W,\mathbf{U})
, let
\mathcal{E}(S,W)
pick the index
i\in[N_{W}]
, with probability
\frac{\rho_{W}\left(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\right)}{\sum_{j\in[N_{W}]}\rho_{W}\left(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[j]\right)}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(W)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\hat{W}\sim P_{\hat{W}|W}}\left[\|W-\hat{W}\|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{U}}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\|\rho_{W}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])\right],

where the equality comes from the fact that for every
i\in[N]
,
\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\sim Q_{\hat{W}}
. Furthermore, let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},W)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\|\rho_{W}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]),

The dependence of
\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},W)
on
\mathbf{U}
shows the dependence on a given draw of the codebook, using the common randomness
\mathbf{U}
. We first claim that for each
w
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{U}}

\displaystyle\big[\big|\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},w)-\mathfrak{I}(w)\big|\big]\leq\sigma_{0,w}b_{w},

(12)

where
\sigma_{0,w}^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\hat{W}\sim P_{\hat{W}|W=w}}\big[\|w-\hat{W}\|^{2}\big]
.
This claim can be proved using
[
31
, Theorem 1.1]
, as shown in Appendix
A
.

Now, due to the choice of the encoder, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{K}\left[\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\|\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[N]}\frac{\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\|\rho_{W}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])}{\sum_{i\in[N]}\rho_{W}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},W)}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\rho_{W}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])}.

(13)

Then, from one hand, for any
\epsilon_{w}>0
, (
12
) concludes that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},w)-\mathfrak{I}(w)\right|>\tau_{w}\right)\leq\sigma_{0,w}b_{w}/\tau_{w},

(14)

and from the other hand, due to
[
31
]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum\nolimits_{i\in[N]}\rho_{w}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])-1\Big|>\epsilon_{w}\Big)\leq b_{w}/\epsilon_{w}.

(15)

Moreover, whenever
\left|\mathfrak{I}_{N}(\mathbf{U},w)-\mathfrak{I}(w)\right|\leq\tau_{w}
and
\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in[N]}\rho_{w}(\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i])-1\|\leq\epsilon_{w}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{K}\left[\|w-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\|\right]\leq\frac{\tau_{w}+\mathfrak{I}(w)\epsilon_{w}}{1-\epsilon_{w}}.

(16)

Let
\tau_{w}=\sigma_{0,w}\epsilon_{w}
. Then, for every
w
, with probability at least
1-2b_{w}/\epsilon_{w}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{K}\left[\|w-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\|\right]\leq\frac{2\sigma_{0,w}\epsilon_{w}}{1-\epsilon_{w}}.

(17)

Letting
\epsilon_{w}=\sqrt{b_{w}}
completes the proof of this part.

Part ii.
The generalization bound can be established using
[
29
, Theorem 1]
. To show this, it is sufficient to show that the learning algorithm
\tilde{A}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{n}\to\hat{\mathcal{W}}
, defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(S)=\mathcal{D}\left(K,W_{\epsilon}\right)=\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}\left[\mathcal{E}\left(S,\mathcal{A}(S)\right)\right]+W_{\epsilon},

is
(\log(N_{W}),\varepsilon_{,}\delta;d_{m})
-compressible in the sense defined in
[
29
, Definition 2]
. The condition on the rate is trivial due to the way the codebook
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{U},N}
is constructed and the encoder is defined. It remains to bound the distortion constraint

\displaystyle\varepsilon{=}\sup_{\nu_{W}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(W)}\mathbb{E}_{W,K}\left[\gen(S,\mathcal{D}\left(K,W_{\epsilon}\right))^{2}{-}\gen(S,\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K])^{2}\right].

Now, for any
\alpha_{W}
and any distribution
\nu_{W}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(W)
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38092pt\varepsilon_{\alpha_{W},\nu_{W}}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{W,K}\left[\gen(S,\mathcal{D}\left(K,W_{\epsilon}\right))^{2}-\gen(S,\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K])^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}_{K}\|W_{\epsilon}\|>\alpha_{W}\right)\right]+2\times

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\mathbb{E}_{W,K}{\Big[}\big|\gen(S,\mathcal{D}\left(K,W_{\epsilon}\right)){-}\gen(S,\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K])\big|\Big|\mathbb{E}\|W_{\epsilon}\|{\leq}\alpha_{W}{\Big]}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}_{K}\|W_{\epsilon}\|>\alpha_{W}\right)\right]+4\mathfrak{L}\mathbb{E}_{W}[\alpha_{W}],

where
W\sim\nu_{W}
,
(a)
is concluded since
\ell(z,w)\in[0,1]
, and
(b)
is derived by Lipschitzness and since
W_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{D}\left(K,W_{\epsilon}\right)-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]
. Now, combining the above inequality with

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq\inf\nolimits_{\alpha_{W}}\sup\nolimits_{\nu_{W}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}(W)}\varepsilon_{\alpha_{W},\nu_{W}},

proves the first bound on
\varepsilon
.

Next, note that by assumption
\|W_{\epsilon}\|\leq\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\|
. Hence,
\varepsilon_{\alpha_{W},\nu_{W}}
is upper bounded further by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(\mathbb{E}_{K}\big[\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K](U)\|\big]{>}\alpha_{W}\right)\right]{+}4\mathfrak{L}\mathbb{E}_{W}[\alpha_{W}].

Now, using (
17
) by letting
\alpha_{W}\mapsto\frac{2\sigma_{0,W}\epsilon_{W}}{1-\epsilon_{W}}
and
\epsilon_{W}=\frac{\sqrt{b_{W}}}{\sqrt{b_{W}}+\sqrt{4\mathfrak{L}\sigma_{0,W}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\alpha_{W},\nu_{W}}\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}/\epsilon_{W}\right]+8\mathfrak{L}\,\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\frac{\sigma_{0,W}\epsilon_{W}}{1-\epsilon_{W}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}\right]+8\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}}\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\sqrt{b_{W}\sigma_{0,W}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}\right]+8\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}\right]\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[\sigma_{0,W}\right]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}\right]+8\sqrt{\mathfrak{L}\varepsilon_{\nu_{W}}\mathbb{E}_{W}\left[b_{W}\right]}.

This concludes that
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\delta}
, which completes the proof.

Part iii.
This part can be concluded similar to

25

[
25

25

Proof:

Using the Lipschitz continuity assumption and the definition of

, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\big(\big\|W-\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[K]\big\|>\epsilon\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(\forall i\in[N]\colon\big(W,\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{U}}[i]\big)\notin\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}\big).

33

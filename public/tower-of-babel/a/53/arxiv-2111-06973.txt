Universal families of Eulerian multiple zeta values in positive characteristic
Kwun Chung, Tuan Ngo Dac, Federico Pellarin

Abstract
. We study positive characteristic multiple zeta values associated to general curves over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} together with an 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -rational point ∞ \infty as introduced by Thakur. For the case of the projective line these values were defined as analogues of classical multiple zeta values. In the present paper we first establish a general non-commutative factorization of exponential series associated to certain lattices of rank one. Next we introduce universal families of multiple zeta values of Thakur and show that they are Eulerian in full generality. In particular, we prove a conjecture of Lara Rodríguez and Thakur in [ 28 ] . One of the main ingredients of the proofs is the notion of L L -series in Tate algebras introduced by the third author [ 31 ] in 2012.



Introduction

0.1.
Classical multiple zeta values

Multiple zeta values are positive real numbers that have been involved in certain investigations by Euler in the late eighteenth century. They generalize the values of Riemann’s zeta function at integers
\geq 2
and occur naturally in the computation of their products. Surprisingly, these particular real numbers are ubiquitous in several mathematical and physical theories. For instance, they are connected with periods of mixed Tate motives, and with values of Feynman integrals in perturbative quantum field theory. It is also known that they are related to a certain universal vector bundle with connection on the projective line with the points
0,1,\infty
removed. These, and other properties, made multiple zeta values the center of intensive studies, especially in the last three decades. We refer the reader to the survey of Zagier
[
43
]
and the book of Burgos Gil and Fresán
[
15
]
for a detailed introduction to these topics, as well as for further references.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. By definition, the classical multiple zeta values are the values of the convergent series

\zeta(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})=\sum_{k_{1}>\dots>k_{r}>0}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots k_{r}^{n_{r}}},\quad\text{where }n_{i}\in\mathbb{N},\quad n_{1}\geq 2.

Here
r
is called the
depth
and
w=n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}
the
weight
of the presentation
\zeta(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
. For notational convenience, we set

\zeta(\emptyset)=1
. For
r=1
and
n\geq 2
we recover the special values
\zeta(n)
of the Riemann zeta function.

In 1735, Euler proved that

(0.1)

\frac{\zeta(2n)}{(2\pi)^{2n}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{2}\frac{B_{2n}}{(2n)!}\quad\text{ for all }n\geq 1,

where for
k\geq 0
,
B_{k}
denotes the
k
-th Bernoulli number. In view of this, it is natural to classify tuples
(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
with
n_{i}\geq 1,n_{1}\geq 2
such that the quotient
\zeta(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})/\pi^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}}
is rational. In this case, following

[
27
,
18
]
, we say that the multiple zeta value
\zeta(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
is
Eulerian
.

For example
\zeta(\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{n})
with
n\geq 1
is Eulerian. This follows from the identity

(0.2)

\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\zeta(\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{n})x^{2n+1}=\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi}=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\frac{\pi^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}x^{2n+1},

an elegant proof of which is given by Zagier in
[
44
]
. Further, one can show that
\zeta(\underbrace{3,1,\dots,3,1}_{n})
is Eulerian (conjectured in
[
43
]
and proved in
[
13
]
). We refer the reader to
[
27
, Remark after Conjecture 4.3]
and
[
39
, §7.5]
for more details and more complete references on Eulerian multiple zeta values. Brown formulated a sufficient (and conjecturally necessary) condition for Eulerian multiple zeta values in terms of motivic multiple zeta (see
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
). This condition is not completely effective (see
[
18
, §1]
for more details).

Finally, we mention that Siegel and Klingen were able to extend Euler’s formulas (
0.1
) for Dedekind zeta values
\zeta_{F}(.)
attached to a number field
F
and showed that for a totally real number field
F
and all even
n\geq 2
,
\zeta_{F}(n)
is an algebraic multiple of
\pi^{n[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
(see
[
33
]
for further details).

0.2.
Multiple zeta values in positive characteristic

There is a deep analogy between the arithmetic of number fields and that of global function fields of positive characteristic. Through this analogy one can track similarities in the comparison of the arithmetic over the ring
\mathbb{Z}
on one side, and the arithmetic over the ring
A
of rational functions over a given smooth curve
X
over a finite field, which are regular away from a distinguished point (see below for a rigorous description of the settings). Carlitz, in the years 1930s, inaugurated this study in the case of
X
the projective line (the genus zero case). As a natural consequence, a theory of positive characteristic multiple zeta values associated to
A
emerged, pioneered by Thakur. At this level of generality (
X
a curve over a finite field), the description of the algebraic relations connecting multiple zeta values offers new challenges that do not seem to be immediately visible in the classical setting. It is our aim to shed new light and perspectives on these aspects.

Throughout this paper, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements and characteristic
p>0
. Let
K
be the function field of a geometrically connected smooth projective curve
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\infty
be a place of
K
of degree
d_{\infty}=1
. Let
g
be the genus of
X
and let

(0.3)

A=H^{0}(X\setminus\{\infty\},\mathcal{O}_{X})

be the ring of elements of
K
which are regular outside
\infty
(
A
is called the
base ring
). The
\infty
-adic completion
K_{\infty}
of
K
is equipped with the normalized additive
\infty
-adic valuation
v_{\infty}:K_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{+\infty\}
. Let us set
\deg(\cdot):=-v_{\infty}(\cdot)
. The completion
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
of a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{K}_{\infty}
of
K_{\infty}
comes equipped with a unique valuation extending
v_{\infty}
, which will be again denoted by
v_{\infty}
. We fix a uniformizer
\pi
of
K_{\infty}
so that we can identify
K_{\infty}
and the field of formal Laurent series

\mathbb{F}_{q}((\pi))
. This choice also allows to introduce a sign function
\sgn:K_{\infty}^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.
This is the unique group homomorphism defined by
\sgn(\pi)=1
. We define
A_{+}
to be the set of monic elements of
A
, i.e., the set of
a\in A
such that
\sgn(a)=1
. For all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we also set
A_{+,i}=\{a\in A_{+}:\deg(a)=i\}
.

The genus 0 case

We consider the genus
0
case where
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\infty
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point on it. Then
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
the polynomial ring in
\theta
, a rational function over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
which has a simple pole at
\infty
and which is regular everywhere else on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We denote by
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
the fraction field of
A
and by
K_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((1/\theta))
the completion of
K
at the place associated with
\infty
.

Recall that
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of positive integers. In
[
16
]
Carlitz introduced, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the elements
\zeta_{A}(n)
given by the convergent series

\zeta_{A}(n):=\sum_{a\in A_{+}}\frac{1}{a^{n}}\in K_{\infty}

which, through the previously mentioned analogy, correspond to classical special zeta values in the function field setting and are called the Carlitz zeta values. For any tuple of positive integers
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, Thakur
[
36
]
considered the elements
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
(or
\zeta_{A}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
) of
K_{\infty}
defined by the convergent series

(0.4)

\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s}):=\sum\frac{1}{a_{1}^{s_{1}}\ldots a_{r}^{s_{r}}}\in K_{\infty}

where the sum runs through the set of tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in A_{+}^{r}
with
\deg a_{1}>\ldots>\deg a_{r}
.
These are
Thakur’s multiple zeta values
, characteristic
p
analogues of the classical multiple zeta values.
We call
r
the
depth
of
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
and
w(\mathfrak{s})=s_{1}+\dots+s_{r}
the
weight
of
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
. We extend the definition of
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
to the empty tuple by defining the associated zeta value
\zeta_{A}(\emptyset)
to be 1. We note that Carlitz zeta values
\zeta_{A}(n)
are exactly depth one multiple zeta values. It can be proved that for any
\mathfrak{s}
as above,
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})\in K_{\infty}^{\times}
(see
[
38
]
). A much stronger property holds: Thakur’s multiple zeta values
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
and
1
span an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space which is also an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra, graded by the weights (see
[
17
,
39
]
).

In 1935 Carlitz
[
16
]
introduced analogues
BC_{n}\in K^{\times}
(
n\geq 1
) of the Bernoulli numbers of even order
and proved an analogue of Euler’s result (
0.1
) (see also
[
25
, §9.2]
):

\frac{\zeta_{A}(n)}{\widetilde{\pi}^{n}}=\frac{BC_{n}}{\Gamma_{n}}\quad\text{ for all }n\geq 1,\ n\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}.

Here
\Gamma_{n}\in A
is the
n
-th Carlitz factorial
, and
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}
denotes the Carlitz period which is defined up to multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
(see
[
25
,
36
]
).

Thakur called
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})

Eulerian
(see
[
36
, Definition 5.10.8]
) if
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})/\widetilde{\pi}^{w(\mathfrak{s})}
belongs to
K
. Since
\zeta(ps_{1},\dots,ps_{r})=\zeta(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})^{p}
, we restrict ourselves to
primitive
tuples
\mathfrak{s}
, characterized by the property that not all the coefficients
s_{i}
are divisible by
p
.

Chen
[
20
]
, Lara Rodríguez and Thakur
[
27
]
proved the following remarkable theorem:

Theorem 0.1

(Chen, Lara Rodríguez and Thakur)
.

Assume that
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
. Then for all
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
, the multiple zeta value

\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n}-1,(q-1)q^{n},\ldots,(q-1)q^{n+k-1}\right)

is Eulerian.

Contrary to the classical setting, for
q>2
, it is conjectured that these series exhaust
all
the (primitive) Eulerian tuples when the depth exceeds
2
(see
[
18
, §6.2]
and
[
39
, §7.3]
). The depth is formally defined in §
1.2
.

The higher genus case

The analogy mentioned in the beginning of §
0.2
focuses on the comparison between the arithmetic theory over
\mathbb{Z}
and that over an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra
A
as in (
0.3
). For this reason, Carlitz’s theory only gives an incomplete view.
We now move to include the general situation of the higher genus cases and present our main results.

The definition (
0.4
) of
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
extends without modification to the case of general
A
with
\infty\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
d_{\infty}=\deg(\infty)=1
as in (
0.3
) (see also §
1.2
for precise definitions). Following Lara Rodríguez and Thakur in
[
28
]
, we say that
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
is
Eulerian
if
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})/\widetilde{\pi}_{A}^{w(\mathfrak{s})}
is algebraic over
K
, where
\widetilde{\pi}_{A}:=\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is the period attached to the
standard sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one
associated to
A
(see §
3.1
).

It turns out that for all
n\geq 1
and
n\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}
,
\zeta_{A}(n)
is Eulerian (see
[
25
, §8.18]
and note the proximity with the Klingen-Siegel theorem mentioned above). However, only five other cases of Eulerian multiple zeta values were known by the recent work of Lara Rodríguez and Thakur
[
28
]
: they are all of the form
\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,q(q-1)\right)
or
\zeta_{A}\left(q^{2}-1,(q-1)q^{2}\right)
for one of the four base rings
A
of class number one. The proofs are based on explicit log-algebraicity formulas developed in
[
34
,
37
]
(see for examples
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
30
]
for further developments about log-algebraicity identities). Based on extensive use of computer aided numerical experiments, Lara Rodríguez and Thakur conjectured that the appropriate generalization of the statement of Theorem
0.1
remains valid for these four base rings (see
[
28
, Conjecture 3.1]
).

0.3.
Main results

In this paper we work with the general case where
A
is an arbitrary base ring as given in §
0.2
. We first establish a non-commutative analogue of Zagier’s formula (
0.2
) in our settings, with
A
as in (
0.3
) and
d_{\infty}=1
.

Theorem 0.2

(Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
A
be an arbitrary base ring and let
\phi
be the corresponding standard sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one. Then

\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\right)\tau^{k}=\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{-1}\exp_{\phi}\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]].

In particular, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\zeta_{A}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{k-1}(q-1))
is Eulerian.

Here
\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is any fundamental period attached to
\phi
and
\exp_{\phi}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]]
is the exponential function associated to
\phi
(see §
3.1
). As
\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}
is defined up to multiplication by an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, the formula does not depend on its choice.

We then extend Theorem
0.1
with explicit formulas for a base ring
A
as in the hypotheses of Theorem
0.2
, and prove the aforementioned conjecture of Lara Rodríguez and Thakur in full generality.

Theorem 0.3

(Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let
A
be an arbitrary base ring and let
n\geq 1
be an integer. We denote by
H\subset K_{\infty}
the Hilbert class field of
A
. Then for all
k\geq 0
, there exists an explicitly computable constant
\alpha_{n,k}\in H
(see (
3.8
)) such that

\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n}-1,(q-1)q^{n},\ldots,(q-1)q^{n+k-1}\right)=\alpha_{n,k}\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n+k}-1\right).

In particular,
\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n}-1,(q-1)q^{n},\ldots,(q-1)q^{n+k-1}\right)
is Eulerian.

As pointed out by Lara Rodríguez and Thakur in
[
28
]
, such identities also hold, in the genus zero case, at ‘finite levels’ (that is, at the level of partial sums). However, different phenomena occur in the case of
X
of genus
g>0
. In this case, identities of multiple zeta values (‘infinite level’ identities) are regulated in a different way and Theorems
0.2
and
0.3
do not seem to mirror identities at the finite level. It is in fact the analysis of the interesting phenomenology described in
[
28
]
that brought us to the path that gave rise to the present paper. The reader will also notice similarities in the processes behind the proofs of, on one side,
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
and, on the other side, the proof of
[
24
, Lemma 6.11]
.

We now briefly describe the ingredients of the proofs. Our approach is completely different from that of the previous works. To prove Theorem
0.2
we provide a non-commutative factorization of the so-called Carlitz-Hayes polynomials. Then, passing to the limit, we obtain a non-commutative factorization of the function
\exp_{A}:=\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{-1}\exp_{\phi}\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}
from which Theorem
0.2
follows.

The proof of Theorem
0.3
is based on the key notion of
L
-series in Tate algebras
introduced by the third author in
[
31
]
. This notion has turned out to be very fruitful in function field arithmetic, see for example
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
24
,
26
]
. In our setting, these functions can be identified with rigid analytic functions over the analytification
(X\setminus\{\infty\})^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
of the affine curve
\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. Note that other classical functions of the theory, such as the exponential functions
\exp_{\phi}
and
\exp_{A}
, are rather rigid analytic functions over the rigid affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1,an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
.

We prove a key and unexpected new general identity in Theorem
2.4
among
L
-series in Tate algebras at finite levels. As a consequence, we obtain a harmonic product formula in Proposition
3.5
which allows us to conclude.

Finally, as an application, for class number one base rings, we show in Theorem
4.2
that our explicit expressions match with those conjectured by Lara Rodríguez and Thakur
[
28
, Conjecture 3.3]
.

1.2.
Multiple zeta values of Thakur, and in Tate algebras

We follow Thakur in
[
39
, §3]
and Lara Rodríguez-Thakur in
[
28
, §2]
. To the datum

(K,\infty,\sgn)
we associate certain multiple zeta values

\zeta_{A}\big(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\big)\in K_{\infty}

in the following way.
For
d\geq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the
power sum of degree
d
and order
n
given by

S_{d}(n)=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{1}{a^{n}}\in K.

By convention we define empty sums to be 0 and empty products to be 1. Thus if
A_{+,d}=\emptyset
, then
S_{d}(n)=0
. Note that some such sums can vanish.
Along with this, we also set

S_{<d}(n):=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}S_{i}(n)\in K

and iteratively for

and
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle S_{d}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

\displaystyle:=S_{d}(s_{1})S_{<d}(s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}),

\displaystyle S_{<d}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}S_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in K.

It is easily seen that
S_{d}(n)
tends to
0
in
K_{\infty}
, as

tends to
\infty
, the sequence

S_{<d}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
converges in
K_{\infty}
for any choice of
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. We write

(1.1)

\zeta_{A}\big(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}\big):=\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}S_{<d}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in K_{\infty}.

For notational convenience, we also set

\zeta_{A}(\emptyset):=1\in K_{\infty}.

For an
r
-tuple
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we shall write
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
instead of using the long expression (
1.1
). The
weight
of
\mathfrak{s}
is
w(\mathfrak{s})=s_{1}+\dots+s_{r}
and its

is

r
. We shall also say that the presentation
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
is of
depth
r
and

w(\mathfrak{s})
.

These elements of
K_{\infty}
are the
\infty
-adic multiple zeta values of the papers
[

,
39
,
40
]
. At this level of generality, not all the basic properties of the multiple zeta values of
§

are known to be true. For instance, it is unclear if all the values defined in (

) are
non-zero. Moreover, while it is fairly well known that the

-subvector space they span in

also is an

-algebra (with the product rules being identical to those in the genus zero case), it seems that we miss
a proof that the algebra they generate is graded by the weights.

1.2.1.
A family of rigid analytic functions

We recall that
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is the completion of an algebraic closure
\overline{K}_{\infty}
of

.
We present here the basic

multiple zeta values in Tate algebras
that we study in the paper.
We consider the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

\chi:A\rightarrow A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}

. There is a natural bijection

\mathbb{F}_{q}

We denote by

.
For any given

defines a rational map

which is regular everywhere away from

at
\infty
.
If

\chi_{x}(a)
, or
\chi(a)_{x}
, or
\chi(a)|_{x}
, the image of

by
\chi(a)
and we call it the

\chi(a)
at

.

If
F
is a subfield of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, we consider the
Tate algebra

\mathbb{T}(F):=\widehat{A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}F}_{\|\cdot\|}

with the completion
\widehat{(\cdot)}
relative to
\|\cdot\|
the multiplicative Gauss valuation extending a fixed multiplicative valuation
|\cdot|
over

, trivial over

.
Let
\mathcal{B}=(\beta_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
be a basis of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space

. Note that
\mathcal{I}
is countable set. Every element

of
\mathbb{T}(F)
can be expanded in a unique way as a series

with
\widehat{F}
the completion of
F
, where
f_{i}\rightarrow 0
for the Fréchet filter over
\mathcal{I}
. Then
\|f\|=\sup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}|f_{i}|
. An
entire function

f

over
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}
is by definition one such formal series in
\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
such that additionally,

|f_{i}|\rho^{\deg(\beta_{i})}\rightarrow 0
for all
\rho>1
. Equivalently, the sequence
(f_{i}\chi_{x}(\beta_{i}))_{i}

tends to zero for all

.
We denote by
f(x)
or
f|_{x}
the sum of the convergent series

\sum_{i}f_{i}\chi_{x}(\beta_{i})\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. This is the
evaluation of the entire function

at
x\in X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}
.
It is not difficult to see that this evaluation does not depend on the choice of the basis
\mathcal{B}
, and that the evaluations taken with respect to different bases agree. Also, let
X^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
be the analytification of
X
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. It is easy to verify that if
f=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\beta_{i}f_{i}
is an entire function over
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}
,
then the everywhere convergent series defines a rigid analytic function

X^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. In the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\infty
an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point, this
coincides with the usual notion of an entire function
f:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
(see
[
25
, Chapter 3]
). We denote by
\mathbb{E}
the

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-algebra of entire functions
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

The unique continuous and open
A\otimes 1
-linear automorphism
\tau
of
\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
extending

over

induces an automorphism of
\mathbb{E}
. Explicitly, if

f=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\beta_{i}f_{i}

is the series expansion of the entire function

\mathcal{B}

is another entire function over
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}
, well defined independently of the choice of the basis
\mathcal{B}
. If
x\mapsto x^{(1)}
denotes the Frobenius map

then

for all

.

We consider the rational functions
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}

(1.2)

\mathcal{S}_{d}:=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{\chi(a)}{a}\in A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}K.

Note that they are also entire in the above terminology.

In the present paper, we study the functions
\tau^{n}(Z_{k})
where, for all
k\geq 0
, we define
Z_{k}
by the formal series

Z_{k}:=\sum_{d\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{d}S_{<d}\Big(q-1,(q-1)q,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\Big)

which converges in
\mathbb{T}(K)
. They are all entire functions, as shown by the following lemma:

Lemma 1.1
.

For all
k\geq 0
, the series
Z_{k}\in\mathbb{T}(K)
defines an entire function
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for all
x\in X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}
, the sequence of evaluations

\big(\mathcal{S}_{i}|_{x}\big)_{i\geq 0}

of
\mathcal{S}_{i}
at
x
tends to zero uniformly for the standard admissible covering of
X^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\{\infty\}
.

The proof we give is a simple adaptation of the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 7]
.
We set, for
r\geq 0
,
y_{r}=q^{r+1}-1
which
p
-adically tends to
-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as
r\to\infty
. Note that for all
r\geq 0
, assuming that
A_{+,i}\neq\emptyset
,

\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\chi(a)a^{y_{r}}=\sum_{b\in A(<i)}L(\eta_{i}+b)L_{0}(\eta_{i}+b)^{q-1}\cdots L_{r}(\eta_{i}+b)^{q-1}\in A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}A,

where
\eta_{i}
is any element of
A_{+,i}
,
L
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear form
a\mapsto a\otimes 1

and
L_{j}
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear form
a\mapsto 1\otimes a^{q^{j}}
for
j=0,\ldots,r
. By
[
25
, Lemma 8.8.1]
,
\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\chi(a)a^{y_{r}}
vanishes if
1+(r+1)(q-1)<\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(A(<i))
. We can therefore choose

r=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(A(<i))-\begin{cases}2\quad\text{if }q>2,\\
3\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

assuming at once
i
large enough so that
r
is non-negative. In particular we have, for this choice of
r
, the identity
\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\chi(a)a^{y_{r}}=0
.

We consider
a\in A_{+,i}
. Since
\epsilon(a)=1
, there exists a unique element
\langle a\rangle\in K_{\infty}^{\times}\cap\operatorname{Ker}(\epsilon)
, depending on
\pi
, such that
a=\langle a\rangle\pi^{-i}
. Note that
|\langle a\rangle-1|\leq|\pi|<1
. We have

\langle a\rangle^{-1}-\langle a\rangle^{y_{r}}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\Big(\binom{-1}{j}-\binom{y_{r}}{j}\Big)(\langle a\rangle-1)^{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[[\pi]].

Since
\binom{-1}{j}-\binom{y_{r}}{j}=0
for
j=0,\ldots,q^{r+1}-1
, the above difference has multiplicative valuation strictly smaller than
|\pi|^{q^{r+1}}
. This implies that

\mathcal{S}_{i}\in A\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}K_{\infty}
has the property that there is a finite expansion

\mathcal{S}_{i}=\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}a\otimes\alpha_{a}
with, for
a\in A_{+,i}
,
\alpha_{a}\in K_{\infty}
such that
|\alpha_{a}|<|\pi|^{i+q^{r+1}}
. From this we deduce that
the sequence of entire functions
\mathcal{S}_{i}
tends to zero uniformly for the standard admissible covering of
X^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\setminus\{\infty\}
. The covering is given by the filtered union

\bigcup_{\begin{smallmatrix}\rho\in|\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}|\\
\rho>1\end{smallmatrix}}\operatorname{Spm}\Big(\widehat{A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}_{\|\cdot\|_{\rho}}\Big),

where
\operatorname{Spm}
denotes the maximal spectrum, and where we take the completions with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\rho}
the multiplicative valuation which restricts to
|\cdot|
over
1\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
,
such that
\|a\otimes 1\|_{\rho}=\rho^{\deg(a)}
. By the above discussion, for all
x\in\operatorname{Spm}\big(\widehat{A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}_{\|\cdot\|_{\rho}}\big)
we have, by the maximum principle, and for large values of
i
:

|\mathcal{S}_{i}(x)|\leq\|\mathcal{S}_{i}\|_{\rho}\leq\rho^{i}|\pi|^{i+q^{r+1}}

which tends to zero for any fixed

\rho>1
as
i\rightarrow\infty
.∎

2.
A universal formula

2.1.
Auxiliary results

We recall several results from
[
34
, §0 and §1]
. For all
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set, with appropriate conventions for empty sums and products,

\displaystyle D_{i}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}a,

\displaystyle e_{i}(z)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{a\in A(<i)}(z-a),

\displaystyle\frac{e_{i}(z)}{D_{i}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\geq 0}A_{ik}z^{q^{k}}

\displaystyle S_{i}(n)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\frac{1}{a^{n}},

\displaystyle S_{i}

\displaystyle:=S_{i}(1)=\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\frac{1}{a}.

If
A_{+,i}\neq\emptyset
, then by
[
34
, Eq. (13)]
,

e_{i+1}(z)=e_{i}(z)^{q}-D_{i}^{q-1}e_{i}(z).

Otherwise, if
A_{+,i}=\emptyset
, we get
e_{i+1}(z)=e_{i}(z)
.
In particular, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
,
D_{i+1}A_{(i+1)0}
and
D_{i}^{q}A_{i0}
agree up to a sign so that we can write
D_{i+1}A_{(i+1)0}=\pm D_{i}^{q}A_{i0}
and

A_{i0}=\pm\frac{(D_{0}D_{1}\dots D_{i-1})^{q-1}}{D_{i}}\neq 0.

Now, if
A_{+,i}\neq\emptyset
, by
[
34
, Eq. (18)]
, we have

\frac{A_{i0}}{1-\sum_{k=0}^{i}A_{ik}z^{q^{k}}}=-\sum_{a\in A_{+,i}}\frac{1}{z-a}=\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{i}(n+1)z^{n}.

Consequently,

(2.1)

S_{i}=S_{i}(1)=A_{i0}\neq 0,

and for all
1\leq n\leq q-1
,

(2.2)

S_{i}(n)=S_{i}^{n}.

For all
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, we recall that we have set (see (
1.2
))

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in A_{+,d}}\frac{\chi(a)}{a}.

We additionally set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{<d}:=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\mathcal{S}_{i},\quad S_{<d}:=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}S_{i}.

We put
\mathcal{S}_{\leq d}=\mathcal{S}_{<d+1}
and
S_{\leq d}=S_{<d+1}
.

If
D
is a divisor over
X_{\overline{K}}
, then we denote by
\mathcal{L}(D)
the
\overline{K}
-subvector space of the fraction field of
X_{\overline{K}}
consisting of functions
f
such that
(f)+D
is effective:
(f)\geq-D
. For all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, we define

to be the smallest non-negative integer such that
\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{m}\infty)=m+1
. Note that
\mathcal{L}(j_{m}(\infty))=A(\leq j_{m})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{K}
,
where
A(\leq\ell)
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space spanned by the elements

such that
\deg(a)\leq\ell
. We have
j_{0}=0<j_{1}<j_{2}<...
and for
m
sufficiently large,

by the Riemann-Roch theorem.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
m\geq 1
, the function
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
vanishes at
\Xi,\dots,\Xi^{(m-1)}
.

Proof.

Let us choose an element
\eta_{m}\in A_{+,j_{m}}
.
Then
A_{+,j_{m}}=\eta_{m}+A(\leq j_{m-1})
. Note that by the definition of the sequence
\{j_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}A(\leq j_{m-1})=m
. Since

\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}(\Xi^{(j)})=\sum_{a\in A_{+,j_{m}}}a^{q^{j}-1}=\sum_{b\in A(\leq j_{m-1})}L_{0}(\eta_{m}+b)^{q-1}\cdots L_{j-1}(\eta_{m}+b)^{q-1}

where
L_{i}
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear form
a\mapsto a^{q^{i}}
for

,
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}(\Xi^{(j)})
vanishes for
j=0,\ldots,m-1
in virtue of
[
25
, Lemma 8.8.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The set
\{\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}\}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{L}(j_{m}\infty)
.

Proof.

We observe that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}
has a pole of order
j_{k}
at
\infty
. The integers
j_{0},\dots,j_{m}
being distinct,
\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
are linearly independent over
\overline{K}
. Since
\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{m}\infty)=m+1
, the Lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

The function
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
does not vanish at
\Xi^{(m)}
.

Proof.

We suppose by contradiction that
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
vanishes at
\Xi^{(m)}
. Combining with Lemma
2.1
implies that for all
k\geq m
,
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}
belong to
\mathcal{L}(j_{k}\infty-\Xi-\dots-\Xi^{(m)})
. We choose

sufficiently large so that by the Riemann-Roch theorem,

•

\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{k}\infty)=j_{k}+1-g
,

•

\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{k}\infty-\Xi-\dots-\Xi^{(m)})=j_{k}-(m+1)+1-g
.

The first equation implies
j_{k}+1-g=k+1
. Putting this into the second equation yields

\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{k}\infty-\Xi-\dots-\Xi^{(m)})=j_{k}-(m+1)+1-g=k-m.

By Lemma
2.2
, we know that
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}
are linearly independent over
\overline{K}
. Thus we get a contradiction since

\displaystyle k-m

\displaystyle=\dim_{\overline{K}}\mathcal{L}(j_{k}\infty-\Xi-\dots-\Xi^{(m)})

\displaystyle\geq\dim_{\overline{K}}\text{Vect}(\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}})

\displaystyle=k-m+1.

We conclude that
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
does not vanish at
\Xi^{(m)}
.
∎

2.2.
A key identity

We now prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.4
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, we have

\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}=\mathcal{S}_{\leq i}\cdot S_{i}(q-1).

Proof.

Note that if
i\neq j_{m}
for all
m
, then
A_{+,i}=\emptyset
. Thus
\mathcal{S}_{i}=S_{i}(q-1)=0
and we are done. Therefore, it suffices to prove that for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
,

(2.3)

\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}=\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{m}}\cdot S_{j_{m}}(q-1).

We observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}=S_{j_{m}}(q-1)=S_{j_{m}}^{q-1}.

Here the second equality holds by (
2.2
). By (
2.1
), it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}\neq 0
. Thus (
2.3
) is equivalent to

(2.4)

\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}=\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{m}}.

We now prove (
2.4
) by induction on
m
. For
m=0
, (
2.4
) holds since
\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{m}}=\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}=S_{j_{m}}(q-1)=1
. Suppose that for all
0\leq k\leq m-1
,

\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}=\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{k}}.

We show that

\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}=\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{m}}.

In fact,

F:=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}}{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}\in\mathcal{L}(j_{m}\infty).

By Lemma
2.2
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}
forms a basis of the
\overline{K}
-vector space
\mathcal{L}(j_{m}\infty)
. Therefore, there exist
a_{0},\dots,a_{m}\in\overline{K}
such that

(2.5)

F=a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}.

We show by induction on
0\leq k\leq m-1
that
a_{k}=1
. For
k=0
, we evaluate (
2.5
) at
\Xi
. The left-hand side gives
F|_{\Xi}=1
. By Lemma
2.1
, the right-hand side returns

a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\Big|_{\Xi}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}\Big|_{\Xi}=a_{0}.

We then obtain
a_{0}=1
.

Suppose that for all
j
such that
0\leq j<k
, we have
a_{j}=1
. We have to prove that
a_{k}=1
. We evaluate (
2.5
) at
\Xi^{(k)}
. Note that
1\leq k\leq m-1
. By Lemma
2.1
,

F|_{\Xi^{(k)}}=0.

We get

\displaystyle(a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}})\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+\dots+a_{k}\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+\dots+\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+(a_{k}-1)\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}+\dots+\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}})\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+(a_{k}-1)\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+(a_{k}-1)\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}.

Here the second equality follows from Lemma
2.1
again. The third equality holds as
a_{0}=\dots=a_{k-1}=1
.

Since
k\leq m-1
, by the induction hypothesis and Lemma
2.1
,

\mathcal{S}_{\leq j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}}{{\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}}=0.

We then get

\displaystyle(a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}})\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}=(a_{k}-1)\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}.

Putting all together yields

0=F|_{\Xi^{(k)}}=a_{0}\mathcal{S}_{j_{0}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}+\dots+a_{m}\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}=(a_{k}-1)\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathcal{S}_{j_{k}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(k)}}\neq 0
. We deduce that
a_{k}=1
as desired.

To finish the proof, we show that
a_{m}=1
. We look at the sign of both sides of (
2.5
). The left-hand side gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sgn}(F)=\frac{\widetilde{\sgn}(\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)})}{\mathcal{S}_{j_{m}}^{(1)}\Big|_{\Xi}}=\frac{S_{j_{m}}^{q}}{S_{j_{m}}(q-1)}=S_{j_{m}}.

Here the last equality follows from Lemma
2.2
. The right-hand side equals
a_{m}S_{j_{m}}
. Thus
S_{j_{m}}=a_{m}S_{j_{m}}
. As
S_{j_{m}}\neq 0
, we deduce
a_{m}=1
and the proof is complete.
∎

As a direct consequence, since

Sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld modules

If

-algebra we denote by

the skew polynomial ring in
\tau

the ‘ring of Frobenius power series’ (denoted by

Let
\phi:A\rightarrow\overline{K}_{\infty}[\tau]
be a rank one Drinfeld module such that for all

\phi_{a}=a+\cdots+\sgn(a)\tau^{\deg a}.

Such a Drinfeld module
\phi
is said to be

. In
[
26
]
a sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld module is called a Drinfeld-Hayes module. By

, there always exist sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld modules. Further, if we denote by

the Hilbert class field of

the integral closure of

There exist unique elements

such that

\exp_{\phi},\log_{\phi}\in 1+H[[\tau]]\tau,

\forall a\in A,\quad\exp_{\phi}a=\phi_{a}\exp_{\phi},

\exp_{\phi}\log_{\phi}=\log_{\phi}\exp_{\phi}=1.

We write

\displaystyle\exp_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\epsilon_{i}\tau^{i},\quad\epsilon_{i}\in H,

\displaystyle\log_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}\tau^{i},\quad\lambda_{i}\in H.

The formal series
\exp_{\phi},
and
\log_{\phi}
are respectively called
the exponential series
and
the logarithm series
associated to
\phi
. Additionally,
\exp_{\phi}
defines, in a unique way,
an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear entire function
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
(unlike the functions

which are
entire
X(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\setminus\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
).
In what follows, we identify the operator
\exp_{\phi}
with this entire function
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
hence tolerating an abuse of notation.
The kernel of
\exp_{\phi}
is a projective
A
-module of rank one embedded discretely in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.
By
[
35
, Theorem 5.8]
, the coefficients
\lambda_{i}
are non-zero for all

. Theorem 3.2 of
[
35
]
describes non-vanishing criteria for the coefficients
\epsilon_{i}
’s at a superior level of generality, i.e. with
\infty
a closed point of
X
not necessarily
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational. Note that the condition
d_{\infty}=1
ensures that they are all non-zero.

Drinfeld proved that there is a bijection between the set of sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld module
\phi
and a set of certain functions

called

in the rational function field

of
X_{\overline{K}}
(see
[
22
,
23
,
35
]
). It follows that one can associate a shtuka function

to any sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one
\phi
. The divisor
(f_{\phi})
associated to

can be expressed in the following way:

(f_{\phi})=V_{\phi}^{(1)}-V_{\phi}+\Xi-\infty,

for some effective divisor

of
X(\overline{K})
of degree

. We call it
the Drinfeld divisor
attached to
\phi
. Note that the points
\Xi
and
\infty
do not belong to the support of

(see
[
35
, Corollary 0.3.3]
).

There exists a unique sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one
\phi
, called the
standard
sign-normalized Drinfeld module, such that the kernel of
\exp_{\phi}
is a free
A
-module of rank one. From now on, we only work with this standard Drinfeld module
\phi
. Then,

\operatorname{Ker}(\exp_{\phi})=A\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}

for some element
\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}
, uniquely defined up to multiplication by a factor in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. We also denote by
f
and
V
, respectively, the associated shtuka function and the associated Drinfeld divisor.

We now come back to the multiple zeta values of Thakur, in
K_{\infty}
. We choose an
r
-tuple
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, with
r>0
. We recall from the introduction the following definition (see the first paragraph concerning the higher genus cases).

Definition 3.1
.

The multiple zeta value
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})
is
Eulerian
if
\zeta_{A}(\mathfrak{s})/\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{w(\mathfrak{s})}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is algebraic over
K
.

Remark 3.2
.

We consider the genus
0
case, i.e.,
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\infty
an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point. Then as already noticed,
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
and
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
for some rational function
\theta
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In this case we have
\phi=C
the
Carlitz module
. It is uniquely defined by the identity
C_{\theta}=\theta+\tau
in
K[\tau]
. We write
\widetilde{\pi}=\widetilde{\pi}_{C}
and we have already mentioned that if
q-1\mid n
with
n\geq 1
,
\zeta_{A}(n)
is Eulerian, see
[
25
,
36
]
for more details. By using a positive characteristic variant of Baker’s theory and a seminal result of Anderson and Thakur
[
4
]
, Yu proved the following result for

:
\zeta_{A}(n)/\widetilde{\pi}^{n}\in\overline{K}
if and only if
\zeta_{A}(n)/\widetilde{\pi}^{n}\in K
, and of course the latter property occurs if
q-1\mid n
(see
[
41
, Corollary 2.6]
). This result was generalized for multiple zeta values associated to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
, of arbitrary depth, by Chang
[
17
]
. Chang and Yu explicitly computed in
[
19
,
42
]
the transcendence degree of the subfield of

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
generated by
K
and the zeta values
\zeta_{A}(n)
,
n\geq 1
. The methods of
[
17
,
19
]
do not use Baker’s theory but rest on the transcendence methods of Anderson, Brownawell and Papanikolas
[
3
]
, and Papanikolas
[
29
]
.

Recall that
\exp_{\phi}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\epsilon_{i}\tau^{i}
with
\epsilon_{i}\in H
. Let us consider

\exp_{A}(z):=z\prod_{a\in A\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\frac{z}{a}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}.

This product converges for all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and defines an entire
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
function
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with kernel
A\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
that we identify with
an operator

\exp_{A}=\sum_{k\geq 0}e_{i}\tau^{i}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]].

The link between the operators
\exp_{A}
and
\exp_{\phi}
is given by the following identity in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[[\tau]]
:

(3.1)

\exp_{A}=\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{-1}\exp_{\phi}\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}.

We also have the identity of meromorphic functions over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and of formal series of
K_{\infty}[[z]]
:

\frac{z}{\exp_{A}(z)}=1+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}n\geq 1\\
(q-1)\mid n\end{smallmatrix}}\zeta_{A}(n)z^{n}\in K_{\infty}[[z]].

From it we get

(3.2)

\zeta_{A}(q^{k}-1)=\lambda_{k}\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{q^{k}-1},\quad k\geq 0.

Note that this implies
\lambda_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\geq 0
, which is Theorem 5.8 of
[
35
]
(and essentially the same proof). Further, we deduce that for all
n\geq 1
with
n\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}
,
\zeta_{A}(n)
is Eulerian (see
[
25
, Lemma 8.18.1]
).

3.2.
Carlitz-Hayes polynomials

We know that for all
n\geq 1
there exists a unique polynomial called
the
n
-th Carlitz-Hayes polynomial

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\in K[\tau]

such that, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(z)=z\prod_{a\in A(<n)\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\frac{z}{a}\right).

Here
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(z)
denotes the evaluation of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
at
z
.
Let
\kappa_{n,m}
be the coefficient of
\tau^{m}
in
\mathcal{E}_{n}
seen as a linear combination
of
1,\tau,\tau^{2},\ldots

Proposition 3.3
.

With the above notation, we have

\kappa_{n,m}=(-1)^{m}S_{<n}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{m-1}(q-1))

Proof.

Since the zeros of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
are exactly the elements of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space
A(<n)
with multiplicity
1
, we can write

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}=(1-\alpha_{n-1}\tau)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n-1}

for some
\alpha_{n-1}\in K
. Thus we obtain a non-commutative factorization

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}=(1-\alpha_{n-1}\tau)\cdots(1-\alpha_{1}\tau)(1-\alpha_{0}\tau)

with
\alpha_{0}=1
. It follows that

(3.3)

\kappa_{n,1}=-(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n-1}).

Now, by considering the zeros, degree and the constant term, we see that, for any
a\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\times}
,

\prod_{c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(1-\frac{z}{ca}\right)=1-\frac{z^{q-1}}{a^{q-1}}.

Thus

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(z)=z\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A(<n)\setminus\{0\},\\
a\text{ monic}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{z^{q-1}}{a^{q-1}}\right).

We deduce

(3.4)

\kappa_{n,1}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A(<n)\setminus\{0\},\\
a\text{ monic}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{a^{q-1}}.

Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) yields, inductively,

\alpha_{n-1}=\sum_{a\in A_{+,n-1}}\frac{1}{a^{q-1}}=S_{n-1}(q-1).

We then get an explicit factorization

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}=\Big(1-S_{n-1}(q-1)\tau\Big)\dots\Big(1-S_{1}(q-1)\tau\Big).

The Proposition follows.
∎

Note that the multiple zeta value
\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\right)
has depth
k
. If
k=0
, we have set

\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\right)=\zeta_{A}(\emptyset)=1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

We have the following identity

(3.5)

\exp_{A}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\right)\tau^{k}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]].

In particular, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.6)

\zeta_{A}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{k-1}(q-1))=(-1)^{k}\epsilon_{k}\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{q^{k}-1}.

Proof.

We observe that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(z)
converges uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
to the function
\exp_{A}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]]
. By the previous discussion, (
3.5
) follows immediately and, viewing (
3.1
), it suffices to justify (
3.6
).
∎

As a direct consequence of Theorem
3.4
we deduce that the multiple zeta values
\zeta_{A}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{k-1}(q-1))
are all Eulerian in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Note that the special case of
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
is considered in
[
32
, Proposition 4.4.9]
. Unlike
(
3.2
), (
3.6
) does not seem to deliver non-vanishing properties of the coefficients
\epsilon_{k}
.
We can however consider the reverse implication and deduce that the multiple zeta values
\zeta_{A}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{k-1}(q-1))
are non-zero by using
[
35
, Theorem 3.2]
.

3.3.
Harmonic products

We are ready to prove our main result, by variants of the harmonic products discussed in
[
24
]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have the following identity of entire functions over
\operatorname{Spec}(A)^{an}_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}
:

\displaystyle Z_{0}\cdot\zeta_{A}(q-1,\dots,q^{k-1}(q-1))=Z_{k}+Z_{k-1}^{(1)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle Z_{0}\cdot\zeta_{A}(q-1,\dots,q^{k}(q-1))-Z_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{\leq i}\cdot S_{i}(q-1,q(q-1),\dots,q^{k}(q-1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{\leq i}\cdot S_{i}(q-1)\cdot S_{<i}(q(q-1),\dots,q^{k}(q-1))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{i}^{(1)}\cdot S_{<i}(q(q-1),\dots,q^{k}(q-1))

\displaystyle=Z_{k-1}^{(1)}.

∎

As a consequence, we obtain

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Then for all
k\geq 0
, there exists
\alpha_{n,k}\in H
such that

(3.7)

\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n}-1,(q-1)q^{n},\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1+n}\right)=\alpha_{n,k}\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n+k}-1\right).

Further,

(3.8)

\alpha_{n,k}=(-1)^{k}\lambda_{n+k}^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{n+i}\epsilon_{k-i}^{q^{n+i}}.

In particular, the multiple zeta value
\zeta_{A}(q^{n}-1,q^{n}(q-1),\dots,q^{n+k-1}(q-1))
is Eulerian.

Note that the depth of the multiple zeta value in the left-hand side of (
3.7
) equals
k+1
.
The coefficient
\alpha_{n,k}
is well defined because
\lambda_{n+k}
never vanishes, as previously noticed.
If
k=0
the left-hand side is just
\zeta_{A}(q^{n}-1)
so that
\alpha_{n,k}=1
in this case.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Let us write

\mathcal{F}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}Z_{k}\tau^{k}\in\mathbb{T}(K)[[\tau]].

The coefficient of
1=\tau^{0}
is therefore
Z_{0}
.
We also set

\mathcal{G}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\zeta_{A}\left(q-1,\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1}\right)\tau^{k}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]].

We recall that by (
3.5
),

\mathcal{G}=\exp_{A}\in K_{\infty}[[\tau]].

Hence Proposition
3.5
implies
the identity in
\mathbb{T}(K)[[\tau]]
:

\mathcal{F}-\tau\mathcal{F}=Z_{0}\exp_{A}.

This allows to write

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\tau^{i}(Z_{0})\tau^{i}\exp_{A},

a series expansion which is convergent for the
\tau
-adic topology.
Explicitly, equating the coefficients of
\tau^{k}
, we find

(3.9)

Z_{k}=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\epsilon_{k-i}^{q^{i}}\tau^{i}(Z_{0})\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{q^{k}-q^{i}},\quad k\geq 0.

Applying
\tau^{n}
we get

\tau^{n}(Z_{k})=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\epsilon_{k-i}^{q^{i+n}}\tau^{i+n}(Z_{0})\widetilde{\pi}_{\phi}^{(q^{k}-q^{i})q^{n}}.

Evaluating at
\Xi
the above identity of entire functions and using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) together with the identities

\tau^{i}(Z_{0})_{\Xi}=\zeta_{A}(q^{i}-1),\quad i\geq 0,

we obtain

\zeta_{A}\left(q^{n}-1,(q-1)q^{n},\ldots,(q-1)q^{k-1+n}\right)=\\
=(-1)^{k}\lambda_{n+k}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{n+i}\epsilon_{k-i}^{q^{n+i}}\right)\zeta_{A}(q^{k+n}-1),

and (
3.8
) holds.
This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Applying
\tau^{n}

A particular case of this formula, when

, appears in
[

, §7.2.1]
.

.

Applying
\tau^{n}

4.
Applications

In this section, we consider Drinfeld modules on elliptic curves and ramifying hyperelliptic curves as studied in
[
21
,
26
]
. We then derive a simple formula for the ratio
\alpha_{n,1}
. As a consequence, we prove a conjecture of Lara Rodríguez and Thakur (see Theorem
4.2
).

4.1.
Elliptic curves: the genus 1 case

We work with Drinfeld modules on elliptic curves (i.e.,
g=1
and
d_{\infty}=1
) which were studied in detail in
[
26
]
. We let
E
be the elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
given by

E:\eta^{2}+a_{1}\theta\eta+a_{3}\eta=\theta^{3}+a_{2}\theta^{2}+a_{4}\theta+a_{6},\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

We denote by
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta,\eta]
the ring of functions on
E
and
K
its fraction field. We recall the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\chi:A\to A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
given by
a\mapsto a\otimes 1
. We put
y=\chi(\eta)
and
t=\chi(\theta)
.

The shtuka function attached to the standard sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one is given by

f=\frac{\nu}{\delta}=\frac{y-\eta-m(t-\theta)}{t-\alpha}

where
m=\frac{\eta-\beta^{q}}{\theta-\alpha^{q}}
and
V=(\alpha,\beta)\in E(H)
is the Drinfeld divisor. Recall that for all
i\geq 0
,

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{1}{ff^{(1)}\dots f^{(i-1)}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(i)}},\quad\lambda_{i}=\frac{\delta^{(i+1)}}{\delta^{(1)}f^{(1)}\dots f^{(i)}}\Big|_{\Xi}

The first equality is proved by Thakur
[
35
, Proposition 0.3.6]
as mentioned before. The second equality is shown by Anderson
[
35
, Proposition 0.3.8]
, Green and Papanikolas
[
26
, Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 3.5]
.

By (
3.11
),

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,1}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\lambda_{n}\epsilon_{1}^{q^{n}}}{\lambda_{n+1}}+1\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\delta^{(n+1)}f^{(n+1)}}{\delta^{(n+2)}}\Big|_{\Xi}\frac{1}{(f\big|_{\Xi^{(1)}})^{q^{n}}}+1\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\nu^{(n+1)}}{\delta^{(n+2)}}\Big|_{\Xi}\frac{1}{(f\big|_{\Xi^{(1)}})^{q^{n}}}+1\right).

4.2.
Ramifying hyperelliptic curves

In
[
21
]
, the first author extended the previous results to the case of ramifying hyperelliptic curves. More precisely, letting
g\geq 1
be an integer, we consider a ramifying hyperelliptic curve
X
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is given by

X:\eta^{2}+F_{2}(\theta)\eta=F_{1}(\theta)

with
F_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
,
F_{1}
monic of degree
2g+1
and
F_{2}
of degree at most
g
. Thus the genus of
X
equals
g
. We denote by
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta,\eta]
the ring of functions on
X
and
K
its fraction field. We recall the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\chi:A\to A\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
given by
a\mapsto a\otimes 1
. We put
y=\chi(\eta)
and
t=\chi(\theta)
.

By
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
, the shtuka function attached to the standard sign-normalized Drinfeld module of rank one can be expressed as

f=\frac{\nu}{\delta}

where
\nu=y-\eta-Q
and
\delta,Q\in H[t]
of degree
g
in
t
. Further, we have
\widetilde{\sgn}(\nu)=\widetilde{\sgn}(\delta)=1
. Then by
[
35
, Proposition 0.3.6]
and
[

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{1}{ff^{(1)}\dots f^{(i-1)}}\Big|_{\Xi^{(i)}},\quad\lambda_{i}=\frac{\delta^{(i+1)}}{\delta^{(1)}f^{(1)}\dots f^{(i)}}\Big|_{\Xi}

Thus we obtain a similar expression for

:

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,1}=-\left(\frac{\nu^{(n+1)}}{\delta^{(n+2)}}\Big|_{\Xi}\frac{1}{(f\big|_{\Xi^{(1)}})^{q^{n}}}+1\right).

4.3.
Class number one base rings

Following Thakur
[

]
(see also
[

]
), another important class of base rings consists of class number one rings, i.e., we require that

is a principal ideal domain. There are exactly four of them listed as Examples (i)-(iv) in

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

They considered the following ratios (in

]
, these ratios are denoted by

and conjectured that they are in

in the above four cases. In each of the four cases, we will set

and

and

\begin{split}\alpha_{n,1}&=\frac{1}{X^{24}+\theta X^{16}+(\theta+1)X^{8}+\theta^{2}+\theta}\bigg(X^{22}+(1+\theta)\\
&\phantom{==}\cdot(X^{20}+X^{18}+X^{16})+(\theta^{2}+\theta+1)(X^{12}+X^{10})+X^{9}+\theta X^{8}\\
&\phantom{==}+X^{5}+(Y+\eta)(X^{4}+X^{2})+\theta^{2}+\theta\bigg).\end{split}

Theorem 4.2
.

Conjecture
4.1
holds.

Proof.

In the case of elliptic curve and ramifying hyperelliptic curves, we have proved an expression in terms of the shtuka function
f
for the standard sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld module
\phi

\alpha_{n,1}=-\left(\left.\frac{\nu^{(n+1)}}{\delta^{(n+2)}}\right|_{\Xi}\frac{1}{(f|_{\Xi^{(1)}})^{q^{n}}}+1\right).

Here
\nu,\delta
are respectively the numerator and denominator of the shtuka function
f
, as given in §

and §
4.2
. Upon evaluation and reusing the notations from Conjecture

, we have

(i)

f=\frac{y-\eta-\theta(t-\theta)}{t-\theta-1},\qquad f|_{\Xi^{(1)}}=1,

(4.5)

\begin{split}\alpha_{n,1}&=\frac{\eta+\eta^{2^{n+1}}+\theta^{2^{n+1}+1}+\theta+1}{\theta^{2^{n+2}}+\theta+1}\\
&=\frac{Y^{2}+\eta+\theta X^{2}+\theta+1}{X^{4}+\theta+1}.\end{split}

(ii)

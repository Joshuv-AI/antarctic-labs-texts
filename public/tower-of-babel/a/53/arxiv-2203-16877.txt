Asymptotic behavior of the Dirichlet energy on Poisson point clouds Thanks: Preprint SISSA 06/2022/MATE
Andrea Braides, Marco Caroccia

Abstract
. We prove that quadratic pair interactions for functions defined on planar Poisson clouds and taking into account pairs of sites of distance up to a certain (large-enough) threshold can be almost surely approximated by the multiple of the Dirichlet energy by a deterministic constant. This is achieved by scaling the Poisson cloud and the corresponding energies and computing a compact discrete-to-continuum limit. In order to avoid the effect of exceptional regions of the Poisson cloud, with an accumulation of sites or with ‘disconnected’ sites, a suitable ‘coarse-grained’ notion of convergence of functions defined on scaled Poisson clouds must be given.



1.
Introduction

The object of this paper is an analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of quadratic energies on Poisson random sets. Loosely speaking such sets are characterized by the property that the number of their points contained in a given set has a Poisson probability distribution, and that the random variables related to disjoint sets are independent. Even more loosely, in average the number of points contained in a set is proportional to the Lebesgue measure of the set. We denote by
\eta
such a random set.

In order to define some almost-sure properties of
\eta
we use a discrete-to-continuum approach that has been fruitfully used to derive continuum theories from microscopic interactions (see
[
2
]
). A simple interpretation of this method is as a finite-difference approximation. If
\eta
is a deterministic periodic locally finite discrete set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we can consider real-valued functions
u:\eta\to\mathbb{R}
and quadratic interaction potentials between points on
\eta
. The corresponding Dirichlet-type energy is

(1.1)

\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}(u(x)-u(y))^{2},

where
\langle x,y\rangle
indicates summation over nearest-neighbouring pairs
(x,y)
in
\eta
. We can then introduce a small parameter
\varepsilon
and scale both the environment and the energies accordingly; namely, considering
u:\varepsilon\,\eta\to\mathbb{R}
and

(1.2)

\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}\varepsilon^{d-2}(u(x)-u(y))^{2},

now summing over nearest-neighbouring pairs
(x,y)
in
\varepsilon\,\eta
. By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain a limit continuum energy, of the form

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\bf A}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,{\rm d}x,

where the matrix
\bf A
carries information about the microstructure of the original set
\eta
. Note that in order to perform this passage to the limit we have to embed our energies in a common environment identifying functions on
\eta
with suitable interpolations. The limit is meant in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence, which implies that minimum problems for the limiting energies are approximations of the discrete ones, and can also be performed ‘locally’, by considering interactions only for
x\in\varepsilon\eta\cap U
for a fixed open set
U
.

In order to define analogs of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for the realization of a Poisson cloud
\eta
we face a choice regarding what to consider as ‘interacting sites’, whether nearest neighbours in the sense of Voronoi cells or points ‘close’ in the sense of the ambient space. For the random set
\eta
these two choices are not equivalent since nearest-neighbouring points on
\eta
may be indeed arbitrarily distant in the ambient space, and conversely a very small distance between points of
\eta
does not ensure that they are nearest neighbours in
\eta
. We choose the second option, which also seems closer to applications; namely, we introduce an
interaction radius

\lambda>0
and consider the energies

(1.4)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in Q\cap\varepsilon\eta,\\
|x-y|<\varepsilon\lambda\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{d-2}(u(x)-u(y))^{2},

defined for
u:\varepsilon\,\eta\cap Q\to\mathbb{R}
, where
Q
is the unit coordinate cube centered in
0
(for ease of notation we treat only this case, which anyhow, up to scaling and localization, implies the result for any bounded Lipschitz open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). Note that, if a
\Gamma
-limit of such energies does exist then, thanks to the invariance properties by rotations of
\eta
, it must be
a multiple of the Dirichlet integral (i.e.,
\bf A
in (
1.3
) is equal to a multiple of the identity matrix), which we expect to be almost surely deterministic.

Figure 1.1.
A representation of a regular grid in
Q
with two ‘paths’ highlighted.

The main issue in proving the convergence of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
consists in providing a suitable notion of convergence for discrete functions
u_{\varepsilon}
to a continuum parameter
u
, for which a compactness theorem can be proved under an assumption of boundedness of the energies. While for periodic
\eta
we can use piecewise-constant interpolations on Voronoi cells (or, equivalently, piecewise-affine interpolations on the related Delaunay triangulation), for a Poisson point process we cannot control the behaviour of such interpolation due to the presence of arbitrarily large and arbitrarily small Voronoi cells. Nevertheless, we can prove that the union of ‘regular’ Voronoi cells with suitably controlled dimensions form an infinite connected set in which we find ‘paths’ of cells such that also cells at distance
\lambda
are regular. This is done by exploiting a Bernoulli site-percolation argument. In the planar case
d=2
the complement of this set of Voronoi is composed of isolated sets with controlled dimensions, so that the ambient space can be thought of as a “perforated domain”, in which we do not have a control of the discrete functions only in isolated ‘holes’ of controlled size. This allows to define a suitable convergence by choosing a subset
G_{\varepsilon}
of
\varepsilon\eta
composed of paths mentioned above whose union
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(G_{\varepsilon})
has the geometry of a square grid (see Fig.
1.1
)
We thus use these grids to define a suitable convergence notion: given a sequence of function
u_{\varepsilon}:\varepsilon\,\eta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we say that
u
is the
L^{2}
-limit of
u_{\varepsilon}
if

(1.5)

\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(G_{\varepsilon})}|\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}-u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\rightarrow 0;

namely, if the
L^{2}
-distance between the piecewise-constant extensions
\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}
of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
u
, restricted to the Voronoi cells of the grid
G_{\varepsilon}
vanishes as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Regular grids allow also to give a meaningful notion of boundary-value problems; in particular we can consider affine boundary condition as in the cell problems

(1.6)

\mathrm{m}(\xi;TQ):=\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in\eta\cap(TQ)}\sum_{y\in\eta\cap B_{\lambda}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\ \left|\begin{array}[]{c}v:\eta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
\ v(x)=\xi\cdot x\ \ \text{\rm for all $x\in\eta$ such that}\\
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial(TQ))\leq 2\lambda\end{array}\right.\right\}.

Using subadditive ergodic theorems we then can prove that, if
\xi\neq 0
, the constant
\Xi
given by

\Xi:=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;TQ)}{T^{2}|\xi|^{2}}

exists and is deterministic. Moreover, by the invariance properties of
\eta
it does not depend on
\xi
. This allows to state and prove the main result of the paper, which is the almost sure
\Gamma
-convergence in the planar case
d=2
of functionals (
1.4
) to

(1.7)

\mathcal{F}(u)=\Xi\int_{Q}|\nabla u|^{2}{\rm d}x

with respect to the convergence in (
1.5
). Note that more in general we may consider energies of the form

(1.8)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{a}(u)=\sum_{x,y\in Q\cap\varepsilon\eta}\varepsilon^{d-2}a\Bigl({x-y\over\varepsilon}\Bigr)(u(x)-u(y))^{2},

with
a
positive and with compact support, recovering the case in (
1.4
) as a special case when
a(\xi)
is the characteristic function of the ball centered in
0
and radius
\lambda
. If
a
is radially symmetric the same limit result holds with obvious modifications in the statements.

The convergence theorem can be compared with various results in the literature. Our result is inspired by the recent paper
[
7
]
, where perimeter energies on Poisson random sets are considered. In that context a simpler compactness result can be obtained with respect to the convergence is measure of sets, by using a covering lemma that ensures that the energy cannot concentrate on non-regular Voronoi cells. In the present context, this would correspond to an extension theorem for Sobolev functions from regular sets, which seems hard to obtain due to the random geometry of clusters of non-regular Voronoi cells. Furthermore, we can compare our approach to that in
[
4
,
3
]
, where a notion of stochastic lattice
\eta
is given for which energies of the form (
1.8
) can be considered. Differently from Poisson random sets, stochastic lattices are more regular, in that all Voronoi cells have controlled dimension and hence are regular in the terminology above, a condition that seems a considerable restriction in terms of applications.
The regularity of the lattice implies that functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{a}
are coercive with respect to the
L^{2}
convergence of piecewise-constant interpolations on Voronoi cells. Conversely, in general the limits of functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{a}
, which exist under ergodicity and stationarity assumptions, are not isotropic even if
a
is radially symmetric, except for specially constructed examples
[
16
]
. More general random distributions of sites have been considered within problems in Machine Learning by D. Slepcev
et al.

[
9
,
12
,
18
]
(see also the references therein). In their approach the convergence is given in terms of suitable interpolations of discrete functions using Optimal-Transport techniques. The presence of non-regular Voronoi cells is mitigated by considering kernels
a^{\varepsilon}
with increasing support as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which also allow to obtain isotropy in the limit (see also
[
8
]
for variational limits using a coarse-graining approach). Energies (
1.8
) have a continuum approximation in terms of a convolution double integral, for which random homogenization has been considered in
[
6
]
(see also
[
1
]
). We note that the existence of regular paths can be proved in any dimension
d\geq 2
, but if
d>2
the geometry of regular grids can be thought as a set of “fibers” rather than a perforated domain. We believe that the same asymptotic result holds but with an even more complex argument. Finally, we mention, following a remark by D. Slepcev in a private communication, that our results may also have practical implications for the study of the graph Laplacian. Namely, one can show that if one drops all of the eigenmodes of the graph Laplacian on a low degree random geometric graph where the eigenvector has large
L^{\infty}
norm (in other words concentrates at few nodes) one still recovers the continuum spectrum.

We briefly outline the plan of the paper. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary definitions and notation for Poisson point clouds. This allows to give a definition of Dirichlet energy on a Poisson cloud
\eta
and to prove some asymptotic properties as the Poisson set is scaled by a small parameter
\eta
and correspondingly the energies
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
as in (
1.4
). We introduce the notation for Voronoi cells and define grids of paths of regular cells
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon,t}
depending on an additional parameter
t>0
. This allows to define the convergence of piecewise-constant functions
u_{\varepsilon}
on Voronoi cells to a continuum function
u
as the successive convergence of averages of
u_{\varepsilon}
computed on
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon,t}
at the “mesoscopic scale”
t
to piecewise-constant functions
u^{t}
defined on a square grid and then of such
u^{t}
to a limit
u
as
t\to+\infty
(Definition
2.7
). This is proved to be equivalent to the
L^{2}
convergence on grids as in (
1.5
), and actually independent of the choice of the family of grids. In Section
3
we state the main results of the paper. Theorem
3.1
states the pre-compactness the sense of the convergence above of sequences with equi-bounded Dirichlet energy; Theorem
3.3
is an almost-sure homogenization result characterizing the
\Gamma
-limit of Dirichlet energies as a deterministic quantity as in (
1.7
). Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the Compactness Theorem, based on the geometric properties of the grids that allow the use of Poincaré inequalities. In Section
5
we prove the Homogenization Theorem. The lower bound is obtained by using the Fonseca and Müller blow-up method, which is possible thanks to the use of cut-off functions that are locally constant close to non-regular Voronoi cells. The construction of recovery sequences is also made possible thanks to these ‘regular’ cut-off functions. Finally Section
6
(the Appendix) contains the proof of the existence of regular grids.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

In this section we introduce the main ingredients required to perform our analysis. For the sake of simplicity, and since our analysis will take place in this context, we always consider the ambient space dimension to be two-dimensional, even though some of the definitions and results can be extended to general space dimension.

2.1.
General notation

We let
Q:=\left(-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\right)^{2}
denote the unit coordinate square centered in
0
. We will also write
Q_{r}=rQ
,
Q_{r}(x)=x+rQ
. The same notation applies to
B_{r}(x)
, being
B
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For a Radon measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the space
L^{2}(A;\nu)
is defined as the space of all measurable functions
u:\mathrm{spt}\,(\nu)\cap A\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{A}|u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)<+\infty.

When
\nu=\mathcal{L}^{2}
, the Lebesgue measure, we simply write
L^{2}(A)
. We denote by
\mathrm{Bor}(A)
the collection of all Borel subsets of
A
. The notation
|E|
stands for
the Lebesgue measure of
E
. For a set
A
and for
t>0
we define

(A)_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ \mathrm{dist}(x,A)\leq t\}.

If no confusion arises, the notation
\{x_{i}\in X_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is used for a family
x_{i}
indexed by
I
, such that
x_{i}\in X_{i}

2.2.
Poisson point clouds

Some basic properties of the stationary stochastic point process called
Poisson point cloud
are here recalled. A complete treatment of this subject can be found in
[
10
,
17
]
. In order to formally introduce this notion, we consider the family
\mathbf{N}_{s}
of
simple measures
;
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{s}

\displaystyle:=\biggl\{\left.\sum_{i\in I}\delta_{x_{i}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \right|\ \{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ x_{i}\neq x_{j}\ \text{for all $i,j\in I$, and $I$ a subset of $\mathbb{N}$}\biggr\}

Here and in the sequel,
\delta_{x}
is the
Dirac delta
at
x
. For any Borel set
E\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define the subset
\mathbf{A}_{E,k}:=\{\mu\in\mathbf{N}_{s}\ |\ \mu(E)=k\}
of
\mathbf{N}_{s}
and consider the
\sigma
-Algebra
\mathcal{N}
generated by

\{\mathbf{A}_{E,k}\ |\ E\in\mathrm{Bor}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with intensity
\gamma
is a random element
\eta
on
(\mathbf{N}_{s},\mathcal{N})
; that is, a map from a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
onto
(\mathbf{N}_{s},\mathcal{N})
, such that

1)

\mathbb{P}(\eta\in\mathbf{A}_{E,k})=\displaystyle\frac{(\gamma|E|)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\gamma|E|}
;

2)

denoted by
\eta(B):(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
the random variable induced by
\eta
when fixing
B
, (namely
\eta(B):=\eta(\omega)(B)
for
\omega\in\Omega
) then, for any
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
pairwise disjoint Borel sets we have that
\eta(B_{1}),\ldots,\eta(B_{m})
are independent.

For any Poisson point process
\eta
we can observe that its probability distribution
\mathbb{P}_{\eta}
on
(\mathbf{N}_{s},\mathcal{N})
satisfies

\mathbb{P}(\eta(A)=k):=\mathbb{P}_{\eta}(\mathbf{A}_{E,k})=\mathbb{P}(\eta\in\mathbf{A}_{E,k}):=\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\in\Omega\ |\ \eta(\omega)\in\mathbf{A}_{E,k}\})=\frac{(\gamma|E|)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\gamma|E|}.

We will often make use of the notation
x\in\eta
, or
x\in\eta(\omega)
by meaning that
x\in\mathrm{spt}\,(\eta(\omega))
for some realization
\omega\in\Omega
. Accordingly,
x\in\varepsilon\,\eta(\omega)
will stand for
x\in\varepsilon\,\mathrm{spt}\,(\eta(\omega))
.

Definition
2.1
implies (see
[
15
, Proposition 8.3]
) in particular that the Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with intensity
\gamma
is
stationary
: if we define
\tau_{x}\eta(A):=\eta(A+x)
, then
\tau_{x}\eta
is equal in distribution to
\eta
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This implies in particular that
\mathbb{P}(\eta(\mathbb{R}^{2})<+\infty)=0
(see
[
15
, Proposition 8.4]
). In the sequel, whenever we speak of a Poisson point process we always mean
a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with intensity
\gamma
.

2.3.
Dirichlet energy on point clouds

Let
\eta
be a Poisson point process. Without loss of generality, we fix the intensity to be
\gamma=1
and we carry out our analysis on the unit square
Q
. This is not a restriction since we may localize our energies on regular subsets of
Q
where the analysis applies unchanged, while we can deal with arbitrary bounded regular open sets by rescaling them to subsets of
Q
.

Let
\lambda>0
be a fixed parameter (the
interaction radius
) and for
u\in L^{2}(Q;\eta_{\varepsilon})
, for a subset
A\subset Q
define

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;A):=\sum_{x\in\eta_{\varepsilon}\cap A}\sum_{y\in\eta_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2},

where we have set

\eta_{\varepsilon}(A):=\eta(\varepsilon^{-1}A),\ \ \ \mathrm{spt}\,(\eta_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon\,\mathrm{spt}\,(\eta).

Remark 2.2
.

The definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
takes into account the values of
u
in a
\lambda\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
A
. As a consequence, the energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is subadditive on essentially disjoint sets, namely

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;A\cup B)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;A)+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;B)\ \ \ \text{for all $A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}$, $|A\cap B|=0$}.

Indeed, the energy of a function
u
on some open set
A
, takes into account also the contribution of all those points

\mathcal{B}A:=\{y\in(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus A)\cap\eta_{\varepsilon}\ |\ \text{there exists $x\in A\cap\eta_{\varepsilon}$ such that $|x-y|\leq\lambda\varepsilon$}\}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is not local, since we may not have
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;A)=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v;A)
if
u=v
on
A
due to
the interaction around boundary points. However, if
u=v
on
A
we can infer that

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;A\setminus(\partial A)_{\lambda\varepsilon})=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v;A\setminus(\partial A)_{\lambda\varepsilon}).

We now give some estimates on the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}
by means of a kind of (spatial) Mean Ergodic Theorem. We show that, almost surely, the average number of points in some open set
A
, lying at a distance less then
\lambda\varepsilon
can be bounded from above by the Lebesgue measure of
A
(the proof of Proposition
2.3
follow the lines of the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 8.14]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists a constant
C
, depending on
\lambda
only, such that the following property holds almost surely:

(2.1)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{x\in\eta_{\varepsilon}\cap A}\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}(B_{\lambda\varepsilon}(x))\leq C|A|\

for any
A\subseteq Q
with Lipschitz boundary.

Proof.

We consider a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
and

(2.2)

\frac{1}{C}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{n+1}}\leq C,

where
C>1
is a fixed universal constant. For an open set
A
we define the following objects

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(A):=\{i\in\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ |\ Q_{\lambda}(i)\cap A\neq\emptyset\},\quad\mathcal{Q}(A):=\{Q_{\lambda}(i)\ |\ i\in\mathcal{I}(A)\},\quad N(A):=\#(\mathcal{I}(A)),

and consider, for any
i\in\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the random variable
X_{i}:=\eta(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}
. Note that

\mathbb{E}(X_{i})=e^{-\lambda^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}k^{2}\frac{(\lambda)^{2k}}{k!}=\tau_{\lambda}<+\infty.

Let
R:=(0,a)\times(0,b)
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, we can relabel each square
Q_{\lambda}(i)\in\mathcal{Q}(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)
in such a way that

\frac{1}{N(\varepsilon_{n}^{1-}R)}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n}^{1-}R)}\eta(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}=\frac{1}{N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\sum_{k=1}^{N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}X_{i_{k}}.

If we now invoke the law of large numbers (see for instance
[
15
, Theorem B.11]
), we have that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{1}{N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\sum_{i\in I(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\eta(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}=\tau_{\lambda}=\mathbb{E}(X_{1})

almost-surely. Since
\varepsilon_{n}^{2}N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)\rightarrow\frac{|R|}{\lambda^{2}}

and

\eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}(Q_{\lambda\varepsilon_{n}}(\varepsilon_{n}i))=\eta(Q_{\lambda}(i))
,
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\ \eta_{\varepsilon_{n}}(Q_{\lambda\varepsilon_{n}}(\varepsilon_{n}i))^{2}=\frac{(\varepsilon_{n}^{2}N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R))}{N(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n}^{-1}R)}\eta(Q_{\lambda}(i))\rightarrow\frac{|R|}{\lambda^{2}}\tau_{\lambda}

for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
. Let now

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}(R)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon^{-1}R)}\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{\varepsilon}(Q_{\lambda\varepsilon}(\varepsilon i))^{2},\ \ \ \mu_{n}(R):=\mu_{\varepsilon_{n}}(R),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{R=[0,p]\times[0,q]\ |\ p,q\in\mathbb{Q},\ p,q,\leq 2\}

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega\ \Bigl|\ \mu_{n}(R)\rightarrow\frac{|R|}{\lambda^{2}}\tau_{\lambda}\ \text{for all $R\in\mathcal{R}_{0}$}\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is a countable family of rectangles we have that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})=1
. Let now
R:=[p,p^{\prime}]\times[q,q^{\prime}]\subset Q
with

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle:=[0,p^{\prime}]\times[0,q],\qquad R_{2}:=[0,p]\times[0,q^{\prime}]

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle:=[0,p]\times[0,q],\qquad\ R_{4}:=[0,p^{\prime}]\times[0,q^{\prime}].

so that
R=R_{4}\setminus(R_{1}\cup R_{2}),\ |R|=|R_{4}|-|R_{1}|-|R_{2}|+|R_{3}|
.
Moreover

\mu_{n}(R):=\mu_{n}(R_{4})-\mu_{n}(R_{1})-\mu_{n}(R_{2})+\mu_{n}(R_{3})+s_{n},

with

Since
s_{n}\rightarrow 0

\mu_{n}(R)\rightarrow\frac{|R|}{\lambda^{2}}\tau_{\lambda}
. In particular, having defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\{[p,p^{\prime}]\times[q,q^{\prime}]\subset Q\ |\ p,p^{\prime},q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}\},

\mu_{n}(R)\rightarrow\frac{|R|}{\lambda^{2}}\tau_{\lambda}\ \ \text{for all $\omega\in\Omega_{0}$ and $R\in\mathcal{R}$}

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}
, let
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
be a realization and let
\{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(\omega)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence along which

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon^{-1}R)}\varepsilon^{2}\eta(\omega)(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{-1}R)}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}\eta(\omega)(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}.

Consider
\varepsilon_{n_{k}}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{n_{k}+1}
. By (

\tilde{R}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
with

(for a universal constant

) and such that

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{-1}R\subset\varepsilon_{n_{k}}^{-1}\tilde{R}\ \ \ \text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$}
.
Note that
\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{-1}R)\subset\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n_{k}}^{-1}\tilde{R})
and thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{-1}R)}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}^{2}\eta(\omega)(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n_{k}}^{-1}\tilde{R})}\varepsilon_{n_{k}+1}^{2}\eta(\omega)(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}\leq C\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\varepsilon_{n_{k}}^{-1}\tilde{R})}\varepsilon_{n_{k}}^{2}\eta(\omega)(Q_{\lambda}(i))^{2}.

By taking the limit and exploiting that
\tilde{R}\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
,

We have now introduced all the basic notation and we are thus ready to state our main results, which regard the asymptotic behavior of

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon,t}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{h}^{i}_{m}\cap(Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon,t}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{v}^{j}_{m}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)+\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon,t}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{v}^{j+1}_{m}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t})+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

In particular there are three paths
\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{i}\in\mathrm{Hor}(i;G_{\varepsilon,t})
,
\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{i}\in\mathrm{Ver}(j;G_{\varepsilon,t}),\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{j+1}\in\mathrm{Ver}(j+1;G_{\varepsilon,t})

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{i}\cap(Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon,t}}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{j}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)+\sum_{x\in\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{j+1}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon,t}}[\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t})+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j+1}^{t})]

Note that, by the properties of the grid we have

\frac{1}{M_{\varepsilon,t}}\leq\Upsilon\frac{\varepsilon}{t}
.
Therefore, we conclude.
∎

We now provide a lemma that allows to estimate the difference of
T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})
, defined as in (

), between neighboring squares of the squares partition
\mathcal{Q}_{t}
. Once again, we limit ourselves to prove the statement for horizontal neighboring squares since the vertical case follows in the same way up to changing the notation accordingly.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and

G_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon;\eta_{\varepsilon})
any

\Bigl|(u)^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}-(u)^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j+1}\Bigr|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

Proof.

Let
\bar{\mathbf{h}}^{i},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{j},\bar{\mathbf{v}}^{j+1}
be the paths given by Lemma
4.1
. Let
\mathbf{p}:=\mathbf{p}_{i,j}^{h}
and let

\displaystyle(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}:=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t})}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}u(x),\qquad(u)_{i,j+1}^{\mathbf{p}}:=\frac{1}{\eta_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t})}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}}u(x).

We will make use of the above quantities (namely the average of the function
u
on the skeleton
\mathbf{p}
) to estimate

.

For any
x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}

\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)
which links

to all points

. The construction will be such that we may control the number of times a path path passes through a point
y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
. We say that

is a

if it belongs to some horizontal path
\mathbf{h}\in\mathrm{Hor}(i;G_{\varepsilon,t})
and does not belongs to any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathrm{Ver}(j;G_{\varepsilon,t})

is horizontal, say it belongs to
\mathbf{h}^{i}_{m}
the
m

\mathbf{p}
until we meet
\mathbf{h}^{i}_{m}
and then following
\mathbf{h}^{i}_{m}

is vertical and belongs to
\mathbf{v}^{j}_{l}
we start from
x

\mathbf{p}
until we meet
\mathbf{v}^{j}_{l}
and then follow
\mathbf{v}^{j}_{l}
to reach
y

\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)
, have the following property, which is crucial in what follows. For any
x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
, each point

\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon}

We now divide the rest of the proof in two steps.

Step one:

Comparison between
(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}
and
(u)_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}

2.5

\displaystyle\left|(u)_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\ell(\mathbf{t})\sum_{y\in\mathbf{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)

By invoking property (d) we can further deduce

\displaystyle\left|(u)_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\mathbbmss{1}_{\mathbf{t}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in(G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t})\setminus\mathbf{p}}D_{\varepsilon}u_{n}(y)\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\mathbbmss{1}_{\mathbf{t}}(y)

\displaystyle+\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}}D_{n}u_{n}(y)\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\mathbbmss{1}_{\mathbf{t}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct^{2}}{(\varepsilon)^{2}N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in(G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t})\setminus\mathbf{p}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)+\frac{Ct^{3}}{(\varepsilon)^{3}N_{j}N_{j}^{\prime}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}N_{j}^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t})+\frac{Ct^{3}}{\varepsilon^{3}N_{j}^{\prime}}\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}),

where the last inequality follows from the particular choice of
\mathbf{p}
given by Lemma
4.1
. Since the properties of the grid (property (d) of Theorem
2.6
) also imply that

N_{j}^{\prime}\geq\frac{Ct^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}},

we conclude that

(4.1)

\left|(u)_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

The same exact computation shows also that

(4.2)

\left|(u)_{i,j+1}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-(u)_{i,j+1}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

Step two:

Comparison between
(u)_{i,j+1}^{\mathbf{p}}
and
(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}
. For
x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
let
\mathcal{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}(x)
be the family of paths
\mathbf{t}
on
\mathbf{p}
which link each point of
\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}
to
x
. By considering one path for each
y\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}
we can build
\mathcal{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}(x)
in a way that each
\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{j+1}^{\prime}(x)
contains not more than
Ct/\varepsilon)
points. Moreover, each point
z\in\mathbf{p}
is contained in at most
Ct/\varepsilon
paths. With the notation introduced above, we then compute

\displaystyle\left|(u)_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}-(u)_{i,j+1}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{j}N_{j+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j+1}^{t}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N_{j}N_{j+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{j+1}(x)}\ell(\mathbf{t})\sum_{y\in\mathbf{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct}{\varepsilon N_{j}N_{j+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}\cap(Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{j+1}(x)}\mathbbmss{1}_{\mathbf{t}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}N_{j}N_{j+1}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}\cap(Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\frac{\varepsilon}{t}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u;Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

Conclusion
. By means of Step one and Step two, in particular by collecting (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) and by means of a triangular inequality we conclude.
∎

4.2.
Properties of the convergence for sequences with equibounded energy

We now state and prove some useful properties of the convergence in Definition
2.7
. We start with the following lemma, which ensures that the limit of sequences with equibounded energy, when it exists, is unique and does not depend on the choice of the sequence of regular grids
\{G_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon,t>0}
when
\varepsilon,t\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\{u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(Q;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is a sequence of function satisfying (
3.1
) and
\{G_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon,t>0},\{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon^{\prime};\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon,t>0}
(with possibly
\Upsilon\neq\Upsilon^{\prime}
) are two sequences of regular grids such that

T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{t},\ \ \ T^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{u}^{t}

then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{Q}\left|u^{t}(x)-\bar{u}^{t}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Proof.

Fix
Q_{i,j}^{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t}
and let
\mathbf{p}
,
\bar{\mathbf{p}}
be the union of paths given by Lemma
4.1
relative to
Q_{i,j}^{t}
(and one of its neighbors, say
Q_{i,j+1}^{t}
without loss of generality) and to to the grid
G_{\varepsilon,t},\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}
respectively. Now by construction we have that
\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t},\bar{\mathbf{p}}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
share at least two points. In particular, for any
x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
we can still build a family
\mathcal{P}(x)
of paths that link each
y\in\bar{\mathbf{p}}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
to
x
containing only points in
\mathbf{p}\cup\bar{\mathbf{p}}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
. We can also ensure that each
\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}(x)
contains not more than
Ct/\varepsilon
points and that any point in
z\in\mathbf{p}\cup\bar{\mathbf{p}}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
is contained in at most
Ct/\varepsilon
paths in
\mathcal{P}(x)
. With the notation introduced in the proof of Lemma
4.2
we now compute

\displaystyle\left|(u_{\varepsilon})_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}-(u_{\varepsilon})_{i,j}^{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N_{j}\bar{N}_{j}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{y\in\bar{\mathbf{p}}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N_{j}\bar{N}_{j}}\sum_{x\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}(x)}\ell(\mathbf{t})\sum_{y\in\mathbf{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}),

where we have used the properties of
\mathbf{p},\bar{\mathbf{p}}
and of
\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}(x)
. Now, by recalling that (
4.1
),(
4.2
) are in force respectively on
G_{\varepsilon,t},\mathbf{p}
and
\bar{G_{\varepsilon,t}},\bar{\mathbf{p}}
by means of the same arguments used to prove Step one of Lemma
4.2
, with a triangular inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|(u_{\varepsilon})^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}-(u_{\varepsilon})^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}).

If we sum up over all
i,j
and we observe that the energy of each square is counted at most a finite number of time (independent of
t,n
) we reach

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k_{t}}\left|(u_{\varepsilon})^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}-(u_{\varepsilon})^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{tk_{t}})\leq C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};A).

By means of this last relation we have

\int_{Q}\left|T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})(x)-T^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=t^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{k_{t}}\left|(u_{\varepsilon})^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}-(u_{\varepsilon})^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}_{i,j}\right|^{2}\leq Ct^{2}

In particular, if
T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{{}_{\varepsilon}}}{{\longrightarrow}}u^{t}
,
T^{\bar{G}_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})\stackrel{{{}_{\varepsilon}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{u}^{t}
then

\int_{Q}\left|u^{t}(x)-\bar{u}^{t}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Ct^{2}

and we conclude.
∎

Now we proceed to state and prove Proposition
4.4
and Proposition

, which will give us a useful characterization of the convergence in Definition
2.7
.
We will make use of the notion of piecewise-constant extension introduced in Section

.

Proposition 4.4

(
L^{2}
convergence on the grids)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(Q;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be a sequence satisfying (
3.1
) and assume that
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u
in the sense of Definition
2.7
. Then

(4.4)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(G_{\varepsilon,t})\cap Q}|\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0

for any sequence of regular grids
\{G_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon,t>0}
.

Proof.

By means of similar computations as the ones used in the proof of Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, we can infer also that for any
x\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}
(adopting the same notation)

\displaystyle\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-(u_{\varepsilon})_{i,j}^{\mathbf{p}}\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N_{j}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N_{j}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N_{j}}\sum_{y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(y)\sum_{\mathbf{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{i,j}(x)}\mathbbmss{1}_{\mathbf{t}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct}{N_{j}\varepsilon}\sum_{y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}\setminus\mathbf{p}}D_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(y)+\frac{Ct^{2}}{N_{j}\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{p}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}}D_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ct}{N_{j}\varepsilon}\sum_{y\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q_{i,j}^{t}\setminus\mathbf{p}}D_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(y)+\frac{Ct}{N_{j}\varepsilon}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})

\displaystyle C\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t})

for a constant independent of
t,\varepsilon
. In particular, by collecting (
4.5
) and (

\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-(u_{\varepsilon})_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}\right|^{2}\leq C\mathcal{F}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i,j}^{t}\cup Q_{i,j+1}^{t}),

which, summed up over
x\in G_{\varepsilon,t}
and

, and taking into accounting property (d) of Definition
2.5

(4.6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{x\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q}\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|^{2}\leq Ct^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};A).

In particular, if
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u
, and
\{u^{t}\in X_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\displaystyle t^{2}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{J}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-u_{J}^{t}|^{2}\stackrel{{{}_{\varepsilon}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{x\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u^{t}(x)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{x\in G_{\varepsilon,t}\cap Q}\left|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-T^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}(u_{\varepsilon})(x)\right|^{2}+Ct^{2}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}\left|(u_{\varepsilon})_{i,j}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-u_{J}^{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle Ct^{2}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};A)+Ct^{2}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}\left|(u_{\varepsilon})_{J}^{G_{\varepsilon,t}}-u_{J}^{t}\right|^{2}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}(G_{\varepsilon,t})\cap Q}|\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

and then also (

) holds for any sequence of regular grids
\{G_{\varepsilon,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(\Upsilon;\eta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon,t>0}
.
∎

Proposition 4.5

\frac{t}{2}\leq t_{n}c_{n}\leq 2t

for each horizontal rectangle accordingly. We repeat the same argument for vertical paths and we obtain a grid

. Thanks to (

\tilde{G}_{n,t}\in\mathcal{G}_{t}(2\Upsilon,\eta_{n})

\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J)):=\{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(Q_{t}(J)):\ Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\subset Q_{t}(J)\},\ \qquad r_{n}(J):=\#(\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))).

\displaystyle(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(G_{n}\cap Q_{t}(J^{\prime}))}\sum_{x\in G_{n}\cap Q_{t}(J^{\prime})}u_{n}(x),\quad(u_{n})_{J}^{\tilde{G}_{n,t}}=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J))}\sum_{x\in\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J)}u_{n}(x),

we have

\displaystyle(u_{n})_{J}^{\tilde{G}_{n,t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J))}\sum_{x\in\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J)}u_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J))}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\sum_{x\in\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}u_{n}(x)+R_{n}(J)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\frac{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime}))}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J))}(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}+R_{n}(J)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}+R_{n}(J),

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime}))}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J))}

\displaystyle R_{n}(J)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}(\tilde{G}_{n,t}\cap Q_{t}(J))}\sum_{J^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\sum_{x\in G_{n}\cap Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\cap Q_{t}(J)}u_{n}(x).

Observing that

\displaystyle(u)_{Q_{t}(J)}=\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\cap Q_{t}(J)}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}

\displaystyle\Bigl|T^{\tilde{G}_{n,t}}(u_{n})(x)-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}(u)_{Q_{t}(J)}\mathbbmss{1}_{Q_{t}(J)}(x)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}|(u_{n})_{J}^{\tilde{G}_{n,t}}-(u)_{Q_{t}(J)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ C\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}\Bigl|\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-(u)_{Q_{t}(J)}\Bigr|^{2}+\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}|R_{n}(J)|^{2}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}\Bigl|\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}\Bigr|^{2}+C\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}|R_{n}^{\prime}(J)|^{2},

where we have set

We now concentrate on proving separately the estimates required.

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\Bigl|\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}\Bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigl|\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})\Bigl[(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}\Bigr]\Bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\Bigl|\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})-\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\Bigr|^{2}|(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})^{2}\frac{r_{n}(J)}{t_{n}^{2}}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.11)

\displaystyle+\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\Bigl|\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})-\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\Bigr|^{2}|(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|^{2}

Observe that

r_{n}(J)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{t_{n}^{2}},\ \ \kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})\leq C\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}
,
and then

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})^{2}\frac{r_{n}(J)}{t_{n}^{2}}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{t^{2}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{t^{2}}\int_{Q_{t}(J)}|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}

In particular, (

\begin{split}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}t^{2}\Bigl|\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})_{J^{\prime}}^{G_{n}}-\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}(u)_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}\Bigr|^{2}\leq&C\int_{Q}|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&+\frac{Ct_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Finally, we estimate

\displaystyle t^{2}|R_{n}(J)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\Bigl(\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(\partial Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})(u_{n})^{G_{n}}_{J^{\prime}}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\#(\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J))}\kappa_{n}(J^{\prime})^{2}|(u_{n})^{G_{n}}_{J^{\prime}}|^{2}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ct_{n}}{t}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\cap Q_{t}(J)}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\#(\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))}{t^{2}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))}\Bigl(\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\cap Q_{t}(J)}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)^{2}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{t_{n}}{t}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J)))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})\cap Q_{t}(J)}u(y)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

By collecting (

), (
4.15
) and (
4.16

\displaystyle\int_{Q}

\displaystyle\Bigl|T^{\tilde{G}_{n,t}}(u_{n})(x)-\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}(u)_{Q_{t}(J)}\mathbbmss{1}_{Q_{t}(J)}(x)\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\int_{Q}|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{Ct_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{Ct_{n}}{t}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}_{t}(Q)}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{t_{n}}(\partial Q_{t}(J))}\int_{Q_{t_{n}}(J^{\prime})}(|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ C\int_{Q}|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{Ct_{n}^{2}}{t^{2}}\int_{Q}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{Ct_{n}}{t}\int_{Q}(|T^{G_{n}}(u_{n})(x)|^{2}+|u(x)|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, by taking the limit as

we obtain

Note that
u^{t}\rightarrow u
in
L^{2}(Q)
. In particular we exhibit a sequence of regular grids along which convergence in the sense of Definition
2.7
holds. Lemma
4.3
ensures then that it occurs on any sequence of regular grids, achieving thus the proof.
∎

Proof of the compactness theorem

We are now ready to prove Theorem

Let

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\alpha,\lambda

5.

In this section we prove Theorem

5.1.
The cell problem

We recall the notation for the boundary-value problem

\mathrm{m}(\xi;A):=\inf\biggl\{\sum_{x\in\eta\cap A}\sum_{y\in\eta\cap B_{\lambda}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\ \biggl|\begin{array}[]{c}v:\eta\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
\ v(x)=\xi\cdot x\ \ \text{for all $x\in\eta$ such that}\\
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial A)\leq 2\lambda\end{array}\biggr\}

The first thing we need is the following lemma on the asymptotic behavior of the “cell problem”
\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T})
when the boundary values are fixed on a cube
Q_{T}
and
T
diverges.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
it holds

\Xi|\xi|^{2}:=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T})}{T^{2}}

where
\Xi
is a constant independent of the realization.

Remark 5.2
.

In light of Lemma
5.1
, the constant
\Xi
can be identified as

\Xi:=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(e_{1};Q_{T})}{T^{2}}.

The proof of Lemma
5.1
comes as a consequence of Proposition
5.3
below.

We denote by
M(\vartheta)
a clockwise rotation of an angle
\vartheta
around the origin.

Proposition 5.3
.

For all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists almost surely the limit

f(\xi):=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T})}{T^{d}}

independent of the realization. Moreover, there exists a function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}g(R)=+\infty

and such that, for any sequence
y_{T}
with
|y_{T}|\leq Tg(y_{T})
and any rotation

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}((\xi;M(\vartheta)Q_{T}(y_{T}))}{T^{d}}=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T})}{T^{d}}=f(\xi).

Proof.

Note that

\mathrm{m}(\xi;A\cup B)\leq\mathrm{m}(\xi;A)+\mathrm{m}(\xi;B)

whenever
|A\cap B|=0
. In particular, by arguing exactly as in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 5.1]
, invoking the uniform version of the sub-additive ergodic theorem,
[
14
, Theorem 1]
, we can achieve the existence of
g
and

such that for any family of translations
\{y_{T}\}_{T\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
|y_{T}|\leq Tg(|y_{T}|)
it holds

f(\xi)=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;M(\vartheta)Q_{T}(y_{T}))}{T^{d}}.

as desired.
∎

We now focus on proving Lemma
5.1
. During the proof we find it convenient to explicit the dependence of

and

on

and

and

\mathrm{m}(\xi;A,\eta^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{F}_{1}(v;A,\eta^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime})\ \left|\begin{array}[]{c}v:\eta^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
\ v(x)=\xi\cdot x\ \ \text{for all $x\in\eta^{\prime}$ such that}\\
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial A)\leq 2\lambda^{\prime}\end{array}\right.\right\}.

We also refer, whenever needed, to

as the limit in

\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;A,\eta^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})}{T^{2}}
(which exists because the argument in Proposition

Proof of Lemma

.

We argue as follows. We prove the following two relations on

(5.1)

\displaystyle f(M(\vartheta)\xi)

\displaystyle=f(\xi)\ \ \ \text{for all $\vartheta\in[0,2\pi)$}

Equations (

We proceed then to the proof of (

Step one:

invariance by rotation
. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(v;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\eta\cap Q_{T}}\sum_{y\in\eta\cap B_{\lambda}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in M(\vartheta)\eta\cap(M(\vartheta)Q_{T})}\sum_{w\in M(\vartheta)\eta\cap B_{\lambda}(z)}|v(M(-\vartheta)z)-v(M(-\vartheta)w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}(v(M(-\vartheta)\cdot),M(\vartheta)Q_{T},M(\vartheta)\eta,\lambda).

If
v=\xi\cdot x
on
(\partial Q_{T})_{2\lambda}
, then
v(M(-\vartheta)x)=\xi\cdot(M(-\vartheta)x)
for
x\in(\partial(M(\vartheta)Q_{T}))_{2\lambda}
. In particular

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(M(-\vartheta)\xi;M(-\vartheta)Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)=\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T},M(\vartheta)\eta,\lambda)

By dividing by
T^{2}
and taking the limit, recalling that the limit exists (Proposition
5.3
) , we get

\displaystyle f_{M(\vartheta)\eta}(\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T},M(\vartheta)\eta,\lambda)}{T^{2}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(M(-\vartheta)\xi;M(-\vartheta)Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)}{T^{2}}=f_{\eta}(M(-\vartheta)\xi,\lambda).

Noting that

M(\vartheta)\eta=\eta
in distribution,
we conclude that

\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q,M(\vartheta)\eta,\lambda)=\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)
in distribution.
This equality in distribution implies that

f_{\eta}(\xi,\lambda)=f_{M(\vartheta)\eta}(\xi,\lambda)
in distribution.
Since
f_{\eta}
and
f_{M(\vartheta)\eta}
are independent of the realizations, as stated in Proposition
5.3
, we conclude that

(5.4)

f_{\eta}(\xi,\lambda)=f_{M(\vartheta)\eta}(\xi,\lambda).

Thus, by combining (
5.3
), (
5.4
) we get (
5.1
).

Step two:

positive homogeneity of degree two
. Fix
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(v;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\eta\cap Q_{T}}\sum_{y\in B_{\lambda}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\eta/r\cap Q_{T/r}}\sum_{w\in B_{\lambda/r}(z)}|v(rz)-v(rw)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\mathcal{F}_{1}(\nicefrac{{v(r\cdot)}}{{r}};Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r).

If
v(x)=\xi\cdot x
on
x\in(\partial Q_{T})_{2\lambda}
then
\frac{v(rx)}{r}=\xi\cdot x
on
x\in(\partial Q_{T/r})_{2\lambda/r}
. In particular, we also get that

\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)=r^{2}\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r)
.
By dividing by
T^{2}
and taking the limit, using the existence result in Proposition
5.3
, we get (setting
\tilde{T}=T/r
)

\displaystyle f_{\eta}(\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)}{T^{2}}=\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{r^{2}\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r)}{T^{2}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\tilde{T}\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{\tilde{T}},\eta/r,\lambda/r)}{\tilde{T}^{2}}=f_{\eta/r}(\xi,\lambda/r).

Analogously we have

\mathcal{F}_{1}(v;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)=r^{4}\mathcal{F}_{1}(\nicefrac{{v(r\cdot)}}{{r^{2}}};Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r).

Thus, if
v(x)=(r\xi\cdot x)
on
(\partial Q_{T})_{2\lambda}
then
\frac{v(rx)}{r^{2}}=\xi\cdot x
on
(\partial Q_{T/r})_{2\lambda/r}
. As a consequence we also have the equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(r\xi;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)=r^{4}\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r),

which, dividing by
T
and sending to
T\rightarrow+\infty
, still by Proposition
5.3
yields

\displaystyle f_{\eta}(r\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\rightarrow}\frac{\mathrm{m}(r\xi;Q_{T},\eta,\lambda)}{T^{2}}=r^{4}\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{T/r},\eta/r,\lambda/r)}{T^{2}}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\lim_{\tilde{T}=T/r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{m}(\xi;Q_{\tilde{T}},\eta/r,\lambda/r)}{\tilde{T}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle=r^{2}f_{\eta/r}(\xi,\lambda/r).

By combining (
5.5
),(
5.6
) we thus get
f_{\eta}(r\xi,\lambda)=r^{2}f_{\eta}(\xi,\lambda)
; that is, (
5.2
).

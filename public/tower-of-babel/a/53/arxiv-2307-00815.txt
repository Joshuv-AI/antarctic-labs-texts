Stability conditions on free abelian quotients
Hannah Dell

Abstract
. We study slope-stable vector bundles and Bridgeland stability conditions on varieties which are a quotient of a smooth projective variety by a finite abelian group G G acting freely. We show there is an analytic isomorphism between G G -invariant geometric stability conditions on the cover and geometric stability conditions on the quotient that are invariant under the residual action of the group of irreducible representations of G G , G ^ \widehat{G} . We apply our results to describe a connected component inside the stability manifolds of free abelian quotients when the cover has finite Albanese morphism. This applies to varieties with non-finite Albanese morphism which are free abelian quotients of varieties with finite Albanese morphism, such as Beauville-type and bielliptic surfaces. This gives a partial answer to a question raised by Lie Fu, Chunyi Li, and Xiaolei Zhao: If a variety X X has non-finite Albanese morphism, does there always exist a non-geometric stability condition on X X ? We also give counterexamples to a conjecture of Fu–Li–Zhao concerning the Le Potier function, which characterises Chern classes of slope-semistable sheaves. As a result of independent interest, we give a description of the set of geometric stability conditions on an arbitrary surface in terms of a refinement of the Le Potier function. This generalises a result of Fu–Li–Zhao from Picard rank one to arbitrary Picard rank.

1.
Introduction

Stability conditions on triangulated categories were introduced in
[
Bri07
]
by Bridgeland, who was motivated by the study of Dirichlet branes in string theory. In the same paper, Bridgeland showed that the space of stability conditions on a given triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
, has the structure of a complex manifold. When
X
is a smooth projective variety over
\mathbf{C}
, this leads to the fundamental question: How does the geometry of
X
relate to the geometry of
\mathrm{Stab}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Stab}(\mathrm{D^{b}}(X))
?

In this article, we investigate this question in the case of varieties that are free quotients by finite abelian groups, especially quotients of varieties with finite Albanese morphism such as bielliptic and Beauville-type surfaces.

One approach is via group actions on triangulated categories. We sharpen the correspondence between
G
-invariant stability conditions on
\mathcal{D}
and stability conditions on the
G
-equivariant category
\mathcal{D}_{G}
introduced by Macrì, Mehrotra, and Stellari in
[
MMS09
, Theorem 1.1]
. This is used to control the set of geometric stability conditions on any free quotient by a finite abelian group.

We also study the Le Potier function introduced by Fu, Li, and Zhao in
[
FLZ22
, §3.1]
. We give counter examples to the conjecture stated in
[
FLZ22
, §4]
, and explain how a refinement of the Le Potier function controls the set of geometric Bridgeland stability conditions on any surface.

1.1.
Geometric stability conditions and group actions

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field, and let
G
be a finite abelian group such that
(\Char(k),|G|)=1
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k
-linear idempotent complete triangulated category with an action of
G
in the sense of
[
Del97
]
. This induces an action on
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
, the space of all numerical Bridgeland stability conditions on
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{G}
denote the corresponding category of
G
-equivariant objects. There is a residual action by
\widehat{G}=\Hom(G,k^{\ast})
on
\mathcal{D}_{G}
(see Proposition
2.6
), and
(\mathcal{D}_{G})_{\widehat{G}}\cong\mathcal{D}
by
[
Ela15
, Theorem 4.2]
.
Theorem

2.26
describes an analytic isomorphism between
G
-invariant stability conditions on
\mathcal{D}
and
\widehat{G}
-invariant stability conditions on
\mathcal{D}_{G}
. This builds on
[
Pol07
, Proposition 2.2.3]
and
[
MMS09
, Theorem 1.1]
.

In this paper, we focus on the case where
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
for
X
a smooth projective connected variety over
\mathbf{C}
with a
G
-action. This induces an action on
\mathrm{Coh}(X)
(and hence
\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
) by pullback. In this setting,
(\mathrm{D^{b}}(X))_{G}\cong\mathrm{D^{b}_{G}}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{D^{b}}(\mathrm{Coh}_{G}(X))
, the bounded derived category of
G
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
X
. If
G
acts freely on
X
, we call
Y
a
free abelian quotient
and
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)\cong\mathrm{D^{b}_{G}}(X)
. Furthermore, let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y
denote the quotient map. Then
\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}
decomposes into a direct sum of numerically trivial line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}
according to the irreducible representations
\chi\in\widehat{G}
. The residual action of
\widehat{G}
on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
is given by
-\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\chi}
. The functors
\pi^{*}
and
\pi_{*}
give rise to the isomorphisms in
Theorem

2.26
. More precisely, given a
G
-invariant stability condition
\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}(X)
, there is a stability condition called
(\pi^{*})^{-1}\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}(Y)
with the property that
E\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
is semistable if and only if
\pi^{*}E
is
\sigma
-semistable. The construction with
(\pi_{*})^{-1}
is analogous.

A stability condition
\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Stab}(\mathrm{D^{b}}(X))
is called
geometric
if all skyscraper sheaves of points
\mathcal{O}_{x}
are
\sigma
-stable and of the same phase. In all known examples, the stability manifold contains an open set of geometric stability conditions. We prove that geometric stability conditions are preserved under
Theorem

2.26
:

Theorem
3.3
.

Suppose
G
is a finite abelian group acting freely on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y\coloneqq X/G
denote the quotient map. Consider the residual action of
\widehat{G}
on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
. Then the functors
\pi^{\ast}
and
\pi_{\ast}
induce an analytic isomorphism between
G
-invariant stability conditions on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
and
\widehat{G}
-invariant stability conditions on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
which preserve geometric stability conditions,

(\pi^{\ast})^{-1}\colon(\mathrm{Stab}(X))^{G}\overset{\cong}{\rightleftarrows}(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}\penalty\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\mskip 2.0mu(\pi_{\ast})^{-1}.

Very little is known about how the geometry of a variety
X
relates to the geometry of
\mathrm{Stab}(X)
. Recall that every variety
X
has an algebraic map
\alb_{X}
, the Albanese morphism, to the Albanese variety
\Alb(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X))
. Every morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow A
to another abelian variety
A
factors via
\alb_{X}
. In
[
FLZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
, the authors showed that if
X
has finite Albanese morphism, then all stability conditions on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
are geometric. In this set-up, we obtain a union of connected components of geometric stability conditions on any free abelian quotient of
X
.

Theorem
3.9
.

Let
X
be a variety with finite Albanese morphism. Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting freely on
X
and let
Y=X/G
. Then
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(Y)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}\cong\mathrm{Stab}(X)^{G}
is a union of connected components in
\mathrm{Stab}(Y)
consisting only of geometric stability conditions.

Let
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
denote the set of all geometric stability conditions. When
X
is a surface, we have the following stronger result.

Theorem
3.10
.

Let
X
be a surface with finite Albanese morphism. Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting freely on
X
. Let
S=X/G
. Then
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)\cong(\mathrm{Stab}(X))^{G}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)
is a connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}(S)
.

We explain in
Section

1.3
how to describe
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
explicitly for any surface
S
. Moreover,
Theorem

3.10
applies to the following two classes of minimal surfaces.

Example 1.1

(Beauville-type surfaces,
q=0
)
.

Let
X=C_{1}\times C_{2}
, where
C_{i}
are smooth projective curves of genus
g(C_{i})\geq 2
. Each curve has finite Albanese morphism, and hence so does
X
. Suppose there is a free action of a finite (not necessarily abelian) group
G
on
X
, such that
S=X/G
has
q(S)\coloneqq h^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
and
p_{g}(S)\coloneqq h^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
. Then
\alb_{S}
is trivial. This generalises a construction due to Beauville in
[
Bea96
, Chapter X, Exercise 4]
, and we call
S
a
Beauville-type surface
. These are classified in
[
BCG08
, Theorem 0.1]
. There are 17 families, 5 of which involve an abelian group. In the abelian cases,
G
is one of the following groups:
(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^{3}
,
(\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z})^{4}
,
(\mathbf{Z}/3\mathbf{Z})^{2}
,
(\mathbf{Z}/5\mathbf{Z})^{2}
.

Example 1.2

(Bielliptic surfaces,
q=1
)
.

Let
S\cong(E\times F)/G
, where
E,F
are elliptic curves, and
G
is a finite group of translations of
E
acting on
F
such that
F/G\cong\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Then
q(S)=1
and
\Alb(S)\cong E/G
, so
\alb_{S}
is an elliptic fibration. Such surfaces are called
bielliptic
and were first classified in
[
BD07
]
. There are 7 families, see
[
Bea96
, List VI.20]
.

Let
S
be a Beauville-type or bielliptic surface. As discussed above,
S
has non-finite Albanese morphism. By
Theorem

3.10
,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)\subset\mathrm{Stab}(S)
is a connected component. In particular, if
\mathrm{Stab}(S)
is connected, then the following question would have a negative answer.

Question 1.3

(
[
FLZ22
, Question 4.11]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety whose Albanese morphism is not finite. Are there always non-geometric stability conditions on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
?

This is the converse of
[
FLZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
. In all other known examples, the answer to Question
1.3
is positive (see
Section

1.4
).

1.2.
The Le Potier function

A fundamental problem in the study of stable sheaves on a smooth projective connected variety
X
over
\mathbf{C}
is to understand the set of Chern characters of stable sheaves. This can be used to describe
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
for surfaces (see Theorem
5.10
) and to control wall-crossing and hence indirectly control Brill–Noether phenomena as in
[
Bay18
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
Fey20
]
.

For
X=\mathbf{P}^{2}
, Drézet and Le Potier completely characterised the Chern characters of slope-stable sheaves in terms of a function of the slope,
\delta\colon\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}

[
DL85
, Theorem B]
. In
[
FLZ22
, §3.1]
, the authors define a Le Potier function
\Phi_{X,H}
which gives a generalisation of Drézet and Le Potier’s function to any polarised surface
(X,H)
. They use this to control geometric Bridgeland stability conditions with respect to a sublattice of the numerical
\mathrm{K}
-group of
X
,
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
, coming from the polarisation.

Let
\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{NS}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
, where
\mathrm{NS}(X)
is the Néron–Severi group of
X
, and let
\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
denote the ample cone inside
\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. In
Section

4
we introduce a generalisation of the Le Potier function. We state the version for surfaces below to ease notation.

Definition
5.8
.

Let
X
be a surface. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. We define the Le Potier function twisted by
B
,
\Phi_{X,H,B}\colon\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, by

\Phi_{X,H,B}(x)\coloneqq\limsup_{\mu\rightarrow x}\left\{\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F)-B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)}{H^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}\;:\parbox{150.00023pt}{$F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is $H$-semistable with $\mu_{H}(F)=\mu$}\right\}.

The Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality gives an upper bound for
\Phi_{X,H,B}
(see Lemma
4.7
). If
B=0
, this is the same as
[
FLZ22
, Definition 3.1]
, i.e.
\Phi_{X,H,0}=\Phi_{X,H}
, and the upper bound is
\frac{x^{2}}{2}
.
\Phi_{X,H,B}
naturally generalises to higher dimensions, see Definition
4.5
.

The Le Potier function partially determines the non-emptiness of moduli spaces of
H
-semistable sheaves of a fixed Chern character, which in turn controls wall crossing, along with the birational geometry of these moduli spaces, for example for
\mathbf{P}^{2}

[
LZ19
, Theorem 0.2, Theorem 0.4]
, K3 surfaces
[
BM14
, Theorem 5.7]
, and abelian surfaces
[
MYY12
, Theorem 4.4.1]
.

The Le Potier function is known for abelian surfaces
[
Muk84
, Corollary 0.2]
[
Yos01
]
, K3 surfaces
[
Huy16
, Chapter 10, Theorem 2.7]
, del Pezzo surfaces of degrees
9-m
for
m\leq 6

[
LZ23
, Theorem 7.15]
, Hirzebruch surfaces
[
CH21
, Theorem 9.7]
, and for surfaces with finite Albanese morphism
[
LR21
, Example 2.12(2)]
.

In this paper, we relate the Le Potier function of
X
to the Le Potier function of any free (not necessarily abelian) quotient of
X
by a finite group. We state the version of these results for arbitrary surfaces in the case where
B=0
below.

Proposition
4.11
.

Let
X
be a surface, and let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow X/G=:S
denote the quotient map, and let
H_{S}\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(S)
. Then
\Phi_{S,H_{S}}=\Phi_{X,\pi^{\ast}H_{S}}
.

Proposition
4.11
gives us a way to compute the Le Potier function for varieties that are finite free quotients of varieties with finite Albanese morphism.

Theorem
4.18
.

Let
X
be a surface with
\alb_{X}
finite. Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow X/G=:S
denote the quotient map. Let
H_{X}=\alb_{X}^{\ast}H=\pi^{\ast}H_{S}\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
be an ample class pulled back from
\Alb(X)
and
S
. Then
\Phi_{S,H_{S}}(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
.

In Example
4.19
we explain how to choose appropriate ample classes such that
Theorem

4.18
applies to bielliptic and Beauville-type surfaces. In particular, Beauville-type surfaces provide counterexamples to the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
FLZ22
, §4.4]
)
.

Let
(S,H)
be a polarised surface with
q=0
, then the Le Potier function
\Phi_{S,H}
is not continuous at 0.

This conjecture was motivated by Question
1.3
and the expectation that discontinuities of
\Phi_{S,H}
could be used to show the existence of a wall of the geometric chamber for regular surfaces, as in the cases of rational and K3 surfaces.

1.3.
The Le Potier function and geometric stability conditions

Let
X
be a surface and fix
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. In
[
FLZ22
, Theorem 3.4, Proposition 3.6]
, the authors showed that
\Phi_{X,H}
gives precise control over
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
, the set of geometric numerical Bridgeland stability conditions with respect to a sublattice
\Lambda_{H}\subset\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
(see
Remark

3.13
). When
X
has Picard rank
1
,
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
.

We generalise this to the set of all geometric numerical Bridgeland stability conditions.

Theorem
5.10
.

Let
X
be a surface. There is a homeomorphism of topological spaces

\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)\cong\mathbf{C}\times\left\{(H,B,\alpha,\beta)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathbf{R}^{2}:\alpha>\Phi_{X,H,B}(\beta)\right\}.

In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
is connected. We discuss in Remark
5.37
how Theorem
5.10
could be used to describe the boundary of
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
. This emphasises how
\Phi_{X,H,B}
is a crucial tool for understanding the existence of non-geometric stability conditions on surfaces. In particular, if one can compute the Le Potier function, one should be able to tell whether the boundary of the set of geometric stability conditions has a wall.

1.4.
Survey: Geometric stability conditions

It is still an open question whether geometric stability conditions exist on any smooth projective variety
X
over
\mathbf{C}
. If the answer to
1.3
is negative, then the question also remains as to which geometric properties characterise the existence of non-geometric stability conditions. To give context for these questions and for the results in this paper, we now survey all of the examples where geometric and non-geometric stability conditions are known to exist.

There are the following general results:

•

Varieties with
\alb_{X}
finite:
\mathrm{Stab}(X)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)

[
FLZ22
, Theorem 1.1]

•

Quotients of varieties with
\alb_{X}
finite: Let
Y=X/G
be a free abelian quotient of
X
, and assume
\alb_{X}
is finite. If
G
-invariant stability conditions exist on
X
, then
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(Y)
is a union of connected components of
\mathrm{Stab}(Y)
consisting only of geometric stability conditions, see Theorem
3.9
.

Curves:
The universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
of
\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{R})
acts on
\mathrm{Stab}(X)
(see
[
MS17
, Remark 5.14]
). Up to this action, a geometric stability condition on a curve
C
corresponds to slope-stability for
\mathrm{Coh}(C)
. Hence
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(C)\cong\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbf{R})\cong\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{H}

[
Mac07a
, Theorem 2.7]
.

•

\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{P}^{1})\cong\mathbf{C}^{2}

[
Oka06
, Theorem 1.1]
. Okada’s construction uses
\mathrm{D^{b}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})\cong\mathrm{D^{b}}(\mathrm{Rep}(K_{2}))
where
K_{2}
is the Kroneker quiver. In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{P}^{1})\supsetneq\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(\mathbf{P}^{1})
.

•

Let
C
be a curve of genus
g(C)\geq 1
, then
\mathrm{Stab}(C)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(C)\cong\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbf{R})

[
Bri07
, Theorem 9.1]
,
[
Mac07a
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Surfaces:
There is a construction called
tilting
which gives an open set of geometric stability conditions on any surface, see for example
[
AB13
,
MS17
]
.

A connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}(X)
is known in the following cases. This component always contains
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
, but in some cases non-geometric stability conditions exist.

•

Surfaces with finite Albanese morphism: This connected component is precisely the set of geometric stability conditions which come from tilting. This follows from
[
FLZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
together with
Theorem

5.10
.

•

K3 surfaces: There is a distinguished connected component
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)
described by taking the closure and translates under autoequivalences of the open set of geometric stability conditions
[
Bri08
, Theorem 1.1]
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}
contains non-geometric stability conditions. By
[
Bri08
, Theorem 12.1]
, at general points of the boundary of
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
, either

–

all skyscraper sheaves have a spherical vector bundle as a stable factor, or

–

\mathcal{O}_{x}
is strictly semistable if and only if
x\in C
, a smooth rational curve in
X
.

•

\mathbf{P}^{2}
:
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{P}^{2})
has a simply-connected component,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{P}^{2})
, which is a union of geometric and
algebraic
stability conditions. The construction of the latter uses
\mathrm{D^{b}}(\mathbf{P}^{2})\cong\mathrm{D^{b}}(\mathrm{Rep}(Q,R))
for the associated Beilinson quiver
Q
with relations
R

[
Li17
, Theorem 0.1]
.

•

Enriques surfaces: Suppose
Y
is an Enriques surface with K3 cover
X
, and let
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)
be the connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}(X)
described above. Then there exists a connected component
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(Y)
which embeds into
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)
as a closed submanifold. Moreover, when
Y
is very general,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)\cong\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)

[
MMS09
, Theorem 1.2]
.
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)
has non-geometric stability conditions, hence by Theorem
3.3
so does
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(Y)
.

•

Beauville-type and bielliptic surfaces: Let
S=X/G
. By
Theorem

3.10
there is a connected component
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)\cong(\mathrm{Stab}(X))^{G}
.

Non-geometric stability conditions are also known to exist in the following cases:

•

Rational surfaces: the boundary of
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
contains points where skyscrapers sheaves are destabilised by exceptional bundles. This follows from the same arguments as
[
BM11
, §5]
, where the authors use pushforwards of exceptional bundles on
\mathbf{P}^{2}
to destabilise skyscraper sheaves on
\Tot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(-3))
.

•

Surfaces which contain a smooth rational curve
C
with negative self intersection: these have a wall of the geometric chamber such that
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is stable if
x\not\in C
, and strictly semistable if
x\in C

[
TX22
, Lemma 7.2]
,
[
LR22
, Proposition 5.3]
.

Threefolds:

Fix
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. Denote by
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)
the space of stability conditions such that the central charge factors via the sublattice
\Lambda_{H}\subset\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
(see
Remark

3.13
). If
\rho(X)=1
,
\Lambda_{H}=\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
so
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)=\mathrm{Stab}(X)
. A strategy for constructing stability conditions in
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)
for threefolds was first introduced in
[
BMT13
, §3, §4]
. This uses so-called tilt stability conditions to construct geometric stability conditions whenever a conjectural Bogomolov–Gieseker type inequality is satisfied, i.e. a bound on the Chern characters of stable objects.

Geometric stability conditions in
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)
exist for some threefolds, see
[
BMS16
, Theorem 1.4]
,
[
Ber+17
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
Piy17
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
Kos18
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
Li19
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
Kos20
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
Kos22
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
Liu22
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Below we describe the only threefolds where
\mathrm{Stab}(X)
is known to be non-empty. These are also the only cases where a connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)
was previously known.

•

Abelian threefolds: There is a distinguished connected component
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}^{\dagger}(X)
of
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(X)
which is completely described in
[
BMS16
, Theorem 1.4]
. Stability conditions in
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}^{\dagger}(X)
have been shown to satisfy the support property with respect to
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
, in particular, they lie in a connected component
\mathrm{Stab}^{\dagger}(X)\subset\mathrm{Stab}(X)

[

Theorem 2.26
.

Suppose

. Therefore, by

2.23
and

2.24
,
\sigma_{G}\coloneqq\mathrm{Forg}_{G}^{-1}(\sigma)
is in
(\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{lf}}(\mathcal{D}_{G}))^{\widehat{G}}

Proposition

2.23
again but with
\mathrm{Forg}_{\widehat{G}}
. In particular, let
\sigma_{\widehat{G}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Forg}_{\widehat{G}}^{-1}(\sigma_{G})
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{\widehat{G}}}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{E}\in(\mathcal{D}_{G})_{\widehat{G}}:\mathrm{Forg}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{G}}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{E}\in(\mathcal{D}_{G})_{\widehat{G}}:\mathrm{Forg}_{G}(\mathrm{Forg}_{\widehat{G}}(\mathcal{E}))\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)\}.

By Proposition
2.23
,

,
\sigma_{\widehat{G}}=\sigma
up to rescaling the central charge by
|G|
. From Theorem
2.10
we know that

under this equivalence. Hence we can apply the same argument as in the proof of
[

, Proposition 2.17]
. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{\widehat{G}}}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{Forg}_{G}(\mathrm{Inf}_{G}(\mathcal{E}))\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{D}:\bigoplus_{g\in G}\Phi_{g}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)\right\}.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{\widehat{G}}}(\phi)
. Since
\mathcal{P}(\phi)
is closed under direct summands,
\Phi_{g}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)
for all

. Thus
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)
. Now suppose
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)

Note that if we start instead with a stability condition

\displaystyle(\Hom_{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D}),\mathbf{C}))^{G}

\displaystyle\overset{\cong}{\rightleftarrows}(\Hom_{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D}_{G}),\mathbf{C}))^{\widehat{G}}

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\mapsto Z\circ\mathrm{Forg}_{\widehat{G}}

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}\circ\mathrm{Forg}_{G}

\displaystyle\mapsfrom Z^{\prime}.

∎

Step 2

Suppose that
\sigma_{Y}=(\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{Y}},Z_{\sigma_{Y}})\in(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}
is geometric. Recall

\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{Y}}(\phi)=\{\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y):\pi^{\ast}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi)\},

for all
\phi\in\mathbf{R}
. Fix
x\in X
, and let
y\in Y
be the point corresponding to the orbit
Gx
. By assumption,
\mathcal{O}_{y}
is
\sigma_{Y}
-stable. Let
\phi_{\mathrm{sky}}
denote its phase. Then
\pi^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{y})=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
is closed under direct summands,
g^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
for all
g\in G
. In particular,
\mathcal{O}_{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
. Now suppose that
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is strictly semistable, then there exist
A,B\in\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
such that

A\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{x}\twoheadrightarrow B

is a non-trivial exact sequence in
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
, i.e.
A
is not isomorphic to
0
or
\mathcal{O}_{x}
. By Step 1,
(\pi_{\ast})^{-1}
sends
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
to
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{Y}}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
. Hence we have a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{Y}}(\phi_{\mathrm{sky}})
,

\pi_{\ast}(A)\hookrightarrow\pi_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}_{x})=\mathcal{O}_{y}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{\ast}(B).

However,
\mathcal{O}_{y}
is stable, hence
\pi_{\ast}(A)=0
or
\pi_{\ast}(B)=0
. But
\pi
is finite, hence
\pi_{\ast}
is conservative. Therefore
A=0
or
B=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

3.2.
Group actions and geometric stability conditions on surfaces

We denote by
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
the set of all geometric stability conditions on
X
. We will see in
Theorem

5.5
that if
X
is a surface then
\sigma\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
is determined by its central charge up to shifting by
[2n]
. This means that, to test if
\sigma
is
G
-invariant, we only have to check the central charge:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a group acting on a surface
X
. Then
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
is
G
-invariant if and only if
Z
is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

If
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
is
G
-invariant, then so is
Z
. Suppose
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
and
Z
is
G
-invariant. Fix
g\in G
. Then
g^{\ast}\sigma=(g^{\ast}(\mathcal{P}),Z\circ g^{\ast})
and
\sigma
are both geometric, and skyscraper sheaves have the same phase. By Theorem
5.5
,
\sigma=g^{\ast}\sigma
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(X)
be a finite subgroup. Then the induced action of
G
on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
is trivial.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in G
and
[E]\in\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
. The induced action of
G
on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
is given by
\mathcal{L}\cdot[E]\coloneqq[E\otimes\mathcal{L}]
. Since
\mathcal{L}
is a numerically trivial line bundle,
\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal{L})=e^{c_{1}(\mathcal{L})}
and
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})=0
in
\mathrm{Chow_{num}}(X)
. Therefore,

\mathrm{ch}\mid_{\mathrm{K_{num}}}([E\otimes\mathcal{L}])=\mathrm{ch}\mid_{\mathrm{K_{num}}}([E])\cdot\mathrm{ch}\mid_{\mathrm{K_{num}}}(\mathcal{L})=\mathrm{ch}\mid_{\mathrm{K_{num}}}([E]).

By Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch,
\mathrm{ch}\colon\mathrm{K}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Chow}(X)
induces an injective map
\mathrm{ch}\colon\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{Chow_{num}}(X)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}\cdot[E]=[E\otimes\mathcal{L}]=[E]
in
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
S
be a surface and let
G\subseteq\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(S)
be a finite subgroup. Then every geometric stability condition on
S
is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
. By
Lemma

3.4
it is enough to show that
Z
is
G
invariant. By
Lemma

3.5
,
G
acts trivially on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(S)
. Since
\sigma
is numerical,
Z\colon\mathrm{K}(S)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}
factors via
\mathrm{K_{num}}(S)
, hence
Z
is
G
invariant.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Suppose
G
is a finite abelian group acting freely on a variety
X
, and let
Y\coloneqq X/G
. Then by Proposition
2.6
there is also an action of
\widehat{G}=\Hom(G,\mathbf{C})
on
\mathrm{D^{b}_{G}}(X)\cong\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
. As discussed in
Section

3.1
, the corresponding action on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
is given by tensoring with a numerically trivial line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}
for each

. If

is a surface, then
Corollary

3.6
tells us that every geometric stability condition on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
is
\widehat{G}
invariant.

3.3.
Applications to varieties with finite Albanese morphism

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts on a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
by exact autoequivalences such that the induced action on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D})
is trivial. Then
(\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D}))^{G}
is a union of connected components inside
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
.

Proof.

By
Theorem

2.16
, there is a local homeomorphism

\mathcal{Z}\colon\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbf{Z}}(\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D}),\mathbf{C}).

Let
g\in G
, and denote by
(\Phi_{g})_{\ast}
the induced action of
g
on
\mathrm{K}(\mathcal{D})
and
\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D})
. Recall that the action of
G
on
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
is given by
(\Phi_{g})_{\ast}\cdot\sigma=(\Phi_{g}(\mathcal{P}),Z\circ(\Phi_{g})_{\ast}^{-1})
. The induced action of
G
on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(\mathcal{D})
is trivial, hence
\mathcal{Z}(\sigma)
is
G
-invariant and
\mathcal{Z}(g\cdot\sigma)=\mathcal{Z}(\sigma)
. Furthermore,
G
acts continuously on
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
, and the local homeomorphism
\mathcal{Z}
commutes with this action. Hence the properties of being
G
-invariant and not being
G
-invariant are open in
\mathrm{Stab}(\mathcal{D})
, so the result follows.
∎

We now combine this with the results of
Section

3.1
and
Section

3.2
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
X
be a variety with finite Albanese morphism. Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting freely on
X
and let
Y=X/G
. Then
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(Y)\coloneqq(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}\cong\mathrm{Stab}(X)^{G}
is a union of connected components in
\mathrm{Stab}(Y)
consisting only of geometric stability conditions.

Proof.

X
has finite Albanese morphism, so it follows from
[
FLZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
that all stability conditions on
X
are geometric. In particular, all
G
-invariant stability conditions on
X
are geometric, so from Theorem
3.3
it follows that all
\widehat{G}
-invariant stability conditions on
Y
are geometric. Hence
(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}\subset\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(Y)
.

Recall from Example
3.7
that
\widehat{G}
acts on
\mathrm{D^{b}}(Y)
by tensoring with numerically trivial line bundles. Now we may apply Lemma
3.5
, so it follows that
\widehat{G}
acts trivially on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(Y)
. Hence, by Lemma
3.8
,
(\mathrm{Stab}(Y))^{\widehat{G}}
is a union of connected components.
∎

When
X
is a surface, we will see in
Theorem

5.36
that
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
is connected. Hence we have the following stronger result.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
X
be a surface with finite Albanese morphism. Let
G
be a finite abelian group acting freely on
X
. Let
S=X/G
. Then
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)\cong(\mathrm{Stab}(X))^{G}
. In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)
is a connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}(S)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.9
,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)\subset\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
is a union of connected components. By
Theorem

5.36
,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
is connected. In particular,
\mathrm{Stab}^{\ddagger}(S)=\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(S)
, and this is a connected component of
\mathrm{Stab}(S)
.
∎

4.
The Le Potier function

We compute the Le Potier function of free abelian quotients and varieties with finite Albanese morphism. We apply this to Beauville-type surfaces which provides counterexamples to Conjecture
1.4
. Throughout,
X
will be a smooth projective connected variety over
\mathbf{C}
.

4.1.
H
-stability

Notation 4.1
.

Let
A\cdot B
denote the intersection product of elements of
\mathrm{Chow_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
. If
A\cdot B
is 0-dimensional, we define
A\mathbin{.}B\coloneqq\deg(A\cdot B)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\dim X=n
. Fix an ample class
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. Given
0\neq F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)
, we define the
H-slope
of
F
as follows:

\mu_{H}(F)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)},&\text{if }\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)>0;\\
+\infty,&\text{if }\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)=0.\end{cases}

We say that
F
is
H-(semi)stable
if for every non-zero subobject
E\subsetneq F
,

\mu_{H}(E)<(\leq)\mu_{H}(F/E).

4.2.
The Le Potier function

When studying
H
-stability, a natural question that arises is whether there are necessary and sufficient conditions on a cohomology class
\gamma\in H^{\ast}(X,\mathbf{Q})
for there to exist a
H
-semistable sheaf
F
with
\mathrm{ch}(F)=\gamma
.

The Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality (see
[
Bog79
, §10]
, or
[
HL10
, Theorem 3.4.1]
) gives the following necessary condition for
H
-semistable sheaves on surfaces:

2\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F)\leq\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)^{2}.

This generalises to the following statement for any variety
X
of dimension
n\geq 2
via the Mumford–Mehta–Ramanathan restriction theorem.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
Lan04
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
HL10
, Theorem 7.3.1]
)
.

Assume
\dim X=n\geq 2
. Fix
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. If
F
is a torsion-free
H
-semistable sheaf, then

2\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)(H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F))\leq H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)^{2}.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
B\in\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. The
twisted Chern character
is defined by
\mathrm{ch}^{B}\coloneqq\mathrm{ch}\cdot e^{-B}
. Then

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(F)(H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(F))-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}(\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(F))^{2}=2\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)(H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F))-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)^{2},

hence
Theorem

4.3
also holds for twisted Chern characters.

Now assume
\dim X=n\geq 2
and fix
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. Then
H^{n}>0
. Let
F
be any
H
-semistable torsion-free sheaf. By the twisted version of Theorem
4.3
,

2H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)(H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(F))\leq H^{n}(H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(F)^{2})\leq(H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(F))^{2},

where the final inequality is by the Hodge Index Theorem. Since
F
is torsion free,

\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}\right)^{2}.

Now we expand the expressions for
\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(F)
and
\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(F)
:

\displaystyle\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F)-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)+\frac{1}{2}H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)-H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{H}(F)-\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}}\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

(2)

\nu_{H,B}(F)\coloneqq\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{2}(F)-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(F)}{H^{n}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu_{H}(F)-\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}}{H^{n}}.

For a given
\mu\in\mathbf{R}
, if
\mu_{H}(F)=\mu
, we can therefore ask how large
\nu_{H,B}(F)
can be. These leads us to make the following definition.

Definition 4.5
.

Assume
\dim X=n\geq 2
. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. We define the
Le Potier function twisted by
B
,
\Phi_{X,H,B}\colon\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, by

(3)

\Phi_{X,H,B}(x)\coloneqq\limsup_{\mu\rightarrow x}\left\{\nu_{H,B}(F)\colon\text{$F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)$ is $H$-semistable with $\mu_{H}(F)=\mu$}\right\}.

Remark 4.6
.

If
B=0
, we will write
\Phi_{X,H}\coloneqq\Phi_{X,H,0}
. If
n=2
, then
\Phi_{X,H}
is exactly
[
FLZ22
, Definition 3.1]
.

The above discussion and definition generalises
[
FLZ22
, Proposition 3.2]
:

Lemma 4.7

(
[
FLZ22
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. Then
\Phi_{X,H,B}
is well defined and satisfies

\Phi_{X,H,B}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(x-\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}}\right)^{2}-\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}}{H^{n}}\right].

It is the smallest upper semi-continuous function such that

\nu_{H,B}(F)\leq\Phi_{X,H,B}\left(\mu_{H}(F)\right)

for every torsion-free
H
-semistable sheaf
F
.

4.3.
The Le Potier function for free quotients

Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on
X
. There is an étale covering,
\pi\colon X\rightarrow X/G=:Y
. Then
\mathrm{Pic}(Y)\cong\mathrm{Pic}_{G}(X)
, the group of isomorphism classes of
G
-equivariant line bundles on
X
. Fix
H_{S}\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(Y)
. Then
\pi^{\ast}H_{S}\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
is
G
-invariant. Beauville-type and bielliptic surfaces provide examples of such quotients.

Example 4.8

(Ample classes on Beauville-type surfaces)
.

Let
S=X/G
be a Beauville-type surface, as introduced in Example
1.1
. Then
X=C_{1}\times C_{2}
is a product of curves of genus
g(C_{i})\geq 2
,
q(S)\coloneqq h^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
,
p_{g}(S)\coloneqq h^{2}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=0
so
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})=1
, and
K_{S}^{2}=8
where
K_{S}
is the canonical divisor of
S
.

Assume that there are actions of
G
on each curve
C_{i}
such that the action of
G
on
C_{1}\times C_{2}
is the diagonal action. This is called the
unmixed case
in
[
BCG08
, Theorem 0.1]
and excludes 3 families of dimension 0. To classify ample classes on
S
, we follow similar arguments to
[
GS13
, §2.2]
. Let
p_{i}\colon X\rightarrow C_{i}
denote the projections to each curve. For
i,j\in\mathbf{Z}
, define the
G
-invariant divisor class

[\mathcal{O}(i,j)]\coloneqq p_{1}^{\ast}([\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}(i)])\otimes p_{2}^{\ast}([\mathcal{O}_{C_{2}}(j)])\in\mathrm{NS}_{G}(X).

Moreover,

\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(S)=\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(C_{1})\cdot\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(C_{2})}{|G|}=4\frac{(1-g(C_{1}))(1-g(C_{2}))}{|G|}=4\chi(\mathcal{O}_{S})=4.

Therefore,
\rank\mathrm{NS}(S)=b_{2}(S)=2
and

\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(S)\cong\mathbf{Q}\cdot[\mathcal{O}(1,0)]\oplus\mathbf{Q}\cdot[\mathcal{O}(0,1)].

In particular,
\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(S)\cong\mathbf{R}_{>0}\cdot[\mathcal{O}(1,0)]\oplus\mathbf{R}_{>0}\cdot[\mathcal{O}(0,1)]
.

Lemma 4.9

(
[
HL10
, Lemma 3.2.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a finite morphism of varieties of dimension
n\geq 2
and let
E\in\mathrm{Coh}(Y)
. Let
(H_{Y},B_{Y})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(Y)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(Y)
. Then
E
is
H_{Y}
-semistable if and only if
f^{*}E
is
f^{*}H_{Y}
-semistable. Moreover, if
\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E)\neq 0
, then
\mu_{H_{Y}}(E)=\mu_{f^{*}H_{Y}}(f^{*}E)
and
\nu_{H_{Y},B_{Y}}(E)=\nu_{f^{*}H_{Y},f^{*}B_{Y}}(f^{*}E)
. In particular,
\Phi_{X,f^{*}H_{Y},f^{*}B_{Y}}\geq\Phi_{Y,H_{Y},B_{Y}}
.

Proof.

The claim that
E
is
H_{Y}
-semistable if and only if
f^{*}E
is
f^{*}H_{Y}
-semistable follows from the same arguments as in the proof of
[
HL10
, Lemma 3.2.2]
. If
\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E)\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{f^{\ast}H_{Y}}(f^{\ast}E)

\displaystyle=\frac{\deg((f^{\ast}H_{Y})^{n-1}\cdot f^{\ast}(\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E)))}{\deg((f^{\ast}H_{Y})^{n}\cdot f^{\ast}(\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E)))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\deg(f^{\ast}(H_{Y}^{n-1}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E)))}{\deg(f^{\ast}(H_{Y}^{n}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E)))}\quad\text{($f$ is flat, so $f^{\ast}$ is a ring morphism)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\deg(f)\deg(H_{Y}^{n-1}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E))}{\deg(f)\deg(H_{Y}^{n}\cdot\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E))}\quad\text{(projection formula)}

\displaystyle=\mu_{H_{Y}}(E)

By the same arguments,
\nu_{f^{*}H_{Y},f^{*}B_{Y}}(f^{*}E)=\nu_{H_{Y},B_{Y}}(E)
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Suppose a finite group
G
acts freely on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y\coloneqq X/G
denote the quotient map. If
F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)
is
\pi^{\ast}H_{Y}
-semistable, then
\pi_{\ast}F
is
H_{Y}
-semistable. Moreover, if
\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)\neq 0
, then
\mu_{H_{Y}}(\pi_{*}F)=\mu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y}}(F)
and
\nu_{H_{Y},B_{Y}}(\pi_{*}F)=\nu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y},\pi^{*}B_{Y}}(F)
. In particular,
\Phi_{X,\pi^{*}H_{Y},\pi^{*}B_{Y}}\leq\Phi_{Y,H_{Y},B_{Y}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
F\in\mathrm{Coh}(X)
is
\pi^{\ast}H_{Y}
-semistable. Recall from
Section

3.1
that, under the equivalence
\mathrm{Coh}(Y)\cong\mathrm{Coh}_{G}(X)
,
\pi^{*}\circ\pi_{*}\cong\mathrm{Forg}_{G}\circ\mathrm{Inf}_{G}
, hence

\pi^{\ast}(\pi_{\ast}(F))\cong\mathrm{Forg}_{G}\circ\mathrm{Inf}_{G}(F)=\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{\ast}F.

Since
\pi^{\ast}H_{Y}
is
G
-invariant, it follows that
g^{\ast}F
is
\pi^{\ast}H_{Y}
-semistable for every
g\in G
. In particular,
\bigoplus_{g\in G}g^{\ast}F
is
\pi^{\ast}H_{Y}
-semistable. By Lemma
4.9
,
\pi_{\ast}F
is
H_{Y}
-semistable.

Now suppose
\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)\neq 0
. Since the Chern character is additive,
\mu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y}}(\pi^{\ast}\pi_{\ast}F)=\mu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y}}(F)
. By Lemma
4.9
,
\mu_{H_{Y}}(\pi_{\ast}F)=\mu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y}}(\pi^{\ast}\pi_{\ast}F)=\mu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y}}(F)
, as required.

By the same arguments,
\nu_{H_{Y},B_{Y}}(\pi_{*}F)=\nu_{\pi^{*}H_{Y},\pi^{*}B_{Y}}(F)
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose a finite group
G
acts freely on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\rightarrow Y\coloneqq X/G
denote the quotient map. Let
(H_{Y},B_{Y})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(Y)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(Y)
. Then

.

Proof.

This follows from

and

.

4.4.
The Le Potier function for varieties with finite Albanese morphism

The Le Potier function for surfaces with finite Albanese morphism was known previously
[
LR21
, Example 2.12(2)]
. Below, we give a different proof which works for
\Phi_{X,H,B}
in any dimension. We first need the following definition.

Definition 4.12

(
[
Muk78
, Definitions 4.4, 5.2]
)
.

A vector bundle
E
on an abelian variety
A
is
homogeneous
if it is invariant under translations, i.e. for every
x\in A
,
T_{x}^{\ast}(E)\cong E
, where
T_{x}
is translation on
A
by
x
.
E
is called
semi-homogeneous
if for every
x\in A
, there exists a line bundle
L
on
A
such that
T_{x}^{\ast}(E)\cong E\otimes L
.

See
[
Muk78
, Proposition 5.1]
for some equivalent characterisations for when a vector bundle is semi-homogeneous. We will need the following properties.

Theorem 4.13

(
[
Muk78
, Theorem 4.17, Lemma 6.11]
)
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle with
ch_{0}(E)=r
on an abelian variety
A
.

(1)

E
is homogeneous if and only if
E\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\left(P_{i}\otimes U_{i}\right)
, where each
P_{i}
is a numerically trivial line bundle, and each
U_{i}
is a unipotent line bundle, i.e. an iterated self-extension of
\mathcal{O}_{A}
.

(2)

Suppose
E
is semihomogeneous and consider the multiplication by
r
map,
r_{A}\colon A\rightarrow A
. Then
r_{A}^{*}E\cong\det(E)^{\otimes r}\otimes V
, where
V
is a homogeneous vector bundle with
\mathrm{ch}_{0}(V)=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(r_{A}^{*}E)
and
c_{1}(V)=0
.

There are many
H
-semistable semi-homogeneous vector bundles on any abelian variety.

Proposition 4.14

(
[
Muk78
, Theorem 7.11]
)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety and fix
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(A)
. For every divisor class
C\in\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(A)
there exists a
H
-semistable semi-homogeneous vector bundle
E_{C}
on
A
with
C=\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E_{C})}{\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C})}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E_{C})=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C})\cdot e^{C}
.

Proof.

These vector bundles are constructed as follows: for any
C\in\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(A)
, write
C=\frac{[L]}{l}
, where
[L]
is the equivalence class of
L\in\mathrm{NS}(A)
and
l\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
. Let
l_{A}\colon A\rightarrow A
denote the multiplication by
l
map, and define
F=(l_{A})_{*}((L)^{\otimes l})
. By
[
Muk78
, Proposition 6.22]
,
F
is a semi-homogeneous vector bundle with
C=\delta(F)\coloneqq\frac{\det(F)}{\mathrm{ch}_{0}(F)}
. Moreover,
F
has a filtration by semi-homogeneous vector bundles
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
. By
[
Muk78
, Proposition 6.15]
, each
E_{i}
is
\mu_{H}
-semistable for any
H\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(A)
and satisfies
C=\delta(E_{i})
.

Let
E_{C}\coloneqq E_{1}
and let
r=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C})
. We claim that
\mathrm{ch}(E_{C})=re^{C}
. Consider the multiplication by
r
map
r_{A}\colon X\rightarrow X
. By
Theorem

4.13
(2),
r_{A}^{*}E_{C}\cong\det(E_{C})^{\otimes r}\otimes V
, where
V
is a homogeneous vector bundle, and

(4)

\mathrm{ch}(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})=\mathrm{ch}(V)\cdot\mathrm{ch}\left(\det(E_{C})^{\otimes r}\right)=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})e^{r\det(E_{C})}=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})e^{r^{2}C}.

Now recall that
H^{2i}(A,\mathbf{C})=\bigwedge^{2i}H^{1}(A,\mathbf{C})
. On
H^{1}(A,\mathbf{C})
,
r_{A}^{*}
is multiplication by
r
. It follows that on
\mathrm{Chow}_{\mathrm{num}}^{i}(X)
,
r_{A}^{*}
is multiplication by
r^{2i}
. In particular,
\delta(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})=r^{2}\delta(E_{C})
. Hence (
4
) becomes

\mathrm{ch}(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})e^{\delta(r_{A}^{*}E_{C})}=r_{A}^{*}\left(\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C})e^{\delta(E_{C})}\right)=r_{A}^{*}\left(re^{C}\right).

Since
r_{A}
is flat, the claim follows.
∎

We use this to compute the Le Potier function for abelian varieties.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Fix
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(A)\times\mathrm{NS}_{R}(A)
. Then

\Phi_{A,H,B}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(x-\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}}\right)^{2}-\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}}{H^{n}}\right].

Proof.

For any
k\in\mathbf{Q}
define
C_{k}\coloneqq kH+B
. Then by
Proposition

4.14
, there exists a
\mu_{H}
-semistable vector bundle
E_{C_{k}}
with
C_{k}=\frac{\mathrm{ch}_{1}(E_{C_{k}})}{\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C_{k}})}
and
\mathrm{ch}(E_{C_{k}})=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C_{k}})\cdot e^{C_{k}}
. Let
r=\mathrm{ch}_{0}(E_{C_{k}})
. Hence

\mu_{H}(E_{C_{k}})=\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}rC_{k}}{H^{n}r}=k+\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}},

and

\displaystyle\nu_{H,B}(E_{C_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\frac{1}{2}rC_{k}^{2}-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B\mathbin{.}rC_{k}}{H^{n}r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}\left(k^{2}H^{2}+2kH\mathbin{.}B+B^{2}\right)-H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}(2kH\mathbin{.}B+2B^{2})}{H^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[k^{2}-\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}}{H^{n}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\mu_{H}(E_{C_{k}})-\frac{H^{n-1}\mathbin{.}B}{H^{n}}\right)^{2}-\frac{H^{n-2}\mathbin{.}B^{2}}{H^{2}}\right].

This gives a lower bound for
\Phi_{A,H,B}\left(\mu_{H}(E_{C_{k}})\right)
, which is the same as the upper bound in Lemma
4.7
. Now note that for any
x\in\mathbf{Q}
, we can choose
k
so that
\mu_{H}(E_{C_{k}})=x
. Hence
\Phi_{A,H,B}(x)
attains its upper bound for all
x\in\mathbf{Q}
. Finally, by definition of the Le Potier function, it must attain this upper bound for all
x\in\mathbf{R}
.
∎

Varieties with finite Albanese morphism also have many

-semistable vector bundles.

5.1.
The deformation property and tilting

To prove existence of stability conditions later in this section, we will need the following refinement of Theorem
2.16
:

Proposition 5.1

(
[
BMS16
, Proposition A.5]
,
[
Bay19
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category. Assume

(\Lambda,\lambda)
and a quadratic form
Q
on
\Lambda\otimes\mathbf{R}
. Consider the open subset of
\Hom_{\mathbf{Z}}(\Lambda,\mathbf{C})
consisting of central charges whose kernel is negative definite with respect to
Q
, and let

be the connected component containing

. Let
\mathcal{Z}
denote the local homeomorphism from Theorem
2.16
, and let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathrm{Stab}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{D})
be the connected component of the preimage
\mathcal{Z}^{-1}(U)
containing
\sigma

(1)

the restriction
\mathcal{Z}|_{\mathcal{U}}\colon\mathcal{U}\rightarrow U
is a covering map, and

(2)

any stability condition
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category. Assume
\sigma=(\mathcal{P},Z)\in\mathrm{Stab}_{\Lambda}(\mathcal{D})

(\Lambda,\lambda)
and a quadratic form

\Lambda\otimes\mathbf{R}

be the connected components from Proposition
5.1
. Suppose there is a path

in

\Im{Z_{t}}
is constant and

passing through

\sigma_{t}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
denote the local homeomorphism from Theorem

. By Proposition

(1),
\mathcal{Z}|_{\mathcal{U}}\colon\mathcal{U}\rightarrow U
is a covering map. By the path lifting property, there is a unique path
\sigma_{t}=(\mathcal{Q}_{t},Z_{t})
in
\mathcal{U}
with
\sigma=\sigma_{t_{0}}
. By Proposition
5.1
(2),
\sigma_{t}
satisfies the support property with respect to

for all

. It remains to show that
\mathcal{Q}_{t}(0,1]=\mathcal{P}(0,1]

Fix a non-zero object

. We claim that the set of points in the path
\sigma_{t}
where

is open and closed. Suppose

. Then all Jordan–Hölder (JH) factors of

with respect to
\sigma_{T}
,

, are in
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(0,1]
and satisfy

.

Now suppose

5.3.

In the previous section, we saw that a geometric stability condition on a surface with

Definition 5.8

(H^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)
are nearby rational classes.

Remark 5.20
.

Theorem
5.19
was first proved for K3 surfaces in
[
Bri08
]
, along with the fact that this gives rise to a continuous family. In
[
MS17
, Theorem 6.10]
, the authors first prove the result holds for rational classes
(H,B)
and sketch how to extend this to arbitrary classes. In particular,
\sigma_{H,B}
can be obtained as a deformation of
\sigma_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
for nearby rational classes

satisfies the same support property,

Proposition 5.21
.

Let
X
be a surface. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
and fix
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}
such that
\alpha_{0}>\Phi_{X,H,B}(\beta_{0})
. Suppose
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}\right]
. Define
b\coloneqq\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\in\mathbf{R}
and
a\coloneqq\sqrt{2\alpha-b^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
. Then
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}
and
\sigma_{aH,B+bH}
are the same up to the action of
\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
. Moreover, this is a continuous family in
\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
for
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}\right]
.

Proof.

We abuse notation and consider the central charges as homomorphisms
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}
. We first claim that
\Ker Z_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}=\Ker Z_{aH,B+bH}
as sub-vector spaces of
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
. Fix
u\in\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
. Since
a>0
,
\Im Z_{aH,B+bH}(u)=0
if and only if

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=aH\mathbin{.}B\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)+abH^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)-aH\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)

\displaystyle=a\left(H\mathbin{.}B\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)+\left(\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\right)H^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)-H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=a\left(\beta_{0}H^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)-H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=-a\Im Z_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}(u).

Therefore,
\Im Z_{aH,B+bH}(u)=0
if and only if
\Im Z_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}(u)=0
. Now assume
\Im Z_{aH,B+bH}(u)=0
, so
H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)=\beta_{0}H^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)
. Then
\Re Z_{aH,B+bH}(u)=0
if and only if

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((aH)^{2}-(B+bH)^{2}\right)\mathrm{ch}_{0}+B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}+bH\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)-\mathrm{ch}_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}-\frac{(B+bH)^{2}}{H^{2}}+2b\beta_{0}\right)H^{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}(u)+B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(u)-\mathrm{ch}_{2}(u).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}-\frac{(B+bH)^{2}}{H^{2}}+2b\beta_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}+2b\left(\beta_{0}-\frac{B\mathbin{.}H}{H^{2}}\right)-b^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(2\alpha-b^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}+b^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha.

Therefore,
Z_{aH,B+bH}
and
Z_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}
are the same up to the action of
\GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{R})
. Moreover, by
Theorem

5.19
,
\sigma_{aH,B+bH}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
. Together with
Theorem

5.5
, it follows that
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}=g\cdot\sigma_{aH,B+bH}\in\mathrm{Stab}(X)
for some
g\in\widetilde{\mathrm{GL}}^{+}_{2}(\mathbf{R})
. Then, by definition,
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
. It remains to show this gives rise to a continuous family. By
Proposition

5.13
and
Proposition

5.15
,

\Pi\colon\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{U},\quad\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta}\mapsto(H,B,\alpha,\beta).

is an injective local homeomorphism. Let
V\coloneqq\left\{(H,B,\alpha,\beta):\alpha>\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\beta-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}\right]\right\}
. The restriction
\Pi|_{\Pi^{-1}(V)}
is still an injective local homeomorphism. Moreover, By the arguments above,
\Pi|_{\Pi^{-1}(V)}
is surjective, hence it is continuous.
∎

Lemma 5.23
.

Let
X
be a surface. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
. There exists rational classes
(H^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)
such that, for
a\geq 1
,
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
is negative definite on
\Ker Z_{aH,B}\otimes\mathbf{R}
. In particular,
\Delta_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}^{C_{H}^{\prime}}
gives the support property for
\sigma_{aH,B}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.19
, we have
\sigma_{aH,B}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
for
a\geq 1
, and nearby to
(H,B)
there exist rational classes
(H^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)
such that
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
gives the support property for
\sigma_{H,B}\in\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
. In particular,
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
is negative definite on
\mathrm{K}_{1}\coloneqq\Ker Z_{H,B}\otimes\mathbf{R}
. By Proposition
5.1
, it is enough to show
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
is negative definite on
K_{a}\coloneqq\Ker Z_{aH,B}\otimes\mathbf{R}
for
a\geq 1
.

Recall that
u=(\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(u),\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(u),\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(u))\in\mathrm{K}_{a}
if and only if

a^{2}\frac{H^{2}}{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(u)=\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(u),\quad H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(u)=0.

Let
\Psi_{a}\colon\mathrm{K}_{1}\rightarrow\mathrm{K}_{a}
be the isomorphism of sub-vector spaces of
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
given by

\Psi_{a}\colon v=(\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v),\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(v),\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(v))\mapsto\left(\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v),\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(v),\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(v)+(a^{2}-1)\frac{H^{2}}{2}\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v)\right).

Let
u\in\mathrm{K}_{a}
. Then
u=\Psi_{a}(v)
for some
v\in\mathrm{K}_{1}
. Clearly
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(0)=0
, so we may assume
u\neq 0
. Hence
v\neq 0
, and it is enough to show that
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(\Psi_{a}(v))<0
. Recall that
\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B^{\prime}}=\mathrm{ch}_{1}-B^{\prime}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{0}
, hence
\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B^{\prime}}(\Psi_{a}(v))=\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B^{\prime}}(v)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(\Psi_{a}(v))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B}(v))^{2}-2\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v)\mathrm{ch}_{2}^{B}(v)-2(a^{2}-1)\frac{H^{2}}{2}(\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v))^{2}+C_{H^{\prime}}(H^{\prime}\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{B^{\prime}}(v))^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(v)-2(a^{2}-1)\frac{H^{2}}{2}(\mathrm{ch}_{0}^{B}(v))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(v).

Since
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
is negative definite on
\mathrm{K}_{1}

\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}(\Psi_{a}(v))<0

∎

Lemma 5.25
.

Let
X

\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}

H

Proposition 5.28

X
be a surface. Let
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)

\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}

\alpha_{0}>\Phi_{X,H,B}(\beta_{0})

5.25

\varepsilon>0
such that

(1)

Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}([F])\geq 0
for every
H
-semistable torsion-free sheaf
F

(2)

Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}([T])\geq 0
for every torsion sheaf T, and

(3)

Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}

K_{\alpha_{0}}\coloneqq\Ker Z_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}\subseteq\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}

Proof.

(1) follows immediately for any
\varepsilon>0
from Lemma
5.25
and Lemma
5.27
. For (2), let
C_{H}
be the constant from Lemma
5.17
. Choose
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
\varepsilon_{1}<\frac{\delta^{-1}}{C_{H}}

T
be a torsion sheaf, then

\displaystyle Q^{\delta,\varepsilon_{1}}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}([T])

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}(H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T]))^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T])^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\left(\frac{\delta^{-1}}{\varepsilon_{1}}(H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T]))^{2}+\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T])^{2}\right)

\displaystyle>\varepsilon_{1}\left(C_{H}(H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T]))^{2}+\mathrm{ch}_{1}([T])^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

For (3), fix a norm on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)
and let
U
denote the set of unit vectors in
K_{\alpha_{0}}
with respect to this norm. It will be enough to show there exists
\varepsilon_{2}>0
such that
Q^{\delta,\varepsilon_{2}}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}|_{U}<0

Let
A\coloneqq\{u\in U\mid Q_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\delta}=0\}
. For any

\mathrm{ch}_{0}(a)=0
. The condition that

B\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(a)=\mathrm{ch}_{2}(a)\quad\text{ and }\quad H\mathbin{.}\mathrm{ch}_{1}(a)=0.

H
is ample, so
\mathrm{ch}_{1}(a)^{2}\leq 0

\mathrm{ch}_{1}^{2}(a)=0

\mathrm{ch}_{1}(a)=0

{\left.\kern-1.2ptQ_{BG}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{A}}([E])=\mathrm{ch}_{1}([E])^{2}<0.

\varepsilon_{2}>0

Hence

Q_{BG}([E])=Q_{BG}([\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)])-2(-\mathrm{ch}_{0}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)))\length(\mathcal{H}^{0}(E))\geq Q_{BG}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)).

The same argument applies to
Q_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\delta}
. Hence
Q([E])\geq Q([\mathcal{H}^{-1}(E)])
. The result follows by Proposition
5.28
(1).
∎

Lemma 5.32
.

Let
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})\in\mathrm{Stab}(X)
with support property given by a quadratic form
Q
on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
. Suppose
E\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
is strictly
\sigma
-semistable and satisfies
Q(E)\neq 0
. Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
be the Jordan–Hölder(JH) factors of
E
. Then
Q(A_{i})<Q(E)
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that
Q(A_{1})<Q(E)
. Since
E
is
\sigma
-semistable,
E\in\mathcal{P}(\phi)
for some
\phi\in\mathbf{R}
. By definition,
A_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\phi)
, and hence
E/A_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\phi)
also. Therefore, by the support property,
Q(A_{1})\geq 0
and
Q(E/A_{1})\geq 0
. Moreover, since
A_{1}
and
E/A_{1}
have the same phase, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}
such that
Z(A_{1})-\lambda Z(E/A_{1})=0
. Hence
[A_{1}]-\lambda[E/A_{1}]\in\Ker Z\otimes\mathbf{R}
.

Let
Q
also denote the associated symmetric bilinear form. Now assume
[A_{1}]-\lambda[E/A_{1}]\neq 0
in
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
. By the support property,
Q
is negative definite on
\mathrm{K_{num}}(X)\otimes\mathbf{R}
, hence

0>Q([A_{1}]-\lambda[E/A_{1}])=Q(A_{1})-2\lambda Q(A_{1},E/A_{1})+\lambda^{2}Q(E/A_{1}).

Moreover,
\lambda>0
and
Q(A_{1}),Q(E/A_{1})\geq 0
. It follows that
Q(A_{1},E/A_{1})>0
. Therefore,

Q(E)=Q(A_{1})+Q(E/A_{1})+2Q(A_{1},E/A_{1})>Q(A_{1}).

Otherwise, if
[A_{1}]=\lambda[E/A_{1}]
, then
\mu\coloneqq 1/\lambda>0
and

Q(E)=Q(A_{1})+\mu(\mu+2)Q(A_{1}).

If
Q(A_{1})=0
, then
Q(E)=0
which is a contradiction. Hence
Q(A_{1})>0
, so
Q(E)>Q(A_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 5.33

(
[
Bay19
, Lemma 6.1]
)
.

Let
\sigma=(Z,\mathcal{P})\in\mathrm{Stab}(X)
, and let
Q
be a quadratic form which is negative definite on
\Ker Z\otimes\mathbf{R}
. Suppose
E\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
is strictly
\sigma
-semistable and let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
be the Jordan–Hölder factors of
E
. If
Q(E)<0
, then for some
1\leq j\leq m
,
Q(A_{j})<0
.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
Q(A_{1}),Q(E/A_{1})\geq 0
. Let
Q
also denote the associated symmetric bilinear form. By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.32
, it follows that
Q(A,E/A_{1})>0
. Therefore,

Q(E)=Q(A_{1})+Q(E/A_{1})+2Q(A_{1},E/A_{1})>0,

which is a contradiction. Hence either
Q(A_{1})<0
and we are done, or
Q(E/A_{1})<0
. If
Q(E/A_{1})<0
, we can repeat the argument with
E/A_{1}
and
A_{2}
instead of
E
and
A_{1}
. There are finitely many Jordan–Hölder factors, so this process terminates. Therefore,
Q(A_{j})<0
for some
1\leq j\leq n
.

∎

Lemma 5.34
.

Let
X
be a surface. Fix classes
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
and let
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\in\mathbf{R}
such that
\alpha_{0}>\Phi_{X,H,B}(\beta_{0})
. Choose
\delta,\varepsilon>0
as in Proposition
5.28
. Fix
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbf{R}
such that
\alpha_{1}>\max\left\{\alpha_{0},\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}\right)^{2}-\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}\right]\right\}
. Assume
E\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
is
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha_{1},\beta_{0}}
-semistable. Then it follows that
Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}([E])\geq 0
. In particular,
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha_{1},\beta_{0}}
satisfies the support property with respect to
Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}
.

Proof.

To ease notation, let
Q\coloneqq Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}
. From
Proposition

5.21
, we know that for every
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}
,
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}
and
\sigma_{a_{\alpha}H,B+bH}
, have the same heart when
b=\beta_{0}-\frac{H\mathbin{.}B}{H^{2}}
and
a_{\alpha}=\sqrt{2\alpha-b^{2}+\frac{B^{2}}{H^{2}}}
.

Moreover, by Lemma
5.23
, there exists
(H^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{Q}}(X)
such that
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}
gives the support property for
\sigma_{aH,B+bH}
if
a\geq a_{\alpha_{1}}
. We may assume
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}\in\mathbf{Z}
, since it is true after rescaling by some integer. Furthermore, since
E
is
\sigma_{a_{\alpha_{1}}H,B+bH}
-semistable,
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([E])\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

If
E
is
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha,\beta_{0}}
-stable for
\alpha\gg 0
, then by definition of
a_{\alpha}
,
E
is
\sigma_{aH,B}
-stable for
a\gg 0
. It then follows by
Proposition

5.31
that
Q([E])\geq 0
. Otherwise, there exists some
\alpha_{2}\geq\alpha_{1}
such that
E
is strictly
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha_{2},\beta_{0}}
-semistable. Let
A_{1},\ldots A_{m}
denote the Jordan–Hölder factors of
E
. Then by Lemma
5.32
,
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([A_{i}])<\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([E])
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Each
A_{i}
is
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha_{2},\beta_{0}}
-stable, so
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([A_{i}])\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Assume for a contradiction that
Q([E])<0
. From Lemma
5.33
,
Q([A_{j}])<0
for some
1\leq j\leq m
. Let
E_{2}\coloneqq A_{j}
. We can now repeat this process for
E_{2}
in place of
E_{1}\coloneqq E
, and so on. This gives a sequence
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},\ldots,E_{k},\ldots
and
\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{3}\ldots<\alpha_{k}\ldots
such that
E_{k}\in\mathrm{D^{b}}(X)
is
\sigma_{H,B,\alpha_{k},\beta_{0}}
-semistable,
Q(E_{k})<0
, and
0\leq\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([E_{k+1}])<\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([E_{k}])
for all
k\geq 1
. But
\Delta^{C_{H^{\prime}}}_{H^{\prime},B^{\prime}}([E_{k}])\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
for all
k
, so no such sequence can exist. This gives a contradiction.

Finally, by Lemma
5.29
,
Q^{\delta,\varepsilon}_{H,B,\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}}
is negative definite on
\Ker Z_{H,B,\alpha_{1},\beta_{0}}\otimes\mathbf{R}
.
∎

We are finally ready to apply Corollary
5.2
.

5.21

Remark 5.37
.

There are precisely two types of walls of the geometric chamber for K3 surfaces and rational surfaces. They either correspond to walls of the nef cone (see
[
TX22
, Lemma 7.2]
for a construction) or to discontinuities of the Le Potier function. For K3 surfaces, the second case comes from the existence of spherical bundles which is explained in
[
Yos09
, Proposition 2.7]
. For rational surfaces, the discontinuities correspond to exceptional bundles. This is explained for
\Tot(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{2}}(-3))
in
[
BM11
, §5]
, and the arguments generalise to any rational surface.

It seems reasonable to expect this to hold for all surfaces. The description of the geometric chamber given by Theorem
5.10
also supports this. Indeed, a wall where
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is destabilised corresponds locally to the boundary of
\mathcal{U}
being linear. This boundary is exactly where

(1)

H
becomes nef and not ample. We expect that this only gives rise to walls in the following cases:

•

H
is big and nef. Then
H
induces a contraction of rational curves. This can be used to construct non-geometric stability conditions
[
TX22
, Lemma 7.2]
.

•

H
is nef and induces a contraction to a curve whose fibres are rational curves. In this case, we expect a wall. For example, let
f\colon S\rightarrow C
be a
\mathbf{P}^{1}
-bundle over a curve. We expect the existence of stability conditions on
S
such that all skyscraper sheaves are strictly semistable, and they are destabilised by

\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(x)}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{x}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(x)}(-1)[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{f^{-1}(x)}[1].

(2)

If
\Phi_{X,H,B}
is discontinuous at
\beta
, then
\mathrm{Stab}_{N}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
locally has a linear boundary. We expect this to give rise to non-geometric stability conditions.

(3)

\alpha=\Phi_{X,H,B}(\beta)
. We expect no boundary in this case.

Corollary 5.38
.

Let
X
be a surface. If
\Phi_{X,H,B}
has no discontinuities and no linear pieces for any
(H,B)\in\mathrm{Amp}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)\times\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
, then any wall of
\mathrm{Stab}^{\mathrm{Geo}}(X)
where
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is destabilised corresponds to a class
H^{\prime}\in\mathrm{NS}_{\mathbf{R}}(X)
which is nef and not ample.

The Second-Generation Shifted Boundary Method and Its Numerical Analysis
Nabil M. Atallah, Claudio Canuto, Guglielmo Scovazzi

Abstract
Recently, the Shifted Boundary Method (SBM) was proposed within the class of unfitted (or immersed, or embedded) finite element methods. By reformulating the original boundary value problem over a surrogate (approximate) computational domain, the SBM avoids integration over cut cells and the associated problematic issues regarding numerical stability and matrix conditioning. Accuracy is maintained by modifying the original boundary conditions using Taylor expansions. Hence the name of the method, that shifts the location and values of the boundary conditions. In this article, we present enhanced variational SBM formulations for the Poisson and Stokes problems with improved flexibility and robustness. These simplified variational forms allow to relax some of the assumptions required by the mathematical proofs of stability and convergence of earlier implementations. First, we show that these new SBM implementations can be proved asymptotically stable and convergent even without the rather restrictive assumption that the inner product between the normals to the true and surrogate boundaries is positive. Second, we show that it is not necessary to introduce a stabilization term involving the tangential derivatives of the solution at Dirichlet boundaries, therefore avoiding the calibration of an additional stabilization parameter. Finally, we prove enhanced L 2 L^{2} -estimates without the cumbersome assumption - of earlier proofs - that the surrogate domain is convex. Instead we rely on a conventional assumption that the boundary of the true domain is smooth, which can also be replaced by requiring convexity of the true domain. The aforementioned improvements open the way to a more general and efficient implementation of the Shifted Boundary Method, particularly in complex three-dimensional geometries. We complement these theoretical developments with numerical experiments in two and three dimensions.

Introduction

In this article, we provide improved proofs for well-posedness, numerical stability and convergence of the
shifted boundary method
(SBM) for the Poisson and Stokes problems under a simplified set of assumptions which makes the SBM more generally applicable in the simulation of practical engineering problems of very complex geometry.

We briefly recall the scope and motivation for the SBM, which falls in the broader category of unfitted (or embedded) finite element methods
boffi2003finite
,
burman2010ghost
,
hansbo2002unfitted
,
hollig2003finite
,
hollig2001weighted
,
ruberg2012subdivision
,
ruberg2014fixed
,
schott2015face
,
burman2018cut
,
burman2019dirichlet
,
burman2017cut
,
burman2010fictitious
,
burman2012fictitious
,
burman2014unfitted
,
burman2018shape
,
massing2015nitsche
,
burman2015cutfem
,
kamensky2017immersogeometric
,
xu2016tetrahedral
,
lozinski2016new
.
Many of these methods require the geometric construction of the partial elements cut by the embedded boundary, which can be both algorithmically complicated and computationally intensive, due to data structures that are considerably more complex with respect to corresponding fitted finite element methods.
Furthermore, integrating the variational forms on the characteristically irregular cut cells may also be difficult and advanced quadrature formulas might need to be employed
parvizian2007finite
,
duster2008finite
. Accordingly, it is typical for unfitted methods that a non-negligible portion of the overall wall-clock time for a simulation is spent handling the embedded boundary, when complex geometries are considered.

The SBM approach is instead aimed at avoiding integration over cut cells and all the problematic issues just mentioned, and belongs to the more specific class of approximate domain methods
bramble1972projection
,
bramble1996finite
,
bramble1994robust
,
cockburn2012solving
,
cockburn2014priori
,
cockburn2010boundary
,
bertoluzza2005fat
,
bertoluzza2011analysis
,
glowinski1994fictitious
for some examples).
The SBM is built for minimal computational complexity, in that the location where boundary conditions are applied is
shifted
from the true to an approximate (surrogate) boundary, and, at the same time, modified (
shifted
) boundary conditions are applied in order to avoid a reduction in the convergence rates of the overall formulation. In fact, if the boundary conditions associated to the true domain are not appropriately modified on the surrogate domain, only first-order convergence is to be expected. The shifted boundary conditions are appropriately modified by means of Taylor expansions and are applied weakly, using a Nitsche strategy. This process yields a method which is simple, robust, accurate and efficient.

The shifted boundary method was proposed in
main2018shifted0
for the Poisson and Stokes flow problems and generalized in
main2018shifted
to the advection-diffusion and Navier-Stokes equations, and in
song2018shifted
to hyperbolic conservation laws. In
main2018shifted0
and
main2018shifted
, an analysis of the stability and accuracy of the SBM for the Poisson and advection-diffusion operators was also included, respectively. More recently, the authors of
atallah2020analysis
analyzed the stability and accuracy of the SBM for the Stokes flow equations, in an endeavor to complete the numerical analysis of the method for the fundamental differential operators that combine in the Navier-Stokes equations and many other linear and nonlinear partial differential equations of importance in engineering and physical sciences.

In the present work, we propose
second generation
shifted boundary formulations for the Poisson and Stokes flow problems, and we include their mathematical analysis of stability and accuracy.
These enhanced formulations are obtained by 1) discarding the assumption that the inner product between the normals to the true and surrogate boundaries must be positive
main2018shifted0
and 2) removing a boundary stabilization term constructed with tangential derivatives, which was initially considered necessary for the numerical stability of the method
main2018shifted0
.
Particularly, the assumption that the inner product between the normals to the true and surrogate boundaries must be positive is typically not verified in three dimensional computations involving complex geometries, and the ability to avoid such restriction is paramount for the application of the SBM framework to general engineering problems.
Furthermore, the new proofs of stability and accuracy proposed in this work provide a clear explanation of why the SBM is very effective in the robust treatment of complex geometry problems.

In addition, we provide enhanced proofs for the convergence rates in the
L^{2}
-norm, using the conventional requirement that the boundary of the true domain is smooth, as opposed to the restrictive assumption that the surrogate domain is convex
main2018shifted0
,
main2018shifted
,
atallah2020analysis
. We also note that the assumption of smoothness of the true boundary can be replaced by an assumption of convexity of the true domain.

Finally, in the case of the specific stabilized variational formulation utilized to treat the Stokes operator, we also discard one of the stabilization terms associated with the incompressibility condition. This modification is however less relevant for those practitioners who are interested in pairing the SBM with LBB-stable finite elements.

3
; the analysis of the SBM variational formulation of the Stokes problem is presented in Section
4
; extensive numerical tests are presented in Section
5
and Section
6
; and finally, conclusions are summarized in Section
7
.

2.2
General strategy

The SBM introduced in
main2018shifted0
discretizes the governing equations in
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
rather than
\Omega
. Consequently, the challenge would be to consistently enforce the boundary conditions on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
. To this end, the SBM resorts to a first-order Taylor expansion of the concerned variable at the surrogate boundary in order to
shift
the boundary condition from
\Gamma
to
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
.

To illustrate, consider a scalar field
u
to be the exact solution to a partial differential equation in
\Omega
with a trace
g
on
\Gamma
. Assuming
u
is sufficiently smooth in the strip between
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
and
\Gamma
so as to admit a first-order Taylor expansion pointwise, we can write

\displaystyle u({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})+(\nabla u\cdot\boldsymbol{d})({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})+(R(u,\boldsymbol{d}))({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}})=g(\boldsymbol{x})\;,\quad\quad\mbox{ on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;.

(8)

where the remainder
R(u,\boldsymbol{d})
satisfies

\displaystyle|\,R(u,\boldsymbol{d})\,|=o(\|\,\boldsymbol{d}\,\|)\qquad\text{as }\quad\|\,\boldsymbol{d}\,\|\to 0\,.

Introducing the function
\bar{g}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}}):=g(\boldsymbol{M}_{h}({\tilde{\boldsymbol{x}}}))
on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
, we see that the trace of
u
on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
satisfies

u+\nabla u\cdot\boldsymbol{d}-\bar{g}+R(u,\boldsymbol{d})\ =\ S_{h}u-\bar{g}+R_{h}u=0\,,

(9)

where we have introduced the boundary operator

S_{h}v:=v+\nabla v\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\qquad\text{on \ }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}

(10)

and
R_{h}u
is a short-hand notation for the Taylor expansion remainder
R(u,\boldsymbol{d})
.
Neglecting the higher-order term (with respect to
\|\,\boldsymbol{d}\,\|
) in (
9
), we obtain the final expression of the
shifted
boundary condition

S_{h}u=\bar{g}\,,\quad\mbox{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;,

(11)

which will be weakly enforced on the discretization
u_{h}
of
u
that we are going to introduce.
Similarly, for a vector field
\boldsymbol{u}
, we deduce that its trace
\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}
on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
satisfies

\boldsymbol{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}=\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}\,,

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{v}:=\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla\boldsymbol{v}\,\boldsymbol{d}
on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{R}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}
is the Taylor expansion remainder of
\boldsymbol{u}
on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
.
Again, neglecting the higher-order term in (
12
), we obtain the
shifted
vector boundary condition

\boldsymbol{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{u}=\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}\,,\quad\mbox{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;.

(13)

3.3
Well-posedness and stability

The first step in our analysis of the SBM is to prove that,
for sufficiently fine grids
,
a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,w_{h})
is uniformly coercive. This will immediately imply the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the discrete SBM problem.
Later on, this coercivity result will be used to prove optimal error convergence in the natural norm.

Theorem 2

(Coercivity)
.

Consider the bilinear form
a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,w_{h})

Proof.

By substitution, we have

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,u_{h})

\displaystyle=\;\|\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-2\langle S_{h}u_{h}\,,\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\nabla u_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\,,\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\alpha\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(20)

Applying Assumption

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,u_{h})

\displaystyle\geq\;(1-\epsilon_{1}\,C_{I})\,\|\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\|\,\|\boldsymbol{d}\,\|^{1/2}\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,\|\,\boldsymbol{d}\,\|^{1/2}\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\geq}\;+\left(\alpha-\epsilon^{-1}_{1}\,\right)\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\geq\;(1-\epsilon_{1}\,C_{I})\,\|\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\|\,\|\,\boldsymbol{d}\,\|^{1/2}\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\left(\alpha-\epsilon^{-1}_{1}\right)\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\geq\;(1-\epsilon_{1}\,C_{I}-c_{d}\,\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{\zeta}\,C_{I})\,\|\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\left(\alpha-\epsilon^{-1}_{1}\right)\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(21)

Taking
\epsilon_{1}=(2\,C_{I})^{-1}
and considering sufficiently fine grids so that
c_{d}\,\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leq(4\,C_{I})^{-1}
imply

\displaystyle a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,u_{h})

\displaystyle\geq\;4^{-1}\,\|\,\nabla u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\left(\alpha-2C_{I}\right)\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u_{h}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(22)

Enforcing
\alpha>2\,C_{I}
yields the coercivity statement (
18
) with
C_{a}=\;\min\left(1/4\,,\,\alpha-2C_{I}\right)\;.

∎

Remark 8
.

In the case of linear polynomials, the discretization scheme (
17
) bears some similarities to the one proposed in
bramble1972projection
, a reference that the authors of
main2018shifted0
,
main2018shifted
were unaware of.
In the present work, however, we admit a much greater freedom in the choice of the mapping
\boldsymbol{M}_{h}:\tilde{\Omega}_{h}\to\Omega
, that is, our vector
\boldsymbol{d}
does not need to be aligned with
\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}
as in
bramble1972projection
and can be chosen in such a way to account for general domains whose boundaries have a finite number of corners and/or edges
TheoreticalPoissonAtallahCanutoScovazzi2020
.
Although this difference seems mild at first sight, in reality it makes the work in
bramble1972projection
of not easy applicability in practical engineering problems, and this might explain why a work dating almost half a century ago has gone relatively unnoticed in the community.
On the other hand, our Assumption
1
is comparable to the general assumption made in
bramble1972projection
on the distance between the surrogate and physical boundaries; correspondingly, our coercivity result, Theorem
2
above, is similar to Lemma 6 in
bramble1972projection
.

Interestingly, much of the emphasis in
bramble1972projection
and later works
bramble1996finite
,
bramble1994robust
,
burman2018cut
was on perturbations of body-fitted grids, for which
\|\boldsymbol{d}\|\sim h^{2}
, despite many of the results of
bramble1972projection
apply in a broader sense. From our perspective, the case
\|\boldsymbol{d}\|\sim h^{1+\zeta}
- for
\zeta
positive and arbitrarily small - is significantly more interesting in computational engineering applications.

3.4
Consistency and convergence analysis

From now on, we pose the following regularity assumption on the exact solution
u
of our Dirichlet problem (see
TheoreticalPoissonAtallahCanutoScovazzi2020
):

Assumption 2
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
u_{D}\in H^{3/2}(\Gamma)
.

As a consequence, Theorem
1
guarantees
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with the estimate (
15
). This assumption allows us to keep technicalities at a minimum in the subsequent consistency and convergence analysis. However, we mention that it can be weakened to include e.g. domains with a finite number of corners and/or edges (see
TheoreticalPoissonAtallahCanutoScovazzi2020
), at the expense of more cumbersome mathematical arguments.

The proof of convergence of the SBM for Problem (
14
) using the natural norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{a}
, relies on Strang’s Second Lemma.
With this goal, we introduce the infinite dimensional space

\displaystyle V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})

\displaystyle=V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})+H^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\;\subset H^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h})\,\;,

(23)

an extension of the finite dimensional space
V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
that contains the exact solution
u
, that is
u\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})
.
Here
H^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h})=\prod_{T\in\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}H^{2}(T)
with ‘broken’ norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h},\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}\|\cdot\|_{2,T}
.
It is easily checked that the form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is well-defined also on the space
V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})\times V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
. We associate to
V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})
the norm

\displaystyle\|\,v\,\|^{2}_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}

\displaystyle=\|\,v\,\|^{2}_{a}+|\,h\,w\,|^{2}_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h},\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}\,,

(24)

and we note that if
v\in V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
, then
\|\,v\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}=\|\,v\,\|_{a}
.
At this point, we are ready to state

Lemma 1

(Strang’s Second Lemma)
.

If
u_{h}\in V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
is the solution of (
17
), then

\displaystyle\|\,u-u_{h}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+C^{-1}_{a}\,\|\,a_{h}\,\|_{V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\times V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\right)\,E_{a,h}(u)+C^{-1}_{a}\,E_{c,h}(u)\;.

(25a)

where

E_{a,h}(u)=\inf_{w_{h}\in V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\|\,u-w_{h}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}

(25b)

is the approximation error and

E_{c,h}(u)=\sup_{w_{h}\in V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}\frac{|\,l_{h}(w_{h})-a_{h}(u\,,\,w_{h})\,|}{\|\,w_{h}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}}

(25c)

is the consistency error.

Proof.

The proof is classic, see, e.g.,
AErn:2004a
for details.
∎

From Theorem
2
, we already have that
C_{a}
is a constant independent of the mesh size. Hence, to estimate the discretization error in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}
we need to prove that
\|\,a_{h}\,\|_{V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\times V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})}
is bounded from above and to estimate the approximation and consistency errors in terms of the mesh size
h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}
.

Proposition 1

(Boundedness)
.

There exists a constant
C_{\cal A}>0
, independent of the mesh size, such that

\displaystyle a_{h}(u,w)\leq C_{\cal A}\,\|\,u\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;\|\,w\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\qquad\forall u,w\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})\;.

(26)

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Theorem
13
, we obtain:

\displaystyle a_{h}(u\,,\,w)

\displaystyle=\;(\nabla u\,,\,\nabla w)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\nabla u\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,S_{h}w\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle S_{h}u\,,\,\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\nabla u\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla w\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,S_{h}u\,,\,S_{h}w\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\|\,\nabla u\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,\nabla w\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla u\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}w\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla u\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla w\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\alpha\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}u\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}w\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{\cal A}\,\|\,u\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;\|\,w\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;,

(27)

with
C_{\cal A}=1+2C_{I}+C^{2}_{I}+\alpha
.
∎

The previous result implies that

\displaystyle\|\,a_{h}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})\times V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}=\sup_{u\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;\sup_{w_{h}\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;\frac{a_{h}(u\,,\,w)}{\|\,u\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\;\|\,w\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}}\leq C_{\cal A}\;.

(28)

Proposition 2

(Approximability)
.

There exists a constant
C_{APP}>0
, independent of the mesh size, such that

E_{a,h}(u)\leq C_{APP}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\qquad\forall u\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})\,.

(29)

Proof.

Let
w_{h}=\mathscr{I}_{h}u
in (
25b
), where
\mathscr{I}_{h}u
is the standard piecewise-linear Lagrange interpolant of
u
on the triangulation
\tilde{\cal T}_{h}
. Consequently, the goal is to estimate

\displaystyle\|\,u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}

\displaystyle=\;\|\,\nabla(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+|\,h\,(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h},\tilde{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\|\,\nabla(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

(30)

We begin by stating a classical interpolation result

\displaystyle\|\,h^{-1}\,(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+\|\,\nabla(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leq C_{1}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;,

(31)

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant independent of the mesh size. Applying Theorem
13
and Assumption
1
,

\displaystyle\|\,h^{-1/2}\,S_{h}(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;\|\,h^{-1/2}\,(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{\zeta}\,\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{I}\left(\,\|\,h^{-1}\,(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}+(1+\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{\zeta})\,\|\,\nabla(u-\mathscr{I}_{h}u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}\;\qquad\ \ \left.+h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{\zeta}\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

(32)

Replacing (
3.4
) in (
3.4
) gives (
29
) with

.
∎

In order to bound the consistency error

3.5
L^{2}
-error estimate by a duality argument

In this section, we provide an estimate of the

-norm of the discretization error

based on the ideas recently proposed in
TheoreticalPoissonAtallahCanutoScovazzi2020
. This is a considerable improvement to the same work given in
main2018shifted0
and
atallah2020analysis
as we avoid the convexity assumption of the surrogate domain
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
. We start with the following two preliminary results:

Lemma 3
.

It holds

\displaystyle a_{h}(w\,,\,v)-a_{h}(v\,,\,w)=\langle\nabla w\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla v\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\nabla v\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla w\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\qquad\forall v,w\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})\,.

(37)

Proof.

By substitution in (
17
).
∎

Lemma
3
quantifies the symmetry discrepancy in the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot\,,\,\cdot)
.
The following lemma quantifies the gap in Galerkin orthogonality.

Lemma 4
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
14
), and
u_{h}
the numerical solution of (
3.2
). It holds

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h}\,,\,v_{h})=\langle R_{h}u\,,\,\nabla v_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,R_{h}u\,,\,S_{h}v_{h}\rangle_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})\;.

(38)

Proof.

From (
17
), and following the same steps as in Proposition
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(u-u_{h}\,,\,v_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u\,,\,v_{h})-a_{h}(u_{h}\,,\,v_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(u\,,\,v_{h})-l_{h}(v_{h})

\displaystyle=-\langle S_{h}u-\bar{u}_{D}\,,\,\nabla v_{h}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,(S_{h}u-\bar{u}_{D})\,,\,S_{h}v_{h}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,.

(39)

Finally, from (
9
) with
\bar{g}=\bar{u}_{D}
, we have
S_{h}u-\bar{u}_{D}=-R_{h}u
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4

(Enhanced
L^{2}
-error estimate)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3
to hold. Then, the numerical solution
u_{h}
of (
3.2
) satisfies the following error estimate:

\displaystyle\|\,u-u_{h}\,\|_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leq

\displaystyle\;C\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{3/2}\,l(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})^{1/2}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla u)\,\|_{0,\Omega}\;,

(40)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of mesh size.

Proof.

Given
z\in L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})
, let
\bar{z}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be its extension by
0
outside
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
and let
\psi
be the solution to the following homogeneous Dirichlet problem on
\Omega
:

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=\;\bar{z}\,\ \ \!\qquad\qquad\,\mbox{in }\Omega\;,

(41a)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\;0\,\ \ \!\qquad\qquad\mbox{on }\Gamma\;.

(41b)

Recalling Theorem
1
, the stated assumptions in addition to the fact that
\bar{z}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
imply the regularity result
\psi\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, with the following bound

\displaystyle\|\,\psi\,\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leq\;\|\,\psi\,\|_{2,\Omega}\leq\;Q\,\|\,\bar{z}\,\|_{0,\Omega}=\;Q\,\|\,z\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;,

(42)

where
Q>0
is a non-dimensional constant independent of
\bar{z}
and the mesh size.
The same arguments that led to (
9
) show that
\psi
satisfies

\displaystyle S_{h}\psi+R_{h}\psi=0\qquad\mbox{on }\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}\;.

(43)

We now apply Proposition 3 in
TheoreticalPoissonAtallahCanutoScovazzi2020
, which together with (
42
) yields the bound

\displaystyle\|\,h^{-1/2}\,R_{h}\psi\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\leq C_{DR}\,h_{\Gamma}\,|\,\psi\,|_{2,\Omega\setminus\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\leq C_{DR}\,Q\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,z\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,,

(44)

where
C_{DR}
is a positive constant independent of the mesh size.

Next, consider any
q\in V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})
and note that
\psi
also satisfies the following variational statement:

\displaystyle(z\,,\,q)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}=\;-(\Delta\psi\,,\,q)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}=(\nabla\psi\,,\,\nabla q)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\nabla\psi\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,q\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(45)

Adding residual terms on
\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}
that vanish by definition if applied to the exact solution, we have:

\displaystyle(z\,,\,q)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\;(\nabla\psi\,,\,\nabla q)_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}-\langle\nabla\psi\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,q\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle S_{h}\psi+R_{h}\psi\,,\,\nabla q\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,(S_{h}\psi+R_{h}\psi)\,,\,S_{h}q\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\;a_{h}(\psi\,,\,q)-\langle R_{h}\psi\,,\,\nabla q\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,R_{h}\psi\,,\,S_{h}q\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\;a_{h}(q\,,\,\psi)+\langle\nabla\psi\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla q\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\nabla q\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla\psi\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle R_{h}\psi\,,\,\nabla q\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,R_{h}\psi\,,\,S_{h}q\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;,

(46)

where in the last equality we used Lemma
3
. Picking
q=z=e_{u}:=u-u_{h}
and using Lemma
4
with
v_{h}=\psi_{I}:=\mathscr{I}_{h}\psi
,

\displaystyle\|\,e_{u}\,\|^{2}_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\;a_{h}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)+E_{sym}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)+E_{rem}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)

\displaystyle=\;a_{h}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi-\psi_{I})+E_{sym}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)+E_{rem}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)+E_{ort}(u\,,\,\psi_{I})\;,

(47a)

with

\displaystyle E_{sym}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\;\langle\nabla\psi\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla e_{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\nabla e_{u}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,,\,\nabla\psi\cdot\boldsymbol{d}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;,

(47b)

\displaystyle E_{rem}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\;-\langle R_{h}\psi\,,\,\nabla e_{u}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,R_{h}\psi\,,\,S_{h}e_{u}\rangle_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;,

(47c)

\displaystyle E_{ort}(u\,,\,\psi_{I})

\displaystyle:=\;\langle R_{h}u\,,\,\nabla\psi_{I}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}-\langle\alpha\,h^{-1}\,R_{h}u\,,\,S_{h}\psi_{I}\rangle_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\;.

(47d)

Next, we proceed to bound the four error terms on the right-hand side of (
47
).
Applying Proposition
1
and
Proposition
2
(with
\psi
in place of
u
), yields

\displaystyle a_{h}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi-\psi_{I})

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{\cal A}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\,\|\,\psi-\psi_{I}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{\cal A}\,C_{APP}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\,\|\,\nabla(\nabla\psi)\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{3}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

(48a)

Recalling Assumption
1
, Lemma
2
, equation (
44
), Theorem
11
, and Theorem
13
,

\displaystyle E_{sym}(e_{u}\,,\,\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\;c_{d}\,\hat{h}_{\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}^{\zeta}\,\left(\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla e_{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla\psi\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}+\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla e_{u}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\,\|\,h^{1/2}\,\nabla\psi\cdot\boldsymbol{\nu}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Gamma}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\;c_{d}\,C\,C_{I}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{1/2}\,l(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})^{-3/2}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\,\|\,\psi\,\|_{2,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{4}\,h_{\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}^{1/2}\,l(\tilde{\Omega}_{h})^{1/2}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{V(\tilde{\Omega}_{h};{\tilde{{\mathscr{T}}}_{h}})}\,\|\,e_{u}\,\|_{0,\tilde{\Omega}_{h}}\;.

4
The SBM for the Stokes flow equations

The strong form of the Stokes flow equations with non-homogeneous Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions read

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p\boldsymbol{I})

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{f}\qquad\text{\ \ in \ }\Omega\;,

(50a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\;0\qquad\text{\ \ in \ }\Omega\;,

(50b)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{u}_{D}\qquad\text{on \ }\Gamma_{D}\;,

(50c)

\displaystyle(2\mu\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})-p\boldsymbol{I})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=\;\boldsymbol{t}_{N}\qquad\text{on \ }\Gamma_{N}\;,

(50d)

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})=1/2(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{T})
is the velocity strain tensor (i.e., the symmetric gradient of the velocity),
\mu>0
is the dynamic viscosity,
p
is the pressure,
\boldsymbol{f}
is a body force,
\boldsymbol{u}_{D}
is the value of the velocity on the Dirichlet boundary
\Gamma_{D}\not=\emptyset
and
\boldsymbol{t}_{N}
is the vector-valued normal stress on the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{N}
(where
\partial\Omega=\Gamma=\overline{\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}}
with
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
). The Stokes flow represents a prototype for the application of the SBM to systems of differential equations in mixed form.

4.3
Well-posedness and stability

The first step in our analysis is to prove that the bilinear form

is coercive, under suitable assumptions.
In a second step, we will establish that the bilinear form

satisfies a uniform inf-sup condition.
This will immediately imply the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the discrete SBM problem, and will be lately used to prove its convergence to the exact solution, with optimal error estimates in an appropriate natural norm.

7
Summary

We have provided an enhanced analysis of well-posedness and accuracy for the SBM in the case of the Poisson and Stokes operators. The key improvement with respect to previous work are the removal of an assumption about the angle between the normal to the surrogate boundary and the corresponding normal to the true boundary.
In addition, we have shown that no penalties on the tangential derivative of the Dirichlet boundary condition are required to prove stability and accuracy of the SBM. In addition, particular to the Stokes operator, incorporating an incompressibility constraint stabilization term was also deemed as unnecessary. Furthermore, in the enhanced
L^{2}
-error estimates, we removed the assumption of convexity of the surrogate domain
\tilde{\Omega}_{h}
, relying instead on a conventional assumption of smoothness of the true domain
\Omega
(which could be replaced by convexity of the true domain
\Omega
). These aspects are important in advancing the understanding and development of the SBM, in that they increase the flexibility and simplicity of the method, particularly when the geometry of the boundaries is highly complex.
We have also performed a number of tests to verify the findings of our theoretical analysis.

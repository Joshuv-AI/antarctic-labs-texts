Module Categories of the Generic Virasoro VOA and Quantum Groups
Shinji KOSHIDA

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the equivalence between two ribbon tensor categories. On the one hand, we consider the category of modules of the Virasoro vertex operator algebra with generic central charge (generic Virasoro VOA) generated by those simple modules lying in the first row of the Kac table. On the other hand, we take the category of finite-dimensional type I modules of the quantum group 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) \mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) with q q determined by the central charge. This is a continuation of our previous work in which we examined intertwining operators for the generic Virasoro VOA in detail. Our strategy to show the categorical equivalence is to take those results as input and directly compare the structures of tensor categories. Therefore, we are to execute the most elementary proof of categorical equivalence. We also study the category of C 1 C_{1} -cofinite modules of the generic Virasoro VOA. We show that it is ribbon equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional type I modules of 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) ⊗ 𝒰 q ~ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) \mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\otimes\mathcal{U}_{\tilde{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) , where q q and q ~ \tilde{q} are again related to the central charge.

Introduction

1.1
Background

Two-dimensional conformal field theories (CFT)
[
3
,
9
]
form one of the most developed classes of quantum field theories.
The infinite-dimensional symmetry of CFT allows one to identify the primary fields of the theory and the operator product expansion (OPE) of the primary fields in a closed form, sometimes leading to the exact solution.
Due to their integrability, CFTs have played important roles in string theory
[
23
]
, condensed matter physics
[
24
,
47
]
, and statistical physics
[
4
,
53
]
.

Vertex operator algebras (VOA)
[
18
,
46
]
and their representation theory give an algebraic counterpart of CFTs
[
26
]
.
According to the dictionary, a VOA gives the chiral algebra of a CFT, and the irreducible modules of the VOA correspond to the primary fields of the CFT.
Furthermore, the OPE of primary fields is translated into a tensor product of modules of the VOA.
Therefore, we could say that the study of a CFT partly comes down to the study of the category of modules of the corresponding VOA as a tensor category.

Quantum groups
[
10
]
are algebras whose module categories are clearly tensor categories.
There are several known examples of equivalence between module categories of VOAs and those of quantum groups as tensor categories.
Prominent examples include the Kazhdan–Lusztig-type duality
[
11
,
15
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
50
]
, and the duality between the triplet VOA and the small quantum
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

[
8
,
13
,
20
,
21
,
42
,
54
,
61
]
.
The present work is to give another example, namely, the Virasoro VOA with generic central charge and the quantum enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
with generic quantization parameter denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

1.2
Relation to previous work

This paper is a continuation of our previous work
[
43
]
.
This work might have a different flavor compared to other works concerning categorical equivalence.
To be clear about our point of view and to motivate our method,
it would be worthwhile giving a quick overview of the line of research.

Since the early stages of the research of CFT, hidden quantum group symmetry has been observed in several places
[
14
,
22
,
52
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
62
]
.
In
[
44
]
, the authors established one of the most concrete versions of the connection between a quantum group and CFT;
they gave maps from representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
to certain correlation functions of CFT.
Although their motivation was in application to the theory of Schramm–Loewner evolution
[
2
,
60
]
, their results were also of representation theoretical importance.
In fact, one consequence, among others, of their construction is that the asymptotic behavior of a correlation function when two points are close to each other is governed by the branching and associativity of the tensor product of representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

In our previous work
[
43
]
, we enhanced the results of
[
44
]
in the language of VOA.
Specifically, we identified the generic Virasoro VOA as the relevant VOA, determined the fusion rules among simple modules from the first row of the Kac table, and proved that the associativity of intertwining operators is governed by the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

1.3
Overview of the results

In the present work, we examine the category-theoretical implications of the results of the previous one
[
43
]
,
and add discussion on the braiding as well.
It is standard to parametrize the central charge
c\in\mathbb{C}
of the universal Virasoro VOAs
\mathbb{V}_{c}
(see Section
4
for the definition)
by another parameter
t\in\mathbb{C}
as

c=13-6\big(t+t^{-1}\big)
.
We focus on the case that
t\not\in\mathbb{Q}
is generic and call the VOA
\mathbb{V}_{c}
the generic Virasoro VOA.
The highest weight simple modules of
\mathbb{V}_{c}
are labeled by the conformal weight
h\in\mathbb{C}
.
The Kac table gives the following
table
of conformal weights of particular interest

\displaystyle h_{r,s}=\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}t-\frac{rs-1}{2}+\frac{s^{2}-1}{4}t^{-1},\qquad r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

For each
h_{r,s}
,
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the corresponding simple highest weight
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-module is denoted by
\mathbb{W}_{(r-1,s-1)}
.

In this paper, we follow
[
12
]
for the categorical terminology.
In particular, a
tensor category
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear rigid monoidal category
with a one-dimensional endomorphism space on the unit object.
When it is further equipped with a braiding and a ribbon structure, we call it a
ribbon tensor category
.
Note that we do not assume finiteness.

We will study what we call the first-row category.
It is the category of
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-modules generated by
\mathbb{W}_{(\ell,0)}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as an additive category,
and will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
.
It is not a priori clear that the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
can be equipped with the structure of a ribbon tensor category.
Thus, we first show that it is a ribbon tensor category.
Furthermore, we relate it to another ribbon tensor category of interest;
the category of finite-dimensional
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-modules of type I denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
.
Let us phrase our result in the following way.

Theorem 1.1
.

The first-row category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
is equipped with the structure of a ribbon tensor category.
The resulting ribbon tensor category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)

under the parameter matching
q={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t}
.

We also study the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}
of
C_{1}
-cofinite
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-modules.
It has been proven in
[
6
]
that the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}
is semi-simple and the simple objects are exhausted by
\mathbb{W}_{(k,l)}
,
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Furthermore, it is equipped with the structure of a ribbon tensor category,
and the resulting ribbon tensor category will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
.
In particular, the first-row category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
is a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
.
It also follows from the fusion rules in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
that, as a tensor category,
the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
is generated by the modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
and the modules in the first
column
of the Kac table,
which allows for the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

As a ribbon tensor category,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional type I modules of
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})\otimes\mathcal{U}_{\tilde{q}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
with the parameter matching
q={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t}
,
\tilde{q}={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t^{-1}}
.

1.4
Relation to other works

There are, in fact, various ways to prove our results.
First of all, in
[
6
]
,
they proved that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
is braided equivalent to a certain Kazhdan–Lusztig category of
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
along the way of proving the rigidity of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
.
Therefore, our results can be derived from it, too.

There are also approaches to the Kazhdan–Lusztig-type duality when the quantum group is at a root of unity.
In
[
21
]
, it has been established that the module category of the quantum
{\rm SL}(2)
at a root of unity and a certain module category of the Virasoro VOA in the logarithmic setting are equivalent.
The key tool therein is Ostrik’s functor
[
56
]
that characterizes the module category of the quantum
{\rm SL}(2)
.
We also mention
[
51
]
that studied the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-type structure in the module category of the Virasoro VOA at central charge
25
.
The recent work
[
7
,
45
]
is building a general, and perhaps conceptual, framework towards the Kazhdan–Lusztig duality.
These works address the case when the quantum group parameter is a root of unity, but the methods would extend to the generic case as well.

In spite that the above mentioned works would cover our results, we believe that it is still worthwhile recording our proof because our method is different;
the feature of our method is that it relies on an actual construction of intertwining operators.
Another recent example of a similar approach (to different problems) can be found in
[
55
]
.

.

.

-cofinite modules of the generic Virasoro VOA and prove Theorem

.

2
Quantum group

In this section, we give a brief overview of known facts about the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
and its representations.
We only consider the case where
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is not a root of unity and simply write
\mathcal{U}_{q}
for
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.
Further details about quantum groups can be found in
[
5
,
37
,
48
]
.

2.1
Quantum group
\bm{\mathcal{U}_{q}}

The algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}
is a unital associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by

K
,
K^{-1}
,
E
, and
F
subject to the relations

KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1,\qquad KE=q^{2}EK,\qquad KF=q^{-2}FK,\qquad EF-FE=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}.

We equip
\mathcal{U}_{q}
with the structure of a Hopf algebra in the following way.
The coproduct
\Delta\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}
is given by

\Delta(K):=K\otimes K,\qquad\Delta(E):=E\otimes 1+K\otimes E,\qquad\Delta(F):=F\otimes K^{-1}+1\otimes F.

The counit
\varepsilon\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathbb{C}
and the antipode
S\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathcal{U}_{q}
are defined as

\displaystyle\varepsilon(E)=\varepsilon(F)=0,\qquad\varepsilon(K)=1,\qquad S(E)=-K^{-1}E,

\displaystyle S(F)=-FK,\qquad S(K)=K^{-1}.

Remark 2.1
.

The coproduct used in our previous work
[
43
]
was the opposite:
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}=P\circ\Delta
, where

P\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}
;
A\otimes B\to B\otimes A

is the permutation of tensor components.
As we shall see, the structure constants of associativity (
6j
-symbols; see below) manifestly observed are associated with the opposite coproduct
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
, but we will come back to the original
\Delta
in the end.

2.2
Irreducible representations

For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
is an
(\ell+1)
-dimensional complex vector space with a basis
\big(u^{(\ell)}_{i}\big)_{i=0,1,\dots,\ell}
.
We define a representation homomorphism

\pi_{\ell}\colon\mathcal{U}_{q}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathsf{M}_{\ell})

by

\displaystyle\pi_{\ell}(K)u^{(\ell)}_{i}=q^{\ell-2i}u^{(\ell)}_{i},\qquad\pi_{\ell}(E)u^{(\ell)}_{i}=\llbracket i\rrbracket\llbracket\ell-i+1\rrbracket u^{(\ell)}_{i-1},\qquad\pi_{\ell}(F)u^{(\ell)}_{i}=u^{(\ell)}_{i+1}

for
i=0,1,\dots,\ell
.
Here, we understand
u^{(\ell)}_{i}=0
when
i<0
or
i>\ell
.
The
q
-integers are defined by

\llbracket n\rrbracket:=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is known that
(\pi_{\ell},\mathsf{M}_{\ell})
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are irreducible representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
.
On the other hand, a finite-dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
is isomorphic to either
(\pi_{\ell},\mathsf{M}_{\ell})
or
{(\pi_{\ell}\circ\chi,\mathsf{M}_{\ell})}
, where
\chi
is the automorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
given by
\chi(K)=-K
,
\chi(E)=-E
,
\chi(F)=F
.
In the sequel, we always assume that the vector space
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
is equipped with the representation homomorphism
\pi_{\ell}
and will not specify it. These irreducible representations
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are often referred to as type I.
Otherwise, the type I representations are characterized by that the eigenvalues of
K
are of the form
q^{n}
with some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Clearly,
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
is generated by
u^{(\ell)}_{0}
, which we call a highest weight vector of highest weight
\ell
.
Accordingly, we say that
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
is a highest weight irreducible representation of highest weight
\ell
.

2.3
Tensor product

For
\ell_{1}
,
\ell_{2}
, the tensor product
\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}
is equipped with the structure of a representation of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
by the coproduct
\Delta
.
To emphasize the dependence on the coproduct, we write
\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}
for the tensor product representation.
It decomposes into irreducible representations according to the Clebsch–Gordan rule

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\mathsf{M}_{\ell},

(2.1)

where we defined the set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid|\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}|\leq\ell\leq\ell_{1}+\ell_{2},\,\ell+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\equiv 0\bmod 2\}

of the highest weights appearing in the tensor product.

For each triple
(\ell,\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
such that
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, we fix an embedding homomorphism
\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\colon\mathsf{M}_{\ell}\to\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}
so that

\displaystyle\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\big(u^{(\ell)}_{0}\big)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}c_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(j)u^{(\ell_{1})}_{j}\otimes u^{(\ell_{2})}_{s-j},

\displaystyle c_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(j)=(-1)^{j}\frac{\llbracket\ell_{1}-j\rrbracket!}{\llbracket j\rrbracket!\llbracket s-j\rrbracket!}\frac{\llbracket\ell_{2}-s+j\rrbracket!}{\llbracket\ell_{1}\rrbracket!\llbracket\ell_{2}\rrbracket!}\frac{q^{j(\ell_{1}-j+1)}}{\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{s}},\qquad j=0,1,\dots,s,

(2.2)

where we set
s=(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-\ell)/2
and the
q
-factorials are defined by

\displaystyle\llbracket n\rrbracket!:=\begin{cases}\llbracket n\rrbracket\llbracket n-1\rrbracket\cdots\llbracket 1\rrbracket,&n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\\
1,&n=0.\end{cases}

Accordingly, the family of projections
p^{\ell}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\colon\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}\to\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
,
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
is determined by the properties that

\displaystyle p^{\ell}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\circ\iota_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}},&\ell=\ell^{\prime},\\
0,&\ell\neq\ell^{\prime},\end{cases}\qquad\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2}),

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\circ p^{\ell}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}},

for each
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Recall that there is another coproduct
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
on
\mathcal{U}_{q}
, with which we can form another tensor product representation
\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}
for
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
The rule of decomposition into irreducible representations is the same as in (
2.1
).
For each
\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, we obtain an injective homomorphism
(\iota^{\mathrm{op}})_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\colon\mathsf{M}_{\ell}\to\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}
by

(\iota^{\mathrm{op}})_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}:=P_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}}}\circ\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}
.
Here, we write
P_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}
with vector spaces
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
for the permutation operator

P_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\colon\mathbf{U}\otimes\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}\otimes\mathbf{U}
;
u\otimes v\mapsto v\otimes u
.

2.4
Representation category

Here we describe the category of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
-modules as a ribbon tensor category.
The general theory of tensor categories can be found in
[
1
,
12
]
.

2.4.1
Linear category

We write
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
for the category of finite-dimensional representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}
, which is clearly a
\mathbb{C}
-linear category.
As we have already noted, the simple objects of
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
are exhausted by
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and their twist by the automorphism
\chi
up to isomorphism.
Under our assumption that
q
is not a root of unity, it is also known that
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
is semi-simple although it has infinitely many simple objects.

We define
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
as the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
generated by
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In other words, each object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of
\mathsf{M}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

2.4.2
Tensor structure

We can equip the category
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
with the monoidal bifunctor
-\otimes_{\Delta}-\colon\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})\times\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
defined by means of the coproduct
\Delta
.
The associativity isomorphisms are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}}\colon\ (\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V})\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{W}\to\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}(\mathsf{V}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{W});\qquad(u\otimes v)\otimes w\mapsto u\otimes(v\otimes w)

for
\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}\in\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
,
i.e., the associativity for the underlying vector spaces.
We can take
\mathsf{M}_{0}
as a unit object with respect to this tensor product and choose unit isomorphisms
\lambda_{\mathsf{U}}\colon\mathsf{M}_{0}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{U}
and
\rho_{\mathsf{U}}\colon\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{M}_{0}\to\mathsf{U}
.
In this way,
(\otimes_{\Delta},\alpha,\mathsf{M}_{0},\lambda,\rho)
defines a monoidal structure on the category
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
.
Since
\mathcal{U}_{q}
has an invertible antipode,
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
is also rigid.
Therefore,
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
is a tensor category.

Since the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is closed under the tensor product and the dual, it is a tensor subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathcal{U}_{q})
.
We suppose that the unit isomorphisms are chosen in such a way that
{\lambda_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}=p^{\ell}_{0\ell}}
and
\rho_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}=p^{\ell}_{\ell 0}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then, the monoidal structure on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is uniquely determined.
As a monoidal category,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
starts depending on the parameter
q
.
Thus, we write
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
for the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
equipped with the above tensor structure.

If we take the other coproduct
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
, the same underlying category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is equipped with another monoidal structure
(\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}},\alpha^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{M}_{0},\lambda^{\mathrm{op}},\rho^{\mathrm{op}})
.
This tensor category will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q)
.
Note that, however, as a linear map, each associativity isomorphism
\alpha^{\mathrm{op}}_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}}
,
\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, can be taken as the same
\alpha_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}}
.

At this point, we can discuss the so-called
6j
-symbols.
In our context, the
6j
-symbols of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q)
, instead of those of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
, will be more manifest.
For an arbitrary choice of four
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define the set

I^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}_{\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}:=\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\cap\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{3},\ell_{4})
.
Now, we fix
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

and compare two spaces of homomorphisms:
one is

2.4.3
Braiding and twist

Here we consider the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}
to be equipped with the coproduct
\Delta
(but not
\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}
.) It admits the universal
R
-matrix given by the following formula:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=q^{\frac{1}{2}H\otimes H}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{n}}{\llbracket n\rrbracket!}(F^{n}\otimes E^{n}),

which makes sense in a certain completion of
\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{q}
.
Here,
H
is the symbol that is supposed to behave as
K=q^{H}
.
Its action on
\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V}
with
\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
is well defined.
Indeed, since these representations are finite-dimensional, the infinite sum in the formula of
\mathcal{R}
truncates to a finite sum, and for
u\in\mathsf{U}
and
v\in\mathsf{V}
such that
K.u=q^{m}u
and
K.v=q^{n}v
, we may understand

\displaystyle q^{\frac{1}{2}H\otimes H}.u\otimes v=q^{\frac{mn}{2}}u\otimes v.

We write
R_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}
for the action of
\mathcal{R}
on
\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V}

and set

\displaystyle c_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}:=P_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}R_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}\colon\ \mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V}\to\mathsf{V}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{U}.

The family of morphisms
c_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}
,
\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
gives a braiding structure to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
.

There is a standard procedure to read a twist isomorphism out of the universal
R
-matrix
[
37
, Chapter XIV.6]
.
For each
\mathsf{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
, we can define the twist isomorphism
\theta_{\mathsf{U}}\colon\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{U}
by

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathsf{U}}^{-1}u=(-1)^{H}K\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n-1)/2}\big(q-q^{-1}\big)^{n}}{\llbracket n\rrbracket!}\big(-K^{-1}E\big)^{n}q^{-\frac{1}{2}H^{2}}F^{n}u,\qquad u\in\mathsf{U}.

(2.5)

It turns out that this twist isomorphism gives a ribbon structure.

.

3.1
VOA and modules

Here, we record the definitions of a VOA and its modules following, e.g.,
[
18
,
46
]
, but equivalent sets of axioms are also found in
[
16
,
36
]
.

First, we recall the Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{vir}
.
It is the infinite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{vir}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C
with the Lie bracket

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}C,\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad[C,\mathfrak{vir}]=0.

We also need to fix a convention with formal calculus to phrase the definition of a VOA.
That is, the formal series
(x-y)^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in the two variables
x
and
y
is defined by

\displaystyle(x-y)^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}x^{n-k}(-y)^{k}.

In particular,
(x-y)^{n}
and
(-1)^{n}(y-x)^{n}
are different formal series when
n<0
.
We need this convention to interpret the expression such as

\delta\big(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{0}}\big)
.
Here,
\delta(x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{n}
is called the formal
\delta
-distribution,
and we understand

\displaystyle\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{0}}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x_{0}^{-n}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}x_{0}^{-n}x_{1}^{n-k}(-x_{2})^{k}.

The definition of a VOA goes as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

A VOA consists of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
such that
\dim V_{n}<\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
distinguished vectors
\mathbf{1}\in V_{0}
and
\omega\in V_{2}
,
and a linear map

\displaystyle Y(-,x)\colon\ V\to\mathrm{End}(V)\big[\big[x^{\pm 1}\big]\big],\qquad a\mapsto Y(a,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}x^{-n-1}

subject to the following axioms:

(1)

(Field condition) For all
a,b\in V
,
a_{(n)}b=0
for all sufficiently large
n\gg 0
.

(2)

(Vacuum axiom)
Y(\mathbf{1},x)=\operatorname{id}
and

Y(a,x)\mathbf{1}\in a+V[[x]]x

for all
a\in V
.

(3)

(Virasoro representation) Define
L^{V}_{n}=\omega_{(n+1)}\in\mathrm{End}(V)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the assignment
L_{n}\mapsto L^{V}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
C\mapsto c\cdot\operatorname{id}
with a scalar
c\in\mathbb{C}

gives a representation of
\mathfrak{vir}
.
Furthermore,
L^{V}_{0}|_{V_{n}}=n\operatorname{id}_{V_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
L^{V}_{-1}a=a_{(-2)}\mathbf{1}
,
a\in V
.

(4)

(Translation property) For all
a\in V
,

\displaystyle\big[L_{-1}^{V},Y(a,x)\big]=\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}x}Y(a,x).

(5)

(Jacobi identity) For all
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{0}}\right)Y(a,x_{1})Y(b,x_{2})-x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{-x_{0}}\right)Y(b,x_{2})Y(a,x_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=x_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{0}}{x_{2}}\right)Y\left(Y(a,x_{0})b,x_{2}\right).

The distinguished vectors
\mathbf{1}
and
\omega
are called the vacuum and the conformal vectors, respectively.
The linear map
Y(-,x)
is called the state-field correspondence map, and
the scalar
c
that appeared in the Virasoro representation is called the central charge of the VOA.
Though a VOA is a quadruple
(V,\mathbf{1},\omega,Y)
,
it is common to only refer to
V
to address a VOA.
We will introduce the particular example of our interest in Section
4
.

The modules of a VOA are defined as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

For a VOA
V
, a
V
-module is a pair of a
\mathbb{C}
-graded vector space
W=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}W_{\alpha}
such that
\dim W_{\alpha}<\infty
and
W_{\alpha-n}=0
,
n\gg 0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and a linear map

\displaystyle Y_{W}(-,x)\colon\ V\to\mathrm{End}(W)\big[\big[x^{\pm 1}\big]\big],\qquad a\mapsto Y_{W}(a,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{W}_{(n)}x^{-n-1}

subject to the following axioms:

(1)

(Field condition) For all
a\in V
and
w\in W
,
a^{W}_{(n)}w=0
for all sufficiently large
n\gg 0
.

(2)

(Vacuum axiom)
Y_{W}(\mathbf{1},x)=\operatorname{id}_{W}
.

(3)

(
L_{0}
-action) For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
W_{\alpha}
is a generalized eigenspace of
L_{0}^{W}\coloneqq\omega^{W}_{(1)}
with eigenvalue
\alpha
. In other words,
L^{W}_{0}-\alpha
is nilpotent on
W_{\alpha}
.

(4)

(Jacobi identity) For all
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{0}}\right)Y_{W}(a,x_{1})Y_{W}(b,x_{2})-x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{-x_{0}}\right)Y_{W}(b,x_{2})Y_{W}(a,x_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=x_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{0}}{x_{2}}\right)Y_{W}\left(Y(a,x_{0})b,x_{2}\right).

When we set
L^{W}_{n}=\omega^{W}_{(n+1)}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and
c
to be the same central charge as
V
itself,
the assignment
L_{n}\mapsto L_{n}^{W}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
C\mapsto c\cdot\operatorname{id}_{W}
gives a representation of
\mathfrak{vir}
.

3.2
Intertwining operators/maps

Finally, we come to the notion of intertwining operators.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
V
be a VOA and
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
,
W_{3}
be
V
-modules.
An intertwining operator of type
\binom{W_{3}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
is a linear map

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}(-,x)\colon\ W_{1}\to\mathrm{Hom}(W_{2},W_{3})\{x\}

satisfying the Jacobi identity

\displaystyle x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{0}}\right)Y_{W_{3}}(v,x_{1})\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x_{2})-x_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{-x_{0}}\right)\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x_{2})Y_{W_{2}}(v,x_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad=x_{2}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{x_{1}-x_{0}}{x_{2}}\right)\mathcal{Y}\left(Y_{W_{1}}(v,x_{0})w_{1},x_{2}\right)

for all
v\in V
and
w_{1}\in W_{1}
,
and the
L_{-1}
-derivation property

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}\big(L_{-1}^{W_{1}}w_{1},x\big)=\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}x}\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x)

for all
w_{1}\in W_{1}
.
The set of intertwining operators of type
\binom{W_{3}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
is a vector space denoted by
I\binom{W_{3}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
, whose dimension is called the fusion rule of that type.

Remark 3.4
.

If all three modules are
V
itself,
I\binom{V}{VV}
contains the state-field correspondence map
Y(-,x)
.
If
W_{1}=V
and
W_{2}=W_{3}=W
, then the space of intertwining operators
I\binom{W}{VW}
contains the module map
Y_{W}(-,x)
.

Later, we will need the following transformation of intertwining operators.
Let
\mathcal{Y}
be an intertwining operator of type
\binom{W_{3}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
with
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
, and
W_{3}
being modules for a VOA
V
.
Define

\displaystyle(\Omega\mathcal{Y})(-,x)\colon\ W_{2}\to\mathrm{Hom}(W_{1},W_{3})\{x\}

(3.1)

by

\displaystyle(\Omega\mathcal{Y})(w_{2},x)w_{1}:={\rm e}^{xL^{W_{3}}_{-1}}\mathcal{Y}\big(w_{1},{\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}}x\big)w_{2},\qquad w_{1}\in W_{1},\qquad w_{2}\in W_{2}.

Then, we can show
[
32
, Proposition 7.1]
that
\Omega\mathcal{Y}
is an intertwining operator of type
\binom{W_{3}}{W_{2}W_{1}}
.
Note that
\Omega
is invertible.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we define
\log(z)
so that
\arg(z)\in[0,2\pi)
.
Then specializing the formal variable in an intertwining operator
\mathcal{Y}
at
x={\rm e}^{\log(z)}
makes sense, giving rise to the linear map

\displaystyle F_{\mathcal{Y},z}\colon\ W_{1}\otimes W_{2}\to\overline{W_{3}};\qquad w_{1}\otimes w_{2}\to\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x)w_{2}|_{x={\rm e}^{\log(z)}},

where we define the completion of
W_{3}
by
\overline{W_{3}}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}(W_{3})_{n}
.
In the sequel, we simply write

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},z)w_{2}=\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x)w_{2}|_{x={\rm e}^{\log(z)}}

for the evaluation of the formal variable as long as the branch is chosen as
\arg z\in[0,2\pi)
.
We call such a linear map obtained from an intertwining operator an
intertwining map
associated to
z
.
Since the correspondence
\mathcal{Y}\mapsto F_{\mathcal{Y},z}
is one-to-one, we could say that they are the same notion, but intertwining maps are still more convenient when we define the tensor product of modules.

3.3
\bm{P(z)}
-tensor product

Let
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
be
V
-modules, and fix
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
A
P(z)
-product of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
is a
V
-module
W_{3}
together with an intertwining map
F_{z}
of type
\binom{W_{3}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
associated to
z
.
The
P(z)
-
tensor
product is a universal object among
P(z)
-products.
To be precise, the
P(z)
-tensor product of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
is the
P(z)
-product
(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2},\boxtimes_{P(z)})

such that for any
P(z)
-product
(W_{3},F_{z})
of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, there exists a unique
V
-module homomorphism
\eta\colon W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2}\to W_{3}
satisfying
{F_{z}=\overline{\eta}\circ\boxtimes_{P(z)}}

\textstyle{W_{1}\otimes W_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\boxtimes_{P(z)}}
\scriptstyle{F_{z}}
\textstyle{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists!\eta}
\textstyle{W_{3}.}

It is standard to show that the
P(z)
-tensor product is unique up to isomorphism if it exists.
We note that the
P(z)
-tensor product depends on the choice of a category of modules.

For the
P(z)
-tensor product
(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2},\boxtimes_{P(z)})
,
there exists a unique intertwining operator
{\mathcal{Y}(-,x)}
of type
\binom{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2}}{W_{1}W_{2}}
such that

w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}w_{2}=\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},z)w_{2}
,
w_{1}\in W_{1},w_{2}\in W_{2}
.

The
P(z)
-tensor product defines a bifunctor on the category of modules of interest.
​To see this, let
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
,
W_{3}
,
W_{4}
be
V
-modules and let
f\colon W_{1}\to W_{3}
and
g\colon W_{2}\to W_{4}
be morphisms.
Suppose that the
P(z)
-tensor products
\big(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2},\boxtimes_{P(z)}^{12}\big)
and
\big(W_{3}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{4},\boxtimes_{P(z)}^{34}\big)
exist.
Then the object
W_{3}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{4}
together with
\boxtimes_{P(z)}^{34}\circ(f\otimes g)
gives a
P(z)
-product of
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
.
This means that there exists a unique morphism denoted by
f\boxtimes_{P(z)}g\colon W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{2}\to W_{3}\boxtimes_{P(z)}W_{4}
such that

\displaystyle\boxtimes^{34}_{P(z)}\circ(f\otimes g)=\overline{f\boxtimes_{P(z)}g}\circ\boxtimes_{P(z)}^{12}.

3.4
Composition and iteration

Let
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
,
W_{3}
be
V
-modules and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be such that
|z_{1}|>|z_{2}|>|z_{1}-z_{2}|>0
.
In this setting, let us assume that the composition

W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3})

exists.
Then, associated with it, we have the composition of intertwining maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{1}(-,z_{1})\mathcal{Y}^{2}(-,z_{2})-\colon\ W_{1}\otimes W_{2}\otimes W_{3}\to\overline{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3})},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{1}(-,x)\in I\binom{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3})}{W_{1}W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{Y}^{2}(-,x)\in I\binom{W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}}{W_{2}W_{3}}

are the corresponding intertwining operators.
For
w_{i}\in W_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, we can identify

\displaystyle w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(w_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}w_{3})=\mathcal{Y}^{1}(w_{1},z_{1})\mathcal{Y}^{2}(w_{2},z_{2})w_{3}.

Note that composition of intertwining maps does not automatically make sense,
but we must verify that the infinite sum appearing in the composition absolutely converges.

Let us also assume that the iteration

(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}

exists.
In this case, we get the iteration of the corresponding intertwining maps

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{2}(-,z_{1}-z_{2})-,z_{2})-\colon\ W_{1}\otimes W_{2}\otimes W_{3}\to\overline{(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{1}(-,x)\in I\binom{(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}}{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2}W_{3}},\qquad\mathcal{Y}_{2}(-,x)\in I\binom{W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2}}{W_{1}W_{2}}.

We can make the identification

\displaystyle(w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}w_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}w_{3}=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\left(\mathcal{Y}_{2}(w_{1},z_{1}-z_{2})w_{2},z_{2}\right)w_{3}

for
w_{i}\in W_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.
Similarly to the composition, we need to verify that the iteration of intertwining maps makes sense.

The associativity isomorphism

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{A}_{P(z_{1}),P(z_{2})}^{P(z_{1}-z_{2}),P(z_{2})}\big)_{W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}}\colon\ (W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}\to W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}),

if it exists, is characterized by the property

\displaystyle\overline{\big(\mathcal{A}_{P(z_{1}),P(z_{2})}^{P(z_{1}-z_{2}),P(z_{2})}\big)_{W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}}}\colon\ (w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}w_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}w_{3}\mapsto w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(w_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}w_{3})

for
w_{i}\in W_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.

3.5
Parallel transport

Let
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
be
V
-modules,
and let us take
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Then we may consider the
P(z_{1})
-tensor product
W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}W_{2}
and the
P(z_{2})
-tensor product
W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{2}
.
Depending on a path
\gamma
in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
,
the parallel transport isomorphism
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}\colon W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}W_{2}\to W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{2}
is defined as follows.

As before, we fix a branch of
\log(z_{2})
so that
\arg(z_{2})\in[0,2\pi)
.
Then we write
l_{\gamma}(z_{1})
for the logarithm of
z_{1}
determined by the analytic continuation along
\gamma
from
\log(z_{2})
.
Let
\mathcal{Y}(-,x)
be the corresponding intertwining operator to the
P(z_{2})
-tensor product
W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{2}
.
Then, the parallel transport
\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
is characterized by the property

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{\gamma}\left(w_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}w_{2}\right)=\mathcal{Y}(w_{1},x)w_{2}|_{x={\rm e}^{l_{\gamma}(z_{1})}},\qquad w_{1}\in W_{1},w_{2}\in W_{2}.

3.6
Monoidal structure

The
P(z)
-tensor product depends on
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, so varying
z
, we get a family of tensor products that are related by parallel transport.
Here, we fix a single monoidal structure at

First, we set

and take

For a
V

\lambda_{W}\colon V\boxtimes W\to W

\displaystyle\lambda_{W}\colon\ \mathbf{1}\boxtimes w\mapsto w,\qquad\overline{\rho_{W}}\colon\ w\boxtimes\mathbf{1}\mapsto{\rm e}^{L^{W}_{-1}}w,\qquad w\in W.

To define the associativity isomorphism, we take
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
on the real axis so that

We also take several paths in

\gamma_{1}
from

to
z_{2}
,
\gamma_{2}
from
1
to
z_{1}-z_{2}

\gamma_{3}
from

to
1

\gamma_{4}

z_{2}

Then, given three

\mathcal{A}_{W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}}\colon(W_{1}\boxtimes W_{2})\boxtimes W_{3}\to W_{1}\boxtimes(W_{2}\boxtimes W_{3})

\displaystyle(W_{1}\boxtimes W_{2})\boxtimes W_{3}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{1}}}(W_{1}\boxtimes W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}_{\gamma_{2}}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}\operatorname{id}_{W_{3}}}

\displaystyle(W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1}-z_{2})}W_{2})\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3}\xrightarrow{(\mathcal{A}_{P(z_{1}),P(z_{2})}^{P(z_{1}-z_{2}),P(z_{2})})_{W_{1},W_{2},W_{3}}}W_{1}\boxtimes_{P(z_{1})}(W_{2}\boxtimes_{P(z_{2})}W_{3})

Then,

3.7
Braiding and twist

We can define a braiding on the category by means of the parallel transport.
Let
\gamma
be a path from

to

Given two

and

\displaystyle\overline{c_{W_{1},W_{2}}}\colon\ w_{1}\boxtimes w_{2}\mapsto{\rm e}^{L^{W_{2}\boxtimes W_{1}}_{-1}}\overline{\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}}(w_{2}\boxtimes_{P(-1)}w_{1}),\qquad w_{1}\in W_{1},w_{2}\in W_{2}.

4
Generic Virasoro VOA and modules

In this section, we introduce the generic Virasoro VOA and its modules, and review the results of our previous work
[

]
on the first-row modules.

4.1
Generic Virasoro VOA and the Kac table

For a fixed central charge
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the universal Virasoro VOA
\mathbb{V}_{c}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{c}=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})/\big(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})(C-c)+\textstyle{\sum_{n\geq-1}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})L_{n}\big)

together with the vacuum vector
\mathbf{1}=[1]
and the conformal vector
\omega=[L_{-2}]
.
The state field correspondence map
Y(-,x)
is uniquely determined by

\displaystyle Y(\omega,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}x^{-n-2}.

We parameterize the central charge by another parameter
t
as

\displaystyle c=c(t)=13-6\big(t+t^{-1}\big).

(4.1)

It is known that, when
t\not\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{V}_{c}
is a simple VOA (see, e.g.,
[
35
]
).
In this case, we call
\mathbb{V}_{c}
the generic Virasoro VOA of central charge
c
.

Let us consider modules of
\mathbb{V}_{c}
.
For a conformal weight
h\in\mathbb{C}
, the Verma module
\mathbb{M}(c,h)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}(c,h)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})/\big(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})(C-c)+\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})(L_{0}-h)+\textstyle{\sum_{n\geq 1}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})L_{n}\big).

The Verma module is not only a representation of
\mathfrak{vir}
, but also a
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-module.
Under the parametrization (
4.1
), the conformal weights of the Kac table are given by

\displaystyle h_{r,s}=h_{r,s}(t)=\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}t-\frac{rs-1}{2}+\frac{s^{2}-1}{4}t^{-1},\qquad r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

The Verma module
\mathbb{M}(c(t),h_{k+1,\ell+1}(t))
with
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is reducible, so we write its simple quotient as
\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
.
It is known that
\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
are self-dual:
\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}^{\vee}\simeq\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
,
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
We also set
\mathbb{W}_{\ell}:=\mathbb{W}_{(\ell,0)}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and call them the first-row modules.
We remark that
\mathbb{W}_{0}
is
\mathbb{V}_{c}
itself because the maximal proper submodule of
\mathbb{M}(c(t),h_{1,1}(t))
is generated by
[L_{-1}]
.

In the rest of this section, we focus on the first-row modules.
It will then be convenient to write

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\ell}:=h_{\ell+1,1}=\frac{\ell(\ell+2)}{4}t-\frac{\ell}{2},\qquad\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

(4.2)

for the conformal weights of the first-row modules.
We also fix the highest weight vector
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}
of
\mathbb{W}_{\ell}
as the image of
1\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{vir})
.

4.2
Fusion rules

The fusion rules among the first-row modules have been known for a long time
[
19
]
, and we gave an alternative proof for them in our previous work
[
43
]
.
Let us record the result here.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
19
,
43
]
)
.

For
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\displaystyle\dim I\binom{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{3}}}{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}}=\begin{cases}1,&\ell_{3}\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2}),\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Recall that
\mathrm{Sel}
is the selection rule set of the Clebsch–Gordan rule.

Suppose that
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfy the selection rule
\ell_{3}\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
.
Then there exists a unique intertwining operator of type
\binom{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{3}}}{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}}
up to constant.
We fix the normalization of the intertwining operator

\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}(-,x)\in I\binom{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{3}}}{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}}

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}(\mathbf{w}_{\ell_{1}},x)\mathbf{w}_{\ell_{2}}\in\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}\mathbf{w}_{\ell_{3}}x^{\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{3}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}}}+\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{3}}[[x]]x^{\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{3}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}}+1},

where the constant
\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}=\frac{1}{s!}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{\Gamma(1+tj)\Gamma(1-t(\ell_{1}+1-j))\Gamma(1-t(\ell_{2}+1-j))}{\Gamma(1+t)\Gamma(2-t(2-p+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-s))},

where
s=(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-\ell_{3})/2
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let us remark on a few properties of the intertwining operator
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{3}}(-,x)
when
\ell_{1}=0
or
\ell_{2}=0
.
When
\ell_{1}=0
, as we pointed out in Remark
3.4
,
I\binom{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}{\mathbb{V}_{c}\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}
contains
Y_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}(-,x)
, which now must span the space of intertwining operators.
Furthermore, we can identify
\mathcal{Y}_{0\ell}^{\ell}(-,x)\allowbreak=Y_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}(-,x)
by observing

\mathcal{Y}_{0\ell}^{\ell}(\mathbf{1},x)=\operatorname{Id}_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}=Y_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}(\mathbf{1},x)
.
For the case where
\ell_{2}=0
, the normalization of

gives us

By the Jacobi identity and the
L_{-1}
-derivation property, we can deduce that

\mathcal{Y}_{\ell 0}^{\ell}(\mathbf{w}_{\ell},x)\mathbf{1}={\rm e}^{L_{-1}^{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}}\mathbf{w}_{\ell}
.

5
Category of the first-row modules

In this section, we introduce the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
of the first-row modules of the generic Virasoro VOA
\mathbb{V}_{c}
,
and establish the ribbon tensor equivalence
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
.

5.1
Dual and opposite categories

We first make tiny preliminaries on dual and opposite categories.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category.
The dual category
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
is the category with the same objects as
\mathcal{C}
and the opposite morphisms.
If
\mathcal{C}
is equipped with a monoidal structure, then
\mathcal{C}^{\vee}
naturally becomes a monoidal category.

Next, suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a monoidal category with the monoidal bifunctor denoted by
\otimes
.
The opposite category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
is the monoidal category with the same underlying category as
\mathcal{C}
and the opposite monoidal structure
\otimes^{\mathrm{op}}

X\otimes^{\mathrm{op}}Y:=Y\otimes X
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
.

In the case where
\mathcal{C}
is rigid, the dual and opposite categories are equivalent under a functor such that
X\mapsto X^{*}

[
12
, Chapter 2]
.

Remark 5.1
.

Sometimes the dual category is called opposite, and the opposite category above is called reversed.
However, we follow the terminology in
[
12
]
.

When we apply the above construction to
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q)
, which we have already noticed is rigid, we can say that
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\vee}
and
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\mathrm{op}}
are equivalent.
Let us also see that the category
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\mathrm{op}}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
as a tensor category.
Recall that
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
have the same underlying abelian category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
We take the natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}:=P_{\mathsf{V},\mathsf{U}}\colon\ \mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}^{\mathrm{op}}\mathsf{V}=\mathsf{V}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V}

to be the permutation for each
\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
Indeed,
\Pi_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}}
lives in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}(\mathsf{V}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathsf{U},\mathsf{U}\otimes_{\Delta}\mathsf{V})
as is checked as

\displaystyle\Delta(a)P_{\mathsf{V},\mathsf{U}}(v\otimes u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(a)}a_{(1)}u\otimes a_{(2)}v=P_{\mathsf{V},\mathsf{U}}\Bigl(\sum_{(a)}a_{(2)}v\otimes a_{(1)}u\Bigr)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathsf{V},\mathsf{U}}\left(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(a)(v\otimes u)\right),\qquad v\in\mathsf{V},\quad u\in\mathsf{U},\quad a\in\mathcal{U}_{q}.

Here, we wrote
\Delta(a)=\sum_{(a)}a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}
for
a\in\mathcal{U}_{q}
.
Therefore, the identity functor
\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}
together with the natural isomorphisms
(\Pi_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}})_{\mathsf{U},\mathsf{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}
defines an equivalence between
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\mathrm{op}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
as tensor categories.

In conclusion, we may identify
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\vee}
,
(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}^{\mathrm{op}}(q))^{\mathrm{op}}
, and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
altogether as tensor categories.

5.2
The first-row category
\bm{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}

We define the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
as the full subcategory of the module category of the generic Virasoro VOA
\mathbb{V}_{c}

generated by the first row modules
\mathbb{W}_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
as an additive category.
Therefore, any object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of first row modules,
and the morphism spaces are determined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}(\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}},\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}},&\ell_{1}=\ell_{2},\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Note that, at this point, the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is independent of the parameter
t
.

It is clear that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
as an abelian category.
Nevertheless, we would like to make equivalence functors explicit for later use.
For each object
\mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
, we fix an isomorphism
{f_{\mathbb{U}}\colon\mathbb{U}\to\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{W}_{\ell}^{\oplus m_{\ell}}}
.
Then, we define a functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
as follows.
At the object level, if
\mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{W}_{\ell}^{\oplus m_{\ell}}
, we send

\displaystyle F\colon\ \mathbb{U}\mapsto\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{M}_{\ell}^{\oplus m_{\ell}}.

At the morphism level, we first require that

\displaystyle F\colon\ \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}(\mathbb{W}_{\ell},\mathbb{W}_{\ell})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}(\mathsf{M}_{\ell},\mathsf{M}_{\ell});\qquad\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}\mapsto\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}

for each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For a general
h\colon\mathbb{U}_{1}\to\mathbb{U}_{2}
,
there is a unique way to send
f_{\mathbb{U}_{2}}\circ h\circ f_{\mathbb{U}_{1}}^{-1}
so that
F
induces linear maps on morphism spaces.
Then, we can simply define
F(h)=F\big(f_{\mathbb{U}_{2}}\circ h\circ f_{\mathbb{U}_{1}}^{-1}\big)
.

Similarly, on the side of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, we fix an isomorphism
g_{\mathsf{U}}\colon\mathsf{U}\to\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathsf{M}_{\ell}^{\oplus n_{\ell}}
for each
\mathsf{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
Then, we can define a functor
G\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
in the exactly analogous way as defining
F
.

Now, let us observe that the composition
G\circ F\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}
.
Indeed, the family
(f_{\mathbb{U}})_{\mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}
of the fixed isomorphisms gives a natural isomorphism
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}}\Rightarrow G\circ F
;
for any
\mathbb{U}_{1},\mathbb{U}_{2}\!\in\!\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
and
h\colon\mathbb{U}_{1}\to\mathbb{U}_{2}
,
the commutativity of the diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{U}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{\mathbb{U}_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{G\circ F(\mathbb{U}_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G\circ F(h)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{U}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{\mathbb{U}_{2}}}
\textstyle{G\circ F(\mathbb{U}_{2})}

follows from the definitions of
F
and
G
.
Similarly,
F\circ G\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is shown to be isomorphic to
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}}
.

In the rest of this section, we will see that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
can be equipped with the structure of a ribbon tensor category
following the general framework sketched in Section
3
,
and will write the resulting ribbon tensor category as
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
.
At each step, we will compare the corresponding structure of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
with that of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
,
and thereby prove Theorem
1.1
.

5.3
Tensor structure

First, we show that the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is closed under the
P(z)
-tensor product for any

.
The following formula (

Theorem 5.2
.

For any

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is closed under the

-tensor product.

(5.2)

Proof.

Since the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}
is semi-simple, it suffices to show that the

) and (

Let
\mathbb{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}

\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}

\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}

The object
\mathbb{U}

where

In other words, we can find a family of injections

and projections

Then, for each

\displaystyle w_{1}\boxtimes w_{2}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell}(w_{1},1)w_{2}

and on the other hand, we can see that

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{L^{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}}_{-1}}\overline{\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}}(w_{2}\boxtimes_{P(-1)}w_{1})=\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\big(\Omega\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}\big)(w_{1},1)w_{2}.

Recall the transformation (
3.1
) of intertwining operators.
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\Omega\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}\big)(-,x)\in I\binom{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}}.

In particular, it must be proportional to
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell}(-,x)
since the corresponding space of intertwining operators is one-dimensional.
We can fix the constant of proportionality by looking at the image of the highest weight vectors

\displaystyle\big(\Omega\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}\big)(\mathbf{w}_{\ell_{1}},x)\mathbf{w}_{\ell_{2}}\in\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}{\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}})}\mathbf{w}_{\ell}x^{\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}}}+\mathbb{W}_{\ell}[[x]]x^{\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}}+1}.

Since
\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell}=\mathbf{B}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}
, we can see that

\big(\Omega\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}^{\ell}\big)(-,x)={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}})}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell}(-,x)
.
Therefore, we obtain the formula

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{L^{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}}}_{-1}}\overline{\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}}\left(w_{2}\boxtimes_{P(-1)}w_{1}\right)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}{\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}})}\mathcal{Y}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{\ell}(w_{1},1)w_{2},

which concludes the desired result.
∎

We now see that the braiding in the above theorem matches that on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
.

Theorem 5.10
.

Along the tensor functor
(F,J)\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
,

F(c_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{1}},\mathbb{W}_{\ell_{2}}})=c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}

for each
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

p^{\ell}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}\circ c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}\circ\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}})}\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}

for each
\ell\in\mathrm{Sel}(\ell_{1},\allowbreak\ell_{2})
.
Let us apply
c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}
on
\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\big(u^{(\ell)}_{0}\big)
to find

\displaystyle c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}\circ\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}\big(u^{(\ell)}_{0}\big)\in c_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(0)q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{1}(\ell_{2}-2s)}u^{(\ell_{2})}_{s}\otimes u^{(\ell_{1})}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathbb{C}u^{(\ell_{2})}_{s-j}\otimes u^{(\ell_{1})}_{j}.

Here we set
s=(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-\ell)/2
as before.
Since the composition
p^{\ell}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}\circ c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}\circ\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}
must be proportional to the identity,
we have

\displaystyle p^{\ell}_{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}\circ c_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{1}},\mathsf{M}_{\ell_{2}}}\circ\iota_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\frac{c_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(0)}{c_{\ell}^{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}(s)}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{1}(\ell_{2}-2s)}\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}.

Substituting the explicit formula for the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients (
2.2
), the constant becomes

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(0)}{c_{\ell}^{\ell_{2}\ell_{1}}(s)}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{1}(\ell_{2}-2s)}=(-1)^{s}q^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{1}\ell_{2}-s(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})+s^{2}-s}.

When we recall that
q
is related to
t
by
q={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t}
and use the formula (
4.2
), we can check that this constant coincides with
{\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}(\mathbf{h}_{\ell}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{1}}-\mathbf{h}_{\ell_{2}})}
to complete the proof.
∎

5.5
Ribbon structure

Finally, we compare the ribbon structures.
Let us state the result immediately.

Theorem 5.11
.

Along the functor
(F,J)\colon\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
, we have

F(\theta_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}})=\theta_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}

for each
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

It is already clear that
\theta_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}={\rm e}^{2\pi\mathbbm{i}\mathbf{h}_{\ell}}\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{\ell}}
.
When we apply the formula for
\theta_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}^{-1}
in (
2.5
) to the lowest weight vector
u^{(\ell)}_{\ell}
, we immediately get

\theta_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}^{-1}u^{(\ell)}_{\ell}={\rm e}^{-2\pi\mathbbm{i}\mathbf{h}_{\ell}}u^{(\ell)}_{\ell}
.
Therefore, we get
{\theta_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}={\rm e}^{2\pi\mathbbm{i}\mathbf{h}_{\ell}}\operatorname{id}_{\mathsf{M}_{\ell}}}
concluding the desired result.
∎

6
The category of the
\bm{C_{1}}
-cofinite modules

We move on to looking at the category of
C_{1}
-cofinite modules of
\mathbb{V}_{c}
.
In general, given a VOA
V
and
V
-module
W
,
we may form the
C_{1}
-space of
W
as

\displaystyle C_{1}(W)=\bigl\{\bigl[x^{0}\bigr]Y_{W}(v,x)w\mid v\in V_{>0},\,w\in W\bigr\},

where the symbol
[x^{0}]
takes the coefficient of
x^{0}
, i.e., the constant term, in the following formal series, and
V_{>0}=\bigoplus_{n>0}V_{n}
.
The module
W
is called
C_{1}
-cofinite, if
C_{1}(W)
has a finite codimension:
\dim W/C_{1}(W)<\infty
.

We write
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}
for the category of
C_{1}
-cofinite
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-modules.
Due to
[
6
]
, it is equipped with the structure of a braided tensor category,
resulting in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
depending on the parameter
t
.
In the same paper,
[
6
]
studied the detailed structure of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
.
Combining their results and ours in the previous Section
5
,
Theorem
1.2
is rather straightforward as we will see below.

6.1
Category
\bm{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)}

Let us first see the main input from
[
6
]
regarding the category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)

of
C_{1}
-cofinite
\mathbb{V}_{c}
-modules.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
is a semi-simple braided tensor category with simple objects
\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
,
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Furthermore, we have the following fusion rules:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{(k_{1},0)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(k_{2},0)}=\bigoplus_{k_{3}\in\mathrm{Sel}(k_{1},k_{2})}\mathbb{W}_{(k_{3},0)},\qquad\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}=\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}

for
k_{1},k_{2},k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

In particular, the first-row category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
can be found as a tensor subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
.
Analogously to the first-row category, we may define the first-column category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)

generated by the modules
\mathbb{W}_{(0,s)}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
in the first column of the Kac table.
Recall that the row and column are exchanged under changing the parameter
t
to
t^{-1}
.
Therefore, the first-column category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
is also a braided tensor category and is equivalent to

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(\tilde{q})
with another
{\tilde{q}={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t^{-1}}}
.

We may define the (Deligne) tensor product
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
following
[
12
, Section 4.6]
.
It is an additive category whose objects are direct sums of those of the form
X^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}X^{-}
with
X^{\pm}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{\pm}(t)
.
The space of morphisms from
X^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}X^{-}
to
Y^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}Y^{-}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)}\big(X^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}X^{-},Y^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}Y^{-}\big)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)}\big(X^{+},Y^{+}\big)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)}(X^{-},Y^{-}).

From this definition, it follows that the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
are
\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\overline{\boxtimes}\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}
with
{k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.
The monoidal structures on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{\pm}(t)
are naturally transferred to
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
.
In particular, the associativity on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
is simply the product of those on
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{\pm}(t)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

The functor determined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t);\qquad X^{+}\overline{\boxtimes}X^{-}\mapsto X^{+}\boxtimes X^{-}

and the natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{X^{+}}\boxtimes c_{X^{-},Y^{+}}\boxtimes\operatorname{id}_{Y^{-}}\colon\ X^{+}\boxtimes X^{-}\boxtimes Y^{+}\boxtimes Y^{-}\to X^{+}\boxtimes Y^{+}\boxtimes X^{-}\boxtimes Y^{-},

(6.1)

with
X^{+},Y^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
,
X^{-},Y^{-}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
form an equivalence of tensor categories.
Here,
c
is the braiding in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
.

Proof.

To see that the functor is an equivalence of additive categories, it suffices to show that, for
X^{+},Y^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
and
X^{-},Y^{-}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)}\big(X^{+},Y^{+}\big)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)}(X^{-},Y^{-})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)}\big(X^{+}\boxtimes X^{-},Y^{+}\boxtimes Y^{-}\big),

\displaystyle f\otimes g\mapsto f\boxtimes g

is a linear isomorphism.
It is clearly the case as we can check on simple objects.

It remains to show that the functor along with the isomorphisms (
6.1
) is a tensor functor.
In other words, we need to show that the isomorphisms

\displaystyle\big(\big(X^{+}\boxtimes Y^{+}\big)\boxtimes Z^{+}\big)\boxtimes((X^{-}\boxtimes Y^{-})\boxtimes Z^{-})\to\big(X^{+}\boxtimes\big(Y^{+}\boxtimes Z^{+}\big)\big)\boxtimes(X^{-}\boxtimes(Y^{-}\boxtimes Z^{-}))

and

\displaystyle\big(\big(X^{+}\boxtimes X^{-}\big)\boxtimes\big(Y^{+}\boxtimes Y^{-})\big)\boxtimes\big(Z^{+}\boxtimes Z^{-}\big)\to\big(X^{+}\boxtimes X^{-}\big)\boxtimes\big(\big(Y^{+}\boxtimes Y^{-}\big)\boxtimes\big(Z^{+}\boxtimes Z^{-}\big)\big)

coincide for any choice of
X^{+},Y^{+},Z^{+}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
and
X^{-},Y^{-},Z^{-}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)

under the identification by (
6.1
).
This follows from the identity

\displaystyle c_{X^{-}\boxtimes Y^{-},Z^{+}}\circ c_{X^{-},Y^{+}}=c_{X^{-},Y^{+}\boxtimes Z^{+}}\circ c_{Y^{-},Z^{+}}

and the axioms of a braided tensor category.
∎

6.2
Braiding in
\bm{\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)}

We already know the explicit formulas for the braiding in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{\pm}(t)
.
Furthermore, in the whole category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
, we can trivialize the braiding between
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)
as follows.

For each
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let us fix an intertwining operator

\mathcal{Y}(-,x)\in I\binom{\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}}{\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}}
.
By Theorem
6.1
, we may realize
\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}
as

\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}=\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
along with
\mathcal{Y}(-,1)

and
\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}
as

\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}=\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}
along with
(\Omega^{-1}\mathcal{Y})(-,1)
.
Here, recall (
3.1
) for the transformation
\Omega
.

Proposition 6.3
.

For
k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we assume the above realization of
\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}
and
\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}\boxtimes\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}
.
Then, the braiding
c_{\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)},\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}}
and
c_{\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)},\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}}
are both
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}}
.

Proof.

The identity
c_{\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)},\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}}
follows from the definition of the braiding.
Regarding the other one
c_{\mathbb{W}_{(0,\ell)},\mathbb{W}_{(k,0)}}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{W}_{(k,\ell)}}
, it suffices to show that
\Omega\mathcal{Y}=\Omega^{-1}\mathcal{Y}

for the above fixed intertwining operator
\mathcal{Y}
.
This follows from the identity

{\rm e}^{2\pi\mathbbm{i}(h_{k+1,\ell+1}-h_{k+1,0}-h_{0,\ell+1})}=1

with the conformal weights in the Kac table.
∎

Note that the last identity ensures that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)
has a ribbon structure
with the standard twist operator from Section
3.7
.

6.3
Quantum group dual

Recall that the parameter
t
for the Virasoro algebra and the quantum parameter
q
for
\mathcal{U}_{q}
were related as
q={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t}
.
Let us form the tensor product

\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{\tilde{q}}

of Hopf algebras with
\tilde{q}={\rm e}^{\pi\mathbbm{i}t^{-1}}
.
We set
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})
to be the category of finite-dimensional representations of
\mathcal{U}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{\tilde{q}}
such that the eigenvalues of
K\otimes 1
and
1\otimes K
are of the form
q^{n}
and
\tilde{q}^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, respectively.
It is reasonable to call such modules of type I.
Now, it is clear that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})
is equivalent to the tensor product
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(\tilde{q})
.
Thus, composing Theorem
5.7
with Proposition
6.2
, we get an equivalence

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(\tilde{q})\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{+}(t)\overline{\boxtimes}\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{-}(t)\to\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{vir}}^{1}(t)

of tensor categories.

Furthermore, we can equip
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})
with a braiding by the tensor product of the universal
R
-matrices on
\mathcal{U}_{q}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\tilde{q}}
.
In other words, the two components
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(\tilde{q})
are braided trivially in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})
.
The ribbon structure of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(q,\tilde{q})
is also given by the tensor product of those on the two components.
Due to Proposition
6.3
, the above tensor equivalence is a ribbon equivalence.

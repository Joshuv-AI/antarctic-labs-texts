1.
Introduction

In the present paper we study
singularly nonautonomous
semilinear parabolic problems of the form

(1.1)

\begin{split}&(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u_{{\varepsilon}}=F_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{{\varepsilon}}),\quad{}t>\tau,\\
&u_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=u^{\tau}\in Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X,\end{split}

where
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
is a parameter,
X
is a Banach space,
A_{\varepsilon}(t):D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))\subset X\to X
is a family of sectorial operators (with a certain uniformity in
t
that we shall specify latter),
Y
is a Banach Space continuously embedded in
X
, which we denote by
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
, and
F_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to X
is a nonlinearity.

The term
singularly nonautonomous
expresses the fact that this family
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
is time-dependent, as a counterpart to the semilinear problems where
A_{\varepsilon}(t)=A_{\varepsilon}
, which we refer as
nonsingular
. This terminology, adopted for instance in
[
6
,
10
]
, is not unanimous and, in the case we are considering here, does not refer to any discontinuity or blow-up in time, which can mean in other contexts. We shall adopt it in order to easily distinguish between the case studied to the well established case where there is no time-dependence on the linear operators.

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, Problem (
1.1
) approaches to what we refer as
limiting problem

\begin{split}&(u_{0})_{t}+A_{0}(t)u_{0}=F_{0}(t,u_{0}),\quad t>\tau,\\
&u_{0}(\tau)=u^{\tau}\in Y,\end{split}

whose solution is denoted by
u_{0}(t)=u_{0}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})
and referred as
limiting solution
.

For each
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and under suitable conditions on the family
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and on the nonlinearity
F_{\varepsilon}
, Problems (
1.1
) are well-posed.
We are interested in investigating the behavior of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}(t)
of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, comparing it to the limiting solution
u_{0}(t)
and providing a rate of convergence for those solutions in terms of
\varepsilon
. In order to obtain this convergence, we must first study the associated linear problem.

This type of analysis has already been done when the linear operators in (
1.1
) do not depend on time, that is,
A_{\varepsilon}(t)\equiv A_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
. In this case, each operator
-A_{\varepsilon}
generates a linear semigroup,
\{T_{-A_{\varepsilon}}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq 0\}
, that plays an essential role in solving the semilinear problem. Under suitable assumptions on
F_{\varepsilon}
, the
nonsingular
problem

(1.2)

(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}),\ t>\tau,\quad u_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=u^{\tau}\in Y,\quad\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),

is locally solved by

(1.3)

u_{\varepsilon}(t)=T_{-A_{\varepsilon}}(t-\tau)u^{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A_{\varepsilon}}(t-s)F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s))ds

and we refer to the above expression as
variation of constants formula
.

In papers such as
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
a general routine was conceived and applied in order to guarantee convergence of solutions of Problems (
1.2
) as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This routine is based in a detailed study of the behavior of the linear part. Precisely, the routine consists in first studying the convergence of the linear operator
A_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
to
A^{-1}_{0}
. This information is then used to obtain the convergence of the resolvent operator
(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon})^{-1}
to
(\lambda+A_{0})^{-1}
in some sector and from the resolvent convergence one obtains convergence of the linear semigroup
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
to
T_{-A_{0}}(\cdot)
. By using the variation of constants formula (
1.3
), one can also prove the convergence of the solutions to the limiting solution as a consequence of the linear semigroup convergence.

If the equation
u^{\varepsilon}_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})
is autonomous (
F_{\varepsilon}
does not depend on time), one can continue the analysis and derive the upper semi-continuity of the family of global attractors
\{\mathcal{A_{\varepsilon}}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
and even lower semi-continuity under suitable structural hypothesis on the limiting attractor
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. This is done for instance in
[
3
]
.

A careful analysis of those papers allows us to conclude that a huge effort goes in the direction of ensuring that the linear semigroup
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}}(\cdot)
converges to
T_{-A_{0}}(\cdot)
in an appropriate sense. From this type of Trotter-Kato Approximation Theorem, the convergence of the other elements being studied follows.

The situation changes when we consider singularly nonautonomous problem, since the linear semigroup is not the central element in obtaining the solution of the semilinear problem, as we discuss next. However, we are still able to elaborate for the singularly nonautonomous case (Problems (
1.1
)) a routine similar to the one mentioned in the articles above to treat the matter of convergence for the problems. An approach like this one for the singular nonautonomous case does not exist in the literature so far, and with the results we present in this paper, we shall be able to study perturbation of singularly nonautonomous problem, incorporating several different examples that appears in the literature.

The only matter that we shall not address in this paper is the lower semi-continuity of the pullback attractor associated to Problems (
1.1
), whenever we are able to prove that they exist. We do not pursuit this result due to the fact that there might not exist an elliptic associated problem for the limiting equation

(u_{0})_{t}+A_{0}(t)(u_{0})=F_{0}(t,u_{0})

and we usually can not derive information on the structure of the pullback attractor unless we require some simplifying assumptions on
A_{0}(t)
and
F_{0}(t,\cdot)
with respect to the time-dependence. Since this is not the purpose of this article, we do not look for a result on lower semi-continuity of the family of pullback attractors. Nevertheless, upper-semicontinuity of attractors will be obtained as a consequence of the convergences established for the solutions.

The main difference between the case where
A_{\varepsilon}(t)\equiv A_{\varepsilon}
to the singularly nonautonomous comes from the fact that instead of a linear semigroup associated to
-A_{\varepsilon}
that provides solutions for the semilinear problem through the variation of constant formula (
1.3
), we will have a two parameter family of linear operators

\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),\ t\geq\tau,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\}

that will be essential in describing the solution for the semilinear problem. The existence of such family associated to
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
was established almost simultaneously by Sobolevskiĭ
[
19
]
and Tanabe
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
This family
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
has properties similar to the ones presented by the linear semigroup in the autonomous case. In particular, there is an equivalent variation of constant formula that provides solutions for (
1.1
) given by

(1.4)

u_{\varepsilon}(t)=U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u_{\varepsilon}^{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s))ds.

Taking this into account, the outline we adopt to treat perturbation of singularly nonautonomous parabolic problems consists in the following steps:

(i)

First we prove that, for each fixed time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the linear operator
A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}
converges in an appropriate sense to the linear operator
A_{0}(t)^{-1}
.
We also establish the rate of such convergence in terms of specific characteristics of the problem.

(ii)

We use the previous information to obtain the resolvent convergence of
(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}
to
(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}
in a sector common to all the resolvent sets of all linear operators.

(iii)

Through a well-known formulation for analytic semigroups in terms of its resolvent, we transfer the resolvent convergence to the linear semigroup generated by
-A_{\varepsilon}(t)
, for a fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, that is, we obtain the convergence (with rate) of
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(t)}(\cdot)
to
T_{-A_{0}(t)}(\cdot)
.

(iv)

Using the formulations of the linear process
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
in terms of
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
and
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(t)}(\cdot)
(developed in
[
19
]
which we discuss in the sequel), we obtain the convergence (with rate) of
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
to
U_{0}(t,\tau)
. This result is presented Theorem
2.3
.

(v)

Using the variation of constants formula (
1.4
), we obtain in Theorem
2.5
the convergence (with rate) of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
to the solution
u_{0}(\cdot)
.

To attend the program proposed,
this paper is structured in the following manner: In Section
2
we present the assumptions required for the family of linear operators
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and for the nonlinearities
F_{\varepsilon}
that allow us to prove the results on convergence. We also enunciate in this section the main abstract results on convergence: Theorem
2.3
on the convergence of the linear process and Theorem
2.5
on the convergence of the solutions of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Their proofs are postponed to Section
3
and they depend on following steps
(i)
to
(v)
mentioned above. We then apply those results in two different examples. First, in Section
4
, we consider a family of reaction-diffusion equations in a fixed bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}&(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}-div(a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+u_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}),\quad x\in\Omega,\ t>\tau,\\
&\partial_{n}u_{\varepsilon}=0,\hskip 145.39359ptx\in\partial\Omega.\end{split}

Assuming that
a_{\varepsilon}
converges to
a_{0}
and
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f_{0}
, we derive in this section all the abstract conditions required in Theorems
2.3
and
2.5
that ensures convergence of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to
u_{0}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Moreover, under an additional dissipation assumption on the nonlinearities
f_{\varepsilon}
, we prove that each problem is globally well-posed, defines a nonlinear dissipative process with pullback attractor
\{\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\subset Y,\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and we prove this family of pullback attractors is upper-continuous in
\varepsilon=0
.
Finally, in Section
5
, we apply the abstract theory to a nonautonomous strongly damped wave equation and its fractional approximations in the sense of
[
7
]
.

Before we proceed to the goals proposed, we mention two points that are important to take into account. The first one concerns the linear operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
. For this paper, we shall consider a situation where the domain
D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))=D_{\varepsilon}
remains fixed in
t
and the phase space
X
where the linear operator is defined remains fixed in
t
and
\varepsilon
. This assumption holds for several problems, as we shall see in applications. The situation where the domain of the linear operator is time-dependent and the phase space changes with
\varepsilon
or time shall be addressed in future works.

The second point that we want to highlight is the motivation behind considering
singularly nonautonomous
problems. In general, an evolution system in a Banach space
X
can be represented by an equation

(1.5)

u_{t}=f(t,u),\quad t>\tau,\qquad u(\tau)=u^{\tau}\in Y,

where
Y\hookrightarrow X
and
f:\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{R}\times Y\to X
. However, the function
f
can be highly nonlinear, which makes it difficult to study the problem. To simplify it, we can approximate the above equation around a state
u_{0}
by a linear (or semilinear) evolution equation, and then use the several tools mentioned above (and others in the existent literature) to treat semilinear problems.

This approximation is obtained by considering the Taylor polynomial of
f
around the state
u_{0}
(assuming that
f
has the necessary regularity), that is,

f(t,u_{0}+z)=f(t,u_{0})+\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,u_{0})z+g(t,z),

where
g(t,z)=o(\|z\|_{Y})
when
\|z\|_{Y}\to 0
and
\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,u_{0})\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
is the Frechet Derivative of
f
with respect to the second variable. If we denote
z(t)=u(t)-u_{0}
and
-A(t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(t,u_{0})
, Problem (
1.5
) becomes

z_{t}+A(t)z=f(t,u_{0})+g(t,z),\quad t>\tau,\qquad z(\tau)=0,

which is
singularly nonautonomous
and is in the same format as Problem (
1.1
). Therefore, singularly nonautonomous evolution equations seems to be a good tool to model several real life phenomena and compels the efforts in the direction of describing its dynamics.

2.
Functional setting and main results

In the sequel we provide conditions on the family of linear operators
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
that ensure existence of the linear process
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),\ t\geq\tau,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
as well as convergence of
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
to
U_{0}(t,\tau)
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Once we have convergence of the linear parts established, we provide conditions for the nonlinearities
F_{\varepsilon}
that guarantee convergence of the solutions of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

2.1.
A type of Trotter-Kato Approximation Theorem for the Linear processes
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)

Consider the abstract
singlularly nonautonomous
semilinear problem (
1.1
) and assume that, for each
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a family of linear operators in
X
satisfying:

(P.1)

The operator
A_{\varepsilon}(t):D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))\subset X\to X
is a closed densely defined linear operator, the domain
D_{\varepsilon}=D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))
is fixed in time (but it can change with
\varepsilon
) and there are
constants
{C}>0
and
\mathbf{\varphi}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
(independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
) such that

\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}\subset\rho(-A_{\varepsilon}(t)),\quad\mbox{ for all }\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R},

where
\Sigma_{\varphi}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};|\arg\lambda|\leq\varphi\right\}
and

(2.1)

\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|+1},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

We say in this case that the family
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
is
uniformly sectorial
.

(P.2)

The operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
have the following regularizing property: its resolvent has its image on the Banach space
Y\hookrightarrow X
and there exists
\beta\in(0,1]
such that

(2.2)

\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|+1},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\},

and

(2.3)

\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|^{\beta}+1},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

(P.3)

There are constants
C>0
and
0<\delta\leq 1
(independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
) such that, for any

t,\tau,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\|[A_{\varepsilon}(t)-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)]A_{\varepsilon}(s)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C|t-\tau|^{\delta}.

We say that the function
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(s)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is
\delta-
uniformly Hölder continuous
.

Conditions
(P.1)
and
(P.2)
state that each operator
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
,
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, is sectorial and we can guarantee the existence of a common sector in the spectrum of them all as well as uniform estimate in this sector. Those properties can be seen as a uniform parabolicity for the family of linear operators. Moreover, to say that the resolvent of
A(t)
has its image in
Y
means that
D(A(t))\subset Y
, since
(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}:X\to D(A(t))
.

Condition
(P.3)
states that the Hölder exponent for the maps
t\mapsto A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(s)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
can be chosen uniformly among all families and, as a consequence of this property,

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C,\quad\mbox{ for all }(t,\tau)\mbox{ in a compact set and }\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}).

In this case, the graph norms defined by the operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
and
A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
in
D_{\varepsilon}
,

\|\cdot\|_{D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))}=\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\|_{X}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|\cdot\|_{D(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))}=\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\cdot\|_{X},

respectively, are equivalent. We shall refer to both norms as
\|\cdot\|_{X^{1}}
.

From conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.3)
will be able to derive uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
for the semigroups and linear process associated to the family

\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Nevertheless, in order to obtain properties of convergence as we make
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we shall require further conditions on the linear operators that connect the different problems being studied. Those conditions are stated next:

(P.4)

There exists a continuous function
\xi:[0,\varepsilon_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\xi(0)=0
such that

\sup_{t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\xi(\varepsilon).

(P.5)

There exists a continuous function
\eta:[0,\varepsilon_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\eta(0)=0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq\eta(\varepsilon).

For a fixed
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, each operator
A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
is sectorial with
\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
in the resolvent of
-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
. Henceforth,
-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
generates an analytic semigroup which we denote by
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(\cdot)
(see
[
18
, Theorem 1.5.2]
) given by

(2.4)

T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}d\lambda,

where
\Gamma
is the contour of
\Sigma_{\varphi}
and it is oriented with increasing imaginary part. This linear semigroup solves the linear and homogeneous differential equation

(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)u_{\varepsilon}=0,\ t>0,\quad u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u^{0},

by considering
u_{\varepsilon}(t,u^{0})=T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)u^{0}.

However, the family
\{T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X),\ t\geq 0\}
is not enough to describe the evolution of the system associated to (
1.1
). We must obtain a two parameter family
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq\tau\}
of linear operators associated to
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
that plays in the singularly nonautonomous case
a similar role as the semigroup in the nonsingular case, that is, we should expect
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
to recover the solution of the homogeneous equation

(2.5)

(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u_{\varepsilon}=0,\mbox{ }t>\tau;\quad u_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=u^{\tau},

by considering
u_{\varepsilon}(t)=U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u^{\tau}
. In other words, we expect that
\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=-A_{\varepsilon}(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
. As a matter of fact, we search for the existence of a family of linear operators
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),\ t\geq\tau\}
with the following properties:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A family
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq\tau\}
of bounded linear operators is a
linear process associated to
A_{\varepsilon}(t):D_{\varepsilon}\subset X\to X
if

(1)

U_{\varepsilon}(t,t)=I
and
U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)U_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)=U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
, for all
\tau\leq s\leq t
.

(2)

(t,\tau,x)\mapsto U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)x
is continuous for
t\geq\tau
and for all
x\in X
.

(3)

There exist
C,T>0
such that
\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C
, for all
0\leq t-\tau\leq T
.

(4)

U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau):X\rightarrow D_{\varepsilon}
and
(\tau,\infty)\ni t\mapsto U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)x\in X
is differentiable for each
x\in X
.

(5)

The derivative
\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
is a bounded linear operator in
X
,

\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=-A_{\varepsilon}(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)

and, for
T>0
, there exists
C=C(T)>0
such that

\|\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1},\quad\mbox{for }0\leq t-\tau\leq T.

Conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.3)
ensure the existence of this family, as proved in
[
19
, Theorem 1]
. We briefly mention the ideas behind the construction of such family, since it depends on two auxiliary families of linear operators
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
that will be necessary in the sequel.

Suppose that
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is a family satisfying the homogeneous differential equation given in (
2.5
), that is,
{\partial_{t}}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=-A_{\varepsilon}(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
. Also, assume that there exists another family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
such that
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
is obtained trough the integral equation

(2.6)

U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)ds.

Differentiating in
t
, adding
A_{\varepsilon}(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
on both sides and taking into account that
{\partial_{t}}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=-A_{\varepsilon}(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
, we deduce

0=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)-\int_{\tau}^{t}[A_{\varepsilon}(s)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)ds.

If we set

(2.7)

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau),

then
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
would have to satisfy

(2.8)

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)ds

and it would be a fixed point of the map
S_{\varepsilon}(\Psi)(t)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Psi(s)ds
.

If we had a family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
satisfying (
2.8
),
then we could proceed in the reverse way to obtain
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
. This is the technique employed to construct the linear process in the parabolic case
[
19
,
20
]
and the description of
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
relies on this auxiliary family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
. The next proposition is proved in
[
18
, Section 5.6]
and
[
19
]
. It ensures existence of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
and
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
under the conditions required previously.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a fixed
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
, assume that
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
satisfies
(P.1)
,
(P.2)
and
(P.3)
. Let
\delta\in(0,1]
be the constant of Hölder continuity and
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq\tau\}
the family given by (
2.7
), then:

(1)

\{(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};t>\tau\}\ni(t,\tau)\mapsto\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is continuous in the uniform topology and

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\delta-1},\quad\mbox{ for all }t>\tau,\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

There exists a unique family
\{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq\tau\}
that satisfies (
2.8
) and this family is continuous in terms of the parameters
(t,\tau)
, that is,
\{(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};t>\tau\}\ni(t,\tau)\mapsto\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is continuous and for each
T>0
, there exists
C=C(T)>0
such that

\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\delta-1},\quad\mbox{ for all }0<t-\tau\leq T.

Furthermore, the family of linear operators
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X),t\geq\tau\}
given by

U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)ds

is a
linear process associated to
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and satisfies the conditions in Definition
2.1
.

The fact that
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
given by (
2.6
) satisfies all the conditions in Definition
2.1
can be found in the work of Sobolevskiĭ in
[
19
]
or in the works of Tanabe
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. Those four families of linear operators,
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)
,
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
,
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
, are essential to describe the dynamics of the system associated to (
1.1
). We are then able to enunciate one of the main results on this paper, the Toter-Kato type result on the convergence of the linear process as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, whose proof is postponed to Section
3
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
hold, and let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in the resolvent estimate (
2.3
). For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exist constants
K,C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),
such that

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\theta}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>\tau
, where
\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)=\max\{[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}\}
. In particular,
\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

2.2.
Rate of convergence for the solution of the semilinear problem

In order to obtain existence of global solution and convergence of them as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we need to require some properties on the family of nonlinearities
F_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to X
. Assume that

(NL.1)

Each
F_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)
is Hölder continuous in
t
, globally Lipschitz in
u
and bounded. Moreover, the constants
L>0
of Lipschitz and
M>0
of boundedness for
F_{\varepsilon}
can be chosen uniformly in
\varepsilon
, that is

\displaystyle\|F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq M,\quad\mbox{ for all }(t,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times Y,\ \varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),

\displaystyle\|F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)-F_{\varepsilon}(t,v)\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq L\|u-v\|_{Y},\quad\mbox{ for all }\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),\ t\in\mathbb{R},\ u,v\in Y.

(NL.2)

There exists a continuous function
\gamma:[0,\varepsilon_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\gamma(0)=0
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{u\in Y}\|F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)-F_{0}(t,u)\|_{X}\leq\gamma(\varepsilon).

Remark 2.4
.

Conditions required in
(NL.1)
are very restrictive and usually not found in practice. However, in many situations (like the application considered in Section
4
) we are able to prove that dynamics of the Problems (
1.1
) eventually enters a bounded subset of
Y
, uniformly in
\varepsilon
. If that is the case, we can proceed with a cut-off for the nonlinearities outside this bounded set so that the new family
F_{\varepsilon}
obtained after the cut-off satisfies the assumptions required in
(NL.1)
. The parabolic problems with this new nonlinearity will differ out-side the bounded set, but remains the same inside it, where all the solutions eventually go. Therefore, by restricting our attention to this uniform bounded absorbing set, we can assume that
F_{\varepsilon}
have those desired properties inside it.

It follows from
[
19
, Theorem 7]
that Problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed, that is, there exists

u_{\varepsilon}:[\tau,\tau+T(\varepsilon,\tau,u^{\tau}))\to Y\mbox{ given by }u_{\varepsilon}(t)=U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u^{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s))ds,

solution of (
1.1
), where
T(\varepsilon,\tau,u^{\tau})>0
is the maximal interval of definition of
u_{\varepsilon}(t)
, and it depends on the initial condition and on
\varepsilon
. We denote the solution by
u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})
if we wish to emphasize the initial condition.

In Section
3
, Lemma
3.11
, we shall prove that the boundedness required for
F_{\varepsilon}
, implies that
\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{Y}
remains bounded for
t
in any interval of the form
[\tau,\tau+T]
. Therefore, the solution is globally defined in time and originates a nonlinear process
\{S_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau):Y\to Y,\ t\geq\tau,\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
given by

S_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u^{\tau}=u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau}).

We now present the result on convergence of the solution as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Its proof is postponed to Section
3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
hold, as well as
(NL.1)
and
(NL.2)
. Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in the resolvent estimate (
2.3
). For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists constants
C,K>0
, independent of
\varepsilon,t,\tau
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),

t>\tau
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
u^{\tau}\in Y
, we have

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})-u_{0}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})\|_{Y}\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\left[1+\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}\right]\rho(\theta,\varepsilon),

where

\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)=\max\{[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},\gamma(\varepsilon)\}.

In particular, the convergence of the solution is uniform for
t
in any interval of the form
[\tau+m,\tau+M]
,
0<m<M
, and
u^{\tau}\in B\subset Y
,
B
bounded.

As an immediate consequence of the previous theorem, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.6
.

Assume conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
,
(NL.1)
and
(NL.2)
hold. Let
S_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau):Y\to Y
be the nonlinear process obtained from the solution of (
1.1
). For any compact set
I\subset(0,\infty)
and any bounded set
B\subset Y
, we have

\sup_{t\in I}\ \sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\ \sup_{u^{\tau}\in B}\|S_{\varepsilon}(t+\tau,\tau)u^{\tau}-S_{0}(t+\tau,\tau)u^{\tau}\|_{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

3.
Estimates and rates of convergence

This section is dedicated to obtain estimates and rate of convergence for a series of linear operators, culminating with the proof of the Trotter-Kato type Approximation result for the linear process
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
to
U_{0}(t,\tau)
and the convergence of the solution for the semilinear problem. We shall assume during this entire section that Conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
and
(NL.1)
to
(NL.2)
hold.

3.1.
Resolvent convergence in
\mathcal{L}(X)
and
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)

We first estimate convergence of the resolvent of
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
to the resolvent of
A_{0}(t)
in terms of
\varepsilon
. As an immediate consequence of
(P.1)
and
(P.2)
, we obtain existence of a constant
C>0
(uniform in
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
) such that, for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
,
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.1)

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\ \mbox{ and }\ \|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\leq C.

Since
Y\hookrightarrow X
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for each
u\in Y
,

(3.2)

\|u\|_{X}\leq C\|u\|_{Y}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|I\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\leq C.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}u-A_{0}(t)^{-1}u\|_{X}\leq C\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}u-A_{0}(t)^{-1}u\|_{Y}\leq C\eta(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{X}

and

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}u-A_{0}(t)^{-1}u\|_{Y}\leq\eta(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{X}\leq C\eta(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{Y}.

Hence, we have the following estimates

(3.3)

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C\eta(\varepsilon)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\leq C\eta(\varepsilon)

From the resolvent equality and simple algebra we can prove that the following equalities hold, for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.4)

(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}

=A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}[A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}]A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1},

(3.5)

A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}

=-\lambda(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}A_{\varepsilon}(t)[A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}]A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}.

Expression (
3.4
) implicates that resolvent convergence inside the sector
\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
follows from the convergence of
A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, requested in
(P.5)
, as stated in next proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
or
t\in\mathbb{R}
, such that, for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta(\varepsilon).

Proof.

From the uniform estimate obtained in (
3.1
) for
A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
and
\mathcal{L}(Y)
and Equality (
3.4
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\eta(\varepsilon).

∎

Another estimate on the resolvent in terms of
\varepsilon
that will be useful in the sequel is presented next.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, such that, ,

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C|\lambda|\eta(\varepsilon),\quad\mbox{ for any }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

Proof.

It follows directly from the estimates (
3.1
) for
A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}
in
\mathcal{L}(X)
and
\mathcal{L}(Y)
, from (
3.2
) and from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}-A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|\lambda A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq|\lambda|\|I\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|A_{0}(t)(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C|\lambda|\eta(\varepsilon).

∎

Lastly on the linear operator and its resolvent, we provide an estimate for a situation where we vary both
\varepsilon
and time
t\in\mathbb{R}
simultaneously.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
be the Hölder continuity constant in
(P.3)
.
For any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for all
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0}),

(3.6)

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C|t-\tau|^{\delta(1-\theta)}[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}.

Proof.

From
(P.3)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle=\|[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-[A_{0}(\tau)-A_{0}(t)]A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq C|t-\tau|^{\delta}.

Now, interpolating (
3.7
) and the estimate in
(P.4)
with an exponent
\theta\in[0,1]
, we obtain (
3.6
).
∎

3.2.
Convergence and estimates for the semigroups

Since each operator
A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
is sectorial (with an uniform sector and uniform resolvent estimates in terms of
\varepsilon
and
\tau
), classical theory on semigroups implies that
-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
generates an analytic semigroup, which we denote by
T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(\cdot)
.

If
\Gamma
is the contour of
\Sigma_{\varphi}\subset\rho(-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))
, that is,
\Gamma=\{re^{-i\varphi}:r>0\}\cup\{re^{i\varphi}:r>0\}
and it is oriented with increasing imaginary part, then we have the following expressions

(3.8)

\displaystyle T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}d\lambda,

(3.9)

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}d\lambda,

that can be found in
[
18
, Section 2.5]
. A direct application of estimates (
2.1
) and (
3.1
) in Expressions (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) implies, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C,\quad\mbox{ for all }t\geq 0,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ct^{-1},\quad\mbox{ for all }t>0.

Uniformity of those estimates with respect to
\varepsilon
and
\tau
follows from
(P.1)
and
(P.2)
. We can also obtain an estimate for this semigroup in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, as stated next.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in
(P.2)
. There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, such that, for all
t>0
,

\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq Ct^{\beta-1}.

Proof.

Using estimate (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma}|e^{\lambda t}|\|(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}|d\lambda|\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{r[\cos\varphi]t}\frac{C}{1+r^{\beta}}dr

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{\beta-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{[\cos\varphi]u}\frac{1}{t^{\beta}+u^{\beta}}du=C(\varphi,\beta)t^{\beta-1},

where constant
C
depends on the angle
\varphi
and on
\beta
, but it is independent of
\varepsilon,\tau
and
t
.
∎

We establish next a convergence of the linear semigroups relative to
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in
(P.2)
. For any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, such that, for all
t>0
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ct^{-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq Ct^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.10
) that

(3.14)

\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C.

Consider the curve
\Gamma
parametrized as
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\vee\Gamma_{2}^{-}
where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda=re^{i\varphi};\ r\in[0,\infty)\},\quad\Gamma_{2}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda=re^{-i\varphi};\ r\in[0,\infty)\},

and
\Gamma_{2}^{-}
stands for the reverse path. Using the symmetry of curves
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
and estimate (
3.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|e^{\lambda t}|\|(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{0}(\tau))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}|d\lambda|

\displaystyle\leq C\eta(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{r[\cos\varphi]t}dr

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq C({\varphi})t^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)

Interpolating (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) with exponents
\theta
and
1-\theta
, for
\theta\in[0,1]
, we obtain (
3.12
). In order to estimate (
3.13
), we first note from Lemma
3.4
that

(3.16)

\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\leq Ct^{\beta-1}.

Using
(P.5)
and the integral formulation for the semigroup (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|e^{\lambda t}|\|(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(\tau))^{-1}-(\lambda+A_{0}(\tau))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}|d\lambda|

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varphi}t^{-1}\eta(\varepsilon)

Interpolating (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) with exponents
1-\theta
and
\theta
,
\theta\in[0,1]
, we obtain the desired estimate (
3.13
).

∎

We deduce in the sequel the last estimate on semigroup in terms of
\varepsilon
necessary to our future analysis.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists
C>0
such that, for all

and

\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ct^{-1-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}.

3.11

(3.18)

\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ct^{-1}.

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)-A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

3.3.
Convergence and estimates for the families
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)

In order to achieve our final goal of obtaining rate of convergence for the linear process associated to the family
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, we first need to establish rate of convergences for the auxiliary families
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
. Recall that

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau),

and it follows directly from
(P.3)
and (
3.11
) that

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\delta-1},\quad\mbox{ for any }t>\tau.

The rate of convergence required for the resolvent operators in Properties
(P.4)
and
(P.5)
are transfered to the families
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
as follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\delta
be the constant of Hölder continuity in
(P.3)
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
t>\tau
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\varphi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

where

(3.20)

\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)=\max\{[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}\}.

In particular,
\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
.

Proof.

Using the previous estimates and Expression (
2.7
) for the family
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\varphi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)-[A_{0}(\tau)-A_{0}(t)]A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)-A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|A_{0}(\tau)T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}+C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

since
-1+\delta(1-\theta)<-1+\delta-\theta<0.

∎

As far as estimates for the family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)
, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
be the constant of Hölder continuity in
(P.3)
. There exist constants
C,K>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>\tau
,

\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}.

Proof.

From previous estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\delta-1}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\delta-1}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds.

It follows from Gronwall’s inequality
[
15
, p.190]
that, for any
t>\tau
,

\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\frac{C}{\delta}(t-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)},

where
K>(2C\Gamma(\delta))^{\frac{1}{\delta}}
.
∎

We obtain in the sequel a rate of convergence for the family
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon
converges to zero. Unlike previous results, our auxiliary
\theta
that appears in the estimate needs to be in the open interval
(0,1)
instead of
[0,1]
in order to ensure convergence of integrals that feature in the estimates.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta
be the constant of Hölder continuity in
(P.3)
. There exists
C=C(\theta,\delta)>0
and
K=K(\delta)>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>\tau
, we have

(3.21)

\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

where
\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)
is given in (
3.20
).

Proof.

Using the estimates obtained earlier, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\varphi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)-\varphi_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\varphi_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)(s-\tau)^{\delta-1}ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\delta-1}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)+C(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1}\mathcal{B}(\delta(1-\theta),\delta)\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\delta-1}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)},

where
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Beta function. Taking
\psi(t)=\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}
, we restate the above inequality as

\psi(t)\leq C\left[(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}+(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1}\right]\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)+C\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{\delta-1}\psi(s)ds.

Applying the generalized version of Gronwall’s inequality
[
15
, p.190]
,

\psi(t)\leq C(\delta,\theta)\left[(t-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}+(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1}\right]\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)e^{{K}(t-\tau)},

for
K>(C\Gamma(\delta))^{\frac{1}{\delta}}.
Moreover, if

\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1>0

, then the growth provided by the term

(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1}

can be incorporated to the exponential term
e^{K(t-\tau)}
, correcting the constant if necessary. If

\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1<0

, then

(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)+\delta-1}\leq(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)-1}

for
t-\tau
near zero. In both cases, Inequality (
3.21
) follows from the above estimate.
∎

3.4.
Convergence and estimates for the linear process
U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)

Before we prove Theorem
2.3
, we obtain an estimate for the linear process that will be necessary.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in
(P.2)
. There exists
C,K>0
such that, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>\tau
,

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{K(t-\tau)},

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}.

Proof.

From previous estimates and the expression for the linear process in (
2.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(s-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(s-\tau)}ds\leq C+\frac{C}{\delta}(t-\tau)^{\delta}e^{K(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq C\max\{1,(t-\tau)^{\delta}\}e^{K(t-\tau)}\leq Ce^{K(t-\tau)},

where we incorporated
(t-\tau)^{\delta}
into the growth presented by
e^{K(t-\tau)}
, making adjustments in the constant
C
, if necessary. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\beta-1}(s-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(s-\tau)}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}+\frac{C}{\beta+\delta}(t-\tau)^{\beta+\delta-1}\mathcal{B}(\beta,\delta)e^{K(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{\delta}e^{K(t-\tau)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)},

also adjusting the constant
C
, if necessary.
∎

We are now able to prove Theorem
2.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
:

We first obtain the estimate in
\mathcal{L}(X)
using Expression (
2.6
) for the linear process and the estimates established previously.

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|[\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|[T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)-T_{A_{0}(s)}(t-s)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\|\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(s-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}e^{K(s-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)ds+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}(s-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(s-\tau)}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\theta}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}+\frac{C}{\delta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)(t-\tau)^{\delta(1-\theta)}+C[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}(t-\tau)^{\delta-\theta}\mathcal{B}(1-\theta,\delta)e^{K(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\theta,\delta)(t-\tau)^{-\theta}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon).

Proceeding similarly to the estimate in
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t-\tau)-T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t-\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|[\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau)-\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|[T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(s)}(t-s)-T_{A_{0}(s)}(t-s)]\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|\Phi_{0}(s,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\beta-1}(s-\tau)^{-1+\delta(1-\theta)}e^{K(s-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}(s-\tau)^{\delta-1}e^{K(s-\tau)}ds

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}+Ce^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)(t-\tau)^{\beta+\delta(1-\theta)-1}\mathcal{B}(\beta,\delta(1-\theta))

\displaystyle\qquad+C[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta}(t-\tau)^{\delta+\beta(1-\theta)-1}\mathcal{B}(\beta(1-\theta),\delta)e^{K(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\theta,\delta)(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon).

∎

3.5.
Convergence and estimates of the solution of the semilinear problem

As mentioned at Section
2
, Problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed, that is, there exists

u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})=U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u^{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s))ds,

that solves the problem for
t\in[\tau,\tau+T(\varepsilon,\tau,u^{\tau}))
. We actually have global well-posedness (
T(\varepsilon,\tau,u^{\tau})=\infty
) as a consequence of the following result.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1]
be the constant in the resolvent estimate (
2.3
). There exist constants
C,K>0
such that, for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
T>0
for which
u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\tau,u^{\tau})
is defined in
(\tau,\tau+T]
, we have

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})\|_{Y}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}[1+\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}],\qquad\mbox{for all }t\in(\tau,\tau+T].

In particular, the
\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{Y}
does not blow-up in any finite time interval and it is globally defined.

Proof.

This result follows from the expression for the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
and the estimates obtained previously.

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|u^{\tau}\|_{X}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s,\tau,u^{\tau})))\|_{X}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-s)}Mds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+\frac{CM}{\beta}(t-\tau)^{\beta}e^{K(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-\tau)}[1+\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}].

Therefore, the solution is bounded in any bounded interval
[\tau+m,\tau+T]
, for
0<m<T
, being globally defined.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2.5
that provides a rate at which the solutions converge.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
:
In the sequel we will denote the solution
u_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau,u^{\tau})
by
u_{\varepsilon}(t)
. Let
M>0,L>0
be the boundedness and Lipschitz constant for
F
, respectively, and

\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)=\max\{[\eta(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},[\xi(\varepsilon)]^{\theta},\gamma(\varepsilon)\}.

Using the expression for the solution and rates of convergence established earlier, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{0}(t)\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|u^{\tau}\|_{X}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)-U_{0}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\|F_{0}(s,u_{0}(s))\|_{X}ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\left[\|F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{\varepsilon}(s))-F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{0}(s))\|_{X}+\|F_{\varepsilon}(s,u_{0}(s))-F_{0}(s,u_{0}(s))\|_{X}\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-s)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)Mds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(t-s)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-s)}\left[L\|u_{\varepsilon}(s)-u_{0}(s)\|_{Y}+\gamma(\varepsilon)\right]ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+\frac{CM}{\beta(1-\theta)}(t-\tau)^{\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-s)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{\beta}e^{K(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{\beta}\gamma(\varepsilon)+CL\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-s)}\|u_{\varepsilon}(s)-u_{0}(s)\|_{Y}ds

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}e^{K(t-\tau)}\left[\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+1\right]\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)+CL\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}e^{K(t-s)}\|u_{\varepsilon}(s)-u_{0}(s)\|_{Y}ds,

where we incorporated the terms
(t-\tau)
with a positive exponent to the exponential growth given by
e^{K(t-\tau)}
, making adjustments in the constant
C
, if necessary. Multiplying both sides by
e^{-K(t-\tau)}
and considering
\Psi(t)=e^{-K(t-\tau)}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{0}(t)\|_{Y}
, we obtain

\Psi(t)\leq C(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+1]\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)+CL\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}\Psi(s)ds.

We now apply Gronwall’s inequality
[
15
, p.190]
to conclude that

\Psi(t)\leq\frac{C}{\beta(1-\beta)}(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+1]\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)e^{\tilde{K}(t-\tau)},

where
\tilde{K}>(2CL\Gamma(\beta))^{\frac{1}{\beta}}
. Therefore,

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{0}(t)\|_{Y}\leq\frac{C}{\beta(1-\beta)}(t-\tau)^{-1+\beta(1-\theta)}[\|u^{\tau}\|_{Y}+1]\rho(\theta,\varepsilon)e^{(\tilde{K}+K)(t-\tau)}

and

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)-u_{0}(t)\|_{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

uniformly for
t
in compact subsets of
(\tau,\infty)
, any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
u^{\tau}
in bounded sets of
Y
.

∎

4.
Application to reaction-diffusion equations with varying diffusion coefficients

As a first application of the abstract theory developed in the previous sections, we consider a family in
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
of singularly nonautonomous reaction-diffusion equation in a bounded smooth domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

(4.1)

\begin{split}&(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}-div(a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+u_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}),\hskip 31.2982ptx\in\Omega,t>\tau,\\
&\partial_{n}u_{\varepsilon}=0,\hskip 167.87108ptx\in\partial\Omega,\\
&u_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)=u^{\tau}(x).\end{split}

An autonomous version (where
a_{\varepsilon}
and
f_{\varepsilon}
do not depend on
t
) was completely studied in
[
3
]
and the authors obtained rate of convergence of solutions and attractors in terms of
\varepsilon
. The nonautonomous counterpart (
4.1
) was introduced in
[
6
]
, where the authors studied global well-posedness and existence of pullback attractor, but for a single equations rather than a family of equations parametrized in
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.

We shall apply the abstract theory developed in Section
2
in order to obtain a rate at which solutions of (
4.1
) converge as
{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}
. We assume the following conditions for the problem:

(A.1)

The functions
a_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
are continuously differentiable with respect to the second variable, and
a_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
has its image in a closed interval
[m,M]\subset(0,\infty)
. We also assume that the gradient function (in
x
) of
a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
is bounded, that is,
\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\in[L^{\infty}({\Omega})]^{3}
.

(A.2)

Both functions
a_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
are uniformly
\delta-
Hölder continuous in the first variable
that is, there exists
\delta\in(0,1]
and a constant
C>0
such that

|a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|\leq C|t-s|^{\delta},\quad|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(s,x)|\leq C|t-s|^{\delta},

for all
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\Omega
.

(A.3)

For each
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and satisfies a
polynomial growth condition of order
\rho
, that is, there exists
C
and
1\leq\rho<3
such that

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)-f_{\varepsilon}(t,\psi)|

\displaystyle\leq C|\xi-\psi|(1+|\xi|^{\rho-1}+|\psi|^{\rho-1}),

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\xi|^{\rho}).

(A.4)

We define the quantities

\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|a_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-a_{0}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-\nabla_{x}a_{0}(t,\cdot)\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{3}},

\|f_{\varepsilon}-f_{0}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)-f_{0}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

and we assume that each one of them varies continuously on
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. In particular,they approach to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

The upper bound requested for
\rho
in
(A.3)
will become clear after we specify the phase space in which we pose the problem. Under conditions above, we write Problem (
4.1
) in its abstract form as follows: the linear part of the equation (which is time-dependent) is given by the operator

A_{\varepsilon}(t):D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
where

\displaystyle D=D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))=\left\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega):\partial_{n}u=0\mbox{ in }\partial\Omega\right\}=:H^{2}_{\mathcal{N}},

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046ptA_{\varepsilon}(t)u=-div(a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u)+u,\mbox{ for }u\in D.

This family of linear operators has well-known properties that we gather in the sequel. They follow from classical spectral theory (see
[
5
,
16
,
18
]
) and from the properties required upon
a_{\varepsilon}
in
(A.1)
and
(A.2)
. To simplify notation, we shall omit the domain
\Omega
in the space norms, that is,
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

This family
\{A_{\varepsilon}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}
has the following properties:

(1)

D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))
does not depend on
t
or
\varepsilon
. Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the graph norm
\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
is equivalent to
H^{2}(\Omega)-
norm when restricted to
D
, that is, for any
u\in D
,

C_{1}\|u\|_{H^{2}}\leq\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{2}\|u\|_{H^{2}},

and constants
C_{1},C_{2}
are uniform for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

A_{\varepsilon}(t)
is self-adjoint and has compact resolvent.

(3)

Its spectrum consists entirely of isolated eigenvalues, all of them positive and real, with the first being
1
:

\sigma(A_{\varepsilon}(t))=\{\lambda_{\varepsilon,i}(t);\ i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\mbox{ and }1=\lambda_{\varepsilon,1}(t)\leq\lambda_{\varepsilon,2}(t)\leq...\leq\lambda_{\varepsilon,n}(t)\leq...\}.

(4)

For any
\frac{\pi}{2}<\varphi<\pi
,
\Sigma_{\varphi}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};|\arg\lambda|\leq\varphi\right\}\subset\rho(-A_{\varepsilon}(t))
and

\displaystyle\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|+1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\},

\displaystyle\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|+1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\},

\displaystyle\|(\lambda I+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}+1},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\},

where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
t
(only on
\varphi
).

We restate Problem (
4.1
) as an abstract semilinear evolution problem:

(4.2)

\begin{split}&(u_{\varepsilon})_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u_{\varepsilon}=F_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}),\quad t>\tau,\\
&u_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=u^{\tau}\in H^{1}(\Omega),\end{split}

where
F_{\varepsilon}
is a nonlinearity given by

F_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon})(x)=f_{\varepsilon}(t,u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)).

Since

H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),\mbox{ for all }2\leq r<6,

then the growth condition
(A.3)
required for
f_{\varepsilon}
implies that
F:\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, as long as
1\leq\rho<3
. With the notation of Section
2
,
L^{2}(\Omega)
will play the role of Banach space
X
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
the Banach space
Y
. Moreover, one can easily check that from
(A.3)
we derive, for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)-F_{\varepsilon}(t,v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u-v\|_{H^{1}}\left[1+\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|^{\rho-1}_{H^{1}}\right],

\displaystyle\|F_{\varepsilon}(t,u)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left[1+\|u\|^{\rho}_{H^{1}}\right].

In order to apply the theory developed in Section
2
, we first need to verify that
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
hold for (
4.2
). From Proposition
4.1
, properties

(P.1)
and
(P.2)
already follow. Property
(P.3)
is proved in next lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
(A.1)
and
(A.2)
hold and let
\delta\in(0,1]
be the uniform Hölder exponent for
t\mapsto a_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
and
t\mapsto\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
or
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, such that, for any
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in[0,1]
, the function

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega))

is Hölder continuous with exponent
\delta
, that is,

\|[A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)-A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s)]A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\leq C|t-s|^{\delta},\quad\mbox{for all }\tau,s,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

For any
u\in D
, we have

A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)u-A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s)u=-div\left([a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)]\nabla u\right)

and

\begin{split}\|A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)u-A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s)u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\int_{\Omega}\left|div([a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)]\nabla u(x))\right|^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla_{x}([a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)])\nabla u(x)+[a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)]\Delta u(x)\right|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C|t-s|^{2\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\left|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)\right|}{|t-s|^{\delta}}\right\}^{2}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\\
&\qquad+C|t-s|^{2\delta}\int_{\Omega}\left\{\frac{\left|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(s,x)\right|}{|t-s|^{\delta}}\right\}^{2}|\Delta u(x)|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C|t-s|^{2\delta}\left\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\}\leq C|t-s|^{2\delta}\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Taking the square root on both sides and replacing
u
by
A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w
, then we have for any
w\in L^{2}(\Omega)

\|[{A_{\varepsilon_{1}}}(t)-{A_{\varepsilon_{1}}}(s)]A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|t-s|^{\delta}\|A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{H^{2}}\leq C|t-s|^{\delta}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

It remains to check the properties responsible to make the connections among the problems as
\varepsilon
varies in
[0,1]
. Those are conditions
(P.4)
and
(P.5)
from Section
2
. We start verifying
(P.4)
and we begin by proving an auxiliary result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in[0,1]
and
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\|[A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)-A_{0}(t)]A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Proof.

Take
u=A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w\in D
, where
w
is any element in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We have

\displaystyle\|[A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)-A_{0}(t)]u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|div[(a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{0}(t,x))\nabla u]|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}[|\nabla_{x}(a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{0}(t,x))|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t,x)-a_{0}(t,x)|^{2}|\Delta u|^{2}]dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-a_{0}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2})\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|[A_{\varepsilon_{1}}(t)-A_{0}(t)]A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty})\|A_{\varepsilon_{2}}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty})\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

With the previous lemma, we are now able to prove that
(P.4)
holds for this problem.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. There exists
C>0
independent of
t,\tau,\varepsilon
such that

\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}{(L^{2})}}\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Proof.

Let
w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and consider
u=A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}w
,
v=A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}w
. It follows from the boundedness of
a_{\varepsilon}
and its convergence to

that

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)u-A_{0}(t)v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|div[a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u]-div[a_{0}(t,x)\nabla v]|^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega}|u-v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|div[(a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-a_{0}(t,x))\nabla u]+div[a_{0}(t,x)(\nabla u-\nabla v)]|^{2}dx+C\int_{\Omega}|u-v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\scalebox{1.5}{$\{$}|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-\nabla_{x}a_{0}(t,x)|^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+|a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-a_{0}(t,x)|^{2}|\Delta u|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 49.79231pt+|\nabla_{x}a_{0}(t,x)|^{2}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}+|a_{0}(t,x)|^{2}|\Delta(u-v)|^{2}\scalebox{1.5}{$\}$}dx+C\int_{\Omega}|u-v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)+C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\right)+C\|u-v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2})\|u\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+C\|u-v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

From the choice of

and from Lemma

we obtain

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}w-A_{0}(t)A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2})\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}w\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+C\|A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)^{-1}w-A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2})\|w\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+C\|[A_{0}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(t)]A_{0}(\tau)^{-1}w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2C(\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2})\|w\|^{2}_{H^{2}}.

∎

Therefore, Lemma

states that

holds for

\xi(\varepsilon)=C(\|a_{\varepsilon_{1}}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{x}a_{\varepsilon}-\nabla_{x}a_{0}\|_{\infty}).

Inspired in

3
]

Lemma 4.5
.

Given
g\in X=L^{2}(\Omega)
, a fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, there exists a unique
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{2}_{\mathcal{N}}
solution of

(4.3)

\begin{cases}-div(a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+u_{\varepsilon}=g,\quad x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{n}u_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Moreover,

(1)

there exists
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

(2)

There is also a constant
C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Existence of
u_{\varepsilon}
that solves (
4.3
) follows from the fact that
0\in\rho(A_{\varepsilon}(t))
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. To prove the first statement, we consider the weak formulation of (
4.3
):

\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\varphi=\int_{\Omega}g\varphi,\quad\mbox{ for }\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

By taking
\varphi=u_{\varepsilon}
, using Young’s inequality and the fact that
a_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)\subset[m,M]
, we obtain, for any
\nu>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}+\int_{\Omega}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}=\int_{\Omega}gu_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle m\int_{\Omega}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}+\int_{\Omega}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}|g||u_{\varepsilon}|\leq\int_{\Omega}\left[\left\{\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\frac{|g|^{2}}{2}\right\}+\left\{\frac{\nu^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\right\}\right],

\displaystyle m\int_{\Omega}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}+\left(1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}[u_{\varepsilon}]^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|g|^{2}.

Choosing
\nu
small such that
1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}>0
we obtain

(4.4)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
C=\frac{1}{2\nu^{2}\min\{m,1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}\}}
, which does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
t
.

For the second statement we proceed similarly. Rather than taking
u_{\varepsilon}
as a test function, we choose
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)=\int_{\Omega}g\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(t,x)\nabla u_{0}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)=\int_{\Omega}g\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right).

Equality on the right side implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(t,x)\nabla u_{0}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)+\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)+\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a_{0}(t,x)\nabla u_{0}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right).

We now subtract
\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\nabla u_{0}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)
on both sides, which results

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}[a_{0}(t,x)-a_{\varepsilon}(t,x)]\nabla u_{0}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

If
\frac{1}{C}=\min\{m,1\}
, we obtain from the above inequality and using (
4.4
),

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{2}\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{2}\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Therefore,

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

∎

As an immediate consequence of the previous result, we have the following corollary.

.

The operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to H^{1}(\Omega)
are uniformly bounded for

and
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and they converge to

in the uniform topology. More precisely, for all
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|A_{\varepsilon}(t)^{-1}-A_{0}(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty},

where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
or
t
.

Inequality (

) is the statement required in
(P.5)
, with rate of convergence

given by

\eta(\varepsilon):=C\|a_{\varepsilon}-a_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Since

to

are satisfied, we conclude that each family of linear operators

generates a linear process

and from Theorem

, we obtain that there exist
C,K>0
such that, for any

,

and

,

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\theta}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

\displaystyle\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-U_{0}(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2},H^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\varepsilon),

where

.

4.1.
Local well-posedness, global well-posedness and convergence of the solutions

The results on local and global well-posedness that we present in the sequel can be found in
[
6

ensure that problem (

To obtain global well-posedness, we assume that

\limsup_{|s|\rightarrow\infty}\left[sup_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}\dfrac{f_{\varepsilon}(t,s)}{s}\right]<1,

,

.

.

[

Assume that

5.
Application to a nonautonomous strongly damped wave equations and its fractional approximations

As a second application, we consider the nonautonomous strongly damped wave equation subjected to Dirichlet boundary conditions

(5.1)

\begin{split}&u_{tt}+(-a(t)\Delta_{D})u+2(-a(t)\Delta_{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{t}=f(t,u),\hskip 28.45274ptx\in\Omega,\ t>\tau,\\
&u(t,x)=0,\hskip 182.09746ptx\in\partial\Omega,\ t\geq\tau,\\
&u(\tau,x)=u^{\tau}(x),\ u_{t}(\tau,x)=v^{\tau}(x),\hskip 84.78908ptx\in\overline{\Omega},\ \tau\in\mathbb{R},\end{split}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, is a bounded smooth domain,
\Delta_{D}
is the Laplacian operator with Dirichlet boundary condition and
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a nonlinearity. We shall assume the following additional condition:

(B)

The function

is positive and has its image in a bounded interval of the form
[a_{0},a_{1}]\subset(0,\infty)
. We also assume that it is Hölder continuous with an exponent
\delta\in(0,1]
, that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|a(t)-a(s)|\leq C|t-s|^{\delta},\quad\mbox{for all }t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
E=L^{2}(\Omega)
and denote by
A(t):D(A(t))\subset E\to E
the operator

(5.2)

A(t)u=-a(t)\Delta_{D}u,\quad\mbox{for}\quad u\in D(A(t))=D(\Delta_{D})=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
D(\Delta_{D})
stands for the domain of the Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions. As expected, the multiplication by a real-function
a(t)
does not change the domain of the Laplacian.

Therefore, this linear operator
A(t)
has a time-independent domain and from the well-known properties of the Laplacian operator
[
18
]
and the fact that
a(t)\geq a_{0},
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we deduce that
A(t)
is a positive operator, self-adjoint, sectorial and
-A(t)
generates a compact analytic
C_{0}-
semigroup in
E
.

Consequently, fractional powers of
A(t)
in the sense of Amman
[
1
]
are well-defined. We shall denote by
A(t)^{\alpha}
the power of the linear operator
A(t)
. One can easily deduce from the expressions for fractional power of linear operators that

A(t)^{\alpha}=(-a(t)\Delta_{D})^{\alpha}=[a(t)]^{\alpha}(-\Delta_{D})^{\alpha},\quad\mbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }\alpha\in(0,1],

and the domain of
A(t)^{\alpha}
is the same as the domain of
(-\Delta_{D})^{\alpha}
, that is,

D(A(t)^{\alpha})=D((-\Delta)^{\alpha}),\quad\mbox{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

We then define a scale of Banach spaces given by the fractional powers
(-\Delta_{D})^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

E^{\alpha}=(-\Delta_{D})^{\alpha}\quad\mbox{eqquiped with the norm }\|\cdot\|_{E^{\alpha}}=\|(-\Delta_{D})^{\alpha}\cdot\|_{L^{2}}.

In particular,
E^{0}=E=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
E^{\frac{1}{2}}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
E^{1}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
(see
[
13
]
Theorem 3.6). From the boundedness of
a(\cdot)
, there exists
0<m<M
such that
[a(t)]^{\alpha}\in[m,M]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Therefore,

m\|u\|_{E^{\alpha}}\leq\|A(t)^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq M\|u\|_{E^{\alpha}}

and the graph norm associated to the linear operator
A(t)^{\alpha}
is equivalent to the norm in
E^{\alpha}
. With the above set up and taking
u_{t}=v
, Problem (
5.1
) can be written in the following abstract form

(5.3)

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}+\Lambda(t)\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}=F\left(t,\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}$}\right),\ t>\tau;\qquad\begin{bmatrix}u(\tau)\\
v(\tau)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u^{\tau}\\
v^{\tau}\end{bmatrix}\in E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E,

where
\Lambda(t):D(\Lambda(t))\subset E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E\to E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E
is the linear operator defined in
D(\Lambda(t))=D=E^{1}\times E^{\frac{1}{2}}
and given by

(5.4)

\Lambda(t)\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-I\\
A(t)&2A(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-v\\
A(t)u+2A(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}v\end{bmatrix},

and
F
is the nonlinearity given by

F\left(t,\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}u\\
v\end{bmatrix}$}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
f(t,u)\end{bmatrix}.

We have the following result proved in
[
7
, Lemma 8.1]
concerning spectral properties of
\Lambda(t)
and the calculus of its fractional powers.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
A(t)
and
\Lambda(t)
are as in (
5.2
) and in (
5.4
), respectively, then the following properties hold:

(1)

\Lambda(t)
is a positive operator with fractional powers denoted by
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

(2)

There exists
C>0
and
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
(independent of
\alpha
) such that, for any
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}\subset\rho(-\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}),

and the following estimates hold

\|(\lambda+\Lambda(t)^{\alpha})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)}\leq\frac{C}{1+|\lambda|},\quad\mbox{ for all }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

Therefore, each
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
is sectorial in
E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E
and
-\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
generates an analytic semigroup in
\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)
.

(3)

Given any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we have the following explicitly expression for the fractional powers of
\Lambda(t)
:

(5.5)

\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\alpha)A(t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}&-\alpha A(t)^{\frac{-1+\alpha}{2}}\\
\alpha A(t)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}&(1+\alpha)A(t)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda(t)^{-\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}(1+\alpha)A(t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}&\alpha A(t)^{\frac{-1-\alpha}{2}}\\
-\alpha A(t)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}&(1-\alpha)A(t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\end{bmatrix}

We shall consider fractional versions of Problem (
5.3
), given by

(5.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}u_{\alpha}\\
v_{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}+\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}\begin{bmatrix}u_{\alpha}\\
v_{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}=F_{\alpha}\left(t,\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}u_{\alpha}\\
v_{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}$}\right),\ t>\tau;\qquad\begin{bmatrix}u_{\alpha}(\tau)\\
v_{\alpha}(\tau)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}u^{\tau}\\
v^{\tau}\end{bmatrix}\in E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E,

where

\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
is the fractional power of
\Lambda(t)
and

F_{\alpha}\left(t,\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{bmatrix}u_{\alpha}\\
v_{\alpha}\end{bmatrix}$}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
f_{\alpha}(t,u_{\alpha})\end{bmatrix}.

By analyzing Expression (
5.5
) for the linear operator
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
, we see that
\alpha=1
recovers the original expression for
\Lambda(t)
, so we might expect that as we make
\alpha\to 1^{-}
, the fractional problem (
5.6
) approaches (
5.3
) in a certain sense. We shall verify that this is the case, that is, we prove in the sequel that conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
hold for this family (in
\alpha
) of problems. Therefore, if
U_{\alpha}(t,\tau)
is the linear process associated to
\{\Lambda(t)^{\alpha},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, then we obtain its convergence to the linear process
U(t,\tau)
associated to
\{\Lambda(t),t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

A slightly different singularly nonautonomous wave equation and its fractional perturbations were also considered in
[
17
]
. However, the lack of results on the linear process convergence prevented the authors to proceed with the analysis beyond spectrum convergence of the fractional operators. They were not able to obtain convergence of the associated linear processes or of the solutions, as we shall do in the sequel.

Using the notation developed in Section
2
, we will consider
Y=X=E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E
. Conditions
(P.1)
and
(P.2)
follows directly from Proposition
5.1
. For condition
(P.3)
, we shall need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
a(\cdot)
be the function satisfying
(B)
. For each
\omega>0
, the functions
[a(\cdot)]^{\omega}
and
[a(\cdot)]^{-\omega}
are also Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent
\delta
, that is, for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
,

|[a(t)]^{\omega}-[a(s)]^{\omega}|\leq C_{1}|t-s|^{\delta}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|[a(t)]^{-\omega}-[a(s)]^{-\omega}|\leq C_{2}|t-s|^{\delta}.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be given by
\phi(s)=s^{\omega}
. From the mean value theorem and the fact that
a(t)\in[a_{0},a_{1}]
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain, for some
\theta
between
a(s)
and
a(t)
, in particular
\theta\in[a_{0},a_{1}]
, that

\begin{split}|[a(t)]^{\omega}-[a(s)]^{\omega}|=|\phi(a(t))-\phi(a(s))|\leq|\phi^{\prime}(\theta)||a(t)-a(s)|\leq\theta^{\omega-1}|a(t)-a(s)|\leq C_{1}|t-s|^{\delta}\end{split},

for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,

|[a(t)]^{-\omega}-[a(s)]^{-\omega}|=\left|\frac{[a(s)]^{\omega}-[a(t)]^{\omega}}{[a(t)]^{\omega}[a(s)]^{\omega}}\right|\leq C|[a(s)]^{\omega}-[a(t)]^{\omega}|\leq C_{2}|t-s|^{\delta},\quad\mbox{ for all }t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

∎

Condition
(P.3)
can now be verified.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
(B)
holds and let
\Lambda(t)
be the linear operator in (
5.4
). If
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
denotes the fractional powers of
\Lambda(t)
, then
\mathbb{R}\ni t\to\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau)^{-\alpha}\in\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)
is
\delta-
Hölder continuous, uniformly in
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. In other words, there exists
C>0
such that

\|[\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}-\Lambda(s)^{\alpha}]\Lambda(\tau)^{-\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)}\leq C|t-s|^{\delta},\quad\mbox{ for all }t,s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ and }\alpha\in(0,1].

Proof.

Applying expression (
5.5
) for the fractional powers, we deduce that

[\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}-\Lambda(s)^{\alpha}]\Lambda(\tau)^{-\alpha}=\begin{bmatrix}\Theta_{11}&\Theta_{11}\\
\Theta_{21}&\Theta_{22}\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{11}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+(1-\alpha^{2})\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}},

\displaystyle\Theta_{12}

\displaystyle=\alpha(1-\alpha)(-\Delta_{D})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-1-\alpha}{2}}+\left([a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}-[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\right\},

\displaystyle\Theta_{21}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(1+\alpha)(-\Delta_{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+\left([a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\right\},

\displaystyle\Theta_{22}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha-1}{2}}+(\alpha+1)(\alpha-1)\left([a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}.

We must obtain Hölder continuity of each entry in its appropriate space, that is,
\Theta_{11}
in
\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}})
,
\Theta_{12}
in
\mathcal{L}(E,E^{\frac{1}{2}})
,
\Theta_{21}
in
\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}},E)
and
\Theta_{22}
in
\mathcal{L}(E)
. All of them are similar and follows from Lemma
5.2
. We illustrate how to proceed with
\Theta_{21}
.

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{21}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}},E)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Theta_{21}(-\Delta_{D})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E)}\leq\alpha(1+\alpha)\left(|[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}|a_{1}^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}+|[a(s)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}-[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}|a_{0}^{\frac{-\alpha}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{21}|t-s|^{\delta}.

The other entries follow analogously.
∎

In a similar way, from the Expressions (
5.5
) for the fractional powers, we can deduce property
(P.4)
for the family of linear operators
\{\Lambda(t)^{\alpha},\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}_{\alpha\in(0,1]}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
(B)
holds and let
\Lambda(t)
be the linear operator in (
5.4
) and
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
its fractional powers. There exists a continuous function
\xi:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\xi(1)=0
such that

(5.7)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau)^{-\alpha}-\Lambda(t)\Lambda(\tau)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)}\leq\xi(\alpha).

Proof.

Applying expressions (
5.5
) for the fractional powers, we deduce

\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}\Lambda(\tau)^{-\alpha}-\Lambda(t)\Lambda(\tau)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma_{11}&\Gamma{12}\\
\Gamma{21}&\Gamma{22}\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{11}=(1-\alpha^{2})[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}[a(\tau)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}+(\alpha^{2}-1)[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}[a(\tau)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\left([a(\tau)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}-[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{12}=(1-\alpha)\alpha[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-\alpha-1}{2}}\left([a(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}-[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)(-\Delta_{D})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{21}=\alpha(1+\alpha)(-\Delta_{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(a(t)\left\{[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}-\frac{2}{\alpha(1+\alpha)}\right\}+\left\{\frac{2}{\alpha(1+\alpha)}-[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}\right\}\right),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{22}=[a(t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}[a(\tau)]^{\frac{-1-\alpha}{2}}\left((1+\alpha^{2})[a(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}-\alpha^{2}[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)-[a(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}[a(\tau)]^{-1}\left(2[a(t)]^{\frac{1}{2}}-[a(\tau)]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Note that each entry goes to zero continuously as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
. The presence of
(-\Delta_{D})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
in
\Gamma_{12}
or
(-\Delta_{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
\Gamma_{21}
do not represent any problem in the estimates. Actually, since
\Gamma_{12}
is estimated in
\mathcal{L}(E,E^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\Gamma_{21}
in
\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}},E)
we have

\|\Gamma_{12}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,E^{\frac{1}{2}})}=\|(-\Delta_{D})^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma_{12}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E)}\quad\mbox{and }\quad\|\Gamma_{21}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}},E)}=\|\Gamma_{12}(-\Delta_{D})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E)}

and those powers of the Laplacian disappear when we estimate those terms. Therefore, there exists a continuous function
\xi:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\xi(1)=0
such that (
5.7
) holds.
∎

Even though we are able to prove that
(P.4)
holds, we cannot obtain an explicit formulation for
\xi
in (
5.7
), since it depends on the expression of
a(\cdot)
. Lastly, Condition
(P.5)
holds following the same proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

[
7
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
\Lambda(t)
be the linear operator in (
5.4
) and
\Lambda(t)^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
its fractional. There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\alpha
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, such that

\|\Lambda(t)^{-\alpha}-\Lambda(t)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)}\leq C(1-\alpha).

Since conditions
(P.1)
to
(P.5)
hold, we have the following result, which is a restatement of Theorem
2.3
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\{U(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E),\ t\geq\tau\}
be the linear process associated to
\{\Lambda(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
\{U_{\alpha}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E),\ t\geq\tau\}
the linear process associated to
\{\Lambda(t)^{\alpha},\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}
,
{\alpha\in(0,1]}
. For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists constants
K,C>0
, independent of
\alpha\in{(0,1]}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>\tau
, such that

\|U_{\alpha}(t,\tau)-U(t,\tau)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E^{\frac{1}{2}}\times E)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{-\theta}e^{K(t-\tau)}\ell(\theta,\alpha),

where
\ell(\theta,\alpha)=\max\{(1-\alpha)^{\theta},[\xi(\alpha)]^{\theta}\}
.

Under assumptions on boundedness and Lipschitz continuity for the family of nonlinearities
f_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\times R\to\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, as well as some convergence assumption of
f_{\alpha}
to
f
as

, we derive conditions
(NL.1)
and
(NL.2)

u_{\alpha}(t,\tau,[u^{\tau},v^{\tau}])
of Problem (
5.6

u(t,\tau,[u^{\tau},v^{\tau}])
of Problem (
5.3
) in

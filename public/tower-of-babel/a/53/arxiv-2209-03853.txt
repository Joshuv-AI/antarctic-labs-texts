Siarhei Finski

Abstract.
The main goal of this article is to study for a projective manifold and an ample line bundle over it the relation between metric and algebraic structures on the associated section ring.

More precisely, we prove that once the kernel is factored out, the multiplication operator of the section ring becomes an approximate isometry (up to a normalization) with respect to the
L^{2}
-norms and the induced Hermitian tensor product norm.
We also show that the analogous result holds for the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms if instead of the Hermitian tensor product norm, we consider the projective and injective tensor norms induced by
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms respectively.

Then we show that
L^{2}
-norms associated with continuous plurisubharmonic metrics are actually characterized by the multiplicativity properties of this type.
Using this, we refine the theorem of Phong-Sturm about quantization of Mabuchi geodesics from the weaker level of Fubini-Study convergence to the stronger level of norm equivalences.

Introduction

Many questions from algebraic and complex geometry can be restated in terms of some problems related to the study of the section ring.
For example, finite generation of the canonical ring is of great importance to the minimal model program,
[
7
]
.
The K-stability condition, famously known for its connection with the existence of the constant scalar curvature metrics, is related to the asymptotic study of certain filtrations on the section ring
[
42
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
10
]
.

In this article, we propose to study further properties of the section ring; now in connection with the metric structure of the manifold.

More precisely, we fix a complex projective manifold
X
of dimension
n
and an ample line bundle
L
over
X
.
We consider the
section ring

R(X,L)
, defined as follows

R(X,L)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\oplus_{k=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(X,L^{k}).

(1.1)

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, we define the multiplication map

{\rm{Mult}}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{0}(X,L^{k_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{k_{r}})\to H^{0}(X,L^{k}),

(1.2)

as
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{r}\mapsto f_{1}\cdots f_{r}
.
Those multiplication maps endow

with the structure of a graded ring.
It follows from the
{\rm{Proj}}
-construction that the (graded) ring structure of

carries the same amount of information as the pair
(X,L)
.

We now fix a positive Hermitian metric
h^{L}
on
L
.
This means that for the curvature
R^{L}
of the Chern connection on
(L,h^{L})
, the following closed real
(1,1)
-differential form is positive

\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=c_{1}(L,h^{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}.

(1.3)

For smooth sections
f,f^{\prime}
of
L^{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
L^{2}
-Hermitian product using the pointwise Hermitian products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
on
L^{p}
, induced by
h^{L}
, as follows

\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}(X,h^{L})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{X}\langle f(x),f^{\prime}(x)\rangle_{h}dv_{X}(x),

(1.4)

where
dv_{X}=\frac{1}{n!}\omega^{n}
.
We also denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{p}(X,h^{L})}
the associated norm.
We, finally, denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{p}(X,h^{L})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{p}(X,h^{L})}
the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms, defined as follows

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{p}(X,h^{L})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\int_{X}|f|_{h}dv_{X}(x),\qquad\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{p}(X,h^{L})}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|_{h}.

(1.5)

When restricted to the vector space of holomorphic sections,
H^{0}(X,L^{p})
, we sometimes denote the
L^{2}
-norm by
{\rm{Hilb}}_{p}(h^{L})
, and the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms by
{\rm{Ban}}_{p}^{1}(h^{L})
,
{\rm{Ban}}_{p}^{\infty}(h^{L})
.

Over
R(X,L)
, for
q=1,\infty
, we define the induced
graded
norms

{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}),\qquad{\rm{Ban}}^{q}(h^{L})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{q}(h^{L}).

(1.6)

The first goal of this paper is to study the metric properties of
(R(X,L),{\rm{Ban}}^{1}(h^{L}))
,
(R(X,L),{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L}))
and
(R(X,L),{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}))
in their relation with the multiplication map.
Roughly, our first result states that one can inductively recover the approximate metric structure of the section ring from its multiplicative structure.

To explain this more precisely, let us recall some basic definitions from the theory of normed vector spaces.
Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be two finitely dimensional vector spaces endowed with norms
N_{i}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{i}
,
i=1,2
.
There are several natural constructions of a norm on the tensor product
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
.

Recall that the
projective norm

N_{1}\otimes_{\pi}N_{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{N_{1}\otimes_{\pi}N_{2}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\otimes_{\pi}}
on
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
is defined as

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\otimes_{\pi}}=\inf\Big\{\sum\|x_{i}\|_{1}\cdot\|y_{i}\|_{2};\quad f=\sum x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\Big\},

(1.7)

where the infimum is taken over different ways of partitioning
f
into a sum of decomposable terms.

Recall that the
injective norm

N_{1}\otimes_{\epsilon}N_{2}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{N_{1}\otimes_{\epsilon}N_{2}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\otimes_{\epsilon}}
on
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
is defined as

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\otimes_{\epsilon}}=\sup\Big\{\big|(\phi\otimes\psi)(f)\big|;\quad\phi\in V_{1}^{*},\psi\in V_{2}^{*},\|\phi\|_{1}^{*}=\|\psi\|_{2}^{*}=1\Big\}

(1.8)

where
\|\cdot\|_{i}^{*}
,
i=1,2
, are the dual norms associated to
\|\cdot\|_{i}
.

When both
\|\cdot\|_{i}
are induced by Hermitian products (then we say that
\|\cdot\|_{i}
are Hermitian norms), we will denote by
N_{1}\otimes N_{2}=\|\cdot\|_{N_{1}\otimes N_{2}}=\|\cdot\|_{\otimes}
the
Hermitian tensor product norm
induced by the product of the respective Hermitian products.
See Appendix
B
for relations between the above tensor norms and some of their basic properties, used sometimes implicitly below.

A norm
N_{V}=\|\cdot\|_{V}
on a finitely dimensional vector space
V
naturally induces the norm
\|\cdot\|_{Q}
on any quotient
Q
,
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to Q
of
V
through the following identity

\|f\|_{Q}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf\big\{\|g\|_{V};\quad g\in V,\pi(g)=f\},\qquad f\in Q.

(1.9)

By a slight abuse of notations, we sometimes denote the quotient norm by
[N_{V}]
, i.e. without the reference to the quotient space.
This will not cause any trouble as the quotient will be explicit.

Now, it is standard that there is
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

Our first main result shows that the induced isomorphism quotient map is an approximate isometry (up to a multiplication by a constant) with respect to the

More precisely, in Section

Theorem 1.1

There are

H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})

1-C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\leq\frac{[{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\otimes{\rm{Hilb}}_{l}(h^{L})]}{{\rm{Hilb}}_{k+l}(h^{L})}\cdot\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{k+l}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq 1+C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big).

(1.10)

Similarly, for
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms the analogous results hold. There, instead of Hermitian tensor product norm, one needs to consider the projective and injective tensor product norms respectively.
More precisely, in the notations of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{n}}\cdot\Big(1-C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[{\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{1}(h^{L})\otimes_{\pi}{\rm{Ban}}_{l}^{1}(h^{L})]}{{\rm{Ban}}_{k+l}^{1}(h^{L})}\cdot\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{k+l}\Big)^{n}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n}}

(1.11)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{n}\cdot\Big(1+C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\Big),

\displaystyle 1-C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[{\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(h^{L})\otimes_{\epsilon}{\rm{Ban}}_{l}^{\infty}(h^{L})]}{{\rm{Ban}}_{k+l}^{\infty}(h^{L})}\leq 1+C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big).

Let us now explain briefly the ideas of the proofs of the above results and their places in the context of previous works.

The central idea of our approach in the whole article is to give various interpretations of the multiplication map on the section ring in terms of the restriction morphisms.
This is useful because the latter morphism can be studied by the methods developed previously by author,
[

]
,

Theorem

to the diagonal
\Delta

First, through Künneth theorem, one can view the space of holomorphic sections over

In Theorem

, we then express using this isomorphism the

and

-norms on

,

and

-norms on

.
Second, in Theorem
3.2
, we establish the lower bounds from Theorem
1.1
by relying on some ideas from the proof of the semiclassical trace theorem from
[
27
, §4.3]
applied for the pair of the product manifold

and the diagonal
\Delta
.
The upper bounds are then established in Theorem

using some tools developed previously in the proof of the semiclassical Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem in
[

]
,
[
28
]
,
[

]
, applied here again for the pair
(X\times X,\Delta)

Theorem

[

To show Theorem

, we construct explicitly an inverse of the map
{\rm{Hilb}}
.
This is done using the Fubini-Study operator which associates through the Kodaira map for any norm

We study in Theorem

The main content of Theorem

Note that the existence of the limit of the Fubini-Study metric alone doesn’t determine the equivalence class of a general graded Hermitian metric on

In realms of non-Archimedean geometry, a result, similar to Theorem

One of them is the non-Archimedean GAGA principle, another is the study of spectral radius of norms over reduced k-affinoid algebras.
Our methods, on the contrary, are purely analytic in nature and the only nontrivial ingredient in our proof is the semiclassical version of the Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem from

Interestingly enough, our proof of Theorem

But now the restriction is done with respect to the Kodaira embedding and not with respect to the diagonal in the product manifold.

We prove Theorem

As we will show in Section

is defined as the tensor product
\pi_{1}^{*}E\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}F
, where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are the projections
X\times X\to X
on the first and the second components.

Let
\mu
be an arbitrary measure on
X
.
For a fixed Hermitian metric
h^{L}
on
L
, we define the
L^{2}
-norm, which we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L},\mu)}
on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
in the same way as in (
1.4
), but with the measure
dv_{X}
replaced by
\mu
.
When the metric
\mu
is given by the symplectic volume form of a Kähler metric
\omega
, we denote the above norm by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L},\omega)}
.
Similarly, we let
{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)
and
{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\omega)
be the restrictions of the above norms to
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
.
Sometimes, to strengthen the dependence on the manifold
X
, we denote the above metrics by
{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}_{k}(h^{L})
, etc.
Similarly, we use the notations
{\rm{Ban}}^{1,X}(h^{L})
and
{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty,X}(h^{L})
.
When
h^{L}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
, and
\mu=\frac{1}{n!}c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{n}
is constructed using Bedford-Taylor extension of the Monge-Ampère operator, cf. (
2.18
), we will omit
\mu
from the notation and borrow the notation for psh metrics from the smooth situation
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L})}
.
Similarly, we extend the
L^{1}
-norm and use the analogous notation.

A Hermitian metric
h^{F}
on a vector bundle
F
is called
bounded
if it can be bounded from above and below by some smooth metrics.
For a complex manifold
X
, let
K_{X}
be the canonical line bundle over it, given by
\wedge^{n}(T^{(1,0)*}X)
.
For a submanifold
Y
in
X
, we denote by
{\rm{Res}}_{Y}
the restriction operator from
X
to
Y
.

2
Extension theorem and norms on the section ring

The main goal of this section is to introduce a set of techniques to compare various norms on the section ring.
This will be intimately related with Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem, two versions of which we recall in Section
2.1
, and to Bernstein-Markov property, which we recall in Section
2.2
.
Those preliminaries will be used in Section
2.3
.
There we will recall the definition of the Fubini-Study operator, which will be our main technical tool in Section
4
, and recall that any continuous psh metric can be obtained through approximation using this map.

2.1
Two versions of Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem

The main goal of this section is to recall a version of Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem with uniform constant due to Demailly
[
23
, Theorem 2.8]
and Ohsawa
[
43
]
and the semiclassical version due to the author
[
27
]
.

In the first part of this section, we follow closely the presentation from
[
23
]
and
[
21
]
. Only Theorem
2.5
seems to be new.

Let
Y
be a complex submanifold of a compact complex manifold
X
.
Let
F
be a holomorphic vector bundle
X
.
Ohsawa-Takegoshi theorem addresses the following extension problem.
Given a holomorphic section
f
of
F
on
Y
, we would like to find a holomorphic extension
\tilde{f}
of
f
to
X
, together with a good estimate on the
L^{2}
-norm of
\tilde{f}
in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm of
f
.
It turns out that it is always possible modulo some positivity condition on the curvature of
F
.
To state this result precisely, we need to fix some notations first.

Definition 2.1
.

We call a function
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to[-\infty,+\infty[
on a complex manifold
X

quasi-plurisubharmonic
(quasi-psh) if
\psi
is locally the sum of a psh function and of a smooth function (or equivalently, if
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi
is locally bounded from below).
In addition, we say that
\psi
has
neat analytic singularities
if every point
x\in X
possesses an open neighborhood
U
on which
\psi
can be written as

\psi(z)=c\cdot\log\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}\big|g_{k}(z)\big|^{2}+w(z),

(2.1)

where
c\geq 0
,
g_{k}
are holomorphic over
U
and
w
is smooth.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\psi
is a quasi-psh function on a complex manifold
X
, the
multiplier ideal sheaf

\mathcal{J}(\psi)
is the coherent analytic subsheaf of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
, cf.
[
21
, Proposition 5.7]
, defined by

\mathcal{J}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Big\{f\in\mathscr{O}_{X,x};\quad\text{there exists }U\ni x,\int_{U}|f|^{2}e^{-\psi}d\lambda<+\infty\Big\}.

(2.2)

where
U
is an open coordinate neighborhood of
x
, and
d\lambda
the standard Lebesgue measure in the corresponding open chart of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
We say that the singularities of
\psi
are
log canonical singularities
along the zero variety
Y=V(\mathcal{J}(\psi))
if
\mathcal{J}(\psi)=\mathcal{J}_{Y}
, where
\mathcal{J}_{Y}
is the sheaf of holomorphic sections vanishing along
Y
.

Let us define a bump function
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to[0,1]
as follows

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{for $|x|<\frac{1}{4}$},\\
0,\quad\text{for $|x|>\frac{1}{2}$}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, and
\omega
be a Kähler form on
X
.
Let
Y
be a closed submanifold of
X
of dimension
m
.
Then for
r>0
small enough, the function
\delta_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\setminus Y\to\real
, defined as

\delta_{Y}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2(n-m)\log\big({\rm{dist}}_{X}(x,Y)\big)\cdot\rho\Big(\frac{{\rm{dist}}_{X}(x,Y)}{r}\Big),

(2.4)

is quasi-psh.
Moreover, it has neat analytic log canonical singularities along
Y
.

Proof.

Remark first that only the statement about the quasi-plurisubharmonicity is non-trivial.
For the proof of a similar statement, which works for general analytic submanifolds, see Demailly
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
.
For exactly this statement, proved in a more general setting of manifolds of bounded geometry, a reader may consult
[
27
, Theorem 2.31]
.
∎

Let us now consider quasi-psh function
\psi
with log canonical singularities on
X
such that
Y\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=V(\mathcal{J}(\psi))
is a submanifold.
Let
\mu_{X}
be a fixed measure on
X
with smooth positive density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
X
.
Let us associate in a canonical way to it a measure on
Y
.
We fix
g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(Y)
and let
\tilde{g}
be a compactly supported continuous extension of
g
on
X
.
We define the measure
\mu_{Y}[\psi]
on
Y
through the following limit

\int_{Y}gd\mu_{Y}[\psi]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\limsup_{t\to-\infty}\int_{U_{t}}\tilde{g}e^{-\psi}d\mu_{X},

(2.5)

where
U_{t}\subset X
,
t\in\real
, is defined as
U_{t}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{x\in X;t<\psi(x)<t+1\}
.
It is possible to see that the limit does not depend on the continuous extension
\tilde{g}
, and that one gets in this way a measure with smooth positive density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
Y
, cf.
[
23
, Proposition 4.5]
.

In the special case when
\mu
corresponds to the Riemannian volume form
dV_{X,\omega}
of Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
and
\psi
equals to
\delta_{Y}
from (
2.4
), an easy verification, cf.
[
23
, (2.6)]
, shows that the volume form
\mu_{Y}[\psi]
is related to the Riemannian volume form
dV_{Y,\omega|_{Y}}
induced by
\omega|_{Y}
as follows

\mu_{Y}[\psi]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{2^{n-m+1}\cdot\pi^{n-m}}{(n-m-1)!}dV_{Y,\omega|_{Y}}.

(2.6)

Let us now introduce the function
\gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to\real_{+}
by the following formula

\gamma(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-x),&\text{if }\,x>0,\\
\frac{1}{1+4x^{2}}&\text{if }\,x\leq 0.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Theorem 2.4

(
[
23
, Theorem 2.8, Remark 2.9b)]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold, and
\omega
be a Kähler form on
X
.
Let
(L,h^{L})
be a holomorphic line bundle equipped with a continuous Hermitian metric
h^{L}
on
X
, and let
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to[-\infty,+\infty[
be a quasi-psh function on
X
with neat analytic singularities.
Assume that the submanifold
Y
of
X
defined by
Y=V(\mathcal{J}(\psi))
is smooth and
\psi
has log canonical singularities.
Finally, assume that the
(1,1)
-current

c_{1}(L,h^{L})+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\alpha}{2\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi,

(2.8)

is non-negative for any
\alpha\in[1,2]
.
Then for any section
f\in H^{0}(Y,(L\otimes K_{X})|_{Y})
, there is a holomorphic extension
\tilde{f}\in H^{0}(X,L\otimes K_{X})
of
f
, such that the following
L^{2}
-bound is satisfied

\int_{X}\gamma(\psi)|\tilde{f}|_{\omega,h}^{2}e^{-\psi}dV_{X,\omega}\leq 17\cdot\int_{Y}|f|_{\omega,h}^{2}dV_{Y,\omega}[\psi],

(2.9)

where the pointwise norm is calculated with respect to
h^{L}
and
\omega
.

Now, in the context of this article, the canonical line bundle and the condition (
2.8
) do not appear naturally. Due to this, we will state an easy consequence of Theorem
2.4
, which will be better adapted for our needs.

We introduce first the notations for the following theorem.
Let
X
be a projective manifold,
\omega
is a Kähler metric on
X
and
L
is a fixed ample line bundle on
X
.
Let
Y
be a closed submanifold of
X
.
Let
h^{L}
be a continuous psh metric on
L
.

Theorem 2.5
.

There are
C>0
,
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq p_{0}
, and any section
f\in H^{0}(Y,L|_{Y}^{k})
, there is a holomorphic extension
\tilde{f}\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
of
f
, such that the following
L^{2}
-bound is satisfied

\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L},\omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k}(Y,h^{L},\omega)}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Since
L
is assumed to be ample, by Kodaira theorem it carries a smooth positive metric
h^{L}_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.3
and positivity of
h^{L}_{0}
, we see that there is
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the following
(1,1)
-current is non-negative

p_{0}\cdot c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{0})-c_{1}(K_{X},h^{K_{X}}_{\omega})+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\alpha}{2\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}\delta_{Y},

(2.11)

for any
\alpha\in[1,2]
, where
h^{K_{X}}_{\omega}
is the metric on
K_{X}
induced by
\omega
.
Hence for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we may apply Theorem
2.4
for quasi-psh function
\delta_{Y}
and line bundle
L^{k}\otimes L^{p_{0}}\otimes K_{X}^{-1}
, endowed with the metric
h_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(h^{L})^{k}\otimes(h^{L}_{0})^{p_{0}}\otimes(h^{K_{X}}_{\omega})^{-1}
.
In particular, from Theorem
2.4
and (
2.6
) we see that for any
f\in H^{0}(Y,L|_{Y}^{k+p_{0}})
, there is an extension
\tilde{f}\in H^{0}(X,L^{k+p_{0}})
of
f
to
X
, such that the following estimate is satisfied

\int_{X}\gamma(\delta_{Y})|\tilde{f}|_{1}^{2}e^{-\delta_{Y}}dV_{X,\omega}\leq 17\cdot\int_{Y}|f|_{1}^{2}dV_{Y,\omega|_{Y}},

(2.12)

where the pointwise norm
|\cdot|_{1}
above is taken with respect to
h_{1}
.
Remark now that since
h^{L}
is bounded, there is
C>0
, such that

C^{-1}h^{L}_{0}\leq h^{L}\leq Ch^{L}_{0}.

(2.13)

Now, remark that by taking
r>0
from Lemma
2.3
small, we may achieve that
\delta_{Y}
is non-positive.
Then by an easy fact that for
x>0
, we have

\frac{e^{x}}{1+4x^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{17},

we deduce the following inequality

\gamma(\delta_{Y})e^{-\delta_{Y}}\geq\frac{1}{17}.

(2.14)

By combining (
2.12
), (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we obtain (
2.10
).
∎

In particular, the previous result recovers the well-known fact that the restriction morphism

{\rm{Res}}_{Y}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{0}(X,L^{k})\to H^{0}(Y,L|_{Y}^{k}),

(2.15)

is surjective.
The following result refines this statement on the metric level in more regular setting.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
27
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
h^{L}
be a smooth positive metric on
L
.
There are
C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that under (
2.15
), for any
k\geq p_{1}
, the following relation between the
L^{2}
-norm on the total manifold
X
and the
L^{2}
-norm on the submanifold
Y
is satisfied

1-\frac{C}{k}\leq\frac{[{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}_{k}(h^{L})]}{{\rm{Hilb}}^{Y}_{k}(h^{L})}\cdot k^{\frac{n-m}{2}}\leq 1+\frac{C}{k}.

(2.16)

Remark 2.7
.

See also
[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
for a refinement on the level of holomorphic jets.

2.2
Bernstein-Markov property and comparison of natural norms

The main goal of this section is to compare several natural norms on the section ring associated to psh metrics.
We will first recall the classical construction due to Bedford-Taylor of Monge-Ampère operator on positive
(1,1)
-currents with bounded potentials, thus extending the domain of operators
{\rm{Ban}}^{1}
,
{\rm{Hilb}}
to continuous psh functions. Then we recall several results concerning the Bernstein-Markov property of the Bedford-Taylor version of Monge-Ampère operator.

Assume that a holomorphic line bundle
F
over a complex manifold
Y
is endowed with a bounded metric
h^{F}
.
We define the
(1,1)
-current
c_{1}(F,h^{F})
as follows

c_{1}(F,h^{F})=c_{1}(F,h^{F}_{0})+\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{h^{F}}{h^{F}_{0}}\Big)\Big],

(2.17)

where
h^{F}_{0}
is any smooth Hermitian product on
F
, and by
[g]
we mean the distribution associated to a locally integrable function
g
.
Poincaré-Lelong equation, cf.
[
22
, Theorem V.13.9]
, shows that such a definition is independent of the choice of
h^{F}_{0}
.

Now, adapting the construction of Bedford-Taylor
[
3
]
, cf.
[
22
, §III.3]
, in our situation, we can define inductively the positive
(k,k)
-currents
c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, for any bounded psh Hermitian metric
h^{F}
as follows

c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k+1}=c_{1}(F,h^{F}_{0})\wedge c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k}+\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\Big[\log\Big(\frac{h^{F}}{h^{F}_{0}}\Big)\cdot c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k}\Big].

(2.18)

The term under the brackets in (
2.18
) is well-defined as a
(k,k)
-current since the logarithm function in it is bounded by our assumption on
h^{F}
and
c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k}
is represented by a non-negative
(k,k)
-form with measure coefficients.
By (
2.18
), we see that the De Rham class of
c_{1}(F,h^{F})^{k}
coincides with
c_{1}(F)^{k}
, where the De Rham cohomology is extended to distributions in the usual way.

Following the standard terminology, cf.
[
41
]
,
[
51
]
and
[
5
]
, we say that the pair
(h^{L},\mu)
satisfies the
Bernstein-Markov property
, if for any
\epsilon>0
there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq p_{1}
, the following relation between the
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms holds

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L},\mu)}\geq\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L})}.

(2.19)

Following
[
51
]
and
[
5
, Definition 1.9]
, we say that the pair
(h^{L},\mu)
is
Bernstein-Markov with respect to psh weights
if and only if for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
p\geq 1
and any upper semi-continuous function
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to[-\infty,\infty[
, such that the
(1,1)
-current
\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi+c_{1}(L,h^{L})
is positive, we have

\int e^{p\psi}d\mu\geq C\exp(-\epsilon p)\cdot\sup_{x\in X}e^{p\psi}(x).

(2.20)

Lemma 2.8
.

If a pair
(h^{L},\mu)
is Bernstein-Markov with respect to psh weights, then it has Bernstein-Markov property.
Moreover, in the estimate (
2.19
) one can replace the
L^{2}
-norm by the
L^{1}
-norm.

Proof.

As it was observed in
[
5
, after Remark 1.10]
, to prove the first part of the statement, it suffices to take
p=2k
and
\psi=\frac{1}{k}\log|s|
in (
2.20
), where
s\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
.
The Poincaré-Lelong equation, cf.
[
22
, (13.2)]
, implies that the above
\psi
verifies the positivity requirement stated before (
2.20
) and inequality (
2.20
) reduces to (
2.19
).
The proof of the second part of the statement is analogous to the proof of the first one, one only needs to put
p=k
in (
2.20
) instead.
∎

Theorem 2.9

(Berman-Boucksom-Witt Nyström
[
5
, Proposition 1.13 and Theorem 1.14]
)
.

For any continuous psh metric
h^{L}
on
L
, the pair
(h^{L},\frac{1}{n!}c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n})
satisfies Bernstein-Markov property with respect to psh weights.

We will also need the following result, philosophically similar to Theorem
2.9
, yet simpler.

Proposition 2.10
.

The pair
(h^{L},\mu)
satisfies Bernstein-Markov property with respect to psh weights for any continuous psh metrics
h^{L}
on
L
and any measure
\mu
, majorized from below by some strictly positive volume form on
X
.

Proof.

It follows directly from
[
5
, Theorem 1.14]
or from mean-value inequality.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

For any
h^{L}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
, the following equivalences between the graded norms on
R(X,L)
hold

{\rm{Ban}}^{1}(h^{L})\sim{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L})\sim{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}).

(2.21)

In particular, the maps (
1.15
) are all identical.
Also, for any measure
\mu
, majorized from below by some strictly positive volume form on
X
and having finite volume over
X
, we have

{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L})\sim{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L},\mu).

(2.22)

Proof.

From the fact that the De Rham class of
c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}
is fixed, we readily deduce that

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle Ban}}^{1}(h^{L})\leq{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\cdot\frac{1}{n!}c_{1}(L)^{n},

(2.23)

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle Hilb}}(h^{L})\leq{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{n!}c_{1}(L)^{n}},

\displaystyle{\rm{\displaystyle Hilb}}(h^{L},\mu)\leq{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\cdot\sqrt{{\rm{vol}}(\mu)}.

This along with Theorem
2.9
, Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
2.10
imply the result.
∎

As a sidenote, let us establish the following corollary. Compare it with Theorem
2.6
.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
h^{L}
be a continuous psh metric on an ample line bundle
L
.
Then under (
2.15
), the following equivalence of norms holds

[{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}(h^{L})]\sim{\rm{Hilb}}^{Y}(h^{L}).

(2.24)

Proof.

From Corollary
2.11
and the obvious fact
[{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty,X}(h^{L})]\geq{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty,Y}(h^{L})
, stipulating that the
L^{\infty}
-norm of a section is always no smaller than the
L^{\infty}
-norm of its restriction, we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for
k\geq p_{1}
, we have

[{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}(h^{L})]\geq\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot{\rm{Hilb}}^{Y}(h^{L}).

(2.25)

Now, following the terminology of Theorem
2.5
, we can restate Theorem
2.5
in the following way.
There are
C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq p_{1}
, we have

{\rm{Hilb}}^{Y}(h^{L},\omega)\geq C[{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}(h^{L},\omega)].

(2.26)

From Corollary
2.11
, we see that it implies that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq p_{1}
, the following inequality is satisfied

{\rm{Hilb}}^{Y}(h^{L})\geq\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot[{\rm{Hilb}}^{X}(h^{L})].

(2.27)

But Corollary
2.12
means exactly that both inequalities (
2.25
) and (
2.27
) are satisfied.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

a) From Corollary
2.11
, we see that the analogous result holds for
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms.

b) For positive smooth metrics, the upper bound from (
2.27
) was independently obtained by Randriam
[
46
, Theorem 3.1.10]
in a more general setting of reduced submanifold (i.e. for holomorphic jets), see also Remark
2.7
.
The
L^{\infty}
-version of this result, working more generally for certain non-compact manifolds
X
and non-smooth
Y
but only for strictly positive singular metrics
h^{L}
was established by Randriam
[
47
]
.

2.3
Fubini-Study operator and continuous psh metrics

In this section we recall the definition of the Fubini-Study operator. We will then show that the Hilbert map can be used to approximate any continuous psh metric.

First of all, recall that for
k
sufficiently large so that
L^{k}
is very ample, Fubini-Study operator associates for any Hermitian norm
N_{k}=\|\cdot\|_{k}
on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, a positive metric
FS(N_{k})
on
L
, constructed in the following way.
Consider the Kodaira embedding

{\rm{Kod}}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L^{k})^{*}),

(2.28)

defined as
x\mapsto H^{0}(X,L^{k}\otimes\mathcal{J}_{x})
, where
\mathcal{J}_{x}

Proof.

Explicit evaluation shows that

We will now discuss a result concerning quantization of continuous psh metrics.

3.1
Multiplication map as a restriction morphism, a proof of Theorem
1.1

The main goal of this section is to give a proof of Theorem
1.1
modulo some technical results, which will be later established in this article.
Our main idea is to interpret the multiplication map of the section ring (
1.2
) as an instance of the restriction morphism.
This will allow us to use some of the methods developed in
[

]
,
[
28
]
,
[

to prove Theorem

More precisely, we conserve the notation from Section
1

Let us consider the product manifold
X\times X
and the diagonal submanifold in it, given by
\{(x,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x\in X\}=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta\hookrightarrow X\times X
.
Clearly, we have a natural isomorphism

\Delta\to X,\qquad(x,x)\mapsto x.

(3.1)

Also, under the isomorphism (
3.1
), we have

L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l}|_{\Delta}\to L^{k+l}.

(3.2)

Künneth theorem allows us to interpret the tensor product of cohomologies as the cohomology of the product manifold. In other words, it provides us with the following isomorphism

H^{0}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})\to H^{0}(X,L^{k})\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{l}).

A basic observation of this section is that those isomorphisms can be put into the following commutative diagram

\begin{CD}H^{0}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})@>{{\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}}>{}>H^{0}(\Delta,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l}|_{\Delta})\\
@V{}V{{}}V@V{}V{{}}V\\
H^{0}(X,L^{k})\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{l})@>{{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}}>{}>H^{0}(X,L^{k+l}).\end{CD}

(3.4)

A similar observation was already used by Demailly-Ein-Lazarsfeld
[
24
, proof of Theorem 2.3]
in the proof of subadditivity theorem for multiplier ideals.

We will now explain how this observation allows us to establish Theorem
1.1
.
Before that, let us use it to prove the following well-known result.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

Proof.

Clearly, it is enough to establish the result for

.
We borrow the notation from Section

.
We have a short exact sequence of sheaves

(3.5)

The associated long exact sequence in cohomology gives us

\cdots\to H^{0}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})\xrightarrow{{\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}}H^{0}(\Delta,(L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})|_{\Delta})\\
\to H^{1}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l}\otimes\mathcal{J}_{\Delta})\to\cdots.

(3.6)

Clearly, from (
3.4
), to prove the surjectivity of

From (

[
21

Next we will explain that (

]
and
[

]

The main goal of this section is to study the functional-analytic properties of the normed spaces

by the parallel transport of
l_{0}
with respect to the Chern connection
\nabla^{L}
, done along the curve
\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(tZ)
,
t\in[0,1]
,
Z\in T_{x_{0}}X
,
|Z|<r_{X}
, where
r_{X}
is the infimum of the injectivity radius of
X
.
Now, for
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\real^{2n}
,
|Z|,|Z^{\prime}|\leq\epsilon
, we will denote by
B_{k}^{X}\big(\phi_{x_{0}}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}(Z^{\prime})\big)\in\real
the Bergman kernel, evaluated at
(\phi_{x_{0}}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}(Z^{\prime}))
, and viewed as a real number using the frame
\tilde{l}^{\prime}_{0}
.

Define the function
\mathscr{P}_{n}
, cf.
[
38
, (4.1.84)]
, as follows

\mathscr{P}_{n}(Z,Z^{\prime})=\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(|z_{i}|^{2}+|z^{\prime}_{i}|^{2}-2z_{i}\overline{z}^{\prime}_{i}\big)\Big),\quad\text{for }Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

(3.23)

From
[
38
, §4.1.6]
,
\mathscr{P}_{n}
is the Schwartz kernel of the orthogonal projection from the space of
L^{2}
-integrable functions with respect to the Gaussian measure onto the Bargmann space.

Next result shows that the behavior of the Bergman kernel on any manifold
X
for close parameters is governed by the function
\mathscr{P}_{n}
.

Theorem 3.11
.

There are
\epsilon,c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
x_{0}\in X
,
k\geq p_{1}
,
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\real^{2n}
,
|Z|,|Z^{\prime}|\leq\epsilon
, the following estimate holds

\bigg|\frac{1}{k^{n}}B_{k}^{X}\big(\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z^{\prime})\big)-\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{k}Z,\sqrt{k}Z^{\prime})\bigg|\\
\leq Ck^{-1}\Big(1+\sqrt{k}|Z|+\sqrt{k}|Z^{\prime}|\Big)^{3n+2}\exp\Big(-c\sqrt{k}|Z-Z^{\prime}|\Big).

(3.24)

Proof.

The result follows from the first two terms of the asymptotic expansion of Dai-Liu-Ma
[
15
]
and a result of Ma-Marinescu
[
38
, Remark 4.1.26]
, which states that the second term of the asymptotic expansion vanishes.
The
\kappa
function, see
[
38
, (4.1.28)]
for a definition, doesn’t appear in our expansion since our expansion only uses the two first terms of the expansion of Dai-Liu-Ma, and according to
[
38
, (1.2.19)]
, the appearance of the
\kappa
-term doesn’t affect them.
∎

We are now ready to draw the first consequence of those statements.

Proposition 3.12
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
big enough, the operator
B_{k}^{X}
extends using the Bergman kernel as a bounded operator to the space of
L^{p}
-integrable sections of
L^{k}
, for any
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
Moreover, there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
p\in[1,+\infty]
, the following bound holds

\big\|B_{k}^{X}\big\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X,h^{L})\to L^{p}_{k}(X,h^{L})}\leq 2^{n}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}\Big),

(3.25)

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{k}(X,h^{L})\to L^{p}_{k}(X,h^{L})}
is the operator norm of an operator from the space of
L^{p}
-integrable sections of
L^{k}
to itself.

Remark 3.13
.

Similar results were obtained for Bargmann space, see
[
33
]
.
In fact, according to
[
31
, Theorem 1.1]
, the constant
2^{n}
in this case is sharp for
p=1
.

Proof.

From Theorems
3.10
,
3.11
and (
3.48
), we conclude that there is
C>0
, such that for any
x_{0}\in X
, for
k\geq p_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}\big|B_{k}^{X}(x_{0},x)\big|dv_{X}(x)\leq 2^{n}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}\Big),

(3.26)

\displaystyle\int_{X}\big|B_{k}^{X}(x,x_{0})\big|dv_{X}(x)\leq 2^{n}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}\Big).

Directly from (
3.26
) and Young’s inequality for integral operators, cf.
[
52
, Theorem 0.3.1]
applied for
q=p
,
r=1
in the notations of
[
52
]
, we deduce (
3.25
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.8
.

Instead of (
3.15
), let us establish the following inequalities

\frac{1}{2^{n}}{\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(h^{L})\leq({\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{1}(h^{L}))^{*}\leq{\rm{Ban}}_{l}^{\infty}(h^{L}).

(3.27)

By taking duals, and using the fact that any norm on a finitely dimensional vector space is equal to its double dual, we see that (
3.27
) is equivalent to (
3.15
).

First of all, by the definition of the dual norm, for any
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{k}(X,h^{L})}^{*}=\sup_{g\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})}\frac{|\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L})}|}{\|g\|_{L^{1}_{k}(X,h^{L})}}.

(3.28)

From this, the fact that
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
is a subspace of
L^{1}(X,L^{k})
and the well-known fact that the dual of
(L^{1}(X,L^{k}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{k}(X,h^{L})})
coincides with
(L^{\infty}(X,L^{k}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L})})
, we deduce the right estimate from (
3.27
).

Now, for a fixed
\alpha>0
and
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, consider an element
g_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=B_{k}^{X}(|f|^{\alpha}\cdot f)\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
.
Then according to Proposition
3.12
, we have

\|g_{\alpha}\|_{L^{1}_{k}(X,h^{L})}\leq 2^{n}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}\Big)\cdot\int_{X}|f|^{1+\alpha}(x)dv_{X}(x).

(3.29)

Remark also that we have

\langle f,g_{\alpha}\rangle_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L})}=\langle f,|f|^{\alpha}\cdot f\rangle_{L^{2}_{k}(X,h^{L})}=\int_{X}|f|^{2+\alpha}(x)dv_{X}(x).

(3.30)

In particular, from (
3.30
), we see that
g_{\alpha}\neq 0
.
Recall, finally, that for any continuous non-zero function
h\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\real
, we have

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\frac{\int_{X}|h|^{2+\alpha}(x)dv_{X}(x)}{\int_{X}|h|^{1+\alpha}(x)dv_{X}(x)}=\sup_{x\in X}|h|(x).

(3.31)

The left estimate from (
3.27
) now follows from (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) by putting
g\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=g_{\alpha}
in (
3.28
) and letting
\alpha\to\infty
.

The second estimate from Theorem
3.8
is obtained in an analogous way.
∎

3.3
Bounds on products of holomorphic sections, a proof of Theorem
3.2

The main goal of this section is to give a bound on the norm of a product of two holomorphic sections in terms of their norms. In particular, we prove Theorem
3.2
.
The main caveat of the proof is an introduction of a sequence of operators, called multiplicative defect.
They would allow us to extend our methods from
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
in the current setting.

Before all, let us prove that Theorem
3.2
holds for
L^{1}
-norm.
For this we will rely solely on the results on the study of the asymptotic behavior of the Bergman kernel from Section
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
for the
L^{1}
-norm.

Let us first recall that the Bergman projector
B_{k,l}^{X\times X}
is defined as the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
to
H^{0}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
.
Clearly, under the identification

L^{2}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})\to L^{2}(X,L^{k})\otimes L^{2}(X,L^{l}),

(3.32)

where
\otimes
means here the tensor product of Hilbert spaces, and the identification (
3.3
), we can write

B_{k,l}^{X\times X}=B_{k}^{X}\otimes B_{l}^{X}.

(3.33)

We extend
{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}
to
L^{2}(X,L^{k})\otimes L^{2}(X,L^{l})
by precomposing it with the Bergman projection.
Then, under the isomorphisms from (
3.4
) and (
3.32
), we have the following identity

{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}={\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ B_{k,l}^{X\times X}.

(3.34)

Similarly to (
3.26
), from (
3.34
), we conclude that there are
C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
x_{0},x_{1}\in X
, for
k,l\geq p_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Big|{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}\big(x,(x_{0},x_{1})\big)\Big|dv_{X}(x)\leq 2^{n}\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{\max\{k,l\}}\Big)^{n}\cdot\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}+\frac{C}{l}\Big),

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int_{X\times X}\Big|{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}\big(x_{0},(x,x^{\prime})\big)\Big|dv_{X}(x)dv_{X}(x^{\prime})\leq 4^{n}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k}+\frac{C}{l}\Big).

Directly from (
3.35
) and Young’s inequality for integral operators, cf.
[
52
, Theorem 0.3.1]
applied for
q=p=1
,
r=1
in the notations of
[
52
]
, we deduce the second inequality from (
3.9
).

Let us now show that the first part of the statement for
L^{1}
-norm follows from the second one.
Remark that for any
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
,
g\in H^{0}(X,L^{l})
, we have

\big\|f\otimes g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}=\big\|f\big\|_{L^{1}_{k}(X,h^{L})}\cdot\big\|g\big\|_{L^{1}_{l}(X,h^{L})}.

(3.36)

By the definition of the operator norm and (
3.36
), we deduce that

\big\|f\cdot g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})}\leq\|{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}\|_{L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})\to L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})}\cdot\big\|f\otimes g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}.

(3.37)

We conclude by (
3.9
), (
3.36
) and (
3.37
).
∎

Now, to prove Theorem
3.2
for the
L^{2}
-norm, more work has to be done.
This is due to the fact that the bound provided by the Young’s inequality will no longer coincide asymptotically with the optimal bound.
To remedy this problem, using the surjectivity statement from Proposition
3.1
, we define the
optimal decomposition map

{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})\to H^{0}(X,L^{k})\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{l}),

(3.38)

as follows.
We let
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}(f)=h
if
h
satisfies
{\rm{Mult}}_{k,l}(h)=f
, and
h
has the minimal
L^{2}
-norm among all
h\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{l})
, verifying such an identity (the uniqueness of such
h
follows from the non-degeneracy of the
L^{2}
-Hermitian product).
Clearly, from the commutative square (
3.4
), we see that the optimal decomposition map is just a version of the optimal extension map, studied in
[
27
]
, associated for the pair
(X\times X,\Delta)
.
The difference between our situation here from
[
27
]
is that there we consider only the tensor powers and not
\boxtimes
products.
This being said, most of our techniques from
[
29
]
transfer in the current setting almost directly.

We will now use the methods of
[
29
]
to study
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}
.
Our first goal is to prove the existence of a sequence of operators, relating the restriction maps with optimal decomposition maps.

Theorem 3.14
.

There is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
, there is a unique operator
A_{k,l}\in\text{End}{(H^{0}(X,L^{k+l}))}
, verifying under the isomorphisms (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) the following identity

({\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ B_{k,l}^{X\times X})^{*}={\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}\circ A_{k,l}.

(3.39)

Remark 3.15
.

The sequence of operators
A_{k,l}
, will be later called
multiplicative defect
.
An analogous sequence of operators for more general immersions (and tensor powers of the line bundles instead of
\boxtimes
-products) was introduced in
[
28
, Theorem 4.3]
in their relation with the problem of transitivity of optimal holomorphic extensions.
It was later studied in
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]
in a more general situation of holomorphic jets along submanifold, and used as a tool to extend our previous work
[
27
]
on the semiclassical extension theorem for holomorphic jets instead of sections.

Proof.

It follows from the equality of kernels and images of
({\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ B_{k,l}^{X\times X})^{*}
and
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}
, which relies on Proposition
3.1
.
See
[
28
, Theorem 4.3]
or
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
for details. ∎

Now, we will show that,
A_{k,l}
coincides asymptotically with the identity operator up to a multiplication by an explicit constant.
At least for
k=l
, this is quite an expected result.

In fact, from the commutative diagram (
3.4
) and (
3.39
), the operator
A_{k,l}
relates the extension and the adjoint of the restriction operators.
But from the calculations in
[
28
, (3.10)]
or
[
29
, (3.15)]
, we know that those operators coincide (in a non-asymptotic sense) for the model Bargmann space.
Moreover, by the results of
[
27
, Theorem 1.6]
and
[
29
, Lemma 5.9]
, we know that the extension and restriction operators for general manifolds coincide asymptotically with those model operators after an appropriate rescaling.
So over general manifolds the extension and the adjoint of the restriction operators should asymptotically coincide as well.

The following result shows that despite the fact that we do not posses a general version of extension theorem, which would cover not only the tensor powers, but also line bundles of the form
L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l}
, the property of
A_{k,l}
, described above, persists.

Theorem 3.16
.

There are
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
C>0
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
, we have

\Big\|A_{k,l}-\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{k+l}\Big)^{n}\cdot{\rm{Id}}_{H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})}\Big\|\leq C\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{k+l}\Big)^{n},

(3.40)

where the operator norm of the operators on
H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
is taken with respect to
{\rm{Hilb}}_{k+l}(h^{L})
.

In the proof of Theorem
3.16
, the following result will be of utmost importance.
To state it, we extend the domain of
A_{k,l}
to
L^{2}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})
by precomposing it with the Bergman projection.
Then
A_{k,l}
is a smoothing operator and hence has a smooth Schwartz kernel,
A_{k,l}(x_{1},x_{2})
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
, with respect to
dv_{X}
.
Next lemma provides a comprehensive study of this Schwartz kernel.

Lemma 3.17
.

There are
c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
, we have

\Big|A_{k,l}(x_{1},x_{2})\Big|\leq C(k\cdot l)^{n}\cdot\exp\big(-c(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l})\cdot{\rm{dist}}(x_{1},x_{2})\big).

(3.41)

Moreover, there are
\epsilon,c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
x_{0}\in X
,
k,l\geq p_{1}
,
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\real^{2n}
,
|Z|,|Z^{\prime}|\leq\epsilon
, we have

\bigg|A_{k,l}\big(\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z^{\prime})\big)-\Big(\frac{k\cdot l}{k+l}\Big)^{n}B_{k+l}^{X}\big(\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z^{\prime})\big)\bigg|\\
\leq C(k\cdot l)^{n}\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(1+\sqrt{k+l}|Z|+\sqrt{k+l}|Z^{\prime}|\Big)^{6n+4}\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-c(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l})\cdot|Z-Z^{\prime}|\Big).

(3.42)

Proof.

First of all, from (
3.39
), we see that the following identity holds

A_{k,l}={\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ({\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ B_{k,l}^{X\times X})^{*}.

(3.43)

Remark also the trivial fact that the embedding
\Delta\hookrightarrow X\times X
is totally geodesic once we endow

with the product metric.
From this, we deduce that

d_{X\times X}(z,z^{\prime})=d_{\Delta}(z,z^{\prime}),\qquad\text{for }z,z^{\prime}\in\Delta,

(3.44)

where the metric on
\Delta
is taken with respect to the Kähler form
\omega_{1,1}
.
Also, for any
x_{0}\in X
,
Z\in\real^{2n}
,
|Z|<r_{X}
, we have

\phi_{x_{0}\times x_{0}}^{X\times X}(Z,Z)=(\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z)).

(3.45)

From Theorem
3.10
, (
3.33
), (
3.43
) and (
3.44
), we deduce (
3.41
).

Let us now prove (

).
From Theorem
3.11
, (
3.33
), (
3.43
) and (

), we see that there are
\epsilon,c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any

,

,

\bigg|A_{k,l}\big(\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z^{\prime})\big)-(k\cdot l)^{n}\cdot\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{k}Z,\sqrt{k}Z^{\prime})\cdot\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{l}Z,\sqrt{l}Z^{\prime})\bigg|\\
\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot(k\cdot l)^{n}\cdot\Big(1+\sqrt{k+l}|Z|+\sqrt{k+l}|Z^{\prime}|\Big)^{6n+4}\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-c(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l})\cdot|Z-Z^{\prime}|\Big).

(3.46)

We now remark that from (

\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{k}Z,\sqrt{k}Z^{\prime})\cdot\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{l}Z,\sqrt{l}Z^{\prime})=\mathscr{P}_{n}(\sqrt{k+l}Z,\sqrt{k+l}Z^{\prime}).

(3.47)

3.11

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Remark first, cf.
[

, that there is

, such that for any

3.4
Asymptotics of optimal decomposition map, a proof of Theorem
3.4

The main goal of this section is to study more precisely the asymptotics of optimal decomposition map, introduced in (
3.38
).
Through this study we will establish Theorem
3.4
.

We will begin by showing that for the
L^{2}
-norm, Theorem
3.4
already follows from the analysis on the multiplicative defect from Section
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
for the
L^{2}
-norm.

Directly from Theorem
3.16
, we see that there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that
A_{k,l}
is invertible on
H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
.
We, moreover, deduce that there are
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
C>0
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
, the following inequality holds

\Big|\big\|A_{k,l}^{-1}\big\|-\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n}\Big|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n},

(3.52)

where the operator norm here (as well as below in this proof) is taken with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm.
From Theorem
3.14
, under the isomorphisms from (
3.4
), we have the following identity

({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l})^{*}\circ{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}=\big((A_{k,l})^{*}\big)^{-1}.

(3.53)

Clearly, we have
\|({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l})^{*}\circ{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}\|=\|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}\|^{2}
.
Theorem
3.4
for the
L^{2}
-norm now follows from this observation, (
3.52
) and (
3.53
).
∎

To establish Theorem
3.4
for
L^{1}
and

-norms, we have to do more refined analysis.
This is due to the fact that
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms are not Hermitian ones, and so the above argument using the dual mappings wouldn’t work.

To overcome this, we develop analysis similar in spirit to
[
29
, proof of Theorem 1]
, yet technically a bit more involved.
In particular, we will give an asymptotic formula for the optimal decomposition map (and not only for its norm or the norm of the inverse as it was done in proof of Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
for the
L^{2}
-norm).
To describe this asymptotics, we introduce some further notation to study the geometry of the diagonal embedding.

For
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, not vanishing at the same time, let us consider the orthogonal complement
N_{\alpha,\beta}
of
T\Delta
in
T(X\times X)
with respect to the non-negative
(1,1)
-form

\omega_{\alpha,\beta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\alpha\pi_{1}^{*}\omega+\beta\pi_{2}^{*}\omega

(3.54)

on
X\times X
, where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are the projections from
X\times X
to the first and the second factor respectively.
Clearly,
N_{\alpha,\beta}
is well-defined even if either
\alpha
or
\beta
is equal to
0
, as
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
is always positive-definite along
T\Delta
as long as
\alpha,\beta
do not vanish at the same time. Moreover,
N_{\alpha,\beta}
has constant dimension along
\Delta
and hence forms a (smooth) vector bundle over
\Delta
.

For
y=(x_{0},x_{0})\in\Delta
,
Z_{N}\in N_{\alpha,\beta}
, let
\real\ni t\mapsto\exp_{y}^{X\times X}(tZ_{N})\in X
be the geodesic in
X\times X
in direction
Z_{N}
. The geodesic is taken with respect to the Kähler form
\omega_{1,1}
.
In other words, for
Z_{N}=(Z_{1},Z_{2})
, we have
\exp_{y}^{X\times X}(Z_{N})=(\exp_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z_{1}),\exp_{x_{0}}^{X}(Z_{2}))
.

Clearly, for a certain
r_{\perp}>0
, the above exponential map induces a diffeomorphism of
r_{\perp}
-neighborhood of the zero section in
N_{\alpha,\beta}
(with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
induced by
\omega_{1,1}
) with a tubular neighborhood
U_{\alpha,\beta}
of
\Delta
in
X\times X
.
Such
r_{\perp}
can be chosen independently of
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
since
N_{\alpha,\beta}
can be seen as a piecewise smooth vector bundle over
\Delta\times\Delta_{1}
, where
\Delta_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{(\gamma,\delta)\in\real^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}|\gamma|+|\delta|=1\}
and the projection over
\Delta_{1}
is induced by
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\frac{1}{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(\alpha,\beta)
.

We denote by
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}U_{\alpha,\beta}\to\Delta
the natural projection
(y,Z_{N})\mapsto y
.
Over
U_{\alpha,\beta}
, we identify
\pi_{i}^{*}L
,
i=1,2
, to
\pi_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}(\pi_{i}^{*}L|_{\Delta})
by the parallel transport with respect to the respective Chern connections along the geodesic
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto(y,tZ_{N})\in X
,
|Z_{N}|<r_{\perp}
, previously described.
This gives us a local trivialization in normal directions of the vector bundle
L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l}
in the neighborhood of
\Delta
.

Using the above identifications, we define the operator
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{2}(X,L^{k+l})\to L^{2}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
as follows.
For
g\in L^{2}(X,L^{k+l})
, we let
({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g)(x)=0
,
x\notin U_{k,l}
, and in
U_{k,l}
, we put

({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g)(y,Z_{N})=(B_{k+l}^{X}g)(y)\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}|Z_{N}|_{k,l}^{2}\Big)\rho\Big(\frac{|Z_{N}|}{r_{\perp}}\Big),

(3.55)

where
|\cdot|_{\alpha,\beta}
is the norm induced on
N_{\alpha,\beta}
by the Kähler form
\omega_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real_{+}\to[0,1]
satisfies

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad\text{for $x<\frac{1}{4}$},\\
0,\quad\text{for $x>\frac{1}{2}$}.\end{cases}

(3.56)

Now, for
g\in H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
, the section
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g
satisfies
({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g)|_{\Delta}=g
, but
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g
is not holomorphic over
X\times X
in general.
The main result of this sections, nevertheless, says that this operator approximates well the optimal decomposition map.

Theorem 3.18
.

There are
C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}-{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}\Big\|_{L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})\to L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n},

(3.57)

\displaystyle\Big\|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}-{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})\to L^{\infty}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big).

Remark 3.19
.

For the
L^{2}
-norm, an analogous result can be proved by similar methods.
It would give us an alternative proof of Theorem
3.4
for the
L^{2}
-norm.

Before establishing Theorem
3.18
, let us see how useful it is in proving Theorem
3.4
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
for
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms.

Directly from (
3.55
), the fact that
({\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g)|_{\Delta}=g
and
\exp(-x)\leq 1
for

, we see that for any
g\in H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
, we have

\big\|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}=\big\|g\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})}.

(3.58)

Then from the second bound of (
3.57
) and (
3.58
), we establish that Theorem
3.4
holds for the
L^{\infty}
-norm, and one can moreover take
h\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}f
in the notations of Theorem
3.4
.

Let us now treat the
L^{1}
-norm.
Remark first that the volume form
dv_{k,l}
on
X\times X
associated to
\omega_{k,l}
relates to
dv_{1,1}
as follows

dv_{k,l}=(k\cdot l)^{n}\cdot dv_{1,1}.

(3.59)

Remark also that the restriction of
\omega_{k,l}
to

coincides with
(k+l)\cdot\omega
under the isomorphism (

).
Finally, we see that for the Lebesgue measure
d_{k,l}Z_{N}
on

induced by

\int_{Z_{N}\in N_{k,l,y}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}|Z_{N}|_{k,l}^{2}\Big)d_{k,l}Z_{N}=2^{n}.

(3.60)

Directly from those remarks, we see that there are
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
,
C>0
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
,

\Big(1-C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\Big)\cdot 2^{n}\cdot\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n}\cdot\big\|g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})}\leq\big\|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}\\
\leq\Big(1+C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\Big)\cdot 2^{n}\cdot\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n}\cdot\big\|g\big\|_{L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})}.

(3.61)

We conclude by the first inequality from Theorem
3.18
and (

) that Theorem
3.4
holds for the

-norm, and one can moreover take
h\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}f
in the notations of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

To establish Theorem
3.18
, we proceed in several steps.
First, we show that the Schwartz kernels of both operators,
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}
and
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}
, have exponential decay.
Second, we study the near-diagonal behavior of the Schwartz kernels of those operators and infer that they have the same principal asymptotic terms.
This would imply Theorem
3.18
by some standard techniques.

More precisely, in the first step we prove the following exponential decay estimates.

Lemma 3.20
.

There are
c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
,
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\in X
, the following estimate holds

\Big|R_{k,l}\big((x_{1},x_{2}),x_{0}\big)\Big|\leq C(k+l)^{n}\cdot\exp\Big(-c\cdot\big(\sqrt{k}{\rm{dist}}(x_{1},x_{0})+\sqrt{l}{\rm{dist}}(x_{2},x_{0})\big)\Big),

(3.62)

where
R_{k,l}
is ether
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}
or
{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}
.

Proof.

Directly from Theorem
3.16
, we see that there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that
A_{k,l}
is invertible on
H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
for any
k,l\geq p_{1}
.
From this and Theorem
3.14
, we conclude that

{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}=({\rm{Res}}_{\Delta}\circ B_{k,l}^{X\times X})^{*}\circ A_{k,l}^{-1}.

(3.63)

From Theorem
3.10
, Lemma
3.17
and the usual laws of composition and inversion of operators with exponential decay, cf.
[
27
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
28
, Lemma 4.5]
, we deduce (
3.62
) for

.

Directly from Theorem
3.10
and (
3.55
), we see that

\Big|{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}\big((x_{1},x_{2}),x_{0}\big)\Big|\\
\leq C(k+l)^{n}\cdot\exp\Big(-c\cdot{\rm{dist}}_{k,l}\big((x_{1},x_{2}),x_{0}\big)\Big),

(3.64)

where
{\rm{dist}}_{k,l}((x_{1},x_{2}),x_{0})
is the distance between points
(x_{1},x_{2})
and
(x_{0},x_{0})
in
X\times X
with respect to the Kähler form
\omega_{k,l}

An easy verification shows that

2{\rm{dist}}_{k,l}((x_{1},x_{2}),x_{0})\geq\sqrt{k}{\rm{dist}}(x_{1},x_{0})+\sqrt{l}{\rm{dist}}(x_{2},x_{0}).

(3.65)

This with (
3.64
) clearly finish the proof.
∎

Theorem
3.20
implies that to understand fully the asymptotics of the Schwartz kernel of the optimal decomposition map, it suffices to do so in a neighborhood of a fixed point
(x_{0},x_{0})\in\Delta\subset X\times X
.
Our next result calculates the asymptotics of this Schwartz kernel.

To state this result precisely, we need to introduce a version of

in
X\times X
around
\Delta
.
We fix
k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
y_{0}\in X
.
Let
P^{N}_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T(X\times X)|_{\Delta}\to N_{k,l}
, be the orthogonal projection.
For
Z=(Z_{H},Z_{N})
,
Z_{H}\in T_{y_{0}}X
,
Z_{N}\in N_{k,l,y_{0}}
,
|Z_{H}|\leq r_{X}
,
|Z_{N}|\leq r_{\perp}

\psi_{y_{0}}^{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}B_{0}^{T_{y_{0}}X}(r_{X})\times B_{0}^{N_{k,l,y_{0}}}(r_{\perp})\to X\times X
by

\psi_{y_{0}}^{k,l}(Z_{H},Z_{N})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\exp_{\exp_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{H})}^{X\times X}(Z_{N}(Z_{H})),

(3.66)

where
Z_{N}(Z_{H})
is the parallel transport of

,

\nabla^{N}_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P^{N}_{k,l}\nabla^{T(X\times X)}P^{N}_{k,l}
on

We will implicitly fix an orthonormal basis in

using it.
In this way, we may view

as a map defined on an open neighborhood of

in
\real{}^{n}\times\real^{n}

Let us now fix an element
f_{0}\in L_{y_{0}}
of unit norm.
We define the local orthonormal frame
\tilde{f}
of

around

with respect to
\nabla^{L}

\psi^{k,l}_{y_{0}}(tZ_{H},0)

\psi^{k,l}_{y_{0}}(Z_{H},tZ_{N})
,

,
Z_{H}\in T_{y_{0}}X
,
Z_{N}\in N_{k,l,y_{0}}
,

,

Recall that the exponential coordinates
\phi^{X}
and the frame
\tilde{f}^{\prime}
of
L
in a neighborhood of

were defined in (
3.22
).
For
g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})

g(\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z^{\prime}))\in L_{y_{0}}^{k+l}
,
Z,Z^{\prime}\in Z_{H}\in T_{y_{0}}X
,
|Z|,|Z^{\prime}|\leq R
, for coordinates of

in the frame

\tilde{f}

Our first result gives a near-diagonal expansion of the optimal decomposition map.

Lemma 3.21
.

There are
\epsilon,c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any
y_{0}\in X
,
k,l\geq p_{1}
,
Z_{0},Z_{1},Z_{2}\in T_{y_{0}}X
,

, we have

\bigg|\frac{1}{(k+l)^{n}}{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}\big((\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{1}),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{2})),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{0})\big)-\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{k}Z_{1},\sqrt{k}Z_{0}\big)\cdot\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{l}Z_{2},\sqrt{l}Z_{0}\big)\bigg|\\
\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(1+\sqrt{k}(|Z_{1}|+|Z_{0}|)+\sqrt{l}(|Z_{2}|+|Z_{0}|)\Big)^{12n+8}\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-c\big(\sqrt{k}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}|+\sqrt{l}|Z_{2}-Z_{0}|\big)\Big),

(3.67)

where
\mathscr{P}_{n}
was defined in (
3.23
).

Proof.

It follows directly from Theorems
3.10
,
3.11
, Lemma
3.17
, (
3.63
) and the usual composition rules for the operators with Taylor-type expansions and exponential decay of the Schwartz kernel, cf.
[
27
, Lemma 3.5]
.
∎

We will now state the analogous result for the trivial decomposition map.

Lemma 3.22
.

There are
\epsilon,c,C>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any

,

\bigg|\frac{1}{(k+l)^{n}}{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}\big(\psi_{y_{0}}^{k,l}(Z_{H},Z_{N}),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{0})\big)-\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{k+l}Z_{H},\sqrt{k+l}Z_{0}\big)\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}|Z_{N}|^{2}_{k,l}\Big)\bigg|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(1+\sqrt{k+l}(|Z_{H}|+|Z_{0}|)\Big)^{3n+2}\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-c\cdot\sqrt{k+l}\cdot|Z_{H}-Z_{0}|-c\cdot\frac{kl}{k+l}\cdot|Z_{N}|^{2}\Big).

(3.68)

Proof.

The proof follows directly from Theorem
3.11
and (
3.55
).

We, finally need the following general result for the estimation of operator’s norms.
Let assume that a sequence of operators
R_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{2}(X,L^{k+l})\to L^{2}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
,
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
, is such that its Schwartz kernel satisfies both the estimate (

There are
\epsilon,c,C,Q>0
,
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, such that for any

,

,

\bigg|\frac{1}{(k+l)^{n}}R_{k,l}\big((\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{1}),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{2})),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{0})\big)\bigg|\\
\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(1+\sqrt{k}(|Z_{1}|+|Z_{0}|)+\sqrt{l}(|Z_{2}|+|Z_{0}|)\Big)^{Q}\cdot\\
\cdot\exp\Big(-c\big(\sqrt{k}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}|+\sqrt{l}|Z_{2}-Z_{0}|\big)\Big).

(3.69)

Lemma 3.23
.

There is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for
k,l\geq p_{1}
, the operator
R_{k,l}
extends using Schwartz kernel as an operator
R_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{q}(X,L^{k+l})\to L^{q}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
for
q=1,\infty
, and we have

\displaystyle\big\|R_{k,l}\big\|_{L^{1}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})\to L^{1}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big)\cdot\Big(\frac{k+l}{k\cdot l}\Big)^{n},

(3.70)

\displaystyle\big\|R_{k,l}\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{k+l}(X,h^{L})\to L^{\infty}_{k,l}(X\times X,h^{L}\boxtimes h^{L})}\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{l}\Big).

Proof.

First of all, a calculation of Gaussian integrals shows that there is
C>0
, such that for any
k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, we have

\int_{Z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{Z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{Z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\exp\Big(-c\big(\sqrt{k}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}|+\sqrt{l}|Z_{2}-Z_{0}|\big)\Big)\cdot|f(Z_{0})|dZ_{0}dZ_{1}dZ_{2}\\
\leq\frac{C}{(k\cdot l)^{n}}\|f\|_{L^{1}}.

(3.71)

Now, remark that the triangle inequality implies

\sqrt{k}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}|+\sqrt{l}|Z_{2}-Z_{0}|\geq\big(\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}\big)\cdot\Big|\frac{\sqrt{k}Z_{1}+\sqrt{l}Z_{2}}{\sqrt{k}+\sqrt{l}}-Z_{0}\Big|.

(3.72)

This with a calculation of Gaussian integrals shows that there is
C>0
, such that for any
k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, we have

\sup_{Z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\sup_{Z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{Z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\exp\Big(-c\big(\sqrt{k}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}|+\sqrt{l}|Z_{2}-Z_{0}|\big)\Big)\cdot|g(Z_{0})|dZ_{0}\\
\leq\frac{C}{(k+l)^{n}}\cdot\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(3.73)

Exponential decay assumption on the kernel of
R_{k,l}
implies that only local contributions of the Schwartz kernel of
R_{k,l}
matter in the study of the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-operator norms of
R_{k,l}f
for
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k+l})
.
According to (
3.70
), estimates (
3.71
) and (
3.73
), done in local coordinates, show that
R_{k,l}
extends to an operator
R_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{q}(X,L^{k+l})\to L^{q}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
for
q=1,\infty
, and the announced bounds on the resulting norms hold.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.18
.

First of all, let us denote by
R_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{2}(X,L^{k+l})\to L^{2}(X\times X,L^{k}\boxtimes L^{l})
the operator, given by the difference

R_{k,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}-{\rm{Dec}}_{k,l}^{0}.

(3.74)

Then Lemma
3.20
implies that the Schwartz kernel of

has exponential decay as in (
3.62
).

Now, for a fixed
x_{0}\in X
and arbitrary

, we decompose the vector
(Z_{1},Z_{2})\in T_{x_{0}\times x_{0}}(X\times X)
into tangential and normal part

(Z_{1},Z_{2})=Z_{H}+Z_{N},

(3.75)

where

and

for a given

.
Clearly, we then have

Z_{H}^{0}=\frac{kZ_{1}+lZ_{2}}{k+l},\qquad Z_{N}=\Big(\frac{l(Z_{1}-Z_{2})}{k+l},\frac{k(Z_{2}-Z_{1})}{k+l}\Big).

(3.76)

An easy calculation, using the formula for
\mathscr{P}_{n}
and the above formulas for

shows that

\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{k}Z_{1},\sqrt{k}Z_{0}\big)\cdot\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{l}Z_{2},\sqrt{l}Z_{0}\big)\\
=\mathscr{P}_{n}\big(\sqrt{k+l}Z_{H}^{0},\sqrt{k+l}Z_{0}\big)\cdot\exp\Big(-\frac{\pi}{2}|Z_{N}|^{2}_{k,l}\Big).

(3.77)

Also, up to the second order, the following identity holds

\psi_{y_{0}}^{k,l}(Z_{H}^{0},Z_{N})=(\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{1}),\phi_{y_{0}}^{X}(Z_{2})).

(3.78)

Hence, by (
3.77
) and Lemmas
3.20
,
3.21
,
3.22
, we see that all the assumptions of Lemma
3.23
are satisfied for the operator
R_{k,l}
.
An application of Lemma
3.23
, hence, finishes the proof.

,

,

and
1.8
.
The main tool in this study is the Fubini-Study operator from Section

.

The section is organized as follows.
In Section

, we establish that plurisubharmonic metrics give rise to multiplicatively generated norms.
More precisely, we show Theorem

In Section

,

,

We will also explain how this result falls into a more general picture of approximations of

.
This section is essentially independent from the rest of the text, except for Section

In Section

For any graded multiplicatively generated norm

Theorem

The answer is negative, see Section

Nevertheless, the following result from Section

Assume that for a graded norm

on

, the sequence of metrics

converges uniformly, as

, to a (continuous psh) metric

on

, and there is

and a function

, as

), we have the following bound

4.1
Multiplicatively generated norms from plurisubharmonic metrics

The main goal of this section is to establish that plurisubharmonic metrics give rise to multiplicatively generated norms through the Hilbert map.
In other words, we prove Theorem
1.5
.

The proof of this result is decomposed into two parts: establishing the lower bound and the upper bound.
For the upper bound, the following simple observation is crucial.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
h^{L}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq p_{0}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, under the map (
1.2
), the following inequality holds

{\rm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(h^{L})\leq\Big[{\rm{Ban}}_{k_{1}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\otimes_{\epsilon}\cdots\otimes_{\epsilon}{\rm{Ban}}_{k_{r}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\Big].

(4.2)

Proof.

From Theorem
3.6
, we see that under the Künneth isomorphism

H^{0}(X\times\cdots\times X,L^{k_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L^{k_{r}})\to H^{0}(X,L^{k_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{k_{r}}),

(4.3)

the norm
{\rm{Ban}}_{k_{1}}^{\infty}(h^{L})\otimes_{\epsilon}\cdots\otimes_{\epsilon}{\rm{Ban}}_{k_{r}}^{\infty}(h^{L})

gets pulled-back to the
L^{\infty}
-norm on
H^{0}(X\times\cdots\times X,L^{k_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L^{k_{r}})
.
The result now follows directly from this, the fact that multiplication operator can be seen as the the restriction operator, cf. (
3.4
), and the trivial fact that the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the restriction of a given section is always no bigger than the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the section.
∎

For the lower bound, we will need the following slightly more complicated result.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that the measure
\mu
on
X
can be majorized from below and above by some strictly positive volume forms on
X
.
Then there are
C>0
,
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
h^{L}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq p_{0}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, under the map (
1.2
), the following inequality holds

\Big[{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{1}}(h^{L},\mu)\otimes\cdots\otimes{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{r}}(h^{L},\mu)\Big]\leq C^{r}\cdot{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu).

(4.4)

Proof.

Clearly, by Corollary
B.7
, it is enough to establish this result for
r=2
, as for other
r
it would follow by induction.
Once the definition of the quotient norm is unraveled, it becomes clear that it suffices to prove that for any
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k_{1}+k_{2}})
, there is a tensor
h\in H^{0}(X,L^{k_{1}})\otimes H^{0}(X,L^{k_{2}})
, verifying
{\rm{Mult}}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(h)=f
, such that

\|h\|_{L^{2}_{k_{1}}(X,h^{L})\otimes L^{2}_{k_{2}}(X,h^{L})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(X,h^{L})}.

(4.5)

This, however, follows directly from the interpretation of the multiplication map of the section ring as an instance of the restriction map, see (
3.4
), and a statement similar to Theorem
2.5
, where instead of
L^{k}
, we consider
L^{k_{1}}\boxtimes L^{k_{2}}
.
The proof of the latter statement is done analogously to the proof of Theorem
2.5
, and is left to the interested reader.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

From Corollary
2.11
, Lemma
4.3
, (
2.23
) and the fact that the injective tensor norm is majorized from above by the Hermitian tensor norm, cf. Lemma
B.2
, we conclude that there is a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{N}\to\real
,
f(k)=o(k)
, such that in the notations of Lemma
4.4
, we have

{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\leq\Big[{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{1}}(h^{L})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{r}}(h^{L})\Big]\cdot\exp\Big(f(k_{1})+\cdots+f(k_{r})\Big).

(4.6)

Similarly, from Lemma
4.4
and the second part of Corollary
2.11
, we conclude that there is a function
g\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{N}\to\real
,
g(k)=o(k)
, such that in the notations of Lemma
4.4
we have

{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\geq\Big[{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{1}}(h^{L})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{r}}(h^{L})\Big]\cdot\exp\Big(g(k_{1})+\cdots+g(k_{r})+g(k)\Big).

(4.7)

We deduce Theorem
1.5
from (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).
∎

4.2
Metric properties of the Hilbert map and Mabuchi supremum geometry

The main goal of this section is to study the asymptotic metric properties of the map
{\rm{Hilb}}
.
More precisely, we compare the norms
{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{0}),{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{1})
for
h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
, as
k\to\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

By Corollary
2.11
, we can study the distance between the images of the map
{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}
instead of
{\rm{Hilb}}^{\infty}
.
Trivially, for any metrics
h^{L}_{0}
,
h^{L}_{1}
on
L
, we obtain that

d_{\infty}({\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}_{0}),{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}_{1}))\leq d_{\infty}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}).

(4.8)

Let us now establish the opposite bound.
Our idea is to construct, using the Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem, a holomorphic section of
L^{k}
, with the mass concentrated in a neighborhood of a fixed point.
More precisely, by Theorem
2.9
, Proposition
2.10
and Lemmas
2.8
,
2.17
, we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq p_{1}
,
x\in X
, there is
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, verifying the following inequality

|f(x)|_{1}\geq\exp(-\epsilon k)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L}_{1})},

(4.9)

where
|\cdot|_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are the pointwise norms induced by
h^{L}_{i}
.
Remark, however, that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L}_{0})}\geq|f(x)|_{0},

(4.10)

and we clearly have

|f(x)|_{0}=\Big(\frac{h^{L}_{0}}{h^{L}_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}}(x)\cdot|f(x)|_{1}.

(4.11)

From (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq p_{1}
,
x\in X
, there is
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, such that we have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L}_{0})}\geq\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot\Big(\frac{h^{L}_{0}}{h^{L}_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}}(x)\cdot\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{k}(X,h^{L}_{1})}.

(4.12)

Moreover, one can interchange the roles of
h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}
in (
4.12
). In particular, we obtain that

d_{\infty}({\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}_{0}),{\rm{Ban}}^{\infty}(h^{L}_{1}))\geq d_{\infty}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}).

(4.13)

From (
4.8
) and (
4.13
), we deduce Theorem
1.7
.
∎

Theorem
1.7
goes in line with the general philosophy that the geometry of
\mathcal{H}^{L}
can be approximated by the geometry of the space of norms on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
, as
k\to\infty
, see Donaldson
[
25
]
.
To explain this precisely, we recall the basics of the geometry on the space of Kähler potentials.
To make our presentation compatible with the notations introduced previously, we will only work with Kähler potentials in the integral class
c_{1}(L)
, but everything we say below can be trivially extended to the case of a general (non-integral) Kähler class.

First of all, remark that the tangent space
T\mathcal{H}^{L}
of continuous psh metrics
\mathcal{H}^{L}
on
L
can be seen as a subbundle of the trivial vector bundle

T\mathcal{H}^{L}\subset\mathcal{H}^{L}\times\mathscr{C}^{0}(X),

(4.14)

as follows.
Let
h_{t}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, be a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-path in
\mathcal{H}^{L}
.
Then
\dot{h}_{t}^{L}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(h_{t}^{L})^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}h_{t}^{L}\in\mathscr{C}^{0}(X)
gives the needed tangent vector in the identification.

For
p\in[1,+\infty]
, let us define the
L^{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for
u\in T\mathcal{H}^{L}
as follows

\|u\|_{p}=\Big(\frac{1}{\int c_{1}(L)^{n}}\int_{X}|v|^{p}(x)\cdot c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(4.15)

where
u=(h^{L},v)
in the identification (
4.14
) and
c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}
is the measure constructed from the procedure of Bedford-Taylor, see (
2.17
).

In particular, in the most relevant case for this article,
p=+\infty
, we have

\|u\|_{\infty}={\rm{ess\,sup}}|v|,

(4.16)

where the essential supremum over
X
is taken with respect to the measure
c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}
.

For
p=2
, this (Hermitian) norm in the context of Kähler potentials was defined by Mabuchi.
For other
p
, it was studied by Darvas in
[
16
]
.

We define the
L^{\infty}
-length
{\rm{len}}(l)
, of a piece-wise
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-curve
l(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, as

{\rm{len}}(l)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big\|\dot{h}_{t}^{L}\big\|_{\infty}dt.

(4.17)

The normalizing factor
\frac{1}{2}
, usually omitted in this setting, is here to make things compatible with our previous normalizations, adapted to the study of norms and not metrics.
We define the supremum (
L^{\infty}
)-pseudodistance
{\rm{dist}}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
between
h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
as follows

{\rm{dist}}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})=\inf{\rm{len}}(l),

(4.18)

where the infimum is taken among all possible piece-wise
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-paths
l
from
h^{L}_{0}
to
h^{L}_{1}
.

The fact that
{\rm{dist}}
is actually a distance (i.e. separates distinct points) is a nontrivial result in this infinite dimensional setting.
Since
L^{\infty}
-distance introduced above obviously dominates
L^{p}
-distances for any
p\in[1,\infty[
, for smooth endpoints, the fact that
{\rm{dist}}
is actually a distance follows from the work of Chen
[
13
]
, who proved that the
L^{2}
-pseudodistance is actually a distance. For general endpoints it follows from Darvas
[
16
]
, who refined this statement for
p=1
.

Our interest in this metric comes from the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

The distance
{\rm{dist}}
from (
4.18
), and the
d_{\infty}
from (
1.18
), coincide.

Remark 4.6
.

If we combine Theorems
1.7
and
4.5
, we see that the limit of the Goldman-Iwahori distances on the space of norms on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
coincides with the
L^{\infty}
-Mabuchi distance on
\mathcal{H}^{L}
.
It complements the results of Chen-Sun
[
14
]
, who proved a similar result relating the distance induced by the Hilbert-Shmidt norm on the space of norms on
H^{0}(X,L^{k})
and the Mabuchi distance on
\mathcal{H}^{L}
, Berndtsson
[
6
]
who generalized the last statement for the
L^{p}
-distances with smooth positive exteremities,
1\leq p<+\infty
, and Darvas-Lu-Rubinstein
[
18
]
, who generalized the statement of Berndtsson for paths with non-smooth extremities.

The proof of this result is decomposed into a number of statements, which essentially follow from the works of Berndtsson, Darvas and Lempert.
To explain it in details, we need to recall the notion of weak geodesics.
Let
h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}
be two smooth positive metrics on
L
.
A geodesic equation (with respect to
L^{2}
-metric) for a smooth curve
h^{L}_{t}
connecting
h^{L}_{0}
and
h^{L}_{1}
was found by Mabuchi
[
40
]
.
Later, Semmes
[
50
]
and Donaldson
[
25
]
independently discovered that this equation can be understood as a complex Monge-Ampère equation.

More precisely, they proved that if
h^{L}_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, is a geodesic segment in the sense of Mabuchi, then for a Hermitian metric
\tilde{h}^{L}
on
L
, over
X\times S_{1,2}
,
S_{1,2}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{s\in\mathbb{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}1\leq\operatorname{Re}s\leq 2\}
, defined as
\tilde{h}^{L}|_{X\times u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{\operatorname{Re}u-1}
,
u\in S_{1,2}
, the following identity over
X\times S_{1,2}
holds

c_{1}(L,\tilde{h}^{L})^{n+1}=0.

(4.19)

Analogously to the smooth setting, a segment
h^{L}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, is called
weak geodesic
if the complexification of it,
\tilde{h}^{L}
, constructed as above, satisfies (
4.19
) in the Bedford-Taylor sense.

The existence and uniqueness of weak geodesics
h^{L}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, with smooth positive extremities
h^{L}_{0}
,
h^{L}_{1}
, was established by Chen
[
13
]
.
Chen, moreover, proved that in the above notations,
c_{1}(L,\tilde{h}^{L})
is a bounded
(1,1)
-form.
By standard regularity results, it implies that the path
h^{L}_{t}
viewed as a metric over
X\times[0,1]
is
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}
, for any
0\leq\alpha<1
.
From the general theory of Dirichlet problem for complex Monge-Ampère equations of Bedford-Taylor
[
2
, Theorem D]
, weak geodesics exist more generally for
h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
.
Those geodesics are moreover unique by the “minimum principle”, see
[
2
, Theorem A]
.
The following result explains their relevance for us.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
h^{L}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, be a weak geodesic.
Then
{\rm{dist}}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{h}_{0}^{L}\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Lempert
[
35
, Theorem 8.1]
.
In fact, in the notations of Lempert, the Lagrangian
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is convex and invariant under strict rearrangements, see
[
35
, Definition 2.1]
for necessary definitions.
Hence,
[
35
, Theorem 8.1]
applies.
∎

The next result we need relates the right-hand side from Lemma
4.7
with weak geodesics.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
h^{L}_{t}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
,
t\in[0,1]
, be a weak geodesic with smooth positive extremities
h^{L}_{0}
,
h^{L}_{1}
.
Then the essential supremums of the velocities of
h^{L}_{t}
are constant in
t\in[0,1]
, and, despite the fact that
h^{L}_{t}
are not necessarily strictly positive, they coincide with the supremums.
More precisely, for any
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,1]
, we have

\|\dot{h}_{t_{0}}^{L}\|_{\infty}=\sup|\dot{h}_{t_{0}}^{L}|=\sup|\dot{h}_{t_{1}}^{L}|=\|\dot{h}_{t_{1}}^{L}\|_{\infty}=2\cdot d_{\infty}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}).

(4.20)

Proof.

First of all, by an estimate of Darvas
[
17
, Theorem 3.4]
, for any
t\in[0,1]
, we have

2\cdot d_{\infty}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})\geq\sup|\dot{h}_{t}^{L}|.

(4.21)

Now, since the path
h^{L}_{t}
is
\mathscr{C}^{1}
, for any
x\in X
, we have

\log\frac{h^{L}_{1}(x)}{h^{L}_{0}(x)}=\int_{0}^{1}\dot{h}_{t}^{L}(x).

(4.22)

In particular, we obtain that

\Big|\log\frac{h^{L}_{1}(x)}{h^{L}_{0}(x)}\Big|\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\dot{h}_{t}^{L}(x)|.

(4.23)

By combining (
4.21
) with (
4.23
) and using the fact that
\sup_{x\in X}|\dot{h}_{t}^{L}(x)|
is continuous for
t\in[0,1]
, we obtain that for any
t\in[0,1]
, we have

2\cdot d_{\infty}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})=\sup_{x\in X}|\dot{h}_{t}^{L}(x)|.

(4.24)

On the other hand, since
h^{L}_{0}
is smooth and positive,
c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{0})^{n}
is a smooth (strictly) positive volume form.
Hence the essential supremum of
|\dot{h}_{0}^{L}|
coincides with the supremum.
In other words, we have the following identity

\sup_{x\in X}|\dot{h}_{0}^{L}(x)|=\|\dot{h}_{0}^{L}\|_{\infty}.

(4.25)

Recall, however, that by a result of Berndtsson
[
6
, Proposition 2.2]
, for any function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{0}(\real)
, and any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1]
, the following conservation law holds

\int_{X}f(\dot{h}_{t_{1}}^{L})c_{1}(L,h_{t_{1}}^{L})^{n}=\int_{X}f(\dot{h}_{t_{2}}^{L})c_{1}(L,h_{t_{2}}^{L})^{n}.

(4.26)

In particular, by choosing a sequence of functions
f
appropriately, we obtain that

\|\dot{h}_{t_{1}}^{L}\|_{\infty}=\|\dot{h}_{t_{2}}^{L}\|_{\infty}.

(4.27)

A combination of (
4.24
), (
4.25
) and (
4.27
) finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

A combination of Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
gives the result for smooth positive extremities.
Remark, however, that any
h^{L}\in\mathcal{H}^{L}
can be approximated uniformly by smooth positive metrics
h^{L}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
To see this, consider the approximation
h^{L}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=FS({\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}))
, and use Theorem
2.15
.
The result now follows by the fact that Theorem
4.5
holds for
h^{L}_{k}
, by approximation and triangle inequality.
∎

4.3
Fubini-Study operator on graded multiplicatively generated norms

The main goal of this section is to study the convergence of the Fubini-Study operator associated to graded pieces of multiplicatively generated norms, i.e. to prove Theorem
4.1
.

The proof of this result decomposes into a series of small statements.
Our first lemma, which is a generalization of Theorem
1.1
, was the starting point for the formulation of Definition
1.3
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any smooth positive metric
h^{L}
on
L
, there are
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
C>0
, such that for any
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq p_{0}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, under the map (
1.2
), we have

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Big(1-\frac{C}{k_{i}}\Big)\leq\frac{[{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{1}}(h^{L})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\rm{Hilb}}_{k_{r}}(h^{L})]}{{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})}\cdot\Big(\frac{k_{1}\cdots k_{r}}{k}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Big(1+\frac{C}{k_{i}}\Big).

(4.28)

Remark 4.10
.

In particular, the graded norms
{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L})
are multiplicatively generated for smooth positive
h^{L}
, and we can take
f(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f^{\prime}(k)=\frac{n}{2}\log(k)+2+\log\dim H^{0}(X,L^{k})
in Definition
1.3
.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
B.7
by induction.
∎

In the next lemma we study how the Fubini-Study maps associated for different parameters
k
relate to each other.

Lemma 4.11
.

Assume that
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
L^{p_{0}}
is very ample. Then for any Hermitian graded norm
N=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}N_{k}
on
R(X,L)
and for any
r\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq p_{0}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, the following identity is satisfied

{\rm{FS}}(N_{k_{1}})\cdots{\rm{FS}}(N_{k_{r}})={\rm{FS}}([N_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes N_{k_{r}}]).

(4.29)

Proof.

For simplicity, let us establish the result for
N=2
, as the general case is an easy adaptation of this particular one.
To simplify the notation, let us fix two sufficiently big numbers
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
.
Recall that the Segre embedding

\begin{CD}X@>{{\rm{Kod}}_{k}\times{\rm{Kod}}_{l}}>{}>\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L^{k})^{*})\times\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L^{l})^{*})\\

This isomorphism becomes an isometry if the tautological line bundle over

({\rm{Mult}}_{k,l})^{*}

Remark, however, that by Lemmas

This now finishes the proof by the commutativity of square (

4.4
An example of a graded Hermitian norm on the section ring

The main goal of this section is emphasize that the equivalence class of a graded Hermitian metric cannot be determined from the convergence of the Fubini-Study operator.

Proposition 4.15
.

There is a graded Hermitian norm
N=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}N_{k}
on
R(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(1))
, for which
FS(N_{k})^{\frac{1}{k}}
converge uniformly, as
k\to\infty
, to a fixed smooth positive metric on
\mathscr{O}(1)
, but
N
is not equivalent to
{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{L})
for any continuous psh metric
h^{L}
on
\mathscr{O}(1)
.

Proof.

Let us identify
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
, where
V
is a vector space generated by two elements:
x
and
y
.
Let us consider a Hermitian metric
H
on
V
, which makes
x
and
y
an orthonormal basis, and denote by
h^{FS}
the induced Fubini-Study metric on
\mathscr{O}(1)
.
Recall that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a classical isomorphism

{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(V)\to H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(V^{*}),\mathscr{O}(k)).

(4.41)

Under this isomorphism, for any
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
,
a+b=k
, an easy calculation shows that we have

\big\|x^{a}\cdot y^{b}\big\|_{{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{FS})}^{2}=\frac{a!b!}{(k+1)!}.

(4.42)

We draw the attention of the reader that the above norm doesn’t coincide with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(H)}
on
{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(V)
induced by
H
. In fact, an easy calculation gives

\big\|x^{a}\cdot y^{b}\big\|_{{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(H)}^{2}=\frac{a!b!}{k!}.

(4.43)

Let us consider a Hermitian norm
H_{k}
on
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(V^{*}),\mathscr{O}(k))
, such that the basis
x^{a}\cdot y^{b}
is orthogonal and in the above notations, we have

\big\|x^{a}\cdot y^{b}\big\|_{H_{k}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }|a-b|\leq 1\\
\big\|x^{a}\cdot y^{b}\big\|_{{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{FS})},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.44)

We will now verify that
H_{k}
satisfies the assumptions of the proposition.
For simplicity, we only work with metrics
H_{k}
with
k
even.
By Stirling’s formula and (
4.42
), we have

\frac{\big\|x^{k}\cdot y^{k}\big\|^{2}_{H_{2k}}}{\big\|x^{k}\cdot y^{k}\big\|^{2}_{{\rm{Hilb}}_{2k}(h^{FS})}}\geq 2^{k}.

(4.45)

Hence, the graded norm
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}
is not equivalent to
{\rm{Hilb}}(h^{FS})
.
We will now show that the Fubini-Study metric of
H_{k}
converges uniformly, as
k\to\infty
, to
h^{FS}
.
This would clearly imply by Theorem
2.15
that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}
satisfies the assumptions of the proposition.

For this, from Lemma
2.14
, we see that for any
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, not simultaneously equal to zero, the following identity between the Fubini-Study metrics of
H_{k}
and
{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{FS})
holds

1-\frac{FS({\rm{Hilb}}_{2k}(h^{FS}))}{FS(H_{2k})}\Big([ax^{*}+by^{*}]\Big)=\Big(\frac{(2k)!}{k!k!}-1\Big)\cdot\frac{|ab|^{k}}{(|a|+|b|)^{2k}}.

(4.46)

However, by mean inequality and Stirling’s formula, we obtain that there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\Big|\Big(\frac{(2k)!}{k!k!}-1\Big)\cdot\frac{|ab|^{k}}{(|a|+|b|)^{2k}}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}.

(4.47)

From (
4.46
), (
4.47
) and Theorem
2.15
,
FS(H_{k})^{\frac{1}{k}}
converges uniformly, as
k\to\infty
, to
h^{FS}
.
∎

As the following example of László Lempert shows, a phenomenon similar to Proposition
4.15
holds in the non-asymptotic regime as well.

Proposition 4.16

(László Lempert)
.

There are sequences of Hermitian metrics
H_{\delta}
,
H^{\prime}_{\delta}
,
\delta\in]0,1]
over
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}(2))
, such that, as
\delta\to\infty
,
\frac{FS(H_{\delta})}{FS(H^{\prime}_{\delta})}\to 1
, but there is
c>0
such that
d_{\infty}(H_{\delta},H^{\prime}_{\delta})>c
for any
\delta\in]0,1]
.

Remark 4.17
.

See also Lempert
[
36
]
for a related result about injectivity of the Fubini-Study map.

Proof.

We use similar notations as in the proof of Proposition
4.15
.
Let
H_{\delta}
(resp.
H^{\prime}_{\delta}
) be so that the basis
(s_{1,\delta},s_{2,\delta},s_{3,\delta})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(xy,2xy+2\delta y^{2},x^{2}+y^{2})
(resp.
(2s_{1,\delta},\frac{1}{2}s_{2,\delta},s_{3,\delta})
) is orthonormal with respect to
H_{\delta}
(resp.
H^{\prime}_{\delta}
). We now verify that such choice satisfies the assumptions of the proposition.
The second condition is obvious since
H_{\delta}(s_{1,\delta},s_{1,\delta})=1
, but
H^{\prime}_{\delta}(s_{1,\delta},s_{1,\delta})=\frac{1}{4}
.
Now, from Lemma
2.14
, we have

\frac{FS(H_{\delta})}{FS(H^{\prime}_{\delta})}=\frac{4|s_{1,\delta}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|s_{2,\delta}|^{2}+|s_{3,\delta}|^{2}}{|s_{1,\delta}|^{2}+|s_{2,\delta}|^{2}+|s_{3,\delta}|^{2}},

(4.48)

where
|\cdot|
is any norm on
\mathscr{O}(2)
.
From (
4.48
),
\frac{FS(H_{\delta})}{FS(H^{\prime}_{\delta})}
tends to
1
uniformly, as
\delta\to 0
.
∎

4.5
Inductive construction of the Hilbert map, a proof of Theorem
4.2

The main goal of this section is to establish Theorem
4.2
.
The proof is done using the following result providing the inductive construction of the Hilbert map.

Assume for simplicity that
L
is very ample and the multiplication map
{\rm{Mult}}_{1,1}
is surjective (hence, all multiplication maps are surjective as well).
Let
H_{1}
be any Hermitian norm on

Then using the map (
1.2

.

The graded Hermitian norms

Remark 4.19
.

a) The Fubini-Study operators for both of those norms coincide by Theorem

It doesn’t, however, imply the equivalence of the graded norms by Section

b)

Not only in Lemma

c) From Corollary

is multiplicatively generated.

4.18
is a special case of Theorem
4.2

4.11
.
Our proof of Theorem
4.2

4.18
.

Before proceeding with the proof of Theorem
4.18

4.2
.

4.2

4.18
.

Let us fix

,

|f(k)|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{5}k.

(4.50)

Now, let us fix
p_{0}=\max\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
,

[N_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes N_{k}]

1.2
) on
H^{0}(X,L^{kr})

r

4.18

p_{3}\geq p_{0}

d_{\infty}\Big([N_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes N_{k}],{\rm{Hilb}}_{rk}(FS(N_{k})^{\frac{1}{k}})\Big)<\frac{\epsilon}{5}r.

(4.51)

From Corollary
2.11
, (
4.8
), (
4.49
) and (
4.51

Proof of Theorem
4.18
.

Our proof is essentially based on two ingredients.
First, we need to verify Theorem
4.18
for the projective space by an explicit calculation.
Second, we apply the semiclassical version of Ohsawa-Takegoshi extension theorem, Theorem
2.6
, for the Kodaira embedding to extend Theorem
4.18
from projective spaces to general manifolds.

Let us first do the calculations for the projective space
X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
, where
V
is a fixed vector space of dimension
n
.
In this case, we take
L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathscr{O}(1)
.
Then through the identification of

with
V
, we may view the Hermitian metric
H_{1}
on

as a Hermitian metric on

.

on

A direct calculation shows that the Hermitian norm

induced on

by

under the identification (
4.41
).
Now, as it was already exemplified during the proof of Proposition

, but the following identity holds

{\rm{Hilb}}_{k}(FS(H_{1}))=\sqrt{\frac{k!}{(k+n-1)!}}\cdot{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(H_{1}).

(4.60)

Note that the factor in (

(in particular, it is subexponential).

4.18
holds in this particular case.

Now, to establish Theorem
4.18
in its full generality, let us consider the Kodaira embedding
{\rm{Kod}}_{1}
from (

We denote by

(4.61)

{\rm{Mult}}_{1,\cdots,1}
from (

) factorizes under the identification (

) of

through the symmetrization,

, and the restriction operator as follows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(X,L)^{\otimes k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm{Sym}}^{k}(H^{0}(X,L))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,L)^{*}),\mathscr{O}(k))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(X,L^{k}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm{Mult}}_{1,\cdots,1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm{Sym}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm{Res}}_{{\rm{Kod}},k}}

(4.62)

Now, the proof goes as follows.
From the first part of the proof of Theorem

, we obtain that the quotient metric on

associated to

is equivalent under the isomorphism (

or Corollary

∎

4.6
Mabuchi geodesics and geodesics on the space of norms

The main goal of this section is to prove a relation between Mabuchi geodesics and geodesics in the space of norms, announced in Theorem

Appendix A
On almost sub-additive sequences of functions

The main goal of this section is to study almost sub-additive sequences.
More precisely, in Section
A.1
, we state the analogue of Fekete’s lemma.
In Section
A.2
, we study the uniform convergence of sub-additive sequences of functions and state the analogue of Dini’s theorem.

A.1
A version of Fekete’s lemma for almost sub-additive sequences

In this section we discuss a generalization of Fekete’s lemma, cf.
[
26
]
, studying sub-additive sequences of real numbers.
We will show that the conclusion of the lemma is still valid if the sequence is sub-additive up to a small error term.

Recall that Bruijn-Erdős in
[
19
, Theorem 23]
considered sequences for which the sub-additivity condition
a_{m+n}\leq a_{m}+a_{n}
is replaced by a weaker one

a_{m+n}\leq a_{m}+a_{n}+g_{m+n}.

(A.1)

As they show, a conclusion of Fekete’s lemma only holds under the assumption

\sum\frac{g_{n}}{n^{2}}<+\infty.

(A.2)

As this condition doesn’t appear quite naturally in geometric setting, such as Bernstein-Markov property, we find it unsuitable for the encapsulation of the features of the
L^{2}
-norm as we do it in Definition
1.3
.
We, thus, consider an alternative condition, which demands more from the sequence
a_{n}
and less from the error term.

Definition A.1
.

We say that a sequence of numbers
a_{n}\in\real
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is
almost sub-additive
if for any
r\geq 2
,
k;k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq p_{0}
,
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k
, the following inequality holds

a_{k}\leq a_{k_{1}}+\cdots+a_{k_{r}}+f_{k_{1}}+\cdots+f_{k_{r}}+f_{k},

(A.3)

where
f_{k}
, verifies
f_{k}=o(k)
, as
k\to\infty
.
We say that
a_{k}
is
almost super-additive
if the above inequality holds with a reverse sign.
In the latter case we denote the sequence
f_{k}
by
g_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma A.2

(Chen
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Assume that a sequence
a_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is almost sub-additive and non-negative.
Then the sequence
\frac{a_{n}}{n}
has a finite limit, as
n\to\infty
.

Corollary A.3
.

Assume that a sequence
a_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is both almost sub-additive and almost super-additive.
Then the sequence
\frac{a_{n}}{n}
has a finite limit, as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
a_{n}
is super-additive, we have

a_{n}\geq nc_{n},

(A.4)

where
c_{n}
is defined as
c_{n}=a_{1}+\frac{g_{n}}{n}
for a sequence
g\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathbb{N}\to\real
from Definition
A.1
.
Clearly, by our assumption on
g_{n}
, the sequence
c_{n}
is bounded from below by
c\in\real
.
We can now apply Lemma
A.2
for a sequence
a_{n}-nc
to establish the result.
∎

A.2
Uniform convergence of almost sub-additive sequences of functions

The main goal of this section is to study the uniform convergence of almost sub-additive sequences of functions.
The following definition will be particularly useful later on.

Definition A.4
.

A sequence of functions
a_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, defined over a topological space
Y
will be called
uniformly almost sub-additive
if for any
x\in Y
, the sequence
a_{k}(x)
is almost sub-additive, and in the definition of almost sub-additivity the sequences
f_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f_{n}(x)
can be chosen in such way that the convergence
\frac{f_{k}}{k}\to 0
holds locally uniformly on
Y
, as
k\to\infty
. Similarly, we define the notion of
uniformly almost super-additive
sequences of functions.

The following lemma is an analogue of Dini’s theorem in our setting.

Lemma A.5
.

Assume that the sequence of non-negative functions
a_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, defined over a compact space
Y
, is uniformly almost sub-additive.
Assume also that the pointwise limit
a\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
of
\frac{a_{k}}{k}
, as
k\to\infty
, which exists by Lemma
A.2
, is a continuous function.
Then the convergence of
\frac{a_{k}}{k}
to
a
is uniform.

Proof.

A straightforward modification of the proof of Dini’s theorem.
∎

The next lemma is an almost-subadditive analogue of a well-known statement for decreasing sequences of functions.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
Y
be any topological space.
Assume
a_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, is a sequence of positive continuous functions which is uniformly almost sub-additive. Then the pointwise limit
a\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
of
\frac{a_{k}}{k}
, as
k\to\infty
, which exists by Lemma
A.2
, is upper semicontinuous.

Proof.

A straightforward modification of the proof for decreasing sequences of functions.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
Y
be a compact topological space.
Assume that
a_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Y\to\real
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, is a sequence of continuous functions which is uniformly almost sub-additive and uniformly almost super-additive at the same time.
Then the sequence
\frac{a_{k}}{k}
has a continuous limit
a
, and the convergence of
\frac{a_{k}}{k}
to
a
is uniform.

Proof.

Uniform almost super-additivity implies that there is
C>0
such that
a_{k}\geq-Ck
, see (
A.4
) and the paragraph after for the details.
An easy verification shows that the sequence
h_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=a_{k}+Ck
then satisfies all the assumptions of Lemma
A.6
and hence the limit
a
is upper semicontinuous.
We repeat the same argument for the sequence
-a_{k}
to obtain that
a
is lower semicontinuous.
Hence,
a
is continuous.
The result now follows from Lemma
A.5
, applied to
h_{k}
.
∎

The following identity holds

A trivial verification.

\displaystyle=\big(\mathscr{C}^{0}(K_{1}\times K_{2}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{1\times 2}}\big),

where the tensor product in the second line means the tensor product of Hilbert spaces.
In particular, under the natural isomorphism
\real{}^{n}\otimes\real^{m}\to\real^{n\cdot m}
, for norms on

A Weakly Turbulent solution to the cubic Nonlinear Harmonic Oscillator on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} perturbed by a real smooth potential decaying to zero at infinity
Maxine Chabert

Abstract
We build a smooth real potential V ⁡ ( t , x ) V(t,x) on ( t 0 , + ∞ ) × ℝ 2 (t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2} decaying to zero as t → ∞ t\to\infty and a smooth solution to the associated perturbed cubic Nonlinear Harmonic Oscillator whose Sobolev norms blow up logarithmically as t → ∞ t\to\infty . Adapting the method of Faou and Raphael for the linear case, we modulate the solitons associated to the Nonlinear Harmonic Oscillator by time-dependent parameters solving a quasi-Hamiltonian dynamical system whose action grows up logarithmically, thus yielding logarithmic growth for the Sobolev norm of the solution.



Keywords—
Nonlinear Harmonic Oscillator, weak turbulence, solitons, modulations, perturbing potential, backward integration

Introduction

1.1
Setting of the problem and main result

We introduce the linear operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C})
associated to the quantum Harmonic Oscillator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.1)

H:=-\Delta+|x|^{2}

where for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we denote
|x|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}
and
\Delta
is the Laplace operator. To this operator are associated the modified Sobolev function spaces defining the domain of
H

(1.2)

\forall r\geqslant 0\hskip 18.49988ptH_{x}^{r}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C})\hskip 9.24994ptH^{\frac{r}{2}}u\in L^{2}\right\}

An open problem is to exhibit a solution to the nonlinear defocusing Schrödinger-type equation

(1.3)

i\partial_{t}u=Hu+u|u|^{2}

whose
H_{x}^{r}
norm blows up in infinite time i.e. such that for some
r>0

(1.4)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{H_{x}^{r}}=+\infty

As the
L^{2}
norm is preserved by equation (
1.3
), such a phenomenon would be the result of energy transfer from low to high frequencies generating growth of Sobolev norms, thanks to the nonlinearity (indeed, should we remove the
u|u|^{2}
term, the Sobolev norms would be preserved).

In this paper, we make a step toward this direction by building a smooth solution to the similar equation

(1.5)

i\partial_{t}u=Hu+u|u|^{2}+Vu

where
V(t,x)
is a smooth real potential such that
V
decays to zero when
t\to\infty
in
H_{x}^{r}
norms, that is

(1.6)

\forall r\geqslant 0\hskip 18.49988pt\lim_{t\to\infty}\|V(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{x}^{r}}=0

We are able to exhibit a smooth solution
u
of (
1.5
) whose
H_{x}^{1}
norm has infinite limit when
t\to\infty
. Precisely, we prove the following theorem

Theorem 1.1
.

There are a
t_{0}>0
, a potential
V(t,x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
and a function
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C})
such that

(1.7)

\forall(t,x)\in(t_{0},+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\hskip 18.49988pti\partial_{t}u(t,x)=Hu(t,x)+u(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{2}+V(t,x)u(t,x)

Moreover, for all
r\geqslant 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}

(1.8)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|\partial_{t}^{k}V(t,x)\|_{H_{x}^{r}}=0

Finally, there are constants
c,C>0
such that

(1.9)

c\left(\log t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\|u(t)\|_{H_{x}^{1}}\leqslant C\left(\log t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 18.49988ptt\to\infty

1.2
Earlier works

Firstly, the question of finding solution to (
1.3
) with unbounded
H_{x}^{r}
norm as

(1.10)

i\partial_{t}u=-\Delta u+u|u|^{2}

Now, it is reasonable to compare the Laplacian operator on the torus to the Harmonic Oscillator on the Euclidian space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as they both have a discreet spectrum formed of a sequence of eigenvalues. Thus, the question of transfer of energy from low frequencies to high frequencies in NLS is very similar to the same question for the Nonlinear Harmonic Oscillator on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(
1.3
).

Important progress has been made which seem to indicate a positive answer to Bourgain’s problem. Kuskin considered in
[

Secondly, our approach is close to the study of the linear case in
[

]
. The question of growth of Sobolev norm for

where

is a smooth time-dependent family of pseudodifferential operators of order

The seminal work of Bourgain
[

establishes that when

1.3
Strategy of proof

We start by studying the equivalent of eigenfunctions for the nonlinear operator
Hu+u|u|^{2}
which are so-called
solitons
, defined as positive solutions to the time-independent elliptic equation

(1.14)

-\Delta Q+|x|^{2}Q+Q|Q|^{2}=\lambda Q

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Their interest is that for each such soliton

(1.15)

u(t,x):=e^{-i\lambda t}Q(x)

is a solution to the unperturbed equation (
1.3
). Section 3 is therefore devoted to establishing important results on solitons.

We will afterwards in section 4
modulate
the periodic solution (
1.15
). Using the approach of
[
FR20
]
for the linear case, the symmetries of equation (
1.3
) yield the ansatz of solution, called a
bubble

(1.16)

u(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda s(t)}\frac{1}{L(t)}e^{-i\frac{b(t)|x|^{2}}{4L(t)^{2}}}Q\left(\frac{x}{L(t)}\right)

where
(L,b)
are smooth real-valued functions and
s(t)
is defined by
\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}>0
such that
s(t)\sim t
when
t\to\infty
. There holds that
u
is a solution to (
1.3
) provided
(L,b)
is a solution to a Hamiltonian dynamical system. This system is parameterized by its preserved
action

(1.17)

E(L,b):=\frac{1}{L^{2}(t)}\left(\frac{b^{2}(t)}{4}+1\right)+L^{2}(t)

Now, the
H_{x}^{1}
norm of the solution (
1.16
) is related to
E
as we will prove

(1.18)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H_{x}^{1}}^{2}\approx E(L,b)C(Q)

where
C(Q)
is a constant depending only of
Q
. Therefore, modulation enables us to build a solution
u
with arbitrarily large
H_{x}^{1}
norm from the soliton
Q
.

KAM theory allows for slightly perturbing the Hamiltonian dynamical system on
(L,b)
to a quasi-Hamiltonian dynamical system where the energy
E(L,b)(t)
grows logarithmically as
t\to\infty
. We will not detail the proof as we use directly a proposition from
[
FR20
]
.

As we slightly perturb the Hamiltonian dynamical system, (
1.16
) doesn’t solve (
1.5
) exactly anymore. Thus, in section 5 and 6, we show that we may refine the ansatz to the exact solution

(1.19)

u(t,x)=e^{-i\lambda s(t)}\frac{1}{L(t)}e^{-i\frac{b(t)|x|^{2}}{4L(t)^{2}}}\left(Q\left(\frac{x}{L(t)}\right)+w\left(s(t),\frac{x}{L(t)}\right)\right)

where
w(s,y)
is a perturbation, that is its
H_{x}^{1}
norm decays to zero as
s\to\infty
. Thus, the
H_{x}^{1}
norm of
u
is equivalent to
E(L,b)(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}
which yields that it grows as
\left(\log(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The problem thus amounts to showing that there is a solution to the equation satisfied by
w
which decays to zero at infinity. We will use a backward integration + Cauchy sequence scheme, that is we first build
w^{M}
as solution such that
w^{M}(M)=0
at
s=M
. We will control uniformly in
M
the decay of
w^{M}
on
(s_{0},M)
, and show that
w^{M}
is a Cauchy sequence in an appropriate function space. Thus, we may extract its limit, which is a solution decaying to zero as
s\to\infty
. It is there that the need for a compensating potential
V
, which we may choose decaying to zero along with all its derivatives in the
H_{x}^{k}
norms, will appear. Let us stress that the need for
V
seems circumstantial to our particular proof, and thus one can hope to find a solution to the unperturbed equation (
1.3
) exhibiting weak turbulence using similar techniques.

Let us remark finally that adapting our proof should allow for logarithmic estimates on the growth of the
H_{x}^{k}
norm of
u
for all
k\geqslant 1
. However, this would greatly increase the technical complexity of the proof without any new original idea. Thus, we have limited ourselves to the
H_{x}^{1}
norm as the growth mechanism is clearer.

Throughout the rest of this paper, we will only consider radial functions, thus all the functions

2.1
Notations

We denote by

\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with integrable squared absolute value. We denote by

. Unless otherwise stated,

will designate

. We introduce two scalar products over

: first, the usual hermitian scalar product

(2.1)

(f,g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\overline{g}

so

may be viewed as a complex Hilbert space. However we can also see

\langle f_{1}+if_{2},g_{1}+ig_{2}\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{1}g_{1}+f_{2}g_{2}

so

can be viewed as a real Hilbert space. As

is just the real part of

, they both induce the same norm. For a complex-valued function

, we will always denote

3.1
Existence, Uniqueness

This subsection is devoted to the demonstration of

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
\lambda>2
there exists a unique nonnegative and nontrivial solution
Q_{\lambda}
to (
1.14
) in
H_{x}^{1}
. Moreover,
Q\in H_{x}^{k}
for all

. Finally, there holds

on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

This proposition ensures in particular that

has
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

(

]
), and

and all its derivatives decay to zero as

.

Let us start the proof with a functional analysis lemma

Lemma 3.1
.

The function space
H_{x}^{1}
is compactly embedded into
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all

Proof.

Following for example
[
BB11
, p.281]

(3.2)

\|u\|_{H_{x}^{1}}^{2}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|xu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Assume

in
H_{x}^{1}
(weak convergence). Then we have that
(u_{n})
is bounded in
H_{x}^{1}
. Find

\forall n,\|u_{n}\|_{H_{x}^{1}}\leqslant M
. Choose

\int_{|x|\geqslant R}|u_{n}|^{2}\leqslant R^{-2}\|xu_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leqslant M^{2}R^{-2}\leqslant\delta

Thus,

is compactly embedded into

Now, given

as it is bounded in

\mathcal{E}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{H}u,u\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle H_{+}H_{-}H_{+}u_{1},u_{1}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle H_{-}H_{+}H_{-}u_{2},u_{2}\rangle

The energy
\mathcal{E}
is preserved by the flow
\exp(s\mathcal{L})
. Moreover, there exists

such that

(5.24)

\forall u\in H_{x}^{3}\hskip 18.49988ptc\langle H^{3}u,u\rangle\leqslant\mathcal{E}(u)+E(u)+\langle u_{2},\rho\rangle^{2}\leqslant C\langle H^{3}u,u\rangle

Proof.

That
\mathcal{E}
is preserved is straightforward from the observation

(5.25)

\langle\mathcal{H}_{3}\mathcal{L}u,u\rangle=0\hskip 18.49988pt\forall u

As for the energy estimates, the right inequality is straightforward, and for the left one we write for example that
H_{+}H_{-}H_{+}=H^{3}+B
where
B
is a sum of terms of the form
A_{1}A_{2}A_{3}
where
A_{i}
is one of
H,Q^{2}-\lambda,3Q^{2}-\lambda
with at most 2 of the

equals to
H
. Thus we see that
|\langle Bu,u\rangle|\leqslant C\|u\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}
. Now, lemma (
2.5
) helps us bound this quantity by
\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H_{x}^{3}}^{2}+C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Therefore

(5.26)

\langle H_{+}H_{-}H_{+}u_{1},u_{1}\rangle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|u_{1}\|_{H_{x}^{3}}^{2}-C\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and we know that
\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}
can be bounded using

and
\langle u_{2},\rho\rangle
. We bound
\|u_{2}\|_{H_{x}^{3}}
in the same way.

∎

Now, to bound the

norm (which is an algebra norm) of a function

, one needs only bound
\mathcal{E}(u)
,

, and
\langle u_{2},\rho\rangle^{2}
.

5.4
Computation of
\exp(s\mathcal{L})
and more spectral theory

We need to have an explicit expression for the linearized flow
\exp(s\mathcal{L})
. We first give a
formal
computation : we can heuristically compute

(5.27)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{2n}=(-1)^{n}\begin{pmatrix}(H_{-}H_{+})^{n}&0\\
0&(H_{+}H_{-})^{n}\end{pmatrix}\\
&\mathcal{L}^{2n+1}=(-1)^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0&H_{-}(H_{+}H_{-})^{n}\\
-H_{+}(H_{-}H_{+})^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

Next, we see that
H_{-}
and
H_{+}
are nonnegative, so we can properly define over
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R})
the unbounded operator

(5.28)

A:=\sqrt{H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{-}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}}

Moreover, as
H_{+}
is invertible, we compute

(5.29)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{2n}&=(-1)^{n}\begin{pmatrix}H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{2n}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
0&H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{2n}H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}\\
\mathcal{L}^{2n+1}&=(-1)^{n}\begin{pmatrix}0&H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{2n+2}H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
-H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{2n}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

which yields

(5.30)

\exp(s\mathcal{L})=\begin{pmatrix}H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cos(s A)H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}&H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A\sin(sA)H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
-H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}s\text{sinc}(sA)H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}&H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cos(sA)H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

where we define
\text{sinc}(x):=\frac{\sin(x)}{x}=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}(-1)^{n}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}

Now, this is only formal. However, if we define
\exp(s\mathcal{L})
by (
5.30
), we see that as
A
is defined on
H_{x}^{3}
then for all
u_{0}\in H_{x}^{3}
there holds as expected that
\exp(s\mathcal{L})u_{0}
is the solution to
\partial_{s}v=\mathcal{L}v
with initial condition
v(0)=u_{0}
.

Lemma 5.5

(Spectrum of
A
)
.

Let
\eta>0
. There exists
\alpha>0
such that whenever
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
,
A
is a nonnegative operator over

, self-adjoint, with compact resolvent. Moreover, its spectrum is formed of simple eigenvalues

with

(5.31)

\forall n\geqslant 0,\hskip 18.49988pt|\mu_{n}-4n|\leqslant\eta

Finally, as
H_{-}
has a nontrivial kernel, so has
A
. Therefore,
\mu_{0}=0

Proof.

Take
\eta
as in the statement, and
\delta>0
small, to be fixed later. Without loss of generality, we assume
\delta<1
.

We study
A^{2}=H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{-}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}=H_{+}^{2}-2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. First, let us study the resolvent of
A^{2}
: we must ask ourselves, given a
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
, when is
A^{2}-\zeta
invertible. To this effect, we introduce
0<\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<...
the spectrum of
H_{+}
, where
\lambda_{n}=4n+\varepsilon_{n}
and we have that the
L^{\infty}
norm of
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}
has limit zero when
\varepsilon\to 0
, and we set
\phi_{0},\phi_{1},...
an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}
such that
H_{+}\phi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}
. We set
D_{0}
the disk of center
0
and radius
\delta
,
D_{1}
the disk of center
16
and radius
\delta
, then
D_{n}
the disk of center
16n^{2}
and radius
n\delta
. We set
\Gamma_{n}
the boundary circle of
D_{n}
positively oriented. We finally set
\Sigma=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 0}D_{n}
.

Find
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\Sigma
. We will show that, provided we take
\varepsilon>0
small enough (depending on
\delta
only),
A^{2}-\zeta
is invertible. First, the nth eigenvalue of
H_{+}^{2}
is
16n^{2}+4n\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{n}^{2}
. Provided
\varepsilon
is small enough, we have for all
n\geqslant 1
that

(5.32)

|16n^{2}-\lambda_{n}^{2}|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta n

and for
n=0
that
|\lambda_{0}^{2}|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta
. Thus,
H_{+}^{2}-\zeta
is invertible and we are able to formally write

(5.33)

(A^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}=(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\left(I-2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\right)^{-1}

where
I
is the identity over
L^{2}
. Our goal is to prove that
2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}
is a bounded operator over
L^{2}
with operator norm strictly smaller than
1
which will prove the invertibility of
A^{2}-\zeta
. To this effect, we first observe that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that given any
v\in H_{x}^{1}
there holds

(5.34)

\langle H_{+}(Q^{2}v),Q^{2}v\rangle\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}\langle H_{+}v,v\rangle

Indeed, we can write
H_{+}=H+B
where
B
is a bounded operator over
L^{2}
, so

(5.35)

\begin{split}\langle H_{+}(Q^{2}v),Q^{2}v\rangle&=\langle H(Q^{2}v),Q^{2}v\rangle+\langle B(Q^{2}v),Q^{2}v\rangle\\
&\leqslant\|\nabla(Q^{2}v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|xQ^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B\|\|Q^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

Now, as
\nabla{Q^{2}v}=Q^{2}\nabla v+2Q\nabla Qv
, proposition (
3.4
) enables us to conclude.

Find
u=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\alpha_{n}\phi_{n}\in L^{2}
. There holds

(5.36)

\begin{split}\left\|2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}\|H_{+}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{|\lambda_{n}^{2}-\zeta|}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}\end{split}

Given
n\geqslant 1
, as
\zeta
is outside
D_{n}
,
|\lambda_{n}^{2}-\zeta|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}n\delta
and thus
\frac{\lambda_{n}}{|\lambda_{n}^{2}-\zeta|}\leqslant\frac{4n+\varepsilon_{n}}{\frac{1}{2}n\delta}\leqslant\frac{10}{\delta}
. Similarly, we have
\frac{\lambda_{0}}{|\lambda_{0}^{2}-\zeta|}\leqslant\frac{10}{\delta}
. Thus, in terms of operator norm over
L^{2}
,

(5.37)

\left\|2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\right\|\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}

where
C^{\prime}
is a constant. Provided
\varepsilon
is small enough, the right-hand side is strictly smaller than
1
and thus
I-2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}
is invertible and formula (
5.33
) is correct.

First, this ensures that
A^{2}
has compact resolvent (indeed its resolvent is the product of a bounded operator over
L^{2}
and of a compact one, thus is it compact). Therefore we already know that
A^{2}
has a discreet spectrum and that there is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}
formed of eigenfunctions for
A^{2}
. Moreover, as we know that
A^{2}
has no spectrum outside
\Sigma
, its eigenvalues are located within the
D_{n}
’s. We now prove that
A^{2}
has exactly one simple eigenvalue in
D_{n}
. Indeed, following Kato
[
Kat13
, p.178]
we know that the spectral projector over the eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues located within
D_{n}
can be written as

(5.38)

\pi_{n}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}(A^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}d\zeta

However, using equation (
5.37
), we see that

(5.39)

\left\|\left(I-2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\right)^{-1}-I\right\|\leqslant\frac{C\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}

for a constant
C
when
\varepsilon
is small enough, which is an immediate consequence of the formula

(5.40)

(I-B)^{-1}-I=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 1}B^{n}

whenever
\|B\|<1
. Thus, if we write

(5.41)

\pi_{n}:=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}d\zeta

there holds

(5.42)

\begin{split}\left\|\pi_{n}^{\varepsilon}-\pi_{n}\right\|&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|\int_{\Gamma_{n}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\left(\left(I-2H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2}H_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\right)^{-1}-I\right)d\zeta\right\|\\
&\leqslant\frac{C\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}\left\|(H_{+}^{2}-\zeta)^{-1}\right\|d\zeta\\
&\leqslant\frac{C\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{n}}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}n\delta}d\zeta\\
&\leqslant C\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}\end{split}

For a constant
C
which is independent of
n
and of
\varepsilon
. This upper bound can be made arbitrarily small when
\varepsilon\to 0
: should it be smaller than
1
strictly, this ensures that
\pi_{n}^{\varepsilon}
and
\pi_{n}
have the same range (see
[
Kat13
, Lemma 4.1 p.34]
) which is equal to
1
as
H_{+}
has simple eigenvalues.

Therefore, provided
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, and this depending only of
\delta
, we have proved that
A^{2}
has a discreet spectrum formed of simple eigenvalues
0\leqslant\mu_{0}^{2}<\mu_{1}^{2}<....
with
\mu_{n}^{2}=16n^{2}+\alpha_{n}
, where
|\alpha_{n}|\leqslant\delta max(1,n)
(indeed,
\mu_{n}^{2}\in D_{n}
).

Thus,
A
has the discreet spectrum formed of simple eigenvalues
0\leqslant\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<...
and we see that
|\mu_{0}|\leqslant\sqrt{\delta}
, and moreover that for
n\geqslant 1

\begin{split}|\mu_{n}-4n|&=\left|\sqrt{16n^{2}+\alpha_{n}}-4n\right|\\
&=4n\left|\sqrt{1+\frac{\alpha_{n}}{16n^{2}}}-1\right|\\
&\leqslant 4nC\frac{\alpha_{n}}{16n^{2}}\\
&\leqslant C\delta\end{split}

where
C
is a constant. Thus, provided we initially choose

(5.44)

\max(\sqrt{\delta},C\delta)<\eta

we have proved the statement.
∎

We fix until the end
\psi_{0},\psi_{1},...
an

orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions for

.

Another change of function

Extracting the solution to the linearized equation, set

f^{M}
is a solution to

\partial_{s}f^{M}=\mathcal{L}f^{M}+IK(s)f^{M}-IK(s)r^{M}(s)+IN(s)

where we set

and

.

6.2
Estimate of

The estimate of
\|r^{M}(s)\|_{H_{x}^{3}}
is a consequence of the
oscillatory nature
of

. Indeed, as we will see, one can explicitly write

as a sum of integrals of the form

(6.4)

\int_{s}^{M}\text{oscillatory function}\times\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma\log(\sigma)}

Using somehow fastidious integration by parts, such integrals can always be bounded by
\frac{C}{s\log s}
.

Now, it will appear in the proof that the eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
of
A
may cause cancellation in the oscillations, thus forbidding to conclude the estimates on

. To prevent that, we here choose the value of
\alpha
: we set

(6.5)

\alpha:=\frac{\langle|y|^{2}Q,H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}\rangle}{\langle Q^{2},H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}\rangle}

such that

is orthogonal to
H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}
, or to say it differently that

is orthogonal to
\psi_{1}
. We need to justify that
\alpha
is well defined, that is we need to prove that
\langle Q^{2},H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}\rangle\neq 0
provided
\varepsilon>0
is small enough. We compute to this effect that
H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{1}\to\frac{1}{4}h_{1}
in

when

(this follows from the computations of the proof of lemma (

)). Moreover using (

) we see that
Q^{2}\sim C\varepsilon h_{0}^{2}
when
\varepsilon\to 0
in

with

Thus, it is enough to know that the scalar product
\langle h_{0}^{2},h_{1}\rangle
is nonzero, which is proved in
[
FR20
]
.

We prove in this subsection the following lemma

Lemma 6.1
.

There is a constant

(6.6)

Now, if we denote

An estimate

We will need the following technical result

There is a constant

\left|\int_{s}^{M}\langle r_{1}^{M}(\sigma),Q\rangle\right|\leqslant\frac{B_{0}}{s\log s}

Proof.

This lemma is essentially a consequence of the oscillatory nature of

on the one hand, and on the other hand of the particular algebraic fact that

H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(|y|^{2}Q-\alpha Q^{2})=\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 1}\alpha_{n}\psi_{n}

thus,

\begin{split}r_{1}^{M}(s)&=H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}A\int_{s}^{M}\frac{\sin(4\sigma)}{\sigma\log\sigma}\sin((s-\sigma)A)H_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(|y|^{2}Q-\alpha Q^{2})d\sigma\\

where we can remove both

(6.29)

\int_{s}^{M}\langle r_{1}^{M}(\sigma),Q\rangle=\\
\langle\displaystyle\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\left(\int_{s}^{M}\left(\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n+4))-\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n - 4))}{\tau\log\tau}d\tau\right)d\sigma\right)\alpha_{n}\psi_{n},AH_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q\rangle

We then compute

\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n-4))}{\tau\log\tau}d\tau=\left[-\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n-4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)\tau\log\tau}\right]_{\sigma}^{M}\\
-\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n-4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)}\frac{\log\tau+1}{\tau^{2}(\log\tau)^{2}}d\tau

Thus

(6.31)

\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n-4))}{\tau\log\tau}d\tau=-\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - M(\mu_n - 4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)M\log M}+\frac{\sin(4\sigma)}{(\mu_{n}-4)\sigma\log\sigma}\\
-\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n-4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)}\frac{\log\tau+1}{\tau^{2}(\log\tau)^{2}}d\tau

First, there holds

(6.32)

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{M}\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - M(\mu_n - 4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)M\log M}d\sigma&=-\frac{\cos(4M)}{\mu_{n}(\mu_{n}-4)M\log M}+\frac{\cos(s\mu_n - M(\mu_n-4))}{\mu_{n}(\mu_{n}-4)M\log M}\\

with all the right-hand terms bounded by

Moreover,

(6.33)

\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\sin(\sigma\mu_n - \tau\mu_n-4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)}\frac{\log\tau+1}{\tau^{2}(\log\tau)^{2}}d\tau=\left[-\frac{\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n - 4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)^{2}}\frac{\log\tau+1}{\tau^{2}(\log\tau)^{2}}\right]_{\sigma}^{M}\\
-\int_{\sigma}^{M}\frac{\cos(\sigma\mu_n - \tau(\mu_n - 4))}{(\mu_{n}-4)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\tau^{3}(\log\tau)^{2}}-(1+\log\tau)\frac{2\tau(\log\tau)^{2}+2\tau\log\tau}{\tau^{4}(\log\tau)^{4}}\right)d\tau

The computations are exactly the same for the other term

&\geqslant E(L,b)\min(\|xQ\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\geqslant C\log(t)\end{split}

and

(7.12)

\begin{split}\|u_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{1}}^{2}&=\left(L^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{4L^{2}}\right)\|xQ\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leqslant E(L,b)\max(\|xQ\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\leqslant C^{\prime}\log(t)\end{split}

c\left(\log(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\|u_{0}(t)\|_{H_{x}^{1}}\leqslant C\left(\log(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and we have similar bounds for

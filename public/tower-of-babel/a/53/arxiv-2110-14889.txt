The Kakeya Set conjecture over ℤ / N ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} for general N N
Manik Dhar

Abstract
We prove the Kakeya set conjecture for ℤ / N ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z} for general N N as stated by Hickman and Wright [ 15 ] . This entails extending and combining the techniques of Arsovski [ 1 ] for N = p k N=p^{k} and the author and Dvir [ 6 ] for the case of square-free N N . We also prove stronger lower bounds for the size of ( m , ϵ ) (m,\epsilon) -Kakeya sets over ℤ / p k ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}} by extending the techniques of [ 1 ] using multiplicities as was done in [ 17 , 10 ] . In addition, we show our bounds are almost sharp by providing a new construction for Kakeya sets over ℤ / p k ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}} and ℤ / N ​ ℤ {\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}} .

Introduction

We are interested in proving lower bounds for the sizes of sets in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
which have large intersections with lines in many directions. We first define the set of possible directions a line can take in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
.

Definition 1.1

(Projective space
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
)
.

Let
N=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\ldots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are distinct primes. The Projective space
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
consists of vectors
u\in({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
up to unit multiples of each other such that
u
(mod
p_{i}^{k_{i}}
) has at least one unit co-ordinate for every
i=1,\ldots,r
.

For each direction in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
we pick a representative in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. This allows us to treat
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
as a subset of
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}

Definition 1.2

(

Let

|S\cap L|\geq m

We now define sets that have large intersections with lines in many directions.

Definition 1.3

(
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya Sets)

Let

m

An
(N,1)
-Kakeya set in

is simply called a Kakeya set. In other words, a Kakeya set is a set that contains a line in every direction.

1.1
Proof Overview

We first start with a brief overview of the approach introduced in
[
6
]
. Given a Kakeya set
S
in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
, we construct a matrix
K_{S}
whose columns are indexed by points in
({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
and its rows are indexed by a direction in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
where the
d
th row would be supported on the line in direction
d
contained in
S
. This ensures that the non-zero columns of
K_{S}
correspond to points in
S
, which implies that the rank of
K_{S}
then lower bounds the size of
S
. The goal is to construct a suitable
K_{S}
and find a matrix
E
such that
K_{S}E
is a matrix independent of
S
whose rank can be analyzed easily. For prime
N=p
,
E
can be a matrix whose columns contain the evaluation of a monomial on
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
. In this case
K_{S}
ends up being a ‘decoder’ matrix where each row outputs the evaluation of a monomial on a direction given its evaluations on a line in that direction. This turns out to be a reformulation of Dvir’s polynomial method proof
[
9
]
. When
N=pq
(or in general is square-free) as
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{p}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
we define
E
as a tensor of matrices one acting on the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
part and the other on the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
part. This allows for an inductive argument to give Kakeya set lower bounds for square-free
N
. To get quantitatively stronger bounds we can use the evaluations of (Hasse) derivatives and multiplicities as was done in
[
17
,
10
]
.

In
[
1
]
for prime power
N
points in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{n}
are embedded in
{\mathbb{C}}_{p}^{n}
(the
p
-adic complex numbers which is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}
as a field) using
p^{k}
th roots of unity. The proof then implements the strategy of
[
6
]
using matrices with polynomial entries.
U
is a matrix whose columns are the evaluations of monomials over the embedding of
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
in the torus. Let
L
be a line in direction
d
. The key statement is that some linear combination (with polynomial coefficients) of the rows in
U
corresponding to points in
L
can generate the
d
th row of a ‘Vandermonde’ matrix
M
after applying a mod
p
operation (using the
p
-adic structure). The rank of
M
can be easily lower bounded. The linear combination being taken here corresponds to a row of
K_{S}
for a Kakeya set
S\subseteq({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
containing
L
in direction
d
. The key statement can be reformulated as saying that

K_{S}U\mod p=M

(1)

The argument can be completed by saying that the non-zero columns of
S
correspond to points in
S
, alternatively we note that we only use rows in
E
which correspond to points in
S
. This shows the rank of
M
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
lower bounds the size of
S
. The argument in
[
1
]
is only for
(p^{k},1)
-Kakeya sets. This is because, apriori it is not clear how to define a suitable
K_{S}
(equivalently how to decode from lines with only some points contained in
S
). The revised version of the paper
[
2
]
has a very different approach to give
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya bounds for the prime power case (the bounds there are quantitatively weaker than the ones here).

We now discuss how we improve the prime power bound quantitatively. Using simple linear algebraic arguments and the Chinese remainder theorem for a suitable polynomial ring we show that given
m

L
in direction
d
we can decode the
d
th row of

up to degree

shows are almost sharp). We also develop these ideas without using the theory of
p

To get bounds for general

[
6
]
with some small technical improvements to get better constants.

Rank and crank of matrices with polynomial entries

Definition 2.1

(Rank of matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle
)
.

Given a field
{\mathbb{F}}
and a matrix
M
with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle
where
f(z)
is a non-constant polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]
, we define the
{\mathbb{F}}
-rank of
M
denoted
\textsf{rank}_{\mathbb{F}}M
as the maximum number of
{\mathbb{F}}
-linearly independent columns of
M
.

In our proof, it will also be convenient to work with the following extension of rank for sets of matrices.

Definition 2.2

(crank of a set of matrices)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
f(z)
a non-constant polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]
. Given a finite set
T=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\}
of matrices over the ring
{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle
having the same number of columns we let
\textsf{crank}_{\mathbb{F}}(T)
be the
{\mathbb{F}}
-rank of the matrix obtained by concatenating all the elements
A_{i}
in
T
along their columns.
†
†

†

In
[
6
]
crank was defined using the rows of the matrices. The two definitions are equivalent when the entries of the matrices are from the field
{\mathbb{F}}
following from simple linear algebra. In our setting, the column and row definitions are not necessarily identical and the column version is better for our purposes.

We will use a simple lemma which follows from the definition of crank.

Lemma 2.3

(crank bound for multiplying matrices)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
f(z)
a non-constant polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]
. Given matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
of size
a\times b
and matrices
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
of size

with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}[x]/\langle f(z)\rangle
we have

\textsf{crank}_{\mathbb{F}}\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\geq\textsf{crank}_{\mathbb{F}}\{H_{i}\cdot A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}.

Proof.

A dependence on the columns of the matrix obtained by concatenating
A_{i}
s will be represented by a non-zero vector
w\in{\mathbb{F}}^{b}
such that
A_{i}w=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. This would imply
(H_{i}\cdot A_{i})w=0
for all
i
as well, completing the proof.
∎

Given a matrix
A

{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle

{\mathbb{F}}

Lemma 2.4

(Unique representation of elements in
{\mathbb{F}}[z\text{]}/\langle f(z)\rangle

.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
f(z)

{\mathbb{F}}[z]

{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle

is a unique element in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle
. When we refer to an element

Definition 2.5

(Coefficient matrix of
A
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}

f(z)

{\mathbb{F}}[z]

The key property of the coefficient matrix immediately follows from its definition.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
f(z)
a non-constant polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}[z]
of degree
d>0

A

{\mathbb{F}}[z]/\langle f(z)\rangle

{\mathbb{F}}
-linear combination of a subset of columns of

{\mathbb{F}}
-linear combination of the same subset of columns of

Definition 2.7

Lemma 2.8

Given matrices

(A_{1}\otimes A_{2})\cdot(B_{1}\otimes B_{2})=(A_{1}\cdot B_{1})\otimes(A_{2}\cdot B_{2}).

We want to prove a crank bound for a family of matrices with a tensor product structure. This statement is an analog of Lemma 4.8 in

]

Lemma 2.9

Corollary 3.14

(Computing a polynomial from its evaluations)

Let
{\mathbb{F}}

Proof.

-linear map which can compute the coefficients of

4
Kakeya Set bounds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}

We first prove two helper lemmas. We recall that
\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
is the set of linear isomorphisms over
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
. They are represented by matrices whose determinants are units in
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The first lemma implies that given a large subset
D\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
there exists a
W
in
\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
M_{p^{\ell},n}(W\cdot D)
has high rank where

W\cdot D=\{W\cdot u|u\in D\}

that is the set of directions obtained by rotating the elements in
D
by
W
. In general, we also prove that there exists a
W
such that
M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W})
has high rank where

D^{\prime}_{W}=\{u\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}|u\text{ (mod }p^{k}\text{)}\in W\cdot D\}

that is the set of directions in
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}
which under the mod
p^{k}
map give an element in
W\cdot D
. We prove the statement using a random rotation argument.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}},\epsilon\geq 0
and
p
be a prime. Given a set
D\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
containing at least an
\epsilon
fraction of elements in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
then there exists a matrix
W\in\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
such that,

\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W})\geq\epsilon\cdot\binom{p^{\ell}\ell^{-1}+n}{n}.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.7
we know that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
rank of
M_{p^{\ell},n}(({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1})
is at least
\binom{p^{\ell}l^{-1}+n}{n}
. This means there exists a set of rows
V
of size at least
\binom{p^{\ell}l^{-1}+n}{n}
in
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(({\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}))
which are
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-linearly independent.

We pick
W\in\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
uniformly at random and as in the statement of the lemma consider the set
D^{\prime}_{W}=\{u\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}|u\text{ (mod }p^{k}\text{)}\in W\cdot D\}
. The key claim about
D^{\prime}_{W}
is the following.

Claim 4.2
.

Any given row
v\in V
will appear in
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W}))
with probability at least
\epsilon
.

Proof.

As
\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
acts transitively on the set
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
and
|D|\geq\epsilon|{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}|
we see that
D^{\prime}_{W}
will contain a particular
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
with probability at least
\epsilon
. The row
v\in V
will be within
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(u))
for some
u\in{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
. This means that
v\in V
will appear in
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W}))
with probability at least
\epsilon
.
∎

Now by linearity of expectation we see that the expected number of rows
V
appearing in
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W}))
is at least
\epsilon|V|
. This means there exists a choice of
W
such that an
\epsilon
fraction of elements in
V
do appear in
\text{Coeff}(M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W}))
. As all these rows are linearly independent then by Lemma
2.6
we see that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-rank of

must be at least
\epsilon|V|\geq\epsilon\binom{p^{\ell}l^{-1}+n}{n}
.
∎

The second lemma lower bounds the size of

-Kakeya sets by the rank of a sub-matrix of

Theorem

.

Let

with

-Kakeya set

-Kakeya set

in

|S|\geq\epsilon\cdot\left(\frac{m^{n}}{(k+1)^{n}}\right)(1+n/p)^{-n}.

Set
\ell=k+\lceil\log_{p}(n)\rceil
. Let
D
be the set of directions in
{\mathbb{P}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n-1}
which have
m
-rich lines. We know
D
contains at least an
\epsilon
fraction of directions. By Lemma

there exists a matrix
W\in\text{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k}{\mathbb{Z}})
such that,

\text{rank}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}M_{p^{\ell},n}(D^{\prime}_{W})\geq\epsilon\cdot\binom{p^{\ell}\ell^{-1}+n}{n},

where
D^{\prime}_{W}=\{u\in({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\ell}{\mathbb{Z}})^{n}|u\text{ (mod }p^{k}\text{)}\in W\cdot D\}.

We now note
W\cdot S
is also an
(m,\epsilon)
-Kakeya set with

as the set of directions with
m
-rich lines. We now apply Lemma

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p^{k}p^{a}(k+a)^{-1}+i}{p^{k}p^{a}m^{-1}+i}

Theorem

The construction will be inductive. For

2.8
we have
M_{p^{\ell},n}=M_{p^{\ell},1}^{\otimes n}=\overline{L}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
. As
L_{p^{\ell}}
was invertible with its inverse also having entries in
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
we see that
\overline{L}_{p^{\ell}}
is also invertible and
\left(\overline{L}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}\right)^{-1}M_{p^{\ell},n}=\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
. Using Lemma
2.3
we have that,

\textsf{rank}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}M_{p^{\ell},n}\geq\textsf{rank}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}.

As
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}
is upper triangular so will
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
be. Therefore, to lower bound the rank of
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
we can lower bound the number of non-zero diagonal elements.

The diagonal elements in
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
correspond to the product of diagonal elements chosen from
n
copies of
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}
. Recall, the rows and columns of
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}
are labelled by
j\in\{0,\ldots,p^{\ell}-1\}
with

\displaystyle D_{m}(j,j)=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}(z^{j}-z^{i})

Setting
z-1=w
we note that
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z]/\langle z^{p^{\ell}}-1\rangle
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[w]/\langle w^{p^{\ell}}\rangle
.
D_{p^{\ell}}(j,j)
can now be written as

\displaystyle D_{m}(j,j)=(1+w)^{j(j-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^{j}((1+w)^{i}-1)

Using Lucas’s Theorem (Theorem
A.2
) we see that the largest power of
t
which divides
(1+w)^{l}-1
is the same as the largest power of
p
which divides
l
. For any
j\leq p^{\ell}-1
, therefore the largest power of
w
which divides
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}(j,j)
is at most

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\lfloor\log_{p}(j)\rfloor}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{t}}\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{t+1}}\right\rfloor\right)p^{t}

\displaystyle=j+\sum\limits_{t=1}^{\lfloor\log_{p}(j)\rfloor}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{p^{t}}\right\rfloor p^{t-1}(p-1)

\displaystyle\leq j(1+\lfloor\log_{p}(j)\rfloor(1-1/p))

\displaystyle\leq j(\ell-(\ell-1)/p).

(10)

Consider the set of tuples
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
j_{1}+\ldots+j_{n}\leq\lceil p^{\ell}/\ell\rceil
. Using (
10
) we see that the diagonal entry in
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
corresponding to the tuple will be divisible by at most
w^{\lceil p^{\ell}/\ell\rceil(\ell-(\ell-1)/p)}
. It is easy to check that
\lceil p^{\ell}/\ell\rceil(\ell-(\ell-1)/p)\leq p^{\ell}-1
which will guarantee that the
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})
’th diagonal entry of
\overline{D}_{p^{\ell}}^{\otimes n}
is non-zero.

This gives us at least
\binom{\lceil p^{\ell}\ell^{-1}\rceil+n}{n}
non-zero diagonal entries proving the desired rank bound.
∎

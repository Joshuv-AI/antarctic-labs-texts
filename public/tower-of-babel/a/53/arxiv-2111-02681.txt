Revisiting asymptotic stability of solitons of nonlinear Schrödinger equations via refined profile method
Scipio Cuccagna, Masaya Maeda

Abstract
In this paper, we give an alternative proof for the asymptotic stability of solitons for nonlinear Schrödinger equations with internal modes. The novel idea is to use “refined profiles” developed by the authors for the analysis of small bound states. By this new strategy, we able to avoid the normal forms. Further, we can track the functions appearing in the Fermi Golden Rule hypothesis.

This is a significant advance because

the linearization

has a pair of eigenvalues very close to the origin. The nonlinear interaction of the corresponding discrete modes with the continuous modes, should be responsible for transient nature of the patterns observed. The longevity of these patterns is connected with the smallness of the eigenvalues of the pair, because
the nonlinear interaction, which leads to radiation induced dissipation on discrete modes, is related to the fact that multiples of the eigenvalues are in the continuous spectrum, see the definition of
resonant multi-indexes under (

becomes essential, in view of the large number of steps required in normal forms arguments. Now it turns out that with a well chosen

Lemma 4.10

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}}}\|\mathbf{z}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{0}\epsilon.

Proof.

We start by computing the time derivative of the localized action

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(E(\varphi)+\omega_{*}Q_{0}(\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle(-\Delta+\omega_{*})\varphi+g(|\varphi|^{2})\varphi,D\varphi\dot{\Theta}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\rm i}D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta},D\varphi\dot{\Theta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle{\rm i}D\varphi(\dot{\Theta}-\widetilde{\Theta}),D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle H\eta+F,D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle+\left\langle{\rm i}\partial_{t}\eta,D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\eta,{\rm i}D^{2}\varphi(\widetilde{\Theta},\widetilde{\Theta})+D\mathcal{R}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle-\left\langle F,D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle+\left\langle\eta,{\rm i}D^{2}\varphi(\dot{\Theta},\widetilde{\Theta})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\eta,D\mathcal{R}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle-\left\langle\eta,{\rm i}D^{2}\varphi(\dot{\Theta}-\widetilde{\Theta},\widetilde{\Theta})\right\rangle-\left\langle F,D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle,

(4.28)

where we have used (
3.2
) in the 1st line,
\left\langle{\rm i}w,w\right\rangle=0
in the 2nd line, (
3.10
) in the 3rd line, (
3.7
) in the 4th line.
From (
4.5
) and (
4.10
) we have

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\eta,{\rm i}D^{2}\varphi(\dot{\Theta}-\widetilde{\Theta},\widetilde{\Theta})\right\rangle\|_{L^{1}}+\|\left\langle F,D\varphi\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\epsilon\left(C_{0}\epsilon\right)^{2}.

(4.29)

For the 1st term of the last line of (
4.28
),

\displaystyle-\left\langle\eta,D\mathcal{R}\widetilde{\Theta}\right\rangle=\left\langle\eta,D_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{R}({\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z})\right\rangle-\left\langle\eta,D\mathcal{R}\left(\widetilde{\Theta}-(0,0,0,0,-{\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z})\right)\right\rangle

where

.
Following the argument of Lemma
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\eta,D\mathcal{R}\left(\widetilde{\Theta}-(0,0,0,0,-{\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z})\right)\right\rangle\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim C_{0}^{2}\epsilon^{3}.

(4.31)

For the 1st term in the r.h.s. of (

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta,D_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{R}\left({\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z}\right)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle G_{-\theta,-\mathbf{y},0}\eta,{\rm i}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}}}\lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}G_{\mathbf{m}}\right\rangle

(4.32)

\displaystyle+\left\langle G_{-\theta,-\mathbf{y},0}\eta,,{\rm i}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}}}\lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}\left(G_{0,0,\mathbf{v}}G_{\mathbf{m}}-G_{\mathbf{m}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\eta,D_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{R}_{1}\left({\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z}\right)\right\rangle.

1.21
) and (
4.2

\displaystyle\|\left\langle G_{-\theta,-\mathbf{y},0}\eta,,{\rm i}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}}}\lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}\left(G_{0,0,\mathbf{v}}G_{\mathbf{m}}-G_{\mathbf{m}}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\eta,D_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{R}_{1}\left({\rm i}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mathbf{z}\right)\right\rangle\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\epsilon(C_{0}\epsilon)^{2}.

(4.33)

For the 1st term in the r.h.s. of (
4.32
), since
\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}}=\{\mathbf{m},\overline{\mathbf{m}}\ |\ \lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})>\omega_{*}\}
, setting

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}+}:=\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}},\ \lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})>\omega_{*}\},

\displaystyle=\left\langle G_{-\theta,-\mathbf{y},0}\eta,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}+}\end{subarray}}\left({\rm i}\lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}G_{\mathbf{m}}+{\rm i}\lambda(\omega_{*},\overline{\mathbf{m}})\mathbf{z}^{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}G_{\overline{\mathbf{m}}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\widetilde{G}_{-\theta,-\mathbf{y},0}\zeta,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{min}+}\end{subarray}}{\rm i}\lambda(\omega_{*},\mathbf{m})\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbf{m}}\right\rangle.

Critical Relaxed Stable Matchings with Ties in the Many-to-Many Setting Journal: SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics
Meghana Nasre, Prajakta Nimbhorkar, Keshav Ranjan

Abstract
We study the many-to-many bipartite matching problem in the presence of preferences where ties, as well as lower quotas, may appear on both sides of the bipartition. The input is a bipartite graph G = ( 𝒜 ∪ ℬ , E ) G=(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B},E) , where each vertex in 𝒜 ∪ ℬ \mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B} has a positive upper quota and a non-negative lower quota denoting the maximum and minimum number of vertices that can be assigned to it from its neighborhood. Additionally, each vertex specifies a preference ordering, possibly containing ties, over its neighbors. A critical matching is a matching which fulfills vertex lower quotas to the maximum possible extent. We seek to compute a matching that is critical as well as optimal with respect to the preferences of vertices. Stability, a well-accepted notion of optimality in the presence of two-sided preferences, is generalized to weak-stability in the presence of ties. However, a matching that is critical as well as weakly stable may not exist. Popularity is another well-investigated notion of optimality for the two-sided preference model; however, in the presence of ties (even without lower quotas), a popular matching may not exist. We, therefore, consider the notion of relaxed stability, which was introduced and studied by Krishnaa, Limaye, Nasre, and Nimbhorkar (JoCO 2023). We show that a critical matching that is relaxed stable always exists, although computing a maximum-size relaxed stable matching turns out to be NP-hard. Our main contribution is a 3 2 \frac{3}{2} -approximation algorithm for computing a maximum-size critical relaxed stable matching.

Introduction

Preference-based many-to-many matching problems model applications like assigning students to courses
[
4
]
and workers to firms
[
30
]
where vertices on both sides of the bipartition have preferences over their neighbors and can accept multiple partners. In the student-course allocation problem, students typically have a
minimum
requirement on the number of courses they need to complete in a semester, and a course may be offered only if there is a
minimum
number of registrants. Similarly, in the worker-firm assignment, certain workers
must
be assigned jobs, and firms may need a
minimum
number of workers for their operations. These
minimum
requirements can be specified as
lower quotas
by the corresponding agents to denote the minimum number of agents they need to get assigned to in any matching. Therefore, it is natural to have lower quotas on both sides of the bipartition. Lower quotas
[
1
,
10
,
24
]
have been considered in the literature in the context of matching residents to hospitals (
e.g.
National Resident Matching Program in the U.S.A.) where rural hospitals often face the problem of being understaffed
[
29
,
31
]
.
Ties
in preferences is yet another important practical consideration in matching problems. For instance, hospitals with a large number of applicants often find it difficult to generate strict preference lists
[
13
]
. Similarly, in the case of course allocation, it is natural for teachers to have all the students with equal scores in a single tie in their preference lists.

In this work, we explore the generalized matching model where
ties
as well as
lower quotas
on both sides of the bipartition are explored in the
many-to-many setting
.
Our setting generalizes several models, such as the one-to-one setting of the stable marriage problem
[
9
,
15
]
, the many-to-one setting of the Hospital/Residents problem with ties
[
13
]
, and the many-to-many assignment problem
[
3
]
– all of these works are without lower quotas. Recently, lower quotas have been considered in the presence of strict preference lists
[
1
,
10
,
24
,
26
,
16
,
27
]
and in the presence of ties in preferences
[
8
,
23
]
. However, none of these works consider ties as well as lower quotas on
both sides
of the bipartition. In the conference version of our work
[
25
]
, we consider this model for the one-to-one setting.
In parallel and independent of our work, Csáji
[
6
]
considered
a generalized problem involving critical edges and free edges for the same model in the one-to-one setting.
Therefore, it leaves the model of ties as well as lower quotas in the
many-to-many
setting unexplored. Recently, in an unpublished work
[
5
]
, Csáji generalized his work for the one-to-one setting to matroid constraints for the vertices. Ours is a simple proposal based algorithm and can be viewed as an extension of the well-known Gale and Shapley algorithm
[

]
– thus making it practical to implement.

Formally, the input to our problem is a bipartite graph
G=(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B},E)
, where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are two sets of vertices and
E
denotes the set of all the acceptable vertex-pairs. Every vertex
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
ranks a subset of vertices in its neighborhood in
G
, and this ordering is called the
preference list
of
u
, denoted by
Pref(
u
)
. We say that a vertex strictly prefers a neighbor with a smaller rank over another neighbor with a larger rank. If a vertex is allowed to be indifferent between some of its neighbors and is allowed to assign the same rank to such neighbors, it is referred to as a
tie
. If ties are not allowed, the preference lists are said to be
strict
. For a vertex
u
, let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be its neighbors in
G
. We use
v_{1}\succ_{u}v_{2}
to denote that
u
strictly prefers
v_{1}
over
v_{2}
and
v_{1}\succeq_{u}v_{2}
to denote that
u
either strictly prefers
v_{1}
over
v_{2}
or is indifferent between them. In addition, every vertex
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
has an associated lower quota
q^{-}(u)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}
and an upper quota
q^{+}(u)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
q^{-}(u)\leq q^{+}(u)
. The upper quota
q^{+}(u)
denotes the maximum number of neighbors that
u
can accommodate in any assignment. The lower quota
q^{-}(u)
of a vertex
u
denotes the minimum number of neighbors that must be assigned to
u
in any assignment. If for a vertex
u
,
q^{-}(u)>0
then we call
u
an
lq-vertex
otherwise
u
is a non-
lq
vertex.

A
matching

M
in
G
is a subset of the edge set
E
such that
|M(u)|\leq q^{+}(u)
for each vertex
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
, where
M(u)
denotes the set of neighbors assigned to
u
in
M
. Note that this definition of matching deviates from the classical one used in graph theory. We still use the term
matching
as done in the literature
[
3
,
10
]
. In the matching
M
, a vertex
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
is called

if
|M(u)|=q^{+}(u)
,

if
|M(u)|<q^{+}(u)
,
deficient
if
|M(u)|<q^{-}(u)
, and
surplus
if
|M(u)|>q^{-}(u)
. A matching
M
is

if no vertex in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
is deficient in
M
. Feasible matchings are desirable since they always satisfy the lower quotas of every vertex. Unfortunately, the existence of a feasible matching is not guaranteed
[
27
]
. When a feasible matching does not exist, we seek to compute a
critical matching
that fulfils lower quotas to the maximum possible extent. For a matching
M
, we define the deficiency of a vertex
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
as
\max\{0,q^{-}(u)-|M(u)|\}
. The deficiency,
def(M)
, of a matching
M
is equal to the sum of the deficiencies of all the vertices
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
in
G
.

Definition 1

(Critical Matching)
.

A matching
M
in
G
is critical if there is no matching
N
in
G
such that
\mbox{{\sf$def(N)$}}<\mbox{{\sf$def(M)$}}
.

In this work, we are interested in computing a critical matching that is
optimal
with respect to the preferences of the vertices. Two of the most extensively investigated optimality notions for the bipartite matching problem with preferences on both sides are
stability
and
popularity
.

Definition 2

(Stable Matchings)
.

Given a matching
M
, a pair
(a,b)\in E\setminus M
is called a blocking pair with respect to the matching
M
if
(i)
either
|M(a)|<q^{+}(a)
or
b\succ_{a}b^{\prime}
for some
b^{\prime}\in M(a)
and
(ii)
either
|M(b)|<q^{+}(b)
or
a\succ_{b}a^{\prime}
for some
a^{\prime}\in M(b)
. The matching
M
is stable if there is no blocking pair w.r.t.
M
.

We remark that for a pair
(a,b)
to block a matching
M
, both
a
and
b

strictly
prefer each other over some of their current partners in
M
. Note that this stability notion is referred to as the
weak stability

[
15
]
as two other notions of stability,
strong
and
super
, are also formulated in the literature. In this paper, we do not consider strong and super stability as they are not guaranteed to exist, and we use the term
stability
to indicate weak stability.

Stable matching in the presence of ties and
lq
-vertices:
It is known that every instance of the stable marriage problem with ties admits a stable matching
[
15
]
, and it can be efficiently computed. When preferences are strict, all stable matchings have the same size. However, this need not be the case in the presence of ties in preferences. That is, stable matchings need not have the same size in the presence of ties in preferences. Moreover, the problem of computing a maximum or minimum size stable matching is NP-hard
[
22
]
. When we have
lq
-vertices as a part of the input, a stable matching, which is also critical, may not exist even without ties
[
27
]
. Moreover, in the presence of ties, deciding whether the given instance admits a stable and critical matching is NP-hard (see Claim
1
in Section
2.3
).

As mentioned earlier,
popularity
is another well-investigated notion of optimality for the matching problem with preferences on both sides. Informally, a matching
M
is
popular
in a set of matchings if no majority of vertices wish to deviate from
M
to any other matching in that set.

Popular matching in the presence of
lq
-vertices:
Since in the presence of
lq
-vertices, stability and criticality are not simultaneously guaranteed, this alternate notion of optimality is extensively investigated in the literature for the settings involving strict preferences
[
24
,
26
,
16
,
27
]
. The goal is to compute
popular
among the set of critical matchings. It is known
[
16
,
26
]
that an instance with strict preference lists
always
admits a matching that is popular amongst critical matchings, and such a matching can be computed efficiently. Hence, it is natural to consider popularity in the presence of
lq
-vertices and ties. However, when ties are present in the preferences only on one side of the bipartition (even with no
lq
-vertices), popular matchings are not guaranteed to exist
[
2
]
, and deciding whether a popular matching exists is an NP-hard problem. In light of this, we explore the notion of
relaxed stability
.

Relaxed stability in the presence of ties and
lq
-vertices:
The notion of relaxed stability was introduced and studied by Krishnaa
et al
.
[
19
]
for the Hospital/Residents problem with lower quotas (
HRLQ
). In their setting, preferences are assumed to be strict. The
HRLQ
setting is a many-to-one preference-based matching problem where each resident has an upper quota equal to one, and each hospital
h
is associated with an upper quota
q^{+}(h)
and a lower quota
q^{-}(h)\leq q^{+}(h)
. To minimize the deficiency of a matching, certain residents may be
forced
to be matched to some
lq
-hospitals. The notion of relaxed stability ignores the blocking pairs involving only such residents. That is, in a matching
M
, if a resident matched to
h
participates in a blocking pair, and the hospital
h
is
surplus
(
i.e.

|M(h)|>q^{-}(h)
), then
M
is not relaxed stable.

Definition 3

(Relaxed Stability in
HRLQ

[
19
]
)
.

A matching
M
is relaxed stable if, no unmatched resident blocks
M
and for a blocking resident
r
, the hospital
h=M(r)
satisfies
|M(h)|\leq q^{-}(h)
.

A matching
M
is called critical relaxed stable matching (
CRITICAL-RSM
) if it is
critical
as well as
relaxed stable
. In the
HRLQ
setting
[
19
]
, preferences are strict, upper quotas and lower quotas are allowed only for hospitals. In contrast, we allow ties in preferences and
lq
-vertices to appear on
both sides
of the bipartition in the many-to-many setting.

Our Contribution:

Our first contribution is to generalize the above notion of relaxed stability to the many-to-many setting in the presence of ties in the preference lists and lower quotas on both sides of the bipartition. We prove that for an
HRLQ
instance, our notion of relaxed stability (see Definition
6
) is equivalent to the one by Krishnaa
et al
.
[
19
]
(Definition
3
). Our next contribution is to show that a
CRITICAL-RSM
always exists in our setting. We remark that when
q^{-}(u)=0
and
q^{+}(u)=1
for all
u\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
, an instance of our setting is the same as the stable marriage setting with ties but without
lq
-vertices, and hence the set of
CRITICAL-RSM
is the same as the set of stable matchings. This implies that computing a maximum size critical
RSM
is NP-hard
[
22
]
and hard to approximate
[
11
]
. For the problem of computing a stable matching of the maximum size in the presence of ties, the current best approximation factor
[
18
,
21
,
28
]
is
\frac{3}{2}
. The main result (Theorem
1
) provides the same approximation size guarantee for a maximum size
CRITICAL-RSM
problem in our generalized setting. More specifically, we give an efficient algorithm for computing a
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation of the maximum size
CRITICAL-RSM
for the many-to-many setting where ties and lower quotas appear on both sides of the bipartition.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G=(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B},E)
be a bipartite graph where each vertex
v\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
has an associated lower quota
q^{-}(v)
, an upper quota
q^{+}(v)\geq q^{-}(v)
and a preference ordering possibly containing ties over its neighbors. Then

always admits a matching

such that

is

and can be computed efficiently. Moreover,
|M|\geq\frac{2}{3}|M_{max}|
, where

is a maximum size
CRITICAL-RSM
in

.

The model investigated in this paper which involves both ties and

-vertices on

[

]
for computing a

-approximation of a maximum size stable matching in the presence of two-sided ties

2.1
Király’s algorithm for the many-to-many setting

In this section, we present the extension of Király’s algorithm
[
18
]
for many-to-one setting to the many-to-many setting for computing a
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation of a maximum size stable matching. The algorithm is a proposal-based algorithm where vertices in
\mathcal{A}
propose and vertices in
\mathcal{B}
accept or reject the proposals. As long as a vertex
a
is under-subscribed and has not proposed to all vertices in its preference list

a
)
, the vertex
a
proposes to its
favorite
neighbor (we define it formally in Definition
5
). If
a\in\mathcal{A}
remains under-subscribed after proposing all the vertices in
Pref(
a
)
, the vertex
a
achieves the ‘
*
’ status, denoted by
a^{*}
. Subsequently,
a^{*}
starts proposing vertices from the beginning of
Pref(
a
)
. As considered in
[
18
]
, the
*
status of a vertex

can be interpreted as improving the rank of

in the preference list of any neighbor

by some
0<\epsilon<1
. Thus, for a vertex

, its neighbor, say
b
, prefers
a^{*}
over any vertex

with which

is in a tie in

does not have the
*
status. However, any vertex

such that

in the preference list of

continues to remain better preferred even over

. We adapt the definition of
uncertain proposal
and
favorite neighbor
from
[
18
]
for the one-to-one setting to the many-to-many setting. To the best of our knowledge, Király’s algorithm for the many-to-many settings has not been documented. Hence, for the sake of completeness, we give the generalized algorithm for computing a
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation of the maximum size stable matching in the many-to-many setting and its analysis in
A
.

If

3

2.2
Overview of the multi-level popular critical matching algorithm

We now briefly describe the algorithm by Nasre
et al

[

]
for computing a maximum-size popular critical matching in the many-to-many setting with strict preferences and lower quotas on both sides of the bipartition. Let
{s}

start at level

2.3
Stable critical matching problem is NP-complete

In this section, we consider the stable critical matching problem. Given an instance
G=(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B},E)
of the many-to-many setting with two-sided ties and lower quotas on both sides of the bipartition, the stable critical matching problem asks whether
G
admits a matching that is stable as well as critical. We show that the stable critical matching problem is NP-complete even for the many-to-one setting. As mentioned earlier, Goko
et al
.
[
8
]
considered a similar problem called
HRT
-
MSLQ
. In the
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem, we are given an instance
G=(\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{H},E)
where (i)
q^{-}(r)=0
and
q^{+}(r)=1
for all
r\in\mathcal{R}
, (ii)
q^{+}(h)>0
and
0\leq q^{-}(h)\leq q^{+}(h)\leq|\mathcal{R}|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and (iii) the preference lists for all
v\in\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{H}
are complete and may contain ties. Recall that the satisfaction ratio for a hospital
h\in\mathcal{H}
w.r.t. a given matching
M
is defined as
S_{M}(h)=\min\{1,\frac{|M(h)|}{q^{-}(h)}\}
. Here, the assumption is that if
q^{-}(h)=0
, then
S_{M}(h)=1
because the lower quota is automatically satisfied in this case. The
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem asks to maximize the total satisfaction ratio over all stable matchings in the given instance. That is, if
\mathcal{M}_{s}
denotes the set of all stable matchings in the given instance, then
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem asks us to find a matching
M\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
such that
\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}S_{M}(h)
is maximized.

Theorem 13 in
[
8
]
shows that
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem is NP-hard for one-to-one setting. Computing a critical matching is polynomial-time solvable
[
16
,
27
]
. So we can verify whether the given solution is critical or not. Also, since the stability of a matching is verifiable in linear time, it is easy to see that our problem is in NP. In the following claim, we prove that when
q^{+}(h)=1
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, then the optimal solution of
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem for the given instance is the same as the stable critical matching in that instance. This implies that the stable critical matching problem is NP-complete even in a very restricted case of our setting.

Claim 1
.

Let
G
be an instance of
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem with
q^{+}(h)=1
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then,
M
is a stable critical matching in
G
if and only if
M
is a stable matching with the maximum satisfaction ratio in
G
.

Proof:

Let
M
be a critical stable matching in
G
. Clearly,
M
is a stable matching. Thus, we need to show that
M
has the maximum satisfaction ratio in
G
. Recall that the preference lists of all agents are complete as
G
is an instance of
HRT
-
MSLQ
problem. If
|\mathcal{R}|\geq|\mathcal{H}|
, then all hospitals are matched in
M
, and
M
trivially has the maximum satisfaction ratio in
G
. So, let us assume that
|\mathcal{R}|<|\mathcal{H}|
. Clearly, some hospitals must be unmatched in
M
. Assume that the satisfaction ratio of the matching
M
is
k
. We claim that no stable matching
M^{\prime}\neq M
has a satisfaction ratio greater than
k
. This is because if the satisfaction ratio of
M^{\prime}
is greater than
k
, then
M^{\prime}
must match more hospitals with
q^{-}(h)=1
than the matching
M
. This contradicts that
M
is a critical matching. Thus,
M
has the maximum satisfaction ratio in
G
.

Now, to prove the other direction, let us assume that
M
is a stable matching with the maximum satisfaction ratio in
G
. If there exists any stable matching
M^{\prime}\neq M
such that
M^{\prime}
matches more hospitals with
q^{-}(h)=1
than the matching
M
. Then,
\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}S_{M}^{\prime}(h)>\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}S_{M}(h)
. This contradicts that
M
is a stable matching with the maximum satisfaction ratio. Therefore, the number of hospitals with
q^{-}(h)=1
matched in
M
is the maximum for any stable matching. Thus,
M
is a critical stable matching.
∎

Conclusion

In this paper, we study the many-to-many bipartite matching problem with two-sided preferences, where preferences may involve ties and lower quotas are present for agents on both sides of the bipartition. We prove the existence of a critical relaxed stable matching in this setting. Since computing a maximum-size stable matching is NP-hard in instances with ties, even without lower quotas, finding a maximum-size critical relaxed stable matching is also NP-hard. We present a polynomial-time algorithm that computes a
\frac{3}{2}
-approximation of the maximum-size critical relaxed stable matching. Our approximation factor matches the best-known bound for maximum size (weakly) stable matching in the presence of ties
[
21
,
18
,
28
]
.

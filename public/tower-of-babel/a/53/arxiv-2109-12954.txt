Abstract
Let 𝒞 \mathscr{C} be a Krull-Schmidt n n -exangulated category and 𝒜 \mathscr{A} be an n n -extension closed subcategory of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . Then 𝒜 \mathscr{A} inherits the n n -exangulated structure from the given n n -exangulated category in a natural way. This construction gives n n -exangulated categories which are not n n -exact categories in the sense of Jasso nor ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories in the sense of Geiss–Keller–Oppermann in general. Furthermore, we also give a sufficient condition on when an n n -exangulated category 𝒜 \mathscr{A} is an n n -exact category. These results generalize work by Klapproth and Zhou. Keywords: ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories; n n -exact category; n n -extension closed subcategories; n n -exangulated categories 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10

Introduction

The notion of extriangulated categories was introduced by Nakaoka and Palu in
[
NP
]
as a simultaneous generalization of
exact categories and triangulated categories. Exact categories (abelian categories are also exact categories) and triangulated categories are extriangulated categories, while there are some other examples of extriangulated categories which are neither exact nor triangulated, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
,
NP1
]
. Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
, Remark 2.18]
proved extension closed subcategories of extriangulated categories are extriangulated categories. For example, let
A
be an artin algebra and
K^{[-1,\hskip 1.42262pt0]}({\rm proj}A)
the category of complexes of finitely generated projective
A
-modules concentrated in degrees
-1
and
0
, with morphisms considered up to homotopy. Then
K^{[-1,\hskip 1.42262pt0]}({\rm proj}A)
is an extension closed subcategory of the bounded homotopy category
K^{b}({\rm proj}A)
which is not exact and triangulated, see
[
INP
, Example 6.2]
.
This construction gives extriangulated categories which are not exact and triangulated.

In
[
GKO
]
, Geiss, Keller and Oppermann introduced
a new type of categories, called
(n+2)
-angulated categories, which generalize
triangulated categories: the classical triangulated categories are the special case
n=1
. They appear for example as
n
-cluster tilting subcategories of triangulated categories which are closed under the
n
th power of the shift functor.
Later, Jasso
[
Ja
]
introduced
n
-exact categories which are categories inhabited
by certain exact sequences with
n+2
terms, called
n
-exact sequences. The case
n=1

corresponds to the usual concepts of exact categories.
An important source of examples of
n
-exact categories are
n
-cluster
tilting subcategories, see
[
Ja
, Theorem 4.14]
.
Recently, Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN
]
introduced the notion of
n
-exangulated categories. It should be noted that the case
n=1
corresponds to extriangulated categories. As typical examples we have that
n
-exact (
n
-abelian categories are also
n
-exact categories) and
(n+2)
-angulated categories are
n
-exangulated, see
[
HLN
, Proposition 4.5 and Proposition 4.34]
. There are also some
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
n
-exact nor

(n+2)
-angulated, see
[
HLN
,
LZ
,
HZZ1
]
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category and
\textbf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
denote the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. We define
\textbf{C}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the full
subcategory of
\textbf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
consisting of complexes whose components are zero in all degrees outside of
\{0,1,\cdots,n+1\}
.
Let
\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. We define
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}
to be the restriction of
\mathbb{E}
onto
\mathscr{A}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{A}
.
For any
\delta\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}}(C,A)
, take a distinguished
n
-exangle
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
with
X_{i}
in
\mathscr{A}
for
i=1,\cdots,n
. Define
\mathfrak{t}(\delta)=[X_{\bullet}]
, where the homotopy equivalence class is taken in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{({\mathscr{A};\hskip 1.42262ptA,\hskip 1.42262ptC})}
.

Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN
, Proposition 2.35]
proved that if
\mathfrak{t}
-inflations are closed under composition and
\mathfrak{t}
-deflations are closed under composition, then
(\mathscr{A},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{A}},\mathfrak{t})

is an
n
-exangulated category. Zhou
[
Z
, Theorem 3.4]
recently also proved that
this result holds in an
(n+2)
-angulated category, but he showed that
this hypothesis (
\mathfrak{t}
-inflations are closed under composition and
\mathfrak{t}
-deflations are closed under composition) of Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN
, Proposition 2.35]
is automatically satisfied
in an
(n+2)
-angulated category.
Based on this idea, we prove the first main result in the article, which is a higher couterpart of Nakaoka-Palu’s result. Moreover, the construction gives
n
-exangulated categories which are not

n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated in general.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
3.2
for details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be a Krull-Schmidt
n
-exangulated categor and

\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then

is an
n
-exangulated category.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\Theta)
be a Krull-Schmidt

-angulated category and
\mathscr{A}
be an
n
-extension closed subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Klapproth
[

]
proved that

has the structure of an

[

gave a description of when an

-exangulated category can become

-exact category. Based on this fact, combined with Theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
3.16
for details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be a Krull-Schmidt
n
-exangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives,

\mathscr{A}

for

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some elementary definitions and facts on

\mathscr{C}

For any pair of objects

\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)

\mathbb{E}
-extension
or simply an

. We also write such

{}_{A}\delta_{C}
when we indicate

. The zero element
{}_{A}0_{C}=0\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
is called the

\mathbb{E}
-extension
. For any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
, let
\delta\oplus\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})
be the
element corresponding to
(\delta,0,0,{\delta}{{}^{\prime}})
through the natural isomorphism
\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})\simeq\mathbb{E}(C,A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A^{\prime})
.

For any
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},C)
,
\mathbb{E}(C,a)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{E}(c,A)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)
are simply denoted by

and

, respectively.

Let
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
be any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions. A
morphism

(a,c)\colon\delta\to{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
of extensions is a pair of morphisms

and

\mathscr{C}
, satisfying the equality

a_{\ast}\delta=c^{\ast}{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
.
Then the functoriality of
\mathbb{E}
implies
\mathbb{E}(c,a)=a_{\ast}(c^{\ast}\delta)=c^{\ast}(a_{\ast}\delta)
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.7]

Let
\bf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
whose components are zero in the degrees outside of
\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}
. Namely, an object in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
is a complex
X_{\bullet}=\{X_{i},d_{i}^{X}\}
of the form

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}.

We write a morphism
f_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
simply
f_{\bullet}=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})
, only indicating the terms of degrees
0,\ldots,n+1
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.11]

By Yoneda lemma, any extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
induces natural transformations

\delta_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(-,C)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(-,A)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta^{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,-)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(C,-).

For any
X\in\mathscr{C}
, these
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}
are given as follows.

(1)

(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(X,C)\to\mathbb{E}(X,A)\ :\ f\mapsto f^{\ast}\delta
.

(2)

\delta^{\sharp}_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to\mathbb{E}(C,X)\ :\ g\mapsto g_{\ast}\delta
.

We simply denote
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}(g)
by
\delta_{\sharp}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}(g)
, respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.9]

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},n
be as before. Define a category
\AE:=\AE^{n+2}_{(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E})}
as follows.

(1)

A pair
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
is an object of the category
\AE

and

(d_{0}^{X})_{\ast}\delta=0~~\textrm{and}~~(d^{X}_{n})^{\ast}\delta=0.

We also denote it by

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-2}^{X}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}.

For such pairs
\langle X_{\bullet},\delta\rangle
and
\langle Y_{\bullet},\rho\rangle

.

Definition 2.4
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.13]

An
n
-exangle
is an object

\AE

(1)

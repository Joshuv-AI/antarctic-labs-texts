The Petersen–Wilhelm conjecture on principal bundles
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi †, Lino Grama ∗, Llohann D. Sperança ⋆

Abstract
. This paper studies Cheeger deformations on S 3 , SO ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{S}^{3},\mathrm{SO}(3) principal bundles to obtain conditions for positive sectional curvature submersion metrics. We conclude, in particular, a stronger version of the Petersen–Wilhelm fiber dimension conjecture to the class of principal bundles. We prove any π : SO ⁡ ( 3 ) , S 3 ↪ 𝒫 → B \pi:\mathrm{SO}(3),\mathrm{S}^{3}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}\rightarrow B principal bundle over a positively curved base admits a metric of positive sectional curvature if, and only if, the submersion is fat, in particular, dim B ≥ 4 \dim B\geq 4 . The proof combines the concept of “good triples” due to Munteanu and Tapp [ MT11 ] , with a Chaves–Derdzisnki–Rigas type condition to nonnegative curvature. Additionally, the conjecture is verified for other classes of submersions.

1.
Introduction

Riemannian Submersions play an essential role in Riemannian Geometry with a lower curvature bound. The Horizontal Curvature Equation (
[
O’N66
,
Gra67
]
) highlights a key reason for this: if
\pi:M\rightarrow B
is a Riemannian submersion,
B
has a lower sectional curvature bound at least as large as any lower sectional curvature bound for
M
. One can often use it as a practical tool to construct new examples of Riemannian manifolds, especially of nonnegative or positive sectional curvature. Indeed, we remark that apart from spheres and the recent constructions in
[
PW09
,
Dea11
,
GVZ11
]
, all known examples of closed manifolds with positive sectional curvature arise via a Riemannian submersion (
[
Ber61
,
Wal72
,
Esc82
,
Baz96
,
EH98
,
Zil14
,
EZ14
]
).

Still, positive sectional curvature appears to be a highly restrictive condition. Excluding the rank one symmetric space, examples of compact manifolds admitting positive curvature have only been found in dimensions 6,7,12,13, and 24 (
[
Zil14
]
). Surprisingly, it remains an open and fundamental problem of Riemannian geometry: finding properties that distinguish the class of compact simply connected manifolds admitting nonnegative sectional curvature from its sub-class of positive sectional curvature. Seeking such distinguishment conditions, in the realm of Riemannian submersions, Petersen and Wilhelm conjectured dimension restrictions on the fibers of a positively curved submersion:

Conjecture A

(Petersen–Wilhelm Fiber dimension conjecture)
.

If
F\hookrightarrow M\rightarrow B
is a Riemannian submersion from a positively curved closed manifold
M
, then

\dim F<\dim B.

Several works already appear seeking to solve Conjecture
A
, for instance:
[
GAR18
,
Spe17a
,
Spe18
]
. Of particular interest to the results here obtained is
[
Spe18
]
. More precisely, we often consider
dual-holonomy fields
. Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Considering the natural decomposition
TM=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathcal{H}
where
\mathcal{V}
stands for the sub-bundle of
TM
containing the vectors tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, if
c:I\rightarrow M
is a geodesic such that
\dot{c}(s)\in\mathcal{H}_{c(s)}
for any
s\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
a vector field
\nu
such that
\nu(s)\in\mathcal{V}_{c(s)}
for every
s\in I
is named a dual-holonomy field if

(1)

\nabla_{\dot{c}}\nu=S_{\dot{c}}\nu-A^{*}_{\dot{c}}\nu.

Analogously, basic horizontal fields along a vertical curve
\gamma
are horizontal solutions of

(2)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}X=-A^{*}_{X}\dot{\gamma}-S_{X}(\dot{\gamma}).

In the above equations,
S_{X}
always stands for the second fundamental form of a leaf. The tensor
A^{*}_{X}
is
g
-dual of the O’Neill tensor
A_{X}Y=\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]^{\mathbf{v}}
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
. Additionally, the former equations are equivalent definitions of
holonomy
and
basic horizontal fields
, up to changing the sign
+
to
-
in the term
S_{\dot{c}}\nu
on Equation (
1
). Moreover, in the totally geodesic case, both dual-holonomy and holonomy fields along horizontal geodesics and basic horizontal fields
along vertical geodesics are the Jacobi fields induced
by, respectively, local horizontal lifting and holonomy transport.

We state our main result, which settles Conjecture
A
to the class of principal bundles:

Theorem B

(Strong Petersen–Wilhelm’s conjecture for principal fiber bundles)
.

Any
\mathrm{SO}(3),\mathrm{S}^{3}
principal bundle
\pi:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow B
over a positively curved manifold admits a metric of positive sectional curvature if, and only if,
\pi
is
fat
. In particular,
\dim B\equiv 0\pmod{4}
.

We recall that for a Riemannian submersion, being
fat
is an intrinsic property of its horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. Namely, a Riemannian submersion
\pi:M\rightarrow B
is said to be
fat
if, for every non-zero
X\in\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{V}=[\widetilde{X},\mathcal{H}]^{\mathbf{v}}
for some horizontal extension of
X
, where we denote by
\mathcal{V}
the vertical distribution associated to
\pi
. We must take some care here: in the literature, the terminology for fatness is often widely employed once assumed the preexistence of a Riemannian metric with totally geodesic leaves.

It is worth noting that verifying Petersen–Wilhem conjecture on principal bundles is reduced to the case of
\mathrm{SO}(3),~\mathrm{S}^{3}
-principal bundles. Assuming the total space of a principal bundle has a metric of positive sectional curvature implies that the structure group
G
acting by isometries has rank
1
, see Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 4.1 in
[
Wal72
]
, what reduces
G
to either
\mathrm{SO}(3),\mathrm{S}^{3},~\mathrm{S}^{1}
but the long exact homotopy sequence for fibrations implies
\dim B\geq 2
otherwise
B
is diffeomorphic to a circle (assuming connectedness), and
\mathcal{P}
has infinite fundamental group, a contradiction with the Bonnet–Myers Theorem.

It is also remarkable that Theorem
B
can be seen as the analogous
can one lift positive sectional curvature?
to the question raised by Searle and Wilhelm in
[
SW15
]
.

To accomplish the proof of Theorem
B
, we base our computations on the concept of “good triples” introduced by Munteanu and Tapp in
[
MT11
]
. Namely, a triple
\{X,V,\mathcal{A}\}
is said to be a good triple provided if
\exp(tV(s))=\exp(sX(t))
for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
V(s),X(t)
denote the Jacobi fields
along
\exp(sV)
and
\exp(tX)
, respectively, that satisfy
V(0)=V,X(0)=X
and

V^{\prime}(0)=\mathcal{A}=X^{\prime}(0)
. On the one hand, these are very rare in the sense that, given
\{X,V\}
, there are only finitely many choices of
\mathcal{A}
making
\{X,V,\mathcal{A}\}
to be a good triple provided if
X
or
V
satisfies a generic property named “weakly regular”, Definition 9.1 in
[
MT11
]
. On the other hand, good triples occur naturally in totally geodesic Riemannian
foliations by a horizontal X and a vertical V (or vice-versa). In this case,
\mathcal{A}=-A^{*}_{X}V
, see for instance, Proposition 1.4 in
[
MT11
]
.

A key curvature identity provided in Proposition 1.6 in
[
MT11
]
allows us to approach the problem of producing and/or obstructing positive sectional curvature for some Riemannian foliations. We prove a sort of generalization to it under the assumption of totally geodesic leaves:

Theorem C

(=Theorem
4.3
)
.

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a compact Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
with totally geodesic leaves and bounded holonomy. Then
A^{*}_{X}\dot{\gamma}
is basic along the geodesic
\gamma(s):=\exp(sV)
, where
V\in\mathcal{V}
and
X\in\mathcal{H}
is basic. Moreover,

(1)

\left\langle R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)(A^{*}_{X}V),A^{*}_{X}V\right\rangle=0
, where
R_{\textsl{g}}
denotes the Riemann curvature tensor associated with the Levi-Civita connection related to
g

(2)

if
Y
is a basic vector field then

\left\langle R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\right\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}A_{Y}^{*}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle=\langle A^{*}_{(\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{X}V)}V-\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V,Y\rangle.

In particular,

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V=0
if, and only if,

\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V=A^{*}_{(\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{X}V)}V

(3)

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V=0
if, and only if,
(\nabla_{X}A^{*})_{X}V=0
. Such equality is always true in locally symmetric spaces.

(4)

if the foliation is fat then
\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,X\rangle=0
.

We remark that Theorem
C
should be clear from the results in the first version of the paper
[
Spe17b
]
. Moreover, the bounded holonomy condition is translated in the case of foliations generated by the connected components of the fibers of submersion, requiring that its structure group is compact. See
[
Spe18
, Definition 1.3]
for further details.

The proof of Theorem
B
is obtained by looking carefully at the case of bundles with totally geodesic fibers. Theorem A in
[
SSW15
]
implies that it is always possible to get, using a Cheeger deformation, a metric with totally geodesic leaves. Although their approach is curvature independent, we combine a Chaves–Derdzinski–Rigas type condition (as in Theorem
1.1
), first appearing in
[
CDR92
]
, with the maintenance of positive curvatures under Cheeger deformations and Theorem
C
to prove:

Theorem D

(=Theorem
4.2
)
.

For any integer
p\geq 2
, any
\mathrm{SO}(3),\mathrm{S}^{3}
Riemannian principal bundle
\pi:(\mathcal{P},\textsl{g})\rightarrow(B,\textsl{h})
with compact structure group over a positively curved manifold admits an invariant
C^{p}
-metric with positive sectional curvature and totally geodesic fibers if, and only if,
\pi
is fat.

At this point, it is crucial to note that Theorem
C
provides the real analysis to ensure the positivity of sectional curvature. Such a theorem deals completely with the case of totally geodesic leaves. Additionally, for the general case, one notes that assuming the existence of a non-fat point, in particular, the presence of an orthonormal set
X\in\mathcal{H},V\in\mathcal{V}
with
A^{*}_{X}V=0
recovers
R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V=0
, indicating that the proof to this case could be derived from the employed technique used in the totally geodesic setting, this is what we explore (see the proof of Corollary
4.6
).

We digress a bit illustrating how the proof of Theorem
B
can be obtained in the totally geodesic case by simply combining Theorem
C
with the classical condition of Chaves–Derdzinski–Rigas.

A sketch of the proof of Theorem
B

Recall the Chaves–Derdzisnki–Rigas result:

Theorem 1.1

(Chaves–Derdzisnki–Rigas,
[
CDR92
]
)
.

Let
\pi:G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{P}\rightarrow B
to be a principal bundle with
G=\mathrm{S}^{3},\mathrm{SO}(3)
and
\dim B\geq 2
. Fix a Riemannian metric
h
at
B
and a bi-invariant inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Then there is
t>0
such that
\textsl{g}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\pi^{\ast}\textsl{h}+t\langle\omega,\omega\rangle
has positive sectional curvature if, and only if,
for any point
p\in\mathcal{P}
and any mutually orthonormal vectors
X,Y\in T_{\pi(p)}B
and any non-zero element
u\in\mathrm{Ad}(\mathcal{P}):=\mathcal{P}\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}\mathfrak{g}
over
\pi(p)
it holds that

(3)

R^{\mathbf{h}}(X,Y,Y,X)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle u,\Omega(X,X_{k})\right\rangle^{2}>\langle u,(\nabla_{X}\Omega)(X,Y)\rangle^{2},

where
R^{\mathbf{h}}
is the curvature tensor of
(B,\textsl{h})
,
\Omega
is the curvature of the connection form
\omega
,
\nabla
is the covariant derivative induced by
\omega
and
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
is an arbitrary orthonormal basis to
T_{\pi(p)}B
.

Remark 1
.

We often name inequality (
3
) as
the CDR condition
.

We first remark that there is a hidden
Cheeger deformation
(Section
2
) on Theorem
1.1
: note that
\mathrm{Ad}
corresponds to the representation
\mathrm{Ad}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
so the following action

g\in G,~g\star(p,V):=(gp,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(V))

defines a free and isometric action in
\mathcal{P}\times\mathfrak{g}
that recovers
\mathrm{Ad}(\mathcal{P})
as quotient. Also, observe that
\Omega
can be identified with the usual O’Neill tensor via

, where the superscript

stands to the corresponding action vector obtained from

. Equation (

K_{B}(X,Y)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle U^{*},A_{X}X_{k}\right\rangle^{2}>\langle U^{*},(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}Y\rangle^{2}

where

is an action vector (see Section

) for needed definitions).

Since

one gets that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle U^{*},A_{X}X_{k}\right\rangle^{2}=|A^{*}_{X}U^{*}|^{2}

thus translating the CDR condition in

K_{B}(X,Y)|A^{*}_{X}U^{*}|^{2}>\langle U^{*},(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}Y\rangle^{2}.

We sketch how to show that if
\pi
is fat, then
\langle U^{*},(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}Y\rangle=0
, concluding the proof since inequality (

is verified under totally geodesic fibers’ assumption.

To conclude that
\langle U^{*},(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}Y\rangle
we observe that, as consequence of Theorem

, in the totally geodesic setting (see Corollary

)

(a)

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)=0
for every

, for every

(b)

if the foliation is fat then

and every

.

From Theorem 1.5.1 in

one gets

, therefore, it suffices to show

for any basic vectors

and every

.

Under the fatness assumption, any horizontal vector

can be uniquely written as

where

and

. This holds since the fatness condition ensures that if

then

We now have

\displaystyle R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)

\displaystyle=R_{\textsl{g}}(X,aA^{*}_{X}V+Z,aA^{*}_{X}V+Z,V)

\displaystyle=a^{2}R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V,V)+2aR_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V)+R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Z,Z,V).

Item (

) guarantees that

. Item (

) in Theorem

. Finally, item (

. Indeed, once

is a tensor, we can extend

The proof is done once one checks that the fatness assumption ensures

is parallel to

such that

.

which admits a transversal pair of smooth foliations by geodesics of

(s,t)\mapsto f(s,t)\in M
.

Proposition 1.4 in

ensures that if

is a vertical vector at

consists of a

. In this manner, if

denotes the Jacobi field along

with

and

, and

is a doubly ruled parameterized surface. As in

, such a doubly ruled parameterized surface is the
vertizontal surface induced by lifting a single geodesic in the base space to points
along a geodesic in a fiber.

Items (

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
with bounded holonomy (Definition 1.3 in
[
Spe18
]

\mathcal{F}

g

(1)

A^{*}_{X}V
is basic along the geodesic generated by
V
provided if
X
is basic

(2)

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V,V)=0
. Moreover,
R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V,A^{*}_{X}V,Y)=\langle\nabla_{X}A_{Y}^{*}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

(3)

if the foliation is fat then
R_{\textsl{g}}(X,V,A^{*}_{X}V,X)=0
.

Proof.

(1)

Recall that under the assumption of totally geodesic leaves, a basic vector field
Z
can be characterized by
\nabla_{V}Z=-A^{*}_{Z}V
. Moreover, since the fibers are totally geodesic, we have that
\nabla_{V}
preserves both
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
. On the other hand, if
X
and
Y
are basic horizontal fields, then
-A^{*}_{X}V
is a horizontal vector and

\displaystyle\langle-\nabla_{V}A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=-V\langle A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-V\langle V,A_{X}Y\rangle-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle V,\nabla_{V}(A_{X}Y)\rangle-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle V,\nabla_{V}(A_{X}Y)+A_{X}A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle.

Finally, since
A_{X}Y
is a Killing field whenever
X
and
Y
are basic it holds that

-\langle V,\nabla_{V}(A_{X}Y)\rangle=\langle V,\nabla_{V}(A_{X}Y)\rangle

hence
\langle V,\nabla_{V}(A_{X}Y)\rangle=0
and

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{V}A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle A_{Y}(A^{*}_{X}V),V\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{A^{*}_{X}V}Y,V\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Y,A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V\rangle.

Hence, we conclude that
\nabla_{V}(A^{*}_{X}V)=-A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V
, i.e.,
A^{*}_{X}V
is a horizontal field satisfying the equation that characterizes basic fields.

(2)

Note that if
X
is basic and
[X,V]=0
then assuming (
1
), denote by
s\mapsto\gamma(s)
the geodesic generated by
V
and by
t\mapsto c(t)
the geodesic generated by
X
. Let
X(s)
the Jacobi field along
\gamma

\frac{\nabla}{ds}X(s)=-A^{*}_{X(s)}(\dot{\gamma}(s))
and
\nu(t)
be the dual-holonomy (hence Jacobi) field along
c
which satisfies
\frac{\nabla}{dt}\nu(t)=-A^{*}_{\dot{c}(t)}\nu(t).
As discussed earlier, and following Proposition 1.4 in
[
MT11
]
, one recalls that
f(s,t)=\exp(t\cdot X(s))=\exp(s\cdot V(t))
is a doubly ruled parameterized surface so

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial s}|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}_{\textsl{g}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}_{\textsl{g}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\frac{\nabla}{ds}\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle+2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,\frac{\nabla}{ds}A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle\frac{\nabla}{ds}\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle-R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V+\frac{\nabla}{dt}\frac{\nabla}{ds}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle-R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V-\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\frac{d}{dt}\langle A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\frac{d}{dt}\langle V,A_{A^{*}_{X}V}A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-2\frac{d}{dt}0

\displaystyle=-2\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle.

We now prove that
\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{Y}V,A_{X}^{*}V\rangle
. This follows easily since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\frac{\nabla}{ds}\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle-\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle+\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}\frac{\nabla}{ds}A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle-\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle-\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{A^{*}_{X}V}V,Y\rangle-\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle+X[\langle V,A_{Y}(A^{*}_{X}V)\rangle]-\langle\frac{\nabla}{dt}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle+X\langle A^{*}_{Y}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-\langle\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{Y}V\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,Y\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}A_{Y}^{*}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle.

So if

.

(3)

Due to the fatness assumption, fixing any vertical vector

But since

\displaystyle\lambda X

\displaystyle=[\omega_{V}(\omega_{V}(X,\cdot)^{\sharp},\cdot)]^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=[\omega_{V}(\textsl{g}(A_{X}\cdot,V)^{\sharp},\cdot)]^{\sharp}

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(M,\textsl{g})
be a Riemannian manifold with a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
induced by the fibers of a Riemannian submersion with a compact structure group. If the foliation is totally geodesic for
g
, then

(1)

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)=0
for every
Y\in\ker~A_{X}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}

(2)

if the foliation is fat then
R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)=0
for every basic vectors
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
and every
V\in\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

(1)

This follows from applying the Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection: One arrives at

3\textsl{g}(\nabla_{X}(A_{X}Y),V)=X\textsl{g}(Y,A^{*}_{X}V)-\textsl{g}(\nabla_{X}V,A_{X}Y).

Since we can extend
X,Y
by
\nabla^{\mathbf{h}}_{X}X=\nabla^{\mathbf{h}}_{Y}Y=\nabla^{\mathbf{h}}_{X}Y=0
and
V
by a holonomy field along the geodesic generated by
X
one gets that
\textsl{g}(\nabla_{X}(A_{X}Y),V)=R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)
. Due to the extension of
V
to holonomy fields one gets
\textsl{g}(\nabla_{X}V,A_{X}Y)=\textsl{g}(S_{X}V,A_{X}Y)=0
. Moreover, if
Y\in\ker~A_{X}
then
Y\perp A^{*}_{X}V
and the claim follows.

(2)

If
X=0
or
Y=0
there is nothing to prove. Moreover, if
X
is parallel to
Y
, then the result holds triviality due to the anti-symmetries of the Riemann tensor. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume
X,Y\neq 0
and
X\perp Y
. We also assume that
V\neq 0
; otherwise, the claim is trivially true.

Due to the fatness assumption, one derives that the map
A^{*}_{X}:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\{X\}\right)^{\perp}
is injective. Moreover, that
Z\perp\mathrm{im}~A^{*}_{X}
if, and only if,
Z\in\ker~\{A_{X}:\mathcal{H}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\{X\}\right)^{\perp}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\}
. We thus have a unique decomposition
Y=yA^{*}_{X}V+Z
for
Z\in\ker~\{A_{X}:\mathcal{H}\cap\left(\mathbb{R}\{X\}\right)^{\perp}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\}
.

Since

\displaystyle R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Y,Y,V)

\displaystyle=R_{\textsl{g}}(X,yA^{*}_{X}V+Z,yA^{*}_{X}V+Z,V)

\displaystyle=y^{2}R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V,V)+R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Z,Z,V)+2yR_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V)

\displaystyle=0+R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Z,Z,V)+2yR_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V),

where the last equality comes from item
(2)
. Also observe that item
(3)
ensures
R_{\textsl{g}}(X,Z,Z,V)=0.
Furthermore, item
(2)
once more guarantees

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V)=-\langle\nabla_{X}A^{*}_{Z}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle.

Since
R_{\textsl{g}}
is a tensor we can extend
A^{*}_{X}V
via horizontal lifts to the computations ensuring that
\nabla^{\mathbf{h}}_{X}A^{*}_{X}V=0
,
and so

R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V)=X\langle A^{*}_{Z}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle.

Now the proof is done since, due to the fatness assumption, fixing any vertical vector
V\in\mathcal{V}_{p}
, we can write
aZ=A^{*}_{A^{*}_{Z}V}V
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
(recall the proof of item (
3
) of Theorem
C
) and so
R_{\textsl{g}}(X,A^{*}_{X}V,Z,V)=X\langle aZ,V\rangle
. ∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\pi\colon(M,\mathcal{H})\to(B,b)
be a submersion with an adapted metric

Proof.

According to Theorem 1.5.1 in
[

\displaystyle\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,X\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\nabla_{X}A)_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,V\rangle-\langle S_{X}A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,V\rangle-\langle V,S_{X}A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}(A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V),V\rangle-\langle A_{X}(\nabla_{X}(A^{*}_{X}V)),V\rangle-2\langle A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,S_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=X\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,\nabla_{X}V\rangle-\langle A_{X}(\nabla_{X}(A^{*}_{X}V)),V\rangle-2\langle A_{X}A^{*}_{X}V,S_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=X|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}(S_{X}V)\rangle-\langle\nabla_{X}(A^{*}_{X}V),A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=X|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}(S_{X}V)\rangle-\frac{1}{2}X|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}X|A^{*}_{X}V|^{2}-\langle A^{*}_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}(S_{X}V)\rangle.

So
\langle R(X,V)A^{*}_{X}V,X\rangle=0

. On the other hand, the right-hand-side of Equation (

) (the WNN condition), computed extending
V
by holonomy fields along the geodesic generated by

, is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle(\nabla_{X}A^{*})_{X}V+A_{X}^{*}S_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}(A^{*}_{X}V)+A^{*}_{X}(S_{X}V)+A_{X}^{*}S_{X}V,A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}(A^{*}_{X}V),A^{*}_{X}V\rangle+2\langle A^{*}_{X}(S_{X}V),A^{*}_{X}V\rangle

Remark 3

According to Section 2 in

Given vector bundles

and

with Euclidean metrics and a bundle map

Corollary 4.8
.

Let

be a compact manifold with a Riemannian foliation

with totally geodesic leaves. Then

(B)

(\nabla_{X}A^{*})_{A^{*}_{X}V}V=0

The main idea is to observe if

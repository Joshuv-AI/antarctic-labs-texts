THEORY OF HYPERSURFACES OF A FINSLER SPACE WITH THE GENERALIZED SQUARE METRIC
Sonia Rani, Vinod Kumar, Mohammad Rafee

Abstract
The emergence of generalized square metrics in Finsler geometry can be attributed to various classification concerning ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -metrics. They have excellent geometric properties in Finsler geometry. Within the scope of this research paper, we have conducted an investigation into the generalized square metric denoted as F ⁡ ( x , y ) = [ α ⁡ ( x , y ) + β ⁡ ( x , y ) ] n + 1 [ α ⁡ ( x , y ) ] n F(x,y)=\frac{[\alpha(x,y)+\beta(x,y)]^{n+1}}{[\alpha(x,y)]^{n}} , focusing specifically on its application to the Finslerian hypersurface. Furthermore, the classification and existence of first, second, and third kind of hyperplanes of the Finsler manifold has been established. AMS Subject Classification: 53B40, 53C60 Key Words and Phrases: Finsler Space, Finsler metric, Generalized square metric, Hypersurface of Finsler space, Hyperplane.

We know that a hypersurface is an embedded subspace of codimension 1. That is, a subspace of dimension less that 1 than the dimension of a given manifold is called hypersuraface of the given manifold or space. For the sake of our study we are dealing with hypersurfaces rather than the subspaces of arbitrary dimensions. In this paper we are specifically confined to Finslerian hypersurfaces

Let us proof the following propositions in context of Finslerian hypersurfaces.

\displaystyle b_{i}y^{i}=0,i.e.,\beta=0

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

be a Finsler manifold, where

, is a generalized square metric and

\displaystyle=eb_{i}b_{j}

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(M,F)
be a Finsler manifold, where
F(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{(\alpha+\beta)^{n+1}}{\alpha^{n}}
,
n\in N
, is a generalized square metric and
M^{n-1}
be its associated hypersurface. Then the hypersurface
M^{n-1}
will not be hyperplane of third kind.

Proof.

We know from sufficient conditions of Lemma
3.9
a hypersurface becomes a hyperplane of third kind if
H_{\alpha}=0,

H_{\alpha\beta}=0
and
M_{\alpha\beta}=0
. Now we consider these three sufficient conditions one by one.

1.

If
H_{\alpha}=0
, then we get the condition
2b_{ij}=b_{i}e(x)b_{j}(x)+b_{j}e(x)b_{i}(x)
, which has already been proved above and is termed as the condition of hyperplane of first kind.

2.

If
H_{\alpha\beta}=0
, then we get the condition
b_{ij}=eb_{i}b_{j}
, which has already been proved above and is termed as the condition of hyperplane of second kind.

3.

Now put
M_{\alpha\beta}=0
in Equation (
4.15
), we get

\displaystyle 0=\frac{(n+1)}{2\alpha}\left(\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}}{1+n(n+1)}}\right)h_{\alpha\beta}

which implies that no condition could be deduced to satisfy
M_{\alpha\beta}=0
, i.e., it is impossible to find a condition under which a hypersurface becomes a hyperplane of third kind, as term on the R.H.S. of the above equation can never be zero.

Finally, we shown that hypersurface
M^{n-1}
is not a hyperplane of third kind.
∎

Pion-proton correlation in neutrino interactions on nuclei
Tejin Cai, Xianguo Lu, Daniel Ruterbories

Abstract
In neutrino-nucleus interactions, a proton produced with a correlated pion might exhibit a left-right asymmetry relative to the lepton scattering plane even when the pion is absorbed. Absent in other proton production mechanisms, such an asymmetry measured in charged-current pionless production could reveal the details of the absorbed-pion events that are otherwise inaccessible. In this study, we demonstrate the idea of using final-state proton left-right asymmetries to quantify the absorbed-pion event fraction and underlying kinematics. This technique might provide critical information that helps constrain all underlying channels in neutrino-nucleus interactions in the GeV regime.



I
Introduction

In the GeV regime, neutrinos interact with nuclei via neutrino-nucleon quasi-elastic scattering (QE), resonant production (RES), and deeply inelastic scattering (DIS). These primary interactions are embedded in the nucleus, where nuclear effects can modify the event topology. For example, in interactions where no pion is produced outside the nucleus, one could find contributions from both RES that is followed by pion absorption in the nucleus—a type of final-state interactions (FSIs)—and two-particle-two-hole (
2p2h
) excitation
Nieves et al. 2012
besides QE. This admixture of underlying channels complicates the experimental studies of neutrino oscillations
Abe et al. 2015
;
Acciarri et al. 2015
where interaction cross sections and neutrino energy reconstruction are severely affected by nuclear dynamics.

The recent development of data analysis techniques
Lu et al. 2016
;
Furmanski and Sobczyk 2017
allows the for separation of QE from non-QE processes in the aforementioned zero-pion (
0\pi
) topology. And yet, the remaining RES—referred to as
absorbed-pion events
in this work—and
2p2h
contributions occupy very similar phase space not only in the single-particle kinematics but also in the kinematic imbalance exploited by those techniques
Lu et al. 2018
;
Abe et al. 2018
. Their experimental evidence is the otherwise unaccounted for measured excess of event rates
Rodrigues et al. 2016
;
Gran et al. 2018
. Complementary to the
0\pi
topology, pion production
Eberly et al. 2015
;
Altinok et al. 2017
has provided important constraints on both primary pion production and pion FSIs (cf. for example, Ref.
Mosel and Gallmeister 2018
;
Stowell et al. 2019
). Had there been no
2p2h
contributions, details of absorbed-pion events could have been better determined. The lack of experimental signature to identify either process
Niewczas and Sobczyk 2016
;
Weinstein et al. 2016
is one of the biggest challenges in the study of neutrino interactions in the GeV regime. In this paper, we examine the phenomenon of pion-proton correlation and discuss the method of using final-state (i.e., post-FSI) protons to study absorbed-pion events.

II
Methodology

In neutrino-nucleon scattering where a proton and a pion are produced,

\displaystyle\nu\textrm{p}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{-}\textrm{p}\pi^{+},

(1)

\displaystyle\nu\textrm{n}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{-}\textrm{p}\pi^{0},

(2)

with
\nu
,
p
,
n
,
\ell^{-}
, and
\pi
being the neutrino, proton, neutron, charged lepton, and pions, respectively, the (lepton) scattering plane is defined as spanned by the momenta of the incoming and outgoing leptons,
\vec{p}_{\nu}
and
\vec{p}_{\ell}
. With a stationary initial nucleon, momentum conservation requires that the final-state proton and pion occupy either side of the scattering plane. If we define the direction of
\vec{p}_{\nu}\times\vec{p}_{\ell}
to be the right of the plane, a proton on the left means a pion on the right, and a left-right asymmetry of the proton indicates an opposite asymmetry of the pion (Fig.
1
). This is a spatial correlation between the pion and proton as a result of momentum conservation. In general neutrino interactions where a proton is produced, we define the proton left-right asymmetry (in the lab frame unless otherwise stated) as

\displaystyle A_{\textrm{LR}}\equiv\frac{N_{\textrm{L}}-N_{\textrm{R}}}{N_{\textrm{L}}+N_{\textrm{R}}},

(3)

where
N_{\textrm{L~(R)}}
stands for the event rate of the proton being on the left (right) of the scattering plane. The left-right asymmetries reported in existing measurements, as quoted in later discussions, are defined for the pion and hence need to be flipped (i.e. multiplied by
-1
) to translate to the corresponding proton asymmetries in reactions (
1
) and (
2
).

In neutrino interactions on nuclei where there are no pions in the final state,

\displaystyle\nu\textrm{A}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{-}\textrm{p}\textrm{X},

(4)

with
A
being the target nucleus and
X
a hadronic system that doesn’t include any pions, reactions (
1
) and (
2
) take place on the constituent nucleons. The pion is subsequently absorbed in FSIs, while the proton propagates through the nucleus, carrying all primary information, including
A_{\textrm{LR}}
. A detector then measures the final-state proton. However, protons from QE and
2p2h
are indistinguishable from these correlated protons. As a result, all measured primary information from reactions (
1
) and (
2
) is diluted. The extent to which
A_{\textrm{LR}}
is reduced depends on the absorbed-pion fraction
S_{\pi}
in the sample. In other words, by measuring the attenuation of the proton
A_{\textrm{LR}}
, we could obtain
S_{\pi}
in reaction (
4
).

This method relies on the following assumptions. First, there is a pion-proton correlation unique to pion production on free nucleons. In this work, we consider the left-right asymmetry with respect to the scattering plane. In the 1970s and 1980s, hydrogen and deuterium bubble chamber experiments provided the only measurements on (quasi-)free nucleon targets. With a deuterium target, ANL reported
A_{\textrm{LR}}=0.053\pm 0.035
of
\pi^{+}
in reaction (
1
) with a neutrino beam energy distribution that peaked near 0.9 GeV
Radecky et al. 1982
. In 2017, MINERvA reported
A_{\textrm{LR}}=0.15\pm 0.10
of
\pi^{0}
in the
\textrm{p}\pi^{0}
rest frame from reaction (
2
) on a hydrocarbon target at the Low-Energy NuMI beam with peak energy about 3 GeV
Altinok et al. 2017
. These asymmetries indicate the polarized production of resonances, dominated by
\Delta(1232)
, and are ascribed to the interference between the resonant and nonresonant amplitudes
Adler 1968
;
Adler 1975
;
Sobczyk et al. 2018
;
Kabirnezhad 2018
. Most recently, T2K measured the left-right asymmetry in
\pi^{+}
production on hydrocarbon over its off-axis neutrino beam spectrum peaking at 0.6 GeV and reported
A_{\textrm{LR}}=0.038\pm 0.046

Abe et al. 2019a
. It is consistent with a linear extrapolation of the previous measurements as a function of the beam energy. Currently, the bubble chamber data are dominantly constraining theory calculations on the nucleon-level asymmetry. It would no longer be sufficient because of the required precision and the potential beam-energy dependence for current and future experiments. A new method to measure pion-proton production on hydrogen has been proposed in Ref.
Lu et al. 2015
, which would provide renewed access to those asymmetries on free protons. The second assumption is that, the correlation needs to survive nuclear effects, such as Fermi motion and nucleon FSIs. In the following, we investigate the impact of nuclear effects on
A_{\textrm{LR}}
and demonstrate the idea of measuring absorbed-pion events with
A_{\textrm{LR}}
attenuation.

Monte Carlo Calculation

In this work, we use the neutrino event generator

2.12

\displaystyle 0.45~\textrm{GeV}/\textit{c}<

\displaystyle\nu\textrm{n}\to\ell^{-}\textrm{n}\pi^{+},

(7)

In order to study
A_{\textrm{LR}}
attenuation in a controlled way, we removed the
genie

\phi^{\ast}

0<\phi^{\ast}<180^{\circ}
. The following weighting schemes are used for comparison
(0^{\circ}\leq\phi^{\ast}<360^{\circ})
:

\displaystyle w_{1}(\phi^{\ast})

\displaystyle=1+\sgn(\phi^{\ast}-180^{\circ})\cdot A_{0},

(8)

\displaystyle w_{2}(\phi^{\ast})

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\pi}{2}A_{0}\sin\phi^{\ast},

(9)

where
\sgn(x)
is the sign function and
A_{0}
is the input

in the primary pion-nucleon rest frame. A similar approach can be found in Ref.
Sánchez 2016
. Because
N_{\textrm{L,R}}
are integrals in the respective regions, their asymmetry is not sensitive to the modulation of the weights away from the left-right boundary,
\phi^{\ast}=0^{\circ}
and
180^{\circ}
. Therefore, the
\sgn
-and
\sin
-weighting schemes are general as they characterize different transitional behaviors—abrupt and smooth, respectively. The weight is applied to all RES channels before FSI. The post-FSI proton
A_{\textrm{LR}}
is then calculated for the leading (i.e. highest-momentum) proton for the absorbed-pion subsample and the overall sample, labeled by
A_{\textrm{RES}}
and
A_{\textrm{All}}
, respectively.

To characterize the impact of nuclear effects on the asymmetry, we organize the simulated events according to the transverse momentum imbalance
Lu et al. 2016
;
Lu et al. 2018
;
Abe et al. 2018
,

\displaystyle\delta p_{\textrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\left|\vec{p}_{\textrm{T}}^{\,\mu}+\vec{p}_{\textrm{T}}^{\,\textrm{p}}\right|,

(10)

where
\vec{p}_{\textrm{T}}^{\,\mu\,(\textrm{p})}

is the transverse projection of the initial nucleon momentum due to Fermi motion; in full it represents the sum of the initial nucleon momentum and any intranuclear momentum exchange including FSI. Higher-order accuracy in describing the Fermi motion can be achieved by the 3-dimensional momentum imbalance introduced in Ref.
Furmanski and Sobczyk 2017

IV
Discussion

Comparing the two Adler angle
\phi^{\ast}
-weighting schemes [Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
)],
w_{1}
is more susceptible to the primary “wobbling” due to Fermi motion because of its abrupt transition at the left-right boundary (
\phi^{\ast}=0^{\circ}
and
180^{\circ}
). This explains the larger reduction of the asymmetry, namely smaller
A_{\textrm{RES}}/A_{0}
, for
w_{1}
in both Figs.
2
and
3
. Neither scheme has kinematic dependence. In reality, however, the asymmetry is a function of the primary nucleon and pion kinematics. This requires precise phase-space matching (i.e. comparing the same final-state muon and proton phase space) between the
0\pi
and
1\pi
samples such that the only difference between the two samples is the pion FSI. Since pion FSI is decoupled from the primary pion-nucleon production, the phase-space matched asymmetry from the
1\pi
sample can be used to infer the
0\pi
one. In addition, the
1\pi
-tagging in this work does not have any phase space restriction. One would need to study the experimental threshold effect of the tagging techniques.

The effect of the absorbed-pion knockout in
A_{\textrm{RES}}^{0\pi}
can be quantified by the event rates of the leading protons,
n^{\textrm{N}}
and
n^{\pi}
, originating from the primary nucleon (
N
) and pion, respectively. Assuming the asymmetries from these two components are exactly opposite, and they are of the same size as in
A_{\textrm{RES}}^{1\pi}
, we have

\displaystyle A_{\textrm{RES}}^{0\pi}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{n^{\textrm{N}}-n^{\pi}}{n^{\textrm{N}}+n^{\pi}}\frac{n^{\textrm{N}}_{\textrm{L}}-n^{\textrm{N}}_{\textrm{R}}}{n^{\textrm{N}}},

(16)

where L and R denote the left and right parts of the event rates. The first factor on the RHS is the asymmetry between the two leading-proton origins, denoted as
A_{\textrm{KO}}
, and the second one is identified as
A_{\textrm{RES}}^{1\pi}
following the assumption. From Eq. (
15
), we have

\displaystyle f_{A}\simeq A_{\textrm{KO}}.

(17)

Therefore, the experimental observable
C_{\textrm{LR}}
reads

\displaystyle C_{\textrm{LR}}\simeq\frac{1}{f_{N}^{1\pi}}S_{\pi}A_{\textrm{KO}}.

(18)

It has sensitivities to both the absorbed-pion event rate and kinematics. In addition to the leading proton asymmetry in
0\pi
events, one could consider the asymmetry of the subleading proton. The corresponding
A_{\textrm{KO}}
will flip sign, as a leading proton from the primary nucleon means a subleading proton from the primary pion. By combining the leading and subleading proton asymmetries in a two-proton
0\pi
sample, useful information on
f_{A}
could be extracted. In the few GeV regime, the pion production and the corresponding pion-proton correlation in this
de facto
shallow inelastic scattering (SIS) region is not well studied. This gives rise to significant uncertainties in
f_{N}^{1\pi}
. The SIS background could be reduced by restricting the invariant mass of the hadronic system,
W
, via calorimetry, like for example in Ref.
Le et al. 2019
, or by restricting the tagged pion momentum to reduce high-
W
contributions.

The required sample size
N_{\textrm{All}}
of an asymmetry measurement depends on the asymmetry
A_{\textrm{All}}
and the targeted relative statistical uncertainty
\varepsilon
:

\displaystyle N_{\textrm{All}}\simeq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}A_{\textrm{All}}^{2}}\textrm{, for }A_{\textrm{All}}^{2}\ll 1.

(19)

Therefore, for a primary resonant asymmetry
A_{\textrm{RES}}
of 0.05–0.2, a measurement of
A_{\textrm{All}}
with 30% (relative) statistical uncertainty would require an order of 10–100K
0\pi
-events in the several-GeV neutrino energy region. In the sub-GeV region where the pion production is close to the threshold, the smaller amount of pion absorption only allows for sensitivity to larger
A_{\textrm{RES}}
. On the other hand, if we measure proton
A_{\textrm{All}}
as a function of the muon kinematics, the determination of left/right only requires angular but not momentum measurement. This would greatly increase the sample size. This analysis strategy is of particular interest to non-magnetised trackers. For example, the MINERvA Low-Energy
0\pi
measurement
Lu et al. 2018
selects elastically scattered and contained protons to ensure the proton momentum-by-range precision. The sample size could increase by a factor of about 3 (roughly the ratio between total and elastic proton-nucleus cross sections) if the proton momentum is not required, giving about 12K signal events. Furthermore, the MINERvA data using the NuMI Medium-Energy beam peaking at about 6 GeV
Valencia et al. 2019
would provide about 10 times more statistics than the Low-Energy data, enabling a sensitivity to
A_{\textrm{RES}}\simeq 0.05
. Finally, the systematic uncertainties of measuring the asymmetry would be better controlled than for a cross-section measurement because of the cancellation of the flux uncertainty which is currently at the 10% level.

V
Summary and OUtlook

In this work, we discuss the idea of pion-proton correlation and its application in the study of absorbed-pion events using final-state protons. Pion left-right asymmetries have been previously measured in neutrino CC production of overtly pion-proton final states, as an indication of the
\Delta(1232)
-resonance
\phi^{\ast}
-polarisation. We demonstrate with simulations that the correlated proton asymmetry,
A_{\textrm{LR}}
, can be observed in neutrino
0\pi
production on nuclei.

Compared to electron-nucleus and pion-nucleus (cf., for example, Ref.
Pinzon Guerra et al. 2019
) scattering where the absorbed pion can be inferred to by the missing energy-momentum, there has been no direct evidence of pion absorption in neutrino
0\pi
events. Measuring the proton left-right asymmetry alone would be the first demonstration. This could possibly be done with the existing T2K
Abe et al. 2018
;
Abe et al. 2019b
, MicroBooNE
Acciarri et al. 2017
, NOvA
Ayres et al. 2004
, and MINERvA
Lu et al. 2018
;
Valencia et al. 2019
data. The
genie
-predicted asymmetry-flip observed at
\delta p_{\textrm{T}}\sim 0.8~\textrm{GeV}/\textit{c}
is closely related to the kinematics of the absorbed pions and its knockout. The crossover appears to be a robust experimental observable as it is shown to be independent of the weighting scheme and the strength of the input asymmetry. The reproduction of a measured crossover would be an important benchmark for nuclear-effect modeling.

We further introduce
C_{\textrm{LR}}
, the asymmetry ratio between
0\pi
and
1\pi
events, as an experimental probe for the absorbed-pion fraction
S_{\pi}
up to the
genie
-predicted plateau at
\delta p_{\textrm{T}}\sim 0.4~\textrm{GeV}/\textit{c}
. It would be interesting to investigate further how this could constrain
2p2h
contributions, as it has been conjectured that the
\delta p_{\textrm{T}}
region 0.3–0.4
~\textrm{GeV}/\textit{c}
is where current
2p2h
models have largest deficit
Lu et al. 2018
. The full potential of the proposed techniques could be realised by the existing MINERvA Medium-Energy
Valencia et al. 2019
and the future SBND
Mcconkey 2018
data sets.

The pion-proton correlation considered in this work comes from pion production with the interference between resonant and nonresonant amplitudes. This would be the dominant interaction channel at the DUNE
Acciarri et al. 2015
energy. However, similar correlation also exists in rare processes such as neutrino deeply virtual meson production
Kopeliovich et al. 2012
;
Siddikov and Schmidt 2019
. Finally, even though we have only discussed neutrino interactions, the antineutrino counterparts,

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}\textrm{p}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{+}\textrm{p}\pi^{-},

(20)

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}\textrm{p}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{+}\textrm{n}\pi^{0},

(21)

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}\textrm{n}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{+}\textrm{n}\pi^{-},

(22)

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}\textrm{A}

\displaystyle\to\ell^{+}\textrm{p}\textrm{X},

(23)

can be studied analogously. By revealing the absorbed-pion fraction in antineutrino interactions, these additional measurements could be useful for the
CP

The kinematics of the primary pion production considered in this work [Eqs. (

\displaystyle\nu\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\to\mu\Delta,

(24)

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle\to\textrm{N}\pi,

(25)

where
\mathcal{N}
is the in-coming nucleon, and
\Delta
is the
\Delta
resonance (or more generally the 4-momentum sum of the out-going nucleon
N
and pion
\pi
). The angular distributions of
N
and
\pi
are determined by the decay [Eq. (
25
)] kinematics in the
\Delta
rest frame (quantities in this frame are denoted by “
∗
”), where the Adler angles
\theta^{\ast}
and
\phi^{\ast}
are defined: if we denote the usual Cartesian basis vectors as
\hat{x}^{\ast}
,
\hat{y}^{\ast}
, and
\hat{z}^{\ast}
, and the nucleon 3-momentum
\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\cdot\hat{x}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left|\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\right|\sin\theta^{\ast}\cos\phi^{\ast},

(26)

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\cdot\hat{y}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left|\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\right|\sin\theta^{\ast}\sin\phi^{\ast},

(27)

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\cdot\hat{z}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left|\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}}^{\,\ast}\right|\cos\theta^{\ast}.

(28)

Throughout this work, we use the angles of the nucleon, instead of the pion, unless otherwise specified.

In the lab frame, if
\mathcal{N}
is stationary, such as in the scattering on hydrogen, the
\Delta
momentum is along the direction of the lepton momentum transfer,

\displaystyle\vec{q}\equiv\vec{p}_{\nu}-\vec{p}_{\mu},

(29)

where
\vec{p}_{\nu}
is the neutrino momentum. Therefore, in the
\Delta
rest frame,
\hat{z}^{\ast}
and
\hat{y}^{\ast}
are naturally defined by
\vec{p}_{\nu}^{\,\ast}-\vec{p}_{\mu}^{\,\ast}
and
\vec{p}_{\nu}^{\,\ast}\times\vec{p}_{\mu}^{\,\ast}
, respectively
Radecky et al. 1982
.

However, when
\mathcal{N}
is subject to Fermi motion, as is the dominant case in current experimental situations, there are two prescriptions to obtain the Adler angles:

(1)

via a direct boost from the lab frame to the
\Delta
rest frame which we denote by the subscript “1”, standing for “one-boost frame”. Then
\hat{z}^{\ast}_{1}
and
\hat{y}^{\ast}_{1}
are defined by
\vec{p}_{\nu 1}^{\,\ast}-\vec{p}_{\mu 1}^{\,\ast}
and
\vec{p}_{\nu 1}^{\,\ast}\times\vec{p}_{\mu 1}^{\,\ast}
, respectively. The Adler angles
\theta^{\ast}_{1}
and
\phi^{\ast}_{1}
are calculated from Eqs. (
26
)–(
28
) with
\vec{p}_{\textrm{N}1}^{\,\ast}
.

(2)

via a first boost from the lab frame to the
\mathcal{N}
rest frame, followed by a second one to the
\Delta
rest frame which we denote by the subscript “2” for “two-boost frame”. The corresponding basis vectors are similarly obtained as before, and so are the Adler angles
\theta^{\ast}_{2}
and
\phi^{\ast}_{2}
.

These two
\Delta
rest frames are identical if the Fermi motion of
\mathcal{N}
is collinear with
\vec{q}
in the lab frame; but in general, the respective momenta of a given particle are different. At first glance, the two-boost frame seems the only correct one because the Fermi motion is removed by its first boost. As a matter of fact, the two frames only differ by a rotation, known as the Wigner rotation
Wigner 1939
, and since angles are preserved under a rotation, we have
\phi^{\ast}_{1}=\phi^{\ast}_{2}
and
\theta^{\ast}_{1}=\theta^{\ast}_{2}
.

1.
Introduction

A classical topic in complex analysis and related field is to study double integral estimates for Dirichlet type spaces or Besove type spaces.
Recall that the Dirichlet integral of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is

\mathfrak{D}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\frac{f(\zeta)-f(\eta)}{\zeta-\eta}\right|^{2}|d\zeta||d\eta|,

where
\mathbb{T}
is the boundary of the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. By this integral, in 1931 J. Douglas
[
15
]
studied the theory of minimal surfaces, and in 1940 A. Beurling
[
12
]
proved that the Fourier series of a Dirichlet function converges everywhere except on a set of logarithmic capacity zero. Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the space of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. The Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
consists of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)=(1/\pi)dxdy
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. It is known that
every function
f
in
\mathfrak{D}
has nontangential limit denoted by
f(\zeta)
for almost every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
.
For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, it is also well known that
f\in\mathfrak{D}
if and only if
\mathfrak{D}(f)<\infty
.

For
p>1
, the Besov space
B_{p}
is the space of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}d\lambda(z)<\infty,

where

d\lambda(z)=\frac{dA(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}

is the Möbius invariant measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Clearly,
B_{2}
is equal to the Dirichlet space
\mathfrak{D}
.
For
\alpha>-1
and
p>\max\{1,2/(2+\alpha)\}
, in 1988 J. Arazy, S. Fisher and J. Peetre (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 6.4]
and
[
4
, Remark, p. 1026]
) showed that a function
f\in B_{p}
if and only if the following integral

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f\circ\varphi_{a}(z)-f(a)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{p/2}d\lambda(a)

is finite for
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
where
\varphi_{a}(z)=(a-z)/(1-\overline{a}z)
is a Möbius map interchanging the points
0
and
a
. In
[
4
]
, this characterization was used to investigate
the properties of Hankel operators on weighted Bergman spaces. In 1991 K. Zhu
[
34
]
gave another double integral estimates for Besov spaces; that is, if
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, then

f\in B_{p}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(w)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{4}}dA(z)dA(w)<\infty.

Recall that for
p>0
and
\alpha<p/2
, the standard weighted Besov space
B_{p}(\alpha)
consists of functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-1-2\alpha}dA(z)<\infty.

If
p=2
, then
B_{p}(\alpha)
is the Dirichlet type space
\mathfrak{D}_{\alpha}
.
In 1993 R. Rochberg and Z. Wu
[
29
]
obtained a double integral characterization of Dirichlet type spaces
\mathfrak{D}_{\alpha}
and applied this characterization to study Hankel type operators. In 2008 D. Blasi and J. Pau

[
13
]
generalized this characterization from
\mathfrak{D}_{\alpha}
to
B_{p}(\alpha)
by a different method. It is known from
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
that if
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
p>1
,
\sigma,\tau>-1
and

\alpha\leq 1/2
such that
\min(\sigma,\tau)+2\alpha>-1
, then
f\in B_{p}(\alpha)
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(w)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{3+\sigma+\tau+2\alpha}}(1-|w|^{2})^{\sigma}(1-|z|^{2})^{\tau}dA(z)dA(w)<\infty.

This double integral characterization was also used to study Hankel type operators; see
[
13
, Section 4]
. We refer to
[
7
, Section 4]
and
[
31
]
for some recent results associated with double integral estimates for some analytic function spaces. See A. Reijonen
[
28
]
for recent results on Besov type spaces induced by some radial weights.

A function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, integrable over
\mathbb{D}
, is called a weight. If
\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then we say that
\omega
is radial. Suppose
0<p<\infty
and
(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}\omega(z)
is a weight on
\mathbb{D}
. Denote by
B_{p}(\omega)
the Besov type space consisting of those functions
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{B_{p}(\omega)}=|f(0)|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}\omega(z)dA(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

If
\omega(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2\alpha}
, then
B_{p}(\omega)=B_{p}(\alpha)
.

In this paper, for all
0<p<\infty
, we give a complete description of radial weights
\omega
such that an analytic function
f
belongs to
B_{p}(\omega)
if and only if
f
satisfies certain double integral estimates. This result is new even for
B_{p}(\alpha)
when
0<p\leq 1
and
\alpha<p/2
. As applications, we consider geometric descriptions of functions in Besove type space with doubling weights. We also apply these double integral estimates to characterize the boundedness of Hankel type operators from
B_{p}(\omega)
with radial Békollé-Bonami weights to the corresponding nonanalytic version of Besov type spaces, which is also new for
B_{p}(\alpha)
when
1<p<\infty
and
1/2<\alpha<p/2
. The compactness of these Hankel type operators is also investigated.

Throughout this paper, we write
a\lesssim b
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
a\leq Cb
. If
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
, then we write
a\thickapprox b
.

2.
Double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces

This section is devoted to investigate double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces. In particular, for
0<p<\infty
we characterize completely radial weights
\omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}\omega(z)dA(z)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{4+\tau+\sigma}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\tau}(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{\sigma}\omega(\zeta)dA(z)A(\zeta)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, where
\sigma
and
\tau
are real numbers in certain ranges. This result is new even for the space
B_{p}(\alpha)
when
0<p\leq 1
and
\alpha<p/2
.
For
p=2
, the double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces also complete corresponding conclusions in
[
8
, p. 1725]
and
[
17
, p. 210]
, respectively.

2.1.
Littlewood-Paley estimates for weighted Bergman spaces and a Forelli-Rudin type estimate

In this subsection, we recall some Littlewood-Paley estimates for weighted Bergman spaces. We also give an elementary proof of a well-known Forelli-Rudin type estimate. All of these estimates are tools to prove our main theorems in this section.

Suppose
0<p<\infty
and
\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. The Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
consists of complex-valued measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

For
0<p<\infty
and a weight
\omega
in
\mathbb{D}
, the weighed Bergman space
A^{p}_{\omega}
is the space of analytic functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\omega dA)
. If
\omega(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>-1
, then
A^{p}_{\omega}
is the standard weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, it is well known that the growth rate of the following functions are often comparable in some sense:

f(z),(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z),(1-|z|^{2})^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(z),(1-|z|^{2})^{3}f^{\prime\prime\prime}(z),\cdots.

This kind of estimates is usually called Littlewood-Paley estimates.
We refer to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
20
,
22
]
for the study of Littlewood-Paley estimates for
A^{p}_{\omega}
; that is, to characterize weight functions
\omega
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)-f(0)|^{p}\omega(z)dA(z)\thickapprox\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}\omega(z)dA(z)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. When weight
\omega
is radial, this question has been recently solved completely in
[
22
]
.

The pseudo-hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{D}
is defined by

\rho(z,w)=\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|,\ \ z,\ w\in\mathbb{D}.

For
0<r<1
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, denote by

\Delta(a,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\rho(a,z)<r\}

the pseudo-hyperbolic disk of center
a
and radius
r
.

The following Littlewood-Paley estimates for
A^{p}_{\omega}

[
9
]
will be useful in this paper. For a given weight
\omega
, the conditions appeared in the following theorems are easy to verify.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
p>0
and
\omega
is a weight. If there
exist two constants
r\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\omega(\zeta)\leq\omega(z)\leq C\omega(\zeta)

for all
z
and
\zeta
satisfying
\rho(z,\zeta)<r
, then there exists another positive
constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)-f(0)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem B
.

Suppose
p>0
,
\omega
is a weight, and there exist
t_{0}\geq 0

and
s_{0}\in[-1,0)
with the following property: for any
t>t_{0}
and
s>s_{0}
there
is a positive constant
C=C(t,s)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(\zeta)(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{s}\,dA(\zeta)}{|1-z\overline{\zeta}|^{2+s+t}}\leq\frac{C\omega(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{t}}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then there exists another positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)-f(0)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

We recall some well-known estimates as follows (see
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
).

Lemma C
.

Let
\beta
be any real number. Then

\int^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{d\theta}{|1-ze^{-i\theta}|^{1+\beta}}\thickapprox\begin{cases}1&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \beta<0,\\
\log\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \beta=0,\\
\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \beta>0,\end{cases}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Also, suppose
c
is real and
t>-1
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\bar{z}w|^{2+t+c}}dA(w)\thickapprox\begin{cases}1&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c<0,\\
\log\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c=0,\\
\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{c}}&\kern 5.0pt\text{if}\ \ \ c>0,\end{cases}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

The following Forelli-Rudin type estimate
[
19
]
is very useful in the analysis of some function spaces. A rather complicated proof of this estimate was given in
[
32
]
. Later, for
s>-1
,
r>0
,
t>0
, and
t+1<s+2<r
, a simple proof was given in
[
30
, pp. 27-28]
, where the case of
t<s+2\leq t+1<r
is missing.

Lemma D
.

Suppose
s>-1
,
r>0
,
t>0
, and
t<s+2<r
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}|1-\overline{w}\zeta|^{t}}dA(w)\leq C\,\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2+s-r}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{t}}

for all
z,\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.

We present an elementary proof of Lemma
D
here.
For
z
,
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, by a change of variables
w=\varphi_{z}(a)
and Lemma
C
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}}{|1-\overline{w}z|^{r}|1-\overline{w}\zeta|^{t}}dA(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{z}a|^{4+2s-r}|1-\overline{\zeta}\varphi_{z}(a)|^{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{t}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{z}a|^{4+2s-r-t}|1-a\overline{\varphi_{z}(\zeta)}|^{t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{t}}\int_{\{a\in\mathbb{D}:|1-\overline{z}a|\geq|1-a\overline{\varphi_{z}(\zeta)}|\}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{z}a|^{4+2s-r-t}|1-a\overline{\varphi_{z}(\zeta)}|^{t}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{t}}\int_{\{a\in\mathbb{D}:|1-\overline{z}a|\leq|1-a\overline{\varphi_{z}(\zeta)}|\}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{z}a|^{4+2s-r-t}|1-a\overline{\varphi_{z}(\zeta)}|^{t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{t}}\left(\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{a}b|^{4+2s-r}}+\sup_{b\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{s}dA(a)}{|1-\overline{a}b|^{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{s+2-r}}{|1-\overline{z}\zeta|^{t}},

which finishes the proof of Lemma
D
.

2.2.
Weighted Bergman spaces and doubling weights

In this subsection, we recall characterizations of Carleson measures for
A^{p}_{\omega}
induced by doubling weights and also give some estimates related to these weights.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
be the class of radial weights
\omega
on
\mathbb{D}
for which
\hat{\omega}(r)=\int_{r}^{1}\omega(s)ds
admits the doubling property
\hat{\omega}(r)\leq C\hat{\omega}(\frac{1+r}{2})
for all
r\in[0,1)
, where
C=C(\omega)>1
. A weight in
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is usually called a doubling weight. If there exist

K=K(\omega)>1
and
C=C(\omega)>1
such that the radial weight
\omega
satisfies

\hat{\omega}(r)\geq C\hat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1-r}{K}\right),\ \ 0\leq r<1,

then we say that
\omega\in\check{\mathcal{D}}
. The intersection
\hat{\mathcal{D}}\cap\check{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}
.
The classes of these weights arose naturally in the study of some analytic function spaces and related operator theory. For instance, by
[
22
]
, for a radial weight
\omega
, certain Bergman projection
P_{\omega}
is bounded from
L^{\infty}
to the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
if and only if
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
;
P_{\omega}:\ L^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is bonded and onto if and only if
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
; the classes
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mathcal{D}
also characterize completely Littlewood-Paley estimates for weighted Bergman spaces with radial weights. See
[
21
,
23
]
for properties of these weights. Our investigation in this section will guide us to find new and more significance of the class
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
; that is, the weight in
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
describes precisely certain double integral estimate for Besov type spaces.

For a space
X
of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
and
0<p<\infty
, a nonnegative Borel measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is said to be a
p
-Carleson measure for
X
if the identity operator
I_{d}:X\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
is bounded; that is,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}d\mu(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X}

for all
f\in X
. If
I_{d}:X\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
is compact, then we say that
\mu
is a vanishing
p
-Carleson measure for
X
.

For a doubling weight
\omega
and
0<p,q<\infty
, J. Peláez and J. Rättyä
[
25
]
characterized nonnegative Borel measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
such that the differentiation operator of order
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is bounded from
A_{\omega}^{p}
into
L^{q}(\mathbb{D},d\mu)
. In particular, they gave the following result.

Theorem E
.

Suppose
0<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mu
is a nonnegative Borel measure on
\mathbb{D}
. Then
\mu
is a
p
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
if and only if the function

a\mapsto\frac{\mu(S(a))}{\int_{S(a)}wdA},\ \ a\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\},

is essentially bounded, where

S(a)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\left|\frac{\arg z-\arg a}{2\pi}\right|<\frac{1-|a|}{2},\ |z|\geq|a|\right\}

is the Carleson box with vertex at
a
.

The following characterizations of doubling weights can be found in
[
21
]
.

Lemma F
.

Suppose
\omega
is a radial weight. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
;

(ii)

there exists a positive constant
\beta
depending only on
\omega
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\omega}(r)}{(1-r)^{\beta}}\lesssim\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta}}

(2.1)

for all
0\leq r\leq t<1
;

(iii)

there exists a positive constant
\gamma
depending only on
\omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\omega(s)}{(1-s)^{\gamma}}ds\lesssim\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\gamma}}

(2.2)

for all
0\leq t<1
;

(iv)

there exists a nonnegative constant
\lambda
depending only on
\omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(z)dA(z)}{|1-\overline{\xi}z|^{\lambda+1}}\lesssim\frac{\hat{\omega}(\xi)}{(1-|\xi|)^{\lambda}}

(2.3)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
;

(v)

there exists a positive constant
\eta
depending only on
\omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}s^{x}\omega(s)ds\lesssim\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}s^{y}\omega(s)ds

(2.4)

for all
0<x\leq y<\infty
.

Of course, constants
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\lambda
and
\eta
in Lemma
F
are not unique. In fact, if
\beta
satisfies (ii) in Lemma
F
, then any constant bigger than
\beta
also satisfies (ii). The same phenomenon occurs for
\gamma
,
\lambda
and
\eta
.
Now we give the following relation among these parameters. The infimum below is useful in our results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then

\displaystyle\inf\{\beta:\ \beta\ \text{satisfies (ii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\gamma:\ \gamma\ \text{satisfies (iii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\lambda:\ \lambda\ \text{satisfies (iv) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{\eta:\ \eta\ \text{satisfies (v) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}.

Proof.

Checking that proof of Lemma
F

[
21
]
, we get

\{\gamma:\ \gamma\ \text{satisfies (iii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}\subseteq\{\lambda:\ \lambda\ \text{satisfies (iv) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\},

which yields

\displaystyle\inf\{\lambda:\ \lambda\ \text{satisfies (iv) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\{\gamma:\ \gamma\ \text{satisfies (iii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}.

(2.5)

Now suppose
\beta
satisfies (ii) in Lemma
F
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
,
\beta+\epsilon
also satisfies the same property. Hence

\hat{\omega}(0)\lesssim\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta+\epsilon}}

for all
0\leq t<1
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\omega(s)}{(1-s)^{\beta+\epsilon}}ds=

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(0)-\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta+\epsilon}}+(\beta+\epsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\hat{\omega}(s)}{(1-s)^{\beta+\epsilon+1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta+\epsilon}}+(\beta+\epsilon)\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(1-s)^{\epsilon+1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\beta+\epsilon}{\epsilon}\right)\frac{\hat{\omega}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta+\epsilon}},

(2.6)

which means that when
\beta
satisfies (ii) in Lemma
F
,

\beta+\epsilon\in\{\gamma:\ \gamma\ \text{satisfies (iii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

for any
\epsilon>0
. Thus

\displaystyle\inf\{\gamma:\ \gamma\ \text{satisfies (iii) in Lemma \ref{property of doub w}}\}

\displaystyle\leq

Let

\beta+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{x})}{(1-(1-\frac{1}{x}))^{\beta+\varepsilon}}+\int_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{x}}\frac{\omega(s)}{(1-s)^{\beta+\varepsilon}}ds

2.3.
Double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces

In this subsection, we give a complete description of radial weights
\omega
such that double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces hold.

For real parameters
p
and nonnegative functions
\omega
on
\mathbb{D}
, we will write
\omega_{[p]}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{p}\omega(z)
and
dA_{p}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{p}dA(z)
for convenience.
Now we give Theorem
2.2
, one side of double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
which always holds. It is worth mentioning that the weight function
\omega
in the following theorem is not necessarily radial.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
p>0
,
\tau>-1
and
\omega
is a nonnegative function on
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\sigma
be a real number such that
\omega_{[\sigma]}
is a weight. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{4+\tau+\sigma}}\omega(\zeta)dA_{\tau}(z)dA_{\sigma}(\zeta)\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dA_{p-2}(z)

(2.14)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, by a change of variable

, we get

\displaystyle J_{1}(\zeta):

\displaystyle=(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{4+\tau+\sigma}}dA_{\tau}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(u)-f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(0)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}dA_{\tau}(u).

(2.15)

From Proposition 4.5 and Lemma 4.30 in
[
33
]
,

\displaystyle|1-\overline{\zeta}u|\thickapprox|1-\overline{\zeta}\eta|,1-|u|^{2}\thickapprox 1-|\eta|^{2}\thickapprox|1-\overline{u}\eta|,

(2.16)

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
, and all
u
and
\eta
in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
\rho(u,\eta)<1/2
.
The comparison constants in (
2.16
) are independent of
\zeta
,
u
and
\eta
. These facts allow us to apply a Littlewood-Paley estimate for Bergman spaces with nonradial weights
\omega_{\zeta}(u)=(1-|u|^{2})^{\tau}/{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}
(see Theorem
A
and its proof in
[
9
]
). We get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(u)-f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(0)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}dA_{\tau}(u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|(f\circ\varphi_{\zeta})^{\prime}(u)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}dA_{\tau+p}(u),

(2.17)

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
\zeta
and
f
.
Bearing in mind (
2.15
), (
2.16
), (
2.3
), the change of variable with

and the sub-mean value property for

, we deduce

\displaystyle J_{1}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|(f\circ\varphi_{\zeta})^{\prime}(u)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}dA_{\tau+p}(u)

\displaystyle\thickapprox\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{\sigma}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{\sigma+\tau+4}}dA_{\tau+p}(z)

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{p-4}\int_{\Delta(\zeta,1/2)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{p-2}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. This implies (
2.14
). The proof is complete.
∎

The conditions of
\tau
and
\sigma
in Theorem
2.2
are only used to ensure the convergence of the integrals in the left-hand of (
2.14
).

and
{\omega_{[p-2]}}

To understand well the existence of parameters

,

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dA_{p-2}(z)

for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Take

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{np+1}(1-r)^{p-2}\omega(r)dr

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{4+\tau+\sigma}}\omega(\zeta)dA_{\tau}(z)A_{\sigma}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}dA(\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(u)-f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(0)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}u|^{\tau-\sigma}}dA_{\tau}(u)

\displaystyle 2^{\sigma-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}dA(\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(u)-f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(0)|^{p}dA_{\tau}(u)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{2}}dA(\zeta)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(f\circ\varphi_{\zeta}(u))^{\prime}|^{p}dA_{\tau+p}(u)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{\tau}\omega(\zeta)dA(\zeta)}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{2\tau+4}}\right)dA_{\tau+p}(z).

where

B

B_{p}(\omega)

In this section, based on double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)

B_{p}(\omega)
spaces when
0<p<\infty
and
{\omega_{[p-2]}}\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}

For any
z
,
\zeta
in
\mathbb{D}

\beta(z,\zeta)
the distance between
z
and
\zeta

\beta(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1+\rho(z,\zeta)}{1-\rho(z,\zeta)}.

Suppose

D(z,r)
under
f
. Recall that

O_{r}(f)(z)=\sup\{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|:\ \ \zeta\in D(z,r)\}

is the oscillation of
f
at
z
in the Bergman metric. The mean oscillation of
f
at
z
in the Bergman metric is

MO_{r}(f)(z)=\frac{1}{|D(z,r)|}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(\zeta)-\widehat{f_{r}}(z)|dA(\zeta),

where
\widehat{f_{r}}
is the averaging function given by

\widehat{f_{r}}(z)=\frac{1}{|D(z,r)|}\int_{D(z,r)}f(u)dA(u).

Applying Theorem
2.4
, we obtain the following conclusion which generalizes Theorem B in
[
34
]
from the Besov space
B_{p}
,
p>1
, to the Besov type space
B_{p}(\omega)
for
0<p<\infty
and
{\omega_{[p-2]}}\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}

The proof of Theorem B in
[
34
]
used the Hölder inequality. Theorem

below includes the case of

for which the Hölder inequality is invalid.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose

,

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)

2.4

\displaystyle|f(D(z,r))|\leq\int_{D(z,r)}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}dA(\zeta).

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|D(\zeta,r)|}\int_{D(\zeta,r)}|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p}dA(u)

(3.3)

for all

\displaystyle\left[\int_{D(z,r)}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}dA(\zeta)\right]^{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|D(z,r)|^{p/2}\sup\{|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}:\ \ \zeta\in D(z,r)\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1-|z|)^{p-2}\int_{D(z,2r)}|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p}dA(u)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\int_{D(z,2r)}\frac{|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p}(1-|u|^{2})^{\tau+p}(1-|z|^{2})^{x+2}}{|1-\overline{z}u|^{4+\tau+x}}dA(u)

z\in\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left[\int_{D(z,r)}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}dA(\zeta)\right]^{p/2}\omega(z)d\lambda(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p}(1-|u|^{2})^{\tau+p}(1-|z|^{2})^{x+2}}{|1-\overline{z}u|^{4+\tau+x}}dA(u)\omega(z)d\lambda(z)

\displaystyle\thickapprox

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(u)|^{p}(1-|u|^{2})^{\tau+p}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{x}\omega(z)}{|1-\overline{z}u|^{4+\tau+x}}dA(z)dA(u)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

3.2
), we get that the function

belongs to

.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. It follows from
[

f\in B_{p}(\omega)

and
\tau

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f(z)-f(\zeta)|^{p}}{|1-\overline{\zeta}z|^{4+\tau+\sigma}}\omega(\zeta)dA_{\tau}(z)A_{\sigma}(\zeta)\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dA_{p-2}(z)

4.
Hankel type operators related to
B_{p}(\omega)
spaces with radial Békollé-Bonami weights

In this section, applying double integral estimates for Besov type spaces
B_{p}(\omega)

B_{p}(\omega)
with radial Békollé-Bonami weights. This result is new for

For

is the real number with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1

[
11
]
proved that

P_{s}f(z)=(s+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(1-\overline{\zeta}z)^{s+2}}dA_{s}(\zeta)

L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)
to
A^{p}_{\eta}

\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
are not necessarily radial. From Proposition 6 in
[
24
]
, any radial weight in
\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
. See
[
10
,
11
]
for these weights.

For
s>-1
, by the Bergman projection
P_{s}
, it is possible
to define a (small) Hankel type operator
h_{s,f}
on
\mathcal{P}
by

h_{s,f}g=\overline{P_{s}(f\overline{g})},\ \ \ g\in\mathcal{P}.

We refer to
[
27
,
33
]
for more results on Hankel type operators.

Let
p>0
and let
\omega_{[p-2]}
be a weight. Denote by
S_{p}(\omega)
the Sobolev type space of smooth functions

such that

\|u\|_{S_{p}(\omega)}=|u(0)|+\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p-2}\omega(z)dA(z)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Also, if
\omega(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2\alpha}

S_{p}(\omega)
as
S_{p}(\alpha)
.
Clearly,
B_{p}(\omega)
is a subset of all analytic functions in
S_{p}(\omega)
.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}
the class of polynomials on
\mathbb{D}
. If

\mathcal{P}
is dense in
B_{p}(\omega)
(cf.
[
18
]

For
s>-1

\widetilde{P}_{s}
given by

\widetilde{P}_{s}u(z)=u(0)+\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial w}(w)\frac{1-(1-\overline{w}z)^{1+s}}{\overline{w}(1-\overline{w}z)^{1+s}}(1-|w|^{2})^{s}dA(w).

Then one can define a (small) Hankel type operator on
\mathcal{P}
with certain symbol
f
by

h_{f}^{s}g=\overline{\widetilde{P}_{s}(f\overline{g})},\ \ \ g\in\mathcal{P}.

As explained by D. Blasi and J. Pau
[
13
, p. 402]
,
\widetilde{P}_{1-2\alpha}
defines a projection from
S_{p}(\alpha)
to
B_{p}(\alpha)
.
For
f
analytic in
\mathbb{D}
,
the boundedness of
h_{f}^{1-2\alpha}
from
B_{p}(\alpha)
to
S_{p}(\alpha)
was also studied in
[
13
]
. Considering the same topic with weights
\omega
, one should define a Hankel type operator by the projection from

S_{p}(\omega)
to
B_{p}(\omega)
. From this way, the results in Section 2 will not be used in the study. Our purpose of this section is to present how to apply double integral estimates for
B_{p}(\omega)
obtained in Section 2. Thus we still focus on the operator
h_{f}^{s}
in this section. In particular, some special cases of our main result can complete or improve some results before.

4.1.
The boundedness of Hankel type operators from
B_{p}(\omega)
to
S_{p}(\omega)

In this subsection, we investigate the boundedness of
h^{s}_{f}:B_{p}(\omega)\to S_{p}(\omega)
with analytic symbol
f
.

We first recall Proposition 5 in
[
26
]
as follows.

Proposition G
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
and write
\tilde{w}(r)=\hat{\omega}(r)/(1-r)
for all
0\leq r<1
. Then
\omega\in\check{\mathcal{D}}
if and only if
\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\thickapprox\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\tilde{\omega}}}
for all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

Remark
.
In fact, by the proof of the proposition above in
[
26
]
, if
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
, then
\tilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{R}
. Here
\mathcal{R}
is the regular class of weights consisting of all
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\hat{\omega}(r)\thickapprox(1-r)\omega(r),\,\,\,r\in(0,1).

Also, if
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
, then
\hat{\tilde{\omega}}(r)\thickapprox\hat{\omega}(r)
for all
0\leq r<1
. Then it is also clear that

U(\omega)=U(\tilde{\omega})
and
L(\omega)=L(\tilde{\omega})
.

We also need the following result (cf.
[
21
, Lemma 3.1]
).

Lemma H
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then there is real number
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\omega)
such that for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and each
a\in\mathbb{D}
the function
F_{a,p}(z)=\left(\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{1-\overline{a}z}\right)^{(\lambda+1)/p}

is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfies

|F_{a,p}(z)|\thickapprox 1,\ \ z\in S(a),\ \ a\in\mathbb{D},

and

\|F_{a,p}\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\thickapprox\int_{S(a)}\omega dA,\ \ a\in\mathbb{D}.

Now we give the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
-1<s<\infty
, and
\eta
is a radial weight in
\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
. Let

and let

be a bounded operator from
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
to
L_{\eta}^{p}
.
Then

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})|f(a)|<\infty.

Proof.

Bear in mind that any radial weight in
\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
.
By Proposition
G
, we have

\displaystyle\|h_{s,f}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}

\displaystyle=\|P_{s}(f\overline{g})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}

\displaystyle=\|P_{s}(f\overline{g})\|_{A_{\eta}^{p}}

\displaystyle\thickapprox\|P_{s}(f\overline{g})\|_{A_{\tilde{\eta}}^{p}}=\|h_{s,f}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)}

(4.1)

for all
g\in B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
.
For any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, set

g_{a}(z)=\frac{z^{n+1}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{n+1}},\ \ \ z\in\mathbb{D},

where
n
is a positive integer.
By Lemma
H

is large enough,

\displaystyle\|g_{a}\|_{B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})}\lesssim\frac{(1-|a|)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hat{\eta}(a)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(1-|a|)^{n+2}}

(4.2)

for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Note that
h_{s,f}
is bounded from
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
to
L_{\eta}^{p}
, and
P_{s}f=\overline{h_{s,f}1}
. Hence
P_{s}f
belongs to
A^{p}_{\eta}
. Then
P_{s}f=f
. For any fixed
\lambda>-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle f^{(n+1)}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s+1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{s}dA(z)}{(1-a\overline{z})^{s+2}}\right)^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n,s)(s+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\overline{z}^{n+1}f(z)}{(1-a\overline{z})^{n+1}}\frac{1}{(1-a\overline{z})^{2+s}}(1-|z|^{2})^{s}dA(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n,s,\lambda)(s+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\overline{z}^{n+1}f(z)}{(1-a\overline{z})^{n+1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{dA_{\lambda}(w)}{(1-w\overline{z})^{2+s}(1-a\overline{w})^{\lambda+2}}\right)dA_{s}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n,s,\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left((s+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\overline{z}^{n+1}f(z)}{(1-a\overline{z})^{n+1}}\frac{dA_{s}(z)}{(1-w\overline{z})^{2+s}}\right)\frac{dA_{\lambda}(w)}{(1-a\overline{w})^{\lambda+2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(n,s,\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{h_{s,f}(g_{a})(w)}\frac{dA_{\lambda}(w)}{(1-a\overline{w})^{\lambda+2}},

where
C(n,s)
is a constant depending on
n
and
s
, and
C(n,s,\lambda)
is a constant depending on
n
,
s
and
\lambda
.
It follows from Hölder’s inequality, the boundedness of
h_{s,f}
, (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle(1-|a|^{2})^{n+2}|f^{(n+1)}(a)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1-|a|^{2})^{n+2}\|h_{s,f}g_{a}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda}dA(w)}{\tilde{\eta}(w)^{\frac{{p^{\prime}}}{p}}|1-a\overline{w}|^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|h_{s,f}\|_{B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}(1-|a|)^{\frac{1}{p}}{\hat{\eta}}(a)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda}dr}{\tilde{\eta}(r)^{\frac{{p^{\prime}}}{p}}(1-|a|r)^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{{p^{\prime}}}}

(4.3)

for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
.
Since
\tilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{R}
, there exist
-1<\alpha,\beta<\infty
such that

\frac{\tilde{\eta}(t)}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}
and
\frac{\tilde{\eta}(t)}{(1-t)^{\beta}}

are essentially increasing and essentially decreasing respectively (cf.
[
23
, p. 11]
).
Note that
-\frac{p^{\prime}\beta}{p}-2p^{\prime}+2=(\beta+2)(1-p^{\prime})<0
.
If
\lambda
is large enough, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|a|}\frac{(1-r)^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda}dr}{{\tilde{\eta}}(r)^{p^{\prime}/p}(1-|a|r)^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(1-|a|)^{\beta}}{{\tilde{\eta}}(|a|)}\right)^{p^{\prime}/p}\int_{0}^{|a|}\frac{(1-r)^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda-\frac{{p^{\prime}}\beta}{p}}dr}{(1-|a|r)^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(1-|a|)^{\beta}}{{\tilde{\eta}}(|a|)}\right)^{p^{\prime}/p}\int_{0}^{|a|}(1-r)^{-\frac{{p^{\prime}}\beta}{p}-2{p^{\prime}}+1}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|a|)^{-2{p^{\prime}}+2}}{{\tilde{\eta}}(a)^{p^{\prime}/p}},

and

\displaystyle\int_{|a|}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda}dr}{{\tilde{\eta}}(r)^{p^{\prime}/p}(1-|a|r)^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}-1}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(1-|a|)^{\alpha}}{{\tilde{\eta}}(|a|)}\right)^{p^{\prime}/p}\int_{|a|}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{{p^{\prime}}\lambda-\frac{{p^{\prime}}\alpha}{p}}dr}{(1-|a|r)^{(\lambda+2){p^{\prime}}-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|a|)^{-2{p^{\prime}}+2}}{{\tilde{\eta}}(a)^{p^{\prime}/p}}

for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
.
Combining this with (
4.3
), we get

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})^{n+2}|f^{(n+1)}(a)|<\infty,

which is equivalent to

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|a|^{2})|f(a)|<\infty.

The proof is complete.
∎

The following theorem is the main result in this section and the double integral estimates for Besov type spaces will be used in its proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
1<p<\infty
,
-1<s<\infty
,
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
\eta\in\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
is a radial weight. Let
\eta,p,s
satisfy further one of the following conditions:

(a)

U(\eta)<p-1
,
p\leq 2
, and

U(\eta)-\frac{L(\eta)}{p-1}<p-1+\frac{sp}{p-1}
;

(b)

U(\eta)<p-1
,
p\geq 2
, and
U(\eta)-L(\eta)<ps+1
;

(c)

p-1\leq U(\eta)<ps+p
,
s>0
, and
L(\eta)>p-1-ps
.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

h_{s,f}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)
is a bounded operator;

(ii)

h_{F}^{s}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to S_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
is a bounded operator, where
F
is in
H(\mathbb{D})
satisfying
F^{\prime}=f
on
\mathbb{D}
;

(iii)

d\mu(z)=|f(z)|^{p}\eta(z)dA(z)
is a
p
-Carleson measure for
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
. For
g\in\mathcal{P}
, it is easy to check that for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{\partial h_{F}^{s}g}{\partial z}(z)=0

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{F}^{s}g}{\partial\overline{z}}(z)

\displaystyle=(s+1)\overline{\int_{\mathbb{D}}\overline{g(w)}f(w)\frac{(1-|w|^{2})^{s}}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{s+2}}dA(w)}

\displaystyle=h_{s,f}g(z).

Hence

|\nabla h_{F}^{s}g(z)|\thickapprox|h_{s,f}g(z)|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then the equivalence between (i) and (ii) follows.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Suppose
\mu
is a
p
-Carleson measure for
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
. Since
\eta\in\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
, the Bergman projection
P_{s}:L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)\to A_{\eta}^{p}
is bounded. Then, for any
g\in B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
, we get

\displaystyle\|h_{s,f}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|P_{s}(f\overline{g})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}^{p}\lesssim\|fg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}^{p}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})}^{p}.

Thus
h_{s,f}
is bounded.

(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
.
Suppose
h_{s,f}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)
is bounded.
Note that any radial weight in
\mathcal{B}_{p,s}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}
. By (
4.1
), Proposition
G
and the remark after it, we have

\tilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{R},\,\,\,L(\tilde{\eta})=L(\eta),\,\,\,U(\tilde{\eta})=U(\eta),

and

\|h_{s,f}\|_{B_{p}(\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)}\thickapprox\|h_{s,f}\|_{B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}.

Thus
h_{s,f}:\ B_{p}(\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)
is also a bounded operator.
Here,

\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{2-p}\tilde{\eta}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{2-p}\frac{\hat{\eta}(z)}{1-|z|},\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

We claim that

\|f\overline{g}-\overline{h_{s,f}g}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)}\lesssim\|g\|_{B_{p}(\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]})}

for all
g\in B_{p}(\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]})
. By this claim, the boundedness of
h_{s,f}:B_{p}(\tilde{\eta}_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\tilde{\eta}dA)
, and Proposition
G
, we see that

\mu
is a
p
-Carleson measure for
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
. Thus, it suffices to prove the claim for case (a), case (b) and case (c) respectively.
As explained in Lemma
4.1
,
P_{s}f=f
. This, together with (i), yields

\displaystyle f(z)\overline{g(z)}-\overline{h_{s,f}g(z)}=(s+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{f(w)\overline{(g(z)-g(w))}}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{2+s}}dA_{s}(w).

(4.4)

for any

4.2.
The compactness of Hankel type operators from
B_{p}(\omega)
to
S_{p}(\omega)

This subsection is devoted to consider the compactness of Hankel type operators corresponding to Theorem
4.2
. This result is also new for
\omega(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{1-2\alpha}
.

A linear operator from one normed linear space to another normed linear space is compact if the image of the unit ball under the operator has compact closure.

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
-1<s<\infty
and
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Suppose
\eta
is a weight and there
exist two constants
r\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\eta(\zeta)\leq\eta(z)\leq C\eta(\zeta)

for all
z
and
\zeta
satisfying
\rho(z,\zeta)<r
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

h_{s,f}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)
is a compact operator if and only if

\|h_{s,f}g_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta dA)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
whenever
\{g_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
that
converges to 0 uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
;

(ii)

I_{d}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},|f(z)|^{p}\eta(z)dA(z))
is a compact operator if and only if
\|g_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},|f(z)|^{p}\eta(z)dA(z))}\rightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
whenever
\{g_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
that converges to 0 uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

For any
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, the assumes on
\eta
and the subharmonicity of
|g|^{p}
yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Delta(z,r)}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}\eta(z)\int_{\Delta(z,r)}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{p}dA(\zeta)

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\eta(z)(1-|z|)^{2}

(4.33)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
C_{1}
is a positive constant independent of
z
and
g
. Clearly,
g(z)=\int_{0}^{z}g^{\prime}(\zeta)d\zeta+g(0)
. Hence the point evaluations on
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
are bounded functionals. Similarly,
the point evaluations on
A^{p}_{\eta}
are also bounded. For
z\in\mathbb{D}
satisfying
f(z)\not=0
, the point evaluation induced by
z
on
A^{p}_{|f|^{p}\eta}
is also bounded. Next, the proof of this lemma is quite standard (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 3.11]
). We omit it.
∎

We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose
\eta\in\check{\mathcal{D}}
and there
exist two constants

and

such that

C^{-1}\eta(\zeta)\leq\eta(z)\leq C\eta(\zeta)

for all

and

satisfying

. Then
I_{d}:B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\eta(z)dA(z))
is a compact operator.

Proof.

Let
\{g_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
B_{p}(\eta_{[2-p]})
that converges to 0 uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. Similar to (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\infty>\sup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)dA(\zeta)\gtrsim\sup_{n}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g_{n}^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\eta(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{2};

that is,

\displaystyle|g_{n}^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\eta(z)\lesssim G(z)

for all

and all

, where

. For

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{p}dA(\zeta)=0.

Note that
\eta\in\check{\mathcal{D}}
. By
[

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)dA(\zeta)\lesssim|g_{n}(0)|^{p}+\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)(1-|\zeta|^{2})^{p}dA(\zeta)

for all

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g_{n}(\zeta)|^{p}\eta(\zeta)dA(\zeta)=0.

By Lemma
4.6
, the proof is complete.
∎

Now we give the main result in this subsection.

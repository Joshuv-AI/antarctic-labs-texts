Mean Field Optimization Problem Regularized by Fisher Information
Julien CLAISSE, Giovanni CONFORTI, Zhenjie REN, Songbo WANG

Abstract
Recently there is a rising interest in the research of mean field optimization, in particular because of its role in analyzing the training of neural networks. In this paper by adding the Fisher Information as the regularizer, we relate the regularized mean field optimization problem to a so-called mean field Schrödinger (MFS for short) dynamics. We develop an energy-dissipation method to show that the marginal distributions of the MFS dynamics converge exponentially quickly towards the unique minimizer of the regularized optimization problem. Remarkably, the MFS dynamics is proved to be a gradient flow on the probability measure space with respect to the relative entropy. Finally we propose a Monte Carlo method to sample the marginal distributions of the MFS dynamics.



1
Introduction

Recently the mean field optimization problem, namely

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}}{\mathfrak{F}}(p),\quad\mbox{for a function ${\mathfrak{F}}:{\cal P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$, where ${\cal P}$ is a set of probability measures},

attracts increasing attention, in particular because of its role in analysing the training of artificial neural networks. The Universal Representation Theorem (see e.g.
[
11
]
) ensures that a given continous function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
can be approximated by the parametric form:

f(x)\approx\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\varphi(a_{i}\cdot x+b_{i}),\quad\mbox{with $c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\penalty\ a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\penalty\ b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}$ for $1\leq i\leq N$},

where
\varphi
is a fixed non-constant, bounded, continuous activation function. This particular parametrization is called a two-layer neural network (with one hidden layer). In order to train the optimal parameters, one need to solve the optimization problem:

\inf_{(c_{i},a_{i},b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}L\left(f(x_{j}),\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\varphi(a_{i}\cdot x_{j}+b_{i})\right),

where
L:(y,z)\mapsto L(y,z)
is a loss function, typically convex in
z
. Here we face an overparametrized, non-convex optimization, and have no theory for an efficient solution. However it has been recently observed (see e.g.
[
18
,
12
,
5
,
14
]
) that by lifting the optimization problem to the space of probability measures, namely

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}L\Big(f(x_{j}),\mathbb{E}^{p}[C\varphi(A\cdot x+B)]\Big),

with random variables
(C,A,B)
taking values in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
following the distribution
p
, one makes the optimization convex (the function
F:p\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{M}L\big(f(x_{j}),\mathbb{E}^{p}[C\varphi(A\cdot x+B)]\big)
is convex), and has extensive tools to find the minimizers.

Unlike in
[
5
]
where the authors address the mean field optimization directly, in
[
18
,
12
]
the authors add the entropy regularizer
H(p):=\int p(x)\log p(x)dx
, that is, they aim at solving the regularized optimization problem:

(1.1)

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}}F(p)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}H(p).

Recall the definition of the linear derivative
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
and the intrinsic derivative
D_{p}F
(see
2.2
below) for functions on the space of probability measures. In
[
12
]
the authors introduce the mean field Langevin (MFL for short) dynamics:

dX_{t}=-D_{p}F(p_{t},X_{t})dt+\sigma dW_{t},

where
p_{t}={\rm Law}(X_{t})
and
W
is a standard Brownian motion, and prove that the marginal laws
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of the MFL dynamics converge towards the minimizer of the entropic regularization (
1.1
). In the following works
[
19
,
4
]
it has been shown that the convergence is exponentially quick.

In this paper we try to look into the mean field optimization problem from another perspective, by adding the Fisher information
I(p):=\int|\nabla\log p(x)|^{2}p(x)dx
instead of the entropy as the regularizer, namely solving the regularized optimization

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}}{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p),\quad{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p):=F(p)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}I(p).

By convexity and calculus of variation (see
4.3
), it is not hard to see that
p^{*}\in\argmin_{p\in{\cal P}}{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p)
if

(1.2)

\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}(p^{*},x):=\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p^{*},x\right)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left(2\Delta\log p^{*}+\left|\nabla\log p^{*}\right|^{2}\right)={\rm constant}.

We shall introduce the mean field Schrödinger (MFS for short) dynamics:

\partial_{t}p_{t}=-\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}(p_{t},\cdot)p_{t},

prove its wellposedness and show that its marginal distributions
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
converges (uniformly) towards the minimizer of the free energy function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
. One crucial observation is that the free energy function decays along the MFS dynamics:

\frac{d{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{t})}{dt}=-\int\left|\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}(p_{t},x)\right|^{2}p_{t}(dx).

In order to prove it rigorously, we develop a probabilistic argument (coupling of diffusions) to estimate
(\nabla\log p_{t},\nabla^{2}\log p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Remarkably, the estimate we obtain is uniform in time. Using the energy dissipation we can show that
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
converges exponentially quickly with help of the convexity of
F
and the Poincaré inequality. Another main contribution of this paper is to show that the MFS dynamics is a gradient flow of the free energy function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
on the space of probability measures, provided that the ‘distance’ between the probability measures is measured by relative entropy. Finally it is noteworthy that MFS dynamics is numerically implementable, and we shall briefly propose a Monte Carlo simulation method.

Related works.

Assume
F
to be linear, i.e.
F(p):=\int f(x)p(dx)
with a real potential function
f
and denote the wave function by
\psi:=\sqrt{p}
. Then the function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
reduces to the conventional energy function in quantum mechanics, composed of the potential energy
\langle\psi,f\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}
and the kinetic energy
\sigma^{2}\langle\nabla\psi,\nabla\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}.
Meanwhile, the MFS dynamics is reduced to the semigroup generated by the Schrödinger operator:

(1.3)

\partial_{t}\psi=-\mathcal{H}\psi,\quad\text{with }\mathcal{H}:=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta+\frac{1}{2}f.

The properties of the classical Schrödinger operator, including its longtime behavior, have been extensively studied in the literature, see e.g. the monographs
[
20
,
16
]
.
There are also profound studies in cases where
F
is nonlinear, notably the density functional theory
[
9
,
10
]
. However, to our knowledge there is no literature dedicated to the category of convex potential
F:{\cal P}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and studying the longtime behavior of such nonlinear Schrödinger operator by exploiting the convexity. In addition, the probabilistic nature of our arguments seems novel.

Using the change of variable:
u:=-\log p^{*}
, the first order equation (
1.2
) can be rewritten as

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta u-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p^{*},x)=\mbox{\rm constant}.

So the function
u
solves an ergodic Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, and its gradient
\nabla u
is the optimal control for the ergodic stochastic control problem:

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sup_{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\alpha_{t}|^{2}+\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p^{*},X^{\alpha}_{t})\right)dt\right],

where

\displaystyle dX^{\alpha}_{t}=\alpha_{t}dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t}.

Further note that the probability
p^{*}=e^{-u}
coincides with the invariant measure of the optimal controlled diffusion:
dX^{*}_{t}=-\nabla u(X^{*}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2}dW_{t}
, so that
p^{*}
is the Nash equilibrium of the corresponding ergodic mean field game. For more details on the ergodic mean field game, we refer to the seminal paper
[
15
]
, and for more general mean field games we refer to the recent monographs
[
2
,
3
]
. Our convergence result of the MFS dynamics
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
towards
p^{*}
offers an approximation to the equilibrium of the ergodic mean field game.

Our result on the gradient flow, as far as we know, is new to the literature. It is well known to the community of computational physics that the normalized solution
(\psi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to the imaginary time Schrödinger equation (
1.3
) is the gradient flow of the free energy
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
on the
L^{2}
-unit ball. On the other hand, in
[
21
]
the authors discuss the (linear) optimization problem without Fisher information regularizer, and formally show that the dynamics,

\partial_{t}p=-fp,

is the gradient flow of the potential functional
\int fdp
on the space of probability measures provided that the distance between the measures are measured by the relative entropy. Inspired by these works, we prove in the current paper that the solution to the variational problem:

p^{h}_{i+1}:=\argmin_{p\in{\cal P}}\left\{{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p)+h^{-1}H(p|p^{h}_{i})\right\},\quad\mbox{for $h>0,\penalty\ i\geq 0$},

converges to the continuous-time flow of the MFS dynamics as
h\rightarrow 0
. This result can be viewed as a counterpart of seminal paper
[
13
]
on the Wasserstein-
2
gradient flow.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we formulate the problem and state the main results of the paper. The proofs are postponed to the subsequent sections. In
Section

3
, we show that the MFS dynamic is well-defined and admits an important decomposition as the exponential of a sum of a convex and a Lipschitz function. Then we study the long time behavior of this dynamic in
Section

4
and we prove that it converges exponentially fast to the unique minimizer of the mean field optimization problem regularized by Fisher information. Finally we establish in
Section

5
that the MFS dynamic corresponds to the gradient flow with respect to the relative entropy. Some technical results including a refined reflection coupling result are also gathered in Appendix.

2
Main Results

2.1
Free Energy with Fisher Information

Denote by
{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of all probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite second moments, endowed with
{\cal W}_{2}
the Wasserstein distance of order
2
.
We focus on the probability measures admitting densities, and denote the density of
p\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
still by
p:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
if it exists. In particular we are interested in the probability measures of density satifying:

{\cal P}_{H}:=\left\{p\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\penalty\ \sqrt{p}\in H^{1}\right\}.

In this paper we study a regularized mean field optimization problem, namely, given a potential function
F:{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we aim at solving

(2.1)

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}_{H}}\penalty\ {\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p),\quad\mbox{with}\quad{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p):=F(p)+\sigma^{2}I(p),

where
\sigma>0
and
I
is the Fisher information defined by

(2.2)

I(p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{p}(x)|^{2}dx.

In the literature,
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
is called the Ginzburg–Landau energy function with temperature
\sigma
. Note that for
p\in{\cal P}_{H}
and
p>0,
it holds

4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{p}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\nabla\log p(x)\right|^{2}p(x)dx.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the potential function
F
is smooth, convex and coercive as stated in the following assumption.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a function
F:{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
{\cal C}^{1}
if there exist
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}:{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
continuous with quadratic growth in the second variable such that for all
p,q\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

F(q)-F(p)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\big(tq+(1-t)p,x\big)(q-p)(dx)dt.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
F\in{\cal C}^{1}
is
{\cal W}_{2}
-continuous and
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
is defined up to constant. We call
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
the linear derivative and we may further define the intrinsic derivative
D_{p}F(p,x):=\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p,x)
.

Assumption 2.3
.

Assume that
F
is
{\cal C}^{1},
convex and

F(p)\geq\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}p(dx)\quad\mbox{for some $\lambda>0$.}

The following proposition states that the bias caused by the regularizer vanishes as the temperature
\sigma\rightarrow 0
. It ensures that the Fisher information is efficient as regularizer in this mean field optimization problem.

Proposition 2.4
.

It holds

\lim_{\sigma\rightarrow 0}\left(\inf_{p\in{\cal P}_{H}}\penalty\ {\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p)\right)=\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}\penalty\ F(p).

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0,
let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
be such that
F\left(p\right)<\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}F\left(p\right)+\varepsilon
.
By truncation and mollification, define
p_{K,\delta}:=p_{K}*\varphi_{\delta}
where
p_{K}:=\frac{p\mathbbm{1}_{|x|\leq K}}{p(|x|\leq K)}
and
\varphi_{\delta}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi\delta)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\exp(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2\delta})
.
It is clear that
p_{K,\delta}
converges to
p
in
{\cal W}_{2}
as
K\to\infty
and
\delta\to 0.
Additionally, one easily checks by direct computation that
I(p_{K,\delta})<+\infty.

By
{\cal W}_{2}
–continuity of
F,
we deduce by choosing
K
large and
\delta
small enough that

\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{H}}\mathfrak{F}^{\sigma}\left(p\right)\leq F\left(p_{K,\delta}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}I\left(p_{K,\delta}\right)\leq F\left(p\right)+\varepsilon+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}I\left(p_{K,\delta}\right)\leq\inf_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{2}}F\left(p\right)+2\varepsilon+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}I\left(p_{K,\delta}\right).

We conclude by taking the limit
\sigma\to 0
.
∎

For the gradient flow analysis of
Section

2.3
below, we shall actually consider a slightly more general mean field optimization problem.
Namely, we aim at minimizing the following generalized free energy function: for all
p\in{\cal P}_{H},

(2.3)

{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p):=F(p)+\sigma^{2}I(p)+\gamma H(p),

where
\gamma\geq 0
and
H
is the entropy defined as

H(p):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(x)\log p(x)dx.

By considering the limit of the rate of change
\frac{{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p+t(q-p))-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p)}{t}
as
t\to 0,
a formal calculus leads to define by abuse of notation

(2.4)

\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p,\cdot)\penalty\ :=\penalty\ \frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p,\cdot\right)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\log p-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla\log p\right|^{2}+\gamma\log p-\lambda(p),

where
\lambda(p)\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that

(2.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p,x)p(x)dx=0.

The details of this calculation can be found within the proof of
4.3
below.
Note also that equivalent formulas for
\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}
can be obtained by observing that

\Delta\log p+\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\log p\right|^{2}=\frac{\Delta p}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\left|\nabla p\right|^{2}}{p^{2}}=2\frac{\Delta\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{p}}.

2.2
Mean Field Schrödinger Dynamics

Given the definition in
eq.

2.4
, we will consider the following generalized mean field Schrödinger (MFS for short) dynamics

\partial_{t}p_{t}=-\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},\cdot\right)p_{t}.

Thanks to the normalization in
eq.

2.5
, the mass of
p_{t}
is conserved to
1
.
Writing the functional derivative explicitly, we have the following dynamics

(2.6)

\partial_{t}p_{t}=-\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},\cdot\right)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\log p_{t}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla\log p_{t}\right|^{2}+\gamma\log p_{t}-\lambda_{t}\right)p_{t}

where
p_{t}=p\left(t,\cdot\right)
and
\lambda_{t}=\lambda(p_{t})
satisfies

\lambda_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},x\right)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\log p_{t}(x)-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla\log p_{t}(x)\right|^{2}+\gamma\log p_{t}(x)\right)p_{t}(x)dx.

In particular, the important case
\gamma=0
is called the MFS dynamics, namely,

(2.7)

\partial_{t}p_{t}=-\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},\cdot\right)p_{t}.

Intuitively the generalized MFS dynamics follows the direction of steepest descent as it moves in the opposite direction of the derivative
\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}.

To ensure that it is indeed converging towards a minimizer of
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma},

the crucial assumption in this paper is that the derivative
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
decomposes into the sum of a convex potential and a Lipschitz perturbation as stated below.

Assumption 2.5
.

The linear derivative admits the decomposition
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p,x\right)=g\left(x\right)+G\left(p,x\right)
where
g
and
G(p,\cdot)
are
C^{2}
such that

(i)

g
is
\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}
–convex and has bounded Hessian, i.e.,

\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}I_{d}\leq\nabla^{2}g\leq\bar{\kappa}I_{d},\quad\mbox{for some $\bar{\kappa}\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}$}.

(ii)

G
is
{\cal W}_{1}
–continuous in
p
and Lipschitz continuous in
x
, i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,p\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

|G(p,x)-G(p,y)|\leq L_{G}|x-y|.

(iii)

\nabla G
is Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,p,q\in{\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

|\nabla G(p,x)-\nabla G(q,y)|\leq L_{G}\left(|x-y|+{\cal W}_{1}(p,q)\right).

Assumption 2.6
.

The initial distribution admits the decomposition
p_{0}(x)=e^{-(v_{0}(x)+w_{0}(x))}
where
v_{0}
and
w_{0}
are
C^{1}
such that

(i)

v_{0}
is
\underaccent{\bar}{\eta}_{0}
–convex and
\nabla v_{0}
is Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

|\nabla v_{0}(x)-\nabla v_{0}(y)|\leq\bar{\eta}_{0}|x-y|,\quad\left(\nabla v_{0}(x)-\nabla v_{0}(y)\right)\cdot(x-y)\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\eta}_{0}|x-y|^{2}.

(ii)

w_{0}
and
\nabla w_{0}
are both Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

|w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y)|+|\nabla w_{0}(x)-\nabla w_{0}(y)|\leq L_{0}|x-y|.

In the sequel, we assume that Assumptions
2.3
,
2.5
and
2.6
hold. First we show that the generalized MFS dynamic is well-defined and that it decomposes as the exponential of a sum of a convex and a Lipschitz function. The proof is postponed to
Section

3.2
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Under the assumptions above, the generalized MFS dynamics
eq.

2.6
admits a unique positive classical solution
p\in C^{3}(Q)\cap C(\bar{Q}).

In addition, it admits the decomposition
p_{t}=e^{-(v_{t}+w_{t})}
where there exist
\underaccent{\bar}{\eta},\bar{\eta},L>0,
such that

(2.8)

\underaccent{\bar}{\eta}I_{d}\leq\nabla^{2}v_{t}\leq\bar{\eta}I_{d},\qquad\|\nabla w_{t}\|_{\infty}\vee\|\nabla^{2}w_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq L,\qquad\forall\,t>0.

Then we study the long-time behaviour of the generalized MFS dynamics and establish convergence toward the unique minimizer of the generalized free energy function. The proof is postponed to
Section

4.3
. It essentially relies on energy dissipation which can be derived formally as follows:

\frac{d}{dt}{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p_{t},x)\partial_{t}p_{t}(x)dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p_{t},x)\right|^{2}p_{t}(x)dx,

See
4.4
below for a proof.
It follows that the generalized free energy monotonously decreases along the generalized MFS dynamics (
2.6
). Intuitively, the dissipation of energy only stops at the moment
\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p^{*},\cdot)=0
. Since
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}
is (strictly) convex, it is a sufficient condition for
p^{*}
to be the minimizer, see
4.3
below.

Theorem 2.8
.

Under the assumptions above, the solution
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to
eq.

2.6
converges uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
p^{*}
, the unique minimizer of
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}
in
{\cal P}_{H}
.
In addition, the optimizer
p^{*}
satisfies
eq.

2.8
and it is a stationary solution to
eq.

2.6
, i.e.,

(2.9)

\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p^{*},\cdot)=0.

Remark 2.9
.

By
4.7
below, the family of distributions
(p_{t})_{t\geq 0}
admits uniform Gaussian bounds and thus it also converges to
p^{*}
for the

–norm or the
{\cal W}_{p}
–distance for any
p\geq 1.

Remark 2.10
.

In case that the function
p\mapsto F(p)
is linear, i.e.,
F(p)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)p(dx)
with some potential
f
, the function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
is the classical energy function in quantum mechanics composed of the potential energy
F
and the kinetic one
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{p}(x)|^{2}dx
. Let
p^{*}
be the minimizer of
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
, and denote by
\psi^{*}:=\sqrt{p^{*}}
the corresponding wave function.
Then the first order equation (
2.9
) reads

-\sigma^{2}\Delta\psi^{*}+f\psi^{*}=c\psi^{*},\quad\mbox{with}\quad c={\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})=\min_{p\in{\cal P}_{H}}{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p).

It is well known that
c
is the smallest eigenvalue of the Schrödinger operator
-\sigma^{2}\Delta+f
and that
\psi^{*}
is the ground state of the quantum system.

Further we shall prove that the convergence for the MFS dynamics (with
\gamma=0
) is exponentially quick. See
Section

4.4
below for a proof. As a byproduct, we establish a functional inequality in
4.9
which may carry independent interest.

Theorem 2.11
.

There exists a constant
c(\underaccent{\bar}{\eta},\bar{\eta},L,d,\sigma)>0
such that

(2.10)

{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{t})-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})\leq e^{-ct}({\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{0})-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})).

Moreover, it holds

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}I(p_{t}|p^{*})\leq e^{-ct}({\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{0})-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})),

where
I(p_{t}|p^{*}):=\int p_{t}|\nabla\log(p_{t}/p^{*})|^{2}
is the relative Fisher information.

2.3
Gradient Flow with Relative Entropy

In this paper, we shall further investigate the gradient flow of the free energy function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}
with respect to the relative entropy.
First, given
h>0
and a distribution
\tilde{p}
satisfying
2.6
, consider the variational problem:

(2.11)

\inf_{p\in{\cal P}_{H}}\left\{{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p)+h^{-1}H(p|\tilde{p})\right\}.

where
H(p|\tilde{p}):=\int p\log(p/\tilde{p})
is the relative entropy.
In view of
2.6
, we have the decomposition
\tilde{p}=e^{-\tilde{u}}
with
\tilde{u}=\tilde{v}+\tilde{w}
. Denoting by

\tilde{F}(p):=F(p)+h^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{u}(x)p(dx)

the new potential function, we may rewrite the objective function in the optimization (
2.11
) in the form of the generalized free energy function (
2.3
),
i.e.

\tilde{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,h^{-1}}(p)=\tilde{F}(p)+\sigma^{2}I(p)+h^{-1}H(p).

Moreover, the new potential function
\tilde{F}
still satisfies
2.5
with
\tilde{g}=g+h^{-1}\tilde{v}
and
\tilde{G}=G+h^{-1}\tilde{w}
.
Therefore, the following result is a straightforward consequence of
2.8
.

Corollary 2.12
.

If
\tilde{p}
satisfies
2.6
, the minimization problem (
2.11
) admits a unique minimizer
p^{*}\in{\cal P}_{H}
still satisfying
2.6
(with different coefficients) and it satisfies the first order condition

\frac{\delta\tilde{{\mathfrak{F}}}^{\sigma,h^{-1}}}{\delta p}(p^{*},\cdot)=0.

Now given
p^{h}_{0}:=p_{0}
satisfying
2.6
, we may define a sequence of probability measures using the variational problem (
2.11
):

(2.12)

p^{h}_{i}:=\argmin_{p\in{\cal P}_{H}}\left\{{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p)+h^{-1}H(p|p^{h}_{i-1})\right\},\quad\mbox{for $i\geq 1$}.

It corresponds to the so–called
minimizing movement scheme
in the optimal transport literature.
According to
2.12
, the minimizer
p^{h}_{i}
is well-defined and it satisfies the first order condition:

(2.13)

\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}\left(p^{h}_{i},\cdot\right)+h^{-1}(\log p^{h}_{i}-\log p^{h}_{i-1})=\int h^{-1}(\log p^{h}_{i}-\log p^{h}_{i-1})p^{h}_{i}.

Thus we expect as
h\to 0
that the minimizing movement scheme
p^{h}
converges to the corresponding gradient flow
p
satisfying

\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},\cdot\right)+\partial_{t}\log p_{t}=0,

which corresponds to the MFS dynamics (
2.7
).

This result is proved rigorously in
Section

5.3
below. By slightly abusing the notations, define the continuous-time flow of probability measures:

p^{h}_{t}:=p^{h}_{i},\quad\mbox{for $t\in[hi,h(i+1))$}.

Theorem 2.13
.

The sequence of functions

(p^{h})_{h>0}
converges, uniformly on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for any
T>0,
to
p
the MFS dynamics (
2.7
).

Remark 2.14
.

In view of
5.1
below, the family of distributions
(p^{h})_{h>0}
admits uniform Gaussian bounds and thus we also have for any
p\geq 1,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big\|p_{t}^{h}-p_{t}\big\|_{L^{p}}\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}0,\qquad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\cal W}_{p}\big(p_{t}^{h},p_{t}\big)\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}0.

2.4
Numerical Simulation

In this section we shall briefly report how to sample
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{i}_{t}}
to approximate the probability law
p_{t}
in the MFS dynamics (
2.7
), without pursuing mathematical rigorism.

Observe first that the MFS dynamics (
2.7
) can be rewritten as

\partial_{t}p_{t}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta p_{t}-\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p_{t},\cdot\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla\log p_{t}\right|^{2}-\lambda_{t}\right)p_{t}.

This can viewed as the Fokker–Planck equation describing the marginal distribution of a Brownian motion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
killed at rate
\eta(t,x):=\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t},x)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla\log p_{t}(x)\right|^{2}
conditionned on not being killed.
In other words,
the particle
X
moves freely in the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a Brownian motion
(\sigma W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
before it gets killed with conditional probability

\mathbb{P}\left(\penalty\ \mbox{X gets killed in}\penalty\ [t,t+\Delta t]\penalty\ |\penalty\ X_{t}\right)\approx\eta(t,X_{t})\Delta t,\quad\mbox{for small $\Delta t$}.

Meanwhile the killed particle gets reborn instantaneously according to the distribution
p_{t}
. This interpretation of the MFS dynamics offers an insight on how to sample the marginal law
p_{t}.
However, in order to evaluate the death rate
\eta(t,X_{t}),
one needs to evaluate
|\nabla\log p_{t}|^{2}
, which can be hard if not impossible in practice.
This difficulty forces us to find a more sophisticated way to sample
p_{t}
.

Now observe that
\psi_{t}:=\sqrt{p_{t}}
solves the PDE:

(2.14)

\partial_{t}\psi_{t}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\psi_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t},\cdot)-\lambda_{t}\right)\psi_{t}.

Then introduce two scaling of
\psi_{t}
, namely,
\bar{\psi}_{t}:=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{s}\,ds}\psi_{t}
and
\hat{\psi}_{t}:=(\int\psi_{t})^{-1}\psi_{t}
so that

\partial_{t}\bar{\psi}_{t}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\bar{\psi}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t},\cdot)\bar{\psi}_{t},\qquad\partial_{t}\hat{\psi}_{t}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta\hat{\psi}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t},\cdot)-\hat{\lambda}_{t}\right)\hat{\psi}_{t},

where the constant
\hat{\lambda}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that
\hat{\psi}_{t}
is a probability density. Observe that:

•

By the Feynman–Kac formula, the function
\bar{\psi}
has the probabilistic representation:

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\Big(-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t-s},x+\sigma W_{s})ds\Big)\psi_{0}(x+\sigma W_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\exp\Big(-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t-s},x+\sigma W^{j}_{s})ds\Big)\psi_{0}(x+\sigma W^{j}_{t}),

where the latter is the standard Monte Carlo approximation of the expectation.

•

The probability law
\hat{\psi}
is the marginal distribution of a Brownian motion killed at rate
\eta(t,x):=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{t},x)
conditioned on not being killed.
It can be sampled by simulating a large number
(\hat{X}^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
of independent Brownian particles killed at rate
\eta
which upon dying are instantaneously reborn by duplicating one of the living particles.

•

Eventually, the distribution
p_{t}
can be approximately sampled as the following weighted empirical measure

p_{t}=\frac{\bar{\psi}_{t}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\psi}_{t}(x)\hat{\psi}_{t}(x)dx}\hat{\psi}_{t}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{\psi}(t,\hat{X}^{i}_{t})}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bar{\psi}(t,\hat{X}^{k}_{t})}\delta_{\hat{X}^{i}_{t}}.

Remark 2.15
.

In particular, in view of
2.10
, the Monte Carlo method above offers an efficient way to sample the ground state of a high dimensional quantum system. To our knowledge there is little discussion on similar numerical schemes in the literature.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

3.3

Proof.

(i). We first show that the SDE (

) below admits a unique strong solution. Define
\psi(t,x):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}u(t,x)}.
By
A.2
in appendix, we have

(3.6)

\psi(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{t-s},x+\sigma W_{s})-\gamma u(t-s,x+\sigma W_{s})\big)ds}\psi_{0}(x+\sigma W_{t})\right].

Now consider the continuous paths space
C([0,T])
as the canonical space. Denote by
\bar{\mathbb{F}}:=(\bar{\cal F}_{t})_{t\leq T}
the canonical filtration and
\bar{X}
the canonical process. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the probability measure such that

is a
\mathbb{P}

We may define an equivalent probability measure

on the canonical space via

(3.7)

\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}\Big|_{\bar{\cal F}_{T}}=\Lambda_{T}:=\frac{e^{-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-s},\bar{X}_{s})-\gamma u(t-s,\bar{X}_{s})\big)dt}\psi_{0}(\bar{X}_{T})}{\psi(T,x)}.

By Itô’s formula, we may identify that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Lambda_{T}\,|\,\bar{\cal F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-s},\bar{X}_{s})-\gamma u(t-s,\bar{X}_{s})\big)dt}\psi(T-t,\bar{X}_{t})}{\psi(T,x)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\nabla u(t-s,\bar{X}_{s})\cdot d\bar{X}_{s}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8}|\nabla u(t-s,\bar{X}_{s})|^{2}ds\right).

Using the Girsanov’s theorem, we may conclude that the SDE

(3.8)

X_{t}=x-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla u(T-s,X_{s})ds+\sigma W_{t},

admits a weak solution. In addition, since

(ii). Next we observe that
\nabla u

(3.9)

By denoting

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}Y_{t}dt+\sigma dW_{t},&X_{0}=x,\\

where

. Introduce the norm

\|(Y,Z)\|_{{\cal D}}:=\sup_{t\leq T}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{t}|^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\right]\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We are going to show that

By

e^{-C_{T}(1+|x|^{2})}\leq\psi(t,x)\leq C_{T},\qquad|\nabla\psi(t,x)|\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}).

On the other hand, by the definition of

\Lambda_{T}\leq C_{T}e^{C_{T}(|x|^{2}+\sup_{t\leq T}|\bar{X}_{t}|^{2})}.

Now we may provide the following estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|Y_{t}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leq T}|\nabla u(T-t,X_{t})|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\Lambda_{T}\sup_{t\leq T}|\nabla u(T-t,\bar{X}_{t})|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}e^{C_{T}|x|^{2}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[(1+\sup_{t\leq T}|\bar{X}_{t}|^{2})e^{C_{T}\sup_{t\leq T}|\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}}\right].

In particular, if

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[(1+\sup_{t\leq T}|\bar{X}_{t}|^{2})e^{C_{T}\sup_{t\leq T}|\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}}\right]<\infty
.

Moreover, by Itô’s formula, we obtain

\displaystyle d|Y_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(2\gamma|Y_{t}|^{2}-2Y_{t}\cdot\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-t},X_{t})+|Z_{t}|^{2}\right)dt+2Y_{t}\cdot Z_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\left((2\gamma-1)|Y_{t}|^{2}-|\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-t},X_{t})|^{2}+|Z_{t}|^{2}\right)dt+2Y_{t}\cdot Z_{t}dW_{t}.

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}}|Z_{t}|^{2}dt\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[|Y_{T\wedge\tau_{n}}|^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[|Y_{0}|^{2}]\\
+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}}\Big((1-2\gamma)|Y_{t}|^{2}+|\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-t},X_{t})|^{2}dt\Big)\right].

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{t}|^{2}dt\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[|Y_{T}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\Big((1-2\gamma)|Y_{t}|^{2}+|\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(m_{T-t},X_{t})|^{2}dt\Big)\right]<\infty.

\|(Y,Z)\|_{{\cal D}}<\infty

(iii). It is known (see e.g.

4.1

The aim of this section is to derive a first order condition to characterize the minimizer of the generalized free energy
{\cal F}^{\sigma,\gamma}.

Recall that
{\cal F}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p)=F(p)+\sigma^{2}I(p)+\gamma H(p)
with parameters
\sigma>0,

\gamma\geq 0,
and
I(p)=\int|\nabla\sqrt{p}|^{2},

H(p)=\int p\log p.

Proposition 4.1
.

The function
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}
is convex on
{\cal P}_{H}.
Additionally, if it admits a minimizer
p^{*}\in\mathcal{P}_{H}
such that
1/p^{*}\in L_{\text{loc}}^{\infty}
, then it is unique.

Proof.

It follows from the convexity of
F
by
2.3
, the convexity of
H
by convexity of
x\mapsto x\log(x)
and
4.2
below.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p,q\in{\cal P}_{H}
and
\alpha,\beta>0
. Then we have

I\left(\alpha p+\beta q\right)\leq\alpha I\left(p\right)+\beta I\left(q\right).

If in addition
1/p\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}
, then the equality holds if and only if
p=q
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi=\sqrt{p},\psi=\sqrt{q}
. We have by using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle I\left(\alpha p+\beta q\right)

\displaystyle=\int\left|\nabla\sqrt{\alpha\varphi^{2}+\beta\psi^{2}}\right|^{2}=\int\frac{\left(\alpha\varphi\nabla\varphi+\beta\psi\nabla\psi\right)^{2}}{\alpha\varphi^{2}+\beta\psi^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int\frac{\left(\alpha\varphi^{2}+\beta\psi^{2}\right)\left(\alpha\left(\nabla\varphi\right)^{2}+\beta\left(\nabla\psi\right)^{2}\right)}{\alpha\varphi^{2}+\beta\psi^{2}}=\alpha I\left(p\right)+\beta I\left(q\right).

The equality holds if and only if
\varphi\nabla\psi=\psi\nabla\varphi
.
If in addition
\frac{1}{p}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}
then
\frac{1}{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}
and
\frac{\psi}{\varphi}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}
which is a distribution in sense of Schwartz. Its derivative satisfies

\nabla\left(\frac{\psi}{\varphi}\right)=\frac{\varphi\nabla\psi-\psi\nabla\varphi}{\varphi^{2}}=0.

Therefore
\frac{\psi}{\varphi}
is constant a.e.,
i.e.
,
p
and
q
are proportional.
∎

If a probability measure

In particular, if
\frac{\delta{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}}{\delta p}(p,\cdot)=0,

{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}.

Proof.

We have
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma,\gamma}(p)=F(p)+\sigma^{2}I(p)+\gamma H(p).
We deal with each of these three terms separately. Adding the three subsequent inequalities gives the desired inequality. The second assertion then follows immediately from
4.1
. Throughout the proof, we denote

for

(i). By convexity of

Since

we conclude by passing to the limit

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\int|\nabla f_{t}|^{2}d\hat{p}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}+F_{t}\right)dp_{t}-\int\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\nabla\hat{u}_{t}|^{2}+F_{t}\right)d\hat{p}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int F_{t}(dp_{t}-d\hat{p}_{t})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}(I(p_{t})-I(\hat{p}_{t}))

\displaystyle\geq\int F_{t}(dp_{t}-dp^{*})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}(I(p_{t})-I(p^{*})),

where the last inequality follows from the optimality of
\hat{p}_{t}
in
eq.

4.10
.

By
4.9
and the above computations, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{d{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{t})}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(C\sigma)^{2}}{C_{P}}\left(\int F_{t}(dp_{t}-dp^{*})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}(I(p_{t})-I(p^{*}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{(C\sigma)^{2}}{C_{P}}\left({\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{t})-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})\right),

where the last inequality is due to
4.3
.
Therefore, the exponential convergence of the free energy (
2.10
) follows with
a constant
c=\frac{(C\sigma)^{2}}{C_{P}}
.

In order to obtain the exponential convergence of the relative Fisher information, define
f^{*}_{t}:=\sqrt{p_{t}/p^{*}}
,
{\cal L}^{*}:=\Delta-\nabla u^{*}\cdot\nabla
, and repeat the previous computation:

\displaystyle I(p_{t}|p^{*})

\displaystyle=4\int|\nabla f^{*}_{t}|^{2}dp^{*}=-4\int f^{*}_{t}{\cal L}^{*}f^{*}_{t}dp^{*}

\displaystyle=4\sigma^{-2}\left(\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p^{*},\cdot)(dp_{t}-dp^{*})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}(I(p_{t})-I(p^{*}))\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sigma^{-2}\left({\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{t})-{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{*})\right).

∎

Proposition 5.1
.

For some
\underaccent{\bar}{C},\bar{C},\underaccent{\bar}{c},\bar{c}>0,
we have for all
h>0,i\leq N_{h},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\underaccent{\bar}{C}e^{-\underaccent{\bar}{c}|x|^{2}}\leq p^{h}_{i}(x)\leq\bar{C}e^{-\bar{c}|x|^{2}}.

In addition, it holds

\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}\|\nabla p_{i}^{h}\|_{\infty}<+\infty.

Proof.

The Gaussian bounds are a direct consequence of

–
5.6
below. As for the second part, it follows from the identity
\nabla p_{i}^{h}=p_{i}^{h}\nabla\log p^{h}_{i}
by using the Gaussian upperbound above and the fact that

according to Lemmas
5.5
and
5.6
below.
∎

Recall that the mapping
p^{h}_{i}
is a solution to the stationary MFS equation
eq.

2.13
.
In other words, if we denote
u^{h}_{i}:=-\log(p_{i}^{h}),
it holds

(5.1)

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta u^{h}_{i}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla u^{h}_{i}\right|^{2}+\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p^{h}_{i},\cdot\right)+h^{-1}u^{h}_{i-1}-h^{-1}u^{h}_{i}=\lambda^{h}_{i},

with

(5.2)

\lambda^{h}_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}\left(p^{h}_{i},\cdot\right)+h^{-1}\left(u^{h}_{i-1}-u^{h}_{i}\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta u^{h}_{i}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla u^{h}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)p^{h}_{i}.

The key point is to observe that we have the decomposition

with
v^{h}_{i}
uniformly convex and
w^{h}_{i}
uniformly Lipschitz. It comes from using arguments similar to Section
3.1
.
In this setting there is a slight ambiguity in the definition of
v^{h}_{i}
(and thus
w^{h}_{i}
) due to the normalizing constant
\lambda_{i}^{h}
.
Let us define
v^{h}_{i}
as the solution to

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta v^{h}_{i}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla v^{h}_{i}\right|^{2}+g+h^{-1}v^{h}_{i-1}-h^{-1}v^{h}_{i}=0.

Lemma 5.2
.

The function
(v^{h}_{i})_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
are uniformly
\eta
–convex for some
\eta>0
.

Proof.

Observe that
v^{h}_{i}
corresponds to the stationary solution to
eq.

3.4
with parameter
\gamma=h^{-1}
and convex term
g+h^{-1}v^{h}_{i-1}
instead of

Due to
3.5
,

is
\eta^{h}_{i}
–convex with

\displaystyle\eta^{h}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{h^{-2}+4\sigma^{2}\Big(\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}+h^{-1}\eta^{h}_{i-1}\Big)}-h^{-1}}{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{h^{-2}+4\sigma^{2}\Big(\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}+h^{-1}\min\big(\eta^{h}_{i-1},\sqrt{\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}}/\sigma\big)\Big)}-h^{-1}}{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\min\big(\eta^{h}_{i-1},\sqrt{\underaccent{\bar}{\kappa}}/\sigma\big).

Recall that

. Finally we obtain that

is

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

The Hessian’s
(\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i})_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
are uniformly bounded.

Proof.

As in
3.5
, we may obtain the following probabilistic representation:

\displaystyle\nabla v^{h}_{i}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{s}{h}}\Big(\nabla g(X_{s})+h^{-1}\nabla v^{h}_{i-1}(X_{s})\Big)ds+e^{-\frac{t}{h}}\nabla v^{h}_{i}(X_{t})\right],

with

\displaystyle X_{s}=x-\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla v^{h}_{i}(X_{r})dr+\sigma W_{s}.

Let
X^{\prime}
satisfy the same SDE with initial value
x^{\prime}
. Since
v^{h}_{i}
is
\eta
–convex, it follows from the same arguments as
eq.

3.13
that

\left|X_{t}-X^{\prime}_{t}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta t}{2}}\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|.

Further we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\nabla v^{h}_{i}(x)-\nabla v^{h}_{i}(x^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{s}{h}}(\bar{\kappa}+h^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i-1}\|_{\infty})\left|X_{s}-X^{\prime}_{s}\right|ds+e^{-\frac{t}{h}}\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\left|X_{t}-X^{\prime}_{t}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\frac{1}{h}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta}{2})s}(\bar{\kappa}+h^{-1}\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i-1}\|_{\infty})ds+e^{-(\frac{1}{h}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta}{2})t}\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\Big)\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|.

Letting
t\rightarrow\infty,
we get

\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\bar{\kappa}h+\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i-1}\|_{\infty}}{1+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta h}{2}}.

Therefore, we deduce by induction that

\|\nabla^{2}v^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \frac{2\bar{\kappa}}{\sigma^{2}\eta}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta h}{2}\right)^{i}}\bigg)+\frac{\bar{\eta}_{0}}{\left(1+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta h}{2}\right)^{i}}\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \frac{2\bar{\kappa}}{\sigma^{2}\eta}+\bar{\eta}_{0}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

The gradients
(\nabla w^{h}_{i})_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
are uniformly bounded.

Proof.

Observe that
w^{h}_{i}=u^{h}_{i}-v^{h}_{i}
satisfies

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta w^{h}_{i}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla v^{h}_{i}\cdot\nabla w^{h}_{i}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\left|\nabla w^{h}_{i}\right|^{2}+G\left(p^{h}_{i},\cdot\right)+h^{-1}w^{h}_{i-1}-h^{-1}w^{h}_{i}=\lambda^{h}_{i}.

As in
3.6
, we observe that
w^{h}_{i}
is the value function of the following stochastic control problem:

w^{h}_{i}(x)=\inf_{\alpha}\Expect\Big[\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{s}{h}}\left(G\left(p^{h}_{i},X^{\alpha}_{s}\right)+h^{-1}w^{h}_{i-1}(X^{\alpha}_{s})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\alpha_{s}|^{2}-\lambda^{h}_{i}\right)ds+e^{-\frac{t}{h}}w^{h}_{i}\left(X^{\alpha}_{t}\right)\Big],

with

dX^{\alpha}_{s}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(\nabla v^{h}_{i}\left(X^{\alpha}_{s}\right)+\alpha_{s}\right)ds+\sigma dW_{s},\quad X^{\alpha}_{0}=x.

Further as in
eq.

3.14
, we may estimate

\left|w^{h}_{i}(x)-w^{h}_{i}(x^{\prime})\right|\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(\frac{1}{h}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta}{2})s}(L_{G}+h^{-1}\|\nabla w^{h}_{i-1}\|_{\infty})+e^{-(\frac{1}{h}+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta}{2})t}\|\nabla w^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\Big)|x-x^{\prime}|.

Letting
T\rightarrow\infty,
we obtain

\|\nabla w^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{L_{G}h+\|\nabla w^{h}_{i-1}\|_{\infty}}{1+\frac{\sigma^{2}\eta h}{2}}.

Therefore, we deduce by induction that

\|\nabla w^{h}_{i}\|_{\infty}\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \frac{2L_{G}}{\sigma^{2}\eta}+L_{0}.

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

The Hessians
(\nabla^{2}u^{h}_{i})_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
are uniformly bounded.

Proof.

As in the proof of
3.7
, the Feynman–Kac formula ensures that

\displaystyle\nabla u^{h}_{i}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{h}}\left(\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p^{h}_{i},X_{t})+h^{-1}\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(X_{t})\right)dt\right],

with

\displaystyle X_{t}=x-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u^{h}_{i}(X_{s})ds+\sigma W_{t}.

Let
Y
satisfy the same SDE starting from
y
. By the reflection coupling in
A.7
, it holds

{\cal W}_{1}\left(p^{X}_{t},p^{Y}_{t}\right)\leq Ce^{-ct}|x-y|,

where
p^{X}
and
p^{Y}
are the marginal distribution of
X
and
Y
respectively.
Then it follows by Kantorovitch duality that

\displaystyle\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i}(x)-\nabla u^{h}_{i}(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}Ce^{-\frac{t}{h}-ct}|x-y|+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{h}}h^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(X_{t})-\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(Y_{t})\big|\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{Ch}{1+ch}|x-y|+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{h}}h^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(X_{t})-\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(Y_{t})\big|\right]dt.

Next apply the same estimate on
\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(X_{t})-\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}(Y_{t})\big|
, and obtain

\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i}(x)-\nabla u^{h}_{i}(y)\big|\leq\frac{2Ch}{1+ch}|x-y|\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t_{1}}{h}}h^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t_{2}}{h}}h^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i-2}\big(X^{(1)}_{t_{1}+t_{2}}\big)-\nabla u^{h}_{i-2}\big(Y^{(1)}_{t_{1}+t_{2}}\big)\big|\right]dt_{2}dt_{1},

with

X^{(1)}_{0}=x,\quad dX^{(1)}_{t}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla u^{h}_{i}\big(X^{(1)}_{t}\big)dt+\sigma dW_{t},&\mbox{for $t\in[0,t_{1})$}\\
-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla u^{h}_{i-1}\big(X^{(1)}_{t}\big)dt+\sigma dW_{t},&\mbox{for $t\geq t_{1}$}.\end{cases}

By repeating the procedure, we eventually obtain for
i\geq 1

\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i}(x)-\nabla u^{h}_{i}(y)\big|\leq\frac{Chi}{1+ch}|x-y|\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{h}\sum_{j=1}^{i}t_{j}}h^{-i}\mathbb{E}\left[\Big|\nabla u_{0}\Big(X^{(i-1)}_{\sum_{j=1}^{i}t_{j}}\Big)-\nabla u_{0}\Big(Y^{(i-1)}_{\sum_{j=1}^{i}t_{j}}\Big)\Big|\right]dt_{i}\cdots dt_{1},

with

X^{(i-1)}_{0}=x,\penalty\ \penalty\ dX^{(i-1)}_{t}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\nabla u^{h}_{j}\big(X^{(i-1)}_{t}\big)dt+\sigma dW_{t},\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{for $t\in[t_{i-j},t_{i+1-j})$}.

Again it follows from the reflection coupling that

{\cal W}_{1}\left(p^{X^{(i-1)}}_{t},p^{Y^{(i-1)}}_{t}\right)\leq Ce^{-ct}|x-y|,

where
p^{X^{(i-1)}},\penalty\ p^{Y^{(i-1)}}
are the marginal distribution of
X^{(i-1)},\penalty\ Y^{(i-1)}
respectively. In particular, the constants
c,\penalty\ C
do not depend on
(t_{1},\cdots,t_{i-1})
by Lemmas
5.2
–
5.4
. Finally we get

\displaystyle\big|\nabla u^{h}_{i}(x)-\nabla u^{h}_{i}(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Chi}{1+ch}|x-y|+C\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(\frac{1}{h}+c)\sum_{j=1}^{i}t_{j}}h^{-i}|x-y|dt_{i}\cdots dt_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C(T+1)|x-y|,

and the desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The vectors
\big(\nabla u^{h}_{i}(0)\big)_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
are uniformly bounded.

Proof.

The proof follows similar arguments as
4.5
and
4.6
.
First we observe that the sequence
{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i})
is non–increasing as

{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i})\leq{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i})+h^{-1}H(p^{h}_{i}|p^{h}_{i-1})\leq{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i-1})+h^{-1}H(p^{h}_{i-1}|p^{h}_{i-1})={\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i-1}),

by using
eq.

2.12
for the second inequality.
In addition, it follows from Assumption

that

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}p^{h}_{i}(x)dx+\sigma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{p^{h}_{i}}(x)|^{2}dx\leq{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p^{h}_{i}).

Therefore we have

\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}\left\{\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}p^{h}_{i}(x)dx+\sigma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\sqrt{p^{h}_{i}}(x)|^{2}dx\right\}\leq{\mathfrak{F}}^{\sigma}(p_{0}).

Since we have proved that

, we deduce that

Finally we obtain
\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}|\nabla u^{h}_{i}(0)|<\infty
.
∎

5.2
Equicontinuity in Time

We aim to show the equicontinuity in time of the family
(p^{h})_{h>0}
as stated in the proposition below. We also demonstrate as a preliminary step and for later use that the family of function
(t\mapsto\lambda^{h}_{\lfloor t/h\rfloor})_{h>0}
defined by (
5.2
) is bounded and equicontinuous.

Proposition 5.7
.

There exists constants
C,c>0
such that for all
h>0,

i<j\leq N_{h},

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

|p^{h}_{j}(x)-p^{h}_{i}(x)|\leq C\exp(-c|x|^{2})(j-i)h.

Additionally, the sequence
(\lambda_{i}^{h})_{h.i\leq N_{h}}
is uniformly bounded,i.e.,

\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}|\lambda_{i}^{h}|<+\infty,

and there exists a modulus of continuity
\varpi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for all
h>0,

i<j\leq N_{h},

|\lambda_{j}^{h}-\lambda_{i}^{h}|\leq\varpi((j-i)h).

Proof.

(i).
Formulas for
\lambda_{i}^{h}
.

The normalization condition for
u_{i}^{h},i\leq N_{h},
writes

\displaystyle 1=\int\exp(-u_{i}^{h})

\displaystyle=\int\exp(-u_{i-1}^{h})\exp\left(-h\frac{u_{i}^{h}-u_{i-1}^{h}}{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\int p_{i-1}^{h}\exp\left(-h\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta u_{i}^{h}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\nabla u_{i}^{h}|^{2}+\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},\cdot)-\lambda_{i}^{h}\right)\right).

where the latter follows from
eq.

5.1
.
This allows us to obtain the following formula for
\lambda_{i}^{h}
:

(5.3)

\lambda_{i}^{h}=-\frac{1}{h}\log\int p_{i-1}^{h}\exp(-hB_{i}^{h}),

where

B_{i}^{h}:=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta u_{i}^{h}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}|\nabla u_{i}^{h}|^{2}+\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},\cdot).

By writing the normalization in the backward way,

1=\int\exp(-u_{i-1}^{h})=\int\exp(-u_{i}^{h})\exp\left(h\frac{u_{i}^{h}-u_{i-1}^{h}}{h}\right)=\int\exp(-u_{i}^{h})\exp(h(B_{i}^{h}-\lambda_{i}^{h})),

we obtain a similar formula

(5.4)

\lambda_{i}^{h}=\frac{1}{h}\log\int p_{i}^{h}\exp(hB_{i}^{h}).

We apply Jensen’s inequality to
eq.

5.3
and
eq.

5.4
to obtain

(5.5)

\int p_{i}^{h}B_{i}^{h}\leq\lambda_{i}^{h}\leq\int p_{i-1}^{h}B_{i}^{h}.

Additionally, estimates from
5.5
and
5.6
gives us the bound

(5.6)

\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}|B_{i}^{h}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

Note that the same holds for
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},\cdot)
as
(p_{i}^{h})_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
belong to a
{\cal W}_{1}
–compact set due to the Gaussian bound.
Thus, by
5.1
, we prove the second claim
\sup_{h,i\leq N_{h}}|\lambda_{i}^{h}|<+\infty
.

(ii).
Time regularity of
p_{i}^{h}
.
According to the HJB equation (
5.1
) and Step (i) above, it holds

(5.7)

|u_{j}^{h}(x)-u_{i}^{h}(x)|=\left|h\left(\sum_{s=i+1}^{j}B_{s}^{h}-\sum_{s=i+1}^{j}\lambda_{s}^{h}\right)\right|\leq C(j-i)h(1+|x|^{2}).

Using further the bound from
5.1
, we obtain

(5.8)

|p_{j}^{h}(x)-p_{i}^{h}(x)|=|\exp(-u_{j}^{h}(x))-\exp(-u_{i}^{h}(x))|\leq p_{j}^{h}(x)\vee p_{i}^{h}(x)\,|u_{j}^{h}(x)-u_{i}^{h}(x)|\\
\leq C(j-i)h\exp(-c|x|^{2})(1+|x|^{2})\leq C(j-i)h\exp(-c|x|^{2}),

which is our first claim.
This implies the
\mathcal{W}_{1}
–regularity of
p_{i}^{h}
as follows:

(5.9)

\mathcal{W}_{1}(p_{j}^{h},p_{i}^{h})\leq\int|x||p_{j}^{h}(x)-p_{i}^{h}(x)|dx\leq C(j-i)h\int|x|\exp(-c|x|^{2})\leq C(j-i)h.

(iii).
Uniform continuity of
\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
.

Thanks to the estimate in
5.1
,
\{p_{i}^{h}\}_{h,i\leq N_{h}}
forms a relatively compact set in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
, and the
\mathcal{W}_{1}
–continuity of
p\mapsto\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p,0)
becomes uniform. That is, there exists a m.o.c.
\varpi_{0}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\left|\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},0)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},0)\right|\leq\varpi_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{1}(p_{i}^{h},p_{j}^{h})),\quad\forall h>0,\forall i\leq N_{h},\forall j\leq N_{h}.

Integrating along the straight line from
0
to any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and using the assumptions on
\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},x)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},0)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},0)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\left|x\cdot\left(\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},tx)-\nabla\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},tx)\right)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq\varpi_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{1}(p_{i}^{h},p_{j}^{h}))+L_{G}|x|\mathcal{W}_{1}(p_{i}^{h},p_{j}^{h}).

Combining with
eq.

5.9
,we deduce that the exists a m.o.c.
\varpi_{1}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

(5.10)

\left|\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},x)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},x)\right|\leq(1+|x|)\varpi_{1}((j-i)h).

(iv).
Time regularity of
\lambda_{i}^{h}
.

We first note that thanks to
eq.

5.5
we can approximate
\lambda_{i}^{h}
by
\int p_{i}^{h}B_{i}^{h}
, up to a uniform
O(h)
error. More precisely,

|r_{i}^{h}|:=\left|\lambda_{i}^{h}-\int p_{i}^{h}B_{i}^{h}\right|\leq\left|\int(p_{i}^{h}-p_{i-1}^{h})B_{i}^{h}\right|\\
\leq Ch\int\exp(-c|x|^{2})(1+|x|^{2})\leq Ch,

where we used
eq.

5.6
and
eq.

5.8
.
It suffices then to study the difference

\int p_{j}^{h}B_{j}^{h}-p_{i}^{h}B_{i}^{h}=\int p_{i}^{h}(B_{j}^{h}-B_{i}^{h})+\int(p_{j}^{h}-p_{i}^{h})B_{j}^{h}=:\delta+\delta^{\prime}.

We bound the second part, using again
eq.

5.6
and
eq.

5.8
,

|\delta^{\prime}|\leq C(j-i)h\int\exp(-c|x|^{2})(1+|x|^{2})\leq C(j-i)h.

As for the first part, we decompose it into
three terms, each of which we treat separately:

\delta=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\int p_{i}^{h}(\Delta u_{j}^{h}-\Delta u_{i}^{h})-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\int p_{i}^{h}(|\nabla u_{j}^{h}|^{2}-|\nabla u_{i}^{h}|^{2})\\
+\int p_{i}^{h}\left(\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{j}^{h},\cdot)-\frac{\delta F}{\delta p}(p_{i}^{h},\cdot)\right)=:\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}.

We apply integration by parts to the first term, using the previous estimates on
\nabla u_{i}^{h},p_{i}^{h}
and the time regularity result of
\nabla u_{i}^{h}
from
5.8
below,

\int_{0}^{1}r\kappa^{+}(r)dr<\infty
and

(x-y)\cdot\big(b(t,x)-b(t,y)\big)\leq\kappa(|x-y|)|x-y|^{2},\quad\mbox{for all $t\in[0,T],$ $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.$}

Remark A.6
.

If
b(t,x)=-(\alpha(t,x)+\nabla\beta(t,x))
with
\alpha
bounded and
\beta

\eta
–convex in
x
, i.e.,

\left(\nabla\beta(t,x)-\nabla\beta(t,y)\right)\cdot\left(x-y\right)\geq\eta\left|x-y\right|^{2},

then the function
b
satisfies
A.5
(ii) with
\kappa(r)=\frac{2\|\alpha\|_{\infty}}{r}-\eta.

Theorem A.7
.

Let
A.5
hold. Consider the following two diffusion processes

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+\sigma dW_{t},\qquad dY_{t}=(b+\delta b)(t,Y_{t})dt+\sigma dW_{t},

and denote their marginal distributions by
p^{X}_{t}:={\cal L}(X_{t})
and
p^{Y}_{t}:={\cal L}(Y_{t})
. Then we have

(A.7)

{\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{t},p^{Y}_{t})\leq Ce^{-c\sigma^{2}t}\Big({\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{0},p^{Y}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{c\sigma^{2}s}\mathbb{E}\big[|\delta b(s,Y_{s})|\big]ds\Big),\quad\mbox{for all $t\geq 0$},

where the constants
C
and
c
only depend on the function
\kappa(\cdot)/\sigma^{2}
.

Remark A.8
.

It follows immediately from
A.7
that if
\pi
is an invariant distribution of the process
X
then

{\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{t},\pi)\leq Ce^{-c\sigma^{2}t}{\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{0},\pi),\quad\mbox{for all $t\geq 0$}.

In particular, it is unique and it is the limiting distribution of
X.

Proof.

We first recall the reflection-synchronuous coupling introduced in
[
8
]
. Introduce Lipschitz functions
{\rm rc}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto[0,1]
and
{\rm sc}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto[0,1]
satisfying

{\rm rc}^{2}(x,y)+{\rm sc}^{2}(x,y)=1.

Fix a small constant
\eta>0
. We impose that
{\rm rc}(x,y)=1
whenever
|x-y|>\eta
and
{\rm rc}(x,y)=0
if
|x-y|\leq\eta/2
.
The so-called reflection-synchronuous coupling is the strong solution to the following SDE system:

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=b(t,X_{t})dt+{\rm rc}(X_{t},Y_{t})\sigma dW^{1}_{t}+{\rm sc}(X_{t},Y_{t})\sigma dW^{2}_{t},

\displaystyle dY_{t}

\displaystyle=(b+\delta b)(t,Y_{t})dt+{\rm rc}(X_{t},Y_{t})(I-2e_{t}\langle e_{t},\cdot\rangle)\sigma dW^{1}_{t}+{\rm sc}(X_{t},Y_{t})\sigma dW^{2}_{t},

where
W^{1},W^{2}
are
d
-dimensional independent standard Brownian motion and

e_{t}=\frac{X_{t}-Y_{t}}{|X_{t}-Y_{t}|}\quad\mbox{for $X_{t}\neq Y_{t}$, \quad and}\quad e_{t}=u\quad\mbox{for $X_{t}=Y_{t},$}

with
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
a fixed arbitrary unit vector. We denote by
{\rm rc}_{t}:={\rm rc}(X_{t},Y_{t})
and define
r_{t}:=|X_{t}-Y_{t}|
. Observe that

dr_{t}=\langle e_{t},b(t,X_{t})-b(t,Y_{t})-\delta b(t,Y_{t})\rangle dt+2{\rm rc}_{t}\sigma dW^{\circ}_{t},

where
W^{\circ}
is a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion, see
[
7
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Next we construct an important auxiliary function
f
as in
[
8
, Section 5.3]
. First define two constants:

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\inf\{R\geq 0:\penalty\ \kappa(r)\leq 0,\penalty\ \mbox{for all $r\geq R$}\},

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\inf\{R\geq R_{1}:\penalty\ \kappa(r)R(R-R_{1})\leq-4\sigma^{2},\penalty\ \mbox{for all $r\geq R$}\}.

Further define

\varphi(r)=e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{r}u\kappa^{+}(u)du},\quad\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\varphi(u)du,\quad g(r)=1-\frac{c}{2}\int_{0}^{r}\Phi(u)\varphi(u)^{-1}du,

where the constant
c=\left(\int_{0}^{R_{2}}\Phi(r)\varphi(r)^{-1}dr\right)^{-1}
, and eventually define the auxiliary function

f(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\varphi(u)g(u\wedge R_{2})du.

One easily checks that

r\varphi(R_{1})\leq\Phi(r)\leq 2f(r)\leq 2\Phi(r)\leq 2r,\quad\mbox{for all $r>0$}.

Note also that
f
is increasing and concave. In addition,
f
is linear on
[R_{2},+\infty)
, twice continuously differentiable on
(0,R_{2})
and satisfies

(A.8)

2\sigma^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq-r\kappa^{+}(r)f^{\prime}(r)-c\sigma^{2}f(r),\quad\mbox{for all $r\in(0,\infty)\backslash\{R_{2}\}.$}

This inequality follows easily by direct computation on
[0,R_{2})
and we refer to
[
8
, Eqn (5.32)]
for a detailed justification on
(R_{2},+\infty)
.
Then we have by Itô–Tanaka formula as in
[
8
, Eqn (5.26)]
that

df(r_{t})\leq\left(f^{\prime}_{-}(r_{t})\langle e_{t},b(t,X_{t})-b(t,Y_{t})-\delta b(t,Y_{t})\rangle+2\sigma^{2}{\rm rc}_{t}^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r_{t})\right)dt+2{\rm rc}_{t}f^{\prime}_{-}(r_{t})\sigma dW^{\circ}_{t}.

Further note that

\langle e_{t},b(t,X_{t})-b(t,Y_{t})\rangle\leq 1_{r_{t}<\eta}|b|_{Lip}\eta+1_{r_{t}\geq\eta}r_{t}\kappa^{+}(r_{t}).

Together with the fact that
f^{\prime}\leq 1,

f^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
and
{\rm rc}_{t}1_{r_{t}\geq\eta}=1,
we deduce that

de^{c\sigma^{2}t}f(r_{t})\leq e^{c\sigma^{2}t}\Big(2{\rm rc}_{t}f^{\prime}_{-}(r_{t})\sigma dW^{\circ}_{t}+|\delta b(t,Y_{t})|dt+1_{r_{t}<\eta}(c\sigma^{2}f(r_{t})+|b|_{Lip}\eta)dt\\
+1_{r_{t}\geq\eta}\big(c\sigma^{2}f(r_{t})+r_{t}\kappa^{+}(r_{t})f^{\prime}(r_{t})+2\sigma^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r_{t})\big)dt\Big).

It follows from
eq.

A.8
that

de^{c\sigma^{2}t}f(r_{t})\leq e^{c\sigma^{2}t}\left(2{\rm rc}_{t}f^{\prime}_{-}(r_{t})\sigma dW^{\circ}_{t}+\Big(|\delta b(t,Y_{t})|+(c\sigma^{2}+|b|_{Lip})\eta\Big)dt\right).

Taking expectation on both sides, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[e^{c\sigma^{2}t}f(r_{t})-f(r_{0})]\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{c\sigma^{2}s}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\delta b(s,Y_{s})|\big]+(c\sigma^{2}+|b|_{Lip})\eta\Big)ds.

Again due to the construction of
f
we have

{\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{t},p^{Y}_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}[r_{t}]\leq 2\varphi(R_{1})^{-1}\mathbb{E}[f(r_{t})]\\
\begin{aligned} &\leq 2\varphi(R_{1})^{-1}e^{-c\sigma^{2}t}\left(\mathbb{E}[f(r_{0})]+\int_{0}^{t}e^{c\sigma^{2}s}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\delta b(s,Y_{s})|\big]+(c\sigma^{2}+|b|_{Lip})\eta\Big)ds\right)\\
&\leq 2\varphi(R_{1})^{-1}e^{-c\sigma^{2}t}\left({\cal W}_{1}(p^{X}_{0},p^{Y}_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{c\sigma^{2}s}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[|\delta b(s,Y_{s})|\big]+(c\sigma^{2}+|b|_{Lip})\eta\Big)ds\right).\end{aligned}

By passing to the limit
\eta\rightarrow 0
, we finally obtain the estimate (
A.7
).
∎

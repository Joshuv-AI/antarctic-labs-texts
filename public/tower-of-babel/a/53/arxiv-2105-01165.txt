Explicit formulas for the inverses of Toeplitz matrices, with applications
AKIHIKO INOUE

Abstract
. We derive novel explicit formulas for the inverses of truncated block Toeplitz matrices that correspond to a multivariate minimal stationary process. The main ingredients of the formulas are the Fourier coefficients of the phase function attached to the spectral density of the process. The derivation of the formulas is based on a recently developed finite prediction theory applied to the dual process of the stationary process. We illustrate the usefulness of the formulas by two applications. The first one is a strong convergence result for solutions of general block Toeplitz systems for a multivariate short-memory process. The second application is closed-form formulas for the inverses of truncated block Toeplitz matrices corresponding to a multivariate ARMA process. The significance of the latter is that they provide us with a linear-time algorithm to compute the solutions of corresponding block Toeplitz systems.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
be the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
.
We write
\sigma
for the normalized Lebesgue measure
d\theta/(2\pi)
on

([-\pi,\pi),\mathcal{B}([-\pi,\pi)))
, where
\mathcal{B}([-\pi,\pi))
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
on
[-\pi,\pi)
; thus we have
\sigma([-\pi,\pi))=1
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we write

for the Lebesgue space of measurable functions

such that
\|f\|_{p}<\infty

Let

(1.1)

\gamma(k):=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-ik\theta}w(e^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(1.2)

w\in L^{d\times d}_{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
w(e^{i\theta})
is a positive Hermitian matrix
\sigma

(1.3)

\displaystyle w^{-1}\in L^{d\times d}_{1}(\mathbb{T}).

Let
\{X_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued, centered, weakly stationary process

w

\gamma
.

is

(see Section 10 of
[
21

In this paper,

]

.

-valued outer functions on

\mathbb{T}
such that

(see
[

]
; see also Section

We note that the unitary matrix valued function

attached to

Let

\left(T_{n}(w)^{-1}\right)^{s,t}=\langle X^{\prime}_{s},P_{[1,n]}X^{\prime}_{t}\rangle,\qquad s,t\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

stands for the Gram matrix
(see Section
3
) and

P_{[1,n]}X^{\prime}_{t}
denotes the best
linear predictor of
X^{\prime}_{t}
based on the observations

(see Section
2
for the precise definition).
Moreover, for

,
A\in\mathbb{C}^{dn\times dn}
and

,

for the
(s,t)
block of

; thus

.
The equality (

) enables us to apply the

-related methods
developed in
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
and others to derive

.

We illustrate the usefulness of the explicit formulas for
T_{n}(w)^{-1}
by two applications.
The first one is a strong convergence result for solutions of block Toeplitz systems.
For this application, we assume (
1.2
) as well as the following condition:

(1.6)

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\|\gamma(k)\|<\infty
and

\min_{z\in\mathbb{T}}\det w(z)>0
.

Here, for
a\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
,
\|a\|
denotes the
operator norm of
a
.
The condition (
1.6
) implies that
\{X_{k}\}
with spectral density

is a

short-memory
process.
We note that (
1.3
) follows from (
1.2
) and (
1.6
)
(see Section
4

Under (
1.2
) and (
1.6
), for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a
\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
-valued sequence

\{y_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|y_{k}\|<\infty

(1.7)

Z_{n}=(z_{n,1}^{\top},\dots,z_{n,n}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{dn\times d}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ z_{n,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d},\ k\in\{1,\dots,n\},

(1.8)

T_{n}(w)Z_{n}=Y_{n},

where

(1.9)

Y_{n}:=(y_{1}^{\top},\dots,y_{n}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{dn\times d}.

Also, let

(1.10)

Z_{\infty}=(z_{1}^{\top},z_{2}^{\top},\dots)^{\top}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ z_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d},\ k\in\mathbb{N},

be the solution to the corresponding infinite block Toeplitz system

(1.11)

T_{\infty}(w)Z_{\infty}=Y_{\infty},

where

(1.12)

T_{\infty}(w):=\left(\begin{matrix}\gamma(0)&\gamma(-1)&\gamma(-2)&\cdots\cr\gamma(1)&\gamma(0)&\gamma(-1)&\cdots\cr\gamma(2)&\gamma(1)&\gamma(0)&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{matrix}\right)

and

(1.13)

Y_{\infty}:=(y_{1}^{\top},y_{2}^{\top},\dots)^{\top}.

Then, our result (Theorem
4.1
) reads as follows:

(1.14)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|z_{n,k}-z_{k}\|=0.

We explain the background of the result (
1.14
).
As above, let
\{X_{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued, centered, weakly stationary process
that has spectral density
w
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the finite and infinite predictor coefficients
\phi_{n,k}\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
,

k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
\phi_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, of
\{X_{k}\}
are defined by

P_{[1,n]}X_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{n,k}X_{n+1-k}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad P_{(-\infty,n]}X_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\phi_{k}X_{n+1-k},

respectively; see Section
3
for the precise definitions of

P_{[1,n]}
and
P_{(-\infty,n]}
.
We note that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{k}\|<\infty
holds under (
1.2
) and (
1.6
)
(see Section
4
below and (2.16) in
[
16
]
).

Baxter’s inequality
in
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
states that,
under (
1.2
) and (
1.6
),
there exists
K\in(0,\infty)
such that

(1.15)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\phi_{n,k}-\phi_{k}\|\leq K\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{k}\|,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular, we have

(1.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\phi_{n,k}-\phi_{k}\|=0.

If we put
\tilde{w}(e^{i\theta}):=w(e^{-i\theta})
,
then,
(\phi_{n,1},\dots,\phi_{n,n})
is the solution to the block Toeplitz system

T_{n}(\tilde{w})(\phi_{n,1},\dots,\phi_{n,n})^{*}=(\gamma(1),\dots,\gamma(n))^{*},

called the
Yule–Walker equation
, while
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots)

is the solution to the corresponding
infinite block Toeplitz system

T_{\infty}(\tilde{w})(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots)^{*}=(\gamma(1),\gamma(2),\dots)^{*}.

Clearly,
\tilde{w}
satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.6
) since so does

.
Therefore, our result (
1.14
) can be viewed as an extension to (
1.16
).
It should be noted, however, that we prove (
1.14
) directly,
without proving an analogue of Baxter’s inequality (
1.15
).

The convergence result (
1.16
) has various applications in time series analysis, such as
the autoregressive sieve bootstrap (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
and the references therein), whille
Toeplitz systems of the form (
1.8
) appear in various fields, such as filtering of signals.
Therefore the extension (
1.14
), as well as the other results explained below,
may potentially be useful in such fields.
We note that Baxter’s inequality (
1.15
), hence (

), is also proved for

[

]
and
[

]
, respectively.
The FARIMA processes have singular spectral densities

elsewhere. However,

) for

The second application of the explicit formulas for
T_{n}(w)^{-1}
is

T_{n}(w)^{-1}
with rational

that
corresponds to a univariate (

) or multivariate (

) ARMA

More precisely, we assume that

w(e^{i\theta})=h(e^{i\theta})h(e^{i\theta})^{*},\qquad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi),

where

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mbox{the entries of $h(z)$ are rational functions in $z$ that have}

\displaystyle\mbox{no poles in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, and $\det h(z)$ has no zeros in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$.}

The closed-form formulas for

.
The significance of the formulas for

, algorithm to compute the solution

(1.19)

).

) for

Algorithms for Toeplitz linear systems that run faster than

are called

. While our algorithm is restricted to the class of

corresponding to ARMA processes, the class is important in applications,
and the linear-time algorithm is ideally superfast in the sense that there is no
algorithm faster than

.

Recall

The Hardy class

.
We write

for the Hardy class on

]

.

).

\displaystyle h(z)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{b}_{n,u,\ell}^{1}&=\beta_{n+1-u+\ell}^{*},\\
\tilde{b}_{n,u,\ell}^{2k}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{n,u,m}^{2k-1}\beta_{n+1+m+\ell},\qquad\tilde{b}_{n,u,\ell}^{2k+1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{n,u,m}^{2k}\beta_{n+1+m+\ell}^{*}.\end{aligned}\right.

.
Since

Theorem 2.1
.

We assume (

(i)

For

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left(T_{n}(w)^{-1}\right)^{s,t}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s\wedge t}\tilde{a}_{s-\ell}^{*}\tilde{a}_{t-\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{u=1}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{n,u,\ell}^{2k-1}a_{n+1-s+\ell}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{n,u,\ell}^{2k}\tilde{a}_{s+\ell}\right\}^{*}\tilde{a}_{t-u}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{j}\frac{y^{n}}{1-xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\left\{\frac{1}{r!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{r}\frac{1}{1-xy}\right\}\left\{\frac{1}{(j-r)!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{j-r}y^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j-r}\frac{x^{r}y^{n+r-j}}{(1-xy)^{r+1}},

hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i!j!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{j}\frac{x^{m}y^{n}}{1-xy}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j-r}y^{n+r-j}\sum_{q=0}^{i}\left\{\frac{1}{q!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{q}\frac{1}{(1-xy)^{r+1}}\right\}\left\{\frac{1}{(i-q)!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{i-q}x^{m+r}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j-r}y^{n+r-j}\sum_{q=0}^{i}\left\{\binom{q+r}{q}\frac{y^{q}}{(1-xy)^{q+r+1}}\right\}\left\{\binom{m+r}{i-q}x^{m+q+r-i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q=0}^{i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{n}{j-r}\binom{q+r}{q}\binom{m+r}{i-q}\frac{x^{m+q+r-i}y^{n+q+r-j}}{(1-xy)^{q+r+1}}.

Thus, the proposition follows.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+\ell}{i}\binom{j+\ell}{j}x^{n+\ell-i}y^{\ell}=\sum_{q=0}^{i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{j}{r}\binom{r+q}{q}\binom{n+r}{i-q}\frac{x^{n+r+q-i}y^{r+q}}{(1-xy)^{r+q+1}}.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{D}
.
Since

x^{n}y^{j}/(1-xy)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}x^{n+\ell}y^{j+\ell}
,
we have

\frac{1}{i!j!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{j}\frac{x^{n}y^{j}}{1-xy}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+\ell}{i}\binom{j+\ell}{j}x^{n+\ell-i}y^{\ell}.

On the other hand, by Proposition
A.1
,
we have

\frac{1}{i!j!}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)^{j}\frac{x^{n}y^{j}}{1-xy}=\sum_{q=0}^{i}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{j}{r}\binom{r+q}{q}\binom{n+r}{i-q}\frac{x^{n+r+q-i}y^{r+q}}{(1-xy)^{r+q+1}}.

Comparing, we obtain the proposition.
∎

We are ready to prove Lemma
5.3
.

Proof.

By (
5.5
)–(
5.8
) and Proposition
A.2
, we have,
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu\in\{1,\dots,K\}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,m_{\mu}\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}p_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)a_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_{0}}p_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)\rho_{0,\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{{\nu}=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{\nu}}\left\{\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell-n}{i-1}\binom{\ell+j-1}{j-1}p_{\mu}^{\ell-i+1-n}\overline{p}_{\nu}^{\ell}\right\}\rho_{\nu,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_{0}}p_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)\rho_{0,\ell}+\sum_{{\nu}=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{\nu}}\varphi_{n}^{{\mu},{\nu}}(i,j)\rho_{\nu,j}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\overline{p}_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)\tilde{a}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_{0}}\overline{p}_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)\tilde{\rho}_{0,\ell}

\displaystyle+\sum_{{\nu}=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{\nu}}\left\{\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell-n}{i-1}\binom{\ell+j-1}{j-1}\overline{p}_{\mu}^{\ell-i+1-n}p_{\nu}^{\ell}\right\}\tilde{\rho}_{\nu,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m_{0}}\overline{p}_{\mu,i}(\ell-n)\tilde{\rho}_{0,\ell}+\sum_{{\nu}=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{\nu}}\overline{\varphi}_{n}^{{\mu},{\nu}}(i,j)\tilde{\rho}_{\nu,j}.

Thus, the lemma follows.
∎

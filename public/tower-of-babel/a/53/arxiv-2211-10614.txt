Nonlocal metric dimension of graphs
Sandi Klavžar, Dorota Kuziak

Abstract
Nonlocal metric dimension dim n ​ ℓ ​ ( G ) {\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G) of a graph G G is introduced as the cardinality of a smallest nonlocal resolving set, that is, a set of vertices which resolves each pair of non-adjacent vertices of G G . Graphs G G with dim n ​ ℓ ​ ( G ) = 1 {\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=1 or with dim n ​ ℓ ​ ( G ) = n ⁡ ( G ) − 2 {\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n(G)-2 are characterized. The nonlocal metric dimension is determined for block graphs, for corona products, and for wheels. Two upper bounds on the nonlocal metric dimension are proved. An embedding of an arbitrary graph into a supergraph with a small nonlocal metric dimension and small diameter is presented.

Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph,
X\subseteq V(G)
, and
u,v\in V(G)
. Then
u
and
v
are
resolved
by
X
if there exists
x\in X
such that
d_{G}(u,x)\neq d_{G}(v,x)
, where
d_{G}(y,z)
denotes the shortest-path distance between vertices
y
and
z
of
G
. A set
X
such that each pair of vertices of
G
is resolved by
X
is a
resolving set
of
G
. A smallest resolving set is a
metric basis
of
G
, its cardinality is the
metric dimension
of
G
denoted by
\dim(G)
. This concept is one of the central ones in the field of metric graph theory, and its popularity stems, among other things, from the fact that it has found many applications in fields as diverse as computer science, chemistry, biology, and social sciences. For a better understanding of the concept and its applications see the recent survey
[
18
]
, while for a comprehensive survey on its variants see the other recent survey
[
11
]
.

In 2010, Okamoto, Phinezy, and Zhang
[
12
]
introduced the local metric dimension of a graph as a natural version of the metric dimension. In this version, which was further researched in particular in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
17
]
, we need to resolve only pairs of adjacent vertices. The dual concept, in which all pairs of non-adjacent vertices are to be resolved, has surprisingly not been considered in the literature. In this paper we fill this gap.

If
X\subseteq V(G)
resolves each pair of non-adjacent vertices, then we speak of a
nonlocal resolving set
. A smallest nonlocal resolving set is a
nonlocal metric basis
of
G
, its cardinality is the
nonlocal metric dimension
of
G
and will be denoted by
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)
. By definition,

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq\dim(G)

(1)

holds for every graph
G
. The difference can be arbitrary large as already demonstrated by complete graphs for which we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{n})=0
and
\dim(K_{n})=n-1
for every
n\geq 1
.

We proceed as follows. In the next section we first recall definitions, notation, and a result needed later. Then we characterize the graphs with the nonlocal metric dimension equal to
1
, and show that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=\dim(G)
holds for bipartite graphs
G
. In Section
3
we determine the nonlocal metric dimension for block graphs and for corona products. The latter result is reduced to a join of a graph with
K_{1}
. This motivates us to determine the nonlocal metric dimension of wheels in Section
4
. In the subsequent section we prove two upper bounds on the nonlocal metric dimension and characterize graphs
G
with
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n(G)-2
. In Section
6
, we embed an arbitrary graph into a supergraph with a small nonlocal metric dimension and small diameter. In the concluding section directions for further study are indicated.

2
Preliminaries

Unless stated otherwise, graphs considered will be connected. The order of a graph
G
will be denoted by
n(G)
. If
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
and
u\in V(G)
, then the
metric representation of u with respect to
X
is the vector
r(u|X)=(d_{G}(u,x_{1}),\dots,d_{G}(u,x_{k}))
. The diameter
{\rm diam}(G)
of
G
is the largest distance between pairs of vertices of
G
. The clique number of
G
is the order of a largest complete subgraph in
G
and denoted by
\omega(G)
. As usual, the chromatic number of
G
is denoted by
\chi(G)
. The edge cover number
\beta^{\prime}(G)
of
G
is the smallest number of edges such that each vertex is incident with at least one of these edges. The join
G+H
of graphs
G
and
H
is obtained from the disjoint union of a copy of
G
and a copy of
H
by adding an edge between each vertex of
G
and each vertex of
H
. The complement of
G
will be denoted by
\overline{G}
. For a positive integer
k
we will use the notation
[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
.

A vertex of degree at least
3
in a tree
T
is called a
branch vertex
. A leaf
u
of
T
is called a
terminal leaf
of a branch vertex
v
of
T
if
d_{T}(u,v)<d_{T}(u,w)
for every other branch vertex
w
of
T
. A branch vertex
v
of
T
is an
exterior branch vertex
of
T
if it has at least one terminal leaf. The path from an exterior branch vertex to its terminal leaf is called a
terminal path
. Let
n_{1}(T)
denote the number of leaves of
T
, and let
{\rm ex}(T)
denote the number of exterior branch vertices of
T
. In
[
5
,
7
,
15
]
it was proved that if
T
is a tree that is not a path, then

\dim(T)=n_{1}(T)-{\rm ex}(T).

(2)

We already mentioned that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{n})=0
for
n\geq 1
. Moreover, as soon as
G
is not complete,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\geq 1
. Clearly,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(P_{n})=1
for
n\geq 3
. It is also not difficult to see that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(C_{n})=2
for
n\geq 4
.

To characterize graphs
G
with
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=1
, we need the following concept. If
x\in V(G)
and
k\geq 0
, then
L_{k}(x)=\{u\in V(G):\ d_{G}(x,u)=k\}
is a
distance level
of
x
. In particular,
L_{0}(x)=\{x\}
, and
L_{1}(x)
is the (open) neighborhood of
x
.

Proposition 2.1

If
G
is a non-complete graph, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=1
if and only if there exists a vertex
x
such that
L_{k}(x)
induces a complete graph for every
k\leq{\rm diam}(G)
.

Proof.
Assume first that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=1
and let
\{x\}
be a nonlocal metric basis. If
u,v\in L_{k}(x)
for some
k\geq 1
, then
d_{G}(x,u)=d_{G}(x,v)=k
and hence
u
and
v
must be adjacent. Thus
L_{k}(x)
induces a complete graph. (
L_{0}(x)
is trivially complete.)

Conversely, let
x
be a vertex whose all distance levels induce complete graphs. Therefore, if
u,v\in V(G)
and
uv\notin E(G)
, then
d_{G}(x,u)\neq d_{G}(x,v)
and so
u
and
v
are resolved by
x
.

\square

In general
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)
can be arbitrary smaller than
\dim(G)
. This cannot happen if
G
is bipartite as the next result asserts.

Proposition 2.2

If
G
is a bipartite graph with
n(G)\geq 3
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=\dim(G)
.

Proof.
By (
1
) we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq\dim(G)
. Let
X
be a nonlocal metric basis of
G
. As
n(G)\geq 3
,
G
is not complete and hence
|X|\geq 1
.
Consider arbitrary vertices
u,v\in V(G)
. If
uv\notin E(G)
, then
u
and
v
are resolved by
X
because
X
is a nonlocal resolving set. If
uv\in E(G)
, then
u
and
v
are resolved by
X
since
G
is bipartite. It follows that
X
is also a resolving set. Hence
\dim(G)\leq|X|={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)
and we are done.

\square

3
Block graphs and corona products

Proposition
2.2
implies that if
T
is a tree with
n(T)\geq 3
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(T)=\dim(T)
. This fact generalizes to block graphs as follows. Let
G
be a block graph. The
block-cutpoint tree

\widehat{G}
of
G
is the tree whose vertices are the blocks and the cut-vertices of
G
, and a block
B
is adjacent to a cut-vertex
v
if
v\in V(B)
, cf.
[
19
, Definition 4.1.20]
.

Theorem 3.1

If
G
is a block graph with
n(G)\geq 3
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=\dim(\widehat{G})
.

Proof.
We can extend the terminology for trees from the previous section to block graphs as follows. Let
G
be a block graph. A block
B
of
G
is called a
branch block
if the vertices of
B
have at least three independent neighbors outside of
B
. A branch block
B
of
G
is an
exterior branch block
of
G
if in
\widehat{G}
there exist
t\geq 1
terminal paths emanating from a cut-vertex of
B
. If so, we say that
B
has
t

rays
.

Let now
X
be a nonlocal resolving set of
G
. If
B
is an exterior branch block of
G
with
t
rays, then we claim that
X
has at least
t-1
vertices in these rays, each one from a different ray. Indeed, otherwise there exist two rays
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
with no vertex from
X
. Let
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
be attached to
B
at vertices
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
(note that
u_{1}=u_{2}
is possible), and select neighbors
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, respectively, Then
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are not adjacent and have the same metric representation with respect to
X
. This proves the claim.
Each exterior branch block of
G
corresponds to an exterior branch vertex of
\widehat{G}
. Since
\widehat{G}
is a tree, (
2
) implies that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\geq\dim(\widehat{G})
.

To prove that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq\dim(\widehat{G})
holds, let
X
be a metric basis of
\widehat{G}
. We may without loss of generality assume that every element of
X
is a leaf of
\widehat{G}
. Then each element
x\in X
corresponds to a terminal block
B_{x}
of
G
, that is, a block which contains exactly one cut-vertex. For every
x\in X
, let
w_{x}
be an arbitrary but fixed vertex of
B_{x}
which is not a cut-vertex. Set
Y=\{w_{x}:\ x\in X\}
. Since we do not need to resolve adjacent vertices, it is straightforward to check that
Y
is a nonlocal resolving set of
G
. Hence
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq|Y|=|X|=\dim(\widehat{G})
.

\square

The metric dimension of block graphs was studied in
[
8
]
. It was proved that the metric dimension of a block graph
G
is equal to the metric dimension of a tree, obtained from
G
by replacing each block
B
of size at least
3
by a star whose leaves are the vertices of
B
. The local metric dimension of block graphs was investigated in
[
14
]
.

Let
G
and
H
be graphs where
V(G)=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n(G)}\}
. The
corona product
of
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\odot H
, is a graph obtained from the disjoint union of a copy of
G
and
n(G)
copies of
H
, denoted by
H_{i}
,
i\in[n(G)]
. The product
G\odot H
is then constructed by making
g_{i}
adjacent to every vertex in
H_{i}
for each
i\in[n(G)]
. Let further
\widetilde{H_{i}}
be the subgraph of
G\odot H
induced by
V(H_{i})\cup\{g_{i}\}
. Clearly,
\widetilde{H_{i}}\cong H+K_{1}
. We will use this notation in the rest of the section.

The metric dimension of corona products has been investigated in
[
9
,
20
]
. The local metric dimension of corona products has been studied in
[
13
]
and more generally of generalized hierarchical products in
[
10
]
. In particular, in
[
13
]
it is proved for the local metric dimension
{\rm dim_{\ell}}
that if
G
is a connected graph and
H
is a graph of radius at least
4
, then
{\rm dim_{\ell}}(G\odot H)=n(G)\cdot{\rm dim_{\ell}}(H+K_{1})
. In general,
{\rm dim_{\ell}}(G\odot H)\leq n(G)\cdot{\rm dim_{\ell}}(H+K_{1})
, see
[
10
]
. Here we add the following formula for the nonlocal metric dimension.

Theorem 3.2

If
G
is a graph and
H
a non-complete graph, then

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)=n(G)\cdot{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H+K_{1}).

Proof.
Let
X
be a nonlocal metric basis of
G\odot H
and let
X_{i}=X\cap V(\widetilde{H_{i}})
. If
u,v\in V(H_{i})
and
w\in V(G\odot H)\setminus V(H_{i})
, then
d_{G\odot H}(u,w)=d_{G\odot H}(v,w)
. Since also
d_{G\odot H}(u,g_{i})=d_{G\odot H}(v,g_{i})=1
, any two non-adjacent vertices from
H_{i}
must be resolved by some vertex from
H_{i}
. Therefore,
X_{i}
is a nonlocal resolving set of
\widetilde{H_{i}}
and so
|X_{i}|\geq{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(\widetilde{H_{i}})={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H+K_{1})
. Consequently,

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)=|X|=\sum_{i=1}^{n(G)}|X_{i}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n(G)}{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(\widetilde{H_{i}})=n(G)\cdot{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H+K_{1}).

Let now
Y
be a nonlocal metric basis of
H\odot K_{1}
. Since
H
is not complete,
|Y|\geq 1
. For each
i\in[n(G)]
, let
Y_{i}
be the copy of
Y
in
\widetilde{H_{i}}
. Note that
g_{i}\notin Y_{i}
. We claim that
\cup_{i=1}^{n(G)}Y_{i}
is a nonlocal resolving set of
G\odot H
. Indeed, if vertices
u
and
v
from
V(H_{i})
are non-adjacent, then they are resolved by some vertex from
Y_{i}
. Assume next
u\in V(\widetilde{H_{i}})
and
v\in V(\widetilde{H_{j}})
, where
i\neq j
. If
y_{i}\in Y_{i}
, then
d(y_{i},u)\leq 2
and
d(y_{i},v)\geq 3
, hence
u
and
v
are again resolved. We have thus seen that
\cup_{i=1}^{n(G)}Y_{i}
is a nonlocal resolving set and henceforth,

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)\leq|\cup_{i=1}^{n(G)}Y_{i}|=n(G)\cdot|Y|=n(G)\cdot{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H+K_{1}).

We conclude that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)=n(G)\cdot{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H+K_{1})
.

\square

In the case when the second factor of a corona product is complete, we can bound the nonlocal metric dimension as follows.

Theorem 3.3

If
G
is a graph and
n\geq 1
, then

\dim(G)\leq{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot K_{n})\leq n(G).

Proof.
To prove the upper bound we claim that
V(G)
is a nonlocal resolving set of
G\odot K_{n}
. Indeed, let
u,v\in V(G\odot K_{n})\setminus V(G)
be two non-adjacent vertices. Then
u\in H_{i}
and
v\in H_{j}
for some
i
and
j
, where
i\neq j
. As
d_{G\odot K_{n}}(u,g_{i})=1
and
d_{G\odot K_{n}}(v,g_{i})\geq 2
, the claim follows. Hence
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot K_{n})\leq n(G)
.

Let now
X
be a nonlocal metric basis of
G\odot H
; then
|X|={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)
. Let
Y\subseteq V(G)
be the set obtained from
X
by replacing each vertex
u\in X\cap V(\widetilde{H_{i}})
by
g_{i}
. Note that this in particular means that if
g_{i}\in X
, then also
g_{i}\in Y
. Clearly,
|Y|\leq|X|
. We claim that
Y
is a resolving set of
G
. For this sake consider arbitrary vertices
g_{i},g_{j}\in V(G)\subseteq V(G\odot H)
. Let
u\in V(H_{i})
and
v\in V(H_{j})
. Since
X
is a nonlocal metric basis of
G\odot H
and
uv\notin E(G\odot H)
, there exists a vertex
w\in X
such that
d_{G\odot H}(u,w)\neq d_{G\odot H}(v,w)
. If
w\in\widetilde{H_{i}}\cup\widetilde{H_{j}}
, then
g_{i}\in Y
or
g_{j}\in Y
and there is nothing to be proved. Suppose henceforth that
w\in\widetilde{H_{k}}
, where
k\neq i,j
. If
w\neq g_{k}
, then

1+d_{G}(g_{i},g_{k})+1=d_{G\odot H}(u,w)\neq d_{G\odot H}(v,w)=1+d_{G}(g_{j},g_{k})+1,

and if
w=g_{k}
, then

\displaystyle 1+d_{G}(g_{i},g_{k})

\displaystyle=1+d_{G}(g_{i},w)

\displaystyle=d_{G\odot H}(u,w)\neq d_{G\odot H}(v,w)

\displaystyle=1+d_{G}(g_{j},w)=1+d_{G}(g_{j},g_{k}).

In either case we get
d_{G}(g_{i},g_{k})\neq d_{G}(g_{j},g_{k})
. As
g_{k}\in Y
, we have proved that
Y
is a resolving set of
G
which in turn implies that

\dim(G)\leq|Y|\leq|X|={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot H)

and we are done.

\square

If
r,s\geq 3
, then it can be checked that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{r,s}\odot K_{n})=r+s-2={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{r,s})=\dim(K_{r,s})
. This shows the sharpness of the lower bound of Theorem
3.3
. On the other hand, if
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(P_{m}\odot K_{n})=2
. Since
\dim(P_{m})=1
and
n(K_{m})=m
, the case
m=2
shows the tightness of the upper bound, while the cases
m\geq 3
demonstrate that the intermediate values are also attainable.

4
Nonlocal metric dimension of wheels

In Theorem
3.2
, the nonlocal metric dimension of a corona product
G\odot H
is reduced to the nonlocal metric dimension of the join of
H
with
K_{1}
. To determine the latter deserves a special attention. Here we solve it for the join of a cycle with
K_{1}
, that is, for wheels. Recall that the wheel graph
K_{1}+C_{n}
of order
n+1\geq 4
is denoted by
W_{1,n}
.

As a consequence of
[
13
, Corollary 5(iv)]
we obtain
{\rm dim_{\ell}}(W_{1,n})=\lceil n/4\rceil
. The metric dimension of
W_{1,n}
was independently studied in
[
4
,
16
]
, where it is proved that if
n\geq 7
, then

\dim(W_{1,n})=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n+2}{5}}\right\rfloor.

Clearly,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,3})=1
and
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,4})={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,5})={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,6})=2
. The main result of this section reads as follows.

Theorem 4.1

If
n\geq 7
, then

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor.

Note that if
n\geq 7
, then
\dim(W_{1,n})={\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})
if and only if
n\bmod 5\in\{0,1,3\}
. Otherwise,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})=\dim(W_{1,n})-1
. In the rest of the section we prove Theorem
4.1
, for which some additional terminology is needed.

Let
V(C_{n})=\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
with natural adjacency. Operation with vertices will be done modulo
n
. Let
v
be the central vertex of the wheel
W_{1,n}
, where
n\geq 7
. Notice first that,
v
does not belong to any nonlocal metric basis, since for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
v
and
i
are adjacent. Let
X\subset V(C_{n})
be a set of vertices such that
|X|\geq 2
. A
gap
of
X
is a set of vertices
A_{i,j}=\{i+1,\dots,j-1\}
,
|i-j|\geq 1
, of
C_{n}
such that
i,j\in X
and
\{i+1,\dots,j-1\}\cap X=\emptyset
. We call
i
and
j

neighboring vertices
of
X
. Two gaps
A_{i,j}
and
A_{j,k}
are called
neighboring gaps
.

Lemma 4.2

Let
n\geq 7
, and let
S
be a nonlocal metric basis of
W_{1,n}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

|A_{i,j}|\leq 4
for each gap of
S
.

(ii)

|A_{i,j}|\geq 3
holds for at most one gap of
S
.

(iii)

If
|A_{i,j}|\geq 2
, then each of the two neighboring gaps of
A_{i,j}
contains at most one vertex.

(iv)

Moreover,
W_{1,n}
contains a nonlocal metric basis
S^{\prime}
that fulfils conditions (i)-(iii) and in addition has no gap of size
3
.

Proof.
(i) Consider the gap
A_{i,j}
and suppose that
j\geq i+6
, where
i\leq n-1
. Then the vertices
i+2
and
i+4
have the same metric representation with respect to
S
. As they are not adjacent, we have a contradiction.

(ii) Suppose there exist two distinct gaps
A_{i,i+k}
and
A_{j,j+k^{\prime}}
, where
k,k^{\prime}\in\{4,5\}
. Then for two non-adjacent vertices
i+2
and
j+2
we have
r(i+2|S)=r(j+2|S)
, a contradiction.

(iii) Let
A_{i,j}
be a gap with
j\geq i+3
. Consider now a neighboring gap, without loss of generality let it be
A_{i^{\prime},i}
, and suppose that
i-i^{\prime}>2
. Then the vertices
i-1
and
i+1
have the same metric representation with respect to
S
. Again, as they are not adjacent, we have a contraction.

(iv) Assume that
S
contains a gap
A_{i,i+4}
. By (ii), this gap is the only gap of size
3
. Moreover, by (iii), there are the following cases to be considered. Assume first that
i+5\notin S
. Then
i+6
must belong to
S
. Let
S^{\prime}=(S\cup\{i+5\})\setminus\{i+4\}
. Having in mind that
i+2
and
i+3
are adjacent, it is straightforward to verify that
S^{\prime}
is a required nonlocal metric basis. The case when
i-1\notin S
is treated analogously. The last case to consider is when
i-1,i+5\in S
. Then
i+6\notin S
and the set
S^{\prime}=(S\cup\{i+6\})\setminus\{i+4\}
is a required nonlocal metric basis.

\square

Lemma 4.3

Let
n\geq 7
. If
X\subseteq V(C_{n})
satisfies conditions (i)-(iii) of Lemma
4.2
, then
X
is a nonlocal resolving set of
W_{1,n}
.

Proof.
Notice that by Proposition
2.1
,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})\geq 2
, for
n\geq 7
. Let
u\in V(W_{1,n})\setminus X
. As the central vertex
v
is adjacent to any other vertex, we may assume that
u\neq v
. We then distinguish three cases.

Case 1
:
u
belongs to a gap
A_{i,i+2}
(gap of size
1
).

Then
u=i+1
and
u
has distance
1
to
i,i+2\in X
and distance
2
to all other vertices of
X
. Hence any other vertex has different metric representation with respect to
X
because
n\geq 7
.

Case 2
:
u
belongs to a gap
A_{i,i+3}
(gap of size
2
).

Let without loss of generality
u=i+1
. Then
u
has distance
1
to
i
and distance
2
to all other vertices of
X
. By condition (iii) from Lemma
4.2
, there is no any other vertex with the same metric representation.

Case 3
:
u
belongs to a gap
A_{i,i+j}
,
j\in\{4,5\}
.

If
u=i+1
, then
u
has distance
1
to
i
and distance
2
to all the other vertices of
X
. Hence, by condition (iii) of Lemma
4.2
, there is no other vertex with the same metric representation. The case when
u=i+j-1
is done similarly. Now, if
u\in\{i+2,i+j-2\}
, then
r(u|X)=(2,\dots,2)
. If
j=4
, then there is only one such vertex, and if
j=5
, there are two such vertices. In the latter case these two vertices are adjacent. By condition (ii) from Lemma
4.2
, no other vertex has this metric representation.

\square

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
4.1
. We construct a set
S\subseteq V(W_{1,n})
with
|S|=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
depending on the residue class modulo
5
.

Case 1
:
n\bmod 5\in\{0,1\}
.

Then
n=5k
or
n=5k+1
, where
k\geq 2
. In both cases let
S=\{5i,5i+2:i\in[k-1]\}\cup\{0,5k-1\}
. Notice that
|S|=2k=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

Case 2
:
n\equiv 2\bmod 5
.

Then
n=5k+2
, where
k\geq 1
. Let
S=\{5i,5i+2:1\leq i\leq k-1\}\cup\{0,5k\}
. Then
|S|=2k=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

Case 3
:
n\bmod 5\in\{3,4\}
.

Then
n=5k+3
or
n=5k+4
, where
k\geq 1
. In both cases let
S=\{5i,5i+2:i\in[k]\}\cup\{0\}
. Then
|S|=2k+1=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

In each case, the set
S
fulfils the conditions of Lemma
4.3
, hence
S
is a nonlocal resolving set of
W_{1,n}
and thus
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})\leq\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

To prove that
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(W_{1,n})\geq\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
, consider an arbitrary nonlocal resolving set
X
of
W_{1,n}
. In view of Lemma
4.2
(iv) we may without loss of generality assume that
X
has no gaps of size
3
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1
:
|X|=2r
, where
r\geq 1
.

By Lemma
4.2
(iii), at most
r
gaps contain two or four vertices, and by Lemma
4.2
(ii), at most one of these gaps contains
4
vertices. Hence, there is at most
2(r-1)+4+r=3r+2
vertices belonging to the gaps. Since
n\leq 2r+(3r+2)
, we have that
|X|=2r\geq 2\left(\frac{n-2}{5}\right)
. Since
2r
is an integer we conclude that
|X|\geq\left\lceil{\frac{2}{5}n-\frac{4}{5}}\right\rceil=\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

Case 2
:
|X|=2r+1
, where
r\geq 1
.

Again, By Lemma
4.2
(iii), at most
r
gaps contain two or four vertices, and by Lemma
4.2
(ii), at most one of these gaps contains
4
vertices. Hence in this case there is at most
2(r-1)+4+(r+1)=3r+3
vertices belonging to the gaps. Since
n\leq 2r+1+(3r+3)
, we have that
|X|=2r+1\geq\left\lceil{\frac{2}{5}n-\frac{3}{5}}\right\rceil\geq\left\lfloor{\frac{2n}{5}}\right\rfloor
.

This proves Theorem
4.1
.

5
Upper bounds

In this section we prove two upper bounds on the nonlocal metric dimension. Along the way we show that complete bipartite graphs can be characterized as the graphs
G
with
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n(G)-2
.

Proposition 5.1

If
G
is a graph, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq n(G)-\omega(G)
. In particular, if
n(G)\geq 2
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq n(G)-2
.

Proof.
Since
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{n})=0
for
n\geq 1
, the result holds for complete graphs. Hence assume in the rest that
G
is not complete and
n(G)\geq 3
.

Let
Q
be a clique of
G
of order
\omega(G)
. Then we claim that
V(G)\setminus V(Q)
is a nonlocal resolving set. Indeed, let
u
and
v
be non-adjacent vertices. At least one of them is not in
Q
, say
u
. Then
d_{G}(u,u)=0<d_{G}(u,v)
and since
u\in V(G)\setminus V(Q)
, the claim is proved. Hence
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq n(G)-n(Q)=n(G)-\omega(G)
. The second assertion,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq n(G)-2
, follows using the same argument except that we consider an arbitrary
K_{2}
as a complete subgraph.

\square

In view of Proposition
5.1
we next describe graphs that attain the bound
n(G)-2
.

Proposition 5.2

If
G
is a graph, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n(G)-2
if and only if
G=K_{s,t}
, where
s\geq 1
and
t\geq 2
.

Proof.
If
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n(G)-2
, then by (
1
) we have
\dim(G)\geq n(G)-2
. Since the only graphs
G
with
\dim(G)=n(G)-1
are complete graphs, we can restrict our attention to the graphs
G
with
\dim(G)=n(G)-2
. In
[
5
]
it is proved that
\dim(G)=n(G)-2
if and only if
G
is one of the following graphs:
K_{s,t}
(
s\geq 1,t\geq 2
),
K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}}
(
s\geq 1,t\geq 2
), and
K_{s}+(K_{1}\cup K_{t})
(
s\geq 1,t\geq 1
). By (
1
) we know that in each of these cases we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq n(G)-2
. Hence we need to determine in which of these cases we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\geq n(G)-2
.

Consider first complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
and let
S
and
T
be its bipartition sets, where
|S|=s
and
|T|=t
. If
u,v\in S
, then
u
and
v
have the same distance to all the other vertices. If follows that for each nonlocal metric basis
X
we have
|X\cap S|=s-1
. Analogously we get
|X\cap T|=t-1
. Hence
|X|=(s-1)+(t-1)=n(K_{s,t})-2
.

Consider next joins
K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}}
,
s\geq 1
,
t\geq 2
. The case
s=1
has already been treated in the above paragraph, so assume that
s,t\geq 2
. Then
\omega(K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}})=s+1\geq 3
, and hence by Proposition
5.1
we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}})\leq n(K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}})-\omega(K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}})<n(K_{s}+\overline{K_{t}})-2
.

The last class of graphs to be considered is
K_{s}+(K_{1}\cup K_{t})
,
s\geq 1
,
t\geq 1
. If
s=t=1
, then
G=P_{3}
which has already been considered earlier. And if
s\geq 2
or
t\geq 2
, then
\omega(G)\geq 3
and we conclude as in the previous case that none of the graphs qualifies for the theorem.

\square

Theorem 5.3

If
G
is a graph of girth at least
7
, then
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)\leq\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
. Moreover, if
G
is a tree, then the equality holds if and only if
G
is obtained from a star by subdividing all but one of the edges at most once.

Proof.
Note first that the assertion is true for
K_{2}
since
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(K_{2})=0
. It is also clear that the result holds for paths since
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(P_{n})=1
for
n\geq 3
. Hence in the rest
n(G)\geq 4
and
\Delta(G)\geq 3
.

Assume first that there exists an edge cover
S
of
G
with
|S|=\beta^{\prime}(G)\geq 2
, such that
S
contains edges
xy
and
xy^{\prime}
, where
y\neq y^{\prime}
. We distinguish two cases.

Case 1
:
\deg_{G}(y)=\deg_{G}(y^{\prime})=1
.

Then
\deg_{G}(x)\geq 3
. We define a set
X\subset V(G)
as follows. Put into
X
all the neighbors of
x
but
y^{\prime}
. Complete the construction of
X
by putting into it an arbitrary vertex from each of the edges
f\in S
which has no vertex yet in
X
. Let
x^{\prime}
be a neighbor of
x
different from
y
and
y^{\prime}
. Since
G
is triangle-free, the vertex
x^{\prime}
is covered either by the edge
xx^{\prime}
, or by an edge
x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime}
, where
x^{\prime\prime}
is not adjacent to
x
. Therefore,
|X|\leq\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
. Note also that
x\notin X
. We claim that
X
is a nonlocal resolving set. For this sake select arbitrary vertices
u,v\notin X
such that
uv\notin E(G)
. If
u\in\{x,y^{\prime}\}
, then because
\deg_{G}(y)=1
, the vertices
u
and
v
are resolved by
y
. Assume next that
u,v\notin\{x,y^{\prime}\}
. Then there exist vertices
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
such that
uu^{\prime},vv^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in X
. We claim that
u
and
v
are resolved by
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
. If this is not the case, then since
d_{G}(u,u^{\prime})=1
we have
d_{G}(v,u^{\prime})=1
, and because
d_{G}(v,v^{\prime})=1
we have
d_{G}(u,v^{\prime})=1
. But this implies that
uu^{\prime}vv^{\prime}u
is a
4
-cycle, a contradiction with our girth assumption.

Case 2
:
\deg_{G}(y)\geq 2
.

Let
y^{\prime\prime}
be a neighbor of
y
different from
x
. Then we infer that
yy^{\prime\prime}\notin S
. Indeed, if
yy^{\prime\prime}\in S
, then
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{xy\}
is an edge cover of
G
of cardinality
\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
. Hence
y^{\prime\prime}
is an end-vertex of some edge
y^{\prime\prime}y^{\prime\prime\prime}\in S
. Define now a set
X\subset V(G)
as follows. First put
x
and
y^{\prime\prime}
into
X
. In addition, for any edge
f\in S\setminus\{xy,xy^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}y^{\prime\prime\prime}\}
put into
X
an arbitrary vertex from
f
. Note that
|X|\leq\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
. We claim that
X
is a nonlocal resolving set. Let
u,v\notin X
such that
uv\notin E(G)
. Then we see that in every case there exist vertices
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
such that
uu^{\prime},vv^{\prime}\in E(G)
and
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in X
. In particular, if
u=y
and
v=y^{\prime}
, then set
u^{\prime}=y^{\prime\prime}
and
v^{\prime}=x
. Similarly, if
u=y
and
v=y^{\prime\prime\prime}
, then select
u^{\prime}=x
and
v^{\prime}=y^{\prime\prime}
. And if
u=y^{\prime}
and
v=y^{\prime\prime\prime}
, then set
u^{\prime}=x
and
v^{\prime}=y^{\prime\prime}
. In any case we see that the vertices
u
,
v
,
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
lead to a
4
-cycle.

Assume second that every edge cover
G
has cardinality
n(G)/2
. Let
S
be such an edge cover. Again we distinguish two cases.

Case 1
: every edge from
S
has an end-vertex of degree
1
.

Let
S=\{x_{i}y_{i}:\ i\in[n(G)/2]\}
and assume without loss of generality that
\deg(x_{i})=1
for all
i\in[n(G)/2]
. Set
X=\{x_{2},\ldots,x_{n(G)/2}\}
. We claim that
X
is a nonlocal resolving set. Let
u
and
v
be arbitrary non-adjacent vertices from
V(G)\setminus X
. Then at least one of
u
or
v
is from
\{y_{2},\ldots,y_{n(G)/2}\}
, say
u=y_{i}
,
i\geq 2
. But then
x_{i}
resolves
u
and
v
, and the claim is proved.

Case 2
: there is an edge
xy\in S
such that
\deg_{G}(x)\geq 2
and
\deg_{G}(y)\geq 2
.

Let
x^{\prime}
be a neighbor of
x
different from
y
, and let
y^{\prime}
be a neighbor of
y
different from
x
. By the assumption on
S
, there exist vertices
x^{\prime\prime}
and
y^{\prime\prime}
such that
x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}\in S
. Let
X\subset V(G)
be the set containing
x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}
, and an arbitrary vertex from each edge
f\in S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{xy,x^{\prime}x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}\}
. We claim that
X
is a nonlocal resolving set. Let
f=ww^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
, where
w^{\prime}\in X
. Then since
d_{G}(w,w^{\prime})=1
and since each of the vertices
x
,
y
,
x^{\prime\prime}
, and
y^{\prime\prime}
has either
x^{\prime}\in X
or
y^{\prime}\in X
as a neighbor, we get that
X
resolves
w
from each of the vertices from
\{x,y,x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\}
. Similarly we infer that the pairs
x^{\prime\prime},y
, and
x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime\prime}
, and
x,y^{\prime\prime}
are resolved by
X
. The only pairs left to be considered are
x,x^{\prime\prime}
and
y,y^{\prime\prime}
. Suppose on the contrary that the pair
x,x^{\prime\prime}
is not resolved by
X
. Since
d_{G}(x,y^{\prime})=2
, we must have
d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},y^{\prime})=2
. Then
y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\in E(G)
, or
y^{\prime\prime}x^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
, or there is a new vertex
z
such that
y^{\prime}z,zx^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
. In either case we have a cycle of length at most
6
in
G
which is not possible. The other pair
y,y^{\prime\prime}
is treated analogously.

Let now
G
be a tree. Let
W
be the set of exterior branch vertices of
T
, and for a vertex
w\in W
, let
p(w)
be the number of terminal paths of
w
. Then we have

\beta^{\prime}(T)\geq\sum_{w\in W}p(w).

On the other hand, by (
2
) and Proposition
2.2
we have

{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(T)=\sum_{w\in W}(p(w)-1).

Hence
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
can hold only when
|W|=1
. Suppose hence that
G
is a tree with only one exterior branch vertex. Then
G
is obtained from a star
K_{1,n}
,
n\geq 3
, by subdividing each of its edges an arbitrary number of times. By (
2
),
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n-1
. Moreover, if at least one edge of
K_{1,n}
has been subdivided at least two times, then
\beta^{\prime}(G)\geq n+1
. Suppose next that each edge of
K_{1,n}
has been subdivided exactly once. Then
\beta^{\prime}(G)=n+1
. So we are left with trees
G
obtained from
K_{1,n}
by subdividing all but one of the edges at most once. In this case we have
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=n-1=\beta^{\prime}(G)-1
.

\square

6
Embeddings

In our final result we prove that any graph can be embedded as an induced subgraph into a supergraph with a small nonlocal metric dimension and small diameter. More precisely, the following holds.

Theorem 6.1

Every connected graph
G
is an induced subgraph of a graph
H
with
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(H)\leq\left\lceil\log(\chi(\overline{G}))\right\rceil
and
{\rm diam}(H)\leq 4
. Moreover, if
2^{k-1}\leq\chi(\overline{G})<2^{k}
, for some integer
k
, then
{\rm diam}(H)\leq 3
.

Proof.
If
\chi(\overline{G})=1
, then
\overline{G}
is an edge-less graph and hence
G
is complete. Then, by definition,
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G)=0
and hence the conclusion holds in this case by embedding the complete graph into itself. We may thus assume in the rest that
\chi(\overline{G})=s\geq 2
. Let
k
be the unique integer with
2^{k-1}<s\leq 2^{k}
.

Let
X_{0},\ldots,X_{s-1}
be the color classes of
\overline{G}
under some optimal coloring of it. By our assumption,
2^{k-1}<s\leq 2^{k}
. Then the sets
X_{0},\ldots,X_{s-1}
respectively induce complete subgraphs of
G
. Since
G
is connected, we may without loss of generality assume that there exists an edge connecting a vertex from
X_{0}
by a vertex from
X_{s-1}
. Construct now a graph
H
as follows. First take the disjoint union of
G
and
K_{k}
. For each
X_{i}
write
i
in its binary representation, say
i=i_{1}\ldots i_{k}
. Then for each
j
such that
i_{j}=0
, add all the edges between the vertex
j\in V(K_{k})
and
X_{i}
. See Fig.
1
for an example of the construction where
G
is the Petersen graph
P
. For it note that
\chi(\overline{P})=5
.

A result for the standard metric dimension which would be parallel to Theorem
6.1
is not possible. The reason is the following. Imagine a connected, non-complete graph
G
and having a color class (in an optimal coloring of it) of order
2^{r}
,
r\geq 2
. Then
G
contains a clique of the same order and hence in whichever graph
H
we embed
G
as an induced subgraph, we have
\omega(H)\geq 2^{r}
. Then
[
5
, Theorem 1]
which asserts that if
G
is a graph with
{\rm diam}(G)=d
, then

, where
f(n,d)
is the least positive integer

for which

, implies that
\dim(H)\geq r
. So
\dim(H)
can be arbitrary larger than
\left\lceil\log(\chi(\overline{G}))\right\rceil
(actually, arbitrary larger than
\chi(\overline{G})
for that matter).

7
Concluding remarks

In this article we have introduced the concept of nonlocal metric dimension, which seems to deserve wider interest, not least because it is in some sense a complementary concept to the established local metric dimension. Many problems about this new concept remain open, let us mention here just a few that follow from our results.

In Theorem
3.2
we have proved a formula for the local metric dimension of corona products with the second factor being not complete. Extending this theorem (as done in
[
10
]
for the local metric dimension) to generalized hierarchical products is open. In view of Theorem
3.3
it would be interesting to prove a formula for
{\rm dim}_{\rm n\ell}(G\odot K_{n})
. Theorem
5.3
is proved for graphs of girth at least
7
. We suspect that the condition can be relaxed to girth at least
5
. Moreover, the edge cover number in triangle-free graphs (that is, of girth at least
4
) is equal to the clique cover number. Hence extending Theorem
5.3
to arbitrary graphs, where the upper bound would be stated as a function of the clique covering number would be of interest.

Quantitative De Giorgi Methods in Kinetic Theory for Non-Local Operators
Amélie Loher

Abstract
. We derive quantitatively the Harnack inequalities for kinetic integro-differential equations. This implies Hölder continuity. Our method is based on trajectories and exploits a term arising due to the non-locality in the energy estimate. This permits to quantitatively prove the intermediate value lemma for the full range of non-locality parameter s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) . Our results recover the results from Imbert and Silvestre [ 22 ] for the inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation in the non-cutoff case. The paper is self-contained.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Problem Formulation

We consider non-local kinetic equations of the form

(1.1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mathcal{L}f+h,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}},~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where we assume
h=h(t,x,v)
is a real scalar field in
L^{\infty}
and for a non-negative measurable kernel
K:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
we define

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}f(t,x,v):=\textrm{PV}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}K(t,x,v,w)\big[f(t,x,w)-f(t,x,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w.

The integral is to be understood in the principal value sense, denoted with
PV
. The question we raise is whether solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfy Hölder continuity and Harnack inequalities. The Weak Harnack inequality and Hölder continuity have been obtained by Cyril Imbert and Luis Silvestre in
[
22
]
, yet part of their proof relies on an argument by contradiction. Our aim is to develop a constructive method to deduce the results.

1.2.
Contribution

Our contribution consists in a quantitative proof of regularity for non-local kinetic equations of type (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Our results are applicable to the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation. We prove Harnack inequalities and Hölder continuity. The technical assumptions that are required for these statements will be discussed below (
1.8
)-(
1.12
).
In short they define a suitable notion of ellipticity on the non-local coefficients of (
1.1
). The analogue statement for local equations requires the coefficients to be uniformly elliptic, that is the eigenvalues of the diffusion matrix lie in
[\lambda,\Lambda]
for given
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.
The parameter
s\in(0,1)
arises from the non-locality of (
1.1
) and is hidden in the conditions on the kernel.

1.3.
Ellipticity Class of
K

We introduce the ellipticity class of the kernel
K
in (
1.2
) as follows.
Even though the kernel depends in general on time and space we will often omit to write out this dependency for the sake of brevity.
We let
s\in(0,1)
,
\bar{R}>0
and
0<\lambda<\Lambda
. Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be supported in
B_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
. Then we assume that
K:[-\bar{R}^{2s},0]\times B_{{\bar{R}}^{1+2s}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,+\infty)
is coercive in the sense that

(1.8)

\lambda\int_{B_{\bar{R}/2}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}/2}}\frac{\left|\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}w\leq\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big(\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\big)^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v,

and

(1.9)

\lambda\int_{B_{\bar{R}/2}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}/2}}\frac{\left|\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\right|}{\left|v-w\right|^{\frac{d+2s}{2}}}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}w\leq\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\right|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

Moreover, we require the following upper bound for
r>0

(1.10)

\forall v\in B_{\bar{R}}\quad\int_{B_{r}(v)}K(v,w)\left|v-w\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\leq\Lambda r^{2-2s}.

Finally, instead of the usual symmetry assumption
K(v,w)=K(w,v)
, which corresponds to the divergence form structure of local equations, we assume the following cancellation

(1.11)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\Bigg|\textrm{PV}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|\leq\Lambda,

and if
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
we assume for all
r>0

(1.12)

\forall v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\quad\Bigg\lvert\textrm{PV}\int_{B_{r}(v)}(v-w)\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\rvert\leq\Lambda\big(1+r^{1-2s}\big).

We remark that the coercivity (
1.8
) together with the cancellation assumption (
1.11
) imply

(1.13)

\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\left|\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}w\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(\varphi(v)-\varphi(w)\big)K(v,w)\varphi(v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v+\Lambda\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2},

where
\varphi
is supported in
B_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
.
Moreover the upper bound (
1.10
) is equivalent to

(1.14)

\displaystyle\forall v\in B_{\bar{R}},\forall r>0\quad\int_{B_{2r}(v)\setminus B_{r}(v)}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\leq\Lambda r^{-2s},

\displaystyle\forall w\in B_{\bar{R}},\forall r>0\quad\int_{B_{\bar{R}}\cap B_{2r}(w)\setminus B_{r}(w)}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}v\leq\Lambda r^{-2s}.

We also note that (
1.12
) holds for
s\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big)
as a consequence of (
1.11
) and (
1.10
). These assumptions are inspired from the work of Imbert and Silvestre
[
22
]
.
In particular it is clear that the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta_{v})^{s}
satisfies (
1.8
)-(
1.12
). The reason why we do not want to restrict the kernel to the fractional Laplacian is so that we are able to apply our result to the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation.

1.4.
Non-cutoff Boltzmann equation

The Boltzmann equation is given by

(1.15)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=Q(f,f),

where the collision operator
Q
has the form

Q(f,g):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\big[f(w_{*})g(w)-f(v_{*})g(v)\big]B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*},

with

w=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma,\quad w_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}{2}\sigma,

and
\cos\theta
is defined as

\cos\theta:=\frac{v-v_{*}}{\left|v-v_{*}\right|}\cdot\sigma,\quad\sin(\theta/2):=\frac{w-v}{\left|w-v\right|}\cdot\sigma.

The cross-section
B
is given by

B(r,\cos\theta)=r^{\gamma}b(\cos\theta),\quad b(\cos\theta)\approx\left|\sin(\theta/2)\right|^{-(d-1)-2s},

with
\gamma\in(-d,1]
and
s\in(0,1)
. A solution to (
1.15
) describes the density of particles at time
t
and location
x
with velocity
v
. The collision operator can be rephrased as

Q(f,g)=\int\big[g(w)-g(v)\big]K_{f}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w+\rm{lower~order~terms},

where the kernel
K_{f}
depends implicitly on the solution
f
and is determined by

(1.16)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(w_{*})\big[g(w)-g(v)\big]B(\left|v-v_{*}\right|,\cos\theta)\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}v_{*}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[g(w)-g(v)\big]K_{f}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w.

We define the following macroscopic quantities associated to (
1.15
)

(1.17)

\displaystyle M(t,x):=\int f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v,

\displaystyle E(t,x):=\int f(t,x,v)\left|v\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}v,

\displaystyle H(t,x):=\int f(t,x,v)\ln f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v.

They describe the mass density, the energy and the entropy respectively. Under the physically meaningful assumption that
M
is uniformly bounded from above and below, and that
E
and
H
are both uniformly bounded above, it can be checked that
K_{f}
satisfies (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) with
\lambda
depending on the mass, energy and entropy of
f
and
\Lambda
depending on the mass, energy and
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
. We refer the interested reader to Section 3 and Lemma A.6 of
[
22
]
. The fact that the Boltzmann kernel satisfies (
1.9
) can be demonstrated in the same way as
[
22
, Lemma A.6]
and is stated in
A
below. Therefore Theorem
1.2
implies

Theorem 1.5

(Imbert-Silvestre)
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(-d,1]
be such that
\gamma+2s\leq 2
. Let
f
be a non-negative solution to the Boltzmann equation (
1.15
) for
(t,x,v)\in(-3,0]\times B_{1}\times B_{1}
. Assume that
f
is essentially bounded in
(-3,0]\times B_{1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and that there exists
M_{0},M_{1},E_{1}
such that
M_{0}\leq M(t,x)\leq M_{1},~E(t,x)\leq E_{1}
for all
(t,x)\in(-3,0]\times B_{1}
. Then
f
is Hölder continuous in
Q_{1/2}:=\big(-(1/2)^{2s},0\big]\times B_{(1/2)^{1+2s}}\times B_{1/2}
and for
\alpha\in(0,1)
there holds

[f]_{C^{\alpha}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C,

where
C
depends on
s,d,\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}},M_{0},M_{1},E_{1}
.

Remark 1.6
.

The entropy
H
satisfies a uniform bound from above in terms of
M_{1},E_{1}
and
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Thus we do not require the additional assumption
H\leq H_{1}
in Theorem
1.5
.

1.5.
Invariant transformations

Let
f
solve (
1.1
). Then for
r\in[0,1]
the scaled function
f_{r}(t,x,v)=f(r^{2s}t,r^{1+2s}x,rv)
satisfies

\partial_{t}f_{r}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{r}=\mathcal{L}_{r}f_{r}+h_{r},

where
\mathcal{L}_{r}
is the non-local operator associated to the scaled kernel

K_{r}(t,x,v,w)=r^{d+2s}K(r^{2s}t,r^{1+2s}x,rv,rw),

and the source is scaled as

h_{r}(t,x,v)=r^{2s}h(r^{2s}t,r^{1+2s}x,rv).

For
r\in[0,1]
the scaled kernel
K_{r}
satisfies (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) in the larger radius
\frac{\bar{R}}{r}
instead of
\bar{R}
. Moreover,
\left\|h_{r}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq r^{2s}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}
.

Furthermore, the equation is invariant under Galilean transformations
z\to z_{0}\circ z=(t_{0}+t,x_{0}+x+tv_{0},v_{0}+v)
with
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2d}
. If
f
is a solution of (
1.1
) then its Galilean transformation
f_{z_{0}}(z)=f(z_{0}\circ z)
solves

\partial_{t}f_{z_{0}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{z_{0}}=\mathcal{L}_{z_{0}}f_{z_{0}}+h_{z_{0}},

where
\mathcal{L}_{z_{0}}
is the non-local operator associated to the translated kernel

K_{z_{0}}(t,x,v,w)=K(z_{0}\circ z,v_{0}+w),

and with source

h_{z_{0}}(t,x,v)=h(z_{0}\circ z).

Again the modified kernel
K_{z_{0}}
satisfies (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) provided that
K
does.

In view of these invariances we introduce kinetic cylinders

Q_{r}(z_{0}):=\Big\{(t,x,v):-r^{2s}\leq t-t_{0}\leq 0,\left|v-v_{0}\right|<r,\left|x-x_{0}-(t-t_{0})v_{0}\right|<r^{1+2s}\Big\},

for
r>0
and
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+2d}
. For later reference, we also introduce the cylinder shifted to the past

Q^{-}_{r}(z_{0}):=Q_{r}\big(z_{0}-(2r^{2s},2r^{2s}v_{0},0)\big),

so that in particular for
z_{0}=0

Q^{-}_{r}:=Q_{r}\big(-2r^{2s},0,0\big)=\big(-3r^{2s},-2r^{2s}\big]\times B_{r^{1+2s}}\times B_{r}.

Similarly the cylinder shifted to the future is denoted as

Q^{+}_{r}(z_{0}):=Q_{r}\big(z_{0}+(2r^{2s},2r^{2s}v_{0},0)\big).

Finally, if we merely use the space and velocity domain of a cylinder, we denote it as
Q^{t}_{r}:=B_{r^{1+2s}}\times B_{r}
where
t\in\mathcal{I}
for some specified interval
\mathcal{I}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.

1.6.
Further remarks

Our equation involves a transport term, which transfers some regularity of the velocity variable to the space variable. It also involves a non-local diffusion in the
v
variable.
Our motivation to study the regularity of this type of equation is linked to the question of well-posedness for smooth classical solutions of the inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation without cut-off. There are linear kinetic equations whose solutions in the hydrodynamic limit are described by a fractional diffusion
[
29
,
30
,
4
]
. Indeed diffusion limits of the linear Boltzmann equation with a heavy-tailed distribution of infinite variance as an equilibrium distribution give rise to a fractional diffusion equation
[
30
]
. Such heavy-tailed distribution functions arise in astrophysical plasmas
[
31
]
or also in granular gases through dissipative collision mechanisms
[
12
]
. However, the only source of fractional diffusion at the kinetic level stems from long range interactions of the Boltzmann collision kernel.

In the limit case
s\to 1
, equation (
1.1
) models the local kinetic Fokker-Planck equation, whose study is motivated by applications to the Landau equation
[
16
]
. For the local case, there is a non-constructive method discussed in
[
16
]
. A constructive proof first appeared in a series of works
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
for ultraparabolic equations that has further been developed in
[
18
]
to local kinetic Fokker-Planck type equations. The construction is based on a Poincaré-type inequality and Kruzhkov’s method
[
28
]
. A novel constructive approach for local kinetic Fokker-Planck type equations has been devised by Guerand and Mouhot in
[
19
]
. Their method relies on trajectories. For general
s\in(0,1)
, Cyril Imbert and Luis Silvestre (together with Clément Mouhot in
[
20
,
21
]
) made important contributions in a series of papers
[
35
,
22
,
24
,
20
,
21
]
that culminated in the final work
[
23
]
. In
[
35
]
, Silvestre proves for a certain range of
s
that any solution
f
is a priori essentially bounded provided that the mass, energy and entropy defined above (
1.17
) satisfy uniform bounds from above and that the mass is bounded below.
With an additional non-degeneracy assumption in
[
22
]
, they obtain the Weak Harnack inequality with a quantitative argument when
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Note that their method, which uses barrier functions, can be extended to
s\in(0,1)
under the additional symmetry assumption
K(v,v+w)=K(v,v-w)
, cf. section 7 in
[
22
]
. When
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
they prove an Intermediate Value Lemma using an argument by contradiction. In
[
24
]
, Imbert and Silvestre derive Schauder estimates for kinetic equations, which can then be bootstrapped for the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation to obtain smooth solutions
[
23
]
.
Let us also mention a work by Stokols
[
36
]
, which combines the method of
[
16
]
with fractional estimates from
[
5
]
to obtain a non-constructive proof of Hölder continuity in the non-local case. The assumptions on the kernel in
[
36
]
are stronger than ours: the kernel is assumed to satisfy pointwise upper and lower bounds in
(v,w)
, as well as the symmetry conditions
K(v,w)=K(w,v)
and
K(v,v+w)=K(v,v-w)
. We need more general assumptions to be able to apply our results to the Boltzmann equation.

The paper presents a constructive proof of Hölder continuity and Harnack inequalities using a De Giorgi-type argument. De Giorgi methods were originally established for non-linear elliptic equations by De Giorgi
[
15
]
. Moser then showed how to deduce a Harnack inequality
[
32
,
33
]
. The Harnack inequalities are local regularity results. In particular, the Weak Harnack inequality implies Hölder continuity
[
14
]
. By using an argument based on trajectories, we derive the Intermediate Value Lemma, which in turn implies Hölder continuity for the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation. In order to cover the full range of non-locality parameter
s\in(0,1)
we have to carefully exploit the cross-term arising in the energy estimate similar to Section 4 in
[
5
]
. This is a purely non-local effect. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first Intermediate Value Lemma for non-local kinetic equation covering the full range
s\in(0,1)
.

The Not-so-Strong Harnack inequality is obtained by combining the Weak Harnack inequality with De Giorgi’s first lemma. The Strong Harnack inequality for non-local equations has been discussed in
[
6
,
7
,
9
]
for the elliptic case and in
[
3
,
10
,
11
,
26
]
for the parabolic case. Most of these works consider non-local operators with symmetric kernels that verify a pointwise upper and lower bound. In general the Strong Harnack inequality fails for non-local equations, if the function is not assumed to be non-negative
[
25
]
. Instead one gets an additional term from the tail of the negative part of the function on the right hand side
[
26
]
. The parabolic Harnack inequality without tail term has so far only been proved using probabilistic tools such as heat kernel estimates
[
10
,
11
]
. There are no analogous results in the hypo-elliptic case. In
[
27
]
, they prove the Harnack inequality with a tail term on the right hand side for non-local parabolic equations. Under certain assumptions
[
27
, Conditions (a) and (b) on p. 47]
the non-local tail term can be bounded by a local quantity, which could then be absorbed on the left hand side of the inequality via a standard covering argument, yielding the Strong Harnack inequality. However, these additional assumptions do not naturally follow. But from the Weak Harnack inequality it is a straight-forward consequence to prove a weaker non-linear Strong Harnack inequality for essentially bounded solutions in the sense of Theorem
1.3
.

1.7.
Structure of the article

The core of the proof relies on the following method:

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{r@{\mskip\thickmuskip}l}\textrm{Energy estimate}\mskip 5.0mu plus 5.0mu&\xrightarrow[0\leq f\leq 1]{}\textrm{First De Giorgi Lemma}\\
\textrm{Weak Poincaré inequality}\mskip 5.0mu plus 5.0mu&\xrightarrow[0\leq f\leq 1]{}\textrm{Second De Giorgi Lemma}\\
\end{array}\right\}\quad\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt}\textrm{Weak Harnack}.

Note that in the final statement we require our function to be essentially bounded for
v
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The reason behind this is to be able to take care of the tails of the non-local operator. The energy estimate and the weak Poincaré inequality hold for any sub-solution of the equation, whereas for the First and Second De Giorgi Lemma we need to assume that
f\leq 1
almost everywhere.

In Section
2
we fix the notation, discuss definitions of weak solutions for (
1.1
) and state a result of boundedness in
H^{s}\times H^{s}
of the bilinear form (
2.1
).

For De Giorgi’s First Lemma we prove an energy estimate in Section
3
and gain integrability in Lebesgue spaces using estimates on the fundamental solution of the fractional Kolmogorov equation. We deduce De Giorgi’s First Lemma
4.1
by a classical
De Giorgi iteration
in Section
4
.

De Giorgi’s Second Lemma, Theorem
1.4
, is proved in Section
5
by first proving a weak Poincaré inequality in
L^{1}
using the trajectorial approach of
[
19
]
. The difficulties that arise for small values of
s
are dealt with by introducing suitable level functions inspired from
[
5
]
.

We deduce Hölder continuity, Theorem
1.2
, and the Harnack inequalities, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
, in Section
6
. Hölder continuity follows by standard arguments either from the Weak Harnack inequality or by the measure-to-pointwise estimate. To prove the Harnack inequalities, we use a covering argument, which we adapt from
[
19
]
. The geometric construction for the covering had to account for the specific scaling of the fractional diffusion. Once we have obtained the Weak Harnack inequality, we combine it with the First De Giorgi Lemma to deduce the non-linear Strong Harnack inequality.

2.
Weak Formulation

2.1.
Notation

A constant is called universal, if it only depends on the dimension, the fractional exponent
s
and
\lambda,\Lambda
in (
1.8
)-(
1.12
). We use the notation
a\lesssim b
if there exists a universal constant
C
such that
a\leq Cb
, and
a\approx b
if
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
. Moreover, we say that
a\lesssim_{d}b
if
a\leq Cb
where
C=C(d)
. For a real number
a
we denote
a_{+}=\sup(a,0)\geq 0
and
a_{-}=\inf(a,0)\leq 0
.

For a given domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we denote with
\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)
the space that is equipped with the norm

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|f(v)-f(w)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

The space
H^{s}(\Omega)
is correspondingly equipped with the norm

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}(\Omega)}:=\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\Omega)}+\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The space
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is defined as the closure of the space of smooth functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support contained in
\Omega
, where the closure is taken with respect to the
H^{s}(\Omega)
norm. We denote the dual of
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
with
H^{-s}(\Omega)
.

Finally the transport operator is denoted by

\mathcal{T}:=\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}.

2.2.
Bilinear Form

We introduce the bilinear form associated to the operator
\mathcal{L}
in (
1.2
) for
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big(\varphi,g\big)(t,x)

\displaystyle:=-\int\big(\mathcal{L}\varphi\big)(t,x,v)g(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int\int_{\left|v-w\right|>\varepsilon}K(t,x,v,w)\big[\varphi(t,x,v)-\varphi(t,x,w)\big]g(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

In the sequel, we will abuse notation by ignoring the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and understanding some integrals in the principal value sense. Moreover, even though
K
can depend on time and space in general, we will subsequently often omit to write out this dependency explicitly. The following theorem has first been proved in
[
22
, Corollary 5.2]
. We give a different proof for the anti-symmetric part of the operator.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\bar{R}>0
,
\Lambda>0
and
K:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an non-negative kernel satisfying (
1.10
), (
1.11
) and (
1.12
). Then for
f,g\in H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
supported in
B_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
there holds

\mathcal{E}(f,g)\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},

where
C
depends on
s,d
and
\Lambda
.

Proof.

By density of
C^{\infty}
in Sobolev spaces it suffices to consider smooth
f,g
. We divide the proof into the symmetric and anti-symmetric part of the kernel: we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(v)-f(w)\big)\big(g(v)-g(w)\big)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v}_{=:I_{1}(f,g)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(f(v)-f(w)\big)\big(g(v)+g(w)\big)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v}_{=:I_{2}(f,g)},

where the integrals are understood in a principal value sense.

For
I_{1}
we use Young’s inequality and we dyadically decompose the domain of integration which gives for
h=f
or
h=g

(2.2)

I_{1}(h,h)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{+\infty}P(2^{k}),

where for
r>0

P(r):=\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\left|h(v)-h(w)\right|^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v,

with
\Sigma_{r}=\big\{(v,w)\in B_{\bar{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:r\leq\left|v-w\right|<2r\big\}
.
Let
m=\frac{v+w}{2}
and consider an intermediate point
u\in B_{\frac{r}{4}}(m)
. We use
\left|h(v)-h(w)\right|^{2}\leq 2\left|h(v)-h(u)\right|^{2}+2\left|h(u)-h(w)\right|^{2}
to bound

\displaystyle P(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{4}}(m)}K(v,w)\Big(\left|h(v)-h(u)\right|^{2}+\left|h(u)-h(w)\right|^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\left|h(v)-h(u)\right|^{2}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{v,u}}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\left|h(u)-h(w)\right|^{2}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{w,u}}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}v\Bigg)\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}w,

where we used Fubini’s theorem and write
\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}:=\big\{(v,w)\in B_{\bar{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\frac{r}{4}\leq\left|v-w\right|<\frac{5r}{4}\big\}
and
\Omega_{v,w}
for the set containing the
u
corresponding to any pair
(v,w)
. We note that
\Omega_{v,w}\subset B_{2r}(v)\setminus B_{r}(v)
. Using (
1.14
) we get

\displaystyle P(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\Lambda}{r^{d+2s}}\Bigg(\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\left|h(v)-h(u)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\left|h(u)-h(w)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\Bigg(\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\frac{\left|h(v)-h(u)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-u\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\tilde{\Sigma}_{r}}\frac{\left|h(u)-h(w)\right|^{2}}{\left|u-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}u\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}.

We can apply this final estimate to each term in the sum (
2.2
) and use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain

(2.3)

I_{1}(f,g)\lesssim\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

For
I_{2}
we distinguish the far and near part for
0<R<\frac{\bar{R}}{2}
to be determined below

(2.4)

\displaystyle I_{2}(f,g)=\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}\cap\left|v-w\right|<R}\dots}_{=:I_{21}(f,g)}+\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{\left|v-w\right|>R}\dots}_{=:I_{22}(f,g)}.

We rewrite
I_{21}
with Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle I_{21}(f,g)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}\times B_{\bar{R}}}\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)f(v)g(v)\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v}_{=:I_{211}(f,g)}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}\times B_{\bar{R}}}\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)f(v)g(w)\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v}_{=:I_{212}(f,g)}.

Then for
I_{211}
we use the cancellation assumption (
1.11
) and (
1.12
). We get

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{211}(f,g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg|\textrm{PV}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|\left|f(v)g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|f(v)g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}.

For
I_{212}
we use Taylor’s theorem to write

(2.6)

\displaystyle I_{212}(f,g)=\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}f(v)

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[g(v)+\nabla g(v)\cdot(w-v)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(w-v)^{T}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigg)(w-v)\Bigg]\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

We can bound the first term as above in (
2.5
). For the second term, in case that
s\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big)
, we use (
1.10
) so that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg\lvert\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]

\displaystyle\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}(w-v)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\rvert\left|f(v)\nabla g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda R^{1-2s}\int_{B_{R}}\left|f(v)\nabla g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda R^{1-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda R^{1-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{s}.

We used the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and chose
R^{2}=\frac{\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}}{\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}}}
.
Else if
s\in\big[1/2,1)
, we use (
1.12
), so that with this choice of
R
we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg\lvert\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]

\displaystyle\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}(w-v)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\rvert\left|f(v)\nabla g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda\big(1+R^{1-2s}\big)\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|f(v)\nabla g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\big(1+R^{1-2s}\big)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\big(1+R^{1-2s}\big)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\Big(\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{s}\Big).

For the last term in (
2.6
), we have with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
1.10
)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}f(v)(w-v)^{T}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigg)(w-v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg|\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|^{2}\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg|\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|

\displaystyle\times\Bigg|\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\Bigg|\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigg|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda R^{1-s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
\tau\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
we perform the change of variable
v\to\tau w+(1-\tau)v
, whereas for
\tau\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
we perform the change of variable
w\to\tau w+(1-\tau)v
. We get in the former case with (
1.10
) and Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}

\displaystyle\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|K(\tilde{v},w)-K(w,\tilde{v})\right|\chi_{\left|\tilde{v}-w\right|<R}\left|w-\tilde{v}\right|^{2}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tilde{v})\right|^{2}(1-\tau)^{-3}\,{\rm d}\tilde{v}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tilde{R}})},

where
0<\tilde{R}
is chosen such that
B_{(1-\tau)\bar{R}}(\tau w)\subset B_{\tilde{R}}
for all
w\in B_{\bar{R}}
and
\tau\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
. In the latter case we have similarly

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}

\displaystyle\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\left|K(v,\tilde{w})-K(\tilde{w},v)\right|\chi_{\left|v-\tilde{w}\right|<R}\left|\tilde{w}-v\right|^{2}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tilde{w})\right|^{2}\tau^{-3}\,{\rm d}\tilde{w}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tilde{R}})},

when we choose
0<\tilde{R}
such that
B_{\tau\bar{R}}((1-\tau)v)\subset B_{\tilde{R}}
for all
v\in B_{\bar{R}}
. Thus we get overall

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\chi_{\left|v-w\right|<R}

\displaystyle f(v)(w-v)^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau(w-v)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda^{2}R^{2-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tilde{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{s}.

Therefore we have obtained

(2.7)

\displaystyle I_{21}(f,g)\lesssim_{\Lambda}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}^{s}.

For the far part
I_{22}
in (
2.4
) we apply Taylor’s theorem

\displaystyle I_{22}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}\big[f(v)-f(w)\big]\big[g(v)+g(w)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\big[f(v)-f(w)\big]\Bigg[2g(v)+\nabla g(v)\cdot(w-v)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(w-v)^{T}\cdot\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\Bigg)\cdot(w-v)\Bigg]\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

The trickiest term to estimate is the last one. We get with Fubini and Cauchy-Schwarz

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\big[f(v)-f(w)\big]\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|^{2}\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{\bar{R}})}\Bigg\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigg|\int_{B_{\bar{R}}\setminus B_{R}(w)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}v\Bigg|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda R^{1-s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\Lambda R^{1-s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{\bar{R}})}\Bigg\{\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

With the same change of variables as above we find for
\tilde{R}
sufficiently large such that
B_{(1-\tau)\bar{R}}(\tau w)\subset B_{\tilde{R}}
for all
w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)
and
\tau\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(\tilde{v})}K(\tilde{v},w)\left|w-\tilde{v}\right|^{2}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tilde{v})\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}\tilde{v}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Similarly

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\left|w-v\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{\tilde{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,\tilde{w})\left|\tilde{w}-v\right|^{2}\left|\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tilde{w})\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tilde{w}\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}.

Thus with our choice of
R
we get

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)

\displaystyle\big[f(v)-f(w)\big]\left|w-v\right|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{D}^{2}g(\tau w+(1-\tau)v)\,{\rm d}\tau\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{s}.

Overall we find

\displaystyle I_{22}(f,g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}\big(f(v)-f(w)\big)^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}g(v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{\bar{R}}}\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}\big(f(v)-f(w)\big)^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(v)}\left|w-v\right|^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla g(v)\right|\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+C\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Lambda R^{-s}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\Lambda R^{1-s}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda R^{-s}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}+\Lambda R^{1-s}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Lambda R^{2-2s}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|\textrm{D}^{2}g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{s}{2}}+\Lambda\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1-s}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{s}.

We used the same proof as for the symmetric part to deduce the third inequality, repeatedly the Cauchy Schwarz inequality, the upper bound (
1.10
), the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and the choice of
R
as above.

Now we use Littlewood-Paley theory inspired from the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
22
]
. We denote with
\Delta_{i}
the Littlewood-Paley projectors defined by
\Delta_{i}f(z)=\int_{\xi:2^{i}<\left|\xi\right|<2^{i+1}}\hat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot z}\,{\rm d}\xi
where

is the Fourier transform of

. We decompose

, using the convention that all low modes are contained in
\Delta_{0}
so that the index

. Note that for

\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\approx 2^{is}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, we bound as in

]
(for a justification see

]
)

\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{H^{2}}^{s}\lesssim 2^{si}\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{H^{s}}.

and

\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{s}{2}}\lesssim\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{H^{s}}.

Then

(2.9)

\displaystyle I_{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}I_{2}(\Delta_{i}f,\Delta_{j}g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\leq i}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{H^{2}}^{s}+\sum_{i<j}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{2}}^{s}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\Big(\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|^{1-\frac{s}{2}}_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|^{\frac{s}{2}}_{H^{2}}+\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i,j}\Big(2^{-s\left|i-j\right|}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\Delta_{j}g\right\|_{H^{s}}\Big)+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-sk}\Bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\Delta_{i+k}g\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|\Delta_{i+k}f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|_{H^{s}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{1-sk}\Bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left\|\Delta_{i}f\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left\|\Delta_{i}g\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s}}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s}}.

Note that splitting the sum into
j\leq i
and
i<j
works since the adjoints of the corresponding integral operators satisfy the same bounds as in (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) due to the cancellation assumptions. Thus we conclude with (
2.3
) and (
2.9
).
∎

\displaystyle=\int_{\textrm{supp }\varphi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{\delta}(v)}f_{1}(w)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\varphi(v)\,{\rm d}v,

3.1.
Kolmogorov’s fundamental solutions

In this subsection, we consider the fractional Kolmogorov equation given by

(3.1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+(-\Delta)^{s}f=h-m,

for some
h\in L^{2}\big([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},H^{-s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\big)
and some non-negative measure
m\in M^{1}\big([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\big)
with finite mass. Then there exists
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{2}\big([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\big)
so that
h=h_{1}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}h_{2}
and

\displaystyle\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},H^{-s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\approx\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
0\leq f
solve (
3.1
) in
[-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
for
h=h_{1}+(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}h_{2}
with
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
,
0\leq m\in M^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
and with
f(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v)\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
. Then

(3.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})},

where
2\leq p<2+\frac{2s}{d(1+s)}
.
Moreover, for
\sigma\in\left[0,\frac{s}{1+2s}\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,v}W_{x}^{\sigma,1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle+\left\|m\right\|_{M^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Proof.

Equation (
3.1
) admits a fundamental solution, see for example Theorem 1.1 in
[
34
]
or Section 2.4 in
[
22
]
, given by

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d+1}}\big[h(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},v^{\prime})-m(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\big]J\left(t-t^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}-(t-t^{\prime})v^{\prime},v-v^{\prime}\right)\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f_{0}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})J\left(t,x-x^{\prime}-(t-t^{\prime})v^{\prime},v-v^{\prime}\right)\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime},

where
(t,x,v)\in(0,\tau)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
and

J(t,x,v)=\frac{C_{d}}{t^{d+\frac{d}{s}}}\mathcal{J}\left(\frac{x}{t^{1+\frac{1}{2s}}},\frac{v}{t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}\right),\qquad\hat{\mathcal{J}}(\varphi,\xi)=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{1}\left|\xi-\tau\varphi\right|^{2s}\,{\rm d}\tau\right).

Since
m
and
J
are non-negative, we deduce that

\displaystyle 0\leq f(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d+1}}h(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},v^{\prime})J\left(t-t^{\prime},x-x^{\prime}-(t-t^{\prime})v^{\prime},v-v^{\prime}\right)\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f_{0}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})J\left(t,x-x^{\prime}-(t-t^{\prime})v^{\prime},v-v^{\prime}\right)\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}.

We remark that for any
r\geq 1
there holds for

\displaystyle\left\|J(t,\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}=t^{-d(1+\frac{1}{s})(1-\frac{1}{r})}\left\|\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})},

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}J(t,\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}=t^{-d(1+\frac{1}{s})(1-\frac{1}{r})-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

In particular for
r=p^{*}:=\frac{2d(1+s)+2s}{2d(1+s)+s}\in(1,2)
we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|J(t,\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}=t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left\|\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})},

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}J(t,\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}=t^{-\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{p^{*}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

We define as in Section 2.4 of
[
22

the modified convolution

\displaystyle f*_{t}g(x,v):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}f(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})g(x-x^{\prime}-tv^{\prime},v-v^{\prime})\,{\rm d}x^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}.

We remark that the modified convolution satisfies the usual Young inequality independent of

\displaystyle\left\|f*_{t}g\right\|_{L^{r}_{x,v}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}_{x,v}}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q}_{x,v}}

for

. Following the proof of Proposition 2.2 in

, we split

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|h_{1}(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\left\|\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}(t-t^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\,{\rm d}t^{\prime},

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|h_{2}(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}J(t-t^{\prime},\cdot,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|h_{2}(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}_{v}\mathcal{J}\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}(t-t^{\prime})^{-\alpha}\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Since

f_{1}
and

\displaystyle\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\leq C\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\tau^{\frac{1}{q}-\alpha}.

This implies (
3.2
).

) we follow the idea of Lemma 10 in

\displaystyle+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle+\left\|m\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\big).

The decomposition above holds for all
\varepsilon>0
; thus we can conclude the proof with the same justification as in the proof of Lemma 10 in
[
19
]
: Let
\sigma\in\big[0,\frac{s}{1+2s}\big)
. Using the notation
\langle\zeta\rangle:=(1+\left|\zeta\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
we can decompose

(3.4)

\displaystyle(1-\Delta_{x})^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}f(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}\langle\zeta\rangle^{\sigma}f(t,\varphi,v)\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}a_{k}(\zeta)f(t,\varphi,v)\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}b_{k}(\zeta)(1-\Delta_{\varphi})^{\frac{l}{2}}f(t,\varphi,v)\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta,

where
a_{k}(\zeta):=\langle\zeta\rangle^{\sigma}\phi_{k}
and
b_{k}(\zeta):=\langle\zeta\rangle^{\sigma-l}\phi_{k}
with
\phi_{k}(\zeta):=[\eta(2^{-k}\zeta)-\eta(2^{-k+1}\zeta)]
for
k\geq 0
where
\eta
is a smooth function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta=1
in
B_{1}(0)
and
0
outside
B_{2}(0)
. For
k=-1
we define
\phi_{-1}(\zeta):=\sum_{k<-1}[\eta(2^{-k}\zeta)-\eta(2^{-k+1}\zeta)]
. Note that for a given function
F=F(\varphi)
there holds

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Big\lvert\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}a_{k}(\zeta)F(y)\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta\Big\rvert\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k\sigma}\left\|F\right\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Big\lvert\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}b_{k}(\zeta)(1-\Delta_{\varphi})^{\frac{l}{2}}F(y)\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta\Big\rvert\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(\sigma-l)}\left\|F\right\|_{W^{l,1}}.

Therefore we find for the solution
f
to the fractional Kolmogorov equation

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,v}W^{\sigma,1}_{x}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle=\left\|(1-\Delta)_{x}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Bigg\lvert\sum_{k\geq-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Big[e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}a_{k}(\zeta)f_{\varepsilon}(t,\varphi,v)

\displaystyle+e^{i\zeta\cdot(x-\varphi)}b_{k}(\zeta)(1-\Delta_{\varphi})^{\frac{l}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}^{\perp}(t,\varphi,v)\Big]\,{\rm d}\varphi\,{\rm d}\zeta\Bigg\rvert\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq-1}\tau\left(2^{k\sigma}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2s}}+2^{k(\sigma-l)}\varepsilon^{-l(1+\frac{1}{2s})-\frac{1}{2s}}\right)\left(\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|m\right\|_{M^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\tau}{\delta}\Big(\left\|h_{1}\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|m\right\|_{M^{1}([-\tau,0]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\Big),

where we choose
\sigma=\frac{s}{1+2s}-\delta\in\big[0,\frac{s}{1+2s}\big)
,
\varepsilon=2^{-2ks(\frac{s}{1+2s}-\frac{\delta}{2})}
and
l>\frac{4s-3\delta(1+2s)}{(1+2s)(\delta(1+2s)+2-2s)}
for some small
\delta>0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Energy estimates

The following two lemmas are used to extract information from the equation.

Lemma 3.2

(Local energy estimate)
.

Let
f
be a non-negative sub-solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
Q_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
for some
\bar{R}\geq 1
where
0\leq K
satisfies (
1.8
)-(
1.12
). Let
Q_{r}(z_{0})\subset Q_{R}(z_{0})\subset Q_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
with
0<r<R<\min\Big\{1,\frac{\bar{R}}{2}\Big\}
be given. Assume
0\leq f\leq 1
a.e. in
\big(-R^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}\big]\times B_{R^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+t_{0}v_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0})}

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{\tau}_{r}(z_{0})}f^{2}(\tau,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(z_{0})}\frac{\left|f(w)-f(v)\right|^{2}}{\left|v-w\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\lambda,\Lambda,v_{0}}\int_{Q^{-r^{2s}+t_{0}}_{R}(z_{0})}f_{0}^{2}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+(R-r)^{-2}\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|+\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z,

where
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})
,
f_{0}(x,v)=f\left(-r^{2s}+t_{0},x,v\right)
and

Q^{\tau}_{r}(z_{0})=\left\{(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}:(\tau,x,v)\in Q_{r}(z_{0})\right\}.

Proof.

For
t\in\big(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}\big]
we let
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
be a cut-off function with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
, such that
\psi=0
in
Q_{r}^{t}(z_{0})
and
\psi=1
outside
Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})
.
We test (
1.1
) with the truncated function
(f-\psi)_{+}=\sup\big(0,f-\psi\big)
. We notice that

f=(f-\psi)_{+}+(f-\psi)_{-}+\psi
and
\nabla_{x}(f-\psi)^{2}_{+}=2(f-\psi)_{+}\big(\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}\psi\big)
. This yields for a.e.
t\in(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}h(f-\psi)_{+}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\mathcal{T}f(f-\psi)_{+}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(f,(f-\psi)_{+})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\,{\rm d}}{\,{\rm d}t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\mathcal{(}f-\psi)_{+}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{E}((f-\psi)_{+},(f-\psi)_{+})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{E}((f-\psi)_{-},(f-\psi)_{+})\,{\rm d}x+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(f-\psi)_{+}\mathcal{T}\psi\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v.

Due to the coercivity of
K
(
1.13
) we know that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big((f-\psi)_{+},(f-\psi)_{+}\big)+\Lambda\left\|(f-\psi)_{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\lambda\left\|(f-\psi)_{+}\right\|_{H^{s}_{v}}^{2}.

Moreover we notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\big((f-\psi)_{-},(f-\psi)_{+}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[(f-\psi)_{-}(v)-(f-\psi)_{-}(w)\big](f-\psi)_{+}(v)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f-\psi)_{-}(w)(f-\psi)_{+}(v)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v\geq 0,

since
(f-\psi)_{+}\cdot(f-\psi)_{-}=0
. This term will be fully exploited for the Second De Giorgi Lemma, Theorem
1.4
. Finally to bound
\mathcal{E}(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+})
we distinguish the symmetric and the skew-symmetric part

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}\big(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+}\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[(f-\psi)_{+}(v)-(f-\psi)_{+}(w)\big]\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[(f-\psi)_{+}(v)+(f-\psi)_{+}(w)\big]\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v.

Realising that
\left|g_{+}(v)-g_{+}(w)\right|\leq\big(\chi_{\{g>0\}}(v)+\chi_{\{g>0\}}(w)\big)\left|g_{+}(v)-g_{+}(w)\right|
we find

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{sym}}\big(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+}\big)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[(f-\psi)_{+}(v)-(f-\psi)_{+}(w)\big]\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{sym}}\big((f-\psi)_{+},(f-\psi)_{+}\big)

\displaystyle+\int\int\big[\chi_{\{f-\psi>0\}}(v)+\chi_{\{f-\psi>0\}}(w)\big]\left|\psi(w)-\psi(v)\right|^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|(f-\psi)_{+}\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{1}_{v}}^{2}\int\chi_{\{f-\psi>0\}}(v)\,{\rm d}v,

where we used Theorem
2.1
and the upper bound (
1.14
). For the skew-symmetric part we have

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{skew}}

\displaystyle(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big[(f-\psi)_{+}(v)+(f-\psi)_{+}(w)\big]\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int\int\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]\big[(f-\psi)_{+}(v)+(f-\psi)_{+}(w)\big]\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\int\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big](f-\psi)_{+}(v)\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\Bigg(\int\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)\,{\rm d}v.

We estimate

\displaystyle\textrm{PV}\int\big[\psi(w)-\psi(v)\big]

\displaystyle\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w=\int_{B_{r}(v)}\dots+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(v)}\dots

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{r}(v)}(w-v)\cdot\nabla_{v}\psi(v)\big[K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big]\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{r}(v)}\left|w-v\right|^{2}\left\|D_{v}^{2}\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\,{\rm d}w+\Lambda r^{-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda r^{1-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{1}_{v}}+\Lambda r^{2-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{2}_{v}}+\Lambda r^{-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

For the tail we used the upper bound (
1.14
). For the near part we used (
1.10
) to bound

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(v)}\left|v-w\right|^{2}\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\,{\rm d}w\lesssim\Lambda r^{2-2s}.

Moreover, in case that
s\in(0,1/2)
we again get by (
1.10
)

\int_{B_{r}(v)}\left|v-w\right|\left|K(v,w)-K(w,v)\right|\,{\rm d}w\lesssim\Lambda r^{1-2s},

and in case that
s\in[1/2,1)
we use the cancellation assumptions (
1.12
)

\displaystyle\Bigg|\textrm{PV}\int_{B_{r}(v)}(v-w)\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg|\lesssim\Lambda r^{1-2s}.

Thus we find

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{skew}}(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+})

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(r^{1-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{1}_{v}}+r^{2-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{2}_{v}}+r^{-2s}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{2}_{v}}\int(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}v,

so that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}(\psi,(f-\psi)_{+})\leq\frac{1}{8}\left\|(f-\psi)_{+}\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{1}_{v}}^{2}\int\chi_{\{f-\psi>0\}}(v)\,{\rm d}v+C\left\|\psi\right\|_{C^{2}_{v}}\int(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}v.

Gathering the estimates we obtained so far we get for any
t\in(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{(}f-\psi)_{+}^{2}(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\lambda\int_{-r^{2}s+t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left\|(f-\psi)_{+}\right\|_{H^{s}_{v}}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\mathcal{(}f-\psi)_{+}^{2}(r^{2s}+t_{0},x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+C\left\|\psi\right\|_{C_{v}^{2}}\int_{-r^{2}s+t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle+\left\|\psi\right\|_{C_{v}^{1}}^{2}\left|\{f-\psi>0\}\cap(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]\right|+C\frac{v_{0}+R}{(R-r)^{1+2s}}\int_{-r^{2}s+t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2+}}(f-\psi)_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle+C\Lambda\int_{-r^{2}s+t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}(f-\psi)^{2}_{+}(v)\,{\rm d}z+\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z.

Since
0\leq(f-\psi)_{+}\leq 1
the
L^{1}
norm and
L^{2}
norm are bounded by
\left|\{f-\psi>0\}\cap(-r^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]\right|\leq\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|
. Moreover, we chose
\psi
such that
\left\|D_{v}\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R-r}
,
\left\|D^{2}_{v}\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(R-r)^{2}}
and
\left\|D_{x}\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(R-r)^{1+2s}}
.
Finally we note that
(f-\psi)_{+}=f
in
Q_{r}(z_{0})
,
(f-\psi)_{+}\leq f
everywhere and
(f-\psi)_{+}=0
outside
Q_{R}(z_{0})
and conclude.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Local gain of integrability)
.

Let
f
be a non-negative sub-solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
Q_{R}(z_{0})
with a non-negative kernel satisfying (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) for some
\bar{R}\geq 1
. Let
Q_{r}(z_{0})\subset Q_{R}(z_{0})\subset Q_{\frac{\bar{R}}{2}}
for some
0<r<R\leq 1
. Assume
0\leq f\leq 1
a.e. in
\big(-R^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}\big]\times B_{R^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+t_{0}v_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Given
p\in\Big[2,2+\frac{2s}{d(1+s)}\Big)
,
f
satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{p}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\lesssim_{\lambda,\Lambda,v_{0}}

\displaystyle(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})}f^{2}_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+(R-r)^{-4}\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|+(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z,

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{1}_{t,v}W^{\sigma,1}_{x}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}\lesssim_{\lambda,\Lambda,v_{0}}

\displaystyle(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})}f^{2}_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+(R-r)^{-4}\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|+(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z,

where
z_{0}=(t_{0},x_{0},v_{0})
,
f_{0}(x,v)=f\big(-r^{2s}+t_{0},x,v\big)
and
Q^{\tau}_{r}(z_{0})=\left\{(x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}:(\tau,x,v)\in Q_{r}(z_{0})\right\}
.

Proof.

We first recall a clever observation made by Imbert and Silvestre
[
22
]
. If
f(t,x,v)=0
then
\mathcal{T}f=0
a.e. and
\mathcal{L}f(v)\geq 0
. In particular any sub-solution
f
of (
1.1
) satisfies

\mathcal{T}f\leq\mathcal{L}f-\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(w)K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)\chi_{\{f=0\}}+h=\Big(\mathcal{L}f\Big)\chi_{\{f>0\}}+h.

Now we write
\mathcal{I}:=(-{R}^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]
and for
t\in\mathcal{I}
we let
\varphi_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that
\varphi_{1}=1
on
Q_{r}^{t}(z_{0}):=B_{r^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0})\times B_{r}(v_{0})
and
\varphi_{1}=0
outside
Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})
. Consider
g:=f\varphi_{1}
. There holds

\partial_{t}g+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g=\big(\mathcal{T}f\big)\varphi_{1}+f\big(\mathcal{T}\varphi_{1}\big)+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\leq\big(\mathcal{L}f\big)\chi_{\{f>0\}}\varphi_{1}+h\varphi_{1}+f\big(\mathcal{T}\varphi_{1}\big)+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g.

Note that we have by the energy estimate
3.2

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}(z_{0}))}\leq CN,

where

N=\int_{Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})}f_{0}^{2}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\mathcal{C}\Bigg(\frac{R+r}{4},R\Bigg)\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|+\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z

with
\mathcal{C}(r,R)=(R-r)^{-2}
.
Moreover, since
f\in H^{s}_{v}
with support in
Q_{R}(z_{0})
we know by Theorem
2.1
that
\mathcal{L}f\in H^{-s}_{v}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R+r}{4}(v_{0})}}\big(\mathcal{L}f\big)\chi_{\{f>0\}}\varphi_{1}(v)\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{R+r}{4}(v_{0})}}\int_{B_{\frac{r}{4}(v)}}\dots\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v+\int_{B_{\frac{R+r}{4}(v_{0})}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{\frac{r}{4}(v)}}\dots\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{C}^{1/2}\Bigg(r,\frac{R+r}{4}\Bigg)\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}_{v}(B_{\frac{R+r}{2}(v_{0})})}

\displaystyle+C\Lambda\Bigg(\frac{r}{4}+v_{0}\Bigg)^{-2s}\int_{B_{\frac{R+r}{4}(v_{0})}}\chi_{\{f>0\}}\varphi_{1}(v)\,{\rm d}v.

Further we have

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}(z_{0}))}+C\Big(\frac{r}{4}+v_{0}\Big)^{-2s}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}(z_{0}))}+C\Big(\frac{r}{4}+v_{0}\Big)^{-2s}\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0})}.

Therefore
g
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g

\displaystyle\leq\big(\mathcal{L}f\big)\chi_{\{f>0\}}\varphi_{1}+h\varphi_{1}+f\big(\mathcal{T}\varphi_{1}\big)+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g=:H,

with right hand side
H=\big(\mathcal{L}f\big)\chi_{\{f>0\}}\varphi_{1}+h\varphi_{1}+f\big(\mathcal{T}\varphi_{1}\big)+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\in L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
.
The energy estimate
3.2
implies

\displaystyle\left\|H\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{C}\Big(r,\frac{R+r}{4}\Big)\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}(z_{0})))}+C\Big(\frac{r}{4}+v_{0}\Big)^{-4s}\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|

\displaystyle+\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}+\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}(\dot{H}^{s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}))}+\frac{(\left|v_{0}\right|+1)^{2}}{(R-(\frac{R+r}{4}))^{4s}}\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\lambda,\Lambda,v_{0}}\mathcal{C}\Big(r,\frac{R+r}{4}\Big)N+\mathcal{C}^{2}\Big(r,\frac{R+r}{4}\Big)\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}.

It remains to quantify the measure of the difference between solution and sub-solution.
Note that for
(x,v)
outside
Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})
there holds
g=0
and thus
\mathcal{T}g=0
. Moreover, outside
Q_{\frac{R+r}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})

\displaystyle\left|(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g(t,x,v)\right|

\displaystyle=C\Bigg\lvert{\int_{B_{r+\frac{R-r}{2}}(v_{0})}}g(t,x,w)\left|w-v\right|^{-(d+2s)}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\left|v\right|-v_{0}-\frac{R+r}{2}\Bigg)^{-d-2s}\left|B_{\frac{R+r}{4}}(v_{0})\right|\chi_{\Big\{\left|x\right|\leq\big(\frac{R+r}{4}\big)^{1+2s}+x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0}\Big\}}.

Thus
\mathcal{T}g+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g=(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\in L^{2}
outside
B_{(\frac{R+r}{4})^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0})\times B_{\frac{R+r}{2}}(v_{0})
.
Consider now a second smooth cut-off function
0\leq\varphi_{2}\leq 1
such that
\varphi_{2}=1
in
B_{(\frac{R+r}{4})^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0})\times B_{\frac{R+r}{2}}(v_{0})
and
\varphi_{2}=0
outside
Q_{\frac{3(R+r)}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})
.
We let

\tilde{H}=H\varphi_{2}+(1-\varphi_{2})(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g.

We claim
\tilde{H}\in L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})
. Indeed
(1-\varphi_{2})(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\in L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}
. Moreover, if a function
a\in H^{s}
then also
a\varphi_{2}\in H^{s}
. Thus by duality it also follows that
H\varphi_{2}\in L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}
.
With this definition there holds in
\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}

\mathcal{T}g+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\leq\tilde{H}

with equality outside
Q_{\frac{3(R+r)}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})
.

Let
m
be a non-negative measure whose support is contained in
\mathcal{I}\times Q_{\frac{3(R+r)}{4}}^{t}(z_{0})
given by

m:=\tilde{H}-\mathcal{T}g-(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g.

To control this measure, we test it against a test function
\varphi_{3}
that is equal to
1
in the support of
m
such that
\varphi_{3}
vanishes outside
Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})
. We get

\displaystyle\left\|m\right\|_{M^{1}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\varphi_{3}\,{\rm d}m=\int_{\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\varphi_{3}\big(\tilde{H}-\mathcal{T}g-(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g\big)\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Big(g(t_{0},x,v)-g\big(t_{0}-R^{2s},x,v\big)\Big)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Big(v\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi_{3}-(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}\varphi_{3}\Big)g+\tilde{H}\varphi_{3}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{C}\Bigg(\frac{3(r+R)}{4},R\Bigg)\left\|g\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|\tilde{H}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{C}\Bigg(\frac{3(r+R)}{4},R\Bigg)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}+\left\|H\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

Thus
g
solves the fractional Kolmogorov equation with right hand side

\mathcal{T}g+(-\Delta)_{v}^{s}g=\tilde{H}-m.

Using Lemma
3.1
we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\lesssim\left\|\tilde{H}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}\leq\left\|H\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

and

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|^{2}_{L^{1}_{t,v}W_{x}^{\sigma,1}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\tilde{H}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}+\left\|m\right\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{C}^{2}\Bigg(\frac{3(r+R)}{4},R\Bigg)\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{1}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}+\left\|H\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t,x}H^{-s}_{v}(\mathcal{I}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2d})}.

We conclude by noting that
0\leq f\leq 1
a.e. so that the
L^{1}
and the
L^{2}
norm are bounded by
\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|
.
∎

4.
First Lemma of De Giorgi

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Q_{r}(z_{0})\subset Q_{R}(z_{0})
with
0<r<R\leq 1
and
t_{0}<r^{2s}
. Let
f
be a non-negative sub-solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
Q_{R}(z_{0})
with a non-negative kernel
K
satisfying (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) for
\bar{R}\geq 1
. Assume
0\leq f\leq 1
a.e. in
(-R^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]\times B_{R^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
there holds: if

\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}f^{2}\,{\rm d}z\leq\varepsilon,

then for any
2<p<2+\frac{2s}{d(1+s)}
there exists a large constant
C>1
depending only on
s,d
and
p
such that

f\leq\min\Bigg\{C\Big[1+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\Big]^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}}(R-r)^{-\beta_{1}}\varepsilon^{\beta_{2}},\frac{1}{2}\Bigg\}\quad a.e.\textrm{ in }Q_{r}(z_{0}),

where
\beta_{1}=\frac{2p}{2p-2}>0
and
\beta_{2}=\frac{p-2}{2(2p-2)}>0
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We do a De Giorgi iteration, as was done in
[
22
, Lemma 6.6]
and
[
17
, Lemma 3.8]
. For an intuitive description of this technique, we refer the reader to
[
8
]
. Let
\tilde{\tau}:=r^{2s}-t_{0}
and
\hat{\tau}:=R^{2s}-t_{0}
. Then
0<\tilde{\tau}<\hat{\tau}
. Let
L>0
to be determined and consider

\displaystyle l_{k}=L(1-2^{-k}),

\displaystyle r_{k}=r+2^{-k}(R-r),

\displaystyle t_{k}=-\tilde{\tau}-2^{-k}(\hat{\tau}-\tilde{\tau}).

Define
Q_{k}(z_{0})=(t_{k},t_{0}]\times B_{r_{k}^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t_{k}-t_{0})v_{0})\times B_{r_{k}}(v_{0})
and

A_{k}:=\int_{Q_{k}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k})^{2}_{+}\,{\rm d}z.

Then
A_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
by assumption. We want to prove that
A_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
. This then proves the lemma.

We observe that
(f-l_{k+1})_{+}
is a sub-solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with source term

\tilde{h}(t,x,v)=\chi_{\{f(v)>l_{k+1}\}}h(t,x,v),

and such that
0\leq(f-l_{k+1})_{+}\leq 1
a.e. in
(-R^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]\times B_{R^{1+2s}}(x_{0}+(t-t_{0})v_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
With Chebyschev’s inequality we can estimate

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left|\{f>l_{k+1}\}\cap Q_{k}(z_{0})\right|=\left|\big\{(f-l_{k})_{+}>2^{-k-2}L\big\}\cap Q_{k}(z_{0})\right|\leq 2^{2k+4}L^{-2}A_{k}.

Thus

\left\|\tilde{h}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{k}(z_{0}))}\leq C\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}2^{2k+4}L^{-2}A_{k}.

We now pick
t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
such that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{k}^{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k})_{+}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\leq\frac{1}{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}\int_{Q_{k}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k})_{+}^{2}\,{\rm d}z\leq C2^{k}A_{k}.

Then we apply Lemma
3.3
and (
4.1
) to find

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}+t_{0}}}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{t}_{k+1}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k+1})_{+}^{p}\,{\rm d}z\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{4k+4}}{(R-r)^{4}}\left|\{f>l_{k+1}\}\cap Q_{k}\right|+C\frac{2^{k}}{(R-r)^{2}}A_{k}+C\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\frac{2^{2k+4}}{(R-r)^{2}L^{2}}A_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{6k+8}}{(R-r)^{4}L^{2}}A_{k}+C\frac{2^{k}}{(R-r)^{2}}A_{k}+C\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\frac{2^{2k+4}}{(R-r)^{2}L^{2}}A_{k}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{6k}(R-r)^{-4}L^{-2}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]A_{k},

where we used
l_{k+1}\geq l_{k}
.

Using
Q_{k+1}\subset Q_{k}
and Chebyschev’s inequality we get

\displaystyle A_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{Q_{k+1}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k+1})^{p}_{+}\,{\rm d}z\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left|\{f>l_{k+1}\}\cap Q_{k+1}(z_{0})\right|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,v_{0}}2^{6k}(R-r)^{-4}L^{-2}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]A_{k}\left|\big\{f>l_{k+1}\big\}\cap Q_{k}(z_{0})\right|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,v_{0}}2^{6k}(R-r)^{-4}L^{-2}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]A_{k}\Big(2^{2k+4}L^{-2}A_{k}\Big)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,v_{0}}2^{8k}(R-r)^{-4}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]L^{-2(1+\frac{p-2}{p})}A_{k}^{1+\frac{p-2}{p}}.

To determine how large we can pick
L
, we consider the sequence
A_{k}^{*}:=A_{0}Q^{-k}
for some
Q>1
to be determined and for
k\geq 0
. Then we require
A_{k}^{*}
to satisfy the reverse non-linear recurrence, that is

(4.3)

A_{k+1}^{*}\geq 2^{8k}(R-r)^{-4}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]L^{-2(1+\frac{p-2}{p})}A_{k}^{1+\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Equivalently,

1\geq 2^{8k}(R-r)^{-4}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]L^{-2(1+\frac{p-2}{p})}A_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}Q^{-k(\frac{p-2}{p})}Q.

Choose
Q
such that
2^{8}Q^{-\frac{p-2}{p}}\leq 1
or

2^{8}\leq Q^{\frac{p-2}{p}}.

Then (
4.3
) holds if

L^{2(1+\frac{p-2}{p})}\geq(R-r)^{-4}\left[1+(R-r)^{2}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]A_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}Q.

Thus, if we choose

L=A_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{2(2p-2)}}(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{2p-2}}2^{\frac{4p^{2}}{(p-2)(2p-2)}}\left[1+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]^{\frac{p}{2p-2}},

then, since
\frac{p-2}{p}>0
and
A_{0}\leq\varepsilon
, we deduce
A_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.
In particular, for almost every
z\in Q_{r}(z_{0})

f\leq L.

And therefore for almost every
z\in Q_{r}(z_{0})
there holds

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\leq A_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{2(2p-2)}}(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{2p-2}}2^{\frac{4p^{2}}{(p-2)(2p-2)}}\left[1+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}^{\frac{p-2}{2p-2}}(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{2p-2}}2^{\frac{4p^{2}}{(p-2)(2p-2)}}\left[1+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right]^{\frac{p}{2p-2}}.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

First we remark that if instead of
f\leq 1
a.e. we just assume
f
to be essentially bounded, then the gain of integrability in Lemma
3.3
would yield

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{p}(Q_{r}(z_{0}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\lambda,\Lambda,v_{0}}(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}^{t}(z_{0})}f^{2}_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+(R-r)^{-4}\left(\sup_{z\in\Omega_{t,x}(R,z_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f\right)^{2}\left|\{f>0\}\cap Q_{R}(z_{0})\right|+(R-r)^{-2}\int_{Q_{R}(z_{0})}h^{2}\,{\rm d}z,

where
\Omega_{t,x}(R,z_{0}):=(-R^{2s}+t_{0},t_{0}]\times B_{R^{1+2s}}(z_{0})
.
Using this we can further note that the nonlinear relation between the
L^{\infty}
and the
L^{2}
norm of the function can be linearised at the price of a non-local tail term. Indeed, going back to (
4.2
), if for some
\delta\in(0,1]
we choose
L
such that

L^{2}\geq\delta\left(\sup_{z\in\Omega_{t,x}(R,z_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\right)^{2},

then

\displaystyle\Bigg(\int_{t_{k+\frac{1}{2}+t_{0}}}^{t_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{Q^{t}_{k+1}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k+1})_{+}^{p}\,{\rm d}z\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq C2^{6k}(R-r)^{-4}\delta^{-1}A_{k}.

Thus we deduce

\displaystyle A_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{Q_{k+1}(z_{0})}(f-l_{k+1})^{p}_{+}\,{\rm d}z\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}\left|\{f>l_{k+1}\}\cap Q_{k+1}(z_{0})\right|^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,v_{0}}2^{6k}(R-r)^{-4}\delta^{-1}A_{k}\big(2^{2k+4}L^{-2}A_{k}\big)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,v_{0}}2^{8k}(R-r)^{-4}\delta^{-1}L^{-\frac{2(p-2)}{p}}A_{k}^{1+\frac{p-2}{p}}.

The same barrier argument as before shows that we have to choose
L
such that

L^{2(\frac{p-2}{p})}\geq(R-r)^{-4}\delta^{-1}A_{0}^{\frac{p-2}{p}}Q.

Therefore, we pick

L=\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\sup_{z\in\Omega_{t,x}(R,z_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f+(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}\Bigg)+A_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}2^{\frac{4p^{2}}{(p-2)^{2}}}\delta^{-\frac{p}{2(p-2)}},

and we deduce that
for almost every
z\in Q_{r}(z_{0})
there holds

(4.4)

\displaystyle f\leq C(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}\delta^{-\frac{p}{2(p-2)}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{z\in\Omega_{t,x}(R,z_{0})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f+\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}(R-r)\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}.

To derive a linear relation between the
L^{\infty}
and the
L^{2}
norm of
f
, we would need to absorb the non-local tail term on the left hand side. Thus we require an estimate of the tail in terms of local quantities. If we were able to replace the tail by the essential supremum on any finite ball in the velocity domain, then using a standard covering argument, see e.g.
[
13
, Lemma 8.18]
, we would have obtained
for almost every
z\in Q_{r}(z_{0})

\displaystyle f\leq C(R-r)^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}+C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}.

Combined with the Weak Harnack inequality (
1.3
), this would allow us to derive the (proper) Strong Harnack inequality, in the sense that

\sup_{\tilde{Q}^{-}_{\frac{r_{0}}{4}}(z_{0})}f\leq C\inf_{Q_{\frac{r_{0}}{4}}(z_{0})}f+C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}(z_{0}))}.

We refer to Section
6.1
below. Note, however, that it is not clear that the non-local tail in (
4.4
) can be bounded by local quantities.

5.
Second Lemma of De Giorgi

5.1.
Weak Poincaré

This is where we move along trajectories in order to obtain a hypoelliptic Poincaré-type inequality with an error term. The idea comes from Guerand and Mouhot
[
19
]
. This inequality allows us to pick up an intermediate set in De Giorgi’s Second Lemma, Theorem
1.4
. The proof of the inequality (
5.1
) does not use De Giorgi’s First Lemma.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f
be a non-negative sub-solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
(-9,0]\times Q^{t}_{3}
with a non-negative kernel
K
satisfying (
1.8
)-(
1.12
) for
\bar{R}=6
. Given any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
0<\sigma<\frac{s}{1+2s}
there holds

(5.1)

\displaystyle\big\|\big(f-\langle f\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}}\big)_{+}\big\|_{L^{1}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{(-3^{2s},0]\times B_{3^{1+2s}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|f(v)-f(w)\right|^{2}K(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}z

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\left|\textrm{PV}\int_{(-3^{2s},0]\times B_{3^{1+2s}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(v)\big(K(v,w)-K(w,v)\big)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}z\right|

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\sigma}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,v}W^{\sigma,1}_{x}(Q_{2})}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{3})},

where
Q^{-}_{1}:=Q_{1}(-2,0,0)=(-3,-2]\times Q_{1}^{t}
and
\langle f\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}}=\fint_{Q^{-}_{1}}f=\frac{1}{\left|Q^{-}_{1}\right|}\int_{Q^{-}_{1}}f
.

Remark 5.2
.

Note that the right hand side is finite for a solution
f\in L^{2}\big((-9,0]\times B_{3^{1+2s}},L^{1}_{v,\textrm{loc}}\cap H^{s}_{v,\textrm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\big)
due to Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
. In particular, if
f
is assumed to be essentially bounded then the right hand side is finite. Indeed, the symmetric non-local term is bounded by the
H^{s}
norm of
f
, which is bounded by Lemma
3.2
, the anti-symmetric part appearing on the right hand side can be bounded by the
L^{1}
norm of
f
due to the cancellation (
1.11
), and finally, Lemma
3.3
bounds the term
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,v}W^{\sigma,1}_{x}(Q_{2})}
by the right hand side of the energy estimate, Lemma
3.2
.

Proof.

Consider for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
a small function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)
,
0\leq\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
with support in
B_{1^{1+2s}}\times B_{1}
, so that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=1
in
B_{(1-\varepsilon)^{1+2s}}\times B_{(1-\varepsilon)}
and with
\left|\nabla_{y}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
,
\left|\nabla_{w}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
. We then split

\displaystyle\left\|(f-\langle f\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}})_{+}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|(f-\langle f\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}})_{+}\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigg\{\fint_{Q^{-}_{1}}\big[f(t,x,v)-f(s,y,w)\big]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\}_{+}\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{1}(Q_{1})}\fint_{Q^{-}_{1}}\big(1-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\big)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{Q_{1}}\Bigg\{\fint_{Q^{-}_{1}}\big[f(t,x,v)-f(s,y,w)\big]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w\Bigg\}_{+}\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{d(1+s)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})},

where we used
\langle f\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}}\leq\langle f\rangle_{Q^{-}_{1}}
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Consider the first term. For fixed
t,x,v
we decompose the trajectory
(t,x,v)\rightarrow(s,y,w)
into four sub-trajectories in
Q_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle(t,x,v)\xrightarrow{\nabla_{x}}(t,x+\varepsilon w,v)\xrightarrow{\nabla_{v}^{s}}\Big(t,x+\varepsilon w,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\Big)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}\Big(s,y,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\Big)\xrightarrow{\nabla_{v}^{s}}(s,y,w).

We split the integrand along these trajectories

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)-f(s,y,w)

\displaystyle=\left[f(t,x,v)-f(t,x+\varepsilon w,v)\right]

\displaystyle+\left[f(t,x+\varepsilon w,v)-f\left(t,x+\varepsilon w,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\left[f\left(t,x+\varepsilon w,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)-f\left(s,y,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\left[f\left(s,y,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)-f(s,y,w)\right].

We integrate against
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)
on
Q^{-}_{1}
yielding

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Q^{-}_{1}}\left[f(t,x,v)-f(t,x+\varepsilon w,v)\right]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w,

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Q^{-}_{1}}\left[f(t,x+\varepsilon w,v)-f\left(t,x+\varepsilon w,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)\right]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w,

\displaystyle I_{3}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Q^{-}_{1}}\left[f\left(t,x+\varepsilon w,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)-f\left(s,y,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)\right]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w,

\displaystyle I_{4}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle:=\int_{Q^{-}_{1}}\left[f\left(s,y,\frac{x+\varepsilon w-y}{t-s}\right)-f(s,y,w)\right]\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y,w)\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}w.

For
I_{2}
we find

\displaystyle\fint_{Q_{1}}

\displaystyle\left|I_{2}\right|\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle\underset{Q_{r}(z_{0})}{\mathrm{osc}}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}r_{0}^{\alpha(n-1)}\max\left\{\underset{Q_{1}}{\mathrm{osc}}f,e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right\}\underset{Q_{r}(z_{0})}{\textrm{osc }}f_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}r_{0}^{\alpha(n-1)}r^{\alpha}\max\left\{\underset{Q_{1}}{\mathrm{osc}}f,e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right\}\underset{Q_{1}}{\textrm{osc }}f_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq r_{0}^{\alpha n}e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left(1+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right)\max\left\{\underset{Q_{1}}{\mathrm{osc}}f,e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right\}

\displaystyle=r^{\alpha}e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left(1+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right)\max\left\{\underset{Q_{1}}{\mathrm{osc}}f,e^{2(3+2^{18d+46})}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}\right\}.

Appendix A
Coercivity estimate for the non-cutoff Boltzmann Kernel

We consider a non-negative function
f
with mass bounded from above and below, energy and entropy bounded from above. We denote with
K_{f}
the non-negative kernel for the non-cutoff Boltzmann equation. It is determined by (
1.16
) and can be expressed as

K_{f}(v,v^{\prime})\approx\Bigg(\int_{w\cdot(v-v^{\prime})=0}f(v+w)\left|w\right|^{\gamma+1+2s}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{-(d+2s)},

with non-locality parameter
s\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(-d,1]
, see
[
35
]
. In
[
35
]
, Silvestre determines a cone of directions with vertex
v
in which the Boltzmann kernel is bounded below, see also
[
22
, Appendix]
. We denote with
A=A(v)\in\partial B_{1}
the set of directions where
K_{f}(v,v^{\prime})\geq\lambda(1+\left|v\right|^{1+2s+\gamma})\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{-(d+2s)}
for all
v^{\prime}
such that
\frac{(v-v^{\prime})}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|}\in A
. The existence of
A
and further properties are stated in
[
22
, Lemma A.3]
. Consistent with the notation of
[
22
]
we denote with
\Xi(v)
the symmetric cone of values of
v^{\prime}
such that
\frac{(v-v^{\prime})}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|}\in A
. Then for any
v^{\prime}\in\Xi(v)
there holds
K_{f}(v,v^{\prime})\geq\lambda(1+\left|v\right|^{1+2s+\gamma})\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{-(d+2s)}
. In particular,
[
22
, Lemma A.3]
states a universal lower bound on the density of
\Xi(v)
. This can be used to prove a coercivity estimate
[
22
, Lemma A.6]

\int\int\frac{\left|g(v)-g(v^{\prime})\right|^{2}}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{d+2s}}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v\leq C\int\int(g(v)-g(v^{\prime}))^{2}K(v,v^{\prime})\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v

for any kernel with a set of directions where it is pointwisely lower bounded and for any
g
.
We claim that in fact we can use the exact same tools to show a similar coercivity estimate on the square root of the kernel:

Lemma A.1
.

Let
K:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be any non-negative kernel such that there is a set of directions
A(v)
where
K(v,v^{\prime})\geq\lambda(1+\left|v\right|^{1+2s+\gamma})\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{-(d+2s)}
for all
v^{\prime}
such that
\frac{(v-v^{\prime})}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|}\in A
. Assume that the
(d-1)
-dimensional measure of
A=A(v)
is at least
\mu(v):=c(1+\left|v\right|)^{-1}
. Let
R>0
and
\mu=\inf{\{\mu(v):v\in B_{R}\}}
. Then there is a constant
C=C(\lambda,s,d,\mu)
such that for any function
g
supported in
B_{R}
we have

\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{\left|g(v)-g(v^{\prime})\right|}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{d/2+s}}\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v\leq C\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\left|g(v)-g(v^{\prime})\right|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,v^{\prime})\,{\rm d}v^{\prime}\,{\rm d}v.

The lemma follows similarly to
[
22
, Lemma A.6]
.

Proof.

If we denote with
\Xi(v)
the symmetric cone of values such that
\frac{(v-v^{\prime})}{\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|}\in A
, then for any
v^{\prime}\in\Xi(v)
we have the lower bound

K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,v^{\prime})\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\left|v\right|^{1+2s+\gamma})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v-v^{\prime}\right|^{-\frac{d+2s}{2}}

by positivity of
K
. Moreover, the assumptions on
A
imply that for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{2R}
there is a constant
C_{0}
so that

\left|\Xi(v_{1})\cap\Xi(v_{2})\cap B_{C_{0}\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}(v_{2})\right|\geq c\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|^{d},

see
[
22
, Lemma A.4, A.5]
. We can choose
c_{0}>0
small enough such that

\left|\Xi(v_{1})\cap\Xi(v_{2})\cap B_{C_{0}\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}(v_{2})\setminus B_{c_{0}\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}(v_{1})\setminus B_{c_{0}\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}(v_{2})\right|\geq c\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|^{d}.

For this choice of
C_{0},c_{0}
we define

N_{r}(v):=\int_{B_{C_{0}r}(v)\setminus B_{c_{0}r}(v)}\left|g(v)-g(w)\right|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w.

Then for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{2R}
using the triangle inequality we get for
r:=\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|

\displaystyle N_{r}(v_{1})+N_{r}(v_{2})

\displaystyle\geq c\Bigg(\int_{\Xi(v_{1})\cap B_{C_{0}r}(v_{1})\setminus B_{c_{0}r}(v_{1})}\left|g(v_{1})-g(w)\right|\left|v_{1}-w\right|^{-\frac{d+2s}{2}}\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle+\int_{\Xi(v_{2})\cap B_{C_{0}r}(v_{2})\setminus B_{c_{0}r}(v_{2})}\left|g(v_{2})-g(w)\right|\left|v_{2}-w\right|^{-\frac{d+2s}{2}}\,{\rm d}w\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\Xi(v_{1})\cap\Xi(v_{2})\cap B_{C_{0}r}(v_{2})\setminus B_{c_{0}r}(v_{1})\setminus B_{c_{0}r}(v_{2})}\left|g(v_{1})-g(v_{2})\right|r^{-\frac{d+2s}{2}}\,{\rm d}w

\displaystyle\geq c\left|g(v_{1})-g(v_{2})\right|r^{\frac{d-2s}{2}}.

Thus by choice of
r

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}

\displaystyle\frac{\left|g(v_{1})-g(v_{2})\right|}{\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|^{s+\frac{d}{2}}}\,{\rm d}v_{1}\,{\rm d}v_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\big(N_{r}(v_{1})+N_{r}(v_{2})\big)\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|^{-d}\,{\rm d}v_{2}\,{\rm d}v_{1}

\displaystyle=2\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}N_{r}(v_{1})\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|^{-d}\,{\rm d}v_{2}\,{\rm d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{0}^{2R}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}N_{r}(v_{1})r^{-1}\,{\rm d}\sigma\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\left|g(v)-g(w)\right|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,w)\int_{C_{0}^{-1}\left|v_{1}-w\right|}^{c_{0}^{-1}\left|v_{1}-w\right|}r^{-1}\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\left|g(v)-g(w)\right|K^{\frac{1}{2}}(v,w)\,{\rm d}w\,{\rm d}v_{1}.

∎

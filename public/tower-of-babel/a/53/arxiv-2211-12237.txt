On a reduced digit-by-digit component-by-component construction of lattice point sets
Peter Kritzer, Onyekachi Osisiogu

Abstract
In this paper, we study an efficient algorithm for constructing point sets underlying quasi-Monte Carlo integration rules for weighted Korobov classes. The algorithm presented is a reduced fast component-by-component digit-by-digit (CBC-DBD) algorithm, which useful for to situations where the weights in the function space show a sufficiently fast decay. The advantage of the algorithm presented here is that the computational effort can be independent of the dimension of the integration problem to be treated if suitable assumptions on the integrand are met. The new reduced CBC-DBD algorithm is designed to work for the construction of lattice point sets, and the corresponding integration rules (so-called lattice rules) can be used to treat functions in different kinds of function spaces. We show that the integration rules constructed by our algorithm satisfy error bounds of almost optimal convergence order. Furthermore, we give details on an efficient implementation such that we obtain a considerable speed-up of a previously known CBC-DBD algorithm that has been studied in [ 8 ] . This improvement is illustrated by numerical results.



Keywords:
Numerical integration; lattice points; quasi-Monte Carlo methods; component-by-component construction; digit-by-digit construction; weighted function spaces; fast implementation.

1
Introduction

In this paper we study numerical integration of functions defined over the
d
-dimensional unit cube.
For an integrable function
f:[0,1)^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote the integral of
f
by

I_{d}(f):=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f({\bm{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}},

and we study the efficient construction of high-dimensional
quadrature rules

of the form

Q_{N}(f,({\bm{x}}_{k})_{k=0}^{N-1}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\,f({\bm{x}}_{k})

for numerically approximating
I_{d}
. We assume that the integrand
f
lies in a Banach space
({\mathcal{F}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\mathcal{F}}})
,
and that the integration nodes
{\bm{x}}_{0},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\in[0,1)^{d}
are to be chosen deterministically.
Here
Q_{N}
is called a
quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC)
rule, which is a special case of an equal-weight integration rule.
One way to measure the quality of a QMC integration rule
Q_{N}
is to consider the
worst-case error
in the unit ball of the
space
({\mathcal{F}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\mathcal{F}}}),
i.e.,

e_{N,d}(Q_{N},{\mathcal{F}}):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in{\mathcal{F}}\\
\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|I_{d}(f)-Q_{N}(f,({\bm{x}}_{k})_{k=0}^{N-1})\right|.

In general, it is highly non-trivial to choose the set of integration nodes such that the resulting rule has a low worst-case error.
There are two main types of QMC point sets
\mathcal{P}=\{{\bm{x}}_{0},{\bm{x}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\}
,
namely
lattice point sets
and
digital nets
. In this paper we consider lattice point sets,
which are obtained by considering discrete subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
that are closed under both addition and subtraction
and contain
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
as a subset; by intersecting such a discrete set with the
d
-dimensional
unit cube
[0,1)^{d}
, we then obtain a lattice point set. We are interested in a special kind of
lattice point sets that is essentially based on one
generating vector

{\bm{z}}
.
These lattice point sets were first introduced by Korobov
[
14
]
and independently by Hlawka
[
12
]

and are among the most prominently studied QMC point sets to approximate multivariate integrals
(see standard textbooks such as
[
5
,
13
,
19
,
22
]
, and also
[
6
]
).
For a natural number
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an integer generating vector
{\bm{z}}\in\{1,2,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
, a point set with points of the form

{\bm{x}}_{k}:=\left\{\frac{k}{N}{\bm{z}}\right\}=\left(\left\{\frac{kz_{1}}{N}\right\},\ldots,\left\{\frac{kz_{d}}{N}\right\}\right)\in[0,1)^{d},\qquad\text{for }k=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

is called a
rank-1 lattice point set
, which we shall sometimes denote by
P({\bm{z}},N)
.
For vectors
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we apply
\{\cdot\}
component-wise,
where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of
x
. A QMC rule using such a point set as integration nodes
is called a
(rank-1) lattice rule
with generating vector
{\bm{z}}
. We remark that, given
N
and
d
,
a rank-1 lattice rule is completely determined by the choice of the generating vector
{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{d}
,
where we write
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}:=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
. However, it should be obvious that not every choice of a generating vector
{\bm{z}}

also yields a rank-1 lattice rule with good quality for approximating the integral, and it is usually necessary to
tailor the choice of the integration nodes to the function space
{\mathcal{F}}
under consideration. For lattice rules we consider
certain Banach spaces which are based on assuming sufficient decay of the Fourier coefficients of
their elements to guarantee certain smoothness properties. These spaces are called weighted Korobov classes,
which we will denote by
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
, where
d
denotes the number of variables the functions depend on,

\alpha>1
is a real number frequently referred to as the smoothness parameter, and
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}

is a sequence of strictly positive reals, which model the importance of different coordinates.
The idea of these additional parameters
\gamma_{j}
, which we refer to as weights in the definition of the function spaces
under consideration, goes back to Sloan and Woźniakowski
[
23
]
, see also
[
24
,
10
]
,
and this will be made more precise by incorporating the weights in the norm of the space
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
in Section
2
.
If we denote the variables in the integration problem by
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
,
a small value of
\gamma_{j}
corresponds to a low influence of the variable
x_{j}
,
while a large
\gamma_{j}
means high influence of
x_{j}
.
Regarding the role of these weights in integration problems, we favor a situation in which the weights decay
sufficiently fast for coordinates with increasing indices, which helps in vanquishing the curse of dimensionality
that is inherent to many high-dimensional problems. Indeed, under certain summability conditions on the weights,
it is even possible to obtain bounds on the integration error that do not depend on the dimension of the problem at all.
Such a situation is called
strong polynomial tractability
, see, e.g.,
[
20
]
, for a general reference.

Regarding the construction of generating vectors, for dimensions
d\leq 2
explicit constructions of good generating vectors are available,
see, e.g.,
[
22
,
19
]
, but there are no explicit constructions of good generating vectors known for
d>2
;
how to find a generating vector
{\bm{z}}
that guarantees a low worst-case error of integration in a given function space is
a crucial question regarding rank-1 lattice rules. One way to find good generating vectors is the component-by-component (CBC)
construction, which is based on a greedy algorithm choosing one component of the generating vector at a time.
It was shown in
[
18
]
for prime
N
(see also
[
2
]
for the case of composite
N
)
that it is possible to find generating vectors yielding essentially optimal results for certain spaces of
d
-variate
functions by the CBC construction. Furthermore, it was shown in
[
21
]
that a fast CBC construction
can reduce the computational cost of these algorithms to only
\mathcal{O}(dN\log N)
, by making use
of a clever ordering of the points of a rank-1 lattice, and the Fast Fourier Transform.

In the paper
[
4
]
the weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in a given function space
were incorporated in the CBC construction of lattice rules.
To be more precise, depending on the weight
\gamma_{j}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
the search space for the
j
th component
z_{j}
of the generating vector
{\bm{z}}

can be shrinked as compared to the usual CBC construction,
and this reduction is the motivation for the modified CBC algorithm to be called the
“reduced” CBC construction. Under suitable assumptions on the integrands considered, it
was shown in
[
4
]
that the lattice point sets obtained by the reduced CBC construction can
still yield excellent results when used as integration nodes in a QMC rule, while the
construction cost can be made independent of the dimension
d
of the integration problem.
It was also shown in
[
4
]
that the fast CBC construction principle of Nuyens and Cools,
which was mentioned above, can be adapted to the reduced CBC construction.

A different construction algorithm for generating vectors of good lattice rules inspired by articles of Korobov (see
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
,
and
[
17
]
for an English translation) was dealt with in the recent paper
[
8
]
, where a so-called component-by-component digit-by-digit
(CBC-DBD) algorithm was presented. In this approach, one constructs the generating vector
{\bm{z}}
in a component-by-component (CBC) fashion, in which each component
z_{j}

is assembled digit-by-digit (DBD). That is, for a number
N=2^{m}
of points we greedily construct the components
z_{j}
bit-by-bit starting from the least significant bit.
It was shown that the rules constructed by the CBC-DBD algorithm yield a convergence rate that is arbitrarily close to the optimal rate.
It is also possible to have a fast implementation of the CBC-DBD algorithm which has a computational cost of
\mathcal{O}(dN\log N)
,
and also numerical results on the performance of the CBC-DBD algorithm are presented in
[
8
]
. We remark that the error analysis for the CBC-DBD algorithm
is such that no prior knowledge of the smoothness parameter
\alpha
is required to construct the generating vector.
The resulting generating vector will still deliver the near optimal rate of convergence, for arbitrary smoothness parameters
\alpha>1
,
and this result can be stated independently of the dimension, assuming that the weights satisfy certain summability conditions.

In the present paper, we would like to study a combination of the reduced CBC algorithm from
[
4
]

and the CBC-DBD construction algorithm approach from
[
8
]
, and present a
reduced (fast) CBC-DBD algorithm

for generating vectors of good lattice rules with a large number of lattice points in high dimensions, assuming
sufficiently fast decaying weights. This new algorithm will work by constructing the generating vector
{\bm{z}}

in a component-by-component (CBC) fashion in which each component
z_{j}
is assembled digit-by-digit (DBD).
However, the search space for each component will be reduced in comparison to what is shown in
[
8
]
,
thus speeding up the construction method. We will show that for suitable choices of weights the construction cost of
the reduced fast CBC-DBD algorithm can be made independent of the dimension.
The main aim of this paper is to show that one can reduce the construction cost
of the lattice rule by making the search for later components smaller than the earlier ones, while still achieving (strong) polynomial tractability.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In the subsequent section (Section
2
),
we outline the general setting, and then our construction algorithm as well as the main result
are stated and proved in Section
3
. Section
4
contains remarks on how to efficiently implement our
newly found algorithm and some numerical results.

Regarding notation, let
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
be the set of natural numbers, let
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
and let
{\mathbb{Z}}
be the set of integers. Additionally, let the set of non-zero integers be denoted by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{*}:={\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
.
For sets of components we use fraktur font, e.g.,
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
. To denote the projection of a vector
{\bm{x}}\in[0,1)^{d}

or
{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
to the components in a set
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]:=\{1,\dots,d\}
, we write

{\bm{x}}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}
or
{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}:=(\ell_{j})_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}
, respectively.

2
Setting and overview

We consider one-periodic real-valued
L_{2}
functions defined on
[0,1]^{d}
with absolutely convergent Fourier series

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})=\sum_{{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}{\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}}):=\int_{[0,1]^{d}}f({\bm{x}})\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}{\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}},

where
\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})
are the Fourier coefficients of
f
and

{\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{x}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}x_{j}=\ell_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{d}x_{d}
is the vector dot product.

It is known that rank-1 lattice point sets, as introduced in Section
1
, have a
property commonly referred to as the
character property
. Indeed, for a rank-1 lattice point set
with generating vector
{\bm{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, it is true that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})\,k/N}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }{\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;({\rm{mod}}\;N)},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We introduce the dual of a rank-1 lattice, which is of great importance in representing
the error of approximating an integral by a lattice rule
Q_{N}
.

Definition 1

(Dual lattice)
.

Let
N\geq 2
be the number of points and let
{\bm{z}}\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}

be the generating vector of a rank-1 lattice point set
P({\bm{z}},N)
.
The dual lattice
\Lambda^{\top}({\bm{z}},N)
of a rank-1 lattice
P({\bm{z}},N)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\Lambda^{\top}({\bm{z}},N):=\{{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\ |\ {\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;N)}\}.

For a non-empty set
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d],
we can further define

\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{u}}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathfrak{u}}({\bm{z}}_{\mathfrak{u}}):=\{{\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}_{*}\ |\ {\bm{\ell}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\equiv 0{\;(\operatorname{mod}\;N)}\}.

Then, to obtain the integration error for a given function
f
in terms of its Fourier coefficients, we interchange
the order of summation and use the character property of the lattice points, and get

\displaystyle Q_{N}(f,{\bm{z}})-I_{d}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})\,k/N}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\\
{\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\equiv 0{\;({\rm{mod}}\;N)}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})\,\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}}),

where, for
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\delta_{N}(a):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}ak/N}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }a\equiv 0{\;({\rm{mod}}\;N)},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For a vector
{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, smoothness parameter
\alpha>1
, and strictly positive weights

{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, we define

r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}}):=\prod_{j\in\operatorname{supp}({\bm{\ell}})}\gamma_{j}\left|\ell_{j}\right|^{\alpha},

(1)

where
\operatorname{supp}({\bm{\ell}}):=\{j\in[d]:\ell_{j}\neq 0\}
is the support of
{\bm{\ell}}
.
As usual, we define the empty product as one such
that
r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{0}})=1
. Formally, we will also use a function
r_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}
below,
which is obtained by replacing
\alpha
by 1 in (
1
).

We then define our function space
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
of one-periodic real-valued
L_{2}
functions defined on
[0,1]^{d}

with absolutely convergent Fourier series. The norm in this space is given as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}}:=\sup_{{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})|\,r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}}),

and for
\alpha>1
, dimension
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and positive weight sequence

{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, our weighted function space is

E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}:=\left\{f\in L^{2}([0,1]^{d})\colon\|f\|_{E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}}<\infty\right\}.

(2)

It is known (see, for example,
[
5
]
) that the worst-case error of an

N
-point rank-1 lattice rule generated by
{\bm{z}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
in the space
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
is given by

e_{N,d,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in\mathcal{D}\end{subarray}}r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{-1}({\bm{\ell}})=\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})}.

(3)

We remark that the worst-case error (
3
) is sometimes referred to as
P_{\alpha}
in the literature on
lattice rules. As pointed out above, it is the main goal of the present paper to state a new effective construction method
for good instances of
{\bm{z}}
such that
e_{N,d,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})
is reasonably small.

It is well known, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
, that the optimal convergence rate of the worst-case integration error in the space

E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
is of order
{\mathcal{O}}(N^{-\alpha})
. Furthermore, we would like to remark that in the literature
on lattice rules often a slightly different, but related, function space than
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
is studied. Indeed,
by modifying the norm in the space to
\left(\sum_{{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|\hat{f}({\bm{\ell}})|^{2}\,r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})\right)^{1/2}
,
one obtains a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, which is often referred to as “Korobov space”. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
)
that the worst-case error in that space corresponds to the square root of the worst-case error in
E_{d,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}
,
and so all results shown in the present paper can directly be carried over to related results for the Korobov space.
Note also that, by using embedding results, any results on the error in
E_{s,{\bm{\gamma}}}^{\alpha}

immediately yield results on the worst-case error of “tent-transformed” lattice rules in certain Sobolev
spaces of functions whose mixed partial derivatives of order
1
and
2
in each variable are square integrable
(with
\alpha=2
and
\alpha=4
in our notation, respectively), see
[
1
,
5
,
7
,
9
]
for further details.

2.1
The quality criterion used in this paper

In this section and the following we shall always assume that the number
N
of points equals
2^{m}
, where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Furthermore, we assume that we are given a sequence of non-negative integers
(w_{j})_{j\geq 1}
ordered in a non-decreasing fashion,
to be more precise,
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots
, and we put
Y_{j}:=2^{w_{j}}
for
j\in[d]
.
The numbers
w_{j}
for
j\geq 1
are also called
reduction indices
, as they will help to reduce the
computational cost of the CBC-DBD algorithm studied in this paper.
In what follows, we set
d^{*}
as the largest
j
such that
w_{j}<m
, i.e.,
d^{*}:=\max\{j\in{\mathbb{N}}:w_{j}<m\}
.
Moreover, we will usually (unless stated otherwise) assume that the generating vector
{\bm{z}}
of a
lattice rule under consideration takes the form
{\bm{z}}=(Y_{1}z_{1},\dots,Y_{d}z_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
where
z_{j}\in\{1,3,5,\dots,2^{m-w_{j}}-1\}
for
j\in[d^{*}]
, and
z_{j}
is odd if
j>d^{*}
(in fact, we
will often choose
z_{j}=1
for
j>d^{*}
, but any choice of odd numbers
z_{j}
for those
j
would yield the same results for
our lattices rules).

We define the following quality measure
T_{\bm{\gamma}}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}})
for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\bm{w}}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}

with
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots
, by

T_{\gamma}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}}):=\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})}{r_{1,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})},

(4)

where the set
M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}
is defined as follows. Let

M_{N,j,w_{j}}:=\{-(2^{\max(0,m-w_{j})}-1),\ldots,2^{\max(0,m-w_{j})}-1\}\qquad\text{for }j\in[d].

Furthermore, we write

\{M_{N,j,w_{j}}^{*}:=\left(\{-(2^{\max(0,m-w_{j})}-1),\ldots,2^{\max(0,m-w_{j})}-1\}\setminus\{0\}\right).

Note that

M_{N,j,w_{j}}^{*}=\emptyset
for
j>d^{*}
. Furthermore, let

M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}=M_{N,1,w_{1}}\times\cdots\times M_{N,d,w_{d}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}^{*}=M_{N,1,w_{1}}^{*}\times\cdots\times M_{N,d,w_{d}}^{*}.

Then, for
\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]
,
by
M_{N,\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|,{\bm{w}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}
and
M_{N,\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|,{\bm{w}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}^{*}
, we denote the
projections of
M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}
and
M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}^{*}
onto the components with indices
in
{\mathfrak{u}}
, respectively.
Likewise we define the auxiliary quantity

T_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}}):=\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})}.

Let us denote the Riemann zeta function by
\zeta(\alpha):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha>1
.
Moreover, we write, for a non-empty set
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]
,

\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}:=\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\gamma_{j},

and we put
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
.

The following proposition shows that we can use
T_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}})
as a suitable approximation to
the worst-case error of a rank-1 lattice rule.

Proposition 1
.

Let
N=2^{m}
, let
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive weights, let

{\bm{z}}=(Y_{1}z_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}z_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, and let
{\bm{w}}=(w_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
with
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots
. Furthermore, assume that all
z_{j}
,
j\in[d]
, are odd, and that

z_{j}\in\{1,3,5,\dots,2^{m-w_{j}}-1\}
for
j\in[d^{*}]
.
Then, for
\alpha>1
, we have that

\displaystyle e_{N,d,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})-T_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})}-\sum_{{\bm{0}}\neq{\bm{\ell}}\in M_{N,d,{\bm{w}}}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}\cdot{\bm{z}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{\ell}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\,\frac{(4\zeta(\alpha))^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}}{2^{\alpha\max(0,m-\max_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}w_{j})}}.

Proof.

We can rewrite the difference
e_{N,d,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}({\bm{z}})-T_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}(N,{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}})
as

\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]}\left(\sum_{{\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{*}^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}})}-\sum_{{\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\in M_{N,|{\mathfrak{u}}|,{\bm{w}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}^{*}}\frac{\delta_{N}({\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\cdot{\bm{z}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}})}{r_{\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}_{\mathfrak{u}}}({\bm{\ell}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}})}\right),

motivating us to define, for
{\mathfrak{u}}\neq\emptyset
,
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[d]
,

Definition 2
.

Let
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
be an odd integer, let
m,d^{*}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\bm{w}}=(w_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of reduction indices in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

with
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots
, and let
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}

be a sequence of positive weights. For

and

we define the quality function

as

\displaystyle h_{s,v,m,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(x):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=v}^{m-w_{s}}\frac{1}{2^{t-v}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{w_{s}}-1}\ \ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=\ell 2^{t}+1\\
k\equiv 1{\;({\rm{mod}}\;2)}\end{subarray}}^{(\ell+1)2^{t}-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[s]\\
s\in{\mathfrak{u}}\end{subarray}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\{s\}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\\
j\neq s\end{subarray}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\left(\pi z_{j}\frac{k}{2^{t}}\,\frac{2^{m-w_{s}}}{2^{m-w_{j}}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\gamma_{s}\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi kx/2^{v})}\right)\right),

where we assume that

.

Based on the quality function

, we formulate the following reduced component-by-component digit-by-digit
(CBC-DBD) algorithm.

Algorithm 1

Reduced component-by-component digit-by-digit construction

Input:
Integer

, dimension

with

.

, set

.

for

to

for

end

for

Theorem 2
.

Let

,

,

, and that we have chosen
z_{s,v-1}

By Lemma
3
and Definition
2

\frac{1}{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}\sum_{\overline{z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},z_{s,v-1}+z2^{v-1}+\overline{z}2^{v})

\displaystyle\operatorname*{argmin}_{z\in\{0,1\}}\frac{1}{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}\sum_{\overline{z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},z_{s,v-1}+z2^{v-1}+\overline{z}2^{v})

(15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}\sum_{\overline{z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{m-w_{s}-v}}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},z_{s,v-1}+z2^{v-1}+\overline{z}2^{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m-w_{s}-v+1}}\sum_{\overline{z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{m-w_{s}-v+1}}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},\underbrace{z_{s,v-2}+\tilde{z}\,2^{v-2}}_{=z_{s,v-1}}+\bar{z}\,2^{v-1}),

according to Algorithm

, selected in the previous step of the algorithm. Noting that the inequality in (

\displaystyle\operatorname*{argmin}_{z\in\{0,1\}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},z_{s,m-w_{s}-1}+z2^{m-w_{s}-1})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{m-w_{s}-1}}\sum_{\overline{z}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2^{m-w_{s}-1}}}H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s-1},1+\overline{z}2),

\displaystyle\times\log\left(\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\pi k/2)}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\not\equiv 0{\;({\rm{mod}}\;2^{m-w_{r}})}\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq[r]\\

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{2^{w_{s}}-1}\ \ \sum_{t=1}^{m-w_{s}}\frac{2^{t-1}-1}{2^{t-1}}(\log 4)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=\ell 2^{t}+1\\

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\\

\displaystyle H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{s-1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s-1]})

\displaystyle+(\log 4)\,\gamma_{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\

\displaystyle H_{s-1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s-1]})

Theorem 3
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
N=2^{m}
,
{\bm{w}}=(w_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
0=w_{1}\leq w_{2}\leq\cdots
,
and let
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})

Observe that due to Theorem

\displaystyle H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s]})\leq H_{s-1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s-1]})+(\log 4)\left[\gamma_{s}(2^{m}-2^{w_{s}})+\gamma_{s}H_{s-1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s-1]})\right]

\displaystyle H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s]})

\displaystyle(1+\gamma_{s}\log 4)\left[(1+\gamma_{s-1}\log 4)H_{s-2,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s-2]})+\gamma_{s-1}(\log 4)(2^{m}-2^{w_{s-1}})\right]

\displaystyle H_{s,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}({\bm{z}}_{[s]})

\displaystyle H_{1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1})\prod_{j=2}^{s}(1+\gamma_{j}\log 4)+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}(2^{m}-2^{\min_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}w_{j}})

\displaystyle H_{1,N,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}(z_{1})\prod_{j=2}^{s}(1+\gamma_{j}\log 4)+\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{2:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}(\log 4)^{\left|{\mathfrak{u}}\right|}2^{m}.

Theorem 4
.

Proof.

We know from Proposition

al
Taking into account that we have product weights

.

involves two cases regarding the worst-case error

.

4
Fast implementation of the construction scheme

In this section we discuss the efficient implementation of the introduced reduced CBC-DBD algorithm and analyze its complexity.

4.1
Implementation and cost analysis of the reduced CBC-DBD algorithm

We recall the definition of the quality function in Definition
2
,
and we see that for product weights
h_{d,v,m,{\bm{\gamma}},{\bm{w}}}
can be rewritten as follows.
Let
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
be an odd integer, let
m,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be positive product weights.
For
1\leq s\leq d
,

For integers

in Algorithm

, so we do not need to

In Algorithm

and

, throughout we use
b=2
and
\alpha=2
. Corollary
1
assures independence of
the dimension
d
whenever the chosen weights
\gamma_{j}
satisfy

\sum_{j\geq 1}\gamma_{j}b^{w_{j}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\gamma_{j}2^{w_{j}}<\infty.

(22)

However, the sequence of weights
\gamma_{j}=1/j^{3}
does not always satisfy Condition (
22
) if we
choose the reduction indices as
w_{j}=\lfloor p\log_{2}j\rfloor
, since

\sum_{j\geq 1}\gamma_{j}2^{w_{j}}=\sum_{j\geq 1}j^{-3}2^{\lfloor p\log_{2}j\rfloor}\geq\sum_{j\geq 1}j^{-3}2^{p\log_{2}j-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}j^{p-3}.

The latter series only convergence if whenever
3-p>1
, and this is not
satisfied for our choices of
p
made in Figures
1
and
2
,
which is
p=2
and
p=7/2
, respectively. Thus, this gives rise to the difference in the errors obtained by the reduced CBC-DBD and non-reduced CBC-DBD algorithms.
This phenomenon is to be expected and shows that the reduction indices and the weights must be balanced carefully, and that in general the reduced algorithm
works better for situations where the weights
\gamma_{j}
decay fast.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a combination of the CBC-DBD algorithm introduced in
[
8
]
and the reduced construction method in
[
4
]
for constructing good lattice rules for numerical integration in weighted Korobov spaces. In particular, we have aimed to gain from the reduced construction method to shrink the computational cost as compared to the non-reduced CBC-DBD algorithm. We showed that the reduced CBC-DBD construction with quality measure independent of the smoothness parameter
\alpha
, similarly to
[
8
]
also formulated for product weights, yields lattice rules which admit error convergence rates that are arbitrarily close to the optimal convergence order.
We remark that there has recently been considerable interest in finding algorithms that guarantee some degree of universality with respect to the smoothness parameter
and/or the weights (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
), and also our result can be seen as a step in this direction.
Furthermore, the errors can be bounded independently of the dimension if the weights satisfy suitable summability conditions. In addition to these theoretical results, we have derived a fast implementation of the considered algorithm. Numerical tests confirm our main findings.

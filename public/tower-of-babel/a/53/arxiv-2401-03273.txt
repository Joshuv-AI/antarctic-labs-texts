Recent development in biconservative submanifolds
Bang-Yen CHEN

Abstract
A submanifold ϕ : M → 𝔼 m \phi:M\to\mathbb{E}^{m} is called biharmonic if it satisfies Δ 2 ​ ϕ = 0 \Delta^{2}\phi=0 identically, according to the author. On the other hand, G.-Y. Jiang studied biharmonic maps between Riemannian manifolds as critical points of the bienergy functional, and proved that biharmonic maps φ \varphi are characterized by vanishing of bitension τ 2 \tau_{2} of φ \varphi . During last three decades there has been a growing interest in the theory of biharmonic submanifolds and biharmonic maps. The study of H H -submanifolds of 𝔼 m \mathbb{E}^{m} were derived from biharmonic submanifolds by only requiring the vanishing of the tangential component of Δ 2 ​ ϕ \Delta^{2}\phi . In 2014, R. Caddeo et. al. named a submanifold M M in any Riemannian manifold “biconservative” if the stress-energy tensor S ^ 2 \hat{S}_{2} of bienergy satisfies div ​ S ^ 2 = 0 {\rm div}\,\hat{S}_{2}=0 . Caddeo et. al. also shown that a Euclidean submanifolds is an H H -submanifold if and only if the tangential component of τ 2 \tau_{2} vanishes and hence the notions of H H -submanifolds and of biconservative submanifolds coincide for Euclidean submanifolds. The first results on biconservative hypersurfaces were proved by T. Hasanis and T. Vlachos, where they called such hypersurfaces H-hypersurfaces in 1995. Since then biconservative submanifolds has attracted many researchers and a lot of interesting results were obtained. The aim of this article is to provide a comprehensive survey on recent developments on biconservative submanifolds done most during the last decade.

1
Introduction

As a special topic in the theory of finite type submanifolds, the notion of biharmonic submanifold
M
of a Euclidean
m
-spaces
\mathbb{E}^{m}
was introduced in the 1980s by the author as an isometric immersion
\phi:M\to\mathbb{E}^{m}
of a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
into
\mathbb{E}^{m}
with biharmonic position vector field
\phi
, i.e.,
\Delta^{2}\phi=0
(see, e.g.,
[
15
,
41
]
), where
\Delta
denotes the Laplacian of
M
. According to the famous Beltrami’s formula
\Delta\phi=-nH
, the condition “
\Delta^{2}\phi=0
” is equivalent to “
\Delta H=0
”, where
H
denote the mean curvature vector.

It is well-known that biharmonic surfaces in
\mathbb{E}^{3}
are minimal, which leads to the following conjecture of the author
[
15
]
posed in 1991.

Biharmonic Conjecture:

Biharmonic submanifolds of Euclidean spaces are minimal.

This conjecture was verified for many cases in a lot of papers under additional
geometric properties (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
26
,
40
,
41
,
48
,
53
,
57
,
61
,
75
,
90
]
). However, this conjecture remains open til now.

Independently, biharmonic maps were studied by G.-Y. Jiang in
[
65
,
68
]
, in much abstract form; in terms of a variational problem for maps between Riemannian manifolds as critical points of the
L^{2}
-norm of the tension field. More precisely, a biharmonic map
\varphi:M\to N
between two Riemannian manifolds is a critical point of the
bienergy functional
:

E_{2}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\|\tau(\varphi)\|^{2}dv,

where
M
is a compact manifold and
\tau(\varphi)={\rm Trace}(\nabla d\varphi)
is the tension field of the map
\varphi
. Jiang proved in
[
65
]
that biharmonic maps are characterized by the Euler-Lagrange equation or the biharmonic equation:

\tau_{2}(\varphi)=-\Delta^{\phi}\tau(\varphi)-{\rm Trace}\,R^{N}(d\varphi(\cdot),\tau(\varphi))d\varphi(\cdot)=0,

(1.1)

where
\tau_{2}(\varphi)
is called the
bitension
of
\varphi
,
R^{N}
is the Riemann curvature tensor of
N
,
and

\Delta^{\phi}Z={\rm Tr}(\nabla^{\phi}\nabla^{\phi}-\nabla^{\phi}_{\nabla^{M}})Z.

In 2002, R. Caddeo, S. Montaldo and C. Oniciuc
[
5
]
observed that the condition “
\tau_{2}(\varphi)=0
” is equivalent to the condition “
\Delta^{2}\varphi=0
” for Euclidean submanifolds.
Consequently, these two definitions of biharmonicity coincide when the ambient space is Euclidean. During last three decades there has been a growing interest in the theory of biharmonic submanifolds and biharmonic maps (see, e.g.,
[
90
]
).

The notion of biconservative submanifolds is derived from the theory of biharmonic submanifolds by only requiring the vanishing of the tangential component of
\Delta^{2}\phi
for Euclidean submanifolds or the tangential component of
\tau_{2}(\varphi)
for arbitrary Riemannian submanifolds.
The first results on biconservative hypersurfaces were obtained by T. Hasanis and T. Vlachos in
[
61
]
, where biconservative hypersurfaces are called
H
-hypersurfaces
.

One of advantages of studying biconservative submanifolds is that one will reveal the influence of the tangential component of the biharmonic equation on submanifolds. Hence, it may give us informations on the biharmonic conjecture. Moreover, it would provide some informations that how much one can rely on just the vanishing of tangential part of the biharmonic equation as well as how many results remain valid under this half condition.

Since the last decade, the study of biconservative submanifolds attracts many researchers and a lot of interesting results have been obtained.
The main purpose of this paper is to present a comprehensive survey of recent developments on biconservative submanifolds. It
is author’s intention that this paper would provide useful references for graduate
students as well as for researchers working on this interesting and very active research topic in differential geometry.

2
Stress-Energy tensor and biconservative submanifolds

According to D. Hilbert in
[
62
]
, the
stress-energy tensor
associated with a variational problem is a symmetric
2
-covariant tensor
\hat{S}
conservative at critical points, i.e.,
{\rm div}\,\hat{S}=0
. In the context of harmonic maps
\varphi
between two Riemannian manifolds the stress-energy tensor have been studied in details by P. Baird and J. Eells in
[
1
]
and A. Sanini in
[
100
]
.

The Euler-Lagrange equation associated to the energy is equivalent to the vanishing of the tension field
\tau_{1}(\varphi)={\rm Trace}\,(\nabla d\varphi)
(see, e.g.,
[
42
]
), and the tensor

\hat{S}=\frac{1}{2}|d\varphi|^{2}g-\varphi^{\ast}\tilde{g},

which satisfies
{\rm div}\,\hat{S}=-\langle\tau_{1}(\varphi),d\varphi\rangle
. Hence, we have
{\rm div}\,\hat{S}=0
when the map
\varphi
is harmonic.

For isometric immersions, the condition
{\rm div}\,\hat{S}=0
is always satisfied, because
\tau_{1}(\varphi)
is normal.
The study of the stress-energy tensor for the
bienergy was initiated G.-Y. Jiang
[
66
]
and afterwards by E. Loubeau, S. Montaldo, and C. Oniciuc
[
72
]
.
Its expression is given by

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{2}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\tau_{1}(\varphi)|^{2}\langle X,Y\rangle+\langle d\varphi,\nabla\tau_{1}(\varphi)\rangle\langle X,Y\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle d\varphi(X),\nabla_{Y}\tau_{1}(\varphi)\rangle-\langle d\varphi(Y),\nabla_{X}\tau_{1}(\varphi)\rangle,

and it satisfies the condition

{\rm div}\,\hat{S}_{2}=-\langle\tau_{2}(\varphi),d\varphi\rangle,

(2.1)

and thus it conform to the principle of a stress-energy tensor for the bienergy.

For an isometric immersion
\varphi:(M,g)\to(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
, equation (
2.1
) becomes

{\rm div}\,\hat{S}_{2}=-\tau_{2}(\varphi)^{T}.

(2.2)

Based on these facts, R. Caddeo, S. Montaldo, C. Oniciuc and P. Piu
[
6
]
defined the notion of
biconservative submanifolds
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A submanifold of a Riemannian manifold is called
biconservative
if it satisfies
{\rm div}\,\hat{S}_{2}=0
.

3
Basics of submanifolds

For general references on submanifolds we refer to
[
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
.

Let
(M,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian
n
-manifold. We denote by
\nabla
the Levi–Civita connection of
(M,g)
.
Then the Riemann curvature tensor
R
of
M
is the
(1,3)
tensor field given by

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z

for
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
, where
{\mathfrak{X}}(TM)
denotes the space consists of all (smooth) vector fields in the tangent bundle
TM
and let
[\;\,,\;]
be the Lie bracket.
At any point
p\in M
, for a given basis
\{X,Y\}
of a plane section
\pi\subset T_{p}M
, we put

Q(X,Y)=g(X,X)g(Y,Y)-g(X,Y)^{2}.

We call the plane section
\pi

nondegenerate
whenever
Q(X,Y)\neq 0
.
For a nondegenerate plane section
\pi
, the sectional curvature
K(\pi)
is defined by

K(\pi)=\frac{g(R(X,Y)Y,X)}{Q(X,Y)}.

The
Ricci tensor

Ric_{p}
at a point
p\in M
is given by

{\rm Ric}_{p}(X,Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}g(R(E_{i},X)Y,E_{i}),

where
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}M
;
and the
scalar curvature
is defined by

\tau=\sum_{i<j}K(E_{i},E_{j}).

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with
\dim M\geq 3
is called an
Einstein manifold
if its Ricci tensor satisfies
{\rm Ric}=cg
for some constant
c
.

Remark 3.1
.

In this article, by a
real space form
we mean a Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature. We denote an
m
-dimensional real space form of constant curvature
c
simply by
R^{m}(c)
.

For an isometric immersion
\phi:M\to\tilde{M}
of a pseudo-Riemannian submanifold
(M,g)
into another pseudo-Riemannian manifold
(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
, we
denote the Levi–Civita connections of
M
and
\tilde{M}
by
\nabla
and
\widetilde{\nabla}
, respectively. Then the formulas of Gauss and Weingarten of
M
in
\tilde{M}
are given respectively by

\tilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y),

(3.1)

\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\xi=-A_{\xi}X+D_{X}\xi,

(3.2)

where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(T^{\perp}M)
, where
\mathfrak{X}(T^{\perp}M)
is the space consists of all (smooth) normal vector fields of
M
.

The
h,A
and
D
given in (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) are called the
second fundamental form
,
shape operator
and
normal connection
of
M
, respectively.
The shape operator
A_{\xi}
is a self-adjoint endomorphism of
T_{p}M
. Furthermore, the shape operator and the second fundamental form are related by

\left<h(X,Y),\xi\right>=\left<A_{\xi}X,Y\right>,

where
\left<\,\;,\;\right>
denotes the inner product associated with the metric.
The
mean curvature vector
of
M
is given by

H=\frac{1}{n}\,{\rm Trace}\,h=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{j}h(E_{j},E_{j}).

(3.3)

A submanifold
M
is called
CMC
if
M
has constant mean curvature
\alpha
, where

\alpha=\|H\|
, and
M
is called
PMCV
it its mean curvature vector
H
is parallel in the normal bundle, i.e.,
DH=0
(see, e.g.,
[
8
,
23
]
).
Moreover,
M
is called
minimal
(resp.,
totally geodesic
) if its mean curvature vector (resp., its second fundamental form) vanishes identically. Furthermore,
M
is called
totally umbilical
if it satisfies

h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)H,\;\;\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(TM);

and
M
is called
pseudo-umbilical
if it shape operator satisfies

A_{H}=\lambda I

for some function
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
I
is the identity map.

Let
R
and
\tilde{R}
denote the Riemann curvature tensors of
M
and of
\tilde{M}
, respectively, and
R^{D}
the curvature tensor of the normal bundle
T^{\perp}M
defined by

R^{D}(X,Y)\xi=D_{X}D_{Y}\xi-D_{Y}D_{X}\xi-D_{[X,Y]}\xi.

Then the equations of Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci of
M
are given respectively by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle R(X,Y;Z,W)={\tilde{R}}(X,Y;Z,W)+\tilde{g}(h(X,W),h(Y,Z))

(3.4)

\displaystyle-\tilde{g}(h(X,Z),h(Y,W)),

({\tilde{R}}(X,Y)Z)^{\perp}=(\bar{\nabla}_{X}h)(Y,Z)-(\bar{\nabla}_{Y}h)(X,Z),

(3.5)

R^{D}(X,Y;\xi,\eta)={\tilde{R}}(X,Y;\xi,\eta)+g([A_{\xi},A_{\eta}](X),Y),

(3.6)

where
[A_{\xi},A_{\eta}]=A_{\xi}A_{\eta}-A_{\eta}A_{\xi}
and
\overline{\nabla}h
is defined by

(\overline{\nabla}_{X}h)(Y,Z)=D_{X}h(Y,Z)-h(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-h(Y,\nabla_{X}Z).

In particular, if the ambient space
\tilde{M}
is a real space form
R^{m}(c)
, then the equations of Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci reduce to

\displaystyle

\displaystyle g(R(X,Y)Z,W)=g(A_{h(Y,Z)}X,W)-g(A_{h(X,Z)}Y,W)

(3.7)

\displaystyle+c\,(g(X,W)g(Y,Z)-g(X,Z)g(Y,W)),

(\overline{\nabla}_{X}h)(Y,Z)=(\overline{\nabla}_{Y}h)(X,Z),

(3.8)

\tilde{g}(R^{\perp}(X,Y)\xi,\eta)=g([A_{\xi},A_{\eta}]X,Y).

(3.9)

Let
(\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s},g_{0})
denote the pseudo-Euclidean
m
-space whose metric tensor is given by

g_{0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}dx_{i}^{2}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}dx_{j}^{2},

(3.10)

where
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
is the rectangular coordinate system of

\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
. Then
(\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s},g_{0})
is called the
pseudo-Euclidean
m
-space
with
index
s
.

For
c>0
, if we put

S^{m}_{s}(c)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}^{m+1}_{s}:\left<x,x\right>=\frac{1}{c^{2}}>0\right\},

(3.11)

H^{m}_{s}(-c)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{E}^{m+1}_{s+1}:\left<x,x\right>=-\frac{1}{c^{2}}<0\right\},

(3.12)

where
\left<\;,\>\right>
is the indefinite inner product on
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
, then
S^{m}_{s}(c)
and
H^{m}_{s}(-c)

are complete pseudo-Riemannian manifolds with index
s
and of constant
sectional curvature
c>0
and
-c<0
, respectively.
In particular the pseudo-Riemannian manifolds
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{1},S^{m}_{1}(c)
and
H^{m}_{1}(-c)
are called the
Minkowski spacetime
,
de Sitter spacetime
and
anti-de Sitter
spacetime
, respectively. These three spacetimes are known as the
Lorentzian space forms.

In the following, by a
nondegenerate
submanifold of a Lorentzian space form, we mean a submanifold which is spacelike or timelike.

4
Two basic formulas and biconservative equations

The following formula of Beltrami is basic in submanifold theory.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
2
]

If
\phi:M\to\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
is an isometric immersion of a pseudo-Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
into a pseudo-Euclidean
m
-space
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
, then we have

\Delta\phi=-nH.

(4.1)

An immediate consequence of Proposition
4.1
is the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Every spacelike minimal submanifold
M
in a pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
is non-closed.

Another application of Theorem
4.1
is the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

Every spacelike minimal submanifold
M
in a pseudo-hyperbolic space
H^{m}_{s}(-1)
is non-closed.

The theory of finite type submanifolds was initiated by the author in late 1990s (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
11
,
12
,
35
,
17
,
26
]
. In particular, the following
result is basic and very useful in the study of finite type submanifolds.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
9
,
12
]

If
\phi:M\to\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
is an isometric immersion of a pseudo-Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
into
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
, then we have

\Delta H=\Delta^{D}H+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}h(A_{H}e_{i},e_{i})+(\Delta H)^{T},

(4.2)

where

(\Delta H)^{T}=\frac{n}{2}\nabla\|H\|^{2}+2\,{\rm Trace}(A_{DH})

(4.3)

is the tangential component of
\Delta H
,
\Delta^{D}
is the Laplacian associated with the normal connection
D
,
\|H\|^{2}=\left<H,H\right>
, and

{\rm Trace}(A_{DH})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}A_{D_{E_{i}H}}E_{i}

for an orthonormal frame
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\}
of
TM
.

Theorem
4.4
implies immediately the following.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
M
be a pseudo-Riemannian submanifold of
\mathbb{E}^{m}_{s}
. If
M
is PMCV, then it is biconservative.

For submanifolds in an arbitrary Riemannian manifold, we have the following result from
[
5
,
89
]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

If
\phi:M\to\tilde{M}^{m}
is an isometric immersion of a Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M,g)
into another Riemannian manifold
(\tilde{M}^{m},\tilde{g})
, then

\phi
is biharmonic if and only if the following two equations are satisfied:

2\,{\rm Trace}(\nabla A_{DH})+\frac{n}{2}\nabla{\|H\|}^{2}=-2\sum_{i=1}^{n}({\tilde{R}}(e_{i},H)e_{i})^{T},

(4.4)

\Delta^{D}H-\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(A_{H}E_{i},E_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}({\tilde{R}}(E_{i},H)E_{i})^{\perp},

(4.5)

where
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\}
is an orthonormal local frame of
TM
.

Theorem
4.6
implies the following.

Corollary 4.7
.

If
\phi:M\to\tilde{M}^{m}
is an isometric immersion of a Riemannian
n
-manifold
(M,g)
into another Riemannian manifold
(\tilde{M}^{m},\tilde{g})
, then

\phi
is biconservative if and only if equation (
4.4
) holds identically.

Using the Codazzi equation, one easily find the next result.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
\phi:M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{m}
be a nondegerate submanifold of
\tilde{M}
. Then we have

{\rm Trace}(\nabla A_{H})=\frac{n}{2}grad\left(\|H\|^{2}\right)+{\rm Trace}\,A_{\nabla_{\cdot}^{\perp}H}(\cdot)+{\rm Trace}\left(R^{N}(\,\cdot\,,H)\,\cdot\,\right)^{T}.

Because every biharmonic submanifold is biconservative, the family of biconservative submanifolds
is much richer than the family of biharmonic submanifolds.

When the ambient space
\tilde{M}^{m}
is a real space form, Corollary
4.7
reduces to the following.

Corollary 4.9
.

[
6
]

If
M
is an
n
-dimensional submanifold of a real space form of constant curvature

, then
M
is biconservative if and only if we have

2\,{\rm Trace}(\nabla A_{DH})=-\frac{n}{2}\nabla{\|H\|}^{2}

(4.6)

In particular, if

[

If

Let

5
Geometry of biconservative surfaces in
R^{3}(c)

Let
(M^{2},g)
be a Riemannian 2-manifold. Then we can write the metric
g
in terms of an isothermal coordinates as
g=e^{2\rho}(dx^{2}+dy^{2})
, where
\rho\in C^{\infty}(M^{2})
. As usual, let us put

\frac{\partial}{\partial{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}}\right),\;\;\;\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial{y}}\right).

Then
g(A_{H}(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}}),\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}})
is called
holomorphic
if

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}}g\!\left(A_{H}\!\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}}\right),\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}}\right)=0
holds.

5.1
Characterizations of biconservative surface

For surfaces of an arbitrary Riemannian manifold, we have the following characterization result from
[
76
,
81
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\phi:(M^{2},g)\to(\tilde{M}^{m},\tilde{g})
be a surface in a Riemannian
m
-manifold
\tilde{M}^{m}
. Then the following four statements are equivalent:

(a)

M
is a biconservative surface;

(b)

g(A_{H}(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}}),\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}})
is holomorphic;

(c)

A_{H}
is a Codazzi tensor.

(d)

the mean curvature
\alpha=\|H\|
is constant.

Remark 5.2
.

In the case of biharmonic surfaces, the fact that
g(A_{H}(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}}),\tfrac{\partial}{\partial{z}})

is holomorphic if and only if
M
is CMC was proved in
[
73
]
.

From Corollary
4.12
we know that CMC surfaces in any real 3-space form are biconservative, thus the study of non-CMC biconservative surfaces is more interesting.

Definition 5.3
.

A maximal biconservative surfaces with
\nabla\alpha\neq 0
everywhere is called a
standard biconservative surface
, and the domain of their defining immersion endowed with the induced metric is called an
abstract standard biconservative surface
.

\mathbb{E}^{3}

If
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g=c(\cosh u)^{6}(du^{2}+dv^{2}))\,(c>0)

11.1
General results for biconservative submanifolds

Let
\phi:M^{n}\to\tilde{M}^{m}
be a submanifold. Computing
\tau_{2}(\varphi)
by splitting it in the tangent and in the normal part and using (

Proposition 11.1
.

Let

be a submanifold. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

M
is biconservative;

(b)

{\rm Trace}A_{\nabla^{\perp}_{\cdot}H}(\cdot)+{\rm Trace}(\nabla A_{H})+{\rm Trace}({\tilde{R}}(\,\cdot\,,H)\,\cdot\,)^{T}=0

(c)

\frac{n}{2}\nabla\|H\|^{2}+2\,{\rm Trace}(A_{\nabla^{\perp}_{\cdot}H}(\cdot))+2\,{\rm Trace}({\tilde{R}}(\,\cdot\,,H)\,\cdot\,)^{T}=0

(d)

,

where
{\tilde{R}}
denotes the curvature tensor of

As an easy consequence of Proposition

Proposition 11.2
.

Let

be a PMC submanifold of a Riemannian manifold
\tilde{M}^{m}
. Then

{\rm Trace}\,(\overline{R}(\,\cdot\,,H)\,\cdot\,)^{T}=0.

Proposition 11.3
.

[

Let

be a submanifold. Then we have:

(1)

the stress-bienergy tensor of

(11.1)

(2)

;

(3)

the relation between the divergence of

and the divergence of

(4)

12
Biconservative hypersurfaces in BCV-spaces

Three-dimensional simply-connected homogeneous manifolds have been classified according to the dimension
d
of their isometry group which is equal to 3, 4 or 6. If
d=6
, one obtains the real space forms.

The Bianchi–Cartan–Vranceanu spaces (simply called
BCV-spaces
) are three-dimensional homogeneous Riemannian manifolds with isometry group of dimension 4 or 6.
Such spaces can be described by the following 2-parameter family of Riemannian metrics,
Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu metrics
, which are defined by

g_{\kappa,\tau}=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{F^{2}}+\left(dz+\tau\,\frac{ydx-xdy}{F}\right)^{2},\;\;\kappa,\tau\in\mathbb{R},

(12.1)

defined on
{\mathcal{M}}=\{(x,y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\colon F(x,y)>0\}
with
F=1+\dfrac{\kappa}{4}(x^{2}+y^{2}).

It is well-known that
BCV-spaces admit a Riemannian submersion, called
Hopf fibration
, over a surface with constant Gauss curvature
\kappa
:

\pi:{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}=({\mathcal{M}},g_{\kappa,\tau})\to M^{2}(\kappa)=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2},g_{F}=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{F^{2}}\right)

such that
\pi(x,y,z)=(x,y)
.
The vector field
\partial_{z}=\partial/\partial z
is tangent to the fibers of the Hopf fibration, which is known as the
Hopf vector field
.

B. Daniel
[
36
]
considered the angle
\theta
between the normal vector field of a surface in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
and
\partial_{z}
in terms of
\cos\theta
. Moreover, he proved that the angle function
\theta
is a fundamental invariant for surfaces in BCV-spaces.

The 2-parameter family of metrics given by (
12.1
) includes all the
3
-dimensional homogeneous metrics whose isometry group has dimension
4
or
6
, except for the hyperbolic space, according to

(a)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong\mathbb{E}^{3}
, if
\kappa=\tau=0
;

(b)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong S^{3}\left(\frac{\kappa}{4}\right)\setminus\{\infty\}
, if
\kappa=4\tau^{2}\neq 0
;

(c)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong(S^{2}(\kappa)\setminus\{\infty\})\times\mathbb{R}
, if
\kappa>0
and
\tau=0
;

(d)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong H^{2}(\kappa)\times\mathbb{R}
, if
\kappa<0
and
\tau=0
;

(e)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong\mathrm{SU}(2)\setminus\{\infty\}
, if
\kappa>0
and
\tau\neq 0
;

(f)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,R)}
, if
\kappa<0
and
\tau\neq 0
;

(g)

{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\cong{\rm Nil}_{3}
, if
\kappa=0
and
\tau\neq 0
.

Montaldo, Onnis and Passamani
[
78
]
studied biconservative surfaces in
a Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
and proved the following results.

Proposition 12.1
.

Let
M
be a constant angle surface in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
with angle
\theta\in[0,\pi]
. If
M
is biconservative, then it is CMC.

Proposition 12.2
.

Let
M
be a non-minimal biconservative surface in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
with
\kappa\neq 4\tau^{2}
. If
M
is CMC, then it is a Hopf tube over a curve with constant geodesic curvature
(
via the Hopf fibration
\pi:{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}\to M^{2}(\kappa))
.

Theorem 12.3
.

If
M
is a non-minimal biconservative surface in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
with
\kappa\neq 4\tau^{2}
, then the following three statements are equivalent:

(a)

M
is a constant angle surface;

(b)

M
is a CMC surface;

(c)

M
is a Hopf tube over a curve with constant geodesic curvature.

Theorem 12.4
.

Let
M
be a surface of revolution in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa,\tau}
which is not a real space form and with
\tau\neq 0
. If
\theta\in(0,\pi)
and the mean curvature
\alpha
of
M
is nowhere zero, then
M
is a biconservative surface if and only if

is a Hopf circular cylinder.

The notion of
trapped surfaces
was introduced by R. Penrose in
[
96
]
, which
plays a important role in cosmology and general relativity. A black hole is a trapped region in a space-time enclosed by a marginally trapped surface.
In term of mean curvature vector, a spacelike surface in a given space-time is called
marginally trapped
if its mean curvature vector field is lightlike. More generally, a nondegenerate submanifold of a pseudo-Riemannian manifold is called
quasi-minimal
if its mean curvature vector is lightlike at every point (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
24
,
30
,
98
]
).

[
21
,
32
]

[

]

[
111
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Theorem 16.1
.

\mathbb{E}^{4}_{2}

Biconservative submanifolds in complex space forms

A submanifold
M
of a Kähler manifold
(\tilde{M},J,g)
equipped with the almost complex structure
J
and Kählerian metric
g
is called
totally real
if we have (see
[
34
]
)

J(T_{p}M)\subset T^{\perp}_{p}M,\;\;\forall p\in M.

In particular, if
J(T_{p}M)=T^{\perp}_{p}M
,
\forall p\in M
, then
M
is called
Lagrangian
.

A Kähler manifold
(\tilde{M},J,g)
is called a
complex space form
if it has constant holomorphic sectional curvature. The curvature tensor field

\tilde{R}
of a complex space form
\tilde{M}(c)
of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
c
satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{R}(\bar{X},\bar{Y})\bar{Z}=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4}\Big\{\langle\bar{Y},\bar{Z}\rangle\bar{X}-\langle\bar{X},\bar{Z}\rangle\bar{Y}+\langle J\bar{Y},\bar{Z}\rangle J\bar{X}

\displaystyle-\langle J\bar{X},\bar{Z}\rangle J\bar{Y}+2\langle J\bar{Y},\bar{X}\rangle J\bar{Z}\Big\},

for vector fields
\bar{X},\bar{Y},\bar{Z}\in{\mathfrak{X}}(T\tilde{M}(c))
.

For a submanifold
M^{n}
in a Kähler manifold, we put

JH=(JH)^{T}+(JH)^{\perp},

where
(JH)^{T}
and
(JH)^{\perp}
denote the tangential and normal components of
JH
, where
H
is the mean curvature vector.

Next, we present the results on biconservative submanifolds in complex space forms obtained by H. Bibi, the author, D. Fetcu and C. Oniciuc in
[
3
]
.

-space forms

[

]
.

.

20.4

The Segre embedding of two complex projective spaces

and

where
(z_{0},\ldots,z_{h})
and

(w_{0},\ldots,w_{p})
are the homogeneous coordinates of

and
CP^{p}(4)
, respectively.
This embedding was introduced by C. Segre in 1891 (see
[
99
]
). It is well-known that the Segre embedding

is also a Kählerian
embedding.
When

,
the Segre embedding is nothing but the complex quadric

Q_{2}=CP^{1}\times CP^{1}
in

Q_{2}=\Bigg\{(z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\in CP^{3}:\sum_{j=0}^{3}z_{j}^{2}=0\Bigg\}.

Bibi et. al. constructed in
[

]
examples with the Segre embedding of non-PMC, CMC, biconservative submanifolds in complex projective spaces as follows.

Theorem 20.13
.

Let

be a curve of nonzero curvature

in

. Then we have:

(a)

via the Segre embedding

of

into

if and only if

is constant. Moreover,

(b)

is a proper biharmonic submanifold of

if and only if

These examples show that the existence of biconservative submanifolds of dimension greater than two is much less rigid.

21.1
Shape operator of timelike hypersurface
M^{3}_{1}

The shape operator of the timelike hypersurface
M^{3}_{1}
in
\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
can be put into one of the four matrix forms, denoted by Types I, II, III and IV.

For Type I and Type IV, the matrix representation of the induced metric on
M^{3}_{1}
, with respect to an orthonormal frame of the tangent bundle
TM^{3}_{1}
, is

\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

and the corresponding shape operator
S
can be put into matrix forms as

S_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}&0\\
0&0&\lambda_{3}\end{pmatrix},\;\;\;S_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&\mu&0\\
-\mu&\kappa&0\\
0&0&\eta\end{pmatrix},\;\;(\mu\neq 0),

respectively.
For Type II and Type III, the matrix representation of the induced metric
on
M^{3}_{1}
, with respect to a pseudo-orthonormal frame of
of
TM^{3}_{1}
, is

\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

and the corresponding shape operator
S
can be put into matrix forms as

S_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0&0\\
1&\kappa&0\\
0&0&\mu\end{pmatrix},\;\;\;S_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa&0&0\\
0&\kappa&1\\
-1&0&\kappa\end{pmatrix},

respectively.

21.2
k
-th mean curvature of hypersurfaces
M^{n}_{1}

Following the four possible matrix representations of the shape
operator
S_{i}\,(i=1,2,3,4)
of
M^{3}_{1}
in the previous subsection, we define its principal curvatures, denoted by unified notations

\kappa_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
, according to
[
94
]
, as follow.

For Type I, we put
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}
,

, where
\lambda_{i}
’s were given in

.

For Type II, we put
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\kappa
and
\kappa_{3}=\mu
, where
\mu
was given in

.

For Type III, we put
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{3}=\kappa
, where
\kappa
was given in
S_{3}
.

For Type IV, we put
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{1}+i\mu
,
\kappa_{2}=\kappa_{1}-i\mu

, where
\kappa,\mu,\eta
were given in

The
characteristic polynomial
of the shape operator

on
M^{3}_{1}

is

Q(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{3}(t-\kappa_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^{k}s_{k}t^{3-k},

where

-th mean curvature of

Similar definitions holds for hypersurfaces

-biconservative hypersurfaces

The operator

is an extension of the ordinary Laplace operator

which stands for the linearized operator of the first variation of

-th mean curvature function (see, e.g.,

where

[

,

]

The next definition extended the notions of biharmonic and biconservative hypersurfaces to

-biharmonic and

-biconservative hypersurfaces.

.

-biharmonic

\phi(s,t)=\frac{a-bs}{\sqrt{4-b^{2}}}\left(\cos(\sqrt{4-b^{2}}t),\sin(\sqrt{4-b^{2}}t,\frac{2}{b}\right)

a,b

(6)

\phi(s,t)=(a\pm 2s)\left(-t^{2},t,t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

\phi(s,t)=(f(s),s\cosh t,s\sinh t),

23
Generalized biconservative surfaces in
S^{3}_{1}(1)

D. Yang and Z. Zhao

107

23.1
Generalized biconservative surfaces in de Sitter 3-space
S^{3}_{1}(1)

In
[
107
]
, Yang and Zhao proved the following classification theorem for
generalized biconservative spacelike surfaces in
S^{3}_{1}(1)
.

Theorem 23.1
.

If
\phi:M\to S_{1}^{3}(1)\subset\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}

is a non-CMC generalized biconservative spacelike surface in the
de Sitter space
S_{1}^{3}(1)
, then
\phi=\phi(s,t)
is locally one of the following surfaces of revolution:

(1)

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\left(\sqrt{4\!-\!b^{2}}\cos s+\frac{ab}{\sqrt{4-b^{2}}}\sin s,f\sinh\sqrt{c}t,\frac{2\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{4-b^{2}}}\sin s,f\cosh\sqrt{c}t\right)
, where
c=4-a^{2}-b^{2}>0
,
f=a\cos s+b\sin s
, and
a,b
are constants;

(2)

\phi\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\,\left(\sqrt{b^{2}-4}\cos s-\!\frac{ab}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4}}\sin s,f\cos\sqrt{c}t,f\sin\sqrt{c}t,\frac{2\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4}}\sin s\right)
, where
c=a^{2}+b^{2}-4
,
f=a\cos s+b\sin s
, and
a,b
are constants such that
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-2,2]
;

(3)

\phi\,=\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{c}}{\sqrt{4-b^{2}}}\sin s,f\cos\sqrt{c}t,f\sin\sqrt{c}t,\sqrt{4-b^{2}}\cos s+\frac{ab}{\sqrt{4-b^{2}}}\sin s\right)
, where
c=a^{2}+b^{2}-4>0
,
f=a\cos s+b\sin s
, and
a
and
b
are constants with
a\neq 0
and
b\in\{-2,2\}
;

(4)

\phi=\left(\cos s-\dfrac{a}{4}\sin s,\dfrac{f}{a}\cos at,\dfrac{f}{a}\sin at,\cos s+\left(\dfrac{2}{a}-\dfrac{a}{4}\right)\sin s\right)
, where

f=a\cos s+b\sin s
, and
a,b
are constants such that
b\in(-2,2)
;

(5)

\phi=\left(\cos s-\dfrac{a}{2}ft^{2},\sin s-\dfrac{b}{2}ft^{2},ft,ft^{2}\right)
, where
f=a\cos s+b\sin s
, and
a,b
are constants satisfying
a^{2}+b^{2}=4
;

(6)

\phi=\left(s\sinh t,\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\cos f,\sqrt{1+s^{2}}\sin f,s\cosh t\right)
, where

f=\pm\int^{s}\frac{(2+k)ds}{(1+s^{2})s^{k/(2+k)}\sqrt{(2+k)^{2}s^{-2k/(+k)}-s^{2}-1}},

with

s\in(0,1)
and
k\neq 0,-1,-2
;

(7)

\phi=\left(s\cos t,s\sin t,\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\cosh f,\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\sinh f\right)
, where

f=\pm\int^{s}\frac{(2+k)ds}{(1-s^{2})s^{k/(2+k)}\sqrt{(2+k)^{2}s^{-2k/(2+k)}-s^{2}+1}},

with

s\in(0,1)
and
k\neq 0,-1,-2
;

(8)

\phi=\left(s\cos t,s\sin t,\sqrt{s^{2}-1}\sinh f,\sqrt{s^{2}-1}\cosh f\right)
, where

f=\pm\int^{s}\frac{(2+k)ds}{(s^{2}-1)s^{k/(2+k)}\sqrt{(2+k)^{2}s^{-2k/(2+k)}-s^{2}+1}},

with

s\in(1,\infty)
and
k\neq 0,-1,-2
;

(9)

\phi=\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(st^{2}+sf^{2}-\frac{1}{s}-s\right),st,sf,\frac{1}{2}\left(st^{2}+sf^{2}-\frac{1}{s}+s\right)\right)
, where

f=\int^{s}\frac{(2+k)ds}{s^{2}\sqrt{(2+k)^{2}-s^{(4+4k)/(2+k)}}},

s\in(0,(2+k)^{(2+k)/(2+2k)})
, and
k\neq 0,-1,-2
.

Theorem 23.2
.

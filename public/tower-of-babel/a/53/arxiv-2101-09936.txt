Optimal investment in illiquid market with search frictions and transaction costs Thanks: Department of mathematics, Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology; jchoi@unist.ac.kr, gangtaeung@unist.ac.kr
Jin Hyuk Choi, Tae Ung Gang

Abstract
. We consider an optimal investment problem to maximize expected utility of the terminal wealth, in an illiquid market with search frictions and transaction costs. In the market model, an investor’s attempt of transaction is successful only at arrival times of a Poisson process, and the investor pays proportional transaction costs when the transaction is successful. We characterize the no-trade region describing the optimal trading strategy. We provide asymptotic expansions of the boundaries of the no-trade region and the value function, for small transaction costs. The asymptotic analysis implies that the effects of the transaction costs are more pronounced in the market with less search frictions.



Keywords
: stochastic control, optimal investment, illiquidity, transaction costs, search frictions

2.
The Model

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},{\mathbb{P}})
be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions. Under the filtration, we assume that
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion and
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process with intensity
\lambda>0
. We assume that
B
and
P
are independent processes.

We consider a market that has two different types of illiquidity: (i) the investor’s attempt of trading is successful only when the Poisson process
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
jumps,
5
5

5

Therefore, bigger
\lambda
implies more frequent trading opportunities (less search frictions), on average.
and (ii) the investor needs to pay proportional transaction costs for successful trading.

To be more specific, we consider a financial market consisting of a bond and a stock, whose price processes
(S_{t}^{(0)})_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are given by the following stochastic differential equations (SDEs):

(2.1)

\displaystyle dS_{t}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=S_{t}^{(0)}rdt

(2.2)

\displaystyle dS_{t}

\displaystyle=S_{t}\left(\mu dt+\sigma dB_{t}\right),

where
\mu,r,\sigma,S_{0}^{(0)},S_{0}
are constants and
\sigma,S_{0}^{(0)},S_{0}
are assumed to be strictly positive.

The proportional transaction costs are described by two constants
\underline{\epsilon}\in[0,1)
and
\bar{\epsilon}\in[0,\infty)
: the investor gets
(1-\underline{\epsilon})S_{t}
for selling one share of the stock, and pays
(1+\bar{\epsilon})S_{t}
for purchasing one share of the stock.

Let
W_{t}^{(1)}
be the amount of wealth invested in the stock and
W_{t}^{(0)}
be the amount of wealth in the bond, at time
t\geq 0
. If the investor tries to obtain the stock worth
M_{s}
at time
s\in[0,t]
, then

(2.3)

\begin{split}W_{t}^{(1)}&=w_{0}^{(1)}+\int_{0}^{t}W_{s-}^{(1)}\big(\mu ds+\sigma dB_{s}\big)+\int_{0}^{t}M_{s}dP_{s},\\
W_{t}^{(0)}&=w_{0}^{(0)}+\int_{0}^{t}W_{s-}^{(0)}rds+\int_{0}^{t}\left((1-\underline{\epsilon})M_{s}^{-}-(1+\bar{\epsilon})M_{s}^{+}\right)dP_{s},\end{split}

where we use notation
x^{\pm}=\max{\{0,\pm x\}}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and the constants
w_{0}^{(1)}\geq 0
and
w_{0}^{(0)}\geq 0
are given model parameters that represent the initial position of the investor.

The trading strategy
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called
admissible
if
it is a predictable process and the corresponding total wealth process
W:=W^{(0)}+W^{(1)}
is nonnegative all the time. This nonnegativity condition is equivalent to
W^{(0)}_{t}\geq 0
and
W^{(1)}_{t}\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
, because the rebalancing times are discrete.
6
6

6

Indeed, for
s>t
and
A=\{W^{(1)}_{t}<0,W^{(0)}_{t}>0\}
or
\{W^{(1)}_{t}>0,W^{(0)}_{t}<0\}
, we observe that

{\mathbb{P}}(W_{s}<0\,|\,A)\geq{\mathbb{P}}(P_{t}=P_{s}\textrm{ and }W_{s}<0\,|\,A)>0.

Therefore, an admissible strategy
M
satisfies

(2.4)

\displaystyle-W_{t-}^{(1)}\leq M_{t}\leq\frac{W_{t-}^{(0)}}{1+\bar{\epsilon}},\quad t\geq 0.

For an admissible strategy
M
and the corresponding solutions
W^{(1)}
and
W^{(0)}
of the SDEs (
2.3
), let
X_{t}:=W_{t}^{(1)}/W_{t}
be the fraction of the total wealth invested in the stock market at time
t
. Then, the inequalities in (
2.4
) imply that
0\leq X_{t}\leq 1
and the application of Ito’s formula produces the following SDE for
(W,X)
:

(2.5)

\begin{split}dW_{t}&=\left(r(1-X_{t-})+\mu X_{t-}\right)W_{t-}dt+\sigma X_{t-}W_{t-}dB_{t}-\left(\bar{\epsilon}M_{t}^{+}+\underline{\epsilon}M_{t}^{-}\right)dP_{t},\\
dX_{t}&=X_{t-}(1-X_{t-})\left((\mu-r-\sigma^{2}X_{t-})dt+\sigma dB_{t}\right)+\left(\frac{M_{t}+\left(\bar{\epsilon}M_{t}^{+}+\underline{\epsilon}M_{t}^{-}\right)X_{t-}}{W_{t-}-\bar{\epsilon}M_{t}^{+}-\underline{\epsilon}M_{t}^{-}}\right)dP_{t},\end{split}

where the initial conditions are
W_{0}=w_{0}:=w_{0}^{(1)}+w_{0}^{(0)}
and
X_{0}=x_{0}:=w_{0}^{(1)}/w_{0}
. We assume that the initial total wealth is strictly positive,
w_{0}>0
. The nonnegativity of
w_{0}^{(1)}
and
w_{0}^{(1)}
imply
0\leq x_{0}\leq 1
.

Let a constant
T>0
represent the terminal time of trading. We assume that the investor’s goal is to maximize the expected log-utility of the total wealth at the terminal time.
That is, we analyze the following optimal investment problem:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{(M_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}[\ln(W_{T})],

where the supremum is taken over all admissible trading strategies.

3.
Value function

Let
V
be the value function of the control problem (
2.6

(3.1)

\displaystyle V(t,x,w)=\sup_{(M_{s})_{s\in[t,T]}}{\mathbb{E}}[\ln(W_{T})|{\mathcal{F}}_{t}]\Big|_{(X_{t},W_{t})=(x,w)}.

As usual, the scaling property of the wealth process and the property of log-utility enable us to conjecture the form of the value function as

V(t,x,w)=\ln(w)+v(t,x)

for a function
v
(we verify this in Theorem

(3.2)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}0=v(T,x),\\
0=v_{t}+x(1-x)(\mu-r-\sigma^{2}x)v_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}(1-x)^{2}v_{xx}+(\mu-r)x+r-\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}x^{2}-\lambda v\\
\qquad+\lambda\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\frac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x<y\}}-\ln\left(\frac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x>y\}}\right),\end{cases}\end{split}

There exists a unique

\begin{split}\begin{cases}0=v(T,x),\\

\qquad+\lambda\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\frac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x<y\}}-\ln\left(\frac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x>y\}}\right).\end{cases}\end{split}

4.
Optimal strategy

In this section, we show that the optimal strategy can be characterized in terms of the
no-trade region
. We start with the construction of the
candidate
boundary points
\underline{y}
and
\bar{y}
of the no-trade region in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
t\in[0,T)
, there exist
0\leq\underline{y}(t)\leq\bar{y}(t)\leq 1
such that

\begin{split}\big\{\underline{y}(t)\big\}=\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}\Big(v(t,y)-\ln(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)\Big),\\
\big\{\bar{y}(t)\big\}=\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}\Big(v(t,y)-\ln(1-\underline{\epsilon}y)\Big).\end{split}

To be more specific, the following statements hold:

(i) The map
y\mapsto v(t,y)-\ln(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)
strictly increases (decreses) on
y\in[0,\underline{y}(t)]
(
y\in[\underline{y}(t),1]
).

If
0<\underline{y}(t)<1
, then
\underline{y}(t)
is the unique solution of the equation
v_{x}(t,x)=\frac{\bar{\epsilon}}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}
.

(ii) The map
y\mapsto v(t,y)-\ln(1-\underline{\epsilon}y)
strictly increases (decreses) on
y\in[0,\bar{y}(t)]
(
y\in[\bar{y}(t),1]
).

If
0<\bar{y}(t)<1
, then
\bar{y}(t)
is the unique solution of the equation
v_{x}(t,x)=-\frac{\underline{\epsilon}}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}
.

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T)
be fixed. We consider the map
z\in[0,\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}}]\mapsto{\tilde{V}}(t,1-(1+\bar{\epsilon})z,z)
, where
{\tilde{V}}
is defined in (
3.11
). Theorem
3.4
and (
3.12
) imply that this map is differentiable (we denote the partial derivatives as
{\tilde{V}}_{a}
and
{\tilde{V}}_{b}
), and the map is strictly concave due to Proposition
3.5
. Therefore, its derivative

(4.1)

\displaystyle D(t,z):=-(1+\bar{\epsilon}){\tilde{V}}_{a}(t,1-(1+\bar{\epsilon})z,z)+{\tilde{V}}_{b}(t,1-(1+\bar{\epsilon})z,z)

is strictly decreasing in
z\in(0,\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}})
, and there exists a unique
\underline{z}(t)\in[0,\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}}]
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D(t,z)>0&\textrm{for $z\in(0,\underline{z}(t))$},\\
D(t,z)<0&\textrm{for $z\in(\underline{z}(t),\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}})$}.\\
\end{cases}

Obviously, in case
\underline{z}(t)\in(0,\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}})
,
\underline{z}(t)
is the unique solution of
D(t,z)=0
.

By Theorem
3.4
and (
3.12
), we observe that for
y\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle v(t,y)-\ln(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)=V\left(t,y,\tfrac{1}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right)={\tilde{V}}\left(t,\tfrac{1-y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y},\tfrac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right).

We take derivative with respect to
y
above and use (
4.1
) to obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big(v(t,y)-\ln(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)\Big)=\tfrac{1}{(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)^{2}}\,D\left(t,\tfrac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right).

Now we define
\underline{y}(t):=\frac{\underline{z}(t)}{1-\bar{\epsilon}\underline{z}(t)}\in[0,1]
. Since the map
y\mapsto\frac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}
is strictly increasing on
[0,1]
, the definition of
\underline{z}(t)
in (
4.2
) implies that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D\left(t,\frac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right)>0&\textrm{for $y\in\left(0,\underline{y}(t)\right)$},\\
D\left(t,\frac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right)<0&\textrm{for $y\in\left(\underline{y}(t),1\right)$}.\\
\end{cases}

Also, in case
\underline{y}(t)\in(0,1)
,
\underline{y}(t)
is the unique solution of
D\left(t,\frac{y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}\right)=0
.
From (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we conclude that statement (i) holds and

\big\{\underline{y}(t)\big\}=\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}\Big(v(t,y)-\ln(1+\bar{\epsilon}y)\Big).

By the same way, we conclude that
\max_{y\in[0,1]}\big(v(t,y)-\ln(1-\underline{\epsilon}y)\big)
has the unique maximizer denoted by
\bar{y}(t)
and statement (ii) holds.

It only remains to check the inequality
\underline{y}(t)\leq\bar{y}(t)
. If
\bar{y}(t)=1
, then
\underline{y}(t)\leq\bar{y}(t)
is obvious. If
\bar{y}(t)<1
, then
v_{x}(t,\bar{y}(t))\leq\frac{-\underline{\epsilon}}{1-\underline{\epsilon}\bar{y}(t)}\leq\frac{\bar{\epsilon}}{1+\bar{\epsilon}\bar{y}(t)}
by (ii). From (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we obtain
\underline{y}(t)\leq\bar{y}(t)
.
∎

In the next theorem, we explicitly characterize the optimizer
\hat{y}
in (
3.8
) in terms of
\underline{y}
and
\bar{y}
in Lemma

.

Theorem 4.2
.

For
t\in[0,T)

in (

has the following expression:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\hat{y}(t,x)=\begin{cases}\underline{y}(t),&\textrm{if }x\in\left[0,\underline{y}(t)\right)\\
x,&\textrm{if }x\in\left[\underline{y}(t),\bar{y}(t)\right]\\
\bar{y}(t),&\textrm{if }x\in\left(\bar{y}(t),1\right]\\
\end{cases}

where
\underline{y}(t)
and
\bar{y}(t)
are uniquely determined in Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

We may rewrite the maximization in (
3.8

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\max_{y\in[0,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x<y\}}-\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x>y\}}\right)\\
&=\max\left\{\max_{y\in[0,x]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)\right),\max_{y\in[x,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)\right)\right\}.\end{split}

Using Lemma
4.1
, we observe that

(4.7)

\begin{split}\max_{y\in[0,x]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}v(t,x),&\textrm{if }x\leq\bar{y}(t)\\
v(t,\bar{y}(t))-\ln\left(\frac{1-\underline{\epsilon}\bar{y}(t)}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right),&\textrm{if }x>\bar{y}(t)\end{cases}\\
\max_{y\in[x,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}v(t,\underline{y}(t))-\ln\left(\frac{1+\bar{\epsilon}\underline{y}(t)}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right),&\textrm{if }x<\underline{y}(t)\\
v(t,x),&\textrm{if }x\geq\underline{y}(t)\end{cases}\end{split}

where the maximizers are unique. Combining (

\begin{split}&\max_{y\in[0,1]}\left(v(t,y)-\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\bar{\epsilon}y}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x<y\}}-\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\underline{\epsilon}y}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right)1_{\{x>y\}}\right)\\
&=\begin{cases}v(t,\underline{y}(t))-\ln\left(\frac{1+\bar{\epsilon}\underline{y}(t)}{1+\bar{\epsilon}x}\right),&\textrm{if }x\in\left[0,\underline{y}(t)\right)\\
v(t,x),&\textrm{if }x\in\left[\underline{y}(t),\bar{y}(t)\right]\\
v(t,\bar{y}(t))-\ln\left(\frac{1-\underline{\epsilon}\bar{y}(t)}{1-\underline{\epsilon}x}\right),&\textrm{if }x\in\left(\bar{y}(t),1\right]\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

and conclude that the corresponding unique maximizer is as in (

).

(

(

and

\displaystyle 0<{\mathbb{E}}\left[\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right]\leq 2e^{(|r-\mu|+\sigma^{2})(s-t)},

where the second equality is from (

x<\bar{y}(t)
.
Obviously, the last integral in (
4.18
) is nonnegative if
\mu-r-\lambda\underline{\epsilon}\geq 0
. If
\mu-r-\lambda\underline{\epsilon}<0
, then
\mu-r-\lambda<0
due to
\underline{\epsilon}\in[0,1)
and we observe that

\int_{t}^{T}e^{(\mu-r-\lambda)(s-t)}\left(\mu-r-\lambda\underline{\epsilon}\right)ds=\frac{\mu-r-\lambda\underline{\epsilon}}{\mu-r-\lambda}\left(e^{(\mu-r-\lambda)(T-t)}-1\right)>-\underline{\epsilon},

where we use
y_{\infty}>0
for the inequality. The above inequality and (
4.18
) imply

\lim_{x\downarrow 0}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big(v(t,x)-\ln(1-\underline{\epsilon}x)\Big)>0,

and we conclude
\bar{y}(t)>0
by Lemma
4.1
.

(iii) Suppose that
0<y_{\infty}<1
and
\bar{\epsilon}>0
(the case of
\underline{\epsilon}>0
can be treated similarly). According to (ii),
\underline{y}(t)<1
and
\bar{y}(t)>0
. Therefore, there are only two possibilities:
\underline{y}(t)=0
or
0<\underline{y}(t)<1
. In case
\underline{y}(t)=0
, we immediately obtain
\underline{y}(t)<\bar{y}(t)
since
\bar{y}(t)>0
. In case
0<\underline{y}(t)<1
, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

v_{x}(t,\underline{y}(t))=\frac{\bar{\epsilon}}{1+\bar{\epsilon}\,\underline{y}(t)}>\frac{-\underline{\epsilon}}{1-\underline{\epsilon}\,\underline{y}(t)},

and this inequality, together with (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), implies
\underline{y}(t)<\bar{y}(t)
.

(iv) Suppose that
0<y_{\infty}
and
\bar{\epsilon}=0
(the case of
y_{\infty}<1
and
\underline{\epsilon}=0
can be treated similarly). Lemma
4.1
implies that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\underline{y}(t)>0\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(t,x)>0.

The second equality in (
4.18
) can be written as

(4.20)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{T}e^{(\mu-r-\lambda)(s-t)}\left(\mu-r+\lambda\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(s,x)\cdot 1_{\left\{\underline{y}(s)=0<\bar{y}(s)\right\}}\right)ds,

where we substitute
\bar{\epsilon}=0
and use
\bar{y}(s)>0
by (ii). Define
t^{*}
as

(4.21)

\displaystyle t^{*}:=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T):\,\,\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(s,x)>0\textrm{ for all }s\in[t,T)\right\}.

Due to (
4.16
) and the strict positivity of
\mu-r
, for
t
close enough to
T
, the integrand in (
4.20
) is strictly positive and
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(t,x)>0
. Therefore, the set in (
4.21
) is non-empty and
t^{*}<T
. Suppose that
t^{*}>0
. For
0\leq\delta\leq t^{*}
, we apply (
4.21
) and (
4.19
) to expression (
4.20
) and obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(t^{*}-\delta,x)

\displaystyle>\int_{t^{*}}^{T}e^{(\mu-r-\lambda)(s-t^{*}+\delta)}\left(\mu-r\right)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t^{*}-\delta}^{t^{*}}e^{(\mu-r-\lambda)(s-t^{*}+\delta)}\left(\mu-r+\lambda\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(s,x)\cdot 1_{\left\{\underline{y}(s)=0<\bar{y}(s)\right\}}\right)ds.

The first integral above is greater than a strictly positive number independent of
\delta
, and the second integral can be made arbitrary close to zero as
\delta\downarrow 0
, due to (
4.16
). Therefore, there exists
\delta^{*}\in(0,t^{*}]
such that
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(t^{*}-\delta,x)>0
for all
\delta\in[0,\delta^{*}]
. Then,
\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}(s,x)>0
for all
s\in[t^{*}-\delta^{*},T)
, and this contradicts to the definition of
t^{*}
in (
4.21
). Therefore, we conclude that
t^{*}=0
, and now (
4.19
) implies that
\underline{y}(t)>0
for
t\in(0,T)
. Lastly,
\underline{y}(0)>0
is obtained by (
4.19
) and (
4.20
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Straightforward interpretations of Proposition
4.4
are as follows:

(i) The existence of the transaction costs for selling (buying, resp.) the stock makes the investor not to sell (buy, resp.) the stock when it is close to the terminal time. For short period of time, the benefit of rebalancing is small.

(ii) If
0<y_{\infty}
(
y_{\infty}<1
, resp), then the investor never rebalances to the zero-holding of the stock (bond, resp.). However, if the initial holding of the stock (bond, resp.) is zero, then the investor may not try to leave the state of zero-holding of the stock (bond, resp.), depending on the size of the transaction costs.

(iii) If
0<y_{\infty}<1
and there exist transaction costs, then the no-trade interval is non-trivial (with strictly positive length) all the time.

(iv) If
0<y_{\infty}
(
y_{\infty}<1
, resp.) and there is no cost for buying (selling, resp.) the stock, then just holding the bond (stock, resp.) and setting zero balance in the stock (bond, resp.) is suboptimal, even with the search frictions and transaction costs.

Remark 4.6
.

Obviously, if
y_{\infty}\leq 0
(
y_{\infty}\geq 1
, resp.), then there is no reason for buying (selling, resp.) the stock, so
\underline{y}(t)=0
(
\bar{y}(t)=1
, resp.) for
t\in[0,T)
.

5.
Asymptotic analysis

In this section, we provide asymptotic analysis for small transaction costs. To be specific, we focus on the first order approximation of the no-trade region and the value function with respect to the transaction cost parameter around zero. To consider non-trivial cases (see Remark
4.6
), we assume that the Merton fraction
y_{\infty}
is between zero and one, and we set
\bar{\epsilon}=\underline{\epsilon}
for convenience.

Assumption 5.1
.

In this section, we assume that
0<y_{\infty}<1
and
\bar{\epsilon}=\underline{\epsilon}=\epsilon
for
\epsilon\in[0,1)
.

Notation 5.2
.

(Current section only)

(i) To emphasize their dependence on the transaction cost parameter
\epsilon
, we denote
v,\underline{y},\bar{y},\hat{y},L,L_{y}
by
v^{\epsilon},\underline{y}^{\epsilon},\bar{y}^{\epsilon},\hat{y}^{\epsilon},L^{\epsilon},L_{y}^{\epsilon}
. In particular, when
\epsilon=0
, they are denoted by
v^{0},\underline{y}^{0},\bar{y}^{0},\hat{y}^{0},L^{0},L_{y}^{0}
.

(ii) Under Assumption
5.1
, Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.4
imply that

(5.1)

\displaystyle 0<\hat{y}^{0}(t,x)=\underline{y}^{0}(t)=\bar{y}^{0}(t)<1\quad\textrm{for}\quad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times[0,1].

In words,
\hat{y}^{0}(t,x)
is independent of variable
x
(just a function of
t
) and its value equals
\underline{y}^{0}(t)
and
\bar{y}^{0}(t)
. For convenience, we abuse notation and write
\hat{y}^{0}(t)
for
\hat{y}^{0}(t,x)
(i.e.,
\hat{y}^{0}(t)=\underline{y}^{0}(t)=\bar{y}^{0}(t)
).

We start with the technical lemma that is used in the proof of the asymptotic result.

Lemma 5.3
.

(i) Let
\epsilon=0
. For
t\in[0,T)
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\quad v_{x}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))=0,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in(0,1)}v_{xx}^{0}(t,x)<0.

(ii) Let
F:[0,T)\times(0,1)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be defined as

(5.4)

\displaystyle F(t,x):=\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)\cdot\sgn\left(\hat{y}^{0}(s)-Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)\right]ds.

Then, for
t\in[0,T)
,

(5.5)

\displaystyle-1<F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))<1.

(iii) Suppose that
x_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\,\,\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}x_{\epsilon}=x_{0}
. Then, for
t\in[0,T)
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,x_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=v_{x}^{0}(t,x_{0}),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}v_{xx}^{\epsilon}(t,x_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=v_{xx}^{0}(t,x_{0}).

Proof.

See Appendix.
∎

The following theorem provides the first order approximation of the no-trade boundaries.

Theorem 5.4
.

For
t\in[0,T)
,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)=\hat{y}^{0}(t)-\frac{F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-1}{v_{xx}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\cdot\epsilon+o(\epsilon),

(5.9)

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(t)=\hat{y}^{0}(t)-\frac{F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))+1}{v_{xx}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\cdot\epsilon+o(\epsilon),

where
F
is defined in (
5.4
). In particular, for small enough
\epsilon>0
, we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle 0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)<\hat{y}^{0}(t)<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(t)<1.

Proof.

For
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,1)
, the expression (
4.11
) implies that

(5.11)

\begin{split}\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,x)-v_{x}^{0}(t,x)\right|&=\left|\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)L_{y}^{\epsilon}(s,Y_{s}^{(t,x)})\right]ds\right|\\
&\leq\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)\right]ds\cdot\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\\
&\leq\lambda T\cdot e^{(\mu-r+\sigma^{2})T}\cdot\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},\end{split}

where the first inequality is due to (
4.13
) and the positivity of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}
in (
4.14
), and the second inequality is due to (
4.15
) and Assumption
5.1
.

Proposition
4.4
and Assumption
5.1
imply that
\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)<1
and
\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(t)>0
for
t\in[0,T)
. By Lemma
4.1
, if
0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)<1
, then
v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))=\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon\cdot\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)}
, and if
\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)=0
, then
-\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon x}<v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,x)<\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon x}
for
x\in(0,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(t))
. In any case, we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))\right|\leq\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},

where
v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,0):=\lim_{x\downarrow 0}v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,x)
is well-defined (see (
4.18
) for details) for the case of
\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)=0
.
We use the mean value theorem and (
5.2
) to obtain

(5.13)

\begin{split}\inf_{x\in(0,1)}\left|v_{xx}^{0}(t,x)\right|\cdot\left|\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)-\hat{y}^{0}(t)\right|&\leq\left|v_{x}^{0}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))-v_{x}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))\right|\\
&\leq\left|v_{x}^{0}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))-v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))\right|+\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))\right|.\end{split}

The above inequality, together with (
5.3
), (
5.11
), and (
5.12
), we conclude that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)-\hat{y}^{0}(t)=O(\epsilon)\quad\textrm{for}\quad t\in[0,T).

By the same way, we also obtain

(5.15)

\displaystyle\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(t)-\hat{y}^{0}(t)=O(\epsilon)\quad\textrm{for}\quad t\in[0,T).

The expression of
L_{y}^{\epsilon}
in (
4.10
), together with (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), implies the following limit:

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{L_{y}^{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}1,&\textrm{if }x\in(0,\hat{y}^{0}(t))\\
-1,&\textrm{if }x\in(\hat{y}^{0}(t),1)\\
\end{cases}\quad\textrm{for}\quad t\in[0,T).

For
(s,x)\in(t,T)\times(0,1)
, the observation
{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{s}^{(t,x)}=\hat{y}^{0}(t)\right)=0
and (
5.16
) produce

(5.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{L_{y}^{\epsilon}(s,Y_{s}^{(t,x)})}{\epsilon}=\sgn\left(\hat{y}^{0}(t)-Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

For
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times(0,1)
, the limit (
5.17
) and the inequalities (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) enable us to use the dominated convergence theorem to obtain

(5.18)

\begin{split}\frac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,x)-v_{x}^{0}(t,x)}{\epsilon}&=\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x}Y_{s}^{(t,x)}\right)\tfrac{L_{y}^{\epsilon}(s,Y_{s}^{(t,x)})}{\epsilon}\right]ds\xrightarrow[\epsilon\downarrow 0]{}F(t,x).\end{split}

The inequality
0<\hat{y}^{0}(t)<1
and (
5.14
) imply that
0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)<1
for small enough
\epsilon>0
. Hence, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

(5.19)

\displaystyle v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))=\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon\,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)}\quad\textrm{for small enough $\epsilon>0$.}\quad

By the mean value theorem, there exists
k(\epsilon)
such that

(5.20)

\displaystyle v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))-v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))=v_{xx}^{\epsilon}(t,k(\epsilon))\left(\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)-\hat{y}^{0}(t)\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}k(\epsilon)=\hat{y}^{0}(t).

Now we obtain (
5.8
) as follows:

(5.21)

\begin{split}\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)-\hat{y}^{0}(t)}{\epsilon}&=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t))-v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}{\epsilon\cdot v_{xx}^{\epsilon}(t,k(\epsilon))}\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{v_{xx}^{\epsilon}(t,k(\epsilon))}\left(\frac{1}{1+\epsilon\,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(t)}-\frac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-v_{x}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}{\epsilon}\right)\\
&=-\frac{F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-1}{v_{xx}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))},\end{split}

where the first equality is from (
5.20
), the second equality is due to (
5.19
) and (
5.2
), and the third equality is due to (
5.18
) and (
5.7
) with the limit in (
5.20
).
We also obtain (
5.9
) by the same way.

Due to (
5.3
) and (
5.5
), we observe that
-\frac{F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-1}{v_{xx}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}<0
and
-\frac{F(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))+1}{v_{xx}^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}>0
. Therefore, (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) imply that (
5.10
) holds for small enough
\epsilon>0
.
∎

The following theorem provides the first order approximation of the value function.

Theorem 5.5
.

For
t\in[0,T)
,

(5.22)

\displaystyle v^{\epsilon}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))=v^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-\left(G(t)+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}G(s)ds\right)\cdot\epsilon+o(\epsilon),

where
G:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined as

(5.23)

\displaystyle G(t):=\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}-\hat{y}^{0}(s)\right|\right]ds

Proof.

For
\epsilon>0
, we define a function
\psi^{\epsilon}:[0,T)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
as

(5.24)

\displaystyle\psi^{\epsilon}(t):=\frac{v^{\epsilon}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))-v^{0}(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}{\epsilon}.

Using (
4.8
) and (
4.12

(5.25)

\begin{split}\psi^{\epsilon}(t)&=\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\psi^{\epsilon}(s)ds+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}{\mathbb{E}}\left[I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right]ds,\end{split}

where the random variables
I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)},I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)},I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}
are defined as

(5.26)

\begin{split}I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}&:=\left(\tfrac{v^{\epsilon}(s,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s))-v^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\epsilon\,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)}{1+\epsilon\,Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}}\right)\right)\cdot 1_{\left\{Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\leq\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\right\}},\\
I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}&:=\left(\tfrac{v^{\epsilon}(s,Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))})-v^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}\right)\cdot 1_{\left\{\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\right\}},\\
I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}&:=\left(\tfrac{v^{\epsilon}(s,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s))-v^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\epsilon\,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)}{1-\epsilon\,Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}}\right)\right)\cdot 1_{\left\{\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\leq Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\right\}}.\end{split}

The mean value theorem produces

(5.27)

\displaystyle\left|\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\left(\tfrac{1+\epsilon\,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)}{1+\epsilon\,x}\right)\right|\leq 1\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left|\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\ln\left(\tfrac{1-\epsilon\,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)}{1-\epsilon\,x}\right)\right|\leq\tfrac{1}{1-\epsilon}\quad\textrm{for }x\in(0,1).

By Assumption
5.1
and Proposition
4.4
(ii), we obtain
\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1
and
\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)>0
for
s\in[0,T)
. The definition of

(5.28)

\displaystyle 1_{\left\{Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\leq\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\right\}}\leq 1_{\big\{0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\big\}},\quad 1_{\left\{\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\leq Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}\right\}}\leq 1_{\big\{0<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\big\}}.

The concavity of
v^{\epsilon}
on
x
variable (see Proposition
3.5
) and (
5.27

5.28
) imply

(5.29)

\begin{split}\left|I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right|&\leq\left(\max\left\{\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s))}{\epsilon}\right|,\,\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}\right|\right\}\cdot\left|\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)-\hat{y}^{0}(s)\right|+1\right)\cdot 1_{\left\{0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\right\}},\\
\left|I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right|&\leq\max\left\{\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))})}{\epsilon}\right|,\,\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}\right|\right\}\cdot\left|Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}-\hat{y}^{0}(s)\right|\cdot 1_{\left\{\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\right\}},\\
\left|I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right|&\leq\left(\max\left\{\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s))}{\epsilon}\right|,\,\left|\tfrac{v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))}{\epsilon}\right|\right\}\cdot\left|\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)-\hat{y}^{0}(s)\right|+\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}\right)\cdot 1_{\left\{0<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\right\}}.\end{split}

(5.30)

\displaystyle\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\hat{y}^{0}(s))\right|\leq\lambda T\cdot e^{(\mu-r+\sigma^{2})T}\cdot\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have the following inequalities:

(5.31)

\begin{split}&\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s))\right|\cdot 1_{\left\{0<\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\right\}}\leq\epsilon,\quad\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s))\right|\cdot 1_{\left\{0<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<1\right\}}\leq\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},\\
&\left|v_{x}^{\epsilon}(s,x)\right|\cdot 1_{\left\{\underline{y}^{\epsilon}(s)<x<\bar{y}^{\epsilon}(s)\right\}}\leq\tfrac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\quad\textrm{for}\quad x\in(0,1).\end{split}

Now we apply (
5.30
) and (
5.31
) to (

(5.32)

\begin{split}\left|I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right|\leq C\frac{1}{1-\epsilon},\end{split}

where
C
is a constant independent of
(t,s,\epsilon)
.
Also, Theorem
5.4
, the mean value theorem, and (
5.6
) imply that

(5.33)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\left(I_{1}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{2}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}+I_{3}^{(t,s,\epsilon)}\right)=-\left|Y_{s}^{(t,\hat{y}^{0}(t))}-\hat{y}^{0}(s)\right|\quad\textrm{almost surely.}

The uniform boundedness in (

(5.34)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}J^{\epsilon}(t)=-G(t)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{t}^{T}J^{\epsilon}(s)ds=-\int_{t}^{T}G(s)ds,

where
G
is defined in (

) and the function
J^{\epsilon}:[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined as

\displaystyle=\psi^{\epsilon}(t)-\lambda\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\psi^{\epsilon}(s)ds=-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}\psi^{\epsilon}(s)ds\right).

We integrate both sides above to obtain

Using (

\displaystyle\psi^{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=J^{\epsilon}(t)+\lambda\int_{t}^{T}J^{\epsilon}(s)ds.

Finally, (

Corollary 5.6

Remark 5.9
.

Proposition
5.8
implies that the effects of the transaction costs are more pronounced (more widening effect of the no-trade region and more diminishing effect of the value function) in the market with less search frictions. For better understanding of the result, it would be helpful to observe that the following two extreme cases (
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=\infty
) are consistent with the result:

(i) If we consider an extreme case of
\lambda=0
(i.e., no trading opportunity), the transaction costs do not affect the investor anyway. In words, in the market with very severe search frictions, the effects of the transaction costs are negligible.

(ii) If we consider an extreme case of
\lambda=\infty
(i.e., continuous trading opportunities), it is well known that the width of the no-trade region is the order of
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
and the decrease of the value function is the order of
\epsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}
. In contrast, when
\lambda<\infty
, Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
5.5
say that both the width of the no-trade region and the decrease of the value function are the order of
\epsilon
. Obviously, for small enough
\epsilon
, we have
\epsilon<\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}
and
\epsilon<\epsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}
. This implies that in the market with the search frictions (
\lambda<\infty
), the effects of the transaction costs are less pronounces, compared to the market with no search friction (
\lambda=\infty
).

6.
Conclusion

This paper presents an utility maximization problem of the terminal wealth, in a market with two different types of the illiquidity: search frictions and transaction costs. We show the existence of the solution to the HJB equation that is regular enough, and provide the verification argument. The optimal trading strategy is characterized by a no-trade region, where the boundary points of the no-trade region are uniquely determined by the strict concavity of the value function. In Proposition 4.4, we provide some conditions under which the investor wants to achieve zero/positive stock/bond holdings.
We provide the asymptotic expansions of the boundaries of the no-trade region and the value function for small transaction costs. We further show that reduction of the search frictions amplifies the effects of the transaction costs (more widening effect of the no-trade region and more diminishing effect of the value function).

As a future research, we plan to investigate a joint limiting behavior of the no-trade region and the value function for small transaction costs and large arrival rate. For that purpose, we think it could be useful to
consider some stationary versions of the problem with a hope to obtain explicit formulas independent of the time variable.

(C.9)

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|Y_{s}^{(t,x)}-x\right|\right]}{\sqrt{s-t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tfrac{x(1-x)}{x+(1-x)e^{(r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})u^{2}-\sigma uz}}\cdot\tfrac{\big|1-e^{(r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})u^{2}-\sigma uz}\big|}{u}\cdot\tfrac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dz\bigg|_{u=\sqrt{s-t}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{|r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}|u^{2}+\sigma u|z|}-e^{-|r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}|u^{2}-\sigma u|z|}}{u}\cdot\tfrac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dz\bigg|_{u=\sqrt{s-t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{(|r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}|+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})u^{2}}\left(1+\int_{0}^{\frac{\sigma u}{\sqrt{2}}}\frac{2e^{-z^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}dz\right)-e^{(-|r-\mu+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}|+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2})u^{2}}\left(1-\int_{0}^{\frac{\sigma u}{\sqrt{2}}}\frac{2e^{-z^{2}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}dz\right)}{u}\Bigg|_{u=\sqrt{s-t}},

where we use
|1-e^{a}|\leq e^{|a|}-e^{-|a|}
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
to obtain the inequality.
The last expression converges to
\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}\sigma}{\sqrt{\pi}}
as
u\downarrow 0
, therefore, it is bounded on
u\in(0,\sqrt{T}]
. Then, we conclude (
5.42
).

Observe that the integrand in (
C.9
) is bounded by
e^{C|z|}\cdot\tfrac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
for a constant
C
independent of
(u,x,z)\in(0,\sqrt{T}]\times(0,1)\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Since this bound is integrable with respect to
z
, we apply the dominated convergence theorem to (
C.9
) and obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{s\downarrow t}\tfrac{{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|Y_{s}^{(t,x)}-x\right|\right]}{\sqrt{s-t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x(1-x)\sigma|z|\cdot\tfrac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}dz=\sigma\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\,x(1-x).

Therefore, we conclude (
5.43
).

Appendix D
Supplementary Lemmas

Lemma D.1
.

Let
F:[0,T]\times[0,1]^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function. We define
f:[0,T]\times[0,1]\to[0,1]
as

(D.1)

\displaystyle f(t,x):=\max\left\{z:z\in\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}F(t,x,y)\right\},

then
f
is upper semicontinuous (which is obviously Borel-measurable).

Proof.

This type of result is well-known (e.g., see p. 153 in
[
7
]
), but we give a short proof here for the sake of self-containedness.

Since
F
is continuous,
\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}F(t,x,y)
is a nonempty closed subset of
[0,1]
, so the maximum element of
\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}F(t,x,y)
exists and
f
is well-defined. Let
\{(t_{n},x_{n})\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[0,T]\times[0,1]
be a sequence converging to
(t_{\infty},x_{\infty})
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t_{n},x_{n})
exists. Then, by definition of
f
,

(D.2)

\displaystyle F(t_{n},x_{n},f(t_{\infty},x_{\infty}))\leq F(t_{n},x_{n},f(t_{n},x_{n})).

We let
n\to\infty
above and using the continuity of
F
to obtain

(D.3)

\displaystyle F(t_{\infty},x_{\infty},f(t_{\infty},x_{\infty}))\leq F(t_{\infty},x_{\infty},\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t_{n},x_{n})).

This implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t_{n},x_{n})\in\argmax_{y\in[0,1]}F(t_{\infty},x_{\infty},y)
, and the definition of
f
ensures

f(t_{\infty},x_{\infty})\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}f(t_{n},x_{n}).

Therefore,
f
is upper semicontinuous.
∎

Lemma D.2
.

Let
f(s,x):[0,t]\times(0,1)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function, and
g(\lambda):[1,\infty)\rightarrow(0,1)
be a function satisfying
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}g(\lambda)=x_{\infty}\in(0,1)
. Then, for
\alpha\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
and
t>0
,

(D.4)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(s,g(\lambda))ds=c_{\alpha}\cdot f(0,x_{\infty}),\end{split}

where
c_{0}=1
,
c_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}
,
c_{2}=1
,
c_{3}=\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}
,
c_{4}=2
. Also, there exists a constant
C
such that

(D.5)

\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}ds\leq C,\quad\textrm{for}\quad(t,\lambda,\alpha)\in[0,\infty)\times[1,\infty)\times\{0,1,2,3,4\}.

Proof.

Let
\eta>0
be a given constant. The uniform continuity of
f
on a compact set containing the point
(0,x_{\infty})
, together with
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}g(\lambda)=x_{\infty}\in(0,1)
, implies that there exists
\delta>0
such that

(D.6)

\displaystyle\left|f(s,g(\lambda))-f(0,x_{\infty})\right|\leq\eta\quad\textrm{for any}\quad(s,\lambda)\in[0,\delta]\times\left[\tfrac{1}{\delta},\infty\right).

Simple computations and (
D.6
) produce

\begin{split}\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\Big|\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}f(s,g(\lambda))ds-&f(0,x_{\infty})\Big|=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\bigg|\int_{0}^{\delta}\lambda e^{-\lambda s}(f(s,g(\lambda))ds-f(0,x_{\infty}))ds\\
&+\int_{\delta}^{t}\lambda e^{-\lambda s}(f(s,g(\lambda))ds-f(0,x_{\infty}))ds-e^{-\lambda t}f(0,x_{\infty})\bigg|\leq\eta.\end{split}

Since
\eta>0
can be arbitrary small, we conclude (
D.4
) for the case of
\alpha=0
. The other cases in (
D.4
) can be obtained by the same way as above, using the following expressions:

(D.7)

\begin{split}&\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s^{\frac{1}{2}}ds=-\sqrt{\lambda t}e^{-\lambda t}+\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda t}}e^{-s^{2}}ds\xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2},\\
&\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s\,ds=1-(1+\lambda t)e^{-\lambda t}\xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}1,\\
&\lambda^{\frac{5}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s^{\frac{3}{2}}ds=-\tfrac{(3+2\lambda t)\sqrt{\lambda t}e^{-\lambda t}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda t}}e^{-s^{2}}ds\xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4},\\
&\lambda^{3}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda s}s^{2}ds=2-(2+\lambda t(2+\lambda t))e^{-\lambda t}\xrightarrow[\lambda\to\infty]{}2.\\
\end{split}

One can easily observe that
\sup_{x>0}x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-x}<\infty
for
\alpha\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
. This observation and the explicit expressions in (
D.7
) produce the bound (
D.5
).
∎

Hodge-Arakelov inequalities for family of surfaces fibered by curves
Mohammad Reza Rahmati

Abstract
. The Hodge numerical invariants of a variation of Hodge structure over a smooth quas–projective variety are a measure of complexity for the global twisting of the limit mixed Hodge structure when it degenerates. These invariants appear in inequalities which they may have correction terms, called Arakelov inequalities. One may investigate the correction term to make them into equalities, also called Arakelov equalities. We investigate numerical Arakelov type (in)equilities for a family of surfaces fibered by curves. Our method uses Arakelov identities in a weight 1 and also in a weight 2 variations of Hodge structure (cf. [ 19 ] ), in a commutative triangle of fibrations. We have proposed to relate the degrees of the Hodge bundles in the two families. We also compare the Fujita decomposition of Hodge bundles in these fibrations. We examine various identities and relations between Hodge numbers and degrees of the Hodge bundles in different levels.

1.
Introduction

Of important invariants of a variation of Hodge structure (VHS) that measure the complexity of their twist are the chern classes of their Hodge bundles. A question in this line is how the degeration of VHS affect these chern classes. The numerical data of a geometric variation of Hodge structure also provide tools to study the iso-triviality of the family. They also give bounds to determine global invariants associated to the singularities of the fibrations. For example one can answer simple questions on fibrations in low dimensions that, if they must have singular fibers and even some bounds for the number of singular fibers. The Arakelov identities involve the degrees of the Hodge bundles in VHS given by geometric fibration. In fact the vanishing of the degrees of all the Hodge bundle give a criteria to say whether the fibration is iso-trivial. These equalities have been systematically studied in low dimensions 1, 2, 3 and 4,

Theorem 5.13
.

In a commutative triangle fibration of surfaces fibred by curves we have

(113)

\delta_{X/S}^{2,0}\geq\frac{1}{2}(h_{X/Y}^{1,0}-h_{0,X/Y}^{1,0})(2g(S)-2)+h_{X/Y}^{1,0}

Moreover we have
\delta_{X}^{2,0}\ \geq\ \delta_{X/Y}^{1,0}
where the sub-indices denote the corresponding fibration.

Proof.

In a fibration of the form (8) for a generic fiber we shall have
X_{s}\mapstochar\rightarrow Y_{s}\mapstochar\rightarrow s\in S
, where
Y_{s}
is a curve and
X_{s}
is a surface. The variety
X_{s}
is also fibred over
Y_{s}
. Again a generic fiber over a point
p_{s}\in Y_{s}
is a curve namely
W_{p_{s}}
. Therefore apart from a finite number of points of degeneracies on
S

(114)

X_{s}=U_{s}\times W_{p_{s}},\qquad s\in T\subset S.

where
U_{s}
is open in
Y_{s}
. In both of the directions of
s
and
Y_{s}
there are finitely many singular fibers. By (114) the local system
\mathcal{H}
obtained from the middle cohomology of the fibration

is given as

where
\mathcal{V}
and

HS. It follows that

(116)

\mathcal{H}^{2,0}=\mathcal{V}^{1,0}\otimes\ \mathcal{L}^{1,0}

\displaystyle\delta^{2,0}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\delta^{1,0}(\mathcal{V})rank(\mathcal{L}^{1,0})+h^{1,0}(\mathcal{V})\deg(\mathcal{L}^{1,0})

Theorem 5.14
.

If the local system
\mathcal{V}=g_{*}\left(R^{1}h_{*}\mathbb{C}\right)
is irreducible with regular singularities, then

(118)

h^{p}(H^{1}(S,\mathcal{V}))=-\delta^{p}(V)-\left(h^{p}(V)-h^{p-1}(V)\right)(2g(S)-2)+\delta^{p-1}+\sum_{s}\nu_{s}(\bar{\theta})(V)

where
V=\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}
and
h^{i}(V)=\dim Gr_{F}^{i}(V)
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{V}=g_{*}\left(R^{1}h_{*}\mathbb{C}\right)
and
V=\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}
. Consider the resolution

(119)

{H}^{\bullet}:0\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nabla}}{{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega_{S}^{1}

Then by our assumption its only non-zero cohomology
H^{1}(S,\mathcal{V})
is a HS of
wt=2
,

(120)

F^{p}(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega_{S}^{\bullet})=F^{p-\bullet}\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega_{S}^{\bullet}

We know that the spectral sequence

(121)

\mathbb{H}^{r+s}(S,Gr_{F}^{r}(\Omega_{S}^{\bullet}\otimes\mathcal{V}))\Rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{r+s}(S,\mathcal{V})

degenerates at
E_{1}
. Computing the Euler characteristics on
Gr_{F}^{p}
of (119), gives

(122)

\displaystyle h^{p}\left(H^{1}(S,\mathcal{V})\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(Gr_{F}^{p}\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(S,V))=\chi(\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(S,Gr_{F}^{p}V)),\qquad\text{degeneration at}\ E_{1}

\displaystyle=-\chi(S,Gr_{F}^{p}V)+\chi(S,\Omega_{S}^{1}\otimes Gr_{F}^{p-1}V)

Applying the Riemann-Roch theorem we get

(123)

h^{p}(H^{1}(S,\mathcal{V}))=-\delta^{p}(V)-h^{p}(V)(2g(S)-2)+\delta^{p-1}+h^{p-1}(V)(2g(S)-2)+\sum_{s}\nu_{s}(\bar{\theta})(V)

where we have used
\deg(gr_{F}^{p-1}V)=\delta^{p-1}(V)+\sum_{s}\nu_{s}(\bar{\theta})(V)
.

∎

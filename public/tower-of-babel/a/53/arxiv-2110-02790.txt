Some approximate solutions to the spread of influenza A virus infection
Boldizsár Kalmár

Abstract
. We study differential equations describing the spread of influenza A virus infection based on a mathematical model. We look for surface trajectories of the dynamical system in hand and their asymptotic behaviour.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 35B40; Secondary 35Q92.

1.
Introduction

We consider the system of differential equations

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}T(x,t)&=&-\beta T(x,t)V(x,t)\\
\partial_{t}E_{1}(x,t)&=&\beta T(x,t)V(x,t)-\frac{n_{E}}{\tau_{E}}E_{1}(x,t)\\
\partial_{t}E_{i}(x,t)&=&\frac{n_{E}}{\tau_{E}}E_{i-1}(x,t)-\frac{n_{E}}{\tau_{E}}E_{i}(x,t)\\
\partial_{t}I_{1}(x,t)&=&\frac{n_{E}}{\tau_{E}}E_{n_{E}}(x,t)-\frac{n_{I}}{\tau_{I}}I_{1}(x,t)\\
\partial_{t}I_{j}(x,t)&=&\frac{n_{I}}{\tau_{I}}I_{j-1}(x,t)-\frac{n_{I}}{\tau_{I}}I_{j}(x,t)\\
\partial_{t}V(x,t)&=&p\sum_{j=1}^{n_{I}}I_{j}(x,t)-cV(x,t)+D_{PCF}a+v_{a}W(x,t)\\
\partial_{x}V(x,t)&=&W(x,t)\end{array}

where
i=2,\ldots,n_{E}
and
j=2,\ldots,n_{I}
.
We replace the value
\partial_{xx}V(x,t)
in the model
[
BQRY20
]

by the constant
a
, so in this paper
we deal with only the case of constant
\partial_{xx}V(x,t)
.
The constants

D_{PCF}
and
v_{a}
correspond to
the diffusion rate and to the upward transport
in the periciliary fluid. In the equations

c
denotes the viral clearance rate,

p
denotes the virion producing rate and
T
denotes the fraction of uninfected target cells.
We denote by
n_{I}
and
n_{E}
the numbers of age classes of infectious and eclipse phases, see
[
BQRY20
]
.

In the present paper, in the case of a system of equations with
n_{E}=0
,
we propose a heuristic which shows that around

T(x,t)=\frac{c}{\tau_{I}p\beta}
the system changes its behaviour.
We obtain that if
\tau_{I}p\beta T>c
, then
the trajectories tend to have asymptotic behaviour while for

\tau_{I}p\beta T<c
they do not.
Denote by

c_{E}
and
c_{I}
the values
\frac{n_{E}}{\tau_{E}}
and
\frac{n_{I}}{\tau_{I}}
.

To solve the equations,
we are looking for the smooth functions

T,E_{i},I_{j},V,W\colon\thinspace\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}.

This means we are looking for
a map

S\colon\thinspace\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x\\
t\end{array}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}T(x,t)\\
E_{1}(x,t)\\
\vdots\\
E_{n_{E}}(x,t)\\
I_{1}(x,t)\\
\vdots\\
I_{n_{I}}(x,t)\\
V(x,t)\\
W(x,t)\end{array}\right]\mbox{, \ \ \ \ \ }S^{\prime}(x,t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{x}T(x,t)&\partial_{t}T(x,t)\\
\partial_{x}E_{1}(x,t)&\partial_{t}E_{1}(x,t)\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\partial_{x}E_{n_{E}}(x,t)&\partial_{t}E_{n_{E}}(x,t)\\
\partial_{x}I_{1}(x,t)&\partial_{t}I_{1}(x,t)\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\partial_{x}I_{n_{I}}(x,t)&\partial_{t}I_{n_{I}}(x,t)\\
\partial_{x}V(x,t)&\partial_{t}V(x,t)\\
\partial_{x}W(x,t)&\partial_{t}W(x,t)\end{array}\right]

such that at every
(x_{0},t_{0})

the graph of the linear map

(x,t)\mapsto S^{\prime}(x_{0},t_{0})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x\\
t\end{array}\right]

is the same as
the
graph of the linear map

\Phi_{T,E_{1},\ldots,E_{n_{E}},I_{1},\ldots,I_{n_{I}},V,W}\colon\thinspace\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x\\
t\end{array}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\alpha&-\beta TV\\
\gamma_{0}&\beta TV-c_{E}E_{1}\\
\gamma_{1}&c_{E}E_{1}-c_{E}E_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\gamma_{n_{E}-1}&c_{E}E_{n_{E}-1}-c_{E}E_{n_{E}}\\
\delta_{0}&c_{E}E_{n_{E}}-c_{I}I_{1}\\
\delta_{1}&c_{I}I_{1}-c_{I}I_{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
\delta_{n_{I}-1}&c_{I}I_{n_{I}-1}-c_{I}I_{n_{I}}\\

a&\psi\end{array}\right]\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x\\

for all the points

(T,E_{1},\ldots,E_{n_{E}},I_{1},\ldots,I_{n_{I}},V,W)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{E}+n_{I}+3},

where

\alpha,\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n_{E}-1},\delta_{0},\ldots,\delta_{n_{I}-1},\psi\colon\thinspace(T,E_{1},\ldots,E_{n_{E}},I_{1},\ldots,I_{n_{I}},V,W)\to\mathbb{R}

are
some given smooth functions.

If the linear maps

\Phi_{T,E_{1},\ldots,E_{n_{E}},I_{1},\ldots,I_{n_{I}},V,W}

have rank two, then their graphs
form a
2
-distribution
in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{E}+n_{I}+3}
and we are looking for integral surfaces (graphs of the solutions

) of this distribution.

2

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

(-1)^{n_{E}+n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)=(c_{E}+\lambda)^{n_{E}}(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}(c+\lambda)\lambda+\beta Tc_{E}^{n_{E}}p\left(c_{I}^{n_{I}}-(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\right)

for the characteristic polynomial of
A
.

Proof.

We compute the determinant of

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc|cccccc}-c_{E}-\lambda&0&\cdots&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0&\beta T&0\\
c_{E}&-c_{E}-\lambda&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&c_{E}&-c_{E}-\lambda&0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&0\\

0&\cdots&0&0&0&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&\cdots&0&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0&0\\
0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&p&\cdots&\cdots&p&-c-\lambda&v_{a}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&-\lambda\end{array}\right]

It is equal to

(-c_{E}-\lambda)^{n_{E}}(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}}(-c-\lambda)(-\lambda)+(-1)^{n_{E}+n_{I}}\beta Tc_{E}^{n_{E}}\det B,

where

B=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0&\cdots&0\\
0&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0&0\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0\\
p&\cdots&\cdots&p&v_{a}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&0&-\lambda\end{array}\right]

and

\det B=(-\lambda)\det B_{n_{I}}\mbox{, \ \ \ \ }B_{k}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0&\cdots\\
0&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda&0\\
\vdots&&&\\
0&\cdots&c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda\\
p&\cdots&\cdots&p\end{array}\right]

where
3\leq k\leq n_{I}
.
We have

\det B_{k}=c_{I}\det B_{k-1}+(-1)^{k+1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{k-1}

for
k\geq 3
and

\det B_{2}=\det\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{I}&-c_{I}-\lambda\\
p&p\end{array}\right]=c_{I}p-p(-c_{I}-\lambda).

Hence

\det B=(-\lambda)\left(c_{I}\det B_{n_{I}-1}+(-1)^{n_{I}+1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}\right)=\\
(-\lambda)\left(c_{I}\left(c_{I}\det B_{n_{I}-2}+(-1)^{n_{I}}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-2}\right)+(-1)^{n_{I}+1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}\right)=\\
(-\lambda)\left(c_{I}^{2}\det B_{n_{I}-2}+c_{I}(-1)^{n_{I}}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-2}+(-1)^{n_{I}+1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}\right)=\\
(-\lambda)(c_{I}^{3}\det B_{n_{I}-3}+c_{I}^{2}(-1)^{n_{I}-1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-3}+c_{I}(-1)^{n_{I}}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-2}+\\
(-1)^{n_{I}+1}p(-c_{I}-\lambda)^{n_{I}-1})=\cdots=\\

This means that

(-1)^{n_{E}+n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)=(c_{E}+\lambda)^{n_{E}}(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}(c+\lambda)\lambda+\\
\beta Tc_{E}^{n_{E}}p(-\lambda)(c_{I}^{n_{I}-1}+c_{I}^{n_{I}-2}(c_{I}+\lambda)+c_{I}^{n_{I}-3}(c_{I}+\lambda)^{2}+\cdots\\

∎

Corollary 2.2
.

If
n_{E}=0
, then

(-1)^{n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)=(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}(c+\lambda)\lambda+\beta Tp\left(c_{I}^{n_{I}}-(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\right).

3.
Results

Recall that
c
denotes the viral clearance rate,

p
denotes the virion producing rate and
T
denotes the fraction of uninfected target cells.
We denote by
n_{I}
and
n_{E}
the numbers of age classes of infectious and eclipse phases and we have
c_{I}=n_{I}/\tau_{I}

and
c_{E}=n_{E}/\tau_{E}

3.1.
The case of
n_{E}=0

If we suppose that
n_{E}=0
, then
by Corollary
2.2
we have

(-1)^{n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)=(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}(c+\lambda)\lambda+\beta Tp\left(c_{I}^{n_{I}}-(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\right)=\\
(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\left((c+\lambda)\lambda-\beta Tp\right)+\beta Tpc_{I}^{n_{I}}=(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\left(\lambda^{2}+c\lambda-\beta Tp\right)+\beta Tpc_{I}^{n_{I}},

denote
(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\left(\lambda^{2}+c\lambda-\beta Tp\right)
by
F(\lambda)
. Of course
F(0)<0
so

0
is between the roots

\frac{-c\pm\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp}}{2}

of
\lambda^{2}+c\lambda-\beta Tp
.
This implies for example that

The derivative of
(-1)^{n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)
is the same as the derivative of
F(\lambda)
, which is

n_{I}(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}(c+\lambda)\lambda+(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\lambda+(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}(c+\lambda)-\beta Tpn_{I}(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}=\\
(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}\left(n_{I}(c+\lambda)\lambda+(c_{I}+\lambda)(c+2\lambda)-\beta Tpn_{I}\right)=\\
(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}-1}\left((n_{I}+2)\lambda^{2}+(cn_{I}+c+2c_{I})\lambda+cc_{I}-\beta Tpn_{I}\right).

Observe that

(-c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2\leq-c_{I}

is impossible since

0<c_{I}\leq(c-\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2<0

is impossible.

3.1.1.
The case of
-c_{I}\leq(-c-\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2

Suppose that

-c_{I}\leq(-c-\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2

so
F(\lambda)
has two or three different roots, more simply

c_{I}\geq(c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2

2(c_{I}-c/2)\geq\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp},

which is equivalent to

4(c_{I}-c/2)^{2}\geq c^{2}+4\beta Tp

because

2(c_{I}-c/2)\leq-\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp}

is not possible, so

c_{I}^{2}-cc_{I}\geq\beta Tp,

and note that this also implies that

c_{I}>c.

If
n_{I}
is even, then

(c_{I}+\lambda)^{n_{I}}\left((c+\lambda)\lambda-\beta Tp\right)+\beta Tpc_{I}^{n_{I}}

has a horizontal tangency
at

\lambda_{a}=-c_{I}

and a local minimum at

\lambda_{b}=\frac{-c(n_{I}+1)-2c_{I}+\sqrt{\left(c(n_{I}+1)+2c_{I}\right)^{2}-4(n_{I}+2)(cc_{I}-\beta Tpn_{I})}}{2(n_{I}+2)}

\lim_{\pm\infty}F=\infty.

Since

F(0)=-\beta Tpc_{I}^{n_{I}}
,
we have
F(\lambda_{2})<0
so it also has
at most one singular point between
\lambda_{a}
and
\lambda_{b}

We have that

(-1)^{n_{I}}\det(A-\lambda I)=F(\lambda)+\beta Tpc_{I}^{n_{I}}

is equal to
0

\lambda=0
.
These imply that
if

cc_{I}=\beta Tpn_{I},

then
the root of
\det(A-\lambda I)

is only
\lambda_{0}=0

cc_{I}<\beta Tpn_{I},

then

\lambda_{0}=0

\lambda_{1}>0

cc_{I}>\beta Tpn_{I},

the roots are
\lambda_{0}=0
and some
-c_{I}<\lambda_{1}<0

If
n_{I}\geq 3
is odd, then

\lambda=-c_{I}
is just an inflection point and then we have the analogous result about the roots
but in every case there is another root
\lambda_{2}<-c_{I}
as well.

3.1.2.
The case of
(-c-\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2<-c_{I}<(-c+\sqrt{c^{2}+4\beta Tp})/2

In the case of

Proposition 3.4
.

The geometric multiplicity
of every real eigenvalue is one.

Proof.

We have to solve the equation

(A-\lambda I)x=0,

which is just

\begin{array}[]{ccc}(-c_{I}-\lambda)I_{1}+\beta TV=0,\\
c_{I}I_{1}+(-c_{I}-\lambda)I_{2}=0,\\
\ldots\\
c_{I}I_{n_{I}-1}+(-c_{I}-\lambda)I_{n_{I}}=0,\\
p\sum_{i=1}^{n_{I}}I_{i}+(-c-\lambda)V+v_{a}W=0,\\
-\lambda W=0.\end{array}

This implies
W=0
and since
substituting
the first
n_{I}
equations into

p\sum_{i=1}^{n_{I}}I_{i}+(-c-\lambda)V=0

results
\det(A-\lambda I)=0
, which can be seen after a short computation, the solution of

(A-\lambda I)x=0
is

W=0,\mbox{\ \ \ }V\in\mathbb{R},\mbox{\ \ \ }I_{k}=\frac{c_{I}^{k-1}}{(c_{I}+\lambda)^{k}}\beta TV,

which means that the geometric multiplicity is equal to
1

for
\lambda\neq 0
.
If
\lambda=0
, then
the solution is

V\in\mathbb{R},\mbox{\ \ \ }I_{1}=\beta TV/c_{I},\mbox{\ \ \ }I_{k}=I_{k-1},\mbox{\ \ \ }p\sum_{i=1}^{n_{I}}I_{i}-cV+v_{a}W=0

so
the geometric multiplicity is equal to
1
.
∎

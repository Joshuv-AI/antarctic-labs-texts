On the Notion of a Function of Bounded Variation and of Riemann-Stieltjes Integral with Strong Partitions on Hyperbolic Intervals
G. Y. Tellez-Sanchez, J. Bory-Reyes

Abstract
In this paper we provided a classification for partitions of intervals on the hyperbolic plane. Given a partition, to be named strong, we define a notion of a hyperbolic-valued functions of bounded variation and a kind of Riemann-Stieltjes integral. A condition relating to both concepts appears to be natural for the existence of the integral, as it occurs in real analysis.



Keywords and Phrases.
Hyperbolic numbers, partitions, function of bounded variation, Riemann-Stieltjes integral.

30G35, 28B15, 26B15.

2
Hyperbolic-valued function theory

2.1
Hyperbolic Numbers

The ring of hyperbolic numbers is generated by real numbers and one imaginary unit
{\bf k}\not\in{\mathbb{R}}
with the property that
{\bf k}^{2}=1
, which we can write as

\mathbb{K}:={\mathbb{R}}[{\bf k}]=\{t+s{\bf k}\ |\ t,s\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

In a similar way like complex numbers,
\mathbb{K}
can be identified with the Euclidean plane and it is customary to call hyperbolic numbers plane.

Complex and hyperbolic numbers are different number systems because the first has the structure of field meanwhile the second is a commutative unit ring with zero divisors.

There are two important zero divisors in
\mathbb{K}
:

{\bf e}_{1}:=\frac{1+{\bf k}}{2},\quad{\bf e}_{2}:=\frac{1-{\bf k}}{2}.

They are idempotent elements and the product of both numbers is equal to zero.

Although
{\bf e}_{1}
and
{\bf e}_{2}
are zero divisors, every element
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
is represented in an unique real linear combination of these idempotent elements.

\alpha=t+s{\bf k}\Rightarrow\alpha=(t+s){\bf e}_{1}+(t-s){\bf e}_{2}.

\alpha=a_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+a_{2}{\bf e}_{2}\Rightarrow\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})+\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}-a_{2}){\bf k}.

When
\alpha
is taken as
a_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+a_{2}{\bf e}_{2}
, it will be said that
\alpha
is in idempotent representation.

Using idempotent representation, the hyperbolic numbers plane is identify with
{\mathbb{R}}{\bf e}_{1}+{\mathbb{R}}{\bf e}_{2}
and there exists a ring isomorphism between
\mathbb{K}
and the direct product
{\mathbb{R}}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
, see for instance
[
17
,
11
]
. The real line is endowed in
\mathbb{K}
by the mapping
x\mapsto x{\bf e}_{1}+x{\bf e}_{2}
. Also, the idempotent projections of a subset
A\subset\mathbb{K}
are given by

A_{{\bf e}_{1}}:=\{a\in{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ \exists b\in{\mathbb{R}},\ a{\bf e}_{1}+b{\bf e}_{2}\in A\},

A_{{\bf e}_{2}}:=\{b\in{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ \exists a\in{\mathbb{R}},\ a{\bf e}_{1}+b{\bf e}_{2}\in A\}.

2.2
Partial order

On the realization of hypercomplex numbers, a partial order relation is defined in
[
9
]
. For two hyperbolic numbers
\alpha=a_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+a_{2}{\bf e}_{2}
and
\beta=b_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+b_{2}{\bf e}_{2}
are related by
\preceq
, if

a_{1}\leq b_{1}\ \land\ a_{2}\leq b_{2}.

One can check that this relation is reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric and represents a good generalization for the total order in the real numbers.

Strict order for hyperbolic numbers is defined by the rule

\alpha\prec\beta\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a_{1}<b_{1}\ \land\ a_{2}<b_{2}.

Here, strict order is handled in a different way like that defined at
[
8
]
, where it is considered only one of the two cases:
a_{1}=b_{1}
or
a_{2}=b_{2}
.

Closed and open intervals are defined relative to the partial order as the sets

\mbox{Closed: }[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{K}\ |\ \alpha\preceq\xi\preceq\beta\},

\mbox{Open: }(\alpha,\beta)_{{\bf k}}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{K}\ |\ \alpha\prec\xi\prec\beta\}.

The length of a hyperbolic interval
\mathfrak{I}=[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
, is by definition, the number:

\lambda_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{I}):=\beta-\alpha.

(2-E1)

A closed interval
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
is said to be degenerated when
a_{1}=b_{1}
or
a_{2}=b_{2}
. This type of intervals are identified with a line segment bounded by
\alpha
and
\beta
.

2.3
Holomorphic hyperbolic-valued functions

The conjugate of a hyperbolic number
\alpha=t+s{\bf k}
is another hyperbolic number denoted by
\alpha^{\dagger}:=t-s{\bf k}
. But when
\alpha
is in idempotent representation
\alpha=a_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+a_{2}{\bf e}_{2}
, its conjugate is getting by
\alpha^{\dagger}:=a_{2}{\bf e}_{1}+a_{1}{\bf e}_{2}
.

We will consider the hyperbolic-valued module of a hyperbolic number
\alpha
defined by

|\alpha|_{k}:=|a_{1}|{\bf e}_{1}+|a_{2}|{\bf e}_{2}.

Since
|\alpha|_{{\bf k}}=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}}
, it is reasonable that the behaviour of hyperbolic module should in some sense approximate the behaviour of real module. Moreover, the hyperbolic-valued module generates a hyperbolic-valued metric (see
[
8
,
7
,
9
]
) given by

D_{{\bf k}}=|\alpha-\beta|_{{\bf k}}

with the properties

1.

D_{{\bf k}}(\alpha,\beta)=0
, if and only if
\alpha=\beta
.

2.

D_{{\bf k}}(\alpha,\beta)=D_{{\bf k}}(\beta,\alpha)
.

3.

D_{{\bf k}}(\alpha,\gamma)\preceq D_{{\bf k}}(\alpha,\beta)+D_{{\bf k}}(\beta,\gamma)
.

We call
D_{{\bf k}}
the usual hyperbolic metric over
\mathbb{K}
.

With the structure of hyperbolic metric space, the concept of continuous function was introduced in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
F
be a hyperbolic-valued function over
\Omega\subset\mathbb{K}
. We say that
F
is a
\mathbb{K}
-continuous function in
\xi\in\Omega
, when given a hyperbolic positive number
\epsilon
there exists another hyperbolic positive number
\delta
such that
D_{{\bf k}}(\xi,\zeta)\prec\delta
, with the condition

D_{{\bf k}}(F(\xi),F(\zeta))\prec\epsilon.

We will need the requirement on hyperbolic-valued functions
F=F_{1}+F_{2}{\bf k}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
that it have real-valued components
F_{1},F_{2}
of one variable, which is guaranteed on the idempotent representation if, for instance,
F
satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann type system

\frac{\partial F_{1}(\xi)}{\partial x_{1}}=\frac{\partial F_{1}(\xi)}{\partial x_{2}},\quad\land\quad\frac{\partial F_{1}(\xi)}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{\partial F_{2}(\xi)}{\partial x_{1}},

(2-E2)

for
\xi=x_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+x_{2}{\bf e}_{2}\in\Omega
.

When
F=F(\xi)=F_{1}(x_{1}){\bf e}_{1}+F_{2}(x_{2}){\bf e}_{2}
fulfill the system
2-E2
, we obtain what is known as
\mathbb{K}
-derivable or holomorphic hyperbolic-valued function, with derivative

F^{\prime}(\xi):=dF_{1}(x_{1}){\bf e}_{1}+dF_{2}(x_{2}){\bf e}_{2},

where
dF_{1},dF_{2}
are the real derivatives of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively. For a deeper discussion of this result and properties of holomorphic hyperbolic-valued functions we refer the reader to
[
11
,
17
,
18
]
.

A holomorphic function defined over an open connected set
\Omega
, is extended to
\Omega_{{\bf e}_{1}}{\bf e}_{1}+\Omega_{{\bf e}_{2}}{\bf e}_{2}
, to be the open interval
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})_{{\bf k}}
with

\omega_{1}=\inf\Omega_{{\bf e}_{1}}{\bf e}_{1}+\inf\Omega_{{\bf e}_{2}}{\bf e}_{2},\quad\omega_{2}=\sup\Omega_{{\bf e}_{1}}{\bf e}_{1}+\sup\Omega_{{\bf e}_{2}}{\bf e}_{2}.

This result appears in
[
17
]
.

3
Classification of Hyperbolic Partitions

A non-degenerated hyperbolic interval can be consider like a rectangle in the Euclidean plane, therefore it is possible to divide the interval
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
by a classical partition
P=P_{1}\times P_{2}
, where
P_{1}\subset[a_{1},b_{1}]
and
P_{2}\subset[a_{2},b_{2}]
are partitions from its respective intervals. A shortcoming of this division is the inability to obtain the length of
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
equals the sum of the length of the union of sub-rectangles of
P
, see
[
19
]
.

Example 3.1
.

Let the interval
\mathfrak{I}=[0,1]_{{\bf k}}
divided by the following nine sub-intervals:

\small\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathfrak{I}_{1}=\left[0,\frac{1}{3}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{2}=\left[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{3}=\left[\frac{2}{3},1\right]_{{\bf k}},\\
\mathfrak{I}_{4}=\left[\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{1},\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{5}=\left[\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{1},1{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{6}=\left[\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{2},1{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}},\\
\mathfrak{I}_{7}=\left[\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{2},\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{8}=\left[\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{2},\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+1{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}},&\mathfrak{I}_{9}=\left[\frac{1}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{2},\frac{2}{3}{\bf e}_{1}+1{\bf e}_{2}\right]_{{\bf k}}.\end{array}

By the definition (
2-E1
), the sum of the lengths of all sub-intervals
\mathfrak{I}_{j}
, with
j\in\{1,...,9\}
, is not equal to the length of
[0,1]_{{\bf k}}
.

\lambda_{k}\left(\mathfrak{I}\right)=1\neq 3=\sum_{j=1}^{9}\lambda_{{\bf k}}\left(\mathfrak{I}_{j}\right).

We now indicate how that difficulty can be circumvented.

Definition 3.2
.

Any finite collection
S_{1},...,S_{n}
of sub-intervals into which
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
is divided:

[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}S_{j},

will be called a regular partition if

\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left(S_{j}\right),

where
\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure in the Euclidean plane.

Remark 3.3
.

Definition
3.2
is not restricted to the case of sub-rectangles generated by the Cartesian product of partitions from real intervals like in Example
3.1
.

Let us introduce the following natural definition of hyperbolic interval partition.

Definition 3.4
.

Any collection
\mathcal{I}
of sub-intervals into which
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
is divided:

[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}I

is said to be a weak partition if

\lambda_{{\bf k}}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\lambda_{{\bf k}}(I).

In

Definition 3.6
.

Let

\rho_{s}\neq\rho_{t}

\mathfrak{P}

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{{\bf k}}([\rho_{j-1},\rho_{j}]_{{\bf k}})=\lambda_{{\bf k}}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}).

p_{s_{j}}\in P

4
Hyperbolic Functions of Bounded Variation

Let
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\subset\mathbb{K}
and
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
a hyperbolic-valued function with idempotent representation
F=F_{1}{\bf e}_{1}+F_{2}{\bf e}_{2}
.

If
\mathfrak{P}=\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},...,\rho_{n}\}
is a strong partition of
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
, then we can consider the quantity

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}&|F(\rho_{j+1})-F(\rho_{j})|_{{\bf k}}=\\
&\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(|F_{1}(\rho_{j+1})-F_{1}(\rho_{j})|{\bf e}_{1}+|F_{2}(\rho_{j+1})-F_{2}(\rho_{j})|{\bf e}_{2}\right)=\\
&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|F_{1}(\rho_{j+1})-F_{1}(\rho_{j})|\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|F_{2}(\rho_{j+1})-F_{2}(\rho_{j})|\right){\bf e}_{2}=\\
&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2}.\end{split}

(4-E5)

Let
\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})
denotes the family of all strong partitions for
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
and it defines the set

\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F):=\left\{\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2}\ \big|\ \mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})\right\}.

Definition 4.1
.

A hyperbolic-valued function
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is said to have bounded variation, if

\operatorname{Sup}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F)\right)\prec\infty.

The supremum is taken like in references
[
14
,
13
,
10
,
12
]
. On a nonempty set
A\subset\mathbb{K}
,

\operatorname{Sup}(A):=\sup(A_{{\bf e}_{1}}){\bf e}_{1}+\sup(A_{{\bf e}_{2}}){\bf e}_{2}.

So, to say that a function has bounded variation is equivalent to require that

\left(\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F)\right)_{{\bf e}_{1}}\text{ and }\quad\left(\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F)\right)_{{\bf e}_{2}}

are bounded sets of real numbers.

Definition 4.2
.

The total variation of a function of bounded variation
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is given by

\mathcal{V}_{[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}}(F):=\operatorname{Sup}\left(\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F)\right).

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}}(F)&=\sup_{\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\sup_{\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2}.\end{split}

Components of a function that satisfies Def.
4.1
do not necessarily have Vitali variation finite (see
[
31
,
30
,
29
,
28
]
for definition of Vitali variation). A function of finite Vitali variation takes account the four vertex from every sub-interval (or sub-square) in the real plane generated in a regular partition. Meanwhile the hyperbolic version only takes two extremes (or two vertex) from every interval in a strong partitions, which is a proper subset of the regular partition.

Special case to consider are functions when every component depends only on the respective component of the variable. So, let
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a function such that
F\left(\xi\right)=F_{1}(x_{1}){\bf e}_{1}+F_{2}(x_{2}){\bf e}_{2}
. Eq.
4-E5
transforms to:

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}&|F(\rho_{j+1})-F(\rho_{j})|_{{\bf k}}=\\
&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}|F_{1}(p_{j+1,1})-F_{1}(p_{j,1})|\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}|F_{2}(p_{j+1,2})-F_{2}(p_{j,2})|\right){\bf e}_{2}.\end{split}

(4-E6)

Remark 4.3
.

Eq.
3-E3
implies that the sums in Eq.
4-E5
are taken over the projections
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}}
,
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{2}}
, which are partitions of the real intervals
\left([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\right)_{{\bf e}_{1}}=[a_{1},b_{1}]
and
\left([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\right)_{{\bf e}_{2}}=[a_{2},b_{2}]
respectively.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{P}([a_{j},b_{j}])
the collection of all partitions of the real interval
[a_{j},b_{j}]
for every
j\in\{1,2\}
and introduce the following sets

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathcal{P}([a_{j},b_{j}])}(F_{j})=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n_{P}-1}|F_{j}(p_{j+1})-F_{j}(p_{j})|\ |\ P\in\mathcal{P}([a_{j},b_{j}])\right\}.\end{split}

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a function with every component depending on the respective component of the variable. Then

\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F)=\sum_{\mathcal{P}([a_{1},b_{1}])}(F_{1}){\bf e}_{1}+\sum_{\mathcal{P}([a_{2},b_{2}])}(F_{2}){\bf e}_{2}.

Proof.

Suppose we are given a point of the set
\sum_{\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})}(F).

By Remark
4.3
, the projections
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{2}}
are partitions over
[a_{1},b_{1}]
and
[a_{2},b_{2}]
respectively and it takes the form in the Eq.
4-E6
.

Reciprocally, two partitions
P\in\mathcal{P}([a_{1},b_{1}])
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}([a_{2},b_{2}])
define a strong partition
\mathfrak{P}
(see Eq.
3-E4
). Partition
\mathfrak{P}
fulfill with
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}}=P
and
\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{2}}=Q
, even if the process generates
n_{\mathfrak{P}}=n_{P}+n_{Q}
points, where
n_{P}
and
n_{Q}
denote the cardinality of
P
and
Q
, no additional elements in the sum are added, since in degenerated intervals
|F_{1}(p_{j+1})-F_{1}(p_{j})|=0
or
|F_{2}(p_{j+1})-F_{2}(p_{j})|=0
, implying that

\begin{split}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{1}\right)&{\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}\Delta_{\mathfrak{P},j}F_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2}=\\
&\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{P}-1}|F_{1}(p_{j+1})-F_{1}(p_{j})|\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{Q}-1}|F_{2}(q_{j+1})-F_{2}(q_{j})|\right){\bf e}_{2}.\end{split}

∎

It is combination of Def.
4.1
with Thm.
4.4
, that makes definition of hyperbolic-valued functions of bounded variation allowable.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a function given by Eq.
4-E6
. The function
F
has hyperbolic bounded variation if and only if the idempotent component functions
F_{1}:[a_{1},b_{1}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
F_{2}:[a_{2},b_{2}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
are functions of real bounded variation.

On account of this result, the set of all points of discontinuity of a hyperbolic function of bounded variation is well defined, which is due to the fact that a real function of bounded variation only has jump discontinuities and therefore the set of all points of discontinuity is numerable, see
[
27
, Sec. 6.8]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a hyperbolic-valued functions of bounded variation which every component only depends of the respective variable component. Then, the set of all points of discontinuity of
F
consists of a numerable union of perpendicular line segments to idempotent axes.

Proof.

The components
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
from
F
are real functions of bounded variation so, there exist two set
\{x_{1,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[a_{1},b_{1}]
and
\{x_{2,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset[a_{2},b_{2}]
do consist of all discontinuities for
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively.

For every point
y\in[a_{2},b_{2}]
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the point
x_{1,n}{\bf e}_{1}+y{\bf e}_{2}
is a point of discontinuity of
F
. Thus, the set of discontinuities points contains the union

\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}x_{1,n}{\bf e}_{1}+[a_{2},b_{2}]{\bf e}_{2}.

Similarly, the set of discontinuities points of
F
contains the union

\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}[a_{1},b_{1}]{\bf e}_{1}+x_{2,n}{\bf e}_{2}.

It follows that the union

\mathfrak{D}(F):=\left(\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}x_{1,n}{\bf e}_{1}+[a_{2},b_{2}]{\bf e}_{2}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}[a_{1},b_{1}]{\bf e}_{1}+x_{2,n}{\bf e}_{2}\right)

contains all point of discontinuity of
F
, which is clear from the fact that if there exists
\xi=x{\bf e}_{1}+y{\bf e}_{2}
a discontinuity point of
F
, then
x
will be a discontinuity point of
F_{1}
or
y
a discontinuity point of
F_{2}
, which imply that
x\in\{x_{1,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
or
y\in\{x_{2,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

The set of all discontinuity points of a hyperbolic-valued function of bounded variation whose components depend only of the respective component of the variable, has a zero Lebesgue measure in the Euclidean plane.

Proof.

The idea of the proof lies in the fact that every line has zero measure in the Euclidean plane and numerable union of them is again zero measure.
∎

Note that similar result fails with hyperbolic Lebesgue measure, defined in
[
6
]
, replacing Lebesgue measure. Since, the Lebesgue measure is identified with
\mu=\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\bf e}_{1}+\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\bf e}_{2}
, where
\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is the Lebesgue measure on the real line, the set
x_{1,n}{\bf e}_{1}+[a_{2},b_{2}]{\bf e}_{2}
has not zero measure for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which is due to the fact that
[a_{2},b_{2}]
is not a zero measure real set.

5
Hyperbolic-valued Riemann-Stieltjes Integral

The diameter of a real partition
P
is defined as the maximum into the set of all lengths of successive intervals generated by
P
,

\operatorname{diam}(P)=\max\{\lambda([p_{j+1},p_{j}])\ |\ j\in\{0,...,n-1\}\}.

It is clear that there exists a natural extension of the notion of diameter of a partition to the case of strong partitions if we replace maximum by supremum, but this is not beneficial in the effort to formulate a Riemann-Stieltjes integral.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a strong partition of
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
. The diameter of
\mathfrak{P}
is defined to be the hyperbolic number

\operatorname{diam}_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P})=\operatorname{diam}(\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}}){\bf e}_{1}+\operatorname{diam}(\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{2}}){\bf e}_{2}.

The relation between the concepts of diameter and strong partition yields a notion of Riemann-Stieltjes Integral.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
and
G:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be two hyperbolic functions. A hyperbolic number
\mathcal{I}
is called the Riemann-Stieltjes integral of
F
respect to
G
, if for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
there exists a
\delta\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
such that

\left|S_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P},F,G)-\mathcal{I}\right|_{{\bf k}}=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}F(\gamma_{j})\left|G(\rho_{j+1})-G(\rho_{j})\right|_{{\bf k}}-\mathcal{I}\right|_{{\bf k}}\prec\epsilon,

for any strong partition
\mathfrak{P}\in\mathcal{P}([\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}})
that fulfill the property
\operatorname{diam}_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P})\prec\delta
and whatever selection
\gamma_{j}\in[\rho_{j+1},\rho_{j}]_{{\bf k}}
, with
j\in\{0,...,n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1\}.

The quantity
S_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P},F,G)
is called the Riemann-Stieltjes sum. In addition, when such
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{K}
exists, it will be denoted by
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}:=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}G
.

If
F
and
G
in Def.
5.2
are assumed to have components that only depend of the respective component from the variable, then the Riemann-Stieltjes sum is analogue to Eq.
4-E6
and hence

\begin{split}S_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P},F,G)&=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}F_{1}(y_{j,1})\left|G_{1}(p_{j+1,1})-G_{1}(p_{j,1})\right|\right){\bf e}_{1}\\
&+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n_{\mathfrak{P}}-1}F_{2}(y_{j,2})\left|G_{2}(p_{j+1,2})-G_{2}(p_{j,2})\right|\right){\bf e}_{2}.\end{split}

Since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are real valued functions defined on the respective projections of
[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}
, likewise
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are real valued functions defined on the whole real line, hence the Riemann-Stieltjes sum over the hyperbolic plane is the idempotent sum of two classic Riemann-Stieltjes sums on the partition generated by projections of
\mathfrak{P}
:

S_{{\bf k}}(\mathfrak{P},F,G)=S(\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{1}},F_{1},G_{1}){\bf e}_{1}+S(\mathfrak{P}_{{\bf e}_{2}},F_{2},G_{2}){\bf e}_{2}.

This construction generalized that of
[
27
, Sec. 7.3]
.

From now on all functions will be considered with components depending only of the respective component from the variable.

Component-wise operation shows that hyperbolic Riemann-Stieltjes integral can be computed using classical Riemann-Stieltjes integration over the real line.

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}G=\left(\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}F_{1}dG_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}F_{2}dG_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2},

(5-E7)

The preceding observations, leads to the following result:

Theorem 5.3
.

A hyperbolic function
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is hyperbolic Riemann-Stieltjes integrable with respect to a hyperbolic function
G:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
, if and only if the components
F_{1}:[a_{1},b_{1}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
F_{2}:[a_{2},b_{2}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
are real Riemann-Stieltjes integrable functions respect to
G_{1}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and
G_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.

With the identity function
Id_{{\bf k}}:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
, the Riemann integral defined in
[
17
, Ch. IV]
of a function
F
is recovered when it is computed the hyperbolic Riemann-Stieltjes integral respect to
Id_{{\bf k}}
. Also, the last is equivalent to the Lebesgue integral introduced in
[
6
, Sec. 3]
with the hyperbolic-valued Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{K}
.

Results in
[
17
]
requires non-self-intersecting continuous loop (Jordan curve). The straight line that joins the two extreme points of a hyperbolic interval gives a loop of this kind. Therefore, for every strong partition
\mathfrak{P}
the union of lines that join every sub-interval
[\rho_{j+1},\rho_{j}]_{{\bf k}}
, where
j\in\{0,...,n-1\}
, is a Jordan loop and

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}Id_{{\bf k}}=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}\xi=\left(\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}F_{1}dx_{1}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}F_{2}dx_{2}\right){\bf e}_{2}.

For Lebesgue measure
\mu_{{\bf k}}:=\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\bf e}_{1}+\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\bf e}_{2}
, it is necessary to take into account that the Lebesgue integral is defined in a component-wise way and that the real Lebesgue integral restricted to a closed interval reduces to the Riemann integral,

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}\xi=\int_{[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}}Fd\mu_{{\bf k}}=\left(\int_{[a_{1},b_{1}]}F_{1}d\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}\right){\bf e}_{1}+\left(\int_{[a_{2},b_{2}]}F_{2}d\mu_{{\mathbb{R}}}\right){\bf e}_{2}

A primary study of a Riemann integration of hyperbolic-valued functions was presented in
[
21
]
. In this paper, are established some basic properties and results on the introduced notion of integration. Def. 5.2 naturally generalizes and strengthens that Riemann integral.

Let us mention an important property of the hyperbolic Riemann-Stieltjes integral, when the integrator is an holomorphic function.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be an holomorphic and continuously differentiable function,
F:[\alpha,\beta]\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
a hyperbolic Riemann-Stieltjes integrable function with respect to
G
. Then

\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}Fd_{{\bf k}}G=\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}FG^{\prime}d_{{\bf k}}\xi.

Proof.

Since
G
is holomorphic hyperbolic function, the derivative
G^{\prime}(\xi)=G_{1}^{\prime}(x){\bf e}_{1}+G_{2}^{\prime}(y){\bf e}_{2}
exists, for
\xi\in\mathbb{K}
. By the continuously differentiability of
G
its idempotent components have continuous derivatives of any order. Therefore
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are functions of bounded variation and
[
27
, Thm. 7.8]
makes easy to see that

\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}F_{1}dG_{1}=\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}F_{1}G_{1}^{\prime}dx_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}F_{2}dG_{2}=\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}F_{2}G_{2}^{\prime}dx_{2}.

Combining these equalities the result is obtained.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The assertion of Thm.
5.4
does not follow if requirement on
G
to be a continuously differentiable function is omitted from the hypotheses. Unlike happen in the complex analysis context, hyperbolic holomorphic functions does not have derivatives of all orders, see
[
17
,
33
,
32
]
.

Thm.
5.4
establishes a direct relation between Riemann-Stieltjes and Riemann integrals, when integrability of
F
respect to
G
is required. So, it is convenient to see under what conditions this integrability holds.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
F:[\alpha,\beta]_{{\bf k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a continuous hyperbolic function and
G:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a hyperbolic function of bounded variation. Then
F
is Riemann-Stieltjes integrable with respect to
G
.

Proof.

The components
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
of a continuous hyperbolic function
F
, are real continuous functions and the Cor.
4.5
shows that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are real functions of bounded variation. Therefore, the integrals

\int_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}F_{1}dG_{1}\quad and\quad\int_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}F_{2}dG_{2}

exist, which is clear from
[
27
, Thm. 7.27]
. Finally, by Thm.
5.3
, the result is obtained.
∎

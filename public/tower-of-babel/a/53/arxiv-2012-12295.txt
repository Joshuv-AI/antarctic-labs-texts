Modulation spaces associated to tensor products of amalgam spaces
Hans G. Feichtinger, Stevan Pilipović, Bojan Prangoski

Abstract
. We identify the modulation spaces associated to tensor products of amalgam spaces having a large class of Banach spaces as their local component. As consequences of the main results, we describe the modulation spaces associated to tensor products of various L p L^{p} spaces.

1.
Introduction

The modulation spaces were introduced by the first author
in a technical report prepared 1983 and published in 2002 (
[
20
]
).
Subsequently the theory was further developed in the context
of
coorbit spaces

by him and Gröchenig in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
. We
refer to the monograph
[
27
]
for an overview. Nowadays, they are studied by many authors and are widely accepted as an indispensable tool in time-frequency analysis.

A natural generalisation of the classical modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\eta}
was introduced in
[
11
]
in the following way. Let
X
be a Banach space of tempered distributions laying in between
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, invariant under translations and modulations and satisfying certain technical conditions (see Section
2.1
); so called translation-modulation invariant Banach spaces. The modulation spaces
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
(denoted as
\mathcal{M}^{X}
in
[
11
]
) associated to
X
consists of all tempered distributions in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose image under the short-time Fourier transform belongs to
X
. Taking
X
to be the weighted mixed-norm Lebesgue space
L^{p,q}_{\eta}
,
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
reduces to
M^{p,q}_{\eta}
. These generalised modulation spaces satisfy all of the important basic properties as the classical ones (see
[
11
]
). Unlike
L^{p,q}_{\eta}
, the space
X
does not need to be solid (in the sense of
[
17
]
) and this generalised framework allows one to consider a wide variety of modulation spaces
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
. An interesting choice for
X
beyond
L^{p,q}_{\eta}
is to take a completed
\pi
-tensor product (also called the projective tensor product
[
34
]
) of two
L^{p}
spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
X=L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(these are rarely solid; see
[
11
, Remark 3.10]
). In fact,
[
11
, Proposition 5.1 (ii)]
asserts that
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}\subseteq L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
X=L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}
with
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2
. The main goal of this article is to identify
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
when
X
is a tensor product. As a consequence of one of our main results we can explicitly describe
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
when
X=L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}
with
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2
(see Corollary
6.1
and Remark
6.2
):

{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{1})=\mathcal{F}M^{p_{2},1},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2.

The amalgam spaces
[
16
,
15
]
(often called Wiener amalgam spaces) will play a key role in our analysis. As it turns out, it is easier to identify
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
when
X
is a tensor product of two amalgam type spaces and then deduce from it various results concerning tensor products of
L^{p}
spaces. In the first main result of the article (Theorem
4.2
) we prove that
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
is an amalgam space of the form
W(E,L^{1}_{\eta})
when
X
is the completed
\pi
-tensor product of a large class of amalgam spaces; here
E\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space of tempered distributions (which satisfies the same general properties as
X
). This is interesting in itself as it partially answers the
converse question: when
W(E,L^{p}_{\eta})
can be regarded as a (generalised) modulation space? Theorem
4.2
claims this is always the case when
p=1
for any so called translation-modulation invariant Banach space
E
. When
E=\mathcal{F}L^{r}
, the amalgam space
W(\mathcal{F}L^{r},L^{p})
is exactly the Fourier image of the classical modulation space
M^{r,p}
(cf. Lemma
3.5
). For
p=1
these amalgam spaces can be considered as a modulation spaces as well.

We also consider
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
when
X
is the completed
\epsilon
-tensor product (also called the injective tensor product
[
34
]
) of a class of two amalgam spaces. Our second main result (Theorem
5.1
) shows that in this case
{\mathcal{M}[{X}]}
is an amalgam space of the form
W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
, where
L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}
denotes that the elements of the amalgam space vanish at infinity with weight
\eta
.

The article is organised as follows. In Section
2
we recall the definitions and basic properties of the translation-modulation invariant Banach spaces and the generalised modulation spaces. Section
3
is devoted to the amalgam spaces and their properties, especially when they are constructed from translation-modulation invariant Banach spaces. Sections
4
and
5
contain the main results: Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
5.1
. In the last section we collect a number of important consequences (and special cases) of the two main results.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

A positive measurable function
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is said to be a
polynomially bounded weight
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if there are
C,\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle\eta(x+y)\leq C\eta(x)(1+|y|)^{\tau},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Such functions
\eta
are also called
moderate
with respect to the Beurling weight
(1+|y|)^{\tau}
,
\tau\geq 0
. Given a polynomially bounded weight
\eta
, we denote by
L^{p}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
the weighted
L^{p}
space of measurable functions
f
with norm
\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\eta}}=\|f\eta\|_{L^{p}}
. When
p=\infty
, we also consider the closed subspace
L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of
L^{\infty}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

consisting of all
f\in L^{\infty}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
which satisfy the following:
for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that
\|f\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash K)}\leq\varepsilon
. Additionally,
we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space

L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;
it is a closed subspace of
L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and we equip it with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}
. When
\eta=1
, we employ the notations
L^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
instead of
L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of all continuous
functions which vanish at infinity with supremum norm). Although we will
never do so, there is no loss of generality in assuming
\eta
to be continuous when working with either one of these space because one can always find an equivalent continuous polynomially bounded weight
\tilde{\eta}
, i.e. satisfying
C^{\prime-1}\tilde{\eta}(x)\leq\eta(x)\leq C^{\prime}\tilde{\eta}(x)
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for some
C^{\prime}>0
, defining the same weighted spaces.

We use the following version of the Fourier transform:
\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}f(x)dx
,
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The translation and modulation operators
T_{x}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
M_{\xi}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, are defined as follows:
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
and
M_{\xi}f(t)=e^{2\pi it\xi}f(t)
. The short-time Fourier transform (from now, always abbreviated as STFT) of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with window
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
is defined as

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\langle f,\overline{M_{\xi}T_{x}g}\rangle=(f,M_{\xi}T_{x}g),\quad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
stands for the sesquilinear form induced by the inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. When
f
is a measurable function with polynomial growth, we have

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi it\xi}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}dt,\quad x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
,
V_{g}f
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
; furthermore
V_{g}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is continuous and it restricts to a continuous operator
V_{g}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. The adjoint
V_{g}^{*}
of
V_{g}
is a continuous operator from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
into
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

\displaystyle V_{g}^{*}\Phi(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{2\pi i\xi t}\Phi(x,\xi)g(t-x)dxd\xi,\quad\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

and it extends to a continuous operator
V_{g}^{*}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
,
V_{g_{1}}^{*}V_{g_{2}}=(g_{1},g_{2})\operatorname{Id}
on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

2.1.
Translation-modulation invariant Banach spaces of distributions and their duals. Modulation spaces

Here and throughout the rest of the article, for any two locally convex spaces
X
and
Y
,
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
stands for the space of continuous linear mappings from
X
into
Y
while
\mathcal{L}_{b}(X,Y)
for this space equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on all bounded subsets of
X
. When
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(X,Y)}
the operator norm on the Banach space
\mathcal{L}_{b}(X,Y)
induced by the norms
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
. If
X=Y
, we will often abbreviate these notations as
\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\mathcal{L}_{b}(X)
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(X)}
. Finally,
X\hookrightarrow Y
will always mean that
X
is continuously and densely included into
Y
.

A Banach space
E
is said to be a
translation-modulation invariant Banach spaces of distributions
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or, a TMIB space for short) if it satisfies the following conditions
[
11
]
(cf.
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
).

(I)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow E\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(II)

T_{x}(E)\subseteq E
and
M_{\xi}(E)\subseteq E
, for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(III)

There exist
\tau,C>0
such that
1
1

1

T_{x}
and
M_{\xi}
are continuous on
E
because of (I), (II) and the closed graph theorem

\omega_{E}(x)=\|T_{x}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)}\leq C(1+|x|)^{\tau}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nu_{E}(\xi)=\|M_{-\xi}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)}\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{\tau}.

We recall the basic facts of TMIB spaces and refer to
[
11
, Section 3]
(see also
[
12
]
) for the complete account. We start by pointing out that
E
is always separable and
\omega_{E}
and
\nu_{E}
are measurable submultiplicative locally bounded functions which satisfy
\omega_{E}(0)=\nu_{E}(0)=1
. The space
E
is a Banach module with respect to convolution over the Beurling (convolution) algebra
L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}
and a Banach module with respect to multiplication over the Wiener-Beurling algebra
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}

[
2
,
33
]

2
2

2

sometimes denoted by
A_{\nu_{E}}
; the multiplication operation on
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}
is defined via the Fourier transform and the convolution on
L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}
(when
\nu_{E}
is bounded from below,
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the multiplication reduces to ordinary pointwise multiplication). To be precise, the convolution and multiplication operations on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
uniquely extend to continuous bilinear mappings
*:L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}\times E\rightarrow E
and
\cdot:\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}\times E\rightarrow E
such that

\displaystyle\|f*e\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}}\|e\|_{E},\quad\forall f\in L^{1}_{\omega_{E}},\,\forall e\in E,

\displaystyle\|g\cdot e\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\|e\|_{E},\quad\forall g\in\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}},\,\forall e\in E,

and
f_{1}*(f_{2}*e)=(f_{1}*f_{2})*e
, for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}
,
e\in E
, and
g_{1}\cdot(g_{2}\cdot e)=(g_{1}\cdot g_{2})\cdot e
, for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}
,
e\in E
.

The Fourier image of
E
(i.e. the associated Fourier space)
\mathcal{F}E=\{\mathcal{F}f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})|\,f\in E\}
is again a TMIB space with norm
\|\mathcal{F}f\|_{\mathcal{F}E}=\|f\|_{E}
,
\mathcal{F}f\in\mathcal{F}E
, and

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathcal{F}E}(x)=\check{\nu}_{E}(x)=\nu_{E}(-x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\nu_{\mathcal{F}E}(\xi)=\omega_{E}(\xi).

Similarly,
\mathcal{F}^{-1}E=\{\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})|\,f\in E\}
with norm
\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}E}=\|f\|_{E}
is also a TMIB space. If
f\mapsto\check{f}
restricts to a well-defined isometry on
E
, then
\mathcal{F}E=\mathcal{F}^{-1}E
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}E}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}E}
. This holds, for example, when
E=L^{p}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
E=\mathcal{C}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the case
\eta(x)=(1+|x|)^{s}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
E
is a TMIB space such that
\omega_{E}
is bounded from above then
e\mapsto\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|T_{x}e\|_{E}
,
E\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, is a norm on
E
equivalent to its original norm and with respect to this new norm the translation operators
T_{x}
are isometries on
E
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A Banach space
E
is said to be a
dual translation-modulation invariant Banach space of distributions
(a DTMIB space for short) if it is the strong dual of a TMIB space. If
E
is a DTMIB space then
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq E\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the inclusions are continuous. Furthermore, the translation and modulation operators are continuous on
E
and, setting
E=F^{\prime}
with
F
a TMIB space, we always have
\omega_{E}=\check{\omega}_{F}
and
\nu_{E}=\nu_{F}
, where, as before,
\omega_{E}(x)=\|T_{x}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)}
and
\nu_{E}(\xi)=\|M_{-\xi}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(E)}
. However, in general,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
may fail to be dense in
E
(a typical example is
E=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
); but, when
E
is the strong dual of a reflexive TMIB space,
E
is also a TMIB space. Given a DTMIB space
E
, one can introduce convolution with elements of
L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}
and multiplication with elements of
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}
by duality and
E
becomes a Banach module with respect to convolution over
L^{1}_{\omega_{E}}
and a Banach module with respect to multiplication over
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}
. Because of this, both the TMIB and the DTMIB spaces can be considered as Banach spaces of distributions with two module structures in the sense of
[
4
,
17
]
(see also
[
26
]
).

Remark 2.2
.

Let
E
be a TMIB space and denote
\check{E}^{\prime}=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})|\,\check{f}\in E^{\prime}\}
with norm

\displaystyle\|f*e\|_{L^{\infty}_{1/\check{\omega}_{E}}}=\|(f*e)/\check{\omega}_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\check{f}\|_{E^{\prime}}\|e\|_{E},\quad f\in\check{E}^{\prime},\,e\in E;

If

2.2.
Tensor products of TMIB spaces. Modulation spaces associated to tensor products of TMIB spaces

Given any two TMIB spaces
E
and
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
yields that their completed
\epsilon
-tensor product
E\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}F
is a TMIB space on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. The same result also yields that the completed
\pi
-tensor product
E\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}F
is also a TMIB space whenever either
E
or
F
additionally satisfies the weak approximation property (we refer to
[
31
, Chapter 8 Section 43]
for the latter notion). We claim that every TMIB space satisfies the weak approximation property; in fact the following result shows that it always satisfies a stronger property, namely the weak sequential approximation property (see
[
30
, Section 1]
for the latter notion). In the following result, for
X
and
Y
two locally convex spaces, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{c}(X,Y)
the space
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on all compact convex circled subsets of
X
; when
X
is complete it is the same as the topology of uniform convergence on all precompact (or, equivalently, relatively compact) subsets of
X
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E
be a TMIB space. Then there exists
S_{n,m}\in E^{\prime}\otimes E
,
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}S_{n,m})=\operatorname{Id}\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\,\mathcal{L}_{c}(E,E).

In particular,
E
satisfies the weak sequential approximation property.

Proof.

Let
\chi,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\varphi(0)=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(x)dx=1
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, set
\varphi_{n}(x)=\varphi(x/n)
and
\chi_{n}(x)=n^{d}\chi(nx)
. Define
G_{n}:E\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
G_{n}(f)=\chi_{n}*(\varphi_{n}f)
; of course they are well defined and continuous operators. By
[
11
, Corollary 3.3]
,
G_{n}(f)\rightarrow f
in
E
, for all
f\in E
, and thus the Banach-Steinhaus theorem implies
G_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Id}
in
\mathcal{L}_{c}(E,E)
.

Take a Schauder basis
\varphi_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, for
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; for example the Hermite functions. Then

\displaystyle\varphi=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle f_{j},\varphi\rangle\varphi_{j}\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
f_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, are the coefficient functionals. Set

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\otimes\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Then the Banach-Steinhaus theorem
[
35
, Theorem 4.5, p. 85]
implies
\tilde{S}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Id}
in
\mathcal{L}_{c}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\mathcal{L}_{b}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(as
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Montel space). For
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle S_{n,m}=\tilde{S}_{n}\circ G_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{m}(\check{\chi}_{m}*f_{j})\otimes\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq E^{\prime}\otimes E.

Now, for each fixed
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
S_{n,m}\rightarrow G_{m}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, in
\mathcal{L}_{c}(E,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and consequently in
\mathcal{L}_{c}(E,E)
as well. As we already proved that
G_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Id}

Lemma 3.2

Proof.

With

The only non trivial part of

is the density of

,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash J_{n_{1}}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}f\|_{E}^{p}\eta(\mathbf{k})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\varepsilon/(4C(1+\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}})).

E

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E
be a TMIB or a DTMIB space on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

and

if and only if

,

. Furthermore,

(3.7)

\displaystyle f\mapsto\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|fT_{x}\chi\|_{E}^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{W(E,L^{p}_{\eta})}

For any

,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty)

Proof.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty

the subspace of

consisting of those elements for which (

(the fact that it is a norm will follow from the second part of the proof). Without losing generality, we can assume
\chi(0)\neq 0

and the new seminorm is equivalent to the old one (because
\eta
is a polynomially bounded weight). There is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{k_{0}}}\phi_{\mathbf{r}}=1
on
\operatorname{supp}\chi

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\chi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{k_{0}}}\|fT_{x+\mathbf{r}}\phi\|_{E}\right)^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{k_{0}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|fT_{x+\mathbf{r}}\phi\|_{E}^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|fT_{x}\phi\|_{E}^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|fT_{x}\phi\|_{E}^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}.

Let
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

be arbitrary but fixed. Then

\displaystyle\|fT_{x}\phi\|_{E}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}T_{x}\phi\|_{E}\leq\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}f\|_{E}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{p}}\leq C_{3}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}f\|_{E}^{p}\eta(x)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}.

in each of the integrals. As

is a polynomially bounded weight, we deduce

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{p}}\leq C_{4}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}f\|_{E}^{p}\eta(t)^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{5}\|f\|_{W(E,L^{p}_{\eta})}.

We turn our attention to the opposite inclusion. As

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\phi}\subseteq(-1/n_{0},1/n_{0})^{d}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}T_{\mathbf{k}/n_{0}}\tilde{\phi}(x)=1,\,\,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
f\in X_{p}
. For
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2n_{0}+1}}\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}T_{t+\mathbf{r}/n_{0}}\tilde{\phi}\|_{E}\leq\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2n_{0}+1}}\|fT_{t+\mathbf{r}/n_{0}}\tilde{\phi}\|_{E}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W(E,L^{p}_{\eta})}\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2n_{0}+1}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|fT_{t+\mathbf{r}/n_{0}}\tilde{\phi}\|_{E}^{p}\eta(t)^{p}dt\right)^{1/p}.

\|\cdot\|_{X_{p}}
being equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{W(E,L^{p}_{\eta})}
.

The proof for
W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
is analogous and we omit it; since
x\mapsto\|fT_{x}\chi\|_{E}
is continuous, this function belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if it belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\eta,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

We end this section with the following result which connects the classical modulation spaces with the amalgam spaces having
\mathcal{F}L^{p}_{\eta}
as a global component. This result has appeared in other works in the past (cf.
[
5
,
8
,
19
]
); we provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be two polynomially bounded weights and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
. Then
M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

Set
g=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
. In the proof, we employ the following identity (see
[
27
, Lemma 3.1.1, p. 39]
): for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it holds that

(3.8)

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{F}f\cdot T_{\xi}\phi)(-x),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{-1}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})
. Employing (
3.8
) and the fact
\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p_{1}}_{\check{\eta}_{1}}=\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}
with
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p_{1}}_{\check{\eta}_{1}}}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}
, we infer

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|(\mathcal{F}f)(T_{\xi}\phi)\|^{p_{2}}_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(\xi)^{p_{2}}d\xi\right)^{1/p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|(\mathcal{F}f)(T_{\xi}\phi)\|^{p_{2}}_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(\xi)^{p_{2}}d\xi\right)^{1/p_{2}}.

Let
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\xi\in\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}
be arbitrary but fixed. Then

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{F}f)(T_{\xi}\phi)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|(\mathcal{F}f)(T_{\xi}\phi)\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\leq C_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}f\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}\leq C_{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\mathcal{F}f\|^{p_{2}}_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(\xi)^{p_{2}}d\xi\right)^{1/p_{2}}.

We introduce the change of variables
t=\xi+\mathbf{r}
in each of the integrals. As
\eta_{2}
is a polynomially bounded weight, we deduce

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}\leq C_{3}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\mathcal{F}f\|^{p_{2}}_{\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(t)^{p_{2}}dt\right)^{1/p_{2}}\leq C_{3}\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}.

Assume now
f\in M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}
. For
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}
, we infer

\displaystyle\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\phi_{\mathbf{k}})*(\mathcal{F}^{-1}T_{t+\mathbf{r}}\phi)\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}T_{t+\mathbf{r}}\phi\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}.

Denoting by
\theta
the characteristic function of
[-1,1]^{d}
, we deduce

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\theta(t-\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(t)\right)^{p_{2}}dt\right)^{1/p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{2}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{2}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}T_{t+\mathbf{r}}\phi\|^{p_{2}}_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(t)^{p_{2}}dt\right)^{1/p_{2}}.

Introducing the change of variables
\xi=t+\mathbf{r}
together with the fact that
\eta_{2}
is polynomially bounded weight, we conclude

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{3}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{k}+[-1,1]^{d}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}T_{\xi}\phi\|^{p_{2}}_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(\xi)^{p_{2}}d\xi\right)^{1/p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}T_{\xi}\phi\|^{p_{2}}_{L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}}\eta_{2}(\xi)^{p_{2}}d\xi\right)^{1/p_{2}}.

Because of (
3.8
), the very last term is just
\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}
. Furthermore, the above also implies
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\mathcal{F}f=\mathcal{F}(f*\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi_{\mathbf{k}})\in\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}
,
\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which, in turn, yields that
\mathcal{F}f
is locally in
\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}}
(i.e. in
(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})_{\operatorname{loc}}
). As
I
is just
\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}
, the proof is complete.
∎

The case when
p_{1}=p_{2}
in the above lemma has been discussed in great detail in
[
19
]
. There it has been shown that, under certain conditions on the weights
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
, the Fourier image of
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p}_{\eta_{2}})
is exactly
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p}_{\eta_{2}},L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})
; this gives the Fourier invariance of the spaces
M^{p,p}
(cf.
[
27
, Theorem 11.3.5, p. 236]
).

4.
Modulation spaces associated to a class of completed
\pi
-tensor products of TMIB spaces

In this section we identify the modulation spaces associated to a large class of completed
\pi
-tensor products of TMIB spaces. In order to better illustrate the key ideas in the main result, we first consider the case
{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}
,
p\in[1,2]
; of course the claim in the next lemma is a consequence of the main result: Theorem
4.2
(see Corollary
6.1
below).

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
p\in[1,2]
,
{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

First we prove the inclusion
{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
. Every element of
L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}
can be represented as an absolutely convergent series

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\otimes\psi_{j}

where
\varphi_{j},\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
are bounded subsets of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, is such that
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
(see
[
35
, Theorem 6.4, p. 94]
and
[
31
, Theorem 6, p. 188]
). Pick
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
satisfying
\|g\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}
and thus
V_{g}f\in L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}
. Consequently
V_{g}f
has the form (
4.1
). The key ingredient is that

(4.2)

V_{g}^{*}(\varphi_{j}\otimes\psi_{j})=(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g),\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

(cf.
[
11
, Equation (5.1)]
), which, in turn, implies

(4.3)

\displaystyle f=V_{g}^{*}V_{g}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}V_{g}^{*}(\varphi_{j}\otimes\psi_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g).

Now,
[
16
, Theorem 3]
implies

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}*g\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{p})}\leq C_{1}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{W(L^{1},L^{p})}\|g\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{1})}\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{1})},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Denoting by
q
the Hölder conjugate to
p
and employing Remark
3.1
, we infer

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g)\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}(\varphi_{j}*g)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j}\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{q})}\|\varphi_{j}*g\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{p})}.

As
p\in[1,2]
, we have the continuous inclusion
L^{p}=W(L^{p},L^{p})\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{q},L^{p})
(see
[
15
, Lemma 1.2
iv)
]
) and consequently
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
gives the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{F}L^{p}\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{q})
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p}}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g)\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})}\leq C_{3}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{p}}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{1})},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Consequently, the series in (
4.3
) is absolutely summable in
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
and thus
f\in W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
.

We turn our attention to the opposite inclusion. Remark
3.1
implies that
f\in W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
is characterised by the finiteness of the expression

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}<\infty.

The key idea is that we can split
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
into two parts so as
f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
is of the same form as the summands in (
4.3
). To make this precise, pick
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0\leq g\leq 1
and
g=1
on
[-2,2]^{d}
. Then
g*g(x)\geq 1
,
\forall x\in[-1,1]^{d}
. Take nonnegative
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\psi=1/(g*g)
on
[-1,1]^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\psi)((T_{\mathbf{k}}g)*g),\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}T_{\mathbf{k}}\psi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}\leq\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}\|T_{\mathbf{k}}\psi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}}=\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

As
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p}}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{p}}
, we deduce
\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}g)\otimes\mathcal{F}(f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}T_{\mathbf{k}}\psi)
is absolutely summable in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to some
\tilde{f}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (cf. Remark
3.1
)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|f\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})}.

Since

\displaystyle V_{g}^{*}\tilde{f}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\psi)((T_{\mathbf{k}}g)*g)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=f,

[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
verifies that
f\in{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}
and the inclusion
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}
is continuous. Now the open mapping theorem yields that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}}]}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})}
are equivalent.
∎

Our goal is to prove a general version of Lemma
4.1
where each of the spaces
L^{p}
is replaced by a TMIB space. The inequality (
4.4
) hints that the local and global behaviour of
V_{g}f
in each of the variables
x
and
\xi
“come separately”. This suggests to look at tensor products of amalgam spaces as they naturally keep track of the local a global behaviour separately. Another observation from the above proof, albeit not obvious at a glance, is that the local component of
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{1})
came from the second
L^{p}
in
L^{p}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p}
. The significance of the first
L^{p}
was in its global growth; aside from the fact
\|T_{x}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{b}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=1
, the local behaviour did not really mattered. This will become more transparent in the general result (Theorem
4.2
) and its proof.

4.1.
The general case

The goal of this section is to identify the modulation spaces associated to completed
\pi
-tensor products of a large class of amalgam spaces constructed out of TMIB spaces. The main result is the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E
and
F
be two TMIB spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be polynomially bounded weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
is equal to all of the following spaces with equivalent norms:

(i)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
, with
p_{1},p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
satisfying
p^{-1}_{1}+p^{-1}_{2}\geq 1
;

(ii)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
;

(iii)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}]}
.

Remark 4.3
.

The condition
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, can be replaced with:
\omega_{F}
bounded from above. When the latter holds true, Remark
2.1
implies that one can always find an equivalent norm on
F
such that, with respect to this new norm,
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we need the following preliminary result; its proof is a natural generalisation of the second part of the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E
,
F
,
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be as in Theorem
4.2
. Then
W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
is continuously included into the modulation space
{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
.

Proof.

Take
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0\leq g\leq 1
and
g=1
on
[-2,2]^{d}
. Pick nonnegative
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}((-1,1)^{d})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)dx=1
, and define
\chi=\varphi*g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
\chi
is nonnegative and
\chi=1
on
[-1,1]^{d}
(and thus on
\operatorname{supp}\phi
as well). Denote
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}=T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Let
f\in W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
and denote
\psi_{\mathbf{k}}=\mathcal{F}(f\phi_{\mathbf{k}})\in\mathcal{F}E
,
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Notice that

\displaystyle\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{E}\leq\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\|\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}=\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{E}\theta_{[-1,1]^{d}}(t-\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{r})\eta_{2}(t)dt\leq C^{\prime}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}.

We deduce
\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})
with

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}\leq C^{\prime}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|T_{\mathbf{k}}(\varphi\phi_{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{k}})\|_{F}\theta_{[-1,1]^{d}}(t-\mathbf{m})\eta_{1}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{F}\theta_{[-1,1]^{d}}(t-\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{k})\eta_{1}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{F}\theta_{[-1,1]^{d}}(t-\mathbf{m})(1+|t|)^{\tau}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{\prime\prime}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\|\varphi\|_{W(F,L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{\tau}})}.

The above estimates imply that the series
\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\otimes\psi_{\mathbf{k}}
is absolutely summable in
W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})
to some
\tilde{f}\in W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})
. Indeed,

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\otimes\psi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}\\
\leq C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\theta_{[-1,1]^{d}}(t-\mathbf{k})\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})dt\leq C_{2}\|f\|_{W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})}.

Now, as
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}T_{\mathbf{k}}\chi=\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
,
\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we infer

\displaystyle V^{*}_{g}(\tilde{f})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}V^{*}_{g}(\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\otimes\psi_{\mathbf{k}})=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{\mathbf{k}})(\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}*g)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}T_{\mathbf{k}}\chi=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=f,

which, in view of
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
, concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Because of Lemma
4.4
and (
3.3
), the following continuous and dense inclusions hold true for all
1\leq p_{1}\leq p^{\prime}_{1}<\infty
and
1\leq p_{2}\leq p^{\prime}_{2}<\infty
:

\displaystyle W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p^{\prime}_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p^{\prime}_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]},

\displaystyle W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}]}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})}]},

\displaystyle W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}]}.

Thus, it suffices to prove that
X\subseteq W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
where
X
is either the space in
(ii)
or the space in
(iii)
or
{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q
its Hölder conjugate, since then the equivalence of the norms will follow from the open mapping theorem. We prove
{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})}]}\subseteq W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
, with
p,q\in(1,\infty)
being Hölder conjugate as the proof when
X
is either one of the spaces in
(ii)
or
(iii)
is analogous.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
be such that
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
. Let
f\in{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
. Thus (see
[
35
, Theorem 6.4, p. 94]
and
[
31
, Theorem 6, p. 188]
)

\displaystyle V_{g}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}\otimes\psi_{j}

where
\varphi_{j},\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, are such that
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
and
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
are bounded subsets of
W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})
and
\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})
respectively; furthermore
\lambda_{j}
are complex numbers such that
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|<\infty
. We infer

(4.5)

\displaystyle f=V^{*}_{g}V_{g}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g),

where the series is absolutely summable in
{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
. Pick
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi=1
on
\operatorname{supp}\phi
and
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subseteq[-2,2]^{d}
. Denote
\chi_{\mathbf{k}}=T_{\mathbf{k}}\chi
. As
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}=\phi_{\mathbf{k}}
, we infer

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g)\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\leq\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}\|(\varphi_{j}*g)\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
be such that
(1+4\pi^{2}|\cdot|^{2})^{-m}\nu_{E}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{j}*g)\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1-\Delta)^{m}((\varphi_{j}*g)\chi_{\mathbf{k}})\right)(1+4\pi^{2}|\cdot|^{2})^{-m}\nu_{E}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\sum_{\beta\leq 2\alpha}\frac{m!}{(m-|\alpha|)!\alpha!}{2\alpha\choose\beta}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\varphi_{j}*\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\partial^{\beta}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\sum_{\beta\leq 2\alpha}\frac{m!}{(m-|\alpha|)!\alpha!}{2\alpha\choose\beta}\|(\varphi_{j}*\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\partial^{\beta}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{j}*\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\partial^{\beta}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left\|\left((\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{n}})*(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\right)\partial^{\beta}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Notice that
\operatorname{supp}((\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{n}})*(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g))\subseteq\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}+[-2,2]^{d}
. Thus, if
\operatorname{supp}((\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{n}})*(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g))\cap\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\neq\emptyset
then
\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}\in\mathbf{k}+[-4,4]^{d}
. Since
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Remark
2.2
yields

\displaystyle\|(\varphi_{j}*\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\partial^{\beta}\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}\chi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{4}}\left\|(\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}})*(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\beta}\chi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{4}}\|\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{F}\|\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g\|_{\check{F}^{\prime}}.

Plugging this in (
4.7
) and employing Minkowski inequality, we infer

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|(\varphi_{j}*g)\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}^{p}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle{}\quad\leq C_{1}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\sum_{\beta\leq 2\alpha}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{4}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{F}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r})\|\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g\|_{\check{F}^{\prime}}(1+|\mathbf{m}|)^{\tau}\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle{}\quad\leq 9^{d}C_{1}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{F}^{p}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{n})^{p}\right)^{1/p}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\sum_{\beta\leq 2\alpha}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{2\alpha-\beta}g\|_{\check{F}^{\prime}}(1+|\mathbf{m}|)^{\tau}.

By Remark
3.1
,
(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\varphi_{j}\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{F}^{p}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{n})^{p})^{1/p}\leq C\|\varphi_{j}\|_{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})}
. Since
\check{F}^{\prime}
is the strong dual of the TMIB space
\check{F}
, (
3.3
) implies that
\partial^{\gamma}g\in W(\check{F}^{\prime},L^{1}_{(1+|\cdot|)^{\tau}})
,
\forall\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, which, in view of Remark
3.1
, yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\partial^{\gamma}g\|_{\check{F}^{\prime}}(1+|\mathbf{m}|)^{\tau}<\infty,\quad\forall\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Thus, (
4.6
) gives

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g)\|_{W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{1}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}^{q}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|(\varphi_{j}*g)\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}^{p}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})\phi_{\mathbf{k}}\|_{E}^{q}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})^{q}\right)^{1/q}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})}.

In view of Remark
3.1
, this implies the series in (
4.5
) is absolutely summable in
W(E,L^{1}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}})
and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

and

be two TMIB spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be polynomially bounded weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that

,

. Then
W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},0})
is equal to all of the following spaces with equivalent norms:

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
, with

(ii)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})}]}
, with

;

(iii)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}]}
, with
p_{1}\in[1,\infty)
;

(iv)

{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}]}
.

Remark 5.2
.

For the same reasons as in Remark
4.3
, the condition
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,

, can be replaced with:
\omega_{F}
bounded from above.

For the proof of Theorem
5.1
, we need the following two results.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E
,
F
,
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Then for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
e\in W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},0})
, the family

(5.1)

\displaystyle\{(T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi)\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e)|\,\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

is summable in all of the following spaces

(i)

W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})
, with
p_{1}^{-1}+p_{2}^{-1}\leq 1
,

;

(ii)

W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})
, with
p_{2}\in[1,\infty)
;

(iii)

W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})
, with

(iv)

W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})

Proof.

For
1\leq p_{1}\leq p^{\prime}_{1}<\infty
and
1\leq p_{2}\leq p^{\prime}_{2}<\infty
, (
3.3
) gives the following continuous inclusion

\displaystyle W(F,L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})\subseteq W(F,L^{p^{\prime}_{1}}_{\eta_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{p^{\prime}_{2}}_{\eta_{2}}).

Thus, to prove
(i)
it suffices to verify it when
p_{1}=p\in(1,\infty)
and
p_{2}=q\in(1,\infty)
are Hölder conjugate.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
e\in W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},0})
. There is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subseteq(-k_{0},k_{0})^{d}
. As
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are polynomially bounded weights, there exists
C^{\prime}\geq 1
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{s})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r})\leq C^{\prime}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k}),\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,\forall\mathbf{r}\in J_{1},\,\forall\mathbf{s}\in J_{k_{0}}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
be equicontinuous subsets of
W(F,L^{p}_{\eta_{1}})^{\prime}_{b}
and
W(E,L^{q}_{\eta_{2}})^{\prime}_{b}
respectively. Because of Lemma
3.2
, without losing generality, we can assume

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{f^{\prime}\in W(F^{\prime},L^{q}_{1/\eta_{1}})|\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{W(F^{\prime},L^{q}_{1/\eta_{1}})}\leq C_{1}\},\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,C_{1}\geq 1,

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e^{\prime}\in W(E^{\prime},L^{p}_{1/\eta_{2}})|\,\|e^{\prime}\|_{W(E^{\prime},L^{p}_{1/\eta_{2}})}\leq C_{2}\},\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,C_{2}\geq 1.

As

, in view of Remark
3.1
, there exists

such that

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e\|_{E}\leq\varepsilon\cdot(2\cdot 3^{d}(2k_{0}+1)^{d}C^{\prime}\tilde{C}^{2}C_{1}C_{2}\|\varphi\|_{F})^{-1}=\varepsilon^{\prime},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash J_{n_{0}},

where
\tilde{C}\geq 1
is the maximum of the two constants

in Remark
3.1
for the spaces
W(F^{\prime},L^{q}_{1/\eta_{1}})
and
W(E^{\prime},L^{p}_{1/\eta_{2}})
. Then for finite
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
J_{n_{0}}\subseteq\Phi_{1}\cap\Phi_{2}
, and
f^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
and

, we infer

\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime},\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi)\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e)-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{2}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi)\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e)\right\rangle\right|\\
\leq\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}\backslash J_{n_{0}}}|\langle f^{\prime},T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi\rangle||\langle e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e\rangle|+\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{2}\backslash J_{n_{0}}}|\langle f^{\prime},T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi\rangle||\langle e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e\rangle|=I_{1}+I_{2}.

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in J_{k_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}\backslash J_{n_{0}}}|\langle\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{s}}f^{\prime},T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi\rangle||\langle\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in J_{k_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}\backslash J_{n_{0}}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{s}}f^{\prime}\|_{F^{\prime}}\|T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi\|_{F}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e\|_{E}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}\|\varphi\|_{F}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{s}\in J_{k_{0}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}\backslash J_{n_{0}}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{s}}f^{\prime}\|_{F^{\prime}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})^{-1}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})^{-1}.

Analogously,

and consequently,

\displaystyle\sup_{f^{\prime}\in A^{\prime},\,\,e^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle f^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime},\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{1}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi)\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e)-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\Phi_{2}}(T_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi)\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e)\right\rangle\right|\leq\varepsilon,

which completes the proof of
(i)
. To prove
(ii)
it suffices to verify the claim for

as this spaces is continuously included into the rest of the spaces in
(ii)

(i)
. For
(iii)

(ii)
, and finally,

follows from either one of
(i)
,
(ii)
or
(iii)
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

,

,

and

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Since all of the spaces in
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
are continuously included into the space in
(iv)
, in view of Lemma

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}]}\subseteq W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},0})

as the continuity of the inclusion will follow from the open mapping theorem. By
[
11
, Proposition 4.4]
it suffices to show that

(5.3)

\displaystyle V^{*}_{g}(W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0}))\subseteq W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},0}),\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}.

g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\backslash\{0\}
and pick
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\operatorname{supp}g\subseteq(-n_{0},n_{0})^{d}
. Let

; hence

\displaystyle u=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\varphi_{j}\otimes\psi_{j},\quad\mbox{with}\,\,\varphi_{j},\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,j=1,\ldots,s.

Consequently

\displaystyle V^{*}_{g}u=\sum_{j=1}^{s}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})(\varphi_{j}*g).

Set
\tilde{g}(x,y)=g(y-x)
; clearly
\tilde{g}\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Let
e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
be arbitrary but fixed. Denoting by
1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
the functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is identically equal to
1
, we infer

(5.4)

\displaystyle\langle e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}V^{*}_{g}u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle 1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\prime}),\tilde{g}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\varphi_{j}\otimes(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi_{j})\right\rangle=\langle 1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\prime}),\tilde{g}(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\tilde{g}(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\prime})),(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\rangle.

It is straightforward to check that if
\mathbf{m}\not\in\mathbf{k}+J_{n_{0}+2}
then
\tilde{g}(\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\prime}))=0
. Pick
\chi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi=1
on
\operatorname{supp}\phi
. Denoting
\chi_{\mathbf{n}}=T_{\mathbf{n}}\chi
,
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we infer

(5.5)

\displaystyle|\langle e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}V^{*}_{g}u\rangle|\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{n_{0}+2}}|\langle\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}e^{\prime}),\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\rangle|.

Denote by
X
the TMIB space
W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})
; its norm is given by

\displaystyle\|v\|_{X}=\sup_{\|f^{\prime}\|_{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})^{\prime}_{b}}\leq 1}\sup_{\|e^{\prime}\|_{W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})^{\prime}_{b}}\leq 1}|\langle f^{\prime}\otimes e^{\prime},v\rangle|,\quad v\in X.

For any
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\mathbf{m}}\|_{F^{\prime}}}{\eta_{1}(\mathbf{m})}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\frac{\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{F^{\prime}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{F}}}}{\eta_{1}(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{r})}\leq C_{1}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{F}}}\|\phi\|_{F^{\prime}}/\eta_{1}(\mathbf{n}).

Consequently, Lemma
3.2

(iii)
together with Remark
3.1
imply that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})^{\prime}_{b}}\leq C^{\prime}_{1}/\eta_{1}(\mathbf{n}),\quad\forall\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Similarly, for any
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\mathbf{m}}e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}}{\eta_{2}(\mathbf{m})}\leq\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{1}}\frac{\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{r}}\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}\|e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}}{\eta_{2}(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{r})}\leq C_{2}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}^{2}\|e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}/\eta_{2}(\mathbf{n}),

and, again, Lemma
3.2

(iii)
and Remark
3.1
imply
\phi_{\mathbf{n}}e^{\prime}\in W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})^{\prime}_{b}
with

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mathbf{n}}e^{\prime}\|_{W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})^{\prime}_{b}}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}\|e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}/\eta_{2}(\mathbf{n}),\quad\forall\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Plugging these bounds in (
5.5
), we infer

\displaystyle|\langle e^{\prime},\phi_{\mathbf{k}}V^{*}_{g}u\rangle|\leq\frac{C_{3}\|e^{\prime}\|_{E^{\prime}}}{\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{n_{0}+2}}\|\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\|_{X},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

As
e^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
was arbitrary, we deduce

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}V^{*}_{g}u\|_{E}\leq C_{3}\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in J_{n_{0}+2}}\|\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\|_{X},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Notice that
\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Our immediate goal is to estimate the norm of the latter in
\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{X}}
. In view of
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{X}=\nu_{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})}\otimes\nu_{W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})}\leq\nu_{F}\otimes\nu_{E};

the very last inequality is easy to verify. As

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1))(t,\xi)=e^{2\pi i(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r})(t+\xi)}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi(t+\xi)\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(\xi),

we deduce
\|\tilde{g}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{r}}\otimes 1)\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{X}}}\leq\|\chi\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{F}L^{1}_{\nu_{E}}}
, for all
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbf{r}\in J_{n_{0}+2}
. Hence

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\mathbf{k})\eta_{2}(\mathbf{k})\|\phi_{\mathbf{k}}V^{*}_{g}u\|_{E}\leq C_{4}\|(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1})(u)\|_{X},\quad\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Since
\operatorname{Id}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}^{-1}:W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})\rightarrow X
is continuous, Remark
3.1
yields

\displaystyle\|V^{*}_{g}u\|_{W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2},0})}\leq C_{5}\|u\|_{W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The validity of (
5.3
) follows from the density of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
W(F,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(E,L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})
.
∎

5.1
. The same observation we made at the very end of Section
4.1
holds equally well for Theorem
5.1
: the space
F
has no effect on the resulting modulation space; only the global behaviour of the first amalgam space matters.

4.2
and Theorem

We devote this section to deriving a number of consequences of the two main results. We start by considering the modulation spaces associated to tensor products of

spaces.

Corollary 6.1
.

The following assertions hold true.

(a)

{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{1})
, for all
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2
.

(b)

{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}L^{p_{2}}}]}={\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{C}_{0}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}L^{p_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{\infty}_{0})
, for all
2\leq p_{2}\leq p_{1}<\infty
.

(c)

Let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be two polynomially bounded weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}]}={\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1},0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}]}=W(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0},L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0}).

Proof.

(a)
If
p_{2}=1
then
p_{1}=1
and the claim follows from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
(see
[
34
, Section 2.3]
), since
{\mathcal{M}[{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}]}=M^{1,1}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{1},L^{1})
.

By
[
15
, Lemma 1.2
iv)
]
we have
L^{p}=W(L^{p},L^{p})\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{q},L^{p})
, for all
p\in[1,2]
, where
q
is the Hölder conjugate to
p
. Consequently,
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
gives
\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p}=\mathcal{F}L^{p}\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{q})
which yields the continuous inclusion:

(6.1)

\displaystyle L^{p}\subseteq\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p},L^{q}),\quad\forall p\in(1,2].

Let
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2
with
p_{2}>1
and denote by
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
the Hölder conjugate indexes to
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
respectively. As
W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{1})\subseteq\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p_{2}}
(by (
3.5
)), employing (
6.1
), we deduce

\displaystyle W(L^{p_{1}},L^{p_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{1})\subseteq L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}\subseteq W(L^{p_{1}},L^{p_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{q_{2}}).

Now the claim follows from Theorem
4.2
, since
p_{1}^{-1}+q_{2}^{-1}\geq 1
(equivalent to
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
).

(b)
Let
2\leq p_{2}\leq p_{1}<\infty
and denote by
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
the Hölder conjugate indexes to
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
respectively. Employing
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
15
, Lemma 1.2
iv)
]
, we infer the continuous inclusions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{q_{2}})\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{q_{2}},L^{p_{2}})\subseteq W(L^{p_{2}},L^{p_{2}})=L^{p_{2}}.

Since
\mathcal{F}^{-1}L^{p_{2}}=\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}}\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{\infty}_{0})
(by (
3.5
)), we deduce the continuous inclusions:

\displaystyle W(L^{p_{1}},L^{p_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{q_{2}})\subseteq L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}L^{p_{2}}\subseteq W(L^{p_{1}},L^{p_{1}})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{\infty}_{0}).

Now Theorem
5.1
gives
{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}L^{p_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{\infty}_{0})
, since
p_{1}^{-1}+q_{2}^{-1}\leq 1
(equivalent to
p_{2}\leq p_{1}
). The proof of
{\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{C}_{0}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}L^{p_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{2}},L^{\infty}_{0})
is analogous since
\mathcal{C}_{0}=W(\mathcal{C}_{0},L^{\infty}_{0})
.

(c)
The first equality follows from
\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1},0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. As (cf. (
3.5
))

\displaystyle W(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0},L^{1})\subseteq\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0}\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0},L^{\infty}_{0}),

\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{2},0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1},0}=W(\mathcal{C}_{0},L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})
, we have the continuous inclusions:

\displaystyle W(\mathcal{C}_{0},L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0},L^{1})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{1},0}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{C}_{\eta_{2},0}\subseteq W(\mathcal{C}_{0},L^{\infty}_{\eta_{1},0})\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{F}W(\mathcal{F}\mathcal{C}_{\check{\eta}_{2},0},L^{\infty}_{0})

and the claim follows from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In view of Lemma
3.5
, Corollary
6.1

(a)
implies

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{L^{p_{1}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}L^{p_{2}}}]}=\mathcal{F}M^{p_{2},1},\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq 2.

Next, we derive two rather general corollaries of Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
5.1
which can be employed for deducing a variety of special instances.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
E
and
F
be two TMIB spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\omega_{F}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\eta
be a polynomially bounded weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
E
and
F
satisfy the following conditions for some
p\in[1,\infty)
:

(a)

there exists a TMIB space
E_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
W(E_{1},L^{1}_{\eta})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{-1}E\subseteq W(E_{1},L^{q}_{\eta})
with
q
the Hölder conjugate index to
p
;

(b)

F\subseteq W(F_{1},L^{p})
for some TMIB space
F_{1}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\omega_{F_{1}}(x)=1
,
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Then
{\mathcal{M}[{F\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E}]}=W(E_{1},L^{1}_{\eta})
with equivalent norms.

In particular, if
E
satisfies
(a)
with
E_{1}=\mathcal{F}L^{p}_{\eta_{1}}
for some polynomially bounded weight
\eta_{1}
, then
{\mathcal{M}[{F\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p}_{\eta_{1}},L^{1}_{\eta})
with equivalent norms.

Proof.

Since all of the spaces are continuously included into
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the closed graph theorem implies that all of the inclusions in
(a)
and
(b)
are continuous. Assume first
p\in(1,\infty)
and hence
q\in(1,\infty)
. As
W(F,L^{1})\subseteq F
(by (
3.5
)), we infer the continuous inclusions:

\displaystyle W(F,L^{1})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E_{1},L^{1}_{\eta})\subseteq F\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E\subseteq W(F_{1},L^{p})\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}W(E_{1},L^{q}_{\eta}),

and the result follows from Theorem
4.2
.

When
p=1
, we can not directly apply Theorem
4.2
; notice that
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta})
is not necessarily even a TMIB space (
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
may fail to be dense in it). However, as
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
\mathcal{F}^{-1}E
, we deduce that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}E
is continuously included into the closure of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta})
. The latter space is
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
. Indeed,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
and
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
is a closed subspace of
W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta})
. Thus, the second inclusion in
(a)
can be strengthen as
\mathcal{F}^{-1}E\subseteq W(E_{1},L^{\infty}_{\eta,0})
. Now, the rest of the proof can be done in an analogous fashion as for the case
p\in(1,\infty)
.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
be two polynomially bounded weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfy
\eta_{1}=\check{\eta}_{1}
and
\eta_{2}=\check{\eta}_{2}
.

(i)

For any

satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{1}_{\eta_{2}})\quad\mbox{with equivalent norms.}

(ii)

For any

p_{1}^{-1}+p_{2}^{-1}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{M^{p_{1},p_{2}}\hat{\otimes}_{\epsilon}M^{p_{1},p_{2}}_{\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}}}]}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{\infty}_{\eta_{2},0})\quad\mbox{with equivalent norms.}

For any

and

,

, Lemma 3.1.1, p. 39]

(6.3)

\displaystyle V_{g}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}f)(x,\xi)=V_{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}g}(\mathcal{F}f)(-x,-\xi),\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(i)
, we apply Corollary

\displaystyle W(E_{1},L^{1}_{\eta})\subseteq W(\mathcal{F}L^{p_{1}}_{\eta_{1}},L^{p_{2}}_{\eta_{2}})=\mathcal{F}^{-1}E\subseteq W(E_{1},L^{q}_{\eta}).

.

The proof of
(ii)

\mathcal{Q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Corollary 6.7

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{Q}_{s}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{Q}_{s}}]}\subseteq W(L^{2}_{v_{s}},L^{1}_{v_{s}})\cap W(\mathcal{F}L^{2}_{v_{s}},L^{1}_{v_{s}}),

\displaystyle W(L^{2}_{v_{s}},L^{1}_{v_{s}})+W(\mathcal{F}L^{2}_{v_{s}},L^{1}_{v_{s}})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}[{\mathcal{Q}_{s}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{Q}_{s}}]}.

We start by pointing out the following two identities (see

[

(c)

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}L^{2}_{v_{s}}=W(\mathcal{F}L^{2}_{v_{s}},L^{2}),\,\,\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{F}\mathcal{Q}_{s}=\mathcal{Q}_{s},\,\,\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

[

(b)

Multiplicative Maps on Generalized n n -matrix Rings


Abstract
Let ℜ \mathfrak{R} and ℜ ′ \mathfrak{R}^{\prime} be two associative rings (not necessarily with the identity elements). A bijective map φ \varphi of ℜ \mathfrak{R} onto ℜ ′ \mathfrak{R}^{\prime} is called a m m -multiplicative isomorphism if φ ( x 1 ⋯ x m ) = φ ( x 1 ) ⋯ φ ( x m ) \varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{m})=\varphi(x_{1})\cdots\varphi(x_{m}) for all x 1 , ⋯ , x m ∈ ℜ . x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\in\mathfrak{R}. In this article, we establish a condition on generalized n n -matrix rings, that assures that multiplicative maps are additive on generalized n n -matrix rings under certain restrictions. And then, we apply our result for study of m m -multiplicative isomorphism and m m -multiplicative derivation on generalized n n -matrix rings.

Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
be two associative rings (not necessarily with the identity elements). We denote by
\mathfrak{Z}(\mathfrak{R})
the center of
\mathfrak{R}.
A bijective map
\varphi
of
\mathfrak{R}
onto
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
is called a
m
-multiplicative isomorphism
if

\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{m})=\varphi(x_{1})\cdots\varphi(x_{m})

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\in\mathfrak{R}.
In particular, if
m=2
then
\varphi
is called a
multiplicative isomorphism
. Similarly, a map
d
of
\mathfrak{R}
is called a
m
-multiplicative derivation
if

d(x_{1}\cdots x_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{1}\cdots d(x_{i})\cdots x_{m}

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\in\mathfrak{R}.
If
d(xy)=d(x)y+xd(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{R}
, we just say that
d
is a
multiplicative derivation
of
\mathfrak{R}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{1},{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{2},\cdots,{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{n}
be rings and
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}

({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i},{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j})
-bimodules with
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ii}={\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Let
\varphi_{ijk}:{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}\otimes_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}}{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{jk}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ik}
be
({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i},{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{k})
-bimodules homomorphisms with
\varphi_{iij}:{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}\otimes_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
and
\varphi_{ijj}:{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}\otimes_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
the canonical isomorphisms for all
i,j,k\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Write
a\circ b=\varphi_{ijk}(a\otimes b)
for
a\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij},

b\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{jk}.
We consider

(i)

{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
is faithful as a left
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}
-module and faithful as a right
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}
-module with
i\neq j,

(ii)

if
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
is such that
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}m_{ij}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}=0
then
m_{ij}=0
with
i\neq j.

Let

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}\,=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}r_{11}&m_{12}&\ldots&m_{1n}\\
m_{21}&r_{22}&\ldots&m_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
m_{n1}&m_{n2}&\ldots&r_{nn}\\
\end{array}\right)_{n\times n}~:~\underbrace{r_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}~(={\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ii}),~m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}}_{(i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\})}\right\}

be the set of all
n\times n
matrices
[m_{ij}]
with the
(i,j)
-entry
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
for all
i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. Observe that, with the obvious matrix operations of addition and multiplication,
{\mathfrak{G}}\,
is a ring iff
a\circ(b\circ c)=(a\circ b)\circ c
for all
a\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ik}
,
b\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{kl}
and
c\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{lj}
for all
i,j,k,l\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
. When
{\mathfrak{G}}\,
is a ring, it is called a generalized
n-
matrix ring.

Note that if
n=2,
then we have the generalized matrix ring. We denote by
\bigoplus^{n}_{i=1}r_{ii}
the element

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}r_{11}&&&\\
&r_{22}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&r_{nn}\\
\end{array}\right)

in
{\mathfrak{G}}\,.

Set
{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{ij}=\left\{\left(m_{kt}\right):~m_{kt}=\left\{{\begin{matrix}m_{ij},&\textrm{if}~(k,t)=(i,j)\\
0,&\textrm{if}~(k,t)\neq(i,j)\end{matrix}},~i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}\right\}.\right.
Then we can write
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{G}}\,=\bigoplus_{i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{ij}.
Henceforth the element
a_{ij}
belongs
{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{ij}
and the corresponding elements are in
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{1},\cdots,{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{n}
or
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}.
By a direct calculation
a_{ij}a_{kl}=0
if
j\neq k.

We define natural projections
\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}:{\mathfrak{G}}\,\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}

(1\leq i\leq n)
by

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}r_{11}&m_{12}&\ldots&m_{1n}\\
m_{21}&r_{22}&\ldots&m_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
m_{n1}&m_{n2}&\ddots&r_{nn}\\
\end{array}\right)\longmapsto r_{ii}.

The following result is a characterization of center of generalized
n
-matrix ring.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{G}}\,
be a generalized
n-
matrix ring. The center of
{\mathfrak{G}}\,
is

\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{G}}\,)=\left\{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}r_{ii}~\Big|~r_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}r_{jj}\mbox{ for all }m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij},~i\neq j\right\}.

Furthermore,
\mathfrak{{\mathfrak{Z}}\,}({\mathfrak{G}}\,)_{ii}\cong\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{G}}\,))\subseteq\mathfrak{{\mathfrak{Z}}\,}({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i})
, and there exists a unique ring
isomorphism
\tau^{j}_{i}
from
\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))
to
\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))

i\neq j
such that
r_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})
for all
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}.

Proof.

Let
S=\left\{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}r_{ii}~\Big|~r_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}r_{jj}\mbox{ for all }m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij},~i\neq j\right\}.
By a direct calculation we have that if
r_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i})
and
r_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}r_{jj}
for every
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
for all
i\neq j
, then
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}r_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)
; that is,
\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i})\right)\cap S\subseteq{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,).
To prove that
S={\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,),
we must show that
{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)\subseteq S
and
S\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}).

Suppose that

x=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}r_{11}&m_{12}&\ldots&m_{1n}\\
m_{21}&r_{22}&\ldots&m_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
m_{n1}&m_{n2}&\ddots&r_{nn}\\
\end{array}\right)\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,).

Since
x\big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\big)=\big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\big)x
for all
a_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i},
we have
a_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}a_{jj}
for
i\neq j
. Making
a_{jj}=0
we conclude
a_{ii}m_{ij}=0
for all
a_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}
and so
m_{ij}=0
for all
i\neq j
which implies that
x=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}r_{ii}
. Moreover, for any
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
as

x\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&m_{ij}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&m_{ij}&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0&\ldots&0\end{array}\right)x,

then
r_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}r_{jj}
for all
i\neq j
which results in
{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)\subseteq S
. Now suppose
x=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}r_{ii}\in S.
Then for any
a_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}

(i=1,\cdots,n-1),
we have
(r_{ii}a_{ii}-a_{ii}r_{ii})m_{ij}=r_{ii}(a_{ii}m_{ij})-a_{ii}(r_{ii}m_{ij})=(a_{ii}m_{ij})r_{jj}-a_{ii}(m_{ij}r_{jj})=0
for all
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}

(i\neq j)
and hence
r_{ii}a_{ii}-a_{ii}r_{ii}=0
as
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
is left faithful
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}
-module.
Now for
i=n
we have
m_{in}(r_{nn}a_{nn}-a_{nn}r_{nn})=m_{in}(r_{nn}a_{nn})-m_{in}(a_{nn}r_{nn})=(m_{in}r_{nn})a_{nn}-(m_{in}a_{nn})r_{nn}=(r_{ii}m_{in})a_{nn}-r_{ii}(m_{in}a_{nn})=0
and hence
r_{nn}a_{nn}-a_{nn}r_{nn}=0
as
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{in}
is right faithful
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{n}
-module. Therefore
r_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}),

i=1,\cdots,n
. Hence,
S\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}).

The fact that
\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))\subseteq{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i})
for
i=1,\cdots,n
are direct consequences of
{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)=S\subseteq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}).
Now we prove the existence of the ring isomorphism
\tau^{j}_{i}:\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))\longrightarrow\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))
for
i\neq j
. For this, let us consider a pair of indices
(i,j)
such that
i\neq j
. For any
r=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}r_{kk}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)
let us define
\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})=r_{jj}
. The application is well defined because if
s=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}s_{kk}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)
is such that
s_{ii}=r_{ii}
, then we have
m_{ij}r_{jj}=r_{ii}m_{ij}=s_{ii}m_{ij}=m_{ij}s_{jj}
for all
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
. Since
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
is right faithful
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}
-module, we conclude that
r_{jj}=s_{jj}
. Therefore, for any
r_{ii}\in\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)),
there exists a unique
r_{jj}\in\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)),
denoted by
\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})
. It is easy to see that
\tau^{j}_{i}
is bijective. Moreover, for any
r_{ii},s_{ii}\in\pi_{{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}}({\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,))
we have

m_{ij}\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii}+s_{ii})=(r_{ii}+s_{ii})m_{ij}=m_{ij}(r_{jj}+s_{jj})=m_{ij}\big(\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})+\tau^{j}_{i}(s_{ii})\big)
and
m_{ij}\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii}s_{ii})=(r_{ii}s_{ii})m_{ij}=r_{ii}(s_{ii}m_{ij})=(s_{ii}m_{ij})\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})=s_{ii}\big(m_{ij}\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})\big)=m_{ij}\big(\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})\tau^{j}_{i}(s_{ii})\big)
.
Thus
\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii}+s_{ii})=\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})+\tau^{j}_{i}(s_{ii})
and
\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii}s_{ii})=\tau^{j}_{i}(r_{ii})\tau^{j}_{i}(s_{ii})
and so
\tau^{j}_{i}
is a ring isomorphism.
∎

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{G}}\,
be a generalized
n-
matrix ring and
i\neq j
such that:

(i)

a_{ii}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}=0
implies
a_{ii}=0
for
a_{ii}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}
;

(ii)

{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}b_{jj}=0
implies
b_{jj}=0
for all
b_{jj}\in{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}
.

Then
u{\mathfrak{G}}\,=0
or
{\mathfrak{G}}\,u=0
implies
u=0
for
u\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,
.

Proof.

First, let us observe that if
i\neq j
and
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}a_{ii}=0,
then we have
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}a_{ii}m_{ij}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}=0
, for all
m_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}
, which implies
a_{ii}m_{ij}=0
by condition
(ii)
of the Definition
1.1
. It follows that
a_{ii}{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{ij}=0
resulting in
a_{ii}=0
. Hence, suppose
u=\bigoplus_{i,j\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}}u_{ij}
, with
u_{ij}\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{ij}
, satisfying
u{\mathfrak{G}}\,=0
. Then
u_{kk}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{k}=0
which yields
u_{kk}=0
for
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, by condition
(i)
. Now for
k=n
,
u_{nn}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{n}=0,
we have
{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{i}m_{in}u_{nn}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{n}=0
, for all
m_{in}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{in}
, which implies
m_{in}u_{nn}=0
by condition
(ii)
of the Definition
1.1
. It follows that
{\mathfrak{M}}\,_{in}u_{nn}=0
which implies
u_{nn}=0
. Thus
u_{ij}{\mathfrak{R}}\,_{j}=0
and then
u_{ij}=0
by condition
(ii)
of the Definition
1.1
. Therefore
u=0
. Similarly, we prove that if
{\mathfrak{G}}\,u=0
then
u=0
.
∎

The Main Theorem

Follows our main result which has the purpose of generalizing Theorem
2.1
in
[
3
]
.
Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
B:{\mathfrak{G}}\,\times{\mathfrak{G}}\,\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{G}}\,
be a biadditive map such that:

(i)

B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pp},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{qq})\subseteq{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pp}\cap{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{qq}
;
B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pp},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs})\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs}
and
B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pp})\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs}
;

B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pq},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs})=0
;

(ii)

if
B(\bigoplus_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}c_{pq},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{nn})=0
or
B(\bigoplus_{1\leq r<n}{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rr},\bigoplus_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}c_{pq})=0
, then
\bigoplus_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}c_{pq}=0
;

(iii)

B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{nn},a_{nn})=0
implies
a_{nn}=0
;

(iv)

if
B(\bigoplus_{p=1}^{n}c_{pp},{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs})=B({\mathfrak{G}}\,_{rs},\bigoplus_{p=1}^{n}c_{pp})=0
for all
1\leq r\neq s\leq n
, then
\bigoplus_{p=1}^{n-1}c_{pp}\oplus(-c_{nn})\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)
;

(v)

B(c_{pp},d_{pp})=B(d_{pp},c_{pp})
and
B(c_{pp},d_{pp})d_{pn}d_{nn}=d_{pp}d_{pn}B(c_{nn},d_{nn})
for all
c=\bigoplus_{p=1}^{n}c_{pp}\in{\mathfrak{Z}}\,({\mathfrak{G}}\,)
;

(vi)

B\big(c_{rr},B(c_{kl},c_{nn})\big)=B\big(B(c_{rr},c_{kl}),c_{nn}\big)
.

Suppose
f:{\mathfrak{G}}\,\times{\mathfrak{G}}\,\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{G}}\,
a map satisfying the following conditions:

(vii)

f({\mathfrak{G}}\,,0)=f(0,{\mathfrak{G}}\,)=0
;

(viii)

B\big(f(x,y),z\big)=f\big(B(x,z),B(y,z)\big)
;

(ix)

B\big(z,f(x,y)\big)=f\big(B(z,x),B(z,y)\big)

for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,
. Then
f=0
.

Proof.

Following the ideas of Ferreira in
[
3
]
we divide the proof into the four cases. Then, let us consider arbitrary elements
x_{kl},u_{kl},a_{kl}\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{kl}

(k,l\in\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\})
.

First case.
In this first case the reader should keep in mind that we want to show

f\big(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk}\big)=0.

From the hypotheses of the theorem, we have

\displaystyle B\left(f\big(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\linebreak\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk}\big),a_{nn}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\big(B\left(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},a_{nn}\right),B\left(\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk},a_{nn}\right)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\big(0,B\left(\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk},a_{nn}\right)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Now by condition
(i)
, this implies that

B\left(\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pq},a_{nn}\right)=0.

Since

\displaystyle B\left(\sum_{1\leq p<n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pp},a_{nn}\right)=0,

\displaystyle B\left(\sum_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}\linebreak f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pq},a_{nn}\right)\in\bigoplus_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pq}

and

B\left(f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\linebreak\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{nn},a_{nn}\right)\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{nn},

then

\sum_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pq}=0\mbox{~by~ condition ~(ii)~}.

Next, we have

\displaystyle B\left(a_{nn},f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(B(a_{nn},\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii}),B(a_{nn},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(0,B(a_{nn},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

which implies

\sum_{1\leq p,q\leq n}B\left(a_{nn},f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pq}\right)=0.

It follows that

B\left(a_{nn},\sum_{1\leq p<n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pp}\right)=0,

B\left(a_{nn},\sum_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pq}\right)\in\bigoplus_{1\leq p\neq q\leq n}{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{pq}

and

B\left(a_{nn},f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{nn}\right)\in{\mathfrak{G}}\,_{nn}.

Hence,

B\left(a_{nn},f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\linebreak\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{nn}\right)=0

which yields

f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{nn}=0

by condition
(iii)
. Yet, we have

\displaystyle B\left(\sum_{1\leq p<n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pp},a_{rs}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\left(f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk}),a_{rs}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(B(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},a_{rs}),B(\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk},a_{rs})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(B(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},a_{rs}),0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

and

\displaystyle B\left(a_{rs},\sum_{1\leq p<n}f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})_{pp}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\left(a_{rs},f(\sum_{1\leq i<n}x_{ii},\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq n}x_{jk})\right)

\displaystyle=

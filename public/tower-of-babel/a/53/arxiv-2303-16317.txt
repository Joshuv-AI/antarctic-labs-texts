Operator learning with PCA-Net: upper and lower complexity bounds
Samuel Lanthaler slanth@caltech.edu

Abstract
PCA-Net is a recently proposed neural operator architecture which combines principal component analysis (PCA) with neural networks to approximate operators between infinite-dimensional function spaces. The present work develops approximation theory for this approach, improving and significantly extending previous work in this direction: First, a novel universal approximation result is derived, under minimal assumptions on the underlying operator and the data-generating distribution. Then, two potential obstacles to efficient operator learning with PCA-Net are identified, and made precise through lower complexity bounds; the first relates to the complexity of the output distribution, measured by a slow decay of the PCA eigenvalues. The other obstacle relates to the inherent complexity of the space of operators between infinite-dimensional input and output spaces, resulting in a rigorous and quantifiable statement of a “curse of parametric complexity”, an infinite-dimensional analogue of the well-known curse of dimensionality encountered in high-dimensional approximation problems. In addition to these lower bounds, upper complexity bounds are finally derived. A suitable smoothness criterion is shown to ensure an algebraic decay of the PCA eigenvalues. Furthermore, it is shown that PCA-Net can overcome the general curse for specific operators of interest, arising from the Darcy flow and the Navier-Stokes equations.

Introduction

The application of neural networks
(
Goodfellow et al. 2016
)
to computational science and engineering is receiving growing interest. At their core, many problems of scientific interest involve the approximation of an underlying operator, which defines a mapping between two infinite-dimensional spaces of functions.
Neural operators

(
Anandkumar et al. 2020
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
;
Lu et al. 2021
;
Kovachki et al. 2023
)
are a generalization of neural networks to such an infinite-dimensional setting. They aim to approximate, or “learn”, operators from data given in the form of input and output pairs. Neural operators hold promise as surrogate models to accelerate and complement traditional numerical methods in many-query problems, and they can be used for data-driven discovery of the underlying input-output map, even when no mathematical model is available.

Recent years have seen the emergence of several neural operator architectures. This includes deep operator networks (DeepONet)
(
Lu et al. 2021
)
, building on early work on operator learning in
(
Chen and Chen 1995
)
. We also mention subsequent extensions of DeepONets, for example
(
Jin et al. 2022
;
Prasthofer et al. 2022
;
Seidman et al. 2022
;
Lanthaler et al. 2023b
;
Patel et al. 2022
)
.
DeepONets have been deployed with success in a variety of applications
(
Di Leoni et al. 2021
;
Mao et al. 2021
;
Cai et al. 2021
)
. Another popular approach is based on a class of neural operators introduced in
(
Anandkumar et al. 2020
;
Kovachki et al. 2023
)
. Here, neural operators are defined in close analogy with conventional neural networks, where the weight matrices in the hidden layers are generalized to integral operators. Special cases of this framework include the graph neural operator
(
Anandkumar et al. 2020
;
Li et al. 2020
)
and the Fourier neural operator (FNO)
(
Li et al. 2021
)
. In this context, we also mention related frameworks in
(
Raonic et al. 2023
;
Tripura and Chakraborty 2023
;
Gupta et al. 2021
)
. Another notable, and somewhat distinct, approach to operator learning is the operator-valued random feature model proposed in
(
Nelsen and Stuart 2021
)
.

Universal approximation results for many of these frameworks are known in a variety of settings; the universality of DeepONets is established in
(
Chen and Chen 1995
;
Lu et al. 2021
;
Lanthaler et al. 2022
)
, FNOs are shown to be universal in
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
, another universal approximation result for neural operators is derived in
(
Kovachki et al. 2023
)
. We also mention recent work
(
Lanthaler et al. 2023a
)
, which proves a general universal approximation result for the so-called “averaging neural operator” (ANO), a minimal architecture that is at the core of many other frameworks, thereby allowing to unify much of the analysis of this emerging zoo of neural operator architectures.

Going beyond universality, it is crucial to improve our understanding of the required computational complexity of neural operators in order to assess when the methods will be effective. Pertinent numerical experiments may be found in
(
De Hoop et al. 2022
)
. Relevant analysis of linear problems from this point of view has been given in
(
Boullé and Townsend 2022
;
de Hoop et al. 2023
)
. The required complexity of neural network-based methods for specific PDE operators of interest are studied from a approximation theoretic point of view, in e.g.

(
Kovachki et al. 2021
;
Schwab and Zech 2019
;
Kutyniok et al. 2022
;
Lanthaler et al. 2022
;
Ryck and Mishra 2022
)
.
A focus of these papers is on beating the
“curse of dimensionality”
.

Since the input and output spaces are infinite-dimensional in these problems, clarification may be needed as to the meaning of beating the curse of dimensionality: it is interpreted as identifying conditions under which the required size (number of tunable parameters) of the operator approximation grows only algebraically
with the inverse of the desired error.
This notion of the ”curse of dimensionality” is specific to operator learning and somewhat distinct from the conventional meaning of this term in the context of high-dimensional problems. To disambiguate the well-known curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional approximation problems from the analogue of this curse in infinite-dimensional approximation (i.e. operator learning), we will refer to the latter as the “curse of parametric complexity”. The present work will provide further clarification and motivation for the use of this term in the context of operator learning, and will connect the curse of dimensionality with this curse of parametric complexity.

The focus of the present work is the so-called
PCA-Net
, a methodology which combines ideas from principal component analysis with neural networks
(
Hesthaven and Ubbiali 2018
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
.
Principal component analysis (PCA) is a standard tool for dimension reduction in high-dimensional statistics and unsupervised learning
(
Jolliffe 2002
)
. In
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
, a combination of PCA with neural networks has been proposed as a data-driven operator learning framework. As indicated above, the goal of operator learning is to approximate an unknown operator
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, mapping between two infinite-dimensional spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
. Given data in the form of pairs of inputs and outputs, we seek to determine an accurate, data-driven approximation of
\Psi^{\dagger}
. The PCA-Net operator learning architecture achieves this goal by (i) using PCA to reduce the dimensions of the input and output spaces and (ii) approximating a map between the resulting finite-dimensional latent spaces
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
. First analysis, including a universal approximation result, have been derived in
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
. Furthermore, in the same work, the efficacy of the proposed architecture has been demonstrated empirically for prototypical problems, including the solution operator of the viscous Burgers equation and the Darcy flow equation. However, so far, a detailed mathematical analysis providing a theoretical underpinning for this empirically observed efficiency of PCA-Net, has been outstanding.

The present work fills this gap by developing relevant approximation theory for PCA-Net. The main contributions of this paper are the following:

•

Universal approximation:
We prove a novel universal approximation theorem for PCA-Net, Theorem
6
, under significantly relaxed conditions on the distribution of the data-generating measure and the underlying operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
compared to previous work; the universality of PCA-Net is here shown under natural minimal conditions, which are in fact necessary for PCA to be well-defined on the input and output spaces.

•

Curse of parametric complexity:
A rigorous result is proven which demonstrates that the curse of parametric complexity, an infinite-dimensional scaling limit of the conventional curse of dimensionality, cannot be overcome by PCA-Net in general (cp. Theorem
9
); more precisely, this result shows that it is impossible to derive algebraic complexity bounds when considering general classes of operators, such as the class of all Lipschitz- or even
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-continuous operators. Hence, we conclude that at this level of generality, the curse is unavoidable.

•

Overcoming the curse of parametric complexity:
Given the negative result on the general curse, we argue that a central challenge in operator learning is to identify the relevant class of operators which
do allow
for efficient approximation by a given operator learning framework. To gain further insight into the relevant mathematical structure that can be leveraged by PCA-Net, we restrict attention to two prototypical PDE operators of interest arising from the Darcy flow and Navier-Stokes equations. In both cases, we show that PCA-Net can overcome the general curse of parametric complexity; algebraic error and complexity estimates are established in Theorems
18
and
25
, demonstrating that these operators belong to a restricted class which is efficiently approximated by PCA-Net.

.

1.2
Notation

Throughout the following discussion,
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
denote separable Hilbert spaces. We will use
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\langle{\,\cdot\,},{\,\cdot\,}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
to denote the norm and inner product on
\mathcal{H}
; if it is clear from the context, we may occasionally omit the subscript to aid readability. On finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces,
|{\,\cdot\,}|
is used for the Euclidean norm, and
|{\,\cdot\,}|_{\infty}
denotes the maximum-norm. The space of probability measures on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. We denote by
u\sim\mu
a random variable distributed according to probability measure
\mu
.
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[F(u)]
denotes the expectation of
F
with respect to
\mu
. We consistently use
\Psi^{\dagger}
to denote the underlying (truth) operator, and
\Psi
will denote a (PCA-Net) approximation of
\Psi^{\dagger}
. For two numbers
a,b
, we will write
a\sim b
for equivalence up to constants, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that
C^{-1}a\leq b\leq Ca
. Similarly
a\lesssim b
,
a\gtrsim b
denotes inequality up to a constant, i.e.
a\leq Cb
and
Ca\geq b
, respectively. On occasion, we write a subscript
a\lesssim_{k}b
to emphasize the dependence of the implied constant
C=C(k)
on a given parameter
k
. We follow the convention that constants in estimates can change their value from line to line; their dependence on the relevant parameters will always be indicated. Other notation is introduced as needed.

2
PCA-Net methodology

PCA-Net combines principal component analysis (PCA) for dimension reduction of the input and output spaces, with a neural network mapping between the resulting finite-dimensional latent spaces. Before summarizing PCA-Net, we first review PCA in subsection
2.1
, and derive two high-probability estimates for the PCA projection error in subsection
2.2
. Next, we summarize how PCA-Net combines PCA with a neural network, resulting in an operator learning architecture in subsection
2.3
.

2.1
Principal Component Analysis

In the present section, we provide necessary background material on PCA, and we prove a high-probability estimate for the PCA projection error, building on previous results
(
Reiß and Wahl 2020
;
Milbradt and Wahl 2020
)
.

PCA.

Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
, and a projection dimension
d
, PCA aims to minimize the average reconstruction error
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u-Pu\|^{2}]
over the set
\Pi_{d}
of orthogonal projections
P:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
of rank
d
. It is well-known (e.g.
(
Jolliffe 2002
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
;
Reiß and Wahl 2020
)
) that this can be achieved by considering the covariance operator
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[u\otimes u]
, which is diagonalizable; i.e., there exists a sequence
{\lambda}_{1}\geq{\lambda}_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
of eigenvalues and corresponding orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
{\phi}_{1},{\phi}_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{H}
, such that
\Sigma{\phi}_{j}={\lambda}_{j}{\phi}_{j}
for all
j
. The optimal PCA projection of dimension
d
,
{P}_{\leq d}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, can then be written as a composition
{P}_{\leq d}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}
, where the optimal PCA encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}
is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(u):=(\langle u,\phi_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle u,\phi_{d}\rangle),

(1)

and the corresponding PCA decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}
is the mapping,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{H},\quad\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta_{j}\phi_{j}.

(2)

It can be shown that
{P}_{\leq d}
defines an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{H}
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u-{P}_{\leq d}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]=\min_{P\in\Pi_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u-Pu\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}].

In the following we denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu):=\min_{P\in\Pi_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u-Pu\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}],

(3)

this optimal PCA projection error. One can show, e.g.
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
, Thm. 3.8)
, that
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu)
is related to the PCA eigenvalues by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu)=\sum_{j>d}\lambda_{j}.

(4)

Empirical PCA.

Empirical PCA applies the above procedure to the empirical distribution
\mu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{u_{k}}
, obtained by sampling from
\mu
: Given a finite number of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
, define the covariance operator
{\Sigma}_{N}
, by

\displaystyle{\Sigma}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes u_{k}.

(5)

Letting
\widehat{\lambda}_{1}\geq\widehat{\lambda}_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
and
\widehat{\phi}_{1},\widehat{\phi}_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{H}
denote the eigenvalues and corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis. The empirical PCA projection
\widehat{P}_{\leq d}
of dimension
d
is given by
\widehat{P}_{\leq d}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
are defined as in (
1
), (
2
), but replacing the eigenvectors
\phi_{j}
by their empirical counterparts
\widehat{\phi}_{j}
.

2.2
Projection error of empirical PCA

We first note that the empirical PCA projection error approximates the optimal projection error, provided that a sufficient amount of data is available. We state the following result in high probability.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
be a probability measure with finite second moments,
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]<\infty
. Then for any
\delta,\epsilon>0
, there exists a requisite amount of data
N_{0}=N_{0}(\mu,d,\delta,\epsilon)
, such that the encoding error for empirical PCA with dimension
d
, and based on
N\geq N_{0}
samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
, satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu)+\epsilon,

(6)

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

The proof of Proposition
1
relies on a well-known bound on the excess risk for empirical PCA in terms of the Hilbert-Schmidt distance
\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{2}
of the true and empirical covariance operators
(
Reiß and Wahl 2020
, e.g. Sect. 2.2)
. This is combined with a general Monte-Carlo estimate to prove Proposition
1
. We present the details in Appendix
A
.

The result of Proposition
1
is purely qualitative, as it doesn’t give us any estimate on the required amount of data
N
. Our next goal is to establish a
quantitative
bound, under additional assumptions on
\mu
. We will call a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})

sub-Gaussian
, if there exists
K_{\mu}\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}}\right]^{1/p}\leq K_{\mu}\sqrt{p},\quad\forall\,p\geq 1.

(7)

According to this definition (
7
),
\mu
is sub-Gaussian if the real-valued random variable
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, with
u\sim\mu
, is sub-Gaussian in the conventional sense. The moment bound (
7
) is one of many equivalent characterizations of real-valued sub-Gaussian random variables (see e.g.
(
Vershynin 2018
, Sect. 2.5.1)
). We then have:

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space, and let
\mu
be a sub-Gaussian probability measure on
\mathcal{H}
. Fix
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. The encoding error for empirical PCA with dimension
d
, and based on
N\geq\log(2/\delta)
samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
, satisfies the following upper bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu)+\sqrt{\frac{Qd\log(2/\delta)}{N}}.

(8)

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Here,
Q=Q(K_{\mu})
depends only on the constant
K_{\mu}
in (
7
).

Proposition
2
above is a natural high-probability analogue of a previous result on empirical PCA of
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
, derived in expectation in that work. Proposition
2
uses the same bound on the PCA excess risk as Proposition
1
, but combines it with a general Bernstein concentration bound for
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variables to derive quantitative rates. We include the details in Appendix
A
.

Remark 3
.

We note that under more fine-grained information on the underlying measure
\mu
, considerable improvements to the upper bound of Proposition
2
are possible; this has e.g. been achieved in
(
Milbradt and Wahl 2020
)
for a different notion of a “sub-Gaussian” distribution, requiring that

\displaystyle\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[|\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{p}]^{1/p}}{\sqrt{p}}\leq K^{\prime}_{\mu}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[|\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}]^{1/2},\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{H},

(9)

for a constant
K_{\mu}^{\prime}
depending only on
\mu
.
The condition (
9
) is stronger than (
7
); Assuming (
9
), it can in fact be shown that the empirical PCA projection error is of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\mu)\right)
whenever
N\gtrsim d\log(1/\delta)
, thereby achieving essentially optimal PCA convergence rates. However, in the present context, PCA will be applied for dimension reduction on both the input and output spaces under a non-linear mapping
\Psi^{\dagger}
. Unfortunately, even if
\mu
satisfies (
9
), it is unclear whether this property is preserved under the push-forward by
\Psi^{\dagger}
, i.e. whether a bound of the form (
9
) continues to hold for
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
. In contrast, the bound (
7
) is robust under such a push-forward. Therefore, we contend ourselves with the more pessimistic bound of Proposition
2
, and leave potential improvements, possibly building on
(
Milbradt and Wahl 2020
)
, as a challenge for future work.

2.3
PCA-Net architecture

We next recall the PCA-Net architecture proposed in
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
, which combines empirical PCA with a neural network mapping, to approximate an underlying operator
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
. In the following, let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be separable Hilbert spaces and let
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a non-linear operator. The goal of the PCA-Net methodology is to approximate
\Psi^{\dagger}
from a finite number of input-/output-samples
\{u_{k},\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
. To this end, PCA-Net combines an encoding
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
, a neural network
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
and a decoding
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}\to\mathcal{Y}
(cp. Figure
1
).

Diagrammatic illustration of PCA-Net based on a PCA encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, a neural network
\psi
, and a PCA decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Here,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
are chosen as an empirical PCA encoder and decoder, respectively. A precise definition is given below, see equations (
12
), (
13
). Given these ingredients, the resulting PCA-Net is defined as the mapping

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y},\quad\Psi(u):=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u).

(10)

The encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
and decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
perform a dimension reduction on the input and output spaces. The neural network
\psi
approximates a mapping on the resulting finite-dimensional latent spaces.

Associated with the encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, we will define a natural PCA decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
on
\mathcal{X}
. Similarly
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is accompanied by an encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
.
The intuition is that these underlying encoder/decoder pairs, and the neural network
\psi
, should satisfy the following approximate identities
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
:

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\approx\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\approx\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{Y}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\approx\Psi^{\dagger}.

Here,
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{Y}}
denote the identity mappings on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively. Our analysis of the PCA-methodology aims to quantify the accuracy of these approximations when the encoders/decoders are defined by PCA and
\psi
is chosen to be a neural network.

PCA encoding and decoding.

We will now specify the particular choice of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. In the following, we assume that the input samples
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
are i.i.d. samples from a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
. Note that the output-samples
\{\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
are then i.i.d. with respect to the corresponding push-forward measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
on
\mathcal{Y}
. For a given choice of latent dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, we apply empirical PCA to the samples
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{X}
and
\{\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{Y}
. In the following, we denote by

\displaystyle\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{1},\dots,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\in\mathcal{X},\quad\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{1},\dots,\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}\in\mathcal{Y},

(11)

the empirical PCA bases on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively. We emphasize that the empirical PCA bases are themselves random variables, as they depend on the random input-/output-samples
\{u_{k},\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
. The first basis,
\{\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
, defines an encoder on
\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}},\quad\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u):=(\langle u,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle u,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\rangle),

(12)

with corresponding decoder,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(\xi):=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\xi_{j}\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}
.
Similarly,
\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{1},\dots,\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
defines an encoder on
\mathcal{Y}
,

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v):=(\langle v,\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
,
with corresponding decoder,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\to\mathcal{Y},\quad\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\eta):=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\eta_{j}\phi^{\mathcal{Y}}_{j}.

(13)

Neural networks.

Given a depth
L\in\mathbb{N}
, layer widths
d_{k}
(
k=0,\dots,L+1
), and weights and biases
A_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k+1}\times d_{k}},b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k+1}}
,
k=0,\dots,L
, a
(deep) neural network
(DNN)
\psi
is a mapping
\xi\mapsto\psi(\xi)
, defined as a composition of non-linear layers,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \xi_{0}&:=\xi,\\
\xi_{k+1}&:=\sigma(A_{k}\xi_{k}+b_{k}),\quad\text{for }k=0,\dots,L-1,\\
\psi(\xi)&:=A_{L}\xi_{L}+b_{L}.\end{aligned}\right.

(14)

The non-linear layers are expressed in terms of an activation function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which is applied componentwise. In the following, we will restrict attention to the ReLU activation function
\sigma(\xi):=\max(\xi,0)
. Extension of our results to more general choices of
\sigma
is possible.

Given a DNN
\psi
, we define
\mathrm{size}(\psi):=\sum_{k=0}^{L}(\|A_{k}\|_{0}+\|b_{k}\|_{0})
as the total number of non-zero weights and biases in the architecture, and we define
0pt(\psi):=L
as the number of layers. With these definitions,
\mathrm{size}(\psi)
and
0pt(\psi)
provide a measure of the complexity of the DNN.

Remark 4
.

In the present work, we employ the above notion of size as a measure of the complexity of a neural network. This is sufficient for our present purposes, but it should be pointed out that this notion of complexity does not take into account the number of bits which would be required to encode each parameter in a practical implementation. Recent work such as
Yarotsky 2018
;
Siegel 2022
, and references therein, has shown that it is possible for very carefully constructed ReLU neural networks to achieve unexpectedly high convergence rates for the approximation of Sobolev functions. This comes at the expense of requiring a large number of bits to encode the individual weights, e.g.
(
Siegel 2022
, discussion on page 5)
.

Neural network training.

Ideally, the neural network would be chosen as a minimizer of the expected loss

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\psi):=\mathbb{E}_{(\xi,\eta)}\left[|\psi(\xi)-\eta|^{2}\right],

where the expectation is over pairs
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}
of encoded input-/output-pairs of the form
\xi=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)
,
\eta=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\Psi^{\dagger}(u))
, with
u\sim\mu
.
In practice, the neural network
\psi
in (
10
) is usually trained to minimize the following empirical loss,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\psi):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\psi(\xi_{k})-\eta_{k}|^{2},

where
\xi_{k}:=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
\eta_{k}:=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k}))\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
are the encoded input and output samples.

Remark 5
.

In the present work, we will not address the practical training of the neural network
\psi
, analysis of which is a notoriously difficult problem. Instead, we will contend ourselves with an “approximation theoretic” approach; it will be shown that certain
\psi
exists, but no a priori guarantee is given that this
\psi
can be found by the numerical optimization of the empirical loss
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
. The samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
will, however, enter our analysis of the empirical PCA encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
and decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

3
Approximation theory

This section develops approximation theory for PCA-Net, and is divided into four subsections. First, in Section
3.1
, we discuss a new universality theorem for PCA-Net. Next, we point out two potential obstacles to efficient operator learning with PCA-Net in Section
3.2
. As explained there, one such obstacle relates to the complexity of the output distribution on
\mathcal{Y}
, which could in principle lead to an arbitrarily slow decay of the PCA-Net error with the PCA-dimension
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
(cp. Proposition
8
). The second potential obstacle to efficient operator learning relates to the inherent complexity of the space of operators between infinite-dimensional spaces (cp. Theorem
9
); this makes rigorous a notion of “curse of dimensionality” for operator learning intuited in earlier work
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
;
Lanthaler et al. 2022
)
. The lower bounds of Section
3.2
are complemented by upper bounds in the subsequent sections; In Section
3.3
, we show that a suitable smoothness condition rules out the first obstacle, ensuring an algebraic decay of the PCA encoding error on
\mathcal{Y}
. Finally, in Section
3.4
, we demonstrate that PCA-Net can leverage additional structure for two operators of interest, allowing it to overcome the general curse of dimensionality when approximating the solution operator of the Darcy flow and Navier-Stokes equations.

3.1
Universal approximation

We first state the following universal approximation theorem in high probability:

Theorem 6

(Universal approximation)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be separable Hilbert spaces and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a
\mu
-measurable mapping. Assume the following moment conditions,

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}],\;\;\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}]<\infty.

Then for any
\delta,\epsilon>0
, there are dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{X}}}(\epsilon,\delta)
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\epsilon,\delta)
, a requisite amount of data
N=N({d_{\mathcal{X}}},{d_{\mathcal{Y}}},\mu,\Psi^{\dagger})
, a neural network
\psi=\psi({\,\cdot\,};\{u_{k}\})
depending on this data, such that the PCA-Net,
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{Y}}\right]\leq\epsilon,

with probability at least
1-\delta
in the input data
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\sim\mu
.

We have written
\Psi(u)=\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})
to emphasize the dependency of the PCA-Net encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
and the neural network
\psi=\psi({\,\cdot\,};\{u_{k}\})
on the given data
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
.
The detailed proof, included in Appendix
B
, combines well-known universal approximation results for neural networks with the high-probability bounds on the PCA projection error in Proposition
1
in Section
2.2
, above; more precisely, it is shown in Lemma
32
that the PCA error can be decomposed into an error due to the encoding on
\mathcal{X}
, the decoding error on
\mathcal{Y}
and a neural network approximation error. The encoding and decoding errors are expressed in terms of the PCA projection error, which can be bounded by invoking Proposition
2
. The neural network approximation error can then be made arbitrarily small by a suitable choice of the neural network
\psi
.

Remark 7
.

As already pointed out in Remark
5
, the practical training of the neural network
\psi
is not discussed in the present work. Hence the above theorem should be viewed as a pure existence result, in the sense that the proof does not provide an explicit algorithmic recipe for determining the neural network
\psi
, given the data
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
.

Theorem
6
shows that PCA-Net is able to approximate almost arbitrary operators to any desired accuracy, provided a sufficient number of data points are available for empirical PCA, and provided that the underlying neural network is sufficiently large. A previous universal approximation result in
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
, Theorem 3.1)
was stated only for Lipschitz continuous
\Psi^{\dagger}
and under an assumption of finite fourth moments. In contrast, Theorem
6
requires no regularity condition on the underlying operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
, and shows that a bound on the second moments of the input measure
\mu
and the push-forward measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}
suffices for universal approximation. We have formulated Theorem
6
in high-probability, whereas
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
, Theorem 3.1)
is derived in expectation. This last difference is mostly for consistency with the high probability results in later sections; indeed, Theorem
6
is in fact derived from a corresponding result in expectation (cp. Proposition
31
).

The main drawback of universal approximation results is that they are purely qualitative, and do not provide any information about the required size of the data or the neural network; hence, universal approximation cannot provide information on the efficiency of operator learning with PCA-Net. Deriving more quantitative bounds is particularly relevant in view of the two potential obstacles to efficient operator learning, elaborated upon in the next section.

3.2
Obstacles to effective operator learning

Theorem
6
does not provide any quantitative information on the required complexity to achieve a given accuracy. For the practical success of PCA-Net in operator learning, it is crucial that the PCA-Net approximation is not only possible in principle, but also efficient in practice; we interpret “efficiency” as the statement that the PCA dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, the requisite amount of data
N
and the size of the neural network
\psi
, that is required to achieve a desired accuracy
\epsilon>0
, should grow at most at an algebraic rate
\epsilon^{-\gamma}
, with quantifiable exponent
\gamma>0
. As explained in this section, there are at least two potential obstacles to the efficiency of PCA-Net.

Complexity of the output distribution.

The first potential reason for the inefficiency of PCA-Net is a consequence of the following lower bound on the approximation error.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure, and let
\Psi^{\dagger}\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
be an operator. Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
be the PCA eigenvalues of the push-forward measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
on
\mathcal{Y}
. Then we have the following lower bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\geq\sum_{j>{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}\lambda_{j},

(15)

for any PCA-Net
\Psi
with PCA dimension
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
.

The proof of Proposition
8
is an almost verbatim repetition of the argument in
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
, Theorem 3.6)
; we won’t repeat the proof here. As a consequence of (
15
), the approximation error that can be achieved with a PCA dimension
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
is lower bounded by the decay of the PCA eigenvalues of the push-forward measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
, i.e. by the optimal PCA projection error
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)=\sum_{j>{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}\lambda_{j}
. In particular, if this decay is very slow, e.g.
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)\gtrsim\log({d_{\mathcal{Y}}})^{-1}
, then an
exponentially
large PCA dimension
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\epsilon)\sim\exp(\epsilon^{-1})
is required, which entails that also an exponential number of samples
N\gtrsim\exp(\epsilon^{-1})
and an exponential neural network size
\mathrm{size}(\psi)\gtrsim\exp(\epsilon^{-1})
are required: The bound on
N
follows from the fact that the empirical PCA covariance operator (
5
) is of rank at most
N
, and hence the determination of
d_{\mathcal{Y}}
non-trivial PCA eigendirections requires at least
N\geq d_{\mathcal{Y}}
samples. Furthermore, since the neural network
\psi
must have
d_{\mathcal{Y}}
non-trivial output components, at least
d_{\mathcal{Y}}
non-zero weights are required. The first potential obstacle thus relates to the
complexity of the output space
, encoded in the PCA eigenvalue decay of the measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
. We note in passing that the problem of a slow eigenvalue decay can sometimes be ameliorated by replacing the linear decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
by a non-linear mapping, leading to improved results both theoretically and empirically
(
Seidman et al. 2022
;
Lanthaler et al. 2023b
)
.

Curse of dimensionality and parametric complexity.

The last section shows that the complexity, or “size”, of the output space
\mathcal{Y}
can be one obstacle to operator learning with PCA-Net. A second potential obstacle to efficient operator learning is that the space of operators
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
itself is very large.

It is well-known that the task of approximating a high-dimensional function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by ordinary methods, such as polynomial approximation, suffers from a curse of dimensionality, where the number of degrees of freedom needed to achieve a desired accuracy scales exponentially in
d
. Indeed, optimal error bounds for interpolation are typically of the form,

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in D}|f(\xi)-p(\xi)|\lesssim\|f\|_{C^{k}}N^{-k/d},

(16)

where
p
denotes the (e.g. polynomial) interpolant and
N
represents the number of degrees of freedom of the interpolation space. Such upper bounds deteriorate for large
d
, with convergence rates becoming arbitrarily slow. Similar results have also been established for neural networks, see e.g.
(
Yarotsky 2017
;
Achour et al. 2022
;
Siegel 2022
)
, including upper and lower bounds on the required number of weights. In the context of operator learning, the underlying input and output spaces are infinite-dimensional. Given the unfavorable scaling of (
16
) with the dimension
d
, it is thus far from obvious that operator learning should be practicable at all.

In analogy with the above, we next introduce the notion of an
algebraic convergence rate
: Given a set
\mathcal{C}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
of operators, we say that
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}>0
is an algebraic convergence rate for
\mathcal{C}
, if for any
\Psi^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist a constant
C=C(\Psi^{\dagger})>0
, depending only on properties of the operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
, and a PCA-Net mapping
\Psi:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\leq C\,\mathrm{size}(\psi)^{-\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}},

(17)

The central point is that the convergence rate
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
in (
17
) should be uniform over
\mathcal{C}
, and that only the multiplicative constant
C
depends on
\Psi^{\dagger}
, thus allowing the approximation of any operator
\Psi^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}
to a desired accuracy,
at a rate specified by
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
.
This is a natural analogue of (
16
). In (
17
), the linear encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}
and decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\to\mathcal{Y}
are allowed to be arbitrary; In particular, we do not restrict the dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
. Even though the additional dependence on
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
is also of practical relevance, we postulate that any operator
\Psi^{\dagger}\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
, or indeed class of operators
\mathcal{C}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
, which is “efficiently” approximated by PCA-Net, must (at the very least) possess a finite algebraic convergence rate
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
in the above sense (
17
).

The next result shows that for often considered classes of operators
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, such as the set of all Lipschitz continuous or
r
-times Fréchet differentiable operators, efficient operator learning by PCA-Net is in fact impossible. In analogy with the finite-dimensional curse of dimensionality, we refer to this as the “curse of parametric complexity”:

Theorem 9

(Curse of parametric complexity)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be separable Hilbert spaces, with
\dim(\mathcal{X})=\infty
,
\dim(\mathcal{Y})\geq 1
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a non-degenerate Gaussian measure. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{C}^{k}
denote the set of all
k
-times Fréchet differentiable operators
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
whose
k
-th total derivative is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{X}
. For
any

\gamma>0
, there exists
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}
and a constant
c_{\gamma}>0
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\geq c_{\gamma}\mathrm{size}(\psi)^{-\gamma},

for any PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
. In particular, there
cannot
exist a finite algebraic convergence rate
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
for
\mathcal{C}^{k}
, as in (
17
).

Remark 10
.

Theorem
9
makes precise a notion of “curse of dimensionality” in operator learning with PCA-Net, which was anticipated in earlier work by
Kovachki et al. 2021
;
Lanthaler et al. 2022
. As will be explained, this result can indeed be interpreted as a scaling limit of the conventional curse of dimensionality. In contrast to the approximation of a high-dimensional function in
d\gg 1
dimensions, the goal of operator learning is to approximate a mapping defined on an infinite-dimensional space, where
d=\infty
. The “curse of parametric complexity” can be viewed as a scaling limit of the finite-dimensional curse of dimensionality, when
d\to\infty
.

Remark 11
.

The underlying input/ouptut spaces
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
are fixed in our setting. In particular, if
\mathcal{X}
is a function space e.g. consisting of functions
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
for some domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the dimension
n
of this domain
D
is fixed. The curse of parametric complexity identified in Theorem
9
is independent of the presence (or absence) of an additional curse of dimensionality that may arise due to the (potentially large) dimension
n
of the domain.

Remark 12
.

To simplify the statement of Theorem
9
, it is assumed that
\mu
is a non-degenerate Gaussian. Inspection of the proof shows that the non-degeneracy requirement could be relaxed to only assuming that the covariance operator
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[u\otimes u]
have infinitely many non-trivial eigendirections (with eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}\neq 0
), thus allowing for some degeneracy. The crucial assumption is that the input distribution
\mu
be “truly infinite-dimensional”.

Sketch of proof.

The proof of Theorem
9
is based on the following lower bound for ReLU neural network approximation of functions in the unit cube of the Sobolev space
W^{k,\infty}([0,1]^{d})
,

F_{k,d}:={\left\{f\in W^{k,\infty}([0,1]^{d})\,\middle|\,\|f\|_{W^{k,\infty}}\leq 1\right\}},

which is derived in the present work and may be of independent interest:

Proposition 13
.

Fix
k,d\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists
c_{k,d}>0
depending only on
k
and
d
,
f\in F_{k,d}
and an absolute constant
\lambda>0
independent of both
d
and
k
, such that for any neural network
\psi
, we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\|f-\psi\|_{L^{2}([0,1]^{d})}\geq c_{k,d}\,\mathrm{size}(\psi)^{-\lambda k/d}.

(18)

This finite-dimensional lower bound (
18
) builds on the recent work
(
Achour et al. 2022
)
. Given the lower bound of Proposition
13
, the proof of Theorem
9
is then based on the intuition that
d
can be chosen arbitrarily large if the underlying input space
\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional, and hence the exponent in (
18
) can be arbitrarily small; additional work is needed to make this intuition rigorous. Detailed proofs of Theorem
9
and Proposition
13
are given in Appendix
C
.
∎

In particular, Theorem
9
shows that it is
impossible
to derive algebraic error and complexity estimates for PCA-Net, when e.g. assuming only Lipschitz regularity of
\Psi^{\dagger}
. It should be emphasized that this result holds even when the relevant space of input and output functions can be efficiently approximated; indeed, no restriction on the decay of the PCA eigenvalues is assumed in Theorem
9
, allowing them to decay at an arbitrarily fast rate on both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
. In fact, Theorem
9
even allows for
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{R}
, in which case reconstruction on
\mathcal{Y}
is
trivial
(in contrast, the assumptions do imply that infinitely many PCA eigenvalues on
\mathcal{X}
are non-zero). As alluded to above, the reason for the obstacle to efficient operator learning expressed by Theorem
9
is the intrinsic complexity of the space of all
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-regular operators (or functionals), defined on an infinite-dimensional input space
\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 14
.

Under slightly stronger assumptions than Theorem
9
(in particular, assuming an algebraic decay of the PCA eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
), one can likely show that there in fact exists
\Psi^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}
and constants
c,\gamma>0
, depending only on
\mu
and
k
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\geq c\log\left(\mathrm{size}(\psi)\right)^{-\gamma}.

for any PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus, achieving accuracy
\epsilon
requires an
exponential complexity
of the underlying neural network,
\mathrm{size}(\psi)\gtrsim\exp(c\epsilon^{-1/\gamma})
. Similar exponential lower bounds will be the subject of forthcoming work
(
Lanthaler and Stuart 2023
)
.

Given the negative result of Theorem
9
, we posit that a central challenge in operator learning is to
identify
the relevant class of operators
\mathcal{C}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
, which allow for efficient approximation by a given operator learning framework, and which possess a prescribed (finite) algebraic convergence rate
\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}
in (
17
).

3.3
Quantitative encoding error bounds

As pointed out above, one potential obstacle to the efficacy of PCA-Nets is a slow decay of the PCA eigenvalues on the output function space. In this section, we provide a general estimate on the PCA projection error,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\nu)=\sum_{\ell>d}\lambda_{\ell}
, based on smoothness properties of the underlying functions. Given a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we recall that the Sobolev space
H^{s}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the space of all functions
u:D\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
possessing
L^{2}
-integral weak derivatives up to order
s
. We note that
H^{s}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space. We then have:

Proposition 15
.

Let
n,n^{\prime}
be integers. Let
\mathcal{Y}=H^{s}(D;\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}})
,
s\geq 0
, be a Sobolev space defined on either a Lipschitz domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
or the periodic torus
D=\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Assume that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Y})
is a probability measure and that there exists
\zeta>0
, such that
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\nu}\left[\|u\|_{H^{s+\zeta}}^{2}\right]<\infty
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n,n^{\prime})>0
, such that

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\nu)\leq Cd^{-2\zeta/n}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\nu}\left[\|u\|_{H^{s+\zeta}}^{2}\right],\quad\forall\,d\in\mathbb{N}.

We recall that
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d}(\nu)
is the minimal projection error over all projections
P
of rank
d
(cp.
3
).

Thus, smoothness of the output functions ensures an algebraic decay of the PCA eigenvalues and thus rules out the first potential obstacle to efficient operator learning by PCA-Net, pointed out after Proposition
8
.
A proof of Proposition
15
is provided in Appendix
D
. Proposition
15
extends a result in
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
, Prop. 3.14)
, which was restricted to the periodic case. The main novel ingredient in our proof here, and which allows the generalization to arbitrary Lipschitz domains
D
, is based on a general Sobolev extension result by Stein
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
, e.g. Appendix B)
.

3.4
Overcoming the curse of dimensionality

The last section provides a general criterion to tame the lower bound of Proposition
8
, which could limit the efficiency of PCA-Net when the relevant distribution of outputs in
\mathcal{Y}
is very complex (cf. Section
3.2
). As proved in Theorem
9
, a second obstacle to efficient operator learning is the
curse of dimensionality
. It would be very desirable to find a useful mathematical characterization of the entire class of operators
\mathcal{C}\subset L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{Y})
for which an algebraic convergence rate, as in (
17
), can be established. At present, this appears a very distant goal. Instead, in this section, we aim to develop additional intuition of the basic mechanisms that PCA-Net can exploit to achieve algebraic convergence rates for specific examples. To this end, we consider two prototypical operators arising in the context of PDEs; the Darcy flow and Navier-Stokes equations.

3.4.1
Darcy flow

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. We consider the operator
\Psi^{\dagger}:a\mapsto w
, mapping the coefficient field
a
to the solution
w
of the following elliptic problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\nabla\cdot\left(a(x)\nabla w(x)\right)&=f(x),&&(x\in D),\\
w(x)&=0,&&(x\in\partial D).\end{aligned}\right.

(19)

Here,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given smooth domain, the right-hand side
f
is fixed, and we assume Dirichlet boundary conditions. Let
H^{1}_{0}(D)
be the Sobolev space consisting of weakly differentiable functions
w:D\to\mathbb{R}
which vanish on the boundary
\partial D
, and whose gradient is square-integrable. It is well-known, e.g.
(
Evans 2010
, Chapt. 6)
, that if the coefficient field
a\in L^{\infty}(D)
, satisfies two-sided (coercivity and upper) bounds

\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leq a(x)\leq\Lambda<\infty,\quad\forall\,x\in D,

(20)

for constant
\lambda,\Lambda
,
and if
f\in H^{-1}(D)
belongs to the dual space of
H^{1}_{0}(D)
, then there exists a unique solution
w\in H^{1}_{0}(D)
to (
19
). Furthermore, there exists a constant
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda,D)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.

(21)

It is thus natural to consider the output space
\mathcal{Y}=H^{1}_{0}(D)
. We will follow the celebrated work by Cohen, Devore and Schwab
(
Cohen et al. 2010
)
, and subsequent extensions in
(
Cohen et al. 2011
;
Schwab and Zech 2019
;
Opschoor et al. 2022
;
Opschoor et al. 2020
)
, and consider the following setting: We assume that the underlying measure
\mu
can be written as the law of random coefficient fields
a(x)=a(x;\bm{z})
, of the parametrized form

\displaystyle a(x;\bm{z})=\overline{a}(x)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}z_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}(x),

(22)

where
\bm{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots)
is a sequence of random variables (not necessarily independent), such that
|z_{\ell}|\leq 1
. In our analysis, we will assume that the functions
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{X}
are orthonormal in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathcal{X}{\hookrightarrow}L^{\infty}
possesses a continuous embedding. Note that the series (
22
) converges, if

\displaystyle 0\leq\gamma_{\ell}\leq M\ell^{-1-\alpha},

(23)

decays at an algebraic rate with constants
M,\alpha>0
. We will assume this bound (
23
). To ensure coercivity (cp. Remark
16
below) we assume that there exists
\kappa>0
, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\|\rho_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}\overline{a}_{\mathrm{min}},

(24)

with
\overline{a}_{\mathrm{min}}:=\essinf_{x\in D}\overline{a}(x)>0
.

Remark 16
.

The upper bound (
24
) ensures uniform coercivity, since

a(x;\bm{z})\geq\overline{a}_{\mathrm{min}}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\|\rho_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq\frac{1}{1+\kappa}\overline{a}_{\mathrm{min}}=:\lambda>0,

for any
\bm{z}\in U:=[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
. On the other hand, the assumed embedding
\mathcal{X}{\hookrightarrow}L^{\infty}(D)
implies that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{\mathcal{X}}
, and hence (
23
) ensures a uniform upper bound,

\displaystyle a(x;\bm{z})

\displaystyle\leq\|\overline{a}\|_{L^{\infty}}+CM\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-1-\alpha}=:\Lambda<\infty.

In particular, all
a
in the support of the probability measure
\mu
satisfy the two-sided bounds (
20
), and hence the elliptic PDE (
19
) is well-posed.

Remark 17
.

We do not assume any
(explicit) knowledge
of the functions
\overline{a}
,
\rho_{\ell}
, the parameters
\gamma_{\ell}
,
\alpha
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
or indeed any information on the law of the joint random variable
\bm{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\dots)\in[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, the random variables
z_{1},z_{2},\dots
need not be independent. For the following arguments, it is sufficient that an expansion of the form (
22
) exists.

Given this setting, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 18
.

Under the setting and the prevailing assumptions of this section and with
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
the law of
a({\,\cdot\,};\bm{z})
given by (
22
). For any
\delta,\eta>0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
satisfying the error bound,

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(a)-\Psi(a)\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}\right]\leq C\epsilon,

with probability at least
1-\delta
, and with constant
C=C(\mu,\eta)>0
depending only on
\mu
and
\eta
. With the same implied constant, the required PCA dimensions are at most
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}=d\sim\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\eta}
and the required number of samples for PCA is at most
N\sim d^{1+4\alpha}\log(1/\delta)
. Furthermore, the following complexity bounds hold for the ReLU network
\psi
,

\mathrm{size}(\psi)\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}-\eta},\quad 0pt(\psi)\leq C\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2},

independently of the data
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\sim\mu
.

We provide a sketch of the proof of Theorem
18
at the end of this subsection.

Remark 19
.

Regarding the fudge factor
\eta>0
, we note that in our estimates leading to Theorem
18
, the constant
C=C(\mu,\eta)
blows up as
\eta\to 0
, i.e.
C(\mu,\eta)\to\infty
. Therefore, while
\eta>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small, our estimates break down when
\eta=0
.

Remark 20
.

Theorem
18
shows that approximation of the Darcy flow operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
is possible with algebraic bounds on the PCA dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, the number of required PCA samples
N
, and the number of neural network parameters,
\mathrm{size}(\psi)
. In fact, even under a mild decay rate
\alpha>1
, the required size of
\psi
is at most
linear
in the desired accuracy.

We conjecture that the scaling of
N\sim d^{1+4\alpha}
in Theorem
18
is highly pessimistic; indeed, under a potential improvement of the empirical PCA estimate of Proposition
2
, as discussed in Remark
3
, the much more favorable scaling
N\sim d
appears natural.

Sketch of proof of Theorem
18
.

To prove Theorem
18
, we first define a parametric mapping
\mathcal{F}:U\to\mathcal{Y}
, where
U=[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathcal{Y}=H^{1}_{0}(D)
, by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\bm{z}):=\Psi^{\dagger}\left(\overline{a}+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}z_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}\right).

(25)

This mapping has been studied in a series of papers
(
Cohen et al. 2010
;
Cohen et al. 2011
;
Schwab and Zech 2019
;
Opschoor et al. 2022
;
Opschoor et al. 2020
)
, and is known to allow for a convergent Taylor series expansion (in the variables
\bm{z}\in U
). To state the next Lemma, which implies a suitable convergence of Taylor series, we recall that
\bm{\nu}
is called a multi-index in this infinite-dimensional context, if
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots)
is a sequence of non-negative integers, such that
\nu_{\ell}=0
for almost all
\ell
. For
\bm{z}\in U
and a multi-index
\bm{\nu}
, we define the monomial
\bm{z}^{\bm{\nu}}=\prod_{\nu_{\ell}\neq 0}z_{\ell}^{\nu_{\ell}}
. The following lemma follows immediately from
(
Cohen et al. 2011
, Thm. 1.3)
.

Lemma 21
.

Assume the setting and prevailing assumptions of this section. Then there exists a set of coefficients
t_{\bm{\nu}}\in\mathcal{Y}
(the Taylor coefficients), indexed by multi-indices
\bm{\nu}
, such that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a set
\Lambda_{m}
of multi-indices
{\bm{\nu}}
, with cardinality
|\Lambda_{m}|=m
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{z}\in U}\|\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})-\sum_{\bm{\nu}\in\Lambda_{m}}t_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{z}^{\bm{\nu}}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq Cm^{-\alpha+\eta},

(26)

for any fixded constant
\eta>0
. Here
C=C(\mathcal{F},\mu,\eta)>0
is a constant depending on
\eta
, but is independent of
m
.

For completeness, we provide the details in Appendix
E.1
.
As a consequence of this lemma, we can estimate the optimal PCA encoding errors not only on
\mathcal{X}
but also on
\mathcal{Y}
. Indeed, we will derive the following result.

Proposition 22
.

Under the setting and prevailing assumptions of this section. For any
\eta>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{F},\mu,\eta)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mu)\leq C{d_{\mathcal{X}}}^{-2\alpha-1},\quad\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)\leq C{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{-2\alpha+\eta}.

The
\mathcal{X}
estimate of Proposition
22
is a straightforward consequence of the assumed expansion (
22
). The
\mathcal{Y}
estimate follows from the observation that (
26
) provides a bound of the PCA projection error by comparing it to the projection onto
\mathrm{span}{\left\{t_{\bm{\nu}}\,\middle|\,{\bm{\nu}}\in\Lambda_{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\right\}}\subset\mathcal{Y}
. Details of the required argument are given in Appendix
E.2
. Since the input measure
\mu
and its push-forward
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
are concentrated on a bounded set of functions in the respective norms on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, Proposition
2
immediately implies that empirical PCA with
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}=d
and with a sufficient number of
N\gtrsim d^{1+4\alpha}\log(\delta/2)
samples achieves, up to a constant and with high probability, the same asymptotic error as the optimal PCA projection on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
.

The main remaining challenge is then to construct a neural network
\psi
, such that the composition
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
approximates
\Psi^{\dagger}
to within a prescribed tolerance. The construction of such
\psi
relies on a neural network approximation result for the parametric mapping
\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})
. The following result follows from
(
Opschoor et al. 2020
, Theorem 4.11)
(the present statement is closer in formulation to
(
Schwab and Zech 2019
, Theorem 3.9)
, and has appeared with slightly sharper bounds in
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
)
):

Lemma 23
.

Under the prevailing assumption and with
\mathcal{F}
defined by (
25
). Let
\eta>0
be a small (fudge) constant. There exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{F},\mu,\eta)>0
, depending only on
\mathcal{F}
, on the decay rate
\alpha
and on
\eta
, such that for any PCA encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, and for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a ReLU network
\psi^{\star}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
,
z\mapsto\psi^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
with

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{z}\in[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})-\psi^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq Cm^{-\alpha+\eta},

(27)

and such that

\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\star})\leq Cm\left(\log(m)^{2}+d_{\mathcal{Y}}\right)
and
0pt(\psi^{\star})\leq C\log(m)^{2}
.

The main additional problem in the present context of PCA-Net is that the neural network
\psi
in the definition of a PCA-Net,
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, acts on the
encoded input

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a)
, with encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
obtained from empirical PCA. Therefore, we cannot directly access the coefficients
z_{1},z_{2},\dots
in the parametric expansion (
22
) of
a
, and indeed there is no way to exactly recover these coefficients from the PCA encoding of
a
. A careful discussion of this “compatibility issue” between the PCA encoding and the a priori expansion (
22
) is therefore necessary. This is the main issue addressed in Appendix
E.3
, where we show that such
\psi
can indeed be constructed and the additional error due to the incompatibility can be controlled. This then leads to the statement of Theorem
18
.

3.4.2
Navier-Stokes equations

In the previous section, we showed that the solution operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
of the Darcy flow equations can be efficiently approximated by PCA-Net. With some additional effort, this result could likely be extended to a more general class of so-called
(\bm{b},\epsilon)
-holomorphic operators, e.g.
(
Schwab and Zech 2019
, Section 2.1)
. In particular, the relevant underlying class of operators is here characterized by
analytic regularity
; this assumption goes well beyond
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-regularity, and thereby, PCA-Net can overcome the general curse of dimensionality of Theorem
9
, for this restricted class of operators.

However, many operators of interest, in particular in the context of advection dominated problems such as hyperbolic PDEs, are not holomorphic in this sense. In the present section, we therefore discuss another mechanism by which polynomial complexity estimates can be obtained, even in the absence of holomorphy: namely, PCA-Net can efficiently emulate numerical methods. Following ideas developed in
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
;
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
, and starting from a known (and convergent) numerical method, such an emulation result provides an upper bound on the required complexity of
\Psi
, by showing that a specific choice of the neural network weights can emulate the given numerical method. The derivation of explicit estimates requires us to fix a particular numerical method for the analysis, but the intuition behind these emulation results is that
\Psi
can, in principle, efficiently emulate a very rich class of numerical methods. This includes methods with high convergence rates, which the neural network can explore during optimization; this expressive power of neural networks can thus provide a theoretical rationale for their efficiency within the PCA-Net methodology.

In the following, we will focus on an emulation result for the Navier-Stokes equations, based on spectral methods. The underlying idea is similar to a recent emulation result for Fourier neural operators
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
; however, while Fourier neural operators very naturally (and by design) provide the necessary ingredients to build a spectral method, the PCA-Net methodology requires an extension of the results of
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
, including a detailed discussion of compatibility of the PCA-projection with such emulation results. In addition, at a more technical level, it is here shown that the smoothness assumption on the activation function, which was
essential
in all proofs of
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
, can be substantially relaxed. Indeed, the results of the present work are based on the popular ReLU activation function
\sigma(x)=\max(x,0)
, leading to comparable complexity estimates as in
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
)
differing only by
\log
-factors.

To illustrate the general approach, we consider the periodic Navier-Stokes equations in spatial dimension
n=2
, over a fixed time interval
[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\\
{\mathrm{div}}(u)=0,\,u(t=0)=\overline{u}.\end{gathered}\right.

Here
u:\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the flow vector field,
p:\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is the scalar pressure and
\nu\geq 0
is the viscosity (we allow
\nu=0
, corresponding to the incompressible Euler equations). We have denoted by
\overline{u}:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
the (divergence-free) initial data. Since the solution operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
associated with (
3.4.2
) is only known to be well-defined in two spatial dimensions, we focus on this case. We however point out that all results readily extend to the three-dimensional case, under additional (unproven) smoothness assumptions.

Remark 24
.

Classical well-posedness results for the Navier-Stokes (
\nu>0
) and Euler (
\nu=0
) equations, e.g.
(
Majda and Bertozzi 2001
)
and references therein, imply in the two-dimensional case
n=2
, that if the random initial data
\overline{u}\sim\mu
, is uniformly bounded
\|\overline{u}\|_{H^{r}}\leq\overline{M}
for
r>n/2+1
, then the corresponding solution
u(t)
at a fixed later time
t\in[0,T]
satisfies a similar bound

\|u(t)\|_{H^{r}}\leq M
,
for some
M=M(T,\overline{M})
.

The main result of the present section is Theorem
25
, below:

Theorem 25
.

Consider the two-dimensional, periodic Navier-Stokes equations. Fix parameters
M,T>0
, and integer
r>n/2+1
. Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is a probability measure on initial data
\overline{u}
of (
3.4.2
), such that
\|\overline{u}\|_{H^{r}}\leq M

\mu
-almost surely. Let
\Psi^{\dagger}
be the forward solution operator of the Navier-Stokes equations, mapping initial data to the solution at the final time
T
,
\Psi^{\dagger}:u(0)\mapsto u(T)
.
For any
\epsilon,\delta>0
, there exists a PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right]\leq C\epsilon,

with probability at least
1-\delta
, and with a constant
C=C(M,r,T)>0
. The PCA dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
are bounded by
{d_{\mathcal{X}}},{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/r}
, the requisite amount of data
N\leq C{d_{\mathcal{X}}}^{1+2r}\log(1/\delta)
and the neural network
\psi

satisfies the complexity bounds

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{size}({\psi})&\leq C\epsilon^{-1/r}\left(\epsilon^{-1/2}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2}+\epsilon^{-1/r}\right),\\
0pt({\psi})&\leq C\epsilon^{-1/2}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

A sketch of the proof of Theorem
25
is provided below.

Remark 26
.

In fact,
\psi
in Theorem
25
can be written as an
n_{T}
-fold composition

{\psi}=Q\circ\underbrace{{\psi}_{\ast}\circ\dots\circ{\psi}_{\ast}}_{n_{T}\text{-fold}}\circ R,

where the mappings
R:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{H}}
,
Q:\mathbb{R}^{d_{H}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
are linear (input and output layers),
n_{T}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/2}
, and
{\psi}_{\ast}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{H}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{H}}
is a ReLU neural network with “hidden layer” dimension
d_{H}\leq C\epsilon^{-1/r}
, such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{size}({\psi}_{\ast})&\leq C\epsilon^{-1/r}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2},\\
0pt({\psi}_{\ast})&\leq C\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

Sketch of proof of Theorem
25
.

The derivation of the quantitative error and complexity bounds of Theorem
25
is based on an
emulation result
; the idea is to show that for any choice of PCA bases, there exists a ReLU neural network
\psi
which can efficiently emulate a numerical method which is known to converge at a precisely quantifiable rate. The complete details of the required argument, including all proofs, will be provided in Appendix
F
. Here, we instead give a general overview of the main ideas, to aid intuition.

As a first step towards this emulation result, we review a convergent spectral scheme in section
F.1
. Then, we construct a ReLU neural network emulation of this spectral scheme in section
F.2
, leading to Algorithm
1
and the neural network size estimates of Lemma
39
. The constructed neural network emulator defines a mapping from the truncated Fourier coefficients of the initial data
u(0)
, to (an approximation of) the truncated Fourier coefficients of the solution
u(T)
. The relevant set of truncated Fourier modes with cut-off parameter
K\in\mathbb{N}
is given by

\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{K}:={\left\{k=(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,\middle|\,|k|_{\infty}:=\max(|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|)\leq K\right\}}.

The mapping on these truncated Fourier coefficients defines a mapping between two-finite dimensional Euclidean spaces, upon identifying
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2\mathcal{K}}
. This mapping can be represented by an ordinary neural network. Given this construction, we then proceed to analyze the approximation error of the neural network emulation in section
F.3
, leading to the following proposition. This is our core emulation result, essentially stating the fact that ReLU neural networks can indeed efficiently emulate the underlying spectral scheme.

Proposition 27
.

Let
M,\,r,\,T>0
be given. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
K\sim\epsilon^{-1/r}
, and a ReLU neural network
\widehat{\psi}:\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{K}
, such that

\|\widehat{\psi}(\widehat{u}(0))-\widehat{u}(T)\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq\epsilon,\qquad\text{whenever }\,\|u(0)\|_{H^{r}}\leq M,

where
\widehat{u}(t)=\{\widehat{u}_{k}(t)\}_{|k|_{\infty}\leq K}
denote the Fourier coefficients of the solution
u(t)
of (
3.4.2
), with initial data
u(0)
. Furthermore, we have the following complexity estimates:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{size}(\widehat{\psi})&\leq C\epsilon^{-2/r-1}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2},\\
0pt(\widehat{\psi})&\leq C\epsilon^{-1}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

The constant
C=C(M,T,r)>0
depends only
M,T,r
, but is independent of
\epsilon
. Furthermore,
\widehat{\psi}
can be written as a
n_{T}
-fold composition
\widehat{\psi}=\widehat{\psi}_{\ast}\circ\dots\circ\widehat{\psi}_{\ast}
, where
\widehat{\psi}_{\ast}:\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}
is a ReLU neural network with

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{size}(\widehat{\psi}_{\ast})&\leq C\epsilon^{-2/r}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2},\\
0pt(\widehat{\psi}_{\ast})&\leq C\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2},\end{aligned}\right.

and
n_{T}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}
, corresponding to the number of time-steps of the underlying scheme.

In fact, Proposition
27
is the two-dimensional case of a general
d
-dimensional result derived in the appendix (cf. Proposition
43
). Given this neural network emulation result, the remaining issue is that the empirical PCA encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
and decoder
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}
do
not
act on Fourier coefficients. And hence, additional work is necessary to suitably adapt the construction of the neural network in Proposition
27
, ultimately resulting in an efficient PCA-Net approximation
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
in Section
F.4
. We summarize the result in the following lemma:

Lemma 28
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi(u)-\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon+C\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu}\left[\|v-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

where
\psi
is a neural network of size

\mathrm{size}(\psi)\leq C\epsilon^{-2/r}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\log(\epsilon^{-1})^{2}+(d_{\mathcal{X}}+d_{\mathcal{Y}})\right),\quad 0pt(\psi)\leq C\epsilon^{-1}\log(\epsilon^{-1}),

and
C=C(M,r,T)>0
is a constant independent of
\epsilon,d_{\mathcal{X}},d_{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Finally, the smoothness bound on the PCA eigenvalues of Proposition
15
can be used to estimate the PCA projection errors with high probability, see Lemma
45
for details. Combining the above lemma with the estimates on the PCA-projection errors, results in Theorem
25
.

4
Conclusion

PCA-Net is a data-driven operator learning methodology introduced in
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
;
Hesthaven and Ubbiali 2018
)
, which (i) uses PCA to reduce the dimensions of the input and output spaces and (ii) uses neural networks to approximate a map between the resulting finite-dimensional latent spaces.
The main aim of the present work is to develop relevant approximation theory for PCA-Net.

Our first main result is a novel universal approximation theorem for PCA-Net, Theorem
6
. Compared to previous work
(
Bhattacharya et al. 2021
)
, this theorem establishes universality under significantly relaxed conditions on the distribution of the data-generating measure and the underlying operator
\Psi^{\dagger}
. The present assumptions are in fact minimal conditions to ensure that PCA is well-defined on the input and output spaces.

The next main contribution of the present work is a detailed discussion of two potential obstacles to efficient operator learning with PCA-Net in Section
3.2
; the first obstacle relates to the complexity of the output distribution. The second obstacle relates to the inherent complexity of the space of operators between infinite-dimensional input and output spaces, and gives rigorous meaning to the notion of a curse of dimensionality; Theorem
9
shows that is impossible to derive algebraic complexity bounds, when considering general classes of operators, such as the class of all Lipschitz- or even
\mathcal{C}^{k}
-continuous operators. Hence, we conclude that at this level of generality, the curse of dimensionality is inevitable.

Given this negative result demonstrating the curse of dimensionality over general classes of operators, we posit that a central challenge in the approximation theory of PCA-Net (and other operator learning methodologies) is to identify and characterize operators, and classes of such operators, which allow for efficient approximation by the methodology. To obtain first insight into this problem for PCA-Net, we focus our attention on two prototypical PDE operators of interest, arising from the Darcy flow and Navier-Stokes equations. In both cases, we show that PCA-Net can overcome the general curse of dimensionality, establishing algebraic error and complexity estimates in Theorems
18
and
25
. This demonstrates that these operators belong to a restricted class which is efficiently approximated by PCA-Net. In the case of Darcy flow, our proof relies on the analytic regularity (holomorphy) of the underlying operator. For the Navier-Stokes equations, we rely on an emulation result, showing that PCA-Net can emulate a known spectral scheme to efficiently approximate the underlying operator.

It is an open challenge for future work to improve our understanding of the relevant class of operators for which operator learning is feasible, and to derive a useful mathematical characterization of relevant features that enable efficient approximation by PCA-Net and other operator learning architectures. Future work could also aim to derive more precise lower bounds which characterize the curse of dimensionality. In this context, we mention the forthcoming article
(
Lanthaler and Stuart 2023
)
, where similar ideas are refined and considerably generalized, and
exponential
lower bounds are derived for a more general class of neural operators, albeit with respect to the supremum norm; those results do not translate to any lower bounds in the
L^{2}_{\mu}
-norm considered here. In a different research direction, we point out that the present work has focused only on an approximation theoretic point of view, leaving out important questions related to optimization and generalization errors, given a finite amount of data. A significant challenge for future work is to address the practical training of the underlying neural network, and in particular, to determine bounds on the amount of training data that is necessary to achieve a desired accuracy.

Finally, it could be of interest to link the results of the present work with recent work on overcoming the curse of dimensionality in the solution of high-dimensional PDEs, e.g.
(
Chow et al. 2017
;
Chow et al. 2019
;
Darbon and Osher 2016
;
Darbon et al. 2020
;
Marwah et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2021
;
Marwah et al. 2023
)
. In the context of these high-dimensional PDEs, the focus has usually been on approximating an individual solution rather than learning the underlying solution operator. It would be of interest to further explore the possibility of operator learning for such high-dimensional PDEs, with the aim of deriving complexity estimates that exhibit good scaling with respect to the infinite-dimensional input and output function spaces, and in addition, scale favorably with respect to the spatial dimension
n\gg 1
of the underlying spatial domain on which the PDE is defined. We leave these general research directions as interesting avenues for future work.

Appendix A
PCA projection error bound

Let
\widehat{P}_{\leq d}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the empirical PCA projection, given samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
. We recall that the excess risk is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\widehat{P}_{\leq d}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]-\min_{P\in\Pi_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-Pu\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right].

(30)

The following general bound on the excess risk is well-known

(
Reiß and Wahl 2020
, Sect. 2.2)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\leq\sqrt{2d}\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS},

(31)

where
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{HS}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, and where
\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes u_{k}
the empirical covariance operator, and
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Sigma}]
is its expectation. We recall that the set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
HS(\mathcal{H})
is itself a Hilbert space with norm
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{HS}
, and that
\|A\|_{HS}^{2}={\mathrm{tr}}(A^{T}A)
can be expressed in terms of the trace.

A.1
Proof of Proposition
1

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}
be the excess risk (
30
).
Note that the claimed bound (
6
) in Proposition
1
can be written in terms of the excess risk, as

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\leq\epsilon
.
Rather than proving the high-probability result of Proposition
1
directly, we will deduce it from the following expectation result:

Lemma 29
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
be a probability measure with finite second moments,
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]<\infty
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
and integer
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a requisite amount of data
N=N(\mu,d,\epsilon)
, such that the encoding error for empirical PCA with dimension
d
, and based on
N
samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
, satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\left[\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\right]\leq\sqrt{2d}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}\right]\leq\epsilon.

The corresponding high-probability result, claimed in Proposition
1
, then follows readily from Lemma
29
, as shown next.

Proof of Proposition
1
from Lemma
29
.

Let
\epsilon,\delta>0
be given. To deduce the claimed high-probability bound of Proposition
1
, we apply Lemma
29
with
\epsilon
replaced by
\epsilon^{\prime}:=\epsilon\delta
, to deduce that there exists a required amount of data
N=N(\epsilon,\delta)
, such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\right]\leq\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon\delta.

Using the non-negativity of the random variable
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}
together with Markov’s inequality, this now immediately implies,

\mathrm{Prob}\left[\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}>\epsilon\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\right]}{\epsilon}\leq\delta,

i.e. with probability at least
1-\delta
, we have
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\leq\epsilon
.
∎

We finally come to the proof of the result in expectation, Lemma
29
.

Proof of Lemma
29
.

We define
Z:=u\otimes u
, with
u\sim\mu
, as an
(HS(\mathcal{H}),\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{HS})
-valued random variable; note that
Z
takes values in the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
HS(\mathcal{H})
. Note that
\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}
is the sum of i.i.d. copies of
Z
. Given a cut-off
M>0
, we define random variables
Z_{k,\leq M}:=(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})1_{[\|u_{k}\|\leq M]}
,
Z_{k,>M}:=(u_{k}\otimes u_{k})1_{[\|u_{k}\|>M]}
. Then, by the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k,\leq M}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,\leq M}]\right\|_{HS}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|Z_{k,>M}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,>M}]\|_{HS}

We next observe that, upon taking expectations, the second contribution can be estimated by

\mathbb{E}\left[\|Z_{k,>M}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,>M}]\|_{HS}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\|Z_{k,>M}\|_{HS}\right]=2\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u\|^{2}1_{[\|u\|>M]}\right].

On the other hand, since
\|Z_{k,\leq M}\|_{HS}=\|u_{k}\|^{2}1_{\|u\|\leq M}\leq M^{2}
is uniformly bounded, we can apply Jensen’s inequality and the standard Monte-Carlo estimate on the expectation of the first term to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k,\leq M}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,\leq M}]\right\|_{HS}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k,\leq M}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{k,\leq M}]\right\|_{HS}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}}{\sqrt{N}}.

Combining these estimates, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}\right]\leq\frac{M^{2}}{\sqrt{N}}+2\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u\|^{2}1_{[\|u\|>M]}\right].

(32)

By assumption, the second moment
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u\|^{2}]
is finite. Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
M=M(\epsilon,d,\mu)>0
, such that

2\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u\|^{2}1_{[\|u\|>M]}\right]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2\sqrt{2d}}.

Choosing next
N=N(\epsilon,d,\mu)
sufficiently large, we ensure that
M^{2}/\sqrt{N}\leq\epsilon/2\sqrt{2d}
. With this choice of
N
, it follows from (
32
), that

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}\right]\leq\epsilon/\sqrt{2d},

and hence

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\left[\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\right]\leq\sqrt{2d}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}\right]\leq\epsilon.

∎

A.2
Proof of Proposition
2

We first recall a vector-valued Bernstein inequality that is applicable on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
(
Caponnetto and De Vito 2007
;
Maurer and Pontil 2021
;
Rudi and Rosasco 2017
)
. The following result is an immediate consequence of
(
Pinelis and Sakhanenko 1986
, Cor. 1, p. 144)
:

Theorem 30

(Vector-valued Bernstein inequality)
.

Let
Z
be an
H
-valued random variable, where
H
is a separable Hilbert space. Suppose there exist positive numbers
b>0
and
\sigma>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\|{Z-\mathbb{E}Z}\|_{H}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}p!\sigma^{2}b^{p-2}\quad\text{for all}\quad p\geq 2.

(33)

Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, denoting by
\{Z_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
a sequence of
N
iid copies of
Z
, it holds that

\mathrm{Prob}\left\{\left\|{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}-\mathbb{E}Z}\right\|_{H}\leq\frac{2b\log(2/\delta)}{N}+\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}\log(2/\delta)}{N}}\right\}\geq 1-\delta.

(34)

A random variable
Z
satisfying the
Bernstein moment condition
(
33
) is subexponential in the sense that
\|{Z-\mathbb{E}Z}\|_{H}
is subexponential on
\mathbb{R}
. The parameters
\sigma^{2}
and
b
represent the variance and variance proxy/subexponential norm, respectively.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Let
\widehat{P}_{\leq d}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the empirical PCA projection, given samples
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
. We recall that the excess risk is defined by

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\widehat{P}_{\leq d}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]-\min_{P\in\Pi_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-Pu\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right].

Note that the claimed bound (
8
) in Proposition
2
can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\leq\sqrt{\frac{Qd\log(2/\delta)}{N}}

(35)

We again rely on the following general bound on the excess risk (cp. eq.
31
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{PCA}}_{\leq d}\leq\sqrt{2d}\|\Sigma-\widehat{\Sigma}\|_{HS}.

(36)

with
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{HS}
the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, and where
\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}\otimes u_{k}
the empirical covariance operator, and we have
\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\Sigma}]
. We recall that the set of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
HS(\mathcal{H})
is itself a Hilbert space with norm
\|{\,\cdot\,}\|_{HS}
. Let
Z:=u\otimes u
,
u\sim\mu
, be an
HS(\mathcal{H})
-valued random variable. Then
\widehat{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}
is the sum of i.i.d. copies of
Z

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}
is sub-Gaussian by assumption, it follows that
\|Z\|_{2}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
is sub-exponential, and as a consequence,
\|Z-\mathbb{E}Z\|_{2}

30
applied on the Hilbert space

, depending only on the law
\mu
of

Z
), such that

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}Z_{k}-\mathbb{E}[Z]\right\|_{2}\leq q\max\left\{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{N},\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{N}}\right\},

with probability at least
1-\delta
.
By assumption, we have

, and hence upon defining

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\Sigma}-\Sigma\|_{HS}\leq q\max\left\{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{N},\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{N}}\right\}\leq\sqrt{\frac{Q\log(2/\delta)}{2N}},

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Substitution in (

) now yields (
35
).
∎

Universal approximation

B.1
Proof of Theorem
6

The high-probability result of Theorem
6
can easily be derived from the following result in expectation:

Proposition 31

(Universal approximation in expectation)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be separable Hilbert spaces and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a
\mu
-measurable mapping. Assume the following moment conditions,

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}],\;\;\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}]<\infty.

Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there are dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{X}}}(\epsilon)
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\epsilon)
, a requisite amount of data
N=N({d_{\mathcal{X}}},{d_{\mathcal{Y}}},\mu,\Psi^{\dagger})
, and a neural network
\psi
such that the PCA-Net,
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{k}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})\|^{2}_{\mathcal{Y}}\right]\right]\leq\epsilon.

Here, the expectation is over the given data
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\sim\mu
, which determine the input-/output-pairs
\{u_{k},\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{k})\}_{k=1}^{N}
.

We first show that Proposition
31
implies Theorem
6
.

Proof of Theorem
6
from Proposition
31
.

Define
Z\in\mathbb{R}
by

Z:=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}.

Note that
Z
is itself a random variable due to its dependence on the random data
\{u_{k}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}
. Applying Proposition
31
with
\epsilon^{\prime}:=\epsilon\delta
instead of
\epsilon
, we conclude that there exist dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, a requisite amount of data
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and a neural network
\psi
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{k}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}[Z]\leq\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon\delta.

We can now estimate the probability of the event
\{Z>\epsilon\}
,

\mathrm{Prob}[Z>\epsilon]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{k}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\left[Z\right]}{\epsilon}\leq\delta.

In particular with this choice of
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
,
N
and
\psi
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq\epsilon,

with probability at least
1-\delta
.
∎

It thus remains to prove Proposition
31
, which is the main aim of this appendix. The detailed proof is the subject of the following section.

B.2
Proof of Proposition
31
(in expectation)

We are interested in the following PCA-Net approximation error,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

(37)

where we have suppressed the dependence of
\Psi(u)=\Psi(u;\{u_{k}\})
on the data, arising from the data-dependence of empirical PCA.
We also recall that a PCA-Net is written as a composition of three mappings
\Psi(a)=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
. Intuitively, we expect each of these mappings to give a contribution to the overall error. Our analysis of
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}
will thus be based on a decomposition of the approximation error
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}
in terms of three contributions: PCA encoding/decoding errors
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}
and
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}
on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, and a neural network error
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\psi}
. At times, it is more convenient not to consider the encoding and neural network error separately, we will denote the combined error (associated with the composition
\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
) by
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\psi}^{\ast}
.

Lemma 32

(Error decomposition)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a
\mu
-measurable, non-linear operator, with
\mathbb{E}_{u\in\mu}[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}]<\infty
. Let
\Psi(u):=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)
be a PCA-Net with PCA dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
. Then the approximation error satisfies the following estimates,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\psi}^{\ast}\leq\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\dagger})\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\psi}.

(38)

Here we have introduced the following encoding errors on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{X}}_{\leq{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

(39a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu}\left[\|v-\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{\leq{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}v\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

(39b)

with
\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{X}}_{\leq{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}u:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)
and
\widehat{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{\leq{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}u:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(u)
the empirical PCA projections,
and we introduce the following neural network errors,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

(40a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\alpha\sim(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}})_{\#}\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(\alpha)-\psi(\alpha)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

(40b)

The derivation of Lemma
32
is straight-forward, and has been provided in detail for the conceptually similar DeepONet architecture in
(
Lanthaler et al. 2022
, Theorem 3.3)
. We will not repeat the derivation here.

We next state a general approximation result for operators, which will be a key ingredient in our proof of the universal approximation result, Proposition
6
. This next Lemma
33
is formulated with respect to the supremum norm, which prepares the stage for a corresponding approximation result in the
L^{2}_{\mu}
-norm in Lemma
34
.

Lemma 33
.

Let
\Psi^{\dagger}:K\to\mathcal{Y}
be a continuous mapping, where
K\subset\mathcal{X}
is compact, and
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
are Banach spaces. Assume that
\sup_{u\in K}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
for some
M>0
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a Lipschitz continuous
\Psi^{\star}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, such that

\sup_{u\in K}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\epsilon,

and
\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
, for all
u\in\mathcal{X}
.

Our proof of Lemma
33
uses only classical tools of topology; we suspect that similar results may have been derived before. For completeness, we provide a detailed proof, below.

Proof.

To prove the above lemma, we first construct a suitable Lipschitz continuous partition of unity
\{\psi_{j}:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]\}
on
K
, which has a Lipschitz continuous extension to all of
\mathcal{X}
. Then, we show that an interpolation of
\Psi^{\dagger}
of the form
\Psi^{\star}(u):=\sum_{j}\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{j})\psi_{j}(u)
satisfies the claimed properties.

Since
K
is compact,
\Psi^{\dagger}
is in fact uniformly continuous. In particular, there exists
\delta>0
, such that for all
u,u^{\prime}\in K
, we have

\displaystyle\|u-u^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\delta\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\dagger}(u^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\epsilon.

(41)

Using the compactness of
K
, let
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be a finite collection of elements of
K
, such that the open balls of radius
\delta>0
form a cover of
K
, i.e.

K\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B_{\delta}(u_{j}).

Define a “hat” function
\rho_{\delta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, by

\rho_{\delta}(r):=\begin{cases}1-r/\delta,&\quad(0\leq r\leq\delta),\\
0,&\quad(r>\delta).\end{cases}

We note that
\Psi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

\displaystyle\Psi(u):=\mathrm{dist}(u,K)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rho_{\delta}(\|u-u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}),

(42)

is a Lipschitz continuous function, and that there exists
\delta_{0}>0
, such that
\Psi(u)\geq\delta_{0}
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}
. To see the latter, we note that since
K
is compact, there exists
0<\delta_{0}\leq\delta
, such that

K\subset B_{\delta_{0}}(K):={\left\{u\in\mathcal{X}\,\middle|\,\mathrm{dist}(u,K)<\delta_{0}\right\}}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B_{\delta}(u_{j}).

After possibly decreasing
\delta_{0}
, we may wlog assume that
\delta_{0}<1/2
and
\delta_{0}<\delta/2
. It then follows from (
42
) that, for any
u\notin B_{\delta_{0}}(K)
, we have

\Psi(u)\geq\mathrm{dist}(u,K)\geq\delta_{0}
. On the other hand, if
u\in B_{\delta_{0}}(K)
, then there exists
u_{j}
, such that
\|u-u_{j}\|<\delta_{0}\leq\delta/2
. And hence,

\Psi(u)\geq\rho_{\delta}(\|u-u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}})\geq 1-1/2=1/2\geq\delta_{0}.

Thus,
\Psi(u)\geq\delta_{0}
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}
. Next, define

\psi_{j}(u):=\frac{\rho_{\delta}(\|u-u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}})}{\Psi(u)},\quad(j=1,\dots,N).

Then
\psi_{j}
is globally Lipschitz continuous for all
j=1,\dots,N
,
0\leq\psi_{j}\leq 1
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)=1
for all
u\in K
, i.e. the
\psi_{j}
form a partition of unity on
K
. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\leq 1
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}\setminus K
. Given this construction, we now define

\Psi^{\star}(u):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{j}).

Since
\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{j})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\sup_{u\in K}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
for all
j=1,\dots,N
, and since
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\leq 1
, it follows that for any
u\in\mathcal{X}
:

\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{j})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)M\leq M.

This shows the upper bound
\sup_{u\in\mathcal{X}}\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
. We finally claim that
\sup_{u\in K}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\epsilon
. To prove this, we note that for
u\in K
:

\displaystyle\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\dagger}(u_{j})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}(u)\epsilon=\epsilon,

where we have used the uniform continuity (
41
) of
\Psi^{\dagger}
in the second inequality and the fact that
\psi_{j}(u)\neq 0
, only if
\|u-u_{j}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\delta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Based on Lemma
33
, we can prove the following
L^{2}_{\mu}
-approximation result:

Lemma 34
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be separable Banach spaces, let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
. Assume that
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a Borel measurable mapping, such that
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]<\infty
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a bounded Lipschitz continuous mapping
\Psi^{\star}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, such that

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}<\epsilon,

and
\sup_{u\in\mathcal{X}}\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}<\infty

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Define
\Psi^{\dagger}_{M}
by

\Psi^{\dagger}_{M}(u):=\begin{cases}\Psi^{\dagger}(u),&(\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M),\\
M\frac{\Psi^{\dagger}(u)}{\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}},&(\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}>M).\end{cases}

For sufficiently large
M>0
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\dagger}_{M}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon/2
. It will thus suffice to show that there exists
\Psi^{\star}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
which is globally Lipschitz, satisfies
\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}
, and

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\star}(u)-\Psi^{\dagger}_{M}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon/2.

To simplify notation, we will drop the subscript
M
, and assume directly that
\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M
for all
u\in\mathcal{X}
. We now prove the existence
\Psi^{\star}

K\subset\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{X}\setminus K)\leq\left(\frac{\epsilon}{8M}\right)^{2},\qquad\Psi^{\dagger}|_{K}:K\to\mathcal{Y}\text{ is continuous}.

(43)

By Lemma
33
, there exists Lipschitz continuous
\Psi^{\star}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{X}}\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M

\sup_{u\in K}\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq\epsilon/4.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[1_{K}(u)\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[1_{\mathcal{X}\setminus K}(u)\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/4+2M\left[\mu\left(\mathcal{X}\setminus K\right)\right]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon/2.

The last inequality follows from the choice of
K
in (
43
). This proves the claim.
∎

After these preparatory results, we can now finally provide the detailed proof of Proposition
31
(i.e. universal approximation in expectation):

Proof of Proposition
31
.

By assumption,
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is Borel measurable and has finite second moment,
\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]<\infty
. Our goal is to show that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist PCA dimensions
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, a requisite amount of data
N
, depending only on
\epsilon
, and a neural network
\psi
, such that the PCA-Net approximation
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
of
\Psi^{\dagger}
, with empirical PCA encoder/decoder and based on
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}\limits^{\vbox to1.80835pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle iid$}\vss}}}\mu
i.i.d. samples, satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{k}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}-\Psi\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon.

To this end, let
\epsilon>0
be given. The proof will follow in six steps 1–6, each constructing and estimating a different part of the PCA-Net
\Psi
. We start from Lemma
32
, which gives the following general error decomposition:

\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast},

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu}\left[\|v-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2},

Step 1: (Bounding the decoding error on
\mathcal{Y}
)

The first error,
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}
, is the PCA encoding error on
\mathcal{Y}
. To control this error, we first choose
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\epsilon)
sufficiently large, such that the (optimal) PCA projection error of the push-forward measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
on
\mathcal{Y}
, is bounded by

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)\equiv\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu}\left[\|v-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\mathrm{opt}}\circ\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon/8.

Next, invoking Lemma
29
for the probability measure
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
and with
\epsilon
replaced by
\epsilon/8
, it follows that there exists a requisite amount of data
N=N(\Psi^{\dagger},\mu,\epsilon)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\mathbb{E}_{v\sim\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu}\left[\|v-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{8}+R_{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(44)

Step 2: (Lipschitz approximation
\Psi^{\star}\approx\Psi
)

Next, we aim to estimate the approximation error
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}
. To this end, we note that for
\Psi^{\star}\approx\Psi^{\dagger}
a Lipschitz continuous approximation of
\Psi^{\dagger}
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

where we used that the PCA encoding
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
is a contraction in the last step. Note that the last term is independent of the PCA encoding, and only depends on the approximation
\Psi^{\star}\approx\Psi^{\dagger}
. Using Lemma
34
, we can find a Lipschitz continuous bounded mapping
\Psi^{\star}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4},\quad\sup_{u\in\mathcal{X}}\|\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M<\infty,

(45)

for some constant
M>0
, depending only on
\epsilon
.

Step 3: (Decomposition in
\mathcal{X}
-encoding and neural network approximation errors)

Next, we continue to bound the first term in the estimate on
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}^{\ast}_{\mathcal{X}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}

\displaystyle\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}(u)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\star})\;\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\star})\;\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}},

where we denote

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)-\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\right)_{\#}\mu}\left[\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\star}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)-\psi(w)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\right]^{1/2}.

Step 4: (Bounding the encoding error on
\mathcal{X}
)

Again, employing the estimate from Lemma

similar to the argument leading to (

), we note that there exists a PCA dimension

and a requisite amount of data

, such that we can bound the encoding error

by

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4\,\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\star})}.

This ensures that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\star})\,\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}[\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}]\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(46)

Step 5: (Bounding the neural network approximation error)

Finally, we define the Lipschitz continuous function
G:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
by
G(w):=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\ast}\circ\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
, and denote
\nu:=\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\right)_{\#}\mu
. We note that (
45
) implies that

\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|G(w)|=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(\Psi^{\ast}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}(w)))|\leq\sup_{u\in\mathcal{X}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\|_{\mathcal{X}\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}})}\|\Psi^{\ast}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\leq M,

where we made use of the fact that the PCA-encoder
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is contractive, i.e.

|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)|\equiv\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}})}\leq\|v\|_{\mathcal{Y}},\quad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

For any
R>0
, we then obtain the following estimate on
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{M+\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}}|\psi(w)|\right\}\nu\left(\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\setminus[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{w\in[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|G(w)-\psi(w)|.

Since
\nu
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
, by choosing
R>0
sufficiently large, we can ensure that

3M\nu\left(\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\setminus[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\right)^{1/2}\leq\epsilon/8.

Next, by the universal approximation theorem of ReLU neural networks, there exists a (sufficiently large) DNN
\widetilde{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, such that

\sup_{w\in[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|G(w)-\widetilde{\psi}(w)|\leq\epsilon/8.

Finally, adding the two-layer ReLU network
\gamma_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}

\gamma_{R}(x):=\min(\max(x,-R),R)
ensures that

satisfies

\displaystyle\psi(w)=\widetilde{\psi}(w),\quad\forall\,w\in[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}},

Thus, with this construction of

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{M+\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}}|\psi(w)|\right\}\nu\left(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\setminus[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{w\in[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|G(w)-\psi(w)|

\displaystyle=3M\nu\left(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\setminus[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\right)^{1/2}+\sup_{w\in[-R,R]^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}|G(w)-\widetilde{\psi}(w)|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/8+\epsilon/8=\epsilon/4.

Thus, for this neural network
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(47)

Step 6: (Conclusion)

To conclude, we have shown that for any
\epsilon>0
, we can choose sufficiently large
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{X}}}(\mu,\Psi^{\dagger},\epsilon)
,
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}(\mu,\Psi^{\dagger},\epsilon)
, a sufficient requisite amount of data
N=N(\mu,\Psi^{\dagger},\epsilon)
, and we can construct a ReLU neural network
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, such that the resulting PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
, satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}-\Psi\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}+\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\star})\,\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\Psi^{\star}}+\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(u)-\Psi^{\star}(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

where the expectation of the terms on the right is bounded by (
44
),(
45
), (
46
) and (
47
).
Taking expectation over the data
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}
, we conclude that the constructed PCA-Net satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\{u_{j}\}\sim\mu^{\otimes N}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}-\Psi\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq\epsilon.

This concludes our proof of the universal approximation property of PCA-Nets.
∎

Appendix C
Curse of Dimensionality

Our goal in this appendix is to prove Theorem
9
. To this end, it suffices to prove the following

Claim 1
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be Hilbert spaces, with
\dim(\mathcal{X})=\infty
,
\dim(\mathcal{Y})\geq 1
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
be a non-degenerate Gaussian measure. For any
\gamma>0
, there exists a
k
-times Fréchet differentiable operator

and a constant
c_{\gamma}>0
, such that for any PCA-Net
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma}(u)-\Psi(u)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right]\geq c_{\gamma}\mathrm{size}(\psi)^{\gamma}.

(48)

To prove this claim, it is sufficient to prove that such
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
exists with the

that
\mathrm{Im}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma})

\mathcal{Y}
. In this case, we can clearly identify
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\gamma}
with a mapping
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
, and thus, our goal is to prove the proposition in the (most constrained) case, where the output space

Let

. We now use the fact that

u-\Pi_{V_{d}}u=\sum_{k>d}\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle\phi_{k},

and combine this with the following two-sided bounds,

\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}\phi_{k}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and the identity,

\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k>d}\gamma_{k}^{s}|c_{k}|^{2},

which are valid for any sequence
(c_{k})
for which the last expression is finite.
Using these bounds with
c_{k}=\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle
, we can estimate (with
C
changing from line to line):

\displaystyle\|u-\Pi_{V_{d}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}\phi_{k}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left\|\sum_{k>d}c_{k}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\phi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{k>d}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}^{s}|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}=C\sum_{k>d}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}^{s+\zeta}}{\gamma_{k}^{\zeta}}|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\gamma_{d}^{\zeta}}\sum_{k>d}\gamma_{k}^{s+\zeta}|\langle u,\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{d^{2\zeta/n}}\|u\|_{H^{s+\zeta}}^{2},

and where the constant

only depends on

s
and
\zeta

.
In particular, this implies that

\displaystyle R_{d}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\min_{V_{d}}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\Pi_{V_{d}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right]\leq Cd^{-2\zeta/n}\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u\|_{H^{s+\zeta}}^{2}\right].

, and such that

. Here,

can be thought of as an extension operator, which extends a function

. And

can be though of as a restriction operator, restricting

d

(which is defined on periodic functions) satisfies

\mathrm{supp}(L_{\#}\mu)\subset\mathrm{Im}(L)
is contained in the image of

, it follows that

. Since

We thus conclude from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{u\sim\mu}\left[\|u-\Pi_{V_{d}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{u\sim L^{+}_{\#}L_{\#}\mu}\left[\|u-\Pi_{V_{d}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right]

can be estimated trivially by

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}\leq\sup_{\bm{z}\in U}\left\|\psi\left(\langle a(\bm{z}),\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\rangle\right)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}\left(a(\bm{z})\right)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Let us define a mapping
\mathcal{F}:U\to\mathcal{Y}
, where
U=[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
, by

\mathcal{F}(\bm{z}):=\Psi^{\dagger}\left(\overline{a}+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}z_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}\right).

We note that by Lemma
23
, for any
\eta>0
, and any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a neural network
\psi^{\star}:[-1,1]^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{z}\in U}\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})-\psi^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}})}\leq Cm^{-\alpha+\eta/2},

(61)

and with a constant
C=C(\mathcal{F},\alpha,s)>0
(we choose
\eta/2
rather than
\eta
for later convenience). Furthermore, we have the following complexity estimate:

\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\star})\leq Cm(\log(m)^{2}+{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}),\quad 0pt(\psi^{\star})\leq C\log(m)^{2}.

Our next goal will be to find a map
\psi^{\star\star}:\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\to[-1,1]^{m}
, such that for any
a=a(\bm{z})
, the composition

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}},\quad\psi(w):=\psi^{\star}\circ\psi^{\star\star}(w),

provides a good approximation

\displaystyle\psi(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a(\bm{z})))

\displaystyle=\psi(\langle a(\bm{z}),\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle a(\bm{z}),\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{m}\rangle)

\displaystyle\approx\psi^{\star}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\approx\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z}).

We note that by the orthonormality of the
\rho_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{X}
, we have

z_{\ell}=\gamma_{\ell}^{-1}\langle a-\overline{a},\rho_{\ell}\rangle,

so that in principle, the coefficients
z_{\ell}
could be readily obtained from knowledge of
a=a(\bm{z})
. The main difficulty here is that the neural network
\psi
only has access to the PCA projections
\langle a,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\rangle
, which allows us to reconstruct the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}a:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a)
onto the empirical PCA subspace in
\mathcal{X}
, but it doesn’t generally allow us to reconstruct
a
itself. Instead, we define

\widetilde{z}_{\ell}:=\gamma_{\ell}^{-1}\langle\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a}),\rho_{\ell}\rangle=\gamma_{\ell}^{-1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}\langle a,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\rangle\langle\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j},\rho_{\ell}\rangle\right\}-\gamma_{\ell}^{-1}\langle\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\overline{a},\rho_{\ell}\rangle.

We note that the map
\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, taking
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d_{\mathcal{X}}})
with
\alpha_{j}:=\langle a,\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\rangle
and mapping it to
(\widetilde{z}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{z}_{m})
, with
\widetilde{z}_{\ell}=\widetilde{z}_{\ell}(\alpha)
as above, defines an affine mapping.
Based on these
\widetilde{z}_{\ell}(\alpha)
, we now define
\psi^{\star\star}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to[-1,1]^{m}
,
\alpha\mapsto(\psi^{\star\star}_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\psi^{\star\star}_{m}(\alpha))
by

\psi^{\star\star}_{\ell}(\alpha):=\mathrm{shrink}\left(\widetilde{z}_{\ell}(\alpha)\right),\quad\text{where }\mathrm{shrink}(z):=\begin{cases}1&z>1,\\
z&z\in[-1,1],\\
-1&z<-1.\end{cases}

We point out that
\psi^{\star\star}
can be represented by a ReLU neural network, with

\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\star\star})\leq Cm{d_{\mathcal{X}}},\quad 0pt(\psi^{\star\star})\leq 2.

We now consider the composition
\psi(\alpha):=\psi^{\star}\circ\psi^{\star\star}(\alpha)
: Let
\bm{z}\in U
be given. For the following estimate, we denote
\widehat{z}_{\ell}:=\mathrm{shrink}(\widetilde{z}_{\ell}(\alpha))
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
having components
\alpha_{j}=\langle a(\bm{z}),\phi^{\mathcal{X}}_{j}\rangle
. We note that, by definition,
\psi^{\star\star}(\alpha)=(\widehat{z}_{1},\dots,\widehat{z}_{m})
. Let us denote
\widehat{\bm{z}}=(\widehat{z}_{1},\dots,\widehat{z}_{m},0,0,\dots)\in U
. By construction, we then have

\displaystyle\left\|\psi(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a(\bm{z})))-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\psi^{\star}(\widehat{z}_{1},\dots,\widehat{z}_{m})-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\psi^{\star}(\widehat{z}_{1},\dots,\widehat{z}_{m})-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\widehat{\bm{z}})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\widehat{\bm{z}})-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}

\displaystyle\leq Cm^{-\alpha+\eta/2}+\left\|\mathcal{F}(\widehat{\bm{z}})-\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}},

(62)

where we have made use of (
61
) in the last step, and the fact that the PCA encoding
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}
is a contractive mapping. We note that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{F}(\widehat{\bm{z}})-\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}&=\left\|\Psi^{\dagger}\Big(\overline{a}+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}\widehat{z}_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}\Big)-\Psi^{\dagger}\Big(\overline{a}+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}z_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}\Big)\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}}\\
&\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\dagger})\left\|\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}[\widehat{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C^{\ast}\mathrm{Lip}(\Psi^{\dagger})\left\|\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}[\widehat{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}.\end{aligned}

(63)

Where the last estimate follows from the assumed embedding
\mathcal{X}{\hookrightarrow}L^{\infty}
.
To estimate the last expression, we note that by the properties of the shrink operator, since
\widehat{z}_{\ell}=\mathrm{shrink}(\widetilde{z}_{\ell})
, and the fact that
z_{\ell}\in[-1,1]
, we have

|\widehat{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}|=|\mathrm{shrink}(\widetilde{z}_{\ell})-\mathrm{shrink}(z_{\ell})|\leq|\widetilde{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}|.

Furthermore, we note that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\gamma_{\ell}z_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}=P_{m}(a-\overline{a}),

with
P_{m}
the orthogonal projection onto
\mathrm{span}\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}\}
, and similarly, by definition of
\widetilde{z}_{\ell}
, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\gamma_{\ell}\widetilde{z}_{\ell}\rho_{\ell}=P_{m}\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a}).

Together with the orthonormality of the
\rho_{\ell}
, and the fact that
\widetilde{z}_{\ell}=0
for
\ell>m
, this then implies that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}[\widehat{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}^{2}|\widehat{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}|^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{\ell}^{2}|\widetilde{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}|^{2}\\
&=\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\gamma_{\ell}[\widetilde{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\sum_{\ell>m}\gamma_{\ell}^{2}z_{\ell}^{2}\\
&\leq\|\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a})-(a-\overline{a})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\sum_{\ell>m}\gamma_{\ell}^{2},\end{aligned}

(64)

where we made use of the fact that
P_{m}
is an orthogonal projection, and hence

\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\gamma_{\ell}[\widetilde{z}_{\ell}-z_{\ell}]\rho_{\ell}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}=\|P_{m}\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a})-P_{m}(a-\overline{a})\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\|\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a})-(a-\overline{a})\|_{\mathcal{X}},

to pass to the last line.

By assumption on
\gamma_{\ell}\leq C\ell^{-1-\alpha}
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell>m}\gamma_{\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\ell>m}\ell^{-2-2\alpha}\leq C\int_{m}^{\infty}\ell^{-2-2\alpha}d\ell=\frac{C}{1+2\alpha}m^{-1-2\alpha}.

We also note that the Lipschitz constant of
\Psi^{\dagger}:\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
depends only on
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and the domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, combining (
62
), (
63
) and (
64
) with the last estimate, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a)-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\Psi^{\dagger}(a)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}(a(\bm{z}))-\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\mathcal{F}(\bm{z})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(m^{-2\alpha+\eta}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a})-(a-\overline{a})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right),

for a constant
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda,d,\Psi^{\dagger},\alpha,\eta)>0
. In the last estimate, we have absorbed the additional term proportional to
m^{-2\alpha-1}
in the first (larger) term
m^{-2\alpha+\eta}
. Taking the expectation over
a\sim\mu
, and noting that
\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}\overline{a}-\overline{a}=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}[\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}a-a]
implies,

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}\left[\|\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}(a-\overline{a})-(a-\overline{a})\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}\left[\|\Pi_{\mathcal{X}}a-a\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right]=\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2},

we conclude that

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}{}^{2}\leq C\left(m^{-2\alpha+\eta}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\right),

where we are free to choose
\eta>0
, and
C=C(\lambda,d,\Psi^{\dagger},\alpha,\eta)>0
is independent of
m
,
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
. Combining the above estimates, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(a)-\Psi(a)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}]=\widehat{\mathscr{E}}^{2}\leq 2\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\ast}{}^{2}+2\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq C\left(m^{-2\alpha+\eta}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\right).

Next, we recall that
\mu
and
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
are concentrated on bounded sets; indeed, this is the case by assumption for
\mu
, and is a consequence of the a priori bound (
21
) in the case of
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
. Invoking Proposition
2
, we can therefore estimate

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}(\mu)+\sqrt{\frac{Q_{\mathcal{X}}{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\log(1/\delta)}{N}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}(\mu)+\sqrt{\frac{Q_{\mathcal{Y}}{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}\log(1/\delta)}{N}},

with probability at least
1-\delta
, and with constants
Q_{\mathcal{X}},Q_{\mathcal{Y}}
depending only on
\mu
and
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu
, respectively.
The optimal PCA projection errors have been bounded in Proposition
22
, showing that
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{{d_{\mathcal{X}}}}(\mu)\leq C{d_{\mathcal{X}}}^{-2\alpha-1}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{opt}}_{{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}}(\Psi^{\dagger}_{\#}\mu)\leq C{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}^{-2\alpha+\eta}
. Therefore, with the assumed choice of
{d_{\mathcal{X}}}={d_{\mathcal{Y}}}=d
and number of PCA samples
N\geq d^{1+4\alpha}\log(1/\delta)
, we can now estimate

\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}\leq Cd^{-2\alpha+\eta},

with probability at least
1-\delta
.

To summarize: Fix
\eta>0
. We have shown that there exists a constant
C=C(\lambda,\Lambda,d,\Psi^{\dagger},\alpha,\eta)>0
, such that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a neural network
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{X}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathcal{Y}}}
, satisfying the complexity estimate

\mathrm{size}(\psi)\leq Cm(\log(m)^{2}+d),\quad 0pt(\psi)\leq C\log(m)^{2},

and such that the (squared) PCA-Net error
\widehat{\mathscr{E}}^{2}
for
\Psi=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}\circ\psi\circ\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{X}}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\mu}[\|\Psi^{\dagger}(a)-\Psi(a)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}+\widehat{\mathscr{E}}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}+m^{-2\alpha+\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(d^{-2\alpha+\eta}+m^{-2\alpha+\eta}),

with probability at least
1-\delta
in the
N\sim d^{1+4\alpha}\log(1/\delta)
PCA samples.
Setting
m\sim d\sim\epsilon^{-1/(2\alpha-\eta)}=\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\eta^{\prime}}
, for some
\eta^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}(\eta)
, now yields the claimed error and complexity bounds.
∎

Appendix F
Proofs for Navier-Stokes

This appendix contains the background and proof of the PCA-Net emulation result for the Navier-Stokes equations, Theorem
25
. For simplicity, the Theorem in the main text was only stated for
n=2
. Here, we allow for arbitrary
n
under a suitable smoothness assumption on the underlying solutions. As indicated in the main text, the relevant smoothness assumption is only known to hold for spatial dimension

.

F.1
A convergent numerical scheme

For
K\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{P}_{K}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto divergence-free real-valued vector fields of the form,

u_{K}(x):=\sum_{|k|_{\infty}\leq K}\widehat{u}_{k}e^{i\langle k,x\rangle}.

We will also denote
L^{2}_{K}:=\mathbb{P}_{K}(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}))
as the image of
L^{2}
under
\mathbb{P}_{K}
.

We now consider the numerical discretization of (
3.4.2
), obtained by the following recursion: Let
\Delta t>0
be a (small) time-step. We assume we are given (discrete) initial data
u^{0}_{K}\in L^{2}_{K}
,
u^{0}_{K}\approx\mathbb{P}_{K}\overline{u}
. For
m\geq 0
, determine
u^{m+1}_{K}\in L^{2}_{K}
by solving

\displaystyle\frac{u^{m+1}_{K}-u^{m}_{K}}{\Delta t}=-\mathbb{P}_{K}\left(u^{m}_{K}\cdot\nabla u^{m+1/2}_{K}\right)+\nu\Delta u^{m+1/2}_{K},

(65)

where
u^{m+1/2}_{K}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(u^{m+1}_{K}+u^{m}_{K}\right)
. The recursion (
65
) is equivalent to a recursion for the corresponding Fourier coefficients
\widehat{u}_{k}^{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
{|k|_{\infty}\leq K}
, where

u^{m}_{K}(x)=\sum_{|k|_{\infty}\leq K}\widehat{u}^{m}_{k}e^{i\langle k,x\rangle},

which are uniquely defined by (and equivalent to) the scheme (
65
): The Fourier transformed version of (
65
) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{u}^{m+1}_{k}-\widehat{u}^{m}_{k}}{\Delta t}=-\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}\left(u^{m}_{K}\cdot\nabla u^{m+1/2}_{K}\right)-\nu|k|^{2}\widehat{u}^{m+1/2}_{k},

(66)

where we introduce the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}\left(u^{m}_{K}\cdot\nabla u^{m+1/2}_{K}\right):=\left(1-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^{2}}\right)\sum_{|\ell|_{\infty},|k-\ell|_{\infty}\leq K}i(\ell\cdot\widehat{u}_{k-\ell}^{m+1/2})\widehat{u}_{\ell}^{m},

(67)

which is the Fourier transform of the non-linear term
\mathbb{P}_{K}\left(u^{m}_{K}\cdot\nabla u^{m+1/2}_{K}\right)
.

In
(
Kovachki et al. 2021
, Lem. 53)
, it has been shown that this numerical scheme is well-defined for time-steps satisfying
\|\overline{u}\|_{L^{2}}K^{n/2+1}\Delta t\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and that, if
t\mapsto u(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes a smooth solution of (
3.4.2
), and
t^{m}:=m\Delta t
, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{u(t^{m+1})-u(t^{m})}{\Delta t}&=-\mathbb{P}_{K}\left(u(t^{m})\cdot\nabla\frac{1}{2}\left[u(t^{m+1})+u(t^{m})\right]\right)\\
&\qquad+\nu\Delta\frac{1}{2}\left[u(t^{m+1})+u(t^{m})\right]+R_{K}^{m}(u),\end{aligned}

(68)

where the remainder
R_{K}^{m}(u)
can be estimated by

\displaystyle\|R_{K}^{m}(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\Delta t^{2}+M^{-2r}\right),

(69)

for any
r\geq n/2+1
, and where the constant
C>0
depends only on

\|u\|_{C_{t}H^{r}_{x}},\|u\|_{C^{1}_{t}H^{r-1}_{x}}
. Here, we have introduced
C_{t}H^{r}_{x}:=C([0,T];H^{r}_{x})
,
C_{t}^{1}H^{r-1}_{x}:=C^{1}([0,T];H^{r-1}_{x})
, given as the space of vector fields
u:[0,T]\to H^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
with continuous dependence on time, and the space of vector fields
u:[0,T]\to H^{r-1}_{x}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
with continuously differentiable dependence on time, respectively.

Neural network emulation of scheme (
65

) can be efficiently emulated by a neural network. To this end, we will first construct a neural network
\mathscr{N\!\!L}_{M,\epsilon}(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

We note that the mapping
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\mapsto\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
(\widehat{u},\widehat{v})\mapsto\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}\left(u_{K}\cdot\nabla v_{K}\right)
can be written as a composition,

\displaystyle(\widehat{u}_{k},\widehat{v}_{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\mapsto(\widehat{u}_{k},k\otimes\widehat{v}_{k})_{k\in\mathcal{K}}

\displaystyle\mapsto(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2K}}

\displaystyle\mapsto([u_{K}\cdot\nabla v_{K}](x_{j}))_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2K}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}\left(u_{K}\cdot\nabla v_{K}\right).

The first mapping can trivially be represented by a matrix multiplication with
O(|\mathcal{K}|)
non-zero entries, the second step can be efficiently computed via the fast Fourier-transform, which can be represented exactly by a ReLU neural network with
O(|\mathcal{K}|\log(|\mathcal{K}|))
non-zero entries (and depth
O(\log(|\mathcal{K}|))
). Thus, there exists a (absolute) constant
C=C(n)>0
, and a ReLU neural network
\psi^{\star}:\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\times\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}\to[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\times[\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}]^{\mathcal{J}}
, such that

\psi^{\star}(\widehat{u}_{k},\widehat{v}_{k})=(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))_{j\in\mathcal{J}},

and
\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\star})\leq C|\mathcal{K}|\log(|\mathcal{K}|)
,
0pt(\psi^{\star})\leq C\log(|\mathcal{K}|)
.
Similarly, for the last mapping, there exists a ReLU neural network
\psi^{\star\star}:[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{K}}
, such that

\psi^{\star\star}(w(x_{j}))=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}(w),\quad\forall\;w\in L^{2}_{2K},

and
\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\star\star})\leq C|\mathcal{K}|\log(|\mathcal{K}|)
,
0pt(\psi^{\star\star})\leq C\log(|\mathcal{K}|)
.
The main task is thus to show that the point-wise multiplication

(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))\mapsto u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}),

can be suitably approximated by a neural network. To this end, we will rely on the following lemma:

Lemma 46

(
(
Ryck and Mishra 2021
, Lemma 2.6)
)
.

Let
M,L>0
and denote by
\times:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
(x,y)\mapsto xy
the multiplication operator. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a realization of a ReLU neural network
\widehat{\times}_{m}:[-M,M]\times[-L,L]\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
\widehat{\times}_{m}
satisfies for all
y\in[-L,L]
the error bound

\|\times({\,\cdot\,},y)-\widehat{\times}_{m}({\,\cdot\,},y)\|_{\mathrm{Lip}([-M,M];\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{M+L}{2^{m+1}}.

Furthermore, it holds that
\mathrm{size}(\widehat{\times}_{m})\lesssim m
,
0pt(\widehat{\times}_{m})=m+1
.

We now note that for any
w\in L^{2}_{2K}
,
w(x)=\sum_{|k|_{\infty}\leq 2K}\widehat{w}_{k}e^{i\langle k,x\rangle}
, we have

\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=(2\pi)^{n}\sum_{|k|_{\infty}\leq 2K}|\widehat{w}_{k}|^{2}=\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{|\mathcal{J}_{2K}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{2K}}|w(x_{j})|^{2}.

and we have

\|u_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CK^{n/2}\|u_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{n}K^{n/2}\|\widehat{u}_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{n}K^{n/2}M,

and

\|\nabla v_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CK^{n/2}\|\nabla v_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{n}K^{n/2+1}\|\widehat{v}_{k}\|_{\ell^{2}}\leq C_{n}K^{n/2+1}M.

Applying Lemma
46
, it follows that there exists a neural network
\psi^{\ast}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

\|\psi^{\ast}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot\nabla v_{K}(x_{j})\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq\epsilon,

and
\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\ast})\leq C\log(MK/\epsilon)
,
0pt(\psi^{\ast})\leq C\log(MK/\epsilon)
, where
C=C(n)>0
is independent of
M,K,\epsilon
. Clearly, the parallelized mapping
\psi^{\epsilon}:[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\times[\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\to[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}
, defined by the point-wise application
(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))\mapsto\psi^{\ast}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2K}
, can be represented by a neural network with
\mathrm{size}(\psi^{\epsilon})\leq C|\mathcal{J}_{2K}|\log(MK/\epsilon)
. Furthermore, let
\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}
denote the coefficients
|k|_{\infty}\leq K
of the (fast) Fourier transform of
\psi^{\epsilon}
based on the values on the grid
x_{j}
,
j\in\mathcal{J}_{2K}

\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}=\psi^{\star\star}\circ\psi^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_{K}(u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{J}_{2K}|^{1/2}}\|\psi^{\epsilon}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot\nabla v_{K}(x_{j})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{J}_{2K})}

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{J}_{2K}|^{1/2}\|\psi^{\epsilon}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot\nabla v_{K}(x_{j})\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathcal{J}_{2K})}

In particular, replacing

\psi:[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\times[\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}]^{\mathcal{J}}\to[\mathbb{R}^{n}]^{\mathcal{J}}

\mathrm{size}(\psi)\leq C|\mathcal{K}|\log(MK/\epsilon),\quad 0pt(\psi)\leq C\log(MK/\epsilon),

where
C=C(n)>0
is independent of
M,K,\epsilon
,
and

Furthermore, we note that for any

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla w_{K}(x_{j}))\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{K})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Lip}(\psi^{\ast}(u_{K}(x_{j}),{\,\cdot\,})-u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot({\,\cdot\,}))+\mathrm{Lip}(u_{K}(x_{j})\cdot({\,\cdot\,}))

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon+\|u_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla v_{K}(x_{j}))-\widehat{\psi}_{\epsilon}(u_{K}(x_{j}),\nabla w_{K}(x_{j}))\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{K})}^{2}

Proof.

\displaystyle F(\widehat{w})_{k}:=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\nu|k|^{2}}\left[\widehat{u}^{m}-\Delta t\,\mathscr{N\!\!L}\left(\widehat{u}^{m},\frac{\widehat{u}^{m}+\widehat{w}}{2}\right){}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\nu|k|^{2}\widehat{u}^{m}\right].

\widehat{u}^{m+1}

\widehat{u}^{m+1}=F(\widehat{u}^{m+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta t}{1+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\nu|k|^{2}}\left\|\mathscr{N\!\!L}\left(\widehat{u}^{m},\frac{\widehat{u}^{m}+\widehat{w}}{2}\right)-\mathscr{N\!\!L}\left(\widehat{u}^{m},\frac{\widehat{u}^{m}+\widehat{v}}{2}\right)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

B_{M}(0)\subset X
denote the closed ball of radius
M
. Let
F:B_{M}(0)\to X
be a contractive mapping with
\gamma=\mathrm{Lip}(F)<1
, and assume that
\|F(0)\|\leq(1-\gamma)M
. Denote
R:=\|F(0)\|/(1-\gamma)\leq M
. Then there exists a unique fixed point
w^{\infty}\in B_{M}(0)
of
F
, and the norm of
w^{\infty}
is bounded by
\|w^{\infty}\|\leq R
. Furthermore, the recursively defined sequence
w^{0}:=0
,
w^{k+1}:=F(w^{k})
, is well-defined for all
k=0,1,\dots
, and satisfies the a priori estimates:

\|w^{k}\|\leq R,\quad\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|\leq\gamma^{k}R,\quad\|w^{k}-w^{\infty}\|\leq\gamma^{k}R.

Proof.

Set
w^{0}:=0
. Given
w^{k}
, we define
w^{k+1}:={F}(w^{k})
, if
w^{k}\in B_{M}(0)
, otherwise we terminate the sequence. Let

k_{0}=\sup{\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}\,\middle|\,w^{1},\dots,w^{k}\text{ are well-defined}\right\}}.

We claim that
k_{0}=\infty
, and that in fact
\|w^{k}\|\leq R
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose this was not the case. Then we must have
k_{0}<\infty
, and
w^{k_{0}}\notin B_{M}(0)
. We now note that for any
k<k_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{w}^{k+1}-{w}^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\|F(w^{k})-F(w^{k-1})\|\leq\gamma\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\dots\leq\gamma^{k}\|{w}^{1}-{w}^{0}\|=\gamma^{k}\|F(0)\|,

where we used the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, and the fact that
w^{0}=0
and
w^{1}=F(w^{0})=F(0)
, in the last step.
Combined with the telescoping sum
{w}^{k_{0}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}-1}[{w}^{\ell+1}-{w}^{\ell}]
, this estimate implies that

\|{w}^{k_{0}}\|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}-1}\|{w}^{\ell+1}-{w}^{\ell}\|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}-1}\gamma^{\ell}\|F(0)\|\leq\frac{\|F(0)\|}{1-\gamma}=R,

and hence
w^{k_{0}}\in B_{R}(0)\subset B_{M}(0)
. This clearly contradicts the definition of
k_{0}
. Hence, we must have
k_{0}=\infty
. Thus, the recursive sequence
w^{0}=0
,
w^{k+1}=F(w^{k})
is well-defined for any
k=0,1,\dots
, and the above argument shows furthermore that
\|w^{k}\|\leq R
, for all
k
. In fact, since the terms of the series
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}[w^{\ell+1}-w^{\ell}]
are summable in norm, we conclude that there exists a limit

w^{\infty}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}[w^{\ell+1}-w^{\ell}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}[w^{\ell+1}-w^{\ell}]=\lim_{k\to\infty}w^{k}.

Passing to the limit, it is now straight-forward to show that
w^{\infty}
is a fixed point of
F
, that
\|w^{\infty}\|\leq R
, and that we have

\displaystyle\|w^{k}-w^{\infty}\|

\displaystyle=\|F(w^{k-1})-F(w^{\infty})\|\leq\gamma\|w^{k-1}-w^{\infty}\|

\displaystyle\leq\dots\leq\gamma^{k}\|w^{0}-w^{\infty}\|=\gamma^{k}\|w^{\infty}\|\leq\gamma^{k}R,

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. To show that
w^{\infty}
is unique, we assume that
v^{\infty}
is another fixed point of
F
. Then

\|w^{\infty}-v^{\infty}\|=\|F(w^{\infty})-F(v^{\infty})\|\leq\gamma\|w^{\infty}-v^{\infty}\|,

is only possible if
\|w^{\infty}-v^{\infty}\|=0
.
∎

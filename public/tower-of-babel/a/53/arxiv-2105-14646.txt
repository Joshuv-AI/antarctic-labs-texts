Abstract




abstract.

We investigate the property of elimination of imaginaries for some special cases of ordered abelian groups. We show that certain Hahn products of ordered abelian groups do not eliminate imaginaries in the pure language of ordered groups. Moreover, we prove that, adding finitely many constants to the language of ordered abelian groups, the theories of the finite lexicographic products
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
have definable Skolem functions.

Introduction

Proving elimination of imaginaries is essentially a definability problem. Indeed, the intuitive idea for a structure eliminating imaginaries is that some quotient structures, in general not definable, admit canonical codings which essentially define them. Our aim is to investigate this property for some special cases of ordered abelian groups.

An ordered abelian group
G
is an abelian group endowed with a linear order compatible with the group operation:
a<a^{\prime}\implies a+b<a^{\prime}+b
for all
a,a^{\prime},b\in G
. The order is
discrete
if there exists a minimal positive element,
dense
otherwise. Both classes of structures are axiomatizable in
L_{oag}=\Set{0,+,-,<}
and they play an important role in the model theory of valued fields. Gurevich and Schmitt, from the 60s to the 80s, gave a notable contribution to the model theory of ordered abelian groups, providing, among other things, a test for elementary equivalence of two ordered abelian groups
[
6
]
, and a proof that the theory of ordered abelian groups is NIP
[
7
]
.

In the literature there are partial results on elimination of imaginaries in ordered abelian groups. Examples of ordered abelian groups that eliminate imaginaries are divisible ordered abelian groups
G\equiv(\mathbb{Q},+,<)
, see
[
11
]
, and discrete ordered abelian groups
G\equiv(\mathbb{Z},+,<)
, see
[
3
]
and Appendix A of
[
4
]
. One can ask whether every ordered abelian group has elimination of imaginaries in
L_{oag}
. We solve negatively the question producing examples of ordered abelian groups that do not eliminate imaginaries in
L_{oag}
. Indeed, we prove that elimination of imaginaries fails for the theory of any lexicographic products of
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, over a well-ordered index set
\alpha>1
, and for the theory of the lexicographic product
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Q}
.

A property related to the elimination of imaginaries is the existence of definable Skolem functions. In particular, definable Skolem functions allow to reduce the goal of finding a code for every imaginary to coding just one dimensional definable sets. We prove that, once we add finitely many judiciously chosen elements as new constants to
L_{oag}
, the theories of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, for any
n\geq 1
, have definable Skolem functions.

Notation and preliminaries

First of all, we recall all definitions and notions that we will use in this paper.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an
L
-structure,

-definable equivalence relation on

-equivalence classes of

of

, and we say that

Definition 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

An
L
-structure
\mathfrak{M}
has

if for any positive integer

, any
0
-definable equivalence relation

on
\mathfrak{M}^{m}
and any

-class

, there is an
L
-formula
\vartheta(\bar{x},\bar{z})
such that

for some
unique

. Moreover, such a tuple

is called a

for

.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an
L
-structure and

. By

we denote the set

\text{Fix}(S)=\Set{a\in\mathfrak{M}\mid f(a)=a\text{ for all }f\in S}.

Let

. By
\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{M}/A)
and
\text{Stab}_{\mathfrak{M}}(A)
we denote the group of all automorphisms of
\mathfrak{M}
fixing

A
setwise, respectively. Namely,

\displaystyle\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{M}/A)=\Set{\varphi\in\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{M})\mid\varphi(a)=a\text{ for every }a\in A}

\displaystyle\text{Stab}_{\mathfrak{M}}(A)=\Set{\varphi\in\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{M})\mid\varphi(A)=A}.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an
L
-structure that admits elimination of imaginaries and let
\bar{b}
be a canonical parameter for an
E
-class
X
, where

is a
0
-definable equivalence relation on
\mathfrak{M}^{m}
. Then

is fixed by the same automorphisms of

which leave

invariant. Therefore, if
\mathfrak{M}
eliminates imaginaries, for every

-class

there exists a tuple

such that, for any automorphism

of
\mathfrak{M}

if and only if

.

Definition 1.3
.

An
L
-structure
\mathfrak{M}
has
uniform elimination of imaginaries
if for any positive integer
m
, and any
0
-definable equivalence relation
E(\bar{x},\bar{y})
on
\mathfrak{M}^{m}
, there is an
L
-formula
\vartheta(\bar{x},\bar{z})
such that for every
E
-class
X
, there is a unique tuple
\bar{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}
such that
X=\vartheta(\mathfrak{M}^{m},\bar{b})
.

In other words,
\mathfrak{M}
uniformly eliminates imaginaries if one can find a formula
\vartheta(\bar{x},\bar{z})
as in Definition
1.1
depending only on the equivalence relation
E(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and not on the equivalence class
X
. Notice that uniform elimination of imaginaries is preserved under elementarily equivalence.

We say that a theory
T
in
L
has (uniform) elimination of imaginaries if every model of
T
has (uniform) elimination of imaginaries. The uniformity of elimination of imaginaries, in the sense of Definition
1.3
, holds under the condition stated in the following well-known result (see, for example,
[
14
, Theorem 16.16]
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
T
be an
L
-theory. Suppose
T
has elimination of imaginaries and, for every model of
T
,
dcl(\emptyset)
contains at least two elements. Then
T
has uniform elimination of imaginaries.

Note that the existence of at least two
0
-definable elements in order to have uniform elimination of imaginaries cannot be avoided. Indeed, both classes of the equivalence relation
(x_{1}=x_{2}\land y_{1}=y_{2})\lor(x_{1}\neq x_{2}\land y_{1}\neq y_{2})
are definable without parameters by the formulas
x_{1}=x_{2}
and
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, respectively, but there is no way of using a unique tuple to pick out one of these formulas.

Another form of elimination of imaginaries is the following.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that an
L
-structure
\mathfrak{M}
has
weak elimination of imaginaries
if for any positive integer

, any
0
-definable equivalence relation

on
\mathfrak{M}^{m}
and any

-class

, there are a
L
-formula
\vartheta(\bar{x},\bar{z})
and a

such that

if and only if

.

We say that a theory

in

has weak elimination of imaginaries if every model of

has weak elimination of imaginaries.

Fact 1.6

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
T
be an expansion of the theory of linear order. If

T
eliminates imaginaries.

Throughout the paper, unless otherwise stated, we work in the language of ordered abelian groups
L_{oag}
, and "definable" will mean definable with parameters.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group, and
f,g\in G
such that
f<g
. The notations
[f,g]
and
(f,g)
stand for the intervals
\{h\in G\mid f\leq h\leq g\}
and
\{h\in G\mid f<h<g\}
, respectively. The absolute value of

is
\lvert f\rvert=\max\{f,-f\}
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer,
\equiv_{m}
denotes the binary relation on
G
defined by

f\equiv_{m}g\text{ if and only if }f-g\in mG,

Clearly, the relation
\equiv_{m}
is a
0
-definable equivalence relation.

A subgroup
\Delta
of an ordered abelian group
G
is
convex
in

if, for every

such that
0<g<h
and

, we also have
g\in\Delta
. The set of all convex subgroups of
G
is linearly ordered by inclusion and is denoted by
\mathcal{CS}(G)
. The order type of the collection of all proper convex subgroups of
G
is called the
rank
of
G
. We now introduce the following two families of definable convex subgroups,
\{H_{n}(f)\}_{f\in G\setminus nG}
and
\{H_{n}^{+}(f)\}_{f\in G}
, see
[
8
]
for details. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in G\setminus nG
,
H_{n}(f)
denotes the largest convex subgroup
H
of
G
not intersecting
f+nG
. In particular,

H_{n}(f)=\Set{h\in G\mid\Braket{h}^{conv}\cap f+nG=\emptyset},

where
\Braket{h}^{conv}
is the smallest convex subgroup of
G
containing
h
. For every
f\in G
,
H_{n}^{+}(f)
denotes the definable convex subgroup

H_{n}^{+}(f)=\underset{\Set{g\in G\setminus nG\mid f\in H_{n}(g)}}{\bigcap}H_{n}(g).

Let
(I,<)
be an ordered set, and for each
i\in I
let
G_{i}
be an ordered abelian group. Consider the direct product of the groups
G_{i}
,
\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}
. For every
f\in\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}
, the
support
of
f
is the set

supp(f)=\{i\in I\mid f(i)\neq 0\}.

Let

H=\Set{f\in\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}\mid supp(f)\text{ is a well-ordered subset of }(I,<)}.

It is easily checked that
H
is a subgroup of
\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}
and is an ordered abelian group by defining

f<g\text{ if and only if }f(i)<g(i),\text{ where }i=\min supp(g-f),

for any
f,g\in H
. The group
H
endowed with this order is the
Hahn product
of the family
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and is denoted by
\mathscr{H}_{i\in I}G_{i}
. The ordered subgroup

\Set{f\in\mathscr{H}_{i\in I}G_{i}\mid supp(f)\text{ is finite }}

is the
lexicographic sum
of the family
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and is denoted by
\sum_{i\in I}G_{i}
.

The lexicographic sum and the Hahn product of a family of ordered abelian groups are indistinguishable by first order properties. Indeed, the following holds.

Proposition 1.7

(
[
15
]
, Corollary 6.3)
.

Let
(I,<)
be an ordered set and for each
i\in I
let
G_{i}
be an ordered abelian group. The lexicographic sum
\sum_{i\in I}G_{i}
is an elementary substructure of the Hahn product
\mathscr{H}_{i\in I}G_{i}
.

We denote the successor of an ordinal
\alpha
by
\alpha+1
and, if a set

has order type

S
is denoted by
\alpha^{*}
.

2.
Lexicographic products of
\mathbb{Z}

In this section, we consider the Hahn product of the group of integers,
\mathbb{Z}
, with the usual order over a well-ordered set
I
with
\lvert I\rvert>1
. Henceforth, we identify
I
with the corresponding ordinal
\alpha
, and
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
will denote
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
and
\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. Note that, since
\alpha
is well-ordered, the domain of
\Lambda
coincides with the direct product
\prod_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. This allows us to refer to the Hahn product
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
as the lexicographic product of
\mathbb{Z}
over
\alpha
. We assume that
\alpha>1
since the case
\alpha=1
is already studied in
[
3
]
.

If
f\in\Lambda
,
f\neq 0
, then we define
v(f)=\min supp(f)
. Furthermore, we add the symbol
\infty
to
\alpha
and put
i<\infty
for all
i<\alpha
. Hence, we set
v(0)=\infty
. One can easily prove the following properties.

Fact 2.1
.

(i)
v(f)=v(-f)
for any
f\in\Lambda
;

(ii)
v(f+g)\geq\min\{v(f),v(g)\}
for any
f,g\in\Lambda
;

(iii) Let
f,g\in\Lambda
such that
0<g<f
. Then
v(f)\leq v(g)
.

Let
i<\alpha
,
e_{\{i\}}
will denote the following element of
\Lambda
:

(1)

(e_{\{i\}}(j))_{j<\alpha}\text{ where }e_{\{i\}}(j)=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }j=i\\
0\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Note that, for every
i,j<\alpha
such that
i<j
,
0<e_{\{j\}}<e_{\{i\}}
. Moreover, the family
\{e_{\{i\}}\}_{i<\alpha}
generates
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
as an abelian group.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\alpha=\beta+1
for some ordinal
\beta
, then
\Lambda=\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is discrete and, in particular,
e_{\{\beta\}}
is the minimal positive element of
\Lambda
. Otherwise, if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal,
\Lambda
is dense.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\beta+1
and suppose
f\in\Lambda
is such that
0<f\leq e_{\{\beta\}}
. Then
v(f)=\beta
and
0<f(\beta)\leq 1
. Since
f(\beta)\in\mathbb{Z}
,
f(\beta)=1
and
f=e_{\{\beta\}}
.

Let
\alpha
be a limit ordinal and
f\in\Lambda
,
f>0
. Let
i=v(f)
. Then
f(i)>0
and
f(j)=0
for every
j<i
. Clearly,
0<e_{\{i+1\}}<f
.
∎

Note that, since
\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\preceq\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, if
\alpha=\beta+1
,
e_{\{\beta\}}
is the minimal positive element of
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. Otherwise,
\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is dense.

Now we aim at characterizing the convex subgroups of
\Lambda
. To this purpose, for every
i\in\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
, define
\Lambda_{i}=\Set{f\in\Lambda\mid v(f)\geq i}
. Trivially,
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda
,
\Lambda_{\infty}=\{0\}
and, for every
0<i<\infty
,
\Lambda_{i}
is a (non-trivial) convex subgroup of
\Lambda
. Let

F\colon i\in\alpha\cup\{\infty\}\mapsto\Lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{CS}(\Lambda)

It is clear that
F
is injective and order-reversing:
i\leq j
implies
\Lambda_{j}\subseteq\Lambda_{i}
, for every
i,j\in\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
. The following proposition implies that
F
is also surjective.

We recall that an endsegment of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
is a subset of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
that is closed upward. Let
\mathcal{ES}(\alpha\cup\{\infty\})
denote the set of all endsegments of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

There exists an order-preserving bijection between the set of all endsegments of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
and the set of all convex subgroups of
\Lambda=\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
C
be an endsegment of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
and
\Delta_{C}=\Set{f\in\Lambda\mid v(f)\in C}
. By Fact
2.1
,
\Delta_{C}
is a convex subgroup of
\Lambda
. Consider the function

K\colon C\in\mathcal{ES}(\alpha\cup\{\infty\})\mapsto\Delta_{C}\in\mathcal{CS}(\Lambda).

Trivially, if
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{ES}(\alpha\cup\{\infty\})
are such that
C_{1}\subseteq C_{2}
, then
\Delta_{C_{1}}\subseteq\Delta_{C_{2}}
. So
K
is order-preserving.

Let
\Delta
be a convex subgroup of
\Lambda
. Then
C_{\Delta}=\Set{v(f)\in\alpha\cup\{\infty\}\mid f\in\Delta}
is an endsegment of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
. Indeed,
\infty\in C_{\Delta}
, and let
k<\alpha
with
j<k
for some
j\in C_{\Delta}
. Then there exists
f\in\Delta
such that
j=v(f)
and, without loss of generality, we can suppose
f>0
. Clearly,
0<e_{\{k\}}<f
and
v(e_{\{k\}})=k
. By convexity of
\Delta
,
e_{\{k\}}\in\Delta
and
k\in C_{\Delta}
. Therefore, the function

H\colon\Delta\in\mathcal{CS}(\Lambda)\mapsto C_{\Delta}\in\mathcal{ES}(\alpha\cup\{\infty\}).

is order-preserving. Moreover,
H(K(C))=C
and
K(H(\Delta))=\Delta
for every
C\in\mathcal{ES}(\alpha\cup\{\infty\})
and
\Delta\in\mathcal{CS}(\Lambda)
. Then,
H=K^{-1}
, and so

is bijective.
∎

Note that any endsegment

of
\alpha\cup\{\infty\}
is either
\{\infty\}
or
C=\Set{j<\alpha\mid j\geq i}\cup\{\infty\}
for some
i<\alpha
. This implies that the
\Lambda_{i}
’s are exactly all the convex subgroups of
\Lambda
.

By Proposition

, we can determine the order type of the set of all proper convex subgroups of
\Lambda
.

Corollary 2.4
.

For every ordinal
\alpha>1
,
\Lambda=\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
is of rank

.

In particular, if
\alpha
is finite, i.e.

, there are exactly

proper convex subgroups of
\Lambda
.

Note that, since

\mathcal{CS}(\Gamma)=\Set{\Delta\cap\Gamma\mid\Delta\in\mathcal{CS}(\Lambda)},

the sets
\Gamma_{i}=\Set{f\in\Gamma\mid v(f)\geq i}
are exactly all the convex subgroups of
\Gamma
, and Proposition

and Corollary

hold also for
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The family
\{\Lambda_{i+1}\}_{i+1<\alpha}
is uniformly definable.

Proof.

Let
i<\alpha
be such that
i+1<\alpha
. Then
\Lambda_{i+1}=H_{2}(e_{\{i\}})
and, so, is definable. Indeed, for every

, we have

. Hence,
H_{2}(e_{\{i\}})=\Set{f\in\Lambda\mid v(f)>i}=\Set{f\in\Lambda\mid v(f)\geq i+1}=\Lambda_{i+1}
.
∎

More generally, for every

, we have

H_{n}(f)=\begin{cases}\Lambda_{i+1}&\text{if $i+1<\alpha$}\\
\{0\}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where

.

Now we are able to prove

Corollary 2.6
.

All convex subgroups of

are definable.

Proof.

We have only to show the definability of
\Lambda_{i}
for

limit ordinal. Let

be a limit ordinal, then

\Lambda_{i}=\underset{\Set{j<\alpha\mid j<i}}{\bigcap}\Lambda_{j}=\underset{\Set{j<\alpha\mid j<i\text{ and $j$ is not limit }}}{\bigcap}\Lambda_{j}=H_{2}^{+}(e_{\{i\}}).

∎

It is clear that the above statements hold also for

.

2.1.
The group of automorphisms of the lexicographic sum of
\mathbb{Z}

In this subsection, we will describe the group of automorphisms of
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
.

First of all, we characterize the automorphisms of
\Gamma
when
\alpha
is finite, i.e.
\alpha=n
for some natural number
n>1
. In this case,
\Gamma=\sum_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\mathbb{Z}=\mathscr{H}_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\mathbb{Z}
, and we will denote
\Gamma
by its domain
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
z(i)=z_{i}
for every
0\leq i\leq n-1
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a discrete ordered abelian group. Therefore, every element of the convex subgroup

{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{n-1}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid v(z)\geq n-1}=\Set{(0,\dots,0,m)\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}}

is
0
-definable. Moreover, each convex subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
0
-definable, as it is proved in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

For every
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid v(z)\geq i}
is
0
-definable.

Proof.

For
i=0
it is trivial. So, let
0<i\leq n-1
and fix a prime
p
. For every
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i}
if and only if the set
\Set{w+p\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid-z\leq w\leq z}
of the
\equiv_{p}
- equivalence classes of elements in the interval
[-z,z]
has cardinality at most
p^{n-i}
. Indeed, let
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{i}
. Then
[-z,z]\subseteq(-e_{\{i-1\}},e_{\{i-1\}})
. Since, for every
0\leq j\leq n-1
the
\equiv_{p}
- equivalence classes in
(-e_{\{j\}},e_{\{j\}})
are exactly
p^{n-1-j}
, it follows that
\lvert\Set{w+p\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid-z\leq w\leq z}\rvert\leq p^{n-i}
. Conversely, if
z\notin\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{i}
, then
v(z)<i
and
[-z,z]
contains the interval
[-e_{\{i-1\}},e_{\{i-1\}}]
. Therefore
|\Set{w+p\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid-z\leq w\leq z}|\geq p^{n-i}+1
.

Clearly, the set of
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\lvert\Set{w+p\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid-z\leq w\leq z}\rvert\leq p^{n-i}
is
0
-definable.
∎

Consider the following upper triangular matrix of size
n\times n
, whose elements are in
\mathbb{Z}

(2)

K_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&k_{12}&\dots&k_{1n}\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&k_{n-1n}\\
0&\dots&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
K_{n}
is invertible and its inverse is an upper triangular matrix on
\mathbb{Z}
of the same form, with all the entries of the main diagonal equal to
1
. Then the function

f_{K_{n}}\colon z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mapsto zK_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

where
zK_{n}
is the matrix product of the row vector
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
and
K_{n}
, is a group automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Moreover, it is straightforward to show that
f_{K_{n}}
is order-preserving. Therefore, every matrix as in (
2
) determines an automorphism of the ordered group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We now prove that every automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is obtained in this way and it can be represented by a matrix as in (
2
).

Note that, since
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a free module of rank

, every group automorphism
\varphi
of

is given by an invertible matrix

M
over

with

such that

for every

. Indeed, the matrix

(3)

\vdots\\
\varphi(e_{\{n\}})\end{pmatrix}

represents
\varphi
in the above sense. Hence, it suffices to prove that if
\varphi
preserves the order on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then

is of the form (

).

Let
0\leq i\leq n-1
and
m
be any element in
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, consider

A_{i,m}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid v(z)=i\text{ and }z_{v(z)}=m}.

Note that, for
i=n-1
,

is the singleton of
(0,\dots,0,m)
. For

,

is the set

, and, for
0<i<n-1
,
A_{i,m}=\{0\}^{i}\times\{m\}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-(i+1)}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

For every
0\leq i\leq n-1
and every
m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, the set
A_{i,m}
is
0
-definable.

Proof.

For
i=n-1
it is clear. Let
0\leq i<n-1
be fixed and consider the set
A_{i}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid v(z)=i}
. By Lemma
2.7
, since
A_{i}={\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i+1}
,
A_{i}
is
0
-definable. Let
\alpha(x)
and
\beta(x)
be the formulas defining
A_{i}
and
{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}_{i+1}
, respectively. Since
A_{i,m}=\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\mid-z\in A_{i,-m}}
, it suffices to prove the statement for
m>0
. If
m=1
, then
A_{i,1}
is defined by the formula

\psi_{1}(x)\coloneq\alpha(x)\land 0<x\land\forall y\big((\alpha(y)\land 0<y\land y<x)\implies\beta(y-x)\big).

Indeed, it is trivial that any element
z
in
A_{i,1}
satisfies
\psi_{1}(x)
. Conversely, let
x
be an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfying
\psi_{1}(x)
. Then
v(x)=i
and
x_{v(x)}>0
. If
x_{v(x)}>1
, then
v(x-e_{\{i\}})=i
. Since
0<e_{\{i\}}<x
, we get a contradiction and
x_{v(x)}=1
. Let
m>1
and suppose by induction that
A_{i,m-1}
is defined by the formula
\psi_{m-1}(x)
. Consider the formula

\gamma_{m-1}(x)\coloneq\forall y(\psi_{m-1}(y)\implies y<x).

Then,
A_{i,m}
is defined by the formula

\psi_{m}(x)\coloneq\alpha(x)\land\gamma_{m-1}(x)\land\forall y\big(\gamma_{m-1}(y)\land y<x)\implies\beta(y-x)\big).

Indeed, let
x
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfy
\psi_{m}(x)
. Then
v(x)=i
and
x_{v(x)}>m-1
. If
x_{v(x)}>m
, then

, and we get a contradiction. Therefore,
x_{v(x)}=m
and

.
∎

Now we are able to prove the following

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of the lexicographic sum

, where

. Then there exists an upper triangular matrix

as in (

For every

, where

,

fixes

{\varphi(e_{\{i\}})}_{i}=1

Henceforth we focus on the case
\alpha\geq\omega
. Recall that the chain

(4)

\{0\}=\Gamma_{\infty}\subset\dots\subset\Gamma_{n}\subset\dots\subset\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}

represents the set of all convex subgroups of

. Note that, contrary to the finite case, it is not a well-ordered set, since it contains an infinite descending chain.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of
\Gamma
. For every

, there exists a finite subset

of

Then, we have

an infinite matrix

such that

for every

has only finitely many nonzero elements.

In order to generalize the result of Theorem
2.9
, we now determine which conditions

has to satisfy, for preserving the order on
\Gamma
. We will prove that also in the infinite case

is upper triangular with all the entries of the main diagonal equal to

, i.e.

(5)

m_{ik}=\begin{cases}0\text{ if }i>k\\

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of
\Gamma
. Then
\varphi
fixes
\Gamma_{i}
setwise for every

.

Proof.

Note that, for every

is a convex subgroup of

. It follows that

induces the order-preserving bijection
\Gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{CS}(\Gamma)\mapsto\varphi(\Gamma_{i})\in\mathcal{CS}(\Gamma)

. We prove that the automorphism induced by

on

is the identity. Clearly,
\varphi(\Gamma_{0})=\Gamma_{0}
. Let
0<i<\alpha
and suppose, by induction,
\varphi(\Gamma_{j})=\Gamma_{j}
for every

. Therefore,
\varphi(\Gamma_{i})\subseteq\Gamma_{i}
. Let
\Delta\in\mathcal{CS}(\Gamma)
be such that

. Then,

for some

. It follows that
\Gamma_{h}\subseteq\Gamma_{i}
and, since

is order-preserving,
\Gamma_{i}\subseteq\varphi(\Gamma_{i})
. Therefore,
\varphi(\Gamma_{i})=\Gamma_{i}
, and the statement is proved.
∎

From Lemma
2.10
, it follows immediately that

Corollary 2.11
.

If
\varphi
is an automorphism of

, then

fixes

Let

be such that
i+1<\alpha
. Then
\Set{f\in\Gamma\mid v(f)=i}=\Gamma_{i}\setminus\Gamma_{i+1}
and, by Lemma

, it is fixed setwise. Furthermore, if
\alpha=\beta+1
for some
\beta<\alpha
, each element of
\Gamma_{\beta}
is
0
-definable and
\Gamma_{\beta}
is fixed pointwise.
∎

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of
\Gamma
. Then there exists

with

’s as in (

) such that

for every

.

Fix

and consider

. Set

for every

. Then by Corollary
2.11
,

for

for

. Note that Lemma

implies that each

. Therefore

(\mod m_{ii})

Summarizing, we have shown that, for any ordinal

,

3.
Failure of elimination of imaginaries

We now prove that in both cases
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
and
\Lambda=\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, there exist some imaginaries of the ordered abelian group that can not be eliminated. We will first focus on the case of the lexicographic sum.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>1
be an ordinal. Then
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
does not admit elimination of imaginaries in the pure language of ordered abelian groups.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\Gamma
admits elimination of imaginaries.

Let
p
be a prime and fix an element
a\in\Gamma
such that
a(0)\notin p\mathbb{Z}
. Let
X=[a]_{\equiv_{p}}
be the
\equiv_{p}
- equivalence class of
a
. Since
\equiv_{p}
is
0
-definable, there exists a canonical parameter
\bar{b}
for
X
, let
\bar{b}=(b_{j})_{j<\mu}
for some positive integer
\mu
.

Let
\varphi
be an automorphism of
\Gamma
and
M
the matrix
(m_{ik})_{i,k<\alpha}
such that
\varphi(f)=fM
for every
f\in\Gamma
. Recall that
M
is either finite or infinite depending on the cardinality of
\alpha
, and that, for every
i,k<\alpha
,
m_{ii}=1
and
m_{ik}=0
for
k<i
. Suppose
\varphi\in\text{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
. Then
\varphi(a)\equiv_{p}a
, namely,
(\varphi(a))(i)-a(i)\in p\mathbb{Z}
for every
i<\alpha
. Therefore, from
(\varphi(a))(1)=a(0)m_{01}+a(1)
and
a(0)\notin p\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
m_{01}\in p\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, since
\varphi\in\text{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
then, by Fact
1.2
,
\varphi\in\text{Aut}(\Gamma/\bar{b})
, namely,
\varphi(b_{j})=b_{j}
for every
j<\mu
. In particular, since for every
0<k<\alpha
,
(\varphi(b_{j}))(k)=b_{j}(k)+\sum_{0\leq i<k}(b_{j}(i))m_{ik}
for every
j<\mu
, it follows that

(6)

\sum_{0\leq i<k}(b_{j}(i))m_{ik}=0\text{ for every }0<k<\alpha\text{ and for every }j<\mu.

Let
h<\alpha
be such that
h+1<\alpha
, and consider the following matrix
\tilde{M}^{h}=(\tilde{m}_{ik}^{h})_{i,k<\alpha}
where

\tilde{m}_{ik}^{h}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i=k\\
p&\text{if }i=h,k=h+1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

In particular,
\tilde{M}^{h}
is an upper triangular matrix, with all entries of the diagonal equal to
1
. Since for every
f\in\Gamma
,
(f\tilde{M}^{h})(h+1)=p(f(h))+f(h+1)
and
(f\tilde{M}^{h})(k)=f(k)
for every
k<\alpha
,
k\neq h
,
f\tilde{M}^{h}\in\Gamma
and
\tilde{M}^{h}
induces the function

\varphi_{\tilde{M}}\colon f\in\Gamma\mapsto f\tilde{M}^{h}\in\Gamma.

In particular,
\varphi_{\tilde{M}^{h}}(f)\equiv_{p}f
for every
f\in\Gamma
. Moreover,
\varphi_{\tilde{M}^{h}}
is a group automorphism and is order-preserving, hence
\varphi_{\tilde{M}^{h}}\in\text{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
. From (
6
) it follows that
p(b_{j}(h))=0
for every
j<\mu
, and, so,
b_{j}(h)=0
for every
j<\mu
. Now we need to distinguish two cases,
\alpha
limit ordinal and
\alpha
successor ordinal.

case
\alpha
limit:

Since for every
h<\alpha
,
h+1<\alpha
, we obtain
b_{j}=0
for every
j<\mu
.

case
\alpha=\beta+1
for some
\beta<\alpha
:

Then
b_{j}(h)=0
for every
h<\beta
and for every
j<\mu
. In particular, for every
j<\mu
,
b_{j}\in\Gamma_{\beta}
, i.e.
b_{j}=k_{j}e_{\{\beta\}}
for some
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
e_{\{\beta\}}
is the minimal positive element of
\Gamma
,
b_{j}
is
0
-definable for every
j<\mu
.

Therefore, in both cases, all the automorphisms of
\Gamma
fix
\bar{b}
. Hence, by Fact
1.2
, any automorphism of
\Gamma
fixes
X
. This is clearly false. Indeed, for example, the automorphism
\psi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
defined by
\psi(f)=f\bar{M}
, for every
f\in\Gamma
, and
\bar{M}=(\bar{m}_{ik})_{i,k<\alpha}
with

\bar{m}_{ik}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if either }i=k\text{ or }i=0,k=1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

does not fix
X
since
\bar{m}_{01}\notin p\mathbb{Z}
. The contradiction follows from the existence of a canonical parameter for

, and so
\Gamma
does not admit elimination of imaginaries.
∎

Using the same argument as in Theorem
3.1
, we can prove the failure of elimination of imaginaries in

also for

. Indeed, let
p
be a prime,

be such that

and

. Fix

, then the automorphism
\varphi_{h}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda

(7)

f(j)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for every

, fixes

setwise. Therefore, if
\bar{b}
is a canonical parameter for

, we obtain that
\bar{b}
is a tuple of

’s, with

, if

for some

, and

otherwise. In both cases we have a contradiction. Hence, we have proved

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
be an ordinal. Then
\Lambda=\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
does not admit elimination of imaginaries in the pure language of ordered abelian groups.

Summarizing, the argument used in

and

consists in determining, for a fixed

.

Let

be an ordinal. Then

Proof.

Let

3.1.
Weak elimination of imaginaries

From Fact
1.6
, one can deduce that the behaviour of the theories of
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Q}
in terms of "coding" imaginaries is even worse. Indeed, both
Th(\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
and
Th(\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Q})
do not have even weak elimination of imaginaries. Using a similar argument to that used in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we now provide a direct proof of the failure of weak elimination of imaginaries for the theory of
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal,
\alpha>1
. Then
T=Th(\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
does not have weak elimination of imaginaries in the pure language of ordered abelian groups.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that
T
has weak elimination of imaginaries. For simplicity, consider
\Gamma=\sum_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, let
p
be a prime and consider
X=[a]_{\equiv_{p}}
the
\equiv_{p}
-equivalence class of
a\in\Gamma
such that
a(0)\notin p\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exist a formula
\vartheta(x,\bar{w})
and
B
a finite set of
|\bar{w}|
-tuples such that
X=\vartheta(\Gamma,\bar{b})
if and only if
\bar{b}\in B
.

Let
\mu=|\bar{w}|
and
\bar{b}=(b_{0},\dots,b_{\mu})\in B
. Then
\bar{b}
is not
0
-definable, since
X
is not
0
-definable. In particular, if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, then
b_{j}\neq 0
for every
j\leq\mu
, otherwise, if
\alpha=\beta+1
for some
\beta<\alpha
, then
b_{j}\notin\Gamma_{\beta}
for every
j\leq\mu
. Fix
j\leq\mu
and consider
b_{j}
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
j=0
. Let
h=v(b_{0})<\infty
. If
\alpha=\beta+1
, then
h<\beta
. In any case, since
h+1<\alpha
, we can consider the function
\varphi_{h}\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
, defined as in (
7
). Therefore, since
\varphi_{h}\in\text{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
, we have
X=\vartheta(\Gamma,\varphi_{h}(\bar{b}))
and
\varphi_{h}(\bar{b})\in B
. In particular,
\varphi_{h}(\bar{b})=(\varphi_{h}(b_{0}),\dots,\varphi_{h}(b_{\mu}))
and, by definition of
\varphi_{h}
, we have
(\varphi_{h}(b_{0}))(h+1)=p(b_{0}(h))+b_{0}(h+1)
. Then, from
b_{0}(h)\neq 0
, it follows that
(\varphi_{h}(b_{0}))(h+1)\neq b_{0}(h+1)
and
\varphi_{h}(b_{0})\neq b_{0}
. Therefore,
\varphi_{h}(\bar{b})\neq\bar{b}
. Now consider
\varphi_{h}^{\delta}=\underbrace{\varphi_{h}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{h}}_{\delta\text{ times}}
for any natural number
\delta>0
. For every
\delta>0
,
\varphi_{h}^{\delta}\in\text{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
and, then,
\varphi_{h}^{\delta}(\bar{b})\in B
. Clearly,
(\varphi_{h}^{\delta}(b_{0}))(h+1)=\delta p(b_{0}(h))+b_{0}(h+1)
. It follows that
\{\varphi_{h}^{\delta}(\bar{b})\}_{\delta\in\mathbb{N}}
is an infinite sequence of pairwise distinct elements of
\Gamma
. This gives a contradiction since
B
is finite.
∎

Similarly, we can provide a direct proof of the failure of weak elimination of imaginaries for the theory of
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Q}
.

4.
definable Skolem functions

In this section, we prove that the theories of
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, with
n\geq 1
, have definable Skolem functions once finitely many new constants are added to the language of ordered abelian groups. We recall that

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an
L
-structure. We say that
Th(\mathfrak{M})
has definable Skolem functions if for every
L
-formula
\varphi(\bar{x},y)
, there is an
L
-formula
\psi(\bar{x},y)
such that

(8)

Th(\mathfrak{M})\vdash\forall\bar{x}(\exists y\varphi(\bar{x},y)\to(\exists!y\psi(\bar{x},y)\land\forall y(\psi(\bar{x},y)\to\varphi(\bar{x},y)))).

First of all, note that the convex subgroup
C=\{0\}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
is
0
-definable in
L_{oag}
by the formula
\gamma(x)
which says that all elements of
[0,\lvert x\rvert]
are divisible by
2
. We highlight

Fact 4.2
.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group.

(1) If
G\equiv\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, with
n\geq 1
, then
G
has
n

0
-definable proper convex subgroups
G^{(0)}=\{0\}<G^{(1)}<\dots<G^{(n-1)}<G^{(n)}=G
such that
G^{(i)}/G^{(i-1)}\equiv\mathbb{Z}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

(2) If
G\equiv\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, with
n\geq 1
, then
G
has
n+1

0
-definable proper convex subgroups
G^{(-1)}=\{0\}<G^{(0)}<\dots<G^{(n-1)}<G^{(n)}=G
such that
G^{(0)}\equiv\mathbb{Q}
and
G^{(i)}/G^{(i-1)}\equiv\mathbb{Z}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

We rely on the following fact from
[
20
]
. Consider the first order language
L_{Weis}=\Set{0,1^{(1)},1^{(2)},\dots,1^{(n)},+,-,<,(\equiv_{m})_{m>0}}
, where
1^{(1)}
,
1^{(2)}
,
\dots,1^{(n)}
are new constant symbols. In
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we interpret
1^{(1)},1^{(2)}
,
\dots,1^{(n)}
as
(0,\dots,0,1),(0,\dots,0,1,0),\dots,

(1,0,\dots,0)
, respectively. In
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
we interpret
1^{(1)},1^{(2)}
,
\dots,1^{(n)}
as
(0,\dots,0,1,0),

(0,\dots,0,1,0,0),\dots,(1,0,\dots,0)
, respectively. Namely, we expand
L_{oag}
by the equivalence relations
\equiv_{m}
, for each positive integer
m
, and
n
constants
1^{(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, for a representative of the smallest positive element in each discretely ordered quotient
G^{(i)}/G^{(i-1)}
, where either
G\equiv\mathbb{Z}^{n}
or
G\equiv\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
for some
n\geq 1
. In
[
20
]
, Weisfenning proved that

Fact 4.3
.

Both
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
admit elimination of quantifiers.

Let
G
be either a model of
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
or a model of
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, every formula
\sigma(x,\bar{a})
in
L_{Weis}
with parameters
\bar{a}\subset G
, is equivalent modulo
G
to a positive boolean combinations of formulas of the following forms

\displaystyle(1)\quad kx\equiv_{m}t(\bar{a}),\text{or }kx\nequiv_{m}t(\bar{a}),

\displaystyle(2)\quad kx=t(\bar{a}),kx<t(\bar{a}),\text{or }t(\bar{a})<kx,

where
k>0
is a natural number. Let
t(\bar{a})=g\in G
. Since
[G:mG]<\infty
, every formula in (1) is equivalent to a finite disjuction of formulas of the form
kx\equiv_{m}g
. If such a formula defines a nonempty set, then there exists
h\in G
such that
kx\equiv_{m}g
is equivalent to
x\equiv_{m^{\prime}}h
for some
m^{\prime}>0
. So we may assume that all formulas in (1) are of the form
x\equiv_{m}g
. Moreover, every formula in (2) defines a set which is a finite union of cosets of
2G
intersected with intervals, see Theorem 12 and Theorem 15 in
[
2
]
. Therefore, every definable set in
G
is a finite union of cosets of subgroups
mG
intersected with intervals with endpoints in
G\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, for some positive integer
m
. Hence,
G
is coset-minimal. We recall that

Definition 4.4
.

A totally ordered group (with possibly extra structure) is
coset-minimal
if every definable set is a finite union of cosets of definable subgroups intersected with intervals.

In
[
12
]
, it was proven that the groups elementarily equivalent to either
\mathbb{Q}
, or
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, or
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, for some
n\geq 1
, are exactly the coset-minimal pure (modulo some constants) groups. Moreover, since any
\equiv_{m}
-equivalence class is
0
-definable, any such group provides an example of a quasi o-minimal structure (see
[
1
]
).

We show that the theories of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
have definable Skolem functions in
L_{Weis}
and in a suitable language expanding
L_{Weis}
, respectively.
In
[
16
]
, using proof-theoretic arguments, Scowcroft identified the following sufficient condition for a model complete theory in order to have definable Skolem functions.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
L
be a first order language with at least one constant symbol and
T
be a model complete theory in
L
. Let
\Sigma
be a set of
\forall\exists
-axioms for
T
and
\Delta
be the set of all quantifier free
L
-formulas
\delta(\bar{u},\bar{v})
such that
\forall\bar{u}\exists\bar{v}\delta(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in\Sigma
. Suppose for each
\delta(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in\Delta
there is an
L
-formula
\gamma_{\delta}(\bar{u},\bar{v})
such that
T\vdash\forall\bar{u}\exists!\bar{v}\ \gamma_{\delta}(\bar{u},\bar{v})
and
T\vdash\forall\bar{u}\forall\bar{v}(\gamma_{\delta}(\bar{u},\bar{v})\to\delta(\bar{u},\bar{v}))
. Then
T
has definable Skolem functions.

Note that by Fact
4.3
the theories
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
are model-complete and, hence, can be axiomatised by
\forall\exists
-sentences. Consider the following sets of sentences introduced in
[
17
, pp. 149-150]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{0}=&\forall x\forall y\forall z((x+y)+z=x+(y+z));\\
&\forall x(x+0=x);\\
&\forall x(x-x=0);\\
&\forall x\forall y(x+y=y+x);\\
&\forall x(\neg(x<x));\\
&\forall x\forall y\forall z(x<y<z\to x<z);\\
&\forall x(x=0\lor 0<x\lor x<0);\\
&\forall x\forall y(0<x\land 0<y\to 0<x+y).\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{1}=&\ 0<21^{(i)}<1^{(i+1)}\text{ for each $i$ such that }1\leq i\leq n-1;\\
&\forall x(2x<1^{(i)}\lor 1^{(i)}<2x)\text{ for each $i$ such that }1\leq i\leq n;\\
&\forall x(2x<1^{(i)}\to mx<1^{(i)})\text{ for each $i$ such that }1\leq i\leq n\text{ and }m\geq 2;\\
&\forall x(x\equiv_{m}0\leftrightarrow\exists y\exists z(-1^{(1)}<2z<1^{(1)}\land x=my+z))\text{ for each }m>0;\\
&\forall x(\underset{0\leq q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\leq m-1}{\bigvee}(x\equiv_{m}q_{1}1^{(1)}+\dots+q_{n}1^{(n)}))\text{ for each }m>1;\\
&\forall x(-1^{(1)}<2x<1^{(1)}\to\exists y(x=my))\text{ for each }m>1.\end{split}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}=\forall x(\neg(0<x<1^{(1)})).

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}=\exists x(0<x<1^{(1)}).

It was shown in
[
17
]
that the theories
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
are axiomatized by
\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{3}
, respectively. Therefore we are able to use Scowcroft’s criterion for the existence of definable Skolem functions in model complete theories. It follows easily that

Theorem 4.6
.

The theory
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
has definable Skolem functions.

Proof.

We just need to show the existence of definable Skolem functions for the non-universal axioms:

\displaystyle\forall x(x\equiv_{m}0\leftrightarrow\exists y\exists z(-1^{(1)}<2z<1^{(1)}\land x=my+z))\text{ for each }m>0,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\forall x(-1^{(1)}<2x<1^{(1)}\to\exists y(x=my))\text{ for each }m>1.

It is sufficient to note that, in any model
G
of
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, if
g\in G
is such that
g=mh
for some
h\in G
, then
h
is unique. Indeed, let
g,h,k\in G
be such that
g=mh+k
and
-1^{(1)}<2k<1^{(1)}
. Trivially, by the interpretation of
1^{(1)}
, the inequalities
-1^{(1)}<2k<1^{(1)}
imply
k=0
. Hence,
k
is unique and, so also
h
in
g=mh
is unique.
∎

In order to prove the same result for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, for any
n\geq 1
, fix an element
c
in
C=\{0\}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
such that
c>0
. Then the quantifier elimination in
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
is not affected by adding
c
as a new constant to
L_{Weis}
. Let
L_{Weis}(c)
denote
L_{Weis}\cup\{c\}
. Now an axiomatization of
Th_{L_{Weis}(c)}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
is given by
\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{3}
and the following axiom:

0<2c<1^{(1)}.

Theorem 4.7
.

The theory
Th_{L_{Weis}(c)}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
has definable Skolem functions.

Proof.

We just need to show the existence of definable Skolem functions for the non-universal axioms:

\displaystyle\forall x(x\equiv_{m}0\leftrightarrow\exists y\exists z(-1^{(1)}<2z<1^{(1)}\land x=my+z))\text{ for each }m>0,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\forall x(-1^{(1)}<2x<1^{(1)}\to\exists y(x=my))\text{ for each }m>1,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\exists x(0<x<1^{(1)}).

Note that
0<c<1^{(1)}
. Then, as in the proof of Theorem
4.6
, it suffices to note that, in any model
G
of
Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q})
, if
g\in G
is such that
g=mh
for some
h\in G
, then
h
is unique. Indeed, let
g,h,k\in G
be such that
g=mh+k
and
-1^{(1)}<2k<1^{(1)}
. In particular, there exists
k^{\prime}\in G
such that
k=mk^{\prime}
, and
g=m(h+k^{\prime})
. Therefore, we may assume
k=0
.
∎

By the characterization of dp-minimal ordered groups in
[
10
]
, for any
n\geq 1
, the finite lexicographic products
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
are dp-minimal. The author has recently learned that Vicaria
[
19
]
has indentified a suitable many-sorted language in which the class of dp-minimal ordered abelian groups eliminates imaginaries. We aimed at identifying a single-sorted language that could suffice for eliminating imaginaries for the theory of the ordered groups
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, for any
n\geq 1
. The languages
L_{Weis}
and
L_{Weis}(c)
seemed to be promising for this goal, since in these languages we can eliminate the
\equiv_{p}
-equivalence classes and,
by Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
4.7
, there are definable Skolem functions. Indeed, it is well known that, provided that one can uniformly associate a canonical parameter to every unary definable set, the existence of definable Skolem functions is a sufficient condition for uniform elimination of imaginaries (see
[
9
, Lemma 4.4.3]
).

The following example pointed out to the author by M. Hils shows that unfortunately this is not plausible, and a many-sorted language seems unavoidable. Consider the lexicographic product
G=\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
in
L_{Weis}
(or in some expansion
L
of
L_{Weis}
by adding new constants) and suppose
G
admits elimination of imaginaries. By Fact
4.3
, we have that every infinite definable set
X\subseteq G^{m}
is uncountable. Then, in particular, the set of canonical parameters of cosets
a+\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
, with
a\in G
, is uncountable. This is clearly false, since
\frac{\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}{\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. The contradiction follows from the existence of a canonical parameter for
a+\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and so
G\models Th_{L_{Weis}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
does not admit elimination of imaginaries. Similar arguments can be used to show that the theory of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, for any
n>1
, and the theory of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Q}
, for any
n\geq 1
, do not admit elimination of imaginaries in any expansion
L
of
L_{oag}
by adding new constants.

Remark 4.8
.

It is still unsolved the problem of eliminating imaginaries for the theories of
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathscr{H}_{i<\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Q}
with
\alpha

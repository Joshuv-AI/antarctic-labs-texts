Lipschitz continuity of the solutions to the Dirichlet problems for the invariant laplacians Thanks: The first author was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China grant 11971453.
Congwen Liu, Heng Xu

Abstract
. This short note is motivated by an attempt to understand the distinction between the Laplace operator and the hyperbolic Laplacian on the unit ball of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , regarding the Lipschitz continuity of the solutions to the corresponding Dirichlet problems. We investigate the Dirichlet problem { Δ ϑ ​ u = 0 , in ​ 𝔹 n , u = ϕ , on ​ 𝕊 n − 1 , \begin{cases}\Delta_{\vartheta}u=0,&\text{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{n},\\ u=\phi,&\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\end{cases} where Δ ϑ := ( 1 − | x | 2 ) ​ { 1 − | x | 2 4 ​ Δ + ϑ ​ ∑ j = 1 n x j ​ ∂ ∂ x j + ϑ ⁡ ( n 2 − 1 − ϑ ) ​ I } . \Delta_{\vartheta}:=(1-|x|^{2})\bigg\{\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{4}\Delta+\vartheta\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\vartheta\left(\frac{n}{2}-1-\vartheta\right)I\bigg\}. We show that the Lipschitz continuity of boundary data always implies the Lipschitz continuity of the solutions if ϑ > 0 \vartheta>0 , but does not when ϑ ≤ 0 \vartheta\leq 0 .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
be the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
the unit sphere.

It is known, even for
n=2
, that the Lipschitz continuity of the boundary data
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

does not imply the Lipschitz continuity of the solution of the Dirichlet boundary value problem

(1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0,&\text{in }\,\mathbb{B}^{n},\\
u=\phi,&\text{on }\,\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.\end{cases}

See
[
2
, Example 1]
for a counterexample. In
[
2
]
, the authors also showed that
the Lipschitz continuity of
\phi
implies the Lipschitz continuity of the solution
provided that the harmonic extension
P[\phi]
of
\phi
is a
K
-quasiregular mapping.
Kalaj
[
9
]
obtained a related result, but under additional assumption of
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of

\phi
.

Recently, Chen et al.
[
4
]
investigate solutions of the hyperbolic Poisson equation
\Delta_{h}u=\psi
,
where

\Delta_{h}u(x):=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Delta u(x)+2(n-2)(1-|x|^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}u(x)

is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.
Among the other things, the authors showed that, in contrast with the above,
the Lipschitz continuity of
\phi
implies the Lipschitz continuity of the solution
of the Dirichlet boundary value problem

(2)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{h}u=0,&\text{in }\,\mathbb{B}^{n},\\
u=\phi,&\text{on }\,\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\end{cases}

with no other assumption than that
n\geq 3
. See
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
.

This short note is motivated by an attempt to understand the distinction between the two boundary value problems.
To this end, we consider a family of differential operators

\Delta_{\vartheta}u(x):=(1-|x|^{2})\bigg\{\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{4}\Delta u(x)+\vartheta\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}(x)+\vartheta\left(\frac{n}{2}-1-\vartheta\right)u(x)\bigg\}.

We call
\Delta_{\vartheta}
the (Möbius) invariant Laplacians, since

\Delta_{\vartheta}\left\{\left(\mathrm{det}\,\psi^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\frac{n-2-2\theta}{2n}}f(\psi(x))\right\}=\left(\mathrm{det}\psi^{\prime}(x)\right)^{\frac{n-2-2\theta}{2n}}\left(\Delta_{\vartheta}f\right)(\psi(x))

for every
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
and for every Möbius transformation
\psi
(see
[
7
, Proposition 3.2]
).
These differential operators are closely related to polyharmonic functions. In particular, the differential operator

L_{\vartheta}:=\frac{4}{1-|x|^{2}}\Delta_{\vartheta}
plays a crucial role in the modified Almansi representation
for polyharmonic functions in
[
8
]
as well as in the cellular decomposition theorem for polyharmonic functions in

[
3
,
8
]
. We refer the reader to
[
6
,
7
,
11
]
for more information about these differential operators.

We investigate the Dirichlet problem

(3)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{\vartheta}u=0,&\text{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{n},\\
u=\phi,&\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\end{cases}

which includes as special cases the Dirichlet problems (
1
) and (
2
), in view of

\Delta_{0}=\frac{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}{4}\Delta\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta_{\frac{n}{2}-1}=\frac{1}{4}\,\Delta_{h}.

Unlike in
[
2
,
4
]
, for convenience, we work with functions instead of mappings.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
n\geq 2
and
\vartheta>-1/2
.

(i)

If
\vartheta\leq 0
, there exists a Lipschitz continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}

such that the solution of (
3
)
is not Lipschitz continuous.

(ii)

If
\vartheta>0
, the Lipschitz continuity of boundary data always
implies the Lipschitz continuity of the solutions.

Remark 1
.

When
\vartheta=n/2-1
,
\Delta_{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{4}\,\Delta_{h}
and the assumption
\vartheta>0
reads
n>2
.
Theorem
1.1
explains why the assumption
n\geq 3
in
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
is necessary.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we gather some technical lemmas needed for Sections 3 and 4, and fix our
notation.

According to
[
6
]
, the Poisson kernel associated to
\Delta_{\vartheta}
is given by

P_{\vartheta}(x,\zeta):=c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-|x|^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}}{|x-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta}},\qquad(x,\zeta)\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

with
c_{n,\vartheta}:=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\vartheta\right)\Gamma(1+\vartheta)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma(1+2\vartheta)}
and the Poisson integral of a function
\phi\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is given by

(4)

\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi](x):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}P_{\vartheta}(x,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta),

where
\sigma
is the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
normalized so that
\sigma(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=1
.

We record the following from
[
6
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Dirichlet problem (
3
)
has a solution for all
\phi\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
if and only if
\vartheta>-1/2
.
Moreover, when
\vartheta>-1/2
, the solution is given by
u=\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]
.

Straightforward calculation yields the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
k=1,\ldots,n
, we have

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(x,\zeta)~=~

\displaystyle-2(1+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-|x|^{2})^{2\vartheta}x_{k}|x-\zeta|^{2}}{|x-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}

\displaystyle\quad-~(n+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-|x|^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}(x_{k}-\zeta_{k})}{|x-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}.

A number of hypergeometric functions will appear throughout.
We use the classical notation

\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(a,\,b;\,c;\,\lambda\right):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}

with
c\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots
, where
(a)_{k}
stands for the Pochhammer
symbol, which is defined as

(a)_{k}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }k=0,\\
a(a+1)\ldots(a+k-1),&\text{ if }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

We refer to
[
1
, Chapter 2]
for the properties of these
functions. Here, we only record two formulas for later reference.

(6)

\>{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(a,\,b;\,c;\,\lambda\right)~=~

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)^{c-a-b}\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(c-a,\,c-b;\,c;\,\lambda\right).

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(a,\,b;\,c;\,\lambda\right)~=~

\displaystyle\frac{ab}{c}\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(a+1,\,b+1;\,c+1;\,\lambda\right).

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

(8)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}P_{\vartheta}(x,\zeta)~d\sigma(\zeta)~=~c_{n,\vartheta}\,\>{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(-\vartheta,\,\frac{n}{2}-1-\vartheta;\,\frac{n}{2};\,|x|^{2}\right)

and for
k=1,\ldots,n
,

(9)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(x,\zeta)~d\sigma(\zeta)~=~C(n,\vartheta)\,\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(1-\vartheta,\,\frac{n}{2}-\vartheta;\,\frac{n}{2}+1;\,|x|^{2}\right)x_{k},

with

C(n,\vartheta):=\frac{-2\vartheta(n-2-2\vartheta)}{n}\,c_{n,\vartheta}.

Proof.

The first identity follows from the formula (
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
)

(10)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|x-\zeta|^{2\lambda}}~=~\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\lambda,\,\lambda-\frac{n}{2}+1;\,\frac{n}{2};\,|x|^{2}\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{n},

and (
6
). The identity (
9
) follows by
differentiating both sides of (
8
) and applying (
7
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

When
\vartheta>0
, the functions

x~\longmapsto~\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(x,\zeta)~d\sigma(\zeta),\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

are all bounded on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Proof.

It follows from (
9
) that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(x,\zeta)~d\sigma(\zeta)\right|~\lesssim~

\>{}_{2}\!F_{1}\left(1-\vartheta,\,\frac{n}{2}-\vartheta;\,\frac{n}{2}+1;\,|x|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle~=~

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-\vartheta)_{j}(\frac{n}{2}-\vartheta)_{j}}{j!\,(\frac{n}{2}+1)_{j}}|x|^{2j}.

Here and below, we use the notation
f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
if there exists a uniform constant
C>0
,
such
f_{1}\leq Cf_{2}
. Also,
f_{1}\approx f_{2}
means that
f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
and
f_{2}\lesssim f_{1}
.
Note that the coefficients in the last series are of order
j^{-1-2\vartheta}
, as
j\to\infty
.
This proves the assertion.
∎

The following lemma may be well known, we include a short proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
p>q\geq 0
. We have

(11)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\zeta-e_{1}|^{q}}{|\zeta-re_{1}|^{n-1+p}}\,d\sigma(\zeta)~\lesssim~\frac{1}{(1-r)^{p-q}},\qquad r\in[0,1).

Proof.

The special case
q=0
of the lemma is well-known, see for instance
[
5
, Lemma 2.9]
.

For
q>0
, noting that

|\zeta-e_{1}|^{q}\leq\left(|\zeta-re_{1}|+|re_{1}-e_{1}|\right)^{q}\leq 2^{q}\big[|\zeta-re_{1}|^{q}+(1-r)^{q}\big],

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\zeta-e_{1}|^{q}}{|\zeta-re_{1}|^{n-1+p}}\,d\sigma(\zeta)~\lesssim~

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\zeta-re_{1}|^{n-1+p-q}}

\displaystyle\quad~+~(1-r)^{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\zeta-re_{1}|^{n-1+p}},

and (
11
) follows from the special case
q=0
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
, Part (i)

In the case
\vartheta=0
, as is mentioned in the introduction,
[
2
, Example 1]
is such an example.

Now we assume that
\vartheta<0
. Take
\phi\equiv 1
. By (
9
) and (
7
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi](x)~=~

\displaystyle C(n,\vartheta)\,\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(1-\vartheta,\,\frac{n}{2}-\vartheta;\,\frac{n}{2}+1;\,|x|^{2}\right)\,|x|^{2}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle C(n,\vartheta)\,\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\vartheta,\,1+\vartheta;\,\frac{n}{2}+1;\,|x|^{2}\right)\,|x|^{2}\,(1-|x|^{2})^{2\vartheta}.

Since
\vartheta<0
, by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
2.4
, we see that the hypergeometric function in the last line is bounded both from above and below, hence

(12)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi](x)~\approx~(1-|x|^{2})^{2\vartheta}

near the boundary
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.
Note that

\big|\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]\right)(x)\big|~\geq~\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi](x)\right|.

So (
12
) implies that
\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]\right)
is unbounded in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]
is not Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Remark 2
.

When
n=3
, there is a more explicit example:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[1](x)~=~

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}+\vartheta\right)\Gamma(1+\vartheta)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)\Gamma(1+2\vartheta)}\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(-\vartheta,\,\frac{1}{2}-\vartheta;\,\frac{3}{2};\,|x|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle 2^{-1-2\vartheta}\frac{(1+|x|)^{1+2\vartheta}-(1-|x|)^{1+2\vartheta}}{|x|},

which is clearly not Lipschitz continuous when
\vartheta<0
. Here, in the last equality we have used the formula (see
[
10
, p. 39]
)

(1+z)^{1-2a}-(1-z)^{1-2a}=2z(1-2a)\>_{2}\!F_{1}\left(a,\,a+\frac{1}{2};\,\frac{3}{2};\,z^{2}\right).

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
, Part (ii)

Let
\phi
be a Lipschitz continuous function on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, i.e.,

|\phi(\zeta)-\phi(\eta)|\leq L|\zeta-\eta|
for some constant
L
and all
\zeta,\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

We shall show that

(13)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]\right)(x)|<\infty,

which would clearly imply the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]
.

To do this, we note that

(14)

\left|\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]\right)\right|=\left|\left(\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi\circ T]\right)\right)\circ T^{-1}\right|

holds for every
\phi\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and every orthogonal transformation
T
.
Also, given any orthogonal transformation
T
, the function
\phi\circ T
is also
Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, with the same Lipschitz constant.
For any
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
, we can choose an orthogonal transformation
T
such that
T^{-1}x=re_{1}
with
r=|x|
,
so that

\left|\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]\right)(x)\right|=\left|\left(\nabla\left(\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi\circ T]\right)\right)(re_{1})\right|.

We are reduced to prove the following.

Claim 1
.

\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1})
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, are bounded
functions of
r
on
[0,1)
.

We consider two different cases.

Case 1
:
k\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
.

By taking
x=re_{1}
in (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1},\zeta)~d\sigma(\zeta)~=~0,

and hence

(15)

\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1},\zeta)[\phi(\zeta)-\phi(e_{1})]~d\sigma(\zeta).

Now, taking
x=re_{1}
in (
5
), we get

\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1},\zeta)=(n+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\,\frac{(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}\zeta_{k}}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}.

Substituting this into (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1})\right|~\lesssim~

\displaystyle(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\zeta_{k}|\,|\phi(\zeta)-\phi(e_{1})|}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}d\sigma(\zeta)

\displaystyle~\lesssim~

\displaystyle(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\zeta-e_{1}|^{2}d\sigma(\zeta)}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}},

where we used the condition
|\phi(\zeta)-\phi(e_{1})|\leq L|\zeta-e_{1}|
and the easy observation that
|\zeta_{k}|\leq|\zeta-e_{1}|
in the last inequality.
This, together with Lemma
2.5
, shows that
\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{k}}(re_{1})
is a bounded
function of
r
on
[0,1)
.

Case 2
:
k=1
.

It follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{1}}(re_{1},\zeta)\right|~\leq~

\displaystyle 2(1+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{2\vartheta}}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta}}~+~(n+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}|r-\zeta_{1}|}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle 2(1+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{2\vartheta}}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta}}~+~(n+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}|\zeta-e_{1}|}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad~+~(n+2\vartheta)c_{n,\vartheta}\frac{(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}(1-r)}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}.

Combining with the condition
|\phi(\zeta)-\phi(e_{1})|\leq L|\zeta-e_{1}|
, this yields

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{1}}

\displaystyle(re_{1})\bigg|

\displaystyle~\leq~

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{1}}(re_{1},\zeta)\phi(e_{1})d\sigma(\zeta)\right|~+~\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{1}}(re_{1},\zeta)[\phi(\zeta)-\phi(e_{1})]d\sigma(\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim~

\displaystyle\underbrace{|\phi(e_{1})|\,\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{\partial P_{\vartheta}}{\partial x_{1}}(re_{1},\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)\right|}_{I_{1}}~+~\underbrace{(1-r^{2})^{2\vartheta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta}}}_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad~+~\underbrace{(1-r^{2})^{1+2\vartheta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{|\zeta-e_{1}|}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}d\sigma(\zeta)}_{I_{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad~+~\underbrace{(1-r^{2})^{2+2\vartheta}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|re_{1}-\zeta|^{n+2\vartheta+2}}}_{I_{4}}.

Since
\vartheta>0
, by Lemma
2.4
,
I_{1}
is bounded. Also, in view of Lemma
2.5
,
I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}
are all bounded.
Consequently,
\frac{\partial\mathcal{P}_{\vartheta}[\phi]}{\partial x_{1}}(re_{1})
is a bounded
function of
r
on
[0,1)
.

This concludes the proof of the claim and hence the proof of the theorem.

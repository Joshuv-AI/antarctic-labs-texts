Constructions of symplectic forms on 4-manifolds
Peter Lambert-Cole

Abstract
. Given a symplectic 4-manifold ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) with rational symplectic form, Auroux constructed branched coverings to ( ℂ ​ ℙ 2 , ω F ​ S ) (\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS}) . By modifying a previous construction of Lambert-Cole–Meier–Starkston, we prove that the branch locus in ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} can be assumed holomorphic in a neighborhood of the spine of the standard trisection of ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} . Consequently, the symplectic 4-manifold ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) admits a cohomologous symplectic form that is Kähler in a neighborhood of the 2-skeleton of X X . We define the Picard group of holomorphic line bundles over the holomorphic 2-skeleton. We then investigate Hodge theory and apply harmonic spinors to construct holomorphic sections over the Kähler subset.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

The motivation for this work is the geography problem for symplectic 4-manifolds and the symplectic BMY inequality. The Bogomolev-Miyaoka-Yau inequality for complex surfaces states that if
X
is a surface of general type, then

(1.1)

c_{1}^{2}\leq 3c_{2}

Miyaoka’s proof proceeds via complex algebraic geometry
[
Miy77
]
, while Yau’s proof derives from his solution to the Calabi conjecture
[
Yau77
]
. It is conjectured that the same inequality holds for closed symplectic 4-manifolds.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed, minimal symplectic 4-manifold satisfying
c_{1}(X)^{2}\geq 0
. Then
c_{1}(X)^{2}\leq 3c_{2}(X)
.

One aim of this paper is to investigate whether it is possible to mimic these methods from complex algebraic geometry in the symplectic case. The approach we take it to construct explicit symplectic forms that appear well-adapted to the smooth topological problem. We give two constructions, of a so-called
Kähler trisection
and a
symplectic 1-cocycle
. The underlying approach is the same in both cases, but produces slightly different geometric structures.

1.2.
Construction

The constructions in this paper are slight variations of the construction in
[
LCMS21
]
, with modifications according to the geometric structures of interest.

Given an integral symplectic form
\omega
on
X
, Auroux constructed branched coverings
\pi_{k}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{2}
for
k\gg 0

[
Aur00
]
. The symplectic form
k[\omega]
can be recovered by pulling back the Fubini-Study form
\omega_{FS}
by
\pi_{k}
and perturbing to be nondegenerate. The smooth topology of the branched covering depends
a priori
on the integral cohomology class of
\omega
and is independent of choices for
k
sufficiently large. Auroux and Katzarkov strengthened this result, so that the branch locus in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is a so-called quasiholomorphic curve
[
AK00
]
. Such a branch locus can be algebraically encoded by a factorization of the full twist in the braid group. In
[
LC23
]
, it was shown that the branch locus
\mathcal{R}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
can be smoothly isotoped into transverse bridge position with respect to the standard trisection of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. The trisection of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
then pulls back to a trisection of
X
, along with the relevant geometric structure.

1.3.
Holomorphic trisections and Kähler-like forms

An
almost-Kähler manifold
is a triple
(X,\omega,J)
where
\omega
is a symplectic form and
J
is an almost-complex structure satisfying the compatibility condition
\omega(JX,JY)=\omega(X,Y)
everywhere. The pair
(\omega,J)
determine a Riemannian metric
g(X,Y)=\omega(X,JY)
on
X
. The pair
(\omega,J)
determines a Kähler structure if
J
is integrable.

Our first main result is a construction of almost-Kähler structures that are integrable over the interesting part of the smooth topology of
X
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed, symplectic 4-manifold with rational symplectic form. There exists an almost-Kahler structure
(X,\omega^{\prime},J)
with
[\omega^{\prime}]=[\omega]\in H^{2}_{DR}(X)
and a smooth handle decomposition of
X
such that
J
is integrable over the 2-skeleton.

This follows immediately from the next theorem, as a trisection determines an ‘inside-out’ smooth handle decomposition contained in the spine of the trisection.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed, symplectic 4-manifold with rational symplectic form. There exists an almost-Kahler structure
(X,\omega^{\prime},J)
with
[\omega^{\prime}]=[\omega]\in H^{2}_{DR}(X)
and a trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
such that
J
is integrable in a neighborhood of the spine
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{T}
.

Moreover, there exists a decomposition
X=\nu(\mathcal{S})\cup Z
such that

(1)

(\nu(\mathcal{S}),\omega^{\prime},J)
is Kähler with strictly pseudoconcave boundary,

(2)

Z
is a (disconnected) subcritical Weinstein domain.

There is a decomposition of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
as in Theorem
1.3
depicted in terms of its moment polytope in Figure
1
. The standard trisection of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup Z_{3}
arises by taking the unit bidisk in each of the three standard affine charts. Letting
Z^{\prime}_{\lambda}\subset Z_{\lambda}
be a small shrinking with strictly pseudoconvex boundary, we let
Z=Z^{\prime}_{1}\cup Z^{\prime}_{2}\cup Z^{\prime}_{3}
and
\nu(\mathcal{S})
be its complement, which has strictly pseudoconcave boundary. Pulling this decomposition back by a symplectic branched covering yields the decomposition of Theorem
1.3
.

.
The decomposition of Theorem
1.3
applied to the moment polytope of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

Since every subcritical Weinstein domain embeds symplectically in
(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{std})
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed, symplectic 4-manifold with rational symplectic form. There exists an almost-Kahler structure
(X,\omega^{\prime},J)
with
[\omega^{\prime}]=[\omega]\in H^{2}_{DR}(X)
such that
J
is integrable, except on three trivial symplectic cylinders of the form
\left([a,b]\times Y_{\lambda},d(e^{t}\alpha)\right)
, where
Y_{\lambda}
is
S^{3}
or
\#_{k}S^{1}\times S^{2}
for some
k>0
.

Remark 1.5
.

The subtlety that
[\omega^{\prime}]
and
[\omega]
are merely cohomologous is due to the fact that we appeal to the result of
[
LC23
]
, where the branch locus is only
smoothly
isotoped into transverse bridge position, as opposed to the Hamiltonian isotopy used in
[
LCMS21
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

Viewed with respect to the inward-pointing coorientation, Theorem
1.3
states that each boundary component admits a strongly pseudoconvex CR-structure. If this CR-structure can be holomorphically embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, then the Kähler structure could be extended across the subcritical Weinstein domain. However, generic CR-structures on 3-manifolds do not holomorphically embed in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and furthermore the existence of non-Kähler symplectic 4-manifolds implies that these boundary components cannot always be filled in holomorphically.

Since
J
is integrable over the spine, we can attempt to mimic objects from algebraic geometry. In particular, we introduce the Picard group
\text{Pic}(X,J,\mathcal{T})
of holomorphic line bundles (Definition
5.1
). In fact, every isomorphism class of complex line bundle admits a holomorphic representative in some neighborhood of the 2-skeleton.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic 4-manifold with rational symplectic form. There exists an almost-Kähler structure
(X,\omega^{\prime},J)
, a trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
, and decomposition
X=\nu(\mathcal{S})\cup Z
as in Theorem
1.3
such that for any
\alpha\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, there is some open subneighborhood
\nu(\mathcal{S})^{\prime}\subset\nu(\mathcal{S})
of the 2-skeleton and a holomorphic line bundle
L
over
\nu(\mathcal{S})^{\prime}
such that
c_{1}(L)=\alpha
.

Neither Corollary
1.2
nor Corollary
1.4
require a smooth trisection decomposition, only Auroux’s branched covering result. One only needs to apply Lemma
4.7
to the 1-skeleton of the branch curve to realize the holomorphic structures. However, constructing a holomorphic structure on every smooth line bundle as in Theorem
1.7
does require the smooth trisection.

We then attempt Hodge theory over the integrable region
\nu(\mathcal{S})
, viewed as a Kähler manifold with pseudoconcave boundary. We can recover the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations in subcritical dimensions (Theorem
5.2
). To extract more information, we use harmonic spinors. Every holomorphic line bundle in the Picard group can be extended to a complex line bundle on
X
. After choosing a Hermitian connection
A
, we obtain a
\text{Spin}^{\mathbb{C}}
Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{A}=\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A}+\mathcal{D}^{-}_{A}
on the bundle
\Lambda^{0,*}(L)
; the index of this operator is given by the classical Riemann-Roch formula. Over the integrable region
\nu(\mathcal{S})
, the space of even harmonic spinors
\text{ker}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A})
decomposes into
L
-valued
(0,0)
- and
(0,2)
-forms. In particular, if
\chi(L)>0
, we obtain holomorphic sections of
L
or its Serre dual
K-L
over
\nu(\mathcal{S})
. The vanishing locus of such a section is a symplectic surface in
\nu(\mathcal{S})
whose boundary is transverse to the contact structure on
\partial\nu(\mathcal{S})
.

1.4.
Symplectic cone

The
symplectic cone
of a smooth 4-manifold
X
is the subset of
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
that can be realized by symplectic forms. Similarly, the
Kähler cone
of a complex surface
X
is the subset of
H^{1,1}(X)
that can be realized by Kähler forms. Determining the symplectic cone of a general 4-manifold remains an open problem in general, although it is understood for some classes of 4-manifolds. Seiberg-Witten invariants give some restrictions on the symplectic cone. And determining the symplectic cone of
X\#k\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}
is connected to the problem of symplectic ball packing
[
Bir01
]
. In the Kähler case, the Nakai-Moishezon criterion for ampleness of holomorphic line bundles gives information about the Kähler cone. There exists a symplectic Nakai-Moishezon-type criterion in the case when
b_{2}^{+}=1

[
Bir97
,
Bir01
,
LL01
]
, but there is no corresponding general theorem.

Using harmonic spinors and Theorem
1.7
, we can construct partial holomorphic sections of over the spine
\nu(\mathcal{S})
. In particular, if
D^{2}>0
then either
nD
or
K-nD
admit many holomorphic sections over the spine
\nu(\mathcal{S})
. The projective holomorphic embedding

\iota:\nu(\mathcal{S})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{N}

determined by this linear system gives a Kähler form
\iota^{*}(\omega_{FS})
over the spine
\nu(\mathcal{S})
that dominates the contact structures along the boundary
\partial\nu(\mathcal{S})
. In other words, it gives
\nu(\mathcal{S})
the structure of a weak symplectic cap with
\omega
in a prescribed cohomology class.

If this form satisfies the stronger condition of being contact-type along the boundary — in other words, being a
strong
symplectic cap — then the boundary components can be filled in with symplectic 1-handlebodies to obtain a symplectic form
\omega
on the closed 4-manifold
X
with
\omega
in a prescribed integral cohomology class of positive square.

However, it is not immediately obvious how to ensure this stronger condition or how to incorporate the restrictions from Seiberg-Witten theory.

1.5.
Symplectic 1-cocycles

The notion of a
Weinstein trisection
of
(X,\omega)
was introduced in
[
LCM22
]
. It is a trisection decomposition
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup X_{3}
such that there exists a subcritical Weinstein structure on each sector
X_{\lambda}
compatible with the global symplectic form. Lambert-Cole, Meier and Starkston later showed that every closed, symplectic 4-manifold admits a Weinstein trisection
[
LCMS21
]
. A converse condition was introduced in
[
LC22a
]
to ensure that a given trisection admits a global symplectic structure.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
X
be a closed, oriented 4-manifold and let
\mathcal{T}
be a trisection of
X
with central surface
\Sigma
and 3-dimensional sectors
H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}
. A
symplectic 1-cocycle
for
(X,\mathcal{T})
is a triple
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})
of closed, nonvanishing 1-forms on the three handlebodies such that the restrictions
\{\beta_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda|_{\Sigma}}\}
satisfy the following conditions

(1)

the cocycle condition:

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}=0

(2)

the pairwise positivity condition

\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}\geq 0

(3)

they all vanish at the same
2g-2
points
b_{1},\dots,b_{2g-2}
in
\Sigma
and there exists a smooth function
f:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and neighborhoods
\{b_{i}\in U_{i}\}
such that
f|_{U_{i}}=\pm 1
and
\sum_{i}f(b_{i})=0
and

\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}=\beta_{\lambda}+d(sf)

on a collar neighborhood of
\partial H_{\lambda}=\Sigma
.

Recall that a boundary component of a symplectic manifold is
strongly concave
if there exists an inward-pointing Liouville vector field
\rho
. This is equivalent to the criterion that the form
\alpha=\omega(\rho,-)
, which is a primitive for
\omega
, is a negative contact form on that boundary component (with respect to the
outward-pointing
coorientation).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
X
be a closed, oriented 4-manifold and let
\mathcal{T}
be a trisection of
X
with central surface
\Sigma
. Let
(\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2},\widetilde{\beta}_{3})
be a symplectic 1-cocycle for
(X,\mathcal{T})
. Then

(1)

there is a symplectic form
\omega
on
\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
and three inward-pointing Liouville vector fields for
\omega
along each of the three boundary components
\widehat{Y}_{1},\widehat{Y}_{2},\widehat{Y}_{3}
.

(2)

if each of the induced contact structures
(\widehat{Y}_{\lambda},\widehat{\xi}_{\lambda})
are tight, then there exists a global symplectic form
\omega
on
X
.

In this paper, we prove two important existence statements about symplectic 1-cocycles and trisections. In particular, we show that the criterion in
[
LC22a
]
is completely general.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a closed, symplectic 4-manifold. Then

(1)

there exists a trisection
\mathcal{T}
and symplectic 1-cocycle for
(X,\mathcal{T})
such that all three induced contact structures on
\partial\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
are tight.

(2)

every stabilization of
\mathcal{T}
admits a symplectic 1-cocycle such that the induced contact structures on
\partial\nu(H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3})
are tight.

1.6.
Comparison

A key fact is that any two smooth trisections
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
of
X
admit a common stabilization
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. The branch locus in the symplectic constructions described here can be perturbed to achieve a smooth stabilization of the trisection in the cover. Consequently, we can directly compare two distinct symplectic structures on some
X
via a common trisection.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
be two rational symplectic forms on
X
. There exists a single smooth trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
such that the conclusions of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.10
hold for both
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, with respect to
\mathcal{T}
.

2.
Trisections

Trisections are 4-dimensional generalization of Heegaard splittings of 3-manifolds. In this section, we will describe the basic terminology and background results of the theory. At this point, there is quite a voluminous literature defining the basic building blocks of the theory:

(1)

trisections of 4-manifolds
[
GK16
]

(2)

relative trisections of 4-manifolds with boundary
[
CGPC18
]

(3)

bridge trisections of surfaces
[
MZ17
,
MZ18
]

(4)

constructing branched coverings via surfaces in bridge position
[
LCM22
,
LCMS21
]

(5)

bridge trisections for properly embedded surfaces in 4-manifolds with boundary
[
Mei20
]

The two key ideas we exploit in this paper are:

(1)

Trisections pull back under branched coverings when the branch locus is in bridge position. This has been used to construct trisections of projective surfaces
[
LCM22
]
and symplectic 4-manifolds
[
LCMS21
]
.

(2)

In a simple branched covering, an (interior) perturbation of the branch locus downstairs results in an (interior) stabilization of the trisection upstairs.

2.1.
Trisections

Let
X
be a closed, smooth 4-manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

A
(g;k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
-
trisection

\mathcal{T}
of
X
is a decomposition
X=Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}\cup Z_{3}
such that for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,

(1)

Each
Z_{\lambda}
is diffeomorphic to
\natural_{k_{\lambda}}(S^{1}\times B^{3})
for
k_{\lambda}\geq 0
,

(2)

Each double intersection
H_{\lambda}=Z_{\lambda-1}\cap Z_{\lambda}
is a 3-dimensional, genus
g
handlebody, and

(3)

the triple intersection
\Sigma=Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}\cap Z_{3}
is a closed, genus
g
surface.

We refer to
\Sigma
as the
central surface
of the trisection, the genus
g
of
\Sigma
as the
genus
of the trisection, and the union
H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}
as the
spine
of the trisection.

A trisection of
X
can be encoded by a
trisection diagram
that describes how each
H_{\lambda}
is attached to
\Sigma

[
GK16
, Definition 3]
. In particular, since
H_{\lambda}
is a 1-handlebody, the gluing is determined by how a complete system of compressing disks is attached to
\Sigma
. These compressing disks are attached along a cut system of curves in
\Sigma
and these curves can be depicted on
\Sigma
itself.

2.2.
Trisections of spheres

The
n

\mathbb{R}^{2}

Definition 2.2
.

The

D=D^{s}_{1}\cup D^{s}_{2}\cup D^{s}_{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the unit disk is defined by choosing the following subsets (in radial coordinates):

\displaystyle D^{s}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{0\leq\theta\leq\frac{2\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle D^{s}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{2\pi}{3}\leq\theta\leq\frac{4\pi}{3}\right\},

\displaystyle D^{s}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{4\pi}{3}\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\right\}.

It is symmetric under rotation by

(
[

)

The

of

\mathcal{T}_{std}=\{\pi^{-1}(D^{s}_{i})\cap S^{k}\}

Example 2.4
.

The standard trisection of
S^{2}
. The triple intersection of
\mathcal{T}_{std}
consists of the North and South poles; the double intersections are three meridians connecting the poles; and the sectors are the three bigons bounded by the pairs of meridians.

Example 2.5
.

The standard trisection of
S^{3}
. The triple intersection consists of a single

, unknotted in
S^{3}
; each double intersection is a
D^{2}
Seifert disk for the unknot; and each sector of the trisection is a 3-ball. In addition, we can give two other useful interpretations:

(1)

The lens space

is decomposed into three lenses: i.e take

(2)

The genus 0 open book decomposition of

. Recall that
S^{3}
has an open book decomposition
(B,\phi)
where the binding

\phi:S^{3}\smallsetminus\nu(B)\cong S^{1}\times D^{2}\rightarrow S^{1}

\left\{\phi^{-1}(\theta_{1}),\phi^{-1}(\theta_{2}),\phi^{-1}(\theta_{3})\right\}

The following lemma is immediate from the definition.

Lemma 2.6

Let

.

2.3.
Connected sum and stabilization

.

Let

Recall from Example

A

There is an equivalence between trisection decompositions and handle decompositions

(

Let

(2)

2.5.
Bridge position for surfaces

Meier and Zupan generalized bridge position for links in a 3-manifold to bridge position for surfaces in a 4-manifold
[
MZ17
,
MZ18
]
. Meier has recently extended the theory of bridge trisections to properly embedded surfaces in 4-manifolds with boundary
[
Mei20
]
. We will give a brief overview, focusing only on the relevant case
X\cong B^{4}
.

A properly embedded tangle
\tau
in 3-manifold with boundary
M
is
trivial
if the arcs of
\tau
can be simultaneously isotoped to lie in
\partial M
. A
disk-tangle

\mathcal{D}\subset X
in a 4-manifold is a collection of properly embedded disks. A disk-tangle is
trivial
if the disks can be simultaneously isotoped to lie in the boundary. All of the trivial tangles and disk-tangles we consider will be of the following form. Let
\Sigma
be a compact surface with nonempty boundary and let
Q
be a collection of points in
\Sigma
. Then

(1)

Q\times[0,1]
is a trival tangle in
\Sigma\times[0,1]
, and

(2)

Q\times[0,1]^{2}
is a trivial disk-tangle in
\Sigma\times[0,1]^{2}

Recall that a link
L\subset Y
is in
bridge position
with respect to a Heegaard splitting
Y=H_{1}\cup_{\Sigma}H_{2}
if each intersection
\tau_{\lambda}=L\cap H_{\lambda}
is a trivial tangle. Extending, we say that a properly embedded, closed surface
\mathcal{K}
in a closed 4-manifold

is in
bridge position
with respect to a trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
if

(1)

\mathcal{K}
intersects the central surface
\Sigma
of the trisection transversely in
2b
points,

(2)

\mathcal{K}
intersects each handlebody
H_{\lambda}
along a trivial tangle
\tau_{\lambda}
.

(3)

\mathcal{K}
intersects each 4-dimensional sector
X_{\lambda}
along a trivial disk tangle.

The parameter
b
is the
bridge index
of the surface
\mathcal{K}
.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is a properly embedded surface in
B^{4}
, let
\mathcal{T}
be a relative trisection of
B^{4}
, and suppose that
\partial\mathcal{K}
is braided with respect to the induced open book decomposition on
\partial B^{4}
.

Definition 2.18
.

We say that
\mathcal{K}
is in

with respect to
\mathcal{T}
if

(1)

\mathcal{K}
intersects the central surface
\Sigma
transversely in

(2)

\mathcal{K}
intersects each handlebody

along a trivial tangle, and

(3)

\mathcal{K}
intersects each sector
X_{\lambda}
along a trivial disk-tangle.

If

is the braid index of
\partial\mathcal{K}
, we say that

is the

of
\mathcal{K}
.

2.6.
Bridge perturbation

For motivation, recall that a
bridge perturbation
of a link
L\subset Y
in bridge position is a local modification that increases the bridge index within the same ambient isotopy class. We can describe the move abstractly and in coordinates:

(1)

Abstractly
Near a bridge point
x\in\Sigma
, we can choose a local neighborhood
U
and coordinates
(x,y,)
on
U
such that (a) the Heegaard surface intersects
U
along the
xy
-plane, and (b) the link
L
intersects
U
along the
z
-axis. Now choose an arc
\delta
in
U
, such that one endpoint lies on
L
and the other on
\Sigma
. This determines a finger-move isotopy of
L
through
\Sigma
.

(2)

In coordinates
. In the coordinates on
U
from above, we can isotope
L
to be the linear graph
L=(t,0,t)
. By an isotopy, we can further isotope
the link to be the cubic graph
L^{\prime}=(t,0,t^{3}-\epsilon t)
.

We can define an analogous procedure for surfaces in bridge position. Following
[
Mei20
, Definition 9.9]
, we first define a
finger-perturbation
abstractly.

Definition 2.19

(
[
Mei20
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a surface in relative bridge position with respect to a relative trisection
\mathcal{T}
. Let
x
be a bridge point and
U
a neighborhood of
X
such that
\mathcal{K}
intersects
U
along a small disk. The intersection of
\mathcal{K}
with
U\cap H_{\lambda}
is an arc
\tau_{\lambda}
with one endpoint at
x
. A
sector-
\lambda
finger perturbation
of
\mathcal{K}
is the surface
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
obtained by a finger-move isotopy along a small arc
\delta
in
U\cap H_{\lambda+1}
, with one endpoint on
\tau_{\lambda+1}
and the other in
\Sigma
.

The finger move increases the bridge index by 1; it increases the number of components of
\mathcal{K}\cap X_{\lambda+1}
by one (since the sector
X_{\lambda+1}
of the trisection is opposite to the handlebody
H_{\lambda}
); and it fixes the number of components of
\mathcal{K}\cap X_{\lambda-1}
and
\mathcal{K}\cap X_{\lambda}
.

We can also describe this move visually in 3 dimensions by projecting away one dimension. Let
\mathcal{K}
be a surface in bridge position with respect to a trisection
\mathcal{T}
. We can locally choose coordinates
(x,y,z)
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
near a bridge point
\mathcal{K}\cap\Sigma
such that

\mathcal{K}=\{z=0\}

and the trisection is given by pulling back the standard trisection of the
xy
-plane by the projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

A bridge perturbation consists of locally replacing
\mathcal{K}
by

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\{x=z^{3}-yz\}

Intuitively, this is accomplished by
pleating
the surface
\mathcal{K}
through the center of the trisection.

Restricted to
\mathcal{K}
, the projection
\pi
is a homeomorphism. Restricted to
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
, however, there is a critical locus that projects to a semicubical cusp
(3t^{2},-2t^{3})
. Over the interior of the cusp, the map is 3-to-1; over the exterior it is 1-to-1; at the origin there is a cusp singularity; and everywhere else is a fold singularity.

2.7.
Branched covers of surfaces in relative bridge position

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
X
be smooth 4-manifolds with boundary, let
\mathcal{K}\subset X
be a properly embedded surface, and let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be an
n
-fold simple branched covering determined by a homomorphism
\rho:\pi_{1}(X\setminus\mathcal{K})\rightarrow S_{n}
.

(1)

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is a relative trisection of
X
and
\mathcal{K}
is in relative bridge position with respect to
\mathcal{T}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}=\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{T})
is a relative trisection of
\widetilde{X}
.

(2)

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
isotopic to
\mathcal{K}
by a sector-
\lambda
interior bridge perturbation. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
be the induced relative trisections of
\widetilde{X}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
by a sector-
(\lambda+1)
interior stabilization.

Proof.

Part (1) is the relative version of branched covering constructions of trisections, such as
[
LCMS21
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
LCM22
, Proposition 3.1]
, and
[
MZ18
, Proposition 13]
.

An interior bridge perturbation increases the bridge index by 1 and the number of intersection points
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cap\Sigma
by two. Therefore, since the map
\pi
is a simple branched covering, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula implies that the Euler characteristic of the central surface
\widetilde{\Sigma}
upstairs decreases by 2 or equivalently the genus increases by 1. The number of components of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cap X_{\lambda-1}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cap X_{\lambda}
stays the same, while the number of components of
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cap X_{\lambda+1}
increases by 1. Therefore, the Euler characteristic of
\widetilde{X}_{\lambda-1},\widetilde{X}_{\lambda}
stay the same; since they are 1-handlebodies this implies that their smooth topology stays the same. However, the Euler characteristic of
\widetilde{X}_{\lambda+1}
decreases by 1. Thus
\widetilde{X}^{\prime}_{\lambda+1}
is obtained from
\widetilde{X}_{\lambda+1}
by attaching a 1-handle. In summary, this exactly corresponds to the trisection stabilization.
∎

2.8.
Singular surfaces

The branch loci for symplectic branched coverings
\pi:(X,\omega)\rightarrow(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS})
are singular surfaces, with self-transverse nodes and cusp singularities that are locally a cone on the right-handed trefoil. We give a brief summary of
[
LCMS21
, Sections 3 and 4]
, which extends the (bridge) trisection machinery to singular surfaces.

A
singular knotted surface

\mathcal{K}
in
X
is the image of a smooth immersion, away from a finite number of critical points, such that all multiple points are transverse double points and in a small neighborhood of each critical value, the surface is diffeomorphic to the cone on a knot in
S^{3}
. A
singular disk tangle

\mathcal{D}
in
Z
bounded by
L\subset Y=\partial D
is a collection of properly embedded singular disks such that (a) each component of
\mathcal{D}
bounded by an unknot is smoothly embedded and boundary-parallel; (b) for each component of
\mathcal{D}
with knotted boundary
K
, there exists an embedded 4-ball
B
in
Z
such that
B\cap Y
is a 3-ball containing
K
and the intersection of
\mathcal{D}
with
B
is a smooth cone on
K
; (c) two components of
\mathcal{D}
are disjoint if their boundary knots are split and intersect transversely, away from the singular points, if their boundary knots are linked. A singular surface
\mathcal{K}
is
X
is in
bridge trisected position
with respect to a trisection if it is transverse to the central surface; the intersection
\tau_{\lambda}=H_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{K}
is a boundary-parallel tangle; and the intersection of
\mathcal{K}
with each 4-dimensional sector is a singular disk tangle.

A
singular branched covering

\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
with singular branch locus
\mathcal{K}\subset X
is a proper, smooth map such that (a) away from the singular points of
\mathcal{K}
, the map is a branched covering, and (b) for each singular point
p
of
\mathcal{K}
, there exists a 4-ball neighborhood
U
such that
\pi{{}^{-}1}(U)
is a collection of
\widetilde{U}_{!}\cup\dots\cup\widetilde{U}_{k}
and the restriction of
\pi
to
\widetilde{U}_{i}
is either a diffeomorphism or the cone on a branched covering
S^{3}\rightarrow S^{3}
. A trisection
\mathcal{T}
of
X
pulls back to a trisection of
\widetilde{X}
under a singular branched covering, provided that
\widetilde{X}
is connected
[
LCMS21
, Theorem 4.1]
.

3.
Existence of symplectic 1-cocycles

3.1.
Standard trisection of
(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS})

The standard trisection of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is induced by the toric structure (see citations). In homogeneous coordinates
\{[z_{1}:z_{2}:z_{3}]\}
on
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\{|z_{\lambda-1}|=1\,\text{ and }|z_{\lambda}|,|z_{\lambda+1}|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle H_{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=\{|z_{\lambda-1}|=|z_{\lambda}|=1\,\text{ and }|z_{\lambda+1}|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=\{|z_{1}|=|z_{2}|=|z_{3}|=1\}

The central surface
\Sigma
is a torus. Since its Euler characteristic is 0, every harmonic 1-form is nonvanishing. Therefore, symplectic data for this trisection consists of a triple of 1-forms
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
on the handlebodies such that their restrictions to
\Sigma
satisfy

\beta_{\lambda}\wedge\beta_{\lambda+1}>0

These 1-forms can be obtained by tracing the Fubini-Study form
\omega_{FS}
through the the Cech-DeRham isomorphism, as explained in
[
LC22b
, Section 2.1]
. With respect to polar coordinates
z_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
, this isomorphism sends
\omega_{FS}
to the triple
(2d\theta_{1},2d\theta_{2},2(-d\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))
.

Transverse bridge position and geometrically transverse surfaces

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a trisection of

, let
\mathcal{K}
be a surface transverse to the trisection
\mathcal{T}

3.3.
Existence of branch locus

Auroux, building on techniques introduced by Donaldson, proved that every closed symplectic 4-manifold admits a branched covering over
(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS})

[
Aur00
]

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is ‘quasiholomorphic’
[
AK00
]

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
akin to a genuine holomorphic curve. In
[
LCMS21
]

, where

is the degree of the branch locus in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}

Theorem 3.3

(
[
AK00
]
)

Every closed symplectic 4-manifold admits a symplectic singular branched covering over

[

3.6.
First Chern class

Let
\pi:(X,\omega)\rightarrow(\mathbb{CP}^{2},\omega_{FS})
be a symplectic branched covering as constructed in this paper. We can interpret the first Chern class of
(X,\omega)
geometrically in terms of the Euler class of the foliations
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}=\text{ker}(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
on
H_{\lambda}
determined by a symplectic 1-cocycle
\{\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda}\}
. The discussion follows
[
LC22a
, Section 3]
, where it was remarked that compact leaves minimize the (generalized) Thurston norm.

Let
B
denote the zeros of
\beta_{\lambda}
on
\Sigma=\partial H_{\lambda}
. There is an intersection pairing

\langle,\rangle:H_{1}(H_{\lambda},B)\times H_{2}(H_{\lambda},\Sigma\setminus B)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

Choose a vector field
v_{\lambda}
directing the kernel of
\beta_{\lambda}
on
\Sigma
and extend it to a section of
\text{ker}(\widetilde{\beta}_{\lambda})
over
H_{\lambda}
. Generically, this section will vanish along a smooth 1-complex representing a well-defined Euler class
e(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})\in H_{1}(H_{\lambda},B)
of the foliation. If
L
is a compact leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
, then evaluating
e(\mathcal{F}_{\lambda})
on
L
gives its Euler characteristic.

Proposition 3.9
.

The triple
(e(\mathcal{F}_{1}),e(\mathcal{F}_{2}),e(\mathcal{F}_{3}))
is a 1-cocyle that represents the first Chern class of
(X,\omega)
.

Proof.

To ensure that we obtain a 1-cocycle, we can assume that the vector fields satisfy the cocycle condition
v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}=0
.

The first Chern class of
(X,\omega)
is given by the formula

c_{1}(X,\omega)=\pi^{*}(c_{1}(\mathbb{CP}^{2}))-PD(\mathcal{R})

where
\mathcal{R}\subset X
is the (smooth) ramification locus of
\pi
. By construction, since the symplectic 1-cocycle is constructed via a branched covering over the standard trisection of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, every regular leaf

of
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
is compact and is the
k
-fold branched cover of a disk leaf in

. Therefore, if the proposition holds with respect to the standard trisection of

, then it will hold in general by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula.

5.
Hodge Theory

Since we can assume that
(X,\omega)
admits a integrable complex structure over the 2-skeleton, we can investigate complex geometric objects.

5.1.
Picard group

The first important object is the Picard group of holomorphic line bundles.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,J)
be an almost-complex 4-manifold with holomorphic trisection
\mathcal{T}
. The
Picard group
of
(X,J,\mathcal{T})
is the group of holomorphic line bundles over the spine of
\mathcal{T}
, up to biholomorphic equivalence, that admit smooth trivializations along
\partial\mathcal{S}
. The group structure is given by tensor product.

One immediate question regarding the Picard group concerns the Hodge structure induced on
H^{2}_{DR}(X)
. The first Chern class gives a map

c_{1}:Pic(X,J,\mathcal{T})\rightarrow H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(X;\mathbb{C})

For Kähler surfaces, the image is precisely the integral
(1,1)
-classes. However, unlike the Kähler case, we can modify the construction and assume that every class in
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
can be represented by a holomorphic line bundle.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Start with some class
\alpha\in H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
such that
\int_{X}\omega\wedge\alpha>0
. Then by Proposition
3.10
, the Poincare-dual of
\alpha
can be represented by a surface that is geometrically transverse to the holomorphic trisection of
(X,\omega)
. Applying Corollary
4.9
, this surface can be
C^{1}
-approximated by a holomorphic curve in some neighborhood
\nu(\mathcal{S})^{\prime}
of the spine. This is an effective divisor, which immediately determines a holomorphic line bundle whose first Chern class is Poincare-dual to the divisor.

To prove the theorem, note that
H^{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is finitely-generated and we can assume it is generated by cohomology classes with positive symplectic area. Therefore, we can realize a generating set as holomorphic line bundles, then composing by tensor product realize a holomorphic line bundle with any given first Chern class.
∎

5.2.
Smooth Hodge decomposition

Let
(X,\omega,J)
be the symplectic structure constructed in the main theorem. Then
\mathcal{S}
is a compact Riemannian 4-manifold with boundary. Let
d
be the exterior derivative on
k
-forms and
d^{*}
is adjoint with respect to the metric. Define

\mathcal{D}=(d+d^{*})\qquad\Delta=\mathcal{D}^{2}=dd^{*}+d^{*}d

Define the space of
harmonic forms

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\Delta}=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(X):\Delta\alpha=0\}

and
harmonic fields

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\mathcal{D}}=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(X):(d+d^{*})\alpha=0\}

Note that harmonic fields are
d
- and
d^{*}
-closed, but harmonic forms are not necessarily
d
- and
d^{*}
-closed on a compact manifold with boundary. Moreover, in the case of nonempty boundary, the spaces of harmonic forms and fields are in general larger than DeRham cohomology groups of the manifold itself.

Since
X
is almost-complex, we get splittings
\Omega^{k}(X)=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Omega^{p,q}(X)
of
k
-forms into their
(p,q)
-components. We also have operators

\overline{\partial}:\Omega^{p,q}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q+1}(X)\qquad\partial:\Omega^{p,q}(X)\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q}(X)

and their metric adjoints
\overline{\partial}^{*},\partial^{*}
and Laplacians

\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}=(\overline{\partial}+\overline{\partial}^{*})^{2}\qquad\Delta_{\partial}=(\partial+\partial^{*})^{2}

Let
U
be a connected open set on which
J
is integrable. Then we have a fiberwise equality

\Delta=2\Delta_{\overline{\partial}}=2\Delta_{\partial}

The latter two Laplacians clearly preserve
(p,q)
-bigrading. Consequently, we get decompositions

\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\Delta}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{\Delta}

over
U
. Complex conjugation gives an isomorphisms

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{\Delta}\cong\mathcal{H}^{q,p}_{\Delta}

and the Hodge star gives isomorphisms

\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{\Delta}\cong\mathcal{H}^{n-p,n-q}_{\Delta}

Wedging with the Kähler form
\omega
induces maps

L_{\Delta}:\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{p+1,q+1}_{\Delta}

which has a metric adjoint
\Lambda_{\Delta}
. The Kähler identifies imply that
\oplus\mathcal{H}^{p,q}_{\Delta}
is a
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
-modules.

However, we do not necessarily get a decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{k}_{\mathcal{D}}
into
(p,q)
-summands. We let
H^{p,q}_{d}(X)
denote the vector space of
d
-closed
(p,q)
-forms modulo
d
-exact
(p,q)
-forms. On this subspace, we can partial recover the classical Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations.

Theorem 5.2

(Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations)
.

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional complex manifold with strictly pseudoconcave boundary. Let
\rho
be a strictly plurisubharmonic function defining the boundary. Suppose that

admits a Kähler form with Kähler potential
\rho
near

. Then there is a nondegenerate pairing

for

.

Proof.

Since the Kähler form is compatible with the boundary, we will show that we can replace
\omega
with some
\omega^{\prime}
that vanishes along
\partial X
. Then the pairing
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
is given by composing the wedge product

H^{p,q}_{d}\times H^{q,p}_{d}\rightarrow H^{p+q,p+q}_{d}\subset H^{2(p+q)}_{DR}(X)

with the pairing

H^{2(p+q)}_{DR}(X)\times H^{2(n-p-q)}_{DR}(X,\partial X)\rightarrow H^{2n}_{DR}(X,\partial X)\cong\mathbb{C}

In particular,

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\int_{X}\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge(\omega^{\prime})^{n-p-q}

which is well-defined since
\omega^{\prime}\in H^{2}_{DR}(X,\partial X)
.

Let
\rho
be the defining function and let
f
be a function such that

f^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0

and
f^{\prime}(t)>0
and
f^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq 0
for
t>0
and
f(t)=t
for

. Then

\omega^{\prime}=i\partial\overline{\partial}f(\rho)

is a 2-form such that
\omega^{\prime}|_{\partial X}=0
but
(\omega^{\prime})^{n}>0
on the interior of
X
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\alpha\in H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(X)
be a global holomorphic
(p,0)
-form for

. Then
\alpha
is

-closed.

In particular, if

Proof.

We have that

\int_{X}|d\alpha|^{2}dvol=\int_{X}d\alpha\wedge d\overline{\alpha}\wedge\omega^{n-p-1}=\int_{\partial X}\alpha\wedge d\overline{\alpha}\wedge\omega^{n-p-1}=0

. This implies that

.
∎

5.3.
Dolbeault cohomology

By construction, the spine
\nu(\mathcal{S})
is a Kähler surface with pseudoconcave boundary. Consequently, we have a Frölicher spectral sequence

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\overline{\partial}}(\nu(\mathcal{S}))\Rightarrow H^{\bullet}_{d}(\nu(\mathcal{S}))

and for each

obtain the bound

\sum_{p+q=k}\text{dim}H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(\nu(\mathcal{S}))\geq\text{dim}H^{k}_{d}(\nu(\mathcal{S}))

which is weaker than the Hodge decomposition for a closed Kähler surface.

Let

be a holomorphic vector bundle over
\nu(\mathcal{S})
. One can then define the Dolbeault cohomology groups
H^{p,q}_{\overline{\partial}}(\nu(S),E)
. Moreover, since the boundary of
\nu(\mathcal{S})
is strictly pseudoconcave, we can apply the classical Andreotti-Grauert theorem.

Theorem 5.4

(Andreotti-Grauert
[
AG62
]
)
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
\nu(\mathcal{S})
. The rank of
H^{p,0}(\nu(\mathcal{S}),E)
is finite for all
p
.

However,
q=1
is the critical dimension and there is no finiteness result. With suitable
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann boundary conditions along pseudoconcave boundary, Epstein proved finite-dimensionality for
\overline{\partial}
-harmonic sections of holomorphic bundles for all
q

[
Eps07a
,
Eps07b
,
Eps08
]
.

Given a complex surface

decomposed along a real hypersurface

such that

has pseudoconvex boundary and

has pseudoconcave boundary, Andreotti and Hill proved a Meyer-Vietoris sequence relating the Dolbeault cohomologies of

and the Kohn-Rossi cohomology of the CR structure induced on

]
. The failure of a comparable sequence here may shed light on non-Kähler symplectic structures.

5.4.
Harmonic spinors

In order to get finite-dimensional spaces of solutions, we take advantage of harmonic spinors.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a Kähler surface
X
and let
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)
be the space of
(p,q)
-forms on
X
valued in
E
. Given the Kähler metric and complex structure, we have the Dolbeault operator
\overline{\partial}
and its adjoint
\overline{\partial}^{*}

\overline{\partial}:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{p,q+1}(E)\qquad\overline{\partial}^{*}:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(E)

The combined operator
\mathcal{D}=\sqrt{2}(\overline{\partial}+\overline{\partial}^{*})
is a Spin-
\mathbb{C}
Dirac operator

\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{A}^{p,even/odd}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{p,odd/even}(E)

For fixed
p
, the index of the operator is the Euler characteristic of the Dolbeault cohomology valued in
E
:

\text{ind}(\mathcal{D})=\chi(E)=\text{dim}H^{p,0}(E)-\text{dim}H^{p,1}(E)+\text{dim}H^{p,2}(E)

Generalizing this construction, now suppose
(X,\omega,J)
is merely an almost-Kähler 4-manifold and
E
is complex vector bundle with Hermitian connection
A
. We can define Dolbeault operators

\overline{\partial}_{A}:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{p,q+1}(E)\qquad\overline{\partial}^{*}_{A}:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{p,q-1}(E)

as before. In addition, we have bundles of positive and negative spinors

S_{+}=\mathcal{A}^{p,0}(E)\oplus\mathcal{A}^{p,2}(E)\qquad S_{-}=\mathcal{A}^{p,1}(E)

and the sum
\mathcal{D}_{A}=\sqrt{2}(\overline{\partial}_{A}+\overline{\partial}^{*}_{A})
is a Dirac operator

\mathcal{D}_{A}:S_{+}\oplus S_{-}\rightarrow S_{-}\oplus S_{+}

that exchanges positive and negative spinors. In particular, the Dirac operator splits into the sum of two operators
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}_{A}:S_{\pm}\rightarrow S_{\mp}
.
The Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{A}
is a Fredholm operator with index

\text{ind}(\mathcal{D}_{A})=\text{dim ker }\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A}-\text{dim coker }\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A}

By the Atiyah-Singer index theorem, we have that

\text{ind}(\mathcal{D}_{A})=\chi(E)=\int_{X}ch(E)td(X)

where
ch(E)
is the Chern character of
E
and
td(X)
is the Todd class of
X
.

On a general almost-Kähler manifold, the linear subspace
\text{ker }\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A}
no longer splits into
(p,0)
- and
(p,2)
-graded subspaces. However, suppose that
J
is integrable over some subset
U
. The 2-form
\omega
acts fiberwise by Clifford multiplication and satisfies

\mathcal{D}_{A}(\omega\cdot\psi)=\left(\sqrt{2}(\overline{\partial}+\overline{\partial}^{*})\omega\right)\cdot\psi+\omega\cdot\mathcal{D}_{A}\psi=\omega\cdot\mathcal{D}_{A}

fiberwise over
U
. In particular, the kernel of
\mathcal{D}_{A}
is invariant under the action of
\omega
by Clifford multiplication. Since
\mathcal{A}^{p,0}(E)
and
\mathcal{A}^{p,2}(E)
are the
(\pm 2i)
-eigenspaces for
\omega
acting by Clifford multiplication, we get a locally-constant decomposition

\text{ker}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A})=\text{ker}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A})^{(p,0)}\oplus\text{ker}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A})^{(p,2)}

over
U
. Given an element
(\psi_{0},\psi_{2})\in\text{ker}(\mathcal{D}^{+}_{A})
, we get holomorphic sections

\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{p,0}(E|_{U})\qquad\ast\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{2-p,0}(E^{*}\otimes K|_{U})

where
\ast:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{2-p,2-q}(E^{*}\otimes K)
is the Hodge star operation.

5.5.
Partial sections in symplectic 4-manifolds

Within this section, we restrict to
(X,J,\mathcal{T})
obtained as branched covers of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. While the Hodge-Riemann bilinear relations (Theorem
5.2
) only hold in subcritical dimensions, we can recover the Hodge index theorem on the Picard group by using harmonic spinors.

Given a holomorphic line bundle
L\in\text{Pic}(X,J,\mathcal{T})
, since it is smoothly trivial along
\partial\mathcal{S}
, we can extend it over all of
X
as a trivial line bundle over each Weinstein sector
Z_{\lambda}
. After choosing a Hermitian connection
A
on this line bundle, we can extract cohomological information from the harmonic spinors and partial holomorphic sections described in the previous subsection.

We begin with a lemma.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
L\in\text{Pic}(X,J,\mathcal{T})
be a holomorphic line bundle and
\psi\in H^{0,0}_{U}(L,A)
a partial section. If
D=\psi^{-1}(0)
is smooth, then after a
C^{\infty}
-small perturbation of the boundary, the surface
D
intersects each component of
\partial\mathcal{S}
along a link transverse to the contact structure.

Proof.

By a
C^{\infty}
-small perturbation, we can modify the defining function
\rho_{\lambda}
on the boundary so
that
(\rho_{\lambda},\psi):X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{C}
is transverse to 0. The link
\Lambda_{\lambda}=D\cap\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
is the regular level set of this map and it represents
PD(c_{1}(L))
in
H_{1}(Y)
; since
L
is smoothly trivial over
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
, this link is nullhomologous. Secondly, the contact structure is exactly the field of complex tangencies along
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
. By a
C^{2}
-small perturbation of the boundary, we can assume it intersects
D
transversely along a smooth 1-manifold. Positivity of intersections of complex lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
implies that the link is positively transverse to the contact structure.
∎

Proposition 5.10

(Vanishing)
.

Let
L\in\text{Pic}(X,J,\mathcal{T})
with
c_{1}(L)
nonzero and suppose that
\omega\cdot L\leq 0
. Then
h^{0,0}_{U}(L,A)=0
for any
A
.

Proof.

Suppose that the Betti number is nonzero, which means we can find a nontrivial partial section
\psi
with corresponding effective partial divisor
D=\psi^{-1}(0)
. We can split
D
into its irreducible components
D=D_{1}\cup\dots\cup D_{n}
. Each is smooth away from a compact set of singular points. As in the previous lemma, we can perturb the boundary so that
D_{i}
intersects the boundary along a transverse link. Any singular points in the interior can be smoothed so that
D_{i}
is a symplectic surface with transverse boundary.

The boundary
\partial D=\partial D_{1}\cup\dots\partial D_{n}
is a nullhomologous transverse link in each
\widehat{Y}_{\lambda}
. We can cap it off in
Z_{\lambda}
with a properly embedded surface
F_{\lambda}
. By Stokes’s Theorem

\int_{F_{\lambda}}\omega=\int_{\Lambda_{\lambda}}d^{\mathbb{C}}\rho_{\lambda}\geq 0

with equality if and only if
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is empty. Here
\rho_{\lambda}
is the plurisubharmonic function defining the boundary and
d^{\mathbb{C}}\rho_{\lambda}
is the induced contact form, which is a primitive for
\omega
.

Consequently, we obtain a closed surface
\overline{D}=D\cup F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup F_{3}
in
X
representing
L
and with positive symplectic area, which is a contradiction.
∎

Via the Riemann-Roch theorem for surfaces, if
L
is a holomorphic line bundle, we have

\chi(L)=\frac{1}{2}L\cdot(L-K)+\chi_{h}(X)

where
K
is the canonical class of
(X,J)
and
\chi_{h}(X)=\frac{1}{12}(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2})
is its holomorphic Euler characteristic.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
L\subset\text{Pic}(X,J,L)
be a holomorphic line bundle.

(1)

If
\chi(L)>0
, then either
[L]
or
[K-L]
in
H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
can be represented by an effective partial divisor.

(2)

If
\chi(L)>0
and
\omega\cdot L>\omega\cdot K
, then
[L]
can be represented by an effective partial divisor.

(3)

If
L^{2}>0
and
\omega\cdot L>0
, then
mL
can be represented by a partial effective divisor for positive
m
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Part (1) follows since by the definition of Euler characteristic:

\chi(L)=h^{0}(L)-h^{1}(L)+h^{2}(L)

Part (2) follows from Part (1), because
K-L
will have negative symplectic area, hence the symplectic surface must represent
[L]
. Part (3) follows from parts (1) and (2) because

\chi(mL)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}L^{2}+O(m)

which is positive for
m
sufficiently large. Moreover,
\omega\cdot(mL)=m(\omega\cdot L)>\omega\cdot K
for
m\gg 0
as well.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
L\in\text{Pic}(X,J,\mathcal{T})
and let
H
denote the class of
\omega
. There exist
m,n>0
such that
mH+nL
can be represented by a partial effective divisor

Proof.

Consider
H^{\prime}=m^{\prime}H+L
. Then
(H^{\prime})^{2}>0
and
H\cdot H^{\prime}=m^{\prime}H^{2}+H\cdot L>0
for
m^{\prime}>0
sufficiently large, so
nH^{\prime}=(nm^{\prime})H+nL

n
sufficiently large.
∎

6.
BMY Inequality

6.1.
Miyaoka divisors

Let
X
be a complex surface of general type, let
\Omega_{X}
denote its holomorphic cotangent bundle, let
K_{X}=\text{det}(\Omega_{X})
be its canonical bundle, and let
S^{m}\Omega_{x}
denote the
m^{\text{th}}
-symmetric power of the (complex) cotangent bundle (which has complex rank
m+1
). Define

Q_{\alpha}=S^{m}\Omega_{x}\otimes K^{-m\alpha}

where
m\gg 0
is a large integer and
\alpha
is some rational number such that
m\alpha
is integral. The Serre dual of
Q_{\alpha}
is

Q^{*}_{\alpha}\otimes K=S^{m}T\otimes K^{1+m\alpha}=S^{m}\Omega\otimes K^{1-m(1-\alpha)}=Q_{1-\alpha}\otimes K

Let
\beta=\frac{c_{2}(X)}{c_{1}(X)^{2}}
(so the Bogomolev-Miyaoka-Yau inequality states that
\beta\geq\frac{1}{3}
). The Euler characteristic of
Q
is

\chi(Q)=\left(-\frac{1}{6}c_{2}(X)+\frac{1}{6}c_{1}(X)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha c_{1}(X)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}c_{1}(X)^{2}\right)m^{3}+O(m^{2})

Therefore, for
m
sufficiently large, the Euler characteristic is positive if and only if

\beta\leq(3\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+1)

In particular, this forces either
Q_{\alpha}
or
Q^{*}_{\alpha}=Q_{1-\alpha}\otimes K
to admit a holomorphic section.

Now consider
Q_{m,n}=S^{m}\Omega\otimes K^{-n}
. Holomorphic sections of
Q_{m,n}
can be identified with effective divisors of the line bundle
mH-n\pi^{*}(K)
on
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X})
, the projectivization of the holomorphic cotangent bundle
\Omega_{X}
. Miyaoka proves that no such effective divisors can exist if
\frac{n}{m}>\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}=\beta
.

The BMY inequality follows since if
\beta<\frac{1}{3}
, we can choose an
\alpha
such that

\beta\leq(3\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta<\alpha,1-\alpha

The first inequality implies the existence of holomorphic sections, while the second rules it out. Therefore, we cannot have
\beta<\frac{1}{3}
and, equivalently, must have
c_{1}^{2}\leq 3c_{2}
.

Now let
(X,\omega)
be a closed symplectic 4-manifold and let
(X,\omega^{\prime},J)
be the symplectic model from Theorem
1.3
. The almost-complex structure
J
induces a complex structure on
T^{*}X
, which is holomorphic over the spine
\mathcal{S}
of the trisection. We can then form
\mathbb{P}(T^{*}X)
as the (complex) projectivization of the cotangent bundle, which is a
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-bundle over
X
that is holomorphic over
\mathcal{S}(X)
. Moreover, we have line bundles
K
, representing the canonical class, and
H
, representing the class of the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
-fiber, that are holomorphic over
\mathcal{S}(X)
and
\mathbb{P}(T^{*}X)|_{\mathcal{S}(X)}
, respectively.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\alpha
satisfy the inequality

\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}\leq(3\alpha^{2}-3\alpha+1)

Then for
m\gg 0
, there exists a holomorphic partial section of
Q_{\alpha}
and corresponding effective partial divisor
W
in
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X})
over
\nu(\mathcal{S})
representing either

mH-(m\alpha)K\qquad\text{or}\qquad mH-(1-m(1-\alpha))K

In addition,
W
is a symplectic 4-manifold with three strongly concave boundary components.

Proof.

The existence of a holomorphic partial section follows from the fact that the Euler characteristic of the complex bundle
Q_{\alpha}
is positive for
\beta,\alpha
satisfying the inequality. Therefore, either
Q_{\alpha}
or its Serre dual
Q_{1-\alpha}\otimes K
admit a partial holomorphic section
\psi
.

Viewing
\psi
as a holomorphic section of a line bundle over
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X})
, the vanishing locus of
\psi
is an effective divisor
W_{\psi}
. The composition of the inclusion
\iota:W_{\psi}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X})
with the bundle projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X})\rightarrow X
is a holomorphic branched covering. Therefore, the pulled back 2-form
(\pi\circ i)^{*}(\omega)
can be perturbed to a symplectic form on
\omega
. In addition, the plurisubharmonic defining functions on
\partial\mathcal{S}
pull back to plurisubharmonic defining functions on
W_{\psi}
.
∎

6.2.
Stability of the cotangent bundle

For the most part, the proofs of Bogomolev (see
[
Rei78
]
or
[
BHPVdV04
]
for example) and Miyaoka’s inequalities follow from computations with Chern numbers and intersection theory, which carry over immediately to the symplectic case. However, a key aspect for Bogomolev and for Miyaoka is that the holomorphic cotangent bundle
\Omega_{X}
is stable. In this case, this means that if
L
is a holomorphic sub-line bundle of
\Omega_{X}
, then the dimension of
H^{0}(mL)
grows at most linearly in
m
. A critical part of the argument is that a holomorphic section
\psi
of
L
is a global holomorphic 1-form, hence by Hodge theory it must be
closed
. This makes it possible to argue as in the classical Castelnuovo-de Franchis theorem that there is some holomorphic map
\pi:X\rightarrow C
to a curve such that
L=\pi^{*}(L^{\prime})
for some line bundle on
C
, hence the space of sections can grow at most linearly. However, it is not necessarily true that a global holomorphic 1-form on a Kähler surface with strongly pseudoconcave boundary must be closed with respect to the DeRham exterior derivative.

Moreover, the Kobayashi-Hitchen correspondence between stable holomorphic bundles and Hermitian Yang-Mills metrics breaks down on Kähler surfaces with boundary. Donaldson
[
Don92
]
and Li-Zhang
[
LZ05
]
solved the Dirichlet problem for Hermitian Yang-Mills metrics. In particular, given any real number
\lambda
, any holomorphic bundle
E
, and any Hermitian metric
h
for
E
along the boundary of a Kähler manifold, there is a Hermitian metric
H
extending
h
whose curvature satisfies

2i\Lambda F_{H}=\lambda\text{Id}

where
\Lambda
is the adjoint operator to wedging with the Kähler form. For example, one can find always find a metric on the holomorphic cotangent bundle whose curvature form satisfies the weaker Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality
c_{2}-4c_{1}^{2}\geq 0
over the interior. However, the true Chern number computation includes the boundary term, given by the Chern-Simons-Dirac functional of the connection along the boundary. The key obstruction arises from the fact that one can prescribe the Hermitian
metric
along the boundary, but one cannot prescribe its
curvature
along the boundary under the Hermitian Yang-Mills flow.

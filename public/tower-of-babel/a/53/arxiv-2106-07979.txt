Fractional operators and their commutators on generalized Orlicz spaces
Arttu Karppinen

Abstract
. In this paper we examine boundedness of fractional maximal operator. The main focus is on commutators and maximal commutators on generalized Orlicz spaces for fractional maximal functions and Riesz potentials. We prove their boundedness between generalized Orlicz spaces and give a characterization for functions of bounded mean oscillation. To best of our knowledge, these results are also new in the special case of double phase spaces.

46E30 (42B25, 42B35)

1.
Introduction

The Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator is one of the most central operators in modern harmonic analysis and theory of partial differential equations. Its boundedness properties have been deeply connected to various Sobolev–Poincaré type inequalities and density of smooth functions in spaces related to non-standard growth conditions, for instance. For that reason, a systematic study of various variational integrals or partial differential equations under non-standard growth conditions has often started with proving boundedness of the maximal operator in a related function space, see for instance
[
6
,
17
]
.

The maximal function has also a fractional variant introduced in
[
22
]
and its boundedness properties have also been intensively studied. This version is also related to fractional integrals such as Riesz potentials. Chianhi
[
7
]
first proved necessary and sufficient conditions for boundedness of fractional maximal operator between different Orlicz spaces. Here the condition between Orlicz functions was described by ”global domination”. In
[
13
]
this condition was proven to be equivalent to presentation used also in this article. The boundedness results have also been investigated in the variable exponent case, see
[
5
,
9
,
21
]
.

On the other hand, a boundedness of commutators
[T,b]f=T(bf)-bTf
of various operators
T
and functions
b
in harmonic analysis has been a widely studied topic. One of the most influential paper regarding commutators in harmonic analysis is by Coifman, Rochberg and Weiss
[
8
]
. They gave a characterization of
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
in terms of boundedness of commutator
[T,b]
in
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
when
T
is a Calderón–Zygmund singular integral operator. Similar characterization of Lipschitz spaces was given by Janson
[
20
]
:
[T,b]
is bounded from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, where
1<p<q<\infty
if and only if
b\in\Lambda_{\beta}
and
\beta=n\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)
. These type of results have successfully been used for research regarding products of functions from real Hardy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

[
4
]
and giving new characterization of well known function spaces such as
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, Lipschitz spaces and even Campanato spaces
[
23
]
.

When the operator is a maximal function instead of a Calderón–Zygmund operator, the boundedness results of commutators were studied in
[
3
]
and for fractional maximal function in
[
26
]
.
These results have had their generalizations from standard
L^{p}
-spaces to their non-standard counterparts such as variable exponent Lebesgue and Orlicz spaces
[
13
,
27
,
28
]
and more refined Morrey type spaces
[
2
,
14
,
25
]
.

In this paper we study boundedness of fractional operators and their commutator variants in generalized Orlicz spaces (also known as Musielak–Orlicz spaces). We prove that similar structural assumptions required for boundedness of Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator in generalized Orlicz spaces also imply boundedness of the fractional variant. This is closely related to boundedness of Riesz potentials, which have already been studied in
[
15
]
. Here we give a slightly different framework for defining the target space and record that these results do not require the generating
\varphi
-functions to be
N
-functions. Assumptions for these boundedness results are in turn inherited by the commutators and we use the boundedness results to give a characterization for functions of bounded mean oscillation
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Also, commutators related to Riesz potentials are investigated.

Generalized Orlicz spaces include many of the widely studied function spaces such as classical
L^{p}
spaces, Orlicz spaces, variable exponent spaces and double phase spaces as special cases. Developing theory in this general setting allows us to capture many delicate phenomena not present in
L^{p}
-scale and describe them with improved accuracy using assumptions intrinsic to the problem at hand. Main results achieved here are, to best of our knowledge, new already in the double phase situation. The methodology in this paper is to give transparent proofs without resorting to extrapolation arguments or other abstract arguments.

However, the improved generality comes with some penalties. The connection between boundedness of the maximal operator in a generalized Orlicz space and its dual space is not yet fully understood and therefore not utilized here. For this reason, the assumptions remain technical instead of just the boundedness assumption. However, Diening has obtainted some partial results in this direction
[
11
]
. Additionally, the assumption linking the domain and target space is known to be sharp in Orlicz spaces, but the non-autonomous nature of generalized Orlicz spaces leaves this as an open question. To best of our knowledge, this is the case already in variable exponent spaces.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In the second section we define all related function spaces together with operators we study. Third section is devoted to fractional maximal operator. Here we prove its boundedness and connect our assumptions to previous study related to Riesz potential. In the fourth section we prove boundedness of commutators related to Riesz potential. The fifth section briefly discusses commutators of Hardy–Littlewood maximal function and proofs owe to pointwise estimates proved in
[
1
]
. In the final section give boundedness results of commutators related to fractional maximal function and give a new characterization of
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

The generalized Orlicz space

comprises of measurable functions

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,\lambda f(x))\,dx<\infty

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{f(x)}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leqslant 1\right\}.

If the set
\Omega
is clear from the context, we abbreviate
\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega)}
as
\|f\|_{\varphi}
. A comprehensive presentation of generalized Orlicz spaces can be found in
[
16
]
.

The conjugate

Lemma 2.5

(Norm conjugate formula, Theorem 3.4.6 in
[

]

If

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega)}\approx\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{\varphi^{\ast}}(\Omega)\leqslant 1}}\int_{\Omega}|f(x)g(x)|\ dx.

We extend the classical Hölder’s inequality to generalized Orlicz spaces as follows.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 3.2.11 in
[

Let

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x)g(x)\,dx\leqslant 2\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{\varphi^{\ast}}(\Omega)}.

We can also have a

Lemma 2.7

(Generalized Hölder’s inequality)
.

Let
\varphi_{i}\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
for

. If for all

and almost every

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x,t)\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x,t)\leqslant\varphi_{3}^{-1}(x,t),

then

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{L^{\varphi_{3}}(\Omega)}\lesssim 2\|f\|_{L^{\varphi_{1}}(\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{\varphi_{2}}(\Omega)}

for every

and
g\in L^{\varphi_{2}}(\Omega)
. If the
\Phi
-functions are convex, then ”
\lesssim

\leqslant
”.

Proof.

Let us first suppose that
\varphi_{i}\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
. We start the proof with an associated Young’s inequality. Directly from the definitions we deduce
\varphi_{1}(x,\varphi_{i}^{-1}(x,t))\leqslant t\leqslant\varphi_{i}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{i}(x,t))
. We have two cases: either
\varphi_{1}(x,s)\leqslant\varphi_{2}(x,t)
or
\varphi_{1}(x,s)>\varphi_{2}(x,t)
. In the first situation

\displaystyle st

\displaystyle\leqslant\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{1}(x,s))\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{2}(x,t))\leqslant\varphi_{1}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{2}(x,t))\varphi_{2}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{2}(x,t))

\displaystyle\leqslant\varphi_{3}^{-1}(x,\varphi_{2}(x,t)).

Applying
\varphi_{3}(x,\cdot)
to both sides, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(x,st)\leqslant\varphi_{2}(x,t).

If on the other hand we are in situation 2, similar estimates yield
\varphi_{3}(x,st)\leqslant\varphi_{1}(x,s)
, so we have the Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(x,st)\leqslant\max\{\varphi_{1}(x,s),\varphi_{2}(x,t)\}\leqslant\varphi_{1}(x,s)+\varphi_{2}(x,t).

Now we can prove the generalized Hölder’s inequality. Let
\varepsilon>0
and without loss of generality assume that
\|f\|_{\varphi_{1}}=\|g\|_{\varphi_{2}}=1
. Now the convexity of
\varphi_{3}
and the previous Young’s inequality yield

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{3}\left(x,\dfrac{f(x)g(x)}{2(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)\,dx\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}\left(x,\dfrac{f(x)}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\,dx+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{2}\left(x,\dfrac{g(x)}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\,dx\leqslant 1.

In other words,
\|fg\|_{\varphi_{3}}\leqslant 2(1+\varepsilon)^{2}
and thus the claim follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Suppose now we are not in the convex regime, that is
\varphi_{i}\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
. There exist
\psi_{i}\in\Phi_{c}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi_{i}\simeq\psi_{i}

[
16
, Lemma 2.2.1]

\psi_{i}
and equivalence, we end up with

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}\left(x,\frac{st}{L^{3}}\right)\leqslant\psi_{3}\left(x,\frac{st}{L^{2}}\right)\leqslant\psi_{1}\left(x,\frac{s}{L}\right)+\psi_{2}\left(x,\frac{t}{L}\right)\leqslant\varphi_{1}(x,s)+\varphi_{2}(x,t)

where

is the largest equivalence constant of the three
\Phi
-functions. Denoting the constant of
(aInc)
1
by
a

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{3}\left(x,\dfrac{f(x)g(x)}{2aL^{3}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)\,dx\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{1}\left(x,\dfrac{f(x)}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\,dx+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{2}\left(x,\dfrac{g(x)}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\,dx\leqslant 1.

Thus

2.2.
Other function spaces and operators

Next we introduce other function spaces appearing in this paper. Functions of bounded mean oscillation, that is functions
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfying

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx<\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002ptb_{Q}=\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}b(x)\,dx

for every cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, play an essential role in the theory of commutators. They form a function space
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
when equipped with a seminorm

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\ast}=\sup_{Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx.

Additionally we define the negative part of a function
b^{-}(x):=-\min\{b(x),0\}
and similarly the positive part
b^{+}(x)=\max\{b(x),0\}
. It immediately follows that for every
x
we have
|b(x)|-b(x)=2b^{-}(x)
.

The space of compactly supported and smooth functions is denoted as
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. This space is dense in
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
and
(aDec)

[
16
, Theorem 3.7.15]
.

Lastly, we define various operators investigated in this paper. All of the maximal operators are derived from non-centred Hardy–Littlewood maximal function

\displaystyle Mf(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy.

This is comparable with the centred variant

\displaystyle M^{c}f(x):=\sup_{Q(x,r)}\dfrac{1}{|Q(x,r)|}\int_{Q(x,r)}|f(y)|\,dy

as they enjoy pointwise inequalities
M^{c}f(x)\leqslant Mf(x)\leqslant 2^{n}M^{c}f(x)
. We can also have a restricted maximal function in each cube
Q_{0}
as

\displaystyle M_{Q_{0}}f(x):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\ni x\\
Q\subset Q_{0}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy

We also define the sharp maximal function as

\displaystyle M^{\sharp}f(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx.

If a measurable function
b
is given, we define the maximal commutator as

\displaystyle M_{b}f(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b(y)||f(y)|\,dy.

On the other hand, if a measurable function
b
is given, the commutator of a maximal function is defined as

\displaystyle[M,b]f(x):=M(bf)(x)-b(x)Mf(x).

For
\alpha\in(0,n)
, the main focus of this article is the fractional maximal function

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy

and its variations similar to previously given to the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function.

Closely related to fractional maximal function is the Riesz potential of a function
f

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}f(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\dfrac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy.

For the Riesz potential we also have the variants

\displaystyle[I_{\alpha},b]f(x)=I_{\alpha}(bf)(x)-b(x)I_{\alpha}f(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptI_{b,\alpha}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\dfrac{|b(x)-b(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}f(y)\,dy.

Note that
I_{b,\alpha}
is often denoted in the literature as
|I_{\alpha},b|
or
|b,I_{\alpha}|
, but to unify notation with maximal functions we use a different notation.

Boundedness of Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator in generalized Orlicz spaces with the current framework was proven by Hästö
[

]
. In particular, if
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfies

(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(aInc)

\displaystyle\|Mf\|_{\varphi}\leqslant C_{M}\|f\|_{\varphi}

for all

3.
Boundedness of the fractional maximal operator

In this section we prove boundedness properties of the fractional maximal operator. The result follows also from pointwise inequality
M_{\alpha}f(x)\lesssim I_{\alpha}f(x)
and
[
15
]

[

but here we give a direct proof for the result and simplify the presentation of function spaces.
To obtain this, we exploit the fact, that

Remark 3.1
.

In the paper of Harjulehto and Hästö, the target space of
I_{\alpha}
was constructed in a slightly different way than in our presentation. Let us show that they are in fact equivalent:
We show that under suitable structural conditions on
\varphi
, the Riesz potential maps functions from
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, where
\varphi
and
\psi
satisfy the relation
\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\psi^{-1}(x,t)
. In
[
15
]
the function target space is
L^{\varphi_{\alpha}^{\sharp}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, where
\varphi^{\sharp}
is a convex
\Phi
-function equivalent to
\varphi(x,\lambda^{-1}(x,t))
and
\lambda(x,t):=t\varphi(x,t)^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}
. Recall that equivalence of
\Phi
-functions is an equivalent condition to comparability of their inverses. The claim is invariant under equivalence of
\Phi
-functions so we can assume that
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi_{s}(\Omega)
and therefore by Lemma
2.2
(a)

\displaystyle\big(\varphi_{\alpha}^{\sharp}\big)^{-1}(x,t)\approx\lambda(x,\varphi^{-1}(x,t))=\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\ \varphi(x,\varphi^{-1}(x,t))^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}=t^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx\psi^{-1}(x,t).

Thus
\varphi_{\alpha}^{\sharp}\simeq\psi
and they generate the same function spaces with comparable norms and we resume to denote
\psi
for the function generating the target space.

We collect these observations to the following Lemma. Note that the now
(A1)
is valid for cubes of all size, not just those with
|Q|\leqslant 1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi
satisfy Assumptions
2.11
. Then there exist
\tilde{\varphi},\psi,\tilde{\psi}\in\Phi_{s}(\Omega)
such that

\varphi\sim\tilde{\varphi},\hskip 10.00002pt\psi\sim\tilde{\psi},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\psi^{-1}(x,t),\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,t)

and

\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,t)\lesssim\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(y,t)

for every cube
Q
, almost every

and every

.

The first main result is a direct proof, without extrapolation arguments, that the fractional maximal operator is bounded.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1<p\leqslant q<\infty
,
\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be such that

\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\psi^{-1}(x,t).

Let
r\in(\frac{\alpha}{n},\frac{1}{p}]
. Assume that
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(aInc)
p
and
(aDec)
1/r
. Then the fractional maximal operator
M_{\alpha}:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

Proof.

Recall the centred fractional maximal function

\displaystyle M^{c}_{\alpha}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}\dfrac{1}{|Q(x,r)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q(x,r)}|f(y)|\,dy

and recall that
M^{c}_{\alpha}f(x)\approx M_{\alpha}f(x)
. Thus, we can prove the boundedness for the centred fractional maximal operator.

Let
\Phi
-functions
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\psi}
be as in Lemma
3.2
.
Let
Q(x,r)
be any cube and split a function
f
as

f=f_{1}+f_{2},\hskip 10.00002ptf_{1}(y)=f(y)\chi_{Q(x,r)}(y),\hskip 10.00002ptf_{2}(y)=f(y)\chi_{Q(x,r)^{c}}(y).

By sublinearity of the maximal operator we have
M^{c}_{\alpha}f(x)\leqslant M^{c}_{\alpha}f_{1}(x)+M_{\alpha}^{c}f_{2}(x)
. We start by estimating the last term

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}^{c}f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\dfrac{1}{|Q(x,t)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q(x,t)\cap Q(x,r)^{c}}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>r}\dfrac{1}{|Q(x,t)|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q(x,t)}|f(y)|\,dy.

We continue the estimates with Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.2
(b)

\displaystyle M^{c}_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>r}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}|Q(x,t)|^{\alpha/n-1}\|\chi_{Q(x,t)}\|_{\tilde{\varphi}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>r}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}|Q(x,t)|^{\alpha/n-1}\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}\left(\tilde{\varphi}^{\ast}\right)^{-1}(y,|Q(x,t)|^{-1})\,dy\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>r}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}|Q(x,t)|^{\alpha/n-1}\left(\fint_{Q(x,t)}\dfrac{1}{|Q(x,t)|}\dfrac{1}{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(y,|Q(x,t)|^{-1})}\,dy\right)^{-1}.

Using convexity of the function
z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}
with Jensen’s inequality and Lemma
3.2
we continue

\displaystyle M^{c}_{\alpha}f_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>r}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}|Q(x,t)|^{\alpha/n}\fint_{Q(x,t)}\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(y,|Q(x,t)|^{-1})\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}\sup_{t>r}t^{\alpha}\fint_{Q(x,t)}\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(y,t^{-n})\,dy

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}\sup_{t>r}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,t^{-n})\leqslant\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n}).

For
f_{1}
we have
M^{c}_{\alpha}f_{1}(x)\lesssim r^{\alpha}M^{c}f(x)\approx r^{\alpha}Mf(x)
(see for example
[
18
, Lemma. (b)]
), so returning to non-centred maximal functions we see

\displaystyle|M_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\alpha}Mf(x)+\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n})

\displaystyle\lesssim Mf(x)\dfrac{\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n})}{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n})}+\|f\|_{\varphi}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n}).

Recall that
\tilde{\varphi}\in\Phi_{s}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
so it is continuous and surjective from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,\infty)
. Thus we can choose
r
in such a way that
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(x,r^{-n})=\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}
, where
C_{M}
is the constant in (
2.13
), and get

\displaystyle|M_{\alpha}f(x)|\lesssim\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi}^{-1}\left(x,\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\dfrac{Mf(x)}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right)\right).

Since
\tilde{\psi}
is increasing and a strong
\Phi
-function, which allows us to use Lemma
2.2
(a), it follows that

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}\left(x,\dfrac{|M_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{\psi}\left(x,\tilde{\psi}^{-1}\left(x,\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right)\right)\right)=\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\dfrac{Mf(x)}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right).

Integrating both sides we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\tilde{\psi}\left(x,\dfrac{|M_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right)\,dx\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\tilde{\varphi}\left(x,\dfrac{Mf(x)}{C_{M}\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}}\right)\,dx\leqslant 1.

In other words
\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{\tilde{\psi}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\tilde{\varphi}}
. But
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\tilde{\psi}
are weakly equivalent to
\varphi
and
\psi
so they have comparable norms, respectively, and all in all

\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}.\qed

4.
Commutators of Riesz potential

It turns out that the boundedness of
[I_{\alpha},b]
can be tackled with the sharp maximal function. Our methods are based on
[

]
. There is no pointwise inequality between between a function
f
and the sharp maximal function
M^{\sharp}f
but a norm estimate can be achieved as is done in Lemma
4.3
. However, to prove this we need to investigate smooth functions with compact support and vanishing integral. We denote

\displaystyle C^{\infty}_{0,0}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx=0\right\}.

For bounded domains
\Omega
we define
L^{\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)
analogously but for for domains with
|\Omega|=\infty
we define
L^{\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)=L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfies
(A0)
and
(aDec)
q
. If
|\Omega|<\infty
or
\varphi
satisfies
(aInc)
p
with
p>1
, then
C^{\infty}_{0,0}(\Omega)
is dense in
L^{\varphi}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let us first investigate the case of a bounded domain. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy
\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)\,dx=1
. Since
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
and
(aDec)
, compactly supported smooth functions are dense in
L^{\varphi}_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
, and therefore we have a sequence
(\tilde{f}_{k})\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\tilde{f}_{k}\to f
in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
. Hölder’s inequality implies that
\|f-\tilde{f_{k}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f-\tilde{f_{k}}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{\Omega}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}
so
(\tilde{f}_{k})
converges to
f
also in
L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx=0.

Now we define
f_{k}:=\tilde{f_{k}}-\eta\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\,dx
and notice that
f_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{0,0}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\|f-f_{k}\|_{\varphi}\leqslant\|f-f_{k}\|_{\varphi}+\|\eta\|_{\varphi}\left|\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f_{k}}(x)\,dx\right|.

Letting
k\to\infty
, we see that
\|f-f_{k}\|_{\varphi}\to 0
and the proof for bounded
\Omega
is complete.

Suppose then that
|\Omega|=\infty
and
\varphi
satisfies
(aInc)
. Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be an increasing sequence of bounded domains satisfying

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}\Subset\Omega\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}=\Omega\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|\Omega_{j}|\geqslant 1.

For each
\Omega_{j}
, associate a non-negative function
\eta_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying
\int_{\Omega_{j}}\eta_{j}(x)\,dx=1
and
\eta_{j}(x)\lesssim|\Omega_{j}|^{-1}\chi_{\Omega_{j}}
. This can be done by mollifying functions
|\Omega_{j}|^{-1}\chi_{\Omega_{j}}
for example. From the pointwise inequality and Lemma
2.8
we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\eta_{j}\|_{\varphi}\lesssim|\Omega_{j}|^{-1}\|\chi_{\Omega_{j}}\|_{\varphi}\lesssim\max\{|\Omega_{j}|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}},|\Omega_{j}|^{-1+\frac{1}{q}}\}\to 0,

when
j\to 0
. By density of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists functions
(\tilde{f_{k}})\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\tilde{f}_{k}\to f
in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
and
\|\tilde{f}_{k}\|_{\varphi}\leqslant\|f\|_{\varphi}+1
. Similarly to the case of bounded domain, we define
f_{k}:=\tilde{f_{k}}-\eta_{j_{k}}\int_{\Omega_{k}}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\,dx
, where
j_{k}
is an increasing sequence chosen later. By construction

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f_{k}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{j_{k}}}\eta_{j_{k}}(x)\,dx\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{k}(x)\,dx=0,

so
(f_{k})\subset C^{\infty}_{0,0}(\Omega)
. Hölder’s inequality for the integral term inside the norm yields

\displaystyle\|f-f_{k}\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-\tilde{f_{k}}\|_{\varphi}+\|\eta_{j_{k}}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}\|\tilde{f}_{k}\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|f-\tilde{f_{k}}\|_{\varphi}+\|\eta_{j_{k}}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}(\|f\|_{\varphi}+1).

The first term on the right-hand side converges to
0
when
k\to\infty
. On the other hand, since all domains
\Omega_{k}
have finite measure,
(A0)
implies that
\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\in L^{\varphi^{\ast}}(\Omega)

[
16
, Theorem 3.7.13]
. Exploiting (
4.2
), we can choose the sequence
(j_{k})
in such a way that
\|\eta_{j_{k}}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}\leqslant 2^{-k}
. This choice ensures us that the last term on the previous display converges to
0
as
k\to\infty
. All together, we have shown that
f_{k}\to f
in
L^{\varphi}(\Omega)
.∎

Let us now prove the required inequality for the sharp maximal function.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}(\Omega)
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(aInc)
and
(aDec)
, then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\lesssim\|M^{\sharp}f\|_{L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

for all
f\in L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proof.

By the pointwise inequality
M^{\sharp}f(x)\leqslant 2Mf(x)
and the assumptions on
\varphi
, the sharp maximal operator is bounded in
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. This implies that
M^{\sharp}f_{j}\to M^{\sharp}f
in
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
Mg_{j}\to Mg
in
L^{\varphi^{\ast}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for all convergent sequences
(f_{j})\subset C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
(g_{j})\subset C^{\infty}_{0,0}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})

[

, for these functions we have the estimate

\displaystyle|\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle|:=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}f_{j}(x)g_{j}(x)\,dx\right|\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}M^{\sharp}f_{j}(x)Mg_{j}(x)\,dx=\langle M^{\sharp}f_{j},Mg_{j}\rangle.

Therefore for general
f\in L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
g\in L^{\varphi^{\ast}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
we have

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}|\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle|\lesssim\lim_{j\to\infty}\langle M^{\sharp}f_{j},Mg_{j}\rangle=\langle M^{\sharp}f,Mg\rangle\lesssim\|M^{\sharp}f\|_{\varphi}\|Mg\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|M^{\sharp}f\|_{\varphi}\|g\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}.

Taking supremum over functions

satisfying

the claim follows by the Norm conjugate formula, Lemma
2.5
.

(aDec)

\|[I_{\alpha},b]f\|_{L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\|f\|_{L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}

(2)

and

By Lemma

\displaystyle\psi_{s}^{-1}(x,t)\approx\psi^{-1}(x,t)^{s}\approx t^{-\frac{\alpha s}{n}}\varphi^{-1}(x,t)^{s}\approx t^{-\frac{\alpha s}{n}}\varphi_{s}^{-1}(x,t).

1/rs
. Since
\frac{p}{s}>1
and
rs\in(\frac{\alpha s}{n},\frac{s}{p}]
, Lemma
4.5
shows that
I_{\alpha s}
is bounded from
L^{\varphi_{s}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to
L^{\psi_{s}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and likewise

\displaystyle\|I_{\alpha s}(|f|^{s})\|_{\psi_{t}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\lesssim\||f|^{s}\|_{\varphi_{s}}^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Coming back to (
4.7
) and definition of
\varphi
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\|[I_{\alpha},b]f\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\left(\|f\|_{\varphi}+\|f^{s}\|^{1/s}_{\varphi_{s}}\right)\lesssim\|b\|_{{}_{\ast}}\|f\|_{\varphi}.

Since
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
and
(aDec)
, we know that
C^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is dense in
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Now we get the result for all
f\in L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
since the commutator is linear and bounded in a dense set.
∎

5.
Maximal commutators

In this section we show boundedness of both maximal commutators
M_{b}:{L^{\varphi}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
M_{b,\alpha}:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. We start with the simpler non-fractional version but first we collect some lemmas. The first Lemma was essentially proven in
[
1
, Corollaries 1.11, 1.12]
. Authors’ main results lead to pointwise estimates

\displaystyle M_{b}f(x)\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}M^{2}f(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|[M,b]f(x)|\lesssim(\|b^{+}\|_{\ast}+\|b^{-}\|_{\infty})M^{2}f(x)

where
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, so it holds for any (quasi) Banach space where
M
is bounded, especially for
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Similar approach cannot directly be used for
M_{b,\alpha}
since this operator is not bounded from
L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
to itself.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(aInc)
, then

\displaystyle\|M_{b}f\|_{\varphi}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\|f\|_{\varphi}.

If additionally
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then also the commutator
[M,b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

It is straightforward to prove that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is also a necessary condition for boundedness of the maximal commutator and we get the following characterization of
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\varphi\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfy
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
and
(aInc)
. Then
M_{b}:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded if and only if
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proof.

If
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, the claim follows directly from Lemma
5.1
.

Let us then assume that
M_{b}:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded. Take any cube
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Using Hölder’s inequality (Lemma
2.6
), the assumption and Lemma
2.9
in this order, we see that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left(\int_{Q}|b(x)-b(y)|\chi_{Q}(y)\,dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}M_{b}\chi_{Q}(x)\,dx\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|M_{b}\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}\leqslant C.

Taking the supremum over all cubes
Q
, we see that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

As the fractional commutators are closely related, we get the boundedness of maximal fractional commutator also in a similar fashion.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
1<p\leqslant q<\infty
,
\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be such that

\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\psi^{-1}(x,t).

Let
r\in(\frac{\alpha}{n},\frac{1}{p}]
. Assume that
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(aInc)
p
and
(aDec)
1/r
. Then
M_{b,\alpha}:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded and

\displaystyle\|M_{b,\alpha}f\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\|f\|_{\varphi}

if and only if
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Since we have the pointwise estimate

\displaystyle M_{b,\alpha}f\lesssim I_{b,\alpha}(|f|),

Theorem
4.6
(2) immediately yields
\|M_{b,\alpha}f\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|I_{b,\alpha}(|f|)\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|b\|_{\ast}\|f\|_{\varphi}
.

Let us then assume that
M_{b,\alpha}
is bounded and
Q
is an arbitrary cube. This and Hölder’s inequality give

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{Q}\left(\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b(y)|\chi_{Q}(y)\,dy\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\int_{Q}M_{b,\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\|M_{b,\alpha}\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\frac{\alpha}{n}}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}.

Now we need to estimate
\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}
in terms of
\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}
. This is done with the help of Lemma
2.10
:

(5.4)

\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\approx\dfrac{1}{\fint_{Q}\varphi^{-1}\left(x,\frac{1}{|Q|}\right)\,dx}\approx\dfrac{1}{\frac{1}{|Q|^{\alpha/n}}\fint_{Q}\psi^{-1}\left(x,\frac{1}{|Q|}\right)\,dx}\approx|Q|^{\alpha/n}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}.

Therefore Lemma
2.9
and the previous two displays yield

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}<C.

Taking supremum over all cubes
Q
we see that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

6.
Commutators of fractional maximal operators

In this section we combine previous estimates of maximal operators to show boundedness of commutators of fractional maximal operators generalizing the ideas of Zhang, Si and Wu in
[
27
,
28
]
.

In
[
3
]
the authors showed the following characterization for functions with bounded mean oscillation and negative part. The proof of
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is similar to the proof of Lemma
6.3
. However, the
L^{1}
nature of assumption with non-fractional maximal function is needed for
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and it is not known if assumption like the norm estimate in Lemma
6.3
with fractional maximal function implies this.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)|\,dx<\infty.

Then
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

The next result is a weaker form of Proposition
6.8
, but it will be used later when we have commutator with
|b|
which is clearly non-negative.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let Assumptions
2.11
hold. If
0\leqslant b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then the commutator
[M_{\alpha},b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

Proof.

As
|b(x)|=b(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, we see that

\displaystyle|[M_{\alpha},b]f(x)|

\displaystyle=|M_{\alpha}(bf)(x)-b(x)M_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}b(y)|f(y)|\,dy-\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}b(x)|f(y)|\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b(y)||f(y)|\,dy=M_{b,\alpha}f(x).

By Theorem
5.3
we see that the commutator is bounded.
∎

Let us separate the following result as a Lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let Assumptions
2.11
hold and suppose
[M_{\alpha},b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded, where
b\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and

\displaystyle\dfrac{\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}}<C

for every cube
Q
and some finite constant
C
independent of
Q
.

Proof.

Let us first prove the norm estimate. First of all, we have the following identities for maximal functions of a characteristic function

(6.4)

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)=M_{\alpha,Q}\chi_{Q}(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}\chi_{Q}(x)\,dx=|Q|^{\alpha/n}

and

(6.5)

\displaystyle M_{\alpha}(b\chi_{Q})(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1-\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}b(x)\chi_{Q}(x)\,dx=M_{\alpha,Q}b(x).

These combined with the assumption that
[M_{\alpha},b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}(b))\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-\alpha/n}\|(b|Q|^{\alpha/n}-M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle\leqslant|Q|^{-\alpha/n}\|bM_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}-M_{\alpha}(b\chi_{Q})\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-\alpha/n}\|[M_{\alpha},b]\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\lesssim|Q|^{-\alpha/n}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}.

We proceed as in (
5.4
) to estimate
\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\lesssim|Q|^{\alpha/n}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}

for every cube
Q
, so we have proved the desired norm estimate.

Now we are ready to prove that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Let us denote for any cube
Q
sets
E:=\{x\in Q:b(x)\leqslant b_{Q}\}
and
F:=\{x\in Q:b(x)>b_{Q}\}
. It is immediate that
E=F^{c}
and this combined with

\displaystyle\int_{Q}b(x)-b_{Q}\,dx=0

yields

\displaystyle\int_{E}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx=\int_{F}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx.

Also, for any
x\in E
it holds that
b(x)\leqslant b_{Q}\leqslant|b_{Q}|\leqslant|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)
. Therefore

(6.6)

\displaystyle|b(x)-b_{Q}|\leqslant|b(x)-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}(b)(x)|

in the set
E
.

Let us estimate the mean oscillation of the function
b

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{|Q|}\int_{E}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx\leqslant\dfrac{2}{|Q|}\int_{E}|b(x)-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{2}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)|\,dx.

We estimate the last integral with Hölder’s inequality, use the previously proven norm estimate and Lemma
2.9
to get

(6.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-b_{Q}|\,dx&\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}\\
&\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}\leqslant C.\end{split}

Thus, by definition,
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

In the next Proposition we characterize
\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
in terms of boundedness of commutators with fractional maximal operators. In Theorem
6.10
we improve condition
(3)
to a more intrinsic condition containing the fractional maximal operator. The boundedness of the standard maximal commutator
[M,b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
was covered in Lemma
5.1
.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let Assumptions
2.11
hold. If
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

(2)

The commutator
[M_{\alpha},b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

(3)

We have

\sup_{Q}\dfrac{\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}<\infty.

Proof.

Recall that the assumptions on
\varphi
guarantee the existence of
\psi
which satisfies
(A0)
–
(A2)
,
(aInc)
q
,
(aDec)
1/(r-α/n)
and the relation between their inverses
[
16
, Lemma 5.2.3]
. We proceed to prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. For the first implication we start with the following pointwise estimates

\displaystyle|[M_{\alpha},b]f(x)

\displaystyle-[M_{\alpha},|b|]f(x)|

\displaystyle=|M_{\alpha}(bf)(x)-b(x)M_{\alpha}f(x)-M_{\alpha}(|b|f)(x)+|b(x)|M_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|M_{\alpha}(bf)(x)-M_{\alpha}(|b|f)(x)|+|2b^{-}(x)M_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle=2b^{-}(x)M_{\alpha}f(x),

where the inequality follows directly from
|b(x)|-b(x)=2b^{-}(x)
. In other words, we have a direct estimate for the commutator

\displaystyle|[M_{\alpha},b]f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|[M_{\alpha},b]f(x)-[M_{\alpha},|b|]f(x)|+|[M_{\alpha},|b|]f(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2b^{-}(x)M_{\alpha}f(x)+|[M_{\alpha},|b|]f(x)|.

Since also
|b|\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, recalling
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
we can combine Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
6.2
to get

\displaystyle\|[M_{\alpha},b]f\|_{\psi}\leqslant 2\|b^{-}\|_{\infty}\|M_{\alpha}f\|_{\psi}+\|[M_{\alpha},|b|]f\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|f\|_{\varphi}.

Next we prove
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
and start with Lemma
6.3
, which yields

(6.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}&=\|(b-|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{\alpha,Q}b+|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{\alpha,Q}b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\\
&\leqslant\|(b-|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+\|(|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{\alpha,Q}b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\\
&\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\|(M_{\alpha,Q}b-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}.\end{split}

Letting
x\in Q
and taking account (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) we have

\displaystyle[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=M_{\alpha}(b\chi_{Q})(x)-|b(x)|M_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)=M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-|b(x)|M_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}|b(x)|.

together with

\displaystyle|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{Q}b(x)-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}|b(x)|M\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{Q}b(x)-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}|b(x)|.

We subtract the last display from the previous and get

\displaystyle[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)=M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}M_{Q}b(x).

This and (
6.9
) combine to

\displaystyle\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}-|Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+\|[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}.

As
0\leqslant|b|\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, the commutators are bounded (Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
6.2
), and thus

\displaystyle\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}+|Q|^{-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}+\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}.

Finally, using (
5.4
) for the second term on the right-hand side, we have

\displaystyle\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\lesssim\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}.

Then we show
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. Hölder’s inequality, the assumption and Lemma
2.9
yield

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|(b-M_{Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}\leqslant C.

Now Lemma
6.1
shows that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

Now we prove our main theorem regarding commutators of fractional maximal operators. The main difference to previous Proposition is that the norm condition includes fractional maximal operator instead of a standard Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator. Also, it is enough for the norm condition to hold for any weak
\Phi
-function
\eta
with structural conditions guaranteeing boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator.

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
1<p\leqslant q<\infty
,
\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be such that

\varphi^{-1}(x,t)\approx t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\psi^{-1}(x,t).

Let
r\in(\frac{\alpha}{n},\frac{1}{p}]
. Assume that
\varphi
satisfies
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(aInc)
p
and
(aDec)
1/r
. If
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
b^{-}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

(2)

The commutator
[M_{\alpha},b]:L^{\varphi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\psi}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

(3)

We have

(6.11)

\sup_{Q}\dfrac{\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})}}<\infty

for some
\eta\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for which
M:L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to L^{\eta}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is bounded.

(4)

Condition (
6.11
) holds for all
\eta\in\Phi_{w}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
satisfying
(A0)
,
(A1)
,
(A2)
,
(aInc)
and
(aDec)
.

Proof.

Equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
was proven in Proposition
6.8
. As the implication
(4)\Rightarrow(3)
is immediate, we proceed to prove
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
and
(2)\Rightarrow(4)
.

We first assume
(3)
. Choosing any cube
Q
we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}||Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)|\,dx=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

We first estimate the term
J_{1}
with Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.9
and the assumption as follows

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}\lesssim\dfrac{\|(b-|Q|^{-\alpha/n}M_{\alpha,Q}b)\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}}{\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}}\leqslant C.

For
J_{2}
let us choose any
x\in Q
. Then (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) guarantee us that
M\chi_{Q}(x)=1
and
M(b\chi_{Q})(x)=M_{Q}b(x)
for the maximal function and
M_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)=|Q|^{\alpha/n}
and
M_{\alpha}(b\chi_{Q})(x)=M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)
for the fractional maximal function. With these observations we estimate the integrand in
J_{2}
as a sum of commutators

\displaystyle\big||Q|^{-\alpha/n}

\displaystyle M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)\big|\leqslant\left|Q|^{-\alpha/n}|M_{\alpha,Q}b(x)-|Q|^{\alpha/n}|b(x)|\right|+\left||b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)\right|

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-\alpha/n}|M_{\alpha}(b\chi_{Q})(x)-|b(x)|M_{\alpha}\chi_{Q}(x)|+||b(x)|M\chi_{Q}(x)-M(b\chi_{Q})(x)|

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-\alpha/n}|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|+|[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|.

This yields

\displaystyle J_{2}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|\,dx+\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|\,dx.

Now the assumption
(3)
and a similar argument as in latter part of Lemma
6.3
show that
b\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
which further implies that
|b|\in\operatorname{BMO}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
6.2
to show that
[M_{\alpha},|b|]
and
[M,|b|]
are bounded operators. The first term can be shown to be bounded in the following way using Hölder’s inequality, the boundedness of the commutator, (
5.4
) and Lemma
2.9

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\alpha/n}}\int_{Q}|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\alpha/n}}\|[M_{\alpha},|b|]\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|^{1+\alpha/n}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\psi^{\ast}}\leqslant C.

In a similar fashion the second term is shown be bounded

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}(x)|\,dx\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|[M,|b|]\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{\varphi}\|\chi\|_{\varphi^{\ast}}\leqslant C.

Thus we have shown that

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|b(x)-M_{Q}b(x)|\,dx<C,

from which we know due to Lemma

On well-posedness of Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic-hyperbolic liquid crystal model Thanks: August 7, 2026
Ning Jiang, Yilong Luo

Abstract
. We establish the following well-posedness results on Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic-hyperbolic liquid crystal model: 1, if the dissipation coefficients β = μ 4 − 4 ​ μ 6 > 0 \beta=\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}>0 , and the size of the initial energy E i ​ n E^{in} is small enough, then the life span of the solution is at least − O ⁡ ( ln ⁡ E i ​ n ) -O(\ln E^{in}) ; 2, for the special case that the coefficients μ 1 = μ 2 = μ 3 = μ 5 = μ 6 = 0 \mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\mu_{5}=\mu_{6}=0 , for which the model is the Navier-Stokes equations coupled with the wave map from ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} to 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} , the same existence result holds but without the smallness restriction on the size of the initial data; 3, with further constraints on the coefficients, namely α = μ 4 − 4 ​ μ 6 − ( | λ 1 | − 7 ​ λ 2 ) 2 η − 2 ​ ( 7 ​ | λ 1 | − 2 ​ λ 2 ) 2 | λ 1 | > 0 \alpha=\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}-\tfrac{(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})^{2}}{\eta}-\tfrac{2(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}|}>0 and μ 2 < μ 3 \mu_{2}<\mu_{3} , the global classical solution with small initial data can be established. A relation between the Lagrangian multiplier and the geometric constraint | d | = 1 |\mathrm{d}|=1 plays a key role in the proof.



1.
Introduction

The hydrodynamic theory of liquid crystals was established by Ericksen
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and Leslie
[
12
,
13
]
in the 1960’s (see also Section 5.1 of
[
19
]
). The so-called Ericksen-Leslie system consists of the following equations of
(\rho(x,t),\mathrm{u}(x,t),\mathrm{d}(x,t)
, where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
n\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}(\rho\mathrm{u})=0\,,\\
\rho\dot{\mathrm{u}}=\rho\mathrm{F}+\mathrm{div}\hat{\sigma}\,,\\
\rho_{1}\dot{\omega}=\rho_{1}\mathrm{G}+\hat{\mathrm{g}}+\mathrm{div}\pi\,.\end{array}\right.

The system (
1
) represents the conservations laws of mass, linear momentum and angular momentum respectively. Here,
\rho
is the fluid density,
\rho_{1}\geq 0
is an inertial constant,
\mathrm{u}=(\mathrm{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{u}_{n})^{\top}
is the flow velocity,
\mathrm{d}=(\mathrm{d}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{d}_{n})^{\top}
is the direction field of the liquid molecules with the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. Furthermore,
\hat{g}
is the intrinsic force associated with
\mathrm{d}
,
\pi
is the director stress,
\mathrm{F}
and
\mathrm{G}
are external body force and external director body force, respectively. The superposed dot denotes the material derivative
\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla
. The following notations

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla^{\top}\mathrm{u})\,,\quad\mathrm{B}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}-\nabla^{\top}\mathrm{u})\,,

\displaystyle\omega=\dot{\mathrm{d}}=\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}+(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathrm{d}\,,\quad\mathrm{N}=\omega-\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}\,,

represent the rate of strain tensor, skew-symmetric part of the strain rate, the material derivative of
\mathrm{d}
and the rigid rotation part of director changing rate by fluid vorticity, respectively.

The constitutive relations for
\hat{\sigma}
,
\pi
and
\hat{\mathrm{g}}
are given by:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-p\delta_{ij}+\sigma_{ij}-\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{k,i}}\mathrm{d}_{k,j}\,,

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{j,i}}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{g}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathrm{d}_{i}-\beta_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i,j}-\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{i}}+\mathrm{g}_{i}\,.

Here
p
is the pressure, the vector
\beta=(\beta_{1}\,,\cdots\,,\beta_{n})^{\top}
and the scalar function
\gamma
are Lagrangian multipliers for the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, and
W
is the Oseen-Frank energy functional for the equilibrium configuration of a unit director field:

(1.5)

\displaystyle 2W=

\displaystyle k_{1}(\mathrm{div}\mathrm{d})^{2}+k_{2}|\mathrm{d}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+k_{3}|\mathrm{d}\times(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}

\displaystyle+(k_{2}+k_{4})\left[\mathrm{tr}(\nabla\mathrm{d})^{2}-(\mathrm{div}\mathrm{d})^{2}\right]\,,

where the coefficients
k_{1},\ k_{2},\ k_{3}
, and
k_{4}
are the measure of viscosity, depending on the material and the temperature.

The kinematic transport
\mathrm{g}
is given by:

(1.6)

\mathrm{g}_{i}=\lambda_{1}\mathrm{N}_{i}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{A}_{ij}

which represents the effect of the macroscopic flow field on the microscopic structure. The material coefficients
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
reflects the molecular shape and the slippery part between the fluid and the particles. The first term of (
1.6
) represents the rigid rotation of the molecule, while the second term stands for the stretching of the molecule by the flow.

The stress tensor
\sigma
has the following form:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{2}\mathrm{N}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{N}_{j}+\mu_{4}\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mu_{5}\mathrm{A}_{ik}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{A}_{jk}\mathrm{d}_{k}\,.

These coefficients
\mu_{i}(1\leq i\leq 6)
which may depend on material and temperature, are usually called Leslie coefficients, and are related to certain local correlations in the fluid. Usually, the coefficients
\mu_{4}>0
,
\mu_{i}\geq 0
for
(1\leq i\leq 6
,
i\neq 4)
. Moreover, the following relations are frequently introduced in the literature.

(1.8)

\lambda_{1}=\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\,,\quad\lambda_{2}=\mu_{5}-\mu_{6}\,,\quad\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=\mu_{6}-\mu_{5}\,.

The first two relations are necessary conditions in order to satisfy the equation of motion identically, while the third relation is called
Parodi’s relation
, which is derived from Onsager reciprocal relations expressing the equality of certain relations between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium. Under Parodi’s relation, we see that the dynamics of an incompressible nematic liquid crystal flow involve five independent Leslie coefficients in (
1.7
).

For simplicity, in this paper, we assume the external forces vanish, that is,
\mathrm{F}=0
,
\mathrm{G}=0
, and the density is constant, i.e.
\rho=1
, which immediately yields the incompressibility
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,.
Moreover, we take
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=1
,
k_{4}=0
in (
1.5
), then

2W=|\mathrm{d}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}\times(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+\mathrm{tr}(\nabla\mathrm{d})^{2}\,.

Since
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, this can be further simplified as
2W=|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}
, which implies

(1.9)

\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{i}}=0,\ \tfrac{\partial W}{\partial(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})}=\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i}\,.

Taking
\beta_{i}=0
, then
\pi_{ij}
reduces to

(1.10)

\partial_{j}\pi_{ji}=\partial_{j}\left(\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})}\right)=\partial_{j}(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})=\Delta\mathrm{d}_{i}\,.

Thus the third equation of (
1
) is

(1.11)

\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+\gamma\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}-\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Since
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, it is derived from multiplying
\mathrm{d}
in (
1.11
) that

(1.12)

\gamma\equiv\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}})=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

The detailed derivation of (
1.12
) will be given later with deeper comments on its meanings and implications.

Combining the first equality of (
1.4
) and (
1.9
), one can obtain that

\mathrm{div}\hat{\sigma}=-\nabla p-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\sigma\,,

where
(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})_{ij}=\sum_{k}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\,.
On the other hand, it can yield that by (
1.7
)

\mathrm{div}\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{ij}\equiv\big(\tilde{\sigma}(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}})\big)_{ij}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k})

\displaystyle+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{j}+\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k})+\mu_{5}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj}\,.

Hence, Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic-hyperbolic liquid crystal model reduces to the following form:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+\gamma\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}-\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,,\end{array}\right.

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, where the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
is given by (
1.12
).

In this paper, our main concern is the Cauchy problem of (
1.13
) with the initial data:

(1.14)

\mathrm{u}|_{t=0}=\mathrm{u}^{in}(x),\ \dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{t=0}={\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}(x),\ \mathrm{d}|_{t=0}=\mathrm{d}^{in}(x)\,,

where
\mathrm{d}^{in}
and
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}
satisfy the constraint and compatibility condition:

(1.15)

|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1\,,\quad{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0\,.

Let us remark that a particularly important special case of the parabolic-hyperbolic system of Ericksen-Leslie’s model is that the term
\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}
vanishes. Namely, the coefficients
\mu_{i}^{\prime}s
,
(1\leq i\leq 6,i\neq 4)
of
\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}
are chosen as 0, which immediately implies
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
. Consequently, the system (
1.13
) reduces to a model which is Navier-Stokes equations coupled with a wave map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+(-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2})\mathrm{d}\,.\end{array}\right.

1.1.
\rho_{1}=0,\lambda_{1}=-1
, parabolic model

When the coefficients
\rho_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=-1
in the third equation of (
1.13
), the system reduces to the parabolic type equations, which are also called Ericksen-Leslie’s system in the literatures. The static analogue of the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system is the so-called Oseen-Frank model, whose mathematical study was initialed from Hardt-Kinderlehrer-Lin
[
8
]
. Since then there have been many works in this direction. In particular, the existence and regularity or partial regularity of the approximation (usually Ginzburg-Landau approximation as in
[
16
]
) dynamical Ericksen-Leslie’s system was started by the work of Lin and Liu in
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
.

For the simplest system preserving the basic energy law

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=\Delta\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\,,\quad|\mathrm{d}|=1\,,\end{array}\right.

which can be obtained by neglecting the Leslie stress and specifying some elastic constants. In 2-D case, global weak solutions with at most a finite number of singular times was proved by Lin-Lin-Wang
[
15
]
. The uniqueness of weak solutions was later on justified by Lin-Wang
[
20
]
and Xu-Zhang
[
29
]
. Recently, Lin and Wang proved global existence of weak solution for 3-D case in
[
21
]
.

For the more general parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, local well-posedness is proved by Wang-Zhang-Zhang in
[
27
]
, and in
[
10
]
regularity and existence of global solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was established by Huang-Lin-Wang. The existence and uniqueness of weak solutions, also in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was proved by Hong-Xin and Li-Titi-Xin in
[
9
]

[
14
]
respectively. Similar result was also obtained by Wang-Wang in
[
25
]
. For more complete review of the works for the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, please see the reference listed above.

1.2.
\rho_{1}>0
, parabolic-hyperbolic model

If
\rho_{1}>0
, (
1.13
) is a parabolic-hyperbolic system for which there is very few works comparing the corresponding parabolic model. The only notable exception might be for the most simplified model, say, in (
1
), taking
\mathrm{u}=\mathbf{0}
, the spacial dimension is
1
. For this case, the system (
1
) can be reduced to a so-called nonlinear variational wave equation. Zhang and Zheng (later on with Bressan and others) studied systematically the dissipative and energy conservative solutions in series work starting from late 90’s
[
30
,
31
,

,

,

,

.

For the multidimensional case, to our best acknowledgement, there was no mathematical work on the original parabolic-hyperbolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system (
1.13
). Very recently, De Anna and Zarnescu
[
3
]
considered the inertial Qian-Sheng model of liquid crystals which couples a hyperbolic type equation involving a second order derivative with a forced incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. It is a system describing the hydrodynamics of nematic liquid crystals in the Q-tensor framework. They proved global well-posedness and twist-wave solutions. Furthermore, for the inviscid version of the Qian-Sheng model, in
[
7
]
, Feireisl-Rocca-Schimperna-Zarnescu proved a global existence of the
dissipative solution
which is inspired from that of incompressible Euler equation defined by P-L. Lions
[
22
]
.

It is well-known that the geometric constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
brings difficulties (particularly in higher order nonlinearities) on the Ericksen-Leslie’s system (
1.13
), even in the parabolic case
\rho_{1}=0
. In
[
27
]
, Wang-Zhang-Zhang treated the parabolic case to remove this constraint by introducing a new formulation of parabolic version of (
1.13
). The key feature of their formulation is projecting the equation of
\mathrm{d}
(the third equation of (
1.13
) with
\rho_{1}=0
) the space orthogonal to the direction
\mathrm{d}
. However, for the genuine parabolic-hyperbolic system (
1.13
) with
\rho_{1}>0
, this technique seems not work. Indeed, projecting the third equation of the system (
1.13
) into the
orthogonal
direction of
\mathrm{d}
, we have:

(1.22)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}-\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot(\Delta\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d})\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathrm{I}
denotes the
n\times n
identical matrix. If taking
\rho_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=-1
, (
1.22
) is exactly what was employed in
[
27
]
. But for the case
\rho_{1}>0
, the third equation in (
1.22
) include only
(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}
, while the
parallel
part of the second derivative term, i.e.
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}
is not included so it will have trouble on the energy estimate. In
[
27
]
, this is not a problem since
\rho_{1}=0
, the leading order term
\dot{\mathrm{d}}-\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}
is automatically orthogonal to
\mathrm{d}
.

Our approach is projecting the third equation of (
1.13
) to the direction parallel to
\mathrm{d}
:

\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\gamma\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot(\Delta\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d})\,,

from which we can determine the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}
. Now, the key point is: with
\gamma
given in this form, if the initial data
\mathrm{d}^{in},\ {\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}
satisfy
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
and the compatibility condition
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
, then for the
solution
to (
1.13
), the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
will be
forced
to hold. Hence, these constraints need only be given on the initial data, while in the system (
1.13
), we do not need the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
explicitly any more. These facts will be stated and proved in Section 2.

We remark that spiritually this is similar to
[
23
]
in which they used an unconstrained formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations, namely, the incompressibility is not required in the equations, but only imposed on the initial data.

1.3.
Main results.

In this paper, we establish the following three well-posedness results: first, if the viscosity
\mu_{4}
is large, namely,
\beta\equiv\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}>0
, and the initial energy
E^{in}
is small enough, then the life span of the Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic-hyperbolic liquid crystal system (
1.13
)-(
1.14
) is at least
-O(\ln E^{in})
. Second, for the special case
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\mu_{5}=\mu_{6}=0
, same existence and life span of the system (
1
)-(
1.14
) hold, but without the smallness restriction on the initial energy. Third, if the viscosity
\mu_{4}
is even larger, namely,
\alpha\equiv\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}-\tfrac{(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})^{2}}{\eta}-\tfrac{2(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}|}>0
and furthermore, a damping condition is satisfied, i.e.
\mu_{2}<\mu_{3}
(i.e.
\lambda_{1}<0
) on the model (
1.13
), where
\eta=\frac{1}{2}\min\big\{1,\frac{1}{\rho_{1}},\frac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}\big\}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, we can prove the existence of a unique global smooth solution to the parabolic-hyperbolic liquid crystal system (
1.13
)-(
1.14
) with small initial data. More precisely, the main results are stated in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let integer
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
, and the initial data
\mathrm{u}^{in},\ {\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
,
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
. The initial energy is defined as
E^{in}\equiv|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(I). If
\beta\equiv\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}>0
, and the initial energy
E^{in}
is small enough, namely,

E^{in}<\epsilon_{0}\equiv\min\Big\{1,\tfrac{\rho_{1}\beta^{2}}{[96C(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{6})+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2})]^{2}},\tfrac{\rho^{2}_{1}\beta^{4}}{[96C(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{6})+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2})]^{4}}\Big\}\,,

where
C=C(n,s)>0
is a constant which will be determined in Lemma
4.1
, then, there exists a unique solution
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to the system (
1.13
)-(
1.14
), where
0<T\leq\frac{1}{48C_{1}}\ln\frac{1}{E^{in}}
.

Moreover, the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
satisfies the energy bound

\left(|\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)(t)+\frac{1}{4}\beta\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq E^{in}+12C_{1}T\sqrt{E^{in}}

for all
0\leq t\leq T
, where
C_{1}=C\Big[1+\tfrac{1+2(|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2})}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\rho_{1}})^{3}}+\tfrac{(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}-\lambda_{2})^{2}+(|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2})^{2}}{\rho_{1}\beta}\Big]>0
.

(II). If
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\mu_{5}=\mu_{6}=0
and the initial energy
E^{in}<\infty
, then there is a unique solution
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to the hyperbolic-type system (
1
)-(
1.14
), where
0<T<\frac{1}{4C_{2}}\ln\frac{(E^{in}+1)^{2}}{E^{in}(E^{in}+2)}
.

Moreover, the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
satisfies

\left(|\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)(t)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\widetilde{C}_{2}(E^{in},T)

for all
0\leq t\leq T
, where
C_{2}=C(1+\tfrac{1}{\mu_{4}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|_{H^{s}})>0
,
\widetilde{C}_{2}(E^{in},T)=E^{in}+2C_{2}T\prod\limits_{i=0}^{2}\Big[\big(1-Y(E^{in})e^{4C_{2}T}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+i-1\Big]>0
,
Y(E^{in})=\tfrac{E^{in}(E^{in}+2)}{(E^{in}+1)^{2}}\in(0,1)
and
C=C(n,s)>0
is determined in Lemma
4.1
.

(III). Assume that
\alpha\equiv\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}-\tfrac{(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})^{2}}{\eta}-\tfrac{2(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}|}>0
and
\mu_{2}<\mu_{3}
(i.e.
\lambda_{1}<0
), where
\eta=\frac{1}{2}\min\big\{1,\frac{1}{\rho_{1}},\frac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}\big\}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. If the initial data satisfy
E^{in}\leq\epsilon_{1}\equiv\frac{1}{|\lambda_{1}|+2}\min\big\{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{0},\frac{\theta^{2}}{(8C_{3})^{2}}\big\}
, where
C_{3}=4C^{\prime}\big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\big)\big(1+\mu_{1}+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2}+\mu_{6}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2}+\rho_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\big)>0
,
\theta=\min\big\{\alpha,\eta,\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}|\big\}>0
and the constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(n,s)>0
, then there exists a unique global solution
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to the parabolic-hyperbolic system (
1.13
)-(
1.14
). Moreover, the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)+\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}t\leq 2(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}\,.

To prove the theorem, one of the key is that the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
is not needed so that we can construct the approximate system. Next, we derive the energy estimate for the approximate system from which we use standard compactness method to prove the local existence of the smooth solution which automatically obey
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. With further damping property and new estimate, we extend the local solution to global in time.

The organization of this paper is as follows: in the next section, we justify the relation between the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. In Section 3, we construct the approximate equation of (
1.13
). As mentioned above, in the approximate construction we do not need the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. In section 4, we provide an apriori estimate of the approximate system. Then the solution to the approximate equation and their compactness will be proved in Section 5.

2.
Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1

In this section, we prove the following Lemma on the relation between the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and the geometric constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
is a classical solution to the Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic-hyperbolic system (
1.13
)-(
1.14
) satisfying
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for some
T\in(0,\infty)
, where
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
.

If the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
is required, then the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
is

(2.1)

\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Conversely, if we give the form of
\gamma
as (
2.1
) and
\mathrm{d}
satisfies the initial data conditions
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
,
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
and
|\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
, then
|\mathrm{d}|=1\,.

Proof.

If
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, we multiply
\mathrm{d}
in the third equation of the system (
1.13
) and then we get

\displaystyle\gamma|\mathrm{d}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\Delta\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{div}\nabla\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d})-\rho_{1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d})+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})

\displaystyle-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Since
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, the above equation reduces to

\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Conversely, if we give
\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}
, from the above calculation and the initial conditions we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)^{2}(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)-\Delta(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)=2\gamma(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\,,\\
(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\big|_{t=0}=2{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0\,,\\
(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\big|_{t=0}=|\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}-1=0\,.\end{array}\right.

Let
h=|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1
. Then
h
solves the following Cauchy problem for a given smooth
\mathrm{u}
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}\ddot{h}-\lambda_{1}\dot{h}-\Delta h=2\gamma h\,,\\
\dot{h}|_{t=0}=0\,,\\
h|_{t=0}=0\,.\end{array}\right.

For a given smooth divergence-free
\mathrm{u}
we consider the following flow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}X(t,x)=\mathrm{u}(t,X(t,x))\,,\\
X(0,x)=x\,.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then by substituting it into the equation (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\rho_{1}\partial_{t}^{2}h(t,X(t,x))-\lambda_{1}\partial_{t}h(t,X(t,x))-\Delta h(t,X(t,x))=2\gamma(t,X(t,x))h(t,X(t,x))\,,\\
\partial_{t}h(t,X(t,x))|_{t=0}=0\,,\\
h(t,X(t,x))|_{t=0}=0\,.\\
\end{array}\right.

By letting
\psi(t,x)=h(t,X(t,x))
, it can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\rho_{1}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi-\lambda_{1}\partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\psi=2\gamma(t,X(t,x))\psi\,,\\
\partial_{t}\psi|_{t=0}=0\,,\\
\psi|_{t=0}=0\,.\\
\end{array}\right.

Our goal is to verify
\psi(t,x)=0
for all times
t
. Noticing that
|\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
and
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\,,\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
, we deduce that by Sobolve embedding

|\gamma|_{L^{1}(0,T,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty\,.

We denote by
Z(t,x)\equiv\lambda_{1}\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+2\gamma(t,X(t,x))\psi(t,x)
. Multiplying by
\partial_{t}\psi
in the equation (
2
) and integrating by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}_{L^{2}})=

\displaystyle\left\langle Z,\partial_{t}\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|Z|_{L^{2}}|\partial_{t}\psi|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}|Z|_{L^{2}}\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\,,

which implies that

\partial_{t}\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}|Z|_{L^{2}}\,.

Then by integrating on
[0,t]
we have

(2.12)

\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi(t,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\leq\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi(0,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\int_{0}^{t}|Z(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Let
G(t)\equiv\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla\psi(t,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}
. One notices that

\displaystyle|Z(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+2|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\Big(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}|\partial_{t}\psi(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+|\psi(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\Big)

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+2|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)G(t)\,,

and

\displaystyle|\psi(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle|\psi(0,\cdot)|_{L^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{t}\psi(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\psi(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\int_{0}^{t}G(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

According to the inequalities (
2.12
), (
2
) and (
2
), we observe that for all
0\leq t\leq T

G(t)\leq G(0)+\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)G(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\,,

where
R(t)=\frac{1+|\lambda_{1}|}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+2|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\in L^{1}([0,T])
. Then it is derived from Gronwall inequality and the fact
G(0)=0
that

0\leq G(t)\leq G(0)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)=0

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Consequently,
\psi(t,x)=0
holds for all times
t
and then the proof of Lemma
2.1
is finished.

∎

We remark that if let
\rho_{1}=0
in (
2.2
) (in this case, the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
will make the corresponding change), and the initial data
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
, then we can also prove at later time
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. This is the case considered in
[
27
]
, although it is not treated in this way there.

3.
Approximate system

3.1.
The approximate system of (
1.13
).

We first construct the approximate system of (
1.13
):

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})^{\top}=\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,\\
\displaystyle(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})^{\top}\big|_{t=0}=(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{in},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{in})^{\top}\,.\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle(F_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\tfrac{1}{\rho_{1}}G_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),H_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}))^{\top}

\displaystyle F_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\Delta\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}]

\displaystyle-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\odot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})+\mathcal{P}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\,,

\displaystyle G_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\Delta\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\rho_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}]+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\gamma(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle+\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{B}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}))+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{A}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,

\displaystyle H_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})=

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}]\,.

Here the mollifier operator
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
is defined as
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}({\bf 1}_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\mathcal{F}(f))
and
\mathcal{F}
is the standard Fourier transform,
\mathcal{P}
is the Leray projection,
\mathrm{B}_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}-\nabla\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}{}^{\top})
,
\mathrm{A}_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}+\nabla\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}{}^{\top})
. Furthermore, we still use
\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}
denote the approximate material derivative of
\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}
:

(3.3)

\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}=\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}]\,,

and
\tilde{\sigma}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})
the approximate stress tensor:

\displaystyle(\tilde{\sigma}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}))_{ji}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{k}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{p}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu_{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}^{\epsilon}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{B}_{ki})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{\epsilon})+\mu_{3}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{j}^{\epsilon}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{B}_{kj})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu_{5}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}_{ki})_{\epsilon}+\mu_{6}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}_{kj})_{\epsilon}\,,

and
\gamma(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})
the approximate Lagrangian multiplier:

(3.4)

\gamma(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})=-\rho_{1}|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}){}^{\top}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{A}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,.

3.2.
The local existence of the approximate system (
3.1
).

The following standard technical lemma will be frequently used later in this paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)The following calculus inequality

|\mathrm{v}\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\leq|\mathrm{v}|_{L^{\infty}}|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}+|\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\mathrm{v}|_{H^{s}}

holds for all
\mathrm{v},\ \mathrm{u}\in H^{s}\bigcap L^{\infty}
.

(2)For any
\mathrm{u}\in H^{s}
,
|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\rightarrow 0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, and the inequality
|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C\epsilon|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}
holds for some positive constant
C
.

(3)For any
\mathrm{u}\in H^{s}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\bigcup\{0\}
, the inequalities hold:

|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s+k}}\leq\frac{C(s,k)}{\epsilon^{k}}|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}

and

|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C(k)}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}+k}}|\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}

Proof.

These properties are standard and we omit the proof. The details can be found in
[
24
]
for instance.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s>\frac{n}{2}+2
. Then there exists a unique solution
(\mathrm{u}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})^{\top}\in C([0,T_{\epsilon});H^{s}\times H^{s}\times H^{s})
to the system (
3.1
) for the maximal
T_{\epsilon}>0
.

Proof.

We need only to verify the Lipschitz continuity of the functions
\mathcal{F}_{\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
in the product space
H^{s}\times H^{s}\times H^{s}
. In general, we define the norm on a space
X_{1}\times X_{2}\times X_{3}
as
\left|(a,b,c)^{\top}\right|^{2}_{X_{1}\times X_{2}\times X_{3}}\equiv|a|^{2}_{X_{1}}+|b|^{2}_{X_{2}}+|c|^{2}_{X_{3}}\,
.

By Sobolev embedding and Lemma
3.1
, for any
\mathrm{u}^{1},\mathrm{u}^{2},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{2},\mathrm{d}^{1},\mathrm{d}^{2}\in H^{s}

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\odot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1})-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\odot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\odot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\odot\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\odot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\odot\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\{\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\Delta(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{H^{s}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\left.\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\Delta(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{H^{s}}+\left|\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{H^{s}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left|\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\frac{C}{\epsilon^{s}}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}\left|\nabla(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}+1}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left|\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\frac{C}{\epsilon^{s+1}}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left|\nabla(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}+2}}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{L^{2}}\left|\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}+s+1}}\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{L^{2}}\left|\nabla(\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2})\right|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}+s+2}}\left(\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{1}\right|_{L^{2}}+\left|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{L^{2}}\right)\left|\mathrm{d}^{1}-\mathrm{d}^{2}\right|_{H^{s}}\,,

and

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\partial_{j}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{p}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{1}]-\mu_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\partial_{j}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{p}^{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}^{2}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{2}]\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\partial_{j}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}_{k}^{1}-\mathrm{d}_{k}^{2})\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{p}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{1}]\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\partial_{j}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{d}_{p}^{1}-\mathrm{d}_{p}^{2})\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{1}]\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\left|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\partial_{j}[\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{k}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{p}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{kp}-\mathrm{A}^{2}_{kp})_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{i}^{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{j}^{1}]\right|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(|\lambda_{1}|-\eta\rho_{1})|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\eta|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\mu_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{s}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}(\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(5.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mu_{6}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(1+\mu_{1}+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2}+\mu_{6}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2}+\rho_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}})\Big(|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}+|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{i}_{H^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\big(|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}+|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\big)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\,,

where the constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(n,s)>0
.

Notice that
|\lambda_{1}|-\eta\rho_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}|
and

\displaystyle(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\tfrac{(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})^{2}}{2\eta}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,,

\displaystyle(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|\lambda_{1}||\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\tfrac{(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}|}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,.

Then the inequality (
5.3
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\eta\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla^{s+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\lambda_{1}||\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\alpha|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(|\lambda_{1}|-\eta\rho_{1})|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\eta|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(1+\mu_{1}+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2}+\mu_{6}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2}+\rho_{1}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}})\Big(|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}+|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{i}_{H^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\big(|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}+|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\big)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\,,

where
\alpha=\mu_{4}-4\mu_{6}-\tfrac{(|\lambda_{1}|-7\lambda_{2})^{2}}{\eta}-\tfrac{2(7|\lambda_{1}|-2\lambda_{2})^{2}}{|\lambda_{1}|}>0
. We denote by

\mathcal{E}(t)\equiv|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla^{s+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\lambda_{1}||\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

and

\mathcal{D}(t)\equiv|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\,,

then the following inequality holds :

(5.36)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}(t)+\theta\mathcal{D}(t)\leq C_{3}\sum_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(t)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\mathcal{D}(t)\,,

where the constant
C_{3}=4C^{\prime}\big(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\big)\big(1+\mu_{1}+|\lambda_{1}|-\lambda_{2}+\mu_{6}-\rho_{1}\lambda_{2}+\rho_{1}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\big)>0
and
\theta=\min\big\{\alpha,\eta,\frac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}|\big\}>0
.

If
E^{in}\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda_{1}|+2}\min\{1,\frac{\theta^{2}}{(8C_{3})^{2}}\}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(0)=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\eta\rho_{1}|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}+\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla^{s+1}\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\lambda_{1}||\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+2\eta\rho_{1}|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+2\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\lambda_{1}||\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\tfrac{3}{2}\rho_{1}|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(\tfrac{1}{2}|\lambda_{1}|+2)|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|\lambda_{1}|+2)(|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}\leq 1\,,

which implies that

C_{3}\sum_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(0)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq 4C_{3}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(0)}\leq 4C_{3}\sqrt{(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta\,.

We define
T^{*}=\sup\big\{T>0;C_{3}\sum\limits_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(t)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq\theta\ \textrm{ holds\ for\ all}\ t\in[0,T]\big\}
. By the continuity of
\mathcal{E}(t)
we know that
T^{*}>0
. Then for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}(t)+\left(\theta-C_{3}\sum\limits_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(t)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\right)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}(t)\leq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}(t)+\left(\theta-C_{3}\sum\limits_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(t)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\right)\mathcal{D}(t)\leq 0\,,

which implies that
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\leq 1
for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]
and consequently

C_{3}\sum\limits_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(t)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq C_{3}\sum\limits_{q=1}^{4}[\mathcal{E}(0)]^{\frac{q}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta<\theta\,.

By the definition of
T^{*}
we know that
T^{*}=+\infty
and

\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{E}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{E}(0)\leq(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}\,.

Noticing that
1-\eta\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
1-\eta\rho_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\eta\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\nabla^{s+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\eta\rho_{1}|\lambda_{1}||\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}(1-\eta)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+(1-\eta\rho_{1})|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}})\,,

we obtain the following inequality :

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)+\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}t\leq 2(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}\,.

If we want to extend the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
constructed in Part (I) of Theorem
1.1
to
[0,\infty)
, the condition
2(|\lambda_{1}|+2)E^{in}\leq\epsilon_{0}
is required. So there exists an
\epsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{|\lambda_{1}|+2}\min\big\{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{0},\frac{\theta^{2}}{(8C_{3})^{2}}\big\}>0
such that if
E^{in}\leq\epsilon_{1}
, then there is a unique solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
to the system (
1.13
). Then the proof of Part (III) of Theorem
1.1
is finished.

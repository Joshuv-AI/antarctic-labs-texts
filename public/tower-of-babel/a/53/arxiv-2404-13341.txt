Nirenberg problem on high dimensional spheres: Blow up with residual mass phenomenon
Mohameden AHMEDOU, Mohamed BEN AYED, Khalil EL MEHDI a : Department of Mathematics, Giessen University, Arndtstrasse 2, 35392, Giessen, Germany.b : Department of Mathematics, College of Science, Qassim University, Buraydah 51452, Saudi Arabia.c : Faculté des Sciences de Sfax, Université de Sfax, Route Soukra 3000, Sfax, Tunisia.d : Faculté des Sciences et Techniques, Université de Nouakchott, Nouakchott, Mauritania.

Abstract




Abstract:

In this paper, we extend the analysis of the subcritical approximation of the Nirenberg problem on spheres recently conducted in
[
29
,
28
]
. Specifically, we delve into the scenario where the sequence of blowing up solutions exhibits a non-zero weak limit, which necessarily constitutes a solution of the Nirenberg problem itself. Our focus lies in providing a comprehensive description of such blowing up solutions, including precise determinations of blow-up points and blow-up rates. Additionally, we compute the topological contribution of these solutions to the difference in topology between the level sets of the associated Euler-Lagrange functional. Such an analysis is intricate due to the potential degeneracy of the involved solutions. We also provide a partial converse, wherein we construct blowing up solutions when the weak limit is non-degenerate.

Key Words:
Nirenberg problem, Blow-up analysis, Partial Differential Equations.

AMS subject classification
: 58J05, 35A01, 58E05.

Introduction and main Results

Given a smooth positive function
K
defined on the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
n\geq 3
, equipped with its standard metric
g_{0}
, the Nirenberg problem aims to determine a Riemannian metric
g
conformally equivalent to
g_{0}
such that the scalar curvature
R_{g}=K
. This can be formulated as solving the following nonlinear problem involving the critical Sobolev exponent:

(\mathcal{N}_{K})\qquad L_{g_{0}}u\,=\,Ku^{(n+2)/(n-2)},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
L_{g_{0}}:=-\Delta_{g_{0}}\,+\,{n(n-2)}/{4}
denotes the conformal Laplacian.

This problem has garnered significant attention over the past half-century, with extensive research contributions.. See
[
4
,
6
,

,
11
,
14
,

,

,

,

,

,
26
,

,

]
and the references therein.

Due to Kazdan-Warner topological obstructions
[
12
,
23
]
, the Nirenberg problem is not solvable for every function
K
. Thus, the central inquiry revolves around identifying sufficient conditions on
K
for the problem to be solvable.

Regarding existence results, Euler-Poincaré type criteria have been established by Bahri-Coron
[
7
]
and by Chang-Gursky-Yang
[
15
]
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and by Ben Ayed et al.
[
11
]
and Yanyan Li
[
27
]
on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
. Furthermore, these results have been extended to higher-dimensional spheres under various conditions, including flatness conditions near the critical point of
K

[
26
]
, in the perturbative framework
[
14
]
, or under pinching conditions
[
19
]
and
[
30
]
.

The Nirenberg problem has a variational structure. Indeed its solutions correspond to the positive critical points of the functional

I_{K}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K|u|^{p+1},\quad\mbox{ with }p:=\frac{n+2}{n-2},

(1)

defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
equipped with the scalar product

\langle u,w\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla u\nabla w+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uw

and its associated norm

\|u\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\mid\nabla u\mid^{2}+\frac{n(n-2)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{2}.

The functional
I_{K}
fails to satisfy the Palais-Smale condition and the reason for such a lack of compactness is the existence of almost solutions of the equation
(\mathcal{N}_{K})
. These almost solutions called
bubbles
are defined as follows:

\widetilde{\delta}_{(a,\lambda)}(x)=c_{0}\frac{\lambda^{(n-2)/{2}}}{\bigl(2+(\lambda^{2}-1)(1-\cos d_{g_{0}}(x,a))\bigr)^{(n-2)/{2}}},\quad\mbox{ with }\,\,a\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\mbox{ and }\,\,\lambda>0,

(2)

where
d_{g_{0}}
is the geodesic distance on
(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{0})
and
c_{0}=\left(n(n-2)\right)^{(n-2)/4}
.

After performing a stereographic projection
\Pi
with the
point
-a
as pole, the function
\widetilde{\delta}_{(a,\lambda)}
is
transformed into

\delta_{(0,\lambda)}(y)=c_{0}\frac{\lambda^{(n-2)/{2}}}{(1+\lambda^{2}\mid y\mid^{2})^{(n-2)/{2}}},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3)

which is a solution of the problem
[
13
]

-\Delta u=u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},\,u>0\,\quad\mbox{ in }\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}.

One way to overcome the lack of compactness of the functional
I_{K}
, which goes back to Yamabe
[
37
]
, is to lower the exponent and first consider the subcritical approximation

(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})\qquad L_{g_{0}}u\,=\,Ku^{((n+2)/(n-2))-\tau},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\mathbb{S}^{n},

and its associated Euler-Lagrange functional

I_{K,\tau}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K|u|^{p+1-\tau},\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\mbox{ with }p:=\frac{n+2}{n-2}.

Thanks to elliptic estimates either the solution
u_{\tau}
remains uniformly bounded as
\tau\to 0
and hence converges strongly to a solution
\omega
of the Nirenberg problem
(\mathcal{N}_{K})
or it blows up. In the latter case, following Schoen
[
32
]
, Yanyan Li
[
26
,
27
]
, Chen-lin
[
17
,
18
]
or Druet-Hebey-Robert
[
21
]
, one performs a refined blow up analysis.
It follows from such a blow up analysis that on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
only single blow up can occur, whereas on
\mathbb{S}^{4}
multiple blow up can occur, yet they are isolated simple, see
[
7
,
15
,
26
,
27
]
. However, the scenario shifts notably in higher-dimensional spheres. While on
\mathbb{S}^{5},\mathbb{S}^{6}
blowing up solutions have finite energy
[
18
]
, Chen-Lin
[
16
]
constructed a sequence blowing up solutions of infinite energy. A fact that underscores the profound challenge presented by higher-dimensional spheres. Recentyl Malchiodi-Mayer
[
29
,
28
]
have shown, that finite energy blowing up solutions having zero weak limit are all isolated simple.
Motivated by the intricate question of multiplicity of solutions to the Nirenberg problem, we undertake in this paper a systematic analysis of the scenario where a sequence of energy bounded solution of
(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})
has a non zero weak limit. We point out that such a weak limit is necessarily a solution of
(\mathcal{N}_{K})
and
it is known that such a situation does not occur if the dimension of the sphere is less than six, see
[
11
,
26
,
27
,
6
]
. For spheres of dimension
n\geq 7
our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

Let
n\geq 7
,
0<K\in C^{3}({\mathbb{S}^{n}})
having only non-degenerate critical points
y_{1},\cdots,y_{q}
with
\Delta K(y_{i})\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq q
.
Let
(u_{\tau})
be a sequence of energy bounded solutions of
(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})
converging weakly but not strongly
u_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\omega\neq 0
. Then
\omega
is a solution of
(\mathcal{N}_{K})
. Furthermore there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
u_{\tau}
has to blow up and takes the following form

u_{\tau}\,=\,\omega\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{K(a_{i,\tau})^{(n-2)/4}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i,\tau},\lambda_{i,\tau}}+v_{\tau},

where
\tilde{\delta}
is defined in (
2
) and
v_{\tau}\to 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
.

Furthermore we have that:

i)

a_{i,\tau}
converges to a critical point
y_{i}
of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
.

ii)

\lambda_{i,\tau}\to\infty
,
\tau\ln\lambda_{i,\tau}\to 0
and
\lambda_{i,\tau}d_{g_{0}}(a_{i,\tau},y_{i})\to 0
as
\tau\to 0.

iii)

For
i\neq j,

d_{g_{0}}(a_{i,\tau},a_{j,\tau})\,\geq\,\frac{1}{2}\min\{d_{g_{0}}(y_{k},y_{\ell}):y_{k}\neq y_{\ell}\in\mathcal{K}^{\infty}\}
where

\mathcal{K}^{\infty}:=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:\nabla K(y)=0\mbox{ and }\Delta K(y)<0\}.

(4)

iv)

There exists a dimensional constant
\kappa_{1}(n)>0
such that

\frac{1}{\lambda_{i,\tau}^{2}}=-\kappa_{1}(n)\frac{K(y_{i})}{\Delta K(y_{i})}\,\tau(1+o_{\tau}(1)).

(5)

In addition, if
\omega
is a non-degenerate critical point of
I_{K}
, then
u_{\tau}
is a non-degenerate critical point of
I_{K,\tau}
and

\mbox{Morse index}(I_{K,\tau},u_{\tau})=(N+1)+\mbox{Morse index}(I_{K},\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(n-\mbox{Morse index}(K,y_{i})).

Regarding the proof strategy of Theorem
1.1
, it is essential to provide some insights. While conventional blow-up analysis techniques typically rely heavily on precise pointwise
C^{0}
-estimates of the blowing-up solutions
u_{\tau}
and extensively employ Pohozaev identities
[
17
,
18
,
21
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
32
]
, our approach diverges from this path.
Our strategy hinges on deriving balancing conditions governing the parameters of concentration, utilizing refined asymptotic estimates of the gradient within the so-called ”neighborhood at infinity”. These balancing conditions emerge through testing the equation with vector fields representing the leading terms of the gradient concerning the concentration parameters. By analyzing these conditions, we can extract all pertinent information regarding the blow-up phenomenon.
We highlight that the potential degeneracy of the weak limit introduces an additional challenging dimension to the problem. Estimating parameters within the nontrivial kernel of the linearized operator becomes particularly arduous in such scenarios.
It is worth noting that circumventing the reliance on pointwise estimates and Pohozaev identities can be advantageous, especially in the exploration of non-compact variational problems where non-simple blow-ups may arise, such as in the singular mean-field equation with quantized singularities
[
10
,
25
,
35
,
36
,
20
]
, and in the Nirenberg problem on half-spheres
[
3
]
. The presence of non-simple blow-up points significantly complicates the task of establishing pointwise
C^{0}
-estimates, making our method particularly valuable in such contexts.

In the next theorem we provide, under generic condition, the following converse of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
n\geq 7
,
0<K\in C^{3}({\mathbb{S}^{n}})
. Let
y_{1},\cdots,y_{N}
be distinct non-degenerate critical points of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
and
\omega
be a non-degenerate solution of
(\mathcal{N}_{K})
. Then, as
\tau\to 0
, there exists a unique solution
u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}}
of
(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})
satisfying:
u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}}-\omega
develops exactly one bubble at each point
y_{i}
and converges weakly to zero in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
as
\tau\to 0
. More precisely there exist
\lambda_{1,\tau}
,…,
\lambda_{N,\tau}
having the same order as
\tau^{-1/2}
and points
a_{i,\tau}\to y_{i}
for all
i
such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\bigg|u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}}-\omega-\sum_{i=1}^{N}K(a_{i})^{\frac{2-n}{4}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i,\tau},\lambda_{i,\tau}}\bigg|\bigg|\to 0\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle I_{\tau}(u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}})\to\frac{1}{n}||\omega||^{2}+\frac{1}{n}S_{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}K(a_{i})^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\right),

as
\tau\to 0
, where

S_{n}:=c_{0}^{2n/(n-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{n}}dx.

(6)

In addition,
u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}}
is non-degenerate for
I_{K,\tau}
and

\mbox{Morse index}(I_{K,\tau},u_{\tau,\omega,y_{1},...,y_{N}})=(N+1)+\mbox{Morse index}(I_{K},\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(n-\mbox{Morse index}(K,y_{i})).

We would like to underscore that our main findings in this paper, namely Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, serve as fundamental components in our subsequent work

2
]
. Specifically, they play a pivotal role in demonstrating the existence of infinitely many solutions to Nirenberg’s problem under perturbative conditions.

The subsequent sections of the paper are structured as follows:
In Section 2, we perform a finite-dimensional reduction while

The refined blow-up analysis of finite energy approximated solutions to problem

1.2
.
Lastly, in the appendix, we gather some indispensable estimates crucial for substantiating various assertions throughout this paper.

Finite dimensional reduction

Let
\omega

(\mathcal{N}_{K})

Next for

k_{1}
be such that
\sigma_{k_{1}}\geq 1-\underline{c}_{1}/4
. We decompose
N_{+}(\omega)
as follows:

N_{+}(\omega):=(\oplus_{k_{0}\leq k\leq k_{1}}N_{k}(\omega))\oplus(\oplus_{k>k_{1}}N_{k}(\omega)):=N_{+}^{0,1}(\omega)\oplus N_{+}^{1}(\omega).

Note that

is a fixed finite dimensional space.
Now, since

, it holds that

v_{+}:=v_{0}^{+}+v_{1}^{+}\qquad\mbox{ where }v_{0}^{+}\in N_{+}^{0,1}(\omega)\mbox{ and }v_{1}^{+}\in N_{+}^{1}(\omega).

(26)

Hence, using the orthogonality of the spaces
N_{+}^{0,1}(\omega)
and
N_{+}^{1}(\omega)
, it follows that

\displaystyle Q_{\omega,a,\lambda}(v_{+})=\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}+\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-p\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\delta_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\{(v_{0}^{+})^{2}+(v_{1}^{+})^{2}+2v_{0}^{+}v_{1}^{+}\}

\displaystyle-p\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\omega^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\{(v_{0}^{+})^{2}+(v_{1}^{+})^{2}\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}-\frac{n+2}{n-2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\omega^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(v_{0}^{+})^{2}=Q_{\omega}(v_{0}^{+})\geq\sigma_{k_{0}}\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{ (by using \eqref{h2}) }

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(v_{0}^{+})^{2}\leq\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}=o(\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|v_{0}^{+}||v_{1}^{+}|\leq\|v_{0}^{+}\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|v_{1}^{+}|=o(\|v_{0}^{+}\|\|v_{1}^{+}\|)=o(\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2})

\displaystyle-p\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\omega^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(v_{1}^{+})^{2}=Q_{\omega}(v_{1}^{+})-\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}\geq(\sigma_{k_{1}}-1)\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}\geq-(\underline{c}_{1}/4)\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}

where, for the last formula, we have used (
25
) and the choose of
k_{1}
. Combining these estimates, we get

Q_{\omega,a,\lambda}(v_{+})\geq\{\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}-p\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(v_{1}^{+})^{2}\}+\sigma_{k_{0}}\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}-\frac{\underline{c}_{1}}{4}\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+o(\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}).

(27)

Note that the function
v_{1}^{+}
is not necessarily in
E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
. For this reason we write
v_{1}^{+}:=\sum t_{i}\psi_{i}+\tilde{v}_{1}^{+}
with
\tilde{v}_{1}^{+}\in E_{a,\lambda}^{\perp}
where the
\psi_{i}
’s are the functions
\widetilde{\delta}_{j}
’s and their derivatives with respect to
\lambda_{j}
and
a_{j}^{k}
. Let
\psi_{i}\in\{\widetilde{\delta}_{i},\lambda_{i}\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i},(1/\lambda_{i})\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}/\partial\alpha_{i}^{k}\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle t_{i}+o(\sum|t_{k}|)

\displaystyle=\langle v_{1}^{+},\psi_{i}\rangle=\langle v,\psi_{i}\rangle-\langle v_{0},\psi_{i}\rangle-\langle v_{-},\psi_{i}\rangle-\langle v_{0}^{+},\psi_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=O\Big(\int\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p}(|v_{0}|+|v_{-}|+|v_{0}^{+}|)\Big)=o(\|v_{0}\|+\|v_{-}\|+\|v_{0}^{+}\|)=o(\|v\|)

by using the fact that these functions are in fixed finite dimensional spaces. Thus we derive that
\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}=\|\tilde{v}_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+o(\|v\|^{2})
and therefore, using (
24
), we get

\displaystyle\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}-p\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(v_{1}^{+})^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{v}_{1}^{+}\|^{2}-p\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{a_{i},\lambda_{i}}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}(\tilde{v}_{1}^{+})^{2}+o(\|\tilde{v}_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+\|v\|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{c}_{1}\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+o(\|v\|^{2}).

(28)

Combining (
26
), (
27
) and (
28
), we get

Q_{\omega,a,\lambda}(v_{+})\geq\frac{1}{4}\underline{c}_{1}\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+\sigma_{k_{0}}\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2}+o(\|v_{1}^{+}\|^{2}+\|v_{0}^{+}\|^{2})+o(\|v\|^{2})\geq c\|v_{+}\|^{2}+o(\|v\|^{2}).

Thus the result follows.

Next, we prove the following proposition which gives the estimate of the infinite dimensional part of

Proposition 2.4

Let

Using (
18

(29)

Since

, using Lemma
2.3

v_{\tau}^{0}=o(\|v_{\tau}\|)
. For the other parts, note that

\displaystyle\sum\alpha_{j}\langle\widetilde{\delta}_{j},v_{\tau}^{-}\rangle+\langle u_{\alpha,\beta},v_{\tau}^{-}\rangle+\|v_{\tau}^{-}\|^{2}

Using (

14

\|.\|_{\infty}\leq c\|.\|

\displaystyle\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-1-\tau}v_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{-}

\displaystyle=\int K\omega^{p-1}(v_{\tau}^{-})^{2}+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{-}\|)

(31)

where we have used the orthogonality of
v_{\tau}^{-}
,
v_{\tau}^{0}
and
v_{\tau}^{+}
with respect to
\int K\omega^{p-1}..
. Thus (
30
) and (

-Q_{\omega}(v_{\tau}^{-})+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{-}\|)=\sum\alpha_{j}\langle\widetilde{\delta}_{j},v_{\tau}^{-}\rangle+\langle u_{\alpha,\beta},v_{\tau}^{-}\rangle-\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{-}:=\ell(v_{\varepsilon}^{-}).

(32)

Observe that, using

(33)

\displaystyle\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{-}

\displaystyle=\int K{u}_{\alpha,\beta}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{-}+\sum O\Big(\int u_{\alpha,\beta}^{p-1}\delta_{i}|v_{\tau}^{-}|+\int\delta_{i}^{p-\tau}|v_{\tau}^{-}|\Big)

\displaystyle=\int K{u}_{\alpha,\beta}^{p}v_{\tau}^{-}+O\Big(\|v_{\tau}^{-}\|\Big(\tau+\sum\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{(n-2)/2}}\Big)\Big).

(34)

Therefore, combining (
19
), (
32
)-(

(35)

It remains to estimate the

-part. Recall that

and it is not necessarily in

\displaystyle\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-1-\tau}v_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int K(\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i})^{p-1-\tau}v_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}+\int Ku_{\alpha,\beta}^{p-1-\tau}v_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p-1}v_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}+\int K\omega^{p-1}(v_{\tau}^{0}+v_{\tau}^{-}+v_{\tau}^{+})v_{\tau}^{+}+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p-1}(v_{\tau}^{+})^{2}+\int K\omega^{p-1}(v_{\tau}^{+})^{2}+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|),

(36)

by using the fact that

,

Q_{\omega,a,\lambda}(v_{\tau}^{+})+o(\|v_{\tau}\|\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|)=-\sum\alpha_{j}\langle\widetilde{\delta}_{j},v_{\tau}^{+}\rangle-\langle u_{\alpha,\beta},v_{\tau}^{+}\rangle+\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}:=-\ell(v_{\tau}^{+}).

\displaystyle\int K\overline{u}_{\tau}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}=

\displaystyle\sum\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K(\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i})^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}Ku_{\alpha,\beta}^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}

(38)

Concerning the remainder terms, it follows that

\displaystyle\int(\widetilde{\delta}_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{j})^{p/2}|v_{\tau}^{+}|\leq\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|\Big(\int(\widetilde{\delta}_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{j})^{n/(n-2)}\Big)^{(n+2)/(2n)}\leq c\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|\varepsilon_{ij}^{\frac{n+2}{2(n-2)}}\ln^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}(\varepsilon_{ij}^{-1}),

(39)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p/2}|v_{\tau}^{+}|\leq\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}n}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{n/(n-2)}\Big)^{(n+2)/(2n)}\leq c\|v_{\tau}^{+}\|\frac{\ln^{(n+2)/(2n)}(\lambda_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{(n+2)/4}},

(40)

where we have used the following estimate (see

(41)

For the other integrals in (
38

v_{\tau}^{+}\in N_{+}(\omega)

(42)

Furthermore, using Lemma

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K(\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i})^{p-\tau}v_{\tau}^{+}=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{i}^{p-\tau}c_{0}^{-\tau}K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{\tau(n-2)/2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p}v_{\tau}^{+}+O\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|K(x)-K(a_{i})|\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p}|v_{\tau}^{+}|

(43)

Proposition 3.1

Let
u:=u_{\alpha,\beta}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}+v\in V(\omega,N,\mu)

1.2

Proof
.
We first observe that:

(46)

We take

(50)

(51)

Now, since we assumed that

is small then expanding

around

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p+1-\tau}

by using (

For the second integral in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\overline{u}^{p-1-\tau}v\widetilde{\delta}_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{p-1-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p-\tau}v+O\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|v|(\widetilde{\delta}_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{j})^{p/2}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|v|(\widetilde{\delta}_{i}u_{\alpha,\beta})^{p/2}\Big)

Next we provide a balancing condition involving the rate of the concentration

Proposition 3.2

Let

Proof
.
We take

\langle\overline{v},\lambda_{i}\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}/\partial\lambda_{i}\rangle=0

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\delta}_{i},\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\delta_{i}^{p}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\delta_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=0,

(53)

\displaystyle\langle u_{\alpha,\beta},\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\rangle=p\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}^{p-1}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}u_{\alpha,\beta}=O\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\delta_{i}^{p}\Big)=O\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{(n-2)/2}}\Big).

(E1)

[

Concerning the second part, let

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K\overline{u}^{p-1-\tau}{v}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}=

Concerning the first integral of the right hand side of (

\displaystyle+(p-\tau)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}K(\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{i})^{p-\tau-1}(\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}\widetilde{\delta}_{j}+u_{\alpha,\beta})\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq j}O\Big(\int(\widetilde{\delta}_{k}\widetilde{\delta}_{j})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}+\int(u_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{\delta}_{k})^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\Big).

The estimate of the first one of the remainder terms is given in (

Concerning the first term of (

4
Asymptotic behavior of blowing up solutions

The focus in this section is a refined blow up analysis of finite energy solutions of the subcritical approximated problem
(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})
. Namely, our aim is to prove Theorem
1.1
. To do this, we need to fix some important facts.

Let
(u_{\tau})
be a sequence of energy bounded solutions of
(\mathcal{N}_{K,\tau})
converging weakly but not strongly
u_{\tau}\rightharpoonup\omega\neq 0
. It follows from Propositions
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
(by taking
u_{\tau}
instead of
u
) that the following system holds:

\displaystyle|1-\lambda_{i}^{-\tau\frac{n-2}{2}}\alpha_{i}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}K(a_{i})|=O\Big(R_{\alpha_{i}}+R(\tau,a,\lambda)^{2}\Big)\quad\forall\,\,i\leq N,

\displaystyle(E_{i})\qquad-{c_{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}+{\alpha_{i}}\Big(c_{4}\frac{\Delta K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{2}K(a_{i})}+2c_{5}\tau\Big)=O(R_{2,\lambda})\quad\forall\,\,i\leq N,

\displaystyle(F_{i})\qquad\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|}{\lambda_{i}}\leq c\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{3}}+\sum_{j\neq i}\varepsilon_{ij}+R^{2}(\tau,a,\lambda)+\sum\frac{\ln\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{n/2}}\Big)\quad\forall\,\,i\leq N,

where
R(\tau,a,\lambda)
is defined in Proposition
2.4
and

R_{2,\lambda}:=\sum_{j\neq k}\varepsilon_{kj}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}\ln(\varepsilon_{kj}^{-1})+\tau^{2}+\sum\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{5/2}}+\sum\frac{|\nabla K(a_{k})|^{2}}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}.

(63)

Now, we order the
\lambda_{i}
’s as:
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}
and we start by the following estimate.

Lemma 4.1

For each solution
u_{\tau}
in
V(\omega,N,\tau)
, it holds

\sum_{k\neq i}\varepsilon_{ik}+\sum\frac{|\nabla K(a_{k})|}{\lambda_{k}}+\tau\leq c\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}.

Furthermore,
R_{2,\lambda}
, defined in (
63
), satisfies :
R_{2,\lambda}=o(1/\lambda_{1}^{2(n-1)/(n-2)})
.

Proof
.
We first notice that

\displaystyle-\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}-\lambda_{j}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial\lambda_{j}}\geq 0\,\,\mbox{ for each }i\neq j\quad\mbox{ and }\quad-\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{ij}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\geq c\varepsilon_{ij}\quad\mbox{ if }\lambda_{i}\geq c^{\prime}\lambda_{j}.

(64)

Hence, summing
2^{i}\alpha_{i}(E_{i})
, we obtain

\sum_{k\neq i}\varepsilon_{ik}+\tau\leq c\Big(R_{2,\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}\Big).

The result follows by using
(F_{i})
and the definition of
R_{2,\lambda}
given in (
63
).

Lemma 4.2

For
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, we define

\Gamma_{i}:=\lambda_{i}^{2}\sum_{k\neq i}\varepsilon_{ik}+\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|/\lambda_{i}}{\sum\varepsilon_{ki}+1/\lambda_{i}^{2}}.

(65)

Let
D_{1}^{\prime}:=\{i:\lim\Gamma_{i}=\infty\}
and
D_{1}:=\{1,\cdots,N\}\setminus D_{1}^{\prime}
.

(i)
For each
i\in D_{1}
, there exists a critical point
y_{i}
of
K
such that
\lambda_{i}|a_{i}-y_{i}|\leq C
.

(ii)
If there exists an index
i\in D_{1}^{\prime}
, then it holds that

\sum_{j\geq i}\Big(\frac{|\nabla K(a_{i})|}{\lambda_{i}}+\sum_{k\neq j}\varepsilon_{kj}+\tau\Big)=o\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{2(n-1)/(n-2)}}\Big).

(iii)
There exists a critical point
y
of
K
such that
a_{1}
converges to
y
.

Proof
.
Let
i\in D_{1}
, it follows that
\Gamma_{i}
is bounded and therefore it holds that
|\nabla K(a_{i})|\leq C/\lambda_{i}
which implies the first assertion. Concerning the second one, let
i\in D_{1}^{\prime}
, summing
2^{j}\alpha_{j}(E_{j})+(F_{j})/m
for
j\geq i
where
m
is a small constant, it holds that

\sum_{j\geq i}\Big(\frac{|\nabla K(a_{j})|}{\lambda_{j}}+\sum_{k\neq j}\varepsilon_{kj}+\tau\Big)=O\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}+R_{2,\lambda}\Big).

Since
i\in D_{1}^{\prime}
, it follows that
1/\lambda_{i}^{2}
is small with respect to the left hand side. Thus the proof follows from the estimate of
R_{2,\lambda}
(see Lemma
4.1
).

Concerning the proof of Claim
(iii)
, observe that, if
1\in D_{1}
, the result follows from Claim
(i)
, however, if
1\in D_{1}^{\prime}
, from Claim
(ii)
, we deduce that
|\nabla K(a_{1})|=o(\lambda_{1}^{-n/(n-2)})
which implies the result in this case. Thus the proof of the lemma is complete.

Lemma 4.3

For each
i\neq j
, it holds that :
\varepsilon_{ij}=o(1/\lambda^{2}_{1})
.

Proof
.
Observe that, if
i
or
j
belongs to
D_{1}^{\prime}
, then the result follows from Lemma
4.2
. In the other case, that is
i,j\in D_{1}
, using again Lemma
4.2
, there exist critical points
y_{i}
and
y_{j}
such that
\lambda_{k}d(a_{k},y_{k})\leq c
for
k=i,j
. Two cases may occur:

(a)

Either
y_{i}\neq y_{j}
, and in this case we get
d(a_{i},a_{j})\geq c
and therefore the result follows easily,

(b)

or
y_{i}=y_{j}
. Since we have
\lambda_{k}d(a_{k},y_{k})\leq c
and
\varepsilon_{ij}
is small, it follows that
\lambda_{i}/\lambda_{j}\to 0
or
\infty
. Taking
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{j}
and using the fact that
\Gamma_{j}
is bounded, it holds

\varepsilon_{ij}\leq\frac{c}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}=c\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}=o\big(\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\big)=o\big(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}\big).

Hence the proof is completed.

Next we are going to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Using the above lemmas, Equations
(E_{i})
can be improved and we get

(E_{i}^{\prime}):\qquad 2c_{5}\tau+c_{4}\frac{\Delta K(a_{i})}{\lambda_{i}^{2}K(a_{i})}=o\big(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}\big).

(66)

Now, we claim that

(a)\quad\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{1}}\leq C\qquad\quad\mbox{ and }\qquad\quad(b)\quad D_{1}^{\prime}=\emptyset.

(67)

Arguing by contradiction, assume that
\lambda_{N}/\lambda_{1}\to\infty
. It follows from
(E_{N}^{\prime})
that
\tau=o(1/\lambda_{1}^{2})
. Putting this information in
(E_{1}^{\prime})
and using Claim
(iii)
of Lemma
4.2
, we derive a contradiction. Hence all the
\lambda_{i}
’s are of the same order.
Concerning assertion
(b)
, it follows from assertion
(a)
and Lemma
4.1
. This completes the proof of the claim.

Combining (
67
) and assertion
(i)
of Lemma
4.2
, we derive that, for each
i
, there exists
y_{j_{i}}
such that
\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},y_{j_{i}})\leq c
. Assume that, for
i\neq k
, we have
y_{j_{i}}=y_{j_{k}}
. Thus we obtain that
\lambda_{i}\lambda_{k}d(a_{i},a_{k})^{2}\leq c
. This information and the fact that the
\lambda_{i}
’s are of the same order contradict the smallness of
\varepsilon_{ik}
. Therefore we derive that
d(a_{i},a_{k})\geq c
for each
i\neq k
which implies that
\varepsilon_{ik}=O(1/\lambda_{i}^{n-2})
for each
k\neq i
. Now, putting the previous informations in the right hand side of
(F_{i})
we derive that
\lambda_{i}d(a_{i},y_{j_{i}})\to 0
as
\tau\to 0
.

Regarding (
5
), it follows from
(E_{i}^{\prime})
and the fact that the
\lambda_{i}
’s are of the same order.

Finally, concerning the morse index of the solution
u_{\tau}
, the result is done for
\omega=0
in
[
28
]
. However, for
\omega\neq 0
and non-degenerate, it follows that
u_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha\omega
. In this case, we need to add the contribution of
\alpha
and
\omega
in the morse index formula. Note that, by easy computations, we see that

D^{2}I_{K,\tau}(u_{\tau})(\frac{\partial u_{\tau}}{\partial\alpha},\frac{\partial u_{\tau}}{\partial\alpha})=D^{2}I_{K,\tau}(u_{\tau})(\omega,\omega)+O\left(\Big|\Big|\frac{\partial v_{\tau}}{\partial\alpha}\Big|\Big|\right).

Note that

and
\Big|\Big|\frac{\partial v_{\tau}}{\partial\alpha}\Big|\Big|=o(1)
(see Lemma
6.3
).
This implies that the variable
\alpha
belongs to the negative space. For the contribution of

, we see that it is equal to

\mbox{ dim }(N_{-}(\omega))=\mbox{ morse index }(I_{K},\omega).

5
Construction of blowing up solutions for the approximate problem

Let
n\geq 7
,
0<K\in C^{3}({\mathbb{S}^{n}})

y_{1},\cdots,y_{N}
be distinct non-degenerate critical points of
K
with
\Delta K(y_{i})<0
and
\omega

(\mathcal{N}_{K})

1.2
we assume that
\omega
is non-degenerate and therefore the variable
\beta
introduced in the function

has to be equal to zero, that is,
u_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha\omega
.

As in
[
9
]
, the strategy of the proof is the following: we will define a set
M_{\tau}
whose elements are some points
\mathcal{M}:=(\alpha,\lambda,x,v)\in(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N+1}\times(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}\times({\mathbb{S}}^{n})^{N}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
where

satisfies (
15

N
is the number of the bubbles in the desired constructed solution. Precisely, let

\displaystyle M_{\tau}:=\{\mathcal{M}:=

\displaystyle(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}\times(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{N}\times(\mathbb{S}^{n})^{N}\times H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}):

(68)

\displaystyle v\mbox{ satisfies \eqref{eq:V0}};\,\,|{\alpha_{i}^{4/(n-2)}K(x_{i})}-1|<\tau\ln^{2}\tau\,\,\forall\,\,i\geq 1;

\displaystyle|\alpha_{0}^{4/(n-2)}-1|<\tau\ln^{2}\tau\,\,;\,\,C^{-1}\tau\leq\lambda_{i}^{-2}\leq C\tau\,\,;\,\,\,d(x_{i},y_{i})\leq C\tau\,\,\forall\,i\geq 1\},

We remark that, for

In addition, we define a function

\displaystyle\Psi_{\tau}:M_{\tau}\,\rightarrow\mathbb{R};\quad\mathcal{M}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)\mapsto I_{K,\tau}\big(\alpha_{0}\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\widetilde{\delta}_{x_{i},\lambda_{i}}\,+\,v\big)

(69)

Recall that the variable

Proposition 5.1

Let
\mathcal{M}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)\in M_{\tau}

\displaystyle(E_{\alpha_{i}})\qquad\frac{\partial\Psi_{\tau}}{\partial\alpha_{i}}(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)=0,\,\,\forall\,i=0,1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle(E_{\lambda_{i}})\qquad\frac{\partial\Psi_{\tau}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)=B_{i}\langle\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}^{2}},v\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{ij}\langle\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial x_{i}^{j}\partial\lambda_{i}},v\rangle,\,\ \forall\,i=1,\cdots,N,

\displaystyle(E_{x_{i}})\qquad\frac{\partial\Psi_{\tau}}{\partial x_{i}}(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\lambda,x,v)\lfloor_{T_{x_{i}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\,=\,B_{i}\langle\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{i}\partial x_{i}},v\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}C_{ij}\langle\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\delta}_{i}}{\partial x_{i}^{j}\partial x_{i}},v\rangle,\,\ \forall\,i=1,\cdots,N,

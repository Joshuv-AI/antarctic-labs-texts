A Review of Brown 1971 (in)admissibility results under scale mixtures of Gaussian priors
Yuzo Maruyama, William, E. Strawderman

Abstract
Brown’s 1971 paper “Admissible estimators, recurrent diffusions and insoluble boundary value problems” is a landmark in the admissibility literature. It nearly completely settles the issue of admissibility/inadmissibility for estimating the mean of a multivariate normal distribution with identity covariance under sum of squared error loss. We revisit this wonderful tour de force on its 50th anniversary and present an alternative and more direct proof of the result for generalized Bayes estimators corresponding to priors which are a subclass of scale mixtures of spherical normals.

Introduction

Brown’s 1971 paper
“Admissible estimators, recurrent diffusions and insoluble boundary value problems” is a landmark in the admissibility literature.
It nearly completely settles the issue of admissibility for estimating the mean of a multivariate normal distribution with identity covariance under sum of squared error loss.
We revisit this wonderful tour de force on its 50th anniversary and present an alternative and more direct proof of the result for generalized Bayes estimators corresponding to priors which are a subclass of scale mixtures of spherical normals.

Specifically, let

\displaystyle X\sim N_{d}(\mu,I)

with density

\displaystyle\phi(x-\mu)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x-\mu\|^{2}}{2}\right).

Consider estimation of
\mu

under quadratic loss

\|\delta-\mu\|^{2}
.
Let
\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)
and
m_{\Pi}
be the prior measure and the corresponding marginal density given by

\displaystyle m_{\Pi}(x)=\int\phi(x-\mu)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu).

(1.1)

Then the (generalized) Bayes estimator under
\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mu\phi(x-\mu)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)}{\int\phi(x-\mu)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)}

(1.2)

\displaystyle=x+\frac{\int(\mu-x)\phi(x-\mu)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)}{\int\phi(x-\mu)\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)}

(1.3)

\displaystyle=x+\nabla_{x}\log m_{\Pi}(x).

(1.4)

We are interested in determining admissibility/inadmissibility of generalized Bayes estimators,
for which
Brown 1971
has given an essentially complete solution.
Among the many results of
Brown 1971
, the following result, Theorem 3.6.1 seems to be the most often quoted.

Theorem
Suppose
\Pi
is spherically symmetric. Hence
m_{\Pi}(x)=m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})
. If

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(t)}<\infty,

(1.5)

then
\delta_{\Pi}
is inadmissible. If the integral is infinite and the risk of
\delta_{\Pi}
is
bounded or equivalently

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu}E[\|\nabla_{x}\log m_{\Pi}(\|X\|^{2})\|^{2}]<\infty,

(1.6)

then
\delta_{\Pi}
is admissible.

In
Brown 1971
, however, this statistically important result
is derived as a corollary of quite deep mathematical results, which many find difficult to follow.

Brown and Hwang 1982
provide a sufficient condition for admissibility of generalized Bayes estimators, which is
much more readable than
Brown 1971
and is
based directly on the prior
\pi(\mu)
.
However, the sufficient condition for admissibility is
a proper subset of Brown’s. For example,
some important priors including
Stein 1974
’s prior

\displaystyle\pi_{S}(\mu)=\|\mu\|^{2-d}

(1.7)

which do satisfy Brown’s conditions do not satisfy Brown-Hwang’s.

Maruyama and
Takemura 2008
and
Maruyama 2009
somewhat enlarged the class of admissible generalized Bayes estimators when the prior density of
\mu
is spherically symmetric.
However, these three papers
Brown and Hwang 1982
,
Maruyama and
Takemura 2008
and
Maruyama 2009

do not seriously consider the boundary between admissibility and inadmissibility, which
Brown 1971
does.

In this review paper, we assume the prior density is given by

\displaystyle\pi(\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|\mu\|^{2}}{2g}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g),

(1.8)

with non-negative measure
\Pi
on
g
, and are going to determine admissibility/inadmissibility
of generalized Bayes estimators for a certain subclass of mixture priors in terms of
\Pi
with a completely self-contained proof.
If
\Pi
is finite or proper, the corresponding Bayes estimator is admissible.
Thus we are mainly interested in the case of infinite
\Pi
. However,
we do not exclude the case of a finite measure
\Pi
.

By the identity

\displaystyle\|x-\mu\|^{2}+\frac{\|\mu\|^{2}}{g}=\frac{g+1}{g}\left\|\mu-\frac{g}{g+1}x\right\|^{2}+\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{g+1},

the marginal likelihood
m_{\pi}
is

m_{\pi}(\|x\|^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x-\mu)\pi(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g),

(1.9)

which is finite for all
x
if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{(g+1)^{d/2}}<\infty.

(1.10)

Throughout the paper, we assume the prior
\Pi
satisfies (
1.10
).
By (
1.4
), together with
m_{\pi}
given in (
1.9
),
the generalized Bayes estimator under
\pi(\mu)
given by (
1.8
) is written as

\displaystyle\delta_{\pi}=x+\nabla_{x}\log m_{\pi}(\|x\|^{2})=\left(1-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2-1}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}\right)x.

(1.11)

In earlier studies, the prior (
1.8
) with some regular varying mixing density
has been used in order to establish minimaxity or both minimaxity and admissibility
of the estimator (
1.11
). See Remark
3.2
.
The class of mixing priors we consider is the following generalization of such mixing densities.

Suppose
\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)
in (
1.8
) has a regularly varying density
of the form

\displaystyle\pi(g;a,b,c)=(g+1)^{a}\left(\frac{g}{g+1}\right)^{b}\left\{\log(g+1)+1\right\}^{c},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\text{for }a<d/2-1,\quad b>-1,\quad c\in\mathbb{R},

(1.13)

where (
1.13
) is necessary and sufficient for (
1.10
).
A Tauberian theorem (see, e.g., Theorem 13.5.4 in
Feller 1971
) gives

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{t^{d/2-1}m_{\pi}(t;a,b,c)}{\pi(t;a,b,c)}=\Gamma(d/2-1-a)2^{d/2-1-a}

(1.14)

where

\displaystyle m_{\pi}(\|x\|^{2};a,b,c)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)\pi(g;a,b,c)\mathrm{d}g.

(1.15)

Hence the integrability (or non-integrability) of (
1.5
)
of is equivalent to integrability (or non-integrability) of

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)},

(1.16)

where, as in Lemma
B.2
,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)}\begin{cases}<\infty&\text{either }a>0\text{ or }\{a=0\text{ and }c>1\},\\
=\infty&\text{either }a<0\text{ or }\{a=0\text{ and }c\leq 1\}.\end{cases}

Further the risk of the corresponding (generalized) Bayes estimator is bounded since

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\|\nabla_{x}\log m_{\pi}(\|x\|^{2};a,b,c)\|^{2}<\infty,

(1.17)

which is shown in Lemma
B.1
.
Hence, given the result of
Brown 1971
, we have a following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose the prior
\Pi(g)
has a regularly varying density,
\pi(g;a,b,c)
given by (
1.12
).
Then admissibility/inadmissibility of the the corresponding (generalized) Bayes estimator
with
\pi(g;a,b,c)
is determined by
non-integrability/integrability of

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)},

(1.18)

or equivalently, of (
1.5
).

In this paper, we will provide a self-contained proof of this result.
Further, our more general sufficient condition for admissibility in Theorem
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{g+1}<\infty

(1.19)

includes the case where the risk of the estimator is not bounded. See Remark
3.1
.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
Section
2
deals with inadmissibility and
follows a technique used in
Dasgupta and
Strawderman 1997
.
Section
3
is concerned with admissibility.
An appendix is devoted to technical results needed in the development.

2
Inadmissibility

For the inadmissibility part of Brown’s theorem, the following proof is essentially due to
Dasgupta and
Strawderman 1997
, which relates inadmissibility to solving Riccati differential equations.
Following
Brown 1971
and
Dasgupta and
Strawderman 1997
,
we do not assume (
1.8
) but just spherical symmetry on
\Pi
.
Hence the result is presented in terms of
m_{\Pi}
(
1.1
) and
\delta_{\Pi}
(
1.4
),
not
m_{\pi}
(
1.9
) and
\delta_{\pi}
(
1.11
).

Theorem 2.1
.

The generalized Bayes estimator
\delta_{\Pi}
under the spherically symmetric prior
\Pi(\mathrm{d}\mu)

given by (
1.4
),
is inadmissible if

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(t)}<\infty,

(2.1)

where
m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2}):=m_{\Pi}(x)
.

Notice that the statement of Theorem
2.1
is equivalent to the inadmissibility condition of
Brown 1971
.
Hence, by (
1.14
),
the inadmissibility part of Theorem
1.1
follows.
More concretely, by Lemma
B.2
, the integrability (
1.18
)
corresponds to the case either
a>0
or {
a=0
and
c>1
} and hence
we have a following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

The generalized Bayes estimator with the mixing density
\pi(g;a,b,c)

is inadmissible if either
a>0
or
{
a=0
and
c>1
}
.
For these values of
a
and
c
, the integral
(
1.5
) and

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)}

(2.2)

converges.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

By the
Stein 1974
identity, the risk function of an estimator of
the form

\displaystyle\delta_{h}(x)=x+h(x)=(x_{1}+h_{1}(x),\dots,x_{d}+h_{d}(x))^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}

(2.3)

is given by

\displaystyle R(\mu,\delta_{h})

\displaystyle=E\left[\|\delta_{h}(X)-\mu\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=E\left[\|X-\mu\|^{2}\right]+E_{\mu}\left[\|h(X)\|^{2}\right]+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}E_{\mu}\left[(X-\mu)^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}h(X)\right]

\displaystyle=E\big[\hat{R}_{h}(X)\big],

where
\hat{R}_{h}(x)
is called the SURE (Stein Unbiased Risk Estimate) and is given by

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{h}(x)=d+\|h(x)\|^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}h_{i}(x).

(2.4)

With
h(x)=\nabla\log m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})=2xm^{\prime}_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})/m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})
in (
2.4
),
the SURE of
\delta_{\Pi}
given by (
1.4
), is

\hat{R}_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})=d-4w\left(\frac{m^{\prime}_{\Pi}(w)}{m_{\Pi}(w)}\right)^{2}+4d\frac{m^{\prime}_{\Pi}(w)}{m_{\Pi}(w)}+8w\frac{m^{\prime\prime}_{\Pi}(w)}{m_{\Pi}(w)},

(2.5)

where
w=\|x\|^{2}
.
Similarly the SURE of the estimator

\displaystyle\delta_{\Pi,k}=\delta_{\Pi}-2\frac{k(\|x\|^{2})}{m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})}x

(2.6)

is

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\Pi,k}(\|x\|^{2})=\hat{R}_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})+\Delta(\|x\|^{2})

(2.7)

where

\displaystyle\Delta(w)=4w\frac{k^{2}(w)}{m_{\Pi}(w)}\left(\frac{1}{m_{\Pi}(w)}-\frac{dk(w)+2wk^{\prime}(w)}{wk^{2}(w)}\right).

(2.8)

Now let
q(w)=w^{d/2}k(w)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\Pi}(w)}-\frac{dk(w)+2wk^{\prime}(w)}{wk^{2}(w)}=2w^{d/2}\left\{\frac{1}{2w^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(w)}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}w}\left(\frac{1}{q(w)}\right)\right\}.

(2.9)

Assume

\displaystyle c=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(t)}<\infty.

(2.10)

Then a solution of the differential equation
\Delta(w)=0
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{*}(w)}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{w}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(t)}+c.

(2.11)

Hence, under (
2.10
), the estimator

\displaystyle\delta_{\Pi,k_{*}}=\delta_{\Pi}-2\frac{k_{*}(\|x\|^{2})}{m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})}x

(2.12)

with
k_{*}(w)=w^{-d/2}q_{*}(w)
has the same risk as that of
\delta_{\Pi}
.
Since quadratic loss is strictly convex, the estimator given by the average of
\delta_{\Pi}
and
\delta_{\Pi,k_{*}}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\Pi}-\frac{k_{*}(\|x\|^{2})}{m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})}x,

(2.13)

strictly improves on
\delta_{\Pi}
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note

\displaystyle\lim_{w\to 0}wk_{*}(w)

\displaystyle=\lim_{w\to 0}\frac{w^{-d/2+1}}{-(1/2)\int_{1}^{w}\mathrm{d}t/\{t^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(t)\}+c}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\lim_{w\to 0}\frac{(-d/2+1)w^{-d/2}}{-1/\{2w^{d/2}m_{\Pi}(w)\}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle=(d-2)m_{\Pi}(0).

(2.16)

Then the shrinkage factor of
\delta_{\Pi}-\{k_{*}(\|x\|^{2})/m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})\}x
, defined by

\displaystyle 1+2\frac{m^{\prime}_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})}{m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})}-\frac{k_{*}(\|x\|^{2})}{m_{\Pi}(\|x\|^{2})},

(2.17)

approaches
-\infty
as
\|x\|^{2}\to 0
, which implies that
the average estimator (
2.13
) is dominated by its positive-part estimator.
Hence the average estimator improves on
\delta_{\Pi}
, but is still inadmissible.

Generally speaking, for a generalized Bayes and inadmissible estimator
\delta_{\Pi}
,
it is difficult to find an admissible estimator which dominates
\delta_{\Pi}
.
Interestingly, on page 863 of
Brown 1971
, there is some related discussion on this topic.

Admissibility

Admissibility of (generalized) Bayes estimators is covered in this section.
Subsection
3.1
gives the form of Blyth’s method used,
while Subsection
3.2
gives an admissibility result under general
\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)
.
Subsection
3.3
considers slowly varying mixing density,
\pi(g;a,b,c)
with
a=0
,
in order to study the boundary between admissibility and inadmissibility.

3.1
Blyth’s method

In Subsection
3.2
a form of Blyth’s method (
Blyth 1951
),
given in Lemma

3.1

below, is applied to establish admissibility of a class of generalized Bayes estimators. It utilizes a sequence of proper priors of the form

\displaystyle\pi_{i}(\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|\mu\|^{2}}{2g}\right)h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)

where
h_{i}(g)
satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)<\infty
for any fixed
i

and
\lim_{i\to\infty}h_{i}(g)=1
for any fixed
g
.
Specific choice of
h_{i}
will be given by (
3.9
) in Section
3.2
and (
A.2
) in Appendix
A
.

Under the prior
\pi_{i}(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle m_{i}(\|x\|^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x-\mu)\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)

and

\delta_{i}=x+\nabla_{x}\log m_{i}(\|x\|^{2})=\left(1-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2-1}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}\right)x.

(3.1)

The Bayes risk difference between
\delta_{\pi}
and
\delta_{i}
, with respect to
\pi_{i}(\mu)
, is

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=\int\left\{R(\delta_{\pi},\mu)-R(\delta_{i},\mu)\right\}\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu,

which is rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\|\delta_{\pi}-\mu\|^{2}-\|\delta_{i}-\mu\|^{2}\right\}\phi(x-\mu)\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu\mathrm{d}x

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\{\big(\|\delta_{\pi}\|^{2}-\|\delta_{i}\|^{2}\big)m_{i}(\|x\|^{2})-2(\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i})^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu\phi(x-\mu)\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg\}\mathrm{d}x

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i}\|^{2}m_{i}(\|x\|^{2})\mathrm{d}x.

(3.4)

The following form of Blyth’s sufficient condition shows that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Delta_{i}=0
implies admissibility. The result applies to general sequences of prior densities under the given conditions and not just those constructed as above.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\pi_{i}(\mu)
is an increasing (in
i
) sequence of proper priors,

\lim_{i\to\infty}\pi_{i}(\mu)=\pi(\mu)
and

\pi_{i}(\mu)>0
for all
\mu
. Then
\delta_{\pi}
is admissible if
\Delta_{i}
satisfies

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Delta_{i}=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\delta_{\pi}
is inadmissible and hence that there exists a
\delta^{\prime}
satisfies

R(\mu,\delta^{\prime})\leq R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})

(3.5)

for all
\mu
and

R(\mu,\delta^{\prime})<R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})\text{ for some }\mu_{0}.

(3.6)

By (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}(x)-\delta^{\prime}(x)\|^{2}\phi(x-\mu_{0})\mathrm{d}x>0.

Further we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}(x)-\delta^{\prime}(x)\|^{2}\phi(x-\mu)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}(x)-\delta^{\prime}(x)\|^{2}\frac{\phi(x-\mu)}{\phi(x-\mu_{0})}\phi(x-\mu_{0})\mathrm{d}x.

Since the ratio
\phi(x-\mu)/\phi(x-\mu_{0})

is continuous in
x
and positive, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}(x)-\delta^{\prime}(x)\|^{2}\phi(x-\mu)\mathrm{d}x>0

for all
\mu
.

Set
\delta^{\prime\prime}=(\delta_{\pi}+\delta^{\prime})/2
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\delta^{\prime\prime}-\mu\|^{2}=\frac{\|\delta_{\pi}-\mu\|^{2}+\|\delta^{\prime}-\mu\|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta^{\prime}\|^{2}}{4},

and

\displaystyle R(\mu,\delta^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=E\left(\|\delta^{\prime\prime}-\mu\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle<(1/2)E\left(\|\delta^{\prime}-\mu\|^{2}\right)+(1/2)E\left(\|\delta_{\pi}-\mu\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{R(\mu,\delta^{\prime})+R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq R(\mu,\delta_{\pi}),

for all
\mu
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})-R(\mu,\delta_{i})\right\}\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})-R(\mu,\delta^{\prime\prime})\right\}\pi_{i}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{R(\mu,\delta_{\pi})-R(\mu,\delta^{\prime\prime})\right\}\pi_{1}(\mu)\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle>0

which contradicts
\Delta_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.
∎

Note that the integrand of
\Delta_{i}
in (
3.4
) tends to
0
as
i
tends to infinity.
Hence, in using the lemma to show admissibility, the bulk of the remainder of the proof consists in showing that the integrand is bounded by an integrable function.
Then, by the dominated convergence theorem,
\lim_{i\to\infty}\Delta_{i}=0
is satisfied so that
\delta_{\pi}
is admissible.
Establishment of this dominating function constitutes much of the technical development for the admissibility results given in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
.

3.2
A general admissibility result for mixture priors

This subsection is devoted to establishing the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

The (generalized) Bayes estimator
\delta_{\pi}
given by (
1.11
) is admissible if

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{g+1}<\infty.

(3.7)

Clearly any proper prior on
g
satisfies (
3.7
). Further, even if the prior is improper, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)=\infty
,
the corresponding generalized Bayes estimator is admissible under (
3.7
).
Further, by Lemma
B.2
,
we have a following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

The (generalized) Bayes estimator with mixing density
\pi(g;a,b,c)
given by
(
1.12
)
is admissible if either
a<0
or
{
a=0
and
c<-1
}
.
For these values of
a
and
c
, the integral

1.5
and

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)}

(3.8)

diverges.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let

\displaystyle h_{i}^{2}(g)=\frac{i}{g+i},

(3.9)

which is increasing in
i
and is such that
\lim_{i\to\infty}h_{i}(g)=1
, for any fixed
g
.
The prior
\pi_{i}(\mu)
is proper for any fixed
i
since the mixture distribution is proper, because

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{2}_{i}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)\leq i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{g+1}<\infty.

(3.10)

The integrand of
\Delta_{i}
given in (
3.4
) is

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i}\|^{2}m_{i}(w)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=w\left(\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-1}F(w,g)h_{i}^{2}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}F(w,g)h_{i}^{2}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-1}F(w,g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{\int_{0}^{\infty}F(w,g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(w,g)h_{i}^{2}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g),

(3.12)

where
w=\|x\|^{2}
and

\displaystyle F(w,g)=(g+1)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{w}{2(g+1)}\right).

(3.13)

Applying
the inequality

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\right)^{2}\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}^{2},

(3.14)

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
to (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i}\|^{2}m_{i}(w)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq 2w\left(\frac{\{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-1}F(w,g)h_{i}^{2}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)\}^{2}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}F(w,g)h_{i}^{2}(g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}+\frac{\{\int_{0}^{\infty}(g+1)^{-1}F(w,g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)\}^{2}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}F(w,g)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}\right).

(3.16)

Further applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the first and second terms of (
3.16
),
we have

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i}\|^{2}m_{i}(w)\leq 4w\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{F(w,g)}{(g+1)^{2}}\Pi(\mathrm{d}g).

This is precisely the bound required in order to apply the dominated convergence theorem to demonstrate that

, since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\delta_{\pi}-\delta_{i}\|^{2}m_{i}(\|x\|^{2})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{(g+1)^{d/2+2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2(g+1)}\right)\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|y\|^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|y\|^{2}}{2}\right)\mathrm{d}y\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)}{g+1}

\displaystyle<\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

3.3
On the boundary between admissibility and inadmissibility

For the class of densities
\pi(g;a,b,c)
, Corollaries
2.1
and
3.1
settle the issue of admissibility/inadmissibility for all values of

and
c
except for the cases {
a=0
and
|c|\leq 1
}, where, in particular, the case
a=0
,
b=0
,
c=0
is corresponding to
the
Stein 1974
prior given by (

) since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\|\mu\|^{2}}{2g}\right)\mathrm{d}g=\Gamma(d/2-1)2^{d/2-1}\|\mu\|^{2-d}.

(3.17)

For the cases {
a=0
and
|c|\leq 1
}, the non-integrability

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{g\pi(g;a,b,c)}=\infty

(3.18)

follows.
Theorem
1.1
shows that these values, near the boundary between admissibility and inadmissibility correspond to admissibility.
Recall that Theorem
1.1
is a corollary of
Brown 1971
.
Here, we provide a self-contained proof of the admissibility result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume the measure
\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)
has the density
\pi(g;a,b,c)
with
a=0
,
b\geq 0
and
|c|\leq 1
.
Then the corresponding generalized Bayes estimator is admissible.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
will be given in Section
A
of the Appendix.
Unlike Corollaries
2.1
and
3.1
, we assume

in Theorem

,
which is needed for an integration by parts. See (

) in Appendix for the details.

Remark 3.2
.

Here is a remark on the regular varying mixing density
\pi(g;a,b,c)
given by (
1.12
),

\displaystyle\pi(g;a,b,c)=(g+1)^{a}\left(\frac{g}{g+1}\right)^{b}\left\{\log(g+1)+1\right\}^{c}.

(3.19)

The term
(g+1)^{a}
comes from
Strawderman 1971
and
Berger 1976
, whereas

\{g/(g+1)\}^{b}
comes from
Faith 1978
.
In these three papers, conditions for minimaxity or both minimaxity and admissibility
of the (generalized) Bayes estimator (
1.11
) have been established.

In this paper, we introduce the term
\left\{\log(g+1)+1\right\}^{c}
, mainly in order to
clarify the boundary between admissibility and inadmissibility under
a=0
.
Additionally, by Theorem 1 of
Fourdrinier, Strawderman and
Wells 1998
, we have a following result on minimaxity based on

\displaystyle(g+1)\frac{\{\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}g\}\pi(g;a,b,c)}{\pi(g;a,b,c)}=a+\frac{b}{g}+\frac{c}{\log(g+1)+1}.

(3.20)

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
-d/2+1+\max(0,-2c)\leq a<d/2-1
and
b\geq 0
.
Then the (generalized) Bayes estimator with the mixing density
\pi(g;a,b,c)
is minimax.

4
Concluding remarks

We have revisited Brown’s monumental 1971, paper on admissibility, and have given an independent proof of one of his main results for the subclass of scale mixture priors with mixing density of the form

\displaystyle\pi(g;a,b,c)=(g+1)^{a}\left(\frac{g}{g+1}\right)^{b}\left\{\log(g+1)+1\right\}^{c}.

(4.1)

For this class of densities,
we have established that Brown’s condition for admissibility/inadmissibility, based on the non-integrability/integrability on
[1,\infty)
of
1/\{t^{p/2}m(t)\}

(where
m(\|x\|^{2})
is the spherically symmetric marginal density) is equivalent to non-integrability/integrability of
1/\{g\pi(g)\}
on
[1,\infty)
.
We established a sharp bound between admissibility and inadmissibility for this class.
We also established some results, applicable to more general classes of mixing distributions,
that may be of independent interest.
In particular, we have shown that integrability of
\Pi(\mathrm{d}g)/(g+1)
on
[0,\infty)
suffices to establish admissibility for any generalized mixing prior distribution,
independently of whether the resulting generalized Bayes estimator has bounded risk.

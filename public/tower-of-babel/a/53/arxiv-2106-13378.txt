Schubert polynomials, the inhomogeneous TASEP, and evil-avoiding permutations
Donghyun Kim, Lauren K. Williams

Abstract
. Consider a lattice of n sites arranged around a ring, with the n n sites occupied by particles of weights { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,\ldots,n\} ; the possible arrangements of particles in sites thus corresponds to the n ! n! permutations in S n S_{n} . The inhomogeneous totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (or TASEP) is a Markov chain on S n S_{n} , in which two adjacent particles of weights i < j i<j swap places at rate x i − y n + 1 − j x_{i}-y_{n+1-j} if the particle of weight j j is to the right of the particle of weight i i . (Otherwise nothing happens.) When y i = 0 y_{i}=0 for all i i , the stationary distribution was conjecturally linked to Schubert polynomials [ LW12 ] , and explicit formulas for steady state probabilities were subsequently given in terms of multiline queues [ AL14 , AM13 ] . In the case of general y i y_{i} , Cantini [ Can16 ] showed that n n of the n ! n! states have probabilities proportional to double Schubert polynomials. In this paper we introduce the class of evil-avoiding permutations , which are the permutations avoiding the patterns 2413 , 4132 , 4213 2413,4132,4213 and 3214 3214 . We show that there are ( 2 + 2 ) n − 1 + ( 2 − 2 ) n − 1 2 \frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^{n-1}+(2-\sqrt{2})^{n-1}}{2} evil-avoiding permutations in S n S_{n} , and for each evil-avoiding permutation w w , we give an explicit formula for the steady state probability ψ w \psi_{w} as a product of double Schubert polynomials. (Conjecturally all other probabilities are proportional to a positive sum of at least two Schubert polynomials.) When y i = 0 y_{i}=0 for all i i , we give multiline queue formulas for the 𝐳 \mathbf{z} -deformed steady state probabilities, and use this to prove the monomial factor conjecture from [ LW12 ] . Finally, we show that the Schubert polynomials arising in our formulas are flagged Schur functions, and we give a bijection in this case between multiline queues and semistandard Young tableaux.

2.
Background on partitions, permutations and Schubert polynomials

We let
S_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n
letters, which is a Coxeter group
generated by the simple reflections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1}
, where
s_{i}
is the simple
transposition exchanging
i
and
i+1
. We let

w_{0}=(n,n-1,\ldots,2,1)
denote the longest permutation.

For
1\leq i<n
, we have the
divided difference operator

\partial_{i}
which acts on polynomials

P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
as follows:

(\partial_{i}P)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{P(\ldots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots)-P(\ldots,x_{i+1},x_{i},\ldots)}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}.

If
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{m}}
is a reduced expression for a permutation
w
, then

\partial_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{i_{m}}
depends only on
w
, so we denote this operator
by
\partial_{w}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
be two sets of variables,
and let

\Delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\prod_{i+j\leq n}(x_{i}-y_{j}).

To each permutation
w\in S_{n}
we associate the
double Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\partial_{w^{-1}w_{0}}\Delta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),

where the
divided difference operator
acts on the
x
-variables.

Definition 2.2
.

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
is a weakly
decreasing sequence of positive integers.
We set
\operatorname{length}(\lambda):=r
and call it the
length
of
\lambda
.
We let
\operatorname{mul}(\lambda)
be the multiplicity of the largest part
\lambda_{1}
in
\lambda
,
and let
\lambda_{\operatorname{last}}
denote the smallest part of
\lambda
.

For example, if
\lambda=(6,6,4,3,3)
then
\operatorname{length}(\lambda)=5
,
\operatorname{mul}(\lambda)=2
,
and
\lambda_{\operatorname{last}}=3
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a permutation
w\in S_{n}
,
its
(Lehmer) code
is the vector

c(w)=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
where
v_{i}=|\{j>i\ |\ w_{j}<w_{i}\}|
.
If
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is the code of a permutation, we let
c^{-1}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
denote the permutation with code
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
. The

shape

\lambda(w)
of
w
is the
partition obtained by sorting the components of the code.

Example 2.4
.

If
w=(1,3,5,4,2)
, then
c(w)=(0,1,2,1,0)
and

\lambda(w)=(2,1,1)
.

The following is well-known.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given a vector
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, there exists a permutation
w\in S_{n}
such that
c(w)=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
if and only if
c_{i}+i\leq n
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a permutation
w
is
vexillary
if and only if there does not
exist a sequence
i<j<k<\ell
such that
w(j)<w(i)<w(\ell)<w(k)
. Such a permutation
is also called
2143
-avoiding.

Definition 2.7
.

The
flag
of a vexillary permutation
w
is defined in terms of
its code
c(w)
as follows. If
c_{i}(w)\neq 0
, let
e_{i}
be the greatest integer
j\geq i
such that

c_{j}(w)\geq c_{i}(w)
. The flag
\phi(w)
is then the sequence of integers
e_{i}
, ordered to be increasing.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X_{i}
denote the family of indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}
.
For
d=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
a weakly increasing sequence of
n
integers, we define
SSYT(\lambda,d)
to be the set of semistandard tableaux
T
with shape
\lambda
for which the entries in the
i
th row are bounded above by
d_{i}
.
We also define the
flagged Schur function

s_{\lambda}(X_{d_{1}},\ldots,X_{d_{n}})=\sum_{T\in SSYT(\lambda,d)}\mathbf{x}^{\operatorname{type}(T)}.

Remark 2.9
.

There are also flagged
double
Schur functions, which can be defined in terms of
tableaux or via a Jacobi-Trudi type formula
[
Man01
, Section 2.6.5]
. Below we use Lascoux’s multi-Schur function notation, see
[
Man01
, Definition 2.6.4]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
Man01
, Corollary 2.6.10]

If
w
is a vexillary permutation with shape
\lambda(w)
and with flags
\phi(w)=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
and
\phi(w^{-1})=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
, then we have

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})=s_{{\lambda}(w)}(X_{f_{1}}-Y_{g_{m}},\ldots,X_{f_{m}}-Y_{g_{1}}),

i.e. the double Schubert polynomial of
w
is a flagged double Schur polynomial.

-Grassmannian permutations

Proposition 3.2

Let

then we must have

Remark 3.3

Remark 3.4

If

This means

Definition 3.5

Corollary 3.15

.

Proof.

4.

\mathbf{z}
-deformation of steady state probabilities

In
[

]

\psi_{w}(\mathbf{z})=\psi_{w}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
which recovers the usual steady state probability
\psi_{w}
for

\mathbf{z}=\infty,
” or in other words, when one
reads off the coefficient
\operatorname{LC_{z}}(\psi_{w}(\mathbf{z}))
of the largest monomial in
\mathbf{z}.

In this section we
define some operators on states and polynomials, and then recall some results of

[
Can16
]
.

Recall that indices
of states are considered modulo
n
, so that for
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\operatorname{St}(n)
,
we have
w_{n+1}=w_{1}
.
In this section we will also consider the indices for variables
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})

n

Definition 4.1
.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the simple transposition
s_{i}
acts on the state

by

s_{i}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{i},w_{i+1},\ldots,w_{n})=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{i+1},w_{i},\ldots,w_{n}).

We also define the action of
s_{n}

s_{n}(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n-1},w_{n})=(w_{n},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n-1},w_{1}).

And the
shift operator

\sigma
acts on the state
w
by

\sigma(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})=(w_{2},\ldots,w_{n},w_{n+1}).

If
f
is a multivariate polynomial
in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
, then
s_{i}
acts on

by permuting the variables, i.e.

s_{i}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i},z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{n})=f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i+1},z_{i},\ldots,z_{n}).

And if

is an ordered set of variables, we
let

\sigma(\mathbf{z})=(z_{2},z_{3},z_{4},\ldots).

Given a polynomial

\mathbf{z}^{\mathbf{m}}:=z_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{m_{n}}

the

of the term

is
m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}

and the degree of

is the maximum degree achieved
by its terms.
If

has a unique term

c_{\mathbf{m}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{m_{n}}

of maximum degree, we say it has a
unique leading term
, and we
define the

leading coefficient

\operatorname{LC_{z}}(f)
to be the coefficient

c_{\mathbf{m}}
.

For example,

\operatorname{LC_{z}}\left(\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-y_{n+1-j})^{j-i-1}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\big(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}(z_{i}-x_{j})\prod\limits_{j=i+1}^{n}(z_{i}-y_{n+1-j})\big)\right)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-y_{n+1-j})^{j-i-1}.

Definition 9.1

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{n-1}(\sigma^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1}(\mathbf{z});\mathbf{x}_{\hat{h}};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}(x_{h}-y_{i})\prod\limits_{i=2}^{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1)}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq h\end{subarray}}^{n-\lambda_{1}}(z_{i}-x_{m})\prod\limits_{i=3}^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1}(z_{i}-x_{n+1-\lambda_{1}})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{(\lambda_{1},\lambda^{\prime})}^{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})|_{z_{1}=x_{h}}\prod\limits_{i=3}^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1}(z_{i}-x_{n+1-\lambda_{1}})=LHS|_{z_{1}=x_{h}}.

Proposition A.5
.

Choose
\lambda\in\operatorname{Val}(n)
with
\operatorname{mul}(\lambda)=b>1
. Write
\lambda=((\lambda_{1})^{b},\tilde{\lambda})
for some
\tilde{\lambda}
. We have

(A.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b},\tilde{\lambda})}^{n}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{b-1}(z_{i}-y_{n+1-b})\prod\limits_{i=b+2}^{b+\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}(z_{i}-x_{n+1-\lambda_{1}})

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{z}}_{b}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b-1},\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda})}^{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\operatorname{mul}((\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda}))-1}(z_{b+1}-y_{n+1-b-i})\right)

Proof.

We view both sides as polynomials in
z_{1}
and analyze their degrees to use interpolation. By (
5.1
),
\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b},\tilde{\lambda})}^{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
is of degree at most
(n-\lambda_{1}-b)
in
z_{1}
so the right hand side is of degree at most
(n-\lambda_{1}-b+1)
in
z_{1}
. Likewise,
\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b-1},\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda})}^{n}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})
is of degree at most
(n-\lambda_{1}-b+1)
in
z_{1}
so the right hand side is of degree at most
(n-\lambda_{1}-b+1)
in
z_{1}
. Since
b>1
, it is enough to show that they coincide when we plug in
z_{1}=x_{a}
for
1\leq a\leq n-\lambda_{1}
.

We use induction on
b
. First assume
b=2
. Setting
z_{1}:=x_{a}
on both sides,
A.3
gives

\displaystyle LHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{(\lambda_{1},\tilde{\lambda})}^{n-1}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n-1}(x_{a}-y_{l})\prod\limits_{i=4}^{2+\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}(z_{i}-x_{n+1-\lambda_{1}})

\displaystyle RHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{z}}_{2}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{(\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda})}^{n-1}(\sigma(\mathbf{z});\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n-1}(x_{a}-y_{l})\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq a\end{subarray}}^{n-\lambda_{1}}(z_{2}-x_{m})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\operatorname{mul}((\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda}))-1}(z_{3}-y_{n-1-i})\right).

So the equality
LHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}=RHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}
comes from

A.4
, with the variables shifted by
\mathbf{x}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}}
and
\mathbf{z}\rightarrow\sigma(\mathbf{z})
.

For
b>2
, assume the statement holds for
(b-1)
. Setting
z_{1}:=x_{a}
on both sides,
A.3
gives

\displaystyle LHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b-1},\tilde{\lambda})}^{n-1}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n-b+1}(x_{a}-y_{l})\prod\limits_{i=2}^{b-1}(z_{i}-y_{n+1-b})\prod\limits_{i=b+2}^{b+\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}(z_{i}-x_{n+1-\lambda_{1}})

\displaystyle RHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{z}}_{b}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{((\lambda_{1})^{b-2},\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda})}^{n-1}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}};\mathbf{y})\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n-b+1}(x_{a}-y_{l})\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\operatorname{mul}((\lambda_{1}-1,\tilde{\lambda}))-1}(z_{b+1}-y_{n+1-b-i})\right).

The equality
LHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}=RHS|_{z_{1}=x_{a}}
comes from the
induction hypothesis with the variables shifted by
\mathbf{x}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{\hat{a}}
and
\mathbf{z}\rightarrow\sigma(\mathbf{z})
.
∎

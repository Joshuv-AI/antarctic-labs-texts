Mutations of noncommutative crepant resolutions in geometric invariant theory
Wahei Hara, Yuki Hirano

Abstract
. Let X X be a generic quasi-symmetric representation of a connected reductive group G G . The GIT quotient stack 𝔛 = [ X ss ​ ( ℓ ) / G ] \mathfrak{X}=[X^{\rm ss}(\ell)/G] with respect to a generic ℓ \ell is a (stacky) crepant resolution of the affine quotient X / G X/G , and it is derived equivalent to a noncommutative crepant resolution (=NCCR) of X / G X/G . Halpern-Leistner and Sam showed that the derived category D b ⁡ ( coh ⁡ 𝔛 ) \operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathfrak{X}) is equivalent to certain subcategories of D b ⁡ ( coh ⁡ [ X / G ] ) \operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}[X/G]) , which are called magic windows. This paper studies equivalences between magic windows that correspond to wall-crossings in a hyperplane arrangement in terms of NCCRs. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

A crepant resolution is one of the best modifications of singularities.
This can be regarded as a higher dimensional analog of minimal resolutions of surface singularities, and in the terminology from minimal model theory, a crepant resolution can be paraphrased as a smooth minimal model of the singularity.

As a noncommutative analog of the notion of crepant resolutions, Van den Bergh introduced noncommutative crepant resolutions (= NCCRs)
[
38
,
39
]
.
In both commutative and noncommutative cases, the existence of such a resolution is not always true.
There are two large classes of singularities for which the study of NCCRs (and crepant resolutions) is well established.
One is the class of quotient singularities arising from quasi-symmetric representations of reductive groups, which was first studied in
[
30
]
, and the other is the class of (
3
-fold) compound du Val singularities, studied in
[
37
,
40
]
.
To investigate the latter class, Iyama and Wemyss
[
22
]
introduced an operation called
mutation
, which produce a new NCCR from the original one.
By Kawamata
[
24
]
, it is known that all minimal models (and hence all crepant resolutions) are connected by iterated flops, and mutations can be regarded as a noncommutative counterpart of flops. Indeed, it is proved in
[
40
]
that the derived equivalences associated to 3-fold flops, which are established in
[
5
,
7
]
, correspond to derived equivalences associated to mutations of NCCRs. This interpretation and the technique of mutations of NCCRs provides the main ingredients for the study of Bridgeland stability conditions for 3-fold flops
[
17
,
18
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to import such a technology established by
[
22
]
to deepen the study of NCCRs for quotient singularities arising from quasi-symmetric representations,
by studying the combinatorics associated to the representation,
and by accessing to the ideas from
[
11
,
30
]
.

1.2.
Exchanges and mutations of modifying modules

The present section, Section
1.3
, and Section
1.4
explain the setting of this paper, and recall some terminologies, notations, and known results that are needed to state our results.
The precise statements of main results are given in Section
1.5
.

Let
R
be a normal equidimensional Gorenstein ring. A finitely generated reflexive
R
-module
M
is said to be
modifying
if the endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
is Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module. A
noncommutative crepant resolution
(=NCCR) of
R
is the endomorphism ring
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
of some modifying
R
-module
M
such that the global dimension of
\Lambda
is finite. If
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
is an NCCR, we say that
M
gives an NCCR.
The following is one of the central problems about NCCRs.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
38
]
)
.

Let
R
be an equidimensional normal Gorenstein ring.
Then all crepant resolutions and all NCCRs of
R
are derived equivalent.

In relation to this derived equivalence problem, Iyama and Wemyss introduced the notion of mutation of NCCRs, which is defined in the following way.
Let
M,N
and
L
be finitely generated reflexive
R
-modules. A
right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-approximation
of
M
is a morphism

\alpha\colon K\to M

from
K\in\operatorname{add}L
such that the induced morphism
\alpha\circ(-)\colon\operatorname{Hom}(N,K)\to\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)
is surjective. A
right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-exchange
of
M
is the kernel of a right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-approximation of
M
. Although right exchanges are not unique, we denote by
\varepsilon_{(L,N)}(M)
one of right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-exchanges of
M
by abuse of notation. By definition, there is an exact sequence

0\to\varepsilon_{(L,N)}(M)\hookrightarrow K\xrightarrow{\alpha}M,

where
\alpha
is a right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-approximation of
M
. Dually a
left
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-exchange
of
M
is defined to be the
R
-dual of a right
(\operatorname{add}L^{*})_{N^{*}}
-exchange of
M^{*}
, and one of them is denoted by
\varepsilon^{-}_{(L,N)}(M)
.
If
L=N
, the right and left exchanges are simply written by
\varepsilon_{N}(M)
and
\varepsilon^{-}_{N}(M)
, respectively. Suppose that
M=N\oplus N^{c}
for some
R
-module
N^{c}
. The
right mutation

\mu_{N}(M)
and
left mutation

\mu_{N}^{-}(M)
of
M
at
N
are defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{N}(M)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=N\oplus\varepsilon_{N}(N^{c})

\displaystyle\mu_{N}^{-}(M)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=N\oplus\varepsilon^{-}_{N}(N^{c}).

For a positive integer
m
, an
m
-times iterated exchange is written by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{(L,N)}^{\pm m}(M)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\underbrace{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{(L,N)}\bigl(\cdots\varepsilon^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\varepsilon^{\pm}_{(L,N)}}_{m}(M))\cdots\bigr)

and similarly

\mu_{N}^{\pm m}(M)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=N\oplus\varepsilon_{N}^{\pm m}(N^{c})
.
By
[
22
]
, if
M
gives an NCCR of
R
, so do
\mu_{N}(M)
and
\mu_{N}^{-}(M)
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
,
\operatorname{End}_{R}(\mu_{N}(M))
and
\operatorname{End}(\mu^{-}_{N}(M))
are all derived equivalent. Thus mutation enables us to obtain a new NCCR from the original one, and it produces a canonical derived equivalence

\Phi_{N}\colon\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\operatorname{End}_{R}(M))\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\operatorname{End}_{R}(\mu^{-}_{N}(M))),

which is called a
mutation functor
at
N
.

It is natural to ask if two given NCCRs are connected by (iterated) mutations or not.
It is known that, for many types of singularities, their natural NCCRs are actually connected by mutations
[
8
,
9
,
13
,
26
,
34
,
40
]
.
One of the main aims of this paper is to present a similar result for NCCRs associated to the quotient of quasi-symmetric representations, which are recalled in the next section.

1.3.
Quasi-symmetric representation

Let
G
be a connected reductive group,

\operatorname{\sf{M}}
the character lattice of a maximal torus
T\subseteq G
,
and
W
the Weyl group of
G
.
The Weyl group
W
naturally acts on the lattice
\operatorname{\sf{M}}
.
Let
X
be a
G
-representation with
T
-weights
\upbeta_{1},\ldots,\upbeta_{d}
, and assume that
X
is
quasi-symmetric
, i.e. the equality
\sum_{\upbeta_{i}\in L}\upbeta_{i}=0
holds for every 1-dimensional subspace

in the real space
\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{\sf{M}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
.
Consider a
W
-invariant convex polytope

\bm{\Sigma}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}\upbeta_{i}\middle|a_{i}\in[0,1]\right\}\subset\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}.

We always assume that
\bm{\Sigma}
linearly spans
\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

For a generic element
\ell\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}
, and consider the GIT quotient stack

\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=[X^{\rm ss}(\ell)/G].

To study the derived category of the stack
\mathfrak{X}
, Halpern-Leistner and Sam
[
11
]
introduced
magic window categories
,
which are defined using a certain
W
-invariant convex polytope
\bm{\nabla}\subseteq\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
a periodic locally finite hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}^{W}
in
\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}
(Definition
3.6
).
More precisely, the magic window category
\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla})
is associated to each element
\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
, and it is defined as the thick subcategory of
\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}[X/G])
generated by

-equivariant vector bundles of the form
V(\chi)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
with
\chi\in(\delta+\bm{\nabla})\cap\operatorname{\sf{M}}^{+}
, where
V(\chi)
denotes the irreducible
G
-representation with highest weight
\chi
, and
\operatorname{\sf{M}}^{+}
denotes the set of dominant weights. Furthermore,
[
11
]
proved that the restriction functor

\mathop{\sf res}\nolimits\colon\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla})\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathfrak{X})

is an equivalence for all
\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
.

Recall that
G
acts on the ring
\Bbbk[X]
of regular functions on
X
by
(g\cdot\varphi)(x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\varphi(g^{-1}x)
, and the ring
R\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\Bbbk[X]^{G}
of
G
-invariant regular functions defines a quotient singularity
X/G\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Spec}R
.
A quasi-symmetric representation
X
is said to be
generic
if
{\rm codim}(X\backslash X^{\rm ts},X)\geq 2
, where
X^{\rm ts}
is a
G
-invariant open subspace of
X
defined by

X^{\rm ts}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{x\in X\mid\mbox{the orbit $G\cdot x$ is closed and the stabilizer $G_{x}$ is trivial}\}.

If
X
is generic, then
the natural map

\varphi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to\operatorname{Spec}R

is a (stacky) crepant resolution of
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

For each
\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}
,
put
\mathcal{C}_{\delta}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=(\delta+\bm{\nabla})\cap\operatorname{\sf{M}}^{+}
.
If
\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
,
the magic window
\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla})
has a canonical tilting object
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}}V(\chi)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
and hence
\mathcal{V}_{\delta}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\mathop{\sf res}\nolimits(\mathcal{T}_{\delta})
is a tilting bundle on
\mathfrak{X}
.
In particular, the bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\delta}
produces an equivalence

\operatorname{{\bf R}Hom}(\mathcal{T}_{\delta},-)\colon\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla})\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda_{\delta}).

between the magic window and the derived category of finitely generated right modules over
\Lambda_{\delta}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{T}_{\delta})
.
Assume that
X
is generic.
For a character
\chi\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}
, the corresponding reflexive
R
-module
M(\chi)
is defined by
M(\chi)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\left(V(\chi)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\right)^{G}
. Then

M_{\delta}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}}M(\chi)

.
The next section explains what happens when the parameter

1.4.
Wall crossing and fundamental group action

For each
\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
, let
C(\delta)
be the connected component of
\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
that contains
\delta
.
We say that an ordered pair
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
of elements
\delta,\delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
is an
adjacent pair
if
C(\delta)
and
C(\delta^{\prime})
are not equal and they share a codimension one wall.
Let
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
be an adjacent pair, and
\mathfrak{X}=[X^{\rm ss}(\ell)/G]
the GIT quotient stack, where
\ell\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\delta^{\prime}-\delta\in\operatorname{\sf{M}}^{W}
.
The ordered pair
(\delta,\delta^{\prime})
corresponds to a wall crossing in
\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}
, and it induces an equivalence

(1.A)

\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla})\xrightarrow{\mathop{\sf res}\nolimits}\operatorname{D^{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\mathop{\sf res}\nolimits^{-1}}\mathcal{M}(\delta^{\prime}+\bm{\nabla})

of magic windows. In
[
11
]
, it is proved that the equivalences of magic windows of the form (

) defines a group action

\uprho\colon\pi_{1}\bigl(\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}\bigr)\to\operatorname{Auteq}\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla}).

This action is an analog of the group action on the derived category of 3-fold flops
[
23
]
, which acts on Bridgeland stability conditions for 3-fold flops as a deck transformation of some regular covering map
[
18
]
. Furthermore, the action
\uprho
can be extended to a group action

\widetilde{\uprho}\colon\pi_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{X})\to\operatorname{Auteq}\mathcal{M}(\delta+\bm{\nabla}),

where
\mathcal{K}_{X}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bigl(\operatorname{\sf{M}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{W}\backslash\mathcal{H}^{W}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigr)/\operatorname{\sf{M}}^{W}
is the
stringy Kähler moduli space
(= SKMS) of
X
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Notation is same as above.

(1)

If

\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L^{\prime},N)}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\mathcal{S}),

(2)

\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N^{\prime})}(\mathcal{S}),

(3)

For another full subcategory

\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\mathcal{S})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{(L,N)}(\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}).

Proof.

(1), (2) and the first assertion in (3) are obvious. The second assertion in (3) follows from the fact that, if

\alpha\colon K\to M
and

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

If
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N,M\oplus N)\in\operatorname{CM}R
, then

M\in\mathcal{E}^{-}_{(L,N)}\mathcal{E}_{(L,N)}(M)
holds.
Dually, assuming
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M\oplus N,N)\in\operatorname{CM}R
implies that
M\in\mathcal{E}_{(L,N)}\mathcal{E}^{-}_{(L,N)}(M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{Hom}(N,M\oplus N)
is Cohen-Macaulay,
and consider an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ker}\alpha\to K\xrightarrow{\alpha}M,

where
\alpha
is a right
(\operatorname{add}L)_{N}
-approximation.
Put
\mathbb{F}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N,-)
.
Then the definition of the approximation gives an exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{F}\operatorname{Ker}\alpha\to\mathbb{F}K\to\mathbb{F}M\to 0.

Now applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\mathbb{F}R)
to this sequence together with the reflexive equivalence proves that the dual sequence

0\to M^{*}\to K^{*}\to(\operatorname{Ker}\alpha)^{*}

is exact.

By the assumption,
\mathbb{F}M
is Cohen-Macaulay, and
\operatorname{End}(N)
is
d
-sCY
[
22
, Lemma 2.22]
. Applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{F}(-),\mathbb{F}N)
to the original sequence and using the vanishing
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\operatorname{End}(N)}(\mathbb{F}M,\operatorname{End}(N))=0
imply that the sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{F}M,\mathbb{F}N)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{F}K,\mathbb{F}N)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{F}\operatorname{Ker}\alpha,\mathbb{F}N)\to 0

remains exact.
Since all modules in the original sequence are reflexive, the reflexive equivalence and the duality yield an isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{End}(N)}(\mathbb{F}M,\mathbb{F}N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N^{*},M^{*})

and similar isomorphisms for

and
\operatorname{Ker}\alpha
, which imply the exactness of the sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}(N^{*},M^{*})\to\operatorname{Hom}(N^{*},K^{*})\to\operatorname{Hom}(N^{*},(\operatorname{Ker}\alpha)^{*})\to 0.

Thus the dual morphism

K^{*}\to(\operatorname{Ker}\alpha)^{*}

is a right
(\operatorname{add}L^{*})_{N^{*}}
-approximation with the kernel

, which proves the first assertion. The second assertion follows from a similar argument.
∎

The following says that exchanging a direct summand of a modifying module gives a new modifying module in nice situations.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
N\oplus M
be a modifying
R

such that
\operatorname{Hom}(N,L)\in\operatorname{CM}R

.

We give the proofs for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

. The following equivalence holds.

M\in\operatorname{CM}R\Longleftrightarrow M^{*}\in\operatorname{CM}R.

Proof.

We may assume that
R
is local. Since
M
is reflexive, it is enough to show the direction
(\Rightarrow)
. Since
R
is Gorenstein, its injective dimension is finite. Thus the result follows from
[

, Proposition 3.3.3 (b)]
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein normal ring, and let

. Then

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)\in\operatorname{CM}R\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(N,M)\in\operatorname{CM}R.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the direction (
\Rightarrow
). Assume that
\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)\in\operatorname{CM}R
. Then Lemma
2.10
implies that
\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)^{*}\in\operatorname{CM}R
. But by Lemma
[
22
, Lemma 2.9]
, there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)^{*}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)
, which shows that
\operatorname{Hom}(N,M)\in\operatorname{CM}R
.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Assume that

and
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}^{m}_{(L,N)}(M)

By definition, there are

Indeed, applying the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(N,-)
to the exact sequence

Since

This section discusses tilting bundles over algebraic stacks.
We start from recalling some basic facts on the derived categories of algebraic stacks.

For an algebraic stack
\mathfrak{Y}
, let
\operatorname{D}(\mathfrak{Y})
be the (unbounded) derived category of modules over
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{Y}}

Let
\operatorname{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathfrak{Y})
be the triangulated full subcategory of complexes with quasi-coherent cohomologies.
Similarly, if

\operatorname{D}_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{Y})

\operatorname{D}(\mathfrak{Y})
(resp.​
\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathfrak{Y})

The following fact is standard.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be a quasi-compact separated algebraic stack with finite stabilizers. Then the natural functor

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}(\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathfrak{Y})\to\operatorname{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathfrak{Y})

gives an equivalence of categories.
If
\mathfrak{Y}
is in addition noetherian, then the natural functors

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}^{\ast}(\operatorname{coh}\mathfrak{Y})\to\operatorname{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ast}(\operatorname{Qcoh}\mathfrak{Y})\to\operatorname{D}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\ast}(\mathfrak{Y})

Proof.

This is proved in

Lemma 2.21

Let

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{Y}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{F},f_{*}\mathcal{E}),~\text{and}

Proof.

Denote by

the smooth locus of

Proof.

3.21

.

Since
\chi+\rho\in\partial(\delta_{0}+(1/2)\bm{\Sigma})
,
F\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=F_{\chi}(\delta_{0})
exists. Since
\chi+\rho
lies in the relative interior of
F
, Lemma
3.23
shows that there exists

such that

-(\chi+\rho)+2(\delta_{0}-(1/2)\upbeta_{F}^{+})=\delta+(1/2)\sigma.

By Lemma
3.8
(1), we may assume that

. Then the equation (

Proposition 3.25
.

Let

(1)

\upbeta_{F_{\chi^{\prime}}(\delta_{0})}^{+}=-w_{0}(\upbeta_{F_{\chi}(\delta_{0})}^{+})
.

(2)

\mu_{(\delta,\delta^{\prime})}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\delta}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime}}\,\,\,\,\raisebox{4.0pt}{$\sim$}{\kern-11.00008pt\longrightarrow}\,\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime}}\backslash\mathcal{C}_{\delta}

Proof.

(1) Since
\rho+\chi+\upbeta_{F_{\chi}(\delta_{0})}^{+}
lies in the relative interior of the face
F_{\chi}(\delta_{0})^{*}
,
the element
\rho+\chi^{\prime}=w_{0}(\rho+\chi+\upbeta_{F_{\chi}(\delta_{0})}^{+})
lies in the relative interior of the face

\displaystyle\mu_{(\delta^{\prime},\delta)}(\chi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(\chi^{\prime}+\upbeta_{F_{\chi^{\prime}}(\delta_{0})}^{+})^{+}

∎

.

.

,

\displaystyle\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{F}^{+}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\bigcup_{1\leq m\leq d_{F}^{+}-1}\raisebox{1.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{m}\mathcal{W}_{F}^{+}.

Proposition 3.27
.

Assume that
\chi
is dominant and

is generic.
Then the following hold.

(1)

If
\langle\ell,\lambda\rangle>0
for some

, then the restriction of

to
[X^{\rm ss}(\ell)/G]

(2)

and

.

(3)

The terms

-equivariant locally free sheaves of the form

where

for all

(2) Since

. Since

, and this flag induces a filtration

(3.U)

whose factors

for some

.

, the cohomology group

(3.V)

denotes

for all

for all

.

4.1.
Wall crossing and tilting equivalence

This section shows that wall-crossings of magic windows correspond to equivalences that are induced by tilting modules.

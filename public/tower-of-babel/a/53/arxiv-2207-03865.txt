A new perspective on the fictitious space lemma
X.Claeys

Abstract




Introduction

In the present contribution we propose a new proof of the
so-called fictitious space lemma. For the proof, we exhibit
an explicit expression for the inverse of additive Schwarz preconditionners
in terms of Moore-Penrose pseudo inverse of the map associated to
the decomposition over the subdomain partition.

We will first briefly recall the definition of the pseudo-inverse of a matrix
and some of its remarkable properties. We will then explain how this concept
can be used to reformulate the fictitious space lemma in a very compact form.
We will then give an aletrnative proof of the fictious space lemma.
As a remarkable feature, this proof does not rely on Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
as opposed to previous proofs provided by Nepomnyaschikh
[
5
,
4
]
,
Griebel and Oswald
[
2
]
or Dolean, Jolivet and Nataf
[
1
]
,
see also
[
6
]
. The present proof applies directly in the infinite dimensional case.

1
Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse

Assume given Hilbert spaces
\mathrm{H}
(resp.
\mathrm{V}
) equipped with the norms

\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{H}}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{V}}
) and consider
a surjective map
\mathrm{R}:\mathrm{V}\to\mathrm{H}
. Define
\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{y}\}):=\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H},\;\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{y}\}
. The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse
of this map, denoted
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{V}
is defined, for all
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{H}
, by

\mathrm{R}\cdot\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{y}\quad\;\;\text{and}\;\;\quad\|\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathrm{V}}=\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{y}\})}\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathrm{V}}.

(1)

The property above directly implies that
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}
is injective.
Let us denote
\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{R})^{\perp}
. For any
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{H}
, since
the restricted operator
\mathrm{R}|_{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}}:\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}\to\mathrm{H}
is a bijection,
there exists a unique
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
such that
\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{y}
.
Besides, if
\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{V}
is another element satisfying
\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{y}

then
\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{R})
so that
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{V}}
and thus,
by Pythagore’s rule,

\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}+\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}=\|\boldsymbol{x}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{V}}^{2}

(2)

As a consequence
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{V}
solves the minimization
problem (
1
) i.e.
\boldsymbol{x}=\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{y}
.
From this discussion we conclude that
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}=(\mathrm{R}|_{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}})^{-1}
.

The property
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}=\mathrm{Id}
implies that
(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R})^{2}=\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{R}^{\dagger})\mathrm{R}=\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}
i.e.
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}

is a projector. Because
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}
is injective we obtain

\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R})=\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{R})
.Besides for any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{V}_{R}

satisfying
\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{y}
, we have seen that
\boldsymbol{x}=\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{y}=\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x}

which implies
\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R})
. As a conclusion, since

\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{R})
and
\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{R}}
are orthogonal by definition, we conclude that

\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}
is an orthogonal projection,
which rewrites

(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{V}}=(\boldsymbol{x},\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{V}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{V}.

(3)

2
Weighted pseudo-inverse

Keeping the notations from the previous section,
consider continuous operator
\mathrm{B}:\mathrm{V}\to\mathrm{V}
, and
assume this operator is self-adjoint so that
it induces a scalar product
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{B}}:=(\mathrm{B}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{V}}

and a norm
\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathrm{B}}:=\sqrt{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})_{\mathrm{B}}}
.
To each such
\mathrm{B}
can be associated a so-called "weighted pseudo-inverse"

\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{V}
defined, for all
\forall\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{H}
by

\mathrm{R}\cdot\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{y}\quad\;\;\text{and}\quad\;\;\|\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\boldsymbol{y}\|_{\mathrm{B}}=\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{y}\})}\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{\mathrm{B}}.

(4)

The operator
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}
satisfies the same
properties as
\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}
except that
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{V}}
is this
times replaced by
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{B}}
. In particular (
3
) rewrites

(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{B}}=(\boldsymbol{x},\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{B}}

for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{V}
. Taking account of the expression of
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{B}}

this is equivalent to

\mathrm{B}\,\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{R}=(\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}\mathrm{R})^{*}\mathrm{B}

(5)

where, for any continuous linear operator
\mathrm{M}:\mathrm{V}\to\mathrm{V}
we denote
\mathrm{M}^{*}

its adjoint with respect to
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{V}}
defined by

(\mathrm{M}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{V}}=(\boldsymbol{x},\mathrm{M}^{*}\boldsymbol{y})_{\mathrm{V}}
for all
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathrm{V}
.
Property (
5
) leads to a lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}=(\,(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger})^{*}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\,)^{-1}

Proof:

Since
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{B}}=\mathrm{Id}
by construction, the lemma is
a consequence of (
5
) through direct calculation

(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*})\cdot((\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger})^{*}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger})=\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R})^{*}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}=\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{B})\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\mathrm{R}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}=(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger})^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
.

\Box

3
Re-interpretation of the fictious space lemma

In this section, we provide a new proof of the fictitious space lemma relying
on the concept weighted pseudoinverse. As a preliminary, let us recall a classical
caracterisation of extremal eigenvalues of self-adjoint operators
(see e.g. theorem 1.2.1 and theorem 1.2.3 in
[
3
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\mathrm{H}
is an Hilbert space equipped with the
scalar product
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{H}}
and let

\mathrm{T}:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{H}
be a bounded operator that is self-adjoint for

(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{H}}
. Denoting
\sigma(\mathrm{T})
the spectrum of

\mathrm{T}
, we have

\inf\sigma(\mathrm{T})=\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(\mathrm{T}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})_{\mathrm{H}}}{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})_{\mathrm{H}}}\quad\quad\quad\sup\sigma(\mathrm{T})=\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{H}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{(\mathrm{T}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})_{\mathrm{H}}}{(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})_{\mathrm{H}}}

This lemma holds independently of the choice of the scalar product,
provided that
\mathrm{T}
be self-adjoint with respect to it. As a consequence of
the previous lemma, if
\alpha(\;,\;)

and
\beta(\;,\;)
are two scalar products over
\mathrm{H}
and
\mathrm{T}

is self-adjoint with respect to both, then

\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{H}\setminus\{0\}}\alpha(\mathrm{T}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})/\alpha(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})=\inf_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathrm{H}\setminus\{0\}}\beta(\mathrm{T}\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})/\beta(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x})
, and a similar
result holds for the supremum.

Now we recall the fictitious space lemma, adopting the same formulation
of this result as
[
1
, Lemma 7.4]
and
[
2
, p.168]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathrm{H}
and
\mathrm{V}
be two Hilbert spaces equipped with the
scalar products
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{H}}
and
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{V}}
.
Let
\mathrm{A}:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{H}
(resp.
\mathrm{B}:\mathrm{V}\to\mathrm{V}
) be a
bounded operator that is positive definite self-adjoint
with respect to
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{H}}
(resp.
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{V}}
), and denote

(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{A}}:=(\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{H}}
(resp.
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{B}}:=(\mathrm{B}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{V}}
).
Suppose that there exists a surjective bounded linear operator
\mathrm{R}:\mathrm{V}\to\mathrm{H}
,
and constants
c_{\pm}>0
such that

i)

for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}
there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{V}

with
\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{u}
and
c_{-}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{B}}\leq(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}
,

ii)

(\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{v},\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq c_{+}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{B}}
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{V}
.

Then, denoting
\mathrm{R}^{*}:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{V}
the linear map defined by
(\mathrm{R}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{H}}=(\boldsymbol{u},\mathrm{R}^{*}\boldsymbol{v})

for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{V},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{H}
, we have

c_{-}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq c_{+}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}.

(6)

In addition, if
c_{\pm}
are the optimal constants satisfying i)-ii)
then the bounds in (
6
) are optimal as well.

Proof:

We simply reformulate
i)-ii)
by means of the weighted pseudo-inverse. If

i)
holds then, for any
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}
we have

c_{-}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\leq(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{u}\})
.
Taking the inifimum and using (
4
), we obtain
c_{-}\|\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathrm{B}}\leq(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}
. On the other hand, it is clear that, if

c_{-}\|\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathrm{B}}\leq(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}
then

i)
holds.

Next if
ii)
holds, then we have
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq c_{+}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathrm{B}}\;\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{u}\})
and for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}
. Taking the infinimum over

\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{R}^{-1}(\{\boldsymbol{u}\})
and using (
4
), we conclude that

(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq c_{+}\|\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\;\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}
,
and this is equivalent due to the optimality condition in (
4
).
To conclude we have just shown that conditions
i)-ii)
in Lemma
3.2

are actually equivalent to

c_{-}(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u},\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{B}}\leq(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}\leq c_{+}(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u},\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{B}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}.

(7)

Next define
\mathrm{S}:=(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger})^{*}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}
, which is obviously bounded positive
definite self-adjoint so it induces a scalar
product
(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{S}}:=(\mathrm{S}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})_{\mathrm{H}}
and a norm
\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathrm{S}}:=\sqrt{(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}}
.
We can re-write
(\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u},\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{B}}=(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}
, and

(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{A}}=(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}
according to Lemma
2.1
.
Hence (
7
) can be re-written

c_{-}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}\leq(\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}\mathrm{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}\leq c_{+}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u})_{\mathrm{S}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathrm{H}.

(8)

To conclude the proof there only remains to observe that,
since
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}=\mathrm{S}^{-1}
, then
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{B}^{-1}\mathrm{R}^{*}\mathrm{A}
is
self-adjoint with respect to both
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{S}}
and
(\;,\;)_{\mathrm{A}}
.
As a consequence, Lemma
3.1
combined with (
8
)
implies (
6
).
\Box

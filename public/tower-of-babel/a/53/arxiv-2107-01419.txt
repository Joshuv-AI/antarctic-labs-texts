A uniform Sobolev inequality for ancient Ricci flows with bounded Nash entropy
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
This note is a continuation of [ CMZ21 ] . We shall show that an ancient Ricci flow with uniformly bounded Nash entropy must also have uniformly bounded ν \nu -functional. Consequently, on such an ancient solution there are uniform logarithmic Sobolev and Sobolev inequalities. We emphasize that the main theorem in this paper is true so long as the theory in [ Bam20c ] is valid, and in particular, when the underlying manifold is closed.



1
Introduction

Qi S Zhang
[
Zhq07
]
proved a uniform Sobolev inequality for Ricci flows on closed manifolds. His method depends on Perelman’s monotonicity formula. Indeed, Perelman’s
\mu
-functional is known to be increasing in time, and by this reason, is bounded from below by the geometric data of the initial manifold. On the other hand, the
\mu
-functional is the logarithmic Sobolev constant. The Sobolev inequality then follows from these facts.

If the underlining Ricci flow is an ancient solution, then there is no such “initial manifold” to make use of when estimating the
\mu
-functional. Nevertheless, Perelman’s asymptotic soliton
[
Per02
, Proposition 11.2]
provides a substitute. This is exactly how
[
CMZ21
, Theorem1.4]
is proved. In that theorem, the authors show that, on an ancient solution admitting an asymptotic soliton, the
\mu
-functional at any time and at any scale is bounded from below by the entropy of the asymptotic shrinker. This also means that the
\nu
-functional is uniformly bounded.

However, Perelman’s asymptotic soliton is not known to exist in general cases. This fact is a great restriction to the application of the logarithmic Sobolev and Sobolev inequalities proved in
[
CMZ21
, Theorem 1.4, Corollary 1.5]
. In this article, we shall remove this restriction by showing that Bamler’s tangent flow at infinity
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 1.41]
, which is known to be a metric soliton, can also be used to estimate the
\mu
-functional for the ancient solution. For most of the definitions which appear in this article, the reader will find a brief introduction in
[
CMZ21
, Section 2]
; the notations in
[
CMZ21
]
are completely adopted in this article.

Let
(M^{n},g(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]}
be a complete ancient Ricci flow. Throughout this article, we shall make a technical assumption that
g(t)
has bounded curvature within each compact time interval, namely,

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\big|{\Rm}_{g_{t}}\big|<\infty\quad\text{ for all }\quad-\infty<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq 0.

(1.1)

Note that the curvature bound may depend on the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, and is not uniform on
M\times(-\infty,0]
. We further assume the existences of a point
p_{0}

\in M
and a constant
Y\in(0,\infty)
, such that for all
\tau>0
, the Nash entropy based at
(p_{0},0)
is bounded from below by
-Y
, i.e.,

\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)\geq-Y\quad\text{ for all }\quad\tau>0.

(1.2)

To apply the results in
[
Bam20c
]
, we make an additional assumption: if the sequence in
[
Bam20c
, (1.2)]
is taken to be

\left\{\big((M,g_{i}(t))_{t\in(-\infty,0]},(\nu^{i}_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,0]}\big)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty},

where
\tau_{i}\to\infty
,
g_{i}(t):=\tau_{i}^{-1}g(\tau_{i}t)
, and
\nu^{i}_{t}=\nu_{p_{0},0\,|\,\tau_{i}t}
, then the theorems in
[
Bam20c
]
(especially
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 1.6]
and all the statements contained in
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 1.15]
) are still valid. In particular, this is the case if
M^{n}
is closed. Furthermore, we strongly believe that all the results in
[
Bam20c
]
can be verified with only minor modifications under the assumptions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) alone. In other words, the assumption that the manifolds in
[
Bam20c
, (1.2)]
are closed can be replaced by (
1.1
).

The main theorem of this article is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Under the assumptions stated above, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{t\leq 0}\nu(g(t))=\mu_{\infty}>-\infty,

(1.3)

where

\mu_{\infty}:=\inf_{\tau>0}\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)=\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{N}_{p_{0},0}(\tau)\geq-Y.

In particular, this is true for closed ancient Ricci flows with bounded Nash entropy.

Once the
\nu
-functional is known to be bounded, the following logarithmic Sobolev inequalities and Sobolev inequalities are simply the consequences of some straightforward computations (c.f.
[
Zhq07
,
LW20
]
).

Corollary 1.2

(The logarithmic Sobolev and Sobolev inequalities)
.

Under the assumptions as stated above, we have

(1)

Logarithmic Sobolev inequality: for any compactly supported locally Lipschitz function
u
on
(M,g(t))
, where
t\leq 0
, and positive scale
\tau>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{2}\log u^{2}dg_{t}-\left(\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}\right)\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}+\left(\mu_{\infty}+n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)\right)\int_{M}u^{2}dg_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\tau\int_{M}(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2})dg_{t}

.

(2)

Sobolev inequality: for any compactly supported locally Lipschitz function
u
on
(M,g(t))
, where
t\leq 0
, we have

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}dg_{t}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C(n)e^{-\frac{2\mu_{\infty}}{n}}\int_{M}(4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2})dg_{t}.

Here
\mu_{\infty}
is the lower bound of the
\nu
-functional in (
1.3
). In particular, this is true for closed ancient Ricci flows with bounded Nash entropy.

As an application of the Sobolev inequality, we shall also prove the following volume growth lower bound for steady gradient Ricci solitons. Note that we do not make any curvature assumptions in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete steady gradient Ricci soliton normalized in the way that

\Ric=\nabla^{2}f,\quad R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

Suppose that the induced ancient Ricci flow satisfies the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
.
Fix a point
o\in M.

Then

|B_{r}(o)|\geq c(n)e^{\mu_{\infty}}r^{n/2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad r>10,

where
c(n)
is a dimensional constant and
\mu_{\infty}
is as defined in Theorem
1.1
. Here, we denote by
|\Omega|
the volume of a measurable subset
\Omega\subset M
and by
B_{r}(o)
the geodesic ball centered at
o
with radius
r.

This result generalizes the previous results on volume growth obtained in
[
MS13
]
without too much extra restrictions. And the volume lower bound here is in a similar form as that for shrinking Ricci solitons proved in
[
LW20
, Proposition 6]
.

In dimension three, it is a consequence of the Hamilton-Ivey pinching estimate that any complete steady gradient Ricci soliton has nonnegative sectional curvature (see
[
CBl09
]
and the references therein). When the curvature of the soliton is strictly positive, i.e., the manifold does not split locally, a result of Catino-Mastrolia-Monticelli
[
CMM16
]
gives the following lower bound of the volume growth:

|B_{r}(o)|\geq cr^{2}\quad\text{ for all $r$ large enough}.

(1.4)

It will be interesting to see if the quadratic growth in (
1.4
) is sharp or not. Under the additional condition that the scalar curvature attains its maximum, we show that the steady gradient Ricci soliton must have quadratic volume growth. We do not impose any non-collapsed condition. In particular, the result also applies to the flying-wing examples constructed by Lai
[
Lai20
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
(M^{3},g,f)
is a three dimensional complete steady gradient Ricci soliton with positive sectional curvature. Assume that the scalar curvature attains its maximum somewhere on
M
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all large
r
, it holds that

C^{-1}r^{2}\leq|B_{r}(o)|\leq Cr^{2}.

(1.5)

\displaystyle f(\cdot,\tau_{i}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{i}(\cdot,t)-\frac{n}{2}\log\left(\frac{\tau_{\tau_{i}t}}{\tau_{i}|t|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{i}(\cdot,t)-\frac{n}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\tau_{0}+t_{0}}{\tau_{i}|t|}\right),

we have, by the definition of
\mathcal{N}
and
\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{i}
,

\mathcal{N}(\tau_{i}t)=\overline{\mathcal{N}}^{i}(|t|)-\frac{n}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\tau_{0}+t_{0}}{\tau_{i}|t|}\right)\quad\text{ for all $i$ large enough}.

Taking
i\rightarrow\infty
, the lemma then follows immediately.
∎

With the preparations above, we are ready to show that the limit in Lemma
2.7
is a lower bound of
\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g_{t_{0}},u_{0},\tau_{0})
and thereby prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the proof, we shall retain the notations in (
2.11
). First of all, we compute

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}fu\,dg_{t}=-\int_{M}u\Box fdg_{t}+\int_{M}f\Box^{*}u\,dg_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{M}u\Box f\,dg_{t}=\int_{M}\left(-\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\Delta f\right)u\,dg_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2\Delta f-|\nabla f|^{2}+R-\frac{n}{2\tau_{t}}\right)u\,dg_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}+R\right)u\,dg_{t}-\frac{n}{2\tau_{t}}.

Hence

\displaystyle-\tau_{t}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{N}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\tau_{t}\left(|\nabla f|^{2}+R\right)+f-n\right)u\,dg_{t}-\mathcal{N}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{W}}(t)-\mathcal{N}(t),

and

-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tau_{t}\mathcal{N}(t)\right)=\overline{\mathcal{W}}(t).

By the monotonicity of
\overline{\mathcal{W}}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}\big(\tau_{-2\tau_{i}}\mathcal{N}(-2\tau_{i})-\tau_{-\tau_{i}}\mathcal{N}(-\tau_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}\int_{-2\tau_{i}}^{-\tau_{i}}\frac{d}{dt}\big(\tau_{t}\mathcal{N}(t)\big)dt=\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}\int_{-2\tau_{i}}^{-\tau_{i}}\overline{\mathcal{W}}(t)dt\leq\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g_{t_{0}},u_{0},\tau_{0}).

Letting
i\to\infty
, and using (
2.7
), we have

\mathcal{N}_{x^{\infty}}(\tau)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\tau_{-2\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\mathcal{N}(-2\tau_{i})-\frac{\tau_{-\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\mathcal{N}(-\tau_{i})\right)\leq\overline{\mathcal{W}}(g_{t_{0}},u_{0},\tau_{0}).

(2.12)

Since
t_{0}
,
u_{0}
, and
\tau_{0}
are arbitrarily fixed, this finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3
Applications to Steady Ricci Solitons

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete steady gradient Ricci soliton satisfying

\Ric=\nabla^{2}f,\quad R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

By
[
CBl09
]
,
R\geq 0
everywhere on
M
, and as a consequence

|\nabla f|\leq 1.

A complete steady gradient Ricci soliton generates a canonical solution
g(t)
to the Ricci flow. If
\phi_{t}
is the flow of the vector field
-\nabla f
with
\phi_{0}=\text{ id }
, then
\phi_{t}
exists for all time because of
|\nabla f|\leq 1
, and
(g(t):=\phi_{t}^{*}g)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
solves the Ricci flow equation with
g(0)=g
. We prove a volume lower bound for steady gradient solitons whose canonical form
g(t)
satisfy all the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
. The argument only requires a Sobolev inequality on
M
, so we shall show a slightly more general statement:

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that we have a Sobolev inequality on
(M^{n},g,f):

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dg\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{\rm Sob}\int_{M}4|\nabla u|^{2}+Ru^{2}\,dg,

for any compactly supported and locally Lipschitz function
u
, where
C_{\rm Sob}<\infty
is the Sobolev constant.
Fix a point
o\in M.

Then there is a constant
c
depending on
n
and
C_{\rm Sob}
such that

|B_{r}(o)|\geq cr^{n/2}\quad\text{ for all }\quad r>10.

Proof.

The proof follows verbatim as Theorem 3.1.5 in
[
SC
]
with minor changes. For simplicity, we write

V(r):=|B_{r}(o)|.

We choose a Lipschitz test function

u(x):=(r-d(x,o))_{+}.

Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,dg\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{r^{2}}{4}V(r/2)^{\frac{n-2}{n}},

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1,\ \text{a.e.},

\displaystyle\int_{M}Ru^{2}=\int_{B_{r}(o)}u^{2}\Delta f

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}(o)}u^{2}\partial_{r}f-\int_{B_{r}(o)}\nabla f\cdot\nabla u^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{r}(o)}u\leq 2rV(r),

where we integrated by parts and used the fact that
|\nabla f|\leq 1.

Hence,

\frac{r^{2}}{4}V(r/2)^{a}\leq C_{\rm Sob}(4+2r)V(r)\leq 4C_{\rm Sob}(1+r)V(r),

where
a=\frac{n-2}{n}.
So

\displaystyle V(r)\geq c_{1}\frac{r}{2}V(r/2)^{a},

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }r\geq 1;

\displaystyle V(r)\geq c_{1}\frac{r^{2}}{4}V(r/2)^{a},

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }r\in(0,1),

where
c_{1}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{16C_{\rm Sob}},1\right\}.

Fixing
r>10
and let
k=k(r)\geq 1
be the integer such that

1\leq 2^{-k}r<2.

Iterating the inequality above, we have

\displaystyle V(r)\geq(c_{1}r)^{\sum_{j=0}^{k}a^{j}}2^{-\sum_{j=0}^{k}(j+1)a^{j}}V\left(2^{-1-k}r\right)^{a^{1+k}}.

Write
s=2^{-1-k}r\in[1/2,1).

For any
m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle V(s)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}(s/2)^{2}V(s/2)^{a}

\displaystyle\geq\cdots

\displaystyle\geq(c_{1}s^{2})^{\sum_{i=0}^{m}a^{i}}2^{-\sum_{i=0}^{m}2(i+1)a^{i}}V(2^{-1-m}s)^{a^{1+m}}.

Since
a\in(0,1),
we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}V\left(2^{-m}s\right)^{a^{m}}=1.

Therefore, taking
m\to\infty,
we have

V(s)\geq c(n)c_{1}^{n/2}s^{n}\geq c(n)c_{1}^{n/2},

where we have used the fact
s\in[1/2,1).

So

V(r)\geq c(n)c_{1}^{n/2}r^{\frac{n}{2}(1-a^{1+k})}.

By the definition of
k
, we have

r^{-\frac{n}{2}a^{1+k(r)}}\geq c(n)\quad\text{ if }\quad r>10.

Hence, for
r>10,
we have

V(r)\geq c(n)c_{1}^{n/2}r^{n/2}.

∎

Remark.
The estimate on the integral involving
R
are similar to Theorem 5.1 in
[
MS13
]
(c.f.
Lemma 4.3 in
[
D16
]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we can see that if the Sobolev constant is
C_{\rm Sob}=C(n)e^{-\frac{2\mu_{\infty}}{n}}
, then the constant
c
in the previous proposition can be taken as

c=c(n)C_{\rm Sob}^{-n/2}=c(n)e^{\mu_{\infty}}.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

We end this section by establishing the quadratic volume estimate of positively curved three dimensional steady gradient Ricci soliton.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Catino-Mastrolia-Monticelli
[
CMM16
, Corollary 1.7]
showed that a 3 dimensional steady gradient soliton with
\liminf_{r\to\infty}r^{-2}|B_{r}(o)|=0
must either be flat or split isometrically as a quotient of
\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is the cigar soliton. The lower estimate in (
1.5
) then follows from the positively curved condition. To get the upper bound on the volume, we look at the area growth of the level sets. Since
\nabla^{2}f=\Ric>0
and
R
attains its maximum, by a result of Cao-Chen
[
CC12
, Proposition 2.3]
, there exist positive constants
\alpha

\in(0,1)
and
c_{0}
such that for any
x

\in M
, it holds that

\alpha\,{\rm dist}(x,o)-c_{0}\leq f(x)\leq{\rm dist}(x,o)+c_{0}.

(3.1)

By the convexity of
f
and the identity
\nabla R=-2\Ric(\nabla f)
,
f
and
R
have the same unique critical point, say
p_{0}
, then
R(p_{0})=1-|\nabla f|^{2}(p_{0})=1
and
\max_{M}R=1
. Moreover, by the Morse Lemma, the level sets
\Gamma_{s}:=\{x:\,f(x)=s\}
are all diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
for all
s>\min_{M}f
. Using
\Ric>0
and
\nabla R=-2\Ric(\nabla f)
, we can find
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a large positive constant
s_{0}
such that on
\{x:\,f(x)\geq s_{0}\}
, it holds that

R\leq\max_{M}R-\varepsilon_{0}=1-\varepsilon_{0}\,;

|\nabla f|^{2}=1-R\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

(3.2)

As
\Gamma_{s}
is a level set of
f
, its second fundamental form
A_{s}
(w.r.t. the normal
\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
) is given by
\frac{\nabla^{2}f}{|\nabla f|}=\frac{\Ric}{|\nabla f|}\geq 0
. For any
q

\in\Gamma_{s}
, we can find an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2}
which is an eigenbasis of
A_{s}
with eigenvalues
\sigma_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,2
. Let
\tilde{h}_{s}
be the induced metric on
\Gamma_{s}
. Then by the Gauss equation

\begin{split}K_{s}&=R_{1221}+\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}>0\,;\\
2K_{s}&=R-2\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})+2\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\\
&\geq R-2\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}),\end{split}

(3.3)

where
K_{s}
and
\mathbf{n}
denote the Gauss curvature of
(\Gamma_{s},\tilde{h}_{s})
and the normal vector
\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}
, respectively. We then consider the flow
\psi_{s}
of the vector field
\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}
with
\psi_{s_{0}}=\text{ id }
. When restricted on
\Gamma_{s_{0}}
,
\psi_{s}:\Gamma_{s_{0}}\longrightarrow\Gamma_{s}
are diffeomorphisms for all
s\geq s_{0}
. Let
h_{s}
be the pull back metric
\psi_{s}^{*}\tilde{h}_{s}
on
\Gamma_{s_{0}}
. Then we may compute

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}h_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{s}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}}g=\psi_{s}^{*}\left(\frac{2\nabla^{2}f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\right)=\psi_{s}^{*}\left(\frac{2\Ric}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\right),

where
\mathcal{L}_{\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}}
is the Lie derivative with respect to
\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}}
.
We denote by
dh_{s}
the volume form induced by the metric
h_{s}
, then, applying (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}dh_{s}&=\psi_{s}^{*}\left(\frac{R-\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\right)dh_{s}\\
&\leq\psi_{s}^{*}\left(\frac{2K_{s}+\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n})}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\right)dh_{s}\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\psi_{s}^{*}(2K_{s}+\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}))\,dh_{s}.\end{split}

(3.4)

By virtue of
\nabla R=-2\Ric(\nabla f)
, we have
\psi_{s}^{*}(\Ric(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}))=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}R\circ\psi_{s}
. It follows from (
3.4
) and
R\leq 1
that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(e^{\frac{R\circ\psi_{s}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\,dh_{s}\right)&\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\psi_{s}^{*}(2e^{\frac{R}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}K_{s})\,dh_{s}\\
&\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}e^{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\psi_{s}^{*}(2K_{s})\,dh_{s}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Hence by the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem, we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{\Gamma_{s_{0}}}e^{\frac{R\circ\psi_{s}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\,dh_{s}&=\int_{\Gamma_{s_{0}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(e^{\frac{R\circ\psi_{s}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\,dh_{s}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{8\pi e^{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}}{\varepsilon_{0}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

Integrating the above differential inequality with respect to
s
, we can choose a
s_{1}>s_{0}
such that for all
s\geq s_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\text{ Area }(\Gamma_{s})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{s_{0}}}e^{\frac{R\circ\psi_{s}}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\,dh_{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi e^{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}}{\varepsilon_{0}}(s-s_{0})+e^{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{0}}}\text{ Area }(\Gamma_{s_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cs.

We used
R\geq 0
in the first inequality. The Coarea formula then implies that for all
s\gg s_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle|\{x:\,f(x)\leq s\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{x:\,f(x)<s_{1}\}|+|\{x:\,s_{1}\leq f(x)\leq s\}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\{x:\,f(x)<s_{1}\}|+\frac{Cs^{2}}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}s^{2}.

By (
3.1
), for all large
r
, we have

|B_{r}(o)|\leq|\{x:\,f(x)\leq r+c_{0}\}|\leq 4C^{\prime}r^{2}.

∎

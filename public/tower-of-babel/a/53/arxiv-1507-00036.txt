Linkage of finite 𝖦 C \operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}} -dimension modules
Arash Sadeghi

Abstract
. Let R R be a semiperfect commutative Noetherian ring and C C a semidualizing R R –module. We study the theory of linkage for modules of finite 𝖦 C \operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}} -dimension. For a horizontally linked R R –module M M of finite 𝖦 C \operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}} -dimension, the connection of the Serre condition ( S n ) (S_{n}) with the vanishing of certain relative cohomology modules of its linked module is discussed.

13C15, 13D07, 13D02, 13H10

1.
Introduction

The theory of linkage of algebraic varieties was introduced by
C. Peskine and L. Szpiro
[
18
]
. Recall that two ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}

in a Gorenstein local ring
R
are said to be linked by a Gorenstein ideal
\mathfrak{c}
if
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}
such that

\mathfrak{a}=(\mathfrak{c}):\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{b}=(\mathfrak{c}):\mathfrak{a}
. The first main theorem in
the theory of linkage was due to C. Peskine and L. Szpiro. They
proved that over a Gorenstein local ring
R
with linked ideals

\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
,
R/\mathfrak{a}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
R/\mathfrak{b}

is. They also gave a counter-example to show that the above
statement is no longer true if the base ring is Cohen-Macaulay but
non-Gorenstein. Attempts to generalize this
theorem lead to several development in linkage theory, especially by
C. Huneke and B. Ulrich (
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
). In
[
20
, Theorem 4.1]
, Schenzel proved that,
over a Gorenstein local ring
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, the Serre
condition
(S_{r})
for
R/{\mathfrak{a}}
is equivalent to the vanishing of
the local cohomology groups
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R/{\mathfrak{b}})=0
for all
i
,

\dim(R/{\mathfrak{b}})-r<i<\dim(R/{\mathfrak{b}})
, provided
\mathfrak{a}
an
\mathfrak{b}
are
linked by a Gorenstein ideal
\mathfrak{c}
.

In
[
17
]
, Martsinkovsky and Strooker generalized the notion of
linkage for modules over non-commutative semiperfect Noetherian
rings. They introduced the operator
\lambda=\Omega\mathsf{Tr}
and showed
that ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are linked by zero ideal if and only if

R/\mathfrak{a}\cong\lambda(R/\mathfrak{b})
and
R/\mathfrak{b}\cong\lambda(R/\mathfrak{a})

[
17
, Proposition 1]
.

In
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, Dibaei and Sadeghi studied the theory of linkage for modules of finite
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}}
-dimension.
In this paper, we continue our study and obtain new results in this direction.

Now we describe the organization of the paper. In Section 2, we collect preliminary notions, definitions and some known results
which will be used in this paper.

In Section 3, for a horizontally linked
R
–module
M
, the connections of its invariants

\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}}
-dimension, depth and relative reduced grade with respect to a semidualizing module are studied. The associated prime ideals of

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{\tiny{\operatorname{\mathsf{r.grade}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}}(\lambda M)}}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)
is determined in terms of
M
, for any horizontally linked module
M
(see Lemma
3.4
). As a consequence, it is shown that under certain conditions the depth of a horizontally linked module
is equal to the relative reduced grade of its linked module. For a horizontally linked
R
–module
M
of finite
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}}
-dimension, the connection of the Serre condition
(S_{n})
with the vanishing of certain relative cohomology modules of its linked module is studied.
As a consequence, we obtain the following result for the linkage of ideals (see also Theorem
3.17
for more general case).

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring and all

R
–modules
M
,
N
,
\cdots
are finite (i.e. finitely
generated). Whenever,
R
is assumed local, its unique maximal ideal
is denoted by
\mathfrak{m}
.

For a finite presentation
P_{1}\overset{f}{\rightarrow}P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
of an
R
–module
M
, its transpose,
\mathsf{Tr}M
, is
defined as
\coker f^{*}
, where
(-)^{*}:=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(-,R)
, which
satisfies in the exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow M^{*}\rightarrow P_{0}^{*}\rightarrow P_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}M\rightarrow 0.

Moreover,
\mathsf{Tr}M
is unique up to projective equivalence. Thus all
minimal projective presentations of
M
represent isomorphic
transposes of
M
. The syzygy of a module
M
, denoted by
\Omega M
, is the kernel of
an epimorphism
P\overset{\alpha}{\rightarrow}M
, where
P
is a
projective
R
–module, so that it is unique up to projective
equivalence. Thus
\Omega M
is uniquely determined, up to
isomorphism, by a projective cover of
M
.

Martsinkovsky and Strooker
[
17
]
generalized
the notion of linkage for modules over non-commutative semiperfect
Noetherian rings (i.e. finitely generated modules over such rings have projective covers). They introduced the operator
\lambda:=\Omega\mathsf{Tr}
and showed that ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are linked by zero ideal if and only if
R/\mathfrak{a}\cong\lambda(R/\mathfrak{b})
and
R/\mathfrak{b}\cong\lambda(R/\mathfrak{a})
[
17
, Proposition1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
17
, Definition 3]

Let
R
be a semiperfect ring. Two
R
–modules
M
and
N
are said
to be

if
M\cong\lambda N
and

N\cong\lambda M
. Also,
M
is called horizontally linked (to

\lambda M
) if
M\cong\lambda^{2}M
.

Note that a commutative ring
R
is semiperfect if and only if it is
a finite direct product of commutative local rings
[
14
, Theorem
23.11]
. A
stable
module is a module with no non-zero
projective direct summands. Let
R
be a semiperfect ring,
M
a
stable
R
–module and
P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
a minimal projective presentation of
M
. Then
P_{0}^{*}\rightarrow P_{1}^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}M\rightarrow 0
is a minimal projective
presentation of
\mathsf{Tr}M

[
1
, Theorem 32.13]
. The following
induced exact sequences

(
2.1
.1)

0\longrightarrow M^{*}\longrightarrow P_{0}^{*}\longrightarrow\lambda M\longrightarrow 0,

(
2.1
.2)

0\longrightarrow\lambda M\longrightarrow P_{1}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}M\longrightarrow 0,

which will be quoted in this paper.

An
R
–module
M
is called a
syzygy module
if it is
embedded in a projective
R
–module. Let
i
be a positive integer,
an
R
–module

is said to be an

th syzygy if there exists an
exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow P_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}

with the
P_{0},\cdots,P_{i-1}
are
projective. By convention, every module is a
0
th syzygy.

Here is a characterization of a horizontally linked module.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
17
, Theorem 2 and Proposition 3]

Let
R
be a semiperfect ring. An
R
–module

is horizontally
linked if and only if it is stable and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M,R)=0
,
equivalently

Theorem 2.5
.

For a semidualizing
R
–module

and an
R
–module

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}\mathsf{-dim}}_{R}(M)=0
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C)
for all

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}\mathsf{-dim}}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M)=0

(iii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}\mathsf{-dim}}_{R}(M)=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)\neq 0,i\geq 0\}

Definition 2.6
.

Let

–module. The

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}^{R}(\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,M),C)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(C,M)

with exact rows. Therefore,
\mathsf{Tr}M\underset{R}{\otimes}C\cong\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M

(ii)

Set

&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\lambda_{C}M,C)\\
&\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i+1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C),\\
\end{array}

for all

(iii)

For an

0\rightarrow L\rightarrow\overset{m}{\oplus}C\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{n+1}^{C}M\rightarrow 0

In

For a finite

\coker f^{\triangledown}

Lemma 2.12

For an
R

Proof.

Let

\pi:(P_{0})^{\triangledown}\rightarrow(P_{1})^{\triangledown}\rightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M\rightarrow 0.

By applying the functor

\begin{CD}&&&&&&&&\\
\ \ &&&&P_{1}@>{}>{}>P_{0}@>{}>{}>M@>{}>{}>0&\\
&&&&@V{}V{\cong}V@V{}V{\cong}V\\
\ \ &&&&P^{\triangledown\triangledown}_{1}@>{}>{}>P^{\triangledown\triangledown}_{0}@>{}>{}>\mathsf{Tr}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}}}^{\pi}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M)@>{}>{}>0&\\

with exact rows. Therefore,

Remark 2.15
.

(i)

By
[

Hence we obtain the following exact sequence

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a semiperfect ring,

a horizontally linked
R
–module and
\underline{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}}_{R}(M,C)=0

The following statements hold true:

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}\mathsf{-dim}}_{R}(M)=0

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)
for all

(ii)

If
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C_{p}}\mathsf{-dim_{R_{p}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in X^{n-1}(R)
,
then
M
satisfies
\widetilde{S}_{n}
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)=0
for all
i
,

0<i<n
.

Proof.

As
M
is horizontally linked,
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C)=0
by Remark
2.15
(i). We have the following isomorphism:

(
3.1
.1)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i+1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C),

for all
i>0
by Remark
2.11
(ii).

(i) By Theorem
2.5
(i),
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}\mathsf{-dim}}_{R}(M)=0
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C)
for all
i>0
and this is equivalent to say that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,C)=0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)
for all
i>0
by (
3.1
.1).

(ii) By Theorem
2.14
,
M
satisfies
\widetilde{S}_{n}

if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,C)=0
for all
i
,
1\leq i\leq n
and this is equivalent to say that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{\mathcal{I}_{C}}(\lambda M,R)=0
for all
i
,
0<i<n
by (
3.1
.1).
∎

.5)

\mathfrak{p}

\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=n

5.

In this section we study the connection of the Serre condition
(S_{n})

-dimension with the vanishing of the local cohomology groups of its linked module. First we bring the following Lemma which is a generalization of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be
R
–modules. If
N\in\operatorname{\mathcal{B}_{\it C}}
, then there is a natural exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,N)\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(M^{\triangledown},N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{2}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,N)\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

Let
P_{1}\overset{g}{\rightarrow}P_{0}\overset{f}{\rightarrow}M\rightarrow 0
be a projective presentation of
M
. By applying the functor
(-)^{\triangledown}
on the projective presentation of

(
5.1
.1)

0\longrightarrow M^{\triangledown}\overset{f^{\triangledown}}{\longrightarrow}(P_{0})^{\triangledown}\overset{{\alpha}_{1}}{\longrightarrow}L\longrightarrow 0,

(
5.1
.2)

0\longrightarrow L\overset{{\alpha}_{2}}{\longrightarrow}(P_{1})^{\triangledown}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M\longrightarrow 0,

\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(C,N)=0
for all

By applying the functor

on the exact sequences (

.1) and (

.2), we obtain the following exact sequences:

(
5.1
.3)

0\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(L,N)\overset{{\alpha_{1}}^{\dagger}}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}((P_{0})^{\triangledown},N)\overset{{f^{\triangledown}}^{\dagger}}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(M^{\triangledown},N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(L,N)\rightarrow 0

(
5.1
.4)

0\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}((P_{1})^{\triangledown},N)\overset{{\alpha_{2}}^{\dagger}}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(L,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{Tr}_{C}M,N)\rightarrow 0

Note that the homomorphism evaluation
\theta_{i}:P_{i}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}((P_{i})^{\triangledown},N)

\begin{CD}P_{1}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,N)@>{\varphi}>{}>P_{0}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,N)@>{\psi}>{}>M\otimes_{R}\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(C,N)\rightarrow 0\\
@V{}V{\beta}V@V{}V{\theta_{0}}V@V{}V{\theta}V\\

\end{CD}

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Assume that
M
is a horizontally linked
R
–module of finite
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C}}
-dimension and that
\lambda M\in\mathcal{A}_{C}
(e.g.
\mathcal{I}_{C}
-
\id_{R}(\lambda M)<\infty
). The following are equivalent:

(i)

M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay;

(ii)

\lambda M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay;

(iii)

M
satisfies
(S_{n})
for some
n>\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R-\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(\lambda M)
;

(iv)

\lambda M
satisfies
(S_{n})
for some
n>\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R-\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(M)
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iv) follows from
[
5
, Corollary 4.7]
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii). By Example
2.7
(i),
\mathsf{Tr}M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{A}_{\it C}}
, and so
\underline{\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}}_{R}(M,C)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}^{R}(\mathsf{Tr}M,C)=0
. By Corollary
5.3
,
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\lambda M\otimes_{R}C)=0
for all
i
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R-n<i<\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R
. Set
t=\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(\lambda M)
. Note that
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(\lambda M\otimes_{R}C)=t
by Lemma
2.16
.
Hence
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\lambda M\otimes_{R}C)=0
for all
i
,
i<t
. As
t>\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}R-n
, we conclude that
\lambda M\otimes_{R}C
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. Therefore
\lambda M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay by using Lemma
2.16
again.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii). If
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay then
M
satisfies
(S_{n})
for all
n
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
and
M
a horizontally linked
R
–module.
Assume that
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C_{p}}\mathsf{-dim_{R_{p}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
and that
\lambda M\in\operatorname{\mathcal{A}_{\it C}}
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i)

M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\lambda M
is;

(ii)

If
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay, then

\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\lambda M,R)\text{ for all }i,0<i<d,

\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\lambda M)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)\text{ for all }i,0<i<d;

In particular, If
M
is not maximal Cohen-Macaulay, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{depth}}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{r.grade}}(\lambda M)
.

Proof.

By
[
22
, Lemma 1.2 , Lemma 1.4]
,
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay module if and only if
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay module
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
–module for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. Hence the first assertion is clear by Corollary
5.6
.

(ii) If
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{G}_{\it C_{p}}\mathsf{-dim_{R_{p}}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
by Theorem
2.5
(iv).
Hence
\hh^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(\lambda M,R)
for all
i
,
0<i<d
by Corollary
3.10
(ii). By Theorem
4.5
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{G-dim}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec(R)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
and so
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)
is finite length for all
i>0
. Now the second isomorphism follows from
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
.
∎

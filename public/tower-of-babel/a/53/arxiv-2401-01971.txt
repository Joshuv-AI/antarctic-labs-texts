Bordered and Framed Toeplitz and Hankel Determinants with Applications in Integrable Probability


Abstract
. Bordered and framed Toeplitz/Hankel determinants have the same structure as Toeplitz/Hankel determinants except in a small number of matrix rows and/or columns. We review these structured determinants and their connections to orthogonal polynomials, collecting well-known and perhaps less well-known results. We present some applications for these structured determinants to ensembles of non-intersecting paths and the six-vertex model, with an eye towards asymptotic analysis. We also prove some asymptotic formulas for the probability of non-intersection for an ensemble of continuous time random walks for certain choices of starting and ending points as the number of random walkers tends to infinity.

1.1.
Definitions and motivation

For
\phi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
denote the
n\times n
(pure) Toeplitz determinant by

(1.1)

D_{n}[\phi]=\underset{0\leq j,k\leq n-1}{\det}\{\phi_{j-k}\},

where

(1.2)

\phi_{j}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\phi(\zeta)\zeta^{-j}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta},\qquad j\in{\mathbb{Z}},

is the
j
-th Fourier coefficient of
\phi
, and
{\mathbb{T}}
denotes the unit circle oriented in the counterclockwise direction. More generally we can consider Toeplitz matrices with respect to a measure on
{\mathbb{T}}
. Indeed for a finite measure
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, define
\varphi_{j}
as

(1.3)

\varphi_{j}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\zeta^{-j}\textrm{d}\varphi(\zeta),\qquad j\in{\mathbb{Z}},

and the corresponding
n\times n
Toeplitz determinant as

(1.4)

D_{n}[\varphi]=\underset{0\leq j,k\leq n-1}{\det}\{\varphi_{j-k}\}.

For a finite measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that all moments exist, i.e.,

\mu_{k}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{k}\textrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty,\quad k=0,1,2,3,\dots,

the Hankel matrix of size

corresponding to the measure

{\bf H}_{n}[\mu]=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-1}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\cdots&\mu_{2n-2}\end{pmatrix}.

We will denote the determinant of this matrix as

{H}_{n}[\mu]:=\det{\bf H}_{n}[\mu].

Often in applications involving Toeplitz and Hankel determinants, the problem may be perturbed in such a way that the Toeplitz/Hankel matrix is altered in only a few rows or columns
[
4
,
10
,
52
]
. As a motivating example, consider the enumeration of non-intersecting random walks. That is, we consider
n
independent simple random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t),\dots,X_{n}(t)
. At each discrete time step, the simple random walk either increases or decreases by 1, each with equal probability. We additionally restrict the random walks in the following way.

•

At time
t=0
the walkers begin at the points
X_{j}(0)=2x_{j}
with
x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{n}
and
x_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
j
.

•

At the terminal time
2T\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
the walkers end at the points
X_{j}(2T)=2y_{j}
with
y_{1}<y_{2}<\dots<y_{n}
and
y_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
j
.

•

The paths do not intersect, i.e.,
X_{j}(t)\neq X_{k}(t)
for
j\neq k
and
t=0,1,\dots,2T
.

The Lindström–Gessel–Viennot (LGV) formula
[
70
,
51
]
states that the number of such collections of paths is given as a determinant

\det\left[\#(2x_{j},2y_{k};2T)\right]_{j,k=1}^{n},

where
\#(2x_{j},2y_{k};T)
is the number of paths beginning at
2x_{j}
and ending at
2y_{k}
after
2T
time-steps. Since this is a binomial coefficient, the number of collections of non-intersecting paths beginning at
2x_{1}<\cdots<2x_{n}
and ending at
2y_{1}<\cdots<2y_{n}
is

(1.5)

\det\left[\begin{pmatrix}2T\\
T+y_{k}-x_{j}\end{pmatrix}\right]_{j,k=1}^{n}.

The matrix can be written as

(1.6)

\begin{pmatrix}2T\\
T+y_{k}-x_{j}\end{pmatrix}_{j,k=1}^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\phi(\zeta)\zeta^{-(y_{k}-x_{j})}\frac{\textrm{d}\zeta}{2\pi\textrm{i}\zeta}\end{pmatrix}_{j,k=1}^{n},\qquad\phi(\zeta)=\zeta^{-T}\left(\zeta+1\right)^{2T}=\left(\zeta+1/\zeta+2\right)^{T},

i.e.,
\phi(\zeta)
is the generating function for the number of paths of a random walker with fixed displacement after
2T
time steps. If the
x_{j}=y_{j}
are equally spaced then (
1.5
) is a Toeplitz determinant. If most of the starting/ending points are equally spaced but a few are not, then the Toeplitz matrix may be altered in a few rows/columns. For example if
x_{j}=j
for
j=1,\dots,n
,
y_{k}=k
for
k=1,2,\dots,n-m
, and
y_{k}>n-m
are arbitrary for
k=n-m+1,\dots,n
, we get
the a determinant of the form

(1.7)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}&\phi_{1}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-1}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-1}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-1}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-1}\\
\phi_{-1}&\phi_{0}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-2}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-2}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-2}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\phi_{-n+1}&\phi_{-n+2}&\cdots&\phi_{-m}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-n}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-n}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-n}\end{pmatrix},

which has a Toeplitz structure in the first
n-m
columns, and the remaining
m
columns consist of descending Fourier coefficients. More generally we can consider the top
m
starting points to be arbitrary as well. That is, if we take
x_{j}=y_{j}=j
for
j=1,2,\dots,n-m
and
x_{j}>n-m
and
y_{j}>n-m
to be arbitrary for
j=n-m+1,\dots,n
, then (
1.5
) becomes

(1.8)

\det\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}&\phi_{1}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-1}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-1}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-1}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-1}\\
\phi_{-1}&\phi_{0}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-2}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-2}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-2}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\phi_{1-(n-m)}&\phi_{2-(n-m)}&\cdots&\phi_{0}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-(n-m)}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-(n-m)}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-(n-m)}\\
\phi_{1-x_{n-m+1}}&\phi_{2-x_{n-m+1}}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-x_{n-m+1}}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-x_{n-m+1}}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-x_{n-m+1}}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-x_{n-m+1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\phi_{1-x_{n}}&\phi_{2-x_{n}}&\cdots&\phi_{n-m-x_{n}}&\phi_{y_{n-m+1}-x_{n}}&\phi_{y_{n-m+2}-x_{n}}&\cdots&\phi_{y_{n}-x_{n}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

which has a Toeplitz structure in the top-left
(n-m)\times(n-m)
block, descending Fourier coefficients in the first
n-m
entries of each of the last
m
columns, and ascending Fourier coefficients in each of the first
n-m
entries of the last
m
rows. The bottom-right block of size
m\times m
has no particular structure.

1.2.
Toeplitz/Hankel determinants and orthogonal polynomials

Let us start with the OPRL characterization for Hankel determinants. Let
\mathscr{I}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mu
be a measure with finite moments on
\mathscr{I}
. Consider the associated set of monic orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}(z)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
,
\deg P_{n}(z)=n
, satisfying the orthogonality conditions

(1.17)

\int_{\mathscr{I}}P_{n}(x)x^{k}\textrm{d}\mu(x)=h_{n}\delta_{nk},\qquad k=0,1,\cdots,n,

which is equivalent to

(1.18)

\int_{\mathscr{I}}P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)\textrm{d}\mu(x)=h_{n}\delta_{nk},\qquad k,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}.

If
\det\bm{H}_{n}[\mu]\neq 0
, the polynomial
P_{n}(z)
has the following determinantal representation

(1.19)

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\det\bm{H}_{n}[\mu]}\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n}&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\vdots&\vdots\\

-\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\textrm{i}}{h_{n-1}}P_{n-1}(z)&-\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\int_{\mathscr{I}}\frac{P_{n-1}(x)w(x)}{x-z}\textrm{d}x\end{pmatrix},\qquad n\geq 1,

•

is analytic.

•

RH-Y2

as

RH-Y3

1.3.
Bordered Toeplitz/Hankel determinants and orthogonal polynomials

We start with the extension of (
1.19
) to several variables. Let
{H}_{n}^{B}[\mu;z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]
be the determinant

(1.31)

H_{n}^{B}[\mu;z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]:=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m-1}&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m}&z_{1}&z_{2}&\cdots&z_{m}\\
\mu_{2}&\mu_{3}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m+1}&z_{1}^{2}&z_{2}^{2}&\cdots&z_{m}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\cdots&\mu_{2n-m-2}&z_{1}^{n-1}&z_{2}^{n-1}&\cdots&z_{m}^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.

This determinant may also be expressed in terms of orthogonal polynomials.

Proposition 1.9
.

The determinant
{H}_{n}[\mu;z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]
is given as

(1.32)

{H}_{n}^{B}[\mu;z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]=H_{n-m}^{B}[\mu]\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n-m}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-m}(z_{m})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z_{m})\end{pmatrix}.

The above proposition is well known in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Equation (5.5)]
) though we were unable to find a proof written explicitly anywhere. The formula (
1.32
) is rather similar to a formula of Christoffel (
[
73
, Theorem 2.5]
, and Proposition
1.9
can be proved based on that formula. In this paper we give a different proof based on the Dodgson Condensation identity. This proof is presented in Section
5
. Using an identical argument we can prove the analogous result for the system of bi-orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
T_{n}[\varphi;z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}]
be the following
n\times n
multi-bordered Toeplitz determinant

(1.33)

T_{n}[\varphi;z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}]:=\det\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{0}&\varphi_{1}&\cdots&\varphi_{n-m-1}&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\varphi_{-1}&\varphi_{0}&\cdots&\varphi_{n-m-2}&z_{1}&z_{2}&\cdots&z_{m}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{-n+1}&\varphi_{-n+2}&\cdots&\varphi_{-m}&z_{1}^{n-1}&z_{2}^{n-1}&\cdots&z_{m}^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.

It holds that

(1.34)

{T}_{n}[\varphi;z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]=\sqrt{D_{n-m}[\varphi]D_{n-m+1}[\varphi]}\det\begin{pmatrix}Q_{n-m}(z_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-m}(z_{m})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{n-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-1}(z_{m})\end{pmatrix},

and

(1.35)

{T}_{n}[\tilde{\varphi};z_{1},\dots,z_{m}]=\sqrt{D_{n-m}[\varphi]D_{n-m+1}[\varphi]}\det\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{Q}_{n-m}(z_{1})&\cdots&\widehat{Q}_{n-m}(z_{m})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\widehat{Q}_{n-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&\widehat{Q}_{n-1}(z_{m})\end{pmatrix},

where
Q_{k}(z)
and
\widehat{Q}_{k}(z)
are the biorthogonal polynomials on the unit circle given by (
1.25
) and (
1.26
), and the measures
\varphi
and
\tilde{\varphi}
are related by
\textrm{d}\tilde{\varphi}(z):=\textrm{d}\varphi(z^{-1})
.

Using Propositions
1.9
and
1.10
, it is simple to give a similar formula for any bordered Hankel or Toeplitz determinant as a (multiple) integral.

Corollary 1.11
.

The bordered determinants
{H}_{n}^{B}[\mu;\vec{\bm{\nu}}_{m}]
and
D^{B}_{n}[\phi;\vec{\bm{\psi}}_{m}]
are given by

(1.36)

{H}_{n}^{B}[\mu;\bm{\nu}_{m}]=H_{n-m}[\mu]\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{n-m}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-m}(z_{m})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
P_{n-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&P_{n-1}(z_{m})\end{pmatrix}\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z_{1})\cdots\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z_{m}),

(1.37)

D^{B}_{n}[\varphi;\vec{\bm{\psi}}_{m}]=\sqrt{D_{n-m}[\varphi]D_{n-m+1}[\varphi]}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{T}}\det\begin{pmatrix}Q_{n-m}(z_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-m}(z_{m})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
Q_{n-1}(z_{1})&\cdots&Q_{n-1}(z_{m})\end{pmatrix}\prod_{j=1}^{m}z^{-n+1}_{j}\textrm{d}\psi^{(j)}(z_{j}).

Proof.

By multilinearity of the determinant,

(1.38)

{H}_{n}^{B}[\mu;\bm{\nu}_{m}]=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m-1}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\textrm{d}\nu^{(2)}(z)&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z)\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\textrm{d}\nu^{(2)}(z)&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z)\\
\mu_{2}&\mu_{3}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m+1}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{2}\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{2}\textrm{d}\nu^{(2)}(z)&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{2}\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\cdots&\mu_{2n-m-2}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{n-1}\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{n-1}\textrm{d}\nu^{(2)}(z)&\cdots&\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{n-1}\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z)\end{pmatrix}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m-1}&1&1&\cdots&1\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m}&z_{1}&z_{2}&\cdots&z_{m}\\
\mu_{2}&\mu_{3}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m+1}&z_{1}^{2}&z_{2}^{2}&\cdots&z_{m}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\cdots&\mu_{2n-m-2}&z_{1}^{n-1}&z_{2}^{n-1}&\cdots&z_{m}^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}\textrm{d}\nu^{(1)}(z_{1})\textrm{d}\nu^{(2)}(z_{2})\cdots\textrm{d}\nu^{(m)}(z_{m}),

which is (
1.36
) using (
1.32
). Equation (
1.37
) follows in a similar way.
∎

1.4.
Framed Toeplitz/Hankel determinants and orthogonal polynomials

It was shown in
[

(based on an idea introduced in
[

In this section we extend those results to multi-framed Toeplitz determinants and present their analogues for (multi-)framed Hankel determinants as well. The formulas established in this section for an
m
-framed Toeplitz/Hankel determinant involve an
m\times m
determinant using the reproducing kernel of the OPRL or BOPUC. For
m=1
such formulas were written in Theorem 1.9 of
[
52

, and a similar approach establishes analogous formulas for the single-framed Hankel determinants. However for
m\geq 2
, unlike what we show in this work, no determinantal formulas were shown in
[
52
]
. In fact, for
m\geq 2
, there exists an alternative (and perhaps less efficient) approach to write an
m
-framed Toeplitz/Hankel determinant in terms of the reproducing kernels of orthogonal polynomials. This alternative representation is achieved by expressing the
m
-framed determinant in terms of several single-framed determinants, utilizing successive applications of proper Dodgson Condensation identities (see Section
5.1
). Consequently, as a result of Theorem 1.9 in
[
52
]
, the
m
-framed determinant can be expressed in terms of the reproducing kernels of orthogonal polynomials. This idea is displayed in section 3.3 of
[
52
]
and will not be pursued in this section.

The reproducing kernel for the OPRL (
1.17
) is defined as

(1.39)

K_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{P_{k}(x)P_{k}(y)}{h_{k}}.

For an
m\times m
matrix
A=(a_{jk})_{j,k=1}^{m}
, and variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
, and
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}
, define the determinant

H_{n}^{F}(\mu;x_{1},\dots,x_{m};y_{1},\dots,y_{m};A)
as

H_{n}^{F}[\mu;x_{1},\dots,x_{m};y_{1},\dots,y_{m};A]:=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m-1}&1&\cdots&1\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m}&x_{1}&\cdots&x_{m}\\
\mu_{2}&\mu_{3}&\cdots&\mu_{n-m+1}&x_{1}^{2}&\cdots&x_{m}^{2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-m-1}&\mu_{n-m}&\cdots&\mu_{2(n-m-1)}&x_{1}^{n-m-1}&\cdots&x_{m}^{n-m-1}\\
1&y_{1}&\cdots&y_{1}^{n-m-1}&a_{11}&\dots&a_{1m}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&y_{m}&\cdots&y_{m}^{n-m-1}&a_{m1}&\dots&a_{mm}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
A=(a_{jk})_{j,k=1}^{m}
. We have

(1.40)

H_{n}^{F}[\mu;x_{1},\dots,x_{m};y_{1},\dots,y_{m};A]=H_{n-m}[\mu]\det\left[A-\left(K_{n-m-1}(x_{k},y_{j})\right)_{j,k=1}^{m}\right],

where
K_{n}(x,y)

is the reproducing kernel of the system of orthogonal polynomials on the real line given by (
1.39
).

The above result, which will be proved in Section
5.2
, implies a general formula for framed Hankel determinants.

Corollary 1.14
.

Let
A=(a_{jk})_{j,k=1}^{m}

(1.41)

H_{n}^{F}(\mu;\nu^{(1)},\dots,\nu^{(m)};\eta^{(1)},\dots,\eta^{(m)};A)=H_{n-m}[\mu]\det\left[A-\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{n-m-1}(x_{k},y_{j})\textrm{d}\nu^{(k)}(x_{k})\textrm{d}\eta^{(j)}(y_{j})\right)_{j,k=1}^{m}\right],

where
K_{n}(x,y)

is the reproducing kernel of the system of orthogonal polynomials on the real line given by (
1.39
).

Proof.

The proof is nearly identical to that of (
1.36
).

3.
Width of non-intersecting processes

The paper
[
8
]
considers the width of various ensembles of non-intersecting paths: non-intersecting Brownian bridges, discrete random walks, and continuous time random walks. For the simplest initial conditions on the random paths, the distribution of the width of the ensemble is described as the ratio of two Hankel determinants for the non-intersecting Browinan bridges, and the ratio of two Toeplitz determinants for the non-intersecting random walks. In this section we describe how the initial conditions may be perturbed slightly so that the Toeplitz/Hankel determinants from
[
8
]
are replaced with bordered or framed Toeplitz/Hankel determinants.

3.1.
Non-intersecting Brownian bridges with wanderers

Consider

The six-vertex model with DWBC

The six-vertex model is stated on a square lattice of size
n\times n
, with arrows drawn on edges satisfying
ice rule
: at each vertex, exactly two adjacent arrows point inward and two point outward. There are six possible configurations of arrows at each vertex, and we label them as shown in Figure
5
.

Figure 5
.
The six arrow configurations allowed at a vertex.

We can give a weight
w_{i}>0
to each vertex type
i=1,2,\dots,6
, and define the weight of an arrow configuration
\sigma
as

w_{n}(\sigma)=\prod_{i=1}^{6}w_{i}^{N_{i}(\sigma)},

where
N_{i}(\sigma)
is the number of vertices of type
i
in the configuration
\sigma
. The Gibbs measure is then given by the normalized weight of a configuration

(4.1)

\mu_{n}(\sigma)=\frac{w_{n}(\sigma)}{Z_{n}},\quad Z_{n}=\sum_{\sigma}w(\sigma).

The quantity
Z_{n}
is the partition function for the model, and the sum defining it is over all allowable configurations
\sigma
on the
n\times n
lattice, which may be restricted by some boundary conditions as well as by the ice-rule. For certain boundary conditions known as
Domain Wall Boundary Conditions
(DWBC), the partition function may be expressed as a Hankel determinant. In DWBC all arrows on the left and right boundary point outward, and all arrows on the top and bottom boundaries point inward. See Figure
6
for an example of an arrow configuration with DWBC on the
5\times 5
lattice. The determinantal formula for the partition function of the DWBC six-vertex model is known as the
Izergin–Korepin formula
.

For fixed boundary conditions like DWBC, the Gibbs measure of the six-vertex model is invariant under reversal of arrows (see e.g.,
[
21
, Chapter 5, Section 2]
), so we may give the weight
a>0
to the vertices of type 1 and 2; the weight
b>0
to the vertices of type 3 and 4; and the weight
c>0
to the vertices of type 5 and 6. Then the phase diagram for the model is as follows.

•

Ferroelectric phase
. If
a>b+c
(resp.
b>a+c
) then vertices of Type 1 and 2 (resp. Type 3 and 4) are dominant. In the ferroelectric phase it is typical to parametrize the weights as

(4.2)

a\equiv a(t,\gamma)=\sinh(t-\gamma),\quad b\equiv b(t,\gamma)=\sinh(\gamma+t),\quad c\equiv c(\gamma)=\sinh(2\gamma),\quad 0<|\gamma|<t.

•

Disordered phase
. If the quantities
a,b
, and
c
form a triangle, then there is no dominant weight.
In the disordered phase it is typical to parametrize the weights as

(4.3)

a\equiv a(t,\gamma)=\sin(\gamma-t),\quad b\equiv b(t,\gamma)=\sin(\gamma+t),\quad c\equiv c(\gamma)=\sin(2\gamma),\quad|t|<\gamma<\pi/2.

•

Antiferroelectric phase
. If
c>a+b
, then vertices of Type 5 and 6 are dominant. In the antiferroelectric phase it is typical to parametrize the weights as

(4.4)

a\equiv a(t,\gamma)=\sinh(\gamma-t),\quad b\equiv b(t,\gamma)=\sinh(\gamma+t),\quad c\equiv c(\gamma)=\sinh(2\gamma),\quad|t|<\gamma.

Figure 6
.
A six-vertex configuration on the
5\times 5
lattice with DWBC. Type 5 vertices are marked in red, and type-6 vertices are marked in blue. The domain wall boundary conditions and the ice rule ensure that there is exactly one type-5 vertex along each boundary, and no more than
k
type 5 vertices in the
k

k

In order to describe the Izergin–Korepin formula in its most general form, it is convenient to introduce vertex weights which depend on the location of the vertex in the
n\times n
lattice as well as the vertex type. To do so, fix
n=1,2,\dots
, and
2n+1
complex parameters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n};\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n};\gamma
. For a configuration
\sigma
of the six-vertex model in an
n\times n
square with DWBC and one of the parametrizations (
4.2
) – (
4.4
), we define the weight of the vertex at
(x,y)
to be

\omega(x,y;\sigma)=\begin{cases}a(\psi_{y}-\chi_{x},\gamma),&\quad\text{if }\sigma(x,y)\text{ is of Type 1 or 2,}\\
b(\psi_{y}-\chi_{x},\gamma),&\quad\text{if }\sigma(x,y)\text{ is of Type 3 or 4,}\\
c(\gamma),&\quad\text{if }\sigma(x,y)\text{ is of Type 5 or 6.}\end{cases}

The inhomogeneous partition function is then defined as

(4.5)

Z_{n}(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n};\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n};\gamma)=\sum_{\sigma}\prod_{x=1}^{n}\prod_{y=1}^{n}\omega(x,y;\sigma),

where the sum is over all configurations satisfying DWBC.

The Izergin–Korepin formula states that for any of the parametrizations (
4.2
) – (
4.4
),

(4.6)

Z_{n}(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n};\,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n};\,\gamma)\\
=\frac{\prod\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\bigl(a(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)b(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)\bigr)}{\prod\limits_{i<j}\bigl(b(\chi_{i}-\chi_{j},0)b(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j},0)\bigr)}\det\left[\frac{c(\gamma)}{a(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)b(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}.

The partition function for the homogeneous model is then obtained by taking all parameters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}
to be the same, and
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}
to be the same. For convenience we can take
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}=0
and
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}=t
. The determinant vanishes when two of the
\psi

\chi

\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}\to 0
and
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}\to t

\varphi(t)=\frac{c(\gamma)}{a(t,\gamma)b(t,\gamma)},

so that the Izergin–Korepin determinant is

Z_{n}(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n};\,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n};\,\gamma)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\bigl(a(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)b(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i},\gamma)\bigr)}{\prod\limits_{i<j}\bigl(b(\chi_{i}-\chi_{j},0)b(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j},0)\bigr)}\det\left[\varphi(\psi_{j}-\chi_{i})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}.

We can take
\chi_{i}\to 0
in sequential order row-by-row. By expanding

\psi_{j}

Z_{n}(0^{n};\,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n};\,\gamma)=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\bigl(a(\psi_{j},\gamma)b(\psi_{j},\gamma)\bigr)^{n}}{\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}j!\right)\prod\limits_{i<j}\bigl(b(\psi_{i}-\psi_{j},0)}\det\left[\varphi^{(i-1)}(\psi_{j})\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}.

\psi_{j}\to t

7.
An illustration of asymptotics: continuous time non-intersecting paths

Each one of the applications discussed in sections
2
,
3

4
require its own careful treatment in a separate work. However, in this section we showcase the asymptotic analysis associated with one of the aforementioned applications, that is finding the transition probability of the non-intersecting paths modeled by (

[
10
]

[
52
]

The asymptotic behavior of Toeplitz determinants with respect to smooth functions can be described by the Szegő–Widom theorem
[
78
,
74
,
28
]
, which is formulated as

(7.1)

D_{n}[\phi]\sim G[\phi]^{n}E[\phi],\qquad n\to\infty,

where the terms
G[\phi]
and
E[\phi]
are defined by:

(7.2)

G[\phi]=\exp\left([\log\phi]_{0}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad E[\phi]=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}k[\log\phi]_{k}[\log\phi]_{-k}\right).

This theorem holds true when the function
\phi
is suitably smooth, does not vanish on the unit circle, and possesses a winding number of zero. We refer to
[
46
]
for a comprehensive survey of the Szegő theorem, including an intriguing account of its historical developments.

Let us consider the Toeplitz determinant generated by the symbol

(7.3)

P_{X}(z;T)=e^{\frac{T}{2}(z+1/z-2)},

first introduced in (
3.15
). This is a Szegő-type symbol, and thus the Szegő limit theorem provides us with the large-size asymptotics. Indeed,

(7.4)

D_{n}[e^{\frac{T}{2}(z+1/z-2)}]=e^{-nT}D_{n}[e^{\frac{T}{2}(z+1/z)}]=e^{-nT}G[\phi]^{n}E[\phi]\left(1+O(e^{-cn})\right),\qquad\mbox{for any}\quad c>0,

where

(7.5)

G[\phi]=[\log\phi]_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\log(\phi(\tau;t))\frac{\textrm{d}\tau}{2\pi\textrm{i}\tau},

(7.6)

E[\phi]=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}k[\log\phi]_{k}[\log\phi]_{-k},

and

(7.7)

\phi\equiv\phi(z;T):=e^{\frac{T}{2}(z+1/z)}.

Remark 7.1
.

The error term in (
7.4
) is
O(e^{-cn})
for
any

c>0
, because
e^{-c}
is the radius of the contour
\Gamma_{0}
shown in Figure
7
and due to the analytic properties of
\phi

where

and

are given by (

). Theorem

\begin{split}&\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]\\
&=D_{n}[\phi]\left\{\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{\tilde{\eta}(z_{2};n+1)\tilde{\psi}(z_{1};n+1)}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\det\begin{pmatrix}X_{11}(z_{2};n)&X_{21}(z_{2};n+1)\\
X_{11}(z_{1};n)&X_{21}(z_{1};n+1)\end{pmatrix}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\right\},\end{split}

by (
7.50
). Recall that the integral representation for the modified Bessel function of integer order
j
is

(7.52)

I_{j}(T)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{T\cos\theta}\cos(j\theta)\textrm{d}\theta=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}z^{-j}e^{\frac{T}{2}(z+z^{-1})}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z},

which is precisely the

given by (

), so we have

(7.53)

\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}=I_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}(T).

Now we focus on computing the double integral in (
7.51
). From (
A.19
) in
\Omega_{1}
(see Figure

) we have

\displaystyle X_{11}(z;n)

\displaystyle=z^{n}\alpha(z)\phi^{-1}(z)\left(1+O(\rho^{-n})\right),\qquad n\to\infty,

\displaystyle X_{21}(z;n)

\displaystyle=-\alpha^{-1}(z)\left(1+O(\rho^{-n})\right),\qquad n\to\infty.

, (

) can be written as

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]}{D_{n}[\phi]}-\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}\\

Plugging in (

) and using the scalar jump condition (

(7.57)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]}{D_{n}[\phi]}-\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}\\

&-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)^{n}\alpha_{+}(z_{2})\alpha^{-1}_{-}(z_{1})z^{1-x_{n+1}}_{2}z^{y_{n+1}}_{1}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}+O(\rho^{-n}).\end{split}

For a fixed
\mathfrak{r}>1
, define
{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{\mp}:=\left\{z\ :\ |z|=\mathfrak{r}^{\pm 1}\right\}
. We now deform the contours of integration to rewrite the previous equation as

(7.58)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]}{D_{n}[\phi]}-\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\alpha(z_{1})\alpha^{-1}(z_{2})z^{1-x_{n+1}}_{2}z^{y_{n+1}}_{1}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}\\
&-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)^{n}\alpha(z_{2})\alpha^{-1}(z_{1})z^{1-x_{n+1}}_{2}z^{y_{n+1}}_{1}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}+O(\rho^{-n})\\
&=-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\alpha(z_{1})\alpha^{-1}(z_{2})z^{-x_{n+1}}_{2}z^{y_{n+1}}_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}_{1}}{z^{k}_{2}}\right)\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}\\
&-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\left(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\right)^{n}\alpha(z_{2})\alpha^{-1}(z_{1})z^{1-x_{n+1}}_{2}z^{y_{n+1}-1}_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}_{2}}{z^{k}_{1}}\right)\frac{\textrm{d}z_{1}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z_{2}}{2\pi\textrm{i}z_{2}}+O(\rho^{-n})\\
=&-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\alpha(z)z^{n+1+k-x_{n+1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\alpha^{-1}(z)z^{y_{n+1}-n-k-1}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\\
&-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}\alpha(z)z^{y_{n+1}+k}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}\alpha^{-1}(z)z^{-x_{n+1}-k}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]+O(\rho^{-n}).\end{split}

Using (
7.8
) we obtain

(7.59)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]}{D_{n}[\phi]}-\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}\\
=&-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}e^{\frac{Tz}{2}}z^{n+1+k-x_{n+1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}e^{\frac{T}{2z}}z^{y_{n+1}-n-k-1}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\\
&-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}e^{\frac{Tz}{2}}z^{y_{n+1}+k}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}e^{\frac{T}{2z}}z^{-x_{n+1}-k}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]+O(\rho^{-n}).\end{split}

By the Cauchy Theorem we obtain

(7.60)

\begin{split}&\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n+1}[\phi;\psi(z;n+1),\eta(z;n+1);\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}]}{D_{n}[\phi]}-\phi_{y_{n+1}-x_{n+1}}\\
&=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{+}}e^{\frac{Tz}{2}}z^{n+1+k-x_{n+1}}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]\left[\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{\mathfrak{r}}_{-}}e^{\frac{T}{2z}}z^{y_{n+1}-n-k-1}\frac{\textrm{d}z}{2\pi\textrm{i}z}\right]+O(\rho^{-n}).\end{split}

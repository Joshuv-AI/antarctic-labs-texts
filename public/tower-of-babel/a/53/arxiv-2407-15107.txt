Momentum Space Feynman Integral for the Bound State Aharonov-Bohm Effect
Alviu Rey Nasir, Jingle Magallanes, Herry Pribawanto Suryawan, José Luís Da Silva

Abstract
We construct the Feynman integral for the Schrödinger propagator in the polar conjugate momentum space, which describes the bound state Aharonov-Bohm effect, as a well-defined white noise functional.



1
Introduction

In quantum mechanics, Feynman integrals have been shown to produce quantum propagators that solve the corresponding Schrödinger equation for the system (see, e.g., Ref.
[
9
]
). As a mathematically rigorous framework justifying the heuristic formulation of the Feynman integrals, White Noise Analysis (WNA)
[
13
]
has become a handy tool for a physicist to derive quantum propagators for select quantum systems in either the coordinate space or the momentum space; see, e.g., Refs.
[
15
,
4
]
.

Regarding the Feynman path integrals in the polar coordinates, Peak and Inomata first published their expression in terms of the Hamiltonian that contains the conjugate momenta of the polar coordinates
[
19
]
. Gerry and Singh
[
10
]
were the first to investigate the Feynman path integral approach to the Aharonov-Bohm (AB) effect, a quantum mechanical phenomenon initially established by Aharonov and Bohm
[
1
]
. Using WNA, Bernido and Bernido
[
3
]
derived the propagators for the particle on a circle and with the bound state AB potential in the coordinate space. However, in these cases, they did not consider and obtain propagators in the conjugate momentum space. On the other hand, the case of the momentum space AB effect (but not the bound state) in the Cartesian coordinates has already been considered (see, e.g., Ref.
[
7
]
). The case of the bound state AB effect in the conjugate momentum space has not yet been considered elsewhere.

Inspired by the advantageous role of momentum variables in experimental design, this paper focuses on a quantum system for the bound state AB effect
[
1
,
17
]
in the conjugate momentum space using the WNA framework. We show that the propagator reduces to that for the case of the particle on a circle when the magnetic flux is switched off.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give a brief review of the WNA. In Section
3
, we describe the quantum system considered and define the Feynman integral for the system in the WNA framework. We derive its propagator and show that it solves the corresponding Schrödinger equation in the conjugate momentum space. In Section
4
, we write an alternative result within the WNA framework as a perturbation on a particle-on-a-circle system employing experimentally confirmed values.
Finally, in Section
5
, we conclude and give a brief discussion of the results obtained.

2
White Noise Analysis

In this section, we give a rather short introduction to WNA; see, e.g., Refs.
[
14
,
4
]
for more details. The starting point is the basic nuclear triple

S_{d}\subset L_{d}^{2}\subset S_{d}^{\prime},

(1)

where
L_{d}^{2}
is the real separable Hilbert space of square integrable functions with norm

|f|_{0}^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{j}^{2}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad f\in L_{d}^{2}

and
S_{d}
is the nuclear space of
d
-dimensional vectors where each component is a Schwartz test function.
S_{d}^{\prime}
is the topological dual of
S_{d}
, the so-called vector-valued tempered distributions. The dual pairing between
S^{\prime}_{d}
and
S_{d}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The space
S^{\prime}_{d}
is provided with the
\sigma
-algebra of the cylinder sets
\mathscr{B}
. We define the Gaussian measure on the measurable space
(S^{\prime}_{d},\mathscr{B})
using Minlos’ theorem by fixing the characteristic function,

\int_{S^{\prime}_{d}}\exp(\mathrm{i}\langle\omega,\varphi\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\right),\quad\varphi\in S_{d}.

The triple
(S^{\prime}_{d},\mathscr{B},\mu)
is called the vector-valued white noise space. Let
L^{2}(\mu):=L^{2}(S^{\prime}_{d},\mathscr{B},\mu;\mathbb{C})
be the complex Hilbert space with a scalar product

(\!(F,G)\!)=\int_{S_{d}^{\prime}}\bar{F}(\omega)G(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega),\quad F,G\in L^{2}(\mu).

Choosing a special subspace
(S_{d})^{1}
of test functionals, we construct a Gelfand triple around
L^{2}(\mu)

(S_{d})^{1}\subset L^{2}(\mu)\subset(S_{d})^{-1}.

(2)

The elements of the space
(S_{d})^{-1}
are called generalized white noise functionals or Kondratiev distributions, and the well-known Hida distributions
(S_{d})^{*}
(or generalized Brownian functionals) form a subspace, that is,
L^{2}(\mu)\subset(S_{d})^{*}\subset(S_{d})^{-1}
. The dual pairing between
(S_{d})^{-1}
and
(S_{d})^{1}
is given as a bilinear extension of the inner product in
L^{2}(\mu)
and is denoted by
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle_{\mu}
. Distributions can be characterized by the so-called
T
-transform; see Theorem
2.2
below. Given
\Phi\in(S_{d})^{-1}
, there exists
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that we may define

T\Phi(\xi):=\left\langle\!\!\left\langle\Phi,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\langle\cdot,\xi\rangle}\right\rangle\!\!\right\rangle_{\mu},\quad\xi\in U_{p,q}:=\big\{\xi\in S_{d}\mid|\xi|_{p}^{2}<2^{-q}\big\}.

(3)

It should be noted here that the definition of the
T
-transform can be extended via analytic continuation to the complexification of
S_{d}
which is denoted by
S_{d,\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
U\subseteq S_{d,\mathbb{C}}
be an open set and
F:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
a given function. Then
F
is holomorphic on
U
iff for all
\varphi_{0}\in U

1.

for any
\varphi\in S_{d,\mathbb{C}}
the map
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto F(\varphi_{0}+z\varphi)\in\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of zero in
\mathbb{C}
,

2.

there exists an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
\varphi_{0}
such that
F
is bounded on
U^{\prime}
.

F
is holomorphic at zero iff
F
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of zero.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
U\subseteq S_{d,\mathbb{C}}
be an open set and
F:U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic at zero, then there exists a unique
\Phi\in(S_{d})^{-1}
such that
T\Phi=F
. Conversely, given
\Phi\in(S_{d})^{-1}
, then
T\Phi
is holomorphic at zero. The correspondence between
F
and
\Phi
is bijective if we identify holomorphic functions that coincide on an open neighborhood of zero.

In applications, we have to handle the convergence of sequences of distributions from
(S_{d})^{-1}
as well as integrals of
(S_{d})^{-1}
-valued functions. The following two corollaries are a consequence of Theorem
2.2
and will be applied in what follows.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\left(\Phi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(S_{d})^{-1}
be a sequence such that there exists
U_{p,q}\subset S_{d}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, so that

1.

all
T\Phi_{n}
are holomorphic on
U_{p,q}
,

2.

there exists a
C>0
such that
|T\Phi_{n}(\varphi)|\leq C
for all
\varphi\in U_{p,q}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

3.

\left(T\Phi_{n}(\varphi)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
for all
\varphi\in U_{p,q}
.

Then
\left(\Phi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly in
(S_{d})^{-1}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(\Lambda,\mathscr{A},\nu)
be a measure space and
\lambda\mapsto\Phi_{\lambda}
a mapping from
\Lambda
to
\left(S_{d}\right)^{-1}
. We assume that there exist
U_{p,q}\subset S_{d}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, such that

1.

T\Phi_{\lambda}
is holomorphic on
U_{p,q}
for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

2.

the mapping
\lambda\mapsto T\Phi_{\lambda}(\varphi)
is measurable for every
\varphi\in U_{p,q}
,

3.

there exists
C\in L^{1}(\Lambda,\mathscr{A},\nu)
such that

|T\Phi_{\lambda}(\varphi)|\leq C(\lambda)

(4)

for all
\varphi\in U_{p,q}
and for
\nu
-almost all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Then
\Phi_{\lambda}
is Bochner integrable. In particular,

\int_{\Lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\lambda)\in\left(S_{d}\right)^{-1}

(5)

and we may interchange dual pairing and integration

\left\langle\!\!\!\left\langle\int_{\Lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\lambda),\xi\right\rangle\!\!\!\right\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\Lambda}\langle\!\langle\Phi_{\lambda},\xi\rangle\!\rangle_{\mu}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\lambda),\quad\xi\in(S_{d})^{1}.

(6)

Lemma 2.5

(cf. Lemma 2.17 in Ref.
[
5
]
)
.

Let
{K}
be a
d\times d
block operator matrix on
L^{2}_{d,\mathbb{C}}
, such that
{\bm{N}}:={\mathrm{Id}}+{K}
is bounded with bounded inverse. Furthermore, assume that
\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})
exists and is different from zero. Let
M_{\bm{N}^{-1}}
be the matrix given by an orthogonal system
(\eta_{k})_{k=1,\dots,J}
,
J\in\mathbb{N}
, of nonzero functions of
L^{2}_{d,\mathbb{C}}
in the bilinear form
(\cdot,{\bm{N}}^{-1}\cdot)
, that is,
(M_{{\bm{N}}^{-1}})_{i,j}=(\eta_{i},{\bm{N}}^{-1}\eta_{j}),\;\;1\leq i,j\leq J
. Under the assumption that for every
f\in L^{2}_{d,\mathbb{C}}
either

\big(f,\Re(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})f\big)_{0}>0\quad\mathrm{or}\quad\Re(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\big(f,\Im(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})f\big)_{0}\neq 0,

(7)

where
M_{\bm{N}^{-1}}=\Re(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})+\mathrm{i}\Im(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})
with real matrices
\Re(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})
and
\Im(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})
, then

\displaystyle\Phi_{{K}}:=\mathrm{N}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\cdot,{K}\cdot\rangle)\cdot\exp(\mathrm{i}\langle\cdot,g\rangle)\prod_{k=1}^{J}\delta_{0}(\langle\cdot,\eta_{k}\rangle-y_{k}),

(8)

for
g\in L^{2}_{d,\mathbb{C}}
,
t>0
,
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R},k=1,\dots,J
, exists as a Hida distribution.

Moreover for
f\in S_{d}

T\Phi_{{K}}(f)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{J}\det(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}})}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K}))}}\\
\times\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\langle{f+g},({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})^{-1}{(f+g)}\rangle\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}(u^{\top}(M_{\bm{N}^{-1}}^{-1})u)\right),

(9)

where

u=\big(\mathrm{i}y_{1}+(\eta_{1},\bm{N}^{-1}(f+g)),\dots,\mathrm{i}y_{J}+(\eta_{J},\bm{N}^{-1}(f+g)\big).

(10)

Remark 2.6

(cf. Remark 2.13 in Ref.
[
5
]
)
.

In case of a divergent or undefined factor
1/\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})}
, the normalization constant
\mathrm{N}
can be chosen properly to “eliminate” the factor in Eq. (
9
).

The Feynman Integrals for the Bound State AB Effect

Consider an illustration of the bound state AB system as depicted in Figure 1.

Figure 1:
An illustration of the paths taken by a spinning electron around the solenoid from which came the magnetic flux “inducing” Aharonov-Bohm effect.

Charged
Particle

The circular path taken by a charged particle of charge
-e
has a radius
R
that is distant from the radius
R_{0}
of the solenoid from which the magnetic flux
\phi
comes. The charged particle can either go
cw
or
ccw
with a conjugate momentum
p_{\theta,0}
starting on the angular position
\theta_{0}
at time
t_{0}\geq 0
, and will gain a conjugate momentum of
p_{\theta}
on an angular position
\theta
at a later time
t>t_{0}
. In a later calculation, we shall pin the final conjugate momentum at
p_{\theta}^{\prime}
. Note that the charged particle does not pass through a region where the magnetic flux is present, nor does it penetrate the solenoid. In other words, the magnetic flux is in the excluded region.

The AB potential for the bound state has the form (See, e.g., Refs.
[
10
,
3
,
17
,
21
]
)

V_{\mathrm{AB}}:=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\dot{\theta}=\alpha\dot{\theta},

(11)

where
\hbar=h/(2\pi)
with
h
being the Planck’s constant,
c
is the speed of light in vacuum,
\alpha:=-e\phi/(2\pi\hbar c)
is the magnetic flux parameter, and
\dot{\theta}:=\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}t
is the angular velocity.

In the polar coordinates,
\vec{r}=(r,\theta)
, the Hamiltonian of the system is

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle H(r,\theta,\dot{\theta},t,t_{0})

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m_{0}v^{2}+V_{\mathrm{AB}}=\frac{1}{2}m_{0}(\dot{r}^{2}+r^{2}\dot{\theta}^{2})+V_{\mathrm{AB}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m_{0}R^{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}+V_{\mathrm{AB}},

where
m_{0}
is the mass of the particle. With
p_{\theta}:=m_{0}R\dot{\theta}
, the momentum conjugated to the angular position
\theta
, we have

H=C_{1}p_{\theta}^{2}-C_{2}p_{\theta},\quad C_{1}:=\frac{1}{2m_{0}},\quad C_{2}:=\frac{-\alpha}{m_{0}R}.

(13)

From Refs.
[
4
,
3
]
, we model the parametric expressions for
\theta
and
p_{\theta}
as an adaptation therein but with slight modifications,

\theta(s)=\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}(s-t_{0}+a)+\langle\omega_{\theta},E\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},s)}\rangle,\quad E:=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m_{0}R^{2}}},\;a\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\;|a|\leq t

(14)

and

p_{\theta}(s)=p_{\theta,0}+D\omega_{\theta}(s),\quad D:=\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}},\qquad 0\leq t_{0}\leq s\leq t,

(15)

which are chosen to conform with the Heisenberg uncertainty principle: we want to do a “measurement” (i.e., in this case, obtain a propagator) in the conjugate momentum space, therefore we cannot ascertain the initial value of
\theta
(that’s why
\theta
is expressed in terms of
p_{\theta,0}
and not of
\theta_{0}
). Furthermore,
a
is chosen to physically mean that as the electron “enters” the circular path before
t_{0}
, it will have to “pick up” a certain momentum and so at
t_{0}
it is already at a certain angular position
(p_{\theta,0}a)/(m_{0}R)
.

We model the action of the classical paths as an adaptation of Refs.
[
4
,
19
,
16
]

S=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big(R\theta(s)\dot{p_{\theta}}(s)+H(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(16)

Note that with Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
) (in the variable
\omega=(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p})
)

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\theta(s)p_{\theta}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\theta(s)\dot{p}_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

From which follows

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\theta(s)\dot{p}_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\big(\theta(s)p_{\theta}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=\theta(t)p_{\theta}(t)-\theta(t_{0})p_{\theta}(t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=\left(\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}(t-t_{0}+a)+\big<\omega_{\theta},E\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},t)}\big>\right)p_{\theta}(t)-\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}ap_{\theta}(t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}\big[p_{\theta}(t)(t-t_{0})+a\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0}\right)\big]+\big<\omega_{\theta},Ep_{\theta}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},t)}\big>-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(17)

where we have set
\omega_{\theta}(t_{0})=0
,
which implies that the starting momentum
is fixed at
p_{\theta,0}
on
t_{0}
.
Therefore, the action
S
in Eq. (
16
) becomes

S(\omega)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\big(-R\theta(s)\dot{p}_{\theta}(s)-H(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=-\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}}\left[p_{\theta}(t)(t-t_{0})+a\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0}\right)\right]-\big<\omega_{\theta},REp_{\theta}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},t)}\big>\\
+R\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\dot{\theta}(s)p_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}H(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=-\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}}\left[p_{\theta}(t)(t-t_{0})+a\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0}\right)\right]-\big<\omega_{\theta},REp_{\theta}(t)\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},t)}\big>\\
+\frac{1}{m_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}p^{2}_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}H(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(18)

With the Hamiltonian
H
in Eq. (

) and
\omega=(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p})
, we have

S(\omega)\\
=-\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}(t)-\frac{1}{2}p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0})+a\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0}\right)\right]-\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m_{0}}}\langle\omega_{\theta},(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0},t)}\rangle\\

(27)

Furthermore, for
\eta_{t}
given in Eq. (

\big<\eta_{t},\bm{N}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(f+g)\big>=\frac{\varepsilon D}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon CD+\frac{D}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Calculating further and substituting the functions
C
and
D
yields

\big<\eta_{t},\bm{N}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(f+g)\big>\\
=\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

This implies that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})+\langle\eta_{t},\bm{N}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(f+g)\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, we have

\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{\top}(M_{\bm{N}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}^{-1})u\right)=\exp\Biggl\{\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\varepsilon\hbar m_{0}}\Biggl[\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})+\Biggl(\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Biggr)\Biggr]^{2}\Biggr\}\\
=\exp\Biggl\{\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\varepsilon\hbar m_{0}}\Biggl[-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})^{2}+2\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\Biggl(\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Biggr)+\Biggl(\frac{\varepsilon\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+\varepsilon\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\hbar m_{0}}}{t-t_{0}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Biggr)^{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}.

(29)

Therefore, we have for
f=0
the
T
-transform of the integrand, Eq. (
)

TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}},\varepsilon}(0)\\
=\sqrt{\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\pi\varepsilon\hbar m_{0}}}\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\Biggl[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\Biggr]\Biggr\}\\
\times\exp\Biggl\{\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\varepsilon\hbar m_{0}}\Biggl[-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})^{2}+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\left(\frac{-\alpha}{R}-(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0})\right)\Biggr]\Biggr\}

(30)

where we have chosen the normalization
\mathrm{N}=\sqrt{\det(Id+K)}
. “Switching off” the perturbation, that is, taking the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have obtained a propagator for the bound state AB effect in 2D in the polar conjugate momentum space

TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(0)\\
=\delta\big(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\big)\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\Biggl[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\Biggr]\Biggr\}\\
=\delta\big(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\big)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})\Biggr].

(31)

Similar to that in Ref.
[
4
]
, the delta function here serves to conserve the magnitude of the conjugate momentum.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
p_{\theta}^{\prime},p_{\theta,0},t>t_{0}\geq 0
, the propagator

K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0}):=TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(0)=\delta\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}-\frac{e\phi}{\pi\hbar cR}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr],

(32)

solves the Schrödinger equation

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
=\frac{(p_{\theta}^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{0}}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})K(p_{\theta,1},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0}),

(33)

where

W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})\theta/\hbar}V(\dot{\theta}(s)),

(34)

and

V(\dot{\theta}(s))=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\dot{\theta}(s),

(35)

with the initial condition

\lim_{t\searrow t_{0}}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})=\delta(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}).

(36)

Proof.

Note that, using
\alpha:=(-e\phi)/(2\pi\hbar c)
we can write
V(\dot{\theta}(s))
as

V(\dot{\theta}(s))=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\dot{\theta}(s)=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\frac{p_{\theta}(s)}{m_{0}R}=\alpha\frac{p_{\theta}(s)}{m_{0}R}.

(37)

Imposing that the conjugate momentum has always been conserved, that is, in this case,
p_{\theta}^{\prime}=p_{\theta,1}
and
p_{\theta}=p_{\theta}^{\prime}
, we have the following

W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})=\alpha\frac{p_{\theta,1}}{m_{0}R}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(0)\theta/\hbar}=\alpha\frac{p_{\theta,1}}{m_{0}R}.

(38)

Now,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})K(p_{\theta,1},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
=\frac{\alpha}{m_{0}R}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}\,(p_{\theta,1})\delta\left(p_{\theta,1}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr]\\
=\frac{\alpha}{m_{0}R}\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr]\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}\,(p_{\theta,1})\delta\left(p_{\theta,1}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\\
=\left(\frac{\alpha p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr].

(39)

Therefore,

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
=\frac{(p_{\theta}^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{0}}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})K(p_{\theta,1},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
\implies\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)\delta(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0})\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr]\\
=\frac{(p_{\theta}^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{0}}\delta(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0})\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr]\\
+\left(\frac{\alpha p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr]\\
\implies\frac{p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}}\Big(p_{\theta,0}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\Big)=\frac{(p_{\theta,0})^{2}}{2m_{0}}+\frac{\alpha p_{\theta,0}}{m_{0}R}.

(40)

∎

In the absence of the magnetic flux, that is,
\alpha=0
, the propagator, Eq. (
31
), reduces to that for the case of a particle on a circle going less than a single winding around the circle (either cw or ccw), given by

TI_{0}(0):=TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}=0}(0)=\delta\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p^{2}_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}(t-t_{0})\Biggr],

(41)

which also solves the corresponding Schrödinger equation.

3.2
With Winding Case

Let us now consider the case where the charged particle is allowed to have the possibility of at least one complete winding in either
cw
or
ccw
direction (or a combination of both). Furthermore, we impose the assumption that the winding number has a direct effect not only on the angular position of the charged particle but also on its conjugate momentum. To account for this, we modify the expression of the delta function in Eq. (
20
) to have the form

\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta}^{\prime}+2\pi m_{0}R\frac{l-l_{0}}{t-t_{0}}\right),

(42)

where
l
is the integer quantum number associated with the winding (see, e.g., Refs.
[
8
,
10
,
3
]
), which takes negative values when cw (of
|l+1|
times around the circle) and positive when ccw (of
l
times around the circle), and
l_{0}
is the value of
l
at time
t_{0}
. Thus, the Feynman integrand for this case has the form

\displaystyle I_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}\exp\Biggl[{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}S(\omega)}\Biggr]\exp\Biggl[{\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\omega^{2}_{\theta}(s)+\omega^{2}_{p}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s}\Biggr]

(43)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta}^{\prime}+2\pi m_{0}R\frac{l-l_{0}}{t-t_{0}}\right).

Employing the Poisson sum formula (see, e.g., Refs.
[
12
,
2
]
), which, in our case (setting
l_{0}=0
, where we begin with zero winding at
t_{0}
) is

\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta}^{\prime}+2\pi m_{0}R\frac{l}{t-t_{0}}\right)=\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\pi m_{0}R}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)(t-t_{0})}{m_{0}R}\right],

(44)

we have (compare with Eq. (
))

I_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}=\mathrm{N}\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\pi m_{0}R}\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}(t)+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}(t)-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\exp\Biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),K(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p})\rangle\Biggr)\exp\Biggl(\mathrm{i}\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),g\rangle\Biggr)\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right]\exp\bigg(\mathrm{i}\left\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),g+k_{l}\right\rangle\bigg),

(45)

where
k_{l}:=l\frac{(t-t_{0})}{m_{0}R}\eta_{t}
.

Taking the
T
-transform of
I_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}
at the point
f=(f_{\theta},f_{p_{\theta}})
, yields

TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(f)\\
=\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\pi m_{0}R}\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right]\int_{S_{2}^{\prime}}\mathrm{N}\exp\Biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),K(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p})\rangle\Biggr)\\
\times\exp\bigg(\mathrm{i}\left\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),g+k_{l}\right\rangle\bigg)\exp\bigg(\mathrm{i}\left\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),f\right\rangle\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega)\\
=\frac{t-t_{0}}{2\pi m_{0}R}\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right]\\
\times T\left(\mathrm{N}\exp\Biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\langle(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p}),K(\omega_{\theta},\omega_{p})\rangle\Biggr)\right)(f+g+k_{l}).

(46)

In particular, following Lemma
10
, we have

T\left(\mathrm{N}\exp\Biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\langle\cdot,K\cdot\rangle\Biggr)\right)(f+g+k_{l})\\
=\left(\frac{2\pi m_{0}R}{t-t_{0}}\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})}}\\
\times\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{f+g+k_{l}},({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})^{-1}{(f+g+k_{l})}\right\rangle\right)\\
=\left(\frac{2\pi m_{0}R}{t-t_{0}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle{f+g+k_{l}},({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})^{-1}{(f+g+k_{l})}\right\rangle\right),

(47)

where we have chosen the normalization
\mathrm{N}=\left(({2\pi m_{0}R}/{(t-t_{0})})\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})}\right)
in such a way to also “eliminate”
(t-t_{0})/2\pi m_{0}R
and the divergent factor
1/{\sqrt{\det({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})}}
.

With

\left\langle{f+g+k_{l}},({\mathrm{Id}}+{K})^{-1}{(f+g+k_{l})}\right\rangle\\
=\int_{[t_{0},t)^{c}}\left(f^{2}_{\theta}(s)+f^{2}_{p_{\theta}}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s-\mathrm{i}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f^{2}_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(48)

we have

TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(f)=\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{1}{2}\Biggl[\int_{[t_{0},t)^{c}}\left(f^{2}_{\theta}(s)+f^{2}_{p_{\theta}}(s)\right)\,\mathrm{d}s-\mathrm{i}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f^{2}_{p_{\theta}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Biggr]\Biggr\}\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right].

(49)

Setting
f=0
, we obtain the quantum propagator in the polar conjugate momentum space for the bound state AB effect for
t>t_{0}

TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(0)=\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right].

(50)

We note here that Eq. (
50
) suggests that as the conservation of the magnitude of the polar conjugate momentum is imposed, that is, in the limit
p_{\theta}^{\prime}\rightarrow p_{\theta,0}
, the propagator reduces to that in Eq. (
31
).

Proposition 3.2
.

If
p_{\theta}^{\prime}=p_{\theta,0}
,
t>t_{0}\geq 0
, then the propagator

\displaystyle K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle TI_{V_{\mathrm{AB}}}(0)

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right],

solves the Schrödinger equation

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
=\frac{(p_{\theta}^{\prime})^{2}}{2m_{0}}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}p_{\theta,1}W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})K(p_{\theta,1},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0}),

(52)

where

W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})\theta/\hbar}V(\dot{\theta}(s)),

(53)

and

V(\dot{\theta}(s))=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\dot{\theta}(s),

(54)

with the initial condition

\lim_{t\searrow t_{0}}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})=\delta(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}).

(55)

Proof.

This can be verified explicitly by trying to solve directly the Schrödinger equation using the propagator Eq. (
51
). The equality of the Schrödinger equation can be shown to hold only when preserving the magnitude of the conjugate momentum.

We begin with the lhs of Eq. (
52
):

\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})\\
=\mathrm{i}\hbar\exp\Biggl\{-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2\hbar m_{0}}\left[\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}+\frac{2\alpha}{R}\right)(t-t_{0})+\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)(t-t_{0}+2a)\right]\Biggr\}\\
\times\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}l\frac{t-t_{0}}{m_{0}R}\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\right]\\
\times\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-\frac{e\phi}{\pi\hbar cR}+p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\Big)+\left(\mathrm{i}l\frac{1}{m_{0}R}\right)\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)\Biggr].

(56)

Now, note that the term
\left(\mathrm{i}l\frac{1}{m_{0}R}+\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\right)\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)
has no counterpart in the rhs of the Schrödinger equation since the only expression

W(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})=-\frac{e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,1})\theta/\hbar}\dot{\theta}

(57)

that could contain this term has the potential term
\alpha:=\frac{-e\phi}{2\pi\hbar c}
. Thus, the only way around this is when
p_{\theta}^{\prime}=p_{\theta,0}
, so as to make
\left(\mathrm{i}l\frac{1}{m_{0}R}+\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\right)\left(p_{\theta,0}-p_{\theta}^{\prime}\right)=0
and the Schrödinger equation is satisfied.

∎

Having examined the above two cases, namely, “Incomplete Winding Case” and “With Winding Case” (see Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
, respectively), we are therefore led to the following main result.

Theorem 3.3

(Particle in a Bound State AB Potential Momentum Space Propagator)
.

For a charged particle undergoing a bound state AB effect as described and depicted in Fig.
1
, the momentum- (specifically the polar-conjugate-momentum-) space propagator is given by

K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})=\delta\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}}{2m_{0}\hbar}\Big(p_{\theta,0}-\frac{e\phi}{\pi\hbar cR}\Big)(t-t_{0})\Biggr].

(58)

This further implies that the magnitude of the conjugate momentum of the particle is conserved through the time interval
[t_{0},t]
with
t>t_{0}\geq 0
, regardless of the winding number
l
taken by the motion of the particle.

Remark 3.4

(Particle on a Circle Momentum Space Propagator)
.

The main result also implies that, for the case where the magnetic flux
\phi=0
, which is the case of a particle on a circle, the momentum- (specifically the polar-conjugate-momentum-) space propagator is given by

K(p_{\theta}^{\prime},t|p_{\theta,0},t_{0})=\delta\left(p_{\theta}^{\prime}-p_{\theta,0}\right)\exp\Biggl[-\frac{\mathrm{i}p_{\theta,0}^{2}}{2m_{0}\hbar}(t-t_{0})\Biggr],

(59)

which also obeys the conservation of momentum regardless of the winding number similar to that in the main result above.

4
Alternative WNA Approach in Achieving the Bound State AB Effect

Here we outline another WNA approach for the bound state AB effect based on a perturbation series of the Feynman integrand for the particle on a circle.

4.1
Particle on a Circle Perturbed by a Potential that is Growing Exponentially in Angular Velocity

Definition 4.1

(cf. Ref.
[
18
]
)
.

We define a potential
V:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

V(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{\beta x}\,\mathrm{d}m(\beta)\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(60)

with
m
being a complex measure on the Borel sets on
\mathbb{R}
fulfilling the condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{C|\beta|}\,\mathrm{d}|m|(\beta)<\infty,\qquad\forall C>0.

(61)

For each
s\in[t_{0},t]
, setting
x:=\mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}s=:\dot{\theta}(s)
, we have adapted a version of the exponentially growing potential,
V(\dot{\theta}(s))
, which we call a class of potentials that are exponentially growing in angular velocity.

We first set up the Feynman integrand as a perturbation series of the form

I_{V}=I_{0}\exp\Biggl(-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}V(\dot{\theta}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\Biggr)\\
=I_{0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\mathrm{i}/\hbar)^{n}}{n!}\int_{[t_{0},t)^{n}}\mathrm{d}^{n}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}m(\beta_{j})\,\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\dot{\theta}(s_{j})}\\
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\mathrm{i/\hbar})^{n}}{n!}\int_{[t_{0},t)^{n}}\mathrm{d}^{n}s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}m(\beta_{j})\,I_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\dot{\theta}(s_{j})}.

(62)

Then, we need to show that
I_{V}
is a white noise object before we can proceed to solve the propagator.

Consider the functional
\Psi:=I_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\dot{\theta}(s_{j})}
. As an adaptation of Refs.
[
6
,
18
]
, we can have our version for the polar coordinates starting with Eq. (
14
) to have

p_{\theta}(s_{j})=p_{\theta,0}+D\omega_{\theta}(s_{j})=p_{\theta,0}+\big<\omega_{\theta},D\delta_{s_{j}}\big>,

(63)

or

4.2

\alpha=\frac{ke}{2\pi\hbar c}\phi=\frac{ke}{2\pi\hbar c}\frac{2\pi\hbar c}{ne}=\frac{k}{n},\quad k,n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},

5
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we have demonstrated that within the WNA framework, ignoring the winding, a charged particle in a bound state AB potential maintains the conservation of the magnitude of the conjugate momentum. This phenomenon can be attributed to the conservation of the total angular momentum of the system
\vec{L}
. Classically,
\vec{L}=\vec{r}\times\vec{p}
, and since the magnitude of
\vec{r}

However, one question that remains is: Why were we unable to incorporate the winding number information into the momentum space propagator? The explanation is rooted in the Heisenberg uncertainty principle; in this scenario, determining the conjugate momentum of a charged particle at a specific time results in the loss of information about its precise position, including the winding number. Using WNA methods, our findings thus verify the physics of the bound state AB effect with constant radius and magnetic flux. These results can serve as a springboard for the design of experiments to further analyze the bound state AB effect.

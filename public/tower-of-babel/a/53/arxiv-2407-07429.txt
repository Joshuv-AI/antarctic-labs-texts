Time evolutions of information entropies in a one-dimensional Vlasov-Poisson system
K. Maekaku, H. Sugama, T.-H. Watanabe

Abstract
A one-dimensional Vlasov-Poisson system is considered to elucidate how the information entropies of the probability distribution functions of the electron position and velocity variables evolve in the Landau damping process. Considering the initial condition given by the Maxwellian velocity distribution with the spatial density perturbation in the form of the cosine function of the position, we derive linear and quasilinear analytical solutions that accurately describe both early and late time behaviors of the distribution function and the electric field. The validity of these solutions is confirmed by comparison with numerical simulations based on contour dynamics. Using the quasilinear analytical solution, the time evolutions of the velocity distribution function and its kurtosis indicating deviation from the Gaussian distribution are evaluated with the accuracy of the squared perturbation amplitude. We also determine the time evolutions of the information entropies of the electron position and velocity variables and their mutual information. We further consider Coulomb collisions which relax the state in the late-time limit in the collisionless process to the thermal equilibrium state. In this collisional relaxation process, the mutual information of the position and velocity variables decreases to zero while the total information entropy of the phase-space distribution function increases by the decrease in the mutual information and demonstrates the validity of Boltzmann’s H-theorem.

I
Introduction

Landau damping is one of the most intriguing physical processes involving
collective interactions between waves and particles in collisionless plasmas.
[
14
]

Despite the time reversibility of the Vlasov-Poisson equations,
their solution shows that, as time progresses,
plasma oscillations are Landau-damped
and the electric field energy is converted into the kinetic energy of particles.
The effects of Landau damping are universally observed in various plasma wave-particle resonance phenomena,
such as drift waves and geodesic acoustic mode (GAM) oscillations in slab and toroidal magnetic field configurations
in space and fusion plasmas, and have been
the subject of extensive theoretical research.
[
2
,
24
,
17
,
12
,
18
,
28
,
1
,
7
,
11
,
26
,
29
,
22
,
23
,
20
]

The most commonly used theoretical model to study Landau damping
is the one-dimensional Vlasov-Poisson system
which consists of electrons with ions treated as uniform background positive charge with infinite mass.
The solution of the linear initial value problem derived by Case and Van Kampen for this system
is well known.
[
2
,
24
,
17
,
12
]

The behavior of the late-time solution exhibiting Landau damping
can be well approximated by using only eigenfrequencies with the slowest damping rate.
In this study, the effect of an infinite number of complex eigenfrequencies is included to
represent the early-time behavior of the electric field.
Incidentally, it is shown in Ref.
[
28
]
that the plasma dispersion function can be expressed in the form of an infinite continued fraction. From this fact, we can also understand that an infinite number of complex eigenfrequencies exist as zeros of the dielectric function.
It is also shown in Ref.
[
1
]
that a very high number of poles are required for the correct calculation of density and pressure at an early stage in the Vlasov-Poisson system.

When treating the position
x
and velocity
v
of electrons as random variables, denoted as
X
and
V
respectively,
the information entropy
[
13
]

S_{p}(X,V)\equiv-\int\int p(x,v,t)\log p(x,v,t)\,dx\,dv
derived from the joint probability density function
p(x,v,t)
of
(X,V)
is known to be one of
the Casimir invariants in the Vlasov-Poisson system,
[
3
,
15
]

and it does not depend on time
t
.
On the other hand, the entropies
S_{p}(X)\equiv-\int p_{X}(x,t)\log p_{X}(x,t)\,dx
and
S_{p}(V)\equiv-\int p_{V}(v,t)\log p_{V}(v,t)\,dv
determined from the marginal probability density functions
p_{X}(x,t)=\int p(x,v)\,dv
and
p_{V}(v,t)=\int p(x,v)\,dx
of
X
and
V
respectively, are allowed to vary with time.
In the present work, we derive novel analytical expressions that accurately represent the early-time behaviors of
the linear solutions for the electric field and the distribution function by series expansions in time and velocity variables.
Combining these early-time expressions with the late-time approximate solutions,
we accurately determine the time evolution of the spatially averaged velocity distribution function of electrons
obtained from quasilinear theory.
[
5
,
25
]

Using these linear and quasilinear analytical solutions, we clarify the time evolution of
the information entropies
S_{p}(X)
,
S_{p}(V)
,
and the mutual information
I(X,V)\equiv S_{p}(X)+S_{p}(V)-S_{p}(X,V)
associated with the Landau damping.
We here note that, in Ref.
[
4
]
, the energy conversion in phase space density moments in the Vlasov-Maxwell system is investigated by separating the kinetic entropy [which corresponds to
S_{p}(X,V)
] to the two parts [which correspond to
S_{p}(X)
and
S_{p}(X,V)-S_{p}(X)
].
Also, in Ref.
[
9
]
, the role of the entropy production in momentum transfer in the Vlasov-Maxwell system is discussed based on the information theory.

We note that
the quasilinear solution for the background velocity distribution function at time
t
is obtained by integrating the product of the electric field and the linear solution of the perturbed distribution function over time from the initial time to
t
.
Therefore, using only the linear solutions represented by a few complex eigenfrequencies is insufficient.
We emphasize that, to describe the time evolutions of the entropies and the mutual information,
it is necessary to use accurate expressions of the linear solutions near the initial time
as derived in this study.

The validity of the obtained linear and quasilinear solutions is confirmed by comparison with simulation results
based on contour dynamics.
[
27
,
19
]

We also obtain the velocity distribution
p_{V}
in the limit of
t\rightarrow+\infty
and show how
it deviates from the Gaussian distribution.
In addition, we consider the effects of Coulomb collisions, which relax the distribution function to the thermal equilibrium state,
decrease the mutual information
I(X,V,t)
to zero, increase the entropy
S_{p}(X,V)
,
and thus validate Boltzmann’s H-theorem.
[
10
]

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Sec. II, the basic equations of the Vlasov-Poisson system are presented,
and the solution to its linear initial value problem is provided using the Laplace transform.
The validity of the linear analytical solutions for the electric field and the perturbed distribution function is confirmed
by comparison with contour dynamics simulations with small initial perturbation amplitudes.
In Sec. III, using approximate expressions for complex eigenfrequencies with large absolute values,
an approximate integral formula for the electric field incorporating the effects of an infinite number of complex eigenfrequencies is derived.
In addition, the linear analytical solutions for the electric field and the distribution function near the initial time
are expressed as series expansions in time and velocity variables.
In Sec. IV, using the results of Secs. II and III,
a quasilinear analytical solution describing the time evolution of the background velocity distribution function of electrons is derived,
and its validity is confirmed by contour dynamics simulations as well.
By using this quasilinear analytical solution, the time evolution of the electron kinetic energy increases due to Landau damping,
the velocity distribution function in the limit of
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and
physical quantities, such as the kurtosis representing a deviation from the Gaussian distribution, are accurately determined.
In Sec. V, the time evolution of the information entropies of the position and velocity variables of electrons and
the mutual information is determined with an accuracy of the order of the squared perturbation amplitude.
In Sec. VI, the changes in the entropies and the mutual information from the collisionless process
to the thermal equilibrium state due to Coulomb collisions are evaluated.
Finally, conclusions and discussion are given in Sec. VII.

II
Linear Analysis of Vlasov-Poisson system

II.1
Vlasov-Poisson system

Under the assumption that there is no magnetic field,
the Vlasov equation for electrons is given by

\frac{\partial f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)}{\partial t}+{\bf v}\cdot\frac{\partial f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)}{\partial{\bf x}}-\frac{e}{m}{\bf E}({\bf x},t)\cdot\frac{\partial f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)}{\partial{\bf v}}=0,

(1)

where
-e
and
m
are the electron charge and mass, respectively,
and
E(x,t)
represents the electric field.
The distribution function
f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)
of electrons
is defined such that

f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)d^{3}xd^{3}v
represents the number of electrons in the phase space volume element

d^{3}x\;d^{3}v
around
({\bf x},{\bf v})
at time
t
.
We ignore the motion of ions that have the uniform density
n_{0}
and infinite mass.
Then, the electric field
{\bf E}({\bf x},t)
is determined by Poisson’s equation,

\nabla\cdot{\bf E}({\bf x},t)=4\pi e\left(n_{0}-\int d^{3}v\;f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)\right).

(2)

We now assume that
f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)
and
{\bf E}({\bf x},t)
are independent of
y
and
z
, and that
E_{y}=E_{z}=0
.
Integration of
f({\bf x},{\bf v},t)
with respect to
v_{y}
and
v_{z}
is done to define

f(x,v_{x},t)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{y}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{z}\;f({\bf x},{\bf v},t).

(3)

Then, from Eq. (
1
), we obtain the Vlasov equation
in the
(x,v)
phase space space
as

\frac{\partial f(x,v,t)}{\partial t}+v\frac{\partial f(x,v,t)}{\partial x}-\frac{e}{m}E(x,t)\frac{\partial f(x,v,t)}{\partial v}=0,

(4)

where
v=v_{x}
and
E(x,t)=E_{x}(x,t)
are used.
Poisson’s equation in Eq. (
2
) is rewritten as

\frac{\partial E(x,t)}{\partial x}=4\pi e\left(n_{0}-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f(x,v,t)\right).

(5)

Hereafter, we consider the structure of the distribution function
f(x,v,t)
on the two-dimensional
(x,v)
phase space instead of the six-dimensional
({\bf x},{\bf v})
space.
The number of electrons in the area element
dx\;dv
about the point
(x,v)
in the phase space
at time
t
is given by
f(x,v,t)dx\;dv
.

We now write

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}[f_{1}(k,v,t)\exp(i\;k\;x)],

\displaystyle E(x,t)

\displaystyle=

Laplace transform

We now use the Laplace transform to solve the linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations

III
Approximate representation of linear solutions in early time

III.1
Approximate expression for complex eigenfrequencies with large absolute values

We here recall that the complex-valued eigenfrequency
\omega_{\mu}
is determined by
Eq. (
29
) using the plasma dispersion function
Z(\zeta)
.
When
|\zeta|\gg 1
,
|{\rm Re}\zeta|>|{\rm Im}\zeta|
, and
{\rm Im}\zeta<0
,
we have

1+\zeta Z(\zeta)=i\sqrt{\pi}\zeta e^{-\zeta^{2}}+{\cal O}(\zeta^{-2}).

(39)

Therefore, it is found from Eqs. (
29
) and (
39
)
that, when

|\zeta_{\mu}|\gg 1
and
{\rm Im}\zeta_{\mu}<0
,

\zeta_{\mu}
satisfies

i\sqrt{\pi}\zeta_{\mu}e^{-\zeta_{\mu}^{2}}=-\kappa^{2}+{\cal O}(\zeta_{\mu}^{-2}).

(40)

Using Eq. (
40
) and

\zeta_{\mu}\equiv|\zeta_{\mu}|e^{i\theta_{\mu}}
,
we obtain

\zeta_{\mu}\simeq i\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{\zeta_{\mu}^{2}}=i\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\exp[|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}(\cos 2\theta_{\mu}+i\sin 2\theta_{\mu})],

(41)

from which we get

|\zeta_{\mu}|\simeq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\exp(|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\cos 2\theta_{\mu}),

(42)

and

e^{i\theta_{\mu}}\simeq i\exp(i|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\sin 2\theta_{\mu}).

(43)

Therefore,
\theta_{\mu}
satisfies

\theta_{\mu}\simeq\frac{\pi}{2}+|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\sin 2\theta_{\mu}+2\pi n,

(44)

where
n
is an integer.
Then, we define
\delta_{\mu}
by

2\theta_{\mu}=-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta_{\mu}

(45)

to have

|\zeta_{\mu}|\simeq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\exp(|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\sin\delta_{\mu}),

(46)

and

\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\kappa^{2}}|\zeta_{\mu}|\right)\simeq|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\sin\delta_{\mu}\simeq|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\delta_{\mu},

(47)

which leads to

\delta_{\mu}\simeq\frac{1}{|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\kappa^{2}}|\zeta_{\mu}|\right),

(48)

where

0<\delta_{\mu}\ll 1

is assumed.
Substituting Eq. (
45
) into Eq. (
44
),
we have

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\delta_{\mu}}{2}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}-|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\cos\delta_{\mu}+2\pi n

(49)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}-|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{\mu}^{2}}{2}\right)+2\pi n,

which yields

|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}\simeq\frac{3\pi}{4}-\frac{\delta_{\mu}}{2}+2\pi n.

(50)

Thus,
the approximate value of

\zeta_{\mu}\equiv|\zeta_{\mu}|e^{i\theta}

for
|\zeta_{\mu}|\gg 1
is given by

|\zeta_{\mu}|\simeq\sqrt{\left(2n+\frac{3}{4}\right)\pi}

(51)

with

\theta_{\mu}=-\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\delta_{\mu}}{2}\simeq-\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{2|\zeta_{\mu}|^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\kappa^{2}}|\zeta_{\mu}|\right)

(52)

where the integer
n
needs to be large,
n\gg 1
, to satisfy the condition

|\zeta_{\mu}|\gg 1
.
Recall that when
\omega_{\mu}
is a complex-valued eigenfrequency,

-\omega_{\mu}^{*}
is so, too.
Then, the approximate values of complex-valued eigenfrequency with large absolute values
are denoted by
\omega_{n}
and
-\omega_{n}^{*}
which are given by

\frac{\omega_{n}}{kv_{T}}\equiv\zeta_{n}\equiv\sqrt{\left(2n+\frac{3}{4}\right)\pi}\;\;e^{i\theta_{n}},

(53)

\frac{-\omega_{n}^{*}}{kv_{T}}\equiv-\zeta_{n}^{*}\equiv\sqrt{\left(2n+\frac{3}{4}\right)\pi}\;\;e^{i(\pi-\theta_{n})}

(54)

and

\theta_{n}\equiv-\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{2|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\kappa^{2}}|\zeta_{n}|\right)

(55)

where the integer
n
is used instead of
\mu

to specify different approximate eigenfrequencies.
The approximation is good for
n\gg 1
.

In Fig.
4
,
the exact complex eigenfrequencies and
the approximate eigenfrequencies
for
k\lambda_{D}=1/2
are plotted as red and blackdots,
respectively, in the region where
{\rm Re}(\omega)>0
.
Here, Eqs. (
53
) and (
55
) with
n=0,1,2,\cdots
are
used to evaluate the approximate eigenfrequencies.
It is observed that the approximate values approach the exact values
as the magnitude of the complex eigenfrequencies increases.

III.2
Approximate expression for effects of an infinite number of complex-valued eigenfrequencies

We here consider an infinite series,

\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}{\rm Re}[e^{i\zeta_{n}\tau}/(C-\zeta_{n}^{2})]
,
where
C
is a positive constant and
\tau\equiv kv_{T}t
.
This form of the infinite series is included in Eq. (
37
) for
E(k,t)
.
When
n\gg 1
and
|\zeta_{n}|\gg C
,
we use Eqs. (
53
) and (
55
) to
make the following approximations,

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left[\frac{e^{i\zeta_{n}\tau}}{C-\zeta_{n}^{2}}\right]

(56)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\exp(|\zeta_{n}|\tau\sin\theta_{n})\cos(2\theta_{n}+|\zeta_{n}|\tau\cos\theta_{n})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\delta_{n}+\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

where

|\zeta_{n}|\equiv\sqrt{\left(2n+\frac{3}{4}\right)\pi},\hskip 14.22636pt\delta_{n}\equiv 2\theta_{n}+\frac{\pi}{2}\equiv\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\kappa^{2}}|\zeta_{n}|\right).

(57)

For
\tau=0
, using Eq. (
56
) and
|\delta_{n}|\ll 1

gives

{\rm Re}\left[\frac{1}{C-\zeta_{n}^{2}}\right]\simeq-\frac{\delta_{n}}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}.

(58)

For
\tau>0
, we can take
N\gg 1
such that

|\zeta_{N}|\tau/\sqrt{2}\gg\delta_{N}>0
.
Then, Eq. (
56
) is rewritten as

{\rm Re}\left[\frac{e^{i\zeta_{n}\tau}}{C-\zeta_{n}^{2}}\right]\simeq-\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

(59)

where
n\geq N
and
|\zeta_{n}|^{2}\propto n
.
We find that, for a large but fixed integer
N
,

\displaystyle S_{N}^{N^{\prime}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N}^{N^{\prime}}{\rm Re}\left[\frac{e^{i\zeta_{n}\tau}}{C-\zeta_{n}^{2}}\right]

(60)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=N}^{N^{\prime}}\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

does not uniformly converge to

S_{N}(\tau)\equiv\lim_{N^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty}S_{N}(\tau)
because

|S_{N}^{N^{\prime}}(\tau)-S_{N}(\tau)|<\epsilon

requires
N^{\prime}\geq N^{\prime}(\epsilon,\tau)

where

N^{\prime}(\epsilon,\tau)\propto|\zeta_{N^{\prime}(\epsilon,\tau)}|^{2}\propto\delta(\epsilon)/\tau^{2}

which diverges as
\tau\rightarrow+0
.
Therefore, the number of the complex-valued eigenfrequencies, which are
necessary to be included for accurately evaluating
E(k,t)
, diverges to infinity
as
t\rightarrow+0
.
On the other hand,
when
v
is fixed, the series expansion for representing
f_{1}(k,v,t)
uniformly converges.
It is because the extra factor
1/(\omega_{n}-kv)
included in the series expansion
accelerates the convergence as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Now, we use

\displaystyle 2\pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\zeta_{n+1}|^{2}-|\zeta_{n}|^{2}=(|\zeta_{n+1}|+|\zeta_{n}|)(|\zeta_{n+1}|-|\zeta_{n}|)

(61)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 2|\zeta_{n}|(|\zeta_{n+1}|-|\zeta_{n}|)

to write

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76219pt\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}{\rm Re}\left[\frac{e^{i\zeta_{n}\tau}}{C-\zeta_{n}^{2}}\right]\simeq-\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

(62)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}\frac{(|\zeta_{n+1}|-|\zeta_{n}|)}{|\zeta_{n}|}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{|\zeta_{n}|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|\zeta_{N}|}^{+\infty}\frac{d|\zeta|}{|\zeta|}\exp\left(-\frac{|\zeta|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{|\zeta|\tau}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|\zeta_{N}|\tau/\sqrt{2}}^{+\infty}dx\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{|\zeta_{N}|\tau/\sqrt{2}}dx\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{x},

where

\int_{0}^{+\infty}dx\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{x}=\frac{\pi}{4}

(63)

is used.

Now, using Eqs. (
37
), (
62
), and

{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{e^{-i\omega_{\mu}t}}{1+\kappa^{2}-\omega_{\mu}^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}}\biggr]=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{e^{-i\zeta_{\mu}\tau}}{(1+\kappa^{2})/2\kappa^{2}-\zeta_{\mu}^{2}}\biggr],

(64)

we can express
E(k,t)
as

\displaystyle E(k,t)

(65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\;\alpha\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{ek}(1+\kappa^{2})\sum_{{\rm Re}\zeta_{\mu}>0}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{e^{-i\zeta_{\mu}\tau}}{(1+\kappa^{2})/2\kappa^{2}-\zeta_{\mu}^{2}}\biggr]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-i\;\alpha\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{ek}(1+\kappa^{2})

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\biggl(\sum_{0<{\rm Re}\zeta_{\mu}<{\rm Re}\zeta_{N}}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{e^{-i\zeta_{\mu}\tau}}{(1+\kappa^{2})/2\kappa^{2}-\zeta_{\mu}^{2}}\biggr]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|\zeta_{N}|\tau/\sqrt{2}}^{+\infty}dx\frac{e^{-x}\sin x}{x}\biggr)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle E_{N}(k,t),

where
\zeta_{n}\equiv\omega_{n}/kv_{T}
and

\zeta_{n}\tau\equiv\omega_{n}t
.
Recall that we need to choose
N\gg 1
such that

|\omega_{N}|t/\sqrt{2}\gg\delta_{N}>0
.
However,
when
N\gg 1
,

E(k,t=0)\simeq E_{N}(k,t=0)=i(4\pi n_{0}e/k)\alpha=i(m/e)(\omega_{p}/k)\alpha

also holds (as shown later)
and
E(k,t)\simeq E_{N}(k,t)
for any
t\geq 0
.

From the residue theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\zeta\frac{Z(\zeta)e^{-i\zeta\tau}}{1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{\mu}\frac{\kappa^{2}(1+\kappa^{2})e^{-i\omega_{\mu}t}}{1+\kappa^{2}-\omega_{\mu}^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\sum_{{\rm Re}\omega_{\mu}>0}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{\kappa^{2}(1+\kappa^{2})e^{-i\omega_{\mu}t}}{1+\kappa^{2}-\omega_{\mu}^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}}\biggr]

where
\tau>0
.
Using the asymptotic expansion of
Z(\zeta)
for
|\zeta|\gg 1
and
taking the limit for
\tau\rightarrow+0
, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+0}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\zeta\frac{Z(\zeta)e^{-i\zeta\tau}}{1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+0}\sum_{{\rm Re}\omega_{\mu}>0}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{\kappa^{2}(1+\kappa^{2})e^{-i\omega_{\mu}t}}{1+\kappa^{2}-\omega_{\mu}^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}}\biggr]=i.

However, for
\tau=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{C}d\zeta\frac{Z(\zeta)}{1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]}

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\sum_{{\rm Re}\omega_{\mu}>0}{\rm Re}\biggl[\frac{\kappa^{2}(1+\kappa^{2})}{1+\kappa^{2}-\omega_{\mu}^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}}\biggr]=\frac{i}{4}(3-\kappa^{2}),

which is derived using the residue theorem.

In Eq. (

), the integral
\int_{C}d\zeta
is done
along the circle

\zeta
-plane

is given by

\theta
of
\zeta

from

.

) and (

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+0}

\sum_{\mu}
results in
different values.

.
We use Eq. (
68
) to confirm that

Approximate solution in early time

We here use the asymptotic expansion of
Z(\zeta)
for
|\zeta|\gg 1
given in
Eq. (
169
) to derive

\displaystyle 1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\kappa^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{\zeta^{2n}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\frac{\Gamma(N+1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\zeta e^{-\zeta^{2}}\int_{i\infty}^{\zeta}\frac{e^{s^{2}}}{s^{2N}}ds.

Then, for
|\zeta|\gg 1
and

-\pi/4<\arg\zeta<5\pi/4
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Z(\zeta)}{1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\zeta}\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}e_{n}(\kappa^{2})\zeta^{-2n}+{\cal O}(\zeta^{-2N-2})\right],

where
e_{n}(\kappa^{2})

(n=1,2,\cdots)
are recursively defined by

e_{n}(\kappa^{2})\equiv\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\Gamma(1/2)}+\kappa^{-2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(n-j+1/2)}{\Gamma(1/2)}e_{j}(\kappa^{2}),

(74)

with
e_{0}(\kappa^{2})\equiv 1
.
Thus, we find

\displaystyle e_{1}(\kappa^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa^{-2},

\displaystyle e_{2}(\kappa^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}+\kappa^{-2}\left(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}e_{1}(\kappa^{2})\right)=\frac{3}{4}+\kappa^{-2}+\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{-4},

\displaystyle\cdots.

(75)

We now use Eq. (
73
) to get

\displaystyle\int_{C}\frac{d\zeta}{2\pi}\frac{Z(\zeta)e^{-i\zeta\tau}}{1+\kappa^{-2}[1+\zeta Z(\zeta)]}

(76)

\displaystyle=

IV
Analytical solution based on quasilinear theory and its verification by numerical simulation

IV.1
Spatially averaged distribution function

In this subsection, formulas that hold rigorously
for the spatially averaged distribution function and its associated entropy are
derived without using linear approximations based on small perturbation amplitudes.
The distribution function
f(x,v,t)
is a periodic function of
x
and
the period length is given by
L\equiv 2\pi/k
.
We use

\langle\cdots\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\cdots\;dx,

(87)

to denote the average with respect to
x
.
The Vlasov equation is averaged in
x
to yield

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\langle f\rangle(v,t)+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[\langle f\rangle(v,t)\,a(v,t)\right]=0,

(88)

where
\langle f\rangle(v,t)\equiv\langle f(x,v,t)\rangle
is
the electron distribution function averaged in
x
,
and

a(v,t)
represents the average acceleration of electrons defined by

\langle f\rangle(v,t)\,a(v,t)\equiv-\frac{e}{m}\langle E(x,t)\,f(x,v,t)\rangle.

(89)

From Eq. (
88
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\langle f\rangle(v,t)\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}\right]+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[\langle f\rangle(v,t)\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}a(v,t)\right]

\displaystyle=\langle f\rangle(v,t)\,m\,v\,a(v,t),

(90)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\,m\,v\,a(v,t),

(91)

where
m\,v\,a(v,t)
represents the
x
-averaged rate of change of the kinetic energy of
an electron with velocity
v
at time
t
caused by the electric field.
The total energy conservation of the Vlasov-Poisson system is written as

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}+\frac{1}{8\pi}\langle E^{2}\rangle\right]=0.

(92)

The Gibbs entropy
S[f]
per unit length in the
x
-direction is defined as a functional of the
distribution function
f(x,v,t)
by

S[f]\equiv-\left\langle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f(x,v,t)\log f(x,v,t)\right\rangle,

(93)

which is found to be an invariant,

\frac{d}{dt}S[f]=0.

(94)

We now use the
x
-averaged distribution function
\langle f\rangle(v,t)
to
define the entropy density
S[\langle f\rangle]
by

S[\langle f\rangle]\equiv-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\log\langle f\rangle(v,t),

(95)

which is not an invariant but a function of time
t
.
From the viewpoint of information theory,
[
13
]

the entropy density
S[\langle f\rangle]
is given from the average of

S(v,t)\equiv-\log\langle f\rangle(v,t)=-\log[\langle f\rangle(v,t)(dv/n_{0})]+\log(dv/n_{0}),

(96)

which represents the Shannon information content (or self-entropy)

-\log[\langle f\rangle(v,t)(dv/n_{0})]=-\log[P_{V}(v_{j})]

[see Eq. (
211
)]
plus an additional constant given by
\log(dv/n_{0})

for the probability
\langle f\rangle(v,t)(dv/n_{0})
of finding the electron velocity
in the interval

[v-\frac{1}{2}dv,v+\frac{1}{2}dv]

where
dv
is regarded as an infinitesimal positive constant.
Supplementary explanations on information entropies
in the Vlasov-Poisson system are presented in Appendix E.
We now consider
S(v,t)
as the information entropy (except an additional constant)
of the electron with the velocity
v
at time
t
.
From Eq. (
88
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+a(v,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right)S(v,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+a(v,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right)\log f(v,t)

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial a(v,t)}{\partial v}.

Here, we define
u(v_{0},t)
which satisfies the differential equation,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(v_{0},t)=a(u(v_{0},t),t)

(98)

with the initial condition

u(v_{0},t=0)=v_{0}.

(99)

Then,
u(v_{0},t)
represents the velocity of the electron which has
the initial velocity
v_{0}
and the history of acceleration

a(v,t^{\prime})

(0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t)
.
The interval
[v_{0}-\frac{1}{2}dv_{0},v_{0}+\frac{1}{2}dv_{0}]

in the
v
-space evolves to the interval

[u(v_{0},t)-\frac{1}{2}du(v_{0},t),u(v_{0},t)+\frac{1}{2}du(v_{0},t)]

at time
t

when the velocities of the electrons in the interval
are given by
u(v^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(
v_{0}-\frac{1}{2}dv_{0}\leq v^{\prime}\leq v_{0}+\frac{1}{2}dv_{0}
,

0\leq t^{\prime}\leq t)
.
We note that the number (or probability) of the electrons found in the interval

[u(v_{0},t)-\frac{1}{2}du(v_{0},t),u(v_{0},t)+\frac{1}{2}du(v_{0},t)]

is invariant in time,

\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t),t)\;du(v_{0},t)=\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)\;dv_{0}.

(100)

Using Eqs. (
88
) and (
97
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S(u(v_{0},t),t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\biggl[\biggl(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+a(v,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\biggr)\log\langle f\rangle(v,t)\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t)}

(101)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl[\frac{\partial a(v,t)}{\partial v}\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t)}

which implies that

\partial a(v,t)/\partial v
represents the rate of change in
the information entropy
S(v,t)\equiv-\log\langle f\rangle(v,t)

of the electron with the velocity
v
at time
t

along the trajectory
u(v_{0},t)
in the
v
-space.
Then, the increase of
S(v,t)
along the trajectory during the
time interval
[0,t]
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta S(u(v_{0},t),t)\equiv S(u(v_{0},t),t)-S(v_{0},0)

\displaystyle=-\log\biggl[\frac{\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t)}{\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)}\biggr]=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\biggl[\frac{\partial a(v,t^{\prime})}{\partial v}\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t^{\prime})},

(102)

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t)=\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)\;\exp\bigl[-\Delta S(u(v_{0},t),t)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)\;\exp\biggl(-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\biggl[\frac{\partial a(v,t^{\prime})}{\partial v}\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t^{\prime})}\biggr).

(103)

We also find that the rate of change in the entropy of
S[\langle f\rangle]
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}S[\langle f\rangle]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\;S(v,t)

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\langle f\rangle(v,t)\;\frac{\partial a(v,t)}{\partial v}.

Another formula is obtained as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),0)=\biggl[a(v,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\log\langle f\rangle(v,0)\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t)},

(105)

which is integrated in time
t
to derive

\log\biggl[\frac{\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),0)}{\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)}\biggr]=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\biggl[a(v,t^{\prime})\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\log\langle f\rangle(v,0)\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t^{\prime})}

(106)

and

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),0)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt=\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\biggl[a(v,t^{\prime})\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\log\langle f\rangle(v,0)\biggr]_{v=u(v_{0},t^{\prime})}\biggr).\hskip 22.76219pt

(107)

Then, using Eqs. (
103
) and (
107
), we obtain

\log\biggl[\frac{\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t)}{\langle f\rangle(v_{0},0)}\biggr]=\Omega_{t}(v_{0}),

(108)

and

\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t)=\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),0)\;\exp\Omega_{t}(v_{0}),

(109)

where the function
\Omega_{t}(v_{0})
is defined by

\displaystyle\Omega_{t}(v_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;\Omega(u(v_{0},t^{\prime}),t^{\prime}),

(110)

with

\Omega(v,t)\equiv-a(v,t)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\log\langle f\rangle(v,0)-\frac{\partial a(v,t)}{\partial v}.

(111)

Now, we consider the case in which the initial
x
-averaged distribution function

\langle f\rangle(v,0)=f_{0}(v)

is given by the Maxwellian equilibrium distribution function in Eq. (
25
).
Then, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\log f_{0}(v)=-\frac{m\;v}{T},

(112)

which is substituted into Eq. (
107
) to obtain

f_{0}(u(v_{0},t))=f_{0}(v_{0})\;\exp\biggl(-\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(u(v_{0},t),t)}{T}\biggr),

(113)

where

\Delta{\cal E}(u(v_{0},t),t)\equiv m\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;u(v_{0},t^{\prime})\;a(u(v_{0},t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})

(114)

represents the change in the kinetic energy of the electron with the initial velocity
v_{0}

caused by the acceleration due to the electric field during the time interval
[0,t]
.
Then, we can rewrite Eqs. (
111
), (
110
), and (
109
) as

\Omega(v,t)=\frac{m}{T}v\;a(v,t)-\frac{\partial a(v,t)}{\partial v},

(115)

\Omega_{t}(v_{0})=\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(u(v_{0},t),t)}{T}-\Delta S(u(v_{0},t),t),

(116)

and

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle(u(v_{0},t),t)=f_{0}(u(v_{0},t))\;\exp\Omega_{t}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=f_{0}(u(v_{0},t))\exp\biggl[\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(u(v_{0},t),t)}{T}-\Delta S(u(v_{0},t),t)\biggr],\hskip 22.76219pt

(117)

respectively.

Here, we should note that

\Omega_{t}(v_{0})
defined in Eq. (
116
)
corresponds to the dissipation function employed
by Evans and Searles to present their fluctuation theorem.
[
8
,
6
]

The fluctuation theorem by Evans and Searles is based on
the time-reversible Liouville equation and gives
the formula for the ratio of the probabilities that the dissipation
function takes the positive and negative values with the same absolute value.
The Vlasov equation differs from the Liouville equation treated by Evans and Searles
in that it contains the electron’s acceleration term determined from the distribution
function through Poisson’s equation.
Therefore, the fluctuation theorem cannot be directly applied to
the function
\Omega_{t}(v_{0})
in our case.
However, as explained in Appendix E,
we can show
the non-negativity of the expected value of
\Omega_{t}(v_{0})
,
which leads to the inequality in the form of the second law of thermodynamics.

IV.2
Analysis to second order in perturbation amplitude

In this subsection, we investigate
the time evolution of the
x
-averaged
distribution function considered in Sec. IV.A
up to the second order in the perturbation amplitude
\alpha

based on the results of the linear analysis in Sec. II.
Recall that the initial condition for the distribution function is given by

\displaystyle f(x,v,t=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}(v)+{\rm Re}[f_{1}(k,v,t=0)\exp(i\;k\;x)]

(118)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}(v)[1+\alpha\cos(k\,x)],

where
f_{0}(v)
is the Maxwellian in Eq. (
25
)
and
f_{1}(k,v,t=0)=\alpha f_{0}(v)
is used.
We have already derived the linear solution of the perturbed distribution function

f_{1}(x,v,t)={\rm Re}[f_{1}(k,v,t)\exp(i\;k\;x)]
for
t>0
, from which
the electric field

E(x,t)={\rm Re}[E(k,t)\exp(i\;k\;x)]
is obtained.
Hereafter, we represent the order of magnitude of the small perturbation amplitude by

\alpha
.
Then, the linear solutions

f_{1}(x,v,t)={\rm Re}[f_{1}(k,v,t)\exp(i\;k\;x)]={\cal O}(\alpha)

and

E(x,t)={\rm Re}[E(k,t)\exp(i\;k\;x)]={\cal O}(\alpha)

are used to derive

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle(v,t)a(v,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{m}\langle E(x,t)f(x,v,t)\rangle

(119)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{2m}{\rm Re}[E^{*}(k,t)f_{1}(k,v,t)],

where higher order terms than
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
are neglected.
Now, we can write

\langle f\rangle(v,t)=f_{0}(v)+f_{2}(v,t),

(120)

where we keep terms only up to
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
and
f_{2}(v,t)

represents the
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
part given by

\displaystyle f_{2}(v,t)=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left[\frac{e}{m}\langle E(x,t^{\prime})f(x,v,t^{\prime})\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{2m}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;{\rm Re}\left[E^{*}(k,t^{\prime})\frac{\partial f_{1}(k,v,t^{\prime})}{\partial v}\right].

(121)

We can also write

\langle f\rangle(v,t)a(v,t)=f_{0}(v)a(v,t)+{\cal O}(\alpha^{4}),

(122)

and neglect the
{\cal O}(\alpha^{4})
part to obtain

a(v,t)=-\frac{e}{2m}{\rm Re}\left[E^{*}(k,t)\frac{f_{1}(k,v,t)}{f_{0}(v)}\right],

(123)

and

\displaystyle f_{2}(v,t)=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial v}[f_{0}(v)a(v,t)]

\displaystyle=f_{0}(v)\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\left[\frac{mv}{T}a(v,t^{\prime})-\frac{\partial a(v,t^{\prime})}{\partial v}\right]

\displaystyle=f_{0}(v)\left[\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(v,t)}{T}-\Delta S(v,t)\right],

(124)

where

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal E}(v,t)\equiv\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;m\,v\,a(v,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2}v\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;{\rm Re}\left[E^{*}(k,t^{\prime})\frac{f_{1}(k,v,t^{\prime})}{f_{0}(v)}\right],

(125)

and

\displaystyle\Delta S(v,t)\equiv S(v,t)-S(v,0)=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;\frac{\partial a(v,t^{\prime})}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2m}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\;{\rm Re}\left[E^{*}(k,t^{\prime})\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left(\frac{f_{1}(k,v,t^{\prime})}{f_{0}(v)}\right)\right].

(126)

Since
a(v,t)={\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
,
the variation in the electron velocity during the time interval
[0,t]

is of
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
.
Neglecting
{\cal O}(\alpha^{4})
effects,
we can regard
\Delta{\cal E}(v,t)
as
the change in the kinetic energy
of the electron with the initial velocity
v
caused by the acceleration due to the electric field
during the time interval
[0,t]
.
Recall that
\partial a(v,t)/\partial v
represents the rate of change in
the information content
S(v,t)\equiv-\log\langle f\rangle(v,t)
associated with the
distribution of electrons in the velocity space.
Then,
\Delta S(v,t)
represents the change in the information content
(or the information entropy) of the electron with the initial velocity
v
)
during the time interval
[0,t]
.

From Eq. (
124
), we find

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f_{2}(v,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f_{0}(v)\left[\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(v,t)}{T}-\Delta S(v,t)\right]=0

(127)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(t)}{T}\equiv\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f_{0}(v)\;\Delta{\cal E}(v,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f_{0}(v)\;\Delta S(v,t)\equiv\Delta S[\langle f\rangle](t)

(128)

which implies that, to the second order in
\alpha
,
the change in the information (or Gibbs) entropy

V
Time evolution of information entropies

In this section, we consider the time evolution of information
entropies in the Vlasov-Poisson system.
For that purpose, we use the solution of the initial value problem in
Sec. II that gives the perturbed density and the electric field of

{\cal O}(\alpha)
as

n_{1}(x,t)=n_{1}(t)\cos(k\;x),\hskip 14.22636ptE(x,t)=E_{1}(t)\sin(k\;x),

(134)

where

E_{1}(t)=-\frac{4\pi e}{k}n_{1}(t).

(135)

Here,
n_{1}(t)
and
E_{1}(t)
satisfy the initial conditions,

n_{1}(t=0)=\alpha\;n_{0},\hskip 14.22636ptE_{1}(t=0)=-\alpha\frac{4\pi en_{0}}{k},

(136)

and they approach zero as

t\rightarrow+\infty
,

n_{1}(x,\infty)=0,\hskip 14.22636ptE(x,\infty)=0.

(137)

The electric field energy density at time
t

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi}\langle E(x,t)^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(\frac{4\pi e}{k}\right)^{2}\langle[n_{1}(x,t)]^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}[E_{1}(t)]^{2}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(\frac{4\pi e}{k}\right)^{2}[n_{1}(t)]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{0}T}{2(k\lambda_{D})^{2}}\frac{\langle n_{1}(x,t)^{2}\rangle}{n_{0}^{2}}=\frac{n_{0}T}{4(k\lambda_{D})^{2}}\frac{[n_{1}(t)]^{2}}{n_{0}^{2}}.

(138)

Also, from the energy conservation law, we obtain

\displaystyle n_{0}\Delta{\cal E}(t)+\frac{1}{8\pi}\langle E(x,t)^{2}\rangle=n_{0}\Delta{\cal E}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\langle[E(x,0)]^{2}\rangle=\alpha^{2}\frac{n_{0}T}{4(k\lambda_{D})^{2}},

(139)

where
\Delta{\cal E}(t)
represents the change in the kinetic energy of the electron
during the time interval
[0,t]

given by Eq. (
128
).

We regard the electron’s position and velocity as random variables which are
represented by
X
and
V
, respectively, as explained in Appendix E.

and

[see Eq. (

and

and

195

\displaystyle S_{p}(X)\equiv S[p_{X}]\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p_{X}(x,t)\log p_{X}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}\frac{dx}{L}\;\left(\frac{n(x,t)}{n_{0}}\right)\log\left(\frac{n(x,t)}{n_{0}}\right)+\log(L)

\displaystyle\simeq\log(L)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}\frac{dx}{L}\left(\frac{n_{1}(x,t)}{n_{0}}\right)^{2}.

(140)

In the last line of Eq. (

VI
Collisional relaxation to thermal equilibrium

Coulomb collisions are neglected in the Vlasov equation (
1
).
However, even if the collision frequency is very small but finite,
Coulomb collisions eventually relax the distribution function to the Maxwellian

f_{M\infty}=n_{M\infty}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi T_{M\infty}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left[-\frac{m}{2T_{M\infty}}(v-u_{M\infty})^{2}\right]

(153)

at
t=+\infty
while conserving total particles’ number, momentum, and energy.
The Maxwellian distribution function

f_{M\infty}(v)
is the equilibrium solution of the Boltzmann equation
that is given by including the collision term in the Vlasov equation.
When the collision operator acts on the Maxwellian, the collision term
vanishes.
Then, substituting
f_{M\infty}(v)
into the left-hand side into the Vlasov equation,
it also vanishes.
It can be proven from the above-mentioned fact that

n_{M\infty}
,
T_{M\infty}
, and
u_{M\infty}
are all independent
of
t
and
x
.
Under the initial condition in Eq. (
118
),
we can use conservation laws for particles’ number, energy, and momentum
to derive

n_{M\infty}=n_{0},\hskip 14.22636ptu_{M\infty}=0,\hskip 14.22636pt\frac{n_{0}T_{M\infty}}{2}=\frac{n_{0}T_{0}}{2}+\frac{\langle[E(x,0)]^{2}\rangle}{8\pi}.

(154)

Thus, the distribution function in the thermal equilibrium state reached
by the collisional relaxation is given by

f_{M\infty}=n_{0}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi T_{M\infty}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{mv^{2}}{2T_{M\infty}}\right).

(155)

Here, ions are treated as uniform background positive charge with infinite mass,
so no energy exchange between electrons and ions
due to collisions is considered to occur.
From Eq. (
154
), we also have

\frac{\Delta T_{M\infty}}{T}=\frac{T_{M\infty}-T}{T}=\frac{\langle[E(x,0)]^{2}\rangle}{4\pi n_{0}T}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2(k\lambda)^{2}}=2\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(\infty)}{T}.

(156)

From the equilibrium probability distribution function

p_{M}(x,v)=f_{M\infty}(v)/(n_{0}L)
and its marginal probability
distribution functions
p_{MX}(x)=1/L
and

p_{MV}(v)=f_{M\infty}(v)/n_{0}
,
we can define the entropies

S[p_{M}]
,
S[p_{MX}]
, and
S[p_{MV}]

in the thermal equilibrium
where

p_{M}=p_{MX}\cdot p_{MV}

(157)

holds so that the random variables
X
and
V
are statistically
independent, and accordingly, the mutual information
of
X
and
V
vanishes,

I_{M}(X,V)\equiv S[p_{M}]-S[p_{MX}]-S[p_{MV}]=0.

(158)

The deviation of the entropy
S[p_{MX}]
from the initial value
S[p_{X}](t=0)

is given by

\displaystyle\Delta S[p_{MX}]\equiv S[p_{MX}]-S[p_{X}](t=0)

\displaystyle=\left\langle[1+\alpha\cos(kx)]\log[1+\alpha\cos(kx)]\right\rangle\simeq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}.

(159)

We see from
Eqs. (
142
) and (
159
)
that
\Delta S[p_{MX}]
and
\Delta S[p_{X}](\infty)

agree with each other up to
{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
.
Next, the deviation of
S[p_{MV}]

VII
Conclusions and Discussion

In this paper,
the one-dimensional Vlasov-Poisson system describing a plasma consisting of electrons and uniformly distributed ions
with infinite mass is considered.
Using analytical solutions and contour dynamics simulations,
we elucidate how the information entropies determined from the distribution functions of the electron position and velocity variables evolve
in the Landau damping process.
Under the initial condition given by the Maxwellian velocity distribution with the perturbed density distribution in the form of the cosine function,
linear and quasilinear analytical solutions describing the time evolutions of the electric field and the distribution function
are obtained and shown to be in good agreement with results from numerical simulations based on contour dynamics.

A novel approximate integral formula including the effect of an infinite number of complex eigenfrequencies to correctly evaluate the electric field is presented.
In addition, the linear analytical solutions for the electric field and the distribution function
near the initial time are expressed as series expansions in time and velocity variables.
The quasilinear analytical solution describing the time evolution of the spatially averaged velocity distribution function is
obtained, and its validity is confirmed by the contour dynamics simulation results.
These analytical expressions of the linear and quasilinear solutions are useful
for verification of the accuracy of simulations of the Vlasov-Poisson system
using methods other than the contour dynamics as well.
Using the quasilinear analytical solution,
it becomes possible to accurately determine the time evolutions of the electron kinetic energy
and the background velocity distribution function associated with the Landau damping.
Furthermore, the time evolutions of the information entropies of the electron position
and velocity variables, and the mutual information are
determined with an accuracy of the order of the squared perturbation amplitude
\alpha^{2}
.
It is well known that, in a collisionless process, the information entropy determined from the joint probability density distribution function of position and velocity variables (or the phase-space distribution function) is one of the Casimir invariants.
On the other hand,
the decrease in the squared mean of spatial density fluctuations increases the information entropy of the position variable,
and the ratio of the increase in the electron kinetic energy to the temperature equals the increase in
the information entropy of the velocity variable to the order of
\alpha^{2}
.
The sum of these increases in the information entropies of the position and velocity variables
yields the mutual information that is initially zero.

The relative entropy obtained by comparing the velocity distribution at time
t

with the initial distribution is a positive quantity of order of
\alpha^{4}
.
This leads to the fact that, even in the collisionless process,
the inequality in the form of the second law of thermodynamics
holds in the relation between the heat transfer from the Maxwellian velocity distribution
to the electric field
and the conditional entropy of the electron position variable for
a given velocity distribution.

When Coulomb collisions are taken into account,
they relax the distribution function at
t\rightarrow+\infty
in the collisionless process
further to the thermal equilibrium state.
In this relaxation, the mutual information of the position and velocity variables decreases to zero, although
the information entropies of the position and velocity variables do not change to the order of
\alpha^{2}
.
Then, the entropy determined from the phase-space distribution increases by the amount of the decrease in the mutual information.
It indicates the validity of Boltzmann’s H-theorem.
Future extensions of the present work include studies on
the position dependence of the phase-space distribution function of order
\alpha^{2}
, which is not included in the quasilinear solution,
and the analysis of the information entropies and the mutual information of the position and velocity variables
to the order of
\alpha^{4}
.

The Contour Dynamics (CD) method was proposed for solving inviscid and incompressible motions in fluid mechanics

In the Vlasov-Poisson system,

T
from the two-dimensional phase space to itself
is defined by

T(x,v)=(x,-v),

(190)

which satisfies

represents the identity map.
We can easily confirmed that,

is a solution of the Vlasov equation,

f(T(x,v),-t)=f(x,-v,-t)
is a solution as well.
It should be noted that
the property of the time reversal map mentioned above
holds whether the linear or nonlinear case is considered.
When the initial condition in Eq. (
118
) is employed,
the initial distribution function

satisfies
the condition

f(x,-v,t=0)=f(x,v,t=0)
.
Then, for the solution

under this initial condition,

f(x,-v,-t)
becomes the solution satisfying the same initial condition.
Thus, from the uniqueness of the solution under the same initial condition,
we find that

holds for any time

and that

E(x,-t)=E(x,t)
results from Poisson’s equation.

It is related to the distribution function in Eq. (
4
) by

p(x,v)=\frac{1}{n_{0}L}f(x,v),

(193)

where
n_{0}
is the average number density of electrons.
Here, the functions
p
and
f
depends on time
t
although

t
is omitted from the arguments of these functions for simplicity.
The marginal probability distribution functions for
X
and
V
are
defined by

p_{X}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)=\frac{1}{L}\frac{n(x)}{n_{0}}

(194)

and

p_{V}(v)=\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p(x,v)=\frac{1}{n_{0}}\langle f\rangle(v),

(195)

respectively.
Here, the electron density is denoted by
n(x)
.

The information (or Shannon) entropy
[
13
]
is originally defined for probability distributions
of discrete variables.
Rigorously speaking,
for probability distributions of continuous variables,
it should be called the differential entropy.
When dividing the interval
[-L/2,L/2)
of the variable
x
is divided into

N_{x}
intervals of equal width,
the central value
x_{i}
in each interval is given by

x_{i}=i\Delta x,\hskip 14.22636pt\Delta x=\frac{L}{N_{x}},\hskip 14.22636pti=-\frac{(N_{x}-1)}{2},\cdots,\frac{(N_{x}-1)}{2}.

(196)

In the same way, when dividing the interval
[-v_{\rm max},v_{\rm max}]

of the variable
v
into
N_{v}
intervals of equal width,
the central value
v_{j}
in each interval is given by

v_{j}=j\Delta v,\hskip 14.22636pt\Delta v=\frac{2v_{\rm max}}{N_{v}},\hskip 14.22636pti=-\frac{(N_{v}-1)}{2},\cdots,\frac{(N_{v}-1)}{2}.

(197)

Setting

z_{ij}=(x_{i},v_{j}),\hskip 14.22636ptN_{z}=N_{x}N_{v},

(198)

and letting
v_{\rm max}
be sufficiently large,
we consider the
(x,v)
space as a collection of
N_{z}
cells,
each of which is centered at
z_{ij}=(x_{i},v_{j})
.
Then, we approximate continuous variables
(x,v)
by discrete ones

z_{ij}=(x_{i},v_{j})
, and relate
p(x,v)
to the probability distribution function
P(x_{i},v_{j})

of the discrete variables
(x_{i},v_{j})
by

p(x,v)\Delta x\Delta v=P(x_{i},v_{j}),

(199)

which satisfies the normalization condition,

\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)=\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})=1.

(200)

Using
P(x_{i},v_{j})
,
the information entropy is defined by

S[P]\equiv\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})S(x_{i},v_{j})\equiv-\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})\log P(x_{i},v_{j}),

(201)

where

S(x_{i},v_{j})\equiv-\log P(x_{i},v_{j})

(202)

represents the self-entropy which is also called
the self-information or information content.
Similarly, we employ
p(x,v)
to define the differential entropy by

\displaystyle S[p]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)s(x,v)

(203)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)\log p(x,v)

where

s(x,v)\equiv-\log p(x,v).

(204)

We can easily derive the following relations,

S(x_{i},v_{j})\equiv s(x,v)-\log(\Delta x\Delta v),

(205)

and

S[P]\equiv S[p]-\log(\Delta x\Delta v).

(206)

Here, we should carefully note that

S(x_{i},v_{j})
and
S[P]
are non-negative while

s(x,v)
and
s[p]
can take both positive and negative values but satisfy
the conditions,

s(x,v)\geq\log(\Delta x\Delta v),\hskip 14.22636ptS[p]\geq\log(\Delta x\Delta v).

(207)

From the marginal probability distribution functions

p_{X}(x)
and
p_{V}(v)
,
we can define the marginal distribution functions
P_{X}(x_{i})
and
P_{V}(v_{j})

of the discrete variables

x_{i}
and
v_{j}
by

P_{X}(x_{i})=\sum_{j}P(x_{i},v_{j}),\hskip 14.22636ptP_{v}(v_{j})=\sum_{i}P(x_{i},v_{j}),

(208)

which are related to
p_{X}(x)
and
p_{V}(v)
by

P_{X}(x_{i})=p_{X}(x)\Delta x,\hskip 14.22636ptP_{V}(v_{j})=p_{V}(v)\Delta v.

(209)

Then,

P_{X}(x_{i})
and
P_{V}(v_{j})

are used to define the information entropies

S[P_{X}]
and
S[P_{V}]
as

\displaystyle S[P_{X}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i}P_{X}(x_{i})S_{X}(x_{i})\equiv-\sum_{i}P_{X}(x_{i})\log P_{X}(x_{i})

\displaystyle S[P_{V}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{i}P_{V}(v_{j})S_{V}(v_{j})\equiv-\sum_{i}P_{V}(v_{j})\log P_{V}(v_{j}),\hskip 14.22636pt

(210)

where the self-entropies
S_{X}(x_{i})
and
S_{V}(v_{j})

are defined by

S_{X}(x_{i})\equiv-\log P_{X}(x_{i}),\hskip 14.22636ptS_{V}(v_{j})\equiv-\log P_{V}(v_{j}).

(211)

Similarly, we use
p_{X}(x)
and
p_{V}(v)
to
the entropies
S[p_{X}]
and
S[p_{V}]
by

\displaystyle S[p_{X}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p_{X}(x)s_{X}(x)\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p_{X}(x)\log p_{X}(x)

\displaystyle S[p_{V}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v)s_{V}(v)\equiv-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v)\log p_{V}(v),

where

s_{X}(x)\equiv-\log p_{X}(x),\hskip 14.22636pts_{V}(v)\equiv-\log p_{V}(v).

(213)

We can also confirm the following relations,

\displaystyle S_{X}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle s_{X}(x)-\log(\Delta x)\geq 0

\displaystyle S_{V}(v_{j})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle s_{V}(v)-\log(\Delta v)\geq 0

(214)

and

\displaystyle S[P_{X}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle S[p_{X}]-\log(\Delta x)\geq 0

\displaystyle S[P_{V}]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle S[p_{V}]-\log(\Delta v)\geq 0.

(215)

The conditional entropies
S_{P}(V|X)

and
S_{P}(X|V)
are defined from
the probability distribution functions
P(x_{i},v_{j})
,
P_{X}(x_{i})
, and
P(v_{j})
by

\displaystyle S_{P}(V|X)\equiv-\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})\log\left(\frac{P(x_{i},v_{j})}{P_{X}(x_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle=S[P]-S[P_{X}]=S_{P}(X,V)-S_{P}(X)\geq 0,

\displaystyle S_{P}(X|V)\equiv-\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})\log\left(\frac{P(x_{i},v_{j})}{P(v_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=S[P]-S[P_{V}]=S_{P}(X,V)-S_{P}(V)\geq 0.

(216)

Similarly, the conditional entropies
S_{p}(V|X)
and

S_{p}(X|V)
are defined from
p(x,v)
,
p_{X}(x)
,
and
p_{V}(v)
by

\displaystyle S_{p}(V|X)\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\int_{\textendash\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)\log\left(\frac{p(x,v)}{p_{X}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=S[p]-S[p_{X}]=S_{p}(X,V)-S_{p}(X)\geq\log(\Delta v),

\displaystyle S_{p}(X|V)\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\int_{\textendash\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)\log\left(\frac{p(x,v)}{p_{V}(v)}\right)

\displaystyle=S[p]-S[p_{V}]=S_{p}(X,V)-S_{p}(V)\geq\log(\Delta x).

(217)

To represent the mutual dependence of the random variables

X
and
V
,
the mutual information
I(X,V)

is defined by

\displaystyle I(X,V)\equiv-\sum_{i,j}P(x_{i},v_{j})\log\left(\frac{P(x_{i},v_{j})}{P_{X}(x_{i})P_{V}(v_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle=S_{P}(X)+S_{P}(V)-S_{P}(X,V)

\displaystyle=S_{P}(X)-S_{P}(X|V)=S_{P}(V)-S_{P}(V|X)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx\int_{\textendash\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v)\log\left(\frac{P(x,v)}{P_{X}(x)P_{V}(v)}\right)

\displaystyle=S_{p}(X)+S_{p}(Y)-S_{p}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=S_{p}(X)-S_{p}(X|V)=S_{p}(V)-S_{p}(V|X)\geq 0.

(218)

The mutual information takes the same non-negative value whether it is defined from
the distribution functions of the discrete variables or from those of the continuous variables.

Hereafter, we express the time dependence of the distribution function by writing

p(x,v,t)=f(x,v,t)/(n_{0}L)
,

p_{X}(x,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v,t)=n(x,t)/(n_{0}L)
,
and

p_{V}(v,t)=\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p(x,v,t)=\langle f\rangle(v,t)/n_{0}
.
It is well known that, for the solution
f(x,v,t)
of the Vlasov equation in Eq. (
4
),
the entropy
S[p]
defined from
p(x,v,t)=f(x,v,t)/(n_{0}L)
by

S[p]\equiv S_{p}(X,V)\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p(x,v,t)\log p(x,v,t)

(219)

is an invariant,

\frac{d}{dt}S[p]\equiv\ \frac{d}{dt}S_{p}(X,V)=0.

(220)

On the other hand,
the entropies defined from
p_{X}(x,t)
and
p_{V}(v,t)
by

S[p_{X}]\equiv S_{p}(X)\equiv-\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dx\;p_{X}(x,t)\log p_{X}(x,t)

(221)

and

S[p_{V}]\equiv S_{p}(V)\equiv-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v,t)\log p_{V}(v,t)

(222)

depend on time
t
in general.

The relative entropy (or Kullback-Leibler divergence) of the distribution
p_{V}(v,t)
at time
t

relative to the initial distribution
p_{V}(t,0)
is defined as

S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v,t)\log\left(\frac{p_{V}(v,t)}{p_{V}(v,0)}\right),

(223)

which takes only non-negative values and vanishes
if and only if
p_{V}(v,t)=p_{V}(v,0)
.
The non-negativity is derived from the inequality
\log(x^{-1})=-\log x\geq 1-x
for
x>1
.
The relative entropy
S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)
is the average difference
between the information quantities,

s_{V}(v,0)-s_{V}(v,t)=-\log p_{V}(v,0)-(-\log p_{V}(v,t))=\log[p_{V}(v,t)/p_{V}(v,0)]
, and represents the amount of information lost
when approximating the correct velocity distribution
p_{V}(v,t)
at time
t
by the initial distribution function
p_{V}(v,0)
.
From Eq. (

\Omega_{t}(v_{0})=\log\left[\frac{p_{V}(u(v_{0},t),t)}{p_{V}(u(v_{0},t),0)}\right],

(224)

and

\displaystyle S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{0}\;p_{V}(v_{0},0)\log\biggl[\frac{p_{V}(u(v_{0},t),t)}{p_{V}(u(v_{0},t),0)}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv_{0}\;p_{V}(v_{0},0)\;\Omega_{t}(v_{0}).

(225)

When the initial distribution function is the Maxwellian,
using Eqs. (
117
) and (
225

S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)=\frac{\Delta{\cal E}(t)}{n_{0}T}-\Delta S[P_{X}](t)\geq 0.

(226)

Here,
\Delta{\cal E}(t)
is the increase in the kinetic energy of electrons per unit volume

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal E}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;f_{0}(v_{0})\Delta{\cal E}(u(v_{0},t),t)

(227)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\langle E^{2}\rangle(t)-\langle E^{2}\rangle(0)}{8\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n_{0}\Delta Q(X|V).

We now call two systems described by
p_{X}(x)
and

as the
X
-system and the

Then, we can regard

\Delta Q(X|V)=-\Delta{\cal E}/n_{0}

-system.
Recalling that
\Delta S[P_{X}](t)

(228)

From Eqs. (

), and (

S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)=\Delta S_{P}(X|V)-\frac{\Delta Q(X|V)}{T}\geq 0.

Since the initial distribution

we regard the

.

When the values of

are close to each other,
we obtain

\displaystyle S(p_{V},t||p_{V},0)=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v,t)\log\left[\frac{p_{V}(v,0)}{p_{V}(v,t)}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v,t)\left[-\left(\frac{p_{V}(v,0)}{p_{V}(v,t)}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p_{V}(v,0)}{p_{V}(v,t)}-1\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;p_{V}(v,t)\left(\frac{p_{V}(v,0)}{p_{V}(v,t)}-1\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\;\frac{[p_{V}(v,t)-p_{V}(v,0)]^{2}}{p_{V}(v,0)}.

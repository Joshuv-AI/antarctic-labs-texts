A compact formula for the quantum fluctuations of energy
Rajeev Singh

Abstract
A formula to calculate the quantum fluctuations of energy in small subsystems of a hot and relativistic gas is derived. We find an increase in fluctuations for subsystems of small sizes, but we agrees with the energy fluctuations in the canonical ensemble if the size is large enough. Not only one can use our expression to find the limit of the concepts of energy density or fluid element in connection to relativistic heavy-ion collisions, but also in other areas of physics where one studies matter with high temperature and velocity.

2
Basic definitions

We assume a subsystem
S_{a}
of the larger
thermodynamic system
S_{V}
which contain the spinless boson particles having mass
m
. This system
S_{V}

is defined by the canonical ensemble and
specified by the temperature
T
, where
\beta=1/T
. Note that, volume
V
of
S_{V}
is larger than the volume of
S_{a}
, and
V
is large enough for doing integrals over the momentum of the particles.
We characterize our bosonic system by a quantum scalar field which is in thermal equilibrium as

[
52
]

\phi(t,{\bm{x}})=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}~2\omega_{\bm{k}}}}\left(a_{\bm{k}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}e^{-ik\cdot x}+a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}e^{ik\cdot x}\right),

(1)

with
a_{\bm{k}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}
and
a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}
being the annihilation and creation operators, respectively, and
\omega_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{{\bm{k}}^{2}+m^{2}}
is the particle’s energy. For the calculation of thermal averaging we need to
know the following expectation values of the products of two and four creation and/or annihilation operators
[
53
,
54
]
. Other combinations of creation and annihilation operators can be obtained easily from the following expression

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}}a_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}}),

(2)

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}}a^{\dagger}_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}a_{{\bm{p}}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}a_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\bigg(\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{p}})~\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{p}}^{\prime})+\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{p^{\prime}}})~\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-{\bm{p}})\bigg)f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}).

(3)

where
f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})
is the Bose–Einstein distribution function,
f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})=1/(\exp[\beta~\omega_{{\bm{k}}}]-1)
.

Now we define
[
52
]
the following operator
\mathcal{H}_{a}
which characterizes the energy density of a
finite
size subsystem
S_{a}
which is placed at the origin of the coordinate system where
\mathcal{H}(x)\equiv(\dot{\phi}^{2}+({\bf{\nabla}\phi)}^{2}+m^{2}\phi^{2})/2
is the Hamiltonian density for the free real scalar field

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{a}=\frac{1}{(a\sqrt{\pi})^{3}}\int d^{3}{\bm{x}}~\mathcal{H}(x)~\exp\left(-\frac{{\bm{x}}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right).

(4)

We used above the Gaussian profile in order to remove boundary effects which might come from the sharp boundaries.

Now one can easily obtain the thermal average of
\mathcal{H}_{a}
as
[
1
]

\displaystyle\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle=\int\frac{d^{3}{{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\omega_{{\bm{k}}}~f\left(\omega_{{\bm{k}}}\right)\equiv\varepsilon(T).

(5)

To arrive at the Eq. (
5
), we used normal ordering in order to remove the infinite vacuum contribution coming from the zero-point energy term. Eq. (
5
) is also independent of the size
a
reflecting the spatial uniformity of the thermodynamic system
S_{V}
.
In order to calculate the quantum fluctuation of energy of the subsystem
S_{a}

we use the formula for variation and the normalized standard deviation, given respectively as

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(a,m,T)

\displaystyle=\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}::\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle-\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle^{2}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(a,m,T)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}::\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle-\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle^{2})^{1/2}}{\langle:\mathcal{H}_{a}:\rangle}\,.

(7)

Using Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
), we find

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(a,m,T)

\displaystyle=\int dK~dK^{\prime}f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})(1+f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}))\bigg[(\omega_{{\bm{k}}}\omega_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}+{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{k}}^{\prime}+m^{2})^{2}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})^{2}}

\displaystyle+(\omega_{{\bm{k}}}\omega_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}+{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bm{k}}^{\prime}-m^{2})^{2}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}({\bm{k}}+{\bm{k}}^{\prime})^{2}}\bigg].

(8)

where
dK=d^{3}{{k}}/((2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{{\bm{k}}})
and neglected a temperature independent divergent term which may be related to the pure vacuum energy fluctuation
[
55
]
.

Eq. (
8
) is the main result of ours which determine the quantum fluctuations of energy of the “Gaussian” subsystems
S_{a}
of
the larger thermodynamic system
S_{V}
.
Numerically we can calculate the results for any system of size
a
, temperature
T
, and
mass
m
, the plots of which will be shown below.

One should also keep in mind the factor of degeneracy which is important when we study the thermodynamic properties of particles. This degeneracy factor is related to the internal degrees of freedom, for instance, spin, isospin or color charge. Therefore to take into consideration in our framework, the
g
copies of particles, one needs to consider the
g
copies of scalar field which is given by both creation and annihilation operators which commute for different species of particles. Hence, the following replacements are required
for the calculation of the quantum fluctuations

\varepsilon\rightarrow g\varepsilon,\quad\sigma^{2}\rightarrow g\sigma^{2}.

(9)

3
Thermodynamic limit

We believe that in the limit of large system size, that is,

a\rightarrow\infty

(but still with
a^{3}\ll V
),
our quantum fluctuation expression should agree with classical statistical mechanics
[
1
]
. Hence, we use the following representation of the Dirac delta function in Eq. (
8
)

\displaystyle\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{p}})=\lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{a^{3}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{p}})^{2}}.

(10)

which give rise to the formula valid in the large size
a
limit

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{g}{(2\pi)^{3/2}a^{3}}\int\frac{d^{3}{{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\omega_{{\bm{k}}}^{2}~f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})(1+f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})).

Note that we calculate the quantum fluctuation for massless particles as
\sigma^{2}\sim T^{5}/a^{3}
, therefore RHS of the last equation can be written as

c_{V}=\frac{d\varepsilon}{dT}=\frac{g}{T^{2}}\int\frac{d^{3}{{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}~\omega_{{\bm{k}}}^{2}~f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})(1+f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})).

(11)

where
c_{V}
is
the specific heat at constant volume.
Hence, in large
a
limit
[
56
]

\displaystyle V_{a}\sigma_{n}^{2}=\frac{T^{2}c_{V}}{\varepsilon^{2}}=V\frac{\langle H^{2}\rangle-\langle H\rangle^{2}}{\langle H\rangle^{2}}\equiv V\sigma^{2}_{H},

(12)

with
V_{a}=a^{3}(2\pi)^{3/2}
and
H

being the Hamiltonian of
S_{V}
,
and RHS of Eq. (
12
)
is the normalized energy fluctuation in the thermodynamical system
S_{V}

[
1
]
.
Here,
V_{a}=a^{3}(2\pi)^{3/2}

is the volume of
S_{a}
.

5
Conclusions

In this work we have derived the expression for the quantum fluctuation of energy for the subsystems of a hot and relativistic gas which beautifully agrees with the thermodynamic fluctuation expression for the large system size.
We also explained and outlined the possible consequences of our results for the description of relativistic heavy-ion systems.

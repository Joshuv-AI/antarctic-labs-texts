Optimal one-sided approximants of circular arc Journal: Computer Aided Geometric Design
Ada Šadl Praprotnik, Aleš Vavpetič, Emil Žagar

Abstract
The optimal one-sided parametric polynomial approximants of a circular arc are considered. More precisely, the approximant must be entirely in or out of the underlying circle of an arc. The natural restriction to an arc’s approximants interpolating boundary points is assumed. However, the study of approximants, which additionally interpolate corresponding tangent directions and curvatures at the boundary of an arc, is also considered. Several low-degree polynomial approximants are studied in detail. When several solutions fulfilling the interpolation conditions exist, the optimal one is characterized, and a numerical algorithm for its construction is suggested. Theoretical results are demonstrated with several numerical examples and a comparison with general (i.e. non-one-sided) approximants are provided.

Introduction

Circular arcs are among the most fundamental
geometric objects used in the design of planar curves. Joining two arcs
together in a biarc is quite a popular procedure
in engineering, specially in robotics (see
[
1
]
and references therein).
Although circular arcs can be considered as canonical objects in a particular computer-based
design system, it is often desirable to work with polynomial objects
only. Since a circular arc does not possess an exact polynomial
representation, this leads to the problem of good
(or the optimal) approximation
of circular arcs by parametric polynomial curves. Several authors
studied this problem in the frame of computer-aided geometric
design (CAGD), from the pioneering paper

[
2
]
to the recent
results on optimal approximation in

[
3
]

and
[
4
]
.
Meanwhile, many authors considered a relaxed problem.
They have either
used the simplified radial distance as a measure of optimality,
or they have fixed some free parameters to make the problem easier to handle
(see
[
5
]
and a
comprehensive list of references therein).

In this paper, we focus on the optimal one-sided approximation, i.e.,
the one where an approximant of a circular arc is entirely in or
entirely out of the underlying circle.
This might be of interest when
an approximant must not cross a circular arc for some
reason (e.g., a design of circular molds). In both cases, the inner
and outer ones will be studied in detail, and
optimal solutions will be provided according to the Hausdorff distance.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review some basic definitions and discuss the number of free parameters for
approximants of different degrees and the order of the geometric continuity. Sections
3
,
4
, and
5
deal with one–parametric optimization problems related the optimal one-sided
G^{0},G^{1}
and
G^{2}
approximants of degree
2,3
and
4
, respectively. In Sections
6
and
7
, we move further to the two parametric problems and construct the optimal one-sided cubic
G^{0}
and quartic
G^{1}
approximants. Some numerical
examples and comparison of the obtained results are presented in Section
8
. The paper concludes with Section
9
where we give some final remarks and suggestions for possible future work.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\boldsymbol{c}:[-\varphi,\varphi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
0<\varphi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, denote the standard parameterization of a canonical
unit circular arc given by

\boldsymbol{c}(\alpha)=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)^{T}
.
Note that any other
arc can be easily translated, rotated and scaled to the canonical one, and these transformations do not affect the optimality.
We are looking for a polynomial
approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
of
\boldsymbol{c}
, where
\boldsymbol{p}:[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a parametric polynomial curve of degree
n
that interpolates two boundary points of
\boldsymbol{c}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{p}(\mp 1)=(\cos\varphi,\mp\sin\varphi)^{T}
. Let

\psi(t)=\left\|\mathbf{p}(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}-1,\quad t\in[-1,1],

where
\left\|.\right\|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm, be the simplified radial
error function. Our goal is to find
\boldsymbol{p}

which minimizes
\left|\psi\right|
over
[-1,1]
. In addition,
we shall require that
\psi
is either non-positive or non-negative,
and refer to the corresponding
\boldsymbol{p}
as an inner or an outer approximant, respectively. Note that the radial error
is given by
\psi_{r}=\sqrt{\psi+1}-1
, and it implies the Hausdorff distance
(see
[
6
]
, e.g.). For the
inner and outer approximation, we also observe that
optimality
of
\psi_{r}
coincides
with optimality of
\psi
, thus it is
enough to consider
\psi
only.

It will turn out convenient to express the polynomial curve

\boldsymbol{p}
in the Bernstein-Bézier form as

\boldsymbol{p}(t)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}^{n}(t)\boldsymbol{b}_{i},\quad t\in[-1,1],

where
\boldsymbol{b}_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,n
, are control points and
B_{i}^{n}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,n
, are reparameterized Bernstein basis polynomials over interval
[-1,1]
, i.e.,

B_{i}^{n}(t)=\binom{n}{i}\left(\frac{1+t}{2}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{n-i}.

The interpolation of boundary points implies that the interpolant

\boldsymbol{p}
is a
G^{0}
approximant. If we additionally require that
also tangent directions are interpolated, then we deal with
G^{1}

approximant, and if we further interpolate the signed curvature,
then the approximant is called a
G^{2}
approximant.

Since a circular arc
\boldsymbol{c}
is symmetric across the abscissa,
the approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
must possess this property too.
This reduces the number of free parameters
in
\boldsymbol{p}
from
2n+2
to
n+1
. Since
\boldsymbol{p}
is a
G^{0}

approximant, the number of free parameters is further reduced to
n-1
.
Since
\psi
depends on these
n-1
free parameters, our goal is
to determine them in a way that the corresponding
\boldsymbol{p}

minimizes

\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|\psi(t)\right|.

(1)

The number of free parameters depends on the degree
n

of the approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
, and on the order of geometric
continuity. The bigger the difference between the degree and
the order is, the harder the optimization of (
1
).
Our discussion will first deal with the cases where the difference
is two, followed by the analysis of cases where it is three.
However, rising the degree
n
also makes the problem much more
difficult to handle, so we shall restrict it to degrees up to four.

In the following analyses,
\cos\varphi
and
\sin\varphi

will often be involved, and we will use the abbreviations

c=\cos\varphi
and
s=\sin\varphi
. Note that
0\leq c<1

and
0<s\leq 1
.

3
Optimal quadratic
G^{0}
approximants

In the quadratic
G^{0}
case control points determining the approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
are given as

\boldsymbol{b}_{0}=(c,-s)^{T}
,

\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(\xi,0)^{T}
, and
\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=(c,s)^{T}
,
where
\xi>0
. The function
\psi
is a quartic polynomial
depending only on the parameter
\xi
and can be expressed as

\displaystyle\psi(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(1-t^{2}\right)\left(2\left(1-t^{2}\right)\xi^{2}+4c\left(1+t^{2}\right)\xi-8+2c^{2}\left(1-t^{2}\right)\right).

We observe that
\psi(\cdot,\xi)
increases on
(0,\infty)

for all
t\in(-1,1)
and
0\leq c<1
.
Thus, the optimal inner approximant is uniquely determined by the maximal
\xi>0

for which
\max_{t\in(-1,1)}\psi(t,\xi)=0
and the
optimal outer approximant is also uniquely given by the minimal
\xi>0

for which
\min_{t\in(-1,1)}\psi(t,\xi)=0
. Let us denote them as
\xi^{\lor}
and
\xi^{\land}
,
respectively.

Since
\psi(\cdot,\xi)
is symmetric on
(-1,1)
, the parameter
\xi^{\lor}
is obtained
as a solution of the equation
\psi(0,\xi^{\lor})=0
, i.e.,
\xi^{\lor}=2-c
and
the parameter
\xi^{\land}
is determined as a solution of the
equation
\psi^{\prime}(1,\xi)=0
, i.e.,
\xi^{\land}=\tfrac{1}{c}
. Note that in the latter case
we get the
G^{1}
approximant.

4
Optimal cubic
G^{1}
approximants

In this case, the control points of an approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
are given as

\boldsymbol{b}_{0}=(c,-s)^{T},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(c,-s)^{T}+\xi(s,c)^{T},\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=(c,s)^{T}+\xi(s,-c)^{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=(c,s)^{T},

where
\xi>0
. Note that the
G^{1}
condition at the boundary points has already
been incorporated. The error function
\psi
is a polynomial of degree
6
, which can
be written as

\displaystyle\psi(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\left(1-t^{2}\right)^{2}\left(9\xi^{2}\left(c^{2}t^{2}+1-c^{2}\right)+12\xi\left(2-t^{2}\right)c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}-4\left(4-t^{2}\right)\left(1-c^{2}\right)\right).

Again,
\psi(t,\cdot)
is an increasing function for all
t\in(-1,1)
and
0\leq c<1
. Since in addition
\psi(\cdot,\xi)
is
a polynomial of degree six with a positive leading coefficient,
the parameter
\xi^{\land}
of the optimal outer approximant must fulfil the condition

\psi(0,\xi^{\land})=0
, and consequently
\xi^{\land}=\tfrac{4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}{3(1+c)}
.

On the other hand, the condition for the approximant being an inner one is
that
t=\pm 1
are triple zeros of
\psi
, i.e.,
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\pm 1,\xi)=0
which implies the solution

\xi^{\lor}=\tfrac{2}{3}\sqrt{1-c^{2}}(\sqrt{3+c^{2}}-c)
.

5
Optimal quartic
G^{2}
approximants

As we will see in the following, the analysis gets
more complicated here as in the previous two cases.
Considering
G^{1}
and
G^{2}
conditions, we can
express control points of an approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
as

\boldsymbol{b}_{0}=(c,-s)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(c,-s)^{T}+\xi(s,c)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=\left(\tfrac{3-4\xi^{2}}{3c},0\right)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=(c,s)^{T}+\xi(s,-c)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{4}=(c,s)^{T},

where again
\xi>0
. The free parameter
\xi
was chosen similarly as in the
previous section. The control point
\boldsymbol{b}_{2}
is not defined for
c=0
,
but this case can be obtained as the limit
c\to 0
.
The function
\psi
is now a polynomial of degree
8
, and
with

\beta_{1,2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}-\sqrt{3\pm 8c+6c^{2}-c^{4}}\right)
,

\beta_{3,4}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}+\sqrt{3\mp 8c+6c^{2}-c^{4}}\right)
,

\alpha_{1,2}=\mp\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\left(\sqrt{3+c^{2}}\pm c\right)
,
it can be written as

\psi(t,\xi)=\frac{(t^{2}-1)^{3}}{64c^{2}}\left(16(\xi-\alpha_{1})^{2}(\xi-\alpha_{2})^{2}t^{2}-16(\xi-\beta_{1})(\xi-\beta_{2})(\xi-\beta_{3})(\xi-\beta_{4})\right),

so its leading coefficient is
\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi))=\tfrac{1}{64c^{2}}16(\xi-\alpha_{1})^{2}(\xi-\alpha_{2})^{2}
.
Unfortunately,
\psi(t,\cdot)
is no longer a monotone function, and a more involved analysis is needed.

Observe first that

\psi(0,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{4c^{2}}(\xi-\beta_{1})(\xi-\beta_{2})(\xi-\beta_{3})(\xi-\beta_{4})

and

\frac{\partial{\psi}}{\partial\xi}(0,\xi)=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}(\xi-\gamma_{1})(\xi-\gamma_{2})(\xi-\gamma_{3})
,
where
\gamma_{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}
and

\gamma_{1,3}=\tfrac{1}{2}(c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\mp\sqrt{3+6c^{2}-c^{4}})
.
Let

\text{td}(\xi):=\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(1,\xi)=\frac{48\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi^{3}}{c}+48\xi^{2}-12\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{c}+c\right)\xi+12\left(1-c^{2}\right).

Then
\text{td}(0)=12(1-c^{2})>0
,
\text{td}^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{12}{c}\sqrt{1-c^{2}}(3+c^{2})<0
,
and
\text{td}(\beta_{3})=12(1-c)^{4}\left((2+c)-\sqrt{1+c}\sqrt{3+c}\right)^{2}>0
.
Furthermore,
\text{td}^{\prime}(\xi)=\tfrac{144}{c}\sqrt{1-c^{2}}(\xi-\delta_{1})(\xi-\delta_{2})
,
where

\delta_{1,2}=\tfrac{1}{6\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}(-2c\mp\sqrt{9-2c^{2}-3c^{4}})
, and

\text{td}(\delta_{2})=-\frac{48\left(1-c^{2}\right)^{3}\left(27-18c^{2}-c^{4}\right)}{c\left(8c\left(27\left(1-c^{2}\right)^{2}+22c^{2}-18c^{4}\right)+4\left(9-2c^{2}-3c^{4}\right)\sqrt{9-2c^{2}-3c^{4}}\right)}<0.

It is easy to see that
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\gamma_{1},\delta_{1}\leq 0

and
\beta_{2}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq\delta_{2}\leq\beta_{3}\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\gamma_{3}\leq\beta_{4}

for all
c
and
\beta_{2}\geq 0
if and only if
c\geq\tfrac{3}{5}
.

5.1
Optimal inner approximant

For
\xi^{\lor}=\beta_{3}
we have
\psi(t,\xi^{\lor})=-\tfrac{1}{4}(1-c)^{4}(2+c-\sqrt{3+4c+c^{2}})^{2}t^{2}(1-t^{2})^{3}
. A parameter
\xi>\beta_{4}
does not induce an inner approximant since
\psi(0,\xi)>0
. Suppose
\beta_{3}<\xi\leq\beta_{4}
. Since
td
is increasing on
[\beta_{3},\beta_{4}]
there is
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\psi(t,\xi)<\psi(t,\xi^{\lor})
for
t\in(1-\varepsilon,1)
. If
\xi
induces a better approximant as
\xi^{\lor}
, then the graphs of
\psi(\cdot,\xi)
and
\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor})
have two intersection on
(0,1)
, therefore ten intersections on
[-1,1]
, which is not possible. If
c<\tfrac{3}{5}
, there are no
\xi\in[0,\xi^{\lor}]
which induce an inner approximant.
Suppose
c\geq\tfrac{3}{5}
and
0\leq\xi\leq\beta_{2}
. Since

\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor}))<\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\beta_{2}))
,
the parameter
\xi^{\lor}
induces a better approximant then
\beta_{2}
. Since
td
is decreasing on
[0,\beta_{2}]
a parameter
\xi<\beta_{2}
induces a worse approximant then
\beta_{2}
. Therefore,
\xi^{\lor}
induces the optimal inner approximant.
In the limit case, when
c=0
, we get
\xi^{\lor}=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
and the abscissa of the control point
\boldsymbol{b}_{2}
is

\lim_{c\to 0}\tfrac{3-4\beta_{3}^{2}}{3c}=\tfrac{2}{3}(4-\sqrt{3})
.

5.2
Optimal outer approximant

Let
\xi^{\land}
be the only zero of
td
on the interval
[\delta_{2},\infty)
. By the above calculations
\xi^{\land}<\beta_{3}
, so
\xi^{\land}
induces an outer approximant. If
\xi>\xi^{\land}
, then
\text{td}(\xi)>0
, hence
\xi
does not induce an outer approximant. Suppose that
0\leq\xi<\xi^{\land}
. If
\text{td}(\xi)<0
, then
\xi
does not induce an outer approximant. Let
\text{td}(\xi)\geq 0
. Since

\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi))>\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\land}))
the graphs of
\psi(\cdot,\xi)
and
\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\land})
intersect on
(1,\infty)
. Hence, the graphs can not have an intersection on the interval
(-1,1)
, so
\xi
induces a worse approximant as
\xi^{\land}
.
In the limit case, when
c=0
, we get
\xi^{\land}=\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
and
the abscissa of the control point
\boldsymbol{b}_{2}
is

\tfrac{8\sqrt{3}}{9}
.

In all previous cases, only one parameter was involved in the
optimization process. Now we move to the two–parametric
optimization.

6
Optimal cubic
G^{0}
approximants

This section deals with cubic one-sided approximants of
\boldsymbol{c}
. The control points determining the approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
are

\boldsymbol{b}_{0}=(c,-s),\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(\xi,-\eta),\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=(\xi,\eta)\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=(c,s),

where
\xi>0
. The function
\psi
is of degree
6
and it is of the form

\psi(t,\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{16}\left(t^{2}-1\right)\left(\left(3\eta-\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\right)^{2}t^{4}+\left(16\left(1-c^{2}\right)-9\left(\eta+\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\right)^{2}+9(\xi-c)^{2}\right)t^{2}+\left(16-\left(3\xi+c\right)^{2}\right)\right),

hence

\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi,\eta))=\frac{1}{16}\left(\sqrt{1-c^{2}}-3\eta\right)^{2},\quad\psi(0,\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{16}\left(3\xi+c\right)^{2}-1,\quad\psi^{\prime}(1,\xi,\eta)=3\left(1-\xi c-\eta\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\right).

(2)

We deal with the two-parametric optimization problem here, and the analysis
is much more involved than in the previous cases. Let us consider the inner and the outer
approximation separately again.

6.1
Optimal inner approximant

Following the idea of equioscillation of
the best polynomial approximant in the functional case, we might expect
equioscillation of
\psi
. Since
it is an inner approximant, it should touch the abscissa from below
as many times as possible. Thus, the guess is that its graph
looks like the one in
Figure

1
(left).
Since
\psi
is of degree
6
and it has two simple zeros at

\pm 1
, the remaining two zeros on
(-1,1)

should be double ones and
related to the maxima of the Chebyshev polynomial of degree six.
Consequently, they equal to
\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
and we must have

\psi\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi,\eta\right)=0,\quad\psi^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{1}{2},\xi,\eta\right)=0.

(3)

It will be shown that the above system has a unique solution with
\xi>c
and
that this solution implies the optimal approximant. Observe that (
3
) is an algebraic system of equations for
\xi
and
\eta
. Two polynomials of a Gröbner basis for the ordering
\eta\prec\xi

are
p_{1}(\cdot)=(\cdot-c)g_{1}(\cdot)
and
g_{2}
, where

\displaystyle g_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=2187\xi^{3}+3807c\xi^{2}+(1953c^{2}-3840)\xi+245c^{3}-4352c,

\displaystyle g_{2}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=-8\left(1-c^{2}\right)\eta+\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\left(27\xi^{2}+10c\xi+3c^{2}-40\right).

The zero
\xi=c
of
p_{1}
is not admissible, since the corresponding
polynomial approximant reduces to the line segment, which is not the
optimal one. Thus, we are restricted to the positive zeros of
g_{1}
. Let

I=(\xi_{min},\xi_{max})
, where

\xi_{min}=\frac{1}{27}\left(-5c+8\sqrt{2}\sqrt{9-c^{2}}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\xi_{max}=\frac{4-c}{3}.

Note that
c<1<\xi_{min}<\xi_{max}
for all
c\in[0,1)
.

Lemma 1
.

The only positive zero of
g_{1}
is in
I
.

Proof.

We have

g_{1}\left(-\tfrac{37}{27}c\right)=\tfrac{8192}{9}c(1-c^{2})\geq 0
,

g_{1}(0)=c(245c^{2}-4352)\leq 0
,

g_{1}(\xi_{min})=-\frac{2048(1-c^{2})^{2}}{4c+\sqrt{2}\sqrt{9-c^{2}}}<0
,
and
g_{1}(\xi_{max})=64(1-c)^{3}>0
.
Since the leading coefficient of
g_{1}
is positive,

g_{1}
has two non-positive zeros and the unique positive one
in
I
. This completes the proof.
∎

Since
g_{2}(\cdot,\eta)
is a linear polynomial, the unique solution of
the system (
3
) is
(\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})
,
where
\xi^{\lor}
is the unique zero of
g_{1}
in
I
, and

\eta^{\lor}=\frac{27{\xi^{\lor}}^{2}+10c\xi^{\lor}+3c^{2}-40}{8\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}.

(4)

Let
J=(\eta_{min},\eta_{max}
), where

\eta_{min}=\frac{\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}{3}\quad{\rm and}\quad\eta_{max}=\frac{3-c}{1+c}\frac{\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}{3}.

Lemma 2
.

If
(\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})
is the solution of the system (
3
),
then
\eta^{\lor}\in J
.

Proof.

Let
f(\xi)=\frac{27{\xi}^{2}+10c\xi+3c^{2}-40}{8\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}
.
Then

f^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{27\xi+5c}{4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}

and
f
is strictly increasing on
I
. Thus

f(\xi_{min})=\frac{\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}{3}<f(\xi)<\frac{3-c}{1+c}\frac{\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}{3}=f(\xi_{max})
.
Since
\xi^{\lor}\in I
and

\eta^{\lor}=f(\xi^{\lor})
by (
4
),
the proof is complete.
∎

We shall see in the following that
(\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})

induces the polynomial approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
with

(\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\xi,\eta)\end{subarray}}{\rm argmin}\left(\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|\psi(t,\xi,\eta)\right|\right),

i.e., the optimal one.

Let us suppose that
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
induces a better
approximant. If
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu<\xi^{\lor}
, then by
(
2
) we have

\psi(0,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\leq\psi(0,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})<0
.
Thus
\left|\psi(0,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\right|\geq\left|\psi(0,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})\right|
, and

\psi(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
does not provide a smaller error than

\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})
.

If
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\geq\xi^{\lor}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu>\eta^{\lor}
, then by (
2
)

\psi^{\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)<\psi^{\prime}(1,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})\quad{\rm and}\quad\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu))>\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})).

Consequently,
\psi(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
and

\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})
must intersect six times in
[-1,1]

(see

6.2
Optimal outer approximant

We will show that the optimal outer approximant is
G^{2}
approximant. From the equations
\psi(0,\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=0
and
\psi^{\prime}(1,\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=0
we get
\xi^{\land}=\tfrac{4-c}{3}
and
\eta^{\land}=\tfrac{3-4c+c^{2}}{3\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}
. Suppose that a pair
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
induces a better outer approximant. From
\psi(0,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\geq\psi(0,\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=0
we get
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\geq\xi^{\land}
. From

1-\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-c\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\tfrac{1}{3}\psi^{\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\leq\tfrac{1}{3}\psi^{\prime}(1,\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=1-\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\eta^{\land}-c\xi^{\land}=0

we get
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\geq\tfrac{1}{c}\left(\sqrt{1-c^{2}}(\eta^{\land}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+c\xi^{\land}\right)
. From

\tfrac{1}{108}\left((3\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}+27(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+c)^{2}-108\right)=\psi(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\\
\leq\max\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=\psi(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3},\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})=\tfrac{1}{108}\left((3\eta^{\land}+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}+27(\xi^{\land}+c)^{2}-108\right)

we get
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq\eta^{\land}
. From the last two inequalities we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(3\eta^{\land}+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}+27(\xi^{\land}+c)^{2}-(3\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}-27(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+c)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(3\eta^{\land}+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}+27(\xi^{\land}+c)^{2}-(3\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+5\sqrt{1-c^{2}})^{2}-27(\tfrac{1}{c}\left(\sqrt{1-c^{2}}(\eta^{\land}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+c\xi^{\land}\right)+c)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{3}{c^{2}}\left(\eta^{\land}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\right)\left((9-6c^{2})(\eta^{\land}-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+2c^{2}(4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}-3\eta^{\land})+18c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi^{\land}\right)\geq 0.

The last inequality follows since
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq\eta^{\land}\leq\tfrac{4}{3}\sqrt{1-c^{2}}
. Hence all inequalites are equalities, so
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=(\xi^{\land},\eta^{\land})
.

7
Optimal quartic
G^{1}
approximants

In the
G^{1}
quartic case the control points of an approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
are given as

\boldsymbol{b}_{0}=(c,-s)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{1}=(c,-s)^{T}+\xi(s,c)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{2}=\left(\eta,0\right)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{3}=(c,s)^{T}+\xi(s,-c)^{T},\boldsymbol{b}_{4}=(c,s)^{T},

where
\xi>0
due to the
G^{1}
continuity.
The function
\psi
is again a polynomial of degree
8
and can be written as

\psi(t,\xi,\eta)=(t^{2}-1)^{2}\left(a_{2}(\xi,\eta)t^{4}+a_{1}(\xi,\eta)t^{2}+a_{0}(\xi,\eta)\right),

where

\displaystyle a_{2}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi,\eta))=\frac{1}{64}\left(3\eta-4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi-3c\right)^{2},

\displaystyle a_{1}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{64}\left(32c^{2}\xi^{2}+32\xi^{2}-64c\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi-18\eta^{2}+36c\eta-34c^{2}+16\right),

\displaystyle a_{0}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\psi(0,\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{64}\left((3\eta+4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi+5c)^{2}-64\right),

and its second derivative is
\psi^{\prime\prime}(1,\xi,\eta)=8\xi^{2}+6c\eta-6
.

7.1
Optimal inner approximant

It will turn out that the optimal inner quartic

approximant is
actually the optimal
G^{2}
approximant from

Section

5.1
. Recall that, in this case, we have

\psi(0,\xi,\eta)=0

\psi^{\prime\prime}(1,\xi,\eta)=0
,
and the appropriate solution which induces the best approximant

\xi^{\lor}=\frac{c\left(1-c^{2}\right)+(1-c)^{2}\sqrt{3+4c+c^{2}}}{2\sqrt{1-c^{2}}},\quad\eta^{\lor}=\frac{1}{3}\left(8-7c+2c^{3}-2(1-c)^{2}\sqrt{3+4c+c^{2}}\right),

with the error
\frac{1}{1024}\left(27(1-c)^{4}\left((2+c)-\sqrt{3+4c+c^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)\leq\frac{27\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)^{2}}{1024}

Suppose that a pair
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\neq(\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})

G^{2}
approximant. First, we show
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\geq 0
.

Suppose that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu<0
. Since
\psi(0,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\geq\min_{t\in[-1,1]}\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})=:m
, we have
3\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+5c<-8\sqrt{1+m}<-2
. Then

\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu))=\frac{1}{64}\left(-3\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+3c\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{64}\left(8\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+8c+2\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{64}\left(8c+2\right)^{2}\\
>\frac{1}{4}(1-c)^{4}\left(7+8c+2c^{2}-2(2+c)\sqrt{3+4c+c^{2}}\right)=\text{lc}(\psi(\cdot,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})).

Together with the inequality

\psi(t,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\xi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\eta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)<\psi(t,\xi^{\lor},\eta^{\lor})<0

7.2
Optimal outer approximant

To simplify the coefficients of the polynomial
\psi
,
we introduce two new variables

u=3\eta-4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi,\quad v=3\eta+4\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\xi

which implies

\xi=\tfrac{v-u}{8\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}
and
\eta=\tfrac{1}{6}(u+v)
.
Now
\psi
can be written as
\psi_{uv}(t,u,v)=\psi(t,\xi(u,v),\eta(u,v))
and
we get

\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u,v))=\frac{1}{64}\left(u-3c\right)^{2},\quad\psi_{uv}(0,u,v)=\frac{1}{64}\left((v+5c)^{2}-64\right),\quad\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u,v)=\frac{(v-u)^{2}}{8(1-c^{2})}+c(v+u)-6.

(8)

Observe that
\xi>0
due to the
G^{1}
interpolation condition
and
\psi_{uv}(0,u,v)\geq 0
since the approximant is the outer one. This implies

v>u\quad{\rm and}\quad\left|v+5c\right|\geq 8.

(9)

Let us first assume that
v\geq 8-5c
.

It is clear that
\psi_{uv}
is symmetric and has two double zeros at
\pm 1
.
Following the idea of equioscillation of the best polynomial approximant in the functional
case, we might expect that
\psi_{uv}
equioscillates. Since it is an outer
approximant, it may touch the abscissa several times. Thus, the guess is that its
graph looks like on
Figure

1
(right). But then its four double
zeros on
[-1,1]
must be related to the minima of the Chebyshev polynomial of degree eight.
Consequently, the two double zeros on
(-1,1)
are equal to
1-\sqrt{2}
and

\sqrt{2}-1
. Since these points are also minima of
\psi_{uv}
, we must have

\psi_{uv}\left(\sqrt{2}-1,u,v\right)=0,\quad\psi_{uv}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{2}-1,u,v\right)=0.\quad

(10)

This leads to the system of two algebraic equations for
u
and
v
.
Its Gröbner basis according to the ordering
v\prec u

are polynomials
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, given as

p_{1}(u)=\frac{1}{1-c^{2}}\left(\alpha_{4}u^{4}+\alpha_{3}u^{3}+\alpha_{2}u^{2}+\alpha_{1}u+\alpha_{0}\right),

(11)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{4}

\displaystyle=c^{2}(c^{2}+1),

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}

\displaystyle=-8(3+\sqrt{2})c^{5}+4(4+5\sqrt{2})c^{3}+4(4+3\sqrt{2})c,

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=2(125+68\sqrt{2})c^{6}-2(229+182\sqrt{2})c^{4}+4(140+97\sqrt{2})c^{2}-4(65+46\sqrt{2}),

(12)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=-88(14+9\sqrt{2})c^{7}+12(116+89\sqrt{2})c^{5}-84(16+11\sqrt{2})c^{3}+4(50+36\sqrt{2})c,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=5(457+312\sqrt{2})c^{8}-(1655+1236\sqrt{2})c^{6}+12(16+9\sqrt{2})c^{4}+4(163+114\sqrt{2})c^{2}+272+192\sqrt{2},

and

p_{2}(u,v)=\frac{1}{c^{2}-1}\left(\beta_{1}v+\beta_{0}(u)\right),

where

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=-2\left(4c^{8}+4\left(4+\sqrt{2}\right)c^{6}-\left(1+6\sqrt{2}\right)c^{4}+2c^{2}+3+2\sqrt{2}\right),

\displaystyle\beta_{0}(u)

\displaystyle=36\left(3-2\sqrt{2}\right)c^{9}+16\left(3\sqrt{2}-5\right)c^{8}u+\left(329-198\sqrt{2}+\left(12-8\sqrt{2}\right)u^{2}\right)c^{7}+\left(142\sqrt{2}-253\right)c^{6}u

\displaystyle+\left(169-198\sqrt{2}+\left(55-34\sqrt{2}\right)u^{2}\right)c^{5}+\left(174\sqrt{2}-51+\left(2\sqrt{2}-3\right)u^{2}\right)c^{4}u+\left(4\left(43+9\sqrt{2}\right)-5\left(6\sqrt{2}-7\right)u^{2}\right)c^{3}

\displaystyle-\left(16\left(7+3\sqrt{2}\right)u+\left(3-2\sqrt{2}\right)u^{3}\right)c^{2}+2\left(23+16\sqrt{2}\right)u-4c\left(10+9\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2}u^{2}\right).

Let us define the domain of interest for
(u,v)
as

{\mathcal{D}}=(3c,\tilde{u})\times[8-5c,\infty),

where

\tilde{u}=kc^{2}-(2k-3)c+k,\quad k=2\sqrt{\frac{2\sqrt{2}-1}{7}}.

Note that
{\mathcal{D}}
is a subset of (
9
).
We shall prove in the following that there exists a unique
solution
(u^{\land},v^{\land})\in{\mathcal{D}}
of (
10
)
with
u^{\land}
the unique zero of
p_{1}
on
I=(3c,\tilde{u})

and

v^{\land}=-\tfrac{\beta_{0}(u^{\land})}{\beta_{1}}\geq 8-5c
.
Moreover, this solution is
optimal
, i.e., it induces
the polynomial approximant
\boldsymbol{p}
with

\psi_{u^{\land}v^{\land}}
for which

(u^{\land},v^{\land})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v)\end{subarray}}{\rm argmin}\left(\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|\psi_{uv}(t,u,v)\right|\right).

Let us first confirm the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

The polynomial
p_{1}
has precisely one zero
u^{\land}

on
I
, and the system (
10
)
has precisely one solution
(u^{\land},v^{\land})
in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

By (
11
) and (
12
)
the leading coefficient of
p_{1}
is

\tfrac{c^{4}+c^{2}}{1-c^{2}}
, thus

\lim_{u\to\pm\infty}p_{1}(u)=\infty
.
A computer algebra system reveals that

(1+c)p_{1}(-6\sqrt{2}-5)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{7}a_{i}c^{i}(1-c)^{7-i}
,

(1-c^{2})p_{1}(0)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{4}b_{i}c^{2i}(1-c^{2})^{4-i}
,

p_{1}(3c)=16\left(17+12\sqrt{2}\right)\left(1-c^{2}\right)^{3}
, and

49(1+c)p_{1}(\tilde{u})=16c(1-c)^{3}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{7}d_{i}c^{i}(1-c)^{7-i}
,
where
a_{i}<0
,
i=0,1,\dots,7
,
b_{i}>0
,
i=0,1,\dots,4
, and

d_{i}<0
,
i=0,1,\dots,7
. Thus

p_{1}(-6\sqrt{2}-5)<0
,
p_{1}(0)>0
,
p_{1}(3c)>0
, and

p_{1}(\tilde{u})<0
and the result of the lemma follows
since
p_{1}
is quartic.
∎

Let us confirm that the solution
(u^{\land},v^{\land})
induces the optimal
approximant. If not, then there exists a pair

(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\in{\mathcal{D}}
,
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\neq(u^{\land},v^{\land})
,
for which

\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|\psi_{uv}(t,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\right|<\max_{t\in[-1,1]}\left|\psi_{uv}(t,u^{\land},v^{\land})\right|.

If
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu>v^{\land}
, then

\psi_{uv}(0,\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)>\psi_{uv}(0,\cdot,v^{\land})
since
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu>8-5c

by (
9
), and
(u,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
can not induce better approximant than

(u^{\land},v^{\land})
. Thus
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq v^{\land}
.
Consider first the case
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu>u^{\land}
. By
5
,
u^{\land}>3c
and
(
8
) implies

\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(t,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu))>\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(t,u^{\land},v^{\land}))
.
If
\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land})>\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, then the graphs of
\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u^{\land},v^{\land})
and

\psi_{uv}(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
intersect on
(1,\infty)
. Moreover, if
(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
induces a better approximant as

(u^{\land},v^{\land})
, then the graphs of
\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u^{\land},v^{\land})
and

\psi_{uv}(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
would have to intersect at least eight times on
[-1,1]
,
which is not possible since

\psi_{uv}(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)

is a polynomial of degree
at most
8
(see
Figure

2
(right)).
Similarly, if
\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land})\leq\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, the above-mentioned graphs would have to
intersect at least ten times on
[-1,1]
which is again a contradiction.
Finally, let us consider the case
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq v^{\land}
and

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq u^{\land}
.
If
\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land})\leq\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, then the graphs of
\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u^{\land},v^{\land})
and

\psi_{uv}(\cdot,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
would have to intersect at least ten times on
[-1,1]
which is again an obvious contradiction.
So, we are left with the case

\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land})>\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
. The following lemma reveals
that, in this case, the approximant is not an outer one.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq v^{\land}
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\leq u^{\land}
. If
\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land})>\psi_{uv}^{\prime\prime}(1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, then

\psi_{uv}(\sqrt{2}-1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)<0
.

Proof.

Let us define

f(u,v)=\psi^{\prime\prime}_{uv}(1,u,v)-\psi^{\prime\prime}_{uv}(1,u^{\land},v^{\land}),\quad g(u,v)=\psi_{uv}(\sqrt{2}-1,u,v).

(13)

Observe that
f(u,v)=0
is a parabola and
g(u,v)=0
is an ellipse (see
Figure

3
, right)
with leading coefficients of their tangent lines at
(u,v)
equal to

\displaystyle c_{p}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{-4c^{3}+4c+u-v}{4c^{3}-4c+u-v},

\displaystyle c_{e}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{(12\sqrt{2}-17)u+2c(15-11\sqrt{2}+(11\sqrt{2}-15)c^{2}+(7-5\sqrt{2})cu)+(3-2\sqrt{2})v}{(2\sqrt{2}-3)u+v+2c(1+\sqrt{2}-c((1+\sqrt{2})c+(\sqrt{2}-1)v))},

respectively. With the help of a computer algebra system, it can be again shown that

c_{p}(u,v),c_{e}(u,v)>0
and
c_{p}(u,v)>c_{e}(u,v)

for
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Thus, by (
9
) and
5
, all these inequalities
hold for
u=u^{\land}
and
v=v^{\land}
.
The parabola and the ellipse intersect at
(u^{\land},v^{\land})
, and

f(0,0)=-\tfrac{1}{8(1-c^{2})}(v^{\land}-u^{\land})^{2}-c(v^{\land}+u^{\land})<0
,

g(0,0)=-\tfrac{16\sqrt{2}-20+(68\sqrt{2}-97)c^{2}}{4}<0
.
Since, in addition, the ordinate of the intersection of the tangent line to ellipse with
v
-axis is positive (which can be again
checked by computer algebra system), and the parabola is
by (
8
) and (
13
)
symmetric about the line
v=u
, the parabola and ellipse are concave at
(u^{\land},v^{\land})
. This implies

\psi_{uv}(\sqrt{2}-1,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muu\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muv\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)<0
and the result of the lemma is confirmed.
∎

Finally, consider
v\leq-8-5c
. By (
9
) we have
u<v
, therefore

u<-8-5c
, and
\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u,v))=\tfrac{1}{64}(u-3c)^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{64}(-8-5c-3c)^{2}=(1+c)^{2}
.
From the above calculation we have
\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u^{\land},v^{\land}))\leq\tfrac{1}{64}(\tilde{u}-3c)^{2}=\frac{1}{112}\left(2\sqrt{2}-1\right)(1-c)^{4}\leq\text{lc}(\psi_{uv}(\cdot,u,v))
. Hence, if the pair
(u,v)
induces a better outer approximant than the pair
(u^{\land},v^{\land})
, then the error graphs of induced approximants have at least ten intersections, which is impossible.
We can now finally formulate the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 7
.

The optimal quartic
G^{1}
outer approximant is given by the unique solution of
the nonlinear algebraic system (
10
) on
the domain
{\mathcal{D}}
.

9
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented several optimal one-sided approximants of a circular arc given by an inner angle
\varphi\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
. Detailed analyses of parabolic

G^{0}
, cubic
G^{0}
,
G^{1}
,
G^{2}
, and
quartic
G^{1}
,
G^{2}
cases have been considered.
Optimal inner and outer approximants are given
explicitly as a solution of a (at most) quartic algebraic
equation. The uniqueness of the optimal solution was
confirmed in all cases.

The problem of constructing the optimal inner and outer
approximants gets increasingly complicated as the gap
between the order of geometric continuity and the degree of
the polynomial is growing. Thus, the topics for future work include the construction of higher-degree one-sided approximants with low-order geometric approximation. The first such case
might be the quartic
G^{0}
approximation.

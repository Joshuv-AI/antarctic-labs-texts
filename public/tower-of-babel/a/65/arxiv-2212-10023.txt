Abstract
In this paper, we study L p L^{p} -boundedness ( 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 ) of the covariant Riesz transform on differential forms for a class of non-compact weighted Riemannian manifolds without assuming conditions on derivatives of curvature. We present in particular a local version of L p L^{p} -boundedness of Riesz transforms under two natural conditions, namely the curvature-dimension condition, and a lower bound on the Weitzenböck curvature endomorphism. As an application, the Calderón-Zygmund inequality for 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 on weighted manifolds is derived under the curvature-dimension condition as hypothesis.

2
Heat kernel estimates

2.1
Preliminaries

Let us first recall the interior product.

Definition 2.1
.

The interior product
X\mathop{\lrcorner}a\in\Omega^{k-1}
corresponds to the contraction of
a\in\Omega^{k}
with a vector field
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and is defined as

X\mathop{\lrcorner}a\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k-1}\right):=a\left(X,X_{1},\ldots,X_{k-1}\right),\quad\forall X_{1},\ldots,X_{k-1}\in\Gamma(TM).

The interior product is an anti-derivation, i.e.,

X\mathop{\lrcorner}\,(a\wedge b)=(X\lrcorner\,a)\wedge b+(-1)^{k}a\wedge(X\mathop{\lrcorner}b)\quad\forall a\in\Omega^{k},\ b\in\Omega^{1}.

The Weitzenböck formula relates the weighted Hodge-de Rham Laplacian to the weighted Bochner Laplacian on
(M,g)
.

Theorem 2.2

(Weitzenböck formula)
.

For all differential
k
-forms
\eta\in\Omega^{k}
, we have

\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\eta=\square\eta-\nabla_{\nabla h}\eta+\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta)-({\rm Hess}\,h)^{(k)}(\eta),

where
\mathscr{R}^{(k)}:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\Omega^{k}
is given by

\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\theta^{j}\wedge\left(e_{i}\mathop{\lrcorner}\mathrm{R}\big(e_{j},e_{i}\big)(\eta)\right)

and
(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits h)^{(k)}:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\Omega^{k}
by

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits h)^{(k)}(\eta)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}e_{i}(e_{j}(h))\theta^{j}\wedge\left(e_{i}\mathop{\lrcorner}\eta\right),

for any orthonormal frame
\left(e_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq m}
and corresponding dual frame
(\theta^{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}
such that
\nabla e_{i}=0
,
\nabla\theta^{j}=0
and
\theta^{j}(e_{i})=\delta_{i}^{j}
.

Proof.

It is well known that

\displaystyle{\rm d}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta^{j}\wedge\nabla_{e_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{\mu}^{(k)}(\cdot)=-\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\rm e}^{-h}e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}({\rm e}^{h}(\cdot)).

Let
\Delta^{(k)}
be the usual Hodge Laplacian acting on
k
-form. Since orthonormal frames
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
and dual frames
\left(\theta^{j}\right)_{1\leq j\leq m}
satisfy
\nabla e_{i}=0
and
\nabla\theta^{j}=0
, we obtain for
\eta\in\Omega^{k}
, using the summation convention,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\eta

\displaystyle=-{\rm e}^{-h}e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}({\rm e}^{h}(\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta))-\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}({\rm e}^{-h}e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}({\rm e}^{h}\eta))

\displaystyle=-e_{j}(h)e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}(\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta)-e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}(\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta)

\displaystyle\quad-\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}(e_{j}(h)e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\eta)-\theta^{j}\wedge\nabla_{e_{j}}(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}\eta)

\displaystyle=-e_{j}(h)e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}(\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta)-e_{i}(e_{j}(h))\theta^{i}\wedge(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{j}}\eta)-e_{j}(h)\theta^{i}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\eta)+\Delta^{(k)}\eta

\displaystyle=-e_{j}(h)\nabla_{e_{j}}\eta+e_{j}(h)(\theta^{i}\wedge(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta))-e_{i}(e_{j}(h))\theta^{i}\wedge(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\eta)-e_{j}(h)(\theta^{i}\wedge(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\nabla_{e_{i}}\eta))

\displaystyle\quad+\square\eta-\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta)

\displaystyle=-e_{j}(h)\nabla_{e_{j}}\eta-e_{i}(e_{j}(h))\theta^{i}\wedge(e_{j}\mathop{\lrcorner}\eta)+\square\eta-\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\nabla h}\eta-({\rm Hess}\,h)^{(k)}(\eta)+\square\eta-\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta),

where the last equation follows from the fact that

\Delta^{(k)}\eta=\square\eta-\mathscr{R}^{(k)}(\eta).\qed

By the usual abuse of notation, the corresponding self-adjoint realizations will again
be denoted by the same symbol, i.e.
\Delta_{\mu}
and
\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu}

respectively. By local parabolic regularity, for all square-integrable
k
-forms
a\in\Omega^{k}_{L^{2}}
, the time-dependent
k
-form

(0,\infty)\times M\ni(t,x)\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}t}a\in\Lambda^{k}T_{x}^{*}M

has a smooth representative which extends smoothly to
[0,\infty)\times M
if
a
is smooth.
In addition, there exists a unique smooth heat kernel of
\mathrm{e}^{-\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}t}
with respect to the measure
\mu
, which is understood as a map

\displaystyle(0,\infty)\times M\times M\ni(t,x,y)\mapsto{\rm e}^{-\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}t}(x,y)\in{\rm Hom}(\Lambda^{k}T^{*}_{y}M,\Lambda^{k}T^{*}_{x}M)

such that

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}}a(x)=\int_{M}{\rm e}^{-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}}(x,y)a(y)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y).

Let

-K:=\min\left\{\big((\mathscr{R}-\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits h)^{(k)}v,v\big)_{g}\colon v\in\Lambda^{k}T_{x}M,\ |v|=1,\ x\in M\right\}.

Recall the notation
\lambda_{k}(x)
defined as the smallest eigenvalue of
(\mathscr{R}-\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits h)^{(k)}(x),x\in M
and let

V_{k}(x)=\lambda_{k}^{-}(x)=(|\lambda_{k}(x)|-\lambda_{k}(x))/2.

Then by
[
22
]
,

\big|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\big|\leq p^{V_{k}}_{t}(x,y).

We conclude that to estimate
\big|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\big|
,
it suffices to estimate the Schrödinger heat kernel
p^{V_{k}}_{t}(x,y)
. There is
a lot of previous work dealing with
Schrödinger heat kernels on manifolds, see for instance
[
14
,
19
,
28
,
31
,
32
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
M
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold satisfying (
LD
) and (
UE
). Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1/4)
, there exists
\tilde{\sigma}>0
depending only on the constants in (
LD
) and (
UE
) and a constant
C>0
such that

\big|\exp{(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)})}(x,y)\big|\leq\frac{C{\rm e}^{\tilde{\sigma}t}}{\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}\exp\left(-\alpha\rho(x,y)^{2}/t\right),\quad\forall x,y\in M,\,t>0.

If
\sigma_{1}=0
and
\sigma_{2}=0
, then
\tilde{\sigma}=0
.

Proof.

Let
P_{t}^{V_{k}}
be the semigroup generated by the operator
-\Delta_{\mu}+V_{k}
and
p_{t}^{V_{k}}(x,y)
the corresponding heat kernel.
We recall that

\displaystyle\left|\exp{(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)})}(x,y)\right|\leq p_{t}^{V_{k}}(x,y),\quad x,y\in M,\ t>0.

From the assumptions (
LD
) and (
UE
), one can derive that
p_{t}^{V_{k}}
satisfies
the Gaussian estimate:

\displaystyle p^{V_{k}}_{t}(x,y)\leq\frac{C{\rm e}^{\tilde{\sigma}t}}{\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}\exp\left(-\alpha\rho(x,y)^{2}/t\right)

for
x,y\in M
and
t>0
, by the same argument as in
[
17
]
; see
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
for a similar argument.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If the local volume doubling property (
LD
) holds, then for any
\gamma>0
, there exist positive constants
C_{\gamma}
and
\tilde{c}:=\sigma_{1}^{2}/2\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq\sqrt{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\leq C_{\gamma}\,\mu\big(B(y,\sqrt{s})\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma t/s}\mathrm{e}^{\tilde{c}s}

(2.1)

for
s,t>0
and
x,y\in M
.

Proof.

By (
1.5
), it is easy to see that for all
\gamma>0
,

s,t>0
and
y\in M
, there exist two positive constants

C_{\gamma}
(depending on
\gamma
and the constants in
(
1.5
)) and
\tilde{c}=\sigma_{1}^{2}/2\gamma
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\rho(x,y)\geq\sqrt{t}}{\rm e}^{-2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\int_{M}{\rm e}^{-\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{s}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu(B(y,(i+1)\sqrt{s})){\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\mu(B(y,\!\sqrt{s}))\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{m+1}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}{\rm e}^{\sigma_{1}i\sqrt{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}\mu(B(y,\!\sqrt{s}))\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{m+1}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}}{\rm e}^{\gamma i^{2}/2+\sigma_{1}^{2}s/(2\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}{\rm e}^{\sigma_{1}^{2}s/(2\gamma)}\mu(B(y,\!\sqrt{s}))\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(i+1)^{m+1}{\rm e}^{-\gamma i^{2}/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\gamma}\mu(B(y,\!\sqrt{s}))\,{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/s}{\rm e}^{\tilde{c}s},

(2.2)

where the third inequality comes from condition (
1.5
).
∎

By means of this estimate, we obtain immediately the following consequence.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
M
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold satisfying (
LD
) and (
UE
). Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1/4)
and
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
, there exists some constant
C>0
such that

\int_{M}\left|\exp{(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)})}(x,y)\right|^{2}{\rm e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\leq\frac{C{\rm e}^{2C_{0}t}}{\mu\big(B(y,\sqrt{t})\big)},

for all
y\in M
and
t>0
, where
C_{0}:=\tilde{\sigma}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{c}
and the constants
\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{c}
defined in Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
respectively.

Proof.

Letting
t
tend to
\infty
in inequality (
2.1
), we obtain

\int_{M}\mathrm{e}^{-2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\leq C_{\gamma}\,\mu\Big(B\big(y,\sqrt{t}\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{e}^{\tilde{c}t},\quad t>0.

By Theorem
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that there exists
\tilde{\sigma}>0
depending only on the constants

\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
and a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\exp{(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)})}(x,y)\right|^{2}{\rm e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{{\rm e}^{2\tilde{\sigma}t}}{\mu\big(B(y,\sqrt{t})\big)^{2}}\int_{M}{\rm e}^{\frac{-(2\alpha-2\gamma)\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{{\rm e}^{(2\tilde{\sigma}+\tilde{c})t}}{\mu(B\big(y,\sqrt{t})\big)}.

We then complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
M
be a complete non-compact Riemannian manifold satisfying the assumptions as in Theorem
1.2
. Fix
\alpha\in\left(0,{1}/{4}\right)

. Then for any

for all
y\in M
,
t>0
, where the constant
C_{0}
is defined as in Theorem
2.5

Proof.

For
R>0
, let
\psi
be a
C^{2}
function on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\psi(r)=1
for

for

for some positive constant

(see
[
24
]
). An argument of Calabi, which is also used in

[
9
]

\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(\cdot,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(\cdot,y))
for

According to the integration by parts formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\left(\psi(\rho(x,y))4\gamma\frac{\rho(x,y)}{t}+\psi^{\prime}(\rho(x,y))\right)\left(\nabla_{\nabla\rho}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\psi(\rho(x,y))\left(\nabla^{*}_{\mu}\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=:{\rm I}+{\rm II}.

Then there exists
\alpha>\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle{\rm I}=\int_{M}4\gamma\frac{\rho(x,y)}{t}\left(\nabla_{\nabla\rho}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\psi^{\prime}(\rho(x,y))\left(\nabla_{\nabla\rho}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}4\gamma\frac{\rho(x,y)}{t}\left(\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\left({\rm d}\rho\otimes\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{R}\int_{M}\left|\left(\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\left({\rm d}\rho\otimes\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\right|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))^{1/2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|\cdot\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma^{\prime}\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{R}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|\cdot\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))^{1/2}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\left(\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\left(4\gamma^{\prime}-2\gamma\right)\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{R}\left(\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\left(\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-A}{2}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C^{2}}{(1-A)t}\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\left(4\gamma^{\prime}-2\gamma\right)\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c^{2}}{(1-A)R^{2}}\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

where

\displaystyle\int_{M}4\gamma\frac{\rho(x,y)}{t}\left(\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\,\left(\mathrm{d}\rho\otimes\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-A}{2}\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)+\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right)\frac{C{\rm e}^{2C_{0}t}}{\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}

for some generic constant

. As

\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}=\Box-\nabla_{\nabla h}+\mathscr{R}^{(k)}-(\operatorname{Hess}\,h)^{(k)},

and

, we then have

\displaystyle{\rm II}=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\nabla^{*}_{\mu}\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\left(\left(\mathscr{R}^{(k)}-(\mathrm{Hess}\,h)^{(k)}\right)\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{2\gamma\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+\int_{M}(V_{k}(x)-\sigma_{3})\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{3}\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+A\int_{M}\left|{\rm d}|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)|\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{3}\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Using Kato’s inequality we further obtain

\displaystyle{\rm II}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+A\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{3}\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

By Cauchy’s integral formula, we get for
w\in\mathscr{B}(M)
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\Omega_{L^{2}}^{k}

\displaystyle\Big\langle{\Delta}^{(k)}_{\mu}\mathrm{e}^{-t{\Delta}_{\mu}^{(k)}}a_{1},\,wa_{2}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{z:\,|z-t|=t/2}\frac{\Big\langle\mathrm{e}^{-z{\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}}}a_{1},wa_{2}\Big\rangle}{(z-t)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigg|\leq(2\pi)^{-1}\pi t\sup_{z\,:|z-t|=t/2}\Bigg|\frac{\Big\langle\mathrm{e}^{-z{\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}}}a_{1},wa_{2}\Big\rangle}{(z-t)^{2}}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t}{2}\sup_{z:\,|z-t|=t/2}\left\|\,|\mathrm{e}^{-z{\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}}}a_{1}|\,\sqrt{w}\,\right\|_{2}\left\|\,|a_{2}|\,\sqrt{w}\,\right\|_{2}(t/2)^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{t}\left\|\,|a_{1}|\,\sqrt{w}\,\right\|_{2}\left\|\,|a_{2}|\,\sqrt{w}\,\right\|_{2},

which implies for

\Big|\big\langle\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\mathrm{e}^{-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}/2}a_{1},a_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}({\kern 0.8pt\cdot\kern 0.8pt},y)}{t}}\big\rangle\Big|\leq\frac{C}{t}\Big\|a_{1}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\gamma\rho^{2}({\kern 0.8pt\cdot\kern 0.8pt},y)}{t}}\Big\|_{2}\Big\|a_{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\gamma\rho^{2}({\kern 0.8pt\cdot\kern 0.8pt},y)}{t}}\Big\|_{2}.

and

, we then obtain by Theorem
2.5
,

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{M}\left(\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y),\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right)_{g}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{t}\left(\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}/2\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{M}\left|\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{t}\left(\frac{C_{1}{\rm e}^{C_{0}t}}{\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t/2}))}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{C_{2}{\rm e}^{2C_{0}t}}{\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C{\rm e}^{3/2C_{0}t}}{t\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{B(y,R)}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\left|\nabla\exp\left(-t\Delta_{\mu}^{(k)}\right)(x,y)\right|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{2\gamma\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t}}\psi(\rho(x,y))\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+\sigma_{3}t){\rm e}^{2C_{0}t}}{t\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}+\frac{C{\rm e}^{2C_{0}t}}{R^{2}\mu(B(y,\sqrt{t}))}.

We then complete the proof by letting

tend to
\infty
.∎

The object of our interest is for suitable

Lemma 3.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
suppose
\Delta_{\mu}-V_{k}+\sigma

for some constant

\nabla\left({\Delta}^{(k)}_{\mu}+\sigma\right)^{-1/2}

Proof.

We use the Bochner formula for

(

\Delta_{\mu}-V_{k}+\sigma

\displaystyle\|\nabla a\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\square_{\mu}a,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu}a,a\rangle-\langle(\mathscr{R}^{(k)}-(\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits h)^{(k)})a,a\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\langle\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu}a,a\rangle+\int_{M}(V_{k}-\sigma)|a|^{2}(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)+\sigma\|a\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu}a,a\rangle+A\int_{M}\big|{\rm d}|a|\big|^{2}(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)+\sigma\|a\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu})^{1/2}a\|^{2}_{2}+A\|\nabla a\|^{2}_{2}+\sigma\|a\|^{2}_{2},

where the last inequality comes from the Kato inequality (see e.g.

This implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla a\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-A}\|(\Delta^{(k)}_{\mu}+\sigma)^{1/2}a\|^{2}_{2}.

We complete the proof by letting

Lemma 3.3

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{\|u\|_{2}=1}\left|\int_{M}\left(\sum_{i}\int_{M}\frac{{\rm e}^{-\sigma^{\prime}t_{i}-\alpha^{\prime\prime}\frac{\rho^{2}(x,y)}{t_{i}}}}{\mu(B(x,\sqrt{t_{i}}))}\mathbbm{1}_{\tilde{B}_{i}}(y)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\right)u(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right|

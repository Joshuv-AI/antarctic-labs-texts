Abstract
. A typical example of a Cartan calculus consists of the Lie derivative and the contraction with vector fields of a manifold on the derivation ring of the de Rham complex. In this manuscript, a second stage of the Cartan calculus is investigated. In a general setting, the stage is formulated with operators obtained by the André–Quillen cohomology of a commutative differential graded algebra A A on the Hochschild homology of A A in terms of the homotopy Cartan calculus in the sense of Fiorenza and Kowalzig. Moreover, the Cartan calculus is interpreted geometrically with maps from the rational homotopy group of the monoid of self-homotopy equivalences on a space M M to the derivation ring on the loop cohomology of M M . We also give a geometric description to Sullivan’s isomorphism, which relates the geometric Cartan calculus to the algebraic one, via the Γ 1 \Gamma_{1} map due to Félix and Thomas.

3.
Algebraic Cartan calculi

In this section, following
Fiorenza and Kowalzig
[
FK20
]
,
we introduce the Cartan calculus with a slight generalization. Moreover, we give examples of the calculi by using a Sullivan model of the free loop space of a simply-connected space and the Hochschild chain complex of a differential graded algebra (DGA).

3.1.
Homotopy Cartan calculus with slight generalization

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\delta)
be a chain complex
and
(\mathfrak{h},d,B)
a mixed complex.

Definition 3.1

(c.f.
[
FK20
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A tuple

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},e,L,S)
consisting of
linear maps
e,L,S\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
of degrees 1, 0 and
-1
, respectively, is
a
homotopy pre-Cartan calculus

if the equalities

(3.2)

\displaystyle L_{\theta}=B(e_{\theta})+d(S_{\theta})+S_{\delta\theta},

(3.3)

\displaystyle d(e_{\theta})+e_{\delta\theta}=0\ \ \text{and}

(3.4)

\displaystyle B(S_{\theta})=0

hold for any
\theta\in\mathfrak{g}
. The linear maps
e
and
L
are called a
contraction operator
(or
cap product
) and a
Lie derivative
, respectively.

The first two conditions imply that

e
and
L
are chain maps of degree
1
and
0
, respectively.

Definition 3.5
.

[c.f.
[
FK20
, Definition 3.7]
]
Let
(\mathfrak{g},\delta,[\ ,\ ])
be a dg Lie algebra and

(\mathfrak{h},d,B,[\ ,\ ])
a mixed Lie algebra; see
2.6
.
A
homotopy Cartan calculus

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},e,L,S,T)

is a homotopy pre-Cartan calculus
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},e,L,S)
equipped with
a linear map
T\colon\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
satisfying the following equalities
for any
\theta,\rho\in\mathfrak{g}

(3.6)

\displaystyle[e_{\theta},L_{\rho}]-e_{[\theta,\rho]}=d(T_{\theta,\rho})-T_{\delta\theta,\rho}-(-1)^{\mathopen{|}\theta\mathclose{|}}T_{\theta,\delta\rho},

(3.7)

\displaystyle[S_{\theta},L_{\rho}]-S_{[\theta,\rho]}=B(T_{\theta,\rho}).

Here

is called a
Gelfan’d-Daletskiĭ-Tsygan homotopy
.

Remark 3.8
.

These definitions are equivalent to
[
FK20
, Definitions 3.1 and 3.7]

if
(\mathfrak{h},d,B,[\ ,\ ])
is
the tuple
(\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{C}}),[d^{\prime},\ ],[B^{\prime},\ ],[\ ,\ ])

which is given by a mixed complex
({\mathcal{C}},d^{\prime},B^{\prime})

In this case,
we may call the calculus a homotopy Cartan calculus on the mixed complex
{\mathcal{C}}
.

The following is one of fundamental properties of a homotopy Cartan calculus.

Lemma 3.9

(c.f.
[
FK20

Let

be a homotopy Cartan calculus,
Then the map

In particular, we see that a homotopy Cartan calculus

-module structure to

via the map

is a morphism of

-modules.

If the linear map

in a homotopy Cartan calculus is trivial, then the map

is regarded as a morphism of

-modules, where the

-module structure of

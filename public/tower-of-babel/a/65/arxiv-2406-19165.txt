Abstract
. Let C φ C_{\varphi} be a composition operator acting on the Hardy space of the unit disc H p H^{p} ( 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty ), which is embedded in a C 0 C_{0} -semigroup of composition operators 𝒯 = ( C φ t ) t ≥ 0 . \mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}. We investigate whether the commutant or the bicommutant of C φ C_{\varphi} , or the commutant of the semigroup 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , are isomorphic to subalgebras of continuous functions defined on a connected set. In particular, it allows us to derive results about the existence of non-trivial idempotents (and non-trivial orthogonal projections if p = 2 p=2 ) lying in such sets. Our methods also provide results concerning the minimality of the commutant and the double commutant property, in the sense that they coincide with the closure in the weak operator topology of the unital algebra generated by the operator. Moreover, some consequences regarding the extended eigenvalues and the strong compactness of such operators are derived. This extends previous results of Lacruz, León-Saavedra, Petrovic and Rodríguez-Piazza, Fernández-Valles and Lacruz and Shapiro on linear fractional composition operators acting on H 2 H^{2} .

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

For
1\leq p<\infty,
we consider the classical Hardy space
H^{p},
which is the space formed by all the holomorphic functions
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that the quantity

\left\lvert\left\lvert f\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}=\left(\sup\limits_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p}

is finite. In particular,
H^{p}
is a Banach space endowed with the norm
\left\lvert\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}
for every
1\leq p<\infty.

In general, if
\mathcal{X}
is a complex Banach space and
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
is the Banach algebra of linear bounded operators acting on
\mathcal{X}
, we consider the
commutant
and
bicommutant
of an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
, defined as:

{\{T\}^{\prime}}=\{A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X}):AT=TA\}\qquad{\{T\}^{\prime\prime}}=\bigcap\limits_{A\in{\{T\}^{\prime}}}{\{A\}^{\prime}},

respectively.

The study of the commutant and the bicommutant of an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
provides important consequences regarding the structure of the operator. In this respect, the commutant and bicommutant of composition operators acting on
H^{p}
have been widely considered in the recent years. Recall that, given a holomorphic map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
, it defines a composition operator
C_{\varphi}
that acts as

C_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi,\qquad f\in H^{p}.

These operators are well known to be bounded on
H^{p}
for all
1\leq p<\infty
by means of the Littlewood’s subordination Theorem.

Our starting point are the works of Lacruz, León-Saavedra, Petrovic and Rodríguez-Piazza
[
17
,
18
]
, in which they studied the minimality of the commutant and the bicommutant of composition operators
C_{\varphi}
acting on
H^{2}
induced by linear fractional maps. More specifically, they characterized whether
C_{\varphi}
has
minimal commutant
, in the sense that

\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}^{\sigma}={\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}},

where
{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})=\{p(C_{\varphi}):p\ \textnormal{is a polynomial}\}
and
\overline{A}^{\sigma}
stands for the closure in the weak operator topology of a set
A\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
. Likewise, they characterized whether
C_{\varphi}
has the
double commutant property
, in the sense that

\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}={\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

For more on these properties and (bi)commutants of composition operators, see the references in the aforementioned works.

In this work, we are primarily interested in studying the existence of non-trivial idempotents (that is, operators
J\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
such that
J^{2}=J
and
J\neq 0,Id_{H^{p}}
) lying in the commutant or the bicommutant of composition operators, and of non-trivial orthogonal projections, which are self-adjoint idempotents, whenever
p=2
.

Recall that, if such an idempotent
J
lies in the commutant (or respectively, in the bicommutant) of
T\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
, then the ranges of
J
and its complement
I-J
, namely
\textnormal{ran}(J)

\textnormal{ran}(I-J)
, verify that
H^{p}=\textnormal{ran}(J)+\textnormal{ran}(I-J)
, and are non-trivial closed invariant subspaces for
T
(or respectively, hyperinvariant subspaces, which are invariant under every operator
A\in{\{T\}^{\prime}}
). If
p=2
and
J
is a non-trivial orthogonal projection, these subspaces turn out to be
reducing
for the operator
T
, meaning that
\textnormal{ran}(I-J)=\textnormal{ran}(J)^{\perp}
and
H^{2}=\textnormal{ran}(J)\oplus\textnormal{ran}(J)^{\perp}.

In this sense, the idea of studying the existence of reducing subspaces for composition operators is not novel. For instance, in
[
16
]
it is shown that every composition operator
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2})
such that
\varphi(0)=0
has non-trivial reducing subspaces. Moreover, in
[
21
]
the lattice of invariant subspaces of composition operators induced by non-automorphic linear fractional parabolic maps acting on
H^{2}
is characterized, deriving as a consequence the non-existence of non-trivial reducing subspaces for such operators. For more works in this line for different classes of operators, see
[
14
,
15
]
and the references therein.

The existence of idempotents lying on
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
or
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
will be carried out by studying the
functional commutant property
and the
functional bicommutant property
for composition operators which, roughly speaking, ask such sets to be algebraically isomorphic to certain subalgebras of continuous functions. These general concepts, along with a result that connects this notion with the existence of non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
or
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
, will be introduced in Section
2
.

Regarding composition operators, our methods provide results not only for linear fractional composition operators, but for a quite larger class of composition operators, namely those which can be embedded in a
C_{0}-
semigroup. Recall that a one parameter family
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
is said to be a
C_{0}
-semigroup if:

(1)

T_{0}=Id_{\mathcal{X}}.

(2)

T_{t+s}=T_{t}T_{s}
for all
t,s\geq 0.

(3)

T_{t}\rightarrow Id_{\mathcal{X}}
in the strong operator topology when
t\rightarrow 0.

Notice that, given a
C_{0}-
semigroup, one may also study the existence of non-trivial idempotents in the commutant of the semigroup, that is, the Banach algebra

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\bigcap\limits_{t>0}{\{T_{t}\}^{\prime}}.

Observe that, in general, if
T=T_{t_{0}}
for some
t_{0}>0
,

{\{T\}^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq{\{T\}^{\prime}}.

Now, in order to study
C_{0}-
semigroups of composition operators, let us introduce the
holomorphic semiflows
. A one parameter family
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of holomorphic functions mapping
\mathbb{D}
to itself is said to be a
holomorphic semiflow
(or a semigroup of holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, or simply semigroup in
\mathbb{D}
) if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\varphi_{0}(z)=z
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}.

(ii)

\varphi_{t+s}=\varphi_{t}\circ\varphi_{s}
for every
t,s\geq 0.

(iii)

If
t\rightarrow s,
then
\varphi_{t}\rightarrow\varphi_{s}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
for
t,s\geq 0
.

It is well known, by the seminar work of Berkson and Porta
[
1
]
, that a one parameter family of composition operators
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}-
semigroup if and only if
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a holomorphic semiflow. It is worth remarking that every linear fractional map which is not loxodromic can be embedded in a holomorphic semiflow, that is, there exists a holomorphic flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}
. In particular, its induced composition operator can be embedded in a
C_{0}-
semigroup. It is an open problem to decide whether a given holomorphic map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
can be embedded in a holomorphic semiflow (see, for instance,
[
27
, Section 4.3 and Chapter V]
).

The holomorphic semiflows can be classified in terms of the configuration of common fixed points of the holomorphic maps that comprise it. A holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
elliptic
if there exists
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}(z_{0})=z_{0}
for some
t_{0}>0.
If the holomorphic semiflow is not elliptic, then there exists a unique point
\tau\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\varphi_{t}
converges uniformly on compact subsets to the constant function
\tau
when
t\rightarrow\infty
. This point
\tau
is called the
Denjoy-Wolff point
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.
It is known that the functions
\varphi_{t}^{\prime}
has non-tangential limits
e^{t\alpha}
at the Denjoy-Wolff point
\tau,
with
\alpha\leq 0
. Thus, the holomorphic semiflow is said to be
hyperbolic
if
\alpha<0
and
parabolic
if
\alpha=0.

Our results regarding the (bi)commutant of composition operators which can be embedded in
C_{0}
-semigroups will depend on the nature of the holomorphic semiflow, regarding the classification discussed above. In order to present our main results, we need to introduce some geometric aspects of the semiflows.

For every non-elliptic holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
there exists a univalent function
h:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

(1.1)

\varphi_{t}(z)=h^{-1}(h(z)+t)\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},\ t\geq 0.

Such a function
h
is called the
Koenigs function
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. The set
\Omega:=h(\mathbb{D})
is said to be the
Koenigs domain
of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.
Such a domain is
convex at infinity
, that is, if
z\in\Omega
and
t\geq 0
, then
z+t\in\Omega
. Moreover, if there exists another univalent map
g:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying
g(\mathbb{D})=h(\mathbb{D})
and (
1.1
), then
g(z)=h(z)+a
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}

[
4
, Proposition 9.3.10]
.

The geometry of the Koenigs domain encodes valuable information for the associated holomorphic semiflow. For instance, a non-elliptic holomorphic semiflow is hyperbolic if and only if its Koenigs domain is contained in a horizontal strip
[
7
]
. In this sense, the geometry of the Koenigs domain will also play a fundamental role in our results.

Now, we can state our main result regarding hyperbolic semiflows, which are studied in Section
3
. It reads as follows:

Theorem 1.
(Theorems
3.4
and
3.6
).
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Then, for every
1\leq p<\infty,

(i)

Let
\Omega
be the associated Koenigs domain of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional bicommutant property. In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

(ii)

The commutant of
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
is not abelian, so
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant property or minimal commutant.

We also show that, under the hypotheses on (
i
), if
\mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
, then
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
does not contain any non-trivial idempotents (Corollary
3.5
). Moreover, we also prove a result providing sufficient conditions for
C_{\varphi}
to have the double commutant property (Theorem
3.7
).

Parabolic semiflows are considered in Section
4
. In order to state our main results in this setting, we need to distinguish two cases. A parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is said to be of
positive hyperbolic step
if for some
z\in\mathbb{D}
the quantity

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}k_{\mathbb{D}}(\varphi_{t}(z),\varphi_{t+1}(z))

is positive, where
k_{\mathbb{D}}
denotes the hyperbolic distance in
\mathbb{D}
. If the previous situation does not hold,
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of
zero hyperbolic step
.

Our main result regarding the bicommutant is the following:

Theorem 2.
(Theorem
4.5
).
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain. Assume that either

(i)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected; or

(ii)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step,
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega.

Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional bicommutant property for
1\leq p<\infty
. In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

As in the hyperbolic case, the previous result can also be stated for the commutant of
\mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
(Corollary
4.6
). On the other hand, the situation for
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
can be quite different, regarding the geometric picture of the Koenigs domain:

Theorem 3.
(Theorems
4.8
and
4.10
).
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1},
and let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain.

(i)

If
\Omega
is contained in a half plane,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\infty,
then
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional commutant property for
1\leq p<\infty
. In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}.

(ii)

If
\Omega
is contained in a horizontal half-plane of the form
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)>-\eta\},\eta>0
,
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
is not abelian. In particular,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant property or minimal commutant.

As a particular instance,
(i)
can be applied to deduce that, if
\varphi
is a non-automorphic parabolic linear fractional map, then
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
does not commute with any non-trivial idempotent (Corollary
4.9
), which extends the aforementioned result of Montes-Rodríguez, Ponce-Escudero and Shkarin.

For the elliptic semiflow case, which is considered in Section
5
, we study the more general situation of holomorphic maps
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
fixing a point in
\mathbb{D}
. In this respect, our main result is the following:

Theorem 4.
(Theorem
5.1
).
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
and consider
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
,
1<p<\infty.
Then, there exist non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.
As a matter of fact,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant or bicommutant property.

Let us point out that our methods rely on some properties of the infinitesimal generator of the
C_{0}-
semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0},
which is a linear closed densely-defined unbounded operator given by

(1.2)

\Delta f=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{C_{\varphi_{t}}f-f}{t}=Gf^{\prime}\qquad(f\in\textnormal{Dom}(\Delta)),

where
G
is a unique holomorphic function
G:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying

\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}(z)}{\partial t}=G(\varphi_{t}(z)),\qquad(t\geq 0,z\in\mathbb{D}).

A remarkable property is that the point spectrum of
\Delta
can be related with the point spectrum of the operators of the semigroup. More specifically, the following equality

(1.3)

\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi_{t}}\mid_{H^{p}})=\{e^{t\lambda}:\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\}\qquad(t>0)

follows from
[
12
, Chapter IV, Theorem 3.7]
and the fact that
C_{\varphi_{t}}
is injective for all
t>0.
It is also notable that
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
if and only if the function
e^{\lambda h}
belongs to
H^{p}
, where
h
is the Koenigs function of
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.
In such a case, the eigenspace
\ker(\Delta-\lambda I)
is one-dimensional and it is spanned by
e^{\lambda h}

[
28
]
.

The characterization of the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generator for non-elliptic semigroups, which was carried out by Betsakos in
[
2
]
, will be essential to state our results, along with the results of Bracci, Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Yakubovich
[
5
]
, who established results about the density of the eigenfunctions of the infinitesimal generator. Since these results depend on the kind of holomorphic semiflows we are considering, we will present these statements separately for hyperbolic semiflows and parabolic semiflows.

It is also worth mentioning that our methods will also provide characterizations of the extended eigenvalues and the strong compactness of such operators (see Subsections
3.3
and
4.2
). These two notions have been studied for linear fractional composition operators in
H^{2}
in
[
13
,
19
,
25
]
. For more on the subjects, see the references in these works.

2.
The functional (bi)commutant property

In this section, we introduce the functional commutant property and the functional bicommutant property, and study their connection with the existence of non-trivial idempotents lying in such sets. In order to introduce these concepts, given a subset
G\subset\mathbb{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(G)
the algebra consisting of all continuous functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, equipped with the pointwise sum and multiplication of functions. Notice that we are not endowing
\mathcal{C}(G)
with any topology, since we are only interested in the algebraic properties of the set.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
has the
functional commutant property
(respectively,
functional bicommutant property
) if there exist a connected subset
G\subset\mathbb{C}
, a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G)
and an algebraic isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\{T\}^{\prime}}
(respectively,
\Phi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\{T\}^{\prime\prime}}).

Although this definition is, at least to the author’s knowledge, novel in the literature, the concept of the functional commutant (or bicommutant) property for an operator has been implicitly studied and already encompasses several examples.

For instance, in
[
26
]
the authors showed that under quite reasonable hypothesis on a Hilbert space of holomorphic functions,
{\{M_{z}\}^{\prime}}=H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, and it is easy to construct an algebra isomorphism
\Phi:H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow{\{M_{z}\}^{\prime}}
, so the operator
M_{z}
has the functional commutant property. This can also be used to prove that the classical Cesàro operator has the functional commutant property. Similar ideas are also managed by Sarason
[
23
]
when describing the commutants of the Volterra operator on
H^{2}
and of some truncated Toeplitz operators. On the other hand, it was proved in
[
18
, Theorems 3.4 and 5.6]
that the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
has the functional bicommutant property whenever
\varphi
is a hyperbolic linear fractional map with no fixed points in
\mathbb{D}
.

Now, let us state the aforementioned relation between the functional commutant (or bicommutant) property and the existence of idempotents lying in such algebras.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
and assume it has the functional commutant property (respectively, the functional bicommutant property). Then, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{T\}^{\prime}}
(respectively,
{\{T\}^{\prime\prime}}
).

Proof.

Let us prove the result for the commutant, the proof for the bicommutant is analogous. By contradiction, assume that there exists a non-trivial idempotent
J\in{\{T\}^{\prime}}.
Thus, since
T
has the functional commutant property, there exist a connected subset
G\subset\mathbb{C}
, a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(G)
and an algebra isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}.
In particular, there exists
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\Phi(f)=J.
Since
\Phi
is an algebra isomorphism, it is straightforward to check that
f
is also a non-trivial idempotent, that is,
f^{2}=f
and
f
is not the zero or the constant function
f\equiv 1.
But
f
is a continuous function defined on a connected set
G,
so
f^{2}=f
implies that
f\equiv 0
of
f\equiv 1,
which yields the desired contradiction.
∎

To finish this section, let us state a simple lemma that will be important in what follows:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and suppose that either

(i)

T
has the functional commutant property; or

(ii)

T
has minimal commutant.

Then,
{\{T\}^{\prime}}
is abelian and
{\{T\}^{\prime}}={\{T\}^{\prime\prime}}.

3.
Hyperbolic semiflows

We begin by presenting a slight reformulation of the description of the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generators associated to hyperbolic semigroups, which was carried out by Betsakos:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
2
]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semiflow, let
\Omega
be the associated Koenigs domain and
\gamma
be the width of the smallest horizonal strip containing
\Omega.
Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup of composition operators acting on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty,
and let
\Delta
be its infinitesimal generator.

(i)

If
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip, then

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

(1)

If
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip, there exists a horizontal strip contained in
\Omega
with width

\beta_{\textnormal{max}}=\max\{\beta>0:\Omega\ \textnormal{contains a horizontal strip of width }\beta\}.

Moreover,

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.

Now, we state a result about the density of the linear manifolds spanned by the eigenvectors of the infinitesimal generator
\Delta
. This result is just a small modification of
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, in order to consider only the interior of the point spectrum to obtain the density of certain linear manifolds spanned by eigenvectors:

Proposition 3.2
.

[
5
]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic semiflow, let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then, if
h
is the Koenigs function associated to
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, the linear manifold

\Span\{e^{\lambda h}:\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}}))\}

is dense in
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty.

Proof.

It is equivalent to show that
\Span\{e^{\lambda z}:\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}}))\}
is dense on the Hardy space
H^{p}(\Omega)
associated to the domain
\Omega
, that is, the Banach space of all the holomorphic functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that there exists a harmonic function
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
|f(z)|^{p}\leq u(z)
for every
z\in\Omega
(see
[
30
]
for more on
H^{p}
spaces of general domains).

Now, by the convexity at infinity of
\Omega
it is clear that

\{e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}\subseteq\{e^{\lambda z}\in H^{p}(\Omega):\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\}.

As a consequence of our hypotheses on
\Omega
and
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
we deduce that
\Span\{e^{\lambda z}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega):\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\}
is weak-star dense in
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
, so it is weak dense on
H^{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty.
Thus, Mazur’s Lemma (see
[
10
, Corollary 3, Chapter 2]
) ensures the density in
H^{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
, and the density in
H^{1}(\Omega)
follows from the continuity of the injection
H^{p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega).

∎

It will also be important to handle a slightly stronger property concerning the density of eigenfunctions of operators. An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
has
rich point spectrum
if
\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(T))\neq\emptyset
and, for every open disc
D\subset\sigma_{p}(T)
, the linear manifold

\Span\bigcup\limits_{z\in D}\ker(T-zI)

is dense in
\mathcal{X}.
This concept was first introduced in
[
20
]
in order to study the extended eigenvalues of Cesàro operators.

Before stating the aforementioned result, let us recall that
(H^{p})^{*}
can be identified with
H^{q}
for
1<p<\infty
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, while
(H^{1})^{*}
may be identified with
BMOA

[
30
]
. In particular, for each functional
\delta\in(H^{p})^{*}
there exists a unique
g\in H^{q}
(or
BMOA
, respectively) such that

\delta(f)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{i\theta})\overline{g(e^{i\theta})}d\theta\qquad(f\in H^{p}).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then,
C_{\varphi}
has rich point spectrum.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
and (
1.3
) it is clear that
\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi}))\neq\emptyset.
Let
D\subset\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi})
be an open disc. We are showing that

\Span\{e^{\lambda h}:e^{\lambda}\in D\}

is dense in
H^{p}
, which will yield the result. For such a task, observe that the map

\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}}))\mapsto e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}

is a vector-valued holomorphic function, since for every
g\in H^{q}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
if
p>1
of
g\in BMOA
if
p=1
the map

\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}}))\mapsto\langle e^{\lambda h},g\rangle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{\lambda h(e^{i\theta})}\overline{g(e^{i\theta})}d\theta

is holomorphic by means of a standard application of Morera’s Theorem. Now, assume that
1<p<\infty
and let
g\in H^{q}
(again, the case
p=1
is equivalent considering
g\in BMOA
) satisfying
\langle e^{\lambda h},g\rangle=0
for every
e^{\lambda}\in D.
By the Identity Theorem, it follows then that
\langle e^{\lambda h},g\rangle=0
for every
\lambda\in\textnormal{int}(\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})),
and Proposition
3.2
yields that
g=0.
This shows the density of
\Span\{e^{\lambda h}:e^{\lambda}\in D\}
, and the proof is done.
∎

3.1.
The functional (bi)commutant property

Now, we can state the main result for hyperbolic semiflows. The proof includes various ideas from
[
18
, Section 3]
:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional bicommutant property for every
1\leq p<\infty.
In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

Our aim is to construct a connected open subset
G\subset\mathbb{C}
, a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{C}(G)
and an algebra isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.
Let
G=\textnormal{int}(\sigma(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})),
which by Theorem
3.1
may be a vertical half-plane or a vertical strip. Observe that both sets are connected open sets. We will work with the two situations indifferently, since the proof works the same for both cases.

Let
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
and consider
\lambda\in G.
Observe that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
, and in particular
C_{\varphi_{t}}e^{\lambda h}=e^{\lambda t}e^{\lambda h}
for every
t>0
by (
1.3
). Now,

(3.1)

Xe^{\lambda h}=e^{-\lambda t}XC_{\varphi_{t}}e^{\lambda h}=e^{-\lambda t}C_{\varphi_{t}}(Xe^{\lambda h}).

That is,
Xe^{\lambda h}
is a common eigenvector for the semigroup
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
, so there exists a map
\nu_{X}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
Xe^{\lambda h}=\nu_{X}(\lambda)e^{\lambda h}.
We claim that
\nu_{X}
is, indeed, a holomorphic function. For such a task, recall that the map
\lambda\in G\mapsto e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
is a vector-valued holomorphic function, as it was shown in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Now, consider
\mathbbm{1}\in H^{p}
to be the constant function
\mathbbm{1}(z)\equiv 1
. Recall that
\langle f,\mathbbm{1}\rangle=f(0)
for every
f\in H^{p}.
Thus,

\langle Xe^{\lambda h},\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\nu_{X}(\lambda)\langle e^{\lambda h},\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\nu_{X}(\lambda)e^{\lambda h(0)},

so
\nu_{X}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda h(0)}\langle Xe^{\lambda h},\mathbbm{1}\rangle
for every
\lambda\in G
. Hence,
\nu_{X}
is a product of holomorphic functions and is holomorphic as well, as claimed.

Now, observe that
\nu_{X}(\lambda)\in\sigma_{p}(X)
for every
\lambda\in G
, so
|\nu_{X}(\lambda)|\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert X\right\rvert\right\rvert
for every
\lambda\in G
. That is,
\nu_{X}\in H^{\infty}(G).
Then, we can define the map
\Psi:X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}\mapsto\nu_{X}\in H^{\infty}(G),
noticing that

(3.2)

Xe^{\lambda h}=\Psi(X)(\lambda)e^{\lambda h}\qquad(\lambda\in G).

Previous equality (
3.2
) guarantees the uniqueness of the definition, so
\Psi
is well defined. Let us prove that
\Psi
is an injective algebra homomorphism. The linearity follows immediately as a consequence of (
3.2
). To see that
\Psi
is multiplicative, let
X,Y\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
and observe that for every
\lambda\in G

\Psi(XY)(\lambda)e^{\lambda h}=XYe^{\lambda h}=X(\Psi(Y)(\lambda)e^{\lambda h})=\Psi(X)(\lambda)\Psi(Y)(\lambda)e^{\lambda h},

so
\Psi(XY)=\Psi(X)\Psi(Y)
, as we wanted. To prove the injectivity of
\Psi
, if
\Psi(X)=0
, then
Xe^{\lambda h}=0
for every
\lambda\in G
by (
3.2
). At this point, it is enough to apply Proposition
3.2
to deduce that
X=0
, so
\Psi
is injective.

Thus,
\Psi
is an injective algebra homomorphism and
\Psi:{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow\textnormal{ran}(\Psi)
is an algebra isomorphism. Thus, we may consider
\Phi:=\Psi^{-1}:\textnormal{ran}(\Psi)\rightarrow{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
, which is again an algebra isomorphism. By construction, it is an immediate fact that
\textnormal{ran}(\Psi)
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}(G)
, since it contains
H^{\infty}(G)
as a subalgebra. As a consequence, we have shown that
C_{\varphi}
has the functional bicommmutant property, and by Theorem
2.2
it has no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

∎

A careful reading of the proof shows that the fact
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
has been only used to derive that
X
commutes with
C_{\varphi_{t}}
for all
t>0.
Hence, it is straightforward to deduce the following consequence:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hyperbolic flow such that its Koenigs domain
\Omega
verifies that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components, and denote
\mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
it associated
C_{0}-
semigroup acting on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is abelian and does not contain any non-trivial idempotent.

At this point, it is natural to ask whether every composition operator satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
3.4
has not only the functional bicommutant property, but also the functional commutant property. In this sense, the following result holds:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Then,
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
is not abelian. In particular,
C_{\varphi}
does not have either the functional commutant property or minimal commutant.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
and
h
be the Koenigs domain and the Koenigs function associated to
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, respectively. Since it is a hyperbolic semiflow,
\Omega
is contained in a horizontal strip. Then, the functions
e^{ixh}
belong to
H^{\infty}
(they are analytic and bounded on
\mathbb{D}
) for every
x\in\mathbb{R}.
Moreover, by Theorem
3.1
and (
1.3
) it follows that
C_{\varphi_{t}}e^{ixh}=e^{itx}e^{ixh}
for every
x,t\in\mathbb{R}.
Now, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the multiplication operator
M_{e^{ixh}},
which is bounded on
H^{p}
. Then, for every
f\in H^{p}
and
t,x\in\mathbb{R}

C_{\varphi_{t}}M_{e^{ixh}}f=C_{\varphi_{t}}(e^{ixh})\cdot C_{\varphi_{t}}f=e^{itx}e^{ixh}C_{\varphi_{t}}f=e^{itx}M_{e^{ixh}}C_{\varphi_{t}}f.

That is,

(3.3)

C_{\varphi_{t}}M_{e^{ixh}}=e^{itx}M_{e^{ixh}}C_{\varphi_{t}}f

At this point, it is easy to consider
x_{0}=2\pi
to obtain a multiplication operator
M_{e^{ix_{0}h}}
lying in the commutant of
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
that
C_{\varphi_{t_{0}}}
does not commute with
M_{e^{ix_{0}h}}
by means of (
3.3
). This shows that
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
is not abelian, and Lemma
2.3
completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
The double commutant property

We can exploit the construction of the proof of Theorem
3.4
to deduce the double commutant property for a subclass of the composition operators considered:

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. With the notation of Theorem
3.1
, assume further that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip and that there exists
C>0
such that

(3.4)

\left\lvert\left\lvert p(C_{\varphi})\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq C\cdot\sup\{|p(z)|:|z|\leq e^{\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}}\}

for every polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the double commutant property for every
1<p<\infty.

Before proceeding with the proof, let us take a closer look at the inequality (
3.4
):

Remark 3.8
.

Consider the Hilbertian Hardy space
H^{2}
. It is known, by the von Neumann’s inequality, that for every contraction
T\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2})
(that is,
\left\lvert\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq 1
), the following inequality

\left\lvert\left\lvert p(T)\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\sup\{|p(z)|:|z|\leq 1\}

holds for every complex polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. In general, if
T
is any non-zero operator, one may consider
\frac{T}{\left\lvert\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right\rvert}
and apply the previous inequality to obtain

\left\lvert\left\lvert p(T)\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\sup\{|p(z)|:|z|\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert T\right\rvert\right\rvert\}.

In addition, it is known by
[
28
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
that
r(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{p}})=e^{\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}}
, where
r(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{p}})
denotes the spectral radius of a composition operator
C_{\varphi}
acting on
H^{p}
. So, in the Hilbert space case, (
3.4
) turns into
\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{2}}\right\rvert\right\rvert=r(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{2}}).
For instance, it holds if

\varphi_{t}(z)=e^{-t}z+1-e^{-t}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D},t\geq 0)

is the hyperbolic semiflow of non-automorphic linear fractional maps (
[
9
, Theorem 3]
), so we recover the result in
[
18
]
.

On the other hand, if
p\neq 2,
a von-Neumann type-inequality does not hold in general. In this setting, in order to obtain composition operators
C_{\varphi}
verifying (
3.4
), it is enough to ask the operator
C_{\varphi}/\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}\right\rvert\right\rvert
to be
polynomially bounded
(see, for instance,
[
6
, Definition 2.3.2]
), together with the equality
\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{p}}=r(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{p}}).
Whether the operator
C_{\varphi}/\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}\right\rvert\right\rvert
is polynomially bounded is an open problem, at least to the author’s knowledge.

Now, we present the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
. Again, this proof is based on various ideas from
[
18
, Section 3]
. Consider the map
\Psi:{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow H^{\infty}(G)
constructed in the proof of Theorem
3.4
and recall that, since we are assuming that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip, then
G=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:-\infty<\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}\}.
Now, if
r=e^{\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}},
then the exponential function maps
G
onto
D(0,r)\setminus\{0\}.

Let
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.
We are showing that
\Psi(X)(\lambda)=\Psi(X)(\lambda+2\pi ik)
for every
\lambda\in G
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, which, together with the Factorization Theorem
[
22
, p. 286]
, will yield that there exists
\xi_{X}
holomorphic on
D(0,r)\setminus\{0\}
such that
\Psi(X)(\lambda)=\xi(e^{\lambda})
for every
\lambda\in G.

We have

\Psi(X)(\lambda+2\pi ik)e^{(\lambda+2\pi ik)h}=Xe^{(\lambda+2\pi ik)h}=Xe^{(2\pi ik)h}e^{\lambda h}.

At this point, observe that
e^{(2\pi ik)h}\in H^{\infty}
, so the multiplication operator
M_{e^{(2\pi ik)h}}
is bounded. Indeed, it is straightforward to check that it commutes with
C_{\varphi},
so it also commutes with
X
. Thus, we have

Xe^{(2\pi ik)h}e^{\lambda h}=XM_{e^{(2\pi ik)h}}e^{\lambda h}=M_{e^{(2\pi ik)h}}Xe^{\lambda h}=e^{(2\pi ik)h}\Psi(X)(\lambda)e^{\lambda h}=\Psi(X)(\lambda)e^{(\lambda+2\pi ik)h},

so
\Psi(X)(\lambda)=\Psi(X)(\lambda+2\pi ik)
, as we wanted. Thus,
\Psi(X)(\lambda)=\xi(e^{\lambda})
for every
\lambda\in G
for some holomorphic function
\xi_{X}
on
D(0,r)\setminus\{0\}.
But
\xi_{X}
is bounded, so the singularity on
0
is removable and
\xi_{X}
is holomorphic on
D:=D(0,r).

As a consequence, we can define
\tilde{\Psi}:{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow H^{\infty}(D)
as
\tilde{\Psi}(X)=\xi_{X}
. In particular, it follows that

(3.5)

Xe^{\lambda h}=\Psi(X)e^{\lambda h}=\tilde{\Psi}(X)(e^{\lambda})e^{\lambda h}\qquad(\lambda\in G),

and by construction
\tilde{\Psi}
is an injective algebra homomorphism.

Now, to finish the proof, we are showing that
\Psi
maps
\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}^{\sigma}
onto
H^{\infty}(D)
, which will yield that
\Psi
is surjective and, by injectivity,
\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}^{\sigma}={\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}},
as we want to prove.

Consider then
\xi\in H^{\infty}(D)
, we are constructing a sequence of polynomials
p_{n}
such that
p_{n}(C_{\varphi})
converges to some operator
X
in the weak operator topology satisfying
\tilde{\Psi}(X)=\xi.
By
[
18
, Lemma 2.10]
there exists a sequence of polynomials
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges pointwisely to
\xi
and satisfy that
\left\lvert\left\lvert p_{n}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{\infty}(D)}\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{\infty}(D)}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Thus, consider
X_{n}=p_{n}(C_{\varphi})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}.
We have, by (
3.4
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\lvert\left\lvert X_{n}\right\rvert\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\lvert p_{n}(C_{\varphi})\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert\left\lvert p_{n}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{\infty}(D)}\leq C\left\lvert\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\right\rvert_{H^{\infty}(D)},

so the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is norm bounded. Thus, since
1<p<\infty,
there exists a subsequence
(X_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges in the weak operator topology to some
X\in\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}^{\sigma}.
We are showing that
\tilde{\Psi}(X)=\xi,
which will finish the proof. For such a purpose, consider
\lambda\in G
and
f\in H^{q}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and observe that

\begin{split}\langle Xe^{\lambda h},f\rangle=\langle\lim\limits_{k}X_{n_{k}}e^{\lambda h},f\rangle=\langle\lim\limits_{k}\tilde{\Psi}(X_{n_{k}})(e^{\lambda})e^{\lambda h},f\rangle=\langle\lim\limits_{k}p_{n_{k}}(e^{\lambda})e^{\lambda h},f\rangle=\langle\xi(e^{\lambda})e^{\lambda h},f\rangle,\end{split}

so we deduce that
Xe^{\lambda h}=\xi(e^{\lambda})
for every
\lambda\in G
, so by (
3.5
)
\tilde{\Psi}(X)=\xi,
as we wanted to show.
\Box

Remark 3.9
.

It is also worth mentioning that not every composition operator belonging to a hyperbolic semigroup has the double commutant property, although its associated Koenigs domain has desirable geometric properties. For instance, if one considers
\varphi
to be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
, it is always embedded in a hyperbolic group of automorphisms whose Koenigs domain is a horizontal strip. It was shown in
[
18
]
that the induced composition operator acting on
H^{2}
does not enjoy the double commutant property.

Indeed, in
[
18
, Theorem 5.6]
it is proved that
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi},C_{\varphi}^{-1})}^{\sigma}.
The methods are quite similar to the ones presented in the proof of Theorem
3.7
, with the main difference that the exponential maps the point spectrum of the associated infinitesimal generator onto an annulus instead of a punctured disc, which in some sense ‘forces’ to consider
C_{\varphi}^{-1}
in order to approximate with polynomials in the weak operator topology.
Following this line of thinking, consider now
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
a hyperbolic semiflow such that
\Omega
properly contains a horizontal strip. In particular, the induced composition operators
C_{\varphi_{t}}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2})
are not invertible. This raises the question whether the equality
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{{\textnormal{alg}}(C_{\varphi})}^{\sigma}
cannot hold in this setting.

3.3.
Extended eigenvalues and strong compactness

Given an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
, a complex number
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is said to be an
extended eigenvalue
for
T
is there exists
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
such that
TA=\lambda AT.
In such a case, we say that
A
is an
eigenoperator
for
T
associated to the extended eigenvalue
\lambda
. The set of extended eigenvalues of
T
is denoted as
\textnormal{Ext}(T)
.

Recall that, in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we have shown the identity (
3.3
), which in particular shows that
M_{e^{ixh}}
is an eigenoperator for
C_{\varphi_{t}}
associated to the extended eigenvalue
e^{itx}.
Exploiting such a property, we are able to state the following result, which describes the extended eigenvalues of composition operators embedded in hyperbolic semigroups:

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then,

(a)

Assume that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip, and let
\rho=\inf\{\textnormal{Re}(z):z\in\Omega\}.

(i)

If
\rho>-\infty
, then
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})=\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}.

(ii)

If
\rho=-\infty,
then
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}.

(b)

Assume that
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip. Then
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})=\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

First, let us assume that
\Omega
does not contain any horizontal strip. By Theorem
3.1
and (
1.3
), together with the fact that
C_{\varphi}
is injective, it follows that

D(0,e^{\pi/p\gamma})\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\overline{D(0,e^{\pi/p\gamma})}\setminus\{0\}.

Now, assume that
\rho>-\infty.
In particular, it follows that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{\infty}
for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)<0.
Moreover,
C_{\varphi}e^{\lambda h}=e^{\lambda}e^{\lambda h}
. By considering the multiplication operator
M_{e^{\lambda h}}
and imitating the argument used to show (
3.3
), we deduce that

M_{e^{\lambda h}}C_{\varphi}=e^{\lambda}C_{\varphi}M_{e^{\lambda h}}

for every
\lambda
with
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda<0).
This shows that
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi}).
For the reverse inclusion, first observe that
0\notin\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})
since
C_{\varphi}
is injective. Thus, it is enough to recall that
C_{\varphi}
has rich point spectrum by Lemma
3.3
and apply
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
to deduce that every
\lambda\in\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})
has modulus
|\lambda|\leq 1
, so
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})=\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\},
as we wanted.

Suppose now that
\rho=-\infty.
In this case, we have that
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{\infty}
if and only if
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)=0.
Then, by (
3.3
) we deduce that
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi}).
On the other hand, by recalling that
0\notin\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})
and again using that
C_{\varphi}
has rich point spectrum, we deduce that
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}.

To finish the proof, assume that
\Omega
contains a horizontal strip. In such a case, again by Theorem
3.1
and (
1.3
) we have

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:e^{-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}}<|\lambda|<e^{\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}}\}\subseteq\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi}\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:e^{-\frac{\pi}{p\beta_{\textnormal{max}}}}<|\lambda|\leq e^{\frac{\pi}{p\gamma}}\}.

By (
3.3
) we deduce that
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi}).
Finally, it is enough to recall again that
C_{\varphi}
has rich point spectrum and apply
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
to deduce that
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})=\mathbb{T}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

We end this section with a consequence regarding the strong compactness of composition operators embedded in
C_{0}
-semigroups. An algebra
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
is said to be
strongly compact
if every bounded subset of
\mathcal{A}
is relatively compact in the strong operator topology. In particular, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})
is strongly compact if
{\textnormal{alg}}(T)
is a strongly compact algebra.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a hyperbolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume that
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
has at most two connected components. Then,
C_{\varphi}
is strongly compact.

Proof.

This result follows as a byproduct of Proposition
3.2
and
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

4.
Parabolic semiflows

In this section, we consider composition operators
C_{\varphi}
induced by symbols
\varphi
that can be embedded in a parabolic semiflow. As we noted previously in Section
1
, we will distinguish between zero hyperbolic step and positive hyperbolic step semiflows.

The kind of hyperbolic step determines some geometric aspects of the Koenigs domain of the semiflow. The following result holds, as a byproduct of
[
4
, Theorem 9.3.5 and Proposition 9.3.10]
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semiflow, and
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain. Then:

(i)

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step, then

\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}(\Omega-t)\in\{\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)>\nu\},\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)<\nu\}\}

for some
\eta\in\mathbb{R}.

(ii)

If
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step, then

\bigcup\limits_{t\geq 0}(\Omega-t)=\mathbb{C}.

In particular,
\Omega
is not contained in any horizontal half-plane.

Unlike the hyperbolic setting, the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generator for parabolic semiflows is not totally understood yet. In order to illustrate some known situations, we recollect some results from Betsakos that can be found in
[
2
, Section 6]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
2
]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semiflow, and let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain. Let
(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathcal{L}(H^{p})

(1\leq p<\infty)
be the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup of composition operators, and let
\Delta
be its infinitesimal generator.

(i)

\displaystyle{\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\subseteq\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)\leq 0\}}.

(ii)

Assume that
\Omega
is contained in a horizontal half-plane of the form
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)>-\eta\},

\eta>0
, and that
\partial\Omega
is contained in a horizontal strip. Then,

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Re}(\lambda)=0,\textnormal{Im}(\lambda)\geq 0\}.

(iii)

Assume that
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega
is contained in a horizontal strip. Then,

\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})=\{0\}.

Observe that the spectral picture described in
(iii)
can only happen if the semiflow is of zero hyperbolic step, due to Proposition
4.1
.

Now, we state a result concerning the connectedness of
\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
, which will be crucial in order to state the functional (bi)commutant property for parabolic semiflows:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(\varphi)_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semiflow, let
\mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup of composition operators acting on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and let
\Delta
be its infinitesimal generator. Then,
\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
is connected.

Proof.

First, recall that, by (
1.2
),
\Delta\mathbbm{1}=0,
so
0\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta).
We are showing that
\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
is star-shaped at
0
, that is, the segment
[0,\lambda]\subset\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
for every
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta),
which implies the connectedness of
\sigma_{p}(\Delta).
Let
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
and
t\in(0,1)
. Recall that
t\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta)
if and only if
e^{t\lambda h}\in H^{p}
. We have, for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

|e^{t\lambda h}|^{p}=e^{pt\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h)}.
Now, if
z\in\mathbb{D}
is such that
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h(z))\leq 0
, then
e^{pt\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h(z))}\leq 1,
and if
z\in\mathbb{D}
is such that
\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h(z))>0
, then
e^{pt\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h(z))}\leq e^{p\textnormal{Re}(\lambda h(z))}=\left|e^{\lambda h(z)}\right|^{p}.
Hence,
|e^{t\lambda h(z)}|^{p}\leq\max\{1,|e^{\lambda h(z)}|^{p}\}
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and since
e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
, we deduce that
e^{t\lambda h}\in H^{p}
as well, and the proof is done.
∎

Regarding the density of the associated eigenfunctions, the next result holds, following the lines of Proposition
3.2
:

Proposition 4.4
.

[
5
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic semiflow, and let
\Omega
be its associated Koenigs domain. Assume that either

(i)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected; or

(ii)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step,
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega.

Then, if
h
is the Koenigs function associated to
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0},
the linear manifold

\Span\{e^{\lambda h}:\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\}

is dense on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty.

4.1.
The functional (bi)commutant property

With the previous results in hand, we can state our main result for the functional bicommutant property for this setting:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain. Assume that either

(i)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected; or

(ii)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step,
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega.

Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional bicommutant property for
1\leq p<\infty
. In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
G=\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})
, and recall that it is a connected set by Lemma
4.3
. We will construct an algebra isomorphism from
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
to a certain subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}(G).
For this purpose, let
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}
and observe that, for every
\lambda\in G,
(
3.1
) holds. As in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, it follows that there exists a map
\nu_{X}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
Xe^{\lambda h}=\nu_{X}(\lambda)e^{\lambda h}.
We are showing that
\nu_{X}
is continuous. Observe that the map
\lambda\in G\mapsto e^{\lambda h}\in H^{p}
is continuous considering the weak topology of
H^{p}.
Thus, arguing as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we deduce that
\nu_{X}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda h(0)}\langle Xe^{\lambda h},\mathbbm{1}\rangle,
which yields the continuity of
\Psi.
Thus,
\nu_{X}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
for every
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.

At this point, we may define the map
\Psi:{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(G),
which satisfies the identity (
3.2
). Hence, it is enough to mimic the arguments of the proof of Theorem
3.4
, having into account that the linear manifold

\Span\{e^{\lambda h}:\lambda\in G\}

is dense on
H^{p}
by Proposition
4.4
, to deduce that
\Psi
is an injective algebra homomorphism. Finally, consider
\Phi:=\Psi^{-1}:\textnormal{ran}(\Psi)\rightarrow{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}},
which is an algebra isomorphism. Then,
C_{\varphi}
has the functional bicommutant property and the proof is done.
∎

As in the hyperbolic case (see Corollary
3.5
), the previous proof allows us to derive the following result, regarding the commutant of the
C_{0}-
semigroup:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a parabolic flow, let Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and denote by
\mathcal{T}=(C_{\varphi_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
it associated
C_{0}-
semigroup acting on
H^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Assume that either

(i)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of positive hyperbolic step,
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
is connected; or

(ii)

(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step,
\Omega
is contained in a half-plane and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega.

Then,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is abelian and does not contain any non-trivial idempotent.

Unlike the case of the hyperbolic semiflows (see Theorem
3.6
) there are situations in which one can derive the functional commutant property for composition operators embedded in parabolic semiflows. In this respect, we introduce the following lemma, which is a straightforward adaptation of an argument exposed by Shapiro for linear fractional parabolic composition operators
[
25
, p. 862]
:

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function, and assume that

(4.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\infty,

where
\varphi_{n}=\underbrace{\varphi\circ\ldots\circ\varphi}_{n\text{ times}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, if
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi}),

\ker(C_{\varphi}-\lambda I)
is one-dimensional.

It is necessary to remark that, if (
4.1
) holds for a holomorphic map
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
embedded in a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, then it is of zero hyperbolic step
[
3
, Lemma 2.6]
, and its associated Koenigs domain is not contained in any horizontal half-plane.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1},
and let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain. Assume that it is contained in a half-plane and
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
. If

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\infty,

then
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
has the functional commutant property for
1\leq p<\infty
. In particular, there exist no non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Since
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\infty
, it follows that
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is of zero hyperbolic step and the eigenfunctions
e^{\lambda h}
has multiplicity one for
C_{\varphi}
for all
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})
by Lemma
4.7
. Then, if
X\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
, observe that

Xe^{\lambda h}=Xe^{-\lambda}C_{\varphi}e^{\lambda h}=e^{-\lambda}C_{\varphi}(Xe^{\lambda h}),

so we deduce that there exists a map
\nu_{X}:\sigma_{p}(\Delta\mid_{H^{p}})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
Xe^{\lambda h}=\nu_{X}Xe^{\lambda h}.
From now, it is enough to argue as in the proof of Theorem
4.5
to deduce the result.
∎

Let us deal now with another interesting consequence. The next result extendes the Montes-Rodríguez, Ponce-Escudero and Shkarin result for non-automorphic parabolic linear fractional maps discussed in Section
1
:

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a linear fractional non-automorphic parabolic map. Then,
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
does not commute with any non-trivial idempotent for all
1\leq p<\infty.

Proof.

By conjugation, we may assume that

\varphi_{a}(z)=\frac{(2-a)z+a}{-az+2+a},

where
\textnormal{Re}(a)>0
. This function
\varphi_{a}
can be easily shown to be embedded in a parabolic semiflow of the form
(\varphi_{at}(z))_{t\geq 0}
, whose Koenigs function is given by
h(z)=\frac{1}{a}\frac{1+z}{1-z}.
As a consequence, the Koenigs domain
\Omega
is a non-horizontal half-plane, so the hypotheses of Theorem
4.8
on
\Omega
are verified.

Then, if we show that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\infty,

we can apply Theorem
4.8
and the proof will be done. We have that
\varphi_{n}(0)=\frac{na}{2+na}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N},
so
|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\frac{n|a|}{\sqrt{4+4n\textnormal{Re}(a)+n^{2}|a|^{2}}}.
Hence, by considering
1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|
and multiplying by the conjugate of the numerator we obtain

1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|=\frac{4+4n\textnormal{Re}(a)}{4+4n\textnormal{Re}(a)+n^{2}|a|^{2}+n|a|\sqrt{4+4n\textnormal{Re}(a)+n^{2}|a|^{2}}}.

At this point, it is enough to apply the limit comparion test with the sequence
1/n
to deduce that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}1-|\varphi_{n}(0)|
diverges, as we wished. This ends the proof.
∎

On the other hand, there are some geometric configurations of the Koenigs domain of parabolic semigroups that lead to non-abelian commutants, which prevent
C_{\varphi}
to have the functional commutant property or minimal commutant.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be its Koenigs domain and assume it is contained in a horizontal half-plane of the form
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)>-\eta\},\eta>0
. Then,
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
is not abelian. In particular,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant property or minimal commutant.

Proof.

Let
h
be the Koenigs function associated to
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}.
Observe that, by the hypothesis on
\Omega,
the functions
e^{ixh}
belongs to
H^{\infty}
for every
x\geq 0.
Then, one may consider the multiplication operator
M_{e^{ixh}}.
Repeating the arguments of the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we deduce that (
3.3
) also holds for this situation, and the same ideas exposed in the aforementioned proof yields that
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
is not abelian.
∎

The previous result can be applied to show, for instance, that if
\varphi
is a parabolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
then
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
does not have abelian commutant for any
1\leq p<\infty.
Since it is an automorphism, it can be embedded in a parabolic group whose Koenigs domain is an upper or a lower half-plane, so the conditions of Theorem
4.10
are satisfied.

4.2.
Extended eigenvalues and strong compactness

We finish this section with two results about the extended eigenvalues and the strong compactness of composition operators whose symbol is embedded into a parabolic semiflow.

With respect to extended eigenvalues, one might consider different cases regarding the geometry of the point spectrum of the infinitesimal generator. We prefer to present a less general but simpler result, which will totally characterize the extended eigenvalues for some geometrical pictures of the Koenigs domain:

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that there exists a parabolic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\varphi=\varphi_{1}.
Let
\Omega
be the Koenigs domain and assume it is contained in a horizontal half-plane of the form
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\textnormal{Im}(w)>-\eta\},\eta>0
and that

is contained in a horizontal strip. Assume further
\textnormal{int}(\overline{\Omega})=\Omega
and

is connected. Then,

\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})=\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

As it was shown in the proof of Theorem
4.10
, the identity (
3.3
) holds for
C_{\varphi_{t}}
(
t\geq 0
) and
x\geq 0.
Moreover, using the same arguments that in the proof of Theorem

, we deduce that
\mathbb{T}\subseteq\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi}).
Finally, by Theorem
4.2
and (

) we deduce that
\sigma_{p}(C_{\varphi})=\mathbb{T}
, and applying Proposition
4.4
together with
[

, Lemma 2.6]
it follows that
\textnormal{Ext}(C_{\varphi})\subseteq\mathbb{T}
, and the proof is done.
∎

Finally, we deal with the strong compactness:

with

Let

5.
Elliptic semiflows

In this section, we deal with composition operators
C_{\varphi}
such that its symbol
\varphi
can be embedded in an elliptic semiflow. Recall that a holomorphic semiflow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is elliptic if there exists some
t_{0}>0
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
. Indeed, one can see (for instance,
[
4
, Remark 8.3.4]
) that
\varphi_{t}
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
for every
t\geq 0.
Therefore, our results will focus on the more general setting of holomorphic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
with fixed points in the unit disc, instead of considering just symbols
\varphi
that may be embedded in an elliptic semigroup.

It is not difficult to generate non-trivial idempotents (or orthogonal projections in the case
p=2
) lying in the bicommutant of
C_{\varphi}
whenever
\varphi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
, which, in particular, implies that
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant (or bicommutant) property. These kinds of results were first stated by Guyker
[
16
]
for
p=2.
We include here a proof of the existence of such idempotents for every
1<p<\infty
, for the sake of completeness of the manuscript.

To prove such a result, let us recall that, given
f\in H^{p}
and
g\in H^{q}
where
1<p<\infty
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, the operator
f\otimes g\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
is a rank-one operator that acts as

(f\otimes g)h=\langle g,h\rangle f.

It is straightforward to check that the operator
\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
is an idempotent, and in particular an orthogonal projection when
p=2.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
and consider
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{p})
,
1<p<\infty.
Then, there exist non-trivial idempotents lying in
{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime\prime}}.
As a matter of fact,
C_{\varphi}
does not have the functional commutant or bicommutant property.

Proof.

By conjugation, we may assume without loss of generality that the fixed point of
\varphi
in
\mathbb{D}
is
0
. Recall that, if
\varphi
is an automorphism, then
\varphi(z)=\omega z,
with
\omega\in\mathbb{T}.
We may also assume without loss of generality that
\textnormal{Arg}(\omega)
is not a rational multiple of
\pi
, since otherwise
C_{\varphi}
is scalar and has plenty of non-trivial idempotents in its bicommutant
[
29
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Thus, let us show that
\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
lies in the bicommutant of
C_{\varphi}.
Let
A\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
. We have

A\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}=(A\mathbbm{1})\otimes\mathbbm{1}\qquad\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}A=\mathbbm{1}\otimes(A^{*}\mathbbm{1}).

We are showing that
A\mathbbm{1}=\lambda\mathbbm{1}
and
A^{*}\mathbbm{1}=\overline{\lambda}\mathbbm{1}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, which will finish the proof. Recall that
C_{\varphi}\mathbbm{1}=\mathbbm{1}
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}\mathbbm{1}=\mathbbm{1},
and since
A\in{\{C_{\varphi}\}^{\prime}}
and
A^{*}\in{\{C_{\varphi}^{*}\}^{\prime}}
it follows that
A\mathbbm{1}\in\ker(C_{\varphi}-I)
and
A^{*}\mathbbm{1}\in\ker(C_{\varphi}^{*}-I).
Thus, if we show that the eigenvalue
1
has multiplicity one for both
C_{\varphi}
and
C_{\varphi}^{*}
we will be done. It will follow from the fact that if
A\mathbbm{1}=\lambda\mathbbm{1}
and
A^{*}\mathbbm{1}=\mu\mathbbm{1},
we would have

\lambda=\langle A\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\langle\mathbbm{1},A^{*}\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\langle\mathbbm{1},\mu\mathbbm{1}\rangle=\overline{\mu},

so
\mu=\overline{\lambda},
as we wanted. We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:

\varphi
is an automorphism. Then, there exists
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\varphi(z)=\omega z,
where we may assume
\textnormal{Arg}(\omega)
is not a rational multiple of
\pi.
In addition,
C_{\varphi}^{*}f=f(\overline{\omega}z).
If
C_{\varphi}f=f
or
C_{\varphi}^{*}f=f,
an easy computation involving Taylor series yields that
f
is constant, so this case is proved.

Case 2:

\varphi
is not an automorphism. Let us denote as
\varphi_{n}=\underbrace{\varphi\circ\ldots\circ\varphi}_{n\text{ times}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Now, by
[
4
, Proposition 1.8.3]
it follows that
\varphi_{n}\rightarrow 0
in compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}.
If
C_{\varphi}f=f,
then

f(\varphi_{n}(z))=(C_{\varphi_{n}}f)(z)=(C_{\varphi}^{n}f)(z)=f(z)

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.
As a consequence,
f(z)=f(0)
for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, so
f
is constant, as we wished. Now, let
f\in H^{q}
with
1/p+1/q=1
. Assume
C_{\varphi}^{*}f=f,
which implies
C_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}f=f
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then, for every
g\in H^{p}
we have

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle C_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}f,g\rangle=\langle f,C_{\varphi_{n}}g\rangle.

Now,
C_{\varphi_{n}}g
converges uniformly on compact subsets to
g(0)\mathbbm{1}
and
\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi_{n}}g\right\rvert\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\lvert C_{\varphi}^{n}g\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\left\lvert g\right\rvert\right\rvert
since
\varphi_{n}(0)=0
. These two facts, along with
[
8
, Chapter 20, Proposition 3.1.5]
, imply that
C_{\varphi_{n}}g
converges to
g(0)\mathbbm{1}
in the weak topology, so
\langle f,C_{\varphi_{n}}g\rangle\rightarrow\overline{g(0)}f(0),
which implies that
f
is constant, and the proof is done.
∎

At this point, it is worth noticing that in
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
it was shown that for a certain family of univalent symbols
\varphi
fixing a point in
\mathbb{D}
, the induced composition operators
C_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2})
have minimal commutants, so in particular they have the double commutant property. Nevertheless, as it is shown in Theorem

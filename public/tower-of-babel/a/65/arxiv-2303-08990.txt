Notes on the L ∞ L_{\infty} -approach to local gauge field theories
Maxim Grigoriev and Dmitry Rudinsky

Abstract
It is well known that a Q Q -manifold gives rise to an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra structure on the tangent space at a fixed point of the homological vector field. From the field theory perspective this implies that the expansion of a classical Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV) formulation around a vacuum solution can be equivalently cast into the form of an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra. In so doing, the BV symplectic structure determines a compatible cyclic structure on the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra. Moreover, L ∞ L_{\infty} quasi-isomorphisms correspond to so-called equivalent reductions (also known as the elimination of generalized auxiliary fields) of the respective BV systems. Although at the formal level the relation is straightforward, its implementation in field theory requires some care because the underlying spaces become infinite-dimensional. In the case of local gauge theories, the relevant spaces can be approximated by nearly finite-dimensional ones through employing the jet-bundle technique. In this work we study the L ∞ L_{\infty} -counterpart of the jet-bundle BV approach and generalise it to a more flexible setup of so-called gauge PDEs. It turns out that in the latter case the underlying L ∞ L_{\infty} -structure is analogous to that of Chern-Simons theory. In particular, the underlying linear space is a module over the space-time exterior algebra and the higher L ∞ L_{\infty} -maps are multilinear. Furthermore, a counterpart of the cyclic structure turns out to be degenerate and possibly nonlinear, and corresponds to a compatible presymplectic structure the gauge PDE, which is known to encode the BV symplectic structure and hence the full-scale Lagrangian BV formulation. Moreover, given a degenerate cyclic structure one can consistently relax the L ∞ L_{\infty} -axioms in such a way that the formalism still describes non-topological models but involves only finitely-generated modules, as we illustrate in the example of Yang-Mills theory.

Our main focus is the
L_{\infty}
-approach to local gauge theories. The manifestly local BV description of gauge field theories is well-known and operates in terms of the jet bundles. In this case the underlying BV configuration space is a suitable jet bundle equipped with the ghost degree and the BRST differential. The associated
L_{\infty}
-structure can be defined (at least locally in space-time) on sections of a natural graded vector bundle associated to a given solution (vacuum) of the underlying gauge theory. In so doing all the
L_{\infty}
-maps turn out to be multidifferential operators. The cyclic structure is determined by the BV symplectic structure.

A more flexible generalization of BV on jet bundles is known as gauge PDE or generalized AKSZ approach. In this approach a gauge theory is described as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded fiber bundle over

L_{\infty}
-counterpart of this framework. The underlying
L_{\infty}

T[1](\text{spacetime})
, and all the
L_{\infty}
-maps, save for the differential, are multilinear over the space-time exterior algebra while the differential is the de Rham differential + a linear piece.

Preliminaries

2.1
L_{\infty}
- algebras and gauge systems

Let
\EuScript{H}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\EuScript{H}:=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\EuScript{H}_{k}
, with the degree denoted by
\mathrm{gh}(\cdot)
, which is equipped with a collection
l_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,...
of multilinear maps:

l_{i}:\EuScript{H}^{\times i}\rightarrow\EuScript{H}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{gh}(l_{i})=1\,.

(2.1)

These maps are assumed to be graded symmetric and satisfying the generalized graded Jacobi identities. In particular, the first map is the differential
l_{1}:=\mathcal{D}
, while the second map
l_{2}
defines an algebra structure that satisfies a version of Jacobi identity modulo
\mathcal{D}
-exact terms. For instance if only
\mathcal{D}
and
l_{2}
are nonvanishing,
L_{\infty}
-algebra is just a differential graded Lie algebra (note that in the present exposition the degree is suspended by
1
with respect to the standard choice for differential graded Lie algebras). For the first three maps the generalized Jacobi identity reads as

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}(\phi))=0,

(2.2)

\mathcal{D}(l_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}))+l_{2}(\mathcal{D}(\phi_{1}),\phi_{2})+(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|}l_{2}(\phi_{1},\mathcal{D}(\phi_{2}))=0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle l_{3}(l_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}),\phi_{3})+(-1)^{|\phi_{2}||\phi_{3}|}l_{3}(l_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{3}),\phi_{2})+(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|(|\phi_{2}|+|\phi_{3}|)}l_{3}(l_{2}(\phi_{2},\phi_{3}),\phi_{1})

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}(l_{3}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}))+l_{3}(\mathcal{D}(\phi_{1}),\phi_{2},\phi_{3})+(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|}l_{3}(\phi_{1},\mathcal{D}(\phi_{2}),\phi_{3})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|+|\phi_{2}|}l_{3}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\mathcal{D}(\phi_{3}))=0,

while the general formula can be found in e.g.
[
10
,
11
]
. The first of the above relations implies that
\mathcal{D}
is a differential and hence
\EuScript{H}
is a cohomological complex. The second says that
\mathcal{D}
is a derivation of
l_{2}
, while
the third implies that
l_{2}
defines a Lie algebra structure in the cohomology of
\mathcal{D}
.

The data of the
L_{\infty}
algebra encodes a gauge system or, more precisely, a gauge system formulated in terms of BV-BRST formalism, i.e. where in addition to equations of motion one specifies gauge generators, gauge for gauge generators and higher gauge generators. An element
\varphi\in\EuScript{H}_{0}
is called a solution if

\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,1}\frac{1}{i!}l_{i}(\varphi,\dots,\varphi)=0,

(2.5)

so that the above relations can be regarded as equations of motion. It is easy to check that the following transformation

\delta_{\epsilon}\varphi:=\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,0}\frac{1}{i!}l_{i+1}(\epsilon,\varphi,\dots,\varphi)\,,

(2.6)

where
\epsilon
is a generic element of
\EuScript{H}_{-1}
, which is identified as a gauge parameter, is a symmetry of(
2.5
). Analogously, one defines gauge for gauge symmetries

\delta_{\epsilon_{-1}}\epsilon:=\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,0}\frac{1}{i!}l_{i+1}(\epsilon_{-1},\varphi,\dots,\varphi)\,,

(2.7)

where
\epsilon_{-1}
is a generic element of
\EuScript{H}_{-2}
, regarded as a gauge for gauge parameter. Generalization to higher gauge symmetries is straightforward.

It is important to note that the above representation of equations of motion and gauge transformations is not fully-applicable to systems containing fermionic fields and/or fermionic gauge parameters. To incorporate fermions one equips
\EuScript{H}
with an addition structure –
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
Grassman degree which defines a graded symmetry properties of the maps
l_{i}
. In what follows, we assume that
|-|
denotes the Grassmann degree. In particular, if fermions are not present the Grassmann degree is simply the ghost degree modulo 2. However, the subtlety is that if fermions are present it is difficult to interpret
\varphi\in\EuScript{H}_{0}
as a classical field configuration because components of
\varphi
are real numbers (recall that
\EuScript{H}
is a linear space) rather then anticommuting generators of the Grassmann algebra. Recall, that at the classical level fermionic fields are described by anticommuting variables. Nevertheless, at the purely algebraic level all the information of the underlying gauge system is captured by the
L_{\infty}
-maps. Moreover, fermionic fields naturally appear if one passes to the associated BV-BRST description, as we will recall in the next Section.

In applications, we are mostly interested in Lagrangian systems which, in this approach, correspond to cyclic
L_{\infty}
algebras. By definition
(\EuScript{H},l_{i})
is cyclic if it is equipped with a bilinear form:

\kappa:\EuScript{H}\otimes\EuScript{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptgh(\kappa)=-1\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\kappa(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(-1)^{(|\phi_{1}|+1)(|\phi_{2}|+1)}\kappa(\phi_{2},\phi_{1})\,,

(2.8)

compatible with the
L_{\infty}
-structure in the following way:

\kappa(\phi_{1},l_{i}(\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{i+1}))=(-1)^{|\phi_{1}||\phi_{2}|}\kappa(\phi_{2},l_{i}(\phi_{1},\phi_{3},\dots,\phi_{i+1}))\,.

(2.9)

Note that
\kappa(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\neq 0
iff the degree of
\phi_{1}
is equal to
1-|\phi_{2}|
, therefore
(-1)^{(|\phi_{1}|+1)(|\phi_{2}|+1)}=1
and then
\kappa
is in fact symmetric, i.e.
\kappa_{AB}=\kappa_{BA}
, where
\kappa(e_{A},e_{B})=\kappa_{AB}
is a matrix of bilinear form and
e_{A}
denotes a basis in
\EuScript{H}
. Note that one can equivalently regard
\kappa
as a symplectic structure of degree
-1
. Such an interpretation appears useful in discussing the relation to BV formalism.

Given a cyclic
L_{\infty}
algebra
\EuScript{H}
one can define a formal power series
S[\varphi]
on
\EuScript{H}_{0}
as follows

S[\varphi]:=\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,1}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\kappa(\varphi,l_{i}(\varphi,\dots,\varphi))\,.

(2.10)

It is interpreted as an action functional of the underlying gauge theory. In particular, (
2.5
) describes the stationary points of (
2.10
) while (
2.6
) describes its gauge symmetries.

Example 2.1
.

As an instructive example let us consider the
L_{\infty}
-formulation of Chern-Simons theory, see e.g.
[
9
,
14
]
. Suppose we have a compact orientable manifold
X
,
dim(X)=3
and denote by
(\Omega^{\bullet}(X),d)
its de Rham complex. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real Lie algebra equipped with an invariant inner product. We take as
\EuScript{H}=\Omega^{\bullet}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{g}[1]
, i.e. differential forms with values in
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. For instance, an element
\varphi\in\EuScript{H}_{0}
can be written as
\varphi=dx^{\mu}\varphi^{a}_{\mu}t_{\alpha}
, where
t_{\alpha}
denote a basis in
\mathfrak{g}[1]
.

The
L_{\infty}
maps are defined as follows:

l_{1}(\phi):=\mathsf{d}\phi,\hskip 20.00003ptl_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):=(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|}[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}]\,,

(2.11)

where
[\phi_{1},\phi_{2}]
denotes a Lie bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
extended to
\EuScript{H}
by linearity over
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
. The
L_{\infty}
relations are satisfied thanks to
d^{2}=0
, graded Leibnitz rule for
d
, and the Jacobi identity for
\mathfrak{g}
.
The cyclic structure on
\EuScript{H}
is defined as

\kappa(\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):=(-1)^{|\phi_{1}|}\int_{X}{\rm Tr}(\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2})\,,

(2.12)

where
{\rm Tr}
denotes an invariant bilinear product in
\mathfrak{g}
extended to
\EuScript{H}
by linearity over
\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
. It is symmetric for elements with degree
|\phi_{1}|+|\phi_{2}|=1
and equal to zero for others, i.e. (
2.12
) has the same symmetry as (
2.8
).

In this way we have arrived at the cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\left(\EuScript{H},l_{i},\kappa\right)
, where
l_{i}
are given by (
2.11
). The associated classical action (
2.10
) takes the following form:

S_{CS}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}<\varphi,\mathsf{d}\varphi+\frac{1}{3}[\varphi,\varphi]>.

(2.13)

and is precisely the action of Chern-Simons theory. The equations of motion (
2.5
) read as:

\mathsf{d}\varphi+\frac{1}{2}[\varphi,\varphi]=0\,,

(2.14)

while the gauge transformations are as follows:

\delta_{\epsilon}\varphi=\mathsf{d}\epsilon+[\varphi,\epsilon],

(2.15)

with
\epsilon\in\EuScript{H}_{-1}=\Omega^{0}(X)\otimes\mathfrak{g}[1]
.

Let us note that the assignment of ghost degree that we employed in the above example is not so common in the literature, though
[
15
]
. An alternative is to suspend the degree by 1 so that the ghost degree is simply the form degree, see e.g.
[
28
,
13
,
14
]
. Although our choice is slightly unnatural in this example it is more useful for explaining the relation with the BV formulation.

2.2
L_{\infty}
-morphisms and minimal models

Natural morphisms of
L_{\infty}
algebras are those that preserve the structure up to homotopy. What is important is that
L_{\infty}
- morphism is not a linear map in general. More specifically,
\chi:(\EuScript{H},l_{i})\rightarrow(\EuScript{H}^{\prime},l_{i}^{\prime})
between two
L_{\infty}
-algebras
(\EuScript{H},l_{i})
and
(\EuScript{H}^{\prime},l^{\prime}_{i})
is a set of multilinear graded antisymmetric maps
\chi_{i}:\EuScript{H}^{\times i}\rightarrow\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
of degree
0
. The conditions that these maps satisfy look rather cumbersome but, as we are going to see in the next section, can be compactly written in the language of
Q
-manifolds so that we refrain from giving their explicit form here and refer instead to e.g.
[
21
,
11
]
.

The
L_{\infty}
-morphism
\chi
is called
quasi-isomorphism
if the map
\chi_{1}:\EuScript{H}\rightarrow\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
is a quasi-isomorphism of the underlying complexes, i.e.
H^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(\EuScript{H})\cong H^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}(\EuScript{H}^{\prime})
.
It is important to note that just like complexes of vector spaces quasi-isomorphic
L_{\infty}
-algebras are not necessarily isomorphic as linear spaces and among quasi-isomorphic
L_{\infty}
-algebras there is a minimal one, called minimal-model
[
29
,
30
]
. More precisely:

Definition 2.1
.

A minimal model of
(\EuScript{H},l_{i})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
(\EuScript{H}^{\prime},l^{\prime}_{i})
such that
(\EuScript{H},l_{i})
and
(\EuScript{H}^{\prime},l^{\prime}_{i})
are quasi-isomorphic and

l^{\prime}_{1}=0
.

In particular, as a linear space
\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(\EuScript{H})
.

Let us briefly recall how the minimal model, i.e. maps
l^{\prime}_{i\geq 2}
defined on
\EuScript{H}^{\prime}=H^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(\EuScript{H})
, can be found for a given
L_{\infty}
-algebra. To this end let us define the following three linear maps: the first two are the embedding
\mathbf{e}:\EuScript{H}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\EuScript{H}
(recall that
\EuScript{H}^{\prime}\cong H^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(\EuScript{H})
) and the projection
\mathbf{p}:\EuScript{H}\twoheadrightarrow\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
, which are both of degree
0
and satisfy
\textbf{p}\circ\textbf{e}=\text{id}
, where
id
is an identity map on
\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
. The third map is a
contracting homotopy

\textbf{h}:\EuScript{H}\rightarrow\EuScript{H}
that has degree
-1
and satisfies:

\text{id}_{\EuScript}{H}-\textbf{e}\circ\textbf{p}=\textbf{h}\circ\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{D}\circ\textbf{h}\,.

(2.16)

The quasi-isomorphism
\chi
between
(\EuScript{H},l_{i})
and
(\EuScript{H}^{\prime},l^{\prime}_{i\geq 2})
is now determined by the following maps (where we only give explicitly some of them)

\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}):=\textbf{e}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}),

(2.17)

\chi_{2}(\phi^{\prime}_{1},\phi^{\prime}_{2}):=-(\textbf{h}\circ l_{2})(\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}),\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{2}))

(2.18)

\displaystyle\chi_{3}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}.\phi^{\prime}_{2},\phi^{\prime}_{3}):=-\sum_{Permutations}\frac{1}{2!}(\textbf{h}\circ l_{2})\left((\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}),\chi_{2}(\phi^{\prime}_{2},\phi^{\prime}_{3}))+(\chi_{2}(\phi^{\prime}_{1},\phi^{\prime}_{2}),\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{3})\right)-

(2.19)

\displaystyle-\sum_{Permutations}\frac{1}{3!}(\textbf{h}\circ l_{3})(\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}),\chi_{2}(\phi^{\prime}_{2}),\chi_{3}(\phi^{\prime}_{3}))

where
\phi^{\prime}_{1},\phi^{\prime}_{2},\phi^{\prime}_{3}\in\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
.
The
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\EuScript{H}^{\prime}
is determined as

l^{\prime}_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}):=0,\hskip 20.00003ptl^{\prime}_{2}(\phi^{\prime}_{1},\phi^{\prime}_{2}):=(\textbf{p}\circ l_{2})(\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{1}),\chi_{1}(\phi^{\prime}_{2})),\hskip 20.00003pt\ldots

(2.20)

where, again, we only gave the first two maps. All higher maps are defined recursively in a similar way, see e.g
[
11
]
.

3
Q-manifolds and
L_{\infty}
-algebras

In this section we recall the basics of
Q
-manifolds and the relation between
Q
-manifolds and
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

3.1
Q
-manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

A
Q
-manifold is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded supermanifold endowed with an odd vector field
Q
of degree
1
such that
[Q,Q]=2Q^{2}=0
. Such a vector field is called homological.

Let us give here a couple of standard examples:

Example 3.1
.

Given a (graded) manifold
M
, the shifted tangent bundle
T[1]M
is naturally a
Q
-manifold, with the
Q
-structure being the de Rham differential
d_{M}=\theta^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
where
x^{i},i=1,\cdots,d
are coordinates on
M
and
\theta^{i}
are coordinates on the fibers of
T[1]M
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. Consider
\mathfrak{g}[1]
as
\mathbb{Z}
-graded supermanifold. Consider a
Q
-field
Q=-\frac{1}{2}f^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}c^{\beta}c^{\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{\alpha}}
,
where
c^{\alpha}
are linear coordinates on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
and
f^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}
the structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
. The nilpotency of
Q
-field is equivalent to the Jacobi identity in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Natural maps of
Q
-manifolds, sometimes called
Q
-maps, are maps of the underlying graded supermanifolds that respect the
Q
structure, i.e.
\phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
satisfies
\phi^{*}\circ Q_{2}=Q_{1}\circ\phi^{*}
. The
Q
-map condition encodes the properties of
L_{\infty}
-morphism
[
8
]
. For more details on morphisms of graded manifolds see e.g.
[
31
]
.

Q
-manifolds give an alternative way to describe gauge systems. Roughly speaking
Q
-manifold is a geometrical object that corresponds to a BV formulation, where a symplectic structure is not specified or even does not exist. From the field theory perspective this corresponds to describing gauge systems at the level of equations of motion. Let us recall how the data of a gauge system is encoded in a given
Q
-manifold. For future convenience we do it in geometrical terms following
[
32
]
. An explicit description in terms of coordinates was initially in
[
15
,
33
]
.

Solutions of the underlying gauge system, described by
(M,Q)
, can be identified as points of the body of
M
, where
Q
vanishes. These can be identified as
Q
maps from a trivial
Q
-manifold
(pt,0)
to
(M,Q)
. In other words
\sigma:pt\rightarrow M
is a solution if

\sigma^{*}\circ Q=0\,.

(3.1)

In other words
\sigma(pt)
is a fixed point of
Q
. Gauge transformation of a given map
\sigma
can be defined as

\delta_{\epsilon_{\sigma}}\sigma^{*}=\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}\circ Q,

(3.2)

where the gauge parameter map
\epsilon_{\sigma}:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1}
has degree
-1
and satisfies

\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(fg):=\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(f)\sigma^{*}(g)+(-1)^{|f|}\sigma^{*}(f)\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(g),

(3.3)

for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
. In fact it is enough to consider gauge parameters of the following form:
\xi^{*}_{\sigma}=\sigma^{*}\circ Y
, where
Y
is a vector field on
M
of degree
-1
so that (
3.3
) is satisfied by construction. It is easy to check that (
3.2
) defines an infinitesimal deformation of the solution
\sigma
. Gauge for gauge symmetries can be defined in analogously.

3.3
Symplectic/cyclic structures and BV formalism

From the geometrical perspective BV system is a symplectic
Q

-manifold

(3.11)

In other words,

If

Let us now turn to the extra structure induced by

on

for

-structure,

Other way around, given a cyclic

4.1
Jet-bundle BV description

A systematic framework to describe local gauge theories such that the locality is realized in a manifest way is provided by the jet-bundle BV approach.

Let us briefly recall the basics of jet bundles. Given a smooth locally-trivial fiber bundle
\mathcal{F}\to X
, it defines its associated jet bundle
J^{k}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow X
for any integer
k\,{\geqslant}\,0
, which is also a bundle over
J^{k-1}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow X
(note that
J^{0}(\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}
). It is useful to work with an infinite jet bundle limit
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
which is the projective limit of
J^{k}(\mathcal{F})
as
k\to\infty
. As functions on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
one takes local functions, i.e. those that arise from finite jet bundle (i.e. depend on finite number of coordinates). By some abuse of notations we denote local functions by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}))
). Any section
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{F}
has a canonical jet prolongation
\bar{\alpha}:X\to J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
. There is the canonical Cartan distribution
\mathcal{C}\subset T(J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}))
generated by tangent spaces to prolonged sections. In local coordinates, it is generated by vector fields, called total derivatives,

D_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\psi^{A}_{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}+\psi^{A}_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}_{\nu}}+\dots,

(4.1)

where
x^{\mu},\psi^{A}_{\mu\ldots}
are standard local coordinates on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
induced by local coordinates
x^{\mu}
on
X
and local coordinates
\psi^{A}
on the fibers of
\mathcal{F}
. If
\bar{\alpha}
is a prolongation of
\alpha:X\to\mathcal{F}
then for any local function
f
one has
(\bar{\alpha}^{*}(D_{\mu}f))=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}(\bar{\alpha}^{*}(f))
. Evolutionary vector fields are those preserving Cartan distribution. For instance, a vertical evolutionary
V
satisfies
[D_{\mu},V]=0
. The algebra of local differential forms on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
is bigraded by horizontal and vertical form degree. Accordingly, de Rham differential decomposes as
d=\mathrm{d_{h}}+\mathrm{d_{v}}
. In local coordinates
\mathrm{d_{h}}=dx^{\mu}D_{\mu}
. Further details and original references can be found in e.g.
[
49
,
50
]
.

In the jet-bundle BV approach
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded fiber bundle over space-time manifold
X
,
\dim{X}=n
, whose sections are fields, ghosts (for ghosts), and antifields. The information about the equations of motion and (gauge for) gauge symmetries is encoded in the vertical evolutionary vector field
s
, called BRST differential, defined on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
and satisfying

\frac{1}{2}[s,s]=s^{2}=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{gh}(s)=1\,,\hskip 20.00003pt[D_{\mu},s]=0\,.

(4.2)

In what follows we refer to the above data as to a local BV system at the level of equations of motion. In the case of Lagrangian BV systems, in addition to
s
,
bundle
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
is equipped with a closed
(n,2)
-form
\omega
of ghost degree
-1
which is nondegenerate on vertical evolutionary vector fields. The systematic formulation of Lagrangian BV in terms of jet bundles was put forward in
[
41
,
51
]
. The equation of motion version was proposed in
[
15
,
52
]

The data of a local BV system encode the equations of motion and the gauge (for gauge) symmetries of the underlying gauge system. More specifically, a section
\sigma:X\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
is called a solution if its jet prolongation
\bar{\sigma}:X\rightarrow J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
satisfies

\bar{\sigma}^{*}\circ s=0,

(4.3)

where
\bar{\sigma}^{*}
denotes the pullback map determined by
\bar{\sigma}
. In other words solutions belong to the zero locus of
s
. Note that
\bar{\sigma}^{*}
preserves the ghost degree and hence for a function
f
of nonvanishing ghost degree one gets
(\bar{\sigma}^{*})(f)=0
, simply because on
X
there are no functions of nonvanishing degree.

To describe gauge transformations of a given section
\sigma
let
\epsilon
be a vertical vector field on
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathrm{gh}(\epsilon)=-1
and
\bar{\epsilon}
be its prolongation to
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
.
It follows,
{\epsilon_{\sigma}}^{*}:C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}))\to C^{\infty}(X)
defined by
{\epsilon_{\sigma}}^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ\bar{\epsilon}

satisfies
\bar{\epsilon}^{*}(fg):={\epsilon}^{*}(f){\sigma}^{*}(g)+(-1)^{|f|}{\sigma}^{*}(f)\bar{\epsilon}^{*}(g)
for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}))
and hence defines an infinitesimal gauge transformations of
\bar{\sigma}^{*}
:

\delta{\bar{\sigma}}^{*}=\bar{\epsilon}_{\sigma}^{*}\circ s\,.

(4.4)

It is easy to check that
\bar{\sigma}^{*}+\bar{\epsilon}_{\sigma}^{*}\circ s
is again a prolongation of a solution, indeed
\bar{\epsilon}_{\sigma}^{*}(sD_{\mu}f))=\bar{\sigma}^{*}(\bar{\epsilon}(sD_{\mu}f))=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}\bar{\epsilon}_{\sigma}^{*}(sf))
because
\bar{\epsilon}
is evolutionary and
\bar{\sigma}
is a prolongation of
\sigma
. In a similar way one defines gauge for gauge symmetries. In the case of a Lagrangian BV system the above definition reproduces the equations of motion and gauge transformation encoded in the BV master action. The component form of the above construction of equations of motion and gauge transformations in terms of
s
can be found in
[
15
]
, see also
[
33
]
.

4.2
L_{\infty}
description of local BV systems

We now turn to
L_{\infty}
-structure underlying a local BV system. Consider a vertical bundle
V\mathcal{F}:=ker(\pi_{*})\subset T\mathcal{F}
, where
\pi_{*}
is the differential of
\pi:\mathcal{F}\to X
.
Given a fixed solution
\sigma_{0}:X\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
it determines a pullback bundle
\sigma^{*}_{0}(V\mathcal{F})
over
X
. The fiber of
\sigma^{*}_{0}(V\mathcal{F})
over
x\in X
is a vertical subspace of
T_{\sigma_{0}(x)}E
. In this way we get a graded
vector
bundle
\sigma^{*}_{0}(V\mathcal{F})
over
X
, which we denote as
\mathcal{N}
for brevity. The space of sections
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
is a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
. Locally, it is given by functions on
X
with values in a graded vector space, the typical fiber of
\mathcal{N}
.

Let us restrict to local analysis and introduce new local coordinates on
\mathcal{F}
such that the chosen solution
{\sigma}_{0}
is given by
\psi^{\prime A}=0
. In the associated jet-bundle coordinates
\psi^{\prime A}_{\mu\ldots}
its prolongation
\bar{\sigma}_{0}
is defined by
\psi^{\prime A}_{\mu\ldots}=0
. The expansion of the BRST-differential
s
into the sum of terms homogeneous in
\psi^{\prime A}_{(\mu)}
reads as:

s=s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\dots,

(4.5)

where
s_{k}
is the term of total homogeneity degree
k-1
. For example the linear term
s_{1}
is determined by its action on
\psi^{A}
(here and below we omit the prime):

s_{1}(\psi^{A})=\Omega^{A}_{B}(x)\psi^{B}+\Omega^{A|\mu}_{B}(x)\psi^{B}_{\mu}+\Omega^{A|\mu\nu}_{B}(x)\psi^{B}_{\mu\nu}+\ldots.

(4.6)

This, in turn, is determined by a finite order linear differential operator:
\Omega:\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
so that

s_{1}\Psi=\Omega\Psi
, where
\Psi=\psi^{A}e_{A}
is a distinguished linear function on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{F})
with values in
\mathcal{N}
and where we assumed that
\Omega
acts on fields by total derivatives. Let us give a component expression of the action of
\Omega
on a given section
v=e_{A}v^{A}(x)
:

\Omega(v)=\Omega(e_{A}v^{A})=e_{B}\Omega^{B}_{A}(x,\partial_{x})v^{A}(x),

(4.7)

where
e_{A}
denotes a local frame of
\mathcal{N}
induced by a coordinate system
\psi^{A}
. Note that nilpotency of
s_{1}
implies
\Omega^{2}=0
so that
\Omega
can be understood as a BRST-operator for some first-quantized system, see e.g.
[
15
]
.

In a similar way,
s_{2}
is determined by

s_{2}(\psi^{A})=\frac{1}{2}\left(I^{A}_{BC}(x)\psi^{B}\psi^{C}+2I^{A|\mu}_{BC}(x)\psi^{B}_{\mu}\psi^{C}+I^{A|\mu\nu}_{BC}(x)\psi^{B}_{\mu}\psi^{C}_{\nu}+\dots\right),

(4.8)

where dots denote terms quadratic in
\psi^{A}_{\dots}
and terms involving higher derivatives. Again,
s_{2}
is one to one with a bilinear graded-symmetric differential operator
I_{2}:\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\otimes\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
. More precisely, if
\Psi^{M}
is a collective notation for fiber coordinates
\psi^{A}_{\mu\ldots}
we can write
s_{2}\psi^{A}=(I_{2})^{A}_{MN}\Psi^{M}\Psi^{N}
with
(I_{2})^{A}_{MN}=(-1)^{|M||N|}(I_{2})^{A}_{NM}
, defining
I_{2}
via
I_{2}(v,w)=e_{A}(I_{2})^{A}_{MN}\bar{v}^{*}(\Phi^{M})\bar{w}^{*}(\Phi^{N})
, where
\bar{v},\bar{w}
are prolongations of sections
v,w:X\to\mathcal{N}
and
\Phi^{M}
are fiber coordinates on
J^{\infty}(\mathcal{N})
defined in the same way as
\Psi^{M}
. It is instructive to decompose the multiindex
N
as
(A,(\mu))
and to write
I_{2}
explicitly as:

I_{2}(v,w)=I_{2}(e_{B}v^{B},e_{C}w^{C})=e_{A}(I_{2})^{A}_{(B(\mu)),(C(\nu))}(x)\partial_{(\mu)}v^{B}(x)\partial_{(\nu)}w^{C}(x)\,.

(4.9)

Proceeding as above, we arrive at the collection of multilinear graded-symmetric differential operators:

\Omega:\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\,,\hskip 20.00003ptI_{n\,{\geqslant}\,2}:\Gamma^{\otimes n}(\mathcal{N})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\,.

(4.10)

Strictly speaking they are defined only locally, i.e. for sections on the coordinate patches of
X
. This gives an
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
over a coordinate patch. On the overlap of patches the respective
L_{\infty}
structures are related by an
L_{\infty}
-isomorphism.

In terms of
\mathcal{N}
the field configurations can be identified with degree zero sections of
\mathcal{N}
(if fermionic fields are present this identification requires some care) and the condition that
\varphi\in\Gamma^{(0)}(\mathcal{N})
is a solution takes the standard form:

\Omega\varphi+\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,2}\frac{1}{i!}I_{i}(\varphi,\ldots,\varphi)=0\,.

(4.11)

Similarly, a gauge transformation of
\varphi\in\Gamma^{(-1)}(\mathcal{N})
can be also written in the form (
2.6
) as

\delta_{\epsilon}\varphi=\Omega\,\epsilon+\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,2}\frac{1}{(i-1)!}I_{i}(\epsilon,\varphi,\dots,\varphi),

(4.12)

where
\epsilon\in\Gamma^{(-1)}(\mathcal{N})
is a gauge parameter. and
\varphi\in\Gamma^{(0)}(\mathcal{N})
is a degree zero section. In a similar way we can define gauge for gauge transformations. Parameters of such transformations are elements of
\Gamma^{(-k)}(\mathcal{N})
, with
k\,{\geqslant}\,2
.

In contrast to equations of motion and gauge symmetries the extension of the action functional (
2.10
) to the local field theory setup is straightforward only at the formal level. The problem is that the cyclic structure (BV symplectic form) involves integration over
X
and is well defined on sections with compact support only, while interesting field configurations often do not belong to this class. In the local field theory setup the standard way out is to work in terms of Lagrangians rather than action functionals. As we are going to see and discuss in more details in Section
4.5
, the Lagrangians correspond to fiberwise (pre)symplectic structures.

To summarise, the Taylor expansion of BRST-differential
s
around some fixed solution gives us the
L_{\infty}
-structure on the space of sections
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
of the graded vector bundle
\mathcal{N}
induced by the chosen solution. All the structure maps are given by multilinear graded symmetric differential operators of ghost degree 1 and, in general, are defined only locally.

4.3
Gauge PDEs

A more flexible and invariant way to describe local gauge theories is based on the approach developed in
[
15
,
53
,
32
]
. In this approach a general local gauge theory is represented in the special 1st order form, generalizing the AKSZ formulation of topological models and the unfolded formalism of higher spin theories. More precisely, a gauge theory is encoded in the geometrical object known as gauge PDE
[
32
]
.

More specifically, a gauge PDE is a locally trivial fiber bundle
\pi:E\to T[1]X
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded supermanifolds such that
\pi^{*}\circ\mathrm{d_{X}}=Q\circ\pi^{*}
, where
\mathrm{d_{X}}
is the de Rham differential of
X
, seen as a vector field on
T[1]X
. In addition it is required to be locally equivalent to a non-negatively graded gauge PDE and to satisfy some regularity conditions.

Let us spell out explicitly the structure of
Q
in general coordinates. Let
(x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu},\psi^{A})
be coordinates adapted to a local trivialization (
x^{\mu}
originate from coordinates on
X
and
\psi^{A}
on the typical fiber). Using
\pi^{*}\circ\mathrm{d_{X}}=Q\circ\pi^{*}

one finds that
Q
has the following form

Q=\theta^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+q^{A}(x,\theta,\psi)\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}.

(4.13)

In the case if the trivialisation can be chosen such that
q^{A}=q^{A}(\psi)
the
Q
-bundle is locally-trivial and we are dealing with the globalization of an AKSZ sigma model (with possibly infinite-dimensional target space). See
[
32
,
53
]
for further details.

It is easy to see that gauge PDEs encode gauge systems. Indeed, field configurations are identified with sections
\sigma:T[1]X\to E
. Solutions are
Q
-sections, i.e. sections satisfying

\mathrm{d_{X}}\circ\sigma^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ Q,

(4.14)

The gauge transformation of a given section
\sigma
is given by

\delta\sigma^{*}=\mathrm{d_{X}}\circ\epsilon^{*}+\epsilon^{*}\circ Q\,,

(4.15)

where
\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}:C^{\infty}(E)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]X)
has degree
-1
, satisfies
\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(fg):=\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(f)\sigma^{*}(g)+(-1)^{|f|}\sigma^{*}(f)\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(g)
and

\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}(\pi^{*}\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]X)
. The latter condition exclude gauge transformations involving
X
reparameterisations because such transformations generally transform sections to generic maps
T[1]X\to E
. Nevertheless they preserve equations (
4.14
) and can be regarded as gauge symmetries of the parameterised version of the theory, see
[
53
]
. Note that it is sufficient to consider gauge parameters of the following form
\epsilon_{\sigma}^{*}=\sigma^{*}\circ Y
, where
Y
,
\mathrm{gh}(Y)=-1
is a vertical vector field on
E
, in which case all the conditions are satisfied automatically.

4.4
L_{\infty}
description of gauge PDEs

We now describe the general construction of
L_{\infty}
-structure associated to generic gauge PDEs. Suppose we are given with a generic gauge PDE
(E,T[1]X,Q)
and let
\sigma_{0}:T[1]X\rightarrow E
be a fixed solution. Just like in the analogous discussion of local BV systems
\sigma_{0}
defines a vector bundle
\mathcal{N}=\sigma_{0}^{*}(VE)
but now it is a graded vector bundle over
T[1]X
. Recall that
VE:=ker(\pi_{*})\subset TE
, where
\pi:E\to T[1]X
is a canonical projection. The typical fiber of
\mathcal{N}
is just the typical tangent space to a fiber of
E
. Note that
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
is naturally a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]X)
which, in turn, can be seen as an exterior algebra of
X
.

Just like in the analysis of local BV systems it is convenient to introduce new coordinates
(x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu},\psi^{A})
such that
\sigma_{0}^{*}(\psi^{A})=0
. Expanding
Q
in
\psi^{A}
gives:

Q=\theta^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}+\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,1}\frac{1}{i!}\psi^{B_{1}}\cdots\psi^{B_{i}}q^{A}_{B_{1}\cdots B_{i}}(x,\theta)\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}\,,

(4.16)

where coefficients
q^{A}_{B_{1}\cdots B_{i}}(x,\theta)
are graded-symmetric in lower indexes. A more detailed expression for the linear term in
Q
reads as

Q_{1}=\psi^{B}\Omega^{A}_{B}(x)\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}+\theta^{\mu}\left(\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}$}\kern 1.0pt}+(-1)^{|\psi^{B}|}\psi^{B}q^{A}_{\mu B}(x)\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}\right)+\cdots,

(4.17)

where dots denote terms of higher orders in
\theta
. It is clear that the second term
determines a linear connection. The nilpotency of
Q_{1}
then implies

\Omega^{A}_{B}(x)\Omega^{B}_{C}(x)=0.

(4.18)

It also follows from
Q^{2}_{1}=0
that the linear connection determined by
Q_{1}
is flat modulo
\Omega
-exact terms. In particular, it induces a flat connection in the cohomology of
\Omega
in
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
.

Let us now focus on the space of sections
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
of
\mathcal{N}
, which is a
graded module
over
C^{\infty}(T[1]X)
(recall that
\mathcal{N}
is a vector bundle over
T[1]X
). If we restrict ourselves to local analysis, as we do in what follows,
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
is isomorphic to differential forms on
X
with values in the graded vector space
\mathcal{F}
, which is a typical fiber of
\mathcal{N}
. As a degree in
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
we take the sum of the form degree and the ghost degree in
\mathcal{F}
, which, in turn, is induced from the degree in the fibers of

.

The homological vector field

induces a linear map

. To define it in coordinate-free terms let us identify
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
as the quotient of the vertical vector fields on
E
, which we denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{V}(E)
, modulo those that vanish on
\sigma_{0}(T[1]X)
seen as a submanifold of

. This quotient is clearly a module over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]X)
. Then

can be defined as follows:

l_{1}(\alpha)=[([Q{,}\,v])]\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha=[v]\,,

In contrast to

the higher

Suppose we are given a graded Lie algebra

Example 4.1
.

Let us consider a trivial

Homological vector field (

l_{1}(v^{a}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt})=(\mathrm{d_{X}}v^{a}+\theta^{\mu}q_{\mu b}^{a}v^{b})\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptl_{2}(u,v)=(-1)^{|u|}U_{ab}^{c}u^{a}v^{b}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{c}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{c}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{c}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{c}}$}\kern 1.0pt}

Example 4.2

l_{1}v=(\mathrm{d_{X}}v^{A}+(-1)^{|v|}\theta^{\mu}q^{A}_{\mu B}v^{B})\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}

(4.36)

where for simplicity we assumed

Example 4.3

Let

This defines a Weyl star product for functions on

We then extend our space by Grassmann odd ghost variable
c

gh(c)=1
and take
\mathcal{A}
to be the algebra of formal power series in
\hbar

that are polynomials in
p_{a},c
with coefficients in formal power series in

\hbar

Consider a graded manifold
M_{\mathcal{A}}
associated with
\mathcal{A}[1]

\Psi(y,p,c)=\textbf{A}(y,p)+c\textbf{F}(y,p)

The ghost degree prescription is such that

and hence

,

q\Psi=-\frac{1}{2\hbar}[\Psi{,}\,\Psi]_{*}\,.

(4.38)

We then take the following gauge PDE:

(E,Q)=(M_{\mathcal{A}},q)\times(T[1]X,\mathrm{d_{X}})
, where

(in this case it is an AKSZ sigma model). In the component terms

Q(x^{\mu})=\theta^{\mu},\hskip 10.00002ptQ(\textbf{A})=-\frac{1}{2\hbar}[\textbf{A},\textbf{A}]_{*},\hskip 10.00002ptQ(\textbf{F})=-\frac{1}{\hbar}[\textbf{A},\textbf{F}]_{*}.

(4.39)

If

\sigma^{*}(\textbf{A})=\theta^{\mu}{A}_{\mu}(x|y,p)\equiv A(x,\theta|y,p)
and
\sigma^{*}(\textbf{F})={F}(x|y,p)\equiv F(x|y,p)

\mathrm{d_{X}}A+\frac{1}{2\hbar}[A,A]_{*}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{d_{X}}F+\frac{1}{\hbar}[A,F]_{*}=0\,,

(4.40)

The gauge transformations read as

\delta A=\mathrm{d_{X}}\lambda+\frac{1}{\hbar}[A,\lambda]_{*},\hskip 20.00003pt\delta F=\frac{1}{\hbar}[F,\lambda]_{*}\,,

(4.41)

where

is a gauge parameter. These equations are familiar in the context of Fedosov deformation quantization of cotangent bundles
[

,

and in the higher spin theory context
[

]

[

]
(see also
[

]

We now pick a particular solution and spell-out explicitly the emerging

A_{0}=\theta^{\mu}\left(e^{a}_{\mu}p_{a}+\omega^{a}_{\mu b}y^{b}p_{a}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptF_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{ab}p_{a}p_{b},

(4.42)

where

are identified as, respectively, the vielbein, the Lorentz connection, and the standard Minkowski metric. The equation of motion imply:

\mathrm{d_{X}}e^{a}+\omega^{a}_{b}e^{b}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{d_{X}}\omega^{a}_{b}+\omega^{a}_{c}\omega^{c}_{b}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\omega^{a}_{c}\eta^{cb}+\eta^{ac}\omega^{b}_{c}=0\,,

(4.43)

so that the underlying geometry is flat and is that of a Minkowski space. Introducing new fiber coordinates (such that the vacuum solution (

\textbf{A}=0,\textbf{F}=0

in the fiber coordinates

\begin{gathered}Q(\textbf{A})=-\frac{1}{2\hbar}[\textbf{A},\textbf{A}]_{*}-\theta^{\mu}\left(e^{a}_{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}+\omega^{a}_{\mu b}y^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}-\omega^{a}_{\mu b}p_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{b}}\right)\textbf{A},\\

(4.44)

Applying the general procedure we infer an underlying

-structure on the section of
\mathcal{N}
. In the case at sections of
\mathcal{N}
are differential forms on

with values in

l_{1}(\Phi)=\left(\nabla+\sigma-cp^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}\right)\Phi,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003ptl_{2}(\Phi,\Phi)=\frac{(-1)^{|\Phi|}}{2\hbar}[\Phi{,}\,\Phi]_{*}\,,

(4.45)

where
\Phi=\Phi(x,\theta|y,p,c)
and

\nabla=\theta^{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\omega^{a}_{\mu b}y^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}+\omega^{a}_{\mu b}p_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{b}}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma=-\theta^{\mu}e^{a}_{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{a}}\,.

(4.46)

The pair

4.5
Lagrangians and presymplectic structures

So far we have focused on the description of local gauge systems at the level of equations of motion. Now we would like to briefly discuss how Lagrangian local BV systems can be described in the gauge PDE approach. It turns out that the BV Lagrangian and the BV symplectic structure can be encoded in a compatible presymplectic structure defined on the gauge PDE.

More specifically, let
(E,Q,T[1]X)
be a gauge PDE. Vertical differential forms on
E
are equivalence classes of forms modulo the ideal
\mathcal{I}
in the exterior algebra, which is generated by elements of the form
\pi^{*}\alpha
,
where
\alpha
is a form of positive form degree on
T[1]X
. Note that the notion is general and applies to generic fiber bundles as well. Note also that the de Rham differential and the Lie derivative along projectable vector fields are well defined on equivalence classes (vector field
Y
on
E
projects to
y
on
T[1]X
if
\pi^{*}\circ y=Y\circ\pi^{*}
).

By definition a compatible presymplectic structure on
(E,Q,T[1]X)
is a closed vertical 2-form
\omega
of ghost degree
\dim{X}-1
satisfying
d\omega=0
and
L_{Q}\omega\in\mathcal{I}
. We also fix a presymplectic potential
\chi
such that
\omega=d\chi
and assume that
i_{Q}L_{Q}\omega=0
. It follows there exists
\mathcal{L}
such that

i_{Q}\omega+d\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{I}
and we have all the ingredients to
define BV action as

S_{BV}[\widehat{\sigma}]=\int_{T[1]X}\widehat{\sigma}^{*}(\chi)(\mathrm{d_{X}})+\widehat{\sigma}^{*}(\mathcal{L})\,,

(4.48)

where
\widehat{\sigma}
is a super-section of
E\to T[1]X
. In this form the expression is identical to that of the AKSZ action but the present framework is more general. The classical action is obtained by replacing
\widehat{\sigma}
with
a section
\sigma
or, equivalently, setting to zero all the nonvanishing ghost degree coordinates on the space of supersection.

The interpretation of the above BV-AKSZ-like action is that one should take a symplectic quotient of the space of supermaps for the AKSZ-like presymplectic structure, induced by
\omega
on the space of super-sections, giving a genuine local BV system whose interpretation is standard. Of course one should assume that the induced presymplectic structure is regular. For further details on the presymplectic gauge PDEs we refer to
[
78
,
79
]
(see also
[
80
,
81
,
82
]
for earlier relevant works).

As we have already seen, given a fixed solution
\sigma_{0}
to
(E,Q,T[1]X)
the space of sections of the associated vector bundle
\mathcal{N}=\sigma_{0}^{*}(VE)
is equipped with
L_{\infty}
-structure (as before we restrict to local analysis). It is clear that
\omega
defines a fiberwise symplectic form
\omega_{0}
on
\mathcal{N}
.
In fact it also defines a higher multilinear maps (higher jets of
\omega
). More precisely, working in coordinates
x^{\mu},\theta^{\mu},\psi^{A}
such that
\sigma^{*}_{0}(\psi^{A})=0

the presymplectic form can be written as

\omega=\frac{1}{2}d\psi^{A}d\psi^{B}\omega_{AB}(x,\theta,\psi),

(4.49)

and its Taylor expansion in fiber coordinates reads as:

\omega=\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\dots=\sum_{i\,{\geqslant}\,0}\omega_{i},

(4.50)

where a superscript denotes homogeneity in the fiber coordinates. While
\omega_{0}
is a bilinear (over
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(T[1]X)
) form on
\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
,
\omega_{k>0}

can be interpreted as higher multilinear maps
\Gamma^{\otimes(k+2)}(\mathcal{N})\to\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
. Note that, in general, these maps are defined only locally and depend on the choice of trivialisation.

The compatibility conditions,
\omega_{i}
ought to satisfy, are obtained by expanding
L_{Q}\omega\in\mathcal{I}
. In particular, for the linear operator
\bar{\Omega}=l_{1}-\mathrm{d_{X}}
determined by the linear part of
Q
one gets:

\begin{gathered}\omega_{0}(\bar{\Omega}V,W)+(-1)^{|V|}\omega_{0}(V,\bar{\Omega}W)=(\mathrm{d_{X}}\omega_{0})(V,W)\,,\\
(\mathrm{d_{X}}\omega_{0})(V,W)=\mathrm{d_{X}}(\omega_{0}(V,W))-\omega_{0}(\mathrm{d_{X}}{V},W)-(-1)^{|V|}\omega_{0}(V,\mathrm{d_{X}}{W})\,.\end{gathered}

(4.51)

The first relation is naturally interpreted as the
\bar{\Omega}
-invariance of the cyclic structure modulo total derivatives.

Before giving a simple example of this structure let us note that the above interpretation of presymplectic gauge PDEs suggests that all the coordinates which are in the kernel of
\omega
can be safely ignored as the respective fields never show up in the BV master action. This suggests the notion of a weak presymplectic gauge PDE, where the condition
Q^{2}=0
is replaced with
\omega(Q,Q)=0
, see
[
79
]
for more details. In the
L_{\infty}
language this corresponds to ”pre-
L_{\infty}
” structure encoded by such
Q
and possibly degenerate compatible bilinear forms
\omega_{i}
. Note that for such weak presymplectic gauge PDEs the solutions are defined as those of the Lagrangian system with action (
4.48
), i.e.
\sigma_{0}
is a solution if
\sigma_{0}^{*}(\omega_{AB})(\mathrm{d_{X}}\sigma_{0}^{*}(\psi^{B})-\sigma_{0}^{*}(Q^{B}))=0
.

Example 4.4
.

Consider as
\mathcal{N}
a trivial vector bundle over
T[1]X
, with
X
a Minkowski space, with the fiber being
\left((X^{*}\wedge X^{*})\oplus\mathbb{R}[-1]\right)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a reductive Lie algebra equipped with the nondegenerate invariant inner product denoted by
{\rm Tr}
. A generic section of this bundle can be written as:

\Phi=C(x,\theta)\otimes e_{1}+F_{\mu\nu}(x,\theta)\otimes e_{0}^{\mu\nu}

(4.52)

where
e_{1},\mathrm{gh}(e_{1})={-1}
and
e^{\mu\nu}_{0},\mathrm{gh}(e^{\mu\nu}_{0})=0
denote basis in
\mathbb{R}[-1]
and
X^{*}\wedge X^{*}
respectively,
and where by some abuse of notations
F_{\mu\nu},C
are
\mathfrak{g}
-valued.

Thanks to the linearity over
\theta^{\mu}
it is enough to define pre-
L_{\infty}
maps for sections of the form
\alpha(x)e_{1}

and
\beta_{\mu\nu}(x)e_{0}^{\mu\nu}
:

\begin{gathered}l_{1}(\alpha e_{1})=(\mathrm{d_{X}}\alpha)\,e_{1}\,,\qquad l_{1}(\beta_{\mu\nu}e^{\mu\nu}_{0})=(\mathrm{d_{X}}\beta_{\mu\nu})\,e^{\mu\nu}_{0}-\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\beta_{\mu\nu}e_{1}\\
l_{2}(\alpha e_{1},\gamma e_{1})=-[\alpha{,}\,\gamma]e_{1}\,,\qquad l_{2}(\beta_{\mu\nu}e^{\mu\nu}_{0},\alpha e_{1})=2[\beta_{\mu\nu}{,}\,\alpha]e_{0}^{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad l_{2}(\beta_{\mu\nu}e^{\mu\nu}_{0},\gamma_{\rho\sigma}e^{\rho\sigma}_{0})=0\,.\end{gathered}

(4.53)

Here and below we drop the tensor product sign.
Note that
l_{1},l_{2}
does not satisfy the Leibniz rule. For instance for
v=B_{\mu\nu}(x)e^{\mu\nu}_{0}
one gets

l_{1}(l_{2}(v,v)+l_{2}(l_{1}(v),v)+l_{2}(v,l_{1}(v))=\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}[B_{\mu\nu},B_{\rho\sigma}]e^{\rho\sigma}_{0}
. As we are going to see the above
l_{1},l_{2}
are determined by a
Q
-structure whose square is not equal to zero.

The symplectic structure is field-independent, i.e.
\omega=\omega_{0}
and is given by

\omega(\alpha e_{1},\beta e_{1})=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\omega(\alpha e_{0}^{\mu\nu},\beta e_{0}^{\rho\sigma})=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\omega(\alpha e_{0}^{\mu\nu},\beta e_{1})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}{(\alpha\beta)}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho\sigma}\theta^{\rho}\theta^{\sigma}\,,

(4.54)

where
\alpha,\beta
are generic
\mathfrak{g}
-valued functions in
x,\theta
and for simplicity we restricted ourselves to
dim(X)=4
, extension to generic dimension
>1
is straightforward. Now, the analog of an
L_{\infty}
-action functional can be written as

S[\Phi^{(0)}]=\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}\omega(\Phi^{(0)},l_{1}\Phi^{(0)})+\frac{1}{3!}\omega(\Phi^{(0)},l_{2}(\Phi^{(0)},\Phi^{(0)}))\right)\,.

(4.55)

where
\Phi^{(0)}=A_{\mu}(x)\theta^{\mu}e_{1}+B_{\mu\nu}(x)e_{0}^{\mu\nu}
is a component of
\Phi
of total ghost degree
0
. In terms of component fields one gets (modulo total derivative):

S[A,B]=\int d^{4}x{\rm Tr}\left(B^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+[A_{\mu}{,}\,A_{\nu}]\right)-\frac{1}{2}B^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}),

(4.56)

which is a standard first-order action of the YM theory, see e.g.
[
83
]
.

Let us for completeness give a description of the above system as a presymplectic gauge PDE. The underlying bundle
E
is just
\mathcal{N}
where the fibers are now understood as graded manifolds rather than graded linear spaces. Denoting the fiber coordinates associated to
e_{1}
and
e^{\mu\nu}_{0}
by
C
and
F_{\mu\nu}
respectively, the pre-
Q
-structure is defined as

\begin{gathered}Q(x^{\mu})=\theta^{\mu},\quad Q(C)=-\frac{1}{2}[C,C]+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}F_{\mu\nu},\qquad Q(F_{\mu\nu})=[F_{\mu\nu},C]\end{gathered}

(4.57)

and the presymplectic structure is given by
[
80
]
:

\omega=d\chi\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\chi=\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}(\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\theta^{\rho}\theta^{\sigma}F^{\mu\nu}\,dC)\,.

(4.58)

Note that
Q^{2}\neq 0
but the weaker condition holds:
\omega(Q,Q)=0
, see
[
79
]
. It is easy to check that with this choice of
E,X,Q,\chi
action (
4.48
) indeed gives first-order action (
4.56
). Taking symplectic quotient of the space of super-sections results in the full-scale BV formulation of the YM theory, see
[
79
]

Finally, the

Appendix A
Globalisation of the
L_{\infty}
structure

Here we briefly recall the Fedosov-like construction for cotangent bundles and show that it can be used to obtain a globally well-defined curved
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\Gamma(TM)
induced by the homological vector field
Q
on
M
.

Let
M

be a graded supermanifold and let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free affine connection in
TM
.
Consider the vector bundle
W(M)=\widehat{S}(T^{*}M)\otimes TM
, whose fiber we identify with a Lie algebra of formal vector fields on the tangent space. Here
\widehat{S}(T^{*}M)
denotes a completion of the graded symmetric tensor algebra of
T^{*}M
. There is a natural Lie bracket
[-{,}\,-]_{W}
on
\Gamma(W)
determined by the fiberwise Lie bracket of formal vector fields. To make contact with the version
[
84
,
74
]
(see also
[
34
,
85
,
75
]
for applications in the field theory context) of Fedosov quantization
[
86
]

specialised to the case of cotangent bundles, note the identification of
\Gamma(W)
with a Lie subalgebra of
\widehat{S}(T^{*}M)\otimes S(TM)
, equipped with the standard fiberwise Weyl star-product.

The standard fact is that there exists a flat nonlinear connection
D
in
W
of the following form

D=\nabla+[r{,}\,\cdot]_{W}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptD(Dv)=0\,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall v\in\Gamma(W)

(A.1)

where
\nabla
is the covariant differential in
\Gamma(W)
determined by
\Gamma
extended to
W
and
r
is a
W
-valued
1
-form on
M
. The existence of such
r
is a simple corollary of the Fedosov-like construction for the cotangent bundles: in this case the minimal Fedosov-like connection takes values in
W
seen as subbundle of the Weyl bundle (i.e. is linear in momenta).

Furthermore, Any section
v_{0}
of
TM
has a unique extension to
v\in\Gamma(W)
such that
Dv=0
and
v|_{TM}=v_{0}
, where
v|_{TM}
denotes a projection of
v
to
\Gamma(TM)
seen as a direct summand in
\Gamma(W)
. Moreover,

\left([v{,}\,u]_{W}\right)\big|_{TM}=[v_{0}{,}\,u_{0}]\,,

(A.2)

where
[v_{0}{,}\,u_{0}]
is the standard Lie bracket on
\Gamma(TM)
. Note that
D[v{,}\,u]_{W}=0
because by construction
Dv=0=Du
and both
\nabla
and
[r{,}\,-]_{W}
differentiate
[-{,}\,-]_{W}
.

Let
v_{0},u_{0}
are two vector fields on
M
such that
[u_{0}{,}\,v_{0}]=0
. Their unique covariantly constant lifts
v,u
satisfy
[v{,}\,u]_{W}=0
because
D[v{,}\,u]_{W}=0
and
[v_{0}{,}\,w_{0}]=0
by the assumption. If
M
is a
Q
-manifold, we apply this to
v_{0}=u_{0}=Q
and find that
[q{,}\,q]_{W}=0
, where
q\in\Gamma(W)
is a unique lift of
Q
satisfying
Dq=0
. At the same time, this is equivalent to having a formal homological vector field on each fiber of
TM
, giving a curved-
L_{\infty}
structure on
\Gamma(TM)
.

Appendix B
Notations and conventions

Consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
\EuScript{H}:=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\EuScript{H}_{k}
. Elements of subspace
\EuScript{H}_{k}
are called homogeneous of degree
k
, and we shall denote the Grassmann parity by
|\phi|
, where
\phi\in\EuScript{H}
. The degree shift by
k
is defined as:
(\EuScript{H}[k])_{l}=\EuScript{H}_{k+l}
.
For example, if
\EuScript{H}
is a real vector space, then the degree-shifted vector space
\EuScript{H}[1]
consists of vectors degree
-1
while coordinates on the associated graded manifold
\mathscr{M}_{\EuScript}{H}
are of degree
1
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a
Z
-graded supermanifold. We use the Koszul sign convention, i.e. the graded commutativity reads as
ab=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba
. Vector fields on
\mathscr{M}
act on functions as left derivations, i.e.
V(fg)=(Vf)g+(-1)^{|f|}f(Vg)
. Let
\psi^{A}
be a local coordinates on
\mathscr{M}
then vector filed
V
can be written as
V=V^{A}(\psi)\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}$}\kern 1.0pt}
.

It is convenient to identify differential form on
\mathscr{M}
as functions on the odd tangent bundle
T[1]\mathscr{M}
, which are polynomial in fiber coordinates. Introducing coordinates
(\psi^{A},d\psi^{A})
, where
\psi^{A}
are base coordinates,the coordinate expression for the de Rham differential reads as

df(\psi)=d\psi^{A}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\psi^{A}},

(B.1)

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\psi^{A}}
denotes the left derivative.

The components components of a differential form are introduced as follows:

\alpha(\psi,d\psi)=\frac{1}{n!}d\psi^{A_{n}}\dots d\psi^{A_{1}}\alpha_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}(\psi).

(B.2)

It follows the graded antisymmetry property of components is determined by
d\psi^{A}d\psi^{B}=(-1)^{(|A|+1)(|B|+1)}\\
d\psi^{B}d\psi^{A}
. For example, the components of
2
-form have the following symmetry property:

\omega_{AB}=(-1)^{(|A|+1)(|B|+1)}\omega_{BA}.

(B.3)

The coordinate expression for the interior product reads as

i_{V}=V^{A}(\psi)\frac{\partial}{\partial d\psi^{A}},

(B.4)

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial d\psi^{B}}d\psi^{A}=\delta^{A}_{B}
. It is clear that
Vf=i_{V}df
for any function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
. Note that
|i_{V}|=|V|-1
.
The components of
\alpha
can be expressed as follows

\alpha_{A_{1}\dots A_{n}}=i_{A_{1}}\dots i_{A_{n}}\alpha(\psi,d\psi),\hskip 10.00002pti_{A}=\frac{\partial}{\partial d\psi^{A}}.

(B.5)

The Lie derivative is defined by

L_{V}=i_{V}d+(-1)^{|V|}di_{V}\,.

(B.6)

Note that
|L_{V}|=|V|
.

Introduction

Finding bounds of the eigenvalue for the Laplacian on a given manifold is a key aspect in Riemannian geometry. Aa an example, studying eigenvalues that appear as solutions of the Dirichlet or Neumann boundary value problems for curvature functions, is interesting topic in geometric analysis. In recent years, because of the theory of self-adjoint operators, the spectral properties of linear Laplacian studied extensively. As an important example, mathematicians generally are interested in the spectrum of the Laplacian on compact manifolds with or without boundary or noncompact complete manifolds due to in these two cases the linear Laplacians can be uniquely extended to self-adjoint operators (see

Since the study of the properties of spectrum of Laplacian (specially in Dirichlet condition) in infinitely stretched regions has applications in elasticity, electromagnetism and quantum physics, it attracts attention of many mathematicians and physicists. Recently Mao has proved the existence of discrete spectrum of linear Laplacian on a class of

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Consider the radial functions
\bar{u}_{\epsilon},\bar{v}_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{S}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{p}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}\left(r,.\right)|^{p}dv_{can},

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\epsilon}^{q}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|v_{\epsilon}\left(r,.\right)|^{q}dv_{can},

where
V
stands for
{\rm vol}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m-1},can\right)
. By taking derivative with respect to

\displaystyle p\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{p-1}\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\epsilon}=\frac{p}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p-2}u_{\epsilon}\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial r}dv_{can},

and the similar context holds for

and

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{p-1}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p-1}|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial r}|dv_{can}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{V}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial r}|^{p}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which concludes that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|\bar{u}^{\prime}_{\epsilon}|^{p}\leq\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}}{\partial r}|^{p}dv_{can}\leq\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|du_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}.

Since
\frac{\alpha+1}{2}+\frac{\beta+1}{2}=1
, Hölder’s inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|v_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|v_{\epsilon}|^{q}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1}{q}},

and again by Hölder’s inequality we see

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|\bar{v}_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}

\displaystyle=V.\int_{0}^{\pi}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|\bar{v}_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}\sin r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle=V.\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{V}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|v_{\epsilon}|^{q}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1}{q}}\sin r^{m-1}\Big]dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\alpha+1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|v_{\epsilon}|^{q}dv_{can}\right)^{\frac{\beta+1}{q}}\sin r^{m-1}\Big]dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\pi}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|v_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}\right)\sin r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|v_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}.

From (

), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p}dv_{can}

\displaystyle=V.\int_{0}^{\pi}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p}\sin r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}|du_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can}\Big]f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}\sin r^{m-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}|du_{\epsilon}|^{p}dv_{can},

and in the similar way

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}|\bar{v}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{q}dv_{can}\leq\int_{S^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}|dv_{\epsilon}|^{q}dv_{can}.

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}^{+}|^{q}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}^{-}|^{\alpha+1}|\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}^{-}|^{\beta+1}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}dv_{can}}\Big[\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}^{-}|^{p}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}^{-}|^{q}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}\Big].

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\epsilon,t}=t\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}^{+}+\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}^{-},

then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}can\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}dv_{can}}\Big[\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}|^{p}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}|^{q}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}dv_{can}}\Big[\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}|^{p}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}|d\tilde{v}_{\epsilon}|^{q}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m-p}{2}}dv_{can}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\epsilon\right).

Above inequalities with the Theorem
4.1
and
p\geq q
together give us

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}can\right){\rm vol}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}can\right)^{\frac{p}{m}}\geq V^{\frac{p}{m}}.\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\epsilon\right).\epsilon^{\frac{p}{m}}=\infty.

Now set

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}={\rm vol}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}can\right)^{-\frac{2}{m}}\tilde{f}_{\epsilon},

then we get

\displaystyle{\rm vol}\left(S^{m},h_{\epsilon}can\right)=1,

and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},h_{\epsilon}can\right)=\infty.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Someone may consider the situation
q<m<p
, in this case we just take the radial function
f_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{S}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}\left(r\right)=\epsilon^{\frac{4q}{m\left(m-q\right)}}.\chi_{[0,\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon]\cup[\frac{\pi}{2}+\epsilon,\pi]}\left(r\right)+\chi_{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon,\frac{\pi}{2}+\epsilon\right)}\left(r\right),

and then

\displaystyle R_{\epsilon}\left(u,v\right):=\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|u|^{\alpha+1}|v|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}}

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|du|^{p}dv_{can}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|dv|^{q}dv_{can}\Big],

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\epsilon\right)=\inf_{u,v\neq 0}\Big\{R_{\epsilon}\left(u,v\right)|\left(u,v\right)\in W_{0}^{1,p}\times W_{0}^{1,q}\setminus\{0\}\Big\}.

Now by the definition of
\bar{u}_{\epsilon}
and
\bar{v}_{\epsilon}
in the Theorem
4.1
and by Hölder’s inequality we see

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}dv_{can}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|u_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}dv_{can},

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|\bar{u}^{\prime}|^{p}dv_{can}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|du|^{p}dv_{can},

also the same way holds for
v
and
q
. These together give us

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{m}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}|^{\alpha+1}|\bar{v}_{\epsilon}|^{\beta+1}dv_{can}}\Big[\frac{\alpha+1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|\bar{u}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{p}dv_{can}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta+1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{m}}f_{\epsilon}^{\frac{q-m}{2}}|\bar{v}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}|^{q}dv_{can}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\lambda_{1,p,q}^{+},\lambda_{1,p,q}^{-}\}.

So by the same way as Theorem
4.1
we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\lambda_{1,p,q}\left(\epsilon\right).\epsilon^{\frac{q}{m}}=\infty.

It means that the same context as Corollary
4.2
holds in the case
q<m<p
.

5.
The
\left(p,q\right)
-Laplacian equation, a new characterization

One may consider the
\left(p,q\right)
-Laplacian equation as

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u+\Delta_{q}u=div\left(\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}+|\nabla u|^{q-2}\right)\nabla u\right),

for
1<q<p<\infty
and also

\displaystyle u\in W_{0}^{1,p}\left(M\right)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}\left(M\right).

Also it can be written as

(5.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta_{q}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,

where for arbitrary
v\in W_{0}^{1,p}\cap W_{0}^{1,q}
, it is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla vd\mu+\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\nabla vdv

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{M}|u|^{p-2}uvdv,

and
\lambda
is called its eigenvalue associated to the eigenvector
u
. Similar to the previous one, in this case the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the
\left(p,q\right)
-Laplacian equation (
5.1
) is defined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)=\inf_{u\neq 0}\Big\{\frac{1}{\int_{M}|u|^{p}dv}\left(\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{q}dv\right)|u\in W_{0}^{1,p}\left(M\right)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}\left(M\right)\setminus\{0\}\Big\}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider
M
as an
m
-dimensional compact manifold and
1<q<p\leq m
. If
\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the
\left(p,q\right)
-Laplacian equation (
5.1
), then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{|\frac{p}{2}-1|}\Big[m^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(V_{c}^{n}\left(M,[g]\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{m}}+m^{\frac{q}{m}}\left(V_{c}^{n}\left(M,[g]\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{m}}\Big].

Before giving the proof for this theorem we consider the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider
\phi
as same as Lemma
3.3
. If
\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the
\left(p,q\right)
-Laplacian equation (
5.1
), then

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{|1-\frac{p}{2}|}\Big[\int_{M}|d\psi|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi|^{q}dv\Big],

where
\psi=\gamma\circ\phi
and
\gamma\in G\left(n\right)
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)
we see

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\frac{\int_{M}|d\psi_{i}|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi_{i}|^{q}dv}{\int_{M}|\psi_{i}|^{p}dv},

thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\int_{M}|d\psi_{i}|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi_{i}|^{q}dv}{\int_{M}|\psi_{i}|^{p}dv}.

First, let
p\geq q\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}=|d\psi|^{p}.

Also in the similar way for
q
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{q}\leq|d\psi|^{q}.

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|\psi_{1}|^{2}=1
and the map
x\rightarrow x^{\frac{p}{2}}
is concave we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|\psi_{i}|^{p}\geq\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}=\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}},

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\Big[\int_{M}|d\psi|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi|^{q}dv\Big].

Now let
1<q\leq p<2
, since
|\psi_{i}|<1
and also
|\psi_{i}|^{2}\leq|\psi_{i}|^{p}
and

\displaystyle 1={\rm vol}\left(M,g\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|\psi_{i}|^{2}dv\leq\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|\psi_{i}|^{p}dv,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}=\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}|d\psi|^{p},

similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|d\psi_{i}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}=\left(n+1\right)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}|d\psi|^{q},

which finally give us

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{|1-\frac{p}{2}|}\Big[\int_{M}|d\psi|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi|^{q}dv\Big].

∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Consider
\phi:\left(M,g\right)\rightarrow\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},can\right)
as a conformal immersion. From Lemma
5.2
there exists
\gamma\in G\left(n\right)
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{|\frac{p}{2}-1|}\Big[\int_{M}|d\psi|^{p}dv+\int_{M}|d\psi|^{q}dv\Big].

By Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|d\psi|^{p}dv\leq\left(\int_{M}|d\psi|^{m}dv\right)^{\frac{p}{m}},

also the similar context holds for
q
. Since
\gamma\circ\phi:\left(M,g\right)\rightarrow\left(\mathbb{S}^{m},can\right)
is a conformal immersion and

\displaystyle\left(\gamma\circ\phi\right)^{*}can=\frac{|d\left(\gamma\circ\phi\right)|^{2}}{m}g,

we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}|d\left(\gamma\circ\phi\right)|^{p}dv

\displaystyle=m^{\frac{p}{2}}{\rm vol}\left(M,\left(\gamma\circ\phi\right)^{*}can\right)

\displaystyle\leq m^{\frac{p}{2}}\sup_{\gamma\in G\left(n\right)}{\rm vol}\left(M,\left(\gamma\circ\phi\right)^{*}can\right),

also the similar context holds for
q
. Therefore, these together and by taking
inf
respect to
\phi
we find that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p,q}^{D}\left(M\right)\leq\left(n+1\right)^{|\frac{p}{2}-1|}\Big[m^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(V_{c}^{n}\left(M,[g]\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{m}}+m^{\frac{q}{m}}\left(V_{c}^{n}\left(M,[g]\right)\right)^{\frac{q}{m}}\Big].

∎

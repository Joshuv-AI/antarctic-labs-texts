A free boundary Monge-Ampère equation and applications to complete Calabi-Yau metrics
Tristan C. Collins, Freid Tong, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. Let P P be a convex body containing the origin in its interior. We study a real Monge-Ampère equation with singularities along ∂ P \partial P which is Legendre dual to a certain free boundary Monge-Ampère equation. This is motivated by the existence problem for complete Calabi-Yau metrics on log Calabi-Yau pairs ( X , D ) (X,D) with D D an ample, simple normal crossings divisor. We prove the existence of solutions in C ∞ ​ ( P ) ∩ C 1 , α ​ ( P ¯ ) C^{\infty}(P)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{P}) , and establish the strict convexity of the free boundary. When P P is a polytope, we obtain an asymptotic expansion for the solution near the interior of the codimension 1 1 faces of ∂ P \partial P .

1.
Introduction

Suppose
X
is a Fano manifold (i.e.
-K_{X}>0
), and suppose that
D\in|-K_{X}|
is a divisor. The complement
Y=X\setminus D
admits a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
which has a simple pole along
D
and is naturally an open Calabi-Yau manifold of infinite volume. It is a natural question, dating back to the third author’s ICM address
[
19
]
to construct complete Calabi-Yau metrics on
Y
. Such metrics, particularly those with sub-Euclidean volume growth, are fundamental for understanding the collapsing behavior of compact Kähler-Einstein manifolds; see, e.g.
[
1
,
9
,
14
,
5
]
for some recent examples.

The first major result concerning the existence of complete Calabi-Yau metrics is due to Tian and Yau
[
15
,
16
]
, who addressed the case when
D
is smooth and irreducible. The work of Tian-Yau develops a robust package for solving the complex Monge-Ampère equation with “good” asymptotics, extending to the non-compact case the techniques pioneered by the third author in the solution of the Calabi conjecture
[
18
]
. These results can be used to produce complete Calabi-Yau metrics in any case where a good ansatz metric at infinity is known and many examples are found in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Understanding the existence of complete Calabi-Yau metrics when
D
is singular (eg. with simple normal crossings) is a long-standing problem in the field. Hein
[
8
]
constructed complete Calabi-Yau metrics on the complement of singular elliptic fibers in rational elliptic surfaces (for which
-K_{X}
is effective, but not ample) utilizing the semi-flat metrics discovered by Greene-Shapere-Vafa-Yau
[
7
]
. Recently, the first author and Li
[
4
]
constructed complete Calabi-Yau metrics on
X\setminus D
when
X
is Fano,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X>2
, and
D=D_{1}+D_{2}\in|-K_{X}|
is a simple normal crossings divisor with
D_{1}\equiv_{\mathbb{Q}}D_{2}
. In
[
4
]
a general inductive strategy was proposed for constructing complete Calabi-Yau metrics on general Fano manifolds
X
equipped with an snc anti-canonical divisor
D=D_{1}+\cdots D_{k}
for
k<\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
and
D_{i}
ample
1\leq i\leq k
. Roughly speaking, the basic idea of this inductive approach is that the Calabi-Yau metric on
X\setminus(D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k})
should asymptote near
D_{i}
to the complete Calabi-Yau metric on
D_{i}\setminus(\sum_{j\neq i}^{k}D_{j})
. Technically, the inductive approach amounts to solving a certain real Monge-Ampère equation with singularities along the boundary of a polytope and obtaining a complete asymptotic expansion for the solution near the boundary. Crucially, the leading sublinear term in this expansion must be related to the solution of a related Monge-Ampère equation in fewer variables. The purpose of this paper is to address the existence of solutions to this real Monge-Ampère equation and to show that near the generic point of the boundary, these solutions have leading order asymptotics determined by the asymptotics of the complete Calabi-Yau metrics constructed by Tian and Yau
[
15
]
.

To introduce our problem, let
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex set containing the origin and consider the following boundary value problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}v=v^{-(n+2)}(-v^{\star})^{-k}&\text{ in }P\\
v^{\star}=0&\text{ on }\partial P.\end{cases}

Here
v^{\star}
is the function defined by

v^{\star}(y):=\langle y,\nabla v(y)\rangle-v(y),

which can be interpreted as the composition of the Legendre transform of
v
with the gradient map
\nabla v
. Similar types of equations have been studied by the second and third authors in
[
17
]
, where the focus was on the Dirichlet problem. The boundary condition in (
1.1
) is the natural generalization of the boundary condition considered in
[
4
]
, and it asks for
v^{\star}
to vanish on the boundary. This new boundary condition is equivalent to a free-boundary problem when re-casted in terms of the Legendre transform. Indeed if we let
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Legendre transform of
v
,

u(x):=\sup_{y\in P}(\langle x,y\rangle-v(y)),

then the equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following equation for
u
,

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\det D^{2}u=(u^{\star})^{n+2}(-u)_{+}^{k}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\nabla u(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\overline{P},\end{cases}

where
\chi_{\{u<0\}}
is the characteric function of the set
\{u<0\}
. If we denote
\Omega=\{u<0\}
, then the equation (
1.2
) is elliptic inside
\Omega
, while it is degenerate outside
\Omega
. The set
\partial\Omega=\{u=0\}
behaves like a free-boundary, and knowledge of
u
inside
\Omega
determines
u
globally by taking the minimal convex extension of
u|_{\Omega}
.

Remark 1
.

There is a separate transformation that we can apply to equation (
1.2
). Namely, if we define new coordinates
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

z=\frac{x}{-u(x)}\text{ for }x\in\Omega

and a function of the new coordinate
\phi(z)
by

\phi(z)=\frac{1}{-u(x)},

then by
[
17
, Section 5]
, the function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the Monge-Ampère equation

\det D^{2}\phi=\frac{c}{\phi^{n+2+k}}.

This equation was studied by Klartag in
[
11
]
, where it was called the
k
-moment measure. Klartag solved this equation together with the second boundary condition. In our case, we would like
u
to satisfy a second boundary condition, which in terms of
\phi
is equivalent to the boundary condition

\left\{\frac{\nabla\phi(z)}{-\phi^{\star}(z)}:z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}=P.

Our first main theorem is the following existence result for the Monge-Ampère equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P
be an open convex set that contains the origin, then the equation (
1.1
) admits a solution
v\in C^{\infty}(P)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{P})
for some
\alpha>0
.

Remark 2
.

Notice that the right-hand side of the equation (
1.1
) is singular at the boundary, hence the solution cannot be
C^{2}
up to the boundary, and
C^{1,\alpha}
is the best one can hope for.

A corollary of this result is the solution of the following singular Monge-Ampère equation, which can be thought of as the solutions of an optimal transport problem between non-compact domains.

Corollary 1.1
.

The boundary-value problem

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}\det D^{2}\varphi=1\text{ in }(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n+1}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}=0\text{ on }\partial(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n+1}.\end{cases}

admits a positive homogenous solution
\varphi\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n+1}})\cap C^{\infty}((\mathbb{R}_{+})^{n+1})
.

The situation when
P
is a simplex is particularly important as it is related to the study of complete Calabi-Yau manifolds
[
4
]
and so we pay particular attention to the regularity of the free boundary in this case.
The boundary of a simplex
\partial P
has a stratification
\partial P_{0}\subset\partial P_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\partial P_{n-1}
where
\partial P_{k}
is the
k
skeleton consisting of the faces up to dimension
k
. In this case, we have the following theorem which describes the leading sublinear behavior of
v
at the top stratum of the boundary
\partial P_{n-1}\setminus\partial P_{n-2}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

In the case when
P
is a simplex, for any point
\mathbb{x}\in\partial P_{n-1}\setminus\partial P_{n-2}
, if we let
\vec{n}
be the inward-pointing normal vector to
\partial P
at
x
and define the local coordinate system
\tilde{x}_{n}=\vec{n}\cdot(x-\mathbb{x})
, and
(\tilde{x}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{n-1})
be orthogonal coordinates that’s tangential to
\partial P
, then in this coordinate system, one has the following expansion for
x
sufficiently close to
\mathbb{x}

(1.4)

v(x)=v(\mathbb{x})+\nabla v(\mathbb{x})\cdot(x-\mathbb{x})+p\tilde{x}_{n}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}P_{ij}(\tilde{x}_{i}-\mathbb{\tilde{x}_{i}})(\tilde{x}_{j}-\mathbb{\tilde{x}_{j}})+O\left(\left(|x-\mathbb{x}|^{2}+\tilde{x}_{n}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}\right)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)

for some
\alpha>0
, where
P_{ij}=D^{2}v(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{x}_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\tilde{x}_{j}})
is a positive definite matrix and
p>0
is a positive constant.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is via a min-max argument, while Theorem
1.2
is related to results of Jhaveri-Savin
[
10
]
on optimal transport with degenerate densities. This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we discuss the geometric origins of equation
1.1
and the connection with complete Calabi-Yau metrics. We also pose a conjecture concerning a Liouville theorem for solutions to a certain optimal transport problem between non-compact domains. In Section
3
we construct the functional whose critical points are formal solutions of (
1.1
). In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
5
we discuss regularity issues and prove Theorem
1.2
. Furthermore, we establish the strict convexity of the free boundary in general.

2.
Geometric motivation

In this section, we describe the relationship between the solution of the free-boundary problem (
1.1
) and complete Calabi-Yau metrics. Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n+1+k
and
D\in|-K_{X}|
an ample anticanonical divisor. Assume that
D=D_{1}+\ldots,D_{n+1}
is simple normal crossing with
D_{i}
ample. Then an important question is to ask whether
M=X\setminus D
admits a complete Calabi-Yau metric.

In
[
4
]
, it is realized that to construct such a model metric, one has to first construct the metric in the ”generic region”, which corresponds to a neighborhood of the top intersections
D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{n+1}
. The intersection
Z=D_{1}\cap\cdots\cap D_{n+1}
is a compact, connected Calabi-Yau manifold by the adjunction formula and the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem. Let us assume for simplicity that
D_{i}\in|d_{i}L_{0}|
for some ample line bundle
L_{0}\rightarrow X
(see
[
4
]
for discussion on the general case). A neighborhood of
Z\subset X
is modeled on a neighborhood of the zero section in the bundle

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n+1}L_{0}^{d_{i}}\rightarrow Z.

While such an identification is not holomorphic, the errors introduced by the identification are essentially negligible for the construction and we shall ignore them henceforth (see
[
4
]
for precise, quantitative discussion).
By the third author’s result
[
18
]
we can find a metric
h
on
L_{0}
, unique up to scaling, such that
-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log h=\omega_{CY}
, the unique Calabi-Yau metric on
Z
in the class
c_{1}(L_{0})|_{Z}
. Consider the real coordinates
x_{i}=-\frac{1}{d_{i}}\log|\sigma_{i}|_{h}
where
\sigma_{i}
is a local trivialization of
L_{0}^{d_{i}}
. We now look for a function
\phi
defined on
\{x_{i}\gg 1\forall i\}
such that
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
defines a complete Calabi-Yau metric. A straightforward calculation
[
4
]
shows
u
must satisfy

\quad D^{2}\phi>0,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}>0

and solve the real Monge-Ampère equation

(2.1)

\det(D^{2}\phi)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}=c

for some constant
c>0
, whose value may always be taken to be
1
by rescaling
u
. Note that for
n=0
, this equation reduces to the ODE whose solution is given by
\phi(x)=x^{\frac{k+2}{k+1}}
, which is precisely the asymptotics of the Tian-Yau metric
[
15
]
. It is natural to look for solutions of (
2.1
) which are homogeneous. An easy calculation shows that the natural scaling yields

\phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}\right)^{1+\frac{n+1}{n+k+1}}v\left(\frac{x_{1}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n+1}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}}\right)

If we define variables
y_{i}=\frac{(n+1)x_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{i}}-1
for
1\leq i\leq n
, then
v(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
is defined on
P=\cap_{i}\{-1\leq y_{i}\}\cap\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\leq 1\}
, the standard simplex with barycenter at the origin. The calculation in Proposition
5.1
shows that (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

\det(D^{2}v)=\frac{c}{(v)^{n+2}(-v^{\star})^{k}}.

which is precisely (
1.1
). The remaining question is how to impose boundary conditions on (
2.1
), or equivalently (
2.2
). This question was answered in the case
n+1=2
in
[
4
]
by the first author and Li by exploiting an inductive structure in the problem. The relevant boundary data, given in terms of
\phi
or
v
is

(2.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\big|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}=0,\text{ or, equivalently }v^{\star}\big|_{\partial P}=0.

We remark that (
2.3
) is the natural generalization of the boundary data introduced in
[
4
]
in the case
n+1=2
. However, it is not at all clear whether this boundary data is appropriate for
n+1>2
. The current work can be viewed as giving strong evidence that this choice of boundary data is correct. To illustrate this, let us briefly discuss the inductive nature of the existence problem for Calabi-Yau metrics on
X\setminus D
. We make the basic observation that if
J=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\subset\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
is an index set of length
\#J<n+1
, then

X_{J}:=\bigcap_{j\in J}D_{j}

is a Fano variety, and
D^{J}:=\sum_{i\in J^{c}}(D_{i}\cap X_{J})\in|-K_{X_{J}}|
is an anti-canonical divisor with simple normal crossings. In terms of the variables
(x_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})
, the region near
X_{J}
, but away from
X_{K}
for
\#K>\#J
corresponds to the region

\{\frac{x_{\ell}}{x_{j}}\ll 1:\ell\in J^{c},j\in J\}\cap\{\frac{x_{\ell}}{x_{j}}\sim O(1):\ell,j\in J\}

Inductively, it is natural to expect that the Calabi-Yau metric on
X_{J}
(assuming it exists) should determine the asymptotics of
\phi
near
X_{J}
. Note that in terms of the function
u
, the region near
X_{J}
corresponds to the region near the interior of the dimension
\#J^{c}
faces of
\partial P
. In the case that
\#J=n
,
X_{J}
is Fano of dimension
k+1
and
D^{J}
consists of a single smooth, anti-canonical divisor for which a Calabi-Yau metric exists by
[
15
]
. In this case, the inductive structure predicts that near
\{x_{i}\ll 1\}\cap\{x_{j}\gg x_{i}\,\,\forall j\neq i\}
we should have an expansion

\phi=a(x^{\prime})+b(x^{\prime})x_{i}+c(x^{\prime})x_{i}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+\text{ higher order}.

where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})
. This is precisely the expansion obtained in Theorem
1.2
. In the case considered in
[
4
]
, (
2.2
) reduces to an ODE and an expansion of this sort can be obtained directly from power series techniques. In the general case, it remains to address the regularity near the lower dimensional faces which seems to depend heavily on regularity theory for optimal transport with degenerate densities in polyhedral domains. In turn, such a regularity theory seems closely related to establishing regularity of the free boundary in (
1.2
). A key result would be to resolve the following conjectural Liouville theorem generalizing the results of
[
10
]
.

Conjecture 1

(Liouville Theorem)
.

Let
\varphi:(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a solution to the Monge-Ampere equation

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}\det D^{2}\varphi=1\text{ in }(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}=0\text{ on }\partial(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}.\end{cases}

Then
\varphi
is of the form

\varphi(x)=c+\sum_{i=l+1}^{l+m}v_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{l}P_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}+p\varphi_{l,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l})

where
p>0
and
c,v_{i}
are constants,
P_{ij}
is a positive-definite
l\times l
matrix and
\varphi_{l,k}
is the homogenous solution to

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial\varphi_{l,k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{k}\det D^{2}\varphi_{l,k}=1\text{ in }(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{l}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial\varphi_{l,k}}{\partial x_{i}}=0\text{ on }\partial(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{l}\end{cases}

obtained from Corollary
1.1
with
n+1=l
.

A different, but closely related question is to understand the behavior of degenerating Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. In the situation when the hypersurfaces degenerate into two pieces, the results of
[
14
,
9
]
give a complete description of the resulting behavior of the Calabi-Yau metrics. Recently, Y. Li has shown that when the hypersurfaces degenerate to
k
pieces for
k<n
, the potentials of the Calabi-Yau metrics converge to a solution of a different optimal transport problem on a simplex with degenerate densities
[
12
]
. Conjecture
1
also relates the solutions of (
1.3
) to the boundary behavior of such kind of optimal transport problem.

3.
Monge-Ampère functionals and their variations

Let
v:\overline{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a positive convex function and we denote by
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
its Legendre transform,

u(x):=\sup_{y\in\overline{P}}(\langle x,y\rangle-v(y)).

Note that since
v
is positive, we have

u(0)=-\inf v<0,

and therefore the set
\Omega=\{u<0\}
is a convex set containing the origin. Let us denote the set of all
v
by

\mathcal{C}^{+}(\overline{P})=\{v:\overline{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\big|v\text{ is convex }\}.

The Legendre dual of this class of functions is the class of convex functions

\mathcal{C}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\big|\phi_{P}-C\leq u<\phi_{P}\text{ for some }C>0\}

and
\phi_{P}
is the support function of
P
.

For any
v\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
, consider the functionals

I(v)=\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-u)_{+}^{k+1}(x)dx

and

J(v)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{P}\frac{dy}{(v(y))^{n+1}}.

Let
v_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{+}
be a smoothly varying family of functions, then the first variations of
I
and
J
are given by

\frac{d}{dt}I(v_{t})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dot{u}(-u)_{+}^{k}

and

\frac{d}{dt}J(v_{t})=-\int_{P}\frac{\dot{v}}{v^{n+2}}.

Lemma 1
.

The functional
J^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(v)
is concave function on
\mathcal{C}^{+}(\overline{P})
.

Proof.

Let
v_{t}=v_{0}+tv^{\prime}
be a linear path of functions in
\mathcal{C}^{+}(\overline{P})
. Then we can compute

(3.1)

\frac{d}{dt}J(v_{t})=-\int_{P}\frac{v^{\prime}}{v_{t}^{n+2}}

and

(3.2)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J(v_{t})=(n+2)\int_{P}\frac{(v^{\prime})^{2}}{v_{t}^{n+3}}.

It follows from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality that

\left(\frac{d}{dt}J(v_{t})\right)^{2}\leq\frac{n+1}{n+2}J(v_{t})\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}J(v_{t})\right),

which implies that
J^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}
is concave.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For any convex
v:\overline{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we have

(n+1)J(v)\geq\frac{|\{u<0\}^{\circ}|}{(-\inf u)}.

Proof.

If we assume
u
is smooth and strictly convex, then by
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have

\int_{\{u<0\}}\frac{-u\det D^{2}u}{(u^{\star})^{n+1}}=|\{u<0\}^{\circ}|.

From this, it follows by a change of variables formula that

\frac{|\{u<0\}^{\circ}|}{(-\inf u)}\leq\int_{\{u<0\}}\frac{\det D^{2}u}{(u^{\star})^{n+1}}=\int_{\nabla u(\{u<0\})}\frac{1}{v^{n+1}}\leq\int_{P}\frac{1}{v^{n+1}}.

For a general convex
u
which is not necessarily smooth and strictly convex, we can consider the following functions as a smooth approximation for
u
,

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=u\star\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon\phi(x)

where
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier with support
B_{\varepsilon}(0)
, and
\phi(x)>0
is a smooth, strictly convex function with
\nabla\phi(\mathbb{R}^{n})=B_{1}(0)
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and strictly convex function with
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=P_{\varepsilon}
where
P_{\varepsilon}=P+B_{\varepsilon}
and
+
is the Minkowski sum. If we let
v_{\varepsilon}(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\langle x,y\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x))
be the Legendre transform of
u_{\varepsilon}
, then
v
is smooth and strictly convex and we have

\int_{P_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}}\geq\frac{|\{u_{\varepsilon}<0\}^{\circ}|}{(-\inf u_{\varepsilon})}.

We would like to take the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the right-hand side converges to
\frac{|\{u<0\}^{\circ}|}{(-\inf u)}
. For the left-hand side, we note that since
u_{\varepsilon}(0)\to u(0)
,
v_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded from below and hence
\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}}
is uniformly bounded from above, we can also easily check that
\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}(y)}
converges to
\frac{1}{v^{n+1}(y)}
pointwise for all
y
, and hence by the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{P_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{v_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}}=\int_{P}\frac{1}{v^{n+1}}

and we are done.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

The function
f(x)=J(v-\langle x,\cdot\rangle)
is strictly convex function defined on a convex set
\Omega=\{u<0\}
that goes to
\infty
at
\partial\Omega
, and moreover,
f(x)
attains its minimum at a unique point
x_{0}\in\Omega
at which

(3.3)

\int_{P}\frac{y}{(v(y)-\langle x_{0},y\rangle)^{n+2}}dy=0.

Proof.

For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let us denote
v_{x_{0}}(y):=v(y)-\langle x_{0},y\rangle
. The Legendre transform of
v_{x_{0}}
is given by
u_{x_{0}}(x)=u(x+x_{0})
. For
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we have
u_{x_{0}}(0)<0
, therefore
v_{x_{0}}(y)\geq-u_{x_{0}}(0)>0
hence
f(x)
is well-defined and finite for
x\in\Omega
. We also have
\{u_{x_{0}}<0\}=\Omega-x_{0}
, hence by Lemma
2
,
f(x)
must go to
\infty
as
x
approach
\partial\Omega
. To see that
f(x)
is convex, let
v_{t}(y)=v(y)-t\langle x_{0},y\rangle
, and let
f(t):=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{P}\frac{1}{v_{t}^{n+1}}
, then

(3.4)

hence

is strictly convex. Since

is a strictly convex twice differentiable function defined on a convex set

∎

Let us define the space

\mathcal{C}_{n+2}(P):=\{v:\overline{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\big|\int_{P}\frac{y}{v^{n+2}}dy=0\}

Proposition 3.2
.

For any convex

, we have

J(v)\leq\frac{n+2}{n+1}\frac{|\Omega^{\circ}|}{|u(0)|}.

Proof.

By the definition of

where
\phi_{\Omega}(x)=\sup_{y\in\Omega}\langle x,y\rangle
is the support function of

. Then we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle J(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{P}v(x)^{-n-1}\,dx

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup\{\phi_{\Omega}(x),|u(0)|\}^{-n-1}\,dx

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus|u(0)|\Omega^{\circ}}\phi_{\Omega}(x)^{-n-1}\,dx+\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{|u(0)|\Omega^{\circ}}|u(0)|^{-n-1}\,dx

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega^{\circ}|}{n+1}\int_{0}^{|u(0)|^{-n-1}}t^{\frac{-n}{n+1}}dt+\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{|\Omega^{\circ}|}{|u(0)|}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{n+1}\frac{|\Omega^{\circ}|}{|u(0)|}

Corollary 3.1
.

If
v\in\mathcal{C}_{n+2}(P)
, then

J(v)\leq\frac{C(n)}{|\Omega|(-\inf u)},

where

is some constant depending only on dimension. Moreover
\Omega=\{u<0\}
is balanced about the origin.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
, we have

(n+1)J(v)\geq\frac{|\Omega^{\circ}|}{(-\inf u)}.

On the other hand by John’s Lemma, there exist ellipsoids

centered at some point

such that

. By Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
applied to the function

gives

If we combine the two inequalities, we have

4.1.

Let us define the functional

\mathcal{E}(v):=-\log I(v)+J^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(v).

Lemma 3

There exist constants

First, we note that

To bound

Theorem 4.1
.

The functional
\mathcal{E}
has a minimizer in
\mathcal{C}_{n+2}(P)

Before we prove the theorem, we will need the following Lemma, which says that the energy
\mathcal{E}

Lemma 4

Given any

(1)

I(v)=I(\tilde{v})
.

(2)

(3)

Proof.

Let

\tilde{v}(y)\leq v(y)+\langle x_{0},y\rangle

\tilde{u}(x)\geq u(x-x_{0})

but we also note that
\tilde{v}
is the Legendre transform of the convex function

\hat{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u(x-x_{0})&\text{ if }x\in x_{0}+\Omega\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

therefore from the fact that
\hat{u}^{\star\star}=\hat{u}

. From this, we must have

Now we prove claim 2. We note that by Proposition
3.1
, we have

J(v)\leq J(v-\langle x_{0},\cdot\rangle).

and

\tilde{v}(y)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}(\langle x,y\rangle-u(x))+\langle y,x_{0}\rangle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\langle x,y\rangle-u(x))+\langle y,x_{0}\rangle=v(y)+\langle y,x_{0}\rangle

For the third claim, it’s clear that
\tilde{v}\geq\phi_{\tilde{\Omega}}

\tilde{v}(y)=\phi_{\tilde{\Omega}}(y)
for

. Fix any

, and notice that

on

, therefore it suffices to show that the supporting hyperplane

touches

\tilde{v}(y)=\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)=\langle x,y\rangle\leq\phi_{\tilde{\Omega}}(y)
which implies
\tilde{v}(y)=\phi_{\tilde{\Omega}}(y)

only at interior points, then

∎

Since
u
is strictly convex, there exists a compactly supported section
S_{h}(\mathbb{x})
of
\mathbb{x}
, such that
\partial\{\tilde{x}_{1}>0,\tilde{x}_{2}>-\tilde{x}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{n+1}>-\tilde{x}_{1}\}\cap\overline{S}_{h}=\overline{S}_{h}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}=0\}
.

Without loss of generality, let us assume that
\tilde{\mathbb{x}}=(0,\ldots,0)
by a translation of the coordinates. Then by the results of Jhaveri and Savin
[
10
, Section 4]
, we have

\frac{1}{M}\text{Id}\leq D^{2}_{\tilde{x}^{\prime}}\varphi\leq M\text{Id on }C_{r}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\},

and there exist
\sigma>0
such that

\frac{\varphi_{\tilde{x}_{1}}(\tilde{x})-\varphi_{\tilde{x}_{1}}(0)}{\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}\in C^{\sigma}(C_{r}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\})

where
C_{r}
is a neighborhood of
0
of the form

C_{r}=(-r^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}},r^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}})\times B_{r^{1/2}}(0)

for some small enough
r>0
. Denote

\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{x}):=\varphi(\tilde{x})-\varphi(0)-\langle\tilde{x},\nabla\varphi(0)\rangle,

which satisfies
\tilde{\varphi}(0)=|\nabla\tilde{\varphi}(0)|=0
.
Since
f(x)=\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{x}_{1}}(\tilde{x})}{\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}
is a well-defined Hölder continuous function on
C_{r}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\}
, it has a value at
0
and

|f(x_{1},x^{\prime})-f(0)|\leq C(|x_{1}|^{\sigma}+|x^{\prime}|^{\sigma})

which implies

\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{x}_{1}}(\tilde{x})=f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}+O(|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{k+1}+\sigma})+O(|x^{\prime}|^{\sigma}|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{\frac{1}{k+1}}).

Integrating this from
(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}^{\prime})
to
(0,\tilde{x}^{\prime})
gives

\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}^{\prime})-\tilde{\varphi}(0,\tilde{x}^{\prime})=\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x_{1}}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+O(|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}+\sigma})+O(|x^{\prime}|^{\sigma}|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}})

and moreover since
\frac{1}{M}\text{Id}\leq D^{2}_{\tilde{x}^{\prime}}\tilde{\varphi}\leq M\text{Id}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{C}|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}\leq\tilde{\varphi}(0,\tilde{x}^{\prime})\leq C|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2},

which implies on
C_{r}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\}
, we have

\frac{1}{C}|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x_{1}}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+O(|\tilde{x}|^{\sigma}|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}})\leq\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}^{\prime})\leq C|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+O(|\tilde{x}|^{\sigma}|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}).

If we define the rescalings

\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}^{\prime})=\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(t^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}}\tilde{x}_{1},t^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{x}^{\prime})}{t}

then
u_{t}
also satisfy

(\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}_{t}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}})^{k}\det D^{2}\tilde{\varphi}_{t}=1,

with

\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}_{t}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}=0\text{ on }C_{rt^{-1}}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}=0\}

and for any
R>0
, we have

\frac{1}{C}|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{k+2}{k+1}}+O(t^{\frac{\sigma}{2}})\leq\tilde{\varphi}_{t}\leq C|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{k+2}{k+1}}+O(t^{\frac{\sigma}{2}})

on
C_{R}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\}
. Hence by
[
2
]
, there exist a subsequence
t_{j}\to 0
such that
\tilde{\varphi}_{t_{j}}
converges in
C^{\infty}_{loc}(C_{R}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}>0\})
for any
R>0
, to a function
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
which satisfies

(\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}_{0}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}})^{k}\det D^{2}\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=1,

Moreover since

\frac{\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}_{t}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}}{\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}=\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{\tilde{x}_{1}}(t^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}}\tilde{x}_{1},t^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{x}^{\prime})}{(t^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}}\tilde{x}_{1})^{\frac{1}{k+1}}}=f(t^{\frac{k+1}{k+2}}\tilde{x}_{1},t^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{x}^{\prime}),

it follows by taking a limit as
t\to 0
that

\frac{\partial\tilde{\varphi}_{0}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{1}}=f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{1}{k+1}},

which implies by the Liouville theorem
[
10
, Theorem 1.4]
that

\tilde{\varphi}_{0}(x)=P(\tilde{x}^{\prime})+\frac{k+1}{k+2}f(0)\tilde{x}_{1}^{\frac{k+2}{k+1}}

where
P
is a uniformly convex quadratic function. It follows that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist sufficient small
t_{j}
, such that

\sup_{C_{1}\cap\{\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0\}}|\tilde{\varphi}_{0}-\tilde{\varphi}_{t_{j}}|\leq\varepsilon,

and hence by
[
10
, Proposition 5.1]
, for any
0<\beta<1
, there exist a linear transformation
A=\text{diag}(a_{1},A^{\prime})
such that

\tilde{\varphi}_{t}(A\tilde{x})=\tilde{\varphi}_{0}(\tilde{x})+O\left((|\tilde{x}^{\prime}|^{2}+|\tilde{x}_{1}|^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}})^{1+\frac{\beta}{2}}\right).

for
t>0
sufficiently small, which proves the result.
∎

Given the expansion of
\varphi
, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We define
\varphi
to be the homogenous extension of
v
as in Proposition
5.1
, then we see that

v(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=\varphi^{\frac{n+1+k}{2n+2+k}}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i})

where
\varphi
satisfy equation
1.3
. By Proposition
5.2
, we know that
\varphi
admits an expansion of the form (
5.19
) near
\mathbb{x}
. Plugging this expansion into the equation above gives

(5.22)

v(y)=v(\mathbb{y})+\nabla v(\mathbb{y})\cdot(y-\mathbb{y})+p^{\prime}x_{1}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}+\sum_{i,j=2}^{n}Q_{ij}(y_{i}-\mathbb{y}_{i})(y_{j}-\mathbb{y}_{j})+O\left(\left(|y|^{2}+y_{1}^{1+\frac{1}{k+1}}\right)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)

for some
\alpha>0
.
∎

Finally, we prove a basic global convexity property for the free boundary.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
u
solves (
1.2
). Then the free boundary
\{u=0\}
is strictly convex, in the sense that it does not contain a line segment.

Proof.

This result is closely related to a result of Savin
[
13
]
concerning the structure of the free boundary in the obstacle problem. We sketch the details for the reader’s convenience. First, it is straightforward to check directly that the measure

d\mu=(u^{\star})^{n+2}(-u)^{k}_{+}

is doubling on
\{u\leq 0\}
. Precisely, there is a constant
C>1
, depending on
n,k
and a constant
A>0

A^{-1}\leq\inf_{\{u<0\}}u^{\star}\leq\sup_{\{u<0\}}u^{\star}\leq A

such that, for any convex set
K
with center of mass
x_{0}\in\{u\leq 0\}
we have

\mu(K)\leq C\mu(\frac{1}{2}K)

where
\frac{1}{2}K
denotes the rescaling of
K
about
x_{0}
; see
[
10
, Lemma 2.6]
for a general result. Since
u
is bounded in compact sets and the graph of
u
does not contain a line, it follows from
[
3
, Lemma 1]
that, for any point
x_{0}\in\{x=0\}
and any
h>0
we can find
p_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the section

S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})=\{y:u(y)<h+p_{h}\cdot(y-x_{0})\}

is non-empty, bounded and has
x_{0}
as center of mass. Thanks to the mass doubling property, we obtain from
[
3
, Lemma 3]
(see also
[
13
, Lemma 2.7]
) the following balancing property

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
x\in S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})
has
u(x)\leq p_{h}\cdot(x-x_{0})
, then
S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})
is balanced around
x
in the sense that

(1+\eta)x-\eta S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})\subset S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})

for
\eta
depending on the doubling constant and
n
. In other words, if
[y_{1},y_{2}]
is a line segment passing through
x
, with
y_{i}\in\partial S_{h}^{c}(x_{0})
, then

\frac{|y_{1}-x|}{|y_{2}-x|}>\eta.

Suppose, for the sake of a contradiction, that
\{u=0\}
contains a line segment. By rotation and translation, we may assume that this segment is given by
te_{n}
for
t\in[0,A]
, and
u(te_{n})>0
for
t<0
, and
t>A
. Let
0<a\ll A
, and let

S_{h}^{c}(a)=\{y:u(y)<h+p_{h,a}\cdot(y-ae_{n})\}

be the section centered at
ae_{n}
. First we claim that for
h
small we have
p_{h,a}\cdot e_{n}<0
. Indeed, if
p_{h,a}\cdot e_{n}\geq 0
, then
Ae_{n}\in S_{h}^{c}(a)
, which, by balancing implies
-\eta\frac{A}{2}e_{n}\in S^{c}_{h}(a)
. But
u(-\eta\frac{A}{2}e_{n})>\delta
for some
\delta>0
, contradicting
p_{h,a}\cdot e_{n}<0
if
h\ll\delta
.

Thus, for all
h
sufficiently small,
S_{h}^{c}(a)
is balanced around
0
by Lemma
6
. Define

\displaystyle t_{h,a}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{t>a:te_{n}\in S^{c}_{h}(a)\}

\displaystyle b_{h,a}

\displaystyle:=\sup\{t>0:-te_{n}\in S^{c}_{h}(a)\}

Since
S_{h}^{c}(a)
is balanced around
0
and
t_{h,a}>a
we see that
b_{h,a}>\eta a>0
for all
h
small. This implies a uniform lower bound
u(b_{h,a})>\delta^{\prime}>0
. It follows that
t_{h,a}\rightarrow a
as
h\rightarrow 0
, contradicting balancing around
a
.

∎

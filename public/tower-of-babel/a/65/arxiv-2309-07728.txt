Predictions of the Strange partner of T c ​ c T_{cc} in the quark delocalization color screening model
Xuejie Liu 1, Dianyong Chen 2,4, Hongxia Huang 3, Jialun Ping 3

Abstract
Inspired by the detection of T c ​ c T_{cc} tetraquark state by LHCb Collaboration, we preform a systemical investigation of the low-lying doubly heavy charm tetraquark states with strangeness in the quark delocalization color screening model in the present work. Two kinds of configurations, the meson-meson configuration and diquark-antidiquark configuration, are considered in the calculation. Our estimations indicate that the coupled channel effects play important role in the multiquark system, and a bound state with J P = 1 + J^{P}=1^{+} and a resonance state with J P = 0 + J^{P}=0^{+} have been predicted. The mass of the bound state is evaluated to be ( 3971 ∼ 3975 ) (3971\sim 3975) MeV, while the mass and width of the resonance are determined to be ( 4113 ∼ 4114 ) (4113\sim 4114) MeV and ( 14.3 ∼ 16.1 ) (14.3\sim 16.1) MeV, respectively.

Quark delocalization color screening model and the resonanting group method

II.1
Quark delocalization color screening model

The QDCSM is an extension of the native quark cluster model
DeRujula:1975qlm
;
Isgur:1978xj
;
Isgur:1978wd
;
Isgur:1979be
and also developed with aim of addressing multiquark systems. For the tetraquark system, the Hamiltonian reads,

H=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left(m_{i}+\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}\right)-T_{CM}+\sum_{j>i=1}^{4}V(r_{ij}),\\

(1)

where
T_{CM}
is the center-of-mass kinetic energy, who is usually subtracted without losing generality since we mainly focus on the internal relative motions of the multiquark system. The interplay is two body potential, which includes color-confining potential
V_{\mathrm{CON}}
, one-gluon exchange potential
V_{\mathrm{OGE}}
, and the potential results from Goldstone-boson exchange,
V_{\chi}
, i.e.,

V(r_{ij})=V_{\mathrm{CON}}(r_{ij})+V_{\mathrm{OGE}}(r_{ij})+V_{\chi}(r_{ij}).

(2)

In the present work, we focus on the
S-
wave low-lying positive
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system with positive parity. In this case, the spin-orbit and tensor interactions vanish and the potential
V_{\mathrm{OGE}}(r_{ij})
becomes,

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{OGE}}(r_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{s}^{q_{i}q_{j}}\bm{\lambda}^{c}_{i}\cdot\bm{\lambda}^{c}_{j}

(3)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{r_{ij}}-\frac{\pi}{2}\delta(\bm{r}_{ij})(\frac{1}{m^{2}_{i}}+\frac{1}{m^{2}_{j}}+\frac{4\bm{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{j}}{3m_{i}m_{j}})\right],

where
m_{i}
is the quark mass,
\bm{\sigma}_{i}
and
\bm{\lambda^{c}}_{i}
are the Pauli matrices and SU(3) color matrices, respectively. The
\alpha_{s}^{q_{i}q_{j}}
is the quark-gluon coupling constant, which offers a consistent description of mesons from light to heavy-quark sector. The values of
\alpha_{ij}
are associated with the quark flavors and in the present work they are fixed by reproducing the mass difference of the low-lying mesons with
S=0
and
S=1
.

The confining potential
V_{\mathrm{CON}}(r_{ij})
is,

V_{\mathrm{CON}}(r_{ij})=-a_{c}\bm{\lambda^{c}_{i}\cdot\lambda^{c}_{j}}\left[f(r_{ij})+V_{0_{q_{i}q_{j}}}\right],

(4)

where the
V_{0_{q_{i}q_{j}}}
is determined by the mass differences of the theoretical esmations and experimental measurement of each kind of meson, which is also quark flavor related parameter. In the QDCSM, the function
f(r_{ij})
is defined as,

f(r_{ij})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r_{ij}^{2}&\qquad\mbox{if }i,j\mbox{ occur in the same cluster},\\
\frac{1-e^{-\mu_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}}}{\mu_{ij}}&\qquad\mbox{if }i,j\mbox{ occur in different cluster},\\
\end{array}\right.

(5)

where the color screening parameter
\mu_{ij}
relevant to the light quarks can be determined by fitting the deuteron properties,
NN
and
NY
scattering phase shifts
Chen:2011zzb
;
Ping:1993me
;
Wang:1998nk
, which are
\mu_{qq}=0.45
,
\mu_{qs}=0.19

and
\mu_{ss}=0.08
. The parameter
\mu_{ij}
satisfy the relation
\mu_{qs}^{2}=\mu_{qq}\mu_{ss}
, where
q
represents
u
or
d
quark. When extending to the heavy-quark case, we found that the dependence of the parameter
\mu_{cc}
is rather weak in the calculation of the spectrum of
P_{c}
states by taking the value of
\mu_{cc}
from
10^{-4}
to
10^{-2}\ \mathrm{fm}^{-2}

Huang:2015uda
. Moreover, when
\mu_{ij}
is rather small, the exponential function can be approximated to be,

\displaystyle e^{-\mu_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\mu_{ij}r_{ij}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\mu_{ij}^{2}r_{ij}^{4}).

(6)

in the small
r
region. Accordingly, the confinement potential between two clusters is approximated to be,

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{CON}}(r_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a_{c}\bm{\mathbf{\lambda}}^{c}_{i}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{\lambda}}^{c}_{j}~\left(\frac{1-e^{-\mu_{ij}\mathbf{r}_{ij}^{2}}}{\mu_{ij}}+V_{0_{ij}}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-a_{c}\bm{\mathbf{\lambda}}^{c}_{i}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{\lambda}}^{c}_{j}~(r_{ij}^{2}+V_{0_{ij}}),

which is the same with the expression of two quarks in the same cluster. Thus, when the value of the
\mu_{ij}
is very small, the screened confinement will return to the quadratic form, which is why the results are insensitive to the value of
\mu_{cc}
. So in the present work, we take
\mu_{cc}=0.01\ \mathrm{fm}^{-2}
. Then
\mu_{sc}
and
\mu_{uc}
are obtained by the relation
\mu^{2}_{sc}=\mu_{ss}\mu_{cc}
and
\mu^{2}_{uc}=\mu_{uu}\mu_{cc}
, respectively.

T_{cc\bar{s}}

\pi

\displaystyle V^{\chi}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{K}(\bm{r}_{ij})\sum_{a=4}^{7}\lambda_{i}^{a}\cdot\lambda_{j}^{a}+

(8)

\displaystyle V_{\eta}(\bm{r}_{ij})\left[\left(\lambda_{i}^{8}\cdot\lambda_{j}^{8}\right)\cos\theta_{P}-(\lambda_{i}^{0}\cdot\lambda_{j}^{0})\sin\theta_{P}\right],

with

\displaystyle V_{\chi}(\bm{r}_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{g_{ch}^{2}}{{4\pi}}}{\frac{m_{\chi}^{2}}{{\ 12m_{i}m_{j}}}}{\frac{\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}}{{\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}-m_{\chi}^{2}}}}m_{\chi}

(9)

\displaystyle\left\{(\bm{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{j})\left[Y(m_{\chi}\,r_{ij})-{\frac{\Lambda_{\chi}^{3}}{m_{\chi}^{3}}}Y(\Lambda_{\chi}\,r_{ij})\right]\right\},

\displaystyle~~~~~~\chi=\{K,\eta\},

where
Y(x)=e^{-x}/x
is the standard Yukawa function. The
\bm{\lambda^{a}}
is the SU(3) flavor Gell-Mann matrix. The mass of the
K
and
\eta
meson is taken from the experimental value
ParticleDataGroup:2018ovx
. The chiral coupling constant,
g_{ch}

\frac{g_{ch}^{2}}{4\pi}=\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2}\frac{g_{\pi NN}^{2}}{4\pi}{\frac{m_{u,d}^{2}}{m_{N}^{2}}},

(10)

where the SU(3) flavor symmetry only broken by the different masses of the light quarks. All the other model parameters are the same as the ones in Ref.
Liu:2021xje
, where three different sets of parameters were used to study the
c\bar{c}s\bar{s}

,

In the QDCSM, the single-particle orbital wave functions in the ordinary quark cluster model are the left and right centered single Gaussian functions, which are,

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}(\bm{S_{i}})=\left(\frac{1}{\pi b^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{-\frac{(\bm{r_{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{S_{i}})^{2}}{2b^{2}}},

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta}(-\bm{S_{i}})=\left(\frac{1}{\pi b^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}e^{-\frac{(\bm{r_{\beta}}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{S_{i}})^{2}}{2b^{2}}}.\

(11)

The quark delocalization is realized by writing the single-particle orbital wave function as a
linear combination of the left and right Gaussians, which are,

\displaystyle{\psi}_{\alpha}(\bm{S_{i}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\phi}_{\alpha}(\bm{S_{i}})+\epsilon{\phi}_{\alpha}(-\bm{S_{i}})\right)/N(\epsilon),

\displaystyle{\psi}_{\beta}(-\bm{S_{i}},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\phi}_{\beta}(-\bm{S_{i}})+\epsilon{\phi}_{\beta}(\bm{S_{i}})\right)/N(\epsilon),

\displaystyle N(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon e^{{-S}_{i}^{2}/4b^{2}}},

(12)

where
\epsilon(\bm{S}_{i})
is the delocalization parameter determined by the dynamics of the quark system rather than free parameters. In this way, the system can choose its most favorable configuration through its dynamics in a larger Hilbert space.

In the present work, the RGM is employed to carry out the dynamical calculation. When dealing with the two-cluster system in this method, one can only consider the relative motion between the clusters, while the two clusters are frozen inside
Kamimura:1981oxj
. So the wave function of the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
system can be constructed as,

\displaystyle\psi_{4q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left[\left[\psi_{A}(\bm{\rho}_{A})\psi_{B}(\bm{\rho}_{B})\right]^{[\sigma]IS}\otimes\chi_{L}(\textbf{R})\right]^{J},

(13)

where the symbol
\mathcal{A}
is the antisymmetry operator, which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-P_{13}.

(14)

where the
P_{13}
indicates the exchange of the particle positions with numbers
1
and
3
from the Fig.
2
.
[\sigma]=[222]
gives the total color symmetry. The symbols
I
,
S
,
L
, and

T_{cc\bar{s}}
system, respectively.
\psi_{A}
and
\psi_{B}
are the wave functions of the two-quark cluster, which are,

\displaystyle\psi_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi b^{2}}\right)^{3/4}e^{-\bm{\rho_{A}}^{2}/(4b^{2})}\eta_{I_{A}}S_{A}\chi_{A}^{c},

\displaystyle\psi_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi b^{2}}\right)^{3/4}e^{-\bm{\rho_{B}}^{2}/(4b^{2})}\eta_{I_{B}}S_{B}\chi_{B}^{c},

(15)

where
\eta_{I}
,

, and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bm{r}_{q_{1}}-\bm{r}_{\bar{q}_{2}}},\ \ \ \ \bm{\rho_{B}}={\bm{r}_{q_{3}}-\bm{r}_{\bar{q}_{4}}},

\displaystyle\bm{R_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{1}{\bm{r}_{q_{1}}}+m_{2}{\bm{r}_{\bar{q}_{2}}}}{m_{1}+m_{2}},

\displaystyle\bm{R_{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{3}{\bm{r}_{q_{3}}}+m_{4}{\bm{r}_{\bar{q}_{4}}}}{m_{3}+m_{4}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{R_{A}-R_{B}},

Form the variational principle, after variation with respect to the relative motion wave function
\chi\bm{(}R)=\sum_{L}\chi_{L}\bm{(}R)
, one obtains the RGM equation, which is,

\displaystyle\int H\left(\bm{R,R^{\prime}}\right)\chi\left(\bm{R^{\prime}}\right)d\bm{R}^{\prime}=E

\displaystyle\int N\left(\bm{R,R^{\prime}}\right)\chi\left(\bm{R^{\prime}}\right)d\bm{R}^{\prime},

(17)

where

\displaystyle\chi\bm{(R)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\sum_{L}\left(\frac{1}{\pi b^{2}}\right)^{3/4}\sum_{i}^{n}C_{i,L}

(18)

\displaystyle\times\int e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bm{(}R-S_{i})^{2}/b^{2}}Y^{L}\left(\hat{\bm{S}_{i}}\right)d\hat{\bm{S}_{i}},

where

III.2
Resonance States

In the bound state estimations, we find one bound state with
J^{P}=1^{+}
while there is no bound state in the
J^{P}=0^{+}
and
J^{P}=2^{+}
systems. In the following, we will employ the real scaling method to explore the possible resonance states in the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system. To determine whether these resonance states could be detected by the open channels, we perform a channel coupling estimation by including all the meson-meson and diquark-antidiquark channels in the estimations.

The real scaling method is developed to identify the genuine resonances from the states with discrete energies with finite volume
real_Method
. In this method, a factor
\textbf{S}_{m}
, which is the distance between two clusters, is adopted to scale the finite volume. So with the increase of the distance between two clusters, the continuum state will fall off toward its threshold, the energy of the bound state remains unchanged, while a resonance state will tend to be stable. If the energy of a scattering state is far away from the one of the resonance, the coupling between the resonance and the scattering states is rather weak, and the energy of the resonance is almost stable. When the energy of the scattering state approaches the one of the resonance due to the increasing of
\textbf{S}_{m}
, the coupling will become strong, and if
\textbf{S}_{m}
increases further, the energy gap between the resonance and scattering states will increase and the coupling will become weak again. In this way, an avoided crossing structure appears. This is a general feature of two interacting energy levels. Because of the continuum nature of the scattering states, the avoided crossing structure will show up repeatedly with the increasing of
\textbf{S}_{m}
as shown in Fig.
3
and the resonance line corresponds to the energy of the resonance state. In addition, from the slopes of resonance and scattering states, the decay width can be estimated by,

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4|V_{min}(S)|\frac{\sqrt{|k_{r}||k_{c}|}}{|k_{r}-k_{c}|}

(22)

where
k_{r}
and
k_{c}
are the slopes of the resonance and scattering states, respectively. While,
V_{min}(S)
is the minimal energy difference between the resonance and the scattering state at avoided crossing point. This method has been successfully applied to investigate the pentaquark
Hiyama:2005cf
;
Hiyama:2018ukv
, the dibaryon
Xia:2021tof
, and the tetraquark systems
Jin:2020jfc
;
Liu:2021xje
;
Liu:2022vyy
.

In the present work, we expand the spacial wave function with a set of gaussians with differences
\textbf{S}_{m}
,
(m=1,2,3,\ldots,n)
and the distance with the relative motion of two clusters can be scaled. So we calculate the energy eigenvalues of the
T_{cc\bar{s}}

(S_{m})
between two clusters from 4.0 to 9.0 fm to check if there is any resonance state. Here, we take the results of the QDCSM1 as examples, which are shown in Fig.

IV
Summary

In the present work, the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system with the quantum number
J^{P}=0^{+},1^{+},2^{+}
are systemically investigated to search for the possible bound state and resonance state by using the RGM in the QDCSM framework. In the model, both meson-meson and diquark-antidiquark configurations are taken into account, and the single-channel and the coupled channel calculations are preformed to obtain the energy of the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system. In addition, a stabilization calculation is carried out to seek for possible resonance states. Furthermore, to check whether the estimated results are parameter dependent, three different sets of model parameters are employed in the calculation and we find the qualitative results of three sets of model parameters for the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system are very similar.

From the present estimations, we find that the coupled channel effects plays important role in the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark system. After taking the coupled channel effects into consideration, we predict one bound state with the energy to be
3971.1\sim 3974.8
MeV and
J^{P}=1^{+}
. Moreover, one resonance state with
J^{P}=0^{+}
is also obtained, the resonance mass and width are estimated to be
4113\sim 4114
MeV and
14.3\sim 16.1
MeV, respectively. The predictions in the present work could be experimentally detected in the future by LHCb and Belle II. Additionally the theoretical and further experimental investigations for properties of the
T_{cc\bar{s}}
tetraquark could pave the way for possible doubly and triply tetraquark states.

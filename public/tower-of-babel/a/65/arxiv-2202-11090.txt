Total insecurity of communication via strong converse for quantum privacy amplification
Robert Salzmann, Nilanjana Datta

Abstract
Quantum privacy amplification is a central task in quantum cryptography. Given shared randomness, which is initially correlated with a quantum system held by an eavesdropper, the goal is to extract uniform randomness which is decoupled from the latter. The optimal rate for this task is known to satisfy the strong converse property and we provide a lower bound on the corresponding strong converse exponent. In the strong converse region, the distance of the final state of the protocol from the desired decoupled state converges exponentially fast to its maximal value, in the asymptotic limit. We show that this necessarily leads to totally insecure communication by establishing that the eavesdropper can infer any sent messages with certainty, when given very limited extra information. In fact, we prove that in the strong converse region, the eavesdropper has an exponential advantage in inferring the sent message correctly, compared to the achievability region. Additionally we establish the following technical result, which is central to our proofs, and is of independent interest: the smoothing parameter for the smoothed max-relative entropy satisfies the strong converse property.

The setup of quantum privacy amplification

Strong converse of secure communication:
Most of the literature on privacy amplification was concerned with establishing security proofs showing that if the distance to the decoupled state can be made small using a suitable hash function, the probability with which Eve can guess the generated key is also small. On the other hand, the implications of large distance to decoupled state, as is the case in the strong converse region discussed above, are less obvious.

In order to clarify in what sense large conversion error implies insecurity in the communication with the generated key, we consider Alice encrypting a message using the key, and evaluate the extra side information Eve needs to have in order to guess the message with certainty. In contrast, in the strong converse region, in which the conversion error approaches 1 exponentially fast, Eve can infer the message even for sets of messages

In fact, we show that almost all

Purified distance and generalised trace distance

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sigma^{\otimes n}\Lambda_{n}(s,\varepsilon)\right))}{n}\geq s,\quad\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{\otimes}(\mathbbm{1}-\Lambda_{n}(s,\varepsilon))\right))}{n}\geq B(s|\rho\|\sigma)-\varepsilon.

Choosing
s=s_{r}
where

\displaystyle s_{r}:=\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}\big(\alpha r-(\alpha-1)D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\big)

and noting that
s_{r}
is the unique solution to the equation
B(s|\rho\|\sigma)=s-r
, we get for all
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(2^{nr}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sigma^{\otimes n}\Lambda_{n}(s_{r},\varepsilon)\right))}{n}\geq s_{r}-r,\quad\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{\otimes}(\mathbbm{1}-\Lambda_{n}(s_{r},\varepsilon))\right))}{n}\geq s_{r}-r-\varepsilon.

Therefore, using now (
4
) we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\mathcal{D}(\rho^{\otimes n},\widetilde{\rho}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\big(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}(\mathbbm{1}-\Lambda_{n}(s_{r},\varepsilon))\big)+2^{nr}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\sigma^{\otimes n}\Lambda_{n}(s_{r},\varepsilon)\right)\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq s_{r}-r-\varepsilon.

Hence, by definition

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(1-\varepsilon^{\mathcal{D}}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n},nr))}{n}\geq s_{r}-r-\varepsilon=\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(\alpha-1)\left(r-D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\right)-\varepsilon,

which finishes the proof of (
11
) since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary.

We now consider
\rho
and
\sigma
to be commute and establish the converse in (
13
). For that we define the sub-normalised states
\widetilde{\rho}_{n}=T_{n}\rho^{\otimes n}T_{n}
, where
T_{n}=\{\rho^{\otimes n}\leq 2^{nr}\sigma^{\otimes n}\}
denotes the Neyman-Pearson test. Using that
\rho
and
\sigma
commute we see by definition
\widetilde{\rho}_{n}\leq 2^{nr}\sigma^{\otimes n}.
Moreover, the generalised trace distance can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\rho^{\otimes n},\widetilde{\rho}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{\otimes n}-\widetilde{\rho}_{n}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\mathrm{Tr}(\rho^{\otimes n})-\mathrm{Tr}(\widetilde{\rho}_{n})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|(\mathbbm{1}-T_{n})\rho^{\otimes n}(\mathbbm{1}-T_{n})\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\mathrm{Tr}(T_{n}\rho^{\otimes n})\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathrm{Tr}(T_{n}\rho^{\otimes n}).

Here we have used for the first equality that since
\rho^{\otimes n}
commutes with
\sigma^{\otimes n}
and therefore also with
T_{n}
, the cross terms vanish, i.e.
T_{n}\rho^{\otimes n}(\mathbbm{1}-T_{n})=(\mathbbm{1}-T_{n})\rho^{\otimes n}T_{n}=0.

Using now Lemma
1
we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(1-\mathcal{D}(\rho^{\otimes n},\widetilde{\rho}_{n}))}{n}=B(s_{r}|\rho\|\sigma)=\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(\alpha-1)(r-D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)),

and therefore

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log(1-\varepsilon^{\mathcal{D}}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n},nr)}{n}\leq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(\alpha-1)(r-D_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma),

which finishes the proof.

5
Strong converse rates of privacy amplification against a quantum adversary

The initial state of a privacy amplification protocol is given by the classical-quantum state

\displaystyle\rho_{XE}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{x}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes\rho_{E}^{x},

(15)

with the classical system
X
belonging to Alice and Bob and the quantum system
E
belonging to Eve.

The objective of Alice and Bob is to apply a hash function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
to decouple their part of the system from Eve’s system, the latter playing the role of quantum side information. Their operation results in the state

\displaystyle\rho^{f}_{ZE}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\left(|z\rangle\!\langle z|\otimes\sum_{x\in{\cal X}:\atop{x\in f^{-1}(z)}}p_{x}\rho_{E}^{x}\right).

(16)

The minimal errors in this conversion, measured in trace distance and purified distance, respectively, are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z})

\displaystyle=\min_{f}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{Z}E}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|\mathcal{Z}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right\|_{1},

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}(\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z})

\displaystyle=\min_{f}P\left(\rho^{f}_{ZE},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}}}{|\mathcal{Z}|}\otimes\rho_{E}\right),

where the minimum is over all functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}.
The
optimal key length
which can be distilled from a c-q state
\rho_{XE}
in that manner with
\varepsilon\geq 0
error measured in metric
d\in\{\mathcal{D},P\}
is given by

\displaystyle l^{\varepsilon,d}(X|E)=\sup\left\{\log|\mathcal{Z}|\,\Big|\,\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z})\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

Note that
l^{\varepsilon,P}(X|E)
is essentially given by the smoothed conditional min-entropy as we have for every
0<\eta\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
the relation
[
20
, Theorem 8]

H^{\varepsilon,P}_{\min}(X|E)\geq l^{\varepsilon,P}(X|E)\geq H^{\varepsilon-\eta,P}_{\min}(X|E)-\log\left(1/\eta^{4}\right)-3.

(17)

Let us go to the
n
-copy setting in which Alice and Bob possess strings
x^{(n)}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\cal X}^{n}
with the
x_{i}
’s being values taken by a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with common p.m.f.
p_{x}
,
x\in{\cal X}
. The initial state of the privacy amplification process is in this case:

\displaystyle\rho_{X^{n}E^{n}}:=\sum_{x^{(n)}\in{\cal X}^{n}}p_{x^{(n)}}|x^{(n)}\rangle\langle x^{(n)}|\otimes\rho_{E^{n}}^{x^{(n)}},

(18)

and the desired final state is
\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{\otimes n}
.

For a privacy amplification rate
R\geq 0
we consider
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
to be such that
|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|=\left\lfloor{2^{nR}}\right\rfloor
.

The next theorem shows that if the randomness extraction rate in a transformation from
\mathcal{X}^{n}
to
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is larger than the conditional entropy
H(X|E)
, the distance of the corresponding decoupled state to any possible
\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}
, defined analogously to (
16
), goes exponentially fast to 1 as
n
goes to infinity. By that it provides the strong converse of privacy amplification. Moreover, it provides bounds on the corresponding strong converse rates.

Theorem 3

Let
\rho_{XE}
be a c-q state,
R\geq 0
and
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
with
|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|=\left\lfloor{2^{nR}}\right\rfloor.
Then we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}\frac{(1-\alpha)}{2}(R-H_{\alpha}(X|E)),

(19)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\Delta_{P}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(1-\alpha)(R-H_{\alpha}(X|E)).

(20)

Remark 4

Note that the right hand side of both (
20
) and (
19
) are strictly positive if only if
R>H(X|E)
. This in turn proves the full strong converse, since
H(X|E)
is the optimal achievable extraction rate of privacy amplification against a quantum adversary (c.f. (
2
)). In other words, the optimal extraction rate of privacy amplification against a quantum adversary satisfies the so-called strong converse property: i.e., an attempt to extract private bits at a rate higher than
H(X|E)
leads to the conversion error going to 1 exponentially fast as
n\to\infty.

Remark 5

Note that in the concurrent and independent work
[
18
]
, the lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{-\log\left(1-\overline{\Delta}_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(1-\alpha)(R-H_{\alpha}(X|E)),

(21)

has been established. Here,
\overline{\Delta}_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})=\mathbbm{E}_{h_{n}}\,\frac{1}{2}\|\rho^{h_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho^{\otimes n}_{E}\|_{1}
denotes the average trace distance from the decoupled state, with
\mathbbm{E}_{h_{n}}
being the average over all strongly 2-universal hash functions (see
[
18
]
for more details). Note that due to the factor of
1/2
the right-hand side of our (
19
) is smaller than the right-hand side of (
21
). This factor arises solely from the fact that we use our result for purified distance, (
20
), together with the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality
[
6
]
. If the following conjectured inequality (
22
) holds:

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon,\mathcal{D}}_{\text{min}}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\rho_{X^{n}E^{n}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\geq}}H^{\varepsilon,\mathcal{D}}_{\text{min}}(Z_{n}|E^{n})_{\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}},

which is the analogue of (
22
) but with smoothing considered in terms of the generalised trace distance instead of purified distance, we can obtain a lower bound on the strong converse exponent (
19
) with an improvement of a factor of
2.
The resulting bound would then match the lower bound (
21
) from
[
18
]
. In fact, since we consider the minimal conversion error,
\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})
, rather than the average one
\overline{\Delta}_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})
, this would give a stronger result on the strong converse exponent of privacy amplification.

Remark 6

In
[
11
]
, the authors obtained alternative bounds on the strong converse exponent for quantum privacy amplification, using a figure of merit which is different from the ones considered in this paper. The figure of merit in
[
11
]
is the fidelity (between the final c-q state of privacy amplification protocol and a decoupled state corresponding to uniform shared randomness of the classical system and any possible quantum state of Eve) optimized over all possible states of Eve.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We first prove (
20
) which essentially follows by the same method as in
[
12
, Theorem 8]
, but we still carry it out for completeness. From
[
12
, Proposition 10]

we know for all
\varepsilon\geq 0
and
f_{n}:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon,P}_{\text{min}}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\rho_{X^{n}E^{n}}}\geq H^{\varepsilon,P}_{\text{min}}(Z_{n}|E^{n})_{\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}}.

(22)

This gives for all
r\in\mathbbm{R}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{P}\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\otimes\rho_{E^{n}},r\right)\geq\varepsilon^{P}\left(\rho_{X^{n}E^{n}}\big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\otimes\rho_{E^{n}},r\right).

(23)

By definition we have

\displaystyle P\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}E^{n}},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right)\geq\varepsilon^{P}\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}\Big\|{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}\otimes\rho_{E},-\log|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|\right)\geq\varepsilon^{P}\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}E^{n}}\Big\|{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}\otimes\rho_{E},-nR\right),

where we have used
\log|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|=\log\left\lfloor{2^{nR}}\right\rfloor\leq nR
for the last inequality.
Combining this with (
23
) and using that
f_{n}
was arbitrary gives

\displaystyle\Delta_{P}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon^{P}\left(\rho_{X^{n}E^{n}}\Big\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\otimes\rho_{E^{n}},-nR\right).

Using now Theorem
2
gives

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\Delta_{P}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(1-\alpha)\left(R+D_{\alpha}\left(\rho_{XE}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes\rho_{E}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}(1-\alpha)\left(R-H_{\alpha}\left(X\big|E\right)\right).

For (
19
) we use that by the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality
[
6
]
we have for all functions
f_{n}:\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{Z_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1-P^{2}\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{Z_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(1+P\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{Z_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right)\right)\left(1-P\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\left(1-P\left(\rho^{f_{n}}_{Z_{n}E^{n}},\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E}^{n}\right)\right)}

and therefore by (
20
)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log\left(1-\Delta_{P}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})\right)}{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}\frac{(1-\alpha)}{2}(R-H_{\alpha}(X|E)_{\rho})

which finishes the proof.

6
Strong converse for secure communication

In the following we consider
Z_{n}
to be a sequence of classical systems,
E_{n}
a sequence of quantum systems. Moreover, we consider sequences of c-q states denoted by

\displaystyle\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}=\sum_{z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}p_{z_{n}}|z_{n}\rangle\!\langle z_{n}|\otimes\rho^{z_{n}}_{E_{n}},

(24)

where we assume the system
Z_{n}
to be held by Alice and
E_{n}
to be held by an evesdropper, Eve.
we write
\rho_{E_{n}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{Z_{n}}(\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}})=\sum_{z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}p_{z_{n}}\rho^{z_{n}}_{E_{n}}
for the corresponding reduced states of Eve’s system.

Here, we want to understand in what sense the condition

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}=1

(25)

implies insecurity of the keys Alice can generate from
\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}
. The example we have in mind is
\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}
being the resulting state after privacy amplification with privacy amplification rate
R>H(X|E)
, where we have already seen that the convergence in (
25
) happens exponentially fast (c.f. Theorem
3
).

We consider the scenario in which Alice is using a generated key
z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}
to encode a message
m
, where the message is taken out of a subset
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}.
For the encoding she uses an
encryption scheme
, i.e. a map

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{Z}_{n}\times\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle(z_{n},m)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m),

which is bijective in both entries for the other entry fixed. The encoded message
\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)
is then sent publicly. Given a party has access to the key
z_{n}
, they can then decode
m
due to bijectivity of the function
\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}.
As an example of such an encryption scheme we can think of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
being a set of bit strings and take the one-time pad encoding
\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)=z_{n}\oplus m,
where
\oplus
denotes the component-wise addition modulo 2.

Since the encoded message is sent publicly, Eve has access to the c-q state

\displaystyle\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}=\sum_{Z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}p_{z_{n}}\,|\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)\rangle\!\langle\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)|\otimes\rho^{Z_{n}}_{E_{n}}=(U_{m}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}})\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}(U^{*}_{m}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}}).

Here, we have denoted the unitary
U_{m}
on system
Z_{n}
defined by
U_{m}|z_{n}\rangle=|\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)\rangle.
In order to infer which message
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}
has been sent, Eve needs to distinguish the states
\left(\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}
by picking a suitable POVM
\Lambda\equiv\left(\Lambda_{m}\right)_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}.
Given that Alice choses the message
M=m
, the probability that Eve’s guess, denoted by
\hat{M}
, is correct is given by

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)=\mathrm{Tr}(\Lambda_{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}).

Moreover, if Alice message is distributed by some distribution
M\sim q_{m}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
then the optimal probability with which Eve guesses correctly on average, the
guessing probability
in the following, is given by

\displaystyle p_{\text{guess}}(M|\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M),E_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\\
\text{ POVM}\end{subarray}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}q_{m}\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\\
\text{ POVM}\end{subarray}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}q_{m}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right).

(26)

However, in order for Eve to be able to pick the POVM sensefully, i.e. the maximiser in (
6
), she needs to have additonal side information, which is knowledge of the set
\mathcal{M}_{n}
and the distribution
q_{m}.
Here, we say her additional side information is large if
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is small. In the following we will quantify how much additional side information Eve needs to have in order to guess Alice’s message with certainty.

We first consider the case in which (
25
) does not hold, i.e. the trace distance between
\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}
and the decoupled state is strictly smaller than 1 uniformly in
n.
In that case, it is only possible for Eve to have certainty of the sent message if she has strong additional side information which is that
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is finite uniformly in
n
, i.e.
\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}|\mathcal{M}_{n}|<\infty.

Proposition 7

Let
\delta\in[0,1)
and
\left(\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}\right)_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}
be a sequence of c-q states such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}\leq\delta.

(27)

Moreover, assume Alice uses an encryption scheme
g
as defined above and chooses to send a message
M
which she picks uniformly out of the set
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, i.e.
q_{m}=1/|\mathcal{M}_{n}|
for all
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}.
Then Eve’s guessing probability is bounded by

\displaystyle p_{\text{guess}}(M|\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M),E_{n})\leq\delta+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{n}|}.

In particular, if
\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}|\mathcal{M}_{n}|=\infty
then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}p_{\text{guess}}(M|\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M),E_{n})\leq\delta<1.

Proof.

From (
6
) and unitary invariance of the trace norm we get for all
m\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}=\sup_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}\leq\delta.

Using this we get

\displaystyle p_{\text{guess}}(M|\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M),E_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\\
\text{ POVM}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{n}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\Lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\\
\text{ POVM}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{n}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{m}\,\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right)+\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{n}|}+\delta.

In the case where (
25
) does hold, the situation is drastically different to the one described in Proposition
7
. Let
c_{n}\in[0,1]
be the speed of convergence in (
25
), i.e.

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}\right|\leq c_{n}

(28)

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}=0.
We will show that for all encryption schemes and almost all sets of messages with
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|\ll c_{n}^{-1}
, Eve’s guessing probability will converge to 1 as
n\to\infty
(c.f. Proposition
8
). In particular, in the strong converse region of privacy amplification, i.e. for privacy amplification rate
R>H(X|E)
, Theorem
3
gives that
c_{n}
converges exponentially fast to 0 with exponential decay rate being bounded from below as

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{-\log c_{n}}{n}\geq\sup_{0\leq\alpha\leq 1}\frac{(1-\alpha)}{2}\left(R-H_{\alpha}(X|E)\right).

Hence, the sets of messages for which Eve can guess correctly can be made even exponentially large in
n
, which can be considered as small additional side information needed. Moreover, it can be shown that the convergence of Eve’s guessing probability towards 1 happens exponentially fast in that region.

Therfore, to conclude, we see by Proposition
7
that in the achievable region of privacy amplification for privacy amplification rate
R\leq H(X|E)
in which

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{n}\to\mathcal{Z}_{n})=0,

(compare
[
16
]
)
Eve can infer the sent message correctly only if she already had complete knowledge of it to start with (i.e.
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|=1
). Whereas, the following Proposition
8
gives in the strong converse region for
R>H(X|E)
, Eve only needs very limited additional side information (
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|
possibly scaling exponentially in
n
) while still being able to infer the message correctly. Hence, our result serves as a strong converse for secure communication.

Proposition 8

Let
\left(\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}\right)_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}
be a sequence of c-q states such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}=1,

(29)

with speed of convergence
c_{n}\in[0,1]
, i.e.

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right\|_{1}\right|\leq c_{n}

(30)

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}=0.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}
be a set of messages with
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|\leq c_{n}^{(\varepsilon-1)}
chosen uniformly at random, which Alice encodes using an encryption scheme
\mathcal{E}
as above. Then Eve can find a measurement
\Lambda
such that for all
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}

\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)>1-c_{n}^{\varepsilon/2}

(31)

with probability greater than
1-2c_{n}^{\varepsilon/2}.

Hence, in particular for all
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)=1

almost surely.

Remark 9

Note that by Proposition
8
we immediately also get the lower bound on the average guessing probability (for all possible distributions
q_{m}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
)

\displaystyle p_{\text{guess}}(M|\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M),E_{n})>1-c_{n}^{\varepsilon/2}

with probability greater than
1-2c_{n}^{\varepsilon/2}
over the set
\mathcal{M}_{n}
chosen uniformly at random from the set of subsets of
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
with cardinality constraint
|\mathcal{M}_{n}|\leq c_{n}^{(\varepsilon-1)}.

Proof of Proposition
8
.

Using the well-known expression for the trace distance of two states
\rho
and
\sigma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\max_{0\leq\Lambda\leq\mathbbm{1}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda(\rho-\sigma)\right)=\max_{\pi\text{ orthogonal projection}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi(\rho-\sigma)\right),

assumption (
30
) gives that there exists a sequence of orthogonal projections
\left(\pi_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{n}\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}\right)\geq 1-c_{n},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{n}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{Z_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\right)\leq c_{n}.

Moreover, define as above for
m\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}

\displaystyle\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}=\sum_{z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}p_{z_{n}}|z_{n}\oplus m\rangle\!\langle z_{n}\oplus m|\otimes\rho^{z_{n}}_{E_{n}}=(U_{m}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}})\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}(U_{m}^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}}),

with unitary
U_{m}
on system
Z_{n}
defined by
U_{m}|x\rangle=|\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}(m)\rangle.
Furthermore, we write
\pi_{n}^{m}=(U_{m}^{*}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}})\pi_{n}(U_{m}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n}}).

Note

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}=\sum_{z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}p_{z_{n}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho^{z_{n}}_{E_{n}}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}},

where we have used for the first equality that for every
z_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}
the function
\mathcal{E}_{z_{n}}
is a bijection.
For
K_{n}\leq\lfloor 1/c_{n}^{(1-\varepsilon)}\rfloor
being a natural number let
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\left(m_{1},\cdots,m_{K_{n}}\right)\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{K_{n}}
be random vector such that each component of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is picked uniformly at random from
\mathcal{Z}_{n}.
For every
k\in[K_{n}]:=\{1,\cdots,K_{n}\}
we can calculate the expectation

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{m_{k}}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\sum_{l\in[K_{n}]\setminus\{k\}}\pi^{m_{l}}_{n}\right)\right]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|^{K_{n}}}\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{K_{n}}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{m_{k}}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\sum_{l\in[K_{n}]\setminus\{k\}}\pi^{m_{l}}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|^{K_{n}-1}}\sum_{m_{1},\cdots,m_{k-1},m_{k+1},\cdots,m_{K}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\sum_{l\in[K_{n}]\setminus\{k\}}\pi^{m_{l}}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in[K_{n}]\setminus\{k\}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}}{|\mathcal{Z}_{n}|}\otimes\rho_{E_{n}}\pi_{n}\right)\leq|\mathcal{M}_{n}|c_{n}\leq c_{n}^{\varepsilon}

Define for all
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
the subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E_{n}}
denoted by
V_{m}=\operatorname{supp}(\pi_{n}^{m})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\{m\}}\pi_{n}^{m^{\prime}})^{\perp}
and
\Lambda_{n}^{m}
the orthogonal projection on
V_{m}.
Note, that by definition all
\Lambda^{m}_{n}
have mutually orthogonal supports and hence
\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\Lambda^{m}_{n}\leq\mathbbm{1}
, which means we can extend the family
(\Lambda_{n}^{m})_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}
to a POVM (i.e. by redefining
\Lambda^{m}_{n}=\mathbbm{1}-\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\{m\}}\Lambda^{m^{\prime}}_{n}
for one fixed
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
).

Using the notation
P_{V}
for the orthogonal projection onto a subspace
V\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E_{n}}
we note for all
m\in\mathcal{M}_{n}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda_{n}^{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{n}^{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)-\mathrm{Tr}\left(P_{\operatorname{supp}(\pi^{m}_{n})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\{m\}}\pi_{n}^{m^{\prime}})}\,\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{n}\rho_{Z_{n}E_{n}}\right)-\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(P_{\operatorname{supp}(\sum_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\setminus\{m\}}\pi_{n}^{m^{\prime}})}\,\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{n}^{m}\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\right)-\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{m}_{\mathcal{E}_{Z_{n}}(M)E_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
m^{\prime}\neq m\end{subarray}}\pi_{n}^{m^{\prime}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-c_{n}-c_{n}^{\varepsilon}\geq 1-2c_{n}^{\varepsilon}.

Moreover, by Markov’s inequality, we know that for every
\delta>0

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\big[\,\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)\leq 1-\delta\big]\leq\frac{\mathbbm{E}_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left[1-\mathbbm{P}_{\Lambda,n}(\hat{M}=m|M=m)\right]}{\delta}\leq\frac{2c_{n}^{\varepsilon}}{\delta}

Hence, choosing
\delta=c_{n}^{\varepsilon/2}
gives the desired result.

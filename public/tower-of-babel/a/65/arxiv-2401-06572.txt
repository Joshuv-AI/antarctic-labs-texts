A rigidity result for coisotropic submanifolds in contact geometry
Stephane Geudens, Alfonso G. Tortorella

Abstract
. We study coisotropic deformations of a compact regular coisotropic submanifold C C in a contact manifold ( M , ξ ) (M,\xi) . Our main result states that C C is rigid among nearby coisotropic submanifolds whose characteristic foliation is diffeomorphic to that of C C . When combined with a classical rigidity result for foliations, this yields conditions under which C C is rigid among all nearby coisotropic submanifolds.



Introduction

This paper is devoted to deformations of a compact coisotropic submanifold
C
in a contact manifold
(M,\xi)
. We will additionally assume that the coisotropic submanifold
C
is generic, in the sense that
C
is transverse to the hyperplane distribution
\xi
. Such coisotropic submanifolds are called regular. The coisotropic deformation problem of a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
is known to be quite involved. First, it is highly non-linear, because deformations are given by Maurer-Cartan elements of a suitable
L_{\infty}
-algebra
[
10
]
. Second, the deformation problem is obstructed
[
12
]
, meaning that there may exist first order deformations which are not tangent to any path of deformations. All of this is in sharp contrast with the deformation problem of a compact Legendrian submanifold
L\subset(M,\xi)
. The latter is linear hence unobstructed, and moreover compact Legendrians are rigid, in the sense that smooth Legendrian deformations are generated by contact isotopies.

A regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
carries a characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Its properties, especially its instability, seem to play an important role in the coisotropic deformation problem of
C
, as demonstrated by the following results:

•

In
[
12
, §3.1]
the second author constructed an obstructed example, featuring a compact regular coisotropic submanifold
C
which has arbitrarily
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-small deformations whose characteristic foliation is not diffeomorphic to that of
C
.

•

A compact regular coisotropic submanifold
C
is called integral if its characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is simple, i.e. given by the fibers of a fiber bundle
C\rightarrow C/\mathcal{F}
. When deformed within the class of integral coisotropic submanifolds,
C
is rigid under contact isotopies, as proved by the second author
[
12
]
. In particular, this deformation problem is unobstructed.

These results are our motivation for considering a restricted version of the coisotropic deformation problem of
C
. Instead of allowing all
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-small coisotropic deformations of
C
, we will only consider those whose characteristic foliation is diffeomorphic to that of
C
. In other words, we deform
C
within the class

\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C):=\big\{C^{\prime}\subset(M,\xi)\ \text{regular coisotropic}:\ \exists\ \text{foliated diffeomorphism}\ (C,\mathcal{F})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}(C^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\big\}.

Our main result (see Thm.
4.1
) states that
C
is rigid, when deformed inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
.

Main Theorem
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact regular coisotropic submanifold, and let
C_{t}
be a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
with
C_{0}=C
.
Then there exists an isotopy of contactomorphisms
\psi_{t}\colon(M,\xi)\to(M,\xi)
, locally defined around
C
, such that
C_{t}=\psi_{t}(C)
for small enough
t
.

Clearly, for a path of regular coisotropic submanifolds to be generated by a contact isotopy, the diffeomorphism type of the characteristic foliation needs to be constant along that path. Our result shows that constancy of the foliation is in fact the only obstruction.
When the characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is simple, our theorem reduces to the rigidity result for integral coisotropic submanifolds from
[
12
]
mentioned above.

Our Main Theorem is a rigidity result for the
restricted
deformation problem of
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. It can be combined with a well-known rigidity theorem for foliations to obtain a rigidity result for the classical coisotropic deformation problem, which allows
all
coisotropic deformations of
C
(see Cor.
5.1
). The rigidity result for foliations we are referring to – which appeared in various versions
[
7
,
3
,
2
]
– concerns Hausdorff foliations, i.e. those
\mathcal{F}
for which the leaf space
C/\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff when endowed with the quotient topology. Such a foliation has a generic leaf
L
, to which all leaves in an open dense subset are diffeomorphic.

Corollary
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact, connected regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff and that its generic leaf
L
satisfies
H^{1}(L)=0
. If
C_{t}
is a smooth path of coisotropic submanifolds with
C_{0}=C
, then there is an isotopy
\psi_{t}
of locally defined contactomorphisms such that
C_{t}=\psi_{t}(C)
for small
t
.

Overview of the paper.

In Section
1
, we collect some background information concerning coisotropic submanifolds in contact geometry. We recall that first order deformations of a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
, modulo those induced by contact isotopies, are given by the cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
. Here
\ell:=TC/(TC\cap\xi)
is the quotient line bundle on
C
, which carries a canonical
T\mathcal{F}
-representation
\nabla^{\ell}
. With the aim of this note in mind, we also recall that first order deformations of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
, modulo those induced by isotopies, are given by the first foliated cohomology group with values in the normal bundle
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
.

In Sections
2
and
3
, we study the deformation problem of
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
at the infinitesimal level.
We first argue what are the appropriate first order deformations when deforming
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. By the above, these should be closed elements in
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
which in some way give rise to a trivial class in
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
. To achieve this assignment, we construct a non-canonical chain map (see Def.
2.4
and Thm.
2.14
)

\Phi_{G}\colon\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),-d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\big),

which depends on a choice of complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}\subset TC
. We then show that first order deformations of
C
are closed elements in
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
whose image under
\Phi_{G}
is exact (see Thm.
2.6
). It turns out that the map induced in cohomology
[\Phi]:H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
is canonical (see Thm.
2.19
) and injective (see Prop.
3.1
), hence first order deformations of
C\subset\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
are in fact trivial in
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
. This means exactly that
C
is infinitesimally rigid under contact isotopies, when deformed inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. In particular, this deformation problem is unobstructed (see Cor.
3.4
). This is the contact analog of an unobstructedness result for coisotropic submanifolds in symplectic geometry due to the first author
[
5
]
.

The infinitesimal rigidity result proved in Section
3
raises the question whether
C
is actually rigid inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. We show in Section
4
that this is indeed the case, by establishing our Main Theorem mentioned above. The proof relies on an extension of Gray stability for contact structures to the case of pre-contact structures (see Prop.
4.3
) and a uniqueness result concerning coisotropic embeddings in contact manifolds (see Prop.
4.5
). In Section
5
, we prove the above Corollary concerning coisotropic submanfolds with Hausdorff foliation.

Throughout the paper, we make frequent use of the fact that (pre-)contact structures can be viewed as (pre-)symplectic forms on a certain Lie algebroid, called Atiyah algebroid
[
13
]
. This point of view allows one to study (pre-)contact structures using (pre-)symplectic geometry. We recall the details of this correspondence in the Appendix.

1.
Background and setup

1.1.
Coisotropic submanifolds in contact geometry

We recall some background information about contact manifolds and their coisotropic submanifolds, following
[
10
, §5]
. Let
M
be a manifold with a hyperplane distribution
\xi
. Denoting the quotient line bundle by
L:=TM/\xi
, the distribution
\xi
is the kernel of the
structure form

\theta\in\Gamma(T^{*}M\otimes L)
of
(M,\xi)
, given by

\theta:TM\rightarrow L:X\mapsto X\ \text{mod}\ \xi.

The
curvature form

\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\xi^{*}\otimes L)
of
(M,\xi)
is defined by

\omega(X,Y)=\theta([X,Y]),\hskip 28.45274ptX,Y\in\Gamma(\xi),

and we denote the associated vector bundle morphism by

\omega^{\flat}:\xi\rightarrow\xi^{*}\otimes L:X\mapsto\omega(X,-).

The characteristic distribution of
(M,\xi)
is given by
\ker\omega^{\flat}
. It is an involutive distribution which is singular in general.

Definition 1.1
.

We call
(M,\xi)
a
pre-contact
manifold if the characteristic distribution
\ker\omega^{\flat}
has constant rank. In that case, we refer to
\text{rk}(\ker\omega^{\flat})
as the
rank
of
(M,\xi)
. The foliation
\mathcal{F}
integrating
\ker\omega^{\flat}
is the
characteristic foliation
of
(M,\xi)
.

The curvature form
\omega
of
(M,\xi)
vanishes exactly when
\xi
is integrable. Consequently, we say that
\xi
is maximally non-integrable if
\omega
is non-degenerate, i.e.
\ker\omega^{\flat}
is trivial.

Definition 1.2
.

We call
(M,\xi)
a
contact
manifold if
\xi
is maximally non-integrable.

Let
C
be a submanifold of a contact manifold
(M,\xi)
. The intersection
\xi_{C}:=TC\cap\xi
is a generically singular distribution on
C
– if
C
is transverse to
\xi
, then
\xi_{C}
is a hyperplane distribution on
C
. Denote by
\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}
the bundle (possibly of varying rank) of
\omega
-orthogonals of
\xi_{C}
in
(\xi,\omega)
, i.e.

\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}=\{v\in\xi:\ \omega(v,w)=0\ \ \forall w\in\xi_{C}\}.

Definition 1.3
.

A submanifold
C
of a contact manifold
(M,\xi)
is
coisotropic
if
(\xi_{C})_{p}
is a coisotropic subspace of
(\xi_{p},\omega_{p})
for all
p\in C
, i.e.
\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}\subset\xi_{C}
. We call
C

regular coisotropic
if
C
is coisotropic and transverse to
\xi
.

In this note, we focus on regular coisotropic submanifolds.

Remark 1.4
.

A submanifold
C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)
is regular coisotropic exactly when
\xi_{C}
is a pre-contact structure of rank
2n-k
. Indeed, because
\ker(\omega|_{\xi_{C}}^{\flat})=\xi_{C}\cap\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}
, we have the following equivalences:

\displaystyle C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)\ \text{is regular coisotropic}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\Leftrightarrow\dim(\xi_{C})_{p}=k\ \text{and}\ \big(\xi_{C}\cap\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}\big)_{p}=\big(\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}\big)_{p}\ \ \ \forall p\in C,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\Leftrightarrow\dim(\xi_{C})_{p}=k\ \text{and}\ \dim\big(\xi_{C}\cap\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}\big)_{p}=2n-k\ \ \ \forall p\in C,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\Leftrightarrow\xi_{C}\ \text{is pre-contact of rank}\ 2n-k.

Hence, a regular coisotropic submanifold
C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)
carries a characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
, which integrates the distribution
\xi_{C}^{\perp_{\omega}}
and has
(2n-k)
-dimensional leaves.

We also mention an alternative characterization of regular coisotropic submanifolds, which will be important in Section
4
. It is reminiscent of the definition of coisotropic submanifolds in symplectic geometry, relying on the fact that contact structures can be viewed as symplectic Atiyah forms. For the necessary background and notation concerning Atiyah forms, we refer to the Appendix.

Proposition 1.5

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be a contact manifold with quotient line bundle
L
, and denote by
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(L)
its symplectic Atiyah form. Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a submanifold transverse to
\xi
, with restricted line bundle
L_{C}:=L|_{C}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

C
is a regular coisotropic submanifold of
(M,\xi)
,

(2)

the pullback
i^{*}\Omega\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(L_{C})
is a pre-symplectic Atiyah form with kernel
(DL_{C})^{\perp_{\Omega}}
. Here
(DL_{C})^{\perp_{\Omega}}
denotes the
\Omega
-orthogonal of
DL_{C}
inside
DL
.

A special case of
[
9
, Thm. 3]
provides a normal form for a contact manifold
(M,\xi)
around a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
, similar to Gotay’s theorem in symplectic geometry
[
6
]
. Before introducing the local model, we have to fix some notation. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact manifold
(C,\xi_{C})
. Denote by
\ell:=TC/\xi_{C}
its line bundle, and by
\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
its structure form. The local model lives on a neighborhood of the zero section of the vector bundle
p:T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell\rightarrow C
. Its construction depends on a choice of complement
G
to the characteristic distribution
T\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
. The model involves the following elements of
\Omega^{1}\big(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell,p^{*}\ell\big)
:

i)

We pull back the structure form
\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
to
p^{*}\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}\big(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell,p^{*}\ell\big)
,

ii)

We define
\theta_{G}\in\Omega^{1}\big(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell,p^{*}\ell\big)
as follows. For
\alpha\in(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)_{x}
and
v\in T_{\alpha}(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
,

(\theta_{G})_{\alpha}(v)=\big\langle\alpha,\text{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}(dp(v))\big\rangle.

Here
\text{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}:TC\rightarrow T\mathcal{F}
is the projection in the splitting
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
.

It turns out that the kernel of

\gamma_{G}:=p^{*}\theta_{C}+\theta_{G}\in\Omega^{1}\big(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell,p^{*}\ell\big)

defines a contact structure on a neighborhood
U
of
C\subset T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell
. We call
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
the
contact thickening
of
(C,\xi_{C})
. This is the local model for
(M,\xi)
around
C
.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
9
]

If
C\subset(M,\xi)
is a regular coisotropic submanifold, then a neighborhood of
C
in
M
can be identified with the contact thickening
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
.

By Thm.
1.6
, studying
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-small coisotropic deformations of a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
amounts to studying sections of the contact thickening
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
whose graph is coisotropic. Note here that small enough coisotropic deformations of
C
are automatically regular. The following result describes regular coisotropic sections of
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
. For any section
\eta\in\Gamma(U)
, the following are equivalent:

i)

\text{Graph}(\eta)\subset(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
is regular coisotropic,

ii)

\ker\big(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
is a pre-contact structure of rank
(2n-k)
on
C
.

Recall that we fixed a complement
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
. The map
\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}
appearing in
ii)
above is the dual of the projection
\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}:TC\rightarrow T\mathcal{F}
along
G
.

Proof.

The section
\eta\in\Gamma(U)
defines a diffeomorphism

\sigma_{\eta}:T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell\rightarrow T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell:(x,\alpha)\mapsto(x,\alpha+\eta(x)),

which is just the translation by
\eta
. Note that
\sigma_{\eta}
preserves the fibers of
p:T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell\rightarrow C
, hence it induces a well-defined pullback on
\Omega^{1}(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell,p^{*}\ell)
. Since
\sigma_{\eta}
takes
C
to
\text{graph}(\eta)
, we have that

\text{graph}(\eta)
is regular coisotropic for
\ker\gamma_{G}
exactly when
C
is regular coisotropic for
(\sigma_{-\eta})_{*}\big(\ker\gamma_{G}\big)=\ker\big(\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\gamma_{G}\big)
. We claim to have the following expression for
\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\gamma_{G}
.

Claim:

\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\gamma_{G}=\gamma_{G}+p^{*}\big(\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
.

To prove the claim, we only have to check that
\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\theta_{G}=\theta_{G}+p^{*}\big(\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
. To this end, we compute for
\alpha\in(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)_{x}
and
v\in T_{\alpha}(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
,

\displaystyle\big(\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\theta_{G}\big)_{\alpha}(v)

\displaystyle=\big(\theta_{G}\big)_{\alpha+\eta(x)}((\sigma_{\eta})_{*}v)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\alpha+\eta(x),\text{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}(p_{*}(\sigma_{\eta})_{*}v)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big(\theta_{G}\big)_{\alpha}(v)+\big\langle\eta(x),\text{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}(p_{*}v)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big(\theta_{G}\big)_{\alpha}(v)+\big(p^{*}\big(\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)\big)_{\alpha}(v).

The result now follows from Rem.
1.4
, since the pullback of
\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\gamma_{G}
to
C
reads

i^{*}(\sigma_{\eta}^{*}\gamma_{G})=\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta.\qed

Remark 1.8
.

The proof of Lemma
1.7
shows that the graph of any section
\eta\in\Gamma(U)
is automatically transverse to the hyperplane distribution
\ker\gamma_{G}
. Therefore, the regularity requirement is automatically met. To see why
\text{graph}(\eta)\pitchfork\ker\gamma_{G}
, note that this is equivalent with
C\pitchfork\ker\big(\gamma_{G}+p^{*}\big(\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)\big)
, which in turn is equivalent with
\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta
being nowhere zero. The latter statement holds, for if
\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta
would vanish at some point, then
\theta_{C}
would vanish there, which is impossible.

Lemma
1.7
has the advantage that it describes regular coisotropic deformations of
C
in terms of information that lives on the manifold
C
itself. It
shows in particular that small coisotropic deformations of
C
induce deformations of the pre-contact structure
\xi_{C}
with the same rank, i.e. there is an assignment

\text{Def}_{coiso}(C)\rightarrow\text{Def}_{pre-contact}(\xi_{C}):\eta\mapsto\ker\big(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big).

1.2.
Coisotropic deformations with fixed characteristic foliation

Assume we are given a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. We aim to study coisotropic deformations
C^{\prime}
of
C
, which are small enough so that they remain regular and whose characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
. More precisely, we are interested in
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-small elements of the following space.

Definition 1.9
.

We define
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
to be the space of regular coisotropic submanifolds
C^{\prime}\subset(M,\xi)
for which there exists a foliated diffeomorphism
(C,\mathcal{F})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}(C^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
.

Thanks to Thm.
1.6
, we can pass from
(M,\xi)
to the contact thickening
(U,\gamma_{G})
. Doing so turns the problem into studying coisotropic sections
\eta\in\Gamma(U,\gamma_{G})
whose characteristic foliation is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
. In this respect, we remark the following.

Lemma 1.10
.

For any coisotropic section
\eta\in\Gamma(U,\gamma_{G})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

there is a diffeomorphism
\psi:C\rightarrow\text{graph}(\eta)
which takes
\mathcal{F}
to the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact structure
\ker(i^{*}\gamma_{G})
,

(2)

there is a diffeomorphism
\phi:C\rightarrow C
which takes
\mathcal{F}
to to the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact structure
\ker\big(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
.

Proof.

The section
\eta
defines a diffeomorphism
\tau_{\eta}:C\rightarrow\text{graph}(\eta)
which, as shown in the proof of Lemma
1.7
, pulls back
i^{*}\gamma_{G}\in\Omega^{1}(\text{graph}(\eta),p^{*}\ell|_{\text{graph}(\eta)})
to
\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
. The inverse of
\tau_{\eta}
is just the restriction of the bundle projection
p|_{\text{graph}(\eta)}
. Hence, given
\psi:C\rightarrow\text{graph}(\eta)
as in
(1)
above, we can set
\phi:=p|_{\text{graph}(\eta)}\circ\psi
. Conversely, given given
\phi
as in
(2)
above, we can set
\psi=\tau_{\eta}\circ\phi
.
∎

With Lemma
1.10
in mind, we now introduce a space
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
which serves as a local model for
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. The space
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
is the main object of study in this note.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
and contact thickening
(U,\gamma_{G})
. We define
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
to be the space of sections
\eta\in\Gamma(U)
such that

\begin{cases}\text{Graph}(\eta)\ \text{is coisotropic in}\ (U,\gamma_{G})\\
\text{There is a foliated diffeomorphism}\ (C,\mathcal{F})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\big(C,\mathcal{F}_{\eta}\big)\end{cases}.

Here
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}
is the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact structure
\ker\big(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
.

1.3.
First order deformations of coisotropic submanifolds and foliations

There are two classes of geometric structures whose deformation theory is important in this note, namely coisotropic submanifolds of contact manifolds and foliations. We will now recall the cochain complexes underlying first order deformations of these objects.

1.3.1.
First order deformations of coisotropic submanifolds

Let
C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. First order deformations of
C
as a coisotropic submanifold – or equivalently, as a regular coisotropic submanifold (see Rem.
1.8
)– are
1
-cocycles in the complex
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)
, which is defined as follows.
Note that the line bundle
\ell=TC/\xi_{C}
carries a flat
T\mathcal{F}
-connection
\nabla^{\ell}
, given by

\nabla^{\ell}_{X}\theta_{C}(Y)=\theta_{C}([X,Y]),\hskip 14.22636ptX\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}),Y\in\mathfrak{X}(C),

(1)

where
\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
is the structure form of
\xi_{C}
. This connection induces a differential
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}
on
\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
, defined by the usual Koszul formula

\displaystyle d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\eta(X_{0},\ldots,X_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\nabla^{\ell}_{X_{i}}\big(\eta(X_{0},\ldots,X_{i-1},\widehat{X_{i}},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{m})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\eta\big([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{0},\ldots,\widehat{X_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{X_{j}},\ldots,X_{m}\big).

(2)

The resulting complex
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)
is the one that governs first order deformations of
C
. This follows in particular from the main result in
[
10
, §5]
, which shows that coisotropic deformations of
C
correspond with Maurer-Cartan elements of a certain
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell)[1]
whose unary bracket coincides with the differential
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}
. Below, we present a simple geometric proof for this fact which avoids the use of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
C^{k+1}\subset(M^{2n+1},\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
and local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
. If
\eta_{t}
is a smooth path of coisotropic sections of
U
starting at the zero section
\eta_{0}=0
, then
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}
is closed with respect to
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.7
, we get a smooth family of pre-contact forms
\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
. Denoting the characteristic foliation of
\ker(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})
by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, we have a smooth family of rank
2n-k
distributions
T\mathcal{F}_{t}
on
C
.
Choose a local frame
X_{1}^{t},\ldots,X_{2n-k}^{t}
of
T\mathcal{F}_{t}
that is smoothly depending on
t
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})(X^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle)=0,

(3)

\displaystyle(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})([X^{t}_{i}

\displaystyle,X^{t}_{j}])=0,

(4)

for all
i,j=1,\ldots,2n-k
.
Differentiating the equation (
3
) at time
t=0
and using the hypothesis
\eta_{0}=0
, one gets that

\theta_{C}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{X}^{0}_{i})+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}(X^{0}_{i})=0,

(5)

for all
i=1,\ldots,2n-k
.
Similarly, differentiating the equation (
4
) at time
t=0
and using
\eta_{0}=0
as well as the equality (
5
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\theta_{C}([\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{X}^{0}_{i},X^{0}_{j}])+\theta_{C}([X^{0}_{i},\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{X}^{0}_{j}])+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}([X^{0}_{i},X^{0}_{j}])

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{\ell}_{X^{0}_{j}}(\theta_{C}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{X}^{0}_{i}))+\nabla^{\ell}_{X^{0}_{i}}(\theta_{C}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{X}^{0}_{j}))+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}([X^{0}_{i},X^{0}_{j}])

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\ell}_{X^{0}_{j}}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}(X^{0}_{i}))-\nabla^{\ell}_{X^{0}_{i}}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}(X^{0}_{j}))+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}([X^{0}_{i},X^{0}_{j}])

\displaystyle=(d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})(X^{0}_{j},X^{0}_{i}),

for all
i,j=1,\ldots,2n-k
.
This proves that
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}
vanishes.
∎

We now consider first order deformations of
C\subset(M,\xi)
that are geometrically trivial, in the sense that they arise from applying a contact isotopy of
(M,\xi)
to
C
. Recall that a contact isotopy is the flow of a time-dependent contact vector field on
(M,\xi)
.

Remark 1.13
.

Given a contact manifold
(M,\xi)
with quotient line bundle
L=TM/\xi
and structure form
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M,L)
, the space of contact vector fields
\mathfrak{X}_{\xi}(M)
fits in a canonical direct sum decomposition
[
10
, Prop. 5.5]

\mathfrak{X}(M)=\mathfrak{X}_{\xi}(M)\oplus\Gamma(\xi).

It follows that there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

X_{(-)}:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{\xi}(M):\lambda\mapsto X_{\lambda},

(6)

which is defined by setting
X_{\lambda}
to be the unique contact vector field satisfying
\theta(X_{\lambda})=\lambda
.

It turns out that first order deformations of
C
arising from contact isotopies are cohomologically trivial, i.e. one-coboundaries in the complex
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)
. This also follows from the main result in
[
10
]
, since the notion of gauge equivalence in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell)[1]
described there agrees with the equivalence relation by contact isotopies. Again, we believe it is instructive to give a geometric proof which avoids the use of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Proposition 1.14
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
and local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
.
If a smooth path
\eta_{t}
of coisotropic sections of
U
, starting at
\eta_{0}=0
, is generated by a contact isotopy of
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
, then
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}
is a
1
-coboundary in the complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be a contact isotopy of
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
generating
\eta_{t}
, i.e.

\varphi_{t}(C)=\eta_{t}(C).

It yields an isotopy
\psi_{t}:=p\circ\varphi_{t}|_{C}
of
C
satisfying

\varphi_{t}|_{C}=\eta_{t}\circ\psi_{t}.

(7)

We claim that
(\psi_{t})_{*}
matches the pre-contact distributions
\ker\theta_{C}
and
\ker(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})
.
To prove this, note that

\ker\theta_{C}=TC\cap\ker\gamma_{G}=\big((\varphi_{t}|_{C})_{*}\big)^{-1}\ker\gamma_{G},

where we used that
\varphi_{t}
is a contactomorphism. On the other hand,

\ker(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})=\big((\eta_{t})_{*}\big)^{-1}\ker\gamma_{G}.

Along with the relation (
7
), this proves the claim.
It follows that there is a unique isotopy
\Psi_{t}\colon\ell\to\ell
of line bundle automorphisms covering
\psi_{t}\colon C\to C
, such that

\theta_{C}=\Psi_{t}^{\ast}(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t}).

(8)

Denote by
\Delta_{t}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D}\ell)
the time-dependent derivation generating
\Psi_{t}
; its symbol is the time dependent vector field
X_{t}:=\sigma(\Delta_{t})\in\mathfrak{X}(C)
generating
\psi_{t}
.
This means in particular that, for all
\lambda\in\Gamma(\ell)
and
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(C)
,

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Psi_{t}^{\ast}\lambda=\Delta_{0}\lambda\quad\text{and}\quad\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\psi_{t}^{\ast}Y=[X_{0},Y].

Consequently, we have for all
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
and
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(C)
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(\Psi_{t}^{\ast}\alpha)(Y)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Psi_{t}^{\ast}\left(\alpha\big((\psi_{t}^{-1})^{*}Y\big)\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}(\alpha(Y))-\alpha([X_{0},Y]).

(9)

If we evaluate both sides of (
8
) on arbitrary
Y\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})
and then differentiate at time
t=0
using (
9
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\theta_{C}(Y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(\Psi_{t}^{\ast}(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t}))(Y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(\Psi_{t}^{\ast}\theta_{C})(Y)+\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t}(Y)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}(\theta_{C}(Y))-\theta_{C}([X_{0},Y]))+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}(Y)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\ell}_{Y}(\theta_{C}(X_{0}))+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}(Y)

\displaystyle=(d_{\nabla^{\ell}}(\theta_{C}(X_{0}))+\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})(Y).

This shows that
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}=-d_{\nabla^{\ell}}(\theta_{C}(X_{0}))
, i.e. the
1
-cocycle
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}
in
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})
is actually a coboundary, with primitive given by
-\theta_{C}(X_{0})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Prop.
1.12
and Prop.
1.14
motivate the following definition.

Definition 1.15
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be regular coisotropic with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

i)

A
first order deformation
of
C
is an element
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
that is
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}
-closed.

ii)

We call
C

infinitesimally rigid
if the cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
vanishes.

In this note, we are interested in (un)obstructedness of first order deformations.

Definition 1.16
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
and local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
. A first order deformation
\eta
of
C
is said to be
unobstructed
if there exists a smooth path of coisotropic sections
\zeta_{t}\in\Gamma(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
satisfying
\zeta_{0}=0
and
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta_{0}}=\eta
. Otherwise,
\eta
is said to be
obstructed
.

It is well-known that a regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
has obstructed first order deformations in general, see e.g.
[
12
, §3.1]
for such an example. We will prove in this note that the coisotropic deformation problem of
C\subset(M,\xi)
becomes unobstructed if one restricts to deformations whose characteristic foliation is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
.

1.3.2.
First order deformations of foliations

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a foliation on a manifold
C
. There is a flat
T\mathcal{F}
-connection on the normal bundle
N\mathcal{F}:=TC/T\mathcal{F}
, called the Bott connection, which is defined by

\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}_{X}(Y\ \text{mod}\ T\mathcal{F})=[X,Y]\ \text{mod}\ T\mathcal{F},\quad X\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}),Y\in\mathfrak{X}(C).

We obtain a differential
d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}
on
\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes N\mathcal{F})
given by the same formula as (
1.3.1
), and we denote by
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\big)
the resulting complex. Heitsch
[
8
]
showed that this complex and its cohomology govern infinitesimal deformations of
\mathcal{F}
, in the following sense.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be a smooth path of foliations with
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}
. Pick a complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}
and identify
G\cong N\mathcal{F}
. If
C
is compact, then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
T\mathcal{F}_{t}
remains transverse to
G
for
0\leq t\leq\epsilon
. Hence, there exist
\eta_{t}\in\Gamma(T^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes N\mathcal{F})
such that

T\mathcal{F}_{t}=\text{graph}(\eta_{t})=\{X+\eta_{t}(X):X\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F})\}.

Lemma 1.17

(
[
8
]
)
.

In the setup described above, we have:

(1)

The infinitesimal deformation
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}
is closed with respect to
d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}
.

(2)

If the path
\mathcal{F}_{t}
is generated by an isotopy
\phi_{t}
, i.e.
T\mathcal{F}_{t}=(\phi_{t})_{*}T\mathcal{F}
, then the corresponding infinitesimal deformation is exact. Indeed,

\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}=d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}(V_{0}\ \text{mod}\ T\mathcal{F}),

where
V_{t}
is the time-dependent vector field corresponding with the isotopy
\phi_{t}
.

Lemma
1.17
motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.18
.

Let
C
be a manifold with a foliation
\mathcal{F}
.

i)

A
first order deformation
of
\mathcal{F}
is an element
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
that is
d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}
-closed.

ii)

We call
\mathcal{F}

infinitesimally rigid
if the cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
vanishes.

2.
First order deformations with fixed characteristic foliation

In this section, we argue what are first order deformations when deforming a regular coisotropic submanifold
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. We show that first order deformations of
C
give rise to trivial first order deformations of
\mathcal{F}
, via a chain map which we introduce below. While the construction of this chain map involves a choice of complement to the characteristic distribution
T\mathcal{F}
, the induced map in cohomology turns out to be completely canonical.

2.1.
Smooth Paths in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)

We first need to define a suitable notion of smoothness for paths in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
and
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A path
C_{t}
in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
is smooth if there is a smooth path of embeddings
\Phi_{t}:C\hookrightarrow M
such that
C_{t}=\Phi_{t}(C)
and
\Phi_{t}:(C,\mathcal{F})\rightarrow(C_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t})
is a foliated diffeomorphism.

It will be important that a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
comes with a smooth path of foliated diffeomorphisms to
(C,\mathcal{F})
. By passing to the contact thickening
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
of
C\subset(M,\xi)
, Def.
2.1
induces a notion of smoothness for paths in
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A path
\eta_{t}
in
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
is smooth if there is a smooth path of embeddings
\Phi_{t}:C\hookrightarrow U
such that
\text{graph}(\eta_{t})=\Phi_{t}(C)
and
p\circ\Phi_{t}:(C,\mathcal{F})\rightarrow(C,\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{t}})
is a foliated diffeomorphism. Here
\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{t}}
is the foliation of the pre-contact structure
\ker(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t})
.

2.2.
Description of first order deformations with fixed foliation

To argue what are first order deformations when deforming a regular coisotropic submanifold
C
within the class
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
, we work in the local model
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
introduced in Def.
1.11
. We have to figure out what properties are satisfied by the infinitesimal deformation
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}
coming from a smooth path
\eta_{t}
in
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
.
One would expect that this infinitesimal deformation gives rise to a trivial infinitesimal deformation of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
, i.e. a
1
-coboundary in the Bott complex
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\big)
.
We will show that this does indeed happen, by means of a certain map
\Phi_{G}
which is defined in terms of a complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}
.

We first introduce some notation. In the sequel, we will freely use concepts related to the Atiyah algebroid and der-complex of a line bundle. See the Appendix for more details.

Let
C
be any manifold.

•

Assume that
\xi_{C}
is a pre-contact distribution on
C
, with line bundle
\ell=TC/\xi_{C}
and structure form
\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
.

•

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the characteristic foliation of
(C,\xi_{C})
. Fix a complement
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
.

•

The pre-contact form
\theta_{C}
corresponds with a pre-symplectic Atiyah form
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(\ell)
, see Prop.
A.5
. Denote
K=\ker\varpi
. The symbol map
\sigma:D\ell\rightarrow TC
restricts to a Lie algebroid isomorphism

\sigma:K\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}T\mathcal{F}.

Clearly, the chosen complement
G
of
T\mathcal{F}
gives rise canonically to a complement
\widetilde{G}
of
K
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The relation
\sigma(\widetilde{G})=G
establishes a canonical 1-1 correspondence between:

i)

vector subbundles
G\subseteq TC
such that
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
,

ii)

vector subbundles
\widetilde{G}\subseteq D\ell
such that
D\ell=K\oplus\widetilde{G}
and
\mathbbm{1}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{G})
.

The definition of the map
\Phi_{G}
involves the following ingredients:

•

The complement
\widetilde{G}
yields a projection
\text{pr}_{K}:D\ell\rightarrow K
.

•

The pre-symplectic Atiyah form
\varpi
restricts to an isomorphism

\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}:\widetilde{G}\rightarrow\big(\widetilde{G}\big)^{*}\otimes\ell.

Hence, if
\beta\in N^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell\simeq G^{*}\otimes\ell
, then
\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma^{*}\beta)
is a well-defined element of
\widetilde{G}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We define

\Phi_{G}\colon\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell)\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})

by setting

\left\langle\beta,\Phi_{G}(\eta)\big(\sigma(\Delta_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(\Delta_{n})\big)\right\rangle=d_{D}(\operatorname{pr}_{K}^{\ast}\sigma^{*}\eta)\left(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n},\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma^{\ast}\beta)\right).

(10)

for
\eta\in\Omega^{n}(\mathcal{F},\ell),\beta\in\Gamma(N^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
and
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}\in\Gamma(K)
.

Remark 2.5
.

The action of
\Phi_{G}
on
\eta\in\Omega^{n}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
can be equivalently described as follows.
First apply the der-differential
d_{D}
to
\operatorname{pr}_{K}^{\ast}\sigma^{*}\eta\in\Omega_{D}^{n}(\ell)
, which yields

d_{D}(\operatorname{pr}_{K}^{\ast}\sigma^{*}\eta)\in\Gamma(\wedge^{n+1}K^{\ast}\otimes\ell)\oplus\Gamma(\wedge^{n}K^{\ast}\otimes\big(\widetilde{G}\big)^{*}\otimes\ell)\oplus\Gamma(\wedge^{n-1}K^{\ast}\otimes\wedge^{2}\big(\widetilde{G}\big)^{*}\otimes\ell).

Then pick the component in
\Gamma(\wedge^{n}K^{\ast}\otimes\big(\widetilde{G}\big)^{*}\otimes\ell)
and apply to it the VB morphism

-\operatorname{id}\otimes(\sigma\circ\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1})\colon\wedge^{\bullet}K^{\ast}\otimes\big(\big(\widetilde{G}\big)^{*}\otimes\ell\big)\longrightarrow\wedge^{\bullet}K^{\ast}\otimes G.

The result is the element
\sigma^{*}\left(\Phi_{G}(\eta)\right)\in\Omega^{n}(K,N\mathcal{F})
, which determines
\Phi_{G}(\eta)\in\Omega^{n}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
.
A choice of splitting
TC=T\mathcal{F}\oplus G
yields the local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
.
Assume that
\eta_{t}\in\Gamma(U)
is a smooth path of coisotropic sections, with
\eta_{0}=0
.

(1)

The infinitesimal deformation
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}
is closed in
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
be the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact form
\theta_{t}:=\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{\ast}\eta_{t}
. For small enough
t
, there is a smooth path
\zeta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
such that
T\mathcal{F}_{t}=\text{graph}(\zeta_{t})
. Moreover,

\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})=\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta}_{0}.

(3)

If
\eta_{t}
is a smooth path in
\text{Def}^{U}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
, then
\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}})
is exact in
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\big)
.

Proof.

(1)

This holds by Prop.
1.12
.

(2)

The pre-contact form
\theta_{t}
corresponds to a pre-symplectic Atiyah form
\varpi_{t}
, given by

\varpi_{t}:=d_{D}(\sigma^{\ast}\theta_{t})=\varpi+d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\eta_{t}).

(11)

We set
K_{t}:=\ker\varpi_{t}
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

T\mathcal{F}_{t}
is transverse to
G
, i.e. there exists
\zeta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},G)
such that
T\mathcal{F}_{t}=\text{graph}(\zeta_{t})
,

(b)

K_{t}
is transverse to
\widetilde{G}
, i.e. there exists
\mu_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(K,\widetilde{G})
such that
K_{t}=\text{graph}(\mu_{t})
.

Compactness of
C
implies that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the equivalent conditions
(a)
and
(b)
hold for
-\epsilon<t<\epsilon
.
In this case,
\zeta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},G)
and
\mu_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(K,\widetilde{G})
are related by

\zeta_{t}\circ\sigma|_{K}=\sigma\circ\mu_{t}.

(12)

We now compute for
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
,

0=\varpi_{t}^{\flat}(\Delta+\mu_{t}(\Delta))=\varpi^{\flat}(\mu_{t}(\Delta))+(d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\eta_{t}))^{\flat}(\Delta+\mu_{t}(\Delta)),

where we used (
11
) and the fact that
K=\ker\varpi
.
Differentiating at time
t=0
and using that
\eta_{0}=0
and
\mu_{0}=0
, we get

0=\varpi^{\flat}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mu_{0}}(\Delta))+(d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}))^{\flat}(\Delta).

With Def.
2.4
in mind, this implies that for any
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
and
\beta\in\Gamma(G^{*}\otimes\ell)
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left\langle\varpi^{\flat}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mu_{0}}(\Delta)),\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma^{\ast}\beta)\right\rangle+\left\langle(d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0}))^{\flat}(\Delta),\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma^{\ast}\beta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\sigma^{\ast}\beta,\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mu_{0}}(\Delta)\right\rangle+\left\langle\beta,\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})(\sigma(\Delta))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\beta,\sigma(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mu_{0}}(\Delta))\right\rangle+\left\langle\beta,\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})(\sigma(\Delta))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\big\langle\beta,\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta_{0}}(\sigma(\Delta))\big\rangle+\left\langle\beta,\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})(\sigma(\Delta))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\beta,\big(\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})-\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta}_{0}\big)(\sigma(\Delta))\right\rangle,

using (
12
) in the fourth equality.
This shows that
\Phi_{G}(\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta}_{0})=\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta}_{0}
.

(3)

By part
(2)
, we only have to show that
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\zeta}_{0}
is exact in
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}})
.
Since
\eta_{t}
is a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}^{U}(C)
, according to Def.
2.2
there exists a smooth family of foliated diffeomorphisms

\phi_{t}\colon(C,T\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow(C,T\mathcal{F}_{t}).

Precomposing
\phi_{t}
with
\phi_{0}^{-1}
, we can assume that

Proposition 2.8
.

The VB isomorphism
\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}\colon D_{\perp}\ell\to J^{1}_{\perp}\ell
intertwines the
K
-representations on
D_{\perp}\ell
and
J^{1}_{\perp}\ell
, i.e. for
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
and
\square\in\Gamma(D\ell)
we have

\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\nabla^{D_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K))=\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K)).

Proof.

Since
d_{D}\varpi=0
and
K=\ker\varpi
, the Cartan formula implies that for
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
,

\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}\varpi=\iota_{\Delta}d_{D}\varpi+d_{D}\iota_{\Delta}\varpi=0.

Hence, we can compute for any
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
and
\square,\square^{\prime}\in\Gamma(D\ell)
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}\varpi)(\square,\square^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Delta(\varpi(\square,\square^{\prime}))-\varpi([\Delta,\square],\square^{\prime})-\varpi(\square,[\Delta,\square^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\ell}_{\Delta}\left\langle\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K),\square^{\prime}\ \text{mod}\ K\right\rangle-\left\langle\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}\big(\nabla^{D_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K)\big),\square^{\prime}\ \text{mod}\ K\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\left\langle\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K),\nabla^{D_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\square^{\prime}\ \text{mod}\ K)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K)),\square^{\prime}\ \text{mod}\ K\right\rangle-\left\langle\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}\big(\nabla^{D_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K)\big),\square^{\prime}\ \text{mod}\ K\right\rangle.

This shows that

\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K))=\varpi_{\perp}^{\flat}\big(\nabla^{D_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta}(\square\ \text{mod}\ K)\big)

for all
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
and
\square\in\Gamma(D\ell)
, as we wanted to prove.
∎

\Phi_{G}\colon\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)\longrightarrow\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),-d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\big).

(26)

Proof.

From Lemma
2.11
and Lemma

we already know that the left and top arrows are chain maps. Moreover, Cor.
2.10
yields a chain map

\wedge^{\bullet}(\sigma|_{K}^{-1})^{*}\otimes(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}):\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\right),

hence changing signs shows that the right arrow is a chain map

-\wedge^{\bullet}(-\sigma|_{K}^{-1})^{*}\otimes(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}):\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right)\longrightarrow\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),-d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}\right).

It remains to check that the diagram commutes.
Pick
\eta\in\Omega^{b}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
.
For
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b}\in\Gamma(K)
and
\beta\in\Gamma(N^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
, we can compute

\displaystyle\left\langle\beta,\Phi_{G}(\eta)\big(\sigma(\Delta_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(\Delta_{b})\big)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\eta)\left(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b},\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\beta)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=(-1)^{b}d_{D}((\sigma\circ\operatorname{pr}_{K})^{\ast}\eta)\left(\varpi^{\flat}|_{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\beta),\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=(-1)^{b}\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}(\eta)\big)\big(\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\beta),\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=(-1)^{b}\left\langle\tau\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}(\eta)\big)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b}),\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\beta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=-(-1)^{b}\left\langle\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\beta,\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}\left(\tau\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}(\eta)\big)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=-(-1)^{b}\left\langle\beta,(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp})\left(\tau\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}(\eta)\big)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{b})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\left\langle\beta,-\left(\wedge^{\bullet}(-\sigma|_{K}^{-1})^{*}\otimes(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp})\circ\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}\circ(\sigma|_{K})^{*}\right)(\eta)\big(\sigma(\Delta_{1}),\ldots,\sigma(\Delta_{b})\big)\right\rangle.

This shows that the diagram commutes, hence the proof is finished.

2.4.
The induced map in cohomology

The chain map
\Phi_{G}
depends on the choice of complement
G
. This dependence is made explicit by Thm.
2.14
, which shows that
\Phi_{G}
is given by the composition

\Phi_{G}=-\wedge^{\bullet}(-\sigma|_{K}^{-1})^{*}\otimes(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}))\circ\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}\circ(\sigma|_{K})^{*}.

The maps appearing above are all canonical, except for
d_{1,0}^{G}
. In this section we show that, by contrast, the map induced in cohomology by
\Phi_{G}
is canonical, i.e. independent of the complement
G
. To prove this, it suffices to show that the map induced in cohomology by the chain map

\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}:\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)\rightarrow\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right)

is canonical. To do so, we will first construct a canonical map
F_{\text{can}}:H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
, and then show that
F_{\text{can}}
agrees with the map induced in cohomology by
\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}
. The construction of
F_{\text{can}}
presented below is the specialization to the Lie pair
(D\ell,K)
of a general construction which works for any Lie pair. In particular, for the Lie pair
(TC,T\mathcal{F})
, the construction recovers the well-known transverse differentiation map
d_{\nu}:H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N^{*}\mathcal{F})
in foliation theory (see e.g.
[
5
, §2.3]
).

The map
F_{\text{can}}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
will arise as the composition of two separate canonical maps. To construct the first component of
F_{\text{can}}
, recall the following complexes:

•

we have the de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(\ell),d_{D})
of
D\ell
with values in
\ell
, whose cohomology we denote by
H^{\bullet}_{D}(\ell)
,

•

we have the de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})
of
K
with values in
\ell
, whose cohomology we denote by
H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)
,

•

we have the subcomplex
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D,K}(\ell),d_{D})\subset(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(\ell),d_{D})
given by

\Omega^{n}_{D,K}(\ell)=\{\eta\in\Omega_{D}^{n}(\ell)\,\mid\,\eta(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})=0,\ \text{for all}\ \Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}\in\Gamma(K)\},

whose cohomology we denote by
H^{\bullet}_{D,K}(\ell)
.

These ingredients fit in a short exact sequence of complexes and chain maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D,K}(\ell),d_{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(\ell),d_{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
incl
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{K}^{\ast}}

where
\iota_{K}\colon K\to D\ell
is the inclusion of the Lie subalgebroid
K\subset D\ell
.
The corresponding long exact sequence in cohomology is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}_{D,K}(\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}_{D}(\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(K,\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n+1}_{D,K}(\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n+1}_{D}(\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n+1}(K,\ell)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{d}}

where the connecting map
\mathfrak{d}\colon H^{n}(K,\ell)\to H^{n+1}_{D,K}(\ell)
is defined by setting

\mathfrak{d}[\iota_{K}^{\ast}\alpha]=[d_{D}\alpha].

Since the der-complex is acyclic (see Rem.
A.3
), the connecting map is an isomorphism

\mathfrak{d}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}H^{\bullet+1}_{D,K}(\ell).

(27)

This is the first component of the map
F_{\text{can}}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
we aim to construct.

We now construct the second component of
F_{\text{can}}
.
Note that for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{D,K}^{n+1}(\ell)
there exists a unique
p(\alpha)\in\Omega^{n}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
such that

\alpha(\square,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})=\left\langle p(\alpha)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}),\overline{\square}\right\rangle,

(28)

for
\square\in\Gamma(D\ell)
and
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}\in\Gamma(K)
. Here we denoted
\overline{\square}:=\square\ \text{mod}\ K\in\Gamma(D_{\perp}\ell)
.
The relation (
28
) defines a map

p\colon\Omega^{\bullet+1}_{D,K}(\ell)\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell).

Lemma 2.15
.

We get a chain map

p\colon\big(\Omega^{\bullet+1}_{D,K}(\ell),d_{D}\big)\longrightarrow\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),-d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right).

Proof.

Pick
\alpha\in\Omega^{n+1}_{D,K}(\ell)
.
For any
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1}\in\Gamma(K)
and
\square\in\Gamma(D\ell)
, we compute

\displaystyle\left\langle p(d_{D}\alpha)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1}),\overline{\square}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=(d_{D}\alpha)(\square,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\cancel{\square(\alpha(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1}))}+\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\Delta_{i}\big(\alpha(\square,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{i},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\phantom{=}-\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\alpha([\Delta_{i},\square],\Delta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{i},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\phantom{=}-\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\alpha(\square,[\Delta_{i},\Delta_{j}],\Delta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{j},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=-\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}\left\langle\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}_{\Delta_{i}}\big(p(\alpha)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{i},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1})\big),\overline{\square}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\phantom{=}-\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\left\langle p(\alpha)\big([\Delta_{i},\Delta_{j}],\Delta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{i},\ldots,\widehat{\Delta}_{j},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1}\big),\overline{\square}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=-\left\langle\left(d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}p(\alpha)\right)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n+1}),\overline{\square}\right\rangle.

This shows that
p\circ d_{D}=-d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\circ p
, so the proof is finished.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

If we choose a distribution
G\subset TC
complementary to
T\mathcal{F}
, then
\Omega^{\bullet}_{D,K}(\ell)
identifies with the subcomplex

\bigoplus_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i\geq 1}{j\geq 0}}\Omega_{D}^{i,j}(\ell)\subset\big(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(\ell),d_{D}\big),

where we used the bi-degree notation introduced in (
19
). The map
p\colon\Omega_{D,K}^{\bullet+1}\to\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
then identifies with the following composition of chain maps

\left(\bigoplus\nolimits_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i\geq 1}{j\geq 0}}\Omega_{D}^{i,j}(\ell),d_{D}\right)\overset{\operatorname{proj}}{\longrightarrow}\big(\Omega_{D}^{1,\bullet}(\ell),d_{0,1}^{G}\big)\overset{\tau}{\longrightarrow}\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),-d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right),

see Lemma
2.12
. This provides an alternative proof for Lemma
2.15
.

Since
p
is a chain map, it induces a map in cohomology

[p]\colon H^{\bullet+1}_{D,K}(\ell)\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),

(29)

which is the second component of the desired canonical map
F_{\text{can}}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\rightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
.

Definition 2.17
.

We define
F_{\text{can}}:=[p]\circ\mathfrak{d}
, i.e.

F_{\text{can}}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),\ [\iota_{K}^{\ast}\alpha]\longmapsto[p(d_{D}\alpha)].

We think of
F_{\text{can}}
as a kind of “transverse differentiation map” for the Lie pair
(K,D\ell)
.
Now we observe that this canonical map
F_{\text{can}}
arises from the (non-canonical) chain map given by
\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}
, for any choice of complement
G
.

Lemma 2.18
.

For any choice of complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}\subset TC
, the chain map

\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}:\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)\rightarrow\left(\Omega^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell),d_{\nabla^{J^{1}_{\perp}\ell}}\right)

induces a map in cohomology which is canonical (i.e. independent of
G
). In fact, we have

[\tau\circ d_{1,0}^{G}]=F_{\text{can}}\colon H^{\bullet}(K,\ell)\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(K,J^{1}_{\perp}\ell).

Proof.

Fix
\alpha\in\Omega^{n}(K,\ell)\simeq\Omega_{D}^{0,n}(\ell)
with
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\alpha=0
. Since
\alpha=\iota_{K}^{*}\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha
with
\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha\in\Omega^{n}_{D}(\ell)
, the definition of
F_{\text{can}}
shows that

F_{\text{can}}[\alpha]=[p(d_{D}(\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha))].

(30)

For any
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}\in\Gamma(K)
and
\square\in\Gamma(\widetilde{G})
, we compute

\displaystyle\left\langle p(d_{D}(\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha))(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}),\overline{\square}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(d_{D}(\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha))(\square,\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle=(d^{G}_{1,0}\alpha)(\overline{\square},\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\tau(d^{G}_{1,0}\alpha)(\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}),\overline{\square}\right\rangle.

This shows that
p(d_{D}(\text{pr}_{K}^{*}\alpha))=(\tau\circ d^{G}_{1,0})(\alpha)
. Along with (
30
), this shows that

F_{\textnormal{can}}[\alpha]=[\tau\circ d^{G}_{1,0}][\alpha].\qed

Finally, we obtain the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.19
.

For any choice of complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}\subset TC
, the chain map

\Phi_{G}\colon(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}})\longrightarrow(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}),-d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}})

induces a map in cohomology which is canonical (i.e. independent of
G
).

Proof.

It follows immediately from Thm.
2.14
and Lemma
2.18
.
∎

Consequently, we can denote the map in cohomology unambiguously by

[\Phi]:H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell)\longrightarrow H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F}).

3.
Infinitesimal rigidity

In this section, we show that a compact regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
is infinitesimally rigid inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. This means that every first order deformation of
C
in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
is induced by a contact isotopy of
(M,\xi)
. Infinitesimal rigidity implies in particular that the deformation problem of
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
is unobstructed.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
[\Phi]:H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
is injective.

Proof.

Pick
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
such that
d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\eta=0
, and
assume that
[\Phi_{G}][\eta]=0
for some (hence any) complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}
.
This means that there exists
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(C)
s.t.

\Phi_{G}(\eta)=d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}(Z\ \operatorname{mod}\ T\mathcal{F}).

(31)

Claim:

\eta=-d_{\nabla^{\ell}}(\theta_{C}(Z))
, where
\theta_{C}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
is the pre-contact form on
C
.

Note that
\theta_{C}\in\Gamma(N^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\ell)
and
\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\theta_{C}=\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}\varpi\in\Gamma(J^{1}_{\perp}\ell)
, where
\varpi
is the pre-symplectic Atiyah form corresponding to
\theta_{C}
.
We can compute for any
\Delta\in\Gamma(K)
,

\displaystyle\big\langle\theta_{C},\Phi_{G}(\eta)\big(\sigma(\Delta)\big)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\theta_{C},(\sigma_{\perp}\circ\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp})\left(\tau\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}\eta\big)\right)(\Delta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\tau\big(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}\eta\big)(\Delta),\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\theta_{C})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}\eta\right)\big(\varpi_{\perp}^{\sharp}(\sigma_{\perp}^{\ast}\theta_{C}),\Delta\big)

\displaystyle=-\left(d_{1,0}^{G}\sigma^{*}\eta\right)\big(\overline{\mathbbm{1}},\Delta\big)

\displaystyle=-\mathbbm{1}(\eta(\sigma(\Delta)))-\cancel{\sigma^{*}\eta(\text{pr}_{K}[\Delta,\mathbbm{1}])},

using Thm.
2.14
in the first equality and Eq.
21
in the last equality. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left\langle\theta_{C},d_{\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}}(Z\ \text{mod}\ T\mathcal{F})\big(\sigma(\Delta)\big)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\theta_{C}\left(\nabla^{N\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma(\Delta)}Z\ \text{mod}\ T\mathcal{F}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta_{C}\big([\sigma(\Delta),Z]\big)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\ell}_{\sigma(\Delta)}\theta_{C}(Z)

\displaystyle=\big(d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\theta_{C}(Z)\big)(\sigma(\Delta)).

In view of Eq. (
31
), this shows that
\eta=-d_{\nabla^{\ell}}(\theta(Z))
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We compare first order deformations of coisotropic submanifolds –under the constraint that the diffeomorphism type of the characteristic foliation is fixed– in symplectic and contact geometry. First, let
C
be a compact coisotropic submanifold of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. As shown in
[
5
]
, when deforming
C
inside the class of coisotropic submanifolds whose characteristic foliation is diffeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}
, first order deformations are closed foliated one-forms
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F})
whose cohomology class lies in the kernel of the transverse differentiation map
d_{\nu}:H^{1}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N^{*}\mathcal{F})\cong H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
. This kernel is infinite dimensional in general, see
[
5
, Ex. 4.4]
. For the same deformation problem in contact geometry, we showed that first order deformations are closed elements
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)
whose cohomology class lies in the kernel of
[\Phi]:H^{1}(\mathcal{F},\ell)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{F},N\mathcal{F})
. By contrast, this kernel is now trivial because of Prop.
3.1
.

We can now refine Def.
2.7
as follows.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. When deforming
C
inside
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
, first order deformations of
C
are
1
-coboundaries in
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F},\ell),d_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big)
.

is infinitesimally rigid with respect to the action by contact isotopies, if we deform
C
within the class

. This implies unobstructedness of the deformation problem.

.

Let

be a compact regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation

. Then

is infinitesimally rigid with respect to the action by contact isotopies, when deforming

inside

inside

Proof.

Denote by

the local model around

in

such that

Recall from §

that

is a

-valued contact form, hence the section

gives rise via the isomorphism (

) to a contact vector field

with flow

. Compactness of

implies that there exists

such that the flow lines of

for each

is a contactomorphism, it takes the characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
of

to that of the coisotropic submanifold

. Hence,

is a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}^{U}(C)

, by the proof of Lemma

Claim:

\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\eta_{0}}=d_{\nabla^{l}}\lambda
.

. Setting

4.
Rigidity

In the previous section, we saw that a compact regular coisotropic submanifold
C\subset(M,\xi)
is infinitesimally rigid in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. This raises the question whether
C
is actually rigid in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. We show below that this is indeed the case. More precisely, we prove that every smooth path
C_{t}
in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
deforming
C
is generated by a contact isotopy of
(M,\xi)
, for small enough times
t
. This extends a rigidity result due to the second author
[
12
]
, which concerns the special case in which the characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by a fibration.
The theorem to which we devote this section is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact regular coisotropic submanifold, and let
C_{t}
be a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
with
C_{0}=C
.
Then there exists an isotopy of contactomorphisms
\psi_{t}\colon(M,\xi)\to(M,\xi)
, locally defined around
C
, such that
C_{t}=\psi_{t}(C)
for small enough
t
.

The proof of this theorem relies on two results which may be interesting in their own right.
The first result is an extension of the Gray stability theorem for contact structures
[
4
, Thm. 2.2.2]
to the case of pre-contact structures (see Prop.
4.3
).
The second one is a uniqueness statement concerning coisotropic embeddings in contact manifolds (see Prop.
4.5
). To prove the announced extension of Gray stability, we need an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given a line bundle
\ell\to C
, consider a smooth path of pre-contact forms
\theta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the characteristic foliation of
(C,\theta_{t})
.
Assume that there exists an isotopy of foliated diffeomorphisms
\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}\colon(C,\mathcal{F}_{0})\longrightarrow(C,\mathcal{F}_{t})
with corresponding time-dependent vector field
V_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(C)
.
If
Z\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{t})
, then

\left(\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}\right)(Z)=\theta_{t}([V_{t},Z]).

Proof.

We know that
Z=(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t})_{*}(X)
for some
X\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{0})
. Consider the family of vector fields
W_{s}\in\Gamma(T\mathcal{F}_{s})
for
s\in[0,1]
defined by

W_{s}=(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{s})_{*}(X).

In particular, we have
W_{t}=Z
. Differentiating the equality
\theta_{s}(W_{s})=0
at time
s=t
gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}\theta_{s}\right)(W_{t})+\theta_{t}\left(\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}W_{s}\right)=\left(\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}\right)(Z)+\theta_{t}\left(\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}W_{s}\right).

The proof is finished if we show that

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}W_{s}=-[V_{t},Z].

(33)

Denoting by
U_{t}
the time-dependent vector field corresponding to the inverse isotopy
\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}^{-1}
, we have

\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}W_{s}=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=t}(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{s}^{-1})^{*}(X)=(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}^{-1})^{*}\big([U_{t},X]\big)=\big[(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}^{-1})^{*}(U_{t}),Z\big].

(34)

It is well-known that the time-dependent vector fields
U_{t}
and
V_{t}
are related by

U_{t}=-(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t})^{*}V_{t},

see
[
1
, §1.1]
. Inserting this equality into (
34
), we see that the equation (
33
) holds.
∎

We can now state and prove the anticipated generalization of the Gray stability theorem.
In what follows, given a line bundle automorphism
\Phi\colon\ell\to\ell
, we use the pushforward of derivations
D\Phi\colon D\ell\rightarrow D\ell
and the pullback of Atiyah forms

, which are recalled in the Appendix.

Proposition 4.3
.

Given a line bundle
\ell\to C
, consider a smooth path of pre-contact forms
\theta_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(C,\ell)
and let
\varpi_{t}\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(\ell)
be the corresponding smooth path of pre-symplectic Atiyah forms.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the characteristic foliation of
(C,\theta_{t})
and by

the kernel of
\varpi_{t}
, so that

.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists an isotopy of foliated diffeomorphisms
\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}\colon(C,\mathcal{F}_{0})\longrightarrow(C,\mathcal{F}_{t})
,

(2)

there exists an isotopy of LB-automorphisms
\Phi_{t}\colon\ell\to\ell
such that
\Phi_{t}^{\ast}\varpi_{t}=\varpi_{0}
,

(3)

there exists an isotopy of LB-automorphisms
\Phi_{t}\colon\ell\to\ell
such that
\Phi_{t}^{\ast}\theta_{t}=\theta_{0}
.

Proof.

(2)
\Longleftrightarrow
(3)

This is easily proved using the relation
\varpi_{t}=d_{D}(\sigma^{\ast}\theta_{t})
or equivalently, the relation
\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}\varpi_{t}=\sigma^{\ast}\theta_{t}
, together with the natural identities
\Phi_{t}^{\ast}\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}=\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}\Phi_{t}^{\ast}
and
\Phi_{t}^{\ast}d_{D}=d_{D}\Phi_{t}^{\ast}
.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1)

Denote by
\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}\colon C\to C
the isotopy of diffeomorphisms underlying
\Phi_{t}\colon\ell\to\ell
.
Then the hypothesis

can be written explicitly as follows:

(\varpi_{0})_{x}(\delta_{x},\delta_{x}^{\prime})=(\Phi_{t})_{x}^{-1}\left((\varpi_{t})_{\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t}(x)}\left((D_{x}\Phi_{t})\delta_{x},(D_{x}\Phi_{t})\delta_{x}^{\prime}\right)\right),

for all

and

.
This implies that the isomorphism

induces an isomorphism

Since

induces a Lie algebroid isomorphism

and
\sigma\circ(D\Phi_{t})=(\underline{\smash{\varphi}}_{t})_{*}\circ\sigma

descends to an isomorphism

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2)

Following the usual Moser argument, we will obtain the desidered isotopy of LB automorphisms as the flow of a suitable smooth path in

.
For any isotopy of LB automorphisms

generated by a smooth path

), this shows that there is an isotopy
\Phi_{t}
satisfying

if and only if we can find smooth paths

and

Here we used that

\displaystyle\left\langle\sigma^{\ast}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\theta}_{t}+j^{1}\big(\theta_{t}(V_{t})\big),\Delta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\theta}_{t}(\sigma(\Delta))+\Delta\big(\theta_{t}(V_{t})\big)

. This finishes the proof.

Remark 4.4
.

A weaker version of the implication
(1)
\Longrightarrow

in the above proposition has appeared in
[

, Section 4.2]
.
There one claims (without proof) that, if the foliation

satisfying

for any

We proved a stronger statement, namely that the isotopy

for any

.

As anticipated, the second ingredient for the proof of our main result is the following uniqueness statement concerning coisotropic embeddings in contact manifolds.

5.
Application to the classical coisotropic deformation problem

Thm.
4.1
is a rigidity result for a restricted version of the coisotropic deformation problem, which only allows deformations inside the class
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
. Combining this result with a well-known rigidity theorem for foliations, we obtain a rigidity result for the classical coisotropic deformation problem, which allows
all
coisotropic deformations of
C
.

The rigidity result in foliation theory we are referring to is due to Hamilton
[
7
]
and Epstein-Rosenberg
[
3
]
. It concerns compact manifolds
C
endowed with a Hausdorff foliation
\mathcal{F}
, meaning that the leaf space
C/\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff when endowed with the quotient topology. If
C
is connected, then there exists a generic leaf
L
such that all leaves in a saturated dense open subset are diffeomorphic to
L
. Hamilton and Epstein-Rosenberg showed that, if the generic leaf satisfies
H^{1}(L)=0
, then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is rigid, i.e. any foliation
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}
on
C
close enough to
\mathcal{F}
is conjugate to
\mathcal{F}
by a diffeomorphism. We will use a smooth
1
-parameter version of this rigidity statement due to Del Hoyo-Fernandes
[
2
]
, which concerns smooth paths of foliations
\mathcal{F}_{t}
deforming
\mathcal{F}
rather than foliations
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}
close to
\mathcal{F}
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
C\subset(M,\xi)
be a compact, connected regular coisotropic submanifold with characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is Hausdorff and that its generic leaf
L
satisfies
H^{1}(L)=0
. Then
C
is rigid, i.e. if
C_{t}
is a smooth path of coisotropic submanifolds with
C_{0}=C
, then there is an isotopy
\psi_{t}
of locally defined contactomorphisms such that
C_{t}=\psi_{t}(C)
for small enough
t
.

Proof.

By Thm.
4.1
, we only have to show that
C_{t}
is a smooth path in
\text{Def}_{\mathcal{F}}(C)
for small enough
t
. Working in the local model
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
for
(M,\xi)
around
C
, the smooth path
C_{t}
is identified with a smooth path of regular coisotropic submanifolds
\text{graph}(\eta_{t})
, for sections
\eta_{t}\in\Gamma(U)
with
\eta_{0}=0
. By Lemma
1.10
, we only need to check whether there exists a smooth path of foliated diffeomorphisms

\varphi_{t}:(C,\mathcal{F})\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\left(C,\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{t}}\right),

where
\mathcal{F}_{\eta_{t}}
is the characteristic foliation of the pre-contact structure
\ker\big(\theta_{C}+\operatorname{pr}_{T\mathcal{F}}^{*}\eta\big)
. The assumptions on
\mathcal{F}
ensure that such diffeomorphisms
\varphi_{t}
exist for small enough
t
, by
[
2
]
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

Consider the manifold
C:=S^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
with hyperplane distribution
\xi_{C}
given at points
(p,q)\in S^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
by

(\xi_{C})_{(p,q)}=T_{p}S^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}\left(\partial_{\theta_{1}}\right)_{q}\oplus\mathbb{R}\left(\cos\theta_{1}\partial_{\theta_{2}}-\sin\theta_{1}\partial_{\theta_{3}}\right)_{q}.

Note that
\xi_{C}
is the kernel of the nowhere vanishing one-form

\theta_{C}=\sin\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}+\cos\theta_{1}d\theta_{3}.

We claim that
\xi_{C}
is a pre-contact distribution with characteristic foliation given by the fibers of the bundle
S^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{3}
. To see this, we trivialize the quotient line bundle
\ell=TC/\xi_{C}
via
\theta_{C}
, so that the curvature form
\omega\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\xi_{C}^{*}\otimes\ell)
is identified with
-d\theta_{C}|_{\xi_{C}\times\xi_{C}}
. Because

\displaystyle\theta_{C}\wedge d\theta_{C}

\displaystyle=\left(\sin\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}+\cos\theta_{1}d\theta_{3}\right)\wedge\left(\cos\theta_{1}d\theta_{1}\wedge d\theta_{2}-\sin\theta_{1}d\theta_{1}\wedge d\theta_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=d\theta_{1}\wedge d\theta_{2}\wedge d\theta_{3},

it follows that the curvature form has constant rank. Hence
\xi_{C}
is a pre-contact distribution, and its characteristic foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{(p,q)}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\ker\left(d\theta_{C}|_{\xi_{C}\times\xi_{C}}\right)_{(p,q)}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in(\xi_{C})_{(p,q)}:\iota_{v}(\theta_{C}\wedge d\theta_{C})=0\right\}

\displaystyle=T_{p}S^{2}.

A choice of complement
G
to
T\mathcal{F}
allows one to construct the contact thickening
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
of the pre-contact manifold
(C,\xi_{C})
, see §
1.1
. Moreover,
(C,\xi_{C})
embeds as a regular coisotropic submanifold into the contact manifold
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
. It follows from Cor.
5.1
that
C
is rigid, when deformed as a coisotropic submanifold of
(U,\ker\gamma_{G})
.

We review some background material about the Atiyah algebroid of a line bundle and the corresponding der-complex. We also recall that pre-contact structures can be viewed as pre-symplectic forms on this algebroid, as shown in

Definition A.1
.

Let
L\rightarrow M
be a line bundle. An
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\square:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow\Gamma(L)
is called a
derivation
of
L
if there exists some
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that

\square(f\lambda)=X(f)\lambda+f\square(\lambda),\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\lambda\in\Gamma(L),f\in C^{\infty}(M).

The vector field
X
is unique; it is called the
symbol
of
\square
and will be denoted by
\sigma(\square)
.

The space of derivations of
L
is the module of sections of a vector bundle
DL\rightarrow M
. The fiber
(DL)_{x}
over a point
x\in M
consists of the
derivations of
\mathbf{L}
at
\mathbf{x}
, i.e.
\mathbb{R}
-linear maps
\delta:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow L_{x}
for which there exists a (necessarily unique) tangent vector
v\in T_{x}M
such that
\delta(f\lambda)=v(f)\lambda_{x}+f(x)\delta(\lambda)
for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\lambda\in\Gamma(L)
. Here
v
is also called the symbol of
\delta
, again denoted by
\sigma(\delta)
. The vector bundle
DL
satisfies
\text{rk}(DL)=\dim(M)+1
.

Definition A.2
.

The vector bundle
DL\rightarrow M
is a transitive Lie algebroid, called the
Atiyah algebroid
of
L
. Its Lie bracket is the commutator of derivations
[-,-]
, and the anchor map is given by the symbol
\sigma:DL\rightarrow TM
.

The Lie algebroid
DL
has a tautological representation
\nabla
on
L
, given by

\nabla_{\square}\lambda=\square\lambda,\hskip 28.45274pt\square\in\Gamma(DL),\lambda\in\Gamma(L).

Accordingly, we obtain the
der-complex

\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L):=\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}(DL)^{*}\otimes L)
of
L
, consisting of
L
-valued
Atiyah forms
. One has the usual Cartan calculus involving the following operations.

(1)

The differential
d_{D}:\Omega^{k}_{D}(L)\rightarrow\Omega^{k+1}_{D}(L)
is defined by the usual Koszul formula

\displaystyle d_{D}\eta(\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\triangle_{i}\big(\eta(\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{i-1},\widehat{\triangle_{i}},\triangle_{i+1},\ldots,\triangle_{k+1})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\eta\big([\triangle_{i},\triangle_{j}],\triangle_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\triangle_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{\triangle_{j}},\ldots,\triangle_{k+1}\big).

(2)

For any
\square\in\Gamma(DL)
, the contraction
\iota_{\square}:\Omega^{k}_{D}(L)\rightarrow\Omega^{k-1}_{D}(L)
is given by

(\iota_{\square}\eta)(\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{k-1})=\eta(\square,\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{k-1}).

(3)

For any
\square\in\Gamma(DL)
, the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\square}:\Omega^{k}_{D}(L)\rightarrow\Omega^{k}_{D}(L)
is given by

(\mathcal{L}_{\square}\eta)(\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{k})=\square\big(\eta(\triangle_{1},\ldots,\triangle_{k})\big)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\eta(\triangle_{1},\ldots,[\square,\triangle_{i}],\ldots,\triangle_{k}).

(45)

These satisfy the usual identities, for
\square,\triangle\in\Gamma(DL)
:

\begin{cases}d_{D}\iota_{\square}+\iota_{\square}d_{D}=\mathcal{L}_{\square},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\iota_{\triangle}-\iota_{\triangle}\mathcal{L}_{\square}=\iota_{[\square,\triangle]},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}-\mathcal{L}_{\triangle}\mathcal{L}_{\square}=\mathcal{L}_{[\square,\triangle]},\\
d_{D}\mathcal{L}_{\square}-\mathcal{L}_{\square}d_{D}=0.\end{cases}

(46)

Remark A.3
.

i)

There is a canonical isomorphism
(DL)^{*}\otimes L\cong J^{1}L
, where
J^{1}L
is the first jet bundle of
L
. The differential

d_{D}:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow\Gamma((DL)^{*}\otimes L)

corresponds under this isomorphism to the first jet prolongation
j^{1}:\Gamma(L)\rightarrow\Gamma(J^{1}L)
.

ii)

The der-complex
\big(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L),d_{D}\big)
is acyclic. Indeed, the contraction
\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}
by the identity derivation
\mathbbm{1}\in\Gamma(DL)
satisfies
d_{D}\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}+\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}d_{D}=\text{id}
.

Definition A.4
.

Let
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(L)
be an Atiyah form such that
\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}\varpi
is nowhere zero. Then
\varpi
is called
pre-symplectic
(resp. symplectic) if
\varpi
is
d_{D}
-closed and the bundle morphism
\varpi^{\flat}:DL\rightarrow(DL)^{*}\otimes L
has constant rank (resp. is an isomorphism).

A pre-symplectic Atiyah form
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(L)
defines a Lie subalgebroid

K:=\ker\varpi^{\flat}\subset DL.

Proposition A.5
.

[
13
]

Let
L\rightarrow M
be a line bundle.

(1)

The relation

\sigma^{*}\theta=\iota_{\mathbbm{1}}\varpi

gives a one-to-one correspondence between
L
-valued pre-contact forms
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M,L)
and pre-symplectic Atiyah forms
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}_{D}(L)
.

(2)

If
\theta
and
\varpi
correspond to each other, then the symbol map
\sigma:DL\rightarrow TM
induces a Lie algebroid isomorphism

K\subset DL\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}T\mathcal{F}\subset TM,

where
T\mathcal{F}
is the characteristic distribution of the pre-contact structure
\theta
.

Assume we are given line bundles
L\rightarrow M
and
L^{\prime}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
. A bundle map
(\varphi,\underline{\varphi}):L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
is called
regular
if
\varphi
is fiberwise invertible. Such a map
(\varphi,\underline{\varphi})
induces a morphism of Lie algebroids
D\varphi:DL\rightarrow DL^{\prime}
covering
\underline{\varphi}
, which is defined as follows. First note that there is a well-defined pullback map on sections
\varphi^{*}:\Gamma(L^{\prime})\rightarrow\Gamma(L)
, given by

(\varphi^{*}\lambda^{\prime})_{x}=\left(\varphi|_{L_{x}}\right)^{-1}\lambda^{\prime}_{\underline{\varphi}(x)},\hskip 28.45274pt\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma(L^{\prime}),x\in M.

Using this, one can define
D\varphi:DL\rightarrow DL^{\prime}
by setting

\big(D\varphi(\delta)\big)(\lambda^{\prime})=\varphi\big(\delta(\varphi^{*}\lambda^{\prime})\big),\hskip 28.45274pt\delta\in(DL)_{x},\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma(L^{\prime}).

Furthermore, one obtains a chain map
\varphi^{*}:\big(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L^{\prime}),d_{D}\big)\rightarrow\big(\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L),d_{D}\big)
which extends the pullback on sections
\varphi^{*}:\Gamma(L^{\prime})\rightarrow\Gamma(L)
. It is explicitly given by

\big(\varphi^{*}\eta^{\prime}\big)_{x}(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})=\left(\varphi|_{L_{x}}\right)^{-1}\left(\eta^{\prime}_{\underline{\varphi}(x)}\big(D\varphi(\delta_{1}),\ldots,D\varphi(\delta_{k})\big)\right),

for
\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega^{k}_{D}(L^{\prime})
and
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k}\in(DL)_{x}
for some
x\in M
.

Example A.6
.

Consider the particular case in which
\underline{\varphi}:N\hookrightarrow M
is the inclusion of a submanifold, and
\varphi:L_{N}\hookrightarrow L
is the inclusion of the restricted vector bundle
L_{N}:=L|_{N}
. Then the map
D\varphi:DL_{N}\hookrightarrow DL
is an embedding whose image consists of those
\delta\in DL
for which
\sigma(\delta)\in TN
. This allows us to view
DL_{N}
as a Lie subalgebroid of
DL
. Moreover, we have a pullback map
\varphi^{*}:\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L)\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}_{D}(L_{N})
.

At last, we recall the interpretation of derivations as infinitesimal bundle automorphisms. Given a line bundle
L\rightarrow M
, let
\square_{t}\in\Gamma(DL)
be a smooth family of derivations. It generates a smooth family of local line bundle automorphisms
\varphi_{t}
, uniquely determined by

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}^{*}\lambda=\varphi_{t}^{*}\big(\square_{t}\lambda\big),\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\lambda\in\Gamma(L),

(47)

with initial condition
\varphi_{0}=\text{Id}
. The automorphisms
\varphi_{t}
cover the local flow
\underline{\varphi}_{t}
of the time-dependent vector field
\sigma(\square_{t})
. The formula (
47
) extends to time-dependent Atiyah forms via

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi_{t}^{*}\eta_{t}=\varphi_{t}^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\square_{t}}\eta_{t}+\frac{d}{dt}\eta_{t}\right),\hskip 14.22636pt\eta_{t}\in\Omega^{k}_{D}(L).

On partitions with bounded largest part and fixed integral GBG-rank modulo primes
Alexander Berkovich, Aritram Dhar

Abstract
. In 2009, Berkovich and Garvan introduced a new partition statistic called the GBG-rank modulo t t which is a generalization of the well-known BG-rank. In this paper, we use the Littlewood decomposition of partitions to study partitions with bounded largest part and fixed integral value of GBG-rank modulo primes. As a consequence, we obtain new elegant generating function formulas for unrestricted partitions, self-conjugate partitions, and partitions whose parts repeat a finite number of times.

is not exposed in the region marked
N+1
. Thus, again using (
2.1
), the following equations hold:

(3.7)

N+1\geq k+v_{0},

which implies that

(3.8)

v_{0}\leq N+1-k,

and

(3.9)

N\geq-k+v_{1},

which implies that

(3.10)

v_{1}\leq N+k.

Hence, from (
3.8
) and (
3.10
), we have
l(\hat{\pi}_{0})\leq N+k
and
\#(\hat{\pi}_{0})\leq N+1-k
which when combined with Remark
1
and (
3.6
) gives us the required generating function in Theorem
1.2
for
\nu=1
. Analogously, one can prove the required generating function for
\nu=0
which gives us the complete proof of Theorem
1.2
.∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
\pi=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)\in\mathcal{SCP}
be a partition with
\lambda_{1}\leq tN+\nu
where
0\leq\nu\leq t-1
and consider the Littlewood decomposition of
\pi

\phi_{1}(\pi)=(\pi_{\text{$t$-core}},\pi_{\text{$t$-quotient}})

where
\pi_{\text{$t$-quotient}}=(\hat{\pi}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\pi}_{t-1})
.

Now, let
t
be any odd prime and
\text{GBG}^{(t)}(\pi)=k
be an integer. It is then easy to verify that
\text{GBG}^{(t)}(\pi)=k
is an integer if and only if

r_{0}(\pi,t)=k+r

and

r_{i}(\pi,t)=r

for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}
and
1\leq i\leq t-1
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\vec{n}(\pi,t)=(k,0,0,\ldots,0,0,-k),

where
n_{0}(\pi,t)=k
,
n_{t-1}(\pi,t)=-k
, and
n_{i}(\pi,t)=0
for
1\leq i\leq t-2
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|\pi_{\text{$t$-core}}|

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2}\cdot 2k^{2}+(t-1)\cdot(-k)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=tk^{2}-(t-1)k.

Now, let us consider the pair of partitions
(\hat{\pi}_{0},\hat{\pi}_{t-1})
from
\pi_{\text{$t$-quotient}}
and observe that
\hat{\pi}_{0}=\hat{\pi}^{\prime}_{t-1}
. Therefore, this pair contributes to the generating function as

(3.12)

\displaystyle q^{t(|\hat{\pi}_{0}|+|\hat{\pi}_{t-1}|)}=q^{2t|\hat{\pi}_{0}|}.

Now, by (
3.5
), we have
\#(\hat{\pi}_{0})\leq N-k+\lceil\frac{\nu}{t}\rceil
and
\#(\hat{\pi}_{t-1})\leq N+k
which is the same as
l(\hat{\pi}_{0})\leq N+k
. So, using Remark
1
and (
3.12
), we get the term

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}2N+\lceil\frac{\nu}{t}\rceil\\
N+k\end{matrix}\right]_{q^{2t}}

in the required generating function.

Next, for
1\leq i\leq(t-3)/2
, we consider the pair of partitions
(\hat{\pi}_{i},\hat{\pi}_{t-1-i})
from
\pi_{\text{$t$-quotient}}
and observe that
\hat{\pi}_{i}=\hat{\pi}^{\prime}_{t-1-i}
. Therefore, this pair contributes to the generating function as

(3.14)

\displaystyle q^{t(|\hat{\pi}_{i}|+|\hat{\pi}_{t-1-i}|)}=q^{2t|\hat{\pi}_{i}|}.

Now, by (
3.5
), for
1\leq i\leq(t-3)/2
, we have
\#(\hat{\pi}_{i})\leq N+\lceil\frac{\nu-i}{t}\rceil
and
\#(\hat{\pi}_{t-1-i})\leq N+\lceil\frac{\nu-(t-1-i)}{t}\rceil=N+\lfloor\frac{\nu+i}{t}\rfloor
which is the same as
l(\hat{\pi}_{i})\leq N+\lfloor\frac{\nu+i}{t}\rfloor
. So, using Remark
1
and (
3.14
) followed by taking the product over all values of
i
, we get the term

(3.15)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}2N+\lceil\frac{\nu-i}{t}\rceil+\lfloor\frac{\nu+i}{t}\rfloor\\
N+\lfloor\frac{\nu+i}{t}\rfloor\end{matrix}\right]_{q^{2t}}}

in the required generating function.

Finally, observe that
\hat{\pi}_{\frac{t-1}{2}}=\hat{\pi}^{\prime}_{\frac{t-1}{2}}
and similarly, by (
3.5
), we have
\#(\hat{\pi}_{\frac{t-1}{2}})\leq N+\big\lceil\frac{\nu-\frac{t-1}{2}}{t}\big\rceil
. Thus, using Remark
2
, we get the term

(3.16)

\displaystyle(-q^{t};q^{2t})_{N+\big\lceil\frac{\nu-\frac{t-1}{2}}{t}\big\rceil}

in the required generating function.

Hence, combining (
3.11
), (
3.13
), (
3.15
), and (
3.16
), we have the desired generating function which completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The proof of Theorem
1.4
comes from the combinatorial bijection of extracting parts which repeat
t
times in succession from the
t
-residue diagram of a partition to form a new pair of partitions.

Let

\pi=(1^{tq_{1}+f_{1}},2^{tq_{2}+f_{2}},3^{tq_{3}+f_{3}},\ldots,N^{tq_{N}+f_{N}}),

where
0\leq f_{j}\leq t-1
and
q_{j}\geq 0
for
1\leq j\leq N
.

Then, it can be seen that
\pi
is in one-to-one correspondence with the pair of partitions
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
where

\pi_{1}=(1^{tq_{1}},2^{tq_{2}},3^{tq_{3}},\ldots,N^{tq_{N}})

and

\pi_{2}=(1^{f_{1}},2^{f_{2}},3^{f_{3}},\ldots,N^{f_{N}}).

Now, note that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\pi_{1}}q^{|\pi_{1}|}=\dfrac{1}{(q^{t};q^{t})_{N}},

and so, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\pi}q^{|\pi|}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\pi_{1}}q^{|\pi_{1}|}\cdot\sum\limits_{\pi_{2}}q^{|\pi_{2}|}

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q^{t};q^{t})_{N}}\sum\limits_{\pi_{2}}q^{|\pi_{2}|},

where (
3.18
) follows from (
3.17
).

Hence, from (
3.18
), we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\pi_{2}}q^{|\pi_{2}|}=(q^{t};q^{t})_{N}\sum\limits_{\pi}q^{|\pi|},

where
\pi_{2}
is a partition whose parts repeat no more than
t-1
times.

Also, observe that
\text{GBG}^{(t)}(\pi)=\text{GBG}^{(t)}(\pi_{2})=k
since removal of parts which repeat
t
times in succession keeps the GBG-rank mod
t
value of
\pi
invariant.

Therefore, using the above observation and replacing
N
by
tN+\nu
in (
3.19
) for
0\leq\nu\leq t-1
along with Theorem
1.1
gives us the desired generating function
\tilde{G}_{tN+\nu,t}(k,q)
.∎

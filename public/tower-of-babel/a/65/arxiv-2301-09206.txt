On some multiplicative properties of large difference sets
I.D. Shkredov

Abstract




Annotation.

In our paper we study multiplicative properties of difference sets
A-A
for large sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
in the case of composite
q
.
We obtain a quantitative version of a result of A. Fish about the structure of the product sets
(A-A)(A-A)
.
Also, we show that the multiplicative covering number of any difference set is always small.

Introduction

The landmark question about solvability of equations of the form
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
, where
f\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and the variables
x_{j}\in X_{j}
belong to some “large” but unspecified sets
X_{j}
of the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
was firstly posed, probably, in
[
11
]
.
Interesting in its own right the problem has a clear connection with the sum–product phenomenon
[
20
]
due to the fact that as a rule the polynomial
f
includes both the addition and the multiplication.
This theme becomes rather popular last years, e.g., see

[
7
]
—
[
11
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
18
]
and many other papers.

The question about a partial resolution of some specific equations
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
in large subrings of rings
\mathbb{Z}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}/(q\mathbb{Z})
for composite
q
was firstly considered by A. Fish in
[
5
]
(nevertheless, let us remark that a similar problem was formulated in
[
8
, Problem 5]
). In particular, in
[
5
, Corollary 1.2]
A. Fish considered the polynomial
f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{3}-x_{4})
and proved the following result.

Theorem 1

Let
q
be a
positive integer,

A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be sets,
|A|=\alpha q
,
|B|=\beta q
, and suppose that
\alpha\geqslant\beta
.
Then there is
d|q
with

d\leqslant F(\beta)

(1)

and such that

d\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{q}\subseteq(A-A)(B-B)\,.

(2)

Here we use the following standard notation
[
20
]
, namely,
given two sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, define
the
sumset

of
A
and
B

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

In a similar way we define the
difference sets

A-A
, the
higher sumsets

2A-A
is
A+A-A
and, further, the
products sets

AB:=\{ab~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}

the
higher product sets
and so on.
Finally, if

and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, then we write

\lambda\cdot A=\{\lambda a~:~a\in A\}\,.

It is easy to see that in this generality one cannot have

in inclusion (
2

and thus we indeed need this additional (but small) divisor

In contrary, for prime

the divisor

can be omitted and the questions of this type were studied in
[
9
]

and

[
18
]
.
In paper
[
5
]
the dependence on

The signs

(3)

Theorem 4

Let

be a positive integer,
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be a set,

.

An effective version of Fish’s theorem

Having a positive integer
n
and a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
(or just
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
), as well as a divisor
q_{*}|q
, we write

\pi_{q_{*}}(A)=\{(a_{1}\pmod{q_{*}},~\dots~,~a_{n}\pmod{q_{*}})~:~(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q_{*}}\,.

We need a simple regularization result similar to
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 5

Let
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
M\geqslant 2
be real numbers,
n
be a positive integer, and
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
be a set,
|A|=\delta q^{n}
.
Then there is
q_{*}|q
, and a set
A_{*}\subseteq A
,

|\pi_{q/q_{*}}(A_{*})|=1

such that
q_{*}=\frac{q}{q_{1}\dots q_{s}}
,
M\leqslant q_{j}\leqslant\delta^{-\varepsilon^{-1}}
,

s
is the least number with
\delta M^{\varepsilon s}>1

and
for all
\tilde{q}|q_{*}
,
\tilde{q}\geqslant M
one has

\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\tilde{q}}}|A_{*}\cap\pi^{-1}_{\tilde{q}}(\xi)|\leqslant\frac{|A_{*}|}{\tilde{q}^{1-\varepsilon}}\,.

(7)

P r o o f.

Suppose not. Then for a certain
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
and
q_{1}|q
,
q_{1}\geqslant M
we find
A^{\prime}:=A\cap\pi^{-1}_{q_{1}}(\xi)
with
|A^{\prime}|\geqslant\frac{|A|}{q^{1-\varepsilon}_{1}}
.
Clearly,
|\pi_{q_{1}}(A^{\prime})|=1
and the density of
A^{\prime}
in the appropriate shift of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q/q_{1}}
is at least
\delta q^{\varepsilon}_{1}\geqslant\delta M^{\varepsilon}
.
Hence applying the same procedure to the set
A^{\prime}

and to the new module
q/q_{1}
,
we see that our algorithm must stop after at most
s
steps.
Notice that condition (
7
) holds automatically if
\tilde{q}\geqslant\delta^{-\varepsilon^{-1}}
and hence
at the final step of our procedure we find a set
A_{*}\subset A
,
|\pi_{q/q_{*}}(A_{*})|=1
, having all required properties.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to obtain the main result of this section, which implies Theorem
4
from the introduction.
Our proof uses the Fourier analysis (its standard facts can be found in
[
20
]
, say) and classical
estimates for the Kloosterman sums.
Having a group
{\mathbf{G}}
, we define for any function
f:{\mathbf{G}}\to\mathbb{C}
and a representation
\rho\in\widehat{{\mathbf{G}}}

the Fourier transform of
f
at
\rho
by the formula

\widehat{f}(\rho)=\sum_{g\in{\mathbf{G}}}f(g)\rho(g)\,.

(8)

Theorem 6

Let
q
be a
positive integer,

A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be sets,
|A|=\alpha q
,
|B|=\beta q
, and suppose that
\alpha\geqslant\beta
.
Then there is
d|q
with

d\ll\exp(C\beta^{-4})\,,

(9)

where
C>0
is an absolute constant and such that

d\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{q}\subseteq(A-A)(B-B)\,.

(10)

In addition

d\ll\beta^{-O(\omega(q))}\,.

(11)

P r o o f.

Let
q=p_{1}^{\rho_{1}}\dots p_{t}^{\rho_{t}}
, where
p_{j}
are different primes,
p_{1}<\dots<p_{t}
.
Also, let
M\geqslant 2
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be parameters, which we will choose later.
First of all, we remove all divisors less than
M
from
q
.
More precisely, for any
p_{j}
,
j\in[t]
let
\gamma_{j}\leqslant\rho_{j}
be the maximal nonnegative integer such that
p^{\gamma_{j}}_{j}\leqslant M
.
Clearly,
\gamma_{1}\geqslant\gamma_{2}\geqslant\dots\gamma_{t}\geqslant 0
and let
t_{0}\leqslant t
be the maximal
j
with
\gamma_{j}\neq 0
.
Thus
t_{0}\leqslant\pi(M)
.
Now we define

Q_{1}:=\prod_{j=1}^{t_{0}}p_{j}^{\gamma_{j}}\leqslant M^{t_{0}}\leqslant\min\{M^{\pi(M)},M^{\omega(q)}\}

(12)

and take
A_{1}\subseteq A
such that a shift of
A_{1}
belongs to
\mathbb{Z}_{q/Q_{1}}
and has density at least
\alpha
.
In particular,
|\pi_{Q_{1}}(A_{1})|=1
and
of course such a shift exists by the Dirichlet principle.
Similarly, we can do the same with the set
B
so as not to lose the density.
Secondly, we apply Lemma
5

with
n=1
,
A=A_{1}
to regularize the set
A_{1}
and find a set
A_{*}\subseteq A_{1}
and a module
q_{*}
that
satisfies
(
7
) and all other restrictions.
Again, using the Dirichlet principle, we take
B_{*}\subseteq B
such that the density of
B
does not decrease.
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{*}}
be an arbitrary number and we first suppose that
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q_{*}}
.
To prove
\lambda\in(A-A)(B-B)
it is
enough
to show that
\lambda\in(A_{*}-A_{*})(B_{*}-B_{*})
or, equivalently, in terms of the Fourier transform
it suffices
obtain
the inequality

\frac{|A_{*}|^{2}|B_{*}|^{2}}{q_{*}}>\frac{1}{q_{*}}\sum_{r\neq 0}|\widehat{B}_{*}(r)|^{2}\cdot\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{*}}e_{q_{*}}\left(\frac{\lambda r}{a_{1}-a_{2}}\right):=\sigma\,.

(13)

Now clearly,

\sigma\leqslant\frac{1}{q_{*}}\sum_{q_{2}|q_{*},\,q_{2}>1}\,\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q_{2}}}|\widehat{B}_{*}(zq_{*}q^{-1}_{2})|^{2}\left|\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{*}}e_{q_{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda z}{a_{1}-a_{2}}\right)\right|\,.

(14)

In terms of the Kloosterman sums

K_{q}(\lambda,r):=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}}e_{q}\left(\frac{\lambda}{x}+rx\right)

and the density function

\eta_{q_{2}}(\xi):=|\{a\in A_{*}~:~a\equiv\xi\pmod{q_{2}}\}|

(15)

one has (recall that
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q_{*}}
and

)

\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A_{*}}e_{q_{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda z}{a_{1}-a_{2}}\right)=\sum_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{2}}}\eta_{q_{2}}(\xi_{1})\eta_{q_{2}}(\xi_{2})e_{q_{2}}\left(\frac{\lambda z}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\right)=q^{-1}_{2}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{2}}}|\widehat{\eta}_{q_{2}}(\xi)|^{2}K_{q_{2}}(\lambda z,\xi)

(16)

\leqslant 2\sqrt{q_{2}}\tau(q_{2})\|\eta_{q_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

In the last line we have applied the well–known bound for the Kloosterman sum and the Parseval identity.
Now to estimate
\|\eta_{q_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}
we use the regularity property of
A_{*}
and
derive

\|\eta_{q_{2}}\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\|\eta_{q_{2}}\|_{\infty}\|\eta_{q_{2}}\|_{1}\leqslant\frac{|A_{*}|^{2}}{q^{1-\varepsilon}_{2}}\,.

(17)

Further let us obtain a lower bound for divisors
q_{2}
.
Since
|\pi_{Q_{1}}(A_{1})|=1
, it follows that for all
q_{1}|Q_{1}
, we have

\frac{1}{q_{1}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}}|\widehat{\eta}_{q_{1}}(\xi)|^{2}K_{q_{1}}(\lambda z,\xi)=\frac{|A_{*}|^{2}}{q_{1}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{1}}}K_{q_{1}}(\lambda z,\xi)=0\,.

Thus one can see that summations in (
14
)
is taken over
q_{2}\geqslant M
.
Choosing
\varepsilon=1/4
, say, and
using the last fact,
we get in view of the Parseval identity that

\sigma\ll M^{-1/4}\frac{|A_{*}|^{2}}{q_{*}}\sum_{q_{2}|q_{*},\,q_{2}>1}\,\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q_{2}}}|\widehat{B}_{*}(zq_{*}q^{-1}_{2})|^{2}\leqslant M^{-1/4}|A_{*}|^{2}|B_{*}|\,.

Returning to
(
13
), we obtain a contradiction provided
|B_{*}|\gg q_{*}M^{-1/4}
.
In other words, we have for a certain
s\geqslant 0
,
\alpha M^{s/4}>1
that

|B|M^{s/4}\ll qM^{-1/4}

and this implies

.
Thus in view of our restriction to the divisors of
q_{*}
, the condition
\alpha\geqslant\beta
, the first bound for
Q_{1}
from (
12
), and the bound for
s
, which follows from Lemma

, we get

d\ll M^{\pi(M)}\exp(O(\log^{2}(1/\alpha)/\log(1/\beta))\ll\exp(O(\beta^{-4}))

as required.

Now let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{q_{*}}
be an arbitrary element.
Write
\lambda=q^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}
, where
q^{\prime}|q
and
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q_{*}/q^{\prime}}
.
Using the Dirichlet principle, choose a subset of

of density at least
\beta
such that all elements of a shift of

are divisible by
q^{\prime}
. Then our inclusion
can be rewritten as
\lambda^{\prime}\in(A_{*}-A_{*})(B^{\prime}-B^{\prime})
modulo

and we can apply the arguments above replacing module

to

.

To obtain (

) we use the second bound for

from (

) and derive as above

On the general case

In

Let

be a positive number and
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{2}

,

d\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{q}\subseteq\{(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})~:~(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A},\,(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}\}\,,

(18)

provided

In this section we study the number
N_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)
of the solutions
to the equation

(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})\equiv\lambda\pmod{q}\,,\quad\quad(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A},\,(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}\,.

(19)

We consider the squarefree case for simplicity and

one more time that our sets

,

]

Theorem 7

Let

be a squarefree number,
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{2}

,

d\cdot\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}\subseteq\{(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})~:~(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A},\,(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathcal{B}\}

(22)

In particular, equality (

and

P r o o f.

We start with (
20
).
The proof follows the arguments of the proof of Theorem

and thus we use the notation from this result.
In particular, writing
q=p_{1}^{\rho_{1}}\dots p_{t}^{\rho_{t}}
,

with

,

we define

such that (

holds and further we take

The only difference is that one should use Lemma

with

to regularize the two–dimensional set

and let

.
For a moment we assume that

, say, and we will choose the parameter

Finally, with some abuse of the notation we do not use new letters

Now for

and

let us write

if the pair

satisfies (

Then clearly,

N_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}}I(a,b)\,.

(23)

Without loosing of the generality we assume that
\lambda=1
.
Obviously,
I(a,b)=I(b,a)
and we can rewrite the matrix
I(a,b)
as

I(a,b)=\sum_{j=1}^{q^{2}}\mu_{j}u_{j}(a)\overline{u}_{j}(b)

\mu_{j}

One can easily check that
u_{1}(x)=q^{-1}(1,\dots,1)

and
\mu_{1}=|\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}|=\varphi(q)
.
Writing
I^{\prime}(a,b)=I(a,b)-\mu_{1}u_{1}(a)\overline{u}_{1}(b)

N_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)-\frac{|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{B}|\varphi(q)}{q^{2}}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A},b\in\mathcal{B}}I^{\prime}(a,b):=N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)\,.

(24)

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)^{2}\leqslant|\mathcal{B}|\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{b}I^{\prime}(a,b)I^{\prime}(a^{\prime},b)=|\mathcal{B}|\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}(I^{\prime})^{2}(a,a^{\prime}):=|\mathcal{B}|\cdot\sigma\,.

(25)

Here
(I^{\prime})^{2}
is the second power of the matrix
I^{\prime}
.
Similarly,

I^{2}(a,a^{\prime})=\sum_{b}I(a,b)I(a^{\prime},b)
and the last quantity coincides with the number of the solutions to the equation

a_{2}-a^{\prime}_{2}=\frac{a^{\prime}_{1}-a_{1}}{(a_{1}+x)(a^{\prime}_{1}+x)}\,,

(26)

where

,
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
a^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})
.
Assume that

and rewrite our equation (

x^{2}+(a_{1}+a^{\prime}_{1})x+a_{1}a^{\prime}_{1}+\frac{a_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1}}{a_{2}-a^{\prime}_{2}}=0\,,

, Lemma 13, Section 5 and Section 6]
, we obtain

N_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)-\frac{|A||B||G|\varphi(q)}{q^{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{|A||B|}|G|q^{-1/k}\,,

where
k\sim\log q/\log K
.
Hence
N_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)>0
, provided
K\gg(\sqrt{\alpha\beta}(\log\log q)^{-1})^{-O(1)}
.
The last condition is equivalent to
M\gg\tau(q)\delta^{-2}(\sqrt{\alpha\beta}(\log\log q)^{-1})^{-O(1)}
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

5
On the covering numbers of difference sets

Let us recall the definition of the covering number of a set, e.g., see
[
1
]
.

Definition 13

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a finite abelian group with the group operation
+
, and let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
We write

{\rm cov}^{+}(A)={\rm cov}(A)=\min\{|X|~:~X\subseteq{\mathbf{G}},\,A+X={\mathbf{G}}\}

and the quantity
{\rm cov}^{+}(A)
is called the (additive)
covering number
of
A
.

Having a finite ring
\mathcal{R}
with two operations
+,\times
we underline which covering number we use, writing
{\rm cov}^{+}
or
{\rm cov}^{\times}
.
It is known
[
1
, Corollary 3.2]
that for any set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
one has
{\rm cov}^{+}(A)=O\left(\frac{|{\mathbf{G}}|}{|A|}\log|A|\right)
and
the last
bound is tight.
In this section we study difference sets
A-A
,
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
and show that
{\rm cov}^{\times}(A-A)
is always small.
First of all, let us make a remark about a connection between
{\rm cov}^{+}
and
{\rm cov}^{\times}
in a ring
\mathcal{R}
.

Proposition 14

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a finite ring, and
S\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be a set.
Then

{\rm cov}^{\times}(S-S)\leqslant{\rm cov}^{+}(S)\,,

(49)

provided all numbers
1,\dots,{\rm cov}^{+}(S)
belong to
\mathcal{R}^{*}
.

P r o o f.

Let
S+X=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
and
|X|={\rm cov}^{+}(S):=k
.
For any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
consider
jg
, where
j=0,1,\dots,k
.
By the pigeonhole principle there are different
j_{1}\neq j_{2}
such that
j_{1}g\in S+x
and
j_{2}g\in S+x
with the same
x\in X
.
It implies that
(j_{1}-j_{2})g\in S-S
and hence
g\in(j_{1}-j_{2})^{-1}(S-S)
, provided
(j_{1}-j_{2})^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}^{*}
.
It remains to notice that
[-k,k]^{-1}\cdot(S-S)=[k]^{-1}\cdot(S-S)
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

By the well–known consequence of the Ruzsa covering lemma
[
20
, Section 2.4]
, we have for any finite group
{\mathbf{G}}
and a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
that for a certain set
Z\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
one has

{\mathbf{G}}\subseteq A-A+Z\,,\quad\quad|Z|\leqslant|{\mathbf{G}}|/|A|\,.

(50)

In particular, it means that
{\rm cov}^{+}(A-A)\leqslant|{\mathbf{G}}|/|A|
.
Thus Proposition
14
gives us

Corollary 15

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a finite ring,
A\subseteq\mathcal{R}
be a set, and
|A|=\alpha|\mathcal{R}|
.
Then

{\rm cov}^{\times}(2A-2A)\leqslant\alpha^{-1}\,,

provided all numbers
1,\dots,[\alpha^{-1}]
belong to
\mathcal{R}^{*}
.

Using
the same
method, one can
estimate
the multiplicative covering number of a Bohr set in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(
p
is a prime number)

\mathcal{B}(\Gamma,\varepsilon)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}~:~\|x\gamma/p\|\leqslant\varepsilon,\,\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\}\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1],\,\quad\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}\,,

namely, we have

{\rm cov}^{\times}(\mathcal{B}(\Gamma,\varepsilon))\leqslant\varepsilon^{-|\Gamma|}\,.

It is interesting to decrease the number of summands in Corollary
15
.
To this end
let
us obtain the main result of this section.

Theorem 16

Let
q
be a positive integer,
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be a set,
|A|=\alpha q
.
Suppose that the least prime factor of
q

greater than

2\alpha^{-1}+3
.
Then

{\rm cov}^{\times}(A-A)\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha}+1\,.

(51)

More concretely,
[k_{*}]^{-1}\cdot(A-A)=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
for a certain
k_{*}\leqslant\alpha^{-1}+1
.

P r o o f.

Let
p_{1}
be the least prime factor of
q
.
By our assumption we know that
p_{1}\geqslant 2\alpha^{-1}+3
.
Write
p_{1}=2k+1
and take
\Lambda=\{0,1,\dots,k_{*}\}
, where
\lceil\alpha^{-1}-1\rceil+1=k_{*}\leqslant k
.
Then one has
Y:=(\Lambda-\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}
.
First of all, consider
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{*}
and form the set
n\cdot\Lambda+A
.
Since
|\Lambda||A|=(k_{*}+1)\alpha q>q
, it follows that there are different
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda
such that

n\lambda_{1}+a_{1}\equiv n\lambda_{2}+a_{2}\pmod{q}\,,

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
.
Hence
n\in Y^{-1}(A-A)
and thus
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{*}\subseteq Y^{-1}(A-A)
.
Also, notice that as in Proposition
14
one has
Y^{-1}(A-A)=[k_{*}]^{-1}\cdot(A-A)
.

Now let
n=n^{\prime}q_{1}
, where
q_{1}|q
and
n^{\prime}
is coprime to
q
.
By the pigeonhole principle there is
B\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q/q_{1}}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
such that
q_{1}B+s\subseteq A
and the density of
B
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q/q_{1}}
is at least
\alpha
. In particular, we have
q_{1}(B-B)\subseteq A-A
.
By the same argument as above one has
n^{\prime}\equiv y^{-1}(b_{1}-b_{2})\pmod{q/q_{1}}
, where
y\in Y
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in B
.
It follows that
n\equiv y^{-1}(a_{1}-a_{2})\pmod{q}
as required.
Thus we have proved that
[k_{*}]^{-1}(A-A)=\mathbb{Z}_{q}

and hence

{\rm cov}^{\times}(A-A)\leqslant k_{*}\leqslant\alpha^{-1}+1
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Remark 17

After the paper was written the author was informed by A. Fish that Theorem
16
holds in greater generality, namely, for any measure preserving system the same is true for the set of return times of a set of positive measure.

Theorem
16
implies a consequence about the multiplicative covering numbers of the intersections of difference sets
in the spirit of paper
[
19
]
, see
[
19
, Theorems 1,3]
.

Corollary 18

Let
q
be a positive integer, and
A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be sets,
|A_{i}|=\alpha_{i}q
,
i\in[k]
.
Suppose that the least prime factor of
q

greater than

2(\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k})^{-1}+3
.
Then

{\rm cov}^{\times}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i}-A_{i})\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}}+1\,.

(52)

P r o o f.

Put
A_{\vec{s}}=A_{1}\cap(A_{2}-s_{1})\cap\dots(A_{k}-s_{k-1})
, where
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k-1}_{q}
.
We have
\sum_{\vec{s}}|A_{\vec{s}}|=|A_{1}|\dots|A_{k}|
and hence there is
\vec{s}_{*}
such that
|A_{\vec{s}_{*}}|\geqslant\alpha_{1}\dots\alpha_{k}q
.
Clearly, for any
\vec{s}
one has

A_{\vec{s}}-A_{\vec{s}}\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}(A_{i}-A_{i})\,.

Applying Theorem
16
with
A=A_{\vec{s}_{*}}
, we obtain bound (
52
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

As we have seen before
Corollary
15
and Theorem
16
give us some bounds for the multiplicative covering numbers of difference sets.
On the other hand, one can see that Theorem
16
does not hold for, say, nonzero shifts of Bohr sets, for the sumsets
A+A
, for the higher sumsets
nA
,
n>2
and so on.
Indeed, consider the following

Example.

Let
p
be a prime number and
S=[p/3,2p/3)
or
S=\pm[p/6,p/3)
to make
S
symmetric.
Then the equation
a+b\equiv c\pmod{p}
has no solutions in
a,b,c\in S
.
Further, we have
|S|\gg p
but it is easy to see that
{\rm cov}^{\times}(S)
is unbounded.
Indeed, if
SX=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for a set
X
with
|X|=O(1)
, then we obtain a coloring of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with a finite number of colors and every color has no solutions to our equation
a+b\equiv c\pmod{p}
. It gives us a contradiction with the famous Schur theorem, see
[
13
]
(actually, it implies
{\rm cov}^{\times}(S)\gg\log p/\log\log p
).

In particular, we see that
{\rm cov}^{\times}(X+s)
can be much larger than
{\rm cov}^{\times}(X)
for a set
X
and a nonzero
s
.

Proposition
14
implies that any syndetic
(i.e. having bounded gaps between its consecutive elements)
set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
,

|S|\gg p
has
{\rm cov}^{\times}(S-S)=O(1)
. On the other hand, thanks to inclusion (
50
) any set of the form
A-A
, where
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
|A|\gg p
is syndetic (with the gap depending on
A
but not just on
p/|A|
, of course). Thus it is natural to ask about
a
generalization of Theorem
16
to the family of syndetic sets. Nevertheless, taking
S=\{1+kM\}_{k\in[(p-1)/M]}
,
M\geqslant 4
and
p\equiv 2\pmod{M}
, say, we see that
S
is a syndetic set and
S
has no solutions to the equation
a+b\equiv c\pmod{p}
.
Thus as in the example above we see that
{\rm cov}^{\times}(S)
is unbounded.

Remark 19

A dual form of Theorem
16
has no place, namely, there is a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
|A|\gg p
such that
{\rm cov}^{+}(A/A)\gg\log p
.
In other words,
{\rm cov}^{+}(A/A)
is close to the maximal possible value.
To see this just take
A
to be the set of all quadratic residues, e.g., see
[
12
, Proposition 14]
.

Finally, let us give another proof of a variant of Theorem
2
via our covering
Theorem
16
.
Notice that the number
d
below can be a non–divisor of
q
.

Theorem 20

Let
q
be a
positive integer,

A,B\subset\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be sets,
|A|=\alpha q
,
|B|=\beta q
, and let us assume that
\alpha\geqslant\beta
.
Suppose that the least prime factor of
q

greater than

2\beta^{-1}+3
.
Then there is
d\neq 0
with

d\leqslant\alpha^{-\beta^{-1}-1}\,,

(53)

and such that

d\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{q}\subseteq(A-A)(B-B)\,.

(54)

P r o o f.

Applying Theorem
16
with
A=B
, we find a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
n:=|X|\leqslant\beta^{-1}+1
such that
X(B-B)=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
\vec{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
.
Considering the collection of the sets
A^{n}+j\cdot\vec{x}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
,
j\geqslant 1
, we see that there is
0<d\leqslant\alpha^{-n}
with
d\cdot X\subseteq A-A
.
Hence

(A-A)(B-B)\supseteq d\cdot X(B-B)\supseteq d\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{q}

as required.
It remains to notice that

d\leqslant\alpha^{-n}\leqslant\alpha^{-\beta^{-1}-1}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

6
Concluding remarks

Let us discuss other approaches to Theorem
2
.
First of all, recall the well–known Furstenberg’s result
[
6
]
.

Theorem 21

Let
n
be a positive integer,
\delta\in(0,1]
be a real number, and
S
be a set of size
n
.
Then for all sufficiently large
N\geqslant N(\delta,n)
an arbitrary set

A\subseteq[N]\times[N]
,
|A|\geqslant\delta N^{2}
contains the set
\alpha+\beta\cdot S
for some
\alpha
and
\beta\neq 0
.

Quantitative bounds for

N(\delta,n)

from
Theorem
21
can be found in
[
14
]
.

Corollary 22

Let
q
be a prime number,
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
,
|\mathcal{A}|=\delta q^{2}
and
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
|A|=\alpha_{*}q
,
|B|=\beta_{*}q
.
Then there is a decreasing positive function
\varphi
such that
if
\min\{\alpha_{*},\beta_{*},\delta\}\geqslant\varphi(q)
, then formula (
18
) takes place for
\mathcal{B}=A\times B
, any
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}
and
d=1
.

P r o o f.

Take
S=S_{1}=[k]\times[k]
or
S=S_{2}=\{(2j,2j)~:~j\in[k]\}
for a certain positive integer
k
.
Applying Theorem
21
with
n=|S|
and
A=\mathcal{A}
, we see that for some
\alpha
,
\beta\neq 0
the following holds
\alpha+\beta\cdot S\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and hence to solve (
18
) with
d=1
it is sufficiently to find for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}_{q}
some elements
a\in\beta^{-1}(A-\alpha)
,
b\in\beta^{-1}(B-\alpha)
and
(t_{1},t_{2})\in S
such that

(t_{1}-a)(t_{2}-b)\equiv\lambda\pmod{q}\,.

If for a certain absolute constant
C>0
one has
k\gg\min^{-C}\{\alpha_{*},\beta_{*},\delta\}
, then
for
S=S_{2}
the last equation has a solution thanks to the famous Bourgain–Gamburd machine
[
4
]
(see details in
[
17
]
, say) and for
S=S_{1}
(actually, for any dense subset of
S_{1}
) the latter fact was
obtained
in
[
17
, Theorem 3]
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

for composite

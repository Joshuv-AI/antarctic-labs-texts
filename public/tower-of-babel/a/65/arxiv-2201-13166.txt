Boundedness of the solutions of a kind of nonlinear parabolic systems
Emilia Anna Alfano, Luisa Fattorusso, Lubomira Softova

Abstract
. We deal with nonlinear systems of parabolic type satisfying componentwise structural conditions. The nonlinear terms are Carathéodory maps having controlled growth with respect to the solution and the gradient and the data are in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces. Under these assumptions we obtain essential boundedness of the weak solutions.

1.
Introduction

This paper aims the boundedness of the weak solutions to the following nonlinear divergence form systems

{{\bm{u}}}_{t}-{\rm div\,}{\bm{A}}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})+{{\bm{b}}}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})=0\qquad(x,t)\in Q_{T}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

n\geq 1,
is a bounded domain,
Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
,
T>0,
is a cylinder in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and the nonlinear operators

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}(x,t,{\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle:\ Q_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}

\displaystyle{\bm{b}}(x,t,{\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})

\displaystyle:\ Q_{T}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

where
\displaystyle{\bm{A}}=\{A_{i}^{\alpha}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}^{1\leq\alpha\leq N}
and
{\bm{b}}=(b^{1},\cdots,b^{N})

are Carathéodory maps, that is, these are measurable in
(x,t)\in Q_{T}
for every
({\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}
and continuous in
({\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})
for almost all (a.a.)
(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

Concerning linear divergence form equations of elliptic type, it is well known by the result of De Giorgi and Nash
[
15
,
28
]
that any weak solution is locally Hölder
continuous assuming that the leading coefficients are only
L^{\infty}.
Almost ten years were necessary to show, via a counterexample, that this is not true for divergence form elliptic systems under the same minimal conditions on the coefficients because of the lack of maximum principle (cf.
[
16
]
).
Notice that Giusti and Miranda in
[
21
]
proved that the De Giorgi-Nash result does not hold for quasi-linear systems, even if the coefficients are analytic functions.

In general, in order to obtain essential boundedness of the solutions, it is necessary to add some more restrictions on the structure of the operators considered.
The simplest example is given by systems in diagonal form, or the so-called
decoupled
systems.

Example 1.1
.

Let
{\bm{u}}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a weak solution of
{\rm div\,}({\bm{A}}(x,D{\bm{u}}))=~0
in
\Omega
with

A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,D{\bm{u}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\beta=1}^{N}\delta_{\alpha\,\beta}A_{ij}^{\alpha\,\beta}(x)D_{j}u^{\beta}

where
\delta_{\alpha\,\beta}
is the Kronecker delta. Then each component
u^{\alpha}
solves a single elliptic equation and
\sup_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u^{\alpha}
for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,N.

As it concerns nonlinear elliptic type systems, in order to study the regularity properties of the operator, more structural conditions are required. For instance, divergence form operators

{\rm div\,}{\bm{A}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})={\bm{b}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})

(1.2)

have been studied under various componentwise conditions.

In
[
29
]
Nečas and Stará analyzed quasi-linear systems which are diagonal for large values of
u^{\alpha}
. Precisely, for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,N
, it is assumed that

0<\theta_{\alpha}\leq u^{\alpha}\Longrightarrow A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\beta=1}^{N}\delta_{\alpha\,\beta}A_{ij}^{\alpha\,\beta}(x,{\bm{u}})D_{j}u^{\beta}

where the operator
\big\{A_{ij}^{\alpha\beta}\big\}_{1\leq i,j\leq n}^{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N}
is supposed to be bounded and elliptic. Then each solution of the system
{\rm div\,}{\bm{A}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})=0
verifies

\sup_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}\leq\max\{\theta_{\alpha};\,\sup_{\partial\Omega}u^{\alpha}\}.

In
[
24
]
Leonetti and Petricca analyzed nonlinear elliptic systems

-{\rm div\,}{\bm{A}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})+{\bm{b}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})={\bm{f}}(x)\quad x\in\Omega

where they impose componentwise coercivity condition on the principal part and positivity of the lower order terms operator for large values of
u^{\alpha}
that is there exists
\theta_{\alpha}>0
such that for each
u^{\alpha}\geq\theta_{\alpha},

\alpha=1,\ldots,N
one has

\begin{cases}\nu|{{\bm{z}}}^{\alpha}|^{p}-M_{\alpha}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,{\bm{u}},{{\bm{z}}})z_{i}^{\alpha}\qquad x\in\Omega\\[5.0pt]
0\leq b^{\alpha}(x,{\bm{u}},{{\bm{z}}}).\end{cases}

(1.3)

A bound for
\|{\bm{u}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})}
has been obtained under that hypotheses when the
x
-behavior of the nonlinear terms
{\bm{A}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})
and
{\bm{b}}(x,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})
is controlled in Lebesgue (
[
24
]
) and Morrey (
[
18
,
31
,
33
,
34
]
) spaces.

Concerning nonlinear elliptic and parabolic divergence form operators satisfying quite general growth conditions, there are known results on local boundedness of the solution, i.e. in any open set
B\Subset\Omega,
without any assumptions on the boundary. In this context we can mention the results of Marcellini et al. treating as the question of local boundedness so the Lipscitz regularity of the solutions under
p,q
-growth conditions on the nonlinear operator, see
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
25
,
26
]
and the references therein.

In our case we interested to obtain global regularity, up to the boundary of the cylinder
Q_{T}.
For this goal, following the technique developed in
[
23
]
, we assume a priori that the solution is bounded on the parabolic boundary
\partial Q=\Omega\cup S_{T}.
Further, estimating the level sets of the solution in cylinders with small high
Q_{\tau}=\Omega\times(t_{1},t_{2})
with
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T,t_{2}-t_{1}=\tau
we can estimate the
L^{\infty}
norm of
{\bm{u}}
in
Q_{\tau}.
Then covering
Q_{T}
with a finite number of such sub-cylinders and applying the global boundedness result in any of them we can proceed up to the upper boundary
\Omega\times\{t=T\}.

Some more results concerning boundedness and regularity of the solutions for nonlinear elliptic and parabolic operators can be found in

As it concerns non-coercive operators, we can mention the works of Boccardo

2.
Definitions and auxiliary results

We say that the domain
\Omega
satisfies the (A) condition if there exists a positive constant
A_{\Omega}<1
such that, for each ball
{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}
centered in some point
x\in\partial\Omega
and
\rho\leq{\rm diam\,}\Omega
, there holds

|\Omega\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}|\leq(1-A_{\Omega})|{\mathcal{B}}_{\rho}|\,.

(A)

For the function spaces that we are going to use we adopt the notations introduced in
[
23
]
.

1.

We say that
{{\bm{u}}}=(u^{1},\ldots,u^{N}):Q_{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
belongs to
L^{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
if
u^{\alpha}\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,N
and

\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}=\left(\int_{Q_{T}}|{{\bm{u}}}|^{2}\,dxdt\right)^{1\over 2}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}|u^{\alpha}(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt\right)^{1\over 2}.

2.

For any
q,r\geq 1
we consider the anisotropic Lebesgue space
L^{q,r}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
equipped with mixed norm

\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}=\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}|u^{\alpha}(x,t)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{r\over q}dt\right]^{1\over r}.

In the particular case
q=2
,
r=\infty
, we have

\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2,\infty;Q_{T}}=\underset{t\in[0,T]}{{\rm esssup\,}}\|{{\bm{u}}}(\,\cdot\,,t)\|_{2;\Omega}

and for
q=r
we obtain the parabolic Lebesgue space
L^{q}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

3.

W_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
W_{2}^{1,1}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
are the Banach spaces consisting of all
{{\bm{u}}}\in L^{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for which the following norms are finite

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{W_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T})}=\,

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}+\|D{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{W_{2}^{1,1}(Q_{T})}=\,

\displaystyle\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}+\|D{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}+\|{{\bm{u}}}_{t}\|_{2;Q_{T}}.

The space
\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T};\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the last norm.

4.

V_{2}(Q_{T};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a subspace of
W_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{V_{2}(Q_{T})}=\|{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2,\infty;Q_{T}}+\|D{{\bm{u}}}\|_{2;Q_{T}}<\infty.

(2.1)

Namely,

V_{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})=L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W_{2}^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{N}))

endowed with the norm (
2.1
).

5.

V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
consists of all
{{\bm{u}}}\in V_{2}(Q_{T};\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuous in
t
with respect to the norm of
L^{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, that is

\lim_{\Delta t\rightarrow 0}\|{{\bm{u}}}(\cdot,t+\Delta t)-{{\bm{u}}}(\cdot,t)\|_{2;\Omega}=0

(2.2)

endowed with the norm (
2.1
).
As usual, we denote by

\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with respect to the norm (
2.1
).

For reader’s convenience, we recall the
Hölder inequality on anisotropic Lebesgue spaces
and some of its modifications (cf.
[
23
, Ch. II]
). Precisely, for any exponents
q\geq q_{1}\geq 1,

r\geq r_{1}\geq 1,
there hold

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{Q_{T}}u(x,t)\,v(x,t)\,dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|{u}\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}\|{v}\|_{\frac{q}{q-1},\frac{r}{r-1};Q_{T}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|uv\|_{q_{1},r_{1};Q_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}\|v\|_{\frac{qq_{1}}{q-q_{1}},\frac{rr_{1}}{r-r_{1}};Q_{T}}.

(2.4)

Now, we give also some properties of the spaces
V_{2}(Q_{T})
and
\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T}).
Let
u\in\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T})
, then from the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality for the
\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
-functions there exists a constant
C_{q}=C_{q}(n,q)
such that

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{q,\Omega}\leq C_{q}\|u(\cdot,t)\|^{1-\alpha}_{2,\Omega}\|Du(\cdot,t)\|^{\alpha}_{2,\Omega}\quad\text{ for a.a. }t\in[0,T]

with
\alpha=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{q},
and where
q
is as in (
2.6
). Hence, we have the following imbedding type inequality in
\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T})

\|u\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}\leq C_{q}\|u\|_{2,\infty;Q_{T}}^{1-\alpha}\,\|Du\|_{2,\alpha r;Q_{T}}^{\alpha}.

Taking
\alpha={2/r},
where
r
is as in (
2.6
), we get a series of inequalities and the first one is

\|u\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}\leq C_{q}\|u\|_{2,\infty;Q_{T}}^{1-{2\over r}}\,\|Du\|_{2;Q_{T}}^{2\over r}

(2.5)

where
\displaystyle{1\over r}+{n\over 2q}={n\over 4},
and
r
and
q
satisfy

\begin{cases}r\in[2,\infty]\quad&q\in[2,{2n\over n-2}],\quad\mbox{ for }n\geq 3\\[4.0pt]
r\in(2,\infty)\quad&q\in[2,\infty),\quad\ \,\mbox{ for }n=2\\[4.0pt]
r\in[4,\infty]\quad&q\in[2,\infty],\quad\ \ \mbox{ for }n=1.\end{cases}

(2.6)

Estimating the right-hand side of (
2.5
) by Young’s inequality

ab\leq\frac{2}{r}\ a^{r\over 2}+\frac{r-2}{r}\ b^{r\over r-2},\qquad r>2

we obtain the second inequality

\|u\|_{q,r;Q_{T}}\leq C_{q}{\frac{2}{r}}\,\|Du\|_{2;Q_{T}}+C_{q}\frac{r-2}{r}\,\|u\|_{2,\infty;Q_{T}}\,\leq\beta\,\|u\|_{V_{2}(Q_{T})}

(2.7)

where
\beta=\beta(n,q,r)
and
q
and
r
are as in (
2.6
).

One more notion that we need is that of
the Steklov average.

For a function
\zeta(x,t)\in\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{[-h,T]})
that is zero for
t\leq 0
and
t\geq T-h,
we define
the Steklov average in the future
as

\zeta_{h}(x,t)={1\over h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\zeta(x,\tau)\,d\tau

(2.8)

and
the Steklov average in the past
as

\zeta_{\overline{h}}(x,t)={1\over h}\int_{t-h}^{t}\zeta(x,\tau)\,d\tau.

(2.9)

We notice that, for each fixed
h
,
\zeta_{h}(x,t)
is defined in
\overline{Q}_{T-h}=\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T-h]
and
\zeta_{\overline{h}}(x,t)
is defined in
\overline{Q}_{[-h,T]}=\overline{\Omega}\times[-h,T],

where
0<h<T.

It is well known that if
\zeta\in L^{q,r}(Q_{T})
with
q,r\geq 1
, then
\zeta_{h}
approximates
\zeta
with respect to the norm in
L^{q,r}.

Furthermore, if
\zeta\in\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T})
, then
\zeta_{h}\in\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{T-h})
and
\zeta_{h}
approximates
\zeta
with respect to the norm in
V_{2}
(cf.
[
23
, Ch. II]
).
Moreover, if we take
\zeta\in\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}(Q_{T}),
then
\zeta_{\overline{h}}\in\overset{\circ}{W}{}^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{[h,T]}).

We recall that the Steklov averages satisfy these two properties:

\begin{split}\left(\zeta_{\overline{h}}\right)_{t}&=\left(\zeta_{t}\right)_{\overline{h}}\\
\int_{0}^{T}u(x,t)\,\zeta_{\overline{h}}(x,t)\,dt&=\int_{0}^{T-h}u_{h}(x,t)\,\zeta(x,t)\,dt.\end{split}

(2.10)

The last equation holds for any square summable function, such that
\zeta(x,t)=0
for
t\leq 0
and for
t\geq T-h.

The following result permits to give a total estimate for the maximum of the modulus of the solutions in the whole domain of the definition.
Let
u
in
V_{2}(Q_{T})
. Denote by

u^{k}=\max\{u(x,t)-k;0\},

k>0
for all
(x,t)\in Q_{T}
and define the set

A^{k}(t)=\{x\in\Omega:\,u(x,t)>k,\,t\in[0,T]\}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
23
]
, Ch. II, Theorem 6.1)
.

Suppose that
\|u\|_{\infty;S_{T}}\leq M_{0}
,
M_{0}\geq 0
, and

\|u^{k}\|_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T})}\leq Ck\mu^{{1+\varkappa}\over r}(k)

(2.11)

hold for
k\geq M_{0}
, where
\mu(k)=\int_{0}^{T}|A^{k}(t)|^{r\over q}\,dt
,
q
and
r
as in (
2.6
) and
0<\varkappa<1
. Then

\|u\|_{\infty;Q_{T}}\leq 2M_{0}\left[1+2^{{2\over\varkappa}+{1\over\varkappa^{2}}}(\beta c)^{1+{1\over\varkappa}}T^{{1+\varkappa\over r}}|\Omega|^{{1+\varkappa\over q}}\right]

where
\beta
is the constant from (
2.7
) and
C
is an arbitrary constant.

3.
Statement of the problem

Recall that we are going to study the boundedness of the weak solutions to the system

u^{\alpha}_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\big(A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\big)+{b^{\alpha}}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})=0

(3.1)

in
Q_{T}
with
\alpha=1,\cdots,N
.
In order to obtain our results we impose the following assumptions regarding the structure and the asymptotic behaviour of the nonlinear operators
{\bm{A}}
and
{\bm{b}}.

(H_{1})

Controlled growth conditions:
there exist a positive constant
\Lambda
and functions
\varphi_{1}\in L^{2}(Q_{T})
and
\varphi_{2}\in L^{q_{0},r_{0}}(Q_{T}),
with
r_{0},q_{0}\geq 1,

{1\over r_{0}}+{n\over 2q_{0}}=1+{n\over 4},
such that

\displaystyle|{{\bm{A}}}(x,t,{\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left(\varphi_{1}(x,t)+|{{\bm{u}}}|^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+|{{\bm{z}}}|\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle|{{\bm{b}}}(x,t,{\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})|

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left(\varphi_{2}(x,t)+|{\bm{u}}|^{1+\frac{4}{n}}+|{\bm{z}}|^{\frac{n+4}{n+2}}\right)

(3.3)

for a.a.
(x,t)\in Q_{T}
and all
({\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}.

(H_{2})

Componentwise coercivity of the differential operator:
there exist positive constants
\nu,\mu
and
k_{0}
such that for all
\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and for
|{u^{\alpha}}|\geq k_{0}
, it holds

\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{\alpha}_{i}(x,t,{\bm{u}},{\bm{z}})z^{\alpha}_{i}\geq\nu|{{\bm{z}}}^{\alpha}|^{2}-\mu|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta}-{|u^{\alpha}|}^{2}\psi(x,t)

(3.4)

for a.a.
(x,t)\in Q_{T}.
The function
\psi\in L^{q_{2},r_{2}}(Q_{T})
is as in (
3.11
).

(H_{3})

Componentwise sign condition:
for
|{u^{\alpha}}|\geq k_{0}
we have

-b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},{{\bm{z}}})u^{\alpha}\leq|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\psi(x,t)+\mu|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta}+\Lambda|{\bm{z}}^{\alpha}|^{2}

(3.5)

for a.a.
(x,t)\in Q_{T}
and for all
{{\bm{z}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times n}.

The vector function
{\bm{u}}\in V^{1,0}_{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is a
weak solution
of (
3.1
) if, for any
0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq T
and each
\alpha=1,\ldots,N
it holds

\begin{split}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}&u^{\alpha}(x,t)\eta_{t}^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dxdt\\
&+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{\alpha}_{i}(x,t,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})D_{i}\eta^{\alpha}(x,t)\\
&+b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\eta^{\alpha}(x,t)\Big\}\,dxdt\\
&+\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}(x,t)\eta^{\alpha}(x,t)\Big|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\,dx=0\end{split}

(3.6)

for any
\bm{\eta}(x,t)\in{\overset{\circ}{W}}{}^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{T};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

The definition (
3.6
) has sense if the integrals are convergent and this is possible because of the
growth conditions

(H_{1}).

Now, we take as test function in (
3.6
) the Steklov average

\zeta^{\alpha}_{\overline{h}}={1\over h}\int_{t-h}^{t}\zeta^{\alpha}(x,\tau)\,d\tau

where
\zeta^{\alpha}\in\overset{\circ}{V}{}_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T-h}).

Then, (
3.6
) becomes

\begin{split}-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}&u^{\alpha}(x,t)({\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}})_{t}(x,t)\,dxdt\\
&+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,t,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})\,D_{i}\,\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}(x,t)\\
&+b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\,\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}(x,t)\Big\}\,dxdt\\
&+\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}(x,t)\zeta^{\alpha}_{\overline{h}}(x,t)\bigg|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\,dx=0\end{split}

(3.7)

where
0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq T-h.

By integration by parts and (
2.10
) the first term in (
3.7
) becomes

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle u^{\alpha}(x,t)\left(\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}\right)_{t}(x,t)\,dxdt=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}(x,t)\left(\zeta_{t}^{\alpha}\right)_{\overline{h}}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}_{h}(x,t)\zeta_{t}^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}_{h}(x,t)\zeta^{\alpha}(x,t)\bigg|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\,dx+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{u_{ht}^{\alpha}}(x,t)\zeta^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}u^{\alpha}(x,t)\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}(x,t)\bigg|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\,dx+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{u_{ht}^{\alpha}}(x,t)\zeta^{\alpha}(x,t)\,dxdt

where
u_{h}^{\alpha}
is the Steklov averege of
u^{\alpha}
(see (
2.8
)). Hence,

\begin{split}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\!\!\int_{\Omega}&\Big[u_{ht}^{\alpha}(x,t)\zeta^{\alpha}(x,t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,t,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})D_{i}\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}(x,t)\\
&+b^{\alpha}(x,t,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})\zeta_{\overline{h}}^{\alpha}(x,t)\Big]\,dxdt=0.\end{split}

(3.8)

For some positive constant
k
we take

\zeta^{\alpha}(x,t)=\xi^{2}(x,t)\max\{u_{h}^{\alpha}(x,t)-k;0\}=:\xi^{2}u_{h}^{\alpha,k}

(3.9)

where
\xi(x,t)
is an arbitrary nonnegative continuous piecewise-smooth function that is equal to zero on the lateral boundary
S_{T}.
Observing that

D_{t}u^{\alpha,k}=D_{t}u^{\alpha},\quad D_{i}u^{\alpha,k}=D_{i}u^{\alpha}

we transform the first term in (
3.8
) as follows

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}u_{ht}^{\alpha}\,u_{h}^{\alpha,k}\,\xi^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\int_{\Omega}\big(u_{h}^{\alpha,k}\big)^{2}\xi^{2}(x,t)\Big|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\big(u_{h}^{\alpha,k}\big)^{2}\xi(x,t)\xi_{t}(x,t)\,dxdt.

Now, we pass to the limit in (
3.8
) as
h\rightarrow 0
, obtaining

\begin{split}{1\over 2}\,&\|u^{\alpha,k}(\,\cdot,t)\xi(\,\cdot,t)\|_{2,\Omega}^{2}\Big|_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\Big[-\left(u^{\alpha,k}\right)^{2}\xi(x,t)\,\xi_{t}(x,t)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\alpha}(x,t,{\bm{u}},D{\bm{u}})\,D_{i}(u^{\alpha,k}\,\xi^{2}(x,t))\\
&+b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\,u^{\alpha,k}\,\xi^{2}(x,t)\Big]\,dxdt=0.\end{split}

(3.10)

The validity of the limit in all of the terms follows by the properties of the Steklov average (see
[
23
]
).

In what follows, we assume that the weak solution
{\bm{u}}
of (
3.1
)
belongs to
L^{q_{1},r_{1}}(Q_{T},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
where the exponents
q_{1},r_{1}>1
and the other parameters to be used in the sequel, satisfy

\begin{cases}\text{ for }n\geq 2&\\[5.0pt]
(\delta-2)\left(\dfrac{1}{r_{1}}+\dfrac{n}{2q_{1}}\right)<1,\ &\delta>2,\quad\dfrac{1}{r_{2}}+\dfrac{n}{2q_{2}}<1,\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{q_{1}}{\delta-2},\,q_{2}\in\left(\dfrac{n}{2},\infty\right],&\dfrac{r_{1}}{\delta-2},\,r_{2}\in\left(1,\infty\right]\\[10.0pt]
\psi\in L^{q_{2},r_{2}}(Q_{T})&q_{2}\in\left(\dfrac{n}{2},\infty\right],\,r_{2}\in(1,\infty]\\[10.0pt]
\text{ for }n=1&\\[5.0pt]
\dfrac{q_{1}}{\delta-2},\,q_{2}\in[1,\infty],&\dfrac{r_{1}}{\delta-2},\,r_{2}\in(1,\infty)\\[10.0pt]
\psi\in L^{q_{2},r_{2}}(Q_{T})&q_{2}\in[1,\infty],\,r_{2}\in(1,\infty).\end{cases}

(3.11)

Suppose that the solution
{\bm{u}}
is essentially bounded on the parabolic boundary
\partial Q_{T}
with some constant
M_{0}>0,
that is
\|{\bm{u}}\|_{\infty;\,\partial Q_{T}}\leq M_{0};
we take
k\geq\max\{k_{0},M_{0}\},
where
k_{0}>0
is the constant from
(H_{2}),
and observe that
u^{\alpha,k}
is equal to zero on the parabolic boundary
\partial Q_{T}
. Thus, in (
3.10
) we can choose
t_{0}=0
and
\xi=1,
and hence
\xi_{t}=0,
that gives

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|{u}^{\alpha,k}(\,\cdot,t_{1})\|^{2}_{2,A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}&+\int_{Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}A^{\alpha}_{i}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\,D_{i}u^{\alpha,k}\\
&+b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\,u^{\alpha,k}\Big]\,dxdt=0\end{split}

(3.12)

where the integration is taken on the sets

\displaystyle A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega:u^{\alpha}(x,t_{1})>k,\;t_{1}\in[0,T]\}

\displaystyle Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,t_{1}]:u^{\alpha}(x,t)>k\}.

4.
Main result

The conditions imposed above permit us to obtain a componentwise maximum principle and, hence, also essential boundedness of the solution of (
3.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\bm{u}}\in V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{T},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{q_{1},r_{1}}(Q_{T},{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
be a weak solution of the system (
3.1
) which is essentially bounded on the boundary
\partial Q_{T}.

Assume that the terms

{{\bm{A}}}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},{{\bm{z}}})
and
{{\bm{b}}}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},{{\bm{z}}})
satisfy conditions (
3.2
)-(
3.5
) with
\nu-\Lambda>0.
Then

\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\infty;Q_{T}}\leq K

(4.1)

with a constant
K
depending on known quantities and
\|{u^{\alpha}}\|_{q_{1},r_{1};Q_{T}}.

If we take
\mu=0
in (
3.4
)-(
3.5
), then the constant
K
does not depend on the last norm.

Proof.

By (
3.12
), taking into account
\displaystyle{u^{\alpha,k}\over u^{\alpha}}<1
, (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{2,A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}+\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\left(\nu|Du^{\alpha}|^{2}-\mu|{u^{\alpha}}|^{\delta}-{|u^{\alpha}|}^{2}\psi\right)\,dxdt\\
&\leq-\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}b^{\alpha}(x,t,{{\bm{u}}},D{{\bm{u}}})\,u^{\alpha}(x,t)\displaystyle\frac{u^{\alpha,k}(x,t)}{u^{\alpha}(x,t)}\,dxdt\\
&\leq\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\left(|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\psi+\mu|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta}+\Lambda|Du^{\alpha}|^{2}\right)\,dxdt.\end{split}

(4.2)

Hence

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{2,A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}+(\nu-\Lambda)\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}|Du^{\alpha}|^{2}\,dxdt\\
&\leq 2\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\left(\mu|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta}+|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\psi\right)\,dxdt.\end{split}

(4.3)

Keeping in mind that
\nu-\Lambda>0
, we obtain

\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{2;A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}+\|Du^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{2;Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}\leq C\int_{Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}\left(|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta}+|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\psi\right)\,dxdt

(4.4)

where
C
depends on
\mu
and
\nu-\Lambda.

Let us estimate the integrals in the right-hand side. For the first one, using the inequalities in §
2
, we obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}&=\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}|u^{\alpha}|^{\delta-2}\cdot|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\,dxdt\\
&\leq\left[\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left(\int_{A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}|u^{\alpha}|^{q_{1}}dx\right)^{\frac{r_{1}}{q_{1}}}dt\right]^{\frac{\delta-2}{r_{1}}}\\
&\quad\times\left[\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\left(\int_{A^{\alpha,k}(t_{1})}|u^{\alpha}|^{\overline{q}_{1}}dx\right)^{\frac{\overline{r}_{1}}{\overline{q}_{1}}}dt\right]^{\frac{2}{\overline{r}_{1}}}\\
&=\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\|u^{\alpha}\|^{2}_{\overline{q}_{1},\overline{r}_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\\
&\leq 2\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\biggl[\|u^{\alpha}-k\|^{2}_{\overline{q}_{1},\overline{r}_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+k^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|)^{\frac{\overline{r}_{1}}{\overline{q}_{1}}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{2}{\overline{r}_{1}}}\bigg]\end{split}

(4.5)

where
\displaystyle\overline{q}_{1}={2q_{1}\over q_{1}-(\delta-2)}
,
\displaystyle\overline{r}_{1}={2r_{1}\over r_{1}-(\delta-2)}
. We notice that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\leq\overline{r}_{1}<\infty,\quad 2\leq\overline{q}_{1}<{2n\over n-2}\quad&\mbox{ for }n\geq 2\\
\displaystyle 2<\overline{r}_{1}<\infty,\quad 2\leq\overline{q}_{1}\leq\infty\quad&\mbox{ for }n=1\end{cases}

(4.6)

and

\displaystyle{1\over\overline{r}_{1}}+{n\over 2\overline{q}_{1}}>{n\over 4}.
If
n>2
we can find an opportune
0<\varkappa_{1}<1,
such that
\displaystyle{1\over\overline{r}_{1}}+{n\over 2\overline{q}_{1}}={n\over 4}(1+\varkappa_{1}).
In case of
n=2
we consider
\overline{q}_{1}\in[2,\infty).

Introduce

\widehat{q}_{1}=\overline{q}_{1}\left(1+\varkappa_{1}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{r}_{1}=\overline{r}_{1}\left(1+\varkappa_{1}\right).

Direct calculations show that
\widehat{q}_{1}
and
\widehat{r}_{1}
verify (
2.6
)
and by (
2.4
) we obtain

\begin{split}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\overline{q}_{1},\overline{r}_{1};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}&\leq\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\widehat{q}_{1},\widehat{r}_{1};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\|\chi_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\|^{2}_{{\widehat{q}_{1}\over\varkappa_{1}},{\widehat{r}_{1}\over\varkappa_{1}};Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}\\
&\leq\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\widehat{q}_{1},\widehat{r}_{1};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\left(t_{1}^{{1\over\widehat{r}_{1}}}|\Omega|^{{1\over\widehat{q}_{1}}}\right)^{2\varkappa_{1}}.\end{split}

(4.7)

In the case
q_{1}=\delta-2,
which is possible if
n=1,
we obtain
\overline{q}_{1}=\infty
and hence also
\widehat{q}_{1}=\infty.
Then (
4.7
) becames

\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\infty,\overline{r}_{1};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\leq\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\infty,\widehat{r}_{1};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,t_{1}^{{2\varkappa_{1}\over\widehat{r}_{1}}}.

(4.8)

This permits us to apply (
2.7
). In the case
n\geq 2
we obtain

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq 2\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\Biggl[\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}^{2}\\
&\quad\times\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|)^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{1}}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}dt\right)^{\frac{2}{\overline{r}_{1}}-\frac{2}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\\
&\left.+k^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|)^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{1}}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}dt\right)^{\frac{2(1+\varkappa_{1})}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\right]\\
&\leq 2\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\Bigg\{\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}^{2}\big(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\big)^{2\varkappa_{1}}\\
&+k^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\,|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{1}}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{2(\varkappa_{1}+1)}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\Bigg\}\end{split}

(4.9)

where

\beta=\beta(n,\overline{q}_{1},\overline{r}_{1})

and
\displaystyle{\widehat{q}_{1}\over\overline{q}_{1}}={\widehat{r}_{1}\over\overline{r}_{1}}=\varkappa_{1}+1.

If
q_{1}=\delta-2,
in view of (
4.8
), we have

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq 2\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{\delta-2,r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\left(\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}^{2}t_{1}^{\frac{2\varkappa_{1}}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}+k^{2}\,t_{1}^{\frac{2(\varkappa_{1}+1)}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\right).\end{split}

(4.10)

We trate
I_{2}
analogously. By the Hölder inequality (
2.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}I_{2}=\int_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}|u^{\alpha}|^{2}\psi\,dxdt&\leq\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\overline{q}_{2},\overline{r}_{2},Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}^{2}\end{split}

(4.11)

where
\displaystyle\overline{q}_{2}=\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}
,
\displaystyle\overline{r}_{2}=\frac{2r_{2}}{r_{2}-1}
. We notice that

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{1\over\overline{r}_{2}}+{n\over 2\overline{q}_{2}}>{n\over 4}&\\[6.0pt]
2\leq\overline{q}_{2}<\dfrac{2n}{n-2},\quad 2\leq\overline{r}_{2}<\infty,\quad&\mbox{ for }n\geq 2\\[6.0pt]
2\leq\overline{q}_{2}\leq\infty,\quad\quad\ \,2<\overline{r}_{2}<\infty,\quad&\mbox{ for }n=1.\end{cases}

As before, we can find
0<\varkappa_{2}<1
such that
\displaystyle{1\over\overline{r}_{2}}+{n\over 2\overline{q}_{2}}={n\over 4}(1+\varkappa_{2})
and

\widehat{q}_{2}=\overline{q}_{2}(1+\varkappa_{2})
and
\widehat{r}_{2}=\overline{r}_{2}(1+\varkappa_{2})
verify (
2.6
) and by
(
2.4
)

\displaystyle\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\overline{q}_{2},\overline{r}_{2};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\widehat{q}_{2},\widehat{r}_{2};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\|\chi_{Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\|^{2}_{{\widehat{q}_{2}\over\varkappa_{2}},{\widehat{r}_{2}\over\varkappa_{2}};Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{\widehat{q}_{2},\widehat{r}_{2};Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\left(t_{1}^{{1\over\widehat{r}_{2}}}|\Omega|^{{1\over\widehat{q}_{2}}}\right)^{2\varkappa_{2}}.

Hence, using (
2.7
), we get

\begin{split}I_{2}\leq 2\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2};Q_{T}}&\,\bigg\{\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}})}\big(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{2}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\big)^{2\varkappa_{2}}\\
&+k^{2}\left[\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{2}}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\,dt\right]^{2(\varkappa_{2}+1)\over\widehat{r}_{2}}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(4.12)

In case of
q_{2}=1
and hence
\overline{q}_{2}=\infty,

n=1,
we obtain

\begin{split}I_{2}\leq 2\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2};Q_{T}}&\,\left(\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q^{\alpha,k}_{t_{1}})}\,t_{1}^{\frac{{2\varkappa_{2}}}{\widehat{r}_{2}}}+k^{2}\,t_{1}^{2(\varkappa_{2}+1)\over\widehat{r}_{2}}\right).\end{split}

(4.13)

Combine (
4.9
), (
4.12
) and (
4.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|u^{\alpha,k}&\|^{2}_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}\\
&\leq C\Biggl\{\|u^{\alpha,k}\|_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}^{2}\Bigg[\beta^{2}\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\big(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\big)^{2\varkappa_{1}}\\
&+\beta^{2}\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2},Q_{T}}\big(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{2}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\big)^{2\varkappa_{2}}\Bigg]\\
&+\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,k^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\,|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{1}}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{2(\varkappa_{1}+1)}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\\
&+k^{2}\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2},Q_{T}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{2}}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\,dt\right)^{2(\varkappa_{2}+1)\over\widehat{r}_{2}}\Biggl\}.\end{split}

(4.14)

Taking
t_{1}
such small that

C\beta^{2}\left(\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\left(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\right)^{2\varkappa_{1}}+\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2},Q_{T}}\left(t_{1}^{\frac{1}{\widehat{r}_{2}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\right)^{2\varkappa_{2}}\right)<{1\over 2}

we obtain

\begin{split}\|u^{\alpha,k}\|^{2}&{}_{V_{2}^{1,0}(Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k})}\\
&\leq C\,k^{2}\Bigg[\|u^{\alpha}\|^{\delta-2}_{q_{1},r_{1},Q_{t_{1}}^{\alpha,k}}\,\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\,|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{1}}{\widehat{q}_{1}}}\,dt\right)^{\frac{2(\varkappa_{1}+1)}{\widehat{r}_{1}}}\\
&+\|\psi\|_{q_{2},r_{2},Q_{T}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|A^{\alpha,k}(t)|^{\frac{\widehat{r}_{2}}{\widehat{q}_{2}}}\,dt\right)^{2(\varkappa_{2}+1)\over\widehat{r}_{2}}\Bigg].\end{split}

(4.15)

Analogously in case of
n=1
.

Since (
4.15
) is equivalent to (
2.11
) in Theorem
2.1
we deduce that
u^{\alpha}
is essentially bounded from above in a cylinder with high
t_{1}
which is small enough.
In order to extend this result in the whole cylinder we consider
u^{\alpha}
successively in
Q_{2}=\Omega\times(t_{1},2t_{1})
,
Q_{3}=\Omega\times(2t_{1},3t_{1})
and so on, covering in such way the whole cylinder
Q_{T}.

Moreover, it is possible to estimate
u^{\alpha}(x,t)
from below. To this aim, we apply the result just obtained to the function
\tilde{u}^{\alpha}(x,t)=-u^{\alpha}(x,t)
which verifies equations similar to
u^{\alpha}(x,t)
, i.e.

{\tilde{u}^{\alpha}}_{t}-{\rm div\,}\tilde{A}^{\alpha}(x,t,\tilde{{\bm{u}}},D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})+\tilde{b}^{\alpha}(x,t,\tilde{{\bm{u}}},D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})=0\qquad(x,t)\in Q_{T},

where

\tilde{A}^{\alpha}(x,t,\tilde{{\bm{u}}},D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})=-{A}^{\alpha}(x,t,-\tilde{{\bm{u}}},-D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})

and

\tilde{b}^{\alpha}(x,t,\tilde{{\bm{u}}},D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})=-{b}^{\alpha}(x,t,-\tilde{{\bm{u}}},-D\tilde{{\bm{u}}})

satisfy the conditions (
3.11
)-(
3.5
). Thus, by Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\|u^{\alpha}\|_{\infty;Q_{T}}\leq K,

where
K=K(M_{0},k_{0},q_{i},r_{i},n,\varkappa_{i},|\Omega|,T,\|u^{\alpha}\|_{q_{1},r_{1};Q_{T}}).

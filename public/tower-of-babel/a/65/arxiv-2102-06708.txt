A pedagogical note on the computation of relative entropy of two n n -mode gaussian states
K. R. Parthasarathy

Abstract
A formula for the relative entropy S ( ρ | | σ ) = Tr ρ ( log ρ − log σ ) S(\rho||\sigma)={\rm Tr}\,\rho\,(\log\,\rho-\log\,\sigma) of two gaussian states ρ \rho , σ \sigma in the boson Fock space Γ ⁡ ( ℂ n ) \Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{n}) is presented. It is shown that the relative entropy has a classical and a quantum part: The classical part consists of a weighted linear combination of relative Shannon entropies of n n pairs of Bernouli trials arising from the thermal state composition of the gaussian states ρ \rho and σ \sigma . The quantum part has a sum of n n terms, that are functions of the annihilation means and the covariance matrices of 1-mode marginals of the gaussian state ρ ′ \rho^{\prime} , which is equivalent to ρ \rho under a disentangling unitary gaussian symmetry operation of the state σ \sigma . A generalized formula for the Petz-Rényi relative entropy S α ( ρ | | σ ) = − 1 α − 1 log Tr ρ α σ 1 − α , 0 < α < 1 S_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)=-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha},\ 0<\alpha<1 for gaussian states ρ \rho , σ \sigma is also presented. Furthermore it is shown that S α ( ρ | | σ ) S_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma) converges to the limit S ( ρ | | σ ) S(\rho||\sigma) as α \alpha increases to 1. In memory of Robin L Hudson



1
Introduction

Every gaussian state in
\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is completely determined by its annihilation mean and covariance matrix. A finer analysis of the covariance matrix reveals that such a guassian state is composed of a product of
n
thermal states (including the vacuum state) and an entangling unitary gaussian symmetry operator arising from the group generated by phase translations and symplectic transformations. Every thermal state, when viewed in a product basis, gives rise to a natural Bernoulli (binomial) trial with success and failure probabilities corresponding to
detection
or
no-detection
of a particle.

In this paper we present a formula for the relative entropy
S(\rho||\sigma)={\rm Tr}\,\rho\,(\log\,\rho-\log\,\sigma)
of two gaussian states
\rho,\ \sigma
in
\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. It is shown that the relative entropy
S(\rho||\sigma)
contains two distinguished components: the first part is a weighted linear combination of classical Shannon relative entropies
H(p_{1j}:p_{2j}),j=1,2,\ldots,n
of
n
pairs of Bernouli trials arising from the thermal components of the gaussian states
\rho
and
\sigma
. The second part is purely quantum and it contains a sum of
n
terms, each of which is arising from the annihilation mean
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and the
2\times 2
covariance matrices
T_{k}
of 1-mode marginal states
\rho(\alpha_{k},T_{k})
of a gaussian state
\rho^{\prime}=U^{\dagger}\,\rho\,U
where
U=W(\boldsymbol{\ell})\,\Gamma(L)
corresponds to the disentangling gaussian symmetry of the state
\sigma
.

Extending the above concepts we derive an explicit formula for the Petz-Rényi relative entropy
S_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)=-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\alpha}\sigma^{1-\alpha},\ 0<\alpha<1
for two gaussian states
\rho
,
\sigma
in
\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. We show that in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 1
the Petz-Rényi relative entropy
S_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)
reduces to the relative entropy
S(\rho||\sigma)
as expected.

Structure theorem for

For
0<s\leq\infty
, we may associate a single mode gaussian

state
\rho(s)
in
\Gamma(\mathbb{C})

\rho(s)=(1-e^{-s})\,e^{-s\,a^{\dagger}\,a}

\rho(\infty)=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|

yields a natural binomial distribution with probability for success equal to

, where success stands for the event that the number of particles detected is greater than or equal to 1. Then, failure is equivalent to
no particle count
with probability

The von Neumann entropy of the thermal state

is given by

\displaystyle S\left(\rho(s)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Tr}\rho(s)\,\log\,\rho(s)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H(e^{-s})}{1-e^{-s}}.

Here
H(p)=-p\,\log\,p-(1-p)\,\log\,(1-p),\ 0\leq p\leq 1
is the Shannon function.
Note that
\rho(s)\rightarrow\rho(\infty)=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|
as

. The state
\rho(\infty)
being pure,

.

Consider any
n
-mode gaussian state

with mean
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

. Then, there exist

\displaystyle C=\left(L^{-1}\right)^{T}\,D(\mathbf{s})L^{-1},\ \ D(\mathbf{s})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{0}({\mathbf{s}})&0_{n}\\

Lemma 1

Let

{\mathcal{H}}

\langle\psi|\rho\,|\psi\rangle<1

Proof.

Write

\{|\psi_{k}\rangle,\,k=0,1,\ldots\}

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle\psi_{k}|\rho\,|\psi_{k}\rangle=1.

For all

Then

(5.3)

Observation 2
: Using Lemma
4
we have

S(\rho||\sigma)=S(\rho^{\prime}||\rho(t_{1})\otimes\rho(t_{2})\otimes\ldots\otimes\rho(t_{n}))
. Since
\rho^{\prime}
and
\rho
are unitarily equivalent it follows that
S(\rho^{\prime})=S(\rho)
.

Observation 3
: By Lemma
2
it follows that

S(\rho||\sigma)=-S(\rho)-\sum_{k}\,{\rm Tr}\,\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},\,T_{k})\,\log\,\rho(t_{k}).

If
t_{k}=\infty
, then
\rho(t_{k})=|\Omega\,\rangle\langle\Omega\,|
(vacuum state). If, in addition,

\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},\,T_{k})\neq|\Omega\,\rangle\langle\Omega\,|
, then by Lemma
1
we have

-{\rm Tr}\,\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},\,T_{k})\,\log\,\rho(t_{k})=\infty.

Since

-{\rm Tr}\,\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},\,T_{k})\,\log\,\rho(t_{k})>0\,\ \forall\ j,
it follows that
S(\rho||\sigma)=\infty
.

Observation 4
: If
\rho(t_{k})=|\Omega\,\rangle\langle\Omega\,|,\ {\rm whenever}\ t_{k}=\infty,

and by Lemma
3

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\,\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},\,T_{k})\,\log\,\rho(t_{k})=\log\,(1-e^{-t_{k}})-\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left(\,{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}-1\right),\ \ {\rm if}\ t_{k}<\infty.

we have

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S(\rho)+\sum_{k:\,t_{k}<\infty}\,\left\{-\log\,(1-e^{-t_{k}})+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}-1\right]\right\}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\infty,\ \ {\rm if\ }\exists\ k\ {\rm such\ that\ }t_{k}=\infty\,{\rm\ and\ }\rho((m_{\rho})_{k},T_{k})\neq|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|,\\
\\
-S(\rho)+\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,t_{k}<\infty}\,\left(-\log\,(1-e^{-t_{k}})+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left({\rm Tr}\,T_{k}+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}-1\right)\right),\ \ {\rm otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let
0<s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\ldots\leq s_{n}\leq\infty
,
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq\infty
and
S(\rho||\sigma)<\infty
. Then

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S(\rho)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\left\{-\log\,(1-e^{-t_{k}})+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}-1\right]\right\}

(5.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\left\{\frac{-H(e^{-s_{k}})}{(1-e^{-s_{k})}}-\log\,(1-e^{-t_{k}})+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}-1\right]\right\}.

Drop the suffix
k
for the
k
-th term of the summation in (
5.20
) for convinience of computation and express it as

\frac{-H(e^{-s})}{(1-e^{-s})}-\log\,(1-e^{-t})+\frac{t}{2}\,\left({\rm Tr}\,T+2\,|m_{\rho}|^{2}-1\right).

(5.21)

The first two terms of (
5.21
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{-H(e^{-s})}{(1-e^{-s})}-\log\,(1-e^{-t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H(p_{1}:p_{2})}{(1-e^{-s})}-\frac{t\,e^{-s}}{(1-e^{-s})}

(5.22)

where

\displaystyle H(p_{1}:p_{2})=p_{1}\,\log\,p_{1}+(1-p_{1})\,\log\,(1-p_{1})-p_{1}\,\log\,p_{2}-(1-p_{1})\,\log\,(1-p_{2}),\ \ p_{1}=e^{-s},\ p_{2}=e^{-t}

(5.23)

is the classical Shannon relative entropy of a binomial trial with probability of success
p_{1}
with respect to another with probability of sucess
p_{2}
.
Thus (
5.21
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{H(e^{-s}:e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-s})}+\frac{t}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T-\left(1+\frac{2\,e^{-s}}{1-e^{-s}}\right)+2\,|m_{\rho}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H(e^{-s}:e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-s})}+\frac{t}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T-\coth\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)+2\,|m_{\rho}|^{2}\right].

(5.24)

Substituting (
5.24
) in (

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\left\{\frac{H(e^{-s_{k}}:e^{-t_{k}})}{(1-e^{-s_{k}})}+\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}-\coth\left(\frac{s_{k}}{2}\right)+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}\right]\right\}

whenever
S(\rho||\sigma)<\infty.

Remark 1
.

Relative entropy
S(\rho||\sigma)
of two gaussian states
\rho
and
\sigma
consists of a classical part
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\,\frac{H(e^{-s_{k}}:e^{-t_{k}})}{(1-e^{-s_{k}})}
and a quantum part
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\frac{t_{k}}{2}\,\left[{\rm Tr}\,T_{k}-\coth\left(\frac{s_{k}}{2}\right)+2\,|(m_{\rho})_{k}|^{2}\right].

Remark 2
.

Note that
\coth\left(\frac{s_{k}}{2}\right)
is equal to the trace of the
2\times 2
covariance matrix of the single mode gaussian state
\rho(s_{k})
.

Remark 3
.

Note that

\displaystyle e^{-s_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Success\ probability}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{Pr}(\#{\rm\ particles\ }\geq 1\ {\rm\ in\ the\ thermal\ state}\ \rho(s_{k})),

\displaystyle e^{-t_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Success\ probability}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{Pr}(\#{\rm\ particles\ }\geq 1\ {\rm\ in\ the\ thermal\ state}\ \rho(t_{k}))

whereas
1-e^{-s_{k}}
and
1-e^{-t_{k}}
are respectively the probabilities of the ”no particle count” in the thermal states
\rho(s_{k})
and
\rho(t_{k})
.

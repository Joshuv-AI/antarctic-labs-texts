Optimal Combinatorial Neural Codes with Matched Metric δ r \delta_{r} : Characterization and Constructions
Aixian Zhang, Xiaoyan. Jin, Keqin Feng

Abstract
. Based on the theoretical neuroscience, G. Cotardo and A. Ravagnavi in [ 6 ] introduced a kind of asymmetric binary codes called combinatorial neural codes (CN codes for short), with a “matched metric” δ r \delta_{r} called asymmetric discrepancy, instead of the Hamming distance d H d_{H} for usual error-correcting codes. They also presented the Hamming, Singleton and Plotkin bounds for CN codes with respect to δ r \delta_{r} and asked how to construct the CN codes 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} with large size | 𝒞 | |{\mathcal{C}}| and δ r ​ ( 𝒞 ) . \delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}). In this paper we firstly show that a binary code 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} reaches one of the above bounds for δ r ​ ( 𝒞 ) \delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}) if and only if 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} reaches the corresponding bounds for d H d_{H} and r r is sufficiently closed to 1. This means that all optimal CN codes come from the usual optimal codes. Secondly we present several constructions of CN codes with nice and flexible parameters ( n , K , δ r ​ ( 𝒞 ) ) (n,K,\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})) by using bent functions.

Firstly we introduce some bounds of the CN codes

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[

]

[A]

If

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

(1)

Proof.

(1) Let

If
{\mathcal{C}}

Since

\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil\geq d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}).
But

by Lemma
2.3
(1),
we get
d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil
and

reaches the Singleton bound for

Moreover,

\displaystyle\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})-1<\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\leq d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})-1<\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}

\displaystyle(\mbox{since}\ \ \frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})\ \mbox{by Lemma }\ref{thm-cr}\ (1))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})>\frac{r+1}{2}(d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})-1).

Therefore

reaches the Singleton bound for
\delta_{r}

reaches the Singleton bound for

d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})\geq\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil
and

2d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})\geq 2\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil=2d>n.

Furthermore, from

\frac{2d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})}{2d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})-n}\leq\frac{2d}{2d-n}.

{\mathcal{C}}

Moreover,

\displaystyle d=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\lceil\frac{2\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})}{r+1}\rceil=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})>\frac{r+1}{2}(d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})-1).

This gives the first statement of (1). If

{\mathcal{C}}
reaches the Hamming bound for
\delta_{r}
, then

2.3

Definition 2.5
.

For

an

HH^{T}=nI_{n}\ \ \ (I_{n}\ \mbox{is the identity matrix of order }n).

It is well-known that for any Hadamard matrix of order

should be divided by

One of the famous conjecture in combinatorial theory is that for all

and
n\geq 4,

there exists a Hadamard matrix of order

This conjecture is verified for all

up to a very large number.

Let
H=(h_{ij})_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}
be a Hadamard matrix of order
n=4m.
We can assume that
h_{i0}=1\ (0\leq i\leq n-1)

without losing of generality. Namely,

H=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\\
\vdots&H^{\prime}\\
1&\end{array}\right],~H^{\prime}=(h_{ij})\ (0\leq i\leq n-1,1\leq j\leq n-1).

Let
h_{ij}=(-1)^{c_{ij}},c_{ij}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}=\{0,1\}
and

c_{i}=(c_{i1},c_{i2},\cdots,c_{i,n-1})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}_{2}\ \ (0\leq i\leq n-1).

Consider the binary code
{\mathcal{C}}=\{c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{n-1}\},K=|{\mathcal{C}}|=n.
From

we can see that

for all

Therefore the parameters of

is

From

We hope that such binary code

(n,K,\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}))
by choosing suitable subset

and

|S|

for
f,g\in S,f\neq g,

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c_{f},c_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)+g(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}\right).

(3)

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})=\mbox{min}\{\delta_{r}(c_{f},c_{g})\mid f,g\in S,f\neq g,\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}\}.

Proof.

(1) Consider the mapping

\varphi_{V}:{\mathbb{B}}_{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2},\varphi_{V}(f)=c_{f}=(f(x))_{x\in V}.

This is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear mapping and
\mbox{ker}(\varphi_{V})=I(V).
Therefore

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{C}}|=|S|

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\varphi_{V}:S\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2}\ \mbox{is injective}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mbox{for}\ f,g\in S,\ f\neq g,\ \mbox{we have}\ c_{f}\neq c_{g}.

But

c_{f}\neq c_{g}\Leftrightarrow c_{f-g}\neq 0\Leftrightarrow f-g\not\in I(V).

This completes the proof of (1).

(2) For
f,g\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m},

\displaystyle d_{10}(c_{f},c_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sharp\{x\in V:f(x)=1,g(x)=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{x\in V}\left(1-(-1)^{f(x)})(1+(-1)^{g(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(|V|-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)+g(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}\right).

\displaystyle d_{01}(c_{f},c_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{10}(c_{g},c_{f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(|V|-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)+g(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c_{f},c_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rd_{10}(c_{f},c_{g})+d_{01}(c_{f},c_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)+g(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}-\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}\right).

(3) Let
\delta_{r}(c_{f},c_{g})=rd_{10}(c_{f},c_{g})+d_{01}(c_{f},c_{g})=\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}),

then

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\delta_{r}(c_{g},c_{f})=rd_{01}(c_{f},c_{g})+d_{10}(c_{f},c_{g}).

Therefore
(r-1)(d_{10}(c_{f},c_{g})-d_{01}(c_{f},c_{g}))\leq 0.
If
r>1,
then

d_{10}(c_{f},c_{g})\leq d_{01}(c_{f},c_{g}),
which means that

\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}
.
If
r=1,
then
\delta_{r}(c_{f},c_{g})=\delta_{r}(c_{g},c_{f})=d_{H}(c_{g},c_{f})

and we can choose
f
and
g
such that
\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{g(x)}\leq\sum\limits_{x\in V}(-1)^{f(x)}.

This completes the proof of (3).
∎

(n,K,d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}))

In this paper we just consider a simple case by taking bent functions and determine the minimum discrepany
\delta_{\gamma}({\mathcal{C}})

{\mathcal{C}}.

Definition 3.2
.

For a boolean function
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}\ (m\geq 2),
the Walsh transformation of
f(x)

is the function

W_{f}(y)=\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x)+x\cdot y},

where for
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}),y=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{m})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},

x\cdot y=\sum^{m}\limits_{i=1}x_{i}y_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.

f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
is called a bent function if for all

y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},W_{f}(y)=2^{\frac{m}{2}}
or
-2^{\frac{m}{2}}.

It is well known that for all even
m,m\geq 2,
there exists bent function
f(x)
in
{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
.
We use the following properties on the bent function.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
be a bent function,
m=2k.

(1) For each
0\neq a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x+a)+f(x)}=0.

(2) Let
D_{f}=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}:f(x)=1\}
be the support of
f,
then

|D_{f}|=2^{m-1}+\varepsilon 2^{k-1}
and
W_{f}(0)=\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x)}=-\varepsilon 2^{k},

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1.
If
|D_{f}|=2^{m-1}+2^{k-1},
then
f(x)+1
is also a bent function and

|D_{f+1}|=2^{m-1}-2^{k-1}.

Now we present three constructions of CN codes by boolean functions.

Construction A

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
be a bent function,
m=2k\neq 4.
For
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},

let
f_{a,b}(x)=f(x+a)+b\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
(which is also a bent function). Consider the following binary code

{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{f}=\{c(a,b)=(f_{a,b}(x))_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}\mid a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2},

where
n=2^{m}.

Theorem 3.4
.

The code
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{f}
defined above has parameters

(n=2^{m},K=2^{m+1},\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})=\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}-\frac{r-1}{4}2^{k+1}).

Proof.

It is obvious that
n=2^{m}
and
K\leq\sharp\{(a,b):a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\}=2^{m+1}.

Now we compute
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})
by Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.3
.
For codewords
c(a,b)
and
c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),\ c(a,b)\neq c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f_{a,b}(x)+f_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}(x)}-\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f_{a,b}(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(2^{m}-\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x+a)+f(x+a^{\prime})+b+b^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x+a^{\prime})+b^{\prime}}-\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x+a)+b}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(2^{m}-(-1)^{b+b^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x+a)+f(x+a^{\prime})}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(((-1)^{b^{\prime}}-(-1)^{b})\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{f(x)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(2^{m}-(-1)^{b+b^{\prime}}2^{m}\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(((-1)^{b^{\prime}}-(-1)^{b})(-\varepsilon 2^{k})\right)

where
\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}=1
if
a=a^{\prime}
and
\delta_{a,a^{\prime}}=0
otherwise. Therefore for

(a,b)\neq(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}+\frac{r-1}{4}((-1)^{b}-(-1)^{b^{\prime}})\varepsilon 2^{k},&\mbox{if}\ a\neq a^{\prime},\\
\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m+1}+\frac{r-1}{4}(-1)^{b}\varepsilon 2^{k+1},&\mbox{if}\scriptstyle a=a^{\prime}\mbox{and}\atop\scriptstyle b+b^{\prime}=1.\end{array}\right.

By Theorem
3.1
(3), the coefficient of
\frac{r-1}{4}
in the right-hand side should not be positive.
Then we can see that the minimal value of the right-hand side is

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})=\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}-\frac{r-1}{4}2^{k+1},

and
d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=\delta_{1}({\mathcal{C}})=2^{m-1},\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})-d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=\frac{r-1}{4}(2^{m}-2^{k+1})>0
for
r>1.

Moreover, for
(a,b)\neq(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),d_{H}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))\geq 2^{m-1}>0.
We get
K=|{\mathcal{C}}|=2^{m+1}.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

The parameters of
{\mathcal{C}}
is
(n=2^{m},K=2^{m+1},d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=2^{m-1}).
For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},
let

{\mathcal{C}}^{(\alpha)}=\{(c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n-1})|(\alpha,c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n-1})\in{\mathcal{C}}\}.

If
c(a,b)=(c_{0},c_{1},\cdots,c_{n-1}),
then
c(a,b+1)=(c_{0}+1,c_{1}+1,\cdots,c_{n-1}+1).
Therefore
|{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}|=|{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}|=2^{m}.

Namely, the parameters of
{\mathcal{C}}^{(\alpha)}
is
(n=2^{m}-1,K=2^{m},d_{H}=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=2^{m-1})
and

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{(\alpha)})=\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})
for
\alpha=0
and
1.
By
\frac{2d_{H}}{d_{H}-n}=2^{m}=K,

we know that both of
{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
reaches the Plotkin bound for
d_{H}.
By Theorem
2.4
(1),

{\mathcal{C}}^{(0)}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{(1)}
reaches the Plotkin bound for
\delta_{r}
if and only if

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}-\frac{r-1}{4}2^{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{(\alpha)})>\frac{r+1}{2}(d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{(\alpha)})-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{2}(2^{m-1}-1),

which means that
r<\frac{2^{k}+1}{2^{k}-1}.

Construction B

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
be a bent function,
m=2k\neq 4,D_{f}=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}|f(x)=1\}

be the support of
f,n=|D_{f}|=2^{m-1}+\varepsilon 2^{k-1},\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}.

For
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},
we get
c(a,b)=(ax+b)_{x\in D_{f}}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2}.
Then

{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}(f)=\{c(a,b)\mid a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2}

is a linear code and
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}})=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})
for all
r\geq 1
(Lemma
2.3
(2)).
The following result shows that if we omit the zero codewords
c(0,0)=0
from
{\mathcal{C}},
then for
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{C}}\backslash\{0\},

the value of
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
can be larger than
d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
when
r>1.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
be a bent function,
m=2k\geq 4,|D_{f}|=2^{m-1}+\varepsilon 2^{k-1},\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}

and

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(f)

=\{c(a,b)=(a\cdot x+b)_{x\in D_{f}}|a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},(a,b)\neq(0,0)\}.

Then the parameters of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is

(n=|D_{f}|,K=2^{m+1}-1,\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\frac{r+1}{4}(n-2^{k-1})-\frac{r-1}{4}(2^{m-1}-2^{k})),

d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(n-2^{k-1})
and for
r=1,\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})>d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a\cdot x+b+a^{\prime}\cdot x+b^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a^{\prime}\cdot x+b^{\prime}}-\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a\cdot x+b}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-(-1)^{b+b^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{(a+a^{\prime})x}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{4}\left((-1)^{b^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a^{\prime}\cdot x}-(-1)^{b}\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a\cdot x}\right).

For any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in D_{f}}(-1)^{a\cdot x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}\left(1-(-1)^{f(x)}\right)(-1)^{a\cdot x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}}(-1)^{a\cdot x}-W_{f}(a)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(2^{m}\delta_{a}-W_{f}(a)\right),

where
\delta_{a}=1
for
a=0
and
\delta_{a}=0
otherwise. Therefore

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n-(-1)^{b+b^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}(2^{m}\delta_{a+a^{\prime}}-W_{f}(a+a^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{b^{\prime}}(2^{m}\delta_{a^{\prime}}-W_{f}(a^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{r-1}{4}\left(-\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{b}(2^{m}\delta_{a}-W_{f}(a))\right).

If
a\neq a^{\prime},
then

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(n+(-1)^{b+b^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}W_{f}(a+a^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{b^{\prime}}(2^{m}\delta_{a^{\prime}}-W_{f}(a^{\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{b}(2^{m}\delta_{a}-W_{f}(a))\right).

The minimum value of the right-hand side is

A=\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m-1}+\varepsilon 2^{k-1}-2^{k-1})+\frac{r-1}{4}(-2^{m-1}-2^{k}).

If
a=a^{\prime},
then
b\neq b^{\prime},
namely
(b,b^{\prime})=(0,1)
or
(1,0).

Then by assumption
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\neq(0,0),
we have
a=a^{\prime}\neq 0.
In this case, by (
3.2
)

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(n+|D_{f}|)+\frac{r-1}{4}(2\cdot(-1)^{b^{\prime}}W_{f}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}+\varepsilon 2^{k})+\frac{r-1}{4}(2\cdot(-1)^{b^{\prime}}W_{f}(a)).

The minimal value of the right-hand side is

B=\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}+\varepsilon 2^{k})-\frac{r-1}{4}2^{k+1}.

Since

B-A=\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m-1}+(\varepsilon+1)2^{k-1})+\frac{r-1}{4}(2^{m-1}-2^{k})>0.

We get

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=A=\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m-1}+(\varepsilon-1)2^{k-1})-\frac{r-1}{4}(2^{m-1}+2^{k}).

Moreover,

d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\delta_{1}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(2^{m-1}+(\varepsilon-1)2^{k-1})=\frac{1}{2}(n-2^{k-1})

and
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})-d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\frac{r-1}{4}(\varepsilon+1)2^{k-1}.

Namely, if
r>1
and
\varepsilon=1,
then
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})>d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}).

For
(a,b)\neq(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),

\displaystyle d_{H}(c(a,b),c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}(2^{m-1}+(\varepsilon-1)2^{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2^{m-1}-2^{k})>0

which implies that
c(a,b)\neq c(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}).
Therefore
K=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}|=2^{m+1}-1.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Construction C

Let
m=2k\geq 4.
A subset
S
of
{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
is called bent set if for any distinct boolean functions

f
and
g
in
S,

f+g
is bent. It is proved in
[
2
]
that the maximal size
\mid S\mid

of a bent set
S
in
{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
is
2^{m-1}.
Such maximal bent set in
{\mathbb{B}}_{m}
has been presented in
[
3
]

for all even
m\geq 4
as following.

We view
{\mathbb{F}}^{m-1}_{2}
as the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}},
then
{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}
can be viewed as

{\mathbb{F}}^{m}_{2}={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
with inner product

((x,x_{m}),(y,y_{m}))={\rm Tr}(xy)+x_{m}y_{m}\ \ x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}},x_{m},y_{m}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2},

where
{\rm Tr}:{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is the trace mapping
{\rm Tr}(x)=\sum^{m-2}_{\lambda=0}x^{2^{\lambda}}.

Consider the following (quadratic) boolean function in
{\mathbb{B}}_{m},

f(x,x_{m}):{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2},

f(x,x_{m})={\rm Tr}(\sum^{k}_{j=1}x^{2^{j}+1})+x_{m}{\rm Tr}(x)\ \ (k=\frac{m}{2}).

For all
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}},
let
f_{u}(x,x_{m})=f(ux,x_{m}).
It is known in
[
3
]
that

K_{m}=\{f_{u}(x,x_{m})|u\in F_{2^{m-1}}\},|K_{m}|=2^{m-1}

is a bent set in
{\mathbb{B}}_{m},
called Kerdock bent set since it is closely related to the Kerdock codes.
From
f_{0}(x,x_{m})\equiv 0,
we know that for each
u\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ast}_{2^{m-1}},f_{u}(x,x_{m})=f_{u}(x,x_{m})+f_{0}(x,x_{m})

is also bent.

Let
L_{m}
be the set of linear functions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.
Namely,

L_{m}=\{l_{a,a_{m}}(x,x_{m})={\rm Tr}(ax)+a_{m}x_{m}|(a,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\},

|L_{m}|=2^{m}.

We get new larger subset of
{\mathbb{B}}_{m},

S_{m}=\{f_{u}(x,x_{m})+l_{a,a_{m}}(x,x_{m})|(u,a,a_{m})\in F^{2}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\},

|S_{m}|=2^{2m-1}.
Then we get the following binary codes with length
n=2^{m},

{\mathcal{C}}_{m}=\{c(u,a,a_{m})=(f_{u}(x,x_{m})+l_{a,a_{m}}(x,x_{m}))\},

where
(u,a,a_{m})
run through
F^{2}_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.

It is easy to see that

c(u+u^{\prime},a+a^{\prime},a_{m}+a_{m}^{\prime})=c(u,a,a_{m})+c(u^{\prime},a^{\prime},a_{m}^{\prime}).

Therefore
{\mathcal{C}}_{m}
is a linear code and
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}_{m})=d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}_{m}).
As construction B, we take

{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m}={\mathcal{C}}_{m}\backslash\{(0,0,0)\},
the following computation shows that
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m})

is bigger than
d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m})
if
r>1.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
m=2k\geq 4,n=2^{m}
and

{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m}=\{c(u,a,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}_{2}|(0,0,0)\neq(u,a,a_{m})\in F_{2^{m-1}}\times F_{2^{m-1}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\}.

Then the parameters of binary code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m}
is

(n=2^{m},K=|{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{m}|=2^{2m-1}-1,\delta_{r}),
where

\delta_{r}=\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast})\geq(r+1)2^{m-2}-(3r-1)2^{k-2}.
Particularly,

d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast})=\delta_{1}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast})=2^{m-1}-2^{k}
and

\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast})-d_{H}({\mathcal{C}}^{\ast})=(r-1)2^{k-1}(2^{k-1}-3)>0
when
k\geq 3
and
r>1.

Proof.

For
c=c(u,a,a_{m}),c^{\prime}=c(u^{\prime},a^{\prime},a_{m}^{\prime})
and

(u,a,a_{m})\neq(u^{\prime},a^{\prime},a_{m}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{(x,x_{m})}(-1)^{f_{u+u^{\prime}}(x,x_{m})+{\rm Tr}((a+a^{\prime})x)+(a_{m}+a^{\prime}_{m})x_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{(x,x_{m})}(-1)^{f_{u^{\prime}}(x,x_{m})+{\rm Tr}(a^{\prime}x)+a^{\prime}_{m}x_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{r-1}{4}\left(\sum\limits_{(x,x_{m})}(-1)^{f_{u}(x,x_{m})+{\rm Tr}(ax)+a_{m}x_{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}-W_{f_{u+u^{\prime}}}(a+a^{\prime},a_{m}+a^{\prime}_{m}))

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}(W_{f_{u^{\prime}}}(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m})-W_{f_{u}}(a,a_{m})).

(1) If
u,u^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ast}_{2^{m-1}}
and
u\neq u^{\prime},
then
f_{u},f_{u^{\prime}}

and
f_{u+u^{\prime}}
are bent. We get

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}-2^{k})-\frac{r-1}{4}2\cdot 2^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r+1)2^{m-2}-(3r-1)2^{k-2}=A.

(2) If
u=u^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ast}_{2^{m-1}},

then
(a,a_{m})\neq(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m}),f_{u+u^{\prime}}\equiv 0
and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(x,x_{m})}(-1)^{f_{u+u^{\prime}}(x,x_{m})+{\rm Tr}((a+a^{\prime})x)+(a_{m}+a^{\prime}_{m})x_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m-1}}}(-1)^{{\rm Tr}((a+a^{\prime})x)}\sum\limits_{x_{m}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}(-1)^{(a_{m}+a_{m}^{\prime})x_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

We get
\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})\geq\frac{r+1}{4}2^{m}-\frac{2r-2}{4}2^{k}\geq A.

(3) If
u=0
and
u^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ast}_{2^{m-1}},
then
(a,a_{m})\neq(0,0)
and

\displaystyle\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}-W_{f_{u^{\prime}}}(a+a^{\prime},a_{m}+a^{\prime}_{m}))

\displaystyle+\frac{r-1}{4}(W_{f_{u^{\prime}}}(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m})-0)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}-2^{k})-\frac{r-1}{4}2^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r+1)2^{m-2}-r2^{k-1}>A.

Similarly, if
u^{\prime}=0
and
u\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\ast}_{2^{m-1}},
we also have

\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})\geq(r+1)2^{m-2}-r2^{k-1}>A.

(4) At last,
u=u^{\prime}=0.

Then
(a,a_{m})\neq(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m}),(a,a_{m})\neq(0,0),(a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m})\neq(0,0).

We get

\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})=\frac{r+1}{4}(2^{m}-0)+\frac{r-1}{4}(0-0)=(r+1)2^{m-2}>A.

Therefore
\delta_{r}({\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{m})\geq A=(r+1)2^{m-2}-(3r-1)2^{k-2}.
Moreover, for

(u,a,a_{m})\neq(u^{\prime},a^{\prime},a^{\prime}_{m}),

\delta_{r}(c,c^{\prime})\geq A=(r+1)2^{k-1}(2^{k-1}-1)-(r-3)2^{k-2}>0,

which means that the codewords
c=c(u,a,a_{m})
and

are distinct. Therefore the size of

is

4.
Conclusion

Combinatorial neural codes are binary codes with asymmetric matched metric
\delta_{r}

in stead of usual Hamming distance
d_{H}.
The Hamming, Singleton and Plotkin bounds for

\delta_{r}
are presented in
[
6
]
in order to judge the goodness of
such codes using the theoretic neuroscience. In this paper we show that a binary code reaches the
Hamming, Singleton or Plotkin bound for
\delta_{r}
if and only if the code reaches
the corresponding bound for
d_{H}
and
r
sufficiently closed to 1.

Since the parameters
(n,K,d_{H})
of binary codes reaching one of above three bounds
for
d_{H}
are very limited, it is meaningful to present more combinatorial neural codes with
nice and flexible parameters
(n,K,\delta_{r}).
In this paper we consider the methods to
construct binary codes from boolean functions given by Ding
[
11
]
, present three
constructions by bent functions. Many binary codes constructed
by other types of boolean functions with respect to Hamming distance
d_{H}

have been done in past years (see survey paper
[
16
]
). It is hoped that the asymmetric
discrepacy
\delta_{r}
can be determined or estimated for such codes
(omitted the zero codeword for linear code case by Theorem ). Moreover, other kinds of binary
codes can also be considered to have good and flexible parameters
(n,K,\delta_{r}).

For example, we will show that binary irreducible cyclic codes are one of nice candidates
of combinatorial neural codes in sequential paper. The value of
\delta_{r}
for such codes
can be computed by Gauss sums over the finite field extension over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}.

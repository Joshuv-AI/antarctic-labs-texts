Identifying Young diagrams among residue multisets
Salim Rostam

Abstract
To any Young diagram we can associate the multiset of residues of all its nodes. This paper is concerned with the inverse problem: given a multiset of elements of ℤ / e ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z} , does it comes from a Young diagram? We give a full solution in level one and a partial answer in higher levels for Young multidiagrams, using Fayers’s notions of core block and weight of a multipartition. We apply the result in level one to study a shift operation on partitions.

Introduction

Studying the representation theory of a finite group
G
over a field
k
of characteristic zero reduces to find the irreducible representations of
G
. If
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the symmetric group on
n
letters, the irreducible representations are indexed by the
partitions
of
n
, that is, non-increasing sequences of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{h}>0)
with sum
\lvert\lambda\rvert=n
. If the field
k
is of positive characteristic
p
, some representations may not be written as a direct sum of irreducible ones. Hence, we are also interested in the
blocks
of the group algebra, that is, indecomposable two-sided ideals. These blocks are parametrised by the
p
-cores
of the partitions of
n
, in particular, any block is uniquely determined by its
p
-core and its
p
-weight
. Note that the set of irreducible representations can be parametrised by the
p
-regular
partitions of
n
.

More generally, we can replace the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
by a
complex reflection group
. The set of
irreducible
complex reflection groups consists of an infinite family
\{G(r,p,n)\}_{r,p,n}
where
r,p,n
are positive integers with
p\mid r
, and also a finite number of exceptions (see
[
ShTo
]
). The complex reflection group
G(r,1,n)
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and can be seen as the set of
n\times n
monomial matrices with non-zero entries in the set of complex
r
-roots of unity, while
G(r,p,n)
is a certain subgroup of
G(r,1,n)
of index
p
. We can then study the representation theory of a
Hecke algebra

\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
of
G(r,1,n)
, where
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=(\mathsf{s}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{s}_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is a multicharge (
[
ArKo
,
BrMa
,
BMR
]
). The algebra
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
is a particular deformation of the group algebra of
G(r,1,n)
and
q\in k
is the deformation parameter. Assume that
q\in k\setminus\{0\}
and let
e\geq 0
be its multiplicative order (with
e\coloneqq 0
if
q
is of infinite order). The representation theories of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
and
G(r,1,n)
are deeply linked. For instance, if
r=1
then
G(1,1,n)\simeq\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and the situation is the following: if
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
is semisimple then its irreducible representations are indexed by the partitions of
n
, otherwise they are parametrised by the
e
-regular partitions of
n
and the blocks are parametrised by the
e
-cores of partitions of
n
.
In the general case
r\geq 1
, if
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
is semisimple then its irreducible representations are indexed by the
r
-partitions
of
n
, that is,
r
-tuples
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)}\bigr)
with
\lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rvert=\lvert\lambda^{(1)}\rvert+\dots+\lvert\lambda^{(r)}\rvert=n
. On the contrary, if
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
is non-semisimple then the situation is more complex. First, its irreducible representations can be indexed by a non-trivial generalisation of
e
-regular partitions, known as
Kleshchev

r
-partitions (see
[
Ar01
,
ArMa
]
). Similarly, the naive generalisation of
e
-cores to
r
-partitions, the
e
-multicores
, do not parametrise in general the blocks of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
. Namely, Lyle and Mathas
[
LyMa
]
proved that the blocks of
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(q)
are parametrised by the multisets of
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-residues modulo
e
of the
r
-partitions of
n
. To make things more explicit, let
Q=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
be a free abelian group. If
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a multipartition, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
c^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
the number of
i
-nodes of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. Then the block
corresponding
to
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is

\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\coloneqq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\alpha_{i}\in Q,

and we say that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}

lies
in
\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
.
During their proof, Lyle and Mathas used a generalisation of the
e
-weight to
r
-partitions, introduced by Fayers
[
Fa06
]
. If
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)}\bigr)
is an
r
-partition, its
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight
is given by

\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}c^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}_{\mathsf{s}_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\left(c^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})-c^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}_{i+1}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\right)^{2}.

In particular, two
r
-partitions that lie in the same block (
i.e.

\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
) have the same
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight.
Fayers proved that if
r=1
then this definition of
\mathsf{s}
-weight coincides with the usual notion of
e
-weight for partitions. For instance, if the partition
\lambda
is an
e
-core we have:

c_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\left(c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

(in level
1
we may omit to write the multicharge, assuming, without loss of generality, that
\mathsf{s}=\mathsf{0}
). Moreover, while the fact that the
e
-weight of a partition is non-negative follows from the definition, this is a non-trivial statement for the
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight in higher levels (see
[
Fa06
]
).

In level one, a partition is an
e
-core if and only if its
e
-weight is zero. In higher levels, we still have an implication: if an
r
-partition has
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight at most
r-1
then it is an
e
-multicore. However, we can find
e
-multicores of arbitrary large
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight. Moreover, we can have two
r
-partitions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}
lying in the same block, thus having the same
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight, but with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
(respectively
\boldsymbol{\mu}
) being (resp. not being) an
e
-multicore. In order to obtain a more satisfying notion of core of a multipartition, Fayers
[
Fa07a
]
introduced the notion of
core block
and
reduced
e
-multicore
(the latter expression is due to
[
LyMa
]
): an
e
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced
if any multipartition
\boldsymbol{\mu}
such that
\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
is an
e
-multicore, in which case the block associated with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a
core block
. If
r=1
then every
e
-core is
\mathsf{s}
-reduced and every block is a core block. Now if
r>1
, to any multipartition we can still associate a unique core block, however there is no canonical choice for a reduced
e
-multicore inside this core block. Note that Jacon–Lecouvey
[
JaLe
]
managed to define what they have called the
(e,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}})
-core
of a multipartition (they also use the notion of
reduced
(e,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}})
-core
, which is different from the notion of reduced
e
-multicore that we use here). The
(e,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}})
-core of an
r
-partition is again an
r
-partition, and the situation is then entirely similar to the level one case, namely, for the combinatorics of blocks. However, the
(e,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}})
-core of a multipartition is associated with a possibly different multicharge, which depends on the multipartition.

Now let
p\mid r
. We can use Clifford theory to study the representation theory of
G(r,p,n)
, and this involves the following natural
shift
operation of order
p
on
r
-partitions:

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)}\bigr)\longmapsto\prescript{\sigma}{}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\coloneqq\bigl(\lambda^{(r-d+1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)},\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r-d)}\bigr),

where
d\coloneqq\frac{r}{p}
. Typically, if
S^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is an irreducible
G(r,1,n)
-module then the number of irreducible constituents of
S^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
seen as an
G(r,p,n)
-module will only depend on the cardinality of the orbit of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
under the shift operation (see, for instance,
[
Ar95
,
ChJa
,
GeJa
,
HuMa
]
).
Now take
e\geq 2
and assume that
p
also divides
e
. There is a map
\sigma:Q\to Q
of order
p
such that if
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is a multicharge satisfying some compatibility conditions, we have the relation

\alpha^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}\bigl(\prescript{\sigma}{}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=\sigma\cdot\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}).

(1.1)

In
[
Ro19a
]
, the author proved that if
r\geq 2
then a block
\alpha\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
that is
stuttering
, that is
\sigma\cdot\alpha=\alpha
, always corresponds to a
stuttering
multipartition, that is, a multipartition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
satisfying
\prescript{\sigma}{}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

The aim of this paper is to study the natural generalisation of Fayers’s weight function to
Q=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
, and then apply this result in level one to define a shift operation on partitions so that a relation such as (
1.1
) holds. More precisely, let
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a multicharge and for any
\alpha=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q
define its
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-weight by:

\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{\mathsf{s}_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\left(c_{i}-c_{i+1}\right)^{2}\in\mathbb{Z},

so that
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}\bigl(\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\bigr)=\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
for any
r
-partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. We also define the set

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\coloneqq\bigl\{\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):\boldsymbol{\lambda}\text{ is an }r\text{-partition}\bigr\}\subseteq Q.

Since
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\geq 0
for any
r
-partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, we have an inclusion
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\subseteq\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq 0\}
. The paper is mainly concerned in studying a reverse inclusion. More precisely, we prove the following results.

Theorem A

(Corollaries
3.9
and
3.27
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
e>0
. We have

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\supseteq\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)>N^{\prime}_{r,e}-r\bigr\},

where
N^{\prime}_{r,e}\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor
.

Theorem B

(Propositions
2.40
and
3.11
)
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
e\geq 0
. Assume that
r=1
or
(r,e)\in\bigl\{(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\bigr\}
. Then

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq 0\bigr\}.

In particular, given a multiset of elements of
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
, we can easily determine whether it comes from a Young diagram by computing its weight.

Note that Theorem
A
does not hold when
e=0
, whereas Theorem
B
does (when
r=1
). If
e>0
, a set of the form
\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq C\bigr\}
for
C\geq 0
is never empty (and even always infinite), so that the inclusion of Theorem
A
is not trivially true. The main idea of the proof of Theorem
A
is to prove that the weight function is bounded above by a constant
N_{r,e}
on the set of
reduced

e
-multicores. Note that this assertion is wrong if we only consider
e
-multicores, which can have arbitrarily large weight. With some calculations involving binary matrices, we then give a sharp estimation of the bound
N_{r,e}
. Interestingly, as it is mentioned in the statement, Theorem
B
gives a simple criterion to determine whether an element of
Q
, in particular, a multiset of residues, actually comes from a partition. To the author’s knowledge, such a result was not already known and is a non-trivial generalisation of an old result of Robinson–Thrall
[
RoTh
]
from
e=0
to
e\geq 0
. We will also give a procedure to compute all the corresponding partitions.

Now assume that
r=1
, take
p
dividing
e
and let
\lambda
be a partition. Shifting the components of the
e
-quotient
of
\lambda
, we define another partition
\sigma\lambda
. Using Theorem
B
, we prove the following analogue of (
1.1
) in level one.

Theorem C

(Corollary
4.10
)
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. With
e^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{e}{p}
, we have

\alpha(\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)\iff\lvert\sigma\lambda\rvert=\lvert\lambda\rvert\iff c_{0}(\lambda)=c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda).

In particular, if
\lambda
is a partition of
n
then
\sigma\lambda
is a partition of
m
with possibly
m\neq n
. Finally, we complete the study of stuttering blocks initiated in
[
Ro19b
]
, by giving its analogue in level one. It turns out that a similar equivalence holds, now with an additional condition on the weight of the block.

Theorem D

(Lemma
4.15
and Corollary
4.17
)
.

Assume that
r=1
and let
\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
. The block
\alpha
corresponds to a partition
\lambda
satisfying
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
if and only if
\sigma\cdot\alpha=\alpha
and

.

Background on core blocks

We recall here the combinatorics of blocks of Ariki–Koike algebras. We will write
\mathbb{N}
for
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
e\in\mathbb{N}
. If
e>0
we identify
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
and
\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, and if
e=0
then
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
.

2.1
Partitions

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\lambda
be a
partition
of
n
, that is, a non-increasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{h}>0)
with sum
n
. We will write
\lvert\lambda\rvert\coloneqq n
and
h(\lambda)\coloneqq h
. We denote by
\emptyset
the empty partition, which satisfies
\lvert\emptyset\rvert=h(\emptyset)=0
. The Young diagram associated with
\lambda
is the region of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
given by

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\coloneqq\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:1\leq a\leq h(\lambda)\text{ and }1\leq b\leq\lambda_{a}\right\}.

An element of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
is a
node of
\lambda
. More generally, we will call
node
any element of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
. A node
\gamma\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
is
addable
(respectively,
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
is
removable
) if
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\cup\{\gamma\}
(resp.
\mathcal{Y}\setminus\{\gamma\}
) is the Young diagram of a partition.
A
rim hook
of
\lambda
is a subset of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
of the following form:

r^{\lambda}_{(a,b)}\coloneqq\left\{(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda):a^{\prime}\geq a,\,b^{\prime}\geq b\text{ and }(a^{\prime}+1,b^{\prime}+1)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\right\},

where
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
. We say that
r^{\lambda}_{(a,b)}
is an
l
-rim hook
if it has cardinality
l
. Note that
1
-rim hooks are exactly removable nodes.
The set
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\setminus r^{\lambda}_{(a,b)}
is the Young diagram of a certain partition
\mu
, obtained by
unwrapping
or
removing
the rim hook
r^{\lambda}_{(a,b)}
from
\lambda
.
Conversely, we say that
\lambda
is obtained from
\mu
by
wrapping on
or
adding
the rim hook
r^{\lambda}_{(a,b)}
. We say that
\lambda
is an
e
-core
if
\lambda
has no
e
-rim hook. Note that if
e=0
then every partition is an
e
-core, and if
e=1
then the empty partition
\emptyset
is the only
e
-core. In particular, the combinatorics of
1
-cores is very easy, and all the future statements in the paper can easily be proven for
e=1
.

Let

Lemma 2.33
.

If
\lambda
and

are two

-cores with

.

Proof.

The statement is clear if

and

and

2.4

Let

An element of

-rim hook of

for some

be a

. The

-residue

Lemma 2.52
.

Assume that
e>0
. Let
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{e})\in\mathbb{Z}^{e}
such that
z_{1}+\dots+z_{e}=0
. Then
\lVert z\rVert^{2}\geq 2(z_{1}+\dots+z_{\mathsf{s}})
for any
\mathsf{s}\in\{1,\dots,e\}
.

Proof.

First, note that for any integer
t\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
t^{2}\geq 2t
unless if
t=1
. We define the following two sets:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,\mathsf{s}\}:z_{i}=1\right\},

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,e\}:z_{i}\leq 0\right\},

in particular
I\cap J=\emptyset
.
We have

0=z_{1}+\dots+z_{e}\geq\sum_{i\in J}z_{i}+\sum_{i\in I}z_{i},

so that
\lvert I\rvert+\sum_{i\in J}z_{i}\leq 0
. For any
i\in J
we have
z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
thus

z_{i}^{2}-2z_{i}\geq z_{i}^{2}\geq-z_{i},

hence we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert z\rVert^{2}-2(z_{1}+\dots+z_{\mathsf{s}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in I}(z_{i}^{2}-2z_{i})+\sum_{i\in J}(-z_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}(-1)-\sum_{i\in J}z_{i}

\displaystyle=-\lvert I\rvert-\sum_{i\in J}z_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

as desired.
∎

-cores with arbitrary large number of
0
-nodes (see, for instance, Example
2.44
). If
e=0
one can simply consider the partition
\lambda^{[h]}\coloneqq(h,\dots,h)
where
h\geq 1
is repeated
h
times, and see that
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\lambda^{[h]},\emptyset)=\mathsf{w}^{0}(\lambda^{[h]})+c^{0}_{0}(\lambda^{[h]})=0+h=h
.

2.5
Blocks for multipartitions

The combinatorics between an
r
-partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and
\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
is more intricate than in the case
r=1
. For instance, the natural notion of
e
-multicore no more suffices to distinguish the blocks between two
r
-partitions. However, as in the level
1
case the quantities
\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
determine the blocks of the associated
Ariki–Koike algebra
(see
[
LyMa
]
).

Example 2.57
.

Let
r=2
,
e=2
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=(0,1)
. The bipartitions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl((1),(1)\bigr)
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\bigl((2),\emptyset\bigr)
lie in the same block
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}
, however
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a bicore whereas
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is not.

Again, we define

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\coloneqq\left\{\alpha^{\mathsf{s}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):\boldsymbol{\lambda}\text{ is an }r\text{-partition of }n\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Remark 2.58
.

As we mentioned in §
2.1
, the case
e=1
is particularly easy to deal with, and indeed we have here
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=\mathbb{N}\alpha_{0}=Q_{+}
.

Since
\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\alpha^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}(\lambda^{(j)})
we have
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}Q^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}
, however the sum is not direct. In particular, using the
1{:}1
-parametrisation of §
2.3
for each
Q^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}
, we can give a naive parametrisation of
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
but this parametrisation will not be
1{:}1
. We will rather aim at a generalisation of Lemma
2.39
and Proposition
2.40
, looking for an implicit description of
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
. It turns out that the following simple result, which comes from Lemmas
2.8
and
2.20
, is the key of what follows.

Proposition 2.59
.

Let
\alpha\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
and
h\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\alpha+h\delta\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
.

We have the following (weak) generalisation of Corollary
2.43
(see also
[
Fa07b
, Theorem 4.7]
).

Proposition 2.60
.

Assume
e>0
. For any
\alpha\in Q
and any
h\geq-\max\bigl\{\mathsf{w}^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}(\alpha):j\in\{1,\dots,r\}\bigr\}
we have

\alpha+h\delta\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}.

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. If
h\geq-\mathsf{w}^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}(\alpha)
then by Corollary
2.43
we have
\alpha+h\delta\in Q^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}
. We conclude the proof since
Q^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}\subseteq Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}Q^{\mathsf{s}_{k}}
.
∎

As we saw in Example
2.57
, the raw notion of
e
-multicores is not adapted to the study of blocks.

Definition 2.61

(
[

]
)

Let

if

;

•

By Lemmas

and

, a block

In particular, if

If

The notion of core block is related to the notion of

Definition 2.65

(

Lemma 2.66
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)}\bigr)
be an
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced multicore. Then for each
1\leq j<k\leq r
, the bipartition
\bigl(\lambda^{(j)},\lambda^{(k)}\bigr)
is an
(\mathsf{s}_{j},\mathsf{s}_{k})
-reduced bicore.

Proof.

We prove the contraposition. Assume that
\bigl(\lambda^{(j)},\lambda^{(k)}\bigr)
is not an
(\mathsf{s}_{j},\mathsf{s}_{k})
-reduced bicore. Then we can find a bipartition
\bigl(\mu^{(j)},\mu^{(k)}\bigr)
in its block which is not a bicore, in particular we can remove an
e
-rim hook to
\mu^{(j)}
or
\mu^{(k)}
. But then the multipartition

\bigl(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(j-1)},\mu^{(j)},\lambda^{(j+1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k-1)},\mu^{(k)},\lambda^{(k+1)},\dots,\lambda^{(r)}\bigr)

lie in the same block as
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and is not an

-multicore, thus
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is not a reduced
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-multicore.
∎

Lemma 2.67
.

Assume that
e>0
and let
\alpha\in Q
. There is a unique integer
h\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha-h\delta
is a core block and we have
h=\max\bigl\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\alpha-k\delta\in Q^{\mathsf{s}}\bigr\}
. Moreover, we have
\alpha\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\iff h\geq 0
.

Proof.

Write
H\coloneqq\bigl\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:\alpha-k\delta\in Q^{\mathsf{s}}\bigr\}
. The set
H
is non-empty by Proposition
2.60
and bounded above by (
2.48
) and Proposition
2.51
. If
h\coloneqq\max H
, then by definition we have
\alpha-h\delta\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
and
\alpha-(h+1)\delta\notin Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
thus
\alpha-h\delta
is a core block. We now prove the unicity. If
h^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
is such that
\alpha-h^{\prime}\delta
is a core block then in particular
\alpha-h^{\prime}\delta\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
thus
h^{\prime}\leq h
by maximality of
h
. Now if
h^{\prime}<h
then

\alpha-(h^{\prime}+1)\delta=(\alpha-h\delta)+(h-h^{\prime}-1)\delta,

but the left-hand side is not in
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
since
\alpha-h^{\prime}\delta
is a core block while the right-hand side is in
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
by Proposition
2.59
, thus
h^{\prime}=h
. Finally, we have
\alpha\in Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\iff 0\in H\iff h\geq 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Let

is a core block.

We say that a multicharge

Proposition 2.73

(
[

, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Assume that
e>0
and let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
be an
e
-multicore. The following assertions are equivalent.

1.

The
e
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced.

2.

There exist a compatible multicharge
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}
and integers

\mathfrak{b}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\in\{\mathfrak{b}_{i},\mathfrak{b}_{i}+e\},

for all

and

.

3.

{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}
and

{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\gamma_{i,jk}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{k}+1,

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
and
j,k\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

Corollary 2.74
.

Assume that
e>0
. An
e
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced if and only if there exists a compatible multicharge
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}
such that

\gamma^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}}_{i,jk}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq 1,

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
and
j,k\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

Proof.

If the
e
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced then by Proposition
2.73
2
we can write
\mathfrak{b}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}^{\prime}}_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}+\epsilon_{ij}e
for all

and

, where

and

for all

.

is

3
Implicit description of the blocks

We will give in this section the central results of the paper. We will first give a formula to compute the weight as a map from binary matrices. Let
r\geq 2
and
e\geq 1
.

3.1
Weight of a tuple of subsets

We first begin with the following standard fact.
To an
r
-tuple of subsets
(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
of
\{1,\dots,e\}
we can associate the
e\times r
binary matrix
\mathcal{E}
such that the characteristic vector
\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}
of
E_{j}
is the
j
-th column of
\mathcal{E}
for any
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. Conversely, to an
e\times r
binary matrix
\mathcal{E}
we can associate the
r
-tuple
(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
of subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
such that for any
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
the
j
-th column of
\mathcal{E}
is exactly
\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}
.

Now recall from Proposition
2.73
2
that to any
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced
e
-multicore we can associate an
e\times r
binary matrix. Conversely, given an
e\times r
binary matrix
\mathcal{E}=(\epsilon_{ij})
and integers
\mathfrak{b}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{b}_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathfrak{b}_{i}=i\pmod{e}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,e\}
, we can construct an
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced
e
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}(\mathcal{E},(\mathfrak{b}_{i}))
, where the multicharge
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
is determined by (
2.14
). Note that the integers

\gamma_{i,jk}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\epsilon_{ij}-\epsilon_{ik},

(3.1)

do not depend on the choice of
(\mathfrak{b}_{i})
so that, recalling Proposition
2.55
, the weight
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
only depends on
\mathcal{E}
. Now if
(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
is the
r
-tuple of subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
corresponding to
\mathcal{E}
, we can thus write:

\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})\coloneqq\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}).

Example 3.2
.

Take
e=3
and
r=2
. We consider the following subsets of
\{1,2,3\}
:

E_{1}=\{1,2,3\},\quad E_{2}=\{3\},

which correspond to the
3\times 2
binary matrix
\mathcal{E}=(\epsilon_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}
. We also consider the following integers:

\mathfrak{b}_{1}=1,\quad\mathfrak{b}_{2}=5,\quad\mathfrak{b}_{3}=-3,

which satisfy the condition
\mathfrak{b}_{i}=i\pmod{e}
.
For
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and
j\in\{1,2\}
we define
\mathfrak{b}_{ij}=\mathfrak{b}_{i}+\epsilon_{ij}e
. We obtain:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{11}

\displaystyle=1+3=4,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{12}

\displaystyle=1+0=1,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{21}

\displaystyle=5+3=8,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{22}

\displaystyle=5+0=5,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{31}

\displaystyle=-3+3=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{32}

\displaystyle=-3+3=0.

Recall that
\mathfrak{b}_{ij}
is the largest element of
\beta^{\mathsf{s}_{j}}(\lambda^{(j)})
congruent to

e
. We obtain that the charged beta-number associated with
\lambda^{(1)}
is:

\displaystyle\beta^{\mathsf{s}_{1}}(\lambda^{(1)})

\displaystyle=\{4,1,-2,-5,-8,\dots\}\sqcup\{8,5,2,-1,-4,-7,\dots\}\sqcup\{0,-3,-6,-9,\dots,\}

\displaystyle=\{8,5,4,2,1,0,-1,-2,-3,-4,\dots\},

thus the associated charged abacus is:
\cdots
\cdots

,

thus

\displaystyle\beta^{\mathsf{s}_{2}}(\lambda^{(2)})

,

thus

We can deduce the associated multicharge

for

of subsets of

we have associated the
(6,4)
-reduced
3
-multicore
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\bigl((3,1,1),(2)\bigr)
, and the calculation of

follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
r=2
and let
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
. We have:

\mathsf{w}(E_{1},E_{2})=\min(\lvert E_{1}\rvert,\lvert E_{2}\rvert)-\lvert E_{1}\cap E_{2}\rvert.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{b}_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathfrak{b}_{i}=i\pmod{e}
, let
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
be the multicharge associated with
(\mathfrak{b}_{i})
and let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
be the
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced
e
-multicore associated with
(\mathfrak{b}_{i})
and pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
of subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
. By (
3.1
), for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we have

\gamma_{i,12}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\mathbf{1}_{1}(i)-\mathbf{1}_{2}(i),

so that
\gamma_{i,12}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=1
if and only if
i\in E_{1}\setminus E_{2}
. Hence, recalling (
2.54
),

\displaystyle\gamma_{il,12}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=2

\displaystyle\iff\gamma_{i,12}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=1\text{ and }\gamma_{l,12}^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=-1

\displaystyle\iff i\in E_{1}\setminus E_{2}\text{ and }l\in E_{2}\setminus E_{1},

so that

A\coloneqq\#\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}:\gamma_{il,12}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=2\text{ for some }l\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}\right\}=\begin{cases}\lvert E_{1}\setminus E_{2}\rvert,&\text{if }\lvert E_{2}\setminus E_{1}\rvert\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and similarly

B\coloneqq\#\left\{l\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}:\gamma_{il,12}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=2\text{ for some }i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}\right\}=\begin{cases}\lvert E_{2}\setminus E_{1}\rvert,&\text{if }\lvert E_{1}\setminus E_{2}\rvert\neq 0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Looking whether
0
is in
\bigl\{|E_{1}\setminus E_{2}|,|E_{2}\setminus E_{1}|\bigr\}
or not, we obtain:

\min(A,B)=\min\bigl(|E_{1}\setminus E_{2}|,|E_{2}\setminus E_{1}|\bigr).

Recalling from Proposition
2.55
that
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},E_{2})=\min(A,B)
, we thus obtain:

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(E_{1},E_{2})

\displaystyle=\min\Bigl(\lvert E_{1}\rvert-\lvert E_{1}\cap E_{2}\rvert,\>\lvert E_{2}\rvert-\lvert E_{1}\cap E_{2}\rvert\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\min(\lvert E_{1}\rvert,\lvert E_{2}\rvert)-\lvert E_{1}\cap E_{2}\rvert,

as announced.
∎

Note that if
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
then by Lemma
2.50
we have:

\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}\Bigl[\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{k}\rvert)-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{k}\rvert\Bigr].

(3.4)

We conclude this subsection by the following particular case of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
E\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
we have:

\mathsf{w}(E,E)=\mathsf{w}(E,\emptyset)=\mathsf{w}(E,\{1,\dots,e\})=0.

Remark 3.6
.

Recalling Remark

then we recover the fact that
\mathsf{w}(E)=0
for any subset

3.2

Recall from Remark

-multicores.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

and

. There exists a constant

such that for all multicharge
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
and each

\boldsymbol{\lambda}
that is an
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
-reduced

\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq C.

Note that the statement is wrong if

(see Remark

Proof.

We first prove the case
r=2
, which is clear since by Proposition
2.55
and Corollary
2.74
we have
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq e
(we could also have use Lemma
3.3
). We now assume that
r\geq 3
. Since
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is a multicore, by Lemma
2.50
we have

\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}\mathsf{w}^{(\mathsf{s}_{j},\mathsf{s}_{k})}\bigl(\lambda^{(j)},\lambda^{(k)}\bigr).

By Lemma
2.66
we know that each
\bigl(\lambda^{(j)},\lambda^{(k)}\bigr)
is a reduced
(\mathsf{s}_{j},\mathsf{s}_{k})
-bicore, thus using the case
r=2
we find

\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})\leq e\binom{r}{2},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

We denote by
N_{r,e}
the smallest possible constant
C
as in Theorem
3.7
, that is:

N_{r,e}=\mathrm{sup}\bigl\{\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}):{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\text{ a multicharge and }\boldsymbol{\lambda}\text{ an }{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\text{-reduced }e\text{-multicore}\bigr\}<\infty.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that
e>0
.
We have

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\supseteq\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)>N_{r,e}-r\bigr\}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in Q
and assume that
\alpha\notin Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
. We will prove that
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\leq N_{r,e}-r
.
By Lemma
2.67
, we can find
h\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\alpha-h\delta
is a core block, and since
\alpha\notin Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
we have
h<0
. Now by Theorem
3.7
we have
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha-h\delta)\leq N_{r,e}
thus by (
2.48
) we have
\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\leq N_{r,e}+rh\leq N_{r,e}-r
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Note that the inclusion of Corollary
3.9
is not trivial, that is, the set
\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)>N_{r,e}-r\bigr\}
is not empty, and in fact it is even infinite (by (
2.48
)).

Remark 3.10
.

The result of Corollary
3.9
does not hold if
e=0
. For instance, assume that
r=2
and take
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=(0,\mathsf{s})
for
\mathsf{s}\geq 0
. For any
h\geq 1
, as in Remark
2.56
we consider the partition
\lambda^{[h]}=(h,\dots,h)
where
h
is repeated
h
times and we define

\alpha^{[h]}\coloneqq\alpha^{0}(\lambda^{[h]})+\alpha_{h+\mathsf{s}+1}=\sum_{\lvert i\rvert<h}(h-\lvert i\rvert)\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{h+\mathsf{s}+1}.

Let
(c_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{Z}}
so that
\alpha^{[h]}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
. Since
c_{h+\mathsf{s}}=0<c_{h+\mathsf{s}+1}
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=(0,\mathsf{s})
, we have
\alpha^{[h]}\notin Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
(by (
2.25
) and (
2.28
)), however

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha^{[h]})

\displaystyle=2c_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(c_{i}-c_{i+1})^{2}

\displaystyle=2c_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=-h}^{h-1}(c_{i}-c_{i+1})^{2}+(c_{h+\mathsf{s}}-c_{h+\mathsf{s}+1})^{2}+(c_{h+\mathsf{s}+1}-c_{h+\mathsf{s}+2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2h-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=-h}^{h-1}1^{2}+1^{2}+1^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2h-\frac{1}{2}(2h+2)

\displaystyle=h-1.

Note that this calculation remains valid for
e\geq 2(h+1)+\mathsf{s}
. This proves that we can find elements in
Q\setminus Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
of arbitrarily large weight.

We will give in §
3.3
more information about the constant
N_{r,e}
. In particular, in the following particular cases the situation is the same as in level one (cf. Proposition
2.40
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Assume that
(r,e)\in\bigl\{(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\bigr\}
. Then
Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}=\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq 0\bigr\}
.

Proof.

We will see in Proposition
3.13
(respectively, Proposition
3.33
) that
N_{r,e}=1
(resp.
N_{r,e}=2
) if
r=2
and
e\in\{2,3\}
(resp.
r=3
and
e=2
). In these three cases we have
N_{r,e}\leq r-1
, thus

\left\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq 0\right\}\subseteq\left\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq N_{r,e}-r+1\right\}.

We conclude the proof by Proposition
2.51
and Corollary
3.9
since we then find

Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}\subseteq\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)\geq 0\}\subseteq\left\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}(\alpha)>N_{r,e}-r\right\}\subseteq Q^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}.

∎

3.3
Optimality

We now assume that
e\geq 2
. The aim of this subsection is to give sharp bounds for
N_{r,e}
. Note that we have seen during the proof of Theorem
3.7
that
N_{r,e}\leq\frac{er(r-1)}{2}
. In fact, we will see that this first bound is roughly the value of
N_{r,e}
multiplied by a factor of
4
(see Corollary
3.29
). We will also compute
N_{r,e}
for small values of
e
or
r
(see §
3.3.1
and §
3.3.3
). We first begin by gathering some elementary results.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
.

1.

We have:

k\binom{e-1}{k}=(e-1)\binom{e-2}{k-1},

and:

(e-k)\binom{e}{k}=e\binom{e-1}{k}.

2.

The quantity
k(e-k)
is maximal for
k=\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor
, the corresponding maximal value being
\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.

Proof.

1.

The first identity is standard. For the second one, using the identity
\binom{n}{m}=\binom{n}{n-m}
twice and the first one we have:

(e-k)\binom{e}{k}=(e-k)\binom{e}{e-k}=e\binom{e-1}{e-k-1}=e\binom{e-1}{k}.

2.

The first assertion is clear. For the second one, the result is clear if
e
is even, and if
e
is odd then
\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor=\frac{e-1}{2}
and:

\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\left(e-\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\right)=\frac{e-1}{2}\frac{e+1}{2}=\frac{e^{2}-1}{4}=\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

∎

3.3.1
Values for small parameters

We will here compute
N_{r,e}
when
r=2
or
e=2
.

Proposition 3.13

(Case
r=2
)
.

We have
N_{2,e}=\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to prove that for any
E,F\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
we have
\min(\lvert E\rvert,\lvert F\rvert)-\lvert E\cap F\rvert\leq\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor
and that equality happen for some
E,F
. Writing
E=(E\cap F)\sqcup E^{\prime}
and
F=(E\cap F)\sqcup F^{\prime}
, we have

\min(\lvert E\rvert,\lvert F\rvert)-\lvert E\cap F\rvert=\min(\lvert E^{\prime}\rvert,\lvert F^{\prime}\rvert)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor,

since
E^{\prime}\cap F^{\prime}=\emptyset
. We conclude the proof since equality holds when
\lvert E\rvert=\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor
and
F=E^{c}
.
∎

Proposition 3.14

(Case
e=2
)
.

We have
N_{r,2}=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,2\}
. By Lemma
3.5
, if
E_{j}=\emptyset
or
E_{j}=\{1,2\}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
then
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
does not decrease if we replace
E_{j}
by
\{1\}
. Hence, we can assume that for all
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
we have
E_{j}=\{1\}
or
E_{j}=\{2\}
. Let
s\in\{0,\dots,r\}
so that, after reordering, we have
E_{1}=\dots=E_{s}=\{1\}
and
E_{s+1}=\dots=E_{r}=\{2\}
. Then by Lemmas
2.50
and
3.5
we have

\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})=s(r-s)\,\mathsf{w}(\{1\},\{2\})=s(r-s),

and this concludes the proof by Lemma
3.12
2
.
∎

3.3.2
Bounds

Recall that we always assume that
e\geq 2
(recall from Remark
3.6
that
N_{r,1}=0
), with the exception of Lemma
3.15
. We will first give a lower bound for
N_{r,e}
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
r\geq 2
. The sequence
(N_{r,e})_{e\geq 1}
is superadditive, in other words:

N_{r,e+e^{\prime}}\geq N_{r,e}+N_{r,e^{\prime}},

for any
e,e^{\prime}\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
and
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e^{\prime}\}
. For any
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
we define
F^{\prime}_{j}\coloneqq\{i+e:i\in F_{j}\}\subseteq\{e+1,\dots,e+e^{\prime}\}
. Note that
E_{j}\cap F^{\prime}_{k}=\emptyset
for all
1\leq j<k\leq r
. For any
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, we define
G_{j}\coloneqq E_{j}\sqcup F^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e+e^{\prime}\}
. For any
1\leq j<k\leq r
we have

\displaystyle\min(\lvert G_{j}\rvert,\lvert G_{k}\rvert)

\displaystyle=\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert+\lvert F_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{k}\rvert+\lvert F_{k}\rvert)

\displaystyle\geq\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{k}\rvert)+\min(\lvert F_{j}\rvert,\lvert F_{k}\rvert),

and, recalling that
E_{j}\cap F^{\prime}_{k}=\emptyset
,

\displaystyle G_{j}\cap G_{k}

\displaystyle=(E_{j}\sqcup F^{\prime}_{j})\cap(E_{k}\sqcup F^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle=(E_{j}\cap E_{k})\sqcup(F^{\prime}_{j}\cap F^{\prime}_{k}),

so that
\mathsf{w}(G_{j},G_{k})\geq\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})+\mathsf{w}(F_{j},F_{k})
. By Lemma
2.50
we obtain
\mathsf{w}(G_{1},\dots,G_{r})\geq\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})+\mathsf{w}(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})
and this conclude the proof since
N_{r,e+e^{\prime}}\geq\mathsf{w}(G_{1},\dots,G_{r})
and by taking the maximum on
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}
and
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
.
∎

Corollary 3.16
.

For any
e\geq 2
we have
N_{r,e}\geq\bigl\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rfloor\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
.

Proof.

Since
N_{r,1}=0
we have

N_{r,e}\geq N_{r,2\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor}\geq\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor N_{r,2}
by Lemma
3.15
and we conclude by Proposition
3.14
.
∎

We will now give an upper bound for
N_{r,e}
. The next proposition is the key of the next results.
We write
{\mathcal{P}_{e}}\coloneqq\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,e\})
for the powerset of
\{1,\dots,e\}
, the set of subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
. We denote by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1}
the
1
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
, given by
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=\sum_{E\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}\lvert x_{E}\rvert
for all
x=(x_{E})_{E\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
we write
\langle x,y\rangle\coloneqq x^{\intercal}y
for the canonical scalar product. We have
\langle x,x\rangle=\lVert x\rVert^{2}
, where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the Euclidean norm as in Section
2
.
Let
A_{e}=(a_{E,F})_{E,F\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
be the matrix given by
a_{E,F}\coloneqq\mathsf{w}(E,F)
for all
E,F\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}
. The matrix
A_{e}
is symmetric of size
2^{e}
with all its diagonal entries being
0
and only non-negative entries. We denote by
q_{e}
the quadratic form given by
q_{e}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\langle x,A_{e}x\rangle
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
.

Proposition 3.17
.

.

1.

If
x\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
then
q_{e}(x)\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

We have:

N_{r,e}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}\\
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r\end{subarray}}q_{e}(x).

Proof.

We have a one-to-one correspondence between unordered
r
-tuples
(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
of subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
and elements
x=(x_{E})_{E\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}\in\mathbb{N}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
with
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r
given as follows:

x_{E}=\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:E_{j}=E\}.

For such elements, by Lemmas
2.50
and
3.5
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}a_{E_{j},E_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq r}a_{E_{j},E_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{E,F\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}a_{E,F}x_{E}x_{F}

\displaystyle=q_{e}(x),

which concludes the proof.
∎

We will now study the quadratic form
q_{e}
. Our first aim is to prove that it suffices to study the restrictions of
q_{e}
to the subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
corresponding to subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
of same cardinalities.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
E,F\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
. We have
\mathsf{w}(E,F)=\mathsf{w}(E^{c},F^{c})
.

Proof.

A simple calculation gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(E^{c},F^{c})

\displaystyle=\min(\lvert E^{c}\rvert,\lvert F^{c}\rvert)-\lvert E^{c}\cap F^{c}\rvert

\displaystyle=\min(e-\lvert E\rvert,e-\lvert F\rvert)-\lvert(E\cup F)^{c}\rvert

\displaystyle=e-\max(\lvert E\rvert,\lvert F\rvert)-e+\lvert E\cup F\rvert

\displaystyle=-\max(\lvert E\rvert,\lvert F\rvert)+\lvert E\rvert+\lvert F\rvert-\lvert E\cap F\rvert

\displaystyle=\min(\lvert E\rvert,\lvert F\rvert)-\lvert E\cap F\rvert

\displaystyle=\mathsf{w}(E,F).

∎

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
. Let
m\coloneqq\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert E_{j}\rvert
and assume that
m<e
. Let
j_{0}
such that
\lvert E_{j_{0}}\rvert=m
and let
x\in\{1,\dots,e\}\setminus E_{j_{0}}
. For
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, we define

\widetilde{E}_{j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}E_{j},&\text{if }j\neq j_{0},\\
E_{j_{0}}\cup\{x\},&\text{if }j=j_{0}.\end{cases}

Then

\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r})=\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})+\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}\\
-\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}.

Proof.

Let
j\neq k\in\{1,\dots,e\}
. If
j,k\neq j_{0}
then
\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{j},\widetilde{E}_{k})=\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})
. If
k=j_{0}
then, recalling that
\mathbf{1}_{j}
denotes the characteristic vector of
E_{j}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{j},\widetilde{E}_{j_{0}})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{j_{0}}\sqcup\{x\})

\displaystyle=\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,m+1)-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{j_{0}}\rvert-\mathbf{1}_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{j_{0}})-\mathbf{1}_{j}(x),&\text{if }\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m,\\
\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{j_{0}})+1-\mathbf{1}_{j}(x),&\text{if }\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m.\end{cases}

Thus, by Lemma
2.50
we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r})-\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
j\neq j_{0}\end{subarray}}\bigl[\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{j},\widetilde{E}_{j_{0}})-\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{j_{0}})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\bigr\}-\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:x\in E_{j}\bigr\}.

Writing

\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\bigr\}=\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}\\
\sqcup\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\},

and

\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:x\in E_{j}\bigr\}=\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:x\in E_{j}\text{ and }\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\bigr\}\\
\sqcup\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:x\in E_{j}\text{ and }\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\bigr\},

gives

\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r})-\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})=\begin{multlined}\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}\\
-\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:x\in E_{j}\text{ and }\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\bigr\},\end{multlined}

as announced.
∎

Proposition 3.20
.

There exist
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
with
\lvert E_{1}\rvert=\dots=\lvert E_{r}\rvert
such that
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})=N_{r,e}
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
. It suffices to prove that we can find
\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
satisfying
\lvert\widetilde{E}_{1}\rvert=\dots=\lvert\widetilde{E}_{r}\rvert
such that
\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r})\geq\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
.

Let
m\coloneqq\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert E_{j}\rvert
and
M\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert E_{j}\rvert
.
If
m=M
then we are done, thus we now assume that
m<M
. Let
j_{m},j_{M}\in\{1,\dots,e\}
such that
\lvert E_{j_{m}}\rvert=m
and
\lvert E_{j_{M}}\rvert=M
. We have
e-m+M>e
by assumption, thus
E_{j_{m}}^{c}\cap E_{j_{M}}\neq\emptyset
, in particular we can pick
x\in E_{j_{m}}^{c}\cap E_{j_{M}}
. We first assume that:

\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}\geq\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}.

(3.21)

We define:

\displaystyle N_{m}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\},

\displaystyle N_{M}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=M\}.

By Lemma
3.19
applied with the family
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}
and
x\notin E_{j_{m}}
, we can construct a family
\widetilde{E_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
satisfying
\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{E}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{r})\geq\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
such that either

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=M,

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=m+1,

or

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=M,

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=m,

\displaystyle\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert=m\bigr\}

\displaystyle=N_{m}-1,

\displaystyle\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert\widetilde{E}_{j}\rvert=M\bigr\}

\displaystyle=N_{M},

thus in both cases we conclude by induction on
(M-m,N_{m}+N_{M})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
.

Thus, we now assume that (
3.21
) fails, that is,

\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}<\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}.

(3.22)

Defining
F_{j}\coloneqq E_{j}^{c}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,e\}
, we have

\displaystyle\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert F_{j}\rvert>e-M\text{ and }x\notin F_{j}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert<M\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\geq\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=m\text{ and }x\in E_{j}\bigr\}

\displaystyle>\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert>m\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}\text{ (by\penalty\ \eqref{equation:induction_nremax_same_size_converse})}

\displaystyle\geq\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert E_{j}\rvert=M\text{ and }x\notin E_{j}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\#\bigl\{j\in\{1,\dots,r\}:\lvert F_{j}\rvert=e-M\text{ and }x\in F_{j}\bigr\}.

Since we have chosen
x
such that
x\notin F_{j_{M}}
with
\lvert F_{j_{M}}\rvert=e-M=\min_{1\leq j\leq r}\lvert F_{j}\rvert
, we can apply Lemma
3.19
to the family
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
and
x\notin F_{j_{M}}
. We thus find a family
\widetilde{F}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{F}_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
satisfying
\mathsf{w}(\widetilde{F}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{F}_{r})>\mathsf{w}(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})
, and this concludes the proof since
\mathsf{w}(F_{1},\dots,F_{r})=\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{r})
by Lemmas
2.50
and
3.18
.
∎

For any
k\in\{0,\dots,e\}
, let
q_{e,k}
be the restriction of
q_{e}
to the subspace
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
is the subset of
{\mathcal{P}_{e}}
given by the subsets
E\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
of size
k
. We have
q_{e,k}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,A_{e,k}x\rangle
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
, where
A_{e,k}=(a_{E,F})_{E,F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
is the real symmetric matrix of size
\binom{e}{k}
given by

a_{E,F}=\mathsf{w}(E,F)=k-\lvert E\cap F\rvert,

for all
E,F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
. In particular, as for
A_{e}
, the matrix
A_{e,k}
has only
0
entries in the diagonal.
By Lemmas
3.5
and
3.18
and Proposition
3.20
, we have

N_{r,e}=\max_{1\leq k\leq\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}\\
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r\end{subarray}}q_{e,k}(x).

(3.23)

The matrix
A_{e,k}
appears at some places in the literature (see, for instance,
[
Ry81
,
Ry82
]
).
The result of Lemma
3.24
is maybe well-known; we provide a proof for convenience.

Lemma 3.24
.

Recall that
e\geq 2
and let
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
. The eigenvalues of
A_{e,k}
are the following:

\displaystyle k\binom{e-1}{k}

,

\displaystyle\text{ with multiplicity }1,

\displaystyle 0

,

\displaystyle\text{ with multiplicity }\binom{e}{k}-e,

\displaystyle-\binom{e-2}{k-1}

,

\displaystyle\text{ with multiplicity }e-1.

Moreover, the constant vector
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
is an eigenvector for the eigenvalue
k\binom{e-1}{k}
.

Proof.

We follow the computation of the eigenvalues for adjacency matrices of strongly regular graphs (see, for instance,
[
GoRo
]
). For any
m,n\geq 1
, we denote by
J_{m,n}
the
m\times n
matrix filled with ones, and we define
J_{n}\coloneqq J_{n,n}
. We also write
I_{n}
for the
n\times n
identity matrix.

We first note that for any
E\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}a_{E,F}

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}(k-\lvert E\cap F\rvert)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(k-\ell)\binom{k}{\ell}\binom{e-k}{k-\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\ell\binom{k}{\ell}\binom{e-k}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}k\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}\binom{e-k}{e-k-\ell},

thus, using Vandermonde’s identity we obtain,

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}a_{E,F}

\displaystyle=k\binom{e-1}{e-k-1}

\displaystyle=k\binom{e-1}{k},

so that the constant vector
\mathbf{1}
is an eigenvector of
A_{e,k}
with eigenvalue
k\binom{e-1}{k}
.

Now let
B=(b_{iE})_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq e\\
E\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}\end{subarray}}
be the
e\times\binom{e}{k}
incidence matrix associated with the elements of
\{1,\dots,e\}
and
\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
. In other words, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,e\}
and
E\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
we have
b_{iE}=\mathbf{1}_{E}(i)
, where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
is the characteristic vector of
E\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
. The matrix
B^{\intercal}B
is square with rows and columns indexed by
\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
and for any
E,F\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}
we have

(B^{\intercal}B)_{EF}=\sum_{i=1}^{e}\mathbf{1}_{E}(i)\mathbf{1}_{F}(i)=\lvert E\cap F\rvert,

so that
A_{e,k}=kJ_{\binom{e}{k}}-B^{\intercal}B
. We will now compute
A_{e,k}^{2}
. First, note that the matrix
BB^{\intercal}
is square of size
e
and for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,e\}
we have

(BB^{\intercal})_{ij}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}\mathbf{1}_{E}(i)\mathbf{1}_{E}(j)=\begin{cases}\binom{e-1}{k-1},&\text{if }i=j,\\
\binom{e-2}{k-2},&\text{if }i\neq j\end{cases}

(with the convention
\binom{e-2}{k-2}=0
if
k=1
),
so that

BB^{\intercal}=\binom{e-2}{k-2}J_{e}+\left(\binom{e-1}{k-1}-\binom{e-2}{k-2}\right)I_{e}=\binom{e-2}{k-2}J_{e}+\binom{e-2}{k-1}I_{e}.

It is clear that
B^{\intercal}J_{e}
is a scalar multiple of
J_{\binom{e}{k},e}
, similarly

J_{\binom{e}{k}}^{2},\quad J_{\binom{e}{k}}B^{\intercal}B\text{ and }J_{\binom{e}{k},e}B,

are scalar multiples of
J_{\binom{e}{k}}
.
We deduce that

\displaystyle A_{e,k}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(kJ_{\binom{e}{k}}-B^{\intercal}B\right)^{2},

\displaystyle=\alpha J_{\binom{e}{k}}+B^{\intercal}(BB^{\intercal})B

\displaystyle=\beta J_{\binom{e}{k}}+\binom{e-2}{k-1}B^{\intercal}B

\displaystyle=\gamma J_{\binom{e}{k}}-\binom{e-2}{k-1}A_{e,k},

(3.25)

for some scalars
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Now let
x
be any eigenvector of
A_{e,k}
orthogonal to
\mathbf{1}
and let
\lambda
be its associated eigenvalue. Since
\langle x,\mathbf{1}\rangle=0
we have
J_{\binom{e}{k}}x=0
and thus (
3.25
) gives

A_{e,k}^{2}x=-\binom{e-2}{k-1}A_{e,k}x,

thus
\lambda^{2}=-\binom{e-2}{k-1}\lambda
thus
\lambda=0
or
\lambda=-\binom{e-2}{k-1}
. Now if
m
(respectively
m_{0}
) denotes the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
-\binom{e-2}{k-1}
(resp.
0
), what precedes proves that
k\binom{e-1}{k}
is of multiplicity
1
and we have

\displaystyle 1+m_{0}+m

\displaystyle=\binom{e}{k},

\displaystyle k\binom{e-1}{k}-m\binom{e-2}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A_{e,k})=0,

recalling that the diagonal entries of
A_{e,k}
are
0
. By Lemma
3.12
we find that
m=k\binom{e-1}{k}/\binom{e-2}{k-1}=e-1
and
m_{0}=\binom{e}{k}-e
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
we have

q_{e,k}(x)\leq\frac{\lVert x\rVert_{1}^{2}}{2e}k(e-k).

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
we have

x=\frac{\langle x,\mathbf{1}\rangle}{\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{2}}\mathbf{1}+x_{\perp},

where
x_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
is orthogonal to
\mathbf{1}
. By Lemma
3.24
, the vector
x_{\perp}
is a sum of eigenvectors for
A_{e,k}
with non-positive eigenvalues, thus
q_{e,k}(x_{\perp})\leq 0
and:

q_{e,k}(x)=\frac{\langle x,\mathbf{1}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{4}}q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1})+q_{e,k}(x_{\perp})\leq\frac{\langle x,\mathbf{1}\rangle^{2}}{\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{4}}q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1}).

Now
\lvert\langle x,\mathbf{1}\rangle\rvert\leq\lVert x\rVert_{1}
by the triangle inequality and, again by Lemma
3.24
,

q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1})=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{1},A_{e,k}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\frac{k}{2}\binom{e-1}{k}\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{2}.

Hence, we have

q_{e,k}(x)\leq\frac{\lVert x\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{2}}\frac{k}{2}\binom{e-1}{k}=\frac{\lVert x\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\binom{e}{k}}\frac{k}{2}\binom{e-1}{k}.

We obtain the announced result since
\frac{\binom{e-1}{k}}{\binom{e}{k}}=\frac{e-k}{e}
by Lemma
3.12
.
∎

Corollary 3.27
.

We have

N_{r,e}\leq\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

For any
k\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
, by Proposition
3.26
we know that
q_{e,k}(x)\leq\frac{r^{2}}{2e}k(e-k)
if
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r
. By (
3.23
) this concludes the proof since
k(e-k)\leq\bigl\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
by Lemma
3.12
2
.
∎

Definition 3.28
.

For any
r\geq 0
and
e\geq 1
we define
N^{\prime}_{r,e}\coloneqq\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\bigl\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor\Bigr\rfloor
.

Combining Corollaries
3.16
and
3.27
yields the following final bounds.

Corollary 3.29
.

For any
e,r\geq 2
we have

\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq N_{r,e}\leq N^{\prime}_{r,e}\leq\frac{er^{2}}{8}

We have the following particular case.

Proposition 3.30
.

Assume that
e,r\geq 2
are both even. Then

N_{r,e}=N^{\prime}_{r,e}=\frac{er^{2}}{8}.

We end this part by the following result, which will be useful later.

Lemma 3.31
.

For any
r\geq 0
and
e\geq 1
we have:

N^{\prime}_{r,e}=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda\right\rfloor,

where
k\coloneqq\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor
and
\lambda\coloneqq k\binom{e-1}{k}
(the largest eigenvalue of
A_{e,k}
).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.12
1
we have
\frac{\binom{e-1}{k}}{\binom{e}{k}}=\frac{e-k}{e}
. Thus, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}k\binom{e-1}{k}.

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2e}k(e-k)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.12
2
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.3
More values for small parameters

Let
r,e\geq 2
.
We now aim to compute the value of
N_{r,e}
for
r\in\{3,4\}
or
e\in\{3,\dots,6\}
. For these values, we will see that, except for
r=3
, we have
N_{r,e}=N^{\prime}_{r,e}
(see Proposition
3.47
).

With small
r

We begin by the following result, which will be in fact interesting only in the case
r=3
.

Lemma 3.32
.

If
r\geq 3
then
N_{r,e}\leq\lfloor\frac{(r-1)re}{6}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
and define
a_{jk}\coloneqq\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})=\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{k}\rvert)-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{k}\rvert
for all
1\leq j<k\leq r
. By (
3.4
) and since
a_{jk}\in\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to show that

\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}a_{jk}\leq\frac{(r-1)re}{6}.

Let
1\leq j<k<l\leq r
; note that this is possible since
r\geq 3
. We have

\displaystyle a_{jk}+a_{kl}+a_{jl}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{k}\rvert)+\min(\lvert E_{k}\rvert,\lvert E_{l}\rvert)+\min(\lvert E_{j}\rvert,\lvert E_{l}\rvert)\\
-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{k}\rvert-\lvert E_{k}\cap E_{l}\rvert-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{l}\rvert\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\leq\lvert E_{j}\rvert+\lvert E_{k}\rvert+\lvert E_{l}\rvert-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{k}\rvert-\lvert E_{k}\cap E_{l}\rvert-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{l}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert E_{j}\cup E_{k}\cup E_{l}\rvert-\lvert E_{j}\cap E_{k}\cap E_{l}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert E_{j}\cup E_{k}\cup E_{l}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq e.

Now, in the sum
\sum_{1\leq j<k<l\leq r}(a_{jk}+a_{kl}+a_{jl})
each couple
(j,k)
for
1\leq j<k\leq r
appears exactly
r-2
times, thus

\sum_{1\leq j<k<l\leq r}(a_{jk}+a_{kl}+a_{jl})=(r-2)\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}a_{jk}.

Thus, we find that

(r-2)\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}a_{jk}\leq e\binom{r}{3},

and thus

\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq r}a_{jk}\leq e\frac{r(r-1)}{6},

whence the result.
∎

Proposition 3.33

(Case
r=3
)
.

We have
N_{3,e}=e
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.16
and Lemma
3.32
we have

2\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\leq N_{3,e}\leq e,

(3.34)

in particular
N_{3,e}=e
if
e
is even. (Note that if we use Corollary
3.16
instead of Lemma
3.32

we only obtain
N_{r,e}\leq\frac{9e}{8}
.) Thus, we can now assume that
e
is odd and let us write
e=2e^{\prime}+3
with
e^{\prime}\geq 0
. We first assume that
e^{\prime}\geq 1
. By Lemma
3.15
we have

N_{3,2e^{\prime}}+N_{3,3}\leq N_{3,e}.

Since
2e^{\prime}
is even we have
N_{3,2e^{\prime}}=2e^{\prime}
, thus we obtain, using (
3.34
) for the right inequality,

e-3+N_{3,3}\leq N_{3,e}\leq e.

Thus, to conclude it suffices to prove that
N_{3,3}=3
. We already know by (
3.34
) that
N_{3,3}\leq 3
, thus it suffices to find
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
such that
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})=3
. Using (
3.4
), this equality is satisfied with
E_{i}\coloneqq\{i\}
for any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.35

(Case
r=4
)
.

We have:

N_{4,e}=\begin{cases}2e,&\text{if }e\text{ is even},\\
2e-1,&\text{if }e\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
e
is even then we know by Proposition
3.30
that
N_{4,e}=\frac{e4^{2}}{8}=2e
. We thus now assume that
e
is odd. By Corollary
3.27
we obtain

N_{4,e}\leq\frac{8}{e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor<\frac{8}{e}\frac{e^{2}}{4}=2e,

thus
N_{4,e}\leq 2e-1
. If
e>3
, as in the proof of Proposition
3.33
, using Lemma
3.15
we have

N_{4,e-3}+N_{4,3}\leq N_{4,e},

thus, since
e-3
is even,

2e-6+N_{4,3}\leq N_{4,e}.

Hence, to conclude it suffices to prove that
N_{4,3}=5
. We have seen that
N_{4,3}\leq 2\cdot 3-1=5
, thus it suffices to find
E_{1},\dots,E_{4}\subseteq\{1,\dots,e\}
with
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{4})=5
. Setting
E_{i}\coloneqq\{i\}
for all
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and
E_{4}\coloneqq E_{3}
, we have

\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }j\in\{1,2\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for any
1\leq j<k\leq 4
, thus
\mathsf{w}(E_{1},\dots,E_{4})=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq 4}\mathsf{w}(E_{j},E_{k})=3+2=5
. This concludes the proof.
∎

With small
e

The next results will mainly consist in using the fact that
\mathbf{1}
is an eigenvector of
A_{e,k}
.

Proposition 3.36

(Case
e=3
)
.

We have
N_{r,3}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{3}\bigr\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.27
we have
N_{r,3}\leq\frac{r^{2}}{6}\bigl\lfloor\frac{9}{4}\bigr\rfloor=\frac{r^{2}}{3}
, hence
N_{r,3}\leq\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{3}\bigr\rfloor
. Thus, by (
3.23
) it suffices to find
x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,1}}
with
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r
such that
q_{e,1}(x)=\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{3}\bigr\rfloor
. To that extent, write
r=3a+h
with
a\geq 1
and
h\in\{-1,0,1\}
and consider
x\coloneqq a\mathbf{1}+h\mathbf{1}_{E}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,1}}
, where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
is any characteristic vector of an element of
\mathcal{P}_{e,1}
. Note that
x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,1}}
and
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=a\binom{3}{1}+h=3a+h=r
indeed. We have, recalling that
a_{E,E}=0
and from Lemma
3.24
that
A_{e,1}\mathbf{1}=\binom{2}{1}\mathbf{1}=2\,\mathbf{1}
,

\displaystyle q_{e,1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,A_{e,1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{2}\langle\mathbf{1},A_{e,1}\mathbf{1}\rangle+h^{2}\langle\mathbf{1}_{E},A_{e,1}\mathbf{1}_{E}\rangle+2ah\langle\mathbf{1},A_{e,1}\mathbf{1}_{E}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(2a^{2}\lVert\mathbf{1}\rVert^{2}+4ah\right)

\displaystyle=3a^{2}+2ah

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}-h^{2}}{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{r^{2}}{3},&\text{if }r=0\pmod{3},\\
\frac{r^{2}-1}{3},&\text{if }r=\pm 1\pmod{3},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{3}\right\rfloor.

∎

Proposition 3.37

(Case

)
.

We have
N_{r,4}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2}\bigr\rfloor
and
N_{r,6}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{3r^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor=3\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
.

Proof.

If
r

. Now if

we have

\displaystyle 2\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq N_{r,4}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle 3\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq N_{r,6}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Now we have

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right\rfloor

which concludes the proof for

. For

which concludes the proof.

We will now deal with the case

.

Definition 3.38
.

For any
r\geq 0
and
e\geq 2
, we define

Q_{r,e}\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}\\
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r\end{subarray}}q_{e,k}(x),

where
k\coloneqq\bigl\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rfloor
.

Recalling (
3.23
) and Corollary
3.29
, for any
r\geq 2
and
e\geq 2
we have

Q_{r,e}\leq N_{r,e}\leq N^{\prime}_{r,e}.

(3.39)

Remark 3.40
.

We will see that when
e=5
then
Q_{r,e}=N_{r,e}
(Lemma
3.44
). It is unclear whether this equality holds in full generality, in particular we state the next results with the quantity
Q_{r,e}
.

Recall from Definition
3.28
that
N^{\prime}_{r,e}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\bigl\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor\bigr\rfloor
.

Lemma 3.41
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
e\geq 2
. Assume that
Q_{r,e}=N^{\prime}_{r,e}
. Then
Q_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}=N^{\prime}_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}
, where
k\coloneqq\bigl\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rfloor
.

Proof.

By assumption, we can find
x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
with
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r
such that
q_{e,k}(x)=N^{\prime}_{r,e}
(with
k=\bigl\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rfloor
). We have, where
\lambda\coloneqq k\binom{e-1}{k}
is the highest eigenvalue of
A_{e,k}
(see Lemma
3.24
),

\displaystyle q_{e,k}(x+\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle=q_{e,k}(x)+q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1})+\langle x,A_{e,k}\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=Q_{r,e}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}+\lambda r,

thus
Q_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}\geq Q_{r,e}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}+\lambda r
. Note that
\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}=q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1})
is an integer by Proposition
3.17
1
. Now, recalling the equality
N^{\prime}_{r,e}=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda\right\rfloor
from Lemma
3.31
we find:

\displaystyle N^{\prime}_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}+\binom{e}{k}^{2}+2\binom{e}{k}r}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}+\lambda r\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2\binom{e}{k}}\lambda\right\rfloor+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}+\lambda r.

Finally, we have

\displaystyle N^{\prime}_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}

\displaystyle\geq Q_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}

\displaystyle=Q_{r,e}+\left(Q_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}-Q_{r,e}\right)

\displaystyle\geq N^{\prime}_{r,e}+\left(N^{\prime}_{r+\binom{e}{k},e}-N^{\prime}_{r,e}\right)

\displaystyle=N^{\prime}_{r+\binom{e}{k},e},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.42
.

Let
r\geq 0
and
e\geq 2
. Let
k\coloneqq\bigl\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\bigr\rfloor
and assume that there exists
x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
with
\lVert x\rVert_{1}=r
such that
q_{e,k}(x)=N^{\prime}_{r,e}
(in particular, we have
Q_{r,e}=N^{\prime}_{r,e}
). Then
Q_{\binom{e}{k}-r,e}=N^{\prime}_{\binom{e}{k}-r,e}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbf{1}-x\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{P}_{e,k}}
since the entries of
x
are at most
1
.
With similar calculations as in Lemma
3.41
, we thus have:

\displaystyle Q_{\binom{e}{k}-r,e}

\displaystyle\geq q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1}-x)

\displaystyle=q_{e,k}(x)+q_{e,k}(\mathbf{1})-\langle x,A_{e,k}\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=Q_{r,e}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}-\lambda r

\displaystyle=N^{\prime}_{r,e}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\binom{e}{k}-\lambda r

\displaystyle=N^{\prime}_{\binom{e}{k}-r,e},

thus we conclude by (
3.39
).
∎

Remark 3.43
.

Assume that
r=1
. We have
N^{\prime}_{1,e}\leq\frac{e}{8}
by Corollary
3.29
, thus if

of Lemma

with

, the assumption of Lemma

is also satisfied (using (
3.39
)).

We give a last preliminary result, which is a refinement of (

) (see Remark

).

Lemma 3.44
.

Assume that
e=5
. For any

we have
Q_{r,e}=N_{r,e}
.

Proof.

By (
3.23
), Proposition
3.26
and Corollary
3.16
, it suffices to prove that

\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2e}k^{\prime}(e-k^{\prime})\right\rfloor<\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor,

for all

. In our setting, this reduces to proving

\left\lfloor\frac{2r^{2}}{5}\right\rfloor<2\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

(3.45)

If

\bigl\lfloor\frac{2r^{2}}{5}\bigr\rfloor\leq\frac{2r^{2}}{5}<\frac{r^{2}}{2}=2\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
thus (
3.45
) holds. If

\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor=\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}
and

\frac{2r^{2}}{5}<\frac{r^{2}-1}{2}\iff 4r^{2}<5r^{2}-5,

thus (
3.45
) holds since

is odd.
∎

Proposition 3.46

(Case
e=5
)
.

For any

N_{r,5}=N^{\prime}_{r,5}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{3r^{2}}{5}\bigr\rfloor
.

Proof.

By Lemma

for

It remains to prove

.

All in one

3.3.4
Asymptotics

In this section, we will describe the asymptotic behaviour of the two sequences
(N_{r,e})_{e\geq 2}
and
(N_{r,e})_{r\geq 2}
for
r,e\geq 2
.
We first recall the following basic fact, which will be used without any further reference.

Lemma 3.48
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of real numbers that converges to
+\infty
and let
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the sequence
\bigl(\frac{\lfloor xu_{n}\rfloor}{u_{n}}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}
converges to
x
.

We also recall the following standard result.

Lemma 3.49

(Fekete’s superadditive lemma)
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a superadditive sequence. Then
\bigl(\frac{u_{n}}{n}\bigr)_{n\geq 1}
has a limit in
[-\infty,+\infty]
as
n\to+\infty
.

By Lemmas
3.15
and
3.49
, we know that the sequence
\left(\frac{N_{r,e}}{e}\right)_{e\geq 1}
has a limit
N_{r,\infty}\in[0,+\infty]
. By Corollary
3.29
we have

\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\leq N_{r,\infty}\leq\frac{r^{2}}{8},

(3.50)

in particular if
r
is even then
N_{r,\infty}=\frac{r^{2}}{8}
.

Remark 3.51
.

By Propositions
3.13
,
3.33
and
3.35
, we have
N_{2,\infty}=\frac{1}{2}
,
N_{3,\infty}=1
and
N_{4,\infty}=2
.

Our aim is now to give a similar result when
r
grows to infinity.

Proposition 3.52
.

The sequence
\left(\frac{N_{r,e}}{r^{2}}\right)_{r\geq 2}
converges to
N_{\infty,e}\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}\\
\lVert y\rVert_{1}=1\end{subarray}}q_{e}(y)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.29
, we know that the sequence
\bigl(N_{r,e}/r^{2}\bigr)_{r\geq 2}
is bounded. Hence, to prove that it converges to
N_{\infty,e}
it suffices to prove that any converging subsequence converges to
N_{\infty,e}
. We thus consider a converging subsequence of
\bigl(N_{r,e}/r^{2}\bigr)_{r\geq 2}
and let
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
be its limit. To avoid the notation being overloaded, we still denote this subsequence by
\bigl(N_{r,e}/r^{2}\bigr)_{r\geq 2}
. By Proposition
3.17
, we have
N_{r,e}/r^{2}\leq N_{\infty,e}
for any
r\geq 2
thus
\ell\leq N_{\infty,e}
. We will now prove the reverse inequality.

Let
x=(x_{E})\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
. Defining
\lvert x\rvert\coloneqq(\lvert x_{E}\rvert)_{E\in{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
we have
\lVert\lvert x\rvert\rVert_{1}=\lVert x\rVert_{1}
. Since the entries of
A_{e}
are non-negative we have
q_{e}(x)\leq q_{e}(\lvert x\rvert)
. Hence, if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{{\mathcal{P}_{e}}}
is such that
q_{e}(x)=\max_{\lVert y\rVert_{1}=1}q_{e}(y)
then we can assume that
x
has only non-negative coordinates. In particular, we can take a sequence of rational vectors with non-negative entries
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with limit
x
and satisfying
\lVert x_{n}\rVert_{1}=1
for all
n\geq 1
, where the entries of
x_{n}
have the same denominator
b_{n}
. Noting that
b_{n}
is not necessarily coprime with its associated numerator, we can always assume that
b_{n}\to\infty
. Then by Proposition
3.17
we have
N_{b_{n},e}\geq q_{e}(b_{n}x_{n})=b_{n}^{2}q_{e}(x_{n})
, thus since
q_{e}
is continuous we obtain
\ell\geq q_{e}(x)=N_{\infty,e}
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

By Corollary
3.29
we have

\frac{1}{4}\left\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\right\rfloor\leq N_{\infty,e}\leq\frac{1}{2e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

We will be able here to determine the exact value of the limit
N_{\infty,e}
.

Corollary 3.53
.

For any
e\geq 2
we have

N_{\infty,e}=\frac{1}{2e}\left\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Since
Q_{0,e}=N^{\prime}_{0,e}=0
, we deduce from Lemma
3.41
and (
3.39
) that
Q_{r,e}=N_{r,e}=N^{\prime}_{r,e}=\bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{2e}\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\rfloor\bigr\rfloor
as soon as
r
is of the form
r=\alpha\binom{e}{\lfloor\frac{e}{2}\rfloor}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. This proves that
(N_{r,e}/r^{2})_{r}
has a subsequence that converges to
\frac{1}{2e}\bigl\lfloor\frac{e^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
and this concludes the proof.
∎

4
Application in level one

Let
e\geq 2
. We will here apply the implicit description of the set
Q^{\mathsf{s}}
given at Proposition
2.40
to study a shift operation on partitions, and relate it to a shift operation on blocks that is naturally defined in higher levels.

In this whole section we assume that we are in level one, that is, we have
r=1
. In particular, without loss of generality we can assume that the charge is zero and therefore we will often omit to write the corresponding superscripts. For instance, the weight of
\alpha=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q
is given by

\mathsf{w}(\alpha)=c_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}(c_{i}-c_{i+1})^{2},

and, recalling Lemma
2.24
, if
\lambda
is an
e
-core and
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
then
x_{i}(\lambda)=y_{i}(\lambda)
.
The only case where we keep the superscript is for
Q^{\mathsf{0}}=\{\alpha(\lambda):\lambda
is a partition
\}
, to avoid confusion with the ambient abelian group
Q\supseteq Q^{\mathsf{0}}
. Finally, we define

\displaystyle Q^{\mathsf{0}}_{*}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\alpha(\lambda):\lambda\text{ is an }e\text{-core}\}.

We recall from Lemma
2.39
that
Q^{\mathsf{0}}_{*}=\bigl\{\alpha\in Q:\mathsf{w}(\alpha)=0\bigr\}
. Let
e^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
and let
p\in\{2,\dots,e\}
be the order of
e^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
.

4.1
Shifting blocks

Definition 4.1
.

We define the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\sigma:Q\to Q
by
\sigma\cdot\alpha_{i}\coloneqq\alpha_{i-e^{\prime}}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
.

The map
\sigma
is an automorphism of
Q
of order
p
. As we saw in the introduction, the automorphism
\sigma
appears together with a shift operation
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mapsto\prescript{\sigma}{}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
on
r
-partitions when
r\geq 2
. This shift operation on
r
-partitions is defined by a (power of a) cyclic permutation of the components of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, and we have (under suitable conditions on the multicharge
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}
):

\alpha^{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}}\bigl(\prescript{\sigma}{}{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})=\sigma\cdot\alpha^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{s}}}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})

(see (
1.1
)). In this Section
4
we are interested in the case
r=1
. As we saw in Definition
4.1
, the operation
\alpha\mapsto\sigma\cdot\alpha
on
Q
is still defined, but it is unclear what the operation
\lambda\mapsto\prescript{\sigma}{}{\lambda}
should be. Our aim is to study some properties of such an operation.

Note that :

\sigma\cdot\delta=\delta,

(4.2)

recalling that
\delta=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{i}\in Q
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q
. We have
\mathsf{w}(\sigma\cdot\alpha)=\mathsf{w}(\alpha)+c_{e^{\prime}}-c_{0}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\sigma\cdot\alpha=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i+e^{\prime}}\alpha_{i},

thus

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}(\sigma\cdot\alpha)

\displaystyle=c_{e^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\left(c_{i+e^{\prime}}-c_{i+e^{\prime}+1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{e^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\left(c_{i}-c_{i+1}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{w}(\alpha)-c_{0}+c_{e^{\prime}}.

∎

Using Lemma
2.39
and Proposition
2.40
, we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
. Then

\sigma\cdot\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}\iff c_{0}\leq c_{e^{\prime}}+\mathsf{w}(\alpha),

the right-hand side being an equality if and only if
\sigma\cdot\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}_{*}
. In particular, if
\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}_{*}
then

\sigma\cdot\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}\iff c_{0}\leq c_{e^{\prime}},

the right-hand side being an equality if and only if
\sigma\cdot\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}_{*}
.

4.2
Shifting partitions

In the continuation of Corollary
4.4
, given a partition
\lambda
such that
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
we show how to obtain the partitions that lie in
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)
. The constructions and results that we present in this subsection can be obtained using
Scopes isometries
(see
[
Sc
]
and for instance
[
Fa06
]
), a Scopes isometry consisting in swapping two (adjacent) runners in the
e
-abacus.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\lambda
be an
e
-core. We denote by
\sigma\lambda
the unique
e
-core such that

x(\sigma\lambda)=\bigl(x_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda),\dots,x_{e-1}(\lambda),x_{0}(\lambda),\dots,x_{e^{\prime}-1}(\lambda)\bigr).

Note that
\sigma\lambda
is well-defined by Proposition
2.27
since
x_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)+\dots+x_{e-1}(\lambda)+x_{0}(\lambda)+\dots+x_{e^{\prime}-1}(\lambda)=0
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\lambda
be an
e
-core and let
z\coloneqq c_{0}(\lambda)-c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)
. We have

\alpha(\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)+z\delta.

Proof.

By (
2.25
) we have
x_{0}(\lambda)+\dots+x_{e^{\prime}-1}(\lambda)=z
, moreover by Corollary
2.32
we have
c_{0}(\sigma\lambda)=c_{0}(\lambda)
. Hence, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
we have by (
2.25
)

\displaystyle c_{i}(\sigma\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\sigma\lambda)-x_{0}(\sigma\lambda)-\dots-x_{i-1}(\sigma\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\lambda)-x_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)-\dots-x_{e^{\prime}+i-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{0}(\lambda)+z-x_{0}(\lambda)-\dots-x_{e^{\prime}+i-1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{e^{\prime}+i}(\lambda)+z.

We conclude since

\alpha(\sigma\lambda)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}(\sigma\lambda)\alpha_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}\bigl(c_{e^{\prime}+i}(\lambda)+z)\alpha_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}}c_{i}(\lambda)\alpha_{i-e^{\prime}}+z\delta=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)+z\delta.

∎

The next result shows that Definition
4.5
fits with what we can expect between
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)
and
\alpha(\sigma\lambda)
when
\lambda
is an
e
-core.
Recall from Definition
2.70
that any
\alpha\in Q
has an associated core.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition and let
\overline{\lambda}
be its
e
-core. Then
\alpha(\sigma\overline{\lambda})
is the core of
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)
.

Proof.

By definition, the partition
\sigma\overline{\lambda}
is an
e
-core thus the block
\alpha(\sigma\overline{\lambda})
is a core block (recalling Remark
2.62
). The assertion then follows from Lemma
2.67
and Proposition
4.6
. ∎

In particular, it follows from Remark
2.71
that if
\lambda
is a partition such that
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
then
\sigma\overline{\lambda}
is the common
e
-core of the partitions that lie in
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)
.
We now propose a generalisation of Definition
4.5
when
\lambda
is not necessarily an
e
-core.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition and for any
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, let
R_{i}
be the
i
-th runner of the
e
-abacus of
\lambda
. We define
\sigma\lambda
to be the partition whose
e
-abacus is obtained as follows: for any
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, the
i
-th runner is
R_{i+e^{\prime}}
(where addition is modulo
e
).

Remark 4.9
.

The partition
\sigma\lambda
is the (unique) partition with
e
-core
\sigma\overline{\lambda}
and
e
-quotient
\bigl(\lambda^{[e^{\prime}]},\dots,\lambda^{[e-1]},\lambda^{[0]},\dots,\lambda^{[e^{\prime}-1]}\bigr)
. In particular by (
2.22
) we have
\mathsf{w}_{e}(\lambda)=\mathsf{w}_{e}\bigl(\sigma\lambda\bigr)
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. We have

\alpha\left(\sigma\lambda\right)=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)\iff\lvert\sigma\lambda\rvert=\lvert\lambda\rvert\iff c_{0}(\lambda)=c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
we have
c_{0}(\lambda)-c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)=c_{0}(\overline{\lambda})-c_{e^{\prime}}(\overline{\lambda})
, thus from (
4.2
), Proposition
4.6
and Remark
4.9
we obtain

\alpha(\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)+\left(c_{0}(\lambda)-c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)\right)\mathbf{1}.

Since
\lvert\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)\rvert=\lvert\alpha(\lambda)\rvert=\lvert\lambda\rvert
, we deduce that
\lvert\sigma\lambda\rvert=\lvert\lambda\rvert+c_{0}(\lambda)-c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Is is easy to construct an
e
-core (and thus, a partition) that satisfies the equivalent conditions of Corollary
4.10
. Indeed, if
\lambda
is an
e
-core then by (
2.25
) we have
c_{0}(\lambda)=c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)
if and only if
x_{0}(\lambda)+\dots+x_{e^{\prime}-1}(\lambda)=0
.

4.3
Some properties

In this subsection, we always assume that
e^{\prime}
divides
e
. In particular, the order
p
of
e^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
is
p=\frac{e}{e^{\prime}}
. We will count both modulo
e
and
e^{\prime}
, thus to avoid ambiguities we will add a prime when we compute modulo
e^{\prime}
. For instance, for any partition
\lambda
and for any
i^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,e^{\prime}-1\}
we will denote by
c_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\lambda)
the number of nodes
\gamma
of
\lambda
such that
\mathrm{res}(\gamma)=i^{\prime}\pmod{e^{\prime}}
.
The next lemma is immediate.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
i^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,e^{\prime}-1\}
. For any partition
\lambda
we have

c_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}c_{i^{\prime}+ke^{\prime}}(\lambda).

Let
Q^{\prime}=\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/e^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}\alpha^{\prime}_{j}
be a free abelian group with basis
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/e^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}}
. We consider the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\pi:Q\to Q^{\prime}
determined by

\pi(\alpha_{i})\coloneqq\alpha^{\prime}_{i\bmod{e^{\prime}}},

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
. Note that
i\bmod e^{\prime}
is well-defined since we have assumed that
e^{\prime}
divides
e
. It follows from the definition of the automorphism
\sigma
that

\pi(\sigma\cdot\alpha)=\pi(\alpha)\in Q^{\prime},

for any
\alpha\in Q
. We then deduce from Lemma
2.34
the following result.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
so that
\sigma\cdot\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
. If a partition
\lambda
(respectively,
\mu
) lies in
\alpha
(resp.
\sigma\cdot\alpha
) then
\lambda
and
\mu
share the same
e^{\prime}
-core.

Recall the classical result that any
e^{\prime}
-core is an
e
-core (cf. Lemma
2.13
).

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\lambda
be an
e
-core and assume that
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
. Then
\lambda
is an
e^{\prime}
-core.

Proof.

Since
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
we have
c_{i}(\lambda)=c_{i+e^{\prime}}(\lambda)
for any
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. We deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\left(c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=p\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\left(c_{i^{\prime}}(\lambda)-c_{i^{\prime}+1}(\lambda)\right)^{2},

and, by Lemma
4.12
,

\displaystyle c_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=pc_{i^{\prime}}(\lambda),

for any
i^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,e^{\prime}-1\}
.
By Proposition
2.31
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{w}_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=c_{0}^{\prime}(\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\left(c_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}(\lambda)-c_{i^{\prime}+1}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=pc_{0}(\lambda)-\frac{p^{2}}{2}\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\left(c_{i^{\prime}}(\lambda)-c_{i^{\prime}+1}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=pc_{0}(\lambda)-\frac{p}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\left(c_{i}(\lambda)-c_{i+1}(\lambda)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=p\mathsf{w}_{e}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=0,

thus
\lambda
is an
e^{\prime}
-core.
∎

The previous proposition involves a block
\alpha\in Q
satisfying
\alpha=\sigma\cdot\alpha
. We say that such a block is
stuttering
. In a similar spirit as in
[
Ro19b
]
, we will now study the relationship between stuttering blocks and stuttering partitions, that is, partitions
\lambda
satisfying
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition that satisfies
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
. Then
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
and
p\mid\mathsf{w}_{e}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear by Corollary
4.10
since
\lvert\sigma\lambda\rvert=\lvert\lambda\rvert
. For the second assertion, since
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
then we know by Remark
4.9
that the
e
-quotient of
\lambda
is

\left(\lambda^{[0]},\dots,\lambda^{[e^{\prime}-1]},\lambda^{[0]},\dots,\lambda^{[e^{\prime}-1]},\dots,\lambda^{[0]},\dots,\lambda^{[e^{\prime}-1]}\right),

where the sequence
\lambda^{[0]},\dots,\lambda^{[e^{\prime}-1]}
is repeated
p
times. Thus by (
2.22
) we have

\mathsf{w}_{e}(\lambda)=p\sum_{i=0}^{e^{\prime}-1}\bigl\lvert\lambda^{[i]}\bigr\rvert,

which concludes the proof.
∎

The aim is now to give a converse statement for Lemma
4.15
, proving Theorem
D
from the introduction.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
\lambda
be an
e
-core. If
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
then
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
c_{0}(\lambda)=c_{e^{\prime}}(\lambda)
. Hence, by Corollary
4.10
we have
\alpha(\sigma\lambda)=\sigma\cdot\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)
, thus
\lambda
and
\sigma\lambda
lie in the same block. This concludes since
\lambda
is an
e
-core (recalling Lemma
2.34
).
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Let
\alpha\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
. If
\alpha=\sigma\cdot\alpha
and
p\mid\mathsf{w}(\alpha)
then there exists
\lambda
with
\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha
satisfying
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a partition with
\alpha(\mu)=\alpha
and let
\overline{\mu}
be the
e
-core of
\mu
. By Lemma
2.8
and (
4.2
), since
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\mu)=\alpha(\mu)
we have
\sigma\cdot\alpha(\overline{\mu})=\alpha(\overline{\mu})
. Thus, by Proposition
4.16
we have
\sigma\overline{\mu}=\overline{\mu}
. By assumption and (
2.9
), we can write
\mathsf{w}_{e}(\mu)=wp
with
w\in\mathbb{N}
. Now let
\lambda
be the partition whose
e
-abacus is obtained as follows: for each
i\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
, we slide
w
times the rightmost bead on runner
ie^{\prime}
of the
e
-abacus corresponding to
\overline{\mu}
. By Lemmas
2.8
and
2.20
, the partition
\lambda
satisfies
\alpha(\lambda)=\alpha(\overline{\mu})+pw\mathbf{1}=\alpha(\overline{\mu})+\mathsf{w}_{e}(\mu)\mathbf{1}=\alpha(\mu)
, and by construction
\sigma\lambda=\lambda
.
∎

Note that for any
h\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
4.2
) the block
\alpha=h\mathbf{1}\in Q^{\mathsf{0}}
satisfies
\alpha=\sigma\cdot\alpha
, however, we have
p\mid\mathsf{w}(\alpha)
if and only if
p\mid h
.

Instantaneous blowup and singular potentials on Heisenberg groups
Gisele R. Goldstein, Jerome A. Goldstein, Alessia E. Kogoj, Abdelaziz Rhandi, Cristian Tacelli

Abstract
. In this paper we generalize the instantaneous blowup result from [ 3 ] and [ 15 ] to the heat equation perturbed by singular potentials on the Heisenberg group.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
47D07, 47D08; 35J10, 35K20

1.
Introduction

The problem of existence and nonexistence of nonnegative solutions to the heat equation with singular potentials
V_{c}^{*}(x)=\dfrac{c}{|x|^{2}},\,x\in\Omega_{N}
,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)=\varDelta u(x,t)+V_{c}^{*}(x)u(x,t)\quad(x,t)\in\Omega_{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega_{N},\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega_{N}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{R}^{N}\,\hbox{\ if }N\geq 2,\\
(0,\infty)\,\hbox{\ if }N=1,\end{array}\right.
was settled and solved by Baras and Goldstein
[
3
]
. For
\Omega_{1}=(0,\infty)
one has to add a Dirichlet boundary condition at
0
. For simplicity we assume in the sequel that
N\geq 3
and set
C_{*}(N):=\left(\frac{N-2}{2}\right)^{2}
.

Obviously, the phenomenon
of existence and nonexistence is caused by the singular potential
V_{c}^{*}
, which is controlled by Hardy’s inequality

C_{*}(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\varphi(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}\,dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

together with its optimal constant
C_{*}(N)
. Moreover
V_{c}^{*}
belongs to a borderline case where the strong maximum principle and Gaussian bounds fail, cf.
[
2
]
.

Let
W_{n}(x)=\inf\{V_{c}^{*}(x),n\}
be the cutoff potential,
with

c>C_{*}(N)
. Let
u_{n}
be the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial_{t}u_{n}}{\partial t}-\varDelta u_{n}-W_{n}u_{n}=0,\quad\hbox{\ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
\\
u_{n}(x,0)=u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Here
0\not\equiv u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or, more generally,
u_{0}
grows no
faster than
e^{|x|^{2-\varepsilon}}
at infinity. Since
W_{n}
is bounded,
u_{n}
exists. If a
positive solution
u
to (
1.1
) were to exist, then
0<u_{n}\leq u
which is a contradiction, since

u_{n}(x,t)
tends to infinity at all spatial points and at all
positive times (see
[
3
, Theorem 2.2.(ii)]
). This is called
instantaneous blowup
.

Given nonnegative functions
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,0\leq V\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
0\leq f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
, Baras and Goldstein
[
3
]
considered the problem of finding a function
u
such that

(P)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\leq u\hbox{\ on }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\,V(\cdot)u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)),\\
\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\varDelta u+Vu+f\,\hbox{\ in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)),\\
\\
\mathrm{esslim}_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x,t)\psi(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{0}(x)\psi(x)\,dx,\,\mbox{for all}\ \psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)):=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
with the usual topology and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
the dual of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
, is the space of all distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
.

Consider the potential

W_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{c}{|x|^{2}}\quad\hbox{\ if }x\in B_{1},\\
\\
0\quad\quad\hbox{\ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Here
B_{1}
can be replaced by
B_{\delta}
for every fixed
\delta>0
, where
B_{r}
denotes the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of center
0
and radius
r>0
.
Baras and Goldstein
[
3
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

Let
0\leq c\leq C_{*}(N)
and let
V\geq 0
be a measurable potential satisfying
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1})
, where
B_{1}
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
0\leq f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T))
. If
V\leq W_{0}
in
B_{1}
,
then
(P)
has a positive solution if

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)\,dx<\infty\,,\quad\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x,s)|x|^{-\alpha}dxds<\infty,

where
\alpha
is the smallest root of
\alpha(N-2-\alpha)=c
.
If
V\geq W_{0}
in
B_{1}
, and if
(P)
has a solution u, then

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}|x|^{-\alpha}u_{0}(x)\,dx<\infty,\quad\int_{0}^{T-\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}f(x,s)|x|^{-\alpha}dxds<\infty,

for each
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
and each
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\alpha
as above.
If either
u_{0}\neq 0
or
f\neq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\varepsilon)
for each
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
, then
given
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there is a
C=C(\varepsilon,\Omega^{\prime})>0
such that

(1.3)

u(x,t)\geq\frac{C}{|x|^{\alpha}}\text{ if }(x,t)\in\Omega^{\prime}\times[\varepsilon,T).

(ii)

If
c>C_{*}(N)
,
V\geq W_{0}
and either
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
or
f\not\equiv 0
, then
(P)
does not have a positive solution.

Many extensions of the above result have been done by several authors, cf.
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
.
In this article we present a new result of this type replacing the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by the sub-Laplacian
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
(also known as the Kohn Laplacian) on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
. For the definitions see Section
2
.

For this purpose let us
consider, for
w=(z,l)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
, the problem

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(w,t)=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u(w,t)+V_{*}(w)u(w,t)+f(w,t)&t>0,\,w\in\mathbb{H}^{N},\\
\\
u(w,0)=u_{0}(w),&w\in\mathbb{H}^{N}.\end{array}\right.

Assume
u_{0}\geq 0
,
f\geq 0
and as
V_{*}
choose
the corresponding critical potential in the case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N}

V_{*}(w)=c\frac{|z|^{2}}{|z|^{4}+l^{2}},\quad w=(z,l)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}.

We thus look at the problem

(P)_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u+V_{*}u+f&\text{in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
\\
u(w,0)=u_{0}(w)&w\in\mathbb{H}^{N},\end{array}\right.

with
u_{0}\geq 0
and
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
a.e.
Set
V_{n}(w)=\min\{V_{*}(w),n\}
,
f_{n}(w,t)=\min\{f(w,t),n\}
. Let
u_{n}
be the unique nonnegative solution of

(P_{n})_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u_{n}+V_{n}u_{n}+f_{n}&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
\\
u_{n}(w,0)=u_{0}(w)&w\in\mathbb{H}^{N},\end{array}\right.

and assume that
u_{n}
exists.
We only need to assume that the heat equation with no potential has a global positive solution
when
u_{0}
is the initial value, see (
2.6
) and Theorem
2.2
below. It is sufficient that
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
and
u_{0}
grows no faster
than
e^{d^{2}(w)-\varepsilon}
at infinity, where
d(\cdot)
is the function given by (
2.4
).

Let

C^{*}(N)=N^{2}.

We will prove, for
u_{n}
the solution of
(P_{n})_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
, that

(I)

If
0<c\leq C^{*}(N)
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w,t)=u(w,t),\quad(w,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),

exists and is a solution of
(P)_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
.

(II)

If
c>C^{*}(N)
, then

(1.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w,t)=+\infty

for all
(w,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T).

The conclusion in (II), namely (
1.5
), is known as
instantaneous blowup
, or (IBU) for short.

In the existence case
(I)
, by the maximum principle for
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
,
it is clear that we can replace
V_{n},V_{*}
by
\tilde{V}_{n},\tilde{V}_{*}
where
\tilde{V}_{n}\leq V_{n}
,
\tilde{V}_{*}\leq V_{*}
a.e. for each
n
.
Similarly, for the nonexistence result
(II)
, we can replace
V_{n},V_{*}
by
\tilde{V}_{n},\tilde{V}_{*}
where

\tilde{V}_{n}\geq V_{n}
,
\tilde{V}_{*}\geq V_{*}
a.e. (at least in a neighborhood of the origin).

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we recall the definitions of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
and the sub-Laplacian
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
. We also give some known properties of
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
that we need in this paper.
In Section
3
we state and prove the main results of this paper. In the Appendix we prove some technical lemmas that we use in the proof of the main results.

This paper treats the same basic problem as did
[
15
]
. There, the existence part of Theorem 3.4 was proved, using a different method. But part (ii) of Theorem 3.4 is much stronger than the corresponding result of
[
15
]
.

In 1999, X. Cabré and Y. Martel
[
5
]
gave a different approach to a more general problem. The paper
[
3
]
treated a potential
V\geq 0
with only one singularity, at the origin, while
[
5
]
allowed for a much more general potential which
one takes to be
0\leq W\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}).
In
[
5
]
the authors defined the “generalized first eigenvalue” of the Schrödinger operator
-\varDelta-W
as

\displaystyle\sigma_{W}=\inf_{u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}-W(x)|u(x)|^{2})\ dx\right\}

\displaystyle\left(\hbox{\ or }\sigma_{W}=\inf_{u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}),\,\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}-W(x)|u(x)|^{2})\ dx\right\}\hbox{\ if }N\leq 2\right).

Note that for
W(x)=\frac{c}{|x|^{2}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\sigma_{W}=-\infty
if
c>C_{*}(N)
and
\sigma_{W}>-\infty
if
c\leq C_{*}(N).
Roughly speaking, in
[
5
]
the existence of positive solutions, when
\sigma_{W}>-\infty
and for
\sigma_{W}=-\infty
, was obtained; further the authors proved that there is no globally defined pointwise solution that is exponentially bounded in time. This is a much weaker conclusion than the instantaneous blowup (IBU).

In the
\mathbb{H}^{N}
setting, the authors in
[
15
]
used the method of
[
5
]
and proved nonexistence of globally defined (in
(x,t)
) positive solutions that grow at most exponentially for
c>C^{*}(N).
But the question of (IBU) remained open until now.

2.
Notations and preliminaries

The Heisenberg group and its sub-Laplacian play a crucial role in several branches of harmonic analysis, complex geometry and partial differential equations (see e.g.
[
8
,
11
,
19
,
20
]
; see also the survey papers
[
18
,
21
]
).

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{N},N\in\mathbb{N}
, is the stratified Lie group of step two

(2.1)

\ (\mathbb{R}^{2N+1},\circ,D_{\lambda}).

If we denote the generic point of
\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
by
w=(z,l)=(x,y,l)
, with
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
l\in\mathbb{R}
, the composition law
\circ
is defined by

(x,y,l)\circ(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},l^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},l+l^{\prime}+2(x^{\prime}\cdot y-y^{\prime}\cdot x)),

where
x\cdot y
denotes the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In (
2.1
),
D_{\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
denotes the anisotropic dilation

D_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2N+1},D_{\lambda}(z,l)=(\lambda z,\lambda^{2}l).

The family
(D_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
is a group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, that is,

D_{\lambda}((z,l)\circ(z^{\prime},l^{\prime}))=(D_{\lambda}(z,l)\circ D_{\lambda}(z^{\prime},l^{\prime})).

The real number

Q:=2N+2

is called the
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{H}^{N}
since it appears in the formula

|D_{\lambda}(A)|=\lambda^{Q}|A|,

where
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}
is a Lebesgue measurable set and
|A|
stands for the Lebesgue measure of
A
.

A basis for the Lie algebra of left invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is given by

X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}+2y_{j}\partial_{l},\quad Y_{j}=\partial_{y_{j}}-2x_{j}\partial_{l},\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

One easily calculates that

(2.2)

\displaystyle[X_{j},X_{k}]=[Y_{j},Y_{k}]=0\ \mbox{for every}\ j,k=1,\ldots,N,\ \mbox{and}\ [X_{j},Y_{k}]=-4\delta_{jk}\partial_{l}.

These are the canonical commutation relations of Quantum Mechanics for position and momentum, whence
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is called the Heisenberg group.

The subelliptic gradient is the gradient taken with respect to the horizontal directions
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})
and the
sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
is

\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2})=\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{H}},

and it can be explicitly also written as

\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}=\varDelta_{z}+4|z|^{2}\partial_{l}^{2}+4\partial_{l}T,

where

\varDelta_{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}+\partial_{y_{j}}^{2})

and

T=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(y_{j}\partial_{x_{j}}-x_{j}\partial_{y_{j}}).

From (
2.2
) it immediately follows that

\mathrm{rank\ }\mathrm{Lie\ }(X_{1},\ldots,X_{N},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{N})(z,l)=2N+1

at any point
(z,l)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+1}.
Then, by a celebrated theorem of Hörmander,
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
is hypoelliptic, that is, every distributional solution of
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u=f
is smooth
whenever
f
is smooth.

The operator
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
is left translation invariant on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
and
D_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree two. Moreover
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
has a fundamental solution (with a pole at the origin) given by

(2.3)

\gamma(w)=c_{N}\left(\frac{1}{d(w)}\right)^{Q-2}\!\!\!\!\!\!=\,c_{N}\left(\frac{1}{d(w)}\right)^{2N}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!,\ \quad w\neq(0,0),

where

(2.4)

d(w)=(|z|^{4}+l^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\,\hbox{\ for }w=(z,l)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}

defines the metric
\rho(w,\tilde{w}):=d(\tilde{w}^{-1}\circ w)
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, and
\tilde{w}^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
\tilde{w}
in the group
\mathbb{H}^{N}
.

In the following lemma we summarize some properties of
d
and its gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}
which one can obtain by simple computations, see
[
4
, Proposition 5.4.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
d(w)=(|z|^{4}+l^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}},\,w=(z,l)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d(w)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|z|^{2}(|z|^{4}+l^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}d(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q-1}{d(w)}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}d(w)|^{2},

(2.5)

\displaystyle-\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}d^{-\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cd^{-\alpha}(w)\frac{|z|^{2}}{|z|^{4}+l^{2}}

for
w\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, where
C:=\alpha(Q-2-\alpha)=\alpha(2N-\alpha)
. So,
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}d^{-\alpha}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
if and only if
2N-\alpha>0
.

It is known that the left translation invariance of
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
implies that the semigroup
e^{t\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}
is given by a right convolution

(2.6)

e^{t\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}f(w)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}f(\tilde{w})p_{t}(\tilde{w}^{-1}\circ w)\,d\tilde{w},\quad t>0,\,w\in\mathbb{H}^{N},

where
(w,t)\mapsto p_{t}(w)
is the fundamental solution of
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}\right)u=0
. Hence, by hypoellipticity,
p_{t}(w)
is a
C^{\infty}
function on
\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,\infty)
and
\|p_{t}\|_{1}=1
. Moreover,
p_{t}
satisfies the following Gaussian estimates, cf.
[
22
, Theorem IV.4.2 and Theorem IV.4.3]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The heat kernel
p_{t}
satisfies

Ct^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left(-c\frac{d^{2}(w)}{t}\right)\leq p_{t}(w)\leq C_{\varepsilon}t^{-\frac{Q}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{-d^{2}(w)}{4(1+\varepsilon)t}\right)

for some positive constants
C,\,c,\,C_{\varepsilon}
, any
\varepsilon>0,\,w\in\mathbb{H}^{N}
and
t>0
.

3.
The main results

In this section we make the following hypotheses.

Hypotheses 3.1
.

•

0\leq V\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N});

•

0\leq f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T));

•

0\leq u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(or more generally
u_{0}
can be a positive finite Radon measure).

We consider the problem

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u+Vu+f&\text{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)),\\
{\rm esslim}_{t\to 0^{+}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(w,t)\psi(w)\,dw=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u_{0}(w)\psi(w)\,dw&\text{ for all }\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),\\
u\geq 0&\text{ on }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
\\
Vu\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)).\end{array}\right.

Here
{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{H}^{N})=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(resp.
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T))=C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T))
) with the usual topologies and
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{H}^{N})
(resp.
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{T}:=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T))
) is its dual space.
We also consider the approximating problem

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u_{n}+V_{n}u_{n}+f_{n}&\text{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{T},\\
\\
{\rm lim}_{t\to 0^{+}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u_{n}(w,t)\psi(w)\,dw=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u_{0}(w)\psi(w)\,dw,&\mbox{ for all }\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}).\\
\end{array}\right.

Here

\displaystyle f_{n}=\min\{f,n\},

\displaystyle V_{n}\in L^{\infty},\,0\leq V_{n}\leq V,\,V_{n}\uparrow V\,\,a.e.

By the variation of parameters formula, (
3.2
) has a unique bounded nonnegative solution obtained by solving the integral equation

(3.3)

u_{n}(t)=e^{t\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}V_{n}(\cdot)u_{n}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}f_{n}(s)\,ds,

where
\left(e^{t\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is the semigroup generated by
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}
on
\mathbb{H}^{N}
.
We note that
V_{n}
is a bounded multiplication operator on
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{H}^{N}\right)
for all
p\in[1,+\infty)
.
Since
\{V_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence, clearly
\{u_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence, as well.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose there is a
(w_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w_{0},t_{0})<\infty
. Then (
3.1
)
has a nonnegative solution on
\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T_{0})
for all
0<T_{0}<t_{0}
given by

(3.4)

u(w,t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w,t)\quad a.e.\text{ in }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T_{0}).

Moreover, if (
3.1
) has a nonnegative solution in
\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w,t)<\infty
a.e. in
\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
3.1
), then
u_{n}\leq u
for all
n
, so

\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{n}(w,t)\leq u(w,t)
a.e. in
\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)
. This establishes the last part of the proposition.

For the main part, we start by considering

U_{n}=e^{t}u_{n},\quad t>0.

Then

\frac{\partial U_{n}}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}U_{n}+(V_{n}+1)U_{n}+e^{t}f_{n},

and, using the variation of parameters formula, we obtain

(3.5)

e^{t_{0}}u_{n}(w_{0},t_{0})\geq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{s}(e^{(t_{0}-s)\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}(V_{n}+1)u_{n}(s))(w_{0})\,ds,\quad(w_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),

since
e^{\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}}u_{0}\geq 0
and
f_{n}\geq 0.

On the other hand, it follows from the Gaussian estimates in Theorem
2.2
that

The inverse square potential in the Euclidean case of

is

and the critical constant is the best constant

The multiplication operator

and the Laplacian both have the same scaling property, namely

for

or
\mathcal{L}=\varDelta
, where

, for

defines a unitary operator on

In the case of the Heisenberg group

\tilde{V}^{*}_{c}(w)=\dfrac{c|z|^{2}}{|z|^{2}+l^{2}}

for

with the best constant being

, for all

Both
\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}
and multiplication by

scale in the same way. Let

\tilde{U}(\lambda)f(z,l)=\lambda^{N+1}f(\lambda z,\lambda^{2}l);

\tilde{U}(\lambda)

for all

As in the Euclidean case, the critical potential is

near the origin. That is, by localizing to the unit ball

with respect to the metric

Lemma A.1
.

For

and

, define

We use an induction argument. Assume (
A.1
) is true for

We will show

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}u+V(\cdot)u+B(\cdot)u+f&\text{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)),\\
\\
{\rm esslim}_{t\to 0^{+}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u(w,t)\psi(w)\,dw=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u_{0}(w)\psi(w)\,dw&\text{ }\forall\ \psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),\\
\\
u\geq 0&\text{ on }\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
\\
Vu\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T)),\end{array}\right.

has a solution.

Proof.

Let
u_{n}
be the
solution of (
3.2
). We know that
u_{n}\uparrow u
,
u
being a solution of (
3.1
).
Suppose that
v_{n}
solves

(A.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}v_{n}+\left(V_{n}(\cdot)+B(\cdot)\right)v_{n}+f_{n}&\text{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{T},\\
\\
{\rm lim}_{t\to 0^{+}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}v_{n}(w,t)\psi(w)\,dw=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}u_{0}(w)\psi(w)\,dw,&\forall\psi\in{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{H}^{N}),\\
\end{array}\right.

where
f_{n}=\min\{f,n\}
and
V_{n}=\min\{V,n\}
. Fix
\lambda\geq\|B\|_{\infty}
, and consider

U_{n}=e^{\lambda t}u_{n}.

So
U_{n}
satisfies

\frac{\partial U_{n}}{\partial t}=\varDelta_{\mathbb{H}}U_{n}+(V_{n}+\lambda)U_{n}+e^{t}f_{n}.

By the Maximum Principle we have

v_{n}(w,t)\leq U_{n}(w,t)\leq e^{\lambda t}u(w,t)\,\hbox{\ for a.e. }(w,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T).

Clearly
\{v_{n}\}
is an increasing sequence and since
u,Vu\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T))
, it follows by the Monotone Convergence
theorem that
v_{n}\uparrow v
and
(V_{n}+B)v_{n}\uparrow(V+B)v
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{H}^{N}\times(0,T))
, and
v
gives a solution of
(
A.6
).
∎

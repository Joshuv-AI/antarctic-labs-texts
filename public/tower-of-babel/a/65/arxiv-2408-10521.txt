Rationality of the multivariate growth series for algebraic sets of virtually abelian groups
Yusuke Nakamura

Abstract
. We prove the rationality of the multivariate relative growth series for algebraic sets of virtually abelian groups, which had been conjectured by Evetts and Levine.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 20F65; Secondary 05A15, 05E16

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a group,
S\subset G
be a finite subset, and

\omega:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be a positive integer weight.
Let
S^{*}
be the set of all words from
S
.
For a word
\sigma=s_{1}\cdots s_{\ell}\in S^{*}
, we define
\omega(\sigma):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\omega(s_{i})
.
For
g\in G
, we define
\omega(g)
as the smallest
\omega(\sigma)
for all words
\sigma
that represent
g
.
The
growth series

\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t)
of
G
with respect to
S
and
\omega
is defined as a formal power series

\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{G,S,\omega}(i)t^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]],

where
f_{G,S,\omega}(i):=\#\{g\in G\mid\omega(g)=i\}
.
In
[
2
]
, Benson proved that the growth series
\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t)
is a rational function when
G
is a virtually abelian group.

An
n
-dimensional periodic graph

(\Gamma,L)
is a pair of a directed graph
\Gamma
(that may have loops and multiple edges) and a free abelian group
L
of rank
n
such that
L
freely acts on
\Gamma
and its quotient graph
\Gamma/L
is finite (see Definition
2.2
).
For a vertex
x_{0}
of
\Gamma
,
the
growth series

\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
of
\Gamma
with respect to
x_{0}
is defined by

\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i)t^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]],

where
f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i)
is defined as the number of vertices of
\Gamma
whose distance from
x_{0}
is
i
.
The sequence
(f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i))_{i}
is studied in both areas of crystallography and combinatorics (cf.
[
11
]
*Introduction).
Furthermore, this is also studied in relation to the Ehrhart theory (cf.
[
16
,
15
]
).

In
[
13
]
, Grosse-Kunstleve, Brunner and Sloane conjectured that the growth series
\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
of periodic graphs
\Gamma
are always rational functions.
In
[
17
]
, the author, Sakamoto, Mase and Nakagawa proved that this conjecture is true (see Theorem
2.3
).
This result is a generalization of Benson’s theorem since the Cayley graphs of virtually abelian groups are periodic graphs (see Remark
2.7
).
In the proof of Theorem
2.3
, they use the commutative monoid theory, and the proof is essentially different from Benson’s original proof of Theorem
2.6
, where he uses his theory of “polyhedral sets”.

The rationality of the growth series
\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t)
is also proved for hyperbolic groups in
[
4
,
12
]
and for the integer Heisenberg group in
[
8
]
.
Furthermore, some variants of growth series, such as the conjugacy growth series and the relative growth series, have been studied for many groups (cf.
[
14
,
7
,
1
,
6
,
10
]
).

In
[
9
]
, Evetts and Levine generalize Benson’s theorem to the univariate relative growth series for algebraic sets of virtually abelian groups.
For a positive integer
d
, a subset
U\subset G^{d}
is called
algebraic
if
U
is the set of solutions of a finite system of equations with
d
variables whose coefficients are in
G
(see Definition
4.1
).
The
univariate (relative) growth series

\mathbb{S}^{\rm uni}_{U,S,\omega}(t)
of
U
with respect to
S
and
\omega
is defined by

\mathbb{S}^{\rm uni}_{U,S,\omega}(t):=\sum_{{\bf x}\in U}t^{\omega({\bf x})}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t]],

where
\omega({\bf x}):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\omega(x_{i})
.
Furthermore, the
multivariate (relative) growth series

\mathbb{S}_{U,S,\omega}({\bf z})
is defined by

\mathbb{S}_{U,S,\omega}({\bf z}):=\sum_{{\bf x}\in U}z_{1}^{\omega(x_{1})}z_{2}^{\omega(x_{2})}\cdots z_{d}^{\omega(x_{d})}\in\mathbb{Z}[[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]].

In
[
9
]
, for any algebraic sets of any virtually abelian groups, the univariate growth series are proved to be rational functions (
[
9
]
*Theorem 4.3), and furthermore, the multivariate growth series are proved to be holonomic functions (
[
9
]
*Corollary 4.21).
However, the rationality of the multivariate growth series remained a conjecture (
[
9
]
*Conjecture 5.1).
The purpose of this paper is to give an affirmative answer to this conjecture.

Theorem 1.1

(
=
Theorem
4.4
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group,
S\subset G
a finite subset, and
\omega
a positive integer weight.
Then, for any algebraic subset
U\subset G^{d}
of
G
, its multivariate growth series
\mathbb{S}_{Y,S,\omega}({\bf z})
is a rational function.

Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of Theorem
3.5
, which is the corresponding theorem for periodic graphs.
The idea of the proof of Theorem
3.5
is to use an algebraic structure on the
graded growth set

B:=\{(i,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}\mid d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq i\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma},

which is studied in
[
17
]
(see Appendix
A
).
In
[
15
]
, further structure of
B
, which is not used in this paper, is studied as an analogy to the Ehrhart ring.
We believe that these ideas have potential for further applications to similar studies of virtually abelian groups.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce some notations on graphs and groups, and we also summarize facts on commutative monoid theory which will be used in Section
3
.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
3.5
.
In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
4.4
.
In Appendix
A
, we explain the algebraic structure of the graded growth set
B
proved in
[
17
]
.
The proofs in Appendix
A
are essentially the same as those in
[
17
]
, but streamlined for mathematicians.

2.1.
Growth series of periodic graphs

In this paper, a
graph
means a directed weighted graph which may have loops and multiple edges.
More precisely, a graph
\Gamma=(V_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma},s_{\Gamma},t_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma})

consists of the set
V_{\Gamma}
of vertices, the set
E_{\Gamma}
of edges,
the source function
s_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to V_{\Gamma}
,
the target function
t_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to V_{\Gamma}
, and
the weight function
w_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We abbreviate
s_{\Gamma}
,
t_{\Gamma}
and
w_{\Gamma}
to
s
,
t
and
w

when no confusion can arise.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph.
A
walk

p
is a sequence
e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{\ell}
of edges
e_{i}\in V_{\Gamma}
satisfying
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
.
We define the
weight

w(p)
of
p
by
w(p):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w(e_{i})
.
For
x,y\in V_{\Gamma}
, we define
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
as the smallest weight
w(p)
of any walk
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{\ell}
with
x=s(e_{1})
and
y=t(e_{\ell})
.
Then, for
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is defined as

f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i):=\#\{y\in V_{\Gamma}\mid d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)=i\}.

The sequence
(f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i))_{i}
is called the
growth sequence
of
\Gamma
with respect to
x_{0}
.
The
growth series

\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
of
\Gamma
with respect to
x_{0}
is defined as its generating function

\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i)t^{i}.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
16
]
)
.

(1)

Let
\Gamma
be a graph, and let
G
be a group.
A
G
-action
on
\Gamma
means
G
-actions on
V_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\Gamma}
which preserve the edge relations and the weight function, i.e., for any
u\in G
and
e\in E_{\Gamma}
, we have

s_{\Gamma}(u(e))=u(s_{\Gamma}(e)),\quad t_{\Gamma}(u(e))=u(t_{\Gamma}(e)),\quad w_{\Gamma}(u(e))=w_{\Gamma}(e).

(2)

Let
n
be a positive integer.
An
n
-dimensional periodic graph

(\Gamma,L)
is
a free abelian group
L\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of rank
n
and a graph
\Gamma
with an
L
-action satisfying the following two conditions:

•

The
L
-actions on
V_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\Gamma}
are free, i.e., no non-trivial element of
G
fixes an element of
V_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\Gamma}
.

•

Their quotient sets
V_{\Gamma}/L
and
E_{\Gamma}/L
are finite sets.

The growth series of periodic graphs are known to be rational functions.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
17
]
*Theorem 2.2)
.

Let
(\Gamma,L)
be a periodic graph, and let
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
.
Then, its growth sequence
(f_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(i))_{i}
is quasi-polynomial type.
In particular, its growth series
\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
is a rational function.

2.2.
Growth series of groups

Let
G
be a group,
S\subset G
a finite subset, and

\omega:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
a positive integer weight.
Let
S^{*}
be the set of all words from
S
.
For a word
\sigma=s_{1}\cdots s_{\ell}\in S^{*}
, we define

\omega(\sigma):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\omega(s_{i})
.
For
g\in G
, we define
\omega(g)
as the smallest
\omega(\sigma)

for all words
\sigma
that represent
g
.
By convention, we define
\omega(g)=\infty
if
g
cannot be represented by
S
.
Then, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

f_{G,S,\omega}(i)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is defined as

f_{G,S,\omega}(i):=\#\{g\in G\mid\omega(g)=i\}.

The sequence
(f_{G,S,\omega}(i))_{i}
is called the
growth sequence
of
G
with respect to
S
and
\omega
.
The
growth series

\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma,S,\omega}(t)
of
G
with respect to
S
and
\omega
is its generating function

\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{G,S,\omega}(i)t^{i}.

Remark 2.4
.

We need not to assume that
S
generates
G
even in Theorems
2.6
and
4.4
.

Remark 2.5
.

The (right)
Cayley graph

\Gamma=\Gamma_{G,S,\omega}
of
G
with respect to
S
and
\omega
is defined as follows:

•

V_{\Gamma}:=G
.

•

E_{\Gamma}:=G\times S
.

•

For
e=(g,s)\in E_{\Gamma}
, we define

s_{\Gamma}(e)=g,\qquad t_{\Gamma}(e)=gs,\qquad w_{\Gamma}(e)=\omega(s).

Then, the number
f_{G,S,\omega}(i)
defined above
coincides with the growth sequence
f_{\Gamma,1_{G}}(i)
of the graph
\Gamma
defined in Section
2.1
.

A group
G
is called
virtually abelian
if there exists an abelian subgroup
H
of
G
with finite index
[G:H]<\infty
.
In
[
2
]
, Benson proved that the growth series of virtually abelian groups are rational functions.

Theorem 2.6

(Benson
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group,
S\subset G
a finite subset, and
\omega
a positive integer weight.
Then, the sequence
(f_{G,S,\omega}(i))_{i}
is of quasi-polynomial type.
In particular, its growth series
\mathbb{S}_{G,S,\omega}(t)
is a rational function.

Remark 2.7
.

When
G
is a finitely generated virtually abelian group, we may take a free abelian subgroup
L<G
of finite rank and of finite index.
Since
L
is a subgroup of
G
, the left
L
-action on
G
induces a free
L
-action on the Cayley graph
\Gamma=\Gamma_{G,S,\omega}
. Furthermore, since
L
is a subgroup of
G
with finite index, the quotient graph
\Gamma/L
is a finite graph.
Therefore, the Cayley graph
\Gamma
is a periodic graph.
Hence, Theorem
2.6
can be seen as a special case of Theorem
2.3
.

Benson’s original proof of Theorem
2.6
uses his theory of “polyhedral sets”.
On the other hand, the proof of Theorem
2.3
uses the theory of commutative monoid explained in Subsection
2.3
, and the argument is quite different from Benson’s argument.

2.3.
Some facts in commutative monoid theory

The key tool of this paper is the rationality of the Hilbert series of finitely generated modules (Theorem
2.9
).
This is an important theorem in the context of algebraic geometry and commutative algebra, but it is also useful in enumerative combinatorics.

A commutative monoid
M
is called
integral
if it has the cancellation property (i.e., for any
a,b,c\in M
,
a+b=a+c
implies
b=c
).
For a commutative monoid
M
, an
M
-module
just means a set with an
M
-action.
An
M
-module
X
is called
finitely generated
if
X=\bigcup_{x\in X^{\prime}}(M+x)
holds for some finite subset
X^{\prime}\subset X
.
An
M
-module
X
is called a
free
M
-module
if there exists a subset
X^{\prime}\subset X
such that any
x\in X
can be uniquely expressed as
x=a+y
with
a\in M
and
y\in X^{\prime}
.
For more detail on commutative monoid and its module theory, refer to
[
3
]
and
[
17
]
.

Theorem
2.8
(1) below is well-known in the theory of commutative monoid (cf.
[
18
]
*Ch. I, Theorem 2.1.17.6,
[
3
]
*Corollary 2.11).
Theorem
2.8
(2) is proved in
[
17
]
*Theorem A12.
We note that Theorem
2.8
(2) can be seen as a discrete version of Motzkin’s theorem (
[
3
]
*Theorem 1.27).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
M
be an integral commutative monoid, and let
X
be a free
M
-module.
Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
be finitely generated submonoids of
M
.
Let
Y_{i}\subset X
be a finitely generated
N_{i}
-submodule for
i=1,2
.
Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

N_{1}\cap N_{2}
is a finitely generated monoid.

(2)

Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
is a finitely generated
(N_{1}\cap N_{2})
-module.

The following theorem due to Hilbert and Serre is well-known in the theory of commutative algebra.
The series
H_{Y}
is called the
Hilbert series
.

Theorem 2.9

(cf.
[
3
]
*Theorem 6.37)
.

Let
d
be a positive integer.
Let
M
be a commutative monoid, and let
X
be an
M
-module.
Let
N^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{d}\times M
be a finite subset, and
let
N\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times M
be the submonoid generated by
N^{\prime}
.
Let
Y\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times X
be a finitely generated
N
-submodule.
We define a function
h_{Y}
by

h_{Y}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0};\quad{\bf a}\mapsto\#\{x\in X\mid({\bf a},x)\in Y\}

Then, the series

H_{Y}({\bf t}):=\sum_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}h_{Y}({\bf a}){\bf t}^{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}]]

is a rational function.
Here,
{\bf t}^{\bf a}
denotes
t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{d}^{a_{d}}
for
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
.

3.
Rationality of the growth series of periodic graphs

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
3.5
.
Throughout this section, we fix an
n
-dimensional periodic graph
(\Gamma,L)
.
We also fix
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
.

We define a
graded growth set

B
of
\Gamma
with respect to
x_{0}
by

B:=\{(i,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}\mid d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq i\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}.

Note that
V_{\Gamma}
has a free
L
-module structure by the free
L
-action on
V_{\Gamma}
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}
has a free
\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L\bigr)
-module structure.
The key point of the proof of Theorem
3.5
is to use the following algebraic structure of
B
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

For each subset
S\subset V_{\Gamma}/L
, there exist subsets
M_{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L
and

X_{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}
with the following conditions:

(1)

We have
B=\bigcup_{S\in 2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}}X_{S}
.

(2)

Each
M_{S}
is a finitely generated submonoid of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L
.

(3)

Each
X_{S}
is a finitely generated
M_{S}
-module.

In Appendix
A
, we give constructions of
M_{S}
and
X_{S}
, and we also give a proof of Theorem
3.1
for readers’ convenience.
In this section, we fix
M_{S}
and
X_{S}
that satisfy the properties (1)-(3).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
d
be a positive integer.
We identify the two sets
\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L\bigr)^{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times L^{d}

(resp.
\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}\bigr)^{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times V_{\Gamma}^{d}
) by the map

\bigl((a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{d},b_{d})\bigr)\mapsto\bigl((a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}),(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d})\bigr).

(1)

For a subset
Y\subset V_{\Gamma}^{d}
,
we define
B_{Y}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times V_{\Gamma}^{d}
by

B_{Y}:=B^{d}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times Y\bigr).

(2)

For
{\bf S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})\in\bigl(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}\bigr)^{d}
, we define
M_{\bf S}
and
X_{\bf S}
by

\displaystyle M_{\bf S}

\displaystyle:=M_{S_{1}}\times\cdots\times M_{S_{d}}\subset\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L\bigr)^{d},

\displaystyle X_{\bf S}

\displaystyle:=X_{S_{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{S_{d}}\subset\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}\bigr)^{d}.

For subsets
N\subset L^{d}
and
Y\subset V_{\Gamma}^{d}
,
we also define

M_{N,\bf S}:=M_{\bf S}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times N\bigr),\qquad X_{Y,\bf S}:=X_{\bf S}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times Y\bigr).

In the following lemma, we assume that
N
is a submonoid of
L^{d}
.
Since
V_{\Gamma}^{d}
is an
L^{d}
-module,
V_{\Gamma}^{d}
naturally has an
N
-module structure.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d
be a positive integer.
Let
N
be a finitely generated submonoid of
L^{d}
.
Let
Y
be a finitely generated
N
-submodule of
V_{\Gamma}^{d}
.
For any subset
\Lambda\subset\bigl(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}\bigr)^{d}
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}M_{N,\bf S}
is a finitely generated monoid.

(2)

\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}X_{Y,\bf S}
is a finitely generated
\bigl(\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}M_{N,\bf S}\bigr)
-module.

Proof.

For each
{\bf S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})\in\bigl(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}\bigr)^{d}
,
the product
M_{\bf S}=M_{S_{1}}\times\cdots\times M_{S_{d}}
is a finitely generated monoid since
M_{S_{1}},\ldots,M_{S_{d}}
are finitely generated monoids by Theorem
3.1
(2).
Since
N
is a finitely generated monoid,
M_{N,\bf S}=M_{\bf S}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times N\bigr)
is also a finitely generated monoid by Theorem
2.8
(1).
Therefore, the intersection
\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}M_{N,\bf S}
is also a finitely generated monoid again by Theorem
2.8
(1).

For each
{\bf S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{d})\in\bigl(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}\bigr)^{d}
,
the product
X_{\bf S}=X_{S_{1}}\times\cdots\times X_{S_{d}}
is a finitely generated
M_{\bf S}
-module since each
X_{S_{i}}
is a finitely generated
M_{S_{i}}
-module by Theorem
3.1
(3).
Since
Y
is a finitely generated
N
-module,
X_{Y,\bf S}=X_{\bf S}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\times Y\bigr)
is also a finitely generated
M_{N,\bf S}
-module by Theorem
2.8
(2).
Therefore, the intersection
\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}X_{Y,\bf S}
is a finitely generated
\bigl(\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}M_{N,\bf S}\bigr)
-module again by Theorem
2.8
(2).
∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
d
be a positive integer, and let

be a subset.
For
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}

\displaystyle B_{Y,\bf a}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in Y\ \big|\ \text{$d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y_{i})\leq a_{i}$ for each $1\leq i\leq d$}\bigr\},

\displaystyle S_{Y,\bf a}

is the vertex of

Then, we define the

\mathbb{B}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z}):=\sum_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}\#B_{Y,\bf a}{\bf z}^{\bf a},\qquad\mathbb{S}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z}):=\sum_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}\#S_{Y,\bf a}{\bf z}^{\bf a}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
d
be a positive integer.
Let
N
be a finitely generated submonoid of
L^{d}
.
Let

be a finitely generated

-submodule of

.
Then the multivariate growth series
\mathbb{B}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})
and
\mathbb{S}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})
of

are rational functions.

Proof.

Since we have
\mathbb{S}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})=(1-z_{1})\cdots(1-z_{d})\mathbb{B}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})
,
it is sufficient to show that
\mathbb{B}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})
is a rational function.

For each
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
,
we have
B_{Y,\bf a}=B_{Y}\cap\bigl(\{{\bf a}\}\times V_{\Gamma}^{d}\bigr)
.
Since we have
B=\bigcup_{S\in 2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}}X_{S}
by Theorem
3.1
(1), we have

B_{Y}=\bigcup_{{\bf S}\in(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L})^{d}}X_{Y,{\bf S}}.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, in order to show the rationality of

\mathbb{B}_{Y,x_{0}}({\bf z})=\sum_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}\#\Biggl(\Biggl(\bigcup_{{\bf S}\in(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L})^{d}}X_{Y,{\bf S}}\Biggr)\cap\bigl(\{{\bf a}\}\times V_{\Gamma}^{d}\bigr)\Biggr){\bf z}^{\bf a},

it is sufficient to show the rationality of the series

H_{\Lambda}({\bf z}):=\sum_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}\#\left(\left(\bigcap_{{\bf S}\in\Lambda}X_{Y,\bf S}\right)\cap\bigl(\{{\bf a}\}\times V_{\Gamma}^{d}\bigr)\right){\bf z}^{\bf a}

for each subset
\Lambda\subset\bigl(2^{V_{\Gamma}/L}\bigr)^{d}
.
By Theorem
2.9
, the rationality of each

follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Following

In

*Corollary 4.16, Evetts and Levine prove that algebraic subsets of virtually abelian groups are “coset-wise polyhedral” (see

By using the language of commutative monoids and modules, their result can be described as follows.

Proposition 4.3

(
[

]

Let

By Corollary 4.16 in
[

(see

]

Proposition 3.11 and Lemma 3.10 in

show that any polyhedral set

We prove the rationality of multivariate growth series of algebraic sets of finitely generated virtually abelian groups.
In

Theorem 4.4

Abstract
In this paper, the works on the analytical volume analysis for the controllable regions of the linear discrete-time (LDT) systems in papers [ 17 ] and [ 16 ] are discussed further and a new theorem on the analytical computing for the finite-time controllability zonotope (controllable region) of LDT systems are proven. And then, three analytical factors describing the control capability of the systems are deconstructed successfully from the analytical volume expression of the controllable region. Finally, the theorem is generalized to three cases: the narrow controllable region, the matrix A A with n n negative eigenvalues, the linear continuous-time systems.

The zonotope spanned by the

V_{n}\left(C_{n}(Z_{m})\right)=\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega_{1,m}^{n}}\left|\det\left[z_{i_{1}},z_{i_{2}},\cdots,z_{i_{n}}\right]\right|

The definition of the controllable region

In the dynamics analysis and control theory fields, the LDT systems can be modeled as follows

For many practical engineering systems, the input variables

\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1

Based on the above normalized expression, the controllable region can be defined clearly. The so-called controllable region here is a broad appellation and, in reality, can be divided into two cases, narrow controllable region and reachable region.
The
N

-stpes bounded inputs and it also can be called as the recover region. Correspondingly, the

-steps reachable region is the state region in which all states can be reached from the origin by the

[

For the state controllabilty analysis, the

The

\displaystyle R_{N}^{d}(A)

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}A^{k}Bu_{k},\quad\left\|u_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{rN}c_{i}p_{i},\quad\forall c_{i}\in[0,1],i=\overline{1,rN}\right\}

According to the above definition, the controllable region of the LDT systems is a special zonotope generated by the vectors of the matrix

As discusing above, simlilar to the definition of the braoad controllable region

Definition 3

\displaystyle R_{N}^{c}(A)

As pointed in paper

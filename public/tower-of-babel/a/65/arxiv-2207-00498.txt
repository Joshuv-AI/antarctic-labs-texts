Energy estimates for seminodal solutions to an elliptic system with mixed couplings
Mónica Clapp, and, Mayra Soares

Abstract
We study the system of semilinear elliptic equations − Δ u i + u i = ∑ j = 1 ℓ β i ​ j | u j | p | u i | p − 2 u i , u i ∈ H 1 ( ℝ N ) , i = 1 , … , ℓ , -\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\qquad u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell, where N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 , 1 < p < N N − 2 1<p<\frac{N}{N-2} , and the matrix ( β i ​ j ) (\beta_{ij}) is symmetric and admits a block decomposition such that the entries within each block are positive or zero and all other entries are negative. We provide simple conditions on ( β i ​ j ) (\beta_{ij}) , which guarantee the existence of fully nontrivial solutions, i.e., solutions all of whose components are nontrivial. We establish existence of fully nontrivial solutions to the system having a prescribed combination of positive and nonradial sign-changing components, and we give an upper bound for their energy when the system has at most two blocks. We derive the existence of solutions with positive and nonradial sign-changing components to the system of singularly perturbed elliptic equations − ε 2 Δ u i + u i = ∑ j = 1 ℓ β i ​ j | u j | p | u i | p − 2 u i , u i ∈ H 0 1 ( B 1 ( 0 ) ) , i = 1 , … , ℓ , -\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\qquad u_{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{1}(0)),\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell, in the unit ball, exhibiting two different kinds of asymptotic behavior: solutions whose components decouple as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 , and solutions whose components remain coupled all the way up to their limit. Introduction

Consider the system of nonlinear elliptic equations

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
N\geq 4
,
\beta_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
1<p<\frac{N}{N-2}
.

This system arises as a model for various physical phenomena, for instance, in the study of standing waves for a mixture of Bose-Einstein condensates of
\ell
different hyperfine states which overlap in space; see
[
11
]
. The coefficient
\beta_{ij}
represents the interaction force between the components
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
. It can be attractive (
\beta_{ij}>0
), repulsive (
\beta_{ij}<0
) or null (
\beta_{ij}=0
). We make the following assumptions:

(B_{1})

The matrix
(\beta_{ij})
is symmetric and admits a block decomposition as follows: For some
1\leq q\leq\ell
there exist
0=\ell_{0}<\ell_{1}<\dots<\ell_{q-1}<\ell_{q}=\ell
such that, if we set

\displaystyle I_{h}:=\{i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}:\ell_{h-1}<i\leq\ell_{h}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{h}:=I_{h}\times I_{h},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{K}_{h}:=\big\{(i,j)\in I_{h}\times I_{k}:k\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\smallsetminus\{h\}\big\},

then
\beta_{ii}>0,\ \beta_{ij}\geq 0\text{ for all }(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h},
and
\beta_{ij}<0\text{ for all }(i,j)\in\mathcal{K}_{h},\quad h=1,\ldots,q
.

(B_{2})

For each
h=1,\ldots,q

and whose set of edges is
E_{h}:=\{\{i,j\}:i,j\in I_{h},\ i\neq j,\ \beta_{ij}>0\}
is connected.

According to the decomposition given by
(B_{1})
, a solution
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
to (
1.1
) may be written in block-form as

\boldsymbol{u}=(\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{q})\qquad\text{with \ }\overline{u}_{h}=(u_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,u_{\ell_{h}}).

\boldsymbol{u}
is said to be

is different from zero and it is said to be

if every component

is different from zero. We shall call it

if at least one component in each block

is nontrivial.

If

the system is called

and it is called
competitive
if

. These kinds of systems have been studied extensively. Systems with mixed couplings were already investigated in the seminal paper by Lin and Wei
[
13

[

Our main interest is to study the existence of fully nontrivial solutions to the system (
1.1
) with a prescribed set of positive and sign-changing components - referred to in the literature as

We stress that obtaining an upper bound for the least energy of a solution to the system to (
1.1
) is interesting even in the case when all components are positive. Indeed, as shown by Lin and Wei in
[
13
]
a positive least energy solution does not exist when

; see also Proposition

below. On the other hand, in
[

Sirakov established the existence of solutions with positive radial components, while sign-changing and seminodal solutions have been exhibited, e.g., in

We write

for the usual norm in the Sobolev space

\mu_{h}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\overline{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{h}}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

To prove this result we introduce suitable symmetries that produce, by construction, a change of sign on some of the components. Then, using concentration compactness techniques, we carry out a careful analysis of the effect of the symmetries on the behavior of symmetric minimizing sequences for the system (
1.1
); see Theorem
2.7
. This approach was taken in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
to establish existence of solutions with a prescribed number of positive and sign-changing components for the competitive system (
1.1
) with

. However, the symmetries used there are too restrictive and do not allow energy bounds to be obtained. In this paper we consider general symmetries, admitting finite orbits. This makes it more difficult to show existence, due to the lack of compactness of the variational functional. On the other hand, the compactness condition given in Corollary 2.8 immediately gives energy estimates. But showing that this condition is fulfilled requires, in turn, precise knowledge about the asymptotic decay of the components of the system. This information does not seem to be available so far. This is why the upper energy bounds in Theorem
1.1
are given only for a system with at most two blocks; see Remark
5.2
.

Regarding our assumptions on the matrix

but not the other way around. The constant

Proposition 2.5
.

The following statements hold true:

(i)

c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\geq\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}
.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Fix}(G)\neq 0
, then

c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}
.

(iii)

If
q\geq 2
and
c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, then
c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
is not attained.

Proof.

(i):
Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{q})\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. As
\beta_{ij}<0
for
(i,j)\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
, we have

0<\|\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\int_{\Omega}\beta_{ij}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}\qquad\text{for every \ }h=1,\ldots,q.

Hence, there exist
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{q})\in(0,\infty)^{q}
such that
s_{h}\overline{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}
. Setting
\boldsymbol{s}:=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{q})
and applying Lemma
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}(s_{h}\overline{u}_{h})\leq\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{s}\boldsymbol{u})\leq\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u}).

Hence,
c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\geq c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{1}+\cdots+c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{q}
, as claimed.

(ii):
As
\mathrm{Fix}(G)\neq 0
we may choose
q
different points
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{q}\in\mathrm{Fix}(G)
. Setting
Q_{h}:=\{h\}
and iterating Lemma
2.4
we obtain

c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}.

The equality follows from
(i)
.

(iii):
We argue by contradiction. Assume that
\boldsymbol{u}=(\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{q})\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u})=c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. There are two possibilities. If for every
h
there exists

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in I_{h}\times I_{k}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}\neq 0,

then
\|\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}<\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}
and, hence, there exists

such that

and

c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}\leq\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}(s_{h}\overline{u}_{h})=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}\|s_{h}\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}<\dfrac{p-1}{2p}\|\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}\qquad\text{for every \ }h=1,\ldots,q.

It follows that

c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u})=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}\sum_{h=1}^{q}\|\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}>\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{q}c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h},

contradicting our assumption. On the other hand, if there exists
h
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{(s,r)\in I_{h}\times I_{k}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{sr}|u_{s}|^{p}|u_{r}|^{p}=0\qquad\text{for every \ }k\neq h,

Hence,

is a solution to the system

with

. So

for some

and it solves the equation

As

∎

j\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{\ell}\beta_{i_{0}j}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}=\sum_{j\in N_{h}(i_{0})}\beta_{i_{0}j}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i_{0}^{*}}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq h\end{subarray}}^{q}\sum_{j\in I_{k}}|\beta_{i_{0}j}|\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{i_{0}^{*}}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\Big(\min_{\{i,j\}\in E_{h}}\beta_{ij}\Big)\left[\frac{S^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}{\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\beta_{ij}}\right]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{K}_{h}}|\beta_{ij}|\left[\frac{pd_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}{(p-1)S^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\right]^{p}\Big(\min_{h=1,\ldots,q}\max_{i\in I_{h}}\beta_{ii}\Big)^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}>0.

But then, for any
q
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
\boldsymbol{t}(\varepsilon)\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{t}(\varepsilon)\boldsymbol{u}_{\varepsilon})<\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u})=c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
for
\varepsilon
small enough. This is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

If
(B_{1})
,
(B_{2})
and
(B_{3}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
hold true, then every least energy block-wise nontrivial solution to
(\mathscr{S}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
is fully nontrivial.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
be such that
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u})=c^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. Then, for each
h\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
there exists
j_{0}\in I_{h}
such that
u_{j_{0}}\neq 0
. Let
i\in I_{h}
. By assumption
(B_{2})
there exist
j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}=i\in I_{h}
such that
j_{i}\in N_{h}(j_{i-1})
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then Lemma
3.2
implies that
u_{i}\neq 0
. This shows that every component of
\overline{u}_{h}
is nontrivial for every
h
.
∎

Our aim now is to obtain some energy bounds for solutions with positive and sign-changing components. We start with the cooperative case.

4
The cooperative system

Throughout this section we assume
(B_{1})
and
(B_{2})
.
If
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a continuous homomorphism, we consider the functional
J^{\phi}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\phi}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J^{\phi}(w):=\frac{1}{2}\|w\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2p}

and the Nehari manifold

\mathcal{M}^{\phi}:=\Big\{w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\phi}:w\neq 0,\ \|w\|^{2}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2p}\Big\}

associated to the problem

(4.1)

-\Delta w+w=|w|^{2p-2}w,\qquad w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\phi}.

Set

\mathfrak{c}^{\phi}:=\inf_{w\in\mathcal{M}^{\phi}}J^{\phi}(w).

If
G
is the trivial group, i.e., if there are no symmetries, we omit the superscript from the notation and set

\mathfrak{c}:=\inf_{w\in\mathcal{M}}J.

It is well known that this value is attained at a positive radial solution
\omega
to the problem

(4.2)

-\Delta w+w=|w|^{2p-2}w,\qquad w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We also consider the function
F_{h}:\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{h}-\ell_{h-1}}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

F_{h}(\overline{s}):=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{h}}s_{i}^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\displaystyle\sum_{{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}}\beta_{ij}|s_{i}|^{p}|s_{j}|^{p},\qquad\overline{s}=(s_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,s_{\ell_{h}}),

and the set

M_{h}:=\Big\{\overline{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{h}-\ell_{h-1}}:\overline{s}\neq\overline{0},\ \displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{h}}s_{i}^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\beta_{ij}|s_{i}|^{p}|s_{j}|^{p}\Big\}.

Note that

\inf_{M_{h}}F_{h}=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}\mu_{h},

with
\mu_{h}
as defined in (
1.2
). Clearly,
\mu_{h}>0
and it is attained at some
\overline{t}=(t_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,t_{\ell_{h}})\in M_{h}.

In the following statement we use the Notation
2.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The following statements hold true:

(i)

If
\overline{s}=(s_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,s_{\ell_{h}})\in M_{h}
and
w\in\mathcal{M}^{\phi_{h}}
, set
{\overline{s}w:=(s_{\ell_{h-1}+1}w,\ldots,s_{\ell_{h}}w)}
. Then
\overline{s}w\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and

\mathcal{J}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{s}w)=\frac{2p}{p-1}F_{h}(\overline{s})J^{\phi_{h}}(w).

(ii)

c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}
.

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}
is attained at
w\in\mathcal{M}^{\phi_{h}}
and
\mu_{h}
is attained at
\overline{t}\in M_{h}
, then
\overline{t}w
is a least energy nontrivial solution to the system
(\mathscr{S}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
, and it is fully nontrivial.

Proof.

(i):
For any
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\phi_{h}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and
\overline{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{h}-\ell_{h-1}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}\|s_{i}w\|^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|s_{i}w|^{p}|s_{j}w|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}|s_{i}|^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\beta_{ij}|s_{i}|^{p}|s_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}.

If
\overline{s}\in M_{h}
and
w\in\mathcal{M}^{\phi_{h}}
, by definition, it follows that
\overline{s}w\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. Raising each side of this identity to the power
\dfrac{p}{p-1}
and multiplying it by
\dfrac{p-1}{2p}
we obtain

\mathcal{J}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{s}w)=\dfrac{2p}{p-1}F_{h}(\overline{s})J^{\phi_{h}}(w),

as claimed.

(ii):
From
(i)
we get
c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\leq\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}
. To prove the opposite inequality, set

\displaystyle S_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\overline{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\\
\|\overline{u}_{h}\|\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|\overline{u}_{h}\|^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|u_{i}|^{p}|u_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}},\qquad S^{\phi_{h}}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\phi_{h}}\\
w\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w|^{2p}\right)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}.

It is straightforward to verify that
c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}(S_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
and
\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}(S^{\phi_{h}})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}
. If
(\overline{u}_{n})
is a sequence in
\mathcal{N}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
such that
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{u}_{n})\to c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, then

\displaystyle S^{\phi_{h}}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}|u_{in}|_{2p}^{2}\leq\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}\|u_{in}\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|u_{in}|^{p}|u_{jn}|^{p}\leq\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\beta_{ij}|u_{in}|_{2p}^{p}|u_{jn}|_{2p}^{p},

where
|\,\cdot\,|_{2p}
is the norm in
L^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mu_{h})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}S^{\phi_{h}}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}|u_{in}|_{2p}^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\beta_{ij}|u_{in}|_{2p}^{p}|u_{jn}|_{2p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}S^{\phi_{h}}\leq\frac{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in I_{h}}\|u_{in}\|^{2}}{\Big(\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{h}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta_{ij}|u_{in}|^{p}|u_{jn}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}=S_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}+o_{n}(1).

As a consequence,
\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}\leq c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, as claimed.

(iii):
If follows from
(i)
and
(ii)
that
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{h}(\overline{t}w)=\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}^{\phi_{h}}=c_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. Hence,
\overline{t}w
is a least energy nontrivial solution to
(\mathscr{S}_{h}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
. By Theorem
3.3
it is fully nontrivial. ∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\phi_{h}\equiv 1
, then
c_{h}^{G}=\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}
and there exists
\overline{s}=(s_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,s_{\ell_{h}})\in(0,\infty)^{\ell_{h}-\ell_{h-1}}
such that
\overline{s}\omega\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{G}
and
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{G}(\overline{s}\omega)=c_{h}^{G}
, where
\omega
is the positive radial solution to (
4.2
).

Proof.

Since
\omega
is radial, it is
G
-invariant for any
G
. Hence,
\omega\in\mathcal{M}^{G}
and
J^{G}(\omega)=\mathfrak{c}^{G}=\mathfrak{c}
. The value
\mu_{h}
is attained at
\overline{s}=(s_{\ell_{h-1}+1},\ldots,s_{\ell_{h}})\in M_{h}
with
s_{i}\geq 0
, so Proposition
4.1
implies that
\overline{s}\omega\in\mathcal{N}_{h}^{G}
,
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{G}(\overline{s}\omega)=c_{h}^{G}=\mu_{h}\mathfrak{c}
and
\overline{s}\omega
is fully nontrivial. Hence,
s_{i}>0
for every
i
.
∎

If

Lemma 4.4
.

If
m\geq 2
and
m\geq 5
whenever
\phi_{h}=\theta
, then there exist
C_{0},R_{0}>0
such that

J^{\phi_{h}}(\sigma_{hR})\leq|G^{\prime}_{m}\zeta|\,\mathfrak{c}-C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-dR}\qquad\forall\ R\geq R_{0},

where
d:=|\zeta-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/m}\zeta|

Proof.

Consider the case
\phi_{h}=\theta
and
\zeta=(1,0,0)
, and set
G:=G_{m}^{\prime}
. The
G
-orbit of
\zeta
is

G\zeta=\{\zeta_{1}^{+},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{+},\zeta_{1}^{-},\ldots,\zeta_{m}^{-}\}\quad\text{where \ }\zeta_{j}^{+}:=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/m}\zeta,\quad\zeta_{j}^{-}:=\tau\zeta_{j}^{+},

so

\widehat{\sigma}_{hR}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\omega(x-R\zeta_{j}^{+})-\omega(x-R\zeta_{j}^{-})\right).

For

set

\Psi(y):=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\omega^{2p-1}(x)\omega(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}x.

From the well known decay estimates for

\lim_{y\to\infty}\Psi(y)|y|^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\exp|y|=b>0.

Hence, for large enough

\frac{\Psi(Ry)}{\Psi(Rz)}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-R(|y|-|z|)}\qquad\text{if \ }|y|\geq|z|.

Here, and in what follows,

denotes a positive constant independent of
R
. Set

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{R}^{+}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{m}\Psi(R\zeta_{i}^{+}-R\zeta_{j}^{+}),\qquad\varepsilon_{R}^{-}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{m}\Psi(R\zeta_{i}^{-}-R\zeta_{j}^{-}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{R}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\Psi(R\zeta_{i}^{+}-R\zeta_{j}^{-}).

Then,
\varepsilon_{R}^{+}=\varepsilon_{R}^{-}
. Since
m\geq 5
, we have that
|\zeta_{i}^{+}-\zeta_{j}^{-}|>|\zeta_{1}^{+}-\zeta_{2}^{+}|=d
for every

. Therefore,

\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{R}}{\varepsilon_{R}^{+}}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\Psi(R\zeta_{i}^{+}-R\zeta_{j}^{-})}{\Psi(R\zeta_{1}^{+}-R\zeta_{2}^{+})}\leq C\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-R(|\zeta_{i}^{+}-\zeta_{j}^{-}|-d)}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-Ra},

where

. This shows that
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{R}=o(\varepsilon_{R}^{+})
as
R\to\infty
.
Since
\omega
solves (

), for any

we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\nabla\omega(x-y)\cdot\nabla\omega(x-y^{\prime})+\omega(x-y)\omega(x-y^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\omega^{2p-1}(x-y)\omega(x-y^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x=\Psi(y^{\prime}-y).

So, using

, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|\widehat{\sigma}_{hR}\|^{2}}{|\widehat{\sigma}_{hR}|_{2p}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2m\|\omega\|^{2}+2\varepsilon_{R}^{+}+o(\varepsilon_{R}^{+})}{\left(2m\|\omega\|^{2}+2(2p-1)\varepsilon_{R}^{+}+o(\varepsilon_{R}^{+})\right)^{\frac{2}{2p}}}\leq(2m\|\omega\|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}-C\varepsilon_{R}^{+}.

As a consequence,

J^{\phi}(\sigma_{hR})=\dfrac{p-1}{2p}\Big(\frac{\|\widehat{\sigma}_{hR}\|^{2}}{|\widehat{\sigma}_{hR}|_{2p}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\leq 2m\,\mathfrak{c}-C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-dR},

as claimed.

The proof for

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{t}_{2}\sigma_{2R})=\mu_{2}J^{\phi_{2}}(\sigma_{2R})\leq\mu_{2}(|G\zeta|\mathfrak{c}-C_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-dR}),

where
d:=|\zeta-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/m}\zeta|
.

Define
\boldsymbol{w}_{R}=(w_{1R},\ldots,w_{\ell R})=(\overline{w}_{1R},\overline{w}_{2R})
with
\overline{w}_{1R}:=\overline{t}_{1}w_{1}
and
\overline{w}_{2R}:=\overline{t}_{2}\sigma_{2R}
. Then
\boldsymbol{w}_{R}
satisfies (
2.1
) if

is large enough, so there exist

and, for each

, a pair

such that
\boldsymbol{u}_{R}:=(s_{1R}\overline{w}_{1R},s_{2R}\overline{w}_{2R})\in\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. From the last statement in Lemma
2.2
(ii)
we derive

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|s_{iR}w_{iR}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\sum_{i,j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|s_{iR}w_{iR}|^{p}|s_{jR}w_{jR}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{J}_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{w}_{1R})+\mathcal{J}_{2}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\overline{w}_{2R})-\frac{1}{2p}\sum_{i\in I_{1},\,j\in I_{2}}\beta_{ij}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|s_{1R}w_{iR}|^{p}|s_{2R}w_{jR}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}+|G\zeta|\mu_{2}\mathfrak{c}-C\mathrm{e}^{-Rd}-\frac{1}{2p}\sum_{i\in I_{1},\,j\in I_{2}}\beta_{ij}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|s_{1R}w_{iR}|^{p}|s_{2R}w_{jR}|^{p}.

Since
\omega
and
\widehat{\omega}
solve (
4.2
) we have that

|w_{iR}(x)|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-|x|}\quad\forall\ i\in I_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|w_{jR}(x)|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-|x-R\zeta|}\quad\forall\ j\in I_{2},

. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{iR}|^{p}|w_{jR}|^{p}\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{e}^{-p|x|}\,\mathrm{e}^{-p|x-R\zeta|}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-Rp}\qquad\forall\ i\in I_{1},\ j\in I_{2}.

Since

when

, it follows that

This, together with (
5.1
), yields our claim.
∎

Remark 5.2

To extend the previous argument to a system with more than two blocks one needs some information on the decay of the components of solutions to a system. Namely, if we knew that a minimizer

The singularly perturbed system

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of
O(N)
,
\phi_{h}:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be continuous homomorphisms satisfying
(A_{\phi_{h}})
for each
h\in Q
and define
\phi_{i}:=\phi_{h}
for all
i\in I_{h}
. Let
\Omega
be a
G
-invariant bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
0\in\Omega
. We consider the system of singularly perturbed elliptic equations

(\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})\qquad\begin{cases}-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u_{i}+u_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|u_{j}|^{p}|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i},\\
u_{i}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{\phi_{i}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon>0
,
1<p<\frac{N}{N-2}
,
N\geq 4
, and the matrix
(\beta_{ij})
satisfies assumption
(B_{1})
.

Note that
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell})
solves
(\mathscr{S}_{\varepsilon,\Omega}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
iff
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}=(\widetilde{u}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{u}_{\ell})
with
\widetilde{u}_{i}(z):=u_{i}(\varepsilon z)
solves the system

(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{i}+v_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{ij}|v_{j}|^{p}|v_{i}|^{p-2}v_{i},\\
v_{i}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{\phi_{i}},\qquad i=1,\ldots,\ell,\end{cases}

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\varepsilon z\in\Omega\}
.

A
least energy block-wise nontrivial solution to this system
is defined as in Section
2
, replacing
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. If
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}
is a least energy block-wise nontrivial solution to
(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon_{n}}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
, then, arguing as in
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
, one sees that
(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n})
is a minimizing sequence for the functional
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
associated to the system
(\mathscr{S}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
on
\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume
(B_{1})
and
(B_{2})
. The following statements hold true:

(i)

For every
\varepsilon>0
the system
(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
has a least energy block-wise nontrivial solution.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Fix}(G)\neq 0
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, every least energy block-wise nontrivial solution to the system
(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
is fully nontrivial.

Proof.

(i):
It follows from
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
that
(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
has a least energy blockwise nontrivial solution.

(ii):
Arguing by contradiction, assume that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\varepsilon_{n}\in(0,{1/n})
and a least energy block-wise nontrivial solution
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}
to
(\mathscr{S}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon_{n}}}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
which is not fully nontrivial. Passing to a subsequence, we may choose
i\in I_{k}
such that
\widetilde{u}_{in}=0
for all
n
.

As stated above,
(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n})
is a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{J}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
on
\mathcal{N}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. If
\mathrm{Fix}(G)\neq 0
Proposition
2.5
states that no subsystem of
(\mathscr{S}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}})
having at least two blocks has a least energy block-wise nontrivial solution. So we derive from Theorem
2.7
that, after passing to a subsequence, there exist a sequence
(\xi_{kn})
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, a closed subgroup
G_{k}
of
G
, and a least energy nontrivial solution
\overline{v}_{k}
to the cooperative system

(\mathscr{S}_{k}^{\boldsymbol{\phi}|G_{k}})\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{i}+v_{i}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in I_{k}}\beta_{ij}|v_{j}|^{p}|v_{i}|^{p-2}v_{i},\\
v_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\phi_{i}|G_{k}},\qquad i\in I_{k},\end{cases}

such that

,
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}|g\xi_{kn}-\overline{g}\xi_{kn}|=\infty
for any

with

, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\|\widetilde{u}_{in}-\displaystyle\sum_{[g]\in G/G_{k}}\phi_{i}(g)(v_{i}\circ g^{-1})(\ \cdot\ -g\xi_{kn})\Big\|=0.

Since we are assuming that

for all

, this implies that
v_{i}=0
, which is a contradiction because

is fully nontrivial by Theorem
3.3
.

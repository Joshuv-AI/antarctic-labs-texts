Classification of solutions to equations involving Higher-order fractional laplacian
Zhuoran Du, Zhenping Feng, Jiaqi Hu, Yuan Li

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the following higher-order fractional equation

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{p+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)=u_{+}^{\gamma},\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{+}^{\gamma}dx<+\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n}{n-2p-\alpha})
,
n>2p+\alpha
,
0<\alpha<2
,
p\geq 1
is an integer,
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
and the higher-order fractional Laplacian is defined by
(-\Delta)^{p+\frac{\alpha}{2}}:=(-\Delta)^{p}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
. It is well-known that the integral form of fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}

(0<\alpha<2)
is

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)=C_{n,\alpha}P.V.\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+\alpha}}dy,

where the constant
C_{n,\alpha}=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}{-\pi^{n/2}\Gamma(-\frac{\alpha}{2})}
.

Denote

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):=\Big\{u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}\big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|}{1+|x|^{n+\alpha}}dx<+\infty\Big\}.

It is known that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u
is well defined for
u\in C_{loc}^{[\alpha],\{\alpha\}+\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
[\alpha]
denotes the integer part of
\alpha
,
\{\alpha\}:=\alpha-[\alpha]
and any
\varepsilon>0
. In order to guarantee that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\in C^{2p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have to assume
u\in C_{loc}^{2p+[\alpha],\{\alpha\}+\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
(see
[
14
]
,
[
19
]
), and hence
u
is a classical solution of the equation in (
1
) in the sense that
(-\Delta)^{p+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u
is point-wise well-defined in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For decades, many researchers are interested in the classification of solutions to semi-linear elliptic equations. We now recall some relevant results. The following conformally invariant equations

(2)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u=u^{\frac{n+\beta}{n-\beta}}~\mbox{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n},~0<\beta<n

have been extensively studied (see
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
and the reference therein). In case
\beta=2
, (
2
) becomes the well-known Yamabe equation. Cafferelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
2
]
(see also
[
6
]
) classified all positive solutions of Yamabe equation. For general
0<\beta<n
, Chen, Li and Ou in
[
8
]
(see also
[
18
]
) classified all positive
L_{loc}^{\frac{2n}{n-\beta}}
solutions to the equivalent integral equation of (
2
) by the method of moving planes in integral forms. For
0<\beta<2
, Chen, Li and Li in
[
7
]
developed a direct method of moving planes for fractional Laplace equation and complete the classification of all nonnegative solutions. When
\beta=3
is an odd integer, Dai and Qin in
[
10
]
derived the classification of nonnegative classical solutions to (
2
) with the assumption
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u^{\frac{n+3}{n-3}}}{|x|^{n-3}}dx<+\infty
. Recently, Cao, Dai and Qin in
[
3
]
extended the result of
[
10
]
to general case
0<\beta<n
.
Their classification results completely improved the results in
[
10
]
without assumption on integrability. Precisely they proved the super poly-harmonic properties for nonnegative solutions by making full use of the the Poisson representation formula for
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(0<\alpha<2)
and developing some new integral estimates on the outer-spherical average
\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{R^{\alpha}}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dr
and iteration techniques.
Based on super poly-harmonic properties, they established integral representation formula, Liouville’s Theorem and classification of classical solutions to higher-order equations involving fractional Laplacian.

For (
1
), classification of nonconstant solutions have been established for some particular
p,\alpha
and
\gamma
. For
p=1,\alpha=0
and
\gamma=\frac{n}{n-2}
, Wang and Ye

classified all nonconstant solutions of (

).
Suzuki and Takahashi

extended the results of
[

from the exponent

to more general exponent. Precisely, they considered the problem

-\Delta v=v^{\gamma}_{+}~~\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{n}},~~n>2,~~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{+}^{\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{2}}dx<+\infty,

where
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n+2}{n-2})
. For the case
p=2,\alpha=0
and
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n}{n-4}]
, Chammakhi, Harrabi and Selmi
[

]
completed the classification of all sign-changing solutions of (

). The first, second and fourth authors

]
to the corresponding polyharmonic equation, namely for the case of any integer

\gamma\in(1,\frac{n+4}{n-4})
. They also considered nonconstant solutions of the following fractional equation (namely

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v=v^{\gamma}_{+}~~\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{n}},~~\alpha\in(0,2),~~n\geq 2,~~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v_{+}^{\frac{n(\gamma-1)}{\alpha}}dx<+\infty,~~~~~

To classify nonconstant solutions of (
1
) for general
0<\alpha<2
,
p\geq 1
and
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n}{n-2p-\alpha})
, we need to establish the following super poly-harmonic properties.

Theorem 1
.

Assume
\gamma\in(1,\frac{n}{n-2p-\alpha})
,
n>2p+\alpha
,
0<\alpha<2
and
p\geq 1
is an integer. Suppose
u
is a nonconstant classical solution to (
1
) satisfying
u(x)=o(|x|^{\alpha+\frac{4}{\alpha}})
. Then for every
i=0,1,\ldots,p-1
,

(-\Delta)^{i+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)\geq 0,\ \ \ \ \forall~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

As well-known, establishing equivalent integral equation of (1.1) is beneficial to classifying solutions to higher order equations. As a consequence of super poly-harmonic properties for equation (
1
), we can derive the equivalence between the differential equation (
1
) and the following integral equation

(5)

u(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{R_{2p+\alpha,n}}{|x-y|^{n-2p-\alpha}}u_{+}^{\gamma}(y)dy+C_{0},

where
C_{0}<0
and the Riesz potential’s constant
R_{\mu,n}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-\mu}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}2^{\mu}\Gamma(\frac{\mu}{2})}
for
0<\mu<n
(see
[
16
]
).

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, suppose u is a nonconstant classical solution to (
1
) satisfying
u(x)=o(|x|^{\alpha+\frac{4}{\alpha}})
. Then u is a solution to integral equation (
5
), and vice verse. Moreover, the support of
u_{+}
is compact.

Apparently, for (
1
), any non-positive constant is its solution and any positive constant is not its solution. Hence we only consider nonconstant solutions. Actually, there is no positive solutions to (
1
) from the nonexistence of positive solutions in the subcritical exponent case of Theorem 1.9 in
[
3
]
. All negative nonconstant solutions of (
1
) can be ruled out by the Liouville theorem for fractional poly-harmonic functions (see Theorem 1.3 in
[
3
]
). Consequently any nonconstant solutions of (
1
) are sign-changing solutions. From this observation and Theorem
2
, the following classification results can be obtained by applying the method of moving planes in integral forms.

Theorem 3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, suppose
u
is a sign-changing classical solution to equation (
1
) satisfying
u(x)=o(|x|^{\alpha+\frac{4}{\alpha}})
at infinity, then
u
is symmetric about some point
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}<0
, where
r=|x-x_{0}|
.

Throughout this paper, we will use
C
to denote a positive constant which may change from line to line and even within the same line.

Preliminaries

The purpose of this section is to introduce several useful properties which will be crucial to the forthcoming sections.

Proposition 1
.

[
1
]

Let
R>0
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
h\in C^{\alpha+\varepsilon}(B_{R}(x_{0}))\cap C(\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0}))
,

&0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

Then
u
is the unique point-wise continuous solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)=h(x),&\ \ \ \ &x\in B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
&u(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

The Green function is defined by

G_{R}^{\alpha}(x,y):=\frac{C_{n,\alpha}}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{R}}{s_{R}}}\frac{b^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{(1+b)^{\frac{n}{2}}}db,\ \ \ \ \ x,y\in B_{R}(x_{0})

with

,
t_{R}=\Big(1-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{|y-x_{0}|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big)
, and
G_{R}^{\alpha}(x,y)=0
if
x
or
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
,
where

C_{n,\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{2^{\alpha}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma^{2}(\frac{\alpha}{2})}\ \mbox{if}\ \ n\neq\alpha,\ \ \ \ C_{1,1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\ \mbox{if}\ n=\alpha=1.

Proposition 2
.

[
1
]

Let
R>0
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

[

&\leq C_{\delta}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\overline{u}(r)|}{r^{1+\alpha}}dr+C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\overline{u}(r)|}{r^{1+\alpha+\delta}}dr\\
&\leq C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{{|\bar{u}}(r)|}{r^{1+\alpha}}dr\\
&\leq C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\overline{|u|}(r)}{r^{1+\alpha}}dr<+\infty,\end{split}

which leads to a contradiction with (
19
). Thus
k=0
.

From
k=0
, we conclude that

(21)

\bar{v}_{0}(r)\leq\bar{v}_{0}(0):=-c_{0}<0,\ \ \ \ \forall r>0.

Thus for any
R>0
, (
3
), (
10
), (
18
) and (
21
) yield that

|\partial B_{1}|\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{R^{\alpha}|\bar{u}(r)|}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dr\geq C\int_{0}^{\frac{R}{2}}r^{\alpha-1}dr+C\int_{\frac{R}{2}}^{R}r^{\alpha-1}\Big(\frac{R^{2}}{r^{2}}-1\Big)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}dr+u(x_{0})\geq CR^{\alpha}-\tilde{C},

where positive constants
C
and
\tilde{C}
are independent of
R
. Thus there exists
R_{0}
large enough such that

(22)

\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{R^{\alpha}|\bar{u}(r)|}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dr\geq CR^{\alpha},\ \ \ \ \forall R>R_{0}.

Obviously

\int_{N}^{+\infty}\frac{\overline{|u|}(r)}{r^{1+\alpha}}=C\int_{|x-x_{0}|>N}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+\alpha}}dx=\mbox{o}_{N}(1)

as
N\to+\infty
due to
u\in\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Therefore

(23)

\int_{2R}^{+\infty}\frac{R^{\alpha}|\bar{u}(r)|}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dr\leq CR^{\alpha}\int_{2R}^{+\infty}\frac{{|\bar{u}}(r)|}{r^{1+\alpha}}\leq\mbox{o}_{R}(1)R^{\alpha},

as
R\to+\infty
. It follows from (
22
) and (
23
) that there exists
R_{1}\geq R_{0}
large enough such that

(24)

\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{R^{\alpha}|\bar{u}(r)|}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dr\geq\frac{C}{2}R^{\alpha},\ \ \ \ \forall R>R_{1}.

On the other hand, from (
20
), we derive that

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{R^{1+\alpha}}\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{R^{\alpha}|\bar{u}(r)|}{r(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}drdR&\leq\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\bar{u}(r)|}{r}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\frac{1}{R(r^{2}-R^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dRdr\\
&\leq C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{|\bar{u}(r)|}{r^{2+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\frac{1}{(r-R)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}dRdr\\
&\leq C\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{{|\bar{u}}(r)|}{r^{1+\alpha}}<+\infty,\end{split}

which contradicts with (
24
). Hence Theorem
1
is proved.
\square

Lemma 1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem

, suppose u is a nonconstant classical solution to (1.1) satisfying

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{R_{2p,n}}{|x-y|^{n-2p}}u_{+}^{\gamma}(y)dy+C_{p},

Firstly, we prove that

R>0
, denote

and

on

G_{R}^{2}(x,y)=R_{2,n}\Big[\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{(|x|\cdot|\frac{Rx}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{y}{R}|)^{n-2}}\Big],\ \ \ \ x,y\in B_{R}(0),

v_{1}^{R}(x)\in C^{2}(B_{R}(0))\cap C({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v_{1}^{R}(x)=u_{+}^{\gamma},&\ \ \ \ &x\in B_{R}(0),\\
&v_{1}^{R}(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

w_{1}^{R}(x):=(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u-v_{1}^{R}

1
with (
27

w_{1}^{R}(x)\in C^{2}(B_{R}(0))\cap C({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w_{1}^{R}(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in B_{R}(0),\\
&w_{1}^{R}(x)\geq 0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

The maximum principle implies that

(28)

w_{1}^{R}(x)=(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)-v_{1}^{R}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Now for each fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, letting
R\to+\infty
in (

(29)

(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{R_{2,n}}{|x-y|^{n-2}}f_{1}(y)dy=:v_{1}(x)\geq 0.

Since
v_{1}-v_{1}^{R}
is smooth in
B_{R}(0)
for any
R>0
, one can obtain that
v_{1}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
satisfies

(30)

-\Delta v_{1}(x)=u_{+}^{\gamma}(x),\ \ \ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Set
w_{1}(x):=(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)-v_{1}(x)

1

29

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w_{1}(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\\
&w_{1}(x)\geq 0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then by the Liouville Theorem for harmonic function, we can infer that

w_{1}(x)=(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)-v_{1}(x)\equiv C_{1}\geq 0.

Therefore

(31)

(-\Delta)^{p-1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{R_{2,n}}{|x-y|^{n-2}}u_{+}^{\gamma}(y)dy+C_{1}=:f_{2}(x)\geq C_{1}\geq 0.

Next, for arbitrary
R>0
, denote

v_{2}^{R}(x):=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}G_{R}^{2}(x,y)f_{2}(y)dy.

Then we have

(32)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v_{2}^{R}(x)=f_{2}(x),&\ \ \ \ &x\in B_{R}(0),\\
&v_{2}^{R}(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(0).\end{aligned}\right.

Set
w_{2}^{R}(x):=(-\Delta)^{p-2+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u-v_{2}^{R}
. By Theorem
1
, (
31

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta w_{2}^{R}(x)=0,&\ \ \ \ &x\in B_{R}(0),\\

The maximum principle implies that for any
R>0

(33)

w_{2}^{R}(x)=(-\Delta)^{p-2+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)-v_{2}^{R}(x)\geq 0,\ \ \ \ \forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Now for each fixed
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, letting
R\to+\infty

(34)

(-\Delta)^{p-2+\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(x)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{R_{2,n}}{|x-y|^{n-2}}f_{2}(y)dy=:v_{2}(x)\geq 0.

Taking

It follows immediately that

Lemma 2

Lemma 3

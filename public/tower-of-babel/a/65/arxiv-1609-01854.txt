A Note on the Speed of Perfect State Transfer
Alastair Kay, Weichen Xie, Christino Tamon

Abstract
In Phys. Rev. A 74, 030303 (2006), Yung showed that for a one-dimensional spin chain of length N N and maximum coupling strength J max J_{\max} , the time t 0 t_{0} for a quantum state to transfer from one end of the chain to another is bounded by J max ​ t 0 ≥ π ​ N / 4 J_{\max}t_{0}\geq\pi N/4 (even N N ) and J max ​ t 0 ≥ π ​ N 2 − 1 / 4 J_{\max}t_{0}\geq\pi\sqrt{N^{2}-1}/4 (odd N N ). The proof for even N N was elegant, but the proof for odd N N was less so. This note provides a proof for the odd N N case that is simpler, and more in keeping with the proof for the even case.



The benefit of this simplified proof is that it can be used elsewhere. For example, in the study of speed limits for synthesising quantum states using identical Hamiltonian structures
Kay 2017
, where the phase conditions on the eigenvalues are slightly different, these differences are readily incorporated in the optimisation. Studies of fractional revival
Genest et al. 2016
are another option.

A tridiagonal
N\times N
matrix with real diagonal elements
B_{n}
and positive off-diagonal elements
J_{n}
,

H=\sum_{n=1}^{N}B_{n}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}J_{n}\left(\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle n+1\right|+\left|n+1\right\rangle\left\langle n\right|\right),

is capable of perfect transfer (
e^{-iHt_{0}}\left|1\right\rangle=e^{i\phi}\left|N\right\rangle
) if and only if
H
is symmetric, meaning
B_{n}=B_{N+1-n}
and
J_{n}=J_{N-n}
, and the ordered eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}>\lambda_{n+1}
satisfy
e^{-i\lambda_{n}t_{0}}=(-1)^{n+1}e^{i\phi}
for some real parameters
t_{0}
(the transfer time) and
\phi

Kay 2010
. The symmetry imposes that the eigenvectors satisfy
S\left|\lambda_{n}\right\rangle=(-1)^{n+1}\left|\lambda_{n}\right\rangle
where

S=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|n\right\rangle\left\langle N+1-n\right|.

N even:
We repeat Yung’s proof
Yung 2006
. If we calculate
\text{Tr}(SH)
for both the matrix directly, and for the eigenvector decomposition, we have

2J_{N/2}=\sum_{n=1}^{N/2}\lambda_{2n-1}-\lambda_{2n}.

We know that
\lambda_{2n-1}\geq\lambda_{2n}+\frac{\pi}{t_{0}}
in order for it to satisfy the required phase property for the eigenvalues, so

2J_{\max}\geq 2J_{N/2}\geq\frac{N}{2}\frac{\pi}{t_{0}},

immediately yielding the claimed relation.

N odd:
This more involved case starts similarly,

B_{(N+1)/2}=\text{Tr}(HS)=\sum_{n}\lambda_{2n-1}-\sum_{n}\lambda_{2n}.

Next, we evaluate
\text{Tr}(SH^{2})

\text{Tr}(SH^{2})=4J_{(N-1)/2}^{2}+B_{(N+1)/2}^{2}=\sum_{n}\lambda_{2n-1}^{2}-\sum_{n}\lambda_{2n}^{2}.

Let the vector
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{N},\lambda_{2},\dots\lambda_{N-1})
. Then we can express

4J_{(N-1)/2}^{2}=\underline{\lambda}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\text{1}_{(N+1)/2}-\textbf{1}&\textbf{1}\\
\textbf{1}&-\text{1}_{(N-1)/2}-\textbf{1}\end{array}\right)\underline{\lambda},

where
1
is the all-ones matrix of the appropriate size, sizes of the blocks being specified by the subscript of the
1
. However, there are many equivalent matrices (since, for example,
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
appears in two terms). One such is

\tilde{A}=2\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-U&\text{1}+U\\
U&-\text{1}-U\end{array}\right)

where
U
is the upper triangular component of
\textbf{1}=\text{1}+U+U^{T}
(of the appropriate size) – just evaluate
\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{A}^{T})
. Note that rows for terms
\lambda_{2n-1}
and
\lambda_{2n}
are identical up to a sign, as are columns for
\lambda_{2m}
and
\lambda_{2m+1}
. Hence

4J_{(N-1)/2}^{2}=2\sum_{n=1}^{(N-1)/2}(\lambda_{2n-1}-\lambda_{2n})\sum_{m=n}^{(N-1)/2}(\lambda_{2m}-\lambda_{2m+1}).

Since
\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n+1}\geq\frac{\pi}{t_{0}}
, we bound this by

4J_{(N-1)/2}^{2}\geq 2\frac{\pi^{2}}{t_{0}^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{(N-1)/2}\left(\frac{N+1}{2}-n\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{t_{0}^{2}}\frac{N^{2}-1}{4}.

This leaves the claimed result

J_{\max}t_{0}\geq\frac{\pi\sqrt{N^{2}-1}}{4}.

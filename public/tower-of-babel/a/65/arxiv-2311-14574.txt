Two Galois connections for left quasigroups
Marco Bonatto

Abstract
. We investigate two Galois connection between the congruence lattice and the lattice of subgroups of the displacement group of left quasigroups. Such connections were already studied for racks and quandles. We introduce the class of left quasigroups having congruence determined by subgroups (resp. orbits) and we extend a known result for quandles.



Introduction

Left quasigroups are rather combinatorial objects, nevertheless several algebraic structures arising in different ares of mathematics have an underlying left quasigroup structure. For instance racks and quandles coming from low dimensional topology
[
Joy82
,
Mat82
]
and the study of Hopf algebras
[
AG03
]
are examples of highly structured left quasigroups.

A tool for the study of racks is the
left multiplication group
and its subgroups. In particular the
displacement group
reflects a lot of properties of racks. In particular properties as
abelianness
and
nilpotency
in the sense of commutator theory
[
H.76
,
FM87
]
are completely determined by the correspondent properties of the displacement group
[
BS21
]
.

In
[
BS21
]
, a monotone Galois connection between the lattice of congruences and the lattice of normal subgroups of the left multiplication group of racks has been defined by the pair of operators
(\mathrm{Dis}_{*},\mathrm{con}_{*})
. If this Galois connection provides an isomorphism between the two lattices we said that a rack has the
congruence determined by subgroups
(shortly, CDSg). We also discovered a second Galois connection defined by the pair of mappings
(\mathrm{Dis}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
and later on in
[
Bon21
]
we introduced the same pair of operators between the lattice of congruences of left quasigroups and the lattice of
admissible subgroups
(in the case of racks, admissible subgroups and normal subgroup of the left multiplication group are the same thing). We say that a left quasigroup has the
congruence determined by orbits
(shortly, CDOs) whenever this second Galois connection provides an isomorphism between the two lattices.

In this paper we investigate the relation between these two Galois connections in the setting of left quasigroups. In particular, left quasigroups having CDSg and CDOs are compared in Theorem
2.7
. Some results in this direction were already obtained for racks in
[
Bon20
]
. One of the main results of the paper is Theorem
3.8
in which we extend
[
Bon20
, Proposition 3.17]
to idempotent left quasigroups of arbitrary cardinality.

In Section
1
we collect all the basics about left quasigroups and the interplay between congruences and admissible subgroups. Moreover we introduce the pairs of operators
(\mathrm{Dis}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
and
(\mathrm{Dis}_{*},\mathrm{con}_{*})
and how they relate with the
Cayley kernel
and the property of being
faithful
. Section
2
is dedicated to the Galois connections defined by the operators introduced in the previous section and to left quasigroups having CDSg and CDOs. The last section is about nilpotent left quasigroups having CDSg (we also provide some basic notions of commutator theory and central extensions).

1.
Preliminary results

1.1.
Left quasigroups

A left quasigroup is a binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
such that the following identities hold:

x\cdot(x\backslash y)\approx y\approx x\backslash(x\cdot y).

We will denote the
\cdot
operation just by juxtaposition in the rest of the paper. The
left and right multiplication mappings
of
Q
are defined as

L_{x}:y\mapsto xy,\quad R_{x}:y\mapsto yx

for every
x\in Q
. According to the axioms above, the map
L_{x}
is a permutation for every
x\in Q
and so we can define the
left multiplication group
of
Q
as
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)=\langle L_{x},\,x\in Q\rangle
. Note that the
\backslash
operation is defined as
x\backslash y=L_{x}^{-1}(y)
for every
x,y\in Q
, so we ofter specify just the
\cdot
operation for left quasigroups, from which it is usually easy to get the left multiplication mappings and their inverses.

Right quasigroups
can be defined analogously as binary algebraic structure
(Q,\cdot,/)
satisfying

(y\cdot x)/x\approx y\approx(y/x)\cdot x.

The right multiplication mappings of right quasigroups are bijections.

The
set of idempotent elements of
Q
is
E(Q)=\{x\in Q\,:\,xx=x\}
. We say that
Q
is:

(i)

idempotent
if
Q=E(Q)
, i.e. the identity
xx\approx x
holds in
Q
;

(ii)

a
rack
if the identity
x(yz)\approx(xy)(xz)
holds (or equivalently
L_{x}\in\mathrm{Aut}(Q,\cdot)
for every
x\in Q
). Idempotent racks are called
quandles
;

(iii)

latin
if the right multiplications are bijective.

Latin left quasigroups are essentially
reducts
of
quasigroups
, namely binary algebraic structures with three binary operations
(Q,\cdot,\backslash,/)
, such that
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
is a left quasigroup and
(Q,\cdot,/)
is a right quasigroup. The two type of structures have different signatures effecting congruences and subalgebras if the underlying set has infinite cardinality. In this paper we consider latin left quasigroups with signature
\{\cdot,\backslash\}
.

A
term
in the language of left quasigroups
\{\cdot,\backslash\}
is either a variable or an expression
t_{1}\cdot t_{2}
,
t_{1}\backslash t_{2}
, where
t_{1},t_{2}
are terms. A
Malt’sev
term is a ternary term
m
such that
m(x,y,y)\approx x\approx m(y,y,x)
holds. We say that a left quasigroup
Q
is Malt’sev if the variety generated by
Q
has a Malt’sev term. Given a left quasigroup
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
any
n
-ary term
t
provides a map
t^{Q}:Q^{n}\longrightarrow Q
called
term operation
.

For further details on the universal algebraic definitions of terms and varieties of algebras see
[
Ber12
]
.

1.2.
Congruences and admissible subgroups

In this section we collect all the results on congruences and admissible subgroups we are using in the following. For further details see
[
Bon21
,
Bon22a
]
.

Let
Q
be a set, we denote the lattice of equivalence relations on
Q
as
Equiv(Q)
. Given
\alpha\in Equiv(Q)
we denote the quotient set with respect to
\alpha
by
Q/\alpha
and the class of
x
(with respect to
\alpha
) by
[x]_{\alpha}
(we often omit the subscrit and we write just
[x]
).

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup. We can define the following operators associating to every equivalence a
pair of subgroups of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}_{*}:\alpha\mapsto\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\langle hL_{x}L_{y}^{-1}h^{-1},\,x\,\alpha\,y,\,h\in\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}^{*}:\alpha\mapsto\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\{h\in\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\,:\,h(x)\,\alpha\,x\,\text{ for all }x\in Q\}.

The two operators
\mathrm{Dis}_{*},\mathrm{Dis}^{*}
are clearly monotone.

The first of the two groups is called the
displacement group relative to
\alpha
and it is normal in
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
by definition. In particular, we denote
\mathrm{Dis}_{1_{Q}}
just by
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
and we call it the
displacement group
of
Q
. According to
[
Bon21
, Lemma 1.4]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\langle L_{x}\rangle\,\text{ for every }\,x\in Q,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}(Q)

\displaystyle=\{L_{x_{1}}^{k_{1}}\ldots L_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}\,:\,x_{1},\ldots x_{n}\in Q,\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\sum_{j}k_{j}=0\}.

A congruence on
Q
is an equivalence relation
\alpha
such that
xy\,\alpha\,zt
and
x\backslash y\,\alpha\,z\backslash t
provided
x\,\alpha\,z
and
y\,\alpha\,t
. We denote the lattice of congruences of
Q
as
Con(Q)
. Given
\alpha\in Con(Q)
, the quotient set
Q/\alpha
is endowed with a well-defined left quasigroup structure defined by

[x]_{\alpha}\cdot[y]_{\alpha}=[x\cdot y]_{\alpha},\,[x]_{\alpha}\backslash[y]_{\alpha}=[x\backslash y]_{\alpha}

for every
[x]_{\alpha},[y]_{\alpha}\in Q/\alpha
. The canonical map
x\mapsto[x]_{\alpha}
provides a surjective morphism of left quasigroups from
Q
to
Q/\alpha
. On the other hand, given a surjective morphism of left quasigroups
f:Q\longrightarrow Q^{\prime}
, the relation
\mathrm{ker}(f)=\{(x,y)\,:\,f(x)=f(y)\}
is a congruence of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}\cong Q/\mathrm{ker}(f)
. Note also that if
\alpha
is a congruence of
Q
and
[x]_{\alpha}\in E(Q/\alpha)
then the block of
x
with respect to
\alpha
is a subalgebra of
Q
.

Let
N\leq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
. We can define two equivalence relation out of
N
, namely:

\displaystyle x\,\mathcal{O}_{N}\,y\,

\displaystyle\text{if and only if }x=h(y)\,\text{ for some }h\in N,

\displaystyle x\,\mathrm{con}_{N}\,y

\displaystyle\text{ if and only if }L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}\in N.

The assignments above define two monotone operators
\mathrm{con}_{*}:N\mapsto\mathrm{con}_{N}
and
\mathcal{O}_{*}:N\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{N}
from the lattice of subgroups of
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
to
Equiv(Q)
.

If
\mathcal{O}_{N}=1_{Q}
(i.e.
N
is transitive on
Q
), we say that
Q
is
connected by
N
. If
\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)
is transitive we simply say that
Q
is
connected
. If all the subalgebras of
Q
are connected we say that
Q
is
superconnected
(in particular
Q
is connected). The class of superconnected left quasigroups has been studied in
[
BF21
]
and
[
Bon22b
]
. Connected idempotent left quasigroups and left quasigroup with a Mal’cev term are connected by their displacement group
[
BF21
, Proposition 3.6]
.

Assume that
\alpha\in Con(Q)
, then we have a morphism of groups defined as:

(1)

\pi_{\alpha}:\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Dis}(Q/\alpha),\quad L_{x_{1}}^{k_{1}}\ldots L_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}\longrightarrow L_{[x_{1}]}^{k_{1}}\ldots L_{[x_{n}]}^{k_{n}}.

Let us denote by
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{x}
the pointwise stabilizer of
x
in
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
. If
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{x}=1
for every
x\in Q
we say that
Q
is
semiregular
. In particular, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{ker}(\pi_{\alpha})\leq\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{[x]}=\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\mathrm{Dis}(Q/\alpha)_{[x]})=\{h\in\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\,:\,h(x)\,\alpha\,x\}

for every
x\in Q
. Moreover, we have the following.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
BS21
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha,\beta\in Con(Q)
such that
\alpha\leq\beta
. Then:

(i)

\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta/\alpha}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta})
.

(ii)

\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta/\alpha}=\pi_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta}).

We focus on the set of
admissible subgroups
contained in the displacement group and its interplay with congruences. The admissible subgroups we are interest in are defined in
[
Bon21
]
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)

\displaystyle=\{N\trianglelefteq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\,:\,N\leq\mathrm{Dis}(Q),\,\mathcal{O}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}\}

\displaystyle=\{N\trianglelefteq\mathrm{LMlt}(Q)\,:\,\mathrm{Dis}_{\mathcal{O}_{N}}\leq N\leq\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\}.

In particular
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha},\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)
for every
\alpha\in Con(Q)

[
Bon21
, Corollary 1.9]
. If
N\in\mathrm{Norm}(Q)
then
\mathcal{O}_{N}\in Con(Q)
and
N\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\mathcal{O}_{N}}

[
Bon21
, Corollary 1.9]
. It is easy to check that

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}\leq\alpha\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}

for every congruence
\alpha
. Given
N\in\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)
, the relation
\mathrm{con}_{N}
does not need to be a congruence (this is the case for
semimedial
and
spelling
left quasigroups including racks, see
[
Bon21
, Theorem 3.5, Theorem 5.6]
).

The set of admissible subgroups is a sublattice of the lattice of normal subgroups of the left multiplication group (see
[
Bon21
, Lemma 1.7]
), and is stable under the usual correspondence between normal subgroups.

Corollary 1.2
.

[
Bon22a
, Proposition 2.4]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha
be a congruence of
Q
. Then the mappings

\displaystyle\{N\in\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)\,:\,\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\leq N\}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q/\alpha)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{\alpha}(N)

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(K)

\displaystyle\leftmapsto K

provides an isomorphism of lattices.

The following lemma shows the interplay between the maps
\pi_{\alpha}
as defined in (
1
) and the operators
\mathcal{O}_{*}
and
\mathrm{con}_{*}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup,
\alpha\in Con(Q)
,
\beta=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}
and
N\in\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)
. If
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\leq N
then:

(i)

\mathcal{O}_{N}/\beta=\mathcal{O}_{\pi_{\beta}(N)}
.

(ii)

\mathrm{con}_{N}/\alpha=\mathrm{con}_{\pi_{\alpha}(N)}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\leq N
we have that
\beta\leq\mathcal{O}_{N}
and
\alpha\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}
.

(i) By definition
[x]_{\beta}\,\mathcal{O}_{N}/\beta\,[y]_{\beta}
if and only if
x=h(y)
for some
h\in N
. Thus, if
x=h(y)
it follows that
[x]_{\beta}=[h(y)]_{\beta}=\pi_{\beta}(h)([y]_{\beta})
and so
[x]_{\beta}\,\mathcal{O}_{N}/\beta\,[y]_{\beta}
. On the other hand if
[x]_{\beta}=\pi_{\beta}(h)([y]_{\beta})=[h(y)]_{\beta}
then
x\,\beta\,h(y)
, i.e.
x=kh(y)
for some
k\in\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}
. Since
\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}\leq N
we have that
hk\in N
and thus
x\,\mathcal{O}_{N}y
.

(ii) If
x\,\mathrm{con}_{N}\,y
then
\pi_{\alpha}(L_{x}L_{y}^{-1})=L_{[x]_{\alpha}}L_{[y]_{\alpha}}^{-1}\in\pi_{\alpha}(N)
. On the other hand, if
[x]_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{con}_{\pi_{\alpha}(N)}\,[y]_{\alpha}
then
L_{x}L_{y}^{-1}\in N\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}=N
.
∎

1.3.
The Cayley kernel

The
Cayley kernel
of a left quasigroup
Q
is the equivalence relation
\lambda_{Q}=\mathrm{con}_{1}
, i.e. given
x,y\in Q
then

x\,\lambda_{Q}\,y\,\text{ if and only if }L_{x}=L_{y}.

In general, the Cayley kernel is not a congruence. If this is the case, we say that
Q
is a
Cayley left quasigroup
(e.g. racks are Cayley left quasigroups).

Remark 1.4
.

[
Bon22a
, Remark 2.7]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha\in Con(Q)
:

(i)

\alpha\leq\lambda_{Q}
if and only if
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}=1
;

(ii)

\lambda_{Q/\alpha}=\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}/\alpha
.

In
[
BS19
]
we show that
strongly abelian congruences
of left quasigroups in the sense of
[
HD88
]
are those below the Cayley kernel. In particular, congruence arising from orbits are related to strongly abelian congruences.

Lemma 1.5
.

[
Bon22a
, Lemma 2.9]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup,
\alpha\in Con(Q)
,
\beta=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}
and
\gamma=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}
. Then
\alpha/\beta\leq\lambda_{Q/\beta}
and
\alpha/\gamma\leq\lambda_{Q/\gamma}
.

A left quasigroup
Q
is said to be
sharp
if given
\alpha,\beta\in Con(Q)
,
\alpha\leq\beta
and
\beta/\alpha\leq\lambda_{Q/\alpha}
then
\alpha=\beta
. The property of being sharp  has also an universal algebraic interpretation: indeed a left quasigroup
Q
is sharp  if and only if every homomorphic image of
Q
omit strongly abelian congruences.

Corollary 1.6
.

The class of sharp  left quasigroups is closed under homorphic images.

Let us show a characterization of sharp  left quasigroups in terms of the operators
\mathcal{O}_{*}
,
\mathrm{Dis}_{*}
and
\mathrm{Dis}^{*}
.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup. The following are equivalent:

(i)

Q
is sharp  .

(ii)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}=\alpha
for every
\alpha\in Con(Q)
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) According to Lemma
1.5
We have
\gamma=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\beta=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}\leq\alpha
and
\alpha/\gamma\leq\lambda_{Q/\gamma}
for every
\alpha\in Con(Q)
. Hence, if
Q
is sharp
\gamma=\beta=\alpha
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
\alpha,\beta\in Con(Q)
. Assume that
\beta\leq\alpha
and
\alpha/\beta\leq\lambda_{Q/\beta}
. Then
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha/\beta}=1
, i.e.
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta}
. So we have that

\alpha=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\beta}}=\beta

and so
\alpha=\beta
.
∎

It is immediate to see that if
Q
is a sharp  left quasigroup, then
\mathcal{O}_{*}
is onto and
\mathrm{Dis}^{*}
and
\mathrm{Dis}_{*}
are injective on
Q
.

A left quasigroup
Q
is
faithful
if
\lambda_{Q}=0_{Q}
(note that faithful left quasigroups are Cayley).

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{O}_{*}=\mathrm{con}_{*}
on
Q
.

(ii)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}=\alpha=\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}=\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}
for every
\alpha\in Con(Q)
.

(iii)

Q/\alpha
is faithful for every
\alpha\in Con(Q)
.

In particular, if (i) holds then
Q
is sharp  .

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Let
\alpha\in Con(Q)
. Since we have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}\leq\alpha\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}}\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}

if
\mathcal{O}_{*}=\mathrm{con}_{*}
equality holds.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) According to Remark
1.4
(ii)
\lambda_{Q/\alpha}=\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}/\alpha
. So we have that
\lambda_{Q/\alpha}=0_{Q}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
N\in\mathrm{Norm}(Q)
and let
\alpha=\mathcal{O}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{N}
. Thus,
\lambda_{Q/\alpha}=\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}/\alpha=0_{Q/\alpha}
i.e.
\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}=\alpha
. Since
N\leq\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}
then
\mathrm{con}_{N}\leq\mathrm{con}_{\mathrm{Dis}^{\alpha}}=\alpha
, and so equality holds.
∎

The property in Lemma
1.8
(iii) is clearly closed under homomorphic images.

2.
Galois connections for left quasigroups

Let
(A,\leq)
and
(B,\leq)
be two posets. A
monotone Galois connection
between these posets consists of two monotone functions:
F:A\longrightarrow B
and
G:B\longrightarrow A
, such that for all
x\in A
and
y\in B
, we have

F(x)\leq y\,\text{ if and only if }\,x\leq G(y).

If
(F,G)
is a Galois connection between two posets, then
FGF=F
and
GFG=G
. In particular, if
F
(resp.
G
) is an isomorphism then
F^{-1}=G
. In such case we say that the pair
(F,G)
provides an isomorphism between the two posets.

The following Galois connection for left quasigroups was first introduced for racks in
[
BS21
]
and then for left quasigroups in general in
[
Bon21
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Bon21
, Theorem 1.10]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup. The pair of mappings
\mathcal{O}_{*}
and
\mathrm{Dis}^{*}
provides a monotone Galois connection between
Con(Q)
and
\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha\in Con(Q)
. According to Lemma

, Corollary

Corollary 2.2
.

The class of left quasigroups having CDOs is closed under homomorphic images.

The following example shows that having CDOs is a relevant property.

Example 2.3
.

Let
Q
be a simple left quasigroup,
N\in\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)
and
\alpha=\mathcal{O}_{N}
. So either
\alpha=0_{Q}
and so
N=1
, or
\alpha=1_{Q}
and thus
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)=\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}\leq N
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Norm}^{\prime}(Q)=\{1,\mathrm{Dis}(Q)\}
. Note that
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)=1
if and only if
\lambda_{Q}=1_{Q}
. We have to discuss two cases:

(i)

Let
\lambda_{Q}=1_{Q}
. If
Q
is idempotent then
Q\cong\mathcal{P}_{2}
, otherwise
Q\cong\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},0,1,1)
for
p

(ii)

If
\lambda_{Q}\neq 1_{Q}

3.
Nilpotency and CDSg property

3.1.
Commutator theory and central extensions

We recall the basics of commutator theory for the reader’s convenience. The commutator theory for arbitrary algebraic structures have been developed in
[
H.76
,
FM87
]
, including the definition of commutator of congruences and the related notions of center, solvability and nilpotency. We will present all the definitions just for left quasigroups.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha,\beta,\delta\in Con(Q)
. We say that
\alpha
centralizes
\beta
over
\delta
, and write
C(\alpha,\beta;\delta)
, if for every
(n+1)
-ary term operation
t
, every pair
x\,\alpha\,y
and every
z_{1}\,\beta\,u_{1}
,
\dots
,
z_{n}\,\beta\,u_{n}
we have

t^{Q}(x,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,\delta\,t^{Q}(x,u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\quad\text{implies}\quad t^{Q}(y,z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,\delta\,t^{Q}(y,u_{1},\dots,u_{n}).

We denote by
[\alpha,\beta]
the
commutator
of
\alpha,\beta\in Con(Q)
, that is the smallest congruence
\delta
such that
C(\alpha,\beta;\delta)
.
A congruence
\alpha
is called:

•

abelian
if
C(\alpha,\alpha;0_{Q})
, i.e., if
[\alpha,\alpha]=0_{Q}
,

•

central
if
C(\alpha,1_{Q};0_{Q})
, i.e., if
[\alpha,1_{Q}]=0_{Q}
.

The largest congruence of
Q
that centralizes
1_{Q}
is called the
center
of
Q
and denoted by
\zeta_{Q}
. A left quasigroup
Q
is called
abelian
if
\zeta_{Q}=1_{Q}
, or, equivalently, if the congruence
1_{Q}
is abelian. We can define a series of congruence of
Q
that plays the same role as the series of centers for groups. The definition is the following:

\zeta_{1}(Q)=\zeta_{Q},\qquad\zeta_{n+1}(Q)/\zeta_{n}(Q)=\zeta_{(Q/\zeta_{n}(Q))}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The left quasigroup
Q
is called
nilpotent
of length
n
if
\zeta_{n}(Q)=1_{Q}
.

The theory of commutators have been adapted to the setting of racks and quandles in
[
BS21
]
. The main results can also be partially applied to the setting of left quasigroups.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
BS21
, Lemma 5.1]

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup,
\alpha,\beta\in Con(Q)
. If
C(\alpha,\beta;0_{Q})
holds then
[\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha},\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta}]=1
and
\left(\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta}\right)_{x}=\left(\mathrm{Dis}_{\beta}\right)_{y}
whenever
x\,\alpha\,y
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(Q,\cdot,\backslash)
be a left quasigroup and
\alpha\leq\zeta_{Q}
. Then
\mathrm{Dis}_{\alpha}
is central in
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)
and
\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{x}=\mathrm{Dis}(Q)_{y}
whenever
x\,\alpha\,y
.

Note that

In this subsection we strengthen

Lemma 3.7

Let

be an idempotent left quasigroup. If

has CDOs then
\mathrm{Aff}(A,1-f,f,0)

Let

is a subquandle of

for some subquandle

containing

and some

. So, it is enough to show that the subquandles containing
0
of
Q^{\prime}
are connected. Let
M
be a subquandle of
Q^{\prime}
containing
0
. Let us denote by

. Note that

and

for every

. So
(1-f)(N)\leq N=f(N)

is a subquandle of

and it contains

.

According to Lemma

if and only if

and

is a congruence of

has CDOs, i.e.

are connected. In particular

Then we have

and so

Equivariant simplicial distributions and quantum contextuality
Cihan Okay, Igor Sikora

Abstract
We introduce an equivariant version of contextuality with respect to a symmetry group, which comes with natural applications to quantum theory. In the equivariant setting, we construct cohomology classes that can detect contextuality. This framework is motivated by the earlier topological approach to contextuality producing cohomology classes that serve as computational primitives in measurement-based quantum computing.



1
Introduction

In quantum theory, measurement statistics is described by a family of probability distributions associated to quantum measurements that can be performed simultaneously.
Such a family of distributions is called contextual if a joint probability distribution cannot describe the measurement statistics.
The sheaf-theoretic approach of
[
1
]
is a natural framework to capture this notion.
More recently, a topological approach has been introduced in
[
2
]
based on chain complexes and tailored for applications in measurement-based quantum computing.
Simplicial distributions introduced in
[
3
]
is a new framework that unifies these two earlier approaches and goes beyond by generalizing the notion of contextuality to measurements and outcomes described by spaces rather than discrete sets.
In
[
2
]
, topological techniques are first introduced to describe proofs of contextuality for (1) parity proofs based on algebraic relations among the observables and (2) symmetry-based proofs that rely on a transformation group
acting on the observables. Moreover, the two kinds of proofs can be related using the theory of chain complexes.
This paper extends this analysis to simplicial distributions.
Our main contribution is the notion of equivariant contextuality with respect to a symmetry group and the construction of cohomology classes, which can detect this type of contextuality. These cohomology classes are related to those introduced in
[
2
]
, which play an important role in quantum computational schemes such as measurement-based quantum computation
[
4
,
5
]
and quantum computation with magic states
[
6
]
for quantifying computational advantage. As a future direction, the authors plan to investigate the computational significance of the cohomology classes introduced in the equivariant setting.

The framework of simplicial distributions is based on simplicial sets, combinatorial models of spaces more expressive than simplicial complexes.
A simplicial distribution on a pair of simplicial sets
X
and
Y
, which represent the measurements and outcomes, is given by a simplicial set map

p:X\to D_{R}(Y)

The simplicial set
D_{R}(Y)
has simplices given by
R
-valued distributions on the set of simplices of
Y
, where
R
is a semiring.
Any simplicial set map
s:X\to Y
can be turned into a simplicial distribution
\delta^{s}=\delta_{Y}\circ s
, called a deterministic distribution, by composing with
\delta_{Y}:Y\to D_{R}(Y)
that sends a simplex of the outcome space to the delta-distribution peaked at that simplex.
A simplicial distribution is called contextual if it cannot be expressed as a probabilistic mixture of deterministic distributions.
Given a group
G
and a pair of simplicial
G
-sets
(X,Y)
, we introduce the notion of
G
-equivariant contextuality.
The basic idea is to require equivariance with respect to
G
in the definitions of simplicial and deterministic distributions.

In quantum theory, commutation relation among operators can be used to define simplicial sets that are variations of the well-known nerve construction in algebraic topology.
Such simplicial sets are called partial groups, a notion introduced in
[
7
]
.
A typical example is defined for the unitary group
U(\mathcal{H})
of a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and consists of the
n
-simplices given by

N(\mathbb{Z}_{d},U(\mathcal{H}))=\{(A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{n}):\,A_{i}^{d}=\mathbb{1},\;\;A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}\;\forall i,j\}.

This space and its variants have been studied in
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Identifying unitary operators that differ by an element in
\langle e^{2\pi i/d}\mathbb{1}\rangle
gives a central partial group extension in the sense of
[
7
]
.
We develop our theory for a central partial group extension of the form

NA\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{\pi}M

where
A
is a finite abelian group and
NA
is the nerve space.
This extension is classified by a cohomology class
[\beta]\in H^{2}(M,A)
. A simplicial distribution
p:E\to D_{R}(NA)
is said to be relative to
i:NA\to E
if the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{R}(NA)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

We show that if
[\beta]\neq 0
, then every such simplicial distribution is contextual (Corollary
4.12
).
In the equivariant setting, we consider a group
G
that acts on
E
by partial group automorphisms fixing
NA
. Our main tool in the equivariant setting is the Borel construction, which sends a simplicial
G
-set
X
to the simplicial set given by
X/\!/G=EG\times_{G}X
. Applying this construction to the partial group extension gives another partial group extension

NA\to E/\!/G\to M/\!/G

Let
[\beta_{G}]\in H^{2}(M/\!/G,A)
denote the cohomology class
classifying this
extension.
On the other hand, we can think of the Borel construction as the diagonal of the bisimplicial set
S_{G}(X)
whose
(p,q)
-simplices are given by
(EG)_{p}\times_{G}X_{q}
.
The total complex of the associated double complex of cochains on this bisimplicial set provides an alternative way of studying the cohomology class
[\beta_{G}]
.
In the total complex, we have two important cocycles:
First of all,
\beta
can be regarded as a
(0,2)
-cocycle.
Given a pseudo-section
\eta:M\to E
of
\pi
(similar to a simplicial set map except that it fails to be compatible with the
d_{0}
face), we have a
(1,1)
-cocycle
\Phi:G\times M_{1}\to A
defined by

\Phi_{g}(x)=(g\cdot\eta(g^{-1}\cdot x))\cdot\eta(x)^{-1}.

Our main result is the following (Theorem
4.8
and Corollary
4.15
):

Theorem
.

Let
NA\xrightarrow{x}E\xrightarrow{\pi}M
be a central partial group extension and
G
be a group acting on
E
by partial group automorphisms that fix
NA
.
The following are equivalent:

1.

The class
[\beta_{G}]
is zero.

2.

There exists
s:M_{1}\to A
such that
d^{v}s=\beta
and
d^{h}s=\Phi
.

3.

The map
\pi
admits a
G
-equivariant section.

Moreover, if
[\beta_{G}]\neq 0
then every
G
-equivariant distribution
p:E\to D_{R}(NA)
relative to

is
G
-equivariantly contextual.

There is a counterpart to the partial group approach, which uses cofiber sequences of spaces. We develop this approach in Section
2
and prove a result analogous to the theorem above (Theorem
3.12
).
Equivariant distributions naturally arise in quantum theory.
The nerve space
N(\mathbb{Z}_{d},U(\mathcal{H}))
can be regarded as the total space of a partial group extension and a quantum state (density operator) can be used to obtain a simplicial distribution relative to the fiber, which is
N\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of equivariant distributions and equivariant contextuality. We give a simple example, torus with the involution action, to discuss these basic notions (Section
2.6
). We also introduce distributions on the simplicial circle.
Section
3
is about the relative version of contextuality and mainly focuses on introducing the cohomological notions. The total complex of the Borel construction is studied in this section. We end the section by elaborating on the torus example.
Quantum distributions are discussed in Section
4
. We introduce
partial group extensions and group actions on them. Our main theorem stated above is proved in this section. The counterpart to the torus example is the nerve of the dihedral group, also discussed in this section. We show how quantum theory naturally gives simplicial distributions relative to a subspace. We finish with the
Mermin star construction, a well-known example of a contextual scenario introduced in
[
11
]
, analyzed in the equivariant setting. Our methods extend
the earlier topological study presented in
[
2
]
by demonstrating that this construction produces equivariantly contextual simplicial distributions.

2.1
Simplicial distributions

Let
R
be a commutative zero-sum-free semiring, i.e.,
a+b=0
implies
a=b=0
for all
a,b,\in R
.
The
distribution monad

D_{R}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
is defined as follows:

•

The set
D_{R}(U)
of
R
-distributions on a set
U
consists of functions
p:U\to R
of finite support, i.e.,
|\{u\in U:\,p(u)\neq 0\}|<\infty
,
such that
\sum_{u\in U}p(u)=1
.

•

Given a function
f:U\to V
the function
D_{R}(f):D_{R}(U)\to D_{R}(V)
is defined by

p\mapsto\left(v\mapsto\sum_{u\in f^{-1}(v)}p(u)\right).

Let
\mathbf{\Delta}
denote the simplex category with objects
[n]=\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
where
n\geq 0
and morphisms given by ordinal maps
\theta:[m]\to[n]
. This category is generated by morphisms of the form

•

coface maps
d^{i}:[n-1]\to[n]
that skip
i
in the target, and

•

codegeneracy maps
s^{j}:[n+1]\to[n]
that has a double preimage at
j
.

A simplicial set is a functor

X:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}

A simplicial set map
f:X\to Y
between two simplicial sets is a natural transformation. We will denote the category of simplicial sets by
\mathbf{sSet}
.
Alternatively,
a simplicial set is a sequence of sets
X_{n}
, where
n\geq 0
, together with the face
d_{i}
and the degeneracy maps
s_{j}
dual to the coface
d^{i}
and the codegeneracy
s^{j}
maps.
In this description a simplicial set map consists of a family of functions
\{f_{n}:X_{n}\to Y_{n}\}_{{\geq 0}}

compatible with the face and degeneracy maps. Given
\sigma\in X_{n}
we will write

f_{\sigma}\in Y_{n}
for the value of the function
f
on
\sigma
.
The standard
d
-simplex
\Delta[d]
is the simplicial set with
n
-simplices given by the set of ordinal maps
[n]\to[d]
. In other words, this is the functor represented by
[d]
.
We will write
\sigma^{01\cdots d}
for the
d
-simplex in
\Delta[d]
corresponding to the identity map
\text{id}:[d]\to[d]
. Observe that every other
n
-simplex can be obtained from
\sigma^{01\cdots n}
by an application of face and degeneracy maps.

The functor
D_{R}
extends to a monad on simplicial sets

D_{R}:\mathbf{sSet}\to\mathbf{sSet}

sending a simplicial set
X:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
to the composite functor
D_{R}(X):\mathbf{\Delta}^{\text{op}}\xrightarrow{X}\mathbf{Set}\xrightarrow{D_{R}}\mathbf{Set}
; see
[
12
]
.
We will write
\delta_{X}:X\to D_{R}(X)
for the unit of this monad that sends
x
to the delta-distribution with a peak at
x
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be simplicial sets.
A
simplicial distribution
on
(X,Y)
is a simplicial set map
p:X\to D_{R}(Y)
.
A
deterministic distribution
is a simplicial distribution of the form
\delta^{s}:X\xrightarrow{s}Y\xrightarrow{\delta_{Y}}D_{R}(Y)
where
s:X\to Y
is a simplicial set map.
We write
\mathsf{sDist}(X,Y)
and
\mathsf{dDist}(X,Y)
for the sets of simplicial and deterministic distributions, respectively.
There is a comparison map

\Theta:D_{R}(\mathsf{dDist}(X,Y))\to\mathsf{sDist}(X,Y)

that sends a distribution
d=\sum_{r}d(r)\,\delta^{r}

to the simplicial distribution
\Theta(d):X\to D_{R}Y
defined by

\Theta(d)_{\sigma}:\theta\mapsto\sum_{r\,:\,r_{\sigma}=\theta}d(r)

where
\sigma\in X_{n}
,
\theta\in Y_{n}
and
r:X\to Y
runs over simplicial set maps such that
r_{\sigma}=\theta
.
A simplicial distribution is called
non-contextual
if it lies in the image of
\Theta
. Otherwise, it is called
contextual
.

Example 2.2
.

Let us consider simplicial distributions of the form
p:\Delta[2]\to D_{R}(N\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Such a simplicial set map is determined by a distribution
p_{\sigma}\in D_{R}(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2})
where
\sigma=\sigma^{012}
.
Writing
p^{ab}
for the values
p_{\sigma}(a,b)
this distribution is specified by a tuple
(p^{00},p^{01},p^{10},p^{11})\in R^{4}
such that
\sum_{a,b}p^{ab}=1
.
A deterministic distribution
\delta^{s}
for a simplicial set map
s:\Delta[2]\to N\mathbb{Z}_{2}
will be determined by a delta-distribution at
s_{\sigma}=(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
. We write
\delta^{ab}
for this simplicial distribution. Then any simplicial distribution
p
can be written as

p=\sum_{a,b}p^{ab}\delta^{ab},

and hence is non-contextual. More complicated simplicial distributions can be constructed by gluing
2
-simplices. In this case additional relations will be introduced as the faces. The distribution
p_{d_{i}\sigma}
associated to a face of
\sigma
will be an element of
D_{R}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. We will write
(p_{d_{i}\sigma})^{a}
to denote its value as
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. We have
(p_{d_{i}\sigma})^{0}+(p_{d_{i}\sigma})^{1}=1
. Then

(p_{d_{i}\sigma})^{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{00}+p^{10}&i=0\\
p^{00}+p^{11}&i=1\\
p^{00}+p^{01}&i=2.\end{array}\right.

When two
2
-simplices are glued at a common face, say the
i
-th face of
\sigma_{1}
and the
j
-th face of
\sigma_{2}
, then we have

d_{i}p_{\sigma_{1}}=p_{d_{i}\sigma_{1}}=p_{d_{j}\sigma_{2}}=d_{j}p_{\sigma_{2}}.

This way we can construct contextual simplicial distributions. See
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
for examples.

Partial groups

In this section we recall the notion of partial monoids and groups from

We need two ordinal maps:

Definition 2.3
.

A
partial monoid
is a non-empty simplicial set
M
such that

•

M
is reduced, i.e.,
M_{0}=\{\ast\}
,

•

The

is injective for all

is induced by the ordinal map

.

A homomorphism of partial monoids is a simplicial set map.

A simplex

can be identified with the tuple
\mathbf{e}_{n}(x)=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
.
Let
\Pi_{n}:M_{n}\to M_{1}
denote the map induced by the ordinal map

The simplex

will also be denoted by

to emphasize that this element represents the product.
The degenerate simplex

will be denoted by

Let

2.5
Distributions on the circle

The set of
n

\Delta[1]
is given by

\Delta[1]_{n}=\{\theta^{i}|\;0\leq i\leq n+1\}

Let

4
Quantum distributions

Equivariant simplicial distributions arise naturally in quantum theory. In this section we provide an approach based on partial groups, which is complementary to the cofibration approach of the previous sections. Then we apply our theory to equivariant simplicial distributions that are obtained from quantum theory. In Section
4.6
we consider an important example, known as the Mermin star construction, which also has applications to
measurement-based quantum computing
[
4
]
. We show that this construction produces equivariantly contextual simplicial distributions and this kind of contextuality can be detected by the cohomology classes we construct in the theory.

4.1
Extensions of partial groups

We recall the theory of extensions of partial groups from
[
7
]
.
There are two important groups associated to a partial group:

•

The normalizer of the partial group
M
is defined by

N(M)=\{\theta\in M_{1}:\,\exists\,\text{id}_{M}\xrightarrow{\theta}f_{\theta}\}.

•

The center of

is the subgroup

consisting of those

such that
\text{id}_{M}\xrightarrow{\theta}\text{id}_{M}
.

We are interested in the case where

. We have
Z(NA)=N(NA)=A
.
Extensions of partial groups are described by fiber bundles.
Let

be another partial group. The extension of

by

is defined to be a fiber bundle

N\to E\to M

In the theory of partial groups the total space

can be described by a twisted product.
An

-twisting pair for

is a pair of functions
(t,\beta)
where

t:M_{1}\to\text{Aut}(N)\;\;\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\;\beta:M_{2}\to N(N)

satisfying

•

\beta(x,y)
determines a homotopy
t(x\cdot y)\to t(x)\cdot t(y)
,

•

t(1)=\text{id}
and
\beta(x,1)=1=\beta(1,x)
for all
x\in M_{1}
,

•

for all
(x,y,z)\in M_{3}
,

t(x)(\beta(y,z))\cdot\beta(x,y\cdot z)=\beta(x,y)\cdot\beta(x\cdot y,z).

The pair
(t,\beta)
can be used to define a twisting function

and the total space can be described as the twisted product
E=N\times_{\tau}M
; see
[
7
, Theorem 4.5]
. We will not go into the description of
\tau
in general. We will be more explicit when
N=NA
, the case of interest for us. In this paper we will need two extreme cases of extensions:

•

\beta
is trivial: In this case the twisted product is simply denoted by
N\ltimes M
. Our main example will come from an action of a group

on
M
. We will consider
NG\ltimes M
.

•

t
is trivial: Such extensions are called central. We will consider the case where
N=NA
. Note that in this case
\beta:M_{2}\to A
is precisely a
2
-cocycle. We will write
NA\times_{\beta}M
for the twisted product.

4.5
Quantum distributions

We recall some basic constructions from
[
3
, Section 6]
to study simplicial distributions that come from quantum mechanics.
Let
\mathcal{H}
denote a finite dimensional complex Hilbert space. We will write
\text{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
and
\text{Proj}(\mathcal{H})
for the set of positive operators and the subset of projectors.
We define a functor
P_{\mathcal{H}}:\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
:

•

For a set
U
the set
P_{\mathcal{H}}(U)
of projective measurements on
U
consists of functions
\Pi:U\to\text{Proj}(\mathcal{H})
with finite support, i.e.,
|\{u\in U:\,\Pi(u)=\mathbb{0}\}|<\infty
, such that
\sum_{u\in U}\Pi(u)=\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

•

Given a function
f:U\to V
the function
P_{\mathcal{H}}(f):P_{\mathcal{H}}(U)\to P_{\mathcal{H}}(V)
is defined by

\Pi\mapsto\left(v\mapsto\sum_{u\in f^{-1}(v)}\Pi(u)\right).

There is an analogous functor
Q_{\mathcal{H}}
involving
\text{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
instead of the projectors. Note that for
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}
this functor coincides with the distribution monad
D
introduced in Section
2.1
.
In this paper we will focus on the projective version.
Given a simplicial set

we define
P_{\mathcal{H}}(X):X\xrightarrow{X}\mathbf{Set}\xrightarrow{P_{\mathcal{H}}}\mathbf{Set}
.

Definition 4.17
.

A simplicial projective measurement on
(X,Y)
is a simplicial set map
\Pi:X\to P_{\mathcal{H}}(Y)
. We will write
\text{sProj}(X,Y)
for the set of simplicial projective measurements on
(X,Y)
.

Let
\rho
be a density operator, i.e.,
\rho\in\text{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
and
\text{Tr}(\rho)=1
.
By sending a simplicial measurement
\Pi:X\to P_{\mathcal{H}}(Y)
to the simplicial distribution
\rho_{*}(p):X\to D(Y)
defined by

\sigma\mapsto\left(\,\theta\mapsto\text{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{\sigma}(\theta))\,\right)

we obtain a simplicial set map

(31)

\rho_{*}:P_{\mathcal{H}}(Y)\to D(Y)

The trace
\text{Tr}(\rho\Pi_{\sigma}(\theta))
is interpreted
as
the probability of obtaining the outcome
\theta
and in physics literature this is known as the Born rule.
The simplicial set map in (
31

.

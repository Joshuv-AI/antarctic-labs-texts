Infinite Speed of Propagation of Fractional Dispersive Waves
Brian Choi, Steven Walton

Abstract
In this paper, it is shown that all non-trivial solutions of a wide class of dispersive equations, defined by a dispersion relation with minimal hypotheses on its regularity, are compactly supported for at most one time element. With plane waves of different frequencies traveling at different speeds, the finite speed of propagation is not expected for dispersive phenomena, and this intuition is made rigorous by complex-analytic arguments. Furthermore the method developed here applies to time fractional-order systems given by the Caputo derivative where the generalized space-time fractional Schrödinger equation is considered for concreteness. When the energy operator i ∂ t i\partial_{t} and the Riesz derivative ( − Δ ) β 2 (-\Delta)^{\frac{\beta}{2}} are fractionalized to the same order, it is shown that the sharp frequency-localized dispersive estimates yield the time decay of solutions that depends on both the spatial dimension and the long-memory effect. Asymptotically in time, the solution operator strongly, but not uniformly, converges in L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) to the half-wave operator.

Introduction.

A solution to a PDE is said to exhibit
infinite speed of propagation
(ISP) whenever an initial datum given by a function with compact support, thought of as a localized wave, instantly spreads out in its spatial profile. In this paper, we extend the results of
[
21
,
3

w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

i\partial_{t}u=Lu,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\ u(x,0)=\phi(x),

(1)

where

is a Fourier multiplier given by
\widehat{Lf}(\xi)=w(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)
; see
Section

1.1
for notations. By a one-parameter family of unitary operators

and the fundamental solution

, the solution has an analytic form

u(x,t)=U(t)\phi(x)=K_{t}\ast\phi(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi-tw(\xi))}\widehat{\phi}(\xi)d\xi,

from which ISP cannot be observed directly unless

corresponding to the well-known Schrödinger equation or the Airy equation, respectively. Even for those well-known examples, verifying ISP directly for a general

As for the fractional Schrödinger equation, it is not directly accessible to observe how the solution support evolves over time from the analytic representation of the fundamental solution given by the Fox H-function (see

Our interest extends to understanding ISP for time-fractional dispersive phenomena governed by the Caputo derivative

3

Here we restrict attention to the dynamics on the real line. Our goal is to show

4
Fractional Traveling Waves.

This section is concerned with the dispersive (and non-dispersive) properties of (
2
) where it is assumed that
0<\alpha<1,\ \beta>0,\ \alpha\leq\gamma\leq 1
unless specified. The fundamental solution is given by
K_{t}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[E_{\alpha}(i^{-\gamma}t^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\beta})]
for

. By

, the compactness of solution support is preserved in time for the first-order linear systems only for the advection equation when space and time scale linearly, i.e.,
\Delta x\sim\Delta t
. For (
2

\alpha=\beta<1
(when

) since
|\xi|
is not analytic at the origin. When
\alpha=\beta=\gamma<1

although the solution support loses compactness instantaneously.

Let us consider some special cases of (
2

\alpha=\beta=\gamma=2
, (
2
) reduces to the well-known linear wave equation whose exact solution is given by the d’Alembert’s formula. For
\alpha=1,\ \beta=2,\ \gamma=2
, (

\alpha=\gamma
, the explicit formula of the fundamental solution

for (

[

by the Fox H-function as

K_{\alpha,\beta}^{(0)}(x,t)=\frac{1}{|x|}H_{3,3}^{2,1}\left(-\frac{i^{\alpha}|x|^{\beta}}{t^{\alpha}}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}(1,1),(1,\alpha),(1,\frac{\beta}{2})\\
(1,\beta),(1,1),(1,\frac{\beta}{2})\\
\end{array}\right.\right).

(24)

The

-function defined via the Mellin-Barnes integral. See Appendix A of
[
10
]
for an introduction to the topic.

Here we investigate the solutions of (

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
. Let

\widehat{K_{t}^{hw}}(\xi)=e^{-it|\xi|},\ U^{hw}(t)\phi:=K_{t}^{hw}\ast\phi.

Proposition 4.1

For
